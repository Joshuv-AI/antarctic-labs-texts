Abstract
. Given a continuous Gaussian process x x which gives rise to a p p -geometric rough path for p ∈ ( 2 , 3 ) p\in(2,3) , and a general continuous process y y controlled by x x , under proper conditions we establish the relationship between the Skorohod integral ∫ 0 t y s ​ d ⋄ ​ x s \int_{0}^{t}y_{s}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{s} and the Stratonovich integral ∫ 0 t y s ​ d ​ 𝐱 s \int_{0}^{t}y_{s}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{s} . Our strategy is to employ the tools from rough paths theory and Malliavin calculus to analyze discrete sums of the integrals.

1.1.
Background

{sec:background}

In recent decades, two approaches for the analysis of dynamical systems driven by Gaussian processes have been greatly developed: (i) the “probabilistic” approach, which invokes stochastic analysis tools and leads to Itô-Skorohod integration, and (ii) the “pathwise” approach which employs the theory of rough paths and gives rise to Stratonovich integration. In general, one gets a more transparent understanding of the system by using the pathwise approach, while it is more convenient to explore probabilistic properties (e.g. compute the moments for the solution of a noisy dynamical system driven by a Gaussian noise) via the probabilistic approach. One key ingredient to understand the connection between these two approaches is the relationship between Skorohod and Stratonovich integrals.

For a standard Brownian motion, the relationship between Itô and Stratonovich integrals is well-known. It is classically obtained by Itô calculus, although rough paths theory can also be invoked by observing that both Itô and Stratonovich integrals can be regarded as integrals against rough paths lifted from a Brownian motion with different second order terms. For general Gaussian processes (consider fractional Brownian motion as a typical example), however, it is non-trivial to obtain the relationship. Indeed, for a general Gaussian process Itô calculus (or martingale calculus) is not available, and moreover Skorohod integrals cannot be regarded as integrals against rough paths lifted from the corresponding Gaussian processes. We briefly recall some results giving Skorohod-Stratonovich corrections below.

Let
x=(x^{1},\dots,x^{d})
be a
d
-dimensional centered Gaussian process with i.i.d components giving rise to a
p
-geometric rough path, where
2<p<3
(see Section
2.1
for more details about geometric rough paths). Denote by
R
the covariance function of
x
, namely
R(s,t)=\mathbf{E}[x_{t}^{1}x_{s}^{1}]
. We also set
R_{t}=R(t,t).
The correction terms between Skorohod and Stratonovich integrals with respect to
x
have been considered in the following cases:

(i)

In
[
15
]
, the Skorohod-Stratonovich corrections were computed for integrals of the form
\int_{s}^{t}\nabla f(x_{u})\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{u}
for a smooth function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. More specifically,

(1)

\int_{0}^{t}\nabla f(x_{r})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}\nabla f(x_{r})\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\Delta f(x_{r})\mathrm{d}R_{r},

where the integral with respect to
x
on the left-hand side is a Stratonovich integral while the one on the right-hand side is a Skorohod integral. The strategy in
[
15
]
relied on the fact that
\int_{0}^{t}\nabla f(x_{u})\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{u}
is obtained by taking limits of Riemann-Wick sums of the form:

(2)

S^{\Pi_{st},\diamond}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(x_{t_{i}})\diamond{\big({\bf x}^{{\bf 1}}_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}\big)}^{\diamond k},

where
\diamond
stands for the Wick product.
Then the Skorohod-Stratonovich corrections in
[
15
]
were analyzed thanks to a computation of the Wick corrections in (
2
). Notice that an extension of this result to the case of a Gaussian process indexed by
[0,1]^{2}
with Hölder exponent greater than
1/3
was handled in
[
24
]
. There some change of variable formulas were derived for the Stratonovich and Skorohod integrals respectively. As a consequence, the correction terms were computed explicitly. Also note that some preliminary cases for a 1-d fractional Brownian motion had been considered in
[
22
]
.

(ii)

The reference
[
4
]
is concerned with solutions of rough differential equations driven by
x
, where
x
is again a
d
-dimensional centered Gaussian process with i.i.d components giving rise to a
p
-geometric rough path (recall that
2<p<3
). The equation can be written as

(3)

\mathrm{d}y_{r}=\sigma(y_{r})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r},

with a smooth enough coefficient
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and we refer to
[
11
]
for more details about this object. Below we denote by
y_{0}
the initial condition of (
3
), and
J_{u}^{x}
is designated as the Jacobian of the flow map
y_{0}\to y_{u}
. Then the formula for the correction terms in
[
4
]
can be read as

(4)

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\text{tr}[\sigma(y_{r})]\mathrm{d}R_{r}\\
+\int_{[0<r_{1}<r_{2}<t]}\text{tr}\left[J_{r_{2}}^{x}(J_{r_{1}}^{x})^{-1}\sigma(y_{r_{1}})-\sigma(y_{r_{2}})\right]\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

Consider the
i
-th column
\sigma_{i}(x)
of the coefficient matrix
\sigma(x)
as a vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
1\leq i\leq d
. If the Lie bracket
[\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}]=\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j\leq d
, then the solution
y_{t}
to (
3
) is of the form
y_{t}=\varphi(x_{t},y_{0})
with
(\nabla_{x}\cdot\varphi)(x,y_{0})=\text{tr}[\sigma(x,y_{0})]
and
J_{t}^{x}=\sigma(y_{t})
(see
[
1
, Proposition 24]
). Clearly in this case, (
4
) coincides with (
1
), noting that the last term on the right-hand side of (
4
) now vanishes. Therefore, relation (
4
) is indeed compatible with (
1
).

1.2.
Main result and strategy

{sec:main-result}

In this paper, we consider a
d
-dimensional centered Gaussian process
x
with i.i.d components. Let
y
be a
controlled
process relative to
x
. That is, the increments
\delta y_{st}:=y_{t}-y_{s}
can be decomposed along the increments of
x
as follows:

(5)

\delta y_{st}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{s}^{x;ij}\,{\bf x}_{st}^{{\bf 1};j}+r_{st}^{i},~\text{ for }i=1,\dots,d,

where
y^{x}
has finite
p
-variation and the remainder
r
has finite
\frac{p}{2}
-variation (one can alternatively use Hölder spaces in this definition). Notice that controlled processes are the natural class of functions for which a proper rough integration with respect to
x
can be constructed (see e.g
[
13
]
).
The following is the main result of this paper (see Theorem
3.1
below for a more precise statement). Under proper conditions on
x
and
y
,

(6)

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{x;ii}\mathrm{d}R_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{[0<r_{1}<r_{2}<t]}\left(\mathbf{D}^{i}_{r_{1}}y^{i}_{r_{2}}-y_{r_{2}}^{x;ii}\right)\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

On the left-hand side of (
6
), the integral
\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}
is understood in the rough path sense (see Proposition
2.20
below for further details). On the right-hand side of the same equation,
\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}
stands for the Skorohod integral,
y^{x}
is defined by (
5
),
R
is the covariance function alluded to above and
\mathbf{D}
represents the Malliavin derivative (notions of Malliavin calculus will be recalled in Section
2.4
).

Note that our formula (
6
) unifies the previous cases (
1
) and (
4
). Indeed, we have argued that (
4
) can be seen as an extension of (
1
). Furthermore, note that the solution
y
to the rough differential equation (
3
) with a sufficiently regular coefficient function
\sigma(y)
is a controlled process with
y_{s}^{x;ij}=(\sigma(y_{s}))_{ij}
and
\mathbf{D}_{s}y_{t}=J_{t}^{x}(J_{s}^{x})^{-1}\sigma(y_{s})
for
s\leq t
. Therefore it is easy to see that (
6
) is an extension of (
4
), which is the main result of
[
4
]
.

Inspired by
[
4
,
15
]
, our proof of the main result is based on the discrete sums method combined with tools from rough paths theory and Malliavin calculus,
in which some discrete techniques developed in
[
17
]
are also invoked. We outline the idea as follows.

Consider a controlled process
y
with a decomposition given by (
5
), satisfying some path regularity and Malliavin differentiability conditions. Let
\pi=\pi^{n}
denote the uniform partition of
[0,T]
and
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space associated to
x
. Denote

y^{\pi}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(t).

We first prove that (see Lemma
3.2
)

\lim_{n\to\infty}y^{\pi}=y\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathcal{H}).

This enables us to show the convergence of the discrete Skorohod integral
\delta^{\diamond}(y^{\pi})
to the Skorohod integral
\delta^{\diamond}(y)=\int_{0}^{T}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

(7)

\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{t_{m}}^{i}\diamond{\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{m}t_{m+1}}\right]\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega),

where we have written
\pi=\{t_{0},\ldots,t_{n}\}
with
t_{m}=s+m(t-s)/n
for
m=0,\ldots,n.

It is also known from the rough paths theory that the following holds true almost surely:

(8)

\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}=\lim_{|\pi|\to 0}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(y_{t_{k}}^{i}{\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}{\bf x}^{2;ji}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right),

where the left-hand side above stands for the rough paths integral of
y
with respect to
x
.

The Stratonovich-Skorohod correction terms in (
6
) now can be obtained by computing the difference between the right-hand sides in (
7
) and (
8
). When computing the difference, one key ingredient will be the forthcoming Proposition
2.28
. This proposition is inspired by the analogous results in
[
17
]
and establishes a general estimate for weighted sums in the second chaos of the Gaussian process
x
.

To end this subsection, we provide some perspectives for future works. On the one hand, as an application, some central limit theorems for Skorohod integrals could be obtained with the help of our main result, generalizing the results in
[
17
]
and
[
19
]
. On the other hand, noting that in this article the Gaussian rough paths with finite
p
-variation for
p\in(1,3)
are handled, we believe that our methodology can be carried out for rougher
Gaussian paths with
p\geq 3
. It is also interesting to consider the correction terms for the processes arising from delay equations (
[
18
]
), Volterra equations (
[
5
,
6
,
14
]
), etc.

. Take

’s such that

{sec:notation}

For

and

2.
Preliminary material

{sec:preliminary-material}

This section contains some basic tools from rough paths theory and Malliavin calculus, as well as some analytical results, which are crucial for the definition and integration of controlled processes.

2.1.
Rough path above
\mathbf{x}

{sec:rough-path-above-X}

In this subsection we shall recall the notion of a rough path above a signal
x
, and how this applies to Gaussian signals. The interested reader is referred to
[
8
,
11
,
13
]
for further details.

As mentioned in Section
1.3
, for
s<t
and
m\geq 1
, we consider the simplex
\mathcal{S}_{m}([s,t])=\{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in[s,t]^{m};\,u_{1}<\cdots<u_{m}\}
. For notational sake, we just write
\mathcal{S}_{m}
for
\mathcal{S}_{m}([0,T])
. The definition of a rough path above a signal
x
relies on the following notion of increments.

Definition 2.1
.

{def:increments}

Let
k\geq 1
. Then the space of
(k-1)
-increments, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{k}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
or simply
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, is defined as

\mathcal{C}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\equiv\left\{g\in C(\mathcal{S}_{k};\mathbb{R}^{d});\,\lim_{t_{i}\to t_{i+1}}g_{t_{1}\cdots t_{k}}=0,\,i\leq k-1\right\}.

We now introduce a finite difference operator called
\delta
, which acts on increments and is useful to split iterated integrals into simpler pieces.

Definition 2.2
.

{def:delta-on-C1-C2}

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
h\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then for
(s,u,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{3}
, we set

\delta g_{st}=g_{t}-g_{s},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\delta h_{sut}=h_{st}-h_{su}-h_{ut}.

The regularity of increments in
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
will be measured in terms of
p
-variation as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

{def:var-norms-on-C2}

For
f\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
p>0
, we define

\|f\|_{p-{\rm var}}=\|f\|_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}=\sup_{(t_{i})\in\mathcal{D}([0,T])}\left(\sum_{i}|f_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The set of increments in
\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with finite
p
-variation is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p{\rm-var}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Note that for a continuous function
g:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite
p
-variation, if we set
\|g\|_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}=\|\delta g\|_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}
, then we recover its usual
p
-variation.

With these preliminary definitions in hand, we can now introduce the notion of a rough path.

Definition 2.4
.

{def:RP}

Let
x
be a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path with finite
p
-variation for some
p\geq 1
. We say that
x
gives rise to a geometric
p
-rough path if there exists a family

\left\{{\bf x}^{n;i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}_{st};\,(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2},\,n\leq\lfloor p\rfloor,\,i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\right\},

such that
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{1}}_{st}=\delta x_{st}
and

(1) Regularity:

For all
n\leq\lfloor p\rfloor
, each component of
\mathbf{x}^{n}
has finite
\frac{p}{n}
-variation
in the sense of Definition
2.3
.

(2) Multiplicativity:

With
\delta\mathbf{x}^{n}
as in Definition
2.2
, we have

(9)

\delta{\bf x}^{n;i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}_{sut}=\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{n-1}{\bf x}^{n_{1};i_{1},\ldots,i_{n_{1}}}_{su}{\bf x}^{n-n_{1};i_{n_{1}+1},\ldots,i_{n}}_{ut}.

(3) Geometricity:

Let
x^{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of piecewise smooth approximations of
x
. For any
n\leq\lfloor p\rfloor
and any set of indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, we assume that
{\bf x}^{\varepsilon,n;i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}
converges in
\frac{p}{n}
-variation to
{\bf x}^{n;i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}
, where
{\bf x}^{\varepsilon,n;i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}_{st}
is defined for
(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
by

{\bf x}^{\varepsilon,n;i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}}_{st}=\int_{(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\in\mathcal{S}_{n}([s,t])}dx_{u_{1}}^{\varepsilon,i_{1}}\cdots dx_{u_{n}}^{\varepsilon,i_{n}}.

We are now ready to state one of the main assumptions on our standing process
x
.

Hypothesis 2.5
.

{hyp:nth-iterated-intg-x}

Throughout the paper,
x
will designate a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path with finite
p
-variation for
p\geq 1
. We assume that
x
gives rise to a geometric rough path in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

On top of Hypothesis
2.5
, we assume that
x_{t}=(x_{t}^{1},\ldots,x_{t}^{d})
is a continuous centered Gaussian process with i.i.d. components, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
. The covariance function of
x
is given by

(10)

R(s,t):=\mathbf{E}\left[x_{s}^{j}x_{t}^{j}\right],

for any
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.
Throughout the paper, we will also set
R_{t}:=R(t,t)
.

The information on the path regularity of
x
is mostly contained in the rectangular increments
R_{uv}^{st}
of its covariance function
R
, which are defined as

(11)

R_{uv}^{st}:=\mathbf{E}\left[(x_{t}^{j}-x_{s}^{j})\,(x_{v}^{j}-x_{u}^{j})\right].

The regularity of
R
is expressed thanks to some
2
d-variation type quantities. For sake of clarity we first recall the definition of the 2d
\rho
-variation.

Definition 2.6
.

{def:2d-variation}

Let
\rho\in[1,\infty)
. For a general continuous function
R:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, its 2d
\rho
-variation is defined as

(12)

\displaystyle\|R\|_{\rho-{\rm var};[s,t]\times[u,v]}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{i})\in\mathcal{D}([s,t])\\
(t_{j}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{D}\left(\left[u,v\right]\right)\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{t^{\prime}_{j}}\sum_{t_{i}}\left|R_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}^{t_{j}^{\prime}t_{j+1}^{\prime}}\right|^{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}.

where

(13)

R_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}^{t_{j}^{\prime}t_{j+1}^{\prime}}=R(t_{i+1},t_{j+1}^{\prime})-R(t_{i+1},t_{j}^{\prime})-R(t_{i},t_{j+1}^{\prime})+R(t_{i},t_{j}^{\prime}).

Observe that, whenever the function
R
in Definition
2.6
is a covariance function as in (
10
), the rectangular increment
R_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}^{t_{j}^{\prime}t_{j+1}^{\prime}}
can also be written as in (
11
).

In the following definition, we consider each element
((s,t),(u,v))
in
\mathcal{S}_{2}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
as a rectangle and denote it by
[s,t]\times[u,v]
.

Definition 2.7
.

{def:2d-control}

A continuous function
\omega:\mathcal{S}_{2}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called a 2d control, if it is zero on degenerate rectangles, and super-additive in the sense that for all rectangles
A,B
and
C
contained in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
satisfying
A\cup B\subset C
and
A\cap B=\emptyset
,

\omega(A)+\omega(B)\leq\omega(C).

With these elementary notions at hand, we next introduce a hypothesis which allows the use of both rough paths techniques and tools from stochastic analysis for the underlying process
x
.

Hypothesis 2.8
.

{hyp:2d-var-R}

Let
x
be a
d
-dimensional continuous and centered Gaussian process with i.i.d. components, whose initial value is 0 and covariance
R
is given by (
10
). We assume that for some
\rho\in[1,2)
, the function
R
admits a finite 2d
\rho
-variation.

It is well known that for a continuous function
g:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
with finite
p
-variation, the function
[a,b]\mapsto\|g\|^{p}_{p-{\rm var};[a,b]}
is a control. However, for a continuous function
R:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with finite 2d
\rho
-variation, the function
[a,b]\times[c,d]\mapsto\|R\|^{\rho}_{\rho-{\rm var};[a,b]\times[c,d]}
may fail to be super-additive for
\rho>1
(see
[
9
, Theorem 1]
). To regain this property, here we introduce the so-called
controlled 2d
\rho
-variation
for
1\leq\rho<\infty
(this notion is also introduced in
[
9
]
).

Definition 2.9
.

{def:controlled-variation}

Let
\rho\in[1,\infty)
. For a continuous function
R:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, its controlled 2d
\rho
-variation is defined as

\vvvert R\vvvert_{\rho-{\rm var};[s,t]\times[u,v]}:=\sup_{\Pi\in\mathcal{P}([s,t]\times[u,v])}\left(\sum_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t^{\prime}_{j},t^{\prime}_{j+1}]\in\Pi}\left|R_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}^{t^{\prime}_{j}t^{\prime}_{j+1}}\right|^{\rho}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}},

where
R_{t_{i}t_{i+1}}^{t^{\prime}_{j}t^{\prime}_{j+1}}
is given in (
13
),
\Pi
is a partition of
[s,t]\times[u,v]
which is a finite set of essentially disjoint rectangles whose union is
[s,t]\times[u,v]
, and
\mathcal{P}([s,t]\times[u,v])
is the collection of all such partitions.

The norms
\|\cdot\|
and
\vvvert\cdot\vvvert
are comparable thanks to the following property borrowed from
[
9
, Theorem 1]
: for all
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
there exists a constant
C_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}
such that

(14)

C_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\vvvert f\vvvert_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\leq\|f\|_{\rho-{\rm var};[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}\leq\vvvert f\vvvert_{\rho-{\rm var};[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}.

Moreover, the function
[a,b]\times[c,d]\mapsto\vvvert f\vvvert^{\rho}_{\rho-{\rm var};[a,b]\times[c,d]}
is a 2d control (
[
9
, Theorem 1]
).

Remark 2.10
.

{remark:control-2d-variation}

Owing to (
14
), any continuous function
R:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
with finite
2d
\rho
-variation
also has a finite
controlled 2d
\rho^{\prime}
-variation
for all
\rho^{\prime}>\rho.
Furthermore, for all
((s,t),(u,v))\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
,

\|R\|^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}-{{\rm var}};[s,t]\times[u,v]}\leq\omega([s,t]\times[u,v]),

where
\omega
is the 2d control (as introduced in Definition
2.7
) given by

(15)

\omega([s,t]\times[u,v])=\vvvert R\vvvert^{\rho^{\prime}}_{{\rho^{\prime}}-{{\rm var}};[s,t]\times[u,v]}.

Remark 2.11
.

As an example, if the Gaussian process
x
is a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, the covariance
R
of
x
has finite 2d
\rho
-variation with
\rho=\frac{1}{2H}
and Hypothesis
2.8
is satisfied (see
[
9
, Example 1]
). If we choose
\rho^{\prime}>\rho=\frac{1}{2H}
, then the quantity
\|R\|^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}-{{\rm var}};[s,t]\times[u,v]}
is controlled by the 2d control
\vvvert R\vvvert^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}-{{\rm var}};[s,t]\times[u,v]}
. Note that
\vvvert R\vvvert_{\rho-{{\rm var}};[0,T]^{2}}=\infty
if we choose
\rho\leq\frac{1}{2H}
(as shown in
[
9
, Example 2]
).

In the sequel we will also request the function
t\mapsto R(t,t)
to be Hölder continuous. We now state an additional assumption which guarantees this Hölder continuity (see e.g
[
4
,
12
]
for a similar hypothesis).

Hypothesis 2.12
.

{hyp:var-R}

Let
\rho\in[1,2)
be given in Hypothesis
2.8
. We assume that there exists
C<\infty
such that for all
s,t\in[0,T]
the covariance function
R
satisfies

(16)

\|R(t,\cdot)-R(s,\cdot)\|^{\rho}_{\rho-{\rm{var;}}[0,T]}\leq C|t-s|.

Remark 2.13
.

{remark:holder-R}

A direct consequence of Hypothesis
2.12
is that
R_{t}:=R(t,t)
has finite
\rho
-variation, by
[
4
, Lemma 2.14]
. Moreover, recall that by Hypothesis
2.8
, we have
x_{0}=0
and hence
R(0,\cdot)=R(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
. This together with (
16
) implies that
R(t,\cdot)
and
R(\cdot,t)
have finite
\rho
-variation for each fixed
t\in[0,T].

Remark 2.14
.

{remark:holder-R’}

Given
\rho\in[1,2)
, clearly we have, for
0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq T
and
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
,

\|R\|_{\rho-\text{var;}[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}^{2}\leq\|R\|_{\rho-\text{var;}[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[0,T]}\|R\|_{\rho-\text{var;}[0,T]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}.

Furthermore, it is a direct consequence of (
16
) that for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,

\|R\|^{\rho}_{\rho-\text{var;}[s,t]\times[0,T]}\leq C(t-s).

Combining the two inequalities above, we have the following control on the 2d
\rho
-variation of
R
: for some positive constant
C
,

(17)

\|R\|_{\rho-\text{var;}[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}^{2\rho}\leq C(s_{2}-s_{1})(t_{2}-t_{1}).

Remark 2.15
.

{remark:holder-R’’}

Note that for
1\leq\gamma\leq\gamma^{\prime}<\infty,\|R\|_{\gamma^{\prime}-\text{var;}[s,t]\times[u,v]}\leq\|R\|_{\gamma-\text{var;}[s,t]\times[u,v]}
. Therefore, under Hypothesis
2.12
, inequality (
16
) and hence (
17
) hold with
\rho
replaced by
\rho^{\prime}\in(\rho,2)
and
C
depending on
(\rho,\rho^{\prime},T)
.

Remark 2.16
.

{remark2.10}

Clearly (
17
) yields the following relations on squares of the form
[s,t]^{2}
,

(18)

\|R\|_{\rho-\text{var;}[s,t]^{2}}^{\rho}\leq C(t-s).

We say that
R
has finite
Hölder-controlled 2d
\rho
-variation
if
R
satisfies both Hypothesis
2.8
and (
18
). An important consequence of
R
having finite Hölder controlled 2d
\rho
-variation is that
x
has
1/p
-Hölder continuous sample paths for every
p>2\rho
. It is also readily checked that, whenever
x
satisfies (
18
), we have

(19)

\mathbf{E}\left[\left({\bf x}_{st}^{{\bf 1};i}\right)^{2}\right]\leq c\,(t-s)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}.

Remark 2.17
.

Similarly to the argument in
[
3
, Remark 2.4]
, for any process
x
whose covariance function
R
admits a finite
\rho
-variation one can introduce a deterministic time-change
\tau:[0,T]\to[0,T]
such that
\tilde{X}=X\circ\tau
has finite Hölder-controlled 2d
\rho
-variation. That is the time changed process
\tilde{X}
satisfies Hypothesis
2.8
and equation (
18
).

The following result (stated e.g. in
[
11
, Theorem 15.33]
) relates the 2d
\rho
-variation of
R
with the pathwise assumptions allowing to apply the abstract rough paths theory.

Proposition 2.18
.

{prop:Gaussian-rough-path}

Let
x=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{d})
be a continuous centered Gaussian process with i.i.d. components and covariance function
R
defined by (
10
). If
R
satisfies Hypothesis
2.8
, then
x
also satisfies Hypothesis
2.5
provided
p>2\rho
.

Proposition
2.18
asserts that under Hypothesis
2.8
, the Gaussian process
x
is amenable to rough path analysis. In particular, a rough path integral with respect to
x
can be constructed. In this context, the natural class of integrand one might want to consider is the family of controlled processes. Its definition is recalled below.

Definition 2.19
.

{def:ctrld-process}

Consider a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path
x
with finite
p
-variation for some
p\geq 1
. We say that a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path
y
of finite
p
-variation is controlled by
x
, if there exist a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
-valued path
y^{x}
of finite
p
-variation and a
1
-increment process
r\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}{\rm-var}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as defined in Definition
2.3
, such that

(20)

\delta y_{st}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{s}^{x;ij}\,{\bf x}_{st}^{{\bf 1};j}+r_{st}^{i},~\text{ for }i=1,\dots,d.

We are now ready to state the basic integration result for controlled processes, which can be found e.g. in
[
8
,
11
,
13
]
.

Proposition 2.20
.

{prop:intg-ctrld-process}

Let
T>0
be fixed. Let
{\bf x}
be a geometric
p
-rough path lifted from a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path with finite
p
-variation for some
p\in[1,3),
and let
y
be a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path of finite
p
-variation that is controlled by
x
in the sense of Definition
2.19
. Then for
0\leq s<t\leq T
, one can define the integral
\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}
as the limit of the following Riemann sums,

(21)

\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}d{\bf x}_{r}=\lim_{|\pi_{n}|\to 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}y^{i}_{t_{k}}{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{1,i}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ij}\right),

where
\pi_{n}=[s=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=t]
is a partition of
[s,t]
and
|\pi_{n}|=\max\limits_{k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}|t_{k+1}-t_{k}|.
In (
21
), observe that we have also used the convention on inner products put forward in Section
1.3
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(T,p)
depending only on
(T,p)
such that for all
0\leq s<t\leq T
we have

\left|\int_{s}^{t}y_{r}d{\bf x}_{r}-y_{s}{\bf x}^{1}_{st}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{s}^{x;ij}{\bf x}_{st}^{2;ij}\right|\leq C\left(\|{\bf x}^{1}\|_{p-{\rm var}}\|r\|_{\frac{p}{2}-{\rm var}}+\|{\bf x}^{2}\|_{\frac{p}{2}-{\rm var}}\|y^{x}\|_{p-{\rm var}}\right)|t-s|^{3/p},

where we recall that
r
is the increment introduced in (
20
).

Recall that our main objective is to compute some Skorohod-Stratonovich corrections as in
[
4
]
. To this aim we will need a more detailed description of the increments of
y
than the ones given in (
20
). Namely we will assume that
y
is a second order controlled process as defined below (for the definition of controlled processes of general order, we refer to
[
8
, Definition 4.17]
or
[
3
, Definition 5.1]
).

Definition 2.21
.

{def:ctrld-process’}

Consider a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path
x
with finite
p
-variation for some
p\geq 1
. We say that a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued path
y
of finite
p
-variation is a second-order controlled process with respect to
x
, if there exist a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
-valued path
y^{x}
, a continuous
\mathbb{R}^{d^{3}}
-valued path
y^{xx}
, both of which are of finite
p
-variation, and
1
-increment processes
r\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\frac{p}{3}{\rm-var}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),r^{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\frac{p}{2}{\rm-var}}(\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})
as defined in Definition
2.3
, such that for
i=1,\dots,d
and
(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,T])
we have

(22)

\delta y_{st}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{s}^{x;ij}\,{\bf x}_{st}^{{\bf 1};j}+\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}y_{s}^{xx;ijk}\,{\bf x}_{st}^{{\bf 2};jk}+r_{st}^{i}.

In addition, the increment
y^{x}
in (
22
) is a controlled process of order 1, that is for
i,j=1,\dots,d
and
(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,T])
we have

(23)

\delta y_{st}^{x;ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}y_{s}^{xx;ijk}\,{\bf x}_{st}^{{\bf 1};k}+r_{st}^{x;ij}.

2.2.
Higher dimensional Young integrals

In this subsection, we gather some inequalities for Young integrals in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which will feature in our computations throughout the paper. We start by a relation for integrals in the plane borrowed form
[
10
,
23
]
.

Theorem 2.22
.

{2d-Young}

Let
f,R:[0,T]^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous functions with finite
p
-variation and finite
q
-variation respectively for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}>1
. Specifically recalling our Definition
2.6
, we assume
\|f\|_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}<\infty
and
\|R\|_{q-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}<\infty
. Moreover, assume that for all
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,T]
, both
f(s_{1},\cdot)
and
f(\cdot,s_{2})
have finite 1-dimensional
p
-variation as given in Definition
2.3
. Then the
2
d Young-Stieltjes integral of
f
with respect to
R
exists and the following Young’s inequality holds, for
[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]\subset[0,T]^{2}
,

(24)

\left|\int_{[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}f(s_{1},s_{2})dR(s_{1},s_{2})\right|\leq C_{p,q}\Bigg(|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2})|+\|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\\
+\|f(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2})\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]}+\|f\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\Bigg)\|R\|_{q-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\,.

We now state a lemma about integration in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
which will be invoked in order to analyze discretization properties for the Malliavin derivative of a controlled process
y
. Although its proof might be traced back to
[
23
]
, we include it here for the sake of clarity since Lemma

is tailored for our specific needs.

Lemma 2.23
.

{4d-Young}

Let
f,g,R
be continuous functions defined on
[0,T]^{2}
. Similarly to Theorem
2.22
, we assume that
f,g
have finite
p
-variation, as well as
f(s_{1},\cdot),f(\cdot,s_{3}),g(s_{2},\cdot)
and
g(\cdot,s_{4})
for fixed arbitrary
s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4}\in[0,T]
. We also suppose that
R
has finite
q
-variation on
[0,T]^{2}
, with
p,q
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}>1
. Then for
\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1},\ldots,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}\in[0,T]
such that
\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{j}<\bar{s}_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,4
, the following Young integral in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
is well defined:

I^{f,g,R}(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1},\ldots,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}):=\int_{[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}f(s_{1},s_{3})g(s_{2},s_{4})dR(s_{1},s_{2})\mathrm{d}R(s_{3},s_{4}).

Moreover,
I^{f,g,R}(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1},\ldots,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4})
can be bounded as

\displaystyle\left|I^{f,g,R}(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1},\ldots,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4})\right|\leq C_{p,q}~\|R\|_{q-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\|R\|_{q-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})|+\|f(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]}+\|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]}+\|f\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]}\Big)

(25)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(|g(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|+\|g(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}+\|g(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}+\|g\|_{p-{\rm var};[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}\Big)\,.

Proof.

We will divide this proof in several steps.

Step 1:
Decomposition of the integral. We can write

(26)

I^{f,g,R}(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1},\ldots,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4})=\int_{[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}F(s_{3},s_{4})\mathrm{d}R(s_{3},s_{4})

where the function
F
is defined on
[0,T]^{2}
by

(27)

F(s_{3},s_{4})=\int_{[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}f(s_{1},s_{3})g(s_{2},s_{4})\mathrm{d}R(s_{1},s_{2}),

and where we observe that the right-hand side of (
27
) is well defined thanks to Theorem
2.22
. Our strategy in order to estimate
I^{f,g,R}
will rely on some succesive applications of (
24
).
Specifically, with (
26
) in mind, relation (
24
) yields

(28)

\left|I^{f,g,R}(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4})\right|\leq C_{p,q}\Big(|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|+\|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}\\
+\|F(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]}+\|F\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}\Big)\|R\|_{q-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\bar{s}_{3}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}.

We will now estimate the terms in right-hand side of (
28
) separately.

Step 2:
Upper bound for
F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})
. Given
(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})\in[0,T]^{2}
and recalling the definition (

) of

, another application of (

) enables to write

|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|\leq C_{p,q}\Big(|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})g(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|+|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})|\|g(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\\
+|g(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|\|f(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]}+\|f(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]}\|g(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\Big)\|R\|_{q-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}

and we notice that the above expression can be simplified as

\displaystyle|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,q}\Big(|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})|+\|f(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]}\Big)

(29)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(|g(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})|+\|g(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\Big)\|R\|_{q-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}.

Step 3:
Upper bound for
\|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}
. Recall the Definition
2.3
of
p
-variation. We thus have

\|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}=\sup_{\pi}\left(\sum_{i}|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},v_{i+1})-F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},v_{i})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Plugging expression (

\|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}^{p}=\sup_{\pi}\sum_{i}\left|\int_{[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}f(s_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})\Big(g(s_{2},v_{i+1})-g(s_{2},v_{i})\Big)dR(s_{1},s_{2})\right|^{p}.

We now apply (
24
) again and we end up with

(30)

\|F(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3},\cdot)\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}\leq C_{p,q}\|R\|_{q-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}\sum_{k=1}^{4}V_{k},

where the terms

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle=|f(\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})|\sup_{\pi}\left(\sum_{i}\|g(\cdot,v_{i+1})-g(\cdot,v_{i})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

\displaystyle V_{4}

and

, by Definition

\displaystyle V_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\|f(\cdot,\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{3})\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{1},\bar{s}_{1}]}\|g\|_{p-{\rm var},[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{2},\bar{s}_{2}]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{s}_{4},\bar{s}_{4}]}\,.

Hence, plugging (

) and (

2.3.
The Hilbert space associated to
x

{sec:wiener-space-general}

Consider a continuous d-dimensional centered Gaussian process
x
on
[0,T]
with covariance function
R
given by (
10
). Every component of
x
(say
x^{1}
) is a 1-dimensional centered Gaussian process with covariance
R
. In this section we review some basic facts about the related Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of functions for which Wiener integrals with respect to
x
(see e.g.
[
20
]
) are well defined.

The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is the completion of the set of step functions

\mathcal{E}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bf 1}_{\left[0,t_{i}\right]}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}\text{, }t_{i}\in\left[0,T\right],i=1,\dots,n\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

with respect to the inner product

\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bf 1}_{[0,t_{i}]},\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}{\bf 1}_{[0,s_{j}]}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{j}R\left(t_{i},s_{j}\right).

Observe that this inner product can also be written as

(37)

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bf 1}_{[0,t_{i}]},\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}{\bf 1}_{[0,s_{j}]}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}{\bf 1}_{[0,t_{i}]}(t)\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}{\bf 1}_{[0,s_{j}]}(s)\right)~dR(t,s).

One can further relate
\mathcal{H}
to our driving process
x
in the following way:
let
\mathbf{H}
be the closure of the set

{\bf\mathsf{E}}=\left\{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x^{1}_{t_{i}}:a_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\text{ }t_{i}\in\left[0,T\right],i=1,\dots,n\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
. Then the linear map
x^{1}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathsf{E}
defined by
x^{1}({\bf 1}_{[0,t]})=x^{1}_{t}
extends to a linear isometry between
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathbf{H}
. Hence,
\mathbf{H}=\{x^{1}(h),h\in\mathcal{H}\}
and this family is known as the isonormal Gaussian process related to
x^{1}
(see
[
20
, Definition 1.1.1]
). Note that
x^{1}(h)
for
h\in\mathcal{H}
is called the Wiener integral of
h
with respect to
x^{1}
and is usually denoted by
\int_{0}^{T}h(s)dx^{1}_{s}
.

Remark 2.24
.

{representation H norm}

Recall that we have assumed
x_{0}=0
and thus
R(0,0)=0
. Thus relation (
37
) suggests

(38)

\displaystyle\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}h_{1}(s)h_{2}(t)dR(s,t)~~\text{ for }h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H},

whenever the 2D Young’s integral on the right-hand side is well-defined (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Proposition 4]
for details).

Remark 2.25
.

{rmk:H-on-subinterval}

Denoting by
\mathcal{E}([a,b])
the set of step functions in
\mathcal{E}
restricted on
[a,b]\subset[0,T]
, the closure
\mathcal{H}([a,b])
of
\mathcal{E}([a,b])
with respect to the inner product (
37
) then coincides with
\mathcal{H}
restricted on
[a,b]
, and for
f,g\in\mathcal{H}
,

(39)

\left\langle f\,{\bf 1}_{[a,b]},\,g\,{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\langle f,\,g\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}([a,b])}.

2.4.
Malliavin calculus for Gaussian processes

{sec-Mal}

In this subsection, we collect some basic concepts of Malliavin calculus, and we refer to
[
20
]
for more details.

Recall that
x_{t}
is a continuous centered
d
-dimensional Gaussian process with i.i.d. components, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbf{P})
. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that
\mathcal{F}
coincides with the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{x_{t};\,t\in[0,T]\}
. For the
d
-dimensional process
x
, we define an extension of the Wiener integral defined as follows: let
\varphi=(\varphi^{1},\ldots,\varphi^{d})
be an element of
\mathcal{H}^{d}
where we recall that
\mathcal{H}
has been introduced in Section
2.3
. Then we set

(40)

x(\varphi)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}x^{j}(\varphi^{j})\,,

where each term
x^{j}(\varphi^{j})
is a 1-d Wiener integral as in Section
2.3
.

A smooth functional of
x
is a random variable of the form
F=f(x(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,x(\varphi_{n}))
, where
n\geq 1
,
\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\}
is a family of elements of
\mathcal{H}^{d}
and each
x(\varphi_{i})
is understood as in (
40
). Moreover, we assume that the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth and its partial derivatives grow at most polynomially fast. Then, the Malliavin derivative
\mathbf{D}F
of
F
is the
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-valued random variable defined by

(41)

\mathbf{D}F=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}(x(\varphi_{1}),\dots,x(\varphi_{n}))\varphi_{k}.

One can show that
\mathbf{D}
is closable from
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
, and thus one may span the space of the smooth and cylindrical random variables under the norm

\|F\|_{1,2}=\left(\mathbb{E}[F^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{D}F\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

The resulting closure is called Sobolev space
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
.

Remark 2.26
.

{remark:DF}

As seen in (
41
), the Malliavin derivative
\mathbf{D}F
of a functional
F
is a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued process. The
i
-th coordinate of
\mathbf{D}F
corresponds to the Malliavin derivative of
F
with respect to the randomness in
x^{i}
only. It will be denoted by
\mathbf{D}^{i}F
in the sequel.

The divergence operator
\delta^{\diamond}
(also known as the Skorohod integral) is the adjoint operator of the Malliavin derivative operator
\mathbf{D}
defined by the duality relation

\mathbb{E}[F\delta^{\diamond}(u)]=\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathbf{D}F,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}],~~\text{ for all }F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}\text{ and }\text{ for all }u\in\text{Dom }\delta^{\diamond}.

Here
\text{Dom }\delta^{\diamond}
is the domain of the divergence operator
\delta^{\diamond}
, which is the space of
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variables
u\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{d})
such that
|\mathbb{E}[\langle\mathbf{D}F,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}]|\leq c_{F}\|F\|_{2}
with some constant
c_{F}
depending on
F
, for all
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
. In particular,
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathcal{H}^{d})\subset\text{Dom }\delta^{\diamond}.
Note that for
u\in\text{Dom }\delta^{\diamond}
, we have
\delta^{\diamond}(u)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{E}[\delta^{\diamond}(u)]=0
. By convention, we also take the following notation, for
u\in\text{Dom }\delta^{\diamond}
,

(42)

\int_{0}^{T}u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{t}:=\delta^{\diamond}(u).

For our main computations below we shall invoke the following relation taken from
[
22
]
: for any
G\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
0\leq a<b\leq T
we have

(43)

\delta^{\diamond}(G\,{\bf 1}_{[a,b]})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{a}^{b}G^{i}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{t}^{i}=\sum_{i}^{d}G^{i}\diamond\delta x_{ab}^{i},

where
\diamond
stands for the Wick product (see
[
15
]
for a brief account on Wick products). Moreover, according to
[
16
, Proposition 4.7]
, relation (
43
) can be simplified as

(44)

\delta^{\diamond}(G\,{\bf 1}_{[a,b]})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}G^{i}\,\delta x_{ab}^{i}-\langle\mathbf{D}^{i}G^{i},{\bf 1}_{[a,b]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

2.5.
Discrete rough paths techniques

In this subsection, we develop some inequalities about discrete sums in a rough paths context. This kind of sum will feature prominently in the analysis of our Skorohod-Stratonovich corrections.

We state a crucial lemma about convergence of discrete sums in the second chaos of

. It generalizes
[
17
, Lemma 3.4]
to a generic Gaussian process (as opposed to the fractional Brownian motion case handled in
[
17
]

Proposition 2.27
.

{prop:bound-F}

Let
x

\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Gaussian process satisfying Hypotheses
2.8
and
2.12
.
For

we consider the uniform partition on
[0,T]
, namely
t_{k}=\frac{k}{n}T
. We define a process

by
F_{0}^{ij}=0
, and for all
t>0

(45)

F_{t}^{ij}=\sum\limits_{t_{k}=0}^{t_{-}}\Big({\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ij}-\mathbf{E}[{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ij}]\Big)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{t_{k}=0}^{t_{-}}{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ij}~,&i\neq j,\\
\sum\limits_{t_{k}=0}^{t_{-}}\Big({\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ii}-\mathbf{E}[{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ii}]\Big)~,&i=j,\end{cases}

where we recall the notation
t_{-}
from Section
1.3
and where
{\bf x}^{2}
is introduced in Definition
2.4
. Then for all
q\geq 1
,
\rho^{\prime}\in(\rho,2)
,
(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket)
and

the following inequality holds true

(46)

\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\left|\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right|^{q}\right]\right)^{1/q}\leq C\frac{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{n^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}}\,,

where
C=C(q,\rho,T),\beta=\frac{1}{\rho}\in(1/2,1]
for

, and
C=C(q,\rho,\rho^{\prime},T),\beta=\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}\in(1/2,1)
for

.

Proof.

Due to the hyper-contractivity property of the second Wiener chaos, it suffices to show the case
q=2
. In addition, we assume (without loss of generality) that
s=t_{m_{1}}<t=t_{m_{2}}
for
0\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq n
.

Case 1:
i=j
.
In this case, due to the definition (
45
) of
F
and the geometric nature of
{\bf x}
assumed in Definition
2.4
, we have

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ii}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left(\sum_{k=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\left[({\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})^{2}-\mathbf{E}[({\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})^{2}]\right]\right)^{2},

and expanding the square on the right hand side above we get

(47)

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ii}\right)^{2}\right]=\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\left\{\mathbf{E}[({\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})^{2}({\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}})^{2}]-\mathbf{E}[({\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}})^{2}]\mathbf{E}[({\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}})^{2}]\right\}.

In order to evaluate the right-hand side of (
47
) we apply a particular case of Wick’s formula for centered Gaussian random variables
X
and

, which can be stated as:

\mathbf{E}[X^{2}Y^{2}]-\mathbf{E}[X^{2}]\,\mathbf{E}[Y^{2}]=2\left(\mathbf{E}[X\,Y]\right)^{2}.

Plugging this result into (
47
) and recalling the definition (
11
) of

we obtain

(48)

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ii}\right)^{2}\right]=2\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\left(\mathbf{E}\left[{\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}{\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}\right]\right)^{2}=2\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\left(R^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}\right)^{2}.

Therefore invoking elementary properties of
p
-variations we end up with

(49)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ii}\right)^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle 2\sup_{k,l}\left(R^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}\right)^{2-\rho}\|R\|^{\rho}_{\rho-\text{var};[s,t]^{2}}.

On the right-hand side of (

), notice that under Hypothesis
2.12
,
\|R\|^{\rho}_{\rho-\text{var};[s,t]^{2}}
can be upper bounded by

thanks to (

\displaystyle|R_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}|=\left|\mathbb{E}[{\bf x}^{1,i}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}{\bf x}^{1,i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}]\right|\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|{\bf x}^{1,i}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}\right|^{2}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\left|{\bf x}^{1,i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right|^{2}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{C_{T}}{n^{\frac{1}{\rho}}}.

Reporting this information into (

, it is seen that

(50)

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ii}\right)^{2}\right]\leq CT^{2\beta-1}\frac{(t-s)}{n^{2\beta-1}}.

This ends our proof for the case
i=j

Case 2:
i\neq j
.

According to our definition (
45

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{k=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{{\bf 2};ij}\right)^{2}\right]=\sum\limits_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\mathbf{E}\left[{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{{\bf 2};ij}{\bf x}_{t_{l}t_{l+1}}^{{\bf 2};ij}\right].

Therefore, invoking the proofs of

for the computation of

(51)

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right)^{2}\right]=\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}R_{t_{k}v_{1}}^{t_{l}v_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}R(v_{1},v_{2}).

(k,l)
and denote

2.8

\left|\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{l}}^{t_{l+1}}R_{t_{k}v_{1}}^{t_{l}v_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}R(v_{1},v_{2})\right|\leq C\|R\|^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]},

for some constant

depending on

only. Plugging this inequality into (

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right)^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle C(\rho^{\prime},T)\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\|R\|^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]}

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho^{\prime},T)\sup_{k,l}\|R\|^{2-\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]}\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\|R\|^{\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]}.

\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right)^{2}\right]\leq C(\rho^{\prime},T)\sup_{k,l}\|R\|^{2-\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]}\sum_{k,l=m_{1}}^{m_{2}-1}\omega([t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]),

Furthermore, the super-additivity of

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho^{\prime},T)\sup_{k,l}\|R\|^{2-\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]}~\omega([s,t]^{2})

(52)

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho,\rho^{\prime},T)\sup_{k,l}\|R\|^{2-\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\times[t_{l},t_{l+1}]}~(t-s),

where the last inequality is due to (
15
), (
14
), and (
17
). Finally, by Hypothesis

2.12
(and Remark
2.15
), we have

\|R\|^{2\rho^{\prime}}_{\rho^{\prime}\text{-var};[s,t]\times[u,v]}\leq C(\rho,\rho^{\prime},T)(t-s)(u-v),

and therefore setting
\beta=1/\rho^{\prime}
, inequality (
52
) becomes

(53)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\delta F_{st}^{ij}\right)^{2}\right]\leq

\displaystyle C(\rho,\rho^{\prime},T)\left(\frac{T}{n}\right)^{2\beta-1}(t-s).

With (
50
) and (
53
) in hand our claim (
46
) is now easily achieved, which concludes the proof.
∎

Note that (
46
) is still valid for both cases of
i=j
and
i\neq j
, if we choose
\beta=\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}
for any
\rho^{\prime}\in(\rho,2)
. We now give a weighted version of Proposition
2.27
, which plays an important role in our correction computations.

Proposition 2.28
.

{prop:weighted-sum}

Let
x
be a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued Gaussian process satisfying Hypotheses
2.8
and
2.12
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}\in(\rho,2)
be fixed. For
n\geq 1
we consider the uniform partition on
[0,T]
, namely
t_{k}=\frac{k}{n}T
, as well as the process
F
defined by (
45
).
Let now
f
be a controlled process in the
L^{q}(\Omega)
sense, namely such that there exists a process
g
fulfilling (in the matrix sense), for some
\gamma\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2\rho})
and for all
q\geq 1
,

(54)

\|f_{t}\|_{q}+\|g_{t}\|_{q}\leq C,\quad\|\delta f_{st}-g_{s}\,{\bf x}^{{\bf 1}}_{st}\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)^{2\gamma},\quad\|\delta g_{st}\|_{q}\leq C(t-s)^{\gamma}.

Then the following estimate holds true for
(s,t)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\llbracket 0,T\rrbracket)
:

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{t_{k}=s}^{t-}f_{t_{k}}\otimes\delta F_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\Big\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{n^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}},

where
C=C(q,\rho,\rho^{\prime},T)
and
\beta=\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}\in(1/2,1)
.

Proof.

This proposition was proved in
[
17
, Corollary 4.9]
when
x
is a fractional Brownian motion. Although we generalize this result to a wider class of Gaussian processes, our proof goes along the same lines. Therefore we shall omit the details for sake of conciseness.
∎

3.
Correction terms in the case
2\leq p<3

In this section we derive a correction formula for controlled processes which are also in the domain of the Skorohod integral. As mentioned in the introduction, we have restricted our analysis to the case
p<3
. Although we believe that our methodology could be extended to
p<4
, this generalisation would require a cumbersome study of third order integrals and related weighted sums.

Theorem 3.1
.

{thm}

Let
x
be a Gaussian rough path with covariance given by (
10
) satisfying Hypotheses
2.8
and
2.12
with
\rho\in[1,\frac{3}{2})
. This implies that
x
has finite
p
-variation for
p>2\rho
. We can assume
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\rho}>1
, noting that
\rho<\frac{3}{2}.

Let
y
be a second-order controlled process in the sense of Definition
2.21
, and we assume
\mathbb{E}[\|y\|^{2}_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}]<\infty
. In particular, the rough integral
\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}d{\bf x}_{r}
is defined as in Proposition
2.20
, resorting to the convention on inner products of Section
1.3
. We also assume that
y\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathcal{H}^{d})
, so that the Skorohod integral of
y
given in (
42
) is well defined. Furthermore, we suppose that
\mathbf{D}_{0}y
has finite
p
-variation with
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|^{2}_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}]<\infty
, and
\mathbf{D}y
has finite 2d
p
-variation with
\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}]<\infty.
Then for all
t\in[0,T]
we have almost surely

(55)

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}=\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{x;ii}\mathrm{d}R_{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,t])}\left(\mathbf{D}^{i}_{r_{1}}y^{i}_{r_{2}}-y_{r_{2}}^{x;ii}\right)\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}),

where we recall from Section
2.1
that
R_{r}:=R(r,r)
and where the Malliavin derivative
\mathbf{D}^{i}
is introduced in Remark
2.26
.

Proof.

Let
\pi=\pi_{n}
be the uniform partition of order
n
of
[0,t]
, whose generic element is still denoted by
t_{k}=\frac{k}{n}t.
A natural discretization of
y
along
\pi
is given by

(56)

y^{\pi}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1})}(r),\quad r\in[0,t].

Notice that we have assumed that
y\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathcal{H}^{d})
. Hence both divergence integrals
\delta^{\diamond}(y^{\pi})
and
\delta^{\diamond}(y)
, as given in (
42
), are well defined. Moreover, according to (
43
), we have

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{i}\diamond\mathbf{x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{{\bf 1};i},

and owing to (
44
) this can be recast as

(57)

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{\pi}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{i}\mathbf{x}^{1;i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}-\langle\mathbf{D}^{i}y_{t_{k}}^{i},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

In addition, we will prove in the forthcoming Lemma
3.2
that
\delta^{\diamond}(y^{\pi})
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
to
\delta^{\diamond}(y)
. Otherwise stated, for
t\in[0,T]
we have

(58)

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{\pi}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}.

Therefore combining (
57
) and (
58
), we get the following limit in
L^{2}(\Omega)
:

(59)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(y_{t_{k}}^{i}{\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}-\langle{\mathbf{D}}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

On the other hand, owing to the fact that
y
is a controlled process in the sense of Definition
2.19
, Proposition
2.20
asserts that
\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}
is defined as a rough paths integral and hence almost surely we have

(60)

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{i}{\bf x}^{{\bf 1};i}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}{\bf x}^{{\bf 2};ij}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right).

Gathering relations (
59
) and (
60
), we get the following expression for the Stratonovich-Skorohod correction term:

(61)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}-\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\,\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}{\bf x}^{2;ij}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}+\langle{\mathbf{D}}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right),

where the limit on the right-hand side above is understood in probability.
In (
61
), notice that the left-hand side is well defined thanks to the standing assumptions of our Theorem. Hence the right-hand side of (
61
) also makes sense, and we will now identify the limits therein.

In order to compute the limit for the terms
y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}{\bf x}^{2;ij}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}
in (
61
), observe that
y
is a second order controlled process according to Definition
2.21
. Hence
y^{x}
is a controlled process satisfying relation (
23
). Since we have assumed that Hypotheses
2.8
and
2.12
are fulfilled, Proposition
2.28
for the increment
F
can be applied with
f=y^{x}
. Recalling (see (
45
)) that

\delta F_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{ij}={\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ij}-\mathbb{E}[{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2;ij}],

we end up with the following relation, valid for
i,j=1,\dots,d
, where the limit has to be considered in the
L^{1}(\Omega)
sense:

(62)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}\Big({\bf x}^{2;ij}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}-\mathbf{E}\left[{\bf x}^{2;ij}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right]\Big)=0.

In particular, going back to (
61
), we get that for
i\neq j
we have

(63)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i\neq j}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ij}{\bf x}^{2;ij}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}=0.

Let us deal with the left-hand side of (
62
) when
i=j
. Specifically, we will express the limit of the sums
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\mathbf{E}\left[{\bf x}^{2;ii}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right]
as a Young integral. To this aim, notice that
2{\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{2,ii}=\left({\bf x}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}^{1,i}\right)^{2}
due to the geometric assumption in Definition
2.4
. Hence invoking the fact that
R_{t_{k}}=R(t_{k},t_{k})
we have

\displaystyle 2\mathbf{E}\left[{\bf x}^{2;ii}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[(x_{t_{k+1}}^{i}-x_{t_{k}}^{i})^{2}\right]=R_{t_{k+1}}-2R(t_{k+1},t_{k})+R_{t_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(R_{t_{k+1}}-R_{t_{k}}\right)-2\left(R(t_{k+1},t_{k})-R(t_{k},t_{k})\right).

Therefore for all
i=1,\dots,d
, we obtain a decomposition of the form

(64)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\mathbf{E}\left[{\bf x}^{2;ii}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}\right]=\frac{1}{2}I_{n}^{i}-J_{n}^{i}

where
I_{n}^{i},J_{n}^{i}
are respectively defined by

(65)

I_{n}^{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\delta R_{t_{k}t_{k+1}},\quad\text{and }\quad J_{n}^{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\big(R(t_{k+1},t_{k})-R(t_{k},t_{k})\big).

The limit of for the term
I^{i}_{n}
in (
64
) can be computed easily. Indeed, thanks to Remark
2.13
we know that
t\to R_{t}
has finite
\rho
-variation. Furthermore, since
y
is a second order controlled process, Definition
2.21
entails that
y^{x}
has finite
p
-variation. We have also mentioned in Theorem
3.1
that
p^{-1}+\rho^{-1}>1
. Hence classical Young integration arguments reveal that for
i=1,\dots,d
we have almost surely,

(66)

\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{n}^{i}=\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{x;ii}dR_{r}.

As far as the term
J_{n}^{i}
in (
65
) is concerned, let us recast this expression in terms of a 2-d Riemann sum. Namely we define another uniform partition
\{v_{l};0\leq l\leq n-1\}
of
[0,t]
, with
v_{l}=\frac{l}{n}t.
Then we start by writing

(67)

J_{n}^{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\big(R(t_{k+1},v_{k})-R(t_{k},v_{k})\big).

In addition, notice that thanks to Remark
2.13
we have
R(\cdot,0)=0
. Thus an immediate telescoping sum argument yields the following relation, valid for
k=0,\dots,n-1:

R(t_{k+1},v_{k})-R(t_{k},v_{k})=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}R_{v_{l}v_{l+1}}^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}.

Reporting this identity into (
67
), we get

(68)

J_{n}^{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}R_{v_{l}v_{l+1}}^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}=\sum_{0\leq l<k\leq n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}R_{v_{l}v_{l+1}}^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}.

This decomposition prompts us to define a degenerate function
f
in the plane as
f^{i}(u,v)=y_{u}^{x;ii}{\bf 1}_{[0<v<u<t]}
. With this notation in hand, relation (
68
) reads

J_{n}^{i}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{n-1}f^{i}(t_{k},v_{l})R_{v_{l}v_{l+1}}^{t_{k}t_{k+1}}.

In order to analyze the convergence of
J_{n}^{i}
, we now argue as follows: first
R
has a finite 2-dimensional
\rho
-variation. The function
f^{i}(u,v)=y_{u}^{x,ii}{\bf 1}_{[0<v<u<t]}
is also easily seen to have a finite 2-dimensional
p
-variation (owing to the fact that
y^{x,ii}
has finite
p
-variation), and recall that
p^{-1}+\rho^{-1}>1
. Hence standard convergence procedures for 2d-Young integrals show that almost surely

(69)

\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{n}^{i}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{i}(u,v)\,\mathrm{d}R(u,v)=\int_{\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,t])}y_{r_{2}}^{x;ii}\,\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

Summarizing our considerations for the case
i=j
, we gather (
66
) and (
69
) into the decomposition (
64
). We conclude that almost surely,

(70)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}y_{t_{k}}^{x;ii}\mathbb{E}[{\bf x}^{2;ii}_{t_{k}t_{k+1}}]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{x;ii}\mathrm{d}R_{r}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,t])}y_{r_{2}}^{x;ii}\,\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

We now go back to (
61
), and handle the terms
\langle\mathbf{D}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
therein. We write the inner product in
\mathcal{H}
in an explicit way thanks to (
38
), which yields

\langle\mathbf{D}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{D}_{r_{1}}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}}{\bf 1}_{[0,t_{k}]}(r_{1}){\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(r_{2})\,\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

We thus have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\langle\mathbf{D}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{D}_{r_{1}}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}}{\bf 1}_{[0,t_{k}]}(r_{1}){\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}(r_{2})\,\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

We now argue similarly to what we did for (
69
). Namely one of our standing assumptions is that
(r_{1},r_{2})\to\mathbf{D}^{i}_{r_{1}}y_{r_{2}}^{i}{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}(r_{1},r_{2})
has a finite 2-dimensional
p
-variation. Since
R
admits a finite
\rho
-variation and
p^{-1}+\rho^{-1}>1
, standard results concerning convergence of Riemann sums to Young integrals show that almost surely we have

(71)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\langle\mathbf{D}^{i}y^{i}_{t_{k}},{\bf 1}_{[t_{k},t_{k+1}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,t])}\mathbf{D}^{i}_{r_{1}}y^{i}_{r_{2}}\,\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}).

We can now conclude our proof easily. That is plugging (
62
), (
63
), (
70
) and (
71
) into (
61
), we end up with, almost surely,

\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}_{r}-\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}\mathrm{d}^{\diamond}x_{r}\\
=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}y_{r}^{x;ii}\mathrm{d}R_{r}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,t])}y_{r_{2}}^{x;ii}\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{2}([0,t])}\mathbf{D}_{r_{1}}^{i}y_{r_{2}}^{i}\mathrm{d}R(r_{1},r_{2}),

from which the claim (
55
)
is immediately deduced. This concludes the proof.
∎

We close this section by proving a technical result which has been used in order to derive relation (
58
).

Lemma 3.2
.

{lem:con-y}

Assume the same conditions as in Theorem
3.1
. Then
y^{\pi}
defined in (
56
) converges to
y
in
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\mathcal{H}^{d})
, i.e.
\lim\limits_{|\pi|\to 0}\mathbb{E}[\|y^{\pi}-y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}+\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}]=0
.

Proof.

According to (
38
), we have

\|y^{\pi}-y\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}^{2}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\int_{[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\langle y_{t_{i}}-y_{s},y_{t_{j}}-y_{t}\rangle~\mathrm{d}R(s,t),

where we recall that
\pi=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=t\}.
On each rectangle
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
we apply Theorem
2.22
to the function

f_{ij}(s,t)=\langle y_{s}-y_{t_{i}},y_{t}-y_{t_{j}}\rangle,

which is allowed since
f_{ij}
is easily seen to be a function in
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{p-\text{var}}
.

Recall that we have assumed
p^{-1}+\rho^{-1}>1
. Throughout the proof, we choose
p^{\prime}>p
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}>\rho^{\prime}>\rho
satisfying

(p^{\prime})^{-1}+(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}>1~\text{ and }~(p^{\prime})^{-1}+(\rho^{\prime\prime})^{-1}>1.

Since we also have
f_{ij}(t_{i},\cdot)=0
and
f_{ij}(\cdot,t_{j})=0
, we get

(72)

\displaystyle\|y^{\pi}-y\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\Big(\|y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\|y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\Big)\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}.

In order to bound the right-hand side of (
72
), we introduce a new function
\omega_{1}
, defined by

(73)

\omega_{1}([a,b]\times[c,d])=\|y\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[a,b]}\|y\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[c,d]}.

Then it is readily checked that
\omega_{1}
is also a 2d-control in the sense of Definition
2.7
. The following is easily deduced from (
72
):

\displaystyle\|y^{\pi}-y\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{i,j}\Big(\omega([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}

(74)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\Big(\omega_{1}([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\omega([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}},

where
\omega
is the control defined in (
15
). Now both
\omega_{1}
and
\omega
above are 2d-controls. Hence an easy extension of
[
11
, Exercise 1.9]
to a 2d setting shows that
\omega_{1}^{1/p^{\prime}}\omega^{1/{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}
is also a 2d-control. Hence one can resort to the super-additivity property of
\omega_{1}^{1/p^{\prime}}\omega^{1/{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}
in order to deduce the following from (
74
):

(75)

\|y^{\pi}-y\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}^{2}\leq C\sup_{i,j}\Big(\omega([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}\Big(\omega_{1}([0,t]^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Big(\omega([0,t]^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}.

We now turn to an upper bound on
\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}
. To this aim we first express this quantity using the norm in
(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}
induced by (
38
). This yields

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\int_{[0,t_{i+1}]\times[0,t_{j+1}]\times[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\langle\mathbf{D}_{u}y_{t_{i}}-\mathbf{D}_{u}y_{s},\mathbf{D}_{v}y_{t_{j}}-\mathbf{D}_{v}y_{t}\rangle~\mathrm{d}R(u,v)\mathrm{d}R(s,t).

We apply Lemma
2.23
to the right-hand side of (
76
) and get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}\leq C\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,t_{i+1}]\times[0,t_{j+1}]}\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y_{t_{i}}-\mathbf{D}_{0}y_{\cdot}\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}+\|\mathbf{D}_{\cdot}y_{t_{i}}-\mathbf{D}_{\cdot}y_{\cdot}\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,t_{i+1}]\times[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y_{t_{j}}-\mathbf{D}_{0}y_{\cdot}\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}+\|\mathbf{D}_{\cdot}y_{t_{j}}-\mathbf{D}_{\cdot}y_{\cdot}\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,t_{j+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\Big).

As a preliminary step, we also bound the variations on intervals of the form
[0,t_{j}]
by variations on
[0,T]
.
Thus one can bound
\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}
by

(77)

C\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\Big(\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}+\|\mathbf{D}y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\Big)\\
\times\Big(\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}+\|\mathbf{D}y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\Big).

We now wish to apply super-additivity properties of the
p
-variations, as we did for (
75
). However, note that the function
[a,b]\times[c,d]\mapsto\|\mathbf{D}y\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[a,b]\times[c,d]}
may fail to be super-additive (see
[
9
, Theorem 1]
). Hence we need to resort to the controlled 2d variation as introduced in Definition
2.9
. Specifically, it follows from (
77
) that
\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}
can be upper bounded by

(78)

C\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\Big(\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}+\vvvert\mathbf{D}y\vvvert_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[t_{i},t_{i+1}]}\Big)\\
\times\Big(\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}+\vvvert\mathbf{D}y\vvvert_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}\Big).

Notice that the right-hand side of (
78
) is finite, noting that
p<p^{\prime}
and owing to (
14
). Furthermore, noting that the function
[c,d]\mapsto\vvvert\mathbf{D}y\vvvert^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[c,d]}
is a control, we can define the following 2d controls (where we use
[
11
, Exercise 1.9]
again):

(79)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}([a,b]\times[c,d])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[a,b]}\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[c,d]}

(80)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}([a,b]\times[c,d])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vvvert\mathbf{D}y\vvvert^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[a,b]}\vvvert\mathbf{D}y\vvvert^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[c,d]}

(81)

\displaystyle\omega_{4}([a,b]\times[c,d])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[a,b]}\vvvert\mathbf{D}y\vvvert^{p^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]\times[c,d]}.

Now, similarly to (
75
), relation (
78
) entails

(82)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}\sup_{i,j}\Big(\omega([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}\Big(\omega([0,t]^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k=2}^{4}\Big(\omega_{k}([0,t]^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},

where
\omega
is the control given in (
15
). This is our desired bound for the difference
\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y
.

Let us summarize our considerations so far. Gathering inequalities (
75
) and (
82
), we have proved that

(83)

\displaystyle\|y^{\pi}-y\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}+\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H})^{\otimes 2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\|R\|_{\rho^{\prime}-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}\right)\Big(\omega([0,t]^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k=1}^{4}\Big(\omega_{k}([0,t]^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\sup_{i,j}\Big(\omega([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\Big)^{\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime\prime}}},

where the controls
\omega,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\omega_{4}
are respectively defined by (
15
), (
73
), (
79
), (
80
) and (
81
). We can now argue as follows: first,
according to (
17
), (
14
) and (
15
) we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{i,j}\omega([t_{i},t_{i+1}]\times[t_{j},t_{j+1}])=0.

Next we have assumed in Theorem
3.1
that

\mathbb{E}\left[\|y\|^{2}_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}+\|\mathbf{D}_{0}y\|^{2}_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]}+\|\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{p-{\rm var};[0,T]^{2}}\right]<\infty.

Therefore one can take expected valued in (

) in order to get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\|y^{\pi}-y\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d}}^{2}+\|\mathbf{D}y^{\pi}-\mathbf{D}y\|^{2}_{(\mathcal{H}^{d})^{\otimes 2}}\right]=0,

which is our claim. This concludes our proof.

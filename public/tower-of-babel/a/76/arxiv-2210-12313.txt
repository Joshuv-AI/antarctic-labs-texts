A criterion and a Cramér–Wold device for quasi-infinite divisibility for discrete multivariate probability laws
I. A. Alexeev, A. A. Khartov

Abstract
Multivariate discrete probability laws are considered. We show that such laws are quasi-infinitely divisible if and only if their characteristic functions are separated from zero. We generalize the existing results for the univariate discrete laws and for the multivariate laws on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . The Cramér–Wold devices for infinite and quasi-infinite divisibility were proved.



Keywords and phrases
: multivariate probability laws, characteristic functions, infinitely divisible laws, the Lévy representation, quasi-infinitely divisible laws, Cramér–Wold device.

1
Introduction.

Let
F
be a distribution function of a multivariate probability law on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mathbb{R}
is the real line,
d
is a positive integer. Recall that
F
and the corresponding law are called
infinitely divisible
if for every positive integer
n
there exists a distribution function
F_{n}
such that
F=F_{n}^{*n}
, where “
*
”  denotes the convolution, i.e.
F
is the
n
-fold convolution power of
F_{n}
. It is known that
F
is infinitely divisible if and only if its characteristic function

\displaystyle f(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle t,x\rangle}dF(x),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

admits the following
Lévy representation
(see
[
25
, Theorem 8.1]
)

\displaystyle f(t)=\exp\biggl\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle t,Qt\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigr(e^{i\langle t,x\rangle}-1-\tfrac{i\langle t,x\rangle}{1+\|x\|^{2}}\Bigr)\nu(dx)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1)

where
\langle\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the standard scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\|x\|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a fixed vector,
Q
is a symmetric nonnegative-definite
d\times d
matrix, and
\nu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies the following conditions

\displaystyle\nu\bigr(\{\bar{0}\}\bigr)=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min\bigr\{\|x\|^{2},1\bigr\}\nu(dx)<\infty.

Here and below, we denote by
\bar{0}
the zero vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The vector
(\gamma,Q,\nu)
is called a
characteristic triplet
and it is uniquely determined by
f
and hence by
F
.

In the recent paper by Berger, Kutlu, and Lindner
[
5
]
, the notion of quasi-infinitely divisible distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
was introduced. Following them, a distribution function
F
and the corresponding law are called
quasi-infinitely divisible
, if there exist infinitely divisible distribution functions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
such that
F_{1}=F*F_{2}
(in the papers
[
13
]
–
[
15
]
, such property is proposed to be called
rational infinite divisibility
). It was proved in
[
5
]
that
F
is quasi-infinitely divisible if and only if the representation (
1
) holds, where
\nu
is a a signed finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(-r,r)^{d}
for any
r>0
that satisfies
\nu\bigr(\{\bar{0}\}\bigr)=0
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min\bigr\{\|x\|^{2},1\bigr\}|\nu|(dx)<\infty,

where
|\nu|
denotes the total variation of the measure
\nu
(see
[
5
]
for more details). It is seen that quasi-infinitely divisible distributions are natural generalizations of infinitely divisible distributions.

The examples of univariate quasi-infinitely divisible laws can be found in the classical monographs
[
9
]
,
[
19
]
, and
[
20
]
. The first detailed analysis of these laws on
\mathbb{R}
was performed in
[
18
]
, and a lot of results for the univariate case are contained in the works
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
14
]
. The multivariate case was considered in the recent papers
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
23
]
. In these works the authors studied questions concerning supports, moments, continuity, and the weak convergence. The most complete results were obtained for probability laws on the set
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where
\mathbb{Z}
is the set of integers. In particular, the following important fact was stated in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1

Let
F
be the distribution function of the probability law on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
f
be its characteristic function. Then
F
is quasi-infinitely divisible if and only if
f(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In that case,
f
admits the following representation

\displaystyle f(t)=\exp\biggl\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{k}\bigl(e^{i\langle t,k\rangle}-1\bigr)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
, and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}|\lambda_{k}|<\infty
.

It is clear that (
2
) can be rewritten in the form (
1
). Using this theorem, the authors of
[
6
]
also proved the Cramér–Wold device for infinite divisibility of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-valued distributions. We formulate the corresponding result in a simplified form omitting equivalent propositions.

Theorem 2

Let
\xi
be a random vector on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with distribution function
F
. Then
F
is infinitely divisible if and only if for any
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
distribution functions
F_{c}
of random variables
\langle c,\xi\rangle
are infinitely divisible.

Recall that the classical Cramér–Wold device is a fact that a probability distribution of a
d
-dimensional random vector
\xi
is uniquely determined by distributions of all linear combinations of its components, i.e. by distributions of
\langle c,\xi\rangle
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
7
]
). Statements concerning some property for multivariate random vectors that can be expressed by corresponding statements for its linear combinations are also called as Cramér–Wold devices. So Theorem
2
is an interesting particular example of this. The Cramér–Wold device is well known for strict and symmetric stability (see
[
24
]
):
d
-dimensional random vector
\xi
is strictly (symmetric) stable if and only if random variable
\langle c,\xi\rangle
is strictly (symmetric) stable for any
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note that, however, the Cramér–Wold device for infinite divisibility does not hold in general. If a
d
-dimensional random vector
\xi
has infinitely divisible distribution, then the distribution of
\langle c,\xi\rangle
is infinitely divisible, too, for all
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but for
d\geqslant 2
there exist examples that the converse is not true (see
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
).

The purpose of this article is to generalize Theorems
1
and
2
to arbitrary multivariate discrete distribution functions.
More precisely, we obtain a criterion of quasi-infinitely divisibility with similar representation (
2
) and also we prove the Cramér–Wold devices for infinite and quasi-infinite divisibility for such laws. The corresponding results will be formulated in Section 2. The necessary tools, which are also of independent interest, will be formulated in Section 3. All of the mentioned results will be proved in Section 4.

2
Main results

Let us consider a multivariate discrete probability law with the following distribution function

\displaystyle F(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}:\\
x_{k}\in(-\infty,x]\end{subarray}}p_{x_{k}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3)

Here
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, are distinct numbers with probability weights
p_{x_{k}}\geqslant 0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
(the set of positive integers),
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}=1
. We denote by
(-\infty,x]
with
x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},\ldots,x^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the set
(-\infty,x^{(1)}]\times\ldots\times(-\infty,x^{(d)}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
f
be the characteristic function of
F
, i.e.

\displaystyle f(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle t,x\rangle}dF(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}p_{x_{k}}e^{i\langle t,x_{k}\rangle},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4)

We will formulate a criterion for the distribution function
F
to be quasi-infinitely divisible through condition for characteristic function
f
. For the sharp formulation of the result below we need to introduce the set of all finite
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-linear combinations of elements from a set
Y\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}
(
\mathbb{C}
is the set of complex numbers):

\displaystyle\langle Y\rangle=\Bigr\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}y^{(1)}_{k}\colon\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z},\,y_{k}\in Y\Bigr\}\times\ldots\times\Bigr\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}y_{k}^{(d)}\colon\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,z_{k}\in\mathbb{Z},\,y_{k}\in Y\Bigr\},

(5)

where
y_{k}=\bigr(y^{(1)}_{k},\ldots,y^{(d)}_{k}\bigr)
. So
\langle Y\rangle
is a module over the ring
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the generating set
Y
.
It is easily seen that
Y\subset\langle Y\rangle
,
\bar{0}\in\langle Y\rangle
. If a countable set
Y\neq\varnothing
, then
\langle Y\rangle
is an infinite countable set.

Theorem 3

Let
F
be a discrete distribution function of the form (
3
) with characteristic function
f
of the form (
4
). The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

F
is quasi-infinitely divisible;

(b)

\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t)|>0
.

If one of the conditions is satisfied, and hence all, then
f
admits the following representation

\displaystyle f(t)=\exp\biggr\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\bigr(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1\bigr)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(6)

where
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x_{k}\colon p_{x_{k}}>0,k\in\mathbb{N}\}\neq\varnothing
,
\gamma\in\langle X\rangle
,
\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
, and
\sum_{u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{0\}}|\lambda_{u}|<\infty
.

It is easily seen that (
6
) can be rewritten in the form (
1
). So if characteristic function of multivariate discrete probability law is represented by (
6
), then the corresponding distribution function
F
is quasi-infinitely divisible. Also observe that, by this theorem and on account of the conditions and uniqueness of the Lévy representation (
1
), the multivariate discrete distribution function
F
is infinitely divisible if and only if its characteristic function
f
admits representation (
6
) with the same
X
and
\gamma
, but with
\lambda_{u}\geqslant 0
for all
u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
, and
\sum_{u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_{u}<\infty
.

Note that Theorem
3
generalizes Theorem
1
. Indeed, for characteristic function
f
of probability law on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the condition that
f(t)\neq 0
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, is equivalent to the condition that
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|>0
. It follows due to the continuity and
2\pi
-periodicity of the function
|f(t)|
,
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, over each
t_{j}
. Theorem
3
also generalizes the corresponding results from
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
for the discrete distributions in the univariate case.

We now formulate the Cramér–Wold devices for the infinite and quasi-infinite divisibility of multivariate discrete distribution functions.

Theorem 4

Let
\xi
be a discrete random vector with distribution function
F
of the form (
3
). Then
F
is
(
quasi-
)
infinitely divisible if and only if for any
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the distribution functions
F_{c}
of random variables
\langle c,\xi\rangle
are
(
quasi-
)
infinitely divisible.

It is seen that Theorem
2
directly follows from the Theorem
4
. It should be noted that Theorem
4
does not contradict with the results from the
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
, because the distributions from the counterexamples contained the absolutely continuous part.

3
Tools

We will get the main result from more general positions. Namely, we will consequently study admission of the Lévy type representations for a general almost periodic function
h
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is very similar to
f
.

Theorem 5

Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be a function of the following form:

\displaystyle h(t)=\sum_{y\in Y}q_{y}e^{i\langle t,y\rangle},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a nonempty at most countable set,
q_{y}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
y\in Y
, and
0<\sum_{y\in Y}|q_{y}|<\infty
. Assume that
h(\bar{0})=\sum_{y\in Y}q_{y}=1
. If
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|=\mu>0
, then
h
admits the following representation

\displaystyle h(t)=\exp\biggr\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\bigr(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1\bigr)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(7)

where
\gamma\in\langle Y\rangle
,
\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
, and
\sum_{u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}|\lambda_{u}|<\infty
.

It should be noted that the function
h
in Theorem
5
is an
almost periodic function
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with absolutely convergence Fourier series. Recal that (see
[
16
, p. 255]
or
[
21
, Definition 1]
) a function
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is called almost periodic if for any sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists a subsequence
(t_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a continuous function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t+t_{n_{k}})-\varphi(t)\bigr|\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Detailed information about almost periodic functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be found in
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
, and
[
22
]
. Note that in the above literature, the results are formulated in a greater generality, that is, for local compact Abelian (LCA) groups.

We now turn to the following general version of the Theorem
3
.

Theorem 6

Let
h\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be a function of the following form

\displaystyle h(t)=\sum_{y\in Y}q_{y}e^{i\langle t,y\rangle},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a nonempty at most countable set,
q_{y}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
y\in Y
, and
0<\sum_{y\in Y}|q_{y}|<\infty
. Suppose that
h(\bar{0})=\sum_{y\in Y}q_{y}=1
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|>0;

(ii)

There exist a countable set
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and coefficients
r_{z}\in\mathbb{C}
,
z\in Z
,
\sum_{z\in Z}|r_{z}|<\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h(t)}=\sum_{z\in Z}r_{z}e^{i\langle t,z\rangle},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d};

(iii)

h
admits the representation

\displaystyle h(t)=\exp\biggl\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\bigr(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1\bigr)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\gamma\in\langle Y\rangle
,
\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
, and
\sum_{u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{0\}}|\lambda_{u}|<\infty;

(iv)

h
admits the representation

\displaystyle h(t)=\exp\biggr\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle t,Qt\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigr(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1-\tfrac{i\langle t,u\rangle}{1+\|u\|^{2}}\Bigr)\nu(du)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(8)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
Q\in\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
is a matrix,
\nu
is a complex measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\nu(\{\bar{0}\})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min\bigl\{\|x\|^{2},1\bigr\}|\nu|(dx)<\infty.

4
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
5
.
We will sequentially consider the following cases: 1)
Y=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, 2)
Y
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, 3)
Y
is at most countable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(the general case). We always assume that
Y\neq\varnothing
. Each subsequent case will be based on the previous one.

1)
Suppose that
Y=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. It is easy to see that the function
h
is
2\pi
-periodic in all coordinates, i.e. for any
k=1,\ldots,d
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
h(t+2\pi e_{k})=h(t)
, where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d}\}
denotes the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let us consider the distinguished logarithm
t\mapsto\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which satisfies
\exp\{\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)\}=h(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and it is uniquely defined by continuity with the condition
\mathrm{Ln}\,h(\bar{0})=0
(see
[
25
, Lemma 7.6]
). For any
k=1,\ldots,d
we have

\displaystyle\exp\{\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t+2\pi e_{k})\}=h(t+2\pi e_{k})=h(t)=\exp\{\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

So
\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t+2\pi e_{k})-\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)\in 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}
for any
k=1,\ldots,d
and
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
t\mapsto\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t+2\pi e_{k})-\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exist constants
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\frac{\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t+2\pi e_{k})-\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)}{2\pi i},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad k=1,\ldots,d.

Let us define the vector
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d})^{T}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. So the function
t\mapsto\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)-i\langle t,\gamma\rangle
is
2\pi
-periodic in all coordinates. By
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
, one can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)=i\langle t,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\bigr(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1\bigr),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\lambda_{u}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
, and
\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}|\lambda_{u}|<\infty
. Note that in this case
\langle Y\rangle=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

2)
Assume that
Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\}
, where
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
are distinct elements from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. So we have
h(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{y_{k}}e^{i\langle t,y_{k}\rangle}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
n=1
then
Y=\{y_{1}\}
and
q_{y_{1}}=1
. For this case representation (
7
) holds with
\gamma=y_{1}
and
\lambda_{u}=0
for all
u\in\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}
. We next suppose that
n\geqslant 2
. We set
y_{k}=(y_{k}^{(1)},\ldots,y_{k}^{(d)})
,
k=1,\ldots,n
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that for every
j=1,\ldots,d
there exist
k=1,\ldots,n
such that
y^{(j)}_{k}\neq 0
, since otherwise we can turn to the space
\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
with some
d^{\prime}<d
. Next, for every
j=1,\ldots,d
we can choose non-zero
\beta^{(j)}_{1},\ldots,\beta^{(j)}_{m_{j}}\in Y^{(j)}=\{y_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,y_{n}^{(j)}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
that consist a basis in
Y^{(j)}
over
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e. for every
j=1,\ldots,d
and for every

there exist unique values
c^{(j)}_{k,1},\ldots,c^{(j)}_{k,{m_{j}}}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

. So it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\langle Y\rangle=\Bigr\{\sum_{l=1}^{m_{1}}z_{l}^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}\colon\,z_{l}^{(1)}\in\mathbb{Z}\Bigr\}\times\ldots\times\Bigr\{\sum_{l=1}^{m_{d}}z_{l}^{(d)}\beta^{(d)}_{l}\colon\,z_{l}^{(d)}\in\mathbb{Z}\Bigr\}.

Note that for every
j=1,\ldots,d
the values
\beta^{(j)}_{1},\ldots,\beta^{(j)}_{m_{j}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e. the equation

.

We now consider the function

\displaystyle\varphi\bigr(t^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,t^{(1)}_{m_{1}},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{1},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{m_{d}}\bigr)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{y_{k}}\exp\biggr\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}}c^{(j)}_{k,l}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{l}\biggr\},

(9)

where
t^{(j)}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
,

, and

. If for any such

and

we set
t^{(j)}_{l}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}t^{(j)}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\varphi\bigr(t^{(1)}_{1},\ldots,t^{(1)}_{m_{1}},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{1},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{m_{d}}\bigr)=h(t),

(10)

where
t=(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(d)})
. We set
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}m_{1}+\ldots+m_{d}
. Let us fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Since the function
\varphi

\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for any
\tilde{t}_{1}
and
\tilde{t}_{2}
from
\mathbb{R}^{M}
satisfying
\|\tilde{t}_{1}-\tilde{t}_{2}\|<\delta_{e}
we have
\bigr|\varphi(\tilde{t}_{1})-\varphi(\tilde{t}_{2})\bigr|<\varepsilon
. Let us arbitrarily fix the vector

. We set

\displaystyle i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l=1}^{m_{j}}\gamma_{l}^{(j)}\beta_{l}^{(j)}t_{l}^{(j)}

3)
We now turn to the general case:
Y

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Without loss of generality we can set
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\}

. So
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|q_{y_{k}}|<\infty
and
h(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{y_{k}}e^{i\langle t,y_{k}\rangle}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We will approximate
h
by the following functions:

\displaystyle h_{n}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}q_{n,y_{k}}e^{i\langle t,y_{k}\rangle},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where

\displaystyle q_{n,y_{k}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dfrac{q_{y_{k}}}{\sum_{m=1}^{n}q_{y_{m}}},\quad k=1,\ldots,n,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q_{y_{k}}=1
, here
\bigl|\sum_{m=1}^{n}q_{y_{m}}\bigr|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}
for all
n\geqslant n_{0}
with a positive integer
n_{0}
. Let us estimate the approximation error for every

:

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)-h_{n}(t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{n}(q_{y_{k}}-q_{n,y_{k}})e^{i\langle t,y_{k}\rangle}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}q_{y_{k}}e^{i\langle t,y_{k}\rangle}\biggr|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|q_{y_{k}}-q_{n,y_{k}}|+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}|q_{y_{k}}|.

Due to
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}q_{y_{m}}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|q_{y_{k}}-q_{n,y_{k}}|=\biggl|1-\dfrac{1}{\sum_{m=1}^{n}q_{y_{m}}}\biggr|\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}|q_{y_{k}}|=\biggl|\dfrac{\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}q_{y_{m}}}{\sum_{m=1}^{n}q_{y_{m}}}\biggr|\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}|q_{y_{k}}|\leqslant 2A\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}|q_{y_{m}}|.

We used
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|q_{y_{k}}|=A
and
\bigl|\sum_{m=1}^{n}q_{y_{m}}\bigr|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}
in the last inequality. Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)-h_{n}(t)|\leqslant(2A+1)\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}|q_{y_{m}}|,\quad n\geqslant n_{0}.

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{n}|q_{y_{k}}|<\infty
, we have that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)-h_{n}(t)|\to 0
,

. Hence for any fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,\tfrac{1}{4})
there exists a positive integer

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t)-h_{n}(t)\bigr|\leqslant\varepsilon\mu,

(12)

where we set

. So for every

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h_{n}(t)\bigr|\geqslant\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t)\bigr|-\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t)-h_{n}(t)\bigr|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)\mu.

(13)

We now fix

and we represent

,

. Since

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ln}\,h(t)=\mathrm{Ln}\,h_{n}(t)+\mathrm{Ln}\,R_{n}(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let us consider the function

. By the result of part

, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ln}\,h_{n}(t)=i\langle t,\gamma_{n}\rangle+\sum_{u\in\langle Y_{n}\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{n,u}\bigl(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1\bigr),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

with a set

, and numbers
\gamma_{n}\in\langle Y_{n}\rangle
,
\lambda_{n,u}\in\mathbb{C}
for all

,

Proof of Theorem
6
.
The proof will be carried out in the following sequence:
(ii)\overset{\textbf{I}}{\longrightarrow}(i)\overset{\textbf{II}}{\longrightarrow}(iii)\overset{\textbf{III}}{\longrightarrow}(iv)\overset{\textbf{IV}}{\longrightarrow}(i)\overset{\textbf{V}}{\longrightarrow}(ii)
.

\textbf{I}.
Due to
(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\dfrac{1}{h(t)}\right|\leqslant\sum_{z\in Z}|r_{z}|=\frac{1}{\mu}<\infty.

It follows that

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|=\frac{1}{\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|1/h(t)\right|}=\mu>0.

\textbf{II}.
This implication directly follows from Theorem
5
.

\textbf{III}.
It is clear that
(iii)
yields
(iv)
with zero matrix
Q
and the signed measure

\displaystyle\nu(B)=\sum_{u\in B\cap\langle Y\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\quad\text{for every Borel set}\quad B.

\textbf{IV}.
Let us assume the contrary, i.e.
h
has the representation (
8
), however
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|=0
. Since
e^{z}\neq 0
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
, then
h(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence it is sufficient to focus on the case when
h
has the representation (
8
),
h(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|=0
.

Due to (
8
), for every fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have the following representation

\displaystyle\frac{h(t+\tau)h(t-\tau)}{h^{2}(t)}=\exp\biggl\{-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\tau,Q\tau\rangle+2\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}\bigr(\cos\bigr(\langle\tau,u\rangle\bigr)-1\bigr)\nu(du)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{h(t+\tau)h(t-\tau)}{h^{2}(t)}\right|\leqslant\exp\Biggr\{\biggl(\tfrac{1}{2}\|Q\|+\int\limits_{0<\|u\|<1}\|u\|^{2}\bigr|\nu\bigr|(du)\biggr)\|\tau\|^{2}+4\int\limits_{\|u\|>1}\bigr|\nu\bigr|(du)\Biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Hence, for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists
C_{\tau}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{h(t+\tau)h(t-\tau)}{h^{2}(t)}\right|\leqslant C_{\tau}.

Let
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, be a sequence such that
h(t_{n})
tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
. If there exists
R>0
such that
\|t_{n}\|<R
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exists subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
t_{n_{k}}\to t_{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
k\to\infty
. Since
h
is a continuous function,
h(t_{*})=0
that contradicts with the
(iv)
. It follows that
\|t_{n}\|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Since
h
is an almost periodic function, the sequence
(h(\cdot+t_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in the set of continuous functions, i.e. there exists a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and a continuous function
\varphi
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}+\tau)-\varphi(\tau)\bigr|\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

It is obvious that
|\varphi(\tau)|\leqslant C\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|<\infty
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}+\tau)h(t_{n_{k}}-\tau)-\varphi(\tau)\varphi(-\tau)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}-\tau)\bigr|\cdot\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}+\tau)-\varphi(\tau)\bigr|+\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}-\tau)-\varphi(-\tau)\bigr|\cdot|\varphi(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2C\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}+\tau)-\varphi(\tau)\bigr|\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Let us assume that
\varphi(\tau)\varphi(-\tau)=0
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(t_{n_{k}}+\tau)h(t_{n_{k}}-\tau)\bigr|\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

So for any fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle h(t_{n_{k}}+\tau)h(t_{n_{k}}-\tau)\Bigr|_{\tau=-t_{n_{k}}-s}=h(-s)h(2t_{n_{k}}+s)\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Since
h(s)\neq 0
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle h(2t_{n_{k}}+s)\underset{k\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

(20)

Next, it is easy to see that the function
h(2t_{n_{k}}+\cdot\,)
is an almost periodic one. It means that there exists a subsequence
(n_{k_{m}})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that a sequence
\bigl(h(2t_{n_{k_{m}}}+\cdot\,)\bigr)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
has a uniform limit. From (
20
) one can conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr|h(2t_{n_{k_{m}}}+s)\bigr|\underset{m\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Applying this with
s=-2t_{n_{k_{m}}}
, we come to a contradiction with
h(0)=1
. Therefore the assumption
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(t)|=0
is false, i.e.
(i)
follows from
(iv)
.

If
(i)
holds, then
(ii)
follows directly from
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Necessity
. Due to Theorem
3
and comments below, it is easily seen using formula (
6
) that if the distribution function
F
of a discrete random vector
\xi
is (quasi-)infinitely divisible, then for any
c\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
distribution functions
F_{c}
of the random variables
\langle c,\xi\rangle
are (quasi-)infinitely divisible, respectively (there is the case
d=1
for
F_{c}
).

Sufficiency
. Let us consider a discrete random vector
\xi
with distribution function (
3
) and characteristic function (
4
). We write the latter in the expanded form:

\displaystyle f(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(d)})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}t^{(j)}x^{(j)}_{k}\biggr\},

where
x_{k}=(x_{k}^{(1)},\ldots,x_{k}^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(d)}\in\mathbb{R}
.

We now assume that the distribution functions
F_{c}
of
\langle c,\xi\rangle
are quasi-infinitely divisible for any
c=(c^{(1)},\ldots,c^{(d)})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
f_{c}
denote the corresponding characteristic functions. It is easily seen that

\displaystyle f_{c}(t)=f(c^{(1)}t,\ldots,c^{(d)}t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Applying Theorem
3
to
F_{c}
(here the case
d=1
), we conclude that there exists a constant
\mu_{c}>0
such that

\displaystyle\bigl|f(c^{(1)}t,\ldots,c^{(d)}t)\bigr|\geqslant\mu_{c}\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(21)

In order to prove the quasi-infinite divisibility of
F
, according to Theorem
3
, it is sufficient to show that for some
\mu>0

\displaystyle\bigl|f(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(d)})\bigr|\geqslant\mu\quad\text{for all}\quad t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(d)}\in\mathbb{R}.

(22)

We set
X^{(j)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x_{k}^{(j)}:p_{x_{k}}>0,k\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
,
j=1,\ldots,d
. Let us suppose that
X^{(j)}\neq\{0\}
for every
j=1,\ldots,d
, i.e. for every
j
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x_{k}^{(j)}\neq 0
. Therefore for every
j=1,\ldots,d
one can choose non-zero
\beta^{(j)}_{l}\in X^{(j)}
,
l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}
(here
\mathcal{I}^{(j)}
is at most countable index set) such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for some numbers
z^{(j)}_{k,l}\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle x^{(j)}_{k}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\beta^{(j)}_{l},

(23)

where only finite number of
z^{(j)}_{k,l}
are non-zero. Note that the numbers
\beta^{(j)}_{l}
can be chosen as linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
, that is the equation
z_{1}\beta^{(j)}_{l_{1}}+\ldots+z_{n}\beta^{(j)}_{l_{n}}=0
holds with
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and distinct
l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if and only if
z_{1}=\ldots=z_{n}=0
. It follows that the numbers
z^{(j)}_{k,l}
are uniquely determined for
x_{k}^{(j)}
in (
23
). We observe that

\displaystyle\langle X^{(j)}\rangle=\biggr\{z_{1}\beta^{(j)}_{l_{1}}+\ldots+z_{n}\beta^{(j)}_{l_{n}}\colon\,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{Z},\,l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\biggr\},\quad j=1,\ldots,d,

(24)

where
\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle
is determined by (
5
) (the one-dimesional case).

We now propose the procedure of choosing of the numbers
c^{(1)},\ldots,c^{(d)}\in\mathbb{R}
such that the elements of the union system
\bigl\{c^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigr\}\cup\ldots\cup\bigl\{c^{(d)}\beta^{(d)}_{l}
:
l\in\mathcal{I}^{(d)}\bigr\}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
. We first fix any
c^{(1)}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. For every
v\in\langle X^{(2)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}
we define

\displaystyle D^{(2)}_{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigl\{c\in\mathbb{R}:cv\in\langle c^{(1)}X^{(1)}\rangle\bigr\}.

Here and below, for any set
X\subset\mathbb{R}
we denote by
cX
, where
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
\{cx:x\in X\}
.
Observe that every set
D^{(2)}_{v}
is countable. Then the set

\displaystyle D^{(2)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{v\in\langle X^{(2)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}}D^{(2)}_{v}

is countable too. Hence the set
C^{(2)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{R}\setminus D^{(2)}
is not empty. We choose any
c^{(2)}\in C^{(2)}
. Observe that

\displaystyle C^{(2)}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\bigcup_{v\in\langle X^{(2)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}}D^{(2)}_{v}=\bigcap_{v\in\langle X^{(2)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}}\mathbb{R}\setminus D^{(2)}_{v}.

This means that for any
v\in\langle X^{(2)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}
the quantity
c^{(2)}v
can not be a finite linear combination of elements
c^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}
,
l\in\mathcal{I}^{(1)}
, with integer coefficients. Let
v=z_{1}\beta^{(2)}_{l_{1}}+\ldots+z_{n}\beta^{(2)}_{l_{n}}
with some
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\in\mathcal{I}^{(2)}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
c^{(2)}v=z_{1}(c^{(2)}\beta^{(2)}_{l_{1}})+\ldots+z_{n}(c^{(2)}\beta^{(2)}_{l_{n}})
, by the above argument, the elements in the union system
\bigl\{c^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{c^{(2)}\beta^{(2)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigr\}
are linear independent over
\mathbb{Z}
. We next consider the set of all finite linear combitations of
\bigl\{c^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{c^{(2)}\beta^{(2)}_{l}
:
l\in\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigr\}
with integer coefficients. It is the set
\langle c^{(1)}X^{(1)}\cup c^{(2)}X^{(2)}\rangle
. For every
v\in\langle X^{(3)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}
we define

\displaystyle D^{(3)}_{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigl\{c\in\mathbb{R}:cv\in\langle c^{(1)}X^{(1)}\cup c^{(2)}X^{(2)}\rangle\bigr\}.

Every
D^{(3)}_{v}
is countable. Hence the set

\displaystyle D^{(3)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{v\in\langle X^{(3)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}}D^{(3)}_{v}

is countable too. Since the set
C^{(3)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{R}\setminus D^{(3)}
is not empty, we choose any
c^{(3)}\in C^{(3)}
. Observe that

\displaystyle C^{(3)}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\bigcup_{v\in\langle X^{(3)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}}D^{(3)}_{v}=\bigcap_{v\in\langle X^{(3)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}}\mathbb{R}\setminus D^{(3)}_{v}.

Hence for any
v\in\langle X^{(3)}\rangle\setminus\{0\}
the quantity
c^{(3)}v
can not be a finite linear combination of elements of

with integer coefficients. This implies that the elements in the union system
\bigl\{c^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{c^{(2)}\beta^{(2)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{c^{(3)}\beta^{(3)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(3)}\bigr\}

. We next proceed analogously and thus we obtain that the elements of the union system

:

are linearly independent over

as required.

We now prove (
22
). Suppose, contrary to our claim, that (
22
) is false, i.e. for any
\varepsilon>0
there exist
t^{(1)}_{\varepsilon},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|f(t^{(1)}_{\varepsilon},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{\varepsilon})|\leqslant\varepsilon
. So we fix

and such

,

. We first find

such that
\sum_{k=N_{\varepsilon}+1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}\leqslant\varepsilon
(see (
3
) and (
4
),
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}=1
,
p_{x_{k}}\geqslant 0
). Then

\displaystyle\sup_{t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(d)}\in\mathbb{R}}\biggl|\sum_{k=N_{\varepsilon}+1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{k}^{(j)}t^{(j)}\biggr\}\biggr|\leqslant\sum_{k=N_{\varepsilon}+1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}\leqslant\varepsilon.

(25)

Hence we get

\displaystyle\bigl|f(t^{(1)}_{\varepsilon},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{\varepsilon})\bigr|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{k}^{(j)}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\biggr|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{k}^{(j)}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\biggr|-\biggl|\sum_{k=N_{\varepsilon}+1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{k}^{(j)}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\biggr|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{k}^{(j)}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\biggr|-\varepsilon.

Due to representations (
23
), we write:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{k}^{(j)}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\biggl(\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\beta^{(j)}_{l}\biggr)t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\bigl(\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\biggr\}.

Let us fix
c^{(1)},\ldots,c^{(d)}\in\mathbb{R}
such that the elements of the union system
\bigl\{c^{(1)}\beta^{(1)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(1)}\bigr\}\cup\ldots\cup\bigl\{c^{(d)}\beta^{(d)}_{l}:l\in\mathcal{I}^{(d)}\bigr\}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
. By the Kronecker theorem (see
[
17
, p.37]
), for any
\delta>0
we can find
t^{\prime}_{\delta}
such that all following inequalities hold with some integers
n_{l}^{(j)}
:

\displaystyle\bigl|c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta}-\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}-2\pi n_{l}^{(j)}\bigr|<\delta,\quad l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon},\quad j=1,\ldots,d,

(27)

where
\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}
is the set of all
l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}
such that
z^{(j)}_{k,l}\neq 0
for some
k=1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}
. Since only finite number of
z^{(j)}_{k,l}
are non-zero in (
23
), the set
\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}
is finite and the system (
27
) has only finite number of inequalities.
Let us choose
\delta=\delta_{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\max_{k=1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}}\biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}|z^{(j)}_{k,l}|\biggr\}\leqslant\varepsilon.

(28)

Observe that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\biggr|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\biggl|\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\bigl(c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}-\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\bigr)\biggr\}-1\biggr|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\biggl|\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\bigl(c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}-\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}-2\pi n_{l}^{(j)}\bigr)\biggr\}-1\biggr|.

The last equality holds because all
z^{(j)}_{k,l}
and
n_{l}^{(j)}
are integer. Next, using well known inequality
|e^{iy}-1|\leqslant|y|
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and applying (
27
) and (
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\biggl(p_{x_{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}\Bigl(\bigl|z^{(j)}_{k,l}\bigr|\cdot\bigl|c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}-\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}-2\pi n_{l}^{(j)}\bigr|\Bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\max_{k=1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}}\biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}|z^{(j)}_{k,l}|\cdot\delta_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\delta_{\varepsilon}\cdot\max_{k=1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}}\biggl\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}}|z^{(j)}_{k,l}|\biggr\}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Returning to (
26
), we have

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}\biggr\}\biggr|\geqslant\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}\biggr|-\varepsilon.

Note that here we write
\mathcal{I}^{(j)}
instead of
\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}
that is obviously possible by the definition of
\mathcal{I}^{(j)}_{\varepsilon}
.
Thus we get

\displaystyle\bigl|f(t^{(1)}_{\varepsilon},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{\varepsilon})\bigr|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}\biggr|-2\varepsilon.

According to (
23
), we next write

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{I}^{(j)}}z^{(j)}_{k,l}c^{(j)}\beta^{(j)}_{l}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}c^{(j)}x^{(j)}_{k}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}.

Due to (
25
), we get

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}c^{(j)}x^{(j)}_{k}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}\biggr|\geqslant\biggl|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}p_{x_{k}}\exp\biggl\{i\sum_{j=1}^{d}c^{(j)}x^{(j)}_{k}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}}\biggr\}\biggr|-\varepsilon=\bigl|f(c^{(1)}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\ldots,c^{(d)}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}})\bigr|-\varepsilon.

So we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geqslant|f(t^{(1)}_{\varepsilon},\ldots,t^{(d)}_{\varepsilon})|\geqslant|f(c^{(1)}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\ldots,c^{(d)}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}})|-3\varepsilon.

Thus for any
\varepsilon>0
we found

such that

\displaystyle|f(c^{(1)}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}},\ldots,c^{(d)}t^{\prime}_{\delta_{\varepsilon}})|\leqslant 4\varepsilon.

This obviously contradicts to the assumption (

). So (

) holds.

\displaystyle f(t)=\exp\biggr\{i\langle t,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\bigr(e^{i\langle t,u\rangle}-1\bigr)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

,

for all

, and

for all

. Let us write the characteristic function

of

for any

:

\displaystyle f_{c}(t)=\exp\biggl\{it\langle c,\gamma\rangle+\sum_{u\in\langle X\rangle\setminus\{\bar{0}\}}\lambda_{u}\bigr(e^{it\langle c,u\rangle}-1\bigr)\biggr\},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Let us fix

such that the elements of the union system

are linearly independent over

. On account of (

), and that

, we have

for any distinct

. Since

is infinitely divisible (by assumption), we conclude that

for all

.

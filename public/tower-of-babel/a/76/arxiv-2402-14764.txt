A Combinatorial Central Limit Theorem for Stratified Randomization Thanks: The author thanks Xavier D’Haultfœuille for helpful discussions that led to this work.
Purevdorj Tuvaandorj

Abstract
This paper establishes a combinatorial central limit theorem for stratified randomization, which holds under a Lindeberg-type condition. The theorem allows for an arbitrary number or sizes of strata, with the sole requirement being that each stratum contains at least two units. This flexibility accommodates both a growing number of large and small strata simultaneously, while imposing minimal conditions. We then apply this result to derive the asymptotic distributions of two test statistics proposed for instrumental variables settings in the presence of potentially many strata of unrestricted sizes. Keywords: Combinatorial central limit theorem, permutation test, randomization inference, stratification.



1
Introduction

Stratified randomization is frequently utilized to enhance covariate balance in randomized experiments.
In this scheme, blocks or strata are initially created based on pre-treatment variables, and randomization inference is subsequently conducted by randomizing units within each stratum
(see e.g.,
Imbens and
Rubin 2015
, Chapter 9)
. Stratified randomization can be used for obtaining exact tests
(
Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
;
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
;
Zhao and
Ding 2021
;
D’Haultfœuille and Tuvaandorj 2024
)
and efficient estimators
(
Cytrynbaum 2021
;
Armstrong 2022
;
Bai
et al. 2023
)
, and is a natural inferential choice for models with survey data obtained through stratified sampling.

The key tool used when establishing the asymptotic validity of the permutation tests is
the combinatorial central limit theorem (CLT)
(
Hoeffding 1951
)
.
While the standard combinatorial CLT or finite population CLT can be applied to derive the asymptotic distributions of stratified permutation statistics when there is a finite number of large strata

(
Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
;
Li and Ding 2017
)
, its application to general stratified permutations is not straightforward. This complication arises because the number of strata, either large or small, or both, may
grow
with the sample size (for instance, consider the case of having
n^{1/2}
many strata, each of size
n^{1/2}
, where
n
denotes the sample size).

Despite recent contributions by
Liu and
Yang 2020a
and
Liu
et al. 2023
that propose finite population CLTs tailored for such scenarios, a combinatorial CLT accommodating potentially many strata of unrestricted size remains essential for addressing general stratified permutation/rank statistics. In this context, this paper introduces a general form of the combinatorial CLT for stratified randomization, applicable under a
Lindeberg-type
condition. The proof is based on Stein’s method
(see, e.g.,
Chen
et al. 2011
, for a detailed account)
, and in particular, draws from the constructions of
Bolthausen 1984
and
Schneller 1988
. Leveraging this result, we characterize the asymptotic distribution of a test statistic proposed by
Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
and a variant of a test statistic by
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
in the instrumental variables (IVs) setting featuring many strata, under weak assumptions.

A substantial body of literature exists on stratified randomized experiments and large sample results for stratified randomization. For empirical practices and an overview, see, for example,
Bruhn and
McKenzie 2009
,
Imbens and
Rubin 2015
and
Ding 2023
. Papers that consider a coarse stratification involving a finite number of strata, where asymptotic normality is typically derived via finite population or rank CLTs, include
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
,
Li and Ding 2017
,
Zhao and
Ding 2021
. Papers that consider a finely stratified experiment with many strata, where the asymptotic normality is derived via Lindeberg or Lyapunov CLTs, include
Fogarty 2018
,
Bai 2022
and
Bai
et al. 2022
.

Furthermore, while
Liu and
Yang 2020a
and
Liu
et al. 2023
provide finite population CLTs for stratified experiments involving many strata,
D’Haultfœuille and Tuvaandorj 2024
establish a combinatorial CLT with many strata to examine the properties of a stratified permutation subvector test in linear regressions. The CLT for stratified randomization in this paper is sufficiently general to encompass the aforementioned CLTs as it handles both large and small strata simultaneously and requires a weaker Lindeberg-type condition. In particular, finite population CLTs with many strata follow from the result akin to the derivation of usual finite population CLTs from standard combinatorial or rank CLTs.

Stratified permutation is an instance of permutations with
restricted positions

(
Rosenbaum 1984
;
Diaconis
et al. 2001
;
Lei and
Bickel 2021
, see, e.g.,)
; thus, the set of such permutations forms a subset of all permutations of observation indices.
Chen
et al. 2011
provide normal approximation results for a class of restricted permutations — in contrast with the set of entire permutations required in
Hoeffding 1951
’s CLT — but do not consider stratified permutations. The present paper fills this gap in the literature by developing normal approximation for this important class of permutations.

This paper proceeds as follows. Section
2
lays out the basic framework and presents the combinatorial CLT for stratified randomization with an arbitrary number of strata. Section
3
establishes a connection between our result and finite population CLTs. Section
4
applies the combinatorial CLT to derive the asymptotic distribution of two test statistics in IV settings. The proofs are provided in the appendix.

Relation to finite population CLT for stratified randomization

This section explores the connection between the combinatorial CLT of Theorem
2.1
and a finite population CLT.

Bickel and
Freedman 1984
and
Liu and
Yang 2020a
establish finite population CLTs for linear combinations of stratum means under stratified sampling, where the number of strata may diverge.

Consider a finite population divided into non-random
{S}
strata:
(y_{s1},\dots,y_{sn_{s}}),s=1,\dots,{S}
. The stratum-specific mean and variance are defined respectively as
\bar{y}_{s}\equiv n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}y_{si}
and
v_{s}^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}(y_{si}-\bar{y}_{s})^{2}
, while the population mean and weighted variance are given by

\bar{y}\equiv n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}y_{si}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_{s}\bar{y}_{s}\ \ \text{and}\ \ v^{2}\equiv\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_{s}^{2}v_{s}^{2}\frac{n_{s}-n_{1s}}{n_{1s}n_{s}},

(3.1)

where
p_{s}\equiv n_{s}/n
and
n_{1s},2\leq n_{1s}\leq n_{s}-1,
denotes the number of units sampled without replacement from stratum
s
in
Bickel and
Freedman 1984
’s set-up (the
number of treated units in stratum
s
in
Liu and
Yang 2020a
’s set-up). The sampling indicators are
(Z_{s1},\dots,Z_{sn_{s}})\in\{0,1\}^{n_{s}},s=1,\dots,S
, where
Z_{si}=1
if the unit
i
in stratum
s
is sampled, and
0
otherwise. The probability that
\{(Z_{s1},\dots,Z_{sn_{s}})\}_{s=1}^{S}
takes on a value

\{(z_{s1},\dots,z_{sn_{s}})\}_{s=1}^{S}
such that
n_{1s}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}z_{si}
for
s=1,\dots,S,
is
\prod_{s=1}^{S}n_{1s}!(n_{s}-n_{1s})!/n_{s}!
. The weighted sample mean and variance are then defined as

\hat{y}\equiv\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_{s}\hat{y}_{s}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \hat{v}^{2}\equiv\sum_{s=1}^{S}p_{s}^{2}\hat{v}_{s}^{2}\frac{n_{s}-n_{1s}}{n_{1s}n_{s}},

where
\hat{y}_{s}\equiv n_{1s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}Z_{si}y_{si}
and
\hat{v}_{s}^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\sum_{i=1:Z_{si}=1}^{n_{s}}(y_{si}-\hat{y}_{s})^{2}
are the stratum-specific sample mean and variance.
With the notations introduced, the Lindeberg condition employed by
Liu and
Yang 2020b
(Condition A1 and Theorem A1 therein) may be restated as follows: for any
\epsilon>0

\sum_{s=1}^{{S}}\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}w_{s}^{2}\frac{(y_{si}-\bar{y}_{s})^{2}}{v_{s}^{2}}1\left(w_{s}\frac{|y_{si}-\bar{y}_{s}|}{v_{s}}>\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n_{1s}(n_{s}-n_{1s})}{n_{s}}}\right)\right)\to 0

(3.2)

as
n\to\infty
, where

w_{s}^{2}\equiv\frac{p_{s}^{2}v_{s}^{2}(n_{s}-n_{1s})}{v^{2}n_{s}n_{1s}}.

(3.3)

Under the condition in (
3.2
),
Bickel and
Freedman 1984
show that
(\hat{y}-\bar{y})/{\hat{v}}\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right)
as
n\to\infty
. To draw a link between the latter result and Theorem
2.1
, we let
a_{ij}^{s}=\tilde{b}_{si}\tilde{c}_{sj}
as in
Remark
2.2
(6)
and Corollary
2.3
, where
b_{si}
and
c_{sj}
are now defined as

b_{si}\equiv\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n_{1s}}&\text{if}\quad i=1,\dots,n_{1s},\\
0&\text{if}\quad i=n_{1s}+1,\dots,n_{s}\end{cases},\quad c_{sj}\equiv p_{s}y_{sj}.

(3.4)

The choice of
b_{si}
in (
3.4
) appears in
Madow 1948
and
Sen 1995
, among others.
By simple algebra,
\bar{b}_{s}=n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}b_{si}=n_{s}^{-1}
,
\bar{c}_{s}=n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{s}}c_{sj}=p_{s}\bar{y}_{s}
, and

\tilde{b}_{si}=b_{si}-\bar{b}_{s}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{n_{1s}}-\frac{1}{n_{s}}&\text{if}\quad i=1,\dots,n_{1s},\\
-\frac{1}{n_{s}}&\text{if}\quad i=n_{1s}+1,\dots,n_{s}\end{cases},\quad\tilde{c}_{sj}=c_{sj}-\bar{c}_{s}=p_{s}({y}_{sj}-\bar{y}_{s}).

(3.5)

It turns out that the variance

\sigma_{n}^{2}
defined in the condition
(b)
of Theorem

and

in (

) are identical:

\sigma_{n}^{2}=\sum_{s=1}^{{S}}\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\tilde{b}_{si}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\tilde{c}_{si}^{2}\right)=\sum_{s=1}^{{S}}\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\frac{(n_{s}-n_{1s})p_{s}^{2}}{n_{1s}n_{s}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}({y}_{si}-\bar{y}_{s})^{2}\right)=v^{2}.

(3.6)

Then, through a direct calculation given in Section

, the Lindeberg condition in Theorem

(b)
follows from the condition in (

), as summarized in the remark below.

Remark 3.1
.

The condition in (

) implies the Lindeberg condition in Theorem

(b)

4
Stratified randomization inference with IVs

This section presents two applications of the CLT in Theorem
2.1
. First, we establish the asymptotic distribution of a statistic proposed by

Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
. Second, we show that the test of
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
may be conservative in the presence of small strata. We then consider a simple modification of their statistic that yields a correct asymptotic level and derive its null asymptotic distribution.

4.1
Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
statistic

Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
consider an IV setting with non-random
S
strata
s=1,\dots,S
each of size
n_{s}
, given by:

Y-\beta D=r_{C},

where

D=[D_{1}^{\prime},\dots,D_{S}^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
D_{s}=[D_{s1},\dots,D_{sn_{s}}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{s}}
and
n=\sum_{s=1}^{S}n_{s}
, denotes the vector of doses received by the units,

Y=[Y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,Y_{S}^{\prime}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
Y_{s}=[Y_{s1},\dots,Y_{sn_{s}}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{s}}
is the vector of responses exhibited by the units,

r_{C}=[r_{C1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{CS}^{\prime}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
r_{Cs}=[r_{Cs1},\dots,r_{Csn_{s}}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{s}}
is the vector of responses if the units received the control dose, and the scalar parameter
\beta
, with a true value
\beta_{0}
, measures of the dose-response relationship.

Let
h_{s}=[h_{s1},\dots,h_{sn_{s}}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{s}}
be a sorted and fixed IV vector such that

h_{sj}\leq h_{s(j+1)}
for each
j=1,\dots,n_{s}-1
and
s=1,\dots,S
. For
h\equiv[h_{1}^{\prime},\dots,h_{S}^{\prime}]^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, assume that the observed IV
Z
is randomly assigned i.e.

Z=h_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\pi\sim\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{S}_{n})
and
\mathbb{S}_{n}
is the set of all stratified permutations in this setting.

Here,
r_{C}=Y-\beta_{0}D
is assumed to be fixed, hence independent of
Z
. For testing the hypothesis
H_{0}:\beta=\beta_{0}
,

Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
propose the following statistic:

T=q(Y-\beta_{0}D)^{\prime}\rho(Z),

(4.1)

where
q(\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is some method of scoring responses such as the ranks or the aligned ranks, and

\rho(\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is some method of scoring the instruments that satisfies
\rho_{\pi}(h)=\rho(h_{\pi})=\rho(Z)
. Let
\rho(h)=[\rho_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\rho_{S}^{\prime}]^{\prime}
with
\rho_{s}=[\rho_{s1},\dots,\rho_{sn_{s}}]^{\prime}
, and
q(Y-\beta_{0}D)=[q_{1}^{\prime},\dots,q_{S}^{\prime}]^{\prime}
with
q_{s}=[q_{s1},\dots,q_{sn_{s}}]^{\prime}
.

Before we state the result for the asymptotic distribution of the statistic

, we remark that

T=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}q_{si}\rho_{s\pi(i)}
using the notations above. With a convention that
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}[\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}q_{si}\rho_{s\pi(i)}]=0
for stratum of size
n_{s}=1
, it follows from Proposition 1 of
Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
that

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\equiv\operatorname{E}_{\pi}[T]=\sum_{s=1}^{S}{n_{s}}\bar{q}_{s}\bar{\rho}_{s},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\equiv\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}[T]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1,n_{s}\geq 2}^{S}\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\tilde{q}_{si}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\tilde{\rho}_{si}^{2}\right),

(4.3)

where
\bar{q}_{s}\equiv n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}q_{si}
,
\bar{\rho}_{s}\equiv n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\rho_{si}
,
and
\tilde{q}_{si}\equiv q_{si}-\bar{q}_{s}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{si}\equiv\rho_{si}-\bar{\rho}_{s}
.

Imbens and
Rosenbaum 2005
outline strategies for deriving the asymptotic distribution of the statistic
T
in the presence of either a few large strata whose sizes tend to infinity, or many small strata, whose sizes remain bounded, and suggest applying the rank-central limit theorem
(see e.g.,
Hájek
et al. 1999
, Chapter 6)
to the former and standard CLTs to the latter.

Li and Ding 2017
consider the case where
q(\cdot)
is an identity function and
\rho(Z)
is a vector of binary IVs, and derive the null asymptotic distribution of the statistic in (
4.1
). The general CLT provided in Theorem
2.1
allows us to derive the asymptotic distribution of the statistic
T
in (
4.1
) at once under general conditions, allowing for many strata without categorizing them into large and small ones or restricting their sizes. The following proposition gives a formal justification for this argument.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{\lim\inf\ }_{n\to\infty}\sigma^{2}>\lambda>0
, and for some positive constants
M_{0}
and
\delta
, either

(a)

n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}|\rho_{si}|^{4+\delta}<M_{0}
and
n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}|q_{si}|^{4+\delta}<M_{0}
for all
n
; or

(b)

n^{-1/2}\max_{s,i}|\rho_{si}|\to 0
,
n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}|\rho_{si}|^{2}<M_{0}
and
\max_{1\leq s\leq S}n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}|{q}_{si}|^{2+\delta}<M_{0}
for all
n
.

Then, under
H_{0}:\beta=\beta_{0}
,
{\sigma}^{-1}(T-\mu)\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\left(0,1\right)
as
(n-S)\to\infty
.

Remark 4.2
.

(1)

The strata of size
n_{s}=1
are discarded in the statistic
{\sigma}^{-1}(T-\mu)
. Since
n-S
, which corresponds to the effective sample size, tends to infinity, so does the number of observations stratified into strata of sizes
2
or greater.
Consequently, the condition
n\to\infty
in Theorem
2.1
can be replaced by
(n-S)\to\infty
.

For
S
random, a sufficient condition for
(n-S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty
is provided in Lemma 2 of
D’Haultfœuille and Tuvaandorj 2024
.

(2)

Assumption
(b)
on
q_{si}
holds, for example, if
q_{si}=\varphi(i/(n_{s}+1))
, where
\varphi:(0,1)\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is a score function
satisfying
\int_{0}^{1}|{\varphi}(x)|^{2+\delta}dx<M_{0}/2
. This is because
(n_{s}+1)/n_{s}\leq 2
and

(n_{s}+1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}|\varphi(i/(n_{s}+1))|^{2+\delta}\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\varphi(x)|^{2+\delta}dx,

(4.4)

as the LHS of the inequality above is the sum of the areas of
n_{s}

rectangles with sides
|\varphi(i/(n_{s}+1))|^{2+\delta}
and
[i/(n_{s}+1),(i+1)/(n_{s}+1)]
,
i=1,\dots,n_{s},
which lie between the function
|\varphi(x)|^{2+\delta}
and
[0,1]
.

For the Wilcoxon score
\varphi(x)=x
, letting
\delta=1
, the RHS of (
4.4
) is
\int_{0}^{1}x^{3}dx=1/4
. For the normal (or van der Waerden) score
\varphi(x)=\Phi^{-1}(x)
, letting
\delta=2
, we have, through a change of variables,

\int_{0}^{1}(\Phi^{-1}(x))^{4}dx=3
. So the condition on
q_{si}
in Assumption
(b)
is satisfied for these score functions.

4.2
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
statistic

Next, we consider a related rank-based Anderson-Rubin-type statistic
(
Anderson and
Rubin 1949
)
studied in
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
under a super population framework.
The model is

Y_{si}=\alpha_{s}+D_{si}\beta+r_{Csi},\quad i=1,\dots,n_{s};\ s=1,\dots,S,

(4.5)

where
\alpha_{s}
denotes a stratum-specific scalar parameter,
Y_{si}
is the response variable,
D_{si}
is a scalar endogenous regressor,
r_{Csi}
is the error term which, unlike the previous case, is random, and the number of strata
S
is not random. In addition, there is a
k
-vector of IVs,
Z_{si}
, with
k\geq 1
, that does not include
a constant. The model in (
4.5
) may arise, for example, due to stratification based on empirical support points

\{X_{1},\dots,X_{S}\}
of auxiliary regressors
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p},p\geq 1,

i.e.

\alpha_{s}\equiv X_{s}^{\prime}\eta_{s}
, where
X_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and a parameter vector
\eta_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are constant within stratum
s
, but may vary across strata. The hypothesis of interest is again
H_{0}:\beta=\beta_{0}
.

Let
R_{si}
denote the rank of
Y_{si}-\beta_{0}D_{si}
among
Y_{s1}-\beta_{0}D_{s1},\dots,Y_{sn_{s}}-\beta_{0}D_{sn_{s}}
, and

\varphi:(0,1)\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a score function. The

Andrews and
Marmer 2008
statistic (Equation (3.3) therein) may be defined as

B_{n}\equiv n\,A_{n}^{\prime}\Omega_{n}^{-1}A_{n},

(4.6)

where
A_{n}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}A_{ns}
,
A_{ns}\equiv n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}(Z_{si}-\bar{Z}_{s})\varphi\left(\frac{R_{si}}{n_{s}+1}\right)
,

\bar{Z}_{s}\equiv n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}Z_{si}
, and

\Omega_{n}\equiv n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}(Z_{si}-\bar{Z}_{s})(Z_{si}-\bar{Z}_{s})^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}(\varphi(x)-\bar{\varphi})^{2}dx,

(4.7)

with
\bar{\varphi}\equiv\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(x)dx
. Remark here that if
\{r_{Csi}\}_{i=1}^{n_{s}}
are i.i.d. with a continuous distribution,
\{R_{si}\}_{i=1}^{n_{s}}
are uniformly distributed over
\{1,2,\dots,n_{s}\}

(see e.g.,
van der
Vaart 1998
, Lemma 13.1)
, so we can reformulate
A_{n}
as a stratified permutation statistic. We deviate from
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
in a minor way by considering the statistic

B_{n}^{*}\equiv n\,A_{n}^{\prime}\Omega_{n}^{*-1}A_{n},

(4.8)

with a covariance matrix estimator
\Omega_{n}^{*}
defined as

\Omega_{n}^{*}\equiv n^{-1}\sum_{s=1,n_{s}\geq 2}^{S}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}(Z_{si}-\bar{Z}_{s})(Z_{si}-\bar{Z}_{s})^{\prime}\right\}\left\{\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\left(\varphi_{si}-\bar{\varphi}_{s}\right)^{2}\right\},

(4.9)

where
{\varphi}_{si}\equiv\varphi\left(\frac{i}{n_{s}+1}\right)
and
\bar{\varphi}_{s}\equiv n_{s}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\varphi_{si}
.
The estimator in (
4.9
) is preferred to that in (
4.7
) because
the former is the variance of
n^{1/2}A_{n}
(with respect to the distribution of the ranks), whereas the covariance matrix in (
4.7
) is an approximation valid only when the strata are large, a scenario considered by
Andrews and
Marmer 2008
. In fact, when there are many small strata, the term
\int_{0}^{1}(\varphi(x)-\bar{\varphi})^{2}dx

will involve a nonnegligible error as an approximation of
(n_{s}-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\left(\varphi_{si}-\bar{\varphi}_{s}\right)^{2}
. By way of example, let
n_{s}=2
for all
s=1,\dots,S
and
\varphi(x)=\Phi^{-1}(x)
. Simple calculations yield
\bar{\varphi}_{s}=(\Phi^{-1}(1/3)+\Phi^{-1}(2/3))/2=0
and

\frac{1}{n_{s}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\left(\varphi_{si}-\bar{\varphi}_{s}\right)^{2}=(\Phi^{-1}(1/3))^{2}+(\Phi^{-1}(2/3))^{2}\approx 0.37.

(4.10)

This is far below the value
\int_{0}^{1}(\varphi(x)-\bar{\varphi})^{2}dx=1
, so the covariance matrix estimator in (
4.7
) is biased upward as an estimator of the asymptotic variance of
n^{1/2}A_{n}
and the level-
\alpha
test that rejects when
B_{n}
exceeds the
1-\alpha
quantile of
\chi^{2}_{k}
distribution will be conservative. The LHS of (
4.10
) (after rounding) is 0.56, 0.69, 0.89, 0.96, 0.98 for
n_{s}=5,10,50,200,500
, respectively, indicating a reduction in the approximation error with increasing strata sizes. A similar result holds for the Wilcoxon score as well.

The null asymptotic distribution of the statistic
B_{n}^{*}
is provided in the proposition below.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
\displaystyle\operatornamewithlimits{\lim\inf\ }_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{n}^{*})>\lambda>0
, and

(a)

n^{-1/2}\max_{s,i}\|Z_{si}\|\to 0
,
n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{s}}\|Z_{si}\|^{2}<C_{0}
for all
n
, and

\int_{0}^{1}|{\varphi}(x)|^{2+\delta}dx<C_{0}

for some positive constants
\delta
and
C_{0}
;

(b)

\{r_{Csi}:1\leq i\leq n_{s},1\leq s\leq S\}
are independent random variables and for each
s
,
\{r_{Csi}\}_{i=1}^{n_{s}}
have an identical continuous distribution.

Then, under
H_{0}:\beta=\beta_{0}
,
B_{n}^{*}\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\chi^{2}_{k}
as
(n-S)\to\infty
.

(a)

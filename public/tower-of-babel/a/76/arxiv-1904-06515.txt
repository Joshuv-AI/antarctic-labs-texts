Hom-Lie Algebras and Hom-Lie Groups, Integration and Differentiation
Jun JIANG †, Satyendra Kumar MISHRA ‡ and Yunhe SHENG †

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notion of a (regular) Hom-Lie group. We associate a Hom-Lie algebra to a Hom-Lie group and show that every regular Hom-Lie algebra is integrable. Then, we define a Hom-exponential ( 𝖧𝖾𝗑𝗉 \mathsf{Hexp} ) map from the Hom-Lie algebra of a Hom-Lie group to the Hom-Lie group and discuss the universality of this 𝖧𝖾𝗑𝗉 \mathsf{Hexp} map. We also describe a Hom-Lie group action on a smooth manifold. Subsequently, we give the notion of an adjoint representation of a Hom-Lie group on its Hom-Lie algebra. At last, we integrate the Hom-Lie algebra ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( V ) , [ ⋅ , ⋅ ] , 𝖠𝖽 ) (\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot],\mathsf{Ad}) , and the derivation Hom-Lie algebra of a Hom-Lie algebra.

Introduction

The notion of a Hom-Lie algebra first appeared in the study of quantum deformations of Witt and Virasoro algebras in
[
6
]
. Hom-Lie algebras are generalizations of Lie algebras, where the Jacobi identity is twisted by a linear map, called the Hom-Jacobi identity. It is known that
q
-deformations of the Witt and the Virasoro algebras have the structure of a Hom-Lie algebra
[
6
,
9
]
. There is a growing interest in Hom-algebraic structures because of their close relationship with the discrete and deformed vector fields, and differential calculus
[
6
,
10
,
11
]
. In particular, representations and deformations of Hom-Lie algebras were studied in
[
1
,
15
,
17
]
; a categorical interpretation of Hom-Lie algebras was described in
[
3
]
; the categorification of Hom-Lie algebras was given in
[
18
]
; geometric and algebraic generalizations of Hom-Lie algebras were given in
[
4
,
14
,
16
]
; quantization of Hom-Lie algebras was studied in
[
21
]
; and the universal enveloping algebra of Hom-Lie algebras was studied in
[
13
,
20
]
.

The notion of Hom-groups was initially introduced by Caenepeel and Goyvaerts in
[
3
]
. In
[
13
]
, Laurent-Gengoux, Makhlouf and Teles first gave a new construction of the universal enveloping algebra that is different from the one in
[
20
]
. This new construction leads to a Hom-Hopf algebra structure on the universal enveloping algebra of a Hom-Lie algebra. Moreover, one can associate a Hom-group to any Hom-Lie algebra by considering group-like elements in its universal enveloping algebra. Recently, M. Hassanzadeh developed representations and a (co)homology theory for Hom-groups in
[
7
]
. He also proved Lagrange’s theorem for finite Hom-groups in
[
8
]
. The recent developments on Hom-groups (see
[
7
,
8
,
13
]
) make it natural to study Hom-Lie groups and to explore the relationship between Hom-Lie groups and Hom-Lie algebras.

In this paper, we introduce a (real) Hom-Lie group as a Hom-group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
, where the underlying set
G
is a (real) smooth manifold, the Hom-group operations (such as the product and the inverse) are smooth maps, and the underlying structure map
\Phi\colon G\rightarrow G
is a diffeomorphism. We associate a Hom-Lie algebra to a Hom-Lie group by considering the notion of left-invariant sections of the pullback bundle
\Phi^{!}TG
. We define one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups of a Hom-Lie group and discuss a Hom-analogue of the exponential map from the Hom-Lie algebra of a Hom-Lie group to the Hom-Lie group. Later on, we consider Hom-Lie group actions on a manifold
M
with respect to a map
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
and define an adjoint representation of a Hom-Lie group on its Hom-Lie algebra. Finally, we discuss the integration of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
and the derivation Hom-Lie algebra
\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
of a Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

All the results in the paper are under the regularity hypothesis. As appeared in the literature, e.g.,
[
12
]
, Hom-Lie algebras are not necessarily regular. We will explore this more general case in the future.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall some basic definitions and results concerning Hom-Lie algebras and Hom-groups.
In Section
3
, we define the notion of a Hom-Lie group with some useful examples. If
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
is a Hom-Lie group, then we show that the space of left-invariant sections of the pullback bundle
\Phi^{!}TG
has a Hom-Lie algebra structure. Consequently, we deduce a Hom-Lie algebra structure on the fibre of the pullback bundle
\Phi^{!}TG
at
e_{\Phi}
. In this way, we associate a Hom-Lie algebra
\big(\mathfrak{g}^{!},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}\big)
to the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
, where
\mathfrak{g}^{!}:=\Phi^{!}T_{e_{\Phi}}G
. We also show that every regular Hom-Lie algebra is integrable. Next, we define one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups of a Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
in terms of a weak homomorphism of Hom-Lie groups. We establish a one-to-one correspondence with
\mathfrak{g}^{!}
. In the end, we define Hom-exponential map
{\mathsf{Hexp}}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{!}\rightarrow G
and discuss its properties.
In Section
4
, we study Hom-Lie group actions on a smooth manifold
M
with respect to a diffeomorphism
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
. We define representations of a Hom-Lie group on a vector space
V
with respect to a map
\beta\in{\rm GL}(V)
, which leads to the notion of an adjoint representation of a Hom-Lie group on the associated Hom-Lie algebra.
In Section
5
, we consider the Hom-Lie group
({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
, where
V
is a vector space and
\beta\in{\rm GL}(V)
. We show that the triple
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
is the Hom-Lie algebra of the Hom-Lie group
({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
.
In the last section, we show that the derivation Hom-Lie algebra
\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
is the Hom-Lie algebra of the Hom-Lie group of automorphisms of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall definitions of Hom-Lie algebras and Hom-groups.

2.1
Hom-Lie algebras

Definition 2.1
.

A (multiplicative)
Hom-Lie algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
consisting of a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, a skew-symmetric bilinear map (bracket)
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, and a linear map
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
preserving the bracket, such that the following Hom-Jacobi
identity with respect to
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is satisfied:

[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y),[z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(z),[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,\qquad\forall\,x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

A Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is called a
regular Hom-Lie algebra
if
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
an invertible map.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a regular Hom-Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
is a Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie}
is given by
[x,y]_{\rm Lie}=\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

In the sequel, we always assume that
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is an invertible map. That is, in this paper, all the Hom-Lie algebras are assumed to be regular Hom-Lie algebras.

Remark 2.3
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors used a monoidal categorical
approach to give an intrinsic study of regular Hom-type algebraic structures. In particular, a regular Hom-Lie algebra is called a monoidal Hom-Lie algebra there. See
[
2
]
for the categorical framework study of the BiHom-type structures.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Hom-Lie algebras.

(i)

A linear map
f\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is called a
weak homomorphism
of Hom-Lie algebras if

\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}f[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[f(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)),f(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))]_{\mathfrak{h}},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

(ii)

A weak homomorphism
f\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is called a
homomorphism
if
f
also satisfies

\displaystyle f\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ f.

Definition 2.5
.

A representation of a Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a vector space
V
with respect to
\beta\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a linear map
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
such that for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following equations are satisfied

\displaystyle\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\circ\beta=\beta\circ\rho(x),

\displaystyle\rho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\circ\beta=\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\circ\rho(y)-\rho(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y))\circ\rho(x).

For all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, let us define a map
\mathsf{ad}_{x}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
by

\displaystyle\mathsf{ad}_{x}(y)=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall\,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
\mathsf{ad}\colon\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is a representation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
on
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
, which is called the
adjoint representation
.

Let
(\rho,V,\beta)
be a representation of a Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a vector space
V
with respect to the map
\beta\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. Then let us recall from
[
5
]
that the cohomology of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
with coefficients in
(\rho,V,\beta)
is the cohomology of the cochain complex
C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\mathsf{Hom}(\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g},V)
with the coboundary operator
d\colon C^{k}(\mathfrak{g},V)\rightarrow C^{k+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
defined by

\displaystyle(df)(x_{1},\dots,x_{k+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(x_{i})\big(f\big(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{1},\dots,\widehat{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{i}},\dots,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{k+1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\beta f\big(\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-2}x_{i},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-2}x_{j}\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{1},\dots,\widehat{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{i}},\dots,\widehat{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{j}},\dots,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}x_{k+1}\big).

The fact that
d^{2}=0
is proved in
[
5
]
. Denote by
\mathcal{Z}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\rho)
and
\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\rho)
the sets of
k
-cocycles and
k
-coboundaries respectively. We define the
k
-th cohomology group

\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\rho)
to be
\mathcal{Z}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\rho)\!/\!\mathcal{B}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\rho)
.

Let
(\mathsf{ad},\mathfrak{g},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be the adjoint representation. For any 0-Hom-cochain
x\in\mathfrak{g}=C^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, we have

(dx)(y)=[y,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall\,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Thus, we have
dx=0
if and only if
x\in\mathsf{Cen}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\mathsf{Cen}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the center of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Therefore,

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{ad})=\mathcal{Z}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{ad})=\mathsf{Cen}(\mathfrak{g}).

Definition 2.6
.

A linear map
D\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is called a
derivation
of a Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
if the following identity holds:

\displaystyle D[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\phi(x),\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}D\big)(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\big[\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}D\big)(x),\phi(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

We denote the space of derivations of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Let us observe that if
D\circ\phi=\phi\circ D
in the Definition
2.6
, then any derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
-derivation (see
[
17
]
for more details).

Example 2.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Hom-Lie algebra. For each
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathsf{ad}_{x}
is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
that we call an “inner derivation”.

Let us denote the space of inner derivations by
\sf{InnDer}(\mathfrak{g})
. It is immediate to see that

\mathcal{Z}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{ad})=\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),\qquad\mathcal{B}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{ad})=\sf{InnDer}(\mathfrak{g}).

Therefore,

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathsf{ad})=\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g})/\sf{InnDer}(\mathfrak{g})=\sf{OutDer}(\mathfrak{g}).

Here,
\sf{OutDer}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the space of outer derivations of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Let
V
be a vector space, and
\beta\in{\rm GL}(V)
. Let us define a skew-symmetric bilinear bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta}\colon\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{gl}(V)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
by

\displaystyle[A,B]_{\beta}=\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ B\circ\beta^{-1}-\beta\circ B\circ\beta^{-1}\circ A\circ\beta^{-1},\qquad\forall\,A,B\in\mathfrak{gl}(V).

We also define a map
\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}\colon\mathfrak{gl}(V)\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
by

\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A)=\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1},\qquad\forall\,A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V).

With the above notations, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
19
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

The triple
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
is a regular Hom-Lie algebra.

The Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
plays an important role in the representation theory of Hom-Lie algebras. See
[
19
]
for more details.

2.2
Hom-groups

Throughout this paper, we consider regular Hom-groups that is the case when the structure map is invertible, and this notion can be traced back to Caenepeel and Goyvaerts’s pioneering work
[
3
]
. The axioms in the following definition of Hom-group is different from the one in
[
7
,
8
,
13
]
. However, we show that if the structure map is invertible, then some axioms in the original definition are redundant and can be obtained from the Hom-associativity condition.

Definition 2.9
.

A (regular) Hom-group is a set
G
equipped with a product
\diamond\colon G\times G\longrightarrow G
, a bijective map
\Phi\colon G\longrightarrow G
such that the following axioms are satisfied

(i)

\Phi(x\diamond y)=\Phi(x)\diamond\Phi(y)
;

(ii)

the product is Hom-associative, i.e.,

\Phi(x)\diamond(y\diamond z)=(x\diamond y)\diamond\Phi(z),\qquad\forall\,x,y,z\in G;

(iii)

there exists a unique Hom-unit
e_{\Phi}\in G
such that

x\diamond e_{\Phi}=e_{\Phi}\diamond x=\Phi(x),\qquad\forall\,x\in G;

(iv)

for each
x\in G
, there exists an element
x^{-1}\in G
satisfying the following condition

x\diamond x^{-1}=x^{-1}\diamond x=e_{\Phi}.

We denote a Hom-group by
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
.

Remark 2.10
.

The category of sets
(\textbf{Sets},\times,\{\ast\},\tau)
(where
\tau
is the twist) is a symmetric (strict) monoidal category. Algebras (monoids) in
Sets
are also bialgebras since every set
X
has a unique structure of coalgebra in
Sets
, namely
\Delta(x)=(x,x)
and
\varepsilon(x)=\ast
, for all
x\in X
. A Hopf algebra in
Sets
is a group. Let
X
be a set and
\pi
be a permutation. Then
(X,\pi,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a Hom-comonoid, where
\Delta\colon X\rightarrow X\times X
and
\varepsilon\colon X\rightarrow X
are the maps given by
\Delta(x)=\big(\pi^{-1}(x),\pi^{-1}(x)\big)
and
\varepsilon(x)=\ast
. Similarly, if
\phi
is an automorphism of a group
G
, then
(G,\phi)
with structure maps

\displaystyle g\cdot h=\phi(gh),\qquad\eta(\ast)=1_{G},\qquad\varepsilon(g)=\ast,\qquad\Delta(g)=\big(\phi^{-1}(g),\phi^{-1}(g)\big),\qquad S(g)=g^{-1},

is a Hom-group, that is a Hopf algebra in
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}(\textbf{Sets})
in
[
3
, Section 5]
. Thus a monoidal categorical
approach can give an intrinsic study of regular Hom-groups.

Remark 2.11
.

Note that the definition of a Hom-group in
[
8
]
consists of the axiom
\Phi(e_{\Phi})=e_{\Phi}
. In Proposition
2.13
, we show that this axiom is redundant in the regular case. Let us recall the Hom-invertibility condition in the definition of a Hom-group
(G,\Phi)
in
[
13
]
: for each
x\in G
, there exists a positive integer
k
such that

\Phi^{k}\big(x\diamond x^{-1}\big)=\Phi^{k}\big(x^{-1}\diamond x\big)=e_{\Phi},

and the smallest such integer
k
is called the invertibility index of
x\in G
. In the regular case, it is immediate to see that the Hom-invertibility condition is equivalent to the condition (iv) in Definition
2.9
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
(G,\mu,e)
be a group and
\phi\colon G\rightarrow G
be a group automorphism. Then the tuple
(G,\mu_{\phi},e,\phi)
with the product
\mu_{\phi}=\phi\circ\mu,
is a Hom-group. In particular, the tuple
(\mathbb{R},+,0,{\rm Id})
is a Hom-group, which will be used in our later definition of one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups.

It is straightforward to obtain the following properties, which were also given in
[
3
]
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-group. Then we have the following properties.

(i)

\Phi(e_{\Phi})=e_{\Phi};

(ii)

for each
x\in G
, there exists a unique inverse
x^{-1}_{\Phi}
such that

x\diamond x^{-1}_{\Phi}=x^{-1}_{\Phi}\diamond x=e_{\Phi};

(iii)

(x\diamond y)^{-1}=y^{-1}\diamond x^{-1}
,
\forall\,x,y\in G
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
and
(H,\diamond_{H},e_{\Psi},\Psi)
be two Hom-groups.

(a)

A
homomorphism
of Hom-groups is a map
f\colon G\rightarrow H
such that

f(e_{\Phi})=e_{\Psi}
and
f(x\diamond_{G}y)=f(x)\diamond_{H}f(y)
for all
x,y\in G
.

(b)

A
weak homomorphism
of Hom-groups is a map
f\colon G\rightarrow H
such that

f(e_{\Phi})=e_{\Psi}
and

\Psi\circ f(x\diamond_{G}y)=\big(f\circ\Phi(x)\big)\diamond_{H}\big(f\circ\Phi(y)\big)
for all
x,y\in G
.

Let us observe that for a homomorphism
f\colon(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)\rightarrow(H,\diamond_{H},e_{\Psi},\Psi)
, the commutativity condition:
\Psi\circ f=f\circ\Phi
holds. It follows by the definition of a homomorphism and the identities:
\Phi(e_{\Phi})=e_{\Phi},
and
\Psi(e_{\Psi})=e_{\Psi}
. Furthermore, any homomorphism of Hom-groups is also a weak homomorphism, however, the converse may not be true.

3
Hom-Lie groups and Hom-Lie algebras

Let
\mathbb{R}
be the field of real numbers. From here onwards, we consider all manifolds, vector spaces over the field
\mathbb{R}
, and all the linear maps are considered to be
\mathbb{R}
-linear unless otherwise stated.

3.1
Hom-Lie groups

Definition 3.1
.

A real Hom-Lie group is a Hom-group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
, in which
G
is also a smooth real manifold, the map
\Phi\colon G\rightarrow G
is a diffeomorphism, and the Hom-group operations (product and inversion) are smooth maps with respect to the topology of
G
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a Lie group with identity
e
and
\Phi\colon(G,\cdot)\rightarrow(G,\cdot)
be an automorphism. Then the tuple
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi}=e,\Phi)
is a Hom-Lie group, where the product
\diamond
is defined by

a\diamond b=\Phi(a)\cdot\Phi(b),\qquad\forall\,a,b\in G.

Let
V
be a vector space and
\beta\in{\rm GL}(V)
. Then let us define a product
\diamond\colon{\rm GL}(V)\times{\rm GL}(V)\longrightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
by

\displaystyle A\diamond B=\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ B\circ\beta^{-1},\qquad\forall\,A,B\in{\rm GL}(V).

(3.1)

Proposition 3.3
.

The tuple
({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
is a Hom-Lie group, where the product
\diamond
is given by (
3.1
), the Hom-unit is
\beta
, and the map
\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}\colon{\rm GL}(V)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
is defined by

\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A)=\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1},\qquad\forall\,A\in{\rm GL}(V).

Proof.

For all
A,B\in{\rm GL}(V)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A\diamond B)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}\big(\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ B\circ\beta^{-1}\big)=\beta^{2}\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ B\circ\beta^{-2}

\displaystyle=\beta\circ\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A)\circ\beta^{-1}\circ\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(B)\circ\beta^{-1}=\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A)\diamond\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(B).

Thus, condition (i) in Definition
2.9
holds.
For all
A,B,C\in{\rm GL}(V)
, it easily follows that

\displaystyle(A\diamond B)\diamond(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(C))

\displaystyle=\big(\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ B\circ\beta^{-1}\big)\diamond\big(\beta\circ C\circ\beta^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ B\circ\beta^{-1}\circ C\circ\beta^{-2}

\displaystyle=\beta\circ\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A)\circ\beta^{-1}\circ(B\diamond C)\circ\beta^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A)\diamond(B\diamond C),

which implies that the product
\diamond
is Hom-associative. Next, we have

\displaystyle A\diamond\beta=\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ\beta\circ\beta^{-1}=\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A),\qquad\forall\,A\in{\rm GL}(V).

Similarly,

\beta\diamond A=\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta}(A),\qquad\forall\,A\in{\rm GL}(V).

Therefore,
\beta
is the Hom-unit. Finally, we have the following expression

\displaystyle A\diamond\big(\beta\circ A^{-1}\circ\beta\big)=\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ\big(\beta\circ A^{-1}\circ\beta\big)\circ\beta^{-1}=\beta=\big(\beta\circ A^{-1}\circ\beta\big)\diamond A,

for any
A\in{\rm GL}(V)

3.2
The Hom-Lie algebra of a Hom-Lie group

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. For
a\in G
, let us define a smooth map

l_{a}\colon\ G\rightarrow G\qquad\mbox{by}\quad l_{a}(b)=a\diamond b,\qquad\forall\,b\in G.

Then the smooth map
l_{a}\colon G\rightarrow G
is a diffeomorphism (by Definition
3.1
).

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. A smooth section
x\in\Gamma\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
is left-invariant if
x
satisfies the following equation
:

x(f)(a)=x\big(f\circ l_{a}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi}),\qquad\forall\,a\in G,f\in C^{\infty}(G).

(3.6)

Let us denote by
\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
, the space of all left-invariant sections of the pullback bundle
\Phi^{!}TG
. Next, we show that the space
\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
carries a Hom-Lie algebra structure. In fact, we prove that
\big(\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\Phi},\phi\big)
is a Hom-Lie subalgebra of the Hom-Lie algebra
\big(\Gamma\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\Phi},\phi\big)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group and
x\in\Gamma\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
be a left-invariant section. Then we have

x(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ l_{\Phi^{-1}(a)}=x(f\circ l_{a}),\qquad\forall\,a\in G.

(3.7)

Proof.

First, let us note that by using the Hom-associativity condition of the product
\diamond
, we get the following equation:

\Phi\circ l_{(\Phi^{-2}(a)\diamond\Phi^{-1}(b))}=l_{a}\circ l_{b},\qquad\forall\,a,b\in G,

which implies that

f\circ\Phi\circ l_{(\Phi^{-2}(a)\diamond\Phi^{-1}(b))}\circ\Phi^{-1}=f\circ l_{a}\circ l_{b}\circ\Phi^{-1}.

(3.8)

By using the left-invariant property (
3.6
) of the section
x
, we have

x(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ l_{\Phi^{-1}(a)}(b)=x\big(f\circ\Phi\circ l_{(\Phi^{-2}(a)\diamond\Phi^{-1}(b))}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi}),

(3.9)

and

x(f\circ l_{a})(b)=x\big(f\circ l_{a}\circ l_{b}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi}).

(3.10)

Thus, by (
3.8
)–(
3.10
), we deduce that the desired identity (
3.7
) holds.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

The space
\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
of left-invariant sections of the pullback bundle
\Phi^{!}TG
is a Hom-Lie subalgebra of the Hom-Lie algebra
\big(\Gamma\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\Phi},\phi\big)
.

Proof.

First, let us prove that
\phi(x)\in\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
for any
x\in\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
. By (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\phi(x)(f)(a)=\big(\Phi^{-1}\big)^{*}\circ x\circ\Phi^{*}(f)(a)=x(f\circ\Phi)\big(\Phi^{-1}(a)\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\phi(x)(f)(a)}{}=x\big(f\circ\Phi\circ l_{\Phi^{-1}(a)}\Phi^{-1}\big)(e)=x(f\circ l_{a})(e)=\phi(x)\big(f\circ l_{a}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e)

for all
x,y\in\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
, and
a\in G
. This, in turn, implies that
\phi(x)\in\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
.

Now we prove that
[x,y]_{\Phi}\in\Gamma_{L}\big(\Phi^{!}TG\big)
. By (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have the following expressions:

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\Phi}(f)(a)=x\big(y(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)\big(\Phi^{-1}(a)\big)-y\big(x(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)\big(\Phi^{-1}(a)\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{[x, y]_{\Phi}(f)(a)}{}=x\big(y(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ l_{\Phi^{-1}(a)}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi})

\displaystyle\hphantom{[x, y]_{\Phi}(f)(a)=}{}-y\big(x(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\circ l_{\Phi^{-1}(a)}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi}),

and

[x,y]_{\Phi}\big(f\circ l_{a}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi})=x\big(y(f\circ l_{a})\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi})-y\big(x(f\circ l_{a})\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi})

for all

and

[x,y]_{\Phi}(f)(a)=[x,y]_{\Phi}\big(f\circ l_{a}\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)(e_{\Phi}),

Remark 3.10
.

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. Then we get a Lie group structure
(G,\cdot,e_{\Phi})
equipped with the product

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. Let
x
be a section of
\Phi^{!}TG
and
X
be the corresponding section of

. Then

is left-invariant if and only if

(

)

If

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. Let us denote by

in the pullback bundle

\displaystyle[x(e_{\Phi}),y(e_{\Phi})]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}=[x,y]_{\Phi}(e_{\Phi}),

Lemma 3.12

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. If

By (

) and (

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\Phi}(f)=x\big(y(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)\circ\Phi^{-1}-y\big(x(f\circ\Phi)\circ\Phi^{-1}\big)\circ\Phi^{-1}

At the end of this subsection, we show that every regular Hom-Lie algebra is integrable.

Definition 3.13
.

A Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
is called simply connected Hom-Lie group if the underlying manifold

is a simply connected topological space.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a regular Hom-Lie algebra. Then there exists a unique simply connected Hom-Lie group

and
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}=\Phi_{*e_{\Phi}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}

For the Lie algebra

given in Lemma

, it is easy to see that
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Lie algebra isomorphism of

.

We have a unique simply connected Lie group
(G,\cdot)
such that

is the Lie algebra of
(G,\cdot)
. Since
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Lie algebra isomorphism of

and

of the Lie group

such that
\Phi_{*e}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}

is a Hom-Lie group. Finally, by Lemma

[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}=\Phi_{*e}[x,y]_{\rm Lie}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}[x,y]_{\rm Lie}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}(x)=\Phi_{*e}(x)=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}^{!},

∎

3.3
One-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group and
\big(\mathfrak{g}^{!},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}\big)
be its Hom-Lie algebra. Then we define one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups of
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
and prove that there is a one-to-one correspondence between elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{!}
and one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups of
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
.

Definition 3.15
.

A weak homomorphism of Hom-Lie groups

\sigma^{!}\colon\ (R,+,0,{\rm Id})\rightarrow(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)

is called a one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group. Then
\sigma^{!}\colon(R,+,0,{\rm Id})\rightarrow(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
is a one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
if and only if there exists a unique
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}
such that
\sigma^{!}(t)=\Phi(\exp(tx)),
where
\exp
is the exponential map of the Lie group
(G,\cdot,e_{\Phi})
.

Proof.

For all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}
, we have

\Phi\big(\sigma^{!}(t+s)\big)=\Phi\big(\Phi\big(\exp\big((t+s)x\big)\big)\big)=\Phi(\exp(tx)\diamond\exp(sx))=\sigma^{!}(t)\diamond\sigma^{!}(s),

which implies that
\sigma^{!}(t)=\Phi\big(\exp(tx)\big)
is a one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
. For different
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}
, we get a different one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup.

Now, let us assume that
\sigma^{!}\colon(R,+,0,{\rm Id})\rightarrow(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
is a one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
. Then, for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\Phi^{-1}\big(\sigma^{!}(t+s)\big)=\Phi^{-2}\big(\sigma^{!}(t)\diamond\sigma^{!}(s)\big)=\Phi^{-1}\big(\sigma^{!}(t)\cdot\sigma^{!}(s)\big)=\Phi^{-1}\big(\sigma^{!}(t)\big)\cdot\Phi^{-1}\big(\sigma^{!}(s)\big),

which implies that
\Phi^{-1}\big(\sigma^{!}(t)\big)
is a one-parameter Lie subgroup of the Lie group
(G,\cdot,e_{\Phi})
(defined in Remark
3.10
). Thus there exists a unique
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}
, such that
\sigma^{!}(t)=\Phi(\exp(tx))
. The proof is finished.
∎

By Theorem
3.16
, one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroups of Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
are in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathfrak{g}^{!}
. We denote by
\sigma^{!}_{x}(t)
the one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond,e_{\Phi},\Phi)
, which corresponds with
x
.

Example 3.18

Proposition 3.19
.

A map

Let us assume that

(3.12)

which implies that

Conversely, let

be a Lie group homomorphism. Then, by (
3.12
), we deduce that

is a weak homomorphism from
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
to
(H,\diamond_{H},e_{\Psi},\Psi)
.

4
Actions of Hom-Lie groups and Hom-Lie algebras

Let
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group and
M
be a smooth manifold. Let
\theta\colon G\times M\rightarrow M
be a smooth map that we denote by

\theta(a,x)=a\odot x,\qquad\forall\,a\in G,x\in M.

Definition 4.1
.

The map
\theta\colon G\times M\rightarrow M
is called an
action
of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
on the smooth manifold
M
with respect to a map
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

e\odot x=\iota(x)
,
\forall\,x\in M
;

(ii)

(a\diamond_{G}b)\odot x=\Phi(a)\odot\big(\iota^{-1}(\Phi(b)\odot x)\big)
,
\forall\,a,b\in G
,
x\in M
.

We denote this action by
(G,\theta,M,\iota)
.

For all
a\in G
, define
L_{a}\colon M\rightarrow M
by

L_{a}(x)=a\odot x=\theta(a,x),\qquad\forall\,x\in M.

Since
L_{e_{\Phi}}=\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
and
L_{\Phi^{-1}(a\diamond a^{-1})}=L_{a}\circ\iota^{-1}\circ L_{a^{-1}}=\iota
, we have
L_{a}\circ\iota^{-1}\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
, and thus
L_{a}\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M).
Let us define a map
L\colon G\rightarrow{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
by
L(a)=L_{a}
for all
a\in G
.

Theorem 4.2
.

With the above notations, the map
\theta\colon G\times M\rightarrow M
is an action of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
on
M
with respect to
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
if and only if the map
L\colon G\rightarrow{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
is a weak homomorphism from the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
to the Hom-Lie group
({\mathsf{Diff}}(M),\diamond,\iota,\mathsf{Ad}_{\iota}).

Proof.

Let us first assume that the map
\theta\colon G\times M\rightarrow{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
is an action of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
on
M
with respect to the map
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
. Then we have

L(e_{\Phi})=L_{e_{\Phi}}=\iota,\qquad L(a\diamond b)(x)=\theta(a\diamond b,x)=L(\Phi(a))\circ\iota^{-1}\circ L(\Phi(b))(x).

Thus, we have

\mathsf{Ad}_{\iota}\circ L(a\diamond b)=L(\Phi(a))\diamond L(\Phi(b)),

which implies that the map
L\colon(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)\rightarrow({\mathsf{Diff}}(M),\diamond,\iota,\mathsf{Ad}_{\iota})
is a weak homomorphism of Hom-Lie groups.

Conversely, let us assume that
L\colon(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)\rightarrow({\mathsf{Diff}}(M),\diamond,\iota,\mathsf{Ad}_{\iota})
is a weak homomorphism of Hom-Lie groups. Then, it follows that

\theta(e_{\Phi},x)=L(e_{\Phi})(x)=\iota(x),\qquad\forall\,x\in M,

(4.1)

and

\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\iota}\circ L(a\diamond b)\big)(x)=\iota\circ L(\Phi(a))\circ\iota^{-1}\circ L(\Phi(b))\circ\iota^{-1}(x)=\big(\iota\circ L(a\diamond b)\circ\iota^{-1}\big)(x),

which implies that

L(a\diamond b)(x)=L(\Phi(a))\circ\iota^{-1}\circ L(\Phi(b))(x).

Therefore, we get the following identity:

\theta(a\diamond b,x)=\Phi(a)\odot\big(\iota^{-1}(\Phi(b)\odot x)\big).

(4.2)

By (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we deduce that
\theta\colon G\times M\rightarrow M
is an action of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
on the smooth manifold
M
with respect to
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
.
∎

If
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
is a Hom-Lie group, then let us define a map
\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}\colon G\times G\rightarrow G
by

\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(a,b)=\Phi^{-1}(a\diamond_{G}b)\diamond_{G}a^{-1},\qquad\forall\,a,b\in G.

Lemma 4.3
.

The map
\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}\colon G\times G\rightarrow G
gives an action of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
on
G
with respect to the map
\Phi\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(G)
.

Proof.

For all
x\in G
, we have

\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(e_{\Phi},x)=\Phi^{-1}(e_{\Phi}\diamond_{G}x)\diamond_{G}e^{-1}_{\Phi}=\Phi(x),

and

\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(a\diamond_{G}b,x)=\Phi^{-1}\big((a\diamond_{G}b)\diamond_{G}x\big)\diamond_{G}(a\diamond_{G}b)^{-1},\qquad\forall\,a,b\in G.

Let us denote
a\odot b:=\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(a,b)
, then we get the following expression:

\displaystyle\Phi(a)\odot\big(\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(b)\odot x)\big)

\displaystyle=\Phi(a)\odot\big(\Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(b)\diamond_{G}x)\diamond_{G}(\Phi(b))^{-1}\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\Phi(a)\odot\big(\big(\Phi^{-1}(b)\diamond_{G}\Phi^{-2}(x)\big)\diamond_{G}b^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi(a)\diamond_{G}\big(\big(\Phi^{-1}(b)\diamond_{G}\Phi^{-2}(x)\big)\diamond_{G}b^{-1}\big)\diamond_{G}(\Phi(a))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\big(a\diamond_{G}\big(\Phi^{-1}(b)\diamond_{G}\big(\Phi^{-3}(x)\diamond_{G}\big(\Phi^{-2}(b)\big)^{-1}\big)\big)\big)\diamond_{G}(\Phi(a))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\big(\Phi^{-1}(a\diamond_{G}b)\diamond_{G}\big(\Phi^{-2}(x)\diamond_{G}\big(\Phi^{-1}(b)\big)^{-1}\big)\big)\diamond_{G}\Phi(a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\big(\big(\Phi^{-2}(a\diamond_{G}b)\diamond_{G}\Phi^{-2}(x)\big)\diamond_{G}b^{-1}\big)\diamond_{G}\Phi(a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-1}\big((a\diamond_{G}b)\diamond x\big)\diamond_{G}(a\diamond b)^{-1},

which implies that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(a\diamond_{G}b,x)=\Phi(a)\odot\big(\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(b)\odot x)\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(a\diamond_G b,x)}{}=\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}\big(\Phi(a),\big(\Phi^{-1}\big(\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(\Phi(b),x)\big)\big)\big),\qquad\forall\,a,b,x\in G.

Thus, the map
\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}\colon G\times G\rightarrow G
gives an action of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
on the underlying manifold
G
with respect to the map
\Phi\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(G)
.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

Let

V

\rho\colon\ (G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)\rightarrow({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})

\beta\in{\rm GL}(V)
.

Let
\big(\mathfrak{g}^{!},[\cdot,\cdot]_{!},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}\big)

(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)

4.3
,
\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}
gives an action of the Hom-Lie group
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)

G
with respect to the map
\Phi
. Now, let us denote
\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}_{a}:=\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}(a,\cdot)

\displaystyle=\big(\widetilde{\mathsf{Ad}}_{a\diamond_{G}b}\big)_{\triangleright}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi^{-1}((a\diamond_{G}b)\diamond_{G}\Phi(\exp(tx)))\diamond_{G}(a\diamond_{G}b)^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{Ad}}(\Phi(a))\circ\Phi^{-1}_{*}\circ\widehat{\mathsf{Ad}}(\Phi(b))(x)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\widehat{\mathsf{Ad}}(\Phi(a))\Phi^{-1}_{*}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\Phi^{-1}\big(\big(\Phi^{-1}(\Phi(b)\diamond_{G}\Phi(\exp(tx)))\diamond_{G}(\Phi(b))^{-1}\big)\big)

From Lemma

, every Hom-Lie group

Let

\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M
given by

\gamma(t)=\Phi(\exp(tx))\odot m,\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!},m\in M.

Then we get a section of the pullback bundle
\iota^{*}TM
as follows:

\widetilde{x}(m)=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\bigg|_{t=0}\gamma(t),\qquad\forall\,m\in M.

Thus, we have a map
\kappa\colon\mathfrak{g}^{!}\rightarrow\Gamma(\iota^{*}TM)
given by
\kappa(x)=\widetilde{x}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}
. We denote
\kappa(x)(m)
simply by
x\bullet m
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}
,
m\in M
.

Definition 4.6
.

The map
\kappa\colon\mathfrak{g}^{!}\rightarrow\Gamma(\iota^{*}TM)
is called the infinitesimal action of the Hom-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{!}
on the smooth manifold
M
with respect to the map
\iota\in{\mathsf{Diff}}(M)
.

By Lemma
4.5
and Theorem
3.21
, the map

(\widehat{\mathsf{Ad}})_{\triangleright}\colon\ \big(\mathfrak{g}^{!},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}\big)\rightarrow\big(\mathfrak{gl}\big(\mathfrak{g}^{!}\big),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}}\big)

is a weak homomorphism of Hom-Lie algebras. To simplify the notations, we denote
\widehat{\mathsf{ad}}:=\big(\widehat{\mathsf{Ad}}\big)_{\triangleright}
, then we have the following result.

Theorem 4.7
.

With the above notations, we have

\big(\widehat{\mathsf{ad}}(x)\big)\bullet y=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}.

Proof.

By the definition of the infinitesimal action of Hom-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{!}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(\widehat{\mathsf{ad}}(x)\big)\bullet y

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\big(\Phi(\exp(tx))\odot y\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi(\exp(tx))\diamond_{G}\Phi(\exp(sy))\big)\diamond_{G}(\Phi(\exp(tx)))^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\Phi(\exp(tx))\cdot\Phi(\exp(sy))\cdot(\Phi(\exp(tx)))^{-1}=\Phi_{*e_{\Phi}}[x,y],

which implies that
(\widehat{\mathsf{ad}}(x))\bullet y=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}^{!}}
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(G,\diamond_{G},e_{\Phi},\Phi)
be a Hom-Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}^{!}
be the Hom-Lie algebra of
G
. Then, we have

\widehat{\mathsf{Ad}}({\mathsf{Hexp}}(x))={\mathsf{Hexp}}\big(\widehat{\mathsf{ad}}(x)\big),\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{g}^{!}.

Proof.

The proof of the proposition follows from Theorem
3.22
and Lemma
4.5
.
∎

5
Integration of the Hom-Lie algebra
\boldsymbol{(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})}

Let
V
be a vector space and
\beta\in{\rm GL}(V)
. Let us define a map
\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\colon\mathfrak{gl}(V)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
by

\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{A}=\beta+\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}+\frac{1}{2!}\beta\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}\circ A\circ\beta^{-1}+\cdots,\qquad\forall\,A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V).

Let us denote by
e\colon\mathfrak{gl}(V)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
, the usual exponential map. Then the map
\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\colon\mathfrak{gl}(V)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
can be written as follows:

\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{A}=\beta\circ e^{A\circ\beta^{-1}},\qquad\forall\,A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V).

(5.1)

The map
\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\colon\mathfrak{gl}(V)\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
gives rise to a one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
. More precisely, for any
A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, let us define a map

\sigma_{A}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow{\rm GL}(V)
by

\sigma_{A}(t)=\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{tA},\qquad\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

With the above notations,
\sigma_{A}\colon(\mathbb{R},+,0,{\rm Id})\rightarrow({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
is a weak homomorphism of Hom-Lie groups, i.e.,

\sigma_{A}(t+s)=\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\sigma_{A}(t)\big)\diamond\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\sigma_{A}(s)\big),\qquad\forall\,t,s\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

By (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}(t+s)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{(t+s)A}=\beta\circ e^{(t+s)(A\beta^{-1})}=\beta\circ\big(e^{tA\circ\beta^{-1}}e^{sA\circ\beta^{-1}}\big)=\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{tA}\circ\beta^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{sA}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{sA}\diamond\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{tA}=\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\sigma_{A}(t)\big)\diamond\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\sigma_{A}(s)\big),

for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
.∎

Thus, for any
A\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, the map
\sigma_{A}\colon(\mathbb{R},+,0,{\rm Id})\rightarrow({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
is a one-parameter Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
({\rm GL}(V),\diamond,\beta,\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let us consider the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{gl}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta})
. Then the Hom-Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle[A,B]_{\beta}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Big|_{t=0,s=0}\big(\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-3}}\big(\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{sA}\diamond\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{tB}\big)\big)\diamond\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-2}}\big(\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{-sA}\big)\big)\diamond\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\big(\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{-tB}\big).

(5.2)

Proof.

By (
5.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Big|_{t=0,s=0}\big(\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-3}}\big(\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{sA}\diamond\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{tB}\big)\big)\diamond\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-2}}\big(\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{-sA}\big)\big)\diamond\mathsf{Ad}_{\beta^{-1}}\big(\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}^{-tB}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Big|_{t=0,s=0}\big(\beta^{-2}\big(\beta^{3}e^{sA\beta^{-1}}e^{tB\beta^{-1}}e^{-sA\beta^{-1}}e^{-tB\beta^{-1}}\beta^{-2}\big)\beta^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}s}\Big|_{t=0,s=0}\big(\beta e^{sA\beta^{-1}}e^{tB\beta^{-1}}e^{-sA\beta^{-1}}e^{-tB\beta^{-1}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\beta A\beta^{-1}B\beta^{-1}-\beta B\beta^{-1}A\beta^{-1}=[A,B]_{\beta},

which implies that (
5.2
) hold.
∎

6
Integration of the derivation Hom-Lie algebra

Let the triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Hom-Lie algebra. In this section, we consider the derivation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
and show that it is the Hom-Lie algebra of the Hom-Lie group of automorphisms of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

Let us recall from Definition
2.6
, the space of derivations of a Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
that is denoted by
\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Hom-Lie algebra. Then
\big(\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
is a Hom-Lie algebra, which is a subalgebra of the Hom-Lie algebra
\big(\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
given by Proposition
2.8
.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward verification, and we leave the details to readers.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

A linear map
D\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
if and only if the map
D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
given in Lemma
2.2
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
D\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle D[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}D\big)(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\big[\big(\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}D\big)(x),\phi(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

We need to show that
D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
. Equivalently, we need to show the following identity:

D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}[x,y]_{\rm Lie}=\big[D\big(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x)\big),y\big]_{\rm Lie}+\big[x,D\big(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y)\big)\big]_{\rm Lie},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

(6.1)

The left hand side of (
6.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}[x,y]_{\rm Lie}

\displaystyle=D\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-2}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-2}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},

and the right hand side of (
6.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\big[D(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x)),y\big]_{\rm Lie}+\big[x,D(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y))\big]_{\rm Lie}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Thus, it implies that (
6.1
) holds, i.e.,
D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
.

Conversely, if
D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
, then it easily follows that
D
is a derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

Definition 6.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Hom-Lie algebra. An
automorphism
of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a map
\theta\in{\rm GL}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

\theta[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\theta(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\theta(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},\qquad\forall\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

We denote the set of automorphisms of a Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
by
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
. In fact, there is a Hom-Lie group structure on the set
\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Hom-Lie algebra. Then the tuple
\big(\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}),\diamond,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
is a Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
\big({\rm GL}(\mathfrak{g}),\diamond,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
.

Proof.

First, we show that the structure map
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
is an automorphism of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
. It follows from the following expression:

\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Now, let
\theta\in\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
, then we have

\theta\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\theta^{-1}(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\theta^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},

which implies that
\theta^{-1}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\theta^{-1}(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\theta^{-1}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}
for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus
\theta^{-1}\in\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\diamond\theta_{2}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\theta_{1}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ\theta_{2}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\theta_{1}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ\theta_{2}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ\theta_{2}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\big[\theta_{1}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ\theta_{2}(x),\theta_{1}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\circ\theta_{2}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}(\theta_{1}\diamond\theta_{2})(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}(\theta_{1}\diamond\theta_{2})(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},

for all
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
, and
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore,
\theta_{1}\diamond\theta_{2}\in\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}).
Finally, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\theta[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\theta\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[\theta(x),\theta(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}(\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\theta)(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}(\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\theta)(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}},

which implies that
\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\theta\in\mathsf{Aut}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Hence,
\big(\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}),\diamond,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
is a Hom-Lie subgroup of the Hom-Lie group
\big({\rm GL}(\mathfrak{g}),\diamond,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

With the above notations, the triple
\big(\mathsf{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}},Ad_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
is the Hom-Lie algebra of the Hom-Lie group
\big(\mathsf{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}),\diamond,\phi_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}}\big)
.

Proof.

Let us first assume that
D
is a derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
. By (
3.11
), we have
{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}
. Then we prove that
{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)
is an automorphism of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
. For all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we get the following equation:

{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]_{\rm Lie}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]_{\rm Lie}.

(6.2)

By Proposition
6.2
, the map
D\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation of Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
. This, in turn, implies that
e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}
is an automorphism of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
. Thus,

\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]_{\rm Lie}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\big[e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\rm Lie}.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\big[e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\rm Lie}

\displaystyle=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\big[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\big[e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

(6.3)

i.e.,

\big[e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Therefore,

{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\big[\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)(x),\mathsf{Ad}_{\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}{\mathsf{Hexp}}(tD)(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}.

(6.4)

Conversely, we show that if
D\colon\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map and it satisfies (
6.4
), then
D
is a derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Note that (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}[\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)]_{\rm Lie}=\big[e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\big[e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x),e^{tD\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}}\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(y)\big]_{\rm Lie}

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore,
D\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{-1}
is a derivation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rm Lie})
. Subsequently, from Proposition
6.2
, it follows that
D
is a derivation of the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi_{\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

Consider the Hom-Lie algebra
(_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2},[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
given in
[
12
, Example 1]
, where

[e,f]=\frac{1+q}{2}h,\qquad[h,e]=2e,\qquad[h,f]=-2qf

and

\alpha(e)=\frac{q^{-1}+1}{2}e,\qquad\alpha(h)=h,\qquad\alpha(f)=\frac{q+1}{2}f.

Here
\{e,f,h\}
is the basis of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. It is straightforward to deduce that

\alpha([e,f])=\frac{q+1}{2}h,\qquad[\alpha(e),\alpha(f)]=\frac{q^{-1}+1}{2}\frac{q+1}{2}\frac{q+1}{2}h,

which implies that
\alpha
does not preserve
[\cdot,\cdot]
, i.e.,
\alpha
is not an algebraic morphism. So the Hom-Lie algebra
{}_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
is not a multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra. Thus the integration-differentiation approach developed in this paper does not apply to this concrete example. We will study the integration of this more general case in the future.

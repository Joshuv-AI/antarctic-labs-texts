Frame-related sequences in chains and scales of Hilbert spaces
Peter Balazs, Giorgia Bellomonte, Hessam Hosseinnezhad

Abstract
. Frames for Hilbert spaces are interesting for mathematicians but also important for applications e.g. in signal analysis and in physics. Both in mathematics and physics it is natural to consider a full scale of spaces, and not only a single one. In this paper, we study how certain frame-related properties, as completeness or the property of being a (semi-)frame, of a certain sequence in one of the spaces propagate to other spaces in a scale. We link that to the properties of the respective frame-related operators, like analysis or synthesis. We start with a detailed survey of the theory of Hilbert chains. Using a canonical isomorphism the properties of frame sequences are naturally preserved between different spaces. We also show that some results can be transferred if the original sequence is considered, in particular that the upper semi-frame property is kept in larger spaces, while the lower one to smaller ones. This leads to a negative result: a sequence can never be a frame for two Hilbert spaces of the scale if the scale is non-trivial, i.e. spaces are not equal.

•

The

It is known

that we have for

where, as usual,

indicates the adjoint of the operator

.
Other two operators associated to a sequence

are the
frame operator

1

1

of

where

and the

with

where

{\sf Dom}(G_{\psi})=\{{c}\in\ell^{2}:\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(G_{\psi})_{k,l}c_{l}\mbox{ converges }\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and is in }\ell^{2}\}

. The pair

2.2.
Rigged Hilbert spaces

In a formulation using a topology viewpoint, where not all involved spaces have to be normed, see e.g.
[
15
,
44
]
, a
rigged Hilbert space
(RHS) consists of a triplet
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D}^{\times})
where
\mathcal{D}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}
endowed with
a locally convex topology
t
, finer than that induced by the Hilbert norm of
\mathcal{H}
,
and
\mathcal{D}^{\times}
is the conjugate dual of
\mathcal{D}[{t}]
, endowed with the
strong topology
{t}^{\times}:=\beta(\mathcal{D}^{\times},\mathcal{D})
. We have

\mathcal{D}[t]\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\times}[t^{\times}],

where
\hookrightarrow
denotes a continuous
embedding and since
\mathcal{D}^{\times}
contains a subspace that can be identified with
\mathcal{H}
, we will read (
2.2
) as a chain of topological inclusions:
\mathcal{D}[t]\subset\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\times}[t^{\times}]
. These identifications
imply that the sesquilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
that puts
\mathcal{D}

and
\mathcal{D}^{\times}
in duality is an extension of the inner product of

\mathcal{D}
:
B(f,g)=\left\langle{f}\left|{g}\right.\right\rangle
, for every
f,g\in\mathcal{D}
(as usual, to simplify notations we adopt the symbol
\left\langle{\cdot}\left|{\cdot}\right.\right\rangle
for both of
them).

Let now
\mathcal{D}[t]\subset\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\times}[t^{\times}]
be a rigged
Hilbert space,
and let
{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\times})
denote the vector space of all continuous linear maps from
\mathcal{D}[t]
into
\mathcal{D}^{\times}[t^{\times}]
. If
\mathcal{D}[t]
is barreled (e.g. reflexive), an involution
X\mapsto X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}
can be introduced in
{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\times})
by the equality

(2)

\left\langle{X^{\mbox{\scriptsize${\dagger}$}}\eta}\left|{\xi}\right.\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle{X\xi}\left|{\eta}\right.\right\rangle},\quad\forall\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{D}

and
{\mathfrak{L}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}^{\times})
results to be a
†
-invariant vector space.

We will show in Section
3.3.1
how this can be expressed and extended in a Hilbert space setting.

2.3.
Scales of Hilbert Spaces

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be a Hilbert space with inner product
\left<.,.\right>_{0}
.
Let
A
be a self-adjoint, strictly positive, unbounded operator with domain
{\sf Dom}(A)\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}
and range
{\sf Ran}(A)\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}
, without loss of generality let
A=A^{*}\geq 1
.
By assumption, this operator has dense domain, dense range and is closed.
It has closed range, and is therefore onto.
Its inverse
A^{-1}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow{\sf Dom}(A)
is bounded and self-adjoint.

The same argument holds for any
A^{k}
and
A^{-k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Define, for each
k\geq 0
, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{+k}=\left\{{\sf Dom}(A^{k/2}),\|\cdot\|_{+k}\right\}
i.e. the domain of
A^{k/2}
equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{+k}
induced by the inner product

\left\langle{x}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{+k}:=\left\langle{A^{k/2}x}\left|{A^{k/2}y}\right.\right\rangle_{0},x,y\in{\sf Dom}(A^{k/2})
.
Therefore
A^{k}:\mathcal{H}_{+2k}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
is bounded, even unitary.

Define
\mathcal{H}_{-k}
as the completion of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
with respect to the
norm
\|\cdot\|_{-k}
induced by the inner product

\left\langle{f}\left|{g}\right.\right\rangle_{-k}:=\left\langle{A^{-{k/2}}f}\left|{A^{-{k/2}}g}\right.\right\rangle_{0},f,g\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset(\mathcal{H}_{+k})^{\prime}
densely (where we apply the Riesz isomorphism for
\mathcal{H}_{0}\cong(\mathcal{H}_{0})^{\prime}
).
We have for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}

\|f\|_{\left(\mathcal{H}_{+k}\right)^{\prime}}=\sup\limits_{\|g\|_{+k}=1}|\left<f,g\right>_{0}|=\sup\limits_{\|A^{k/2}g\|_{0}=1}\left|\left<f,g\right>_{0}\right|=

=\sup\limits_{\|h\|_{0}=1}|\left<f,A^{-k/2}h\right>_{0}|=\sup\limits_{\|h\|_{0}=1}|\left<A^{-k/2}f,h\right>_{0}|=\|A^{-k/2}f\|_{0}.

and so the norms are equivalent.
Therefore,
(\mathcal{H}_{+k})^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}_{-k}
.
For each fixed
k>0
the triplet

\mathcal{H}_{+k}\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}_{-k}

is a rigged Hilbert space with dense inclusions.
The chain of Hilbert spaces infinite in both sides

...\subset\mathcal{H}_{+k}\subset...\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset...\subset\mathcal{H}_{-k}\subset...

is called
A
-
scale of Hilbert spaces

[
38
]
.

An important property of an
A
-scale
is the invariance of the structure of rigged triple
\mathcal{H}_{+k}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{-k}

under shift along the
A
-scale, i.e. shift of the index
k
, this property has been called the
first invariance principle
of the
A
-scale
[
38
]
:
for any fixed
k>0
and an arbitrary
n
, the triple of spaces
\mathcal{H}_{n+k}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{n-k}

is a new rigged Hilbert space, where in particular the
Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{n-k}

is the dual of
\mathcal{H}_{n+k}
, which becomes apparent, if we do the above construction taking
\mathcal{H}_{n}
as the “central space”.

3.
Hilbert chains

In this section we include a detailed introduction to Hilbert chains, to some extent following
[
18
]
, albeit extended and reformulated.
We have done this to make the manuscript self-contained, stress results not well known in the frame theory community and adapt the respective point-of-view. We have added details and covered new side-aspects.

3.1.
Hilbert Triplets

This section deals with canonical Hilbert triplets and how one can define operators between them.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be a separable, infinite dimensional Hilbert space with scalar product
\left\langle{\cdot}\left|{\cdot}\right.\right\rangle_{0}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{0}
.
Fix any
i\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
be a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
\mathcal{H}_{+i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0}
, complete w.r.to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{+i}
induced by the inner product
\left\langle{\cdot}\left|{\cdot}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}

with

(3)

\|x\|_{0}\leq\|x\|_{+i},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}.

Let
\iota_{+i,0}
be the inclusion of
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
into
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, which by (
3
) is bounded.
We have

(4)

\left\langle{f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{f}\left|{\iota_{+i,0}x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{\iota_{+i,0}^{*}f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{+i},\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{H}_{0},x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}.

Define

(5)

\iota_{0,+i}:=\iota_{+i,0}^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{+i},

where we consider the Hilbert space adjoints, i.e. apply the Riesz isomorphisms for both Hilbert spaces.

We have
\|\iota_{+i,0}\|\leq 1
and
\iota_{+i,0}
is injective (one-to-one) and has dense range, but cannot be onto.
Therefore
\iota_{0,+i}:=\iota_{+i,0}^{*}
has the same properties.

On the other hand, let us note that, naturally,

\left\langle{x}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}\not=\left\langle{\iota_{+i,0}x}\left|{\iota_{+i,0}y}\right.\right\rangle_{0},\quad x,y\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}

because else (
3
) would be an equality and the two spaces
would collapse to only one space.

Let us define now the scalar product

(6)

\left\langle{f}\left|{g}\right.\right\rangle_{-i}:=\left\langle{\iota_{0,+i}f}\left|{g}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{\iota_{0,+i}f}\left|{\iota_{0,+i}g}\right.\right\rangle_{+i},\quad f,g\in\mathcal{H}_{0}

and consider the completion
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{-i}
induced by the inner product defined in (
6
). Then, from
\|\iota_{0,+i}\|\leq 1
it follows that
\|f\|_{-i}\leq\|f\|_{0}

\forall f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and we have:

\mathcal{H}_{+i}\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}_{-i},

with dense inclusions, and

\|x\|_{-i}\leq\|x\|_{0}\leq\|x\|_{+i},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}.

Since
\iota_{0,+i}
maps a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
(i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{0}
) into
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
we consider the (unique!) bounded extension
I_{-i,+i}=\overline{\iota_{0,+i}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{-i},\mathcal{H}_{+i})
.

As
\iota_{0,+i}
has dense range,
I_{-i,+i}
is onto (surjective). By (
6
),
I_{-i,+i}
is an isometry,

\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{\beta}\right.\right\rangle_{-i}=\left\langle{I_{-i,+i}\alpha}\left|{I_{-i,+i}\beta}\right.\right\rangle_{+i},\quad\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{H}_{-i}.

Therefore,
I_{-i,+i}
is a unitary operator, hence

I_{+i,-i}:=I_{-i,+i}^{*}=I_{-i,+i}^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{-i})

is a unitary operator too.
The operators
I_{-i,+i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{-i},\mathcal{H}_{+i})
and
I_{+i,-i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{-i})
are called
Berezanskii canonical isomorphisms
(see e.g.
[
38
]
). This is a particular instance of the Riesz isomorphism, if a particular duality (with the pivot space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
) is chosen.

3.2.
Duality by Pivot Spaces

Now we show that
\mathcal{H}_{0}
can be considered a pivot space of
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
in the sense that the scalar product
\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}
defines a duality relation. Consider the bilinear form
b(f,x)=\left\langle{f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}
defined on
\mathcal{H}_{0}\times\mathcal{H}_{+i}
. Extend it by continuity to the bilinear form

B_{-i,+i}:(\alpha,x)\in\mathcal{H}_{-i}\times\mathcal{H}_{+i}\to\mathbb{C}.

For
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}
we have

\displaystyle|\left\langle{f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\left\langle{\iota_{0,+i}f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}|=|\left\langle{I_{-i,+i}f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|I_{-i,+i}f\|_{+i}\|x\|_{+i}=\|f\|_{-i}\|x\|_{+i}

By a limit argument we obtain

(7)

|B_{-i,+i}(\alpha,x)|\leq\|\alpha\|_{-i}\|x\|_{+i},

for
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{-i},x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}
. Therefore
B_{-i,+i}
is continuous.

Remark 3.1
.

The form
B_{-i,+i}(\cdot,\cdot)
that puts
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
in duality is an extension of the inner product
\left\langle{\cdot}\left|{\cdot}\right.\right\rangle_{0}
and we will use the latter symbol for both of them.

By a limit argument (and the conjugate symmetry of any inner product), we obtain for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{H}_{-i},x,y\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}
:

(8)

\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{I_{-i,+i}\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}\text{ and }\left\langle{x}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}=\left\langle{I_{+i,-i}x}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{0}

and

\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{\beta}\right.\right\rangle_{-i}=\left\langle{I_{-i,+i}\alpha}\left|{\beta}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{I_{-i,+i}\beta}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{I_{-i,+i}\alpha}\left|{I_{-i,+i}\beta}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}.

By Remark
3.1
we see that
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{-i}
is in
(\mathcal{H}_{+i})^{\prime}
with the same norm. On the other hand, any functional
L
on
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
can be represented by a
y\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}
, i.e.

L(x)=\left\langle{y}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}=\left\langle{I_{+i,-i}\,y}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}.

As
I_{+i,-i}
is an isometry we have shown:

Remark 3.2
.

This construction corresponds to considering the dual pair
(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{0})
using
\left\langle{\cdot}\left|{\cdot}\right.\right\rangle_{0}
and choosing
\mathcal{H}_{-i}=\overline{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{\sigma(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{0})}
using the weak dual topology
[
25
]
.
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
is therefore a representation of the dual space of
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
.

Note that this is not a pure abstract baublery, but important for concrete spaces. While it is true that “everything is isomorphic anyway”, this isomorphisms
cannot
be thought of as equality if concrete choices for
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
are worked with, for example in the setting of Sobolev spaces
[
36
]
.
See
[
8
]
for an application of this topology to the more general setting of coorbit spaces of localized frames.

Remark 3.3
.

One might expect that there is a clear link of
I_{+i,-i}
to the inclusion
\iota_{+i,-i}
. But clearly, the relation cannot be trivial, e.g. an extension, as the former operator is bijective, the latter is injective and has dense range and both are bounded on all of
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
.

At the same time, it could be interesting to look at the role of the inclusion of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
into
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
, i.e.
\iota_{0,-i}
in this setting.
But again, it cannot be trivially linked to
I_{-i,+i}
, as e.g.
\iota_{0,-i}
cannot be
\overline{{I_{-i,+i}}_{|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}}}
because else it would be onto. For more on that, see Remark
3.7
.

3.3.
Hilbert Chains

Now let us add a step more. Consider a dense subspace
\mathcal{H}_{+j}
of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{+i}
,
with
j>i
(
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
), complete w.r. to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{+j}
, induced on
\mathcal{H}_{+j}
by the inner product
\left\langle{\cdot}\left|{\cdot}\right.\right\rangle_{+j}
,
such that
\|x\|_{+i}\leq\|x\|_{+j}
with

. Then
\mathcal{H}_{+j}
is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, too, and
\|x\|_{0}\leq\|x\|_{+j}
with

. If we consider the completion
\mathcal{H}_{-j}
of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{-j}
defined like in (

and

, defined like in (

x\in\mathcal{H}_{+j}
, and every
j>i
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
:

\mathcal{H}_{+j}\subset\mathcal{H}_{+i}\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}_{-i}\subset\mathcal{H}_{-j},

where every inclusion is dense.
Indeed, let us prove that
\mathcal{H}_{-i}\subset\mathcal{H}_{-j}
with
j>i
. For
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
we have from (
6
) and (

\|f\|_{-j}^{2}=\left\langle{\iota_{0,+j}f}\left|{f}\right.\right\rangle_{0}\leq\|\iota_{0,+j}f\|_{+i}\|f\|_{-i}=\|I_{-j,+j}f\|_{+i}\|f\|_{-i}\leq

\leq\|I_{-j,+j}f\|_{+j}\|f\|_{-i}=\|f\|_{-j}\|f\|_{-i},

for every
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Because
\mathcal{H}_{-i}\subset\mathcal{H}_{-j}
are the closures of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
with respect to those norms we have the dense inclusions and

\|x\|_{-j}\leq\|x\|_{-i}\leq\|x\|_{0}\leq\|x\|_{+i}\leq\|x\|_{+j},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{+j}.

Since we have
\iota_{+j,0}=\iota_{+i,0}\iota_{+j,+i}
we also have
\iota_{0,+j}=\iota_{+i,+j}\iota_{0,+i}
.
Again note that (in the non-trivial case) we have that
\iota_{-i,-j}I_{+i,-i}\iota_{+j,+i}\not=I_{+j,-j}
.
But, naturally, we have
\iota_{k,i}={\iota_{j,i}}_{{}_{|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}}}}
for
k\geq j\geq i
.

Note that we can change the role of the central pivot space:
for every
r<p
, let

(9)

\iota_{r,p}:\mathcal{H}_{r}\to\mathcal{H}_{p}

be the adjoint of the inclusion of the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}_{p}
into the space
\mathcal{H}_{r}
. As it is known

(10)

\|x\|_{r}\leq\|x\|_{p},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{p}.

Consider the bilinear form

B_{r,p}:(f,x)\in\mathcal{H}_{r}\times\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\left\langle{f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{r}\in\mathbb{C},

then by (
10
) it is continuous for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
and for
x\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
, hence it can be represented as a scalar product both in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
and in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
:

\left\langle{f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{r}=B_{r,p}(f,x)=\left\langle{\iota_{r,p}f}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{p},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}_{r},x\in\mathcal{H}_{p}

where
\iota_{r,p}:\mathcal{H}_{r}\to\mathcal{H}_{p}
is a bounded operator in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{r},\mathcal{H}_{p})
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
\{\mathcal{H}_{r};{r}\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be the family of the Hilbert spaces of a Hilbert chain.
For every
r,p\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
p\geq{r}
the maps
\iota_{r,p}:\mathcal{H}_{r}\to\mathcal{H}_{p}
are injective, such that
\|\iota_{r,p}x\|_{p}\leq\|x\|_{r}
,
\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
,
\iota_{r,r}
is the identity of
\mathcal{H}_{r}
and
\iota_{r,n}=\iota_{p,n}\iota_{r,p}
,
{r}\leq p\leq n
. Hence, the family
\{\mathcal{H}_{r},\iota_{r,p},r,p\in\mathbb{Z},p\geq r\}
is a directed contractive system of Hilbert spaces, see
[
16
]
. It produces two spaces
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\times}
in conjugate duality: the latter is obtained as the inductive limit of the system, whereas the former is proved to be the projective limit of the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{p}
’s, with respect to the adjoint maps of the
\iota_{r,p}
’s.

We will use this in the
A
-scale framework and extend the notion of Berezanskii canonical isomorphism for each ordered pair of spaces
\mathcal{H}_{k}
and
\mathcal{H}_{l}
, both
k,l\neq 0
by defining unitary operators as
I_{k,l}:\mathcal{H}_{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{l}

by means of fractional powers of the operator
A
, its closure and their inverses.

3.3.1.
Different Adjoints

Before going forth, we give some thoughts about the adjoints, as we consider different spaces here, and “the adjoint” depends on which spaces are considered.

Consider a chain of Hilbert spaces as before. Fix any
j,i\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.
For
A:\mathcal{H}_{+i}\to\mathcal{H}_{+j}
we have already indicated by
A^{*}
the Hilbertian adjoint
A^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{+j}\to\mathcal{H}_{+i}
, i.e.

\left\langle{Ax}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{+j}=\left\langle{x}\left|{A^{*}y}\right.\right\rangle_{+i},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i},\forall y\in\mathcal{H}_{+j}.

This adjoint is not always useful, as two Riesz isomorphisms are applied, which is not compatible with a structure where we consider Hilbert spaces included in each other, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{+j}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{+i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{-i}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{-j}
but distinguished from each other. (Note that even though all Hilbert spaces are isomorphic to each other, still it makes a lot of sense to treat them differently, e.g.
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
).
By identifying the dual of
\mathcal{H}_{-j}
with
\mathcal{H}_{+j}
the whole scale would collapse. Therefore, taking
i,j\geq 0
, we now consider another adjoint
A^{\bigstar}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{-j},\mathcal{H}_{-i})
for
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{+j})
, the “pivot adjoint” defined as follows

(11)

\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{Ax}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{A^{\bigstar}\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{-j},x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}.

Here the number in the circle indicates the subscript of the pivot Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
w.r. to which the dual is taken.
We can, very naturally, define the adjoint for any pivot space
\mathcal{H}_{p}\not=\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

The same construction is possible for
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{-j},\mathcal{H}_{-i})
,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{-j},\mathcal{H}_{+i})
or
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{-j})
.

We highlight that the notion of pivot adjoint is a generalization of the involuted
A^{\dagger}
recalled in (
2
).

Moreover, similar to (
4
), if
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{+i},\mathcal{H}_{+j})
, we have for
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}_{-j},x\in\mathcal{H}_{+i}

\displaystyle\left\langle{\alpha}\left|{Ax}\right.\right\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{I_{-j,+j}\alpha}\left|{Ax}\right.\right\rangle_{+j}=\left\langle{A^{*}I_{-j,+j}\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=\left\langle{I_{+i,-i}A^{*}I_{-j,+j}\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{A^{\bigstar}\alpha}\left|{x}\right.\right\rangle_{0}

therefore we deduce that

(12)

A^{\bigstar}=(I_{-i,+i})^{-1}A^{*}I_{-j,+j}=I_{+i,-i}A^{*}I_{-j,+j}

and

\|A^{\bigstar}\|=\|A^{*}\|=\|A\|.

Remark 3.5
.

In general, if
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{p},\mathcal{H}_{q})
, then
A^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{q},\mathcal{H}_{p})
and

(13)

A^{\bigstar}=I_{p,-p}A^{*}I_{-q,q}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{-q},\mathcal{H}_{-p}).

Remark 3.6
.

Because
A^{\bigstar}
is an adjoint, two properties follow immediately:

(1)

The double pivot adjoint of an operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{p},\mathcal{H}_{q})
,
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}

A^{\bigstar\bigstar}=(A^{\bigstar})^{\bigstar}=A\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|A^{\bigstar\bigstar}\|=\|A\|.

(2)

Let

and

,
p,m\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(AB)^{\bigstar}=B^{\bigstar}A^{\bigstar}.
Indeed, if

and

\left\langle{ABx}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{Bx}\left|{A^{\bigstar}y}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{x}\left|{B^{\bigstar}A^{\bigstar}y}\right.\right\rangle_{0}.

As a side remark to (
12

I_{+i,-i}
is a unitary operator with respect to the pair

\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Note that

A^{\bigstar}
also corresponds to the Banach space adjoint of

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\times}
.
It maps
\mathcal{H}_{-j}
into
\mathcal{H}_{-i}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Define

\iota_{0,-i}:=\iota_{+i,0}^{\bigstar}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{-i},

\iota_{0,+1}

The operator
\hat{I}^{-1}

Consider the operator

onto
\mathcal{H}_{0}

\left\langle{x}\left|{y}\right.\right\rangle_{+1}=\left\langle{Fx}\left|{Fy}\right.\right\rangle_{0}~~~(x,y\in\mathcal{H}_{+1}).

Consider

\mathcal{H}_{-1}
and

\mathbf{F}

\left\langle{f}\left|{g}\right.\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle{\mathbf{F}f}\left|{\mathbf{F}g}\right.\right\rangle_{-1}~~~(f,g\in\mathcal{H}_{0}),

and moreover

.

Let us write

From the factorization of

thus obtaining a factorization of

in terms of isometric operators.

Using the Hilbert adjoint we can write

We have the following scheme

Not every infinite chain of Hilbert spaces is a scale, see

Remark 3.10
.

We already know that
I_{+1,-1}=F^{\bigstar}F=(B^{\bigstar})^{-1}F

3.4.
Generator of a scale and shifting of the central space

Can we shift the central space? I.e. how does the dual of
\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}+n}
in respect to
\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}}
look?

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
r<p
and let
{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\times}}^{(r)}
be the dual of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
with respect to the topology of
\mathcal{H}_{r}
(i.e. consider the triplet
\mathcal{H}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{r}\subseteq{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\times}}^{(r)}
).

Then
{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\times}}^{(r)}=\mathcal{H}_{2r-p}
.

Proof.

Let us consider an
A
-scale Hilbert spaces with
A=(\hat{I}^{-1})
, with
\hat{I}
defined as in (
14
), so
by the first invariant principle of
A
-scales, see the Section
3.1
, for
k=p-r
the triple

\mathcal{H}_{r+k}=\mathcal{H}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2r-p}=\mathcal{H}_{r-k},

forms a chain of Hilbert scale.
∎

In other words: the dual of
\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}+n}
in respect to
\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}}
is
\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}-n}
.

Remark 3.12
.

Clearly, if
r<p
then the space
\mathcal{H}_{r}
can be considered the dual space of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
with respect to some space
\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}}
with
\mathcal{H}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{n_{0}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{r}
if and only if
p+r
is an even number. If this is the case,
n_{0}=\frac{p+r}{2}
.

Now, for a fixed
A
-scale, we wonder how the operator
A

changes if we fix another Hilbert space of the scale as the “central” space, for example
\mathcal{H}_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Following
[
38
]
the answer to this question is given by using what has been called

the second invariance principle of the
A-scale
. Indeed, it results that
A
is unitarily equivalent to its image under any
“shift” along the
A
-scale.

Just to fix some ideas let
k\geq 2
, consider the
A
-scale

...\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2+k}\subseteq...\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{+k}\subseteq...\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2}\subseteq...\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2-k}\subseteq...\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{-k}\subseteq...,

and the operators

A_{+k}:=I_{2+k,k}:\mathcal{H}_{2+k}\to\mathcal{H}_{+k}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad A_{-k}:=I_{2-k,-k}:\mathcal{H}_{2-k}\to\mathcal{H}_{-k},

with
I_{p,r}
defined as before (in particular

A=A_{0}=I_{+2,0}
). Then it is easy to see that
[
38
]

A_{+k}=A_{{}_{|_{\mathcal{H}_{2+k}}}}
i.e. it is the
restriction of
A
to
\mathcal{H}_{2+k}
, hence
A_{+k}
is self-adjoint in
\mathcal{H}_{2+k}
. The operator
A_{-k}
is self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}_{2-k}
and it results
A_{-k}=\overline{A}^{\|\cdot\|_{-k}}
the closure of
A
in
\mathcal{H}_{-k}
. Both operators
A_{\pm k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}

are unitary images of the original operator
A
on

\mathcal{H}_{0}
, in fact

A_{+k}=I_{0,k}AI_{2+k,2}=B^{k}F^{0}B^{0}F^{2}B^{2}F^{2+k}=B^{k}F^{2+k}

and

A_{-k}=I_{0,-k}AI_{2-k,2}=B^{-k}F^{0}B^{0}F^{2}B^{2}F^{2-k}=B^{-k}F^{2-k},

i.e. for the sake of brevity, for
p\in\mathbb{Z}

A_{p}=B^{p}F^{2+p}=I_{2+p,p}.

We remark that the operator
A
is essentially self-adjoint in each space
\mathcal{H}_{-k}
,

k\geq 1
.

4.
Frame-related properties on Hilbert scales

We will look at a scale of Hilbert space, and let’s fix:

\mathcal{H}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{r},

i.e.
r\leq p\leq m
.

We look at a sequence
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}

which has a property in some of the other spaces and investigate on how these properties “spread” to the other spaces:
if it is complete, forms a Bessel sequence, a frame, a basis, has a Riesz property, etc.. for a
\mathcal{H}_{r}
(or
\mathcal{H}_{p}
), what can we say about this sequence in the other spaces?

If we allow the sequences to be changed the results are trivial consequences of the following straightforward generalization of
[
23
, Cor. 5.3.4]
:

Corollary 4.1
.

Given two Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
, if
\psi=(\psi_{k})
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
with frame bounds
A,B
and
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
is a unitary operator, then
(U\psi_{k})
is a frame for
\mathcal{K}
with the same frame bounds.

Remark 4.2
.

If we have a unitary operator between two Hilbert spaces, all the frame properties naturally transfer from a Hilbert space to the other.

In the sequel we will use the unitary operators
I_{r,p}
defined like in Remark
3.10
, and get easy results for
\left(I_{r,p}\psi_{k}\right)
. See Corollary
4.8
.

We need some preparation before that.

4.1.
Completeness

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
. Then the following statements hold.

i)

If
(\psi_{k})
is complete in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, then it is also complete in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
for
r\leq p\leq m
.

ii)

If
(\psi_{k})
is complete in
\mathcal{H}_{r}

r\leq m
, then
(I_{r,p}\psi_{k})
is a complete sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
for any
p
.

Proof.

i)
Since
\mathcal{H}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{r}
densely, then for each
f\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
and
\epsilon>0
, there is an element
x\in\mathcal{H}_{p}
with
\|f-x\|_{r}<\epsilon/2
.
Now, let
(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
be a complete sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
. Then there exists
y\in{{\sf span}(\psi_{k})}
such that

\|x-y\|_{p}<\epsilon/2
. So

\|f-y\|_{r}\leq\|f-x\|_{r}+\|x-y\|_{r}\leq\|f-x\|_{r}+\|x-y\|_{p}<\epsilon.

It follows that
\psi=(\psi_{k})
is a complete sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
for
r\leq p\leq m
.

ii)
Trivial by Remark
4.2
.
∎

Later, in Lemma
4.12
, we will see that the converse of Lemma
4.3

(i)
is also true.

4.2.
Unbounded Frame-related operators on Hilbert chains

Let us consider an arbitrary sequence
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}{\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}}
, and as in
[
12
]
define the analysis operator
C_{\psi}^{p}:{\sf Dom}(C_{\psi}^{p})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\ell^{2}
of
(\psi_{k})
by

{\sf Dom}(C_{\psi}^{p})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{p}:\sum_{k}|\left\langle{f}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}|^{2}<\infty\right\}

C_{\psi}^{p}f:=(\left\langle{f}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}),\qquad\forall f\in{\sf Dom}(C_{\psi}^{p}).

In an analogous way we can define the synthesis
operator

D_{\psi}^{p}:{\sf Dom}(D_{\psi}^{p})\subseteq\ell^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{p}

associated with the sequence
\psi
by

{\sf Dom}(D_{\psi}^{p})=\left\{c=(c_{k})\in\ell^{2}:\sum_{k}c_{k}\psi_{k}\mbox{ converges in }\mathcal{H}_{p}\right\}

D_{\psi}^{p}c=\sum_{k}c_{k}\psi_{k},\qquad\forall c\in{\sf Dom}(D_{\psi}^{p}).

As it is known
{\sf Dom}(D_{\psi}^{p})
is dense in
\ell^{2}
, since it contains the finite sequences which form a dense subset of
\ell^{2}
and
C_{\psi}^{p}=(D_{\psi}^{p})^{*}
, hence
C_{\psi}^{p}
is closed.

Clearly, we have for
r\leq p
that

(17)

{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{p}}\subseteq{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{r}}\mbox{, \quad}{\sf Ran}{D_{\psi}^{p}}\subseteq{\sf Ran}{D_{\psi}^{r}}

\ker{D_{\psi}^{p}}\subseteq\ker{D_{\psi}^{r}}\text{ all inclusion are dense. }

Clearly
D_{\psi}^{p}\subset D_{\psi}^{r}
, since their domain are such that

{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{p}}\subseteq{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{r}}
and
D_{\psi}^{p}c=D_{\psi}^{r}c
for every
c\in{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{p}}
. Then, if we look at
D_{\psi}^{p}
as an operator into a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{r}
we can say that
C_{\psi}^{r}\subset(D_{\psi}^{p})^{*}_{r}
where
(D_{\psi}^{p})^{*}_{r}
is the adjoint of
D_{\psi}^{p}
as on operator from
\ell^{2}
into
\mathcal{H}_{r}
.

Furthermore,
let us consider
D_{\psi}^{00}
defined as operator on
c_{00}
the space of finite sequences.
By the above definition we have that
D_{\psi}^{p}
is the closure of
D_{\psi}^{00}
for any
p\geq m
. So,
D_{\psi}^{p}=\overline{D_{\psi}^{00}}^{\mathcal{H}_{p}}
. Let us now use that
r\leq p
and so
\|\cdot\|_{r}\leq\|\cdot\|_{p}
and therefore

D_{\psi}^{r}=\overline{D_{\psi}^{00}}^{\mathcal{H}_{r}}=\overline{\left(\overline{D_{\psi}^{00}}^{\mathcal{H}_{p}}\right)}^{\mathcal{H}_{r}}=\overline{D_{\psi}^{p}}^{\mathcal{H}_{r}}
.
So, in summary

D_{\psi}^{r}=\overline{D_{\psi}^{p}}^{\mathcal{H}_{r}},\quad\mbox{ for }r\leq p.

Let us also introduce the combination of those operators. Consider the “frame operator”
S^{p}_{\psi}:{\sf Dom}(S^{p}_{\psi})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathcal{H}_{p}
of
\psi

S^{p}_{\psi}f:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle{f}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}\psi_{k}

where

{\sf Dom}(S^{p}_{\psi})=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{p}:\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle{f}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}\psi_{k}\mbox{ converges in }\mathcal{H}_{p}\};

and the Gram operator

G^{p}_{\psi}:{\sf Dom}(G^{p}_{\psi})\subseteq\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2},
with

G^{p}_{\psi}(c_{k})_{k}:=\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(G^{p}_{\psi})_{k,l}c_{l}\right)_{k},

where

\displaystyle{\sf Dom}(G^{p}_{\psi})=\{(c_{k})_{k}\in\ell^{2}:

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(G^{p}_{\psi})_{k,l}c_{l}\mbox{ converges }\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(G^{p}_{\psi})_{k,l}c_{l}\right)_{k}\in\ell^{2}\}

and the Gram matrix
((G^{p}_{\psi})_{k,l})_{k,l}
is defined by
(G^{p}_{\psi})_{k,l}=\left\langle{\psi_{l}}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}
,
{k,l}\in\mathbb{N}
.

4.3.
Frame Properties of
I_{r,p}\psi

By using the unitary operator introduced in Remark
3.10
we naturally get

Lemma 4.4
.

For a given
p\in\mathbb{Z}
let

\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
C^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}=C^{p}_{\psi}I_{r,p}
and
C^{p}_{\psi}=C^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}I_{p,r}
.

Proof.

We have

\begin{split}{\sf Dom}(C^{p}_{\psi}I_{r,p})&=\{f\in{\sf Dom}(I_{r,p}):I_{r,p}f\in{\sf Dom}C^{p}_{\psi}\}\\
&=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{r}:(\langle I_{r,p}f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{p})\in\ell^{2}\}\\
&=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{r}:(\langle f,I_{p,r}\psi_{k}\rangle_{r})\in\ell^{2}\}\\
&={\sf Dom}(C^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}).\end{split}

And by the same argument the operators also are the same.

The other result is obtained by multiplying the operator
I_{p,r}
, inverse of
I_{r,p}
, on the right in the equality
C^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}=C^{p}_{\psi}I_{r,p}
.
∎

An analogue result is true for the other frame-related operators. We start with a result for the synthesis operator:

Lemma 4.5
.

For a given
p\in\mathbb{Z}
let

\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
D^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}=I_{p,r}D^{p}_{\psi}
and
D^{p}_{\psi}=I_{r,p}D^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}
.

Proof.

If
c\in{\sf Dom}(D_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}^{r})\subseteq\ell^{2}
then there exists
f\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
such that
D_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}^{r}c=f
, i.e. for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
n\geq n_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}I_{p,r}\psi_{k}-f\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|I_{r,p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}I_{p,r}\psi_{k}-I_{r,p}f\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}\psi_{k}-I_{r,p}f\right\|_{p}<\varepsilon.

Hence
D_{\psi}^{p}c=I_{r,p}f
,
{\sf Dom}(D_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}^{r})={\sf Dom}(I_{p,r}D_{\psi}^{p})
and
D_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}^{r}=I_{p,r}D_{\psi}^{p}
.
The other result is obtained by multiplying the operator
I_{r,p}
, inverse of
I_{p,r}
, on the left in the equality
D_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}^{r}=I_{p,r}D_{\psi}^{p}
.∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For a given
p\in\mathbb{Z}
let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
S^{p}_{I_{r,p}(\psi)}=I_{r,p}S^{r}_{\psi}I_{p,r}
.

Proof.

It is a consequence of the previous Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
and of
(iii)
in
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For a given
p\in\mathbb{Z}
let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{p}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then
C^{p}_{\psi}D^{p}_{\psi}\subseteq G^{p}_{\psi}
and
G^{p}_{\psi}=G^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}
for every
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By
(iv)
in
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
we have that
C^{p}_{\psi}D^{p}_{\psi}\subseteq G^{p}_{\psi}
, for every
p\leq n
.
Now, recall that
\left\langle{\psi_{l}}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}=\left\langle{I_{p,r}\psi_{l}}\left|{I_{p,r}\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{r}
, we have

\displaystyle{\sf Dom}(G^{p}_{\psi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{c\in\ell^{2}:\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle{\psi_{l}}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}c_{l}\mbox{ converges }\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and }\right.

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{k}\left|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle{\psi_{l}}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}c_{l}\right|^{2}<\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{c\in\ell^{2}:\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle{I_{p,r}\psi_{l}}\left|{I_{p,r}\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{r}c_{l}\mbox{ converges }\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and }\right.

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{k}\left|\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle{I_{p,r}\psi_{l}}\left|{I_{p,r}\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{r}c_{l}\right|^{2}<\infty\right\}={\sf Dom}(G^{r}_{I_{p,r}(\psi)}).

∎

Note that all the above results lead to statements saying that if
\psi
is a frame (Bessel sequence, Riesz basis,…) for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{r}
,
I_{r,p}\psi
is one for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
. They all are trivial consequences of Remark

.

.

For a given

If
(\psi_{k})
is a Bessel sequence in

, then
(I_{p,r}\psi_{k})
is a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
.

(2)

If
(\psi_{k})

(3)

If
(\psi_{k})

, then
(I_{p,r}\psi_{k})
is a frame in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
with the same bounds.

(4)

If
(\psi_{k})
and

(I_{p,r}\phi_{k})

(5)

If

is a dual sequence of
(\psi_{k})
in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, then
(I_{p,r}\phi_{k})
is a dual sequence of
(I_{p,r}\psi_{k})
in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
.

(6)

If
(\psi_{k})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, then
(I_{p,r}\psi_{k})

\mathcal{H}_{r}

(7)

If
(\psi_{k})

for every

an orthonormal basis of

\mathcal{H}_{r}
.

(8)

If
(\psi_{k})
is a Riesz basis of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
then
(I_{p,r}\psi_{k})
is a Riesz basis of

On the other hand, if
p<r
then
\psi
is also a sequence in

.
So, in addition to looking at

4.4.
Frame-related Operators for the Original Sequence
\psi

If we do not apply the operator we can still show

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then for every
r\leq p\leq m
,

(18)

C^{r}_{\psi}=C^{p}_{\psi}\iota_{r,p}.

where
\iota_{r,p}
is defined as in (
9
).

Proof.

We have

\left<f,\psi_{k}\right>_{r}=\left<f,\iota_{p,r}\psi_{k}\right>_{r}=\left<\iota_{r,p}f,\psi_{k}\right>_{p}

and

\begin{split}{\sf Dom}(C^{p}_{\psi}\iota_{r,p})&=\{f\in{\sf Dom}(\iota_{r,p});\iota_{r,p}f\in{\sf Dom}C^{p}_{\psi}\}\\
&=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{r};(\langle\iota_{r,p}f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{p})\in\ell^{2}\}\\
&=\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{r};(\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{r})\in\ell^{2}\}\\
&={\sf Dom}(C^{r}_{\psi}).\end{split}

∎

In consequence, we have

Lemma 4.10
.

If
\psi\subset\mathcal{H}_{m}
and
\psi
is a Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
then, for
r\leq p\leq m
,
\psi
is a Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}_{r}
with the same bound.

Proof.

By
[
23
, Cor. 3.2.4 and Theor. 3.2.3]
,

\psi
is a Bessel sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
if and only if
{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{p}}=\ell^{2}
. By (
17
)
{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{p}}\subseteq{\sf Dom}{D_{\psi}^{r}}
, this is true if and only if
\psi
is also a Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}_{r}
.

Furthermore, we have that
\|C_{\psi}^{r}\|\leq\|C_{\psi}^{p}\|
by (
18
).
∎

By
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]

\psi\subset\mathcal{H}_{m}
is an upper semi-frame for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
,
p<m
if and only if it is a total Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
. Then putting together Lemma
4.10
and Lemma
4.3

(i)
we obtain

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
and
r\leq p\leq m
. If
\psi
is an upper semi-frame for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
, then
\psi
is an upper semi-frame for
\mathcal{H}_{r}
with the same bound.

Let us now note that
\iota_{r,p}^{-1}
is a densely defined, bijective operator, which is not bounded. It is “never” bounded, in the sense that if it were bounded, the involved norms would be equivalent and the whole scale of Hilbert spaces would collapse,
however, we can use it to show

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
be an arbitrary sequence. Then for every
r\leq p\leq m
,

(19)

C^{p}_{\psi}=C^{r}_{\psi}\iota_{r,p}^{-1}\quad\text{ on }{\sf Dom}(C_{\psi}^{p})

Therefore,
{\iota_{r,p}}_{{}_{|_{{\sf Dom}(C^{r}_{\psi})}}}
is a bijective operator from
{\sf Dom}(C_{\psi}^{r})
onto
{\sf Dom}(C_{\psi}^{p})
.

Furthermore, if
\psi\subset\mathcal{H}_{m}
and

is complete for
\mathcal{H}_{r}
, then if

, then
\psi
is complete for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
.

Proof.

The first statement is a direct consequence of (
18
).

For the converse of Lemma
4.3
(i) we use
[

:
\psi
is complete in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
if and only if

is injective and since
\iota_{r,p}^{-1}
is injective, this implies that

is injective too, which is equivalent to

being complete in
\mathcal{H}_{p}
.
∎

By Lemma
4.10
we know that a Bessel sequence in

is also one in
\mathcal{H}_{r}
for

. We can show an opposite direction for lower semi-frame.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}

A sequence is a lower semi-frame if and only if the analysis operator is boundedly invertible.
By (

{C^{p}_{\psi}}^{-1}=\iota_{r,p}{C^{r}_{\psi}}^{-1}

Also
\|{C^{p}_{\psi}}^{-1}\|\leq\|{C^{r}_{\psi}}^{-1}\|

4.4.2.
Duality

The sequence
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subset\mathcal{H}_{p}
is a lower semi-frame for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
if and only if
[
20
]
there exists a Bessel sequence

(20)

f=\sum_{k}\left\langle{f}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}\phi_{k}=D^{p}_{\phi}C^{p}_{\psi}(f),\quad\forall f\in{\sf Dom}(C^{p}_{\psi}).

Let
\psi\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
be a lower semi-frame for
\mathcal{H}_{r}
,
r\leq m

it is one in
\mathcal{H}_{m}

\phi\subset\mathcal{H}_{m}

\mathcal{H}_{m}
and therefore also in all spaces
\mathcal{H}_{r}
,
r\leq m
. By assumption (
20
) is valid on
{\sf Dom}(C^{m}_{\psi})

Now let

, then by Lemma
4.13
(
20
)
converges for all
p
, with
r\leq p\leq m
.
Consider

, then

g=\sum_{k}\left\langle{g}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{m}\phi_{k}=\sum_{k}\left\langle{\iota_{p,m}\iota_{p,m}^{-1}g}\left|{\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{m}\phi_{k}=\sum_{k}\left\langle{\iota_{p,m}^{-1}g}\left|{\iota_{m,p}\psi_{k}}\right.\right\rangle_{p}\phi_{k}=

Let

be a lower semi-frame for

for all

.

4.4.3.
A negative result

.

Let
\psi=(\psi_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{m}
be a frame for
\mathcal{H}_{p}
and one for
\mathcal{H}_{q}
, for every

.

Remark 4.17
.

In particular, this means that if two Hilbert spaces, one contained into the other one, do not coincide, a sequence can never be a frame for both of them.

Remark 4.18
.

For this statement it is important we have considered Hilbert spaces and (standard) frames with the sequence space
\ell^{2}
. If we consider Banach spaces, associated to a weighted
\ell^{p}
space
[
30
]
or Hilbert spaces with weighted
\ell^{2}
sequence spaces
[
27
,
29
]
, this result does not hold. Quite the opposite, e.g. for localized frames
[
8
, Theorem 1]
one can show that a frame on the pivot space
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is also a frame for the other (Banach) spaces
\mathcal{H}_{q}
.

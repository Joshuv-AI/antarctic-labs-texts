Relative left Bongartz completions and their compatibility with mutations
Peigen Cao, Yu Wang, Houjun Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce relative left Bongartz completions for a given basic τ \tau -rigid pair ( U , Q ) (U,Q) in 𝗆𝗈𝖽 ⁡ A \operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A . They give a family of basic τ \tau -tilting pairs containing ( U , Q ) (U,Q) as a direct summand. More precisely, to each basic τ \tau -tilting pair ( M , P ) (M,P) with 𝖥𝖺𝖼 M ⊆ ( τ U ) ⊥ ∩ Q ⊥ \operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau U)}\cap Q^{\bot} , we prove that 𝖥𝖺𝖼 ⁡ U ∗ ( 𝒲 ∩ 𝖥𝖺𝖼 ⁡ M ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U*(\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M) is a functorially finite torsion class in 𝗆𝗈𝖽 ⁡ A \operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A such that 𝖥𝖺𝖼 U ⊆ 𝖥𝖺𝖼 U ∗ ( 𝒲 ∩ 𝖥𝖺𝖼 M ) ⊆ ( τ U ) ⊥ ∩ Q ⊥ \operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U*(\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M)\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(\tau U)}\cap Q^{\bot} , where 𝒲 := U ⊥ ∩ ( τ U ) ⊥ ∩ Q ⊥ \mathcal{W}:=U^{\bot}\cap\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot} is the wide subcategory associated to ( U , Q ) (U,Q) . Thus 𝖥𝖺𝖼 ⁡ U ∗ ( 𝒲 ∩ 𝖥𝖺𝖼 ⁡ M ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U*(\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M) corresponds to a basic τ \tau -tilting pair ( M − , P − ) (M^{-},P^{-}) containing ( U , Q ) (U,Q) as a direct summand. We call ( M − , P − ) (M^{-},P^{-}) the left Bongartz completion (or Bongartz co-completion) of ( U , Q ) (U,Q) with respect to ( M , P ) (M,P) . Notice that ( M − , P − ) (M^{-},P^{-}) coincides with the usual Bongartz co-completion of ( U , Q ) (U,Q) in τ \tau -tilting theory if ( M , P ) = ( 0 , A ) (M,P)=(0,A) . We prove that relative left Bongartz completions have nice compatibility with mutations. Using this compatibility we are able to study the existence of maximal green sequences under τ \tau -tilting reduction. We also explain our construction and some of the results in the setting of silting theory.

1.
Introduction

\tau
-tilting theory was introduced by Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
1
]
to complete the classical tilting theory from the viewpoint of mutations. Partial tilting modules and tilting modules in classic tilting theory are enlarged to
\tau
-rigid pairs and
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\tau
-tilting theory. Tilting modules may not be able to do mutations in all directions, while
\tau
-tilting pairs can overcome this drawback. That is why it is interesting to study
\tau
-tilting theory.

In classic tilting theory, Bongartz completion
[
6
]
is an operation to complete a partial tilting module
U
to the ‘maximal’ tilting module
T
containing
U
as a direct summand. Bongartz completion is naturally extended to
\tau
-tilting theory
[
1
]
, which completes a
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
to the ‘maximal’
\tau
-tilting pair containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand. Dually, one can also complete
(U,Q)
to the ‘minimal’
\tau
-tilting pair containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand, which is called the Bongartz co-completion of
(U,Q)
. Notice that the ‘minimal’ completion, i.e., Bongartz co-completion is not always possible in classic tilting theory. However, this is always possible in
\tau
-tilting theory.

In this paper, Bongartz completion is renamed by absolute right Bongartz completion and Bongartz co-completion is renamed by absolute left Bongartz completion. We mainly focus on ‘left type’=‘smaller type’ completions. In the final part of this paper, we give some remarks on ‘right type’=‘bigger type’ completions.

Jasso
[
21
]
introduced
\tau
-tilting reduction in
\tau
-tilting theory in analogous to Calabi-Yau reduction
[
20
]
in cluster tilting theory and silting reduction
[
2
,
19
]
in silting theory. Let
(U,Q)
be a basic
\tau
-rigid pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
.
The key result of
\tau
-tilting reduction is that we can study the set of basic
\tau
-tilting pairs over
A
that contain
(U,Q)
as a direct summand using the set of all basic
\tau
-tilting pairs over a ‘smaller’ algebra
A_{(U,Q)}
. One can see that in
\tau
-tilting reduction, we need to consider the set of basic
\tau
-tilting pairs that contain a particular
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
as a direct summand. When the
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
is given, the absolute left/right Bongartz completion can only give us the ‘minimal’/‘maximal’
\tau
-tilting pair containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand. A natural question is the following:

Question A: How can we get more
\tau
-tilting pairs containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand?

One way is from the viewpoint of mutations. We start with the ‘minimal’/‘maximal’
\tau
-tilting pair containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand and use mutations to get more
\tau
-tilting pairs containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand. The other way is from the viewpoint of completions. In this case, we need to deform the ‘absolute left/right Bongartz completion’ so that we can get more
\tau
-tilting pairs containing
(U,Q)
as a direct summand. The first step along this direction is given in
[
8
]
in the setting of cluster algebras
[
13
]
.

Cluster algebras were invented by Fomin and Zelevinsky
[
13
]
as a combinatorial approach to the dual canonical bases of quantized enveloping
algebras. Such algebras have distinguished generators called
cluster variables
, which are grouped into overlapping sets of fixed size called
clusters
. For the readers who are not familiar with cluster algebras, we propose that one can just view clusters as basic
\tau
-tilting pairs and subsets of clusters as basic
\tau
-rigid pairs.

In cluster algebras, the first-named author and Li
[
8
]
proved that for any cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
and any subset
U
of the ‘initial’ cluster
{\bf x}_{t_{0}}
, there is a canonical method using
g
-vectors
[
15
]
to complete
U
to a cluster
{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}
.
Notice that there are two inputs here. One is a subset
U
of the ‘initial’ cluster and the other one is a cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
. The output is a cluster
{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}
containing
U
as a subset. We can see when
U
is fixed, for each cluster
{\bf x}_{t}
, we can get a completion
{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}
of
U
.

In
[
9
]
, the first-named author of this paper studied the above completions in cluster algebras from the perspective of cluster tilting theory, silting theory and
\tau
-tilting theory. It turns out that the corresponding completions in silting theory and
\tau
-tilting theory are somehow not natural because of the limitation that
U
is a subset of the ‘initial cluster’. This limitation is not a problem in cluster algebras or cluster tilting theory, because we can choose any cluster or cluster tilting object as the initial one. However, this is not the case in silting theory or
\tau
-tilting theory.

Let us explain the reasons in
\tau
-tilting theory. In
\tau
-tilting theory, the
\tau
-tilting pair
(A,0)
is always our canonial initial
\tau
-tilting pair. For any basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,0)\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(A,0)
and any basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
, the author of
[
9
]
completed
(U,0)
to a basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
. Namely, when the basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,0)\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(A,0)
is fixed, for each basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
, we get a completion
(M^{\prime},P^{\prime})
of
(U,0)
. One can see that the requirement
(U,0)\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(A,0)
is somehow not natural. So a natural question is the following:

Question B: Fix a general basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
, can we get a completion of
(U,Q)
for each basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
?

The main motivation of this paper is to study the above question. We found that for any basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
, there does exist a natural completion of
(U,Q)
with respect to
(M,P)
. This means when the basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
is fixed, we can get a family of completions of
(U,Q)
. We call them
relative left Bongartz completions
.

Now the new viewpoint is there should have two inputs for a completion. One is a basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
and the other one is a basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
with
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
. The role of
(M,P)
here is to provide some deformation for the absolute left Bongartz completion. There are two very extreme cases such that the condition
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
is automatically valid. The first one is to make
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=0
and the other one is to make
\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. Let us explain them in more details.

Case (i): Take
(M,P)=(0,A)
. Then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=0
and thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
is valid for any basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
. In this case, our completion will reduce to the absolute left Bongartz completion (i.e., Bongartz co-completion) in
\tau
-tilting theory. That is why in the absolute left Bongartz completion, we only have one input
(U,Q)
, because the other input is always
(0,A)
which plays no role in defining the absolute left Bongartz completion.

Case (ii) Take
(U,Q)\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(A,0)
. Then
\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and thus
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
is valid for any
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
. In this case, our completion will reduce to the case in
[
9
]
.

The relative left Bongartz completions in this paper unify the two very extreme cases above and include many other cases. Now we explain why relative left Bongartz completions are interesting. The key reason is that relative left Bongartz completions have nice compatibility with mutations, cf., Theorem
3.11
. Using this compatibility, we are able to study the existence of maximal green sequences under
\tau
-tilting reduction, cf., Theorem
3.16
. The existence of such sequences for finite dimensional Jacobian algebras
[
12
]
has important consequences in cluster algebras, cf.,
[
22
]
.

We also introduce relative left Bongartz completions in the setting of silting theory and prove that relative left Bongartz completions have nice compatibility with mutations in silting theory, cf., Theorem
4.5
. As everyone has already predicted, we prove that the relative left Bongartz completions in silting theory and
\tau
-tilting theory are compatible, cf., Theorem
4.9
.

There are two reasons for us to introduce relative left Bongartz completions in silting theory. The first one is to make our theory more complete, because silting theory is closely related with
\tau
-tilting theory. The other one is to make the readers have a better understanding of relative left Bongartz completions from different viewpoints.

This paper is organized as follows: Section
2
is preliminaries on
\tau
-tilting theory and silting theory. In Section

3
, we define and construct relative left Bongartz completions in
\tau
-tilting theory. We study the compatibility between relative left Bongartz completions and mutations and use the compatibility to study the existence of maximal green sequences under
\tau
-tilting reduction. Section
4
serves Section
3
in order to make the readers have an understanding of relative left Bongartz completions from the viewpoint of silting theory. In Section
5
, we give remarks on relative right Bongartz completions in
\tau
-tilting theory and silting theory.

2.2.2.
Functorially finite torsion classes

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. For example,
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
or we take
\mathcal{A}
some
wide subcategory
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
, which is a full subcategory
of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
closed under kernels, cokernels and extensions. A
torsion pair

(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in
\mathcal{A}
is a pair of subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
satisfying that

(i)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{A}}(T,F)=0
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
;

(ii)

for any object
X\in\mathcal{A}
, there exists a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow X_{t}\rightarrow X\rightarrow X_{f}\rightarrow 0

with
X_{t}\in\mathcal{T}
and
X_{f}\in\mathcal{F}
. Thanks to the condition (i), such a sequence is unique up to isomorphisms. This short exact sequence is called the
canonical sequence
of
X
with respect to
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.

Notice that in a torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
, we always have
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{T}^{\bot}
and
\mathcal{T}=\prescript{\bot}{}{\mathcal{F}}
.

The subcategory
\mathcal{T}
(resp.,
\mathcal{F}
) in a torsion pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is called a
torsion class
(resp.,
torsion-free class
) in
\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a full subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. It is well known that
\mathcal{C}
is a torsion class if and only if it is closed under extensions and epimorphisms in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
is a torsion-free class if and only if it is closed under extensions and monomorphisms in
\mathcal{A}
.

Using the definition of torsion pair, one can easily show that a torsion class is always contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
and a torsion-free class is always covariantly finite in
\mathcal{A}
.
A torsion class
\mathcal{T}
(respectively, a torsion-free class
\mathcal{F}
) in
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
functorially finite
if it is a functorially finite subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
.

Now we focus on the case
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. A module
M\in\mathcal{C}
is said to be

in
\mathcal{C}
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{Ext}}_{A}^{1}(M,\mathcal{C})=0
. Auslander-Smalø
[
4
]
give a sufficient condition on

such that it has only finitely many indecomposable Ext-projective modules up to isomorphisms.

(

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
is a subcategory of
\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
and by (iii), we have

. Thus the equality
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast(\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast(U^{\bot}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M)
holds.
The proof of the equality
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast(U^{\bot}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M)
is similar to the proof of

]
*Proposition 6.8.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

By Proposition
3.4
(iv)(v), the inclusion
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
is a sufficient condition such that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast(\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M)
is a functorially finite torsion class in
\operatorname{\mathsf{f.tors}}_{(U,Q)}(A)
and we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast(\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M)=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
in this case.
There are two very extreme cases such that the inclusion
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
is automatically valid.

(i)

If we take
(M,P)=(0,A)
, then
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=0
and thus
the inclusion
0=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
holds for any basic
\tau
-rigid pair
(U,Q)
.
In this case, the left Bongartz completion
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(0,A)
of
(U,Q)
with respect to
(0,A)
can be defined and it is just the basic
\tau
-tilting pair corresponding to the functorially finite torsion class
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. Hence, it coincides with the absolute left Bongartz completion of
(U,Q)
given in Definition
2.8
.

(ii)

If we take
(U,Q)\in\operatorname{\mathsf{add}}\nolimits(A,0)
, then
\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}=\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and thus
the inclusion
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
holds for any basic
\tau
-tilting pair
(M,P)
. In this case, the left Bongartz completion
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)
of
(U,Q)
with respect to
(M,P)
can be defined and it coincides with the one introduced in
[
9
]
*Definition 6.9 by the first named author of this paper.

The relative left Bongartz completion in Definition
3.3
unify the two very extreme cases in (i) and (ii) and include many other cases.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(M,P)
be a basic
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and
(U,Q)
a direct summand of
(M,P)
. Then the left Bongartz completion
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)
of
(U,Q)
with respect to
(M,P)
exists and we have
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)=(M,P)
.

Proof.

Since
(U,Q)
is a direct summand of
(M,P)
and by Proposition
2.7
, we have

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}.

Then by Proposition
3.4
(v), the left Bongartz completion
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)
of
(U,Q)
with respect to
(M,P)
exists and
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)
is the basic
\tau
-tilting pair corresponding to the functorially finite torsion class
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
. By
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}U\ast\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\ast\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M

we know that the two basic

-tilting pairs

correspond to the same functorially finite torsion class. Hence, we have
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)=(M,P)
.
∎

In the rest part of this subsection, we show that the relative left Bongartz completions always exist for
\tau
-tilting finite algebras.

Definition 3.7

(

]
*Definition 1.1)

-tilting pairs in

(

.

The algebra

.

Keep the notations in Proposition

3.2.
Compatibility between relative left Bongartz completions and mutations

In this subsection, we show that the relative left Bongartz completions have nice compatibility with mutations.

Recall that a module
D\in\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
is called a
brick
, if
\operatorname{\mathsf{End}}_{A}(D)
is a division ring.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(M,P)
and
(N,R)
be two basic
\tau
-tilting pairs in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

(N,R)
is a left mutation of
(M,P)
.

(b)

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
is a cover, i.e., there exists no torsion class
\mathcal{T}
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
such that

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N\subsetneq\mathcal{T}\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M.

(c)

There exists a unique brick
D
in
N^{\bot}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
up to isomorphisms. In this case, we actually have
N^{\bot}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\operatorname{\mathsf{Filt}}(D)
, where
\operatorname{\mathsf{Filt}}(D)
is the smallest full subcategory of
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
containing
D
and closed under extensions.

Proof.

The equivalence of (a) and (b) follows from
[
1
]
*Theorem 2.33. The equivalence of (b) and (c) can be found in many different references, for example,
[
11
]
*Theorem 3.3 (b),
[
3
,
5
]
.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(U,Q)
be a basic
\tau
-rigid pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A
and
\mathcal{W}:=U^{\bot}\cap\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
the associated wide subcategory.
Let
(M,P)
be a basic
\tau
-tilting pair in
\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A

(N,R)
a left mutation of
(M,P)
. Denote by

the unique brick in Lemma

. Suppose that
\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M
is a subcategory of
\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If the brick

does not belong to

\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M=\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N

(ii)

(iii)

or

is a left mutation of
B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)

\textstyle{(N,R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{B_{(U,Q)}^{-}}
\textstyle{(M,P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{B_{(U,Q)}^{-}}
\textstyle{B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(N,R)}
\textstyle{B_{(U,Q)}^{-}(M,P)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}

\alpha

Since

is a subcategory of

\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N\subsetneq\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}M\subseteq\prescript{\bot}{}{(}{\tau U})\cap Q^{\bot}.

(iii), we have the equalities:

By

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(U,X)=0

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(U,X_{f})=0

(ii) By

\mathcal{T}
and thus

. Since

there exists

but

. Consider the canonical sequence of

with respect to the torsion pair
(\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N,N^{\bot})

\operatorname{\mathsf{mod}}\nolimits A

is a quotient module of

is closed under quotient modules and by

, consider the canonical sequence of

with respect to the torsion pair
(\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N,N^{\bot})

. Since

\mathcal{W}\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Fac}}N
in the poset of torsion classes in

(iii) By (i) and (ii), we have that either

3.3.

We first recall the notion of maximal green sequences for torsion classes in

[

]
has important consequences in cluster algebras
[

]

[

]

-tilting reduction for any finite dimensional algebra.

Definition 3.14

(
[
7
]
*Definition 4.8)

Let

be a torsion class in

for

If

]

be a torsion class in

in

-tilting pairs

\textstyle{(M_{1},P_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

such that

.

“(a)
\Longleftrightarrow
(b)”: This follows from Proposition
4.4
.

“(b)
\Longleftrightarrow
(c)”: Since

, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(U,T[1])\cong\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(U,X_{T}[1])\oplus\operatorname{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathcal{D}}(U,Y_{T}[2])

“(c)

“(d)

Hence, (d) and (e) are equivalent.
∎

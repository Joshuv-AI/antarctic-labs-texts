Determination of the D → π ​ π D\rightarrow\pi\pi ratio of penguin over tree diagrams
Margarita Gavrilova, Yuval Grossman, Stefan Schacht

Abstract
We study the penguin over tree ratio in D → π ​ π D\rightarrow\pi\pi decays. This ratio can serve as a probe for rescattering effects. Assuming the Standard Model and in the isospin limit, we derive expressions that relate both the magnitude and the phase of this ratio to direct CP asymmetries and branching fractions. We find that the current data suggest that rescattering is large. A dedicated experimental analysis with current and future data will be able to significantly reduce the errors on these determinations, and enable us to check if indeed there is significant rescattering in D → π ​ π D\to\pi\pi decays.



I
Introduction

After the discovery of charm CP violation through the measurement of the difference of CP asymmetries of singly-Cabibbo suppressed (SCS) decays
Aaij et al. 2019
, the
recent first evidence of CP violation in a single decay channel,
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

Aaij et al. 2023a
, showed hints for an enhanced
\Delta U=1
contribution to the subleading amplitude
Schacht 2023
. These new developments make searches for charm CP violation in additional channels
particularly interesting in order to probe the effects of non-perturbative QCD and physics beyond the Standard Model (BSM)
Bause et al. 2023
.

CP violation in SCS charm decays has been studied for a long time, especially in the context of U-spin and SU(3)
F
symmetry
Golden and Grinstein 1989
;
Buccella et al. 1995
;
Pirtskhalava and Uttayarat 2012
;
Bhattacharya et al. 2012
;
Hiller et al. 2013
;
Cheng and Chiang 2012
;
Feldmann et al. 2012
;
Grossman and Robinson 2013
;
Nierste and Schacht 2015
;
Müller et al. 2015a
;
Müller et al. 2015b
;
Nierste and Schacht 2017
;
Grossman and Schacht 2019a
;
Cheng and Chiang 2019
.
SU(3)
F
breaking effects have also been modelled accounting for
final-state interactions
Buccella et al. 2013
;
Buccella et al. 2019

or in the context of the factorization-assisted topological-amplitude approach
Li et al. 2012
;
Li et al. 2019
.
Flavor-symmetry methods have been applied to
D
mixing, too, see Refs.
Falk et al. 2002
;
Falk et al. 2004
;
Kagan and Silvestrini 2021
. Note that in this case
a treatment within the heavy quark expansion is consistent with the data due to large theory uncertainties
Lenz et al. 2020
.

In this letter, we study CP violation in
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays. The decay amplitudes of
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays can be written as

\displaystyle A^{f}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{d}A_{d}^{f}-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}A_{b}^{f}\,,

(1)

where
f
denotes the respective final state and
\lambda_{d,b}
are the combinations of Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix elements

\lambda_{d}=V_{cd}^{*}V_{ud}\,,\quad\lambda_{b}=V^{*}_{cb}V_{ub}.

(2)

A_{d}^{f}
and
A_{b}^{f}
are the CKM-leading and -subleading amplitudes, respectively. In the parametrization of decay amplitudes in Eq. (
1
) we use the convention that
A_{d}^{f}
and
A_{b}^{f}
contain strong phases only.

The key quantity we focus on in this letter is the magnitude of the ratio of CKM-subleading over CKM-leading amplitudes

\displaystyle r^{f}

\displaystyle\equiv|A_{b}^{f}/A_{d}^{f}|\,.

(3)

This ratio is also known as “penguin over tree ratio”.
Note that the short distance contributions to the amplitudes
A^{f}_{b}
are very small due to the very effective GIM suppression and we neglect them throughout this letter. The CKM-subleading amplitude
A_{b}^{f}
, to which we refer as penguin, thus contains only the long distance effects, also known as rescattering.

As we explain in more detail below, in the limit of no final state rescattering
r^{f}=0
. Thus, a measurement of
r^{f}
can serve as a probe of rescattering effects in
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays.

The question of the size of the rescattering is a hint to a deeper question about QCD: can we treat QCD as perturbative at the charm scale? If it is perturbative, rescattering should be small, that is
r^{f}\ll 1
. Non-perturbative effects will result in
r^{f}\gtrsim 1
. Thus, a clear way to probe that ratio is called for.

Although there are some first conceptual ideas on the lattice
Hansen and Sharpe 2012
there is at this time no reliable way to calculate
r^{f}
. The available theory estimates for this ratio vary depending on the employed methodology.
Light cone sum rule (LCSR) calculations
Khodjamirian and Petrov 2017
;
Chala et al. 2019
find this ratio to be
\mathcal{O}(0.1)
.
Estimates allowing for larger non-perturbative effects such as large rescattering or nearby resonances result in
r^{f}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)

Soni 2019
;
Schacht and Soni 2022
;
Brod et al. 2012a
;
Grossman and Schacht 2019b
, see also Refs.
Golden and Grinstein 1989
;
Brod et al. 2012b
. Studies employing coupled dispersion relations and rescattering data can be found in Ref.
Bediaga et al. 2023
;
Pich et al. 2023
;
Franco et al. 2012
.
While Ref.
Bediaga et al. 2023
explains the charm CP violation data within the Standard Model (SM), Ref.
Pich et al. 2023
finds that this not possible. Ref.
Franco et al. 2012
finds enhanced charm CP violation to be marginally consistent with rescattering effects.

Although the estimates for
r^{f}
come with a large theory uncertainty, its sensitivity to both rescattering effects and BSM physics
Grossman et al. 2007
;
Altmannshofer et al. 2012
;
Dery and Nir 2019
;
Bause et al. 2023
;
Calibbi et al. 2020
;
Bause et al. 2020
;
Buras et al. 2021
;
Acaroğlu and Blanke 2022
makes it crucial to find clean ways to extract it from experimental data.
A common key assumption employed in the literature in order to be able to compare the CP asymmetry data to the SM at all is that the relative strong phase between CKM-subleading and CKM-leading amplitudes

\delta^{f}\equiv\arg\left(\frac{A_{b}^{f}}{A_{d}^{f}}\right)

(4)

is
\mathcal{O}(1)

Grossman and Schacht 2019b
. It has been pointed out in the literature that strong phases corresponding to
\delta^{f}
can be obtained via the measurements of time-dependent CP violation or quantum-correlated charm decays
Xing 1997
;
Gronau et al. 2001a
;
Bevan et al. 2011
;
Bevan and Meadows 2014
;
Grossman et al. 2012
;
Grossman and Schacht 2019b
;
Xing 2019
;
Schacht 2023
. Additionally, it has been shown that the analogous strong phase in multi-body decays can be determined from a fit to the CP violating time-integrated Dalitz plot
Dery et al. 2021
.
However, even with recent experimental and methodological advances there exist no measurements yet of these strong phases, neither in two-body
Aaij et al. 2021a
;
Pajero and Morello 2022
, nor in three-body
Aaij et al. 2015
;
Aaij et al. 2023b
;
Davis et al. 2023
;
Aaij et al. 2023c
decays.

In this letter, we assume the SM and show that isospin symmetry allows the determination of the relative strong phase between CKM-subleading and CKM-leading
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays from direct CP asymmetries and branching ratios only. This enables at the same time also the extraction of the magnitude of the “penguin over tree” ratio
r^{f}
.

The isospin construction proved very fruitful for the analysis of
B\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays
Gronau and London 1990
;
Lipkin et al. 1991
;
Grossman and Quinn 1998
;
Charles 1999
;
Gronau et al. 2001b
;
Charles et al. 2005
;
Pivk and Le Diberder 2005
;
Gronau and Zupan 2005
.
Also in the case of
B
-decays it was found that the penguin over tree ratio as well as the relevant strong phase can be extracted from direct CP asymmetries and branching ratios, see, for example, Eqs. (7)–(9) and Eqs. (110)–(114) in Ref.
Charles 1999
and the numerical results in Refs.
Charles et al. 2006
;
Charles et al. 2017
.

Isospin symmetry has been applied to
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays in Refs.
Grossman et al. 2012
;
Franco et al. 2012
;
Atwood and Soni 2013
;
Bevan and Meadows 2014
;
Wang 2023a
;
Wang 2023b
;
Kwong and Rosen 1993
.
Note that the hierarchies of the interfering amplitudes in the
D
and
B
systems are very different from each other. Nevertheless, both systems have exactly the same group-theoretical structure under isospin, which implies that the two systems have the same sum rules at the amplitude level
Gavrilova et al. 2022
. Thus we expect that similar isospin relations should hold at the level of observables as well. However, when deriving the implications for the observables, different approximations are used.

Below, after introducing our notation and approximations, we derive new isospin relations that allow the extraction of the ratio of CKM-subleading over CKM-leading amplitudes for
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
. We study the numerical implications of current data and show the prospects of future more precise determinations of the penguin over tree ratio with future LHCb and Belle II data. Afterwards, we conclude.

II
Notation

Regarding Eq. (
1
) we remark that we use a parametrization slightly different from the one frequently used for amplitudes of
D
-decays in the literature, see for example Ref.
Müller et al. 2015b
. In our case the CKM-leading contribution is accompanied by the CKM-factor
-\lambda_{d}
and not
\lambda_{sd}\equiv(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{d})/2
. This choice ensures that the CKM-subleading part
A_{b}^{f}
only contains contributions with isospin
\Delta I=1/2
.
In general, the parametrization with the CKM-factor
\lambda_{sd}
is a convenient choice when studying
U
-spin, while a parametrization with
\lambda_{d}
is better suited for the isospin analysis of
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
. For brevity, we introduce the following notation for the decay amplitudes

\displaystyle A^{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}

\displaystyle\equiv A^{+-}\,,

\displaystyle A^{\pi^{0}\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle\equiv A^{00}\,,

\displaystyle A^{\pi^{+}\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle\equiv A^{+0}\,.

(5)

For the relative strong phases of CKM-leading and CKM-subleading amplitudes we write

\displaystyle\delta^{+-}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{A_{b}^{+-}}{A_{d}^{+-}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\delta^{00}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{A_{b}^{00}}{A_{d}^{00}}\right)\,.

(6)

The phases
\delta^{+-}
and
\delta^{00}
enter the direct CP asymmetries of
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decays, respectively.
We also use the relative strong phase between the CKM-leading contributions of the two decay channels, which we denote as

\displaystyle\delta_{d}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{A_{d}^{+-}}{A_{d}^{00}}\right).

(7)

In the isospin limit, this phase is related to the relative phase between the
\Delta I=1/2
and
\Delta I=3/2
amplitudes which can be extracted from the
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
branching ratios, see Ref.
Selen et al. 1993
.
Direct CP asymmetries are defined as

\displaystyle{a}_{CP}^{f}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{|A^{f}|^{2}-|\overline{A}^{f}|^{2}}{|A^{f}|^{2}+|\overline{A}^{f}|^{2}}\,.

(8)

The normalization of the amplitudes is such that the branching fractions are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{f}=\mathcal{P}^{f}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(|A^{f}|^{2}+|\overline{A}^{f}|^{2}\right)\,,

(9)

where
\mathcal{P}^{f}
are phase space factors given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{f}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\tau_{D}}{16\pi m_{D}^{3}}\sqrt{m_{D}^{2}-(m_{P_{1}}-m_{P_{2}})^{2}}\,\times

\displaystyle\qquad\sqrt{m_{D}^{2}-(m_{P_{1}}+m_{P_{2}})^{2}}\,,

(10)

where
D=D^{0},\,D^{\pm}
and
P_{1,2}=\pi^{0},\,\pi^{\pm}
depending on the specific final state
f
.

III
Isospin Relations

III.1
Approximations

Throughout this letter we consider the SM case only and use the following set of approximations.

(i)

We consider the isospin limit, i.e., we do not take into account isospin breaking effects.

(ii)

We neglect electromagnetic corrections and electroweak penguin contributions that are subleading due to the smallness of their Wilson coefficients.

The approximations
(i)
and
(ii)
are expected to hold at the
\mathcal{O}(1\%)
level
Grossman et al. 2012
.
Next, we use the fact that the involved CKM matrix elements are hierarchical
and use the following approximations:

(iii)

For branching ratios, we neglect contributions which are suppressed by
\mathcal{O}(|\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{d}|)\sim\lambda^{4}
, where
\lambda\approx 0.23
is the Wolfenstein parameter, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{f}=\mathcal{P}^{f}\cdot\absolutevalue{\lambda_d}^{2}|A^{f}_{d}|^{2}\,.

(11)

(iv)

We calculate direct CP asymmetries at leading order in

|\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{d}|

Golden and Grinstein 1989
;
Nierste and Schacht 2017
;
Pirtskhalava and Uttayarat 2012

\displaystyle a_{CP}^{f}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{\lambda_{b}}{-\lambda_{d}}\right)r^{f}\sin(\delta^f)\,.

(12)

Both Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
) rely on
r^{f}
respecting the hierarchy induced by the CKM matrix elements, that is,
r^{f}\ll 10^{4}
. Eq. (
12
) also relies on the convention that
A_{d}^{f}
and
A_{b}^{f}
contain strong phases only.

III.2
Isospin decomposition

We write the isospin decomposition in such a way that all weak phases are explicit and the theory parameters depend on strong phases only
Schacht and Soni 2022
;
Franco et al. 2012
;
Atwood and Soni 2013

\displaystyle A^{+-}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{d}\left(\sqrt{2}\left(t_{1/2}+t_{3/2}\right)\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(\sqrt{2}\,p_{1/2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle A^{00}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{d}\left(2t_{3/2}-t_{1/2}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(-p_{1/2}\right)\,,

\displaystyle A^{+0}

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{d}\left(3\,t_{3/2}\right)\,.

(13)

Here,
t_{1/2}
,
t_{3/2}
are the CKM-leading contributions that correspond to
\Delta I=1/2
and
\Delta I=3/2
transitions, respectively, and
p_{1/2}
is the CKM-subleading
\Delta I=1/2
contribution. Note that assumptions
(i)
and
(ii)
ensure that in Eq. (
13
) there are no contributions with
\Delta I=5/2
, and that there is no
\Delta I=3/2
contribution to the CKM-subleading amplitude.
The notation in Eq. (
13
) is chosen such that one can directly read off
A_{d}^{f}
and
A_{b}^{f}
in the convention of Eq. (
1
).

The isospin decomposition of the CP-conjugate amplitudes
\overline{A}^{f}
takes the same form as Eq. (
13
) up to the complex conjugation of the CKM-factors and unphysical overall phase-factors that do not enter any expressions for observables, see
e.g.
, Ref.
Branco et al. 1999
.

As the matrix elements
t_{1/2}
,
t_{3/2}
, and
p_{1/2}
that enter the amplitudes
A^{f}
and
\overline{A}^{f}
are the same and we have more amplitudes than matrix elements, there exist sum rules between the amplitudes of the system
Grossman et al. 2012
. As a consequence, this results also in relations between the observables that can be chosen as branching ratios and CP asymmetries. For example, from the last line of Eq. (
13
) it follows that
A^{+0}=\overline{A}^{-0}
, or in terms of observables, the well-known relation
Buccella et al. 1993
;
Grossman et al. 2012

\displaystyle a_{CP}^{+0}=0.

(14)

Below we derive new isospin relations that relate branching fractions and CP asymmetries to
r^{f}
and
\sin\delta^{f}
.
We then use these relations to extract the strong phases
\sin\delta^{f}
and
r^{f}

from direct CP asymmetries and branching ratio measurements only.

III.3
Rescattering

In principle, there exists an ambiguity in how one defines the two interfering amplitudes,
e.g.
, we can define them with CKM-coefficients
-\lambda_{d}
and
-\lambda_{b}/2
, as we do in Eq. (
1
), or for example with the CKM factors
(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{d})/2
and
-\lambda_{b}/2
, as frequently done when studying the
U
-spin system of neutral
D
decays, see e.g. Ref.
Grossman and Schacht 2019b
.
Therefore the “ratio of the two interfering amplitudes” is not clearly defined a priori.
An unambiguous definition can be obtained in the language of operator matrix elements.
Using the Hamiltonian and the notation of Ref.
Khodjamirian and Petrov 2017

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\sum_{q=d,s}\lambda_{q}\left(C_{1}Q_{1}^{q}+C_{2}Q_{2}^{q}\right)\right)\equiv\sum_{q=d,s}\lambda_{q}\mathcal{O}^{q}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{q}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\overline{u}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})q\right)\left(\overline{q}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})c\right)\,,

(16)

\displaystyle Q_{2}^{q}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\overline{q}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})q\right)\left(\overline{u}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})c\right)\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}^{q}\rangle^{f}

\displaystyle\equiv\left<f\right|\mathcal{O}^{q}\left|D^{0}\right>\,,

(18)

we write the decay amplitudes as

\displaystyle A(D^{0}\rightarrow f)=\lambda_{d}\langle\mathcal{O}^{d}\rangle^{f}+\lambda_{s}\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle^{f}

(19)

\displaystyle\quad=-\lambda_{d}\left(\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle^{f}-\langle\mathcal{O}^{d}\rangle^{f}\right)-\frac{\lambda_{b}}{2}\left(2\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle^{f}\right)\,,

(20)

and define the penguin over tree ratio as

\displaystyle r^{f}

\displaystyle\equiv\left|\frac{2\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle^{f}}{\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle^{f}-\langle\mathcal{O}^{d}\rangle^{f}}\right|\,,

(21)

where we introduce the conventional factor of two in the numerator due to our convention Eq. (
1
).
Once we fix the operator basis, our definition of
r^{f}
becomes unambiguous.

We next elaborate on what we refer to as rescattering effects. In this letter we associate rescattering with the matrix element of the operator
\mathcal{O}^{s}
. The naive interpretation of rescattering in this case is that the
s\overline{s}
pair rescatters into a
d\overline{d}
pair that then hadronizes, together with the
u\overline{u}
pair, into a pair of pions. Thus we define the no-rescattering limit as the limit in which

\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle_{\text{no rescatt.}}=0\,,

(22)

and consequently from Eq. (
21
)

r^{f}_{\text{no rescatt.}}=0\,.

(23)

The limit of large rescattering on the other hand corresponds to the case
|\langle\mathcal{O}^{s}\rangle|\sim|\langle\mathcal{O}^{d}\rangle|
which implies
r^{f}\gtrsim 1
, depending on the relative phase between
\langle O^{d}\rangle
and
\langle O^{s}\rangle
. The experimental determination of
r^{f}
thus provides a test of rescattering effects in
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays.

We also mention one more useful limit, the
N_{c}\to\infty
limit. A general discussion of the
1/N_{c}
expansion in charm decays can be found in Ref.
Buras et al. 1986
. This limit can be tested by studying a ratio of hadronic matrix elements
Buras et al. 2014
;
Buras 1989
that can be defined as follows

r_{t}\equiv\absolutevalue{\frac{t_{1/2}}{t_{3/2}}}\,.

(24)

In the
N_{c}\rightarrow\infty
limit one finds, completely analogous to the kaon sector
Buras et al. 2014
, that

r_{t}^{N_{c}\rightarrow\infty}=2.

(25)

In kaon physics, the fact that this ratio is much bigger than
2
is referred to as the
\Delta I=1/2
rule. The deviation of
r_{t}
from
2
can be interpreted as a failure of the
1/N_{c}
expansion, sizable rescattering, significant kinematic effects or any combination of the above. The
r^{f}
ratio, on the other hand, provides a clean probe of rescattering effects.

III.4
Observables and Theory Parameters

In our isospin analysis we use three types of parameters to which we refer as
known parameters
,
observables
and
theory parameters
.

We think of the CKM factors
\lambda_{d,b}
and phase space factors
\mathcal{P}^{f}
as known parameters. We assume that these parameters are well known from the independent experimental measurements and we use them as input in our analysis.

The observables in our analysis are the branching fractions and CP asymmetries. For the system of
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays, we thus have five observables

\mathcal{B}^{+-},\quad\mathcal{B}^{00},\quad\mathcal{B}^{+0},\quad a_{CP}^{+-},\quad a_{CP}^{00}\,.

(26)

The theory parameters can be read off Eq. (
13
) and are given by the magnitudes and relative phases of the hadronic matrix elements that appear on the RHS. In particular, without loss of generality, we choose
t_{3/2}
to be real, resulting in a set of five theory parameters

\absolutevalue{t_{3/2}},\quad\absolutevalue{t_{1/2}},\quad\absolutevalue{p_{1/2}},\quad\arg\left(t_{1/2}\right),\quad\arg\left(p_{1/2}\right)\,.

(27)

In what follows, we also use the following combinations of theory parameters in Eq. (
27
)

r_{t}=\absolutevalue{\frac{t_{1/2}}{t_{3/2}}},\quad\delta_{t}=\arg\left(\frac{t_{1/2}}{t_{3/2}}\right),\quad r^{f},\quad\delta^{f},\quad\delta_{d}\,,

(28)

where
r^{f}
,
\delta^{f}
and
\delta_{d}
are defined in Eqs. (
3
), (
4
) and (
7
), respectively. We emphasize that the parameters in Eq. (
28
) are not independent from the parameters in Eq. (
27
) and among the two sets of the theory parameters there are only five that are independent.

The counting of the theory and experimental parameters shows that, in the isospin limit, the system of
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays can be completely solved. That is, all the theory parameters in Eqs. (
27
)-(
28
) can be expressed in terms of the observables listed in Eq. (
26
), possibly up to some discrete ambiguities. In particular, this means that the parameters of interest,
r^{f}
and
\delta^{f}
, can be expressed in terms of branching fractions and direct CP asymmetries.

III.5
Tree parameters

Before we proceed to the determination of
r^{f}
and
\sin\delta^{f}
, we elaborate on how to extract the phase
\delta_{d}

defined in Eq. (
7
) from the
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
branching ratios in the isospin limit, see also Refs.
Franco et al. 2012
;
Selen et al. 1993
.

From the isospin decomposition in Eq. (
13
) and neglecting the CKM-subleading contributions to the amplitudes,
we can solve for the theory parameters that contribute to the CKM-leading parts of the amplitudes, namely
\absolutevalue{t_{1/2}}
,
\absolutevalue{t_{3/2}}
and the relative phase
\delta_{t}
between
t_{1/2}
and
t_{3/2}
. We obtain

\displaystyle|t_{3/2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3\absolutevalue{\lambda_d}}\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+0}}{\mathcal{P}^{+0}}}\,,

(29)

\displaystyle|t_{1/2}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\lambda_{d}|}\sqrt{\left(\frac{1}{3}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}+\frac{1}{3}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{00}}{\mathcal{P}^{00}}-\frac{2}{9}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+0}}{\mathcal{P}^{+0}}\right)}\,,

(30)

\displaystyle r_{t}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{|t_{1/2}|}{|t_{3/2}|}=\sqrt{3\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{B}^{+0}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{+0}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}+3\frac{\mathcal{B}^{00}}{\mathcal{B}^{+0}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{+0}}{\mathcal{P}^{00}}-2}\,,

(31)

\displaystyle\cos\delta_{t}

\displaystyle\equiv\cos\left(\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{t_{1/2}}{t_{3/2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r_{t}}\left(\frac{9}{2}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{B}^{+0}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{+0}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}-r_{t}^{2}-1\right)\,.

(32)

From these relations and Eq. (
13
) we derive the expression for the phase
\delta_{d}
between the CKM-leading amplitudes of
D\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
D\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
as defined in Eq. (
7
). We have

\displaystyle\cos\delta_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{2-r_{t}^{2}+r_{t}\cos\delta_{t}}{\sqrt{\left(2-r_{t}^{2}+r_{t}\cos\delta_{t}\right)^{2}+9r_{t}^{2}\sin^{2}\delta_{t}}}\,,

(33)

and

\displaystyle\sin\delta_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pm 3r_{t}|\sin\delta_{t}|}{\sqrt{\left(2-r_{t}^{2}+r_{t}\cos\delta_{t}\right)^{2}+9r_{t}^{2}\sin^{2}\delta_{t}}}\,.

(34)

Note that
\sin\delta_{d}
is only known up to a sign, as we do not know the sign of
\sin\delta_{t}
.
Our results Eqs. (
31
) and (
32
) agree with the analytical expressions in Ref.
Selen et al. 1993
, once we account for the different conventions of the isospin decomposition.

A comment is in order about the phase
\delta_{d}
. The phase
\delta_{d}
is defined as a relative phase between CKM-leading amplitudes of
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
, two decays with different final states. In general, such a relative phase can not be physical, yet here we solve for
\delta_{d}
in terms of physical observables. The resolution of this apparent paradox is that in the isospin limit
\pi^{\pm}
and
\pi^{0}
are not distinguishable, which makes this phase meaningful in the isospin limit.

III.6
Penguin over Tree Ratio

We employ now the isospin amplitude sum rule
Grossman et al. 2012
;
Grossman and Robinson 2013
;
Atwood and Soni 2013
for the CKM-suppressed amplitudes.
From Eq. (
1
) and the first and second lines of Eq. (
13
) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}A_{b}^{+-}=-A_{b}^{00}\,,

(35)

which implies the following relations for the absolute values and the relative phase of CKM-subleading amplitudes:

\absolutevalue{\frac{A_b^{+-}}{A_b^{00}}}=\sqrt{2}\,,

(36)

\arg\left(\frac{A_{b}^{+-}}{A_{b}^{00}}\right)=\pi\,.

(37)

We note that Eqs. (
35
)–(
37
) are related to the amplitude sum rule in Eq. (14) of Ref.
Grossman et al. 2012
.

In the following we derive the implications of Eqs. (
36
) and (
37
). We obtain two isospin limit relations that relate branching fractions, CP asymmetries and the relative strong phases
\delta^{+-}
and
\delta^{00}
defined in Eq. (
6
). We then use these two relations to solve for the relative strong phases in terms of branching fractions and CP asymmetries. Finally, we use the approximation in Eq. (
12
) to solve for the penguin over tree ratio.

First, we derive the implications of the sum rule between the absolute values of CKM-subleading amplitudes given in Eq. (
36
).
Dividing the expressions Eq. (
12
) for CP asymmetries, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\delta^{+-}}{\sin\delta^{00}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{CP}^{+-}}{a_{CP}^{00}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{00}}{\mathcal{B}^{00}}}\,,

(38)

and

\displaystyle\frac{r^{00}}{r^{+-}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{00}}{\mathcal{B}^{00}}}\,.

(39)

We emphasize that this relation only holds when all of the approximations (
i
)-(
iv
) are satisfied.

Next, we derive the implication of the sum rule between the arguments of the CKM-subleading amplitudes given in Eq. (
37
).
We write
\delta^{+-}
as

\displaystyle\delta^{+-}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{A_{b}^{+-}}{A_{d}^{+-}}\right)=\mathrm{arg}\left(\frac{A_{b}^{+-}A_{d}^{00}A_{b}^{00}}{A_{d}^{+-}A_{d}^{00}A_{b}^{00}}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle=\pi+\delta^{00}-\delta_{d}\,,

(41)

where in the last line we used the isospin relation in Eq. (
37
).
This result allows us to express the LHS of Eq. (
38
) in terms of the phases
\delta^{00}
and
\delta_{d}
. We find

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\delta^{+-}}{\sin\delta^{00}}

\displaystyle=\cot\delta^{00}\sin\delta_{d}-\cos\delta_{d}\,.

(42)

Now, as we have two relations that relate branching ratios, CP asymmetries and relative strong phases, Eqs. (
38
) and (
40
), we can solve for
\sin\delta^{+-}
and
\sin\delta^{00}
. To do so, we substitute the result of Eq. (
42
) into Eq. (
38
) and solve for
\cot\delta^{00}
. We find

\displaystyle\cot\delta^{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin\delta_{d}}\frac{a_{CP}^{+-}}{a_{CP}^{00}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{00}}{\mathcal{B}^{00}}}+\cot\delta_{d}\,,

(43)

from which we can obtain

\displaystyle\sin\delta^{00}=\frac{-\text{sign\,}(a_{CP}^{00})}{\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\delta_{d}}\left(\frac{a_{CP}^{+-}}{a_{CP}^{00}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}\frac{\mathcal{P}^{00}}{\mathcal{B}^{00}}}+\cos\delta_{d}\right)^{2}}}\,.

(44)

Similarly we obtain

\displaystyle\sin\delta^{+-}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\text{sign\,}(a_{CP}^{+-})}{\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\delta_{d}}\left(\frac{a_{CP}^{00}}{a_{CP}^{+-}}\sqrt{2\frac{\mathcal{P}^{+-}}{\mathcal{B}^{+-}}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{00}}{\mathcal{P}^{00}}}+\cos\delta_{d}\right)^{2}}}\,.

(45)

The sign of each of the
\sin\delta^{f}
can be extracted from the sign of
a_{CP}^{f}
using the fact
that
\text{Im\,}\left(-\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{d}\right)<0
.
The ratio of the CKM-subleading over CKM-leading amplitudes follows then from Eq. (
12
)

\displaystyle r^{f}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{a_{CP}^{f}}{\sin\delta^{f}\,\mathrm{Im}\left(-\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{d}\right)}\right|\,.

(46)

Substituting the expressions for
\sin\delta^{f}
in Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) into Eq. (
46
), we arrive at

\displaystyle r^{00}=\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Im}\left(-\lambda_{b}/\lambda_{d}\right)|}\times

\displaystyle\sqrt{(a_{CP}^{00})^{2}+\frac{(a_{CP}^{+-}\sqrt{\mathcal{B}^{+-}\mathcal{P}^{00}}+a_{CP}^{00}\sqrt{2\mathcal{B}^{00}\mathcal{P}^{+-}}\cos\delta_{d})^{2}}{2\mathcal{B}^{00}\mathcal{P}^{+-}\sin^{2}\delta_{d}}}\,,

(47)

and

\displaystyle r^{+-}=\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\text{Im}\left(-\lambda_b/\lambda_d\right)}}\times

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(a_{CP}^{+-}\right)^{2}+\frac{\left(a_{CP}^{00}\sqrt{2\mathcal{B}^{00}\mathcal{P}^{+-}}+a_{CP}^{+-}\sqrt{\mathcal{B}^{+-}\mathcal{P}^{00}}\cos\delta_{d}\right)^{2}}{\mathcal{B}^{+-}\mathcal{P}^{00}\sin^{2}\delta_{d}}}\,,

(48)

where
\sin\delta_{d}
and
\cos\delta_{d}
are given in Eqs. (
31
)-(
34
) in terms of branching fractions only. Eqs. (
44
)-(
III.6
) are the main results of this letter. They allow the extraction of
r^{f}
and
\sin\delta^{f}
from direct CP asymmetries and branching fractions with a theory uncertainty of
\mathcal{O}(1\%)
.

We make the following observations:

•

The measurement of
a_{CP}^{+0}
agrees with the isospin sum rule Eq. (
14
).

•

Although we have currently essentially no information about
\sin\delta^{00}
, we can still infer non-trivial information about
r^{00}
. This can be understood from closer inspection of Eq. (
47
), which contains a contribution independent of
a_{CP}^{00}
. This is a consequence of isospin symmetry, which translates knowledge about
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
into constraints on
D^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
, see also Eq. (
39
).

•

The central values of
r^{00}
(
r^{+-}
) are quite large and
2.6\sigma
(
2.7\sigma
) away from 1.

•

The future data scenario demonstrates that our method will allow to extract the ratio of CKM-subleading over CKM-leading amplitudes with unprecedented precision, the key advantage being that no assumption about the strong phases
\delta^{00}
and
\delta^{+-}
has to be made in order to do so.

•

Once the sign of
a_{CP}^{f}
is determined, we also know the sign of
\sin\delta^{f}
. In our future data scenario this is the case for
\sin\delta^{+-}
.

•

Comparing
r_{t}
to the limit Eq. (
24
), we observe an
\mathcal{O}(1)
enhancement in the “charm
\Delta I=1/2
rule”, in agreement with Ref.
Grossman and Schacht 2019b
.

•

Comparing to the limit of no rescattering Eq. (
23
) we obtain for current data
r^{f}>0
at
3.8\sigma
.

The last observation, if confirmed, implies either very large rescattering effects or BSM physics.

V
Conclusion

We show that the approximate isospin symmetry of the
D\rightarrow\pi\pi
system allows for the extraction of the magnitudes of CKM-subleading over CKM-leading amplitudes from direct CP asymmetries and branching ratios without making assumptions about the relevant strong phase.
This ratio is colloquially known as the “penguin over tree ratio”.
Our main theoretical results are given in Eqs. (
44
)-(
III.6
). Numerically, from current data we obtain

\displaystyle r^{00}

\displaystyle=5.2^{+13.3}_{-2.4}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle r^{+-}

\displaystyle=5.5^{+14.2}_{-2.7}\,.

(50)

The large central values are driven by the sizable direct CP asymmetry found currently in
D\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
.
Using prospects for the development of experimental errors, we demonstrate that our method will allow to probe the puzzle of penguin enhancement in charm decays with unprecedented precision in the future.

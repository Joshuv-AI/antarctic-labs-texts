Dissipative effects in odd viscous Stokes flow around a single sphere
Jeffrey C. Everts, Bogdan Cichocki

Abstract
Odd viscosity (OV) is a transport coefficient in, for example, fluids of self-spinning (active) particles or electrons in an external magnetic field. The key feature of OV is that it does not contribute to dissipation in two spatial dimensions. In contrast, we explicitly show that in the three-dimensional case, OV can contribute indirectly to dissipation by modifying the fluid flow. We quantify the dissipation rate of a single spherical particle moving through a fluid with OV via an exact analytical solution of the generalised stationary creeping flow equations. Our results provide a novel way to quantify the effects of OV by measuring the solid-body motion of a single spherical particle. Moreover, we explicitly demonstrate how complex fluids can be designed in terms of their rheological properties by mixing passive particles with self-spinning active particles.



Introduction
.
– The response of a liquid to local gradients in the fluid velocity
{\bf v(r)}
is characterized by the linear constitutive relation
\sigma_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm{V}}({\bf r})=\eta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}v_{\gamma}({\bf r})
, which describes how a generalised flux (the viscous stress tensor
\bm{\sigma}^{\mathrm{V}}
) is related to its conjugate generalised force (the velocity gradient tensor
\nabla{\bf v}
) via a transport coefficient (the viscosity tensor
\bm{\eta}
). In the presence of external fields
{\bf B}
that are odd under a time-reversal operation (e.g., a magnetic field), microscopic time reversibility dictates that the form of
\bm{\eta}
is constrained by the Onsager-Casimir reciprocal relation (OCRR)
Onsager 1931a
;
Onsager 1931b
;
Casimir 1945
,
\eta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}({\bf B})=\eta_{\gamma\lambda\alpha\beta}(-{\bf B})
. Consequently, the viscosity tensor picks up a non-vanishing antisymmetry part for
{\bf B}\neq 0
, with
\bm{\eta}({\bf B})=\bm{\eta}^{\mathrm{S}}({\bf B})+\bm{\eta}^{\mathrm{A}}({\bf B})
, where
\eta^{\mathrm{S}}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}({\bf B})=\eta^{\mathrm{S}}_{\gamma\lambda\alpha\beta}({\bf B})
and
\eta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}({\bf B})=-\eta^{\mathrm{A}}_{\gamma\lambda\alpha\beta}({\bf B})
.

In active matter
Jülicher et al. 2018
, there are fluids described by a non-vanishing intrinsic angular momentum density
\bm{\ell}
, which can be generated by rotating the system
Delplace et al. 2017
, by using self-spinning particles due to fuel consumption
Nguyen et al. 2014
;
Maggi et al. 2015
;
Aubret et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2020
;
Han et al. 2021
, or external fields
Boymelgreen et al. 2014
;
Kokot et al. 2017
. So-called chiral active fluids are described by a ground state with
\bm{\ell}\neq 0
that breaks time-reversal symmetry. However, care has to be taken when selecting a
single
ground state and using the OCRR with
\bm{\ell}
fixed (although this is common practice). Namely, in the derivation of the OCRR, microscopic time reversibility is assumed on the level of particles, and therefore, the full dynamical properties of
\bm{\ell}
have to be included. Viewing
\bm{\ell}
as a dynamically-generated order parameter, we have
\bm{\mathcal{\ell}}\rightarrow-\bm{\mathcal{\ell}}
under time reversal and an OCRR in the form
\eta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}(\bm{\ell})=\eta_{\gamma\lambda\alpha\beta}(-\bm{\ell})
. Consequently, there is a non-vanishing
\bm{\eta}^{\mathrm{A}}
similar to a fluid in an external magnetic field. This makes chiral active fluids distinctively different from, for example, cholesteric liquid crystals
de Gennes and Prost 1993
(which have
\bm{\eta}^{\mathrm{A}}=0
) that are chiral due to their spatial ordering, but not active chiral due to the underlying time-reversal-invariant order parameter.

The coefficients in
\bm{\eta}^{\mathrm{A}}
are known as OV
Avron 1998
, but terms like gyroviscosity
Ramos 2005
and Hall viscosity
Scaffidi et al. 2017
are also used depending on the system of interest, like magnetised plasmas
Stacey Jr 1989
or electrons in graphene
Berdyugin et al. 2019
, respectively. The effects of OV can be profound: it can generate edge flows like the edge states in topological insulators
Souslov et al. 2019
, anomalous Hall flows
Lou et al. 2022
, transverse flow patterns to an applied pressure gradient
Banerjee et al. 2017
, or azimuthal flow when a particle is pulled by an external force
Khain et al. 2022
. See Ref.
Fruchart et al. 2023
for a comprehensive literature review. Recently, in soft condensed matter, an OV coefficient is realised with a similar magnitude as the shear viscosity
\eta_{\mathrm{s}}
in systems of self-spinning magnetic particles
Soni et al. 2019
, contrasting magnetised polyatomic gases where OV coefficients are orders of magnitude smaller than
\eta_{\mathrm{s}}

Hulsman et al. 1970
. Realisations of OV are also expected to be present in biological matter
Friedrich and Jülicher 2007
;
Petroff et al. 2015
;
Tan et al. 2022
.

Generally, OV does not contribute to the viscous dissipation rate
{\dot{w}({\bf r})}=\sigma_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm{V}}({\bf r})\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}({\bf r})=\eta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\lambda}^{\mathrm{S}}\partial_{\beta}v_{\alpha}({\bf r})\partial_{\lambda}v_{\gamma}({\bf r})
, provided that
{\bf v(r)}
is not affected by OV. Notably, in two spatial dimensions, the effects of OV can be absorbed in the pressure for incompressible flow and hence do not contribute to the flow velocity
Ganeshan and Abanov 2017
(contrasting compressible fluids
Lier et al. 2023
and other systems coupled to a bulk passive fluid
Hosaka et al. 2021
). However, in three spatial dimensions, OV can affect fluid flow and hence indirectly contribute to viscous dissipation
Khain et al. 2022
. There is a lacking quantification of this effect because the simplest three-dimensional system where this effect is present – a single sphere undergoing solid-body motion in an OV liquid in the stationary creeping flow regime– has only been investigated in the limit of small OV (with respect to
\eta_{\mathrm{s}}
) where such dissipative effects identically vanish
Khain et al. 2022
;
Yuan and Olvera de la
Cruz 2023
. This limits the experimental applicability to, for example, the system in Ref.
Soni et al. 2019
, where
\eta_{\mathrm{o}}\approx\eta_{\mathrm{s}}
.

In this Letter, we quantify the dissipation rate of a single spherical particle undergoing solid-body motion in an incompressible active chiral fluid in linear ambient flow, see Fig.
1
. We demonstrate that for this situation, an exact analytical solution exists for the relation between force moments and the solid-body motion of a single sphere. Furthermore, we discuss the dissipated power due to OV and its effects on effective hydrodynamic equations for suspensions of spherical particles in chiral active fluids, a concept not yet considered before within this context in the literature. Going beyond the regime of weak OV, we lay the building blocks for describing Brownian motion, hydrodynamic interactions, and effective hydrodynamic equations of colloidal suspensions in a three-dimensional chiral active fluid.

Model
.
– Consider an incompressible liquid,
\nabla\cdot{\bf v}({\bf r})=0
described by the stress tensor
\bm{\sigma}({\bf r})=-p({\bf r})\bm{\mathsf{I}}+\bm{\eta}:\nabla{\bf v}({\bf r})
, where we defined the single contraction as
{[\bm{\mathsf{A}}\cdot\bm{\mathsf{B}}]_{\alpha...\beta}={A}_{\alpha...\lambda}{B}_{\lambda...\beta}}
and the double contraction as
[\bm{\mathsf{A}}:\bm{\mathsf{B}}]_{\alpha...\beta}={A}_{\alpha...\gamma\lambda}{B}_{\lambda\gamma...\beta}
. Furthermore,
[\bm{\mathsf{I}}]_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
. In the absence of an external magnetic field, we choose the simplest form of the viscosity tensor
\bm{\eta}
(for the general form see Ref.
Khain et al. 2022
) assuming that there is only one type of shear viscosity
\eta_{\mathrm{s}}
, but allowing for a constant non-zero intrinsic angular momentum density
\bm{\ell}
,

\bm{\eta}=2\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\bm{\mathcal{I}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2\eta_{\mathrm{o}}\bm{\mathsf{c}}^{(0)}(\bm{\hat{\ell}})},

(1)

Eq. (
1
) has been derived from a microscopic Hamiltonian of self-spinning particles in Ref.
Markovich and Lubensky 2021
and is experimentally relevant
Soni et al. 2019
. Here, we defined the tensors
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha\beta\sigma\nu}=\delta_{\alpha(\sigma}\delta_{\nu)\beta}-(1/3)\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{\sigma\nu}
and
c_{\alpha\beta\sigma\nu}^{(0)}=\hat{\ell}_{\lambda}(\epsilon_{\lambda\alpha(\sigma}\delta_{\nu)\beta}+\epsilon_{\lambda\beta(\sigma}\delta_{\nu)\alpha})
, with indices between round brackets indicating the symmetric part. For example,
\delta_{\alpha(\sigma}\delta_{\nu)\beta}=(\delta_{\alpha\sigma}\delta_{\nu\beta}+\delta_{\alpha\nu}\delta_{\sigma\beta})/2
. In Eq. (
1
), we characterize the OV by the length of
\bm{\ell}
via
\eta_{\mathrm{o}}>0
, and its direction
\bm{\hat{\ell}}
. This is equivalent to the frequently used convention of fixing
\bm{\hat{\ell}}
but allowing
\eta_{\mathrm{o}}
to be negative. In our formulation, however, the OCRR are manifest by including the dynamical character of
\bm{\ell}
.

Furthermore, in Eq. (
1
), we assume that there are no torque densities; hence,
\bm{\sigma}
is symmetric. Antisymmetric stresses can be, in principle, included
Felderhof 2011
, as well as having an inhomogeneous
\bm{\ell}

Markovich and Lubensky 2021
. For stationary creeping flow, conservation of linear momentum
\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}({\bf r})=-{\bf f(r)}
, with
{\bf f(r)}
a force density acting on the fluid, gives

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\nabla^{2}{\bf v({\bf r}})-\nabla\tilde{p}({\bf r})+\eta_{\mathrm{o}}(\bm{\hat{\ell}}\cdot\nabla)[\nabla\times{\bf v}({\bf r})]=-{\bf f}({\bf r}).

(2)

In Eq. (
2
), the intrinsic angular momentum of the fluid
\bm{\hat{\ell}}
couples to the orbital angular momentum of the fluid
\nabla\times{\bf v}({\bf r})
.
We extracted a total gradient from the viscous stress tensor to define the effective pressure,
\tilde{p}({\bf r})=p({\bf r})+2\eta_{\mathrm{o}}\bm{\hat{\ell}}\cdot[\nabla\times{\bf v}({\bf r})]
. This form is chosen because the incompressibility condition leads to
\nabla^{2}\tilde{p}({\bf r})=\nabla\cdot{\bf f}({\bf r})
, which is of the Stokes form.

Fundamental solution
.
– First, we construct the fundamental solution to Eq. (
2
), which is defined as the response to a point force density. The corresponding Green tensor
\bm{\mathsf{G}}({\bf r})
and effective pressure vector
\bar{{\bf Q}}({\bf r})
satisfy

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{s}}\nabla^{2}G_{\alpha\beta}({\bf r})-\partial_{\alpha}\bar{Q}_{\beta}({\bf r})+\eta_{\mathrm{o}}\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma\lambda}(\bm{\hat{\ell}}\cdot\nabla)\partial_{\gamma}G_{\lambda\beta}({\bf r})

\displaystyle=-\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta({\bf r}),\quad\partial_{\alpha}G_{\alpha\beta}({\bf r})=0.

(3)

Insertion of
\bm{\mathsf{G}}({\bf r})=\int d{\bf k}/(2\pi)^{3}\,\tilde{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}({\bf k})e^{i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}}
, with
\tilde{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}({\bf k})
the Fourier transform of
\bm{\mathsf{G}}({\bf r})
(and with similar definitions for
\tilde{{\bar{\bf{Q}}}}({\bf k})
), we find upon using the incompressibility condition that
\tilde{\bar{{\bf{Q}}}}({\bf k})=-i\hat{\bf k}/k
. Insertion back in Eq. (
3
) gives

\tilde{\bm{\mathsf{G}}}({\bf k})=\frac{1}{\eta_{\mathrm{s}}}\frac{\bm{\mathsf{I}}-\hat{\bf k}\hat{\bf k}+\gamma(\hat{\bf k}\cdot\bm{\hat{\ell}})(\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\hat{\bf k})}{k^{2}[1+\gamma^{2}(\hat{\bf k}\cdot\bm{\hat{\ell}})^{2}]},

(4)

with
\bm{\epsilon}
the Levi-Cevita symbol and
\gamma=\eta_{\mathrm{o}}/\eta_{\mathrm{s}}
. In real space, we conclude that
\bar{\bm{\mathsf{Q}}}({\bf r})=\hat{\bf r}/(4\pi r^{2})
, (like in the Stokes case). The Fourier inverse of Eq. (
4
) has been calculated to linear order in
\gamma
in Ref.
Yuan and Olvera de la
Cruz 2023
and only for components
G_{\alpha z}
(
\alpha=x,y,z
) in Ref.
Khain et al. 2022
. The full tensor can be calculated by using cylindrical coordinates where
\hat{\bm{\ell}}
is the longitudinal axis, followed by contour integration over the axial coordinate
Khain et al. 2022
. The remaining integrals over the tangential and radial coordinate are known
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2014
; see SM
SM
for technical details. We find

\displaystyle\bm{\mathsf{G}}({\bf r})=\frac{1}{4\pi\eta_{\mathrm{s}}(r+\tilde{r})}\Bigg\{\bm{\mathsf{I}}+\hat{\bf r}\hat{\bf r}+\frac{\gamma r}{\tilde{r}}[(\hat{\bm{\ell}}\cdot\bm{\epsilon})-(\hat{\bf r}\cdot\hat{\bm{\ell}})(\hat{\bf r}\cdot\bm{\epsilon})]

\displaystyle-\left(1-\frac{r}{\tilde{r}}\right)\left[\hat{\bf r}\hat{\bf r}+\frac{(\hat{\bf r}\times\hat{\bm{\ell}})(\hat{\bf r}\times\hat{\bm{\ell}})}{1-(\hat{\bf r}\cdot\hat{\bm{\ell}})^{2}}\right]\Bigg\},

(5)

with
\tilde{r}/r=\sqrt{1+\gamma^{2}[1-(\hat{\bf r}\cdot\hat{\bm{\ell}})^{2}]}
. Note that we have the symmetry relation
G_{\alpha\beta}({\bf r};\bm{\hat{\ell}})=G_{\beta\alpha}({\bf r};-\bm{\hat{\ell}})
and for
\gamma\rightarrow 0
, we have
\tilde{r}\rightarrow r
and, therefore, we retrieve the Oseen tensor:
\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow 0}\bm{\mathsf{G}}({\bf r})=(\bm{\mathsf{I}}+\hat{\bf r}\hat{\bf r})/(8\pi\eta_{\mathrm{s}}r)
.

Discussion
.
– Our work provides exact analytical results for interpreting experiments of three-dimensional chiral active fluids with large OV coefficients. Furthermore, there are important consequences of our results. First, our exact analytical solutions are a starting point for describing hydrodynamic interactions in OV fluids beyond the linear
\gamma
approximation, where indirect dissipative OV effects will contribute. Second, our results open the doors to describe (many-body) Brownian motion in a system of self-spinning particles. Third, the singularity method that we used in this work can be employed to derive the grand mobility matrix of spherical particles in systems with spatially constant
\bm{\eta}
. Examples are nematic liquid crystals with a constant director field with strong anisotropy
Stark and Ventzki 2001
;
Kos and Ravnik 2018
or OV fluids with non-vanishing rotational viscosities, which is relevant for experimental systems
Soni et al. 2019
. In these cases, one needs to solve the torque balance in addition to the balance of linear momentum. Finally, we discussed the novel notion of a suspension with a chiral active fluid acting as the medium. Our results on the effective viscosity tensor for the macroscopic hydrodynamics of such suspensions suggest that one can design various complex fluids by mixing self-spinning active fluids with passive particles, which can be important for microfluidic and biological applications.

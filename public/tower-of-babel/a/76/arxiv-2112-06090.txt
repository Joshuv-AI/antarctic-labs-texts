Equidistribution of mass for random processes on finite-volume spaces
Timothée Bénard

Abstract
Let G G be a real Lie group, Λ ⊆ G \Lambda\subseteq G a lattice, and X = G / Λ X=G/\Lambda . We fix a probability measure μ \mu on G G and consider the left random walk induced on X X . It is assumed that μ \mu is aperiodic, has a finite first moment, spans a semisimple algebraic group without compact factors, and has two non mutually singular convolution powers. We show that for every starting point x ∈ X x\in X , the n n -th step distribution μ n ∗ δ x \mu^{n}*\delta_{x} of the walk weak- ∗ \ast converges toward some homogeneous probability measure on X X .

Introduction

In this note we consider a semigroup
\Gamma
acting on a space
X
and study the way the
\Gamma
-orbits distribute in
X
. A first approach might be to describe the orbit closures. In the case where
\Gamma
is a diagonal group acting on a finite-volume homogeneous space, it is known that these orbit closures may be extremely irregular, with transverse sections homeomorphic to a Cantor set. However, Ratner’s theorems
[
8
]
highlight that on the opposite, the orbits of a group generated by Ad-unipotent 1-parameter flows are very regular. Benoist-Quint
[
3
]
recently proved that such regularity also occurs for the action of a semigroup
\Gamma
whose Zariski closure is semisimple. Our goal is to complement Benoist-Quint’s result by estimating how
\Gamma
-orbits equidistribute in their closure from the perspective of random walks supported on
\Gamma
. We first recall the state of the art concerning Benoist-Quint’s theorems.

Let
G
be a real Lie group,
\Lambda\subseteq G
a lattice, and set
X=G/\Lambda
. We consider the action by left multiplication on

of a closed subsemigroup
\Gamma\subseteq G
. Thoughout the text,
\Gamma
will always be assumed to satisfy

(\mathbf{H})

The real algebraic group
\overline{\Ad\Gamma}^{Z}\subseteq\text{Aut}(\mathfrak{\mathfrak{g}})
generated by
\Gamma
in the adjoint representation is semisimple, Zariski connected, with no compact factor.

We also need a precise definition to express the regularity of orbit closures.

Definition

A closed subset

G_{Y}=\{g\in G,\,gY=Y\}

\nu_{Y}
. If the semigroup
\Gamma

G_{Y}
(and acts ergodically on
(Y,\nu_{Y})

Y
is
\Gamma
-invariant
(and
\Gamma

).

Theorem 1

(Equidistribution of mass)

Let
G
be a real Lie group,
\Lambda\subseteq G
a lattice, and
\Gamma\subseteq G

. Let
\mu

\Gamma

\mu^{n}\ast\delta_{x}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\nu_{x}

In particular, we obtain equidistribution of mass when

Proof of Theorem
1

Given a signed measure
m
on a measurable space, we denote by
\lVert m\rVert
its total variation

\lVert m\rVert=\sup\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}|m(A_{i})|

where the supremum is taken over all the measurable countable partitions
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of the space.

Lemma 2
.

Let
H
be a measurable group and
\mu
a probability measure on
H
such that
\mu^{k},\mu^{l}
are not mutually-singular for some distinct integers
k\neq l\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have the convergence

\lVert\mu^{n}-\mu^{n+k-l}\rVert\underset{n\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

Proof.

This result is due to Foguel
[
5
, Theorem 1]
, whose proof is strongly inspired by the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
about conservative ergodic Markov operators.
The argument is a bit tricky. For completeness, we give a more intuitive explanation in the case where
l=0
, i.e.
\mu^{k}(e)>0
for some
k\geq 1
.

We may assume
k=1
.
Decompose
\mu
as
\mu=\alpha\delta_{e}+(1-\alpha)\mu_{0}
where
\alpha>0
and
\mu_{0}
is a probability measure on
H
. Then for
n\geq 0
,

\mu^{n}=(\alpha\delta_{e}+(1-\alpha)\mu_{0})^{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\alpha^{n-i}(1-\alpha)^{i}\mu^{i}_{0}

Hence, using the convention
\binom{n}{i}=0
if
i\geq n+1
,

\displaystyle\lVert\mu^{n}-\mu^{n+1}\rVert

\displaystyle=\lVert\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\left(\binom{n}{i}\alpha^{n-i}(1-\alpha)^{i}-\binom{n+1}{i}\alpha^{n+1-i}(1-\alpha)^{i}\right)\mu^{i}_{0}\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\,\lvert\binom{n}{i}\alpha^{n-i}(1-\alpha)^{i}-\binom{n+1}{i}\alpha^{n+1-i}(1-\alpha)^{i}\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\,\lvert\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=i)-\mathbb{P}(S_{n+1}=i)\rvert

where
S_{n}
denotes the
n
-th step of the Markov chain on
\mathbb{N}
starting at the origin and with i.i.d. increments of law
\alpha\delta_{0}+(1-\alpha)\delta_{1}
. A straightforward computation shows that

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=i)\geq\mathbb{P}(S_{n+1}=i)\iff i\leq(n+1)(1-\alpha)=:m_{n}

Hence,

\displaystyle\lVert\mu^{n}-\mu^{n+1}\rVert\leq\lvert\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\leq m_{n})-\mathbb{P}(S_{n}>m_{n})\rvert+\lvert\mathbb{P}(S_{n+1}\leq m_{n})-\mathbb{P}(S_{n+1}>m_{n})\rvert

By the Central Limit Theorem, each term in the right hand side converges to zero as
n
goes to infinity, whence the result.

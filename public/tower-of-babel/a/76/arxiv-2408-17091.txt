Horizon quantum mechanics for coherent quantum black holes
Wenbin Feng, Andrea Giusti, Roberto Casadio

Abstract
The formalism of the horizon quantum mechanics is applied to electrically neutral and spherically symmetric black hole geometries emerging from coherent quantum states of gravity to compute the probability that the matter source is inside the horizon. We find that quantum corrections to the classical horizon radius become significant if the matter core has a size comparable to the Compton length of the constituents and the system is indeed a black hole with probability very close to one unless the core radius is close to the (classical) gravitational radius.

2
Coherent quantum states for Schwarzschild geometry

A metric of the form in Eq. (
1.1
) can be conveniently described as the mean field of the
coherent state of a (canonically normalised) free massless scalar field

\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\,\Phi=(f-1)/2=V
(see Ref.
[
1
]
for all the details).
From the Klein-Gordon equation

\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)\right]\Phi(t,r)=0\ ,

(2.1)

we obtain the (positive frequency) eigenfunctions

u_{k}={e^{-i\,k\,t}}\,j_{0}(k\,r)\ ,

(2.2)

where
j_{0}=\sin(k\,r)/k\,r
with
k>0
are spherical Bessel functions, which allow us
to write the field operator as

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}k}{2\,\pi^{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\,k}}\left[u_{k}\,\hat{a}(k)+u_{k}^{\ast}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(k)\right]

(2.3)

and its conjugate momentum as

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}=i\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}k}{2\,\pi^{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\,k}{2}}\left[u_{k}\,\hat{a}(k)-u_{k}^{\ast}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(k)\right]\

(2.4)

where
\hat{a}
and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
are the usual annihilation and creation operators.

In particular, we are interested in a coherent state

\displaystyle\ket{V_{M}}=e^{-N_{M}/2}\,\exp\!\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}k}{2\,\pi^{2}}\,g_{k}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}(k)\right\}\ket{0}\ ,

(2.5)

which effectively reproduces (as closely as possible) the Schwarzschild geometry, that is

\displaystyle\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\bra{V_{M}}\hat{\Phi}(t,r)\ket{V_{M}}\simeq V_{M}(r)=-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M}{r}\ .

(2.6)

From

\displaystyle\bra{V_{M}}\hat{\Phi}\ket{V_{M}}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}k}{2\,\pi^{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{2\,\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}}{k}}\,g_{k}\,\cos(k\,t-\gamma_{k})\,j_{0}(k\,r)\,\ ,

(2.7)

we impose
\gamma_{k}=k\,t
for staticity and the coefficients
g_{k}

can be determined by expanding the metric function
V_{M}=V_{M}(r)

on the spatial part of the normal modes (
2.2
), to obtain

\displaystyle g_{k}=-\frac{4\,\pi\,M}{\sqrt{2\,k^{3}}\,m_{\rm p}}\ .

(2.8)

However, the corresponding normalisation factor

\displaystyle N_{M}=4\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}k}{k}

(2.9)

diverges both in the infrared and in the ultraviolet.
The infrared divergence can be eliminated by embedding the geometry in a universe
of finite Hubble radius
r=R_{\infty}
, whereas the ultraviolet divergence could be removed by
assuming the existence of a matter core of finite size
r=R_{\rm s}
.

For the present work, it is convenient to regularise the ultraviolet divergence
by replacing the coefficients in Eq. (
2.8
) with

\displaystyle g_{k}=-\frac{4\,\pi\,M\,e^{-\frac{k^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{4}}}{\sqrt{2\,k^{3}}\,m_{\rm p}}\ ,

(2.10)

which yields the total occupation number

\displaystyle N_{M}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle 4\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\int\limits_{R_{\infty}^{-1}}^{\infty}\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}k}{k}\,e^{-\frac{k^{2}\,R_{s}^{2}}{2}}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle 2\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\,\Gamma\!\left(0,\frac{R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,R_{\infty}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle 4\,\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\ln\!\left(\frac{R_{\infty}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)\ ,

where
\Gamma=\Gamma(a,x)
is the incomplete gamma function and we assumed
R_{\rm s}\ll R_{\infty}
.
The coherent state
\ket{V_{M}}
so defined corresponds to a quantum-corrected metric function

\displaystyle V_{{\rm q}M}=\sqrt{G_{\rm N}}\bra{V_{M}}\hat{\Phi}\ket{V_{M}}=-\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M}{r}\,{\rm erf}\!\left(\frac{r}{R_{\rm s}}\right)\ ,

(2.12)

where
{\rm erf}
denotes the error function and we let
R^{-1}_{\infty}\to 0
.

2.1
Effective energy density

From the definition of the mass function in Eq. (
1.4
) and

\displaystyle 1+2\,V_{{\rm q}M}=1-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m}{r}\ ,

(2.13)

we easily obtain

\displaystyle\rho(r)=-\frac{V_{{\rm q}M}}{4\,\pi\,G_{\rm N}\,r^{2}}\left(1+r\,\frac{V_{{\rm q}M}^{\prime}}{V_{{\rm q}M}}\right)\ .

(2.14)

We next note that the quantum corrected potential (
2.12
) is of the form

\displaystyle V_{{\rm q}M}=V_{M}(r)\,v(r)\ ,

(2.15)

where the function
v
has the asymptotic behaviours

\displaystyle v(r\to 0)\to 0\qquad{\rm and}\qquad v(r\gg R_{\rm s})\to 1\ .

(2.16)

The effective energy density therefore reads

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{M\,v^{\prime}}{4\,\pi\,r^{2}}\ ,

(2.17)

so that Eq. (
2.16
) implies

\displaystyle m(r\to\infty)=M\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{\prime}(x)\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}x=M\ ,

(2.18)

as expected.

In particular, we have
v={\rm erf}({r}/{R_{\rm s}})
and

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{M\,e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{R_{\rm s}^{2}}}}{{2}\,\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}\,R_{\rm s}\,r^{2}}\ ,

(2.19)

which is the same result one would obtain from the Einstein field equations

G^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=8\,\pi\,G_{\rm N}\,T^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
, where
G^{\mu}_{\ \nu}
is the Einstein
tensor for the quantum corrected metric from Eq. (
2.12
).

3
Horizon quantum mechanics

We are interested in a matter source with energy density (
2.19
)
made of a very large number
N
of particles.
For simplicity, we assume that all particles are identical and have a mass
\mu=M/N
.

The (normalised) wavefunction of each particle in position space can be estimated as

\psi_{{\rm S}}(r_{i})\propto\rho^{1/2}\propto\frac{e^{-\frac{r_{i}^{2}}{2\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi^{\frac{3}{4}}\,R_{\rm s}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,r_{i}}\ ,

(3.1)

where
i=1,\ldots,N
.
In momentum space, we then have

\displaystyle\psi_{{\rm S}}(k_{i})=\frac{2\,\pi^{\frac{3}{4}}\,R_{\rm s}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{k_{i}}\,{\rm erfi}\!\left(\frac{k_{i}\,R_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\,e^{-\frac{k_{i}^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{{2}}}\ ,

(3.2)

where
{\rm erfi}
is the imaginary error function.
Notice that the wavefunction (
3.2
)
peaks around
k=R_{\infty}^{-1}
, and the imaginary error function can be approximated
for
k_{i}\,R_{\rm s}\ll 1
as

{\rm erfi}\!\left(\frac{k_{i}\,R_{\rm s}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,k_{i}\,R_{\rm s}\ .

(3.3)

Each particle can therefore be assumed in a state described by

4

Given the approximation (
3.3

3.4
) “underestimates” the exact wavefunction at large

where

The dynamics of each particle is determined by a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle E_{i}^{2}=\mu^{2}+\hbar^{2}\,k_{i}^{2}\ .

Thus, we can rewrite the state (

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{\rm s}^{(i)}}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{E}\int\limits_{\mu}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}E_{i}\,e^{-\frac{\left(E_{i}^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)\,R_{s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\,\ket{E_{i}}\ ,

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{N}}\simeq\frac{1}{N!}\,\sum_{\{\sigma_{i}\}}^{N}\,\left[\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\ket{\psi_{\rm s}^{(i)}}\right]\ ,

3.2
Horizon wavefunction

We can now obtain the horizon wavefunction from the effective single-particle
wavefunction (
3.11
) by setting
r_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,E

and defining
\ket{r_{\rm H}}\propto\ket{2\,\ell_{\rm p}\,E/m_{\rm p}}
.
This yields
\Psi_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})\simeq 0
, for
r_{\rm H}<R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M
, and

\displaystyle\Psi_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})\simeq\mathcal{N}_{\rm H}\left(\frac{r_{\rm H}-R_{\rm H}}{\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{2\,\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}}e^{-\frac{\mu\left(r_{\rm H}-R_{\rm H}\right)R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}}}\ ,

(3.13)

for
r_{\rm H}\geq R_{\rm H}
, where the normalisation constant
\mathcal{N}_{\rm H}

is given in Eq. (
B.2
).

The expectation value of the gravitational radius is computed in Eq. (
B.3
) and
can be written as

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{r}_{\rm H}\,\rangle$}\simeq R_{\rm H}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\mu}^{2}}{R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.14)

which is in perfect agreement with the expression of the energy given in Eq. (

It is again noteworthy that
\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{r}_{\rm H}\,\rangle$}>R_{\rm H}
, although the correction with respect to the
classical expression is negligible for an astrophysical black hole unless the core is of a size
comparable to the Compton length
\lambda_{\mu}

It is also important to recall that

is the horizon radius
only if the core is sufficiently smaller, as we will determine next.

By means of the effective single-particle wavefunction (

More details of the calculation are given in Appendix

Some cases are displayed in Figs.

4
Conclusions and outlook

We have here employed the formalism of the horizon quantum mechanics
[
13
]

in order to verify that coherent state black hole geometries of the Schwarzschild type sourced by a core
of large mass
M
and with a size
R_{\rm s}
larger than Planckian are very likely to display
an outer horizon and be black holes in the usual sense.
For that purpose, we needed to find an explicit description of the (electrically neutral and
spherically symmetric) matter core in terms of a many-particle state that was then expressed
as a superposition of total energy eigenstates.

Our analysis supports the conclusion that the system is indeed a black hole of mass
M\gg m_{\rm p}

if its core made of particles of mass
\mu
has a size

R_{\rm s}\gtrsim\lambda_{\mu}\gg\ell_{\rm p}
but (sufficiently)
smaller than the classical gravitational radius
R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M
.
It would be interesting to generalise the above analysis to include electric charge and
rotation.
Whereas the former case should be rather straightforward, including rotation is going to be much more
problematic since it will require extending the horizon quantum mechanics beyond the perturbative
regime considered in Ref.
[
19
]
.

Clearly, the emerging picture is that the (location of the) horizon in quantum physics
is fuzzy and, at least for sufficiently large matter cores, one would have “quasi” black hole geometries.
In such a picture, the late stage of binary black hole mergers would open a window
into quantum features of the gravitational collapse that might affect the emission of gravitational
waves at the very peak, or during the ring-down phase, for instance by affecting the black hole
Love numbers
[
20
]
and possible echos
[
21
,
22
]
.
Of course, quantitative predictions for such effects would require specific analysis that go beyond
the scope of the present work.

Spectral decomposition and total energy

E_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}E_{i}
and write

\displaystyle C(E)\propto\int\limits_{\mu}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}E_{1}\cdots\int\limits_{\mu}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}E_{N-1}\,{\rm exp}\left\{-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{\left(E_{i}^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)R_{s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}-\frac{\left[\left(E-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N-1}\,E_{i}\right)^{2}-\mu^{2}\right]R_{s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right\}\ .\qquad

(A.3)

It is now convenient to define the function

\displaystyle F(E,{E_{i}})

\displaystyle\!\!\equiv\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}{\left(E_{i}^{2}-\mu^{2}\right)}+\left(E-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,E_{i}\right)^{2}-\mu^{2}

(A.4)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}{\left(\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mu\right)^{2}-N\,\mu^{2}}+\left[E-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}-(N-1)\,\mu\right]^{2}\ ,

where
\mathcal{E}_{i}=E_{i}-\mu
.
By recalling that
M=N\,\mu
, we then obtain

\displaystyle F(E,E_{i})

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mu\right)^{2}-\mu\,M+\left[\left(E-M\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}+\mu\right]^{2}

(A.5)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(E-M\right)^{2}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}+\mu^{2}-2\left(E-M\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}+2\,\mu\left(E-M\right)-2\,\mu\,\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}+2\,\mu\,\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}+(N-1)\,\mu^{2}-\mu\,M

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(E-M\right)^{2}+2\,\mu\left(E-M\right)-2\left(E-M\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left[E-(M-\mu)\right]^{2}-\mu^{2}-2\left(E-M\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}\ .

Plugging this result into Eq. (
A.3
) yields

\displaystyle C(E)

\displaystyle\!\!\propto\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left\{\left[E-(M-\mu)\right]^{2}-\mu^{2}\right\}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cdots\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}_{N-1}

(A.6)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{\frac{R_{s}^{2}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left[2\left(E-M\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\!\!\equiv\!\!

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left\{\left[E-(M-\mu)\right]^{2}-\mu^{2}\right\}}\,I(E,M)\ .

We next note that, since
\mathcal{E}_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,N-1
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\,\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}\ .

(A.7)

A lower bound
I_{-}\leq I
is obtained from the upper bound
\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}=(\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i})^{2}

in Eq. (
A.7
) and is given by

\displaystyle I_{-}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cdots\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}_{N-1}\exp\left[\frac{R_{s}^{2}\,(E-M)}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}\right]\exp\left[-\frac{R_{s}^{2}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}\right]

(A.8)

\displaystyle\!\!\propto\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}^{N-2}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}\exp\left[-\frac{R_{s}^{2}\,\mathcal{E}^{2}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}+\frac{R_{s}^{2}\,(E-M)\,\mathcal{E}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{1-N}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\,_{1}F_{1}\!\left[\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{4\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{1-N}\frac{\left(E-M\right)\,R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\,_{1}F_{1}\!\left[\frac{N}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{4\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]\ ,

where
\Gamma
is the Euler gamma function and
{}_{1}F_{1}
the Kummer confluent hypergeometric function.
An upper bound
I\leq I_{+}
is likewise obtained from the lower bound
\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{2}=0
in
Eq. (
A.7
) and is given by

\displaystyle I_{+}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}_{1}\cdots\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}_{N-1}\exp\left[\frac{R_{s}^{2}\,(E-M)}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}\right]\exp\left[-\frac{R_{s}^{2}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\mathcal{E}_{i}\right)^{2}\right]

(A.9)

\displaystyle\!\!\propto\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}^{N-2}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}\exp\left[-\frac{R_{s}^{2}\,\mathcal{E}^{2}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}+\frac{R_{s}^{2}\,(E-M)\,\mathcal{E}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{1-N}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\,_{1}F_{1}\!\left[\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2^{\frac{N}{2}-1}}\left(\frac{R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{1-N}\frac{\left(E-M\right)\,R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\,_{1}F_{1}\!\left[\frac{N}{2},\frac{3}{2},\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]\ .

For
R_{\rm s}\gg\ell_{\rm p}
and
(E-M)\gtrsim m_{\rm p}
, we can employ the asymptotic behaviour of the Kummer
confluent hypergeometric function,

{}_{1}F_{1}\,\left(a,b,x\right)\sim x^{a-b}\,e^{x}\ ,

(A.10)

for
x\sim(E-M)^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}/\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\gg 1
, which leads to

\displaystyle I_{-}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{N-2}}\,\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\left(E-M\right)^{N-2}\,e^{\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}}{4\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\left[2\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)+\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\right]

(A.11)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}{2^{N-1}\,R_{\rm s}}\left(E-M\right)^{N-2}e^{\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}}{4\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\ ,

where we also used
2\,\Gamma\left((N-1)/2\right)>\Gamma\left(N/2\right)

for
N\gg 1
in the last step.
Likewise,

\displaystyle I_{+}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}{\sqrt{2}\,R_{\rm s}}\,\left(E-M\right)^{N-2}\,e^{\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\left[\Gamma\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)+2\,\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\right]

(A.12)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\,\left(E-M\right)^{N-2}\,e^{\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\ ,

where we used
2\,\Gamma\!\left(N/2\right)>\Gamma\!\left((N-1)/2\right)

for
N\gg 1
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N-1}{2}\right)\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}{2^{N-1}\,R_{\rm s}}\left(E-M\right)^{N-2}e^{\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}}{4\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\lesssim I\lesssim\sqrt{2}\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}{R_{\rm s}}\left(E-M\right)^{N-2}e^{\frac{\left(E-M\right)^{2}R_{\rm s}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\ .

(A.13)

We might note that the above approximation fails for
0<(E-M)\lesssim m_{\rm p}
at fixed value
of
R_{\rm s}\gtrsim\ell_{\rm p}
, for which we instead find the Taylor expansion

\displaystyle I_{+}\sim I_{-}\propto 1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{E-M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)\ .

(A.14)

However, for an astrophysical system of mass
M\gg m_{\rm p}
, this regime can be discarded overall.

By recalling that
N=M/\mu\gg 1
, we finally obtain the bounding functions

\displaystyle C_{-}(E)=\mathcal{N}_{-}\left(\frac{E-M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{M/\mu}e^{-\frac{R_{\rm s}^{2}\,\mu\,(E-M)}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}}\,e^{-\frac{R_{\rm s}^{2}\left(E-M\right)^{2}}{4\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}

(A.15)

and

\displaystyle C_{+}(E)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{+}\left(\frac{E-M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{M/\mu}e^{-\frac{R_{\rm s}^{2}\,\mu\,(E-M)}{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}}\ .

(A.16)

The normalizations
\mathcal{N}_{\pm}
can be obtained from the condition

\displaystyle 1=\int\limits_{M}^{\infty}C^{2}_{\pm}(E)\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}E\ ,

(A.17)

yielding

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{-}^{-2}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{\sqrt{2}\,R_{\rm s}}\right)^{\frac{2\,M}{\mu}}\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{2\,M}{\mu}\right)U\!\left(1+\frac{M}{\mu},\frac{3}{2},\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)

(A.18)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\ell_{\rm p}}{\sqrt{2}\,R_{\rm s}}\right)^{\frac{2\,M}{\mu}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{2\,M}{\mu}\right)U\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu},\frac{3}{2},\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\ ,

where
U=U(a,b,x)
is the Tricomi confluent hypergeometric function, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{+}^{-2}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle m_{\rm p}\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{2\,\mu\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)^{1+\frac{2\,M}{\mu}}\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{2\,M}{\mu}\right)

(A.19)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle m_{\rm p}\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{2\,\mu\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{2\,M}{\mu}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{2\,M}{\mu}\right)\ .

In Section
3.1
, we use the upper bounding function (
A.16
)
in order to estimate the maximum corrections to the total energy.
In fact, the bounds from the spectral coefficients can be used to bound
the expectation value of the total energy as

\displaystyle M+H_{-}\lesssim\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{H}\,\rangle$}\lesssim M+H_{+}\ ,

(A.20)

where

\displaystyle H_{+}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{M}^{\infty}C_{+}^{2}(E)\,E\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}E-M

(A.21)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}C_{+}^{2}(\mathcal{E})\,\mathcal{E}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,(2\,M+\mu)\,}{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle M\,\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}

and

\displaystyle H_{-}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}C_{-}^{2}(\mathcal{E})\,\mathcal{E}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\mathcal{E}=\frac{\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}\,(2\,M+\mu)\,U\!\left(1+\frac{M}{\mu},\frac{1}{2},\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)}{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}\,U\!\left(1+\frac{M}{\mu},\frac{3}{2},\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)}\ .

(A.22)

Employing the definition of the Tricomi confluent hypergeometric function,

\displaystyle U(a,b,x)=\frac{\Gamma\left(1-b\right)}{\Gamma\left(a+1-b\right)}\,_{1}F_{1}\,\left(a,b,x\right)+\frac{\Gamma\left(b-1\right)}{\Gamma\left(a\right)}\,x^{1-b}\,_{1}F_{1}\,\left(a+1-b,2-b,x\right)\ ,

(A.23)

and the asymptotic behaviour of the Kummer confluent hypergeometric function (
A.10
),
we find

\displaystyle U\!\left(1+\frac{M}{\mu},\frac{1}{2},\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\simeq\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+\frac{3}{2}\right)}-\frac{2}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+1\right)}\right]\Gamma\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mu\,R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{1+2\,\frac{M}{\mu}}e^{\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}

(A.24)

and

\displaystyle U\!\left(1+\frac{M}{\mu},\frac{3}{2},\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\right)\simeq\left[\frac{1}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+1\right)}-\frac{2}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right]\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\mu\,R_{\rm s}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{2\,\frac{M}{\mu}-1}e^{\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}}\ ,

(A.25)

from which

\displaystyle H_{-}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle(2\,M+\mu)\,\frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left[\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+1\right)-2\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\left[\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-2\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{M}{\mu}+1\right)\right]}

(A.26)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle 2\,\mu\left(\frac{M}{\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ .

Putting the above bounds together, we obtain

\displaystyle M\left[1+2\,\left(\frac{\mu}{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\lesssim\mbox{$\langle\,\hat{H}\,\rangle$}\lesssim M\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\mu}^{2}}{R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)\ ,

(A.27)

which shows that
\langle\,\hat{H}\,\rangle
cannot be smaller than the classical ADM mass and the
(maximum) relative correction is proportional to the Compton length
\lambda_{\mu}=\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}/\mu
.

Appendix B
Horizon wavefunction and black hole probability

In Section
3.2
, we continue to employ the upper bound on the spectral
decomposition to obtain the horizon wavefunction in Eq. (
3.13
) and estimate
the maximum possible correction to the gravitational radius and minimum probability

P_{\rm BH}
.
Its normalisation is given by

\displaystyle 1=4\,\pi\int\limits_{R_{\rm H}}^{\infty}\left|\Psi_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})\right|^{2}r_{\rm H}^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}r_{\rm H}=4\,\pi\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm H}^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\tilde{r}_{\rm H}}{\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}}e^{-\frac{\mu\,\tilde{r}_{\rm H}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}}}\,\left(\tilde{r}_{\rm H}+R_{\rm H}\right)^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\tilde{r}_{\rm H}\ ,

(B.1)

where
\tilde{r}_{\rm H}=r_{\rm H}-R_{\rm H}
.
The above expression yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\rm H}^{-2}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{8\,\pi\,\ell_{\rm p}^{9}\,m_{\rm p}^{3}}{\mu^{3}\,R_{\rm s}^{6}}\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\mu\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}}\left[\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{2\,\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}+\frac{\mu\,R_{\rm H}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}}\right)+\frac{\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm H}^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{4}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{6}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\right]

(B.2)

\displaystyle\times\,\Gamma\!\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{4\,\pi\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}\,m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}^{2}}{\mu\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\mu\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)\ ,

where we again used
R_{\rm s}\sim R_{\rm H}\gg\ell_{\rm p}
in the last approximation.

The expectation value of the gravitational radius is given by

\displaystyle\bra{\Psi_{\rm H}}\hat{r}_{\rm H}\ket{\Psi_{\rm H}}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle 4\,\pi\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\left|\Psi_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})\right|^{2}r_{\rm H}^{3}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}r_{\rm H}

(B.3)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle R_{\rm H}+R_{\rm H}\,\frac{m_{\rm p}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{3\,\ell_{\rm p}\,\mu}{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}\right)

\displaystyle-R_{\rm H}\,\frac{\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)+\frac{\mu\,R_{\rm H}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{3}\,m_{\rm p}}}{\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}\,\mu}\right)+\frac{\mu\,R_{\rm H}\,R_{\rm s}^{2}}{\ell_{\rm p}^{3}\,m_{\rm p}}\left(1+\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\ell_{\rm p}\,\mu}\right)+\frac{\mu^{2}\,R_{\rm H}^{2}\,R_{\rm s}^{4}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{6}\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle R_{\rm H}\left[1+\frac{\lambda_{\mu}^{2}}{R_{\rm s}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}{R_{\rm H}\,\lambda_{\mu}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle R_{\rm H}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\mu}^{2}}{R_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)\ ,

with
R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M\gg\lambda_{\mu}
the classical Schwarzschild radius and
\lambda_{\mu}\gg\ell_{\rm p}

the Compton length of the matter costituents.

The probability density (
1.9
) for the horizon to be located on the sphere of
radius
r=r_{\rm H}
vanishes for
0\leq r_{\rm H}<R_{\rm H}
, else is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})\simeq 4\,\pi\,\mathcal{N}_{\rm H}^{2}\,r_{\rm H}^{2}\,\left(\frac{r_{\rm H}-R_{\rm H}}{\ell_{\rm p}}\right)^{\frac{m_{\rm p}\,R_{\rm H}}{\mu\,\ell_{\rm p}}}e^{-\frac{\mu\left(r_{\rm H}-R_{\rm H}\right)R_{\rm s}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}}}\ .

(B.4)

The probability density (
1.10
) can be explicitly computed from the
wavefunction (
3.1
) with
r_{i}=r

and the horizon probability density (
B.4
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{<}(r<r_{\rm H})

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(4\,\pi\int\limits_{R_{\rm H}}^{r_{\rm H}}\left|\psi_{\rm S}(r)\right|^{2}r^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}r\right)\mathcal{P}_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})

(B.5)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle{\rm erf}\left(\frac{r_{\rm H}}{R_{\rm s}}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\rm H}(r_{\rm H})\ ,\quad

where
{\rm erf}
denotes the error function.
The black hole probability (
1.12
) now reads

\displaystyle P_{\rm BH}=\int\limits_{R_{\rm H}}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{<}(r<r_{\rm H})\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}r_{\rm H}\ ,

(B.6)

which however can only be computed numerically for specific values
of
R_{\rm H}
,
R_{\rm s}
and
\mu
(see Figs.
1
-
3
).

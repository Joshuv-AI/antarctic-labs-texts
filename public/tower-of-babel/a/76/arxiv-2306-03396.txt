The non-standard Lagrangian from non-uniqueness principle of the real scalar field and fermion field
Suppanat Supanyo 1,2, Monsit Tanasittikosol 1,2, Sikarin Yoo-Kong 3

Abstract
We construct the non-standard Lagrangian, called the multiplicative form, of the homogeneous scalar field and fermion field through the inverse calculus of variations, which the equation of motion still satisfies the Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations, respectively. By employing the non-uniqueness of Lagrangian, we show that the Lagrangians can be written between the linear combination of standard and non-standard Lagrangian. The stability of the ghost field, an unnatural smallness of cosmological constant, and the chiral condensate are discussed by applying these new Lagrangians.



I
Introduction

The non-standard Lagrangian is another approach to explain unsolved problems in modern physics without concerning the renormalizability. In this point of view, the standard renormalizable Lagrangian, which consists of operators with mass dimensions less than four, is assumed to be the lowest approximation from the ultraviolet (UV) complete theory.
As a consequence, the non-standard kinetic energy and potential energy are free to modify the UV physics. The construction schemes of the non-standard Lagrangian are categorized into two approaches: the top-down and bottom-up perspective. From the top-down perspective, for example, the Born-Infeld Lagrangian
Born and Infeld 1934
, in which kinetic energy itself is introduced in square root as the relativistic Lagrangian, can be obtained in an appropriate limit of the string theory
Callan Jr and Maldacena 1998
;
Gibbons 1998
;
Alishahiha et al. 2004
. From the bottom-up perspective, the non-standard form of Lagrangian can be an arbitrary function while preserving the symmetry of the model and satisfying the experimental observation. Frequently, several forms of kinetic energy and potential energy are artificially introduced to explain the new behaviors without systematically mathematical construction, such as the k-essence model
Armendariz-Picon et al. 2000
;
Armendariz-Picon et al. 2001
;
Tian and Zhu 2021
. According to this perspective, this framework is identical to the effective field theory from the bottom-up perspective
Bechtle et al. 2022
;
Brivio and Trott 2019
;
Li et al. 2021
;
Brehmer et al. 2018
to generate the new appropriate interaction.

“The non-uniqueness of Lagrangian”
Mizel 1995
and “the inverse problem of the calculus of variations”
Douglas 1941
;
Hojman and Urrutia 1981
;
Sarlet 1982
are another way to provide the non-standard form of Lagrangian.
In the one degree of freedom system, the form of Lagrangian can be an arbitrary appropriate function instead of
T-V
while the equation of motion (EOM) remains the same form, where
T=m\dot{x}^{2}/2
. For example, the quartic Lagrangian

\displaystyle L=\frac{T^{2}}{3}+2TV-V^{2},

(1)

and the multiplicative Lagrangian
Surawuttinack et al. 2016

\displaystyle L=m\lambda^{2}\left(e^{-\frac{\dot{x}^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}}+\frac{\dot{x}}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\dot{x}}e^{-\frac{v^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}}}dv\right)e^{-\frac{V}{m\lambda^{2}}},

(2)

are alternative descriptions of Newton’s law of motion

\displaystyle m\ddot{x}=-\frac{dV}{dx}.

(3)

Here,
\lambda
is a parameter with the unit of velocity.
These types of non-standard Lagrangians are not broadly discussed in
the
classical mechanics, since this modified Lagrangian does not lead to any effect in the classical EOM. Hence, working with standard form is more pleasant. However, the non-standard Lagrangians can give rise to the modified energy function, which leads to the different quantum theory
Henneaux and Shepley 1982
;
Nappi 1980
. One of the unsolved problems related to the non-uniqueness of Lagrangian is the strong-CP problem
Kuchimanchi 2012
;
Pendlebury et al. 2015
;
Wurm et al. 2019
. The strong CP violation term with arbitrary phase constant is allowed in the Lagrangian without the effect on the classical EOM but this term can give rise to the unnatural small quantum effect observed through neutron electric dipole moment
Kuchimanchi 2012
;
Pendlebury et al. 2015
;
Wurm et al. 2019
. In our hypothesis, the term in Lagrangian, which vanishes in classical EOM, might exist in the other types of fields such as scalar or fermion fields leading to a non-standard form of Lagrangian. However, the non-uniqueness of Lagrangian in the field theory has never been intensively mentioned because of various problems as follows.

\bullet
Renormalizablilty: If the Lagrangian of scalar or fermion fields is in non-standard form, the non-renormalizable term could exist. The renormalization is no longer applicable. Therefore, the non-perturbative method as the lattice calculation is used to compute the observable parameters.

\bullet
Uniqueness: From Henneaux’s work
Henneaux 1984
, the form of the scalar field Lagrangian in four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime seems to be unique.

In the recent work
Supanyo et al. 2022
, the non-uniqueness property of Lagrangian was re-interpreted to construct the non-standard Lagrangian of the complex scalar field. The total Lagrangian of the complex scalar field can be written in the linear combination between the standard and non-standard Lagrangians, which provide the same EOM in the appropriate energy limit. This non-standard Lagrangian is systematically solved from the inverse problem of the calculus of variations and this one is applied to explain various naturalness problems such as, hierarchy problem
Veltman 1981
;
Susskind 1979
and the strong CP-problem, without introducing the symmetry extension or proposing the new elementary particles.
It is quite natural to extend the idea of non-standard Lagrangian construction to the scalar field and fermion field with an insightful mathematical trick since there are the remains of unsolved problems outside the complex scalar field.

In this paper, we apply the non-uniqueness idea to implement the non-standard Lagrangian of fermion and the classical homogeneous background scalar fields. In Sec. II, we will show that, if the real scalar has a homogeneous property, such as the real scalar field in the homogeneous and isotropic universe in the dark energy
Ratra and Peebles 1988
;
Caldwell et al. 1998
;
Carroll 1998
;
Caldwell 2002
;
Carroll et al. 2003
and inflation
Linde 1983
;
Armendáriz-Picón et al. 1999
;
Shaposhnikov and Zenhäusern 2009
;
García-Bellido et al. 2011
;
Zee 1979
;
Futamase and Maeda 1989
;
Fakir and Unruh 1990
;
Bezrukov and Shaposhnikov 2008
, the Lagrangian is not unique. The Lagrangian of real scalar field and fermion field can be written in the multiplicative form. Moreover, we are going to show that the Lagrangian of the classical homogeneous background scalar field and fermion field can be written in the form of the linear combination between standard and non-standard ones while the classical EOM is still intact. In Sec-III, some applications of the non-uniqueness property of Lagrangian in the quantum field theory and cosmology are also discussed. In Sec-IV, this section is the conclusion.

II
Non-standard Lagrangian of the classical background scalar field from the non-uniqueness principle

II.1
Non-uniqueness of homogeneous real scalar field Lagrangian

We will briefly explain the idea of the non-uniqueness principle. In general, the relativistic motion of the real scalar field (
\phi
) is traditionally described through Klein-Gordon equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\phi+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi}=0,

(4)

where
V
is a scalar potential of
\phi
. In four-dimensional spacetime, the Lorentz invariant Lagrangian of this field is unique
Henneaux 1984
and can be written only in the standard form as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Standard}}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-V.

(5)

In other words, there is only a single form of the scalar field Lagrangian that can provide Eq. (
4
). However, according to an isotropic property of the universe, the classical background value of real scalar field
\phi_{b}
is spatially homogeneous. Then, the scalar field can be separated into

\displaystyle\phi(t,\boldsymbol{x})=\phi_{b}(t)+\varphi(t,\boldsymbol{x}),

(6)

where
\varphi(t,x)
is the fluctuation of a quantized field, while, in cosmological fashion,
\varphi(t,x)
is frequently called a perturbed field. The Lagrangian (
5
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Standard}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{Classical}}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{Quantum}}

(7)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Quantum}}=\mathcal{O}(\varphi)
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Classical}}\simeq\frac{\dot{\phi_{b}}^{2}}{2}-V(\phi_{b}).

(8)

We can see that the classical Lagrangian of
\phi_{b}
only depends on time while the spatial fluctuations are encoded in the Lagrangian of
\varphi
.

By analogy
\phi_{b}(t)
as
x(t)
in the Newtonian mechanics, it is obvious that this classical Lagrangian of the real scalar field is not unique as the Lagrangian
\eqref{LMbig1}-\eqref{LMbig2}
. Therefore, there are many non-standard Lagrangians in terms of
\phi_{b}
and
\dot{\phi}_{b}
providing the Klein-Gordon equation (
4
).

II.2
Non-standard Lagrangian of homogeneous real scalar field

In this contribution, we construct the non-standard Lagrangian of the scalar field by employing the inverse problem of the calculus of variations
Douglas 1941
;
Hojman and Urrutia 1981
;
Sarlet 1982
;
Surawuttinack et al. 2016
. We are interested in the multiplicative form of the non-standard Lagrangian given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Non-standard}}=F(X)f(\phi),

(9)

where
\phi=\phi(t,x)
is the real scalar field,
X=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi/2
. The functions
F
and
f
in Eq. (
9
) are unknown and these functions can be determined. By substituting Eq. (
6
) into Eq. (
9
), the multiplicative Lagrangian can be reorganized into the classical background part and the quantum fluctuation part as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Non-standard}}=F\left(X_{b}\right)f(\phi_{b})+\mathcal{O}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi,\dot{\varphi}).

(10)

where
X_{b}=\dot{\phi}_{b}^{2}/2
.
By ignoring the small fluctuation part
\mathcal{O}(\varphi,\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi,\dot{\varphi})
and employing the non-uniqueness, the EOM of the classical background part of Lagrangian (
8
) and (
10
) is required to be

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}_{b}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_{b}}=0.

(11)

Substituting Eq. (
10
) into the Euler-Lagrange equation,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi_{b}}-\partial_{0}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}_{b}}=0,

(12)

we have

\displaystyle 0=\left(F-2X_{b}\frac{\partial F}{\partial X_{b}}\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial\phi_{b}}-\ddot{\phi}_{b}f\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial X_{b}}+2X_{b}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial X_{b}^{2}}\right).

(13)

Then, replacing
\ddot{\phi}_{b}
in the second term with the help of the Klein-Gordon equation (
11
) and applying the separation variable method, Eq. (
13
) can be reorganized into

\displaystyle\frac{df}{d\phi_{b}}=-\frac{1}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}f\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_{b}},

(14)

\displaystyle 2X_{b}\frac{d^{2}F}{dX_{b}^{2}}+\frac{dF}{dX_{b}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}\left(F-2X_{b}\frac{dF}{dX_{b}}\right),

(15)

where
\Lambda^{4}
is an arbitrary positive constant with mass dimension
[\Lambda]=1
and
\epsilon=\pm 1
. We note that, mathematically,
\epsilon
can be
\pm i
, but these complex Lagrangians are not in the scope of our study. By solving Eq. (
14
) and Eq. (
15
) together with Eq. (
10
), the full multiplicative Lagrangian in 4-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime in Eq. (
9
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Non-standard}}=

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda^{2}\sqrt{\epsilon\pi X}e^{\frac{X}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}}\text{erf}\left(\sqrt{\frac{X}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}}\right)+\epsilon\Lambda^{4}\right)e^{-\frac{V+X}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}}-\epsilon\Lambda^{4}.

(16)

The EOM of the classical background field of Lagrangian (
II.2
) is indeed the Klein-Gordon equation, with an exponential factor, as

\displaystyle 0=e^{-\frac{\dot{\phi_{b}^{2}}+2V}{2\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}}\left(\ddot{\phi_{b}}+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_{b}}\right).

(17)

We can conclude that the Lagrangian (
II.2
) is an alternative form to explain the relativistic motion of the homogeneous real scalar field. With this non-uniqueness property, one can choose the standard Lagrangian, non-standard Lagrangian, or both types to obtain the Klein-Gordon equation. As a consequence, the Lagrangian of classical homogeneous real scalar field can be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=\alpha_{0}\mathcal{L}_{\text{Standard}}+\alpha\mathcal{L}_{\text{Non-standard},\Lambda},

(18)

where
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha
are arbitrary constant.

III
Non-standard Lagrangian of fermion field

III.1
Lagrangian

In this section, we construct the multiplicative Lagrangian of fermion field
\psi
coupling with a spacetime dependent current
J(x)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=G(i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi)g(\overline{\psi}J\psi),

(19)

which leads to the Dirac equation as

\displaystyle i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-J\psi=0,

(20)

where
J
can be parametrized in the form

\displaystyle J(x)=a(x)+b_{\mu}(x)\gamma^{\mu}+c_{\mu}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{5}.

(21)

Substituting Eq. (
19
) into Euler-Lagrange equation

\displaystyle 0=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\overline{\psi}}-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\overline{\psi}},

(22)

we have

\displaystyle 0=i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi g\frac{\partial G}{\partial Y}+J\psi G\frac{\partial g}{\partial y},

(23)

where
Y=i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi
and
y=\overline{\psi}J\psi
.
Then, by replacing the derivative term in Eq. (
23
) with Eq. (
20
) and applying the separation variable method, this equation can be reorganized into

\displaystyle\frac{dg}{dy}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}g,

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{dG}{dY}=

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}G,

(25)

where the property of
\epsilon
and
\Lambda
was discussed in previous section, see Eq. (
14
)-(
15
).
By solving Eq. (
24
)-(
25
), the multiplicative Lagrangian for fermion field that provides the Dirac equation (
20
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\epsilon\Lambda^{4}e^{\frac{i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\overline{\psi}J\psi}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}}.

(26)

For the same reason as the case of the real scalar field, the Lagrangian of the fermion field can be rewritten in the form of

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}=\beta_{0}\left(i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\overline{\psi}J\psi\right)+\beta\epsilon\Lambda^{4}e^{\frac{i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-\overline{\psi}J\psi}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}},

(27)

where
\beta_{0}
and
\beta
are arbitrary constant.

We note that the Lagrangian of the fermion field processes the non-uniqueness property in the different way from the Lagrangian of the real scalar field. In the case of the real scalar field, one requires the classical background to be homogeneous, see Eq. (
6
). Then, there exists an alternative form of the Lagrangian. On the other hand, in the case of the fermion field, one can solve the alternative Lagrangian without requiring a homogeneous background. Therefore, the non-uniqueness is applicable to the four-dimensional fluctuation of the spinor field, including the classical interpretation and quantization itself.

Discussions

IV.4
Chiral symmetry breaking

We discuss the feature of multiplicative Lagrangian to induce the non-zero vacuum expectation value of the bilinear fermion operator in the explicit chiral symmetry breaking.
Let us consider the toy model of the fermion field in multiplicative form coupled with complex scalar particle

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle\beta_{0}\left(i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-y\phi\overline{\psi}\psi\right)-\beta\Lambda^{4}e^{\frac{i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-y\psi\overline{\psi}\psi}{\epsilon\Lambda^{4}}}

\displaystyle+\partial_{\mu}\phi^{*}\partial^{\mu}\phi+m\phi^{*}\phi-\lambda(\phi^{*}\phi)^{2},

(66)

where we have defined
\epsilon=-1
,
a(x)=y\phi(x)
,
\gamma_{\mu}a^{\mu}=0
, and
\gamma_{\mu}c^{\mu}=0
. Here, the vacuum expectation value of
\phi
is

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{*}\phi\rangle=\frac{\mu^{2}}{\lambda}=v^{2},

(67)

so the symmetry breaking of
\phi
is required. With ignoring the Goldstone boson and scalar field, the fermion Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}=

\displaystyle\beta_{0}\left(i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-yv\overline{\psi}\psi\right)-\beta\Lambda^{4}e^{-\frac{i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-yv\overline{\psi}\psi}{\Lambda^{4}}}.

(68)

A mass of fermion can be defined from the EOM as

\displaystyle m_{f}=vy.

(69)

Then, we perform the field redefinition

\displaystyle\psi\to\frac{\psi}{\sqrt{\beta+\beta_{0}}}

(70)

to get canonical kinetic energy of fermion in low-energy limit.

\bullet
In the large momentum limit
i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\gg m_{f}\overline{\psi}\psi
, and
i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi\gg\Lambda^{4}
, the exponential term vanishes so the Lagrangian can be reduced into renormalized Lagrangian containing with a pure kinetic energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{f}\simeq\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta_{0}+\beta}i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi.

(71)

Therefore, the chiral symmetry is restored.

\bullet
On the other hand, in the small momentum limit
m_{f}\overline{\psi}\psi\gg i\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi
, the energy density of this fermion approximately contains only mass term

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\simeq\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta_{0}+\beta}m_{f}\overline{\psi}\psi+\beta\Lambda^{4}e^{\frac{m_{f}\overline{\psi}\psi}{(\beta_{0}+\beta)\Lambda^{4}}},

(72)

which explicitly breaks the chiral symmetry. To make the energy density bounded from below and the kinetic energy of fermion in high energy limit be positive, the conditions

\displaystyle\Lambda^{4}>0,~m_{f}>0,~\beta_{0}<0,~\beta<-\beta_{0}

(73)

are required. We find that the ground state of the bilinear fermion operator does not appear at
\langle\overline{\psi}\psi\rangle\neq 0
but, see Fig.
4

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\psi}\psi\rangle=\left(\beta_{0}+\beta\right)\frac{\Lambda^{4}}{m_{f}}\log\left(-\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta}\right).

(74)

With this non-zero value of the bilinear fermion operator, the chiral condensate can happen. Therefore, if the fermion field Lagrangian is written in the standard and multiplicative form, the chiral condensate can be triggered by spontaneous symmetry breaking of scalar particle.

V
Conclusion

The non-uniqueness principle can lead to the modification of the Lagrangian in various approaches.

\bullet
The complex scalar field
Supanyo et al. 2022
: Many non-standard Lagrangians yield the Klein-Gordon equation in the appropriate limit. Thus, within this limit, the Lagrangian is not unique.

\bullet
The real scalar field: By employing the homogeneous property of the classical background scalar field, the classical background Lagrangian is not unique.

\bullet
The fermion field: The fermion Lagrangian is explicitly non-uniqueness in four-dimensional spacetime.

Therefore, the linear-combination Lagrangian can be realized with the invariant of the EOM. Various phenomena in particle physics and cosmology, for example, ghost condensate, cosmological constant, and chiral condensate can be explored.

On Derkachov–Manashov
R

Abstract




In 2001–2013 Derkachov and Manashov with coauthors
obtained simple and natural expressions of
R
-matrices
for the principal series of representations of the groups
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
,

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
,
\mathrm{SO}(1,n)
. The Yang–Baxter identities
for these intertwining operators are kinds of multivariate hypergeometric transformations.
Derivations of the identities are based on calculations
’of physical level of rigor’ with divergent integrals.
Our purpose is a formal mathematical justification of these results.

3.1. Analytic continuation.

Consider a torus
\mathbb{T}^{m}
. Let
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Consider a formal product of operators of the form

V(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}):=B_{n}\dots B_{1},

(3.1)

where each factor
B_{j}
is
A_{pq}(\alpha_{j})
or
J_{r}(\alpha_{j})
. If all
\alpha_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then this operator
is unitary.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be a linear subspace in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
determined by a collection of linear equations
with real coefficients. Let
\mathcal{L}_{i{\mathbb{R}}}\subset\mathcal{L}
be the subspace consisting of vectors with
\alpha_{j}\in i{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem 3.1

Fix a subspace
\mathcal{L}
as above.
For any
\Phi
,
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{k})

the matrix element

\bigl\langle V(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\Phi,\Psi\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{k})}\Bigr|_{\mathcal{L}_{i{\mathbb{R}}}}

(3.2)

admits a meromorphic continuation to the subspace
\mathcal{L}
.

Remark.
It is clear (see the proof below), that
F:=\bigl\langle V(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\Phi,\Psi\bigr\rangle

is a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. We do not claim that a restriction of
F

to
\mathcal{L}
is well defined. There is a possibility that
\mathcal{L}
is contained in the indeterminacy set of
F
(i.e.,
an intersection of the hypersurface of poles and the hypersurface of zeros of a meromorphic function
F
), in the proof we
define a meromorphic function on such
\mathcal{L}
as a certain directional limit.

\boxtimes

Proof.

First, we notice that operator-valued functions
\alpha_{j}\mapsto A_{pq}(\alpha_{j})
are holomorphic in the domain
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\alpha_{j}>0

and strongly continuous upto
the boundary
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\alpha_{j}=0
. Operator-valued functions
\alpha_{j}\mapsto J_{r}(\alpha_{j})
are meromorphic
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\alpha_{j}>0

the boundary. Therefore, the function
V(\alpha,\dots,\alpha_{n})
is meromorphic in the domain
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\alpha_{j}>0

and strongly continuous up to the boundary

Let us write formally the kernel

It is an integral of a product of factors
|z_{r_{l}}-z_{t_{l}}|^{\mu_{l}}
, where
\mu_{l}
is
\alpha_{l}
or

Consider the integral

F(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}):=\bigl\langle V(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\Phi,\,\Psi\bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{k})}=\\

where

actually depends on coordinates

and

and has the form

\gamma(\mu)\,\int\prod_{l}|z_{r_{l}}-z_{t_{l}}|^{\mu_{l}}\,\Theta(z)\,\prod\frac{dz_{h}}{iz_{h}},

(3.4)

where

Poles of the integral are located on hyperplanes of

\sum_{j}b^{(1)}_{j}\mu_{j}+c^{(1)}+2s+1=0,\quad\dots,\quad\sum_{j}b^{(N)}_{j}\mu_{j}+c^{(N)}+2s+1=0,

3.2. In which functional spaces can the operators
\check{R}(\sigma)
be defined?

Theorem
3.1
shows that all operators, which are present in formulas (
1.5
)–(
1.7
),
are well-defined as operators sending
C^{\infty}
-functions on a torus to distributions on the same torus.
But this is not sufficient for a definition of products of such operators.

Operators
\check{R}(\sigma)
have the form

A_{12}(\alpha)\,J_{1}(\beta)\,J_{2}(\gamma)\,A_{12}(\delta),\qquad\text{where $\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta=0$.}

(3.6)

Operators
J(\beta)
,
J(\gamma)
are pseudo-differential, such operators have good properties in Sobolev spaces and other functional spaces
of Sobolev type, see, e.g.,
[
26
]
,
[
25
]
. On the other hand, multiplications by non-smooth functions
in such spaces is a difficult topic, which was a subject of numerous works, see, e.g.,
[
16
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
. Such operators
are defined
in Sobolev spaces only under strong restrictions. As far as I know, existing theorems
do not allow us to define products (
3.6
).

However, there is some hope for the following exotic way (
3.6
).

Denote by
|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\nu}\,C^{\infty}\,(\mathbb{T}^{2})
the space of functions on torus of the form

|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\nu}f(x_{1},x_{2})
, where
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We assume that
the power
\nu
and all other powers below are
\neq-1
,
-2
,
-3
, ….

We start with a space
|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\mu}\,C^{\infty}\,(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

1) The operator
A_{12}(\delta)
sends the space
|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

to

|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\delta+\mu}\,C^{\infty}\,(\mathbb{T}^{2}).

2) The operator

J_{2}(\gamma)
sends the space

|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\delta+\mu}\,C^{\infty}\,(\mathbb{T}^{2})
to the space
|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\gamma+\delta+\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})+C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
,
see, e.g.,
[
24
]
, Sect. 16.

3) The operator
J_{1}(\beta)
sends the space
|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\gamma+\delta+\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})

to the space
|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\beta+\gamma+\delta+\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})+C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Also, it sends the space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
to itself.
So we get an element of the space

|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\beta+\gamma+\delta+\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})+C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}).

4) Finally, applying
A(\alpha)
we come to

|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}).

So we can define an operator (
3.6
) as an operator

|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\,\longrightarrow\,|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\mu}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha}\,C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}).

However, these arguments are not sufficient for a definition of products of such operators.

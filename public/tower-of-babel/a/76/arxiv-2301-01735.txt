In this paper, we focus our attention on a new point of view for the intrinsically Lipschitz graphs in the Franchi-Serapioni-SerraCassano sense

They introduced and analized this notion in order to establish a good notion of rectifiability in a particular metric spaces called subRiemannian Carnot groups

In the usual way, Federer

However, Ambrosio and Kirchheim

The idea is similar to Euclidean case: firstly, they introduce suitable cones called intrinsic cones which are not equivalent with the Euclidean ones; and, then, they say that a map
\varphi
is intrinsic Lipschitz if it is possible to have for any point

Recently, Le Donne and the author generalize this concept in metric spaces (see
[

]
).

A basic difference is the following: in Franchi, Serapioni and Serra Cassano approach, they consider intrinsically Lipschitz maps. On the other hand, in our approach we consider the graphs and this a bit change is so important because:

•

The setting are more general: the class of the metric spaces is larger than the class of Carnot groups.

•

The proofs are elegantly short and simple.

•

We use basic mathematical tools in the proofs.

Definition 1.1

(Intrinsic Hölder section)
.

Let

3.1.

In this section, we consider the case of intrinsically Lipschitz sections, i.e.,

Let
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}\to Y

Normed space: Version 2

In the usual case, we have that
d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for any point
x,y\in X;
on the other hand, in our intrinsic context, in general, we have that

d(f(x),\pi^{-1}(y))\neq d(f(y),\pi^{-1}(x)),

for every

In particular, it holds

(8)

\displaystyle d(f(y),\pi^{-1}(x))-d(f(z),\pi^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle d(f(x),\pi^{-1}(y))-d(f(x),\pi^{-1}(z))

\displaystyle\nleq d(f(y),f(z)),\quad\mbox{for some }x,y,z\in Y.

In fact, for any fixed

if we choose

such that

we deduce that

\displaystyle d(f(y),\pi^{-1}(x))-d(f(z),\pi^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle\leq d(f(y),a)-d(f(z),\pi^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle\leq d(f(y),f(z))+d(f(z),a)-d(f(z),\pi^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle=d(f(y),f(z)),

i.e., the first inequality of (

On the other hand, for the second inequality in (

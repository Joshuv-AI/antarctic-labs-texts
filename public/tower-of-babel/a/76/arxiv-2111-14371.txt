Rotoinversion-symmetric bulk-hinge correspondence and its applications to higher-order Weyl semimetals
Yutaro Tanaka, Ryo Takahashi, Ryo Okugawa, Shuichi Murakami

Abstract
We give a bulk-hinge correspondence for higher-order topological phases protected by rotoinversion C 4 ​ ℐ C_{4}\mathcal{I} symmetry in magnetic systems. Our approach allows us to show the emergence of the chiral hinge modes only from the information of the C 4 ​ ℐ C_{4}\mathcal{I} eigenvalues at the high-symmetry points in the Brillouin zone. In addition, based on the bulk-hinge correspondence, we propose a class of higher-order Weyl semimetals (HOWSMs) being Weyl semimetals with hinge modes and Fermi-arc surface states. The HOWSM is characterized by topological invariants for three-dimensional higher-order topological insulators, and the topological invariants are determined by the C 4 ​ ℐ C_{4}\mathcal{I} symmetry eigenvalues at the high-symmetry points. This HOWSM has chiral hinge modes as a direct consequence of the three-dimensional higher-order topology in the bulk.

On the other hand,

)- or (

The former has codimension
d_{c}=2
, and the latter has codimension
d_{c}=3
, and therefore the former and the latter are second-order and third-order topological phases, respectively.
Moreover, the coexistence of boundary modes with
d_{c}=m-1
and those with
d_{c}=m
have been studied in recent years and is referred to as hybrid-order topological phase
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Kooi et al. 2020
;
Szumniak et al. 2020
;

Murakami et al. 2017
and Dirac semimetals

;

;

;

;
Okugawa and Murakami 2014

According to the classification of topological phases (key space groups) in Ref.

\mathcal{I}
symmetry and those protected by rotoinversion

C_{4}
is a four-fold rotation symmetry.
The chiral hinge modes protected by
\mathcal{I}
symmetry have been explored in detail in Refs.
Khalaf 2018
;
Matsugatani and Watanabe 2018
;
Ono and Watanabe 2018
;
Tanaka et al. 2020b
;
Takahashi et al. 2020
;
Tanaka et al. 2020a
,
and the bulk-hinge correspondence for the HOTPs has been shown in Refs.

;

.
Similarly,

-symmetric HOTPs have been studied in Refs.
Schindler et al. 2018a
;
Ezawa 2018b
;
van Miert and Ortix 2018
. Nevertheless, they are based on
\bm{k}\cdot\bm{p}
surface theory or Wannier approach, and these approaches cannot be applied to some systems as we discuss in Appendix
A
.
Furthermore, the conventional discussions are insufficient in the following two points.
First, the indicator does not explicitly appear in the discussions, and the role of the indicator is not clear in the bulk-hinge correspondence.
Second, the discussions so far make use of certain properties of the models other than the indicator.
Since the topological phase is characterized only by the indicator, its bulk-hinge correspondence should be explained by the indicator alone in principle.
Thus, a general proof of the bulk-hinge correspondence characterized by the indicator has not been given.

In this paper, we show the bulk-hinge correspondence for HOTPs protected by
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry.
Our approach in this paper allows us to show the emergence of the chiral hinge modes only from the information of the
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry eigenvalues at the high-symmetry points.
In addition, we propose a class of the HOWSMs having chiral hinge modes protected by
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry based on our theory of bulk-hinge correspondence.
This HOWSM as a direct consequence of the 3D higher-order topology in the bulk is regarded as a generalization of the 3D HOTIs protected by
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry [Figs.
1
(a) and
1

Unlike the HOWSMs in the previous works
Ghorashi et al. 2020

Wang et al. 2020

Because our HOWSM has the Fermi-arc surface states and the chiral hinge modes, it can be an ideal platform to study the interplay between first-order and second-order topology as the hybrid-order topological phase.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we propose the HOWSM with the chiral hinge modes protected by
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry in terms of a tight-binding model.
In Sec.
III
, we show that the emergence of chiral hinge modes in insulators and WSMs with
C_{4}\mathcal{I}

C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry eigenvalues. It is a proof of bulk-hinge correspondence for the HOTPs protected by
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry. Conclusion and discussion are given in Sec.
IV

II.1
HOWSM protected by
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry

II.1.1
Topological invariants

Here, we discuss topological invariants for the HOTPs protected by
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
, where
C_{4z}
is a four-fold rotational symmetry about the
z
-axis.
The operation
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
changes wave vectors as
(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})\rightarrow(k_{y},-k_{x},-k_{z})
. Therefore, this system has four
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
-invariant points in the
k
-space:
K_{4}=\{\Gamma=(0,0,0)
,
M=(\pi,\pi,0)
,
Z=(0,0,\pi)
,
A=(\pi,\pi,\pi)\}
.
Figure
2
(a) shows 1/8 of the 3D BZ and the high-symmetry points.
To capture the HOTPs protected by
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry, let us introduce the following indices in terms of the numbers of occupied states with symmetry eigenvalues at
K_{4}
:

\displaystyle\chi^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\biggl[

\displaystyle n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(Z)-n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(Z)+n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(A)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(A)-n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(\Gamma)+n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(M)+n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(M)\biggr]\ \ {\rm mod\ 2},

(1)

where
n_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(\bm{k})
,
n_{-\frac{\pi}{4}}(\bm{k})
,
n_{+\frac{3\pi}{4}}(\bm{k})
, and
n_{-\frac{3\pi}{4}}(\bm{k})
are the numbers of occupied states with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
eigenvalues,
e^{+i\frac{\pi}{4}}
,
e^{-i\frac{\pi}{4}}
,
e^{+i\frac{3\pi}{4}}
, and
e^{-i\frac{3\pi}{4}}
at the
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
-invariant wavevector
\bm{k}
, respectively.
Here we assume that the Chern numbers at
k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
are zero.
This ensures that
\chi^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}
are integers.
When the Chern numbers are nonzero, the hinge modes will be hidden by chiral surface states due to a nonzero Chern number. In this paper, to study the hinge modes, we restrict ourselves to systems with the Chern numbers at
k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
being zero.
The topological invariants
\chi^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}
can be defined both for insulators and for WSMs when the bulk and the surfaces of the WSMs are gapped at
k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
. Figures
2
(a) and
2
(b) show two examples of the
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry eigenvalues at the high-symmetry points satisfying
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}=\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}=1
and
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}=1,\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}=0
, respectively.
In Sec.
III
, we will show that when
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
mod 2, chiral hinge modes appear in insulators or WSMs.

II.1.2
Phase transition for the HOWSM

Now we discuss how to construct a HOWSM protected by
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry.
The HOWSM can be realized by adding a perturbation breaking time-reversal (
\mathcal{T}
) symmetry to a 3D non-magnetic Dirac semimetal with
C_{4z}
symmetry.
We start with a 3D Dirac semimetal with two four-fold degenerate points (Dirac points) on the rotation axis [Fig.
2
(c)].
Then, we perturb it to split each Dirac point into a pair of Weyl points on the rotation axis while preserving
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry.
The topological phase transition for the HOWSM can be induced by magnetic doping or an external magnetic field
Wang et al. 2012
;
Du et al. 2015
without breaking
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry in the Dirac semimetals.

II.1.3
Tight-binding model of the HOWSM

According to the above discussion, we introduce a spinful tight-binding model of a HOWSM with

\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
and
\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
mod 2.
This tight-binding model is defined on the simple tetragonal lattice, and each site has four orbitals.
In the following, we set the lattice constants to 1.
The Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\bm{k})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})+\mathcal{H}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(\bm{k})+\mathcal{H}_{z}(\bm{k}),

(2)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})\equiv

\displaystyle\biggl(-m+c\sum_{j=x,y,z}\cos k_{j}\biggr)\sigma_{0}\tau_{z}

\displaystyle-v\biggl(\sum_{j=x,y}\sin k_{j}\sigma_{j}\biggr)\tau_{x},

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(\bm{k})\equiv

\displaystyle v_{s}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})\sigma_{0}\tau_{x}

\displaystyle+v_{t}(\cos k_{x}-\cos k_{y})\sigma_{0}\tau_{y},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{z}(\bm{k})=

\displaystyle v_{z}\sin k_{z}\sigma_{z}\tau_{x}+B_{z}\sigma_{z}\tau_{0},

(5)

where
\sigma_{j}
,
\tau_{j}
(
j=x,y,z
) are the Pauli matrices corresponding to the spin and the orbital degrees of freedom, respectively, and
\sigma_{0}
,
\tau_{0}
are the

\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})
is a model for a 3D Dirac semimetal with
\mathcal{T}
symmetry, where
\mathcal{T}
can be expressed as
\mathcal{T}=-i\sigma_{y}\tau_{0}K
with

being the complex conjugation. On the other hand, the second term of
\mathcal{H}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(\bm{k})
breaks
\mathcal{T}
symmetry.
In addition,
\mathcal{H}_{z}(\bm{k})
makes the Dirac points of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})
split into Weyl points.

II.2
Topological invariants for insulators with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}

So far we mainly discussed HOWSMs with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry. In this subsection, we discuss the topological invariants for insulators with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry. For insulators, we can greatly simplify our indices
\chi^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}
and connect them to the symmetry-based indicators in insulators
Kruthoff et al. 2017
;
Po et al. 2017
;
Bradlyn et al. 2017
;
Watanabe et al. 2018
;
Ono and Watanabe 2018
;
Elcoro et al. 2021
by considering the two-fold rotation (

) symmetry about the

Let
n^{(+\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(\bm{k})
and
n^{(-\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(\bm{k})
denote the number of occupied states with the
+i
and
-i

symmetry at high-symmetry points, respectively.

C_{2z}
symmetry is preserved on the high-symmetry lines
\Gamma
-

and

and

\displaystyle n^{(+\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(\Gamma)=n^{(+\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(Z),\ n^{(-\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(\Gamma)=n^{(-\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(Z),

(7)

\displaystyle n^{(+\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(M)=n^{(+\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(A),\ n^{(-\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(M)=n^{(-\frac{\pi}{2})}_{C_{2z}}(A).

(8)

The states with the

\displaystyle n_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(Z)+n_{-\frac{3\pi}{4}}(Z)=n_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(\Gamma)+n_{-\frac{3\pi}{4}}(\Gamma),

(9)

\displaystyle n_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(A)+n_{-\frac{3\pi}{4}}(A)=n_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(M)+n_{-\frac{3\pi}{4}}(M).

\displaystyle\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\Gamma_{i}\in K_{4}}n_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(\Gamma_{i})\ \ {\rm mod}\ 2.

being zero.
This assumption leads to
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}+\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 0
mod 2.
This allows two possibilities;
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 0
or 1 modulo 2. They are distinguished in terms of the second
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
index. Namely, when
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
mod 2, the second
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=1
, and it is the HOTPs protected by
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry. In this way, our indices match with the symmetry-based indicators in the previous works.

In the previous work
Ono and Watanabe 2018
;
Peng et al. 2021
, a quantity
\mu_{4}
defined by

\mu_{4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{\Gamma_{i}\in K_{4}}\sum_{\alpha}e^{i\alpha}n_{\alpha}(\Gamma_{i}),

(14)

is proposed as a symmetry-based indicator in the magnetic space group
\#81.33
, where
\alpha
runs over
\alpha=\pm\pi/4,\pm 3\pi/4
.
According to Refs.
Ono and Watanabe 2018
;
Peng et al. 2021
,
\mu_{4}=2
or
\mu_{4}=2i
correspond to nontrivial phases.
Furthermore, in the previous work
Elcoro et al. 2021
, the symmetry-based indicator is given by

z_{2}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\Gamma_{i}\in K_{4}}\Bigl(n_{-\frac{\pi}{4}}(\Gamma_{i})-n_{+\frac{3\pi}{4}}(\Gamma_{i})\Bigr)\ \ {\rm mod\ 2},

(15)

in the magnetic space group
\#81.33
. According to Ref.
Elcoro et al. 2021
,
z_{2}=1
corresponds to nontrivial phases.
When the Chern numbers at
k_{z}=0
and
\pi
are zero, both
\mu_{4}=2,2i
and
z_{2}=1
are equivalent to
\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(+)}\equiv\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(-)}\equiv 1
mod 2.
While the indices
\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(\pm)}
detect the same HOTP as the symmetry-based indicators
\mu_{4}
and
z_{2}
, we propose the indices
\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(\pm)}
in this paper to be the useful indices to detect the HOTP protected by
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry from the discussions of spectral flows in Sec.
III
.
In contrast, the formula of the symmetry-based indicator
\mu_{4}
and
z_{2}
may not have a direct physical interpretation.

III
Bulk-hinge correspondence for higher-order topological phases

In this section, we show the bulk-hinge correspondence for HOTPs in insulators and WSMs with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry only from the
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
eigenvalues at the high-symmetry points in the BZ. From this bulk-hinge correspondence, we can confirm that the chiral hinge modes of our model in the previous section originate from the nontrivial indices
\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(\pm)}
introduced in the previous section.
In the following discussion, we show that when our indices
\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(\pm)}
[see Eq.(
II.1.1
)] satisfy
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
mod 2, the chiral hinge modes appear.

III.1
Setup of the problem

Here, we consider a general 3D spinful electronic system with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry.
In addition, we assume that the system is an insulator and a WSM with the bulk being gapped at

and
k_{z}=\pi
.
We do not assume

In the following discussion, we assume that the surfaces are gapped at

and

in the system with finite sizes in the

- and

Here, we consider a large but finite system in the

- and

with

and

III.2
Cutting procedures and spectral flows

Here, we show the bulk-hinge correspondence with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry by considering changes in the energy spectra through changing the cutting parameter.
When the cutting parameter is
\lambda=1
, the wave vectors in the
x
- and
y
-directions are

k_{i}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{i}}m_{i}\ \ (-M\leq m_{i}\leq M)\ \ (i=x,y),

because of the periodic boundary condition in the
x
- and
y
-directions.
On the other hand, in the case with
\lambda=-1
, the wave vectors are given by

k_{i}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{i}}m_{i}+\frac{\pi}{L_{i}}\ \ (-M\leq m_{i}\leq M)\ \ (i=x,y)

because of the antiperiodic boundary condition.
The proof of this is given in Appendix
E.1
.
We note that here
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
are well-defined only for
\lambda=\pm 1
because of the periodic boundary conditions (
\lambda=1
) and the anti-periodic boundary conditions (
\lambda=-1
) [see Figs.
4
(b) and
4
(c)].
For both the cases with
\lambda=1
and
\lambda=-1
, each wave function
\psi_{m}
at non-
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetric momenta
\bm{k}
with
k_{z}=0
or
\pi
can always be mixed with the wave functions at
\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}}\bm{k}
,
(\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}})^{2}\bm{k}
and
(\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}})^{3}\bm{k}
to construct four eigenstates of
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
:

\displaystyle\ket{\phi_{\alpha}}\equiv\ket{\psi_{m}(\bm{k})}+e^{-i\alpha}\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}}\ket{\psi_{m}(\bm{k})}

\displaystyle+e^{-2i\alpha}(\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}})^{2}\ket{\psi_{m}(\bm{k})}+e^{-3i\alpha}(\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}})^{3}\ket{\psi_{m}(\bm{k})},

(19)

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{4z}\hat{\mathcal{I}}\ket{\phi_{\alpha}}=e^{i\alpha}\ket{\phi_{\alpha}},

(20)

where
\alpha=\pm\frac{\pi}{4},\pm\frac{3\pi}{4}
.
Therefore, states at non-
{C}_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetric points do not contribute to
\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{{C}_{4z}\mathcal{I}}
.

On the other hand, the states at
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
-symmetric momenta contribute to
\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(k_{z})|_{\lambda}
.
When
\lambda=1
, the wave vector
(k_{x},k_{y})
can take a value
(0,0)
, but not
(\pi,\pi)
[Fig.
4
(b)].
When
\lambda=-1
, the wave vector
(k_{x},k_{y})
can take a value
(\pi,\pi)
, but not (0,0) [Fig.
4
(c)].
Therefore,
\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(k_{z})|_{\lambda=\pm 1}
is determined by the number of occupied states with the
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
eigenvalues at
\bm{k}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z\mathcal{I}}}(0)|_{\lambda=1}=n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(\Gamma)-n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(\Gamma),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z\mathcal{I}}}(0)|_{\lambda=-1}=n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(M)-n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(M),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z\mathcal{I}}}(\pi)|_{\lambda=1}=n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(Z)-n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(Z),

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z\mathcal{I}}}(\pi)|_{\lambda=-1}=n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(A)-n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(A),

(21)

where
\Gamma
,
M
,

, and

are the high-symmetry points in the BZ defined in Sec.
II.1
[see Fig.
2

Next, we discuss changes of the band structures while continuously changing the parameter
\lambda
from
\lambda=-1
to
\lambda=1
. We call these changes of the band energy in this process “spectral flows”. When the bulk and the surfaces are gapped at
k_{z}=0
and
\pi

(

,
\pm 3\pi/4

From the properties (i) and (ii), we find that when
N_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(k_{z},\lambda)
decreases by an integer

in changing

from

to

in changing
\lambda
from

to

in changing

from

to

N
in changing
\lambda
from
\lambda=0
to

.
In this way, the properties (i) and (ii) restrict changes of
N_{+\frac{\pi}{4}}(k_{z},\lambda)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(0)|_{\lambda=0}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\pm)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}(\pi)|_{\lambda=0}=

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(n_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}(A)-n_{\mp\frac{3\pi}{4}}(A)\right).

\displaystyle\nu^{(\pm)}(k_{z})

sector.

k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
. Therefore, these states crossing the Fermi level are localized at the hinges in the real space, namely, these gapless states are hinge modes.
Therefore, the hinge modes always appear in insulators with
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry when
\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
mod 2.

Under the condition that the bulk and the surfaces are gapped at
k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
,

(d)].
In Fig.

The model of the HOWSM in Sec.

corresponds to this case.
On the other hand, another case of HOWSM is possible, where the Fermi-arc surface states account for the nonzero
\nu^{(\pm)}(\pi)-\nu^{(\pm)}(0)
, and the chiral hinge modes do not appear [Fig.

\chi^{(+)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 1
mod 2 and
\chi^{(-)}_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}\equiv 0
mod 2, it leads to

mod 2 and
\nu^{(-)}(\pi)-\nu^{(-)}(0)\equiv 0
mod 2 [Fig.

The nonzero value of
\nu^{(+)}(\pi)-\nu^{(+)}(0)
means that there should be gapless modes.
Nonetheless, in this case,
\nu^{(+)}(\pi)-\nu^{(+)}(0)
and
\nu^{(-)}(\pi)-\nu^{(-)}(0)
are not equal. Therefore, these gapless modes are not hinge modes because a hinge mode at a given
k_{z}

(\neq 0,\pi)
lying at two hinges related by
C_{2z}
symmetry, leads to two modes, one with
C_{2z}=+i
and the other with
C_{2z}=-i
.
In this way, from Eq. (
III.2
), we can determine whether the gapless modes cross the Fermi level an odd number of times or an even number of times although whether the gapless modes are the hinge modes or the Fermi-arc surface states are not determined only by Eq. (
III.2
).

IV
Conclusion and discussion

In summary, we show that when the topological invariants
\chi_{C_{4z}\mathcal{I}}^{(\pm)}
are nontrivial, the chiral hinge modes appear, and thereby show general bulk-hinge correspondence for HOTPs.
Our approach gives direct evidence to show the bulk-hinge correspondence only from the information of symmetry eigenvalues without relying on Dirac surface theory or on Wannier representations used in the previous works.
In addition, we proposed a class of HOWSMs having the chiral hinge modes protected by rotoinversion
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry in this paper.
Unlike the HOWSMs in the previous works, which are characterized by topological invariants for the 2D HOTI and are realized by stacking the 2D HOTIs, the HOWSM in this paper has the chiral hinge modes characterized by the topological invariants for 3D HOTIs.

Lastly, we discuss the possibilities of the material realizations of our HOWSMs with chiral hinge modes.
Our model
\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})
has similar energy spectra to a Dirac semimetal
{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}

Wang et al. 2013
;
Yi et al. 2014
;
Ali et al. 2014
[see Appendix
F
].
By adding perturbations to this model, we construct both the model of the HOWSMs protected by
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry in Sec.
II
and the model protected by
\mathcal{I}
symmetry in Appendix
B
.
In addition, the recent experiment shows that
{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}
has hinge modes
Li et al. 2020
. In terms of this experimental result and our theoretical results, we propose that our HOWSMs may be realized by adding perturbations such as magnetic doping or an external magnetic field to
{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}

Wang et al. 2013
;
Liu et al. 2014
without breaking
C_{4}\mathcal{I}
symmetry or
\mathcal{I}
symmetry.

Schindler et al. 2018a
and (ii) Wannier approach
van Miert and Ortix 2018
.
In the approach (i), one starts from the surface Dirac Hamiltonian with a Dirac mass term, and show that the Dirac mass term has a different sign between the two surfaces sharing the same hinge, resulting in the emergence of the chiral hinge modes.
In the approach (ii), one calculates the Wannier centers for two pseudo-2D systems corresponding to two subspaces at
k_{z}=0
and
k_{z}=\pi
. When excess corner charges obtained from the Wannier centers in the two subspaces are different, chiral hinge modes appear as a pumping of the corner charges.
However, both of these approaches study only the special cases of
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
-symmetric systems.
Whether they hold in general remains unclear, except in cases that are adiabatically connected to those cases. The approach (i) cannot be applied to cases where the surface cannot be represented by Dirac Hamiltonian. The approach (ii) is based on Wannier representations and assumes that electronic states are described in terms of localized Wannier functions. However, some electronic states, such as those with fragile topology
Po et al. 2018
, cannot be described in terms of localized Wannier states.
On the other hand, our approach allows us to show emergence of the chiral hinge modes only from the
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
symmetry eigenvalues without relying on Dirac surface Hamiltonian or on Wannier representations.

\displaystyle U_{x}U_{y}\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda)(U_{x}U_{y})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=-M}^{M}\sum_{y=-M}^{M}H_{0}(k_{z})\otimes\ket{x,y}\bra{x,y}

\displaystyle+\sum_{x=-M}^{M-1}\sum_{y=-M}^{M}\Bigl[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{x}}}H_{x}\otimes\ket{x+1,y}\bra{x,y}+\rm{H.c.}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\sum_{y=-M}^{M}\Bigl[-\lambda e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{x}}}H_{x}\otimes\ket{-M,y}\bra{M,y}+\rm{H.c.}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\sum_{x=-M}^{M}\sum_{y=-M}^{M-1}\Bigl[e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{y}}}H_{y}\otimes\ket{x,y+1}\bra{x,y}+\rm{H.c.}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\sum_{x=-M}^{M}\Bigl[-\lambda e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{x}}}H_{y}\otimes\ket{x,-M}\bra{x,M}+\rm{H.c.}\Bigr].

(51)

When
\lambda=-1
, this equation can be expressed as

\displaystyle U_{x}U_{y}\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda=-1)(U_{x}U_{y})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{x=-M}^{M}\sum_{y=-M}^{M}H_{0}(k_{z})\otimes\ket{x,y}\bra{x,y}

\displaystyle+\Bigr[H_{x}\otimes\tilde{T}_{x}+H_{y}\otimes\tilde{T}_{y}+\rm{H.c.}\Bigr],

(52)

where
\tilde{T}_{x}\equiv e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{x}}}T_{x}
and
\tilde{T}_{y}\equiv e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{y}}}T_{y}
.
The eigenvalues of
T_{i}
and
\tilde{T}_{i}
(
i=x,y
) are defined by
e^{ik_{i}}
and
e^{i\tilde{k}_{i}}
, respectively, and these wave vectors are give by

k_{i}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{i}}m_{i}\ \ (-M\leq m_{i}\leq M),

(53)

\tilde{k}_{i}=\frac{2\pi}{L_{i}}m_{i}+\frac{\pi}{L_{i}}\ \ (-M\leq m_{i}\leq M),

(54)

with
i=x,y
. From this, we conclude that
U_{x}U_{y}\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda=-1)(U_{x}U_{y})^{-1}
is unitary equivalent to
\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda=1)
with the wave vector being shifted as
k_{i}\rightarrow k_{i}+\frac{\pi}{L_{i}}
.
In other words, by changing
\lambda
from
-1
to 1, the wave vectors are shifted as

k_{i}=2m_{i}\pi/L_{i}\rightarrow(2m_{i}+1)\pi/L_{i}
.

E.2
Proof of
E(k_{z},\lambda)=E(k_{z},-\lambda)
for spectral flows

Here, we show that the spectral flows in the band gap are symmetric under
\lambda\leftrightarrow-\lambda
when
L_{x}

(=L_{y})
is sufficiently large.
By approximating
e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{x}}}\approx 1
and
e^{i\frac{\pi}{L_{y}}}\approx 1
, from Eq. (
E.1
), we obtain the following equation:

U_{x}U_{y}\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda)(U_{x}U_{y})^{-1}\approx\mathcal{H}(k_{z},-\lambda),

(55)

where
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
are sufficiently large.
Therefore, under the unitary transformations
U_{x}
and
U_{y}
,
\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda)
can be transformed into
\mathcal{H}(k_{z},-\lambda)
, and these two Hamiltonian has the same energy:
E(k_{z},\lambda)=E(k_{z},-\lambda)
. In other words, the spectral flows are symmetric under
\lambda\leftrightarrow-\lambda
when
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
are sufficiently large.

E.3
Spectral flows with the opposite rotoinversion eigenvalues

Next, we give a proof that when a state with the energy
E(k_{z},\lambda)
has a
e^{i\alpha}
eigenvalue of
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
(
\alpha=\pm\pi/4
,
\pm 3\pi/4
), a state with the energy
E(k_{z},-\lambda)
has the
-e^{i\alpha}
eigenvalue.
In the previous subsection, we showed that
U_{x}U_{y}\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda)(U_{x}U_{y})^{-1}\approx\mathcal{H}(k_{z},-\lambda)
when
L_{x}
and
L_{y}
are sufficiently large.
Therefore, the following equation holds:

\mathcal{H}(k_{z},-\lambda)U_{x}U_{y}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}=EU_{x}U_{y}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)},

(56)

when
\mathcal{H}(k_{z},\lambda)
has an eigenstate
\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}
with the
e^{i\alpha}
eigenvalue (
\alpha=\pm\pi/4
,
\pm 3\pi/4
) of
C_{4z}\mathcal{I}
.
From this, we obtain the following equation:

\displaystyle C_{4z}\mathcal{I}U_{x}U_{y}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}=

\displaystyle U_{y}U_{x}^{\dagger}C_{4z}\mathcal{I}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(U_{x}^{\dagger})^{2}U_{x}U_{y}e^{i\alpha}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{2\pi}{L_{x}}\hat{x}}U_{x}U_{y}e^{i\alpha}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-e^{i\alpha}U_{x}U_{y}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)},

(57)

where
x\simeq\pm M
. Therefore, the localized states
\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}
and
U_{x}U_{y}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}(k_{z},\lambda)}
at the boundary
x\simeq\pm M
have opposite eigenvalues,
e^{i\alpha}
and
-e^{i\alpha}
, respectively.

Appendix F
Relationship between
\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\vec{k})
and an effective Hamiltonian for
{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}

In this appendix, we show that our model
\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})
for a Dirac semimetal [Eq. (
3
)] has similar energy spectra to an effective
\bm{k}\cdot\bm{p}
model for
{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}
known as a Dirac semimetal
Wang et al. 2013
;
Yi et al. 2014
;
Ali et al. 2014
.

{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}
respects space groups
\#137
(
P4_{2}/nmc1^{\prime}
) and
\#142
(
I4_{1}/acd1^{\prime}
) in room-temperature and high-temperature, respectively
Wang et al. 2013
;
Ali et al. 2014
.
When
{\rm Cd}_{3}{\rm As}_{2}
has such centrosymmetric structures,
the effective Hamiltonian
Wang et al. 2013
around the
\Gamma
point is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(\bm{k})=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}(\bm{k})+\begin{pmatrix}M(\bm{k})&Ak_{+}&0&0\\
Ak_{-}&-M(\bm{k})&0&0\\
0&0&M(\bm{k})&-Ak_{-}\\
0&0&-Ak_{+}&-M(\bm{k})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle+O(k^{2}),

(58)

in terms of the four basis
\ket{S_{J=\frac{1}{2}},J_{z}=\frac{1}{2}}
,
\ket{P_{J=\frac{3}{2}},J_{z}=\frac{3}{2}}
,
\ket{S_{J=\frac{1}{2}},J_{z}=-\frac{1}{2}}
, and
\ket{P_{J=\frac{3}{2}},J_{z}=-\frac{3}{2}}
,
where
\epsilon_{0}(\bm{k})=C_{0}+C_{1}k^{2}_{z}+C_{2}(k^{2}_{x}+k^{2}_{y})
,
k_{\pm}=k_{x}\pm ik_{y}
, and
M(\bm{k})=M_{0}-M_{1}k^{2}_{z}-M_{2}(k^{2}_{x}+k^{2}_{y})
with parameters
M_{0}<0
,
M_{1}<0
, and
M_{2}<0
.
We can rewrite this Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(\bm{k})=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}(\bm{k})+A(k_{x}\sigma_{z}\tau_{x}-k_{y}\sigma_{0}\tau_{y})

\displaystyle+M(\bm{k})\sigma_{0}\tau_{z}+O(k^{2}),

(59)

where
\sigma_{i}
and
\tau_{i}
(
i=x,y,z
) are the Pauli matrices corresponding to
J_{z}=\pm J
and
J=\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}
, respectively.
By using a unitary matrix

\displaystyle U_{D}=\sigma_{x}\frac{\tau_{x}-i\tau_{y}}{2}+\sigma_{z}\frac{\tau_{x}+i\tau_{y}}{2},

(60)

we can obtain the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\bm{k})\equiv

\displaystyle U_{D}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(\bm{k})U^{\dagger}_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}(\bm{k})+A(k_{x}\sigma_{x}\tau_{x}+k_{y}\sigma_{y}\tau_{x})

\displaystyle-M(\bm{k})\sigma_{0}\tau_{z}+O(k^{2}).

(61)

The energy dispersion of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\bm{k})
is given by
E(\bm{k})=\epsilon_{0}(\bm{k})\pm\sqrt{M(\bm{k})+A^{2}k_{+}k_{-}}
, resulting in a pair of Dirac points at
(k_{x},k_{y},k_{z})=(0,0,\pm\sqrt{\frac{M_{0}}{M_{1}}})
. If we set
\epsilon_{0}(\bm{k})
to be zero,
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\bm{k})
is identical to the expansion of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm DSM}(\bm{k})
[Eq. (
3
)] to the second order
k^{2}
around the
\Gamma
point.

Corrections to n s n_{s} and n t n_{t} from high scale physics Preprint: DESY-16-181
Benedict J. Broy

Abstract
When modelling inflaton fluctuations as a free quantum scalar field, the initial vacuum is conventionally imposed at the infinite past. This is called the Bunch-Davies (BD) vacuum. If however an asymptotically Minkowskian past does not exist, this requires modifications. We derive corrections to the scalar spectral index n s n_{s} and the tensor tilt n t n_{t} descending from arbitrary mixed states or from explicit non-BD initial conditions. The former may stem from some pre-inflationary background and can redshift away whereas the latter are induced by a timelike hypersurface parametrising a physical cut-off. In both cases, we find that corrections scale in parts or fully as 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon) where ϵ \epsilon is the first slow-roll parameter. The precise observational footprint is hence dependent on the model driving inflation. Further, we show how the inflationary consistency relation is altered. We thus provide an analytic handle on possible high scale or pre-inflationary physics.

I
Introduction

Cosmic inflation
Linde:1983gd
;
Starobinsky:1980te
has been well established as the leading paradigm to describe the physics of the early universe. Besides solving the horizon and flatness problem, inflation is furthermore predictive as it provides a mechanism
Mukhanov:1990me
seeding structure formation which is in astonishing agreement with recent observations
Bennett:2012zja
;
Ade:2015lrj
;
Ade:2015tva
.

Typically, inflation is taken to be realised by a scalar field with a nearly shift-symmetric potential thus mimicking the equation of state of a cosmological constant. The shift symmetry is broken by a minimum in which the field may settle, hence inducing a graceful exit. Inducing the primordial density perturbation then comes naturally once promoting the inflaton field to a quantum operator. Precisely, the inflaton is decomposed into a classical background field evolving as determined by its potential, and a fluctuating part that is described as a massless quantum scalar field. The equation of motion for the free field then follows from the interplay of the perturbed stress-energy tensor of the inflaton field with the perturbed part of the linearised Einstein tensor. It has the form of that of a harmonic oscillator with time dependent mass. When taking the limit of the infinite past, the simple harmonic oscillator is recovered and one may impose a Minkowski vacuum as a boundary condition for the fluctuation; this is called the Bunch-Davies vacuum
Bunch:1978yq
, and is the conventional procedure whenever an expanding spacetime is asymptotically Minkowskian at the infinite past.

However, problems can arise when questioning the accessibility of the infinite past, or more generally, a Minkowskian limit at some past infinity. A prominent criticism of the standard procedure outlined above was coined the trans-Planckian problem

;
Niemeyer:2000eh
;

;

;

;

;

;

;

;

;

;

;
Kaloper:2002cs
;

;

;
Chung:2003wn
;

;

;

;

;

;

;

could leave a signature in the cosmic microwave background (CMB) when stretched across the (event) horizon during inflation. A true consensus was never reached.

In this paper, we revisit some of the original considerations and argue that whenever the infinite past is not accessible, the BD vacuum may not be imposed. Instead, one has to resort to mixed states as vacua, so called
\alpha
-vacua

;

n_{s}
and
n_{t}
induced by arbitrary mixed states or non-BD initial conditions.

Considering arbitrary mixed states, we find that the correction to the inflationary indices depends on the spectrum of mixed states; namely on the coefficient
|B_{k}|

\delta n_{s,t}\sim 2\epsilon_{V}|B_{k}|^{2}-\frac{d|B_{k}|^{2}}{d\ln k}\thinspace.

(1)

Assuming non-BD initial conditions, one finds oscillatory corrections

\delta n_{s,t}\sim\epsilon_{V}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H_{infl}}\exp{(\epsilon_{V}\thinspace N_{e})}\right)\thinspace,

(2)

where
N_{e}\leq 0
is the number of remaining e-folds,
H_{infl}
is the inflationary energy scale and
\Lambda
is the physical momentum cut-off where the initial vacuum is imposed.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows; we begin with a short review of field operators in time dependent backgrounds and establish our notation. Following a quick discussion of the relevant equation of motion for inflationary fluctuations, we discuss Bunch-Davies and non-BD initial conditions and highlight which scenario requires which choice of vacuum. In the main part, we first derive corrections to
n_{s}

IV

Theory

\displaystyle\pi_{f}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial f^{\prime}}=f^{\prime}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}f\thinspace,

\displaystyle[\hat{f}(\tau,\mathbb{x}),\hat{\pi}_{f}(\tau,\mathbb{y})]

\displaystyle=i\delta^{(3)}(\mathbb{x}-\mathbb{y})\thinspace,

\displaystyle[\hat{f}(\tau,\mathbb{x}),\hat{f}(\tau,\mathbb{y})]

\displaystyle=[\hat{\pi}_{f}(\tau,\mathbb{x}),\hat{\pi}(\tau,\mathbb{y})]=0\thinspace,

[\hat{f}(\tau,{\mathbb{k}}),\hat{\pi}^{\dagger}_{f}(\tau,{\mathbb{k}}^{\prime})]=i\delta^{(3)}({\mathbb{k}}-{\mathbb{k}}^{\prime})\thinspace.

\hat{f}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)=\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau)\thinspace.

(11)

Likewise, we may write the conjugate momentum as

\hat{\pi}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)=-ik\left(\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau)\right)\thinspace.

(12)

We recast the creation and annihilation operators in terms of their values at some fixed time

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=u_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau_{0})+v_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau_{0})\thinspace,

(13)

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=u^{*}_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau_{0})+v^{*}_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}_{{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau_{0})\thinspace,

(14)

10

|u_{k}(\tau)|^{2}-|v_{k}(\tau)|^{2}=1\thinspace.

(15)

Substituting the above into equation (

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=f_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau_{0})+f^{*}_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau_{0})\thinspace,

(16)

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=-i\left(g_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau_{0})-g^{*}_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau_{0})\right)\thinspace,

(17)

with

\displaystyle f_{k}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(u_{k}(\tau)+v_{k}^{*}(\tau)\right)\thinspace,

(18)

\displaystyle g_{k}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{k}{{2}}}\left(u_{k}(\tau)-v_{k}^{*}(\tau)\right)\thinspace,

(19)

and where equation (
18
) is a solution to the field equation of the field
\hat{f}
and equation (
19
) may be obtained from the expression for the conjugate momentum. A vacuum may now be defined at some time
\tau_{0}
as

\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau_{0})|0,\tau_{0}\rangle=0\thinspace.

(20)

Recalling equation (
13

(21)

which immediately sets
v_{k}(\tau_{0})=0
for the above to realise a vacuum. Thus in order to define a vacuum we need mode functions such that this condition is satisfied at the time the vacuum is imposed. Subsequently, we focus on the mode function
f_{k}
rather than the operator
\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}

II.2
Equation of motion in de Sitter space

The equation of motion determining the evolution of the mode functions
f_{k}(\tau)
for the rescaled field
\hat{f}=a(\tau)\hat{\phi}
is the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation
Mukhanov:1990me
(see
Baumann:2009ds
as a standard review)

f^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\left(k^{2}-{z^{\prime\prime}\over z}\right)f_{k}=0\thinspace,

(22)

where
z={\varphi}^{\prime}/H
with
\varphi
being the unperturbed background value of the inflaton field. It resembles the equation of motion of a harmonic oscillator with time dependent mass. From equation (
5
), we infer

g_{k}=f^{\prime}_{k}-\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}f_{k}\thinspace.

(23)

In de Sitter space with
a=-1/{H\tau}
, we have

\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}\approx\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}=\frac{2}{\tau^{2}}\thinspace.

(24)

Equation (
22
) may be thus recast as

f^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{2}{\tau^{2}}\right)f_{k}=0\thinspace.

(25)

The above has the general solution

f_{k}(\tau)=A_{k}\frac{e^{-ik\tau}}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(1-\frac{i}{k\tau}\right)+B_{k}\frac{e^{ik\tau}}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(1+\frac{i}{k\tau}\right)\thinspace,

(26)

and the conjugate momentum (
5
)

g_{k}(\tau)=A_{k}\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}}e^{-ik\tau}-B_{k}\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}}e^{ik\tau}\thinspace.

(27)

As we will recall in the following, the asymptotic past of the spacetime under consideration determines which term of the general solution (
26
) exists; we will show that a non-Minkowskian past creates a non-zero Bogolubov coefficient
B_{k}
whereas it is zero otherwise.

II.3
The Bunch-Davies vacuum

First, we require spacetime to resemble Minkowski space at early times
\tau\to-\infty
. Equation (
22

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}f_{k}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ik\tau}\thinspace,

(28)

for the solutions of (
22
). Imposing this initial condition on the general solution (
26
) sets
B_{k}=0\thinspace,A_{k}=1
and hence

f_{k}(\tau)=\frac{e^{-ik\tau}}{\sqrt{2k}}\left(1-\frac{i}{k\tau}\right)\thinspace,

(29)

as the mode function for the Bunch-Davies vacuum. From equation (
29
) and (
23

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-ik\tau}\left(2-\frac{i}{k\tau}\right)\thinspace,

(30)

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{ik\tau}\frac{i}{k\tau}\thinspace.

(31)

Furthermore, considering condition (
20

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{k}(\tau_{0})|0,\tau_{0}\rangle=

\displaystyle u_{k}(\tau_{0})\hat{a}_{\mathbb{k}}(\tau_{0})|0,\tau_{0}\rangle+v_{k}(\tau_{0})\underbrace{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\mathbb{k}}}(\tau_{0})|0,\tau_{0}\rangle}_{\neq 0}=0\thinspace,

(32)

which is readily satisfied for
\tau_{0}\to-\infty
. Thus requiring spacetime to resemble Minkowski space for early times consistently yields an initial condition for the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation and satisfies the vacuum condition (
20
). Note that a sufficient way to choose (

\tau\rightarrow-\infty

was not required. The limit
k\to\infty
should generally not be taken, as the initial vacuum state ought to be imposed for all modes, i.e. for all
k

\tau\to-\infty

However, scales probed by CMB experiments today correspond to modes whose physical wavelength
a/k
was much below the Planck length
l_{P}
at some point during inflation. As physics below the Planck scale is unknown, it is conceptually unclear whether or not the BD vacuum is the correct choice for sub-Planckian modes. This has been coined the trans-Planckian problem and a true consensus has not yet been reached; especially since alternatives are lacking. In the next subsection, we review a competing proposal, namely
\alpha
-vacua which provide a way to impose a vacuum at finite
\tau

II.4

III.2
Scale dependence

Inflation does not resemble perfect de Sitter but has an end, thus

(51)

(52)

(53)

where the subscript
V
denotes the potential slow-roll parameters. In the following, we evaluate expressions (
50
) with the corrected spectra (
44
), (
47
) and (
48
).

III.3
Corrections from mixed states

We begin with the derivation of the generic form of
\delta n_{s,t}
induced by mixed states. First, we recall that

n_{s}-1=\frac{d\ln\Delta_{s}^{2}}{d\ln k}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{s}^{2}}\frac{d\Delta_{s}^{2}}{dN_{e}}\underbrace{\frac{dN_{e}}{d\ln k}}_{1+\epsilon}\thinspace,

(54)

where we will focus on the
d\Delta_{s}^{2}/dN_{e}
term in the following; we separate

\Delta_{s}^{2}\rightarrow\Delta_{s,0}^{2}+\delta\Delta_{s}^{2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}+\frac{2}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}|B_{k}|^{2},

(55)

and expand the
1/\Delta_{s}^{2}
factor

\frac{1}{\Delta_{s}^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{s}^{2}}{(\Delta_{s}^{2})^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}^{(2)}\left(\frac{|B_{k}|}{H}\right)\right]\thinspace.

(56)

The leading order terms thus are

\frac{1}{\Delta_{s}^{2}}\frac{d\Delta_{s}^{2}}{dN_{e}}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\frac{d(\Delta_{s,0}^{2})}{dN_{e}}+\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{s}^{2})}{dN_{e}}\thinspace,

(57)

where the first term on the right hand side evaluates to the known
2\eta-4\epsilon
. We hence calculate

\displaystyle\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{s}^{2})}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{d}{dN_{e}}\left[\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}|B_{k}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dN_{e}}|B_{k}|^{2}+2|B_{k}|^{2}\frac{d}{dN_{e}}\left[\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}\right]

\displaystyle=2\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dN_{e}}|B_{k}|^{2}+2\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}|B_{k}|^{2}\left(2\frac{d\ln H}{dN_{e}}+\frac{d\epsilon}{dN_{e}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{d}{dN_{e}}|B_{k}|^{2}-2\epsilon|B_{k}|^{2}\right)\thinspace,

(58)

where have assumed
d\epsilon/dN_{e}\sim\mathcal{O}^{(2)}(\epsilon)
and omitted this subleading contribution. We thus obtain

\delta n_{s}\sim-2\left(2\epsilon_{V}|B_{k}|^{2}-\frac{d|B_{k}|^{2}}{d\ln k}\right)\thinspace.

(59)

Considering tensor fluctuations, we separate
\Delta_{t}^{2}=\Delta_{t,0}^{2}+\delta\Delta_{t}^{2}
and approximate
1/\Delta_{t}^{2}\approx 1/\Delta_{t,0}^{2}
, so that the missing piece to calculate is

\displaystyle\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{t}^{2})}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{d}{dN_{e}}\left[H^{2}|B_{k}|^{2}\right]\thinspace

\displaystyle={H^{2}}\left(\frac{d}{dN_{e}}|B_{k}|^{2}-2\epsilon|B_{k}|^{2}\right)\thinspace.

We may now write the corrected expression for
n_{t}
as

\delta n_{t}\sim-2\left(2\epsilon_{V}|B_{k}|^{2}-\frac{d|B_{k}|^{2}}{d\ln k}\right)\thinspace,

(60)

which is equal to the correction to the scalar spectral index.

Note that a suitable spectrum
|B_{k}|^{2}
may in principle account for low-
\ell
phenomenology such as power suppression or specific outliers in the data. Further, a correction
\delta n_{t}
does also change the consistency relation, which we will demonstrate in the final subsection.

III.4
Corrections from
\alpha
-vacua

We continue with the calculation of the scalar spectral index with the example of a physical cut-off as an example. Again, we separate (
47
)

\Delta_{s}^{2}\rightarrow\Delta_{s,0}^{2}+\delta\Delta_{s}^{2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{3}}{\epsilon\Lambda}\sin\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\thinspace,

(61)

and expand the
1/\Delta_{s}^{2}
factor of (
54
) as

\frac{1}{\Delta_{s}^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{s}^{2}}{(\Delta_{s}^{2})^{2}}\approx\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{H}{\Lambda}\right)\right]\thinspace.

(62)

The leading order terms thus are

\frac{1}{\Delta_{s}^{2}}\frac{d\Delta_{s}^{2}}{dN_{e}}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\frac{d(\Delta_{s,0}^{2})}{dN_{e}}+\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{s}^{2})}{dN_{e}}\thinspace,

(63)

where the first term on the right hand side evaluates to
2\eta-4\epsilon
. We hence calculate

\displaystyle\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{s}^{2})}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dN_{e}}\left[\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{3}}{\epsilon\Lambda}\sin\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace=-\frac{2H}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\frac{dH}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{H^{2}}{8\pi^{2}\Lambda\epsilon}\sin\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\left[3\frac{dH}{dN_{e}}-H\frac{d\ln\epsilon}{dN_{e}}\right]\thinspace.

(64)

Multiplying by
1/\Delta_{s,0}^{2}
then has the second term of the right hand side be of
\mathcal{O}(H/\Lambda)\times\mathcal{O}(\epsilon,\eta)
. We therefore write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{s,0}^{2}}\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{s}^{2})}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{H}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\frac{dH}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=2\thinspace\epsilon\thinspace\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\thinspace.

(65)

Having obtained the missing terms of (
63
), we may now write the result

\displaystyle n_{s}-1

\displaystyle=\left[2\eta-4\epsilon+\epsilon\thinspace\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\right]\left(1+\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\approx 2\eta_{V}-6\epsilon_{V}+2\thinspace\epsilon_{V}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\thinspace.

(66)

We hence find that the scalar spectral index
n_{s}
receives oscillatory corrections with an amplitude of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{V})
. Making use of result (
78
), we can recast the above in a form more useful to the phenomenologist and write

n_{s}=1+2\eta_{V}-6\epsilon_{V}+2\thinspace\epsilon_{V}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H_{infl}}\exp{(\epsilon_{V}\thinspace N_{e})}\right)\thinspace,

(67)

which gives the correction to
n_{s}
in terms of the number of e-folds. Figures
1
and
2
show how the power spectrum
\Delta_{s}^{2}
receives oscillatory corrections of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{V})
. Thus the amplitude of the observational signature is dependent on the inflationary model realised in nature.

Figure 1:
Power spectrum
\Delta_{s}^{2}\sim(k/k^{*})^{n_{s}-1}
between
-62<N_{e}<-56
for
V\sim(1-e^{-\kappa\varphi})^{2}
with
\kappa=\sqrt{2/3}
, or equivalently
f(R)=R+\alpha R^{2}
, and pivot scale
k^{*}=0.05\thinspace Mpc^{-1}
. Here,
\Lambda/H=10^{5}
. For plateau type models,
\epsilon_{V}\sim\mathcal{O}(N_{e}^{-2})
whereas
\eta_{V}\sim\mathcal{O}(N_{e}^{-1})
, hence the oscillations are clearly subleading.

. Again,
\Lambda/H=10^{5}
. Since
\eta_{V}=\epsilon_{V}
for

, the oscillations are of
\mathcal{O}(n_{s}-1)
. Oscillations of this type are surely ruled out by PLANCK.

Having obtained the corrected form of
n_{s}
, we now turn to the tensor tilt
n_{t}
. Recalling expressions (
48
) and (
50
), we employ an approach similar to the calculation of
n_{s}
; i.e. we separate
\Delta_{t}^{2}=\Delta_{t,0}^{2}+\delta\Delta_{t}^{2}
and approximate
1/\Delta_{t}^{2}\approx 1/\Delta_{t,0}^{2}
, so that the missing piece to calculate is

\displaystyle\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{t}^{2})}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dN_{e}}\left[\frac{H^{3}}{\Lambda}\sin\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad=-2H\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\frac{dH}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\thinspace+\frac{3H^{2}}{\Lambda}\sin\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\frac{dH}{dN_{e}}\thinspace.

(68)

Now accounting for the factor
1/\Delta_{t,0}^{2}
has the second term of the right hand side be of
\mathcal{O}(1/\Lambda)
. We thus write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{t,0}^{2}}\frac{d(\delta\Delta_{t}^{2})}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{H}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\frac{dH}{dN_{e}}

\displaystyle=2\thinspace\epsilon\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\thinspace,

(69)

which is equal to the corresponding term of the scalar calculation. As we had separated

n_{t}=\frac{d\Delta_{t}^{2}}{d\ln k}=\frac{d\Delta_{t,0}^{2}}{d\ln k}+\frac{\delta\Delta_{t}^{2}}{d\ln k}\thinspace,

(70)

where the first term of the right hand side is the vanilla result
-2\epsilon
, we may now write the corrected expression for
n_{t}
as

n_{t}=-2\left[\epsilon+\epsilon\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H}\right)\right]\thinspace.

(71)

Again, assuming slow roll and making use of expression (
78
), we write

n_{t}=-2\left[\epsilon_{V}+\epsilon_{V}\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H_{infl}}\exp{(\epsilon_{V}\thinspace N_{e})}\right)\right]\thinspace.

(72)

Importantly, the correction does not change the sign of
n_{t}
. Note that both (
67
) and (
72
) do not approach the conventional result for
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
. However, the starting point of the derivation, i.e. (
47
) and (
48
) do.

Further note that the crucial difference to the calculation of corrections from arbitrary mixed states is that the form here is dominated by the oscillatory terms that one had averaged over in the case of mixed states. It is this why the results (
59
) and (
60
) do not reduce to (

72
) once expression (

corrections to the oscillatory results.

III.5
Correction to the consistency relation

As shown by means of equations (
47
) and (
48
), the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
remains unchanged by our considerations. However, as we have just shown that
n_{t}
may obtain corrections of order one (conventional result and correction are of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
), the inflationary consistency relation

r=-8n_{t}

(73)

no longer holds. It is simply verified that, considering (
49
), (
72
), and (
78
), the consistency relation changes to

r=8n_{t}\left[-1+\cos\left(\frac{2\Lambda}{H_{infl}}\exp{(\epsilon\thinspace N_{e})}\right)\right]^{-1}\thinspace.

(74)

Thus measuring the tensor-to-scalar ratio and the tensor tilt may provide a definite answer as to whether or not the duration of inflation is finite
and
a high-scale cut-off of the theory exists, leaving theoretical concerns regarding the use of mixed states as vacua aside for now.

IV
Discussion

In this work, we studied the phenomenology of arbitrary mixed states and non-BD initial conditions. Evaluating expressions for the inflationary observables (
50
) with the corrected spectra (
44
), (
47
) and (
48
), we found corrections
\delta n_{s,t}
scaling partly as
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{V})
in the case of arbitrary mixed states and being oscillatory with an amplitude of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{V})
in the case of non-BD initial conditions. Results (
1
) and (
2
) thus provide an analytic handle on possible high scale corrections to inflationary observables.

Reflecting on the theoretical motivation for this study, mixed states may be caused by non-slow-roll pre-inflationary backgrounds as described in e.g.
Cicoli:2014bja
. There, the duration of inflation is taken to be just the required one, i.e. inflation does not last much longer than
|N_{CMB}|
. Concretely, assuming a non-slow-roll and asymptotically Minkowskian background before inflation can in principle induce non-zero Bogolubov coefficients for the inflaton fluctuations (provided the inflaton field already quantum fluctuates before inflation) which hence can induce the comoving excitations described above.

Further,
\alpha
-vacua display several shortcomings. However, it seems that the conventional BD vacuum may only consistently be imposed at the infinite past. Postulating that an asymptotically Minkowskian background at some past infinity is not accessible then renders the standard procedure inconsistent. This could e.g. be the case if one assumes the inflationary phase to be without a predecessor, i.e. that inflation is indeed the first period in the universe when QFT and GR become applicable in their respective regimes. A consistent formulation of initial conditions, or more generally QFT on curved backgrounds, will surely require a full theory of quantum gravity.

Appendix A
From
H
to
N_{e}
to
k
and back

As Inflation has an end and hence does not resemble perfect de Sitter space, the Hubble parameter
H
is not constant. Variables of interest for phenomenology that parametrise time during inflation are the number of remaining e-folds
N_{e}
(
N_{e}\leq 0
) and the wavenumber
k
of scales at (event) horizon exit. We now outline how to derive expressions for
H(N_{e})
and
H(k)
so that we can cast the derived corrections to
n_{s}
and
n_{t}
in tractable form.

Recall the definition of the Hubble slow-roll parameter

\epsilon=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=-\frac{1}{H}\frac{d\ln H}{dt}=-\frac{d\ln H}{dN_{e}}\thinspace,

(75)

where we have made use of
dN_{e}=dHdt
for the last equality. Solving for
H
yields

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{H}{H_{infl}}\right)=-\int_{-|N_{total}|}^{N_{e}}\epsilon(N_{e}^{\prime})dN_{e}^{\prime}\thinspace,

(76)

where
H_{infl}
and
N_{total}
denote the values of the respective quantities at the onset of inflation. Assuming
\epsilon(N_{total})\sim 0
and
\epsilon
to be only slowly varying with respect to
N_{e}
within the observable range of e-folds, i.e.

\int\epsilon(N_{e}^{\prime})dN_{e}^{\prime}\approx\epsilon N_{e}\thinspace,

(77)

we find the expression for
H
in terms of the number of remaining e-folds to be

H(N_{e})=H_{infl}\thinspace\exp{(-\epsilon\thinspace N_{e})}\thinspace.

(78)

Now recall the (event) horizon exit condition
k=aH
. Taking the derivative with respect to the wavenumber
k
and considering the relation
dN_{e}/d\ln k\approx 1+\epsilon
, one may verify that

\frac{dH}{dk}=-\epsilon\frac{H}{k}\thinspace.

(79)

This describes the change of
H
with respect to the scales exiting the horizon. Again, we assume
\epsilon
to be slowly varying with respect to
k
for observable scales, i.e.

\int\epsilon(k^{\prime})k^{\prime-1}dk^{\prime}\approx\epsilon\ln k\thinspace.

(80)

Expression (
79
) may hence be solved with

H(k)=H_{infl}\left(\frac{k}{k_{H}}\right)^{-\epsilon}\thinspace.

(81)

Once more,
H_{infl}
denotes the value of the Hubble parameter at the onset of inflation. The scale
k_{H}
is the last scale to exit the horizon,
k
hence is within
0<k\leq k_{H}
. As
k
corresponds to the inverse wavelength, larger
k
exit the event horizon later.

The value of
k_{H}
can be inferred as follows; wavenumber
k
and number of remaining e-folds
N_{e}
with
N_{e}\leq 0
are related as
k=a_{end}H_{infl}\thinspace e^{N_{e}}
, where
a_{end}
is the size of the scale factor at the end of inflation (
a_{0}=1
). Depending on the thermal history of the universe,
a_{end}H_{infl}\approx H_{0}e^{\#}
, where
\#\sim 62
. The last scale to exit has
k_{H}=a_{end}H_{infl}\thinspace e^{0}=a_{end}H_{infl}
. Inserting the expressions for
k_{H}
and
k(N_{e})
into (
81
) then readily recovers (
78
).

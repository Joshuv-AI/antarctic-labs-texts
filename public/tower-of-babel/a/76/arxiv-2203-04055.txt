Abstract
. We study the regularity of the free boundary in the parabolic obstacle problem for the fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} (and more general integro-differential operators) in the regime s > 1 2 s>\frac{1}{2} . We prove that once the free boundary is C 1 C^{1} it is actually C 2 , α C^{2,\alpha} . To do so, we establish a boundary Harnack inequality in C 1 C^{1} and C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} (moving) domains, providing that the quotient of two solutions of the linear equation, that vanish on the boundary, is as smooth as the boundary. As a consequence of our results we also establish for the first time optimal regularity of such solutions to nonlocal parabolic equations in moving domains.

35R35, 47G20.

1.
Introduction

The fractional obstacle problem is the following

\displaystyle\min\{(-\Delta)^{s}u,u-\varphi\}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)

\displaystyle=0,

where
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a given function called the obstacle and

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{n,s}p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x)-u(x+y))|y|^{-n-2s}dy,

for some
s\in(0,1)
. The operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
is called the fractional Laplacian.
This problem (and its parabolic version) arises in the study of the optimal stopping problems for stochastic processes for example when modelling the prices of American options. For more information see
[
6
]
.

The study of the fractional obstacle problem was initiated by Silvestre in
[
23
]
and by Caffarelli, Salsa and Silvestre in
[
7
]
. Since then a lot of effort was put in studying this problem and is nowadays quite well understood.
The interest of study in the obstacle problems is twofold. On one hand we are interested in regularity of the solutions, and on the other one we want to understand the set
\partial\{u>\varphi\}
, called the free boundary.
In
[
7
]
they showed the optimal
C^{1+s}
regularity of solutions, moreover they proved that at any free boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\{u>\varphi\}
exactly one of the following two statements holds

\begin{array}[]{lcccccl}\text{(i) }\quad 0&<&cr^{1+s}&\leq&\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u-\varphi)&\leq&Cr^{1+s}\\
\text{(ii) }&&0&\leq&\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u-\varphi)&\leq&Cr^{2}\end{array}\quad\quad\forall r\in(0,r_{0}).

The points satisfying
(i)
are called regular points, they form an open subset of the free boundary, and the free boundary is
C^{1,\alpha}
there for some
\alpha>0
.
Later on it was established that near regular points the free boundary is in fact
C^{\infty}
if the obstacle is
C^{\infty}
(see
[
17
,
18
]
). For results about the degenerate/singular points - the ones satisfying
(ii)
- we refer to
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
and references therein.

Many methods used for establishing these results strongly depend on the tools only available in the case of the fractional Laplacian. Therefore it was also challenging to extend the above mentioned results to a more general class of integro-differential operators. The dichotomy of the regular and degenerate points, as well as the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of the free boundary near regular points was proved in
[
5
]
, and the higher order regularity of the free boundary in
[
1
]
.

Much less is known in the parabolic version of the problem:

\displaystyle\min\{\partial_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u,u-\varphi\}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle u(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=\varphi.

Notice that the nature of the problem strongly depends on the value of the parameter
s
. For
s>\frac{1}{2}
the fractional Laplacian is the leading term (the subcritical regime), while for
s<\frac{1}{2}
the time derivative is (the supercritical regime).
The regularity of solutions was first addressed by Caffarelli and Figalli in
[
4
]
, where they proved that the solutions are
C^{1+s}_{x}
in space and
C^{\min(\frac{1+s}{2s},2)-\varepsilon}_{t}
in time.
Later on in
[
2
]
Barrios, Figalli and Ros-Oton showed that when
s>\frac{1}{2}
we have the analogous dichotomy as in the elliptic case, and that the free boundary is
C^{1,\alpha}
in space and time near regular free boundary points.
Furthermore, very recently in
[
21
]
Ros-Oton and Torres-Latorre improved the regularity of solutions in the supercritical regime
s<\frac{1}{2}
to
C^{1,1}
in space and time, which is optimal. Finally in a forthcoming paper
[
13
]
Figalli, Ros-Oton and Serra extended the results from
[
2
]
to a more general class of operators as well as to the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
.

Despite all these results nothing is known though about the higher regularity of the free boundary. An open question that seems quite challenging is the following:

Is it true that the free boundary is
C^{\infty}
(at least in space) near regular points?

The goal of this paper is to study this question for
s>\frac{1}{2}
and establish that near regular points the free boundary is
C^{2,\alpha}
.
We consider the class of non-local operators of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Lu(x,t)=p.v.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))K(x-y)dy\\
\lambda|y|^{-n-2s}\leq K(y)\leq\Lambda|y|^{-n-2s},\text{ }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\text{ }K\text{ is even, homogeneous},\end{split}

for
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
, called the ellipticity constants. Our main result reads as follows. (We refer to Section
2
for definition of the parabolic Hölder spaces
C^{\beta}_{p}
.)

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
L
be as in (
1.1
), with its kernel
K\in C^{5}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Let
u
be a solution of

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\min\{(\partial_{t}+L)u,u-\varphi\}&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)\\
u(\cdot,0)&=&\varphi&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}

with
\varphi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Then the free boundary
\partial\{u>0\}
is
C^{2+\alpha}_{p}
near regular free boundary points for some
\alpha>0
.

It remains an open problem to improve the regularity from
C^{2,\alpha}
to
C^{\infty}
; see Remark
1.3
below.

To prove this result, we exploit the fact that the normal to the free boundary can be expressed with the quotients of partial derivatives of
u-\varphi
, see
[
1
,
8
,
9
]
. Hence we closely study the boundary behaviour of solutions to

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)w&=&f&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
w&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

for some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
Q_{1}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\text{ }|x|<1,|t|<1\}
, as the mentioned derivatives solve such equation. There was not much attention put into the studying of the boundary regularity of solutions in
moving
domains, the only known results to the bests of our knowledge (
[
3
,
10
,
16
,
22
]
) consider cylindric types of domains. We provide that the solutions grow like distance to the boundary to the power
s
, which in combination with interior regularity estimates gives
C^{s}_{p}
regularity up to the boundary. Refining the argument, and comparing solutions directly amongst each other, we are able to establish the boundary Harnack inequality of the following type.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be
C^{1}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
. Let
\gamma,\varepsilon>0
. Let
L
be an operator of the form (
1.1
), with kernel
K\in C^{1-s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Assume
u_{i}\in C_{p}^{\gamma}(Q_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, solve

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)u_{i}&=&f_{i}&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
u_{i}&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

with
\left[f_{i}\right]_{C^{1-s}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}\leq 1
, and
\left[u_{i}\right]_{C^{\frac{1-s}{2s}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}\leq 1
. Assume also that
u_{2}\geq c_{0}d^{s}
, for some
c_{0}>0
.
Then

\left|\left|\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\right|\right|_{C_{p}^{1-\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega}\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C,

where
C>0
depends only on
n,s,\varepsilon,c_{0},G_{0},\|K\|_{C^{1-s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}
and ellipticity constants.

If additionally
\Omega
is
C^{\beta}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
for some
\beta>1+s
,
K\in C^{2\beta+1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
,

\left[f_{i}\right]_{C^{s}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}\leq 1
, and
\left[u_{i}\right]_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}\leq 1
, then

\left|\left|\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\right|\right|_{C^{2s-\varepsilon}_{p}(\overline{\Omega}\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C,

with
C
additionally depending on
\|K\|_{C^{2\beta+1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}
.

To obtain the boundary Harnack inequality, we develop expansions of the form
u_{1}-Qu_{2}
at boundary points of orders up to
3s-\varepsilon
, where
Q
is a polynomial in space variables of degree
1
. In combination with interior regularity results this yields estimates for the quotient as well.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that in the parabolic non-local setting the interior regularity estimates require global time regularity of solutions (see
[
10
, Lemma 7.1]
).
This is also one of the reasons why we only get
C^{2,\alpha}_{p}
regularity of the free boundary in Theorem
1.1
, for some
\alpha>0
. Namely, the time derivative of the solution to the obstacle problem is globally
C^{\frac{\gamma}{2s}}_{t}
, for some
1-s<\gamma<2-s
(see
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
and
[
13
, Corollary 1.6]
). Hence we can only use the interior estimates for orders up to
\gamma+2s
. Therefore with the method we use, we can get the boundary Harnack estimate of order at most
\gamma+s
, which is smaller than
2
, and hence we can not deduce that the free boundary is better than
C^{2,\alpha}
.

It remains an open problem to decide whether the free boundary is
C^{\infty}
near regular points or not.

As a consequence of our result we also obtain the optimal Hölder regularity of
u
and of
u/d^{s}
, thus extending the results of
[
22
]
to the case of moving domains. More precisely, we prove that any solution of (
1.2
) satisfies

u\in C^{s}_{x,t}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\frac{u}{d^{s}}\in C^{2s-1}_{p},

see Corollary
3.14
. Here
d
is a regularized space-time distance to the boundary.

2.
Notation and preliminary definitions

The ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},
where the first
n
coordinates we denote by
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and the last one with
t
. Sometimes we furthermore split
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
, for
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
.
Accordingly we use the multi-index notation
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n+1}
,
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\alpha_{t})
,
|\alpha|_{p}=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}+2s\alpha_{t}
, and furthermore

\partial^{\alpha}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\circ\ldots\circ\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{\alpha_{n}}\circ\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{\alpha_{t}}.

The gradient operator
\nabla
, differential operator
D
and Laplace operator
\Delta
are taken only in
x
variables.

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
we denote the time slits with
\Omega_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\text{ }(x,t)\in\Omega\}
and the distance function to the boundary in space directions only with
d_{t}(x)=d_{x}(x,t)=\inf_{z\in\partial\Omega_{t}}|z-x|,
while with
d
we denote the (regularized) distance to the boundary of the domain in space-time. We also write
|(x,t)-(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})|_{p}=|x-x^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}
.

We denote by
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
the following cylinder of radius
r
centred at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-r^{2s},t_{0}+r^{2s}).

When
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
, we denote it simply
Q_{r}
.
We denote
Q_{r}^{\prime}=\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}\times(t_{0}-r^{2s},t_{0}+r^{2s})
.

Finally,
C
indicates an unspecified constant not depending on any of the relevant quantities, and whose value is allowed to change from line to line. We make use of sub-indices whenever we will want to underline the dependencies of the constant.

We define the parabolic Hölder seminorms of order
\alpha>0
in the following way

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. For
\alpha\in(0,1]
we define the parabolic Hölder seminorm of order
\alpha
as follows

\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(\Omega)}=\sup_{(x,t),(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\Omega}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2s}}},

and

\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{t}(\Omega)}=\sup_{(x,t),(x,t^{\prime})\in\Omega}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(x,t^{\prime})|}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{\alpha}}.

If
\alpha\in(1,2s]
, we set

\displaystyle\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left[\nabla u\right]_{C^{\alpha-1}_{p}(\Omega)}+\left[u\right]_{C^{\frac{\alpha}{2s}}_{t}(\Omega)}.

For bigger exponents
\alpha>2s
, we set

\left[u\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(\Omega)}=\left[\nabla u\right]_{C^{\alpha-1}_{p}(\Omega)}+\left[\partial_{t}u\right]_{C^{\alpha-2s}_{p}(\Omega)}.

We furthermore define the parabolic Hölder space of order
\alpha

C^{\alpha}_{p}(\Omega)=\{f;\text{ }\left[f\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(\Omega)}<\infty,\text{ }D^{k}\partial_{t}^{l}f\in C^{0}(\Omega)\text{ whenever }k+2sl\leq\alpha\}.

We are now able to define what means that a set is
C^{\alpha}_{p}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
0\in\partial\Omega
,
r>0
, and
\alpha>0
. We say that
\Omega
is
C^{\alpha}_{p}
in
Q_{r}
, if
\partial\Omega\cap Q_{r}
is a graph over the
n
-th coordinate of some function
g\in C^{\alpha}_{p}(Q_{r}^{\prime})
, with
\left[g\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(Q_{r}^{\prime})}\leq G_{0}
, for some
G_{0}>0
.

In the paper we work with a distance function to the boundary. Since we need it to have more regularity than just the euclidean distance, we give the precise statement in the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be
C^{\beta}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
, for some
\beta>1.

We denote by
d
a function satisfying

d\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C^{\infty}_{x}(\{d>0\}),\quad C^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega\cap Q_{1})\leq d\leq C\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega\cap Q_{1})\text{ in }\Omega\cap Q_{1},

|D^{k}d|\leq Cd^{\beta-k},\quad\text{in }\{d>0\},\text{ for all }k>\beta.

Any such function is called a
regularized distance
.

Furthermore we denote by
\psi
a diffeomorphism
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, such that it holds
\psi(\Omega\cap Q_{1})=Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
, and that
\psi\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C^{2}_{x}(\{d>0\})
, with

|D^{k}\psi|\leq Cd^{\beta-k},

for all
k>\beta
, in
\{d>0\}.

The construction of
d
and
\psi
is provided in Lemma
A.1
.

3.
Boundary regularity in moving domains

In this section we prove the results regarding boundary regularity of solutions to (
1.2
). We follow the ideas from
[
10
,
22
]
, but in our setting the domain does not need to be cylindric. The main tool is using contradiction arguments in combination with blow-up techniques. This is why we work with domains that are at least
C^{1}_{p}
near the origin, since they give a half-space after blowing up.
Let us stress that for all the estimates we need to assume that the solutions are already Hölder continuous with some (small) positive exponent. As we use these estimates on the derivatives of the solution to the obstacle problem, which is
C^{1,\alpha}
, this does not cause issues.

3.1.
Hölder estimates

We begin with establishing a-priori boundary estimates for orders smaller than
s
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be
C^{1}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
. Let
\gamma\in(0,s)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Let
L
be an operator of the form (
1.1
). Assume
u\in C_{p}^{\gamma}(Q_{1})
is a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)u&=&f&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
u&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

with
fd^{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})
.
Then

\left[u\right]_{C_{p}^{\gamma}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C\left(||fd^{s}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}+\sup_{R>1}R^{\varepsilon-2s}||u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
n,s,\gamma,\varepsilon,G_{0}
and ellipticity constants.

We prove it in two steps. First we establish a weaker estimate of similar type in the following lemma and then prove that it in fact implies the wanted inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be
C^{1}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
L
be an operator of the form (
1.1
) with the kernel
K
. Assume
u\in C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))
is a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)u&=&f&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
u&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

with
fd^{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})
.
Then for every
\delta>0
there exists
C>0
so that

\left[u\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq\delta\left[u\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}+C\left(||fd^{s}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}+||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}\right).

The constant
C
depends only on
n,s,\varepsilon,\delta,G_{0}
, and ellipticity constants.

Proof.

We can assume that
||fd^{s}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}+||u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}\leq 1
, since otherwise we would divide the equation with a suitable constant. We argue by contradiction. Assume there exists
\delta>0
, so that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist domains
\Omega_{k}
that are
C^{1}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
,
u_{k},f_{k}
and
L_{k}
operators satisfying (
1.1
) so that the suitable equation holds, but

(3.1)

\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{1/2})}>\delta\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}+k.

Pick
(x_{k},t_{k}),(y_{k},s_{k})\in\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{1/2}
so that

\frac{1}{2}\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq\frac{|u_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})-u_{k}(y_{k},s_{k})|}{|x_{k}-y_{k}|^{s-\varepsilon}+|t_{k}-s_{k}|^{\frac{s-\varepsilon}{2s}}}.

We define
\rho_{k}:=|x_{k}-y_{k}|+|t_{k}-s_{k}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}
.
Using (
3.1
) we see that

\rho_{k}^{s-\varepsilon}\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C|u(x_{k},t_{k})-u(y_{k},s_{k})|\leq 2C\leq\frac{C}{k}\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{1/2})},

which yields that
\rho_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty.

Now we have the following dichotomy

\displaystyle\text{Case }1)\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{x}(x_{k},t_{k})}{\rho_{k}}=\infty,

\displaystyle\text{Case }2)\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{d_{x}(x_{k},t_{k})}{\rho_{k}}=:\gamma<\infty.

Let us first treat the case
1)
. We define

v_{k}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}\rho_{k}^{s-\varepsilon}}\left(u_{k}(x_{k}+\rho_{k}x,t_{k}+\rho_{k}^{2s}t)-u_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})\right).

We have
v_{k}(0,0)=0
,
\left[v_{k}\right]_{C^{s-\varepsilon}(A)}\leq 1
for any compact set
A\subset R^{n+1}
, provided that
k
is big enough, and moreover

\displaystyle||v_{k}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}

\displaystyle\geq|v_{k}(\rho_{k}^{-1}(y_{k}-x_{k}),\rho_{k}^{-2s}(s_{k}-t_{k}))|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}\rho_{k}^{s-\varepsilon}}|u_{k}(y_{k},s_{k})-u_{k}(x_{k},t_{k})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\frac{\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega\cap Q_{1/2})}}{\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}}\geq\frac{\delta}{C}

where in the last step we used (
3.1
).
Moreover
v_{k}
satisfy

(3.2)

(\partial_{t}+L_{k})v_{k}=\frac{\rho_{k}^{s+\varepsilon}}{\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}}f_{k,\rho_{k}}\quad\text{in }U_{k}\cap Q_{1/\rho_{k}},

where
U_{k}=\{(x,t);\text{ }(x_{k}+\rho_{k}x,t_{k}+\rho_{k}^{2s}t)\in\Omega_{k}\}
and
f_{k,\rho_{k}}(x,t)=f_{k}(x_{k}+\rho_{k}x,t_{k}+\rho_{k}^{2s}t)
.
Note that
U_{k}
converge to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, while

\left|\frac{\rho_{k}^{s+\varepsilon}}{\left[u_{k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))}}f_{k,\rho_{k}}\right|\leq\frac{\rho_{k}^{s+\varepsilon}d_{k,\rho_{k}}^{-s}}{k}\longrightarrow 0,

locally uniformly in
R^{n+1}
, thanks to (
3.1
). Moreover,
L_{k}
converge (up to a subsequence) to an operator
L_{0}
satisfying (
1.1
), thanks to equi-continuity and uniform boundedness on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Passing to subsequence we get that
v_{k}
converge to a function
v
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The function

\displaystyle+||u_{1}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\int_{B_{1/20}^{c}}|\chi(x+y)-\chi(x^{\prime}+y)|K(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\gamma}{2s}})\left(\int_{B_{1/20}^{c}}\left[u_{1}\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{p}(B_{|y|+1})}K(y)dy+C_{\chi}||u_{1}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\gamma}{2s}})\left(\Lambda C_{0}\int_{B_{1/2}^{c}}\frac{(|y|+1)^{2s-\varepsilon}}{|y|^{n+2s}}dy+C||u_{1}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\gamma}{2s}})\left(CC_{0}+C||u_{1}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right),

where

stands for

Applying the already proven inequality for

and

(which follows from the covering argument) gives

\left[\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega}\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C\left(\left[f_{1}\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}\left[u_{1}\right]_{C^{\gamma}_{p}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}+\left|\left|u_{1}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\right).

To pass from the growth control with the parabolic Hölder seminorms to seminorms in time with

growth, we proceed in the same way as in the moreover case of Lemma

.
The claim is proven.
∎

In the following proposition we establish the expansion for two solutions for orders between

and

. In order to exceed order

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be
C^{1}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma>0
. Let
L
be an operator of the form (
1.1
), with kernel
K\in C^{1-s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
satisfying
\left[K\right]_{C^{1-s}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leq M_{0}
, for some
M_{0}>0
. Assume
u_{i}\in C_{p}^{\gamma}(Q_{1})
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, solve

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)u_{i}&=&f_{i}&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
u_{i}&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

with
\left[f_{i}\right]_{C^{1-s}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}\leq 1
,
\left[u_{i}\right]_{C^{\frac{1-s}{2s}}_{t}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\leq R^{2s-1}
, and
||u_{2}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\leq R^{s}
and
||u_{1}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\leq R^{3s-1-\varepsilon}
. Assume also that
u_{2}\geq c_{0}d^{s}
, for some

.

Then for every
(z,t)\in\partial\Omega\cap Q_{1/2}
we have

\left[\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}-q_{r}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega\cap Q_{r}(z,t))}\leq Cr^{1-s},

where
q_{r}
equals
\operatorname{arg}\min\limits_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{Q_{r}\cap\Omega}(\frac{u_{1}}{u_{2}}-q)^{2}
and
C>0
depends only on
n,s,\varepsilon,c_{0},G_{0},M_{0}
and ellipticity constants.

Proof.

Assume first that
u_{1}\equiv 0
in
Q_{1}^{c}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
(z,t)=(0,0)
.
We argue by contradiction. Suppose that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
\Omega_{k},u_{i,k},f_{i,k},L_{k}
as in the statement, so that

\sup_{k}\sup_{r>0}r^{s-1}\left[\frac{u_{1,k}}{u_{2,k}}-q_{r,k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{r})}=\infty,

with
q_{r,k}=\operatorname{arg}\min\limits_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{Q_{r}\cap\Omega_{k}}(\frac{u_{1,k}}{u_{2,k}}-q)^{2}
.

Then we define

\theta(r)=\sup_{k}\sup_{\rho>r}\rho^{s-1}\left[\frac{u_{1,k}}{u_{2,k}}-q_{\rho,k}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{\rho})},

which is monotone in
r
, finite for
r>0
by Corollary
3.6
, and goes to
\infty
as
r\downarrow 0
, thanks to the contradiction assumption. Hence for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
k_{m}
and
r_{m}
, so that

\frac{m}{4}\leq\frac{\theta(r_{m})}{2}\leq r_{m}^{s-1}\left[\frac{u_{1,k_{m}}}{u_{2,k_{m}}}-q_{r_{m},k_{m}}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{k_{m}\cap Q_{r_{m}})}}\leq\theta(r_{m}).

In particular
r_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty.

We define the blow up sequence

v_{m}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\theta(r_{m})r_{m}^{1-2\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{u_{1,k_{m}}}{u_{2,k_{m}}}-q_{r_{m},k_{m}}\right)(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2s}t).

Notice that by definition of
r_{m},k_{m}
and
q_{r,m}
we have

(3.3)

\left[v_{m}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{1})}\geq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{1}}v_{m}=0,

where
\Omega_{m}=\{(x,t);\text{ }(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2s}t)\in\Omega_{k_{m}}\}
.
Moreover, the way
\theta
is defined gives a control on the growth of
v_{m}
in the following manner

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[v_{m}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{R})}=&\frac{r_{m}^{s-2\varepsilon}}{\theta(r_{m})r_{m}^{1-2\varepsilon}}\left[\frac{u_{1,k_{m}}}{u_{2,k_{m}}}-q_{r_{m},k_{m}}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{Rr_{m}})}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\theta(r_{m})r_{m}^{1-s}}\left[\frac{u_{1,k_{m}}}{u_{2,k_{m}}}-q_{Rr_{m},k_{m}}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-2\varepsilon}(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{Rr_{m}})}\\
\leq&\frac{\theta(Rr_{m})(Rr_{m})^{1-s}}{\theta(r_{m})r_{m}^{1-s}}\leq R^{1-s},\end{split}

for every
R\geq 1.

In combination with
\int_{\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{1}}v_{m}=0
we deduce
||v_{m}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}\cap\Omega_{m})}\leq C
uniformly in
m
. Combining it with the growth of the seminorms, we deduce
||v_{m}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}\cap\Omega_{m})}\leq CR^{1-2\varepsilon}.
Notice that these estimates hold true for general
k,r
, since we only used the definition of
\theta
and not of
r_{m},k_{m}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{|q_{r,k}-q_{2r,k}|}{\theta(r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{||q_{r,k}-q_{2r,k}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}\cap\Omega_{k})}}{\theta(r)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{||u_{1,k}/u_{2,k-q_{r,k}}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}\cap\Omega_{k})}}{\theta(r)}+\frac{||u_{1,k}/u_{2,k-q_{2r,k}}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}\cap\Omega_{k})}}{\theta(r)}

\displaystyle\leq r^{1-2\varepsilon}\left|\left|\frac{1}{\theta(r)r^{1-2\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{u_{1,k}}{u_{2,k}}-q_{r,k}\right)\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{r})}

\displaystyle\text{ }+(2r)^{1-2\varepsilon}\left|\left|\frac{1}{\theta(2r)(2r)^{1-2\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{u_{1,k}}{u_{2,k}}-q_{r,k}\right)\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{k}\cap Q_{2r})}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{1-2\varepsilon}.

Hence as in the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 4.1]
, we conclude that
\frac{q_{r,k}}{\theta(r)}\to 0
as
r\downarrow 0
, uniformly in
k
.

We now define

v_{1,m}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\theta(r_{m})r_{m}^{1+s-2\varepsilon}}\left(u_{1,k_{m}}-q_{r_{m},k_{m}}u_{2,k_{m}}\right)(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2s}t),

and

v_{2,m}(x,t)=\frac{1}{r_{m}^{s}}u_{2,k_{m}}(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2s}t),

so that
v_{m}=\frac{v_{1,m}}{v_{2,m}}.
Thanks to assumptions on
u_{2,k}
we have that
\left[v_{2,m}\right]_{C^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-r_{k_{m}}^{-2s},r_{k_{m}}^{-2s}))}\leq 1
,
||(\partial_{t}+L_{k_{m}})v_{2,m}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}\cap\Omega_{m})}\leq 1
and
v_{2,m}\geq cc_{0}d_{m}^{s}
, were
d_{m}
denotes the distance function in
\Omega_{m}
. Therefore we can apply Corollary
3.6
to
u_{1,m}(M\cdot,M^{2s}\cdot)
and
v_{2,m}(M\cdot,M^{2s}\cdot)
, to get

\displaystyle\left[v_{m}\right]_{C_{p}^{s-\varepsilon}(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{M/2})}\leq C(M)

\displaystyle\left(\left[(\partial_{t}+L_{k_{m}})v_{1,m}\right]_{C^{1-s}_{p}(Q_{M}\cap\Omega_{m})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sup_{R>M}R^{\varepsilon-2s}\left[v_{1,m}\right]_{C^{\frac{1-s}{2s}}_{t}(B_{R}\times(-M^{2s},M^{2s}))}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sup_{R>1}R^{-s-1+\varepsilon}||v_{1,m}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-M^{2s},M^{2s}))}\right),

for every
M\in\mathbb{N}
, for
m
big enough.
Let us now show that all the quantities in the right-hand side are bounded uniformly in
m
. First,

(\partial_{t}+L_{k_{m}})v_{1,m}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\theta(r_{m})r_{m}^{1-s-2\varepsilon}}(f_{1,k_{m}}-q_{r_{m},k_{m}}f_{2,k_{m}})(r_{m}x,r_{m}^{2s}t),

and hence

\displaystyle\left[(\partial_{t}+L_{k_{m}})v_{1,m}\right]_{C^{1-s}_{p}(Q_{M}\cap\Omega_{m})}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{m}^{2\varepsilon}}{\theta(r_{m})}\left[f_{1,k_{m}}-q_{r_{m},k_{m}}f_{2,k_{m}}\right]_{C^{1-s}_{p}(Q_{Mr_{m}\cap\Omega_{k_{m}}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(1+q_{r_{m},k_{m}})}{\theta(r_{m})},

which is bounded by assumption on the seminorms of
f_{i,k}
and the fact that
\frac{q_{r,k}}{\theta(r)}\to 0
as

uniformly in
k
.
The growth term we estimate as follows

\displaystyle\left[v_{1,m}\right]_{C^{\frac{1-s}{2s}}_{t}(B_{R}\times(-M^{2s},M^{2s}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left[v_{m}v_{2,m}\right]_{C^{\frac{1-s}{2s}}_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[v_{m}\right]_{C^{s-2\varepsilon}_{p}(Q_{R})}R^{s-2\varepsilon-1+s}||v_{2,m}||_{L^{\infty}}+\left[v_{2,m}\right]_{C^{\frac{1-s}{2s}}_{t}}||v_{m}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq R^{1-s}R^{2s-1-2\varepsilon}C(M)R^{s}+C(M)R^{2s-1}R^{1-2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C(M)R^{2s-2\varepsilon},

where we used the growth control of

from (

Putting it all together, we get that
||v_{m}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{m}\cap Q_{M})}
and

are uniformly bounded, which implies that

converge to some function

in

in any compact subset of

Moreover

converges to

Hence also

converges to

We claim that

which goes to

Note that the assumption on uniform bounds on

(\partial_{t}+L_{0})(w(x+h,t+\tau)-w(x,t))=0,\quad\quad\text{whenever }x_{n}>0.

and hence

implies that

, and hence

. But passing to the limit quantities in (

is a constant function.

To prove the statement in the case when

Remark 3.8

Let

. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

and ellipticity constants.

Moreover, if additionally

\left[f\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})}\leq 1

\left[u\right]_{C^{\frac{\alpha}{2s}}_{t}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\leq R^{3s-1-\alpha}

, such that

\left[u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s}_{p}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{-1+s-\alpha},

with

additionally depending on
\|K\|_{C^{2\beta+1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.5
, and the fact that

is bounded by

(see
[

, Lemma 3.2]
) we get

3.14

Since

. Using rescaled Proposition
3.1

\displaystyle\left[u(x,t)-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}(x,t)\right]_{C^{s-\varepsilon}_{p}(Q_{r}(z,t_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{-s+\varepsilon}\Big(r^{2s}||d^{s}(f+(\partial_{t}+L)d^{s})||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2r}(z,t_{0}))}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}||u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{Rr}(z,t_{0}))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{2s-1+\varepsilon},

whenever

.

To prove the moreover case, we use Lemma
A.3
, which gives

\displaystyle\left[u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s}_{p}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C

\displaystyle r^{-\alpha-2s}\Big(r^{\alpha+2s}\left[f-q_{(z,t_{0})}(\partial_{t}+L)d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}

\displaystyle+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s-\alpha+\varepsilon}||u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2(R+2)r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}

\displaystyle+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}r^{\alpha}\left[u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(Q_{2r(R+2)})}\Big).

Lemma

are provided above.
Hence we have

(3.7)

\left[u_{1}-q_{(z,t_{0})}u_{2}-Q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s-\varepsilon}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C.

Moreover by Corollary
3.10
we have

\left[u_{2}-q_{2,(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{-1+s-\alpha},\quad\quad\left[u_{2}-q_{2,(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{3s-1}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C.

Note that the first inequality also implies that

see
[

, Lemma A.5]
. Hence for any polynomial

of degree

with

we have

\displaystyle\left[Q(u_{2}-d^{s})\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq

\displaystyle||Q||_{L^{\infty}}\left[u_{2}-q_{2,(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}

\displaystyle+|\nabla Q|\left[u_{2}-q_{2,(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s-1}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}

\displaystyle+\left[Q\right]_{C^{\alpha}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}||\partial_{t}(u_{2}-q_{2,(z,t_{0})}d^{s})||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{1}r^{-1+s-\alpha}+Cr^{s-\alpha}+Cr^{1-\alpha}r^{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C.

Combining it with (

), we get

\left[u_{1}-\tilde{Q}_{(z,t_{0})}u_{2}\right]_{C^{\alpha+2s-\varepsilon}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C,

for

which proves the claim in view of

, Lemma B.2]
.

Proof of Theorem

3.4.
Optimal Hölder estimates

Using the expansion result (Proposition
3.4
) on a solution
u
and
d^{s}
, yields that solutions grow at most as
d^{s}
near the boundary. In combination with interior regularity results, we can prove that the solutions must be
C^{s}
up to the boundary (in space and time, which is better than
C^{s}_{p}
).

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be
C^{\beta}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
, for some
\beta>2s
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
L
be an operator of the form (
1.1
). Assume that
u\in C_{p}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))
,
\gamma>0
, solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)u&=&f&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
u&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

Then

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{C^{s}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C\left(||fd^{1-s}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}\cap\Omega)}+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}||u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\right),

and

\left|\left|\frac{u}{d^{s}}\right|\right|_{C^{2s-1}_{p}(Q_{1/2}\cap\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\left(||fd^{1-s}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}\cap\Omega)}+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}||u||_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-1,1))}\right),

where
C
depends only on
n,s,G_{0}
and ellipticity constants.

Proof.

Dividing
u
with
\left(||fd^{1-s}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}\cap\Omega)}+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}||u||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}\times(-1,1))}\right)
, it suffices to prove that
\left[u\right]_{C^{s}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C
and
\left[\frac{u}{d^{s}}\right]_{C^{2s-1}_{p}(Q_{1/2})}\leq 1
, when it holds
||fd^{1-s}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}\cap\Omega)}+\sup_{R>1}R^{-2s+\varepsilon}||u||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}\times(-1,1))}\leq 1
.

Note that
(\partial_{t}+L)d^{s}
is bounded by
d^{s-1}
(see
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
), so from Corollary
3.5
we get

\left[\frac{u}{d^{s}}\right]_{C^{2s-1}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C.

Moreover by Proposition
3.4

(3.8)

\left[u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{3s-1}_{p}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C,

whenever
Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\Omega
and
d(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 2r
.
From this we deduce that

\left[u\right]_{C_{p}^{s}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C\left[d^{s}\right]_{C_{p}^{s}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C,

in view of
[
19
, Lemma B.5]
.
From
[
19
, Lemma B.2]
we get that

\left[u\right]_{C_{p}^{s}(\Omega\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C,

and hence in particular

\left[u\right]_{C_{x}^{s}(\Omega\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C.

To get the time regularity of solutions we need to work a bit more. Choose
(x,t),(x,t^{\prime})\in\Omega\cap Q_{1/2}
. First if
|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\max\{d(x,t),d(x,t^{\prime})\}
, then we can find
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega
, so that
(x,t),(x,t^{\prime})\in Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\Omega
and
d(x_{0},t_{0})\leq 2r
. Then from (
3.8
) we deduce that

|u(x,t)-u(x,t^{\prime})|\leq\left(\left[u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{s}_{t}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}+\left[d^{s}\right]_{C^{s}_{t}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\right)|t-t^{\prime}|^{s}\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{s},

since
\frac{3s-1}{2s}>s
for
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.
Let now
|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}>\frac{1}{2}\max\{d(x,t),d(x,t^{\prime})\}
. First notice that the regularity of the quotient implies also that

\left[u-q_{(z,t_{0})}d^{s}\right]_{C^{2s-1}_{p}(Q_{r}(z,t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{s},

for all
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
and
q_{(z,t_{0})}
from Proposition
3.4
. Let
(x,t)
be the point closer to the boundary than
(x,t^{\prime})
,
(z,t)
be its closest boundary point at time
t
, and let
(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})
be the closest to
(x,t^{\prime})
. We can estimate

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-u(x,t^{\prime})|\leq

\displaystyle|u(x,t)-q_{(z,t)}d^{s}(x,t)-u(x,t^{\prime})+q_{(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})}d^{s}(x,t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle+|q_{(z,t)}d^{s}(x,t)-q_{(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})}d^{s}(x,t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{I}+\text{II}.

To estimate
II
, we observe that
q_{(z,t)}=\frac{u}{d^{s}}(z,t)
which is a
C^{2s-1}_{p}
function, and hence

|q_{(z,t)}-q_{(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})}|\leq C(|z-z^{\prime}|^{2s-1}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}).
Moreover by triangle inequality and the assumption that
|t-t^{\prime}|
is big, we have that
|z-z^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}.
Hence

\displaystyle\text{II}\leq

\displaystyle|q_{(z,t)}-q_{(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})}||d^{s}(x,t)|+|q_{(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})}||d^{s}(x,t)-d^{s}(x,t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{2s-1}{2s}}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}+C|t-t^{\prime}|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|t-t^{\prime}|^{s}.

Let us turn to the term
I
. We fix
x_{0}
on the line going through
z
and
x
, far enough from the boundary (to be specified later) and define
x_{k}=(1-2^{-k})x+2^{-k}x_{0}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}.
We denote
v_{z}(\cdot)=u(\xi,\tau)-q_{(z,t)}d^{s}(\cdot)
and compute

\displaystyle\text{II}=

\displaystyle|v_{z}(x,t)-v_{z^{\prime}}(x,t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|v_{z}(x_{0},t)-v_{z^{\prime}}(x_{o},t^{\prime})|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|v_{z}(x_{k},t)-v_{z}(x_{k-1},t)|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|v_{z^{\prime}}(x_{k},t^{\prime})-v_{z^{\prime}}(x_{k-1},t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|v_{z}(x_{0},t)-v_{z}(x_{0},t^{\prime})|+|q_{(z,t)}d^{s}(x_{0},t^{\prime})-q_{(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})}d^{s}(x_{0},t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C|x_{0}-z|^{s}|x_{k}-x_{k-1}|^{2s-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C|x_{0}-z^{\prime}|^{s}|x_{k}-x_{k-1}|^{2s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{3s-1}{2s}}+C|t-t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k(2s-1)}|x-x_{0}|^{2s-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|t-t^{\prime}|^{s},

if
x_{0}
is chosen so that
|x_{0}-z|\leq C_{0}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2s}}
and that
(x_{0},t),(x_{0},t^{\prime})\in Q_{r}(y_{0},t_{0})\subset Q_{2r}(y_{0},t_{0})
for some
(y_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega.
Hence also

\left[u\right]_{C_{t}^{s}(\Omega\cap Q_{1/2})}\leq C,

and the claim is proven.
∎

4.
Regularity of the free boundary in space and time

In this section we connect the established boundary Harnack results to the regularity of the free boundary in the parabolic nonlocal obstacle problem in the subcritical regime.
We say that a function
u
solves the parabolic obstacle problem for integro-differential operators, if it holds

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\min\{(\partial_{t}+L)u,u-\varphi\}&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1)\\
u(\cdot,-1)&=&\varphi&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}

for a given function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
called the obstacle and some non-local elliptic operator
L
of the form (
1.1
).
The
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of the free boundary near regular points has been established in
[
2
,
13
]
. Here we prove that the free boundary is
C^{2,\alpha}_{p}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and set
\alpha=\min\{s,2-2s\}
. Suppose that
u
solves (
4.1
) for some operator
L
of the form (
1.1
), with kernel
K\in C^{5}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
. Assume that the obstacle
\varphi\in C^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Suppose that
0\in\partial\{u>\varphi\}
is a regular free boundary point.

Then the normal to the free boundary is
C^{s+\alpha-\varepsilon}_{p}
in
Q_{r_{0}},
for some
r_{0}>0
and every
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

In
[
2
]
(see also
[
13
]
) they prove that there exists
r_{0}>0
so that in
Q_{r_{0}}
the free boundary is
C^{1,\gamma}
for some
\gamma>0
in space-time and that up to a rotation of coordinates
\partial_{n}u\geq c_{1}d^{s}
, for some
c_{1}>0.

We can assume that
r_{0}=1
, since the problem is scale invariant. Let us denote

v=u-\varphi.

Since
u
solves (
4.1
), the partial derivatives of
v
solve

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(\partial_{t}+L)w&=&\partial_{e}f&\text{in }\Omega\cap Q_{1}\\
w&=&0&\text{in }\Omega^{c}\cap Q_{1},\end{array}\right.

for
f=-L\varphi
and
\Omega=\{u>\varphi\}=\{v>0\}.

Remember that
f
is independent of time and by assumption on
\varphi
, we have
f\in C^{1+s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and hence
\partial_{e}f\in C^{s}_{p}(\Omega\cap Q_{1})
. From
[
13
, Corollary 1.6]
(see also
[
4
]
for the case
(-\Delta)^{s}
and with a smaller
\alpha
) we deduce, that all the partial derivatives of
u
are
C^{\frac{\alpha-\varepsilon/2}{2s}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-1,1))
, and hence the same is true for partial derivatives of
v
.
Therefore we can apply Corollary
3.9
, which gives that all quotients
v_{i}/v_{n}
and
v_{t}/v_{n}
are
C^{1-\varepsilon}_{p}(\overline{\Omega}\cap Q_{1/2}),
with bounds on the norms.

Now notice that every component the normal vector
\nu(x,t)
to the level set
\{v=\tau\}
,
\tau>0
can be expressed as

\nu^{i}(x,t)=\frac{\partial_{i}v}{|\nabla_{(x,t)}v|}(x,t)=\frac{\partial_{i}v/\partial_{n}v}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(\partial_{j}v/\partial_{n}v)^{2}+1+(\partial_{t}v/\partial_{n}v)^{2}\right)^{1/2}}.

Letting
\tau\downarrow 0
, we get that the normal vector is
C^{1-\varepsilon}_{p}(\partial\Omega\cap Q_{1/2}).
Hence the free boundary is
C^{2-\varepsilon}_{p}
in
Q_{1/2}
.

Now we can apply Corollary
3.13
, which gives that the quotients
v_{i}/v_{n}
and
v_{t}/v_{n}
are
C^{s+\alpha-\varepsilon}_{p}(\overline{\Omega}\cap Q_{1/2}),
which furthermore implies that the normal to the free boundary is
C^{s+\alpha-\varepsilon}_{p}
in
Q_{1/2}
.
∎

Note that the above proof works already if the initial regularity of the free boundary is
C^{1}_{p}
.

Theorem
1.1
follows.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The claim follows from Proposition
4.1
, noting that
s+\alpha>1
.
∎

. We split it furthermore as follows

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\rho(x,t)\int_{B_{1}}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}D\phi_{t}(x)(y-x)K(D\phi_{t}(x)\langle y-x\rangle)|x-y|^{-n-2s}dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{1}}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}(\rho(y,t)-\rho(x,t))D\phi_{t}(x)(y-x)K(D\phi_{t}(x)\langle y-x\rangle)|x-y|^{-n-2s}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{11}+I_{12}.

The integral
I_{11}
is the term with the lowest order of
|x-y|
, but we exploit the fact that it is almost an evaluation of some homogeneous operator of order
2s
of the function
(x_{n})_{+}^{p}
. To do so, we decouple the space variables in the following way

I_{11}=I_{11}(x,x,t)

where

I_{11}(\xi,x,t)=\rho(x,t)\int_{B_{1}}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}D\phi_{t}(\xi)\langle y-x\rangle K(D\phi_{t}(\xi)\langle y-x\rangle)|x-y|^{-n-2s+1}dy.

Then by
[
20
, Lemma 3.4]
we conclude that

\displaystyle I_{11}(\xi,x,t)=\rho(x,t)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}D\phi_{t}(\xi)\langle y-x\rangle K(D\phi_{t}(\xi)\langle y-x\rangle)|x-y|^{-n-2s+1}dy.

The obtained integral converges and hence
I_{11}
is
C^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}_{t}
.

We proceed with analysing
I_{12}
. We want to bound the time incremental difference of
I_{12}
. Since we are getting a lot of terms, we simplify the notation in the following way: we denote
\Delta_{t}f=f(t)-f(t^{\prime})
, as well as
\Delta_{x}f=f(x)-f(y)
, furthermore
\rho=(Q\nabla d+d\nabla Q)J
, where
J
stands for
|\phi_{t}(y)|.

We furthermore split
I_{12}

I_{12}=\int_{B_{r}(x)}\ldots dy+\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{r}(x)}\ldots dy=B_{12r}+I_{12rc},

for
r=\frac{x_{n}}{2}.

In the region near the pole we need to extract both
|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
, as well as
|x-y|
from the increment of
\rho
. This is done with adding an subtracting
D\rho(x,t)(y-x)
from
\rho(y,t)-\rho(x,t)
. We get

\displaystyle I_{12r}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}(\rho(y,t)-\rho(x,t)-D\rho(x,t)(y-x))D\phi_{t}(x)(y-x)K(D\phi_{t}(x)(y-x))dy

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{r}(x)}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}D\rho(x,t)(x-y)D\phi_{t}(x)(y-x)K(D\phi_{t}(x)\langle y-x\rangle)|x-y|^{-n-2s}dy.

We can write

S_{1}(x,y,t)=\rho(y,t)-\rho(x,t)-D\rho(x,t)(y-x)=\int_{x}^{y}(D\rho(\xi,t)-D\rho(x,t))d\xi\cdot(y-x).

Using the simplified notation we compute

D\rho=D(J)(Q\nabla d+d\nabla Q)+J(2\nabla Q\nabla d+QD^{2}d),

and hence

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}D\rho=

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}(D(J))(Q\nabla d+d\nabla Q)+\Delta_{t}J(2\nabla Q\nabla d+QD^{2}d)

\displaystyle+D(J)(Q\Delta_{t}\nabla d+\Delta_{t}d\nabla Q)+J(2\nabla Q\Delta_{t}\nabla d+Q\Delta_{t}D^{2}d).

Lemma
A.1
gives that

|\Delta_{t}D(J)|,|\Delta_{t}D^{2}d|\leq Cr^{-1}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}},\quad\quad|D(J)|,|D^{2}d|\leq Cr^{\beta-2},

and so we can bound

|\Delta_{t}D\rho(\xi,t)|\leq Cr^{-1}(Q(0)+|x|+r)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}},

since
|Q(\xi)|\leq|Q(0)|+C|\xi|\leq C(|Q(0)+|x|+r)
.

We are now ready to estimate the incremental difference of
I_{12r}
. Whenever the difference operator does not apply on terms with
\rho
, we get
|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
from the other terms, and we are left with

Cr^{\beta-2}\int_{B_{r}(x)}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}|y-x|^{-n-2s+2}dy,

which we can bound with

Cr^{\beta-s-1}
. When the difference operator lands on the term with
\rho,
we get the following

Cr^{-1}(|Q(0)|+|x|+r)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}(y_{n})_{+}^{s-1}|y-x|^{-n-2s+2}dy,

which is bounded by
Cr^{-s}(|Q(0)|+|x|+r)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
.

We proceed with
I_{12rc}
. We can estimate

|\Delta_{t}I_{12rc}|\leq\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{r}(x)}y_{n+}^{s-1}|\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}\rho||x-y|^{-n-2s+1}dy+C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}\int_{B_{1}}y_{n+}^{s-1}|x-y|^{-n-2s+\beta},

where the second term bounds all the other contributions of
\Delta_{t}
that do not come from
\Delta_{x}\rho
. We continue with computing the double incremental difference

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}\rho\leq

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}\left(\Delta_{x}J(Q\nabla d+d\nabla Q)+J(\Delta_{x}Q\nabla d+Q\Delta_{x}\nabla d+\Delta_{x}d\nabla Q)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}J(Q\nabla d+d\nabla Q)+\Delta_{t}J(\Delta_{x}Q\nabla d+Q\Delta_{x}\nabla d+\Delta_{x}d\nabla Q)

\displaystyle+\Delta_{x}J(Q\Delta_{t}\nabla d+\Delta_{t}d\nabla Q)+J(\Delta_{x}Q\Delta_{t}\nabla d+Q\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}\nabla d+\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}d\nabla Q).

Where we have the single increments we do the straightforward estimate. It holds
|\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}J|\leq C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
. The double increment of the distance function is better, since
d\in C^{\beta}_{p}
, so using the fundamental theorem of calculus we can extract
|\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}d|\leq|x-y||t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
.
Hence we get

\displaystyle|\Delta_{t}\Delta_{x}\rho|\leq

\displaystyle C(|Q(0)|+|x|)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}+C|x-y||t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}+C|x-y|^{\beta-1}|t-t^{\prime}|.

Plugging it inside the integral we end up with

|\Delta_{t}I_{12rc}|\leq C\left((|Q(0)|+|x|)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}r^{-s}+|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}r^{-\varepsilon}\right)

in view of
[
1
, Lemma A.9]
and Lemma
A.5
.

The other terms
I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}
are estimated in a similar manner. Let us analyse the term
I_{2}
. Taking the incremental difference and estimating we get

|\Delta_{t}I_{2}|\leq\int_{B_{1}}y_{n+}^{s-1}|\rho(y)||\Delta_{t}S||x-y|^{-n-2s}dy+C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}\int_{B_{1}}y_{n+}^{s-1}|x-y|^{-n-2s+\beta}.

The second integral gives
Cx_{n}^{-\varepsilon}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
. In the first one we split
|\rho(y)|\leq C(|Q(0)|+|x|+|x-y|)
. In the term with
|x-y|
we can use estimate (
5.1
) for
|\Delta_{t}S|,
to get

C|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}\int_{B_{1}}y_{n+}^{s-1}|x-y|^{-n-2s+2}dy,

which is bounded with
Cx_{n}^{-\varepsilon}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}
, thanks to Lemma
A.5
.
The remaining term

(|Q(0)|+|x|)\int_{B_{1}}y_{n+}^{s-1}|\Delta_{t}S||x-y|^{-n-2s}dy

we split into the integrals in regions
B_{r}(x)
and
B_{1}\backslash B_{r}(x)
. Away from the pole we can still use the estimate (
5.1
) and estimate the obtained integral with
[
1
, Lemma A.9]
, to get
C(|Q(0)|+|x|)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}r^{-s}
. In the region near the pole we use (
5.2
), which gives

(|Q(0)|+|x|)r^{-1}|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}\int_{B_{1}}y_{n+}^{s-1}|x-y|^{-n-2s+2}dy

which is also bounded by
C(|Q(0)|+|x|)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}r^{-s}
.

The analysis of the integral
I_{3}
is the same as the one of
I_{2}
, using (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) instead of (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). The term
I_{4}
is the same.

We obtained

|I(x,t)-I(x,t^{\prime})|\leq C\left((|Q(0)|+|x|)r^{-s}+r^{-\varepsilon}\right)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}},

whenever
(x,t),(x,t^{\prime})\in Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
with
(x_{0})_{n}=2r
. This implies the second and the third estimate in the claim, as well as

\displaystyle|I(x,t)-I(x,t^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(|Q(0)|+|x|+r^{s-\varepsilon}\right)|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1-s}{2s}}

\displaystyle\leq C(|Q(0)|+r_{0}^{s-\varepsilon})|t-t^{\prime}|^{\frac{\beta-1-s}{2s}},

if additionally
|x_{0}|\leq r_{0}
.
It follows from
[
19
, Lemma B.2]
, that
\left[I\right]_{C^{\beta-1-s}_{p}(Q_{r_{0}})}\leq C(|Q(0)|+r_{0}^{s-\varepsilon}),
since the space part of the estimate is provided by
[
1
, Corollary 2.3]
.
Since the flattening map
\phi
is Lipschitz the claim is proved.
∎

Appendix A
Technical tools and lemmas

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Omega
be
C^{\beta}_{p}
in
Q_{1}
, for some
\beta\in(2s,2).

Then there exists a function
d\colon\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

d\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C^{\infty}_{x}(\{d>0\}),\quad C^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega\cap Q_{1})\leq d\leq C\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega\cap Q_{1})\text{ in }\Omega\cap Q_{1},

|(\partial_{t})^{l}D^{k}d|\leq C_{k,l}d^{\beta-k-2sl},\quad\text{in }\{d>0\},\text{ whenever }2sl+k>\beta,

\left[D^{2}d\right]_{C^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}_{t}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{-1},

whenever
d(x_{0},t_{0})\leq C_{0}r
and
Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\Omega
.

Furthermore there exists a diffeomorphism
\psi\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C^{\infty}_{x}(\{d>0\},\{x_{n}>0\})
, such that

\psi(\Omega\cap Q_{1})=Q_{1}\cap\{x_{n}>0\},\quad d(\psi^{-1}(x,t))=x_{n},\quad|D^{k}\psi|\leq Cd^{\beta-k},

in
\{d>0\},
for all
k>\beta
and whenever
d(x_{0},t_{0})\leq C_{0}r
and
Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\Omega
, we have

\left[D^{2}\psi\right]_{C^{\frac{\beta-1}{2s}}_{t}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\{|x^{\prime}|\leq 1\}\times(-1,1))
be such that
\Omega\cap Q_{1}=\{x_{n}>f(x^{\prime},t)\}.
We can extend
f
to full space
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, so that its norm does not increase. Let
L=8||\nabla f||_{L^{\infty}}
and
K=16s8^{2s-1}||\partial_{t}f||_{L^{\infty}}
.
Choose
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1})
with
\int\varphi dz=1
,
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-1,1)
with
\int_{-1}^{1}\psi d\sigma=1
, and define

F(x,t,\tau)=x_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}f\left(x^{\prime}-\frac{\tau}{L}z^{\prime},t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma.

Since
\varphi
and
\psi
are compactly supported,
F\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\cap C^{2s}_{\tau}
. We compute

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\tau}F(x,t,\tau)=&\frac{1}{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\nabla f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\cdot z\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma+\\
&+\frac{2s}{K}\tau^{2s-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\sigma\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma,\\
\nabla F=&e_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\nabla f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma,\\
\partial_{t}F=&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma,\end{split}

so thanks to the choice of
L
and
K
, we have

|\partial_{\tau}F(x,t,\tau)|\leq\frac{1}{4},
for
\tau<8
. Therefore, in combination with
F(x,t,0)=x_{n}-f(x^{\prime},t)
, we conclude that for every
(x,t)\in\{x_{n}-f(x,t)\in(0,3)\}
there exists a unique
d=d(x,t)\in(0,8),
so that

(A.2)

d(x,t)=F(x,t,d(x,t)),

as well as

\left|x_{n}-f(x,t)-d(x,t)\right|=\left|F(x,t,0)-F(x,t,d(x,t))\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}d(x,t),

which implies that
d
is comparable to
x_{n}-f(x,t)
.
By implicit function theorem
d
is
C^{1}
in both variables. Differentiating (
A.2
), we get

\nabla d(x,t)=\frac{\nabla F(x,t,d(x,t))}{1-\partial_{\tau}F(x,t,d(x,t))},\quad\partial_{t}d(x,t)=\frac{\partial_{t}F(x,t,d(x,t))}{1-\partial_{\tau}F(x,t,d(x,t))}.

Notice also, that
\partial_{n}d\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Moreover since
F\in C^{\infty}(\{\tau>0\})
(it is a convolution with a
C^{\infty}
function), it also holds
d\in C^{\infty}(\{d>0\})
. To get the expressions for higher order derivatives of
d
, we differentiate (
A.2
) and then insert the suitable derivatives of
F
.
We show here only the computations for the second order derivatives, higher order ones are established analogously. Procedure is always the following. We perform an affine change of variables, to move the variables
x,t
to
\varphi,\psi
, then derive and finally change the coordinates back. The computations are long and technical, therefore we present only the results. We get

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\partial_{i}F

\displaystyle=-\frac{L}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{i}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\partial_{j}\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle=-\frac{L}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\left(\partial_{i}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)-\partial_{i}f(x,t)\right)\partial_{j}\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

where in the last equality we used that
\int\partial_{j}\varphi=0
, since
\varphi
is compactly supported. Similarly we get

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\partial_{\tau}F=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\nabla f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\cdot\partial_{j}(z\varphi(z))\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle+2s\frac{L}{K}\tau^{2s-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\partial_{j}\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\partial_{\tau}F=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\tau L}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\sum_{j,k}\partial_{k}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\partial_{j}(z_{k}z_{j}\varphi(z))\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle-\frac{2s}{\tau L}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\sum_{k}\partial_{k}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)z_{k}\varphi(z)(\sigma\psi(\sigma))^{\prime}dzd\sigma

\displaystyle-\frac{2s}{K}\tau^{2s-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\sigma\nabla\cdot(z\varphi(z))\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle-\frac{(2s)^{2}}{K}\tau^{2s-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\sigma^{2s-1}\varphi(z)(\sigma^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}\psi(\sigma))^{\prime}dzd\sigma.

Using that
f\in C^{\beta}_{p}
, we conclude that all the derivatives above are bounded with
C\tau^{\beta-2}
.
We also compute

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}F=

\displaystyle-\frac{K}{\tau^{2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\varphi(z)\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle\nabla\partial_{t}F=

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\nabla\varphi(z)\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\partial_{t}F=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\nabla\cdot(z\varphi(z))\psi(\sigma)dzd\sigma

\displaystyle+\frac{2s}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\partial_{t}f\left(x-\frac{\tau}{L}z,t-\frac{\tau^{2s}}{K}\sigma\right)\varphi(z)(\sigma\psi(\sigma))^{\prime}dzd\sigma.

Since again in every expression there are derivatives of compactly supported functions, we deduce

|\partial_{tt}F|\leq C\tau^{\beta-4s}
and
|\nabla\partial_{t}F|,|\partial_{\tau}\partial_{t}F|\leq C\tau^{\beta-2s-1}
.

Finally, differentiating (
A.2
) twice, we extract

\displaystyle D^{2}d

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\partial_{\tau}F}\left(D^{2}F+2\nabla\partial_{\tau}F\nabla d^{T}+\partial_{\tau\tau}F\nabla d\nabla d^{T}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}d

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\partial_{\tau}F}\left(\partial_{tt}F+2\partial_{t}\partial_{\tau}F\partial_{t}d+\partial_{\tau\tau}F(\partial_{t}d)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\nabla\partial_{t}d

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\partial_{\tau}F}\left(\nabla\partial_{t}F+\nabla\partial_{\tau}F\partial_{t}d+\partial_{t}\partial_{\tau}F\nabla d+\partial_{\tau\tau}F\nabla d\partial d\right),

where all the arguments are either
(x,t)
or
(x,t,d(x,t))
. The previous computations yield

|D^{2}d|\leq Cd^{\beta-2},\quad\quad|\partial_{tt}d|\leq Cd^{\beta-4s},\quad\quad|\nabla\partial_{t}d|\leq Cd^{\beta-1-2s}.

These estimates imply that
d\in C^{\beta}_{p}(\{d\geq 0\}).
Finally using the estimates for
D^{3}d
and
\partial_{t}D^{2}d
, we get

\left[D^{2}d\right]_{C^{\beta-1}_{p}(Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{-1},

whenever
d(x_{0},t_{0})\leq C_{0}r
and
Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset\Omega
.
To extend
d
to the full set
\{x_{n}>f(x,t)\}
, we take a cut-off
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\left[0,\infty\right)),
such that
\phi=1
in
\left[0,3\right)
,
\phi=0
in
\left[4,\infty\right)
and define

\textbf{d}(x,t)=\phi(d(x,t))d(x,t)+(1-\phi(d(x,t))x_{n},

which satisfies all properties from the claim.

Once we get
d
, we define

\psi(x,t)=(x^{\prime},d(x,t),t).

Then
|D_{x,t}\psi|=|\partial_{n}d|>\frac{3}{4}
, and the derivatives of
\psi
inherit estimates of derivatives of
d
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
a\in(0,2s)
and
b\in(0,1)
.
Let
f,g,Q
be functions on
Q_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})
which satisfy
||f-Qg||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\leq Cr^{a+b}
, and
\left[f-Qg\right]_{C_{p}^{a+b}(Q_{r}(2re_{n},0))}\leq C
. Assume that
g
satisfies
||g^{-1}||_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}(2re_{n},0))}\leq Cr^{-b}
, and
\left[g\right]_{C_{p}^{a}(Q_{r}(2re_{n},0))}\leq Cr^{b-a},
for all
r\in(0,1)
.

Then we have

\left[\frac{f}{g}-Q\right]_{C_{p}^{a}(Q_{r}(2re_{n},t_{0}))}\leq C.

Proof.

We denote
v=f-Qg
. Note that by
[
19
, Lemma B.2]

v
is
C^{\alpha+\beta}_{p}(\mathcal{C})
, where
\mathcal{C}=\cup_{r>0}Q_{r}(2re_{n},0)
. Hence also
\nabla v(0,0)=0
if
a+b>1
, and then also
||\nabla v||_{Q_{r}\cap\mathcal{C}}\leq Cr^{a+b-1}
.

Assume first that
a<1.
Then

\displaystyle\left[\frac{v}{g}\right]_{C^{a}_{p}(Q_{r}(2re_{n},0))}\leq

\displaystyle\left[v\right]||g^{-1}||+||v||\left[g^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{b}r^{-b}+Cr^{a+b}r^{-2b}r^{b-a}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C.

Let now
a>1
.
We estimate

\displaystyle\left[g^{-1}\right]_{C^{a}_{p}}=

\displaystyle\left[g^{-1}\right]_{C^{\frac{a}{2s}}_{t}}+\left[\nabla(g^{-1})\right]_{C^{a-1}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||g^{-2}||\left[g\right]_{C^{\frac{a}{2s}}_{t}}+2\left[g^{-1}\right]_{C^{a-1}_{p}}||g^{-1}\nabla g||+||g^{-2}||\left[\nabla g\right]_{C^{a-1}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{-2b}r^{b-a}+Cr^{-2b}r^{b-a+1}r^{-b}r^{b-1}+Cr^{-2b}r^{b-a}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{-a-b},

where the estimate for
||\nabla g||
follows from the one for
\left[\nabla g\right]_{C^{a-1}_{p}}
. This gives

\displaystyle\left[\frac{v}{g}\right]_{C^{a}_{p}(Q_{r}(2re_{n},0))}\leq

\displaystyle\left[v\right]||g^{-1}||+||v||\left[g^{-1}\right]+\left[v\right]_{C^{a-1}_{p}}||\nabla(g^{-1})||+||\nabla v||\left[g^{-1}\right]_{C^{a-1}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{b}r^{-b}+Cr^{a+b}r^{-a-b}+Cr^{b+1}r^{-2b}r^{b-1}+Cr^{a+b-1}r^{-2b}r^{b-a+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C,

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and let

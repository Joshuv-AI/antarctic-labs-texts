Remarks on the positivity of the cotangent bundle of a K3 surface
Frank Gounelas, John Christian Ottem

Abstract




Abstract.
Using recent results of Bayer–Macrì, we compute in many cases the pseudoeffective and nef cones of the projectivised
cotangent bundle of a smooth projective K3 surface. We then use these results to construct explicit families of smooth curves on which the
restriction of the cotangent bundle is not semistable (and hence not nef). In particular, this leads to a counterexample
to a question of Campana–Peternell.

Keywords.
K3 surface; cotangent bundle; positivity; cones of divisors

1. Introduction

Miyaoka proved that the cotangent bundle of a non-uniruled variety is generically nef, in the sense that its restriction to a sufficiently ample and general complete intersection curve is a nef vector bundle
[
MR84
]
. This in turn has many interesting geometric consequences; see
[
CP11
,
Pet11
]
for more general properties of such vector bundles, in particular for the tangent and cotangent bundles. The starting point of the present paper was the following question of Campana and Peternell:

Question 1.1

(Question 1.6 in
[
CP11
]
)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a birational morphism of smooth projective non-uniruled varieties. Is
f^{*}{}^{1}_{Y}
generically nef?

We answer this question in the negative, by constructing a certain blow-up
f:X\to S
of a K3 surface. Let us sketch the construction. Let
S
be a generic quartic surface and let
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
denote the projectivisation of the cotangent bundle. Let
L={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}({}_{S}^{1})}(1)
be the relative hyperplane bundle and
H
the pullback of the polarisation on
S
. Then one finds that
D=L+2H
is a base-point free ample divisor (see Proposition
3.1
), so a generic element
X\in|D|
is a smooth surface and the projection
\pi:X\to S
is birational. Moreover, as
L\cdot D^{2}<0
, the canonical quotient
\pi^{*}{}^{1}_{S}\to L\to 0
, shows that
\pi^{*}{}^{1}_{S}
is not nef restricted to any smooth curve
C\subset X
in the (ample) linear system
|mD|_{X}|
. In particular
\pi^{*}{}^{1}_{S}
is not generically nef.

This example has other interesting features. For instance, the curve
C
maps isomorphically to a (movable) smooth curve
D\subset S
with the property that
{}^{1}_{S}|_{D}
is not nef, and hence not stable. This addresses
[
CP19
, Example 4.3]
, which asks about explicit examples of curves destabilising the cotangent bundle of a K3. We show more generally the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
be a general polarised K3 surface of degree
d
.

(i)

If
d=2
then there is a positive-dimensional family of smooth curves
C
in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(6)|
so that
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}
is not semistable.

(ii)

If
d=4,6
there are families as above in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(3)|

(
and another in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(4)|
if
d=4)
.

(iii)

If
d=8
there is a family in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(3)|
so that
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}
is strictly semistable.

These results should be compared with the results of Bogomolov and Hein (see Theorem
2.2
), which essentially say that if either the degree of the polarisation or the multiple of the polarisation in which the curve lies is high, then there can be no smooth destabilising curves, and also that a general curve in any multiple of the polarisation is not destabilising.

More generally, one can ask how the sets of all curves and effective divisors on
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
reflect the geometric
properties of
S
. In particular this is closely related to describing the nef and effective cones on
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
,
and in turn the existence of sections of the twisted symmetric differentials
\operatorname{S}^{a}({}^{1}_{S})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(b)
. These
subjects have a long history, going back to the work of Kobayashi
[
Kob80
]
, who showed that a simply connected
Calabi–Yau variety has no symmetric differentials, i.e.,
\textrm{H}^{0}(X,\operatorname{S}^{m}{}^{1}_{X})=0
for all
m\geq 1
. For K3 surfaces, this was extended by Nakayama
[
Nak04
]
, who showed that in fact
L={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})}(1)
is not even pseudoeffective on
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
(i.e., its class is not a limit of effective classes). Very recently this was extended to simply connected Calabi–Yau threefolds by Druel
[
Dru18
, Theorem 6.1]
and by Höring–Peternell
[
HP19
]
to all dimensions.

In general computing these cones explicitly seems like a difficult problem. In some low-degree cases one can use the well-known projective models to study the cohomology of the symmetric powers of the cotangent bundle, but in general the cones seem to depend on the degree
d
in a rather subtle way. We are able to solve the problem at least for infinitely many
d
, using results of Bayer–Macrì.

The starting observation is that
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
embeds in the Hilbert scheme
S^{[2]}
as the exceptional divisor of
the Hilbert–Chow morphism, so one can try restricting extremal divisors from the pseudoeffective and nef cones of

S^{[2]}
to
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
, which are known from the results of Bayer–Macrì
[
BM14
]
: the effective cone

\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(S^{[2]})
(resp. nef cone) is spanned by
B
(resp.
\widetilde{H}
) and one more extremal ray which in what follows
we call the
second extremal ray
(see Theorem
2.4
). We prove that
the restriction of this second ray is indeed extremal for infinitely many
d
, but also that this is not always the case. In what
follows, we will consider the Pell-type equation

(1.1)

x^{2}-4ty^{2}=5.

Theorem B

(See Section
3
)
.

Let
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
be a primitively polarised K3 surface of degree
d=2t
and Picard number one.
First, if
t
is a square and (
1.1
) has no solution, then

\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))=\operatorname{Nef}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))=\left\langle H,L+\frac{2}{\sqrt{t}}H\right\rangle.

Next, the restriction of the second extremal ray of
\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(S^{[2]})
is extremal on
\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))
if the following three conditions hold

(i)

t
is not a square,

(ii)

(
1.1
) has no solutions,

(iii)

the minimal solution
(a,b)
of
x^{2}-ty^{2}=1
has
b
even.

Finally, the restriction of the second extremal ray of
\operatorname{Nef}(S^{[2]})
is extremal on
\operatorname{Nef}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))
if (
1.1
) has a solution.

In particular we know the effective cone of
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
if

d=4,8,12,18,20,24,26,32,34,36,\ldots,

whereas we know the nef cone if

d=2,8,10,18,22,32,38,50,58,62,\ldots.

We emphasise that in the above cases the cones are explicitly computable, the slopes given in terms of solutions to Pell-type equations. We defer to Section
3
for particulars.

As mentioned above, we also prove that the above trick does not work in general: we show that the restriction of the nef
cone of
S^{[2]}
is a strictly smaller subcone of
\operatorname{Nef}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))
in the degree four and six cases (see Section

4
). The cases in which this restriction is extremal depend on the geometry of the minimal models of

S^{[2]}
, and one can find results and a classification in work of Bayer–Macrì, Hassett–Tschinkel, Markman and others.

In the final section we analyse in more detail these contractions in the cases of K3s of degree
d=2,4,6,8
, improving bounds on, or computing the aforementioned slopes, and describe the geometry of the extremal divisors.

Notation.
We work over the complex numbers. For
G
a vector bundle on a variety
X
we say that
G
is pseudoeffective, big, nef, ample if
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}(G)}(1)
is a pseudoeffective, big, nef, ample line bundle on
{\mathbb P}(G)
respectively. We use Grothendieck notation throughout, so that
{\mathbb P}(G)
parameterises one-dimensional quotients of
G
. In particular if
\pi:{\mathbb P}(G)\to X
is the projection, we have a universal quotient line bundle
\pi^{*}G\to{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}(G)}(1)\to 0
, and for a surjection
G_{1}\to G_{2}\to 0
we have an induced inclusion
{\mathbb P}(G_{2})\subseteq{\mathbb P}(G_{1})
so that
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}(G_{1})}(1)|_{{\mathbb P}(G_{2})}={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}(G_{2})}(1)
.

2. Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we will let
S
denote a K3 surface with a primitive ample line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
of
degree
d={\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)^{2}
, which is always an even number, so we will sometimes write
d=2t
. We will mostly assume that
\operatorname{Pic}(S)={\mathbb Z}
, generated by
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
. Let
E={\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
and
\pi:E\to S
the projection. In this case the Picard group of
E
is generated by
L={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})}(1)
and
H=\pi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
. We have the following intersection numbers:

\displaystyle L^{3}=s_{2}({}^{1}_{S})

\displaystyle=-24,\qquad

\displaystyle L\cdot H^{2}

\displaystyle=d,

\displaystyle L^{2}\cdot H=s_{1}({}^{1}_{S})

\displaystyle=0,\qquad

\displaystyle\quad H^{3}

\displaystyle=0.

We denote by
\alpha_{e,d}
and
\alpha_{n,d}
the (positive) real numbers which are the slopes of the pseudoeffective and nef cones of
E
respectively. In other words,

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))=\left\langle H,L+\alpha_{e,d}H\right\rangle

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nef}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S}))=\left\langle H,L+\alpha_{n,d}H\right\rangle

.

These numbers are often called the
pseudoeffective
and
nef thresholds
for
{}^{1}_{S}
. We denote by
S^{[2]}
the Hilbert scheme of length two subschemes on
S
. For a divisor
H
on
S
, we get a divisor
\widetilde{H}
on
S^{[2]}
by taking subschemes incident to
H
. We also have the Hilbert–Chow morphism

\operatorname{HC}:S^{[2]}\to\operatorname{Sym}^{2}S

taking a subscheme to its support. This gives
\operatorname{Pic}(S^{[2]})\simeq\operatorname{Pic}(S)\oplus{\mathbb Z}B
where
B=\frac{1}{2}E
and
E
is the exceptional divisor of
\operatorname{HC}
(the divisor of non-reduced subschemes). Note in particular that
E\simeq{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
. In terms of
L
and
H
, we have the restrictions

\widetilde{H}|_{E}=2H,\qquad B|_{E}=-L,

where the first follows from the fact that
\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)\otimes\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
restricts
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(2)
on the diagonal
\Delta\subset S\times S
.

2.1. Stability of restrictions

As mentioned in the introduction, the positivity of
{}^{1}_{S}
on a curve is closely related to stability. This is because of Hartshorne’s theorem
[
Laz04
, §6.4.B]
which implies that on a smooth curve, a vector bundle of degree zero is nef if and only if it is semistable. In particular,

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
be a K3 surface and
C\subset S
be a smooth curve. Then
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}
is nef if and only if it is semistable.

A consequence of Yau’s Theorem is that
{}^{1}_{S}
is stable with respect to any polarisation. This does not imply that the restriction to each curve
C
is stable, as there can be negative quotients
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}\to Q\to 0
which do not come from quotients of
{}^{1}_{S}
. On the other hand, one can apply results of Bogomolov to show that for any stable vector bundle
G
on a surface
S
, if we take a smooth curve in a (computable) high enough multiple of the polarisation, then the restriction
G|_{C}
is stable. In our case, Bogomolov’s theorem
[
HL10
, Theorem 7.3.5]
implies that if
C\in|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(n)|
for
n>49
then
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}
is stable. In fact, if
S
is a K3 surface this has been extended in various directions by Hein. Hein’s results apply to any K3 surface, but we only state them in the case of Picard number one.

Theorem 2.2

(Hein)
.

Let
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
be a smooth projective K3 surface of degree
2t
and Picard number one.

(1)

If
t(2m-1)>48
then for any smooth curve
C\in|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(m)|
we have that
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}
is a stable vector bundle.

(2)

For any
t\geq 1
,
m\geq 1
,
(t,m)\neq(1,1),(1,2)
and general curve
C\in|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(m)|
, the restriction
{}^{1}_{S}|_{C}
is semistable.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from
[
Hei06
, Theorem 2.8]
. Then (ii) follows from
[
Hei96
, Korollar 3.11]
, and the fact that a general member of
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(m)|
is not hyperelliptic for these values of
t
and
m
.
∎

To be more concrete, for
m=1
we obtain
t>48
, for
m=2
,
t>16
and
m=3
gives
t>9
. On the other hand, for
t\geq 1
we obtain that
m\geq 25
, which is an improvement on the bound of Bogomolov mentioned above.

In other words finding curves which destabilise the cotangent bundle is limited to low degree K3s and low degree multiples of the polarisation, and even more so only to small parts of the linear systems considered.
Constructing such curves in general seems out of reach, so in Section
4
we proceed on a case by case basis.

An initial natural attempt is to consider the ramification curves of a generic projection
S\to{\mathbb P}^{2}
. The starting point here is the fact that in the degree two case, the ramification curve
C
of the degree two cover
f:S\to{\mathbb P}^{2}
is smooth, and the restriction of
{}^{1}_{S}
to it is not semistable (see Section
4.1
). As explained in the proof below, these curves lie in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(3)|
, so Hein’s Theorem
2.2
implies that they cannot be destabilising if
t>9
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
be a K3 surface of degree
2t>2
and
S\subset{\mathbb P}^{t+1}
the induced embedding. For a linear space

denote by

the ramification divisor of the projection
S\to{\mathbb P}^{2}
from
\Lambda

\Lambda

t\leq 3
then
{}^{1}_{S}|_{R_{\Lambda}}
is not semistable, whereas for
t=4

Proof.

From
[

]
we know that since
\Lambda
is general, the projection
S\to{\mathbb P}^{2}
is a morphism and
R_{\Lambda}
is a smooth irreducible curve in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(3)|
. Consider the natural morphism
\phi:{\mathbb P}({}_{S}^{1})\to\operatorname{Gr}(2,t+2)
(see Section

). This factors as

{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})\subset{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{t+1}}|_{S})\subset{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{t+1}})={\mathbb P}(U^{\vee})\xrightarrow{p}\operatorname{Gr}(2,t+2)

where

is the universal subbundle on the Grassmannian and
p
is the bundle projection. Let us consider
\sigma_{2}\subset\operatorname{Gr}(2,t+2)
the Schubert cycle parameterising lines meeting the
(t-2)

\Lambda
. The class
\phi^{*}\sigma_{2}
is represented by a smooth curve
C\subset{\mathbb P}({}_{S}^{1})
whose image in

is a curve whose points correspond to lines meeting

R_{\Lambda}
. We claim that

is negative on this curve.

To compute the class of

, note that
\sigma_{2}=c_{1}^{2}(U^{\vee})-c_{2}(U^{\vee})

{\mathbb P}(U^{\vee})

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}(U^{\vee})}(1)

, and hence by pushing down a degree zero quotient on

. Hence

is semistable as it is an extension of degree zero line bundles, but not stable.
∎

3. The cones of divisors of
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})

We begin with
some simple bounds for the nef and pseudoeffective cones on
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{X})
for a smooth projective variety
X\subset{\mathbb P}^{n}
. A convenient ingredient here is the morphism

(3.1)

f:{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{X})\to{\rm Gr}(2,n+1)

which associates a tangent vector to the corresponding point in the Grassmannian. For
L
the
tautological line bundle on
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{X})
and
H
the polarisation on
X
as in Section
2
, one checks
that
f^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\rm Gr}(2,n+1)}(1)=L+2H
. When
f
maps to a lower-dimensional variety,
L+2H
is extremal in the nef cone
as well as the pseudoeffective cone.

Proposition 3.1
.

The vector bundle
{}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}}(m)
is nef if and only if
m\geq 2
. More precisely,

\operatorname{Nef}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}}))=\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}({\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}}))=\left\langle H,L+2H\right\rangle.

Moreover, if
X\subseteq{\mathbb P}^{n}
is a smooth subvariety, then
L+2H
is nef, and ample if and only if
X
does not contain a line.

Proof.

The equality of the cones follows from the preceding paragraph. The surjection
{}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}}|_{X}\to{}^{1}_{X}
allows us to factor
f
as
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{X})\subset{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}}|_{X})\subset{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}})\to\operatorname{Gr}(2,n+1)
. So
L+2H
is base-point free, and ample if and only if
f
is finite, i.e., when
X
does not contain a line.
∎

Thus, if
X\subset{\mathbb P}^{n}
a smooth subvariety, the slope of the nef cone of
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{X})
(with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}^{n}}(1)|_{X}
) is at most 2. Using results of Section
2.2
, we can improve this in the case of K3 surfaces. Note first that if
S
a K3 surface of degree
2t
, then the nef cone is contained in the positive cone
\{D\,|\,D^{3}\geq 0\}
. However, the boundary divisor
L+\frac{2}{\sqrt{t}}H
of this cone can in general fail to be nef (see Section 4). Nevertheless, the following shows these two cones approximate each other if
t
is large.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
be a K3 surface of degree
d=2t
and Picard number one. Then for
t>1
we have

\alpha_{n,2t}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{t-\frac{5}{4}}}\,\text{ and }\,\alpha_{e,2t}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{t}}.

There are similar lower bounds for

Proof.

From Theorem

is determined by the fundamental solution to the Pell-type equation

\widetilde{H}-\sqrt{t}B
is the boundary of the nef cone. If

is not a square, for a solution

. Rewriting the equation as

\frac{y}{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}-1}{x^{2}}}

shows that

\frac{y}{x}\geq\frac{\sqrt{t-1}}{t}
, and so we find that the divisor
\widetilde{H}-\sqrt{t-1}B
is nef. If

has a solution, then for such a solution

with
x_{1}>0

\widetilde{H}-2t\frac{y_{1}}{x_{1}}B
. It must be that

from which the Pell-type equation gives

\frac{y_{1}}{x_{1}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{4t-5}}{4t}.

This implies that

For the effective slope, if

The lower bound from

The second family was already proven to be destabilising in Proposition

Here

Remark 4.4
.

One could alternatively use cohomological techniques to compute that the divisor given by the restriction
2L+2H=(\widetilde{H}-2B)|_{E}
is extremal in the pseudoeffective cone. We do not reproduce the proof here as it is a lengthy computation, but it follows from the fact that

is the intersection

of a smooth cubic and quadric, so one can take symmetric products of the Euler sequence restricted to

and the cotangent sequence

does not have cubic growth in

.

We begin by describing a birational model of

, as found for example in
[

, Lemma 6.3.1]
, which is induced by
M=2\widetilde{H}-3B
.
Let

be a general cubic fourfold with a node at
p=[0,0,0,0,0,1]

the quadric and cubic in
{\mathbb P}^{4}
respectively. Projecting from

onto this

we obtain a rational map

. In the Fano scheme of lines

of

, the locus of lines through

is parameterised by the complete intersection
S=\{q=c=0\}
in this

which is a smooth K3 surface of degree six and Picard number one. Consider now the map

taking two lines
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
through

and giving the residual line of intersection of

with the plane spanned by
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}

contains a 2-plane through

whose constituent two lines give a unique point of

is birational. Moreover one can prove that

and is smooth otherwise. Finally

is isomorphic to the blowup

Consider now
\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)

Lemma 4.5

The exceptional divisor

is isomorphic to
{\mathbb P}(U^{\vee}|_{S})

Proof.

Note that points of

so that the residual line

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the rank 2 spinor bundle on

\mathcal{S}=U|_{Q}
where

is the universal subbundle
on
\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)

Corollary 4.6

The divisor

is extremal in the effective cone, giving

Proof.

The line bundle

Indeed, regarding

Here
\operatorname{Nef}(S^{[2]})=\overline{\operatorname{Mov}}(S^{[2]})=\langle\widetilde{H},\widetilde{H}-2B\rangle
and
\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(S^{[2]})=\langle B,\widetilde{H}-2B\rangle

The geometry of this fibration is well understood (see

[

as the intersection of three quadrics

defined by taking a

) containing the line in

is singular at precisely six points and we take the genus two curve ramified above these six points. In particular, following the description from Theorem

, it is the class of this curve that spans the Mori cone of

.

5. Final thoughts and questions

5.1. Bott vanishing for K3 surfaces

The following question was raised by Totaro in
[
Tot20
]
.

Question 5.1
.

For which polarised K3 surfaces
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
does
Bott vanishing
hold? That is, for
q>0,p\geq 0
, is

\operatorname{H}^{q}(S,{}^{p}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))=0?

Here one does not assume that
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)
is primitive. As explained in the above reference, by Kodaira vanishing, one need only treat the case
p=1
.
The problem is essentially solved in
[
Tot20
]
, but let us show how one can use our above results to easily obtain Bott vanishing for K3 surfaces
(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
of Picard number one with
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)^{2}>75
. Indeed, using the upper bound for the nef slope
\alpha_{n,2t}
from Proposition
3.2
, we obtain that if
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)^{2}>75
, the divisor
D=3L+H
is ample on
E={\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
. Since
K_{E}=-2L
, one finds that

\operatorname{H}^{1}(S,{}^{1}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))=\operatorname{H}^{1}(E,L+H)=\operatorname{H}^{1}(E,K_{E}+D)=0

by Kodaira vanishing.
In general Bott vanishing can fail; for example because
\chi({}^{1}_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1))
is negative when
S
has low degree.

5.2. Questions

As noted before, we are only able to compute the cones for certain degrees of K3s, leaving several open questions. For instance, we ask for
E={\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
:

Question 5.2
.

What is the second extremal ray of
\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(E)
for a degree 2 K3 surface, or more generally when
S^{[2]}
admits a flop?

Question 5.3
.

What is the second extremal ray of
\operatorname{Nef}(E)
in degree 4 and 6, or more generally when there is a second
divisorial contraction
S^{[2]}\to Z
?

Even though the slopes for
S^{[2]}
are all rational numbers, the situation for
{\mathbb P}({}^{1}_{S})
is less clear:

Question 5.4
.

Let
S
be a K3 surface with Picard number one. Are the cones
\overline{\operatorname{Eff}}(E)
and
\operatorname{Nef}(E)
rational polyhedral?

Abstract
The set of local modes and density ridge lines are important summary characteristics of the data-generating distribution. In this work, we focus on estimating local modes and density ridges from point cloud data in a product space combining two or more Euclidean and/or directional metric spaces. Specifically, our approach extends the (subspace constrained) mean shift algorithm to such product spaces, addressing potential challenges in the generalization process. We establish the algorithmic convergence of the proposed methods, along with practical implementation guidelines. Experiments on simulated and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed methods.



Keywords:
Mean shift, Directional data, Mode clustering, Ridge estimation, Optimization on Product Manifolds.

1
Introduction

The rapid growth of machine learning applications has been accompanied by an increasing prevalence of large-scale data with complex structures. Extracting meaningful low-dimensional summaries from such data can provide critical insights into the underlying scientific problems
(
Izenman 2012
;
Chazal and Michel 2021
)
.
While many methods exist for characterizing low-dimensional structures
(
Fefferman et al. 2016
)
, this work focuses on estimating local modes and density ridges (also known as principal curves/surfaces in
Ozertem and Erdogmus 2011
) from point cloud data because of two reasons. Statistically, local modes and density ridges effectively capture the intrinsic low-dimensional structure of data and can be consistently estimated using kernel density estimator (KDE)
(
Romano 1988
;
Mokkadem and Pelletier 2003
;
Genovese et al. 2014
)
. Practically, the mean shift algorithm
(
Fukunaga and Hostetler 1975
;
Cheng 1995
;
Comaniciu and Meer 2002
)
and its subspace constrained variant
(
Ozertem and Erdogmus 2011
;
Ghassabeh et al. 2013
)
are well-suited for identifying these features in Euclidean spaces.

However, some practical data of interest do not reside on a flat Euclidean space but a nonlinear
q
-dimensional sphere (or any topological space that is homeomorphic to a sphere). For instance, dihedral angles in protein structures are periodic and naturally represented on a unit circle (
q=1
)
(
Zimmermann and Hansmann 2006
)
. Moreover, data may consist of mixture components defined on metric spaces with different geometries. A concrete example arises in astronomy, where each observed object is recorded as a three-element tuple: (right ascension, declination, redshift)
(
Dawson et al. 2016
;
Brown et al. 2018
)
. The first two elements encode its position on the celestial sphere (
q=2
), while the redshift value reflects its linear distance from Earth. In a broader sense, such data are known as spatio-temporal data, combining spatial components (Euclidean or directional) with the temporal elements that may be continual or periodic
(
Hall et al. 2006
)
. For these complicated data structures, standard KDE and mean shift algorithm are no longer applicable.

While the two coordinates

In this paper, we propose methods for estimating local modes and density ridges of a data-generating density function supported on a Cartesian product space
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, using KDE and (subspace constrained) mean shift algorithms. Here,
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
represent either Euclidean/linear spaces
\mathbb{R}^{D}
, or directional spaces

\Omega_{q}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{q+1}:\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}=1\right\}
, where
\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|_{2}
is the usual Euclidean norm.
Although we focus on product spaces with two (topological) factors, our methodology can be readily extended to product spaces with finitely many factors. Furthermore, either or both factors can be topological spaces homeomorphic to
\Omega_{q}
, provided that the homeomorphism is known.
While mode-seeking and ridge-finding problems have been studied independently in
\mathbb{R}^{D}
and
\Omega_{q}

(
Chacón 2020
;
Genovese et al. 2014
;
Zhang and Chen 2023
)
, the generalizations to
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
introduce new challenges. First, the topology of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
differs fundamentally from that of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
or
\Omega_{q}
when one or both factors
\mathcal{S}_{j},j=1,2
are directional. Consider a random sample
\{(\theta_{i},\phi_{i})\}_{i=1}^{1000}
with
\theta_{i}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)
and
\phi_{i}=0
. Depending on the problem context, this dataset may be viewed as either points from a circle on the sphere
\Omega_{2}
(with
(\theta_{i},\phi_{i})
as longitude and latitude) or on the torus
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}
. The periodicity constraints of
\Omega_{2}
and
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}
are different, and these two spaces are not homeomorphic; see
Figure 1
. Second, directly applying existing mode-seeking or ridge-finding methods to
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
independently leads to an
unidentifiability
issue on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. For example, consider two densities
f_{1},f_{2}
, on
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{1}
with the sets of local modes as
\mathcal{M}=\left\{(0,0),(0,\frac{3\pi}{4}),(\frac{\pi}{2},0),(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{4})\right\}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}=\left\{(0,\frac{3\pi}{4}),(\frac{\pi}{2},0)\right\}
, respectively; see also
Section A.4
. While
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
differ, the marginal local modes, which are
0,\frac{\pi}{2}
on the first coordinate and
0,\frac{3\pi}{4}
on the second coordinate, are identical. Applying existing mode-seeking methods coordinatewise would fail to distinguish
f_{1}
from
f_{2}
. To resolve this unidentifiability issue, we propose mean shift-based approaches that identify modes and ridges jointly on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Main Results
.
\bullet
For the mode-seeking problem, we derive two versions of the mean shift algorithm with coordinate-adaptive bandwidths on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and establish their (linear) convergence properties; see
Section 3
.

\bullet
While the formulation of the mean shift algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is natural and intuitive, its extension to subspace-constrained settings is nontrivial and demands careful consideration. We introduce our subspace constrained mean shift (SCMS) algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and rigorously prove its (linear) convergence. Furthermore, we provide a theoretically and empirically valid guideline for tuning the step size parameter in the algorithm; see
Section 4
.

\bullet
The effectiveness of our proposed mean shift and SCMS algorithms on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is demonstrated through simulation studies and real-world applications; see
Figure 2
with more details in
Section 5
and
Section A
.

Related Work
. Kernel density estimator (KDE) has been widely applied across various scientific fields; see
Silverman 1986
;
Scott 2015
;
Chen 2017
for comprehensive reviews. Relevant extensions of KDE include those for directional-linear (DirLin) or directional-directional (DirDir) data
(
Marzio et al. 2011
;
García-Portugués et al. 2013
;
García-Portugués et al. 2015
)
, which utilized directional KDE proposed by
Hall et al. 1987
;
Bai et al. 1988
with product kernels for density estimation, independence testing, and goodness-of-fit testing. Additional researches on bump-hunting with regular KDE include
Parzen 1962
;
Chacón and Duong 2013
;
Chacón and Fernández Serrano 2023
. Building on these works, we extend the usual KDE to address mode-seeking and ridge-finding problems on directional and/or linear product spaces. After the first version of our paper,
García-Portugués and
Meilán-Vila 2023
;
García-Portugués and Meilán-Vila 2024
also studied KDE and ridge-finding algorithms for data on the polysphere to analyze hippocampus shape variation. While their approaches are similar to our methods when
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional, our framework also accommodates cases where one of
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
is linear.

The mean shift algorithm has also been adapted to directional
(
Oba et al. 2005
;
Kafai et al. 2010
;
Zhang and Chen 2021b
)
and manifold data
(
Subbarao and Meer 2006
;
Subbarao and Meer 2009
;
Cetingul and Vidal 2009
)
; see
Carreira-Perpiñán 2015
for a comprehensive review.
It efficiently identifies local modes of KDE and can be modified into a subspace constrained version to detect density ridges
(
Genovese et al. 2014
;
Chen et al. 2014
)
. Convergence properties of the (directional) subspace constrained mean shift (SCMS) algorithm and its extensions have been studied in
Ghassabeh et al. 2013
;
Qiao and Polonik 2021
;
Zhang and Chen 2023
, exhibiting linear convergence to estimated ridges. We will demonstrate that our extended SCMS algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
enriches these approaches.

1.1
Setup and Notation

Our data consist of independent and identically distributed observations
\left\{\bm{Z}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}=\left\{(\bm{X}_{i},\bm{Y}_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
sampled from a distribution on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with density
f
, where
\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}f(\bm{x},\bm{y})\,\omega(d\bm{x},d\bm{y})=1
with
\omega(d\bm{x},d\bm{y})
inducing a volume form on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
under associated Riemannian metrics
(
Pennec 2006
)
. If
\mathcal{S}_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}}
, we assume that the marginal density of
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{j}
has a compact support. Note that
D_{j}
is the intrinsic or manifold dimension of
\mathcal{S}_{j}
, and the ambient space of
\mathcal{S}_{j}
is
\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{j}=\Omega_{D_{j}}\}}}
for
j=1,2
, where
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{j}=\Omega_{D_{j}}\}}
indicates that the directional data space
\Omega_{D_{j}}
has its ambient space as
\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}+1}
. Whenever
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional, we extend the (density) function
f
from
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
to its ambient space
\mathcal{S}\setminus N_{A}
as:

\begin{cases}f(\bm{x},\cdot)\equiv f\left(\frac{\bm{x}}{\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}},\cdot\right)&\text{ if }\quad\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}},\\
f(\cdot,\bm{y})\equiv f\left(\cdot,\frac{\bm{y}}{\left|\left|\bm{y}\right|\right|_{2}}\right)&\text{ if }\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}}
and

N_{A}=\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}:\left(\bm{x}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}},\,\bm{y}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}\right)=\bm{0}\right\}
.
The indicators
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\bm{x}\in\Omega_{D_{j}}\}}
with
j=1,2
are introduced to accommodate the directional data space
\Omega_{D_{j}}
, whose ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}+1}
for
j=1,2
. The set
N_{A}
is nonempty only when any of
\mathcal{S}_{j},j=1,2
is directional. The extension (
1
) is standard for handling directional densities
(
García-Portugués 2013
;
García-Portugués et al. 2013
;
García-Portugués et al. 2015
;
Zhang and Chen 2021b
;
Zhang and Chen 2023
)
. Under some differentiability assumption (see
Section B.1
in the supplement), the
total
gradient and Hessian of
f
in the ambient space of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
are denoted by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla f(\bm{z})&=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}f\\
\nabla_{\bm{y}}f\end{pmatrix}(\bm{z}),\,\,\nabla^{2}f(\bm{z})=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}^{2}f&\nabla_{\bm{y}}\nabla_{\bm{x}}f\\
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\nabla_{\bm{y}}f&\nabla_{\bm{y}}^{2}f\end{pmatrix}(\bm{z}),\end{split}

(2)

where
\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{z})=\left(\frac{\partial f(\bm{z})}{\partial x_{1}},...,\frac{\partial f(\bm{z})}{\partial x_{D}}\right)^{\top}
with
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})
denotes the total gradient inside the (smallest) ambient Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{D}
containing
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, and the same applies to
\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bm{z})
. That is,
D=D_{1}+1
when
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}
(directional space) while
D=D_{1}
when
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}}
(Euclidean space).
We use the big-O notation
h(x)=O(g(x))
or
h(x)\lesssim g(x)
if the absolute value of
h(x)
is upper bounded by a positive constant multiple of
g(x)
for all sufficiently large
x
. If
h(x)\gtrsim g(x)
and
h(x)\lesssim g(x)
, then
h(x),g(x)
are asymptotically equal and it is denoted by
h(x)\asymp g(x)
. In contrast,
h(x)=o(g(x))
when
\lim_{x\to\infty}|h(x)|/g(x)=0
. For random vectors, the notation
o_{P}(1)
is short for a sequence of random vectors that converges to zero in probability, and
O_{P}(1)
denotes a sequence that is bounded in probability.

2
Background

2.1
Kernel Density Estimator on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

Given the observed data
\left\{\bm{Z}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}=\left\{(\bm{X}_{i},\bm{Y}_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
on the product space
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we estimate the underlying density
f
via KDE with product kernels as
(
Hall et al. 2006
;
García-Portugués et al. 2013
;
García-Portugués et al. 2015
)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right),\end{split}

(3)

where
K_{j}:\mathcal{S}_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
is the kernel function for
j=1,2
, and each element of
\bm{h}=(h_{1},h_{2})
is a bandwidth parameter. Depending on the geometry of
\mathcal{S}_{j}
, the radially symmetric kernel function
K_{j}
takes the form:

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{j}(\bm{u})&=C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})\cdot k_{j}\left(\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{C_{k,D_{j}}}{h_{i}^{D_{j}}}\cdot k\left(\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)&\text{ if }\mathcal{S}_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}},\\
C_{L,D_{j}}(h_{j})\cdot L\left(\frac{\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{2}\right)&\text{ if }\mathcal{S}_{j}=\Omega_{D_{j}},\end{cases}\end{split}

(4)

for
j=1,2
, where
k
and
L
are the profiles of linear and directional kernels respectively while
C_{k,D_{j}},C_{L,D_{j}}(h_{j})
are normalizing constants. A key distinction between linear and directional kernels in (
4
) is whether the normalizing constants are separable from the bandwidth parameters. In particular, if
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is directional, then
K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)=C_{L,D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot L\left(\frac{\left|\left|\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{2h_{1}^{2}}\right)=C_{L,D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot L\left(\frac{1-\bm{x}^{T}\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}^{2}}\right)
, which aligns with the usual KDE formulation for directional data
(
Hall et al. 1987
;
Bai et al. 1988
)
. Furthermore,
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
is defined not just on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
but also its ambient space. Finally, if
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
are both directional or Euclidean,
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
can also be defined via a spherically symmetric kernel
(
García-Portugués and Meilán-Vila 2024
)
.

The commonly used linear and directional kernel profiles are
k(s)=e^{-s/2}
and
L(r)=e^{-r}
, corresponding to the Gaussian kernel and von Mises kernel, respectively. The von Mises kernel derives its name from the classical von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution
(
Mardia and Jupp 2000
)
on
\Omega_{q}
, which has the density
f_{\text{vMF}}(\bm{x};\bm{\mu},\kappa)=C_{q}(\kappa)\cdot\exp\left(\kappa\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{x}\right)
, where
C_{q}(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa^{\frac{q-1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{q+1}{2}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{q-1}{2}}(\kappa)}
is a normalizing constant,
\bm{\mu}\in\Omega_{q}
is the directional mean of the vMF distribution,
\kappa\geq 0
is the concentration parameter, and
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(\kappa)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind at order
\alpha
.
Applying Gaussian and/or von Mises kernels to
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
in (
3
), the KDE simplifies to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\frac{C(\bm{H})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\exp\left[-\frac{(\bm{z}-\bm{Z}_{i})^{\top}\bm{H}^{-1}(\bm{z}-\bm{Z}_{i})}{2}\right]\\
&\text{ with }\quad\bm{H}=\mathrm{Diag}\left(h_{1}^{2}\bm{I}_{D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}},h_{2}^{2}\bm{I}_{D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}}\right),\end{split}

(5)

where
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
,
\bm{I}_{D}
is the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
, and
C(\bm{H})\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})
is the normalizing constant from (
3
) and (
4
). For simplicity, we consider a (block) diagonal bandwidth matrix
\bm{H}

(
Wand and Jones 1993
)
, though our theory also applies to the general positive definite bandwidth matrix. Additionally, when
\mathcal{S}_{j}=\Omega_{D_{j}}
, it is more appropriate to apply a single bandwidth parameter
h_{j}
to each coordinate on
\Omega_{D_{j}}
due to the isotropic geometry. While the (subspace constrained) mean shift algorithms can be derived for general kernels in Sections
3
and
4
, we only implement the algorithms with Gaussian and/or von Mises kernels in practice, given their smoothness properties.

2.2
Gradient and Hessian System on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

Given that each component of the product space
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
may be a nonlinear manifold
\Omega_{q}
, we study the Riemannian gradient and Hessian of a density function
f
defined on the tangent spaces of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and their connections to the total gradient and Hessian in the ambient (Euclidean) space.
We assume that the function
f
is at least twice continuously differentiable in an open neighborhood of the ambient space containing
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
; see the full assumptions in
Section B.1
of the supplement.

\bullet

Riemannian Gradient
. The differential of
f
at
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is a linear map
df_{\bm{z}}:T_{\bm{z}}\equiv T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})\to\mathbb{R}
given by
df_{\bm{z}}(\bm{v})=\frac{d}{dt}f\left(\gamma(t)\right)\big|_{t=0}
for any smooth curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and tangent vector
\bm{v}=(\bm{v}_{\bm{x}},\bm{v}_{\bm{y}})\in T_{\bm{z}}\equiv T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
. Then, the
Riemannian gradient

\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})
is a vector field in the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}df_{\bm{z}}(\bm{v})&=\langle\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}),(\bm{v}_{\bm{x}},\bm{v}_{\bm{y}})\rangle=\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})^{\top}\bm{v},\end{split}

(6)

where the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual one in the smallest ambient Euclidean space containing
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. From (
6
), we obtain a vector form of
\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{x},\bm{y})
as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\nabla f(\bm{z})\;\text{ with }\;\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}_{1}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\mathcal{P}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\begin{cases}\mathcal{P}_{1}=\bm{I}_{D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}}-\bm{x}\bm{x}^{\top}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}},\\
\mathcal{P}_{2}=\bm{I}_{D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}}-\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(7)

Here,
\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}
is the projection matrix onto the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
and
\bm{I}_{D}
denotes an identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{D\times D}
. Specifically,
\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}
reduces to the identity matrix when
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is Euclidean, while it acts as a projection operator when either of
\mathcal{S}_{j},j=1,2
is directional.

\bullet

Riemannian Hessian
. The
Riemannian Hessian
of
f
is a symmetric linear map
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
of the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}\equiv T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
into itself defined by

\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})[\bm{v}]=\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})

(8)

for any tangent vector
\bm{v}=(\bm{v}_{\bm{x}},\bm{v}_{\bm{y}})\in T_{\bm{z}}
, where
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}
is the Riemannian connection on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
; see Section 5.5 in
Absil et al. 2009
.
As
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
can be viewed as a (nonlinear) submanifold in its ambient Euclidean space, Proposition 5.3.2 in
Absil et al. 2009
suggests that the Riemannian Hessian
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
can be written as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})[\bm{v}]&=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\Big(\nabla_{\bm{x}}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}),\,\nabla_{\bm{y}}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})\Big)[\bm{v}]\\
&=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\Big[\nabla^{2}f(\bm{z})-\mathrm{Diag}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)\Big][\bm{v}]\;\text{ with }\;\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}_{1}=\bm{x}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{x}}f(\bm{z})\bm{I}_{D_{1}+1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}},\\
\mathcal{A}_{2}=\bm{y}^{\top}\nabla_{\bm{y}}f(\bm{z})\bm{I}_{D_{2}+1}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}},\end{cases}\end{split}

(9)

where
\mathrm{Diag}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)
is a (block) diagonal matrix, and we plug in the formula (
7
) of
\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})
in the second equality.

\mathcal{A}_{j}
is the “corrections” when the space
\mathcal{S}_{j}
is directional/nonlinear for
j=1,2
.
When the space
\mathcal{S}_{j}
is Euclidean,
\mathcal{A}_{j}
becomes a matrix
\bm{0}
.
Using
\bm{v}\in T_{\bm{z}}
and the symmetric property of
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
, we express the Hessian operator
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
as a matrix
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})=\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\Big[\nabla^{2}f(\bm{z})-\mathrm{Diag}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)\Big]\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}.

Furthermore, both
\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})
and
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
reduce to the total gradient
\nabla f(\bm{z})
and Hessian
\nabla^{2}f(\bm{z})
when
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}
are flat Euclidean spaces.

3
Mean Shift Algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

3.1
Mode Estimation on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

The Riemannian gradient
\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})
and Hessian
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
in
Section 2.2
induce the definition of the set of local modes
\mathcal{M}
of
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
as:

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\lambda_{1}(\bm{z})<0\right\},

(10)

where
\lambda_{1}(\bm{z})
is the largest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
. Given the KDE
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
in (
3
), a plug-in estimator of
\mathcal{M}
is given by

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\widehat{\lambda}_{1}(\bm{z})<0\right\}.

(11)

Under some regularity conditions on kernel functions and the density
f
,
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is a consistent estimator of
\mathcal{M}
; see
Section B.2
of the supplement.

3.2
Derivations of the Mean Shift Algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

We assume that the linear and directional kernel profiles
k
and
L
in (
4
) are continuously differentiable.
Under (
3
) and (
4
), the total gradient of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})\\
\nabla_{\bm{y}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}G_{\bm{x}}\left[\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}-\bm{x}\right]\\
G_{\bm{y}}\left[\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}-\bm{y}\right]\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(12)

where, with
C(\bm{H})\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})
, both factors

\displaystyle\begin{split}G_{\bm{x}}&=-\frac{2C(\bm{H})}{nh_{1}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right),\\
G_{\bm{y}}&=-\frac{2C(\bm{H})}{nh_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\end{split}

(13)

are non-negative and
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Thus, the mean shift vector
\Xi(\bm{z})
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi(\bm{z})&=\begin{pmatrix}\Xi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\\
\Xi_{\bm{y}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}-\bm{x}\\
\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}-\bm{y}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

(14)

Unlike the standard mean shift vector in the Euclidean space
(
Comaniciu and Meer 2002
)
,
\Xi(\bm{z})

does not
align with the total gradient estimator
\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
in (
12
), because
G_{\bm{x}}\neq G_{\bm{y}}
in general.
Nevertheless,
\Xi(\bm{z})
and
\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
share the same set of roots within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
.
Specifically, the solutions to
\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\Xi(\bm{z})=\bm{0}
coincide with the set of estimated local modes
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
, satisfying
\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\bm{0}
. Hence,

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{(T)}:=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\cdot\Xi(\bm{z})=\bm{0}\right\}=\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
.
This equivalence arises because, from (
12
) and (
14
), the mean shift vector
\Xi(\bm{z})
is equal to
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
, which is a transformed estimated total gradient with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})=\mathrm{Diag}\left(G_{\bm{x}}\bm{I}_{D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}},G_{\bm{y}}\bm{I}_{D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}}\right).\end{split}

(15)

Since
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})
is nonsingular for any strictly decreasing kernel (
e.g.
, Gaussian or von Mises kernels), the projection of
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\Xi(\bm{z})
onto
T_{\bm{z}}
is zero if and only if the Riemannian gradient estimator
\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
is zero.
Therefore,
\Xi(\bm{z})
remains valid for identifying the local modes of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
.
Here, we introduce two different versions of the mean shift algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
based on
\Xi(\bm{z})
and establish their (linear) convergence in Theorems
1
and
2
.

\bullet

Version A: Simultaneous Mean Shift
. This version updates all the components
\bm{z}^{(t)}=\big(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)}\big)^{\top}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
simultaneously, in which for
t=0,1,...
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{z}^{(t+1)}&\leftarrow\Xi(\bm{z}^{(t)})+\bm{z}^{(t)}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}\\
\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(16)

with extra standardizations
\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\frac{\bm{x}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}}
and/or
\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\frac{\bm{y}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}}
if
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional.
This approach seamlessly integrates the Euclidean and directional mean shift algorithms into a unified iteration formula, as shown in (
16
).

\bullet

Version B: Componentwise Mean Shift
. This version updates the sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}=\left\{(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
in a two-step manner as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\Xi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})+\bm{x}^{(t)}\quad\text{ with }\quad\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\frac{\bm{x}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}\quad\text{ if }\;\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}},\\
&\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\Xi_{\bm{y}}(\bm{x}^{(t+1)},\bm{y}^{(t)})+\bm{y}^{(t)}\quad\text{ with }\quad\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\frac{\bm{y}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}\quad\text{ if }\;\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\end{split}

(17)

for
t=0,1,...
. The formula updates the two components,
\bm{x}^{(t)}
and
\bm{y}^{(t)}
, in an alternating manner: first holding
\bm{y}^{(t)}
constant to update
\bm{x}^{(t)}
and then reversing the roles. This iteration draws inspiration from the coordinate ascent/descent algorithm
(
Wright 2015
)
.

The above two versions can be readily specialized to mean shift algorithms using von Mises and/or Gaussian kernels, and the details are therefore omitted. Under mild conditions on the kernel profiles, we establish the ascending and convergence properties of the mean shift algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, with proofs provided in
Section D
.

Theorem 1
.

Denote the sequence from the mean shift algorithm by
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}=\left\{(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\right\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Assume that

\bullet

(C1)
The kernel profiles
k_{1},k_{2}
(either linear
k
or directional
L
) are strictly decreasing and differentiable on
[0,\infty)
with
k_{1}(0),k_{2}(0)<\infty
.

\bullet

(Weak Condition)
Both
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are convex.

\bullet

(Strong Condition)
The entire product kernel profile
K(r,s)=k_{1}(r)\cdot k_{2}(s)
is convex.

Then, for any fixed bandwidths
\bm{h}
and sample size
n
, we have that

(a) Under
(C1)
and
(Weak Condition)
, the sequence of density estimates
\left\{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
yielded by Version B is non-decreasing and thus converges.

(b) Under
(C1)
and
(Strong Condition)
, the sequence of density estimates
\left\{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
yielded by either Version A or B is non-decreasing and thus converges.

(c) Under the assumptions in (a) or (b), we have that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left|\left|\bm{z}^{(t+1)}-\bm{z}^{(t)}\right|\right|_{2}=0
.

(d) Assume the conditions in (a) or (b). If the local modes of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
are isolated,
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
converges to a local mode of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
when it is initialized within its small neighborhood.

Remark 1
.

When the entire product kernel profile
K(r,s)=k_{1}(r)\cdot k_{2}(s)
is convex, its component functions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are also convex. Thus, the strong condition in Theorem
1
implies the weak condition, whereas the converse is not true in general. For Gaussian and/or von Mises kernel profiles
k(s)=L(s)=e^{-s}
, the conditions in (a,b,c) are satisfied. Lastly, the isolated mode condition of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
in (d) can be derived from regularity conditions on the original density function
f
under the uniform consistency of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
; see Theorem
B.3
in
Section B
of the supplement.

The result in (d) of Theorem
1
is known as the local convergence of the proposed mean shift algorithm.
For its global convergence (from almost every initial point in
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
), one potential strategy is to interpret the algorithm as a (generalized) Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm
(
Carreira-Perpiñán 2007
;
Zhang and Chen 2021a
)
and apply the convergence theory of EM algorithm
(
Wu 1983
;
McLachlan and Krishnan 2008
)
. Another feasible approach leverages the Łojasiewicz property of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
, as explored in
Yamasaki and Tanaka 2023
.
Finally, we establish the following linear convergence of our proposed mean shift algorithms (
16
) and (
17
) under the gradient ascent framework on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with their intrinsic step sizes depending on the bandwidths
h_{1},h_{2}
; see
Section C
for details and its proof.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the conditions of Theorem
B.3
in
Section B.2
of the supplement hold. Given the sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
defined by our mean shift algorithm (
16
) or (
17
), there exist constants
\widetilde{r}_{1}>0,\Upsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
such that

d_{g}(\bm{z}^{(t)},\widehat{\bm{m}})\leq\Upsilon_{1}^{t}\cdot d_{g}(\bm{z}^{(0)},\widehat{\bm{m}})\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{g}(\bm{z}^{(t)},\bm{m})\leq\Upsilon_{1}^{t}\cdot d_{g}(\bm{z}^{(0)},\bm{m})+O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\log h|}{nh^{D_{T}}}}\right)

when
\bm{z}^{(0)}\in\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:d_{g}(\bm{z},\bm{m})\leq\widetilde{r}_{1}\right\}
with
\bm{m}\in\mathcal{M}
,
\max\left\{h_{1},h_{2}\right\}\lesssim h
is sufficiently small, and the sample size
n
is sufficiently large. Here,
D_{T}=D_{1}+D_{2}
is the intrinsic dimension and
d_{g}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the geodesic distance on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

4
SCMS Algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

This section presents the formal definition of (density) ridges of
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and highlights the challenges associated with generalizing the standard SCMS algorithm to this setting, along with our proposed solution.

4.1
Ridge Estimation on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

Given the Riemannian gradient
\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})
and Hessian
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we generalize the definitions of order-
d
Euclidean
(
Eberly 1996
;
Genovese et al. 2014
)
and directional
(
Zhang and Chen 2023
)
density ridges to the product space as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{d}=&\big\{\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\nabla f(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{z})<0\big\},\end{split}

(18)

where
\lambda_{1}(\bm{z})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{D_{T}}(\bm{z})
are eigenvalues of
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
within the tangent space,
D_{T}=D_{1}+D_{2}
is the intrinsic dimension of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, and
V_{d}(\bm{z})=\big(\bm{v}_{d+1}(\bm{z}),...,\bm{v}_{D_{T}}(\bm{z})\big)
with its columns as unit eigenvectors associated with the
(D_{T}-d)
smallest eigenvalues within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
. When
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}
,
(\bm{x},\bm{0})
is an unit eigenvector of
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
associated with eigenvalue 0 and orthogonal to
T_{\bm{z}}
. The same applies to
(\bm{0},\bm{y})
when
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}
.

As in
Section 3.1
, our KDE
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
in (
3
) provides a natural estimator of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}=&\big\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{h}(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\widehat{\lambda}_{d+1}(\bm{z})<0\big\},\end{split}

(19)

where
\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{d+1}(\bm{z})
are the counterparts of
V_{d}(\bm{z})
and
\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{z})
defined by
\mathcal{H}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
. Under some regularity conditions,
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
is a consistent estimator of
\mathcal{R}_{d}
; see
Section B.3
of the supplement for detailed discussions.

4.2
SCMS Algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
: Pitfall and Solution

To identify the estimated density ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
in practice, the SCMS algorithm is one of the most effective off-the-shelf methods. However, generalizing the mean shift algorithm in
Section 3
with the standard technique in
Ozertem and Erdogmus 2011
;
Ghassabeh et al. 2013
to the product space
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
results in an incorrect estimated ridge.

\bullet

Pitfall
. Naively, one may adapt the standard SCMS iteration by updating the sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
via the mean shift vector (
14
) as:

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\bm{z}^{(t)}+\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\Xi(\bm{z}^{(t)})

(20)

with an additional standardization whenever
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional for
t=0,1,...
. Unfortunately, this naive SCMS iteration on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
does not converge to the correct estimated ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
but a transformed estimated ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}^{(T)}
defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}^{(T)}=&\big\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\widehat{\lambda}_{d+1}(\bm{z})<0\big\},\end{split}

(21)

where
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})
is defined in (
15
). This discrepancy arises because the mean shift vector (
14
) does not align with the total gradient estimator (
12
), but instead corresponds to the transformed estimated gradient
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
. As a result, when the factors
G_{\bm{x}},G_{\bm{y}}
in
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})
differ,
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}\neq\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}^{(T)}
.
Asymptotically,
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}^{(T)}
approaches the biased quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{d}^{(T)}

\displaystyle=\big\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\bm{H}\widetilde{F}(\bm{z})^{-1}\nabla f(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{z})<0\big\}

when
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and the sample size
n\to\infty
; see the following proposition with its proof in
Section D
. Consequently, the naive SCMS algorithm leads to an inconsistent estimator of the true ridge when
\mathcal{R}_{d}^{(T)}\neq\mathcal{R}_{d}
.

Proposition 3
.

Assume conditions (A1-2) in
Section B.1
of the supplement. Then,

\displaystyle\left[\bm{H}\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})\right]^{-1}\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})-\widetilde{F}(\bm{z})^{-1}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}}}\right)

for any fixed
\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}\to\infty
, where
\widetilde{F}(\bm{z})
is a nonrandom function depending on
f(\bm{z})
and kernels. See the full version in Proposition
D.1
.

\bullet

Solution
.
Some simple algebra from (
5
) show that
\bm{H}^{-1}\Xi(\bm{z})=\frac{\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})}

under Gaussian and/or von Mises kernels. Based on this observation,
we propose the SCMS algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with the following iterative update rule:

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\bm{z}^{(t)}+\eta\cdot\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\bm{H}^{-1}\Xi(\bm{z}^{(t)}),

(22)

where
\eta>0
is the step size, and additional standardization is applied when
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional for
t=0,1,\dots
.
Unlike the naive SCMS algorithm (
20
), this version introduces an extra tuning parameter, the step size
\eta
, which plays a crucial role in the convergence behavior. As discussed in
Section C.3
, it is inappropriate to use a constant step size, say
\eta=1
, independent of the bandwidth parameters
\bm{h}
.
Normally, iterating (
22
) with smaller step sizes guarantees convergence but may result in slow rates, whereas larger step sizes can accelerate convergence, but may overshoot the targeted ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
.
As a guideline, we suggest adapting the step size to the bandwidth parameters as:

\eta=\min\{\max(\bm{h})\cdot\min(\bm{h}),1\}=\min\left\{h_{1}h_{2},1\right\}

(23)

so that when
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h
are small,
\eta
mimics the asymptotic rate
O(h^{2})
of adaptive step sizes in Euclidean/directional (subspace constrained) mean shift algorithms
(
Cheng 1995
;
Arias-Castro et al. 2016
;
Zhang and Chen 2023
)
. The upper bound
1
in (
23
) prevents
\eta
from being too large, thereby avoiding overshooting the targeted ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
. Unlike the mean shift algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
in
Section 3
, we do not formulate the componentwise SCMS algorithm, because the eigenspace projector
\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}
must be recomputed in each sub-step of its iteration. Given the time complexity
O\left((D_{1}+D_{2})^{3}\right)
for spectral decomposition on
\mathcal{H}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
, the componentwise SCMS approach is less computationally efficient than the approach in (
22
). Under the step size (
23
), we establish the following (linear) convergence results for our proposed SCMS algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
.
Additionally, simulation studies in
Section C.3
of the supplement demonstrates the effectiveness of our step size choice in (
23
).

Theorem 4
.

Assume that the assumptions in Theorem
B.4
and conditions (A4-5) in
Section C.4
of the supplement hold. For a sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
from our SCMS algorithm (
22
) with step size
\eta
dominated by
\max(\bm{h})
, there exist constants

6
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we generalized the (subspace constrained) mean shift algorithms to Euclidean/directional product spaces, established their (linear) convergence properties, and provided practical guidelines for implementation. The utility of our proposed methods was demonstrated on both DirLin and DirDir datasets. In the future, we aim to extend the mean shift and SCMS algorithms to a product space with an infinite number of factors, such as some functional spaces, with the kernelized mean shift algorithm
(
Anand et al. 2013
)
serving as a potential starting point. We also plan to study mode-seeking and ridge-finding problems on product spaces comprising more general topological structures, such as matrix manifolds
(
Sasaki et al. 2022
)
or more complicated (Riemannian) manifolds
(
Zhang et al. 2021
)
.
Additionally, developing data-adaptive bandwidth selection methods for these algorithms remains an important avenue for future research.

A.1
Bandwidth Selection

As studying the choices of bandwidth parameters for the directional and linear data is not the main focus of this paper, we mainly leverage some existing rule-of-thumb bandwidth selectors in the literature. Without loss of generality, we assume that the dataset
\left\{\bm{Z}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}=\left\{(\bm{X}_{i},\bm{Y}_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
is directional-linear. For the bandwidth parameter in the directional part
\left\{\bm{X}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
\Omega_{D_{1}}
, we utilize the rule of thumb in Proposition 2 of
García-Portugués 2013
, where the estimated concentration parameter is given by (4.4) in
Banerjee et al. 2005
. That is,

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\text{ROT}}=\left[\frac{4\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{I}_{\frac{D_{1}-1}{2}}(\widehat{\kappa})^{2}}{\widehat{\kappa}^{\frac{D_{1}+1}{2}}\left[2D_{1}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\frac{D_{1}+1}{2}}(2\widehat{\kappa})+(D_{1}+2)\widehat{\kappa}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\frac{D_{1}+3}{2}}(2\widehat{\kappa})\right]n}\right]^{\frac{1}{D_{1}+4}},\end{split}

(A.1)

with
\widehat{\kappa}=\frac{\bar{R}(D_{1}+1-\bar{R})}{1-\bar{R}^{2}}
, where
\bar{R}=\frac{\left|\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\right|\right|_{2}}{n}
given the directional dataset
\left\{\bm{X}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\Omega_{D_{1}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}+1}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}(\kappa)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind of order
\kappa
. For the bandwidth parameter in the linear part
\left\{\bm{Y}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}

Chen et al. 2016b
as:

h_{\text{NR}}=\bar{S}_{n}\times\left(\frac{4}{D_{2}+4}\right)^{\frac{1}{D_{2}+6}}n^{-\frac{1}{D_{2}+6}}

(A.2)

with
\bar{S}_{n}=\frac{1}{D_{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{D_{2}}S_{n,j}
, where
S_{n,j}
is the sample standard deviation along the
j

\left\{\bm{Y}_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

Another method for selecting the bandwidth
\bm{h}
is through the Least Squares Cross-Validation (LSCV) technique. This approach aims to select a data-adaptive bandwidth by minimizing the mean integrated square error (MISE)
\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}\left[\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})-f(\bm{x},\bm{y})\right]^{2}\omega(d\bm{x},d\bm{y})

\bm{h}_{\text{LSCV}}=\argmin_{\bm{h}}\text{LSCV}(\bm{h}):=\argmin_{\bm{h}}\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})^{2}\omega(d\bm{x},d\bm{y})-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h},-i}(\bm{X}_{i},\bm{Y}_{i}),

(A.3)

where
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h},-i}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{j}}{h_{1}}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{j}}{h_{2}}\right)

3

A.2
Manifold-Recovering Error Measure

Given noisy observations from a hidden manifold structure
\mathcal{C}
, such as the spiral curve example in
Figure 2
, a manifold learning method can learn a collection of discrete data points or a set of solutions to a system of equations that approximate
\mathcal{C}
. For our proposed SCMS algorithm, this set of data points is represented by a discrete sample from the estimated ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
. To quantify the estimation error from
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
to the true manifold structure
\mathcal{C}
, one
cannot
simply rely on the (average) distances of points on
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
to
\mathcal{C}
as:

d_{\text{avg}}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d},\mathcal{C})=\frac{1}{|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}|}\sum_{\bm{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}}d(\bm{z},\mathcal{C}),

(A.4)

where
|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}|
is the cardinality of (a discrete sample from)
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
and
d(\bm{z},\mathcal{C})=\inf\left\{d(\bm{z},\bm{c}):\bm{c}\in\mathcal{C}\right\}
with the distance measure
d(\cdot,\cdot)
defined in the (ambient) metric space containing
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
and
\mathcal{C}
. This is because the estimated ridge
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
(or any other estimated manifold structures) may only approximate a small portion of the true manifold
\mathcal{C}
even with uniform (but noisy) observations from
\mathcal{C}
as inputs; see, for instance, Panel (c) of
Figure 2
in the main paper or Panel (c) of
Figure A.2
below. Thus, we borrow the idea from the definition of Hausdorff distances and define the manifold-recovering error measure as:

d_{H}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d},\mathcal{C}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{|\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}|}\sum_{\bm{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}}d(\bm{z},\mathcal{C})+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}|}\sum_{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}}d(\bm{z},\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d})\right],

(A.5)

where
\mathcal{D}^{\mathcal{C}}
is a set of uniformly sampling points from the true manifold structure
\mathcal{C}
. This error measure averages over the approximation error and coverage of
\mathcal{C}
with the estimated ridge/manifold.

Appendix B
Statistical Consistency

This section is devoted to the discussion of consistency results for

\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})

defined in (
3
) of the main paper (and its derivatives), its induced mode estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
in (
11
), and ridge estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
in (
19
) on the product space
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

We reserve the notation
D^{[\tau]}f=f^{(\tau)}
for the
|[\tau]|
-th order partial derivative operator with the coordinate chart of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, where
[\tau]=(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{D_{T}})
is a multi-index and
D_{T}=D_{1}+D_{2}
is the sum of intrinsic dimensions of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
. For
\ell=0,1,...
, we define the functional norms

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\infty}^{(\ell)}=\max_{\tau:|[\tau]|=\ell}\sup_{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}|f^{(\tau)}(\bm{z})|
.
These functional norms are defined via the derivatives of
f
within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}\equiv T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
. We also define
\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\infty,\ell}^{*}=\max_{i=0,...,\ell}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\infty}^{(i)}
.
For any matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, its maximum norm is defined as
\left|\left|A\right|\right|_{\max}=\max_{i,j}A_{ij}
with
A_{ij}
being the
(i,j)
entry of
A
.

B.1
Assumptions and Consistency of KDE

Besides assuming that the projection of
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{j}
has a compact support when
\mathcal{S}_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}}
is Euclidean for
j=1,2
, we impose the following conditions on
f
as well as the linear and directional kernel profiles
k
and
L
:

•

(A1)
Under the above extension of the (density) function
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we assume that the total gradient
\nabla f(\bm{z})
, total Hessian
\nabla^{2}f(\bm{z})
, and third-order derivative tensor
\nabla^{3}f(\bm{z})
exists and are continuous in
\mathcal{S}\setminus N_{A}
. Moreover, they are assumed to be square integrable on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. Finally,
f
is assumed to have bounded fourth-order derivatives on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

•

(A2)
Given the KDE
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
defined in (
3
) and (
4
) in the main paper, we assume that its kernel profiles
k_{1},k_{2}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
are non-increasing and at least three times continuously differentiable with bounded fourth-order partial derivatives. We also assume that the resulting kernel functions satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}}}\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}K_{j}^{(\tau)}(\bm{x})\,d\bm{x}<\infty
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D_{j}}}\left[K_{j}^{(\tau)}(\bm{x})\right]^{2}d\bm{x}<\infty

for any multi-index with
|[\tau]|\leq 3
if
K_{j}
has a linear kernel profile for
j=1,2
, and
0<\int_{0}^{\infty}|L^{(\ell)}(r)|^{p}r^{\frac{D_{j}}{2}-1}dr<\infty

for all
D_{j}\geq 1
,
p=1,2
, and
\ell=0,1,2,3
when
K_{j}
has a directional kernel profile
L
for
j=1,2
.

•

(A3)
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle=\Bigg\{(\bm{u},\bm{v})\mapsto K_{1}^{(\tau_{1})}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{u}}{h_{1}}\right)K_{2}^{(\tau_{2})}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{v}}{h_{2}}\right):

\displaystyle\bm{x},\bm{u}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\bm{y},\bm{v}\in\mathcal{S}_{2},|[\tau_{1}]|,|[\tau_{2}]|=0,1,2,3,h_{1},h_{2}>0\Bigg\}.

We assume that
\mathcal{K}
is a bounded VC (subgraph) class of measurable functions on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
; that is, there exists constants
A,\upsilon>0
such that

\sup_{Q}N\left(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}_{2}(Q),\epsilon||F||_{\mathcal{L}_{2}(Q)}\right)\leq\left(\frac{A}{\epsilon}\right)^{\upsilon}
for any
0<\epsilon<1
,
where
N(T,d_{T},\epsilon)
is the
\epsilon
-covering number of the pseudometric space
(T,d_{T})
,
Q
is any probability measure with the same support as
P
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, and
F
is an envelope function of
\mathcal{K}
. The constants
A
and
\upsilon
are usually called the VC characteristics of
\mathcal{K}
, and the norm
\left|\left|F\right|\right|_{L_{2}(Q)}
is defined as
\left[\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}|F(\bm{x})|^{2}dQ(\bm{x})\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Conditions (A1) and (A2) are standard assumptions for establishing the (pointwise) consistency results for KDE and its derivatives in directional and/or linear product spaces; see
Wand and Jones 1994
;
Wasserman 2006
for linear/Euclidean KDE and
Hall et al. 1987
;
Klemelä 2000
;
Zhao and Wu 2001
for directional KDE. The VC-typed assumption (A3) is a well-known regularity condition developed by
Giné and Guillou 2002
;
Einmahl and Mason 2005
for the uniform consistency of KDE and its derivatives. The techniques in
Hall et al. 1987
;
Chacón et al. 2011
;
García-Portugués et al. 2013
;
García-Portugués et al. 2015
;
Zhang and Chen 2021b
can be adopted to establish the following pointwise rates of convergence for KDE
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
and its derivatives.

Lemma B.1
.

Assume conditions (A1-2). Then,

\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}^{\ell}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})-\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}^{\ell}f(\bm{z})=O\left(h^{2}\right)+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2\ell}}}\right)

as
\max(\bm{h})=\max\left\{h_{1},h_{2}\right\}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2\ell}\to\infty
for any integer
\ell
, where
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}
is the Riemannian connection for any unit
\bm{v}\in T_{\bm{z}}=T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
so that
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}f(\bm{z})=\langle\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}),\bm{v}\rangle
,
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}^{2}f(\bm{z})=\langle\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}),\bm{v}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})[\bm{v}],\bm{v}\rangle
, and
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}^{3}f(\bm{z})=\langle\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{\bm{v}}\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})[\bm{v}],\bm{v}\rangle
; recall (
8
) and see also Section 5.3 in
Absil et al. 2009
.

The pointwise consistency results in Lemma
B.1
can be improved as the following uniform rates of convergence using the arguments in
Giné and Guillou 2002
;
Einmahl and Mason 2005
;
Chacón et al. 2011
;
Bai et al. 1988
;
Zhang and Chen 2021b
.

Lemma B.2
.

Assume conditions (A1-3). Then,

\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}-f\right|\right|_{\infty}^{(\ell)}=\sup_{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}\left|\left|\nabla_{T_{\bm{z}}}^{\ell}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})-\nabla_{T_{\bm{z}}}^{\ell}f(\bm{z})\right|\right|_{\max}=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\log h|}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2\ell}}}\right)

as
\max(\bm{h})=\max\left\{h_{1},h_{2}\right\}\lesssim h\to 0
and
\frac{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2\ell}}{|\log h|}\to\infty
for any integer
\ell
.

The pointwise and uniform consistency results for
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
only hold within the tangent space/bundle. The (uniform) consistency results are particularly useful for establishing the mode and ridge consistency theory below. More importantly, they serve as a key building block for establishing the (linear) convergence of our proposed SCMS algorithm; see

for details.

B.2
Mode Consistency

Recall that
\mathcal{M}=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\lambda_{1}(\bm{z})<0\right\}\equiv\left\{\bm{m}_{1},...,\bm{m}_{N}\right\}
is the set of local modes of the true (density) function
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, while

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\widehat{\lambda}_{1}(\bm{z})<0\right\}\equiv\left\{\widehat{\bm{m}}_{1},...,\widehat{\bm{m}}_{\widehat{N}}\right\}

denotes the collection of estimated local modes obtained by KDE
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
. Here,
N
refers to the number of true local modes, and
\widehat{N}
is number of estimated local modes. To quantify the difference between two sets of points
\mathcal{M}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
, we leverage the Hausdorff distance as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{Haus}(\mathcal{M},\widehat{\mathcal{M}})&=\max\left\{\sup_{\bm{m}\in\mathcal{M}}d(\bm{m},\widehat{\mathcal{M}}),\sup_{\widehat{\bm{m}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}d(\widehat{\bm{m}},\mathcal{M})\right\}\\
&=\inf\left\{\epsilon>0:\mathcal{M}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\oplus\epsilon,\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}\oplus\epsilon\right\}.\end{split}

(B.1)

Here,
d(\widehat{\bm{m}},\mathcal{M})=\inf\left\{\left|\left|\bm{m}-\widehat{\bm{m}}\right|\right|_{2}:\bm{m}\in\mathcal{M}\right\}
,
\mathcal{M}\oplus\epsilon=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:d(\bm{z},\mathcal{M})\leq\epsilon\right\}
, and other quantities follow similarly.

Theorem B.3
.

Assumes conditions (A1-3) and there exist constants
\lambda^{*},\Theta^{*},C^{*}>0
such that

0<\lambda^{*}\leq|\lambda_{1}(\bm{m})|\quad\text{ for any }\bm{m}\in\mathcal{M}\quad\text{(strict definiteness condition)}

as well as

\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:\left|\left|\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})\right|\right|_{2}\leq\Theta^{*},\lambda_{1}(\bm{z})\leq-\frac{\lambda^{*}}{2}<0\right\}\subset\mathcal{M}\oplus\frac{C^{*}\lambda^{*}}{\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{\infty}^{(3)}},

where we recall that
\lambda_{1}(\bm{z})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{D_{1}+D_{2}}(\bm{z})
are the eigenvalues of the Riemannian Hessian
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
associated with the eigenvectors within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}=T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
. Then, when
\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}-f\right|\right|_{\infty,2}^{*}
is sufficiently small, there exist constant
A^{*},B^{*}>0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{N}\neq N\right)\leq B^{*}e^{-A^{*}nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+4}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{Haus}(\mathcal{M},\widehat{\mathcal{M}})=O\left(h^{2}\right)+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}}}\right)

with
\max(\bm{h})=\max\left\{h_{1},h_{2}\right\}\lesssim h
.

The assumptions imposed in Theorem
B.3
ensure that
f
is strongly concave around its local modes so that the local modes are well-separated. The proof is a direct application of the arguments in Theorem 1 of
Chen et al. 2016b
and Theorem 6 of
Zhang and Chen 2021b
, and is therefore omitted here. The key argument of the proof relies on the Taylor’s expansion of
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
as
(
Pennec 2006
)
:

f(\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}}(\bm{v}))=f(\bm{z})+\langle\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}),\bm{v}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\bm{v}^{\top}\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})\bm{v}+O(\left|\left|\bm{v}\right|\right|_{2}^{3})

(B.2)

for any
\bm{v}\in T_{\bm{z}}
, where
\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}}:T_{\bm{z}}\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is the
exponential map
at
\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
such that the tangent vector
\bm{v}\in T_{\bm{z}}
is mapped to the point
\bm{u}:=\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}}(\bm{v})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
along a curve
\alpha
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with
\alpha(0)=\bm{z},\alpha(1)=\bm{u}
and
\alpha^{\prime}(0)=\bm{v}
. Here,
\alpha:[0,1]\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is the curve of minimum length between
\bm{z}
and
\bm{u}
(i.e., the so-called geodesic on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
).

From (
B.2
), it is not difficult to see that the required condition that
\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}-f\right|\right|_{\infty,2}^{*}
is sufficiently small is satisfied as
\max(\bm{h})=\max\left\{h_{1},h_{2}\right\}\lesssim h\to 0
and
\frac{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+4}}{|\log h|}\to\infty
. Notably, the rate of convergence of
\mathrm{Haus}(\mathcal{M},\widehat{\mathcal{M}})
is identical to the pointwise asymptotic rate of the (Riemannian) gradient estimator. Nevertheless, in order for the rate of convergence of
\mathrm{Haus}(\mathcal{M},\widehat{\mathcal{M}})
to be valid, the true (density) function
f
and its estimator
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
must be uniformly close, including their (Riemannian) Hessians.

Finally, our mode consistency results are closely related to those in the context of modal regression, which focuses on identifying the local modes of the conditional density of an outcome variable given the covariate
(
Einbeck and Tutz 2006
;
Chen et al. 2016a
;
Chen 2018
;
Alonso-Pena 2020
)
. In particular,
Alonso Pena 2022
;
Alonso-Pena and Crujeiras 2023
utilized the mean shift algorithm and studied the asymptotic properties of multi-modal regression for circular-linear and the circular-circular data.

B.3
Ridge Consistency

Recall that our definition of the order-
d
(density) ridge

\mathcal{R}_{d}=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}:V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=\bm{0},\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{z})<0\right\},

which can be regarded as a generalized version of the set of local modes within the eigenspace spanned by the last
(D_{1}+D_{2}-d)
eigenvector of the Riemannian Hessian
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
(i.e., the column of
V_{d}(\bm{z})
). Given a natural estimator
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d}
in (
19
) of the ridge provided by the KDE
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}
, we modify the assumptions in Theorem 4 in
Genovese et al. 2014
and Theorem 4.1 in
Zhang and Chen 2023
to obtain its consistency under the Hausdorff distance.

Theorem B.4
.

Assume conditions (A1-3) and there exist constants
r_{*},\beta_{0}>0
such that

\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{z})\leq-\beta_{0},\quad\lambda_{d}(\bm{z})-\lambda_{d+1}(\bm{z})\geq\beta_{0}\quad\text{ (eigengap condition)}

as well as

\displaystyle(D_{1}+D_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\left|\left|\left[\bm{I}_{D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}+D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}}-V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\right]\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})\right|\right|_{2}\left|\left|\nabla^{3}f(\bm{z})\right|\right|_{\max}\leq\frac{\beta_{0}^{2}}{4}

for any
\bm{z}\in\mathcal{R}_{d}\oplus r_{*}
. Then, when
\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}-f\right|\right|_{\infty,3}^{*}
is sufficiently small,

\mathrm{Haus}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{d},\mathcal{R}_{d})=O\left(h^{2}\right)+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\log h|}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+4}}}\right)

with
\max(\bm{h})=\max\left\{h_{1},h_{2}\right\}\lesssim h
.

The eigengap condition in Theorem
B.4
is a generalization of the strict definiteness condition in Theorem
B.3
. It requires
f
to be (locally) concave within the eigenspace spanned by the last
(D_{1}+D_{2}-d)
eigenvector of the Riemannian Hessian
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
so that every order-
d
ridge
\mathcal{R}_{d}
contains the lower order ridges but is isolated from the ridges with the same order
d
. As our conditions in Theorem
B.4
imply the conditions in
Genovese et al. 2014
;
Zhang and Chen 2023
, the proof follows from their arguments.

Appendix C
Linear Convergence of the Mean Shift and SCMS Algorithms on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

This section is dedicated to formulating the general (subspace constrained) gradient ascent framework on the product space/manifold
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
and establishing the linear convergence of our proposed mean shift and SCMS algorithms (Theorems
2
and
4
in the main paper) under this framework. In particular, we argue that our rule of thumb for the step size
\eta
in the proposed SCMS algorithm (
22
) is well-suited for the linear convergence of the algorithm when the bandwidth parameters
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h
are chosen to be small. In contrast, we demonstrate via the subspace constrained gradient ascent framework and empirical examples that it is inappropriate to set a constant step size, says
\eta=1
, with respect to the bandwidth parameters
h_{1},h_{2}
in our proposed SCMS algorithm (
22
).

C.1
(Subspace Constrained) Gradient Ascent Framework on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

Whenever
\mathcal{S}_{j}=\Omega_{D_{j}}
is a nonlinear manifold for
j=1
or
2
, the gradient ascent method with an objective function
f
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is defined through the exponential map
\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}}:T_{\bm{z}}\equiv T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
as
(
Absil et al. 2009
;
Zhang and Sra 2016
)
:

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}^{(t)}}\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{t}\cdot\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right)

(C.1)

for
t=0,1,...
. Here, we call
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
the
intrinsic step size
of the gradient ascent method, whose subscript
t
indicates its potential dependence with respect to the iteration number
t
. While the exponential map on the entire product space
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
may be difficult to work with, one may resort to the following lemma and express it in terms of the exponential maps on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Lemma C.1
.

Given the exponential maps
\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{x}}:T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\to\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{y}}:T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})\to\mathcal{S}_{2}
, and
\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}}:T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})\to\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
, we have the following property:

\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}}(\bm{v})=\left(\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{x}}(\bm{v}_{\bm{x}}),\,\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{y}}(\bm{v}_{\bm{y}})\right),

where
\bm{v}=(\bm{v}_{\bm{x}},\bm{v}_{\bm{y}})\in T_{\bm{z}}\equiv T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
and
\bm{v}_{\bm{x}}\in T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1}),\bm{v}_{\bm{y}}\in T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
.

Under the product metric, Lemma
C.1
follows easily from the definition of exponential maps. One can start by verifying that the Riemannian connection
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is given by
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}(X_{1}+X_{2})=\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{Y_{1}}^{(1)}(X_{1})+\bm{\bar{\nabla}}_{Y_{2}}^{(2)}(X_{2})
with
\bm{\bar{\nabla}}^{(j)}
being the Riemannian connection of
\mathcal{S}_{j}
respectively and
X_{j},Y_{j}
as differentiable vector fields in
\mathcal{S}_{j}
for
j=1,2
; see Exercise 1 of Chapter 6 in
Do Carmo 2013
. Then, it is justifiable that
\gamma(t)=(\alpha_{1}(t),\alpha_{2}(t))
is the unique geodesic on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
when
\alpha_{j}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{S}_{j},j=1,2
are geodesics on
\mathcal{S}_{j}
; see Chapter 3 of
do Carmo 2016
and Chapter 5 of
Lee 2006
. We omit the detailed proof here.

Let
\Pi_{j}:\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}\to\mathcal{S}_{j}
for
j=1,2
be the projection maps from
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, respectively. By Lemma
C.1
, the above iterative equation (
C.1
) can be written in terms of the gradient ascent methods within
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
as:

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\left(\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{x}^{(t)}}\left[\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}\cdot\Pi_{1}\left(\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right)\right],\,\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{y}^{(t)}}\left[\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}\cdot\Pi_{2}\left(\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right)\right]\right),

(C.2)

where
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(j)},j=1,2
are the corresponding (componentwise) intrinsic step sizes.

By replacing the Riemannian gradient
\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})
in (
C.1
) or (
C.2
) with the subspace constrained (Riemannian) gradient
V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\nabla f(\bm{z}),

we obtain the following iterative formula for the subspace constrained gradient ascent method on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\\
&\leftarrow\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{z}^{(t)}}\left(\widetilde{\eta}_{t}\cdot V_{d}(\bm{z})V_{d}(\bm{z})^{\top}\nabla f(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right)\\
&=\left(\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{x}^{(t)}}\left[\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}\cdot V_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})V_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla f(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right],\,\mathrm{Exp}_{\bm{y}^{(t)}}\left[\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}\cdot V_{d}^{(2)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})V_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla f(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right]\right),\end{split}

(C.3)

where we write the projection matrix

V_{d}(\bm{z})=\left(\bm{v}_{d+1}(\bm{z}),...,\bm{v}_{D_{T}}(\bm{z})\right)=\begin{pmatrix}V_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z})\\
V_{d}^{(2)}(\bm{z})\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{F}\times(D_{F}-d)}

(C.4)

with
D_{F}=D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}+D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}
,
\bm{v}_{d+1}(\bm{z}),...,\bm{v}_{D_{T}}(\bm{z})
are the last
(D_{1}+D_{2}-d)
eigenvectors of
\mathcal{H}f(\bm{z})
within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
, and
V_{d}^{(j)}(\bm{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{F_{j}}\times(D_{F_{j}}-d)}
with
D_{F_{j}}=D_{j}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{j}=\Omega_{D_{j}}\}}
for
j=1,2
.

C.2
Intrinsic Step Size of the Proposed Mean Shift Algorithms Under the Gradient Ascent Framework on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

We first derive the intrinsic step size parameters
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)},\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}
of the simultaneous mean shift algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
(Version A) under the above gradient ascent framework. Recall from
Section 3
in the main paper that the simultaneous mean shift algorithm on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
iterates the following formula until convergence:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\right)^{\top}&=\left(\bm{x}^{(t+1)},\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\right)^{\top}\\
&\leftarrow\left(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)}\right)^{\top}+\Xi(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)}\\
\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(C.5)

with some additional standardization whenever
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional for
t=0,1,...
. Additionally, we remind the reader of the definition of KDE on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right),\end{split}

(C.6)

whose (total) gradient is (c.f. Equation (
12
) in the main paper)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})&=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})\\
\nabla_{\bm{y}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{n}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{h_{1}^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i})\cdot k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\\
\frac{1}{h_{2}^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i})\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}G_{\bm{x}}\left[\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}-\bm{x}\right]\\
G_{\bm{y}}\left[\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}-\bm{y}\right]\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

(C.7)

with

\displaystyle G_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{nh_{1}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle G_{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{nh_{2}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right).

Thus, the mean shift vector
\Xi(\bm{z})
can be written in terms of the (total) gradient estimator
\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
as:

\Xi(\bm{z})=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})/G_{\bm{x}}\\
\nabla_{\bm{y}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})/G_{\bm{y}}\end{pmatrix}.

(C.8)

We consider the intrinsic step size under three different combinations of the product space.

\bullet

Case 1:
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
. Given (
C.8
), the simultaneous mean shift iterative formula can be written as

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\bm{z}^{(t)}+\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})/G_{\bm{x}^{(t)}}\\
\nabla_{\bm{y}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})/G_{\bm{y}^{(t)}}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that under linear kernel profiles, the normalizing constants
C_{k_{1},D_{1}},C_{k_{2},D_{2}}
are independent of the bandwidth parameters
h_{1},h_{2}
. Hence, it is natural to see that the intrinsic step size parameters
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)},\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}
are

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{G_{\bm{x}^{(t)}}}=-\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{G_{\bm{y}^{(t)}}}=-\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}.

The following Lemma
C.2
elucidates the asymptotic rates of
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)},\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}
in terms of the bandwidth parameters.

Lemma C.2
.

Assume conditions (A1-2) and
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
. Then, when
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}\to\infty
, we have that

\displaystyle h_{1}^{2}\cdot G_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}\cdot f(\bm{z})\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\bm{y}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\,d\bm{x}d\bm{y}+O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=O(1)+O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right),

and similarly,

\displaystyle h_{2}^{2}\cdot G_{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}\cdot f(\bm{z})\int_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\bm{y}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\,d\bm{x}d\bm{y}+O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=O(1)+O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right).

The proof of Lemma
C.2
is similar to Theorem 1 in
Cheng 1995
and Lemma 3.2 in
Zhang and Chen 2023
, which makes use of the Taylor’s theorem; see the proof of Proposition
3
in
Section D
for a sketch. It implies that
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)},\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}=O(h^{2})
as the bandwidths
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}\to\infty
.

\bullet

Case 2:
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
. Apart from updating the sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}=\left\{(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
with (
C.5
), we need to standardize
\bm{x}^{(t+1)}
as
\frac{\bm{x}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}}\in\Omega_{D_{1}}
. Hence, a complete mean shift iterative step reads

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow-\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\left|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\right|\right|_{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot L^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-\bm{X}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}}{h_{1}^{2}}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\left|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot L^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-\bm{X}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}}{h_{1}^{2}}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\right|\right|_{2}},

\displaystyle\bm{y}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\bm{y}^{(t)}+\frac{1}{G_{\bm{y}^{(t)}}}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{y}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})

with the directional kernel profile
k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)=L\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)
.
Then, one can observe that when
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h
are small,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{G_{\bm{y}^{(t)}}}=-\frac{h_{2}^{2}}{\frac{2C_{k_{1},D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot C_{k_{2},D_{2}}}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}=O(h^{2}).

To derive
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}
and its asymptotic rate, we denote the angle between
\bm{x}^{(t+1)}
and
\bm{x}^{(t)}
by
\theta_{t}^{(1)}
. Under the gradient ascent framework (
C.2
) (projected to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
), we have the following equality for the geodesic distance
d_{g}(\bm{x}^{(t+1)},\bm{x}^{(t)})
on
\Omega_{D_{1}}
as:

\displaystyle\arccos\left(\left(\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\right)^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}\right)=\theta_{t}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}\left|\left|\Pi_{1}\left(\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right)\right|\right|_{2}=\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\cdot\sin\theta_{t}^{(1)},

where

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=-\frac{C_{k_{1},D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot C_{k_{2},D_{2}}}{nh_{1}^{2}h_{2}^{D_{2}}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot L^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-\bm{X}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}}{h_{1}^{2}}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right).

See
Figure C.1
for a graphical illustration. Indeed,
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
is also a total gradient of
\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
within
\mathcal{S}_{1}
when
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
. Thus, the intrinsic step size on
\Omega_{D_{1}}
is

\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}=\frac{\theta_{t}^{(1)}}{\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\cdot\sin\theta_{t}^{(1)}}.

As
t\to\infty
and the initial point
\bm{z}^{(0)}
is near an estimated local mode
\widehat{\bm{m}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
, we know that
\theta_{t}^{(1)}\to 0
and
\frac{\theta_{t}^{(1)}}{\sin\theta_{t}^{(1)}}\to 1
will be upper bounded. Therefore, the intrinsic step size
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}
is essentially determined by
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})
. The following Lemma
C.3
illuminates the asymptotic rate of the intrinsic step size
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}
.

Assume conditions (A1-2). Then, for any fixed
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}

h_{1}^{2}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}_{\bm{x}}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\bm{x}f(\bm{z})C_{L,D_{1}}+o(1)+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right)

Case 3:
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\Omega_{D_{2}}

\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}=\left\{(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow-\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\left|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{X}_{i}\cdot k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\right|\right|_{2}}

\displaystyle\bm{y}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow-\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)}{\left|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}^{(t)}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}^{(t)}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)\right|\right|_{2}}

with the directional kernel profile
k_{j}\left(\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)=L_{j}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)
for

. Let

\bm{x}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{Y}_{i}\cdot L_{1}\left(\frac{1-\bm{X}_{i}^{\top}\bm{x}^{(t)}}{h_{1}^{2}}\right)L_{2}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-\bm{Y}_{i}^{\top}\bm{y}^{(t)}}{h_{2}^{2}}\right),

\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(j)}=O(h^{2})

C.3
Intrinsic Step Size of the Proposed SCMS Algorithm Under the Subspace Constrained Gradient Ascent Framework on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

Recall from
Section 4
in the main paper that our proposed SCMS algorithm (
22
) on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
with Gaussian and/or von Mises kernels iterates the following equation:

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\bm{z}^{(t)}+\eta\cdot\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\bm{H}^{-1}\Xi(\bm{z}^{(t)})

with an additional standardization whenever
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are directional for
t=0,1,...
, where
\eta>0
is the step size (i.e., a tuning parameter). We now derive the intrinsic step size parameter
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
(or
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)},\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}
) of the SCMS algorithm (
22
) under the subspace constrained gradient ascent framework (
C.3
) on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. There are three different cases based on the configurations of
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

\bullet

Case 1:
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
. Some simple algebra show that
\bm{H}^{-1}\Xi(\bm{z})=\frac{\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})}

under Gaussian and/or von Mises kernels; see also
Chacón and Duong 2013
;
Chacón and Monfort 2013
. Thus, the iterative equation (
22
) becomes

\bm{z}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\bm{z}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\cdot\frac{\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})},

(C.9)

so it is intuitive to see that the intrinsic step size is
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}=\frac{\eta}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}
.

\bullet

Case 2:
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{D_{2}}
. Besides updating the sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}=\left\{(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
with (
C.9
), we need to standardize
\bm{x}^{(t+1)}
as
\frac{\bm{x}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}}
. Hence, a complete SCMS iterative step reads

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\bm{x}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}{\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\bm{x}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right|\right|_{2}},

\displaystyle\bm{y}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\bm{y}^{(t)}+\frac{\eta}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}\cdot\widehat{V}_{d}^{(2)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)}),

where we decompose
\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})
into two parts
\widehat{V}_{d}^{(j)}(\bm{z}),j=1,2
within
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
respectively as in (
C.4
). Compared with (
C.3
), it is natural to obtain that
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}=\frac{\eta}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}
.

As for
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}
within
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}
, we first notice that
\bm{x}
is orthogonal to the columns of
\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z})
for any
\bm{z}=(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
. This is because
(\bm{x},\bm{0})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
is a unit eigenvector of the Riemannian Hessian
\mathcal{H}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
that is normal to the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}=T_{\bm{x}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\times T_{\bm{y}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})
. Given that the columns of
\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})
consist of the last
(D_{1}+D_{2}-d)
eigenvectors of
\mathcal{H}\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
within the tangent space
T_{\bm{z}}
, it follows that
\bm{x}^{\top}\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z})=\bm{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times(D_{1}+D_{2}-d)}
; see
Figure C.2
for a graphical illustration.
If we denote the angle between
\bm{x}^{(t+1)}
and
\bm{x}^{(t)}
by
\theta_{t}^{(1)}
, then we have the following equality for the geodesic distance
d_{g}(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{x}^{(t+1)})
on
\Omega_{D_{1}}
as:

\displaystyle\theta_{t}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}\left|\left|\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right|\right|_{2}=\frac{\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}}{\eta}\cdot\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\tan\theta_{t}^{(1)},

where we leverage the orthogonality between
\bm{x}^{(t)}
and the columns of
\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})
to obtain the second equality; see
Figure C.2
. Thus, the intrinsic step on
\Omega_{D_{1}}
is

\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}=\frac{\eta}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}\cdot\frac{\theta_{t}^{(1)}}{\tan\theta_{t}^{(1)}}.

\bullet

Case 3:
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\Omega_{D_{2}}
. Under the iteration (
C.9
) and extra standardizations
\frac{\bm{x}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{x}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}}
and
\frac{\bm{y}^{(t+1)}}{\left|\left|\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\right|\right|_{2}}
, a complete SCMS iteration on
\Omega_{D_{1}}\times\Omega_{D_{2}}
updates the sequence
\left\{\bm{z}^{(t)}\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}=\left\{(\bm{x}^{(t)},\bm{y}^{(t)})\right\}_{t=0}^{\infty}
as:

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\frac{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\bm{x}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}{\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\bm{x}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}^{(1)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right|\right|_{2}}

and

\displaystyle\quad\bm{y}^{(t+1)}\leftarrow\frac{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\bm{y}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}^{(2)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}{\left|\left|\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\bm{y}^{(t)}+\eta\widehat{V}_{d}^{(2)}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z}^{(t)})^{\top}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})\right|\right|_{2}},

where we again leverage the decomposition of
\widehat{V}_{d}(\bm{z})
in (

). Moreover, since
(\bm{x},\bm{0}),(\bm{0},\bm{y})\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}

implies that

. Let
\theta_{t}^{(1)}
be the angle between
\bm{x}^{(t)}
and
\bm{x}^{(t+1)}
, and denote the angle between
\bm{y}^{(t)}
and
\bm{y}^{(t+1)}
by
\theta_{t}^{(2)}
. Thus, based on the arguments in
Case 2
, we know that the intrinsic steps on
\Omega_{D_{1}}
and
\Omega_{D_{2}}
are

\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(1)}=\frac{\eta}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}\cdot\frac{\theta_{t}^{(1)}}{\tan\theta_{t}^{(1)}}\text{ and }\widetilde{\eta}_{t}^{(2)}=\frac{\eta}{\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}^{(t)})}\cdot\frac{\theta_{t}^{(2)}}{\tan\theta_{t}^{(2)}}.

\mathcal{P}_{\bm{z}}\bm{H}^{-1}\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\nabla\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})=\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\bm{H}^{-1}\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z}).

As for the pointwise convergence of
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\bm{H}^{-1}\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})
, we know from Lemma
B.1
that for any fixed
\bm{z}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\mathcal{S}_{2}
,

\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})-\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}}}\right)

(D.4)

as
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}\to\infty
. It remains to derive the pointwise convergence rate of
\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})^{-1}\bm{H}^{-1}=\left[\bm{H}\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})\right]^{-1}
. Recall that

\bm{H}\widetilde{D}(\bm{z})=\mathrm{Diag}\left(h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}\bm{I}_{D_{1}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}\}}},h_{2}^{2}G_{\bm{y}}\bm{I}_{D_{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{S}_{2}=\Omega_{D_{2}}\}}}\right),

where

\displaystyle h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2C(\bm{H})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle h_{2}^{2}G_{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2C(\bm{H})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\prod_{j=1}^{2}C_{k_{j},D_{j}}(h_{j})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right).

It suggests that
h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}},h_{2}^{2}G_{\bm{y}}
can be viewed as “KDEs” with kernel profiles
-k_{1}^{\prime}(\cdot)k_{2}(\cdot)
and
-k_{1}(\cdot)k_{2}^{\prime}(\cdot)
, respectively. With regards to the pointwise convergence rate of
h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}
, we consider the following two cases of the kernel profile
k_{1}
.

\bullet

Case 1:
k_{1}
is a linear kernel profile
. In this case,
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}}
and

C_{k_{1},D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)=\frac{C_{k,D_{1}}}{h_{1}^{D_{1}}}\cdot k\left(\frac{\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{2}\right).

Then,

h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}=-\frac{C_{k,D_{1}}\cdot C_{k_{2},D_{2}}(h_{2})}{nh_{1}^{D_{1}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}k^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right).

One can follow the arguments in Theorems 1 and 2 of
Chacón et al. 2011
or Lemma 3.2 of
Zhang and Chen 2023
to show that

h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}-\left(-C_{k,D_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{D_{1}}}k^{\prime}\left(\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}/2\right)d\bm{x}\cdot f(\bm{z})\right)=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right)

(D.5)

as
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}\to\infty
.

\bullet

Case 2:
k_{1}
is a directional kernel profile
. In this case,
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Omega_{D_{1}}
and
C_{k_{1},D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot k_{1}\left(\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right)=C_{L,D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot L\left(\frac{\left|\left|\bm{u}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}}{2}\right).

Then, using the fact that
\left|\left|\bm{x}\right|\right|_{2}=\left|\left|\bm{X}_{i}\right|\right|_{2}=1
for any
i=1,...,n
,

h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}=-\frac{C_{L,D_{1}}(h_{1})\cdot C_{k_{2},D_{2}}(h_{2})}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}^{2}}\right)k_{2}\left(\left|\left|\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right|\right|_{2}^{2}\right).

One can follow the arguments in Proposition 1 of
García-Portugués et al. 2013
and Theorem 2 of
Zhang and Chen 2021b
to show that

h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}-\left(-\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}L(r)r^{\frac{D_{1}}{2}-1}dr}{\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{\prime}(r)r^{\frac{D_{1}}{2}-1}dr}\cdot f(\bm{z})\right)=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}}}\right)

(D.6)

as
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}}\to\infty
.

Combining (
D.4
), (
D.5
), and (
D.6
), we obtain by Taylor’s theorem that

\frac{\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})}{h_{1}^{2}G_{\bm{x}}}-\frac{\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})}{\widetilde{F}_{1}}=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}}}\right)

as
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}\to\infty
. A similar argument demonstrates that

\frac{\mathrm{grad}\,\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{z})}{h_{2}^{2}G_{\bm{y}}}-\frac{\mathrm{grad}\,f(\bm{z})}{\widetilde{F}_{2}}=O(h^{2})+O_{P}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}}}\right)

as
h_{1},h_{2}\lesssim h\to 0
and
nh^{D_{1}+D_{2}+2}\to\infty
. The results thus follow.
∎

With an additional condition (A3), one can extend Proposition
3
to an uniform rate of convergence. This stronger result will not provide more insights into the inconsistency of the naive SCMS algorithm, so we omit its presentation.

Proof of Proposition

\widehat{f}_{\bm{h}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{1}\left(\frac{\bm{x}-\bm{X}_{i}}{h_{1}}\right)K_{2}\left(\frac{\bm{y}-\bm{Y}_{i}}{h_{2}}\right)

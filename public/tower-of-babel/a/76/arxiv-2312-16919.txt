Abstract
. The primary objective of this paper is to derive explicit formulas for rank one and rank two Drinfeld modules over a specific domain denoted by 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . This domain corresponds to the projective line associated with an infinite place of degree two. To achieve the goals, we construct a pair of standard rank one Drinfeld modules whose coefficients are in the Hilbert class field of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . We demonstrate that the period lattice of the exponential functions corresponding to both modules behaves similarly to the period lattice of the Carlitz module, the standard rank one Drinfeld module defined over rational function fields. Moreover, we employ Anderson’s t t -motive to obtain the complete family of rank two Drinfeld modules. This family is parameterized by the invariant J = λ q 2 + 1 J=\lambda^{q^{2}+1} which effectively serves as the counterpart of the j j -invariant for elliptic curves. Building upon the concepts introduced by van der Heiden, particularly with regard to rank two Drinfeld modules, we are able to reformulate the Weil pairing of Drinfeld modules of any rank using a specialized polynomial in multiple variables known as the Weil operator. As an illustrative example, we provide a detailed examination of a more explicit formula for the Weil pairing and the Weil operator of rank two Drinfeld modules over the domain 𝒜 \mathcal{A} .



Key words:
Drinfeld module; Drinfeld modular curve; Period lattice; Weil pairing.

1.
Introduction

Drinfeld modules are function field analogues of elliptic curves that were
studied by Drinfeld
[
5
]
in the 70’s for proving the Langlands
conjectures for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. Drinfeld module encompasses a rich arithmetic
theory, and indeed a number of special cases were already studied by Carlitz
and Wade (see
[
3
,
19
]
for instance) prior to Drinfeld’s general
definition. Works of many authors (such as Drinfeld, Hayes, Goss, Gekeler,
Anderson, Brownawell, Thakur, Papanikolas and Cornelissen, to name a few)
established many striking similarities, and yet a few astounding differences
between elliptic curves and Drinfeld modules (particularly in rank 2);
the reader may consult
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
,

[
13
]
and
[
15
]
for a more elaborate discussion. The comparison
of similarities and differences in the statements (and proofs) between
corresponding results in the classical and Drinfeld theories provides valuable
insights on some common themes in number theory.

1.1.
Explicit representations of Drinfeld modules

Many explicit computations of Drinfeld modules are based on
polynomial ring, since polynomial ring is free generated,
and there is no obstruction for constructing Drinfeld modules. Precisely,
for a polynomial ring
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, a rank
r
Drinfeld module

\phi
is represented as a twist polynomial

(1)

\phi_{T}=\iota(T)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}\tau^{i},

where
\tau
is the
q
-th Frobenius endomorphism. Among these Drinfeld
modules, the rank one module of the form

C_{T}=T+\tau

is called Carlitz module. One of the motivations for studying the Carlitz
module is to explore explicit class field theory for the rational function
field as in
[
4
]
.

There are few explicit instances of Drinfeld modules defined over non-polynomial
rings. In
[
6
]
, Dummit and Hayes computed the explicit form of the
sgn-normalized rank-one Drinfeld modules associated with all elliptic curves
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
4\leqslant q\leqslant 13
, where the infinite
place is ramified. Extended this work, Bae and Kang
[
2
]
investigated
the rank one Drinfeld modules on elliptic curves and hyper-elliptic curves
in the case that the infinite place is inert. In
[
8
]
, Green and
Papanikolas made a detailed account of sign-normalized rank one Drinfeld
modules over the coordinate ring of an elliptic curve, which provide a
parallel theory to the Carlitz module.

In the present paper, we focus on the domain

\mathcal{A}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]
, where
x
and

y
verify the relation:

y^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)xy+\zeta^{q+1}x^{2}-x=0,

and
\zeta
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. In
fact, the fraction field of
\mathcal{A}
is equal to the rational function
field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
with
t=\frac{y}{x}
. From a geometric perspective,,
it corresponds to the affine domain of a degree-two-point truncated projective
line. We describe clearly the rank one and rank two Drinfeld modules over

\mathcal{A}
. Considering the leading terms, Drinfeld modules are split
into two types, say
\zeta
-type and
\zeta^{q}
-type. According to
the Hayes module theory, the two types are in one-to-one correspondence
with the ideal classes of
\mathcal{A}
. Among these, we obtain two standard
rank one Drinfeld modules corresponding to the ideal classes of

\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\sigma}
. By abuse of terminology we set
T:=\frac{1}{t-\zeta^{q}}
and

T^{\sigma}:=\frac{1}{t-\zeta}
. Consequently, the poles of pair

(T,\,T^{\sigma})
are located at the truncated point of the projective
line. This notation for
T
is consistent with its usage in the Carlitz
module. With these notations,
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\sigma}
are written
as

\begin{cases}\Psi_{x}=T^{\sigma-1}\tau^{2}+(T^{\sigma}+T^{q-1+\sigma})\tau+x\\
\Psi_{y}=\zeta T^{\sigma-1}\tau^{2}+(tT^{\sigma}+\zeta T^{q-1+\sigma})\tau+y,\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\Psi_{x}^{\sigma}=T^{1-\sigma}\tau^{2}+(T+T^{(q-1)\sigma+1})\tau+x\\
\Psi_{y}^{\sigma}=\zeta^{q}T^{1-\sigma}\tau^{2}+(tT+\zeta^{q}T^{(q-1)\sigma+1})\tau+y.\end{cases}

We then proved that the period lattice of
\Psi
is given by

\frac{\sqrt[q-1]{-[2]}}{[1]}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{[i]}{[i+2]}\right)T^{\sigma-1}\cdot\mathcal{A},

where
[2k-1]=T^{q^{2k-1}}-T^{\sigma}
,
[2k]=T^{q^{2k}}-T
with

k\geqslant 1
, and
\sqrt[q-1]{-[2]}
denotes a
(q-1)
-th root of

-[2]
.
Analogously, the period lattice of
\Psi^{\sigma}
is given
by

\frac{\sqrt[q-1]{-[2]T^{\sigma-1}}}{[1]}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{[i]}{[i+2]}\right)T^{\sigma}\cdot I_{\infty}^{-1},

where
\sqrt[q-1]{-[2]T^{\sigma-1}}
denotes a
(q-1)
-th root of

-[2]T^{\sigma-1}
and
I_{\infty}^{-1}
is a fractional ideal
of
\mathcal{A}
generated by
1,\frac{y}{x}(=t)
.

The construction of rank two Drinfeld modules is involved with the Anderson’s

t
-motives
[
1
]
. For simplicity, we focus on the

\zeta^{q}
-type and obtain the following complete family.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu
be a
(q+1)
-th root of
-T^{\sigma+q}
. There
exists a complete family
\Phi^{J}
of rank two Drinfeld modules over

\mathcal{A}
-fields parameterized by
J
, represented by the pair

(\Phi_{x}^{J},\Phi_{y}^{J})
, where

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}^{J}=

\displaystyle J^{q^{2}+q}\tau^{4}+\frac{J^{q^{2}+q}-J^{q+1}}{1-\zeta^{1-q}}\tau^{3}

(2)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{\zeta^{q-1}J^{q+1}}{(1-\zeta^{q-1})^{2}}+\frac{\nu T^{q(q-1)(1-\sigma)-\sigma}J^{q}}{\zeta^{q}-\zeta}-\frac{TJ}{(\zeta^{q}-\zeta)\nu}+\frac{T^{\sigma q-q}}{\zeta^{q+1}}\Big)\tau^{2}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{T^{\sigma q}+T^{\sigma}}{\zeta}+\frac{(T^{\sigma+q}+x)J}{(1-\zeta^{1-q})\nu}\Big)\tau+x,

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{y}^{J}=

\displaystyle\zeta^{q}J^{q^{2}+q}\tau^{4}+\zeta^{q}\Big(\frac{J^{q^{2}+q}-\zeta^{1-q}J^{q+1}}{1-\zeta^{1-q}}\Big)\tau^{3}

(3)

\displaystyle+\zeta^{q}\Big(\frac{J^{q+1}}{(1-\zeta^{q-1})^{2}}+\frac{\nu T^{q(q-1)(1-\sigma)-\sigma}J^{q}}{\zeta^{q}-\zeta}-\frac{TJ}{(\zeta^{q}-\zeta)\nu}+\frac{T^{\sigma q-q}}{\zeta^{q+1}}\Big)\tau^{2}

\displaystyle+\zeta^{q}\Big(\frac{\zeta^{q}T^{\sigma q}+tT^{\sigma}}{\zeta^{q+1}}+\frac{(\zeta T^{\sigma+q}+y)J}{(\zeta^{q}-\zeta)\nu}\Big)\tau+y.

The wedge product of
\Phi^{J}
, i.e.,

\Psi^{J}=\wedge^{2}\Phi^{J}
is represented by

\begin{cases}\Psi_{x}^{J}=-J^{q+1}\tau^{2}-(\frac{x+T^{q+\sigma}}{\nu})J\tau+x\\
\Psi_{y}^{J}=-\zeta J^{q+1}\tau^{2}-(\frac{y+\zeta T^{q+\sigma}}{\nu})J\tau+y.\end{cases}

1.2.
Weil pairing of Drinfeld modules

As a classical theory, the Weil pairing for elliptic curves
E
serves
as a perfect bilinear form from the
m
-torsion points (
m\geqslant 1
) of
E
to the
m
-th roots
\mu_{m}
of unity, formally written as

E[m]\times E[m]\to\mu_{m}.

Given a Drinfeld module
\phi
over a fixed
A
-field and an ideal

I
of
A
, the group
\ker\phi_{I}
equipped with rank
r

A
-module structure is the counterpart of torsion point group on elliptic
curves. Interpreting the image of the Weil pairing by

\ker\psi_{I}
for some rank one Drinfeld module
\psi
, there shall
be a perfect multilinear map

\operatorname{Weil}_{I}:~\prod_{i=1}^{r}\ker(\phi_{I})\to\ker(\psi_{I}).

We provide a brief interpretation for the relationship between Weil pairing
and Galois action. Let
K
be the fraction field of
A
. Then the set of
torsions
\ker(\phi_{I})
is an
A
-module, which is naturally equipped
with an action of the absolute Galois group

G_{K}=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{sep}}/K)
. Since the Galois action
commutes with the
A
-action on
\ker(\phi_{I})
, we have the representation:

\rho_{\phi}:G_{K}\to\mathrm{Aut}_{A}(\ker(\phi_{I}))\cong\mathrm{GL}_{r}(A/I).

Composing with the determinant map

\det:\mathrm{GL}_{r}(A/I)\to(A/I)^{*}=\mathrm{GL}_{1}(A/I)
, we
get a representation homomorphism

G_{K}\to\mathrm{GL}_{1}(A/I).

Such a homomorphism shall be viewed as

\rho_{\psi}:G_{K}\to\mathrm{Aut}_{A}(\ker\psi_{I})

for some rank one Drinfeld module
\psi
. In this way, the Weil pairing
shall be compatible with Galois actions.

Hamahata gave an explicit construction of rank two Weil pairing in

[
9
]
over the rational function field case. In general, the difficult
part to understand Weil pairing is that in the category of Drinfeld modules
it is not at all obvious how to define tensor products and how to take
subquotients. However, in the equivalent category of Anderson’s pure

t
-motives, the tensor product is closed under the operations of taking
subquotients and tensor products. In this way, van der Heiden

[
16
]
guaranteed the existence of Weil pairing. In particular,
he showed that for those Drinfeld module
\phi
of the form
(
1
)
, the rank one Drinfeld module
\psi
can be represented
by

\psi_{T}=(-1)^{r-1}g_{r}\tau+\iota(T),

which coincides with Hamahata’s construction. Nevertheless, the Weil pairing

\operatorname{Weil}_{I}
can be formally characterized by the following
properties:

(1)

The map
\operatorname{Weil}_{I}
is
A
-multilinear, i.e. it is

A
-linear in each component.

(2)

It is alternating: if
\mu_{l}=\mu_{h}
for
l\neq h
, then

\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=0
.

(3)

It is surjective and nondegenerate: if

\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=0~{\mathrm{for~all}}~\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{i-1},\mu_{i+1},\ldots,\mu_{r}\in\ker\psi_{I},~{\mathrm{then}}~\mu_{i}=0.

(4)

It is Galois invariant:

\sigma\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\sigma\mu_{1},\ldots,\sigma\mu_{r})~{\mathrm{for~all}}~\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{K}/K).

(5)

It satisfies the following compatibility condition for

\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}\in\ker\psi_{IJ}
:

\psi_{J}\operatorname{Weil}_{IJ}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\phi_{J}(\mu_{1}),\ldots,\phi_{J}(\mu_{r})).

Using this alternative definition, J. Katen
[
10
]
provided an explicit,
and elementary proof of the existence of Weil pairing for Drinfeld modules
over rational functional fields, which generalizes the rank two formula
in
[
16
]
. More precisely, for the
I
-torsions

\mu_{1},\cdots\mu_{n}
, the Weil pairing is written as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{i}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})\\
0\leqslant i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\leqslant n-1\end{subarray}}a_{\mathbf{i}}T_{1}^{i_{1}}T_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots T_{n}^{i_{n}}\right)*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{i}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})\\
0\leqslant i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\leqslant n-1\end{subarray}}a_{\mathbf{i}}\mathcal{M}(\phi_{T^{i_{1}}}(\mu_{1}),\ldots,\phi_{T^{i_{r}}}(\mu_{r})),

where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the Moore determinant, and

a_{\mathbf{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
are coefficients depending on

I
. Indicated by this, we arrive at a nice formulation of Weil pairing
for rank
r
Drinfeld modules over arbitrary base fields:

\operatorname{Weil}_{I}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=\operatorname{WO}_{I}^{(r)}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}),

where
\operatorname{WO}_{I}^{(r)}
denotes a non-trivial polynomial
with pairwise symmetric property. In particular, when

A=\mathcal{A}
, by constructing the dual basis of

\mathcal{A}/I
, we are able to derive an explicit expression of

\operatorname{WO}_{I}^{(2)}
, and refer to
O^{\prime}_{I}
or

O^{\prime\prime}_{I}
in
Notation
5.11
.

This paper is organized as follows. First, in Section
2
, we
will deal with the explicit formula of rank one Drinfeld modules over

\mathcal{A}
and give a detailed discussion of the correspondent motive
structure. Using them, in Section
3
, we compute the period lattices
of the standard models of rank one Drinfeld modules. In Section
4
, we construct the modular curve (i.e., the
J
-line) of
rank two Drinfeld modules. Finally, in Section
5
, we discover
the explicit formula of Weil pairing.

2.
Rank one Drinfeld modules

2.1.
Preliminaries

We recall briefly here some definitions and basic notions of Drinfeld modules.
Let
X
be a nonsingular complete curve over a finite field

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Denote by
\infty
a rational point of
X
, which will
be viewed as the infinity of
X
. Let
A
be the Dedekind domain corresponding
to
X\setminus\{\infty\}
. Denote by
d_{\infty}
the degree of

\infty
. Let
K
be the fraction field of
A
, which is also the function
field of
X
. Denote by
K_{\infty}
the unique complete field with
respect to the
\infty
-valuation
v_{\infty}
. The completion

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
of the algebraic closure of
K_{\infty}
plays
an analogous role, in the function field setting, of the complex number
field
\mathbb{C}
. Using the valuation
v_{\infty}
at
\infty
, one
may define the degree of
a\in K
by

\deg(a):=-d_{\infty}\cdot v_{\infty}(a).

For
a\in A
, the degree of
a
coincides with the cardinality of

A/(a)
.

The field
L
equipped with an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebraic homomorphism

\iota:A\to L
is called an
A
-field. The
A
-characteristic of

L
is defined to be

\operatorname{char}_{A}(L)=\ker(\iota)
. If

\operatorname{char}_{A}(L)=0
, we say that
L
has generic characteristic
or infinite characteristic in order to avoid confusion with the usual 0
characteristic.

Let
L\{\tau\}
denote the twist polynomial ring, which is generated by
the
q
-th Frobenius endomorphism
\tau
such that

\tau a=a^{q}\tau
. There exists a natural action of

L\{\tau\}
on
\bar{L}
, given by

\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\tau^{i}:\ell\to\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\ell^{q^{i}}.

The Drinfeld module over the
A
-field
L
is defined to be a non-trivial
algebraic homomorphism

\phi:A\to L\{\tau\},\quad a\mapsto\phi_{a},

such that the constant term of
\phi_{a}
equals
\iota(a)
, where
the non-trivial restriction means
\phi_{a}\not\equiv\iota(a)
. If
the image of
\phi
is contained in
E\{\tau\}
for some subfield

E\subseteq L
, we say that
\phi
is defined over
E
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a twist polynomial over
L
. Given Drinfeld modules

\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
, we say that
\lambda
is an isogeny from

\phi
to
\phi^{\prime}
, if

\lambda\phi_{a}=\phi^{\prime}_{a}\lambda

holds for
a\in A
. If
\lambda
is a nonzero constant polynomial,
then
\lambda
is called an isomorphism. In other words, Drinfeld module

\phi
is isomorphic to its conjugate

\lambda^{-1}\phi\lambda
.

As a not immediate fact, there exists a positive integer
r
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\deg_{\tau}(\phi_{a})=r\deg(a),

for any
a\in A
. The integer
r
in Equation
(
4
)
is
called the rank of
\phi
. Via the Drinfeld module
\phi
, we have the
Drinfeld action of
A
on
\bar{L}
, given by

(5)

\displaystyle a:~\ell\to\phi_{a}(\ell),

for
a\in A
and
\ell\in\bar{L}
. The set

\ker(\phi_{a}):=\{x\in\bar{L}:\phi_{a}(x)=0\}
constitutes
the
a
-torsion points of
\phi
; it is in fact a submodule of

\bar{L}
. If
a\in A
is prime to the characteristic of
L
, then

\ker(\phi_{a})\cong(A/(a))^{\oplus r}

as
A
-modules. In analogy to the elliptic curve setting, the analytic
uniformization theorem establishes an equivalence between the categories
of lattices in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, with morphisms coming from multiplications,
and Drinfeld modules over
A
-field
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, with isogeny
morphisms. In this way, each Drinfeld module over

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is associated to an exponential function

\exp_{\Lambda}
arising from some lattice
\Lambda
of

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
.

Notation 2.2
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
A
. For a Drinfeld module
\phi
over
L
, the
set

\{\phi_{a}~|~a\in I\}

form a left ideal of
L\{\tau\}
. As this ideal in principle, we denote
by
\phi_{I}
the unique monic maximal right-divisor. Using this notation,
we have

(6)

\ker\phi_{I}=\{x\in\ker\phi_{a}~|~\text{for each }a\in I\}.

The twist polynomial
\phi_{I}
is called the
annihilator
of

I
.

2.2.
Domain

We introduce the domain here which represents the truncated projective
line. Let
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
be the rational function field over finite
field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. As a basic fact, the places of
K
can be written
as

\mathbb{P}_{K}=\{P_{g}|g\text{ is an irreducible monic polynomial over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$}\}\cup\{\infty\}.

We consider the degree two place
P_{\pi}
which corresponds to the irreducible
monic polynomial
\pi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
. In the rest of this paper,
we refer to
P_{\pi}
the infinity place of
K
, instead of the symbol

\infty
.

Let
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be a root of
\pi
. Denote by

\mathrm{Tr}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}
the trace map. Then one may reformulate
\pi
as

\pi=(t-\zeta)(t-\zeta^{q})=t^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)t+\zeta^{q+1},

where
\zeta^{q+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and

\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)=\zeta+\zeta^{q}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The coordinate domain of
P_{\pi}
-truncated line is defined by

\mathcal{A}:=\{u\in K~|~v_{Q}(u)\geqslant 0\text{ for $Q\in\mathbb{P}_{K}\setminus\{P_{\pi}\}$ }\}\,{\cup\,\{0\}},

where
v_{Q}(u)
denotes the valuation of
u
at the place
Q
. Let

\mathbb{F}_{\pi}
be the residue field of
K
at
P_{\pi}
, which is
isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\zeta)\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. From
the definition, the fraction field of
\mathcal{A}
is identified with
the rational function field
K=\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
. For

k\geqslant 1
, we view
kP_{\pi}
as a divisor of
K
. From

[
14
]
the Riemann-Roch space associated to
kP_{\pi}
is identical
to the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear space

\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi}):=\{u\in K~|~v_{P_{\pi}}(u)\geqslant-k\text{ and }v_{Q}(u)\geqslant 0\text{ for $Q\in\mathbb{P}_{K}\setminus\{P_{\pi}\}$ }\}\cup\{0\}.

Then
\mathcal{A}
can be expressed as

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi}).

Applying Riemann-Roch theorem, we find that for each

k\geqslant 1
,
\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})
is spanned by some monomials
in variables
x:=\frac{1}{\pi}
and
y:=\frac{t}{\pi}
. It follows
that
\mathcal{A}
is generated by
x
and
y
as an

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra. Indeed, by considering

x=\frac{1}{\pi}
and
y=\frac{t}{\pi}
, we obtain the relation
of the form

(8)

\displaystyle\rho(x,y):=y^{2}-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)xy+\zeta^{q+1}x^{2}-x=0.

Consequently, there exists an algebraic isomorphism

\mathcal{A}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]/(\rho(x,y)).

It yields that the elements of
\mathcal{A}
can be written as

\frac{F(t)}{\pi^{d}}
for some
F(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
with

\deg(F(t))\leqslant 2d
. For further study, we consider the two ideals
of
\mathcal{A}
:

I_{0}:=(x-\zeta^{-q-1},y),\qquad I_{\infty}:=(x,y).

One may check that

I_{\infty}^{2}=(x)\quad\text{ and }\quad I_{0}I_{\infty}=(y).

It is easy to see that the ideal class

\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{A}):=\frac{\text{Ideal group of $\mathcal{A}$}}{\text{Principle ideal group of $\mathcal{A}$}}

is generated by
I_{0}
(or
I_{\infty}
), and therefore,

\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

2.3.
Hayes module

According to the definition, a Drinfeld module of rank
r
over an

\mathcal{A}
-field is equivalent to the pair of twist polynomials

(\phi_{x},\phi_{y})
satisfying that the conditions

\displaystyle\deg_{\tau}(\phi_{x})

\displaystyle=\deg_{\tau}(\phi_{y})=2r,

\displaystyle\phi_{x}\phi_{y}

\displaystyle=\phi_{y}\phi_{x},

and

\phi_{y}\phi_{y}-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)\phi_{x}\phi_{y}=\phi_{x}-\zeta^{q+1}\phi_{x}\phi_{x}.

For a Drinfeld module
\phi
, denote by

\operatorname{LT}_{\phi}(a)
the leading term of
\phi_{a}
. It
is easy to see that

\operatorname{LT}_{\phi}(y)/\operatorname{LT}_{\phi}(x)
is a solution
of quadratic equation
\pi(t)=0
, which leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a Drinfeld module
\phi
over
\mathcal{A}
, we say that

\phi
is a
\zeta
-type (resp.
\zeta^{q}
-type) Drinfeld module
if
\operatorname{LT}_{\phi}(y)/\operatorname{LT}_{\phi}(x)
equals

\zeta
(resp.
\zeta^{q}
).

Now we wish to compute the rank one and rank two Drinfeld modules over

\bar{K}
. We need the function field counterpart of the notion of number-theoretic
sign functions, see
[

Proposition 2.8

Let

(q+1)

Note that

(

\displaystyle\psi_{y}+(\zeta^{-q}-\zeta\psi_{x})=\zeta^{-q}-\frac{T}{u}(\tau-u)=-\frac{T}{u}(\tau-u-\frac{u}{\zeta^{q}T}).

Recalling

(

Now Equation

Then, substituting Equation

One may directly check that

In light of Anderson’s

\tau

For rank one Drinfeld modules, we are able to calculate the associated

Proposition 2.13
.

Let

whose

becomes a root
of
\pi(t)=0
, and

\frac{a}{c}=t-\zeta_{*}^{q},\qquad\frac{b}{c}=\zeta_{*}(t-\zeta_{*}^{q}).

Moreover,

is identical to the form

\psi^{(b/a,a)}
in

.

Proof.

Let
\psi^{u}
be the Hayes module as in
Definition
2.9
. From
Proposition
2.10
, we can assume that
there exists some

, such that

\tilde{\psi}_{f}=\ell^{-1}\psi_{f}^{u}\ell

for any

. Precisely, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{\psi}_{x}=\ell^{q^{2}-1}\tau^{2}-\ell^{q-1}\frac{x+u^{q+1}}{u}\tau+x\\
\tilde{\psi}_{y}=\ell^{q^{2}-1}\zeta_{*}\tau^{2}-\ell^{q-1}\frac{y+\zeta_{*}u^{q+1}}{u}\tau+y,\end{cases}

where
\zeta_{*}
is a root of
\pi
satisfying the equality
(
14
)
. From the motive structure of
\tilde{\psi}
, the relation

\begin{cases}(x\otimes 1)\cdot 1_{\tilde{\psi}}=\ell^{q^{2}-1}\cdot\tau^{2}-\ell^{q-1}\frac{x+u^{q+1}}{u}\cdot\tau+x\cdot 1_{\tilde{\psi}}\\
(y\otimes 1)\cdot 1_{\tilde{\psi}}=\ell^{q^{2}-1}\zeta_{*}\cdot\tau^{2}-\ell^{q-1}\frac{y+\zeta_{*}u^{q+1}}{u}\cdot\tau+y\cdot 1_{\tilde{\psi}}.\end{cases}

Note that we write

Let us introduce the binormial coefficients in function field arithmetic.

For

Let

be the quantities

Here and in the sequel, we denote by

.

Let
L_{0}=1
. For

Thus, combining the last two formulas, we have

\displaystyle L_{j}=

\displaystyle[1][2][3]\cdots[j]T^{(M_{j}^{\prime}-\lceil\frac{j}{2}\rceil)\sigma+N_{j}^{\prime}-\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor-\frac{q^{j+1}-q}{q-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[1][2][3]\cdots[j]T^{(\sigma-1)(q^{j}-1)}.\qed

3.2.
Exponential function and logarithm function

It is convenient to identify
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
with the completion

\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
of the algebraic closure of

\mathbb{F}_{q}(\zeta)((\frac{1}{T}))
with respect to the valuation

v_{\frac{1}{T}}
located at the zero locus of

\frac{1}{T}=t-\zeta^{q}
. Let

f:\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}\to\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
be an

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear function over
\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
. It follows from
[
17
, Corollary 13.2.5]
that such function is given
by the series

f(X)=a_{0}X+a_{1}X^{q}+a_{2}X^{q^{2}}+\cdots.

The first coefficient
a_{0}
is the derivative of
f
. Let

\Lambda
be the lattice of
\Psi
contained in

\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
. The exponential function and the logarithm
function associated to
\Lambda
can be formally defined as follows.

Definition 3.5
.

The exponential function

\exp_{\Lambda}(X):\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}\to\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear function subject to

\begin{cases}\Psi_{x}\cdot\exp_{\Lambda}(X)=\exp_{\Lambda}\cdot(xX)\\
\Psi_{y}\cdot\exp_{\Lambda}(X)=\exp_{\Lambda}\cdot(yX),\end{cases}

whose derivative is identically
1
.

Definition 3.6
.

The logarithm function
\log_{\Lambda}(X)
is the local inverse of the
exponential function, which satisfies

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\log_{\Lambda}(\Psi_{x}(X))=x\log_{\Lambda}(X)\\
\log_{\Lambda}(\Psi_{y}(X))=y\log_{\Lambda}(X).\end{cases}

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\tau
be the
q
-th Frobenius map. Using the binormial coefficients

D_{j},~L_{j}
in
Notations
3.1
and
3.2
, the exponential
and logarithm functions are written as follows:

(21)

\exp_{\Lambda}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{j}}{D_{j}}(X)

and

(22)

\displaystyle\log_{\Lambda}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}X^{q^{j}}}{L_{j}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}\tau^{j}}{L_{j}}(X).

Proof.

Let us assume that

\exp_{\Lambda}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}^{*}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{j}}{D_{j}^{*}}(X)

for some
D^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
and
D_{0}^{*}=1
. We
wish to show that
D_{j}^{*}=D_{j}
for each
j
. From the expression
of
\Psi
and the relation

\Psi_{y}-\zeta\Psi_{x}=\tau+T,

we have

\displaystyle(\Psi_{y}-\zeta\Psi_{x})\cdot\exp_{\Lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=\exp_{\Lambda}(X)^{q}+T\exp_{\Lambda}(X)

(23)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{(D_{j-1}^{*})^{q}}+T\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}^{*}}.

On the other hand, we obtain from
Definition
3.5
that

\displaystyle(\Psi_{y}-\zeta\Psi_{x})\cdot\exp_{\Lambda}(X)

\displaystyle=\exp_{\Lambda}\cdot(yX)-\zeta\exp_{\Lambda}\cdot(xX)

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(y^{q^{j}}-\zeta x^{q^{j}})\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}^{*}}.

Combining Equations
(
23
)
and
(
24
)
that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{(D_{j-1}^{*})^{q}}+T\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}^{*}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(y^{q^{j}}-\zeta x^{q^{j}})\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}^{*}}.

Comparing the coefficients of both sides, we find that
D_{j}^{*}
satisfies
the recursive relation

\frac{1}{(D_{j-1}^{*})^{q}}=\frac{y^{q^{j}}-x^{q^{j}}\zeta-T}{D_{j}^{*}}.

That is

D_{j}^{*}=(D_{j-1}^{*})^{q}(y^{q^{j}}-x^{q^{j}}\zeta-T)=(D_{j-1}^{*})^{q}\frac{t-t^{q^{j}}}{(t-\zeta^{q^{j-1}})^{q^{j}}(t-\zeta^{q})},

which coincides with recursive relation of
D_{j}
. So

D_{j}=D^{*}_{j}
.

In order to compute
\log_{\Lambda}
, we assume that

\displaystyle\log_{\Lambda}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}X^{q^{j}}}{L_{j}^{*}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}\tau^{j}}{L_{j}^{*}}(X),

for some
L^{*}_{j}
and
L_{0}^{*}=1
. It suffices to show that

L_{j}^{*}=L_{j}
for each
j
.

Let us check
L_{1}^{*}
first. Using the expressions of

\Psi_{x}
and
\log_{\Lambda}
, the first formula in Equations
(
20
)
can be rewritten as

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}\tau^{j}}{L_{j}^{*}}\right)\cdot\left(x+\frac{x+T^{\sigma+q}}{T}\tau+T^{\sigma-1}\tau^{2}\right)=x\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}\tau^{j}}{L_{j}^{*}},

i.e.,

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(x^{q^{j}}-x)}{L_{j}^{*}}\tau^{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(x^{q^{j}}+T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j}})}{T^{q^{j}}L_{j}^{*}}\tau^{j+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}T^{(\sigma-1)q^{j}}}{L_{j}^{*}}\tau^{j+2}=0.

Since
L_{0}^{*}=1
, the coefficient of
\tau
in Equation
(
25
)
equals

-\frac{x^{q}-x}{L_{1}^{*}}\tau+\frac{(x+T^{\sigma+q})}{TL_{0}^{*}}\tau=0.

Thus, we have

(26)

\displaystyle L_{1}^{*}=\frac{T(x^{q}-x)}{x+T^{\sigma+q}}=\frac{t-t^{q}}{(t^{q}-\zeta^{q})(t-\zeta^{q})}.

Now we come to compute
L_{j}^{*}
for
j>1
. Equation
(
25
)
can be rewritten as

\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(x^{q^{j}}-x)}{L_{j}^{*}}\tau^{j}-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(x^{q^{j-1}}+T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j-1}})}{T^{q^{j-1}}L_{j-1}^{*}}\tau^{j}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}T^{(\sigma-1)q^{j-2}}}{L_{j-2}^{*}}\tau^{j}=0.

In the same fashion, we apply the expression of
\Psi_{y}
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(y^{q^{j}}-y)}{L_{j}^{*}}\tau^{j}-\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(y^{q^{j-1}}+\zeta^{q^{j-1}}T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j-1}})}{T^{q^{j-1}}L_{j-1}^{*}}\tau^{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}\zeta^{q^{j-2}}T^{(\sigma-1)q^{j-2}}}{L_{j-2}^{*}}\tau^{j}=0.

Combining the
\tau^{j}
-terms of the two equations above, we have

\displaystyle\frac{(y^{q^{j}}-y-\zeta^{q^{j}}(x^{q^{j}}-x))}{L_{j}^{*}}=\frac{(y^{q^{j-1}}+\zeta^{q^{j-1}}T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j-1}})-\zeta^{q^{j}}\cdot(x^{q^{j-1}}+T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j-1}})}{T^{q^{j-1}}L_{j-1}^{*}}.

This formula reduces to

(27)

\displaystyle L_{j}^{*}=L_{j-1}^{*}\frac{t-t^{q^{j}}}{(t-\zeta^{q^{j+1}})(t-\zeta)^{{q^{j}}-q^{j-1}}(t-\zeta^{q})^{q^{j-1}}},

by noticing that

\displaystyle y^{q^{j}}-y-\zeta^{q^{j}}(x^{q^{j}}-x)=\frac{t-t^{q^{j}}}{(t-\zeta^{q})^{q^{j}}(t-\zeta^{q^{j+1}})},

and

\displaystyle\frac{(y^{q^{j-1}}+\zeta^{q^{j-1}}T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j-1}})-\zeta^{q^{j}}\cdot(x^{q^{j-1}}+T^{(\sigma+q)q^{j-1}})}{T^{q^{j-1}}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{t-\zeta}{t-\zeta^{q}}\Big)^{q^{j}-q^{j-1}}.

The proof is completed since the definition of
L_{j}
is exactly composed
of
(
26
)
and
(
27
)
.
∎

3.3.
Exponential function of standard lattice

Notice that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{q}}
is a canonical
rank one
\mathcal{A}
-lattice with generator
1_{\mathcal{A}}
. We wish
to describe the correspondent exponential function in this section.

Definition 3.8
.

Recall
\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{k}\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})
in Equation
(
7
)

\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})
denotes the Riemann-Roch

This definition is valid since the zero locus of

equals

\mathcal{A}

Notation 3.9
.

For
r=0,1,2,\dots
,

\left[\frac{z}{r}\right]
to be the coefficients of the expression:

(28)

\displaystyle\exp_{\Lambda}(z\log_{\Lambda}(X))=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{z}{r}\right]X^{q^{r}}.

Applying

3.7
and equating coefficients of

in both sides of

\left[\frac{z}{r}\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}\frac{z^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}L_{r-j}^{q^{j}}}.

This gives a nice alternative expression of

(29)

\displaystyle\Psi_{a}=\exp_{\Lambda}(a\log_{\Lambda}(X))=\sum_{j=0}^{\deg(a)}\left[\frac{a}{j}\right]\tau^{j}

for each

. Notice that
D_{0}=L_{0}=1
,

D_{1}=L_{1}
, so the first few terms are given as follows:

\displaystyle\left[\frac{z}{0}\right]

\displaystyle=z;

\displaystyle\left[\frac{z}{1}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{L_{1}}+\frac{z^{q}}{D_{1}}=\frac{z^{q}-z}{L_{1}};

\displaystyle\left[\frac{z}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{z}{L_{2}}-\frac{z^{q}}{L_{1}^{q+1}}+\frac{z^{q^{2}}}{D_{2}};

\displaystyle\left[\frac{z}{3}\right]

The Riemann-Roch space
\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})
is spanned by the elements

This implies that each

is of degree

\leqslant 2k
. Applying Equation
(
29
)
, we have

\Psi_{f}=\sum_{j=0}^{2k}\left[\frac{f}{j}\right]\tau^{j},

and
\left[\frac{f}{j}\right]=0
for each

\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})=2k+1=\deg\left(\left[\frac{z}{2k+1}\right]\right).

Therefore, the roots of the polynomial

\mathbb{L}(kP_{\pi})

Proposition 3.10
.

The polynomial

verifies

E_{k}(z)=-L_{2k+1}\cdot\left[\frac{z}{2k+1}\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{2k+1}(-1)^{j}\frac{L_{2k+1}}{D_{j}L_{2k+1-j}^{q^{j}}}z^{q^{j}}.

Therefore,

(30)

E_{\infty}(z)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{2k+1}(-1)^{j}\frac{L_{2k+1}}{D_{j}L_{2k+1-j}^{q^{j}}}z^{q^{j}}.

3.4.
The period lattice of standard Drinfeld modules

Now we are in the position to compute the Carlitz period. Notice that the
zero locus of
\exp_{\Lambda}
is the lattice

\Lambda=\xi_{\mathcal{A}}\cdot\mathcal{A}
, we find that

E_{\infty}(X)=\frac{1}{\xi_{\mathcal{A}}}\exp_{\Lambda}(\xi_{\mathcal{A}}X).

Combining this with the equalities
(
21
)
and
(
30
)
implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\xi_{\mathcal{A}}^{q^{j}}X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}}

\displaystyle=\exp_{\Lambda}(\xi_{\mathcal{A}}X)

\displaystyle=\xi_{\mathcal{A}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{L_{2k+1}}{(-1)^{j}L_{2k+1-j}^{q^{j}}}\frac{X^{q^{j}}}{D_{j}}.

Therefore, we have for each

\xi_{\mathcal{A}}^{q^{j}-1}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{L_{2k+1}}{(-1)^{j}L_{2k+1-j}^{q^{j}}}.

In particular,

is a
(q-1)
-th root of

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{-L_{2k+1}}{L_{2k}^{q}}

Notation 3.11
.

Let us define the partial products

\alpha_{d}:=\prod_{j=2}^{d}\left(1-\frac{[2(j-1)]}{[2j]}\right)

and

\beta_{d}:=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-\frac{[2j-1]}{[2j+1]}\right).

Lemma 3.12
.

Both
\alpha_{d}
and
\beta_{d}
converge when

tends to infinity.
In addition, the following equalities hold:

\alpha_{d}=\frac{[2]^{\frac{q^{2d}-1}{q^{2}-1}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}[2j]}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{d}=\frac{[1][2]^{\frac{q^{2d+1}-q}{q^{2}-1}}}{\prod_{j=0}^{d}[2j+1]}.

Proof.

It follows from definition that

v_{\frac{1}{T}}([k])=-q^{k};

thus we have

v_{\frac{1}{T}}\Big(\frac{[2(j-1)]}{[2j]}\Big)=q^{2j}-q^{2(j-1)}

and

v_{\frac{1}{T}}\Big(\frac{[2j-1]}{[2j+1]}\Big)=q^{2j+1}-q^{2j-1}.

These valuations guarantee the convergences of
\alpha_{d}
and

\beta_{d}
, respectively. Notice that

[2(k+1)]-[2k]=(T^{q^{2}}-T)^{q^{2k}}=[2]^{q^{2k}},

and

[2k+1]-[2k-1]=(T^{q^{2}}-T)^{q^{2k-1}}=[2]^{q^{2k-1}}.

Then

can be rewritten as

\alpha_{d}=\frac{[2]^{q^{2}}}{[4]}\frac{[2]^{q^{4}}}{[6]}\cdots\frac{[2]^{q^{2(d-1)}}}{[2d]}=\frac{[2]^{\frac{q^{2d}-1}{q^{2}-1}}}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}[2j]}.

Similarly,
\beta_{d}
can be rewritten as

\beta_{d}=\frac{[2]^{q}}{[3]}\frac{[2]^{q^{2}}}{[5]}\cdots\frac{[2]^{q^{2d-1}}}{[2d+1]}=\frac{[1][2]^{\frac{q^{2d+1}-q}{q^{2}-1}}}{\prod_{j=0}^{d}[2j+1]}.\qed

Theorem 3.13
.

The Carlitz period

(q-1)

-[2]

Proof.

From

3.4
and

\alpha_{d}\beta_{d}=\frac{[1][2]^{\frac{q^{2d+1}+q^{2d}-q-1}{q^{2}-1}}T^{(q^{2d+1}-1)(\sigma-1)}}{L_{2d+1}},

and

\alpha_{d}\beta_{d-1}=\frac{[1][2]^{\frac{q^{2d}+q^{2d-1}-q-1}{q^{2}-1}}T^{(q^{2d}-1)(\sigma-1)}}{L_{2d}}.

Thus, we have

-\frac{L_{2d+1}}{L_{2d}^{q}}=-[1]^{1-q}[2]\cdot T^{(q-1)(\sigma-1)}\alpha_{d}^{q-1}\beta_{d-1}^{q}\beta_{d}^{-1}.

Taking the limit

Then the lemma follows by taking
(q-1)

5.
Weil pairing

In this section, we would like to represent the Weil pairing of rank

r
Drinfeld modules by a so-called Drinfeld-Moore product. The principle
technique is to derive the Weil operator associated to the ideal
I
of

\mathcal{A}
, which is essentially constructed by the basis and the dual
basis of
\mathcal{A}/I
. An explicit formula for Weil pairing of the rank
two Drinfeld modules is demonstrated by employing the concrete expression
of the rank two Weil operators over
\mathcal{A}
.

5.1.
Dual basis

Now we compute the basis and the dual basis of
\mathcal{A}/I
. Following
the approach in
[
16
]
, we represent the dual basis by means of
torsion differential module.

Let
\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
be the differential module of

\mathcal{A}
. Since the differential of
\rho(x,y)
in Equation
(
8
)
is given by

2ydy-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)(ydx+xdy)+(2\zeta^{q+1}x-1)dx,

it follows that
\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{A}dx\oplus\mathcal{A}dy/\big(2ydy-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)(ydx+xdy)+(2\zeta^{q+1}x-1)dx\big),

as an
\mathcal{A}
-module. Set
\omega_{\pi}:=\frac{dt}{\pi}
. One
may check that
\omega_{\pi}
is the generator of

\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
, namely,

\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}\cong\mathcal{A}\cdot\omega_{\pi}.

Now we fix the ideal
I
of
\mathcal{A}
. Let

I^{-1}\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
be a fractional module in

K\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
and

\Omega_{I}:=I^{-1}\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}/\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}

the correspondent quotient module.

Notation 5.1
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}(\eta)
the residue number of

\eta
at the zero of
t-\zeta
for a differential

\eta\in K\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}=K\cdot dt
.
By abuse of language, we make use of the notation

\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}(\omega):=\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}(1\otimes\omega)
for
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists a perfect pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:~\mathcal{A}/I\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Omega_{I}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Therefore, the dual space of
\mathcal{A}/I
is isomorphic to

\Omega_{I}
.

Proof.

Indeed this result is well-known, so we only sketch a proof. It is easy
to verify that the residue map

\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}:~\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}I^{-1}\Omega_{I}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}(\zeta)

induces a non-degenerate pairing

\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}:~\mathcal{A}/I\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Omega_{I}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}(\zeta).

The composition with the trace map

\mathrm{Tr}:{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\zeta)\to{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
gives a perfect
pairing.
∎

Additionally, we assume that
I
is principle and generated by the element

(82)

P(x,y)=\alpha_{d}+\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\Big(\alpha_{j}x^{d-j}+\beta_{j}yx^{d-j-1}\Big),

where the coefficients

\alpha_{j},~\beta_{j},~\alpha_{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for

j=0,\ldots,d-1
. Then the differential

(83)

\omega_{*}:=\frac{1}{P(t)}\omega_{\pi}

serves as a generator of
I^{-1}\Omega_{\mathcal{A}}
. It is natural
to choose the bases
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
of

\mathcal{A}/I
, respectively, in the following two cases. For

\alpha_{0}\not=0
, we choose

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\left\{1,\,y,\,x,\,yx,\,x^{2},\,\ldots,\,yx^{d-2},\,x^{d-1},\,yx^{d-1}\right\};

and for
\beta_{0}\not=0
, set

\mathcal{B}_{2}=\left\{1,\,y,\,x,\,yx,\,\ldots,\,yx^{d-2},\,x^{d-1},\,x^{d}\right\}.

Notice that the only differences of two bases are exactly the last terms.

Notation 5.3
.

(1)

For determining the dual basis of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
, we introduce
the polynomials
w(e)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]
generated by

\mathcal{B}_{1}
, where the label
e
ranges over

\mathcal{B}_{1}
and splits into the following four types.

Type 1:

e=x^{d-1}y
,

(84)

\displaystyle w(x^{d-1}y)=\frac{(\beta_{0}\zeta+\alpha_{0})(\beta_{0}\zeta^{q}+\alpha_{0})}{\alpha_{0}};

Type 2:

e=x^{d-1-j}y
for
j=1,\ldots,d-1
,

(85)

\displaystyle w(x^{d-1-j}y)=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(\beta_{k}x^{j-k-1}y+\alpha_{k}x^{j-k})+\Big(\alpha_{j}+(\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\beta_{0}}{\alpha_{0}}\zeta^{q+1})\beta_{j}\Big);

Type 3:

e=x^{d-j}
for
j=1,\ldots,d-1
,

\displaystyle w(x^{d-j})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}((x^{j-1-k}y-\mathrm{Tr}\zeta x^{j-k})\alpha_{k}+x^{j-k}\beta_{k-1}-x^{j-k}\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{k})

(86)

\displaystyle+\Big(\beta_{j-1}-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{j}+\frac{\alpha_{j}}{\alpha_{0}}\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{0}\Big);

Type 4:

e=1
,

(87)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w(1)=&\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\biggl(\big((\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\beta_{0}\zeta^{q+1}}{\alpha_{0}})\beta_{k}+\alpha_{k}\big)x^{d-k-1}y+(\frac{\beta_{0}\zeta^{q+1}}{\alpha_{0}}\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k-1}\\
&-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{k})x^{d-k}\biggr)+\left(\beta_{d-1}+\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)\alpha_{d}+2\zeta^{q+1}\frac{\alpha_{d}}{\alpha_{0}}\beta_{0}\right)\\
&+((\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\beta_{0}\zeta^{q+1}}{\alpha_{0}})\beta_{0}+\alpha_{0})x^{d-1}y.\end{aligned}

(2)

Similarly, in order to calculate the dual basis of

\mathcal{B}_{2}
, we list
v(e)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]
for
e
belonging
to the following four types.

Type 1:

e=x^{d}
,

(88)

\displaystyle v(x^{d})=-\frac{(\beta_{0}\zeta+\alpha_{0})(\beta_{0}\zeta^{q}+\alpha_{0})}{\beta_{0}};

Type 2:

e=x^{d-1-j}y
for
j=1,\ldots,d-1
,

(89)

\displaystyle v(x^{d-1-j}y)=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(\beta_{k}yx^{j-k-1}+\alpha_{k}x^{j-k})+\Big(\alpha_{j}-\frac{\alpha_{0}\beta_{j}}{\beta_{0}}\Big);

Type 3:

e=x^{d-j}
for
j=1,\ldots,d-1
,

\displaystyle v(x^{d-j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}((yx^{j-k-1}-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)x^{j-k})\alpha_{k}+\beta_{k-1}x^{j-k}-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{k}x^{j-k})

(90)

\displaystyle+\Big(\beta_{j-1}-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{j}-\big(\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\big)\alpha_{j}\Big);

Type 4:

e=1
,

\displaystyle v(1)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\left((\alpha_{k}-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\beta_{k})x^{d-k-1}y-\Big((\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}})\alpha_{k}-\beta_{k-1}+\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{k}\Big)x^{d-k}\!\right)

(91)

\displaystyle+\Big(-\mathrm{Tr}\zeta\alpha_{d}+\beta_{d-1}-2\frac{\alpha_{0}\alpha_{d}}{\beta_{0}}\Big)-\Big((\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}})\alpha_{0}+\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{0}\Big)x^{d}.

The facts concerning residue numbers below would be very useful.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
G(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be a polynomial. If

\deg(G(t))\leqslant 2j
, then the differential

\frac{G(t)}{\pi^{j}}\omega_{\pi}
is regular except for the place

P_{\pi}
. It follows from the residue theorem that

(92)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}\frac{G(t)}{\pi^{j}}\cdot\omega_{\pi}=0\quad\text{when $\deg(G(t))\leqslant 2j$}.

The differential form
G(t)\pi^{k}\omega_{\pi}
(with

k\geqslant 1
) is regular at
P_{\pi}
, so

(93)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}G(t)\pi^{k}\cdot\omega_{\pi}=0\quad\text{when $k\geqslant 1$}.

Furthermore, it is fairly easy to verify that

(94)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}(a+bt+ct^{2})\omega_{\pi}=b+c\mathrm{Tr}\zeta.

Now we compute the dual bases for the both cases by applying the perfect
pairing in
Lemma
5.2
.

Proposition 5.5
.

As above, we let
\mathcal{B}_{1}
be the basis of
\mathcal{A}/I
when

\alpha_{0}\neq 0
together with polynomials
w(e)
for

e\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
. Then the dual basis of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is represented
by the set

\left\{w(e)\cdot\omega_{*}|e\in\mathcal{B}_{1}\right\}.

Furthermore, for
\beta_{0}\not=0
, there exists an analogous dual
basis for the second basis
\mathcal{B}_{2}
in terms of polynomials

v(e)
with
e\in\mathcal{B}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since the second assertion can be shown in the same way, we only present
the proof for the first assertion. For each

e,\,f\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
, denote the Kronecker delta symbol by

\delta(e,f)=\begin{cases}0,&e\not=f\\
1,&e=f.\end{cases}

The proposition asserts the equality

(95)

\delta(e,f)=\left\langle{f,w(e)\omega_{*}}\right\rangle\quad\text{for $e,\,f\in\mathcal{B}_{1}$}.

We split the procedure for verifying the equality
(
95
)
into
four cases depending on the types listed in
Notation
5.3
.

Case 1:
e=x^{d-1}y
.

First we let
F(t)=P(x,y)\pi^{d}
, i.e.

(96)

\displaystyle F(t)=(\beta_{0}t+\alpha_{0})+(\beta_{1}t+\alpha_{1})\pi+\cdots+(\beta_{d-1}t+\alpha_{d-1})\pi^{d-1}+\alpha_{d}\pi^{d}.

Clearly,
F(t)
is of degree
\leqslant 2d
and coprime with

\pi(t)
. The generator
\omega_{*}
of
\Omega_{I}
in Equation
(
83
)
is now rewritten as

(97)

\displaystyle\omega_{*}=\frac{\pi^{d}}{F(t)}\omega_{\pi}.

From Equation
(
93
)
in
Remark
5.4
, we obtain

(98)

\displaystyle\left\langle{x^{d-1-j}y,\omega_{*}}\right\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}\frac{t\pi^{j}}{F(t)}\omega_{\pi}=0\quad\text{ for $1\leqslant j\leqslant d-1$}.

Applying Equation
(
93
)
again yields

(99)

\displaystyle\left\langle{x^{d-k},\omega_{*}}\right\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}\frac{\pi^{k}}{F(t)}\omega_{\pi}=0\quad\text{for $1\leqslant k\leqslant d$}.

It follows from Equation
(
94
)
that

(100)

\displaystyle\left\langle{x^{d-1}y,\frac{(\beta_{0}\zeta+\alpha_{0})(\beta_{0}\zeta^{q}+\alpha_{0})}{\alpha_{0}}\omega_{*}}\right\rangle=\frac{(\beta_{0}\zeta+\alpha_{0})(\beta_{0}\zeta^{q}+\alpha_{0})}{\alpha_{0}}\mathrm{Tr}\operatorname{Res}_{\zeta}\frac{t}{F(t)}\omega_{\pi}=1.

Therefore, Equations
(
98
)
-
(
100
)
confirm the
equality
(
95
)
for the case
e=x^{d-1}y
.

Case 2:
e=x^{d-1-j}y
, for
j=1\,,\ldots,\,d-1
.

We would like to produce a series of polynomials

\{G_{j}(t)\}_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d-1}
of degree

\deg(G_{j}(t))\leqslant 2j
satisfying the following
\pi
-expansion
condition

(101)

\displaystyle\frac{G_{j}(t)}{F(t)}=1+(t-\mathrm{Tr}{\zeta})g_{j}\pi^{j}+O(\pi^{j+1}),

where
O(\pi^{j+1})
denotes the higher order term and

g_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
needs to be determined.

Substituting expression of
F(t)
(see Equation
(
96
)
) in

tF(t)
yields

\displaystyle tF(t)=

\displaystyle\Big((\beta_{0}\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\alpha_{0})t-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{0}\Big)+\Big((\beta_{1}\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\alpha_{1})t+\beta_{0}-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{1}\Big)\pi+\cdots

(102)

\displaystyle+\Big((\beta_{d-1}\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\alpha_{d-1})t+\beta_{d-2}-\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{d-1}\Big)\pi^{d-1}+(\beta_{d-1}+\alpha_{d}t)\pi^{d}.

It follows from Equations
(

and
(

that

\displaystyle G_{j}(t)=

\displaystyle F(t)+\big(tF(t)-\mathrm{Tr}(\zeta)F(t)\big)g_{j}\pi^{j}+O(\pi^{j+1})

(103)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(\beta_{k}t+\alpha_{k})\pi^{k}+\Big((\beta_{j}+\alpha_{0}g_{j})t+\alpha_{j}-g_{j}\zeta^{q+1}\beta_{0}-g_{j}\mathrm{Tr}\zeta\alpha_{0}\Big)\pi^{j}.

With the assumption
\deg(G_{j}(t))\leqslant 2j
, the coefficient of

in

should be

, i.e.

. Thus, Equations
(

)
and
(

)
show

\frac{G_{j}(t)}{\pi^{j}}\cdot\omega_{*}=\frac{G_{j}(t)}{F(t)}\cdot\pi^{d-j}\omega_{\pi}=\Big(1-\frac{\beta_{j}}{\alpha_{0}}(t-\mathrm{Tr}{\zeta})\pi^{j}\Big)\pi^{d-j}\omega_{\pi}+O(\pi^{d+1})\omega_{\pi}.

(

(

\displaystyle\left\langle{f,\frac{G_{j}(t)}{\pi^{j}}\cdot\omega_{*}}\right\rangle=\delta(f,x^{d-1-j}y),\quad\text{for each}~f\in\mathcal{B}_{1}.

Using Equation
(
103
)
we achieve the equality

\displaystyle\frac{G_{j}(t)}{\pi^{j}}=w(x^{d-1-j}y).

Case 3:
e=x^{d-j}
, for each

.

In this case, we consider the polynomials

\displaystyle\frac{H_{j}(t)}{F(t)}=(t-\mathrm{Tr}{\zeta})+(t-\mathrm{Tr}{\zeta})h_{j}\pi^{j}+O(\pi^{j+1}),

We let

(107)

\displaystyle\frac{I_{d}(t)}{F(t)}

\displaystyle=t+g_{d}+(t-\mathrm{Tr}{\zeta})h_{d}\pi^{d}+O(\pi^{d+1}),

(108)

\displaystyle I_{d}(t)=((\mathrm{Tr}\zeta+\frac{\beta_{0}\zeta^{q+1}}{\alpha_{0}})\beta_{0}+\alpha_{0})t+O(\pi).

5.2.
Representation of Weil pairing

Our goal in this section is devoted to presenting the Weil pairing by means
of the motive structure of Drinfeld modules. Firstly, we need to introduce
the Motive representation of torsion points; for extended details one should
refer to
[
16
]
. We maintain some notations from the previous sections
below. Let
\phi
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
,
I
an ideal of

\mathcal{A}
, and
\phi_{I}
an annihilator of
I
. Recalling

\bar{M}(\phi)
the motive structure of
\phi
, there exists an isomorphism

\ker(\phi_{I})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}/I}((\bar{M}(\phi)/I\bar{M}(\phi))^{\tau},\Omega_{I}).

Through Lang’s Theorem in
[
11
]
, i.e., the

\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\bar{K}\{\tau\}
-isomorphism

(\bar{M}(\phi)/I\bar{M}(\phi))^{\tau}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\bar{K}\cong\bar{M}(\phi)/I\bar{M}(\phi),

it induces a natural isomorphism

(112)

\ker(\phi_{I})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}\otimes\bar{K}\{\tau\}}(\bar{M}(\phi),\Omega_{I}\otimes\bar{K}).

Definition 5.6
.

Given a torsion
\mu\in\ker(\phi_{I})
, the equivalent homomorphism

h_{\mu}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}\otimes\bar{K}\{\tau\}}(\bar{M}(\phi),\Omega_{I}\otimes\bar{K}),

via the isomorphism
(
112
)
is called the motive representation
of
\mu
.

Suppose that
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a basis of
\mathcal{A}/I
along
with the dual basis
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\in\Omega_{I}
. Then

h_{\mu}
can be written as

(113)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(\tau^{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{j}\otimes\tau^{k}(\phi_{x_{j}}(\mu)),

where
\tau^{k}\in\bar{M}(\phi)
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Secondly, we define the notion of Drinfeld-Moore product based on the Moore
determinant.

Definition 5.7
.

The Moore determinant of variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
means the following
determinant

\mathcal{M}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{1}^{q}&\cdots&x_{1}^{q^{r-1}}\\
x_{2}&x_{2}^{q}&\cdots&x_{2}^{q^{r-1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
x_{r}&x_{r}^{q}&\cdots&x_{r}^{q^{r-1}}\end{pmatrix}.

Definition 5.8

(Drinfeld-Moore Product)
.

Let
\phi
be a Drinfeld module of rank
r
over the

\mathcal{A}
-field
L
. Given

\sum_{i}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes a_{2}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{r}^{(i)}\in{\otimes}^{r}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{A}
and

\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{r}\in\bar{L}
, the Drinfeld-Morre
product

{\otimes}^{r}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{A}\times\oplus^{r}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\bar{L})\to\bar{L}

is represented by

(\sum_{i}a_{1}^{(i)}\otimes a_{2}^{(i)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{r}^{(i)})*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{r}):=\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}(\phi_{a_{1}^{(i)}}(\mu_{1}),\phi_{a_{2}^{(i)}}(\mu_{2}),\ldots,\phi_{a_{r}^{(i)}}(\mu_{r})),

where
\mathcal{M}
means the Moore determinant.

It is trivial to check that the Drinfeld-Moore product satisfies the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-multilinear property. That is, for

\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in{\otimes}^{r}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{A}
,

\mu_{1},\mu_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{r},\mu_{r}^{\prime}\in\bar{L}
,

(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})=\mathbf{a}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})+\mathbf{b}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})

and

\mathbf{a}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,(\mu_{i}+\mu_{i}^{\prime}),\ldots,\mu_{r})=\mathbf{a}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{i},\ldots,\mu_{r})+\mathbf{a}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{r}).

Using this notation, the Moore determinant can be written as

\mathbf{1}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
, where

\mathbf{1}
is the unit of

\otimes^{r}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{A}
. For
r=1
, the Drinfeld-Moore
product reduces to the standard action of
\mathcal{A}
on

\bar{L}
, see Equation
(
5
)
. We can produce the Weil pairing
via the Drinfeld-Moore product.

Theorem 5.9
.

Given an ideal
I
of
\mathcal{A}
, there exists a symmetric form

\operatorname{WO}_{I}^{(r)}\in\operatorname{Sym}^{r}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{A}\subseteq{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{r}\mathcal{A},

depending on
I
such that the Weil pairing of any rank
r
Drinfeld
module
\phi
over
\mathcal{A}
-field
L
is represented by

\displaystyle\wedge^{r}\ker(\phi_{I})

\displaystyle\to\bar{L}

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})

\displaystyle\mapsto\operatorname{WO}_{I}^{(r)}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}),

where
\mu_{i}
’s denotes the torsion points of
\phi_{I}
. In particular,
if
\psi=\wedge^{r}\phi
denotes the symmetric product, then

\operatorname{WO}_{I}^{(r)}*_{\phi}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r})
is
annihilated by
\psi_{I}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi_{I}
is an annihilator of
I
. Let us fix some torsion
points
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{r}
and a basis

\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of
\mathcal{A}/I
. Denote by

\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
the dual basis of

\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
, namely,

\langle e_{i},\omega_{j}\rangle=\delta(i,j).

As discussed previously, we let
h_{\mu_{i}}
be the motive representation
of
\mu_{i}
, i.e.

h_{i}(\tau^{k})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{e_{j}}(\mu_{i})^{q^{k}}\omega_{j}.

Denote by
S^{r}
the symmetric group on numbers

0,1,\ldots,r-1
and
\varepsilon(s)
the permutation symbol for

s\in S^{r}
. Assume that

1_{\psi}:=\tau^{0}\wedge\tau^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau^{r-1}

is the basis of
\bar{M}(\psi)
. According to the definition of Drinfeld-Moore
product, for
s=(s_{0},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r-1})\in S^{r}
we have

\displaystyle(h_{1}\wedge h_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge h_{r})(1_{\psi})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in S^{r}}\varepsilon(s)h_{1}(\tau^{s_{0}})\otimes h_{2}(\tau^{s_{2}})\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{r}(\tau^{s_{r-1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\leqslant n}\mathcal{M}(\phi_{e_{j_{1}}}(\mu_{1}),\ldots,\phi_{e_{j_{r}}}(\mu_{r}))\otimes\omega_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega_{j_{r}}

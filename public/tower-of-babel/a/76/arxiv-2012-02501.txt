Stability of supercurrents in a superfluid phase of spin-1 bosons in an optical lattice
Shion Yamashika 1 , Ryosuke Yoshii 2 , and Shunji Tsuchiya 1

Abstract
We study collective modes and superfluidity of spin-1 bosons with antiferromagnetic interactions in an optical lattice based on the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau (TDGL) equation derived from the spin-1 Bose-Hubbard model. Specifically, we examine the stability of supercurrents in the polar phase in the vicinity of the Mott insulating phase with even filling factors. Solving the linearized TDGL equation, we obtain gapless spin-nematic modes and gapful spin-wave modes in the polar phase that arise due to the breaking of S 2 S^{2} symmetry in spin space. Supercurrents exhibit dynamical instabilities induced by growing collective modes. In contrast to the second-order phase transition, the critical momentum for mass currents is finite at the phase boundary of the first-order superfluid-Mott insulator (SF-MI) phase transition. Furthermore, the critical momentum remains finite throughout the metastable SF phase and approaches zero towards the phase boundary, at which the metastable SF state disappears. We also study the stability of spin currents motivated by recent experiments for spinor gases. The critical momentum for spin currents is found to be zero, where a spin-nematic mode causes the dynamical instability. We investigate the origin of the zero critical momentum for spin currents and find it attributed to the fact that the polar state becomes energetically unstable even in the presence of an infinitesimal spin current. We discuss implications of the zero critical momentum for spin currents for the stability of the polar state.



I
Introduction

Ultracold atoms in an optical lattice have opened a versatile research field that lies at the interface of condensed matter physics, statistical physics, and atomic, molecular, and optical physics
Lewenstein et al. 2007
;
Lewenstein et al. 2012
. Spinor bosons in an optical lattice provide with an ideal platform for studying quantum magnetism, quantum phase transitions, and non equilibrium quantum dynamics
García-Ripoll et al. 2004
;
Imambekov et al. 2004
;
Widera et al. 2005
;
Yamashita and Jack 2007
;
Shinozaki et al. 2013
;
Stamper-Kurn and Ueda 2013
;
Fujimoto et al. 2019
. In particular, the superfluid-Mott insulator (SF-MI) phase transition of spin-1 bosons in an optical lattice has attracted much attention. The rich structure of the phase diagram that arises from the interplay between strong correlations and spin degrees of freedom has been a main focus of intense theoretical studies. It exhibits many interesting features including the parity effect of the MI phase
Demler and Zhou 2002
;
Tsuchiya et al. 2004
;
Rizzi et al. 2005
;
Łącki et al. 2011
, the first-order SF-MI transition
Kimura et al. 2005
;
Krutitsky et al. 2005
;
Batrouni et al. 2005
;
Yamamoto et al. 2013
, and the spin-nematic order in the MI phase
Yip 2003
;
Imambekov et al. 2003
;
Zhou and Snoek 2003
;
Snoek and Zhou 2004
;
Rizzi et al. 2005
;
Apaja and Syljuåsen 2006
;
Bernier et al. 2006
;
de Forges de Parny et al. 2013
;
Alavani et al. 2018
;
de Forges de Parny and Rousseau 2018
.
On the other hand, recent progress of experimental studies have made possible to observe the signature of the first-order phase transition
Jiang et al. 2016
and quantum critical dynamics
Austin et al. 2021
in this system.

One of the remarkable features of bosonic superfluids in an optical lattice is dynamical instabilities of supercurrents
Burger et al. 2001
;
Cataliotti et al. 2003
;
Fallani et al. 2004
;
De Sarlo et al. 2005
;
Mun et al. 2007
;
Ferris et al. 2008
;
Wu and Niu 2001
;
Altman and Auerbach 2002
;
Biao and Qian 2003
;
Polkovnikov et al. 2005
;
Altman et al. 2005
.
In fact, this phenomenon is considered to be deeply related with the SF-MI phase transition.
It has been predicted for spineless bosons that the critical superfluid velocity approaches zero at the second-order SF-MI phase transition reflecting the diverging healing length
Altman et al. 2005
;
Polkovnikov et al. 2005
and later it was experimentally confirmed
Mun et al. 2007
.
Given the rich physics associated with the SF-MI phase transition and the recent experimental progress, therefore, it is of particular interest to investigate dynamical instabilities of spin-1 bosons in an optical lattice.
Recently, the critical momentum for mass currents and the stability phase diagram have been calculated numerically using the dynamical Gutzwiller approximation
Asaoka et al. 2016
. The critical momentum in the vicinity of the first-order SF-MI phase transition, however, has not been fully investigated.
Meanwhile, critical dynamics of spin currents has been observed in an antiferromagnetic spin-1 Bose-Einstein condensate
Kim et al. 2017
. In this experiment, spin currents were induced by counter flow of two spin components.
This experiment inspires us to investigate the stability of spin currents in an optical lattice.

In this paper, we study spin-1 bosons with antiferromagnetic interactions in an optical lattice.
Specifically, we examine the stability of supercurrents in the polar phase in the vicinity of the MI phase with even filling factors
based on the time-dependent Ginzburg-Landau (TDGL) equation, which we derive from the spin-1 Bose-Hubbard model.
Our focus is on the superfluidity in the vicinity of the first-order phase transition.
We calculate the critical momentum for mass currents and find that it has a finite value not only
at the first-order SF-MI phase boundary, but throughout the metastable SF phase, in contrast to the second-order case.
We also study the stability of spin currents motivated by the recent experiments for spinor gases
Kim et al. 2017
.
The critical momentum for spin currents is found to be zero. We clarify the origin of the instability of spin currents and briefly discuss about its implications for the stability of the polar phase.

The organization of this paper is as follows: In Sec. II, we introduce the spin-1 Bose-Hubbard model and calculate the metastable phase diagram within the perturbative mean-field theory.
In Sec. III, we study collective modes in the SF phase using the TDGL equation. In Sec. IV, we study the stability of supercurrents and calculate the critical momenta for mass and spin currents. In Sec. V, we investigate the origin of the instability of spin currents and discuss the stability of the polar phase. Finally, we summarize our results in Sec. VI. Details of the calculations in the perturbative mean-field theory are given in Appendix A and B. The derivations of the TDGL equation and the conservation laws are given in Appendix C and D, respectively. We set
\hbar=k_{\rm B}=1
throughout this paper.

II.1
Mott insulating phase

We first study the MI state in the limit of
t=0
, where the Hamiltonian (
1

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\hat{H}^{0}_{i},

(13)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{0}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu\hat{n}_{i}+\frac{U_{0}}{2}\hat{n}_{i}(\hat{n}_{i}-1)+\frac{U_{2}}{2}(\hat{\bm{S}}_{i}^{2}-2\hat{n}_{i}).

(14)

Note that
\hat{\bm{S}}^{2}_{i}
,
\hat{S}_{iz}
and

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{S}}_{i}^{2}|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{i}(S_{i}+1)|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle,

(15)

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{iz}|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{i}|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle,

(16)

\displaystyle\hat{n}_{i}|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{i}|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle.

(17)

|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle
is thus an energy eigenstate of

:

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{0}_{i}|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle=E^{0}(S_{i},n_{i})|S_{i},m_{i},n_{i}\rangle,

(18)

where the energy eigenvalue

is given by

\displaystyle E^{0}(S_{i},n_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mu n_{i}+\frac{U_{0}}{2}n_{i}(n_{i}-1)

(19)

\displaystyle+\frac{U_{2}}{2}[S_{i}(S_{i}+1)-2n_{i}].

S_{i}
takes the minimum value in the ground state due to

Since the orbital part of the wave function in a single lattice site is symmetric under permutation of any two atoms, the spin part has to be also symmetric under permutation of atoms due to Bose statistics. As a result,
S_{i}
is even
(S_{i}=0,2,4,...,n_{i})
for even

, while
S_{i}
is odd
(S_{i}=1,3,5,...,n_{i})
for odd

Wu 1996
.
The ground state of the single-site Hamiltonian (
14
) is thus
|0,0,n_{i}\rangle
for even filling factors, while it is
|1,m_{i},n_{i}\rangle\,(m_{i}=1,0,-1)
for odd filling factors that is triply degenerate. For even filling factors, the ground state
|0,0,n_{i}\rangle
is a spin-singlet insulator, in which all atoms form spin-singlet pairs

as

\displaystyle|0,0,n_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(\frac{n_{i}}{2};0)}}(\hat{\Theta}_{i}^{\dagger})^{\frac{n_{i}}{2}}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle,

(20)

where
\hat{\Theta}^{\dagger}_{i}=(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i0})^{2}-2\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i1}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i-1}
is the creation operator of a spin-singlet pair. The normalization factor is given by
Ho and Yip 2000

\displaystyle f(Q;S)=S!Q!2^{Q}\frac{(2Q+2S+1)!!}{(2S+1)!!}.

(21)

For odd filling factors, there remains a single atom that cannot form a spin-singlet pair in the ground state as

\displaystyle|1,m_{i},n_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(\frac{n_{i}-1}{2};1)}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i\,m_{i}}(\hat{\Theta}^{\dagger}_{i})^{\frac{n_{i}-1}{2}}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle.

(22)

II.2
Perturbative mean-field theory

We employ the perturbative mean-field theory to study the SF-MI phase transition
Tsuchiya et al. 2004
. We introduce the superfluid order parameter
\psi_{\alpha}\equiv\langle\hat{b}_{i\alpha}\rangle\equiv\sqrt{n_{\rm c}}\zeta_{\alpha}
, where
n_{\rm c}=\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1,0}|\psi_{\alpha}|^{2}
is the number of condensate atoms per site and
\zeta_{\alpha}
is the normalized spinor:
\sum_{\alpha=\pm 1,0}\zeta^{*}_{\alpha}\zeta_{\alpha}=1

\displaystyle-t\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle,\alpha}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{i\alpha}\hat{b}_{j\alpha}+\mathrm{h.c.})

\displaystyle\sim-zt\sum_{i,\alpha}[(\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\hat{b}_{i\alpha}+\psi_{\alpha}\hat{b}_{i\alpha}^{\dagger})]+ztN_{\rm s}\sum_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\psi_{\alpha},

(23)

where

the total number of lattice sites. The Hamiltonian (
1

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\hat{H}^{\mathrm{mf}}_{i},

(24)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\mathrm{mf}}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}^{0}+\hat{V}_{i}+zt\sum_{\alpha}\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\psi_{\alpha},

(25)

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-zt\sum_{\alpha}(\psi_{\alpha}\hat{b}_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}+\psi_{\alpha}^{*}\hat{b}_{i\alpha}).

(26)

Here,
\hat{V}_{i}

i

20

The TDGL method is valid in the vicinity of the MI phase, where the order parameter is so small that the perturbative mean-field expansion is allowed. It describes low energy superfluid dynamics, in which the order parameter varies slowly in space and time.

To check the validity of the TDGL equation, we also evaluate the excitation spectrum of collective modes numerically using the time-dependent Gutzwiller ansatz

The validity of the time-dependent Gutzwiller ansatz depends crucially on the number of Fock states involved in the expansion.
This restriction to the number of Fock states as well as the approximation for the many-body wave function as a product of single-site wave functions limits the validity of the time-dependent Gutzwiller ansatz within the low energy regime near the phase boundary, because large numbers of Fock states are required to describe the SF phase in highly excited states and/or away from the phase boundary.
The collective modes calculated by the TDGL equation and the time-dependent Gutzwiller ansatz, therefore, should agree in the low energy regime for small momenta in the vicinity of the MI phase.

Our results do not exclude the possibility of stable spin currents in general.
We expect that spin currents can be stabilized by the quadratic Zeeman effect.
In fact, stable spin currents have been observed in a spin-1 antiferromagnetic Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) of

In the above argument, we have revealed that the instability of spin currents originates from the fact that the energy functional takes its maximum for a superfluid state carrying a spin current (see Fig.

However, it is beyond the scope of the paper to confirm this possibility using the TDGL equation, because it is necessary to derive the TDGL equation taking into account the quadratic Zeeman effect from scratch. Note that the insulating state in the presence of the quadratic Zeeman effect is different from the spin-singlet state in Eq. (

We examine the possibility of stabilizing spin currents by the quadratic Zeeman effect using the discrete Gross-Pitaevskii (DGP) equation, which is valid in the weakly-interacting regime deep in the superfluid phase.

VI
conclusion

To summarize, we have studied the stability of supercurrents in the polar phase of antiferromagnetically interacting spin-1 bosons in an optical lattice using the TDGL equation.
We have calculated the critical momenta for supercurrents in the vicinity of the MI phase with even filling factors.
We found that the critical momentum for mass currents is finite throughout the metastable SF phase,
which demonstrates the superfluidity of the metastable SF state.
The critical momentum for spin currents was found to be zero.
We have found that this instability of spin currents originates from the fact that
the polar state with a spin current corresponds to an energy saddle point,
thereby an infinitesimal spin current causes a dynamical instability.

Although we set
U_{2}/U_{0}
optimal values for validity of the theory that is not realistic in the present experimental status, the obtained results may be applicable to
{}^{23}{\rm Na}
with
U_{2}/U_{0}=0.04
. The first-order phase transition dominates the larger part of the phase boundary and the region of the metastable SF phase in the phase diagram gets larger for smaller
U_{2}/U_{0}

Kimura et al. 2005
;
Yamamoto et al. 2013
. The effect of the first-order phase transition, therefore, may be remarkable and easy to study experimentally for
{}^{23}{\rm Na}
.

Our predictions for the stability of supercurrents can be verified experimentally with current technologies. As for mass currents, we propose a similar setup with Ref.
Mun et al. 2007
, where a mass current is induced by a moving optical lattice. Spin currents can be induced by applying a linear magnetic field gradient that induces counter flow of two spin components as in the experiment for spinor gases
Kim et al. 2017
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\alpha}\left[K\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}^{*}}{zt}\frac{\partial_{\tau}\phi_{i\alpha}}{zt}+J\left|\frac{\partial_{\tau}\phi_{i\alpha}^{*}}{zt}\right|^{2}+(C_{2}-zt)\left|\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}}{zt}\right|^{2}+C_{4}\left|\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}}{zt}\right|^{4}+C_{6}\left|\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}}{zt}\right|^{6}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\sum_{i}\left[C_{4}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}^{*}}{zt}\bm{F}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\phi_{i\beta}}{zt}\right)^{2}+C_{6}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}^{*}}{zt}\bm{F}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\phi_{i\beta}}{zt}\right)^{2}\sum_{\alpha}\left|\frac{\phi_{i\alpha}}{zt}\right|^{2}\right]\right\},

where
K
and
J
are given by

\displaystyle K=\frac{(zt)^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{n+3}{\Delta E_{1,n+1}^{2}}-\frac{n}{\Delta E_{1,n-1}^{2}}\right],

(296)

\displaystyle J=\frac{(zt)^{2}}{3}\left[\frac{n+3}{\Delta E_{1,n+1}^{3}}+\frac{n}{\Delta E_{1,n-1}^{3}}\right].

(297)

By the Fourier transform, the second term in Eq. (
295
) can be written as

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}\tau\sum_{i,j,\alpha}(\hat{t}^{-1})_{ij}\phi_{i\alpha}^{*}\phi_{j\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\bm{k},\omega,\alpha}\frac{|\bar{\phi}_{\alpha}(\bm{k},\omega)|^{2}}{\epsilon_{\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k},\omega,\alpha}\left|\frac{\bar{\phi}_{\alpha}(\bm{k},\omega)}{zt}\right|^{2}[zt+(ka)^{2}],

where

\displaystyle\phi_{i\alpha}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k},\omega}\bar{\phi}_{\alpha}(\bm{k},\omega)e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}_{i}-\omega\tau)},

(299)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2t\sum_{l=1}^{d}\cos(k_{l}a).

(300)

Here,
\bm{r}_{i}\equiv(x_{i}a,y_{i}a,z_{i}a)^{T}
and
a
is the lattice constant.
We have taken the long-wavelength limit, i.e.,
ka\ll 1
in Eq. (
).
In the continuum limit that is effective in the vicinity of the phase boundary, making replacements
\phi_{i\alpha}(\tau)/(a^{d/2}zt)\rightarrow\Psi_{\alpha}(\tau,\bm{r})
,
S^{\rm eff}
reduces to the Ginzburg-Landau (GL) action
S^{\mathrm{GL}}
:

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{GL}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}\tau\int{\rm d}^{d}x\left[E^{0}(0,n)+K{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi}+J(\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})(\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi})+\frac{1}{2m^{*}}(\nabla{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})\cdot(\nabla{\bf\Psi})\right.

(301)

\displaystyle\left.+C_{2}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})+c_{4}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})^{2}+c_{6}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})^{3}+c_{4}^{\prime}\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle^{2}+c_{6}^{\prime}\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle^{2}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})\right].

Here,

c_{4}=a^{d}C_{4},\ c_{4}^{\prime}=a^{d}C_{4}^{\prime},c_{6}=a^{2d}C_{6},\ c_{6}^{\prime}=a^{2d}C_{6}^{\prime},\ {\bf\Psi}=(\Psi_{1},\Psi_{0},\Psi_{-1})^{T}\ {\rm is\ the\ SF\ order\ parameter},\ \langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\Psi_{\alpha}^{*}\bm{F}_{\alpha\beta}\Psi_{\beta}\ {\rm is\ the\ spin\ average},

and
m^{*}=1/(2ta^{2})
is the effective mass.
Setting
\partial_{\tau}\Psi_{\alpha}=0
in Eq. (
301
), the energy functional for a static solution
{\bf\Psi}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal K}(\{{\bf\Psi}\})=\int{\rm d}^{d}x

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{2m^{*}}(\nabla{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})\cdot(\nabla{\bf\Psi})+C_{2}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})+c_{4}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})^{2}+c_{6}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})^{3}+c_{4}^{\prime}\langle\!\langle{\bm{F}}\rangle\!\rangle^{2}+c_{6}^{\prime}\langle\!\langle{\bm{F}}\rangle\!\rangle^{2}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})\right].

From
\frac{\delta S^{\rm GL}}{\delta\Psi_{\alpha}^{*}}=0
, we finally obtain the TDGL equation

\displaystyle iK\partial_{t}\Psi_{\alpha}-J\partial_{t}^{2}\Psi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m^{*}}\Psi_{\alpha}+C_{2}\Psi_{\alpha}+2c_{4}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})\Psi_{\alpha}+3c_{6}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})^{2}\Psi_{\alpha}+c_{6}^{\prime}\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle^{2}\Psi_{\alpha}

(303)

\displaystyle+2c_{4}^{\prime}\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle\cdot\sum_{\beta}(\bm{F}_{\alpha\beta}\Psi_{\beta})+2c_{6}^{\prime}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle\cdot\sum_{\beta}(\bm{F}_{\alpha\beta}\Psi_{\beta}).

Note that Eq. (
303
) is valid for low energy dynamics, in which slow temporal variation of
{\bf\Psi}
is allowed. If
K\neq 0
, the first-order time-derivative term is dominant and the second-order one should be neglected.

Appendix D
CONSERVATION LAWS

In this Appendix, we derive the conservation laws associated with the
{\rm U}(1)
gauge and spin rotational symmetries. We also derive the expressions for mass current (
116
) and spin current (
117
) from the GL action (
301
).

We first study the conservation law associated with the
{\rm U}(1)
gauge symmetry. The following argument is based on Refs.
Sachdev 2011
;
Polkovnikov et al. 2005
. Under an infinitesimal
{\rm U}(1)
gauge transformation
\Psi_{\alpha}\rightarrow e^{i\delta\theta(\bm{r})}\Psi_{\alpha}\sim\Psi_{\alpha}+i\delta\theta(\bm{r})\Psi_{\alpha}
, the variation of the GL action (
301
) is given as

\displaystyle\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\delta\theta\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}\tau\int{\rm d}^{d}x\left\{K\partial_{\tau}({\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi})\right.

(304)

\displaystyle+J\partial_{\tau}[(\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}){\bf\Psi}-{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi}]

\displaystyle+\left.\frac{1}{2m^{*}}\nabla\cdot[(\nabla{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}){\bf\Psi}-{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}\nabla{\bf\Psi}]\right\}.

From
\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}=0
for arbitrary
\delta\theta
, we obtain the continuity equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(K\rho+Jq)+\nabla\cdot\bm{j}_{\rm m}=0,

(305)

where
\rho
and
\bm{j}_{\rm m}
denote the superfluid density and superfluid mass current, respectively, defined as

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}{\bf\Psi},

(306)

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\left[(\partial_{t}{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}){\bf\Psi}-{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\partial_{t}{\bf\Psi})\right],

(307)

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{\rm m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2im^{*}}\left[{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}\nabla{\bf\Psi}-(\nabla{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}){\bf\Psi}\right].

(308)

We clarify the physical meaning of
q
below.
If
K\neq 0
, neglecting
q
, Eq. (
305
) reduces to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\frac{1}{K}\nabla\cdot\bm{j}_{\rm m}=0.

(309)

Equation (
309
) represents the conservation of superfluid density.

If
K=0
, keeping
q
, Eq. (
309
) reduces to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q+\frac{1}{J}\nabla\cdot\bm{j}_{\rm m}=0.

(310)

Before discussing the interpretation of
q
, we show that
K=0
holds for a commensurate filling in the SF phase.
We first note that Eq. (
284
) is invariant under the following local gauge transformation

\displaystyle\begin{split}b_{i\alpha}&\rightarrow e^{i\eta(\tau)}b_{i\alpha},\\
\phi_{i\alpha}&\rightarrow e^{i\eta(\tau)}\phi_{i\alpha},\\
\mu&\rightarrow\mu+i\partial_{\tau}\eta(\tau).\end{split}

(311)

The GL action (
301
) should be invariant under the corresponding transformation
\Psi_{\alpha}\rightarrow e^{i\eta(\tau)}\Psi_{\alpha}
, which requires the condition

\displaystyle K=-\frac{\partial C_{2}}{\partial\mu}.

(312)

We can directly confirm Eq. (
312
) from Eqs. (
274
) and (
296
). On the other hand, in the vicinity of the phase boundary, the ground-state energy can be expanded by the superfluid density
n_{\rm c}
as

\displaystyle E=E^{0}(0,n)+C_{2}n_{\rm c}+O(n_{\rm c}^{2}).

(313)

The expectation value of the filling number is given by

\displaystyle\langle\hat{n}\rangle=-\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mu}\sim n-\frac{\partial C_{2}}{\partial\mu}n_{\rm c}.

(314)

In the MI phase,
\langle\hat{n}\rangle
is an integer because
n_{\rm c}=0
as expected.
Meanwhile, since
n_{\rm c}\neq 0
,
\langle\hat{n}\rangle
takes an integer value only if
-\partial C_{2}/\partial\mu=0
in the SF phase. Thus,
K=0
holds for a commensurate filling in the SF phase. Furthermore, when
K=0
, the TDGL equation (
303
) is invariant under the transformation
\Psi_{\alpha}\leftrightarrow\Psi_{\alpha}^{*}
, i.e., particle-hole symmetric

Polkovnikov et al. 2005
;
Fisher et al. 1989
;
Nakayama et al. 2015
.

Equation (
311
) implies that the transformation
b_{i\alpha}\rightarrow e^{i\eta}b_{i\alpha}
and
\phi_{i\alpha}\rightarrow e^{i\eta}\phi_{i\alpha}
is equivalent to the shift of
\mu
:
\mu\rightarrow\mu-i\partial_{\tau}\eta

Fisher et al. 1989
.
The infinitesimal shift of the chemical potential
\mu\rightarrow\mu+i\partial\delta\eta
is, therefore, equivalent to the transformation of
\Psi_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\Psi_{\alpha}\rightarrow e^{-i\delta\eta(\tau)}\Psi_{\alpha}\sim\Psi_{\alpha}-i\delta\eta(\tau)\Psi_{\alpha}.

(315)

The variation of
S^{\rm GL}
under Eq. (
315
) is given by

\displaystyle\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}=-\delta\mu\int{\rm d}^{d}x(K\rho+Jq),

(316)

where
\delta\mu=i\partial_{\tau}\eta(\tau)
. Comparing Eq. (
316
) with
\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}=(\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}/\delta\mu(\tau))\delta\mu(\tau)
, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\delta\mu(\tau)}=\int{\rm d}^{d}x(K\rho+Jq).

(317)

The left-hand side of Eq. (
317
) represents the deviation of particle number from the static value.
For a commensurate filling, setting
K=0
, Eq. (
317
) reduces to

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\delta\mu(\tau)}=\int{\rm d}^{d}xJq.

(318)

We thus find that
q
means the deviation of particle density from a commensurate filling.

We next derive the conservation law associated with the spin rotational symmetry. We introduce an infinitesimal spin rotation about a unit vector
\bm{n}
:

\displaystyle{\bf\Psi}\rightarrow e^{-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\delta\lambda}{\bf\Psi}\sim{\bf\Psi}-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\delta\lambda{\bf\Psi}.

(319)

The variation of the GL action (
301
) under Eq. (
319
) is given by

\displaystyle\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\delta\lambda\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm d}\tau\int{\rm d}^{d}x\left\{K\partial_{\tau}\left[{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F}){\bf\Psi}\right]\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle J\partial_{\tau}\left[(\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F}){\bf\Psi}-{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\partial_{\tau}{\bf\Psi}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{2m^{*}}\nabla\cdot\left[(\nabla{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F}){\bf\Psi}-{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\nabla{\bf\Psi}\right]\right\}.

\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}=0
for arbitrary
\delta\lambda
yields the continuity equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(K\rho_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}+Jq_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}})+\nabla\cdot\bm{j}_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}=0,

(321)

where
\rho_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}
denotes the magnetization density for the component along
\bm{n}
and
\bm{j}_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}
the spin current for
\rho_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}
. They are defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F}){\bf\Psi},

(322)

\displaystyle q_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\left[(\partial_{t}{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F}){\bf\Psi}-{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\partial_{t}{\bf\Psi})\right],

(323)

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2im^{*}}\left[{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\nabla{\bf\Psi}-(\nabla{\bf\Psi}^{\dagger})(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F}){\bf\Psi}\right].

We clarify the physical meaning of
q^{\bm{n}}_{\rm s}
below.
If
K\neq 0
, neglecting
q^{\bm{n}}_{\rm s}
, Eq. (
321
) represents the conservation of magnetization for the component along
\bm{n}
.

Analogous to Eq. (
311
), we consider the following trans formation under a spin rotation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\vec{b}_{i}&\rightarrow e^{-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\nu(\tau)}\vec{b}_{i},\\
\vec{\phi}_{i}&\rightarrow e^{-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\nu(\tau)}\vec{\phi}_{i},\end{split}

(325)

where
\vec{b}_{i}=(b_{i1},b_{i0},b_{i-1})^{T}
and
\vec{\phi}_{i}=(\phi_{i1},\phi_{i0},\phi_{i-1})^{T}
.
Under the transformation (
325
), Eq. (
284
) is transformed as

\displaystyle S\rightarrow S-\int^{\beta}_{0}{\rm d}\tau\bm{H}(\tau)\cdot\int{\rm d}^{d}x\langle\bm{F}\rangle_{b}.

(326)

Here,
\bm{H}=i\partial_{\tau}\nu\bm{n}
and
\langle\bm{F}\rangle_{b}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}b_{i\alpha}^{*}(e^{i\bm{F}\cdot\bm{n}\nu}\bm{F}e^{-i\bm{F}\cdot\bm{n}\nu})_{\alpha\beta}b_{i\beta}
.
Equation (
326
) shows that the transformation (
325
) is equivalent to application of magnetic field
\bm{H}
in the direction of
\bm{n}
.

The transformation of
{\bf\Psi}
that corresponds to Eq. (
325
) is
{\bf\Psi}\rightarrow e^{-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\nu(\tau)}{\bf\Psi}
.
Under an infinitesimal spin rotation
{\bf\Psi}\rightarrow e^{-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\delta\nu}{\bf\Psi}\sim{\bf\Psi}-i(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{F})\delta\nu{\bf\Psi}
, the variation of Eq. (
301
) is given by

\displaystyle\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}=-\delta\bm{H}(\tau)\cdot\int{\rm d}^{d}x(K\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle+J\bm{q}_{\rm s}),

(327)

where
\delta\bm{H}(\tau)=i\delta\partial_{\tau}\nu(\tau)\bm{n}
. Comparing Eq. (
327
) and

\displaystyle\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}=\frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\delta\bm{H}}\cdot\delta\bm{H},

(328)

we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\delta\bm{H}}=\int{\rm d}^{d}x(K\langle\!\langle\bm{F}\rangle\!\rangle+J\bm{q}_{\rm s}),

(329)

where
\bm{n}\cdot\bm{q}_{\rm s}=q_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}
.
The left-hand side of Eq. (
329
) represents the magnetization along
\bm{H}
. In the case of a commensurate filling, i.e.,
K=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{GL}}}{\delta\bm{H}}=\int{\rm d}^{d}xJ\bm{q}_{\rm s}.

(330)

We thus find that the
q_{\rm s}^{\bm{n}}
represents the deviation of magnetization density from the value for a commensurate filling.

1.
Introduction and Results

Let
G
be a (Hausdorff) compact group. Then
G
has a unique normalized Haar measure denoted by
{\mathbf{m}}_{G}
. The set
{\mathcal{A}}(G):=\{(x,y)\in G\times G\;|\;xy=yx\}
is a measurable set (actually a closed set) of
G\times G
and its measure in
G\times G
will be denoted by
{\rm cp}(G)
. In the case
G
is finite,
{\rm cp}(G)
has been extensively studied for example see
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
and references therein. It is famous (see e.g.
[
2
]
) that if
G
is finite,
{\rm cp}(G)\leq\frac{5}{8}
whenever
G
is non-abelian. The latter is generalized for any non-abelian compact group in
[
2
]
. The rationality of
{\rm cp}(G)
is clear in the case of finite
G
and for compact groups it is proved in
[
5
, Theorem 3.11]
. It is also shown in
[
5
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
that if
{\rm cp}(G)>0
, then
Z(FC(G))
is open in
G
, where
Z(FC(G))
is the center of the FC-center
FC(G)
of
G
, i.e.
FC(G)
consists of elements of
G
whose conjugacy classes are finite. In the latter case the derived subgroup of
FC(G)
is also finite. We will use these results in the sequel.

Here we prove a formula relating the commuting probability of compact groups to one of finite groups.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any compact group
G
there exists a finite group
H
such that
{\rm cp}(G)=\frac{{\rm cp}(H)}{|G:FC(G)|^{2}}
. In particular, if
{\rm cp}(G)>0
, the finite group
H
can be chosen so that it is isoclinic to
FC(G)
and
{\rm cp}(FC(G))={\rm cp}(H)
.

Here we are using the usual convention that if
|G:FC(G)|
is infinite, then
\frac{1}{|G:FC(G)|}=0
.
Recall that, following
[
4
]
, two groups
H
and
K
are called isoclinic if there exist group isomorphisms
\alpha:H/Z(H)\rightarrow K/Z(K)
and
\beta:H^{\prime}\rightarrow K^{\prime}
such that for all
x,y\in H
and all
x^{\prime}\in(xZ(H))^{\alpha}
and
y^{\prime}\in(yZ(H))^{\alpha}
,
[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]=[x,y]^{\beta}
, where for a group
T
,
Z(T)
and
T^{\prime}
are denoting the center and the derived subgroups of
T
, respectively. The alternating group of degree
5
is denoted by
A_{5}
as usual.
In view of Theorem
1.1
it is now possible to generalize some of results in finite case for commuting probability to compact groups. For example we give the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a compact group. Then

(1)
if
{\rm cp}(G)>\frac{1}{4}
then both
G^{\prime}
and
G/Z(G)
are finite.

(2)
if
{\rm cp}(G)>\frac{3}{40}
then either
G
is solvable or, else
G\cong A_{5}\times T
for some abelian group
T
, in which case
{\rm cp}(G)=\frac{1}{12}
.

The second part of Theorem
1.1
has already been proved in
[
1
, Theorem 12]
for finite groups and we will use it in our proof for the compact case.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
n=|G:Z|
and
m=|F:Z|
, where
F
is the FC-center of
G
and
Z
is its center. By
[
5
, Theorem 1.2]
we may assume that
{\rm cp}(G)>0
so that
n
and
m
are both finite. Suppose that
F=\cup_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}Z
and
G=\cup_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}Z
, where
x_{i}\in F
and
y_{j}\in G
.
Let
c_{\ell,k}=1
if
x_{\ell}x_{k}=x_{k}x_{\ell}
and
c_{\ell,k}=0
otherwise.

We use the following in the sequel.

(1)   If
y\in G\setminus F
, then
{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(C_{G}(y))=0
, where
C_{G}(y)=\{a\in G\;|\;ay=ya\}
.

(2)   By (1) if
y_{j}\in G\setminus F
,
{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(y_{j}))=0
. If
y_{j}\in F
, we may assume without loss of generality that
y_{j}=x_{\ell}
for some
\ell
.

(3)
x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(x_{\ell})=\begin{cases}x_{i}Z&{\rm if}\;c_{\ell,i}=1\\
\varnothing&{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}
.

(4) For any
x\in F

C_{G}(x)\cap y_{j}Z=\begin{cases}y_{j}Z&{\rm if}\;x\in C_{G}(y_{j})\\
\varnothing&{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}
.

Let
{\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathcal{A}}(G)}
be the characteristic function of
{\mathcal{A}}(G)=\{(x,y)\in G\times G\;|\;xy=yx\}
.

The following two first equlities are from
[
2
]
.

\displaystyle{\rm cp}(G)

\displaystyle=\int_{G\times G}{\mathbf{1}}_{{\mathcal{A}}(G)}(x,y)d(x,y)=\int_{G}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(C_{G}(x))dx\;\;\;{\rm by\;Fubini\;theorem}

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}\int_{F}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(C_{G}(x))dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{x_{i}Z}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(C_{G}(x))dx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{x_{i}Z}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(C_{G}(x)\cap y_{j}Z)dx

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(y_{j})}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(y_{j}Z)dx=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(y_{j})}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)dx

\displaystyle={\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(y_{j})}{\mathbf{1}}_{x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(y_{j})}(x)dx={\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(y_{j}))

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(x_{i}Z\cap C_{G}(x_{j}))\overset{(3)}{=}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)c_{i,j}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{m}}_{G}(Z)^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}c_{i,j}=\frac{1}{|G:Z|^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}c_{i,j}

The latter, in particular, shows the rationality of
{\rm cp}(G)
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\rm cp}(F)

\displaystyle={\mathbf{m}}_{F\times F}\big(\bigcup_{i,j=1}^{m}\{x_{i}Z\times x_{j}Z\;|\;x_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}x_{i}\}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{m}}_{F\times F}(x_{i}Z\times x_{j}Z)c_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}{\mathbf{m}}_{F}(Z)^{2}c_{i,j}={\mathbf{m}}_{F}(Z)^{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}c_{i,j}

It follows that

(*)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}c_{i,j}=\frac{{\rm cp}(F)}{{\mathbf{m}}_{F}(Z)^{2}}

Thus we also find the following relation.

{\rm cp}(G)=\frac{|F:Z|^{2}}{|G:Z|^{2}}{\rm cp}(F)=\frac{{\rm cp}(F)}{|G:F|^{2}}.

By
[
4
, p. 135, paragraph 4]
, there exists a group
H
such that
Z(H)\leq H^{\prime}
and
H
is isoclinic to
F
. Note that since
F/Z(F)
and
F^{\prime}
are finite,
H
is a finite group. If
d_{i,j}
are defined similar to
c_{i,j}
for
H
, then the isoclinism of
H
and
F
implies that
\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}c_{i,j}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}d_{i,j}
. Note that the same relation as (*) holds for
H
as well, thus

\frac{{\rm cp}(F)}{{\mathbf{m}}_{F}(Z)^{2}}=\frac{{\rm cp}(H)}{{\mathbf{m}}_{H}(Z(H))^{2}}.

Since
{\mathbf{m}}_{F}(Z)=|F:Z|
and
{\mathbf{m}}_{H}(Z(H))=|H:Z(H)|
, the isoclinism again implies that

{\rm cp}(F)={\rm cp}(H)
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

By Theorem
1.1
there exists a finite group
H
isoclinic to
F:=FC(G)
such that
{\rm cp}(G)=\frac{{\rm cp}(H)}{|G:F|^{2}}
.

(1)   Suppose that
{\rm cp}(G)>\frac{1}{4}
. If
G\neq F
, then
\frac{1}{|G:F|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}
and so
{\rm cp}(H)>1
which is impossible. Thus
G=F
. Now
[
5
, Theorem 3.11]
follows that both
G^{\prime}
and
G/Z(G)
are finite.

(2)   Suppose that
{\rm cp}(G)>\frac{3}{40}
. It follows that
{\rm cp}(H)>\frac{3}{40}
and
|G:F|\in\{1,2,3\}
so that
G^{\prime}\leq F
. Now

[
1
, Theorem 12]
implies that
H
is solvable or
H\cong A_{5}\times T
for some abelian group
T
. If
H
is solvable, by isoclinism,
H^{\prime}\cong F^{\prime}
and so
F
is solvable and so is
G
, since
G/F
is cyclic. Now assume that
H\cong A_{5}\times T
for some abelian group
T
so that
{\rm cp}(H)=\frac{1}{12}
. Now (1) implies that
G=F
. By isoclinism,
G/Z(G)\cong A_{5}
and
G^{\prime}\cong A_{5}
. Therefore
G=G^{\prime}Z(G)
and so
G\cong A_{5}\times Z(G)
.

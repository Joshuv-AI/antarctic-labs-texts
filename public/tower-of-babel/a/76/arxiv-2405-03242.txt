Global existence and scattering of small data smooth solutions to a class of quasilinear wave systems on ℝ 2 × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
Fei Hou, Fei Tao, Huicheng Yin [12pt] 1. Department of Mathematics, Nanjing University, Nanjing, 210093, China2. School of Science, Nanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications,Nanjing, 210023, China3. School of Mathematical Sciences and Mathematical Institute,Nanjing Normal University, Nanjing, 210023, China

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with the global existence and scattering of small data smooth solutions to a class of quasilinear wave systems on the product space ℝ 2 × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T} . These quasilinear wave systems include 3D irrotational potential flow equation of Chaplygin gases, 3D relativistic membrane equation, some 3D quasilinear wave equations which come from the corresponding Lagrangian functionals as perturbations of the Lagrangian densities of linear waves, and nonlinear wave maps system. Through looking for some suitable transformations of unknown functions, the nonlinear wave system can be reduced into a more tractable form. Subsequently, by applying the vector-field method together with the ghost weight technique as well as deriving some kinds of weighted L ∞ − L ∞ L^{\infty}-L^{\infty} and L ∞ − L 2 L^{\infty}-L^{2} estimates of solution w w to the 2D linear wave equation □ ​ w = f ⁡ ( t , x ) \Box w=f(t,x) , the global existence and scattering of small data solutions are established. Keywords. Global existence, scattering, quasilinear wave system, L ∞ − L ∞ L^{\infty}-L^{\infty} estimate, L ∞ − L 2 L^{\infty}-L^{2} estimate, ghost weight 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification. 35L05, 35L15, 35L70

Background and main results

In this paper, we study the Cauchy problem of the following 3D quasilinear wave system on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\times\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Box u^{i}=F^{i}(u,\partial u,\partial^{2}u):=\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\sum_{|a|+|b|\leq 1}C^{ab}_{ijk}Q_{0}(\partial^{a}u^{j},\partial^{b}u^{k})+\sum_{j,k,l=1}^{m}C_{ijkl}Q_{0}(u^{j},u^{k})u^{l}\\
&\qquad\quad+\sum_{j,k,l=1}^{m}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{3}Q_{ijkl}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}u^{j}\partial_{\mu}u^{k}\partial_{\nu}u^{l},\qquad i=1,\cdots,m,\\
&(u,\partial_{t}u)(0,x,y)=(u_{(0)},u_{(1)})(x,y),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u=(u^{1},...,u^{m})
,
(t,x,y)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\times\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
\displaystyle\Box:=\Box_{t,x,y}=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{y}^{2}
,
\Delta_{x}=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
,
\partial=(\partial_{0},\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3})=(\partial_{t},\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}},\partial_{y})
,
\displaystyle Q_{0}(f,g)=\partial_{t}f\partial_{t}g-\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}f\partial_{j}g
,
\partial^{a}=\partial_{0}^{a_{1}}\partial_{1}^{a_{2}}\partial_{2}^{a_{3}}\partial_{y}^{a_{4}}
with multi-index
a\in\mathop{\mathbb{N}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits_{0}^{4}
,
and
u_{(r)}=(u_{(r)}^{1},...,u_{(r)}^{m})
for
r=0,1
.
In addition,
(u_{(0)},u_{(1)})
is
2\pi
-periodic in
y
, namely,

(u_{(0)},u_{(1)})(x,y+2\pi)=(u_{(0)},u_{(1)})(x,y)
. Note that
the first term on the right hand side of the first line in (
1.1
) also admits such a form

\sum_{|a|+|b|\leq 1}C^{ab}_{ijk}Q_{0}(\partial^{a}u^{j},\partial^{b}u^{k})=\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}Q_{ijk}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}u^{j}\partial_{\beta}u^{k}+\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{3}Q_{ijk}^{\alpha\beta\mu}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}u^{j}\partial_{\mu}u^{k}.

(1.2)

In addition, the following symmetric conditions are imposed for
i,j,k,l=1,\cdots,m
,

Q_{ijkl}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=Q_{ijkl}^{\beta\alpha\mu\nu}=Q_{jikl}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu},\qquad Q_{ijk}^{\alpha\beta\mu}=Q_{ijk}^{\beta\alpha\mu}=Q_{jik}^{\alpha\beta\mu}.

(1.3)

In order to obtain the global existence of small data solution
u
of (
1.1
),
as in
[
14
]
, the related partial null condition is required for the cubic nonlinearity in
(
1.1
):

For any
(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}\times\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
and
i,j,k,l=1,\cdots,m
, it holds that

\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu,\nu=0}^{2}Q_{ijkl}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\xi_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}\equiv 0.

(1.4)

On the other hand, with respect to (
1.2
), it is easy to check that for
i,j,k,l=1,\cdots,m
and
(\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}\times\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
,

\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{2}Q_{ijk}^{\alpha\beta}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\mu=0}^{2}Q_{ijk}^{\alpha\beta\mu}\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\xi_{\mu}\equiv 0.

(1.5)

There are a lot of physical or geometric models of the second order quasilinear wave equations
or systems such that their nonlinearities admit the forms in (
1.1

For examples, these models include:

(1)
3D irrotational potential flow equation of Chaplygin gases

The compressible isentropic Euler equations on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\times\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
are

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+div(\rho v)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho v)+div(\rho v\otimes v)+\nabla p=0,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

where
(t,x,y)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
,
\nabla=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3})=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}},\partial_{y})
,
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
,

\rho
,
p
stand for the velocity, density, pressure, respectively.
The state equation of Chaplygin gases (see
[
5
]
) is

p(\rho)=P_{0}-\frac{B}{\rho},

(1.7)

where
P_{0}
and
B
are positive constants.
Suppose that (
1.6
) admits the following irrotational smooth initial data with
2\pi
-period on
y
:

\begin{split}&(\rho,v)(0,x,y)=(\bar{\rho}+\rho_{0}(x,y),v_{1}^{0}(x,y),v_{2}^{0}(x,y),v_{3}^{0}(x,y)),\\
&\partial_{i}v_{j}^{0}=\partial_{j}v_{i}^{0},\quad 1\leq i<j\leq 3,\end{split}

(1.8)

where
\bar{\rho}+\rho_{0}(x,y)>0
, and
\bar{\rho}
is
a positive constant which can be normalized so that the sound speed
c(\bar{\rho})=\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\bar{\rho})}=1
. Then by the irrotationality
of (
1.6
), one can
introduce a potential function
\phi
such that
v=\nabla\phi
. Meanwhile,
the Bernoulli’s law
\partial_{t}\phi+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+h(\rho)=0

holds with the enthalpy
h(\rho)
satisfying
h^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{p^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho}
and
h(\bar{\rho})=0
. Therefore, for smooth
irrotational flows,
\phi
fulfills the following second order quasilinear wave equation

\Box\phi=2\partial_{t}\phi\Delta\phi-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}\partial_{k}\phi\partial_{tk}^{2}\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}\Delta\phi-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{ij}^{2}\phi,\quad\Delta=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}+\partial_{3}^{2},

(1.9)

with the initial data
(\phi_{(0)},\phi_{(1)})
as

\begin{split}&\phi_{(0)}(x_{1},x_{2},y):=\int_{-\infty}^{x_{1}}v_{1}^{0}(s,x_{2},y)ds,\\
&\phi_{(1)}=-\frac{1}{2}(|v_{1}^{0}|^{2}+|v_{2}^{0}|^{2}+|v_{3}^{0}|^{2})-h(\bar{\rho}+\rho_{0}).\end{split}

One easily checks that the quadratic nonlinearity in (
1.9
) can be written as

2\partial_{t}\phi\Delta\phi-2\sum_{k=1}^{3}\partial_{k}\phi\partial_{tk}^{2}\phi=2Q_{0}(\partial_{t}\phi,\phi)-2\partial_{t}\phi\Box\phi.

(1.10)

By substituting (
1.10
) into the equation (
1.9
) and replacing
\Box\phi
with (
1.9
),
we can transform the equation (
1.9
) into the following form:

\begin{split}\Box\phi&=2Q_{0}(\partial_{t}\phi,\phi)-4(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}\Delta\phi+4\sum_{k=1}^{3}\partial_{t}\phi\partial_{k}\phi\partial_{tk}^{2}\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}\Delta\phi-\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{ij}^{2}\phi\\
&\quad-2\partial_{t}\phi\Delta\phi|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\partial_{t}\phi\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{ij}^{2}\phi.\end{split}

(1.11)

It should be emphasized that the quartic term
2\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\partial_{t}\phi\partial_{i}\phi\partial_{j}\phi\partial_{ij}^{2}\phi

in the nonlinearity of (
1.11
) does not cause any additional difficulties.
In addition, the cubic nonlinearities of (
1.11
) satisfy the partial null condition (
1.4
).
This means that the 3D irrotational potential flow equation (
1.9
) is a special case of the nonlinear wave
system in problem (
1.1
).

(2)
3D relativistic membrane equation

\partial_{t}\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}\phi}{\sqrt{1-(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}}}\bigg)-div\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{\nabla\phi}{\sqrt{1-(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}}}\bigg)=0,

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sqrt{1+|\nabla\phi|^{2}-(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}}\\
dtdxdy

(t,x,y)\to(t,x,y,\phi(t,x,y))

Here
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,

2\pi
-periodic with respect to the variable

It is easy to know that (

\Box\phi=(|\partial_{t}\phi|^{2}-|\nabla\phi|^{2})(\partial_{t}^{2}\phi-\Delta\phi)-(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi+2\partial_{t}\phi\nabla\phi\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla\phi-\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(|\nabla\phi|^{2}).

Then (

(3)
Some 3D quasilinear wave equations which come from the Lagrangian functionals as perturbations of the
Lagrangian densities of linear waves

\Box\phi=-\partial_{t}\bigg(\big(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)^{k}\partial_{t}\phi\bigg)+div\bigg(\big(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)^{k}\nabla\phi\bigg),\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

with
\nabla=(\partial_{x_{1}},\partial_{x_{2}},\partial_{y})

Note that

can be thought as a perturbation of the Lagrangian density of linear wave.

In addition,

(1+(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2k+1})\partial_{t}^{2}\phi-\Delta\phi+\partial_{t}\bigg(\big(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)^{k}\partial_{t}\phi\bigg)-div\bigg(\big(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)^{k}\nabla\phi\bigg)=0,

\Box\phi=-\partial_{t}\bigg(\big(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)^{k}\partial_{t}\phi\bigg)+div\bigg(\big(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\big)^{k}\nabla\phi\bigg)-(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2k+1}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi,

L(\phi)=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg(-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\bigg)^{k+1}-\frac{(\partial_{t}\phi)^{2k+3}}{(2k+3)(2k+2)}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

(4)
3D wave maps system

Let (
\mathcal{M}
,
g
) be a
m
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold without
boundary. A wave map is a continuous function from the Minkowski space
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{1+3}

\phi=(\phi^{1},...,\phi^{m}):\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\times\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{3}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M},

F(\phi)=\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{1+3}}\langle\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}\phi,\partial_{\alpha}\phi\rangle_{g}dtdx.

Here
\eta
is the standard Minkowski metric on
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{1+3}
, which is represented by the matrix
\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
in
rectangular coordinates. When the local coordinates on
\mathcal{M}
are introduced, the equations of
\phi^{i}~(1\leq i\leq m)
can be written as

\Box\phi^{i}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(\phi)\Big(\partial_{t}\phi^{j}\partial_{t}\phi^{k}-\sum_{l=1}^{3}\partial_{x_{l}}\phi^{j}\partial_{x_{l}}\phi^{k}\Big),~~~1\leq i\leq m,

(1.16)

where
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(\phi)

g
. For more detailed derivation of the wave maps system (
1.16
), one can see 2.1.2 of Chapter 2 in
[
33
]

of (
1.16

function
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}(\phi)
under Riemann normal coordinates, the long time existence of (
1.16
) can be
attributed to the following system of semilinear wave equations

\Box\phi^{i}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\sum_{l=1}^{3}C^{i}_{jkl}Q_{0}(\phi^{j},\phi^{k})\phi^{l},~~~1\leq i\leq m.

(1.17)

It is easy to know that the 3D wave maps system (
1.16
) or (
1.17

the nonlinear wave system in problem (
1.1
) when
\phi(t,x_{1},x_{2},y)
is
2\pi
-periodic with respect to
y
.

\begin{split}\varepsilon&:=\sum_{i+j\leq 2N+1}\|\langle{x}\rangle^{4+i+j}\nabla_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}u_{(0)}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}+\sum_{i+j\leq 2N}\|\langle{x}\rangle^{4+i+j}\nabla_{x}^{i}\partial_{y}^{j}u_{(1)}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}},\end{split}

We next illustrate some other difficulties in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and outline how to overcome them.

\bullet
Invalidity of the scaling vector
S=t\partial_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}

Note that
\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}\times\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
is not scaling invariant in the periodic direction
y
.
Moreover, the action of
S+y\partial_{y}
does not preserve the periodic property, namely,

(S+y\partial_{y})f|_{y=2\pi}\neq(S+y\partial_{y})f|_{y=0}
holds generally provided that
f
is
2\pi
-periodic in
y
.
On the other hand, it is well-known that the following Klainerman-Sobolev inequality plays a key role in
deriving the time decay rate and further proving the global existence of small data smooth solutions to
the
d
-dimensional nonlinear wave equations
(see
[
1
,
2
,
13
,
23
]
)

(1+|t-r|)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+t)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}|\partial\phi(t,x)|\leq C\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq[d/2]+2}\|\hat{Z}^{I}\partial\phi(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.33)

where
\hat{Z}\in\{\partial,x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i},x_{i}\partial_{t}+t\partial_{i},1\leq i,j\leq d,t\partial_{t}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}\partial_{k}\}
and
r=|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+x_{d}^{2}}
.
Unfortunately, the scaling vector
S=t\partial_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
does not commutate with the wave operator
\Box:=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x}-\partial_{y}^{2}
. Even if we perform the Fourier transformation with respect to the periodic variable
y

for the nonlinear equations
\Box_{t,x,y}u^{i}(t,x,y)=F^{i}(u,\partial u,\partial^{2}u)(t,x,y)
as in (
1.22
), then
from (
1.21
), one immediately sees that the zero-modes
u^{i}_{0}
are the solutions of wave equations
and other non-zero modes

(

) solve Klein-Gordon equations with mass
|n|
.
In this case,
S=t\partial_{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
does not commutate with the resulting
Klein-Gordon operators
\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta_{x}+|n|^{2}
(
n\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits\setminus\{0\}
) yet. This fact prevents us
from using the classical Klainerman-Sobolev inequality (

) to show the global existence of (
1.1
).
To overcome this difficulty, our key ingredient is to establish the pointwise decay estimates for the solutions of (
1.1
)
through deriving the space-time decay estimates of zero modes and non-zero modes by some kinds of

L^{\infty}-L^{\infty}
and

estimates in the theory
of linear wave equations. In this process, some basic techniques in wave equations
(see
[
24
,
34
,
29
,
26
]
) and in Klein-Gordon equations (see
[
12
]
)
will be utilized and improved.

\bullet
Loss of derivatives in the highest order energy estimates

Due to the transformation (
1.30
) including the second order derivatives
\partial^{2}u
, it follows from (
1.31
)
and (
1.32

directly since the error

in (

)
contains

. To overcome this difficulty, we return to investigate the quadratic nonlinear problem (

) by
applying S.Alinhac’s ghost weight technique and the lower order energy estimates of

derived from (

Nevertheless, in this procedure, we have to take
some new strategies to derive the precise a priori space-time decays of

by making full use of the null condition structure in (

which is different from the usual

\begin{split}\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-2}|P_{=0}\partial Z^{a}u(t,x,y)|&\leq C\varepsilon\langle{t}\rangle^{0.01}\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2},\\
\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-5}|P_{=0}\partial Z^{a}u(t,x,y)|&\leq C\varepsilon\langle{t}\rangle^{0.02}\langle{x}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{-1/2},\\

(1.34)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq 2N-5}|P_{=0}Z^{a}u(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\langle{t}\rangle^{0.02},

(1.35a)

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq N+7}|P_{=0}Z^{a}u(t,x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{-1/2}\langle{t-|x|}\rangle^{-0.3},

(1.35b)

\displaystyle\sum_{|a|\leq N-1}|P_{=0}Z^{a}u(t,x,y)|

(1.35c)

It should be emphasized that (

When all the difficulties mentioned above are overcome and further the related estimates are established,
the proof of Theorem

2.1
Some basic lemmas

At first, we show some properties on the projection operators
P_{=0}
and
P_{\neq 0}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any real valued functions
f(t,x,y)
and
g(t,x,y)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
, it holds that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}P_{=0}fP_{\neq 0}gdy=0,\quad\|f\|^{2}_{L_{y}^{2}}=\|P_{=0}f\|^{2}_{L_{y}^{2}}+\|P_{\neq 0}f\|^{2}_{L_{y}^{2}},\\
&P_{=0}Zf=ZP_{=0}f,\quad P_{\neq 0}Zf=ZP_{\neq 0}f,\quad Z=\{\partial,L,\Omega\},\\
&P_{=0}\partial_{y}f=\partial_{y}P_{=0}f=0,\quad(\Gamma f)_{n}=\Gamma(f_{n}),\quad\Gamma=\{\partial_{t,x},L,\Omega\},\\
&P_{=0}(fg)=P_{=0}fP_{=0}g+P_{=0}(P_{\neq 0}fP_{\neq 0}g),\\
&P_{\neq 0}(fg)=P_{\neq 0}(P_{=0}fP_{\neq 0}g)+P_{\neq 0}(P_{\neq 0}fP_{=0}g)+P_{\neq 0}(P_{\neq 0}fP_{\neq 0}g),\\
&|P_{=0}f|\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{y}^{\infty}},\quad|P_{\neq 0}f|\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{y}^{\infty}}.\end{split}

(2.1)

Proof.

These properties can be directly verified, we omit the details here.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

For any real valued functions
f(t,x,y)
and
g(t,x,y)
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
, one has

Z^{a}Q_{0}(f,g)=\sum_{b+c\leq a}C_{abc}Q_{0}(Z^{b}f,Z^{c}g),

(2.2)

where
C_{abc}
are some constants.

Proof.

Note that

\begin{split}\partial_{\alpha}Q_{0}(f,g)&=Q_{0}(\partial_{\alpha}f,g)+Q_{0}(f,\partial_{\alpha}g),\\
L_{i}Q_{0}(f,g)&=Q_{0}(L_{i}f,g)+Q_{0}(f,L_{i}g),\\
\Omega Q_{0}(f,g)&=Q_{0}(\Omega f,g)+Q_{0}(f,\Omega g).\end{split}

Then it follows from these identities and direct computation that (
2.2

Next, we introduce some inequalities including the Poincaré inequality and Hardy inequality.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any function
f(y)
,
y\in\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits
, it holds that

\|P_{\neq 0}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}=\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)}\lesssim\|\partial_{y}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)},\quad\bar{f}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{T}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}f(y)dy.

(2.8)

For any function
g(t,x)\in L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
x\in\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}
, it holds that

\Big\|\frac{g}{|x|\ln|x|}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}.

(2.9)

Furthermore, it also holds that for any fixed positive constant
\lambda
,

\Big\|\frac{g\chi}{\langle{|x|-t}\rangle^{1/2+\lambda}}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\lesssim\frac{\langle{t}\rangle^{1/2}\ln(2+t)}{\lambda^{1/2}}\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})},

(2.10)

where
\chi=\chi(\frac{|x|}{\langle{t}\rangle})
,
\chi(s)\in C^{\infty}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits)
,
0\leq\chi(s)\leq 1
and

\chi(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\qquad s\in[1/2,2],\\
&0,\qquad s\not\in[1/3,3].\end{aligned}\right.

(2.11)

Proof.

(
2.8
) is the Poincaré inequality and (
2.9
) is the Hardy inequality.
Set
q(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{s}\frac{dt}{(1+|t|^{2})^{1/2+\lambda}}
. Then

holds.
Hence (
2.10
) can be achieved by the following computation

\begin{split}&\Big\|\frac{g\chi}{\langle{|x|-t}\rangle^{1/2+\lambda}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}\frac{g^{2}\chi^{2}}{(1+|r-t|^{2})^{1/2+\lambda}}rd\omega dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}g^{2}\chi^{2}rd\omega dq(r-t)\\
&\quad\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}g^{2}|q(r-t)||\partial_{r}(\chi^{2}r)|d\omega dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{S}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits}|q(r-t)||g\partial_{r}g|\chi^{2}rd\omega dr\\
&\quad\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\Big(\langle{t}\rangle^{-1}\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}}g^{2}\chi dx+\|g\chi\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\Big)\\
&\quad\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\langle{t}\rangle\ln^{2}(2+t)\int_{\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2}}\frac{g^{2}}{|x|^{2}\ln^{2}|x|}dx+\lambda^{-1}\langle{t}\rangle\ln(2+t)\Big\|\frac{g}{|x|\ln|x|}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}\\
&\quad\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\langle{t}\rangle\ln^{2}(2+t)\|\nabla_{x}g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathop{\mathbb{R}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits^{2})}.\end{split}

∎

(4.6)

\begin{split}&\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{0.95}|P_{\neq 0}w(t,x,y)|\lesssim\langle{t+|x|}\rangle^{0.95}\sum_{n\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}\kern 0.0pt}\nolimits_{*}}|w_{n}(t,x)|\\

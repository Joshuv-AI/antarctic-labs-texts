Cohomology and deformations of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Hom-associative algebras
Taoufik Chtioui , Sami Mabrouk , Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences Gafsa, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques, 6, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the cohomology theory of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Hom-associative algebras. This cohomology can also be viewed as the Hochschild cohomology of a certain Hom-associative algebra with coefficients in a suitable bimodule. Next, we study infinitesimal and formal deformations of an 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator and show that they are governed by the above-defined cohomology. Furthermore, the notion of Nijenhuis elements associated with an 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator is introduced to characterize trivial infinitesimal deformations.

1
Introduction

The concept of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced in the 1960th by G. Baxter
[
1
]
in the study of fluctuation theory in Probability and then the theory was established by G.-C. Rota
[
2
]
mainly in connection with Combinatorics.
Motivated by the study of associative analogues of Poisson structures, Uchino introduced the notion of generalized Rota-Baxter operator (called also
\mathcal{O}
-operator) on a bimodule over an associative algebra in
[
47
]
.
In
[
48
]
, Uchino also introduced the notion of a strong Maurer-Cartan equation on a bimodule
(V;\ell,r)
. A solution of the strong Maurer-Cartan equation can be seen as the associative analogue of a closed
2
-form complementary to a Poisson structure.
Rota-Baxter operators and their generalization
\mathcal{O}
-operators are becoming rather popular due to their interest in various domain like nonassociative algebra and applied algebra. Their deformations and their controlling cohomologies have been studied for Lie algebras in
[
46
]
and for associative algebras in
[
20
]
, see also
[
13
]

[
35
]
.

The first instances of Hom-type algebras appeared in
q
-deformations of algebras of vector fields in physics contexts. It turns out that the Jacobi condition is no longer satisfied in the
q
-deformations of Witt algebra and Virasoro algebra using Jackson derivative. Hartwig, Larsson and Silvestrov
[
28
]
introduced a so called Hom-Lie algebras, generalizing Lie algebras and where the usual Jacobi identity is twisted by a homomorphism. The pending Hom-associative algebras were introduced in
[
42
]
. Various classical structures were generalized within this framework. A huge research activity was dedicated to Hom-type algebras, due in part to the prospect of having a general framework in which one can produce many types of natural deformations which are of interest to both mathematicians and physicists. Cohomology and deformations of Hom-Lie and Hom-associative algebras were studied in
[
4
,
30
,
45
]
. Notice that Cohomology and deformations of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Hom-Lie algebras were discussed in

[
3
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to introduce and study a cohomology controlling deformations of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Hom-associative algebras. Therefore, we consider one parameter formal deformations and provide relevant results about infinitesimal deformations and general
n
-order deformations. In addition, infinitesimal deformations are characterized using Nijenhuis elements. In Section 2, relevant definitions and preliminary results are presented. They deal with Hom-associative algebras and Hom-dendriform algebra, bimodule structures, Rota-Baxter structures,
\mathcal{O}
-operators, Nijenhuis operators, cohomology and graded Lie algebras. Main results of this paper are given in Section 3 in which we provide Cohomologies of
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Hom-associative algebras and their deformations are considered in Section 4.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the relevant definitions and the notion of
\mathcal{O}
-operator on Hom-associative algebra. We also recall Hom-dendriform structures induced from
\mathcal{O}
-operators. Finally, we construct a graded Lie algebra with explicit graded Lie bracket whose Maurer-Cartan elements are given by
\mathcal{O}
-operators.

2.1
Hom-associative and Hom-dendriform algebras

Definition 2.1
.

A Hom-associative algebra is a Hom-module
(A,\alpha)
, consisting of a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
A
and a linear map
\alpha
, together with a bilinear map
\mu:A\times A\rightarrow A,~(a,b)\mapsto a\cdot b
, that satisfies

\displaystyle\alpha(a)\cdot(b\cdot c)=(a\cdot b)\cdot\alpha(c),~~~\text{ for all }~a,b,c\in A.

A Hom-associative algebra is called multiplicative if
\alpha(a\cdot b)=\alpha(a)\cdot\alpha(b)
. By a Hom-associative algebra, we shall always mean a multiplicative one, unless otherwise specified.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra and
M
a vector space. Let
\phi\in gl(M)
and
l:A\otimes M\to M,\ r:M\otimes A\to M
be two linear maps. We say that the tuple
(M,l,r,\phi)
is a bimodule of
A
if for any
x,y\in A
and
v\in M

\displaystyle\phi l(x,v)=l(\alpha(x),\phi(v)),

\displaystyle\ \phi r(v,x)=r(\phi(v),\alpha(x)),

\displaystyle l(xy,\phi(v))=

\displaystyle l(\alpha(x),l(y,v)),

\displaystyle r(\phi(v),xy)=

\displaystyle r(r(v,x),\alpha(y)),

\displaystyle l(\alpha(x),r(v,y))=

\displaystyle r(l(x,v),\alpha(y)).

Unless otherwise stated,
l(x,v)
(resp.
r(x,v)
) may be denoted, depending on the situation, by
xv
or
l(x)v
(resp.
vx
or
r(x)v
).

Recall that if we consider a Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
. Then
(M,l,r,\phi)
is a bimodule of
A
if and only if the vector space
A\oplus M
(the split extension of
A
by
M
) carries a Hom-associative structure with product given by

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{l,r}(y,v)=

\displaystyle(x\cdot y,l(x,u)+r(v,y)),\ \forall x,y\in A,u,v\in M,

\displaystyle(\alpha,\phi)(x,u)=

\displaystyle(\alpha(x),\phi(u)).

This is called the semi-direct product of
A
with
M
.

In
[
4
,
44
]
the authors define a Hochschild-type cohomology of a Hom-associative algebra and study the one parameter formal deformation theory for these type of algebras. Let
(A,\alpha,\mu)
be a Hom-associative algebra and
(M,l,r,\phi)
be a bimodule. For each
n\geq 1
, the group of
n
-cochains is defined as

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\alpha,\phi}(A,M):=\{f:A^{\otimes n}\rightarrow M|~\phi\circ f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=f(\alpha(a_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(a_{n}))\}

and the differential
\delta_{\alpha,\phi}:C^{n}_{\alpha,\phi}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\alpha,\phi}(A,M)
is given by

\displaystyle(\delta_{\alpha,\phi}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-1}(a_{1})f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})

(2.1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(\alpha(a_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(a_{i-1}),\mu(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\alpha(a_{i+2}),\ldots,\alpha(a_{n+1}))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\alpha^{n-1}(a_{n+1}).

for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A
.
The cohomology of this complex is called the Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,l,r,\phi)
and the cohomology groups are denoted by

H^{\bullet}_{\alpha,\phi}(A,M)
.

In
[
47
]
, Uchino gave a characterization of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on an associative algebra. This result is still true for Hom-associative algebras. Recall first the notion of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Hom-associative algebra.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra and
(M,l,r,\phi)
be a bimodule. A linear map

\displaystyle T\phi=\alpha T,\ \quad T(u)T(v)=T(l(T(u))v+r(T(v))u),~~~\text{ for all }u,v\in M.

Example 2.4
.

A Rota-Baxter operator
\mathcal{R}:A\to A
of weight
0
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on

with respect to the adjoint representation
(A,L,R,\alpha)
.

Example 2.5

Let

Example 2.6

Let

.

A linear map

.

Another characterization of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator can be given in terms of Nijenhuis operator on Hom-associative algebras.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra. A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a Nijenhuis operator if
N\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ N
and its Nijenhuis torsion vanishes, i.e.

\displaystyle N(x)\cdot N(y)=N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)),~~\text{ for all }x,y\in A.

Note that the deformed multiplication
\mu_{N}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
given by

\mu_{N}(x,y)=N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y),

defines a new Hom-associative multiplication on
A
, and
N
becomes an algebra morphism from
(A,\mu_{N},\alpha)
to
(A,\mu,\alpha).

In addition
\mu
and
\mu_{N}
are compatible, i.e.
\mu+\lambda\mu_{N}
still a Hom-associative structure for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
. Then the pair
(\mu,\mu_{N})
becomes a Hom-quantum bi-Hamiltonian system.

The following result is straightforward, so we omit details.

Proposition 2.10
.

A linear map
T:M\rightarrow A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
with respect to the bimodule
(M,l,r,\phi)
if and only if
N_{T}=\begin{pmatrix}0&T\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}:A\oplus M\rightarrow A\oplus M
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Hom-associative algebra
A\oplus M
.

Dendriform structures were first introduced by Loday in his study of the periodicity phenomenons in algebraic
K
-theory
[
36
]
. They become rather popular in the last 20 years due to their connection with Rota-Baxter algebras, shuffle algebras and combinatorics
[
6
,
48
,
27
]
. In
[
43
]
, Makhlouf introduced the notion of Hom-dendriform algebra.

Definition 2.11
.

A Hom-dendriform algebra is a vector space
D
together with three linear maps
\prec,\succ:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
and
\alpha:D\to D
satisfying the following three identities

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)=~\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z),

(2.2)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)=~\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z),

(2.3)

\displaystyle(x\prec y+x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)=~\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z),

(2.4)

for all
x,y,z\in D
.

It follows from (
2.2
)-(
2.4
) that the new product
\star:D\otimes D\rightarrow D
defined by
x\star y=x\prec y~+~y\succ x
turns out to be Hom-associative. Thus, a Hom-dendriform algebra can be seen as a splitting of a Hom-associative algebra.

An
\mathcal{O}
-operator has an underlying Hom-dendriform structure on the bimodule.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
T:M\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Hom-associative algebra
A
with respect to the bimodule
M
. Then the vector space
M
carries a Hom-dendriform structure with

\displaystyle u\prec v=r(T(v))u~~~\text{ and }~~~u\succ v=l(T(u))v,~~~\text{ for all }u,v\in M.

Moreover,
(M,\star_{T}=\prec+\succ,\phi)
is a Hom-associative algebra.

3.1

Let

The product

Therefore, from the general principle of Maurer-Cartan elements, we obtain.

Theorem 3.3

Let

\mathcal{O}
-operator on

Moreover, for any linear map

\displaystyle\llbracket T+T^{\prime},T+T^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\alpha}=0~\Longleftrightarrow~d_{T}(T^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\llbracket T^{\prime},T^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\alpha}=0.

In the next paragraph, we interpret this cohomology as the Hochschild cohomology of a certain algebra with coefficients in a suitable bimodule. Then we will use the usual notation for Hochschild cohomology to denote the cohomology of an

3.2
Cohomology of
\mathcal{O}
-operators as Hochschild cohomology

The aim of this section is to show that the cohomology of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator can also be described as the Hochschild cohomology of a certain Hom-associative algebra with coefficients in a suitable bimodule.

Let
T:M\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A
with respect to the bimodule
(M,l,r,\phi)
. Then by Proposition
2.12
the vector space

carries a Hom-associative algebra structure with the product

\displaystyle u\star_{T}v=r(u,T(v))+l(T(u),v),~~~\text{ for }u,v\in M.

(3.5)

According to (
2.10
), there is a Hom-associative algebra structure on

A\oplus M\backsimeq M\oplus A
defined as

\displaystyle(x,u)\cdot_{\overline{T}}(y,v)=

\displaystyle(Tu,0)\cdot_{(l,r)}(y,v)+(x,u)\cdot_{(l,r)}(Tv,0)-(T(l(x,v)+r(u,y),0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(Tu\cdot y,l(Tu,v))+(x\cdot Tv,-r(u,Tv))-(T(l(x,v)+T(r(u,y)),0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{T}(u,y)+l_{T}(v,x),u\star v),

where

l_{T}(u,x)=Tu\cdot x-T(r(u,x)),\ r_{T}(x,u)=x\cdot Tu-T(l(x,u),\ \forall x\in A,u\in M.

(3.6)

Therefore we have the following result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T:M\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on

with respect to the bimodule
(M,l,r,\phi)
. Then

-bimodule structure on

with respect to
\alpha

By Lemma

M
-bimodule structure on the vector space

with coefficients in
A
. We define

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\alpha}(M,A):=\text{Hom}(M^{\otimes n},A),~~\text{ for }n\geq 0

and the differential by

\displaystyle d_{H}(a)(m)=l_{T}(m,a)-r_{T}(a,m)=T(m)\cdot a-T(ma)-a\cdot T(m)+T(am),~~~\text{ for }a\in A=C^{0}_{\alpha}(M,A)

(3.7)

and

\displaystyle(d_{H}f)(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1})=~T(\phi^{n-1}(u_{1}))\cdot f(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n+1})-T(r(\phi^{n-1}(u_{1}),f(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n+1})))

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}~f(\phi(u_{1}),\ldots,\phi(u_{i-1}),r(u_{i},T(u_{i+1}))+l(T(u_{i}),u_{i+1}),\ldots,\phi(u_{n+1}))

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}~f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\cdot T(\phi^{n-1}(u_{n+1}))-(-1)^{n+1}T(l(f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}),\phi^{n-1}(u_{n+1}))).

.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle H^{0}(M,A)=~

\displaystyle\{a\in A|~d_{H}(a)=0\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{a\in A|~a\cdot T(m)-T(m)\cdot a=T(l(a,m)-r(m,a)),~\forall m\in M\}.

From this definition, it is easy to see that if

. This shows that

.

Note that a linear map

\displaystyle T(u)\cdot f(v)+f(u)\cdot T(v)-T(uf(v)+f(u)v)-f(uT(v)+T(u)v)=0,

(3.8)

for all

Proposition 3.5

-operator on

\displaystyle d_{T}f=(-1)^{n}~d_{H}f,~~~~\text{ for }f\in C^{n}_{\alpha}(M,A).

For any

\displaystyle(d_{T}f)(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle T(l(f(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\phi^{n-1}(u_{n+1})))-(-1)^{n}~T(r(\phi^{n-1}(u_{1}),f(u_{2},\ldots,u_{n+1}))

\displaystyle-~

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i-1}~f(\phi(u_{1}),\ldots,\phi(u_{i-1}),T(u_{i})u_{i+1},u_{i+2},\ldots,\phi(u_{n+1}))

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}~(d_{H}f)(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n+1}).

Definition 4.1

Proposition 4.2

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}((f,g)):=\big\{\big((a,u),(f(a),g(u))\big)|~a\in A,u\in V\big\}\subset(A\oplus V)\oplus(B\oplus W)

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi=

\displaystyle T_{t}(u)\cdot T_{t}(v)=

\displaystyle T_{t}(l(T_{t}(u))v+r(T_{t}(v))u),~~~~\text{ for all }t\text{ and }u,v\in M.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\phi=~

\displaystyle\alpha\mathcal{T},,

(4.5)

\displaystyle T(u)\cdot\mathcal{T}(v)+\mathcal{T}(u)\cdot T(v)=~

\displaystyle T(r(\mathcal{T}(v))u+l(\mathcal{T}(u))v)+\mathcal{T}(r(T(v))u+l(T(u))v),

(4.11)

Similarly, (v) gives rise to

\displaystyle r_{x\cdot y-y\cdot x}\circ l_{x}=r_{x\cdot y-y\cdot x}\circ r_{x},~~~~\text{ for }y\in A.

(4.12)

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
T^{1}_{t}=T+t\mathcal{T}_{1}
and

be two equivalent infinitesimal deformations of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator

\mathcal{T}_{1}
and

defines the same cohomology class in

Proof.

The proof follows from equation (

).

Definition 4.8
.

Let

be an
\mathcal{O}

T_{0}=T.

\displaystyle(x\cdot y-y\cdot x)\cdot(x\cdot z-z\cdot x)=0,

(4.13)

\displaystyle x\cdot(l_{T}(u,x)-r_{T}(x,u))-(l_{T}(u,x)-r_{T}(x,u))\cdot x=0,

(4.14)

\displaystyle l_{x\cdot y-y\cdot x}\circ l_{x}=l_{x\cdot y-y\cdot x}\circ r_{x},

(4.15)

\displaystyle r_{x\cdot y-y\cdot x}\circ l_{x}=r_{x\cdot y-y\cdot x}\circ r_{x},

(4.16)

for any
y,z\in A
and

Definition 4.9

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Hom-associative algebra
(A,\alpha)
with respect to a representation
(M,l,r,\phi)
. For any element

,
T_{t}:=T+t\mathcal{T}
, with
\mathcal{T}=d_{H}(x)
, is a trivial infinitesimal deformation of

.

Proof.

First, we need to prove that
T_{t}:=T+t\mathcal{T}
is a infinitesimal deformation generated by
\mathcal{T}=d_{H}(x)
, where
x\in\textrm{Nij}(T)
. That is
\mathcal{T}
must satisfy conditions (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
). First,

\displaystyle\alpha\mathcal{T}(u)=\alpha d_{H}(x)(u)=d_{H}(x)(\phi(u))=\mathcal{T}(\phi(u)),\forall u\in M.

Then
\mathcal{T}
satisfies (
4.5
). The identity (
4.6
) holds immediately since
\mathcal{T}=d_{H}(x)
.
The identity (
4.7
) is also straightforward. We omit details since it follows from a long computation. We refer to
[
46
]
for a similar computation.
Finally, since

is a Nijenhuis element, it follows that the pair

is a morphism of
\mathcal{O}
-operators from

to

. Hence the result follows.

4.2
Formal Deformations

In this section, we study one-parameter formal deformations of
O-operators. Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra with a representation
(M,l,r,\phi)
.
Let
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
be the formal power series ring in one variable
t
and
A[[t]]
be the formal power series in
t
with coefficients in
A
. Then the tuple
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})
is a Hom-associative algebra, where the product
\mu_{t}
and the structure map
\alpha_{t}
are obtained by extending
\mu
and
\alpha
linearly over the ring
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
. Moreover the maps
l
,
r
and
\phi
can be extended linearly over
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
to obtain
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear maps
l_{t}:A[[t]]\otimes M[[t]]\to M[[t]],\ r_{t}:M[[t]]\otimes A[[t]]\to M[[t]]
and
\phi_{t}:M[[t]]\to M[[t]]
. Then
(M[[t]],l_{t},r_{t},\phi_{t})
is a representation of the Hom-associative algebra
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})
.

Definition 4.12
.

Let
T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Home associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
with respect to a representation
(M,l,r,\phi)
. A formal one-parameter deformation of
T
is given by

\displaystyle T_{t}=T_{0}+\sum_{i\geq 1}t^{i}T_{i},\quad\textrm{with}\ T_{0}=T,\ T_{i}\in C^{1}_{\phi,\alpha}(M,A)

such that
T_{t}:M[[t]]\to A[[t]]
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},\alpha_{t})
with respect to
(M[[t]],l_{t},r_{t},\phi_{t})
.

That is

\displaystyle T_{t}\phi_{t}=\alpha_{t}T_{t},

(4.17)

\displaystyle T_{t}(u)\cdot T_{t}(v)=T_{t}(T_{t}(u)v+uT_{t}(v)),\ \forall u,v\in M.

(4.18)

Condition (
4.17
) is immediate since
T_{i}\in C^{1}_{\phi,\alpha}(M,A),\ \forall i
.
For any
k\geq 0
, by equating the coefficients of
t^{k}
from both sides of equation (
4.18
), we obtain the following system of equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}T_{i}(u)\cdot T_{j}(v)=\sum_{i+j=k}T_{i}(T_{j}(u)v+uT_{j}(v)).

(4.19)

The above identity holds for
k=0
, since
T
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. For
k=1
, we get

\displaystyle T(u)\cdot T_{1}(v)+T_{1}(u)\cdot T(v)=T(uT_{1}(v)+T_{1}(u)v)+T_{1}(uT(v)+T(u)v),\forall u,v\in M,

(4.20)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\llbracket T,T_{1}\rrbracket_{\alpha}=0.

(4.21)

That is
d_{T}(T_{1})=0
. Therefore, we get the following proposition.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}T_{i}
be a formal deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
on
A
with respect to the bimodule
M
. Then the
1
-cochain
T_{1}\in C^{1}_{\phi,\alpha}(M,A)
is a
1
-cocycle with respect to the cohomology of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
.

The
1
-cochain
T_{1}
is called the infinitesimal of the deformation
T_{t}
. More generally,
if
T_{i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq(n-1)
and
T_{n}
is a non-zero cochain, then
T_{n}
is called the
n
-infinitesimal of the deformation
T_{t}
.

In
[
19
]
, the author study formal deformations of Hom-dendriform algebras and their relation with the cohomology. Here we provide a connection between deformations of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator with deformations of the corresponding Hom-dendriform algebra. We mean by a deformation of a Hom-dendriform algebra, a formal deformation of its defining two multiplications.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}T_{i}
be a formal deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
. Then the formal sums

\displaystyle u\prec_{t}v=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}uT_{i}(v)\quad\text{ and }\quad u\succ_{t}v=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}T_{i}(u)v

define a formal deformation of the Hom-dendriform structure on
M
.

Corollary 4.15
.

It follows that the formal sum
u\star_{t}v=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}(uT_{i}(v)+T_{i}(u)v)
defines a formal deformation of the Hom-associative structure on
M
.

Now, we consider the equivalence of two formal deformations of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. The definition is motivated by the associative algebra case
[
20
]
.

Definition 4.16
.

Two formal deformations
T_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}T_{i}
and
\overline{T}_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}\overline{T}_{i}
of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
are said to be equivalent if there exists an element
x\in A
satisfying
\alpha(x)=x
, linear maps
f_{i}:A\to A
and
g_{i}:M\to M
, for
i\geq 2
, such that the pair
(f_{t},g_{t})
given by

\displaystyle f_{t}=id_{A}+t(L_{x}-R_{x})+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}f_{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad g_{t}=id_{M}+t(l_{x}-r_{x})+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}g_{i}

defines a formal morphism of
\mathcal{O}
-operators from
T_{t}
to
\overline{T}_{t}
.

With the above notations, for all
y,z\in A
and
u\in M
, the equivalence of two deformations
T_{t}
and
\overline{T}_{t}
corresponds to the following conditions

\displaystyle f_{t}T_{t}=

\displaystyle T_{t}g_{t},

(4.22)

\displaystyle f_{t}(y\cdot z)=

\displaystyle f_{t}(y)\cdot f_{t}(z),

(4.23)

\displaystyle g_{t}(yu)=

\displaystyle f_{t}(y)g_{t}(u),

(4.24)

\displaystyle g_{t}(uy)=

\displaystyle g_{t}(u)f_{t}(y),

(4.25)

\displaystyle f_{t}\alpha=\alpha f_{t},\

\displaystyle g_{t}\phi=\phi g_{t}.

(4.26)

In particular, a formal deformation
T_{t}
of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
is said to be trivial if it is equivalent to
T
(here
T
is regarded as a deformation of itself).

Now, on comparing the coefficients of
t
from both sides of the condition (
4.22
), we get

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)-\overline{T}_{1}(u)=T(u)\cdot x-T(ux)-x\cdot T(u)+T(xu)=d_{H}(x)(u).

Consequently, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 4.17
.

The infinitesimals of equivalent deformations are cohomologous, i.e. they lie on the same cohomology class .

Definition 4.18
.

An
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
is said to be rigid if every deformation
T_{t}
of
T
is equivalent to the deformation
\overline{T}_{t}=T
.

As a cohomological condition of the rigidity, we have the following result which
suggests that the rigidity of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator is a very strong condition.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
T
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Hom-associative algebra
A
with respect to a bimodule
M
. If
Z^{1}(M,A)=d_{H}(\mathrm{Nij}(T))
then
T
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}T_{i}
be any formal deformation of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
. Then by Proposition
4.13
, the linear term
T_{1}
is in
Z^{1}(M,A)
. Therefore, by assumption, we have
T_{1}=d_{H}(x)
, for some
x\in\text{Nij}(T)
. Set

\displaystyle f_{t}:=id_{A}+t(L_{x}-R_{x})\quad\text{ and }\quad g_{t}:=id_{M}+t(l_{x}-r_{x})

and define
\overline{T}_{t}=f_{t}\circ T_{t}\circ g_{t}^{-1}
. Then
T_{t}
is equivalent to
\overline{T}_{t}
. Moreover, from the definition of
\overline{T}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{t}(u)=(id_{A}+t(L_{x}-R_{x}))\circ(\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}T_{i})\circ(id_{M}-t(l_{x}-r_{x})+t^{2}(l_{x}-r_{x})^{2}-\cdots)(u),~~~\text{ for }u\in M.

Hence

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{t}(u)\quad(\text{mod }t^{2})=~

\displaystyle(id_{A}+t(L_{x}-R_{x}))\circ(T+tT_{1})(u-t(xu-ux))\quad(\text{mod }t^{2})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(id_{A}+t(L_{x}-R_{x}))(T(u)+tT_{1}(u)-tT(xu-ux))\quad(\text{mod }t^{2})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle T(u)+t\big(x\cdot T(u)-T(u)\cdot x-T(xu-ux)+T_{1}(u)\big).

The coefficient of
t
is zero as
T_{1}=d_{H}(x)
. See (
3.7
) for instance. Therefore,
\overline{T}_{t}
is of the form
\overline{T}_{t}=T+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}\overline{T}_{i}
. By repeating this argument, one get the equivalence between
T_{t}
and
T
. Hence the proof.
∎

4.3
Order
n
deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator

In this section, We introduce a cohomology
class associated to any order
n
deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator. We prove that
an order
n
deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator is extensible if and only if this cohomology
class is trivial. This cohomology class is called the obstruction class of an order
n

deformation being extensible.
Let
(A,\mu,\alpha)
be a Hom-associative algebra with a representation
(M,l,r,\phi)
.
Let
T:M\to A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A
with respect to
M
.
An order
n
deformation of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
is given by a
\mathbb{K}[[t]]/(t^{n+1})
-linear map

T_{t}=T+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{i}T_{i},\quad T_{i}\in C^{1}_{\phi,\alpha}(M,A)

such that

\displaystyle T_{t}(u)\cdot T_{t}(v)=T_{t}(T_{t}(u)v+uT_{t}(v))\quad\textrm{mod}(t^{n+1}),\ \forall u,v\in M.

Which is equivalent to the following system of equations

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=k}T_{i}(u)\cdot T_{j}(v)=\sum_{i+j=k}T_{i}(T_{j}(u)v+uT_{j}(v)),\quad\textrm{for any}\ k=0,1,\cdots n.

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\llbracket T,T_{k}\rrbracket_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=k,i,j\geqslant 1}\llbracket T_{i},T_{j}\rrbracket_{\alpha},\quad\textrm{for any}\ k=0,1,\cdots n.

(4.27)

Definition 4.20
.

Let
T_{t}=T+\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{i}T_{i}
be an order
n
deformation of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
on
A
with respect to
M
. If there exists a
1
-cochain
T_{n+1}\in C^{1}_{\phi,\alpha}(M,A)
such that
\widetilde{T_{t}}=T_{t}+t^{n+1}T_{n+1}
is an order
(n+1)
deformation of
T
, then we say that
T_{t}
extends to a deformation of order
(n+1)
.
In this case, we say that
T_{t}
is extendable.

Let us observe that the map
\widetilde{T}_{t}:=T_{t}+t^{n+1}T_{n+1}
is an extension of the order
n
deformation
T_{t}
if and only if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\llbracket T_{i},T_{j}\rrbracket_{\alpha}=0.

That is

\displaystyle\llbracket T,T_{n+1}\rrbracket_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n+1\\
i,j>0\end{subarray}}\llbracket T_{i},T_{j}\rrbracket_{\alpha}.

(4.28)

Definition 4.21
.

Let
T_{t}
be an order
n
deformation of the
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T
. Let us consider a
2
-cochain
\Theta_{T}\in C^{2}_{\phi,\alpha}(M,A)
defined as follows

\Theta_{T}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=n+1;~i,j>0}\llbracket T_{i},T_{j}\rrbracket_{\alpha}.

(4.29)

The
2
-cochain
\Theta_{T}
is called the obstruction cochain for extending the deformation
T_{t}
of order
n
to a deformation of order
n+1
.
From equation (
4.29
) and using graded Jacobi identity of the bracket
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket_{\alpha}
, it follows that
\Theta_{T}
is a
2
-cocycle.

The cohomology class
|\Theta_{T}|\in H^{2}(M,A)
is called the obstruction class of
T_{t}
being extendable.

Theorem 4.22
.

An order
n
deformation
T_{t}
extends to a deformation of next order if and only if the obstruction class
[\Theta_{T}]
is trivial.

Proof.

Suppose that a deformation of
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T_{t}
of order
n
extends to a deformation of order
n+1
. Then

\sum_{i+j=n+1}(T_{i}(u)\cdot T_{j}(v)-T_{i}(T_{j}(u)v+uT_{j}(v)))=0.

As a result, we get
\Theta_{T}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=n+1;~i,j>0}\llbracket T_{i},T_{j}\rrbracket_{\alpha}.
So, the obstruction class vanishes.
Conversely, if the obstruction class
|\Theta_{T}|
is trivial, suppose that
\Theta_{T}=-d_{T}\mathcal{T}_{n+1}
for some
1
-cochain
\mathcal{T}_{n+1}
.
Put
\tilde{T}_{t}=T_{t}+\mathcal{T}t^{n+1}
. Then
\tilde{T}
is a deformation of order
n+1
, which means that
T_{t}
is extendable.
∎

.

If
H^{2}(M,A)=0

T
extends to a deformation of next order.

Corollary 4.24
.

If
H^{2}(M,A)=0

1

-operator

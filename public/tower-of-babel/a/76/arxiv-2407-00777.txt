Toda and Laguerre–Freud equations for multiple discrete orthogonal polynomials with an arbitrary number of weights
Itsaso Fernández-Irisarri 1, Manuel Mañas 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we extend our investigation into semiclassical multiple discrete orthogonal polynomials by considering an arbitrary number of weights. We derive multiple versions of the Toda equations and the Laguerre-Freud equations for the multiple generalized Charlier and multiple generalized Meixner II families.

Discrete Multiple Orthogonal Polynomials and Pearson equation

2.1.
Pearson equations

From hereon we will assume that all the weights adhere to the Pearson equations, represented as:

(4)

\theta(x+1)w^{(a)}(x+1)=\sigma_{n}(x)w^{(a)}(x),

where
\theta(x)
and
\sigma_{a}(x)
are polynomials and
a\in\{1,\dots,p\}
.

First, we give an important relation fulfilled by the moment matrix
\mathscr{M}
:

Theorem 2.1
.

The moment matrix satisfies the following equation:

(5)

\theta(\Lambda)\mathscr{M}=L^{+}\mathscr{M}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{p}L^{(a)+}\sigma_{a}(\Lambda^{(n)})\right)^{\top}.

Proof.

Beginning with
\theta(\Lambda)\mathscr{M}
, we expand it as follows:

\displaystyle\theta(\Lambda)\mathscr{M}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\theta(k)X(k)\left(\sum^{p}_{a=1}w^{(a)}(k)X^{(a)}(k)\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\theta(k+1)X(k+1)\left(\sum^{p}_{a=1}w^{(a)}(k+1)X^{(a)}(k+1)\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}X(k+1)\left(\sum^{p}_{a=1}\sigma_{a}(k)w^{(a)}(k)X^{(a)}(k+1)\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}L^{+}X(k)\left(\sum^{p}_{a=1}\sigma_{a}(k)w^{(a)}(k)X^{(a)}(k)^{\top}(L^{(a)+})\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle=L^{+}\mathscr{M}\left(\sum^{p}_{a=1}L^{(a)+}\sigma_{a}(\Lambda^{(a)})\right)^{\top}.

∎

Then, we provide its counterpart for the Hessenberg recurrence matrix:

Proposition 2.2
.

The following equation holds for the Hessenberg matrix
T
if the weights satisfy the Pearson equations:

(6)

\Pi^{-}\theta(T)=\sum^{p}_{a=1}\sigma_{a}(T^{(a)})^{\top}(\Pi^{(a)+})^{\top}.

Proof.

Utilizing equation (
5
) and the Gauss-Borel factorization for the moment matrix, we have

\theta(\Lambda)S^{-1}H\tilde{S}^{-\top}=L^{+}S^{-1}H\tilde{S}^{-\top}\left(\sum^{p}_{a=1}L^{(a)+}\sigma_{a}(\Lambda^{(a)})\right)^{\top}.

If we multiply both sides by
S
on the right and by
\tilde{S}^{\top}H^{-1}
on the left, we obtain:

S\theta(\Lambda)S^{-1}=SL^{+}S^{-1}H\tilde{S}^{-\top}\sum^{p}_{a=1}\sigma_{a}(\Lambda^{(a)})^{\top}\tilde{S}^{\top}H^{-1}H\tilde{S}^{-\top}(L^{(a)+})^{\top}\left(H\tilde{S}^{-\top}\right)^{-1}.

Finally, we get

\theta(T)=\Pi^{+}\sum^{p}_{a=1}\sigma_{a}(T^{(a)})^{\top}(\Pi^{(a)+})^{\top}.

∎

\displaystyle=[\phi,\Psi],

(60)

\displaystyle\vartheta\Psi^{\top}

\displaystyle=[\mu,\Psi^{\top}]+\Psi^{\top},

where
\mu^{(n)}:=(\vartheta_{n}H^{-1})H+H^{-1}\tilde{\phi}^{(n)}H
and
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mu^{(i)}
.

Proof.

Starting from equations

\displaystyle\theta(z)B(z-1)

\displaystyle=\Psi B(z),

\displaystyle\vartheta B(z)

\displaystyle=\phi B(z),

we find:

\displaystyle\vartheta(\theta(z)B(z-1))

\displaystyle=\vartheta(\Psi B(z))=\vartheta\Psi B(z)+\Psi\vartheta B(z)=(\vartheta\Psi+\Psi\phi)B(z),

\displaystyle\vartheta(\theta(z)B(z-1))

\displaystyle=\theta(z)\phi B(z-1)=\phi\Psi B(z).

Equating both equations, we have:

\phi\Psi=\vartheta\Psi+\Psi\phi,

and isolating the term with the derivative of
\Psi
, we obtain (
59
).

To derive (
60
), we first prove that
\vartheta^{(m)}A^{(n)}=\mu^{(m)}A^{(n)}
:

\displaystyle\vartheta^{(m)}A^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\vartheta^{(m)}(H^{-1}\tilde{S}X^{(n)})

\displaystyle=((\vartheta^{(m)}H^{-1})HH^{-1}\tilde{S}+H^{-1}(\vartheta^{(m)}\tilde{S})(H^{-1}\tilde{S})^{-1}(H^{-1}\tilde{S}))X^{(n)}

\displaystyle=((\vartheta^{(m)}H^{-1})H+H^{-1}\tilde{\phi}^{(m)}H)A^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\mu^{(m)}A^{(n)}.

From this equation, we get
\sum_{i=1}^{p}\vartheta^{(i)}A^{(n)}=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu^{(i)}A^{(n)}\rightarrow\vartheta A^{(n)}=\mu A^{(n)}
.

Now, starting from equations

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(z)A^{(n)}(z+1)

\displaystyle=\Psi^{\top}A^{(n)}(z),

\displaystyle\vartheta A^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\mu A^{(n)},

we find:

\displaystyle\vartheta(\sigma_{n}(z)A^{(n)}(z+1))

\displaystyle=\vartheta(\Psi^{\top}A^{(n)})=(\vartheta\Psi^{\top})A^{(n)}+\Psi^{\top}\vartheta A^{(n)}=(\vartheta\Psi^{\top}+\Psi^{\top}\mu)A^{(n)},

\displaystyle\vartheta(\sigma_{n}(z)A^{(n)}(z+1))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n}(z)(\mu A^{(n)}(z+1)+A^{(n)}(z+1))=(\mu+I)\Psi^{\top})A^{(n)}(z),

so equating both equations and simplifying, we obtain:

\vartheta\Psi^{\top}=[\mu,\Psi^{\top}]+\Psi^{\top}.

∎

In the case of

Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, we continue our study of semiclassical multiple discrete orthogonal polynomials, now considering an arbitrary number of weights. We have derived multiple versions of the Toda equations and the Laguerre–Freud equations for the multiple generalized Charlier and multiple generalized Meixner II families.

The investigation of mixed multiple discrete orthogonal polynomials, along with the corresponding Toda and Laguerre-Freud equations, presents an intriguing area of research. Additionally, the exploration of Pearson equations in the multiple scenario for the unit circle appears to be both unexplored and promising.

Localization for Lipschitz monotone quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} via Rellich functions analysis
Hongyi Cao, Yunfeng Shi, Zhifei Zhang

Abstract
. We establish the Anderson localization and exponential dynamical localization for a class of quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with bounded or unbounded Lipschitz monotone potentials via multi-scale analysis based on Rellich function analysis in the perturbative regime. We show that at each scale, the resonant Rellich function uniformly inherits the Lipschitz monotonicity property of the potential via a novel Schur complement argument.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider a class of quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\displaystyle H(\theta)=\varepsilon\Delta+v(\theta+x\cdot{\omega})\delta_{x,y},\ x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(

1.2.
Main new ingredients of the proof

Our proof relies on Green’s function estimates via the multi-scale analysis in the perturbative regime originated from
[
FS83
]
. Of particular importance includes the analysis of Rellich functions in the spirit of
[
FSW90
,
FV21
,
CSZ23
,
CSZ24a
]
together with the adaptation of the Schur complement argument of Bourgain
[
Bou00
]
(c.f.
[
CSZ24b
]
for recent refinement). Although the general scheme of our proof seems expected, almost all of the known elements are not available and have to be replaced by new ingredients or a more complicated setup. The details will be explained as follows.

1.2.1.
Schur complement argument

In
[
Bou00
]
, Bourgain proved the
(\frac{1}{2}-)
-Hölder continuity of the integrated density of states (IDS) for almost Mathieu operator via introducing the Schur complement argument and Weierstrass preparation theorem. The key point is to analyze the roots of the determinants of the finite Dirichlet restriction of
H(\theta)
for a fixed energy
E
. Resonances at the
n
-scale induction there depend on the pairs of the roots
\{\theta_{n,1}(E),\theta_{n,2}(E)\}
. This celebrated method was later largely extended by the authors
[
CSZ24b
]
to prove arithmetic Anderson localization for multi-dimensional quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators with cosine potential. Definitely, all these estimates of
[
Bou00
,
CSZ24b
]
were obtained by fixing the energy parameter
E
and varying the phase parameters
\theta.
As we will see below, in the present work, we employ some type of Schur complement argument via varying energy parameters.

1.2.2.
Rellich functions analysis

In
[
FV21
]
, Forman and VandenBoom developed a novel inductive procedure by constructing the Rellich functions tree to handle the resonances depending on energy, thus removed the crucial symmetrical assumption in
[
FSW90
]
. This scheme was recently extended by the authors
[
CSZ24a
]
to the multi-dimensional case via overcoming the level crossing issue.
More precisely, for a finite subset
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, denote by
H_{\Lambda}(\theta)
the Dirichlet restriction of
H(\theta)
to
\Lambda
. It follows from the perturbation theory of one-parameter self-adjoint operator families that
H_{\Lambda}(\theta)
exhibits
|\Lambda|
branches of Rellich functions. The idea is then based on carefully choosing increasing scales of finite blocks and constructing each generation of the Rellich function, which is resonant with the previous generation.

The main issue is to verify that the Rellich functions maintain the crucial properties of
v
.

In the previous works
[
FV21
,
CSZ24a
]
, this issue was settled by eigenvector perturbation and eigenvalue variation arguments from
[
FSW90
]
. Concretely, restricting
H
to a fixed block
B
, one can try to fix an eigenvalue and its eigenvector and extend it by a small perturbation to a new eigenvalue and the corresponding eigenvector of a bigger block. Then one can use Feynman-Hellman eigenvalue variation formulas to compute the derivatives of the new eigenvalue parameterized by
\theta
. Together with the Cauchy interlacing idea originated from
[
FV21
]
, using the above two arguments can successfully yield the two-monotonicity Morse structure of Rellich functions for quasi-periodic Schrödinger operators with
C^{2}
-cosine type potentials (c.f.
[
CSZ24a
]
for details).

In the monotone potentials setting, the lack of resonances leads to obvious conveniences: One can easily establish the local Lipschitz monotone property of all branches of Rellich functions (c.f. (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) below), and establish the Lipschitz continuity of the integrated density of states by the argument of
[
Kac19
]
(c.f. Theorem 4.1 of
[
Kac19
]
). However, there comes the key obstacle for proving the localization, namely,
the presence of jump discontinuities of
v
at integer points, that will destroy the continuity of the Rellich function, making it extraordinary for the monotonicity property of the Rellich function to hold at these jump discontinuities
. This becomes a very delicate issue for following reasons: Since the size of the Dirichlet block will tend to infinity, one may eventually lose the separation and stability of eigenvalues, making the jump discontinuities unclear. Moreover, the Rellich function of a given block can be very close to other eigenvalues, in which case a rank one perturbation will totally change the order of eigenvalues, making it hard to compare the size of the Rellich function near these discontinuities (c.f. Remark
2.10
), where the previous eigenvector perturbation and eigenvalue variation ideas do not seem to work.

To analyze these jump discontinuities of the Rellich function in the present paper, we construct the the Rellich function in a more quantitative way.
We deal with the root of the characteristic polynomial of a certain scale Dirichlet restriction in a small neighborhood of the value of the Rellich function from the previous scale, and utilize Schur complement argument and Rouché theorem to obtain a unique eigenvalue, which is chosen to be the value of the present scale Rellich function (c.f. Proposition
2.29
). This idea is motivated by the work of Bourgain
[
Bou00
]
, which made use of the Schur complement as a perturbation of quadratic polynomial concerning the phase parameters
\theta
. The new aspect is that we regard the Schur complement as a perturbation of linear function concerning the energy parameters
E
(c.f. Lemma
2.31
)
. Now the issue of jump discontinuities of the Rellich function can be handled by comparing the roots of the two Schur complements with a disparity of a rank one perturbation due to the jump discontinuity of
v
. By carefully estimating the difference of the two Schur complements via the multi-scale analysis argument (c.f. Proposition
2.35
and Lemma
2.36
), we confirm the sign of this difference and ultimately prove that all the jump discontinuities except those at integer points of the Rellich function are non-negative. Hence each generation of the Rellich function maintains the uniform Lipschitz monotonicity property, which enables the whole induction.

1.3.
Outline of the inductive scheme

The main motivation of multi-scale analysis is to establish “good” Green’s function estimates for certain subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, which will be specified by “good” later. Results of this variety are well-established in the literature (c.f. e.g.
[
FS83
,
FV21
,
CSZ24a
]
). We sketch this scheme relative to our specific application for the reader’s convenience.

First, we fix a sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
depending on
L,d,\tau,\gamma
and let
\varepsilon
be such that
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. We choose
\delta_{0}:=\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{20}}\gg\varepsilon
to be the
0
-scale resonance parameter. For fixed
\theta
and
E
, the
0
-resonant points set will be defined as

S_{0}(\theta,E):=\left\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ |v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|<\delta_{0}\right\}.

For a finite set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define the Green’s function

G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E}:=\left(H_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E\right)^{-1}.

If a finite set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfies

\Lambda\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)=\emptyset,

(1.5)

then one can obtain the “good” estimates (i.e., one over the resonance bound of the operator norm and off-diagonal exponential decay of elements) of
G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E}
by the Neumann series argument. If (
1.5
) is not fulfilled, one can still try to estimate the Green’s function via the by-now classical resolvent identity iteration method, which roughly means that if for every
x\in\Lambda
, there exists a “good” subset
U(x)\subset\Lambda
with
x\in U(x)
such that
G_{U(x)}^{\theta,E}
has “good” estimates, then one can obtain “good” estimates of
G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E}
, with a small loss on the exponential decay rate, by iterating the resolvent identity. Hence to employ the resolvent identity method, one needs to associate a “good” block
U(x)
with every
x\in\Lambda\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)
. For this purpose, we will choose
l_{1}:=[|\ln\delta_{0}|^{4}]
(
[\cdot]
denotes the integer part) to be the first scale length and consider the block

B_{1}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ \|y-x\|_{1}\leq l_{1}\}

for each
x\in\Lambda\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)
.
Since the Dirichlet restrictions of the original operator satisfy the following translation property

H_{B_{1}(x)}(\theta)=H_{B_{1}}(\theta+x\cdot\omega),\ \ B_{1}:=B_{1}(o),

(1.6)

it suffices to consider
H_{B_{1}}(\theta)
for all
\theta
.
Since, by self-adjointness, we have

\|G_{B_{1}}^{\theta,E}\|=\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{Spec}(H_{B_{1}}(\theta),E))},

(1.7)

it follows that the issue of operator norm bound turns into the one of the difference between the eigenvalues of
H_{B_{1}}(\theta)
and
E
. Since
x\in\Lambda\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)
, it follows that
|v(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E|<\delta_{0}
. Thus by (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), it suffices to consider the eigenvalue of
H_{B_{1}}(\theta+x\cdot\omega)
that is close to
E
(hence is also close to
v(\theta+x\cdot\omega)
). Since Diophantine property of
\omega
and Lipschitz monotonicity property of
v
provide sufficiently large separation of resonant points, we can utilize the Schur complement argument and the smallness of
\varepsilon
to construct a unique Rellich function
E_{1}(\theta)
of
H_{B_{1}}(\theta)
in a certain neighborhood of
v(\theta)
. Moreover, we can verify that
E_{1}(\theta)
also satisfies the Lipschitz monotonicity property. Now the resonance depends on the difference
|E_{1}(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E|
and we classify the blocks
B_{1}(x)
as “good” or “bad” depending on the difference bigger or smaller than the first scale resonance parameter
\delta_{1}:=e^{-l_{1}^{2/3}}
. Moreover, by the special structure of
B_{1}(x)
, one can derive the off-diagonal exponential decay of the Green’s function from the sub-exponential operator norm bound via the resolvent identity argument. Hence we can establish “good” estimates of
G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E}
provided all the blocks
B_{1}(x)\subset\Lambda
with
x\in\Lambda\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)
being “good”, completing the first inductive step.

For the general inductive step, suppose that for
1\leq k\leq n
, we have inductively constructed increasing block
B_{k}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
centered at origin and the corresponding Rellich function
E_{k}(\theta)
satisfying the Lipschitz monotonicity property. Suppose further that we have established “good” estimates of
G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E}
for those
\Lambda
which do not contain any
k
-scale “bad” block
B_{k}(x)
with
|E_{k}(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E|
smaller than the
k
-scale resonance parameter
\delta_{k}=e^{-l_{k}^{2/3}}
. Then, since resonant points are uniformly separated by Diophantine property of
\omega
and Lipschitz monotonicity property of
E_{k}
, one can properly choose a bigger block
B_{n+1}
, and utilize the Schur complement argument together with the smallness of the perturbation term due to the off-diagonal exponential decay of Green’s function from the inductive hypothesis to construct a unique Rellich function
E_{n+1}(\theta)
of
H_{B_{n+1}}(\theta)
in a certain neighborhood of
E_{n}(\theta)
. Moreover, one can verify that
E_{n+1}(\theta)
also satisfies the Lipschitz monotonicity property. It remains to use resolvent identity to establish “good” estimates of
G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E}
for those
\Lambda
which do not contain any
(n+1)
-scale “bad” block
B_{n+1}(x)
with
|E_{n+1}(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E|<\delta_{n+1}
. Since
\varepsilon_{0}
is small, the loss of exponential decay rate in each scale is super-exponentially small, providing a uniformly positive decay rate.

Finally, the localization results follow from the Green’s function estimates for “good” sets, together with the uniform separation of resonant points due to Diophantine property of
\omega
and Lipschitz monotonicity property of Rellich functions by the standard Schnol’s type argument.

x\in\Lambda

|E-E^{*}|<\delta_{0}/5
, we have

|v(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|\geq|v(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E|-|E-E^{*}|\geq\delta_{0}-\delta_{0}/5>\delta_{0}/2.

Thus
V_{\Lambda}(\theta)
, the diagonal part of
H_{\Lambda}(\theta)
, satisfies
\|(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*})^{-1}\|\leq 2\delta_{0}^{-1}
. Since
\|\Delta\|\leq 2d
, it follows that

\varepsilon\|\left(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}\Delta\|\leq 4d\varepsilon\delta_{0}^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Hence by Neumann series, we have

\displaystyle\left(H_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon\Delta+V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\varepsilon^{l}\left[\left(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}\Delta\right]^{l}\left(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}.

Thus

\displaystyle\|\left(H_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{4d\varepsilon}{\delta_{0}}\right)^{l}\|\left(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\left(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq 4\delta_{0}^{-1}.

Since
\Delta
has
only nearest neighbor matrix elements, provided
\|x-y\|_{1}\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left(H_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=\|x-y\|_{1}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{4d\varepsilon}{\delta_{0}}\right)^{l}\|\left(V_{\Lambda}(\theta)-E^{*}\right)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\delta_{0}}\left(\frac{4d\varepsilon}{\delta_{0}}\right)^{\|x-y\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(\frac{3}{2}\|x-y\|_{1}-\frac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-y\|_{1}}.

∎

2.2.
The first inductive scale

Let
l_{1}:=[|\ln\delta_{0}|^{4}]
be the first inductive length and
B_{1}=Q_{l_{1}}
be the first scale block.
Recall that
H_{B_{1}}(\theta)
is the Dirichlet restriction of
H(\theta)
on
\ell^{2}(B_{1})
. Listing the eigenvalues in non-decreasing order,
H_{B_{1}}(\theta)
has
|B_{1}|
branches of Rellich functions
\lambda_{i}(\theta)

(1\leq i\leq|B_{1}|)
. By the perturbation theory (Min-Max argument) for self-adjoint operators, the Rellich functions
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
are
1
-periodic and continuous on
[0,1)
except on the finite set of points
0=\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\cdots<\alpha_{|B_{1}|}<\alpha_{|B_{1}|+1}=1
, where

\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{|B_{1}|}\}=\{\{-x\cdot\omega\}:\ x\in B_{1}\}

with
\{-x\cdot\omega\}
being the decimal part of
-x\cdot\omega
.
Define

\beta_{x}:=\{-x\cdot\omega\}.

From the equation

H_{B_{1}}(\beta_{x})-H_{B_{1}}(\beta_{x}-0)=-{\bm{e}}_{x}{\bm{e}}_{x}^{\rm T},

it follows that all discontinuities of
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
are caused by negative rank one perturbations due to the
x
-site diagonal element of the matrix jumping from
1
to
0
, as the jumps at finite energies will affect all eigenvalues. Lipschitz monotonicity property (
1.3
) implies the operator inequality

H_{B_{1}}(\theta_{2})-H_{B_{1}}(\theta_{1})\geq L(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})\operatorname{Id}_{B_{1}},\ \ \alpha_{k}\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}<\alpha_{k+1},

(2.1)

where
\operatorname{Id}_{B_{1}}
is the identity operator on
\ell^{2}(B_{1})
.
Hence it follows from the Min-Max argument that the Rellich functions
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
also satisfy the local Lipschitz monotonicity property

\lambda_{i}(\theta_{2})-\lambda_{i}(\theta_{1})\geq L(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}),\ \ \alpha_{k}\leq\theta_{1}\leq\theta_{2}<\alpha_{k+1}.

(2.2)

The goal of this section is to construct the first generation Rellich function
E_{1}(\theta)
, which is a
1
-periodic, single-valued, real-valued Rellich function of
H_{B_{1}}(\theta)
. The construction is done by locally choosing the unique branch of
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
which is resonant with the previous scale Rellich function
E_{0}(\theta)
. We will prove that in our construction, on each small interval
[\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k+1})
,
E_{1}(\theta)
coincides with one branch of
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
, and more importantly,
E_{1}(\theta)
has non-negative jumps at
\alpha_{k}
except
\alpha_{1}(=0)
, that is,

E_{1}(\alpha_{k})\geq E_{1}(\alpha_{k}-0),\ \ 2\leq k\leq|B_{1}|.

It may seem strange at the first sight that the Rellich function
E_{1}(\theta)
has non-negative jumps since negative rank one perturbations will cause all the eigenvalues to drop down. The reason of these non-negative jumps is that
E_{1}(\theta)
coincides with different branches of
\lambda_{i}(\theta)
before and after
\alpha_{k}
. Hence
E_{1}(\theta)
maintains the Lipschitz monotonicity property from
v(\theta)
on the whole interval
[0,1)
. After constructing
E_{1}(\theta)
and verifying the properties of it, for fixed
\theta
and
E
, we relate the first scale resonant points
p\in S_{1}(\theta,E)
to the difference
|E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|
and prove good Green’s function estimates for
1
-good set.

after adding an
\varepsilon
-perturbative Laplacian.
Thus one can verify that
E_{1}(\theta)
coincides with
\lambda_{k}(\theta)
(the
k
-branch of Rellich functions in non-decreasing order) on
[\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k+1})
. The non-negative “jumps” at
\alpha_{k}
follow from a min-max principle argument for rank one perturbation.
However, this easier idea seems difficult to be extended to large scale case since (
2.22
) will be violated as the size of the block increases.

2.2.3.
Green’s function estimates for
1
-good sets

In this part, we relate the resonances to
E_{1}(\theta)
and establish Green’s function estimates for
1
-good sets.
For
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote

B_{1}(p):=B_{1}+p.

Definition 2.11
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
E\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\delta_{1}:=e^{-l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}}
. Define the first scale resonant points set (related to
(\theta,E)
) as

S_{1}(\theta,E):=\left\{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ |E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|<\delta_{1}\right\}.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be a finite set. Related to
(\theta,E)
, we say

•

\Lambda
is
1
-nonresonant if
\Lambda\cap S_{1}(\theta,E)=\emptyset
.

•

\Lambda
is
1
-regular if
p\in\Lambda\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)

\Rightarrow

B_{1}(p)\subset\Lambda
.

•

\Lambda
is
1
-good if it is both
1
-nonresonant and
1
-regular.

Lemma 2.12
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
E\in\mathbb{C}
. If
p\in S_{0}(\theta,E)
and
p\notin S_{1}(\theta,E)
, then for any
E^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|E-E^{*}|<\delta_{1}/2
, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq 10\delta_{1}^{-1},

(2.23)

\displaystyle|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(1-l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{50}})\|x-y\|_{1}},\ \|x-y\|_{1}\geq l_{1}^{\frac{4}{5}}.

(2.24)

Proof.

Since
p\in S_{0}(\theta,E)
, by definition we have
|v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|\leq\delta_{0}
. Thus

|v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|\leq|v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|+|E-E^{*}|\leq\delta_{0}+\delta_{1}/2.

By Proposition
2.3
and translation property,
E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\theta)
is the unique eigenvalue of
H_{B_{1}(p)}(\theta)
in
[v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-10\delta_{0},v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)+10\delta_{0}]
and moreover

\displaystyle\ |E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|

\displaystyle\leq|v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|+|E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{0}+\delta_{1}/2+\varepsilon

\displaystyle<2\delta_{0}.

Hence we get

\operatorname{dist}\left(\operatorname{Spec}(H_{B_{1}(p)}(\theta)),E^{*}\right)=|E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|.

Since
p\notin S_{1}(\theta,E)
, we have

|E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|\geq|E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|-|E-E^{*}|\geq\delta_{1}-\delta_{1}/2\geq\delta_{1}/2.

Since
H_{B_{1}(p)}(\theta)
is self-adjoint, we get

\|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}\|=\frac{1}{\operatorname{dist}\left(\operatorname{Spec}(H_{B_{1}(p)}(\theta)),E^{*}\right)}=\frac{1}{|E_{1}(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E^{*}|}\leq 2\delta_{1}^{-1}.

Thus we finish the proof of (
2.23
).
Denote
B_{1}^{o}(p):=B_{1}(p)\setminus\{p\}
. By Lemma
2.2
, it follows that for any
x\in B_{1}^{o}(p)
, we have

\displaystyle|v(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-E|

\displaystyle\geq|v(\theta+x\cdot\omega)-v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)|-|v(\theta+p\cdot\omega)-E|

\displaystyle\geq 20\delta_{0}-\delta_{0}

\displaystyle>\delta_{0}.

Thus
B_{1}^{o}(p)\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)=\emptyset
. Hence by Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|G_{B_{1}^{o}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq 10\delta_{0}^{-1},

\displaystyle|G_{B_{1}^{o}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-y\|_{1}},\ \|x-y\|_{1}\geq 1.

(2.25)

Let
x,y\in B_{1}(p)
such that
\|x-y\|_{1}\geq l_{1}^{\frac{4}{5}}
. By self-adjointness, we may assume
\|x-p\|_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2}l_{1}^{\frac{4}{5}}
.
Recall the definition of
\partial
and
\partial^{\pm}
. By the resolvent identity, one has

\displaystyle G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,y)=\chi_{B_{1}^{o}(p)}(y)G_{B_{1}^{o}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,y)-\varepsilon\sum_{(w,w^{\prime})\in\partial_{B_{1}(p)}B_{1}^{o}(p)}G_{B_{1}^{o}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,w)G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(w^{\prime},y).

(2.26)

Since
w\in\partial^{-}_{B_{1}(p)}B_{1}^{o}(p)
,
w^{\prime}\in\partial^{+}_{B_{1}(p)}B_{1}^{o}(p)=\{p\}
, one has

\|x-w\|_{1}\geq\|x-p\|_{1}-\|w-p\|_{1}\geq\|x-p\|_{1}-1.

Hence by (
2.25
) and (
2.26
), we have

|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,y)|\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-y\|_{1}}+e^{-\gamma_{0}(\|x-p\|_{1}-1)}|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(p,y)|.

(2.27)

•

If
\|y-p\|_{1}\leq l_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}
, then

\displaystyle\|x-p\|_{1}-1

\displaystyle\geq\|x-y\|_{1}-\|y-p\|_{1}-1

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-(\lambda_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}+1)l_{1}^{-\frac{4}{5}}\right)\|x-y\|_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-2l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{20}}\right)\|x-y\|_{1}.

Bounding the term
|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(p,y)|
in (
2.27
) by
\|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}\|\leq 10\delta_{1}^{-1}=10e^{l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}}
, we get

(
2.27
)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-y\|_{1}}+10e^{-\gamma_{0}(1-2l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{20}})\|x-y\|_{1}}e^{l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(1-2l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{20}}-(l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}+10)l_{1}^{-\frac{4}{5}})\|x-y\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(1-l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{50}})\|x-y\|_{1}}.

•

If
\|y-p\|_{1}>l_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}
, we can use (
2.23
), (
2.25
) and the resolvent identity to bound the term
|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(p,y)|
in (
2.27
) by

\displaystyle|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(p,y)|=|G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(y,p)|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\sum_{(w,w^{\prime})\in\partial_{B_{1}(p)}B_{1}^{o}(p)}|G_{B_{1}^{o}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(y,w)G_{B_{1}(p)}^{\theta,E^{*}}(w^{\prime},p)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(\|y-p\|_{1}-1)}\delta_{1}^{-1}.

Thus

(
2.27
)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-y\|_{1}}+e^{-\gamma_{0}(\|x-p\|_{1}-1)}e^{-\gamma_{0}(\|y-p\|_{1}-1)}\delta_{1}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(1-(l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}+10)l_{1}^{-\frac{4}{5}})\|x-y\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{0}(1-l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{50}})\|x-y\|_{1}}.

Thus we finish the proof of (
2.24
).
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Fix
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
E\in\mathbb{C}
. Assume that a finite set
\Lambda
is
1
-good related to
(\theta,E)
, then for any
E^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|E-E^{*}|<\delta_{1}/5

\displaystyle\|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq 10\delta_{1}^{-1},

(2.28)

\displaystyle|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{*}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{1}\|x-y\|_{1}},\ \|x-y\|_{1}\geq l_{1}^{\frac{5}{6}},

(2.29)

where
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{0}(1-l_{1}^{-\frac{1}{80}})

Proof.

Define the set

. If
S_{0}^{\Lambda}=\emptyset
, then (
2.28
) and (
2.29
) follows from Proposition
2.1
immediately. Thus we assume that

.
To prove (
2.28
), by self-adjointness it suffices to prove

\operatorname{dist}\left(\operatorname{Spec}(H_{\Lambda}(\theta)),E\right)\geq\delta_{1}/2.

(2.30)

Fix
E^{\prime}
such that
|E^{\prime}-E|<\delta_{1}/2
and
E^{\prime}\notin\operatorname{Spec}(H_{\Lambda}(\theta))
.
For any
p\in S_{0}^{\Lambda}
, since
\Lambda
is
1
-regular, it follows that
B_{1}(p)\subset\Lambda
. Since
\Lambda
is
1
-nonresonant, it follows that

2.12
holds true for such

.
Denote
\Lambda_{0}:=\Lambda\setminus S_{0}^{\Lambda}
. Then
\Lambda_{0}\cap S_{0}(\theta,E)=\emptyset
and hence Proposition
2.1
holds true for
\Lambda_{0}
.

•

Let
x\in\Lambda
such that
\operatorname{dist}_{1}(x,S_{0}^{\Lambda})\geq l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}
. By the resolvent identity, one has

G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)=\chi_{\Lambda_{0}}(y)G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)-\varepsilon\sum_{(w,w^{\prime})\in\partial_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{0}}G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,w)G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y).

Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\Lambda}|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{0}}|G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)|+\varepsilon\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(w,w^{\prime})\in\partial_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{0}\\
y\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}|G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,w)||G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y)|.

(2.31)

Since
|E-E^{\prime}|<\delta_{1}/2
, by Proposition
2.1
, the first summation on the right-hand side of (
2.31
) satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{0}}|G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\|G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}\|+\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{0}:\|x-y\|_{1}\geq 1}|G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq 10\delta_{0}^{-1}+\sum_{y:\|x-y\|_{1}\geq 1}e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-y\|_{1}}

In the second summation on the right-hand side of (

), since

It follows that

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(w,w^{\prime})\in\partial_{\Lambda}\Lambda_{0}\\
y\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}|G_{\Lambda_{0}}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,w)||G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2d\varepsilon\sum_{w\in\partial_{\Lambda}^{-}\Lambda_{0}}e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-w\|_{1}}\sup_{w^{\prime}\in\partial_{\Lambda}^{+}\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{y}|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y)|

\displaystyle 2d\varepsilon\sum_{{w:\|x-w\|_{1}\geq l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}-1}}e^{-\gamma_{0}\|x-w\|_{1}}\sup_{w^{\prime}\in\partial_{\Lambda}^{+}\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{y}|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sup_{w^{\prime}\in\Lambda}\sum_{y}|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y)|.

Hence,

\sup_{x:\operatorname{dist}_{1}(x,S_{0}^{\Lambda})\geq l_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(x,y)|\leq 20\delta_{0}^{-1}+\frac{1}{4}\sup_{w^{\prime}\in\Lambda}\sum_{y\in\Lambda}|G_{\Lambda}^{\theta,E^{\prime}}(w^{\prime},y)|.

(2.32)

•

x\in\Lambda

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
eigenfunction of
H(\tilde{\theta})
with
\tilde{\theta}=\theta+x_{\theta,s}\cdot\omega
.
By Lemma
4.12
, we have

|\tilde{\psi}(x)|\leq C(\varepsilon)|\tilde{\psi}(o)|e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x\|_{1}}.

Hence

|\psi_{\theta,s}(x+x_{\theta,s})|\leq C(\varepsilon)|\psi_{\theta,s}(x_{\theta,s})|e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x\|_{1}}.

Rewriting the above inequality yields (
4.17
).
∎

It follows from (
4.17
) and Bessel inequality that

\displaystyle\sum_{s:x_{\theta,s}=p}|\psi_{\theta,s}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{s:x_{\theta,s}=p}|\psi_{\theta,s}(p)|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{200}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-p\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{200}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-p\|_{1}}.

(4.18)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{s:x_{\theta,s}=p}|\psi_{\theta,s}(y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{s:x_{\theta,s}=p}|\psi_{\theta,s}(p)|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{200}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|y-p\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{200}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|y-p\|_{1}}.

(4.19)

It follows from (
4.11
), (
4.18
), (
4.19
) that

\displaystyle|\langle e^{itH(\theta)}{\bm{e}}_{x},{\bm{e}}_{y}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-p\|_{1}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|y-p\|_{1}}

\displaystyle=C(\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|p\|_{1}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|y-x-p\|_{1}}

\displaystyle=C(\varepsilon)^{2}\left(\sum_{\|p\|_{1}\geq\|x-y\|_{1}}+\sum_{\|p\|_{1}<\|x-y\|_{1}}\right).

(4.20)

The first summation in (
4.20
) is bounded by

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}\sum_{\|p\|_{1}\geq\|x-y\|_{1}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|y-x-p\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|p\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}

(4.21)

with

C_{1}=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|p\|_{1}}\leq\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-\frac{1}{10}\|p\|_{1}}.

The second summation in (
4.20
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{\|p\|_{1}<\|x-y\|_{1}}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(2\|x-y\|_{1})^{d}e^{-\frac{1}{400}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}e^{-\frac{1}{800}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}

(4.22)

with

C_{2}=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{(2\|p\|_{1})^{d}}{e^{\frac{1}{800}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|p\|_{1}}}\leq\sup_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{(2\|p\|_{1})^{d}}{e^{\frac{1}{20}\|p\|_{1}}}.

It follows from (
4.20
), (
4.21
) and (
4.22
) that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\langle e^{itH(\theta)}{\bm{e}}_{x},{\bm{e}}_{y}\rangle|\leq C(\varepsilon)^{2}(C_{1}+C_{2})e^{-\frac{1}{800}|\ln\varepsilon|\cdot\|x-y\|_{1}}.

This completes the proof of exponential dynamical localization.

Zeros of higher derivatives of Riemann zeta function
Mithun Kumar Das, Sudhir Pujahari

Abstract
. In this article, we extend the result of Conrey [ 5 , Theorem 2] to shorter intervals for higher-order derivatives of the zeta function. That is we study the mean value of the product of two finite order derivatives of the zeta function multiplied by a mollifier in short intervals. In this process, we obtain better mollifier length in some short intervals compared to the length of mollifier implied by Conrey’s result. These finer studies allow us to refine the error term of some classical results of Levinson and Montgomery [ 14 ] , Ki and Lee [ 12 ] on zero density estimates of ζ ( k ) \zeta^{(k)} . Further, we showed that almost all non-trivial zeros of Matsumoto-Tanigawa’s η k \eta_{k} -function cluster near the critical line.

1.1.
Zeros of derivatives of zeta functions

Let
s=\sigma+it
denote a complex variable, and
\zeta(s)
be the Riemann zeta function, which satisfies the functional equation

h(s)\zeta(s)=h(1-s)\zeta(1-s),

where

The famous Riemann hypothesis, predicted by Riemann in 1859, says that all the complex zeros of the zeta function lie on the critical line
\operatorname{Re}(s)=\frac{1}{2}.
From the work of Speiser
[
16
]
, we know that the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the fact that the derivative of the zeta function does not have zeros in the strip
0<\operatorname{Re}(s)<\frac{1}{2}
. The distribution of zeros of
\zeta^{(k)}
has been extensively studied in the literature (see
[
4
]
,
[
17
]

[
18
]
,
[
19
]

[
20
]
).

In 1974, Levinson and Montgomery
[
14
]
conducted an extensive examination of the distribution of zeros pertaining to the higher derivatives of the zeta function. Specifically, they established that if
\rho_{k}=\beta_{k}+i\gamma_{k}
represents the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta^{(k)},k\geq 1
and
\operatorname{Li}(x)=\int_{2}^{x}(\operatorname{log}{t})^{-1}dt
, then, under the assumption of the Riemann hypothesis, the following expression holds:

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\gamma_{k}\leq T\\
\beta_{k}>\frac{1}{2}\end{subarray}}\Big(\beta_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)=k\frac{T}{2\pi}\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}{\frac{T}{2\pi}}+\frac{T\operatorname{log}{2}}{4\pi}-\frac{T}{2\pi}k\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}{2}-k\operatorname{Li}\Big(\frac{T}{2\pi}\Big)+\operatorname{O}(\operatorname{log}{T}).

Furthermore, they established that
\zeta^{(k)}(s)
, where
k\geq 1
, possesses only a finite number of complex zeros in the region
\operatorname{Re}(s)<\frac{1}{2}
. Unconditionally they demonstrated that for
T^{a}\leq U\leq T
,
a>\frac{1}{2}
,

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\leq\gamma_{1}\leq T+U\\
\beta_{1}>\frac{1}{2}\end{subarray}}\Big(\beta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)=\frac{U}{2\pi}\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}{\frac{T}{2\pi}}+\operatorname{O}(U)\mbox{ and }\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\leq\gamma_{1}\leq T+U\\
\beta_{1}<\frac{1}{2}\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\beta_{1}\Big)=\operatorname{O}(U).

Recently, Ki and Lee
[
12
]
extended the above unconditional result (
2

T^{a}\leq U\leq T
,
a>\frac{1}{2}
,

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\leq\gamma_{k}\leq T+U\\
\beta_{k}>\frac{1}{2}\end{subarray}}\Big(\beta_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)=k\frac{U}{2\pi}\operatorname{log}\operatorname{log}{\frac{T}{2\pi}}+\operatorname{O}(U)\mbox{ and }\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\leq\gamma_{k}\leq T+U\\
\beta_{k}<\frac{1}{2}\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\beta_{k}\Big)=\operatorname{O}(U).

In
[
8

\zeta^{k}(s)
are in the region

Furthermore, assuming the Riemann hypothesis, they demonstrated that, for any

possesses

\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma<\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(1+\epsilon)\operatorname{log}\,\operatorname{log}\,T}{\operatorname{log}\,T},\ \ 0<t<T.

Methodology

In
[
14

applied the Littlewood lemma on a function which is a linear combination of derivatives of

is a mollifier and apply Littlewood lemma, this refines the explicit constant of the error terms. The main technique of our method is the asymptotic estimate of the mean square of the product of finite order derivatives of the Riemann zeta function with mollifier within short intervals. This mean square result obtain here in connection with the technique developed in our previous paper

1.2.

\zeta^{(k)}

In 1989, Conrey

Let

\displaystyle c(P,Q,R)=|P(1)Q(0)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2Ry}|Q(y)P^{\prime}(x)+\theta Q^{\prime}(y)P(x)+\theta RQ(y)P(x)|^{2}dxdy.

Van der Waals Anomaly
Itai Y. Efrat and Ulf Leonhardt

Abstract
In inhomogeneous dielectric media the divergence of the electromagnetic stress is related to the gradients of ε \varepsilon and μ \mu , which is a consequence of Maxwell’s equations. Investigating spherically symmetric media we show that this seemingly universal relationship is violated for electromagnetic vacuum forces such as the generalized van der Waals and Casimir forces. The stress needs to acquire an additional anomalous pressure. The anomaly is a result of renormalization, the need to subtract infinities in the stress for getting a finite, physical force. The anomalous pressure appears in the stress in media like dark energy appears in the energy-momentum tensor in general relativity. We propose and analyse an experiment to probe the van der Waals anomaly with ultracold atoms. The experiment may not only test an unusual phenomenon of quantum forces, but also an analogue of dark energy, shedding light where nothing is known empirically.

III
Renormalization

The field of Casimir physics began with Casimir’s three–page paper

Renormalization is necessary, because the bare stress calculated, for example, using the procedure of Sec. II is infinite. One may view the infinity of the bare vacuum energy and stress as an inevitable feature of quantum field theory or as an artefact of the theory. IV
Possible experiment

Trace anomalies have been theoretically predicted a considerable time ago

At first glance, alkali condensates might not appear as the most likely candidates for demonstrating delicate dielectric effects, as they are dilute gases with low density and hence low dielectric response, but the possibility to optically excite and probe elementary excitations with well–defined spatial structures may very well outweigh this disadvantage.

IV.1

Let us first discuss some consequences of the diluteness of Bose Einstein condensates within the context of the van der Waals anomaly that apply to other dilute gases as well. The renormalized vacuum stresses

According to Barrera

\displaystyle\bm{r}\times\nabla Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,,

Erdelyi

E.2
Decomposition

Any vector function
\bm{V}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle\bm{V}(\theta,\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\Bigl(A_{lm}\bm{Y}_{lm}(\theta,\phi)+B_{lm}\bm{\Phi}_{lm}(\theta,\phi)

(133)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,C_{lm}\bm{\Psi}_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\Bigr)

with the coefficients given by
Barrera

\displaystyle A_{lm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\bm{V}(\theta,\phi)\cdot\bm{Y^{*}}_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,d\Omega\,,

\displaystyle B_{lm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l(l+1)}\int\bm{V}(\theta,\phi)\cdot\bm{\Phi^{*}}_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,d\Omega\,,

\displaystyle C_{lm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l(l+1)}\int\bm{V}(\theta,\phi)\cdot\bm{\Psi^{*}}_{lm}(\theta,\phi)\,d\Omega\,.

(134)

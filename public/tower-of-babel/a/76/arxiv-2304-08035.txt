A source identification problem in a bi-parabolic equation: convergence rates and some optimal results
Subhankar Mondal and M. Thamban Nair

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with identification of a spatial source function from final time observation in a bi-parabolic equation, where the full source function is assumed to be a product of time dependent and a space dependent function. Due to the ill-posedness of the problem, recently some authors have employed different regularization method and analysed the convergence rates. But, to the best of our knowledge, the quasi-reversibility method is not explored yet, and thus we study that in this paper. As an important implication, the Hölder rates for the apriori and aposteriori error estimates obtained in this paper improve upon the rates obtained in earlier works. Also, in some cases we show that the rates obtained are of optimal order. Further, this work seems to be the first one that has broaden the applicability of the problem by allowing the time dependent component of the source function to change sign. To the best of our knowledge, the earlier known work assumed the fixed sign of the time dependent component by assuming some bounded below condition.



Keywords:
bi-parabolic equation, source identification, quasi-reversibility method, apriori and aposteriori parameter choice, optimal rate

1.
Introduction

In the theory of heat transfers, the bi-parabolic equations have many applications
[
28
]
. It is reported, for instance in
[
11
,
19
]
, that the classical parabolic equations fail to describe the heat conduction processes accurately. To overcome this shortcoming, several models were proposed which include the bi-parabolic model proposed in
[
12
]
. In addition to this, bi-parabolic models are used to describe the special phenomenon of process dynamics filter image
[
20
]
and also to model various natural phenomena which includes ice formation, fluid on the lungs, design of distinctive curves on the surface
[
13
]
. Thus, from application point of view the bi-parabolic systems are interesting and relevant topic.

In this paper, we consider an inverse source identification problem associated with a bi-parabolic system. More precisely, let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}(d\geq 1),
be a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
and
\tau>0
be fixed. We consider the following system

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-2\Delta u_{t}+\Delta^{2}u=\psi(t)f(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
u=0=\Delta u,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
u(\cdot,0)=0=u_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u(\cdot,\tau)=h,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\psi\in C([0,\tau])
and
f,h\in L^{2}(\Omega).
The inverse problem is to identify the spatially dependent exact source
f
from the knowledge of exact final time observation
h.
But in practice since we will always have access to a measurement data of the final value, noise in the data is inevitable. In this paper, we consider the deterministic noise model. That is, for
\delta>0,
let
h^{\delta}
be the measured final time noisy observation satisfying

(1.2)

\|h-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\delta.

This type of problem belongs to the class of inverse source identification problem which has a vast literature in the area of inverse problems (cf.
[
18
]
) because of their tremendous real world applications. Indeed, the source identification problem is used in detecting the source of pollution in water surfaces or atmospheric media
[
2
]
, and also for geophysical prospecting
[
23
]
, to name a few.

The inverse source problem that is considered in this paper is known to be ill-posed
[
30
]
, in the sense that a small perturbation in the final time observation may lead to a large deviation in the corresponding source function and thus, some regularization method (cf.
[
9
,
25
]
) has to be employed in order to obtain some stable approximations for the source function. For
\psi\equiv 1
in
[0,\tau]
, an iterative regularization method has been proposed in
[
38
]
to obtain stable approximations for the identification of the spatially dependent source function. For the general source term of the separable form that is considered in this paper, it seems the work on the regularization aspect is in the initial stage. To the best of our knowledge, the very recent paper of Quoc Nam et al.
[
30
]
is the first paper where the authors considered the same problem of source identification that is considered here, and have employed the Tikhonov regularization method for the regularization purpose. Subsequently, in a follow-up work, Tuan
[
35
]
has used a Fourier truncation method for the regularization purpose again for a similar source identification problem and analysed the case when the observed data is in any
L^{p}
spaces. In
[
29
]
the authors employed the quasi-boundary value method for the regularization purpose for the source identification problem that is considered in this paper. To the best of our knowledge, another important type of regularization method, namely the quasi reversibility method (QRM) has not been explored yet in the setting of source identification associated with the bi-parabolic equation (
1.1
). In this paper we employ the QRM for the regularization purpose and obtain the error estimates.

The QRM was popularised by Lattès and Lions
[
21
]
. The idea behind this method is to perturb the governing differential equation by a suitable operator so that solving the resulting problem becomes a well-posed problem. More specifically, in
[
21
]
the authors considered a final value problem of the form

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\mathcal{L}(t)v=0\\
v(\tau)=v_{\tau}\end{cases}

and considered the problem of identifying the initial value
v(0),
where the operator
\mathcal{L}(t)
is a spatial differential operator, for example, one may have
\mathcal{L}(t)
to be the negative spatial Laplacian
-\Delta
or of the form
-\nabla\cdot a(x,t)\nabla
, where
a(x,t)
is sufficiently smooth. In order to solve the backward problem the authors proposed the following perturbed equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\epsilon}+\mathcal{L}(t)v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}^{*}(t)\mathcal{L}(t)v_{\epsilon}=0\\
v_{\epsilon}(\tau)=v^{\epsilon}_{\tau}\end{cases}

for
\epsilon>0
. The problem of obtaining the initial value
v_{\epsilon}(0)
from the final value
v^{\epsilon}_{\tau}
is a well-posed problem and
v_{\epsilon}(0)
gives an approximation of
v(0)
in some suitable topology. Since the work in
[
21
]
, several researchers have analysed the QRM in a wide range of problems. The application of QRM can be found, for example, in
[
3
]
for the elliptic setting,
[
7
,
24
,
27
,
32
,
36
]
for the parabolic setting and
[
22
,
26
]
for the hyperbolic setting. Also, with the increasing work in the backward time fractional diffusion equation, the QRM has found its application in several recent works related to time/space fractional setting, see for instance
[
8
,
37
]
. For more details on QRM and its several implications and modifications, the interested reader may refer to
[
1
,
4
,
5
,
17
,
31
]
.

Motivated by the recent work of Duc et al.
[
8
]
, where a QRM is analysed for a source identification problem associated with a time-space fractional parabolic equation, we analyse a QRM for the inverse problem considered in this paper. More precisely, for
b\geq 2
and
\alpha>0,
we consider the following bi-parabolic system

\begin{cases}v_{tt}-2\Delta v_{t}+\Delta^{2}v=(I+\alpha(-\Delta)^{b})\psi(t)\tilde{f}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v=0=\Delta v,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v(\cdot,0)=0=v_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
v(\cdot,\tau)=\tilde{h},&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

where
I:L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))\to L^{2}(0,\tau;L^{2}(\Omega))
is the identity map, and consider the problem of identifying
\tilde{f}
from the final time measurement
\tilde{h}
using the
mild solution
(see Definition
4.2
). We show that this is an well-posed problem, and the obtained solutions are indeed an approximations for the exact source
f.

We now point out the novelty and main contributions of this paper.

•

In all the earlier known works
[
30
,
35
,
29
]
that considered the identification of
f
from
h
associated with the bi-parabolic system (
1.1
), the analysis has been done by assuming a bounded below assumption on
\psi,
the time dependent component of the source, which ensures a fixed sign property for
\psi
. In contrast to this, our analysis works for a more general case, which includes the fixed sign case and also allows
\psi
to change sign (see Assumption
3.1
). In that sense, this work seems to be the first paper that allows
\psi
to change sign in the context of the considered inverse problem.

•

This work seems to be the first work where the QRM is analysed for the regularization purpose in the context of the bi-parabolic system (
1.1
). As mentioned earlier, the Tikhonov regularization method, quasi-boundary value method and the Fourier truncation method have already been analysed by some authors for the same problem.

•

We obtain Hölder type error estimates for both the apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies using a conditional stability estimates. Under some additional assumptions on
b
, the order of the error estimates in apriori case is better than the previously known rates (see Remarks
4.5
and
4.8
).

•

We show that in some cases the obtained error estimates are of optimal order (in the sense of
worst case error
) for both the apriori and aposteriori cases. This fills the gap in the arguments for the order optimality claim made in
[
8
,
15
]
(see Remark
5.5
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall all the necessary results and state some facts that will be used throughout in this paper. Section
3
perhaps is the base of the paper. In this section, we specify certain assumptions on
\psi
, see their consequences and as a by product we obtain a conditional stability estimate. In Section
4
we consider the regularization by quasi-reversibility method, obtain error estimates for both apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies. Finally, in Section
5
we prove that some of the error estimate that we obtain are of optimal order.

2.
preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic results from the spectral theory of elliptic PDE, and discuss about the solutions associated to (
1.1
), and also present some basic results that will be used later.

First, let us fix some notations that will be used throughout.

•

Let
\mathbb{X}
denote the space of real-valued functions defined on
\Omega
, for e.g.,
L^{2}(\Omega).
Then, for
z\in L^{2}(0,\tau;\mathbb{X}),
we may use the notation
z(t)
to denote
z(\cdot,t)
for almost all
t\in[0,\tau].

•

C([0,\tau];L^{2}(\Omega))
denotes the standard space of all
L^{2}(\Omega)
-valued continuous function in
[0,\tau].

•

If
\chi\in C([0,\tau])
, then
\|\chi\|_{\infty}
denotes the standard
\sup
norm.

•

For
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle
denotes the standard inner-product in
L^{2}(\Omega).

•

For normed linear spaces
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
, and a linear operator
\mathbb{A}:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{Y},

\text{\bf dom}(\mathbb{A})
,
\text{\bf ran}(\mathbb{A})
and
N(\mathbb{A})
shall denote the domain, range and the kernel of
\mathbb{A},
respectively.

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},
it is known that the spectral problem

(2.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=\lambda v\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\quad v=0\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

admits a sequence of eigenvalues satisfying
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\to+\infty
, and the corresponding eigenfunctions
\varphi_{n}
are such that
\varphi_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
and
\{\varphi_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
(see for e.g.,
[
10
]
).

For
p\geq 0,
we define

\mathbb{H}_{p}:=\{\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega):\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
on the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
It can be seen that
\mathbb{H}_{p}
is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{{\mathbb{H}}_{p}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n},\psi\rangle,\quad\phi,\psi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{p},

so that the corresponding norm is given by

\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\phi\in{\mathbb{H}}_{p}.

Clearly, when
p=0,
then
\mathbb{H}_{p}=L^{2}(\Omega).

We now look into the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the following initial boundary value problem which will help to obtain a representation of the source term
f
in terms of
\psi
and
h
. Let
\psi\in C([0,\tau])
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. We consider the following system

(2.2)

\begin{cases}v_{tt}-2\Delta v_{t}+\Delta^{2}v=\psi(t)f(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v=0=\Delta v,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v(\cdot,0)=0=v_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{cases}

Following
[
38
,
30
]
(see also
[
35
]
) the problem (
2.2
) has a solution
u,
given by

(2.3)

u(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}(t)\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},

with

(2.4)

\mu_{n}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}(t-s)}(t-s)\psi(s)\,ds.

Thus, taking into account the final value condition
u(\tau)=h
in (
1.1
), we have

(2.5)

\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\psi(s)\,ds\,\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle=\mu_{n}\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle,

where

(2.6)

\mu_{n}:=\mu_{n}(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\psi(s)\,ds.

In particular, the inverse source identification problem associated with (
1.1
) is same as the problem of solving the operator equation

(2.7)

{\mathbb{T}}f=h,

where
{\mathbb{T}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by

(2.8)

{\mathbb{T}}\varphi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Since
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
and
\psi\in C[0,\tau]
, it can be seen that
|\mu_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. In fact,

(2.9)

|\mu_{n}|\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds=\|\psi\|_{\infty}\frac{1-(1+\tau\lambda_{n})e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\,\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

Hence, from the representation (
2.8
) of
\mathbb{T}
, it follows that
{\mathbb{T}}
is a compact self-adjoint operator with eigenvalues
\mu_{n},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
In particular, we can infer that the inverse problem under consideration, which is same as the problem of solving the operator equation (
2.7
) is an ill-posed problem.
We shall show, under certain assumptions on
\psi
, that
\mu_{n}\not=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that under those conditions, the equation (
2.7
) has a unique solution provided

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}}{|\mu_{n}|^{2}}<\infty,

and in that case the solution
f
is given by

(2.10)

f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{\mu_{n}}\varphi_{n}.

We now state two elementary results in the form of a lemma, whose proofs follow easily from basic calculus.

LEMMA 2.1
.

(i)

Let
a>0
be fixed and
\eta_{a}:[\lambda_{1},\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be defined by
\eta_{a}(s)=(1+as)e^{-as}
. Then
\eta_{a}
is strictly decreasing and hence attains its maximum at
s=\lambda_{1}.

(ii)

Let
\alpha,p>0
, and
b>p.
Let
\eta_{\alpha,b,p}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be defined by
\eta_{\alpha,b,p}(s)=\frac{\alpha s^{b-p}}{1+\alpha s^{b}}.
Then
\eta_{\alpha,b,p}
attains global maximum at
s_{0}=\left(\frac{b-p}{\alpha p}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}
and
\eta_{\alpha,b,p}(s_{0})=\frac{p}{b}\left(\frac{b-p}{p}\right)^{\frac{b-p}{b}}\,\alpha^{\frac{p}{b}}.

3.
assumption on
\psi
, its consequences and a stability result

The results of this section are the base of this paper. We begin with the following assumption.

Assumption 3.1
.

There exists
\tau_{0}\in[0,\tau)
and
\kappa_{1}>0
such that
|\psi|\geq\kappa_{1}
on
[\tau_{0},\tau],
and one of the following holds:

(i)

\psi
does not change sign on
[0,\tau].

(ii)

If
\psi
changes sign on
[0,\tau]
then
\psi
is differentiable and there exists
\kappa_{2}>0
such that
|\psi^{\prime}|\leq\kappa_{2}
on
[0,\tau],
and
|\psi|\leq M:=\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}\kappa_{1}}{4\tau\tau_{0}}
on
I_{\text{sc}}:=\{t\in[0,\tau]:\psi(t)\psi(\tau)\leq 0\}.

Remark 3.2
.

The above assumption on
\psi
is less restrictive than the assumptions made in
[
30
,
35
,
29
]
(where the analysis is done by assuming
|\psi|
is bounded below by a positive constant, which by virtue of continuity implies
\psi
has a fixed sign, i.e., either
\psi>0
or
<0
in
[0,\tau]
) in the following sense: firstly, the bounded below assumption for the fixed sign case does not necessarily has to be on the whole interval
[0,\tau]
, and secondly, the sign changing assumption obviously implies
\psi
can take much general form than only the fixed sign case.

\lozenge

We now give an example of a function that satisfies Assumption
3.1
(ii).

Example 3.3
.

Let
\tau=\pi.
Consider the function

\psi(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{48\sqrt{2}}\cos t,\quad&\text{if}\quad t\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}],\\
\cos t,\quad&\text{if}\quad t\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\pi].\end{cases}

Clearly
|\psi(t)|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
in
[\frac{3\pi}{4},\pi],
and hence
\tau_{0}:=\frac{3\pi}{4}
and
\kappa_{1}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.
Since
\psi(\pi)<0
,
I_{sc}:=[0,\frac{\pi}{2}].
Now, observe that
\max\limits_{I_{sc}}|\psi|=\frac{1}{48\sqrt{2}}\leq M:=\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}\kappa_{1}}{4\tau\tau_{0}}=\frac{1}{48\sqrt{2}}.
Thus,
\psi
satisfies all the requirements of Assumption
3.1
(ii).

\lozenge

Remark 3.4
.

Although the Assumption
3.1
is motivated from
[
8
, Assumption H]
, there is a difference to that. The difference is w.r.t. the bound
M
of
|\psi|
on
I_{\text{sc}}.
Note that
M=\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})}{4\tau}M_{0},
where
M_{0}:=\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})\kappa_{1}}{\tau_{0}}
is the bound in
[
8
]
.

\lozenge

Theorem 3.5
.

Let Assumption
3.1
holds and
\mu_{n}
be as in (
2.6
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

|\mu_{n}|\geq\frac{C}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\quad\forall\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In particular,
\mu_{n}\not=0
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Case 1.
Suppose the item (i) of Assumption
3.1
holds. Then either
\psi(s)>0
or
\psi(s)<0
for all
s\in[0,\tau].
Since
e^{\lambda_{n}(s-\tau)}(\tau-s)\geq 0
for
s\in[0,\tau]
, we obtain

|\mu_{n}|=\Big|\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\lambda_{n}(s-\tau)}(\tau-s)\psi(s)\,ds\Big|=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\lambda_{n}(s-\tau)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds.

Note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\lambda_{n}(s-\tau)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}\lambda_{n}^{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{\lambda_{n}(s-\tau)}(\tau-s)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}[1-(1+\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{0}))e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{0})}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}[1-(1+\lambda_{1}(\tau-\tau_{0}))e^{-\lambda_{1}(\tau-\tau_{0})}].

The last inequality follows from the fact that the function
t\mapsto(1+t)e^{-t}
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, thanks to Lemma
2.1
(i). Since
(1+\lambda_{1}(\tau-\tau_{0}))e^{-\lambda_{1}(\tau-\tau_{0})}<1
, we obtain the required inequality with
C=\kappa_{1}[1-(1+\lambda_{1}(\tau-\tau_{0}))e^{-\lambda_{1}(\tau-\tau_{0})}]
.

Case 2.
Suppose that item (ii) of Assumption
3.1
holds, and let
\tau_{1}=\max\{t\in[0,\tau]:\psi(t)=0\}.
It is easy to observe that
0<\tau_{1}<\tau_{0}.
Let
I_{\text{su}}:=\{t\in[0,\tau]:\psi(t)\psi(\tau)\geq 0\}.
Then
[\tau_{1},\tau]\subset I_{\text{su}}
and
I_{\text{sc}}\subset[0,\tau_{1}].
Let
\tilde{C}
be such that
\tilde{C}>\frac{|\psi(0)|+2\tau\kappa_{2}}{\tau|\psi(\tau)|}.

Sub-case 2 (i).

\lambda_{n}\leq\tilde{C}.

Recall that by Assumption
3.1
(ii) we have
|\psi|\leq M:=\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}\kappa_{1}}{4\tau\tau_{0}}
on
I_{\text{sc}}:=\{t\in[0,\tau]:\psi(t)\psi(\tau)\leq 0\}.
Hence,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}|\mu_{n}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\Big[\int_{I_{\text{su}}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds-\int_{I_{\text{sc}}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\Big[\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds-M\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\Big[\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds-M\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds\Big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\Big[\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds+\kappa_{1}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds-M\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds\Big].

We now obtain some bounds for the integrals

I_{1}:=\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)|\psi(s)|\,ds,\quad I_{2}:=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds,\quad I_{3}:=\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}(\tau-s)\,ds.

It is easy to see that

I_{1}\geq(\tau-\tau_{0})e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}|\psi(s)|\,ds.

Let
\varepsilon=\frac{\tau-\tau_{0}}{2}
. Then

I_{2}\geq\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau-\varepsilon}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}{(\tau-s)}\,ds\geq\frac{\varepsilon{(\tau-\tau_{0}-\varepsilon)}}{e^{\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{0})}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon{(\tau-\tau_{0}-\varepsilon)}}{e^{\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}}{=\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}}{4e^{\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}}}.

Similar to
I_{1}
it is easy to check that
I_{3}\leq\tau\tau_{0}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}.
Thus, using these estimates we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}|\mu_{n}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}[\,(\tau-\tau_{0})e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}|\psi(s)|\,ds+{\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}\kappa_{1}}{4}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}-M\tau{\tau_{0}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-\tau_{1})}\,]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}e^{-\tilde{C}(\tau-\tau_{1})}[\,(\tau-\tau_{0})\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}|\psi(s)|\,ds+{\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}\kappa_{1}}{4}}-M\tau{\tau_{0}}\,]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}e^{-\tilde{C}(\tau-\tau_{1})}[\,(\tau-\tau_{0})\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}|\psi(s)|\,ds+{\frac{(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}\kappa_{1}}{4}}-M\tau{\tau_{0}}\,]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{2}e^{-\tilde{C}(\tau-\tau_{1})}(\tau-\tau_{0})\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}|\psi(s)|\,ds.

Thus, the required inequality is satisfied with

C=\lambda_{1}^{2}e^{-\tilde{C}(\tau-\tau_{1})}(\tau-\tau_{0})\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{0}}|\psi(s)|\,ds.

Sub-case 2 (ii).

\lambda_{n}\geq\tilde{C}.

Recall that by Assumption
3.1
(ii),
\psi
is differentiable in
[0,\tau]
and there exists
\kappa_{2}>0
such that
|\psi^{\prime}(s)|\leq\kappa_{2}
on
[0,\tau].
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}|\mu_{n}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{2}\Big|\big[\frac{\psi(\tau)}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}-\psi(0)(\frac{\tau e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}})\big]-\int_{0}^{\tau}\psi_{s}(s)\big[\frac{(\tau-s)e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]\,ds\Big|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\lambda_{n}^{2}|\psi(0)|\big[\frac{\tau e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]-\lambda_{n}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}|\psi_{s}(s)|\big[\frac{(\tau-s)e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]\,ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\lambda_{n}^{2}|\psi(0)|\big[\frac{\tau e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]-\kappa_{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}\big[\frac{(\tau-s)e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}(\tau-s)}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\lambda_{n}^{2}|\psi(0)|\big[\frac{\lambda_{n}\tau e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}+e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]-\lambda_{n}\kappa_{2}\big[\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{n}\tau e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}+e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]-\kappa_{2}\big[\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}-\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}}\big]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\lambda_{n}^{2}|\psi(0)|\big[\frac{\lambda_{n}\tau e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}+e^{-\lambda_{n}\tau}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\big]-\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{\lambda_{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\frac{|\psi(0)|}{\tau\lambda_{n}}-\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{\lambda_{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\frac{|\psi(0)|}{\tau\tilde{C}}-\frac{2\kappa_{2}}{\tilde{C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\psi(\tau)|-\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\big[\frac{|\psi(0)|+2\tau{\kappa_{2}}}{\tau}\big].

From this we obtain the required inequality with
C=|\psi(\tau)|-\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\big[\frac{|\psi(0)|+2\tau{\kappa_{2}}}{\tau}\big].

∎

Let
\varrho>0
be fixed and
p>0
. We now obtain a conditional stability estimate for the source set

(3.1)

S_{\varrho,\,p}=\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega):\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle g,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq\varrho^{2}\}=\{g\in\mathbb{H}_{p}:\|g\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}}\leq\varrho\}.

Theorem 3.6
.

(Conditional stability estimate)

Let the exact source
f\in S_{\varrho,\,p}
,
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
C
be the constant as in Theorem
3.5
. Then, we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C^{-\frac{p}{p+2}}\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

Proof.

From the Fourier expansion of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{4p}{p+2}}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{\frac{4}{p+2}}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{4p}{p+2}}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-4}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varrho^{\frac{4}{p+2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-4}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

Hence, using (
2.5
) and Theorem
3.5
, we have

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varrho^{\frac{4}{p+2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-4}\Big|\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{\mu_{n}}\Big|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\\
\leq\varrho^{\frac{4}{p+2}}C^{-\frac{2p}{p+2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}.

Hence,
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C^{-\frac{p}{p+2}}\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

∎

4.
the quasi-reversibility method and error estimates

We have already observed that the inverse source problem under consideration is ill-posed (also see,
[
30
, sec 3.1]
). We also illustrate this fact using an example.

Example 4.1
.

(Ill-posed)

Let
\tilde{h}=0
and
\tilde{h}_{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\varphi_{k}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}.
Let
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{f}_{k}
denote the source functions corresponding to the final values
\tilde{h}
and
\tilde{h}_{k},
respectively. Then by (
2.10
), we have
\tilde{f}=0
and
\tilde{f}_{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}\mu_{k}}\,\varphi_{k}.
Thus, we have
\|\tilde{h}-\tilde{h}_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
k\to\infty.
But, from (
2.9
), we have
\|\tilde{f}_{k}-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{1}{\|\psi\|_{\infty}}\,\lambda_{k}
and hence
\|\tilde{f}_{k}-\tilde{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty.

\lozenge

Thus, some some regularization method has to be employed for obtaining stable approximate solutions. As mentioned earlier, we will be using the quasi-reversibility method (QRM) as follows.

Let
b\geq 2
and
\alpha>0.
We consider the following system

(4.1)

\begin{cases}v_{tt}-2\Delta v_{t}+\Delta^{2}v=(I+\alpha(-\Delta)^{b})\psi(t)\tilde{f}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v=0=\Delta v,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v(\cdot,0)=0=v_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
v(\cdot,\tau)=\tilde{h},&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{cases}

Suppose
\psi\in L^{2}(0,\tau),\,\,\tilde{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{H}_{b}
, the domain of
(-\Delta)^{b}
. Suppose
v
is a solution of (
4.1
). Then following
[
30
,
35
]
we observe the following:

Taking the
L^{2}
inner product on both sides of the governing equation of (
4.1
) with
\varphi_{n}
, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}},
we obtain

\psi(t)\langle(I+\alpha(-\Delta)^{b})\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle-2\frac{d}{dt}\langle\Delta v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle+\langle\Delta^{2}v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle.

Now for
\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{H}_{b},
we have
\displaystyle(I+(-\Delta)^{b})\tilde{f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}.
Therefore,

\psi(t)\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b}}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle-2\frac{d}{dt}\langle\Delta v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle+\langle\Delta^{2}v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle\right)

Now, doing repeated integration by parts and making use of the boundary condition in (
4.1
), we obtain

\langle\Delta v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle=\langle v(t),\Delta\varphi_{n}\rangle=-\lambda_{n}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle

and

\langle\Delta^{2}v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle=\langle v(t),\Delta^{2}\varphi_{n}\rangle=\lambda_{n}^{2}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle.

Therefore, we have

(4.2)

\psi(t)\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b}}\left(\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\frac{d}{dt}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle+\lambda_{n}^{2}\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle\right).

Now it is easy to check that

\langle v(t),\varphi_{n}\rangle=(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}(t)\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle,

where
\mu_{n}(t)
is as in (
2.4
). Hence, the solution
v
is given by

(4.3)

v(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}(t)\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},

Now, analogous to the observation (
2.9
), we have

|\mu_{n}(t)|=\Big|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}(t-s)}(t-s)\psi(s)\,ds\Big|\leq\frac{\|\psi\|_{\infty}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

Thus, in order to ensure
v(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we need
\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{H}_{b-2}
only, and not necessarily in
\mathbb{H}_{b},
the domain of
(-\Delta)^{b}
.

In view of the above discussion, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\psi\in L^{2}(0,\tau)
and
\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{H}_{b-2},
a function
v\in C([0,\tau];L^{2}(\Omega))
with the representation given by (
4.3
) is said to be a mild solution of (
4.1
).

\lozenge

From the above discussion, we observe that for
\tilde{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{H}_{b-2},\,b\geq 2,
(
4.1
) has a unique mild solution, and we call it as
v.

Now, taking into account the final value condition
v(\cdot,\tau)=\tilde{h}
, we obtain

\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle=\frac{\langle\tilde{h},\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}.

Thus, the problem of identification of
\tilde{f}
from the final value
\tilde{h}
associated with (
4.1
) is same as the problem of solving the operator equation

(4.4)

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\tilde{f}=\tilde{h},

where
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}:\text{\bf dom}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\phi:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}\langle\phi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}.

Since
|\mu_{n}|\sim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}
, it is easy to check that
\text{\bf dom}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})=\mathbb{H}_{b-2}.
Also, it is easy to check that
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
is a densely defined closed self-adjoint operator. Now, for
b\geq 2,\,\,(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{b}\geq(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2b}
and hence
(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\geq\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}\lambda_{n}^{2}
. In view of this and from Theorem
3.5
, we have

\|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq C\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}\quad\phi\in\text{\bf dom}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}).

Thus,
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{-1}:\text{\bf ran}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a closed operator which is also bounded, and hence
\text{\bf ran}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})
is closed (cf.
[
25
]
). Now from the fact
\text{\bf ran}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})=N(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})^{\perp}=\{0\}^{\perp}
, we have
\text{\bf ran}(\mathbb{T}_{\alpha})=L^{2}(\Omega).
Therefore, it follows that
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
has a bounded inverse with

\|\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{C\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\text{for all}\quad\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Thus, from the above discussion, we observe that solving equation (
4.4
) is an well-posed problem. Moreover, for
\alpha>0
, let
f_{\alpha}:=\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{-1}h
and
f_{\alpha}^{\delta}:=\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{-1}h^{\delta}.
Then, we have

\|f_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{C\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}}\|h-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Recall that,
f_{\alpha}=\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{-1}h=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\varphi_{n}
and
f_{\alpha}^{\delta}=\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}^{-1}h^{\delta}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\langle h^{\delta},\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\varphi_{n}.
Therefore, we have

(4.5)

\langle f_{\alpha},\varphi_{n}\rangle=\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle f_{\alpha}^{\delta},\varphi_{n}\rangle=\frac{\langle h^{\delta},\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}.

Before proceeding further, let us mention that Young’s inequality will be used in many places in the upcoming analysis without mentioning explicitly. The Young’s inequality states that: for
a,b\geq 0
and
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\geq 1
satisfying
\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}=1
, we have
ab\leq\frac{a^{\theta}}{\theta}+\frac{b^{\theta^{\prime}}}{\theta^{\prime}}.

4.1.
Apriori parameter choice

We are now in a position to state and prove one of the main results of this paper, the order of the error estimate for an apriori parameter choice strategy. First we derive some general error estimates in terms of
(\alpha,\delta)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha,\delta>0,b\geq 2
and
h^{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be as in (
1.2
). Let
f\in S_{\varrho,\,p}
for some
\varrho,p>0
and
C
be as in Theorem
3.5
. Then we have the following estimates:

(i)

\|f_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{C}\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}}.

(ii)

\|f-f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}C_{\rm apri1}\,\varrho\,\alpha^{\frac{p}{b}},&\quad\text{if}\quad 0<p<b,\\
C_{\rm apri2}\,\varrho\,\alpha,&\quad\text{if}\quad p\geq b,\end{cases}

where
C_{\rm apri1}=\frac{p}{b}\,\big(\frac{b-p}{p}\big)^{\frac{b-p}{b}}
and
C_{\rm apri2}=\lambda_{1}^{(b-p)}.

Proof.

(i).
First, we observe that for
b\geq 2,

(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{b}\geq(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{2}\geq\alpha^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2b}

and hence
1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b}\geq\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}\lambda_{n}^{2}.

Therefore, from (
4.5
) it follows that

\displaystyle\|f_{\alpha}^{\delta}-f_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{\langle h^{\delta}-h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\Big|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{\frac{4}{b}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{\langle h^{\delta}-h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{\lambda_{n}^{2}\mu_{n}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{\rm Theorem\ref{non_zero_lower_bound}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{\frac{4}{b}}C^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle h^{\delta}-h,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{C^{2}}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\alpha^{\frac{4}{b}}}.

Hence, we obtain

(4.6)

\|f_{\alpha}^{\delta}-f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{C}\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}}.

(ii).
From (
4.5
) we have

\displaystyle\|f-f_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{\mu_{n}}-\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\Big|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big|\frac{\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b}\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2b}}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{2}}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2b-2p}}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{2}}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}.

Now, we consider two cases, namely,
0<p<b
and
p\geq b
.

Case 1.

0<p<b
:
In this case, by Lemma
2.1
(ii), we have

\frac{\alpha^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2b-2p}}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{p}{b}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{b-p}{p}\right)^{\frac{2(b-p)}{b}}\alpha^{\frac{2p}{b}}

and hence

(4.7)

\|f-f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rm apri1}\,\varrho\,\alpha^{\frac{p}{b}},

where
C_{\rm apri1}=\frac{p}{b}\,\left(\frac{b-p}{p}\right)^{\frac{b-p}{b}}.

Case 2.

p\geq b
:
In this case,

\frac{\alpha^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2b-2p}}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})^{2}}\leq\alpha^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2b-2p},

and hence

(4.8)

\|f-f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rm apri2}\,\varrho\,\alpha,

where
C_{\rm apri2}=\lambda_{1}^{(b-p)}.

∎

Now we are in a position to state the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha,\delta>0,b\geq 2
and
h^{\delta}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be as in (
1.2
). Let
f\in S_{\varrho,\,p}
for some
\varrho,p>0.
Then the following holds.

(i)

If
0<p<b
then for
\alpha\sim\left(\frac{\delta}{\varrho}\right)^{\frac{b}{p+2}}
there exists a constant
C_{1}:=C_{1}(C,p,b)>0
such that

\|f-f_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

(ii)

If
p\geq b
then for
\alpha\sim\left(\frac{\delta}{\varrho}\right)^{\frac{b}{b+2}}
there exists a constant
C_{2}:=C_{2}(C,\lambda_{1},p,b)>0
such that

\|f-f_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{b+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{b}{b+2}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
, we have

\|f-f_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}C_{\rm apri1}\,\varrho\,\alpha^{\frac{p}{b}}+\frac{1}{C}\,\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}},&\quad\text{if}\quad 0<p<b,\\
C_{\rm apri2}\,\varrho\,\alpha+\frac{1}{C}\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{\frac{2}{b}}},&\quad\text{if}\quad p\geq b.\end{cases}

Hence the proof follows by appropriately choosing the constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

If
b=2,
then by Theorem
4.4
, the best possible order of the error estimate in the apriori case is
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})
which is better than the order
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{1}{3}})
obtained in
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
using Tikhonov regularization method and the order
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{1}{4}})
obtained in
[
29
, Theorem 3.2]
using modified quasi-boundary value method for the apriori case.

Also, it is easy to observe that if
b\geq 4
then
\frac{b}{b+2}\geq\frac{2}{3}
and hence by Theorem
4.4
it follows that the best possible order can exceed the benchmark order of
\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\frac{2}{3}})
for Tikhonov regularization.

\lozenge

4.2.
Aposteriori parameter choice

We now proceed on to aposteriori case, where we will do the analysis using Morozov-type discrepancy principle, see for e.g.,
[
9
,
25
]
. For
\alpha>0,
let
\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
be defined by

\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}\tilde{h}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\langle\tilde{h},\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha\lambda_{n}^{b})}\varphi_{n}.

Then we have the following result.

LEMMA 4.6
.

(cf.
[
14
]
)
For
\alpha>0,
let
\zeta(\alpha):=\|\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}h^{\delta}-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.
Then

(i)

\zeta
is a continuous function.

(ii)

\lim\limits_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\zeta(\alpha)=0
and
\lim\limits_{\alpha\to+\infty}\zeta(\alpha)=h^{\delta}.

(iii)

\zeta
is strictly increasing.

We are now in a position to state another main result of this paper, namely the error estimates for an aposteriori parameter choice strategies.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let the Assumption
3.1
holds,
f\in S_{\varrho,p}
,
\delta>0
and
h^{\delta}
be as in (
1.2
). Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
b\geq 2.
Suppose that
\delta^{\sigma}<\|h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.
Choose
\xi>1
such that
0<\xi\delta^{\sigma}\leq\|h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.
Then we have the following estimates.

(i)

If
b\neq 2
then for each
\delta>0
there exists a unique
\alpha_{\delta}>0
such that
\|\mathbb{B}_{\alpha_{\delta}}h^{\delta}-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\xi\delta.
Further, there exists constants
C_{4}:=C_{4}(C,\|\psi\|_{\infty},\xi,\lambda_{1},p,b),C_{6}:=C_{6}(C,\|\psi\|_{\infty},\xi,p),C_{\rm apost}:=C_{\rm apost}(C,\xi,p)>0
such that

\|f-f_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\begin{cases}C_{6}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}},&\quad\text{if}\quad 0<p<b-2\\
C_{\rm apost}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}+C_{4}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{b}}\,\delta^{\frac{b-2}{b}},&\quad\text{if}\quad p\geq b-2.\end{cases}

(ii)

If
b=2
then for each
\delta>0
there exists a unique
\alpha_{\delta}>0
such that
\|\mathbb{B}_{\alpha_{\delta}}h^{\delta}-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\xi\delta^{\sigma}.
Further, there exists constant
C_{5}:=C_{5}(C,\|\psi\|_{\infty},\xi,\lambda_{1},p)>0
(and
C_{\rm apost}
as in (i)) such that

\|f-f_{\alpha_{\delta}}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rm apost}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p\sigma}{p+2}}+C_{5}\,\varrho\,\delta^{1-\sigma}.

Proof.

Let
w
be the mild solution (see Definition (
4.2
)) of

\begin{cases}v_{tt}-2\Delta v_{t}+\Delta^{2}v=(I+\alpha(-\Delta)^{b})\psi(t)f_{\alpha}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v=0=\Delta v,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v(\cdot,0)=0=v_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
v(\cdot,\tau)=h,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{cases}

Then it is easy to check that
z:=\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}w
is a solution of

(4.9)

\begin{cases}v_{tt}-2\Delta v_{t}+\Delta^{2}v=\psi(t)f_{\alpha}(x),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v=0=\Delta v,&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\tau),\\
v(\cdot,0)=0=v_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
v(\cdot,\tau)=\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}h,&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.\end{cases}

Now,

\|f-f_{\alpha}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{p}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}\big[\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle-\frac{\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{(1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b})\mu_{n}}\big]^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}\big[\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\big]^{2}\leq\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}_{p}}\leq\varrho^{2}

\|f-f_{\alpha}\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}}\leq\varrho.

Proof of (i).
By Lemma

Also, we have

\displaystyle\|u(\tau)-z(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

Note that

v(\cdot,0)=0=v_{t}(\cdot,0),&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\

and (

Therefore, following the proof of Theorem

, it is easy to check that

and hence

(4.11)

\|f-f_{\alpha_{\delta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rm apost}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}},

where

We now try to obtain a bound for
\alpha_{\delta}
in terms of
\delta
using the discrepancy principle. Now,

\displaystyle\xi\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{B}_{\alpha_{\delta}}h^{\delta}-h^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\langle h^{\delta},\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\langle h,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\langle h^{\delta}-h,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\mu_{n}\,\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\delta.

Therefore,

(\xi-1)\delta\leq\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\mu_{n}\,\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Thus,

(4.12)

(\xi-1)^{2}\delta^{2}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b-2}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}}\right)^{2}\big(\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle\|\psi\|_{\infty}\big)^{2}

Case 1.

b>2
and

In this case,

\displaystyle\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}+1

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{b-2-p}{b}\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b}+\frac{p+2}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b-2-p}{b}\big[(\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{b})^{\frac{b-2-p}{b}}\big]^{\frac{b}{b-2-p}}+\frac{p+2}{b}[1^{\frac{p+2}{b}}]^{\frac{b}{p+2}}

Now the proof follows by choosing the constant
C_{6}
appropriately.

Proof of (ii).

4.6

\|f-f_{\alpha_{\delta}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\rm apost}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p\sigma}{p+2}},

\alpha_{\delta}

\displaystyle\xi\delta^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1+\alpha_{\delta}\lambda_{n}^{2}}\mu_{n}\,\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\delta

5.
Optimality

In this section we analyse the order optimality of the error estimates obtained in the preceding section for the source set
S_{\varrho,p}
. We have already observed in Section
2
that the considered inverse source identification problem is equivalent to solving a linear operator equation

{\mathbb{T}}f=h,

where
{\mathbb{T}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a compact self-adjoint bounded linear operator defined by (
2.8
), that is,

{\mathbb{T}}\varphi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\varphi_{n},\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)

with
\mu_{n}
as in (
2.6
).

We now formulate some useful representation of the source set
S_{\varrho,p}
in terms of the operator
\mathbb{T}.

For this, we observe from Theorem
3.5
and (
2.9
) that

(5.1)

\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{\|\psi\|_{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{|\mu_{n}|}\leq\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{C}.

Thus, in particular we have

(5.2)

\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2p}}{\|\psi\|^{p}_{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{|\mu_{n}|^{p}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2p}}{C^{p}}.

Therefore, from (
5.2
) it follows that

(5.3)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq\varrho^{2}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\langle\tilde{f},\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}}{|\mu_{n}|^{p}}\leq\tilde{\varrho}^{2},

where, for a given
\varrho>0
,
\tilde{\varrho}:=C^{-\frac{p}{2}}\varrho
, and for a given
\tilde{\varrho}>0
,
\varrho:=\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{p}{2}}\tilde{\varrho}
.

Next, recall that the operator
{\mathbb{T}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
defined above
is a compact self-adjoint operator. Hence,
{\mathbb{T}}^{*}{\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{T}}^{2}
is a positive self-adjoint operator with representation

{\mathbb{T}}^{*}{\mathbb{T}}\varphi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{n}^{2}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{n}\rangle\,\varphi_{n},\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega),

and its square-root, conventionally denoted by
|{\mathbb{T}}|
, has the representation

|{\mathbb{T}}|\varphi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{n}|\langle{\varphi},\varphi_{n}\rangle\,\varphi_{n},\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Consequently, for any
\nu>0
, we have

|{\mathbb{T}}|^{\nu}{\varphi}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{n}|^{\nu}\langle{\varphi},\varphi_{n}\rangle\,\varphi_{n},\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega).

We may also observe that, for
\nu>0
if

{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}:=\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega):\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{n}|^{-2\nu}|\langle{\varphi},\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}<\infty\},

then
{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}
is the range of
|{\mathbb{T}}|^{\nu}
so that
{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}
is the domain of the unbounded operator
|{\mathbb{T}}|^{-\nu}
, which has the representation

|{\mathbb{T}}|^{-\nu}\varphi=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{n}|^{-\nu}\langle{\varphi},\varphi_{n}\rangle\,\varphi_{n},\quad\varphi\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\nu}.

Therefore, from (
5.3
), we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2p}|\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq\varrho^{2}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\||\mathbb{T}|^{-\frac{p}{2}}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{\varrho}^{2}

whenever
f\in{\mathcal{D}}_{p/2}
.
Thus, for
r>0
, we have

(5.4)

M_{r,p}:=\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega):g=|\mathbb{T}|^{\frac{p}{2}}\tilde{g},\,\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq r\}=\{g\in{\mathcal{D}}_{p/2}:\||{\mathbb{T}}|^{-p/2}g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq r\}.

Now, it is easy to see that
S_{\varrho,p}\subseteq M_{r_{1},p}
and
M_{r_{2},p}\subseteq S_{\varrho,p}
, where
r_{1}=C^{-\frac{p}{2}}\varrho
and
r_{2}=\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\,\varrho.

We may recall that (cf.
[
9
,
25
]
), corresponding to
\delta>0
, the
worst case error
for a reconstruction method described by an operator
\mathcal{R}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
approximating the inverse of
\mathbb{T}
with respect to a bounded set
\mathcal{M}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
is defined to be the quantity

\triangle(\mathcal{R},\delta,\mathcal{M}):=\sup\{\|\mathcal{R}\tilde{h}-\tilde{f}\|:\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{M},\,\tilde{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\,\|\mathbb{T}\tilde{f}-\tilde{h}\|\leq\delta\}.

It is known that (cf.
[
9
,
25
]
) the worst case error is bounded below by the corresponding
modulus of continuity

\omega(\delta,\mathcal{M}):=\sup\{\|\tilde{f}_{1}-\tilde{f}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}:\tilde{f}_{1},\tilde{f}_{2}\in\mathcal{M}\,,\,\|\mathbb{T}\tilde{f}_{1}-\mathbb{T}\tilde{f}_{2}\|\leq\delta\},

up to a constant multiple, in the sense that

(5.5)

\triangle(\mathcal{R},\delta,\mathcal{M})\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega(2\delta,\mathcal{M})\quad\text{for all}\quad\delta>0.

We also observe some easily verifiable facts:

(5.6)

\omega(\delta_{1},\mathcal{M})\leq\omega(\delta_{2},\mathcal{M})\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2},

and

(5.7)

\omega(\delta,\mathcal{M}_{1})\leq\omega(\delta,\mathcal{M}_{2})\quad\text{for}\quad\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{2},\quad\delta>0.

For more details on results related to modulus of continuity for linear operators, we refer to
[
16
,
25
]
.

We will denote the spectrum of a bounded linear operator
\mathbb{K}
by
{\rm\bf spec}(\mathbb{K}).
The following result is motivated from Trong and Hai
[
34
]
. In its proof, we shall make use of the function
\rho(\cdot)
defined by

\rho(t):=t^{\frac{p+2}{p}},\quad t>0,

and the relation

\rho(t)=t\varphi^{-1}(t),\,t>0,

where

\varphi(t):=t^{\frac{p}{2}}
for
t>0
. Clearly,
\varphi
and
\rho
satisfies the assumption in
[
33
, Assumption 1.1]
, that is,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\varphi(t)=0,

\varphi
is strictly increasing and
\rho
is convex. Note that
|\mathbb{T}|^{2}\varphi(|\mathbb{T}|^{2})=|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}
and
M_{r,p}=\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega):g=[\varphi(|\mathbb{T}|^{2})]^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{g},\,\|\tilde{g}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq r\}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\delta>0,r>0
and
M_{r,p}
be as in (
5.4
). Then the following results hold.

(i)

If
\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\in{\rm{\bf spec}}\big(|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}\big),
then
\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})=r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

(ii)

If
\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\notin{\rm{\bf spec}}\big(|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}\big)
, then for
\delta\leq r\sup\limits_{n}|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}
either

\inf_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\leq\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\leq\sup_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}},

or

\inf_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\leq\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\leq\sup_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

Proof.

Case 1.

\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\in{\rm{\bf spec}}\big(|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}\big).

In this case, by the result of Tautenhahn
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
, it follows that

\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})=r\sqrt{\rho^{-1}\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)}=r\left(\frac{\delta}{r}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+2}}=r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

Case 2.

\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\notin{\rm{\bf spec}}\big(|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}\big).

Recall that the set of eigenvalues of the compact self-adjoint operator
|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}
is precisely the set
\Big\{|\mu_{n}|^{p+2}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\Big\},

and that
|\mu_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to\infty.
Thus, there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
|\mu_{n_{0}}|=\sup\limits_{n}\Big\{|\mu_{n}|:n\in{\mathbb{N}}\Big\}.

Therefore, for
\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\notin{\rm{\bf spec}}\big(|\mathbb{T}|^{p+2}\big)
and for
0<\delta\leq r\sup\limits_{n}|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}
, there are only two possibilities given below as sub-cases.

Sub-case 2(i).
There exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

|\mu_{n}|^{p+2}\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\leq|\mu_{n+1}|^{p+2},

that is,

(5.8)

r\,|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\leq\delta\leq r\,|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}.

Therefore, by (
5.6
) and (
5.8
), we have

(5.9)

\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\geq\omega(r\,|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}},M_{r,p})\underbrace{=}_{{\bf Case1}}r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\left(r\,|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

From (
5.8
) we have

\frac{r}{\delta}\,|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\geq 1

and hence by (
5.9
)

\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\geq r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\,\frac{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}.

Again from (
5.6
) and (
5.8
), we have

(5.10)

\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\leq\omega(r\,|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}},M_{r,p})\underbrace{=}_{{\bf Case1}}r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\left(r\,|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

Now from (
5.8
), we have

\frac{r}{\delta}\,|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\leq 1

and hence by (
5.10
), we have

\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\leq r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\,\frac{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}.

Thus, we have

\inf_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\leq\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\leq\sup_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

Sub-case 2(ii).
There exists
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

|\mu_{n+1}|^{p+2}\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}}\leq|\mu_{n}|^{p+2}.

Then using similar arguments as done for
Sub-case 2(i)
, we obtain

\inf_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\leq\omega(\delta,M_{r,p})\leq\sup_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,r^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

From (

) it follows that
\sup\limits_{n}|\mu_{n}|\leq\frac{\|\psi\|_{\infty}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}.
Therefore, Theorem
5.1
(ii) holds for all
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},
where
\delta_{0}:=r\,\sup\limits_{n}|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}<\infty.

\lozenge

Remark 5.3
.

We now show that the
infimums
and the
supremums
in Theorem
5.1
(ii) are indeed non-zero and finite, respectively. In order to see this, first we recall a result from Courant and Hilbert
[
6
]
, which says that there exist constants
e_{1},e_{2}>0
such that

e_{1}n^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\lambda_{n}\leq e_{2}n^{\frac{2}{d}}\quad\text{for all}\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

From this, we obtain

\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\frac{e_{1}}{e_{2}}\Big(\frac{n}{n+1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n+1}}\leq\frac{e_{2}}{e_{1}}\Big(\frac{n}{n+1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\frac{e_{2}}{e_{1}}.

This together with the inequalities in (
5.1
) imply the required assertion.

Remark 5.4
.

(
Order optimality
)
Let
\triangle(QRM,\delta,S_{\varrho,p})
denote the worst case error for the quasi-reversibilty method. Recall that
M_{r_{2},p}\subseteq S_{\varrho,p},
where
r_{2}=\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{p}{2}}\varrho.
Let
0<\delta\leq\delta_{0},
where
\delta_{0}:=\frac{r_{2}}{2}\,\sup\limits_{n}|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p+2}{2}}
.

Then by Theorem
4.4
(i) for
0<p<b
in the
apriori case
, and for any reconstruction method
\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\triangle(QRM,\delta,S_{\varrho,p})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\,=\frac{C_{1}}{C_{\omega}}\,C_{\omega}\,\varrho^{\frac{2}{p+2}}\,\delta^{\frac{p}{p+2}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\leq}_{{\rm Theorem}\ref{minmax_bounds_mod_cont}}

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{C_{\omega}}\,\omega(2\delta,M_{r_{2},p})\underbrace{\leq}_{\eqref{set_monotone_prop_mod_cont}}\frac{C_{1}}{C_{\omega}}\,\omega(2\delta,S_{\varrho,p})\underbrace{\leq}_{\eqref{uni_lower_bound_for_worst_case_error}}\frac{2C_{1}}{C_{\omega}}\,\triangle(\mathcal{R},\delta,S_{\varrho,p}),

where
C_{\omega}
is accordingly chosen from the set

\Big\{2^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{p}{p+2}},\quad\inf_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,2^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{p}{p+2}},\quad\inf_{n}\frac{|\mu_{n+1}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}{|\mu_{n}|^{\frac{p}{2}}}\,2^{\frac{p}{p+2}}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{-\frac{p}{p+2}}\Big\}.

Similarly, by Theorem
4.7
(i) for
b>2
and
0<p<b-2
in the
aposteriori case
, and for any reconstruction method
\mathcal{R},
we have

\triangle(QRM,\delta,S_{\varrho,p})\leq\frac{2C_{6}}{C_{\omega}}\,\triangle(\mathcal{R},\delta,S_{\varrho,p}).

Therefore, in the apriori case for
0<p<b
and in the aposteriori case for
b>2
and

the error estimates obtained in Theorem
4.4
and Theorem

, respectively, are of optimal order for the source set

.

6.
conclusion

We have considered an inverse source identification problem from final time observation associated with a bi-parabolic system, which is known to be an ill-posed problem. In order to obtain stable approximations of the sought source, we have employed the quasi-reversibilty (QRM) method, which has not been explored yet. We obtained Hölder type error estimates for both the apriori and aposteriori parameter choice strategies. Our rates seems to exceed the rates obtained in earlier works by using Tikhonov regularization and quasi-boundary value method. Moreover, we have also shown the order optimality of the obtained rates for some cases. The arguments used for proving order optimality in this paper, can be used to fill the gap in the arguments of order optimality claimed in some earlier works in different context by different authors.

Moreover, this paper seems to be the first one that has broaden the applicability of the considered problem by enlarging the admissible class of source functions. This is done so by allowing the time dependent component of the source function to change sign. Note that in the context of the considered inverse problem, all previously known works have assumed some fixed sign property of the time dependent component by assuming some bounded below conditions.

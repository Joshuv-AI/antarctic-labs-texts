Local well-posedness for a class of singular Vlasov equations
Thomas Chaub

Abstract
In this article we study a singular Vlasov system on the torus where the force field has the smoothness of a (fractional) derivative D α D^{\alpha} of the density, where α > 0 \alpha>0 . We prove local well-posedness in Sobolev spaces without restriction on the data. This is in sharp contrast with the case α = 0 \alpha=0 which is ill-posed in Sobolev spaces for general data.



1
Introduction and main result

1.1
Context and notations

In this article we focus on a class of nonlinear singular Vlasov systems in the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a prototypal example is

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}{f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
\gamma E=-\nabla_{x}V,\\
(-\Delta_{x})^{\alpha/2}V=\left(\rho-\rho_{0}\right),\\
f(0,x,v)=f^{0}(x,v),\end{array}\right.

where the parameter
\gamma
will be taken to
1
or
-1
, and the equation will be respectively called repulsive or attractive. The function
f
stands for a distribution function in the domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and may represent the distribution of electrons in a plasma, or the density of stars in stellar dynamics. The density associated with
f
will be denoted
\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)dv
. The term
\rho_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}f^{0}(t,x,v)dvdx
corresponds, in the repulsive setting, to the density of ions. In the attractive setting, we remove the mean value of
\rho
as a trick called the Jeans swindle, which is mathematically relevant
[
8
]
. The initial condition,
f^{0}
, can be taken non-negative, and
\alpha>0
is a parameter.

The aim of this work is to give a local well-posedness theory in Sobolev regularity for the general case
\alpha>0
. To introduce our main result, we shall first introduce some notations.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the weighted Sobolev norms

{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}_{r}^{k}}:=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq k}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|v|^{2})^{r}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f|^{2}dvdx\right)^{1/2},

where, for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{d}),\beta=(\beta_{1},...,\beta_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we write

|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i},\quad|\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{i},

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}:=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}...\partial_{x_{d}}^{\alpha_{d}},\quad\partial_{x}^{\beta}:=\partial_{x_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}...\partial_{x_{d}}^{\beta_{d}}.

We will also use the classic Sobolev spaces. We will write
H_{x,v}^{k}
(resp
W^{k,\infty}_{x,v})
the standard Sobolev space for the norm
L^{2}
(resp
L^{\infty}
) for functions depending on
(x,v)
, and
H_{x}^{k}
for functions only depending on
x
.

We will use the following notation and convention for the Fourier transform of a function
U

\widehat{U}(k):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}U(x)e^{-ik\cdot x}dx.

Instead of (
1.1
), we consider a more general system under the form

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}{f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
E=-\nabla_{x}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}U(x-y)\left(\rho(y)-\rho_{0}\right)dy,\\
f(0,x,v)=f^{0}(x,v),\end{array}\right.

making the following assumptions for
U
:

Assumptions 1

(A1)
.

\widehat{U}(0)=0
and there exists
\alpha>0
,
C_{\alpha}>0
such that

(1.3)

\forall k\neq 0,\>k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\quad|\widehat{U}(k)|\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{|k|^{\alpha}}.

In particular, by taking
\widehat{U}(k)=\pm\frac{1}{|k|^{\alpha}}
,
\widehat{U}(0)=0
, we recover (
1.1
).

Note that we can also consider potentials
U=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}U_{i}
such that each
U_{i}
satisfies (
1.3
) for
\alpha_{i}>0
. Then
U
satisfies (
1.3
) with
\alpha=\min(\alpha_{i})_{i\in[1,p]}
. Those types of potentials may appear in some physical models in gravitation (see the discussion below about Manev potentials).

We denote by
\lfloor x\rfloor
the floor value of
x
and

(1.4)

m_{0}=3+\frac{d}{2}+p_{0},\quad p_{0}=\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1,\quad r_{0}=\max\left(d,2+\frac{d}{2}\right).

Our main well-posedness result is the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
f^{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}
with
m>m_{0}
,
2r>r_{0}
. Assuming (A1), then there exists
T>0
for which there exists a unique solution of the system (
1.2
) with initial condition
f^{0}
and such that
f\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
,
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})
.

Note that we consider both the attractive and repulsive case.

Remark 1.1
.

Notice that in the above statement the solution
f
is less regular than the initial condition
f^{0}
. This was expected because of the nature of the equation. Nevertheless, the fact that
f
is in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
is not optimal. With sharper estimates in the following proof, when
\alpha\leq 1
,
f
is expected to be in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1+\alpha}_{2r})
, provided that we use the fractional space
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{2r}
when
s
is not an integer (which has not been defined here). For simplicity, we will only give the proof of Theorem
1
, as the gain of regularity we could obtain does not seem fundamental.

Our results could be easily extended in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
instead of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
assuming that

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\>|\widehat{U}(\xi)|\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{1+|\xi|^{\alpha}}.

The main interest of our theorem is for small values of
\alpha
. Indeed, if we take
\alpha=2
in (
1.1
), the system we obtain is the well-known Vlasov-Poisson system, and a local well-posedness theory is very easy to get. We even have global existence for physical dimensions
d\leq 3
(see
[
12
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
).

For
1\leq\alpha<2
, system (
1.1
) behaves at least like a Burgers type equation and local well-posedness theory follows by standard energy estimates.
The case
\alpha=1
in particular, has physical meaning. It was first introduced by Manev as a correction of the Newtonian potential. The interactions between Newtonian and Manev potentials have been studied (see
[
9
]
), but the Manev potential on its own is also interesting. In the attractive setting, it is called the Pure Stellar dynamic Manev system (PSM)
[
2
]
, and it arises in stellar dynamics.

For
\frac{3}{4}\leq\alpha\leq 1
local well-posedness was recently obtained in (
1.1
) using the additional regularity provided by averaging lemmas in the whole space in
[
3
]
. In addition, they give general conditions such that the system (
1.1
), that they call the
Vasov-Riesz system
because of the introduction of a Riesz type interaction, have finite-time singularity formation for solutions.

Thus, the main contribution of this article corresponds to the case
\alpha\in(0,\frac{3}{4})
. The well-posedness theory of the equation is more challenging, because the apparent lost derivative in
x
on the force field
E
is no longer compensated by regularization. In the critical case
\alpha=0
(which does not enter our framework), named the Vlasov-Dirac-Benney system, it has been proved that the system is in general ill-posed in Sobolev spaces
[
5
]
. Nevertheless, on the Torus and in the repulsive case, we can ensure hypotheses to have the local well-posedness of the system. The first results were obtained in dimension
d=1
by Bardos and Besse
[
1
]
. They proved that the Vlasov-Dirac-Benney system is locally well-posed provided that for all
(t,x)
, there exists a function
m(t,x)
such that
v\mapsto f(t,x,v)
remains compactly supported and is increasing for
v\leq m(t,x)
, decreasing for
v\geq m(t,x)
, in other words,
one bump
shaped functions. More recently, Han-Kwan and Rousset
[
6
]
have proved that the Vlasov-Dirac-Benney system is locally well-posed in any dimension for Sobolev regularity, provided that for all
x
, the profile
v\mapsto f^{0}(x,v)
satisfies a Penrose stability condition, which means that if we define the Penrose function

(1.5)

\mathcal{P}(\gamma,\tau,\eta,f)=1-\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-(\gamma+i\tau)s}\frac{i\eta}{1+|\eta|^{2}}\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{v}\nabla_{v}f)(\eta s)ds,\quad\gamma>0,\tau\in\mathbb{R},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\},

v\mapsto f^{0}(x,v)
must satisfy the following condition, for some
c_{0}>0

(1.6)

\underset{(\gamma,\tau,\eta)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}{\inf}|\mathcal{P}(\gamma,\tau,\eta,f^{0})|\geq c_{0}.

1.2
Sketch of the proof

In order to prove Theorem
1
, we shall define another system, which is a regularized Vlasov-Poisson type system, that depends on a new parameter
\varepsilon>0

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}{f_{\varepsilon}}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\varepsilon}+E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon}=0,\\
E_{\varepsilon}=-\nabla_{x}(U*V_{\varepsilon}),\\
-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{x}V_{\varepsilon}+V_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(.,v)dv,\\
f_{\varepsilon}(0,x,v)=f^{0}_{\varepsilon}(x,v).\end{array}\right.

Taking formally the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain the system (
1.2
), which is exactly the system we want to study. The idea is to use the well-posedness of the system (
1.7
) to get a family of functions
(f_{\varepsilon})
which satisfy uniform estimates. Then, using compactness we extract (in a certain sense that will be defined later) a function
f
which is a solution of the limit system (
1.2
). After that, we will show uniqueness for the solution of (
1.2
) in the class of
\{f\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
,
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})
}.

The first step is to show that we can get a family of functions
(f_{\varepsilon})
defined on a time interval
[0,T]
with
T
being independent of
\varepsilon
. To do so, we introduce the following key quantity

(1.8)

\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(t,f):={\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,t],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}\right)}+{\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert}_{L^{2}\left([0,t],H_{x}^{m}\right)},

and we have the following theorem

Theorem 2
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R^{*}_{+}}
. We assume that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
f^{0}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}
with
m>m_{0}
,
2r>r_{0}
and that there exists
M_{0}>0
so that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
{\left\lVert f^{0}_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}_{m}^{2r}}\leq M_{0}
.

Then there exist
T>0
,
R>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
) and a unique solution
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r})
of (
1.7
) with

(1.9)

\underset{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}{\sup}\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})\leq R.

The proof of this theorem will rely on a bootstrap argument following the strategy of
[
6
]
. For most of the proof, we will try to estimate the key quantity
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(t,f_{\varepsilon})
independently of
\varepsilon
. Of course, if we could give an estimate of
{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,t],\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}\right)}
independently of
\varepsilon
, the proof would be over, but this cannot be done in the general case. This is the reason why we choose to work with
{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}\left([0,t],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}\right)}
(losing one derivative for
f
is enough to give an estimate that does not depend on
\varepsilon
), and
{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}\left([0,t],H_{x}^{m}\right)}
. The fact that it is possible to give an estimate independent of
\varepsilon
on
\rho
without loss of derivatives while it is not for
f
is not trivial and is fundamental here. This is reminiscent of the results on averaging lemmas (see
[
4
]
). Here though, the situation is a bit different, as we will be averaging in
v
but also in time. More precisely, anticipating a bit on the following proof, let us look at the operator
K
(defined in Lemma
8
),

K_{G}(F)(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int(\nabla_{x}F)(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot G(t,s,x,v)dvds.

There is an apparent loss of one derivative over
x
. However, the key proposition
4
shows that
K_{G}
is a bounded operator from
L^{2}([0,T],H^{\alpha}_{x})
into
L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})
(provided that
G
is smooth enough and
\alpha\in(0,1)
), and the bound can be estimated up to a constant by
T^{\alpha/2}
(actually, the result remains true for
\alpha=0

[
6
]
). The apparent loss of a derivative in
x
is somehow compensated by taking the averages in
v
and in time.

Remark 1.2
.

Because of the apparent links with the theory of averaging lemmas
[
4
]
, we could try to apply directly those results to prove our theorem. As stated earlier, a recent work
[
3
]
has proved that, when
x
is in the whole space, the system (
1.1
) is locally well-posed up to
\alpha=3/4
. Nevertheless, standard averaging lemmas cannot cover the whole range
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

Sections 2, 3 and 4 will be dedicated to the proof of Theorem 2. In section 5, we give the elements to conclude the proof of the main Theorem
1
.

2
Beginning of the proof of Theorem 2

2.1
Preliminary lemmas

We present here some lemmas that will be useful for the proof of Theorem
2
. They have been proved in
[
6
]
. We use the notation
[A,B]=AB-BA
to denote the commutator between two operators.

Lemma 1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
\chi=\chi(v)
a non-negative smooth function, with
|\partial^{\alpha}\chi|\leq C_{\alpha}\chi
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
|\alpha|\leq s
.

•

Consider two functions
f=f(x,v)
,
g=g(x,v)
, then for all
k\geq s/2

(2.1)

{\left\lVert\chi fg\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\lesssim{\left\lVert\chi g\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{W^{k,\infty}_{x,v}}+{\left\lVert\chi f\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}{\left\lVert g\right\rVert}_{W^{k,\infty}_{x,v}}.

•

Consider a function
E=E(x)
and a function
F=F(x,v)
. Then for all
s_{0}>d

(2.2)

{\left\lVert\chi EF\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}\lesssim{\left\lVert E\right\rVert}_{H^{s_{0}}_{x}}{\left\lVert\chi F\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}+{\left\lVert\chi F\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}{\left\lVert E\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x}}.

•

Consider a vector field
E=E(x)
and a function
f=f(x,v)
, then for all
s_{0}>1+d
and for all
\alpha,

\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|=s\geq 1

(2.3)

{\left\lVert\chi[\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{v},E(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}]f\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim{\left\lVert E\right\rVert}_{H^{s_{0}}_{x}}{\left\lVert\chi f\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}+{\left\lVert\chi f\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}{\left\lVert E\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x}}.

The main use of this lemma will be for
\chi(v)=(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}
thus yielding estimates for products in the space
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\sigma}
.

Let us introduce another product type estimate.

Lemma 2
.

Consider two functions
f=f(x,v)
,
g=g(x,v)
. Then, for all
s\geq 0
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{2d}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq s
, and
\chi(v)
positive function satisfying
|\partial^{\alpha}\chi|\leq C_{\alpha}\chi
(as in the precedent lemma). We have

(2.4)

{\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}f\partial^{\beta}_{x,v}g\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\chi}f\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}{\left\lVert\chi g\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}+{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\chi}f\right\rVert}_{H^{s}_{x,v}}{\left\lVert\chi g\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

Finally, the following lemma is a very useful commutation formula between
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}
and the transport operator
\mathcal{T}
defined by

(2.5)

\mathcal{T}=\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}.

Lemma 3
.

For every
\alpha,

\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have for every smooth functions
f

(2.6)

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}(\mathcal{T}f)=\mathcal{T}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{\beta_{i}\neq 0}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\overline{\beta}^{i}}f+[\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta},E\cdot\nabla_{v}]f,

where
\overline{\beta}^{i}
is equal to
\beta
except
\overline{\beta}^{i}_{i}=\beta_{i}-1
.

2.2
Setting up the bootstrap

The proof of the theorem will rely on a bootstrap argument. From standard energy estimates (that we shall recall later, see the proof of proposition
2
), we first have the following result.

Proposition 1
.

The system (
1.7
) is locally well-posed in
\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}
for all
m
and
r
satisfying
m>1+d
and
2r>d/2
. In other words, if
f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}
, there exists
T>0
(which depends on
\varepsilon
) so that there exists a unique
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r})
solution of system (
1.7
).

Thanks to the previous proposition, we may define a maximal solution
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}\left([0,T^{*}),\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}\right)
. As a direct consequence, for every
T<T^{*},\quad\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}_{2r}^{m-1}}<+\infty
. In order to define
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})
, we just have to prove that
{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}\left([0,T],H_{x}^{m}\right)}<+\infty
. But because of the definition of weighted Sobolev norms, and with the use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get that

{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}\lesssim{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}_{2r}^{m}}<+\infty.

We have thus shown that the quantity
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})
is well defined and is continuous in
T
for every
T<T^{*}
. This allows us to consider, for
R>0
to be defined later,

T^{\varepsilon}=\sup\left\{T\in[0,T^{*}),\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})\leq R\right\}.

By taking
R
large enough, we have by continuity that
T^{\varepsilon}>0
. Of course
T^{\varepsilon}
depends on
\varepsilon
. We want to show that by taking
R
large enough (but independent of
\varepsilon
),
T^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded from below by some time
T>0
. Only the following two situations can happen

1. Either
T^{\varepsilon}=T^{*}
,

2. Or
T^{\varepsilon}<T^{*}
and
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T^{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})=R
.

Let us analyze the first case. If
T^{\varepsilon}=T^{*}=+\infty
, then
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T^{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})\leq R
for every
T>0
and there is nothing to do, so we only have to consider when
T^{\varepsilon}=T^{*}<+\infty
. Actually, by energy estimates, we can show that this case is impossible. Indeed, we have the following proposition

Proposition 2
.

Assume that
T^{\varepsilon}<+\infty
, then for every
f_{\varepsilon}
solution of (
1.7
), we have for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
the estimate

\underset{[0,T^{\varepsilon})}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}\exp\left[C\left(T^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(T^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}R\right)\right].

Proof.

Let
f_{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
1.7
). We thus have
\mathcal{T}f_{\varepsilon}=0
, and by using (
2.6
)

\mathcal{T}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon})=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{\beta_{i}\neq 0}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\overline{\beta}^{i}}f_{\varepsilon}-[\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta},E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}]f_{\varepsilon}.

Taking the scalar product with
(1+|v|^{2})^{2r}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}
and summing for every
|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq m
, we obtain for the left hand size

\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq m}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{T}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon})\cdot\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}=\frac{d}{dt}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}.

For the first term of the right hand size, we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\left|\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq m}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\chi\mathds{1}_{\beta_{i}\neq 0}\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\overline{\beta}^{i}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot\chi\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}\right|\lesssim{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}.

For the second term, we use (
2.3
) with
s=m,\>\chi(v)=(1+|v|^{2})^{r}
and
s_{0}=m
, from which we deduce

{\left\lVert\chi\left[\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta},E_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}\right]f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}},

and thus by using again Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

\left|\int\chi\left[\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta},E_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\nabla_{v}\right]f_{\varepsilon}\chi\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}\right|\lesssim{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}.

We have, by elliptic regularity,

{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}={\left\lVert\nabla_{x}(U*V_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}.

Putting all together, we have shown that

\frac{d}{dt}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}+1\right){\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}.

We integrate between
0
and
t
for
t\in[0,T^{\varepsilon})
. For some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
, we get

{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}+1\right){\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(s)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2},

and we use the Gronwall inequality to show

\displaystyle\underset{[0,T^{\varepsilon})}{\sup}{{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}+\exp\left[C\left(T^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(T^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}\left([0,T^{\varepsilon}\right),H_{x}^{m})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}+\exp\left[C\left(T^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(T^{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T^{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})\right)\right].

Finally, since
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T^{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})\leq R
, we obtain the expected estimate.
∎

If we use the proposition for
T^{\varepsilon}=T^{*}
, we obtain that

\underset{[0,T^{*})}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r}}^{2}\exp\left[C\left(T^{*}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(T^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}R\right)\right].

This means that the solution could be continued beyond
T^{*}
, and thus contradicts the definition of
T^{*}
, which shows that this case is impossible.

We then have to consider the remaining case,
T^{\varepsilon}<T^{*}
and
\mathcal{N}^{m}_{2r}(T^{\epsilon},f_{\varepsilon})=R
. Choosing
R
large enough, the objective is to find some time
T^{\#}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
, such that the equality

\mathcal{N}^{m}_{2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})=R,

cannot hold for any
T\in[0,T^{\#}]
, which will prove that
T^{\varepsilon}>T^{\#}>0
.

We need to estimate
\mathcal{N}^{m}_{2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})
for
T<T^{\varepsilon}
. The easier part is the term
{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}
. We cannot use the previous estimates because they depend on
\varepsilon
(we used the elliptic regularity provided by the Poisson equation). Nevertheless, we can still give estimates based on energy methods, this time being careful that every estimate must be independent of
\varepsilon
. In this proposition and in the following of this article,
\Lambda
will stand for a generic continuous function, independent of
\varepsilon
, which is non-decreasing with respect to each of its arguments.

Proposition 3
.

For
m>2+d
and
2r>d/2
,
f_{\varepsilon}
solution of (
1.7
) satisfies the estimate

(2.7)

\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}\>+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R),

for all
T\in[0,T^{\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that
|\alpha|+|\beta|=m-1
. We use (
2.6
) and, as before, we take the scalar product with
\left(1+|v|^{2}\right)^{2r}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}f_{\varepsilon}
, and we take the sum for every
|\alpha|+|\beta|=m-1
. Like we did previously, we use (
2.3
) with
s=m-1
,
\chi(v)=\left(1+|v|^{2}\right)^{r}
and
s_{0}=m-1
. We obtain that

(2.8)

\frac{d}{dt}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}^{2}\lesssim{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1+}_{2r}}^{2}+{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}^{2}{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m-1}}.

Integrating in time, we get that there exists a
C>0
such that

\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}+C\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}\left(T+\int_{0}^{T}{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m-1}}dt\right).

We can now use the following estimate, independent of
\varepsilon

(2.9)

{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m-1}}={\left\lVert\nabla_{x}(U*V_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m-1}}\lesssim{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}.

And we still have
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})\leq R
, (because
T\in[0,T^{\epsilon}])
. We have thus shown that

\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}+CR(T+T^{\frac{1}{2}}R),

which concludes the proof of the lemma.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

As said in the introduction, our estimates could be sharper. This can been seen in (
2.9
), which is far from being optimal, because we do not use the regularity provided by the convolution with
U
. Nevertheless, this regularization is not needed to prove Theorem
2
, thus we chose not to exploit it for simplicity.

3
Estimates of the density term

3.1
Introduction of the
f_{I,J}

Now we need to tackle the second term of
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})
, which is the norm of the density
{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}\left([0,T],H_{x}^{m}\right)}
. We can try to apply the operator
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
to (
1.7
) with
|\alpha|=m
, but this involves commutator terms such as
\partial_{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}f_{\varepsilon}
which contain
m
order derivatives of
f_{\varepsilon}
when
|\alpha^{\prime}|=1
, and those cannot be estimated uniformly in
\varepsilon
. To get rid of this problem, we choose to apply a larger class of differential operators to (
1.7
).

Definition 3.1
.

For
I=(i_{1},...,i_{d}),J=(j_{1},...,j_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|I|+|J|=m
, we define

f_{I,J}:=\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}f_{\varepsilon}.

Note that the
(f_{I,J})
contain all the
\partial^{I}_{x}f_{\varepsilon}
with
|I|=m
. By applying
\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}
to (
1.7
), we find that the
(f_{I,J})
satisfy a differential system, which is the purpose of the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

We assume that
m>3+d
and
2r>d
. For all
T<T^{\varepsilon}
, and for all
I,J\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have, for
f_{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
1.7
), that
f_{I,J}
is solution of

(3.1)

\mathcal{T}(f_{I,J})+\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{V}_{I,J},

where

\mathcal{F}=(f_{I,J})_{I,J\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|I|+|J|=m},\quad\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{F}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{i_{k}\neq 0}f_{\hat{I}^{k},\overline{J}^{k}}+\sum_{p=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\mathds{1}_{i_{p}\neq 0}\partial_{x_{p}}E_{\varepsilon}f_{\overline{I}^{p},\hat{J^{k}}},

with

\hat{I}^{k}=(i_{1}...i_{k-1},i_{k}+1,i_{k+1}...i_{d}),\quad\overline{I}^{k}=(i_{1}...i_{k-1},i_{k}-1,i_{k+1}...i_{d}),

and
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{I,J})_{I,J\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
is a remainder, which means that for every
T<T^{\varepsilon}

{\left\lVert\mathcal{V}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}\leq\Lambda(T,R).

We therefore obtain that
\mathcal{F}=(f_{I,J})
satisfies a system which is coupled through the linear term
\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{F}
. We now prove this lemma.

Proof.

We know that
f_{\varepsilon}
solves (
1.7
), so we have

\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}(\mathcal{T}(f_{\varepsilon}))=0.

By using (
2.6
), we obtain

\mathcal{T}(f_{I,J})+\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{V}_{I,J},

with

\mathcal{V}_{I,J}=\sum_{k=2}^{\min(|I|,m-1)}\sum_{\sigma,|\sigma|=k}C_{I,J,k,\sigma}\>\partial^{\sigma}_{x}E_{\varepsilon}\>\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{I-\sigma}_{x}\partial^{J}_{v}f_{\varepsilon}.

Notice that because
|\sigma|=k\geq 2
, we have in particular that
1+|I|-|\sigma|+|J|\leq m-1
. Also, in the case
|I|\leq 1
, we simply have
\mathcal{V}_{I,J}=0
.

We want to estimate
{\left\lVert\mathcal{V}_{I,J}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}
. We have that

{\left\lVert\mathcal{V}_{I,J}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r}}\lesssim{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m-1}}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{r}}.

And, because
{\left\lVert E_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m-1}}\lesssim{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{H_{x}^{m}}
(estimate independent of
\varepsilon
),

{\left\lVert\mathcal{V}_{I,J}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}\lesssim{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})}{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{r})},

and we obtain that

{\left\lVert\mathcal{V}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}\leq\Lambda(T,R),

which ends the proof.
∎

3.2
Straightening the transport vector field

In the following, we make a change of variable to straighten the vector field

\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+E\cdot\nabla_{v}\quad\text{into}\quad\partial_{t}+\Phi(t,x,v)\cdot\nabla_{x},

where
\Phi
is defined in the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
f_{I,J}
a solution of (
3.1
). We consider
\Phi(t,x,v)
a smooth solution of the Burgers equation

(3.2)

\partial_{t}\Phi+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}\Phi=E_{\varepsilon},

such that the Jacobian matrix
(\nabla_{v}\Phi)
is inversible. We define
g_{I,J}
by

g_{I,J}(t,x,v):=f_{I,J}(t,x,\Phi).

Then
g_{I,J}
is solution of the equation

(3.3)

\partial_{t}g_{I,J}+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{I,J}+\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon}\right)(t,x,\Phi)+\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{V}_{I,J}(t,x,\Phi),

where
\mathcal{G}=(g_{I,J})_{I,J\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|I|+|J|=m}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on a simple calculation

\begin{split}\partial_{t}g_{I,J}+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{I,J}+\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{v}^{J}E_{\varepsilon}\cdot\left(\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon}\right)(t,x,\Phi)+\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{V}_{I,J}(t,x,\Phi)\\
+^{t}(\nabla_{v}\Phi)^{-1}\nabla_{v}g_{I,J}\cdot\left(\partial_{t}\Phi+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}\Phi-E_{\varepsilon}\right),\end{split}

hence the result when (
3.2
) is satisfied.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Writing
\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)=|\det\nabla_{v}\Phi(t,x,v)|
, notice that

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{I,J}\mathcal{J}dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{I,J}dv.

Because we introduced the function
\Phi
in our equations, we shall estimate its Sobolev norms, which is the purpose of the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that
m>3+d
, there exists
T_{0}
(depending on
R
but independent on
\varepsilon
) such that for all
T<\min(T_{0},T^{\varepsilon})
, there exists a unique smooth solution on
[0,T]
of the Burgers equation (
3.2
) with initial condition
\Phi|_{t=0}=v
.

Moreover, for all
T<\min(T_{0},T^{\varepsilon})
, we have the following estimates:

(3.5)

\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\Phi-v\right\rVert}_{W^{k,\infty}_{x,v}}+\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\left(1+|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial_{t}\Phi\right\rVert}_{W^{k-1,\infty}_{x,v}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R),\quad k<m-d/2-1,

for
|\alpha|\leq m-1
and
|\beta|\leq m-2

(3.6)

\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}\,\underset{v}{\sup}{\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}\left(\Phi-v\right)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}\,\underset{v}{\sup}{\left\lVert\frac{1}{\left(1+|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial^{\beta}_{x,v}\partial_{t}\Phi\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

A very similar lemma (Lemma 11) has been proved in
[
6
]
, we refer to it for a complete proof.

Now we have to consider our new equation (
3.3
), that we rewrite as

(3.7)

\partial_{t}g_{I,J}+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{I,J}+\partial^{I}_{x}\partial^{J}_{v}E_{\varepsilon}\cdot(\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon})(t,x,\Phi)+\mathcal{M}_{I,J}\mathcal{G}=S_{I,J},

where
S_{I,J}(t,x,v)=\mathcal{V}_{I,J}(t,x,\Phi(t,x,v))
.

The next step is to introduce the flow of the equation that we denote by
X(t,s,x,v),0\leq s,t\leq T
and is given as the solution of

\partial_{t}X(t,s,x,v)=\Phi(t,X(t,s,x,v),v),\quad X(s,s,x,v)=x.

We have to control the Sobolev norms of
X
.

Lemma 7
.

For all
t,s,\>0\leq s\leq t\leq T
and
m>3+d
, we write

X(t,s,x,v)=x+(t-s)\left(v+\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)\right).

We have that
\tilde{X}
satisfies, for
|\alpha|<m-d/2-1
,
|\beta|<m-d/2-2

(3.8)

\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\dfrac{1}{(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial^{\beta}_{x,v}\partial_{t}\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Furthermore, there exists
\hat{T}_{0}(R)>0
small enough such that for
T\leq\min(T_{0},\hat{T}_{0},T^{\varepsilon})
, we get that
x\mapsto x+(t-s)\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)
is a diffeomorphism and that, for
|\alpha|<m-1
,
|\beta|<m-2
,

(3.9)

\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}\underset{v}{\sup}{\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}\underset{v}{\sup}{\left\lVert\dfrac{1}{(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial^{\beta}_{x,v}\partial_{t}\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Finally, there exists
\Psi(t,s,x,v)
such that for
t,s\in[0,T]
and
T\leq\min(T_{0},\hat{T}_{0},T^{\varepsilon})
, we have,

(3.10)

X(t,s,x,\Psi(t,s,x,v))=x+(t-s)v,

with
\Psi
satisfying the estimates, for
|\alpha|<m-d/2-1
,
|\beta|<m-d/2-2

(3.11)

\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}(\Psi(t,s,x,v)-v)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\dfrac{1}{(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial^{\beta}_{x,v}\partial_{t}\Psi(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R),

for
|\alpha|<m-1
,
|\beta|<m-2

(3.12)

\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}\underset{v}{\sup}{\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}_{x,v}(\Psi(t,s,x,v)-v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}\underset{v}{\sup}{\left\lVert\dfrac{1}{(1+|v|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\partial^{\beta}_{x,v}\partial_{t}\Psi(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Once again, a similar lemma (Lemma 13) has already been proved in
[
6
]
.

In order to control the linear part, we define the tensor
\mathcal{M}
by
(\mathcal{M}H)_{I,J}=M_{I,J}H
and for
0\leq s,t\leq T
,
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and we introduce
\mathfrak{M}(t,s,x,v)
as the solution of

\partial_{t}\mathfrak{M}(t,s,x,v)=\mathcal{M}(t,x,\Phi(t,x,v))\mathfrak{M}(t,s,x,v),\quad\mathfrak{M}(s,s,x,v)=I,

whose existence and uniqueness is guaranteed by the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem.

By a Gronwall type argument and thanks to Lemma
6
, we can show that for
k<m-2

(3.13)

\underset{0\leq s,t\leq T}{\sup}\left({\left\lVert\mathfrak{M}\right\rVert}_{W^{k,\infty}_{x,v}}+{\left\lVert\partial_{t}\mathfrak{M}\right\rVert}_{W^{k,\infty}_{x,v}}+{\left\lVert\partial_{s}\mathfrak{M}\right\rVert}_{W^{k,\infty}_{x,v}}\right)\leq\Lambda(T,R).

3.3
Introduction of the average operator

We define in the next lemma the fundamental operator
K_{G}
which was introduced in
[
6
]

Lemma 8
.

For a smooth function
G(t,s,x,v)
, we define the integral operator
K_{G}
acting on
F(t,x)
by

(3.14)

K_{G}(F)(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla_{x}F)(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot G(t,s,x,v)dvds.

For
f_{\varepsilon}
satisfying (
1.7
) and
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\int f_{\varepsilon}dv
, the functions
\partial^{I}_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon}
with
|I|=m
satisfy the equation:

\partial^{I}_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{K\in\{1,...,d\}^{m}}K_{H_{(K,0),(I,0)}}\left(U*\left(\left(I-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\right)^{-1}\partial_{x}^{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}\right)\right)+\mathcal{R}_{I,0},

with

\begin{split}&H_{(K,L),(I,J)}=\\
&\mathfrak{M}_{(K,L),(I,J)}(s,t,x,\Psi(s,t,x,v))(\nabla_{v}f)(s,x-(t-s)v,\Psi(s,t,x,v))\mathcal{J}(t,s,\Psi(s,t,x,v))\tilde{\mathcal{J}}(s,t,x,v),\end{split}

\mathcal{J}=|det\nabla_{v}\Phi(t,x,\Psi(s,t,x,v))|,\quad\tilde{\mathcal{J}}=|det\nabla_{v}\Psi(s,t,x,v)|,

and
\mathcal{R}_{I,0}
satisfies for
T
small enough, the estimate

{\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{I,0}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Proof.

We first introduce the notations

\eta(t,x,v)=\left(\partial^{I}_{x}\partial^{J}_{v}E_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\varepsilon}(s,x,\Phi(t,x,v))\right)_{I,J},\quad S=(S_{I,J})_{I,J}.

This allows us to put the system (
3.7
) under the following equation satisfied by
\mathcal{G}

\partial_{t}\mathcal{G}(t,x,v)+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathcal{G}(t,x,v)+\eta(t,x,v)+\mathcal{M}\mathcal{G}(t,x,v)=S(t,x,v).

Integrating by respect with the time variable the expression
\partial_{t}(\mathfrak{M}(t,s,x,v)\mathcal{G}(t,X(t,s,x,v),v))
we obtain that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(t,x,v)=\mathfrak{M}(0,t,x,v)\mathcal{G}^{0}(X(0,t,x,v),v)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{M}(s,t,x,v){S}(s,X(s,t,x,v),v)ds&\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{M}(s,t,x,v)\eta(s,X(s,t,x,v),v)ds,\end{split}

with
\mathcal{G}^{0}=(g^{0}_{I,J})_{I,J}
. We multiply the equation by
\mathcal{J}
and then with integrate by respect with the variable
v
, which yields

(3.15)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{G}(t,x,v)\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dv=\mathcal{I}_{0}+\mathcal{I}_{F}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{M}(s,t,x,v)\eta(s,X(s,t,x,v),v)\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dvds,

with

\mathcal{I}_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{M}(0,t,x,v)\mathcal{G}^{0}(X(0,t,x,v),v)\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dv,

\mathcal{I}_{F}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{M}(s,t,x,v)S(s,X(s,t,x,v),v)\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dsdv.

We want to show that
\mathcal{I}_{0},\mathcal{I}_{F}
can be considered as remainders. Let us recall some of the previous estimates on
\Phi
and
\mathfrak{M}

(3.16)

\underset{0\leq s,t\leq T}{\sup}{\left\lVert\mathfrak{M}(t,s)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq\Lambda(T,R).

\underset{[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\Phi(t)-v\right\rVert}_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}\leq\Lambda(T,R).

Now we can give the following estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int\mathfrak{M}(t,0,x,v)\mathcal{G}^{0}(X(0,t,x,v),v)\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dv\right|&\\
\leq\underset{0\leq s,t\leq T}{\sup}{\left\lVert\mathfrak{M}(s,t)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}{\left\lVert\mathcal{J}(t)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\int|\mathcal{G}^{0}(X(0,t,x,v),v)|dv&\\
\leq\Lambda(T,R)\sum_{I,J}\int|g^{0}_{I,J}(X(0,t,x,v),v)|dv.\end{split}

Thus, we have

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{0}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq\Lambda(T,R)\sum_{I,J}{\left\lVert\int_{v}{\left\lVert g^{0}_{I,J}(X(0,t,\cdot,v),v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}dv\right\rVert}_{L^{2}[0,T]}.

To estimate the last term, we use the change of variable in
x
,
y=X(0,t,x,v)+tv=x-t\tilde{X}(0,t,x,v)
and, thanks to the estimates on
\tilde{X}

\underset{t,s\in[0,T]}{\sup}{\left\lVert\partial_{x}\tilde{X}(t,s,x,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\leq\Lambda(T,R),

we get that

{\left\lVert g^{0}_{I,J}(X(0,t,\cdot,v),v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}dv\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert g^{0}_{I,J}(\cdot-tv),v\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert g^{0}_{I,J}(\cdot,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Then by Cauchy-Schwarz, we deduce

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{0}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{dv}{(1+|v|^{2})^{r}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{I,J}{\left\lVert g^{0}_{I,J}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r}}.

Again, we can make the following change of variable

\int_{x,v}|g^{0}_{I,J}(x,v)|^{2}dxdv=\int_{x,v}|f^{0}_{I,J}(x,v)|^{2}\mathcal{J}(0,x,v)dxdv\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert f^{0}_{I,J}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r}}^{2}.

Finally, we can use the fact that
{\left\lVert f^{0}_{I,J}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r}}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m}_{r}}\leq R
to conclude that

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{0}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Let us show with similar arguments that

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{f}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq T\Lambda(T,R).

First, we use once again (
3.16
) to show that

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{F}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq\Lambda(T,R)\sum_{I,J}{\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\int_{v}{\left\lVert S_{I,J}(s,X(s,t,\cdot,v),v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}dvds\right\rVert}_{L^{2}[0,T]}.

As we did just above, we use the change of variable in
x
,
y=X(s,t,x,v)+(t-s)v=x-(t-s)\tilde{X}(s,t,x,v)
, and the estimates on
\tilde{X}
to show that

{\left\lVert S_{I,J}(s,X(s,t,\cdot,v),v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert S_{I,J}(s,\cdot,v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Integrating in
v
, we get by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\int_{v}{\left\lVert S_{I,J}(s,X(s,t,\cdot,v),v)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}_{x}}dv\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert S_{I,J}(s)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r}}.

So we finally arrive at

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{F}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}{\left\lVert S_{I,J}(s)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r}}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}[0,T]}\leq\Lambda(T,R)T{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}.

Recalling that
S(t,x,v)=\mathcal{V}(t,x,\Phi(t,x,v))
, we can use one last change of variable, and the estimates on the derivatives of
\Phi
, to show that

{\left\lVert S\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}\leq\Lambda(T,R){\left\lVert\mathcal{V}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{r})}\leq\Lambda(T,R),

where the last inequality comes from the fact that
\mathcal{V}
is a remainder term, which we proved in lemma
4
.

Thus, we have proved that

{\left\lVert\mathcal{I}_{F}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq T\Lambda(T,R).

Let us go back to (
3.15
). Thanks to the results on
\mathcal{I}_{0}
and
\mathcal{I}_{F}
, and using (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}&\partial^{I}_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{R}_{I,0}-\\
&\int_{0}^{t}\int\sum_{K}\mathfrak{M}_{(K,0),(I,J)}(\partial^{K}_{x}E)(s,X(s,t,x,v))\cdot(\nabla_{v}f)(s,X(s,t,x,v),\Phi(s,X(s,t,x,v),v))\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dvds,\end{split}

with

{\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{I,0}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

We can finally use the change of variable
v=\Psi(s,t,x,w)
provided by lemma
7
to obtain that

\partial^{I}_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon}=-\int_{0}^{t}\int\sum_{K}(\partial^{K}_{x}E)(s,x-(t-s)v)\cdot H_{(K,0),(I,0)}(t,s,x,v)dvds+\mathcal{R}_{I,0},

with

\begin{split}&H_{(K,L),(I,J)}=\\
&\mathfrak{M}_{(K,L),(I,J)}(s,t,x,\Psi(s,t,x,v))(\nabla_{v}f)(s,x-(t-s)v,\Psi(s,t,x,v))\mathcal{J}(t,s,\Psi(s,t,x,v))\tilde{\mathcal{J}}(s,t,x,v),\end{split}

which gives the result.
∎

3.4
Focus on the operator
K_{G}

In order to control the norms of the derivatives of
\rho_{\varepsilon}
, we have to understand better the operator
K
. Following
[
6
]
, let us first introduce a new norm.

Definition 3.2
.

For
T>0
, we define

{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}=\underset{0\leq t\leq T}{\sup}\left(\sum_{k}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\underset{\xi}{\sup}\left((1+|k|)^{s_{2}}(1+|\xi|)^{s_{1}}\left|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,k,\xi)\right|\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proposition 4
.

There exists
C>0
such that for every
T>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, for every
G
satisfying
{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}<\infty
and for all
s_{1}>1
,
s_{2}>d/2
, we have

{\left\lVert K_{G}(F)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\leq C\,T^{\alpha/2}\,{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}{\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{\alpha}_{x})},\quad\forall F\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{\alpha}_{x}).

For practical uses, it is convenient to relate the norm
{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}<\infty
to a more tractable norm. From
[
6
]
, we know that if
p>1+d
,
\sigma>d/2
, we can find
s_{2}>d/2
et
s_{1}>1
such that

{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}\leq\underset{0\leq s,t\leq T}{\sup}{\left\lVert G(t,s)\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{\sigma}}.

In the expression of the operator
K_{G}
(
3.14
), there seems to have of loss of a derivative in
x
, but this proposition shows that the operator is actually continuous from
L^{2}([0,T],H^{\alpha}_{x})
into
L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})
provided that
G
is smooth enough. Once again, we emphasize that this is a key propriety in the proof of Theorem
2
. We are able to gain regularity in
x
by integrating over
v
and
t
. This propriety explains why we can estimate the
H^{m}
norm of
\rho_{\varepsilon}
without loss of derivative.

Proposition
4
remains true for
\alpha=0
, this was proved in
[
6
]
.

Proof.

By using Fourier series in
x
, we write that

F(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\hat{F}_{k}(t)e^{ik\cdot x}.

By definition of
K_{G}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{G}F(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}\hat{F}_{k}(s)e^{ik\cdot x}\cdot\int e^{-ik\cdot v(t-s)}G(t,s,x,v)dvds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}\hat{F}_{k}(s)e^{ik\cdot x}ik\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{v}G)(t,s,x,k(t-s))ds,

where
\mathcal{F}_{v}G
is the Fourier transform of
G(t,s,x,v)
with respect to the last variable. By Fourier expanding in the
x
variable, we deduce that

K_{G}F(t,x)=\sum_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}\sum_{l}e^{ik\cdot x}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{F}_{k}(s)ik\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l,k(t-s))ds.

Changing
l
into
l+k
we can rewrite this expression as

K_{G}F(t,x)=\sum_{l}e^{ik\cdot x}\left(\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{F}_{k}(s)ik\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))ds\right).

From the Bessel-Parseval identity, we infer that

{\left\lVert K_{G}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}=\sum_{l}\left|\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}\hat{F}_{k}(s)ik\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))ds\right|^{2}.

By using Cauchy-Schwarz for
t
and
k
, we have

\begin{split}{\left\lVert K_{G}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\sum_{l}(\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}|\hat{F}_{k}(s)&|^{2}|k\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|ds\\
&\cdot\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{t}|k\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|ds),\end{split}

and by integrating in time, we obtain that

\begin{split}{\left\lVert K_{G}\right\rVert}^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}&\lesssim\sum_{l}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}|\hat{F_{k}}(s)|^{2}|k\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|dsdt\\
&\cdot\underset{l}{\sup}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}|k\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|ds)\leq I\cdot II.\end{split}

Let us first consider the term
II
. We observe that for all
s_{1}\geq 0
,

\underset{l}{\sup}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}|k\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|ds\leq

\underset{l}{\sup}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\sum_{k}\left(\underset{0\leq s\leq t}{\sup}\underset{\xi}{\sup}\left[(1+|\xi|)^{s1}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,\xi)|\right]\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|(t-s))^{s_{1}}}ds\right).

We choose
s_{1}>1
. Changing variables, we observe that

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|(t-s))^{s_{1}}}ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\tau^{s_{1}})}d\tau<\infty.

It follows that

II\lesssim\underset{l}{\sup}\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\sum_{k}\underset{0\leq s\leq t}{\sup}\underset{\xi}{\sup}\left[(1+|\xi|)^{s1}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,\xi)|\right].

Next we choose
s_{2}>d/2
so that
\sum\frac{1}{(1+|k|)^{2\,s_{2}}}<\infty
. By using Cauchy-Schwarz, we deduce

\begin{split}II&\lesssim\underset{t\in[0,T]}{\sup}\left(\sum_{k}\underset{0\leq s\leq t}{\sup}\underset{\xi}{\sup}\left((1+|k|)^{s_{2}}(1+|\xi|)^{s_{1}}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,\xi)|\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}.\end{split}

Now let us consider the other term
I
. By using Fubini, we have for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k}|\hat{F_{k}}(s)|^{2}|k\cdot(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{k}|\hat{F_{k}}(s)|^{2}\int_{s}^{T}\sum_{l}|k||(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|dtds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{k}|\hat{F_{k}}(s)|^{2}(1+|k|^{\alpha})\int_{s}^{T}\sum_{l}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|dtds

\displaystyle={\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{\alpha})}^{2}\underset{k}{\sup}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{s}^{T}\sum_{l}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|dt.

As previously, by choosing
s_{1}>1
,
s_{2}>d/2
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{k}{\sup}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{s}^{T}\sum_{l}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,k(t-s))|dt

\displaystyle\leq\underset{k}{\sup}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})(1+|k|(t-s))^{s_{1}}}\sum_{l}\underset{\xi}{\sup}(1+|\xi|)^{s1}|(\mathcal{F}_{x,v}G)(t,s,l-k,\xi)|dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\underset{k}{\sup}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})(1+|k|(t-s))^{s_{1}}}{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}.

To conclude, we have to estimate
\underset{k}{\sup}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})(1+|k|(t-s))^{s_{1}}}dt
. We first use a change of variable

\displaystyle\underset{k}{\sup}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{s}^{T}\frac{|k|}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})(1+|k|(t-s))^{s_{1}}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\underset{k}{\sup}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}\underset{0\leq s\leq T}{\sup}\int_{0}^{|k|(T-s)}\frac{1}{(1+\tau)^{s_{1}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\underset{k}{\sup}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}\int_{0}^{|k|T}\frac{1}{(1+\tau)^{s_{1}}}d\tau.

Then, by Holder inequality, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\underset{k}{\sup}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}\int_{0}^{|k|T}\frac{1}{(1+\tau)^{s_{1}}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\underset{k}{\sup}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{\alpha})}\times(|k|T)^{\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+\tau)^{\frac{s_{1}}{1-\alpha}}}d\tau\right)^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\alpha}.

We have finally shown that

I\lesssim T^{\alpha}{\left\lVert G\right\rVert}_{T,s_{1},s_{2}}{\left\lVert F\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{\alpha})}^{2},

which ends the proof.
∎

3.5
Conclusion of the estimates for
\rho_{\varepsilon}

By Lemma

\partial^{I}_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{K\in\{1,...,d\}^{m}}K_{H_{(K,0),(I,0)}}\left(U*\left(\left(I-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\right)^{-1}\partial_{x}^{K}\rho\right)\right)+\mathcal{R}_{I,0},

with

{\left\lVert\mathcal{R}_{I,0}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}\lesssim T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Let

be given by Assumption (A1). Notice that if

, Assumption (A1) will be satisfied for any real inferior to

, thus we can assume in the following that

.

We get, thanks to Proposition

\displaystyle{\left\lVert K_{H_{(K,0),(I,0)}}\left(U*\left(\left(I-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta\right)^{-1}\partial_{x}^{K}\rho\right)\right)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L_{x}^{2})}

We emphasize that this inequality is independent of

. We can thus estimate all of the

with

. Summing up all of the results we have, we have proved that

{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})}\leq T^{\alpha/2}\Lambda(T,R).

4
End of the proof of Theorem 2

The previous sections have given all the tools to end the proof. Indeed, Section 3 has been dedicated to show that

{\left\lVert\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})}\leq T^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

We have shown at the end of Section 2 that

{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})}\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R),

from which we deduce that for all

\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})\leq{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}+T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R)+T^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Lambda(T,R).

Next, we consider
R
large enough such that

\frac{1}{2}R>{\left\lVert f_{\varepsilon}^{0}\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}.

With
R
being fixed, we can find by continuity a

small enough such that the previous estimates are satisfied and that for every

, we have

T^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda(T,R)+T^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\Lambda(T,R)<\frac{1}{2}R.

Thus, for every

,
\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(t,f_{\varepsilon})<R
and so

. This means that the time
T^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded from below by a certain

which is independent of
\varepsilon
. That concludes the proof of Theorem

.

5
Solutions for the limit problem

Let us recall the limit system, which is the system we want to study in Theorem
1

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}{f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
E=-\nabla_{x}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}U(x-y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,y,v)dv\right)dy,\\
f(0,x,v)=f^{0}(x,v).\end{array}\right.

This system is a non linear transport equation. But taking the formal limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have lost the elliptic propriety that we had for the system (
1.7
). Proving local well-posedness is thus more challenging, and we can use Theorem
2
to find a solution of (
1.2
).

Indeed, this theorem gives us a family of functions
f_{\varepsilon}
solutions of (
1.7
) on a time interval
[0,T]
,
T
being independent of
\varepsilon
. We can use this family of functions to find a solution of the limit system. In the following, we will extract a subsequence that will converge to a certain function
f
. We next have to show that
f
is actually a solution of the limit system. We will start by showing the uniqueness, by similar arguments that we used in Theorem
2
.

5.1
Uniqueness of the solution

Proposition 5
.

Let
f_{1}
,
f_{2}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
with
m>m_{0}
and
2r>r_{0}
be two solutions of (
1.2
) with the same initial condition
f^{0}
. We write
\rho_{i}=\int f_{i}dv
and we suppose that
\rho_{i}\in L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})
. Then
f_{1}=f_{2}
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
f=f_{1}-f_{2}
. We denote by
E_{2}
the force field associated with the density
\rho_{2}
and
E
associated with
\rho=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}
. We have that
f
solves the equation

(5.1)

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{1}+E_{2}\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0.

Let
\Phi
satisfying the Burgers equation (
3.2
) associated with
E_{2}
, with initial condition
\Phi(0,x,v)=v
. As previously, we write
g(t,x,\Phi(t,x,v))=f(t,x,v)
. We obtain that
g
is solution of

\partial_{t}g+\Phi\cdot\nabla_{x}g+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{1}=0.

Recall that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,x,v)\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)dv=\rho(t,x),

for
\mathcal{J}(t,x,v)=|\det\nabla_{v}\Phi(t,x,v)|
.

We consider the characteristics
X
satisfying

\partial_{t}X(t,s,x,v)=\Phi(t,X(t,s,x,v),v),\quad X(s,s,x,v)=x.

Following the steps of Lemma
8
, we have

\rho(t,x)=K_{H}(U*\rho),

with
H(t,s,x,v)=(\nabla_{v}f_{1})(s,x-(t-s)v,\Psi(t,s,x,v))\mathcal{J}(t,x,\Psi(t,s,x,v))\tilde{\mathcal{J}}(t,s,x,v)
.

We use proposition
4
to show that

\begin{split}{\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}&\leq CT^{\alpha/2}{\left\lVert(U*\rho)\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{\alpha}_{x})}\\
&\leq CT^{\alpha/2}{\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert}_{L^{2}([0,T],L^{2}_{x})}.\end{split}

We take
T_{0}
such that
CT_{0}^{\alpha/2}<1
, and deduce that we must have
\rho=0
on
[0,T_{0}]
. Next, we go back to (
5.1
) on
[0,T_{0}]

\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E_{2}\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0.

f
is solution of an homogeneous transport equation, with initial condition being
0
. That means that
f=0
on all
[0,T_{0}]
. To obtain this result on
[0,T]
, we make the same reasoning on
[T_{0},T]
to obtain the result on
[0,2T_{0}]
and so on.

∎

5.2
Existence of the solution

All is left to do to prove Theorem
1
is to show the existence of solutions.

Let (
f_{\varepsilon}
) be the family of functions solutions of (
1.7
) with the initial conditions
f^{0}_{\varepsilon}=f^{0}
. Thanks to Theorem
2
, there exist
T
and
R
independent of
\varepsilon
such that
f_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m}_{2r})
satisfies

(5.2)

\underset{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}{sup}\mathcal{N}_{m,2r}(T,f_{\varepsilon})\leq R.

We get by (
5.2
) that
f_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
. By (
1.2
), we obtain that
\partial_{t}f_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-2}_{2r-1})
. The Ascoli theorem gives the existence of a function
f\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],L^{2}_{x,v})
and a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}
such that
f_{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges to
f
in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],L^{2}_{x,v})
. By interpolation, we actually have convergence in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1-\delta}_{2r-\delta})
for all
\delta>0
. By Sobolev embedding,
f_{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges to
f
in
L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\rho_{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges to
\rho=\int fdv
in
L^{2}([0,T],L_{x}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus, the limit function
f
solves the system (
1.2
).

To conclude, we want to apply Proposition
5
. We only have to show that
f\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
and that
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})
. By weak compacity arguments,
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
and
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T],H_{x}^{m})
. We then use an energy estimate which have been shown previously (the formula (
2.8
)) to show that

\frac{d}{dt}{\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r}}^{2}\leq C.

It follows that
f\in\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{m-1}_{2r})
. We can finally apply Proposition
5
which gives the uniqueness of
f
and concludes the proof of Theorem
1
.

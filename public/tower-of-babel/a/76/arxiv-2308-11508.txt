Rogue peakon, well-posedness, ill-posedness and blow-up phenomenon for an integrable Camassa-Holm type equation Thanks: Corresponding author
Mingxuan Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we study an integrable Camassa-Holm (CH) type equation with quadratic nonlinearity. The CH type equation is shown integrable through a Lax pair, and particularly the equation is found to possess a new kind of peaked soliton (peakon) solution - called rogue peakon , that is given in a rational form with some logarithmic function, but not a regular traveling wave. We also provide multi-rogue peakon solutions. Furthermore, we discuss the local well-posedness of the solution in the Besov space B p , r s B_{p,r}^{s} with 1 ≤ p , r ≤ ∞ 1\leq p,r\leq\infty , s > max ⁡ { 1 + 1 / p , 3 / 2 } s>\displaystyle\max\left\{1+1/p,3/2\right\} or B 2 , 1 3 / 2 B_{2,1}^{3/2} , and then prove the ill-posedness of the solution in B 2 , ∞ 3 / 2 B_{2,\infty}^{3/2} . Moreover, we establish the global existence and blow-up phenomenon of the solution, which is, if m 0 ​ ( x ) = u 0 − u 0 ​ x ​ x ≥ ( ≢ ) ​ 0 m_{0}(x)=u_{0}-u_{0xx}\geq(\not\equiv)0 , then the corresponding solution exists globally, meanwhile, if m 0 ​ ( x ) ≤ ( ≢ ) ​ 0 m_{0}(x)\leq(\not\equiv)0 , then the corresponding solution blows up in a finite time. Keywords: Camassa-Holm type equation, Lax pair, rogue peakon; well-posedness; ill-posedness; Besov space; blow-up phenomenon. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2020): 37K10; 35G25; 35L05

Introduction and rogue peakons

In this paper, we consider the following Camassa-Holm (CH) type equation that was proposed recently in
[
34
,
36

\displaystyle m_{t}+mu+(mu)_{x}=0,\quad m=u-u_{xx}.

(1.1)

The above CH type equation (
1.1

integrable two-component peakon system
[

\displaystyle\begin{cases}m_{t}=F+F_{x}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m(uv-u_{x}v_{x}+uv_{x}-u_{x}v),\\
n_{t}=-G+G_{x}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u(uv-u_{x}v_{x}+uv_{x}-u_{x}v),\\
m=u-u_{xx},\\
n=v-v_{xx},\end{cases}

and

Let

, and

1.2

1.1

Through a straightforward computation, it is not hard for us to see the scalar CH type equation (
1.1
) possesses the following Lax pair
\psi_{x}=U\psi,\>\psi_{t}=V\psi,

U=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}-1&\lambda m\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad V=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{-2}&-\lambda^{-1}(u-u_{x})+\lambda mu\\
0&-\lambda^{-2}\end{pmatrix},

(1.3)

where
\psi
is the 2-dimensional eigenvector,
\lambda
refers to the eigenvalue parameter, and
U,V
satisfy the following compatibility condition – zero curvature representation
U_{t}-V_{x}+[U,V]=0.
Therefore, the CH type equation (

Rogue Peakon

The most attractive property of the CH type equation (
1.1
) is that it has the following so-called
Rogue Peakon
(see the formula given in Eq. (
1.4

Remark 1

Let

. One may compute

\displaystyle u_{x}=-pe^{-|x-q|}sign(x-q),

\displaystyle m=u-u_{xx}=2p\delta(x-q),

\displaystyle m_{t}=2p^{\prime}\delta(x-q)-2pq^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}(x-q)

\displaystyle m_{x}=2p\delta^{\prime}(x-q),

where
\delta
the Dirac delta and
\delta^{\prime}
is its derivative.
Substituting the above formulas to (

\displaystyle(2p^{\prime}+2p^{2}e^{-|x-q|}-2p^{2}e^{-|x-q|}sign(x-q))\delta(x-q)-2p(q^{\prime}-pe^{-|x-q|})\delta^{\prime}(x-q)=0.

Evaluating this on a test function
\varphi

\displaystyle\langle(2p^{\prime}+2p^{2}e^{-|x-q|}-4p^{2}e^{-|x-q|}sign(x-q))\delta(x-q),\varphi\rangle+\langle 2p(q^{\prime}-pe^{-|x-q|})\delta(x-q),\varphi^{\prime}\rangle=0.

The above equation holds for any

p(t)
and

\displaystyle p^{\prime}=-p^{2},\quad q^{\prime}=p.

Then, we arrive at

\displaystyle p(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{t-A},\quad q(t)\ =\ \ln|t-A|+B,

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{t-A}e^{-|x-\ln|t-A|-B|},

(1.4)

Remark 1:
Apparently, the single peakon solution given in Eq. (

\displaystyle u(A^{-})

Background

The system (
1.1
) is highly related to the celebrated Camassa-Holm(CH) equation

\displaystyle m_{t}+2mu_{x}+m_{x}u=0,\quad m=u-u_{xx},

which was introduced in
[
5
]
and was also obtained by Fokas and Fuchssteiner
[
19
]
as a nonlinear partial differential equation with bi-Hamiltonian structures and inherited symmetries. It was shown completely integrable with the remarkable properties
of possessing Lax pair
[
5
]
, great features on the circle
[
12
]
, the integrable hierarchy extension with finite-dimensional constrained flows and algebro-geometric solutions on a symplectic submanifold connected to integrable negative order flows
[
30
]
, inverse scattering transform
[
11
]
and multi-soliton solutions via Darboux transformation
[
35
]
, infinitely many conservation laws
[
25
]
and geometric background of the CH equation
[
32
]
, and explicit solutions including classical soliton, cuspon and peakon solutions
[
5
,
26
,
31
]
.

Local well-posedness for the initial data in
H^{s}
with
s>3/2
was proved in
[
9
,
27
]
, while Danchin have extended the well-posedness to the Besov space
B_{p,r}^{s}
(

,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
) or

in
[
14
,
15
]
. There have been extensive studies on Blow-up phenomenon of solutions, cf.
[

,
8
,
9
,
10
,
23
,

28
]
and references therein. For global existence, McKean

(See also

, which depends on the shape of

. More precisely, it was proved that the Camassa-Holm equation breaks if and only if some portion of the positive part of

lies to the left of some portion of its negative part. Global conservative solution and global dissipative solution for the Camassa-Holm equation have been shown in
[
3

4

. As for the asymptotic properties, persistence properties and unique continuation of solutions of the Camassa-Holm equation were given in

4
Global existence and blow-up phenomenon

Suppose
u(x,t)
is a solution to the Cauchy problem (
1
) in its lifespan. Let us now consider the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{dq(x,t)}{dt}=u(q,t),\qquad 0<t<T,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
q(x,0)=x,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
T
is the lifespan of the solution.
Taking derivative (
4
) with respect to
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dq_{t}}{dx}=q_{tx}=u_{x}(q,t)q_{x},\qquad t\in(0,T).

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q_{x}=\exp\left(\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}(u_{x}(q,s))ds\right),\qquad 0<t<T,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
q_{x}(x,0)=1,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

which is always positive before the blow-up time. Therefore, the function
q(x,t)
is an increasing diffeomorphism of the line before blow-up.
In fact, direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(e^{q(x,t)}m(q,t)q_{x})=e^{q(x,t)}[m_{t}(q,t)+m(q,t)u(q,t)+(m(q,t)u(q,t))_{x}]q_{x}=0.

Hence, the following identity can be proved:

\displaystyle e^{q(x,t)}m(q,t)q_{x}=e^{x}m_{0}(x),

(4.37)

which implies that
m(x,t)
keeps the sign with respect to the initial datum.

By (
4.37
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{q(x,t)}m(q,t)q_{x}dx=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{q(x,t)}m(q,t)dq=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{x}m(x,t)dx.

It follows a conserved quantity as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{x}m(x,t)dx=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{x}m_{0}dx.

We establish the blow-up scenario for the Cauchy problem (
1
) in the following Theorem.

Theorem 4.1

Let
u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s>\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}
and let
T
be the maximal existence time of the solution
u(x,t)
to (
1
) with the initial data

u_{0}(x)
. Then the corresponding solution blows up in finite time if and only if

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow T-}\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left(u+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}\right)=-\infty.

Proof :

On one hand, suppose for any
t\in(0,T]
,
\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left(u+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}\right)\geq-M
with
M>0
.
Multiplying (
1
) by
m
, after integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|m\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}um^{2}+(um)_{x}m)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}\right)m^{2}dx.

By using Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|m(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|m_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}e^{2MT},

then
\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq\|m(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is bounded. This contradicts with the fact that
T
is the maximal time of existence.

On the other hand, the solution does not blow up in
H^{s},s>\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}
, that is
\|u\|_{H^{s}}
is bounded, by Morrey’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|u+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}<+\infty.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

\Box

5
Conclusion and open problem

In this paper, we develop a new type of soliton solution – called
rogue peakon
in the integrable peakon theory. The rogue peakon takes on a rational form at the peak of
a logarithmic function, instead of a regular traveling wave, and is found in the integrable Camassa-Holm (CH) type equation (
1.1
). We also provide multi-rogue peakon solutions with some interesting interactional dynamics as shown in Figures 2 - 6. Moreover, we discuss the local well-posedness of the solution in the Besov space
B_{p,r}^{s}
with
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
,
s>\displaystyle\max\left\{1+1/p,3/2\right\}
or
B_{2,1}^{3/2}
, and then prove the ill-posedness of the solution in
B_{2,\infty}^{3/2}
. It is worth to point out that the proof of ill-posedness of relies on the single rogue peakon (
1.4
) which is a solution in the sense of distribution. For the classical Camassa-Holm equation, Degasperis-Procesi equation and Holm-Staley
b
-family equation, when
m_{0}(x)
does not change sign, the solution usually exists globally. But for our system, we find a new phenomenon, which is if
m_{0}(x)\geq(\not\equiv)0
, then the corresponding
solution
u(x,t)
exists globally, while if
m_{0}(x)\leq(\not\equiv)0
, then the corresponding
solution
u(x,t)
blows up in a finite time. But the global rogue peakon stability keeps open.

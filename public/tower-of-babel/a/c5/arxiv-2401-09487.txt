Corrigendum to “Kim-independence in positive logic” Thanks: The second author is supported by the EPSRC grant EP/X018997/1.
Jan Dobrowolski and Mark Kamsma

Abstract
. The proof of the Independence Theorem for Kim-independence in positive thick NSOP 1 theories from [ DK22 ] contains a gap. The theorem is still true, and in this corrigendum we give a different proof.



1.
Introduction

In
[
DK22
]
we proved various properties for Kim-independence in thick positive NSOP
1
theories. One of these properties is the Independence Theorem
[
DK22
, Theorem 7.7]
. There is a gap in the proof there, however the statement remains true. In this corrigendum we present an alternative proof, based on the proof in full first-order logic from
[
KR20
]
.

Overview.
After discussing some preliminaries in Section
2
, we discuss the gap in Section
3
. We then develop some technical tools in Sections
4
and
5
, where the former focuses on general tools and the latter focuses on so-called spread out trees, an important concept for Kim-independence in NSOP
1
theories. Finally, in Section
6
we present the new proof of the Independent Theorem.

Kho23
]
. We would also like to thank I. Kaplan and N. Ramsey for discussions leading to this proof.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall some basic definitions and conventions, and refer to
[
DK22
, Sections 2 and 3]
for more details, explanations and references.

Convention 2.1
.

We establish some basic conventions.

(i)

We will drop “positive” everywhere, so “formula”, “type” and “theory” always mean “positive (existential) formula”, “positive (existential) type” and “positive theory” (or “h-inductive theory”) respectively.

(ii)

Whenever we say
type
we mean “maximal type”, otherwise we say
partial type
.

(iii)

We work in a monster model
\mathfrak{M}
, which we often drop from the notation.

(iv)

We assume all parameter sets considered to be small (w.r.t. the monster), unless we consider the monster as a parameter set. Lowercase Latin letters
a,b,\ldots
will denote (possibly infinite) tuples in the monster and uppercase Latin letters
A,B,\ldots
will denote parameter sets in the monster. We will use
M
and
N
when these sets are e.c. models. Lowercase Greek letters
\alpha,\beta,\ldots
are used for realisations of global (partial) types (in a bigger monster).

Definition 2.2
.

We recall some basic definitions.

(i)

The notation
\operatorname{d}_{C}(a,b)\leq n
means that there are
a=a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}=b
such that
a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
are on a
C
-indiscernible sequence for all
0\leq i<n
.

(ii)

A theory
T
is called
thick
if being an indiscernible sequence is type-definable. That is, there is a partial type
\Theta((x_{i})_{i<\omega})
such that
\models\Theta((a_{i})_{i<\omega})
iff
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
is indiscernible. This is equivalent to having
\operatorname{d}_{z}(x,y)\leq n
type-definable for all
n<\omega
.

(iii)

For a theory
T
we define the cardinal
\lambda_{T}=\beth_{(2^{|T|})^{+}}
.

(iv)

A Lascar strong type
\Lstp(a/N)
with
M\subseteq N
is called
M
-Ls-invariant
if for any
b,b^{\prime}\in N
with
b\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{M}b^{\prime}
we have
ab\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{M}ab^{\prime}
.

(v)

Suppose that
q(x)
is a global
M
-Ls-invariant type
q(x)
. A
Morley sequence in
q
(over
M
)
is a sequence
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
such that
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{M}(\alpha_{i})_{i<\omega}
where
(\alpha_{i})_{i<\omega}\models q^{\otimes\omega}
.

We recall some facts that will be used throughout, often implicitly.

3.
The gap

The proof of the Independence Theorem for Kim-independence in thick NSOP
1
theories
[
DK22
, Theorem 7.7]
contains a gap, which we outline in this section. The theorem, as stated, is still true. However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, the gap cannot easily be fixed, so we give a different proof.

We refer to the notation used in the proof of
[
DK22
, Theorem 7.7]
. Everything is fine up to the point where it is argued how the theorem follows from what is called “Claim 2” (at the bottom of Page 88). By compactness, an
M
-indiscernible sequence
(g_{i}h_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime\prime}_{i}h^{\prime\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is extracted from the data from Claim 2. However, it may be that the properties
(h^{\prime\prime}_{i}g^{\prime\prime}_{i+1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\models(q^{\prime}|_{z_{0},y})^{\otimes\mathbb{Z}}|_{M}
and
h_{i}g_{i+1}\equiv_{Mh_{>i}g_{>i+1}h^{\prime\prime}_{>i}g^{\prime\prime}_{i+1}}h^{\prime\prime}_{i}g^{\prime\prime}_{i+1}
are not carried over.

In more detail, the intended argument was as follows. By Claim 2 and compactness we find some sequence
(a_{i}b_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}a^{\prime\prime}_{i}b^{\prime\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}
with those properties, where
\lambda
is sufficiently large. Then we would let
(g_{i}h_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime\prime}_{i}h^{\prime\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be an
M
-indiscernible sequence based on
(a_{i}b_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}a^{\prime\prime}_{i}b^{\prime\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}
using
[
DK22
, Lemma 2.17]
. However, the desired properties are only guaranteed for consecutive elements of
(a_{i}b_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}b^{\prime}_{i}a^{\prime\prime}_{i}b^{\prime\prime}_{i})_{i<\lambda}
, whereas all that we get is that for any
i
there are some
j<k<\lambda
such that

g_{i}h_{i}g^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}_{i}g^{\prime\prime}_{i}h^{\prime\prime}_{i}g_{i+1}h_{i+1}g^{\prime}_{i+1}h^{\prime}_{i+1}g^{\prime\prime}_{i+1}h^{\prime\prime}_{i+1}\equiv_{M}a_{j}b_{j}a^{\prime}_{j}b^{\prime}_{j}a^{\prime\prime}_{j}b^{\prime\prime}_{j}a_{k}b_{k}a^{\prime}_{k}b^{\prime}_{k}a^{\prime\prime}_{k}b^{\prime\prime}_{k}.

Re-indexing the sequence (e.g. shifting part of the indices by one) does not work, as there are other conditions that we also need to be satisfied (i.e.
(g^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}_{i+1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\models(q^{\prime}|_{y_{0},z})^{\otimes\mathbb{Z}}|_{M}
and
g_{i}h_{i}\equiv_{Mg_{>i}h_{>i}g^{\prime}_{>i}h^{\prime}_{>i}}g^{\prime}_{i}h^{\prime}_{i}
). We also really need an
M
-indiscernible sequence to apply
[
DK22
, Lemma 2.28]
. Any attempt (that the authors have tried) to make the sequences indiscernible in some other way, for example by trying to work with str-indiscernible trees, ultimately runs into similar issues.

4.
Technical tools

We reformulate the chain condition in a form that will be useful to us.

Lemma 4.1

(chain condition)
.

Let

be a thick NSOP

theory. Suppose that
a\mathop{\mathchoice{\displaystyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\textstyle\kern 5.71527pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 3.87495pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 5.71527pt}{\scriptstyle\kern 2.80048pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 1.89871pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 2.80048pt}{\scriptscriptstyle\kern 1.42882pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\mid$\hss}\lower 0.96873pt\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\smile$\hss}\kern 1.42882pt}}^{K}_{M}b
and that

is a Morley sequence in some global

-Ls-invariant type with

. Then, writing

, we have that

\bigcup_{i<\omega}p(x,b_{i})

does not Kim-divide over

Let

be the global

is a Morley sequence. As

[

, Proposition 4.2]
that there is
Ma
-indiscernible

with

. So we have
(b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\omega}\equiv_{M}(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
and we let

be such that
a(b^{\prime}_{i})_{i<\omega}\equiv_{M}a^{*}(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
. Then
(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
is

for all

.
∎

Proposition 4.2

(Being Ls-invariant is type-definable)
.

Let
T
be a thick theory. Let
C
be some parameter set and let
N\supseteq C
be
(2^{|C|+\lambda_{T}})^{+}
-saturated (possibly
N
is the monster). Define
\Sigma(x)
to be the following partial type over
N

\bigcup\{\operatorname{d}_{C}(xb,xb^{\prime})\leq 2:b,b^{\prime}\in N\text{ are finite tuples such that }\operatorname{d}_{C}(b,b^{\prime})\leq 1\}.

Then a type
q(x)
over
N
is
C
-Ls-invariant iff
\Sigma(\alpha)
for
\alpha\models q
.

Proof.

Let
q(x)
be a
C
-Ls-invariant type over
N
and let
\alpha\models q
. Let
b,b^{\prime}\in N
be finite tuples such that
\operatorname{d}_{C}(b,b^{\prime})\leq 1
. Then there is a
C
-indiscernible sequence
(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
with
b_{0}b_{1}=bb^{\prime}
, which we may assume to be in
N
by saturation. Using saturation again, we find a
\lambda_{T}
-saturated
C\subseteq M\subseteq N
such that
(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
is
M
-indiscernible. In particular this means that
bM\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{C}b^{\prime}M
and so
\alpha bM\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{C}\alpha b^{\prime}M
. It follows that
\alpha b\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{M}\alpha b^{\prime}
and thus by our choice of
M
we get
\operatorname{d}_{C}(\alpha b,\alpha b^{\prime})\leq 2
. As
b,b^{\prime}
were arbitrary, we conclude that
\models\Sigma(\alpha)
.

For the other direction we let
q(x)
be a type over
N
such that for
\alpha\models q
we have
\models\Sigma(\alpha)
. Now let
d,d^{\prime}\in N
be (potentially infinite tuples) such that
d\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{C}d^{\prime}
. Let
n<\omega
be such that
\operatorname{d}_{C}(d,d^{\prime})\leq n
, we claim that
\operatorname{d}_{C}(\alpha d,\alpha d^{\prime})\leq 2n
, which implies the required
\alpha d\equiv^{\textup{Ls}}_{C}\alpha d^{\prime}
. By thickness we have that the partial type
\operatorname{d}_{C}(\alpha d,\alpha d^{\prime})\leq 2n
is given by

\bigcup\{\operatorname{d}_{C}(\alpha b,\alpha b^{\prime})\leq 2n:b\subseteq d\text{ and }b^{\prime}\subseteq d^{\prime}\text{ are finite matching tuples}\}.

So we have reduced the problem to the case where
d
and
d^{\prime}
are finite. By saturation then there are
d=d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}=d^{\prime}
in
N
such that
\operatorname{d}_{C}(d_{i},d_{i+1})\leq 1
for all
0\leq i<n
. By assumption we thus have that
\operatorname{d}_{C}(\alpha d_{i},\alpha d_{i+1})\leq 2
for all
0\leq i<n
. We conclude that
\operatorname{d}_{C}(\alpha d,\alpha d^{\prime})\leq 2n
, as required.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Extending Ls-invariant types)
.

Let
T
be a thick theory. Let
N\supseteq C
be
(2^{|C|+\lambda_{T}})^{+}
-saturated. Suppose that
p(x)=\tp(a/N)
is a
C
-Ls-invariant type, then
p(x)
extends to a unique global
C
-Ls-invariant type

.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma(x)
be the global partial type from ?THM?
expressing
C
-Ls-invariance. We will show that
p(x)\cup\Sigma(x)
is finitely satisfiable. So let
\varphi(x,e)\in p(x)
, where
e
is a tuple of parameters from
N
, and let
\Sigma_{0}(x)\subseteq\Sigma(x)
be finite. Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
and
b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{n}
be the finite tuples that occur in
\Sigma_{0}(x)
, so
\operatorname{d}_{C}(b_{i},b^{\prime}_{i})\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. By saturation of
N
we find
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n},d^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,d^{\prime}_{n}\in N
such that
d_{1}\ldots d_{n}d^{\prime}_{1}\ldots d^{\prime}_{n}\equiv_{Ce}b_{1}\ldots b_{n}b^{\prime}_{1}\ldots b^{\prime}_{n}
. So for all
1\leq i\leq n
we have
\operatorname{d}_{C}(d_{i},d^{\prime}_{i})\leq 1
, and hence
\operatorname{d}_{C}(ad_{i},ad^{\prime}_{i})\leq 2
by ?THM?
applied to

. Now let
a^{*}
be such that

. Then by construction we have that

and

, which proves finite satisfiability of

. By compactness we then find a realisation
\alpha
of
p(x)\cup\Sigma(x)
, so that

is our desired
C
-Ls-invariant type. The uniqueness claim follows from ?THM?
(iii).
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
T
be a thick theory. If
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
is a
C
-indiscernible sequence such that

5.
Spread out trees

We recall various definitions concerning trees and trees of parameters (which we will from now on also simply call trees) from
[

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
from
[

, Definition 5.1]
and we use the same notation, so we assume familiarity with those definitions. We refer to
[
Kam23
]
for the definitions and terminology involving s-indiscernibility, str-indiscernibility and generalised
\EM
-types. We slightly adjust
[
KR20
, Definition 5.7]
to fit our situation.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(a_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}}
be a tree and let
M
be an e.c. model.

(i)

We call
(a_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}}

spread out over
M
if for all
\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
with
\dom(\eta)=[\beta+1,\alpha)
for some
\beta<\alpha
, there is a global
M
-Ls-invariant type
q_{\eta}\supseteq\tp(a_{\unrhd\eta^{\frown}\langle 0\rangle}/M)
such that
(a_{\unrhd\eta^{\frown}\langle i\rangle})_{i<\omega}
is a Morley sequence in
q_{\eta}
over
M
.

(ii)

A
Morley tree over
M
is an str-indiscernible and spread out tree over
M
.

(iii)

A
tree Morley sequence over
M
is a branch in an infinite height Morley tree over
M
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
is a tree Morley sequence over
M
.

(i)

If
b_{i}\subseteq a_{i}
for each
i<\omega
, of matching length and position, then
(b_{i})_{i<\omega}
is a tree Morley sequence over
M
.

(ii)

Fix
1\leq n<\omega
and define
d_{i}=(a_{ni},\ldots,a_{ni+n-1})
for all

. Then
(d_{i})_{i<\omega}
is a tree Morley sequence over
M
.

Proof.

This is essentially
[
KR20
, Lemma 5.9]
, but we work with slightly different definitions, so we go through the proof here. Part (i) is clear, because being a Morley tree is preserved under taking subtuples. For (ii) we let
(b_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
be a Morley tree such that
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
is a branch in
(b_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathcal{T}_{\omega}}
. We may assume that
(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
is the branch indexed by the constant zero functions. We define
j:\mathcal{T}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{T}_{\omega}
so that for

Remark 5.9
.

?THM?

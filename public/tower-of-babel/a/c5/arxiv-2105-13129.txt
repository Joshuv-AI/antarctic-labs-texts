A New Generalization of Rhoades’ Condition
NİHAL TAŞ, NİHAL ÖZGÜR

Abstract
. In this paper, our aim is to obtain a new generalization of the well-kown Rhoades’ contractive condition. To do this, we introduce the notion of an S S -normed space. We extend the Rhoades’ contractive condition to S S -normed spaces and define a new type of contractive conditions. We support our theoretical results with necessary illustrative examples.

[

Let

\mathbf{(S1)}

\displaystyle\mathbf{(S25)}\text{ \ \ \ \ }\mathcal{S}(Tx,Tx,Ty)

2.
S
-Normed Spaces

In this section, we introduce the notion of an
S
-normed
space and investigate some basic concepts related to an
S
-norm. We study
the relationships between an
S
-metric and an
S
-norm (resp. an
S
-norm
and a norm).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a real vector space. A real valued function
\|.,.,.\|:X\times X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an
S
-norm on
X
if the following conditions hold:

\mathbf{(NS1)}

\|x,y,z\|\geq 0
and
\|x,y,z\|=0
if and
only if
x=y=z=0
,

\mathbf{(NS2)}

\|\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda z\|=\mid\lambda\mid\|x,y,z\|
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,y,z\in X
,

\mathbf{(NS3)}

\|x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}\|\leq\|0,x,z^{\prime}\|+\|0,y,x^{\prime}\|+\|0,z,y^{\prime}\|
for all
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in X
.

The pair
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
is called an
S
-normed space.

Example 2.1
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}
and
\|.,.,.\|:X\times X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function defined by

\|x,y,z\|=\mid x\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid z\mid,

for all
x,y,z\in X
. Then
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
is an
S
-normed space.
Indeed, we show that the function
\|x,y,z\|=\mid x\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid z\mid
satisfies the conditions
(\mathbf{NS1})
,
(\mathbf{NS2})

and
(\mathbf{NS3})
.

(\mathbf{NS1})
By the definition, clearly we have
\|x,y,z\|\geq 0

for all
x,y,z\in X
. If
\|x,y,z\|=\mid x\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid z\mid=0
, we obtain
x=y=z=0
.

(\mathbf{NS2})
Let
x,y,z\in X
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda z\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\lambda x\mid+\mid\lambda y\mid+\mid\lambda z\mid=\mid\lambda\mid\mid x\mid+\mid\lambda\mid\mid y\mid+\mid\lambda\mid\mid z\mid

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\lambda\mid(\mid x\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid z\mid)=\mid\lambda\mid\|x,y,z\|.

(\mathbf{NS3})
Let
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in X
. Then
we obtain

\displaystyle\|x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid x+x^{\prime}\mid+\mid y+y^{\prime}\mid+\mid z+z^{\prime}\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mid x\mid+\mid x^{\prime}\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid y^{\prime}\mid+\mid z\mid+\mid z^{\prime}\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mid 0\mid+\mid x\mid+\mid z^{\prime}\mid+\mid 0\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid x^{\prime}\mid+\mid 0\mid+\mid z\mid+\mid y^{\prime}\mid

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|0,x,z^{\prime}\|+\|0,y,x^{\prime}\|+\|0,z,y^{\prime}\|.

Consequently, the function
\|x,y,z\|=\mid x\mid+\mid y\mid+\mid z\mid
satisfies the conditions
(\mathbf{NS1})
,
(\mathbf{NS2})
,
(\mathbf{NS3})
and so
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
is an
S
-normed space.

Now, we show that every
S
-norm generates an
S
-metric.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
be an
S
-normed space. Then the
function
\mathcal{S}:X\times X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)

defined by

S(x,y,z)=\|x-y,y-z,z-x\|

(2.1)

is an
S
-metric on
X
.

Proof.

Using the condition
(\mathbf{NS1})
, it can be easily seen that the
condition
(\mathbf{S1})
is satisfied. We show that the condition
(\mathbf{S2})
is satisfied. By
(\mathbf{NS3})
, we have

\displaystyle S(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x-y,y-z,z-x\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x-a+a-y,y-a+a-z,z-a+a-x\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|0,x-a,a-x\|+\|0,y-a,a-y\|+\|0,z-a,a-z\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(x,x,a)+S(y,y,a)+S(z,z,a),

for all
x,y,z,a\in X
.

Then, the function
\mathcal{S}
is an
S
-metric and the pair
(X,S)
is an

S
-metric space.
∎

We call the
S
-metric defined in (
2.1
) as the
S
-metric generated
by the
S
-norm
\|.,.,.\|
and denoted by
S_{\|.\|}
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Every
S
-normed space is an
S
-metric space.

Example 2.2
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set,
(X,d)
be a metric space and
\mathcal{S}:X\times X\times X\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
be the

\mathcal{S}(x,y,z)=d(x,y)+d(x,z)+d(y,z),

for all
x,y,z\in X
. Then the function is an
S
-metric on
X

[
15
]
.

Let

. If we consider the usual metric

\mathcal{S}(x,y,z)=\mid x-y\mid+\mid x-z\mid+\mid y-z\mid,

for all

\mathcal{S}
is generated by

\displaystyle S(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x-y,y-z,z-x\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid x-y\mid+\mid y-z\mid+\mid z-x\mid

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid x-y\mid+\mid x-z\mid+\mid y-z\mid

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(x,y)+d(x,z)+d(y,z),

Lemma 2.1
.

An
S
-metric
\mathcal{S}
generated by an

-norm on an
S
-normed space

(1)

\mathcal{S}(x+a,y+a,z+a)=\mathcal{S}(x,y,z)

(2)

\mathcal{S}(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda z)=\left|\lambda\right|\mathcal{S}(x,y,z)

The proof follows easily from the Proposition

Example 2.3

Let
X
be a nonempty set and a function

S(x,y,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&;&\text{if }x=y=z\\

for all

\mathcal{S}
is an

. We call this

-metric on

. The pair
(X,S)

is called discrete

-metric space. Now, we prove that this

not be generated by an

-norm. On the contrary, we assume that this

-metric is generated by an

-norm. Then the following equation should be
satisfied
:

S(x,y,z)=\|x-y,y-z,z-x\|,

for all

If we consider the case

\mid\lambda\mid\neq 0,1

\displaystyle S(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda z)

\displaystyle\|0,\lambda(y-z),\lambda(z-x)\|=1

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
be an
S
-normed space. We have

\|0,x-y,y-x\|=\|0,y-x,x-y\|,

for each

Proof.

By the condition
(\mathbf{NS3})
, we get

\|0,x-y,y-x\|\leq\|0,0,0\|+\|0,0,0\|+\|0,y-x,x-y\|=\|0,y-x,x-y\|

(2.2)

and

\|0,y-x,x-y\|\leq\|0,0,0\|+\|0,0,0\|+\|0,x-y,y-x\|=\|0,x-y,y-x\|.

(2.3)

Using (

) and (

\|0,x-y,y-x\|=\|0,y-x,x-y\|
.

We recall the definition of a norm on
X
as follows.

Let
X
be a real vector space. A real valued function
\left\|.\right\|:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a norm on
X
if the following conditions hold:

(\mathbf{N1})

\|x\|\geq 0
for all
x\in X
.

(\mathbf{N2})

\|x\|=0
if and only if
x=0
for all
x\in X
.

(\mathbf{N3})

\|\lambda x\|=\mid\lambda\mid\|x\|
for
all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in X
.

(\mathbf{N4})

\|x+y\|\leq\|x\|+\|y\|
for all
x,y\in X
.

The pair
(X,\|.\|)
is called a normed space.

We show that every norm generates an
S
-norm. We give the following
proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(X,\|.\|)
be a normed space and a function
\|.,.,.\|:X\times X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\|x,y,z\|=\|x\|+\|y\|+\|z\|,

(2.4)

for all
x,y,z\in X
. Then
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
is an
S
-normed space.

Proof.

We show that the function
\|x,y,z\|=\|x\|+\|y\|+\|z\|
satisfies the conditions
(\mathbf{NS1})
,
(\mathbf{NS2})

and
(\mathbf{NS3})
.

(\mathbf{NS1})
It is clear that
\|x,y,z\|\geq 0
and
\|x,y,z\|=0
if and only if
x=y=z=0
.

(\mathbf{NS2})
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,y,z\in X
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\|\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda z\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\lambda x\|+\|\lambda y\|+\|\lambda z\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\lambda\mid\|x\|+\mid\lambda\mid\|y\|+\mid\lambda\mid\|z\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\lambda\mid(\|x\|+\|y\|+\|z\|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid\lambda\mid\|x,y,z\|.

(\mathbf{NS3})
Let
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in X
. Then
we obtain

\displaystyle\|x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x+x^{\prime}\|+\|y+y^{\prime}\|+\|z+z^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x\|+\|x^{\prime}\|+\|y\|+\|y^{\prime}\|+\|z\|+\|z^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|0\|+\|x\|+\|z^{\prime}\|+\|0\|+\|y\|+\|x^{\prime}\|+\|0\|+\|z\|+\|y^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|0,x,z^{\prime}\|+\|0,y,x^{\prime}\|+\|0,z,y^{\prime}\|.

Consequently, the function
\|x,y,z\|=\|x\|+\|y\|+\|z\|
satisfies the conditions
(\mathbf{NS1})
,
(\mathbf{NS2})
,

(\mathbf{NS3})
and so
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
is an
S
-normed space.
∎

We have proved that every norm on
X
defines an
S
-norm on
X
. We call
the
S
-norm defined in (

) as the
S
-norm generated by the norm
\|.\|
. For example, the
S
-norm defined in Example
2.1
is
the
S
-norm generated by the usual norm on
\mathbb{R}
.

There exists an
S
-norm which is not generated by a norm as we have seen in
the following example.

Example 2.4
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set and the function
\|.,.,.\|:X\times X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined by

\|x,y,z\|=\mid x-2y-2z\mid+\mid y-2x-2z\mid+\mid z-2y-2x\mid\text{,}

for all
x,y,z\in X
. Then, the function
\|x,y,z\|
is an
S
-norm
on

, but it is not generated by a norm.

Now, we show that the conditions
(\mathbf{NS1})
,
(\mathbf{NS2})
and
(\mathbf{NS3})
are satisfied.

(\mathbf{NS1})
By the definition, clearly we obtain
\|x,y,z\|\geq 0
and
\|x,y,z\|=0
if and only if
x=y=z=0
for all
x,y,z\in X

(\mathbf{NS2})
We have

\displaystyle\|\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda z\|

\displaystyle\mid\lambda x-2\lambda y-2\lambda z\mid+\mid\lambda y-2\lambda x-2\lambda z\mid+\mid\lambda z-2\lambda y-2\lambda x\mid

\displaystyle\mid\lambda\mid(\mid x-2y-2z\mid+\mid y-2x-2z\mid+\mid z-2y-2x\mid)

\displaystyle\mid\lambda\mid\|x,y,z\|\text{,}

.

(\mathbf{NS3})
Let
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in X
. Then

\displaystyle\|x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mid x+x^{\prime}-2y-2y^{\prime}-2z-2z^{\prime}\mid

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mid

\displaystyle y+y^{\prime}-2x-2x^{\prime}-2z-2z^{\prime}\mid

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mid

\displaystyle z+z^{\prime}-2y-2y^{\prime}-2x-2x^{\prime}\mid

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mid 2x+2z^{\prime}\mid+\mid x-2z^{\prime}\mid+\mid z^{\prime}-2x\mid

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mid

\displaystyle 2y+2x^{\prime}\mid+\mid y-2x^{\prime}\mid+\mid x^{\prime}-2y\mid

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mid

\displaystyle 2z+2y^{\prime}\mid+\mid z-2y^{\prime}\mid+\mid y^{\prime}-2z\mid

-norm on

.

On the contrary, we assume that this

\|x,y,z\|=\|x\|+\|y\|+\|z\|\text{,}

If we consider

is called a

\|x,y,z\|\geq 0

\|x,y,z\|=0

.

Proposition 2.4
.

Every

-normed space is an
S
-normed space.

Proof.

Using the conditions
(\mathbf{NG1})
and
(\mathbf{NG3})
, we see that the
conditions
(\mathbf{NS1})
and
(\mathbf{NS2})
are satisfied. We only show
that the condition
(\mathbf{NS3})
is satisfied.

(\mathbf{NS3})
Let
x,y,z,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in X
. Using
the conditions
(\mathbf{NG2})
and
(\mathbf{NG4})

Definition 2.4
.

Let

be an

-normed space.

(1)

\varepsilon>0

such that

\|0,x_{n}-x,x-x_{n}\|<\varepsilon\text{,}

for all

.

(2)

A sequence
\{x_{n}\}

\underset{n,m,l\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\|x_{n}-x_{m},x_{m}-x_{l},x_{l}-x_{n}\|=0\text{.}

That is, for each

.

Proposition 2.5
.

Every convergent sequence in an
S
-normed space is a Cauchy
sequence.

Proof.

Let the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
be convergent to
x
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|0,x_{n}-x,x-x_{n}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\text{,}

for all
n\geq n_{0}
. We now show that for each
\varepsilon>0
there
exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\|x_{n}-x_{m},x_{m}-x_{l},x_{l}-x_{n}\|<\varepsilon\text{,}

for all
n,m,l\geq n_{0}
. Using the condition
(\mathbf{NS3})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-x_{m},x_{m}-x_{l},x_{l}-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-x+x-x_{m},x_{m}-x+x-x_{l},x_{l}-x+x-x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|0,x_{n}-x,x-x_{n}\|+\|0,x_{m}-x,x-x_{m}\|

\displaystyle+\|0,x_{l}-x,x-x_{l}\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon\text{.}

Consequently, the sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
is a Cauchy sequence.
∎

3.
A Fixed Point Theorem on
S
-Normed Spaces

In this section, we introduce the Rhoades’ condition on an
S
-normed space and denote it by
(\mathbf{NS25})
. We prove a fixed point
teorem using this contractive condition.

At first, we give some definitions and a proposition which are needed in the
sequel.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
be an
S
-normed space and
E\subseteq X
. The closure of
E
, denoted by
\overline{E}
, is the set of
all
x\in X
such that there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
E
converging
to
x
. If
E=\overline{E}
, then
E
is called a closed set.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
be an
S
-normed space and
A\subseteq X
. The subset
A
is called bounded if there exists
r>0
such
that

\|0,x-y,y-x\|<r,

for all
x,y\in A
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
be an
S
-normed space and
A\subseteq X
. The
S
-diameter of
A
is defined by

\delta^{s}(A)=\sup\{\|0,x-y,y-x\|:x,y\in A\}\text{.}

If
A
is bounded then we will write
\delta^{s}(A)<\infty
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
X
be an
S
-Banach space,
A\subseteq X
and
u\in X
.

(1)

The
S
-radius of
A
relative to a given
u\in X
is defined by

r_{u}^{s}(A)=\sup\{\|0,u-x,x-u\|:x\in A\}\text{.}

(2)

The
S
-Chebyshev radius of
A
is defined by

r^{s}(A)=\inf\{r_{u}^{s}(A):u\in A\}\text{.}

(3)

The
S
-Chebyshev centre of
A
is defined by

C^{s}(A)=\{u\in A:r_{u}^{s}(A)=r^{s}(A)\}\text{.}

By Definition
3.3
and Definition
3.4
, it can be easily seen
the following inequality:

r^{s}(A)\leq r_{u}^{s}(A)\leq\delta^{s}(A)\text{.}

Definition 3.5
.

A point
u\in A
is called
S
-diametral if
r_{u}^{s}(A)=\delta^{s}(A)
. If
r_{u}^{s}(A)<\delta^{s}(A)
, then
u
is
called non-
S
-diametral.

Definition 3.6
.

A convex subset of an
S
-Banach space
X
has
S
-normal
structure if every
S
-bounded and convex subset of
A
having
\delta^{s}(A)>0
has at least one non-
S
-diametral point.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
X
is a reflexive
S
-Banach space,
A
is a nonempty,
closed and convex subset of
X
, then
C^{s}(A)
is nonempty, closed and
convex.

Proof.

It can be easily seen by definition of
C^{s}(A)
.
∎

Now we introduce the Rhoades’ condition
(\mathbf{NS25})
on an
S
-Banach
space.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(X,\|.,.,.\|)
be an
S
-Banach space and
T
be
a self-mapping of
X
. We define

\begin{array}[]{r}(\mathbf{NS25})\text{ \ \ }\|0,Tx-Ty,Ty-Tx\|<\max\{\|0,x-y,y-x\|,\|0,Tx-x,x-Tx\|,\\
\|0,Ty-y,y-Ty\|,\|0,Ty-x,x-Ty\|,\|0,Tx-y,y-Tx\|\}\text{,}\end{array}

for each
x,y\in X
,
x\neq y
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
16
]
Let
X
be a Banach space. Then
X
is reflexive
if and only if for any decreasing sequence
\{K_{n}\}
of nonempty, bounded,
closed and convex subsets of
X
,

\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}\neq\emptyset\text{.}

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be an
S
-Banach space. Then
X
is reflexive if and
only if for any decreasing sequence
\{K_{n}\}
of nonempty, bounded, closed
and convex subsets of
X
,

\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}\neq\emptyset\text{.}

Proof.

By the definition of reflexivity the proof follows easily.
∎

Recall that the convex hull of a set
A
is denoted by
conv(A)
and any
member of this set
conv(A)
has the form

\overset{n}{\sum\limits_{i=1}}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\text{,}

where
x_{i}\in A_{i},\alpha_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,...,n
and
\overset{n}{\sum\limits_{i=1}}\alpha_{i}=1
.

Now, we give the following fixed point theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a reflexive
S
-Banach space and
A
be a nonempty,
closed, bounded and convex subset of
X
, having
S
-normal structure. If
T:A\rightarrow A
is a continuous self-mapping satisfying the condition
(\mathbf{NS25})
then
T
has a unique fixed point in
A
.

Proof.

At first, we show that the existence of the fixed point. Let
\mathcal{A}

be the family of every nonempty, closed and convex subsets of
A
. Also we
assume that if
F\in

\mathcal{A}
then
TF\subseteq F
. The family
\mathcal{A}
is nonempty since
A\in

\mathcal{A}
. We can partially order

\mathcal{A}
by set inclusion, that is, if
F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}
then
F_{1}\leq F_{2}
.

In
\mathcal{A}
, if we define a decreasing net of subsets

S=\{F_{i}:F_{i}\in\text{$\mathcal{A}$},i\in I\}\text{,}

then by reflexivity, this net
S
has nonempty intersection. Because it is a
decreasing net of nonempty, closed, bounded and convex subsets of
X
. If we
put
F_{0}=\bigcap\limits_{i\in I}F_{i}
we have that
F_{0}
is in
\mathcal{A}
and is a lower bound of
S
.

Using Zorn’s Lemma, there is a minimal element, denoted by
F
, in
\mathcal{A}
as
S
is any arbitrary decreasing net in
\mathcal{A}
. We see that
this
F
is a singleton.

Assume that
\delta^{s}(F)\neq\emptyset
. Since
F
is nonempty, closed
and convex,
C^{s}(F)
is a nonempty, closed and convex subset of
F
. We
have that

r^{s}(F)<\delta^{s}(F)\text{,}

\delta^{s}(C^{s}(F))\leq r^{s}(F)<\delta^{s}(F)

and so
C^{s}(F)
is a proper subset of
F
.

Let
(F_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of subsets of
F
, defined by

F_{1}=C^{s}(F)\text{ and }F_{m+1}=conv(F_{m}\cup TF_{m})\text{,}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. If we denote the
S
-diameters of these sets
F_{k}
by
\delta_{k}^{s}=\delta^{s}(F_{k})
, we show that

\delta_{k}^{s}\leq r^{s}(F)\text{,}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Using the
(PMI)
, we obtain

(1)

For
k=1
,

\delta_{1}^{s}=\delta^{s}(F_{1})=\delta^{s}(C^{s}(F))\leq r^{s}(F)\text{.}

(2)

If
\delta_{k}^{s}\leq r^{s}(F)
for every
k=1,...,m
then
\delta_{m+1}^{s}\leq r^{s}(F)
.

We note that

\delta_{m+1}^{s}=\delta^{s}(F_{m+1})=\delta^{s}(conv(F_{m}\cup TF_{m}))=\delta^{s}(F_{m}\cup TF_{m})\text{.}

By the definition of
S
-diameter, for any given
\varepsilon>0
there are
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
in
F_{m}\cup T(F_{m})
satisfying

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<\|0,x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|\leq\delta_{m+1}^{s}\text{.}

We obtain the following three cases for
x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}
:

(1)

x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}\in F_{m}
or

(2)

x^{\prime}\in F_{m}
and
y^{\prime}\in TF_{m}
or

(3)

x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}\in TF_{m}
.

Redefining
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
as follows:

(1)

x^{\prime}=x
and
y^{\prime}=y
with
x,y\in F_{m}
,

(2)

x^{\prime}=x
and
y^{\prime}=Ty
with
x,y\in F_{m}
,

(3)

x^{\prime}=Tx
and
y^{\prime}=Ty
with
x,y\in F_{m}
.

We show that in any case

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<r^{s}(F)\text{.}

Case 1
. By the definition of
\delta_{m}^{s}
and the induction
hypothesis, we obtain

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<\|0,x-y,y-x\|\leq\delta_{m+1}^{s}\leq r^{s}(F)

(3.1)

and so
\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<r^{s}(F)
.

Case 2
. We obtain

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|

with
x
,
y\in F_{m}
. Then by the definition of
F_{m}
, we have
x,y\in conv(F_{m-1}\cup TF_{m-1})
and so there is a finite index set
I
such that

x=\sum\limits_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}x_{i}
, with
\sum\limits_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}=1
,
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
and
x_{i}\in F_{m-1}\cup TF_{m-1}
for any

. We can separate the set
I
in two disjoint subsets,
I=I_{1}\cup I_{2}
, such that if
i\in I_{1}
then
x_{i}\in F_{m-1}
and if
i\in I_{2}

then
x_{i}\in TF_{m-1}
.

Now redefining
x_{i}
as
x_{i}=Tx_{i}
with
x_{i}\in F_{m-1}
, we obtain

x=\sum\limits_{i\in I_{1}}\alpha_{i}x_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in I_{2}}\alpha_{i}Tx_{i}\text{.}

Substituting in
\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|
, we get

\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|\leq\sum\limits_{i\in I_{1}}\alpha_{i}\|0,x_{i}-Ty,Ty-x_{i}\|+\sum\limits_{i\in I_{2}}\alpha_{i}\|0,Tx_{i}-Ty,Ty-Tx_{i}\|\text{.}

(3.2)

Applying the condition
(\mathbf{NS25})
to

\begin{array}[]{r}\|0,Tx_{i}-Ty,Ty-Tx_{i}\|<\max\{\|0,x_{i}-y,y-x_{i}\|,\|0,x_{i}-Tx_{i},Tx_{i}-x_{i}\|,\\
\|0,y-Ty,Ty-y\|,\|0,x_{i}-Ty,Ty-x_{i}\|,\|0,Tx_{i}-y,y-Tx_{i}\|\}\text{.}\end{array}

As
x_{i}\in F_{m-1}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|0,x_{i}-y,y-x_{i}\|\leq r^{s}(F),\\

\|0,Tx_{i}-y,y-Tx_{i}\|\leq r^{s}(F)\end{array}

Let us subdivide the index set

\begin{array}[]{c}\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|\leq\sum\limits_{i\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}^{2}}\alpha_{i}\|0,x_{i}-Ty,Ty-x_{i}\|+\sum\limits_{i\in I_{2}^{1}}\alpha_{i}r^{s}(F)\\
+\sum\limits_{i\in I_{2}^{3}}\alpha_{i}\|0,y-Ty,Ty-y\|\text{.}\end{array}

(3.4)

Redefining
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and

\overline{I_{1}}=I_{1}\cup I_{2}^{2}\text{, }\overline{I_{2}}=I_{2}^{1}\text{
and }\overline{I_{3}}=I_{2}^{3}\text{.}

(3.5)

Then we have
I=\overline{I_{1}}\cup\overline{I_{2}}\cup\overline{I_{3}}
,
with
\overline{I_{j}}\cap\overline{I_{k}}=\emptyset
. If
j\neq k
and
\sum\limits_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}=1
then using (

\begin{array}[]{c}\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|\leq\sum\limits_{i\in\overline{I_{1}}}\alpha_{i}\|0,x_{i}-Ty,Ty-x_{i}\|+\sum\limits_{i\in\overline{I_{2}}}\alpha_{i}r^{s}(F)\\
+\sum\limits_{i\in\overline{I_{3}}}\alpha_{i}\|0,y-Ty,Ty-y\|\text{.}\end{array}

(3.6)

If

For each

3.17

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|\leq r^{s}(F).

Case 3
. For
x,y\in F_{m}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{c}\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<\|0,Tx-Ty,Ty-Tx\|<\max\{\|0,x-y,y-x\|,\|0,Tx-x,x-Tx\|,\\
\|0,y-Ty,Ty-y\|,\|0,x-Ty,Ty-x\|,\|0,Tx-y,y-Tx\|\}\end{array}

(3.28)

and repeating what has been done in
Case 2

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<\|0,Tx-Ty,Ty-Tx\|\leq r^{s}(F).

\delta_{m+1}^{s}-\varepsilon<r^{s}(F)
. If
\varepsilon
tends to
0
we get
\delta_{m+1}^{s}\leq r^{s}(F)
.

Let
F^{\infty}=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}F_{n}
. Then
F^{\infty}
is nonempty because
C^{s}(F)\neq\emptyset
.
Since
F_{k}\subseteq F_{k+1}
, we obtain

\delta^{s}(F^{\infty})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\delta^{s}(F_{k})\leq r^{s}(F).

As
F_{k}\subset F
,
F^{\infty}\subseteq F
and so
\delta^{s}(F^{\infty})\leq r^{s}(F)
.

Using the
S
-normal structure of
F
we have
r^{s}(F)<\delta^{s}(F)
and
\delta^{s}(F^{\infty})<\delta^{s}(F)
. So
F^{\infty}
must be a proper
subset of
F
. We obtain that
F^{\infty}
is convex and
TF^{\infty}\subseteq F^{\infty}
.

Let
M=\overline{convF^{\infty}}=\overline{F^{\infty}}
, its diameter is
the same as
F^{\infty}
. So we have

\delta^{s}(M)\leq r^{s}(F)<\delta^{s}(F)

and
M
is closed, nonempty and convex proper subset of
F
. Since
T
is
continuous then
M
is
T
-invariant and

TM=TF^{\infty}\subseteq\overline{TF^{\infty}}\subseteq\overline{F^{\infty}}=M.

So
M\in\mathcal{A}
and
M\subsetneqq F
contradicting the minimality of
F
. Hence, it should be
\delta^{s}(F)=0
. Consequently,
F
has a unique
fixed point under
T
.

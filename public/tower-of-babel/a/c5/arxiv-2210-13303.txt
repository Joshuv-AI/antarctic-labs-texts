Small Cancellation Rings are non-amenable
A. Atkarskaya

Abstract
. We show that small cancellation rings defined in [ 2 ] under some natural extra restrictions are non-amenable and contain free associative algebra with two free generators.



1.
Introduction

The small cancellation theory for groups is well known (see
[
10
]
). In particular, it is known that finitely presented small cancellation groups turned out to be word hyperbolic (when every relation needs at least 7 pieces). In papers
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
we develop group-like small cancellation theory for associative rings. We introduce these rings by generators and defining relations, define small pieces and develop ring analogues of small cancellation conditions. The main result of
[
2
]
is that such rings are non-trivial. Moreover there exists Dehn’s-type algorithm aimed to check whether a given element of a small cancellation ring is trivial or non-trivial (see
[
9
]
).

Now it seems quite important to study further properties of small cancellation rings. M. Gromov conjectured that small cancellation algebras are non-amenable (M. Gromov, private correspondence), see
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, for the definition of amenable associative algebras. In this paper we prove this conjecture. The main result is stated in Theorem
4
and claims that under some natural conditions small cancellation rings are non-amenable.

One can easily check that the free algebra with at least two free generators is non-amenable using the Folner criterion as stated by Bartholdi,
[
4
]
. For groups it is known that if a given group contains the free group
\mathrm{F}_{2}
, then this group is non-amenable. It is known that small cancellation groups satisfying condition
C(4)
together with
T(4)
, as well as satisfying condition
C(7)
(classic or graphical) contain
\mathrm{F}_{2}
(see
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
). So these groups are non-amenable. For associative algebras a similar property does not hold. Indeed, it is well known that solvable groups are amenable. There exist solvable groups that contain the free semigroup with two free generators
\langle x,y\rangle
(see
[
6
]
). Let
G
be such group. By the result of L. Bartholdi,
[
4
]
, the group algebra
kG
is amenable and by construction it contains free associative algebra
k\langle x,y\rangle
. Along with non-amenability, we prove in the paper that under some additional conditions small cancellation rings contain free associative algebra with two free generators, see Theorem
3
.

Let us notice that amenability of monomial algebras was studied in
[
5
]
, in particular it is proved there that non-amenable monomial algebras must contain noncommutative free subalgebras.

It is well known that infinite not virtually cyclic hyperbolic groups contain
\mathrm{F}_{2}
, and hence are non-amenable. We consider small cancellation rings as a step towards (not yet defined) hyperbolic rings, so their non-amenability confirms this direction.

We want to notice that the construction of small cancellation rings is quite technical, so we do not present it in all the details in this paper. Here we state only main facts about small cancellation rings and for the details we constantly refer to paper
[
2
]
.

2.
Introduction to group-like small cancellation conditions for rings

Let
\mathrm{F}
be a free group,
k
be a field, and
k\mathrm{F}
be the corresponding group algebra. The reduced representatives of the elements of
\mathrm{F}
are called words or monomials. Their linear combinations with coefficients from
k
are called polynomials. We denote coefficients from
k
by small Greek letters (unless otherwise is stated). The free generators of
\mathrm{F}
and their inverses are called letters. Let
A,B
be monomials in
\mathrm{F}
. We write the product of
A
and
B
as
A\cdot B
. However, if there is no cancellation in this product, we omit
\cdot
and write it as
AB
.

Given a word
A
, we denote by
|A|
the length of
A
in letters.

Given a set
H\subseteq k\mathrm{F}
, we denote its linear span over the field
k
by
\langle H\rangle
. We denote the ideal of
k\mathrm{F}
generated by
H
by
\langle\langle H\rangle\rangle
.

Let
\mathrm{Rel}\subseteq k\mathrm{F}
be a set of polynomials and
\mathcal{I}=\langle\langle\mathrm{Rel}\rangle\rangle
be an ideal of
k\mathrm{F}
generated by
\mathrm{Rel}
as an ideal. Let
\mathrm{Mon}
be all monomials that participate in the polynomials from
\mathrm{Rel}
. Group-like small cancellation theory for rings states a list of conditions that guarantee non-triviality of the quotient ring
k\mathrm{F}/\mathcal{I}
. In this section we give a short introduction to these conditions. For all the details see
[
2
]
, Section
2
, and
[
3
]
.

Condition 1

(Compatibility Axiom)
.

We require that
\mathrm{Rel}
is closed under multiplication by elements from
k
and under multiplication by letters that cancel with some monomial. For instance, if
a+xb\in\mathrm{Rel}
, where
a,b
are monomials and
x
is a single letter, then
x^{-1}\cdot a+b\in\mathrm{Rel}
as well. This condition is called Compatibility Axiom. For the further axioms we assume that
\mathrm{Rel}
satisfies this condition. In particular, this condition implies that
\mathrm{Mon}
is closed under taking subwords.

The main notion is a small piece.

Definition 1

(small pieces)
.

We assume that
\mathrm{Rel}
satisfies Compatibility Axiom. Let
c\in\mathrm{Mon}
. Let
p,q\in\mathrm{Rel}
be polynomials such that
c
is a subword in a monomial in
p
and in
q
. Namely,
p
contains a monomial
\widehat{a}_{1}c\widehat{a}_{2}
and
q
contains
\widehat{b}_{1}c\widehat{b}_{2}
(
\widehat{a}_{1}
,
\widehat{a}_{2}
,
\widehat{b}_{1}
,
\widehat{b}_{2}
are allowed to be empty). If at least one of
\widehat{b}_{1}\cdot\widehat{a}_{1}^{-1}\cdot p
and
p\cdot\widehat{a}_{2}^{-1}\cdot\widehat{b}_{2}
is not contained in
\mathrm{Rel}
, then
c
is called
a small piece with respect to
\mathrm{Rel}
. By definition, we require that the empty word
1
is always a small piece. The set of all small pieces with respect to
\mathrm{Rel}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}
.

We fix a constant
\tau\geqslant 10
. The central axiom is the following

Condition 2

(Small Cancellation Axiom)
.

Assume
q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\in\mathrm{Rel}
and a linear combination
\sum_{l=1}^{n}\gamma_{l}q_{l}
is non-zero after additive cancellations. Then there exists a monomial
a
in
\sum_{l=1}^{n}\gamma_{l}q_{l}
with a non-zero coefficient after additive cancellations such that either
a
can not be represented as a product of small pieces or every such representation of
a
contains at least
\tau+1
small pieces.

There is also the third condition called Isolation Axiom. This condition is very technical, so we do not give it here. Roughly speaking, it guarantees that monomials that differ only by one small piece in the beginning or at the end can not participate in one polynomial in some closure of
\mathrm{Rel}
called
\mathrm{Add}(\mathrm{Rel})
(see
[
2
]
, Section
2
, Conditions 3a and 3b, and Section
10
, for the definition of
\mathrm{Add}(\mathrm{Rel})
).

The main result of Group-like Small cancellation theory is as follows

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathrm{Rel}\subseteq k\mathrm{F}
satisfy group-like small cancellation conditions. Then the quotient ring
k\mathrm{F}/\langle\langle\mathrm{Rel}\rangle\rangle
is non-trivial.

Remark 3

The formal definition of the partition

5.

equal to

. The latter implies that every virtual member of the chart that is replaced in the process correspond to a unique virtual member of the chart of
v^{\prime}w^{\prime}
(see Definition 6.2 of an admissible replacement, Definition 6.5 of virtual members of the chart in
[
2
]
and Remark 6.2). Hence we can mark virtual members of the chart of
v^{\prime}w^{\prime}
that are replaced, and then do all the replacements in the order from right to left. By Lemma
4
in the latter procedure we replace virtual members of the chart of
\Lambda^{\prime}
-measure
\geqslant\tau+8
as well (see
[
2
]
, Proposition 6.17).

Consider in detail the sequence of replacements that starts from
B
end results in
C_{t}
and the replacements are done from right to left. Namely, let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
be virtual members of the chart of
v^{\prime}w^{\prime}
that correspond to the replacing ones. Notice that there are no
u_{i}
that are contained in
v^{\prime}
, since maximal occurrences in
v^{\prime}
are of
\Lambda^{\prime}
-measure
\leqslant 8
. Then at the first step we have a sequence of replacements
u_{n}=u_{n}^{(0)}\mapsto u_{n}^{(1)}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto u_{n}^{(K_{n})}
such that
u_{n}^{(i)},u_{n}^{(i+1)}
are incident monomials and
\Lambda^{\prime}(u_{n}^{(i)})\geqslant\tau+8
for
0\leqslant i<K_{n}
. At the second step we do the similar replacements for the occurrence that corresponds to
u_{n-1}
in the resulting monomial of the first step, etc.. Denote this sequence by
v^{\prime}w^{\prime}=U^{(n)}\mapsto U^{(n-1)}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto U^{(1)}\mapsto U^{(0)}=B
, where
U^{(s-1)}
is the result of
(n-s+1)
-th step.

Now we multiply the latter sequence by
v^{-1}
from the left and consider the sequence
w=v^{-1}\cdot U^{(K)}\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(K-1)}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(1)}\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(0)}=v^{-1}\cdot B
. First assume that
u_{1}
is also a maximal occurrence in
w
. Then every transformation
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(s)}\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(s-1)}
is a replacement of the same elements from
\mathrm{Mon}
as in
U^{(s)}\mapsto U^{(s-1)}
. Therefore
v^{-1}\cdot B
is a derived monomial of
w
. Now assume that
u_{1}
is not a maximal occurrence in
w
. For
s\neq 1
the transformations
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(s)}\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(s-1)}
and
U^{(s)}\mapsto U^{(s-1)}
are still replacements of the same elements from
\mathrm{Mon}
. Let
\widetilde{u}_{1}
be the maximal occurrence in
U^{(1)}
that corresponds to
u_{1}
. Then
\widetilde{u}_{1}=u_{1}^{\prime\prime}\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime}
, where
u_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is a suffix of
v^{\prime}
(possibly empty). Since
\Lambda^{\prime}(u_{1}^{\prime\prime})\leqslant 8
, we obtain that
\Lambda^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime})\geqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}_{1})-8\geqslant\tau
. Let
d\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime}
be the maximal prolongation of
\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime}
in
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(1)}
. Then
\Lambda^{\prime}(d\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime})\geqslant\tau
, so it is a virtual member of the chart of
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(1)}
. Let the replacement
U^{(1)}\mapsto U^{(0)}
be of the form
\widetilde{u}_{1}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto a
with all intermediate monomials with
\Lambda^{\prime}
-measure
\geqslant\tau+8
. Then the replacement
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(1)}\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(0)}
is of the form
d\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot a
. Notice that
d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}
is a subword of
v^{-1}
, therefore, given a monomial
X
, we have that
\Lambda^{\prime}(d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot X)\geqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(X)-8
. So all the intermediate monomials in
du_{1}^{\prime}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot a
are of
\Lambda^{\prime}
-measure
\geqslant\tau
, hence
v^{-1}\cdot B
is a derived monomial of
w
.

Now we study the replacement
B\mapsto C_{t}
,
\mathsf{MinCov}(C_{t})<\mathsf{MinCov}(B)
. Let
B=LbR
,
C_{t}=Lb_{1}R
, where
b,b_{1}
are incident monomials. Then
\Lambda^{\prime}(b)\geqslant\tau+8
and
\Lambda^{\prime}(b_{1})\leqslant 2
(see
[
2
]
, Lemma 6.1). Let
w=zw^{\prime}
. The above argument implies that either
B=v^{\prime}D
,
v^{-1}\cdot B=zD
, or
B=v^{\prime\prime}aD
and
v^{-1}\cdot B=z^{\prime}\widetilde{a}D
, where
v^{\prime\prime}
is a prefix of
v
,
z^{\prime}
is a prefix of
z
, and
\widetilde{a}=d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot a
. Looking at the position of the replaced occurrence in
B
, we obtain that
v^{-1}\cdot B\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}
is a replacement of a virtual member of the chart of
v^{-1}\cdot B
. So
v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}
is a derived monomial of
v^{-1}\cdot B
and hence of
w
. In particular
f(v^{-1}\cdot C_{t})\leqslant f(v^{-1}\cdot B)
. If
f(v^{-1}\cdot B)<f(w)
, then
f(v^{-1}\cdot C_{t})<f(w)
, so
v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}<_{f}w
and we are done.

Now we shall consider the case
f(v^{-1}\cdot B)=f(w)
, that is, every transformation
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(s)}\mapsto v^{-1}\cdot U^{(s-1)}
is a replacement of a virtual member of the chart by a virtual member of the chart. Recall from above that either
B=v^{\prime}D
,
v^{-1}\cdot B=zD
(where
w=zw^{\prime}
), or
B=v^{\prime\prime}aD
and
v^{-1}\cdot B=z^{\prime}\widetilde{a}D
, where
v^{\prime\prime}
is a prefix of
v
,
z^{\prime}
is a prefix of
z
, and
\widetilde{a}=d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot a
. First assume that
b
have no common part with
v^{\prime\prime}
and
b\neq a
. Since
\mathsf{MinCov}(B)=\mathsf{MinCov}(v^{\prime}w^{\prime})
, the occurrence
a
is not contained in
b
. Therefore
b=b^{\prime\prime}b^{\prime}
, where
b^{\prime}
is a common part with
D
and
\Lambda^{\prime}(b^{\prime\prime})\leqslant 1
(i.e.,
b^{\prime\prime}
is either empty, or a small piece). The corresponding maximal occurrence in
v^{-1}\cdot B
is of the form
cb^{\prime}
, where
\Lambda^{\prime}(c)\leqslant 1
(
c
can be empty), because
f(v^{-1}\cdot B)=f(w)
, so
\widetilde{a}
can not be covered by
c
. Hence the replacement in
v^{-1}\cdot B
is of the form
cb^{\prime}\mapsto c\cdot{b^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1}
. We have that
\Lambda^{\prime}(c\cdot{b^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1})\leqslant 1+1+2=4
. Hence
c\cdot{b^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1}
is not a virtual member of the chart. So
f(v^{-1}\cdot C_{t})<f(v^{-1}\cdot B)=f(w)
and the result follows.

Having
f(v^{-1}\cdot B)=f(w)
, it remains to consider the following two possibilities: the first one is
B=v^{\prime}D
,
v^{-1}\cdot B=zD
,
b
have a common part with
v^{\prime}
, and second one is
B=v^{\prime\prime}aD
,
v^{-1}\cdot B=z^{\prime}\widetilde{a}D
,
b=a
. We study only the latter case, the first one is similar. In this case we make the step back and glue the replacements
U^{(1)}\mapsto B\mapsto C_{t}
. So we have the replacement
\widetilde{u}_{1}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto b_{1}
in
U^{(1)}
and the corresponding replacement
d\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime}\mapsto\ldots\mapsto d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1}
in
v^{-1}\cdot U^{(1)}
with the result
v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}
. We obtain that
\Lambda^{\prime}(d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1})\leqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(d)+\Lambda^{\prime}({u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1})\leqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(d)+10
. Using
\tau\geqslant 16
, we see that
\Lambda^{\prime}(d{u_{1}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\cdot b_{1})\leqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(d)+\tau-6\leqslant\Lambda^{\prime}(d\widetilde{u}_{1}^{\prime})-5
. Therefore
v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}<_{f}w
by the definition of
<_{f}
(see
[
2
]
, Definition 10.1). This completes the proof of Step 2.

Step 3.
Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
be pairwise different monomials such that
\mathsf{MinCov}(v\cdot w_{i})=m
, and the cancellations in
v\cdot w_{i}
has length
\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|v|
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. In the basis decomposition of a non-trivial linear combination
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}v\cdot w_{i}+\mathcal{I}
all monomials have
\mathsf{MinCov}\leqslant m
by the property of the minimal basis (see Lemma
2
). We will show that in this basis decomposition after additive cancellations there exist monomials with
\mathsf{MinCov}
equal to
m
. Furthermore, all of them have a common prefix with
v
of length
>\frac{1}{3}|v|
. In particular, the highest monomial with respect to
<_{f}
is like this.

Without loss of generality we suppose that
w_{1}>_{f}w_{2}>_{f}\ldots>_{f}w_{n}
and
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Step
2
implies that we can write
v\cdot w_{i}=\sum_{j}\beta_{j}^{(i)}b_{j}^{(i)}+\sum_{t}\gamma_{t}^{(i)}C_{t}^{(i)}+\mathcal{I}
, where
b_{j}^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}
,
\mathsf{MinCov}\left(b_{j}^{(i)}\right)=m
for all
i,j
, the monomials
C_{t}^{(i)}
satisfy
\mathsf{MinCov}(C_{t}^{(i)})<m
and
v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}^{(i)}<_{f}w_{i}\leqslant_{f}w_{1}
. By Lemma
2
we obtain that in the basis decomposition of
\sum_{t}\gamma_{t}^{(i)}C_{t}^{(i)}+\mathcal{I}
all basis monomials are with
\mathsf{MinCov}<m
.

Now assume by the contrary, that in the basis decomposition of
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}v\cdot w_{i}+\mathcal{I}
after additive cancellations only monomials with
\mathsf{MinCov}<m
survive. Then it follows from the above that the monomials
b_{j}^{(i)}
are cancelled for all
i,j
. Since
v^{-1}\cdot C_{t}^{(i)}<_{f}w_{i}\leqslant_{f}w_{1}
, multiplying
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}v\cdot w_{i}+\mathcal{I}
by
v^{-1}
from the left, we obtain the decomposition of
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}w_{i}+\mathcal{I}
that consists of monomials that are
<_{f}w_{1}
. So when we make the basis decomposition of this element, there can not appear
w_{1}
by Lemma
2
. This contradicts to the uniqueness of the basis decomposition
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}w_{i}+\mathcal{I}
, since all
w_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
. So some of the elements
b_{j}^{(i)}
are not cancelled in the basis decomposition of
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}v\cdot w_{i}+\mathcal{I}
. To complete the proof of Step
3
we notice that all
b_{j}^{(i)}
by construction (cf. Step 2 and the assumption that the cancellations in
v\cdot w_{i}
has length
\leqslant\frac{1}{2}|v|
) have a common prefix with
v
of length
>\frac{1}{3}|v|
.

Step 4.

We consider a combination
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\alpha_{i}z_{i}
(where not all coefficients are zero). Without loss of generality we can assume that
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
. Let
\mathcal{U}=\{u_{ij}\}_{ij}
be the set of all monomials from
\mathcal{B}
that are not additively cancelled in this linear combination. Recall that
u_{i}
is the highest monomial of
z_{i}
. Since the matrix of basis coefficients of
z_{1},\ldots,z_{l}
was assumed to be upper-triangular,
u_{1}
is not additively cancelled and is the highest monomial with respect to
<_{f}
in
\mathcal{U}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
be such monomials that the cancellations in
v\cdot u_{ij}
has length
<\frac{1}{3}|v|
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
be such that the cancellations in
v\cdot u_{ij}
has length in range
[\frac{1}{3}|v|,\frac{1}{2}|v|]
, and
\mathcal{A}_{3}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
be such that the cancellations in
v\cdot u_{ij}
has length
>\frac{1}{2}|v|
.

We consider monomials of the form
v\cdot u_{ij}
,
u_{ij}\in\mathcal{U}
, with the maximal
\mathsf{MinCov}
among all such monomials. Denote this value by
m
. Since
u_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
, Step 1 implies that monomials
v\cdot u_{ij}
with the maximal
\mathsf{MinCov}
satisfy
u_{ij}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\cup\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Applying Step 3 to these monomials, we obtain that the highest monomial in the basis decomposition of
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\alpha_{i}v\cdot z_{i}+\mathcal{I}
(after additive cancellations) has
\mathsf{MinCov}
equal to
m
and has a common prefix with
v
of length
>\frac{1}{3}|v|
. On the other hand, in every non-trivial linear combination
\sum_{j}\beta_{j}z_{j}
the highest monomial is equal to some
u_{j}
, since the matrix of basis coefficients is upper-triangular. By choice of
v
, a possible common prefix of
u_{j}
and
v
has length
<\frac{1}{3}|v|
. So, the highest monomials in the basis decompositions of
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\alpha_{i}v\cdot z_{i}+\mathcal{I}
and
\sum_{j}\beta_{j}z_{j}+\mathcal{I}
are different, hence
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\alpha_{i}v\cdot z_{i}+\mathcal{I}\neq\sum_{j}\beta_{j}z_{j}+\mathcal{I}
. This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Examples

Theorem
4
says in fact that small cancellation rings introduced in
[
2
]
provide us with a lot of new examples of non-amenable rings. For more specific examples we can look at the particular ring constructed in
[
1
]
. With large enough parameters
\alpha,\beta
for a special word
V=yx^{\alpha}\ldots yx^{\beta}
, which is used in order to invert
1+w
, Theorem
4
implies that the ring
k\mathrm{F}/\langle\langle 1+w-V^{-1}\rangle\rangle
is non-amenable.

Note that for small cancellation groups satisfying
C(4)
together with
T(4)
, or
C(7)
, non-amenability of the corresponding group algebras follows from the work
[
4
]
, since these groups contain
\mathrm{F}_{2}
. In
[
2
]
we prove that a group algebra of small cancellation group satisfying the condition
C(22)
is a small cancellation ring. Using Theorem
4
, one can also see that if the corresponding group satisfies the stronger condition
C(52)
, then its group algebra is non-amenable.

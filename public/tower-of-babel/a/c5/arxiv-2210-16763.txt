Generalized Alexander quandles of finite groups and their characterizations Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary; 20N02, Secondary: 20B05, 53C35. Keywords: generalized Alexander quandles, finite groups, automorphisms.
Akihiro Higashitani, Hirotake Kurihara

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to characterization generalized Alexander quandles of finite groups in the language of the underlying groups. Firstly, we prove that if finite groups G G are simple, then the quandle isomorphic classes of generalized Alexander quandles of G G one-to-one correspond to the conjugacy classes of the automorphism groups of G G . This correspondence can be also claimed for the case of symmetric groups. Secondly, we give a characterization of generalized Alexander quandles of finite groups G G under some assumptions in terms of G G . As corollaries of this characterization, we obtain several characterizations in some particular groups, e.g., abelian groups and dihedral groups. Finally, we perform a characterization of generalized Alexander quandles arising from groups with their order up to 15 15 .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds and motivations

Let us recall what a quandle is.
A set
Q
equipped with a binary operation
*
is said to be a
quandle
if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(Q1)

x*x=x
for any
x\in Q
;

(Q2)

for each
x,y\in Q
, there is a unique
z\in Q
such that
z*y=x
;

(Q3)

for any
x,y,z\in Q
, we have
(x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)
.

A notion of quandles was originally introduced in
[
5
]
in the context of knot theory.
In fact, these axioms correspond to the Reidemeister moves.

In this paper, we employ the following definition of quandles using “point symmetry”, which is a map globally defined at each point.
We say that a set
Q
equipped with a point symmetry
s_{x}:Q\rightarrow Q
for each
x\in Q
is a quandle if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(Q1’)

s_{x}(x)=x
for each
x\in Q
;

(Q2’)

s_{x}
is a bijection on
Q
for each
x\in Q
;

(Q3’)

s_{x}\circ s_{y}=s_{s_{x(y)}}\circ x_{x}
holds for each
x,y\in Q
.

We can directly verify that those definitions are equivalent by letting
s_{x}(\cdot):Q\rightarrow Q
be
(\cdot)*x
.
To be precise, we should denote a quandle
Q
by
(Q,*)
or
(Q,s)
, i.e., a pair of a set
Q
and a binary operation
*
or a point symmetry
s_{x}(\cdot)(x\in Q)
,
but we often write it for
Q
if there is no confusion, while we also write, e.g.,
(Q,s)
or
(Q,s^{\prime})
if we want to distinguish the equipped operations.

The above equivalent definition derives from the theory of symmetric spaces since quandles can be regarded as symmetric spaces “without differential structure”.
In particular, due to the similarity of the theory of homogeneous spaces, homogeneous quandles have been intensively studies
(see, e.g.,
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
).
Here, a quandle
Q
is said to be
homogeneous
if the automorphism group of
Q
acts transitively on
Q
.
Moreover, it is proved that any homogeneous quandles can be obtained
from a triple of a group
G
, its subgroup
K
and its automorphism
\psi
satisfying a certain condition, called a
quandle triplet
.
(See
[
4
, Section 3]
and
[
5
, Section 7]
.)
In particular, in the case where
K=\{e\}
, we can always construct a certain homogeneous quandle, which we will denote
Q(G,\psi)
.
This homogeneous quandle is called a
generalized Alexander quandle
(also known as a
principal quandle
) of
G
and
\psi
.
See Subsection
2.3
for more details.
Note that Alexander quandles correspond to the case where
G
is abelian.

The main object of this paper is generalized Alexander quandles
Q(G,\psi)
for finite groups
G
.
The symbols that appear in the following sections will be explained in Section
2
.

Given a finite group
G
, let
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
be the set of quandle isomorphic classes of
Q(G,\psi)
’s, i.e.,

{\mathcal{Q}}(G):=\{Q(G,\psi):\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)\}/\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}.

The following problem naturally arises.

Problem 1.1

(
[
7
, Problem 3.5]
)
.

Determine
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
for a given group
G
.

Nelson completely solves this problem in the case of finite abelian groups in
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
.
The goal of this paper is to give a more general statement for this problem.

1.2.
Key notion

Let
G
be a finite group with its identity
e
, let
\psi
be an automorphism of
G
and let
Q=Q(G,\psi)
be a generalized Alexander quandle.
In this paper, we introduce an invariant of generalized Alexander quandle:

(1)

\begin{split}P=P(Q)&:=\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)e\\
&=\{x\in G:\exists a_{1},\ldots,\exists a_{r}\in G\text{ such that }x=s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}(e)\}.\end{split}

Namely,
P
is the orbit of
e
by the action of
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q(G,\psi))
.
Note that the same notion has already appeared in
[
1
]
. See Remark
3.5
.

This
P
plays a crucial role for the solution of Problem
1.1
.
In fact,
P
satisfies several important properties on quandle isomorphism and will play key roles for the proofs of our main results.

1.3.
Main results

The first main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite simple group. Then
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
one-to-one corresponds to the set of conjugacy classes of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
.

Although it is well known that the symmetric group on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
is not simple for
n\geq 3
, we immediate obtain the following as a corollary of this theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the symmetric group on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
{\mathcal{Q}}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
one-to-one corresponds to the set of conjugacy classes of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
.

See Subsection
3.3
for the proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
.
Note that this corollary implies
[
7
, Theorem 5.14]
, which claims the same result in the case
n\in\{3,4,\ldots,30\}\setminus\{15\}
,
and gives an affirmative answer for
[
7
, Question 5.15]
.

Next, we consider generalized Alexander quandles satisfying
(P1)
and
(P2)

(definitions of
(P1)
and
(P2)
will appear in Section
3.4
).
We can give an equivalent condition of the quandle isomorphic property
among generalized Alexander quandles satisfying
(P1)
and
(P2)
.
This equivalent condition is the second main result of this paper
and the detail is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be finite groups, let
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
and
\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G^{\prime})
, and let
Q=Q(G,\psi)
and
Q^{\prime}=Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
, respectively.
Assume that
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
satisfy
(P1)
and
(P2)
.
Then
Q\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q^{\prime}
holds if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(A)

|G|=|G^{\prime}|
;

(B)

|\operatorname{Fix}(\psi,G)|=|\operatorname{Fix}(\psi^{\prime},G^{\prime})|
;

(C)

there is a group isomorphism
h
from
P=P(Q)
to
P^{\prime}=P(Q^{\prime})
(i.e.,
P\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P^{\prime}
) satisfying the following conditions:

(C-1)

and

(C-2)

for any

there is
a^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
such that
h(s_{a}(e))=s_{a^{\prime}}^{\prime}(e^{\prime})
holds.

This theorem induces many interesting results.
In fact, there are many generalized Alexander quandles satisfying the conditions
(P1)
and
(P2)
.
The following three corollaries follow from Theorem
1.4
.

Corollary 1.5

(
[

Let

\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G^{\prime})

Let

\rho^{\prime}=\operatorname{id}_{G^{\prime}}-\psi^{\prime}

Q(G,\psi)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})

2.
Terminologies and notation

2.1.
Terminologies for quandles

Throughout this paper, we denote by
(Q,s)
(or
Q
, for short)
a quandle
Q
equipped with the maps
\{s_{x}:x\in Q\}
satisfying (Q1’)–(Q3’) in Introduction.

Let
(Q,s)
and
(Q^{\prime},s^{\prime})
be quandles.
A map
f:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
is called a
quandle homomorphism
if
f
satisfies

f\circ s_{x}=s_{f(x)}^{\prime}\circ f\;\;\text{ for any }x\in Q.

Moreover,
f
is said to be a
quandle isomorphism
when
f
is bijective.
If there is a quandle isomorphism between
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, then we say that
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are
isomorphic as quandles
,
denoted by
Q\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q^{\prime}
.

We collect the notation on quandles used in the paper:

•

Let
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q,s)
(or
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
, for short) be the set of all quandle automorphisms from
(Q,s)
to
(Q,s)
itself,
which is called the
automorphism group
of
Q
.
Note that
s_{x}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
for any
x\in Q
.

•

Let
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q,s)
(or
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
, for short) be the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
generated by
\{s_{x}:x\in Q\}
, which is called the
inner automorphism group
of
Q
.
It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
is a normal subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
.

•

For a quandle
Q
, we call
Q

homogeneous
if for any
x,y\in Q
, there exists a quandle automorphism
f
on
Q
such that
f(x)=y
.
This means that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
acts transitively on
Q
.

•

For a quandle
Q
, we call
Q

connected
if for any
x,y\in Q
, there exists a quandle inner automorphism
f
on
Q
such that
f(x)=y
.
This means that
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
acts transitively on
Q
.

•

For a quandle
(Q,s)
, let
\operatorname{ord}(s_{x})=\min\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}:\underbrace{s_{x}\circ\cdots\circ s_{x}}_{n}=\operatorname{id}\}
.
For a homogeneous quandle
Q
, we see that
\operatorname{ord}(s_{x})
is constant for any
x\in Q
.
In fact, for any
x,y\in Q
, since there is
f\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
such that
y=f(x)
, we see that
if
s_{x}^{m}=\operatorname{id}
, then
f\circ s_{x}^{m}=s_{f(x)}^{m}\circ f
, so we obtain
s_{f(x)}^{m}=\operatorname{id}
, as required.
Thus, we use the notation
\operatorname{ord}(Q)
instead of
\operatorname{ord}(s_{x})
for
x\in Q
in the case
Q
is homogeneous.

, respectively.

G\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}G^{\prime}

H<G

.

•

Let
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)

.

•

Let
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)

.

•

•

, i.e.,
C_{n}^{\times}=\{a\in C_{n}:\gcd(a,n)=1\}
.

\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(C_{n})\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{n}^{\times}
.

•

Let
D_{n}

. We will give our notation for dihedral groups in the beginning of Section
4
.

•

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
(resp.

. It is well known that

2.3.
Quandle triplets and generalized Alexander quandles

Let
G
be a group and let
K
be a subgroup of
G
. Take
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
.
We say that a triple
(G,K,\psi)
is a
quandle triplet
if
K\subset\operatorname{Fix}(\psi,G)
.
Given a quandle triplet
(G,K,\psi)
, we can construct a quandle
Q(G,K,\psi)
as follows:

Q(G,K,\psi)=G/K
as a set and we define
s_{[x]}
for each
[x]\in G/K
by setting

s_{[x]}([y])=[x\psi^{-1}(x^{-1}y)]\;\;\text{for }[y]\in G/K.

It is proved in
[
5
]
that the set
G/K
equipped with this point symmetry becomes a homogeneous quandle.

For the relationship between homogeneous quandles and quandle triplets, the following is known:

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
4
, Section 3]
)
.

For any homogeneous quandle
Q
, there is a quandle triplet
(G,K,\psi)
such that
Q\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(G,K,\psi)
.

We notice that for a given a group automorphism
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
, a triplet
(G,\{e\},\psi)
trivially becomes a quandle triplet.
Let
Q(G,\psi)=Q(G,\{e\},\psi)
, i.e.,
Q(G,\psi)
is the homogeneous quandle
(G,s)
, where
s
is defined by

s_{x}(y)=x\psi(x^{-1}y)\;\;\text{ for any }x,y\in G.

This quandle
Q(G,\psi)
is known as a
generalized Alexander quandle
.
Note that in the case
G
is abelian,
Q(G,\psi)
is called an
Alexander quandle
(see
[
11
]
).

Towards a solution of Problem
1.1
,
we collect some invariants on generalized Alexander quandles developed in
[
7
]
.
Let
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
and let
Q=Q(G,\psi)
and let
Q^{\prime}=Q(G,\psi^{\prime})
.
The following assertions are claimed in
[
7
, Theorem 4.5]
:

(
a
)

If
\psi^{\prime}=\psi^{\tau}
for some
\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
, then
Q\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q^{\prime}
.

(
b
)

If
Q\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q^{\prime}
, then

•

\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q^{\prime})
;

•

\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}\psi=\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}\psi^{\prime}
;

•

|\operatorname{Fix}(\psi,G)|=|\operatorname{Fix}(\psi^{\prime},G)|
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the above argument, we may obtain the similar results when the source group and the target group are different, i.e.,
for
Q=Q(G,\psi)
and
Q^{\prime}=Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
, if
Q\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q^{\prime}
then

•

\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q^{\prime})
;

•

\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}\psi=\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G^{\prime})}\psi^{\prime}
;

•

|\operatorname{Fix}(\psi,G)|=|\operatorname{Fix}(\psi^{\prime},G^{\prime})|
.

3.
Proofs of the main results

In this section, we develop a new invariant
P
(see (
1
)) on generalized Alexander quandles.

Let
Q=Q(G,\psi)
be a generalized Alexander quandle, where
G
is a finite group and
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
.
Given
a\in G
, let

L_{a}:G\rightarrow G\text{ defined by }L_{a}(x)=ax\text{ for }x\in Q.

Then we see that
L_{a}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q)
. In fact,
L_{a}
is a bijection on
Q
since
Q=G
as a set, and

L_{a}\circ s_{x}(y)=ax\psi(x^{-1}y)=ax\psi(x^{-1}a^{-1}ay)=s_{ax}(ay)=s_{L_{a}(x)}\circ L_{a}(y),

i.e.,
L_{a}
is a quandle homomorphism. Note that
L_{a}^{-1}=L_{a^{-1}}
.

3.1.
Normality for
P
and
P^{2}

Given a generalized Alexander quandle
Q=Q(G,\psi)
, let us recall the definition of
P(Q)
given in Introduction.
We discuss some fundamental properties of
P
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x\in P
.
Then there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in G
such that
L_{x}=s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}
.

Proof.

In the definition of
P
,
we can add the assumption
r\equiv 0\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}\psi}

since
s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{t}}(e)=s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{t}}\circ s_{e}^{m}(e)
holds for any
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Hence, for
x\in P
, there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in G
such that
x=s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}(e)
, where we let
r\equiv 0\pmod{\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}\psi}
.
Thus, for any
y\in G
, we see the following:

\displaystyle L_{x}(y)=xy=s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}(e)\cdot\psi^{r}(y)=s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}(y).

∎

In what follows, for a map

3.2.

Proposition 3.8

(cf.
[
1
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
.
Let
P=P(Q(G,\psi))
and
m=\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}(\psi)
.
Then we have

\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q(G,\psi))\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m},

which is a semidirect product of

and

with
\phi:C_{m}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
,

, i.e.,

(x,i)\cdot(y,j)=(x\psi^{i}(y),i+j).

As in

, the semi-direct product in Proposition

may become a direct product in some cases.
The following proposition shows the relationship between the structure of the semi-direct product and the property of
\psi|_{P}
when
P
is centerless, i.e.,
Z(P)=\{e\}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Work with the notation as above. Assume that
P
is centerless.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i) If
\psi|_{P}\in\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)
, i.e.,
\psi|_{P}
is an inner automorphism, then we have

\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q(G,\psi))\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P\times C_{m}.

(ii) If
\psi|_{P}\notin\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)
, i.e.,
\psi|_{P}
is an outer automorphism, then we have

\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{qu}}(Q(G,\psi))\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}\not\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P\times C_{m}.

Proof.

(i)
By
\psi|_{P}\in\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)
, there exists
a\in P
such that
\psi|_{P}=(\cdot)^{a}
.
Since
P
is centerless,
\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P
holds. Then we have
\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)}(\psi|_{P})=\operatorname{ord}_{P}(a)
.
On the other hand,
\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)}(\psi|_{P})
is a divisor of
m=\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}(\psi)
.
Hence
\operatorname{ord}_{P}(a)
is a divisor of
m
.
We define the map
\Psi:P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}\rightarrow P\times C_{m}
by

\Psi((x,i))=(xa^{i},i).

Then it is straightforward to check that this gives a group isomorphism:

•

Since
a\in P
, we see that
xa^{i}\in P
for any
x\in P
and
i\in C_{m}
.
Also since
\operatorname{ord}_{P}(a)
is a divisor of
m
, we see that
xa^{i}=xa^{j}
whenever
i\equiv j\pmod{m}
. Hence, the well-definedness follows.

•

One has
\Psi((x,i)\cdot(y,j))=\Psi((xa^{i}ya^{-i},i+j))=(xa^{i}ya^{j},i+j)=\Psi((x,i))\Psi((y,j)).

•

One has
\Psi((x,i))=\Psi((y,j))\;\Longleftrightarrow\;(xa^{i},i)=(ya^{j},j)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;x=y\text{ and }i\equiv j\pmod{m}
.

•

For any
(x,i)\in P\times C_{m}
, since
xa^{-i}\in P
, we have
\Psi((xa^{-i},i))=(x,i)
, as required.

(ii)
Suppose that there exists a group isomorphism
\Omega\colon P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}\to P\times C_{m}
.
Let
X=\{(x,0):x\in P\}\subset P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}
. Then
X
is a subgroup of
P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}
.
Let
X^{\prime}=\Omega(X)
. Then
X^{\prime}
becomes a subgroup of
P\times C_{m}
.
For each
(x,0)\in X
, we write
(a_{x},j_{x})=\Omega(x,0)
.

Let
Y^{\prime}=\{(e,i):i\in C_{m}\}\subset P\times C_{m}
. Then
Y^{\prime}
is a subgroup of
P\times C_{m}
.
Let
Y=\Omega^{-1}(Y^{\prime})
. Then
Y
becomes a subgroup of
P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}
and
|Y|=|Y^{\prime}|=m
since
\Omega
is a group isomorphism.
For each
(e,i)\in Y^{\prime}
, we write
(b_{i},k_{i})=\Omega^{-1}((e,i))
.

Since
\Omega^{-1}
is also a group isomorphism, we see that

\displaystyle\Omega^{-1}((e,i)(a_{x},j_{x}))

\displaystyle=\Omega^{-1}((e,i))\cdot\Omega^{-1}((a_{x},j_{x}))=(b_{i},k_{i})\cdot(x,0)=(b_{i}\psi^{k_{i}}(x),k_{i})\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Omega^{-1}((e,i)(a_{x},j_{x}))

\displaystyle=\Omega^{-1}((a_{x},i+j_{x}))=\Omega^{-1}((a_{x},j_{x})(e,i))=\Omega^{-1}((a_{x},j_{x}))\cdot\Omega^{-1}((e,i))

\displaystyle=(x,0)\cdot(b_{i},k_{i})=(xb_{i},k_{i}).

Hence, we obtain that
b_{i}\psi^{k_{i}}(x)=xb_{i}\;\Longleftrightarrow\psi^{k_{i}}(x)=b_{i}^{-1}xb_{i}

for any
x\in P
, i.e.,
\psi^{k_{i}}|_{P}
is an inner automorphism on
P
.
Note that
b_{i}
is determined by
\psi
and
k_{i}
since
P
is centerless.
We put
b_{i}=b_{k_{i}}
.
This implies that

Y=\{(b_{i},k_{i}):i\in C_{m}\}=\{(b_{k},k):k\in C_{m}\text{ and }\psi^{k}|_{P}\in\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)\}.

On the other hand,
\psi|_{P}\not\in\operatorname{Inn}_{\mathrm{gr}}(P)
. This implies

. Thus, we have
|Y|\leq m-1
.
This is a contradiction to
|Y|=m
.

Therefore,
P\rtimes_{\phi}C_{m}\not\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P\times C_{m}
, as desired.
∎

]

(resp.

(resp.

3.10
.

Let
s
(resp.

(resp.
Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
).
(i) Since

is a quandle homomorphism,

.

is a quandle isomorphism with

.

(ii) Note that
\psi=s_{e}
holds as maps. Since

is a quandle homomorphism, we have

, i.e.,

.

(iii) Since

is a bijection between
P
and
P^{\prime}

becomes a group homomorphism.
For any

, there exist

As we have already seen,

\displaystyle f(x)=f\circ s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}(e)=s_{f(a_{1})}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ s_{f(a_{r})}^{\prime}(e^{\prime})

holds. Thus, for any

, we have the following:

\displaystyle f(xy)

\displaystyle=f\circ L_{x}(y)=f\circ s_{a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{a_{r}}(y)\;\;\text{ (by Lemma~\ref{lem_Lx})}

\displaystyle=s_{f(a_{1})}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ s_{f(a_{r})}^{\prime}(f(y))

\displaystyle=s_{f(a_{1})}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ s_{f(a_{r})}^{\prime}(e^{\prime})\cdot f(y)\;\;\text{ ($\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)}\psi=\operatorname{ord}_{\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G^{\prime})}\psi^{\prime}$ by Remark~\ref{rem:GneqG'})}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

are integers and

are positive integers.

(i)

A linear congruence

with the variable

has a solution
if and only if

is a multiple of
\gcd(n,c)
.
Moreover if this linear congruence has a solution

,
then the solutions

are of the form

(i=0,1,\ldots,\gcd(n,c)-1)

(ii)

There exists

\gcd(n,p)=1
such that
pc\equiv\gcd(m,c)\pmod{m}
.

(iii)

We have

\{cb:b\in C_{n}\}=\{gy:y\in C_{n}\},

where
g=\gcd(n,c)
.

Proof.

(i)
This is well known (see, e.g.,
[

).

(ii)
Let

. Then there are

such that

. Hence,

Let

Thus, by Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions, there are infinitely many primes

(

).
For each

, we have

Since there are infinitely many such primes

, we can choose a prime
p
with
\gcd(n,p)=1
, as required.

(iii) One inclusion is trivial since
g
divides

. Thus it is enough to show that

is contained in

.
By (i), there is

Hence, one has

∎

is equal to
g
or
2g
.
If
g^{\prime}=g
, then
d^{\prime}
coincides with
g^{\prime}
. Hence
|\operatorname{Fix}(\varphi_{\tilde{a},g},D_{n})|=2g^{\prime}=2g
.
If
g^{\prime}=2g
, then
d^{\prime}
is a proper divisor of
g^{\prime}
. Hence
|\operatorname{Fix}(\varphi_{\tilde{a},g},D_{n})|=g^{\prime}=2g
.
Thus, in all cases, we obtain
|\operatorname{Fix}(\varphi_{\tilde{a},g},D_{n})|=2g
.
This implies
|\operatorname{Fix}(a,C_{2n})|=|\operatorname{Fix}(\varphi_{\tilde{a,g}},D_{n})|
.

Our remaining task is to check that (C) is satisfied. Before it, we have to compute
P=P(Q)
and
P^{\prime}=P(Q^{\prime})
.

•

We see that
P=P(Q)=\langle 2g\rangle<C_{2n}
.

•

By Proposition
4.5
, we see that
P^{\prime}=\langle\sigma^{g}\rangle
.

Note that
P\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}P^{\prime}\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{n/g}
.

We define
h:\langle 2g\rangle\rightarrow\langle\sigma^{g}\rangle
as follows:

h(2gj):=\sigma^{gj}\text{ for }j=0,1,\ldots,n/g-1.

It is trivial that
h
is a group isomorphism. On (C-1), we see that

\displaystyle h\circ a(2gj)=h(2agj)=\sigma^{agj}\;\text{ and }\;\varphi_{\tilde{a},g}\circ h(2gj)=\varphi_{\tilde{a},g}(\sigma^{gj})=\sigma^{\tilde{a}gj}=\sigma^{agj}.

On (C-2), for any
i\in C_{2n}
, we see that

h(s_{i}(e))=h((1-a)i)=h\left(2g\frac{1-a}{2g}i\right)=\sigma^{\frac{1-a}{2}i}.

On the other hand, for
x^{\prime}=\tau^{\epsilon}\sigma^{j}\in D_{n}
, we see from (
7
) that

s_{x^{\prime}}^{\prime}(e)=\sigma^{(-1)^{\epsilon}(1-\tilde{a})j+\epsilon g}=\sigma^{(-1)^{\epsilon}(1-a)j+\epsilon g}.

•

When
g^{\prime}=g
, the linear congruence
(1-a)j\equiv\frac{(1-a)i}{2}\pmod{n}
has a solution
j=z_{0}

by Lemma
4.1
(i).
By letting
\epsilon=0
and
j=z_{0}
, we see that
s_{x^{\prime}}^{\prime}(e)=\sigma^{\frac{(1-a)i}{2}}=h(s_{i}(e))
.

•

When
g^{\prime}=2g
, we see that
n
must be even.
Then we can define the function
\mu
like (
6
).
Put
\ell=\frac{ki}{g}\in C_{n}
. Then
\ell+\mu(\ell)
must be even.
In this case, the linear congruence
(-1)^{\mu(\ell)}(1-a)j\equiv-(\ell+\mu(\ell))g\pmod{n}
has a solution
j=z_{1}

by Lemma
4.1
(i).
By letting
\epsilon=\mu(\ell)
and
j=z_{1}
, we see that

s_{x^{\prime}}^{\prime}(e)=\sigma^{(-1)^{\mu(\ell)}(1-a)z_{1}+\mu(\ell)g}=\sigma^{-(\ell+\mu(\ell))g+\mu(\ell)g}=\sigma^{-\ell g}=\sigma^{-ki}=h(s_{i}(e)).

∎

6.
Generalized Alexander quandles arising from finite groups of small orders

This section is devoted to solving Problem
1.1
in the case where the order of
G
is small
by using Theorem
1.4
together with Corollaries
1.5
,
1.6
and
1.7
.
More concretely, we determine
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\operatorname{GAQ}}(n)
for small integers
n
.
For this purpose, we investigate the generalized Alexander quandles arising from the groups with its orders
p
or
2p
or
p^{2}
, where
p
is a prime.

6.1.
The case
|G|=p

Let
G
be a group with
|G|=p
, where
p
is a prime. Then it is very well known that
G\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p}
.
Moreover, since
C_{p}
is simple, it follows from Theorem
1.2
that

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\operatorname{GAQ}}(p)
bijectively corresponds to
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(C_{p})
, which is isomorphic to
C_{p}^{\times}
as groups. In particular,
|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\operatorname{GAQ}}(p)|=p-1
.

6.2.
The case
|G|=2p

Let
G
be a group with
|G|=2p
, where
p
is an odd prime. (Note that the case
p=2
will be discussed in Subsection
6.3
.)
Then it is well known that
G\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{2p}
or
D_{p}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 4.19]
).
Thanks to Corollary
1.7
, it suffices to discuss only
Q(D_{p},\psi)
, where
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(D_{p})
.
Hence,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\operatorname{GAQ}}(2p)={\mathcal{Q}}(D_{p})
.
On the other hand, we know by Proposition
4.2
that the conjugacy classes for
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(D_{p})
bijectively correspond to

\{\varphi_{a,d}:a\in C_{p}^{\times},\text{$d$ is a divisor of $g$}\}
, where
g=\gcd(p,1-a)
. Note that
C_{p}^{\times}=\{1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
.

•

When
a=1
, we have
g=p
, so the possible
d
’s are
1
and
p=g
.

•

When
a\neq 1
, we have
g=1
, so the possible
d
is only
1=g
.

Therefore,
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(D_{p})
bijectively corresponds to
\{(a,0):a\in C_{p}^{\times}\}\cup\{(1,1)\}\subset C_{p}^{\times}\times C_{p}
.
Those give all different generalized Alexander quandles by (the third condition of) Corollary
1.6
.
In particular,
|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\operatorname{GAQ}}(2p)|=p
.

6.3.
The case
|G|=p^{2}

Let
G
be a group with
|G|=p^{2}
, where
p
is a prime.
Then it is well known that
G\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p^{2}}
or
G\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p}\times C_{p}
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Corollary 4.5 and Exercise 2.68]
).

First, let us consider
{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p^{2}})
and take
Q\in{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p^{2}})
.
Since
P=P(Q)
is a subgroup of
C_{p^{2}}
, we see that
P
is isomorphic to
\{0\}
or
C_{p}
or
C_{p^{2}}
as groups.
Here, we note that
s_{e}
is nothing but a group automorphism of
C_{p^{2}}
, which corresponds to an element of
C_{p^{2}}^{\times}
.
Let
a\in C_{p^{2}}^{\times}
correspond to
s_{e}
. Here, we see from the description
s_{x}(e)=(1-a)x
and Lemma
4.1
(iii) that

P=\{gy:y\in C_{p^{2}}\}\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p^{2}/g}
, where
g=\gcd(1-a,p^{2})
.

•

Note that
P=\{0\}
if and only if
Q
is a trivial quandle (see Remark
3.4
).

•

Let
P\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p}
. Then
g=p
. Thus,
a=kp+1
for some
1\leq k\leq p-1
.
For
a=kp+1
and
a^{\prime}=k^{\prime}p+1
with
1\leq k,k^{\prime}\leq p-1
, let
g=\gcd(1-a,n=p^{2})
and
g^{\prime}=\gcd(1-a^{\prime},n)
, respectively.
Then we see that
g=g^{\prime}=p
and
a\equiv a^{\prime}\pmod{n/g(=p)}
.
Thus,
Q(C_{p^{2}},a)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(C_{p^{2}},a^{\prime})
for any
a=kp+1
and
a^{\prime}=k^{\prime}p+1
by Corollary
5.2
.

•

Let
P\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p^{2}}
. Then
g=1
. Thus, each
a\in C_{p^{2}}
with
g=1
defines different quandles.

Therefore, in this case,
{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p^{2}})
bijectively corresponds to

\{1\}\cup\{p+1\}\cup\{kp+r:0\leq k\leq p-1,2\leq r\leq p-1\}\subset C_{p^{2}}^{\times}.

Next, let us consider
{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p}\times C_{p})
.
Let
G=C_{p}\times C_{p}
and regard
G
as an additive group of a vector space
C_{p}^{2}
over a prime field
C_{p}
.
Then it is known that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}\operatorname{GL}(2,p)
and
a set of representatives of conjugacy classes for
\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
is determined by rational canonical forms as follows:

(8)

\displaystyle\{\lambda I:\lambda\in C_{p}^{\times}\}\cup\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
1&b\end{pmatrix}:a\in C_{p}^{\times},\;b\in C_{p}\right\},

where
I=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

(cf.
[
8
, Chapter 6]
,
[
2
,
9
]
). Note that
I\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
corresponds to a trivial quandle.

•

For
\lambda\in C_{p}^{\times}
with
\lambda\neq 1
, since
1-\lambda\in C_{p}^{\times}
, we see that
\operatorname{Im}(1-\lambda)I=C_{p}\times C_{p}
.
Hence, by Corollary
1.5
, we obtain that each
\lambda\in C_{p}^{\times}
defines different quandles.

•

For
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
1&b\end{pmatrix}
with
a\in C_{p}^{\times}
and
b\in C_{p}
, let us consider
N:=I-M
. Then

\displaystyle N=\begin{pmatrix}1&-a\\
-1&1-b\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}1&-a\\
0&1-a-b\end{pmatrix},\text{ i.e., }\operatorname{rank}N=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }b=1-a,\\
2&\text{if }b\neq 1-a.\end{cases}

If
b\neq 1-a
, then
N
is regular, so
\operatorname{Im}\rho=\operatorname{Im}N=C_{p}\times C_{p}
. Hence, each
M
defines different quandles.
If
b=1-a
, then
\operatorname{Im}\rho
is the image of
\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}
, i.e.,
P\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{p}
.
Moreover, since
M\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}=-a\begin{pmatrix}1\\
-1\end{pmatrix}
, we see that
Q(P,M)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(C_{p},-a)
.
Hence, each
a\in C_{p}^{\times}
defines different quandles.

Therefore, in this case,
{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p}\times C_{p})
bijectively corresponds to (
8
) itself.

Furthermore, from the above discussions, we see the following:

•

Q(C_{p^{2}},1)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(C_{p}\times C_{p},I)
holds;

•

Q(C_{p^{2}},p+1)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(C_{p}\times C_{p},M)
holds, where
M=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix}
;

•

Q(C_{p^{2}},a)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(C_{p}\times C_{p},M)
does not hold for any
a\in C_{p^{2}}^{\times}
and
M
in (
8
) for other cases.
In fact, we have
P(Q(C_{p^{2}},a))=C_{p^{2}}
, while
C_{p^{2}}
cannot be a normal subgroup of
C_{p}\times C_{p}
(cf. Proposition
3.3
(i)).

In particular,

|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\operatorname{GAQ}}(p^{2})|=|{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p^{2}})|+|{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{p}\times C_{p})|-(1+1)=2p^{2}-2p-1.

6.7.
n=16

In the case
n=16
, we encounter the problem. Namely, there are two groups
G
and
G^{\prime}

together with their automorphisms
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
and
\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G^{\prime})
such that
we cannot distinguish whether
Q(G,\psi)\cong_{\mathrm{qu}}Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
or not by using Theorem
1.4
.
In fact, let
G=C_{2}\times Q_{8}
and let
G^{\prime}=(C_{4}\times C_{2})\rtimes C_{2}
.
We take
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(Q_{8})\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}\mathfrak{S}_{4}
with
\operatorname{ord}\phi=3
and
let
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G)
be defined by
\psi((i,x))=(i,\phi(x))
,
and we also take
\psi^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{gr}}(G^{\prime})
with
\operatorname{ord}\psi^{\prime}=3
.
Actually, we see that both
Q(G,\psi)
and
Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
do not satisfy
(P2)
, so we cannot apply Theorem
1.4
,
while all of the invariants we have developed coincide.

Hence, we cannot verify whether those are isomorphic as quandles or not by the current methods.

Remark 6.1
.

From Tables
2
–
11
,
it seems that the isomorphism among finite generalized Alexander quandles with the same cardinality can be determined only by the pair of
P
and
\psi|_{P}
.
However, there exist quandles that are not isomorphic even if the pair of
P
and
\psi|_{P}
are equal.
When
n=16
,
Q(D_{8},1,2)
and
Q(D_{8},5,2)
have
P=P^{\prime}\cong_{\mathrm{gr}}C_{4}
(i.e.,
d=d^{\prime}=2
)
and
\varphi_{1,2}|_{P}=\varphi_{5,2}|_{P^{\prime}}=\mathrm{id}
(i.e.,
1\equiv 5\pmod{\frac{8}{2}}
).
On the other hand,
since
|\operatorname{Fix}(\varphi_{1,2},D_{4})|=8
and
|\operatorname{Fix}(\varphi_{5,2},D_{4})|=4
,
the two quandles
Q(D_{8},1,2)
and
Q(D_{8},5,2)
are not isomorphic.

Counting maximal isotropic subbundles of orthogonal bundles over a curve
Daewoong Cheong, Insong Choe, George H. Hitching

Abstract
. Let C C be a smooth projective curve and V V an orthogonal bundle over C C . Let IQ e ​ ( V ) \mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V) be the isotropic Quot scheme parameterizing degree e e isotropic subsheaves of maximal rank in V V . We give a closed formula for intersection numbers on components of IQ e ​ ( V ) \mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V) whose generic element is saturated. As a special case, for g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 , we compute the number of isotropic subbundles of maximal rank and degree of a general stable orthogonal bundle in most cases when this is finite. This is an orthogonal analogue of Holla’s enumeration of maximal subbundles in [ 20 ] , and of the symplectic case studied in [ 8 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
V
be a vector bundle of rank
r
over a smooth projective curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
. For
1\leq k\leq r-1
, a rank
k
subbundle
E
of
V
is called a
maximal subbundle
if
E
has maximal degree among rank
k
subbundles of
V
. When a bundle
V
has finitely many rank
k
maximal subbundles, the problem of counting these subbundles has been the subject of much attention. For example, Segre
[
33
]
and Nagata
[
27
]
proved that if
d\not\equiv g\mod 2
, then a general stable bundle of rank two has
2^{g}
maximal line subbundles. Results for higher rank cases appeared in
[
25
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
36
]
and Holla
[
20
]
gave a closed formula for the number of maximal subbundles, using Gromov–Witten invariants of the Grassmannian. An analogous formula was obtained in
[
8
]
for Lagrangian subbundles of a symplectic bundle.

The objective of the present work is to give a similar result for orthogonal bundles. For a line bundle
L
over
C
, an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle
over
C
is a vector bundle
V
equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\omega\colon V\otimes V\to L
. A subsheaf
E
of
V
is called
isotropic
if
\omega_{E\otimes E}=0
. By linear algebra, the rank of an isotropic subsheaf is at most
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{rk}\,\,V
. By
[
9
]
, an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle
V
of rank
2n
has an isotropic subbundle of rank
n
if and only if
\det V\cong L^{n}
. On the other hand, any orthogonal bundle of rank
2n+1
has an isotropic subbundle of rank
n
.
In this paper, for most configurations of numerical invariants, we compute the number of isotropic subbundles of rank
n
and maximal degree when this is finite.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
r
and
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
the subscheme of the Quot scheme of
V
which parameterizes isotropic subsheaves of
V
of rank
n:=\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor
and degree
e
.
By Riemann–Roch, the expected dimension of
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
is given by
I(n,\ell,e)
if
r=2n
and
I(n+1,\ell,e+\frac{\ell}{2})
if
r=2n+1
, where
\ell=\deg(L)
and

(1.1)

I(n,\ell,e)\ :=\ -(n-1)e-\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(g-1-\ell).

Now suppose there is an integer
e_{0}
such that that the expected dimension of
\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
is zero.
We show for a general orthogonal bundle
V
that
\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
, if nonempty, is a smooth scheme of dimension zero (Theorem
2.5
).
Hence in this case the length of the scheme
\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
is equal to the number of isotropic subbundles of
V
of rank
n
and degree
e_{0}
, which will be denoted by
N(g,r,\ell,e_{0})
.
The goal of this paper is to provide a formula to compute these numbers.

Our main results are Corollary
7.1
for the even rank case and Proposition
7.2
for the odd rank case, which give formulas to compute
N(g,r,\ell,e_{0})
. These formulas cover almost all cases with small exceptions; see Remark
7.3
.
Explicit counting results for low rank cases are stated in §
7.3
.

Let us briefly explain the strategy for obtaining the formula. For the time being, consider the even rank case
r=2n
; the result for the odd rank case will follow from the even rank case. In contrast to the classical and symplectic cases, in general the scheme
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
has multiple components, most of which parameterize nonsaturated subsheaves. To circumvent this problem, we let
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
be the open subscheme of
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
parameterizing isotropic subbundles (that is, saturated subsheaves), and set

\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)\ :=\ \overline{\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)},

the closure of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
in
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
. Assume that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
is nonempty.
Then the above number
N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})
can be realized as the intersection number

\int_{\mathbb{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)}1.

To compute this, we need to develop an intersection theory on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
. For technical reasons, we will work with labeled schemes
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
, where
\delta\in\{0,1\}
, such that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)=\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{0}\>\cup\>\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{1}
(see §
4.2
).
We first construct some special cycles
\Theta
on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
, and then associate to
\Theta
the intersection number

\int_{\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}}\Theta.

To get a closed formula for this intersection number, we follow the approach of Holla
[
20
]
.
There are two basic operations on orthogonal bundles: Hecke transformation and tensoring by a line bundle. We use these operations, together with deformations of bundles and curves, to connect
V
to the trivial orthogonal bundle. For each vector bundle operation, we record a relation between the intersection numbers for
\Theta
and the ‘transformed’ cycle.
Note that an intersection number for the trivial orthogonal bundle turns out to be a Gromov–Witten invariant (
[
24
]
,
[
32
]
). Keeping track of the relations on the intersection numbers for all the involved operations taken to connect the original bundle
V
to the trivial bundle, we can finally express the intersection number in terms of a Gromov–Witten invariant.

We mention that in some sense our result may have a relation with a topological quantum field theory (TQFT) of Witten
[
37
]
. Marian and Oprea studied a TQFT using an intersection theory on Quot schemes, and showed that the so-called Verlinde number of TQFT is equal to an intersection number on a Quot scheme. See also
[
19
]
for a weighted TQFT. We believe that if an “orthogonal Verlinde number” were suitably defined, one could obtain an analogous result. We leave this as a future project.

This paper is organized as follows. In § 2, we record some results from
[
9
]
on orthogonal bundles and isotropic Quot schemes. In § 3, we review the quantum cohomology of the orthogonal Grassmannian. In § 4, we give basic properties of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
and describe its boundary. In § 5, we deal with degeneracy loci on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
and investigate their relations and behavior under Hecke transforms. In § 6, we define intersection numbers on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
and obtain relations among intersection numbers on related isotropic Quot schemes. In § 7, we give a closed formula for counting maximal isotropic subbundles.

\mathbb{C}
of complex numbers. Unless otherwise stated, the curve

.
Given a subsheaf

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L
be a line bundle. A vector bundle
V
over
C
is said to be
L
-valued orthogonal
if there is a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\sigma\colon V\otimes V\to L
; equivalently, if there is a symmetric isomorphism
V\xrightarrow{\sim}V^{*}\otimes L
. A subbundle, or more generally a subsheaf
E\subset V
is

if
\sigma|_{E\otimes E}=0
.

By linear algebra as for orthogonal vector spaces, an isotropic subbundle has rank at most
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{rk}\,V
.

In view of the isomorphism

, we have
\det(V)^{2}=L^{\mathrm{rk}\,V}
. In particular, if
V
has odd rank, then
\deg L
is even. If
V
has even rank
2n
, then
\det(V)=\eta\otimes L^{n}
for some
\eta
of order two in
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(C)
.
The bundle
\eta
determines whether
V
admits isotropic subbundles of half rank as follows. The next statement follows from
[
9
, Lemma 2.5]
(see also
[
14
, Proposition 3.2]
for a more elementary proof).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Then
V
admits an isotropic subbundle of rank
n
if and only if
\det(V)=L^{n}
.

Concerning the odd rank case, by
[
9
, Lemma 2.7]
we have:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n+1
. Then
V
admits an isotropic subbundle of rank
n
.

2.1.
Stiefel–Whitney classes

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
r
. When
L={{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
and
\det V
is trivial,

determines a principal

-bundle (up to a choice of orientation if

[

is the obstruction to lifting a principal

-bundle to a

-bundle; see for example
[

. As in

, this can be generalized to the case where

is even and

. In this situation, by for example
[

, there exists a line bundle

is
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}
-valued orthogonal of trivial determinant.

Definition 2.4
.

Assume that

is even and

. We define

w_{2}(V)\ :=\ w_{2}(V\otimes M^{-1})+\frac{n\ell}{2}\ \text{in}\ H^{2}(C,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}.

By

2.2.
Zero-dimensional isotropic Quot schemes

.

Suppose that an orthogonal bundle

is general in a component of the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(r;L)
of stable

-valued orthogonal bundles of rank

. We impose the following numerical conditions, with

(1)

If

and that

is even. Set

and assume that

.

(2)

If

, assume that

(n+1)(g-1)
are even. Set

e_{0}\ :=\ -\frac{1}{2}(n+1)(g-1)+\frac{1}{2}n\ell

and assume that

Then the scheme
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
is empty for

, and

Proof.

(1)
r=2n
: In the case

, the (non)emptyness for

follows from
[

, Theorem 1.3]
. In fact, the same argument works for arbitrary

Let
U_{C}(n,e)
be the moduli space of stable bundles over

of rank

and degree

, every orthogonal bundle with a stable degree

corresponds to a point of a projective bundle

with fibers

. By dimension count, we have

\begin{cases}\dim{\mathcal{P}}<\dim{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n;L)&\text{if}\ e>e_{0},\\
\dim{\mathcal{P}}=\dim{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n;L)&\text{if}\ e=e_{0}.\end{cases}

Hence if

, the classifying map
\Phi\colon{\mathcal{P}}\dashrightarrow{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n;L)
is not dominant and thus

is empty for a general

.
Suppose that

. Now the tangent space to
\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
at
[E\subset V]
is identified with
H^{0}(C,L\otimes\wedge^{2}E^{*})
. We claim that

is zero for generic

. This follows from
[
11
, Appendix A]
for

. In general, it suffices to exhibit a single

of rank

and degree
e_{0}
with

. By hypothesis, either

or

is even. When
n
is even, take
\displaystyle E=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n/2}F_{i}
for general bundles

. When

is even, take

for general line bundles of degree
\frac{1}{2}(\ell+1-g)
. Then the vanishing of

can be checked easily.

It follows that

U_{C}(n,e_{0})^{\circ}\ :=\ \{E\in U_{C}(n,e_{0})\>|\>h^{1}(C,L\otimes\wedge^{2}E^{*})=0\}.

is a nonempty open subset of

. For any

as an isotropic subbundle. Therefore the restriction of

to

is quasi-finite. Hence, by dimension count it is dominant. On the other hand, for dimension reasons the restriction of

to

cannot be dominant. Thus a general

admits no isotropic subbundle

with

. It follows that for general

the scheme
\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
is nonempty, smooth and of dimension zero.

Finally, if
V
admits a non-saturated subsheaf
[E\subset V]\in\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
, then its saturation
\overline{E}
is an isotropic subbundle of degree

, which is a contradiction if

is general.

(2)
r=2n+1
: Note that
(\det V)^{2}\cong L^{2n+1}

2.3.
Orthogonal Grassmann bundles

In this subsection we shall work in the following slightly more general situation. Let
\mathscr{C}
be a variety and
\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{C}
a line bundle, and let
\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{C}
be an
\mathscr{L}
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Generalizing the orthogonal Grassmannian
\mathrm{OG}(V)
, let
\mathrm{OG}(\mathscr{V})
be the closed subvariety of the Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{Gr}(n,\mathscr{V})
whose fiber at
x\in\mathscr{C}
is the orthogonal Grassmannian
\mathrm{OG}(n,\mathscr{V}_{x})\subset\mathrm{Gr}(n,\mathscr{V}_{x})
.

Before proceeding, we need a definition and a lemma. Let
M
be an invertible symmetric
r\times r
matrix defining a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. We recall from
[
3
]
that the
conformal orthogonal group

\mathrm{GO}_{r}
is the set

\{A\in\mathrm{GL}_{r}\>|\>{{}^{t}A}MA=p(A)M\hbox{ for some }p(A)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\},

It is easy to see that
\mathrm{GO}_{r}
is the image of the multiplication map
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times\mathrm{O}_{r}\to\mathrm{GL}_{r}
.
We write
\mathrm{GO}_{r}^{0}
for the image of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times\mathrm{SO}_{r}
. Moreover, for
k\geq 1
we denote the group of
k
th roots of unity by
\mathbf{\mu}_{k}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and suppose
A\in\mathrm{GO}_{2n}
. Then
A\in\mathrm{GO}_{2n}^{0}
if and only if
\det(A)=p(A)^{n}
.

Proof.

The “only if” direction is an easy computation.
Conversely, suppose
A\in\mathrm{GO}_{2n}
and
\det A=p(A)^{n}
. Fix
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
s^{2n}=\det A
. Then for any
\zeta\in\mathbf{\mu}_{2n}
, the matrix
\zeta^{-1}s^{-1}A
has determinant
1
. As
A\in\mathrm{GO}_{2n}
, for each
\zeta\in\mathbf{\mu}_{2n}
we have

{{}^{t}(\zeta^{-1}s^{-1}A)}M(\zeta^{-1}s^{-1}A)\ =\ \zeta^{-2}s^{-2}p(A)M.

Now by hypothesis,
p(A)^{n}=s^{2n}
. Hence
p(A)=\xi s^{2}
for some
\xi\in\mathbf{\mu}_{n}
, and we can find
\zeta\in\mathbf{\mu}_{2n}
such that
\zeta^{2}=\xi
. Then
A=(\zeta s)\cdot(\zeta^{-1}s^{-1}A)
belongs to the image of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times\mathrm{SO}_{2n}
, as desired. ∎

3.
Quantum cohomology of orthogonal Grassmannians

In this section, we record some known facts on quantum cohomology of orthogonal Grassmannians. We begin by recalling some basic objects and fixing notation.

3.1.
Notation

A
partition

\lambda
is a weakly decreasing sequence of nonnegative integers
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
. The nonzero
\lambda_{i}
are called the
parts
of
\lambda
. The number of parts is called the
length
of
\lambda
and is denoted
l(\lambda)
. The sum
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}
is called the
weight
of
\lambda
and is denoted
|\lambda|
.

For a positive integer
m
, let
{\mathcal{R}}(m)
be the set of all partitions
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq m
. Such a
\lambda
is called
strict
if
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{i+1}
for
0\leq i<l(\lambda)
. Denote by
{\mathcal{D}}(m)
the set of all strict partitions in
{\mathcal{R}}(m)
.

We shall use the following notation to state the Vafa–Intriligator-type formula in §
3.4
. For
m=2r+1
, set

{\mathcal{T}}_{m}\ :=\ \left\{J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\>|\>-r\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{m}\leq 3r+1\right\},

and for
m=2r
, set

{\mathcal{T}}_{m}\ :=\ \left\{J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\left({\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{m}\>{\big|}\>-r+\frac{1}{2}\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{m}\leq 3r-\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

For
J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{m}
and
\zeta:=\exp\left(\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{m}\right)
, we write
\zeta^{J}:=\left(\zeta^{j_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{j_{m}}\right)
. Define a subset
{\mathcal{I}}_{m}
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{m}
by

{\mathcal{I}}_{m}\ :=\ \left\{J=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{m}\hskip 3.61371pt|\>\zeta^{j_{k}}\neq-\zeta^{j_{l}}\text{ for }k\neq l\right\}.

3.2.
Symmetric polynomials

Let
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
be an
m
-tuple of variables. For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
h_{i}(X)
(resp.,
e_{i}(X)
) be the
i
-th complete (resp., elementary) symmetric function in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
. By convention,
h_{0}(X)=e_{0}(X)=1
and
h_{k}(X)=e_{k}(X)=0
for
k<0
.

The
\widetilde{P}
-polynomials of Pragacz and Ratajski
[
30
]
are indexed by the elements of
{\mathcal{R}}(m)
. We put
\widetilde{P}_{0}(X)=1
and for
\lambda=(a)
with
1\leq a\leq m
, we define

\widetilde{P}_{(a)}(X)\ :=\ \frac{1}{2}e_{a}(X).

Next for
\lambda=(a,b)
with
b>0
, we define

\widetilde{P}_{(a,b)}(X)\ :=\ \frac{1}{4}\left(e_{a}(X)e_{b}(X)+2\sum_{k=1}^{b}(-1)^{k}e_{a+k}(X)e_{b-k}(X)\right).

By convention,
\widetilde{P}_{a,0}(X)=\widetilde{P}_{a}(X)=\frac{1}{2}e_{i}(X)
.

For any partition
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}(m)
with
l(\lambda)\geq 3
, let
M_{\lambda}(X)
be the
r\times r
skew-symmetric matrix for
r:=2\lfloor(l(\lambda)+1)/2\rfloor
whose
(i,j)
-th entry is
\widetilde{P}_{(\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j})}(X)
for
1\leq i<j\leq r
.

Then the
\widetilde{P}
-polynomial associated to
\lambda
is defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{\lambda}(X)\ =\ \textrm{Pfaff}(M_{\lambda}(X)).

By convention, for
1\leq k\leq m
we write
\widetilde{P}_{k}(X)
for
\widetilde{P}_{(k)}(X)
.

For any partition
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}(m)
, the Schur polynomial
S_{\lambda}(X)
is defined by

S_{\lambda}(X)\ :=\ \det\left[h_{\lambda_{i}+j-i}(X)\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\ =\ \det\left[e_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}+j-i}(X)\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq m}

where
\lambda^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate partition of
\lambda
.

Consider the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]^{S_{m}}
of symmetric polynomials with coefficients in
{\mathbb{C}}
. Recall that any symmetric polynomial
Q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
may be expressed as a polynomial in
\alpha_{i}:=\frac{1}{2}e_{i}(X)
for
1\leq i\leq m
. Therefore, in what follows we shall
simply use the notation
Q(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
for the rewriting of
Q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
as a polynomial in
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
if no confusion should arise.

3.3.
Cohomology of orthogonal Grassmannians

The main reference for this subsection is
[
24
]
.

Let
\Gamma={\mathbb{C}}^{2n}
be a vector space equipped with a nondegenerate quadratic form
\sigma\colon\Gamma\otimes\Gamma\to{\mathbb{C}}
. By linear algebra, maximal isotropic subspaces in
\Gamma
have dimension
n
. Let
\mathrm{OG}(n,\Gamma)
or simply
\mathrm{OG}(n)
denote the orthogonal Grassmannian parameterizing isotropic subspaces of dimension
n
in
\Gamma
. This is a variety of dimension
\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The orthogonal Grassmannian
\mathrm{OG}(n)
has two irreducible connected components. Two
n
-dimensional isotropic subspaces
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
belong to the same component if and only if
\dim(\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})\equiv n\mod 2
.

Proof.

See
[
18
, Proposition 2, p. 735]
. ∎

It is well-known that two components of
\mathrm{OG}(n)
are isomorphic to each other. Let us fix one component
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
. Let
\mathbf{E}
be the tautological subbundle of the trivial bundle
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}\times\Gamma
on
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
.
We choose a complete isotropic flag in
\Gamma
:

H_{\bullet}:\ H_{1}\ \subset\ H_{2}\ \subset\ \cdots\ \subset H_{n-1}\subset{H}_{n}

such that
H_{n}
belongs to
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
.
Note that there is another maximal isotropic subspace
H_{n}^{\prime}
containing
H_{n-1}
which does not belong to
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
.

We now write
Z:=\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
. For each strict partition
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
, we define a Schubert variety
Z_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet})\subset Z
by

Z_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet})\ =\ \left\{\Sigma\in Z\ \bigg|\begin{aligned} \ \mathrm{rk}\,\left(\Sigma\rightarrow\Gamma/H_{n-\lambda_{i}}^{\perp}\right)&\leq n-i-\lambda_{i}\hbox{ for }1\leq i\leq l(\lambda)\\
\ \mathrm{rk}\,\left(\Sigma\rightarrow\Gamma/\widetilde{H}_{n}\right)&\leq n-l(\lambda)-1\end{aligned}\right\}

where
\widetilde{H}_{n}=H_{n}
if
l(\lambda)\not\equiv n\mod 2
and
\widetilde{H}_{n}=H_{n}^{\prime}
if
l(\lambda)\equiv n\mod 2
.

The locus
Z_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet})
has codimension
|\lambda|
in
Z
. Let
\tau_{\lambda}
be the cohomology class
Z_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet})
, which is called the
Schubert class associated to
\lambda
. The set
\{\tau_{\lambda}\>|\>\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)\}
forms a basis of
H^{*}(Z)
.
For the variables
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
, we identify
e_{i}(X)=c_{i}(\mathbf{E}^{\vee})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Then the cohomology class corresponding to
\widetilde{P}_{\lambda}(X)
coincides with
\tau_{\lambda}
.

By convention, we write
Z_{k}(H_{\bullet})
and
\tau_{k}
for the
special Schubert variety

Z_{(k)}(H_{\bullet})
and the associated class
\tau_{(k)}\in H^{2k}(Z)
, respectively.

3.4.
A Vafa–Intriligator-type formula

In this subsection, we state a Vafa–Intriligator-type formula for
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
, which computes the Gromov–Witten invariants. We begin by defining these invariants.

The
degree
of a morphism
f\colon C\rightarrow\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
is defined as the intersection number

\int_{[\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}]}f_{*}[C]\cdot\tau_{1}\ =:\ \deg(f).

For the induced isotropic subbundle
E_{f}
of the trivial orthogonal bundle
{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}^{\oplus 2n}
, we have
\deg(E_{f})=-2\deg(f)
.

We now give an informal definition of the Gromov–Witten invariant. For the precise definition, we refer to
[
32
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
be distinct points of
C
. Let
{\lambda^{1}},\ldots,{\lambda^{m}}\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
be strict partitions. Fix a nonnegative integer
d
. The
Gromov–Witten invariant

\langle\tau_{\lambda^{1}},\ldots,\tau_{\lambda^{m}}\rangle_{C,d}
can be informally defined as follows. If

(3.1)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\lambda^{j}|\ =\ \frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(1-g)+2(n-1)d,

then
\langle\tau_{\lambda^{1}},\ldots,\tau_{\lambda^{m}}\rangle_{C,d}
is the number of morphisms
f\colon C\rightarrow\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
of degree
d
with the property that for each
i
, we have
f(p_{i})\in Z_{\lambda^{i}}(\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{\bullet})
for a general
\gamma_{i}\in\mathrm{SO}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
. If (
3.1
) does not hold, then we define
\langle\tau_{\lambda^{1}},\ldots,\tau_{\lambda^{m}}\rangle_{C,d}
to be zero.

Since the Gromov–Witten invariant is independent of the points
p_{i}
and the curve
C
, depending only on the genus
g
(see
[
32
, p. 262]
), we write
\langle\sigma_{\lambda^{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{\lambda^{m}}\rangle_{g,d}
for
\langle\sigma_{\lambda^{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{\lambda^{m}}\rangle_{C,d}
.

The (small) quantum cohomology ring of
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
is defined via the genus zero three-point Gromov–Witten invariants
[
24
]
. Let
q
be a formal variable of degree
2(n-1)
. The ring
qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic as a
{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
-module to
H^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
. The multiplication in
qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{Z}})
is given by the formula

\tau_{\lambda}*\tau_{\mu}\ =\ \sum_{d\geq 0}\sum_{\nu}\left\langle\tau_{\lambda},\tau_{\mu},\tau_{\nu^{\prime}}\right\rangle_{0,d}\tau_{\nu}\>q^{d},

where
\nu
ranges over all strict partitions satisfying
|\nu|=|\lambda|+|\mu|-2(n-1)d
, and as before
\nu^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate partition of
\nu
. Note that the specialization of the (complexified) quantum cohomology ring at
q=1
is given by

qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})_{q=1}\ :=\ qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[q]/(q-1).

As a complex vector space, this is isomorphic to
H^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})
.

Now we can give a Vafa–Intriligator-type formula for
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
for arbitrary genus
g
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
m
partitions
\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{m}\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
and a nonnegative integer
d
, the genus
g
Gromov–Witten invariant
\left\langle\tau_{\lambda^{1}},\tau_{\lambda^{2}},\ldots,\tau_{\lambda^{m}}\right\rangle_{g,d}
for
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
is given by

\left\langle\tau_{\lambda^{1}},\tau_{\lambda^{2}},\ldots,\tau_{\lambda^{m}}\right\rangle_{g,d}\ =\ 4^{d}\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n-1}}S_{\rho_{n-1}}\left(\zeta^{J}\right)^{g-1}\widetilde{P}_{\lambda^{1}}\left(\zeta^{J}\right)\widetilde{P}_{\lambda^{2}}\left(\zeta^{J}\right)\cdots\widetilde{P}_{\lambda^{m}}\left(\zeta^{J}\right)

whenever (
3.1
) holds, and it is zero otherwise.

Proof.

For
g=0
, the formula was given in
[
6
]
. For an arbitrary
g
, we have the formula from
[
5
, p. 1263]
:

(3.2)

\left\langle\tau_{\lambda^{1}},\tau_{\lambda^{2}},\ldots,\tau_{{\lambda}^{m}}\right\rangle_{g,d}\ =\ \mathrm{tr}\left([\mathcal{E}^{g-1}\sigma_{\lambda^{1}}\cdots\sigma_{\lambda^{m}}]\right),

where
\mathcal{E}
is the quantum Euler class (cf.
[
1
]
) of
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}
in
qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})_{q=1}
, and for each
\tau\in qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})_{q=1}
, we denote by
[\tau]
the corresponding quantum multiplication operator on
qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})_{q=1}
. Then the formula follows from
[
7
, Theorem 6.5]
where the eigenvalues of
[\tau]
were
explicitly computed for an arbitrary
\tau\in qH^{*}(\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0},{\mathbb{C}})_{q=1}
. ∎

4.
Isotropic Quot schemes

4.1.
Generalities

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Let
\ell:=\deg(L)
. For each integer
e
, we define the
isotropic Quot scheme

\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
by

\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)\ :=\ \left\{[j\colon E\to V]\in\mathrm{Quot}^{n,n\ell-e}(V)\>|\>E\hbox{ isotropic in }V\right\},

a closed subscheme of
\mathrm{Quot}^{n,n\ell-e}(V)
. We denote by
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
the open subscheme consisting of saturated isotropic subsheaves; that is, isotropic subbundles. As discussed in
[
9
, § 5.1]
, in contrast to the classical and symplectic cases, in general the scheme
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
is neither irreducible nor equidimensional, and even for
e\ll 0
can have arbitrarily many components consisting entirely of unsaturated subsheaves. For these reasons, we replace
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
with a subscheme which is more tractable and better suited to the enumeration problem that we wish to solve.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of degree
n\ell
. We denote by
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
the closure of
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
in
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
. And we write
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
for
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V)
. Abusing language, we shall also refer to
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
as an “isotropic Quot scheme”.

The following is
[
9
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of even rank
2n\geq 4
and of determinant
L^{n}
. Then there is an integer
e(V)
such that:

(a)

If
\ell
is even, then for each
e\leq e(V)
with
e\equiv e(V)\mod 2
, the locus
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
has two nonempty connected irreducible components, both of which are generically smooth of dimension

I(n,\ell,e)\ :=\ (1-n)e+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(\ell+1-g).

Moreover,
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
is empty when
e\not\equiv e(V)\mod 2
.

(b)

If
\ell
is odd, then for each
e\leq e(V)
, the locus
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
is nonempty and irreducible, and generically smooth of dimension
I(n,\ell,e)
.

As in the symplectic case
[
8
, Definition 1.1]
, we shall initially restrict our attention to isotropic Quot schemes which are well behaved in the following sense.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Then we say that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}

has property
{\mathcal{P}}
if every component of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
is generically smooth of the expected dimension
I(n,\ell,e)
. We say that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
has
property
{\mathcal{P}}
if both
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{0}
and
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{1}
have property
{\mathcal{P}}
.

4.2.
Labeling of components of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)

Let
L
be a line bundle of degree
\ell
and
V
an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of determinant
L^{n}
. It follows from Proposition
2.8
that the orthogonal Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{OG}(V)
has two connected components. If
\ell
is odd, then by
[
9
, Theorem 2.13 (b)]
there is a canonical labeling of the components

\mathrm{OG}(V)\ =\ \mathrm{OG}(V)_{0}\ \cup\ \mathrm{OG}(V)_{1},

where a rank
n
isotropic subsheaf of
V
defines a section of
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{\delta}
if and only if
\deg(\overline{E})\equiv\delta\mod 2
. If
\ell
is even, then by
[
9
, Theorem 2.13 (a)]
, all rank
n
subbundles of
V
have degrees of the same parity. In this case, we do not obtain a canonical labeling of the components of
\mathrm{OG}(V)
, so once and for all we fix a labeling
\mathrm{OG}(V)=\mathrm{OG}(V)_{0}\cup\mathrm{OG}(V)_{1}
. This naturally gives rise to labelings
\mathrm{OG}\left(V_{p}\right)=\mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{0}\cup\mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{1}
for each
p\in C
, as well as

\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)\ =\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{0}\ \cup\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)\ =\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{0}\ \cup\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{1}.

Note that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
may be empty for some
\delta
.

For convenience, we define a labeling function
\delta_{V}\colon\mathrm{OG}(V)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
which assigns the labeling to each isotropic subspace of
V
. It should be mentioned that the function
\delta_{V}
has nothing to do with
\delta_{W}
for any
W\ncong V
when
\ell=\frac{2\deg(V)}{\mathrm{rk}\,(V)}
is even.

Remark 4.4
.

Assume that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
is not empty. Fix
[F\to V]\in\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, an element
[E\to V]\in\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
belongs to
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
if and only if
\dim\left(\overline{E}_{p}\cap F_{p}\right)\ \equiv\ n\mod 2
for all
x\in C
.

4.3.
Nonsaturated loci of isotropic Quot schemes

For the analogue of property
{\mathcal{P}}
in the symplectic case used in
[
8
]
, it was also required that a general point of the isotropic Quot scheme correspond to a saturated subsheaf. In the present situation, this follows from the definition of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
. However, in general
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
does have a nonsaturated boundary, whose structure is analyzed in
[
9
, § 5.2]
. We now examine this more closely.

Definition 4.5
.

(a)

We say that a torsion sheaf
\tau
on
C
is
of type

{\mathcal{T}}
if there is a filtration

0\ =\ \tau_{0}\ \subset\ \tau_{1}\ \subset\ \cdots\ \subset\ \tau_{r}\ =\ \tau

where
\tau_{i}/\tau_{i-1}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{x_{i}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
for some
x_{i}\in C
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Clearly a torsion sheaf of type
{\mathcal{T}}
has even degree. Note that the filtration may not be unique.

(b)

We say that an element
[E\to V]
of
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
is
of type

{\mathcal{T}}
if either
E\in\mathrm{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
or the quotient
\overline{E}/E
is of type
{\mathcal{T}}
.

(c)

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\to B\times C
be a family of bundles of rank
n
. We write
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
for the sublocus of the relative Quot scheme
{\mathcal{Q}}uot^{0,2r}({\mathcal{F}})\to B
of elementary transformations
0\to E\to{\mathcal{F}}_{b}\to\tau\to 0
where
\tau
is of type
{\mathcal{T}}
and length
2r
. It is also a scheme over
B
.

The significance of the type
{\mathcal{T}}
property comes from the following (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.7]
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of even rank
2n\geq 4
.

(a)

Every point
[E\to V]
in
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)=\overline{\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)^{\circ}}
is of type
{\mathcal{T}}
.

(b)

Let
e(V)
be as defined in Theorem
4.2
. For
e\leq e(V)
, every point
[E\to V]
in
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V)
of type
{\mathcal{T}}
lies in
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
.

We now study isotropic subsheaves of type
{\mathcal{T}}
more closely, in analogy with
[
2
, § 1]
and
[
8
, § 3.4]
. Firstly, we introduce an explicit parameter space for elementary transformations of type
{\mathcal{T}}
for use in estimating dimension bounds later. This generalizes and simplifies slightly a construction mentioned in
[
10
, Lemma 5.3 (a)]
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\to B\times C
be a family of vector bundles of rank
n\geq 2
, where
B
is an irreducible variety.

(a)

For each
r\geq 0
, there is an irreducible variety
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})\to B
, projective of relative dimension
r(2n-3)
over
B
, parameterizing flags of subsheaves

(4.1)

E_{r}\ \subset\ E_{r-1}\ \subset\ \ \cdots\ \subset\ E_{1}\ \subset\ E_{0}\ =\ {\mathcal{F}}_{b}

where for
1\leq i\leq r
we have
E_{i-1}/E_{i}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{x_{i}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
for some
x_{i}\in C
.

(b)

For
0\leq s\leq r
, there is a morphism
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}^{2s}({\mathcal{F}})
taking (
4.1
) to the truncated flag
E_{s}\subset\cdots\subset E_{0}
.

(c)

There is a classifying map
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{Q}}uot^{0,2r}({\mathcal{F}})
taking (
4.1
) to
[E_{r}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{b}]
, which is surjective and generically finite to
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
.

(d)

There is a universal bundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{2r}\to{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})\times C
satisfying

{\mathcal{E}}_{2r}|_{\{[E_{r}\subset E_{r-1}\subset\cdots\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{b}]\}\times C}\ \cong\ E_{r}

for each
[E_{r}\subset E_{r-1}\subset\cdots\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{b}]\in{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
.

Proof.

We construct the desired objects inductively. We take
{\mathcal{Q}}^{0}({\mathcal{F}})=B
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}={\mathcal{F}}
. For
r\geq 1
, let
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
be the sublocus of
{\mathcal{Q}}uot^{0,2}({\mathcal{E}}_{2r-2})\to B
parameterizing subsheaves
[E_{r}\subset E_{r-1}]
with

\left[E_{r-1}\ \subset\ \ \cdots\ \subset\ E_{1}\ \subset\ E_{0}\ =\ {\mathcal{F}}_{b}\right]\ \in\ {\mathcal{Q}}^{2r-2}({\mathcal{F}})

and
E_{r-1}/E_{r}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{x}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
for some
x\in C
. The locus of such
[E_{r}\subset E_{r-1}]
is naturally identified with the relative Grassmann bundle
\mathrm{Gr}(n-2,{\mathcal{E}}_{2r-2})
, by sending
E_{r}
to
\mathrm{Im}\,\left(E_{r}|_{x}\to E_{r-1}|_{x}\right)
. As the Grassmannian bundle is irreducible and projective of relative dimension
\dim\mathrm{Gr}(n-2,n)+\dim C=2n-3
over
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r-1}({\mathcal{F}})
, we obtain (a). Part (b) is also clear from the construction.

For (c): The existence of the map
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}\to{\mathcal{Q}}uot^{0,2r}({\mathcal{F}})
follows from the universal property of Quot schemes. By construction and by Definition
4.5
, the image of this map is exactly
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
. Moreover, if
[E\to{\mathcal{F}}_{b}]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
is such that
{\mathcal{F}}_{b}/E\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{D}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
for a reduced divisor
D
of degree
r
, then there are precisely
r!
filtrations of the form (
4.1
) lying over
[E\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{b}]
. Thus the classifying map is generically finite. Lastly, taking
{\mathcal{E}}_{r}
to be the restriction of the Poincaré family on
{\mathcal{Q}}uot^{0,2}({\mathcal{E}}_{r-1})
, we obtain (d). ∎

Taking
B
to be a point, we recover the following (see
[

, Lemma 5.3]
).

Corollary 4.8
.

For a fixed bundle
F
of rank
n\geq 2
, the locus
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)
is a closed subset of
\mathrm{Quot}^{0,2r}(F)
which is irreducible and of dimension
r(2n-3)
.

Let now
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle and
e
an integer. For fixed
r\geq 0
, let
{\mathcal{F}}\to\mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+2r}(V)\times C
be the universal bundle. The next statement follows from the definitions and Corollary
4.8
.

Lemma 4.9
.

The association
E\mapsto\overline{E}
defines a surjective morphism

f_{2r}\colon{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})\ \to\ \mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+2r}(V).

If

is an isotropic subbundle of degree

, then

is canonically identified with

. In particular,
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}({\mathcal{F}})
is topologically a fiber bundle over

.

Clearly
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}=\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)
and

4.4.
Dominance of evaluation maps

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. By
[
9
, Lemma 7.1]
and the definition of
e(V)
given after
[
9
, Proposition 7.14]
, there exists an integer
f_{\delta}\geq e(V)
such that
\mathrm{ev}_{f_{\delta},p}\colon\mathbb{IQ}_{f_{\delta}}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}\to\mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{\delta}
is dominant for a general
p\in C
. We shall require the following stronger statement.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Suppose
e\leq e(V)
and
e\equiv f_{\delta}\mod 2
. Then the evaluation map

\mathrm{ev}_{e,p,\delta}\colon\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}\ \to\ \mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{\delta}

is dominant for generic
p\in C
.

Proof.

Suppose that
e<f_{\delta}
and
e\equiv f_{\delta}\mod 2
. We claim that the image of

\mathrm{ev}_{e,p,\delta}\colon\overline{\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}}\ \dashrightarrow\ \mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{\delta}

contains
\mathrm{Im}\,(\mathrm{ev}_{f_{\delta},p,\delta})
. For: If
\Lambda=\mathrm{ev}_{f_{\delta},p,\delta}(F)
then
\Lambda=\mathrm{ev}_{e,p,\delta}(E)
for any type
{\mathcal{T}}
elementary transformation
E
of
F
such that
p\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(F/E)
. Since
e\leq e(V)
, by Theorem
4.6
(b) such an
E
belongs to
\overline{\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}}
.

But by Theorem
4.6
(a), the space
\overline{\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}}
is irreducible and a general element is saturated. Thus the restriction of
\mathrm{ev}_{e,p,\delta}
to the dense subset
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(V)_{\delta}
is also dominant. ∎

5.
Degeneracy loci and Hecke transform

In this section we define degeneracy loci on the schemes
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
and prove various codimension bounds on the nonsaturated parts of these loci. We then prove various facts we shall require on orthogonal Hecke transformations.

5.1.
Degeneracy loci on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle. To ease notation, we write
{\mathbb{X}}:=\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
. Assume that
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}=\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
is nonempty. Write
\pi_{C}\colon{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\times C\to C
for the projection. Over
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\times C
there is an exact sequence of sheaves

(5.1)

0\ \rightarrow\ {\mathcal{E}}\ \rightarrow\ \pi_{C}^{*}V\ \rightarrow\ \widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}},

where
{\mathcal{E}}
is the universal subsheaf and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}={\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\otimes\pi_{C}^{*}L
. For
p\in C
, denote by
{\mathcal{E}}(p)
and
{\mathcal{V}}(p)
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}(p)
the restrictions to
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\cong{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\times\{p\}
of
{\mathcal{E}}
and
\pi_{C}^{*}V
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}
, respectively.

Identifying the fibers
\widetilde{{\mathcal{E}}}(p)\cong{\mathcal{E}}(p)^{\vee}
, we obtain an exact sequence over
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}
:

0\ \rightarrow\ {\mathcal{E}}(p)\ \rightarrow\ {\mathcal{V}}(p)\ \rightarrow\ {\mathcal{E}}(p)^{\vee},

where
{\mathcal{V}}(p)={\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\times V_{p}
is the trivial orthogonal bundle.

We will define maximal isotropic degeneracy loci on
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}
.
As in §
3.3
we fix a complete flag

(5.2)

H_{\bullet}:\ H_{1}\ \subset\ H_{2}\ \subset\ \cdots\ \subset\ H_{n-1}\ \subset\ H_{n}

of isotropic subspaces of
V_{p}
such that
H_{n}\in\mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{\delta}
. As before, we note that there is a unique maximal isotropic subspace
H_{n}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{OG}(V_{p})_{1-\delta}
which contains
H_{n-1}
. For each
i
, denote by
{\mathcal{H}}_{i}=H_{i}\times{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}
the trivial vector bundle on
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}
. For
i=n
, we have two bundles
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.1
.

For each
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
, we define a degeneracy locus by

{\mathbb{X}}_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet};p)_{\delta}\ =\ \left\{\begin{aligned} {[}\psi\colon E\to V{]}&\in{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\>|\>\\
&\mathrm{rk}\,\left({\mathcal{E}}(p)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(p)/{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}_{n-\lambda_{i}}\right)_{\psi}\leq n-i-\lambda_{i}\ \text{for }\ 1\leq i\leq l(\lambda)\\
&\mathrm{rk}\,\left({\mathcal{E}}(p)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(p)/\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}\right)_{\psi}\leq n-l(\lambda)-1\end{aligned}\right\},

where
\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}={\mathcal{H}}_{n}
if
l(\lambda)\not\equiv n\mod 2
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}={\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{\prime}
if
l(\lambda)\equiv n\mod 2
.

Remark 5.2
.

Later we will be mostly concerned with two types of degeneracy loci:

(1) For
\lambda=(k)
with
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we have

(5.3)

{\mathbb{X}}_{(k)}(H_{\bullet};p)_{\delta}=\left\{[\psi\colon E\to V]\in{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\>\bigg|\>\begin{aligned} \mathrm{rk}\,\left({\mathcal{E}}(p)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(p)/{\mathcal{H}}^{\perp}_{n-k}\right)_{\psi}&\leq n-k-1\\
\mathrm{rk}\,\left({\mathcal{E}}(p)\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}(p)/\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}_{n}\right)_{\psi}&\leq n-2\end{aligned}\right\}.

This will be denoted by
{\mathbb{X}}_{k}(H_{\bullet};p)_{\delta}
.

(2) For
\lambda=\rho_{n-1}=(n-1,n-2,\dots,1)
, we have

(5.4)

{\mathbb{X}}_{\rho_{n-1}}(H_{\bullet};q)_{\delta}\ =\ \left\{[\psi\colon E\to V]\in{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\>|\>\psi\left({\mathcal{E}}_{q}\right)\subseteq{\mathcal{H}}_{n}\right\}.

Since this depends only on
H_{n}
, we shall denote it by
{\mathbb{X}}_{\rho_{n-1}}(H_{n};q)_{\delta}
.

Later we need some bounds on the dimension of certain boundary degeneracy loci.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
and
F\subset V
an isotropic subsheaf of rank
n
. Let
p
be a point of
C
at which
F
is saturated. Let
H_{\bullet}
be a complete flag in
V_{p}
as before. Let
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
be such that
F_{p}\to V_{p}/H_{n-k}^{\perp}
is surjective. Then the locus

\{[E\to F]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)\>|\>\mathrm{rk}\,\left(E_{p}\to V_{p}/H_{n-k}^{\perp}\right)\leq n-k-1\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{rk}\,\left(E_{p}\to V_{p}/\widetilde{H}_{n}\right)\leq n-2\}

has codimension at least
k
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)
.

Proof.

For each
m\geq 0
, let
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2m}(F)=:{\mathcal{Q}}^{2m}
be as defined in §
4.3
. By Proposition
4.7
(c), it will suffice to show that the locus of flags of subsheaves of the form (
4.1
) such that
[E_{r}\to F]
belongs to (
5.3
) has codimension at least
k
in
{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}
. For
0\leq m\leq r-1
, we consider the locus

(5.5)

\left\{[E_{r}\subset\cdots\subset E_{1}\subset E_{0}=F]\in{\mathcal{Q}}^{2r}\>|\>(E_{m})_{p}\to V_{p}/H_{n-k}^{\perp}\hbox{ is surjective}\right.\\
\left.\hbox{and }\left(E_{m+1}\right)_{p}\to V_{p}/H_{n-k}^{\perp}\hbox{ is not surjective}\right\}.

Now
V_{p}/H_{n-k}^{\perp}
is naturally isomorphic to
H_{n-k}^{\vee}
. Hence for any element of (
5.5
), we have an exact sequence of vector spaces
0\to K\to(E_{m})_{p}\to H_{n-k}^{\vee}\to 0
, where
K\subset(E_{m})_{p}
is a subspace of dimension
k
. By hypothesis,
\mathrm{Im}\,\left((E_{m+1})_{p}\to(E_{m})_{p}\right)=:\Pi
is a codimension two subspace of
(E_{m})_{p}
intersecting
K
in dimension
k-1
or
k
. In the first case, we have an exact sequence

0\ \to\ \Pi_{1}\ \to\ \Pi\ \to\ \Pi_{2}\ \to\ 0

where
\Pi_{1}\in\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,K)={\mathbb{P}}K^{\vee}
and
\Pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n-k-1,H_{n-k}^{\vee})={\mathbb{P}}H_{n-k}
. The codimension of the locus of such
\Pi
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(E_{m})=\mathrm{Gr}(n-2,E_{m})
is therefore at least

\dim\mathrm{Gr}(n-2,E_{m})-\dim({\mathbb{P}}K^{\vee})-\dim({\mathbb{P}}H_{n-k})\ =\ (2n-3)-(k-1)-(n-k-1)\ =\ n-1.

In the second case, we have an exact sequence

0\ \to\ K\ \to\ \Pi\ \to\ \Pi^{\prime}\ \to\ 0

with
\Pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Gr}(n-k-2,H_{n-k}^{\vee})
. The codimension of the locus of such
\Pi
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(E_{m})
is therefore at least

\dim\mathrm{Gr}(n-2,E_{m})-\dim\mathrm{Gr}(n-k-2,H_{n-k}^{\vee})\ =\ (2n-3)-(n-k-2)\cdot 2\ =\ 2k+1.

For an
E_{m+1}
of either type, any
[E_{r}\to E_{m+1}]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2(r-m-1)}(E_{m+1})
defines an element of (
5.3
). Moreover, it follows from Definition
4.5
that if
[E\to F]
is any type
{\mathcal{T}}
elementary transformation with
(F/E)_{p}
nonzero, then the rank of the map
E_{p}\to F_{p}
is at most
n-2
. Therefore, every
[E_{m}\to F]
belonging to (
5.5
), and hence any elementary transformation of any such
E_{m}
, satisfies the second condition
\mathrm{rk}\,\left(E_{p}\to V_{p}\to V_{p}/\widetilde{H}_{n}\right)\leq n-2
.

In summary, the locus (
5.3
) is of codimension at least
\min\{2k+1,n-1\}
. As
k\leq n-1
, we are done. ∎

We now prove a similar statement for the partition
\rho_{n-1}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
0\leq m\leq n
and
n-m\equiv 0\mod 2
. For a fixed
\Lambda\in\mathrm{OG}(n)_{\delta}
, the set

Z_{\rho_{m}}(\Lambda)\ =\ \{\Sigma\in\mathrm{OG}(n)_{\delta}\>|\>\dim(\Sigma\cap\Lambda)\geq m\}

is of codimension at least
\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)
in
\mathrm{OG}(n)_{\delta}
.

Proof.

For
\Sigma\in Z_{\rho_{m}}(\Lambda)
, since both
\Sigma
and
\Lambda
are isotropic,
\Sigma
fits into a diagram of the form

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{1}^{\perp}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\Sigma_{1}^{\perp}}/{\Sigma_{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

where
\Sigma_{1}\in\mathrm{Gr}(m,\Lambda)
and
\Sigma_{2}\in\mathrm{OG}\left({\Sigma_{1}^{\perp}}/{\Sigma_{1}}\right)
. Thus
Z_{\rho_{m}}(\Lambda)
has codimension at least

(5.6)

\dim\mathrm{OG}(n)-\dim\mathrm{Gr}(m,{\mathbb{C}}^{n})-\dim\mathrm{OG}(n-m)\ =\\
\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)-m(n-m)-\frac{1}{2}(n-m)(n-m-1)\ =\ \frac{1}{2}m(m-1).\qed

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
F\to C
be a vector bundle of rank
n
. Suppose
0\leq m\leq n
and
n-m\equiv 0\mod 2
, and let
\Pi
be an
m
-dimensional subspace of
F_{q}
for some
q\in C
. Then the set

(5.7)

\left\{[E\to F]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)\>|\>\mathrm{Im}\,\left(E_{q}\to F_{q}\right)\subseteq\Pi\right\}

is empty or of codimension at least
\frac{1}{2}(n-m)(n+m-1)
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)
.

In particular, given a vector bundle
F\to C
of rank
n
, the set

\{[E\to F]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)\>|\>E_{q}\to F_{q}\ \text{is zero}\}

has codimension at least
\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)
.

Proof.

We shall prove this by induction on
r
. For
r=1
, the locus (
5.7
) is empty unless
m=n-2
(that is,
2r\geq n-m
). In the latter case, (
5.7
) is a single point of
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}
. Thus, as desired, it has codimension

\dim{\mathcal{T}}^{2}(F)\ =\ 2n-3\ =\ \frac{1}{2}(n-(n-2))(n+(n-2)-1).

Suppose
r\geq 2
. If
2r<n-m
then clearly (
5.7
) is empty, so we assume
2r\geq n-m
. We make an induction hypothesis:

Suppose
r^{\prime}<r
. Let
\Pi\subseteq F_{p}
be a subspace of dimension
m^{\prime}
where
n-m^{\prime}\equiv 0\mod 2
. If
2r^{\prime}\geq n-m^{\prime}
, then the locus

\{[E\to F]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r^{\prime}}(F)\>|\>\mathrm{Im}\,(E_{q}\to F_{q})\subseteq\Pi\}

has codimension at least
\frac{1}{2}(n-m^{\prime})(n+m^{\prime}-1)
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r^{\prime}}(F)
.

Now let
[E\to F]
be an element of (
5.7
). We may assume that
E_{p}\to\Pi
is surjective, as the locus of
[E\to F]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)
where this is not satisfied is a proper (possibly empty) sublocus of (
5.7
). Then for any filtration

E\ =\ E_{r}\ \subset E_{r-1}\ \subset\ E_{2}\ \subset\ E_{1}\ \subset\ E_{0}\ =\ F

realizing
E
as a type
{\mathcal{T}}
elementary transformation of
F
, the image of
(E_{1})_{p}\to F_{p}
is a codimension two subspace containing
\Pi
. Such
[E_{1}\to F]
are parameterized by

\left\{\Xi\in\mathrm{Gr}(n-2,F_{p})\>|\>\Pi\subseteq\Xi\right\}\ \cong\ \mathrm{Gr}\left(n-2-m,F_{p}/\Pi\right),

which has dimension
2(n-m-2)
. Notice that the preimage of
\Pi
by
E_{1}\to F
is a subspace
\widetilde{\Pi}\subseteq(E_{1})_{p}
of dimension
m+2
.

Furthermore,
[E\to E_{1}]
is an element of
{\mathcal{T}}^{2(r-1)}(E_{1})
with
\mathrm{Im}\,\left((E_{r})_{p}\to(E_{1})_{p}\right)
contained in
\widetilde{\Pi}
. As
2r\geq n-m
, also
2(r-1)\geq n-(m+2)
. Hence, by induction, we may assume that the locus of such
[E_{r}\to E_{1}]
has codimension

\frac{1}{2}(n-(m+2))(n+(m+2)-1)\ =\ \frac{1}{2}(n-m-2)(n+m+1)

in
{\mathcal{T}}^{2(r-1)}(E_{1})
. Therefore, (
5.7
) has dimension at most

2(n-m-2)+(r-1)(2n-3)-\frac{1}{2}(n-m-2)(n+m+1),

which one computes is exactly
\dim{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)-\frac{1}{2}(n-m)(n+m-1)
.
The lemma follows. ∎

5.2.
The Hecke transform

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
. Let
\Lambda\subset V_{q}
be an isotropic subspace of dimension
n
. Let
V^{\Lambda}
be the Hecke transform of
V
with respect to
\Lambda\in\mathrm{OG}(V_{q})
which fits into an exact sequence

(5.8)

0\ \rightarrow\ V\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}\ V^{\Lambda}\ \rightarrow\ {\mathcal{O}}_{q}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\ \rightarrow\ 0.

Furthermore,
V^{\Lambda}
is an
L(q)
-valued orthogonal bundle. Restricting this sequence to the fiber at
q
, we get a map
\psi_{q}:V_{q}\to(V^{\Lambda})_{q}
whose kernel is
\Lambda
. Then the image of
\psi_{q}
, denoted by
\widetilde{\Lambda}
, is a maximal isotropic subspace of
(V^{\Lambda})_{q}
which is canonically isomorphic to
V_{q}/\Lambda\cong\Lambda^{\vee}
. Consider an embedding of ordinary Quot schemes

(5.9)

\Psi\colon\mathrm{Quot}_{n,e}(V)\ \hookrightarrow\ \mathrm{Quot}_{n,e}(V^{\Lambda})

which sends
[E\to V]\in\mathrm{Quot}_{e}(V)
to
[\psi(E)\to V^{\Lambda}]
. This restricts to an embedding of isotropic Quot scheme

\Psi\colon\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda}).

But the image does not necessarily lie inside
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda})=\overline{\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda})}
. It depends on
\delta_{V}(\Lambda)
, where
\delta_{V}:\mathrm{OG}(V)\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is the labeling function.

Lemma 5.6
.

The image of
\Psi
lies inside
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda})
only if
\delta_{V}(\Lambda)\equiv n+\delta\mod 2
. In this case,
\Psi
embeds
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
into
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda})_{\epsilon}
, where
\epsilon\equiv\delta_{V^{\Lambda}}\left(\Lambda^{\vee}\right)\mod 2
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.6
(a), an element
\left[E\xrightarrow{\sim}\psi(E)\to V^{\Lambda}\right]
is a limit of isotropic subbundles in
\mathrm{IQ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda})
only if
\left[\psi(E)\to\overline{\psi(E)}\right]
is of type
{\mathcal{T}}
. As saturated subsheaves are dense in
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
and
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V^{\Lambda})
is closed, it suffices to consider a saturated
[E\to V]\in\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
. In this case,
E\subseteq\psi(E)\subseteq E\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(q)
and

\mathrm{length}\left(\overline{\psi(E)}/\psi(E)\right)_{q}\ =\ n-\dim\mathrm{Im}\,\left(\psi(E)_{q}\ \to\ V^{\Lambda}\right)\ =\ \dim(E_{q}\cap\Lambda).

It follows that
[\psi(E)\to V^{\Lambda}]
is of type
{\mathcal{T}}
if and only if

(5.10)

\dim\left(E_{q}\cap\Lambda\right)\ \equiv\ 0\mod\ 2.

By Lemma
3.1
, this is equivalent to
\delta_{V}(\Lambda)\equiv n+\delta\mod 2
.

Let
\epsilon\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
be such that
\Psi
embeds
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
into
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}\left(V^{\Lambda}\right)_{\epsilon}
. Then in particular
\epsilon=\delta_{V^{\Lambda}}\left(\overline{\psi(E)}\right)
. Now

\dim\left(\overline{\psi(E)}\cap\Lambda^{\vee}\right)\ =\ \dim\mathrm{Im}\,\left(E_{q}\to\overline{\psi(E)}_{q}\right)\ =\ n-\dim\left(E_{q}\cap\Lambda\right)\ \equiv\ n\mod 2,

the last equivalence in view of (
5.10
). Thus
\delta_{V^{\Lambda}}\left(\overline{\psi(E)}\right)=\delta_{V^{\Lambda}}\left(\Lambda^{\vee}\right)
by Lemma
3.1
. ∎

To be more general, let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{t}
be distinct points of
C
. For
1\leq j\leq t
, let
\Lambda_{j}
be an element of
\mathrm{OG}\left(V_{q_{j}}\right)_{\epsilon_{j}}
for
\epsilon_{j}\in\{0,1\}
. Let
\widetilde{V}
be the orthogonal bundle obtained by taking Hecke transforms of
V
along
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{t}
respectively. Then
\widetilde{V}
fits into the sequence

(5.11)

0\ \to V\ \to\ \widetilde{V}\ \to\ \bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}{\mathcal{O}}_{q_{j}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\ \to\ 0.

Then
\deg(\widetilde{V})=\deg(V)+tn
. Let
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{j}\in\mathrm{OG}\left(\widetilde{V}_{q_{j}}\right)
be the image of
V_{q_{j}}
. As in the case
t=1
, there is an embedding

(5.12)

\Psi\colon\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}\ \hookrightarrow\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})

if and only if
\delta_{V}(\Lambda_{j})\equiv n+\delta\mod 2
for each
j
.

The following is an analogue of
[
8
, Lemma 4.7]
in the orthogonal case, but its proof requires a different idea. Recall that property
{\mathcal{P}}
was defined in Definition
4.3
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
. Let
V\to C
be an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
such that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
is nonempty. Let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{t}
be distinct points of
C
, and for
1\leq j\leq t
let
\Lambda_{j}
be a point of
\mathrm{OG}(V_{q_{j}})_{\delta+n}
. Let
\Psi\colon\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}\to\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})_{\epsilon}
be the embedding (
5.12
) for a proper choice of
\epsilon
, where
\widetilde{V}
is the Hecke transform along
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{t}
. Then there is an integer
t(V)
such that if

and the choice of the points

and subspaces
\Lambda_{j}
is general, then
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})_{\epsilon}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
.

Here, abusing notation slightly, we regard
\delta+n
as an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, for any orthogonal bundle
W
, there exists
e=e(W)
such that if

, then
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(W)_{\epsilon}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
for
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
whenever it is nonempty. The number
e(W)
depends on
W
, but in fact we can find a uniform such bound for all points in a continuous family of orthogonal bundles. Denote by
{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n,n\ell)
the moduli space of stable orthogonal bundles of rank
2n
and degree
n\ell\in\{0,n\}
. (Note that

{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n,n\ell)\ =\ \bigcup_{L\in\mathrm{Pic}^{\ell}(C)}{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n;L)

where
{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n;L)
is as defined in the proof of Theorem
2.5
.) Then we can find an integer
e_{1}
such that for every
W\in{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n,n\ell)
, if
e\leq e_{1}
then
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(W)_{\epsilon}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
whenever it is nonempty.

For
d=\deg(V)
, the bundle
\widetilde{V}
is an orthogonal bundle of degree
d+nt
which is stable for a general choice of the points
q_{j}
and subspaces
\Lambda_{j}
. Since
d
is divisible by
n
, there is an integer
m
such that
d+nt-2mn\in\{0,n\}
and
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})\cong\mathbb{IQ}_{e-mn}(\widetilde{V}(-mp))
by the map

[E\subset\widetilde{V}]\ \mapsto\ \left[E\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(-mp)\subset\widetilde{V}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(-mp)\right].

Since the bundle
\widetilde{V}\otimes{{{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}}(-mp)
belongs to
{\mathcal{M}}O_{C}(2n,n\ell)
with
\ell\in\{0,1\}
, we see that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})\cong\mathbb{IQ}_{e-mn}(\widetilde{V}(-mp))_{\epsilon}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
if

. We can take

large enough so that this holds, and then
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})_{\epsilon}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
.
∎

6.
Gromov-Witten numbers

In this section, we define the so-called Gromov–Witten numbers on
\mathrm{OG}(V)
. These numbers are defined as intersection numbers on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
. We make the definition first for
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
having property
{\mathcal{P}}
, and then generalize it to arbitrary
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
using the invariance of intersection numbers under deformations.

6.1.
Intersection numbers on
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
having property
{\mathcal{P}}

We remark that unlike type
A
degeneracy loci, orthogonal degeneracy loci may not represent the corresponding Chern classes of vector bundles. See
[
23
, §  4.5]
for a counterexample. In this situation we cannot obtain the desired cycle as a product of cycles in the usual manner of intersection theory. Thus, as in the Lagrangian case, we shall directly construct a cycle necessary in our intersection theory.

Recall that the expected dimension of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)
is

I(n,\ell,e)\ =\ (1-n)e+\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)(\ell+1-g).

More generally, for a nonnegative integer
t
we set

I_{t}(n,\ell,e)\ =\ I(n,\ell,e)-\frac{n(n-1)t}{2}.

The following is an orthogonal analogue of
[
8
, Proposition 5.1]
, whose proof proceeds along similar lines.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
and degree
n\ell
. Assume that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
. Let
t,k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}
be integers with
t\geq 0
and
1\leq k_{i}\leq n-1
, and satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}=I_{t}(n,\ell,e)
. Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{s},q_{1},\ldots,q_{t}
be distinct points of
C
. For
1\leq i\leq s
, let
H_{i,\bullet}
be a complete isotropic flag in
V_{p_{i}}
. For
1\leq j\leq t
, let
\Lambda_{j}\subset V_{q_{j}}
be an isotropic subspace of dimension
n
belonging to
\mathrm{OG}(V_{q_{j}})_{\delta}
. Then for general choices of
\gamma_{i}\in\mathrm{SO}(V_{p_{i}})
and
\eta_{j}\in\mathrm{SO}(V_{q_{j}})
, the following hold.

(a)

The intersection

(6.1)

\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}{\mathbb{X}}_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,\bullet};p_{i})_{\delta}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\eta_{j}\cdot\Lambda_{j};q_{j})_{\delta}\right)

is empty or of dimension zero in
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
.

(b)

The intersection (
6.1
) is equal to

(6.2)

\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}{\mathbb{X}}_{k_{i}}^{\circ}(\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,\bullet};p_{i})_{\delta}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{t}{\mathbb{X}}_{\rho_{n-1}}^{\circ}(\eta_{j}\cdot\Lambda_{j};q_{j})_{\delta}\right)

In particular, every element corresponds to a saturated subsheaf.

(c)

The scheme (
6.1
) is reduced.

Proof.

Firstly, we claim that for a general choice of
\gamma_{i}
and
\eta_{j}
, the locus (
6.1
) is empty or has dimension zero. To see this, note that the group
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{SO}(V_{p_{i}})\times\prod_{j=1}^{t}\mathrm{SO}(V_{q_{j}})
acts transitively on
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{OG}(V_{p_{i}})_{\delta+n}\times\prod_{j=1}^{t}\mathrm{OG}(V_{q_{j}})_{\delta}
. (Note that
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{\delta+n}
is the component containing subspaces and subbundles intersecting
\Lambda
in dimension zero.) Now each
Z_{k_{i}}(H_{i,\bullet})
has codimension
k_{i}
in
\mathrm{OG}(V_{p_{i}})_{\delta+n}
, and each
Z_{\rho_{n-1}}(\Lambda_{j})
has codimension
\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)
in
\mathrm{OG}(V_{q_{j}})_{\delta}
.

Hence by
[
21
, Theorem 2 (i)]
, the intersection (
6.1
) is empty or else equidimensional and of codimension

\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}+t\cdot\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)\ =\ I(n,\ell,e)

in
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
. As
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
, we obtain the claim.

In view of the claim, statements (a) and (b) would follow if we show that the intersection points in (
6.1
) are contained in the saturated part
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
. We shall do this by showing that the nonsaturated part of (
6.1
) is of negative expected dimension, and then use property
{\mathcal{P}}
together with Kleiman’s theorem.

Let
I
be a subset of
\{1,\ldots,s\}
, and let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}
be integers with
0\leq m_{j}\leq n
and
n-m_{j}\equiv 0\mod 2
for each
j
. Consider the set

(6.3)

\left\{[F\to V]\in\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+2r}(V)_{\delta}\>|\>F_{p_{i}}\to V_{p_{i}}/\left(\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,n-k_{i}}^{\perp}\right)\hbox{ not surjective for }i\in I\right.\\
\left.\hbox{ and surjective for }i\not\in I,\hbox{ and }\dim(F_{q_{j}}\cap\eta_{j}\cdot\Lambda_{j})=m_{j}\right\}

Now by Remark
5.2
, for any such
[F\to V]
and for
i\in I
, necessarily the map
F_{p_{i}}\to V_{p_{i}}/H_{i,n}
has rank at most
n-2
. It follows that (
6.3
) is precisely the inverse image in
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+2r}(V)_{\delta}
of

\left(\prod_{i\in I}Z_{k_{i}}(V_{p_{i}};\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,\bullet})\right)\times\left(\prod_{j=1}^{t}Z_{\rho_{m_{j}}}(\eta_{j}\cdot\Lambda_{j};V_{q_{j}})\right)

by the product of evaluation maps

\left(\prod_{i\in I}\mathrm{ev}_{e+2r,p_{i},\delta}\right)\times\left(\prod_{j=1}^{t}\mathrm{ev}_{e+2r,q_{j},\delta}\right).

By
[
24
]
, Lemma
5.4
and
[
21
, Theorem 2 (i)]
, for a general choice of
\gamma_{i}
and
\eta_{j}
the locus (
6.3
) is empty or of codimension

(6.4)

\sum_{i\in I}k_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2}m_{j}(m_{j}-1)

in
\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+2r}(V)_{\delta}
.

Next, for any
[F\to V]
in (
6.3
), we consider the locus

(6.5)

\left\{[E\to F]\in{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(F)\>|\>\left(E_{p_{i}}\to F_{p_{i}}\to V_{p_{i}}/\left(\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,n-k_{i}}^{\perp}\right)\right)\hbox{ is not surjective for $i\not\in I$, and}\right.\\
\left.\mathrm{Im}\,\left(E_{q_{j}}\to F_{q_{j}}\right)\subseteq F_{q_{j}}\cap(\eta_{j}\cdot\Lambda_{j})\right\}

By the proof of Lemma
5.3
, for any such
[E\to F]
, for
i\not\in I
the rank of the map
E_{p_{i}}\to V_{p_{i}}/\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,n}
is at most
n-2
. Hence such an
E
defines a point of
{\mathbb{X}}_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i}\cdot H_{i,\bullet};p_{i})
for all
i\not\in I
also.

By Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.5
, and since the
p_{i}
and
q_{j}
are distinct, for any
\gamma_{i}
and
\eta_{j}
the locus (
6.5
) is empty or of codimension

(6.6)

\sum_{i\not\in I}k_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2}(n-m_{j})(n+m_{j}-1).

(Note that as pointed out in the proof of Lemma
5.7
, a necessary condition for nonemptiness of (
6.5
) is that
2r\geq\sum_{j=1}^{t}(n-m_{j})
.)

Now since the conditions expressed in (
6.3
) and (
6.5
) are purely on the base and the fibers of
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}\to\mathrm{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+2r}(V)_{\delta}
, to compute the codimension of the component of the intersection of
{\mathcal{T}}^{2r}(V)
with (
6.1
) corresponding to this choice of
I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}
, we can simply add the codimensions (
6.4
) and (
6.6
). We obtain

\sum_{i\in I}k_{i}+\sum_{i\not\in I}k_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\left(m_{j}(m_{j}-1)+(n-m_{j})(n+m_{j}-1)\right),

which is exactly
I(n,\ell,e)
.

But since
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
, the nonsaturated locus is of dimension strictly less than
I(n,\ell,e)
. As there are at most finitely many possible values of
r
, and finitely many possible
I
and
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}
for a given
r
, it follows that for a general choice of
\gamma_{i}
and
\eta_{j}
, the intersection of (
6.1
) with the nonsaturated locus is empty.

Part (c) can be proven in the same way as
[
8
, Proposition 5.1 (3)]
. ∎

Recall from §
3.2
that for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
we have defined
\alpha_{i}=\frac{1}{2}e_{i}(X)
, and made a convention to express symmetric polynomials in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}
in terms of the polynomials
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
. Thus a symmetric polynomial
Q
will appear below as
Q(\alpha)
for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
m
be a nonnegative integer and
Q=Q(\alpha)
a homogeneous polynomial of (weighted) degree
I_{m}(n,\ell,e)
. Then we define
\Theta(Q;\,t)
as follows. For integers
t,k_{1},\ldots,k_{s}
as in Proposition
6.1
, if
Q(\alpha)
is a monomial
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{k_{i}}
, then we define
\Theta(Q;\,t)
as the zero-cycle determined by the intersection (
6.1
). Then we can extend this definition of
\Theta(Q;\,t)
to an arbitrary (weighted) homogeneous polynomial
Q(\alpha)
, by linearity of cycles.

Note that in Definition
6.2
, we defined
\Theta(Q;\,t)
for fixed
p_{i}
,
q_{j}
,
H_{i,\bullet}
and
\Lambda_{j}
, and general
\gamma_{i}
and
\eta_{j}
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
V
be an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of degree
v=n\ell
. Suppose that
{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}=\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
. For a nonnegative integer
t
and a homogeneous polynomial

of degree

, define

(6.7)

N_{C,e}^{v}(Q;\,t;\,V)_{\delta}\ :=\ \int_{{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}}\Theta(Q;\,t).

By convention, we set
N_{C,e}^{v}(Q;\,t;\,V)_{\delta}:=0
if
\deg Q(\alpha)\neq I_{t}(n,e,\ell).

When
t=0
, we write
N_{C,e}^{v}(Q;\,V)
for
N_{C,e}^{v}(Q;\,0;\,V)
.

We shall refer to the numbers

as
Gromov–Witten numbers of
\mathrm{OG}(V)_{\delta}
.

Remark 6.4
.

By Proposition
6.1
, we can keep the intersection (
6.1
) zero-dimensional as
p_{i}
,
q_{j}
,
H_{i,\bullet}
and

vary as long as the
\gamma_{i}
and

[

, Proposition 10.2]
, the number

,

,
H_{i,\bullet}
and
\Lambda_{j}
.

6.2.
Invariance of intersection numbers under deformations

We now show that the Gromov–Witten number defined in the previous subsection is invariant under small deformations of the curve and bundle.

Let
\mathscr{C}\to B
be a family of smooth projective curves over an irreducible curve
B
, and
\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{C}
a line bundle of relative degree
\ell
. Let
\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{C}
be an
\mathscr{L}
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n
satisfying
\det\mathscr{V}_{b}\cong\mathscr{L}_{b}^{n}
for each
b\in B
. By Proposition
2.8
, after passing to an étale double cover of
B
, the orthogonal Grassmannian
\mathrm{OG}(\mathscr{V})
has two components, which we endow with a labeling

(6.8)

\mathrm{OG}(\mathscr{V})\ =\ \mathrm{OG}(\mathscr{V})_{0}\cup\mathrm{OG}(\mathscr{V})_{1}.

Consider now the relative isotropic Quot scheme
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V})\to B
. Via the evaluation map
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}^{\circ}(\mathscr{V})\to\mathrm{OG}((\mathscr{V}_{b})_{x})
for any
b\in B
and
x\in\mathscr{C}_{b}
, we obtain a decomposition, possibly with an empty component:

\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V})\ =\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V})_{0}\cup\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V})_{1}.

Assume that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V})_{\delta}
is nonempty. Let
b
be a point of
B
, and
U\subset B
a neighborhood of
b
. Let
\overline{p}\colon U\to\mathscr{C}
be a local section of the family
\mathscr{C}\to B
. Write
\mathscr{V}_{U}
for the restriction of
\mathscr{V}
to
\mathscr{C}_{U}
. For
1\leq k\leq n
, let
\overline{H_{k}}\colon U\to\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathscr{V}_{U})
be sections such that for each
b\in U
,

\overline{H_{1}}|_{b}\ \subset\ \cdots\ \subset\ \overline{H_{n}}|_{b}

is a flag of isotropic subspaces with
\overline{H_{n}}|_{b}\in\mathrm{OG}(\mathscr{V}_{\tilde{p}(b)})_{\delta^{\prime}}
. Then for each
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
, as in the Lagrangian case
[
8
, § 5.2]
, one can define a degeneracy locus
\overline{{\mathbb{X}}}_{\lambda}(\overline{H_{\bullet}};\overline{p})_{\delta}
on
\mathbb{IQ}(\mathscr{V}_{U})_{\delta}
. The degeneracy locus
\overline{{\mathbb{X}}}_{\lambda}(\overline{H_{\bullet}};\overline{p})
can be viewed as a family of degeneracy loci parametrized by
U
.

Now assume that
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V}_{b})
has property
{\mathcal{P}}
for each
b\in B
. Let
t\geq 0
be an integer and
Q(\alpha)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{k_{i}}
a monomial with
\deg Q(\alpha)=I_{t}(n,\ell,e)
. Then, choosing more sections and flags, we can form a relative version of
6.1
, a family of the intersections (
6.1
) parameterized by
U
. If all the sections and flags are sufficiently general, as
\dim U=\dim B=1
, this family is a one-dimensional subscheme of
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{\delta}|_{U}
; and moreover, each component dominates
U
. Thus, for each
b_{1}\in U
, it determines a
0
-cycle
\Theta(Q;\,t)
of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\mathscr{V}|_{b_{1}})_{\delta}
. Therefore, by
[
16
, Proposition 10.2]
, we now obtain the desired invariance property:

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\to B
and
\mathscr{V}\to\mathscr{C}
be as above. For an integer
t\geq 0
and a homogeneous polynomial
Q(\alpha)
of degree
I_{t}(n,\ell,e)
, the intersection number
N_{\mathscr{C}_{b},e}^{v}(Q;\,t;\,\mathscr{V}_{b})_{\delta}
is independent of
b\in B
.

6.3.
Intersection numbers on arbitrary isotropic Quot scheme

In §
6.1
, we defined intersection numbers on isotropic Quot schemes having property
{\mathcal{P}}
. We shall now define intersection numbers on arbitrary isotropic Quot schemes.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle and
\widetilde{V}
a Hecke transform of
V
as in (
5.11
) with respect to subspaces
\Lambda_{j}
belonging to the component
\mathrm{OG}(V_{q_{j}})_{\delta}
for
1\leq j\leq t
. So we have the embedding

\Psi\colon{\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\ :=\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)_{\delta}\ \hookrightarrow\ \mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})_{\epsilon}\ =:\ {\mathbb{Y}}_{\epsilon},

where
\epsilon=\delta_{\widetilde{V}}(\Lambda^{\vee}_{j})
.

Let
p
be a point of
C\setminus\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{t}\}
, and

a complete isotropic flag in

. Then for
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
, it is obvious that the preimage of
{\mathbb{Y}}_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet};p)
by
\Psi
is equal to the degeneracy locus
{\mathbb{X}}_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet};p)
on

; or, equivalently, the equality

(6.9)

{\mathbb{Y}}_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet};p)_{\epsilon}\cap\Psi\left({\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\right)\ =\ \Psi\left({\mathbb{X}}_{\lambda}(H_{\bullet};p)_{\delta}\right)

holds. Thus, using the equality in Corollary
5.9
:

\Psi\left({\mathbb{X}}_{\delta}\right)\ =\ \bigcap_{j=1}^{t}{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\Lambda^{\vee}_{j};q_{j})_{\epsilon},

we obtain the following.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
V
and
\widetilde{V}
be as above. Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
be distinct points in
C
such that the sets
\{p_{i}\}
and
\{q_{j}\}
are distinct. For

, let

be a complete isotropic flag in

. For strict partitions
\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{m}\in{\mathcal{D}}(n-1)
, we have an isomorphism of schemes

(6.10)

\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbb{X}}_{\lambda^{i}}(H_{i,\bullet};p_{i})_{\delta}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\ \left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbb{Y}}_{\lambda^{i}}(H_{i,\bullet};\,p_{i})_{\epsilon}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{t}{\mathbb{Y}}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\Lambda^{\vee}_{j};\,q_{j})_{\epsilon}\right)

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of degree

, and
\widetilde{V}
the Hecke transform of

at points

as above. Assume that

\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(\widetilde{V})_{\epsilon}

{\mathcal{P}}
. Let

be a nonnegative integer and

a homogeneous polynomial of degree

. Then we have

(6.11)

N_{C,e}^{v}\left(Q(\alpha);u;V\right)_{\delta}\ =\ N_{C,e}^{v+tn}\left(Q(\alpha),u+t;\widetilde{V}\right)_{\epsilon}.

Proof.

By linearity of intersection numbers, it suffices to consider the case when
Q(\alpha)
is a monomial of the form
\prod_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{k_{i}}
. Set
m=u+s
. Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m},q_{1},\ldots,q_{t}
and
H_{i,\bullet}
and
\Lambda_{j}
be as above for
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq t
. Let
\gamma_{i}\in\mathrm{SO}(V_{p_{i}})
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
\eta_{j}\in\mathrm{SO}(V_{q_{j}})
for
1\leq j\leq t
.

Now take
\lambda^{i}=(k_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq s
and
\lambda^{i}=\rho_{n-1}
for

, for general

and
\eta_{j}
, the intersection in the righthand side of (

) gives a zero-dimensional scheme determining a cycle

on

. Recall that
N_{C,e}^{v+tn}\left(Q(\alpha);u+t;\widetilde{V}\right)
is the degree of this cycle. Note that for a general choice of
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}
, the cycle with
\eta_{j}=\mathrm{Id}\in\mathrm{SO}(V_{q_{j}})
for

is obtained as a limit of general zero-cycles

on

as

6.5.
Counting isotropic subbundles

Recall that the parameter space

of isotropic subspaces in the vector space

\mathrm{OG}(n)_{0}

(precisely,

; see
[
24
]
. For the trivial bundle

on

Even rank case

Assume
r=2n.
If
\ell
is odd,
N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})=\widetilde{N}_{g,e}^{v}(Q(\alpha);V)_{\delta}
for exactly one
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. If
\ell
is even,

N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})=2\widetilde{N}_{g,e}^{v}(Q(\alpha);V)_{\delta}
by Remark
6.19
. Thus from Corollary
6.18
, we have

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
e_{0}:=-\frac{1}{2}n(g-1-\ell)
. Then the number
N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})
is given as follows.

(1)

Assume that
e_{0}
is even. If
\ell=4m-a
for some
m,a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
0\leq a\leq 3,
then we have

N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})\ =\ \begin{cases}\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle{{A_{1}}\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n-1}}S_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{g-1}\widetilde{P}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{a}}},&\text{if}\ \>a=0,2,\\
\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle{{A_{2}}\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n-1}}S_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{g-1}\widetilde{P}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{a}}},&\text{if}\ \>a=1,3,\\
\end{cases}

where
A_{1}=2^{2mn-e_{0}+1}=\sqrt{2}^{n(a+g-1)+2}
and
A_{2}=2^{2mn-e_{0}}=\sqrt{2}^{n(a+g-1)}
.

(2)

Assume that
n
is odd and
e_{0}
is odd. If
\ell=4k+2-b
for
0\leq b\leq 3
, then

N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})\ =\ \begin{cases}\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle{{B_{1}}\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n-1}}S_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{g-1}\widetilde{P}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{b}}},&\text{if}\ \>b=0,2,\\
\scriptstyle{\scriptstyle{{B_{2}}\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{I}}_{n-1}}S_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{g-1}\widetilde{P}_{\rho_{n-1}}(\zeta^{J})^{b}}},&\text{if}\ \>b=1,3,\\
\end{cases}

where
B_{1}=2^{n(1+2k)-e_{0}+1}=\sqrt{2}^{n(b+g-1)+2}
and
B_{2}=2^{n(1+2k)-e_{0}}=\sqrt{2}^{n(b+g-1)}.

7.2.
The odd rank case

We shall now use a technique applied in
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
and elsewhere to deduce statements of enumerative geometry for isotropic Quot schemes of odd rank orthogonal bundles from those established for bundles of even rank.
Note that if
\mathbb{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)
is a nomempty smooth scheme of dimension zero, we have
\mathrm{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)=\mathbb{IQ}_{e_{0}}(V)=\mathbb{IQ}_{e_{0}}^{\circ}(V)
.

Let
V
be an orthogonal bundle of rank
2n+1
for
n\geq 1
. As observed in
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
, there is a uniquely determined line bundle
M
such that
M^{2}\cong L
and
\det V\cong M^{2n+1}
. Let
m=\deg(M)
. The following confirms and generalizes
[
14
, Conjecture 8.1]
.

Proposition 7.2
.

For
\ell=2m
, let
e_{0}:=-\frac{1}{2}(n+1)(g-1)+mn
. Then we have

N(g,2n+1,\ell,e_{0})\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\cdot N(g,2n+2,\ell,e_{0}+m).

Proof.

By
[
9
, Lemma 8.1]
, we have an isomorphism

\mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V)\ \cong\ \mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+m}(V\perp M)_{\delta}

for both
\delta\in\{0,1\}
. Note that
V\perp M
is an
L
-valued orthogonal bundle of rank
2n+2
. For
e=e_{0}
and for a general
V
, we have seen in Theorem
2.5
that
\mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V)
is a smooth scheme of dimension zero.
By the invariance of the intersection numbers under a deformation which was discussed in §
6.2
, the length of
\mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+m}(V\perp M)_{\delta}
is equal to that of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e+m}(W)_{\delta}
for a general
W
in the same component as
V\perp M
. Since
\mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e+m}(V\perp M)
is isomorphic to the union of two copies of
\mathbb{IQ}_{e}(V)=\mathbb{IQ}^{\circ}_{e}(V)
, the statement follows.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

In Corollary
7.1
and Proposition
7.2
we don’t have a closed formula for
N(g,2n,\ell,e_{0})
when
n
is even and
e_{0}
is odd, and accordingly a formula
N(g,2n+1,\ell,e_{0})
when both
n
and
e_{0}+m
are odd. See the last paragraph of §
6
for more details.
By the way, in
[
14
, Theorem 5.16]
, explicit formulas are obtained for low rank cases, and in particular for any
g
, it is shown that

N(g,4,0,1-g)=2\cdot 2^{g};\ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ N(g,3,0,1-g)=2^{g}.

7.3.
Counting results for orthogonal bundles of low rank

Now we list some explicit counting results obtained from the previous formulas for orthogonal bundles of rank
r
, where
3\leq r\leq 6
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
C
be any curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Let
w_{2}\in\{0,1\}
be the 2nd Stiefel–Whitney class.

(1)

If
g
is odd and
w_{2}=0
, then
N(g,4,0,1-g)=2^{g+1}
.

(2)

If
g
is odd and
w_{2}=0
, then
N(g,3,0,1-g)\ =2^{g}
.

(3)

If either (
g\equiv 1\mod 4
,
w_{2}=0
) or (
g\equiv 3\mod 4
,
w_{2}=1
) then

N\left(g,6,0,\frac{3}{2}(1-g)\right)\ =\ 2^{2g+1}.

(4)

If
g\equiv 2\mod 4
and
w_{2}=0
, then
N\left(g,6,1,\frac{3}{2}(2-g)\right)=2^{2g}
.

(5)

If either (
g\equiv 1\mod 4
,
w_{2}=0
) or (
g\equiv 3\mod 4
,
w_{2}=1
), then

N\left(g,5,0,\frac{3}{2}(1-g)\right)\ =\ 2^{2g}.

Proof.

By direct computation using Corollary
7.1
, parts (1), (3) and (4) are obtained. Using Proposition
7.2
, parts (2) and (5) are consequences of (1) and (3) respectively. Note that when
\ell=0
, the parity of the degree of a rank
n
isotropic subbundle of
V
coincides with
w_{2}(V)
.
∎

Note that this result coincides with that of
[
14
]
, where the numbers were computed by using canonical isomorphisms for low rank orthogonal bundles. In particular, (5) confirms
[
14
, Conjecture 7.8]
.

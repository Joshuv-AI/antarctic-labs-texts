Expansion formulas for elliptic hypergeometric series
Gaurav Bhatnagar, Archna Kumari

Abstract
. We provide an alternate approach to obtaining expansion formulas on the lines of the well-poised Bailey lemma. We recover results due to Spiridonov and Warnaar and one new formula of this type. These formulas contain an arbitrary sequence as an argument, and are thus flexible in the number of parameters they contain. As a result, we are able to derive eight new transformation formulas for elliptic hypergeometric series. These transformation formulas appear to be new even in the basic hypergeometric case, when p = 0 p=0 .

1.
Introduction

There are a large number of summation and transformation formulas for hypergeometric and basic hypergeometric series, that is, series of the form

\sum t_{k}
, where the ratio
t_{k+1}/t_{k}
of successive terms is a rational function of the index
k
, or, a rational function of
q^{k}
. There are relatively fewer results for elliptic hypergeometric series, where the ratio of successive terms is an elliptic function, the index
k
now regarded as a complex variable.
The objective of this paper is to provide a promising general technique, which is quite productive in finding new transformation formulas, and use it to find eight such formulas for elliptic hypergeometric series.

Among the first results for elliptic hypergeometric series are two fundamental formulas given by Frenkel and Turaev
[
11
]
, one of
which sums a
{}_{10}V_{9}
sum, and the other, a transformation formula for
{}_{12}V_{11}
series. Subsequently, Spiridonov
[
21
]
and
Warnaar
[
23
,
24
]
popularized the area, obtained many results, and proposed several conjectures. Further summation and
transformation formulas appear in
[
4
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
26
]
. Gasper and Rahman
[
12
, Chapter 11]
and, more recently, Rosengren
[
17
]
have given accessible
introductions.

As regards general techniques in the study of elliptic hypergeometric series, Warnaar
[
23
]
used matrix inversion to obtain many transformation formulas; Spiridonov
[
21
]
established the well-poised (or simply, the WP) Bailey lemma, and Warnaar
[
24
]
gave several analogous results. In addition, Chu and Jia
[
9
,
10
]
gave a general approach which leads to several identities. We offer a mild modification of the WP Bailey lemma—a slight change of perspective—that leads to several new transformation formulas.

Like the WP Bailey lemma, our approach requires a pair of inverse, infinite, lower-triangular, matrices.
Thus, we require a pair of matrices

{\mathbf{F}}=(F_{km})
and
{\mathbf{G}}=(G_{km})
, with rows and columns indexed by non-negative integers, such that

F_{km}=0=G_{km}
for
m>k
,
and

\sum_{j=k}^{m}F_{kj}G_{jm}=\delta_{km}=\sum_{j=k}^{m}G_{kj}F_{jm}.

Here
\delta_{km}
is the delta function, which is
1
when
k=m
, and
0
, otherwise.

A key role is played by the entries of a matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
given by:

M_{km}(a,b,q,p)=\frac{{\left({aq^{k},q^{-k}};q,p\right)_{m}}}{{\left({bq^{1-k}/a,bq^{1+k}};q,p\right)_{m}}},

(1.1)

where the
q,p
-shifted factorial
{\left({a};q,p\right)_{m}}
and other notations used here are defined shortly.

If a balanced, well-poised sum, which terminates naturally after
k+1
terms, has an additional parameter
a
, then it must contain this factor. Here the index of summation is
m
.
This is due to a classical theorem on elliptic functions (see Rosengren
[
17
, Theorem 4.12, p. 221]
), which forces
the terms of an elliptic hypergeometric series to satisfy a balancing condition.
The balancing condition ensures that there is a numerator parameter of the form
aq^{k}
. Further, the well-poised condition implies a matching parameter in the denominator, of the form
bq^{1+k}
. The well-poised condition is satisfied because:

aq^{k}\cdot bq^{1-k}/a=q^{-k}\cdot bq^{1+k}=bq.

Thus the factors in (
1.1
) are present in the summand of any terminating, well-poised summation theorem for elliptic hypergeometric series.

An essential ingredient of our technique is the following finite version of a lemma from Bhatnagar and Rai
[
5
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\mathbf{F}}=(F_{km})
,
{\mathbf{G}}=(G_{km})
and
{\mathbf{H}}=(H_{km})
be infinite, lower-triangular matrices, with rows and columns indexed by non-negative integers; let
{\mathbf{F}}
and
{\mathbf{G}}
be inverses of each other. Let
{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}=(\alpha_{j})
be a sequence. Then

\sum_{j=0}^{n}H_{nj}\alpha_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}F_{nk}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha_{j}\sum_{m=0}^{k-j}G_{k,j+m}H_{j+m,j}.

(1.2)

The lemma follows by interchanging the summations on the right-hand side (twice). Alternatively, it follows from the matrix equalities

{\mathbf{H}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=({\mathbf{F}}{\mathbf{G}}){\mathbf{H}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}={\mathbf{F}}({\mathbf{G}}{\mathbf{H}}){\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

Lemma
1
is motivated by work on
q
-Lagrange inversion and its connection with matrix inversion; this is work of Gessel and Stanton
[
14
]
, Andrews, Agarwal and Bressoud
[
1
]
, and others
[
5
,
8
,
22
,
24
]
.

The key idea of this paper is to choose
{\mathbf{F}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}
so that
the inner sum on the right-hand side is summable by a known summation theorem. We call the resulting formula an expansion formula, motivated by Gasper and Rahman’s description of
[
12
, Equation (2.2.2)]
. This still leaves an arbitrary sequence
(\alpha_{j})
as a parameter. On further specialization, and application of a (possibly different) summation theorem, it yields a transformation formula.
What makes the application of this approach particularly simple, is that the entries of
{\mathbf{M}}
appear—in some form—in all of
{\mathbf{F}}
,
{\mathbf{G}}
,

{\mathbf{H}}
and
{\mathbf{G}}{\mathbf{H}}
.

Lemma
1
is related to the WP Bailey lemma. The WP Bailey lemma uses a matrix (see (
2.2a
), below) denoted by

{\mathbf{B}}=(B_{km})
. This matrix was given by Bressoud
[
7
]
in the
q
-hypergeometric case.
Let
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
be two sequences connected by

{\boldsymbol{\beta}}={\mathbf{B}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

The pair
({\boldsymbol{\alpha}},{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
that satisfies this relationship is called a well-poised Bailey pair.
The WP Bailey lemma provides another well-poised Bailey pair
({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{\prime})
.
The WP Bailey chain is obtained by iteration; the lattice is obtained by changing one of the parameters; the Bailey tree (and various branches) appear by using analogous results. See Andrews and Berkovich
[
2
]
and Andrews
[
3
]
.

Now, in Lemma
1
, we choose
the matrix
{\mathbf{F}}
to be Warnaar’s
[
23
]
elliptic extension of Bressoud’s matrix. When we further choose
{\mathbf{H}}
so that the inner sum can be summed using Frenkel and Turaev’s
[
11
]
elliptic summation formula, for a terminating, balanced, and very-well-poised
{}_{10}V_{9}
, we obtain an expansion formula. A referee pointed out that this formula is equivalent to the elliptic WP Bailey lemma due to Spiridonov
[
21
, Theorem 4.3]
. The left-hand side of (
1.2
) corresponds to
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}^{\prime}

(={\mathbf{B}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\prime})
written out explicitly in terms of
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
; the right-hand side matches too, when written in terms of
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
.

Similarly, given a summation theorem, we may choose
{\mathbf{H}}
appropriately to ensure that the inner sum is summable by this summation, obtaining a result analogous to the WP Bailey lemma. This is what we do here—with four known summation theorems—keeping
{\mathbf{F}}
to be the aforementioned matrix of Warnaar. In addition to Frenkel and Turaev’s sum, we use two summation theorems due to
Warnaar
[
25
]
, and one from Lee, Rains and Warnaar
[
16
]
. These four summations are used to recover WP Bailey lemma type results, including Spiridonov’s WP Bailey lemma, as well as two of Warnaar’s results
[
24
]
. In addition, we obtain one new Bailey lemma type result. These are the expansion formulas in
§
2
of this paper.

Next, we apply these four summation theorems, and two more due to Warnaar
[
25
]
, to these four expansion formulas. Of the 24 transformation formulas thus obtained, eight are new; these are listed in §
3
. We conclude in §
4
with some remarks on basic hypergeometric series obtained when the nome
p=0
. In particular, we highlight a transformation formula that appears to be a close cousin of a formula of Bailey
[
12
, Equation (2.8.3)]
, and obtain two summation theorems as special cases.

{\big({q^{-n}};q\big)_{k}}=0={\left({q^{-n}};q,p\right)_{k}}\text{ if }k>n.

(1.3)

(4)

The terms

Expansion formulas

In this section, we derive four expansion formulas by using Lemma
1
.
The pair of inverse matrices we use for this derivation are an equivalent form of inverse matrices given by Warnaar
[

]
.

Proposition 2

(Warnaar)
.

Let
{\mathbf{F}}=\big(F_{km}(a,b)\big)
and
{\mathbf{G}}=\big(G_{km}(a,b))
be defined as

\displaystyle F_{km}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta\!\left({aq^{2m}};p\right){\left({bq^{k},q^{-k}};q,p\right)_{m}}{\left({b/a};q,p\right)_{k}}}{{\left({aq^{1+k},aq^{1-k}/b};q,p\right)_{m}}}\Big(\!-\frac{a}{b}\Big)^{m}q^{\binom{m+1}{2}};

(2.1a)

\displaystyle G_{km}(a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta\!\left({bq^{2m}};p\right){\left({aq^{m+1}};q,p\right)_{k-1}}{\left({a/b};q,p\right)_{k}}{\left({q^{-k}};q,p\right)_{m}}}{{\left({bq^{1-k}/a,q};q,p\right)_{m}}{\left({q};q,p\right)_{k}}{\left({bq^{m}};q,p\right)_{k+1}}}\Big(\!-\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{k}q^{m-\binom{k}{2}}.

(2.1b)

Then
{\mathbf{F}}
and
{\mathbf{G}}
are inverses of each other.

It is easy to see that the matrix inversion of
{\mathbf{F}}
and
{\mathbf{G}}
is equivalent to the matrix inversion of
{\mathbf{B}}=\big(B_{km}\big)
and its inverse, with entries:

\displaystyle B_{km}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\left({b};q,p\right)_{k+m}}{\left({b/a};q,p\right)_{k-m}}}{{\left({aq};q,p\right)_{k+m}}{\left({q};q,p\right)_{k-m}}},

(2.2a)

and

\displaystyle B^{-1}_{km}

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta\!\left({aq^{2k}};p\right)}{\theta\!\left({a};p\right)}\frac{\theta\!\left({bq^{2m}};p\right)}{\theta\!\left({b};p\right)}\frac{{\left({a};q,p\right)_{m+k}}{\left({a/b};q,p\right)_{k-m}}}{{\left({bq};q,p\right)_{m+k}}{\left({q};q,p\right)_{k-m}}}\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{k-m}.

(2.2b)

This is the Warnaar’s matrix inversion as written by Bhatnagar and Schlosser
[
6
, Cor. 4.5]
(with
n=1
,
x_{1}=1
).

Remark
.

Note that the matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
given in (
1.1
) is essentially the same as the matrix
{\mathbf{B}}
. Observe that

can be obtained from

by multiplying by suitable diagonal matrices.

In our derivations below, we find it convenient to take

H_{mj}=h_{j}(m)K(m).

This separates out factors of

that depend on both

to be

with some substitutions for

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{\theta\!\left({aq^{2j}};p\right){\left({a,b,c,d,a^{2}q^{k+1}/bcd,q^{-k}};q,p\right)_{j}}q^{j}}{\theta\!\left({a};p\right){\left({q,aq/b,aq/c,aq/d,bcdq^{-k}/c,aq^{k+1}};q,p\right)_{j}}}=\frac{{\left({aq,aq/bc,aq/bd,aq/cd};q,p\right)_{n}}}{{\left({aq/b,aq/c,aq/d,aq/bcd};q,p\right)_{n}}}.

\frac{{\left({aq,c/b,d,bcq/ad};q,p\right)_{n}}}{{\left({c/a,bq,cq/d,ad/b};q,p\right)_{n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{{\left({cq^{n},q^{-n},bq/d,ad/c};q,p\right)_{j}}}{{\left({bq^{1-n}/c,bq^{1+n},d,bcq/ad};q,p\right)_{j}}}A_{j}\\

These identities suggest that our technique should be useful in the study of basic hypergeometric series.

In conclusion, we can say that given a summation theorem, we can attempt to combine it with a known matrix inversion and derive an expansion formula by using Lemma

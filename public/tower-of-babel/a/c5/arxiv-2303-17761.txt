Differential Flatness by Pure Prolongation: Necessary and Sufficient Conditions
Jean Lévine

Abstract
In this article, we introduce the notion of differential flatness by pure prolongation : loosely speaking, a system admits this property if, and only if, there exists a pure prolongation of finite order such that the prolonged system is feedback linearizable. We obtain Lie-algebraic necessary and sufficient conditions for a general nonlinear multi-input system to satisfy this property. These conditions are comprised of the involutivity and relative invariance of a pair of filtrations of distributions of vector fields. An algorithm computing the minimal prolongation lengths of the input channels that achieve the system linearization, yielding the associated flat outputs, is deduced. Examples that show the efficiency and computational tractability of the approach are then presented.



Keywords—
differential flatness; Lie-Bäcklund isomorphism; Lie brackets; distributions of vector fields; prolongation of vector fields; dynamic feedback linearization.

3.2
Vertical Distributions of Purely Prolonged Ones

Before stating the next Lemma, we need to recall the definition of
vertical bundle
. Given an arbitrary
\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and the fiber bundle
\pi_{\mathbf{r}}: X^{(\mathbf{r})}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m+\mid\mathbf{r}\mid}
, with
\pi_{\mathbf{r}}(\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{r})})=\overline{u}^{(\mathbf{r})}
, its vertical space at
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{r})}
, denoted by
\mathrm{V}_{\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{r})}}X^{(\mathbf{r})}
, is the tangent space
\mathrm{T}_{x}X
. Its vertical bundle, denoted by
\mathrm{V}X^{(\mathbf{r})}
, is the vector bundle made of the vertical spaces at each
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{r})}
,
i.e.
the set of linear combinations
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}(\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{r})})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
whose coefficients
\alpha_{i}
are smooth functions that depend at most on
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{r})}
and where
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
are local coordinates of
X
.

The same definition indeed holds for the vertical bundle
\mathrm{V}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty})
associated to the fiber bundle
\pi:X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty}
,
i.e.
the set of linear combinations of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
whose coefficients are smooth functions of
\overline{x}
.

We now establish some comparison

between Lie brackets of the vector fields of the purely prolonged system and those of the original (non prolonged) one. More complete

may be found in Lemma

of the Appendix

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
\mathbf{j}=\left(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{m}

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(j_{p}-k)}},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{l-j_{q}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{q}^{(0)}}\right]=0,\quad\forall k<j_{p},\;\;\forall l\geq j_{q}\penalty\ \mathrm{s.t.\penalty\ }k+l<j_{p}+j_{q}+1.

Proof.

It is immediately seen that

\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(j_{i})}}=\left[f\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l\geq 0}u_{k}^{(l+1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}^{(l)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(j_{i})}}\right]=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(j_{i-1})}}.

Iterating this computation up to

yields (

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{j_{i}+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(j_{i})}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{i}}\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}=(-1)^{(j_{i}+1)}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}(\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{0})})\frac{\partial}{\partial x}=\gamma_{1,i}^{(\mathbf{j})}\frac{\partial}{\partial x},

k\geq j_{m}-1
. Thus,
\dim\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\leq|\mathbf{j}|
for all

.

The definitions (

)–(
3.13
) and Lemma
3.1
readily yield, for all

,

,

\displaystyle\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}=\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\qquad[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Gamma_{l}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Gamma_{k\wedge l}^{(\mathbf{j})},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}^{(\mathbf{j})}\subset\mathrm{V}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty}),\quad[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{l}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\mathrm{V}(X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty}),\quad[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{l}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\cap\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\{0\}.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that, unlike the filtration

that is increasing for

, the mapping

is only non-decreasing in general.

Remark 3.5
.

In our definition of
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
, we consider Lie brackets of the form
\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(0)}}
, as opposed to
[
2
]
where Lie brackets of the form
\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(0)}}
are used.

Proposition 3.2
.

For all
\mathbf{j}
such that
0=j_{1}\leq\ldots\leq j_{m-1}\leq j_{m}
, with
j_{m}
finite, if
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{i},j_{i+1},\ldots,j_{m})
and
\mathbf{j^{\prime}}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{i},j^{\prime}_{i+1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{m})
for some
(j^{\prime}_{i+1},\ldots,j^{\prime}_{m})
, we have
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}})}
for all
k=0,\ldots,(j_{i+1}\vee j^{\prime}_{i+1})-1
.

Moreover, for all
k\geq 0
,

\dim\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{i}j_{p}+(k+1)(m-i)&\displaystyle\mathrm{if\penalty\ }j_{i}\leq k<j_{i+1},\;i=1,\ldots,m-1\\
\displaystyle\mid\mathbf{j}\mid&\displaystyle\mathrm{if\penalty\ }k\geq j_{m}\end{array}\right.

(3.15)

\dim\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle((k+1)i-\sum_{p=1}^{i}j_{p})\vee(n+i)&\displaystyle\mathrm{if\penalty\ }j_{i}\leq k<j_{i+1},\;i=1,\ldots,m-1\\
\displaystyle\left((k+1)m-\mid\mathbf{j}\mid\right)\vee(n+m)&\displaystyle\mathrm{if\penalty\ }k\geq j_{m}\end{array}\right.

(3.16)

and we have

G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(3.17)

Furthermore, the finite integer
k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}
, satisfying (
3.5
), is such that
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Delta_{k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{(\mathbf{j})}
and
\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Gamma_{k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{(\mathbf{j})}
for all
k\geq k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}
.

If, in addition,
\dim\Delta_{k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{(\mathbf{j})}=m+n
, then

n+\mid\mathbf{j}\mid\geq k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}\geq j_{m}\wedge\frac{n+\mid\mathbf{j}\mid}{m}.

(3.18)

Proof.

By definition, the generators of
\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
are independent for all
k
and thus their number is equal to
\dim\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
, hence (
3.15
). The dimension of
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
, in turn, is lesser than, or equal to, the number of its generators, in number
(k+1)+\ldots+(k-j_{i}+1)=(k+1)i-\sum_{p=1}^{i}j_{p}
,
if
j_{i}\leq k<j_{i+1}
(respectively
(k+1)+\ldots+(k-j_{m}+1)=(k+1)m-\mid\mathbf{j}\mid
, if
k\geq j_{m}
),
and, since, according to (
3.9
) of Lemma
3.1
,
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
is contained in
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\}
if
j_{i}\leq k<j_{i+1}
,
i=1,\ldots,m-1
(respectively in
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{m}^{(0)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\}
if
k\geq j_{m}
), its dimension is bounded above by
i+n
(resp.
m+n
), hence (
3.16
)

The proof of (
3.17
) is by induction. For
k=0
, by (
3.12
)-(
3.13
), we indeed have
G_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}^{(j_{2})}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{m}^{(j_{m})}}\right\}=\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}}\right\}\oplus\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}^{(j_{2})}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{m}^{(j_{m})}}\right\}=\Delta_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Gamma_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}
. Thus, (
3.17
) is valid at the order 0.

Assume now that (
3.17
) holds true up to the order
\nu>0
with
j_{r}\leq\nu<j_{r+1}
for some

, assuming that
j_{r}<j_{r+1}
. If
j_{r}=j_{r+1}
, the reader may immediately go to the case
\nu+1=j_{r+1}
below.

At the order
\nu+1
, two cases are possible: either
j_{r}\leq\nu+1<j_{r+1}
or
\nu+1=j_{r+1}
.
In the first case, using Lemma

, we get:

\displaystyle G_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}

\displaystyle=G_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}+ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}G_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Gamma_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}+ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}\Gamma_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}+ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}\Delta_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}+\left\{ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{\nu+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{\nu+1-j_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{r}^{(0)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{r+1}^{(j_{r+1}-\nu-1)}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{m}^{(j_{m}-\nu-1)}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}.

In the second case, namely if
\nu+1=j_{r+1}
,

\displaystyle G_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}

\displaystyle+\left\{ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{\nu+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{\nu+1-j_{r}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{r}^{(0)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{r+1}^{(0)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{r+2}^{(j_{r+2}-\nu-1)}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{m}^{(j_{m}-\nu-1)}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}.

The case

follows the same lines:

G_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Gamma_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}+\left\{ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{\nu+1}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,ad_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{\nu+1-j_{m}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{m}^{(0)}}\right\}=\Gamma_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}

hence (

) is proven and the property of the number of iterations

to simultaneously saturate the dimensions of

and

immediately follows.

Moreover, if
\dim\Delta_{k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{(\mathbf{j})}=m+n
, we must have

since otherwise, using definition (

∎

Remark 3.6
.

The inequality (

) reads
k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}-\frac{\mid\mathbf{j}\mid}{m}\geq\frac{n}{m}
and may thus be interpreted as an estimate of the gap between
k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}
and the average value
\frac{\mid\mathbf{j}\mid}{m}
of the prolongation lengths

, provided that the prolonged system satisfies the strong accessibility rank condition
\dim\Delta_{k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{(\mathbf{j})}=m+n
.

4
Flatness by Pure Prolongation

4.1
Equivalence by Pure Prolongation

Consider the two systems (
2.5
) with
\dim x=n
,
\dim y=n^{\prime}
and
\dim u=\dim v=m
.

Given arbitrary
\mathbf{j}
and
\mathbf{l}
, we recall that the associated prolonged vector fields are

g^{(\mathbf{j})}\triangleq g\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{p=0}^{j_{i}}u_{i}^{(p+1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(p)}},\qquad\gamma^{(\mathbf{l})}\triangleq\gamma\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{p=0}^{l_{i}}v_{i}^{(p+1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{i}^{(p)}}.

The prolonged states are, respectively,
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{j})}
and
\overline{y}^{(\mathbf{l})}
, and the control inputs
u^{(\mathbf{j+1})}
and
v^{(\mathbf{l+1})}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The systems (
2.5
) are equivalent by pure prolongation (in short
P^{2}
-equivalent) at a point
\overline{x}_{0}\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty}
if, and only if, there exist finite
\mathbf{j}
and
\mathbf{l}
such that the prolonged systems of order
\mathbf{j}
and
\mathbf{l}
respectively are equivalent by diffeomorphism and feedback,
i.e.
if, and only if, there exists a local diffeomorphism
\varphi

and a feedback
W
:

\overline{y}^{(\mathbf{l})}=\varphi(\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{j})})\qquad v^{(\mathbf{l+1})}=W(\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{j})},u^{(\mathbf{j+1})})

with
W
invertible with respect to
u^{(\mathbf{j+1})}
for all
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{j})}
in a suitable neighborhood of
\overline{x}_{0}
, such that

\gamma^{(\mathbf{l})}\circ(\varphi\times W)=\varphi_{\star}g^{(\mathbf{j})}.

This equivalence indeed implies that
n+\mid\mathbf{j}\mid=n^{\prime}+\mid\mathbf{l}\mid
.

Remark 4.1
.

The equivalence relation by
diffeomorphism and feedback
is easily seen to be strictly finer than the
P^{2}
-equivalence
(take
\mathbf{j}=\mathbf{l}=\mathbf{0}
), which in turn is strictly finer than the
Lie-Bäcklund equivalence
(see
e.g.
Example
5.4
).

Definition 4.2
.

We say that system (
2.16
) is
flat by pure prolongation
(in short
P^{2}
-flat) at a point
\overline{x}_{0}\in X\times\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\infty}
if, and only if, it is
P^{2}
-equivalent to
\dot{y}_{i}=v_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
and
y
is called
P^{2}
-flat output.

It is therefore immediate to remark that a system is
P^{2}
-flat if, and only if,
there exists a pure prolongation of finite order
\mathbf{j}
such that the prolonged system is feedback linearizable at
\overline{x}_{0}
, thus recovering the definition of linearization by prolongation already introduced in
[
11
,
12
,
2
]
.

Moreover, a
P^{2}
-flat output being obviously a flat output and pure prolongations being particular cases of Lie-Bäcklund isomorphisms, the class of
P^{2}
-flat systems is indeed contained in the class of Lie-Bäcklund equivalence to
0
(modulo the trivial field
\tau
),
i.e.
constitutes a subclass of differentially flat systems.

The next Lemma extends a well-know result (see
e.g.

[
6
,
33
]
) to our context (see also
[
12
]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

We consider system (
3.2
), denoted by
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j})}
, with
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
, assuming, without loss of generality, that
0\leq j_{1}\leq\ldots\leq j_{m}
, possibly up to input renumbering. We denote by
\mathbf{j^{\prime}}=\mathbf{j-j_{1}}=(0,j_{2}-j_{1}\ldots,j_{m}-j_{1})
, and by
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}})}
the corresponding system. Then
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j})}
is
P^{2}
-flat at a given point
(x_{0},\overline{u}_{0})
if, and only if,
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}})}
is also
P^{2}
-flat at this point. Moreover, every
P^{2}
-flat output of
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j})}
at
(x_{0},\overline{u}_{0})
is a
P^{2}
-flat output of
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}})}
at the same point, and conversely.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j})}
is
P^{2}
-flat, there exists
\mathbf{j_{0}}
and
\mathbf{l_{0}}
such that
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j+j_{0}})}
is feedback equivalent to the linear system
y^{(\mathbf{l_{0}+1})}=w
. Therefore, since, by assumption,
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j+j_{0}})}=\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}+j_{1}+j_{0}})}
,
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}})}
is also
P^{2}
-flat.
The converse is trivial as well as the fact that
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j+j_{0}})}
and
\Sigma^{(\mathbf{j^{\prime}+j_{1}+j_{0}})}
have the same
P^{2}
-flat output.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the single input case (
m=1
), this lemma shows that a
P^{2}
-flat system is equivalent, without prolongation, to a linear system. We thus trivially recover the fact that
P^{2}
-flatness and feedback linearizability are equivalent in this case (see
[
6
]
and remark
2.2
)

4.2
Necessary and Sufficient Conditions

The following Proposition is a straightforward adaptation of Theorem
2.2
for an arbitrary order
\mathbf{j}
. Note that, at this stage, nothing is said about a possible choice of
\mathbf{j}
, a question that will be dealt with in subsection
4.3
, theorem
4.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The prolonged system of order
\mathbf{j}
is feedback linearizable at
\overline{0}
if, and only if,

G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
is involutive with locally constant dimension for all
k
and such that
G_{k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\mathrm{T}X^{(\mathbf{j})}
.

Again, flat outputs can be computed via Frobenius theorem, once established the list of
Brunovský’s controllability indices of order
\mathbf{j}
, as follows:

Definition 4.3
.

Consider the sequence of integers

\rho_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\triangleq\dim G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}/G_{k-1}^{(\mathbf{j})}\quad\forall k\geq 1,\qquad\rho_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}\triangleq\dim G_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}=m.

The Brunovský controllability indices of order
\mathbf{j}
are defined by

\kappa_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\triangleq\#\{l\mid\rho_{l}^{(\mathbf{j})}\geq k\},\quad k=1,\ldots,m,

As in the
0
th order case, if the prolonged system of order
\mathbf{j}
is feedback linearizable at
\overline{0}
, we have:

•

\rho_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
’s and
\kappa_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
’s are non increasing sequences,

•

\rho_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\leq m
for all
k
and
\rho_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=0
for all
k\geq k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}+1
,

•

\kappa_{1}^{(\mathbf{j})}=k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}+1,\quad\kappa_{m}^{(\mathbf{j})}\geq 1
,

•

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}}\rho_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\kappa_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\dim G_{k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}}^{(\mathbf{j})}=n+m+\mid\mathbf{j}\mid
.

The list
\kappa_{1}^{(\mathbf{j})},\ldots,\kappa_{m}^{(\mathbf{j})}
is uniquely defined up to input permutation, invariant by prolonged state feedback and prolonged state diffeomorphism, and is thus equal to the list of controllability indices of the associated linear system (
2.15
) with
r_{i}=\kappa_{i}^{(\mathbf{j})}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Moreover, for all
k
and all
i=1,\ldots,m
, and possibly up to a suitable input reordering, we have

G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\bigoplus_{p=1}^{m}\left\{\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(j_{p})}}\mid l=0,\ldots k\lor(\kappa_{p}^{(\mathbf{j})}-1)\right\},\quad G_{\kappa_{1}^{(\mathbf{j})}-1}^{(\mathbf{j})}=G_{k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\mathrm{T}X^{(\mathbf{j})}

and flat outputs
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
are locally non trivial solutions of the system of PDE’s

\left<G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},dy_{i}\right>=0,\;k=0,\ldots,\kappa_{i}^{(\mathbf{j})}-2,\quad\mathrm{with\penalty\ }\quad\left<G_{\kappa_{i}^{(\mathbf{j})}-1}^{(\mathbf{j})},dy_{i}\right>\neq 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

(4.1)

Finally, the mapping

\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{j})}\mapsto(y_{1},\ldots,y_{1}^{(\kappa_{1}^{(\mathbf{j})}-1)},\ldots,y_{m},\ldots,y_{m}^{(\kappa_{m}^{(\mathbf{j})}-1)})

is a local diffeomorphism.

In virtue of Lemma
4.1
, it suffices to restrict our analysis to prolongations of order
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
such that
0=j_{1}\leq\ldots,\leq j_{m}
.

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 4.1
.

A necessary and sufficient condition for
P^{2}
-flatness at
\overline{0}
is that there exists
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
,
0=j_{1}\leq\ldots,\leq j_{m}
, such that

(i)

\overline{\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}=\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
with
\dim\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
locally constant for all
k\geq 0
,

(ii)

[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
for all
k\geq 0
,

(iii)

k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}
is such that
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\mathrm{T}X\times\mathrm{T}\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\mathrm{T}\mathbb{R}^{\mid\mathbf{j}\mid}
for all
k\geq k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}
.

Proof.

By (
3.17
) of Proposition
3.2
, we have

G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
for all
k\geq 0
.
Then,
G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\overline{G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}
implies that

[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}\oplus\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
. Since
\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\overline{\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}
for all
k
, and since
[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\cap\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\{0\}
by Lemma
3.1
and (
3.14
), we deduce that
[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]+[\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
, hence
[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}

and
[\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
,
i.e.

\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\overline{\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}
, for all
k\geq 0
.

Conversely,
[\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})},\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}]\subset\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}

and
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\overline{\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}
for all
k\geq 0
trivially implies that
G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\overline{G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Moreover, since
\dim\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
is constant by construction, the fact that
G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
has locally constant dimension is equivalent to the fact that
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
has locally constant dimension too for all
k\geq 0
, hence (i).

Finally, (iii) is an immediate consequence of the condition that
G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\mathrm{T}X\times\mathrm{T}\mathbb{R}^{m+\mid\mathbf{j}\mid}
for all
k\geq k^{(\mathbf{j})}_{\ast}
, and the theorem is proven.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Expressed in words, the above necessary and sufficient conditions read (i) involutivity with locally constant dimension of the
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
’s, (ii) invariance of
\Delta_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
by
\Gamma_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}
and (iii) strong controllability rank condition (see
e.g.

[
35
,
13
]
).

4.3
The Pure Prolongation Algorithm

From now on, for every sequence
\mathbf{l}=\left(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
, we systematically re-order the indices
\{1,\ldots,m\}
by a suitable permutation
\alpha:\{1,\ldots,m\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that

0=l_{\alpha(1)}\leq\ldots\leq l_{\alpha(m)}
.
Moreover, for simplicity’s sake, the permutation
\alpha
will be omitted. We will thus abusively replace
l_{\alpha(i)}
by
l_{i}
,
i.e.

0=l_{1}\leq\ldots\leq l_{m}
.

In the following algorithm we assume that the computations are done in a suitable open dense neighborhood of
\overline{0}
where all the distributions involved have constant dimension.

4.3.1
Initialization

Consider the filtration
\{G_{k}^{(0)}\mid k\geq 0\}
defined by (
2.19
). If every
G_{k}^{(0)}
satisfies the conditions of theorem
2.2
, the system is feedback linearizable and no prolongation is needed. In particular, for
m=1
, a case where flatness and feedback linearizability are equivalent, the results of this section are pointless.

Otherwise, we have the following alternative:

•

either there must exist
k_{0}\leq n
such that
G_{k_{0}}^{(0)}
is not involutive while every
G_{k}^{(0)}
is involutive for
k<k_{0}
(note that
k_{0}\geq 1
since
G_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}
is always involutive),

•

or the
G_{k}^{(0)}
’s are all involutive but with
\max_{k\geq 0}\dim G_{k}^{(0)}=\dim G_{k_{\star}^{(\mathbf{0})}}^{(0)}<n+m
.

First Case.

There exists
k_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, first index for which
G_{k_{0}}^{(0)}
is not involutive.

Let
H_{1}^{(\mathbf{0})}\neq\{0\}
be any involutive distribution included in
G_{1}^{(\mathbf{0})}
, of the form

H_{1}^{(\mathbf{0})}\triangleq\{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(0)}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}^{(0)}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p_{0}}^{(0)}},\ldots,\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(0)}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p_{0}}^{(0)}}\}

(4.2)

for some
p_{0}\leq m
.

By (
A.3
),
\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(0)}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(0)}}=\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{l})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(0)}}
for all
\mathbf{l}
and, thanks to (
3.10
), we have

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that the
G_{k}^{(0)}
’s are all involutive with
\max_{k\geq 0}\dim G_{k}^{(\mathbf{0})}<n+m
. Then

G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}=\overline{G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}}=G_{k}^{(\mathbf{0})}
for all

and
\mathbf{j}
and
\max_{k\geq 0}\dim G_{k}^{(\mathbf{j})}<m+n
for all
\mathbf{j}
.

Proof.

According to (
A.3
) of lemma

, every
\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(j_{p})}}
is equal to
\pm\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}^{k-j_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(0)}}
+ terms in

∎

We immediately conclude that, in this case, the strong controllability rank condition does not hold, which contradicts (iii) of theorem

6
Concluding Remarks

We have established necessary and sufficient conditions for a system to be flat by pure prolongation,
i.e.
belonging to the equivalence class of
0
with respect to the
equivalence by pure prolongation relation
. These conditions extend preliminary results of
[
6
,
7
,
33
,
34
,
2
,
12
]
thanks to a thorough study of purely prolonged vector fields.
We then deduce a computationally tractable algorithm giving the minimal prolongation in a finite number of steps using only Lie brackets and linear algebra.

Possible extensions of this work towards general flatness necessary and sufficient conditions are under study.

\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{0})}
.

Proof.

By induction.
It is immediately seen that

\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}=[f\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{j_{k}-1}u_{k}^{(l+1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}^{(l)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(j_{i})}}]=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(j_{i-1})}}=-g_{i}^{(j_{i}-1)}

Iterating this computation up to
k=j_{i}
yields (
A.1
). In particular:

\mathrm{ad}^{j_{i}}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}=(-1)^{j_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}=(-1)^{j_{i}}g_{i}^{(0)}.

Then, for
k=j_{i}+1
, using the fact that

[\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}^{(l)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}]=0

for all
i
,
k
and
l\geq 0
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{j_{i}+1}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}

\displaystyle=[g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{j_{i}}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}]=(-1)^{j_{i}}[g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})},g_{i}^{(\mathbf{0})}]

(A.6)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{i}}[f\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=0}^{j_{k}-1}u_{k}^{(l+1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}^{(l)}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{i}}[f\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}]=(-1)^{j_{i}}\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}g_{i}^{(0)}

which proves that (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) hold at the order
k=j_{i}+1
,
i.e.

\nu=1
, the summation of the right-hand side of (
A.3
) being equal to 0 since
\mathbb{I}^{(\mathbf{j})}_{i,1}=\emptyset
.
Furthermore, a direct calculation shows that

(-1)^{j_{i}}\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}g_{i}^{(0)}=(-1)^{(j_{i}+1)}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u_{i}^{(0)}}(\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{0})})\frac{\partial}{\partial x},

(A.7)

which proves that
\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{j_{i}+1}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}\in\mathrm{V}X^{(\mathbf{0})}
, where
\mathrm{V}X^{(\mathbf{0})}
is the vertical bundle of
X^{(\mathbf{0})}
,

i.e.
the set of vector fields that are linear combinations of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}
, and whose coefficients are smooth functions that depend at most on
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{0})}
(but not of
u^{(l)}
for all
l\geq 1
). It results that
[\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}^{(l)}},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}g_{i}^{(0)}]=[g_{p}^{(l)},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}g_{i}^{(0)}]=0
for all
l\geq 1
, all
p=1,\ldots,m
, and all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Assume now that (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) hold up to
k=j_{i}+\nu
, with
\nu>1
,
and that all the brackets
[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},\ldots,[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]
and
[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},[\ldots,[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}^{q}g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]]
depend at most on
\overline{x}^{(\mathbf{0})}
. We immediately deduce that

[\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p_{r+1}}^{(l_{r+1})}},[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},[\ldots[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}^{q}g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]]]=[g_{p_{r+1}}^{(l_{r+1})},[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},[\ldots[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{0})}}^{q}g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]]]=0

for all
l_{r+1}\geq 1
,
p_{r+1}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
q\in\{0,\ldots,\nu-1\}
,
r\geq 1
,
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r}\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
, and
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Thus:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{j_{i}+\nu+1}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}=[g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})},\mathrm{ad}_{g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})}}^{j_{i}+\nu}g_{i}^{(j_{i})}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{i}}\sum_{(r,\mathbf{l},\mathbf{p})\in\mathbb{J}_{i,\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}}[g_{0}^{(\mathbf{j})},c_{\nu,\mathbf{l}}^{\mathbf{p}}u_{p_{r}}^{(l_{r})}\cdots u_{p_{1}}^{(l_{1})}[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},\ldots,[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j_{i}}\sum_{(r,\mathbf{l},\mathbf{p})\in\mathbb{J}_{i,\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}}\sum_{p_{r+1}=0}^{m}c_{\nu,\mathbf{l}}^{\mathbf{p}}u_{p_{r+1}}^{(1)}u_{p_{r}}^{(l_{r})}\cdots u_{p_{1}}^{(l_{1})}[g_{p_{r+1}}^{(0)},[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},\ldots,[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{j_{i}}\sum_{(r,\mathbf{l},\mathbf{p})\in\mathbb{J}_{i,\nu}^{(\mathbf{j})}}\sum_{p_{r+1}=1}^{m}\sum_{l_{r+1}=0}^{j_{p_{r+1}}}c_{\nu,\mathbf{l}}^{\mathbf{p}}u_{p_{r+1}}^{(l_{r+1}+1)}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p_{r+1}}^{(l_{r+1})}}\left(u_{p_{r}}^{(l_{r})}\cdots u_{p_{1}}^{(l_{1})}\right)[g_{p_{r}}^{(0)},\ldots,[g_{p_{1}}^{(0)},g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots]

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{(r,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{\mu})\in\mathbb{K}_{i,\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j)}}}c_{\nu+1,\mathbf{\lambda}}^{\mathbf{\mu}}u_{\mu_{r+1}}^{(\lambda_{r+1})}\cdots u_{\mu_{1}}^{(\lambda_{1})}[g_{\mu_{r+1}}^{(0)},\ldots,[g_{\mu_{1}}^{(0)},g_{i}^{(0)}]\ldots].

Applying the Leibnitz rule to the penultimate line, using the fact that
\mid\mathbf{l}\mid=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r}l_{\alpha}=\nu
by the induction assumption, we get
1+\mid\mathbf{l}\mid=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{r+1}\lambda_{\alpha}=\nu+1
, with
\lambda_{\alpha}\geq 1,\;\alpha=1,\ldots,r+1
, and
0\leq\mu_{\alpha}\leq m
, which proves that the latter formula holds for all
(r,\mathbf{\lambda},\mathbf{\mu})\in\mathbb{K}_{i,\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}\subset\mathbb{J}_{i,\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}
. We leave as an exercise to the reader the proof of the converse inclusion
\mathbb{J}_{i,\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}\subset\mathbb{K}_{i,\nu+1}^{(\mathbf{j})}
. Therefore (
A.2
) is valid for all
\nu
. The proof of (
A.3
) follows exactly the same lines. The lemma is proven.
∎

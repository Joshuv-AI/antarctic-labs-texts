Feng Qi

Abstract
. In the research, with aid of the Faà di Bruno formula, be virtue of several identities for the Bell polynomials of the second kind, with help of two combinatorial identities, by means of the (logarithmically) complete monotonicity of generating functions of several integer sequences, and in light of the Wronski theorem, the author (1) establishes the Taylor power series expansions of several functions involving the inverse (hyperbolic) tangent function; (2) finds out the Maclaurin power series expansion of the Wilf function, which is a composite of the inverse tangent, square root, and exponential functions; (3) expresses the coefficients in the Maclaurin power series expansion of the Wilf function in terms of the Stirling numbers of the second kind; (4) analyzes some properties, including generating functions, limits, positivity, monotonicity, and logarithmic convexity, of the coefficients in the Maclaurin power series expansion of the Wilf function; (5) derives a closed-form formula for a sequence of special values of the Gauss hypergeometric function; (6) discovers a closed-form formula for a sequence of special values of the Bell polynomials of the second kind; (7) presents several infinite series representations of the circular constant and other sequences; (8) recovers an asymptotic rational approximation to the circular constant; (9) and connects several integer sequences by determinants.

1.
Motivations

Throughout this paper, we use the following notations:

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dotsc\},\quad\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dotsc\},\quad\mathbb{Z}=\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\dotsc\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}=(-\infty,\infty),\quad\mathbb{C}=\bigl\{x+y\ti:x,y\in\mathbb{R},\ti=\sqrt{-1}\,\bigr\}.

Let

W(z)=\frac{\arctan\sqrt{2\te^{-z}-1}\,}{\sqrt{2\te^{-z}-1}\,}.

(1.1)

In 2009, Herbert S. Wilf (1931–2012, University of Pennsylvania, USA) proposed the following problem:

If

W(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

(1.2)

find the first term of the asymptotic behaviour of the
a_{n}
’s.

In the conference paper
[
38
]
in 2010, Mark Daniel Ward (Purdue University, USA) considered Wilf’s problem and obtained the following conclusions:

(1)

The function
\sqrt{2\te^{-z}-1}\,
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{L}_{j}
, where

\mathcal{L}_{j}=\{z=x+2j\pi\ti:x\in\mathbb{R},x\geq\ln 2\}.

(2)

The Wilf function
W(z)
defined in (
1.1
) is analytic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{L}_{j}
. Moreover, an appropriate choice for the branch of the
\arctan
function allows
W(z)
to have an analytic continuation to
\bigcup_{j\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{L}_{j}
; however, such an analytic continuation will leave
W(z)
discontinuous on
\bigcup_{j\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1}\mathcal{L}_{j}
.

(3)

The coefficients
a_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the Maclaurin power series expansion (
1.2
) satisfy
a_{n}=\frac{h(n)}{R^{n}}
, where
R=|2\pi\ti+\ln 2|
and
h(n)
is a subexponential factor, i.e.,
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|h(n)|^{1/n}=1
.

(4)

For each positive integer
N
,

\displaystyle a_{n}

\displaystyle=-2\pi\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}d_{j}\frac{\cos\bigl[\bigl(n-j+\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\arctan\frac{2\pi}{\ln 2}\bigr]}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-j\bigr)R^{n-j+1/2}n^{j+1/2}}\Biggl[1+\sum_{k=1}^{N-j-1}\frac{e_{k}\bigl(\frac{1}{2}-j\bigr)}{n^{k}}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad+O\biggl(\frac{1}{R^{n}n^{N+1/2}}\biggr),

where

\displaystyle d_{j}=\bigl[z^{j-1/2}\bigr]\frac{1}{(\te^{z}-1)^{1/2}},\quad\lambda_{k,\ell}=\bigl[v^{k}t^{\ell}\bigr]\frac{\te^{t}}{(1+vt)^{1+1/v}},

\displaystyle e_{k}(\alpha)=\sum_{\ell=k}^{2k}(-1)^{\ell}\lambda_{k,\ell}\prod_{q=1}^{\ell}(\alpha-q),

and
\Gamma
denotes the classical Euler gamma function which can be defined
[
37
, Chapter 3]
by

\Gamma(z)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{n!n^{z}}{\prod_{k=0}^{n}(z+k)},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dotsc\}.

Tian-Xiao He (Illinois Wesleyan University, USA) reviewed the paper
[
38
]
in
MR2735366
as follows:

The coefficient
a_{n}
can be written as
a_{n}=b_{n}\pi-c_{n}
, where
b_{n}
and
c_{n}
are nonnegative rational numbers. In fact,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}=0
, and the rational numbers of the form
\frac{c_{n}}{b_{n}}
provide approximations to
\pi
. A complete expansion of the coefficients
a_{n}
is found by the author. It is probably the best that can be done, given the oscillatory nature of the terms.

Herbert S. Wilf commented on the paper
[
38
]
on 13 December 2010 as follows:

Mark Ward has found a complete expansion of these coefficients. It’s not quite an asymptotic series in the usual sense, but it is probably the best that can be done, given the oscillatory nature of the terms.

In this research, we will establish a closed-form formula involving the Stirling numbers of the second kind

for the coefficients

in (

as the form

in terms of closed-form formulas for

and

.

To smoothly attain our aims above, we will prepare some knowledge, tools, and lemmas in Section

.

For establishing a closed-form formula of the coefficients

, we not only need to derive the Taylor or the Maclaurin power series expansions of the functions

\arctan z,\quad\sqrt{2\te^{-z}-1}\,,\quad\frac{\arctan\sqrt{z}\,}{\sqrt{z}\,},\quad\frac{\arctan z}{z},

(1.3)

but also need to discover a closed-form formula of the Bell polynomials of the second kind

\bell_{n,k}\Biggl(1,0,1,3,16,105,\dotsc,(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k+1}(-1)^{\ell-1}S(n-k+1,\ell)(2\ell-3)!!\Biggr)

(1.4)

for

, where

denotes the Stirling numbers of the second kind.
We will do these works in Section

In Section

of the third function in (

), we will present two forms for the Maclaurin power series expansion of the Wilf function

on the open disc

: the first form is expressed in terms of the Gauss hypergeometric function

{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(n+\frac{1}{2},n+1;n+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1.5)

the second one is represented in terms of a closed-form expression of the coefficients
a_{n}
. Consequently, we deduce a closed-form formula of the Gauss hypergeometric function in (
1.5
).

In Section
5
, in view of related results obtained in Sections
3
and

, in light of a relation between

, we will establish the Maclaurin power series expansions of four functions

\arctanh z,\quad\frac{\arctanh\sqrt{z}\,}{\sqrt{z}\,},\quad\frac{\arctanh z}{z},\quad\frac{\arctanh\sqrt{1-2\te^{-z}}\,}{\sqrt{1-2\te^{-z}}\,}.

In Section
6
, we will go back to the Maclaurin power series expansion

W(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(b_{n}\pi-c_{n})z^{n},\quad|z|<\ln 2

(1.6)

a_{n}

2.
Preliminaries and lemmas

In the theory of complex functions, the inverse tangent function
\Arctan z
is defined by

\Arctan z=\frac{1}{2\ti}\Ln\frac{1+\ti z}{1-\ti z}=\frac{1}{2\ti}\Ln\frac{\ti-z}{\ti+z}

on the punctured complex plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\pm\ti\}
and its principal branch
\arctan z
such that
\arctan 0=0
is taken as

\arctan z=\frac{\ti}{2}[\ln(1-\ti z)-\ln(1+\ti z)]

on the cut complex plane

\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{(-\ti\infty,-\ti],[\ti,\ti\infty)\},

where
\ln z=\ln|z|+\ti\arg z
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
stands for the principal branch of the logarithm
\Ln z
and
\arg z\in(-\pi,\pi]
represents the principal value of the argument
\Arg z
.
On the cut complex plane
\mathcal{A}
, the range of the principal branch
\arctan x
for real numbers
x\in\mathbb{R}
is determined as
\bigl(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\bigr)
.
The principal branch
\arctan z
has a unique zero
z=0
on
\mathcal{A}
.

The power function
f(z)=\sqrt{z}\,
is defined on the whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. In polar coordinates, we can write
z=r\te^{\ti\theta}
for
r\geq 0
and
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
. Then
f(z)=\sqrt{r}\,\te^{\ti(\theta+2k\pi)/2}
for
k=0,1
. On the cut complex plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)
, the principal branch of
f(z)
such that
f(1)=1
is
\sqrt{r}\,\te^{\ti\theta/2}
for
r\geq 0
and
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi)
. The range of the principal branch of
f(z)=\sqrt{z}\,
is the right half complex plane plus the origin point. This range is a subset of
\mathcal{A}
. Therefore, the principal branch of the third function in (
1.3
) is defined on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)
.

The function
2\te^{-z}-1
is analytic on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
and its range is
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{-1\}
.

It is obvious that the Wilf function
W(z)
can be regarded as the composite of three complex functions

h(z)=\frac{\arctan z}{z},\quad f(z)=\sqrt{z}\,,\quad g(z)=2\te^{-z}-1.

When considering the composite of three principal branches of these complex functions
h(z)
,
f(z)
, and
g(z)
, the Wilf function
W(z)
has no definition on points such that

2\te^{-z}-1=2\te^{-x}\cos y-1-(2\te^{-x}\sin y)\ti\in(-\infty,0),

which is equivalent to

2\te^{-x}\cos y-1<0\quad\text{and}\quad 2\te^{-x}\sin y=0.

These points constitute infinitely many parallel and horizontal rays

\{z=x+y\ti\in\mathbb{C}:x>\ln 2,y=2k\pi,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

and infinitely many parallel, horizontal, and straight lines

\{z=x+y\ti\in\mathbb{C}:x\in\mathbb{R},y=(2k+1)\pi,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. In a word, the Wilf function
W(z)
is defined on the region

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\setminus\{z=x+y\ti\in\mathbb{C}:x>\ln 2,y=2k\pi,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle\setminus\{z=x+y\ti\in\mathbb{C}:x\in\mathbb{R},y=(2k+1)\pi,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Consequently, on the open disc
|z|<\ln 2
, the Wilf function
W(z)
is analytic.

By choosing an alternative branch of
\arctan z
, Ward proved in
[
38
, Lemma 2.2]
that the maximum radius around the origin for which the Wilf function
W(z)
has an analytic extension is

|\ln 2+2\pi\ti|=\sqrt{(\ln 2)^{2}+4\pi^{2}}\,>\ln 2.

In what follows, we will proceed by assuming
|z|<\ln 2
, if any.

For a complex constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, the falling and rising factorials of
\alpha
are defined
[
19
, p. 618, 26.1.1]
respectively by

\langle\alpha\rangle_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(\alpha-k)=\begin{cases}\alpha(\alpha-1)\dotsm(\alpha-n+1),&n\in\mathbb{N};\\
1,&n=0\end{cases}

and

(\alpha)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(\alpha+k)=\begin{cases}\alpha(\alpha+1)\dotsm(\alpha+n-1),&n\in\mathbb{N};\\
1,&n=0.\end{cases}

The double factorial of negative odd integers
-(2k+1)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is defined by

[-(2k+1)]!!=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k-1)!!}=(-1)^{k}\frac{(2k)!!}{(2k)!}.

(2.1)

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\dotsc\}
, the Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)
is defined by the series

{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{n}(\beta)_{n}}{(\gamma)_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

(2.2)

If
\alpha
or
\beta
is zero or a negative integer, the series in (
2.2
) terminates after a finite number of terms, and the Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)
is a polynomial in
z
. When
a,b\not\in\{0,-1,-2,\dotsc\}
, the infinite series in (
2.2
) absolutely and uniformly converges on the open unit disc
|z|<1
. If
\Re(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)<0
, the series in (
2.2
) absolutely converges over the unit circle
|z|=1
. If
0\leq\Re(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)<1
, the series in (
2.2
) conditionally converges at all points of the unit circle
|z|=1
except
z=1
. If
\Re(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)\geq 1
, the series in (
2.2
) diverges over the circle
|z|=1
. There exists an analytic continuation of the Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)
to the region
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|>1\}\setminus(1,\infty)
. The Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)
is a univalent analytic function on
\mathbb{C}\setminus(1,\infty)
.
For details and more information, please refer to
[
15
, pp. 64–66, Section 3.7]
and
[
37
, Chapter 5]
.

The Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
can be analytically generated
[
6
, p. 51]
by

\frac{(\te^{x}-1)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S(n,k)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

(2.3)

and can be explicitly computed
[
6
, p. 204, Theorem A]
by

S(n,k)=\begin{cases}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\ell^{n},&n>k\in\mathbb{N}_{0};\\
1,&n=k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{cases}

(2.4)

By the way, we recommend the papers
[
21
,
27
]
for several new results of
S(n,k)
.

The Bell polynomials of the second kind
\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})
for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
can be defined
[
6
, p. 134, Theorem A]
by

\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n-k+1\\
\ell_{i}\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}i\ell_{i}=n\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\ell_{i}!}\prod_{i=1}^{n-k+1}\biggl(\frac{x_{i}}{i!}\biggr)^{\ell_{i}}.

The Faà di Bruno formula can be described
[
6
, p. 139, Theorem C]
in terms of the Bell polynomials of the second kind
\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})
by

\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td t^{n}}f\circ h(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}f^{(k)}(h(t))\bell_{n,k}\bigl(h^{\prime}(t),h^{\prime\prime}(t),\dotsc,h^{(n-k+1)}(t)\bigr),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(2.5)

where
f
and
h
are
(n+1)
-time differentiable functions and
f\circ h
denotes the composite of
f
and
h
.

The Bell polynomials of the second kind
\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})
satisfy the following identities and formulas:

\displaystyle\bell_{n,k}\bigl(\alpha\beta x_{1},\alpha\beta^{2}x_{2},\dotsc,\alpha\beta^{n-k+1}x_{n-k+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{k}\beta^{n}\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1}),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bell_{n,k}(1,1,\dotsc,1)

\displaystyle=S(n,k),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\bell_{n,k}(\alpha,1,0,\dotsc,0)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-k)!}{2^{n-k}}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{n-k}\alpha^{2k-n},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\Biggl(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}x_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\Biggr)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bell_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

(2.9)

and

\bell_{n,k}\biggl(\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{1},\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{2},\dotsc,\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n-k+1}\biggr)\\
=(-1)^{n+k}\frac{(2n-2k-1)!!}{2^{n}}\binom{2n-k-1}{k-1}

(2.10)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
; see
[
5
, p. 412]
,
[
6
, pp. 133 and 135]
,
[
25
, Theorem 4.1]
, and
[
28
, p. 169, (3.6)]
, respectively. These identities and formulas can also be found in the review and survey article

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\ell,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the combinatorial identity

\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}\binom{2n-k-1}{n-1}2^{k}k=\binom{2n-\ell}{n}2^{\ell}n

(2.11)

is valid, where an empty sum is understood to be
0
.

Proof.

We now prove the identity (
2.11
) by the descending induction mentioned in
[
12
, p. 88]
.

It is trivial that, when
n-\ell=0
, the identity (
2.11
) is valid.

Assume that the identity (
2.11
) is valid for some
n-\ell>0
. Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=\ell-1}^{n}\binom{2n-k-1}{n-1}2^{k}k

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}\binom{2n-k-1}{n-1}2^{k}k+\binom{2n-\ell}{n-1}2^{\ell-1}(\ell-1)

\displaystyle=\binom{2n-\ell}{n}2^{\ell}n+\binom{2n-\ell}{n-1}2^{\ell-1}(\ell-1)

\displaystyle=\binom{2n-\ell+1}{n}2^{\ell-1}n.

Inductively, we conclude that the identity (
2.11
) is valid for all
n\geq\ell
. The proof of Lemma
2.1
is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

P(n,k)=k!(2n-2k-1)!!\binom{2n-k-1}{k-1},\quad n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(2.12)

Then

\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\binom{m/2}{\ell}=\begin{cases}0,&k>\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0};\\
\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{(2\ell)!!}P(\ell,k),&\ell\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{cases}

(2.13)

Proof.

In
[
33
, p. 165]
, the equation (12.1) reads that

n!\binom{z}{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}s(n,\ell)z^{\ell},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.14)

Making use of the relation
2.14
and utilizing the explicit formula (
2.4
), we find

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\binom{m/2}{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\biggl[\ell!\binom{m/2}{\ell}\biggr]

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}s(\ell,q)\biggl(\frac{m}{2}\biggr)^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell!}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}\frac{s(\ell,q)}{2^{q}}\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}m^{q}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\frac{k!}{\ell!}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell}s(\ell,q)\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{q}S(q,k)

for
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
0^{0}
was regarded as
1
. Hence, it is easy to see that

\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\binom{m/2}{\ell}=0,\quad 0\leq\ell<k.

In
[
28
, Theorem 2.1]
, the identity

\bell_{n,k}(\langle\alpha\rangle_{1},\langle\alpha\rangle_{2},\dotsc,\langle\alpha\rangle_{n-k+1})=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\langle\alpha\ell\rangle_{n}

(2.16)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
was proved. Taking
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in (
2.16
), applying the relation

\langle z\rangle_{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}s(n,\ell)z^{\ell},\quad z\in\mathbb{C},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

in
[
37
, p. 9, (1.26)]
, interchanging the order of sums, and employing (
2.4
) yield

\displaystyle\bell_{n,k}\biggl(\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{1},\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{2},\dotsc,\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n-k+1}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\biggl\langle\frac{\ell}{2}\biggr\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{m=0}^{n}s(n,m)\biggl(\frac{\ell}{2}\biggr)^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}s(n,m)\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{m}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\ell^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}s(n,m)\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{m}S(m,k)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Combining this with (
2.10
) results in

\sum_{m=k}^{n}s(n,m)\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{m}S(m,k)=(-1)^{n+k}\frac{(2n-2k-1)!!}{2^{n}}\binom{2n-k-1}{k-1}

(2.17)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Substituting the identity (
2.17
) into (
2.15
), we figure out

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\binom{m/2}{\ell}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell}k!\frac{(2\ell-2k-1)!!}{(2\ell)!!}\binom{2\ell-k-1}{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{(2\ell)!!}P(\ell,k)

for
\ell\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The proof of Lemma
2.2
is thus complete.
∎

that an infinitely differentiable function
f
is said to be completely (or absolutely, respectively) monotonic on an interval
I
if it has derivatives of all orders on
I
and satisfies
(-1)^{n}f^{(n)}(x)\geq 0
(or
f^{(n)}(x)\geq 0
, respectively) for all
x\in I
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Recall from
[
7
, Definition 1]
,
[
24
, Definition 1]
, and
[
35
, Definition 5.10 and Comments 5.29]
that an infinitely differentiable and positive function
f
is said to be logarithmically absolutely (or completely, respectively) monotonic on an interval
I
if
[\ln f(x)]^{(n)}\geq 0
(or
(-1)^{n}[\ln f(x)]^{(n)}\geq 0
, respectively) for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in I
.
In
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
7
, Theorem 1]
,
[
24
, Theorem 1]
, and
[
26
, p. 627, (1.4)]
, the following two relations between these two pairs of functions were discovered or reviewed:

(1)

A logarithmically completely monotonic function on an interval
I
is also completely monotonic on
I
, but not conversely.

(2)

A logarithmically absolutely monotonic function on an interval
I
is also absolutely monotonic on
I
, but not conversely.

It is easy to see that, a function
f(x)
is (logarithmically) completely monotonic on an interval
I
if and only if it is (logarithmically) absolutely monotonic on the interval
-I
respectively. These concepts and conclusions will be employed in Section
6
.

3.
Taylor power series expansions of inverse tangent function

In this section, by virtue of some preparations in Section
2
, we now present the Maclaurin and the Taylor power series expansions of the four functions in (
1.3
).

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
th derivative of
\arctan z
is

(\arctan z)^{(n)}=\frac{(n-1)!}{(2z)^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{k}{n-k-1}\frac{(2z)^{2k}}{(1+z^{2})^{k+1}}.

The function
\frac{\arctan z}{z}
can be expanded into the Taylor power series

\frac{\arctan z}{z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\biggl[\frac{\pi}{4}+T(n)\biggr](z-1)^{n},\quad|z-1|<\sqrt{2}\,,

(3.1)

where

T(n)=\begin{cases}0,&n=0;\\
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}\frac{\sin\frac{3k\pi}{4}}{2^{k/2}},&n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by virtue of the Faà di Bruno formula (
2.5
), we arrive at

\displaystyle(\arctan z)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{1}{1+z^{2}}\biggr)^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{(1+z^{2})^{k+1}}\bell_{n-1,k}(2z,2,0,\dotsc,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{(1+z^{2})^{k+1}}2^{k}\bell_{n-1,k}(z,1,0,\dotsc,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{(1+z^{2})^{k+1}}2^{k}\frac{(n-k-1)!}{2^{n-k-1}}\binom{n-1}{k}\binom{k}{n-k-1}z^{2k-n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-1)!}{(2z)^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}\binom{k}{n-k-1}\frac{(2z)^{2k}}{(1+z^{2})^{k+1}},

where we also utilized the identities (
2.6
) and (
2.8
). Accordingly, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\biggl(\frac{\arctan z}{z}\biggr)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(\arctan z)^{(k)}\biggl(\frac{1}{z}\biggr)^{(n-k)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!\arctan z}{z^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(k-1)!}{(2z)^{k-1}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k-\ell-1}\frac{(2z)^{2\ell}(-1)^{n-k}(n-k)!}{(1+z^{2})^{\ell+1}z^{n-k+1}}

\displaystyle\to(-1)^{n}n!\Biggl[\frac{\pi}{4}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2^{k}k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(-2)^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k-\ell-1}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!\biggl[\frac{\pi}{4}+T(n)\biggr]

as
z\to 1
, where we used the identity

\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-2)^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{k-\ell}=(-2)^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{\ell}\binom{k-\ell}{\ell}\\
=2^{k/2}\biggl(\cos\frac{3k\pi}{4}-\sin\frac{3k\pi}{4}\biggr)=2^{(k+1)/2}\sin\frac{3(k+1)\pi}{4}

(3.3)

for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which can be deduced from taking
z=-\frac{1}{2}
in the identity

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n-k}{k}z^{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+4z}\,}\biggl[\biggl(\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4z}\,}{2}\biggr)^{n+1}-\biggl(\frac{1-\sqrt{1+4z}\,}{2}\biggr)^{n+1}\biggr]

(3.4)

or

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n-k}{k}z^{k}=\frac{x^{n+1}-1}{(x-1)(1+x)^{n}},\quad z=-\frac{x}{(1+x)^{2}}

(3.5)

collected as (1.70) and (1.71) on
[
36
, p. 33]
, where
\lfloor{x}\rfloor
denotes the floor function whose value is equal to the largest integer less than or equal to
x
.
Consequently, we obtain the Taylor power series expansion (
3.1
).

The unremovable singular points of
\frac{\arctan z}{z}
closest to the point
z=1
are
\pm\ti
. Therefore, the convergent disc of the Taylor power series expansion (
3.1
) is
|z-1|<\sqrt{2}\,
.
The proof of Theorem
3.1
is complete.
∎

Remark 3.1

(3.6)

We can regard (

.

Theorem 3.2

The function

=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{(2n-1)!!\pi}{4}+\frac{n!}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{2n-k}{n}\frac{2^{k/2}}{k}\sin\frac{3k\pi}{4}\Biggr]\frac{(z-1)^{n}}{n!}

(3.8)

for

, where

is defined by (

Let

as

), in view of the identity (

), and by virtue of (

(3.9)

Theorem 3.3
.

The function

\sqrt{2\te^{-z}-1}\,=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}S(n,k)(2k-3)!!\Biggr]\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\quad|z|<\ln 2,

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{k}\bigl(2\te^{-z}-1\bigr)^{1/2-k}(-1)^{n}\bigl(2\te^{-z}\bigr)^{k}\bell_{n,k}(\underbrace{1,1,\dotsc,1}_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)(-1)^{k-1}(2k-3)!!\bigl(2\te^{-z}-1\bigr)^{1/2-k}\te^{-kz}

\displaystyle\to(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}S(n,k)(2k-3)!!

as
z\to 0
, where we employed the identities (
2.6
) and (

). The series expansion (
3.20
) is thus proved. The proof of Theorem
3.3
is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

For
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the Bell polynomials of the second kind
\bell_{n,k}
satisfy

\bell_{n,k}\Biggl(1,0,1,3,16,105,\dotsc,(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k+1}(-1)^{\ell-1}S(n-k+1,\ell)(2\ell-3)!!\Biggr)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=k}^{n}(-1)^{\ell}S(n,\ell)P(\ell,k),

(3.21)

where
P(\ell,k)
is given by (
2.12
) and the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,\ell)
are generated by (
2.3
).

Proof.

Making use of the formula (
2.9
) yields

\bell_{n+k,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dotsc,x_{n+1})=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{x_{m+1}}{(m+1)!}z^{m}\Biggr]^{k}.

(3.22)

Setting
x_{m}=\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(m)}\big|_{z=0}=\bigl(\sqrt{2\te^{-z}-1}\,\bigr)^{(m)}\big|_{z=0}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
in (
3.22
) leads to

\displaystyle\quad\bell_{n+k,k}\Bigl(\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{\prime}\big|_{z=0},\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{\prime\prime}\big|_{z=0},\dotsc,\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(n+1)}\big|_{z=0}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(m+1)}\big|_{z=0}}{(m+1)!}z^{m}\Biggr]^{k}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{z}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(m)}\big|_{z=0}}{m!}z^{m}\Biggr]^{k}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{\sqrt{g(z)}\,-1}{z}\Biggr]^{k}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{z^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}g^{\ell/2}(z)\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{z^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\bigl(2\te^{-z}-1\bigr)^{\ell/2}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl[\frac{1}{z^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\binom{\ell/2}{q}\bigl(2\te^{-z}-2\bigr)^{q}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\Biggl(\frac{1}{z^{k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}(2q)!!\binom{\ell/2}{q}\sum_{p=q}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}S(p,q)\frac{z^{p}}{p!}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td z^{n}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{p}(2q)!!\binom{\ell/2}{q}S(p,q)\Biggr]\frac{z^{p-k}}{p!}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{p}(2q)!!\binom{\ell/2}{q}S(p,q)\Biggr]\langle p-k\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\quad\times\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z^{p-k-n}}{p!}

\displaystyle=\binom{n+k}{k}(-1)^{k}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}\langle p-k\rangle_{n}\Biggl[\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\sum_{q=0}^{p}(2q)!!\binom{\ell/2}{q}S(p,q)\Biggr]

\displaystyle\quad\times\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{z^{p-k-n}}{p!},

where we used the generating function (
2.3
). Accordingly, we acquire

\bell_{n+k,k}\Bigl(\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{\prime}\big|_{z=0},\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{\prime\prime}\big|_{z=0},\dotsc,\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(n+1)}\big|_{z=0}\Bigr)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{n+k}(2q)!!\binom{\ell/2}{q}S(n+k,q)\Biggr]

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Replacing
n
by
n-k
reduces to

\bell_{n,k}\Bigl(\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{\prime}\big|_{z=0},\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{\prime\prime}\big|_{z=0},\dotsc,\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(n-k+1)}\big|_{z=0}\Bigr)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\Biggl[\sum_{q=0}^{n}S(n,q)(2q)!!\binom{\ell/2}{q}\Biggr].

The series expansion (
3.20
) in Theorem
3.3
implies that

\bigl[\sqrt{g(z)}\,\bigr]^{(n)}\big|_{z=0}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}S(n,k)(2k-3)!!,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consequently, it follows that

\bell_{n,k}\Biggl(-1,0,-1,-3,\dotsc,(-1)^{n-k+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k+1}(-1)^{\ell-1}S(n-k+1,\ell)(2\ell-3)!!\Biggr)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}S(n,\ell)(2\ell)!!\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\binom{m/2}{\ell}.

Employing the identity (
2.6
) results in the closed-form formula

\bell_{n,k}\Biggl(1,0,1,3,16,105,\dotsc,(-1)^{n-k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k+1}(-1)^{\ell-1}S(n-k+1,\ell)(2\ell-3)!!\Biggr)\\
=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}S(n,\ell)(2\ell)!!\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{m}\binom{k}{m}\binom{m/2}{\ell}

(3.23)

for
n\geq k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Further considering the formula (
2.13
) in Lemma
2.2
, we can conclude (
3.21
) from (
3.23
). The proof of Theorem
3.4
is complete.
∎

4.
Maclaurin power series expansions of Wilf function

In this section, by means of the Taylor power series expansions established in Section
3
for the last two functions in (
1.3
), with help of some preparations in Section
2
, we find two forms for the Maclaurin power series expansion of the Wilf function
W(z)
defined by (
1.1
) on the open disc centered at
z=0
with a radius
\ln 2
. We also obtain a closed-form formula of the Gauss hypergeometric function in (
1.5
).

Theorem 4.1
.

For
|z|<\ln 2
, the Wilf function
W(z)
has the Maclaurin power series expansion

W(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Biggl[\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}S(n,k)\frac{(2k)!!}{2k+1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(k+\frac{1}{2},k+1;k+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

(4.1)

where
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)
is the Gauss hypergeometric function defined by (
2.2
) and
S(n,k)
stands for the Stirling numbers of the second kind in (
2.3
).

Proof.

It is well known
[
1
, p. 81, 4.4.42]
that

\arctan z=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{2n+1},\quad|z|\leq 1,\quad z^{2}\neq-1.

From this, it follows that

\frac{\arctan\sqrt{t}\,}{\sqrt{t}\,}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{2n+1},\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

(4.2)

The series expansion (
4.2
) can be reformulated as

\displaystyle\frac{\arctan\sqrt{t}\,}{\sqrt{t}\,}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{[(t-1)+1]^{k}}{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}\sum_{n=0}^{k}\binom{k}{n}(t-1)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}\binom{k}{n}\Biggr](t-1)^{n}

for
0\leq t\leq 1
. This means that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\td t^{n}}\biggl(\frac{\arctan\sqrt{t}\,}{\sqrt{t}\,}\biggr)&=n!\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}\binom{k}{n}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{2n+1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(n+\frac{1}{2},n+1;n+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)\end{split}

(4.3)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Let

H(z)=\frac{\arctan\sqrt{z}\,}{\sqrt{z}\,},\quad\Re(z)>0.

Then the Wilf function
W(z)
can be regarded as the composite
H(g(z))
, where
w=g(z)=2\te^{-z}-1\to 1
as
z\to 0
. Accordingly, from (
4.3
), by the Faà di Bruno formula (
2.5
), we drive

\displaystyle W^{(n)}(z)

\displaystyle=[H(g(z))]^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{(k)}(w)\bell_{n,k}\bigl(g^{\prime}(z),g^{\prime\prime}(z),\dotsc,g^{(n-k+1)}(z)\bigr)

\displaystyle\to\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lim_{w\to 1}H^{(k)}(w)\bell_{n,k}\Bigl(\lim_{z\to 0}g^{\prime}(z),\lim_{z\to 0}g^{\prime\prime}(z),\dotsc,\lim_{z\to 0}g^{(n-k+1)}(z)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{2k+1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(k+\frac{1}{2},k+1;k+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)\bell_{n,k}\bigl(-2,2,\dotsc,(-1)^{n-k+1}2\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{2k+1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(k+\frac{1}{2},k+1;k+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)(-1)^{n}2^{k}\bell_{n,k}(1,1,\dotsc,1)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}S(n,k)\frac{(2k)!!}{2k+1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(k+\frac{1}{2},k+1;k+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)

as
z\to 0
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where we used the identities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).
Consequently, the series expansion of the Wilf function
W(z)
is

W(z)=\frac{\pi}{4}\\
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Biggl[\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}S(n,k)\frac{(2k)!!}{2k+1}{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(k+\frac{1}{2},k+1;k+\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)\Biggr]\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

(4.4)

In
[
37
, p. 109, Example 5.1]
, it is given that

{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\frac{1}{2},1;\frac{3}{2};-z^{2}\biggr)=\frac{\arctan z}{z},\quad|z|<1.

By virtue of Abel’s limit theorem
[
2
, p. 245, Theorem 9.31]
, we can take
z=1
and obtain

{}_{2}F_{1}\biggl(\frac{1}{2},1;\frac{3}{2};-1\biggr)=\frac{\pi}{4}.

Combining this with (
4.4
) brings out the series expansion (
4.1
). The proof of Theorem
4.1
is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.1

satisfies

(4.5)

Proof.

From the series expansion (
3.8
) in Theorem
3.2
, it follows that

\lim_{z\to 1}\biggl(\frac{\arctan\sqrt{z}\,}{\sqrt{z}\,}\biggr)^{(n)}\\

(4.6)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Comparing (
4.6
) with (
4.3
) and simplifying result in the explicit formula (
4.5
). The proof of Corollary

is complete.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In
[

\displaystyle\hskip-20.00003pt=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{\te^{x/2}}{2^{n-k}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\biggl\langle\frac{1}{2}\biggr\rangle_{\ell}\bigl(2-\te^{x}\bigr)^{1/2-\ell}\bell_{k,\ell}\bigl(-\te^{x},-\te^{x},\dotsc,-\te^{x}\bigr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{k}\te^{x/2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{(2\ell-3)!!}{2^{\ell}}\bigl(2-\te^{x}\bigr)^{1/2-\ell}\te^{\ell x}S(k,\ell)

\displaystyle\to-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}2^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{(2\ell-3)!!}{2^{\ell}}S(k,\ell)

as
x\to 0
, where we used the Faà di Bruno formula (
2.5
) and two identities (
2.6
) and (
2.7
).

Combining the series expansions (

) and (

) with the Wronski theorem in (

) leads to two relations (

) and (

) for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The proof of Theorem
6.6
is complete.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

The generating function in (
6.22
) can be alternatively expanded as

\displaystyle\sqrt{\te^{x}(2-\te^{x})}\,

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\te^{x/2}\sqrt{1-\frac{\te^{x}}{2}}\,

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{j}\biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{j}\te^{(j+1/2)x}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{j}\biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\biggl(j+\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\binom{1/2}{j}\biggl(-\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{j}\biggl(j+\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{n}\Biggr]\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Biggl[\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(j+1/2)^{n-1}}{j-1/2}\frac{2^{j+1/2}}{\binom{2j+1}{j+1/2}}\Biggr]\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Accordingly, we obtain an infinite series representation

e_{n}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(j+1/2)^{n-1}}{j-1/2}\frac{2^{j+1/2}}{\binom{2j+1}{j+1/2}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

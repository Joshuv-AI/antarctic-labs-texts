Abstract
. We show in many cases that there exist rational scrolls which are balanced, i.e. they contain the expected number of general linear spaces as rulings. For example, there exist balanced scrolls of degree m ​ k + 1 mk+1 and fibre dimension k k in ℙ 2 ​ k + 1 \mathbb{P}^{2k+1} for all m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 .

As is well known from classical projective geometry,
3 general lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
lie on a unique quadric surface. Extrapolating from there,
one can pose the following
rational scroll interpolation problem
.
Define a
rational
(k,1)
-scroll
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
to be the union of
a family of linear
\mathbb{P}^{k}
s parametrized by a rational curve.
Then given a general collection of
q
linear
\mathbb{P}^{k}
s in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, what is the smallest degree
of a rational
(k,1)
-scroll containing all these as rulings? There is an obvious
expected

answer to this question, namely the smallest such degree
e
is

e=\lceil\frac{(q-1)((k+1)(n-k)-1)+2}{n+1}\rceil.

This expectation is based on the fact that a rational
(k,1)
-scroll of degree
e

corresponds to a rational curve
C

of degree
e
in the Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{G}(k,n)
and if the scroll is to contain
q
general rulings then
the normal bundle
N=N_{C/\mathbb{G}}
must satisfy

(1)

\begin{split}\chi(N(-qP))\geq 0\end{split}

where
P\in C.

Then the question becomes whether the actual degree equals the expected degree.
The case where
k=0
, i.e.
\mathbb{G}(k,n)=\mathbb{P}^{n}
is treated in
[
5
]
,
while the analogous question for Fano hypesurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is treated in

[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
1
]
,

[
6
]
and
[
4
]
.

Now a rational curve
C
of degree
e
in
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
is said to be
balanced

if

\begin{split}&\chi(N(-qP))=h^{0}(N(-qP)),\\
&q:=[(e(n+1)-2)/((k+1)(n+1)-1)]=[\deg(N)/\text{rk}(N)],\end{split}

i.e. if the scroll corresponding to

C
contains the maximum (=expected) number of general
\mathbb{P}^{k}
s as rulings.
The purpose of this paper is to show that for many
k,n
,
\mathbb{G}(k,n)

contains infinitely many families of balanced rational curves. For example, when
k=1

or
n=2k+1
,
the set of degrees
e
of balanced rational curves contains some arithmetic progressions.
See Theorems
13
,
20
,
23
, Corollary
24

as well as Examples
2.2
,
2.3
,

2.4
,
17
and
18
for more precise statements.

The proof is based on a ’balanced plus perfect’ strategy. We use a ’deformation to the normal bundle’
type construction
to degenerate a Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}
to a reducible variety
\mathbb{F}_{1}\cup\mathbb{F}_{2}
where
\mathbb{F}_{1}

is the blowup of
\mathbb{G}
is a Schubert cycle
\Sigma
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is essentially
the projectivization
\mathbb{P}(N_{\Sigma/\mathbb{G}}\oplus\mathcal{O})
. Then we construct genus-0
nodal curves
C_{1}\cup C_{2}

with
C_{i}\subset\mathbb{F}_{i}
balanced,
i=1,2
and at least one of them perfect (having
normal bundle that is a twist of a trivial bundle). Such a curve deforms to a balanced rational curve in

\mathbb{G}
.

The Schubert cycles used here are rather special (e.g. sub-Grassmannians). Hopefully
more general Schubert cycles can lead to more balanced scrolls.

Conventions and preliminaries

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbf{k}
and use the EGA convention for projective
and Grassmannian spaces and bundles. Thus

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{G}(k,n)=G(k+1,n+1)

denotes the space
of
(k+1)
- dimensional quotients or codimension-
(k+1)
subspaces

V^{n-k}\subset\mathbf{k}^{n+1}
with its tautological rank-
(k+1)
quotient
bundle
Q
and tautological rank-
(n-k)
subbundle
S
.
By duality

\mathbb{G}=G(n-k,n+1)

so whenever convenient we may also consider
\mathbb{G}

as parametrizing

(k+1)
- dimensional or codimension-
(n-k)
subspaces

V^{k+1}\subset\mathbf{k}^{n+1}

Thus
\mathbb{G}
carries two
sub- and two quotient bundles, to be denoted
S^{n-k},S^{k+1}
,

Q^{k+1},Q^{n-k}
if needed to avoid ambiguity.
These are related by
(S^{n-k})^{*}=Q^{n-k},(S^{k+1})^{*}=Q^{k+1}
.

0.1.
Balanced bundles

A bundle
A
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
balanced
if
H^{1}(A\otimes A^{*})=0
; if moreover

\text{rk}(A)|\deg(A)
, so
A
is a twist of a trivial bundle,
A
is

perfectly balanced
. The tensor product of a balanced bundle and a perfectly
balanced one is balanced, and slopes add.
A rational curve
C\to X
is (perfectly)
balanced if its normal bundle is.
The slope of
C
is by definition that of its normal bundle.
If
X
is a Grassmannian
C

is said to be quot- or sub- balanced if the appropriate tautological bundle
is balanced. This just means that the respective (mutually dual) projective scrolls
corresponding to
C
are balanced, i.e. projectivizations of balanced bundles.

0.2.
Balanced extensions

Given an exact sequence of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}

0\to E_{1}\to E\to E_{2}\to 0

with respective slopes
s_{1},s,s_{2}
such that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

are balanced and the matching condition

[s_{1}]=[s_{2}]
holds, then
E
is balanced and
[s]=[s_{1}]

(see
[
4
]
, Lemma 25).

If
X
is fibred over
B
, the normal bundle
N_{C/X}
fits in an exact sequence

0\to N_{v}\to N_{C/X}\to N_{C/B}\to 0

where
N_{v}=T_{X/B}|_{C}
and
N_{h}=N_{C/B}
are the vertical and horizontal
normal bundles . The
respective slopes of
N_{v}
and
N_{h}
are called the vertical and horizontal slopes
of
C
and denoted
s_{v}
and
s_{h}
.
If
N_{v}
and
N_{h}
are balanced and their
respective slopes satisfy
[s_{v}]=[s_{h}]
, then
N_{X/X}
is balanced.

0.3.
Balanced kernels

We will also use the following result
which is essentially in
[
4
]
, Lemma 26 (with trivial enahncements):

Lemma 1
.

Let
E
be a rank-
r
bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of the form

(2)

\begin{split}E=r_{0}\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus r_{1}\mathcal{O}(a-1)\oplus...\oplus r_{b}\mathcal{O}(a-b),\ \ r_{0}>0,b\geq 0,r_{1},...,r_{b}\geq 0\end{split}

Then for
p_{1},...,p_{s}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
distinct,
the kernel
E_{s}
of a sufficiently general map to a skyscraper sheaf

E\to\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{s}\mathbf{k}_{p_{i}}
is balanced (resp. perfectly balanced) if

s\geq\sum r_{i}(b-i)

(resp.
s=\sum r_{i}(b-i)
).
In particular, if
E
is balanced with upper rank
r^{+}

then the kernel of a sufficiently general map
E\to\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{s}\mathbf{k}_{p_{i}}

is balanced for any
s
and perfectly balanced if
s
is a multiple of
r^{+}
.

Let
r_{\min}(E),\mu_{\min}(E)
denote respectively the rank and slope of the maximal rank, minimal
slope quotient of
E
(i.e. in the above notation,
r_{b},a-b
).
Then one way to prove the Lemma is to
define the ’unbalanced degree’ of
E
as above as

u(E)=\sum r_{i}(b-i)=\deg(E)-\text{rk}(E)\mu_{\min}(E)

Note
u(E)\geq\text{rk}(E)-r_{\min}(E)
with equality iff
E
is balanced.
Then, taking
s=1
, one can easily prove that, unless
E
is perfectly balanced,
we have
u(E_{1})=u(E)-1
. Since
u(E)\geq 0
, repeating
the modification
E\mapsto E_{1}
eventually leads to a balanced and then perfectly balanced
bundle.

Corollary 2
.

Notations as above, set
\mu_{\min}(E)=a-b
, i.e. the smallest slope of a quotient of
E
.
Then a sufficiently general map
E\to\mathcal{O}(\ell)
has balanced kernel provided

\ell\geq u(E)+\mu_{\min}(E)=\deg(E)-(\text{rk}(E)-1)\mu_{\min}(E).

Proof.

Pick a summand
\mathcal{O}(a)\subset E
and a general map
\mathcal{O}(a)\to\mathcal{O}(\ell)
. Then
applying Lemma
1
to a general map
E/\mathcal{O}(a)\to\mathcal{O}(\ell)/O(a)

yields that the kernel is balanced provided

\ell-a\geq u(E/\mathcal{O}(a))=u(E)-b,

i.e.
\ell\geq u(E)+a-b=u(E)+\mu_{\min}(E)
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
E
be a bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\tau
a torsion sheaf of
the form
\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{k}_{p_{i}}
with
p_{i}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
distinct. Then the kernel

K
of
a general map
E\to\tau
is either balanced or
u(K)=u(E)-m
.

Proof.

An easy induction on the rank of
E
.
∎

Corollary 4
.

Let
E
be a balanced bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
a\leq b\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the kernel
K
of a general surjection
E\oplus\mathcal{O}(a)\to\mathcal{O}(b)
is
either balanced or has
u(K)=u(E)-(b-a)
.

Proof.

Apply the Lemma to the induced map
E\to\coker(\mathcal{O}(a)\to\mathcal{O}(b))
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
E
be a globally generated balanced bundle of slope
s

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then the kernel of a general surjection
E^{*}\oplus a\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}(b)

is balanced provided
b\geq(a-1)s
.

Proof.

Similar to the above, by induction on
a
.
∎

Rational curves in Grassmannians

A curve

corresponds to a surjection on

,
\phi:(n+1)\mathcal{O}\to E
where
E
is a rank-
(k+1)
bundle, i.e.

corresponds
to an
(n+1)

generating

where

0.5.
Schubert cycles, flag resolutions

Here we think of
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
as parametrizing
(k+1)
-dimensional vector
subspaces of
\mathbf{k}^{n+1}
.
Fix a flag
\mathbb{P}^{0}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset...\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
the corresponding vector space flag
V^{1}\subset...\subset V^{n+1}=V
.
Let
(a.)
be a partition
of the form

(3)

\begin{split}n-k\geq a_{0}\geq...\geq a_{k}\geq 0,a_{k+1}:=-1.\end{split}

The associated Schubert cycle of codimension
A=\sum a_{i}
in
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
is defined as

(4)

\begin{split}\Sigma(a.)=\{L:\dim(L\cap\mathbb{P}^{n-k-a_{i}+i})\geq i,i=0,...,k\}.\end{split}

(This is actually contained in
\mathbb{G}(k,n-a_{k})
.)
Schubert cycles are often singular where one of the inequalities in (
4
)
are strict, and can be resolved by considering suitable flags.

We define the
strict flag resolution

\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)\to\Sigma(a.)

as follows.
First define integers
k_{0}<...<k_{u}=k
inductively as follows.

k_{0}=\min(i:a_{i+1}<a_{i}),k_{j+1}=\min(i>k_{j}:a_{i+1}<a_{i}).

These are the ’break points’ of the partition
(a.)
. Thus if the partition
(a.)

is without repetitions then
k_{i}=i,i=0,...,k
. Note that
\Sigma(a.)
depends only
on
a_{k_{0}}>...>a_{k_{u}}
:

(5)

\begin{split}\Sigma(a.)=\{L:\dim(L\cap\mathbb{P}^{n-k-a_{k_{i}}+k_{i}})\geq k_{i},i=0,...,u\}\end{split}

and
A=\sum(k_{i}-k_{i-1})a_{k_{i}}
.
Set

\Sigma_{0}=\mathbb{G}(k_{0},n-k+k_{0}-a_{k_{0}})=G(k_{0}+1,n-k+k_{0}-a_{k_{0}}+1).

This is endowed with a tautological subbundle
S^{0}_{k_{0}+1}
and quotient bundle

Q^{0}_{n-k-a_{0}}
. Note that
\Sigma_{0}
is a point iff
a_{k_{0}}=n-k
.

Then let

F_{1}=V^{n-k+1+k_{1}-a_{k_{1}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}/S^{0}_{k_{0}+1}=Q^{0}_{n-k-a_{0}}\oplus(V^{n-k+1+k_{1}-a_{k_{1}}}/V^{n-k+1+k_{0}-a_{k_{0}}})\otimes\mathcal{O},

\Sigma_{1}=G(k_{1}-k_{0},F_{1}).

This is endowed with tautological bundle
S^{1}_{k_{1}-k_{0}},Q^{1}_{n-k-a_{k_{1}}}

as well as a larger ’cumulative’ tautological subbundle

T^{1}_{k_{1}+1}\subset V^{n-k+1+k_{1}-a_{k_{1}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}
which is filtered with graded

S^{0}\oplus S^{1}
.

In general if
F_{i},\Sigma_{i}
are defined, and on
\Sigma_{i}

we have the quotient bundle
Q^{i}_{n-k-a_{k_{i}}}
and subbundle
T^{i}_{k_{i}+1}
, then let

F_{i+1}=V^{n-k+1+k_{i+1}-a_{k_{i+1}}}\otimes\mathcal{O}/T^{i}=Q^{i}_{n-k-a_{k_{i}}}\oplus(V^{n-k+1+k_{i+1}-a_{k_{i+1}}}/V^{n-k+1-k_{i}+a_{k_{i}}})\otimes\mathcal{O},

\Sigma_{i+1}=G(k_{i+1}-k_{i},F_{i+1}),

and
S^{i+1}_{k_{i+1}-k_{i}},Q^{i+1}_{n-k-a_{k_{i+1}}},T^{i+1}_{k_{i+1}}
be as before.
Finally

\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)=\Sigma_{u}.

Then
\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)
parametrizes partial flags
(L_{k_{0}}\subset...\subset L_{k_{u}}=L_{k})

of the indicated dimensions such that
L_{k_{i}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n-k-a_{k_{i}}+k_{i}},i=0,...,u
.

Obviously
\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)
is smooth, as are each of the morphisms

\Sigma_{i+1}\to\Sigma_{i}
.
Note the surjection
(n+1)\mathcal{O}\to Q^{u}_{n-k}
with kernel
T^{u}_{k+1}
whose graded is

\mathrm{gr}_{.}T^{u}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{u}S^{i}
. This yields a map

\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)\to\mathbb{G}(k,n)
which is birational onto
\Sigma(a.)\subset\mathbb{G}(k,n-a_{k})
.

Lemma 6
.

\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)\to\Sigma(a.)
is a small resolution.

Proof.

Because
a_{k_{i}}-a_{k_{i+1}}>0
, the exceptional locus of the natural map

\Sigma_{i}\to\Sigma_{*}(a.)
, i.e. the degeneracy locus of the map

S_{k_{i+1}-k_{i}}\to(V^{n-k+1+k_{i+1}-a_{k_{i+1}}}/V^{n-k+1-k_{i}+a_{k_{i}}})\otimes\mathcal{O}
,
has codimension
a_{k_{i+1}}-a_{k_{i}}+1>1
. And because
\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)\to\Sigma_{i}

is flat, the same is true for the inverse image of the latter exceptional locus is

\tilde{\Sigma}(a.)
.
∎

The latter is the reason for working with
partial flags as above rather than the more standard full flags.

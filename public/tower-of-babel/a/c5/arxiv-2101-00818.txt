Iterated numerical homogenization for multi-scale elliptic equations with monotone nonlinearity
Xinliang Liu, Eric Chung, and Lei Zhang

Abstract
Nonlinear multi-scale problems are ubiquitous in materials science and biology. Complicated interactions between nonlinearities and (nonseparable) multiple scales pose a major challenge for analysis and simulation. In this paper, we study the numerical homogenization for multi-scale elliptic PDEs with monotone nonlinearity, in particular the Leray-Lions problem (a prototypical example is the p-Laplacian equation), where the nonlinearity cannot be parameterized with low dimensional parameters, and the linearization error is non-negligible. We develop the iterated numerical homogenization scheme by combining numerical homogenization methods for linear equations, and the so-called ”quasi-norm” based iterative approach for monotone nonlinear equation. We propose a residual regularized nonlinear iterative method, and in addition, develop the sparse updating method for the efficient update of coarse spaces. A number of numerical results are presented to complement the analysis and valid the numerical method. Keywords: numerical homogenization, multi-scale elliptic problem, monotone nonlinearity, p p -Laplacian, regularization, sparse updating.

Introduction

Problems with a wide range of coupled temporal and spatial scales are ubiquitous in many phenomena and processes of materials science and biology. Multi-scale modeling and simulation is essential in underpinning the discovery and synthesis of new materials and chemicals with novel functionalities in key areas such as energy, information technology and bio-medicine. In particular, for nonlinear multi-scale problems such as liver surgery, tissue growth, crack propagation, phase transformations, etc.

In this paper, we discuss the design, analysis and implementation of numerical homogenization type multi-scale methods for multi-scale elliptic PDEs with monotone nonlinearity. Let

u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

where

The extensions of these methods to nonlinear problems use nonlinear local problems. For example, for the Leray-Lions flux

For linear problems, one can construct one linear basis function per coarse node that contains the effects of small scales, in contrast, for nonlinear problems, one may need more multi-scale basis functions for each coarse cell. In

In this paper, we take an alternative view for the numerical homogenization of nonlinear elliptic problems. Our view is based on the well-established fact that linear multi-scale problem can be well approximated by the coarse space up to the coarse resolution, even with nonseparable scales. Therefore, our goal is to find a sequence of approximate linear problems and the corresponding coarse spaces, which we call

27

Although the resulting numerical scheme is implicit, we can interpret the preconditioned gradient descent method and Newton’s method as explicit relaxations of the quasi-norm approach, and show similar exponential energy convergence up to some reasonable assumptions. Although we do not need multiple basis functions per coarse node, we update the coarse space at each iteration. Therefore, we propose a sparse updating strategy to compute only a fraction of basis functions per iteration.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

Preliminaries

We briefly describe some preliminary knowledge needed in the paper. We first introduce the weak formulation and N-functions for the monotone nonlinear elliptic problem. We then discuss the regularization of the possible degeneracy/singularity, which can be used to guarantee the well-posedness of linearized problems. In the end, we formulate the finite element method for the nonlinear problem, and also the numerical homogenization for linear problems.

2.1

2.4

3
Numerical Methods

We formulate the numerical methods in this section. For a nonlinear problem, we usually need to approximate the solution iteratively. We will first introduce an ‘ideal’ iterative method with exponential decay of energy error and
convergence rate independent of the heterogeneity, which is based on the quasi-norm formulation. Next, we connect the widely used preconditioned gradient descent (PGD) and Newton’s method with the quasi-norm based approach, and show the exponential energy decay with some reasonable assumptions. As a consequence, we will formulate the iterated numerical homogenization method, and its
L^{2}
residual regularization for the multiscale nonlinear equations.

3.1
Iterative Methods

3.1.1
Quasi-norm based Implicit Method

We formulate an ‘ideal’ iterative method based on the quasi-norm introduced in Lemma
4
. Suppose that
u^{(n)}\in W^{1,\varphi}
is the n-th iterative approximation to
u
, and
w^{(n)}
is an increment such that
u^{(n+1)}=u^{(n)}+w^{(n)}
, which will be specified later.

By Lemma
4
, for any
u^{(n)}
and
w^{(n)}
, we have

\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)}+w^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})\leqslant\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})(w^{(n)})+C_{\mathcal{J}}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}|^{2}.

(27)

We can define the increment
w^{(n)}
by

C_{q}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}|\right)\nabla w^{(n)}\cdot\nabla v=-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})(v),\,\forall v\in W^{1,\varphi},

(28)

where
C_{q}=C_{\mathcal{J}}+1
, such that the following lemma holds.

Lemma 10
.

Under Assumption
1
, also assume that
u^{(n)}\in W_{0}^{1,\varphi}
, there exists a unique
w^{(n)}\in W_{0}^{1,\varphi}
satisfying (
28
). Furthermore, there exists a constant
C_{1}>1
depending on
\varphi
but independent of
\kappa

\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u)\leqslant C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}|^{2},

(29)

and

\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)}+w^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})\leqslant-\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}|^{2}.

(30)

See
A.5
for proof.

Let the energy error be defined as

e^{(n)}:=\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u),

and we have the following theorem for its exponential decay.

Theorem 11
.

For the direction
w^{(n)}
defined in (

), let

Proof.

By lemma

which leads to the desired result.

3.1.2
Explicit Iterative Methods

The quasi-norm based iterative method has the exponential convergence for energy error, and the convergence rate is independent of the oscillatory coefficients
\kappa

. However, at each iteration, the nonlinear equation (

. Usually, we have to resort to some explicit iterative methods for

in practice. This can be done, for example, by using a linearized/approximate operator

, and update

To be more precise, we define the first variation of

Let

be the linear operator corresponding to the preconditioned steepest descent method.
For p-Laplacian it writes

(35)

Also, let

\displaystyle A_{\mathrm{N}}[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{\mathrm{N}},v):=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(|\nabla u^{(n)}|)}{|\nabla u^{(n)}|}\nabla w^{(n)}_{\mathrm{N}}\cdot\nabla v

(36)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\frac{\varphi^{\prime\prime}(|\nabla u^{(n)}|)|\nabla u^{(n)}|-\varphi^{\prime}(|\nabla u^{(n)}|)}{|\nabla u^{(n)}|^{3}}(\nabla u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla w^{(n)}_{\mathrm{N}})(\nabla u^{(n)}\cdot\nabla v),

be the linear operator corresponding to the Newton’s method,

For
A[u^{(n)}]=A_{\mathrm{G}}[u^{(n)}]
or
A[u^{(n)}]=A_{\mathrm{N}}[u^{(n)}]

is then defined analogous to (

C_{n}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)},v)=-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})(v),\text{ for }\forall v\in\mathcal{V}_{h}.

is a scaling factor analogous to

(38)

Remark 13

For the well-posedness of (

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)}+w^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})\leqslant-\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}|^{2}.

3.2
Iterated Numerical Homogenization

In Algorithm
1
, each iteration requires solving elliptic type equation (
37
) which combines the highly oscillatory coefficient
\kappa(x)
and the nonlinearity from
\varphi
. Besides, the gradient
|\nabla u^{(n)}|
may possibly approach zero or grow large during the iteration. Therefore, a proper multi-scale solver can be employed to reduce the computational cost and maintain the coarse mesh accuracy. We are going to iteratively use the numerical homogenization method introduced in Section

to solve (

Let the norm associated with
A[u^{(n)}]
be
\|\cdot\|_{A[u^{(n)}]}
, the corresponding coarse space basis
\phi^{(n)}_{i}
can be defined by

\phi^{(n)}_{i}=\argmin\limits_{\phi\in\mathcal{V}_{i}}\|\phi\|_{A[u^{(n)}]}^{2},

(43)

and the coarse space
\mathcal{V}_{H}^{(n)}:=\mathrm{span}\{\phi^{(n)}_{i}\}
.

We can find the search direction
w^{(n)}_{H}\in\mathcal{V}_{H}^{(n)}
by solving (
37
) in
\mathcal{V}_{H}^{(n)}
, with
A[u^{(n)}]
given by (

) or (
36

C^{\prime}_{n}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{H},v)=-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})(v),\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{V}_{H}^{(n)}.

(44)

Similar to Assumption
38
, we make the following assumption,

Assumption 17
.

There exists
C^{\prime}_{n}>0
such that,

C^{\prime}_{n}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{H},w^{(n)}_{H})\geqslant(C_{\mathcal{J}}+1)\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}_{H}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}_{H}|^{2}.

(45)

For simplicity, we take

in both (
38
) and (
45
). Please see Figure
9
for numerical results and discussions related to Assumptions

and

Lemma 18
.

Suppose that

. There exists positive constants

depending on the shape regularity of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, such that,

\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L_{h}^{2}}\leqslant\frac{C_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}{h}\|a(x,\nabla u^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}},

(46)

where
\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L_{h}^{2}}
is defined as

\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L_{h}^{2}}:=\displaystyle\min_{v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\frac{<\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)}),v_{h}>}{\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}}

Proof.

The proof is based on the scaling argument that for any
v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
,
\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}/h\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}
, where the constant
C_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
depends only on the mesh regularity. Therefore, we have

\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L_{h}^{2}}\leqslant C_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}/h\|a(x,\nabla u^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}
.

Corollary 19
.

For the finite element solution
w^{(n)}_{H}\in\mathcal{V}_{H}^{(n)}
to (
37
), where
\mathcal{V}_{H}^{(n)}
is the space of ”good” basis (global basis or local basis with
\ell\simeq\log H
such that (
25
) holds). We have

\|w^{(n)}_{h}-w^{(n)}_{H}\|_{A[u^{(n)}]}\leqslant\frac{1}{C_{n}\lambda_{\min}(A[u^{(n)}])^{1/2}}H\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L_{h}^{2}}.

(47)

where
\lambda_{\min}(A[u^{(n)}]):=\inf_{x\in\Omega}\inf_{\|v\|=1}v^{T}a[u^{(n)}](x)v
, and
a[u^{(n)}](x)
denotes the elliptic coefficient in the operator
A[u^{(n)}]
.

Theorem 20
.

Under the conditions in Lemma
14
, and furthermore we assume that
1\leqslant C_{n}\leqslant M_{C}
,
\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}_{h}}^{2}\leqslant M_{L}
, and
\lambda_{\min}(A[u^{(n)}])\geqslant M_{A}>0
uniformly for
n\geqslant 0
. For the direction
w^{(n)}_{H}
defined in (
44

u^{(n+1)}:=u^{(n)}+w^{(n)}_{H}
, there exists a constant
C_{H}>0
such that

(e^{(n+1)}-C_{H}H^{2})\leqslant(1-\frac{\theta}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1})(e^{(n)}-C_{H}H^{2}),

(48)

where
C_{H}
depends on
m
,
\Delta_{2}(\varphi,\varphi^{*})
,
M_{C}
,
M_{L}
and
M_{A}
.

\theta
is the constant in Theorem
15
.

Proof.

By Assumption
45
, we have

C_{n}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{H},w^{(n)}_{H})\geqslant(C_{\mathcal{J}}+1)\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}_{H}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}_{H}|^{2},

(49)

Combining Lemma
4
, (
44
), (
47
), and (

), we derive the following inequalities,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)}+w^{(n)}_{H})

\displaystyle\geqslant C_{n}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{H},w^{(n)}_{H})-C_{\mathcal{J}}\int_{\Omega}\kappa(x)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\left(|\nabla u^{(n)}|+|\nabla w^{(n)}_{H}|\right)|\nabla w^{(n)}_{H}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{C_{n}}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{H},w^{(n)}_{H})

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{n}}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{h},w^{(n)}_{h})+\frac{C_{n}}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{h}-w^{(n)}_{H},w^{(n)}_{h}-w^{(n)}_{H})

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{C_{n}}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}A[u^{(n)}](w^{(n)}_{h},w^{(n)}_{h})-\frac{1}{(C_{\mathcal{J}}+1)C_{n}\lambda_{\min}(A[u^{(n)}])}\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(u^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}_{h}}^{2}H^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}(\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)})-\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)}+w^{(n)}_{h}))-\frac{M_{L}}{(C_{\mathcal{J}}+1)M_{A}}H^{2}.

Let
e^{(n+1)}_{H}:=\mathcal{J}(u^{(n)}+w^{(n)}_{H})-\mathcal{J}(u)
, and

e^{(n)}_{H}-e^{(n+1)}_{H}\geqslant\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}(e^{(n)}_{H}-\tilde{e}^{(n+1)})-\frac{M_{L}}{(C_{\mathcal{J}}+1)M_{A}}H^{2}\geqslant\frac{\theta}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1}e^{(n)}_{H}-\frac{M_{L}}{(C_{\mathcal{J}}+1)M_{A}}H^{2}.

It follows by rearranging terms that,

(1-\frac{\theta}{C_{\mathcal{J}}+1})(e^{(n)}_{H}-\frac{M_{L}}{\theta M_{A}}H^{2})\geqslant(e^{(n+1)}_{H}-\frac{M_{L}}{\theta M_{A}}H^{2}),

namely, the energy error decays exponentially up to order

, which concludes the theorem.

3.3

As we mentioned in previous sections, the term

5
Conclusion

In this paper we propose the iterated numerical homogenization method for multi-scale elliptic equations with monotone nonlinearity. We combine the iterative methods inspired by the quasi-norm based approach and the numerical homogenization for each linearized operator in the nonlinear iteration. The residual regularized line search is proposed to ensure the convergence of the iterated numerical homogenization up to coarse resolution. We offer a number of representative numerical examples to illustrate and validate the proposed method.

We plan to study more complicated nonlinear multiscale problems, for example, problems with nonconvex flux, and nonlinear heterogeneous elasticity. We also plan to study the use of nonlocal upscaling techniques
[
21
,
19
]
.

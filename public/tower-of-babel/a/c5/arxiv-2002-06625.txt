The super Mumford form and Sato Grassmannian
Katherine A. Maxwell

Abstract
We describe a supersymmetric generalization of the construction of Kontsevich and Arbarello, De Concini, Kac, and Procesi, which utilizes a relation between the moduli space of curves with the infinite-dimensional Sato Grassmannian. Our main result is the existence of a flat holomorphic connection on the line bundle λ 3 / 2 ⊗ λ 1 / 2 − 5 \lambda_{3/2}\otimes\lambda_{1/2}^{-5} on the moduli space of triples: a super Riemann surface, a Neveu-Schwarz puncture, and a formal coordinate system. We also prove a superconformal Noether normalization lemma for families of super Riemann surfaces.

Introduction

The constant
c_{j}=6j^{2}-6j+1
, proportional to the 2
nd
Bernoulli polynomial, arises independently in studying representations of the Virasoro algebra and by studying the geometry of the moduli space of curves. Manin
[
Man86
]
conjectured based on this numerical coincidence that there exists a direct connection between these seemingly independent mathematical areas. This conjecture was shown to be true in the simultaneous publications
[
ADKP88
,
BS88
,
KNTY88
,
Kon87
]
, which was important to the quantum theory of (bosonic) strings, as it unified path integral quantization and operator quantization.

The numerical coincidence goes as follows. The Witt algebra has natural representations
\varrho_{j}
defined by Lie derivative action on
\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)dz^{\otimes j}
. The Japanese cocycle
\eta
on
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!))
by restriction to the Witt algebra induces the unique central extension of the Witt algebra, known as the Virasoro algebra. Then we find

\displaystyle\varrho_{j}^{*}(\eta)=c_{j}\varrho_{1}^{*}(\eta),

\displaystyle c_{j}=6j^{2}-6j+1.

When the Virasoro algebra is realized as operators on the state space of a string, the string must propagate in in
2c_{j}
(real) spacetime dimensions for both unitarity and Lorentz invariance of the string theory to hold.
On the other hand, for any proper family of curves
\pi\colon\thinspace X\to S
, the Mumford isomorphism is the isomorphism of line bundles

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\cong\lambda_{1}^{c_{j}},

where
\lambda_{j}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\det R\pi_{*}(\omega_{X/S}^{\otimes j})
and
\omega_{X/S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Omega_{X/S}^{1}
. If
S
is the moduli space of curves and
\pi\colon\thinspace X\to S
is the universal curve,
then we find the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\det\Omega^{1}_{S}\cong\lambda_{2}
, and therefore
\omega_{S}\cong\lambda_{1}^{c_{j}}\cong(\pi_{*}\omega_{X/S})^{c_{j}}
. This is important to Polyakov path integration on the moduli space.

An alternative description of the Polyakov measure comes through the connection between representations of the Virasoro algebra and the moduli space; this connection is roughly the following.
The Virasoro algebra acts on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{g,1^{\infty}}
of triples
(C,p,z)
, a Riemann surface, an point, and a parameter. This action can be used to show that there exists a flat holomorphic connection on the line bundle
\lambda_{j}\otimes\lambda_{1}^{-c_{j}}
, which can be used to write differential equations for the Polyakov measure. For another summary, see the introduction of
[
Man88
]
.

In fact,
[
Man86
]
hypothesized this relationship existed in the super setting as well. The numerical coincidence is analogous: The super Witt algebra has representations
\varrho_{j}
defined by Lie derivative action on
\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)[\zeta]\,[dz|d\zeta]^{\otimes j}
such that pulling back the super Japanese cocycle
\eta
(which defines the unique central extension: the Neveu-Schwarz superalgebra) gives

\displaystyle\varrho_{j}^{*}(\eta)=c_{j}\varrho_{1}^{*}(\eta),

\displaystyle c_{j}=-(-1)^{j}(2j-1).

And for any proper family of supercurves
\pi\colon\thinspace X\to S
, the super Mumford isomorphism is the isomorphism of line bundles

\displaystyle\lambda_{j/2}\cong\lambda_{1/2}^{c_{j}},

where
\lambda_{j/2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Ber R\pi_{*}(\omega^{\otimes j}_{X/S})
and
\omega_{X/S}=\Ber\Omega_{X/S}^{1}
.

The goal of the paper is to describe a supersymmetric generalization of the construction of
[
Kon87
]
and
[
ADKP88

, which utilizes a relation between the moduli space of curves with the infinite-dimensional Grassmannian. The consequences of their construction shows that the Chern classes of the line bundles of the Mumford isomorphism are equal. Manin proved a supersymmetric generalization of this result in

by generalizing

of the super Sato Grassmannian and the super Krichever map. Our method depends on showing that the Lie superalgebroid of superconformal vector fields on a family of open super Riemann surfaces is perfect, which follows from a superconformal Noether normalization lemma for families of super Riemann surfaces.

Our use of the super Grassmannian
\Gr\big(\mathbb{C}(\!(z)\!)[\zeta]\,[dz|d\zeta]^{\otimes j}\big)
appears to provide an alternative approach for integrating over the moduli space of SRSs
\mathfrak{M}_{g}

2.1
Action Lie superalgebroids

We recall the definition of a Lie superalgebra. The difference from a classical Lie algebra is the supercommutator, in other words the rule of signs.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie superalgebra
is a super vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with a morphism
[\;,\;]\colon\thinspace\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

•

[x,y]+(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,x]=0

•

the super Jacobi identity
(-1)^{|x||z|}[x,[y,z]]+(-1)^{|y||x|}[y,[z,x]]+(-1)^{|z||y|}[z,[x,y]]=0

In what follows, one may easily determine the classical statements by omitting the sign rule. We also cite sources for the classical versions of these statements.

A Lie superalgebroid is simply the many-object generalization of a Lie superalgebra. That is, a Lie superalgebroid over a point is a Lie superalgebra.

We prefer to work with locally free sheaves, and so denote Lie superalgebroids in script as
\mathcal{E}
. If we work with instead the associated super vector bundle, we denote the Lie superalgebroid in roman as
E
. Note that
TS
denotes the tangent bundle and
\mathcal{T}_{S}
the tangent sheaf of
S
.

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
KM02
, Definition 1.3]
)
.

A Lie superalgebroid on a complex supermanifold
S
is a triple
(\mathcal{E},a,[\;,\;])
such that

•

\mathcal{E}
is the sheaf of sections of
E\to S
a super vector bundle

•

a\colon\thinspace E\to TS
is a (parity preserving) super vector bundle morphism, called the anchor map, where
TS
is the tangent bundle of
S

•

[\;,\;]:\Gamma(S,\mathcal{E})\times\Gamma(S,\mathcal{E})\to\Gamma(S,\mathcal{E})
is a
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear alternating bracket which satisfies for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(S,\mathcal{E})
and
f\in\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})

\bullet
the super Jacobi identity

\bullet

a([X,Y])=[a(X),a(Y)]

\bullet

[X,fY]=(-1)^{|f||X|}f[X,Y]+a(X)(f)Y

Given Lie superalgebroids
(\mathcal{E},a)
and
(\mathcal{F},b)
on the same base

, a Lie superalgebroid morphism

is a (parity preserving) super vector bundle morphism
\varphi\colon\thinspace E\to F
over

such that for all
X,Y\in\Gamma(S,\mathcal{E})

•

•

\varphi([X,Y])=[\varphi(X),\varphi(Y)]

In this paper we will need use of two specific types of Lie superalgebroids: action Lie superalgebroids and Atiyah superalgebras.

Definition 2.3

(cf.
[

•

\displaystyle[V,W]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\varphi}(V)}(W)-(-1)^{|V||W|}\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\varphi}(W)}(V)+[V,W]_{\mathfrak{g}}

2.2
Atiyah Lie superalgebroids

The Atiyah Lie superalgebroid

\displaystyle[D,f](e)=D(fe)-(-1)^{|D||f|}fD(e)

In the standard sense, a holomorphic connection on a line bundle

3.1
Background material on super Riemann surfaces and their superconformal structure

References covering the background material of superalgebras, superspaces, and SRSs in more detail include
[
DM99
,
BBH91
,
Man88a
]
and more recently
[
BHP20
,
FK14
,
CCF11
,
Wit19
]

Definition 3.1
.

A super Riemann surface (SUSY curve, or SRS) is a complex supermanifold of dimension
1|1
with a maximally nonintegrable distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
of rank
0|1
. Precisely, a super Riemann surface is the data
(\Sigma,\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma},\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma})
such that

•

\Sigma
is a complex manifold of dimension
1

•

\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}
is a sheaf on
\Sigma
of supercommutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

•

\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma}
is locally isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}[\xi]

•

\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
is an odd subbundle of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}

•

the induced map by Lie bracket
[\>\>,\>\>]:\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}^{\otimes 2}\to\mathcal{T}_{\Sigma}/\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
is an isomorphism.

Define a family of SUSY curves in the holomorphic category: a proper submersion
\pi:X\to S
of super Riemann surfaces (i.e. relative dimension
1|1
) parameterized by the complex supermanifold
S
with a relative distribution
\mathcal{D}_{X/S}\subset\mathcal{T}_{X/S}
of rank
0|1
such that the map
[\>\>,\>\>]:\mathcal{D}_{X/S}^{\otimes 2}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/S}/\mathcal{D}_{X/S}
is an isomorphism.

Locally on a single SRS, we can always find coordinates (called
superconformal coordinates
)
z|\zeta
such that

\displaystyle D_{\zeta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}+\zeta\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

generates the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{\Sigma}
. In more generality for a family, we can find relative superconformal coordinates
z|\zeta
such that
D_{\zeta}
generates
\mathcal{D}_{X/S}
.
Naturally, we call a change of coordinates
z|\zeta\mapsto x|\xi
superconformal if
D_{\zeta}
and
D_{\xi}
are multiples of one another. Alternatively, the condition

\displaystyle D_{\zeta}x=\xi D_{\zeta}\xi

(4)

is equivalent to the change of coordinates being superconformal.

Definition 3.2
.

The moduli space of super Riemann surfaces
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
is the superspace representing the functor

\displaystyle S\mapsto\left\{\textrm{families of SUSY curves }\pi\colon\thinspace X\to S\right\}.

Similar to the moduli space of classical Riemann surfaces
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, the moduli space of SRSs is representable as a stack, specifically an orbifold or Deligne-Mumford stack for
g\geq 2
. The DM stack structure of
\mathfrak{M}_{g}
and its compactification has recently been studied in papers
[
CV19
,
FKP20
,
MZ19
]
.

The following exact sequence, or equivalently its dual also listed below, provides much information about the structure of SUSY curves.

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{D}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/S}/\mathcal{D}_{X/S}\to 0

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{D}^{\perp}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{X/S}\to 0

Since
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}_{X/S}
is dual to the quotient
\mathcal{T}_{X/S}/\mathcal{D}_{X/S}
, then we find that
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}_{X/S}
is generated in local superconformal coordinates by
dz-\zeta d\zeta
.
Further, the SRS structure gives isomorphisms with powers of the distribution
\mathcal{D}
so that these exact sequences are equivalent to

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{D}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{D}^{\otimes 2}_{X/S}\to 0

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{D}^{\otimes-2}_{X/S}\to\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{X/S}\to 0.

(5)

To be precise, the projection above is the composition
\mathcal{T}_{X/S}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/S}/\mathcal{D}_{X/S}\xleftarrow{\sim}\mathcal{D}^{2}_{X/S}
using the isomorphism induced by the Lie bracket.

We recall the definition of the Berezinian, the supergeneralization of the determinant. Let
A
be a superalgebra. Consider
\mathrm{GL}(m|n,A)
, the supergroup of automorphisms of the free
A
-module
A^{m|n}
. For
X\in\mathrm{GL}(m|n,A)
expressed in as an
(m+n)\times(m+n)
matrix the Berezinian is defined as

\displaystyle X=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\Ber X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\det(A-BD^{-1}C)\textstyle\det^{-1}D.

(6)

We note that
\Ber(\mathrm{exp}X)=\mathrm{exp}(\str X)
, where
\str
is the supertrace. Written in terms of the matrix in
Eq.

6
,
\str X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\tr A-\tr D
.

The parity reversing operator denoted
\Pi
maps a super vector space
V=V_{0}\oplus V_{1}
to the super vector space with even and odd components swapped:
(\Pi V)_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}V_{1}
and
(\Pi V)_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}V_{0}

\Pi
has no analogous classical operator, neither does the
\Pi
-transpose given below.

\displaystyle X^{\Pi}=\begin{bmatrix}D&C\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}

(7)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a vector bundle of rank
m|n
with associated transition functions
\{f_{ij}\}
. Then we define
\Ber\mathcal{F}
to be the bundle with transition functions
\{\Ber f_{ij}\}
. We enforce that
\Ber\mathcal{F}
is rank
1|0
if
n
is even and rank
0|1
if
n
is odd. We denote a trivializing section of
\Ber\mathcal{F}
as

The Berezinian of the exact sequence

induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\Ber\Omega_{X/S}^{1}\cong\Ber\mathcal{D}^{-2}_{X/S}\otimes\Ber\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{X/S}\cong\mathcal{D}^{-2}_{X/S}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{X/S}\cong\mathcal{D}^{-1}_{X/S}.

(8)

We define

Further, properties of the Berezinian of a SRS give an interesting construction of a relative superconformal differential. We define
\delta
as the composition of the classical relative differential

and the quotient

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{X/S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{X/S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

where

in

\displaystyle\delta f=(D_{\zeta}f)\;[dz|d\zeta].

We remark that an equivalent condition for a local coordinate system

to be superconformal is

\displaystyle\delta x=\xi\,\delta\xi

which is a coordinate-independent version of

The vector fields which preserve the superconformal structure

are called superconformal. The sheaf of superconformal vector fields

on a family of super Riemann surfaces is the largest subsheaf of

such that
[\mathcal{T}^{s}_{X/S},\mathcal{D}_{X/S}]=\mathcal{D}_{X/S}

In local superconformal coordinates

\displaystyle[fD_{\zeta},D_{\zeta}]=f(z|\zeta)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{(-1)^{|f|}D_{\zeta}f(z|\zeta)}{2}D_{\zeta}.

This expression in local coordinates shows two facts. Firstly,

has an

Secondly, the map

is a canonical isomorphism (of sheaves of
\mathbb{C}

\mathcal{T}_{X/S}\cong\mathcal{T}^{s}_{X/S}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{X/S}
(as sheaves of
\mathbb{C}
super vector spaces).

Definition 4.1

Definition A.6
.

Define a subspace of

to be

. Explicitly, subspaces

are commensurable when

Lemma A.7
.

Let
K
be any compact subspace. Then
D
is discrete if and only if the natural map
D\oplus K\to H
is Fredholm, that is when the kernel and cokernel are finite dimensional.

\displaystyle 0\to D\cap K\to D\oplus K\to H\to H/(D+K)\to 0

Proof.

Firstly we show that commensurability is transitive, i.e. any two compact subspaces are commensurable to each other. For
K
and
K^{\prime}
compact, using the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\frac{(K\cap H^{+})+(K\cap K^{\prime})}{K\cap K^{\prime}}\to\frac{K}{K\cap K^{\prime}}\to\frac{K}{(K\cap H^{+})+(K\cap K^{\prime})}\to 0,

where the first term is isomorphic to
(K\cap H^{+})/(K\cap K^{\prime}\cap H^{+})
, so we see
\dim(K/(K\cap K^{\prime}))\leq\dim(H^{+}/(K^{\prime}\cap H^{+}))+\dim(K/(K\cap H^{+}))<\infty
. And
\dim(K/(K\cap K^{\prime}))<\infty
similarly.

Next we show that if
D\cap K
and
H/(D+K)
are finite-dimensional for some compact
K
, then
D\cap K^{\prime}
and
H/(D+K^{\prime})
are finite-dimensional for any other compact
K^{\prime}
. Using the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to D\cap K\cap K^{\prime}\to D\cap K^{\prime}\to D\cap K^{\prime}/(D\cap K\cap K^{\prime})\to 0,

we see
\dim(D\cap K^{\prime})\leq\dim(D\cap K)+\dim(K^{\prime}/(K\cap K^{\prime}))<\infty
. Using the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\frac{K}{(D+K^{\prime})\cap K}\cong\frac{D+K^{\prime}+K}{D+K^{\prime}}\to\frac{H}{D+K^{\prime}}\to\frac{H}{D+K+K^{\prime}}\to 0,

we see
\dim(H/(D+K^{\prime}))\leq\dim(K/(K^{\prime}\cap K))+\dim(H/(D+K))<\infty
.

Assume
D
is discrete. Then
D\oplus K^{\prime}=H
for some compact
K^{\prime}
. Thus
D\cap K^{\prime}=0
and
H/(D+K^{\prime})=0
. Then

and

are finite-dimensional by the previous paragraph.

Assume

and
H/(D+K)
are finite-dimensional. We show

is discrete by showing

is compact. It suffices to notice

\displaystyle\frac{K}{K\cap(H/D)}\cong D\cap K,

\displaystyle\frac{H/D}{K\cap(H/D)}\cong\frac{H}{D+K}.

∎

Appendix D
Proof of
4.3
(a condition to be a discrete submodule)

4.3
provides an alternative characterization of a discrete subspace.
We first provide a few lemmas.

Lemma D.1
.

Consider a discrete
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-submodule
L
, such that
L_{U}\oplus\widehat{K}_{U}\to(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}
is an isomorphism.
For every compact
K^{\prime}\supseteq K
, then
L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}
is locally free of finite type and
(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U})=0
.

Proof.

We have the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}_{U}\to L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}\to\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}/\widehat{K}_{U}\to(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}_{U})\to(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U})\to 0

which simplifies to
L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}\cong\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}/\widehat{K}_{U}
.
Further, we see that
\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}/\widehat{K}_{U}\cong\widehat{(K^{\prime}/K)}_{U}
which is locally free of rank
\dim(K^{\prime}/K)<\infty
.
∎

Lemma D.2
.

Consider a discrete
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-submodule
L
.
If
(\widehat{H}_{j})_{S}/(L_{S}+\widehat{K}_{S})=0
, then
L_{S}\cap\widehat{K}_{S}
is locally free of finite type.

Proof.

By
D.1
, we see that locally we can find
K^{\prime}\supseteq K
, so that
L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}
is free of finite type and
(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U})=0
.
Then we have the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}_{U}\to L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}\to\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U}/\widehat{K}_{U}\to 0.

Since
L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}_{U}
is the kernel of a surjective map between free modules of finite type, then the result follows.
∎

Lemma D.3
.

Let
L
be a super
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-submodule which for every
s\in S
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
s
and a compact
K
such that
L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}_{U}
is free of finite type and
(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}_{U})=0
.

Then for every compact
K^{\prime}
,
(\widehat{H}_{j})_{S}/(L_{S}+\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{S})
is locally of finite presentation.

Proof.

Choose compact
K^{\prime\prime}\supseteq(K^{\prime}\cup K)
. Then by
D.1
,
L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime\prime}_{U}
is free of finite type and
(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}^{\prime\prime}_{U})=0
for some
U\ni s
. Then we have the exact sequence

\displaystyle L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}^{\prime\prime}_{U}\to(K^{\prime\prime}/K^{\prime})_{U}\to(\widehat{H}_{j})_{U}/(L_{U}+\widehat{K}^{\prime}_{U})\to 0

from which the result follows.
∎

Proof of
4.3
.

The definition of a discrete module clearly satisfies the condition of
4.3
automatically.

For the other direction, assume
L
is a super
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-submodule which for every

there exists a neighborhood

of

and a compact

L_{U}\cap\widehat{K}_{U}
is free of finite type and

Then there exists

Therefore

∎

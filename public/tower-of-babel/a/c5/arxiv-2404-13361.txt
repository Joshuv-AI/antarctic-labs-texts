Induced orthogonality in semilattices with 0 0 and in pseudocomplemented lattices and posets
Ivan Chajda, Miroslav Kolařík, Helmut Länger

Abstract
On an arbitrary meet-semilattice 𝐒 = ( S , ∧ , 0 ) \mathbf{S}=(S,\wedge,0) with 0 0 we define an orthogonality relation and investigate the lattice 𝐂𝐥 ⁡ ( 𝐒 ) \BCl(\mathbf{S}) of all subsets of S S closed under this orthogonality. We show that if 𝐒 \mathbf{S} is atomic then 𝐂𝐥 ⁡ ( 𝐒 ) \BCl(\mathbf{S}) is a complete atomic Boolean algebra. If 𝐒 \mathbf{S} is a pseudocomplemented lattice, this orthogonality relation can be defined by means of the pseudocomplementation. Finally, we show that if 𝐒 \mathbf{S} is a complete pseudocomplemented lattice then 𝐂𝐥 ⁡ ( 𝐒 ) \BCl(\mathbf{S}) is a complete Boolean algebra. For pseudocomplemented posets a similar result holds if the subset of pseudocomplements forms a complete lattice satisfying a certain compatibility condition.

Introduction

By an orthogonality on a set
S

\perp

A

S

A^{\perp}:=\{x\in S\mid x\perp y\text{ for all }y\in A\}
. A subset
A
of
S
is called
closed
if
A^{\perp\perp}=A
. It is well-known that the lattice of all closed subsets of a given set
S
forms a complete ortholattice. Several authors studied when this lattice is orthomodular, see
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
and, for Hilbert spaces, see also
[
8
]
and
[

]
.

We slightly modify the definition of an orthogonality relation on a set
S
with a distinguished element
0
. We require such a relation
\perp
to be symmetric and to have the property that
0
is the only element
x
of
S
satisfying
x\perp x
. We study lattices of closed subsets of meet-semilattices or lattices with
0
where the orthogonality relation is derived by means of the meet-operation. In the case of pseudocomplemented lattices, the orthogonality relation can be introduced via the pseudocomplementation. It is expected that if a meet-semilattice
\mathbf{S}
with
0
is considered instead of a set and the orthogonality is derived from the meet-operation then the lattice
\BCl(\mathbf{S})
of closed subsets will satisfy a stronger property then orthomodularity. We show that if
\mathbf{S}
is atomic then
\BCl(\mathbf{S})
forms a complete atomic Boolean algebra. Especially, we consider the case when
\mathbf{S}
is either the modular lattice
\mathbf{M}_{n}
or a Boolean algebra. If
\mathbf{L}
is a complete pseudocomplemented lattice then
\BCl(\mathbf{L})
turns out to be a complete Boolean algebra. Several examples illuminating these concepts and results are included in Sections 2 and 3. If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
is a pseudocomplemented poset then we can define orthogonality on
\mathbf{P}
by means of pseudocomplementation in the same way as it was done in the case of pseudocomplemented lattices. It turns out that if its subset
P^{*}
of all pseudocomplements is a complete lattice satisfying a certain compatibility condition then the complete ortholattice
\BCl(\mathbf{P})
of closed subsets of
\mathbf{P}
is again a Boolean algebra. We also characterize finite pseudocomplemented posets whose poset of pseudocomplements forms a meet-semilattice by means of a forbidden configuration.

The result illustrated in Example
2.3
can be extended to arbitrary atomic semilattices with
0
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(S,\wedge,0)
be an atomic meet-semilattice with
0
and let
\At\mathbf{S}
denote the set of all atoms of
\mathbf{S}
. Then
\BCl(\mathbf{S})\cong\mathbf{2}^{\At\mathbf{S}}
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq S
and put

B:=\{x\in S\mid x\not\geq y\text{ for all }y\in\At\mathbf{S}\text{ with }[y)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}.

We show
A^{\perp}=B
. Let
a\in A^{\perp}
. Suppose
a\notin B
. Then there exists some
b\in\At\mathbf{S}
with
a\geq b
and
[b)\cap A\neq\emptyset
. Let
c\in[b)\cap A
. Then
c\in A
and
0<b\leq a\wedge c
contradicting
a\in A^{\perp}
. Hence
a\in B
. Conversely, let
a\in B
. Suppose
a\notin A^{\perp}
. Then there exists some
d\in A
with
a\wedge d\neq 0
. Since
\mathbf{S}
is atomic there exists some
e\in\At\mathbf{S}
with
e\leq a\wedge d
. But then
e\in\At\mathbf{S}
and
[e)\cap A\supseteq\{d\}\neq\emptyset
, but
a\geq e
contradicting
a\in B
. Hence
a\in A
. This completes the proof of
A^{\perp}=B
. For every
C\subseteq\At\mathbf{S}
put

f(C):=\{x\in S\mid x\not\geq y\text{ for all }y\in(\At\mathbf{S})\setminus C\}.

Then
\Cl(\mathbf{S})=\{f(C)\mid C\subseteq\At\mathbf{S}\}
. Now let
C,D\subseteq\At\mathbf{S}
. If
C\subseteq D
then
f(C)\subseteq f(D)
. If, conversely,
f(C)\subseteq f(D)
then

C=(\At\mathbf{S})\cap f(C)\subseteq(\At\mathbf{S})\cap f(D)=D.

This shows that
f
is an isomorphism from the Boolean algebra
\mathbf{2}^{\At\mathbf{S}}
to
\BCl(\mathbf{S})
completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

For each

be a meet-semilattice with

\BCl\left(\prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{S}_{i}\right)=\prod_{i\in I}\BCl(\mathbf{S}_{i}).

For

denote the

\prod\limits_{i\in I}S_{i}
onto

. Then for

we have

\displaystyle A^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\prod_{i\in I}S_{i}\mid x\wedge y=0\text{ for all }y\in A\}=

\displaystyle=\{x\in\prod_{i\in I}S_{i}\mid p_{j}(x)\wedge p_{j}(y)=0\text{ for all }j\in I\text{ and all }y\in A\}=

\displaystyle=\{x\in\prod_{i\in I}S_{i}\mid p_{j}(x)\wedge y_{j}=0\text{ for all }j\in I\text{ and all }y_{j}\in p_{j}(A)\}=\prod_{i\in I}\big(p_{i}(A)\big)^{\perp}.

Hence
\Cl\left(\prod\limits_{i\in I}\mathbf{S}_{i}\right)=\prod\limits_{i\in I}\Cl(\mathbf{S}_{i})

for all

if and only if

for all

3
Pseudocomplemented lattices

We can consider closed subsets also in pseudocomplemented posets and lattices. Recall that a (bounded)
poset

(P,\leq,0,1)
is called
pseudocomplemented
if for each
x\in P
there exists a greatest element
y\in P
such that
x\wedge y
exists and is equal to
0
. This element
y
is called the
pseudocomplement
of
x
and it is denoted by
x^{*}
. Hence,

x\wedge y=0\qquad\text{ if and only if }\qquad y\leq x^{*}.

Thus
∗
is a unary antitone operation on
P
satisfying
x\leq x^{**}
and
x^{***}\approx x^{*}
. We will express the fact that
(P,\leq,0,1)
is pseudocomplemented by the notation

\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1).

In a pseudocomplemented poset
\mathbf{P}
, the orthogonality relation
\perp
is defined by

x\perp y\qquad\text{ if and only if }\qquad y\leq x^{*}.

Similarly as in Section 2 for meet-semilattices with
0
, also here we have that
\perp
is symmetric and that
x\perp x
if and only if
x=0
; of course,
x\perp 0
for each
x\in P
. The definition of
A^{\perp}
for
A\subseteq P
is the same as in the previous sections and
A
is called
closed
if
A^{\perp\perp}=A
.

For every subset
A
of
P
,
A^{*}
denotes the set
\{x^{*}\mid x\in A\}
.

If we consider pseudocomplemented lattices, we can simplify several of the aforementioned concepts and we use the so-called Glivenko Theorem to describe the structure of the lattice of all closed subsets of a complete pseudocomplemented lattice.

First recall the following concepts.

A
pseudocomplemented meet-semilattice
is an algebra
(S,\wedge,{}^{*},0)
of type
(2,1,0)
such that
(S,\wedge,0)
is a meet-semilattice with
0
satisfying

x\wedge y=0\text{ if and only if }y\leq x^{*}.

A
pseudocomplemented lattice
is an algebra
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
of type
(2,2,1,0,0)
such that
(L,\vee,\wedge,0,1)
is a bounded lattice and
(L,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
is a pseudocomplemented poset.

We can describe infima of subsets of
P^{*}
as well as subsets of the form
A^{\perp}
with
A\subseteq P
. This result will be used in the proof of the next theorems.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset and
A\subseteq P
and assume
\bigvee A
to exist. Then the following holds:

(i)

\bigwedge A^{*}
exists and
\bigwedge A^{*}=(\bigvee A)^{*}
,

(ii)

A^{\perp}=(\bigvee A)^{\perp}
.

Proof.

(i)

For every
x\in A
we have
x\leq\bigvee A
and hence
(\bigvee A)^{*}\leq x^{*}
. This shows that
(\bigvee A)^{*}
is a lower bound of
A^{*}
. Now let
a
be a lower bound of
A^{*}
. Then
a\leq x^{*}
for all
x\in A
and hence
x\leq a^{*}
for all
x\in A
. This shows that
a^{*}
is an upper bound of
A
. Therefore
\bigvee A\leq a^{*}
which implies
a\leq(\bigvee A)^{*}
. This shows that
\bigwedge A^{*}
exists and
\bigwedge A^{*}=(\bigvee A)^{*}
.

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle A^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{a\in A}a^{\perp}=\bigcap_{a\in A}\{x\in P\mid x\leq a^{*}\}=\{x\in P\mid x\leq a^{*}\text{ for all }a\in A\}=

\displaystyle=\{x\in P\mid x\leq\bigwedge A^{*}\}=\{x\in P\mid x\leq\left(\bigvee A\right)^{*}\}=\left(\bigvee A\right)^{\perp}.

∎

The following result follows from Lemma
3.1

Corollary 3.2

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)

\Cl(\mathbf{L})=\{x^{\perp}\mid x\in L\}

Proof.

According to (ii) of Lemma
3.1

\Cl(\mathbf{L})=\{A^{\perp}\mid A\subseteq L\}=\{\left(\bigvee A\right)^{\perp}\mid A\subseteq L\}=\{x^{\perp}\mid x\in L\}

since every element
x
of
L
can be written in the form
\bigvee\{x\}
.
∎

Theorem 3.6

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
be a complete pseudocomplemented lattice. Then
\BCl(\mathbf{L})

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.2

\big(\Cl(\mathbf{L}),\vee,\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},L\big)

\{0\}
and greatest element
L

\Cl(\mathbf{L})=\{x^{\perp}\mid x\in L\}

x^{\perp}=(x^{*}]

x\in L
, we have
a^{*}\leq b^{*}
if and only if
a^{\perp}\subseteq b^{\perp}

a^{*}=b^{*}
if and only if
a^{\perp}=b^{\perp}
. We conclude that the mapping
x^{*}\mapsto x^{\perp}

\BCl(\mathbf{L})

(x^{*})^{*}

.

If
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset then

\BCl(\mathbf{P}):=\big(\Cl(\mathbf{P}),\vee,\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},P\big)

is a complete ortholattice with

\displaystyle\bigvee_{i\in I}A_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}\right)^{\perp\perp},

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{i\in I}A_{i}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}

for all families
A_{i},i\in I,
of elements of
\Cl(\mathbf{P})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\bigcap_{i\in I}A_{i}^{\perp}=\left(\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}\right)^{\perp}

for all families
A_{i},i\in I,
of elements of
\Cl(\mathbf{P})
and hence
\big(\Cl(\mathbf{P}),\subseteq\big)
is a complete lattice whose meet-operation coincides with set-theoretical intersection. The unary operation
⟂
on
\Cl(\mathbf{P})
is an antitone involution and because of
A\cap A^{\perp}=\{0\}
for all
A\subseteq P
also a complementation.
∎

It is a question if the situation described in Example

can be generalized, i.e. if for each pseudocomplemented poset
\mathbf{P}
the complete ortholattice
\BCl(\mathbf{P})
of its closed subsets forms a Boolean algebra.

For this, we firstly prove a result similar to the famous theorem of Glivenko but formulated only for those pseudocomplemented posets
(P,\leq;{}^{*},0,1)
where
(P^{*},\leq)
is a meet-semilattice.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset where
(P^{*},\leq)
is a meet-semilattice
(P^{*},\wedge)
and put

x\vee y:=(x^{*}\wedge y^{*})^{*}

for all
x,y\in P^{*}
. Then
(P^{*},\vee,\wedge,{}^{*},0,1)
is a Boolean algebra.

Proof.

First observe that
∗
is an antitone involution on
(P^{*},\leq)
. Hence
(P^{*},\vee,\wedge)
is a lattice and De Morgan’s laws hold. Since
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identity
x\wedge x^{*}\approx 0
, the operation
∗
is a complementation. Now let
a,b,c\in P^{*}
and put
d:=a\vee(b\wedge c)
. Then
a\wedge b\leq d
and hence
a\wedge b\wedge d^{*}=0
whence

(1)

b\wedge d^{*}\leq a^{*}
.

Since
b\wedge c\leq d
we obtain
b\wedge c\wedge d^{*}=0
which implies
b\wedge d^{*}\leq c^{*}
. This together with (1) yields
b\wedge d^{*}\leq a^{*}\wedge c^{*}
. From this we get
b\wedge(a\vee c)\wedge d^{*}=0
and hence

(2)

(a\vee c)\wedge d^{*}\leq b^{*}
.

Now we have
a\leq d
which implies
a\wedge d^{*}=0
. Therefore
a\wedge(a\vee c)\wedge d^{*}=0
from which we get
(a\vee c)\wedge d^{*}\leq a^{*}
. This together with (2) yields
(a\vee c)\wedge d^{*}\leq a^{*}\wedge b^{*}
. From this we derive
(a\vee c)\wedge(a\vee b)\wedge d^{*}=0
and hence
(a\vee c)\wedge(a\vee b)\leq d
showing
(P^{*},\vee,\wedge)
to be distributive.
∎

The question when for a subset
A
of
P
there exists some
b\in P
satisfying
b^{\perp}=A^{\perp}
is solved in the following lemma. However, we must notice that for a pseudocomplemented poset
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
there may happen the following rather pathological situation. Namely, if
(P^{*},\leq)
forms a complete lattice,
A\subseteq P^{*}
and
a
denotes the infimum of
A
in
(P^{*},\leq)
then there may exist some lower bound
b
of
A
in
(P,\leq)
satisfying
b\not\leq a
. In such a case, the infimum of
A
in the whole poset
(P,\leq)
either does not exists or it differs from
a
. In order to avoid such a pathological situation, we must require one more assumption in the next formulations.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset such that
(P^{*},\leq)
is a complete lattice having the property that for each subset
B
of
P^{*}
the infimum of
B
in
(P,\leq)
exists and coincides with the infimum of
B
in
(P^{*},\leq)
and let
A
be a subset of
P
. Then there exists some
b\in P
with
b^{\perp}=A^{\perp}
.

Proof.

Since
\bigwedge A^{*}\in P^{*}
there exists some
b\in P
with
b^{*}=\bigwedge A^{*}
. Now we have

\displaystyle A^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{a\in A}a^{\perp}=\bigcap_{a\in A}\{x\in P\mid x\leq a^{*}\}=\{x\in P\mid x\leq a^{*}\text{ for all }a\in A\}=

\displaystyle=\{x\in P\mid x\leq\bigwedge A^{*}\}=\{x\in P\mid x\leq b^{*}\}=b^{\perp}.

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

If
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset such that
(P^{*},\leq)
is a complete lattice having the property that for each subset
B
of
P^{*}
the infimum of
B
in
(P,\leq)
exists and coincides with the infimum of
B
in
(P^{*},\leq)
then
\Cl(\mathbf{P})=\{x^{\perp}\mid x\in P\}
.

Using several of the results obtained so far we can show that if
(P^{*},\leq)
is a complete lattice having the property that for each subset
B
of

B

(P,\leq)

B

(P^{*},\leq)
, the complete ortholattice of closed subsets of
\mathbf{P}
forms again a Boolean algebra.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(P,\leq,{}^{*},0,1)
be a pseudocomplemented poset such that
(P^{*},\leq)
is a complete lattice having the property that for each subset
B
of
P^{*}
the infimum of
B
in
(P,\leq)
exists and coincides with the infimum of
B
in
(P^{*},\leq)
. Then
\BCl(\mathbf{P})
is a complete Boolean algebra.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.10
,
\big(\Cl(\mathbf{P}),\vee,\cap,{}^{\perp},\{0\},P\big)
is a complete ortholattice with smallest element
\{0\}
and greatest element
P
. According to
Corollary
4.4
we have
\Cl(\mathbf{P})=\{x^{\perp}\mid x\in P\}
. Let
a,b\in P

x^{\perp}=(x^{*}]
for all
x\in P
, we have
a^{*}\leq b^{*}
if and only if
a^{\perp}\subseteq b^{\perp}
. This implies that
a^{*}=b^{*}
if and only if
a^{\perp}=b^{\perp}
. We conclude that the mapping
x^{*}\mapsto x^{\perp}
is a well-defined isomorphism from
(P^{*},\leq)
to
\BCl(\mathbf{P})
. Since the first poset is a Boolean algebra according to Theorem
4.2
, the second one is a Boolean algebra, too. The unary operation of the first Boolean algebra maps
x^{*}
onto
(x^{*})^{*}
. Hence the unary operation of the second Boolean algebra maps
x^{\perp}
onto
(x^{*})^{\perp}
which equals
(x^{\perp})^{\perp}
according to Lemma
3.5
. This means that
⟂
is the unary operation of the second Boolean algebra.
∎

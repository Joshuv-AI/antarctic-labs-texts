Abstract
. We prove the Wolff potential estimates for nonlocal equations with nonstandard growth. As an application, we obtain the Wiener criterion in this framework, which provides a necessary and sufficient condition for boundary points to be regular. Our approach relies on the fine analysis of superharmonic functions in view of nonlocal nonlinear potential theory.

1.
Introduction

Nonlinear potential theory studies harmonic functions associated with nonlinear, possibly degenerate or singular equations. Its origin can be traced to the foundational research of Havin–Maz’ya
[
31
]
, where a systematic investigation of various potentials is carried out in order to analyze fine properties of harmonic functions. They introduced what is nowadays called the Wolff potential, which plays a fundamental role in the potential theory. One of the most powerful applications of Wolff potentials is the Wiener criterion for the
p
-Laplace equation. In
[
33
]
, Maz’ya obtained the sufficiency part of the Wiener criterion by using some quantities related to Wolff potentials. After a long while, Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
17
]
proved pointwise estimates for solutions of
p
-Laplace-type equation in terms of the Wolff potential, finally establishing the necessity part of the Wiener criterion. See also
[
20
,
35
]
for different approaches. We refer the reader to books by Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
14
]
, Malý–Ziemer
[
30
]
, and Adams–Hedberg
[
1
]
for a comprehensive description of nonlinear potential theory.

As a nonlocal counterpart of nonlinear potential theory, nonlocal nonlinear potential theory has recently begun to be actively studied. It was initiated in Kuusi–Mingione–Sire
[
22
]
, where the pointwise bounds by Wolff potentials for SOLA (solutions obtained as limits of approximations) of fractional
p
-Laplace-type equations are obtained for
p\in(2-s/n,n/s)
, where
s\in(0,1)
is the order of differentiability. The restriction
p>2-s/n
is natural when we study SOLA. The range of
p\in(1,n/s]
was recently covered in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
for the pointwise upper estimate by using superharmonic functions instead of SOLA as in
[
17
]
. This upper bound, together with several fine properties of superharmonic functions developed in Korvenpää–Kussi–Palatucci
[
21
]
, led us to the Wiener criterion for the fractional
p
-Laplace-type equations in
[
18
]
. However, the pointwise lower bound for the full range of
p>1
was not known and it is provided here in a more general framework; see
Theorem

1.3
.

The aforementioned papers focus on the standard nonlinearity described by a polynomial of degree
p>1
. In this paper, we aim to extend the theory to a general class of nonlocal nonlinear operators with nonstandard growth. More precisely, building upon the results of Kim–Lee
[
19
]
, we prove pointwise upper and lower bounds of superharmonic functions associated with the operator (
1.3
) below. We then establish the Wiener criterion, which characterizes a regular boundary point in terms of nonlocal nonlinear potential theory.

Let
G:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be given by

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

for some non-decreasing, left-continuous function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
which is not identically zero. Such a function is called a
Young function
. We assume throughout the paper that
G
is differentiable,
g=G^{\prime}
is strictly increasing, and there exist
1<p\leq q
such that

(1.1)

pG(t)\leq tg(t)\leq qG(t)\quad\text{for all}~t\geq 0

and

(1.2)

t\mapsto g(t)/t^{q-1}\quad\text{is non-increasing}.

However, we point out that the assumption (
1.2
) is only necessary to use the results from
[
19
]
and is not actually used in our analysis. We may assume without loss of generality that
G(1)=1
.

We consider an operator

(1.3)

\mathcal{L}u(x)=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where

D^{s}u=D^{s}u(x,y)=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}

denotes a
fractional gradient
of order
s\in(0,1)
and
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
is a measurable kernel satisfying the ellipticity condition
\Lambda^{-1}\leq k(x,y)\leq\Lambda
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
\Lambda\geq 1
.

Let us provide the definition of the Wolff potential which takes the Orlicz growth into account. It will play a crucial role in our analysis.

Definition 1.1

(Wolff potential)
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel measure with finite total mass in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Wolff potential
{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,G}
of the measure
\mu
is defined by

(1.4)

{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,G}(x_{0},R)=\int_{0}^{R}\rho^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}

for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<R\leq\infty
.

The Wolff potential
{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,G}
was first introduced in Fiorenza–Prignet
[
12
]
as a generalization of the classical Wolff potential

{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,p}(x_{0},R)=\int_{0}^{R}\left(\frac{|\mu|(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-sp}}\right)^{1/(p-1)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}.

Our first main results are pointwise upper and lower bounds of superharmonic functions involving the Wolff potential (
1.4
). See
Definition

2.7
for the definition of a superharmonic function.

Theorem 1.2

(Pointwise upper bound)
.

Let
u
be a superharmonic function in
B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then
\mu:=\mathcal{L}u
is a nonnegative Borel measure and

(1.5)

u(x_{0})\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{R/2}(x_{0})}u+{\bf W}_{s,G}^{\mu}(x_{0},R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R/2)\right)

for some
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda)>0
.

Note that
Theorem

1.2
includes the existence of the measure
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
, which is called the
Riesz measure
.

Theorem 1.3

(Pointwise lower bound)
.

Let
u
be a superharmonic function in
B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}
and let
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
be the Riesz measure. Then

(1.6)

\mathbf{W}^{\mu}_{s,G}(x_{0},R/2)\leq C(u(x_{0})+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R))

for some
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda)>0
.

In the standard growth case
G(t)=t^{p}
, on the one hand, the poinwise upper bound (
1.5
) was proved by Kuusi–Mingione–Sire
[
22
]
for SOLA when
p>2-s/n
and Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
for superharmonic functions when
p\in(1,n/s]
under the assumption that
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
exists. On the other hand, the pointwise lower bound (
1.6
) was only known for SOLA when
p\in(2-s/n,n/s)
. Hence,
Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3
are new even for the standard growth case because the existence of
\mu
is proved and the full range of
p>1
is covered. We refer the reader to Malý
[
29
]
and Chlebicka–Giannetti–Zatorska-Goldstein
[
7
]
for the Wolff potential estimates for local operators with Orlicz growth.

Let us next move on to the Wiener criterion, which provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a boundary point to be regular. It was first provided for the Laplacian by Wiener in his pioneering work
[
36
]
, and then extended in Littman–Stampacchia–Weinberger
[
27
]
to general second-order linear operators in divergence form with measurable coefficients. The generalization to quasilinear operators of second-order has been done by Maz’ya
[
33
]
, Gariepy–Ziemer
[
13
]
, Lindqvist–Martio
[
26
]
, and Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
17
]
. Further generalizations can be found in Labutin
[
23
]
for
k
-Hessian operators, Alkhutov–Krasheninnikova
[
2
]
for
p(x)
-Laplacian, and Lee–Lee
[
25
]
for operators with Orlicz growth.

For nonlocal operators, the Wiener criterion has been established by Bliedtner–Hansen
[
4
]
, Hoh–Jacob
[
15
]
, Landkof
[
24
]
, and Björn
[
3
]
for the fractional Laplacian, and by Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
for nonlocal nonlinear operators with the standard
p
-growth. As an application of the Wolff potential estimates, we obtain the Wiener criterion for nonlocal Dirichlet problems associated with
\mathcal{L}
. To describe this, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. We say that a boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is
regular
(with respect to
\mathcal{L}
) if, for each function
\vartheta\in V^{s,G}(\Omega)\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the unique harmonic function
u
(with respect to
\mathcal{L}
) in
\Omega
with
u-\vartheta\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies

(1.7)

\lim_{\Omega\ni x\to x_{0}}u(x)=\vartheta(x_{0}).

Note that the Dirichlet data
\vartheta
is imposed in the complement of
\Omega
because we are concerned with the nonlocal operator. See
Section

2
for the definitions of function spaces and harmonic function. Our second main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4

(Wiener criterion)
.

A boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is regular if and only if

(1.8)

\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega,B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}=\infty.

Theorem

1.4
characterizes a regular boundary point in terms of the
(s,G)
-capacity, which is defined in
Section

5
. It says that a boundary point is regular if and only if the complement of
\Omega
near the boundary point has enough
(s,G)
-capacity. It is easy to check that any domain satisfying the exterior ball condition, exterior cone condition, exterior
(\delta,R)
-Reifenberg flat condition, or exterior corkscrew condition satisfies (
1.8
). Moreover,
Theorem

1.4
implies that the regularity of a boundary point is completely determined by the local geometry of
\partial\Omega
. Such locality for nonlocal equations exhibits sharp contrast with other types of nonlocal equations such as nonlocal minimal surface equations. In the case of nonlocal minimal surface equations, the boundary regularity is deeply influenced by nonlocality of the equations; see Dipierro–Savin–Valdinoci
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
for instance.

Remark 1.5
.

Let us make further remarks on
Theorem

1.4
.

(i)

Theorem

1.4
shows that the regularity of a boundary point depends only on
n
,
s
, and
g
, not on the ellipticity
\Lambda
.

(ii)

In the definition of regular boundary point, a Dirichlet data
\vartheta
is not necessarily bounded nor compactly supported. In fact, the proof of
Theorem

1.4
reveals that a boundary point
x_{0}
is regular if and only if for each bounded
\vartheta\in V^{s,G}(\Omega)\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the unique harmonic function
u
with
u-\vartheta\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies (
1.7
).

(iii)

In our previous work
[
18
]
, we proved
Theorem

1.4
when
G(t)=t^{p}
by dividing the cases into two cases
p\leq n/s
and
p>n/s
. However, such distinction is not available in the current framework since we treat operators with Orlicz growth. Instead, we consider two cases based on whether a single point has
(s,G)
-capacity zero or not. For the latter case, we introduce the notion of
(s,G)
-quasicontinuity to describe the regular behavior of
\mathcal{L}
-potential; see
Section

6
for details. Roughly speaking, this alternative viewpoint presents an implicit way to understand the ‘effective dimension’, which is strongly related to
(s,G)
-capacity.

We also point out that the case
p>n/s
in
[
18
]
was treated incorrectly. The Sobolev embedding theorem was used to conclude that the harmonic function with
u-\vartheta\in V^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuous up to the boundary in this case, but the Sobolev embedding theorem requires some regularity of the boundary of
\Omega
. We correct this mistake here in this paper. Note that there is the same mistake in
[
25
]
.

3
and
Section

4

Theorem

1.2
and
Theorem

1.3

[

17
]
and Lukkari–Maeda–Marola
[

]

\mathcal{L}

Section

6
, we establish the Wiener criterion. Some algebraic inequalities can be found in
Appendix

A
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Growth function

In this section, we provide several algebraic inequalities involving growth functions
G
and
g
. One of the simplest inequalities is the following: for any
a,b\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.1)

g(a)b\leq\varepsilon g(a)a+g(b/\varepsilon)b.

The inequality (
2.1
) follows by splitting the cases
b\leq\varepsilon a
and
b>\varepsilon a
.

It turned out in Kim–Lee
[
19
]
that it is convenient to introduce a function
\bar{g}(t)=G(t)/t
because it satisfies

(2.2)

p\bar{g}(t)\leq g(t)\leq q\bar{g}(t),\quad p^{1/(q-1)}g^{-1}(t)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(t)\leq q^{1/(p-1)}g^{-1}(t),

and

(2.3)

(p-1)\bar{g}(t)\leq t\bar{g}^{\prime}(t)\leq(q-1)\bar{g}(t).

It is worth noticing that the condition (
2.3
) with
\bar{g}
replaced by
g
does not follow from the assumption (
1.1
). We import the following lemma from
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
t,t^{\prime}\geq 0
and
c=p^{1/(q-1)}/q^{1/(p-1)}
.

(i)

For all
\lambda\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}G(t)\leq G(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{q}G(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/q}G^{-1}(t)\leq G^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/p}G^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{p-1}\bar{g}(t)\leq\bar{g}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{q-1}\bar{g}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/(q-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/(p-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}\lambda^{p-1}g(t)\leq g(\lambda t)\leq\frac{q}{p}\lambda^{q-1}g(t),

\displaystyle c\lambda^{1/(q-1)}g^{-1}(t)\leq g^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq c^{-1}\lambda^{1/(p-1)}g^{-1}(t).

(ii)

For all
\lambda\leq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lambda^{q}G(t)\leq G(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{p}G(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/p}G^{-1}(t)\leq G^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/q}G^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{q-1}\bar{g}(t)\leq\bar{g}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{p-1}\bar{g}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/(p-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/(q-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}\lambda^{q-1}g(t)\leq g(\lambda t)\leq\frac{q}{p}\lambda^{p-1}g(t),

\displaystyle c\lambda^{1/(p-1)}g^{-1}(t)\leq g^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq c^{-1}\lambda^{1/(q-1)}g^{-1}(t).

(iii)

It holds that

\displaystyle G(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{q}(G(t)+G(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle G^{-1}(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{1/p}(G^{-1}(t)+G^{-1}(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\bar{g}(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{q-1}(\bar{g}(t)+\bar{g}(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{-1}(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{1/(p-1)}(\bar{g}^{-1}(t)+\bar{g}^{-1}(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle g(t+t^{\prime})\leq\frac{q}{p}2^{q-1}(g(t)+g(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle g^{-1}(t+t^{\prime})\leq c^{-1}2^{1/(p-1)}(g^{-1}(t)+g^{-1}(t^{\prime})).

We close this section with the following lemma, which is a generalization of Lemma 3 in Dyda
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following hold:

(i)

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{i}\geq 0
with
i=1,\dots,m
, we have

\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\right)\leq m^{1/(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bar{g}^{-1}(a_{i}).

(ii)

Let
\beta>1
. There exists a constant
C=C(p,q,\beta)>0
such that for any
a_{i}\geq 0
with
i=1,\dots
, we have

g^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\right)\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta^{i}g^{-1}(a_{i}).

Proof.

(i) We may assume without loss of generality that
a_{1}\geq a_{i}
for all
i=2,\dots,m
. By using
Lemma

2.1
(i), we obtain

\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\right)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(ma_{1})\leq m^{1/(p-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(a_{1})\leq m^{1/(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\bar{g}^{-1}(a_{i}).

(ii) By iterating the part (i) with
m=2
, we have

(2.4)

\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\right)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{1/(p-1)}\right)^{k+1}\bar{g}^{-1}(c_{k})

wherever
c_{k}\geq 0
with
k=0,1,\dots
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large so that
2^{\frac{1}{m(p-1)}}\leq\beta
. Then (
2.4
) and the part (i) imply

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}\right)=\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{km+j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{1/(p-1)}\right)^{k+1}\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{km+j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2m)^{1/(p-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{k/(p-1)}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bar{g}^{-1}\left(a_{km+j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2m)^{1/(p-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta^{i}\bar{g}^{-1}(a_{i}).

The desired inequality follows from (
2.2
).
∎

2.2.
Function spaces

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. We define

\displaystyle\varrho_{L^{G}(\Omega)}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}},

\displaystyle\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Then the
Orlicz
and
(fractional) Orlicz–Sobolev spaces
defined by

\displaystyle L^{G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:\varrho_{L^{G}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle W^{s,G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega):\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle V^{s,G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:u|_{\Omega}\in L^{G}(\Omega),\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\right\},

are Banach spaces with the norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{L^{G}(\Omega)}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+[u]_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

respectively. By
W^{s,G}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
we denote the space of functions that belong to
W^{s,G}(D)
for each open set
D\Subset\Omega
. We also define the space

V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{V^{s,G}(\Omega)},

which was denoted by
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
. By
Theorem

2.3
below,
[\cdot]_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}
is a norm on
V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. We refer the reader to
[
18
, Remark 2.2]
for some remarks on these spaces.

Theorem 2.3

(Fractional Poincaré inequality)
.

[
34
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s)>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(\mathrm{diam}^{s}(\Omega)|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

for all
u\in V_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega)
.

In order to study nonlocal equations, it is now standard to introduce the
tail space
, which is defined by

L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{g}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{(1+|x|)^{n+s}}<\infty\right\}

in the Orlicz setting. Equivalently, we can define

L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{g}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)<\infty~\text{for some $x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $R>0$}\right\},

where

\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)=R^{s}g^{-1}\left(R^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\right)

is a
(nonlocal) tail
. Note that
\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)
is finite for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
if
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As we often analyze the integral in the tail, we denote

T(u;x_{0},R):=g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)}{R^{s}}\right)=R^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}

for notational convenience.

2.3.
Supersolutions and superharmonic functions

We recall the definitions and some properties of supersolutions and superharmonic functions defined in Kim–Lee
[
19
]
. Let us begin with supersolutions. For measurable functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we consider a quantity

\mathcal{E}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}v\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Definition 2.4
.

A function
u\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s,G}(\Omega)
with
u_{-}\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
(weak) supersolution
of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
if

(2.5)

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)\geq 0

In

The following theorem shows the solvability of the nonlocal Dirichlet problem for the operator

.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let

in

Let us next provide the definition of a superharmonic function, a subharmonic function, and a harmonic function.

Definition 2.7
.

A measurable function

is a (

-)

in

everywhere in

,

(ii)

,

(iii)

in

,

(iv)

.

A function

is said to be (
\mathcal{L}
-)

if
-u
is superharmonic in
\Omega
.

.

A function

-)

.

2.4.
Local behavior of supersolutions

In this section, we provide local estimates up to the boundary for solutions; more precisely, the local boundedness for subsolutions and the weak Harnack inequality for supersolutions. Such results, which are key ingredients for the proof of both Wolff potential estimates and the Wiener criterion, were developed in
[

]
based on Moser’s iteration technique.

Theorem 2.12

(
[

)
.

Let

be open and let
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})

\varepsilon,p_{0}>0

\esssup_{B_{R/2}}u_{M}^{+}\leq\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{M}^{+};x_{0},R/2)+C\left(\fint_{B_{R}}(u_{M}^{+})^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p_{0}},

where

M=\esssup_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega}u_{+},\quad u_{M}^{+}(x)=\max\{u_{+}(x),M\},

and

.

Theorem 2.13

(
[

)
.

Let

and

. Let

in

\fint_{B_{\tau_{1}R}}g^{\delta}\left(\frac{u_{m}^{-}}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Cg^{\delta}\left(\essinf_{B_{\tau_{2}R}}\frac{u_{m}^{-}}{R^{s}}\right)+Cg^{\delta}\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{m}^{-})_{-};x_{0},R)}{R^{s}}\right),

where

and

.

The choice

in

3.
Potential upper bound

This section is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

1.2
. We begin with the existence and uniqueness of the nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
in
\Omega
such that
\mathcal{L}u=\mu
in
\Omega
in the sense of distribution for a given superharmonic function
u
in
\Omega
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u
be superharmonic in
\Omega
. Then there exists a unique nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
in
\Omega
such that
\mathcal{L}u=\mu
in
\Omega
in the sense of distribution.

The measure
\mu
given in
Theorem

3.1
is called the
Riesz measure
.

Proof.

Note that superharmonic functions do not belong to
W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
in general, but they have some integrability. Indeed,
[
19
, Theorem 6.6]
shows that
u\in L^{g^{\delta}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap W^{\sigma,g^{\alpha}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for any

\delta\in\begin{cases}(0,\frac{n}{n-sp})&\text{if}~sp<n,\\
(0,\infty)&\text{if}~sp\geq n,\end{cases}\quad\sigma\in(0,s),\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\in(0,\min\{\tfrac{n}{n-sp/q},\tfrac{q}{q-1}\}).

Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be nonnegative and let
D\Subset D^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
be such that
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi\subset D
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define
u_{j}=\min\{u,j\}
, which is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u_{j}=0
in
\Omega
by
Theorem

2.10
(ii). Then functions
v_{j}:=g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}|x-y|^{(s-\sigma)(q-1)}
form a bounded sequence in
L^{\alpha}(D^{\prime}\times D^{\prime},\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x)
since

\iint_{D^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}}|v_{j}|^{\alpha}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\iint_{D^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}}g^{\alpha}(|D^{\sigma}u_{j}|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq C\varrho_{W^{\sigma,g^{\alpha}}(D^{\prime})}(u)\leq C,

where
C
is independent of
j
. This together with the convergence

v_{k}\to v:=g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}|x-y|^{(s-\sigma)(q-1)}\quad\text{a.e.\ in }D^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}

shows that
v_{k}
converges weakly to
v
in
L^{\alpha}(D^{\prime}\times D^{\prime},\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x)
. If we take
\sigma\in(0,s)
sufficiently close to
s
so that
s+(s-\sigma)(q-1)<1
, then we have

\iint_{D^{\prime}\times D^{\prime}}\left(|D^{s}\varphi||x-y|^{-(s-\sigma)(q-1)}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\|\varphi\|_{W^{s+(s-\sigma)(q-1),\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(D^{\prime})}^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}<\infty,

and hence

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{D^{\prime}}\int_{D^{\prime}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{D^{\prime}}\int_{D^{\prime}}g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

On the other hand, we observe that

\left|g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right|\leq\left(g\left(\frac{|u_{j}(x)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)+g\left(\frac{|u_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right)\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{D}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{\prime}}g\left(\frac{|u_{j}(x)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\varphi(x)\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D}g(|u(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\int_{D}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{\prime}}g\left(\frac{|u_{j}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\varphi(x)\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{(1+|y|)^{n+s}},

the dominated convergence theorem yields

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{D^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{\prime}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{D^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{\prime}}g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Thus, we deduce from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) that
\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(u_{k},\varphi)\geq 0
. It implies that the assignment
\varphi\to\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)
is a nonnegative distribution. Therefore, it can be represented by a nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
.
∎

We next provide a pointwise upper bound of superharmonic functions in terms of the Wolff potential. We modify the methods used by Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
17
]
and Lukkari–Maeda–Marola
[
28
]
in proving potential upper bounds for local equations. The following estimate can be understood as a De Giorgi-type reverse Hölder inequality with measure data.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\sigma\in(0,\min\{s,n/p\})
and assume

(3.3)

1<\delta<\frac{(p-1)^{\ast}_{\sigma}}{p-1}=\frac{n}{n-\sigma(p-1)}.

Let
u
be a superharmonic function in
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
and let
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
be the Riesz measure. Then there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(n,p,q,s,\sigma,\Lambda,\delta)>0
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}&\leq C_{0}\left(\theta_{R}^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}+\fint_{B_{R}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{C_{0}}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}+T(u_{+};x_{0},R)\right)\end{split}

for any
\lambda>0
, where
v=u_{+}/R^{s}
.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\sigma,\Lambda,\delta)>0
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\fint_{B_{R/2}}\bar{g}^{\delta}\left(\frac{u_{+}}{(R/2)^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\delta}&\leq C\theta_{R}^{\rho_{1}}\left(\fint_{B_{R}}\bar{g}^{\delta}\left(\frac{u_{+}}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\delta}\\
&\quad+C\theta_{R}^{\rho_{2}}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}+T(u_{+};x_{0},R)\right),\end{split}

where
\rho_{1}=\sigma p/(n\delta)
,
\rho_{2}=1/\delta-p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}
, and
\theta_{R}=|B_{R}\cap\{u>0\}|/|B_{R}|
.

Proof.

Since
p<n/\sigma
, we have
p^{\ast}_{\sigma}=np/(n-\sigma p)<\infty
. We first prove the estimate (
3.4
). For

\tilde{\delta}=\frac{p(q-1)\delta}{q-\delta},

it is easy to check that
p<\tilde{\delta}<p^{\ast}_{\sigma}
. We define a nonnegative function

w=\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta/\tilde{\delta}}-1.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}{\bf 1}_{\{0<v<\lambda\}}+\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}{\bf 1}_{\{v\geq\lambda\}}

\displaystyle\leq{\bf 1}_{\{u>0\}}+\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta p^{\ast}_{\sigma}/\tilde{\delta}}{\bf 1}_{\{v\geq\lambda\}}

\displaystyle\leq{\bf 1}_{\{u>0\}}+(w+1)^{p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}{\bf 1}_{\{u>0\}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\bf 1}_{\{u>0\}}+Cw^{p^{\ast}_{\sigma}},

and hence

(3.6)

\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\theta_{R}+C\fint_{B_{R/2}}w^{p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x

for some
C=C(n,p,\sigma)>0
.

We apply the fractional Sobolev inequality to have

(3.7)

\left(\fint_{B_{R/2}}w^{p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}\leq C\fint_{B_{R/2}}w^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+CR^{\sigma p}\fint_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}|D^{\sigma}w|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Since
w\leq(\bar{g}(2\lambda)/\bar{g}(\lambda))^{\delta/\tilde{\delta}}\leq C
on
\{0<v<\lambda\}
and
w\leq C(\bar{g}(v)/\bar{g}(\lambda))^{\delta/\tilde{\delta}}
on
\{v\geq\lambda\}
by
Lemma

2.1
, we obtain

(3.8)

I_{1}\leq C\theta_{R}+C\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For
I_{2}
, we apply
Lemma

A.1
with
\alpha=\delta/\tilde{\delta}
to have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\fint_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{R}\right)^{(s-\sigma)p}\left(\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta p/\tilde{\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right){\bf 1}_{\{v(x)\neq v(y)\}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\fint_{B_{R/2}}\int_{B_{R/2}}G(|D^{s}u_{+}|)\left(\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta p/\tilde{\delta}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{G(\lambda+t)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=:I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}.

Since
{\bf 1}_{\{v(x)>v(y)\}}\leq{\bf 1}_{\{u(x)>0\}}
, we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2,1}&\leq C\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta p/\tilde{\delta}}{\bf 1}_{\{u(x)>0\}}\int_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{|x-y|}{R}\right)^{(s-\sigma)p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq C\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta p/\tilde{\delta}}{\bf 1}_{\{u(x)>0\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\theta_{R}+C\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

To estimate
I_{2,2}
, we define an auxiliary function

\varphi=1-\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+v)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}

and let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{3R/4})
be a cut-off function satisfying
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{R/2}
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/R
. Since
\delta p/\tilde{\delta}-1=-(\delta-1)/(q-1)
, we have

I_{2,2}\leq\frac{C}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\fint_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G(|D^{s}u_{+}|)\left(\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda+t}\right)(\eta(x)\land\eta(y))^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

By using
\varphi\eta^{q}
as a test function in
\mathcal{L}u=\mu
and applying
Lemma

A.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\fint_{B_{R}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}{\bf 1}_{\{u(x)>0\}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}

\displaystyle\quad-C\frac{R^{s}}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\fint_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\frac{\varphi(x)\eta^{q}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta_{R}+C\fint_{B_{R}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\frac{R^{s}}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\fint_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}g\left(\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right){\bf 1}_{\{u(x)>0\}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

Since
|y-x_{0}|\leq|y-x|+3R/4\leq 4|x-y|
for
x\in B_{3R/4}(x_{0})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})
, we have

(3.10)

I_{2,2}\leq C\theta_{R}+C\fint_{B_{R}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}+T(u_{+};x_{0},R)\right).

Therefore, the claim (
3.4
) follows by combining (
3.6
)–(
3.10
) and using
\theta_{R}\leq\theta_{R}^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}
.

Let us next deduce (
3.5
) from (
3.4
). We set

\displaystyle A_{R}=\left(\fint_{B_{R}}\bar{g}^{\delta}\left(\frac{u_{+}}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\delta}

and
M=1+C_{0}
. If we choose
\lambda
so that

\frac{1}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}=2^{s(q-1)}M^{p^{\ast}_{\sigma}/(\delta p)}\theta_{R}^{1/\delta}A_{R/2}^{-1},

then it follows from
Lemma

2.1
and (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\theta_{R}^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{s(1-q)}\frac{A_{R/2}}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}-C_{0}\theta_{R}^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\fint_{B_{R/2}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(v)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}-C_{0}\theta_{R}^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\left(\frac{A_{R}}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}+\frac{1}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}+\frac{T(u_{+};x_{0},R)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\theta_{R}\left(\frac{A_{R}}{A_{R/2}}\right)^{\delta}+\theta_{R}^{1/\delta}A_{R/2}^{-1}\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}+\theta_{R}^{1/\delta}A_{R/2}^{-1}T(u_{+};x_{0},R)\right).

This implies that

\frac{1}{3}\theta_{R}^{p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}}\leq\left(C_{1}\theta_{R}\left(\frac{A_{R}}{A_{R/2}}\right)^{\delta}\right)\lor\left(C_{1}\theta_{R}^{1/\delta}A_{R/2}^{-1}\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}\right)\lor\left(C_{1}\theta_{R}^{1/\delta}A_{R/2}^{-1}T(u_{+};x_{0},R)\right).

In other words, we have

A_{R/2}\leq\left((3C_{1})^{1/\delta}\theta_{R}^{\rho_{1}}A_{R}\right)\lor\left(3C_{1}\theta_{R}^{\rho_{2}}\frac{\mu(B_{R})}{R^{n-s}}\right)\lor\left(3C_{1}\theta_{R}^{\rho_{2}}T(u_{+};x_{0},R)\right),

where
\rho_{1}=(1-p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma})/\delta=\sigma p/(n\delta)>0
and
\rho_{2}=1/\delta-p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma}>0
. Hence, the desired estimate (
3.5
) follows.
∎

We prove
Theorem

1.2
by iterating
Lemma

3.2
and using the weak Harnack inequality (
Theorem

2.13
).

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

By
Theorem

3.1
there exists a unique nonnegative Borel measure
\mu
in
\Omega
such that
\mathcal{L}u=\mu
in
\Omega
in the sense of distirubition.

For
j=0,1,\cdots
, we set
R_{j}=2^{-j-1}R
and
B^{j}=B_{R_{j}}
. Let
\sigma\in(0,\min\{s,n/p\})
and let
\delta
be any constant satisfying (

). For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
to be determined later, we define
l_{0}=0
and

l_{j+1}=l_{j}+R_{j}^{s}\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\fint_{B^{j}}\bar{g}^{\delta}\left(\frac{(u-l_{j})_{+}}{R_{j}^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\delta}\right)\quad\text{for}~j=0,1,\cdots,

and set

for

Note that we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta_{j}&\leq\frac{1}{|B^{j-1}|}\int_{B^{j-1}\cap\{u>l_{j}\}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}((u-l_{j-1})_{+}/R^{s}_{j-1})}{\bar{g}((l_{j}-l_{j-1})/R^{s}_{j-1})}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{1}{|B^{j-1}|}\int_{B^{j-1}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}((u-l_{j-1})_{+}/R^{s}_{j-1})}{\bar{g}((l_{j}-l_{j-1})/R^{s}_{j-1})}\right)^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x=\varepsilon^{\delta}.\end{split}

By applying
Lemma

3.2
with
u-l_{j}
in
B^{j-1}
for
j\geq 1
and using (
3.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{l_{j+1}-l_{j}}{R_{j}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\rho_{1}\delta}\bar{g}\left(\frac{l_{j}-l_{j-1}}{R_{j-1}^{s}}\right)+C\varepsilon^{\rho_{2}\delta-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j-1})}{R_{j-1}^{n-s}}+T((u-l_{j})_{+};x_{0},R_{j-1})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\rho_{1}\delta}\bar{g}\left(\frac{l_{j}-l_{j-1}}{R_{j-1}^{s}}\right)+C\varepsilon^{\rho_{2}\delta-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j-1})}{R_{j-1}^{n-s}}+2^{-sj}T(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0})+T_{j}\right),

where

T_{j}=R_{j-1}^{s}\int_{B^{0}\setminus B^{j-1}}g\left(\frac{(u(x)-l_{j})_{+}}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}.

Note that we have

\displaystyle T_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=2}^{j}\frac{R_{j-1}^{s}}{R_{j-i+1}^{n+s}}\int_{B^{j-i}\setminus B^{j-i+1}}g\left(\frac{(u(x)-l_{j})_{+}}{R_{j-i+1}^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-si}\fint_{B^{j-i}}g\left(\frac{(u(x)-l_{j})_{+}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-si}\left(\fint_{B^{j-i}}g^{\delta}\left(\frac{(u(x)-l_{j})_{+}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\delta}\left(\frac{|B^{j-i}\cap\{u>l_{j}\}|}{|B^{j-i}|}\right)^{1-1/\delta}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-si}\varepsilon\bar{g}\left(\frac{l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right)\theta_{j-i+1}^{1-1/\delta}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\delta}\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-si}g\left(\frac{l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right)

by using Hölder’s inequality, (
2.2
), and (
3.11
). We apply
Lemma

2.2
(ii) with
\beta=2^{\frac{s}{2(q-1)}}
and use
Lemma

2.1
to deduce

\displaystyle T_{j}\leq C\varepsilon^{\delta}g\left(\sum_{i=2}^{j}\beta^{i}g^{-1}\left(2^{-si}g\left(\frac{l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right)\right)\right)\leq C\varepsilon^{\delta}g\left(\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}\frac{l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right).

We have obtained

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{l_{j+1}-l_{j}}{R_{j}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\rho_{1}\delta}\bar{g}\left(\frac{l_{j}-l_{j-1}}{R_{j-1}^{s}}\right)+C\varepsilon^{\rho_{2}\delta-1+\delta}g\left(\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}\frac{l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i}}{R_{j-i}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon^{\rho_{2}\delta-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j-1})}{R_{j-1}^{n-s}}+2^{-sj}T(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0})\right).

Recall that
\rho_{2}\delta-1+\delta=\delta(1-p/p^{\ast}_{\sigma})>0
. By taking
\bar{g}^{-1}
on both sides and using
Lemma

2.1
, we have

\displaystyle l_{j+1}-l_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{1}\delta}{q-1}}(l_{j}-l_{j-1})+C\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{2}\delta-1+\delta}{q-1}}\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}(l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i})

\displaystyle\quad+C(\varepsilon)\left(R_{j}^{s}\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j-1})}{R_{j-1}^{n-s}}\right)+2^{-\frac{sq}{q-1}j}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0})\right),

which implies that for
k\geq 2

\displaystyle l_{k}

\displaystyle=l_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(l_{j+1}-l_{j})

\displaystyle\leq l_{1}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{1}\delta}{q-1}}l_{k-1}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{2}\delta-1+\delta}{q-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}(l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i})

\displaystyle\quad+C(\varepsilon)\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(R^{s}_{j}\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j-1})}{R_{j-1}^{n-s}}\right)+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0})\right).

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}(l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i})=\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}\sum_{j=i}^{k-1}(l_{j-i+1}-l_{j-i})\leq\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i}l_{k}\leq Cl_{k}

and
\sum_{j}2^{-\frac{sq}{q-1}j}\leq C
, we obtain

l_{k}\leq l_{1}+C\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{1}\delta}{q-1}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{2}\delta-1+\delta}{q-1}}\right)l_{k}+C(\varepsilon)\left({\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,G}(x_{0},R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0})\right).

We now take
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
sufficiently small so that
C(\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{1}\delta}{q-1}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{\rho_{2}\delta-1+\delta}{q-1}})<1/2
, then

l_{k}\leq 2l_{1}+C\,{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,G}(x_{0},R)+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0}).

Note that (
3.11
) implies
\inf_{B^{k-1}}u\leq l_{k}
since
\varepsilon^{\delta}<1
. By the lower semicontinuity of
u
, we have

\displaystyle u(x_{0})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}l_{k}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{s}\bar{g}^{-1}\left(\left(\fint_{B_{R/2}}\bar{g}^{\delta}\left(\frac{u_{+}}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\delta}\right)+C\,\mathbf{W}_{s,G}^{\mu}(x_{0},R)+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{+};x_{0},R_{0}).

Finally, since
\delta<n/(n-\sigma(p-1))<n/(n-sp)
, an application of
Theorem

2.13
together with
Lemma

2.1
finishes the proof.
∎

4.
Potential lower bound

In this section, we prove the lower bound of the Wolff potential estimate. We adapt the method used by Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
16
]
to nonlocal equations with nonstandard growth. We point out that our result is new even for nonlocal equations with standard growth, as previously it was only known for SOLA when
2-s/n<p<n/s
; see Kuusi–Mingione–Sire
[
22
]
.

Let us first provide some lemmas. The first lemma is a Caccioppoli-type estimate, which is well known in the literature. See Byun–Kim–Ok
[
5
]
and Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[
6
]
for instance.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R})
be nonnegative and let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}
. There exists a constant
C=C(p,q,\Lambda)>0
such that for any
l\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G(|D^{s}w_{-}|)(\eta(x)\lor\eta(y))^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G\left(\frac{w_{-}(x)\lor w_{-}(y)}{R^{s}}\right)(R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}g\left(\frac{w_{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where
w=u-l
.

The next lemma is a nonlocal analogue of
[
16
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}
. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{R})
be a function satisfying
|\nabla\eta|\leq c/R
. Then

(4.1)

\fint_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}g(|D^{s}u|)R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq Cg\left(\inf_{B_{R/2}}\frac{u}{R^{s}}\right)+C\,T(u_{-};x_{0},R)

for some constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,c)>0
.

Proof.

For

(4.2)

d=\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R),

we define a function
v=(u+d)/R^{s}
and set
m=\inf_{B_{R/2}}v
. Let
A
denote the left-hand side of (
4.1
) and let

(4.3)

0<\tau<1\land\frac{sp}{(q-1)(n-sp)_{+}}.

Here,
1/(n-sp)_{+}
is understood as
\infty
when
sp\geq n
. By applying the inequality (
2.1
) with

a=|D^{s}u|,\quad b=R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|,\quad\varepsilon=\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t\bar{g}^{\tau}(t)},

and using (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\leq q\fint_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}\varepsilon G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+\fint_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}g\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|\right)R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

The Caccioppoli estimate (see
[
19
, Lemma 3.6 (i)]
for instance) shows that

I_{1}\leq C\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)\left(1+\frac{T((u+d)_{-};x_{0},7R/8)}{g(d/(7R/8)^{s})}\right)\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1-\tau}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x

for some
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,\tau)>0
. Note that the dependence of
C
on
\tau
can be removed by the choice of
\tau
. Since
(u+d)_{-}\leq u_{-}
and
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}
, we obtain by using (
2.2
) and recalling (
4.2
) that

T((u+d)_{-};x_{0},7R/8)\leq CT(u_{-};x_{0},R)\leq C\bar{g}(d/R^{s}).

Thus, we have

(4.4)

I_{1}\leq C\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1-\tau}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Let us next estimate
I_{2}
. Since

R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|\leq R^{s}|\nabla\eta||x-y|^{1-s}\leq c\frac{|x-y|^{1-s}}{R^{1-s}}\leq 2c,

we have by using
Lemma

2.1

I_{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}g\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\left(\frac{R^{s}}{2c}|D^{s}\eta|\right)^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

for some
C=C(p,q,c)>0
. Note that we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq\frac{\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)}{v(x)\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(x))\lor v(y)\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(y))}\geq\frac{1}{v(x)\lor v(y)}\frac{\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)}{\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(x))\lor\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(y))},

and hence

g\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\leq C\bar{g}(v(x)\lor v(y))\left(\left(\frac{\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(x))\lor\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(y))}{\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)}\right)^{p-1}\lor\left(\frac{\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(x))\lor\bar{g}^{\tau}(v(y))}{\bar{g}^{\tau}(m)}\right)^{q-1}\right)

by (
2.2
) and
Lemma

2.1
. This leads us to

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\fint_{B_{3R/4}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(p-1)}(v(x))}{\bar{g}^{\tau(p-1)}(m)}\lor\frac{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(q-1)}(v(x))}{\bar{g}^{\tau(q-1)}(m)}\right)\int_{B_{3R/4}}R^{sp}|D^{s}\eta|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{\bar{g}^{\tau(p-1)}(m)}\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1+\tau(p-1)}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{\bar{g}^{\tau(q-1)}(m)}\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1+\tau(q-1)}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) yields

A\leq C\left(\frac{\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1-\tau}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\bar{g}^{1-\tau}(m)}+\frac{\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1+\tau(p-1)}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(p-1)}(m)}+\frac{\fint_{B_{7R/8}}\bar{g}^{1+\tau(q-1)}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x}{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(q-1)}(m)}\right)\bar{g}(m).

We want to apply the weak Harnack inequality (
Theorem

2.13
) to
u+d
with
\delta=1-\tau
,
\delta=1+\tau(p-1)
, and
\delta=1+\tau(q-1)
. Indeed, these values are admissible by the choice (
4.3
) of
\tau
. Therefore,
Theorem

2.13
with
\tau_{1}=7/8
and
\tau_{2}=1/2
yields

A\leq C\left(1+\frac{\bar{g}^{1-\tau}(T)}{\bar{g}^{1-\tau}(m)}+\frac{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(p-1)}(T)}{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(p-1)}(m)}+\frac{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(q-1)}(T)}{\bar{g}^{1+\tau(q-1)}(m)}\right)\bar{g}(m),

where
T=R^{-s}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u+d)_{-};x_{0},R)
. Since
T\leq d/R^{s}\leq m
, we conclude

A\leq C\bar{g}(m)\leq Cg\left(\inf_{B_{R/2}}\frac{u}{R^{s}}\right)+Cg\left(\frac{d}{R^{s}}\right)

by using (
2.2
) and
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

We now take the measure data into account.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u
be a superharmonic function in
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}
and let
\mu=\mathcal{L}u
be the Riesz measure, then

\frac{\mu(B_{R/2})}{R^{n-s}}\leq Cg\left(\frac{\inf_{B_{R/2}}u-\inf_{B_{R}}u}{R^{s}}\right)+Cg\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u-\inf_{B_{R}}u)_{-};x_{0},R)}{R^{s}}\right)

for some
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda)>0
.

Proof.

By considering
\tilde{u}=u-\inf_{B_{R}}u
, we may assume
\inf_{B_{R}}u=0
. Let
b=\inf_{B_{R/2}}u
and define
v=u\land b
, then

(4.6)

0\leq v\leq b\quad\text{in}~B_{R}\quad\text{and}\quad v=b\quad\text{on}~B_{R/2}.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{5R/8})
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{R/2}
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/R
. By using

as a test function, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}v\,\eta^{q}(x)\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}\eta^{q}\,v(y)\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3R/4}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

For

, we observe that

(4.7)

g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}v\leq g(|D^{s}u|)|D^{s}u|.

Indeed, one may assume
u(x)>u(y)
and easily check the inequality (
4.7
) for the cases

,
u(x)>b\geq u(y)
, and

) and (

), we obtain

I_{1}\leq q\Lambda\int_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}G(|D^{s}w_{-}|)\eta^{q}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}},

where
w(x)=u(x)-b
. We apply
Lemma

4.1
to
w
with
l=b
and use
Lemma

2.1
, then

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}G\left(\frac{w_{-}(x)\lor w_{-}(y)}{(3R/4)^{s}}\right)((3R/4)^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\int_{B_{3R/4}}w_{-}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\sup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\eta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3R/4}}g\left(\frac{w_{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{R}}G\left(\frac{w_{-}}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{R^{s}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)T(w_{-};x_{0},R).

4.2

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{b}{R^{s}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}\int_{B_{3R/4}}g(|D^{s}u|)R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle I_{3}

where

.

Let us prove
Theorem

1.3
by applying

4.3
iteratively.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Let
R_{j}=2^{-j}R
,

, and

for

a_{k}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j}-a_{j-1})\geq c\sum_{j=1}^{k}R_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j})}{R_{j}^{n-s}}\right)-C\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}

for some

,

,

,

, and

, where

T_{j}=\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u-a_{j-1})_{-};x_{0},R_{j-1}).

To estimate the tail terms, we repeat the method exploited in the proof of
Theorem

1.2
. Indeed, we observe that for
j=1,\cdots,k
,

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{T_{j}}{R_{j-1}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=R_{j-1}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}}g\left(\frac{(u(y)-a_{j-1})_{-}}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\quad+R_{j-1}^{s}\sum_{i=2}^{j}\int_{B^{j-i}\setminus B^{j-i+1}}g\left(\frac{(u(y)-a_{j-1})_{-}}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-sj}g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R)}{R^{s}}\right)+C2^{-sj}g\left(\frac{a_{j-1}}{R^{s}}\right)+C\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-si}g\left(\frac{a_{j-1}-a_{j-i}}{R_{j-i+1}^{s}}\right).

Hence, by applying
Lemma

2.2
(i) and then (ii) with
\beta=2^{\frac{s}{2(q-1)}}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-\frac{sq}{q-1}j}(\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R)+a_{k})+CR_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-si}g\left(\frac{a_{j-1}-a_{j-i}}{R_{j-i+1}^{s}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-\frac{sq}{q-1}j}(\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R)+a_{k})+C\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i-si}(a_{j-1}-a_{j-i}).

Since

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=2}^{j}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i-si}(a_{j-1}-a_{j-i})=\sum_{i=2}^{k}2^{-\frac{s}{2(q-1)}i-si}\sum_{j=i}^{k}(a_{j-1}-a_{j-i})\leq Ca_{k},

we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{k}T_{j}\leq Ca_{k}+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R),

which leads us to

\sum_{j=1}^{k}R_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j})}{R_{j}^{n-s}}\right)\leq Ca_{k}+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R).

By taking the limit
k\to\infty
, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\bf W}^{\mu}_{s,G}(x_{0},R/2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{R_{j+1}}^{R_{j}}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-s}}\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho^{1-s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}R_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu(B^{j})}{R_{j}^{n-s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R)

\displaystyle=Cu(x_{0})+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R),

where
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda)>0
.
∎

5.
(s,G)
-capacity and
\mathcal{L}
-potential

We introduce several potential theoretical tools such as
(s,G)
-capacity and the
\mathcal{L}
-potential in our framework. They play a crucial role in the development of nonlocal nonlinear potential theory with Orlicz growth. We provide several fundamental properties of the them.

5.1.
(s,G)
-capacity

We begin with the definition of
(s,G)
-capacity.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set. The
(s,G)
-capacity of
K
with respect to
\Omega
is defined by

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K,\Omega)=\inf_{v\in W(K,\Omega)}\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(v),

where
W(K,\Omega):=\{v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega):\text{$v\geq 1$ on $K$}\}
. If
U\subset\Omega
is open, we define

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(U,\Omega)=\sup_{K\subset U~\text{compact}}\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K,\Omega),

and for an arbitrary set
E\subset\Omega
,

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E,\Omega)=\sup_{E\subset U\subset\Omega,\ \text{$U$ open}}\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(U,\Omega).

Obviously,
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\cdot,\cdot)
is increasing in the first argument and decreasing in the second argument with respect to the set inclusion. Moreover, by approximation, the set
W(K,\Omega)
in
Definition

5.1
can be replaced by

W_{0}(K,\Omega):=\overline{W(K,\Omega)}^{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

A function in
W_{0}(K,\Omega)
is said to be
admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K,\Omega)
.

Recall that the
(s,p)
-capacity in the standard
p
-growth case inherits some properties of
p
-capacity with weight
|t|^{p-1-sp}
by an identification of these two capacities; see Björn
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
, Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
, Lemma 2.17]
, and Maz’ya
[
32
, Theorem 1, p. 512]
for instance. However, to the best of our knowledge, such an identification is not available for the
(s,G)
-capacity in the literature. Therefore, we develop the theory by taking more elementary and direct approach.

Theorem 5.2

(Basic properties of
(s,G)
-capacity)
.

The following hold.

(i)

If
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are compact subsets of
\Omega
, then

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},\Omega)+\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1}\cap K_{2},\Omega)\leq\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1},\Omega)+\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{2},\Omega).

(ii)

If
\{K_{i}\}_{i}
is a decreasing sequence of compact subsets of
\Omega
with
K=\cap_{i}K_{i}
, then

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K,\Omega)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{i},\Omega).

(iii)

(Subadditivity) If
E=\cup_{i}E_{i}\subset\Omega
, then

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E,\Omega)\leq\sum_{i}\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E_{i},\Omega).

Theorem

5.2
says that
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}
is a
Choquet capacity
. See Theorem 2.5 in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
14
]
for instance.

Proof.

(i) Let
u_{1}\in W(K_{1},\Omega)
and
u_{2}\in W(K_{2},\Omega)
. An application of
Lemma

A.3
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}(u_{1}\lor u_{2})|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}(u_{1}\land u_{2})|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u_{1}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u_{2}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Since
u_{1}\lor u_{2}
and
u_{1}\land u_{2}
are admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},\Omega)
and
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1}\cap K_{2},\Omega)
, respectively, we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1}\cup K_{2},\Omega)+\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K_{1}\cap K_{2},\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u_{1}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u_{2}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

The desired inequality follows by taking the infimum over all admissible functions
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
.

Since the proofs of (ii) and (iii) are essentially independent of the nonlocal structure of operators, we refer the reader to the proofs of Theorem 2.2 and Lemma 2.3 in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
14
]
.
∎

The following lemma provides an estimate of the
(s,G)
-capacity of a closed ball
\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
with respect to a concentric ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R/8\leq r\leq R/2
. There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s)\geq 1
such that

C^{-1}r^{n}G(r^{-s})\leq\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})},B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq Cr^{n}G(r^{-s}).

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
. For the upper bound, we choose a cut-off function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2r})
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{r}
, and
|D\eta|\leq C/r
. Since
\eta
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})
, we have

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}\eta|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Moreover, the right-hand side of the display above is estimated as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}\eta|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq Cr^{n}G(r^{-s})

by using
|D^{s}\eta|\leq C(|x-y|/r)^{1-s}r^{-s}
,
|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|\leq 1
, and
Lemma

2.1
. This implies the upper bound.

For the lower bound, we let
u_{j}\in W(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})
be such that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})+1/j\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we obtain, for any
\sigma\in(0,s)
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})+1/j

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}}G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{|x-y|^{\sigma p}}{(3R)^{\sigma p}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{R^{\sigma p}}{|x-y|^{\sigma p}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle=CR^{n}\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{2R}}H(|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}+R^{n}\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{2R}^{c}}H(|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2},

where we set

\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}=\frac{|x-y|^{\sigma p-n}}{R^{n+\sigma p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,\quad\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}=\frac{R^{\sigma p-n}}{|x-y|^{n+\sigma p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y,

and
H(t):=G(t^{1/p})
. Note that
\mu_{1}(B_{R}\times B_{2R}),\mu_{2}(B_{R}\times B_{2R}^{c})<\infty
. Therefore, by applying the Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})+1/j

\displaystyle\geq CR^{n}H\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{2R}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}\right)+CR^{n}H\left(\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{2R}^{c}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq CR^{n}H\left(R^{-n-\sigma p}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}}|D^{s-\sigma}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+CR^{n}H\left(R^{-n+\sigma p}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}^{c}}|D^{s+\sigma}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\right).

On the other hand, since
u_{j}
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})
, we obtain

\displaystyle CR^{n-sp}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}^{c}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

The first inequality follows from Lemma 2.17 in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
. Hence, at least one of the following holds:

(5.1)

\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\geq\frac{C}{4}R^{n-sp}

or

(5.2)

\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}^{c}}|D^{s}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\geq\frac{C}{4}R^{n-sp}.

Suppose that (
5.1
) holds. Since the map

\theta\mapsto R^{\theta p-n}\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}}|D^{\theta}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

is continuous at
\theta=s
, we deduce that, for sufficiently small
\sigma>0
,

\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{2R}}|D^{s-\sigma}u_{j}|^{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\geq\frac{C}{8}R^{n-(s-\sigma)p},

which implies that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})+1/j\geq CR^{n}H(R^{-sp})=CR^{n}G(R^{-s})\geq Cr^{n}G(r^{-s}).

The case when (
5.2
) holds can be considered in a similar way. Letting
j\to\infty
finishes the proof.
∎

Sets having
(s,G)
-capacity zero play a crucial role in the development of the theory. Let us provide the precise definition.

Definition 5.4
.

A set
E
is said to be of
(s,G)
-capacity zero
, or to
have
(s,G)
-capacity zero
, if

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E\cap\Omega,\Omega)=0

for all open sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case, we write
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}E=0
. We write
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}E>0
if
E
is not of capacity zero.

The next lemma shows that one needs to test only a single bounded open set
\Omega
containing
E
in showing that
E
has
(s,G)
-capacity zero.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
E
be bounded and suppose that there exists a bounded neighborhood
\Omega
of
E
with
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E,\Omega)=0
. Then
E
has
(s,G)
-capacity zero.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}
be any open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By following the argument in the proof of Lemma 2.9 in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
14
]
, we may assume that
E\cap\Omega^{\prime}
is compact. Let
u\in W(E\cap\Omega^{\prime},\Omega)
and
v\in W(E\cap\Omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})
, then
uv\in W(E\cap\Omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})
, and so

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E\cap\Omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}(uv)|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G(|u(x)||D^{s}v|)+G(|v(y)||D^{s}u|))\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G(|u(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

The fractional Poincaré inequality (
Theorem

2.3
) shows that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E\cap\Omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Since the last integral can be chosen to be arbitrarily small, we conclude
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E\cap\Omega^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})=0
.
∎

In potential theory, the notion of capacity is used to refine the almost everywhere equivalence of Sobolev functions. For instance, for each
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
there exists a function
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
v=u
a.e. in
\Omega
and that
v
is continuous when restricted to a set whose complement has arbitrarily small
p
-capacity; see Theorem 4.5 in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Malý
[
14
]
. We extend the theory to fractional Sobolev functions in
V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. For this purpose, we provide several definitions.

Definition 5.6
.

(i)

A function
u:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty]
is said to be
(s,G)
-quasicontinuous in
\Omega
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an open set
D\subset\Omega
such that
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D,\Omega)<\varepsilon
and that
u|_{\Omega\setminus D}
is finite valued and continuous.

(ii)

A sequence of functions
\psi_{j}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is said to converge
(s,G)
-quasiuniformly in
\Omega
to a function
\psi
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an open set
D\subset\Omega
such that
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D,\Omega)<\varepsilon
and
\psi_{j}\to\psi
uniformly in
\Omega\setminus D
as
j\to\infty
.

(iii)

We say that a property holds
(s,G)
-quasieverywhere
or simply q.e. if it holds except on a set of
(s,G)
-capacity zero.

Notice that we do not introduce the so-called Sobolev capacity in
Definition

5.6
, but we use condenser capacity
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}
. This is because we are only interested in
u\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
whose norm is comparable to
[u]_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}
by
Theorem

2.3
. In order to establish a similar result for
u\in V^{s,G}(\Omega)
, one would need to introduce the Sobolev capacity.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\psi_{j}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a Cauchy sequence in
V^{s,G}(\Omega)
. There exists a subsequence
\psi_{j_{k}}
and a function
v\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
, which is
(s,G)
-quasicontinuous in
\Omega
, such that
\psi_{j_{k}}
converges
(s,G)
-quasiuniformly in
\Omega
and pointwise q.e. in
\Omega
to
v
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\psi_{j}
is a Cauchy sequence in
V^{s,G}(\Omega)
, there exists a subsequence, which we still denote by
\psi_{j}
, such that the series

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(2^{j}|D^{s}(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+1})|\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

converges. Thus, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
j_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sum_{j=j_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(2^{j}|D^{s}(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+1})|\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

We define
\varphi_{j}=2^{j}(\psi_{j}-\psi_{j+1})
and set

\displaystyle E_{j}^{+}:=\{x\in\Omega:\varphi_{j}(x)>1\},

\displaystyle E_{j}^{-}:=\{x\in\Omega:\varphi_{j}(x)<-1\}.

Then by definition of
(s,G)
-capacity, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E_{j}^{+},\Omega)\leq\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(\varphi_{j})\leq 2\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(\varphi_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E_{j}^{-},\Omega)\leq\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(-\varphi_{j})\leq 2\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(\varphi_{j}).

By putting

E_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{j=j_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\left(E_{j}^{+}\cup E_{j}^{-}\right)

and applying
Theorem

5.2
(iii), we obtain that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(E_{\varepsilon},\Omega)\leq 4\sum_{j=j_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(\varphi_{j})<\varepsilon.

Moreover, since

|\psi_{j}-\psi_{k}|\leq\sum_{l=j}^{k-1}2^{-l}\leq 2^{1-j}\quad\text{in $\Omega\setminus E_{\varepsilon}$}

for
k\geq j\geq j_{\varepsilon}
,
\psi_{j}
converges uniformly in
\Omega\setminus E_{\varepsilon}
. Clearly, it also converges q.e. in
\Omega
to a function
u
which is
(s,G)
-quasicontinuous in
\Omega
.
∎

As a consequence of
Theorem

5.7
we obtain the following theorem, which enables us to analyze the pointwise behavior of Sobolev functions.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
u\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. There exists a function
v\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
v
is
(s,G)
-quasicontinuous in
\Omega
and
u=v
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

5.2.
\mathcal{L}
-potential

We define the
\mathcal{L}
-potential by using
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic functions.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set. Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]
be such that
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\psi=1
on
K
. The
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic function in
\Omega\setminus K
with
u-\psi\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega\setminus K)
is called the
\mathcal{L}
-potential of
K
in
\Omega
and denoted by
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
.

By the comparison principle (see Lemma 5.2 in Kim–Lee
[
19
]
for instance), it is immediate that the definition of
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is independent of the particular choice of
\psi
.

We now propose useful properties of the
\mathcal{L}
-potential. For this purpose, we introduce a generalized notion of
(s,G)
-capacity associated with the operator
\mathcal{L}
, namely,

\mathcal{C}(K,\Omega):=\inf_{v\in W(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}(v),

where

\mathcal{E}(v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}v|)\,\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Note that
\mathcal{C}(K,\Omega)=\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K,\Omega)
when
k(x,y)\equiv 1
. By modifying Lemma 2.16 in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
, one can easily obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and
K\subset\Omega
be a compact set.

(i)

\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(K,\Omega)
. In particular,
\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)\in W_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

Let
\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega):=\{v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega):v=1\text{ in a neighborhood of }K\}
, then

\mathcal{C}(K,\Omega)=\inf_{v\in\widetilde{W}(K,\Omega)}\mathcal{E}(v).

(iii)

\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}
among all functions belonging to
W_{0}(K,\Omega)
, i.e.,

\mathcal{E}(\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega))=\mathcal{C}(K,\Omega).

(iv)

\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
.

We close the section with the following lemma, which investigates the pointwise behavior of the
\mathcal{L}
-potentials.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and

has a representative
v
, which is

-quasicontinuous in

. Moreover,

.

Proof.

It follows from

6.
Wiener criterion

In this section, we establish the Wiener criterion,
Theorem

1.4
, which provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a boundary point to be regular. We mainly follow the argument presented in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
and use regularity estimates obtained in
Section

2
and
Section

3
to prove
Theorem

1.4
.

6.1.
The sufficient condition

In this section, the proof of the sufficiency part of
Theorem

1.4
is provided, namely, we prove that a boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is regular if the Wiener integral diverges. Here, the Wiener integral refers to the integral on the left-hand side of (
1.8
).

Throughout the section, we fix a boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and assume that the Wiener integral diverges. Let
\vartheta\in V^{s,G}(\Omega)\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u
be the unique harmonic function in
\Omega
such that
u-\vartheta\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
u=\vartheta
a.e. outside
\Omega
, we may assume that
u\equiv\vartheta
outside
\Omega
. We will prove that the equality (
1.7
) holds, or equivalently,

(6.1)

\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u\leq\vartheta(x_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\rho\to 0}\inf_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u\geq\vartheta(x_{0}).

It is enough to prove the first inequality of (
6.1
) by symmetry. We argue by contradiction; let us assume

L:=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\sup_{\Omega\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u>\vartheta(x_{0}).

We choose any
l\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\vartheta(x_{0})<l<L
, then the continuity of
\vartheta
shows that there exists a sufficiently small radius
r_{\ast}>0
such that
l\geq\sup_{D_{r}(x_{0})}\vartheta
for any
r\in(0,r_{\ast})
, where

(6.2)

D_{r}(x_{0}):=\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega.

Let
M_{l}(r):=\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(u-l)_{+}
, then it follows from
\lim_{r\to 0}M_{l}(r)=L-l>0
that
M_{l}(r)\geq L-l>0
for any
r>0
. We now define a function

u_{l,r}:=M_{l}(r)-(u-l)_{+},

which is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u_{l,r}=0
in
\Omega
. Note that
(u_{l,r})_{m}^{-}=u_{l,r}
since
(u-l)_{+}=0
in
B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega
. We begin with the following lemma, which is obtained by using a Caccioppoli-type estimate and the weak Harnack inequality.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,L-l)>0
such that

(6.3)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}(x_{0})}G(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq C\rho^{n-s}\left(g\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)+g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)\right)\end{split}

for any
\rho\in(0,r_{\ast}/4)
.

Proof.

We write
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
for simplicity. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{13\rho/8})
be a cut-off function satisfying
\eta\equiv 1
on
\overline{B_{3\rho/2}}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/\rho
. Since
u_{l,4\rho}
is a supersolution in
\Omega
, we obtain by using
\varphi=(u-l)_{+}\eta^{q}\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
as a test function

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}}{|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+2\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}}{|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|}\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{split}

The equality
\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)=(u(x)-l)_{+}(\eta^{q}(x)-\eta^{q}(y))-(u_{l,4\rho}(x)-u_{l,4\rho}(y))\eta^{q}(y)
yields

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}G(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq C\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\frac{(u(x)-l)_{+}}{M_{l}(4\rho)}|D^{s}\eta^{q}|\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad+C\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{7\rho/4}}g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}}{|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|}\frac{\varphi(x)}{M_{l}(4\rho)|x-y|^{s}}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

Thus, it is enough to estimate
I_{1}+I_{2}
by the right-hand side of (
6.3
).

Let us first estimate
I_{1}
. Since
(u(x)-l)_{+}\leq M_{l}(4\rho)
and
|\eta^{q}(x)-\eta^{q}(y)|\leq C\rho^{-1}|x-y|
, we estimate
I_{1}
as

I_{1}\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{s}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho}\right)^{1-s}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

We define
v=(u_{l,4\rho}+d)/\rho^{s}
, with
d>0
to be determined later, and let
m=\inf_{B_{\rho}}v
. By applying (
2.1
) with
a=|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|
,
b=(|x-y|/\rho)^{1-s}
, and

\varepsilon=\bar{g}^{-\beta}(m)\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\frac{\bar{g}^{\beta}(t)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t,

we obtain for any
\beta\in(-1,0)

g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho}\right)^{1-s}\leq q\bar{g}^{-\beta}(m)G(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\frac{\bar{g}^{\beta}(t)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+Cg(\varepsilon^{-1})\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho}\right)^{(1-s)p},

where
C=C(p,q)
is a positive constant. Since

\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\frac{\bar{g}^{\beta}(t)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\frac{\bar{g}^{\beta}(v(x))}{v(x)}\land\frac{\bar{g}^{\beta}(v(y))}{v(y)}\geq\left(\frac{v(x)}{\bar{g}^{\beta}(v(x))}+\frac{v(y)}{\bar{g}^{\beta}(v(y))}\right)^{-1}

we have

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{g}\left(\frac{\bar{g}^{-\beta}(v(x))}{\bar{g}^{-\beta}(m)}v(x)\right)+C\bar{g}\left(\frac{\bar{g}^{-\beta}(v(y))}{\bar{g}^{-\beta}(m)}v(y)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\bar{g}^{\beta(q-1)}(m)\left(\bar{g}^{1-\beta(q-1)}(v(x))+\bar{g}^{1-\beta(q-1)}(v(y))\right).

Consequently,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{s}}\bar{g}^{-\beta}(m)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}G(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\frac{\bar{g}^{\beta}(t)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C}{\rho^{s}}\bar{g}^{\beta(q-1)}(m)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\bar{g}^{1-\beta(q-1)}(v(x))\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho}\right)^{(1-s)p}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Assume that we have taken
d=\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)
, then

g(\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho}+d)_{-};x_{0},2\rho)/\rho^{s})\leq Cg(d/\rho^{s})\leq Cg(m).

Thus, by applying Lemma 3.6 in Kim–Lee
[
19
]
we obtain

I_{1}\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{s}}\bar{g}^{-\beta}(m)\int_{B_{2\rho}}\bar{g}^{1+\beta}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{\rho^{s}}\bar{g}^{\beta(q-1)}(m)\int_{B_{7\rho/4}}\bar{g}^{1-\beta(q-1)}(v)\,\mathrm{d}x.

If we choose
\beta\in(-1,0)
and assume in addition that
\beta
is sufficiently close to 0 so that
1-\beta(q-1)\in(0,n/(n-sp))
when
sp<n
, then the weak Harnack inequality (
Theorem

2.13
) can be employed to conclude

I_{1}\leq C\rho^{n-s}g(m)\leq C\rho^{n-s}\left(g\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)+g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)\right),

where
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda)
is a positive constant.

Let us next estimate
I_{2}
. Since

g(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}}{|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|}\leq Cg\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)+Cg\left(\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)

and
\varphi(x)\leq M_{l}(4\rho)\eta^{q}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{13\rho/8}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}^{c}}g\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{\rho^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n+s}}+C\int_{B_{13\rho/8}}\int_{B_{7\rho/4}^{c}}g\left(\frac{(u_{l,4\rho})_{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{13\rho/8}}g\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}}{\rho^{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}x+C\rho^{n-s}g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right).

By using
Theorem

2.13
, we arrive at

I_{2}\leq C\rho^{n-s}\left(g\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)+g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)\right).

The desired result (
6.3
) now follows from the estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.
∎

The following is a key lemma for the proof of the sufficiency of
Theorem

1.4
.

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,L-l)>0
such that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))\leq C\rho^{n-s}\left(g\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)+g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)\right)

for any
\rho\in(0,r_{\ast}/4)
, where
D_{\rho}(x_{0})
is given by (
6.2
).

Proof.

Let us write
B_{\rho}=B_{\rho}(x_{0})
and
D_{\rho}=D_{\rho}(x_{0})
for simplicity. Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{3\rho/2})
be a cut-off function such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
\overline{B_{\rho}}
,
0\leq\eta\leq 1
, and
|\nabla\eta|\leq C/\rho
. Since
v_{l,4\rho}:=\eta u_{l,4\rho}/M_{l}(4\rho)
is admissible for
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})
, we have

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})\leq\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(v_{l,4\rho})\leq 2\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}v_{l,4\rho}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}=:I.

The triangle inequality and
Lemma

2.1
yield

I\leq C\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(|D^{s}\eta|\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+C\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|\frac{\eta(y)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

By using
u_{l,4\rho}\leq M_{l}(4\rho)
and
Lemma

2.1
again, we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G\left(|D^{s}\eta|\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}G\left(\frac{|x-y|^{1-s}}{\rho}\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n}}+\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(x)}G\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq C\int_{B_{\rho}(x)}G\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{\rho^{s}M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\rho^{ps-p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps-p}}\,\mathrm{d}y+C\int_{B_{\rho}^{c}(x)}g\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{\rho^{s}M_{l}(4\rho)}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\\
&\leq C\rho^{-s}g\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}(x)}{\rho^{s}M_{l}(4\rho)}\right).\end{split}

Thus, it follows from
M_{l}(4\rho)\geq L-l
that

I\leq C\rho^{n-s}\fint_{B_{3\rho/2}}g\left(\frac{u_{l,4\rho}}{\rho^{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}x+\frac{C}{M_{l}(4\rho)}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}\int_{B_{3\rho/2}}G(|D^{s}u_{l,4\rho}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}},

where
C=C(n,p,q,s,L-l)
is a positive constant. We now apply
Theorem

2.13
with
\delta=1
and
Lemma

6.1
to conclude the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to prove the sufficiency of the Wiener criterion.

Proof of the sufficiency of
Theorem

1.4
.

As explained in the beginning of this section, we assume to the contrary that (
6.1
) holds and find a contradiction. By
Lemma

6.2
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-s}}\leq Cg\left(\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)+Cg\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho^{s}}\right)

for any
\rho\in(0,r_{\ast}/4)
. By using this inequality, we derive an estimate for the Wiener integral:

(6.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\rho^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\setminus\Omega,B_{2\rho}(x_{0}))}{\rho^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\frac{M_{l}(4\rho)-M_{l}(\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho+C\int_{0}^{r_{\ast}/4}\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{l,4\rho})_{-};x_{0},4\rho)}{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

On the one hand, one can show that the right-hand side of (
6.4
) is finite by modifying the argument presented in Section 4 in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
and using
Lemma

2.2
and the local boundedness (
Theorem

2.12
) as in the previous sections. On the other hand, the left-hand side of (
6.4
) diverges by (
1.8
), which leads us to a contradiction.
∎

6.2.
The necessary condition

In this section, we prove the necessity of the Wiener criterion. We basically follow the lines of proofs presented in
[
18
]
, where the standard growth case
G(t)=t^{p}
was covered by dividing the cases into two cases
p\leq n/s
and
p>n/s
. However, we encounter a difficulty because such a distinction is not available in the present framework. Moreover, as we pointed out in
Remark

1.5
(iii), the case
p>n/s
in
[
18
]
was treated incorrectly. We overcome such a challenge by considering two cases
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\{x\})>0
and
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\{x\})=0
, and fill the gap by exploiting
Lemma

5.11
regarding
(s,G)
-quasicontinuity.

We first provide a necessary condition for a boundary to be regular in terms of the
\mathcal{L}
-potential. Before we state the result, we consider a lsc-regularization of the
\mathcal{L}
-potential; define
\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(K,\Omega)
in
\Omega
by

\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(K,\Omega)(x)=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)(x)&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\\
\displaystyle\essliminf_{y\to x}\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)(y)&\text{in}~\Omega.\end{cases}

Note that
\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(K,\Omega)
in
\Omega
is superharmonic in
\Omega
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(K,\Omega)=\mathfrak{R}(K,\Omega)
a.e. in
\Omega
by
Theorem

2.9
and
Theorem

2.10
(i).

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that the
(s,G)
-capacity of a single point is zero. Let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. If there exists
\rho>0
such that

(6.5)

\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{8\rho}(x_{0}))(x_{0})<1,

then
x_{0}
is irregular.

We will see that (
6.5
) does not hold for any
\rho>0
when the
(s,G)
-capacity of a single point is positive. This will improve
Lemma

6.3
; see
Lemma

6.4
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
B_{\rho}=B_{\rho}(x_{0})
,
D_{\rho}=D_{\rho}(x_{0})
, and
u_{\rho}=\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
. If (
6.5
) holds for some
\rho>0
, then it holds for
\rho^{\prime}<\rho
as well by the comparison principle; see Lemma 5.2 in Kim–Lee
[
19
]
for instance. Thus, we may choose
\rho_{0}>0
sufficiently small so that
\Omega\cap\partial B_{8\rho}\neq\emptyset
for all
\rho<\rho_{0}
. Let us fix
\rho<\rho_{0}
and
r<\rho
. Let
\vartheta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a function such that
\vartheta\equiv 3/2
on
\overline{B_{r/2}}\setminus\Omega
,
0\leq\vartheta<3/2
in
(B_{r}\setminus\overline{B_{r/2}})\setminus\Omega
, and
\vartheta\equiv 0
on the remaining part of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. Let
v_{r}
be the harmonic function in
\Omega
such that
v_{r}-\vartheta\in V_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega)
. We claim that there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,\rho,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega))>0
such that

(6.6)

v_{r}\leq\frac{1}{2}+u_{\rho}

in
\Omega\cap B_{8\rho}
in the weak sense for all
r<r_{0}
. Indeed, by following the argument given in the proof of Lemma 5.5 in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
, one can easily check that (
6.6
) holds in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
. Thus, it is enough to show (
6.6
) in
\Omega\setminus B_{8\rho}
. Note that
u_{\rho}=0
outside
B_{8\rho}
. Let us fix a point
z\in\Omega\setminus B_{8\rho}
and consider a ball
B_{\rho}(z)
. Since
B_{r}(x_{0})\cap B_{\rho}(z)=\emptyset
and
\vartheta=0
in
(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0}))\setminus\Omega
, we have
\vartheta=0
in
B_{\rho}(z)\setminus\Omega
. By applying the local boundedness (
Theorem

2.12
) to
v_{r}
in
B_{\rho}(z)
and using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(6.7)

\esssup_{B_{\rho/2}(z)}v_{r}\leq\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(v_{r};z,\rho/2)+C(\varepsilon)\rho^{s}G^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}(z)}G\left(\frac{v_{r}}{\rho^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
where
\gamma=2(q-1)n/s_{0}>0
. In order to estimate the terms on the right-hand side of (
6.7
), we introduce the
\mathcal{L}
-potential
w
of
D_{r}
in
B_{R}
, where
R
is the diameter of
\Omega
. By the comparison principle,
v_{r}\leq\frac{3}{2}w
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
0\leq w\leq 1
, we have

(6.8)

\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(v_{r};z,\rho/2)\leq C\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(w;z,\rho/2)\leq C.

Moreover, by
Theorem

2.3
we obtain

(6.9)

\int_{B_{\rho}(z)}G\left(\frac{v_{r}}{\rho^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}G\left(\frac{w}{\rho^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\frac{R}{\rho}\right)^{sq}\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(w).

Furthermore, we observe that

(6.10)

\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(w)=\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{r},B_{R})\leq\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{r}},B_{R})=:\varphi(r),

where
\lim_{r\to 0}\varphi(r)=0
due to the assumption that a single point is of
(s,G)
-capacity zero. By combining (
6.7
), (
6.8
), (
6.9
) and (
6.10
), we arrive at

\esssup_{B_{\rho/2}(z)}v_{r}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon+C_{2}(\varepsilon)\rho^{s}G^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{n}}\left(\frac{R}{\rho}\right)^{sq}\varphi(r)\right).

We take
\varepsilon=1/(4C_{1})
and then take
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,\rho,R)>0
sufficiently small so that
C_{2}(\varepsilon)\rho^{s}G^{-1}(\rho^{-n-sq}R^{sq}\varphi(r))\leq 1/2
. Then we conclude
\esssup_{B_{\rho/2}(z)}v_{r}\leq 1/2
for all
r<r_{0}
, from which (
6.6
) follows.

The comparison principle (see Lemma 5.2 in Kim–Lee
[
19
]
for instance) now shows that

\liminf_{\Omega\ni x\to x_{0}}v_{r}<\frac{3}{2}=\vartheta(x_{0}),

which concludes that the boundary point
x_{0}
is irregular.
∎

The following lemma is an improvement of
Lemma

6.3
, namely, we drop the assumption that
(s,G)
-capacity of a single point is zero.

Lemma 6.4
.

If (
6.5
) holds for some
\rho>0
, then
x_{0}
is irregular.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.3
, it is enough to prove that (
6.5
) does not hold for any
\rho>0
when the
(s,G)
-capacity of a single point is positive. Assume that every single point has a positive
(s,G)
-capacity. By appealing
Lemma

5.11
, we may choose a representative
v_{\rho}
of
\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{8\rho}(x_{0}))
which is
(s,G)
-quasicontinuous in
B_{8\rho}(x_{0})
and not less than 1 q.e. on
D_{\rho}(x_{0})
. Since a single point is of positive capacity, we deduce that
v_{\rho}
is continuous in
B_{8\rho}(x_{0})
and
v_{\rho}\geq 1
on
D_{\rho}(x_{0})
. This in particular implies that
v_{\rho}(x_{0})\geq 1
. Thus, we have

\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{8\rho}(x_{0}))(x_{0})=\liminf_{x\to x_{0}}\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(D_{\rho}(x_{0}),B_{8\rho}(x_{0}))(x)=\liminf_{x\to x_{0}}v_{\rho}(x)=v_{\rho}(x_{0})\geq 1,

implying that (
6.5
) does not hold.
∎

With the help of
Lemma

6.4
, we finally prove the necessary part of
Theorem

1.4
.

Proof of the necessity of
Theorem

1.4
.

We prove that a boundary point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is irregular if the Wiener integral converges, that is,

(6.11)

\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{t},B_{2t})}{t^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}<\infty,

where we denote
B_{t}=B_{t}(x_{0})
and
D_{t}=D_{t}(x_{0})
. Recall that
D_{t}
is defined as in (
6.2
). Let
u_{\rho}=\widehat{\mathfrak{R}}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
and
\mu_{\rho}=\mathcal{L}u_{\rho}
be the Riesz measure in
B_{8\rho}
, then an application of
Theorem

1.2
to
u_{\rho}
in
B_{4\rho}
yields

(6.12)

u_{\rho}(x_{0})\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{2\rho}}u_{\rho}+{\bf W}_{s,G}^{\mu_{\rho}}(x_{0},4\rho)+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)\right).

By
Lemma

6.4
, it is enough to show that the right-hand side of (
6.12
) is less than one for some small
\rho>0
.

For the first term on the right-hand side of (
6.12
), we claim that

(6.13)

\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\inf_{B_{2\rho}}u_{\rho}=0.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\lambda_{\rho}:=\inf_{B_{2\rho}}u_{\rho}>0
and define
v_{\rho}(x)=\min\{u_{\rho}(x)/\lambda_{\rho},1\}
. By testing the equation
\mathcal{L}u_{\rho}=\mu_{\rho}
with
v_{\rho}
, we obtain

(6.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{\rho},v_{\rho})=\int_{B_{8\rho}}v_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\rho}\leq\mu_{\rho}(\overline{B_{\rho}})

since the support of
\mu_{\rho}
is contained in
\overline{B_{\rho}}
. Moreover, by using

g(|D^{s}v_{\rho}|)|D^{s}v_{\rho}|\leq g(|D^{s}(u_{\rho}/\lambda_{\rho})|)\frac{D^{s}u_{\rho}}{|D^{s}u_{\rho}|}D^{s}v_{\rho}\leq\lambda_{\rho}^{-(q-1)}g(|D^{s}u_{\rho}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{\rho}}{|D^{s}u_{\rho}|}D^{s}v_{\rho},

we obtain

(6.15)

\mathcal{E}(v_{\rho},v_{\rho})\leq\lambda_{\rho}^{-(q-1)}\mathcal{E}(u_{\rho},v_{\rho}).

Furthermore, we observe that
v_{\rho}
is admissible for
(s,G)
-capacity of
\overline{B_{\rho}}
with respect to
B_{8\rho}
, and hence

(6.16)

\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(\overline{B_{\rho}},B_{8\rho})\leq\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(v_{\rho})\leq\Lambda\mathcal{E}(v_{\rho},v_{\rho}).

By combining (
6.14
), (
6.15
), (
6.16
), and
Lemma

5.3
, we obtain

(6.17)

\rho^{n}G(\rho^{-s})\leq C\lambda_{\rho}^{-(q-1)}\mu_{\rho}(\overline{B_{\rho}}).

Since
u_{\rho}\in V^{s,G}_{0}(B_{8\rho})
by
Lemma

5.10
(i), its Riesz measure
\mu_{\rho}
belongs to the dual space
(V^{s,G}_{0}(B_{8\rho}))^{\ast}
. Then a standard argument as in Lemma 5.1 in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
18
]
shows that

(6.18)

\mu_{\rho}(E)\leq C\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho}\cap E,B_{8\rho})

for every compact set
E\subset B_{8\rho}
. Thus, (
6.18
) with
E=\overline{B_{\rho}}
and (
6.17
) implies that

\lambda_{\rho}^{q-1}\leq C\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-s}g(\rho^{-s})}.

If there is a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-s}g(\rho^{-s})}\geq 2\alpha,

then we can find a constant
\rho_{0}>0
such that
\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})\geq\alpha\rho^{n-s}g(\rho^{-s})
for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, or

\rho^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-s}}\right)\frac{1}{\rho}\geq\frac{C\alpha^{1/(p-1)}}{\rho}\quad\text{for all }\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}),

which contradicts to (
6.11
). Thus, we have

(6.19)

\liminf_{\rho\to 0}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-s}g(\rho^{-s})}=0,

which in turn implies (
6.13
).

We next estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
6.12
). By using (
6.18
), we have

\begin{split}{\bf W}^{\mu_{\rho}}_{s,G}(x_{0},4\rho)&\leq\int_{0}^{4\rho}t^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mu_{\rho}(\overline{B_{t}})}{t^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{4\rho}t^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{t},B_{8\rho})}{t^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{4\rho}t^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{t},B_{2t})}{t^{n-s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.\end{split}

Thus, it follows from (
6.11
) that
{\bf W}_{s,G}^{\mu_{\rho}}(x_{0},4\rho)\to 0
as
\rho\to 0
.

It remains to estimate the tail term in (
6.12
). Since
u_{\rho}\in V^{s,G}_{0}(B_{8\rho})
, we have

\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)=(2\rho)^{s}g^{-1}\left((2\rho)^{s}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}g\left(\frac{u_{\rho}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\right).

It follows from
Lemma

5.10
(iii) and the ellipticity condition that

\Lambda\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}(v)\geq\mathcal{E}(v)\geq\mathcal{E}(u_{\rho})

for all functions
v\in W_{0}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
. By taking the infimum over
v\in W_{0}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})
, we obatin

\Lambda\,\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})\geq\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{B_{10\rho}\setminus B_{8\rho}}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}G(|D^{s}u_{\rho}|)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

For
x\in B_{10\rho}\setminus B_{8\rho}
and
y\in B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}
, it holds that
|x-y|\leq 18\rho\leq 9|y-x_{0}|
, and hence

\begin{split}\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})&\geq C\int_{B_{10\rho}\setminus B_{8\rho}}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}G\left(\frac{u_{\rho}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|y-x_{0}|^{n}}\\
&\geq C\rho^{n}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}G\left(\frac{u_{\rho}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n}}.\end{split}

By applying (
2.1
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}g\left(\frac{u_{\rho}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\\
&\quad\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}G\left(\frac{u_{\rho}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n}}+\int_{B_{8\rho}\setminus B_{2\rho}}g\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\\
&\quad\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\rho^{-n}\mathrm{cap}_{s,G}(D_{\rho},B_{8\rho})+C\rho^{-s}g(\varepsilon\rho^{-s})\end{split}

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)

\displaystyle\leq C\rho^{s}g^{-1}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-s}}+g(\varepsilon\rho^{-s})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\rho^{s}g^{-1}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\frac{\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(D_{\rho},B_{2\rho})}{\rho^{n-s}}\right)+C\varepsilon.

Taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small and then using (
6.19
), we can make
\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{\rho};x_{0},2\rho)
as small as we want.

Thus, there exists a small
\rho>0
such that
u_{\rho}(x_{0})<1
as required.
∎

Appendix A
Algebraic inequalities

In this section we provide several algebraic inequalities. The following two lemmas are algebraic inequalities that are used in the proof of the upper potential estimate in

.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
v
be a nonnegative function and
\lambda,\alpha>0
. There exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
p
,
q
, and
\alpha
, such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|D^{s}W(v)|^{p}&\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\left|\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\alpha p}{\bf 1}_{\{v(x)\neq v(y)\}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
&\quad+\frac{C}{R^{sp}}G(R^{s}|D^{s}v|)\left|\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\alpha p}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{G(\lambda+t)}\right|,\end{split}

where
W(t)=(\bar{g}(\lambda+t)/\bar{g}(\lambda))^{\alpha}-1
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
v(x)>v(y)
. By using Jensen’s inequality and (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle|D^{s}W(v)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(D^{s}v\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\alpha\frac{\bar{g}^{\alpha-1}(\lambda+t)\bar{g}^{\prime}(\lambda+t)}{\bar{g}^{\alpha}(\lambda)}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\alpha(q-1)}{R^{s}\bar{g}^{\alpha}(\lambda)}\right)^{p}\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\frac{\bar{g}^{\alpha p}(\lambda+t)}{(\lambda+t)^{p}}|R^{s}D^{s}v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Let us define
H(t):=G(t^{1/p})
. Then by applying Young’s inequality and Lemma 2.2 in
[
19
]
, we obtain

\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}{(\lambda+t)^{p-1}}|R^{s}D^{s}v|^{p}\leq H^{\ast}\left(\frac{G(\lambda+t)}{(\lambda+t)^{p}}\right)+H(|R^{s}D^{s}v|^{p})\lesssim G(\lambda+t)+G(R^{s}|D^{s}v|).

Thus, the desired inequality (
A.1
) follows by combining the above inequalities.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\delta>1
. Let
u
be a function defined in
B_{R}
and
\eta
be a function satisfying
0\leq\eta\leq 1
. There exist constants
c,C>0
, depending only on
p
,
q
, and
\delta
, such that

(A.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}(\Phi(v)\eta)\\
&\geq c\frac{1}{R^{s}}G(|D^{s}u_{+}|)\left(\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda+t}\right)(\eta(x)\land\eta(y))^{q}\\
&\quad-C\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{R^{s}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+\max\{v(x),v(y)\})}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}((R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{p}\lor(R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q}){\bf 1}_{\{v(x)\neq v(y)\}},\end{split}

where
v=u_{+}/R^{s}
and
\Phi(t)=1-(\bar{g}(\lambda)/\bar{g}(\lambda+t))^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}
.

Proof.

We may assume
u(x)>u(y)
without loss of generality. Let
A
denote the left-hand side of (
A.2
).

Let us first consider the case
\eta(x)/\eta(y)\in[1/2,2]
. By using (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle D^{s}(\Phi(v)\eta)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\Phi^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\frac{\eta^{q}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}+\Phi(v(y))D^{s}(\eta^{q})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(\delta-1)(p-1)}{q-1}\left(\int_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda+t}\right)\frac{\eta^{q}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}

\displaystyle\quad-q\Phi(v(y))(\eta(x)\lor\eta(y))^{q-1}|D^{s}\eta|.

Since
\eta(y)\leq 2\eta(x)
and
u(x)-u(y)\geq u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)
, we obtain

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(\delta-1)p(p-1)}{(q-1)R^{s}}G(D^{s}u_{+})\left(\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda+t}\right)\eta^{q}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-q^{2}2^{q-1}\bar{g}(D^{s}u)|D^{s}\eta|\Phi(v(y))\eta^{q-1}(x)=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where we used (
1.1
) and (
2.2
). We claim that

\bar{g}(D^{s}u)\frac{|D^{s}\eta|}{\eta(x)}\Phi(v(y))\leq\varepsilon G(D^{s}u_{+})+\bar{g}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{|D^{s}\eta|}{\eta(x)}\right)\frac{|D^{s}\eta|}{\eta(x)}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Indeed, it is trivial when
u(y)\leq 0
since
\Phi(v(y))=0
in this case. If
u(y)>0
, then it follows from
u(x)-u(y)\leq u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)
,
\Phi(v(y))\leq 1
, and the inequality (
2.1
) with
g
replaced by
\bar{g}
. We now take

\varepsilon=\frac{(\delta-1)p(p-1)}{q^{2}(q-1)2^{q}R^{s}}\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda+t}

so that

I_{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}I_{1}-q^{2}2^{q-1}\bar{g}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{|D^{s}\eta|}{\eta(x)}\right)|D^{s}\eta|\eta^{q-1}(x).

Since
\eta(x)\leq 1
and

\varepsilon\geq\frac{c}{R^{s}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{1}{\lambda+v(x)},

we have from

that

\displaystyle\bar{g}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{|D^{s}\eta|}{\eta(x)}\right)\frac{|D^{s}\eta|}{\eta(x)}\eta^{q-1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{s}}\bar{g}\left(\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}(\lambda+v(x))\right)((R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{p}\lor(R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{s}}\bar{g}(\lambda)\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}((R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{p}\lor(R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q}).

This finishes the proof for the case
\eta(x)/\eta(y)\in[1/2,2]
.

Let us next consider the case
\eta(x)/\eta(y)\not\in[1/2,2]
. In this case, we have

(A.3)

\eta(x)\lor\eta(y)\leq 2|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|.

We begin with

A=g(D^{s}u)\frac{\Phi(v(x))\eta^{q}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}-g(D^{s}u)\frac{\Phi(v(y))\eta^{q}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

) and

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{R^{s}}g(D^{s}u)D^{s}u_{+}\left(\fint_{v(y)}^{v(x)}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{\bar{g}(\lambda+t)}\right)^{\frac{\delta-1}{q-1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\lambda+t}\right)\eta^{q}(x)

On the other hand, we utilize (

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\geq-C\bar{g}(\lambda)\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}\left(\frac{R^{s}}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)^{q-1}\frac{\eta^{q}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq-C\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda)}{R^{s}}\left(\frac{\bar{g}(\lambda+v(x))}{\bar{g}(\lambda)}\right)^{\delta}(R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q}

Lemma A.3
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(A.4)

G(|a_{1}\lor a_{2}-b_{1}\lor b_{2}|)+G(|a_{1}\land a_{2}-b_{1}\land b_{2}|)\leq G(|a_{1}-b_{1}|)+G(|a_{2}-b_{2}|).

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}
. If
b_{1}\geq b_{2}
, then the desired inequality (
A.4
) is trivial. Otherwise, (
A.4
) holds if and only if the function

\varphi(t):=G(|t-b_{1}|)-G(|t-b_{2}|)

is non-decreasing. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=g(|t-b_{1}|)\frac{t-b_{1}}{|t-b_{1}|}-g(|t-b_{2}|)\frac{t-b_{2}}{|t-b_{2}|}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}g(t-b_{1})-g(t-b_{2})\quad&\text{if $t\geq b_{2}$}\\
g(t-b_{1})+g(b_{2}-t)\quad&\text{if $b_{1}\leq t<b_{2}$}\\
g(b_{2}-t)-g(b_{1}-t)\quad&\text{if $t<b_{1}$}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq 0.

∎

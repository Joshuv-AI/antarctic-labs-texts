Robert Dicks

1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer. A
partition
of
n
is any non-increasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
. The partition function
p(n)
counts the number of partitions of
n
. We agree that
p(0)=1
and that
p(n)=0
if
n\not\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}.

It is well-known that we have

(1.1)

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p(n)q^{n}=\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}(1-q^{n})^{-1}.

The study of the arithmetic properties of
p(n)
has a long history. For example,
Ramanujan
[
Ram21
]
proved the following congruences for
p(n)
:

(1.2)

p(\ell n+\beta_{\ell})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \ \ \text{ for $\ell=5,7,11,$}

where
\beta_{\ell}:=\frac{1}{24}\pmod{\ell}
.
Since the work of Ramanujan, many authors found more examples of congruences for
p(n)
.
These take the form

(1.3)

p(\ell Q^{m}n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},

where
\ell\geq 5
is prime,
Q
is a prime distinct from
\ell
and
m\geq 1
is an integer.
In his breakthrough work, Ono
[
Ono00
]
showed that for every
\ell\geq 5
, there are infinitely many primes
Q
for which (
1.3
) holds with
m=4
.
Such arithmetic phenomena also occur for the coefficients of a wide class of weakly holomorphic modular forms (see e.g.
[
Tre06
]
and
[
Tre08
]
).
In contrast to this, Ahlgren and Boylan
[
AB03
]
showed that if (
1.3
) holds for
m=0
, then
\ell=5,7,\text{ or }11
.
For
m=1,2
, Ahlgren, Beckwith, and Raum
[
ABR22
]
have shown that congruences of the form (
1.3
) are scarce in a precise sense.

In the 1960s, Atkin discovered examples of congruences (
1.3
) with
m=3
.
In recent work, Ahlgren, Allen, and Tang
[
AAT21
]
study the existence of such congruences.
These come from two families which we describe below.

For primes
13\leq\ell\leq 31
, Atkin
[
Atk68a
, eq.
52
]
gave examples of primes
Q
for which

(1.4)

p\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{ if }\left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\epsilon_{Q},

where
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
By fixing
n
in one of the allowable residue classes modulo
24Q
, we obtain a congruence (
1.3
) with
m=3
.
We refer to these as type I congruences.
For each of
\ell=5,7,\text{ and }13
, Atkin
[
Atk68a
, Thm.
1
,
2
]
showed that for
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
, we have

(1.5)

p\left(\frac{Q^{2}n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{ if }\left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=-1\text{ and }\left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=-1.

We refer to these as type II congruences. By using modular Galois representations, Ahlgren, Allen and Tang proved that there are infinitely many type I congruences for every prime
\ell\geq 5
and that there are infinitely many type II congruences for
\geq 17/24
of the primes
\ell
.

For a positive integer
r
, define the
r
-colored partition function
by

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p_{r}(n)q^{n}:=\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}(1-q^{n})^{-r}=1+rq+\cdots.

We agree that
p_{r}(n)=0
if
n\not\in\{0,1,2,...\}
. Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime.
Many congruences have been proven for
p_{r}(n)
(see e.g.
[
Atk68b
, Thm.
1
]
and
[
Gor83
, Thm.
2
]
).
In this paper, for a wide range of
c\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, we prove congruences of the form
p_{r}(\ell Q^{3}n+\beta_{0})\equiv c\cdot p_{r}(\ell Qn+\beta_{1})\pmod{\ell}
for infinitely many primes
Q
. In particular, when
c=0
, we get congruences analogous to (
1.4
).
Our first result relies on the assumption that
r
and
\ell
are
compatible

(we choose to delay the precise definition to Section
2
for ease of exposition). Moreover, we will prove in Section
5
that
r
and
\ell
are compatible if we have
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
and
\ell>5r+19
. We will also prove in Section
5
that if
1\leq r\leq 39
and
\ell>r+4
, then
r
and
\ell
are compatible whenever
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
.
Stating the result requires some notation. Define

\chi^{(r)}:=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{-4}{\bullet}\right)&\text{if }3\mid r,\\
\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)&\text{if }3\nmid r.\end{cases}

Our first result states that we have type I congruences modulo
\ell
for the functions
p_{r}(n)
when
r
and
\ell
are compatible.
We state these theorems under the assumption that
r<\ell-4
. When
r=\ell-4
, the situation is much simpler and will be discussed in the last section.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r<\ell-4
is an odd positive integer
and that
r
and
\ell
are compatible. Then
there exists a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then we have
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
and

p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\chi^{(r)}(Q)\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{\frac{\ell-r-2}{2}}.

Thus, by choosing
n
in any of the allowable residue classes modulo
24Q
, we obtain congruences of the form

p_{r}(\ell Q^{3}n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

Remark
.

In the above theorem and the theorems which follow, our definition of density is that of natural density.

Remark
.

When
r
and
\ell
are close, the modular form
f_{r,\ell}
which captures the relevant values of
p_{r}(n)
modulo
\ell
has small weight.
We illustrate this when
r=\ell-6
. In this case,
the form
f_{\ell-6,\ell}
is in
S_{\frac{5}{2}}(1,\nu^{6\ell-1}_{\eta})
(see Section
2
for a definition of this space), which has dimension
0
if
\ell\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
For such
\ell
, we have
f_{\ell-6,\ell}=0
, which implies that

p_{\ell-6}\left(\frac{\ell n+\ell-6}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

We prove a more general result from which Theorem
1.1
follows by setting
\alpha=1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r<\ell-4
is an odd positive integer and that
r
and
\ell
are compatible. Suppose that
\alpha\not\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
is an integer.
Then there exists a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
and

p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv(\alpha-1)\chi^{(r)}(Q)p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell n+r}{24}\right)\pmod{\ell}\ \text{ if }\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\chi^{(r)}(Q)\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{\frac{\ell-r-2}{2}}.

Thus, by choosing
n
in any of the allowable residue classes modulo
24Q

we obtain congruences of the form

p_{r}(\ell Q^{3}n+\beta_{0})\equiv(\alpha-1)\chi^{(r)}(Q)p_{r}(\ell Qn+\beta_{1})\pmod{\ell}.

Remark
.

If we make the further assumption that there is no congruence modulo any prime above
\ell
between distinct newforms in
S\left(-r\ell,\frac{\ell-r-1}{2}\right)
(see Section
6
for the definition), Theorem
1.2
also holds when
\alpha\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
.

The next result gives congruences analogous to
(1.4)
and
(1.5)
involving primes
Q
in a different residue class modulo
\ell
. Our result does not rely on the assumption of compatibility.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
r
is an odd positive integer.
Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r<\ell-4
and that

(1.6)

\text{ there exists an integer $a$ with $2^{a}\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}$}.

(1)

There exists a positive density set
S
of primes with the property that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
and there exists
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{ if }\\
\ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\epsilon_{Q}.

(2)

Suppose that all of the integers
-r\leq w<0
with
w\equiv-r\pmod{24}
satisfy
\left(\frac{-rw}{\ell}\right)=1
. Then there exists a positive density set
S
of primes with the property that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
and there exists
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
such that

p_{r}\left(\frac{Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{ if }\ \ \left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\epsilon_{Q}.

Remark
.

The value of
\epsilon_{Q}
in
(1)
and
(2)
can be made explicit for any
r
and
\ell
using the arguments at the end of Section
6
.

Remark
.

The condition on the integers
-r\leq w<0
with
w\equiv-r\pmod{24}
in
(2)
is necessary to ensure that the form
g_{r,\ell}
which we construct in Section
3
is a cusp form.
We can use the same method to prove an analogous result for primes
\ell\geq 5
with the property that
3^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
for some integer
a
. However,
we would need to add the condition that there are no congruences between distinct newforms in the spaces under consideration.

Remark
.

If
r\leq 23
, then the condition on the integers
-r\leq w<0
with
w\equiv-r\pmod{24}
is always satisfied, since the only choice for
w
is
-r
. It also holds for
r=25
(since it is a square) and
r=27
(since
\left(\frac{3}{\ell}\right)=\left(\frac{27}{\ell}\right)
).
For a fixed
r
, we can use the quantitative version of Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, quadratic reciprocity, and the Chinese remainder theorem to prove that the condition holds for a positive proportion of the primes
\ell
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we give some background on modular forms and Galois representations. In Section
3
, we construct for each

and
\ell
important modular forms whose coefficients capture the relevant values of
p_{r}(n)
modulo
\ell
.
In the remaining sections (save for Section

), our main tool is the theory of modular Galois representations.
In Section
4
, we prove that a wide range of
r
and
\ell
are compatible.
In Section
5
, we prove the main technical result which we will need in order to prove Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
6
, we prove Theorems
1.2-1.3
.
Finally, in Section
7
, we discuss congruences for
p_{r}(n)
when
r=\ell-4
; in particular, we give a short proof of
[
Boy04
, Thm.
2.1
,
(2)
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

be a normalized Hecke eigenform. There is a continuous irreducible representation

with semisimple mod
\ell

\overline{\rho}_{f}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
satisfying the following properties.

(1)

If

, then
\rho_{f}
is unramified at
p
and the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is
X^{2}-\iota_{\ell}(a(p))X+p^{k-1}
.

(2)

If

\rho_{f}|_{I_{q}}
is unipotent.
In particular, the prime-to-
\ell

.
If further

is
q

0&\psi\end{matrix}\right),

where
\psi:G_{q}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}_{\ell}
is the unramified character with
\psi({\operatorname{Frob}}_{q})=\iota_{\ell}(a_{q})
.

(3)

Assume that
2\leq k\leq\ell+1
. Then

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is an
\ell
-adic unit, then
\rho_{f}|_{G_{\ell}}
is reducible and we have

\rho_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\chi^{k-1}&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

•

If

is not an

-adic unit, then

is irreducible and
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\omega^{k-1}_{2}\oplus\omega^{\prime(k-1)}_{2}
.

Remark
.

The Galois representations depend on the choice of embedding

, but we have suppressed this from the notation.

\ell\geq 5

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be even integers. Let

and

be prime.

(1)

We have

(2)

satisfies

, then we have

.

(3)

There exists a form

with

.

(4)

For

, we have

Eisenstein series on

3.
The modular forms
f_{r,\ell}
and
g_{r,\ell}

Let

\Delta:=q\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}(1-q^{n})^{24}

be the unique normalized cusp form of weight
12
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Set

\delta_{\ell}:=\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}.

By studying the filtration
w_{\ell}(\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|U_{\ell})
, we prove the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime. Assume that
r
is an odd positive integer with
r\leq\ell-4
.
Then there exists a modular form
f_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r\ell}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
with

(3.1)

f_{r,\ell}\equiv\sum p_{r}\left(\ell n-r\delta_{\ell}\right)q^{n-\frac{r\ell}{24}}\equiv\sum p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

Remark
.

Boylan
[
Boy04
, Theorem
1.3
]
gave the complete list of pairs
(r,\ell)
with
r\leq 47
for which
f_{r,\ell}\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
.

Proof.

Note that

(3.2)

\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}=q^{r\delta_{\ell}}\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(1-q^{n})^{r\ell^{2}}}{(1-q^{n})^{r}}\equiv\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}(1-q^{\ell n})^{r\ell}\cdot\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p_{r}(n-r\delta_{\ell})q^{n}\pmod{\ell}.

Thus, we have

(3.3)

\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|U_{\ell}\equiv\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}(1-q^{n})^{r\ell}\cdot\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}p_{r}(\ell n-r\delta_{\ell})q^{n}\pmod{\ell}.

Define

k:=w_{\ell}\left(\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|U_{\ell}\right).

If
k=-\infty
, then the right hand side of (
3.3
) is congruent to
0\pmod{\ell}
, so there is nothing to prove. Therefore, we assume that
k\neq-\infty
.
By definition, there exists a form
G_{r,\ell}\in M_{k}(1,\mathbb{Z})
with

G_{r,\ell}\equiv\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|U_{\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

By
(4)
of Lemma
2.3
, we see that
w_{\ell}(\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}})=12r\delta_{\ell}
.
By
(1)
of Lemma
2.3
, we have

k\leq\frac{r+2}{2}\ell-\frac{r+2}{2\ell}.

Since
\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|U_{\ell}\equiv\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|T_{\ell}\pmod{\ell}
and
\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\big|T_{\ell}
has weight
r(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{2})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell-1}
, we see by
(2)
of Lemma
2.3
that

(3.4)

k\equiv 0\pmod{\ell-1}.

If
r\leq\ell-4
, we have

\frac{r+2}{2}\ell-\frac{r+2}{2\ell}<\frac{r+3}{2}(\ell-1)

which implies by (
3.4
) that

(3.5)

k\leq\frac{r+1}{2}(\ell-1).

Let
d=\dim(M_{k}(1,\mathbb{Z})).
The space
M_{k}(1,\mathbb{Z})
has a basis
\{f_{0},...,f_{d}\}
of forms such that

(3.6)

f_{i}(z)=q^{i}+O(q^{i+1}),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 0\leq i\leq d-1.

After subtracting an appropriate integral linear combination of these basis elements from
G_{r,\ell}
,
we may assume that

\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(G_{r,\ell})\geq\lceil r(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24\ell})\rceil
.
If
n\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{24\ell}\leq n<\frac{r\ell}{24}
, then
r\ell^{2}-r\leq 24\ell n<r\ell^{2}
; this is a contradiction since
r\leq\ell-4
. This
implies that
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(G_{r,\ell})\geq\lceil r(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24\ell})\rceil\geq\frac{r\ell+1}{24}
, so

f_{r,\ell}:=\frac{G_{r,\ell}}{\eta^{r\ell}}\in S_{k-\frac{r\ell}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r\ell}_{\eta}).

This form satisfies (
3.1
).
Since
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(G_{r,\ell})\geq\frac{r\ell+1}{24}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{\infty}(G_{r,\ell})\leq\frac{k}{12}
, it follows that
k\geq\frac{r\ell+1}{2}>\frac{r-1}{2}(\ell-1)
. By (
3.5
), we get
k=\frac{r+1}{2}(\ell-1)
; the result follows.
∎

We now construct the half-integral weight forms which we will need in order to prove the second assertion of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
r
be an odd positive integer. Let
\ell\geq 5
be a prime such that
r\leq\ell-4
.
Suppose that all of the integers
-r\leq w<0
satisfying
w\equiv-r\pmod{24}
have the property that
\left(\frac{-rw}{\ell}\right)=1
.
Then there exists a form
g_{r,\ell}
in
S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
satisfying

(3.7)

g_{r,\ell}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=-1}p_{r}\left(\frac{n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}.

Proof.

By
(3)
of Lemma
2.3
, there exists a form
H_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{2}+(\ell+1)\frac{\ell-1}{2}}(1,\mathbb{Z})
with

H_{r,\ell}\equiv\left(\frac{-r}{\ell}\right)\Theta^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}\pmod{\ell}.

Recall that
E_{\ell-1}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
. Define
\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{r(\ell^{2}-1)}{2}+(\ell+1)\frac{\ell-1}{2}}(1)
by

\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}:=\Delta^{r\delta_{\ell}}E^{\frac{\ell+1}{2}}_{\ell-1}-H_{r,\ell}.

By (
3.2
), we have

\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}\equiv\sum\left(1-\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)\right)p_{r}(n-r\delta_{\ell})q^{n}\cdot\prod^{\infty}_{n=1}\left(1-q^{\ell n}\right)^{r\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

This implies that

\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}\equiv\eta^{r\ell^{2}}\left(\sum_{\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=0}p_{r}\left(\frac{n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}+2\sum_{\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=-1}p_{r}\left(\frac{n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\right)\pmod{\ell}.

Since
\left(\frac{-rw}{\ell}\right)=1
for all of the integers
-r\leq w<0
which satisfy
w\equiv~-r~\pmod{24}
, we conclude that
\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}
vanishes to order
>\frac{r\ell^{2}}{24}
modulo
\ell
. By arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
using (
3.6
), we may assume that
\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}
vanishes to order
>\frac{r\ell^{2}}{24}
, so

\frac{\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}}{\eta^{r\ell^{2}}}\in S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z}).

We have a form
f_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r\ell}_{\eta})
which satisfies (
3.1
), so the form

f^{\ell}_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-r\ell-\ell}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r}_{\eta})
has the property that

f^{\ell}_{r,\ell}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=0}p_{r}\left(\frac{n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
s\equiv 2^{-1}\pmod{\ell}
, and define
g_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
by

g_{r,\ell}:=s\left(\frac{\widehat{H}_{r,\ell}}{\eta^{r\ell^{2}}}-f^{\ell}_{r,\ell}E^{\frac{r+1}{2}}_{\ell-1}\right)\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=-1}p\left(\frac{n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
Compatibility

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that
r
is an odd positive integer such that
r<\ell-4
.
Recall that

N_{r}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }3\mid r,\\
6&\text{if }3\nmid r\end{cases}

and that

\epsilon_{r}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{8}{r\ell}\right)&\text{if }3\mid r,\\
(-\left(\frac{8}{r\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{r\ell}\right))&\text{if }3\nmid r.\end{cases}

Recall also that
r
and
\ell
are
compatible
if
for every newform
f\in S^{\operatorname{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
, the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.
Finally, recall for each newform
f=\sum a(n)q^{n}
that we have a mod
\ell
reduction
\overline{f}=\sum\overline{a(n)}q^{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[[q]]
.
We now show that we have compatibility for a wide range of
r
and
\ell
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
and
\ell>5r+19
, then
r
and
\ell
are compatible.

Proof.

Let
f=q+\sum_{n\geq 2}a(n)q^{n}\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
be a newform.
By
[
DDT97
, Theorem
2.47(b)
]
, there are four possibilities for the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
:

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is reducible.

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is dihedral, i.e.
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible but
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{G_{K}}
is reducible for some quadratic
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is exceptional, i.e. the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is conjugate to one of
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
,
A_{5}
.

(4)

The image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

Since
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
, we have
2^{\ell-r-3}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
.
By
[
AAT21
, Lemma 3.2]
, we conclude that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible.
By the condition
\ell>5r+19
, we have
\ell-r-2\neq\frac{\ell+1}{2},\frac{\ell+3}{2}
; by the same lemma, we see that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is not dihedral.

Thus, in order to show that
r
and
\ell
are compatible, it suffices to show that the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
cannot be exceptional. To this end, we show that the projective image contains an element of order
>5
.
Suppose that
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is an
\ell
-adic unit. Recall that
\chi
is the
\ell
-adic cyclotomic character.
By part
3
of Theorem
2.1
, we know that

\rho_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\chi^{\ell-r-3}&*\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Recall that
\omega
is the mod
\ell
cyclotomic character. Since
\omega
has order
\ell-1
, we see that the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}

contains an element of order
\geq\frac{\ell-1}{\text{gcd}(\ell-1,\ell-r-3)}
. Since
\ell>5r+19
, we see that
\frac{\ell-1}{\text{gcd}(\ell-1,\ell-r-3)}>5
.

Recall that
\omega_{2}
and
\omega_{2}^{\prime}
are Serre’s fundamental characters of level
2
.
If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is not an
\ell
-adic unit, then part
3
of Theorem
2.1
implies that

\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\left(\begin{matrix}\omega_{2}^{\ell-r-3}&0\\
0&\omega_{2}^{\prime\ell-r-3}\end{matrix}\right).

Since
\omega_{2}/\omega^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}
has order
\ell+1
, we know that the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}

contains an element of order
\frac{\ell+1}{\text{gcd}(\ell+1,\ell-r-3)}
. By the fact that
\ell>5r+19
, we see that
\frac{\ell+1}{\text{gcd}(\ell+1,\ell-r-3)}>5
.
∎

By computing in Magma, for small
r
we can establish compatibility for a wide range of
\ell
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
r
be an odd positive integer satisfying
1\leq r\leq 39
.

(1)

If
\ell>r+4
is a prime such that
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
, then
r
and
\ell
are compatible.

(2)

In particular, if
r=1
or
3
and
\ell>r+4
is prime, then
r
and
\ell
are always compatible.

Proof.

Fix an odd integer
r
such that
1\leq r\leq 39
. Our strategy to prove
(1)
is to compute in Magma to rule out each of the first three possibilities in
Proposition
4.1

as we vary over the primes
\ell>r+4
such that
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
.
By the same proposition, we may assume that
r+4<\ell\leq 5r+19
.
Fix such a prime
\ell
.
Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we conclude that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible for each newform
f\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
.

For each newform
f=q+\sum_{n\geq 2}a_{f}(n)q^{n}\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
,
by
[
AAT21
, Lemma 3.2]
, we see that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is dihedral only if
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
and
\ell=2r+5
or
\ell=2r+7
.
If
p\neq\ell
is prime, then
\chi(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})=p
.
if
\ell=2r+5
or
2r+7
, then we compute in Magma to conclude that there exists
p\in\{5,7\}
with the properties that
p\neq\ell
,
\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})=-1
(which would mean that
\text{tr }\overline{\rho}_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})=0
) and
\overline{a_{f}(p)}\neq 0
.
However, this contradicts the fact that
\text{tr }\overline{\rho}_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})=\overline{a_{f}(p)}
. Therefore,
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is not dihedral for each newform
f\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
.

Thus, to prove
(1)
, it suffices to show for each newform
f\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
does not have exceptional image. From the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we know that this condition holds if

\frac{\ell-1}{\text{gcd}(\ell-1,\ell-r-3)},\frac{\ell+1}{\text{gcd}(\ell+1,\ell-r-3)}>5.

If either of these inequalities fails to hold, then let
p\nmid 6\ell
be a prime. Define
u_{f}(p):=\overline{a_{f}(p)^{2}}/p^{\ell-r-3}
.
If the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is
A_{4},S_{4},\text{ or }A_{5}
, then
we have

(4.1)

u_{f}(p)=0,1,2,4\ \ \ \text{ or }u_{f}(p)^{2}-3u_{f}(p)+1=0,

depending on the order of the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
(see e.g.
[
Rib75
, p. 264]
and
[
Rib85
, p. 189]
). To prove our result, it suffices to check for each newform
f\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
that there exists a prime
p\nmid 6\ell
such that (
4.1
) does not hold.
For such
f
, we compute with Magma to show that there exists
p\in\{5,7\}
such that (
4.1
) does not hold for
u_{f}(p)
.

To prove
(2)
, note that if
r=1
or
3
, then
2^{r+2}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
only fails when
\ell=5
or
7
, respectively; this contradicts the assumption that
\ell>r+4
.
∎

\text{ the polynomial $x^{2}-\alpha x+1$ factors in $\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}$ with roots $\alpha_{1}$ and $\alpha_{2}$}.

Theorem 5.1

,

, and

.
For each newform
f\in S^{\operatorname{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
, the Galois representations
\rho_{f}
and
\overline{\rho}_{f}
can be defined over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda
, respectively. Define
\overline{\alpha}:=\alpha\pmod{\ell}
.
Since
r
and
\ell
are compatible, we know that the image of each
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
,
so we can use
[
AAT21
, Proposition
3.8
]
to find an element
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
such that each
\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)
is conjugate to
\left(\begin{matrix}\overline{\alpha}&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)
.
It follows that the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\sigma)
is congruent to
x^{2}-\alpha x+1
modulo
\lambda
.
Since

\alpha_{1}\not\equiv\alpha_{2}\pmod{\lambda}
,
we can use Hensel’s lemma to factor the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\sigma)
over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
.
Thus,
\rho_{f}(\sigma)
is conjugate to a diagonal matrix with entries
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
in
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
with the properties that
\beta_{1}\equiv\alpha_{1}\pmod{\lambda}
and
\beta_{2}\equiv\alpha_{2}\pmod{\lambda}
.
By induction, we see that
\beta^{\ell^{m-1}}_{1}\equiv\alpha^{\ell^{m-1}}_{1}\pmod{\lambda^{m}}
and
\beta^{\ell^{m-1}}_{2}\equiv\alpha^{\ell^{m-1}}_{2}\pmod{\lambda^{m}}
.
This implies that the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\sigma^{\ell^{m-1}})
is congruent to
x^{2}-(\alpha^{\ell^{m-1}}_{1}+\alpha^{\ell^{m-1}}_{2})x+1\pmod{\lambda^{m}}.

Since
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
has order
\ell^{m-1}
, we have
\sigma^{\ell^{m-1}}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))
.
By composing
\rho_{f}|_{\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))}
with the projection
\tau_{m}:\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda^{m})
, we have a representation
\overline{\rho}_{f,m}:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda^{m})
.
This representation has finite image, so there exists a finite extension
L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}})
such that if
\tau:\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))\rightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))
is the restriction map, then there exists a representation
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,m}:\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda^{m})
such that
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,m}\circ\tau=\overline{\rho}_{f,m}
.
The Chebotarev density theorem implies that there is a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,m}(\text{Frob}_{Q}|_{L})
is conjugate to
\widetilde{\rho}_{f,m}(\sigma^{\ell^{m-1}}\big|_{L})
; the fact that
\text{Frob}_{Q}|_{L}\subset\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))
for such
Q
implies that
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
.
For such
Q
and any newform
f\in S^{\text{new}}_{\ell-r-2}(N_{r},\epsilon_{r})
, we have

f\big|T_{Q}\equiv(\text{tr }\widetilde{\rho}_{f,m}(\text{Frob}_{Q}|_{L}))f\equiv(\alpha^{\ell^{m-1}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}^{\ell^{m-1}})f\pmod{\lambda^{m}}.

∎

Remark
.

We assume that
\alpha\not\equiv\pm 2\pmod{\ell}
so that we can apply Hensel’s lemma. However, we do not need Hensel’s lemma when
m=1
. Thus,
if
m=1
, then we have the result when
\alpha\equiv\pm 2\pmod{\ell}
.

6.
Proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

We now prove Theorems
1.2-1.3
. First, we introduce some notation.
Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime. Suppose that
f\in S_{k}(1,\nu^{r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
with
f\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
, where
r
is an odd positive integer and
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
k\geq\frac{5}{2}
.
We define

S(r,k):=\begin{cases}S^{\operatorname{new}}_{2k-1}\left(2,\left(\frac{8}{r}\right)\right)&\text{if }3\mid r,\\
S^{\operatorname{new}}_{2k-1}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{r}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{r}\right)\right)&\text{if }3\nmid r\end{cases}

and (as in the introduction)

\chi^{(r)}:=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{-4}{\bullet}\right)&\text{if }3\mid r,\\
\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)&\text{if }3\nmid r.\end{cases}

For each squarefree
t
, let

F_{t}\in S(r,k)

be the form with
\text{Sh}_{t}f=F_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}
.
As
t
ranges over all of the squarefree integers, there are only finitely many non-zero possibilities for
F_{t}\pmod{\ell}
. Let
S:=\{F_{t_{1}},...,F_{t_{k}}\}
be a collection which represents all of these possibilities. By (
2.8
), we see that
S
is not empty.

The space
S(r,k)
is spanned by newforms
g_{1},...,g_{d}
.
For
j\in\{1,...,k\}
, write

F_{t_{j}}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}c_{i,j}g_{i},

and let
E
be a number field which contains the coefficients of each
g_{i}
as well as all of the coefficients
c_{i,j}
. Fix a prime
\lambda
of
E
over
\ell
and define

m(f):=\text{max}(1,1-\text{min}(\operatorname{ord}_{\lambda}(c_{i,j})))

(the definition depends on the choice of
\lambda
, but this is not important to us).
Before we prove Theorem
1.2
, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that
f\in S_{k}(1,\nu^{r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
, where
r
is an odd positive integer.
Suppose that the newforms
g_{i}
and the integer
m(f)
are defined as above.
Suppose that
Q\geq 5
is a prime and that
\lambda_{Q}
is an algebraic integer in
E
with the property that

g_{i}\big|T_{Q}\equiv\lambda_{Q}g_{i}\pmod{\lambda^{m(f)}}
for all
i
. Then

f\big|T_{Q^{2}}\equiv\chi^{(r)}(Q)\lambda_{Q}f\pmod{\lambda}.

Proof.

For each
t_{j}
we have

F_{t_{j}}\otimes\chi^{(r)}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}c_{i,j}g_{i}\otimes\chi^{(r)},

and for each
i
we have

\left(g_{i}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\right)\big|T_{Q}=\chi^{(r)}(Q)\left(g_{i}\big|T_{Q}\right)\otimes\chi^{(r)}\equiv\chi^{(r)}(Q)\lambda_{Q}g_{i}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\pmod{\lambda^{m(f)}}.

By the definition of
m(f)
, it follows for each
t_{j}
that

\left(F_{t_{j}}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\right)\big|T_{Q}\equiv\chi^{(r)}(Q)\lambda_{Q}F_{t_{j}}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\pmod{\lambda}.

Thus, for each squarefree
t
, we have

\operatorname{Sh}_{t}\left(f\big|T_{Q^{2}}\right)=\left(\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\right)\big|T_{Q}\equiv\chi^{(r)}(Q)\lambda_{Q}\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\pmod{\lambda}.

The result follows from (
2.8
).
∎

The next result explains how to produce congruences from Lemma
6.1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r
is an odd positive integer, and that
f=\sum a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\in S_{k}(1,\nu^{r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
.
Suppose that
Q\geq 5
is prime.
Suppose that there exists
\alpha_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
with

f\big|T_{Q^{2}}\equiv\alpha_{Q}\alpha Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}f\pmod{\ell}.

Then we have

a(Q^{2}n)\equiv(\alpha-1)\alpha_{Q}Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}a(n)\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\alpha_{Q}\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

This follows from the definition of the Hecke operator in (
2.7
). If
f\big|T_{Q^{2}}=\sum b(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}
, then the third term of
b(n)
does not contribute and if
\left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\alpha_{Q}\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}
, then the middle term of
b(n)
becomes
\alpha_{Q}Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}a(n)
.
∎

We now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r<\ell-4
is an odd positive integer and that
r
and
\ell
are compatible.
By Proposition
3.1
,
there is a modular form
f_{r,\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r\ell}_{\eta})
such that

f_{r,\ell}\equiv\sum p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell n+r}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

Since
\chi^{(-r\ell)}=\chi^{(r)}
, each of its Shimura lifts lands in the space
S\left(-r\ell,\frac{\ell-r-1}{2}\right)\otimes\chi^{(r)}
.

For each squarefree
t
, let
\tilde{F}_{t}\in S(-r\ell,\frac{\ell-r-1}{2})
be the form with
\text{Sh}_{t}f_{r,\ell}=\tilde{F}_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}
.
By
[
Ser76
, Exercise
6.4
]
, we see that there exists a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
and

\tilde{F}_{t}\big|T_{Q}\equiv 2\tilde{F}_{t}\pmod{\ell}.

for all squarefree
t
.
Thus, for all squarefree
t
, we have

\left(\tilde{F}_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\right)\big|T_{Q}\equiv 2\chi^{(r)}(Q)\tilde{F}_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\pmod{\ell},

which means that

\operatorname{Sh}_{t}\left(f\big|T_{Q^{2}}\right)=\left(\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\right)\big|T_{Q}\equiv 2\chi^{(r)}(Q)\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\pmod{\ell}.

It follows from (
2.8
) that

f_{r,\ell}\big|T_{Q^{2}}\equiv 2\chi^{(r)}(Q)f_{r,\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

Thus, for
\alpha\equiv 2\pmod{\ell}
, the result follows from Lemma
6.2
.

Recall the definitions of
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
in (
5.1
).
If
\alpha\not\equiv\pm 2\pmod{\ell}
, then Theorem
5.1
implies that there is a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
and for all of the newforms
g_{i}
in
S(-r\ell,\frac{\ell-r-1}{2})
, we have

(6.1)

g_{i}\big|T_{Q}\equiv(\alpha^{\ell^{m(f_{r,\ell})-1}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}^{\ell^{m(f_{r,\ell})-1}})g_{i}\pmod{\lambda^{m(f_{r,\ell})}}.

Since

(\alpha^{\ell^{m(f_{r,\ell})-1}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}^{\ell^{m(f_{r,\ell})-1}})\equiv(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})^{\ell^{m(f_{r,\ell})-1}}\equiv\alpha^{\ell^{m(f_{r,\ell})-1}}\equiv\alpha\pmod{\lambda},

it follows from Lemma
6.1
that
f_{r,\ell}\big|T_{Q^{2}}\equiv\alpha\chi^{(r)}(Q)f_{r,\ell}\pmod{\lambda}
. Therefore,

f_{r,\ell}\big|T_{Q^{2}}\equiv\alpha\chi^{(r)}(Q)f_{r,\ell}\pmod{\ell},

and the result follows from Lemma
6.2
.
∎

Remark
.

If there are no congruences between distinct newforms in
S(-r\ell,\frac{\ell-r-1}{2})
, then
m(f_{r,\ell})=1
by
[
AAT21
, Lemma
5.3
]
. By the remark following the proof of Theorem
5.1
, this implies that (
6.1
) holds when
\alpha\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
, which gives Theorem
1.2
for this case.

To prove Theorem
1.3
,
we make use of the following consequence of
[
AAT21
, Thm.
4.2
]
.
They only state the result for
f\in S^{\operatorname{new}}_{k}(6)
, but the same argument applies with
S^{\operatorname{new}}_{k}(6)
replaced by
S^{\operatorname{new}}_{k}(2)
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r
is an odd positive integer, and that there exists an integer
a
for which
2^{a}\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
.
Let
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
.
Then there exists a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
and for every
f\in S(r,k)

we have
f\big|T_{Q}\equiv-(-\epsilon_{2})^{a}Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}f\pmod{\ell}
, where
\epsilon_{2}
is the
W_{2}
eigenvalue of
f
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose that
r
is an odd positive integer. Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, that
r<\ell-4
and that there exists an integer
a
with
2^{a}\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
. For each squarefree
t
, let
\tilde{F}_{t}\in S(-r\ell,\frac{\ell-r-1}{2})
be the form with
\text{Sh}_{t}f_{r,\ell}=\tilde{F}_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}
.
By Theorem
6.3
, we see that there exists
\gamma\in\{\pm 1\}
and a positive density set
S
of primes such that if
Q\in S
, then
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
and

\tilde{F}_{t}\big|T_{Q}\equiv\gamma Q^{\frac{\ell-r-4}{2}}\tilde{F}_{t}\pmod{\ell}.

for all
t
.
Thus, for all
t
, we have

\left(\tilde{F}_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\right)\big|T_{Q}\equiv\gamma\chi^{(r)}(Q)Q^{\frac{\ell-r-4}{2}}\tilde{F}_{t}\otimes\chi^{(r)}\pmod{\ell},

which means that

\operatorname{Sh}_{t}\left(f_{r,\ell}\big|T_{Q^{2}}\right)=\left(\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f_{r,\ell}\right)\big|T_{Q}\equiv\gamma\chi^{(r)}(Q)Q^{\frac{\ell-r-4}{2}}\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f_{r,\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

It follows from (
2.8
) that

f_{r,\ell}\big|T_{Q^{2}}\equiv\gamma\chi^{(r)}(Q)Q^{\frac{\ell-r-4}{2}}f_{r,\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

It then follows (after setting
\alpha_{Q}=\gamma\chi^{(r)}(Q)
and
\beta=1
) from Lemma
6.2

that there exists
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
with the property that

p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{ if }\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\epsilon_{Q}.

This proves part
(1)
of Theorem
1.3
.

We now prove part
(2)
.
Under the hypotheses on the integers
-r\leq w<0
which satisfy
w\equiv-r\pmod{24}
,
we have the form
g_{r,\ell}=\sum a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\in S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-r-1}{2}}(1,\nu^{-r}_{\eta},\mathbb{Z})
constructed in Proposition
3.2
. It satisfies

a(n)\equiv p_{r}\left(\frac{n+r}{24}\right)\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \ \text{ when }\left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=-1.

We now argue as in the proof of part
(1)
to conclude that there exists
\epsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
for which

p_{r}\left(\frac{Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{-rn}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\epsilon_{Q}.

The result follows.
∎

7.
The case when
r=\ell-4

When
r=\ell-4
, we obtain results stronger than Theorems
1.1-1.3
. The following result gives a short proof of
[
Boy04
, Thm.
2.1
,
(2)
]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

If
\ell\not\equiv 1\pmod{6}
is prime, then
f_{\ell-4,\ell}=0
. Thus, we have

p_{\ell-4}\left(\frac{\ell n+\ell-4}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{for all $n$}.

Proof.

Note that

(7.1)

f_{\ell-4,\ell}\in S_{\frac{3}{2}}(1,\nu^{4\ell-1}_{\eta}).

Let
s\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
s\equiv 4\ell-1\pmod{24}
and
0<s<24
. By (
2.4
), we see that
f_{\ell-4,\ell}
vanishes to order
\geq s
.
Since
\ell\not\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, we have
s>3
, so
\eta^{-3}f_{\ell-4,\ell}\in S_{0}(1,\nu_{\eta}^{4\ell-4})=\{0\}
. The result follows.
∎

When
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, we can use the fact that
\eta^{3}
is an eigenform for the Hecke operators
T_{Q^{2}}
for all primes
Q\geq 3
to produce the following congruences.

Lemma 7.2
.

If
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{6}
is prime, then
f_{\ell-4}=c\eta^{3}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}
. For all primes
Q\geq 3
, if
Q^{2}\nmid n
, then

p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)\left(Q+1-\left(\frac{12n}{Q}\right)\right)p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell n+r}{24}\right)\pmod{\ell}.

Proof.

Since
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{6}
, we know that
3\mid 4\ell-1
.
We also see by (
7.1
) that

\eta^{-3}f_{\ell-4,\ell}\in M_{0}(1,\nu^{4\ell-4}_{\eta})=M_{0}(1).

Since
f_{\ell-4,\ell}
has integral coefficients, we conclude that
f_{\ell-4,\ell}=c\eta^{3}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}
.
It follows from (
2.7
) for each prime
Q\geq 3
that

\eta^{3}\big|T_{Q^{2}}=\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)(Q+1)\eta^{3}.

Thus, when
Q^{2}\nmid n
, we have

p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell Q^{2}n+r}{24}\right)\equiv\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)\left(Q+1-\left(\frac{12n}{Q}\right)\right)p_{r}\left(\frac{\ell n+r}{24}\right)\pmod{\ell}.

∎

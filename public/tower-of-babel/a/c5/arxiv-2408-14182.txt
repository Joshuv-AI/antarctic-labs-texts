Explicit bounds for Bell numbers and their ratios
Jerzy Grunwald, Grzegorz Serafin

Abstract
In this article, we provide a comprehensive analysis of the asymptotic behavior of Bell numbers, enhancing and unifying various results previously dispersed in the literature. We establish several explicit lower and upper bounds. The main results correspond to two asymptotic forms expressed by means of the Lambert W W function. As an application, some straightforward elementary bounds are derived. Additionally, an absolute convergence rate of the ratio of the consecutive Bell numbers is derived. The main challenge was to obtain satisfactory constants, as the Bell numbers grow rapidly, while the convergence rates are rather slow.



Keywords
:
Bell numbers, ratio, bounds, convergence rates, asymptotics

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11B73, 05A16, 26D07

1
Introduction

The Bell numbers
B_{n}
are classical objects in combinatorial theory and have been studied for more than one and a half centuries. They are subject of numerous studies as they have various applications in other areas of mathematics. In particular,
B_{n}
represents the
n
’th moment of the Poisson distribution with the intensity parameter equal to
1
. In spite of many formulae and relationships involving Bell numbers, there is known no simple explicit formula that is applicable for evaluating them for large
n
. The estimates are therefore strongly desirable. The first remarkable bound is due to de Bruijn
[
5
]
, who showed

\displaystyle\frac{\ln B_{n}}{n}=\left(\ln n-\ln\ln n-1+\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n}+\frac{1}{\ln n}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n}\right)^{2}\right)+O\left(\frac{\ln\ln n}{(\ln n)^{2}}\right).

(1.1)

Nevertheless, one can see that this result is imprecise for two reasons: the constants are unknown and the multiplicative error term
\exp\left({n\ln\ln n/(\ln n)^{2}}\right)
tends to infinity as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The asymptotics of
B_{n}
have been derived in
[
10
]
(see also
[
8
,
12
]
) in terms of the Lambert
W
function, which is the inverse of the function
[-1,\infty)\ni x\rightarrow x\e^{x}
:

\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}\longrightarrow 1,\ \ \ \ \ \text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty,

(1.2)

where

E_{n}^{*}=\frac{\exp\left({\e^{W(n)}+nW(n)-(n+1)}\right)}{\sqrt{1+W(n)}}.

The most precise bounds are due to D. Knuth, who showed in
[
9
, formulae (30) and (31) in Section 7.2.1.5.]
that

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle=E_{n}^{*}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{12\e^{W(n)}}+O(\tfrac{1}{n})\right),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\e^{W(n)}+O(\tfrac{1}{W(n)}).

(1.4)

In fact, he claimed that the error bound in (
1.3
) is
O((\ln n)/n)^{2}
, which seems to be a mistake. This might be verified by following the argumentation in
[
9
]
, and is confirmed by Theorem
4.1
of this article. It is interesting, despite their quality, the above bounds seem to be often overlooked in the context of the Bell numbers.
The convergence rate to another asymptotic form

E_{n}=\frac{n!\exp\left(\e^{R}-1\right)}{R^{n}\sqrt{2\pi(n+1)(R+1)}},\qquad R=W(n+1).

has been derived in
[
7
, Proposition VIII.3.]

\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}=1+O\big(\e^{-W(n+1)/5}\big).

(1.5)

However, the error bound turns out not to be optimal.

All the aforementioned results have been achieved using analytic tools. Recently, some probabilistic approaches have been proposed as well. For instance, the asymptotics have been rediscovered in
[
13
]
. Furthermore, an interesting argument allowed the authors of
[
1
]
to obtain the bound
B_{n}\leq\sqrt{1+W(n)}E_{n}^{*}
. It does not exactly match the asymptotic form, but its proof is extremely short, which deserves some attention.

The main weakness of the results presented above is that one cannot use them to conclude any bound for
B_{n}
for a given
n
. The explicit upper bound

\displaystyle B_{n}\leq\left(\frac{0.792n}{\ln(n+1)}\right)^{n},\hskip 40.0ptn\geq 1,

(1.6)

is derived in
[
2
]
, however, it does not recover the asymptotic behavior of the Bell numbers. Note that the remarkable interest in the article
[
2
]
reveals the requirement for bounds of that type.

In this paper we improve the existing results in several directions. First of all, we establish two-sided estimates of
B_{n}
, which describe asymptotic behaviour of the Bell numbers with precise rates of convergence (see Theorem
4.6
):

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{5}\frac{\ln n}{n}\leq\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}\leq 1,\hskip 40.0ptn\geq 2.

(1.7)

Additionally, in Corollary
4.5
we show that the order of the above error term is optimal. In Theorem
4.1
an even more precise bound is derived, which provides explicit constants in the bound (
1.3
). Nevertheless, it is more complex and plays rather an auxiliary role. Many results are presented in the language of the asymptotic form
E_{n}
as well, however, the one above is the most elegant one. Furthermore,
the exponent appearing in the definition of
E^{*}_{n}
is equal to
\int_{0}^{n}W(x)dx
, which appears to be quite helpful in some of the proofs.
To the authors knowledge, this simplification has not been observed in the context of Bell numbers so far. As an application of the bound (
1.7
) we derive in Proposition
4.7
the following simple elementary bounds

\left(\frac{1}{\e}\frac{n}{\ln n}\right)^{n}\leq B_{n}\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\frac{n}{\ln n}\right)^{n},\hskip 40.0ptn\geq 2.

Here, the main contribution is the lower bound, as the upper one is very similar to (
1.6
).
Another main result of the paper is the following bound of the ratio of two consecutive Bell numbers (see Theorem
5.2
)

\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}\right|\leq\frac{8}{7}\frac{1}{W(n)},\hskip 40.0ptn\geq 1,

which complements (
1.4
) with explicit constants.
Let us point out that even though the function
\e^{W(x)}
(which is the inverse of
x\ln x
) tends to infinity as
x\rightarrow\infty
, the obtained error is an absolute one.

The basic idea of the proofs relies on the saddle point method, that was already used in e.g.,
[
5
,
7
,
9
,
11
]
. these references are books that consider the Bell numbers as just one of many examples, with relatively little attention devoted to them, which was one of the motivations to write this article. Although the starting point of the main proof is not new, many obstacles appear when it comes to the details. This is a consequence of the fact that some of the expressions in the proofs converge very slowly. Some difficulties could be avoided by considering very large
n
, but then verifying the initial values might be beyond the capabilities of current computers, as the Bell numbers grow very rapidly. We therefore propose an approach that ensures some kind of balance between optimization of constants and effort put in. In particular, properties of the Lambert
W
functions are intensively exploited. After all, we leave for numerical verification the obtained bounds for
n\leq 741
, which is equivalent to the condition
W(n+1)\geq 5
. This is possible to perform quickly on an average computer. Additionally, the crucial bounds are customisable, so that one can easily improve the constants if interested in larger indices.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 the Bell numbers and the Lambert
W
function are introduced, and properties of some special sequence are studied, that are frequently used later on. Section 3 contains customisable bounds on integrals constituting the numbers
B_{n}
. Section 4 is devoted to explicit convergence rates of
B_{n}
, and in Section 5 we deal with the ratio
B_{n}/B_{n-1}
. In Appendix, one can find a code in Python of a program that verifies numerically some of the results for the initial Bell numbers.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Bell numbers

The Bell numbers are numbers of partitions of a set of
n
elements.
They satisfy the following recurrence formula

B_{0}=1,\hskip 28.45274ptB_{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{n\choose k}B_{k},

which is usually exploit to produce consecutive values of
B_{n}
. One can also use it to obtain the exponential generating function

\displaystyle B(t):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\e^{\e^{t}-1},\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.1)

The famous Dobiński formula
[
6
]
states that

\displaystyle B_{n}=\frac{1}{\e}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{n}}{k!},\ \ \ n\geq 1.

(2.2)

As mentioned in Introduction, the main two asymptotic forms of the Bell numbers are

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!\exp\left(\e^{R}-1\right)}{R^{n}\sqrt{2\pi(n+1)(R+1)}},\quad R=W(n+1)

(2.3)

\displaystyle E_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left({\e^{W(n)}+nW(n)-(n+1)}\right)}{\sqrt{1+W(n)}},

(2.4)

i.e., we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}=1.

2.2
Lambert
W
function

By
W(x)
,
x\geq 0
, we denote the principal branch of the Lambert
W
function, which is the inverse of the function
[-1,\infty)\ni x\rightarrow x\e^{x}
. Directly from the definition we obtain the relation

\displaystyle\e^{W(x)}=\frac{x}{W(x)}.

(2.5)

Using elementary calculus we get the following formula for the derivative of
W(x)

\displaystyle W^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{x+{\e^{W(x)}}}.

(2.6)

In particular, this means that
W
is concave. Thus, for
0<x<y
we have

\displaystyle W(y)-W(x)

\displaystyle\leq W^{\prime}(x)(y-x)=\frac{y-x}{x+\e^{W(x)}}\leq\frac{y-x}{x}.

(2.7)

The function
W
cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions, but several series representations are available. One of them is given by
[
4
]

W(x)=\ln x-\ln\ln x+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}|s(k+m,k+1)|}{m!}\frac{(\ln\ln x)^{m}}{(\ln x)^{k+m}},

where
s(n,k)
stands for the Stirling numbers of the first kind. Nevertheless, the above series is not easy to work with, thus rather simple bounds of the function
W(x)
are required. One can easily check that

\displaystyle\ln x-\ln\ln x\leq W(x)\leq\ln x,\ \ \ x\geq\e.

(2.8)

The value of the Lambert
W
function at
1
is called the omega constant and equals

\displaystyle\Omega=W(1)=0.567\ldots.

(2.9)

At the end of this section we invoke the formula for the integral of the Lambert
W
function. Namely, by virtue of the standard formula for the integral of an inverse function, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}W(s)ds=\e^{W(x)}+xW(x)-x-1,\hskip 56.9055ptx\geq 0.

(2.10)

2.3
A special sequence

Let us denote

q_{n}:=Q(W(n+1)):=1-\e^{-W(n+1)}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2W(n+1)}-\tfrac{10}{{W(n+1)}^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{{W(n+1)}^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{{W(n+1)}^{4}}}{12\left(1+\tfrac{1}{W(n+1)}\right)^{3}},

which turns out to play an important role in asymptotics of the Bell numbers. In particluar, it approximates
B_{n}/E_{n}
in Theorem
4.1
. Here are some properties of the sequence
q_{n}
, that will be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
W(n+1)\geq 5
(i.e.,
n\geq 742
), then

\displaystyle 1\geq q_{n}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{\e^{-W(n+1)}}{12},

(2.11)

\displaystyle q_{n}+1.6\e^{-2W(n+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

(2.12)

\displaystyle|q_{n+1}-q_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\e^{-W(n+1)}}{10(n+1)}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Regarding (
2.11
), it suffices to show that the expression

\tfrac{3}{2x}+\tfrac{10}{{x}^{2}}+\tfrac{9}{{x}^{3}}-\tfrac{1}{{x}^{4}}

is positive and less than one for
x\geq 5
. Positivity is clear. Furthermore, it is a decreasing function of
x
(the negative sign of
1/x^{4}
does not change much), and its value at
5
is less than one, which proves (
2.11
).

Next, we have

Q(x)+1.6\e^{-2x}=1-\frac{\e^{-x}}{12(1+\frac{1}{x})^{3}}\big(\underbrace{1-\tfrac{3}{2x}-\tfrac{10}{{x}^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{{x}^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{{x}^{4}}-19.2(1+\tfrac{1}{x})^{3}\e^{-x}}_{:=f(x)}\big).

Since the function
f
is increasing on
[5,\infty)
and

f(5)=1-\tfrac{3}{10}-\tfrac{2}{5}-\tfrac{9}{125}-\tfrac{1}{625}-19.2(\tfrac{6}{5})^{3}\e^{-5}=\frac{141.5-20736\e^{-5}}{625}\geq 0,

we get (
2.12
).

Finally, we will derive (
2.13
) bounding the derivative of
Q
. Precisely, we have

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\e^{-x}}{24}\frac{2x^{6}-x^{5}-32x^{4}-72x^{3}-50x^{2}+10x+2}{x^{2}(x+1)^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\e^{-x}}{24}\left[2\left(\frac{x}{x+1}\right)^{4}-\frac{x^{3}}{(x+1)^{4}}-32\frac{x^{2}}{(x+1)^{4}}\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=\frac{\e^{-x}}{24}=}-\left.72\frac{x}{(x+1)^{4}}-\frac{50}{(x+1)^{4}}+\frac{10}{x(x+1)^{4}}+\frac{2}{x^{2}(x+1)^{4}}\right],

and hence, for
x\geq 5
,

\displaystyle|Q^{\prime}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\e^{-x}}{24}\left[2\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^{4}+\frac{5^{3}}{6^{4}}+\frac{32\cdot 5^{2}}{6^{4}}+\frac{72\cdot 5}{6^{4}}+\frac{50}{6^{4}}+\frac{10}{5\cdot 6^{4}}+\frac{2}{5^{2}\cdot 6^{4}}\right]\leq\frac{\e^{-x}}{10}.

Thus, the mean value theorem and the inequality (
2.7
) give us

\displaystyle|q_{n+1}-q_{n}|\leq\sup_{x\in\left(W(n+1),~W(n+2)\right)}|Q^{\prime}(x)|\left(W(n+2)-W(n+1)\right)\leq\frac{\e^{-W(n+1)}}{10(n+1)},

which ends the proof.
∎

3
Customizable bounds

By the Cauchy theorem and the formula for the exponential generating function (
2.1
) we get

B_{n}=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}\frac{\exp\left(\e^{z}-1\right)}{z^{n+1}}dz,

(3.1)

where
\Gamma
is the circle centred at the origin

.
Next, since the function

is holomorphic on

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}:\gamma_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=R+it,t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}:\gamma_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=R-t+i\varepsilon,t\in(0,R-\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{3}:\gamma_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=t-i\varepsilon,t\in(\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}},R),

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{4}:\gamma_{4}(t)

\displaystyle=R\e^{it},t\in(\delta,2\pi-\delta),\delta=\arcsin\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\right).

(3.5)

\Re
\Im
\Gamma_{4}
\Gamma_{1}
\Gamma_{2}
\Gamma_{3}

\varepsilon

This gives us

B_{n}=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

(3.6)

where

J_{i}=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\exp\left(\e^{z}-1\right)}{z^{n+1}}dz.

For any fixed positive integer
n
the optimal
R
turns out to be
W(n+1)
, hence, from now on, we denote

R=R(n)=W(n+1).

Proposition 3.1
.

For
R\geq 5
and
\varepsilon<1
such that
\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}>5
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{1}}{E_{n}}-\left(1-\frac{\e^{-R}}{12}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\exp\left({{-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\e^{R}-\frac{1}{2}R}}\right)+\frac{6}{5}\exp\left(\frac{\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}}{22}-2R\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{7}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}+\tfrac{5}{2}R\right)}{30\left(\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}-5\right)}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Using (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_{1}}\frac{\exp\left(\e^{z}-1\right)}{z^{n+1}}dz=\frac{n!}{2\pi\e}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\e^{F(t)}dt,

where

F(t)=\e^{R+it}-(n+1)\Log(R+it),\ \ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Here,
\Log(\cdot)
stands for the principal value of the complex logarithm. It is easy to calculate that

F^{(k)}(t)=i^{k}\left(\e^{R+it}+(-1)^{k}(n+1)\frac{(k-1)!}{(R+it)^{k}}\right),\ \ \ \ k\geq 1.

(3.8)

Taking
R
such that
R\e^{R}=n+1
(or equivalently
R=W(n+1)
), for
|t|<R
we have

\displaystyle F(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{F^{(k)}(0)}{k!}t^{k}=\e^{R}-(n+1)\ln(R)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}i^{k}\left(\e^{R}+(-1)^{k}(n+1)\frac{(k-1)!}{R^{k}}\right)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\e^{R}-(n+1)\ln(R)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}i^{k}\e^{R}\left(1+(-1)^{k}\frac{(k-1)!}{R^{k-1}}\right)\frac{t^{k}}{k!},

where the last equality is a consequence of
F^{\prime}(0)=i\left(\e^{R}-\frac{R\e^{R}}{R}\right)=0
. Next, we decompose

\displaystyle\e^{F(t)}

\displaystyle=\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}+\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left(\e^{F(t)-F(0)-F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}-1\right).

(3.9)

The two terms on the right-hand side are crucial for the behaviour of the Bell numbers. The first one drives the asymptotic form. Indeed, when integrated over whole real line, we get

\frac{n!}{2\pi\e}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}dt=\frac{n!\e^{F(0)}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\e\sqrt{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}}=\frac{n!\e^{\e^{R}-(n+1)\ln R-\tfrac{1}{2}R}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\e\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{R}}}=E_{n},

(3.10)

and consequently

\displaystyle\left|\frac{n!}{2\pi\e}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}dt-E_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{n!}{\pi\e}\e^{F(0)}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\e^{F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}dt\right|=\frac{n!}{\sqrt{2\pi}\e}\frac{\e^{F(0)}}{\sqrt{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}}\erfc\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e\varepsilon{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}}\e^{F(0)-\varepsilon^{2}|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e\varepsilon\sqrt{\e^{R}|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}}\e^{F(0)-\varepsilon^{2}\e^{R}/2}

\displaystyle=E_{n}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\e^{-\varepsilon^{2}\e^{R}/2-R/2}}{\varepsilon},

where we used (
3.10
) and the inequalities
F^{\prime\prime}(0)\leq-\e^{R}
and
\erfc(t)\leq{\e^{-t^{2}}}/{t\sqrt{\pi}}
,
t>0
.
The latter term in (
3.9
) determines the convergence rate, and will be therefore bounded very precisely. Since
\e^{\overline{F(z)}}=\overline{\e^{F(z)}}
, we may write

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{2\pi\e}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left(\e^{F(t)-F(0)-F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}-1\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\pi\e}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\Re\left[\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left(\e^{F(t)-F(0)-F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}-1\right)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\pi\e}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left(\cos(F_{1}(t))\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1\right)dt,

where

\displaystyle F_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\Im\left(F(t)-F(0)-F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\e^{R}\left(1-\frac{(2k)!}{R^{2k}}\right)\frac{t^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!},

\displaystyle F_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\Re\left(F(t)-F(0)-F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2\right)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\e^{R}\left(1+\frac{(2k-1)!}{R^{2k-1}}\right)\frac{t^{2k}}{(2k)!}.

Let us now estimate the integrand in the last integral. This is a delicate task, since
\cos\left(F_{1}(t)\right)
is decreasing, while
\e^{F_{2}(t)}
is increasing.
We will approximate
\cos(F_{1}(t))\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1
by
F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\tfrac{t^{6}}{2(3!)^{2}}
and estimate the difference between them. This approach is a consequence of multivariate Taylor theorem. We have

\displaystyle\left(\cos(F_{1}(t))\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1\right)-\left(F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\tfrac{t^{6}}{2(3!)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\cos(F_{1}(t))\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1\right)-\left(F_{2}(t)-\tfrac{1}{2}(F_{1}(t))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left(F_{2}(t)-\tfrac{1}{2}(F_{1}(t))^{2}\right)-\left(F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\tfrac{t^{6}}{2(3!)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\e^{F_{2}(t)}\left(\cos(F_{1}(t))-1+\tfrac{1}{2}(F_{1}(t))^{2}\right)+\left(\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1-F_{2}(t)\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}(F_{1}(t))^{2}\left(1-\e^{F_{2}(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left(F_{2}(t)-F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(F_{1}(t)-F^{(3)}(0)\tfrac{t^{3}}{3!}\right)\left(F_{1}(t)+F^{(3)}(0)\tfrac{t^{3}}{3!}\right).

One can easily verify that absolute values of coefficients in both of the series defining
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are decreasing for
R\geq 5
and
|t|<1
, hence we may bound them by their first terms:

\displaystyle|F_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|F^{(3)}(0)|t^{3}}{3!}\leq\frac{\e^{R}|t|^{3}}{3!}\left(1-\frac{2}{R^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{\e^{R}|t|^{3}}{6},

\displaystyle|F_{2}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|F^{(4)}(0)|t^{4}}{4!}\leq\frac{\e^{R}t^{4}}{4!}\left(1+\frac{3!}{5^{3}}\right)\leq\frac{2}{45}{\e^{R}t^{4}}.

Analogously we also get

\displaystyle|F_{1}(t)-F^{(3)}(0)\tfrac{t^{3}}{3!}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|F^{(5)}(0)|t^{5}}{5!}\leq\frac{\e^{R}|t|^{5}}{5!},

\displaystyle|F_{2}(t)-F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|F^{(6)}(0)|t^{6}}{6!}\leq\frac{\e^{R}t^{6}}{6!}\left(1+\frac{5!}{5^{5}}\right)\leq\frac{27}{26}\frac{\e^{R}t^{6}}{6!}.

Using also inequalities
|\cos x-1+\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}|<\tfrac{1}{4!}x^{4}
,
|\e^{x}-1|\leq x\e^{x}
and
|\e^{x}-1-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\e^{x}
,
x>0

\displaystyle\left|\left(\cos(F_{1}(t))\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1\right)-\left(F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\tfrac{t^{6}}{2(3!)^{2}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\e^{F_{2}(t)}\left(\frac{(F_{1}(t))^{4}}{4!}+\frac{(F_{2}(t))^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(F_{1}(t))^{2}F_{2}(t)\right)+\frac{|F^{(6)}(0)|t^{6}}{6!}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|F^{(5)}(0)|t^{5}}{5!}2\frac{|F^{(3)}(0)|t^{3}}{3!}

\displaystyle\leq\e^{2\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/45}\left(\frac{\e^{4R}t^{12}}{4!6^{4}}+\frac{2\e^{2R}t^{8}}{(45)^{2}}+\frac{\e^{2R}t^{6}}{6^{2}}\frac{\e^{R}t^{4}}{45}\right)+\frac{27}{26}\frac{\e^{R}t^{6}}{6!}+\frac{\e^{2R}t^{8}}{6!}

\displaystyle\leq\e^{\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/22}\left(\frac{\e^{4R}t^{12}}{4!6^{4}}+\frac{\e^{2R}t^{8}}{420}+\frac{\e^{2R}t^{6}}{6^{2}}\frac{\e^{R}t^{4}}{45}+\frac{27}{26}\frac{\e^{R}t^{6}}{6!}\right),

where we used
2\cdot 45^{-2}+(6!)^{-1}\leq 1/416
. Consequently

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\pi\e}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left|\left(\cos(F_{1}(t))\e^{F_{2}(t)}-1\right)-\left(F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\tfrac{t^{6}}{2(3!)^{2}}\right)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!\e^{F(0)}}{\pi\e}\e^{2\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/45}\int_{0}^{\infty}\e^{F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left(\frac{\e^{4R}}{4\cdot 6^{5}}t^{12}+\frac{\e^{2R}}{416}t^{8}+\frac{\e^{3R}}{1620}t^{10}+\frac{3\e^{R}}{2080}t^{6}\right)dt.

Applying the bound

\int_{0}^{\infty}\e^{F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}t^{k}dt=\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{2}{F^{\prime\prime}(0)}\right|^{(k+1)/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{2}\right)\leq\frac{2^{(k-1)/2}\e^{-Rk/2}}{\sqrt{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}}\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{2}\right),

and verifying

\Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)=\frac{15\sqrt{\pi}}{8},\ \ \ \Gamma\left(\frac{9}{2}\right)=\frac{7!!\sqrt{\pi}}{16}.\ \ \ \Gamma\left(\frac{11}{2}\right)=\frac{9!!\sqrt{\pi}}{32},\ \ \ \Gamma\left(\frac{13}{2}\right)=\frac{11!!\sqrt{\pi}}{64},

we estimate further

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!\e^{F(0)}}{{\pi}\e\sqrt{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}}\e^{\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/22}{\e^{-2R}}\left(\frac{2^{11/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{13}{2}\right)}{4\cdot 6^{5}}+\frac{2^{7/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{9}{2}\right)}{416}+\frac{2^{9/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{11}{2}\right)}{1620}+\frac{3\cdot 2^{5/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)}{2080}\right)

\displaystyle=E_{n}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\e^{\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/22}{\e^{-2R}}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{13}{2}\right)}{2\cdot 3^{5}\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{9}{2}\right)}{26\sqrt{2}}+\frac{8\Gamma\left(\frac{11}{2}\right)}{405\sqrt{2}}+\frac{3\Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)}{260\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=E_{n}\e^{\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/22}{\e^{-2R}}\left(\frac{385}{1152}+\frac{105}{416}+\frac{21}{36}+\frac{9}{416}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{5}E_{n}\e^{\e^{R}\varepsilon^{4}/22}{\e^{-2R}}.

Eventually, substituting
u=F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\pi\e}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\e^{F(0)+F^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}/2}\left(F^{(4)}(0)\tfrac{t^{4}}{4!}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\tfrac{t^{6}}{2(3!)^{2}}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n!\e^{F(0)}}{\pi\e}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}}\e^{-u}\left(\frac{F^{(4)}(0)}{48}\left|\frac{2}{F^{\prime\prime}(0)}\right|^{5/2}u^{3/2}-\frac{(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}}{144}\left|\frac{2}{F^{\prime\prime}(0)}\right|^{7/2}u^{5/2}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n!\e^{F(0)}}{\pi\e}\left(F^{(4)}(0)\frac{\Gamma\left(5/2\right)\sqrt{2}}{12|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{5/2}}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\frac{\Gamma\left({7/2}\right)\sqrt{2}}{18|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{7/2}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\frac{n!\e^{F(0)}}{\pi\e}\int_{\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}}^{\infty}\e^{-u}\left(F^{(4)}(0)\frac{u^{3/2}\sqrt{2}}{12|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{5/2}}-(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}\frac{u^{5/2}\sqrt{2}}{18|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{7/2}}\right)dt

\displaystyle=E_{n}\left(\frac{F^{(4)}(0)}{8|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{2}}-\frac{5(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}}{24|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -\frac{E_{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{F^{(4)}(0)}{6|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2},\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}\right)-\frac{(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}}{9|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{3}}\Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2},\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}\right)\right),

where
\Gamma(p,x)
stands for the incomplete Gamma function. From Theorem 2.1 in
[
3
]
we have

\Gamma(p,x)\leq\frac{x^{p}\e^{-x}}{x-p+1},\ \ \ \ p\geq 1,x>p-1.

Using this and the equalities
|F^{(k)}(0)|\leq\e^{R}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)
,
R>\sqrt{6}
, for
k=3
and
k=4
, we get for
\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}>5

\displaystyle\left|\frac{E_{n}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{F^{(4)}(0)}{6|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2},\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}\right)-\frac{(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}}{9|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{3}}\Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2},\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq{E_{n}}\frac{\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\left(\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|/2}-\tfrac{5}{2}\right)}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{5/2}\left(\frac{1}{6}\frac{\varepsilon^{5}\e^{3R/2}}{2^{5/2}}+\frac{1}{9}\frac{\varepsilon^{7}\e^{5R/2}}{2^{7/2}}\right)

\displaystyle={E_{n}}\frac{\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}\right)}{\varepsilon^{2}{|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|}-5}\varepsilon^{7}\e^{5R/2}\frac{(1+\tfrac{1}{R})^{5/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{2}{9\cdot 2^{7/2}}\left(\frac{3}{\varepsilon^{2}\e^{R}}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq{E_{n}}\frac{\varepsilon^{7}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}+\tfrac{5}{2}R\right)}{\left(\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}-5\right)}\frac{(1+\tfrac{1}{5})^{5/2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{9\cdot 2^{5/2}}\left(\frac{3}{5}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq{E_{n}}\frac{\varepsilon^{7}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}+\tfrac{5}{2}R\right)}{30\left(\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}-5\right)}.

Additionally,

\displaystyle\frac{F^{(4)}(0)}{8|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{2}}-\frac{5(F^{(3)}(0))^{2}}{24|F^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{3}}

\displaystyle=\e^{-R}\frac{\left(1+\tfrac{6}{R^{3}}\right)}{8\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{2}}-\e^{-R}\frac{5\left(1-\tfrac{2}{R^{2}}\right)^{2}}{24\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\e^{-R}\left(\frac{3\left(1+\tfrac{6}{R^{3}}\right)\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)}{24\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}-\frac{5\left(1-\tfrac{2}{R^{2}}\right)^{2}}{24\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\e^{-R}}{12}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For
0<\varepsilon<\tfrac{1}{2}
and
R\geq 5
it holds

\left|\frac{J_{2}+J_{3}}{E_{n}}\right|\leq{\exp\left({\e^{R}(\cos\varepsilon-1)-{\tfrac{1}{2}R(1-\varepsilon^{2})}}\right)}.

Proof.

First, let us write
J_{2}+J_{3}
in the following form

J_{2}+J_{3}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}^{R}\frac{\exp\left(\e^{t-i\varepsilon}-1\right)}{(t-i\varepsilon)^{n+1}}-\frac{\exp\left(\e^{t+i\varepsilon}-1\right)}{(t+i\varepsilon)^{n+1}}dt.

Next, denoting
\alpha_{t}=\arg(t+i\varepsilon)
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\left(\e^{t-i\varepsilon}-1\right)}{(t-i\varepsilon)^{n+1}}-\frac{\exp\left(\e^{t+i\varepsilon}-1\right)}{(t+i\varepsilon)^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left({\e^{t}\cos\varepsilon}\right)}{\e(t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{(n+1)/2}}\left(\e^{i(-\sin\varepsilon+\alpha_{t})}-\e^{i(\sin\varepsilon-\alpha_{t})}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left({\e^{t}\cos\varepsilon}\right)}{\e(t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2})^{(n+1)/2}}2i\sin\left(\alpha_{t}-\sin\varepsilon\right).

This gives us

\displaystyle\left|J_{2}+J_{3}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}^{R}\frac{\exp\left(\e^{t-i\varepsilon}-1\right)}{(t-i\varepsilon)^{n+1}}-\frac{\exp\left(\e^{t+i\varepsilon}-1\right)}{(t+i\varepsilon)^{n+1}}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e}\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}^{R}\frac{\exp(\e^{t}\cos(\varepsilon))}{\e(\sqrt{t^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}})^{n+1}}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}^{R}\frac{\e^{t}}{\e^{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}}{\exp(\e^{t}\cos(\varepsilon))}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}\e^{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}\cos(\varepsilon)}\left(\e^{\e^{R}\cos\varepsilon}-\e^{\e^{\sqrt{R^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}}\cos\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!\exp\left({\e^{R}\cos\varepsilon-{R(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2})}}\right)}{\pi\e R^{n+1}\cos\varepsilon},

where in the last inequality we simply omitted the latter term in the parentheses.
Consequently, we may write

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{2}+J_{3}}{E_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!\exp\left({\e^{R}\cos\varepsilon-{R(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2})}}\right)}{\pi\e R^{n+1}\cos\varepsilon}\frac{R^{n}\sqrt{2\pi(n+1)(R+1)}}{n!\exp\left(\e^{R}-1\right)}

\displaystyle\leq{\exp\left({\e^{R}(\cos\varepsilon-1)-{\tfrac{1}{2}R(1-\varepsilon^{2})}}\right)}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi(1+\tfrac{1}{R})}}{\pi\cos\varepsilon}.

Finally, the bound

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2\pi(1+\tfrac{1}{R})}}{\pi\cos\varepsilon}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi(1+\tfrac{1}{4})}}{\pi\cos(1/2)}\leq 1

ends the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

For
R\geq 4
and
\varepsilon<1/2
we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{4}}{E_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\exp\left(\e^{R}(\cos(\varepsilon)-1)-\tfrac{1}{2}R\right)+R\exp\left(-\tfrac{2}{R}\e^{R}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\right)\right].

Proof.

Let us rewrite

\displaystyle\left|J_{4}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{n!}{2\pi i}\int_{\delta}^{2\pi-\delta}\frac{\exp\left(\e^{R\e^{it}}-1\right)}{\left(R\e^{it}\right)^{n+1}}Ri\e^{it}dt\right|=\frac{n!}{2\pi\e R^{n}}\left|\int_{\delta}^{2\pi-\delta}{\exp\left(\e^{R\e^{it}}\right)}\e^{-int}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{2\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{\delta}^{2\pi-\delta}\left|{\exp\left(\e^{R\e^{it}}\right)}\e^{-int}\right|dt

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\pi}{\exp\left(\e^{R\cos t}\cos(R\sin t)\right)}dt,

where
\delta=\arcsin\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\right)
. Now we split the last integral into three ones over the intervals
(\delta,\tfrac{6}{5R})
,
(\tfrac{6}{5R},\tfrac{4}{R})
and
(\tfrac{4}{R},\pi)
.

\displaystyle J_{4,1}

\displaystyle:=\left|\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{\delta}^{6/5R}\exp\left(\e^{R\cos t}\cos(R\sin t)\right)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{\delta}^{6/5R}\frac{\e^{R}\sin(R\sin t)R\cos t}{\e^{R}\sin\left(R\sin\delta\right)R\cos\tfrac{6}{5R}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(R\sin t)\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}\e^{R}\sin\left(\varepsilon\right)\cos\tfrac{6}{5R}}\left[-\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(R\sin t)\right)\right]_{t=\delta}^{t=6/5R}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}\left(\varepsilon(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{6})\right)\cos\tfrac{6}{5R}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(\varepsilon)-R\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{2}\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(\varepsilon)-R\right).

where we used
\sin x\geq x(1-x^{2}/6)
,
0\leq x\leq 1
. Next,

\displaystyle J_{4,2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{6/5R}^{4/R}\exp\left(\e^{R\cos(t)}\cos(R\sin(t))\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(R\sin(6/5R))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos\left(\tfrac{6}{5}-\tfrac{6}{25R^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\e^{R}\right),

where in the last inequality we used

\frac{6}{5}-\frac{6}{25R^{2}}\in\left(\frac{6}{5}-\frac{1}{400},\frac{6}{5}\right)\subseteq\left(\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{6}{5}\right).

Thus, using the inequality
\cos x\geq 7/8
,
|x|\leq 1/2
, we get

\displaystyle J_{4,1}+J_{4,2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{2}\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(\varepsilon)-R\right)\left(1+\frac{6}{5}\exp\left(\e^{R}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\cos(\varepsilon))+R\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{2}\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(\varepsilon)-R\right)\left(1+\frac{6}{5}\exp\left(-\tfrac{3}{8}\e^{R}+R\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\e R^{n+1}}\exp\left(\e^{R}\cos(\varepsilon)-R\right).

Eventually, by the inequalities
\cos(x)\leq 1-\frac{1}{4}x^{2}
and
1-\e^{-x}\geq\frac{1}{2}x
,
0\leq x\leq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{4,3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{4/R}^{\pi}{\exp\left(\e^{R\cos t}\cos(R\sin t)\right)}dt\leq\frac{n!}{\pi\e R^{n}}\int_{4/R}^{\pi}\exp\left(\e^{R\cos(4/R)}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n!}{\e R^{n}}\exp\left(\e^{R-4/R}\right)=\frac{n!}{\e R^{n}}\exp\left(\e^{R}-\e^{R}(1-\e^{-4/R})\right)\leq\frac{n!}{\e R^{n}}\exp\left(\e^{R}(1-\tfrac{2}{R})\right).

Summing up, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{4}}{E_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(J_{4,1}+J_{4,2}+J_{4,3}\right)\frac{R^{n}\sqrt{2\pi(n+1)(R+1)}}{n!\exp\left(\e^{R}-1\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp\left(e^{R}(\cos(\varepsilon)-1)-R\right)+R\exp\left(-\tfrac{2}{R}\e^{R}\right)\right)\frac{1}{R}\sqrt{2\pi R\e^{R}(R+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\exp\left(\e^{R}(\cos(\varepsilon)-1)-\tfrac{1}{2}R\right)+R\exp\left(-\tfrac{2}{R}\e^{R}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\right)\right],

where be bounded
\sqrt{2\pi(1+\tfrac{1}{R})}\leq\sqrt{2\pi(1+\tfrac{1}{4})}\leq 3
. This ends the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

For
R\geq 5
and
\varepsilon<1/2
we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}}{E_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4\exp\left(-\tfrac{11}{24}\varepsilon^{2}\e^{R}-\tfrac{3}{8}R\right)+3R\exp\left(-\tfrac{2}{R}\e^{R}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\right).

Proof.

The assertion follows from Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
and the inequality

1-\cos x\geq\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-\frac{1}{4!}\leq\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{12}x^{2}\right)\leq\frac{11}{24}x^{2},

valid for
|x|\leq 1
.
∎

4
Explicit bounds of the Bell numbers

We start this section with some kind of master theorem, as it implies all the subsequent result. It establishes a second order asymptotic of the Bell numbers together with the convergence rate. This provides us with the optimal order of the error in estimates, allows to reach good constants in the first order bounds, and is crucial in approximating the ratio of the consecutive Bell numbers.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
n\geq 1
it holds

\left|\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-\left(1-\frac{\e^{-R}}{12}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right)\right|\leq 1.6\e^{-2R},

where
R=W(n+1)
.

Remark 4.2
.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
relies on Proposition
3.1
, Corollary
3.4
and proper choice of
\varepsilon
. In view of the middle term in (
3.7
), the bound of order
\e^{-2R}
is the best possible to obtain in this approach. Additionally, one requires
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(R)\leq C\e^{-R/4}
, for some
C>0
, in order to achieve this order. Numerical analysis shows that, fixing
R=5
, lies around
1.4
. For simplicity, we choose
C=3/2
, as is does not impact much the final result.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We bound from above the left-hand side of the assertion by

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{1}}{E_{n}}-\left(1-\frac{\e^{-R}}{12}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right)\right|+\left|\frac{J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}}{E_{n}}\right|.

Taking
\varepsilon=\frac{3}{2}\e^{-R/4}
we have
\varepsilon^{2}{\e^{R}}>5
, and Proposition
3.1
gives us

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{1}}{E_{n}}-\left(1-\frac{\e^{-R}}{12}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\e^{-2R}\left[\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\left(-\frac{9}{8}\e^{\frac{R}{2}}+\frac{7}{4}R\right)+\frac{6}{5}\e^{\frac{81}{352}}+\frac{729}{1280}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{9}{8}\e^{\frac{R}{2}}+\frac{11}{4}R\right)}{\frac{9}{4}\e^{\frac{R}{2}}-5}\right].

The function of
R
in the brackets is decreasing, we therefore bound it from above by the value at
R=5
and get

\left|\frac{J_{1}}{E_{n}}-\left(1-\frac{\e^{-R}}{12}\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right)\right|\leq 1.55\e^{-2R}.

Next, we employ Corollary
3.4
. Since
\varepsilon<1/2
for
R\geq 5
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}}{E_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left[4\exp\left(-\tfrac{33}{32}\e^{R/2}+\tfrac{13}{8}R\right)+3R\exp\left(-\tfrac{2}{R}\e^{R}+\tfrac{5}{2}R\right)\right]\e^{-2R}.

Similarly as previously, it is easy to show that the expression in the brackets is decreasing, and therefore

\displaystyle\left|\frac{J_{4}}{E_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left[4\exp\left(-\tfrac{33}{32}\e^{5/2}+\tfrac{65}{8}\right)+15\exp\left(-\tfrac{2}{5}\e^{5}+\tfrac{25}{2}\right)\right]e^{-2R}

\displaystyle\leq 0.05\e^{-2R}.

Combining the results we get the required bound for
R\geq 5
, which corresponds to
n\geq\lceil 5\e^{5}-1\rceil=742
. Numerical calculations confirm the bound holds for
n\in\{1,\ldots,741\}
as well.
∎

As a first consequence of Theorem
4.1
we derive some weaker, but more elegant and convenient in applications, bounds on the ratio
B_{n}/E_{n}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
n\geq 11
we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\e^{-W(n+1)}}{11}\leq\frac{1}{11}\frac{\ln n}{n}.

Furthermore, for
n\geq 311
we have
B_{n}\leq E_{n}.

Proof.

Similarly as in (
4.2
), from Theorem
4.1
we get for
R\geq 5

\displaystyle\left|\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq\e^{-R}\left|-\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{12\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}\right|+\frac{3}{2}\e^{-2R}

\displaystyle\leq\e^{-R}\left(\frac{1}{12}+\frac{3}{2}\e^{-5}\right)\leq\frac{\e^{-R}}{11}\leq\frac{1}{11}\frac{\ln(n+1)}{n+1}\leq\frac{1}{11}\frac{\ln n}{n},

where we used
\tfrac{3}{2\cdot 5}+\tfrac{10}{5^{2}}+\tfrac{9}{5^{3}}<1
. Since
R\geq 5
corresponds to
n\geq 742
, we confirm the proposition numerically for
n=11,\ldots,741
.

The bound
B_{n}\leq E_{n}
for
n\geq 742
is a consequence of Theorem
4.1
and the inequality (
2.12
). Numerical calculations verify the bound for
n=311,\ldots 742
.
∎

So far, the results are formulated by means of the asymptotic form
E_{n}
. However, our preferred form is
E^{*}
, since it allows to establish bounds for greater range of the indices, and is more convenient in some calculations. Below, we present relations between these two quantities.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
n\geq 1
it holds

\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}\right)E_{n}^{*}\leq E_{n}\leq E_{n}^{*}.

Proof.

Rewriting (
2.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!\exp\left({\e^{R}-(n+1)\ln R}\right)}{\e\sqrt{2\pi\e^{R}\left(1+\frac{1}{R}\right)}}=\frac{n!\exp\left(\e^{R}-(n+1)\left(\ln(n+1)-R\right)\right)}{\e\sqrt{2\pi\frac{n+1}{R}\left(1+\frac{1}{R}\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!\exp\left({\int_{0}^{n+1}W(x)dx-(n+1)\left[\ln(n+1)-1\right]}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\frac{n+1}{R}\left(1+\frac{1}{R}\right)}}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1)!\exp\left({\int_{0}^{n+1}W(x)dx}-W(n+1)\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi(n+1)}\left(\frac{n+1}{\e}\right)^{n+1}\sqrt{1+R}}

\displaystyle=E_{n}^{*}\frac{(n+1)!}{\sqrt{2\pi(n+1)}\left(\frac{n+1}{\e}\right)^{n+1}}\exp\left({\int_{n}^{n+1}W(x)dx}-W(n+1)\right),

Since
W
is increasing and concave, and the inequality
W(1)\geq 1/2
due to (
2.9
),
the mean value theorem lets us bound

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}W(x)dx-W(n+1)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{n}^{n+1}(n+1-x)dx\min_{z\in[n,n+1]}W^{\prime}(z)\leq-\frac{1}{2}W^{\prime}(n+1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n+1+\left(1+\frac{1}{W(n+1)}\right)}\leq-\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{n+1},

and similarly

{\int_{n}^{n+1}W(x)dx}-W(n+1)\geq-\frac{1}{2}W^{\prime}(n)\geq-\frac{1}{2n}.

Using also the well known approximation

\e^{1/(12n+1)}\leq\frac{n!}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{\e}\right)^{n}}\leq\e^{1/12n},

and the inequality
e^{x}\geq 1+x
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\e^{\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{4}\right)}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq\e^{-1/(2n)}\geq 1-\frac{1}{2n},

which ends the proof.
∎

From Theorem
4.1
we can directly deduce that

\e^{R}\left(\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-1\right)=-\frac{1-\tfrac{3}{2R}-\tfrac{10}{R^{2}}-\tfrac{9}{R^{3}}+\tfrac{1}{R^{4}}}{12\left(1+\tfrac{1}{R}\right)^{3}}+O(\e^{-R}).

(4.2)

Recalling that
\e^{R}=(n+1)/W(n+1)
and
W(n+1)\sim\ln n
, by Lemma
4.4
we get the asymptotics of the relative error of estimating
B_{n}
by
E_{n}
or
E_{n}^{*}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

We have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{\ln n}\left(\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-1\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{\ln n}\left(\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}-1\right)=-\frac{1}{12}.

This shows that the rate of convergence obtained in Proposition
4.3
and in the next Theorem is of the optimal order.

Theorem 4.6
.

For
n\geq 2
it holds

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{5}\frac{\ln n}{n}\right)E_{n}^{*}\leq B_{n}\leq E_{n}^{*}.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.4
and previous proposition we conclude for
n\geq 311

\displaystyle B_{n}\leq E_{n}\leq E_{n}^{*}.

Next, by Lemma
4.4
and Proposition
4.3
we get for
n\geq 311

\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}-1

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-1\right)\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}+\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}-1\geq-\left|\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-1\right|-\left|\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n}^{*}}-1\right|

\displaystyle\geq-\left(\frac{1}{11}\frac{\ln n}{n}+\frac{1}{2n}\right)=-\frac{\ln n}{n}\left(\frac{1}{11}+\frac{1}{2\ln n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\ln n}{n}\left(\frac{1}{11}+\frac{1}{2\ln(311)}\right)\geq-\frac{1}{5}\frac{\ln n}{n}.

For
n=2,\ldots,310
we verify the assertion numerically.
∎

At the end of this section we provide bounds by means of elementary functions. They do not match the asymptotic forms, however, they give better comprehension of the magnitude of the Bell numbers and seem sufficient for most of the applications.

Proposition 4.7
.

We have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\e}\frac{n}{\ln n}\right)^{n}\leq B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\frac{n}{\ln n}\right)^{n},

\displaystyle n\geq 2,

(4.3)

\displaystyle B_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\e}\frac{n}{\ln n}\big(1+\tfrac{3\ln\ln n}{\ln n}\big)\right)^{n},

\displaystyle n\geq 6.

(4.4)

Proof.

Since
\e^{W(n)}=n/W(n)
, we write

\displaystyle E_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left({\frac{n}{W(n)}+n(\ln n-\ln W(n))-(n+1)}\right)}{\sqrt{1+W(n)}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n}{\e\ln n}\right)^{n}{\exp\left[n\left({\frac{1}{W(n)}+\ln\left(\tfrac{W(n)+\ln W(n)}{W(n)}\right)-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{2n}\ln\big(1+W(n)\big)}\right)\right]}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{n}{\e\ln n}\e^{1/W(n)}\left(1+\tfrac{\ln W(n)}{W(n)}\right)\right]^{n}.

(4.6)

In order to get the upper bound in (
4.3
) we estimate for
W(n)\geq\e
(equivalently
n\geq\lceil\e\e^{\e}\rceil=42
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\e}\e^{1/{W(n)}}\left(1+\tfrac{\ln W(n)}{W(n)}\right)\leq\frac{\e^{1/\e}}{\e}\left(1+\frac{1}{\e}\right)\leq\frac{3}{4},

as required. Furthermore, if
W(n)\geq 5
(equivalently
n\geq 743
), by (
2.5
) and (
2.8
) we have

\displaystyle\e^{1/{W(n)}}\left(1+\tfrac{\ln W(n)}{W(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\left(\e^{1/{W(n)}}-1\right)+\e^{1/{W(n)}}\frac{\ln W(n)}{W(n)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\e^{1/5}\left(\frac{1}{W(n)}+\frac{\ln W(n)}{W(n)}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\e^{1/5}\frac{\ln W(n)}{\ln n}\frac{W(n)+\ln W(n)}{W(n)}\left(\frac{1}{\ln W(n)}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+\e^{1/5}\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n}\left(1+\frac{\ln 5}{5}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\ln 5}+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1+3\,\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n}.

Applying this to (
4.6
) we get (
4.4
).

We turn our attention to the first inequality in (
4.3
).
Using (
4.5
) and the inequality
1+x\leq\e^{x},x\in\mathbb{R}
, we get for
W(n)\geq 1
(i.e.
n\geq 3
)

\displaystyle E_{n}^{*}\geq\left[\frac{n}{\e\ln n}{\e^{\frac{1}{W(n)}-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{2n}W(n)}}\right]^{n}.

Consequently, Proposition
4.6
and the inequality
1-x\geq\e^{-2x}
,
x\in[0,1/2]
, give us

\displaystyle B_{n}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{5}\frac{\ln n}{n}\right)E_{n}^{*}\geq\left[\frac{n}{\e\ln n}\exp\left({\frac{1}{W(n)}-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{W(n)}{2n}-\frac{2}{5}\frac{\ln n}{n^{2}}}\right)\right]^{n}.

Furthermore, by
W(x)\leq\ln(x)
for
x\geq\e
, we get for
n\geq 3

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{W(n)}-\frac{1}{n}-\frac{W(n)}{2n}-\frac{2}{5}\frac{\ln n}{n^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W(n)}\left(1-\frac{W(n)}{n}-\frac{(W(n))^{2}}{2n}-\frac{2}{5}\frac{W(n)\ln n}{n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\ln n}\left(1-\frac{\ln n}{n}-\frac{(\ln n)^{2}}{2n}-\frac{2}{5}\left(\frac{n\ln n}{n}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\ln n}\left(1-\max_{x\geq 1}\frac{\ln x}{x}-\frac{1}{2}\max_{y\geq 1}\frac{(\ln y)^{2}}{y}-\frac{2}{5}\left(\max_{z\geq 1}\frac{\ln z}{z}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\ln n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\e}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{4}{\e^{2}}-\frac{2}{5}\frac{1}{\e^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln n}\left(1-\frac{5\e+12}{5\e^{2}}\right)\geq 0,

which proves the lower bound in the assertion for
n\geq 3
. Numerical verification of the upper bound in (
4.3
) for
2\leq n\leq 42
, the lower one for
n=2
and the bound (
4.4
) for
n\leq 742
ends the proof.
∎

5
Ratios of subsequent Bell numbers

In order to find the asymptotics of the ratio
B_{n}/B_{n-1}
we start with analogous problem for the sequence
E_{n}
approximating the Bell numbers.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
n\geq 1
we have

-\frac{1}{W(n)}\leq\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}\leq 0.

Proof.

For
n\leq 5
we verify the assertion numerically. Assume therefore
n\geq 6
. From (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle E_{n}=\frac{n!\exp\left({\int_{0}^{n+1}W(x)dx-(n+1)\left[\ln(n+1)-1\right]-\tfrac{1}{2}W(n+1)}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\left(1+\frac{1}{W(n+1)}\right)}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}

\displaystyle=\e^{W(n)}\left[\frac{\exp\left({\int_{n}^{n+1}W(x)dx-\tfrac{1}{2}\left[W(n+1)+W(n)\right]-(n+1)\ln(1+\frac{1}{n})+1}\right)}{\sqrt{\left({1+\frac{1}{W(n)}}\right)^{-1}\left({1+\frac{1}{W(n+1)}}\right)}}-1\right].

Monotonicity of
W
and the inequality (
2.7
) give us

\displaystyle 1\leq\sqrt{\frac{1+\frac{1}{W(n)}}{1+\frac{1}{W(n+1)}}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+\frac{{W(n+1)}-{W(n)}}{W(n)W(n+1)\left(1+\frac{1}{W(n+1)}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{1+\frac{{1}}{n(W(n))^{2}}}\leq{1+\frac{{1}}{2n(W(n))^{2}}}.

Furthermore, by the mean value theorem and monotonicity of
W
and
W^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\int_{n}^{n+1}W(x)dx-\tfrac{1}{2}\left[W(n+1)+W(n)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{n}^{n+1/2}W(x)-W(n)dx+\int_{n+1/2}^{n+1}W(x)-W(n+1)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}W^{\prime}(n)-\frac{1}{4}W^{\prime}(n+1)\leq\frac{1}{4}\max_{x\in[n,n+1]}W^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\max_{x\in[n,n+1]}\frac{1+\frac{1}{x+W(x)}}{(x+W(x))^{2}}\leq\frac{1+\frac{1}{n}}{4n^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

Using this and the inequalities
x\geq\ln(x+1)\geq x-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\e^{-1/n}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left({\int_{n}^{n+1}W(x)dx-\tfrac{1}{2}\left[W(n+1)+W(n)\right]-(n+1)\ln(1+\frac{1}{n})+1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{n^{2}}-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n+1}{2n^{2}}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2n}\Big(1-\frac{2}{n}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\e^{-1/3n}\leq 1-\frac{1}{3n}+\frac{1}{18n^{2}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{4n},

where we took advantage of the assumption
n\geq 6
.
Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}

\displaystyle\geq\e^{W(n)}\left(\e^{-1/n}-1\right)\geq-\e^{W(n)}\frac{1}{n}=-\frac{1}{W(n)}

and since
W(n)\geq W(6)\geq\sqrt{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}

\displaystyle\leq\e^{W(n)}\left[\left(1+\frac{{1}}{2n(W(n))^{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{4n}\right)-1\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{W(n)}\left[\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2(W(n))^{2}}+\frac{1}{8n(W(n))^{2}}\right]\leq 0,

which ends the proof.
∎

We are now in position to formulate and prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.2
.

For
n\geq 1
it holds

\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}\right|\leq\frac{8}{7}\frac{1}{W(n)}.

Proof.

By triangle inequality we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\frac{E_{n}q_{n}}{E_{n-1}q_{n-1}}\right|+\left|\frac{E_{n}q_{n}}{E_{n-1}q_{n-1}}-\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}\right|+\left|\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}\right|.

(5.1)

The last term is already bounded in Lemma
5.1
.
Let us deal with the other two. First, we rewrite

\displaystyle\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\frac{E_{n}q_{n}}{E_{n-1}q_{n-1}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{B_{n}-E_{n}q_{n}}{B_{n-1}}+E_{n}q_{n}\left(\frac{E_{n-1}q_{n-1}-B_{n-1}}{B_{n-1}E_{n-1}q_{n-1}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}\frac{\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-q_{n}}{\frac{B_{n-1}}{E_{n-1}}-1+1}+\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n-1}}\left(\frac{q_{n-1}-\frac{B_{n-1}}{E_{n-1}}}{\frac{B_{n-1}}{E_{n-1}}-1+1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}}{1-|\frac{B_{n-1}}{E_{n-1}}-1|}\left[\left|{\frac{B_{n}}{E_{n}}-q_{n}}\right|+\left(1+\frac{|q_{n}-q_{n-1}|}{q_{n-1}}\right)\left|{q_{n-1}-\frac{B_{n-1}}{E_{n-1}}}\right|\right].

Next, for
n\geq\lceil 5\e^{5}\rceil=743
we apply Lemma
5.1
, Proposition
4.3
, the inequalities (
2.11
), (
2.13
) and Theorem
4.1
, which leads to

\displaystyle\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\frac{E_{n}q_{n}}{E_{n-1}q_{n-1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\e^{W(n)}}{1-\tfrac{1}{11}\e^{-W(n)}}\left(2+\frac{\e^{-W(n)}}{10n(1-\e^{-W(n)}/12)}\right)1.6\e^{-2W(n)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\tfrac{1}{11}\e^{-5}}\left(2+\frac{\e^{-5}}{10\cdot 5\e^{5}(1-\e^{-5}/12)}\right)1.6\e^{-W(n)}\leq 4\e^{-W(n)}.

(5.2)

We bound the second term of the right-hand side in (
5.1
) using Lemma
5.1
and the inequalities (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) as follows

\displaystyle\left|\frac{E_{n}q_{n}}{E_{n-1}q_{n-1}}-\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{n}}{E_{n-1}}\frac{1}{q_{n-1}}\left|q_{n}-q_{n-1}\right|\leq\frac{1}{1-\e^{-W(n)}}\frac{1}{10n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\e^{-5}}\frac{1}{10n}\leq\frac{1}{9n}.

(5.3)

Thus, applying (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and Lemma
5.1
to (
5.1
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\frac{B_{n}}{B_{n-1}}-\e^{W(n)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4\e^{-W(n)}+\frac{1}{9n}+\frac{1}{W(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{W(n)}\left(\frac{4W(n)}{\e^{W(n)}}+\frac{1}{9\e^{W(n)}}+1\right)=\frac{1}{W(n)}\left(\frac{20}{\e^{5}}+\frac{1}{9\e^{5}}+1\right)\leq\frac{8}{7}\frac{1}{W(n)}.

This completes the proof for
n\geq 743
. The rest has been verified numerically.
∎

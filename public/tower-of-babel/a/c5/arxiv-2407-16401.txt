Some remarks on regularized Shannon sampling formulas
Melanie Kircheis, Daniel Potts, Manfred Tasche

Abstract
The fast reconstruction of a bandlimited function from its sample data is an essential problem in signal processing. In this paper, we consider the widely used Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula in comparison to regularized Shannon sampling formulas employing alternative window functions, such as the sinh \sinh -type window function and the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function. It is shown that the approximation errors of these regularized Shannon sampling formulas possess an exponential decay with respect to the truncation parameter. The main focus of this work is to address minor gaps in the preceding papers [ 13 , 14 ] and rigorously prove assumptions that were previously based solely on numerical tests. In doing so, we demonstrate that the sinh \sinh -type regularized Shannon sampling formula has the same exponential decay as the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula, but both have twice the exponential decay of the Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula. Additionally, numerical experiments illustrate the theoretical results. Key words : Shannon sampling series, regularization, bandlimited function, approximation error, exponential decay, Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formulas, sinh \sinh -type regularized Shannon sampling formulas. AMS Subject Classifications : 62D05, 42A10, 41A25, 94A20.

Introduction

In signal processing, the fast reconstruction of a bandlimited function from its sample data is of fundamental importance. A function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
is called

bandlimited
with
bandwidth

\delta>0
, if its Fourier transform

\displaystyle({\mathcal{F}}f)(\omega)={\hat{f}}(\omega)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}t\omega}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(1.1)

vanishes for all
|\omega|\geq\delta
. For such a bandlimited function with
\delta\in(0,\,\pi]
the famous Shannon sampling theorem, see
[
34
,
15
,
30
]
, states that

\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{sinc}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t}&\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\,,\\
1&\colon t=0\,,\end{cases}

(1.3)

denotes the
cardinal sine function
. It is known that the Shannon sampling series (
1.2
) converges absolutely and uniformly on whole
\mathbb{R}
.
However, the practical use of (
1.2
) is limited, since its evaluation requires infinitely many samples and its truncated version is not a good approximation due to the slow decay of the cardinal sine function, see
[
12
]
.
In addition to this rather poor convergence, it is known, see
[
9
,
10
,
8
]
, that in the presence of noise in the samples
f(k)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, of a bandlimited function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
the convergence of Shannon sampling series (
1.2
) may even break down completely.
Therefore, it was proposed to consider the regularization of the Shannon sampling series with a suitable window function.
Note that many authors such as
[
7
,
18
,
28
,
20
,
31
]
used window functions in the frequency domain, but the recent study
[
14
]
has shown that it is much more beneficial to employ a window function in the spatial domain, cf.
[
24
,
25
,
31
,
17
,
16
,
6
,
13
]
.
In the following, a
window function

\varphi:\,\mathbb{R}\to[0,\,1]
is an even function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
which decreases on
[0,\,\infty)
and fulfills
\varphi(0)=1
.
By
{\mathbf{1}}_{[-m,\,m]}
we denote the
characteristic function
of the interval
[-m,\,m]
with
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
, i. e., the function

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}_{[-m,\,m]}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\colon t\in[-m,\,m]\,,\\
0&\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-m,\,m]\,.\end{cases}

In this paper, we assume that the bandwidth
\delta
of
f
fulfills the so-called
oversampling condition

0<\delta<\pi
.
Then we recover
f
by the
\varphi
-reg
ularized Shannon sampling formula

\displaystyle\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\varphi(t-k)\,{\mathbf{1}}_{[-m,\,m]}(t-k)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(1.4)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
is the so-called
truncation parameter
. In doing so, we consider the following window functions
\varphi:\,\mathbb{R}\to[0,\,1]
.

Remark 1.1
.

The most popular window function, see e. g.
[
24
,
27
,
29
,
32
,
16
,
6
]
, is the
Gaussian function

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(t)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(1.5)

with
variance

\sigma^{2}>0
.
Note that
this
window function
is
supported on whole
\mathbb{R}
.

Here we prefer window functions which are compactly supported on the interval
[-m,\,m]
, as studied in
[
13
,
14
]
.
The
\sinh
-
type window function
is defined as

\displaystyle\varphi_{\sinh}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sinh\beta}\,\sinh\Big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\tfrac{t^{2}}{m^{2}}}\,\Big)&\colon t\in[-m,\,m]\,,\\
0&\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-m,\,m]\,,\end{cases}

(1.6)

with
shape parameter

\beta>0
, see
[
22
]
. Then the corresponding expression (
1.4
) is termed the
\sinh
-
type regularized Shannon sampling formula
.
The
continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function
is defined as

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\big(I_{0}(\beta\sqrt{1-t^{2}/m^{2}})-1\big)&\colon t\in[-m,\,m]\,,\\
0&\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-m,\,m]\,,\end{cases}

(1.7)

with convenient shape parameter
\beta>0
, see
[
22
]
. Then the corresponding expression (
1.4
) is called the
continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula
.
We remark that these two window functions (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) are well-studied in the context of the nonuniform fast Fourier transform (NFFT), see e. g.
[
21
, Section 6]
and
[
5
,
4
]
.

Due to the definition of the cardinal sine function (
1.3
) we have
\mathrm{sinc}(n-k)=\delta_{n,k}
and therefore the regularized Shannon sampling formula
R_{\varphi,m}f
in (
1.4
) has the
interpolation property

\displaystyle\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(n)=f(n)\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(1.8)

Moreover, the use of the characteristic function
{\mathbf{1}}_{[-m,\,m]}
in (
1.4
) leads to
localized sampling
of
f
, i. e., the
computation of
\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
requires only
2m
samples
f(k)
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
fulfills the condition

|k-t|\leq m
. Especially, for
t\in(0,\,1)
we obtain the finite sum

\displaystyle\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)=\sum_{k=1-m}^{m}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\varphi(t-k)\,.

As in many applications, we use
oversampling
of the given bandlimited function
f
with bandwidth
\delta<\pi
, i. e., the function
f
is sampled on the integer grid
\mathbb{Z}
.

In this paper, we focus on the
\varphi
-reg
ularized Shannon sampling formulas (
1.4
) for the window functions
\varphi
given in Remark
1.1
. To compare the corresponding approaches, we present estimates
of
the uniform approximation error

\displaystyle\|f-R_{\varphi,m}f\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\coloneqq\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|f(t)-\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)\big|\,,

(1.9)

where
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the Banach space of continuous functions
g\colon\!\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
vanishing as
|t|\to\infty

equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\coloneqq\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|
.

Primarily, this work concentrates on addressing minor gaps in the preceding papers
[
13
,
14
]
and rigorously proving the corresponding assumptions that were previously based solely on numerical experiments.

For this purpose, we initially study the uniform approximation error of general
\varphi
-reg
ularized Shannon sampling formulas (
1.4
) in Section
2
.
Afterwards, we specify our findings for the window functions
\varphi
introduced in Remark
1.1
.
In particular, Section
3
deals with the Gaussian window function (
1.5
), while Section
4
is concerned with the
\sinh
-type window function (
1.6
) and Section
5
with the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
1.7
).

2
Approximation error of regularized Shannon sampling formulas

Firstly, we estimate the uniform approximation error of the
\varphi
-reg
ularized Shannon sampling formula (
1.4
), analogously to
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
is bandlimited with bandwidth
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
. Further let
\varphi:\,\mathbb{R}\to[0,\,1]
be an even function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})

which is decreasing on
[0,\,\infty)
with
\varphi(0)=1
,
and let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
be given.

Then the
\varphi
-reg
ularized Shannon sampling formula (
1.4
) satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\|f-R_{\varphi,m}f\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\big(E_{1}(m)+E_{2}(m)\big)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}\,,

with the error constants

\displaystyle E_{1}(m)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]}\bigg|1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}{\hat{\varphi}}(\tau)\,{\mathrm{d}}\tau\,\bigg|\,,

(2.1)

\displaystyle E_{2}(m)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi\,m}\,\sqrt{\varphi^{2}(m)+\int_{m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t}\,.

(2.2)

Proof
. (i) Initially, we consider only the case
t\in(0,\,1)
, where we split the approximation error

\displaystyle f(t)-\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)=e_{1}(t)+e_{2,0}(t)\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,,

into the
regularization error

\displaystyle e_{1}(t)\coloneqq f(t)-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\varphi(t-k)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(2.3)

and the
truncation error

\displaystyle e_{2,0}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\varphi(t-k)-\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\varphi(t-k)\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

(2.4)

(ii) To estimate the regularization error (
2.3
), we start our study by considering the Fourier transform (
1.1
) of the function
\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc}
, i. e., the term

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc})(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Using the convolution property of
\mathcal{F}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
21
, Theorem 2.26]
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc})(\omega)=\big({\hat{\varphi}}\star({\mathcal{F}}\mathrm{sinc})\big)(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\varphi}(\omega-\tau)\,(\mathcal{F}\mathrm{sinc})(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,

and hence by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\mathrm{sinc})(\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,{\mathbf{1}}_{[-\pi,\pi]}(\tau)

we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc})(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

Consequently, using the shifting property of
\mathcal{F}
, the Fourier transform (
1.1
) of the shifted function
\varphi(t-k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
reads as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t-k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega k}\,{\mathcal{F}}(\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc})(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega k}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

Therefore, the Fourier transform of the regularization error
e_{1}
in (
2.3
) has the form

\displaystyle{\hat{e}}_{1}(\omega)={\hat{f}}(\omega)-\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega k}\bigg)\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

(2.5)

Note that since the set of shifted cardinal sine functions
\mathrm{sinc}(\cdot-k)
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
forms an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, i. e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-\ell)\,\mathrm{d}t=\delta_{k,\ell}\,,\quad k,\,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

and the given function
f
can be represented by the Shannon sampling series (
1.2
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(k)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\overline{f(\ell)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-\ell)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\overline{f(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}<\infty\,,

(2.6)

and thus the series

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\omega k}

converges in
L^{2}([-\pi,\pi])
.
Moreover, since
f
is bandlimited with bandwidth
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
, we have
{\hat{f}}(\omega)=0
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-\delta,\,\delta]
,
and thereby the restricted function
\hat{f}\big|_{[-\pi,\,\pi]}
belongs to
L^{2}([-\pi,\pi])
. Hence, this restricted function possesses the
2\pi
-periodic
Fourier expansion

\displaystyle{\hat{f}}(\omega)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{k}(\hat{f})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega k}\,,\quad\omega\in[-\pi,\,\pi]\,,

with the Fourier coefficients

\displaystyle c_{k}(\hat{f})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\hat{f}}(\tau)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\hat{f}}(\tau)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}k\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,f(k)\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

by inverse Fourier transform. In other words, the function
\hat{f}
can be represented in the form

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\omega)={\hat{f}}(\omega)\,{\mathbf{1}}_{[-\delta,\delta]}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}k\omega}\bigg)\,{\mathbf{1}}_{[-\delta,\delta]}(\omega)\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(2.7)

Introducing the auxiliary function

\displaystyle\Delta_{\varphi}(\omega)\coloneqq{\mathbf{1}}_{[-\delta,\delta]}(\omega)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

we see by inserting (
2.7
) into (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle{\hat{e}}_{1}(\omega)={\hat{f}}(\omega)\,\Delta_{\varphi}(\omega)\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

and thereby

\displaystyle\big|{\hat{e}}_{1}(\omega)\big|=\big|{\hat{f}}(\omega)\big|\,\big|\Delta_{\varphi}(\omega)\big|\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Thus, inverse Fourier transform and the definition (
2.1
) yields

\displaystyle|e_{1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|{\hat{e}}_{1}(\omega)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|{\hat{f}}(\omega)\big|\,\big|\Delta_{\varphi}(\omega)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}\big|\Delta_{\varphi}(\omega)\big|\;\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|{\hat{f}}(\omega)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,E_{1}(m)\,\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|{\hat{f}}(\omega)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the Parseval equality
\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|1\cdot{\hat{f}}(\omega)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}1^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigg)^{1/2}\,\bigg(\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\big|{\hat{f}}(\omega)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigg)^{1/2}=\sqrt{2\delta}\,\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{2\pi}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

Consequently, we receive the estimate

\displaystyle|e_{1}(t)|\leq E_{1}(m)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

and hence

\displaystyle\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|e_{1}(t)|\leq E_{1}(m)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

(iii) Now we estimate the truncation error
e_{2,0}(t)
for
t\in(0,\,1)
. By (
2.4
) and
\varphi(t)\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|e_{2,0}(t)|\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}|f(k)|\,|\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)|\,\varphi(t-k)\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

For
t\in(0,\,1)
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}
, we estimate

\displaystyle|\mathrm{sinc}(t-k)|\leq\frac{1}{\pi\,|t-k|}\leq\frac{1}{\pi m}\,,

such that

\displaystyle|e_{2,0}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\pi m}\,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}|f(k)|\,\varphi(t-k)\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

Then the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality implies

\displaystyle|e_{2,0}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\pi m}\,\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}|f(k)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}\varphi^{2}(t-k)\bigg)^{1/2}\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

From (
2.6
) it follows that

\displaystyle|e_{2,0}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\pi m}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}\varphi^{2}(t-k)\bigg)^{1/2}\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

Since by assumption the window function
\varphi
is even and
\varphi\big|_{[0,\infty)}
decreases, we can estimate the series

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}\varphi^{2}(t-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-m}\varphi^{2}(t-k)+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(t-k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(t+k)+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(k-t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(k)+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(k-1)=2\,\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(k)\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

Applying the integral test for convergence of series, we obtain that

\displaystyle 2\,\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(k)=2\,\varphi^{2}(m)+2\,\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(k)<2\,\varphi^{2}(m)+2\,\int_{m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Thus, for each
t\in(0,\,1)
we have by definition (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle|e_{2,0}(t)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi m}\,\bigg(\varphi^{2}(m)+\int_{m}^{\infty}\varphi^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{1/2}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=E_{2}(m)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty\,.

Furthermore, by the interpolation property (
1.8
) of
R_{\varphi,m}f
we have
e_{2,0}(0)=e_{2,0}(1)=0
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,1]}|e_{2,0}(t)|\leq E_{2}(m)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

(iv) By the same technique, the error estimate

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[n,n+1]}\big|f(t)-\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)\big|\leq\big(E_{1}(m)+E_{2}(m)\big)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

can be shown for the interval
[n,\,n+1]
with arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the open interval
(n,\,n+1)
, we decompose the approximation error as

\displaystyle f(t+n)-\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)(t+n)=e_{1}(t+n)+e_{2,n}(t)\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,,

with

\displaystyle e_{1}(t+n)

\displaystyle=f(t+n)-\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f(k)\,\mathrm{sinc}\big(t-(k-n)\big)\,\varphi\big(t-(k-n)\big)

\displaystyle=f(t+n)-\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}f(\ell+n)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-\ell)\,\varphi(t-\ell)\,,

\displaystyle e_{2,n}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{1-m,\ldots,m\}}f(\ell+n)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-\ell)\,\varphi(t-\ell)\,.

As shown in steps (ii) and (iii), we have

\displaystyle\|e_{1}(\cdot+n)\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\|e_{1}\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\,,

\displaystyle|e_{2,n}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq E_{2}(m)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,,\quad t\in(0,\,1)\,.

Furthermore, by the interpolation property (
1.8
) of
R_{\varphi,m}f
, we have
e_{2,n}(0)=e_{2,n}(1)=0
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and thus

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[n,n+1]}\big|e_{2,n}(t)\big|\leq E_{2}(m)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[n,n+1]}\big|f(t)-\big(R_{\varphi,m}f\big)(t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|e_{1}\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}+\max_{t\in[n,n+1]}\big|e_{2,n}(t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big(E_{1}(m)+E_{2}(m)\big)\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,,

which completes the proof.

3
Regularization with the Gaussian function

In this section we consider the Gaussian function (
1.5
) with variance
\sigma^{2}>0
, analogous to
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
. In order to achieve fast convergence of the Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula, we
also study
the choice of this variance
\sigma^{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
is bandlimited with bandwidth
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
. Further let
\varphi_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
be the Gaussian function (
1.5
)
with variance
\sigma^{2}=\frac{m}{\pi-\delta}
and let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
be given.

Then the Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\big\|f-R_{\mathrm{Gauss},m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi m\,(\pi-\delta)}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)/2}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

(3.1)

Proof
. (i) At first, we estimate the regularization error constant (
2.1
) for the Gaussian function (
1.5
).
Since the Fourier transform of
\varphi_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
reads as

\displaystyle{\hat{\varphi}}_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\,t\omega}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sigma\,\mathrm{e}^{-\omega^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

cf.
[
21
, Example 2.6]
, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}(m)=\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}\bigg|1-\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg|\,.

Substituting
s=\tau\sigma/\sqrt{2}
and using the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\sqrt{\pi}
,
we obtain for
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
with
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
that

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\int_{(\omega-\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}^{(\omega+\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{(\omega-\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}^{(\omega+\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\bigg(\int_{-\infty}^{(\omega-\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{(\omega+\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\bigg(\int_{(\pi-\omega)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{(\omega+\pi)\sigma/\sqrt{2}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\,.

Since
\Delta_{\mathrm{Gauss}}
is even, we consider only the case
\omega\in[0,\,\delta]
. Applying the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-(t+a)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathrm{e}^{-a^{2}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2at}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2a}\,\mathrm{e}^{-a^{2}}\,,\quad a>0\,,

we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\Delta_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\pi-\omega)^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}}{(\pi-\omega)\,\sigma}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\pi+\omega)^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}}{(\pi+\omega)\,\sigma}\bigg)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\pi-\omega)^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}}{(\pi-\omega)\,\sigma}\,,\quad\omega\in[0,\,\delta]\,.

Consequently, we have for all
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
that

\displaystyle 0\leq\Delta_{\mathrm{Gauss}}(\omega)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\pi-|\omega|)^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}}{(\pi-|\omega|)\,\sigma}

and hence

\displaystyle E_{1}(m)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\pi-\delta)^{2}\sigma^{2}/2}}{(\pi-\delta)\,\sigma}\,.

(3.2)

(ii) Now we examine the truncation error constant (
2.2
) for the Gaussian function (
1.5
).
By
\varphi_{\mathrm{Gauss}}^{2}(m)=\mathrm{e}^{-m^{2}/\sigma^{2}}
and the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{m}^{\infty}\varphi_{\mathrm{Gauss}}^{2}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=\sigma\,\int_{m/\sigma}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2m}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m^{2}/\sigma^{2}}

we obtain

\displaystyle E_{2}(m)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi m}\,\sqrt{\mathrm{e}^{-m^{2}/\sigma^{2}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2m}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m^{2}/\sigma^{2}}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi m}\,\sqrt{1+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2m}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-m^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\,.

(3.3)

(iii) Finally, we
choose
the variance
\sigma^{2}
of the Gaussian function (
1.5
)
such that

E_{1}(m)
and
E_{2}(m)
possess the same exponential decay with respect to
m
.
From (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) it follows that

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\coloneqq\frac{m}{\pi-\delta}\,.

(3.4)

This yields the estimates

\displaystyle E_{1}(m)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\,(\pi-\delta)}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)/2}\,,

\displaystyle E_{2}(m)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi m}\,\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{2\,(\pi-\delta)}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)/2}\,.

Note that since
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi m\,(\pi-\delta)}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi m}\,\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{2\,(\pi-\delta)}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2(\pi-\delta)+1}{2\pi m}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\pi+1}{4\pi}}<1

and therefore

\displaystyle E_{2}(m)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi m\,(\pi-\delta)}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)/2}\,.

Thus, the Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula with the variance (
3.4
) fulfills the error estimate (
3.1
).
This completes the proof.

bounds on the approximation error of the Shannon sampling formula (

) were shown for the Gaussian function (
1.5
) with suitably chosen variance
\sigma^{2}
, which is basically the same as the one in Theorem

]
.

.

Inspired by
[
6
]
one could define a weak form of optimality of the Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula by saying that the variance

of the Gaussian function (

and
E_{2}(m)

We remark that in

4
Regularization with the sinh-type window function

In this section, we consider the
\sinh
-type window function (
1.6
) with shape parameter
\beta>0
, analogous to
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 4.2]
.

We especially focus on addressing minor gaps in
[
13
,
14
]
by rigorously proving assumptions up to now based solely on numerical tests.

Moreover, we demonstrate that the exponential decay with respect to the truncation parameter
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
is
twice as fast
for the uniform approximation error
\big\|f-R_{\sinh,m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}

as
for the approximation error
\big\|f-R_{\mathrm{Gauss},m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}
in Theorem
3.1
.

To this end, we firstly formulate the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
W>1
and
\beta>0
we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{1}^{W}\frac{J_{1}(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})}{\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu\,\bigg|\leq\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}+\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,,

(4.1)

where
J_{1}
denotes the Bessel function of first order.

Proof.
Substituting
\nu=\cosh t
in (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{W}\frac{J_{1}(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})}{\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu=\int_{0}^{w}J_{1}(\beta\,\sinh t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

with
w=\mathrm{arcosh}W>0
.
Note that it is known by
[
11
, 6.645–1]
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{1}(\beta\,\sinh t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t=I_{1/2}\Big(\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\,K_{1/2}\Big(\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)=\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}>0\,,

where
I_{1/2}
and
K_{1/2}
denote the modified Bessel functions of half order (see
[
1
, 10.2.13, 10.2.14, and 10.2.17]
.
The additional substitution
s=\beta\,\sinh t
yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{1}(\beta\,\sinh t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}\,,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{w}J_{1}(\beta\,\sinh t)\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,,

where
u=\beta\,\sinh w>0
.

Let
j_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote the positive zeros of
J_{1}
.
Note that
j_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,40
, are tabulated in
[
33
, p. 748]
and
that by
(-1)^{k}\,J_{1}^{\prime}(j_{k})>0
, see
[
1
, 9.1.27 and 9.5.2]
, these zeros are simple.
Using these zeros the integral (
4.2
) can be represented as

\displaystyle\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\bigg(\int_{0}^{j_{1}}+\int_{j_{1}}^{j_{2}}+\int_{j_{2}}^{j_{3}}+\,\ldots\bigg)\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,.

(4.3)

The integrand
J_{1}(s)\,(s^{2}+\beta^{2})^{-1/2}
,
s\in[0,\infty)
, is an oscillating function which tends to zero for
s\to\infty
, since we have

\displaystyle\frac{|J_{1}(s)|}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{{\sqrt{s}}\,\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,,\quad s\in[j_{1},\,\infty)\,,

(4.4)

which can be shown by the same technique as in
[
23
, Lemma 6]
.
Thus, the right-hand side of (
4.3
) is a convergent alternating series.

Now we consider the function

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{u}\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,,\quad u\in[0,\,\infty)\,.

(4.5)

We immediately recognize that
\mathcal{I}(u)
has relative maxima at
j_{2n+1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and relative minima at
j_{2n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(j_{k})=\frac{J_{1}(j_{k})}{\sqrt{j_{k}^{2}+\beta^{2}}}=0\,,\quad(-1)^{k}\,\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}(j_{k})=\frac{(-1)^{k}\,J_{1}^{\prime}(j_{k})}{\sqrt{j_{k}^{2}+\beta^{2}}}>0\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

In other words, by the oscillatory behavior of the Bessel function
J_{1}
, the function
\mathcal{I}(u)
increases from
\mathcal{I}(0)=0
to
\mathcal{I}(j_{1})
, then decreases from
\mathcal{I}(j_{1})
to
\mathcal{I}(j_{2})
, increases again from
\mathcal{I}(j_{2})
to
\mathcal{I}(j_{3})
, and so on.
By (
4.2
) and (
4.5
) it is also easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\mathcal{I}(j_{k})=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{j_{k}}\bigg)\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s=\int_{j_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{J_{1}(s)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Thus, combined with the estimate (
4.4
) this yields

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\mathcal{I}(j_{k})\bigg|\leq\int_{j_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{|J_{1}(s)|}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\leq\int_{j_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{\sqrt{s}}\,\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

In addition, from the equivalence relation
\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\,\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{2}
,
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, of the vector norms it follows that
s+\beta\leq\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}
,
s,\beta>0
, and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+\beta^{2}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{s+\beta}\,,\quad s,\beta>0\,.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\mathcal{I}(j_{k})\bigg|\leq\int_{j_{k}}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{{\sqrt{s}}\,(s+\beta)}\,{\mathrm{d}}s\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Since the antiderivative of
s^{-1/2}\,(s+\beta)^{-1}
reads as
\frac{2}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{s}{\beta}}
and
\arctan y\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\mathcal{I}(j_{k})\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\,\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,\Bigg(\frac{\pi}{2}-\arctan\sqrt{\frac{j_{k}}{\beta}}\,\Bigg)=\frac{2\,\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,\arctan\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{j_{k}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,.

Consequently, as this estimate is valid for all relative extreme values
\mathcal{I}(j_{k})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we also obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\mathcal{I}(u)\bigg|\leq\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}

for all
u>0
, which immediately implies

\displaystyle\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\leq\mathcal{I}(u)\leq\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}+\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,.

Since
\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}>0
and
\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}>0
for
\beta>0
this yields

\displaystyle-\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\leq\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}-\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\leq\mathcal{I}(u)\leq\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}+\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\,,

and thereby the assertion (
4.1
).
This completes the proof.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
be given.
Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
is bandlimited with bandwidth
\delta\in\big(0,\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{m-1}{m}\,\pi}\big]
. Further let
\varphi_{\sinh}
be the
\sinh
-type window function (
1.6
)
with shape parameter
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
.

Then the
\sinh
-type regularized Shannon sampling formula satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\big\|f-R_{\sinh,m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\big(4+9\,\sqrt{m\,(\pi-\delta)}\,\big)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

(4.6)

Proof
. (i) Since
\varphi_{\sinh}
in (
1.6
) is compactly supported on
[-m,\,m]
and
\varphi_{\sinh}(m)=0
, we have
E_{2}(m)=0
. Thus, according to Theorem
2.1
, the approximation
error can be estimated by

\displaystyle\big\|f-R_{\sinh,m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}\big|\Delta_{\sinh}(\omega)\big|\,,

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sinh}(\omega)\coloneqq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}{\hat{\varphi}}_{\sinh}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

(4.7)

Following
[
19
, p. 38, 7.58]
, the Fourier transform of (
1.6
) has the form

\displaystyle{\hat{\varphi}}_{\sinh}(\tau)=\frac{m\,\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}\,\sinh\beta}\cdot\begin{cases}(1-\nu^{2})^{-1/2}\,I_{1}\big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\big)&\colon|\nu|<1\,,\\[4.30554pt]
(\nu^{2}-1)^{-1/2}\,J_{1}\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)&\colon|\nu|>1\,,\end{cases}

(4.8)

with the scaled frequency
\nu=\frac{m}{\beta}\,\tau
, where
J_{1}
denotes the Bessel function and
I_{1}
the modified Bessel function of first order.
Substituting
\tau=\frac{\beta}{m}\,\nu
in the integral in (
4.7
), the function
\Delta_{\sinh}
reads as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sinh}(\omega)\coloneqq 1-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\,\int_{-\nu_{1}(-\omega)}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}{\hat{\varphi}}_{\sinh}\big(\tfrac{\beta}{m}\,\nu\big)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,,

(4.9)

with the increasing linear function

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(\omega)\coloneqq\frac{m}{\beta}\,(\omega+\pi)\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

(4.10)

(ii) Now we choose the shape parameter of (
1.6
) in the special form
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle 1=\nu_{1}(-\delta)\leq\nu_{1}(\omega)=\frac{\omega+\pi}{\pi-\delta}\leq\nu_{1}(\delta)=\frac{\pi+\delta}{\pi-\delta}\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

In view of (
4.8
) we split (
4.9
) in the form
\Delta_{\sinh}(\omega)=\Delta_{\sinh,1}-\Delta_{\sinh,2}(\omega)
with

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sinh,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1-\frac{\beta}{\sinh\beta}\,\int_{0}^{1}\frac{I_{1}\big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\big)}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sinh,2}(\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\beta}{2\,\sinh\beta}\,\bigg(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\bigg)\,\frac{J_{1}\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

Using
[
11
, 6.681–11]
and
[
1
, 10.2.13]
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{I_{1}\big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\big)}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}I_{1}(\beta\,\cos\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=\frac{\pi}{2}\,\bigg(I_{1/2}\bigg(\frac{\beta}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{2}=\frac{2}{\beta}\left(\sinh\frac{\beta}{2}\right)^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0<\;}\Delta_{\sinh,1}=1-\frac{2\,\big(\sinh\frac{\beta}{2}\big)^{2}}{\sinh\beta}=\frac{2\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\;<2\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}\,.

(4.11)

By Lemma
4.1
we have

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\big|\Delta_{\sinh,2}(\omega)\big|\leq\frac{\beta}{\,\sinh\beta}\left(\frac{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{\beta}+\frac{{\sqrt{2}}\,\pi}{\sqrt{\beta}}\right)<\left(2+\sqrt{\beta}\,\frac{2\,\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi}}\right)\!\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,,

(4.12)

since by assumption the parameters
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
and
\delta\in\big(0,\,\frac{m-1}{m}\,\pi\big]
are chosen such that
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)\geq\pi
.
Additionally, note that

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi}}=8.902390\ldots<9

holds.

Thereby, the terms (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) have the same exponential decay
m\,(\pi-\delta)
and (
4.9
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle\big|\Delta_{\sinh}(\omega)\big|={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta_{\sinh,1}+\big|\Delta_{\sinh,2}(\omega)\big|\leq\big(4+9\,\sqrt{\beta}\,\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

Thus, the
\sinh
-type regularized Shannon sampling formula with the chosen shape parameter
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
fulfills the error estimate (
4.6
).
This completes the proof.

Note
that already in

, Theorem 6.1]

) with suitably chosen shape parameter

.

, they are basically the same, only adapted to the slightly different settings considered in

]
.

However, it has to be pointed out that the error constant in Theorem
4.2
is somewhat worsened in comparison to our previous findings in
[

,
14
]

4.1
comprises a weaker version of the numerical assumption in

In addition, similar to Section
3

\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
for the

5
Regularization with the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function

In this section, we consider the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
1.7
) with shape parameter
\beta>0
, analogous to
[
14
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Once more, we put special emphasis on addressing minor gaps in
[
14
]
by rigorously proving assumptions that were previously based solely on numerical tests.

Furthermore, we show that the exponential decay with respect to the truncation parameter
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
for the uniform approximation error
\big\|f-R_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}
is
the same as for
the approximation error
\big\|f-R_{\sinh,m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}
in Theorem
4.2
, only with a slightly worse error constant.
To do so, we firstly establish the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all
\beta\geq\pi
we have

\displaystyle\left|I_{0}(\beta)-{\mathbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

(5.1)

where
{\textbf{L}}_{0}
denotes the
modified Struve function

\displaystyle{\textbf{L}}_{0}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/2)^{2k+1}}{\big(\Gamma\big(k+\frac{3}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}=\frac{2x}{\pi}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{\big((2k+1)!!\big)^{2}}\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(5.2)

see
[
1
, 12.2.1]
, and
\mathrm{Si}
is the
sine integral function

\displaystyle\mathrm{Si}(x)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\sin v}{v}\,{\mathrm{d}}v\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(5.3)

Proof
.
By
[
3
, Theorem 1]
the function
h(x)\coloneqq I_{0}(x)-{\mathbf{L}}_{0}(x)
is completely monotonic on
[0,\,\infty)
, i. e., it satisfies
h(x)\geq 0
and
h^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in[0,\,\infty)
.
Thereby, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq h(\beta)\leq h(\pi)\,,\quad\beta\geq\pi\,.

(5.4)

Due to the fact that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Si}^{\prime}(x)=\mathrm{sinc}\big(\tfrac{x}{\pi}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Si}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^{2}}&\colon x\neq 0\,,\\
0&\colon x=0\,,\end{cases}

the sine integral function (
5.3
) has its local maxima at
(2k+1)\pi
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, and its local minima at
2k\pi
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
Moreover, by the definition (
5.3
) the extremal points of the sine integral function become smaller in magnitude for
x\geq\pi
when
x\to\infty
, and
\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathrm{Si}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Thus, for all
\beta\geq\pi
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Si}(2\pi)\leq\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\leq\mathrm{Si}(\pi)\,,

and consequently

\displaystyle-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(2\pi)\leq-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\leq-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\pi)\,,\quad\beta\geq\pi\,.

(5.5)

Combining (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle-0.097176...=-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(2\pi)

\displaystyle\leq h(\beta)-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)

\displaystyle\leq h(\pi)-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\pi)=0.400229\ldots

for all
\beta\geq\pi
, which finally gives (
5.1
).
This completes the proof.

Lemma 5.2
.

The function

\displaystyle g(x)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x}}{x\,(I_{0}(x)-1)}

(5.6)

is monotonously decreasing for
x\geq 1
.

Proof
.
Using
I_{0}^{\prime}(x)=I_{1}(x)
, see
[
1
, 9.6.27]
, the derivative of the function (
5.6
) is given by

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{x}\,(-x-x\,I_{1}(x)+(x-1)\,I_{0}(x)+1)}{x^{2}\,(I_{0}(x)-1)^{2}}\,.

To prove that (
5.6
) is monotonously decreasing for
x\geq 1
, we need to show that
g^{\prime}(x)<0
,
x\geq 1
, or rather

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(x)\coloneqq-x-x\,I_{1}(x)+(x-1)\,I_{0}(x)+1<0,\quad x\geq 1\,.

Note that

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(1)=-1-I_{1}(1)+0+1=-I_{1}(1)=-0.565159\ldots<0\,.

Thus, by showing
\tilde{g}^{\prime}(x)\leq 0
,
x\geq 1
, we see that
\tilde{g}
is negative for all
x\geq 1
, and thereby
g
is monotonously decreasing.

In order to do so, we use the formula
(x\,I_{1})^{\prime}(x)=x\,I_{0}(x)
, see
[
1
, 9.6.28]
, to compute the derivative

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-1-x\,I_{0}(x)+I_{0}(x)+(x-1)\,I_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=(x-1)[I_{1}(x)-I_{0}(x)]-1\,.

Since
I_{1}(x)\leq I_{0}(x)
for all
x\geq 0
by
[
2
, (2.3)]
we have
\tilde{g}^{\prime}(x)<0
for all
x\geq 1
, which completes the proof.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
is bandlimited with bandwidth
\delta\in\big(0,\,\frac{m-1}{m}\pi\big]
. Further let
\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}
be the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
1.7
) with shape parameter
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
and let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{1\}
be given.

Then the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula satisfies the error estimate

\displaystyle\big\|f-R_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\bigg(\frac{7}{8}\,m\,(\pi-\delta)+\frac{7}{\pi}\,m^{2}(\pi-\delta)^{2}\bigg)\,\mathrm{e}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)}\,\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

(5.7)

Proof
.
(i)
Since
\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}
in (
1.7
) is compactly supported on
[-m,\,m]
and
\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}(m)=0
, we have
E_{2}(m)=0
. Thus, according to Theorem
2.1
, the approximation error can be estimated by

\displaystyle\big\|f-R_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f\big\|_{C_{0}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}\big|\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\omega)\big|

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\omega)\coloneqq 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}{\hat{\varphi}}_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

(5.8)

Following
[
19
, p. 3, 1.1, and p. 95, 18.31]
, the Fourier transform of (
1.7
) has the form

\displaystyle{\hat{\varphi}}_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\tau)=\frac{m\,\sqrt{2}}{(I_{0}(\beta)-1)\,\sqrt{\pi}}\cdot\begin{cases}\bigg(\frac{\sinh\big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\bigg)&\colon|\nu|<1\,,\\[8.61108pt]
\bigg(\frac{\sin\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\bigg)&\colon|\nu|>1\,,\end{cases}

(5.9)

with the scaled frequency
\nu=\frac{m}{\beta}\,\tau
.
Substituting
\tau=\frac{\beta}{m}\,\nu
in the integral in (
5.8
), the function
\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}}
reads as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\omega)=1-\frac{\beta}{m\,\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{-\nu_{1}(-\omega)}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}{\hat{\varphi}}_{\mathrm{cKB}}\big(\tfrac{\beta}{m}\,\nu\big)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,,

(5.10)

with the increasing linear function (
4.10
).

(ii) Now we choose the shape parameter of (
1.7
) in the special form
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle 1=\nu_{1}(-\delta)\leq\nu_{1}(\omega)=\frac{\omega+\pi}{\pi-\delta}\leq\nu_{1}(\delta)=\frac{\pi+\delta}{\pi-\delta}\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

In view of (
5.9
) we split (
5.10
) in the form
\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\omega)=\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},1}-\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},2}(\omega)
with

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},1}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2\beta}{\pi\,(I_{0}(\beta)-1)}\,\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{\sinh\big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\pi\,(I_{0}(\beta)-1)}\,\bigg(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{\sin\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

(5.11)

Using
[
11
, 3.997–1]
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh\big(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sinh(\beta\,\cos s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\pi}{2\beta}\,{\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)

with the modified Struve function (
5.2
).
Additionally, by the definition of the sine integral function
(
5.3
)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin(\beta v)}{\beta v}\,{\mathrm{d}}v=\frac{1}{\beta}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\,,

such that we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},1}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2\beta}{\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2\beta}\,{\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)-\frac{1}{\beta}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\bigg(I_{0}(\beta)-{\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\bigg)\,.

Since for
\delta\in\big(0,\,\frac{m-1}{m}\pi\big]
we have
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)\geq\pi
and therefore
I_{0}(\beta)>1
, Lemma
5.1
yields

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|}\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|}\leq\frac{1}{2\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,.

Now we estimate
\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},2}(\omega)
in (
5.11
) for
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
by the triangle inequality as

\displaystyle\big|\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},2}(\omega)\big|\leq\frac{\beta}{\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\bigg(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\bigg)\bigg|\frac{\sin\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\bigg|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

By
[
22
, Lemma 4.1]
we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\sin\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\bigg|\leq\frac{2}{\nu^{2}}\,,\quad\nu\geq 1\,,

and therefore

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},2}(\omega)|\leq\frac{4\beta}{\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu=\frac{4\beta}{\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,.

Thereby, we conclude that

\displaystyle|\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\omega)|\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|}\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB},1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|}+|\Delta_{\mathrm{cKB}2}(\omega)|\leq\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4\beta}{\pi}\bigg)\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

Since by the assumption
0<\delta\leq\frac{m-1}{m}\,\pi
we have

\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)\geq\pi
for
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and
by Lemma
5.2
the function
\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{x}}{x\,(I_{0}(x)-1)}
is monotonously decreasing for
x\geq 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{\beta}}{\beta\,(I_{0}(\beta)-1)}\leq\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{\pi}}{\pi\,(I_{0}(\pi)-1)}=1.644967\ldots<\frac{7}{4}\,.

Hence, this yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4\beta}{\pi}\bigg)

\displaystyle<\frac{7\beta}{4}\,\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4\beta}{\pi}\bigg)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}=\bigg(\frac{7}{8}\,\beta+\frac{7}{\pi}\,\beta^{2}\bigg)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\,.

Thus, the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula with the chosen shape parameter
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
fulfills the error estimate (
5.7
).
This completes the proof.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the regularized Shannon sampling formula (
1.4
) for the widely used Gaussian function (
1.5
), the
\sinh
-type window function (
1.6
), and the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
1.7
).
More precisely, for an arbitrary bandlimited function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap C(\mathbb{R})
with bandwidth
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
we have shown that the uniform approximation error (
1.9
) of the regularized Shannon sampling formulas of
f
possess an exponential decay with respect to the truncation parameter
m
.
In doing so, we have demonstrated that the decay rate
m\,(\pi-\delta)
of the
\sinh
-type
regularized Shannon sampling formula, see Theorem
4.2
, and the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula, see Theorem
5.3
, is
twice as fast as
the decay rate
m\,(\pi-\delta)/2
of the Gaussian regularized Shannon sampling formula, see Theorem
3.1
.
Note that the
\sinh
-type
regularized Shannon sampling formula is even
slightly
better than the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula due to the constant factors in (
4.6
) and (
5.7
), see also Figure
6.1
.

The main focus of this work was to elaborate on previous results in
[
13
,
14
]
.
First and foremost, the goal was to rigorously prove all the necessary ingredients for the proofs, thereby improving upon the results in
[
13
,
14
]
that lacked rigor due to the use of numerical assumptions.

In addition
, we found that the exponential decay of the approximation error of the regularized Shannon sampling formula (
1.4
) depends highly on the shape parameter of the corresponding window function.

Although the optimality
of the variance
\sigma^{2}
of the Gaussian function and of the shape parameter
\beta
of the
\sinh
-type
window function and the continuous Kaiser–Bessel function is
still an open problem, we have strong reason to believe that our choice is best, even though this is currently only based on numerical observations.

We remark that this
further emphasizes the superiority of the
\sinh
-type
regularized Shannon sampling formula of
f
, since the approximation errors of the regularized Shannon sampling formulas were compared for the
presumably optimal
shape parameters each.

Threshold odd solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation in one dimension
Stephen Gustafson, Takahisa Inui

Abstract
. We consider odd solutions to the Schrödinger equation with the L 2 L^{2} -supercritical power type nonlinearity in one dimensional Euclidean space. It is known that the odd solution scatters or blows up if its action is less than twice as that of the ground state. In the present paper, we show that the odd solutions with the action as twice as that of the ground state scatter or blow up.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q55,37K40 etc.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

We consider the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation in one dimensional Euclidean space:

(NLS)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,&(t,x)\in I\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

where
I
denotes a time interval and
p>5
.
It is known that the Cauchy problem of (
NLS
) is locally well-posed in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. That is, there exist
T>0
and a unique solution
u\in C((-T,T):H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
if
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, the energy and mass are conserved by the flow, where the energy
E
and mass
M
are defined by

\displaystyle E(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|u\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},\quad M(u):=\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We also have blow-up alternative. Namely, the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm of the solution diverges at the maximal existence time if the time is finite. See
[
14
]
and
[
5
,
22
]
for these results.
In this paper, we are interested in the global behavior of the solution.

Since the work by Kenig and Merle
[
21
]
, many researchers have investigated the global dynamics of the nonlinear Schrödinger equations below the ground state. See
[
16
,
9
,
17
]
for 3d cubic NLS. For the one dimensional NLS, i.e. (
NLS
), Akahori and Nawa
[
1
]
, where they in fact treated the equations with
L^{2}
-super and energy-subcritical nonlinearity in all dimensions, determined the global behavior of the solutions below the ground state.
The ground state is the solution with the formula
u(t,x)=e^{it}Q(x)
, where
Q(x)
is the radial positive solution of the elliptic equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}Q+Q-Q^{p}=0

and it is explicitly given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle Q(x)=\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\left\{\cosh\left(\frac{p-1}{2}x\right)\right\}^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}

in the one dimensional case.
They showed that if the initial data satisfies the energy and mass condition
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}<E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
, where
\sigma=(p-3)/(p-5)
, then the behavior of the solution is determined by the sign of the virial functional
K
at initial time, where the functional is defined by

\displaystyle K(u):=\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}\|u\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

Precisely, they showed the following: if
K(u_{0})\geq 0
, then the solution is global and scatters in both time directions, i.e. there exist
u_{\pm}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}u_{\pm}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0\text{ as }t\to\pm\infty.

If
K(u_{0})<0
, then the solution blows up in finite time or grows up at infinite time. In addition, if the variance of the solution is finite, i.e.
\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
, then the solution blows up in finite time in both time directions. (See also Fang, Xie, and Cazenave
[
13
]
for the scattering result.)

The global behavior of the solutions with
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
, which are called threshold solutions, was also investigated by Duyckaerts and Roudenko
[
12
]
for 3d cubic NLS. Recently, for one dimensional NLS, Campos, Farah, and Roudenko
[
4
]
studied them, where they treated
L^{2}
-supercritical NLS, including the energy-critical one, in general dimensions (see also
[
11
]
). They showed the following theorems in the one dimensional case:

Theorem

(
[
4
]
)
.

There exist radial (even) solutions
Q^{+}
and
Q^{-}
to (
NLS
) on at least
[0,\infty)
such that

•

M(Q^{\pm})=M(Q)
and
E(Q^{\pm})=E(Q)
,

•

Q^{\pm}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|Q^{\pm}(t)-e^{it}Q\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ce^{-ct}\text{ for all }t>0

for some constants
C,c>0
,

•

K(Q^{+}(0))<0
and
Q^{+}
blows up in finite negative time,

•

K(Q^{-}(0))>0
and
Q^{-}
scatters backward in time.

Theorem

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
u_{0}
satisfy
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
and
u
be the solution to (
NLS
). Then we have the following:

(1)

If
K(u_{0})>0
, then
u
is global and either
u
scatters in both time directions or
u=Q^{-}
up to the symmetries of the equation.

(2)

If
K(u_{0})=0
, then
u=Q
up to the symmetries of the equation.

(3)

If
K(u_{0})<0
and
\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
, then either
u
blows up in finite both time or
u=Q^{+}
up to the symmetries of the equation.

The special solutions
Q^{\pm}
, which converge to the ground state, appear at the threshold, though we do not have them below the ground state.

Now, let us assume that
u_{0}
is an odd function. Then the solution
u
is also odd. In this case,
the second author determined the global behavior of the solutions below the ground state as follows:

Theorem

(
[
19
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
u_{0}
be odd and
u
be the solution to (
NLS
).
Assume that
u_{0}
satisfies
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}<2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
. Then we have the following.

(1)

If
K(u_{0})\geq 0
, then
u
is global and scatters in both time directions.

(2)

If
K(u_{0})<0
, then either
u
blows up in finite time or grows up at infinite time.

Remark 1.1
.

In Theorem 1.2 in
[
19
]
, the second author gave the condition in terms of the action and the frequency
\omega
instead of the energy and mass condition
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}<2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
. As seen in Section
2.1
below, the formulation in
[
19
]

is equivalent to the energy and mass condition.

Since the ground state
e^{it}Q
and the special solutions
Q^{\pm}
are even, the assumption of odd symmetry excludes them. That is why we can determine the global dynamics
of odd solutions above the ground state.

In the odd case, the energy and mass condition
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}<2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
is related to a minimization problem restricted to odd functions just as the relation
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}<E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
is.
Roughly, the condition comes from a minimizing sequence of odd functions
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}=\{Q(\cdot-n)-Q(\cdot+n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
E(\varphi_{n})M(\varphi_{n})^{\sigma}\to 2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, the minimizer is not attained. (See Section
2.1
below for more details.) Therefore, we expect that on the threshold
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}

there are no odd solutions like the ground state and the special solutions.
In the present paper, we will show that odd solutions at the threshold scatter or blow up.

Remark 1.2
.

Odd solutions for (
NLS
) can also be regarded as solutions of NLS on half line
[0,\infty)
with Dirichlet zero boundary condition at the origin. Thus, the result in the paper holds for NLS on half line with this Dirichlet zero boundary. The same goes for NLS on the star graph, which is a metric graph connecting half lines, with Dirichlet zero boundary
condition.

1.2.
Main result

We use
H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
to denote the set of odd functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Assume
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
. Then we have the following:

(1)

If
K(u_{0})>0
, then the solution scatters in both time directions.

(2)

In addition, we assume
\|xu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
. If
K(u_{0})<0
, then the solution blows up in finite time in
both time directions.

Remark 1.3
.

K(u_{0})=0
is not possible under the assumption
u_{0}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}

(see Section
2.1
).

Remark 1.4
.

The assumption of oddness is essential. Indeed, Nguyễn
[
24
]
obtained a ”two peak” solution
u
such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{i\gamma(t)}\sum_{k=1}^{2}Q(\cdot-x_{k}(t))\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim t^{-1}

for all
t>0
, where
x_{1}=-x_{2}
and
|x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)|=2(1+o(1))\log t
as
t\to\infty
. This solution satisfies
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
. We note that the solitons have the same sign, so
u
is not odd.

1.3.
Idea of proof

Our proof of the scattering result is based on the contradiction argument by Duyckaerts, Landoulsi, and Roudenko
[
10
]
, where they consider 3d cubic NLS outside an obstacle (see also
[
23
]
and
[
2
]
). If the statement fails, combining a modulation argument and a concentration compactness argument, we construct a critical element at the threshold which has the following compactness property: There exists
x(t)
such that
u(t,\cdot)-\{\psi(t,\cdot-x(t))-\psi(t,-\cdot-x(t))\}
converges to
0
for some
\psi
. If
x(t)
goes to infinity, then the assumption on the virial functional gives us a contradiction, and thus
x(t)
must be bounded. However, the boundedness of
x(t)
implies a contradiction to a modulation argument.

For the blow-up result, we apply the argument by Duyckaerts and Roudenko
[
12
]
(see also
[
4
]
). If the solution is global, the finite variance and the negativity of the virial functional imply that the modulation parameter
y
is bounded. However, this gives a contradiction.

The main difficulty appears in the modulation argument. For NLS with potential as in
[
23
,
2
]
, we consider the linearization around the ground state
Q
and thus we can control the translation parameter
y
easily. On the other hand, in our setting, we need to consider the linearization around
Q(\cdot-y)-Q(\cdot+y)
. Therefore, it is more difficult to control the translation parameter
y
. This is similar to
[
10
]
. They overcame this difficulty by using the result for NLS on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In our proof, the following plays a very important role:

\displaystyle S(Q(\cdot-y)-Q(\cdot+y))-2S(Q)\approx e^{-2y},

where
S=E+M
(see Lemma
4.6
). Since this is positive, we can control the translation parameter
y
. (Since
S(Q(\cdot-y)+Q(\cdot+y))-2S(Q)\approx-e^{-2y}
, the oddness is very important.)
By using this, we can control the modulation parameters.

1.4.
Notation

We define

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle:=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx.

We use following notation: for
y\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{y}f(x)

\displaystyle:=f(x-y),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{y}f(x)

\displaystyle:=f(x-y)-f(-x-y)=\mathcal{T}_{y}f(x)-(\mathcal{T}_{y}f)(-x).

The function
\mathcal{R}_{y}f
is an odd function even when
f
is neither odd nor even.
For an even function
f
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{y}f(x)=\mathcal{T}_{y}f(x)-\mathcal{T}_{-y}f(x)=f(x-y)-f(x+y).

We define a smooth and even cut-off function
\chi_{R}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
by

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(x):=\begin{cases}1&(|x|>1)\\
0&(|x|<1/2)\end{cases},\quad\chi_{R}(x):=\chi_{1}\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)

and we use

\displaystyle\chi_{R}^{c}(x):=1-\chi_{R}(x),\quad\chi_{R}^{+}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)\chi_{R}(x),\quad\chi_{R}^{-}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,0)}(x)\chi_{R}(x),

where
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
denotes the characteristic function on a set
A
.
We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{R,y}f(x):=\chi_{R}^{+}(x)\mathcal{T}_{y}f(x)-\chi_{R}^{-}(x)\mathcal{T}_{-y}f(x).

We set

\displaystyle\mu_{\omega}(u)

\displaystyle:=2\|\partial_{x}Q_{\omega}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
Q_{\omega}(x)=\omega^{\frac{1}{p-1}}Q(\sqrt{\omega}x)
,
and we denote
\mu=\mu_{1}
for simplicity.

We use the notation
A\lesssim B
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. The notation
A\approx B
means that both
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
hold.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Variational argument

In this section, we consider the variational structure for odd functions.

2.1.1.
Minimizing problem for odd functions

Let
\omega>0
.
We define the action by

\displaystyle S_{\omega}(f)

\displaystyle:=E(f)+\frac{\omega}{2}M(f)=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}.

We consider the following minimizing problems:

\displaystyle l_{\omega}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{S_{\omega}(f):f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},K(f)=0\},

\displaystyle l_{\omega}^{{\rm odd}}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{S_{\omega}(f):f\in H_{{\rm odd}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\},K(f)=0\}.

It is known that
l_{\omega}
is attained by the ground state
Q_{\omega}
, i.e.
l_{\omega}=S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})
, where
we recall
Q_{\omega}(x)=\omega^{\frac{1}{p-1}}Q(\sqrt{\omega}x)
, and
Q_{\omega}
is the radial positive solution to the elliptic equation
-\partial_{x}^{2}Q_{\omega}+\omega Q_{\omega}-Q_{\omega}^{p}=0
. We note
Q=Q_{1}
and set
S:=S_{1}
.

We prove that
l_{\omega}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}=2l_{\omega}
and there is no minimizer.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have
l_{\omega}^{{\rm odd}}=2l_{\omega}(=2S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega}))
.

Proof.

Define a sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}
of odd functions by
\varphi_{n}(x):=\mathcal{R}_{n}Q_{\omega}(x)
. Then
\{\varphi_{n}\}
satisfies
S_{\omega}(\varphi_{n})\to 2S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})
and
K(\varphi_{n})\to 0
.
Take a parameter
\lambda_{n}
such that
K(\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n})=0
. Then
\lambda_{n}\to 1
and thus
S_{\omega}(\lambda_{n}\varphi_{n})\to 2S(Q_{\omega})
. Therefore, we get
l_{\omega}^{{\rm odd}}\leq 2S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})=2l_{\omega}
.

On the other hand, taking a minimizing sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}
of
l^{\rm odd}

We note that
\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\varphi_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\partial_{x}(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\varphi_{n})=\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\partial_{x}\varphi_{n}
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle 2S(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\varphi_{n})=S(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(\cdot)\varphi_{n}(\cdot)+\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(-\cdot)\varphi(-\cdot))=S(\varphi_{n})\to l_{\omega}^{\rm odd},

\displaystyle 2K(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\varphi_{n})=K(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\cdot)\varphi_{n}(\cdot)+\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(-\cdot)\varphi_{n}(-\cdot))=K(\varphi_{n})=0.

This means that
l_{\omega}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}/2\geq l_{\omega}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There is no minimizer of
l_{\omega}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a minimizer, i.e.
S_{\omega}(f)=l_{\omega}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}
and
K(f)=0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle 2S(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f)=S(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(\cdot)f(\cdot)-\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(-\cdot)f(-\cdot))=S(f)=l_{\omega}^{\rm odd}=2l_{\omega},

\displaystyle 2K(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f)=K(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(\cdot)f(\cdot)-\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(-\cdot)f(-\cdot))=K(f)=0.

The uniqueness of the minimizer of
l_{\omega}
implies that
\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f=e^{i\theta}Q_{\omega}(\cdot-y)
. Obviously, this is a contradiction.
∎

2.1.2.
Energy and mass condition

In this section, we prove that the action condition
S_{\omega}(f)\leq 2S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})
is equivalent to the energy and mass condition
E(f)M(f)^{\sigma}\leq 2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
for odd functions
f
.

By properties of the ground state, we have the following:

Lemma 2.3

(The Pohozaev identity)
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+3}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{p-1}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2(p+1)}\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}

and, in particular,

\displaystyle M(Q)=\frac{2(p+3)}{p-5}E(Q).

Lemma 2.4

(Scaling of the ground state)
.

We have

\displaystyle M(Q_{\omega})=\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(Q),\ E(Q_{\omega})=\omega^{\frac{p+3}{2(p-1)}}E(Q).

Proposition 2.5
.

For
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

E(f)M(f)^{\sigma}\leq 2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
.

(2)

There exists
\omega>0
such that
S_{\omega}(f)\leq 2S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})
.

Proof.

For a positive number
M>0
, let
F(M):=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}M^{-\sigma}
.
The function
F
is strictly convex on
(0,\infty)
. By a simple calculation (using the Pohozaev identity and the scaling property), we see that
E=-\frac{\omega}{2}M+2S_{\omega}(Q_{\omega})
is a tangent line of
F
at
M=2\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(Q)=M(Q_{\omega})
. This implies the result.
∎

As a corollary, we see the following:

Corollary 2.6
.

For
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

E(f)M(f)^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
.

(2)

There exists
\omega>0
such that
E(f)=2E(Q_{\omega})
and
M(f)=2M(Q_{\omega})
.

2.1.3.
Reduction by the scaling

As seen in Corollary
2.6
, to prove the main result, we may assume that
M(u_{0})=2M(Q_{\omega})
and
E(u_{0})=2E(Q_{\omega})
for some
\omega>0
. By using the scaling invariance of the equation (
NLS
), we may also assume that
M(u_{0})=2M(Q)
and
E(u_{0})=2E(Q)
. We prove the sufficiency in this section.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
M(u_{0})=2M(Q)
and
E(u_{0})=2E(Q)
. Then we have

(1)

If
K(u_{0})>0
, then the solution scatters in both time directions.

(2)

In addition, we assume finite variance.
If
K(u_{0})<0
, then the solution blows up in finite positive and negative time.

If the above Theorem
2.7
holds, we get the main result, Theorem
1.1
, by a scaling argument.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
2.7
.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
E(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}
and
K(u_{0})>0
.
We have
M(u_{0})>0
by assumption. Thus, there exists
\omega>0
such that
M(u_{0})=\omega^{\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(Q)=M(Q_{\omega})
. Then since we also have

\displaystyle 2^{1+\sigma}E(Q)M(Q)^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}\omega^{-\frac{p+3}{2(p-1)}}E(Q)\{\omega^{\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(Q)\}^{\sigma}=2^{1+\sigma}E(Q_{\omega})M(Q_{\omega})^{\sigma},

we get

\displaystyle E(u_{0})=2E(Q_{\omega})=2\omega^{-\frac{p+3}{2(p-1)}}E(Q).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\omega^{\frac{p+3}{2(p-1)}}E(u_{0})=2E(Q)\text{ and }\omega^{-\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}}M(u_{0})=M(Q).

Let
u_{0,\omega^{-1}}(x):=\omega^{-1/(p-1)}u_{0}(\omega^{-1/2}x)
. Then we get

\displaystyle E(u_{0,\omega^{-1}})=2E(Q)\text{ and }M(u_{0,\omega^{-1}})=M(Q).

We also have

\displaystyle K(u_{0,\omega^{-1}})=\omega^{\frac{p+3}{2(p-1)}}K(u_{0})>0

By Theorem
2.7
, we find that the solution
u_{\omega^{-1}}
with the initial data
u_{0,\omega^{-1}}
scatters.
Since the equation (
NLS
) is invariant under this scaling, the behavior of the solution
u
with
u(0)=u_{0}
is same as that of
u_{\omega^{-1}}
. Thus
u
scatters. This argument also works for the case that
K
is negative.
∎

Thus, it is enough to consider the case of
\omega=1
.

2.1.4.
The virial functional and variational argument

We will show the following in two ways:

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Assume
M(f)=2M(Q)
and
E(f)=2E(Q)
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

K(f)>0
.

(2)

\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<2^{1+\sigma}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

(3)

\mu(f)>0
.

The equivalence of (2) and (3) holds by
M(f)=2M(Q)
. The equivalence of (1) and (3) follows from
E(f)=2E(Q)
:

Lemma 2.9
.

If
E(f)=2E(Q)
, we have
K(f)=\frac{p-5}{4}\mu(f)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle K(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{2}E(f)-\frac{p-5}{4}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-1}{2}2E(Q)-\frac{p-5}{4}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-5}{4}\left(2\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{p-5}{4}\mu(f),

by the Pohozaev identity
E(Q)=\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
∎

We also give a direct proof of the equivalence of (1) and (2) by using the best constant of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for odd functions. The best constant will be also used later.

Lemma 2.10

(The Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for odd functions)
.

We have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\leq C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

for any odd functions
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})

and

\displaystyle C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}:=\sup\left\{\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}:f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}\right\}=2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}

Proof.

By the usual Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have
C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\leq C_{GN}
.
We show
C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\geq 2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}
.
Applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality to
\varphi_{n}:=\mathcal{T}_{n}Q-\mathcal{T}_{-n}Q
, we get

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\leq C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\leq 2^{\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

Therefore, we have
C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\geq 2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}
.

Next, we show
2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}\geq C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}
. Let
\{f_{n}\}\subset H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}\to C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}

We have

\displaystyle(\text{LHS})=2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}\frac{\|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{\|\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}\leq 2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}

Since the left hand side goes to
(C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}})^{-1}
, we get
2^{-\frac{p-1}{2}}C_{GN}\geq C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
If
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2^{1+\sigma}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, then we have
K(f)\geq 0
.
Moreover, if
M(f)^{\sigma}E(f)\leq 2^{1+\sigma}M(Q)^{\sigma}E(Q)
and
K(f)\geq 0
, then we have
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2^{1+\sigma}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

Proof.

We assume that
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}
.
This means that
\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-5}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-5}{2}}
.
By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for odd functions and the assumption, we have

\displaystyle K(f)

\displaystyle\geq\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{(p-1)C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}}{2(p+1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{(p-1)C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}}{2(p+1)}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-5}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{(p-1)C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}}{2(p+1)}2^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-5}{2}}\right)

By the Pohozaev identity, we get

\displaystyle\frac{(p-1)C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}}{2(p+1)}2^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p-5}{2}}=1.

Therefore, we have
K(f)\geq 0
.

Next, assume
K(f)\geq 0
. By the Pohozaev identity and
2^{1+\sigma}M(Q)^{\sigma}E(Q)\geq M(f)^{\sigma}E(f)
, we obtain

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}2^{1+\sigma}M(Q)^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2^{1+\sigma}M(Q)^{\sigma}E(Q)\geq M(f)^{\sigma}E(f).

By the assumption, we have

\displaystyle E(f)\geq E(f)-\frac{2}{p-1}K(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, combining this with (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}2^{1+\sigma}M(Q)^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\frac{p-5}{2(p-1)}M(f)^{\sigma}\|f\|_{H}^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Invariance of the potential-well sets by the flow

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
M(u_{0})=2M(Q)
and
E(u_{0})=2E(Q)
and
u(t)
be a solution to (
NLS
) with

. Then we have the following.

(1)

If
K(u_{0})>0
, then the solution
u(t)
exists globally in both time directions and
K(u(t))>0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

If
K(u_{0})<0
, then the solution
u(t)
satisfies
K(u(t))<0

while the solution exists.

Proof.

Since
u_{0}
is odd, the solution
u(t)
is also odd.
We consider the case of
K(u_{0})>0
. Suppose that there exists a time

such that

. Then by the continuity of the flow, there exists a time
t_{0}
such that

. This and the assumption

and

mean that

, which is not the zero function, is a minimizer of
l^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}
. However, this contradicts the non-existence of the minimizer. Therefore we have
K(u(t))>0
for all

in the existence interval.
We get an a priori bound by Proposition

, and the blow-up alternative implies that the solution exists globally in both time directions. In the case of

, we get the result in the same way.
∎

2.2.2.
Virial identity and its localization

For a solution
u(t)
, we define

\displaystyle J(u(t))

\displaystyle=J_{\infty}(u(t)):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{2}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

Then we have

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(u(t))

\displaystyle=2\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}x\overline{u(t,x)}\partial_{x}u(t,x)dx,

\displaystyle J^{\prime\prime}(u(t))

\displaystyle=8K(u(t)).

Let
\varphi
be an even function in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi(x):=\begin{cases}x^{2},&(|x|<1),\\
0,&(|x|>2).\end{cases}

For a solution
u(t)
, we set

\displaystyle J_{R}(u(t))

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R^{2}\varphi\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

Then, we have

\displaystyle J_{R}^{\prime}(u(t))

\displaystyle=2\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}R(\partial_{x}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\overline{u(t,x)}\partial_{x}u(t,x)dx

and

\displaystyle J_{R}^{\prime\prime}(u(t))

\displaystyle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\left\{|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\right\}dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{R^{2}}(\partial_{x}^{4}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=8K(u(t))+A_{R}(u(t)),

where we set

\displaystyle A_{R}(u(t))

\displaystyle:=-4\int_{|x|>R}\left\{2-(\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\right\}\left\{|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\right\}dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{R<|x|<2R}\frac{1}{R^{2}}(\partial_{x}^{4}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx.

For a function
f\in H^{1}
, we set

\displaystyle F_{R}(f)

\displaystyle:=4\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)\left\{|\partial_{x}f|^{2}-\frac{p-1}{2(p+1)}|f|^{p+1}\right\}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{R^{2}}(\partial_{x}^{4}\varphi)\left(\frac{x}{R}\right)|f|^{2}dx.

Then the above equality is written by

\displaystyle A_{R}(u(t))=F_{R}(u(t))-8K(u(t))=F_{R}(u(t))-F_{\infty}(u(t)),

where we note that
F_{\infty}(f)=8K(f)
.

Proof.

follows from the Pohozaev identity. The latter statement follows from the first statement and the estimate of cross terms by Lemma

.
∎

We use the following Lebesgue exponents:

\displaystyle r:=p+1,\quad a:=\frac{2(p-1)(p+1)}{p+3}\quad\text{and}\quad b:=\frac{2(p-1)(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}-(p-1)-4}.

Let

Linear profile decomposition

We have a linear profile decomposition for odd functions.

Proposition 2.21

(Linear profile decomposition for odd functions)
.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, up to subsequence, we can write

(2.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}e^{-it_{n}^{j}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{x_{n}^{j}}\psi^{j}+R_{n}^{J},\quad\forall J\in\mathbb{N},

where

,
x_{n}^{j}\geq 0
,
\psi^{j}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
R_{n}^{J}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and the following hold:

•

for any fixed
j
, we have :

\displaystyle\text{either }t_{n}^{j}=0\text{ for any }n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ or }t_{n}^{j}\to\pm\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty,

\displaystyle\text{either }x_{n}^{j}=0\text{ for any }n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ or }x_{n}^{j}\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty.

•

orthogonality of the parameters:

|t_{n}^{j}-t_{n}^{k}|+|x_{n}^{j}-x_{n}^{k}|\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty,\quad\forall j\neq k.

•

smallness of the remainder:

\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists J=J(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}R_{n}^{J}\|_{L_{t,x}^{\infty}}<\varepsilon.

•

orthogonality in norms: for any
J\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\mathcal{R}_{x_{n}^{j}}\psi^{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+o_{n}(1),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\partial_{x}\mathcal{R}_{x_{n}^{j}}\psi^{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}R_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+o_{n}(1).

Moreover, we have

\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{q}}^{q}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|e^{-it_{n}^{j}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{x_{n}^{j}}\psi^{j}\|_{L^{q}}^{q}+\|R_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{q}}^{q}+o_{n}(1),\quad q\in(2,\infty),\quad\forall J\in\mathbb{N}.

[
18

\displaystyle\varphi_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}e^{-it_{n}^{j}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathcal{T}_{x_{n}^{j}}\tilde{\psi}^{j}+\tilde{R}_{n}^{J}.

and

, we get (

) and the other statements follows from the linear profile decomposition.

3.
Coercivity of the linearized operator

In this section, we give the coercivity of a linearized operator, which is used in the following modulation argument. Before stating our coercivity, we recall the coercivity statement of
[
4
]

for the linearized equation.

We set

\displaystyle\Phi(f,g)

\displaystyle=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}f(x)\overline{\partial_{x}g(x)}+f(x)\overline{g(x)}

\displaystyle\quad-|Q|^{p-1}(pf_{1}(x)g_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)g_{2}(x))dx,

where
f=f_{1}+if_{2}
and
g=g_{1}+ig_{2}
and we also set
\Phi(f):=\Phi(f,f)
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

Let
h\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy the following orthogonality conditions:

\displaystyle\im\int h\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx=\re\int h\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx=\re\int h(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}dx=0.

Then, there exists a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}h)\geq c\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

See also
[
26
]
and
[
12
]
.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the following coercivity
statement:

Lemma 3.2

(Coercivity)
.

Let
h\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy the orthogonality conditions:

\displaystyle\im\int h\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx=\re\int h\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx=\re\int h\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}dx=0.

Then, there exists a positive constant
c
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h))\geq c\|\chi_{R}h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{R}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

for
R>1
.

Proof.

We set

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):\re\int fQ^{p}dx=\im\int fQdx=\re\int f\partial_{x}Qdx=0\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}

\displaystyle:=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{iQ,Q^{p},\partial_{x}Q\}.

Then, we write
\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h)=\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}+\mathcal{T}_{-y}r
such that
\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}\in\mathscr{A}
and
\mathcal{T}_{-y}r\in\mathscr{B}
.
Since
\mathcal{T}_{-y}r\in\mathscr{B}
, we write

(3.1)

\mathcal{T}_{-y}r=\alpha\partial_{x}Q+\beta iQ+\gamma Q^{p},

where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
By multiplying (
3.1
) by
-iQ
and taking real part and integral, we get

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{1}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}r,iQ\rangle=\frac{1}{\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h)-\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h},iQ\rangle.

We have
\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h},iQ\rangle=0
since
\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}\in\mathscr{A}
and we also have
\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h),iQ\rangle=0
by the orthogonality assumption. Thus,
\beta=0
. By multiplying (
3.1
) by
Q_{\omega}^{p}
and taking integral and real part, we get
\gamma=0
in a similar way. By multiplying (
3.1
) by
\partial_{x}Q
and taking integral and imaginary part, we get

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{1}{\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}r,\partial_{x}Q\rangle=\frac{1}{\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h),\partial_{x}Q\rangle

since
\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}\in\mathscr{A}
. Now, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h),\partial_{x}Q\rangle

\displaystyle=\re\int\chi_{R}^{+}h\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx

\displaystyle=\re\int\chi_{R}^{+}h\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx-\re\int h\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx,

where we use
\re\int h\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx=0
by the orthogonality assumption. By a direct calculation, it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\re\int\chi_{R}^{+}h\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx-\re\int h\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx\right|

\displaystyle=\int|h||\partial_{x}\chi_{R}^{+}|\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|r\|_{H^{1}}=\|\mathcal{T}_{-y}r\|_{H^{1}}=|\alpha|\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|h\|_{L^{2}}

and thus

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}r)|\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now, since
\Phi
is bilinear, we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h))=\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}+\mathcal{T}_{-y}r)=\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h})+\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}r)+2\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h},\mathcal{T}_{-y}r).

Then, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h})\gtrsim\|\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Moreover, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\gtrsim\|\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\|\mathcal{T}_{-y}r\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\gtrsim\|\chi_{R}^{+}h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Now, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}\tilde{h},\mathcal{T}_{-y}r)|\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|\tilde{h}\|_{H^{1}}\|h\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Combining (
3.2
)–(
3.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h))\gtrsim\|\chi_{R}^{+}h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{R}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

By the symmetry, we have
2\|\chi_{R}^{+}h\|_{H^{1}}=\|\chi_{R}h\|_{H^{1}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Modulation

We start with a simple version of modulation.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists
\mu_{0}>0
and a function
\varepsilon:(0,\mu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
such that the following holds. For any
\mu<\mu_{0}
and for all
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
E(f)=2E(Q)
,
M(f)=2M(Q)
and
\mu(f)<\mu
, there exist
(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,\infty)
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|f-e^{i\theta}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\mu).

Proof.

We use a contradiction argument. We suppose that the statement fails.
Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
\mu_{n}
with
\mu_{n}\to 0
and
f_{n}\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
E(f_{n})=2E(Q)
,
M(f_{n})=2M(Q)
and
\mu(f_{n})<\mu_{n}
such that
\inf_{\theta\in\mathbb{R}}\inf_{y\geq 0}\|f_{n}-e^{i\theta}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\|_{H^{1}}>\varepsilon_{0}
.
Since
\mu(f_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and
E(f_{n})=2E(Q)
, we have
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\to 2\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}
and thus

\displaystyle S(f_{n})\to 2S(Q)\text{ and }K(f_{n})\to 2K(Q)=0.

Since
f
is odd, we have
\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and it holds that

\displaystyle S(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n})\to S(Q)\text{ and }K(\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n})\to K(Q)=0.

Therefore,
\{\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n}\}
is a minimizing sequence, and we obtain
(\theta,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}f_{n}\to e^{i\theta}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q
in
H^{1}
by the characterization of the ground state. By the symmetry, we also have
\mathbbm{1}_{(-\infty,0)}f_{n}\to-e^{i\theta}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q
in
H^{1}
. Therefore it holds that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}-e^{i\theta}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\|_{H^{1}}\to 0.

This is a contradiction.
∎

We have the following modulation with orthogonality conditions.

Lemma 4.2

(Modulation)
.

Let
R>0
be sufficiently large.
There exist
\mu_{0}>0
and a function
\varepsilon:(0,\mu_{0})\to(0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon(\mu)\to 0
as
\mu\to 0
such that the following holds. For any
\mu<\mu_{0}
and for all
f\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
E(f)=2E(Q)
,
M(f)=2M(Q)
and
\mu(f)<\mu
, there exist
(\tilde{\theta},y)\in\mathbb{R}\times(R,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}f-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon(\mu)

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}g\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx=0,\quad\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}g\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx=0

where
g=e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}f-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q
.

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle J(\tilde{\theta},y,v)=\begin{pmatrix}J_{1}(\tilde{\theta},y,v)\\
J_{2}(\tilde{\theta},y,v)\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}v-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx\\
\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\tilde{\theta}}v-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx\end{pmatrix}

for
\tilde{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}
,
y\gg R
,
v\in H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}
. Then
J(0,y,\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)=0
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J_{1}}{\partial\tilde{\theta}}(0,y,\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{2}dx+O(e^{-2y})

\displaystyle=-\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(e^{-2y})

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J_{2}}{\partial y}(0,y,\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q+\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(R^{-1}+e^{-2y})

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J(\tilde{\theta},y,v)}{\partial(\tilde{\theta},y)}(0,y,\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx&0\\
0&-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q+\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)dx\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}-\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(e^{-2y})&0\\
0&\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(R^{-1}+e^{-2y})\end{pmatrix}

This is invertible for large
R
and
y
. By the implicit function theorem, we get a function
(\tilde{\theta},y):H_{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\tilde{\theta}(v)}v-\mathcal{R}_{y(v)}Q)\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y(v)}Qdx

\displaystyle=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{-i\tilde{\theta}(v)}v-\mathcal{R}_{y(v)}Q)\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y(v)}Q)dx

\displaystyle=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Let
u
be an odd solution satisfying

(4.3)

\displaystyle M(u(t))=2M(Q)\text{ and }E(u(t))=2E(Q).

We set
I_{\mu_{0}}:=\{t\in I_{\max}:\mu(t)<\mu_{0}\}
, where
I_{\max}
denotes the maximal existence time interval of the solution.
By Lemma
4.2
, we have
C^{1}
functions
\tilde{\theta}=\tilde{\theta}(t)
and
y=y(t)
for
t\in I_{\mu_{0}}
. We set
\theta:=\tilde{\theta}-1
. We also have orthogonality conditions (
4.2
).
We set

(4.4)

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta(t)+it}(\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q(x)+g(t,x))

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta(t)+it}(\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q(x)+\rho(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}Q(x)+h(t,x)),

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle\rho(t):=\frac{\re\int g\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y(t)}Q)^{p}dx}{\int(\chi_{R}^{+})^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{y(t)}Q)^{p+1}dx}.

Then it follows from (
4.2
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\im\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y(t)}Qdx=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y(t)}Q)dx=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y(t)}Q_{\omega})^{p}dx=0.

4.1.
Estimate of the parameters

We give estimates of the parameters.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

\displaystyle|\rho|\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim|\rho|+\|h\|_{H^{1}},

\displaystyle\|h\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim|\rho|+\|g\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{1}}.

Proof.

These follow from
g=\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h
and the definition of
\rho
.
∎

By the above lemma and
\|g\|_{H^{1}}\leq\mu_{0}
, taking
\mu_{0}
sufficiently small, we may assume that
\|h\|_{H^{1}}
and
|\rho|
are sufficiently small.

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\displaystyle|\rho|\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{1}}+(1+y)e^{-2y}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle M(u(t))

\displaystyle=M(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q+\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h)

\displaystyle=2\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx

\displaystyle\quad+2\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Q(\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h)dx+\|\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It holds from
M(u(t))=2M(Q)
that

(4.7)

\displaystyle-2\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx+2\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Q(\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h)dx+\|\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

This implies that

\displaystyle|\rho|\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{1}}+(1+y)e^{-2y}

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\displaystyle|\rho|\approx|\mu(t)|+O(e^{-2y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle=2\int\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx-2\rho\re\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q)dx

\displaystyle\quad-2\re\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}hdx-\|\partial_{x}(\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We note that

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)=-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q+(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}

since
Q
satisfies the elliptic equation.
It holds from integration by parts and (
4.9
) that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\re\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}hdx=-\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Qhdx+\re\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx.

The second term of (
4.10
) is estimated as follows.

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left|\re\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx\right|\lesssim e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}.

Indeed, we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle\re\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx

\displaystyle=\re\int\{\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-\chi_{R}^{-}(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx

\displaystyle\quad+\re\int\{(1-\chi_{R}^{+})(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(1-\chi_{R}^{-})(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx.

The first term in the right hand side is
0
by (
4.6
) and the symmetry. It follows from Lemma
2.15
that the second term is estimated by

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left|\re\int\{(1-\chi_{R}^{+})(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(1-\chi_{R}^{-})(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx\right|\lesssim_{R}e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus we get

(4.14)

\displaystyle\re\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}hdx=-\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Qhdx+O(e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}).

Now we recall (
4.7
). We have

(4.15)

\displaystyle-2\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Qhdx

\displaystyle=-2\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx

\displaystyle\quad+2\rho\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Qdx+\|\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining (
4.8
), (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we get

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle=2\int\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx-2\rho\re\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q)dx

\displaystyle\quad+2\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx-2\rho\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Qdx

\displaystyle\quad+O(|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}).

where we used
\|\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
.
By (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx+\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx=\int(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q\approx e^{-2y}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle 2\rho\left(\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q)dx+\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Qdx\right)

\displaystyle\approx-\mu(t)+e^{-2y}+O(|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}).

By integration by parts and (
4.9
) again, we get

\displaystyle\int\partial_{x}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\partial_{x}(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q)dx+\re\int\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Qdx

\displaystyle=\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Qdx

\displaystyle=2\int\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p+1}dx+O(e^{-2y})

\displaystyle=2\int(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p+1}dx+O(e^{-2y}),

where we used Lemma
2.15
.
Therefore we get

\displaystyle|\rho|\approx|\mu(t)|+e^{-2y}+O(|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}).

This means that

\displaystyle|\rho|\approx\mu(t)+O(e^{-2y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Next we consider the estimate of
\mu
by using
S(u(t))=2S(Q)
.
The following is a key in our proof.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
y
be a positive number.
For sufficiently large
y>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)-2S(Q)\approx e^{-2y}.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)-2S(Q)

\displaystyle=-\int\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q_{d}x-\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{p+1}\left(\|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}-2\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\right).

By (
1.1
), we get

\displaystyle-\int\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx-\int\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx

\displaystyle=-\int(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx+O(e^{-py})

where we used Lemma
2.15
.
Since
|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p+1}
is even, it holds from Lemma
2.15
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}-2\|Q\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}(|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p+1}-|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{p+1})dx+O(e^{-(p+1)y}).

By the Taylor expansion, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p+1}-|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{p+1})dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(p+1)(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q+p(p+1)(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p-1}(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{2}+O((\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{3})dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}(p+1)(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx+O(e^{-3y}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)-2S(Q)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Qdx+O(e^{-3y})

\displaystyle\approx e^{-2y}+O(e^{-3y}).

For large
y
, we obtain the result.
∎

Since
y(t)>R
, the assumption in the above lemma is satisfied by taking
R
large enough.

Lemma 4.7
.

We have

\displaystyle e^{-2y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim|\rho|^{2}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle 0=S(u(t))-2S(Q)=S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q+g)-S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)+S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)-2S(Q).

By Lemma
4.6
, the second term is estimated by

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)-2S(Q)\gtrsim e^{-2y}.

By Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q+g)-S(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)=\langle S^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q),g\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle S^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)g,g\rangle+o(\|g\|_{H^{1}}^{2}),

where we note that

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}^{2}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q+\mathcal{R}_{y}Q-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,

\displaystyle\langle S^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\phi,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\re\int\nabla\phi\overline{\nabla\psi}+\omega\phi\overline{\psi}dx-\re\int|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}(p\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2})\overline{\psi}dx

for
\phi=\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2},\psi\in H^{1}
.

First we give an estimate of
\langle S^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q),g\rangle
. By (
4.9
), we have

\displaystyle\langle S^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q),g\rangle=\langle(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,g\rangle.

By a nonlinear estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|\lesssim\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p-2}+(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p-2}\}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q.

Thus we get

(4.16)

\displaystyle\int|(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q||g|dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p-2}+(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p-2}\}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q|g|dx

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2y}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

By the Young inequality and
\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim|\rho|^{2}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle|\langle S^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q),g\rangle|\lesssim e^{-2y}\|g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}e^{-4y}+|\rho|^{2}+\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

for arbitrary small
\varepsilon>0
.
Next we consider
\langle S^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)g,g\rangle
.
We set

\displaystyle B(\phi,\psi):=\langle S^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)\phi,\psi\rangle.

Since
B
is bilinear and
g=\rho\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q+h
, we have

\displaystyle B(g,g)=|\rho|^{2}B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q,\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q)+2\rho B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q,h)+B(h,h).

It is obvious that

\displaystyle|\rho|^{2}|B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q,\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q)|\lesssim|\rho|^{2}.

Now we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q,h)=B(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,h)+B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,h).

The second term of (
4.17
) is estimated by

(4.18)

\displaystyle|B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,h)|\lesssim e^{-y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}+R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}

since we have

\displaystyle\int|\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q||h|dx=\int|\chi_{R}^{+}-1|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|h|+|1-\chi_{R}^{-}|\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q|h|dx\lesssim e^{-y}\|h\|_{L^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{x}(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q-\mathcal{R}_{y}Q)||\partial_{x}h|dx

\displaystyle=\int|\chi_{R}^{+}-1||\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q||\partial_{x}h|+|1-\chi_{R}^{-}||\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q||\partial_{x}h|dx+O(R^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-y}\|\partial_{x}h\|_{L^{2}}+O(R^{-1}\|h\|_{L^{2}}).

The first term of (
4.17
) is estimated as follows. By (
4.9
) and the fact that
\mathcal{R}_{y}Q
is real-valued, we get

\displaystyle B(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,h)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}-p|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\}hdx

\displaystyle=p\int_{\mathbb{R}}\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\}hdx

\displaystyle\quad+(1-p)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx.

This first term is estimated by

\displaystyle\left|\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\mathcal{R}_{y}Q\}hdx\right|\lesssim e^{-2y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}

as in (
4.16
). We recall (
4.11
) and thus the second term is estimated by

\displaystyle\left|\int\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}-(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p}\}hdx\right|\lesssim e^{-py}\|h\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle|B(\mathcal{R}_{y}Q,h)|\lesssim e^{-2y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}.

Combining (
4.17
), (
4.18
) and (
4.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\rho||B(\mathcal{G}_{R,y}Q,h)|

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-y}|\rho|\|h\|_{H^{1}}+R^{-1}|\rho|\|h\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|^{2}+e^{-2y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+R^{-2}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

by the Young inequality.
At last, we calculate
B(h,h)
. We divide it into three terms as follows.
We have

\displaystyle B(h,h)=B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}h)+2B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h)+B(\chi_{R}^{c}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h).

We consider the middle term
B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h)
. We have

\displaystyle B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h)

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}h),\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{c}h)\rangle+\langle\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h\rangle

\displaystyle-p\langle|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\chi_{R}h_{1},\chi_{R}^{c}h_{1}\rangle-\langle|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\chi_{R}h_{2},\chi_{R}^{c}h_{2}\rangle.

It holds that

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}h),\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{c}h)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int\chi_{R}\chi_{R}^{c}|\partial_{x}h|^{2}dx+O(R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

and

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h\rangle=\int\chi_{R}\chi_{R}^{c}|h|^{2}dx.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.15
, we have

\displaystyle|p\langle|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\chi_{R}h_{1},\chi_{R}^{c}h_{1}\rangle+\langle|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\chi_{R}h_{2},\chi_{R}^{c}h_{2}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\chi_{R}\chi_{R}^{c}|h|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-(p-1)y}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus, we can estimate the middle term as follows.

\displaystyle B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h)\approx\int\chi_{R}\chi_{R}^{c}(|h|^{2}+|\partial_{x}h|^{2})dx+O(R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+e^{-(p-1)y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

In the similar way, we can estimate the third term
B(\chi_{R}^{c}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h)
as follows.

\displaystyle B(\chi_{R}^{c}h,\chi_{R}^{c}h)\approx\int(\chi_{R}^{c})^{2}(|h|^{2}+|\partial_{x}h|^{2})dx+O(R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+e^{-(p-1)y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

Next we consider the first term
B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}h)
.
By the symmetry, we have

\displaystyle B(\chi_{R}h,\chi_{R}h)

\displaystyle=2\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}|\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}h)|^{2}+|\chi_{R}^{+}h|^{2}dx-\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1}\{p(\chi_{R}^{+}h_{1})^{2}+(\chi_{R}^{+}h_{2})^{2}\}dx\right]

\displaystyle=2\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h))+2\int_{0}^{\infty}(|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{p-1}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1})\{p(\chi_{R}h_{1})^{2}+(\chi_{R}h_{2})^{2}\}dx.

Since it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}(|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{p-1}-|\mathcal{R}_{y}Q|^{p-1})\{p(\chi_{R}h_{1})^{2}+(\chi_{R}h_{2})^{2}\}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\{(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p-2}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q+(\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q)^{p-1}\}|h|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

For
\Phi
, by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathcal{T}_{-y}(\chi_{R}^{+}h))\gtrsim\|\chi_{R}h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Therefore, combining all estimates above, we get

\displaystyle e^{-2y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\lesssim|\rho|^{2}+R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+e^{-2y}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}e^{-4y}+\varepsilon\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

where we used

\displaystyle\|\chi_{R}h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int\chi_{R}\chi_{R}^{c}(|h|^{2}+|\partial_{x}h|^{2})dx+\int(\chi_{R}^{c})^{2}(|h|^{2}+|\partial_{x}h|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\gtrsim\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-R^{-1}\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

This implies

\displaystyle e^{-2y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim|\rho|^{2}

by taking small
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
R
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

We have

\displaystyle e^{-2y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim\mu(t)^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle|\rho|^{2}\lesssim\mu(t)^{2}+e^{-4y}+\|h\|_{H^{1}}^{4}.

This implies the result by taking sufficiently small
\mu_{0}
, which ensures the smallness of
\|h\|_{H^{1}}
.
∎

Corollary 4.9
.

We have

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-2y}\lesssim\mu(t)^{2-\delta}\lesssim\mu_{0}^{1-\delta}\mu(t),

where
\delta>0
is arbitrary small.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
4.8
and
\mu(t)<\mu_{0}
that

\displaystyle(1+y)e^{-2y}\lesssim e^{-(2-\delta)y}\lesssim\mu(t)^{2-\delta}\lesssim\mu_{0}^{1-\delta}\mu(t)

for any small
\delta
.
∎

4.2.
Estimate of the derivatives of the parameters

The derivatives of the parameters are estimated as follows.

Lemma 4.10
.

We have

\displaystyle|\theta^{\prime}(t)|+|\rho^{\prime}(t)|+|y^{\prime}(t)|\lesssim\mu(t).

Proof.

Since we have

\displaystyle h=e^{-i\theta(t)-it}u(t)-(\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q+\rho(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}Q),

we get

\displaystyle h^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-i(\theta^{\prime}(t)+1)e^{-i\theta(t)-it}u(t)+e^{-i\theta(t)-it}u^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\quad-(-y^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}^{+}\partial_{x}Q+\rho^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}Q-\rho(t)y^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}^{+}\partial_{x}Q),

where we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{y}^{+}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{T}_{y}f(x)+\mathcal{T}_{-y}f(x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}^{+}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\chi_{R}^{+}(x)\mathcal{T}_{y}f(x)+\chi_{R}^{-}(x)\mathcal{T}_{-y}f(x).

From this, we have

\displaystyle ih^{\prime}+\partial_{x}^{2}h

\displaystyle=\theta^{\prime}(t)(\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q+g)+h

\displaystyle\quad-|\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q+g|^{p-1}(\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q+g)+|\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-|\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}Q|^{p}+|\mathcal{T}_{y}Q|^{p}-|\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-i(-y^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{R}_{y(t)}^{+}\partial_{x}Q+\rho^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}Q-\rho(t)y^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}^{+}\partial_{x}Q)

\displaystyle\quad-\rho(t)\{(\partial_{x}^{2}\chi_{R}^{+})\mathcal{T}_{y}Q-(\partial_{x}^{2}\chi_{R}^{-})\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q\}

\displaystyle\quad-\rho(t)\{(\partial_{x}\chi_{R}^{+})\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q-(\partial_{x}\chi_{R}^{-})\partial_{x}\mathcal{T}_{-y}Q\}

\displaystyle\quad+\rho(t)\mathcal{G}_{R,y(t)}(Q^{p})

where we use (
1.1
) (or (
4.9
)).

Since we have

\displaystyle\im\int h^{\prime}\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Qdx=0

by the orthogonality condition (
4.6
), multiplying
\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q
into this equation and taking the integral and the real part, we obtain

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(t)=O(\|h\|_{H^{1}}+\|g\|_{H^{1}}+|\rho|+e^{-2y}).

Similarly, since we have

\displaystyle\re\int h^{\prime}\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)^{p}dx=0

by the orthogonality condition (
4.6
), multiplying
\chi_{R}^{+}(\mathcal{T}_{y}Q_{\omega})^{p}
into this equation and taking the integral and the imaginary part, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(t)=O(\|h\|_{H^{1}}+|\theta^{\prime}(t)|\|g\|_{H^{1}}+\|g\|_{H^{1}}+e^{-2y}+e^{-2y}|y^{\prime}(t)|).

In the same way, since we have

by the orthogonality condition (

\partial_{x}(\chi_{R}^{+}\mathcal{T}_{y}Q)

5.
Proof of scattering

5.1.
Compactness of a critical element

Suppose that Proposition
2.13
(1) fails.
Then there exists an odd solution
u
with

\displaystyle E(u)=2E(Q),\ M(u)=2M(Q)\text{ and }K(u(t))>0,

where the solution is global by the variational argument, such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{a}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}=\infty.

We call the solution a critical element. We may consider only the positive time direction by time reversibility. Thus we may suppose that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{a}L^{r}_{x}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}=\infty.

Proposition 5.1

(Compactness of a critical element)
.

Let
u
be an odd solution with

(5.1)

\displaystyle E(u)=2E(Q),\ M(u)=2M(Q)\text{ and }K(u(t))>0,

such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{a}L^{r}_{x}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}=\infty.

Then the solution
u
satisfies the following compactness property:
There exists a function
x:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R=R(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x-x(t)|>R\}\cap\{|x+x(t)|>R\}}|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2}dx<\varepsilon

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
\{\tau_{n}\}
be an arbitrary time sequence such that
\tau_{n}\to\infty
. The sequence
\{u(\tau_{n})\}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by (
5.1
) and Proposition
2.8
. By the linear profile decomposition (Lemma
2.21
), we have, up to subsequence,

\displaystyle u(\tau_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{J}e^{-it_{n}^{j}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{x_{n}^{j}}\psi^{j}+R_{n}^{J}

and the properties in the statement hold. We set
\psi_{n}^{j}:=e^{-it_{n}^{j}\partial_{x}^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{x_{n}^{j}}\psi^{j}
for simplicity.

It is easy to show that
J=0
does not occur. Indeed, by the linear profile decomposition, if
J=0
, then
\|e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(\tau_{n})\|_{L_{t}^{a}L_{x}^{r}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
(see Remark
2.2
). Then, by the long time perturbation (Lemma
2.20
), we get
\|u\|_{L_{t}^{a}L_{x}^{r}((\tau_{n},\infty)\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 1
for large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This contradicts that
u
does not scatter.

Thus we have
J\geq 1
. We will prove that
J=1
by contradiction. Suppose that
J\geq 2
and thus we have at least two non-zero functions
\psi^{1}
and
\psi^{2}
.
First, by the linear profile decomposition, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}M(\psi_{n}^{j})+M(R_{n}^{J})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}M(u(\tau_{n}))=M(u_{0})=2M(Q),

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|\partial_{x}\psi_{n}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}R_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u(\tau_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It holds that, for any
j
and for large
n
,

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}\psi_{n}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}<2^{p-1}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\|R_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}R_{n}^{J}\|_{L^{2}}<2^{p-1}\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{\sigma}\|\partial_{x}Q\|_{L^{2}}.

From these and Lemma
2.11
, we get
K(\psi_{n}^{j}),K(R_{n}^{J})>0
. It follows from
E\geq\frac{p-1}{2}K
that
E(\psi_{n}^{j}),E(R_{n}^{J})\geq 0
.
Now, since we also have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}E(\psi_{n}^{j})+E(R_{n}^{J})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}E(u(\tau_{n}))=E(u_{0})=2E(Q),

we obtain
E(\psi_{n}^{j}),E(R_{n}^{J})\leq 2E(Q)
.

Since
J\geq 2
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

for all
j
.
By reordering, we may choose

such that

(Case 1)

\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq J_{1}\Rightarrow t_{n}^{j}=0\ (\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\text{ and }x_{n}^{j}=0\ (\forall n\in\mathbb{N}),

(Case 2)

\displaystyle J_{1}+1\leq j\leq J_{2}\Rightarrow t_{n}^{j}=0\ (\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\text{ and }x_{n}^{j}\to\infty\ (n\to\infty),

(Case 3)

\displaystyle J_{2}+1\leq j\leq J_{3}\Rightarrow|t_{n}^{j}|\to\infty\ (n\to\infty)\text{ and }x_{n}^{j}=0\ (\forall n\in\mathbb{N}),

where we are assuming that there is no

such that

if

. We will define nonlinear profiles associated with
\psi_{n}^{j}
. If there is no

such that

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\left|\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\varphi(x)\partial_{x}f(x)\overline{f(x)}dx\right|\lesssim\mu(f)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\varphi(x)|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}dx.

Proof.

Let

By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg ineqaulity (Lemma
2.10

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}=\|e^{i\lambda\varphi}f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}\leq C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}(\|(e^{i\lambda\varphi}f)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{4}}.

\displaystyle\left|\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{\prime}f^{\prime}\overline{f}dx\right|^{2}\leq\|\varphi^{\prime}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\{\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-1}}\right\}.

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}}{C_{GN}^{{\mathord{\rm odd}}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{p+3}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{4}{p-1}}

Lemma A.1
.

Let

. Then

also belongs to

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|F^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty

The weak derivative of

is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}F^{+}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)\frac{df}{dx}(x)

Indeed, for any
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}F^{+}(x)\frac{d}{dx}\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{d}{dx}\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dx}f(x)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}\frac{d}{dx}f(x)\varphi(x)dx

by the integration by parts and
f(0)=0
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\frac{d}{dx}F^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|\frac{d}{dx}f\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\leq\|\frac{d}{dx}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

Thus, we get

.

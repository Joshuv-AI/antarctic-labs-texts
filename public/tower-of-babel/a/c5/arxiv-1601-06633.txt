Grothendieck-Witt groups of quadrics
Heng Xie

Abstract
. Let k k be a commutative ring containing 1 2 \frac{1}{2} . In this paper, we construct certain homotopy fibration sequences for Grothendieck-Witt spectra of smooth quadric hypersurfaces over k k . As an application, we compute Witt groups of smooth quadric hypersurfaces over certain kinds of bases.



1.
Introduction

Let
(P,q)
be a non-degenerate quadratic form over a commutative ring
k
of rank
n=d+2
. Let
Q_{d}\subset\mathbb{P}^{d+1}_{k}
be the quadric hypersurface
\textnormal{Proj }S(P^{*})/(q)
where
S(P^{*})
is symmetric algebra of
P^{*}
and
q
is considered as an element in
S_{2}(P^{*})
. We denote the connective
K
-theory spectrum and the Grothendieck-Witt theory spectrum by
K
and
GW
respectively; see Section
2
for a review and
[
26
]
for details. In this paper, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a commutative ring with
\frac{1}{2}\in k
. If
d=2m+1
, then there is a stable equivalence of spectra

GW^{[i]}(Q_{d})\approx\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}K(k)\oplus GW^{[i]}(\mathscr{A}).

If
d=2m
, then there is a homotopy fibration sequence of spectra

\textstyle{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m-1}K(k)\oplus GW^{[i]}(\mathscr{A})\rightarrow GW^{[i]}(Q_{d})\rightarrow GW^{[i-d]}(k,\det(P))}

where
\det(P)=\Lambda^{n}P
is the determinant of the projective module
P
. In both cases, the spectrum
GW^{[i]}(\mathscr{A})
fits into a homotopy fibration sequence

\textstyle{GW^{[i]}(k)\longrightarrow GW^{[i]}(\mathscr{A})\longrightarrow GW^{[i+1]}(C_{0}(q)_{\sigma})}

where
C_{0}(q)
is the even part of the Clifford algebra of
q
and
\sigma
is the canonical involution of
C_{0}(q)
. Moreover, this result also holds for
\mathbb{G}W
-spectra.

Algebraic K-theory of quadric hypersurfaces was computed by Swan in the 1980s, cf.
[
28
]
. Swan proved that
K(Q_{d})\approx K(C_{0}(q))\oplus K(k)^{d}
for any commutative ring
k
. Theorem
1.1
above may be viewed as the Grothendieck-Witt analogue to Swan’s result. Grothendieck-Witt theory (aka. Hermitian
K
-theory) as a refinement of algebraic
K
-theory was first studied by Bass, Karoubi and others in terms of algebras with involution, cf.
[
6
]
. The refinement stems from the data of duality. Loosely speaking, the data of duality allow us to study the category of non-degenerate quadratic forms which refines the category of finitely generated projective modules. Recently, Schlichting generalized Grothendieck-Witt theory of algebras with involution to that of exact categories and dg categories with duality, the results of which make Grothendieck-Witt theory bear a heavier burden, cf.
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
26
]
.

Grothendieck-Witt theory performs in Algebraic Geometry like the role of
KO
-theory in Topology (topological
K
-theory of real vector bundles), while algebraic
K
-theory is the algebraic analogue of
KU
-theory (topological
K
-theory of complex vector bundles). In topology,
KO
-theory usually refines
KU
-theory. Moreover, this refinement even helps to give the optimal solution. For example, the main cohomology theory involved in Adams’ solution to vector fields on spheres problem is just
KO
-theory, cf.
[
1
]
.

2.
Review: Grothendieck-Witt theory

Let
k
be a commutative ring containing
\frac{1}{2}
. Let
C(k)
denote the symmetric monoidal category of cochain complexes of
k
-modules. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a small dg category (that is a small category enriched in
C(k)
). The category
Z^{0}\mathcal{A}
of zero cycles (resp.
H^{0}\mathcal{A}
of zero cohomology) is called the underlying category (resp. the homotopy category) of the dg category
\mathcal{A}
. A morphism in the dg category
\mathcal{A}
means a morphism in
Z^{0}\mathcal{A}
. Every dg category
\mathcal{A}
has a pretriangulated-hull
\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}
(which is pretriangulated) such that
\mathcal{A}
is a full dg subcategory of
\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}
,
Z^{0}\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}
is Frobenius exact and
H^{0}\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}
is triangulated, cf. Section 1
[
26
]
. Moreover,
(-)^{\textnormal{ptr}}
is functorial.

A
dg category with duality
is a triplet
(\mathcal{A},*,\textnormal{can})
consisting of a dg category
\mathcal{A}
, a dg functor
*:\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
and a natural transformation
\textnormal{can}:1\rightarrow*\circ*^{\textnormal{op}}
such that
\textnormal{can}_{A}^{*}\circ\textnormal{can}_{A^{*}}=1_{A^{*}}
. A
dg form functor

(\mathcal{A},*_{\mathcal{A}},\textnormal{can}_{\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{B},*_{\mathcal{B}},can_{\mathcal{B}})
is a pair
(F,\varphi)
consisting of a dg functor
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
and a natural transformation
\varphi:F\circ*_{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow*_{\mathcal{B}}\circ F^{\textnormal{op}}
(called
duality compatibility morphism
) such that the diagram

\textstyle{FA\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F(\textnormal{can}_{A})}
\scriptstyle{\textnormal{can}_{FA}}
\textstyle{F(A^{*_{\mathcal{A}}*_{\mathcal{A}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{A^{*}}}
\textstyle{(FA)^{*_{\mathcal{B}}*_{\mathcal{B}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{A}^{*}}
\textstyle{F(A^{*_{\mathcal{A}}})^{*_{\mathcal{B}}}}

commutes. A
dg category with weak equivalences
is a pair
(\mathcal{A},w)
consisting of the following data: a full dg subcategory
\mathcal{A}^{w}\subset\mathcal{A}
and a set of morphisms
w
in
\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}
such that
f\in w
if and only if
f
is an isomorphism in the quotient triangulated category
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A},w):=H^{0}\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}/H^{0}(\mathcal{A}^{w})^{\textnormal{ptr}}
. An
exact dg functor

F:(\mathcal{A},w)\rightarrow(\mathcal{C},v)
of dg categories with weak equivalences consists of a dg functor
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
preserving weak equivalences, i.e.
F(\mathcal{A}^{w})\subset\mathcal{C}^{v}
. A quadruple
\mathscr{A}=(\mathcal{A},w,*,\textnormal{can})
is called a
dg category with weak equivalences and duality
if
(\mathcal{A},w)
is a dg category with weak equivalences, if
(\mathcal{A},*,\textnormal{can})
is a dg category with duality, if
\mathcal{A}^{w}
is invariant under the duality and if
\textnormal{can}_{A}\in w
for all objects
A
in
\mathcal{A}
. An
exact dg form functor

(F,\varphi):(\mathcal{A},w,*,\textnormal{can})\rightarrow(\mathcal{C},v,\#,\textnormal{can})
consists of a dg form functor
(F,\varphi):(\mathcal{A},*,\textnormal{can})\rightarrow(\mathcal{C},\#,\textnormal{can})
such that the dg functor
F:(\mathcal{A},w)\rightarrow(\mathcal{C},v)
is exact. For every dg category
\mathscr{A}
with weak equivalences and duality, there is an associated triangulated category
\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A}=(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A},w),*,\textnormal{can},\lambda)
with duality, cf. Section 3
[
26
]
. Any exact dg form functor
\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
gives a duality preserving functor
\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\mathscr{C}
of triangulated categories with duality. Any dg category
\mathscr{A}
with weak equivalences and duality gives a pretriangulated dg category
\mathscr{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}
with weak equivalences and duality.

Let
\mathscr{A}=(\mathcal{A},w,*,\textnormal{can})
be a dg category with weak equivalences and duality. Its
Grothendieck-Witt group

GW(\mathscr{A})
(resp.
Witt group

W(\mathscr{A})
) is defined to be the Grothendieck-Witt group
GW(Z^{0}\mathscr{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}})
(resp. the Witt group
W(Z^{0}\mathscr{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}})
) where
Z^{0}\mathscr{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}}:=(Z^{0}\mathcal{A}^{\textnormal{ptr}},w,*,\textnormal{can})
is the corresponding exact categories with weak equivalences and duality, cf. Section 1
[
26
]
. The
n
-th shifted version
W^{[n]}(\mathscr{A})
and
GW^{[n]}_{0}(\mathscr{A})
are also defined in
loc. cit.
. Moreover,
GW^{[n]}_{0}(\mathscr{A})
(resp.
W^{[n]}(\mathscr{A})
) is isomorphic to
GW^{n}(\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A})
(resp.
W^{n}(\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A})
) which is just isomorphic to Walter’s Grothendieck-Witt group (resp. Balmer’s Witt group) of a triangulated category with duality, cf. Section 3
[
26
]
. Given a dg category
\mathscr{A}
with weak equivalences and duality, one can define the
n
-th
Grothendieck-Witt spectrum

GW^{[n]}(\mathscr{A})
(cf. Section 5
[
26
]
) and the
n
-th
Karoubi-Grothendieck-Witt spectrum

\mathbb{G}W^{[n]}(\mathscr{A})
(cf. Section 8
[
26
]
). Note that
GW^{[n]}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathbb{G}W^{[n]}(\mathscr{A})
are actually objects in the (large) category
Sp
of spectra. For the following theorems, we consider them as objects in the stable homotopy category
\mathcal{SH}
of spectra. By a stable equivalence of spectra, we mean an isomorphism in
\mathcal{SH}
. Actually, objects in
Sp
and
\mathcal{SH}
are the same, but morphisms are different. One may find details in Appendix B2
[
26
]
.

A sequence
\mathcal{T}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{3}
of triangulated categories is called
exact

\mathcal{T}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{2}

\mathcal{T}_{2}/\mathcal{T}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{3}

is the Verdier quotient. A sequence
\mathscr{A}_{1}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{2}\rightarrow\mathscr{A}_{3}
of dg categories with weak equivalences and duality is

\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}\mathscr{A}_{3}

[

Theorem 2.4

(Localization for
GW
)
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\in\mathscr{A}_{i}

Proof.

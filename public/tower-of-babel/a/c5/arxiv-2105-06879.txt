Prospects for direct CP tests of h ​ q ​ q hqq interactions
Rodrigo Alonso, Cristofero Fraser-Taliente, Chris Hays, Michael Spannowsky

Abstract
We study the prospects for probing the CP structure of h ​ q ​ q hqq interactions using the decays of the lightest baryon Λ q \Lambda_{q} formed in the quark’s hadronization. The low yields of reconstructible events make it unlikely for tests to be performed with the next generation of colliders. In h → b ​ b ¯ → Λ b ​ Λ ¯ b h\to b\bar{b}\to\Lambda_{b}\bar{\Lambda}_{b} decays a CP-sensitive distribution could be measured with a high-luminosity e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider, while in both h → b ​ b ¯ → Λ b ​ Λ ¯ b h\to b\bar{b}\to\Lambda_{b}\bar{\Lambda}_{b} and h → c ​ c ¯ → Λ c ​ Λ ¯ c h\to c\bar{c}\to\Lambda_{c}\bar{\Lambda}_{c} decays such a distribution could be measured with a very high luminosity μ + ​ μ − \mu^{+}\mu^{-} collider. However, we find that only the μ + ​ μ − \mu^{+}\mu^{-} collider can produce enough h → b ​ b ¯ → Λ b ​ Λ ¯ b h\to b\bar{b}\to\Lambda_{b}\bar{\Lambda}_{b} decays to probe a physical CP asymmetry in the h ​ b ​ b hbb vertex.

4
Conclusion

In this work we have explored the feasibility of direct CP tests in Higgs-boson decays to heavy quarks in
future collider experiments. The effect of a CP transformation is encoded at the fundamental level in relative
complex phases for different helicity amplitudes, which we have converted into observables in angular
distributions of the final decay particles. We have found that massive helicity amplitude methods are
particularly well suited for tracing the spin correlations throughout the derivation of results.

A test of CP violation in these decays is challenging due to:
i)
the loss of measureable spin correlations
during hadronization in all but the
\Lambda_{q}
channel, which has a
<10\%
fragmentation fraction; and

ii)
the low
\Lambda_{q}
branching ratio to experimentally reconstructible final states. The Higgs boson
yields therefore need to be very high to approach sensitivity,
{\cal{O}}(10^{9})
events, beyond the reach of

Appendix B
Loss of spin correlation

As pointed out in Sec.
2.4
, the CP-violation effect encoded in spin correlations vanishes if the combined
fragmentation and decay yields a diagonal structure in spin indices.
The simplest example of the loss of spin information is hadronization to a pseudoscalar meson. In this case
eq. (
11
) leads to

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{A}}_{q\to P_{q}\to X}{\mathcal{A}}_{q\to P_{q}\to X}^{\dagger}\right)^{I}_{J}

\displaystyle=C\left(\langle q^{I}S_{\ell}^{K}\rangle+[S_{\ell}^{K}q^{I}]\right)\left([S_{\ell,K}q_{J}]+\langle q_{J}S_{\ell,K}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=2([q_{J}q^{I}]+\langle q^{I}q_{J}\rangle)=4Cm_{q}\delta^{I}_{J}

(71)

given that the spinor
S_{\ell}
is built with the momentum of
q
.

For the case of a vector meson decaying to a meson and a photon, as in the
B^{*}
case, one has

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{A}}_{q\to P_{q}^{*}\to P_{q}\gamma^{+}}{\mathcal{A}}_{q\to P_{q}^{*}\to P_{q}\gamma^{+}}^{\dagger}\right)^{I}_{J}

\displaystyle=C[S_{\ell}^{L}\gamma][q^{I}\gamma]\langle\gamma S_{\ell,L}\rangle\langle\gamma q_{J}\rangle=C\langle\gamma q_{J}\rangle[q^{I}\gamma]\left(-2p_{\gamma}\cdot p_{P_{q}^{*}}\right),

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{A}}_{q\to P_{q}^{*}\to P_{q}\gamma^{-}}{\mathcal{A}}_{q\to P_{q}^{*}\to P_{q}\gamma^{-}}^{\dagger}\right)^{I}_{J}

\displaystyle=C\langle S_{\ell}^{L}\gamma\rangle\langle q^{I}\gamma\rangle[\gamma S_{\ell,L}][\gamma q_{J}]=C\langle\gamma q^{I}\rangle[q_{J}\gamma]\left(2p_{\gamma}\cdot p_{P_{q}^{*}}\right).

(72)

Each term preserves q-quark polarization, but if the experiment is insensitive to photon helicity the sum gives

\displaystyle\langle q^{I}p_{\gamma}q_{J}]-\langle q_{J}p_{\gamma}q^{I}]

\displaystyle=\mbox{Tr}\left(p_{\gamma}\varepsilon_{JJ^{\prime}}\left(\left|q^{K}\right]\langle q^{I}|-\left|q^{I}\right]\langle q^{K}|)\right)\right)=\mbox{Tr}(p_{\gamma}\left|q_{K}\right\rangle\langle q^{K}|)\delta^{I}_{J}

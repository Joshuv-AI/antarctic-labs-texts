Numerical scheme for Erdélyi–Kober fractional diffusion equation using Galerkin–Hermite method
Łukasz Płociniczak, Mateusz Świtała

Abstract
The aim of this work is to devise and analyse an accurate numerical scheme to solve Erdélyi–Kober fractional diffusion equation. This solution can be thought as the marginal pdf of the stochastic process called the generalized grey Brownian motion (ggBm). The ggBm includes some well-known stochastic processes: Brownian motion, fractional Brownian motion and grey Brownian motion. To obtain convergent numerical scheme we transform the fractional diffusion equation into its weak form and apply the discretization of the Erdélyi–Kober fractional derivative. We prove the stability of the solution of the semi-discrete problem and its convergence to the exact solution. Due to the singular in time term appearing in the main equation the proposed method converges slower than first order. Finally, we provide the numerical analysis of the full-discrete problem using orthogonal expansion in terms of Hermite functions. Keywords : anomalous diffusion, Erdélyi–Kober derivative, Galerkin–Hermite method MSC2020 Classification : 35K15, 65M60, 35R11

2
Discretization of the Erdélyi–Kober differential operator

Following
[
10
,
11
]
and
[
9
]
let us define the Erdélyi–Kober fractional integral operator
I_{\eta}^{\gamma,\mu}

I_{\eta}^{\gamma,\mu}\phi(t)\coloneqq\frac{\eta}{\Gamma(\mu)}t^{-\eta(\mu+\gamma)}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\tau^{\eta(\gamma+1)-1}(t^{\eta}-\tau^{\eta})^{\mu-1}\phi(\tau)\,d\tau,\quad t\in[0,T],

(7)

where
\mu>0,\ \eta>0,\ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi\in C_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{f(x)=x^{p}\hat{f}(x),\ p>\lambda,\ \hat{f}\in C[0,\infty)\}
and
\lambda>-\beta(\gamma+1)
.
When
\eta=1
the operator
I_{\eta}^{\gamma,\mu}
becomes the fractional integral operator originally introduced in
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
. Note that if we change the variable according to
x=\tau/t
we get the equivalent form of the Erdélyi–Kober fractional integral which is particularly useful for numerical calculations

I_{\eta}^{\gamma,\mu}\phi(t)=\frac{\eta}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}x^{\eta(\gamma+1)-1}(1-x^{\eta})^{\mu-1}\phi(xt)\,dx,\quad t\in[0,T].

(8)

Moreover, using the above integral operator, we define the Erdélyi–Kober fractional differential operator
[
10
,
9
]
,

D_{\eta}^{\gamma,\mu}\phi(t)\coloneqq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\biggl(\gamma+j+\frac{1}{\eta}t\frac{d}{dt}\biggr)(I_{\eta}^{\gamma+\mu,n-\mu}\phi(t)),\quad t\in[0,T],

(9)

where
n-1<\mu<n,\ n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi\in C_{\lambda}^{n}\coloneqq\{f(x)=x^{p}\hat{f}(x),\ p>\lambda,\ \hat{f}\in C^{n}[0,\infty)\}
, and the parameters
\eta,\ \mu,\ \gamma,\ \lambda
satisfy the same condition as before.

Next, we fix
t=t_{n}=nk
, where
k
is a some small positive constant, and consider definition (
8
) with
\eta=\alpha/\beta,\ \gamma=0
and
\mu=\beta
. To provide a discretization of
I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}
we divide the interval
[0,1]
into
n
equally spaced subintervals, and approximate the value of the integral on each subinterval by the rectangule rule obtaining

L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})\coloneqq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\phi\biggl(\frac{i}{n}t_{n}\biggr)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\phi(t_{i}),

(10)

where

c_{n,i}=\frac{1}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\biggl(\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i-1}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}\biggr).

(11)

Furthermore, let us notice that putting
\eta=\alpha/\beta,\ \gamma=\beta-1
and
\mu=1-\beta
into (
9
) we have

D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\phi(t_{n})=\biggl(\beta+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}\biggr)I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n}).

(12)

Using the discretization operator
L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}
and the finite difference scheme for the first-order derivative, we obtain the discrete Erdélyi–Kober fractional differential operator

G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\phi(t_{n})\coloneqq\beta L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}t_{n}\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t)=\beta L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}t_{n}\frac{1}{k}(L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})-L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n-1})).

(13)

However, the above notion of the discretization of Erdélyi–Kober fractional differential operator is not the only one. Let us notice that the derivative part of the operator
D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
can be rewritten in the following way

\begin{split}t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})&=\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\bigl(1-\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\bigr)^{\beta-1}\phi(\tau t_{n})\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\bigl(1-\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\bigr)^{\beta-1}t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}\phi(\tau t_{n})\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int\limits_{\frac{i-1}{n}}^{\frac{i}{n}}\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\bigl(1-\tau^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\bigr)^{\beta-1}\tau\frac{d}{d\tau}\phi(\tau t_{n})\,d\tau.\end{split}

(14)

Hence, on each subinterval we approximate the derivative with respect to
\tau
by a finite difference scheme and then the alternative discretization of the operator
D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
is

\begin{split}K_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\phi(t_{n})\coloneqq&\beta L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{n,i}n(\phi(t_{i})-\phi(t_{i-1})\\
=&\beta L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}(d_{n,i-1}-d_{n,i})n\phi(t_{i-1})+nd_{n,n}\phi(t_{n})-nd_{n,1}\phi(0),\end{split}

(15)

with

\begin{split}d_{n,i}=&\frac{\beta}{n(\alpha+\beta)}\biggl(n\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+1}\,_{2}F_{1}\biggl(1-\beta,1+\frac{\beta}{\alpha};2+\frac{\beta}{\alpha};\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)\\
\ &-n\biggl(\frac{i-1}{n}\biggr)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+1}\,_{2}F_{1}\biggl(1-\beta,1+\frac{\beta}{\alpha};2+\frac{\beta}{\alpha};\biggl(\frac{i-1}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)\biggr),\end{split}

(16)

where
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
is the Gauss hypergeometric function

\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!},

(17)

with Pochhammer symbol

(a)_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{for }k=0,\\
a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)&\textrm{for }k>0.\end{array}\right.

(18)

The evaluation of the Gauss hypergeometric function may be expensive in practice, hence in further numerical analysis we would rather use
G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
than
K_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
.

The following theorem provides the estimates on the order of discretization errors of the approximation operators
L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta},\ K_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}

G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
as the number of the subintervals of
[0,1]
goes to infinity.

Theorem 1
.

Fix
0<\alpha,\beta<1
and assume that
\phi\in C^{1}([0,T])
and
\psi\in C^{2}([0,T])
. Then, for a fixed
t_{n}\in(0,T)
, where
t_{n}=nk
, the discretization errors corresponding to the operator
L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta},\ K_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}

G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
can be estimated as below.

•

Integral operator

I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})-L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\phi(t_{n})=\frac{t_{n}}{n}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\sigma t_{n})}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}.

(19)

•

Differential operator I

\begin{split}|D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\psi(t_{n})-G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\psi(t_{n})|\leq&\frac{1}{n}\biggl[\beta t_{n}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(\sigma t_{n})|}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}+\frac{t_{n}\beta\Gamma\biggl(\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}+1\biggr)}{\alpha\Gamma\biggl(\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}+\beta+1\biggr)}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(t^{*})|\\
\ &+t_{n}C_{1}|\psi^{\prime}(t^{**})|+\frac{t_{n}^{2}C_{2}}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(t^{***})|\biggr].\end{split}

(20)

•

Differential Operator II

|D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\psi(t_{n})-K_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\psi(t_{n})|\leq\frac{1}{n}\biggl(\frac{t_{n}|\psi^{\prime}(\sigma t_{n})|}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}+\frac{\Gamma\biggl(1+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\biggr)}{\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+\beta+1\biggr)}t_{n}^{2}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(\sigma^{*}t_{n})|\biggr).

(21)

Here,
\sigma,\,\sigma^{*}\in(0,1),\ t^{*},\,t^{**},\,t^{***}\in(0,t_{n})
and
C_{1},\ C_{2}
are some positive constants independent on
n
.

Proof.

The asymptotic relation for Erdélyi–Kober fractional integral operator
I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}
was delivered in
[
37
]
, therefore, we will not provide a detailed proof of it here.

Since we know the order of discretization of Erdélyi–Kober fractional integral operator, we proceed to find the order of discrete representations of the operator
D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
. In the beginning, let us consider the operator
G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}
. Note that Erdélyi–Kober fractional differential operator can be rewritten as

\begin{split}D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}\psi(t_{n})=&\beta I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})=\beta L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})+\beta R_{n}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})\\
=&\beta L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})+\beta R_{n}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}t_{n}\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\widehat{R_{n}},\end{split}

(22)

where
R_{n}
is a discretization error of the Erdélyi–Kober fractional integral operator (
46
) and
\widehat{R_{n}}=t_{n}(\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})+\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n}))
. First difference in
\widehat{R_{n}}
can be estimated in a standard way

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})=&\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-\frac{1}{k}(I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{n}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t^{*})\\
\ =&\frac{\Gamma\biggl(\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}+1\biggr)}{\Gamma\biggl(\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}+\beta+1\biggr)}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\sigma^{*}t^{*})\frac{1}{n},\end{split}

(23)

where
\sigma^{*}\in(0,1)
and
t^{*}\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
. However, to estimate the order of the second term in
\widehat{R_{n}}
we need to investigate this term carefully. Note that using again the Mean Value Theorem for Integrals and Sums, and the relation
c_{n,i}<c_{n-1,i}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
we have

\begin{split}t_{n}\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-t_{n}\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})=&\frac{t_{n}}{k}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{i-1}^{i}(f(n,s)-f(n-1,s))(\psi(ik)-\psi(sk))\,ds\\
\ &+\frac{t_{n}}{k}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{n-1}^{n}f(n,s)(\psi(nk)-\psi(sk))\,ds\\
=&t_{n}\psi^{\prime}(xk)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{i-1}^{i}(f(n,s)-f(n-1,s))(i-s)\,ds\\
\ &+t_{n}\psi^{\prime}(x_{n}k)\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{n-1}^{n}f(n,s)(n-s)\,ds,\end{split}

(24)

where
i-1\leq s<\sigma_{i}<i
,
0<x<n-1,\ n-1<x_{n}<n
and

f(n,s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\biggl(\frac{s}{n}\biggr)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{s}{n}\biggr)^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}\biggr)^{\beta-1}.

(25)

Furthermore, performing a straightforward calculation we get

C_{n}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{i-1}^{i}f(n,s)(i-s)\,ds\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{i-1}^{i}f(n,s)\,ds=\frac{1}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}.

(26)

Moreover, note that
C_{n}
can also be written in the following way

C_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{i}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\frac{n\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha}\biggr)}{\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+\beta+1\biggr)}.

(27)

Next, we use the Intermediate Value Theorem to get

\begin{split}\psi^{\prime}(xk)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{i-1}^{i}f(n,s)(i-s)\,ds+\psi^{\prime}(x_{n}k)\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\int\limits_{n-1}^{n}f(n,s)(n-s)\,ds=\psi^{\prime}(x^{*}k)C_{n},\end{split}

(28)

where
x^{*}\in(x,x_{n})
.
Finally, we have

\begin{split}t_{n}\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})-t_{n}\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}\psi(t_{n})&=t_{n}(\psi^{\prime}(x^{*}k)C_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(xk)C_{n-1})\\
\ &=t_{n}\psi^{\prime}(x^{*}k)(C_{n}-C_{n-1})+t_{n}C_{n}(\psi^{\prime}(x^{*}k)-\psi^{\prime}(xk))\\
\ &=t_{n}\psi^{\prime}(x^{*}k)(C_{n}-C_{n-1})+t_{n}C_{n}(x^{*}-x)k\psi^{\prime\prime}(x^{**}k),\end{split}

(29)

where
x^{**}\in(x,x^{*})
. Let us notice that the difference
|x-x^{*}|
does not increase when
n
becomes larger:
\psi
is continuously differentiable on the interval
(0,t_{n}]
and due to the fact that for increasing
n
the contribution of the integral in second term in (
28
) to
C_{n}
becomes negligible. Next, we use (
27
) to obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}{\alpha}(C_{n}-C_{n-1})=&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\alpha}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n-1}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\frac{\beta\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma\biggl(1+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\biggr)}{\alpha\Gamma\biggl(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+\beta+1\biggr)}\\
=&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\biggl[\frac{1}{\alpha}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n-1}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds\biggr]\\
=&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\biggl[\frac{1}{\alpha}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\biggl(1-\biggl(\frac{i}{n-1}\biggr)^{\alpha/\beta}\biggr)^{\beta}-\frac{i}{n}\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds\biggr]\\
\ &+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\biggl[\frac{i}{n}\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds-\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds\biggr]=S_{1}+S_{2}.\end{split}

(30)

Note, that the absolute value of the second term can be easily bounded from above as follows

\begin{split}|S_{2}|=&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\biggl|\frac{i}{n}\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds-\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds\biggr|\\
=&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}(s-\frac{i}{n})\,ds\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\int\limits_{\frac{i}{n}}^{\frac{i+1}{n}}s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}-1}(1-s^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}})^{\beta-1}\,ds=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{1}{n}.\end{split}

(31)

Considering now
S_{1}
, we evaluate the integral for each

Lemma 1

Proof.

\displaystyle\|U^{n-1}\|^{2}-\alpha kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}-t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}+t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{n-1,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}-\frac{\alpha kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\|U_{x}^{n}\|^{2},

(73)

\displaystyle\|U^{n}\|^{2}+\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n}\biggr)t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|U^{n-1}\|^{2}+t_{n-1}^{\alpha}\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n-1}\biggr)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{n-1,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}

(74)

\displaystyle+\biggl(t_{n}^{\alpha}-t_{n-1}^{\alpha}\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n-1}\biggr)\biggr)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{n-1,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}.

Let us define

E^{n}\coloneqq\|U^{n}\|^{2}+\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n}\biggr)t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}.

(75)

Then, using the notation
E^{n}
we rewrite the last inequality (
74
) in following manner

E^{n}\leq E^{n-1}+\biggl(t_{n}^{\alpha}-t_{n-1}^{\alpha}\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n-1}\biggr)\biggr)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{n-1,i}\|U_{x}^{i}\|^{2}\leq E^{n-1}.

(76)

Finally, we have

E^{n}\leq E^{1}=\|U^{1}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\beta+1)}t_{1}^{\alpha}\|U_{x}^{1}\|^{2}.

(77)

Now, let us consider inequality (
70
) with
n=1
separately

\begin{split}\|U^{1}\|^{2}\leq&\|U^{0}\|^{2}-\frac{2\alpha kt_{1}^{\alpha-1}}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\|U_{x}^{1}\|^{2}-\frac{2t_{1}^{\alpha}}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}\|U^{1}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(78)

From above inequality we immediately get
\|U^{1}\|^{2}\leq\|U^{0}\|^{2}
and
t_{1}^{\alpha}\|U_{x}^{1}\|^{2}/\Gamma(\beta+1)\leq\|U^{0}\|^{2}/2
. Hence, using these inequalities we get

E^{n}\leq\biggl(1+\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\biggr)\|U_{0}\|^{2}\leq 2\|U_{0}\|^{2}.

(79)

From the above inequalities and (
75
) we conclude (
67
).
∎

We are ready to prove the theorem concerning convergence of the error for semi-discrete equation (
57
) when the time step
k
approaches zero.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that the problem (
5
) has a unique solution
u(t,x)
for
0<\alpha,\beta<1
, with
u_{0}(x)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, such that

\displaystyle\|u\|+t^{1-\alpha}\|u_{t}\|+t^{2-\alpha}\|u_{tt}\|\leq

\displaystyle C,

(80)

\displaystyle\|u_{x}\|+t^{1-\alpha}\|u_{tx}\|+t^{2-\alpha}\|u_{ttx}\|\leq

\displaystyle C.

(81)

Then, there exist positive constant
D
such that

\|U^{n}-u(t_{n})\|\leq D\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}k^{\alpha-\mu/2}&\text{for}\quad\alpha\neq\beta,\\
k^{\alpha}((-\log k)^{1/2}+k^{\mu/2})&\text{for}\quad\alpha=\beta,\end{array}\right.

(82)

where
\mu>0
is arbitrary small.

Proof.

We substitute
e^{n}=U^{n}-u(t_{n})
into (
57
) to get

(\overline{\partial}e^{n},\chi)=-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}e_{x}^{n},\chi_{x})+(\kappa_{1},\chi)+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},\chi_{x}),

(83)

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\overline{\partial}u(t_{n})-u_{t}(t_{n}),

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}

\displaystyle=D_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}u_{x}(t_{n}),

and we make use a fact that
u
satisfies eq. (
5
).
For the clarity of the main result we assume that for
n=0
we have
e^{0}=U^{0}-u(0)=0
. Let us now choose
\chi=e^{n}
and obtain

\frac{1}{2}\overline{\partial}\|e^{n}\|^{2}\leq-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(G_{\alpha/\beta}^{\beta-1,1-\beta}e_{x}^{n},e_{x}^{n})+(\kappa_{1},e^{n})+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{n}).

(84)

Next, we follow the same steps as before, in the stability analysis, to get

\begin{split}\|e^{n}\|^{2}\leq&\|e^{n-1}\|^{2}+k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}\biggl(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}\biggl(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{t_{n}}{k}(c_{n-1,i}-c_{n,i})-\beta c_{n,i}\biggr)(\|e_{x}^{i}\|^{2}+\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2})\biggr)\\
\ &-2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\beta c_{n,n}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{t_{n}}{k}c_{n,n})\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}+2k(\kappa_{1},e^{n})+2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{n}),\\
\|e^{n}\|^{2}+&t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|e_{x}^{i}\|^{2}\leq\|e^{n-1}\|^{2}+t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{n-1,i}\|e_{x}^{i}\|^{2}-k\alpha t_{n}^{\alpha-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|e_{x}^{i}\|^{2}\\
\ &-k\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}+2k(\kappa_{1},e^{n})+2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{n}).\end{split}

(85)

Now, with a special care, let us investigate the components with
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
. It is easy to see that

\|\kappa_{1}\|\leq\|\overline{\partial}u(t_{n})-u_{t}(t_{n})\|\leq\int\limits_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\|u_{tt}(t)\|\,dt,

(86)

and thus

\begin{split}k(\kappa_{1},e^{n})\leq k\|\kappa_{1}\|\cdot\|e^{n}\|\leq&\frac{1}{2}kt_{n}^{1-\mu}T\|\kappa_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}\|e^{n}\|^{2}\leq Ck^{2}t_{n}^{1-\mu}\int\limits_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\|u_{tt}(t)\|^{2}\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}\|e^{n}\|^{2}\\
\leq&Ck^{2}t_{n}^{1-\mu}\int\limits_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}t^{2\alpha-4}\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}\|e^{n}\|^{2}\\
\leq&Ck^{2}t_{n}^{1-\mu}\frac{(nk)^{2\alpha-3}-((n-1)k)^{2\alpha-3}}{2\alpha-3}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}\|e^{n}\|^{2}\\
\leq&Ck^{2\alpha-\mu}n^{2\alpha-\mu-3}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}\|e^{n}\|^{2},\end{split}

(87)

where again
\mu
is some arbitrary small positive constant.
For the component with
\kappa_{2}
let us note that we can write

\begin{split}\frac{\alpha}{\beta}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{n})=&\alpha kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}(L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n}),e_{x}^{n})\\
\ &+kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\frac{t_{n}}{k}(L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n-1}))-t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n}),e_{x}^{n}).\end{split}

(88)

Then, using Proposition
1
we have

\begin{split}\alpha kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}(L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n}),e_{x}^{n})\leq&\alpha kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})\|\cdot\|e_{x}^{n}\|\\
\leq&Ckt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{\min\{\alpha/\beta+\alpha,1\}}}\cdot\|e_{x}^{n}\|\leq Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{\min\{1+2\alpha/\beta,3-2\alpha\}}}+\varepsilon_{1}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2},\end{split}

(89)

and

\begin{split}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}(t_{n}\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-t_{n}\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n}),e_{x}^{n})\leq&kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|t_{n}\overline{\partial}L_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-t_{n}\overline{\partial}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})\|\cdot\|e_{x}^{n}\|\\
\leq&Ckt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{\max\{\beta,\alpha/\beta+\alpha-1\}}}\cdot\|e_{x}^{n}\|\\
\leq&Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{1+2{\max\{\beta,\alpha/\beta+\alpha-1\}}-2\alpha}}+kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\varepsilon_{2}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2},\end{split}

(90)

and

\begin{split}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}(t_{n}\frac{1}{k}(I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})-I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n-1}))-t_{n}\frac{d}{dt}I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{n})),e_{x}^{n})\leq&Ckt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\frac{t_{n}^{2\alpha}}{n^{2}}+\varepsilon_{3}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}\\
\leq&Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{3-2\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{3}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(91)

Next, similarly to the stability analysis we substitute

F^{n}=\|e^{n}\|^{2}+\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n}\biggr)t_{n}^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{n,i}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2},

(92)

into last inequality in (
85
), and obtain

\begin{split}F^{n}\leq&F^{n-1}+\biggl(t_{n}^{\alpha}-\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n-1}\biggr)t_{n-1}^{\alpha}\biggr)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}c_{n-1,i}\|e_{x}^{i}\|^{2}\\
\ &-k\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}+2k(\kappa_{1},e^{n})+2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{n})\\
\leq&F^{n-1}-k\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}t_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}+Ck^{2\alpha-\mu}\frac{1}{n^{3-2\alpha+\mu}}+\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}\|e^{n}\|^{2}+Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{\min\{1+2\alpha/\beta,3-2\alpha\}}}\\
\ &+\varepsilon_{1}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}+Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{1+{2\max\{\beta,\alpha/\beta+\alpha-1\}}-2\alpha}}+kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\varepsilon_{2}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2}+Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{\alpha}}{n^{3-2\alpha}}+\varepsilon_{3}kt_{n}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{n}\|^{2},\end{split}

(93)

where we used that fact that for arbitrary
n\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have

n^{\alpha}-(n-1)^{\alpha}\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha}{n-1}\biggr)<0.

(94)

Now, because
\varepsilon_{i},\ i=1,2,3
, are arbitrary positive constant we can assume that they satisfy

\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}-\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}<0.

(95)

Therefore, using above we rewrite inequality (
93
) in the following more compact form

\begin{split}F^{n}\leq&F^{n-1}+\frac{kt_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}F^{n}+Ck^{2\alpha-\mu}\frac{1}{n^{3-2\alpha+\mu}}+Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{1+2\alpha/\beta}}+Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{1+{2\max\{\beta,\alpha/\beta+\alpha-1\}}-2\alpha}}+Ck^{2\alpha}\frac{1}{n^{3-2\alpha}}.\end{split}

(96)

Summing over the above formula from
2
to
n
we get

\begin{split}F^{n}\leq&F^{1}+k\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}\frac{t_{n}^{\mu-1}}{T}F^{i}+Ck^{2\alpha-\mu}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}\biggl[\frac{1}{n^{3-2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{n^{1+2\alpha/\beta}}+\frac{1}{n^{1+2\max\{\beta,\alpha/\beta+\alpha-1\}-2\alpha}}\biggr].\end{split}

(97)

Let us now separately consider the first inequality in (
85
) with
n=1
,

\begin{split}\|e^{1}\|^{2}\leq&\|e^{0}\|^{2}-2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{1}^{\alpha-1}(\beta c_{1,1}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\frac{t_{1}}{k}c_{1,1})\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}+2k(\kappa_{1},e^{1})+2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{1}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{1})\\
\leq&\|e^{0}\|^{2}-2t_{1}^{\alpha}(\alpha+1)c_{1,1}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}+2k(\kappa_{1},e^{1})+2k\frac{\alpha}{\beta}t_{1}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{1}).\end{split}

(98)

Note that we can estimate the terms with
\kappa_{1}
and the differential part in
\kappa_{2}
in the following way

\begin{split}k(\kappa_{1},e^{1})=&(u(t_{1})-u(0)-ku_{t},e^{1})\leq\|\int\limits_{0}^{k}tu_{tt}\,dt\|\cdot\|e^{1}\|\leq\int\limits_{0}^{k}t\|u_{tt}\|\,dt\cdot\|e^{1}\|\\
\leq&\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{*}}\biggl(\int\limits_{0}^{k}t\|u_{tt}\|\,dt\biggr)^{2}+\varepsilon^{*}\|e^{1}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{*}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}k^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{*}\|e^{1}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(99)

and

\displaystyle t_{1}^{\alpha}(I^{0,\beta}_{\alpha/\beta}u_{x}(t_{1})-I^{0,\beta}_{\alpha/\beta}u_{x}(0)-k\frac{d}{dt}I^{0,\beta}_{\alpha/\beta}u_{x}(t_{1}),e_{x}^{1})\leq

\displaystyle Ck^{\alpha}\biggl(\int\limits_{0}^{k}t\|\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}I^{0,\beta}_{\alpha/\beta}u_{x}(t)\|\,dt\biggr)^{2}+kt_{1}^{\alpha-1}\varepsilon_{3}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}

(100)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ck^{2\alpha}+kt_{1}^{\alpha-1}\varepsilon_{1}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\Gamma(\beta+1)}kt_{1}^{\alpha-1}(u_{x}(t_{1})-\Gamma(\beta+1)I^{0,\beta}_{\alpha/\beta}u_{x}(t_{1}),e_{x}^{1})

\displaystyle\leq Ct_{1}^{3\alpha}+\varepsilon_{2}t_{1}^{\alpha}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2},

(101)

\displaystyle t_{1}^{\alpha}(c_{1,1}u_{x}(t_{1})-I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(t_{1})+I_{\alpha/\beta}^{0,\beta}u_{x}(0),e_{x}^{1})\leq

\displaystyle Ct_{1}^{3\alpha}+Ct_{1}^{\alpha}\|u_{0,x}\|^{2}+\varepsilon_{1}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}\leq Ct_{1}^{3\alpha}+Ct_{1}^{\alpha}+\varepsilon_{1}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2},

(102)

which together gives

\begin{split}kt_{1}^{\alpha-1}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{1})\leq&C(k^{3\alpha}+k^{2\alpha}+k^{\alpha})+k^{\alpha}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3})\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2},\end{split}

(103)

and

k(\kappa_{1},e^{1})\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{*}}k^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{*}\|e^{1}\|^{2},

(104)

where
\varepsilon^{*}
is some positive constant satisfying
2\varepsilon^{*}<1
Hence, thanks to above estimations we get

\begin{split}\|e^{1}\|^{2}+(1+\alpha)t_{1}^{\alpha}c_{1,1}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}\leq&-k\frac{\alpha}{\beta\Gamma(\beta)}t_{1}^{\alpha-1}\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}+Ck^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{*}\|e^{1}\|^{2}+Ck^{3\alpha}\\
\ &+Ck^{2\alpha}+Ck^{\alpha}+k^{\alpha}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+t_{1}\varepsilon_{3})\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}\\
\leq&Ck^{\alpha}+Ck^{2\alpha}+\varepsilon^{*}\|e^{1}\|^{2},\end{split}

(105)

where we again choose
\varepsilon_{1},\,\varepsilon_{2},\,\varepsilon_{3}
such that
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}-\alpha/\beta\Gamma(\beta)<0
.
Hence, we get

\begin{split}F^{1}\leq&\frac{C}{1-2\varepsilon^{*}}k^{\alpha}.\end{split}

(106)

what immediately implies
\|e^{1}\|\leq Ck^{\alpha/2}
. Let us note now that from the Sobolev Embedding Theory we have
u(t)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\subset C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,
u(0)=u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}|u_{0,x}(x)|=0
. Thus we have

|(u_{x}(0),e_{x}^{1})|=|(u_{xx}(0),e^{1})|\leq\|u_{xx}(0)\|\cdot\|e^{1}\|\leq Ck^{\alpha/2},

(107)

and thus

\begin{split}k^{\alpha}(\kappa_{2},e_{x}^{1})\leq&C(k^{3\alpha}+k^{2\alpha}+k^{3\alpha/2})+k^{\alpha}(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3})\|e_{x}^{1}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(108)

Choosing again appropriate values for
\varepsilon_{i},\ i\in\{1,2,3\}
, we get
F^{1}\leq Ck^{3\alpha/2}
. Repeating above procedure finitely many times we get
F^{1}\leq Ck^{2\alpha-\mu}
, where
\mu
is arbitrarily small.
Going back to inequality (
97
) for
n\geq 2
, we use Lemma
2
with
\gamma_{i}=t_{i}^{\mu-1}/T
to obtain

\begin{split}F^{n}\leq&Ck^{2\alpha-\mu}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{n}\biggl[\frac{1}{n^{3-2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{n^{1+2\alpha/\beta}}+\frac{1}{n^{1+2\max\{\beta,\alpha/\beta+\alpha-1\}-2\alpha}}\biggr]+CF^{1}.\end{split}

(109)

Finally, using the asymptotic behaviour of the following series

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{i^{2\nu+1-2\alpha}}=\zeta{(1+2\nu-2\alpha)}+\frac{1}{(2\alpha-2\nu)}\frac{1}{n^{2\nu-2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{2n^{1+2\nu-2\alpha}}+\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{1}{n^{2+2\nu-2\alpha}}\biggr),\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(110)

for
\nu\neq\alpha
, where
\zeta(x)
is a Riemann zeta function, and

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{i^{1}}=\biggl(\gamma-\log\biggl(\frac{1}{n}\biggr)\biggr)+\frac{1}{2n}+\mathcal{O}\biggl(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\biggr),\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to\infty,

(111)

for
\nu=\alpha
, where
\gamma
is a Euler’s constant, we get

F^{n}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}Ck^{2\alpha}+F^{1}&\text{for}\quad\alpha\neq\beta,\\
Ck^{2\alpha}(-\log k)+F^{1}&\text{for}\quad\alpha=\beta.\end{array}\right.

(112)

Hence, by the definition of
F^{n}
we obtain the desired inequality

\begin{split}\|e^{n}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}Ck^{\alpha}+(F^{1})^{1/2}&\text{for}\quad\alpha\neq\beta,\\
Ck^{\alpha}(-\log k)^{1/2}+(F^{1})^{1/2}&\text{for}\quad\alpha=\beta.\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(113)

The proof is complete.
∎

Now, we proceed to the analysis of the numerical scheme used to solve (
5
). We discretize the first derivative with respect to time and Erdélyi–Kober fractional differential operator as in (
57
). Next, to approximate the exact solution in the spatial dimension, we use Galerkin–Hermite method. We use Hermite functions due to their rapid decay at the infinity and orthogonality in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. To this end, following
[
40
]
, let us define Hermite polynomial
H_{n}(x),\ n\geq 0
. Using the Rodrigues’ formula

H_{n}(x)\coloneqq(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(115)

Hermite polynomials are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the weight
w(x)=e^{-x^{2}}
. However, in our numerical scheme it is more appropriate to use Hermite functions
\widehat{H}_{n}(x)

\widehat{H}_{n}(x)=\pi^{-\frac{1}{4}}(2^{n}n!)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}H_{n}(x).

(116)

Let us notice the Hermite functions vanish exponentially as
x\to\pm\infty
and are orthonormal in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, that is

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{H}_{n}(x)\widehat{H}_{m}(x)\,dx=\delta_{nm},

(117)

and the inner product of the first-order derivatives satisfies
[
40
]
,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{n}(x)\widehat{H}^{\prime}_{m}(x)\,dx=\widehat{\delta}_{nm}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}{2},&m=n-2,\\
n+\frac{1}{2},&n=m,\\
-\frac{\sqrt{(n+2)(n+1)}}{2},&m=n+2,\\
0,&\mathrm{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

(118)

therefore the stiffness matrix is banded. Let

be the space of polynomials of degree at most

\widehat{P}_{N}\coloneqq\{\widehat{H}_{n}(x)=\pi^{-\frac{1}{4}}(2^{n}n!)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}H_{n}(x),\ H_{n}(x)\in P_{N}\}.

(119)

Finally, we can rewrite the time-discrete problem (

\biggl\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mathrm{Find}\ U^{n}_{N}=U_{N}(t_{n})\in\widehat{P}_{N},\quad\mathrm{such\ that},\\

(120)

with

\widehat{\Pi}_{N}\coloneqq e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\Pi_{N}\bigl(ue^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\bigr),\quad u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(121)

(122)

\widehat{\Pi}_{N}u(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\gamma_{i}\widehat{H}_{i},\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\gamma_{i}=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u(x)\widehat{H}_{i}(x)\,dx.

5
Conclusion

The deterministic fractional diffusion equation describing the evolution of the marginal density function of particle dispersion of the generalized grey Brownian motion involves the Erdélyi–Kober fractional derivative. We proposed two discretization methods of this operator along with estimates of the error of approximation. Theoretical results were supported by numerical experiments. Furthermore, using the Galerkin–Hermite method, the numerical scheme for solving the Erdélyi–Kober fractional diffusion equation was proposed. For the semi-discrete problem with respect to time, we proved the stability and convergence. Due to the singular term in time present in the main equation, the error of the approximation is an order smaller than
1
. Resolving this issue, i.e., providing some higher order methods is one of the objectives of our future studies along with investigations concerning the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of the Erdélyi–Kober fractional diffusion equation.

Modified Poisson-Boltzmann equations and macroscopic forces in inhomogeneous ionic fluids
Yury A. Budkov, Andrei L. Kolesnikov

Abstract
We propose a field-theoretical approach based on the thermodynamic perturbation theory and within it derive a grand thermodynamic potential of the inhomogeneous ionic fluid as a functional of electrostatic potential for an arbitrary reference fluid system. We obtain a modified Poisson-Boltzmann equation as the Euler-Lagrange equation for the obtained functional. Applying Noether’s theorem to this functional, we derive a general mean-field expression for the stress tensor consistent with the respective modified Poisson-Boltzmann equation. We derive a general expression for the macroscopic force acting on the dielectric or conductive body immersed in an ionic fluid. In particular, we derive a general mean-field expression for the disjoining pressure of an ionic fluid in a slit pore. We apply the developed formalism to describe three ionic fluid models of practical importance: nonpolarizable models (including the well-known Poisson-Boltzmann and Poisson-Fermi equations), polarizable models (ions carry nonzero permanent dipole or static polarizability), and models of ion-dipole mixtures (including the well-known Poisson-Boltzmann-Langevin equation). For these models, we obtain modified Poisson-Boltzmann equations and respective stress tensors, which could be valuable for different applications, where it is necessary to estimate the macroscopic forces acting on the dielectric or conductive bodies (electrodes, colloids, membranes, etc .) together with the local electrostatic potential (field) and ionic concentrations.



I
Introduction

Ionic fluids (plasma, electrolyte solutions, molten salts, and room temperature ionic liquids) have recently attracted a lot of researchers’ and chemical engineers’ attention. This was mostly caused by the use of ionic fluids in various applications, including lipid and ion-exchange membranes, biomacromolecules, colloids, batteries, fuel cells, supercapacitors,
etc
. In all these examples, ionic fluids, which either interact with the charged surface of the membrane, macromolecule, colloid, or electrode surface or are confined in charged nanopores, are strongly inhomogeneous. Due to the inhomogeneity of the ionic fluid, the local electrical neutrality is violated, so that its description requires numerical solution of one or another self-consistent field equation for the electrostatic potential with appropriate boundary conditions: the classical Poisson-Boltzmann (PB) equation or its generalizations. The latter are known in the literature as modified PB equations.

The modified PB equations obtained up to now take into account the individual properties of ionic species and the chemical specifics of the surface interacting with them. In this case it is necessary to especially emphasize the consideration of the non-zero size of ions (steric interactions)
Kralj-Iglič and Iglič 1996
;
Borukhov, Andelman, and Orland 1997
;
Maggs and Podgornik 2016
;
Kornyshev 2007
, their specific and structural interactions
Goodwin, Feng, and Kornyshev 2017
;
Blossey, Maggs, and Podgornik 2017
, polarizability/quadrupolarizability and permanent dipole moment of ionic species and solvent (co-solvent) molecules
Frydel 2011
;
Budkov et al. 2020
;
Budkov, Zavarzin, and Kolesnikov 2021
;
Budkov, Kolesnikov, and Kiselev 2015
;
Budkov, Kolesnikov, and Kiselev 2016
;
Budkov et al. 2018
;
Coalson and Duncan 1996
;
Abrashkin, Andelman, and Orland 2007
;
Iglič, Gongadze, and Bohinc 2010
;
Buyukdagli and Ala-Nissila 2013
;
Slavchov 2014
, surface charge regulaton
Podgornik 2018
, specific ion-electrode interactions
Budkov et al. 2018
;
Uematsu, Netz, and Bonthuis 2018
and electrostatic correlations
Buyukdagli 2020
;
de Souza and Bazant 2020
;
Bazant, Storey, and Kornyshev 2011
. However, despite the variety of the modified PB equations, to the best of our knowledge, there is still no first-principles approaches allowing us to derive them from the unified standpoint and, simultaneously, take into account the aforementioned effects. Moreover, for applications, where it is required to calculate the macroscopic forces acting on conductive or dielectric bodies immersed in the ionic fluid (such as electrodes, colloid particles, membranes, micelles, dust particles,
etc
.), it is necessary to have a stress tensor consistent with the modified PB equation.

In this paper, we will present such a field-theoretical approach based on the thermodynamic perturbation theory allowing us to derive the grand thermodynamic potential of an inhomogeneous ionic fluid as a functional of the electrostatic potential for an arbitrary reference system, modified PB equation (as the Euler-Lagrange equation for this functional), and a stress tensor (with use of Noether’s theorem). We will apply this approach to derivation of the modified PB equations and stress tensors for three types of models that can be employed in the applications: (1) unpolarizable ionic fluid models, (2) polarizable ionic fluid models, and (3) polarizable models of the ion-dipole mixture (solvent in salt models).

II
Mean-field theory of ionic fluids

II.1
Model I. Nonpolarizable ionic fluid mean-field models

Let us consider an ionic fluid – a set of two types of ions with electric charges
q_{\pm}
confined in a volume
V
at a temperature
T
contacting with an infinite reservoir with ions (bulk). We assume that the ions are immersed in a dielectric background of constant permittivity,
\varepsilon_{b}
. The ions interact with each other, in addition to the Coulomb interactions, via the short-range repulsive potentials
U_{\alpha\gamma}(\mathbb{r})
,
where indexes
\alpha,\gamma=\pm
enumerate the ions types. Thus, the grand partition function can be written as follows

\Xi=\sum\limits_{n_{+}=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n_{-}=0}^{\infty}\frac{z_{+}^{n_{+}}z_{-}^{n_{-}}}{n_{+}!n_{-}!}Q_{n_{+}n_{-}},

(1)

with the fugacities
z_{\pm}=\Lambda_{\pm}^{-3}e^{\beta\mu_{\pm}}\theta_{\pm}
(expressed via the chemical potentials,
\mu_{\pm}
, the de Broglie thermal wavelengths,
\Lambda_{\pm}
, and internal partition functions,
\theta_{\pm}
, of the ions) of the ionic species and configuration integrals

Q_{n_{+}n_{-}}=\int d\Gamma_{n_{+}n_{-}}\exp\left[-\beta U_{n_{+}n_{-}}\right],

(2)

where
\beta=(k_{B}T)^{-1}
(
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant,
T
is the temperature) and

\int d\Gamma_{n_{+}n_{-}}(\cdot)=\int..\int\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{+}}{d\mathbb{r}_{j}^{(+)}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n_{-}}{d\mathbb{r}_{k}^{(-)}}(\cdot).

(3)

The total potential energy,
U_{n_{+}n_{-}}
, of the interionic interactions can be written as a sum of several basic terms:

U_{n_{+}n_{-}}=U_{n_{+}n_{-}}^{(el)}+U_{n_{+}n_{-}}^{(ev)}+U_{n_{+}n_{-}}^{(ext)}-U_{self}.

(4)

The first term in eq. (
4
) describes the total potential energy of electrostatic interactions

U_{n_{+}n_{-}}^{(el)}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\mathbb{r}\int d\mathbb{r}^{\prime}\hat{\rho}(\mathbb{r})G(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime})\hat{\rho}(\mathbb{r}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{\rho}G\hat{\rho}\right),

(5)

where
G(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime})=1/4\pi\varepsilon_{b}|\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime}|
is the Green function of the Poisson equation;

\hat{\rho}(\mathbb{r})=\hat{\rho}_{i}(\mathbb{r})+{\rho}_{ext}(\mathbb{r}),

(6)

is the local charge density containing microscopic charge density of the ions

\hat{\rho}_{i}(\mathbb{r})=\sum\limits_{\alpha=\pm}q_{\alpha}\hat{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})

(7)

with the microscopic ionic concentrations

\hat{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\alpha}}\delta\left(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}_{j}^{(\alpha)}\right)

(8)

and the local charge density
{\rho}_{ext}(\mathbb{r})
of the external charges. The second term in eq. (
4
) describes the total potential energy of the excluded volume interactions, i.e.

U_{n_{+}n_{-}}^{(ev)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}\left(\hat{c}_{\alpha}U_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}_{\gamma}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int d\mathbb{r}\int d\mathbb{r}^{\prime}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})U_{\alpha\gamma}(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime})\hat{c}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{r}^{\prime}).

(9)

The third term describes the interactions of ions with external fields with potential energies,
u_{\pm}(\mathbb{r})
:

U_{n_{+}n_{-}}^{(ext)}=\sum\limits_{\alpha}\int d\mathbb{r}\hat{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})u_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=\sum\limits_{\alpha}(\hat{c}_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}).

(10)

The last term in eq. (
4
) is the self-energy of the ions determined by the following equation

U_{self}=\left({n_{+}q_{+}^{2}}+{n_{-}q_{-}^{2}}\right)G(0)/2+({n_{+}U_{++}(0)}+{n_{-}U_{--}(0)})/2
.

Futher, using the standard Hubbard-Stratonovich transformations

e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(\hat{\rho}G\hat{\rho})}=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\varphi}{C}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(\varphi G^{-1}\varphi)+i\beta(\hat{\rho}\varphi)},

(11)

e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\hat{c}_{\alpha}U_{\alpha\gamma}\hat{c}_{\gamma})}=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\Phi}{\mathcal{N}_{U}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\Phi_{\alpha}U^{-1}_{\alpha\gamma}\Phi_{\gamma})+i\beta\sum\limits_{\alpha}(\hat{c}_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha})},

(12)

after some algebra, we obtain the following functional integral representation of the grand partition function

\Xi=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\varphi}{C}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\varphi G^{-1}\varphi\right)+i\beta(\rho_{ext}\varphi)}\Xi_{R}[\varphi],

(13)

where
C=\int\mathcal{D}\varphi e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\varphi G^{-1}\varphi\right)}
and
\mathcal{N}_{U}=\int{\mathcal{D}\Phi}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\Phi_{\alpha}U^{-1}_{\alpha\gamma}\Phi_{\gamma})}
are the normalization constants of the Gaussian measures. The inverse electrostatic Green function has the following form

G^{-1}(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime})=-\varepsilon_{b}\nabla^{2}\delta(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime}).

(14)

The grand partition function,
\Xi_{R}
, of the reference fluid with pure repulsive interactions between the particles can be written as the following functional integral

\Xi_{R}=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\Phi}{\mathcal{N}_{U}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\Phi_{\alpha}U^{-1}_{\alpha\gamma}\Phi_{\gamma})+\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bar{z}_{\alpha}\int d\mathbb{r}e^{i\beta(q_{\alpha}\varphi+\Phi_{\alpha}+iu_{\alpha})}\right],

(15)

where
\bar{z}_{\pm}=e^{\frac{\beta{U}_{\pm\pm}(0)}{2}+\frac{\beta q_{\pm}^{2}G(0)}{2}}z_{\pm}\theta_{\pm}
are the renormalized fugacities. The inverse operator,
U^{-1}
, is determined by the integral relation

\int d\mathbb{r}^{\prime\prime}\sum\limits_{\lambda}U_{\alpha\lambda}^{-1}(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime\prime})U_{\lambda\gamma}(\mathbb{r}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime})=\delta_{\alpha\gamma}\delta(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{\prime}).

(16)

Also, using the cumulant expansion, we obtain

\Xi_{R}=\exp\left[\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left<W^{n}[\Phi;\chi]\right>_{c}}{n!}\right],

(17)

where we have introduced the functional

W[\Phi;\chi]=\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bar{z}_{\alpha}\int d\mathbb{r}e^{i\beta\Phi_{\alpha}+i\beta\chi_{\alpha}}

(18)

with the auxiliary variables
\chi_{\alpha}=q_{\alpha}\varphi+iu_{\alpha}
; and
the symbol
\left<(\cdot)\right>_{c}
denotes the cumulant average
Kubo 1962
, so that the following relations take place

\left<W[\Phi;\chi]\right>_{c}=\left<W[\Phi;\chi]\right>,

(19)

\left<W^{2}[\Phi;\chi]\right>_{c}=\left<W^{2}[\Phi;\chi]\right>-\left<W[\Phi;\chi]\right>^{2},

(20)

\left<W^{3}[\Phi;\chi]\right>_{c}=\left<W^{3}[\Phi;\chi]\right>-3\left<W^{2}[\Phi;\chi]\right>\left<W[\Phi;\chi]\right>+2\left<W[\Phi;\chi]\right>^{3},...

(21)

where we have introduced the notation for averaging over the Gaussian measure, i.e.

\left<(\cdot)\right>=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\Phi}{\mathcal{N}_{U}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\Phi_{\alpha}U^{-1}_{\alpha\gamma}\Phi_{\gamma})\right](\cdot).

(22)

Therefore, using the binomial formula, we obtain

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left<W^{n}[\Phi;\chi]\right>_{c}}{n!}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\bar{z}_{+}^{k}\bar{z}_{-}^{n-k}}{k!(n-k)!}\int d\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)}...\int d\mathbb{r}_{k}^{(+)}\int d\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(-)}...\int d\mathbb{r}_{n-k}^{(-)}

\times\left<e^{i\beta\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}\Phi_{+}\left(\mathbb{r}_{s}^{(+)}\right)+i\beta\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n-k}\Phi_{-}\left(\mathbb{r}_{s}^{(-)}\right)}\right>_{c}e^{i\beta\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}\chi_{+}\left(\mathbb{r}_{s}^{(+)}\right)+i\beta\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n-k}\chi_{-}\left(\mathbb{r}_{l}^{(-)}\right)}.

(23)

Then, after the calculations of the Gaussian functional integrals
Zinn-Justin 2002
, we get

\left<e^{i\beta\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}\Phi_{+}(\mathbb{r}_{s}^{(+)})+i\beta\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n-k}\Phi_{-}(\mathbb{r}_{s}^{(-)})}\right>_{c}=e^{\frac{\beta kU_{++}(0)}{2}+\frac{\beta(n-k)U_{--}(0)}{2}}B^{nk}_{R}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)},...,\mathbb{r}_{k}^{(+)};\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(-)},...,\mathbb{r}_{n-k}^{(-)}\right),

(24)

where we have introduced the cluster functions:

B_{R}^{11}\left(\mathbb{r}^{(+)}\right)=B_{R}^{10}\left(\mathbb{r}^{(-)}\right)=1,

(25)

B_{R}^{22}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)},\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(+)}\right)=e^{-\beta U_{++}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)}-\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(+)}\right)}-1,\penalty\ B_{R}^{21}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)};\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(-)}\right)=e^{-\beta U_{+-}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)}-\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(-)}\right)}-1,

B_{R}^{20}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(-)},\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(-)}\right)=e^{-\beta U_{--}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(-)}-\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(-)}\right)}-1,

(26)

B_{R}^{33}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)},\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(+)},\mathbb{r}_{3}^{(+)}\right)=e^{-\beta\left(U_{++}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)}-\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(+)}\right)+U_{++}\left(\mathbb{r}_{1}^{(+)}-\mathbb{r}_{3}^{(+)}\right)+U_{++}\left(\mathbb{r}_{2}^{(+)}-\mathbb{r}_{3}^{(+)}\right)\right)}

(27)

where index

(28)

(29)

Therefore, in the local approximation, the cumulant expansion is simply a cluster expansion

of the bulk pressure as a function of shifted chemical potentials,
\bar{\mu}_{\pm}=\mu_{\pm}+i\chi_{\pm}+{q_{\pm}^{2}G(0)}/{2}+k_{B}T\ln\theta_{\pm}

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left<W^{n}[\Phi;\chi]\right>_{c}}{n!}\approx\beta\int d\mathbb{r}{P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-})}.

(30)

Redefining the chemical potentials of the species

\Xi_{R}=\exp\left[\beta\int d\mathbb{r}P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-})\right],

where

of species and temperature. We would like to note that this approximation is in accordance with the thermodynamic relation

Therefore, the grand partition function of the ionic fluid takes the form

\Xi=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\varphi}{C}\exp\left[-\beta\Omega_{ef}[\varphi]\right],

where we have introduced the following grand thermodynamic potential

\Omega_{ef}[\varphi]=\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi G^{-1}\varphi\right)-i(\rho_{ext}\varphi)-\int d\mathbb{r}P\left(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-}\right),

(33)

as a functional of the fluctuating electrostatic potential,

Then, using the mean-field approximation, i.e. the functional derivative of the GTP to zero

\frac{\delta\Omega_{ef}}{\delta\varphi(\mathbb{r})}=0

(34)

and introducing the self-consistent field potential (saddle-point)

\Delta\psi(\mathbb{r})=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{b}}\left(\rho_{ext}(\mathbb{r})+q_{+}\bar{c}_{+}(\mathbb{r})+q_{-}\bar{c}_{-}(\mathbb{r})\right)

where the local concentrations,

\bar{c}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{r})=\frac{\delta\Omega_{ef}}{\delta{u}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{r})}=\frac{\partial{P\left(T,\bar{\mu}_{+}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{-}(\mathbb{r})\right)}}{\partial{\bar{\mu}_{\gamma}}}=c_{\gamma}\left(T,\bar{\mu}_{+}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{-}(\mathbb{r})\right),

\Omega[\psi]=\int d\mathbb{r}\left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{b}(\nabla{\psi})^{2}}{2}-P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-})\right),

\Delta\psi(\mathbb{r})=\frac{2cq}{\varepsilon_{b}}\frac{\sinh{\beta q\psi(\mathbb{r})}}{1+2cv\left(\cosh\beta q\psi(\mathbb{r})-1\right)},

where
c
is the bulk ionic concentration. In order to take into account the ionic size asymmetry, we can use the asymmetric lattice gas model as the reference system, which was systematically considered in paper
Maggs and Podgornik 2016
in the context of the electric double layer theory. To take into account the ionic size asymmetry, we can also use the hard sphere mixture, for which the local pressure and chemical potentials can be obtained from the following excess free energy density within the Percus-Yevick approximation:

\beta f_{ex}=-n_{0}\ln(1-n_{3})+\frac{n_{1}n_{2}}{1-n_{3}}+\frac{n_{2}^{3}}{24\pi(1-n_{3})^{2}}

(44)

or using the Carnahan-Starling approximation
Roth 2010
;
Maggs and Podgornik 2016
. The auxiliary variables are:
n_{0}=\bar{c}_{+}+\bar{c}_{-}
,
n_{1}=1/2\penalty\ d_{+}\bar{c}_{+}+1/2\penalty\ d_{-}\bar{c}_{-}
,
n_{2}=\pi\penalty\ d_{+}^{2}\bar{c}_{+}+\pi\penalty\ d_{-}^{2}\bar{c}_{-}
,
n_{3}=\pi/6\penalty\ d_{+}^{3}\bar{c}_{+}+\pi/6\penalty\ d_{-}^{3}\bar{c}_{-}
. The pressure and chemical potentials of species (
\alpha=\pm
) can be derived via standard thermodynamic relations
Roth et al. 2002
:

\beta P=n_{0}\frac{1+n_{3}+n_{3}^{2}}{(1-n_{3})^{3}}+\frac{1/12\pi\penalty\ n_{2}^{3}+n_{1}n_{2}(1-n_{3})-3n_{0}n_{3}}{(1-n_{3})^{3}},

(45)

\beta\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}=\ln\frac{\bar{c}_{\alpha}\Lambda_{\alpha}^{3}}{1-n_{3}}+\frac{n_{2}d_{\alpha}}{2(1-n_{3})}+\pi d_{\alpha}^{2}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{1-n_{3}}+\frac{n_{2}^{2}}{8\pi(1-n_{3})^{2}}\right)+\frac{\beta\pi d_{\alpha}^{3}P}{6},

(46)

where
d_{\alpha}
are the ionic effective diameters.

II.2
Model II. Polarizable ionic fluid mean-field models

Now let us consider an ionic fluid, whose ions carry charges
q_{\pm}+e_{\pm}
at the center and additional ’peripheral’ charges
-e_{\pm}
at a fluctuating distance from it, so that the total charges of the ions are
q_{\pm}
. The fluctuating distance,
\mathbb{\xi}
, is described by the distribution functions
g_{\pm}(\mathbb{\xi})
. This model can be used to describe room temperature ionic liquids and molten salts with polarizable/polar ions. A similar ’nonlocal’ description of the bound charge of ions and molecules was also presented in papers
Buyukdagli and Ala-Nissila 2013
;
Budkov 2019
;
Budkov 2020
;
Budkov 2018
. The configuration integrals in the grand partition function (
61
) take the form

Q_{n_{+}n_{-}}=\int d\Gamma_{n_{+}n_{-}}\exp\left[-\beta U_{n_{+}n_{-}}\right],

(47)

where

\int d\Gamma_{n_{+}n_{-}}(\cdot)=\int..\int\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{+}}{d\mathbb{r}_{j}^{(+)}}d\mathbb{\xi}^{(+)}_{j}g_{+}\left(\mathbb{\xi}^{(+)}_{j}\right)\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n_{-}}{d\mathbb{r}_{k}^{(-)}}d\mathbb{\xi}^{(-)}_{k}g_{-}\left(\mathbb{\xi}^{(-)}_{k}\right)(\cdot).

(48)

The total potential energy is determined by the same expression (
4
) with the electrostatic energy expressed via the following local charge density

\hat{\rho}(\mathbb{r})=\hat{\rho}_{i}(\mathbb{r})+{\rho}_{ext}(\mathbb{r}),

(49)

where

\hat{\rho}_{i}(\mathbb{r})=\sum\limits_{\alpha}q_{\alpha}\hat{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})+\sum\limits_{\alpha=\pm}\sum\limits_{j_{\alpha}=1}^{n_{\alpha}}e_{\alpha}\left(\delta\left(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{(\alpha)}_{j_{\alpha}}\right)-\delta\left(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{(\alpha)}_{j_{\alpha}}-\mathbb{\xi}_{j_{\alpha}}^{(\alpha)}\right)\right)

(50)

is the microscopic charge density of the ions taking into account their polarizability (the second term).

Performing the same transformations as in Subsection A, we arrive at

\Xi=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\varphi}{C}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\varphi G^{-1}\varphi\right)+i\beta(\rho_{ext}\varphi)}\Xi_{R},

(51)

where we have introduced the reference grand partition function

\Xi_{R}=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\Phi}{\mathcal{N}_{U}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\Phi_{\alpha}U^{-1}_{\alpha\gamma}\Phi_{\gamma})+W[\Phi;\chi]\right]

(52)

with the functional
W[\Phi;\chi]
determined by eq. (
18
) with

\chi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=q_{\alpha}\varphi(\mathbb{r})+iu_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})+k_{B}T\ln\left[\int d\mathbb{\xi}g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})e^{i\beta e_{\alpha}\left(\varphi(\mathbb{r})-\varphi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\right)}\right].

(53)

Then, performing the same calculations based on the cumulant expansion and adopting the same approximations as in Subsection A, we obtain the GTP in the absence of external volume electric charges

\Omega[\psi]=\int d\mathbb{r}\left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{b}(\nabla{\psi})^{2}}{2}-P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-})\right),

(54)

where

\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=\mu_{\alpha}-q_{\alpha}\psi(\mathbb{r})+k_{B}T\ln\left[\int d\mathbb{\xi}g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})e^{-\beta e_{\alpha}\left(\psi(\mathbb{r})-\psi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\right)}\right].

(55)

Now we would like to specify the probability distribution functions
g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})
. We consider the Gaussian distributions

g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})=(2\pi\sigma_{\alpha}^{2})^{-3/2}\int\frac{d\mathbb{n}_{\alpha}}{4\pi}\exp\left[-\frac{(\mathbb{\xi}-\mathbb{s}_{\alpha})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}}\right],

(56)

averaged over orientations of unit vectors
\mathbb{n}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{s}_{\alpha}/|\mathbb{s}_{\alpha}|
, where
\mathbb{s}_{\alpha}
is the vector of the average distance between charges
\pm e_{\alpha}
. In this case, we obtain

\int d\mathbb{\xi}g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})e^{-\beta e_{\alpha}\left(\psi(\mathbb{r})-\psi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\right)}=\int\frac{d\mathbb{n}_{\alpha}}{4\pi}\int\frac{d\mathbb{\xi}}{(2\pi\sigma_{\alpha}^{2})^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(\mathbb{\xi}-\mathbb{s}_{\alpha})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}}}e^{-\beta e_{\alpha}\left(\psi(\mathbb{r})-\psi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\right)}

\approx\int\frac{d\mathbb{n}_{\alpha}}{4\pi}\int\frac{d\mathbb{\xi}}{(2\pi\sigma_{\alpha}^{2})^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{(\mathbb{\xi}-\mathbb{s}_{\alpha})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}}}e^{-\beta e_{\alpha}\mathbb{\xi}\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})}=\frac{\sinh(\beta p_{\alpha}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|)}{\beta p_{\alpha}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|}e^{\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}(\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))^{2}}{2k_{B}T}},

(57)

where we have used the approximation
\psi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\approx\psi(\mathbb{r})+\mathbb{\xi}\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})
and introduced the permanent dipole moments,
p_{\alpha}=e_{\alpha}|\mathbb{s}_{\alpha}|
, and static polarizabilities,
\gamma_{\alpha}=\beta\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}e_{\alpha}^{2}
, of the ions. Therefore, we obtain the following expressions for the intrinsic chemical potentials of the species

\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=\mu_{\alpha}-q_{\alpha}\psi(\mathbb{r})+\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}(\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))^{2}}{2}+k_{B}T\ln\left[\frac{\sinh(\beta p_{\alpha}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|)}{\beta p_{\alpha}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|}\right].

(58)

Variation of the functional (
54
) with respect to the electrostatic potential,
\psi(\mathbb{r})
, with an account for eq. (
58
) leads to the following self-consistent field equation

-\nabla\cdot(\epsilon(\mathbb{r})\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))=q_{+}\bar{c}_{+}(\mathbb{r})+q_{-}\bar{c}_{-}(\mathbb{r}),

(59)

where

\epsilon(\mathbb{r})=\varepsilon_{b}+\sum\limits_{\alpha=\pm}\left(\gamma_{\alpha}+\frac{p_{\alpha}^{2}}{k_{B}T}\frac{L(\beta p_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{r}))}{\beta p_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{r})}\right)\bar{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})

(60)

is the effective permittivity of the medium,
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{r})=|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|
is the electric field absolute value,
L(x)=\coth x-1/x
is the Langevin function. We would like to mention that eqs. (
59
-
60
), in case of the symmetric lattice gas model, were used in ref.
Budkov, Zavarzin, and Kolesnikov 2021
to model the ionic liquids with a polarizable/polar cation on a charged electrode. A similar theory was also used to model two-component electrolyte solution with strongly polar cations on charged electrode
Budkov et al. 2020
.

II.3
Model III. Mean-field ion-dipole mixture models (solvent in salt models)

Now let us consider the case of an ionic fluid with an admixture of electrically neutral but polarizable molecules. This model can be used to describe room temperature ionic fluids and molten salts with an additive of polar solvents (solvent in salt) or electrolyte solutions with an admixture of highly polar molecules, such as amino acids. As above, we assume that the ions carry charges
q_{\pm}+e_{\pm}
at the center and additional charges
-e_{\pm}
at a fluctuating distance from it. The neutral molecules also have charge
e_{0}
at the center and charge
-e_{0}
placed at a fluctuating distance from the center. The probability distribution functions
g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})
(
\alpha=\pm,0

56
). The grand partition function, thus, can be written as follows

\Xi=\sum\limits_{n_{+}=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n_{-}=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n_{0}=0}^{\infty}\frac{z_{+}^{n_{+}}z_{-}^{n_{-}}z_{0}^{n_{0}}}{n_{+}!n_{-}!n_{0}!}Q_{n_{+}n_{-}n_{0}}

(61)

with the fugacities,
z_{\alpha}=\Lambda_{\alpha}^{-3}e^{\beta\mu_{\alpha}}\theta_{\alpha}
, (
\alpha=\pm,0
) of the ionic species and the configuration integrals

Q_{n_{+}n_{-}n_{0}}=\int d\Gamma_{n_{+}n_{-}n_{0}}\exp\left[-\beta U_{n_{+}n_{-}n_{0}}\right],

(62)

\int d\Gamma_{n_{+}n_{-}n_{0}}(\cdot)=\int..\int\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n_{+}}{d\mathbb{r}_{j}^{(+)}}d\mathbb{\xi}^{(+)}_{j}g_{+}\left(\mathbb{\xi}^{(+)}_{j}\right)\\
\times\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n_{-}}{d\mathbb{r}_{k}^{(-)}}d\mathbb{\xi}^{(-)}_{k}g_{-}\left(\mathbb{\xi}^{(-)}_{k}\right)\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n_{0}}{d\mathbb{r}_{l}^{(0)}}d\mathbb{\xi}^{(0)}_{l}g_{0}\left(\mathbb{\xi}^{(0)}_{l}\right)(\cdot),

(63)

where the total potential energy is determined by the same eq. (
4
). The total local charge density, in this case, can be written in the form

\hat{\rho}(\mathbb{r})=\hat{\rho}_{i}(\mathbb{r})+\hat{\rho}_{d}(\mathbb{r})+{\rho}_{ext}(\mathbb{r}),

(64)

where
\hat{\rho}_{i}(\mathbb{r})
is already defined above by eq. (
50
) and

\hat{\rho}_{d}(\mathbb{r})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{0}}e_{0}\left(\delta\left(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{(0)}_{j}\right)-\delta\left(\mathbb{r}-\mathbb{r}^{(0)}_{j}-\mathbb{\xi}_{j}^{(0)}\right)\right)

(65)

is the microscopic density of the bound charge of the molecules. Then, performing the same transformations as in Subsection A, we obtain

\Xi=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\varphi}{C}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\left(\varphi G^{-1}\varphi\right)+i\beta(\rho_{ext}\varphi)}\Xi_{R}[\varphi],

(66)

where we have introduced the reference grand partition function

\Xi_{R}=\int\frac{\mathcal{D}\Phi}{\mathcal{N}_{U}}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\gamma}(\Phi_{\alpha}U^{-1}_{\alpha\gamma}\Phi_{\gamma})+W[\Phi;\chi]\right]

(67)

with the functional

W[\Phi;\chi]=\sum\limits_{\alpha=\pm,0}\bar{z}_{\alpha}\int d\mathbb{r}e^{i\beta\Phi_{\alpha}+i\beta\chi_{\alpha}},

(68)

where

\chi_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=q_{\alpha}\varphi(\mathbb{r})+iu_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})+k_{B}T\ln\left[\int d\mathbb{\xi}g_{\alpha}(\mathbb{\xi})e^{i\beta e_{\alpha}\left(\varphi(\mathbb{r})-\varphi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\right)}\right],\penalty\ \alpha=\pm

(69)

\chi_{0}(\mathbb{r})=iu_{0}(\mathbb{r})+k_{B}T\ln\left[\int d\mathbb{\xi}g_{0}(\mathbb{\xi})e^{i\beta e_{0}\left(\varphi(\mathbb{r})-\varphi(\mathbb{r}+\mathbb{\xi})\right)}\right].

(70)

After the same approximation, as in Subsection B, in the absence of external volume charge, we arrive at the following GTP

\Omega[\psi]=\int d\mathbb{r}\left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{b}(\nabla{\psi})^{2}}{2}-P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-},\bar{\mu}_{0})\right)

(71)

and self-consistent field equation

-\nabla\cdot(\epsilon(\mathbb{r})\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))=q_{+}\bar{c}_{+}(\mathbb{r})+q_{-}\bar{c}_{-}(\mathbb{r}),

(72)

where

\epsilon(\mathbb{r})=\varepsilon_{b}+\sum\limits_{\alpha=\pm,0}\left(\gamma_{\alpha}+\frac{p_{\alpha}^{2}}{k_{B}T}\frac{L(\beta p_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{r}))}{\beta p_{\alpha}\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{r})}\right)\bar{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})

(73)

is the effective permittivity taking into account polarizability of the ions and molecules and

\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=\mu_{\alpha}-q_{\alpha}\psi(\mathbb{r})+\frac{\gamma_{\alpha}(\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))^{2}}{2}+k_{B}T\ln\left[\frac{\sinh(\beta p_{\alpha}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|)}{\beta p_{\alpha}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|}\right],\penalty\ \alpha=\pm,

(74)

\bar{\mu}_{0}(\mathbb{r})=\mu_{0}+\frac{\gamma_{0}(\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))^{2}}{2}+k_{B}T\ln\left[\frac{\sinh(\beta p_{0}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|)}{\beta p_{0}|\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r})|}\right]

(75)

are the intrinsic chemical potentials, where
p_{0}=e_{0}|\mathbb{s}_{0}|
and
\gamma_{0}=\beta\sigma_{0}^{2}e_{0}^{2}
are the permanent dipole moment and static polarizability of the molecules, respectively. The local concentrations of the species are

\bar{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})=\frac{\partial{P}}{\partial{\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})}}=c_{\alpha}\left(T,\bar{\mu}_{+}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{-}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{0}(\mathbb{r})\right),\penalty\ \alpha=\pm,0.

(76)

We would like to note that in case when
\gamma_{\pm}=\gamma_{0}=p_{\pm}=0
and
p_{0}=p\neq 0
and

P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+},\bar{\mu}_{-},\bar{\mu}_{0})=\frac{k_{B}T}{\Lambda_{+}^{3}}e^{\beta\bar{\mu}_{+}}+\frac{k_{B}T}{\Lambda_{-}^{3}}e^{\beta\bar{\mu}_{-}}+\frac{k_{B}T}{\Lambda_{0}^{3}}e^{\beta\bar{\mu}_{0}},

(77)

we obtain the well-known Poisson-Boltzmann-Langevin (PBL) equation
Abrashkin, Andelman, and Orland 2007
;
Coalson and Duncan 1996

-\nabla\cdot(\epsilon(\mathbb{r})\nabla\psi(\mathbb{r}))=q_{+}c_{+,b}e^{-\beta q_{+}\psi(\mathbb{r})}+q_{-}c_{-,b}e^{-\beta q_{-}\psi(\mathbb{r})},

(78)

where

\epsilon(\mathbb{r})=\varepsilon_{b}+\frac{c_{0,b}p^{2}}{k_{B}T}\mathcal{G}(\beta p\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{r})),

(79)

\mathcal{G}(x)=\sinh xL(x)/x^{2}
,
c_{0,b}
is the bulk concentration of the molecules. Note that we took into consideration the relations for bulk chemical potentials
\mu_{\pm}=k_{B}T\ln(c_{\pm,b}\Lambda_{\pm}^{3})
,
\mu_{0}=k_{B}T\ln(c_{0,b}\Lambda_{0}^{3})
. To take into account the excluded volume of the ions and molecules, we can use a three-component hard-sphere system as the reference system in the Percus-Yevick approximation determined by eqs. (
44
-
46
), where

n_{0}=\bar{c}_{+}+\bar{c}_{-}+\bar{c}_{0}
,
n_{1}=1/2\penalty\ d_{+}\bar{c}_{+}+1/2\penalty\ d_{-}\bar{c}_{-}+1/2\penalty\ d_{0}\bar{c}_{0}
,
n_{2}=\pi\penalty\ d_{+}^{2}\bar{c}_{+}+\pi\penalty\ d_{-}^{2}\bar{c}_{-}+\pi\penalty\ d_{0}^{2}\bar{c}_{0}
,
n_{3}=\pi/6\penalty\ d_{+}^{3}\bar{c}_{+}+\pi/6\penalty\ d_{-}^{3}\bar{c}_{-}+\pi/6\penalty\ d_{0}^{3}\bar{c}_{0}
(
d_{0}
is the effective diameter of molecules).

III
Stress tensor and macroscopic forces in ionic fluids

As it has been shown above, the GTP of an ionic fluid for all considered models in the mean-field approximation can be written as follows

\Omega=\int d\mathbb{r}\omega(\psi,\nabla\psi),

(80)

where we have introduced the GTP density

\omega=-\frac{\varepsilon_{b}(\nabla{\psi})^{2}}{2}-P.

(81)

We assume that the dependence of the GTP density on coordinates stems only from the electrostatic potential
\psi(\mathbb{r})
, so that for simplicity we consider the case
u_{\pm}(\mathbb{r})=0
. The case
u_{\pm}(\mathbb{r})\neq 0
will be considered below.
Variation of the GTP leads to self-consistent field equation (
39
), which can be written as follows

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\psi_{,i}}\right)-\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\psi}=0,

(82)

where
x_{i}
are the Cartesian coordinates and
\psi_{,i}=\partial{\psi}/\partial{x}_{i}
. Note that we use the Einstein rule, implying the summation over repeated indices. Then, with using eq. (
82
), we get

\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\psi_{,k}}\right)\psi_{,i}+\left(\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\psi_{,k}}\right)\frac{\partial\psi_{,k}}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(\psi_{,i}\frac{\partial{\omega}}{\partial{\psi_{,k}}}\right)

(83)

or

\frac{\partial\sigma_{ik}}{\partial{x_{k}}}=0,

(84)

where

\sigma_{ik}=\delta_{ik}\omega-\psi_{,i}\frac{\partial{\omega}}{\partial{\psi_{,k}}}.

(85)

Eq. (
84
) determines the local mechanical equilibrium condition for the ionic fluid with stress tensor,
\sigma_{ik}
. For model I, eq.(
85
) yields

\sigma_{ik}=\varepsilon_{b}\left(\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}^{2}\delta_{ik}\right)-\delta_{ik}P_{0},

(86)

where the first term is the standard Maxwell stress tensor
Landau, Lifshitz, and Pitaevskii 1984
in continuous dielectric media with the electrostatic field components
\mathcal{E}_{i}=-\psi_{,i}
, whereas the second term is the stress tensor of an ionic fluid with the local osmotic pressure
P_{0}(\mathbb{r})=P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{-}(\mathbb{r}))
,
\delta_{ik}
is the unit tensor (Kronecker symbol). For model II, based on the GTP (
54
) and intrinsic chemical potentials (
58
), we obtain the stress tensor with an account for the polarizabilities and permanent dipole moments of the ions. Using eq. (
85
), we obtain the expression for the stress tensor

\sigma_{ik}=\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{i}\mathcal{E}_{k}-\delta_{ik}\left(P_{0}+\frac{\varepsilon_{b}\mathcal{E}^{2}}{2}\right).

(87)

Note that in the case of unpolarizable ions (
p_{\pm}=\gamma_{\pm}=0
,
\epsilon=\varepsilon_{b}
), eq. (
87
) transforms into eq. (
86
). For model III, using eq. (
85
) and taking into account eqs. (
71
), (
74
), and (
75
), we arrive at the same expression (
87
) for the stress tensor with
P_{0}(\mathbb{r})=P(T,\bar{\mu}_{+}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{-}(\mathbb{r}),\bar{\mu}_{0}(\mathbb{r}))
.

We would like to note that the same approach was used in
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
to express the energy-momentum tensor of an arbitrary physical system through its Lagrangian (Noether’s theorem
Bogolyubov and Shirkov 1973
).
Note that even if we cannot obtain the GTP density from the first principles, we still can utilise eq. (
85
) to formulate the stress tensor. However, it would require
ad hoc
assumptions regarding the GTP functional dependence on the electrostatic potential and field.

Now let us consider the case when ions are under the influence of external potential forces with total potential energies
u_{\pm}(\mathbb{r})
. For example, these can be van der Waals or specific interactions between the ions and the charged surfaces (electrodes or membranes, for instance). In this case, the GTP density depends explicitly on the coordinates via the aforementioned potential energies. Analogous calculations result in the following equation

\frac{\partial\sigma_{ik}}{\partial{x_{k}}}-\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bar{c}_{\alpha}\frac{\partial u_{\alpha}}{\partial x_{i}}=0,

(88)

which determines the mechanical equilibrium condition of the ionic fluid in the presence of external volume forces.

Knowledge of the stress tensor allows us to calculate the macroscopic force acting on a conductive (electrode) or dielectric (a membrane or a colloid particle) body immersed in an ionic fluid. It can be calculated as the following integral over the surface of an immersed body

F_{i}=\oint\limits_{S}\sigma_{ik}n_{k}dS,

(89)

where
n_{k}
are the components of the external normal and
dS
is the elementary area. Note that in the absence of volume nonelectrostatic forces (
u_{\pm}(\mathbb{r})=0
), the integration in eq. (
89
) can be performed over any closed surface around a macroscopic body because the ionic fluid is in mechanical equilibrium
Landau, Lifshitz, and Pitaevskii 1984
. In the presence of nonelectrostatic volume forces, as it follows from the mechanical equilibrium, the macroscopic force can be calculated as follows

F_{i}=\oint\limits_{S}\sigma_{ik}n_{k}dS+\int\limits_{V}f_{i}dV,

(90)

where the first integral is taken over an arbitrary closed surface
S
around the macroscopic body, whereas the second integral – over the volume between surface
S
and the surface of the macroscopic body;
f_{i}=-\sum_{\alpha}\bar{c}_{\alpha}{\partial u_{\alpha}}/{\partial x_{i}}
are the components of the total nonelectrostatic volume density force. In order to calculate the macroscopic force by eq. (
89
) or (
90
), it is necessary to solve self-consistent field equation (
35
) with appropriate boundary conditions
1
1

1

The boundary conditions are determined by the nature of immersed body. The boundary conditions for dielectric or conductive body are standard
Landau, Lifshitz, and Pitaevskii 1984
. The boundary conditions for surfaces with regulated charge are proposed were
Podgornik 2018
.
to obtain the local osmotic pressure,
P_{0}(\mathbb{r})
, and local ionic concentrations,
\bar{c}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{r})
, of the ions and the electrostatic field on the body surface and around it. We would like to note that similar theories based on
ad hoc
assumptions were applied in
Neu 1999
;
Trizac and Raimbault 1999
;
Filippov et al. 2006
to describe the electrostatic interactions between charged particles in ionic fluid within the ideal gas and symmetric lattice gas models. We applied our theory to describe the electrosorption-iduced deformation of electrified porous carbon with nonconvex pores in electrolyte solutions
Kolesnikov, Mazur, and Budkov 2022
.

Now it is instructive to discuss how the general mean-field approximation of disjoining pressure for an ionic liquid in a slit nanopore can be derived. Placing the origin of
z
axis on one charged wall and another one at
z=H
, we can calculate the disjoining pressure
Derjaguin, Churaev, and Muller 1987
as follows

\Pi=-\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial H}-P_{b},

(91)

where
P_{b}=P(T,\mu_{+},\mu_{-},\mu_{0})
is the ionic fluid pressure in the bulk. Then, we have

\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial H}=\int\limits_{0}^{H}dz\frac{\delta\Omega}{\delta\psi(z)}\frac{\partial\psi(z)}{\partial H}+\omega-\psi^{\prime}\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\psi^{\prime}}\bigg|_{z=H}-\int\limits_{0}^{H}dz\sum\limits_{\alpha}\frac{\partial P}{\partial u_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial u_{\alpha}}{\partial H}

=\int\limits_{0}^{H}dz\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)\frac{\partial u_{\alpha}(z)}{\partial H}+\sigma_{zz}(H),

(92)

where we took into account that
{\partial P}/{\partial u_{\alpha}}=-{\partial P}/{\partial\bar{\mu}_{\alpha}}=-\bar{c}_{\alpha}
. Therefore, we eventually obtain the general expression for disjoining pressure

\Pi=-\sum\limits_{\alpha}\int\limits_{0}^{H}dz\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)\frac{\partial u_{\alpha}(z)}{\partial H}-\sigma_{zz}(H)-P_{b},

(93)

where
\sigma_{zz}(H)=\omega-\psi^{\prime}{\partial\omega}/{\partial\psi^{\prime}}|_{z=H}={\varepsilon_{b}\mathcal{E}^{2}(H)}/{2}-P_{0}(H)
is the normal stress at
z=H
.
Now let us assume that external potentials are created by identical walls, i.e.

u_{\alpha}(z)=\phi_{\alpha}(z)+\phi_{\alpha}(H-z),

(94)

where
\phi_{\alpha}
is the single wall potential, and taking into account that
\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)=\bar{c}_{\alpha}(H-z)
, we arrive at

\Pi=-\sum\limits_{\alpha}\int\limits_{0}^{H}dz\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)-\sigma_{zz}(H)-P_{b}.

(95)

Eq. (
95
) can be rewritten in a form that is more suitable for applications. Using the mechanical equilibrium equation

\frac{d\sigma_{zz}}{dz}-\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)u_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)=0,

(96)

after the integration from
z=H/2
to
z=H
we obtain

\sigma_{zz}(H)=\sigma_{zz}\left(\frac{H}{2}\right)+\sum\limits_{\alpha}\int\limits_{H/2}^{H}dz\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z)-\sum\limits_{\alpha}\int\limits_{0}^{H/2}dz\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z),

(97)

where we have taken into account eq. (
94
) and condition
\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)=\bar{c}_{\alpha}(H-z)
.
Substituting expression (
97
) for
\sigma_{zz}(H)
in eq.(
95

\Pi=P_{m}-P_{b}-2\sum\limits_{\alpha}\int\limits_{H/2}^{H}dz\bar{c}_{\alpha}(z)\phi_{\alpha}^{\prime}(z),

(98)

where
P_{m}=P_{0}\left({H}/{2}\right)

IV
Concluding remarks

In conclusion, we proposed a field-theoretical approach based on the thermodynamic perturbation theory allowing us to derive the grand thermodynamic potential of the inhomogeneous ionic fluid as a functional of electrostatic potential for an arbitrary reference system. We obtained the modified Poisson-Boltzmann equation as the Euler-Lagrange equation for the grand thermodynamic potential. Using Noether’s theorem, we derived a general mean-field expression for the stress tensor consistent with the respective modified Poisson-Boltzmann equation. We derived a general mean-field expression for the disjoining pressure of an ionic fluid in a slit pore. We applied the developed formalism to describe three ionic liquid models of practical importance: nonpolarizable models (including the Poisson-Boltzmann and Poisson-Fermi equations), polarizable models (the ions have nonzero permanent dipole moment or static polarizability), and ion-dipole mixtures (mixture of polar/polarizable ions and polar/polarizable molecules). For all these models, we derived modified Poisson-Boltzmann equations and respective stress tensors, which can be used in different applications, where it is necessary to estimate the electrostatic potential and ionic concentrations around dielectric or conductive bodies (electrodes, colloids, membranes, dust particles,
etc
.) immersed in an ionic fluid, as well as the macroscopic forces acting on them.

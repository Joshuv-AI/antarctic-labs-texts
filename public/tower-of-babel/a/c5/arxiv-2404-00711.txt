The explicit hypergeometric-modularity method I
Michael Allen, Brian Grove, Ling Long, Fang-Ting Tu

Abstract
. The theories of hypergeometric functions and modular forms are highly intertwined. For example, particular values of truncated hypergeometric functions and hypergeometric character sums are often congruent or equal to Fourier coefficients of modular forms. In this series of papers, we develop and explore an explicit “Hypergeometric-Modularity” method for associating a modular form to a given hypergeometric datum. In particular, for certain length three and four hypergeometric data we give an explicit method for finding a modular form f f such that the corresponding hypergeometric Galois representation has a subrepresentation isomorphic to the Deligne representation of f f . Our method utilizes Ramanujan’s theory of elliptic functions to alternative bases, commutative formal group laws, and supercongruences. As a byproduct, we give a collection of eta quotients with multiplicative coefficients constructed from hypergeometric functions. In the second paper, we discuss a number of applications, including explicit connections between hypergeometric values and periods of these explicit eta quotients as well as evaluation formulae for certain special L L -values.

2.1.
Notation and Hypergeometric Functions

Consider the hypergeometric datum

\textit{HD}=\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\},\boldsymbol{\beta}=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\}\}.

Throughout the paper we will assume that all

and at least two of the

are equal to

[
6
]
by Beukers–Heckman.

2.1.1.
Euler’s integral representation

The discussion of classical hypergeometric functions so far has involved formal power series in
z
which satisfy a certain Fuchsian differential equation. An alternate perspective on classical hypergeometric functions is from the integral representation of Euler. When
\text{Re}(q_{i})>\text{Re}(r_{i})>0
, there is an inductive formula to construct hypergeometric functions, see
[
5
, (2.2.2)]

{}_{1}F_{0}[r_{1};z]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(1-z)^{-r_{1}}

(2.1)

\,_{n}F_{n-1}\left[\begin{matrix}\,\,r_{1}&r_{2}&\cdots&r_{n}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
1&q_{2}&\cdots&q_{n}&\end{matrix}\;;\;z\right]=\\
\frac{\Gamma(q_{n})}{\Gamma(r_{n})\Gamma(q_{n}-r_{n})}\int_{0}^{1}t^{r_{n}}(1-t)^{q_{n}-r_{n}-1}\,_{n-1}F_{n-2}\left[\begin{matrix}\,\,r_{1}&r_{2}&\cdots&r_{n-1}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
1&q_{2}&\cdots&q_{n-1}\end{matrix}\;;\;tz\right]\frac{dt}{t},

where
\Gamma(x)
is the usual Gamma function. As we demonstrated in the introduction, this integral representation plays a key role in our method for associating a modular form to a hypergeometric datum.

2.1.2.
Hypergeometric character sums

In

\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}\left(\frac{i}{M}\right)(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left(\frac{x}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{M}^{i}\equiv x^{(q-1)\frac{i}{M}}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}},\,\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{M}].

For example, if

2.3.1.
Supercongruence background

A key step in the proof of

3.3.

The goal of the next few subsections is to show (

We start with notation. Let

The basic background is as follows. Assume
b_{n}\in R
for all

and consider

\omega(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}x^{n-1}{dx},\quad\ell(x)=\int\omega(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}}{n}x^{n}.

If there is another local uniformizer

such that

and

be a power series in

and

are isomorphic, c.f.
[
38
, A.3]
. See
[
38
, Appendix]
for basic examples and more details. Due to the modular form background of our setting, we will use the following version of the CFGL property, which gives
p
-adic analogues of the Hecke recursions satisfied by Hecke eigenforms.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
7
]
,
[
38
]
)
.

Let

and
\sigma
be as above. Let

for all

. Let

If there exists

such that for all

5.2.1.
The general case

We use the shorthand notation

and

. The proofs in this section rely heavily on the assumption

for all

, as this dictates the

-adic valuations of the hypergeometric coefficients as documented in

and

\mathit{HD}=\mathit{HD}_{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{a_{1}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}\lambda^{k}\left(G_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+k,\boldsymbol{\beta}+k)\right)+\sum_{k=a_{1}+1}^{a_{2}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{pr_{1}},

5.2.2.
The
\lambda=1
case

We will adopt a strategy used in
[
3
,
30
,
34
]
by constructing a rational function
R(t)\in\mathbb{Q}(t)
—depending on
p
—such that the sum of the residues of
R(t)
is congruent modulo
p
to
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)
. Thus, we will be able to translate the corresponding residue sum identity into our desired congruence
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Examining
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)
in (
5.14
) more closely, the desired rational function will satisfy the following conditions when we consider its reduction
\bar{R}(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}(t)
:

(1)

For each
k\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
R(t)
has a pole at
t=-k
. If
n_{k}
is the order of this pole, then
(R(t)(t+k)^{n_{k}})|_{t=-k}\equiv C\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}\pmod{p}
for some constant
C
independent of
k
.

(2)

The order
n_{k}
is equal to
2
for
k\in[0,\ldots,a_{1}]
and
1
for
k\in[a_{1}+1,\ldots,a_{2}]
. This will lead to the appearance of
G_{1}
in the first sum defining
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}
.

Additionally, we wish to balance the degrees of the numerator and denominator of
R(t)
so that the residue at infinity is zero for
\gamma(\mathit{HD})<1
and becomes nonzero exactly when
\gamma(\mathit{HD})=1
, as this will lead to the appearance of the
p
-linear term on the left-hand side of (
2.15
). The choice of such an
R(t)
is not unique, but each choice will give the same first
p
-adic digit for the
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}
error term.
We now define a rational function that can be used to handle all cases listed in Lemma
5.4
. We will need to track the number of elements of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
which are not equal to
1
, and so first we make the following definition:

Definition 5.1
.

For a fixed
\mathit{HD}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}\right\}
of length
n
, let
\hat{n}
be the largest index
2\leq\hat{n}\leq n
such that
q_{\hat{n}}<1
, or
0
if no such index exists.

We define a rational function by

(5.15)

R(t)=\frac{\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n}(t+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}{(t)_{a_{1}+1}^{2}(t+a_{1}+1)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(t+b_{1}+1)_{p-b_{1}-1}^{2}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{\hat{n}}(-t+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}}.

We note that
R(t)
is fully reduced, as the denominator has roots only at integers while the numerator only has roots at integer shifts of the
r_{i}
, which are non-integral.
Our broad strategy will be to consider the sum of the residues of
R(t)
two ways—over
\mathbb{C}
we will use standard residue calculus to show that the sum of the residues is zero if and only if
\gamma(\mathit{HD})\leq 1
, while
p
-adically we will show that the difference between this residue sum and
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)
will be small. Together, this will yield our desired congruence
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Our particular choice of
R(t)
in (
5.15
) is made to make both sides of this calculation as simple as possible. For example, the shifts of the Pochhammer symbols by
ip
in the denominator are not strictly necessary; we make this choice as it guarantees that the poles at each
t=k+ip
with
2\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq p-b_{i}-1
are simple. The perturbation in the numerator by
-r_{i}-p
is made so that the residues of these simple poles will be divisible by
p
. The function
R(t)
has partial fraction decomposition

(5.16)

\displaystyle R(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{a_{1}}\left(\frac{A_{k}}{(t+k)^{2}}+\frac{B_{k}}{t+k}\right)+\sum_{k=a_{1}+1}^{b_{1}}\frac{B_{k}}{t+k}+\sum_{k=b_{1}+1}^{p-1}\left(\frac{A_{k}}{(t+k)^{2}}+\frac{B_{k}}{t+k}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=2}^{\hat{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{p-b_{i}-1}\frac{C_{i,k}}{-t+k+ip}.

Going forward, we will separate the range
[0,\ldots,p-1]
into four intervals, as illustrated in
Figure

2
. Namely, we set

(5.17)

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=[0,\ldots,a_{1}],

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=[a_{1}+1,\ldots,a_{2}]

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=[a_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{1}],

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=[b_{1}+1,\ldots,p-1].

We will eventually relate this decomposition to
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)
by showing that the sum of the residues
B_{k}
over
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
each match to the corresponding sums in the definition of
E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}
. Before doing so, we show that the remaining residues vanish modulo
p
and so will not contribute
p
-adically.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathit{HD},M
, and
p
be as in
Lemma

5.4
, with the additional assumption that
p>\hat{n}+1
where
\hat{n}
is defined as in
5.1
. With

as in (
5.16
) and for all
2\leq i\leq\hat{n}
and
1\leq k\leq p-b_{i}-1

C_{i,k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Proof.

For each fixed pair
(i_{0},k_{0})
, the corresponding residue
C_{i_{0},k_{0}}
can be computed directly as

\displaystyle C_{i_{0},k_{0}}

\displaystyle=(-t+k_{0}+i_{0}p)R(t)\biggr|_{t=k_{0}+i_{0}p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n}(k_{0}+1-r_{i}+(i_{0}-1)p)}{(k_{0}+i_{0}p)_{a_{1}+1}^{2}(k_{0}+a_{1}+1+i_{0}p)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(k_{0}+b_{1}+1+i_{0}p)_{p-b_{1}-1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(-k_{0}+1)_{k_{0}-1}(1)_{p-b_{i_{0}}-k_{0}-1}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{\hat{n}}(-k_{0}+1+(i-i_{0})p)_{p-b_{i}-1}}.

The index of each Pochhammer symbol above is less than or equal to
p
, and so each symbol contains at most one multiple of
p
. As we saw in (
5.8
), for
0\leq a\leq p-1
,
p\mid(t)_{a}
if and only if the index

is larger than the first
p
-adic digit of the negative of the argument,
[-t]_{0}
. In this case, the multiple of
p
appearing in
(t)_{a}
is exactly

. We can use this term by term to evaluate the
p
-adic valuation of the above expression for

. First we note that, together,

(k_{0}+i_{0}p)_{a_{1}+1}(k_{0}+a_{1}+1+i_{0}p)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(k_{0}+b_{1}+1+i_{0}p)_{p-b_{1}-1}

is a product over all
(k_{0}+i_{0}p+j)
with
j
ranging from
0
to

. In particular, exactly one of these three Pochhammer symbols is divisible by
p
, and the
p
-divisible term is exactly

(k_{0}+i_{0}p)+[-k_{0}-i_{0}p]_{0}=(k_{0}+i_{0}p)+(p-k_{0})=(i_{0}+1)p.

Because
0<i_{0}+1\leq\hat{n}+1<p
, the
p
-adic valuation of
(i_{0}+1)p
is exactly one. We could be precise about which of the three Pochhammer symbols this term will appear in for our fixed

, but it will be enough for our purposes to conclude that

(5.18)

\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left((k_{0}+i_{0}p)_{a_{1}+1}^{2}(k_{0}+a_{1}+1+i_{0}p)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(k_{0}+b_{1}+1+i_{0}p)_{p-b_{1}-1}^{2}\right)\leq 2.

Both of the terms
(-k_{0}+1)_{k_{0}-1}=(-1)^{k_{0}-1}(k_{0}-1)!
and
(1)_{p-b_{i_{0}}-k_{0}-1}=(p-b_{i_{0}}-k_{0}-1)!
have

-adic valuation zero. Thus, we now need only consider

\frac{\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n}(k_{0}+1-r_{i}+(i_{0}-1)p)}{\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{\hat{n}}(-k_{0}+1+(i-i_{0})p)_{p-b_{i}-1}}.

In the denominator, the first
p
-adic digit of the negative of the argument is

[k_{0}-1]_{0}=k_{0}-1.

This is smaller than the index if and only if
k_{0}<p-b_{i}
, in which case the multiple of
p
appearing is
(i-i_{0})p
which has valuation
1
as
-p<i-i_{0}<p
and
i\neq i_{0}
. Let
1\leq\ell\leq\hat{n}
be the largest index such that

. Then

(5.19)

\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left(\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{\hat{n}}(-k_{0}+1+(i-i_{0})p)_{p-b_{i}-1}\right)=\ell-2

where we use

to denote the

-adic valuation. The

and

.

The numerator behaves quite similarly. By an analogous argument, we find that

if and only if

, in which case the multiple of

appearing is

. We can not guarantee that

has

-adic valuation zero, but it must have a nonnegative valuation which will be sufficient. As

, the inequality

must hold for all

. By hypothesis,

holds for all

. Therefore,

\nu_{p}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n}(k_{0}+1-r_{i}+(i_{0}+1)p)_{p-a_{i}-1}\right)\geq\ell+1.

Combining (
5.18
), (
5.19
), and (

) then yields

\mathrm{ord}_{p}(C_{i_{0},k_{0}})\geq\ell+1-2-(\ell-2)=1,

as was to be shown.
∎

Lemma 5.6

Let

Proof.

The proof is essentially a simpler version of the argument we just made to show

(5.21)

B_{k}=\frac{d}{dt}(t+k)^{2}R(t)\biggr|_{t=-k}=(t+k)^{2}R(t)\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+k)^{2}R(t)}{(t+k)^{2}R(t)}\biggr|_{t=-k}.

We rewrite

in this manner as it will be simpler to evaluate the
p
-adic valuations of the terms arising from
(t+k)^{2}R(t)
and its logarithmic derivative separately. In particular, we note that logarithmic differentiation transforms the Pochhammer symbol
(t)_{a}
into

\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t)_{a}}{(t)_{a}}=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\frac{1}{t+i}.

Therefore, the
p
-adic valuation of the logarithmic derivative, after specializing
t=-k
, will be bound below by the negative of the largest exponent appearing on any multiple of
p
in the original Pochhammer symbol.

We turn our attention to the
p
-adic valuation of

\displaystyle(t+k)^{2}R(t)\biggr|_{t=-k}=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i=2}^{n}(-k+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}{(-k)_{a_{1}}^{2}(-k+a_{1}+1)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(-k+b_{1}+1)_{k-b_{1}-1}^{2}(1)_{p-k-1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{\prod_{i=2}^{\hat{n}}(k+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}}.

This valuation can be computed via the same direct approach we used in the proof of
Lemma

5.5
, and the results are quite similar. As such, we omit many of the details. First, we find

\mathrm{ord}_{p}((-k)_{a_{1}}^{2}(-k+a_{1}+1)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(-k+b_{1}+1)_{k-b_{1}-1}^{2}(1)_{p-k+1}^{2})=0,

as the one guaranteed multiple of
p
is at the residue
t+k
that we are removing. Next, the terms
(k+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}
contain a multiple of
p
, namely
p(i+1)
which has valuation
1
, if and only if
k>b_{i}
. Thus, letting
2\leq\ell\leq\hat{n}
be the maximal index for which
k>b_{\ell}
, we find

\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{\hat{n}}(k+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}\right)=\ell-1

The terms
(-k+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{1}-1}
appearing in the numerator will be divisible by
p
if and only if
k>a_{i}
. As we saw in the proof of the previous Lemma, our hypotheses ensure this holds for all
1\leq i\leq 2+\ell
. The multiple of
p
which does appear in this case is
-p(1+r_{i})
, which has
p
-adic valuation at least
1
. As this valuation could a priori be larger than
1
, we define

m_{p}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mathrm{ord}_{p}(1+r_{i}).

We conclude

\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n}(-k+a_{i}+1-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}\right)\geq\ell+1+m_{p}.

Therefore,

\mathrm{ord}_{p}\left((t+k)^{2}R(t)\biggr|_{t=-k}\right)\geq 2+m_{p}.

Additionally, we have seen that only linear powers of
p
appear in the denominator
(t+k)^{2}R(t)
, and so the maximal exponent of
p
appearing overall will be
1+m_{p}
. As noted above, this guarantees that

v_{p}\left(\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+k)^{2}R(t)}{(t+k)^{2}R(t)}\right)\geq-1-m_{p}.

Taken together,
v_{p}(B_{k})\geq 1
, as was to be shown.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

With
\mathit{HD},\,M
, and
p
as in
Lemma

5.5
and

where

Proof.

When
\lambda=1
, the error term (

With our rational function

expressed in the partial fraction decomposition (

(5.22)

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}B_{k}+\sum_{i=2}^{\hat{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{p-b_{i}-1}C_{i,k}=-\mathrm{Res}_{t=\infty}(R(t)).

We have already considered the
p

as well as

for

for

First, for

A_{k}=\frac{\prod_{i=2}^{n}(-k+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}{(k!)^{2}(a_{1}-k)!^{2}(-k+a_{1}+1)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}(-k+b_{1}+1)_{p-b_{1}-1}^{2}\prod_{i=2}^{n}(k+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}}.

For example,

\displaystyle(-k+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a_{i}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(-k-pr_{i})}{\Gamma_{p}(-k+1-r_{i}-p)}\left(-pr_{i}\right)^{\nu(p-a_{i}-1,p-a_{i}-1+k)}

A_{k}=\frac{(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+b_{i}}\Gamma_{p}(-k+b_{1}+1)\prod_{i=2}^{n}\Gamma_{p}(-k-pr_{i})\Gamma_{p}(k+1+ip)}{\Gamma_{p}^{2}(k+1)\Gamma_{p}(a_{1}-k+1)\Gamma_{p}^{2}(p-k)\prod_{i=2}^{n}\Gamma_{p}(-k+1-r_{i}-p)\Gamma_{p}(k-b_{i}+(1+i)p)}

5.8

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84526pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:p-gamma-reflect}}}{{=}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=2}^{n}b_{i}}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(k-a_{1})\Gamma_{p}^{2}(k+1-p)\prod_{i=2}^{n}\Gamma_{p}(k+r_{i}+p)\Gamma_{p}(k+1+ip)}{\Gamma_{p}^{2}(k+1)\Gamma_{p}(k-b_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{n}\Gamma_{p}(k+1+pr_{i})\Gamma_{p}(k-b_{i}+(1+i)p)}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:poch-p-gamma}}}{{\equiv}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=2}^{n}b_{i}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)\pmod{p}.

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to-k}\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+k)^{2}R(t)\right)=A_{k}\lim_{t\to-k}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left((t+k)^{2}R(t)\right)}{(t+k)^{2}R(t)}.

\displaystyle\frac{B_{k}}{A_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{p-a_{i}-1}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}{(t+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}-2\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t)_{k}}{(t)_{k}}-2\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+k+1)_{a_{1}-k}}{(t+k+1)_{a_{1}-k}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+a_{1}+1)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}}{(t+a_{1}+1)_{b_{1}-a_{1}}}-2\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+b_{1}+1)_{p-b_{1}-1}}{(t+b_{1}+1)_{p-b_{1}-1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(-t+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}}{(-t+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}}\biggr|_{t=-k}.

Each of these logarithmic derivatives can be evaluated using (
5.9

each of the

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(t+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}{(t+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}

\displaystyle\equiv G_{1}(k+1)-G_{1}(k+r_{i})\pmod{p}.

After computing an analogous reduction we find

\frac{B_{k}}{A_{k}}\equiv-G_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+k,\boldsymbol{\beta}+k)\pmod{p}.

Thus,

B_{k}\equiv(-1)^{1+\sum_{i=2}^{n}b_{i}}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}G_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+k,\boldsymbol{\beta}+k)\pmod{p}.

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=(t+k)R(t)\biggr|_{t=-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i=2}^{n}(-k+1-r_{i}-p)_{p-a_{i}-1}}{(-k)_{a_{1}+1}^{2}(-k+a_{1}+1)_{k-a_{1}-1}(b_{1}-k)!(-k+b_{1}+1)_{p-b_{i}-1}^{2}\prod\limits_{i=2}^{n}(k+1+ip)_{p-b_{i}-1}}

. On

5.5
,

5.6

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Res}_{t=\infty}(R(t))

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{a_{2}}B_{k}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv-(-1)^{\sum_{i=2}^{n}b_{i}}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)\biggr(\sum_{k=0}^{a_{1}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}G_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}+k,\boldsymbol{\beta}+k)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{k=a_{1}+1}^{a_{2}}\frac{(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{k}}{(\boldsymbol{\beta})_{k}}\frac{1}{pr_{1}}\biggr)\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\equiv-(-1)^{\sum_{i=2}^{n}b_{i}}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)E_{\mathrm{Dwork}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)\pmod{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In light of
Section

5.2.1
, we are particularly interested in cases where this error term

vanishes modulo

, which will occur when

is holomorphic at

. Recalling
\gamma(\mathit{HD})=-1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(q_{i}-r_{i})
, this vanishing of

can be rephrased purely arithmetically as follows:

Corollary 5.8
.

With
\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},M
, and

as in

5.5
, if

we have

F_{s+1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)\equiv F_{s}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)F_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};1)\pmod{p^{2}}.

Proof.

We assume that
\gamma(\mathit{HD})\leq 1
, namely
\sum_{i=1}^{n}q_{i}-r_{i}\leq 2
, and multiply both sides by
p-1
. Recalling that
a_{i}=(p-1)r_{i}
and
b_{i}=(p-1)q_{i}
, our equality then becomes

\displaystyle 2p+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}-b_{i})

\displaystyle\geq 2.

It is easy to check that the left-hand side of the above inequality is exactly the degree of the denominator of

minus the degree of the numerator, and so this final inequality implies
\mathrm{Res}_{t=\infty}(R(t))
being zero and hence for the error term

to vanish modulo

.
∎

5.3.1.
The general case

Lemma 5.9
.

Let

. Further set

Remark 5.3.

Formally,

Proof of
Lemma

5.9
.

As before, we let

be the minimal index such that

. If no such

exists, instead take

. By definition,

\displaystyle H_{p}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};\lambda;\bar{\omega}_{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\omega_{p}^{-k}\left((-1)^{n}\lambda\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\mathfrak{g}\left(\omega_{p}^{-(p-1)r_{j}-k}\right)}{\mathfrak{g}\left(\omega_{p}^{-(p-1)r_{j}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle H_{p}(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta};\lambda;\bar{\omega}_{p})

2.5
) to rewrite the above expression in terms of

. To do so we must have our exponent written in the range

may be required if the exponent does not fall into this range. With this in mind and recalling that

(5.28)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}\left(\omega_{p}^{(p-1)(1-r_{j})+k}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{g}\left(\omega_{p}^{(p-1)(-r_{j})+k}\right)&0\leq k\leq a_{j};\\

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\pi_{p}^{r_{j}(p-1)-k}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{r_{j}(p-1)-k}{p-1}\right)&0\leq k\leq a_{j};\\

We can compute

All together we have

(5.29)

\displaystyle H_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-p}\sum_{k=0}^{p-2}\omega_{p}^{k}((-1)^{n}\lambda)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(-p)^{\nu(k,a_{j})}\frac{\Gamma_{p}\left(r_{j}-\frac{k}{p-1}+\nu(k,a_{j})\right)}{\Gamma_{p}(r_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}(-p)^{\nu(k,a_{j})}\Gamma_{p}\biggr(r_{j}-

Turning our attention to the

\displaystyle\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(p\left(r_{j}-\frac{k}{p-1}\right)\right)^{\nu(k,a_{j})}}{\prod_{i=1}^{\hat{n}}\left(p\left(q_{i}-\frac{k}{p-1}\right)\right)^{\nu(k+1,b_{i})}}\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-\frac{k}{p-1},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-\frac{k}{p-1}}\right).

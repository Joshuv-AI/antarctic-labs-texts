Abstract
. Let G = ℤ 2 G=\mathbb{Z}_{2} act freely on a finitistic space X X . If the mod 2 cohomology of X X is isomorphic to the real projective space ℝ ​ ℙ 2 ​ n + 1 \mathbb{RP}^{2n+1} (resp. complex projective space ℂ ​ ℙ 2 ​ n + 1 \mathbb{CP}^{2n+1} ) then the mod 2 cohomology of orbit spaces of these free actions are ℝ ​ ℙ 1 × ℂ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n} (resp. ℝ ​ ℙ 2 × ℍ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n} ) [ 7 ] . In this paper, we have discussed converse of these result. We have showed that if the mod 2 cohomology of the orbit space X / G X/G is ℝ ​ ℙ 1 × ℂ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n} (resp. ℝ ​ ℙ 2 × ℍ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n} ) then the mod 2 cohomology of X X is ℝ ​ ℙ 2 ​ n + 1 \mathbb{RP}^{2n+1} or 𝕊 1 × ℂ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n} (resp. ℂ ​ ℙ 2 ​ n + 1 \mathbb{CP}^{2n+1} or 𝕊 2 × ℍ ​ ℙ n \mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n} ). A partial converse of free involutions on the product of projective spaces ℝ ​ ℙ n × ℝ ​ ℙ 2 ​ m + 1 \mathbb{RP}^{n}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2m+1} (resp. ℂ ​ ℙ n × ℂ ​ ℙ 2 ​ m + 1 \mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1} ) are also discussed.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a compact Lie group acting on a space
X
. An interesting problem is to classify
X
when
G
acts freely on
X
and the orbit space
X/G
is known. First, J. C. Su
[
9
]
worked in this direction. He proved that if
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
or
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, acts freely on a space
X
and the orbit space
X/G
is the mod 2 or integral cohomology projective space
\mathbb{FP}^{n}
,
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, then the space
X
is the mod 2 cohomology
n
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
or integral cohomology
(2n+1)
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
. Su also proved that if
G=\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
q
an odd prime, and
X/G=L_{q}^{2n+1}
(lens space) then
X
is the mod
q
cohomology
(2n+1)
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
. It has also been proved that if
G=\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
X/G=\mathbb{HP}^{n}
(quaternion projective space), then
X
is either integral cohomology
\mathbb{S}^{4n+3}
or
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n}

[
4
]
. Harvey
[
2
]
proved that for semi-free actions of
G=\mathbb{S}^{1}
when the orbit space
X/G
is a
simply connected 4-manifold, then the total space is a simply connected 5-manifold.

The orbit spaces of free actions of
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on projective spaces
X=\mathbb{FP}^{2n+1}
and the product of projective spaces
X=\mathbb{FP}^{n}\times\mathbb{FP}^{m}
, where
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, have been discussed in
[
7
,
8
]
. For
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
X=\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}
(resp.
\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
), it is proved that the mod 2 cohomology of the orbit space
X/G
is
\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}
(resp.
\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n}
)
[
7
]
.
We have discussed converse of these results. We have determined the cohomology classification of a finitistic space
X
if the mod 2 cohomology of orbit space
X/G
is
\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n}
.
For
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
X=\mathbb{RP}^{n}\times\mathbb{RP}^{2m+1}
(resp.
\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{2m+1}
), one of the possibilities of the mod 2 cohomology of the orbit space is
\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{RP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{m}
(resp.
\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{HP}^{m}
)
[
8
]
. In this paper, we have also determined
X
equipped with a free action of
G
when the mod 2 cohomology of the orbit space
X/G
is
\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{RP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{m}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{HP}^{m}
. This gives a partial converse of results proved in
[
8
]
.

All spaces are assumed to finitistic connected spaces. Recall that a paracompact
Hausdorff space
X
is said to be finitistic if every open cover of
X
has a finite dimensional open refinement.
All compact spaces and all finite dimensional paracompact spaces are finitistic spaces. We have considered Čech cohomology with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Throughout the paper,
X\sim_{2}Y
means
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong H^{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
We have proved the following results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
act freely on a finitistic connected space
X
with
X/G\sim_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. Then either
X\sim_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}
or
X\sim_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
act freely on a finitistic connected space
X
with
X/G\sim_{2}\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n}
. Then
X\sim_{2}\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{HP}^{n}
or
X\sim_{2}\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
act freely on a finitistic connected space
X
with
X/G\sim_{2}\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{RP}^{n}\times\mathbb{CP}^{m}
and
\pi_{1}(B_{G})
act trivially on

H^{*}(X)
. Then the cohomology algebra of
X
is one of the following:

(i)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x,y,z]/\langle x^{n+1},y^{m+1},z^{2}+\alpha x^{2}+\beta y\rangle
, where
\deg x=\deg z=1
,
\deg y=2
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
;

(ii)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x,y,z]/\langle x^{2},y^{m+1},z^{2}+\alpha y^{n}\rangle
, where
\deg x=1,\deg y=2,\deg z=n
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\alpha=0
if
n>m
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
act freely on a finitistic connected space
X
with
X/G\sim_{2}\mathbb{RP}^{2}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}\times\mathbb{HP}^{m}
and
\pi_{1}(B_{G})
acts trivially on

H^{*}(X)
. Then the cohomology algebra of
X
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x,y,z]/\langle x^{n+1},y^{m+1},z^{2}+\alpha x^{2}+\beta y\rangle,

where
\deg x=2,\deg y=4,\deg z=2
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we have recalled some results and definitions which are used to prove our main results.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and
G\hookrightarrow E_{G}\to B_{G}
be the universal principal
G
-bundle, where
B_{G}
is the classifying space. Suppose
G
acts freely on a finitistic space
X
. The associated bundle
X\hookrightarrow(X\times E_{G})/{G}\to B_{G}
is a fibre bundle with fibre
X
. Put
X_{G}=(X\times E_{G})/{G}
. Then the bundle
X\hookrightarrow X_{G}\to B_{G}
is called the Borel fibration. We consider the Leray-Serre spectral sequence for the Borel fibration. If
\pi_{1}(B_{G})
acts trivially on
H^{*}(X)
, then the system of local coefficients on
B_{G}
is simple and the
E_{2}
-term of the Leray-Serre spectral sequence corresponding to the Borel fibration is given by

E_{2}^{k,l}=H^{k}(B_{G})\otimes H^{l}(X).

This sequence converges to
H^{*}(X_{G})
as an algebra.
We recall some results which are needed to prove our results:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X_{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}B_{G}
be the Borel fibration. Suppose that the system of local coefficients on
B_{G}
is simple, then the edge homomorphisms

\displaystyle H^{k}(B_{G})\cong E_{2}^{k,0}\longrightarrow E_{3}^{k,0}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow E_{k}^{k,0}\longrightarrow E_{k+1}^{k,0}=E_{\infty}^{k,0}\subset H^{k}(X_{G})

and

H^{l}(X_{G})\longrightarrow E_{\infty}^{0,l}=E_{l+2}^{0,l}\subset E_{l+1}^{0,l}\subset\cdots\subset E_{2}^{0,l}\cong H^{l}(X)

are the homomorphisms

\pi^{*}_{k}:H^{k}(B_{G})\to H^{k}(X_{G})~~~\textrm{and}~~~i^{*}_{l}:H^{l}(X_{G})\to H^{l}(X).

For details about spectral sequences, we refer
[
5
]
. Let
G
act freely on
X
and
h:X_{G}\rightarrow X/G
be the map induced by
G
-equivariant projection
X\times E_{G}\rightarrow X
. Then
h
is a homotopy equivalence
[
1
]
. Now, we recall Gysin sequence of sphere bundles.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
act freely on a finitistic space
X
. The Gysin sequence of the sphere bundle
G\hookrightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}X/G
is:

\displaystyle\cdots\longrightarrow H^{i-1}(X/G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}(X/G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{*}_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{i}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}(X/G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i+1}(X/G)\longrightarrow\cdots

which start with

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle H^{0}(X/G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{*}_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{0}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{0}(X/G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(X/G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p^{*}_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(X)\longrightarrow\cdots

where
\cup:H^{i}(X/G)\to H^{i+1}(X/G)
maps
x\to xe
,
e\in H^{1}(X/G)
denotes the image of
1
under the map
\cup:H^{0}(X/G)\to H^{1}(X/G)
and

is called the Euler class of the sphere bundle.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
act freely on a finitistic space
X
. Suppose that
H^{j}(X)=0
for all
j>n
, then
H^{j}(X/G)=0
for all
j>n
.

3.
Proof of main Theorems

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As
X/G\sim_{2}\mathbb{RP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}
, we have
H^{*}(X/G)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[a,b]/\langle a^{2},b^{n+1}\rangle,

\deg a=1
and
\deg b=2
. By connectedness of

and the exactness of Gysin sequence of
G
-bundle
G\hookrightarrow X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}X/G
, we get

p^{*}_{0}
is an isomorphism and the Euler class
\cup(1)
is
a
. This implies that for

and
\rho_{2n+1}:H^{2n+1}(X)\to H^{2n+1}(X/G)
are isomorphisms. Consequently,
H^{2i+1}(X)\cong H^{2n+1}(X)\cong H^{2i+2}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
with bases
\{u_{2i+1}\}
,
\{u_{2n+1}\}
and
\{x^{i+1}\}
, respectively, where
x=p^{*}_{2}(b)
,
\rho_{2i+1}(u_{2i+1})=ab^{i}
,
\rho_{2n+1}(u_{2n+1})=ab^{n}
. By Proposition
2.3
,
H^{i}(X)=0
for
i>2n+1
. Now, we compute the cohomology algebra of
X
. The Leray-Serre spectral sequence associated to the Borel fibration
X\hookrightarrow X_{G}\to B_{G}
converges to
H^{*}(X_{G})
. It is easy to see that
G
acts trivially on
H^{*}(X)
. Thus, the
E_{2}
-term

of the Leray-Serre spectral sequence associated to the Borel fibration
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X_{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}B_{G}
is
H^{s}(B_{G})\otimes H^{t}(X)
. By the edge homomorphism, we get
d_{r}(1\otimes x)=0
for all

and
d_{r^{\prime}}(1\otimes u_{1})\not=0
for some
r^{\prime}>0
. So,
d_{2}(1\otimes u_{1})=t^{2}\otimes 1
. This gives that
x^{i}u_{1}\not=0
, and hence
u_{2i+1}=x^{i}u_{1}
for all

. Clearly,
x^{n+1}=0
. If

then
X\sim_{2}\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{n}
. If
u_{1}^{2}\not=0

X\sim_{2}\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}
. ∎

∎

-copies) with basis

, respectively. Also,

. Therefore,

\displaystyle H^{4i+1}(X)=H^{4i+3}(X)=0

\displaystyle H^{4i}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{2}\,(2m+n-2i+2\text{-copies}),\text{ and}

ASYMPTOTICS FOR EXPONENTIAL FUNCTIONALS OF RANDOM WALKS
By Wei Xu

Abstract
This paper provides a detailed description for the asymptotics of exponential functionals of random walks with light/heavy tails. We give the convergence rate based on the key observation that the asymptotics depends on the sample paths with either slowly decreasing local minimum or final value below a low level. Also, our thoughtful analysis of the interrelationship between the local minimum and the final value provides the exact expression for the limiting coefficients in terms of some transformations of the random walk.

Introduction

Exponential functionals of random walks have been studied deeply in the past decades because of their wide and important applications in various fields such as mathematical finance, physics and population evolution.
In the general setting, for a one-dimension random walk
S
generated by a sequence of independent copies

\displaystyle I_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{-S_{k}},\quad n=1,2,\cdots

According to the Blumenthal zero-one law, the probability that

is finite,
\mathbf{P}(I_{\infty}<\infty)

Furthermore, the long-term behavior of

S
does not drift to infinity.
In this case, much attention has been drawn to the speed at which

increases to infinity, especially the decay rate of the following expectation

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n})\big]=\mathbf{E}\Big[F\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{-S_{k}}\Big)\Big]

(1.1)

The feature of this work is that both the convergence rate and the expression of the limiting coefficient in each regime are given.
Roughly speaking, the expectation (
1.1
) decays at some exponential rate with different regularly varying modifying factor, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]\sim\psi_{n}\cdot\varrho^{n},

where
\psi_{n}
is a regularly varying sequence vanishing at infinity and determined by the fluctuation of the random walk
S
under the probability law
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}

Particularly, when

the comparison with the asymptotics of the first entrance time
\tau_{0}^{-}

shows that for some constant

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]\sim C\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

Our methodology is based on the fluctuation theory for random walks.
There are two major challenges, which are also two key steps, in the asymptotic analysis of the expectation (
1.1

The first one is to find out the sample paths of random walk that make the main contribution to the expectation (
1.1
).
Enlightened by the asymptotic analysis of survival probabilities of Galton-Watson processes in an i.i.d. random environment; see

More precisely, sample paths with slowly decreasing local minimum make the main contribution in Case I and III; the expectation (
1.1
) in Case II mainly replies on sample paths with either the local minimum attained at the beginning of the time interval or the final value below a low level. In Case IV, the main contribution is made by sample paths with early large step.
The key sample paths in Case V not only have early large step and low final value but also decay slowly before the large step.
Different to other cases, the contribution of any sample path can not be asymptotically ignored in Case VI.
The second challenge is to provide an exact expression of the limiting coefficients.
In order to achieve it, we decompose each key sample path into several parts at its local minimum and then seek out the subdivisions that make the main contribution to the expectation (
1.1
).
For instance, the contribution of the key sample paths after large time
K
is negligible in Case I, III and IV, i.e., the impact of
I_{n}
can be well approximated by that of
I_{K}
.
However, in Case II and V the key sample paths make the main contribution to the expectation (
1.1
) at both the beginning and the end of the time interval, i.e., the impact of
I_{n}
can be well approximated by that of
I_{K}+(I_{n}-I_{n-K})
for large
K
.
Finally, we give the representations for the limiting coefficients in terms of some transformations of these subdivisions.

2
Preliminaries and main results

In this section we first introduce some basic notation and elements of fluctuation theory for random walks.
We then provide the main results in this paper about the asymptotic behavior of exponential functionals of random walks.

2.1
Random walks

Suppose that the random walk
S
is defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbf{P})
.
Let
\mathscr{F}^{S}
and
(\mathscr{F}^{S}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra and the filtration generated by
S
, i.e.,
\mathscr{F}^{S}:=\sigma(S_{k}:k=0,1,\cdots)
and
\mathscr{F}^{S}_{n}:=\sigma(S_{k}:k=0,1,\cdots,n)
.
For any probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathbf{P}_{\mu}
and
\mathbf{E}_{\mu}
the law and expectation of the random walk
S
with initial state
S_{0}
distributed as
\mu
.
When
\mu=\delta_{x}
is a Dirac measure at point
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\mathbf{P}_{x}
for
\mathbf{P}_{\delta_{x}}
and
\mathbf{E}_{x}
for
\mathbf{E}_{\delta_{x}}
.
For simplicity, we also write
\mathbf{P}
for
\mathbf{P}_{0}
and
\mathbf{E}
for
\mathbf{E}_{0}
.

According to its behavior as
n\to\infty
, the random walk
S
can be exactly classified into three types:
(i) drifts to
\infty
:
S_{n}\to\infty
a.s.; (ii) drifts to
-\infty
:
S_{n}\to-\infty
a.s.; (iii) oscillation:
\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_{n}=-\liminf_{n\to\infty}S_{n}=\infty
a.s.
We write
M:=\{M_{n}:n=1,2,\cdots\}
and
L:=\{L_{n}:n=1,2,\cdots\}
for the
running maximum
and
minimum processes
respectively,

\displaystyle M_{n}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}S_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L_{n}:=\min_{1\leq i\leq n}S_{i}.

In addition to
M_{n}
and
L_{n}
, we will also use the following two random times:

\displaystyle\sigma^{+}_{n}:=\inf\big\{0\leq i\leq n:S_{i}=S_{0}\vee M_{n}\big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma^{-}_{n}:=\inf\big\{0\leq i\leq n:S_{i}=S_{0}\wedge L_{n}\big\}

the
first times
up to time
n
that the maximum and minimum are attained.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, denote by
\tau_{x}^{+}
and
\tau_{x}^{-}
the
first entrance times
of
S
in
(x,\infty)
and
(-\infty,x)
respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tau_{x}^{+}:=\inf\{n>0:S_{n}>x\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tau_{x}^{-}:=\inf\{n>0:S_{n}<x\}.

In the sequel, we always denote by
\tilde{S}
an independent copy of
S
. For the quantities introduced to
S
, the corresponding ones for
\tilde{S}
are denoted by tildes, for instance,
\tilde{I}
and
\tilde{\tau}_{x}^{-}
.

The
renewal function

V
associated with the strict descending ladder height process is defined by
V(x)=0
if
x<0
and

\displaystyle V(x):=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(S_{\gamma_{n}^{-}}\geq-x),\quad x\geq 0,

where
\{\gamma_{i}^{-}:i=0,1,\cdots\}
are
strict descending ladder epochs
of
S
with
\gamma_{0}^{-}=0
and

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}^{-}:=\inf\big\{n>\gamma_{i-1}^{-}:S_{n}<S_{\gamma_{i-1}^{-}}\big\},\quad i\geq 1.

It is obvious that
V
is a non-decreasing right-continuous function with
V(0)=1
.
Using the duality lemma, we also have

\displaystyle V(x)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\geq-x,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\geq-x,M_{n}<0).

When the random walk
S
drifts to
-\infty
, we have
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}]<\infty
and
V(x)=\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}]/\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{-}]
for any
x\geq 0
.

The
dual process
of
S
is denoted by
\hat{S}
, that is
\hat{S}=-S
when the starting point is
0
.
Let
\hat{\mathbf{P}}_{x}
be the law of
\hat{S}
under
\mathbf{P}_{-x}
.
For the quantities introduced to
S
, the corresponding ones for
\hat{S}
are denoted by hats, for instance
\hat{I}
,
\hat{\tau}_{x}^{-}
and so on. Specially, the renewal process
\hat{V}
associated with the strict descending ladder height process of
\hat{S}
is equal to the renewal process associated with the strict ascending ladder height process of
S
, i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{V}(x)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\leq x,\sigma_{n}^{+}=n)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\leq x,L_{n}>0).

When
S
drifts to
\infty
, we also have
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{+}]<\infty
and
\hat{V}(x)=\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{+}]/\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{+}]
for any
x\geq 0
.

If the random walk
S
does not drift to
-\infty
, the process
\{V(S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{0}^{-}>n\}}:n=0,1,\cdots\}
is a
\mathbf{P}_{x}
-martingale for any
x\geq 0
.
In this case, we introduce a probability
\mathbf{P}^{\uparrow}_{x}
on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{S})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\uparrow}_{x}(A):=\int_{A}V(S_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{0}^{-}>n\}}\frac{d\mathbf{P}_{x}}{V(x)},\quad A\in\mathscr{F}^{S}_{n},n\geq 0.

It is usually well-known as
Doob’s
h
-transform
of
\mathbf{P}_{x}
. Particularly, under
\mathbf{P}^{\uparrow}
the process
S
turns to be a homogeneous Markov process on
[0,\infty)
with transition function

\displaystyle p^{\uparrow}(x,dy):=\frac{V(y)}{V(x)}\mathbf{P}(x+X\in dy),\quad x,y\geq 0.

Similarly, in the case that
\hat{S}
does not drift to
-\infty
, for any
x\geq 0
we can also introduce Doob’s
h
-transform of
\hat{\mathbf{P}}_{x}
on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\hat{S}}_{n})
defined by the renewal function
\hat{V}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{\downarrow}_{x}(A):=\int_{A}\hat{V}(\hat{S}_{n})\mathbf{1}_{\{\hat{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n\}}\frac{d\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathbf{P}_{x}}{\hat{V}(x)},\quad A\in\mathscr{F}^{\hat{S}}_{n},n\geq 0.

Under
\mathbf{P}^{\downarrow}
the process
\hat{S}
turns to be a homogeneous Markov process on
[0,\infty)
with transition function

\displaystyle p^{\downarrow}(x,dy):=\frac{\hat{V}(y)}{\hat{V}(x)}\mathbf{P}(x-X\in dy),\quad x,y\geq 0.

In order to make our following statements much easier to be understood, we write
S^{\uparrow}
and
S^{\downarrow}
for the two independent Markov processes with transition functions
p^{\uparrow}
and
p^{\downarrow}
respectively.
Their exponential functionals are denoted as
I^{\uparrow}
and
I^{\downarrow}
.
Moreover, we also write
\hat{S}^{\uparrow}
and
\hat{S}^{\downarrow}
for the dual processes of
S^{\uparrow}
and
S^{\downarrow}
respectively and also write
\hat{I}^{\uparrow}
and
\hat{I}^{\downarrow}
for their exponential functionals.
Repeating the preceding argument, we see that
\hat{S}^{\uparrow}
and
\hat{S}^{\downarrow}
are two homogeneous Markov processes with transition functions

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\hat{p}^{\uparrow}(x,dy):=\frac{V(-y)}{V(-x)}\mathbf{P}(x-X\in dy)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{p}^{\downarrow}(x,dy):=\frac{\hat{V}(-y)}{\hat{V}(-x)}\mathbf{P}(x+X\in dy),\quad x,y\leq 0.

As we have mentioned before, the asymptotics of the tail distribution of generic step
X
plays a crucial role in the following classification and asymptotic analysis of exponential functionals of random walks.
Thus we need the Laplace transform of
X

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda):=\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\lambda X}\big],\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Certainly, it may happen that
\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda)=\infty
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda)<\infty\}
,
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}\cap[0,\infty)
and
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}:=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}\cap(-\infty,0]
.
For each
\lambda_{0}\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}
, the process
\{\exp\{\lambda_{0}S_{n}\}\cdot|\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda_{0})|^{-n}:n=0,1,\cdots\}
is a
\mathbf{P}
martingale, which allows us to define a probability measure
\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda_{0})}
on
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{S})

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda_{0})}(A):=\int_{A}\exp\{\lambda_{0}S_{n}\}\cdot|\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda_{0})|^{-n}d\mathbf{P},\quad A\in\mathscr{F}^{S}_{n},n\geq 0.

(2.1)

It is known that the process
S
under
\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda_{0})}
is still a random walk and the generic step
X
has Laplace transform

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\lambda_{0})}[e^{\lambda X}]=\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda_{0}+\lambda)/\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda_{0}),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
V^{(\lambda_{0})}
and
\hat{V}^{(\lambda_{0})}
be the renewal functions associated with the strict descending ladder height processes of
S
and
\hat{S}
under
\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda_{0})}
.

2.2
Main results

We now provide the asymptotic results for the expectation
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]
in which
F
is a
positive, bounded
function on
(0,\infty)
and always satisfies the following assumption:

Assumption 2.1

The set
\mathcal{D}_{F}:=\{\theta>0:\sup_{x>0}x^{\theta}F(x)<\infty\}
is not null and the supremum is denoted as
\theta_{F}\in(0,\infty]
.

To simplify the representation of our main results, let us list the following conditions:

Condition 2.2

For each
\delta>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\delta}>0
such that
|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C_{\delta}|x-y|
for any
x,y\geq\delta
.

Condition 2.3

There exist a constant
K_{0}>0
such that
F(x)\sim K_{0}x^{-\theta_{F}}
as
x\to\infty
.

It is known that when
S
does not drift to infinity, with probability one it visits the negative half-line infinite times and hence
I_{\infty}=\infty
a.s.
For the converse, when
S
drifts to
\infty
, Erickson’s theorem in
[
20
]
shows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}/n\in(0,\infty]
a.s. and hence
I_{\infty}<\infty
a.s.
The first asymptotic result for exponential functionals of random walks is summarized as follows.

Lemma 2.4

The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

I_{\infty}<\infty
a.s.;
(ii)

\mathbf{P}(I_{\infty}<\infty)>0
;
(iii)

S
drifts to
\infty
, i.e.,
S_{n}\to\infty
a.s. as
n\to\infty
.

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the case
I_{\infty}=\infty
a.s., that is the random walk
S
will not drift to
\infty
.
As we have mentioned before, beside of the decay rate of
F
the long-term behavior of the expectation
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]
also heavily depends on the speed at which the random walk
S
approaches to
-\infty
.
The fluctuation of
S
is closely related to the following crucial quantity:

\displaystyle\varrho:=\inf_{\lambda\in[0,\theta_{F}]}\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda).

It is obvious that
\varrho\leq 1
and the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{L}_{X}}^{+}=\{0\}
or
\mathbf{E}[X]=0
; see Figure
1
.
In the sequel of this paper we always make the following assumption:

Assumption 2.5

The infimum of
\mathcal{L}_{X}(\lambda)
over

3.2
Random walk with negative drift

We now list the asymptotic results for random walks with negative drift satisfying Assumption
2.13
and
2.14
.
For every
x\geq 0
, we need the following important stopping time

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{x}:=\inf\{k\geq 1:X_{k}>x\}

the first arrival of step with size larger than
x
.
For each
k\geq 1
, a simple calculation together with Assumption
2.13
induces that the two events
\mathcal{T}^{x}=k
and
X_{k}\geq x
are asymptotically equivalent as
x\to\infty
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{x}=k|X_{k}\geq x)\sim\mathbf{P}(X_{k}\geq x|\mathcal{T}^{x}=k)\to 1,

(3.1)

and hence
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{x}\leq k)\sim k\mathbf{P}(X\geq x)
. The following well-known asymptotic results for the tail-probabilities of the final value
S_{n}
and the first passage time
\tau_{0}^{-}
can be found in many literature, e.g., Theorem 5 in
[
6
]
and Theorem 2.2 in
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.8

For every
x\geq 0
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(S_{n}\geq x)\sim n\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}\geq n)\sim\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}]\mathbf{P}(X\geq an).

By this lemma and the Markov property, for each integer
k\geq 0
we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\sigma_{n}^{-}=k)=\mathbf{P}(\sigma_{k}^{-}=k)\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k)\sim\mathbf{P}(\sigma_{k}^{-}=k)\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{-}]\cdot\mathbf{P}(X\geq an).

The next lemma comes from Remark 3.7 in
[
34
]
, which extends Theorem 3.2 in
[
19
]
.
It shows that an early large step is necessary to keep the random walk staying positive for a long time.

Lemma 3.9

For every integer
k\geq 1
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{an}>n|\tau_{0}^{-}>n)\to 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{an}\leq k|\tau_{0}^{-}>n)\to\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{-}\wedge k]/\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{-}].

Moreover, we also observe that the random walk will stay above a high level for a long time after the first large step.
This implies that the local minimum of the random walk should be attained near the early large step; see the next lemma and it can be proved by slightly modifying the proof for Lemma 4.6 in
[
34
]

Lemma 3.10

Let
b\in(0,a]
and pick an integer
k\geq 0
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers

such that for any

and
n\geq n_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T,\sigma_{n}^{-}=k)\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(X\geq an).

Proof.
By the duality lemma, for
n>T>k
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T,\sigma_{n}^{-}=k)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{an}\geq T-k,\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k)\cdot\mathbf{P}(\sigma_{k}^{-}=k)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{an}\geq T-k\,|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k)\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k).

Using Lemma
3.8
and then Lemma
3.9
, we have for some
C>0

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T,\sigma_{n}^{-}=k)}{\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)}\leq C\cdot\big(1-\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{-}\wedge(T-k)]/\mathbf{E}[\tau_{0}^{-}]\big),

which goes to

as

.

\Box

4.1
Proof for Theorem
2.7

Under Condition

, we first notice that sample paths with slowly decreasing local infimum would attain the local minimum at the beginning of the time interval.
And then, we prove in the next proposition, that the contribution of sample paths with the local minimum being late attained to the expectation

can be asymptotically ignored.

Proposition 4.1

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
k_{0},n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
K\geq k_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\geq K\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n})\big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

Proof.
By Assumption
2.1
, we first have
F(I_{n})\leq C\cdot\exp\{\theta_{F}L_{n}\}
for some
C>0
. Using the Markov property of
S
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\geq K\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{n}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\theta_{F}S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\big]\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k).

From Lemma
3.3
and
3.4
, we have
\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\theta_{F}S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=n\big]=o(\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n))
as
n\to\infty
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
to the foregoing partial sum, we have for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\geq K\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\theta_{F}S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\big]\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

The desired two claims follow directly from the summability of the sequence
\mathbf{E}[e^{\theta_{F}S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k]
; see Lemma
3.4
, and the fact that

\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=0\big]\leq C\mathbf{P}(\sigma_{n}^{-}=0)\sim C\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)
.

\Box

We now consider the contribution of sample paths with the local minimum attained at the beginning of the time interval, i.e.,
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=k]
for each fixed
k\geq 0
. Using the Markov property of
S
and then the duality lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}\cdot\tilde{I}_{n-k}\big);\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-k\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big];\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\big].

(4.1)

Thus it is a crucial step to analyze the asymptotics of the foregoing conditional expectation.

Proposition 4.2

Let
H
be a bounded and Lipschitz continuous function on
(0,\infty)
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
k_{0},n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
K\geq k_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|;\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]=\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|;L_{n}\geq 0\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

Proof.
By the duality lemma, we have
\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|;\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]=\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|;L_{n}=0\big]
. By Remark
3.1
and Lemma
3.8
, the boundedness of
H
induces that
\mathbf{E}[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|;L_{n}=0]\leq C\mathbf{P}(L_{n}=0)=o(\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n))
uniformly in
K
as
n\to\infty

and hence it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|\,;L_{n}>0\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

By the Lipschitz continuity of
H
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})|\,;L_{n}>0\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K+1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{k}};L_{n}>0\big].

(4.2)

By the Markov property of
S
, we see that
\mathbf{E}[e^{-S_{k}};L_{n}>0]
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{k}}\mathbf{P}_{S_{k}}(\tilde{L}_{n-k}>0)\,;L_{k}>0\big].

By the second result in Lemma
3.3
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{S_{k}}(\tilde{L}_{n-k}>0)\leq CV(S_{k})\mathbf{P}(L_{n-k}>0)
and hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[e^{-S_{k}};L_{n}>0]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{k}}V(S_{k})\,;L_{k}>0\big]\cdot\mathbf{P}(L_{n-k}>0).

(4.3)

The fact that
V(x)=O(x)
as
x\to\infty
shows
V(x)e^{-x}\leq Ce^{-x/2}
for all
x\geq 0
.
From Lemma
3.3
and
3.4
, we have for large
k
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{k}}V(S_{k})\,;L_{k}>0\big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{E}[e^{-S_{k}/2};L_{k}>0]=o\big(\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>k)\big).

Taking this and (
4.3
) back into (
4.2
) and then using Lemma
3.2
, we have for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\left|H(I_{n})-H(I_{K})\right|;L_{n}>0\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{k}/2};L_{k}>0\big]\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n),

Here the summation above vanishes as
K\to\infty
; see Lemma
3.4
, and the desired result follows immediately.

\Box

Proposition 4.3

Let
H
be a bounded and continuous function on
(0,\infty)
. For
K\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[H(I_{K})\,|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]=\mathbf{E}\big[H(I_{K}^{\uparrow})\big]>0.

Moreover, the sequence
\mathbf{E}[H(I_{K}^{\uparrow})]
converges to
\mathbf{E}[H(I_{\infty}^{\uparrow})]>0
as

.

Proof.
The first claim follows from Lemma
3.5
.
For the second one, by the dominated convergence theorem we have
\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathbf{E}[H(I_{K}^{\uparrow})]=\mathbf{E}[\lim_{K\to\infty}H(I_{K}^{\uparrow})]
. The continuity of
H
and the fact that
I_{K}^{\uparrow}
increases to

, induce that
\mathbf{E}[\lim_{K\to\infty}H(I_{K}^{\uparrow})]=\mathbf{E}[H(I_{\infty}^{\uparrow})]
.

\Box

Proposition 4.4

For every
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n})\big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big].

Using the Markov property, the independent increments of

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{\theta_{F}S_{k}-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=k]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k]\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n-k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k]\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n-k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k]

and hence
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]
can be bounded by

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{n-K}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k]\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n-k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k].

Recall the sequence
A_{n}
defined in (
2.6
).
Using the first asymptotic equivalence in Lemma
3.6
with
\lambda=\Lambda
,
y=\infty
, the second one with
\lambda=\theta_{F}-\Lambda
,
y=\infty
and then Lemma
3.2
to the foregoing summation, we have for some
C>0
and large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot A_{n}\cdot\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\Big(\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>k]\Big).

Using Lemma
3.6
again, the last summation vanishes as

and then the first claim follows. The second claim follows from the first one and the fact that

\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=0]\leq C\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]
.

We now start to consider the contribution of sample paths with the local minimum attained early to the expectation
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]
, i.e.
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=k]
for

As in the proof for Theorem

Different to Proposition

,

I_{J}

Proposition 4.5

Let
H
be a bounded and Lipschitz continuous function. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
j_{0},n_{0}\geq 0
such that for any
J\geq j_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n})|;\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

Proof.
By the Lipschitz continuity of
H
, we first have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n})|\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J+1}^{n-J}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{j}}\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big].

(4.8)

By the change of measure, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{j}}\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}[e^{-S_{j}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}=\frac{\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-S_{j}-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]}{\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]}.

(4.9)

Using the Markov property and the independent increments of
S
to the numerator of the last fraction, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-S_{j}-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]=\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-S_{j}}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{S_{j}}\big[e^{-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-j}};\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-j\big];\tau_{0}^{-}>j\big].

Together with the fact that
V(x)=O(x)
as
x\to\infty
, the second claim in Lemma
3.6
implies that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{S_{j}}\big[e^{-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-j}};\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-j\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot S_{j}\cdot A_{n-j}

and hence by the fact that
xe^{-x}\leq Ce^{-x/2}
for some
C>0
and any
x\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-S_{j}-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-S_{j}/2};\tau_{0}^{-}>j\big]\cdot A_{n-j}\leq C\cdot A_{j}A_{n-j}.

Taking this back into (
4.9
) and then using the first claim in Lemma
3.6
with
y=\infty
, we have for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{j}}\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{C\cdot A_{j}A_{n-j}}{A_{n}}.

Taking this back into (
4.8
) and then using Lemma
3.2
, we have for some
C>0
and large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(I_{n})-H(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n})|\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\leq C\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}A_{j},

which goes to
0
as
J\to\infty
and the desired result follows.

\Box

Proposition 4.6

Recall
\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy)
defined in (
2.7
) with
\lambda=\Lambda
. Let
H
be a bounded and continuous function on
(0,\infty)
vanishing at infinity.
For each
J\geq 1
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[H(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n})\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\to\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{(0,y)}^{(\Lambda)}\big[H(I^{\uparrow}_{J}+e^{-y}+I^{\downarrow}_{J-1})\big]\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy).

Moreover, the limit coefficient converges as
J\to\infty
to a finite limit given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{(0,y)}^{(\Lambda)}\big[H(I^{\uparrow}_{\infty}+e^{-y}+I^{\downarrow}_{\infty})\big]\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy).

Proof.
By the change of measure

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[H(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n})\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[H(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]}{\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]}.

Applying Lemma
3.7
to the foregoing expectations with
G(x_{1},x_{2})=H(x_{1}+x_{2})
,
f(S)=I_{J}
and
g_{n}(S)=I_{n-J,n}
, we can get the desired convergence immediately.
The second claim follows by the dominated convergence theorem and the fact that
I^{\uparrow}_{\infty},I^{\downarrow}_{\infty}<\infty
a.s. under
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}
; see Remark
2.8
.

\Box

Corollary 4.7

For each
k\geq 0
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=k]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}\!\!\!

\displaystyle\to\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{(0,0,y)}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{k}}F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}(I^{\uparrow}_{\infty}+e^{-y}+I^{\downarrow}_{\infty})\big);\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\big]\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy).

Proof.
Applying Proposition
4.5
together with the continuity of
F
on
(0,\infty)
to the conditional expectation on the right side of (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}\cdot\tilde{I}_{n-k}\big);\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-k\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}\cdot(\tilde{I}_{J}+\tilde{I}_{n-k-J,n-k})\big);\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-k\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}(\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-k)}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}\cdot\lim_{J\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}\cdot(\tilde{I}_{J}+\tilde{I}_{n-k-J,n-k})\big)\,\big|\,\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n-k,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big].

By the change of measure and Lemma
3.6
with
y=\infty
, the first limit on the right side of the last equality equals to
\varrho^{-k}
.
By Proposition
4.6
with

, we have the second limit equals to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{(0,y)}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}(I^{\uparrow}_{\infty}+e^{-y}+I^{\downarrow}_{\infty})\big)\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big]\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy).

Taking these back into (
4.1
) and then using the change of measure, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=k]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}=\mathbf{E}\Big[\varrho^{-k}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{(0,y)}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}(I^{\uparrow}_{\infty}+e^{-y}+I^{\downarrow}_{\infty})\big)\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big]\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy);\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\Big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda S_{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{(0,y)}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F\big(I_{k}+e^{-S_{k}}(I^{\uparrow}_{\infty}+e^{-y}+I^{\downarrow}_{\infty})\big)\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k}\big]\mu_{\hat{V}^{(\Lambda)}}^{(\Lambda)}(dy);\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\Big]

We now start to consider the impact of sample paths with the local minimum attained at the end of the time interval on the expectation
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]
.
Applying the Markov property to

\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n});\sigma^{-}_{n}=n-k]
for

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n-k}+e^{-S_{n-k}}\tilde{I}_{k});\sigma^{-}_{n-k}=n-k\,\big|\,\mathscr{F}^{\tilde{S}}_{k}\big];\tilde{\tau}_{0}^{-}>k\big].

(4.10)

Like the previous argument, we first need to consider the aysmptotics of the conditional expectation.
In the next proposition, we show that it is rarely contributed by sample paths with final value below a very low level.

Proposition 4.8

For every
x\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
y_{0},n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
y\geq y_{0}
and

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(I_{n}+xe^{-S_{n}}\big);S_{n}<-y,\sigma^{-}_{n}=n\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big).

Proof.
By the change of measure, we see that the desired inequality holds if and only if

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F\big(I_{n}+xe^{-S_{n}}\big);S_{n}<-y,\sigma^{-}_{n}=n\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big].

Applying the duality lemma to the expectation on the left side of this inequality, we see it equals to

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F\big(e^{-S_{n}}(1+\hat{I}_{n-1}+x)\big);S_{n}<-y,M_{n}<0\big],

which can be bounded by
C\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{n}};S_{n}<-y,M_{n}<0\big]
because of the assumption that
F(x)\leq Cx^{-\theta_{F}}
.
From Lemma
3.6
, there exists a constant
C>0
such for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{n}};S_{n}<-y,M_{n}<0]}{\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n]}\leq C\cdot\big|\mathcal{L}^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)}_{V}(\infty)-\mathcal{L}^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)}_{V}(y)\big|,

which goes to
0
as
y\to\infty
and hence the desired result follows.

\Box

Proposition 4.9

Suppose
F
is globally Lipschitz continuous on
(0,\infty)
. For each
x,y\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
j_{0},n_{0}>1
such that for any
J\geq j_{0}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\big|F(I_{n}+xe^{-S_{n}})-F(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n}+xe^{-S_{n}})\big|;S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

(4.11)

Proof.
By the Lipschitz continuity of
F
, the expectation on the left side of (

) can be bounded by

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}\big[I_{J+1,n-J};S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{j=J+1}^{n-J}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{j}};S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n\big].

(4.12)

Applying the duality lemma and then the change of measure to
\mathbf{E}\big[e^{-S_{j}};S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n\big]
,
we see that it equals to

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[e^{S_{n-j}-S_{n}};S_{n}\geq-y,M_{n}<0\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{n-j}-(1+\Lambda)S_{n}};S_{n}\geq-y,M_{n}<0\big]\cdot\varrho^{n},

(4.13)

which can be bounded by
e^{(\kappa+1+\Lambda)y}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{S_{n-j}+\kappa S_{n}};M_{n}<0]\cdot\varrho^{n}
for any
\kappa>0
.
By the Markov property and the second claim in Lemma
3.6
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{n-j}+\kappa S_{n}};M_{n}<0\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{n-j}}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{S_{n-j}}[e^{\kappa\tilde{S}_{j}};\tilde{M}_{j}<0];M_{n-j}<0\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{n-j}}S_{n-j};M_{n-j}<0\big]\cdot A_{j}\leq CA_{j}A_{n-j}.

Taking this and (
4.13
) back into (
4.12
) and then using Lemma
3.6
with
\lambda=0
and
y=\infty
, we have
for large
n
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}\big[I_{J+1,n-J};S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n\big]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{C}{A_{n}}\sum_{j=J+1}^{n-J}A_{j}A_{n-j},

which is asymptotically equivalent to
2C\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}A_{j}
as
n\to\infty
; see Lemma
3.2
. Hence the desired result follows as
J\to\infty
because of the summability of the sequence

.

Proposition 4.10

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}[F(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n}+xe^{-S_{n}});S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}

\displaystyle\to\!\!\!

Moreover, the limit coefficient converges as

and then

to

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}[F(I_{J}+I_{n-J,n}+xe^{-S_{n}});S_{n}\geq-y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n]}{\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n)}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

3.6

y=\infty

.

4.11

can be gotten from Proposition

\Box

4.3
Proof for Theorem
2.11

Condition
2.3
and the boundedness of
F
tell us that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exits a constant
x_{0}>0
such that
|F(x)-K_{0}\cdot(1+x)^{-\Lambda}|<\epsilon\cdot(1+x)^{-\Lambda}
for any
x\geq x_{0}
.
Thus we need to consider the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbf{E}[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}]
at first.
By the change of measure and then duality lemma,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}]=\varrho^{n}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big]=\varrho^{n}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+\hat{I}_{n})^{-\Lambda}\big].

According to our previous argument, the dual process
\hat{S}
satisfies Spitzer’s condition with positivity parameter
1-\rho
.
Using Theorem
2.7
with
F(x)=(1+x)^{-\Lambda}
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+\hat{I}_{n})^{-\Lambda}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sim\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{F,3}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\hat{\tau}_{0}^{-}>n)=C_{F,3}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{0}^{+}>n).

It remains to prove that
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[|F(I_{n})-K_{0}(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}|e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big]=o\big(\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{0}^{+}>n)\big)
as
n\to\infty
. From the previous result, we first have for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[|F(I_{n})-K_{0}(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}|e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};I_{n}>x_{0}\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot C\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{0}^{+}>n).

On the other hand, the boundedness of
F
implies that
F(x)\leq C(1+x)^{-\Lambda}
for some
C>0
and
x\geq 0
. By Chebyshev’s inequality we also have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[|F(I_{n})-K_{0}(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda}|e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};I_{n}\leq x_{0}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C(1+x_{0})\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda-1}e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big].

Notice that
(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda-1}\leq e^{(\Lambda+1)S_{n}}
a.s. for any
n\geq 1
.
For each
k\geq 0
, by the Markov property of
S
we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda-1}e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{(\Lambda+1)S_{k}-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\big]\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n-k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k\big].

By the duality lemma, Remark
3.1
and Lemma
3.6
, we have
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k\big]=\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{k}};M_{k}<0\big]\sim\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k\big]
as
k\to\infty
.
Applying Lemma
3.2
together with Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda-1}e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[(1+I_{n})^{-\Lambda-1}e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k\big]\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n-k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k\big]=O(A_{n}),

which is
o(\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\tau_{0}^{+}>n))
; see (
2.6
) and Lemma
3.3
.
Putting all estimates above together, we can get the desired result immediately.

5
Proof for Theorems
2.12
-
2.17

In this section we prove the asymptotic results for the expectation
\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]
with
S
drifting to
-\infty
under
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}
.
Different to the oscillating case, we observe that the slow decreasing of the local minimum of

For simplicity, we again assume

5.1
Proof for Theorem
2.12

By the change of measure and then Condition

, we have

\displaystyle\varrho^{-n}\cdot\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n})]=\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\cdot F(I_{n})\big]=\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}(F(I_{n})-K_{0}I_{n}^{-\Lambda})\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}K_{0}I_{n}^{-\Lambda}].

By the duality lemma and then Lemma
2.4
, the last expectation equals to
K_{0}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[(1+\hat{I}_{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}n-1})^{-\Lambda}]

Thus it suffices to prove
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}|F(I_{n})-K_{0}I_{n}^{-\Lambda}|]\to 0
as

. By Condition
2.3
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}|F(I_{n})-K_{0}I_{n}^{-\Lambda}|\,;I_{n}\geq x\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}I_{n}^{-\Lambda}\big]\leq\epsilon.

Here the last inequality follows from the fact that

On the other hand, by Assumption

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}|F(I_{n})-K_{0}I_{n}^{-\Lambda}|;I_{n}<x\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}\geq-K)+C\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}<-K,I_{n}<x).

Since

By Chebyshev’s inequality and

\displaystyle x\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[I_{n}^{-1};S_{n}<-K\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle x\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{S_{n}};S_{n}<-K\big]\leq xe^{-K},

which vanishes as

and the desired result follows by putting all estimates above together.

Proof for Theorem
2.15

(5.1)

Here these two inequalities follow from the boundedness of

On the other hand, in the next proposition we show that the contribution of sample paths with local minimum attained late,

Proposition 5.1

For every
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma^{-}_{n}\geq K\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n})\big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{P}(\tau_{0}^{-}>n).

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\geq K\big]\leq C\sum_{k=K}^{n}\mathbf{E}\big[e^{\theta_{F}S_{k}};\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

(5.2)

Here the last equality follows from the Markov property of

Notice that the sequence

\Box

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{E}[F(I_{n});X_{k}\geq an]}{\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)}\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[\mathbf{E}[F(I_{k-1}+e^{-S_{k-1}-X}\tilde{I}_{n-k})|X\geq an,\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k-1}]\big].

(5.6)

Proposition 5.2

Let
H
be a bounded and Lipschitz continuous function on
(0,\infty)
.
For every
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
k_{0},n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
K\geq k_{0}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(e^{-X}I_{n})-H(e^{-X}I_{K})|;X\geq an\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot\mathbf{P}(X\geq an).

I_{K,n}:=I_{n}-I_{K}
for

. The boundedness and Lipschitz continuity of

induce that there exists a constant
C>0

|H(x)-H(y)|\leq C(1\wedge|x-y|)
for any
x,y>0
. Hence uniformly in
n\geq K\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[|H(e^{-X}I_{n})-H(e^{-X}I_{K})|;X\geq an\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[1\wedge\big(e^{-X}I_{K,n}\big);X\geq an\big].

b>a
, it is obvious that the foregoing quantities can bounded by the sum of

\displaystyle\varepsilon(b,n):=C\cdot\mathbf{P}(X\in[an,bn))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varepsilon(b,K,n):=C\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[1\wedge\big(e^{-X}I_{K,n}\big);X\geq bn\big].

2.13
induces that
\varepsilon(b,n)/\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)\to C\cdot(1-(a/b)^{\beta})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and then
b\to a+
.
Let
S_{n}^{b}:=S_{n}+bn
, which drifts to
\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Let
I^{b}
be the exponential functional of
S^{b}
and
I_{K,n}^{b}:=I_{n}^{b}-I_{K}^{b}

It is easy to see that

.
By the independence between
X
and
I_{K,n}
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon(b,K,n)\leq C\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[1\wedge\big(e^{-bn}I_{K,n}\big)\big]\cdot\mathbf{P}(X\geq bn)\leq C\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[1\wedge I_{K,n}^{b}\big]\cdot\mathbf{P}(X\geq bn).

2.4

K\to\infty

Applying the dominated convergence theorem to (

\varepsilon(b,K,n)/\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)\to 0

The desired result follows by putting all preceding estimates together.

Proposition 5.3

Let
H
be a continuous, positive, bounded and non-increasing function on
(0,\infty)
. For any
K>0
, we have as
n\to\infty

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{K})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]\to C_{H,K}>0.

Moreover, the sequence
C_{H,K}
decreases to a limit
C_{H}>0
as
K\to\infty
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{n})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]=C_{H}.

Proof.
The first result follows directly from our observation that
\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{K})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]
is non-increasing in
n
, i.e., for any

a sample calculation show that the expectation
\mathbf{E}\big[H(ze^{-X})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]
can written as the sum of
\mathbf{E}\big[H(ze^{-X})\,\big|\,X\geq a(n+1)\big]

\frac{\mathbf{P}(X\in[an,a(n+1)))}{\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)}\Big(\mathbf{E}\big[H(ze^{-X})\,\big|\,X\in[an,a(n+1))\big]-\mathbf{E}\big[H(ze^{-X})\,\big|\,X\geq a(n+1)\big]\Big).

Since
H
is non-increasing, the foregoing difference is non-positive and hence
\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{K})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]\geq\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{K})\,\big|\,X\geq a(n+1)\big]
.
We now turn to prove the second claim.
Because of the monotonicity of
H
and
I_{K}
, the sequence
C_{H,K}
is non-increasing and converges to a limit
C_{H}\in[0,\infty)
as
K\to\infty
.
Moreover, by Proposition
5.2
and the first result,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{n})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[\big(H(e^{-X}I_{n})-H(e^{-X}I_{K})\big)\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]

\displaystyle+\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{K})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big],

which equals to
C_{H}
.
It remains to prove

.
Recall the modified random walk
S^{b}_{n}:=S_{n}+bn
for

. Since

is non-increasing, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{n})\,\big|\,X\geq an\big]\geq\frac{\mathbf{E}\big[H(e^{-X}I_{n});X\geq bn\big]}{\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)}\geq\mathbf{E}\big[H(I_{n}^{b})\big]\cdot\frac{\mathbf{P}(X\geq bn)}{\mathbf{P}(X\geq an)}.

2.13

2.4

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{n-K}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\sigma_{k}^{-}=k]\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n-k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n-k]

\displaystyle\sim\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=K}^{\infty}\big(\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>k]\big)\cdot B_{n}.

The two desired inequalities follows from
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}=0\big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]\leq C\cdot B_{n}
and the summability of the two sequences
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{+}>k]
and
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{k}};\tau_{0}^{-}>k]
; see Lemma
3.12

and (
2.12
)
.

\displaystyle C\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}\geq Y,\tau_{0}^{-}>n\big]

Proposition 5.8

For every
\epsilon>0
and integer
k\geq 0
, there exist two integers

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{n}\geq Y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=n-k\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Recall the drift parameter
a
defined in (
2.10
). We now turn to consider the impact of sample paths with late arrival of the first large step, slowly decreasing local minimum and the final value below some fixed level, i.e. for
b\in(0,a]
and
Y,T\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T].

The next two propositions show that the late arrival of the first large jump will cause the random walk to drift to a low level.
It extends Lemma 9 in
[
8
]
, which considered the case of
\beta>2
.

Proposition 5.9

Let
r>\frac{\beta+1}{\beta-1}
,
b\in(0,a/r)
and
Y\geq 0
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists an integer
n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq n)\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Proof.
Here we just prove this proposition with
\beta\in(1,2)
. For the case of
\beta>2
, it can be proved similarly with the help of Theorem 4.1.2(i) in
[
13
, p.183]
.
We first assume that for some
C>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\leq-x)\leq C\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq x),\quad x\geq 0.

(5.11)

By Lemma 3.1 in
[
14
]
and Assumption
2.13
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}+an>rbn,\mathcal{T}^{bn-a}>n)\leq C\cdot\big|n\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq bn-a)\big|^{r}\leq C\cdot n^{r(1-\beta)}(\ell_{3}(n))^{r}.

From this and the assumption that
rb<a
and
r(\beta-1)>\beta+1
, we have for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}>n)\leq Cn^{r(1-\beta)}(\ell_{3}(n))^{r}=o(B_{n}).

We now consider the general case with
X
satisfying Assumption
2.13
.
For some
R<0
, we define
X^{R}:=X\vee R
and
X^{R}_{i}=X_{i}\vee R
such that
a_{R}:=-\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[X^{R}]\in(rb,a)
.
One can identify that (
5.11
) holds for
X^{R}
with
x>-R
, since
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X^{R}\leq-x)=0
and
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X^{R}\geq x)=\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq x)>0
.
Moreover, for
x\in[0,-R]
we also have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X^{R}\leq-x)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X^{R}\leq-x)}{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq-R)}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq-R)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X^{R}\leq 0)}{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq-R)}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq-R)\leq\frac{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\leq 0)}{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq-R)}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq x).

Thus the random variable
X^{R}
satisfies the inequality (
5.11
) with
C=1\vee\frac{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\leq 0)}{\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq-R)}
.
Let
S^{R}_{n}
be the random walk generated by the sequence
\{X_{i}^{R}:i=1,2,\cdots\}
.
Noting that
S\leq S^{R}
a.s., we have
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq n)\leq\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}^{R}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq n)
.
The previous result induces that
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq n)=o(B_{n})
as
n\to\infty
.

Proposition 5.10

Let
b>0
and
Y\geq 0
.
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
t_{0},n_{0}>1
such that for any
T\geq t_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n)\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Proof.
By the definition of
\mathcal{T}^{bn}
, we have
\{T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n\}\subset\cup_{k=T}^{n}\{X_{k}\geq bn\}
and hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n)\leq\sum_{k=T}^{n}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,X_{k}\geq bn).

(5.12)

For
k\leq n
, by the fact that
L_{k-1}\geq L_{n}
and the Markov property of
S
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,X_{k}\geq bn)

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(L_{k-1}\geq-Y,S_{k-1}\geq-Y,S_{k-1}+X_{k}+(S_{n}-S_{k})\in[-Y,Y],X_{k}\geq bn)

(5.15)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{-Y}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(L_{k-1}\geq-Y,S_{k-1}\in dx)\cdot\int_{bn}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(x+y+\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in[-Y,Y])\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\in dy)

(5.17)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(L_{k-1}\geq-Y)\cdot\sup_{x\geq-Y}\int_{bn}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(x+y+\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in[-Y,Y])\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\in dy).

(5.18)

By Assumption
2.14
, there exist constants
C_{0},n_{0}>0
such that
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\in[bn+i,bn+i+1))\leq C_{0}\cdot B_{n}
for any
i\geq 1
and
n\geq n_{0}
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{bn}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(x+y+\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in[-Y,Y])\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\in dy)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{0}\cdot B_{n}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(x+bn+i+\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in[-Y-1,Y+1])

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{0}\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{bn}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(x+y+\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in[-Y-2,Y+2])dy.

Applying Fubini’s theorem to the last integral, it can be bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-x-y-Y-2}^{-x-y+Y+2}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in dz)dy=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(\tilde{S}_{n-k}\in dy)\int_{-x-y-Y-2}^{-x-y+Y+2}dz\leq 2Y+4.

Taking these back into (
5.13
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,X_{k}\geq bn)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle 2C_{0}\cdot(Y+2)\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(L_{k-1}\geq-Y)\cdot B_{n}.

By Lemma
3.8
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(L_{k-1}\geq-Y)=\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}_{Y}(\tau_{0}^{-}>k-1)\leq C\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq a(k-1))
for any
k>1
. Taking these two upper bound estimates back into (
5.12
), we have for some constant
C>0
independent of
n
and
T
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=T}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq a(k-1)),

which vanishes as
T\to\infty
, since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq a(k-1))<\infty
. The desired result follows.

is made by the sample paths with an early large step.
In detail, for

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\mathcal{T}^{bn}<T\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\mathcal{T}^{bn}>n\big]

where

and

For any

, the second expectation on the right side of this equality is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}>n\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}\leq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}>n\big]

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot e^{\Lambda Y}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[S_{n}\geq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}>n\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};L_{n}\leq-Y\big],

o(B_{n})
as

5.5
and
5.9
.
The third expectation on the right side of (
5.20
) is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n\big]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};L_{n}<-Y,T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}>Y,T\leq\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq n\big].

as
n\to\infty
.
The second term is smaller than

, which is
o(B_{n})
as

5.5
.
For any

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}>Y\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}>Y;\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}>Y;\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{n}>Y;\sigma_{n}^{-}=n-k\big],

which is
o(B_{n})
as
n,Y\to\infty
and then
K\to\infty
; see Proposition
5.6
,
5.7
and
5.8
.
Taking these estimates back into (
5.20
), we have as

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big]\sim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\mathcal{T}^{bn}=k\big].

By (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.8
, for each
k\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\mathcal{T}^{bn}=k\big]\sim\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};X_{k}\geq bn\big],

(5.21)

as
n\to\infty
. Using Proposition
5.5
again, it is also asymptotically equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{k-1}\geq-Y,X_{k}\geq bn\big]

(5.22)

for large
Y
. Moreover, as we have mentioned before, the local minimum is usually attained around the early large step.
For sample paths that stay above a high level before the early large step, their impacts on the expectation
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}]
can be ignored; see the following proposition.

Proposition 5.11

Let
b>0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. For each
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
n_{0},y_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
Y\geq y_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{k-1}\geq Y,X_{k}\geq bn\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Proof.
By Assumption
2.1
, we have
F(I_{n})\leq C\cdot e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}}
for some constant
C>0
depending only on
F
and hence the expectation in the desired inequality can be bounded by
C\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{k-1}\geq Y,X_{k}\geq bn\big]
.
For a constant
\gamma\in(1/\beta,1)
, we write this expectation into

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{k-1}\geq n^{\gamma},X_{k}\geq bn\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};Y\leq S_{k-1}\leq n^{\gamma},X_{k}\geq bn\big].

(5.23)

By Hölder’s inequality and then the independence between
S_{k-1}
and
X_{k}
, the first expectation can be bounded by

\displaystyle\big(\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{2\theta_{F}L_{n}-2\Lambda S_{n}}]\big)^{1/2}\cdot\big(\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{k-1}\geq n^{\gamma})\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\geq bn)\big)^{1/2}.

Using Lemma
3.11
with
x,y=\infty

2.12
), we see that
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{2\theta_{F}L_{n}-2\Lambda S_{n}}]\leq C\cdot B_{n}
and
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\geq bn)\leq C\cdot n\cdot B_{n}
for any
n\geq 1
and some constant
C>0
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{k-1}\geq n^{\gamma},X_{k}\geq bn\big]\leq C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\big(n\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{k-1}\geq n^{\gamma})\big)^{1/2}.

The convolution property of regular varying distributions together with Assumption
2.13
induces that
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{k-1}\geq n^{\gamma})\sim(k-1)\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq n^{\gamma})=o(1/n)
.
Taking this back into the preceding inequality, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};S_{k-1}\geq n^{\gamma},X_{k}\geq bn\big]=o(B_{n}).

(5.24)

On the other hand, by the Markov property of
S
and

L_{n}\leq\min\{S_{k},S_{k+1},\cdots,S_{n}\}
,
the second expectation in (
5.23
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{Y}^{n^{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{k-1}\in dy)\int_{bn}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{y+x}\big[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k}}\big]\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}\in dx)

(5.25)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{Y}^{n^{\gamma}}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{k-1}\in dy)\int_{bn+y}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{x}\big[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k}}\big]\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}+y\in dx).

(5.26)

We first consider the inner integral on the right-hand side of this equality.
It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\int_{bn+y}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{x}\big[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k}}\big]\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}+y\in dx)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{bn+i}[e^{\theta_{F}(\tilde{L}_{n-k}+1)-\Lambda(\tilde{S}_{n-k}-1)}]\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}+y\in[bn+i,bn+i+1)).

2.13

[
12

2.14

C,C_{1},C_{2},n_{0}>0

i\geq 0
,
n\geq n_{0}
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}+y\in[bn+i,bn+i+1))\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{1}\cdot\beta(bn-y+i)^{-1-\beta}\ell_{3}(bn-y+i)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{2}\cdot\beta(bn+i)^{-1-\beta}\ell_{3}(bn+i)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in[bn+i,bn+i+1))

and hence

\displaystyle\int_{bn+y}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{x}\big[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k}}\big]\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{k}+y\in dx)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{bn+i}[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k}}]\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in[bn+i,bn+i+1))

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\int_{bn}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}_{x}[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k}}]\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in dx)=C\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}\tilde{L}_{n-k+1}-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}};\tilde{X}_{1}\geq bn\big].

By Lemma

with

, it can be bounded by

.
Taking this back into (

), there exists a constant

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};Y\leq S_{k-1}\leq n^{\gamma},X_{k}\geq bn\big]\leq C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{k-1}\geq Y)=o(B_{n}).

Taking this and (
5.24
) back into (

Taking the estimate in Proposition
5.11
back into (
5.22
) and then (
5.21
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};\mathcal{T}^{bn}=k\big]\sim\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{k-1}}\cdot F(I_{n})\cdot e^{-\Lambda(S_{n}-S_{k-1})};|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,X_{k}\geq bn\big]

for large

and
Y
. The next proposition shows that for large
Z>0
, the contribution of sample path with
|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|>Z
to the forgoing expectations can be asymptotically ignored.

Proposition 5.12

Let
Y\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
z_{0},n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
Z\geq z_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[F(I_{n})e^{-\Lambda S_{n}};|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|\geq Z\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Proof.
By Assumption
2.1
, it suffices to prove this equality with
F(I_{n})
replaced by
e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}}
.
For
1\leq k\leq n
, notice that
L_{n}\leq S_{k-1}+L_{k,n}
with
L_{k,n}:=\min\{S_{i}-S_{k-1};i=k-1,\cdots,n\}
.
By the independent increments of
S
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n}-\Lambda S_{n}};|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|\geq Z\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}(S_{k-1}+L_{k,n})-\Lambda S_{n}};|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|\geq Z\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)S_{k-1}};|S_{k-1}|\leq Y\big]\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n-k+1}-\Lambda S_{n-k+1}};|S_{n-k+1}|\geq Z\big].

The first expectation on the right side of the last equality can be bounded by
e^{(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)Y}
.
Moreover, notice that
\{S_{n-k+1}\leq-Z\}\subset\{L_{n-k+1}\leq-Z\}
and
\{S_{n-k+1}\geq Z\}\subset\{S_{n-k+1}-L_{n-k+1}\geq Z\}
,
the second expectation can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n-k+1}-\Lambda S_{n-k+1}};L_{n-k+1}\leq-Z\big]+\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n-k+1}-\Lambda S_{n-k+1}};S_{n-k+1}-L_{n-k+1}\geq Z\big].

(5.27)

The first term can be written into

\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n-k+1}-\Lambda S_{n-k+1}}\big]-\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{\theta_{F}L_{n-k+1}-\Lambda S_{n-k+1}};-L_{n-k+1}<Z\big].

Applying Lemma
3.11
to the first expectation with
\lambda_{1}=\theta_{F}-\Lambda
,
\lambda_{2}=\Lambda
,
(x,y)=(\infty,\infty)
and then to the second expectation with
(x,y)=(Z,\infty)
,
we have for some constants
C,n_{0}>0
, the first expectation in (
5.27
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle C\int_{Z}^{\infty}e^{-(\theta_{F}-\Lambda)z}\big(V(dz)+V(z)dz\big)\cdot B_{n},

uniformly in
n\geq n_{0}
.
By the two facts that
\theta_{F}-\Lambda>0
and
V(z)=O(z)
as
z\to\infty
, the preceding integral vanishes as
Z\to\infty
.
Similarly, the second expectation in (
5.27
) also can be uniformly bounded by

\displaystyle C\int_{Z}^{\infty}e^{-\Lambda z}\big(\hat{V}(dz)+\hat{V}(z)dz\big)\cdot B_{n}

and this integral also vanishes as
Z\to\infty
.
Hence the desired result follows.

\Box

Let
r>\frac{\beta+1}{\beta-1}
and
b\in(0,a/r)
.
Combining all estimates above together, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n})]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sim\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|\leq Z,X_{k}\geq bn\big]

for large
Y
,
Z
and
n
.
By the Markov property of
S
, the summand can be written into

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{k-1}}F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n);|S_{k-1}|\leq Y\big]

(5.28)

with

\displaystyle F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n):=\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}}F\big(I_{k-1}+e^{-S_{k-1}}\tilde{I}_{n-k+1}\big);|\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}|\leq Z,\tilde{X}_{1}\geq bn\big|\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k-1}\big].

It is obvious that
F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n)
plays the main role in the asymptotic analysis of the expectation
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n})]
.
Recall the constant
\kappa\in(1,2)
defined in Condition
2.16
.
The next proposition states that
\{|\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}|\leq Z,\tilde{X}_{1}\geq bn\}
is asymptotically equivalent to
\{|\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}|\leq Z,|\tilde{X}_{1}-an|\geq n^{1/\kappa}\}
.

Proposition 5.13

For any
b\in(0,a)
,
Z\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X_{1}\in[bn,an-n^{1/\kappa}]\cup[an+n^{1/\kappa},\infty);|S_{n}|\leq Z\big)\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Proof.
For
\delta\in(0,1)
and large
n\geq 1
, we first have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X_{1}\in[bn,an-n^{1/\kappa}];|S_{n}|\leq Z\big)=\int_{bn}^{an-n^{1/\kappa}}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(|x+S_{n-1}|\leq Z)\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in dx)

(5.29)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{[((a-b)n-n^{1/\kappa})/\delta]}\int_{bn+k\delta}^{bn+(k+1)\delta}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(|x+S_{n-1}|\leq Z)\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in dx).

(5.30)

Notice that
\{|x+S_{n-1}|\leq Z\}\subset\{|bn+k\delta+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+1\}
uniformly in
x\in[bn+k\delta,bn+(k+1)\delta)
. Hence the preceding summand can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(|bn+k\delta+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+1\big)\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X\in(bn+k\delta,bn+(k+1)\delta]\big).

By Assumption
2.14
and (
2.12
), there exist constants
C>0
and
n_{0}>0
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}
and
0\leq k\leq[((a-b)n-n^{1/\kappa})/\delta]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X\in(bn+k\delta,bn+(k+1)\delta]\big)\leq C\cdot\frac{\beta}{bn+k\delta}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\geq bn)\cdot\delta\leq C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\delta.

Taking these two estimates back into (
5.29
),
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X_{1}\in[bn,an-n^{1/\kappa}];|S_{n}|\leq Z\big)\leq C\cdot B_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{[((a-b)n-n^{1/\kappa})/\delta]}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(|bn+k\delta+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+1\big)\cdot\delta.

Notice that
\{|bn+k\delta+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+1\}\subset\{|x+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+2\}
uniformly in
x\in(bn+k\delta,bn+(k+1)\delta]
and
k\geq 0
. Then for large
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X_{1}\in[bn,an-n^{1/\kappa}];|S_{n}|\leq Z\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{bn}^{an-n^{1/\kappa}+1}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(|x+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+2)dx

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{bn}^{an-n^{1/\kappa}+1}\int_{-Z-2-x}^{Z+2-x}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n-1}\in dz)dx

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C(Z+2)\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{-Z-3-an+n^{1/\kappa}}^{Z+2-bn}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n-1}\in dz)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C(Z+2)\cdot B_{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n-1}\geq-Z-3-an+n^{1/\kappa})

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C(Z+2)\cdot B_{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(n^{-1/\kappa}\cdot\big(S_{n-1}+a(n-1)\big)\geq 1/2\big).

Here the constant
C>0
is independent of
n,Z
and may change from line to line.
The statement below Condition
2.16
shows that
n^{-1/\kappa}\cdot\big(S_{n-1}+a(n-1)\big)\to 0
in probability and hence
\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{1}\in[bn,an-n^{1/\kappa}];|S_{n}|\leq Z)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Similarly, we also have for large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(X_{1}>an+n^{1/\kappa};|S_{n}|\leq Z\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{an+n^{1/\kappa}}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(|x+S_{n-1}|\leq Z+2)dx

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(S_{n-1}\leq Z+2-an-n^{1/\kappa})

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\cdot B_{n}\cdot\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}\big(n^{-1/\kappa}\cdot\big(S_{n-1}+a(n-1)\big)\leq-1/2\big),

which also goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

\Box

By this proposition, for large
n
we see that
F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n)
can be well approximated by

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}}F\big(I_{k-1}+e^{-S_{k-1}}\cdot\tilde{I}_{n-k+1}\big);|\tilde{S}_{n-k+1}|\leq Z,|\tilde{X}_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big|\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k-1}\big].

Let
\tilde{I}_{k,n}:=\tilde{I}_{n}-\tilde{I}_{k}
for
0\leq k\leq n
.
In the next proposition, we prove that this conditional expectation will not change too much with
\tilde{I}_{n-k+1}
replaced by
\tilde{I}_{n-k+1-W,n-k+1}
for large
W
.

Proposition 5.14

Let
H
be a bounded and Lipschitz continuous function on
(0,\infty)
. For every
Z,\epsilon>0
, there exist two integers
w_{0},n_{0}\geq 1
such that for any
W\geq w_{0}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big|H(I_{n})-H(I_{n-W,n})\big|;|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]\leq\epsilon\cdot B_{n}.

Proof.
The Lipschitz continuity of
H
induces that
\big|H(I_{n})-H(I_{n-W,n})\big|\leq C\cdot\big(I_{n-W}\wedge 1\big)
for some constant
C>0
independent of
S,n,W
.
Hence the expectation in the desired inequality can be bounded by

\displaystyle Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[\big(I_{n-W}\wedge 1\big);|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[\big(\big(e^{-S_{n}}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1}\big)\wedge 1\big);|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{n}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[\big(\big(e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1}\big)\wedge 1\big);|S_{n-1}+X_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{n}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big].

Here the equality follows from the duality lemma.
By the independence between
X_{n}
and
\{S_{k}:k=0,1,\cdots,n-1\}
, the preceding quantities can be written as

\displaystyle Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot\int_{an-n^{1/\kappa}}^{an+n^{1/\kappa}}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[\big((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1})\wedge 1\big);|S_{n-1}+x_{n}|\leq Z\big]\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X_{n}\in dx_{n}).

An argument similar to that below (
5.13
) induces that the foregoing quantity can be bounded by

\displaystyle Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{an-n^{1/\kappa}}^{an+n^{1/\kappa}}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[\big((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1})\wedge 1\big);|S_{n-1}+x_{n}|\leq Z+2\big]dx_{n}

for large
n
and some constant
C>0
independent of
n
.
By the change of variables, it turns to be

\displaystyle Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot B_{n}\cdot\int_{-Z-2}^{Z+2}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[\big((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1})\wedge 1\big);|S_{n-1}+an-z|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]dz,

which can be bounded by
Ce^{\Lambda Z}\cdot B_{n}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1})\wedge 1)]\cdot 2(Z+1)
.
By the dominated convergence theorem, we have
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,n-1})\wedge 1)]\to\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,\infty})\wedge 1)]
as
n\to\infty
.
Putting all estimates above together, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
W
such that for large
n>1
,

\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}\big|H(I_{n})-H(I_{n-W,n})\big|;|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]\leq C\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}[((e^{Z}\hat{I}_{W-1,\infty})\wedge 1)]\cdot B_{n}.

Since
\hat{I}_{\infty}<\infty
a.s., we have
\hat{I}_{W-1,\infty}\to 0
a.s. as
W\to\infty
and the desired result follows.

\Box

Proposition 5.15

Let
H
be a nonnegative, bounded and continuous function on
(0,\infty)
. For any two integers
W,Z>0
, we have as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\Big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\to\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{-Z}^{Z}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H(e^{-z}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}))\big]dz.

Moreover, the limit coefficient converges as
W\to\infty
and then
Z\to\infty
to

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H(e^{-z}(1+\hat{I}_{\infty}))\big]dz.

Proof.
By the duality lemma and the independent increments of
S
, we first have for
n>W
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]

(5.31)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(e^{-S_{n}}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}));|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{n}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]

(5.33)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{an-n^{1/\kappa}}^{an+n^{1/\kappa}}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+x)}H(e^{-S_{n-1}-x}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}));|S_{n-1}+x|\leq Z\big]\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in dx).

(5.34)

For
\delta>0
,
n\geq 1
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
H_{n,\delta}(i):=\sup_{i\delta\leq x<(i+1)\delta}H(e^{-S_{n-1}-an-x}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}))
.
For each integer
-[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]-1\leq i\leq[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]+1
, we have uniformly in
x\in[an+i\delta,an+(i+1)\delta)
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+x)}H(e^{-S_{n-1}-x}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}));|S_{n-1}+x|\leq Z\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+an+i\delta)}H_{n,\delta}(i);|S_{n-1}+an+i\delta|\leq Z+\delta\big].

Taking this back into the last integral in (
5.31
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{i=-[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]-1}^{[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]}\Big(\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+an+i\delta)}H_{n,\delta}(i);|S_{n-1}+an+i\delta|\leq Z+\delta\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in[an+i\delta,an+(i+1)\delta))\Big).

By Assumption
2.13
and
2.14
, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists an integer
n_{0}>1
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}
and
-[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]-1\leq i\leq[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]+1
,

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)\cdot B_{n}\cdot\delta\leq\mathbf{P}^{(\Lambda)}(X\in[an+i\delta,an+(i+1)\delta))\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot B_{n}\cdot\delta

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\Big]

(5.35)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=-[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]-1}^{[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+an+i\delta)}H_{n,\delta}(i);|S_{n-1}+an+i\delta|\leq Z+\delta\Big]\cdot\delta.

(5.36)

For
y\in\mathbb{R}
, let
H_{\delta}(y):=\sup_{y-2\delta\leq x<y+2\delta}H(e^{-x}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}))
. For each
-[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]-1\leq i\leq[n^{1/\kappa}/\delta]
and
y\in[i\delta,(i+1)\delta)
, we have
H_{n,\delta}(i)\leq H_{\delta}(S_{n-1}+an+y)
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+an+i\delta)}H_{n,\delta}(i);|S_{n-1}+an+i\delta|\leq Z+\delta\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle e^{\Lambda\delta}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+an+y)}H_{\delta}(S_{n-1}+an+y);|S_{n-1}+an+y|\leq Z+2\delta\big].

Taking this back into (
5.35
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)e^{\Lambda\delta}\int_{-n^{1/\kappa}-\delta}^{n^{1/\kappa}+\delta}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda(S_{n-1}+an+y)}H_{\delta}(S_{n-1}+an+y);|S_{n-1}+an+y|\leq Z+2\delta\big]dy

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)e^{\Lambda\delta}\int_{-Z-2\delta}^{Z+2\delta}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H_{\delta}(z);|S_{n-1}+an-z|\leq n^{1/\kappa}+\delta\big]dz.

Here the last equality follows from the change of variables.
The statements below Condition
2.16
yields that
n^{-1/\kappa}|S_{n-1}+an-z|\to 0
uniformly in
|z|\leq Z+2\delta
.
From this and the dominated convergence theorem, we have
\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H_{\delta}(z);|S_{n-1}+an-z|\leq n^{1/\kappa}+\delta\big]\to\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H_{\delta}(z)\big]
as
n\to\infty
.
In addition, the continuity of
H
induces that
H_{\delta}(z)\to H(e^{-z}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}))
as
\delta\to 0+
.
By Fatou’s lemma (as
n\to\infty
) and the dominated convergence theorem (as
\delta,\epsilon\to 0+
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta,\epsilon\to 0+}(1+\epsilon)e^{\Lambda\delta}\int_{-Z-2\delta}^{Z+2\delta}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H_{\delta}(z)\big]dz=\int_{-Z}^{Z}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}H(e^{-z}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}))\big]dz.

On the other hand, a similar argument also can yields that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}H(I_{n-W,n});|S_{n}|\leq Z,|X_{1}-an|\leq n^{1/\kappa}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\int_{-Z}^{Z}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\Big[e^{-\Lambda z}H(e^{-z}(1+\hat{I}_{W-1}))\Big]dz.

Thus the first claim follows. The second one can be gotten by using dominated convergence theorem as
W\to\infty
and then the monotone convergence theorem as
Z\to\infty
.

\Box

Applying the preceding three propositions to
F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n)

Corollary 5.16

For every
b\in(0,a)
and

, we have as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n)\to\int_{-Z}^{Z}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda z}F\big(I_{k-1}+e^{-S_{k-1}-z}(1+\tilde{\hat{I}}_{\infty})\big)\big|\mathscr{F}^{S}_{k-1}\big]dz.

Proof for Theorem
2.17
.

),

\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n})\big]\sim C_{F,5}\cdot B_{n}

For

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});-Y\leq L_{n}\leq S_{n}\leq Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T\big]

\displaystyle+\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});L_{n}\leq-Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T\big]

\displaystyle+\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{n}\geq Y,\mathcal{T}^{bn}\geq T\big].

By the boundedness of

-

\displaystyle\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});L_{n}\leq-Y\big],

.
The third limit can be bounded by

\displaystyle\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{n}\geq Y\big]

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\limsup_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});\sigma_{n}^{-}\in[K,n-K]\big]

\displaystyle+\limsup_{K\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{n}\geq Y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big]

\displaystyle+\limsup_{K\to\infty}\sum_{k=n-K+1}^{n}\limsup_{Y\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{n}\geq Y,\sigma_{n}^{-}=k\big].

By Proposition
5.6
,
5.7
and
5.8
, the three limits on the right side of this inequality equal to
0
. In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n})\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});\mathcal{T}^{bn}\leq T\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});\mathcal{T}^{bn}=k\big].

By (
5.21
) and (
5.22
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n})\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{Y\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{k-1}\geq-Y,X_{k}\geq bn\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{Y\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});S_{k-1}>Y,X_{k}\geq bn\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{Y\to\infty}\lim_{Z\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|>Z,X_{k}\geq bn\big]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{Y\to\infty}\lim_{Z\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n});|S_{k-1}|\leq Y,|S_{n}-S_{k-1}|\leq Z,X_{k}\geq bn\big].

From Proposition
5.11
and
5.12
, both of the first two terms on the right side of the last equality equal to
0
. By (
5.28
), the third term equals to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{Y\to\infty}\lim_{Z\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{k-1}}\cdot B_{n}^{-1}\cdot F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n);|S_{k-1}|\leq Y\big].

For
Y,Z>0
fixed, notice that
F_{\mathscr{F}^{S}}^{b,Z}(k,n)
is uniformly bounded in
n
. By the dominated convergence theorem and Corollary
5.16
, the preceding sum equals to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lim_{Y\to\infty}\lim_{Z\to\infty}\int_{-Z}^{Z}\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{k-1}-\Lambda z}F\big(I_{k-1}+e^{-S_{k-1}-z}(1+\tilde{\hat{I}}_{\infty})\big);|S_{k-1}|\leq Y\big]dz.

Notice that this integral is non-decreasing as
Y,Z\to\infty
.
By the monotone convergence theorem, the limit in the foregoing limit converges to
C_{F,5}(k)
and hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{B_{n}}\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(\Lambda)}\big[e^{-\Lambda S_{n}}F(I_{n})\big]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}C_{F,5}(k)=C_{F,5},

which is finite; see the second claim in Proposition
5.6
.
Here we have got the desired result.

\Box

Abstract
. We first study the Lipschitz spaces Λ d β \Lambda_{d}^{\beta} associated with the Dunkl metric, β ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \beta\in(0,1) , and prove that it is a proper subspace of the classical Lipschitz spaces Λ β \Lambda^{\beta} on ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} , as the Dunkl metric and the Euclidean metric are non-equivalent. Next, we further show that the Lipschitz spaces Λ β \Lambda^{\beta} connects to the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces F ˙ p , D α , q \dot{F}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}} associated with the Dunkl Laplacian △ D \triangle_{\rm D} in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} and to the commutators of the Dunkl Riesz transform and the fractional Dunkl Laplacian △ D − α / 2 \triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2} , 0 < α < N 0<\alpha<\textbf{N} (the homogeneous dimension for Dunkl measure), which is represented via the functional calculus of the Dunkl heat semigroup e − t ​ △ D e^{-t\triangle_{\rm D}} . The key steps in this paper are a finer decomposition of the underlying space via Dunkl metric and Euclidean metric to bypass the use of Fourier analysis, and a discrete weak-type Calderón reproducing formula in these new Triebel–Lizorkin spaces F ˙ p , D α , q \dot{F}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}} .

1.
Introduction and statement of main results

1.1.
Background

It is well-known that the Lipschitz spaces and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces play an important role in modern harmonic analysis and partial differential equations.

In this paper, we establish a theory of Lipschitz spaces in the Dunkl setting
and prove that the commutators of Dunkl Riesz transforms characterize these spaces. Important tools are the reproducing formula built on Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and their duality, which is of independent interest for other function spaces and the related differential equations in Dunkl setting.

It is well known that group structures enter in a decisive way in harmonic analysis. Dunkl theory is associated with a finite reflection group in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It started with the papers

[

,

,
9
]
and was further developed in the papers
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
5
,
17
,

,
22
,

,
25
]
. In
[
3
]
, the authors extended the classical Hardy
H^{1}
to the rational Dunkl setting.
To be more precise, in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the reflection
\sigma_{\alpha}
with respect to the hyperplane
\alpha_{\bot}
orthogonal to a nonzero vector
\alpha
is given by

\sigma_{\alpha}(x)=x-2{\langle x,\alpha\rangle}\alpha\ \|\alpha\|^{-2}.

A finite set
R\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
is called a
root system
if
\sigma_{\alpha}(R)=R
.
Let
R
be a root system in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
normalized so that
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2
for
\alpha\in R
and with
R_{+}
a fixed positive subsystem, and
G
the finite reflection group generated by the reflections
\sigma_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in R
), where
\sigma_{\alpha}(x)=x-\langle\alpha,x\rangle\alpha
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Corresponding to this reflection group, we denote by
\mathcal{O}(x)

the
G
-orbit of a point

x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. There is a natural metric between two G-orbits
\mathcal{O}(x)
and
\mathcal{O}(y)
, given by

d(x,y):=\min\limits_{\sigma\in G}\|x-\sigma(y)\|.

It is clear that
d(x,y)\leq\|x-y\|
. And it is possible that for certain
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
d(x,y)=0
while
\|x-y\|>0
.

The closures of connected components of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\langle x,\alpha\rangle\neq 0
for all
\alpha\in R\}

are called (closed)
Weyl chambers
. We remark that
\|x-y\|=d(x,y)
if and only if
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

belong to the same closed Weyl chamber, see
[
12
]
.

A

\kappa
defined on
R

) is fixed throughout this paper. Let

\displaystyle d\omega(x)=\prod_{\alpha\in R}|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|^{\kappa(\alpha)}dx

be the associated measure in
\mathbb{R}^{N},
see
[
3
]
,
where, here and subsequently,
dx
stands for the Lebesgue measure
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We denote by
\textbf{N}=N+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in R}\kappa(\alpha)
the homogeneous dimension. The measure
d\omega
satisfies

(1.1)

C^{-1}\bigg(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\bigg)^{N}\leqslant\frac{w(B(x,r_{2}))}{w(B(x,r_{1}))}\leqslant C\bigg(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\bigg)^{\textbf{N}}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0<r_{1}<r_{2},

which implies that there is a constant
C>0
such that
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},r>0
and
\lambda\geqslant 1,

(1.2)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\lambda^{N}\omega(B(x,r))\leqslant\omega(B(x,\lambda r))\leqslant C\lambda^{\textbf{N}}\omega(B(x,r)).

The Dunkl (differential) operators
T_{j}
are defined by

T_{j}f(x)=\partial_{j}f(x)+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in R^{+}}\frac{\kappa(\alpha)}{2}\langle\alpha,e_{j}\rangle\frac{f(x)-f(\sigma_{\alpha}(x))}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle},

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{N}
are the standard unit vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The Dunkl Laplacian is then defined as

\triangle_{\rm D}=\sum\limits^{N}_{j=1}T^{2}_{j},
which is equivalent to

\triangle_{\rm D}f(x)=\triangle_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in R}\kappa(\alpha)\Big(\frac{\partial_{\alpha}f(x)}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle}-\frac{f(x)-f(\sigma_{\alpha}(x))}{\langle\alpha,x\rangle^{2}}\Big).

Here
\triangle_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is the standard Euclidean Laplacian.

The operator
\triangle_{\rm D}
is self-adjoint on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
, see
[
3
]
, and generates the heat semigroup

H_{t}f(x)=e^{t\triangle_{\rm D}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\omega(y),

where the heat kernel
h_{t}(x,y)
is a
C^{\infty}
function for all

t>0,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies
h_{t}(x,y)=h_{t}(y,x)>0
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{t}(x,y)d\omega(y)=1.
The size and regularity estimate of

h_{t}(x,y)
were established in
[
3
]
.

Theorem A (
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
).

There are constants
C,c>0
such that

(a)
for every
t>0
and for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

{1\over C{\min\{\omega(B(x,\sqrt{t})),\omega(B(y,\sqrt{t}))\}}}e^{-c\|x-y\|^{2}/t}\leq|h_{t}(x,y)|\leq CV(x,y,\sqrt{t})^{-1}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t},

where
V(x,y,r):=\max\{\omega(B(x,r)),\omega(B(y,r))\}
;

(b)
for every
t>0
and for every
x,y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\|y-y^{\prime}\|<\sqrt{t}
,

|h_{t}(x,y)-h(x,y^{\prime})|\leq C\bigg(\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)V(x,y,\sqrt{t})^{-1}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}.

In
[
11
]
, they improve the upper and lower bounds for Dunkl heat kernel : for all
c_{l}>\frac{1}{4}
and
0<c_{u}<\frac{1}{4}

there are constants

such that

C_{l}\omega(B(x,\sqrt{t}))^{-1}e^{-c_{l}\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t}}\Lambda(x,y,t)\leq h_{t}(x,y)\leq C_{u}\omega(B(x,\sqrt{t}))^{-1}e^{-c_{u}\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{t}}\Lambda(x,y,t)

where

can be expressed by means of some rational functions of
\|x-\sigma(y)\|/\sqrt{t}
.

1.2.
Statement of main results

1.2.1.
Part I: Lipschitz Spaces

We recall the standard Lipschitz space on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and introduce a new Lipschitz space associated with the Dunkl metric
d
.

Definition 1.1
.

For
\beta\in(0,1)
, the Lipschitz space
\Lambda^{\beta}
is the set of functions
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}<\infty.

We now introduce the Lipschitz space
\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}
associated with the Dunkl metric
d
as follows.
For
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}
is the set of functions
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}}:=\sup_{d(x,y)\neq 0}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\beta}}<\infty.

We will show equivalent definitions of these two spaces which will be useful in applications to commutator operators.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\beta\in(0,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
, the following are equivalent to (
1.3
):

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|d\omega(x);

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{q}d\omega(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the sup is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
f_{B}=\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}f(x)d\omega(x)
.

Moreover, the following are equivalent to (
1.4
):

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(\mathcal{O}(B))}\int_{\mathcal{O}(B)}|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{O}(B)}|d\omega(x);

(1.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{O}(B))}\int_{\mathcal{O}(B)}|f(x)-f_{\mathcal{O}(B)}|^{q}d\omega(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the sup is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
f_{\mathcal{O}(B)}=\frac{1}{\omega(\mathcal{O}(B))}\int_{\mathcal{O}(B)}f(x)d\omega(x)
.

Finally, we have the following containment relation.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\beta\in(0,1)
,
\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}\subsetneq\Lambda^{\beta}
.

1.2.2.
Part II: The Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin Space and Commutators

Here and throughout, we denote by
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, the set of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}:=\big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f(x)|^{p}d\omega(x)\big\}^{1/p}<\infty.

We now introduce a new test function space as follows and then introduce the Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
{\mathfrak{R}}>1
be a fixed constant. Let
q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}
and
\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}
be given as in Theorem
3.1
.
For
|\alpha|<1<{\mathfrak{R}}
,
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, define

\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega):\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Big\|\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in Q^{k}}\big({\mathfrak{R}}^{k\alpha}|q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}(f)(x_{Q})|\big)^{q}\chi_{Q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\quad&\text{if}\ \ 1\leq q<\infty\\
\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in Q^{k}}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k\alpha}|q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}(f)(x_{Q})|\chi_{Q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}&\text{if}\ \ q=\infty.\end{cases}

Similarly, define
\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,\psi}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega):\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,\psi}}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,\psi}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Big\|\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in Q^{k}}\big({\mathfrak{R}}^{k\alpha}|\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}(f)(x_{Q})|\big)^{q}\chi_{Q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\quad&\text{if}\ \ 1\leq q<\infty\\
\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in Q^{k}}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k\alpha}|\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}(f)(x_{Q})|\chi_{Q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}&\text{if}\ \ q=\infty.\end{cases}

The Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin space is then given by the following definition.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
|\alpha|<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
.
The Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin space
{\dot{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}
is defined by the collection of distributions
f\in(\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},{\psi}})^{\prime}
such that

(1.9)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}\omega(Q)\lambda_{Q}\psi_{Q}(x,x_{Q})

with
\Big\|\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}\chi_{Q}(\cdot)\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}<\infty,
where the series converges in the distributional sense and
\psi_{Q}=\psi_{j}
if
Q\in Q^{j}.

If
f\in{\dot{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}},
the norm of
f
is defined by

\|f\|_{{\dot{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}:=\inf\Bigg\{\Big\|\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}\chi_{Q}(\cdot)\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\Bigg\},

where the infimum is taken over all

f(x)
in the form (
1.9
).

We remark that if
\Big\|\Big\{\sum\limits_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}|\lambda_{Q}|^{q}\chi_{Q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}<\infty,
then the series in (
1.9
) defines a distribution in
(\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},{\psi}})^{\prime}.

We have the following characterization of the Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin space
{\dot{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}
.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
|\alpha|<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Then

{\dot{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}=\overline{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}},

where
\overline{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}
is the collection of distributions
f\in(\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},{\psi}})^{\prime}
such that there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
with
\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\rightarrow 0
as
n,m\rightarrow\infty.
Moreover,
f_{n}
converges to
f
in
(\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},{\psi}})^{\prime}.

We will establish the connection between the Lipschitz spaces
\Lambda^{\beta}
and the commutator of the Riesz transform
R_{{\rm D},\ell}:=T_{\ell}(-\triangle_{\rm D})^{-{1\over 2}}
associated to the Dunkl Laplacian
\triangle_{\rm D}
. Comparing to the classical known results, the key step here is a finer decomposition of the underlying space via Dunkl metric to bypass the use of Fourier transform.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<\beta<1
.
The function
b\in\Lambda^{\beta}
if and only if the commutator
[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
to
\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}
. Moreover,

\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\sim\|[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}.

Recall the fractional operator
\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}
with
0<\alpha<{\bf N}
, which is defined by

\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)={1\over\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e^{t\triangle_{\rm D}}}(f)(x){dt\over t^{1-\alpha/2}}.

Similarly we are also able to obtain the following estimates of the Lipschitz space
\Lambda^{\beta}
via the commutator
[b,\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}](f)(x):=b(x)\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}(f)(x)-\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}(bf)(x)
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty
,
0<\beta<1
and
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}
.
If
b\in\Lambda^{\beta}
, then the commutator
[b,\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}]
is a bounded operator from

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
to
\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}
. Moreover,
\|[b,\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}
.

Finally, when the parameter
\beta=0
we can demonstrate that the BMO space associated with the Euclidean metric and Dunkl measure
\omega
is the suitable replacement for
\Lambda^{0}
. To be more precise, we recall that
{\rm BMO}_{\rm Dunkl}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
is the set of all

b\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)

such that

\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{{\rm Dunkl}}}:=\sup_{B}{1\over\omega(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|d\omega(x)<\infty,

where the sup is taken over all balls
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose
b\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
,
0<\alpha<{\bf N}
,
1<p<{{\bf N}\over\alpha}
and
{1\over q}={1\over p}-{\alpha\over{\bf N}}
.
If
b\in{\rm BMO}_{{\rm Dunkl}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
, then
[b,\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}]
is a bounded map from

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
with
\|[b,\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{{\rm Dunkl}}}
.

Here we point out that it is still an open question whether the reverse direction of Theorems
1.8
or
1.9
is true.

Throughout the paper we use the notation
X\lesssim Y
to denote that there is an absolute constant
C
so that
X\leq CY
. If we write
X\sim Y
, then we mean that
X\lesssim Y
and
Y\lesssim X
.

2.
The characterization of the Lipschitz spaces

We first show the characterization of the Lipschitz spaces.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
X=\mathbb{R}^{N}/G
be the space of orbits equipped with the metric
d(\mathcal{O}(x),\mathcal{O}(y))=d(x,y)

and the measure
{\bf m}(A)=\omega\big(\cup_{\mathcal{O}(x)\in A}\mathcal{O}(x)\big)
. So
(X,d,{\bf m})
is the space
of homogeneous type of in the sense of Coifman–Weiss (see
[
3
, page 2403]

Hence the proofs of the characterization of the Lipschitz spaces
\Lambda^{\beta}
and
\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}
are similar.
It suffices to prove (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are equivalent.
We first show that (
1.3
) implies (
1.6
).
For every
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{q}d\omega(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f(y)|d\omega(y)\bigg)^{q}d\omega(x)\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}d\omega(y)\bigg)^{q}d\omega(x)\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}.

Hence, (
1.3
) implies (
1.6
).

Next, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}}\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|d\omega(x)\leq\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|^{q}d\omega(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Hence, we get that (
1.6
) implies (
1.5
).

To show (
1.5
) implies (
1.3

Suppose

are the approximation operator to the identity in the space of homogeneous type

satisfying the

[

(i)

\displaystyle|E_{k}(x,y)|\leqslant C\frac{1}{V_{k}(x)+V_{k}(y)+V(x,y)}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k}+\|x-y\|};

(ii)

\displaystyle|E_{k}(x,y)-E_{k}(x^{\prime},y)|\leqslant C\frac{\|x-x^{\prime}\|)}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k}+\|x-x^{\prime}\|}\frac{1}{V_{k}(x)+V_{k}(y)+V(x,y)}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k}+\|x-y\|}
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E_{k}(x,y)d\omega(x)=0\qquad\text{for all}\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{N};

By the method in
[
6

[
19

via (

Now for any
k\in\mathbb{Z},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}|E_{k}f(x)|

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}E_{k}(x,y)(f(y)-f_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})})d\omega(y)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{1\over\omega(B(x,\|x-y\|))}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}+\|x-y\|}|f(y)-f_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{j}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})\backslash B(x,2^{j-1}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}{1\over\omega(B(x,\|x-y\|))}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}+\|x-y\|}|f(y)-f_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\quad+C{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}\int_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}{1\over\omega(B(x,\|x-y\|))}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}+\|x-y\|}|f(y)-f_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{j}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}{1\over\omega(B(x,2^{j-1}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}+2^{j-1}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}|f(y)-f_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\quad+C{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\beta}{1\over\omega((B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})))}\int_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|f(y)-f_{B(x,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\int_{B}|f(x)-f_{B}|d\omega(x).

Hence, (

), (

) and (

) are equivalent.
The proof is completed.

We next show that the Lipschitz space associated to the metric
d
is a proper subset of the Lipschitz space associated the Euclidean norm.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose
f\in\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}
. Then
d(x,y)\leq\|x-y\|
gives

\sup_{d(x,y)\neq 0}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}\leq\sup_{d(x,y)\neq 0}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\beta}}<\infty.

Note that
d(x,y)=0
implies that
y=\sigma(x)
for some
\sigma\in G
.
It is clear that

\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}\leq\sup_{d(x,y)\neq 0}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}+\sup_{\{y=\sigma(x):\sigma\in G\}\atop y\neq x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\|x-y\|^{\beta}}<\infty

since
G
is finite group. Hence
f\in\Lambda^{\beta}
. Thus, we first obtain

\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}\subset\Lambda^{\beta}.

Next, we consider an example in the real line
\mathbb{R}
to show that the containment is proper.
Take the function
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
given by

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-1,\quad x\leq-{\pi\over 2};\\[5.0pt]
\sin x,\ \ \ -{\pi\over 2}<x<{\pi\over 2};\\[5.0pt]
1,\quad x\geq{\pi\over 2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Then it is clear that this
f
belongs to
\Lambda^{\beta}
for every
\beta\in(0,1)
. However, we take
x>\pi
and we take
y=-x-\delta
with
\delta
a small positive number, for example,
\delta<100^{-1}
. Then we have

It is clear that

|f(x)-f(y)|=2>\delta^{\beta}=d(x,y)^{\beta}

for every
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Hence,

\sup_{d(x,y)\neq 0}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\beta}}=\infty,

which shows that

does not belong to
\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}
and so in general we have
\Lambda^{\beta}_{d}\subsetneq\Lambda^{\beta}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
is complete.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\leq C\|R_{1}(f)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,CW}}=C\Big\|\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in Q^{k}}\big({\mathfrak{R}}^{k\alpha}|D_{k}(R_{1}f)(x_{Q})|\big)^{q}\chi_{Q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\leq C({\mathfrak{R}}-1)\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,CW}}

\displaystyle\leq C({\mathfrak{R}}-1)\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}.

Similarly,

\|R_{M}(f)\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\leq C{\mathfrak{R}}^{-M}\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}.

Hence, the inequality (
3.13
) holds and the inequality gives

\|T_{M}^{-1}f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}.

Set
h=T_{M}^{-1}f
. we obtain
f=T_{M}h

which implies
\|h\|_{L^{2}_{\omega}}\sim\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\omega}}
and
\|h\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}.

Since

f=T_{M}h=-\ln{\mathfrak{R}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(x,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q}).

It remains to show that the above series converges in
\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}
.
We observe that

f(x)+\ln{\mathfrak{R}}\sum_{|j|\leq\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(x,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q})=-\ln{\mathfrak{R}}\sum_{|j|>\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(x,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q})

for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega)
. Therefore, we only to show that

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\Big\|\sum_{|j|>\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(x,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q})\Big\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}=0.

Since

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{|j|>\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(x,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q})\Big\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}

\displaystyle=C\Big\|\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{Q\in Q^{k}}\Big({\mathfrak{R}}^{k\alpha}\Big|q_{k}\Big(\sum_{|j|>\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(\cdot,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q})\Big)(x_{Q})\Big|\Big)^{q}\chi_{Q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}},

the same argument as the proof of (
3.11
) yields

\Big\|\sum_{|j|>\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}w(Q)\psi_{j}(x,x_{Q})q_{j}h(x_{Q})\Big\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\leq C\Big\|\Big\{\sum_{|j|>\ell}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}\big({\mathfrak{R}}^{j\alpha}|q_{j}(h)(x_{Q})|\big)^{q}\chi_{Q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

Since
\|h\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\sim\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}
, the right-hand side of the above inequality goes to
0
as
\ell\to\infty
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Given
f\in\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}
and
g\in\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},{\psi}}
, we use Theorem
3.3
to get

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle=\Big|\Big\langle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}\omega(Q)\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}(\cdot,x_{Q})q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}h(x_{Q}),g\Big\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}\omega(Q)|q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}h(x_{Q})||\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}g(x_{Q})|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{Q\in Q^{j}}|q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}h(x_{Q})||\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}g(x_{Q})|\chi_{Q}(x)d\omega(x),

where the second inequality follows since
\psi
is a radial function.
By Hölder’s inequality applied twice,

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big\{\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}({\mathfrak{R}}^{j\alpha}|q_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}h(x_{Q})|)^{q}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big\{\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{Q\in Q^{j}}({\mathfrak{R}}^{-j\alpha}|\psi_{{\mathfrak{R}}^{j}}g(x_{Q})|)^{q^{\prime}}\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\chi_{Q}(x)d\omega(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\|g\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},\psi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{p,{\rm D}}}\|g\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},\psi}}.

The proof is complete.

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose that
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

3.4

Applying Theorem
3.3
, for each
f_{n}
there exists an

To show that
f
has a weak-type discrete Calderón reproducing formula in the distribution sense, for each

3.12

3.3

1.6

by Theorem
3.3
, there exists a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

and

\displaystyle\mbox{(i) }B=B_{1}\cup B_{2},\widetilde{B}=E_{1}\cup E_{2}\mbox{ and }\omega(E_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2}\omega(\widetilde{B}),i=1,2;

\displaystyle\mbox{(ii) }b(x)-b(y)\mbox{ does not change sign for all }(x,y)\in B_{i}\times E_{i},i=1,2;

\displaystyle\mbox{(iii) }|b(x)-m_{b}(\tilde{B})|\leq|b(x)-b(y)|\mbox{ for all }(x,y)\in B_{i}\times E_{i},i=1,2.

We have that, for
(x,y)\in B_{i}\times E_{i},i=1,2
,

|R_{{\rm D},\ell}(x,y)|\geq\frac{1}{\omega(B(\tilde{x}_{0},r))}.

Let
f_{i}=\chi_{E_{i}},i=1,2.

Then the facts (
4.8
) give

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\bigg|\int_{B}[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}](f_{i})(x)a(x)d\omega(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\bigg|\int_{B_{i}}[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}](f_{i})(x)a(x)d\omega(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{i}}\int_{E_{i}}|b(x)-b(y)||R_{{\rm D},\ell}(x,y)||a(x)|d\omega(y)d\omega(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{i}}|b(x)-m_{b}(\widetilde{B})|\frac{1}{\omega(B(\tilde{x}_{0},r))}\int_{E_{i}}d\omega(y)d\omega(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{B_{i}}|b(x)-m_{b}(\widetilde{B})|d\omega(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|d\omega(x).

On the other hand, from duality and the boundedness of
[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\bigg|\int_{B}[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}](f_{i})(x)a(x)d\omega(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]f_{i}\|_{\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\|a\|_{\dot{F}_{p^{\prime},{\rm D}}^{-\beta,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\|a\|_{\dot{F}_{p^{\prime}{\rm D}}^{-\beta,1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\|[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\omega(\widetilde{B})^{1/p}r^{\beta}\omega(B)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}.

Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{\ell(B)^{\beta}\omega(B)}\int_{B}|b(x)-b_{B}|d\omega(x)\lesssim\|[b,R_{{\rm D},\ell}]\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}\to\dot{F}_{p,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
is complete.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
K_{\alpha}(x,y)
be the kernel of fractional operator
\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}
.
Then we have the following estimates:

(a)

\displaystyle|K_{\alpha}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}

for

(b)

\displaystyle|K_{\alpha}(x,y)-K_{\alpha}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\|x-y\|^{2}}d(x,y)^{1+\alpha}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}
if
\|y-y^{\prime}\|<\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)

(c)

\displaystyle|K_{\alpha}(x,y)-K_{\alpha}(x^{\prime},y)|\lesssim\frac{\|x-x^{\prime}\|}{\|x-y\|^{2}}d(x,y)^{1+\alpha}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}
if
\|x-x^{\prime}\|<\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)

Proof.

Note that

\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)={1\over\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e^{t\triangle_{\rm D}}}(f)(x){dt\over t^{1-\alpha/2}}

and

e^{t\triangle_{D}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\omega(y),

where
h_{t}(x,y)
is the Dunkl-heat kernel.
We write the fractional operator as follows:

\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)d\omega(y).

To estimate the kernel
K_{\alpha}(x,y),
we recall the following estimates
for the Dunkl-heat kernel given in
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]

(a)

There are constants
C,c>0
such that

|h_{t}(x,y)|\leqslant C\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}\bigg(1+\frac{\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)^{-2}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t},

for every
t>0
and for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(b)

There are constants
C,c>0
such that

|h_{t}(x,y)-h(x,y^{\prime})|\leqslant C\bigg(\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}\bigg(1+\frac{\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)^{-2}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t},

for every
t>0
and for every
x,y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
\|y-y^{\prime}\|<\sqrt{t}
.

We now estimate the kernel
R_{j}(x,y)
as follows.

\displaystyle|K_{\alpha}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}\frac{t}{\|x-y\|^{2}}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}\frac{dt}{t^{1-\alpha/2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{d(x,y)^{2}}+\int_{d(x,y)^{2}}^{\infty}\bigg)\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}t^{\alpha}dt

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
t\leqslant d(x,y)^{2}
, by using the doubling condition we have
that

\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))\lesssim\Big(\frac{d(x,y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}\omega(B(x,\sqrt{t}))

and hence

We obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}\int_{0}^{d(x,y)^{2}}\Big(\frac{d(x,y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\mathbf{N}}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}t^{\alpha/2}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}\int_{0}^{d(x,y)^{2}}\frac{d(x,y)^{\mathbf{N}}}{t^{\frac{\mathbf{N}}{2}}}\Big(\frac{t}{d(x,y)^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\mathbf{N}}{2}}t^{\alpha/2}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}.

It is clear that for
t\geqslant d(x,y)^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\int_{d(x,y)^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{V(x,y,d(x,y))}\frac{d(x,y)^{4+\alpha}}{t^{2+\alpha/2}}t^{\alpha/2}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{d(x,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}.

To see the smoothness estimates, we write

\displaystyle|K_{\alpha}(x,y)-K_{\alpha}(x,y^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leqslant c\int_{0}^{\infty}|h_{t}(x,y)-h_{t}(x,y^{\prime})|\frac{dt}{t^{1-\alpha/2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\bigg(\int_{0}^{d(x,y)^{2}}+\int_{d(x,y)^{2}}^{\infty}\bigg)|h_{t}(x,y)-h_{t}(x,y^{\prime})|\frac{dt}{t^{1-\alpha/2}}

\displaystyle=:I\!I_{1}+I\!I_{2}.

Since
\|y-y^{\prime}\|<\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)
, we have
d(x,y^{\prime})\leq\frac{3}{2}d(x,y)
.
If
\|y-y^{\prime}\|\geq\sqrt{t}

|h_{t}(x,y)-h_{t}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}\bigg(1+\frac{\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)^{-2}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}.

If

|h_{t}(x,y)-h_{t}(x,y^{\prime})|\lesssim\Big(\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}\bigg(1+\frac{\|x-y\|}{\sqrt{t}}\bigg)^{-2}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}.

\displaystyle I\!I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\int_{0}^{d(x,y)^{2}}\frac{1}{V(x,y,\sqrt{t})}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|y-y^{\prime}\|}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}\int_{0}^{d(x,y)^{2}}\Big(\frac{d(x,y)}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\bf N}\Big(\frac{t}{d(x,y)^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{\bf N}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{dt}{t^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}}}

\displaystyle I\!I_{2}

The estimate of the smoothness in the

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
0<\alpha<{\bf N}
. If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\omega),1<p<q<\infty
with
1/q=1/p-\alpha/{\bf N}
,
then

\|\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.5
, we have

\displaystyle|\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K_{\alpha}(x,y)f(y)d\omega(y)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{d(x,y)\leq s}\frac{d(x,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}|f(y)|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{d(x,y)>s}\frac{d(x,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}|f(y)|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}.

For
J_{1}
, by decomposing the set
\{{d(x,y)\leq s}\}
into annuli and by noting that
s<d(x,y)\leq\|x-y\|
, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-j-1}s<d(x,y)\leq 2^{-j}s}\frac{d(x,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))}|f(y)|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-j-1}s<d(x,y)\leq 2^{-j}s}\frac{(2^{-j}s)^{2+\alpha}}{(2^{-j-1}s)^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,2^{-j-1}s))}|f(y)|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{-j}s)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,2^{-j}s))}\int_{d(x,y)\leq 2^{-j}s}|f(y)|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{\alpha}M(f)(x),

where
M
is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function on the space of homogeneous type
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\|\cdot\|,\omega)
.

For
J_{2}
, by noting that
s<d(x,y)\leq\|x-y\|
, we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{d(x,y)>s}\frac{d(x,y)^{(2+\alpha)p^{\prime}}}{\|x-y\|^{2p^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,y)))^{p^{\prime}}}d\omega(y)\bigg)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{d(x,y)>s}d(x,y)^{\alpha p^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(y,d(x,y)))^{p^{\prime}}}d\omega(y)\bigg)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{d(x,y)>s}\frac{d(x,y)^{\alpha p^{\prime}}}{d(x,y)^{Np^{\prime}}\prod\limits_{\alpha\in R}\big(|\langle\alpha,y\rangle|+d(x,y)\big)^{\kappa(\alpha)p^{\prime}}}\prod_{\alpha\in R}\big(|\langle\alpha,y\rangle|^{\kappa(\alpha)}dy\bigg)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{d(x,y)>s}\frac{1}{d(x,y)^{Np^{\prime}-\alpha p^{\prime}+\sum\limits_{\alpha\in R}\kappa(\alpha)(p^{\prime}-1)}}dy\bigg)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{t>s}\frac{1}{t^{{\bf N}(p^{\prime}-1)-\alpha p^{\prime}+1}}dt\bigg)^{1\over p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim s^{\frac{\bf N}{p^{\prime}}-{\bf N}+\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

Hence

|\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)|\lesssim s^{\alpha}M(f)(x)+s^{\frac{\bf N}{p^{\prime}}-{\bf N}+\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

Choosing
s=\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}}{Mf(x)}\Big)^{\frac{p}{\bf N}}
, we obtain

|\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)|\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}^{\frac{\alpha p}{\bf N}}Mf(x)^{1-\frac{\alpha p}{\bf N}}.

Note that
({\bf N}-\alpha p)q=p{\bf N}
.

\|\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}^{\frac{\alpha p}{\bf N}}\|Mf\|^{1-\frac{\alpha p}{\bf N}}_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}^{\frac{\alpha p}{\bf N}}\|f\|^{1-\frac{\alpha p}{\bf N}}_{L^{p}_{\omega}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

The proof of Theorem
4.6
is complete.

s_{1}
,
we use Hölder’s inequality and (
4.5
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\|(b-b_{B})f_{1}\|^{s}_{L^{s}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{5B}|b(y)-b_{B}|^{s}|f|^{s}d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\leq{\Big(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{5B}|b(y)-b_{B}|^{ss_{1}^{\prime}}d\omega(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}^{\prime}}}}\Big(\frac{1}{\omega(B)}\int_{5B}|f|^{ss_{1}}d\omega(y)\Big)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(B)^{\beta s}\|b\|^{s}_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M^{s}_{ss_{1}}(f)(x)

the
L^{p}_{\omega}
-boundedness of the maximal function
M_{ss_{1}}
implies that

(4.14)

\|h_{12}\|_{\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

We turn to analyze
h_{22}
. Observe that for
z\notin{\mathcal{O}}(5B)
and
y\in B
we have
\|x-y\|\leq d(x,z)/2
.
Let
\Gamma
be a fixed closed Weyl chamber such that
x\in\Gamma
, then Lemma
4.5
gives

\displaystyle|h_{22}(y)-h_{22}(x)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(K_{\alpha}(y,z)-K_{\alpha}(x,z))(b(z)-b_{B})f_{2}(z)d\omega(z)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{\sigma(\Gamma)}|K_{\alpha}(y,z)-K_{\alpha}(x,z)||b(z)-b_{B}||f_{2}(z)|d\omega(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{\sigma(\Gamma)}\frac{\|y-x\|}{\|x-z\|^{2}}d(x,z)^{1+\alpha}\frac{1}{\omega(B(x,d(x,z)))}|b(z)-b_{B}||f_{2}(z)|d\omega(z).

Note that

\omega(B(x,r))\sim r^{N}\prod_{\alpha\in R}\big(|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|+r\big)^{\kappa(\alpha)}\geq r^{\bf N}.

By the same way as in the estimate
J_{\sigma}(x,y)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(B)^{\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}\ell(B)^{\beta}}|h_{22}(y)-h_{22}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\omega(B)^{\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}\ell(B)^{\beta}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\int_{\sigma(\Gamma)}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{\|x-z\|^{2}}\frac{d(x,z)^{1+\alpha}}{\omega(B(\sigma(x),d(x,z)))}|b_{B(\sigma(x),{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}-b_{B}||f_{2}(z)|d\omega(z)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\omega(B)^{\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}\ell(B)^{\beta}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{\|x-z\|^{2}}\frac{d(x,z)^{1+\alpha}}{\omega(B(\sigma(x),d(x,z)))}|b_{B(\sigma(x),{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}-b(z)||f_{2}(z)|d\omega(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}M(f_{2})(\sigma(x))\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}2^{-j(1-\alpha-\beta)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}M(f_{2})(\sigma(x))

for
\alpha+\beta<1
.
Thus, we use Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.6
to obtain

\displaystyle\|h_{22}\|_{\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}

\displaystyle{\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\ell(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{\beta}\omega(B(\cdot,\mathfrak{R}^{k}))}\int_{B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|h_{22}(y)-h_{22}(\cdot)|d\omega(y)\Big\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}}}

\displaystyle{\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\ell(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{\beta}\omega(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}}\int_{B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|h_{22}(y)-h_{22}(\cdot)|\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\ell(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{\beta}\omega(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}}\sup_{y\in B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|h_{22}(y)-h_{22}(\cdot)|\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}{\sum_{\sigma\in G}}\|M(f_{2})(\sigma(\cdot))\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

for
\alpha+\beta<1
.

Note that Theorem
3.12
establishes the equivalence between the Dunkl–Triebel–Lizorkin spaces and the well-understood Triebel–Lizorkin spaces on spaces of homogeneous type. Note again
\omega(B(x,r))\sim r^{N}\prod_{\alpha\in R}\big(|\langle\alpha,x\rangle|+r\big)^{\kappa(\alpha)}\geq r^{\bf N}
for all
0<r<\infty
. From this lower bound of the measure of the ball
B(x,r)
, by using
[
14
, Theorem 5.1 (2) (d)]
, we have
\dot{F}_{u,{\rm D}}^{\beta_{0},2}\hookrightarrow L^{s}_{\omega}\sim\dot{F}_{s,{\rm D}}^{0,2}
for
1/u-1/s=\beta_{0}/{\bf N}
and
u<s
.
Since
\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\beta/2}=\triangle_{\rm D}^{-{(\alpha+\beta)}/2}
,
we get rid of the assumption
\alpha+\beta<1
by the method in
[
20
, Facts 1.9 and 1.10]
and hence

(4.15)

\|h_{22}\|_{\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

Finally, we turn to estimate
h_{\sigma_{j}2}
.
To this end we use the same method in the estimate
g_{\sigma_{j}2}
and Lemma
4.5
to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega(B)^{\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}}\int_{B}|K_{\alpha}(z,y)|d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim{\mathfrak{R}}^{-k\alpha}\int_{B}\frac{d(z,y)^{2+\alpha}}{\|z-y\|^{2}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(z,d(z,y)))}d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}+\|x-\sigma_{j}(x)\|}\sum_{j=-4}^{\infty}2^{-j(1-\alpha)}\int_{d(z,y)\sim 2^{-j}{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}\frac{1}{\omega(B(z,d(z,y)))}d\omega(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}}{{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}+\|x-\sigma_{j}(x)\|}

for
\alpha<1
and hence

\frac{1}{\omega(B)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}\ell(B)^{\beta}}\int_{B}|h_{\sigma_{j}2}(y)-(h_{\sigma_{j}2})_{B}|d\omega(y)\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M(f)(\sigma_{j}(x)).

Thus, we use Theorems
4.2
and
4.6
to obtain

\displaystyle\|h_{\sigma_{j}2}\|_{\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\ell(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{\beta}\omega(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))}\int_{B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|h_{\sigma_{j}2}(y)-(h_{\sigma_{j}2})_{B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)\Big\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\ell(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{\beta}\omega(B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k}))^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\bf N}}}\int_{B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|h_{\sigma_{j}2}(y)-(h_{\sigma_{j}2})_{B(\cdot,{\mathfrak{R}}^{k})}|d\omega(y)\Big\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|M(f_{2})(\sigma(\cdot))\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}

for
\alpha<1
.
Since
\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\beta/2}=\triangle_{\rm D}^{-{(\alpha+\beta)}/2}
,
we get rid of the assumption
\alpha<1
by the method in
[
20
, Facts 1.9 and 1.10]
and hence

(4.16)

\|h_{\sigma_{j}2}\|_{\dot{F}_{q,{\rm D}}^{\beta,\infty}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

By (
4.12
)-(
4.16
), the proof is complete.
∎

From the method of Theorem
1.8
, we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

It suffices to verify that

\|([b,\triangle_{\rm D}^{-\alpha/2}]f)^{\sharp}\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|b\|_{{\rm BMO}_{{\rm Dunkl}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}},

where

Note that the
{\rm BMO}_{{\rm Dunkl}}
space has the similar result to Lemma
4.3

, Lemma 4.1]

1.9

1.8

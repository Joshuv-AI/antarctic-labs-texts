Effective formulas for the geometry of normal homogeneous spaces. Application to flag manifolds.
Dimbihery Rabenoro, Xavier Pennec

Abstract
Consider a smooth manifold and an action on it of a compact connected Lie group with a bi-invariant metric. Then, any orbit is an embedded submanifold that is isometric to a normal homogeneous space for the group. In this paper, we establish new explicit and intrinsic formulas for the geometry of any such orbit. We derive our formula of the Levi-Civita connection from an existing generic formula for normal homogeneous spaces, i.e. which determines, a priori only theoretically, the connection. We say that our formulas are effective: they are directly usable, notably in numerical analysis, provided that the ambient manifold is convenient for computations. Then, we deduce new effective formulas for flag manifolds, since we prove that they are orbits under a suitable action of the special orthogonal group on a product of Grassmannians. This representation of them is quite useful, notably for studying flags of eigenspaces of symmetric matrices. Thus, we develop from it a geometric solution to the problem of perturbation of eigenvectors which is explicit and optimal in a certain sense, improving the classical analytic solution.

Introduction

A flag of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a filtration

of linear subspaces
V_{0}=\{0\}\subset V_{1}\subset\ldots\subset V_{r}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is an equivalent characterization of the flag

is a sequence

1.1
Context and main result

Such spaces have recently been a rising subject of interest in statistical machine learning for generalizing Grassmannians and handling multiple subspaces at the same time, as in
Nishimori Akaho and Plumbley 2006 Nishimori Akaho and Plumbley, 2006

On this basis, new robust variants like the minimal accumulated unexplained p-variance have been proposed, even for manifold-valued data

. The ubiquitous appearance of flags in applications, exemplified in
Ye Wong and Lim 2022 Ye Wong and Lim, 2022

In the particular case of

, where

Thus, one of our motivation was to obtain an explicit formula for the Levi-Civita connection of flag manifolds, in a representation of them that is convenient for computations. We say that such a formula is

1.2
Effective formulas

In practice, one can compare different representations of a given homogeneous space, in function of their efficiency for computations. In a general framework, we precise hereafter what we mean by effective formulas. Consider a manifold
{\mathbb{M}}
and a smooth action on
{\mathbb{M}}
of a compact connected Lie group
{\mathbb{G}}
with a bi-invariant metric. For any orbit
{\mathbb{B}}
, fix a point
b_{0}
in
{\mathbb{B}}
, called its origin. The
main orbital map
is the surjective map
{\pi_{0}}
defined by

{\pi_{0}}:{\mathbb{G}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{B}},\quad{\pi_{0}}(Q)=Q\cdot b_{0}

Let
{\mathbb{K}}\subset{\mathbb{G}}
be the isotropy group of
b_{0}
and
\psi:{\mathbb{G}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}}
the canonical quotient map. Then, by
(
Bendokat Zimmermann and Absil 2020 Bendokat Zimmermann and Absil, 2020
, Proposition A.2)
,
{\mathbb{B}}
is an embedded submanifold of
{\mathbb{M}}
and
{\pi_{0}}
induces a diffeomorphism
\widehat{{\pi_{0}}}:{\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{B}}
such that
{\pi_{0}}=\widehat{{\pi_{0}}}\circ\psi
. Endowed with the quotient metric,

{\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}}
is a
normal homogeneous space
. Then, we endow
{\mathbb{B}}
with the metric for which
\widehat{{\pi_{0}}}
is an isometry. Thus,
{\mathbb{B}}
is called a
realization
of
{\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}}
embedded in
{\mathbb{M}}
. We say that
explicit
formulas for the geometry of any such orbit
{\mathbb{B}}
are
effective
in the sense that they are directly usable, notably in numerical analysis, provided that the ambient manifold
{\mathbb{M}}
is convenient for computations.

In this paper, we establish such effective formulas for the Levi-Civita connection and the sectional curvature of
{\mathbb{B}}
. These formulas simplify when
{\mathbb{B}}
is additionally a
symmetric space
. Contrarily to the Riemannian curvature, the connection is not a tensor, so that an explicit expression is much more difficult to obtain. Thus, we focus below on our method for the connection, based on a
generic
formula for the Levi-Civita connection of normal homogeneous spaces, provided in
Alekseevsky 1996 Alekseevsky, 1996
or in
Arvanitoyeorgos 2003 Arvanitoyeorgos, 2003
. This formula is generic in the sense that it holds only for particular vector fields, the
fundamental
ones, and it is implicit in
Alekseevsky 1996 Alekseevsky, 1996
and
Arvanitoyeorgos 2003 Arvanitoyeorgos, 2003
that it fully determines the connection. However, explicit formulas for arbitrary vector fields are not developed therein, nor elsewhere in the literature.

Furthermore, in the numerical analysis literature, explicit formulas for the connection are derived in particular cases of such orbits, by specific developments on a case-by-case basis. Usually, the connection is obtained by associating to vector fields on such an orbit, other ones that are valued in a vector space, in which the connection is computed through usual derivation. Our method for any such orbit
{\mathbb{B}}
is also based on this principle but we associate to any vector field on
{\mathbb{B}}
, a function valued in the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of
{\mathbb{G}}
. This lifting to
{\mathfrak{g}}
allows to exploit the rich structure of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Thus, the
adjoint representation
plays an important role in our method.

1.3
Our method for the connection

By construction, the map
{\pi_{0}}:{\mathbb{G}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{B}}
is a
Riemannian submersion
, which is essential in our method. Thus, in
{\mathfrak{g}}
, we fix an orthonormal basis
(\epsilon_{k})_{k}
of the horizontal space wrt
{\pi_{0}}
. We obtain readily that any vector field on
{\mathbb{B}}
is decomposed into a linear combination of fundamental vector fields
(\epsilon_{k}^{*})_{k}
, associated to the
(\epsilon_{k})_{k}
. Then, we demonstrate that the coefficients of this decomposition are
smooth functions
on a neighborhood of
b_{0}
in
{\mathbb{B}}
. Thus, we establish a handleable expression for these coefficients, from which we prove their smoothness. The key point is the existence of a
smooth
local section of
{\pi_{0}}:{\mathbb{G}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{B}}
which is induced by some diffeomorphisms between fibers under
{\pi_{0}}
, called
holonomy maps
, built from the Riemannian submersion structure of
{\pi_{0}}
.

From such a decomposition with
smooth coefficients
, we deduce an explicit formula for arbitrary vector fields on
{\mathbb{B}}
from the generic one, by applying the Leibniz rule for affine connections. Then, some factorizations allow us to establish a final explicit formula that is also
intrinsic
, i.e. independent of the choice of the basis
(\epsilon_{k})_{k}
. Notice that a method using only O’Neill’s formula
O'Neill 1966 O’Neill, 1966
for the Riemannian submersion
{\pi_{0}}
would not provide such an explicit and intrinsic formula, for which we have fully exploited the structure of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

1.4
Flag manifolds as normal homogeneous spaces

By results of

(1)

1.6
Application to perturbation theory

In

First, we introduce locally symmetric spaces, although they are not in general homogeneous. For
x\in{\mathbb{M}}
, denote by
{\mathrm{inj}}_{x}
the injectivity radius at
x
. The geodesic symmetry through
x
is the map
s_{x}
defined by

s_{x}:B(x,{\mathrm{inj}}_{x}){\longrightarrow}B(x,{\mathrm{inj}}_{x}),\quad s_{x}(y)={\mathrm{Exp}}_{x}^{{\mathbb{M}}}\left(-\left({\mathrm{Exp}}_{x}^{{\mathbb{M}}}\right)^{-1}\left(y\right)\right).

(19)

By definition, a locally symmetric space is a Riemannian manifold
({\mathbb{M}},g)
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{M}}
,
s_{x}
is an isometry on a neighbourhood of
x
.

2.4.2
Symmetric spaces

A Riemannian globally symmetric space is a connected Riemannian manifold
({\mathbb{M}},g)
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{M}}
, there exists an isometry
s_{x}
of
{\mathbb{M}}
such that
s_{x}(x)=x
and
d_{x}s_{x}=-{\mathrm{Id}}_{T_{x}{\mathbb{M}}}
. Such a space, simply called a
symmetric space
is a locally symmetric one, and is complete. Let
{\mathbb{G}}:=\mathrm{Isom}_{0}({\mathbb{M}})
be the connected component of the identity in the group of isometries of
{\mathbb{M}}
. Then,
{\mathbb{G}}
is a connected Lie group which acts transitively on
{\mathbb{M}}
. So,
{\mathbb{M}}
is identified with a
homogeneous space

{\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}}
. For fixed
x\in{\mathbb{M}}
, the map
\sigma_{x}:{\mathbb{G}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbb{G}}
defined by
Q{\penalty\ \longmapsto\penalty\ }s_{x}\circ Q\circ s_{x}
is an involutive automorphism of
{\mathbb{G}}
called a
Cartan involution
, such that
\left({\mathbb{G}}_{\sigma_{x}}\right)_{0}\subset{\mathbb{K}}\subset{\mathbb{G}}_{\sigma_{x}}
, where
{\mathbb{K}}
is the isotropy group of
x
and
{\mathbb{G}}_{\sigma_{x}}:=\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma_{x})=\left\{u\in{\mathfrak{g}}:\sigma_{x}(g)=g\right\}
. In fact, by results of
O'Neill 1983 O’Neill, 1983
, a symmetric space is equivalent to a
symmetric data

({\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}},\sigma,B)
defined below.

Definition 2
.

A symmetric data is a triple
({\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}},\sigma,B)
, where:

(i)

{\mathbb{G}}
is a connected Lie group and
{\mathbb{K}}
a closed subgroup.

(ii)

\sigma
is an involutive automorphism of
{\mathbb{G}}
such that
\left({\mathbb{G}}_{\sigma}\right)_{0}\subset{\mathbb{K}}\subset{\mathbb{G}}_{\sigma}
, where
{\mathbb{G}}_{\sigma}:=\mathrm{Fix}(\sigma)
.

(iii)

B
is an
{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbb{K}})
-invariant scalar product on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}
, where

{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}:=\left\{u\in{\mathfrak{g}}:d_{e}\sigma(u)=-u\right\}.

Denoting by
{\mathfrak{k}}
the Lie algebra of
{\mathbb{K}}
, by
(
O'Neill 1983 O’Neill, 1983
, Lemma 30)
,
{\mathfrak{k}}=\left\{u\in{\mathfrak{g}}:d_{e}\sigma(u)=u\right\}
. Now,
d_{e}\sigma
is involutive, so that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}
. Furthermore,
{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}
is an
{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbb{K}})
-invariant subspace, which implies that
{\mathbb{G}}/{\mathbb{K}}
is
reductive
. Finally, the following relations hold:

{\llbracket}{\mathfrak{k}},{\mathfrak{k}}{\rrbracket}\subset{\mathfrak{k}},\quad{\llbracket}{\mathfrak{k}},{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}{\rrbracket}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}\quad\textrm{and}\quad{\llbracket}{\mathfrak{m}}_{s},{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}{\rrbracket}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}.

(20)

The condition
{\llbracket}{\mathfrak{m}}_{s},{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}{\rrbracket}\subset{\mathfrak{k}}
implies that
(\ref{condNatRed})
holds. So,
a symmetric space is naturally reductive
.

2.4.3
Normal symmetric spaces

We considered two classes of naturally reductive spaces : normal homogeneous spaces and symmetric spaces. In this subsection, we investigate their intersection.

Let
{\mathbb{G}},{\mathbb{K}},\sigma
be as in
(i)
and
(ii)
of Definition
2
. Let
\overline{B}
be an
{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbb{G}})
-invariant scalar product on

{\mathrm{Ad}}({\mathbb{K}})

{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}\perp{\mathfrak{k}}
wrt

(21)

Remark 1
.

If

.

4.6
Application to perturbation of eigenvectors

In the sequel, we fix
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\in{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
. A perturbation of
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
is a map
{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot):{\mathbb{J}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
, where
{\mathbb{J}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is an open interval containing
x_{0}
such that
{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})={\mathcal{S}}_{0}
. A perturbation of eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
is a map
{\mathcal{Q}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}(\cdot):{\mathbb{J}}{\longrightarrow}O(n)
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{J}}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}(x)
is a matrix of eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{S}}(x)
. In perturbation theory, the question of the existence of a map
{\mathcal{Q}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}(\cdot)
with some regularity, inherited from that of
{\mathcal{S}}(\cdot)
, is called the eigenvector problem.

4.6.1
Flag of eigenspaces of a symmetric matrix

For
{\mathcal{S}}\in{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
, denote by
r({\mathcal{S}})
the number of distinct eigenvalues of
{\mathcal{S}}
and by
\Lambda({\mathcal{S}})
its spectrum:

\Lambda({\mathcal{S}})=\left\{\lambda_{1}({\mathcal{S}})>...>\lambda_{i}({\mathcal{S}})>...>\lambda_{r({\mathcal{S}})}({\mathcal{S}})\right\},

where, for all
1\leq i\leq r({\mathcal{S}})
,
\lambda_{i}({\mathcal{S}})
is an eigenvalue of
{\mathcal{S}}
. For all
i
, let
P_{i}({\mathcal{S}})
be the
eigenprojection
associated to
\lambda_{i}({\mathcal{S}})
, i.e. the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace associated to
\lambda_{i}({\mathcal{S}})
. Set

{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}:=\left(P_{i}({\mathcal{S}})\right)_{1\leq i\leq r({\mathcal{S}})}.

Then, the spectral theorem implies readily that
{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
is a flag, called the
flag of eigenspaces
of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\in{\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}
and
{\mathrm{I}}=(q_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}
the type of its flag of eigenspaces
{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
. Let
Q\in O(n)
. Then,
Q
is a matrix of eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{S}}
iff
{\pi^{\mathrm{I}}}(Q)={\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
Q
is a matrix of eigenvectors of
{\mathcal{S}}
iff its columns generate the flag
{\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
, which is equivalent to
{\pi^{\mathrm{I}}}(Q)={\mathcal{P}}^{{\mathcal{S}}}
, according to Lemma
11
.
∎

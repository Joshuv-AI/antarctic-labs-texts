Learning Algorithm for Relation-Substitutable Context-Free Languages
Takayuki Kuriyama

Abstract
We generalized the class of k , l k,l -substitutable languages (Yoshinala, 2008). Each language in the generalized class is closed under a good substitutability. The substitutability is defined by a recognizable equivalence relation. We show the convergence of our generalized learning algorithm. The size of the characteristic sample is smaller than Yoshinaka’s. Keywords. Context-free languages; Grammatical inference; Identification in the limit;Learning algorithms from text; Formal languages.

k,l

In this section we show that our class is a generalization of the class of Yoshinaka(2008)’s. Assume
\sim
be a recognizable equivalence relation on
\Sigma^{*}

1

An
2
-ary relation
\sim
on
\Sigma^{*}
is
recognizable
if there exist a finite monoid
(M,\circ,1)
, a monoid homomorphism
h:\Sigma^{*}\to M
, and a subset
F
of
M^{2}
such that
x_{1}\sim x_{2}
if and only if
(h(x_{1}),h(x_{2}))\in F
.
. We generalized
k,l
-substitutable class as follows;

Definition 1

For a fixed recognizable equivalence relation
\sim
,
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is
\sim
-substitutable
iff
\forall x,y\in\Sigma^{*},D_{L}(x)\cap D_{L}(y)\neq\varnothing\land x\sim y\Rightarrow D_{L}(x)=D_{L}(y)
.

And we use the following class notations;

Definition 2

\mathcal{C}_{k,l}:=\{L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\mid L\ {\rm is}\ k,l{\rm\mathchar 45substitutable}\}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\sim}:=\{L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}\mid L\ {\rm is}\ \sim{\rm\mathchar 45substitutable}\}
.

First, we show the following fact;

Claim

\forall k,l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\exists\sim
,
\mathcal{C}_{k,l}=\mathcal{C}_{\sim}
.

Proof

Assume
x\sim_{k,l}y:\Leftrightarrow x=y\lor\exists u\in\Sigma^{k}
,
\exists v\in\Sigma^{l}
,
\{x,y\}\in u\Sigma^{*}v
. Clearly
\sim_{k,l}
is an equivalence relation of finite index. And each equivalence class is in form of
\{w\}
or
u\Sigma^{*}v
, so it is regular. Thus
\sim_{k,l}
is a recognizable relation.

Then we show that our class is strictly inclusive. For that, we introduce another example of recognizable equivalence relations;

Symbol Counting with Bound.
For
a\in\Sigma,d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x,y\in\Sigma^{*}
,
x\sim_{\#a,d}y:\Leftrightarrow\#(a,x)=\#(a,y)\leq d\lor(\#(a,x)>d\land\#(a,y)>d)
.
\sim_{\#a,d}
is a recognizable equivalence relation.

Claim

There exists a language
L\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
which is
\sim_{\#a,d}
-substitutable but not
k,l
-substitutable for any
k,l
.

Proof

L=b^{*}\cup b^{*}ab^{*}
is a
\sim_{\#a,d}
-substitutable language for any
d\geq 1
. For any
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
,
L\ni b^{k}bb^{l},b^{k}ab^{l}
so
b\sim_{k,l}a
. And
D_{L}(a)\cap D_{L}(b)\ni(b^{i},b^{j})
. But
D_{L}(b)\setminus D_{L}(a)\ni(b^{i},ab^{j})
.

And on intersection, we have the following result;

Lemma 1

\forall\sim_{1},\sim_{2}\exists\sim
,
\mathcal{C}_{\sim_{1}}\cup\mathcal{C}_{\sim_{2}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\sim}
.

Proof

Assume
x\sim y:\Leftrightarrow x\sim_{1}y\land x\sim_{2}y
. Then
\sim
is a recognizable equivalence relation. If
L\in\mathcal{L}_{1}\cup\mathcal{L}_{2}
, then
L
is
\sim_{i}
-substitutable for some
i

D_{L}(x)\cap D_{L}(y)\neq\varnothing\land x\sim y\Rightarrow D_{L}(x)=D_{L}(y)

By lemma
1

Claim

\forall k,l\in\mathbb{N}
,
\exists\sim

\mathcal{C}_{k,l}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}_{\sim}

For
k,l
-substitutable class,
\forall k\leq k^{\prime},l\leq l^{\prime}

\mathcal{C}_{k,l}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}

\sim\subseteq\sim^{\prime}

Lemma of Typing CFGs with a Finite Monoid

In this section we give a basic lemma for proving the convergence of our learning algorithm.

Lemma 2

Assume
M
be a finite monoid and
h:\Sigma^{*}\to M
is a homomorphism. For any CFG
G
, there exists a CFG
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime},P^{\prime},S^{\prime})
such that
G\approx G^{\prime}
and
\forall A\in V^{\prime}\setminus\{S^{\prime}\}
,
A\Rightarrow^{*}_{G^{\prime}}w
implies
p=h(w)
.

Proof

Assume the given CFG be
(V,\Sigma,P,S)
and in CNF. We define
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},\Sigma,P^{\prime},S^{\prime})
as follows:

V^{\prime}:=\{A_{p}\mid A\in V\land p\in M\}\cup\{S^{\prime}\}
,

P^{\prime}:=\{A_{pq}\to B_{p}C_{q}\mid A\to BC\in P\}\cup\{A_{p}\to a\mid A\to a\in P\land p=h(a)\}
.
Then the following is proved by induction;

(1)

\forall A_{p}\in V^{\prime}\setminus\{S^{\prime}\},A_{p}\Rightarrow^{*}_{G^{\prime}}w
implies
p=h(w)
.

(2)

\forall A_{p}\in V^{\prime}\setminus\{S^{\prime}\},\mathcal{L}(A_{p})=\mathcal{L}(A)\cap h^{-1}(p)
, thus
\mathcal{L}(G)=\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof for
(1)
is as follows: for basic case of
A_{p}\to a\land p=h(a)
, the statement is clear. In induction step, assume
A_{pq}\Rightarrow B_{p}C_{q}\Rightarrow^{*}w_{1}w_{2}\land B_{p}\Rightarrow^{*}w_{1}\land C_{q}\Rightarrow^{*}w_{2}
and
p=h(w_{1})\land q=h(w_{2})
. Thus
pq=h(w_{1})h(w_{2})=h(w_{1}w_{2})
.

Proof for
(2)
is as follows:
w\in\mathcal{L}(A_{pq})\Rightarrow\mathcal{L}(A)\cap h^{-1}(pq)
is proved similarly to
(1)
. Assume
A\Rightarrow BC\Rightarrow^{*}w_{1}w_{2}\land B\Rightarrow^{*}w_{1}\land C\Rightarrow^{*}w_{2}
. By I.H.,
w_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(B_{h(w_{1})})\land w_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(C_{h(w_{2})})
. Thus
w_{1}w_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(A_{h(w_{1})h(w_{2})})=\mathcal{L}(A_{h(w_{1}w_{2})})

Learning Algorithm

In this section, we give a polynomial-time identification learning algorithm for
\sim
-substitutable languages. Assume
L_{*}
be the target
\sim
-substitutable language. The learning algorithm for the class is the following:

Assume we
know
the equivalence relation
\sim
. Given a positive example
K
,
define

\displaystyle\hat{V}(K)

\displaystyle=\{\,[x]\mid\exists uv\in\Sigma^{*},uxv\in K\land x\neq\lambda\,\}\cup\{\hat{S}\},

\displaystyle\hat{P}(K)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} &\{\,[xy]\to[x][y]\mid[xy],[x],[y]\in\hat{V}(K)\,\}\cup{}\\
&\{\,[x]\to[x^{\prime}]\mid x\sim x^{\prime}\land[x],[x^{\prime}]\in\hat{V}(K)\land D_{K}(x)\cap D_{K}(x^{\prime})\neq\varnothing\,\}\cup{}\\
&\{\,[a]\to a\mid[a]\in\hat{V}(K)\land a\in\Sigma\,\}\cup{}\\
&\{\,\hat{S}\to[w]\mid w\in K\},\end{aligned}

and the learning algorithm is:

——

Data:
A sequence of strings
w_{1},w_{2},...

Result:
A sequence of CFGs
G_{1},G_{2},...

Let
\hat{G}
be a CFG generating empty language;

For

n=1,2,...

do

Read the next string
w_{n}
;

If

w_{n}\notin\mathcal{L}(\hat{G})
then

let
\hat{G}=\langle\Sigma,\hat{V}(K),\hat{P}(K),\hat{S}\rangle
where
K=\{w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n}\}

end if

output
\hat{G}

End For

——

Clearly, our algorithm
never
produces illegal strings;

\mathcal{L}(\hat{G}(K))\subseteq L_{*}

Assume

\langle v(\alpha),v(\gamma)\rangle\in D_{L}(v(\beta))

Polynomial Time and Data

The proof of efficiency of our algorithm is similar to Yoshinaka
[
5
]
. Notice that the judgment of “
x\sim y
?” ends in linear order time. And characteristic set which our learning theory demands is strictly smaller than Yoshinaka’s.

First we show that our learning algorithm is of polynomial-time.

Lemma 4

Assume the question “
w_{1}\sim w_{2}?
” is decidable in polynomial time, then the computation of
\hat{G}(K)
with finite
K
ends in polynomial time of the description of
K
.

Proof

We define the description
{\rm Desk}(K):=\Sigma_{w\in K}{\rm Len}(w)
. And denote
{\rm MaxLen}(K):=\max{\{{\rm Len}(w)\mid w\in K\}}
.

(1)
The computation of
[x]\to[y]
with
x\sim y
costs
\mathcal{O}(\#K^{2}\cdot{\rm\{MaxLen}(K)({\rm MaxLen}(K)-1)\}^{2})
time.

(2)
The computation of
S\to[x]
is just reading up the set
K
. This costs
\mathcal{O}({\rm Desk}(K))
time.

(3)
The way of dividing of the string
w
is
{\rm Len}(w)-1
, so the computation of the rule
[xy]\to[x][y]
costs
\mathcal{O}(\#K\cdot{\rm\{MaxLen}(K)({\rm MaxLen}(K)-1)\}\cdot({\rm Len}(w)-1))
time.

And notice that
{\rm Desk}(K)>\#K,{\rm MaxLen}(K)
.

Our characteristic set is by Clark and Eyraud’s definition, so it is polynomial data. Thus the following fact is proved;

Proposition 1

Our learning algorithm is
efficient
, i.e., a homomorphism-substitutable language is identifiable in the limit from polynomial time and data.

not

Claim

L:=\mathcal{L}(G)

G=\{S\to aSS,S\to b\}

L
is not
\sim

Proof

Suppose that

A Mountain-pass Theorem in Hyperbolic Space and Its Application
Junfu Yao

Abstract
. We develop a min-max theory for certain complete minimal hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space. In particular, we show that given two strictly stable minimal hypersurfaces that are both asymptotic to the same ideal boundary, there is a new one trapped between the two. As an application, we show that under a low entropy condition, all the minimal hypersurfaces asymptotic to the same ideal boundary are isotopic.

Introduction

Let
n\geq 2
and
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

n+1

We say a minimal hypersurface,

and the corresponding normal variation,

, of

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{s=0}\text{Vol}(\Sigma_{s})=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\Sigma}g|^{2}+(n-|A_{\Sigma}|^{2})g^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}},

In the first part of the present paper, we prove a mountain pass theorem in hyperbolic space. Specifically, we prove that given two strictly stable minimal hypersurfaces that have the same asymptotic boundary and bound a domain, there is a third minimal hypersurface between the two. for some integer
n
. The proof of the existence of
\Gamma_{L}
is similar.
∎

The next proposition is an asymptotic estimate for the distance between two minimal hypersurfaces that are asymptotic to the same ideal boundary.

Proposition 7.6
.

Suppose
\Gamma_{1}\preceq\Gamma_{2}
(
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}=\emptyset
) are two minimal hypersurfaces that are both
C^{3}
-asymptotic to
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{0\}\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Then, there exist an
\epsilon_{1}>0
and a function
h
, so that

(7.9)

\Gamma_{2}\cap\{y\leq\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\}\subset\big\{p+h(p)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Gamma_{1}}:p\in\Gamma_{1}\subset\{y\leq\epsilon_{1}\}\big\}\subset\Gamma_{2}\cap\{y\leq 2\epsilon_{1}\}.

On the components of
\Gamma_{1}
where
h
is not identically
0
, we have the estimate
\frac{1}{C}y^{n+1}(p)\leq h(p)\leq Cy^{n+1}(p)
for some
C>0
and
p\in\Gamma_{1}\cap\{y\leq\epsilon_{1}\}
.

Proof.

The existence of the function
h
and the upper bound follows from
[
Yao
, Lemma 3.4]
. Moreover, we have
h\big|_{M}=0
,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh\big|_{M}=0
and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h\big|_{M}=0
. By computation,
f=\frac{h}{y}
satisfies the equation

\Delta f+(|A_{\Gamma_{1}}|^{2}-n)f=a(p,h,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h)f+y\left\langle\textbf{b}(p,h,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h),\nabla f\right\rangle,

where
|a|+|\textbf{b}|\leq C(|h|+|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh|+|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h|)
. Since
|A_{\Gamma_{1}}|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(y^{2})
, we choose
\epsilon_{1}
so small that
|A_{\Gamma_{1}}|^{2}-n-a\leq 0
for
y(p)\leq\epsilon_{1}
. Let
g(p)=y^{n}(p)+Ky^{n+1}(p)
. By the same reasoning as in the proof of
[
Yao
, Lemma 3.4]
, we can choose
\epsilon_{1}
and
K
appropriately, so that
\mathcal{L}g=\mathcal{L}(y^{n}+Ky^{n+1})\geq 0
. On the component of
\Gamma_{1}
where
f
is not identically
0
, let
\lambda=\inf_{\{y(p)=\epsilon_{1}\}}f
. If
\lambda=0
, then the strong maximum principle implies that
f\equiv 0
on this component of
\Gamma_{1}
, which leads to a contradiction. Now, we have
\mathcal{L}(f-\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon_{1}^{n}+K\epsilon_{1}^{n+1}}g)\leq 0
,
f\big|_{y=0}=0=\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon_{1}^{n}+K\epsilon_{1}^{n+1}}g\big|_{y=0}
and
f\big|_{y=\epsilon_{1}}\geq\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon_{1}^{n}+K\epsilon_{1}^{n+1}}g\big|_{y=\epsilon_{1}}
. By the maximum principle,
f\geq\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon_{1}^{n}+K\epsilon_{1}^{n+1}}g
in
\Gamma_{1}\cap\{y\leq\epsilon_{1}\}
. This proves the lower bound.
∎

8.
Deformation of Unstable Minimal Hypersurfaces

In this section, we show that an unstable minimal hypersurface can be deformed into a stable one. Following
[
BW19b
]
, we perturb the unstable minimal hypersurface by its first eigenfunciton, and show that the mean curvature flow starting from the perturbed hypersurface moves to a stable minimal hypersurface. To get an isotopy that is regular up to the ideal boundary, we modify the deformation near the ideal boundary in an explicit way.

As a starting point, we prove that for unstable or weakly stable minimal hypersurfaces, the infimum of the energy functional for the stability operator is attained by some function in the weighted Hölder space
y^{\mu}\Lambda^{k,\alpha}_{0}
for
\mu>n
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a minimal hypersurface that is
C^{2}
-asymptotic to
M
. Assume that

\lambda_{1}=\inf_{f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Gamma)\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle+(n-|A_{\Gamma}|^{2})f^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\int_{\Gamma}f^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}}\leq 0.

Then, there is a non-zero function
f\in y^{\mu}\Lambda^{k,\alpha}_{0}(\Gamma)
satisfying
\Delta f+(|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n+\lambda_{1})f=0
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Here
\mu=\frac{n-1+\sqrt{(n+1)^{2}-4\lambda_{1}}}{2}
is the larger root to the equation
t^{2}+(1-n)t+(\lambda_{1}-n)=0
. Moreover, on the component of
\Gamma
where
f
is not identically
0
, we have
\frac{1}{C}y^{\mu}\leq f\leq Cy^{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma^{i}=\Gamma\cap\{y\geq\frac{1}{i}\}
and

\lambda^{i}_{1}=\inf_{f\in H^{1}_{0}(\Gamma^{i})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\Gamma^{i}}\left\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\right\rangle+(n-|A_{\Gamma}|^{2})f^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}}{\int_{\Gamma^{i}}f^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{\mathbb{H}}}.

It is easy to see that
\lambda^{i}_{1}\to\lambda_{1}
as
i\to\infty
. Since
\Gamma^{i}
is a compact manifold with boundary, the standard calculus of variation theory gives the existence of a non-negative function
f^{i}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Gamma^{i})\cap C^{\infty}(\Gamma^{i})
, so that
\Delta f^{i}+(|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n+\lambda^{i}_{1})f^{i}=0
in
\Gamma^{i}
and
f^{i}(p_{0})=1
for a fix point
p_{0}\in\Gamma^{1}\subset\Gamma^{i}
. By choosing
\epsilon_{2}
sufficiently small, we assume that
|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n<0
for
p\in\Gamma\cap\{y(p)\leq\epsilon_{2}\}
. For
i
sufficiently large, the Harnack inequality implies that
f^{i}(p)\leq C(\epsilon_{2})f(p_{0})=C(\epsilon_{2})
for
p\in\Gamma\cap\{y\geq\epsilon_{2}\}
. On the other hand, the maximum principle is valid on
\Gamma\cap\{y\leq\epsilon_{2}\}
. So,
\sup_{\Gamma\cap\{y\leq\epsilon_{2}\}}f^{i}\leq\sup_{\Gamma\cap\{y=\frac{1}{i},\,\epsilon_{2}\}}f^{i}=\sup_{\Gamma\cap\{y=\epsilon_{2}\}}f^{i}\leq C(\epsilon_{2})
. In other words,
f^{i}
is uniformly bounded in
i
. Locally, we can write the Laplace operator over
\Gamma
as
\Delta=y^{2}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu+(2-n)y\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{kj}\partial_{k}y\partial_{j}
. So, we can rewrite the equation for

as

(8.1)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Delta\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muf^{i}+\frac{2-n}{y}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{kj}\partial_{k}y\partial_{j}(f^{i})+\frac{|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n+\lambda^{i}_{1}}{y^{2}}f^{i}=0.

The interior Schauder estimate
[
GT83
, Section 6.1]
gives

\frac{1}{i}\|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muf^{i}\|_{C^{0}(\Gamma\cap\{y\geq\frac{2}{i}\})}+\frac{1}{i^{2}}\|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}f^{i}\|_{C^{0}(\Gamma\cap\{y\geq\frac{2}{i}\})}+\frac{1}{i^{2+\alpha}}[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}f^{i}]_{\alpha;\Gamma\cap\{y\geq\frac{2}{i}\}}\leq C\|f^{i}\|_{C^{0}(\Gamma^{i})}\leq C(\epsilon_{2}).

Taking a convergent subsequence of
\{f^{i}\}
, we get the existence of a
C^{2,\alpha}_{loc}
function
f
satisfying
\Delta f+(|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n+\lambda_{1})f=0
,
f(p_{0})=1
and
\|f\|_{\Lambda^{2,\alpha}_{0}}\leq C(\epsilon_{2})
.

Direct computation shows

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\frac{f^{i}}{y}\right)-2\langle\nabla\left(\frac{f^{i}}{y}\right),y\nabla\frac{1}{y}\rangle+\left(|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n+\lambda_{1}^{i}+2-n+\mathcal{O}(y)\right)\frac{f^{i}}{y}=0.

By shrinking
\epsilon_{2}
if necessary,
\frac{f^{i}}{y}
satisfies the maximum principle on
\Gamma\cap\{\frac{1}{i}\leq y\leq\epsilon_{2}\}
. Hence,
f^{i}\leq Cy
in
\Gamma\cap\{\frac{1}{i}\leq y\leq\epsilon_{2}\}
. Taking
i\to\infty
, we see that
f
can be continuously extended to
0
on
M
. We take
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{1}}=\Delta+(|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n+\lambda_{1})
and notice that
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{1}}(y^{\mu}+K^{\prime}y^{\mu+1})\geq 0
and
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{1}}(y^{\mu}-K^{\prime}y^{\mu+1})\leq 0
for some

. Then, the estimate
\frac{1}{C}y^{\mu}\leq f\leq Cy^{\mu}
follows from an argument similar to the proof of Proposition
7.6
and the fact that
f\in C_{0}(\Gamma\cup M)
.

Finally,
f\in y^{\mu}\Lambda^{k,\alpha}_{0}
for any

follows from
0\leq f\leq Cy^{\mu}
and standard elliptic PDE estimates.
∎

The main theorem of this section is the following:

Theorem 8.2
.

Let

\lambda_{\mathbb{H}}[\Gamma]<\lambda[\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{1}]

\hat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon}=\{p+\epsilon f(p)\textbf{n}_{\Gamma}=p+\epsilon yf\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\Gamma}:\,p\in\Gamma\}

is the first eigenfunction of
\Gamma
. Consider the mean curvature flow

\sup_{p\in\hat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon}}|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muA\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{\hat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon}}|(p)<\infty
if we choose

Lemma 8.3

By choosing
\epsilon

By direct computation (see, e.g.,
[

\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\left(e^{-n\mu t}y^{\mu}\right)\leq 0

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\left(ce^{-n\mu t}y^{\mu}-e^{nt}H_{\Sigma_{t}}\right)\leq-|A_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}e^{nt}H_{\Sigma_{t}}\leq|A_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}\left(ce^{-n\mu t}y^{\mu}-e^{nt}H_{\Sigma_{t}}\right).

At

\displaystyle H_{\hat{\Gamma}_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{\Gamma}+|A_{\Gamma}|^{2}-n)(\epsilon f)+a(p,h,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h)\epsilon f+y\left\langle\textbf{b}(p,h,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h),\nabla\epsilon f\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\epsilon\big(\lambda_{1}f+a(p,h,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h)f+y\left\langle\textbf{b}(p,h,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muh,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}h),\nabla f\right\rangle\big)

where

. Hence, by Proposition

, there is a small

is mean convex and

hypersurface up to the ideal boundary, the perturbed hypersurface

Since

Then, by a non-compact maximum principle (e.g., one can use a variant of the monotonicity formula introduced in
[

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t}
be a mean curvature flow which satisfies
H_{\Sigma_{t}}\geq ce^{-n(\mu+1)t}y^{\mu}
for some
c>0
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{1}
. Suppose the inital hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is

-asymptotic to the ideal boundary and meets the ideal boundary orthogonally. Then, for any

(the maximal existing time), there is a constant

|A_{\Sigma_{t}}|^{2}\leq C_{1}y^{-2\mu}e^{4nt}H_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}

Proof.

When it is clear from the context, we will omit the subscripts and simply write

(resp.

) for

(resp.

[

\frac{d}{dt}|A|^{2}=\Delta|A|^{2}-2|\nabla A|^{2}+2|A|^{2}(|A|^{2}-n)+4n(|A|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}H^{2}).

After computation, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{H^{2}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)=

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{H^{2}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)+4n\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{H^{2}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)+\frac{4}{H^{3}}\left\langle\nabla(|A|^{2}),\nabla H\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{6|A|^{2}}{H^{4}}|\nabla H|^{4}-\frac{2|\nabla A|^{2}}{H^{2}}.

Following the computation in
[

\displaystyle\frac{4}{H^{3}}\left\langle\nabla(|A|^{2}),\nabla H\right\rangle\leq\frac{2}{H}\left\langle\nabla\left(\frac{|A|^{2}}{H^{2}}\right),\nabla H\right\rangle+\frac{6|A|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{H^{4}}+\frac{2|\nabla A|^{2}}{H^{2}}.

. From the assumptions on

Lemma 8.5

\Gamma_{-}
is
C^{3}
-asymptotic. In
\text{cl}(\Omega^{\prime})\cap\{y<\frac{\epsilon}{2}\}

\displaystyle|\textbf{N}_{\epsilon}-(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}|=(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\top}_{n+1}\circ\Pi-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}|.

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Gamma_{-}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cap\{y=0\}

A.1
) follows easily. On the other hand, near the ideal boundary, we have

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\textbf{N}_{\epsilon}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}+(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\top}_{n+1}\circ\Pi).

is
C^{3}
-asymptotic,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\top}_{n+1}\circ\Pi

C^{2}
up to the ideal boundary. So,
|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{\top}_{n+1}\circ\Pi)|\leq C
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let

. There is a constant
C=C(\Gamma_{-},\Omega^{\prime},M_{0},T)
, so that

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}-e^{-\alpha t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}

(A.4)

\displaystyle|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}-I_{n+1}-(e^{\alpha t}-1)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\text{ and }|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}\Phi_{t}|\leq C.

Proof.

Since
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}(p)=\textbf{Y}(\Phi_{t}(p))
, using (
A.1
), one sees that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}\right)\right|\leq C\left|\frac{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}\right|.

So,

(A.5)

|\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}|\leq C\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}

Similarly, one can compute that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}\right)=-\frac{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{(\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})^{2}}\textbf{Y}(\Phi_{t}(p))\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}=-\alpha\frac{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}+\mathcal{O}(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}),

where in the last step, we used (
A.1
). This implies that

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{\alpha t}\frac{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}\right)\right|\leq C(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}).

Taking the integral and noticing that
\Phi_{0}
is the identity map, we get (
A.3
). To prove (
A.4
), notice that
\frac{d}{dt}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\textbf{Y}(\Phi_{t}(p)))=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\textbf{Y}\circ\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}
. Combining this with (

),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}-\alpha(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})\circ\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}\right|\leq C\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}

That is,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-\alpha tA}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}\right)\right|\leq C\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}e^{-\alpha tA},

A.6

\displaystyle|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\Phi_{t}-I_{n+1}-(e^{\alpha t}-1)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\otimes\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}|\leq C\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\leq C\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1},

. This implies
|\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{2}\Phi_{t}|\leq C
.
∎

Lemma A.3

A.1
), (
A.2
), (
A.3
) and (
A.4

\left|(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\nabla\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\left(\frac{(\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})}{\left(\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}\right)}\right)\right|\leq C\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}.

Lemma A.4
.

\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\mathcal{J}^{E}\Phi_{t}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathrm{div}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\mathbf{Y}-Q_{\mathbf{Y}}-\frac{n}{y}\mathbf{Y}\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1},

.

Proof.

To show the differentiability of the map

, it suffices to show the differentiability of the maps

.

Fix

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\psi\in\mathfrak{Y}
. There is a constant
C=C(\Gamma_{-},\Omega^{\prime},M_{0},T)
, so that for
0\leq t\leq T
,
\|\Phi_{t}^{\#}\psi\|_{\mathfrak{Y}}\leq C\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{Y}}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi=f+g
with
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}+\|y^{-n}g\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{Y}}
. By definition,
\Phi_{t}^{\#}\psi=\Phi_{t}^{\#}f+\Phi_{t}^{\#}g=f(\Phi_{t}(p),\nabla_{\textbf{v}}\Phi_{t}(p))+g(\Phi_{t}(p),\nabla_{\textbf{v}}\Phi_{t}(p))
. So,

(A.10)

\displaystyle\|y^{-n}\Phi_{t}^{\#}g\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\frac{\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}{\textbf{x}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1}}\right\|_{\infty}^{n}\|(\Phi_{t}(p)\cdot\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\textbf{e}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{n+1})^{-n}\Phi_{t}^{\#}g\|_{\infty}\leq C\|y^{-n}g\|_{\infty},

where in the last step, we used (
A.5
). Similarly, using (
A.3
), (
A.4
) and (
A.5
), we have

\displaystyle\|y\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}(\Phi_{t}^{\#}f)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|y\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\|_{\infty});

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\Phi_{t}^{\#}f)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\|_{\infty};

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\Phi_{t}^{\#}f)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\|_{\infty});

\displaystyle\|y\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\Phi_{t}^{\#}f)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|y\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\|_{\infty}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\|_{\infty}).

This implies
\|\Phi_{t}^{\#}f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}
. Combining this with (
A.10
), we see that

\|\Phi_{t}^{\#}\psi\|_{\mathfrak{Y}}\leq\|\Phi_{t}^{\#}f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}+\|y^{-n}\Phi_{t}^{\#}g\|_{\infty}\leq C(\|f\|_{\mathfrak{X}}+\|y^{-n}g\|_{\infty})\leq 2C\|\psi\|_{\mathfrak{Y}}.

∎

Analytical algorithm for capacities of classical and classical-quantum channels
Masahito Hayashi

Abstract
We derive an analytical algorithm for the channel capacity of a classical channel without any iteration, while its existing algorithms require iterations and the number of iterations depends on the required precision level. Hence, our algorithm is its first analytical algorithm for this task without any iteration, while this algorithm needs several conditions for the channel. We apply the obtained algorithm to examples, and see how the obtained algorithm works in these examples. Then, we extend it to the channel capacity of a classical-quantum (cq-) channel. Many existing studies proposed algorithms for a cq-channel and all of them require iterations. Our extended analytical algorithm has also no iteration, and outputs the exactly optimum value.

Introduction

One of the key problems in classical and quantum information theory is the maximization of information quantities.
However, it is not so easy to perform such a maximization analytically because
all of existing methods require certain iterations, whose number depends on the required precision level.
The most common maximization problem is
the channel capacity, which is given as the maximization of mutual information
[
6
]

[

[
3

and their related studies
[

9

However, these are iterative approximation algorithms to calculate the maximum of the mutual information.
In addition, the reference
[
4
]
calculated only its upper bound and
the references
[
24
,
10
]
developed other type of method to approximately calculate it.
Hence, they cannot calculate the exact value for the channel capacity.
As variants, the references
[
14
,
25
]
extended the above method to
the wire-tap capacity
[
12

13
]
when the wire-tap channel is degraded.
Also, the references
[

16

17
,
18

19

extended it to
the quantum setting, so called the capacity of classical-quantum channel.
However, these results are also iterative approximation algorithms.

This paper proposes an algorithm to analytically calculate the channel capacity of the classical channel
without iteration.
The proposed algorithm is composed of
solving simultaneous linear equations and calculation of logarithm and exponential
because it employs an information-geometrical structure.
However, the proposed method works under certain conditions.
Since our method is analytical,
we can derive several analytical formulas for the capacity when these conditions are satisfied.
Then, to see this possibility,
we apply our algorithm to several examples, and derive analytical expressions
of the capacities in these examples.
Further,
we extend our analytical algorithm to the calculations of the capacity of classical-quantum channel.

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows.

We consider the input and output alphabets

{\mathcal{X}}:=\{1,\ldots,n_{1}\}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}:=\{1,\ldots,n_{2}\}

We denote the sets of probability distributions on
{\mathcal{X}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}

{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{X}}}
and
{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
, respectively.
For distributions

,
the entropy
H(P)
and the divergence
D(P\|Q)
are defined as

\displaystyle H(P):=

\displaystyle-\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P(x)\log P(x),

(1)

\displaystyle D(P\|Q):=

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}P(x)\log\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}.

(2)

Throughout this paper, the base of the logarithm is chosen to be
the natural logarithm.

A channel from

to
{\mathcal{Y}}
is given as conditional distribution on
{\mathcal{Y}}

{\mathcal{X}}
.
That is, using the notation

For

and
Q_{Y}\in{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
,

W\cdot Q_{X}\in{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
,

W\times Q_{X}\in{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}}
, and
Q_{X}\times Q_{Y}\in\mathcal{P}_{{\mathcal{X}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}}

are defined by

(W\cdot Q_{X})(x,y):=\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}W(y|x)Q_{X}(x)
,

(W\times Q_{X})(x,y):=W(y|x)Q_{X}(x)
, and
(Q_{Y}\times Q_{X})(x,y):=Q_{X}(x)Q_{Y}(y)

The channel capacity of a channel

[
6
]

[

\displaystyle C(W):=

\displaystyle\max_{Q_{X}\in{{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{X}}}}}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}Q_{X}(x)D(W_{x}\|W\cdot Q_{X})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{Q_{Y}\in{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{Y}}}}\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}D(W_{x}\|Q_{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{Q_{X}\in{\cal P}_{{\mathcal{X}}}}\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}D(W_{x}\|W\cdot Q_{X}).

(3)

To discuss

, we assume the following conditions.

(A)

Output system with two elements

First, we consider the case with

When
{\mathcal{X}}
and the channel
W
satisfies
Condition (C’) in this case,

Therefore, in the following, we consider the case with

and

.
We define the distributions

for

by the following vector form:

\displaystyle W_{1}:=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-p\\
p\end{array}\right),\quad W_{2}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1-q\\
q\end{array}\right).

For simplicity, we assume that

We define the

as

V:=(W_{1},W_{1})
.
The inverse matrix is

\displaystyle V^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-p}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q&q-1\\

Then,
\phi(\theta)

\displaystyle\phi(\theta)=

\displaystyle\log\Big(e^{\frac{q(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}}+e^{\frac{-p(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\Big(e^{\frac{q(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}}(1+e^{\frac{-(q+p)(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}+\log\Big(1+e^{\frac{-(q+p)(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}}\Big).

(30)

The capacity is calculated as

\displaystyle C(W)=

\displaystyle\phi(\theta)-h(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ph(q)-qh(p)}{q-p}+\log\Big(1+e^{\frac{-(q+p)(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}}\Big),

.

\displaystyle P_{\theta,Y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{\frac{q(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}-\phi(\theta)}\\
e^{\frac{-p(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}-\phi(\theta)}\end{array}\right).

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{X,*}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{q-p}\Big(q-e^{\frac{-p(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}-\phi(\theta)}\Big)\\
\frac{1}{q-p}\Big(-p+e^{\frac{-p(h(q)-h(p))}{q-p}-\phi(\theta)}\Big)\end{array}\right).

(D)

Theorem 5

Assume that the parameters
\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{n_{2}^{2}-1}
satisfies
the condition

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}^{2}-1}h_{i,j}\theta^{j}=-H(W_{i})+H(W_{n_{2}^{2}})

(116)

for

Then, we have

\displaystyle D(W_{x}\|\rho_{\theta})=\phi(\theta)-H(W_{n_{2}^{2}})

(117)

for any element

.

Proof:

The condition (
116
) implies that

\displaystyle\Tr W_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}^{2}-1}A_{j}\theta^{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}^{2}-1}h_{i,j}\theta^{j}=-H(W_{i})+H(W_{n_{2}^{2}}).

(118)

For any element
x(\neq n_{2}^{2})\in{\mathcal{X}}
, we have

\displaystyle D(W_{x}\|\rho_{\theta})=\Tr W_{x}(\log W_{x}-\log\rho_{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H(W_{x})-\Tr W_{x}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}^{2}-1}A_{j}\theta^{j}-\phi(\theta)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H(W_{x})-\big(-H(W_{x})+H(W_{n_{2}^{2}})-\phi(\theta)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\theta)-H(W_{n_{2}^{2}}).

(119)

Also, we have

\displaystyle D(W_{n_{2}^{2}}\|\rho_{\theta})=\Tr W_{n_{2}^{2}}(\log W_{n_{2}^{2}}-\log\rho_{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H(W_{n_{2}^{2}})-\Tr W_{n_{2}^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{2}^{2}-1}A_{j}\theta^{j}-\phi(\theta)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-H(W_{n_{2}^{2}})-(-\phi(\theta))=\phi(\theta)-H(W_{n_{2}^{2}}).

(120)

The combination of (

) and (
120
) implies the desired statement.

Then, we consider the following condition:

(E)

VI
Conclusion and future study

We have proposed an exact algorithm to calculate the channel capacities of classical and classical-quantum channels.
However, we have various conditions to apply our algorithm.
Therefore, it is a future problem to remove conditions.

Hence, it is an interesting topic to derive an information theoretical characterization of our method.

As another future study, we can consider an extension of our algorithm to wire-tap channel capacity

3

4
.

satisfies the condition (
9
).

\displaystyle{\cal E}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{P_{\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{n_{1}-1},\eta^{n_{1}},\ldots,\eta^{n_{2}-1},Y}|(\eta^{n_{1}},\ldots,\eta^{n_{2}-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}-n_{1}}\}.

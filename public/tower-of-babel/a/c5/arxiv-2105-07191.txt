Bounds on Negative Binomial Approximation to Call Function
Amit N. Kumar

Abstract
In this paper, we develop Stein’s method for negative binomial distribution using call function defined by f z ​ ( k ) = ( k − z ) + = max ⁡ { k − z , 0 } f_{z}(k)=(k-z)^{+}=\max\{k-z,0\} , for k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 and z ≥ 0 z\geq 0 . We obtain error bounds between 𝔼 ​ [ f z ​ ( N r , p ) ] \mathbb{E}[f_{z}(\text{N}_{r,p})] and 𝔼 ​ [ f z ​ ( V ) ] \mathbb{E}[f_{z}(V)] , where N r , p \text{N}_{r,p} follows negative binomial distribution and V V is the sum of locally dependent random variables, using certain conditions on moments. We demonstrate our results through an interesting application, namely, collateralized debt obligation (CDO), and compare the bounds with the existing bounds.

Introduction

The call function is a non-negative real-valued function of the form

\displaystyle f_{z}(k)=(k-z)^{+}=\max\{k-z,0\},\quad\text{for }k\geq 0\text{ and }z\geq 0.

(1.1)

It has been used in several areas of probability and statistics, for example, finance, risk theory, and derivative pricing, among many others. In particular, it has been successfully applied to the collateralized debt obligation (CDO). For more details, see Karoui and Jiao
[
5
]
, Karoui
et al.

[

, Yonghint
et al.

[

For a random variable (rv)
W

\mathbb{E}[f_{z}(W)]
plays an important role in many real-life applications. For example, if
W
is the sum of Bernoulli random variables (rvs) then
\mathbb{E}[f_{z}(W)]
is used to compute the mean value of total percentage loss for each tranche in CDO (see Neammanee and Yonghint
[
11
]
, and Yonghint
et al.

[
16
]
for details). Also, if
W
has a complicated structure, for example,
W
is the sum of locally dependent or independent (but non-identical) rvs, then
\mathbb{E}[f_{z}(W)]
becomes difficult to compute in practice. In such cases, an approximation to standard and easy-to-use distribution is of interest. Approximation to call function has been studied by several authors in the literature, for example, Poisson approximation has been studied by Neammanee and Yonghint
[
11
]
, and Yonghint
et al.

[
16

In this paper, we study negative binomial (NB) approximation to call function using certain conditions on moments. The main advantage of NB distribution over Poisson distribution is the extra flexibility parameter that builds our bounds more shaper compare to the existing bounds for Poisson approximation. Throughout this paper, let
\text{N}_{r,p}
follow NB distribution with probability mass function

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\text{N}_{r,p}=k)=\binom{r+k-1}{k}p^{r}q^{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(1.2)

where
r>1
,

and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}

[

Let

\{\zeta_{i}\}_{i\in J}

[
8

\displaystyle V=\sum_{i\in J}\zeta_{i},

(1.3)

the sum of locally dependent rvs. Note that if
A_{i}=B_{i}=\{i\}
then

is the sum of independent rvs. Throughout this paper, we let
\zeta_{A}=\sum_{i\in A}\zeta_{i}

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[(V-z)^{+}]-\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]\right|.

(1.4)

We use Stein’s method to obtain the bound for the above expression discussed in Section

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

Stein’s Method

Stein’s method (Stein
[
13
]
) is a tool for obtaining error bounds between two probability distributions. This method is mainly based on obtaining the solution of the Stein equation given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}g(k)=f(k)-\mathbb{E}f(X),\quad\text{for }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(2.1)

where
\mathscr{A}
is a Stein operator for a rv
X
such that
\mathbb{E}[\mathscr{A}g(X)]=0
,
f
and
g
are real-valued bounded functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Stein’s method has been developed for NB distribution by Brown and Phillips
[
4
]
and Barbour
et al.

[
1
]
for total variation distance and Wasserstein distance, respectively. In this section, we develop Stein’s method for NB distribution when
f
is a call function, defined in (
1.1
), which is used to obtain upper bounds for the expression given in (
1.4
). The NB approximation via Stein’s method has been studied by several authors such as Barbour
et al.

[
1
]
, Brown and Phillips
[
4
]
, Vellaisamy
et al.

[
14
]
, Wang and Xia
[
15
]
, Kumar and Upadhye
[
9
]
, among many others.

Next, let

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}g(k)=(k-z)^{+}-\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}],\quad\text{for }k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\text{ and }z\geq 0.

(2.2)

Also, let

be the solution of the above equation. Now, replacing

Therefore, to obtain the upper bound for the expression given in (

Next, the Stein operator of

\displaystyle g_{z}(k)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }k=0;\\

For more details, see Section 2 of Kumar
et al.

[

with appropriate changes. Now, we move to obtain uniform and non-uniform upper bound for

Lemma 2.1
.

For
k\geq 0
and
z\geq 0
,

defined in (

(i)

(ii)

|\Delta g_{z}(k)|\leq 2p^{-(r+1)}-p^{-1}

Proof.

(i)

As
g_{z}(0)=0
, it is enough to prove the result for
k\geq 1
. Consider

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}(j-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}\frac{(k-1)!}{(j-1)!}q^{j-k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j-1)}{k(k+1)\ldots(j-1)}q^{j-k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{k(k+1)\ldots(j+k-1)}q^{j}

\displaystyle\leq p^{-(r+1)},\quad\text{(using Lemma \ref{10:po2}(i)).}

(2.8)

Next, consider

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{r(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt1)\ldots(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271ptj\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt1)}{r(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt1)\ldots(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271ptk\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt1)}\frac{(k\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}\hskip-1.9919pt\leq 1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{r(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt1)\ldots(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271ptj\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt1)}{r(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271pt1)\ldots(r\hskip-1.42271pt+\hskip-1.42271ptk\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt1)}\frac{(k\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j-1)}{k(k+1)\ldots j}q^{j-k}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{k(k+1)\ldots(j+k)}q^{j}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{(k+1)\ldots(j+k)}q^{j}\leq\frac{p^{-r}-1}{rq},\quad\text{(using Lemma \ref{10:po2}(ii))}.

(2.9)

Therefore, from Lemma
4.1
(i), we have

\displaystyle 0<\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]\leq p^{-(r+1)}-p^{-1}.

(2.10)

Hence, from (

2.8
), and (

\displaystyle|g_{z}(k)|=\left|\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}[(j-z)^{+}-\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]]\right|\leq p^{-(r+1)}.

This proves (i).

(ii)

Note that, for
k=0
,

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(0)|=|g_{z}(1)|\leq p^{-(r+1)}\leq 2p^{-(r+1)}-p^{-1}.

Now, we prove the result for
k\geq 1
. Let

\displaystyle A_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}(j-z)^{+}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k)}\frac{k!}{j!}q^{j-k-1}(j-z)^{+}

\displaystyle A_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k)}\frac{k!}{j!}q^{j-k-1}\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+j-1)}{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}\frac{(k-1)!}{j!}q^{j-k}\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}].

Then

\displaystyle\Delta g_{z}(k)

\displaystyle=g_{z}(k+1)-g_{z}(k)=A_{1}(k)+A_{2}(k),

Hence, using (
2.8
) and (
2.10

\displaystyle|\Delta g_{z}(k)|\leq|A_{1}(k)|+|A_{2}(k)|\leq 2p^{-(r+1)}-p^{-1}.

This proves (ii).

An Application to CDO

The CDO is a financial tool that transfers a pool of assets such as auto loans, credit card debt, mortgages, and corporate debt, among many others, into a product and sold to investors. The assets are divided into several tranches, that is, the set of repayment. Each tranche has various credit quality and risk levels. The primary tranches in CDOs are senior, mezzanine, and equity. The investors can opt for multiple tranches to invest as per their interest. For more details, see Neammanee and Yonghint

Appendix A: Some Useful Inequalities

Here we give some inequalities and their proofs that have used in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
. Recall that
f_{z}
is a call function, defined in (
1.1
), and
\text{N}_{r,p}
follows the negative binomial distribution, defined in (
1.2
). The following lemma gives uniform and non-uniform upper bounds for
\mathbb{E}[f_{z}(\text{N}_{r,p})]=\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following inequalities hold:

(i)

\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]\leq\frac{rq}{p}
, for
z\geq 0
.

(ii)

\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]\leq\frac{r(r+1)q^{2}}{zp^{2}}
, for
z>1
.

Proof.

(i)

For
z\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k-z)^{+}\binom{r+k-1}{k}p^{r}q^{k}\leq rp^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\binom{r+k-1}{k-1}q^{k}=\frac{rq}{p}.

This proves (i).

(ii)

For
z>1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\text{N}_{r,p}-z)^{+}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\infty}(k-z)\binom{r+k-1}{k}p^{r}q^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{r}}{\left\lceil z\right\rceil}\sum_{k=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\infty}r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)\frac{(k-z)}{(k-1)!}q^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p^{r}}{z}\sum_{k=\left\lceil z\right\rceil}^{\infty}\frac{r(r+1)\ldots(r+k-1)}{(k-2)!}q^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r(r+1)p^{r}}{z}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\binom{r+k-1}{k-2}q^{k}=\frac{r(r+1)q^{2}}{zp^{2}}.

This proves (ii).

∎

Next, the following lemma gives some inequalities related to the parameters
r
and
p
of
\text{N}_{r,p}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The following inequalities hold:

(i)

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{k(k+1)\ldots(j+k-1)}q^{j}\leq p^{-(r+1)}-1,\penalty\ \text{for }k\geq 1}
.

(ii)

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{(k+1)\ldots(j+k)}q^{j}\leq\frac{p^{-r}-1}{rq}-1,\penalty\ \text{for all }k\geq 1}
.

(iii)

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k+1)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{(k+1)\ldots(j+k-1)}q^{j}\leq\frac{p^{-(r+1)}-1}{r}-q},\penalty\ \text{for }k\geq 1
.

(iv)

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k+1)\ldots(r+j+k)}{k(k+1)\ldots(j+k-1)}q^{j}\leq\frac{p^{-(r+2)}-1}{(r+1)q}-1},\penalty\ \text{for }k\geq 2
.

(v)

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+k)\ldots(r+j+k-1)}{k(k+1)\ldots(j+k)}q^{j}\leq\frac{p^{-r}-1}{r(r+1)q^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}},\penalty\ \text{for }k\geq 2
.

Proof.

Note that, for
k=1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+1)\ldots(r+j)}{1.2\ldots j}q^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\binom{r+j}{j}q^{j}=p^{-(r+1)}-1.

Therefore, the inequality (i) holds for
k=1
. Now, suppose it holds for
k=m
, that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+m)\ldots(r+j+m-1)}{m(m+1)\ldots(j+m-1)}q^{j}\leq p^{-(r+1)}-1.

(4.4)

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+m+1)\ldots(r+j+m)}{(m+1)\ldots(j+m)}q^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{m(r+j+m)}{(r+m)(m+j)}\frac{(r+m)\ldots(r+m+j-1)}{m(m+1)\ldots(j+m-1)}q^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(r+m)\ldots(r+m+j-1)}{m(m+1)\ldots(j+m-1)}q^{j}

\displaystyle\leq p^{-(r+1)}-1\quad\text{(using \eqref{10:rs10}).}

This implies that the inequality (i) holds for
k=m+1
, and hence it holds for all
k\geq 1
. Following similar steps, the inequalities (ii)-(v) can be easily proved.
∎

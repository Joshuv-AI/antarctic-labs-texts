A Breiman’s theorem for conditional dependent random vector and its applications to risk theory
Zhaolei Cui, Yuebao Wang 1. School of mathematics and statistics, Changshu Institute of Technology, Suzhou, 215000, China2. Department of Mathematics, Soochow University, Suzhou 215006, China

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we give a Breiman’s theorem for conditional dependent random vector,
where one component has a regularly-varying-tailed distribution with the index
\alpha\geq 0
,
while the other component is non-negative with a more relaxed moment condition.
This result substantially extends and improves some existing related results, such as
Theorem 2.1 of Yang and Wang (Extremes, 2013). We also provide some concrete examples, some interesting properties and a construction method of conditional dependent random vector.
Finally, we apply the above Breiman’s theorem to risk theory,
and obtain two asymptotic estimates of the finite-time ruin probability
and the infinite-time ruin probability of a discrete-time risk model,
in which the corresponding net loss and random discount are conditionally dependent.

Keywords:
Conditional dependence;
Breiman’s theorem; Regular variation; Discrete-time risk model; Ruin probabilities; Asymptotic estimate

Introduction

It is well known that Breiman’s theorem is an important tool
to deal with the tail distribution of product of two components of a random vector,
and it plays a key role in many application fields, such as risk theory, see Section 3 below.

In this paper, unless otherwise stated, let
(X,Y)
be a random vector
with the marginal distributions
F
on
(-\infty,\infty)
and
G
on
(0,\infty)
respectively.
Then
Z=XY
represents the product of these two components and its distribution denoted by
H
.
In risk theory,
X
and
Y
can be interpreted as the net loss and random discount, respectively.
Both of them are the basic research objects of risk theory.
The Breiman’s theorem studies the asymptotics relations of the tail distributions of
Z
and
X
.
Therefore, we should first introduce some concepts and symbols related to distribution.

Let
V
be a distribution, then we say that
\overline{V}=1-V
is the tail distribution of
V
.
If
V
is supported on
(-\infty,\infty)

(
including
[0,\infty)
and
(0,\infty)
)
, then

\overline{V}(x)>0\ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in(-\infty,\infty).

Recall that a distribution
V
on
(-\infty,\infty)
is said to be regularly-varying-tailed with the index
\alpha\geq 0
, denoted by
V\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
, if
\overline{V}(xy)\sim{\overline{V}}(x)y^{-\alpha}
, that is

\lim\overline{V}(xy)\big/{\overline{V}}(x)=y^{-\alpha}\ \ \ \ \text{for each}\ y>0.

Hereafter, all limits refer to
x\to\infty
unless otherwise stated.
For more details on regularly-varying-tailed distribution, see Bingham et al.
[
5
]
etc.
For example,

V\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}\ \ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \ {\overline{V}}(x)\sim x^{-\alpha}L(x),

where
L(\cdot)
is a positive slowly varying function at infinity satisfying

L(xy)\sim L(x)\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for each}\ \ y>0.

The distribution class
\cup_{\alpha\geq 0}\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
is a proper subclass
of the following distribution class introduced by Chistyakov
[
10
]
.
Say that
V
on
[0,\infty)

(
including
(0,\infty)
)
belongs to the subexponential distribution class
\mathscr{S}
, if

\overline{V^{*2}}(x)\sim 2\overline{V}(x),

where
V^{*2}
is the convolution of
V
with itself.
Say that
V
on
(-\infty,\infty)
belongs to the class
\mathscr{S}
, if
V^{+}\in\mathscr{S}
,
where
V^{+}(x)=V(x)\textbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)
for all
x\in(-\infty,\infty)
.

Another distribution class introduced by Feller
[
16
]
also properly includes class
\cup_{\alpha\geq 0}\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
.
Say that distribution
V
on
(-\infty,\infty)
belongs to the dominantly-varying-tailed distribution class
\mathscr{D}
,
if for each
t\in(0,1)
,
\overline{V}(tx)=O\big(\overline{V}(x)\big)
, that is

\limsup\overline{V}(tx)\big/\overline{V}(x)<\infty.

If
V\in\mathscr{D}
, then it has a useful property as follows, which plays a key role in many cases,
see, for example, Theorems 1.C, 2.A, 2.B,
2.1
,
3.1
and
3.2
below.

Proposition 1.A.

A distribution
V\in\mathscr{D}
if and only if for any distribution
U
on
(-\infty,\infty)

satisfying
\overline{U}(x)=o(\overline{V}(x))
, that is

\lim\overline{U}(x)\big/\overline{V}(x)=0,

there exists a positive function
g(\cdot)
on
[0,\infty)
such that

g(x)\downarrow 0,\ \ xg(x)\uparrow\infty\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \overline{U}\big(xg(x)\big)=o\big(\overline{V}(x)\big).

(1.1)

In the proposition, the proof of the necessity is given by Lemma 3.3 of Tang
[
41
]
,
and the proof of sufficiency is attributed to Proposition 3.1 of Zhou et al.
[
51
]
.

In addition, the class
\mathscr{S}
is properly included in the following distribution class.
Say that distribution
V
on
(-\infty,\infty)
belongs to the long-tailed distribution class
\mathscr{L}
,
denoted by
V\in\mathscr{L}
, if for each
t\in(0,\infty)
,

\overline{V}(x-t)\sim\overline{V}(x).

Goldie
[
17
]
pointed out that class
\mathscr{L}
and class
\mathscr{D}
cannot contain each other.
Further, for a distribution
V
, we denote a positive function class by

\mathscr{H}_{V}=\{h(\cdot):\ h(x)\uparrow\infty,\ \ x/g(x)\downarrow 0\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \overline{V}(x-t)\big/\overline{V}(x)\to 1\ \ \text{uniformly for all}\ \ |t|\leq h(x)\}.

Then the following research way for distributions is often used in many occasions.

Proposition 1.B.

(i) A distribution
V\in\mathscr{L}
if and only if set
\mathscr{H}_{V}
is not empty.

(ii) A distribution
V\in\mathscr{S}
if and only if
V\in\mathscr{L}
and for any
h(\cdot)\in\mathscr{H}_{V}
,

\int_{h(x)}^{x-h(x)}\overline{V}(x-y)V(dy)=o\big(\overline{V}(x)\big).

For the proofs of (i) and (ii), please refer to Lemma 2.5 of Cline and Samorodnitsky
[
11
]

and Proposition 1 of Asmussen et al.
[
3
]
, respectively.
For a systematic overview of subexponential distribution class and related distribution classes,
please refer to Embrechts et al.
[
15
]
, Resnick
[
36
]
, Borovkov and Borovkov
[
6
]
and Foss et al.
[
19
]
, and so on.

Many papers have been devoted to studying the tail asymptotics of the product
Z=XY
,
especially the asymptotic relationship between
\overline{H}(x)
and
\overline{F}(x)
.

In the case that
X
and
Y
are independent of each other, the following result was first stated by Breiman
[
7
]
with
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
And the complete conclusion can be found in Corollary 3.6. (iii) of Cline and Samorodnitsky
[

Theorem 1.A.

For the random vector
(X,Y)
, assume that
X
and
Y
are independent of each other.
If
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\infty)

and
\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha+\epsilon}<\infty
for some
\epsilon>0
, then
H\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
and

\overline{H}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x).

(1.2)

Since then, some papers have refined this result, see, for example,
Jessen and Mikosch
[
24
]
, Denisov and Zwart
[
13
]
and Resnick
[
36
]
.
Among them, Proposition 2.1 of Denisov and Zwart
[
13
]
weakens the moment condition of r.v.
Y

under some restrictions for distributions
F
and
G
.

Theorem 1.B.

For the random vector
(X,Y)
, assume that
X
and
Y
are independent of each other
and
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,\infty)

with a positive slowly varying function at infinity
L(\cdot)
.
If
\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}<\infty,\ {\overline{G}}(x)=o({\overline{F}}(x))
and

\lim\sup_{1\leq y\leq x}L(x/y)\big/L(x)<\infty,

(1.3)

then
H\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
and (
1.2
) holds.

When
F
is not necessarily a regularly-varying-tailed,
some corresponding results can be found in Hashorva et al.
[
23
]

for
F
belonging to the max-domain of attraction of the Gumbel or Weibull distribution
and for
G
belonging to the max-domain of attraction of the Weibull distribution,
Arendarczyk and Dȩbicki
[
1
]
for Weibull distributions
F
and
G
,
and Hashorva and Li
[
22
]
and Cui et al.
[
12
]
for semi-regular-varying-tailed
F
, among others.

In addition, based on Embrechts and Goldie
[
14
]
, Cline and Samorodnitsky
[
11
]
and Tang
[
40
]
, Xu et al.
[
47
]
gave an equivalent condition for
H\in\mathscr{S}
under the premise that
F\in\mathscr{S}
.
In particular, if
G
is supported by
[0,a]
for some
a>0
,
then a related result can also refer to Cline and Samorodnitsky
[
11
]
.
Other qualitative results can be found in Tang
[
39
]
, Liu and Tang
[
30
]
and so on.

In the case that
X
and
Y
are dependent, which is a common phenomenon in practice,
Maulik et al.
[
31
]
extended Theorem 1.A to the case that
Y
is asymptotically independent
of
X
(in a sense stronger than the usual concept of asymptotic independence),
Jiang and Tang
[
25
]
extended it to the case that
(X,Y)

follows the two-dimensional Farlie-Gumbel-Morgenstern (FGM) distribution,
see case 1 after Definition
1.1
below.
Furthermore, Yang and Wang
[
48
]
considered the two-dimensional Sarmanov distribution,
see Sarmanov
[
37
]
, which is more general than the FGM distribution.

Definition 1.1
.

Say that the random vector
(X,Y)
follows a bivariate Sarmanov distribution, if

\mathrm{P}(X\leq x,Y\leq y)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\int_{-\infty}^{y}\big(1+\theta\phi_{1}(u)\phi_{2}(v)\big)F(du)G(dv),\ \ \ \ \ x,y\in(-\infty,\infty),

(1.4)

where
\phi_{1}(\cdot)
and
\phi_{2}(\cdot)
are two measurable functions,
called kernels of this distribution, and the
\theta
is a real constant satisfying

\mathrm{E}\phi_{1}(X)=\mathrm{E}\phi_{2}(Y)=0,

(1.5)

and

1+\theta\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(y)\geq 0\ \ \ \ {\rm for\ all}\ x\in D_{X}\ \ \text{and}\ \ y\in D_{Y},

(1.6)

where

D_{X}=\big\{x\in(-\infty,\infty):\ \mathrm{P}\big(X\in(x-\delta,x+\delta)\big)>0\ \ \ {\rm for\ each}\ \delta>0\big\}

and

D_{Y}=\big\{y\in(0,\infty):\ \mathrm{P}\big(Y\in(y-\delta,y+\delta)\big)>0\ \ \ {\rm for\ each}\ \delta>0\big\}.

Clearly, if
\theta=0
, or
\phi_{1}(x)=0
for all
x\in D_{X}
, or
\phi_{2}(y)=0
for all
y\in D_{Y}
, then
X
and
Y
are independent.
So, we say that a random vector
(X,Y)
follows a proper bivariate Sarmanov distribution,
if the parameter
\theta\neq 0
, and the kernels
\phi_{1}(\cdot)
and
\phi_{2}(\cdot)

are not identical to
0
in
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
, respectively.
For some more details on multivariate Sarmanov distributions, one can refer
to Lee
[
28
]
, Kotz et al.
[
27
]
, among others.
Three common choices for the kernels
\phi_{1}(\cdot)
and
\phi_{2}(\cdot)
are listed as follows.

Case 1.
\phi_{1}(x)=1-2F(x)
and
\phi_{2}(y)=1-2G(y)
for all
x\in D_{X}

and
y\in D_{Y}
, leading to the well-known FGM distribution;

Case 2.
\phi_{1}(x)=(e^{-x}-c_{1}){\bf 1}_{[0,\infty)}(x)
with
c_{1}=\mathrm{E}e^{-X}{\bf 1}_{\{X\geq 0\}}\big/\mathrm{P}(X\geq 0)
and
\phi_{2}(y)=e^{-y}-\mathrm{E}e^{-Y}
for all
x\in D_{X}
and
y\in D_{Y}
;

Case 3.
\phi_{1}(x)=x^{p}-EX^{p}
and
\phi_{2}(y)=y^{p}-EY^{p}
for all
x\in D_{X}
and
y\in D_{Y}
.

Proposition 1.1 of Yang and Wang
[
48
]
remarks that the kernels are bounded for any proper bivariate Sarmanov distribution.

Proposition 1.C.

Assume that random vector
(X,Y)
follows a proper bivariate Sarmanov distribution.
Then there exist two positive constants

b_{1}
and
b_{2}
such that

|\phi_{1}(x)|\leq b_{1}\ \ \text{for all}\ x\in D_{X}\ \ \text{and}\ \ |\phi_{2}(y)|\leq b_{2}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ y\in D_{Y}.

Motivated by Theorem 1.B and Jiang and Tang
[
25
]
, using Proposition 1.A,
Theorem 2.1 of Yang and Wang
[
48
]
gives the following result.

Theorem 1.C.

Let
(X,Y)
be a random vector with a bivariate Sarmanov distribution. Assume that
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha\geq 0

with a positive slowly varying function at infinity
L(\cdot)
and the limit
\lim\phi_{1}(x)=d_{1}
exists.
If either (i)
\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha+\epsilon}<\infty
for some
\epsilon>0
;
or (ii)
\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}<\infty,\ {\overline{G}}(x)=o\big({\overline{F}}(x)\big)

and there exist a positive function
g(\cdot)
such that (
1.1
) holds and

\lim\sup_{1\leq y\leq xg(x)}L(x/y)\big/L(x)<\infty,

(1.7)

then it holds that

{\overline{H}}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}\big(s(Y)Y^{\alpha}\big){\overline{F}}(x),

(1.8)

where
s(y)=1+\theta d_{1}\phi_{2}(y)
for all
y>0
.

Further, Remark 2.1 of Yang and Wang
[
48
]
shows that
both Cases 1 and Case 2 satisfy all the conditions of Theorem 1.C.
And Remark 2.2 of Yang and Wang
[
48
]
notes that condition (
1.7
) is properly weaker than condition (
1.3
).

We might as well call Theorems 1.A, 1.B and 1.C the Breiman’s theorem.

Naturally, we prefer to get a Breiman’s theorem in a general dependent structure between
X
and
Y
.
In addition, we intuitively believe that there should be some relationship between moment of
Y
and function
s(\cdot)
.
And we hope that this relationship may help to weaken the moment condition of
Y
on some occasions.

In Section 2, we introduce a conditional dependent structure of random vector
(X,Y)
,
and we give some concrete examples of conditional dependent random vectors,
which show that the conditional dependent structure is relatively large.
We also get some interesting properties and a construction method of conditional dependent random vectors.
Then, using a simple way, we prove a new Breiman’s theorem for conditional dependent random vector
(X,Y)
.
Finally, in Section 3, according to this Breiman’s theorem,
we obtain the asymptotic estimations of finite-time ruin probability and infinite-time ruin probability
in a discrete-time risk model with conditional dependent net loss and random discount.

Breiman’s theorem for conditional dependent random vector

2.1
Concepts, Examples and Properties

We firstly introduce the concept of conditional dependent structure of random vector,
for research and application of which, please see, for example,
Badescu et al.
[
4
]
, Li et al.
[
29
]
, Asimit and Badescu
[
2
]
, Jiang et al.
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,Y)
be a random vector.
For each
x\in(-\infty,\infty)
and
y\in D_{Y}
, say that

\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{P}\big(X>x,Y\in[y,y+t)\big)}{\mathrm{P}\big(Y\in[y,y+t)\big)}

is the conditional distribution of
X
under condition
Y=y
, if the above limit exits.

Say that
(X,Y)
is conditionally dependent (CD),
if there exists a positive measurable function
s(\cdot)
on
[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)\sim\overline{F}(x)s(y)

uniformly for all
y\in D_{Y}
, that is

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{y\in D_{Y}}\big|\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)\big/\big(\overline{F}(x)s(y)\big)-1\big|=0.

(2.1)

Remark 2.1
.

(i). For each pair
x\in(-\infty,\infty)
and
y\notin D_{Y}
, if we define

\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)=\overline{F}(x)\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ s(y)=1,

then (
2.1
) still holds. Therefore, we can also expand the definition from
y\in D_{Y}
to
y>0
.

(ii). Later, we give some concrete examples of CD random vectors,
which show that the CD dependent structure has a wide range.
At the same time, we discuss some interesting properties of CD random vectors as follow.

1. Is
s(\cdot)
bounded? see Proposition
2.4
.

2. Can
s(\cdot)
weaken the moment condition of
Y
? see Example
2.1
.

3. Whether the CD property of a random vector is related to the parameters of its joint distribution?
see Proposition
2.3
.

4. Besides some specific CD random vectors, is there a method to construct CD random vectors?
see Proposition
2.5
.

5. Is
s(y)=1
equivalent to
X
and
Y
independent of each other? see Proposition
2.6
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(X,Y)
be a random vector with a bivariate Sarmanov joint distribution.
If
\lim\phi_{1}(x)=d_{1}
holds for some
d_{1}\in(-\infty,\infty)
,
\phi_{2}(\cdot)
is continuous on
(0,\infty)
and

\displaystyle s(y)=1+\theta d_{1}\phi_{2}(y)>0\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ y\in D_{Y},

(2.2)

then
(X,Y)
is CD with the above function
s(\cdot)
on
D_{Y}
.

Proof.

For each
y\in D_{Y}
, because
\phi_{2}(\cdot)
is continuous on
y
,
then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a
\delta=\delta\big(\phi_{2}(\cdot),y,\varepsilon\big)>0
such that

|\phi_{2}(v)-\phi_{2}(y)|<\varepsilon\ \ \ \text{for any}\ |v-y|<\delta.

Let
t\in(0,\delta)
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\big(X>x,Y\in[y,y+t)\big)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\int_{[y,y+t)}\big(1+\theta\phi_{1}(u)\phi_{2}(y)\big)F(du)G(dv)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\int_{x}^{\infty}\int_{[y,y+t)}\theta\phi_{1}(u)(\phi_{2}(v)-\phi_{2}(y))F(du)G(dv)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle(\text{or}>)\ \mathrm{P}\big(Y\in[y,y+t)\big)\Big(\int_{x}^{\infty}\big(1+\phi_{1}(u)\phi_{2}(y)\big)F(du)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\varepsilon\ (\text{or}-\varepsilon)\int_{x}^{\infty}|\theta\phi_{1}(u)|F(du)\Big).

Further, according to Proposition 1.C, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\mathrm{P}\big(X>x,Y\in[y,y+t)\big)\big/\mathrm{P}\big(Y\in[y,y+t)\big)

(2.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\big(1+\theta\phi_{1}(u)\phi_{2}(y)\big)F(du)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\big(1+\theta d_{1}\phi_{2}(y)\big)F(du)+\int_{x}^{\infty}\theta\phi_{2}(y)\big(\phi_{1}(u)-d_{1}\big)F(du)

Therefore, by (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and
\phi_{1}(x)\to d_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{y\in D_{Y}}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)}{\overline{F}(x)s(y)}-1\Big|=\lim\sup_{y\in D_{Y}}\Big|\frac{\int_{x}^{\infty}\big(1+\theta d_{1}\phi_{2}(y)\big)F(du)}{\overline{F}(x)s(y)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\frac{\int_{x}^{\infty}\theta\phi_{2}(y)\big(\phi_{1}(u)-d_{1}\big)F(du)}{\overline{F}(x)s(y)}-1\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

that is
(X,Y)
is CD.

\Box

Now we pay attention to another dependent structure of two-dimensional random vector,
see, for example, Nelsen

]

Definition 2.2
.

Say that a random vector
(X,Y)
follows a bivariate Frank distribution, if for some
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(X\leq x,Y\leq y)=-\frac{1}{\theta}\ln\bigg(1+\frac{(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y)}-1)}{(e^{-\theta}-1)}\bigg),\ \ \ x,\ y\in(-\infty,\infty).

(2.4)

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(X,Y)
be the random vector with a bivariate Frank joint distribution, then
(X,Y)
is CD with the a positive measurable function

s(\cdot)
on
(0,\infty)
defined in (
2.5
) below.

Proof.

We first show that the following positive measurable function
s(\cdot)
is positive and bounded on
(0,\infty)
. In fact, because
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle 0<\frac{\theta e^{-\theta}}{1-e^{-\theta}}\leq s(y)=\frac{\theta e^{-\theta\overline{G}(y)}}{1-e^{-\theta}}\leq\frac{\theta}{1-e^{-\theta}}<\infty\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ y>0.

(2.5)

Secondly, we give the expression of
\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)
.
From (
2.4
) and

\lim_{v\to 0}(e^{-v}-1)/v=-1,

for each pair
x\in(-\infty,\infty)
and
y\in D_{Y}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}(X>x|Y=y)=1-\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\mathrm{P}\big(X\leq x,Y\in[y,y+t]\big)}{\mathrm{P}\big(Y\in[y,y+t]\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\theta\mathrm{P}\big(Y\in[y,y+t]\big)}\ln\Big(\frac{(e^{-\theta}-1)+(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y+t)}-1)}{(e^{-\theta}-1)+(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y)}-1)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\theta P(Y\in[y,y+t])}\ln\Big(1+\frac{(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y+t)}-e^{-\theta G(y)})}{(e^{-\theta}-1)+(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y)}-1)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\big(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1\big)(e^{-\theta G(y+t)}-e^{-\theta G(y)})}{\theta P(Y\in[y,y+t])\big((e^{-\theta}-1)+(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y)}-1)\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)e^{-\theta G(y)}}{(e^{-\theta}-1)+(e^{-\theta F(x)}-1)(e^{-\theta G(y)}-1)}\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{e^{-\theta\big(G(y+t)-G(y)\big)}-1}{\theta\big(G(y+t)-G(y)\big)}

\displaystyle=

Theorem 3.A.

In the described discrete-time risk model, assume that
\{(X,Y),(X_{i},Y_{i}):

i\geq 1\}
are i.i.d. random vectors.
Under the conditions Theorem 1.C, for each fixed integer

, it holds that

\psi(x,n)\sim\Big(\mathrm{E}\big(s(Y)Y^{\alpha}\big)\big(1-\mathrm{E}^{n}Y^{\alpha}\big)\big/\big(1-\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}\big)\Big){\overline{F}}(x),

(3.5)

where

. Among them, we naturally agree that

(1-\mathrm{E}^{n}Y^{\alpha})/(1-\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha})=n,\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}=1.

(3.6)

Theorem 3.B.

In the described discrete-time risk model, assume that
\{(X,Y),(X_{i},Y_{i}):

i\geq 1\}
are i.i.d. random vectors.
Under the conditions (i) of Theorem 1.C, for each fixed integer

(3.7)

Theorem 3.1
.

In the described discrete-time risk model, assume that
\{(X,Y),(X_{i},Y_{i})
,
i\geq 1\}
are i.i.d.random vectors.
Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
,
(
3.5
) holds with
s(\cdot)
in definition
2.1
for each fixed integer
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

We deal with case (i) and case (ii) together.

To prove (
3.5
), we only need respectively to prove that

\overline{H_{i}}(x)=P(X_{i}Y_{i}\cdots Y_{1}>x)\sim\mathrm{E}^{i-1}Y^{\alpha}\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y){\overline{F}}(x)

(3.8)

and

\psi(x,n)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{H_{i}}(x)

(3.9)

for all integers
1\leq i\leq n
and each
n\geq 1
by induction.

We first prove (
3.8
). According to Theorem
2.1
, (
3.8
) holds for
i=1
obviously, which implies
H_{1}\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
with a slowly varying function at infinity

L_{1}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y)L(x),

where
L(\cdot)
is a slowly varying function at infinity of distribution
F
.

Assume that (
3.8
) holds for each integer
i\geq 1
and
H_{i}\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}

with a slowly varying function at infinity
L_{i}(\cdot)
satisfying

L_{i}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}^{i-1}Y^{\alpha}\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y)L(x).

(3.10)

Clearly, replacing
L(\cdot)
with
L_{i}(\cdot)
, condition (
1.7
) is still satisfied and

{\overline{G}}(x)=o\big({\overline{F}}(x)\big)=o\big(\overline{H_{i}}(x)\big)\ \ \ \ \text{for each}\ i\geq 1.

Recall that
(X_{i},Y_{1}),\ i\geq 1
are i.i.d. random vectors.
Thus, according to Theorem
2.1
, by induction assumption, it holds that

\displaystyle\overline{H_{i+1}}(x)=\mathrm{P}\big((X_{i+1}Y_{i+1}\cdots Y_{2})Y_{1}>x\big)\sim\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}\overline{H_{i}}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}^{(i+1)-1}Y^{\alpha}\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y){\overline{F}}(x),

that is (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) hold for
i+1
and
H_{i+1}\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
.

Secondly, we prove (
3.9
). To this end, we note that for each
n\geq 1
,

\mathrm{P}(S_{n}>x)\leq\psi(x,n)\leq\mathrm{P}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{+}\prod_{j=1}^{i}Y_{j}>x\Big)

and
(X^{+},Y)
also is CD.
This indicates that if we can establish

\mathrm{P}(S_{n}>x)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{H_{i}}(x),

then the asymptotic formula

\mathrm{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{+}\prod_{j=1}^{i}Y_{j}>x\right)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{H_{i}}(x)

(3.11)

should hold as well. Furthermore, since
(X,Y),\ (X_{i},Y_{i}),\ i\geq 1

are i.i.d. random vectors, it holds that

S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{i}Y_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\prod_{j=i}^{n}Y_{j}=T_{n},\ \ \ n\geq 1,

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{=}}
stands for equality in distribution.
Therefore, in order to prove (
3.9
), we only need to prove that

\mathrm{P}(T_{n}>x)=\overline{F_{T_{n}}}(x)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{H_{i}}(x)\sim\Big(\big(1-\mathrm{E}^{n}Y^{\alpha})\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y)\big/(1-\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha})\Big){\overline{F}}(x)

(3.12)

by induction on
n
.

Clearly, according to Theorem
2.1
, by
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
, we know that
(
3.12
) holds for
n=1
and
F_{T_{1}}=H_{1}\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
.

For the sake of brevity, we only prove (
3.12
) for
n=2
in detail.
By the method that has been used many times before, we have

\displaystyle\overline{F_{T_{2}}}(x)\sim\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\mathrm{P}(X_{1}Y_{1}+X_{2}>x/y|Y_{2}=y)G(dy)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{x/y}+\int_{x/y}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}(X_{2}>x/y-z|Y_{2}=y)H_{1}(dy)\Big)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{1}(x)+\mathrm{P}_{2}(x).

Now we deal with
\mathrm{P}_{1}(x)
. To this end, by any
h(\cdot)\in\mathscr{H}_{F}
, we further split

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{1}(x)=\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{-h(x/y)}+\int_{-h(x/y)}^{h(x/y)}+\int_{h(x/y)}^{x/y-h(x/y)}+\int_{x/y-h(x/y)}^{x/y}\Big)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\mathrm{P}(X_{2}>x/y-z|Y_{2}=y)H_{1}(dz)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{11}(x)+\mathrm{P}_{12}(x)+\mathrm{P}_{13}(x)+\mathrm{P}_{14}(x).

By
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
, we know that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{11}(x)\lesssim\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\overline{F}(x/y)s(y)H_{1}(-h(x/y)G(dy)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle H_{1}\big(-h\big(1/g(x)\big)\overline{F}(x)\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\big(y^{\alpha}s(y)L(x/y)\big/L(x)\big)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o(\overline{F}(x)).

According to Proposition 1.B (i), by
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{12}(x)\sim\int_{0}^{xg(x)}s(y)\int_{-h(x/y)}^{h(x/y)}\mathrm{P}(X_{2}>x/y-z)H_{1}(dy)G(dy)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\overline{F}(x/y)s(y)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y)\overline{F}(x).

And according to Proposition 1.B (ii), by (
3.8
) with
i=1
and
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
, using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{13}(x)\sim\int_{0}^{xg(x)}s(y)\int_{h(x/y)}^{x/y-h(x/y)}\mathrm{P}(X_{2}>x/y-z)H_{1}(dz)G(dy)

(3.17)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{xg(x)}s(y)\int_{h(x/y)}^{x/y-h(x/y)}\overline{H_{1}}(x/y-u)F(du)G(dy)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{xg(x)}s(y)o\big(\overline{F}(x/y)\big)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\big(\overline{F}(x)\big).

Finally, by (
3.8
) with
i=1
and
F\in\mathscr{R}_{-\alpha}
again, we get that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}_{14}(x)\leq\int_{0}^{xg(x)}\mathrm{P}\big(X_{1}Y_{1}\in(x/y-h(x/y),x/y]\big)G(dy)

(3.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{xg(x)}o\big(\overline{H_{1}}(x/y)\big)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\big(\overline{F}(x)\big).

Next, we deal with
\mathrm{P}_{2}(x)
. On the one hand, it is obvious to get that

\mathrm{P}_{2}(x)\leq\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}\overline{H_{1}}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y)\overline{F}(x).

(3.19)

On the other hand, according to Fatou Lemma, by (
3.8
), for any
a>1
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf\mathrm{P}_{2}(x)\big/\overline{H_{1}}(x)\geq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\liminf\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}(X_{2}>x/y-z|Y_{2}=y)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\textbf{1}_{\{z>ax/y\}}H_{1}(dz)\textbf{1}_{\{y\leq xg(x)\}}\big/\overline{H_{1}}(x)G(dy)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\liminf\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\big(X_{2}>-(a-1)x/y|Y_{2}=y\big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\textbf{1}_{\{z>ax/y\}}H_{1}(dz)\textbf{1}_{\{y\leq xg(x)\}}\big/\overline{H_{1}}(x)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\liminf\mathrm{P}\big(X_{2}>-(a-1)x/y|y_{2}=y\big)\overline{H_{1}}(ax/y)\big/\overline{H_{1}}(x)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\liminf\overline{H_{1}}(ax/y)\big/\overline{H_{1}}(x)G(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{-\alpha}\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y).

Then let
a\downarrow 1
, combining with (
3.19
), we know that

\mathrm{P}_{2}(x)\sim\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}\mathrm{E}Y^{\alpha}s(Y)\overline{F}(x).

(3.21)

Hence by (
3.13
)-(
3.18
) and (
3.21
), (
3.12
) is holds for

.

This completes the proof of Theorem

On the stability of the spherically symmetric solution to an inflow problem for an isentropic model of compressible viscous fluid
Yucong Huang, Itsuko Hashimoto, Shinya Nishibata

Abstract
We investigate an inflow problem for the multi-dimensional isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The fluid under consideration occupies the exterior domain of the unit ball Ω = { x ∈ ℝ n | | x | ≥ 1 } \Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,|x|\geq 1\} and a constant stream of mass is flowing into the domain from the boundary ∂ Ω = { | x | = 1 } \partial\Omega=\{|x|=1\} . It is shown in [ 4 ] that if the fluid velocity at the far-field is zero, then there exists a unique spherically symmetric stationary solution, denoted by ( ρ ~ , u ~ ) (\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u}) . In this paper, we show that either ρ ~ \tilde{\rho} is monotone increasing or ρ ~ \tilde{\rho} attains a unique global minimum and this is classified by the boundary condition of density. In addition, we derive a set of spatial decay rates for ( ρ ~ , u ~ ) (\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u}) which allows us to prove a time asymptotic stability of ( ρ ~ , u ~ ) (\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u}) using the energy method. More specifically, we prove this under small initial perturbation on ( ρ ~ , u ~ ) (\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u}) provided that the density at the far-field is strictly positive but suitably small, in other words, the far-field state of the fluid is not vacuum but suitably rarefied. The main difficulty for the proof is the boundary terms that appears in the a-priori estimates due to the in-flowing boundary condition. We resolve this issue by reformulating the problem in Lagrangian coordinate system.

Compressible Navier–Stokes equation, Inflow Problem, Stationary wave,

\displaystyle\Delta U\vcentcolon=(\Delta U_{1},\dots,\Delta U_{n}),\qquad\Delta U_{j}\vcentcolon=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}^{2}U_{j},

In the present paper, we consider the model where fluid is flowing into the domain at a constant flux from an inner sphere centred at the origin of radius

Main results

First, we state the existence, uniqueness and property of the stationary solution for the problem (
1.8
)–(
1.11
), which are summarised in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
and
C=C(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
such that if
|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+u_{b}\leq\varepsilon
, then a unique solution
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})
to the problem (
1.8
)–(
1.11
) exists in a certain neighborhood of
(\rho_{+},0)
. Moreover, for
r\geq 1

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}(r)|\leq Cu_{b}r^{1-n},\qquad|\tilde{\rho}(r)-\rho_{+}|\leq C\big\{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\rho_{+}^{2-\gamma}u_{b}^{2}\big\}r^{-2n+2},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d\tilde{u}}{dr}\right|\leq C\Big\{u_{b}+\rho_{+}^{\gamma-1}|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|\Big\}r^{-n},\quad\left|\frac{d\tilde{\rho}}{dr}\right|\leq C\Big\{\rho_{+}^{2}u_{b}+\rho_{+}^{\gamma}\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|}{u_{b}}\Big\}r^{-2n+1}.

(2.13)

Furthermore, the sign of
\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}
determines the behaviour of density
\tilde{\rho}(r)
as follows

(I)

if
\rho_{b}\geq\rho_{+}
, then there exists a unique point
r_{\ast}>1
such that
\tilde{\rho}(r)
is strictly decreasing in
1\leq r<r_{\ast}
and strictly increasing in

with

\min\limits_{r\geq 1}\tilde{\rho}(r)=\tilde{\rho}(r_{\ast}),\qquad\lim\limits_{r\to\infty}\tilde{\rho}(r)=\rho_{+}.

(II)

if
\rho_{b}<\rho_{+}
, then
\rho(r)
is strictly increasing in
r\geq 1
.

The existence of the stationary solution satisfying (
1.11
) is first proved in
[
4
]
.
In Section
3
, we review the well-posedness theorem stated in
[
4
]
. Then, based on their result, we derive the improved decay rates (
2.12
)–(
2.13
). Moreover, we prove that if
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})
solves (
1.8
)–(
1.11
), then the properties
(I)
–

in Lemma
2.1
must hold. The two major improvements we obtained for the decay estimates (
2.12
)–(
2.13
) are as follows: 1. a stronger decay rate on the order of

is proved to hold in contrast to the one stated in
[
4
]

is explicitly determined. These two refinements play an essential role in the analysis of the time asymptotic stability in Section

. This is one of the main results in the present paper and it is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\sigma
be an arbitrary constant
satisfying

.
Suppose
\gamma>1
and the initial data satisfying (

) belongs to the function space,

\displaystyle r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho}),\quad r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho})_{r}\ \in L^{2}(1,\infty),\qquad

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{B}^{1+\sigma}\big([1,\infty)\big),

(2.14a)

\displaystyle r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(u_{0}-\tilde{u}),\quad r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(u_{0}-\tilde{u})_{r}\ \in L^{2}(1,\infty),\qquad

,

and

\displaystyle(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{B}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2},1+\sigma}\times\mathcal{B}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2},2+\sigma}\big([0,T]\times[1,\infty)\big),

\displaystyle(\rho-\tilde{\rho},u-\tilde{u})\in C\big([0,\infty);H^{1}(1,\infty)\big)

converges to the stationary solution

as time tends to infinity. Precisely,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup_{r\geq 1}|(\rho,u)(t,r)-(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})(r)|=0.

(2.15)

Remark 2.3
.

In Theorem
2.2
, only the isentropic case
\gamma>1
is considered. The stability of the stationary solution
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})
for isothermal case
\gamma=1
is an open problem that we plan to investigate further in the future. Moreover, we have also imposed in Theorem

can not be too large, in the sense that it is bounded above by some given constant. For several cases of the inflow problem posed in

The question of whether this restriction can be relaxed is the subject of ensuing research and we believe that the solution to this problem could provide further insight towards the stability of spherically symmetric in-flowing fluid.

Related results.

The compressible Navier-Stokes equation is an important subject in mathematical physics. Particularly, the problems of out-flowing or in-flowing boundary have gained traction in recent decades. We state several previous results, which are relevant to the present paper.

First, for a comprehensive survey of the mathematical theory of compressible Navier-Stokes equation, we refer readers to the book
[
2
]
by S. N. Antontsev, A. V. Kazhikhov and V. N. Monakhov. The first breakthrough research on the large time stability of solution is done by A. Matsumura and T. Nishida in
[
17
]
, where they consider the heat-conductive compressible flow for a general
3
-dimensional exterior domain with adhesion boundary condition (
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and
\partial_{n}\theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0
where
\theta
is the
absolute
temperature). In this case the stationary solution is
(\tilde{\rho},0,\bar{\theta})
where
\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\rho}(x)
is a positive function of spatial variable and
\bar{\theta}
is a positive constant. Under smallness assumptions on the initial perturbation and external forces, it is proved that
(\tilde{\rho},0,\bar{\theta})
is stable globally in time.

When the equation is spherically symmetric with adhesion boundary condition, a pioneering work has been done by N. Itaya
[
9
]
, which establishes the global-in-time existence of a unique classical solution on a bounded annulus domain. In this work, no smallness assumption on the initial data is imposed. Later, T. Nagasawa studies the asymptotic state for the same
problem in
[
23
]
. The paper
[
9
]
has motivated a sequence of developments on the topic of spherically symmetric solution. For instance, A. Matsumura in
[
15
]
constructed a spherically symmetric classical solution to the isothermal model with external forces on a bounded annular domain. Moreover, he also shows the convergence rate to the stationary solution as time tends to infinity is exponential. Subsequently, this result has been extended to the isentropic and heat-conductive models by K. Higuchi in
[
6
]
. The well-posedness of spherically symmetric solution in an unbounded exterior domain is first obtained by S. Jiang
[
10
]
, where the global-in-time existence of a uniqueness classical solution is shown. In addition, a partial result on the time asymptotic stability is proved in
[
10
]
where, for
n=3
,
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2j}}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
with any fixed integer
j\geq 2
. Later, this restriction on the long time stability was fully resolved by T. Nakamura and S. Nishibata in
[
21
]
, where a complete stability theorem was obtained for both
n=2
and
3
with large initial data. We also refer to the paper
[
20
]
by T. Nakamura, S. Nishibata and S. Yanagi, where the time asymptotic stability of the spherically symmetric solution for the isentropic flow is established with large initial data and external forces.

For the general outflow or inflow problem, the stationary solution becomes non-trivial. This leads to a variety of physically interesting time asymptotic behaviours for the solutions. A. Matsumura in
[
16
]
starts the first investigation of this problem for the isentropic model posed on the one dimensional half-space domain. Several kinds of boundary conditions were considered in
[
16
]
, which includes inflow, outflow and adhesion boundary conditions. He formulated
conjectures on the classification of asymptotic behaviours of the solutions in different cases subject to the relation between the boundary data and the spatial asymptotic data. Then the stability theorems for some cases of inflow problem were established by A. Matsumura and K. Nishihara in
[
18
]
where they employed the Lagrangian mass coordinate. Following this work, S. Kawashima, S. Nishibata and P. Zhu in
[
11
]
further examine the outflow problem in one dimensional half space. They prove the long time stability of solutions with small initial perturbation with respect to the stationary solution. A detailed desicription of the convergence rate towards the stationary
solution is found in the work of T. Nakamura, S. Nishibata and T. Yuge
[
20
]
.

For the non-isentropic inflow problem in the half line, T. Nakamura and S. Nishibata in
[
22
]
demonstrate the time asymptotic stability of stationary solutions under a small initial perturbation, for both the subsonic and transonic cases.

The research on the outflow and inflow problems for the spherically symmetric solution in an unbounded exterior domain has been relatively new subject. Under the assumption that velocity at boundary,
|u_{b}|
is sufficiently small, I. Hashimoto and A. Matsumura in
[
4
]
employ the iteration method to obtain the existence of a unique spherically symmetric stationary solutions for both inflow and outflow problems in an exterior domain. More recently, I. Hashimoto, S. Nishibata, S. Sugizaki in
[
5
]
show the stability of spherically symmetric stationary solution for outflow problem under small initial perturbation. Subsequently, Y. Huang and S. Nishibata in
[
7
]
considers the large initial perturbation for the outflow problem and they prove that the stationary solution remains stable in large time without any smallness assumption on the initial data.

3
Stationary solution

3.1
Well-posedness of the stationary solution.

In this subsection, we discuss the existence and uniqueness of the stationary solution
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})
to (
1.8
). Set
\tilde{v}\vcentcolon=1/\tilde{\rho}
to be the specific volume,
v_{b}\vcentcolon=1/\rho_{b}
and
v_{+}=1/\rho_{+}
. Owing to the far-field condition
v\to v_{+}
as
r\to\infty
, we define the unknown
\eta\vcentcolon=\tilde{v}-v_{+}
. Then substituting (
1.10
) into (
1.8b
) yields the ordinary differential equation

m_{b}\mu\left(\frac{\eta_{r}}{r^{n-1}}\right)_{r}=p\big(v_{+}+\eta\big)_{r}+\frac{m_{b}^{2}v_{+}}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2(n-1)}}\Big)_{r}+\frac{m_{b}^{2}}{r^{n-1}}\left(\frac{\eta}{r^{n-1}}\right)_{r},

(3.16a)

where
m_{b}\vcentcolon=\rho_{b}u_{b}
and
p(v):=P\left(v^{-1}\right)
. Here
\eta
satisfies the boundary and far-field conditions

\eta(1)=\eta_{b}\equiv v_{b}-v_{+}\ \text{ and }\ \eta(r)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad r\to\infty.

(3.16b)

Using (
3.16
), we obtain a representation formula for
\eta
, which is suited for the fixed-point theorem for the contraction mapping. First, integrating (
3.16a
) by parts and using the far-field condition (
3.16b
), we have

\displaystyle\eta_{r}(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{n-1}}{m_{b}\mu}\big\{p(v_{+}+\eta(r))-p(v_{+})\big\}+\frac{m_{b}v_{+}}{2\mu}\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{b}\eta(r)}{\mu r^{n-1}}-\frac{m_{b}(n-1)r^{n-1}}{\mu}\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{\eta(s)}{s^{2n-1}}ds.

(3.17)

Let
\eta_{b}\vcentcolon=v_{b}-v_{+}
. Subtracting
\frac{p^{\prime}(v_{+})}{\mu m_{b}}r^{n-1}\eta
on both sides of (
3.17
), we have

\eta_{r}(r)-\displaystyle\frac{p^{\prime}(v_{+})}{\mu m_{b}}r^{n-1}\eta(r)=F[\eta](r),

(3.18)

where

\displaystyle F[\eta](r):=\dfrac{m_{b}v_{+}}{2\mu}\dfrac{1}{r^{n-1}}+\dfrac{m_{b}}{\mu}\dfrac{\eta(r)}{r^{n-1}}-\dfrac{(n-1)m_{b}r^{n-1}}{\mu}\int_{r}^{\infty}\!\!\dfrac{\eta(s)}{s^{2n-1}}\,ds+\dfrac{r^{n-1}}{\mu m_{b}}N\big(\eta(r)\big),

\displaystyle N(\eta(r)):=p(v_{+}+\eta(r))-p(v_{+})-p^{\prime}(v_{+})\eta(r).

Solving (
3.18
), we obtain the representation formula

\displaystyle\eta(r)=\eta_{b}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(1-r^{n})}+\int_{1}^{r}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}F[\eta](s)\,{d}s\quad\text{where }\ \kappa:=\frac{-p^{\prime}(v_{+})}{n\mu}>0,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\eta_{r}(r)=-\frac{n\kappa}{m_{b}}r^{n-1}\eta(r)+F[\eta](r).

(3.20)

(I. Hashimoto and A. Matsumura,

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

(3.23)

Proof.

By the definition of
F[\eta]
and formula (
3.19
), we have

\displaystyle\eta(r)=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\eta_{b}e^{-\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(r^{n}-1)}}_{\textrm{(i)}}+\underbrace{\frac{m_{b}v_{+}}{2\mu}\int_{1}^{r}\!\!s^{1-n}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds}_{\textrm{(ii)}}+\underbrace{\frac{m_{b}}{\mu}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{\eta(s)}{s^{n-1}}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds}_{\textrm{(iii)}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{(1-n)m_{b}}{\mu}\int_{1}^{r}\!\!\!\int_{s}^{\infty}\!\!\!\eta(\tau)\frac{s^{n-1}}{\tau^{2n-1}}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,d\tau}_{\textrm{(iv)}}+\underbrace{\int_{1}^{r}\!\frac{s^{n-1}}{\mu m_{b}}N\big(\eta(s)\big)e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds}_{\textrm{(v)}}.

We estimate the right hand side of above equality term by term. First we impose that
m_{b}\leq\kappa
. Then by (
3.19
) and (
3.22
), this is equivalent to the condition

u_{b}\leq C\rho_{+}^{\gamma},

(3.24)

where
C=C(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
is the constant independent of
\rho_{+}
,
\rho_{b}
and
u_{b}
. Moreover, it holds that
-\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(r^{n}-1)\leq-(r^{n}-1)
for
r\geq 1
, since
\kappa>0
. Thus we have that
\exp\big(-\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(r^{n}-1)\big)\leq\exp\big(-(r^{n}-1)\big)
. Using this, we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(i)}|\leq

\displaystyle|\eta_{b}|\frac{r^{-2n}}{(n-1)^{2}}(n-1)^{2}r^{2n}e^{-(r^{n}-1)}\leq C|\eta_{b}|r^{-2(n-1)}.

Integrating (ii) by parts and using Proposition
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\textrm{(ii)}|=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{b}^{2}v_{+}}{2n\mu\kappa}\bigg|r^{2-2n}-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(r^{n}-1)}+2(n-1)\int_{1}^{r}s^{1-2n}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cu_{b}^{2}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}\{r^{-2(n-1)}+r^{-2n+1}\}\leq Cu_{b}^{2}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}r^{-2(n-1)}.

Using the upper bound (
3.22
) and repeating the same argument used for estimating (ii), we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(iii)}|\leq\frac{m_{b}v_{+}}{4\mu}\bigg|\int_{1}^{r}s^{1-n}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{b}^{2}v_{+}}{4n\mu\kappa}\bigg|r^{2-2n}-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(r^{n}-1)}+2(n-1)\int_{1}^{r}s^{1-2n}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds\bigg|\leq Cu_{b}^{2}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}r^{-2(n-1)}.

Similarly, by Proposition
3.2
and (
3.22
), we also obtain the estimate

\displaystyle|\textrm{(iv)}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)m_{b}}{\mu}\int_{1}^{r}s^{n-1}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\int_{s}^{\infty}\frac{|\eta(\tau)|}{\tau^{2n-1}}\,d\tau ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cm_{b}v_{+}\int_{1}^{r}s^{-2n+2}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds\leq Cu_{b}^{2}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}r^{-2(n-1)}.

By Taylor’s theorem and upper and lower bounds (
3.22
), it holds that for an arbitrary
s\in[1,r]
there exists
\xi_{s}\in(\frac{3}{4}v_{+},\frac{5}{4}v_{+})
such that

\displaystyle\big|N\big(\eta(s)\big)\big|=\frac{1}{2}p^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{s})|\eta(s)|^{2}\leq C\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}|\eta(s)|^{2}.

(3.25)

Using (
3.25
), Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(v)}|\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}}{m_{b}}\int_{1}^{r}s^{n-1}|\eta(s)|^{2}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}}{m_{b}}\sup\limits_{1\leq\tau\leq r}|\tau^{2(n-1)}\eta(\tau)|r^{-2(n-1)}r^{2(n-1)}\int_{1}^{r}s^{-n+1}|\eta(s)|e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}}{m_{b}}\sup\limits_{1\leq\tau\leq r}|\tau^{2(n-1)}\eta(\tau)|\cdot r^{-2(n-1)}u_{b}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}\sup\limits_{r\geq 1}|r^{n-1}\eta(r)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho_{+}C(\rho_{+})\big\{|\eta_{b}|+u_{b}^{2}\big\}r^{-2(n-1)}\sup\limits_{1\leq\tau\leq r}|\tau^{2(n-1)}\eta(\tau)|.

Owing to the estimates (i)–(v), we get for
r\geq 1

\displaystyle|\eta(r)|\leq C\big\{|\eta_{b}|+u_{b}^{2}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}\big\}r^{-2(n-1)}+\rho_{+}C(\rho_{+})\big\{u_{b}^{2}+|\eta_{b}|\big\}r^{-2(n-1)}\sup\limits_{1\leq s\leq r}|s^{2(n-1)}\eta(s)|.

Let
R>1
. Multiplying both sides of the above inequality by
r^{2(n-1)}
with
r\in[1,R]
. Then taking the supremum over
1\leq r\leq R
, we obtain that there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
and
C=C(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
for which if
u_{b}+|\eta_{b}|\leq\varepsilon
then

\sup\limits_{1\leq r\leq R}r^{2(n-1)}|\eta(r)|\leq C\big\{|\eta_{b}|+\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}u_{b}^{2}\big\}\qquad\text{ for an arbitrary }\ R>1.

(3.26)

Taking the limit
R\to\infty
, we obtain the first inequality for (
3.23
).

Next, we derive the estimate of
\eta_{r}
. Applying integration by parts on the formula (
3.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{r}(r)=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{(1-n)v_{+}}{n\mu\kappa}m_{b}^{2}r^{-2n+1}}_{\textrm{(a)}}+\underbrace{\Big\{F[\eta](1)-\frac{n\kappa\eta_{b}}{m_{b}}-\frac{(n-1)v_{+}}{n\mu\kappa}m_{b}^{2}\Big\}r^{n-1}e^{-\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(r^{n}-1)}}_{\textrm{(b)}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1-n}{\mu}r^{n-1}\int_{1}^{r}\Big\{\frac{(3n-2)v_{+}}{n\kappa}\frac{m_{b}^{2}}{s^{3n-1}}+\frac{m_{b}\eta(s)}{s^{2n-1}}\Big\}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds}_{\textrm{(c)}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{r^{n-1}}{\mu}\int_{1}^{r}\!\!\eta_{r}(s)\Big\{\frac{m_{b}}{s^{2(n-1)}}+\frac{N^{\prime}\big(\eta(s)\big)}{m_{b}}\Big\}e^{\frac{\kappa}{m_{b}}(s^{n}-r^{n})}\,ds}_{\textrm{(d)}}.

(3.27)

By (
3.19
), (
3.22
) and the assumption (
3.24
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\textrm{(a)}|\leq C\frac{\rho_{+}^{-1}}{\rho_{+}^{\gamma+1}}\rho_{+}^{2}u_{b}^{2}r^{-2n+1}\leq C\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}u_{b}^{2}r^{-2n+1}\leq Cu_{b}r^{-2n+1}.

Using the definition of
F[\eta]
, (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), we verify that

\displaystyle\Big|F[\eta](1)-\frac{n\kappa\eta_{b}}{m_{b}}\Big|=\bigg|\frac{m_{b}v_{+}}{2\mu}+\frac{m_{b}\eta_{b}}{\mu}-\frac{(n-1)m_{b}}{\mu}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta(s)}{s^{2n-1}}\,ds+\frac{p(v_{+}+\eta_{b})-p(v_{+})}{\mu m_{b}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\{u_{b}+\rho_{+}u_{b}\big(|\eta_{b}|+u_{b}^{2}\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}\big)+\frac{|\eta_{b}|}{u_{b}}\rho_{+}^{\gamma}\Big\}.

By the property of exponential function, the condition
m_{b}\leq n\kappa
and (
3.24
), we have

\displaystyle|\textrm{(b)}|\leq

\displaystyle\Big|F[\eta](1)-\frac{n\kappa\eta_{b}}{m_{b}}-\frac{(n-1)v_{+}}{n\mu\kappa}m_{b}^{2}\Big|r^{-2n+1}(r^{n})^{\frac{3n-2}{n}}e^{-r^{n}+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\{\rho_{+}^{\gamma}\frac{|\eta_{b}|}{u_{b}}+u_{b}+\rho_{+}|\eta_{b}|u_{b}+\rho_{+}u_{b}^{2}\Big\}r^{-2n+1},

Applying Proposition
3.2
, (
3.22
) and (
3.24
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\textrm{(c)}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r^{2n-1}}\frac{u_{b}}{\rho_{+}^{\gamma}}\sup\limits_{r\geq 1}\Big|\frac{(3n-2)v_{+}}{n\kappa}\frac{m_{b}^{2}}{r}+m_{b}r^{n-1}\eta(r)\Big|\leq Cu_{b}r^{-2n+1}.

By mean value theorem, we have
|N^{\prime}\big(\eta(s)\big)|\leq C\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}|\eta(s)|
. Using this, (
3.26
) and Proposition
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(d)}|\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{-2n+1}\sup\limits_{1\leq\tau\leq r}\big\{\tau^{2n-1}\eta_{r}(\tau)\big\}\cdot\rho_{+}^{-\gamma}u_{b}\sup\limits_{s\geq 1}\bigg\{\frac{\rho_{+}u_{b}}{s^{n-1}}+\rho_{+}^{\gamma+1}\frac{s^{n-1}|\eta(s)|}{u_{b}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big\{\rho_{+}|\eta_{b}|+\rho_{+}^{1-\gamma}u_{b}^{2}\big\}r^{-2n+1}\sup\limits_{1\leq s\leq r}\big\{s^{2n-1}\eta_{r}(s)\big\}.

Substituting the estimates for (a)–(d) in (
3.27
), multiplying both sides by
r^{2n-1}
and taking supremum over
r\in[1,R]
for
R>1
, we get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{1\leq r\leq R}r^{2n-1}|\eta_{r}(r)|\leq

\displaystyle C\Big\{\rho_{+}^{\gamma}\frac{|\eta_{b}|}{u_{b}}+u_{b}+\rho_{+}|\eta_{b}|u_{b}+\rho_{+}u_{b}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle+C\big\{\rho_{+}|\eta_{b}|+\rho_{+}^{1-\gamma}u_{b}^{2}\big\}\sup\limits_{1\leq r\leq R}r^{2n-1}|\eta_{r}(r)|.

Thus, there exist
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
and
C=C(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
such that if
|u_{b}|+|\eta_{b}|\leq\varepsilon
then for arbitrary
R>1
,

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{1\leq r\leq R}r^{2n-1}|\eta_{r}(r)|\leq C\Big\{u_{b}+\rho_{+}^{\gamma}\frac{|\eta_{b}|}{u_{b}}\Big\}.

Taking
R\to\infty
yields the second inequality of (
3.23
).
\square

3.30
) holds, applying Rolle’s theorem yields that there exits a unique point
r_{\ast}>1
with
\eta(r_{\ast})=\text{max}_{r\geq 1}\eta(r)
.

Case 2:
\eta_{b}>0
.

We consider two sub-cases
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}\leq 0
or
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}>0
. First, consider the case
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}\leq 0
. We show
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}<0
by contradiction. Suppose otherwise that
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}=0
. Then by repeating the same argument used in the proof of (
3.30
), we get that
\eta_{b}=\eta|_{r=1}\leq-v_{+}
. This leads to
v_{b}\leq 0
, which is a contradiction. Thus, for this sub-case, we must have
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}<0
. Suppose
\eta(r)
is not strictly decreasing, which means there exist two points
1<r_{1}<r_{2}
such that
\eta_{r}\geq 0
in
r\in(r_{1},r_{2})
. Then by intermediate value theorem, there exists a point
\bar{r}>1
such that
\eta_{r}(\bar{r})=0
and
\eta_{rr}(\bar{r})\geq 0
. This is a contradiction to (
3.30
). Therefore we conclude that
\eta(r)
is strictly decreasing. Finally we observe from the formula (
3.20
) and Lemma
3.3
that, we can choose a small constant
\varepsilon>0
for which if
\eta_{b}>0
and
u_{b}+|\eta_{b}|\leq\varepsilon
then
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}\leq 0
. This rules out the second sub-case
\eta_{r}|_{r=1}>0
and we conclude that
r\mapsto\eta(r)
is strictly decreasing with
\eta_{b}>0
and
u_{b}+|\eta_{b}|\leq\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon
small enough.

\square

4
A-priori estimates in Lagrangian coordinate

The main strategy for the proof of Theorem
2.2
is to employ the energy method and derive a-priori estimates in the Lagrangian coordinate. For inflow problem formulated in the Eulerian coordinate, the main difficulty is the boundary term in the estimate of
\psi\vcentcolon=u-\tilde{u}
. To resolve this issue, we use the structure of the equations under the Lagrangian formulation. First we state the local-in-time existence in the Eulerian coordinates

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfies the same conditions stated in Theorem
2.2
. Then there exists a constant
T_{0}>0
, depending only on
\|r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho_{0}-\tilde{\rho})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1+\sigma}}
and
\|r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(u_{0}-\tilde{u})\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2+\sigma}}
, such that the initial-boundary value problem (
1.3
)–(
1.7
) and (
1.11
) has a unique solution satisfying

\displaystyle r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho}),\quad r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}u,\quad r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})_{r}\,,\quad r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}u_{r}\ \in C\big([0,T];L^{2}(1,\infty)\big),

\displaystyle\rho\in\mathcal{B}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2},\,1+\sigma}\big([0,T]\times[1,\infty)\big),\qquad u\in\mathcal{B}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2},\,2+\sigma}\big([0,T]\times[1,\infty)\big),

for an arbitrary
T\in(0,T_{0})
.

The energy norm for the a-priori estimate in Eulerian coordinate is defined by

\displaystyle N_{\textrm{E}}(t):=\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}\Big\{\big\|r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho},u-\tilde{u})\big\|+\big\|r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})_{r}\big\|+\big\|r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(u-\tilde{u})_{r}\big\|\Big\},

(4.33)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} M_{\textrm{E}}^{2}(t):=&\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\big\|r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}(\rho-\tilde{\rho})_{r}\big\|^{2}+\big\|r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}(u-\tilde{u})_{r}\big\|^{2}+\big\|r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}(u-\tilde{u})_{rr}\big\|^{2}\,d\tau\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}u_{b}\big|(u-\tilde{u})_{r}(\tau,1)\big|^{2}+u_{b}^{3}\big|(\rho-\tilde{\rho})_{r}(\tau,1)\big|^{2}\,d\tau.\end{aligned}

(4.34)

By Lemma
4.1
, for a given
\epsilon>0
there exists a time
T(\epsilon)>0
such that if
N_{E}(0)\leq\epsilon
, then
N_{E}(t)\leq 2\epsilon
for all
t\in[0,T(\epsilon)]
. Combining this argument with the Sobolev embedding theorem and Lemma
2.1
, we find constants
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
and
T=T(\varepsilon_{0})>0
such that if
|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+u_{b}+N_{E}(0)\leq\varepsilon_{0}
then

N_{E}(t)\leq 2\varepsilon_{0},\qquad\tfrac{1}{2}\rho_{+}\leq\rho(r,t)\leq 2\rho_{+}\qquad\text{for all }\ (r,t)\in[1,\infty)\times[0,T].

(4.35)

4.1
Reformulation in Lagrangian Coordinate

Let
(\rho,u)(r,t)
be the local-in-time solution with the maximum time of existence
T>0
. Consider the transformation
(r,t)\mapsto X(r,t)\vcentcolon[1,\infty)\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

X(r,t)\vcentcolon=-m_{b}t+\int_{1}^{r}\rho(y,t)y^{n-1}dy\quad\text{ where }\quad m_{b}\vcentcolon=\rho_{b}u_{b}>0.

(4.36)

Since
\frac{1}{2}\rho_{+}\leq\rho(r,t)\leq 2\rho_{+}
in
(r,t)\in[1,\infty)\times[0,T]
, Inverse Function theorem implies that, for each
t\in[0,T]
there exists a unique map
x\mapsto R(x,t)\vcentcolon[-m_{b}t,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
such that
X(R(x,t),t)=x
. Namely,

\displaystyle x=-m_{b}t+\int_{1}^{R(x,t)}\rho(r,t)r^{n-1}dr\quad\text{for each $-m_{b}t\leq x$,}

(4.37a)

\displaystyle R(-m_{b}t,t)=1\quad\text{ and }\quad R(x,t)\to\infty\ \text{ as }\ x\to\infty.

(4.37b)

Using the above construction, we set the Lagrangian space-time domain
\mathcal{Q}(T)
and the Lagrangian snapshot domain
S(t)
at time
t\in[0,T]
to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(T)\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]\;|\;-m_{b}t\leq x<\infty\},

(4.38a)

\displaystyle S(t)\vcentcolon=

\displaystyle\{x\in\mathbb{R}\;|\;-m_{b}t\leq x<\infty\}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,T]$.}

(4.38b)

By the Implicit Function theorem and the regularity of
(\rho,u)
, the derivatives
R_{x}
and
R_{t}
exist and it follows from (
1.3a
) and (
4.37
) that for an arbitrary
(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}(T)

R_{t}(x,t)=u(R(x,t),t),\quad R_{x}(x,t)=\dfrac{R(x,t)^{1-n}}{\rho(R(x,t),t)}.

(4.39)

Therefore
R(x,t)
satisfies the following identity

R(x,t)=R_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}u(R(x,s),s)ds=\Big(1+n\int_{-m_{b}t}^{x}\dfrac{1}{\rho(R(y,t),t)}\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}},

(4.40)

where
R_{0}(x)
is implicitly defined by

x=\int_{1}^{R_{0}(x)}\!\!\rho_{0}(r)r^{n-1}\,dr\ \text{ for $0\leq x<\infty$.}

The transformation from the Eulerian coordinate
(r,t)
to the Lagrangian coordinate
(x,t)
is executed by the equation
r=R(x,t)
. Let
v\vcentcolon=1/\rho
be the specific volume. Using (
4.39
), we obtain equations for
(\hat{u},\hat{v})(x,t):=(u,v)(R(x,t),t)
from (
1.3
) as

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{t}=(r^{n-1}\hat{u})_{x},

(4.41a)

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{t}=\mu r^{n-1}\Big(\frac{(r^{n-1}\hat{u})_{x}}{\hat{v}}\Big)_{x}-r^{n-1}p(\hat{v})_{x},

(4.41b)

where
p(v)=Kv^{-\gamma}
and
r=R(x,t)
. The initial and the boundary conditions
for
(\hat{v},\hat{u})
are derived from (
1.5
) as

\displaystyle\hat{v}(x,0)=\hat{v}_{0}(x):=1/\rho_{0}(R_{0}(x)),\quad\hat{u}(x,0)=\hat{u}_{0}(x):=u_{0}(R_{0}(x)),

(4.42a)

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{0}(0)=v_{b}\equiv 1/\rho_{b},\quad\hat{u}_{0}(0)=u_{b},

(4.42b)

\displaystyle\hat{u}(-m_{b}t,t)=u_{b},\quad\hat{v}(-m_{b}t,t)=v_{b}\equiv 1/\rho_{b}.

(4.42c)

Since the spatial variable in Eulerian coordinate,
r=R(x,t)
, is a function of spatial and temporal variables in the Lagrangian coordinate, the stationary solution
(\tilde{\rho},\tilde{u})
also depends on
(x,t)
. For simplicity we abuse the notation to denote

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(x,t):=\tilde{\rho}(R(x,t)),\quad\tilde{v}(x,t):=1/\tilde{\rho}(R(x,t)),\quad\tilde{u}(x,t):=\tilde{u}(R(x,t)),

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{r}(x,t):=\tilde{\rho}_{r}(R(x,t)),\quad\tilde{v}_{r}(x,t):=-\big(\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{r}}{\tilde{\rho}^{2}}\big)(R(x,t)),\quad\tilde{u}_{r}(x,t):=\tilde{u}_{r}(R(x,t)),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(x,t)\vcentcolon=\mu\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{\partial_{r}(r^{n-1}\tilde{u})}{r^{n-1}}\Big)\Big|_{r=R(x,t)}=\big\{\tilde{\rho}\tilde{u}\partial_{r}\tilde{u}+\partial_{r}P(\tilde{\rho})\big\}\big|_{r=R(x,t)}.

(4.43)

With these constructions, we define the difference functions as

\phi(x,t)\vcentcolon=\hat{v}(x,t)-\tilde{v}(x,t),\quad\psi(x,t)\vcentcolon=\hat{u}(x,t)-\tilde{u}(x,t).

Then
(\phi,\psi)
satisfies the following equations in domain
(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}(T)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}-(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}=\mathcal{R}_{1},

(4.44a)

\displaystyle\psi_{t}-\mu r^{n-1}\Big(\dfrac{(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}}{v}\Big)_{x}+r^{n-1}p^{\prime}(v)\phi_{x}=\mathcal{R}_{2},

(4.44b)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}:=\dfrac{\tilde{v}_{r}\tilde{u}}{\tilde{v}}\phi-\tilde{v}_{r}\psi,\quad\mathcal{R}_{2}:=\dfrac{\tilde{u}_{r}\tilde{u}}{\tilde{v}}\phi-\tilde{v}_{r}\dfrac{p^{\prime}(v)-p^{\prime}(\tilde{v})}{v-\tilde{v}}v\phi-\tilde{u}_{r}\psi.

(4.44c)

Moreover, boundary condition (
1.5
) and (
4.37b
) yield that

\phi(-m_{b}t,t)=0,\quad\psi(-m_{b}t,t)=0,\quad\text{for }\ t\in[0,T].

(4.45)

In what follows, we omit the hat “
\hat{\ }
” to express
functions in the Lagrangian coordinate simply as
(v,u)\equiv(\hat{v},\hat{u})
.

Under this formulation, we define the energy norm in Lagrangian coordinate as

\displaystyle N(t):=\sup\limits_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\big\{\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{L^{2}(S(\tau))}+\|r^{n-1}(\phi_{x},\psi_{x})\|_{L^{2}(S(\tau))}\big\},

(4.46)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} M^{2}(t):=&\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(t)}\Big\{\frac{\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}+r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}+r^{2n-4}\phi_{x}^{2}+r^{4n-6}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big\}\,dxd\tau\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\big\{u_{b}\psi_{x}^{2}+u_{b}^{3}\phi_{x}^{2}\big\}(-m_{b}\tau,\tau)\,d\tau.\end{aligned}

(4.47)

Let
T>0
be the time obtained in the inequality (
4.35
). Using the definition (
4.33
)–(
4.34
) and the differential relations (
4.39
), it is verified that there exists some constant
C(\rho_{+})=C(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
such that for
t\in[0,T]
,

C(\rho_{+})^{-1}N_{E}\leq N\leq C(\rho_{+})N_{E},\quad C(\rho_{+})^{-1}M_{E}\leq M\leq C(\rho_{+})M_{E}.

(4.48)

Consequently, there are constants
(\varepsilon_{0},T)
such that if
|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+u_{b}+N_{E}(0)\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, then

N(t)\leq 2\varepsilon_{0},\qquad\frac{1}{2\rho_{+}}\leq v(x,t)\leq\dfrac{2}{\rho_{+}}\quad\text{for }\ (x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}(T).

(4.49)

The main estimate of this section is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
(\phi,\psi)
solves (
4.44
) in
\mathcal{Q}(T)
for
T>0
such that (
4.49
) holds. Then there exist constants
\alpha=\alpha(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
which is independent of
\rho_{+}
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
, such that if
\rho_{+}\in(0,\alpha]
,
|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|\leq u_{b}^{2}
and
u_{b}<\varepsilon
, then

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}N^{2}(t)+M^{2}(T)\leq C(\rho_{+})\Big\{1+\frac{1}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}N^{2}(0),

where
C(\rho_{+})>0
is a constant which depends only on
\rho_{+}
,
\mu
,
\gamma
,
K
and
n
.

4.2
Relative energy estimate

Define the relative energy as

\mathcal{E}:=\dfrac{|u-\tilde{u}|^{2}}{2}+G[v,\tilde{v}],\quad\text{where }\ G[v,\tilde{v}]:=\int_{1/\tilde{v}}^{1/v}\dfrac{P(z)-P(\tilde{v}^{-1})}{z^{2}}\,dz.

(4.50)

For the isentropic pressure law
P(\rho)=K\rho^{\gamma}
, we have

G[v,\tilde{v}]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tfrac{K}{\gamma-1}(v^{1-\gamma}-\tilde{v}^{1-\gamma})+K\tilde{v}^{-\gamma}(v-\tilde{v})&&\text{if}\quad\gamma>1,\\
&K\big(v/\tilde{v}-1-\log(v/\tilde{v})\big)&&\text{if}\quad\gamma=1.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
T>0
be the existence time in (
4.35
). Then by Taylor’s theorem, it holds that

G[v,\tilde{v}]\sim\rho_{+}^{\gamma+1}\phi^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{E}[v,u]\sim(\rho_{+}^{\gamma+1}\phi^{2}+\psi^{2})\qquad\text{for }\ (x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}(T).

(4.51)

Proposition 4.3

(Hardy-type inequality)
.

Suppose
n\geq 2
and
f(x,t):\mathcal{Q}(T)\to\mathbb{R}
is such that
f(-m_{b}t,t)=0
for
t\in[0,T]
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{f^{2}}{r^{2n}}\,dx\leq C\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{2}\}\int_{S(t)}f_{x}^{2}\,dx.

Proof.

From (
4.49
) and (
4.40
), we have

\displaystyle r(x,t)=\Big(1+n\int_{-m_{b}t}^{x}v(y,t)\,dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq C^{-1}\min\{1,\rho_{+}^{-1}\}^{\frac{1}{n}}(1+x+m_{b}t)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Hence it follows from the change of variable
z:=1+x+m_{b}t
that

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{f^{2}}{r^{2n}}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle C\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{2}\}\int_{S(t)}\frac{|f(x,t)|^{2}}{(1+x+m_{b}t)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{2}\}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{2}}{z^{2}}\Big|_{x=z-m_{b}t-1}\,dz.

(4.52)

Define the function
F(z,t)\vcentcolon=f(z-m_{b}t-1,t)
. Then
F(1,t)=0
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
F_{z}(z,t)=f_{x}|_{x=z-m_{b}t-1}
. Applying Minkowski’s integral inequality, we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{F^{2}}{z^{2}}\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\bigg(\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\!\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\!\frac{z-1}{z}F_{z}\big(1+(z-1)y,t\big)\,dy\bigg)^{2}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\int_{1}^{\infty}\!F_{z}^{2}\big(1+(z-1)y,t\big)\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dy=2\bigg(\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\!F_{z}^{2}(z,t)\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, using the above inequality in (
4.52
) and rewriting in
f(x,t)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{f^{2}}{r^{2n}}\,dx\leq C\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{2}\}\int_{1}^{\infty}\!\!F_{z}^{2}(z,t)\,dz=C\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{2}\}\int_{S(t)}\!f_{x}^{2}(x,t)\,dx.

This concludes the proof.

\square

Lemma 4.4

(Relative Energy Estimate)
.

Let
(v,u)
solve (
4.41
) in
\mathcal{Q}(T)
with
T>0
such that (
4.49
) holds. Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
such that if
u_{b}<\varepsilon
then

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\int_{S(t)}\!\!\mathcal{E}[v,u]\,dx+\mu\!\!\int_{S(t)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}}{v}+\dfrac{(n-1)v\psi^{2}}{2r^{2}}\Big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\tilde{A}_{1}(\rho_{+})\Big\{u_{b}^{2}+|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|^{2}}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\phi_{x}^{2}\,dx,

where

\tilde{A}_{1}(\rho_{+})\vcentcolon=\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{2}\}\cdot\max\{\rho_{+}^{2\gamma+3},\rho_{+}^{4\gamma-1},\rho_{+}^{2\gamma},\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}\}.

Proof.

Taking temporal derivative on
\mathcal{E}
, it follows from equations (
4.44
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[v,u]_{t}+\mu\dfrac{(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}^{2}}{v}+\Big\{r^{n-1}\psi\Big(p(v)-p(\tilde{v})-\dfrac{(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}}{v}\Big)\Big\}_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\gamma-1)m_{b}\dfrac{\tilde{v}_{r}}{r^{n-1}}G[v,\tilde{v}]-\tilde{u}_{r}\psi^{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi\phi,

where
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(r)
is given in (
4.43
). Integrating the above in
x\in[-m_{b}t,N]
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows from the boundary condition (
4.45
) and
the
Leibniz integral rule that

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{-m_{b}t}^{N}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}[v,u]\,dx+\mu\int_{-m_{b}t}^{N}\!\!\dfrac{(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}^{2}}{v}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\Big\{r^{n-1}\psi\Big(\dfrac{(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}}{v}-p(\tilde{v})+p(v)\Big)\Big\}\Big|_{x=N}

\displaystyle-\int_{-m_{b}t}^{N}\!\!\Big\{(\gamma-1)m_{b}\dfrac{\tilde{v}_{r}}{r^{n-1}}G[v,\tilde{v}]+\tilde{u}_{r}\psi^{2}-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi\phi\Big\}\,dx.

(4.53)

From Lemma
4.1
, we get that
r^{n-1}\psi(\cdot,t)\in H^{1}(S(t))
uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
. Thus
r^{n-1}\psi(x,t)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
for each
t\in[0,T]
. Also,
r^{n-1}\psi_{x}\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Q}(T))
. Consequently, we obtain the limit

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\!\Big\{r^{n-1}\psi\Big(\dfrac{(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}}{v}-p(\tilde{v})+p(v)\Big)\Big\}\Big|_{x=N}=0.

Taking limit
N\to\infty
in (
4.53
) and rewriting
(r^{n-1}\psi)_{x}^{2}
using the relation (
4.39
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\mathcal{E}[v,u]\,dx+\mu\int_{S(t)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}}{v}+(n-1)\dfrac{v\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big\}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S(t)}\!\!\Big\{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\psi\phi-(\gamma-1)m_{b}\dfrac{\tilde{v}_{r}}{r^{n-1}}G[v,\tilde{v}]-\tilde{u}_{r}\psi^{2}\Big\}\,dx=\vcentcolon\textrm{(I)}+\textrm{(II)}+\textrm{(III)}.

(4.54)

By Lemma
2.1
, it is verified that

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(r)|\leq

\displaystyle Cr^{-2n+1}\rho_{+}^{\gamma+1}\Big\{\rho_{+}^{\gamma-2}\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|}{u_{b}}+u_{b}\Big\}.

Using this, (
4.49
) and Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(I)}|\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{(n-1)\mu}{4}\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{v\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{(n-1)\mu}\int_{S(t)}|\tilde{\mathcal{L}}|^{2}\dfrac{r^{2}\phi^{2}}{v}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{(n-1)\mu}{4}\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{v\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx+C\rho_{+}^{2\gamma+3}\Big\{u_{b}^{2}+\rho_{+}^{2\gamma-4}\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|^{2}}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}\int_{S(t)}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r^{4n-2}}\,dx.

Using (
4.51
) and the decay estimate (
2.12
) we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(II)}|=

\displaystyle\Big|(\gamma-1)m_{b}\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{\tilde{v}_{r}}{r^{n-1}}G[v,\tilde{v}]\,dx\Big|\leq C\rho_{+}^{2\gamma}\big\{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\rho_{+}^{2-\gamma}u_{b}^{2}\big\}\int_{S(t)}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r^{3n-2}}\,dx.

Using the estimate
|\tilde{u}_{r}(r)|\leq Cu_{b}r^{-n}
from Lemma
2.1
and (
4.49
), we get

\displaystyle|\textrm{(III)}|=\Big|\int_{S(t)}\tilde{u}_{r}\psi^{2}\,dx\Big|\leq 2C\rho_{+}u_{b}\iint_{S(t)}\dfrac{v\psi^{2}}{r^{n}}\,dx.

By choosing the upper bound for
u_{b}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\textrm{(III)}|\leq\dfrac{(n-1)\mu}{4}\int_{S(t)}\dfrac{v\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}\,dx\qquad\text{if }\ u_{b}\leq\dfrac{(n-1)\mu}{8C\rho_{+}}.

Substituting the estimates for
\textrm{(I)}-\textrm{(III)}
into (
4.54
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!\mathcal{E}[v,u](x,\tau)\,dx+\mu\int_{S(t)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}}{v}+\dfrac{n-1}{2}\dfrac{v\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\max\{\rho_{+}^{2\gamma+3},\rho_{+}^{4\gamma-1},\rho_{+}^{2\gamma},\rho_{+}^{\gamma+2}\}\Big\{u_{b}^{2}+|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|^{2}}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}\int_{S(t)}\frac{\phi^{2}}{r^{3n-2}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CA_{1}(\rho_{+})\Big\{u_{b}^{2}+|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|^{2}}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\phi_{x}^{2}\,dx,

where in the last line we used the fact that
2n\leq 3n-2
and Proposition
4.3
.

\square

4.3
H^{1}
-estimates of
\phi

We define the term

\mathcal{F}:=\mu\dfrac{\phi_{x}}{v}-\frac{\psi}{r^{n-1}}.

Then from equations (
1.8
) and (
4.41
), it is verified that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies

\mathcal{F}_{t}+\frac{\gamma K}{\mu}\dfrac{\mathcal{F}}{v^{\gamma}}=(n-1)\frac{\psi^{2}}{r^{n}}-\gamma\frac{p(v)-p(\tilde{v})}{v-\tilde{v}}\frac{\tilde{v}_{r}}{r^{n-1}}\phi+q\frac{\psi}{r^{n-1}},

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\psi_{x}^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{\mu}{2}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!\!\frac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\,dx\leq CA_{2}(\rho_{+})\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}\,dx+C(\rho_{+})\mathcal{D}(t).

Multiplying the second inequality by
\big(2CA_{2}\big)^{-1}\omega
and adding it to the first, we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\Big\{\mathcal{F}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2CA_{2}}\psi_{x}^{2}\Big\}\,dx+\int_{S(t)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{\omega}{2}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}+\dfrac{\mu\omega}{4CA_{2}}\dfrac{}{}\dfrac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\mu^{2}\rho_{b}}{u_{b}}\psi_{x}^{2}(-m_{b}t,t)+C(\rho_{+})\mathcal{D}(t).

(4.66)

Applying (
4.49
) and Proposition
4.7
with
\epsilon=(8C\mu\rho_{b}A_{2})^{-1}u_{b}\omega
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{x}^{2}(-m_{b}t,t)\leq

\displaystyle C\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{-1}\}\Big(1+\frac{\rho_{+}A_{2}}{u_{b}\omega}\Big)\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!\!\dfrac{r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}}{v}dx+\dfrac{u_{b}\omega}{8C\mu\rho_{b}A_{2}}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!\!\dfrac{r^{4n-6}\psi_{xx}^{2}}{v}dx.

Recalling the definition of
A_{2}
in (
4.63
) and
\omega
in (
4.59
), we obtain that

\dfrac{\mu^{2}\rho_{b}}{u_{b}}\psi_{x}^{2}(-m_{b}t,t)\leq\dfrac{\mu\omega}{8CA_{2}}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!\!\dfrac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\,dx+C(\rho_{+})\frac{\mathcal{D}(t)}{u_{b}}+C\frac{A_{3}(\rho_{+})}{u_{b}^{2}}\mathcal{D}(t),

(4.67)

where

A_{3}(\rho_{+})\vcentcolon=\max\{1,\rho_{+}\}\frac{\rho_{+}A_{2}(\rho_{+})}{\omega(\rho_{+})}=\frac{2^{\gamma}\mu}{\gamma K}\rho_{+}^{\gamma}\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{3}\}.

(4.68)

Substituting this in (
4.66
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\Big\{\mathcal{F}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2CA_{2}}\psi_{x}^{2}\Big\}\,dx+\int_{S(t)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{\omega}{2}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}+\dfrac{\mu\omega}{8CA_{2}}\dfrac{}{}\dfrac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big\}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{A_{3}(\rho_{+})}{u_{b}^{2}}\mathcal{D}(t)+C(\rho_{+})\Big\{1+\frac{1}{u_{b}}\Big\}\mathcal{D}(t).

Integrating the above in time and using Corollary
4.5
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\Big\{\mathcal{F}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2CA_{2}}\psi_{x}^{2}\Big\}\,dx\bigg|_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t}+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{\omega}{2}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}+\dfrac{\mu\omega}{8CA_{2}}\dfrac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big\}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{A_{3}(\rho_{+})}{u_{b}^{2}}\cdot A_{1}(\rho_{+})\Big\{u_{b}^{2}+|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|^{2}}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}dxd\tau

\displaystyle+C(\rho_{+})\Big\{1+\dfrac{1}{u_{b}}\Big\}\Big\{u_{b}^{2}+|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|+\frac{|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|^{2}}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}dxd\tau.

First, set
|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|\leq u_{b}^{2}
. Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
such that if
u_{b}+|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|\leq\varepsilon
, then

\displaystyle\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\Big\{\mathcal{F}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2CA_{2}}\psi_{x}^{2}\Big\}\,dx\bigg|_{\tau=0}^{\tau=t}+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{\omega}{2}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}+\dfrac{\mu\omega}{8CA_{2}}\dfrac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big\}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CA_{3}(\rho_{+})A_{1}(\rho_{+})\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}dxd\tau+\frac{\omega(\rho_{+})}{8}\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}dxd\tau.

(4.69)

We emphasize that the constant
C=C(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
appearing in the above inequality is independent of
\rho_{+}
. By the definition of
A_{1}
in (
4.58
) and
A_{3}
in (
4.68
),

\displaystyle A_{3}(\rho_{+})A_{1}(\rho_{+})=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\gamma}\mu}{\gamma K}\max\{1,\rho_{+}^{5}\}\cdot\max\{\rho_{+}^{3\gamma+1},\rho_{+}^{5\gamma-3},\rho_{+}^{3\gamma-2},\rho_{+}^{2\gamma}\}.

Since
\gamma>1
, the above expression implies that there is a constant
\alpha=\alpha(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
which is independent of
\rho_{+}
such that if
\rho_{+}\in(0,\alpha]
then

CA_{3}(\rho_{+})A_{1}(\rho_{+})\leq\frac{\gamma K}{2^{\gamma+3}\mu}\rho_{+}^{\gamma}=\frac{\omega(\rho_{+})}{8}.

Substituting the above into (
4.69
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\Big\{\mathcal{F}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2CA_{2}}\psi_{x}^{2}\Big\}\,dx\bigg|_{\tau=t}+\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!\Big\{\dfrac{\omega}{4}r^{2(n-2)}\mathcal{F}^{2}+\dfrac{\mu\omega}{8CA_{2}}\dfrac{r^{4n-6}}{v}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big\}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S(\tau)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\Big\{\mathcal{F}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2CA_{2}}\psi_{x}^{2}\Big\}\,dx\bigg|_{\tau=0}\leq C(\rho_{+})N^{2}(0).

(4.70)

Using
\mathcal{F}\vcentcolon=\mu v^{-1}\phi_{x}-r^{1-n}\psi
, we apply triangular inequality in (
4.70
) to get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-2)}\big\{\phi_{x}^{2}+\psi_{x}^{2}\big\}\,dx+\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(T)}\!\!\Big(r^{2(n-2)}\phi_{x}^{2}+r^{4n-6}\psi_{xx}^{2}\Big)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{+})N^{2}(0).

Moreover, combining the above estimate with Corollary
4.5
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\mathcal{E}[v,u]\,dx+\mu\!\!\iint_{\mathcal{Q}(T)}\!\!\Big\{r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}+\dfrac{\psi^{2}}{r^{2}}\Big\}\,dx\leq C(\rho_{+})N^{2}(0).

(4.71)

Finally the estimate for
m_{b}\int_{0}^{T}\psi_{x}^{2}(-m_{b}t,t)dt
is obtained by integrating (
4.67
) in
t\in[0,T]
and substituting (
4.70
)–(
4.71
) in the resultant inequality.

\square

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose
(\phi,\psi)
is a solution to (
4.44
) in
\mathcal{Q}(T)
for
T>0
such that (
4.49
) holds. Then there exist constants
\alpha=\alpha(\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
which is independent of
\rho_{+}
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\rho_{+},\mu,\gamma,K,n)>0
, such that if
\rho_{+}\in(0,\alpha]
,
|\rho_{b}-\rho_{+}|\leq u_{b}^{2}
and
u_{b}<\varepsilon
, then

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-1)}\big\{\phi_{x}^{2}+\psi_{x}^{2}\big\}(x,t)\,dx\leq C(\rho_{+})\Big\{1+\frac{1}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}N^{2}(0).

Proof.

Taking
k=2(n-1)
in Lemmas
4.6
, it follows from (
4.49
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-1)}\mathcal{F}^{2}\,dx+\omega(\rho_{+})\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-1)}\mathcal{F}^{2}\,dx\\
\leq&C(\rho_{+})\int_{S(t)}\!\!\psi^{2}\,dx+C(\rho_{+})\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(t)\leq CN^{2}(0)+C\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(t),\end{aligned}

where
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(t)\vcentcolon=\mathcal{D}(t)+\frac{\rho_{b}}{u_{b}}\psi_{x}^{2}(-m_{b}t,t)
. It follows from Lemma
4.10
that

u_{b}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\!\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(t)\,dt\leq C(\rho_{+})N^{2}(0).

Thus applying Grönwall’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-1)}\mathcal{F}^{2}\,dx\leq C(\rho_{+})\Big\{1+\dfrac{1}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}N^{2}(0).

By (
4.71
) and triangular inequality, we get

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-1)}\phi_{x}^{2}\,dx\leq C(\rho_{+})\Big\{1+\dfrac{1}{u_{b}^{2}}\Big\}N^{2}(0).

In addition, combining Lemma
4.9
with
k=2(n-1)
and Corollary
4.5
gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!\big(\mathcal{E}[v,u]+r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}\big)\,dx+C(\rho_{+})^{-1}\int_{S(t)}\!\!\!\big(\mathcal{E}[v,u]+r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho_{+})\bigg\{\int_{S(t)}\!\!\mathcal{E}[v,u]\,dx+\int_{S(t)}r^{2(n-1)}\mathcal{F}^{2}\,dx+\mathcal{D}(t)\bigg\}\leq C(\rho_{+})\big\{N^{2}(0)+\mathcal{D}(t)\big\},

Therefore by the Grönwall lemma used previously, we also obtain

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{S(t)}\!\!r^{2(n-1)}\psi_{x}^{2}\,dx\leq C(\rho_{+})N^{2}(0).

This concludes the proof and the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

\square

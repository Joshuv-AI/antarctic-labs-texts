Logarithmic Sobolev and interpolation inequalities on the sphere: constructive stability results
Giovanni Brigati, Jean Dolbeault, Nikita Simonov

Abstract
. We consider Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities on the sphere which interpolate between the Poincaré inequality and the Sobolev inequality, and include the logarithmic Sobolev inequality as a special case. We establish explicit stability results in the subcritical regime using spectral decomposition techniques, and entropy and carré du champ methods applied to nonlinear diffusion flows.

Introduction and main results

Functional inequalities are essential in many areas of mathematics. The knowledge of optimal constants, or at least good estimates of them, is crucial for various applications. Whether optimality cases are achieved is a standard issue in analysis. The next natural question is to understand how the deficit, say the difference of the two sides of the functional inequality, measures the distance to the set of optimal functions. Such a question has been actively studied in critical Sobolev inequalities, but much less in subcritical interpolation inequalities. In the case of the sphere, a global stability result based on Bianchi–Egnell-type methods was recently obtained for a family of Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities by Frank in
[
32
]
, with the striking observation that only the power
4
of a natural distance is controlled by the deficit. Here we give a more detailed picture, which includes the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, and provide explicit estimates.

On the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
, the
logarithmic Sobolev inequality
can be written as

(LS)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\geq\frac{d}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\,,

where
d\mu
denotes the uniform probability measure. The equality case is achieved by constant functions and
d/2
is the optimal constant as shown by taking the test functions
F_{\varepsilon}(x)=1+\varepsilon\,x\cdot\nu
, for some arbitrary
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Our first result is an improved inequality under an orthogonality constraint, which improves upon
[
23
, Proposition 5.4]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
d\geq 1
. For any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
such that

(1.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{x\,F}\,d\mu=0\,,

we have

(1.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\geq\mathscr{C}_{d}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\,,

with
\mathscr{C}_{d}=\frac{2}{d+2}
.

Since equality in (
LS
) is achieved if and only if
F
is a constant function, the right-hand side in (
1.2
) is an estimate of the distance to the set of optimal functions under the constraint
\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{x\,F}\,d\mu=0
. Alternatively, Theorem
1
amounts to the
improved logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\geq\frac{d+2}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\;\mbox{s.t.}\;\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{x\,F}\,d\mu=0\,.

Without condition (
1.1
), there is no such inequality as (
1.2
). With
F_{\varepsilon}(x)=1+\varepsilon\,x\cdot\nu
as above, as
\varepsilon\to 0
one can indeed check that

\left\|\nabla F_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\frac{d}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{\left\|F_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu=O(\varepsilon^{4})=O\left(\left\|\nabla F_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}\right)\,.

In the absence of an additional constraint, like (
1.1
), such behaviour is in fact optimal. The following estimate arises from the
carré du champ
method.

Proposition 2
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\gamma=1/3
if
d=1
and
\gamma=(4\,d-1)\,(d-1)^{2}/(d+2)^{2}
if
d\geq 2
. Then, for any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\gamma\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}}{\gamma\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+d\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\,.

With
\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=1
, notice that the deficit can be estimated from below by

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(F^{2}\right)}\,d\mu\geq\frac{\gamma}{2\,d}\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}+o\left(\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}\right)

if
\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}
is small enough.

Let
\Pi_{1}F
denote the orthogonal projection of a function
F\in\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})
on the spherical harmonics corresponding to the first positive eigenvalue of
-\,\Delta
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
,
i.e.
,

\Pi_{1}F(x)=(d+1)\,x\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}y\,F(y)\,d\mu(y)\quad\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}\,.

Our main stability result for the logarithmic Sobolev inequality combines the results of Theorem
1
and Proposition
2
as follows.

Theorem 3
.

Let
d\geq 1
. For any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\\
\geq\mathscr{S}_{d}\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{d}{2}\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+\left\|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\,F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)

for some stability constant
\mathscr{S}_{d}>0
.

An explicit estimate of
\mathscr{S}_{d}
is given in Section
4
.

We also consider the subcritical
Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities

(GN)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\geq\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\,,

for any
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
. Here,
d\mu
again denotes the uniform probability measure on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, the critical Sobolev exponent is
2^{*}:=2\,d/(d-2)
if
d\geq 3
and we adopt the convention that
2^{*}=+\infty
if
d=1
or
d=2
. Inequality (
GN
) with
p=1
is equivalent to the Poincaré inequality. If
d\geq 3
, inequality (
GN
) also holds for the critical exponent
p=2^{*}
and it is in fact Sobolev’s inequality with optimal constant on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, but this is out of the scope of our paper which focuses on the subcritical regime
p<2^{*}
. The logarithmic Sobolev inequality (
LS
) is obtained from (
GN
) by taking the limit as
p\to 2
, and the counterpart of the above results for
p\neq 2
, in the
subcritical range
p<2^{*}
, goes as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Assume that
d\geq 1
and
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
. For any function
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
such that the orthogonality condition (
1.1
) holds, we have

(1.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\geq\mathscr{C}_{d,p}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu

with
\mathscr{C}_{d,p}=\frac{2\,d-p\,(d-2)}{2\,(d+p)}
.

Taking
F_{\varepsilon}(x)=1+\varepsilon\,x\cdot\nu
as above shows that (
1.1
) is needed in Theorem
4
. We also have a higher-order estimate of the deficit as a consequence of the
carré du champ
method.

Proposition 5
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
. There is a convex function
\psi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
with
\psi(0)=\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
such that, for any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\geq\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\psi\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\,.

An explicit expression for
\psi
will be given in Section
3
. The two results of Theorem
4
and Proposition
5
can be combined to prove the analogue of Theorem
3
for
p\neq 2
, with an explicit constant: see Section
4
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
. For any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-\frac{d}{p-2}\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\\
\geq\mathscr{S}_{d,p}\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+\left\|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\,F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)

for some explicit stability constant
\mathscr{S}_{d,p}>0
.

Let us give a brief account of the literature. In this paper, we address the distinction between
improved inequalities
(inequalities with improved constants under orthogonality constraints) and
quantitative stability
(as a measure of a distance to the set of optimal functions). There are many adjacent directions of research like, for instance, stability in weaker norms (see for instance
[
25
,
40
]
for Sobolev’s inequality) or notions of stability with no explicit notion of distance. To our knowledge, not so much has been done in subcritical interpolation inequalities (see
[
11
,
32
]
and some references therein), except for the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, for which we refer to
[
28
,
30
]
and
[
39
,
31
,
38
,
41
]
.

The
Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities
(
GN
) on the sphere have been established with optimal constant for any
p\in(2,2^{*})
in
[
10
, Corollary 6.1]
and in
[
7
]
. In dimension
d=2
, Onofri’s inequality is obtained from (
GN
) in the limit as
p\to 2^{*}=+\infty
: see
[
7
,
13
]
. With
p\in[1,2)
or
p>2
but not too large (if
d\geq 2
), inequality (
GN
) was known earlier from
[
3
]
. A Markovian point of view is presented in
[
5
]
, with many more references therein on related questions. On Euclidean space, similar inequalities go back to
[
51
,
34
,
49
]
. The
logarithmic Sobolev inequality
(
LS
) is a well known-limit case as
p\to 2
and can be considered in a common framework with (
GN
). Whenever possible, we shall adopt this point of view. For an overview of early results on the sphere, we refer to
[
36
, Section 6, (iv)]
. The literature on (
LS
) on the circle and on the sphere can be traced back at least to
[
56
]
,
[
46
, Theorem 1, page 268]
with computations based on the ultraspherical operator, and
[
50
]
for a more variational approach. The inequality with optimal constant is stated in
[
3
, inequality (13) page 195]
as a consequence of the
carré du champ
method. Also see
[
4
]
and
[
14
, page 342]
for related results and
[
18
,
22
,
23
]
for a PDE approach based on entropy estimates and the
carré du champ
method. After Schwarz foliated symmetrization, the problem is reduced to a simpler family of interpolation inequalities involving only the ultraspherical operator.

The interest for stability issues was raised by
[
12
]
and the stability result of Bianchi and Egnell in
[
9
]
, on Euclidean space. Over the years, various approaches have been developed, based on compactness methods and contradiction arguments as in
[
9
,
16
]
, spectral analysis and orthogonality conditions as in
[
23
, Proposition 5.4]
and
[
37
]
, or entropy methods and improved inequalities as in
[
2
,
27
,
21
,
23
,
19
]
. For spectral methods, a fruitful strategy relies on the Funk–Hecke formula, which is behind (
2.2
), and the approach of
[
44
,
7
]
, which applies to the stability result for fractional interpolation inequalities of
[
16
]
and
[
29
, Corollary 2.3]
. This is the method we use in Section
2
. Stability issues for (
GN
) have recently been discussed in
[
32
]
with methods of Bianchi–Egnell-type, with the drawback that no estimate of the stability constant is known. This drawback can be cured by a
carré du champ
method as we shall see in Section
3
. Without entering into details, let us mention some recent progress on stability in
[
11
,
20
,
42
,
15
]
for related critical inequalities.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is devoted to the proof by spectral methods of Theorem
7
(see below), which is an extension of Theorems
1
and
4
: under orthogonality constraints, these results are reduced to estimates of improved constants in inequalities (
LS
) and (
GN
), with various refinements based on a decomposition in spherical harmonics. An explicit stability result without constraints corresponding to Propositions
2
and
5
is proved in Section
3
. The proofs of Theorems
3
and
6
, in Section
4
, is based on the spectral decomposition method developed by Frank in
[

]
. We collect the previous estimates (with and without orthogonality constraints) in global results, with explicit constants. Various additional results are stated in two appendices: the extension of the method to interpolation inequalities for the Gaussian measure on Euclidean space and a discussion of its limitations in Appendix
A
, the details of the computations of the
carré du champ
method on the sphere and its application in order to establish improved functional inequalities in Appendix
B
.

2.
Improvements under orthogonality constraints

In this section, we prove Theorems
1
and
4
in the slightly more general framework of Theorem
7
below. Let us consider the generalized entropy functionals

\mathcal{E}_{2}[F]:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]:=\frac{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{p-2}\quad\kern-6.0pt\mbox{if}\quad\kern-6.0ptp\neq 2\,.

With this notation, we can rephrase (
LS
) and (
GN
) as

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\geq d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\,,

for any
p\in[1,2^{*})
. The optimality case is achieved by considering the test function
F_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon\,\varphi_{1}
in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
\varphi_{1}
is an eigenfunction of the Laplace–Beltrami operator such that
-\,\Delta\varphi_{1}=d\,\varphi_{1}
, for instance
\varphi_{1}(x)=x\cdot\nu
for some
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
as in Section
1
.

Let us consider the decomposition into spherical harmonics of
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
,

\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\,,

where
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
is the subspace of spherical harmonics of degree
\ell\geq 0
. See for instance
[
47
,
52
,
8
,
45
]
. For any integer
k\geq 1
, let us define
\Pi_{k}
as the orthogonal projection with respect to
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
onto
\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}
. The following statement extends Theorems
1
and
4
.

Theorem 7
.

Assume that
d\geq 1
,
p\in(1,2^{*})
and let
k\geq 1
be an integer. For any function
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
, we have

(2.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\big|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{k})\,F\big|^{2}}\,d\mu

for some explicit constant
\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}\in(0,1)
such that
\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}\leq\mathscr{C}_{d,p,1}=\frac{2\,d-p\,(d-2)}{2\,(d+p)}
.

The expression for
\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}
is given below in the proof. inequality (
2.1
) can be seen as an improvement of (
LS
) and (
GN
), namely

(1-\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k})\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\geq d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]

for any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
such that
\Pi_{k}F=0
. With
k=1
, this establishes (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), thus proving Theorem
1
if
p=2
, and Theorem
4
if
p\neq 2
.

Proof of Theorem
7
.

Let
(F_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be the decomposition of
F
along
\mathcal{H}_{j}
for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We learn from
[
7
, Ineq. (19)]
or
[
29
, Ineq. (1.6)]
that the
subcritical interpolation inequalities

(2.2)

\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{j}(p)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|F_{j}|^{2}}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)

hold for any
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
with

\zeta_{j}(p):=\frac{\gamma_{j}\big(\tfrac{d}{p}\big)-1}{p-2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma_{j}(x):=\frac{\Gamma(x)\,\Gamma(j+d-x)}{\Gamma(d-x)\,\Gamma(x+j)}\,.

This result is based on the Funk–Hecke theorem (see for instance
[
33
, Section 4]
) and Lieb’s ideas in
[
44
]
. Notice that
\zeta_{j}(p)\geq 0
for any
p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})
. According to
[
29
, Lemma 2.2]
, the function
\zeta_{j}
is strictly monotone increasing on
(1,\infty)
for any
j\geq 2
and the limits

\lambda_{j}=d\,\lim_{p\to 2^{*}}\zeta_{j}(p)

are the eigenvalues of the Laplace–Beltrami on the sphere, with
\lambda_{j}=j\,(j+d-1)
. Hence

d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|F_{j}|^{2}}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\,,

which is the essence of the proof of (
GN
) in
[
7
]
and also the main idea for the proof of the stability result for fractional interpolation inequalities of
[
29
, Corollary 2.3]
. Here we draw some consequences in standard norms for nonfractional operators and identify estimates of the stability constant in the corresponding stability result.

\rhd

The case
p\neq 2
.
Let
x=d/p\in((d-2)/2,d]
if
d\geq 2
and
x\in(0,d]
if
d=1
. We consider

\xi_{j}(x):=\frac{|\gamma_{j}(x)-1|}{j\,(j+d-1)}\,\quad\mbox{and}\quad h_{j}(x)=\frac{j\,(j+d-1)\,(j+d-x)}{(j+1)\,(j+d)\,(j+x)}\,;

notice that
\gamma_{j}(x)>1
for
x<d/2
, while
\gamma_{j}(x)<1
for
x>d/2
. An elementary computation shows that
0<h_{j}(x)<1
. Since
\gamma_{j+1}(x)\,\lambda_{\kappa}=h_{j}(x)\,\lambda_{j+1}\,\gamma_{j}(x)
, we obtain

(2.3)

\xi_{j+1}(x)=h_{j}(x)\,\xi_{j}(x)+(1-h_{j}(x))\,\xi_{j}^{\star}(x)\,,

where

\xi_{j}^{\star}(x):=\frac{1}{1-h_{j}(x)}\left|\frac{h_{j}(x)}{\lambda_{j}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{j+1}}\right|=\frac{|d-2\,x|}{j\,(j+d)\left(2\,x-d+2\right)+d\,x}\,.

Notice that
(\xi_{j}^{\star}(x))_{j\geq 2}
is a monotone decreasing sequence for any fixed, admissible value of
x
. We start at
j=2
with the observation that
\xi_{2}^{\star}(x)<\xi_{2}(x)
if
x
is admissible. This gives, by using (
2.3
), the following estimate

\xi_{3}(x)=h_{2}(x)\,\xi_{2}(x)+\big(1-h_{2}(x)\big)\,\xi_{2}^{\star}(x)<\xi_{2}(x)\,.

Using
\xi_{3}^{\star}(x)<\xi_{2}^{\star}(x)
, we can iterate and conclude by induction that
\xi_{j}(x)<\xi_{2}(x)
for all
j\geq 3
. As a consequence, we obtain

\sup_{j\geq 3}\frac{\zeta_{j}(p)}{j\,(j+d-1)}<\frac{\zeta_{2}(p)}{2\,(d+1)}=\frac{p}{2\,(d+p)}<\frac{1}{d}\quad\forall\,p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})\,.

We deduce from (
2.2
) that

\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|F_{1}|^{2}}\,d\mu+\frac{p}{2\,(d+p)}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}j\,(j+d-1)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|F_{j}|^{2}}\,d\mu\\
=\frac{1}{d}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu+\frac{2\,d-p\,(d-2)}{2\,d\,(d+p)}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\,F|^{2}}\,d\mu\,,

which proves the result with
k=1
and gives the expression for
\mathscr{C}_{d,p,1}
.

Let us consider the case
k>1
. We already know that
\xi_{2}(x)>\xi_{2}^{\star}(x)
. For any
j\geq 2
, we deduce from (
2.3
) that

\xi_{j+1}(x)-\xi_{j+1}^{\star}(x)=h_{j}(x)\,\big(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}^{\star}(x)\big)+\xi_{j}^{\star}(x)-\xi_{j+1}^{\star}(x)\geq h_{j}(x)\,\big(\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}^{\star}(x)\big)

because
j\mapsto\xi_{j}^{\star}(x)
is monotone decreasing. By induction, this proves that
\xi_{j}(x)>\xi_{j}^{\star}(x)
for any
j\geq 2
. As a consequence of (
2.3
),
j\mapsto\xi_{j}(x)
is also monotone decreasing and

\sup_{j\geq k+2}\frac{\zeta_{j}(p)}{j\,(j+d-1)}<\frac{\zeta_{k+1}(p)}{(k+1)\,(k+d)}<\frac{1}{d}\quad\forall\,p\in(1,2)\cup(2,2^{*})\,.

Altogether, for any
k\geq 1
, we have

d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla\Pi_{k}F|^{2}}\,d\mu+\frac{d\,\zeta_{k+1}(p)}{(k+1)\,(k+d)}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu\,,

and the stability constant in (
2.1
) is estimated by

\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}=1-\frac{d\,\zeta_{k+1}(p)}{(k+1)\,(k+d)}\,.

In our method, this constant cannot be improved as shown by a test function such that
F_{j}=0
for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
j\neq 0
and
j\neq k+1
, but this does not prove the optimality of
\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}
.

\rhd

The case
p=2
.
By taking the limit as
p\to 2_{+}
in (
2.2
), we obtain that

\eta_{j}:=\frac{2}{d}\lim_{p\to 2_{+}}\zeta_{j}(p)=\,\psi(j+d/2)-\psi(d/2)\,,

where
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
is the
digamma function
, and

\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{F^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)}\,d\mu\leq\frac{d}{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\eta_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|F_{j}|^{2}}\,d\mu\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\,.

From
\psi(z+1)=\psi(z)+1/z
obtained by differentiating the identity
\Gamma(z+1)=z\,\Gamma(z)
with respect to
z
, we learn that

\eta_{j+1}=\eta_{j}+\frac{2}{d+2\,j}\,.

We claim that

\eta_{2}\leq\eta_{j}\leq\frac{2\,\lambda_{j}}{d\,(d+2)}\quad\forall\,j\geq 2

because there is equality for
j=2
as
\eta_{2}=\frac{4\,(d+1)}{d\,(d+2)}
and
\lambda_{2}=2\,(d+1)
on the one hand, and

\eta_{j+1}-\eta_{j}=\frac{2}{d+2\,j}\leq\frac{2\,(d+2\,j)}{d\,(d+2)}=\frac{2\,(\lambda_{j+1}-\lambda_{j})}{d\,(d+2)}

on the other hand, so that the result follows by induction.

Using
\lambda_{j+1}=\lambda_{j}+(d+2\,j)=\lambda_{j}+2\,z_{j}
where
z_{j}:=j+d/2
, we also have

\frac{\eta_{j+1}}{\lambda_{j+1}}=\frac{\eta_{j}+\frac{1}{z_{j}}}{\lambda_{j}+2\,z_{j}}<\frac{\eta_{j}}{\lambda_{j}}\,,

where the inequality follows from

z_{j}^{2}>\frac{\lambda_{j}}{2\,\eta_{j}}\quad\forall\,j\geq 1\,.

This inequality is indeed true for
j=1
because
\eta_{1}=2/d
and we obtain the result for any
j\geq 1
by induction using

\eta_{j+1}-\eta_{j}=\frac{2}{d+2\,j}\geq\frac{\lambda_{j+1}}{2\,z_{j+1}^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{j}}{2\,z_{j}^{2}}=2\,\frac{4\,j^{2}+2\,(d^{2}+2)\,j+d^{3}}{(d+2\,j)^{2}\,(d+2+2\,j)^{2}}\,.

Altogether, for any
k\geq 1
, we have

d\,\mathcal{E}_{2}[F]\leq\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla\Pi_{k}F|^{2}}\,d\mu+\frac{d\,\eta_{k+1}}{(k+1)\,(k+d)}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu

and the constant in (
2.1
) is given by

\mathscr{C}_{d,2,k}=1-\frac{d\,\eta_{k+1}}{(k+1)\,(k+d)}\,.

In the framework of our method, this estimate of the constant cannot be improved as shown by a test function such that
F_{j}=0
for any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
j\neq 0
and
j\neq k+1
, but again this does not prove the optimality of
\mathscr{C}_{d,p,k}
.∎

3.
Improvements by the
carré du champ
method

We improve upon Frank’s stability result in
[
32
]
by giving a constructive estimate based on the
carré du champ
method, without assuming any additional constraint. Various computations that are needed for a complete proof, most of them already known in the literature, are collected in Appendix
B
.

3.1.
A simple estimate based on the heat flow, below the Bakry–Emery exponent

Let us consider the constant
\gamma
given by

(3.1)

\gamma:=\left(\frac{d-1}{d+2}\right)^{2}\,(p-1)\,(2^{\#}-p)\quad\mbox{if}\quad d\geq 2\,,\quad\gamma:=\frac{p-1}{3}\quad\mbox{if}\quad d=1\,,

where
2^{\#}:=\frac{2\,d^{2}+1}{(d-1)^{2}}
is the
Bakry–Emery exponent
. Notice that
\gamma=2-p
with
1\leq p\leq 2^{\#}
means that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad p=7/4=p_{*}(1)\,,\\
\displaystyle d>1\quad\mbox{and}\quad p=p_{*}(d):=\displaystyle\frac{3+d+2\,d^{2}-2\,\sqrt{4\,d+4\,d^{2}+d^{3}}}{(d-1)^{2}}\,.\end{array}

Let us define

(3.2)

s_{\star}:=\frac{1}{p-2}\quad\mbox{if}\quad p>2\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{\star}:=+\infty\quad\mbox{if}\quad p\leq 2\,.

For any
s\in[0,s_{\star})
, let

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi(s)=\frac{1-(p-2)\,s-\left(1-(p-2)\,s\right)^{-\frac{\gamma}{p-2}}}{2-p-\gamma}&\mbox{if}\quad\gamma\neq 2-p\quad\mbox{and}\quad p\neq 2\,,\\[4.0pt]
\varphi(s)=\frac{1}{2-p}\left(1+(2-p)\,s\right)\log\left(1+(2-p)\,s\right)&\mbox{if}\quad\gamma=2-p\neq 0\,,\\[4.0pt]
\varphi(s)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\left(e^{\gamma\,s}-1\right)&\mbox{if}\quad p=2\,.\end{array}

In
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
(also see
[
21
]
and earlier related references therein) the
improved Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities

(3.4)

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq d\,\varphi\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})

are stated with
\gamma
given by (
3.1
) under the conditions

d\geq 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1\leq p\leq 2^{\#}\quad\mbox{if}\quad d\geq 2\,,\quad p\geq 1\quad\mbox{if}\quad d=1\,.

Why this estimate is based on the heat flow is explained in Appendix
B
. Additional justifications and discussion of the case
p=2
are also given in Appendix
B
.

Since
\varphi(0)=0
,
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=1
, and
\varphi
is convex increasing, with an asymptote at
s=s_{\star}
if
p\in(2,2^{\#})
, we know that
\varphi:[0,s_{\star})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is invertible and
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to[0,s_{\star})
,
s\mapsto\psi(s):=s-\varphi^{-1}(s)
, is convex increasing with
\psi(0)=\psi^{\prime}(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\big(t-\psi(t)\big)=s_{\star}
, and

\psi^{\prime\prime}(0)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{(d+2)^{2}}\left(2^{\#}-p\right)(p-1)>0\quad\forall\,p\in(1,2^{\#})\,.

Proposition 8
.

With the above notation,
d\geq 1
and
p\in(1,2^{\#})
, we have

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq d\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\psi\left(\frac{1}{d}\,\frac{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})\,.

If
p=2
, notice that
\psi
is explicit and given by

\psi(t):=t-\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\log(1+\gamma\,t)\quad\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

The proof of Proposition
2
follows from the observation that
\psi(t)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\,\frac{t^{2}}{1+\gamma\,t}
for any
t\geq 0
.

3.2.
An estimate based on the fast diffusion flow, valid up to the critical exponent

The subcritical range
p\in[2^{\#},2^{*})
corresponding to exponents between the
Bakry–Emery exponent
and the critical Sobolev exponent is not covered in Section
3.1
. In that case, we rely on entropy methods based on a fast diffusion or porous medium equation of exponent
m
, which are detailed in Appendix
B
(with corresponding references), to establish that an improved inequality (
3.4
) holds for any
\varphi=\varphi_{m,p}
, where

(3.5)

\varphi_{m,p}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\exp\left[-\,\zeta\left(\left(1\,-\,(p-2)\,z\right)^{1-\delta}-\left(1\,-\,(p-2)\,s\right)^{1-\delta}\right)\right]\,dz\,,

provided
m\in\mathscr{A}_{p}:=\mathscr{A}_{p}:=\left\{m\in[m_{-}(d,p),m_{+}(d,p)]\,:\,\tfrac{2}{p}\leq m<1\;\mbox{if}\;p<4\right\}
, where

(3.6)

m_{\pm}(d,p):=\frac{1}{(d+2)\,p}\left(d\,p+2\pm\sqrt{d\,(p-1)\,\big(2\,d-(d-2)\,p\big)}\right)\,,

while the parameters
\delta
and
\zeta
are defined by

\displaystyle\delta:=1+\frac{(m-1)\,p^{2}}{4\,(p-2)}\,,

\displaystyle\zeta:=\frac{(d+2)^{2}\,p^{2}\,m^{2}-2\,p\,(d+2)\,(d\,p+2)\,m+d^{2}\left(5\,p^{2}-12\,p+8\right)+4\,d\,(3-2\,p)\,p+4}{(1-m)\,(d+2)^{2}\,p^{2}}\kern-1.0pt\,.

Let
s_{\star}:=1/(p-2)
as in (
3.2
) and consider the inverse function
\varphi_{m,p}^{-1}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to[0,s_{\star})
and
\psi_{m,p}(s):=s-\varphi_{m,p}^{-1}(s)
. Exactly as in the case
m=1
, we have the improved entropy – entropy production inequality

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq d\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\varphi_{m,p}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})\,,

which provides us with the following stability estimate.

Proposition 9
.

With above notation,
d\geq 1
,
p\in(2,2^{*})
and
m\in\mathscr{A}_{p}
, we have

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq d\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\psi_{m,p}\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{d\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})\,.

The function
\varphi_{m,p}
can be expressed in terms of the
incomplete
\Gamma
function
, while
\psi_{m,p}
is known only implicitly.

3.3.
Comparison with other estimates

Let us assume that
p\in(2,2^{*})
. In
[
32
]
, Frank proves the existence of a positive constant
\mathsf{c}_{\star}(d,p)
such that

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq\mathsf{c}_{\star}(d,p)\,\frac{\left(\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F-\overline{F}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{d}{p-2}\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\,,

where
\overline{F}:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F}\,d\mu
, which in particular implies the existence of a positive constant
\mathsf{c}(d,p)
such that

(3.7)

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq\mathsf{c}(d,p)\,\frac{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{d}{p-2}\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\quad\forall\,F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)\,,

for all
p\in(2,2^{*})
. The value of the constant
\mathsf{c}_{\star}(d,p)
found in
[
32
]
is unknown as it follows from a compactness argument, in the spirit of
[
9
]
, but the exponent
4
in the right-hand side of (
3.7
) is optimal.
With
the test functions
F_{\varepsilon}(x)=1+\varepsilon\,x\cdot\nu
for some arbitrary
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, we can indeed check that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}\left(\left\|\nabla F_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F_{\varepsilon}]\right)=\frac{(d+p)\,(p-1)}{2\,d\,(d+3)}\,,

which gives the upper bounds

\mathsf{c}(d,p)\leq\frac{(p-1)\,(d+p)}{2\,(p-2)\,(d+3)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{c}_{\star}(d,p)\leq\frac{d^{2}}{(d+1)^{2}}\,\frac{(p-1)\,(d+p)}{2\,(p-2)\,(d+3)}\,.

Let us notice that
\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq d\,\left\|F-\overline{F}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}
by the Poincaré inequality, so that we have

\left(\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F\kern-0.5pt-\kern-0.5pt\overline{F}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{2}\geq\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{(d+1)^{2}}\left(\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F\kern-0.5pt-\kern-0.5pt\overline{F}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{2}

and, at least if
\mathsf{c}_{\star}(d,p)
and
\mathsf{c}(d,p)
are the optimal constants,

\frac{d^{2}}{(d+1)^{2}}\,\mathsf{c}(d,p)\leq\mathsf{c}_{\star}(d,p)\leq\mathsf{c}(d,p)\,.

We claim that the
carré du champ
method provides us with a constructive estimate of
\mathsf{c}(d,p)
. Let

\phi_{c}(s):=\frac{d}{2\,(1-c)}\left(2\,c\,s-s_{\star}+\sqrt{s_{\star}^{2}+4\,c\,s\,(s-s_{\star})}\right)\,.

Corollary 10
.

Let
p\in(2,2^{*})
. With the notation of Proposition
9
, inequality (
3.7
) holds with

\mathsf{c}=\sup\Big\{c>0\,:\,\exists\,m\in\mathscr{A}_{p}\;\mbox{such that}\;\phi_{c}(s)\leq\varphi_{m,p}(s)\;\forall\,s\in[0,s_{\star})\Big\}\,.

Proof.

With no loss of generality, let us assume that
\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
and define

\mathsf{i}=\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{e}:=\frac{1-\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{p-2}\,,

so that
\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=1-(p-2)\,\mathsf{e}
. With
c=\mathsf{c}(d,p)
, we can rewrite (
3.7
) as

\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\geq\frac{c\,\mathsf{i}^{2}}{\mathsf{i}+\frac{d}{p-2}-d\,\mathsf{e}}\,,

which amounts to

\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\geq\phi_{c}(\mathsf{e})\,.

Since we know that
\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\geq\varphi_{m,p}(\mathsf{e})
, the conclusion follows for the largest possible
c>0
such that
\varphi_{m,p}\geq\phi_{c}
. ∎

4.
Global stability results

We collect the statements of Theorems
3
and
6
into a single result. The whole section is devoted to its proof.

Theorem 11
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
p\in(1,2^{*})
. For any
F\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{S}}^{d},d\mu)
, we have

(4.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla F|^{2}}\,d\mu-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq\mathscr{S}_{d,p}\left(\frac{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}+\left\|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\,F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)

for some explicit stability constant
\mathscr{S}_{d,p}>0
.

The value of
\mathscr{S}_{d,p}
is elementary and explicit but its expression is lengthy. We explain in the proof how to compute it with all necessary details to obtain a numerical expression for
\mathscr{S}_{d,p}
for given
p
and
d
, if needed.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

By homogeneity of (
4.1
), we can assume that
\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
without loss of generality. For clarity, we subdivide the proof into various steps. Let us start with the case
p>2
.

\bullet

An estimate based on the
carré du champ
method.

If
\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}/\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\vartheta_{0}>0
, we know by the convexity of
\psi_{m,p}
that

(4.2)

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[F]\geq d\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,\psi_{m,p}\left(\frac{1}{d}\,\tfrac{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}{\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\right)\geq\frac{d}{\vartheta_{0}}\,\psi_{m,p}\left(\frac{\vartheta_{0}}{d}\right)\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,.

In that case, we conclude from
\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\left\|\nabla\Pi_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|\nabla(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{1})\,F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}
and

\left\|\nabla\Pi_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq\frac{\left\|\nabla\Pi_{1}F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{4}}{\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}}\,.

Let us assume now that
\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}<\vartheta_{0}\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}
. By taking into account (
GN
), we obtain

\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}<\vartheta_{0}\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq\vartheta_{0}\left(\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{p-2}{d}\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\,.

Using
\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
, under the assumption that
\vartheta_{0}<d/(p-2)
, we know that

(4.3)

\vartheta:=\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}<\frac{d\,\vartheta_{0}}{d-(p-2)\,\vartheta_{0}}\,.

Notice that the parameter
\vartheta_{0}
still has to be chosen.

\bullet

An estimate of the average.

Let us estimate
\Pi_{0}F:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F}\,d\mu
. By the Poincaré inequality, we have

1=\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F}\,d\mu\right)^{2}+\left\|(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{0})\,F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F}\,d\mu\right)^{2}+\frac{\vartheta}{d}\,,

and on the other hand we know that
\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F}\,d\mu\right)^{2}\leq\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=1
by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, so that

(4.4)

\frac{d-\vartheta}{d}<\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{F}\,d\mu\right)^{2}\leq 1\,.

We assume in the sequel that

(4.5)

\vartheta<d\,.

\bullet

Partial decomposition on spherical harmonics.

With no loss of generality, let us write

(4.6)

F=\mathscr{M}\left(1+\varepsilon\,\mathscr{Y}+\eta\,G\right)

such that
\mathscr{M}=\Pi_{0}F
and
\Pi_{1}F=\varepsilon\,\mathscr{M}\,\mathscr{Y}
where
\mathscr{Y}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{d}}\,x\cdot\nu
for some given
\nu\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
. Here the functions
\mathscr{Y}
and
G
are normalized so that
\left\|\nabla\mathscr{Y}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=\left\|\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
and

\mathscr{M}^{-2}\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}=\vartheta\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathscr{M}^{-2}\,\left\|F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=1+\frac{1}{d}\,\varepsilon^{2}+\eta^{2}\,\left\|G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,.

We observe that
\Pi_{0}(F-\mathscr{M})=0
. Using (
GN
) and the Poincaré inequality, we have

\left\|F-\mathscr{M}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq\left\|F-\mathscr{M}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{p-2}{d}\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq\frac{p-1}{d}\,\left\|\nabla F\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,.

Similarly, by (
2.1
),
i.e.
,

\frac{d\,p}{2\,(d+p)}\,\left\|\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\left(1-\frac{2\,d-p\,(d-2)}{2\,(d+p)}\right)\left\|\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\geq d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[G]\,,

and the improved Poincaré inequality (
2.1
) written with
p=1
and
k=1

\left\|G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\,(d+1)}\,\left\|\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\,(d+1)}\,,

we have

\left\|G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq\left\|G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}+\frac{p\,(p-2)}{2\,(d+p)}\,\left\|\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\leq C_{p,d}

using
\left\|\nabla G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1
, with
C_{p,d}:=\frac{1}{2\,(d+1)}+\frac{p\,(p-2)}{2\,(d+p)}
. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we also have

\left\|G\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,(d+1)}}\,,

We recall that the eigenvalues of
-\Delta
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
are
\lambda_{k}=k\,(k+d-1)
with
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In preparation for a detailed Taylor expansion as in
[
32
]
, let us consider the function

\mathscr{Y}(x):=\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{d}}\,x\cdot\nu\,,

which is such that
-\Delta\mathscr{Y}=\lambda_{1}\,\mathscr{Y}
with
\lambda_{1}=d
and

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\mathscr{Y}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=1\,,\quad\left\|\mathscr{Y}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=\frac{1}{d}\,,

Appendix A
Improved Gaussian inequalities, hypercontractivity and stability

Whether the results of Theorems
1
,
4
and
7
can be extended to Euclidean case with the Gaussian measure is a very natural question. Spherical harmonics can indeed be replaced by Hermite polynomials and there is a clear correspondence for spectral estimates. The answer is yes for a whole family of interpolation inequalities, but it is no for the logarithmic Sobolev inequality, which is an endpoint of the family.

Let us consider the
normalized Gaussian measure
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
defined by

d\sigma(x)=(2\,\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\,|x|^{2}}\,dx\,.

For any
p\in[1,2)
, Beckner in
[
6
]
established the family of interpolation inequalities

(A.1)

\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}}{2-p}\leq\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)\,.

With
p=1
, inequality (
A.1
) is the
Gaussian Poincaré inequality
while one recovers the Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality of
[
35
]
in the limit as
p\to 2
. For any
p\in[1,2)
, the inequality is optimal: using
f_{\varepsilon}:=1+\varepsilon\,\varphi
as a test function, where
\varphi
is such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\varphi}\,d\sigma=0
, we recover the
Gaussian Poincaré inequality
with optimal constant in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, so that the constant in (
A.1
) cannot be improved. Based on
[
43
,
1
]
, the improved version of the inequality

(A.2)

\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}}{2-p}\leq\frac{p}{2}\,\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)

holds under the additional condition

(A.3)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{x\,f(x)}\,d\sigma=0\,.

Let us give a short proof of (
A.2
). Assume that
f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}f_{k}
is a decomposition on Hermite functions such that
\mathcal{L}f_{k}=-\,k\,f_{k}
where
\mathcal{L}=\Delta-x\cdot\nabla
is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator, and let
a_{k}:=\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, so that

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}k\,a_{k}\,.

Let us consider the solution of

(A.4)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\mathcal{L}u

with initial datum
u(t=0,\cdot)=f
and notice that

\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}a_{k}\,e^{-2\,k\,t}\,.

Hence, if (
A.3
) holds,
a_{1}=0
and

(A.5)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}-\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 2}a_{k}\left(1-e^{-2\,k\,t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-e^{-4\,t}\right)\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}k\,a_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-e^{-4\,t}\right)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}

because
k\mapsto\left(1-e^{-2\,k\,t}\right)/k
is monotone nonincreasing for any given
t\geq 0
. Next, we use Nelson’s hypercontractivity estimate in
[
48
, Theorem 3]
to find
t_{*}>0
such that

\left\|u(t_{*},\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\,.

As noted in
[
35
]
, this estimate can be seen as a consequence of the
Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality

(A.6)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|v|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{\left\|v\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}}\right)}\,d\sigma\leq 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\sigma\quad\forall\,v\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)\,,

and the argument goes as follows. With
h(t):=\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q(t)}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}
for some exponent
q
depending on
t
and
u
solving (
A.4
), we have

\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}=\frac{q^{\prime}}{q^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|u|^{q}}{h^{q}}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{q}}{h^{q}}\right)}\,d\sigma-\frac{4}{h^{q}}\,\frac{q-1}{q^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left|\nabla\left(|u|^{q/2}\right)\right|^{2}}\,d\sigma\leq 0

by (
A.6
) applied to
v=|u|^{q/2}
, if
t\mapsto q(t)
solves the ordinary differential equation

q^{\prime}=2\,(q-1)\,.

With
q(0)=p<2
, we obtain
q(t)=1+\left(p-1\right)e^{2t}
and find that Nelson’s time
t_{*}
is determined by the condition
q(t_{*})=2
which means
e^{-2t_{*}}=p-1
. Replacing
t=t_{*}
in (
A.5
) completes the proof of (
A.2
), which can be recast in the form of a stability result for (
A.1
).

Theorem 12
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
p\in[1,2)
. For any
f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)
such that (
A.3
) holds,

\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2-p}\,\Big(\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\Big)\geq\frac{2-p}{2}\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\,.

As a byproduct of the proof, with
t=t_{*}
in (
A.5
), we have the mode-by-mode interpolation inequality

\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}-\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}}{2-p}\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1-(p-1)^{k}}{k\,(2-p)}\,\left\|\nabla f_{k}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)}^{2}\quad\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},d\sigma)\,,

without imposing condition (
A.3
), for any
p\in[1,2)
. For any
k\geq 1
,

\lim_{p\to 2_{-}}\frac{1-(p-1)^{k}}{k\,(2-p)}=\lim_{p\to 2_{-}}\frac{1-\big(1-(2-p)\big)^{k}}{k\,(2-p)}=1\,,

so that no improvement should be expected by this method. This is very similar to the case of the critical exponent on the sphere of dimension
d\geq 3
. In this sense
p=2
is the critical case in the presence of a Gaussian weight, as
all modes
are equally involved in the estimate of the constant. This is a limitation of the method which does not forbid a stability result for (
A.6
), to be established by other methods.

Let us conclude this appendix with some bibliographic comments on the literature on inequality (
A.1

). Assuming that not only condition (

can be achieved for inequality (
A.1
), which is the counterpart of Theorem

in the Gaussian case. This has been studied in

]
but we can refer to
[

]
for a more abstract setting and later papers,
e.g.
, to
[

]
for results on compact manifolds and generalizations involving weights. For an overview of interpolation between Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities from the point of view of Markov processes, and for some spectral considerations, we refer to
[

. Notice that hypercontractivity appears as one of the main motivations of the founding paper

of the

B.1.
Algebraic preliminaries

Let us denote the
Hessian
by
\mathrm{H}v
and define the
trace-free Hessian
by

\mathrm{L}v:=\mathrm{H}v-\frac{1}{d}\,(\Delta v)\,g_{d}\,.

We also consider the trace-free tensor

\mathrm{M}v:=\frac{\nabla v\otimes\nabla v}{v}-\frac{1}{d}\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\,g_{d}\,,

where
(\nabla v\otimes\nabla v)_{ij}:=\partial_{i}v\,\partial_{j}v
and
\|\nabla v\otimes\nabla v\|^{2}=|\nabla v|^{4}=(g_{d}^{ij}\,\partial_{i}v\,\partial_{j}v)^{2}
using Einstein’s convention. Using

\mathrm{L}:g_{d}=0\,,\quad\mathrm{M}:g_{d}=0\,,

where
a:b
denotes
a^{ij}\,b_{ij}
and
\|a\|^{2}:=a:a
, and

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{L}v\|^{2}=\|\mathrm{H}v\|^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\,(\Delta v)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}=\left\|\frac{\nabla v\otimes\nabla v}{v}\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{d}\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}=\frac{d-1}{d}\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}\,,

we deduce from

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\Delta v\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu-2\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\mathrm{H}v:\frac{\nabla v\otimes\nabla v}{v}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu-2\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\mathrm{L}v:\frac{\nabla v\otimes\nabla v}{v}}\,d\mu-\frac{2}{d}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\Delta v\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}}\,d\mu

a first identity that reads

(B.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\Delta v\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}}\,d\mu=\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu-2\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\mathrm{L}v:\frac{\nabla v\otimes\nabla v}{v}}\,d\mu\right)\,.

The Bochner–Lichnerowicz–Weitzenböck formula on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
takes the simple form

\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta\,(|\nabla v|^{2})=\|\mathrm{H}v\|^{2}+\nabla(\Delta v)\cdot\nabla v+(d-1)\,|\nabla v|^{2}\,,

where the last term,
i.e.
,
\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla v,\nabla v)=(d-1)\,|\nabla v|^{2}
, accounts for the Ricci curvature tensor contracted with
\nabla v\otimes\nabla v
. An integration of this formula on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
shows a second identity,

(B.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{(\Delta v)^{2}}\,d\mu=\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{L}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu+d\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu\,.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}[v]:=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\left(\Delta v+\kappa\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\right)\left(\Delta v+(\beta-1)\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\right)}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{(\Delta v)^{2}}\,d\mu+(\kappa+\beta-1)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\Delta v\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}}\,d\mu+\kappa\,(\beta-1)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu

can be rewritten using (
B.1
) and (
B.2
) as

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}[v]

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{L}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu+d\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+(\kappa+\beta-1)\,\frac{d}{d+2}\left(\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu-2\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\mathrm{L}v:\mathrm{M}v}\,d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\kappa\,(\beta-1)\,\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\Big(\|\mathrm{L}v\|^{2}-2\,b\,\mathrm{L}v:\mathrm{M}v+c\,\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}\Big)}\,d\mu+d\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\Big(\|\mathrm{L}v-\,b\,\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}+\left(c-b^{2}\right)\|\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}\Big)}\,d\mu+d\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\|\mathrm{L}v-\,b\,\mathrm{M}v\|^{2}}\,d\mu+\left(c-b^{2}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu+d\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu

where

b=(\kappa+\beta-1)\,\frac{d-1}{d+2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad c=\frac{d}{d+2}\,(\kappa+\beta-1)+\kappa\,(\beta-1)\,.

Let
\kappa=\beta\,(p-2)+1
. The condition
\gamma:=c-b^{2}\geq 0
amounts to

(B.3)

\gamma=\frac{d}{d+2}\,\beta\,(p-1)+\big(1+\beta\,(p-2)\big)\,(\beta-1)-\left(\frac{d-1}{d+2}\,\beta\,(p-1)\right)^{2}\,,

where
\gamma=-\,\big(A\,\beta^{2}-2\,B\,\beta+C\big)
with

A=\left(\frac{d-1}{d+2}\,(p-1)\right)^{2}+2-p\,,\quad B=\frac{d+3-p}{d+2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad C=1\,.

A necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a
\beta
such that
\gamma\geq 0
is that the reduced discriminant is nonnegative, which amounts to

B^{2}-A\,C=\frac{4\,d\,(d-2)}{(d+2)^{2}}\,\big(p-1\big)\left(2^{*}-p\right)\geq 0\,.

Summarizing, we have the following result, which can be found in
[
22
]
for a general manifold with positive Ricci curvature.

Lemma 13
.

With the above notation, for any smooth function
v
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}
, we have

\mathscr{K}[v]\geq\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu+d\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu

for some
\gamma>0
given in terms of
\beta
by (
B.3
) if
p\in(1,2^{*})
.

Notice that we recover the expression for
\gamma
in (
3.1
) if we take
\beta=1
. The case
p=2
does not add any difficulty compared to
p\neq 2
.

B.2.
Diffusion flow and monotonicity

Assume that
u
is a positive solution of

(B.4)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=u^{-p\,(1-m)}\left(\Delta u+(m\,p-1)\,\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}\right)\,.

In the linear case
m=1
,
u^{p}
solves the heat equation. Otherwise we deal with the nonlinear case either of a fast diffusion flow with
m<1
or of a solution of the porous media equation with
m>1
. We claim that

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=2\,(p-2)\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{u^{-\,p\,(1-m)}\,|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mu\,.

Let us assume that the parameters
\beta
and
m
are related by

(B.5)

m=1+\frac{2}{p}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-1\right)\,.

If
v
is a function such that
u=v^{\beta}
, then
v
solves

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=v^{2-2\,\beta}\left(\Delta v+\kappa\,\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\right)\,,

with
\kappa=\beta\,(p-2)+1
and as a consequence we find that

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=2\,(p-2)\,\beta^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu\,.

Similarly, we find that

(B.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}=-\,2\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\left(\beta\,v^{\beta-1}\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right)\left(\Delta v^{\beta}\right)}\,d\mu=-\,2\,\beta^{2}\,\mathscr{K}[v]\,.

By eliminating
\beta
in (
B.3
) using (
B.5
), we obtain

(B.7)

\gamma=\frac{\gamma_{0}+\gamma_{1}\,d+\gamma_{2}\,d^{2}}{(d+2)^{2}\,\big(2-p\,(1-m)\big)^{2}}\,,

with
\gamma_{0}=4\,(m\,p-1)^{2}
,
\gamma_{1}=-\,4\,p\,\big(\!m-3+p\,(2-m)\,(1+m)\!\big)
,
\gamma_{2}=(m^{2}-2\,m+5)\,p^{2}-12\,p+8
. The condition
\gamma\geq 0
determines the range
m_{-}(d,p)\leq m\leq m_{+}(d,p)
of
admissible
parameters
m
, where
m_{\pm}(d,p)
is given by (
3.6
). Summarizing, we have the following result (also see
[
22
]
).

Lemma 14
.

Assume that
p\in(1,2^{*})
and
m\in[m_{-}(d,p),m_{+}(d,p)]
. If
u
solves (
B.4
), then we have

(B.8)

\frac{1}{2\,\beta^{2}}\,\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-d\,\mathcal{E}_{p}[u]\right)\leq-\,\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu\,,

where
v=u^{1/\beta}
with
\beta
and
\gamma
given in terms of
m
by (
B.3
) and (
B.7
) respectively.

Notice that the case of the linear flow corresponds to the case
m=\beta=1
and
v=u
.

Proof of Lemma
14
.

For a smooth solution, the result follows from (
B.6
) and Lemma
13
. The result for a general solution is obtained by standard regularization procedures. ∎

B.3.
Interpolation

Depending on the value of
p
, we shall consider various interpolation inequalities. Let us define

(B.9)

\delta:=\frac{p-\,(4-p)\,\beta}{2\,\beta\,(p-2)}\quad\mbox{if}\quad p>2\,,\quad\delta:=1\quad\mbox{if}\quad p\in[1,2]\,.

Lemma 15
.

If one of the conditions

(i)

p\in(1,2^{\#})
and
\beta=1
(so that
\delta=1
),

(ii)

p\in(2,2^{*})
,
\beta>1
, and
\beta\leq 2/(4-p)
if
p<4
,

is satisfied, then
u=v^{\beta}
is such that

(B.10)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{|v|^{2}}}\,d\mu\geq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mu\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\delta}\,\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{p}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta\,(p-2)}}}\,.

Case (ii) was originally proved in
[
17
,
18
]
and we refer to
[
21
]
for a proof in the case of the ultraspherical operator.

Proof of Lemma
15
.

In case (i),
v=u
and inequality (
B.10
) is a consequence of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\cdot v}\,d\mu\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

Cases (i) and (ii) follow from two Hölder inequalities.

(1) With
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta-1}{2\,\beta}+\frac{1}{2\,\beta}=1
, we deduce from

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\cdot 1\cdot v}\,d\mu\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{1}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{\beta-1}{2\,\beta}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2\,\beta}}\,,

and the assumption that
d\mu
is a probability measure, the first estimate

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2\,\beta}}}\,.

(2) With
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta\,(p-2)}+\frac{2-(4-p)\,\beta}{2\,\beta\,(p-2)}=1
and
\delta_{0}=\frac{2-\,(4-p)\,\beta}{2\,\beta\,(p-2)}
, Hölder’s inequality shows that

\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{v^{2(\beta-1)}\,|\nabla v|^{2}}\,d\mu=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v}\cdot v^{\frac{p\,(\beta-1)}{p-2}}\cdot v^{2\,\beta\,\delta_{0}}}\,d\mu\\
\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{p}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta\,(p-2)}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\delta_{0}}\,,

from which we deduce the second estimate

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{\frac{|\nabla v|^{4}}{v^{2}}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\,d\mu}{\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu\right)^{\delta_{0}}\,\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}}{|u|^{p}}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta\,(p-2)}}}\,.

The combination of our two estimates proves (
B.10
) with
\delta=\delta_{0}+1/(2\,\beta)
.
∎

Using (
B.5
), condition (ii) in Lemma
15
is changed into the condition that
2/p\leq m<1
and we may notice as in
[
17
,
18
]
that it is always satisfied if we choose
\beta=4/(6-p)
corresponding to an
admissible
fast diffusion exponent
m=(p+2)/(2\,p)
, for any
p\in(2,2^{*})
. By “admissible”, one should understand
m_{-}(d,p)\leq m\leq m_{+}(d,p)
, so that
\gamma
is nonnegative. With the choice of
m=(p+2)/(2\,p)
, we find
\delta=1-p/8
.

B.4.
Improved functional inequalities

Let us denote the
entropy
and the
Fisher information
respectively by

\mathsf{e}:=\frac{1}{p-2}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}-\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathsf{i}:=\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}^{2}\,,

and let
\gamma
and
\delta
be given respectively by (
B.3
) and (
B.9
). Up to the replacement of
u
by
u/\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}
, with no loss of generality, we shall assume that

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}({\mathbb{S}}^{d})}=1\,.

We learn from (
B.8
) and (
B.10
) that

(B.11)

\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\mathsf{e}\right)^{\prime}\leq\frac{\gamma\,\mathsf{i}\,\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}{\beta^{2}\,\big(1-(p-2)\,\mathsf{e}\big)^{\delta}}\,.

Solving the ordinary differential equation in the equality case of (
B.11
) is equivalent to solving

\frac{d}{dt}\big(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\varphi(\mathsf{e})\big)=\frac{\gamma}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}{\big(1-(p-2)\,\mathsf{e}\big)^{\delta}}\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\varphi(\mathsf{e})\right)\,,

where
\varphi
solves

(B.12)

\varphi^{\prime}(s)=1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{\varphi(s)}{\left(1\,-\,(p-2)\,s\right)^{\delta}}\,.

The reader is invited to check that the solution of (
B.12
) with initial datum
\varphi(0)=0
is given by (
3.3
) if
m=1
and by (
3.5
) with
\zeta=2\,\gamma/\big(\beta\,(1-\beta)\big)
in the nonlinear case. We learn from (
B.11
) that

\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\varphi(\mathsf{e})\right)^{\prime}\leq\frac{\gamma}{\beta^{2}}\,\frac{\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}{\big(1-(p-2)\,\mathsf{e}\big)^{\delta}}\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\varphi(\mathsf{e})\right)\,.

This is enough to prove the following result.

Proposition 16
.

With the above notation, we claim that

\mathsf{i}\geq\,d\,\varphi(\mathsf{e})\,.

Proof.

Let us give the scheme of a proof. Let
\tilde{\gamma}:=\gamma/\beta^{2}
, in order to simplify notation. We can argue as follows:

(1)

\mathsf{i}^{\prime}+2\,d\,\mathsf{i}=\left(\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\right)^{\prime}\leq 0
shows that

0\leq\mathsf{i}(t)\leq\mathsf{i}(0)\,e^{-2\,d\,t}

and in particular
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathsf{i}(t)=0
.

(2)

As
t\to+\infty
,
\mathsf{e}
converges to a constant, hence
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathsf{e}(t)=0
.

(3)

From (
B.11
), we learn that

\left(\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\right)^{\prime}\leq d\,\tilde{\gamma}\,\mathsf{e}\,\mathsf{e}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\,d\,\tilde{\gamma}\,(\mathsf{e}^{2})^{\prime}\,,

where the inequality follows from
1-(p-2)\,\mathsf{e}\leq 1
and
\mathsf{i}\geq d\,\mathsf{e}
.

(4)

It follows from
\left(\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\right)^{\prime}\leq 0
that
\mathsf{i}\geq d\,\mathsf{e}
using an integration from any
t\geq 0
to
+\infty
.

(5)

Unless
u
is a constant, we read from
\left(\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}\right)^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\gamma}\,d\,(\mathsf{e}^{2})^{\prime}
that
\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}>\frac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\gamma}\,d\,\mathsf{e}^{2}
, using again an integration from any
t\geq 0
to
+\infty
.

(6)

Take some
\vartheta\in(0,1)
and consider the solution of

(B.13)

\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}(s)=1+\frac{\vartheta\,\tilde{\gamma}\,\overline{\varphi}(s)}{\left(1\,-\,(p-2)\,s\right)^{\delta}}\,,\quad\overline{\varphi}(0)=0\,.

In the spirit of (
B.11
), we have a following chain of elementary estimates:

\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\vartheta\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\right)^{\prime}\leq\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\right)^{\prime}+d\,(1-\vartheta)\,\left(\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\right)^{\prime}\leq\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\right)^{\prime}

and obtain

(B.14)

\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\vartheta\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\right)^{\prime}\leq\frac{\tilde{\gamma}\,\mathsf{e}^{\prime}}{\big(1-(p-2)\,\mathsf{e}\big)^{\delta}}\left(\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\vartheta\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\right)\,.

We know that
\overline{\varphi}(0)=0
and read from (
B.13
) that
\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)=1
and

\overline{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\vartheta\,\tilde{\gamma}\,\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)=\vartheta\,\tilde{\gamma}\,,

so that
\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})-\mathsf{e}\sim\frac{1}{2}\,\vartheta\,\tilde{\gamma}\,\mathsf{e}^{2}
as
\mathsf{e}\to 0
. Using
\mathsf{i}-d\,\mathsf{e}>\frac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\gamma}\,d\,\mathsf{e}^{2}
, we learn that

\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\tilde{\gamma}\,d\,(1-\vartheta)\,\mathsf{e}^{2}\,\big(1+O(\mathsf{e})\big)

for
\mathsf{e}=\mathsf{e}(t)
small enough,
i.e
, for
t>0
large enough.

(7)

It is simple to check from (
B.14
) that
\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\vartheta\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})
cannot change sign.

(8)

We conclude as above that
\mathsf{i}-\,d\,\vartheta\,\overline{\varphi}(\mathsf{e})\geq 0
using an integration from any
t\geq 0
to
+\infty
.

(9)

Finally, we consider the limit as
\vartheta\to 1_{-}
.

Altogether, we conclude that
\mathsf{i}\geq\,d\,\varphi(\mathsf{e})
, where
\varphi
solves (
B.12
). This completes the scheme of the proof of Proposition
16
.
∎

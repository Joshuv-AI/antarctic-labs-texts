Abstract
. A conjecture attributed to Smith states that every pair of longest cycles in a k k -connected graph intersect each other in at least k k vertices. In this paper, we show that every pair of longest cycles in a k k -connected graph on n n vertices intersect each other in at least min ⁡ { n , 8 ​ k − n − 16 } \min\{n,8k-n-16\} vertices, which confirms Smith’s conjecture when k ≥ ( n + 16 ) / 7 k\geq(n+16)/7 . An analog conjecture for paths instead of cycles was stated by Hippchen. By a simple reduction, we relate both conjectures, showing that Hippchen’s conjecture is valid when either k ≤ 6 k\leq 6 or k ≥ ( n + 9 ) / 7 k\geq(n+9)/7 .



1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

It is known that every pair of longest cycles (paths)
in a 2-connected (connected) graph intersect each other in at least two vertices (one vertex).
A conjecture proposed by Grötschel, and attributed to Scott Smith
[
5
, Conjecture 5.2]
, states that, in a
k
-connected graph, with
k\geq 2
, every pair of longest cycles intersect each other in at least
k
vertices.
Years later, Hippchen
[
9
, Conjecture 2.2.4]
conjectured that, for
k
-connected
graphs, every pair of longest paths intersect each other in at least
k
vertices.

Smith’s conjecture has been verified up to
k=7

[
5
]
, and, for a general
k
, it was proved that every pair of longest cycles intersect in at least
ck^{3/5}
vertices, for a constant
c\thickapprox 0.2615

[
2
]
.
For Hippchen’s conjecture, the case
k=3
was proved by Hippchen himself

[
9
, Lemma 2.2.3]
.
Later, the first author show it for
k=4
,
and recently, Cho
et al.
[
3
]
show it for
k=5
.

As we can observe in the literature, efforts on both conjectures has been made independently. The first main contribution of this paper is relate these conjectures. We will show that if
Smith’s conjecture is valid for a fixed
k
, then
Hippchen’s conjecture is valid for
k-1
.
This relationship implies that efforts
can be concentrated on Smith’s conjecture.
So, as Smith’s conjecture is known to be valid for
k\leq 7
, an easy corollary is that Hippchen’s conjecture is true for
k\leq 6
.
(For an independent proof of this fact, see

[
8
]
).

The second main contribution of this paper is
an improvement of a result on Hippchen’s conjecture when
k=\Omega(n)
, where
n
is the number of vertices of the graph.
In
[
6
]
, the first author
showed that Hippchen’s conjecture is valid when

k\geq(n-2)/3
. This result was improved
by Cho
et al.
[
3
]
. They showed
that Hippchen’s conjecture is valid when

k\geq(n+2)/5
.
In this paper, we improve this result, by showing
that Hippchen’s conjecture is valid when

k\geq(n+9)/7
.
In fact, we show the stronger statement
that every pair of longest paths in a
k
-connected graph intersect in at least
\min\{k,8k-n-9\}
vertices.
Moreover, this result is a consequence of an analog result on cycles: we show that every pair of longest cycles intersect in at least
\min\{k,8k-n-16\}
vertices.

In this paper all graphs are simple (without loops or parallel edges) and the notation and terminology are standard.
We also consider simple paths and cycles, that is, repetitions of vertices or edges is not allowed.
The

of a path

(cycle

) is the number of edges it has, and it is denoted by

(

Given a path

Given two set of vertices

-

Two paths are

A graph

-connected
if, for any two distinct

k

It is easy to see that for a

-connected graph on

A set

of a connected graph

It is known that a graph is

Oja’s Algorithm for Streaming Sparse PCA
Syamantak Kumar, Purnamrita Sarkar

Abstract
Oja’s algorithm for Streaming Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for n n data-points in a d d dimensional space achieves the same sin-squared error O ⁡ ( r 𝖾𝖿𝖿 / n ) O(r_{\mathsf{eff}}/n) as the offline algorithm in O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) space and O ⁡ ( n ​ d ) O(nd) time and a single pass through the datapoints. Here r 𝖾𝖿𝖿 r_{\mathsf{eff}} is the effective rank (ratio of the trace and the principal eigenvalue of the population covariance matrix Σ \Sigma ). Under this computational budget, we consider the problem of sparse PCA, where the principal eigenvector of Σ \Sigma is s s -sparse, and r 𝖾𝖿𝖿 r_{\mathsf{eff}} can be large. In this setting, to our knowledge, there are no known single-pass algorithms that achieve the minimax error bound in O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) space and O ⁡ ( n ​ d ) O(nd) time without either requiring strong initialization conditions or assuming further structure (e.g., spiked) of the covariance matrix. We show that a simple single-pass procedure that thresholds the output of Oja’s algorithm (the Oja vector) can achieve the minimax error bound under some regularity conditions in O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) space and O ⁡ ( n ​ d ) O(nd) time. We present a nontrivial and novel analysis of the entries of the unnormalized Oja vector, which involves the projection of a product of independent random matrices on a random initial vector. This is completely different from previous analyses of Oja’s algorithm and matrix products, which have been done when the r 𝖾𝖿𝖿 r_{\mathsf{eff}} is bounded.

5
Conclusion

Oja’s algorithm for streaming PCA has been extensively studied in the recent theoretical literature, typically assuming that
\|X_{i}\|^{2}/\lambda_{1}
is bounded or a slowly growing covariance matrix effective rank
r_{\mathsf{eff}}
. This paper addresses the high-dimensional sparse PCA setting where the effective rank
r_{\mathsf{eff}}
can be as large as
n/\log n
while
v_{1}
is
s
-sparse. In this context, while there has been a vast body of work that achieves minimax error bounds, we are unaware of any single-pass algorithm that works in
O(nd)
time,
O(d)
space, on a general
\Sigma
, without any strong initialization. Surprisingly, our thresholded estimator achieves the minimax error bound of
O(s\log d/n)
, whereas the error rate of Oja’s algorithm is
O(r_{\mathsf{eff}}/n)
. Empirically, the elements of the unnormalized Oja vector do not concentrate in this regime. Through an analysis that uncouples the projection of a product of independent random matrices on
v_{1}
and its orthogonal subspace, we show that the entries of the Oja vector within the support of
v_{1}
are large, while those outside are much smaller.

Lemma A.1
.

(Fact 2.9
[
DKPP23
]
)
For any symmetric
d\times d
matrix A, we have
\operatorname{Var}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[z^{T}Az\right]=2\|A\|_{F}^{2}
. If A is a PSD matrix, then for any
\beta>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[z^{T}Az\geq\beta\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)\right]\geq 1-\sqrt{e\beta}

Proof.

We give a short proof here. Since A is a symmetric matrix, let
A=P\Lambda P^{T}
where
P
is an orthonormal matrix and
\Lambda
is a diagonal matrix. Then, denoting
y:=P^{T}z
we note that
y\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle z^{T}Az

\displaystyle=z^{T}P\Lambda P^{T}z=y^{T}\Lambda y=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[z^{T}Az\right]=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}=\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[\left(z^{T}Az\right)^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}y_{i}^{4}+\sum_{i,j,i\neq j}^{d}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}y_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=3\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i,j,i\neq j}^{d}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}=2\operatorname{Tr}\left(A^{2}\right)+\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)^{2}

To get the tail lower bound, note that it trivially follows if
\beta>1
. Therefore we proceed with
\beta\in\left(0,1\right)
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[z^{T}Az\leq\beta\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{y\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}\leq\beta\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(t\left(\beta\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right)\right)\right],t>0

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\beta\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(-t\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(t\beta\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(1+2\lambda_{i}t\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

Let
t=\frac{1}{2\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-1\right)
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,I\right)}\left[z^{T}Az\leq\beta\operatorname{Tr}\left(A\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-1\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2}\right)\left(1+\left(\frac{1}{\beta}-1\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2}\right)\sqrt{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{e\beta}

Hence proved.
∎

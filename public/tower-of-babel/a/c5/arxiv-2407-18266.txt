Average orders of Goldbach Estimates in Arithmetic Progressions
Thi Thu Nguyen

Abstract
. We obtain asymptotic results on the average numbers of Goldbach representations of an integer as the sum of two primes in arithmetic progressions. We also prove an omega-result showing that the asymptotic result is essentially the best possible.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11P32; Secondary 11P55

1.
Introduction

The Goldbach conjecture that every even integer greater than two can be written as the sum of two primes, is one of the oldest open problems today. Instead of studying directly the Goldbach counting function
g(n)=\sum_{p_{1}+p_{2}=n}1
where
p_{1},p_{2}
are primes, we consider the corresponding problem for a smoother version using logarithms, that is the weighted Goldbach function

G(n)=\sum_{m+l=n}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(l),

where
\Lambda(n)
is the von Mangoldt function. We are interested in the behavior of the average order of magnitude of
G(n)
for
n\in[1,X]
where
X
is a large real number, that is

S(X):=\sum_{n\leq X}G(n).

Fujii showed the following estimate for
S(X)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
4
, Fujii]

Suppose that the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is true. Then we have

S(X)=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2}+H(X)+E(X),

where
H(X)=-2\sum_{\rho}\dfrac{X^{\rho+1}}{\rho(\rho+1)}
is the sum over the non-trivial zeros
\rho
of the Riemann zeta function counted with multiplicity and
E(X)\ll(X\log X)^{4/3}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Assuming the RH, the order of magnitude of
H(X)
is
\mathcal{O}(X^{3/2})
, while unconditionally, it is

H(X)\ll X^{1+B},

where
B=\sup\{Re(\rho):\zeta(\rho)=0\}.

Granville recovered Fujii’s result without assuming RH,
E(X)\ll X^{\frac{2+4B}{3}}\log^{2}X

[
8
,
9
]
. Egami and Matsumoto expected the error term to be
\mathcal{O}(X^{1+\varepsilon})
(
[
3
, Conjecture 2.2]
). In fact, this
conjecture was proved by Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta
[
2
]
who showed in 2010 that the error term is
\mathcal{O}(X\log^{5}X)

under RH and
\Omega(X\log\log X)
unconditionally. It was improved by Languasco-Zaccagnini
[
11
]

in 2012 and using different methods later by Goldson-Yang
[
7
]
in 2017 or Goldston-Suriajaya
[
6
]
in 2023 to
E(X)\ll X\log^{3}X.

We study the case of two primes in arithmetic progressions, which was first introduced by Rüppel
[
13
]
in 2012.
Suzuki
[
15
]
in 2017 obtained an asymptotic formula under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis with real zeros while for the case of common modulus, Bhowmik-Halupczok-Matsumoto-Suzuki
[
1
]
proved an asymptotic formula without assuming Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH).

In this paper, we will show an asymptotic result for the case of different modulus without the GRH. This results in the factor
\log^{3}(X)
in the error term thus we can recover Suzuki’s result
[
15
]
and improve the upper bound of the error term in
[
1
, Theorem 2]
by a factor
\log^{2}X
. To do that, we use the properties of the non-trivial zeros of Dirichlet
L
-function to prove a good estimate unconditionally of the second moments of Chebyshev function in arithmetic progressions (see Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
in section
3
for more details). Moreover, similar to the classical problem, we obtain an omega-result in the arithmetic progressions showing that the asymptotic formula is essentially the best possible.

Let
q_{1},q_{2}
be positive integers and
1\leq a_{1}<q_{1},1\leq a_{2}<q_{2}
be positive integers such that
(a_{1},q_{1})=1
,
(a_{2},q_{2})=1
. We consider the function

G(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+l=n\\
m\equiv a_{1}(q_{1})\\
l\equiv a_{2}(q_{2})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\Lambda(l),

whose summatory function is defined as

S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=\sum_{n\leq X}G(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2}).

We denote by
\chi
a Dirichlet character modulo
q
. Let
\rho_{\chi}
be the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\chi)
and let
B_{q}=\sup\{Re({\rho_{\chi}})\mid L(\rho_{\chi},\chi)=0\}
. We denote by
\widetilde{\beta}
the possible exceptional zero (or Siegel zero),
\widetilde{\chi}
the corresponding exceptional real character modulo
q
and
B_{q}^{\sharp}=\sup\{Re({\rho_{\chi}})\mid L(\rho_{\chi},\chi)=0,\rho_{\chi}\neq\widetilde{\beta}\}
.

In this article, we prove an asymptotic formula for
S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})
and an omega-result, which can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

(A)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{2})}H(X,q_{1},a_{1})+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{1})}H(X,q_{2},a_{2})+\mathcal{Z}(X,\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2})\\
+\mathcal{O}(X^{B^{*}_{q_{1}}+B^{*}_{q_{2}}}\log X\log(q_{1}X)\log(q_{2}X)),\end{split}

where the implicit constant is absolute and

\displaystyle H(X,q,a)=-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{X^{{\rho_{\chi}}+1}}{{\rho_{\chi}}({\rho_{\chi}}+1)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(X,\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi_{1}(q_{1})\\
\chi_{2}(q_{2})\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\overline{\chi}_{1}(a_{1})\overline{\chi}_{2}(a_{2})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{(\rho_{\chi_{1}},\rho_{\chi_{2}})\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\dfrac{\Gamma(\rho_{\chi_{1}})\Gamma(\rho_{\chi_{2}})}{\Gamma(\rho_{\chi_{1}}+\rho_{\chi_{2}}+1)}X^{\rho_{\chi_{1}}+\rho_{\chi_{2}}},

with

\mathcal{Z}_{0}=\{(\rho_{\chi_{1}},\rho_{\chi_{2}}):\rho_{\chi_{i}}\text{ is a non-trivial zero of }L(s,\chi_{i})\text{ and at least one of }\rho_{\chi_{1}},\rho_{\chi_{2}}\text{is a Siegel zero}\},

B^{*}_{q}=B^{*}_{q}(X)=\min(B_{q}^{\sharp},1-\eta),\hskip 85.35826pt\eta=\eta_{q}(X)=\dfrac{c_{1}(\varepsilon)}{\log q+(\log X)^{2/3}(\log\log X)^{1/3}}

,

with some small constant
c_{1}(\varepsilon)>0
.

(B)

We have

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{2})}H(X,q_{1},a_{1})+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{1})}H(X,q_{2},a_{2})+\mathcal{Z}(X,\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2})\\
+\Omega(X\log\log X),\end{split}

where the implicit constant is depends on
q_{1},q_{2}
.

Remark 1.4
.

For the unconditional order of magnitude of
H(X,q,a)
, we have

\displaystyle H(X,q,a)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{X^{{\rho_{\chi}}+1}}{{\rho_{\chi}}({\rho_{\chi}}+1)}

\displaystyle\ll\dfrac{X^{1+B_{q}}}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\sum_{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{1}{|{\rho_{\chi}}({\rho_{\chi}}+1)|}.

By using
[
1
, Lemma 3]
, we obtain

\displaystyle H(X,q,a)

\displaystyle\ll\dfrac{X^{1+B_{q}}}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\left(\log^{2}q+\delta_{1}(\chi)q^{1/2}\log^{2}q\right)\ll X^{1+B_{q}}\log^{2}q,

where
\delta_{1}(\chi)=1
if
\chi
is the exceptional character and
\delta_{1}(\chi)=0
otherwise.
Assuming GRH, we can obtain

H(X,q,a)\ll_{q}X^{3/2}.

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume GRH. Then we have

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{2})}H(X,q_{1},a_{1})+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{1})}H(X,q_{2},a_{2})\\
+\mathcal{O}(X\log X\log(q_{1}X)\log(q_{2}X)),\end{split}

where the implicit constant is absolute.

Remark 1.6
.

Assuming GRH with real zeros, we obtain the same result as Suzuki (
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
).

When
q_{1}=q_{2}=q
, we replace
S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})
by
S(X,q,a_{1},a_{2})
. Then we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2}
be integers with
(a_{1}a_{2},q)=1
, we have

\displaystyle S(X,q,a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi^{2}(q)}-\dfrac{1}{\varphi^{2}(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}(\overline{\chi}(a_{1})+\overline{\chi}(a_{2}))\sum_{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{X^{{\rho_{\chi}}+1}}{{\rho_{\chi}}({\rho_{\chi}}+1)}+\mathcal{Z}(X,\widetilde{\beta},\widetilde{\beta})+\mathcal{O}(X^{2B^{*}_{q}}\log X\log^{2}(qX)),

where the implicit constant is absolute.

This result improves
[
1
, Theorem 2]
where the main term is more explicit and the upper bound
\log^{5}(qX)
is improved by a factor
\frac{\log^{3}(qX)}{\log X}
.
When we take
q=1
, we recover the best known result for
S(X)
.

2.
Some calculations for the proof of Theorem
1.3

To prove the part (A) Theorem
1.3
, we use the circle method to compute the sum. Now, we put
z=re(\alpha)
, where
r=e^{-1/X}
and
e(\alpha):=e^{2\pi i\alpha}
,
|\alpha|\leq 1/2
. Let
q
be a positive integer,
1\leq a<q
be an integer coprime to
q
and
1\leq y\leq X
, let us consider a power series as was done by Hardy-Littlewood
[
10
]
.

\displaystyle F_{a,q}(z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
n\equiv a(q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(n)z^{n}\hskip 28.45274ptI_{q}(z):=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{n\geq 1}z^{n}

\displaystyle\widetilde{I_{q}}(z):=\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi}(a)}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{n\geq 1}n^{\widetilde{\beta}-1}z^{n}\hskip 28.45274ptI(y,z):=\sum_{n\leq y}z^{n}.

Our choice is natural because
F_{a,q}(z)
is a generating function of primes in arithmetic progression with additive property and we expect it to be close to
I_{q}(z)-\widetilde{I}_{q}(z)
by Dirichlet’s theorem.

We will show that

can be expressed in terms of
F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)
,
F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)
in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

We have

\displaystyle S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{2})}H(X,q_{1},a_{1})+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{1})}H(X,q_{2},a_{2})+\mathcal{Z}(X,\widetilde{\beta}_{1},\widetilde{\beta}_{2})

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(X\log X(\log(2q_{1})+\log(2q_{2}))+E(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2}),

where

(4)

\displaystyle E(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=\int^{1}_{0}\left(F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)-I_{q_{1}}(z)+\widetilde{I}_{q_{1}}(z)\right)\left(F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)-I_{q_{2}}(z)+\widetilde{I}_{q_{2}}(z)\right)I\left(X,\dfrac{1}{z}\right)d\alpha.

We now prove Proposition
2.1
.

We first note that

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)-I_{q_{1}}(z)+\widetilde{I}_{q_{1}}(z)\right)\left(F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)-I_{q_{2}}(z)+\widetilde{I}_{q_{2}}(z)\right)\\
&=F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)-F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)I_{q_{2}}(z)-F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)I_{q_{1}}(z)+I_{q_{1}}(z)I_{q_{2}}(z)\\
&+F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{2}}(z)+F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{1}}(z)-I_{q_{1}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{2}}(z)-I_{q_{2}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{1}}(z)+\widetilde{I}_{q_{1}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{2}}(z).\end{split}

From the definitions of
I_{q}(z)
,
\widetilde{I}_{q}(z)
, and
F_{a,q}(z)
, we calculate

\displaystyle F_{a_{1},q_{1}}(z)F_{a_{2},q_{2}}(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\\
m\equiv a_{1}(q_{1})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)z^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\
k\equiv a_{2}(q_{2})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(k)z^{k}=\sum_{n\geq 2}G(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})z^{n},

\displaystyle I_{q_{1}}(z)I_{q_{2}}(z)=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{n\geq 2}(n-1)z^{n},

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{q_{1}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{2}}(z)=\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{1}}(a_{1})\widetilde{\chi_{2}}(a_{2})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(\sum_{m+k=n}m^{\widetilde{\beta}_{2}-1}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{1}-1}\right)z^{n}.

Moreover, for
(i,j)\in S_{0}:=\{(1,2),(2,1)\}
we have

\displaystyle F_{a_{i},q_{i}}(z)I_{q_{j}}(z)=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{j})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,k\geq 1\\
m\equiv a_{i}(q_{i})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)z^{m+k}=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{j})}\sum_{n\geq 2}\psi(n-1,q_{i},a_{i})z^{n},

\displaystyle F_{a_{i},q_{i}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{j}}(z)=\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{j})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,k\geq 1\\
m\equiv a_{i}(q_{i})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}z^{m+k}=\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{j})}\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq n-1\\
m\equiv a_{i}(q_{i})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)(n-m)^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}\right)z^{n},

\displaystyle I_{q_{i}}(z)\widetilde{I}_{q_{j}}(z)=\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,k\geq 1\end{subarray}}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}z^{m+k}=\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{n\geq 2}\left(\sum_{k\leq n-1}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}\right)z^{n}.

where
\psi
is the Chebyshev function in arithmetic progression, defined as

\psi(t,q,a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq t\\
m\equiv a(q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m).

Hence, the right hand side of (
5
) can be rewritten as

\sum_{n\geq 2}B(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})z^{n},

with

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&B(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})=G(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})+\dfrac{n-1}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}+\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{1}}(a_{1})\widetilde{\chi_{2}}(a_{2})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\left(\sum_{m+k=n}m^{\widetilde{\beta}_{2}-1}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{1}-1}\right)\\

m\equiv a_{i}(q_{i})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)(n-m)^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}\right)-\sum_{(i,j)\in S_{0}}\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\left(\sum_{k\leq n-1}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}\right).\end{split}

So that

\displaystyle E(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{n}\sum_{m\leq X}B(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})r^{n}e(n\alpha)r^{-m}e(-m\alpha)d\alpha

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\sum_{m\leq X}B(n,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})r^{n-m}\int_{0}^{1}e((n-m)\alpha)d\alpha

\int_{0}^{1}e(t\alpha)d\alpha=1\text{ if $t=0$ and }0\text{ otherwise.}

Then we obtain the equation

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}S(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})&=E(X,q_{1},q_{2},a_{1},a_{2})-\sum_{n\leq X}\dfrac{n-1}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}-\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{1}}(a_{1})\widetilde{\chi_{2}}(a_{2})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{m+k=n}m^{\widetilde{\beta}_{2}-1}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{1}-1}\right)\\
&+\sum_{(i,j)\in S_{0}}\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q_{j})}\sum_{n\leq X}\psi(n-1,q_{i},a_{i})-\sum_{(i,j)\in S_{0}}\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{j})}\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq n-1\\
m\equiv a_{i}(q_{i})\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)(n-m)^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}\right)\\
&+\sum_{(i,j)\in S_{0}}\dfrac{\widetilde{\chi_{j}}(a_{j})}{\varphi(q_{1})\varphi(q_{2})}\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{k\leq n-1}k^{\widetilde{\beta}_{j}-1}\right).\end{split}

We now estimate the sums in equation (
7
). We start from the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
0<\beta<1
and
0<x<y
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{x<n\leq y}n^{\beta-1}=\dfrac{y^{\beta}-x^{\beta}}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(1).

Consequence, for
X\geq 2
and
1/2<\beta,\beta_{1},\beta_{2}<1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{k\leq n-1}k^{\beta-1}\right)=\dfrac{X^{\beta+1}}{\beta(\beta+1)}+\mathcal{O}(X),

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{m+k=n}m^{\beta_{2}-1}k^{\beta_{1}-1}\right)=\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta_{1})\Gamma(\beta_{2})}{\Gamma(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1)}X^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}+\mathcal{O}(X).

Proof.

If
f
is defined on
[1,x]
monotonic and continuous, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)=\int_{1}^{x}f(t)dt+\mathcal{O}(f(x)+f(1)).

Then we obtain,

\displaystyle\sum_{x<n\leq y}n^{\beta-1}=\int_{x}^{y}t^{\beta-1}dt+\mathcal{O}(x^{\beta-1}+y^{\beta-1})=\dfrac{y^{\beta}-x^{\beta}}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(1),

since
0<\beta<1
.
Using this formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq n-1}k^{\beta-1}=\dfrac{(n-1)^{\beta}-1}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(1)=\dfrac{n^{\beta}}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{k\leq n-1}k^{\beta-1}\right)=\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\dfrac{n^{\beta}}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}(1)\right)=\dfrac{X^{\beta+1}}{\beta(\beta+1)}+\mathcal{O}(X),

since
1/2<\beta<1
.

Similarly, we can prove the remaining formula in this lemma.

\displaystyle\sum_{m+k=n}m^{\beta_{2}-1}k^{\beta_{1}-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq n-1}(n-k)^{\beta_{2}-1}k^{\beta_{1}-1}=\int_{1}^{n-1}(n-t)^{\beta_{2}-1}t^{\beta_{1}-1}dt+\mathcal{O}((n-1)^{\beta_{1}-1}+(n-1)^{\beta_{2}-1})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{n}(n-t)^{\beta_{2}-1}t^{\beta_{1}-1}dt+\mathcal{O}(1)=n^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}-1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{\beta_{2}-1}t^{\beta_{1}-1}dt+\mathcal{O}(1)

\displaystyle=n^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}-1}\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta_{1})\Gamma(\beta_{2})}{\Gamma(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})}+\mathcal{O}(1).

Hence, by (
8
) one has

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{m+k=n}m^{\beta_{2}-1}k^{\beta_{1}-1}\right)=\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta_{1})\Gamma(\beta_{2})}{\Gamma(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+1)}X^{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}}+\mathcal{O}(X).

∎

We now study an explicit formula for the average of
\psi(t,q,a)

\displaystyle\psi(t,q,a)=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\psi(t,\chi),

\displaystyle\psi(t,\chi):=\sum_{n\leq t}\chi(n)\Lambda(n).

Lemma 2.3
.

For a primitive Dirichlet character

\displaystyle\psi(t,\chi)=\delta_{0}(\chi)t-\sum_{{1<\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}}\dfrac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}}{\rho_{\chi}}-\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq 1}}\dfrac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}-1}{\rho_{\chi}}+R(t,T,\chi)

\chi

[

Lemma 2.5

For

(a,q)=1

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\psi(n-1,q,a)=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi(q)}-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{X^{\rho_{\chi}+1}}{\rho_{\chi}(\rho_{\chi}+1)}+\mathcal{O}(X\log(2q)\log(X)).

Proof.

We first remark that

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\psi(n-1,q,a)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
n\equiv a(q)\end{subarray}}(X-n)\Lambda(n)=\int_{1}^{X}\psi(t,q,a)dt+\mathcal{O}(\psi(X,q,a)).

Then from Lemma
2.3
and the formula (

), we obtain for

\displaystyle\psi(t,q,a)=\dfrac{t}{\varphi(q)}-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}}\dfrac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}}{\rho_{\chi}}+\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq 1}}\dfrac{1}{\rho_{\chi}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\log(2q)\log t+\dfrac{t}{T}\log^{2}(tqT)\right).

This leads to the following

(12)

\displaystyle\psi(t,q,a)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{t}{\varphi(q)}-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}}\dfrac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}}{\rho_{\chi}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\log(2q)\log t+\dfrac{t}{T}\log^{2}(tqT)\right),

since there is at most one exceptional character mod
q
.

Substituting (
12
) into (

), we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\psi(n-1,q,a)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{X^{2}}{2\varphi(q)}-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}\dfrac{X^{\rho_{\chi}+1}}{\rho_{\chi}(\rho_{\chi}+1)}+\mathcal{O}\left(X\log(2q)\log X+\dfrac{X^{2}}{T}\log^{2}(qX)\right),

since
\psi(X,q,a)\ll X
.
Therefore Lemma
2.5
is proved when we choose

tending to infinity.
∎

Now we study the the following sum.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
1/2<\beta<1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq n-1\\
m\equiv a(q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)(n-m)^{\beta-1}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{X^{\beta+1}}{\beta(\beta+1)}-\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\rho_{\chi})}{\Gamma(\beta+\rho_{\chi}+1)}X^{\rho_{\chi}+\beta}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\left(X\log(2q)\log X\right).

Proof.

We have

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq n-1\\
m\equiv a(q)\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)(n-m)^{\beta-1}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\int_{1}^{X}\psi(t,\chi)(X-t)^{\beta-1}dt+\mathcal{O}(X).

Using Lemma
2.3
, we calculate the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{X}\left(\delta_{0}(\chi)t-\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}}\dfrac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}}{\rho_{\chi}}+\sum_{|\gamma_{\chi}|\leq 1}\dfrac{1}{\rho_{\chi}}\right)(X-t)^{\beta-1}dt+\mathcal{O}\left(\left(\log(2q)\log X+\dfrac{X\log^{2}(XqT)}{T}\right)\int_{1}^{X}(X-t)^{\beta-1}dt\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{0}(\chi)\dfrac{X^{\beta+1}}{\beta(\beta+1)}-\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}}\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\rho_{\chi})}{\Gamma(\beta+\rho_{\chi})}\dfrac{X^{\rho_{\chi}+\beta}}{\rho_{\chi}+\beta}+\sum_{|\gamma_{\chi}|\leq 1}\dfrac{1}{\rho_{\chi}}\dfrac{X^{\beta}}{\beta}+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{X^{\beta}}{\beta}\left(\log(2q)\log X+\dfrac{X\log^{2}(XqT)}{T}\right)\right).

Then we can infer the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\dfrac{X^{\beta+1}}{\beta(\beta+1)}-\dfrac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi(q)}\overline{\chi}(a)\sum_{{\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T}}\dfrac{\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(\rho_{\chi})}{\Gamma(\beta+\rho_{\chi})}\dfrac{X^{\rho_{\chi}+\beta}}{\rho_{\chi}+\beta}+\mathcal{O}\left(\dfrac{X^{\beta}}{\beta}\left(\log(2q)\log X+\dfrac{X\log^{2}(XqT)}{T}\right)\right).

Hence, this Lemma is completed when we choose

tending to infinity.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a non-trivial zero of

\displaystyle\sum_{\mid\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\mid>1}\dfrac{1}{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|(1+|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|)}\ll\dfrac{\log(q|\gamma_{\chi}|)\log(|\gamma_{\chi}|)}{|\gamma_{\chi}|},

\chi

Proof.

Firstly, to prove (

Case 1:
If

. Then we have

|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\geq|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|-|\gamma_{\chi}|>\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|}{2}.

]

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\

|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|>2|\gamma_{\chi}|\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|^{2}}\ll\dfrac{\log(q|\gamma_{\chi}|)}{|\gamma_{\chi}|}.

Case 2:
If
|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|<\dfrac{1}{2}|\gamma_{\chi}|
. Then we have

|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\geq|\gamma_{\chi}|-|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|>\dfrac{|\gamma_{\chi}|}{2}.

So we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\

Case 3:
If
\dfrac{1}{2}|\gamma_{\chi}|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\leq 2|\gamma_{\chi}|
, we have

|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\leq|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|+|\gamma_{\chi}|\leq 3|\gamma_{\chi}|.

This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\
|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\leq 2|\gamma_{\chi}|\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|(1+|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|)}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\
|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\leq 3|\gamma_{\chi}|\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|(1+|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\
|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|(1+|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\
1<|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|\leq 3|\gamma_{\chi}|\end{subarray}}\dfrac{1}{|\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|(1+|\gamma_{\chi}-\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}|)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}_{\chi}\\

By using Lemma

, we can estimate the last sum to be

\displaystyle\ll\dfrac{\log(q|\gamma_{\chi}|)}{|\gamma_{\chi}|}\left(1+\sum_{1<n\leq 3|\gamma_{\chi}|}\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\ll\dfrac{\log(q|\gamma_{\chi}|)\log(|\gamma_{\chi}|)}{|\gamma_{\chi}|}.

So we obtain the stated result. We can also prove (

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
q\geq 1
and
t\geq 1
, we have

\biggl|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma_{\chi}|\leq 1\\
\rho_{\chi}\neq\widetilde{\beta}\end{subarray}}\frac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}-1}{\rho_{\chi}}\biggr|\ll t^{B_{q}^{\ast}}\log(2q).

Proof.

When

and
\rho_{\chi}\neq\widetilde{\beta}
, we just have

\frac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}-1}{\rho_{\chi}}\ll t^{\beta_{\chi}}+1\ll t^{B_{q}^{\ast}}.

When

and so

, we have

where the contour of integration is the line segment from

,
and then

\displaystyle\frac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}-1}{\rho_{\chi}}

\displaystyle\leq(\log t)\sup_{0\leq\sigma\leq\beta_{\chi}}t^{\beta_{\chi}}\ll t^{\beta_{\chi}}(\log t)\leq t^{\frac{1}{4}}(\log t)\ll t^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll t^{B_{q}^{\ast}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

By Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{a,q}(x)&\ll\dfrac{1}{\varphi^{2}(q)}\int_{0}^{x}\sum_{\chi(q)}|\overline{\chi}(a)|^{2}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq T\end{subarray}}\dfrac{t^{\rho_{\chi}}}{\rho_{\chi}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mid\gamma_{\chi}\mid\leq 1\\

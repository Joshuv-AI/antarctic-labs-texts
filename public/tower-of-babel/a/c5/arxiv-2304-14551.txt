Local limit theorems for random walks on nilpotent Lie groups
Timothée Bénard and Emmanuel Breuillard

Abstract
We establish the (non-lattice) local limit theorem for products of i.i.d. random variables on an arbitrary simply connected nilpotent Lie group G G , where the variables are allowed to be non-centered. Our result also improves on the known centered case by proving uniformity for two-sided moderate deviations and allowing measures with a moment of order 2 ​ ( dim G ) 2 2(\dim G)^{2} without further regularity assumptions. As applications we establish a Ratner-type equidistribution theorem for unipotent walks on homogeneous spaces and obtain a new proof of the Choquet-Deny property in our setting.

Introduction

Given a Lie group
G
and a probability measure
\mu
on
G
, we may ask whether there are sequences
a_{n}>0
and
g_{n}\in G
such that the measures

a_{n}\,\mu^{*n}*\delta_{g_{n}}

(1)

converge weakly towards some non-trivial Radon measure on
G
. The measure
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{g_{n}}
is the law of the random product
X_{1}\ldots X_{n}g_{n}
, where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent random variables with law
\mu
. An estimate of this kind is called a
local (or local central) limit theorem
(LLT for short) on
G
. Such results are known for various kinds of Lie groups (see the historical remarks at the end of the introduction in
Section

1.4
). In this paper we shall answer this question affirmatively for the class of simply connected nilpotent Lie groups. These groups are defined algebraically as those simply connected real Lie groups
G
such that
G^{[s+1]}=\{1\}
for some finite integer
s
, where
G^{[i+1]}:=[G,G^{[i]}]
,
G^{[1]}=G
, is the central descending series. They can also be characterized precisely as the connected subgroups of unipotent upper triangular matrices (see
[
CG90
, Theorem 1.1.1]
).

In the abelian case
G=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the local limit theorem is a classical result
[
GK68
,
Bre92
]
. Given a sum of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued independent random variables
S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}
with common law
\mu
, drift
\chi=\mathbb{E}(X_{1})
and covariance the identity, we have as
n\to+\infty
,

(2\pi n)^{d/2}\,\mathbb{P}(S_{n}-n\chi\in B)=|B|+o_{\mu,B}(1),

(2)

where
B
is any box
B=\prod_{i=1}^{d}[s_{i},t_{i}]
of Euclidean volume
|B|
,
provided
\mu
is non-lattice (i.e. the support of
\mu
is not contained in a coset of a proper closed subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
). A uniform version of the local limit theorem is also available
[
Sto65
]
:

\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathbb{P}(S_{n}-n\chi\in B+x)-(\sqrt{n}_{\star}\mathscr{N})(B+x)|=o_{\mu,B}(n^{-d/2}),

(3)

where
\sqrt{n}_{\star}\mathscr{N}
is the push-forward by the homothety
x\mapsto\sqrt{n}\,x
of the standard Gaussian distribution
\mathscr{N}:=(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2}}dx
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In the context of non-commutative nilpotent Lie groups, the question of a local limit theorem has been investigated by the second-named author
[
Bre05b
]
and by Diaconis and Hough
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
,
under the assumption that the measure
\mu
is centered
in the abelianization
G/[G,G]
. Both
[
Bre05b
]
and
[
DH21
]
deal with the case where the ambient group is the Heisenberg group, that is the simplest instance of a non-commutative nilpotent Lie group, while
[
Hou19
]
contains a generalization to arbitrary simply connected nilpotent Lie groups. As in
[
Bre05b
,
Hou19
]
, we will focus on the
non-lattice
(equivalently aperiodic) case: we work under the assumption (see the remarks in
Section

1.4
) that the projection
\mu_{ab}
of
\mu
under the abelianization map
G\to G/[G,G]
is not supported on a coset of a proper closed subgroup of
G/[G,G]\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Example 1

(Heisenberg group)
.

Let
G
be the
3
-dimensional Heisenberg group, i.e.
\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the group structure
x*y=(x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2},x_{3}+y_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})
. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
whose support is compact and has non-lattice projection in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Assume that
\mu
is
centered
in the sense that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x_{1})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x_{2})=0
. It was shown in
[
Bre05b
]
that for every compactly supported continuous function
f\in C_{c}(G)
, as
N\to+\infty
,

N^{2}\mu^{*N}(f)\rightarrow c_{\mu}\int_{G}fdx

(4)

where
c_{\mu}>0
only depends on
\mu
, and where
dx
refers to the Lebesgue measure (or Haar measure) on
G
.

As in the abelian case, a uniform version of the LLT is also available. For
r>0
, set
D_{r}:x\mapsto(x_{1}r,x_{2}r,x_{3}r^{2})
.
There exists a smooth probability measure
\nu
on
G
such that for every
f\in C_{c}(G)
, for
N\geq 1
,

\mu^{*N}(f)=D_{\sqrt{N}}(\nu)(f)+o_{\mu,f}(1)N^{-2}

(5)

where the term
o_{\mu,f}(1)
goes to
0
uniformly in the multiplicative translates of
f
, i.e. for the family of test functions
f_{g,h}:x\mapsto f(gxh)
where
g,h\in G
. This was shown in
[
Bre05b
]
(one sided uniformity only) where the two-sided uniformity required an additional Cramér assumption on
\mu
, an assumption subsequently lifted in
[
DH21
]
.

Note that
eq.

5
implies that
\nu
is unique and coincides with the limiting measure in the central limit theorem, in other terms
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N})\rightarrow\nu
weakly (see
[
CR78
,
BB23
]
). Combining (
4
) and (
5
) we see that
c_{\mu}=u(0)
, where
u
is the (smooth) density of
\nu
with respect to
dx
. This density is nowhere-vanishing. In fact
\nu
corresponds to the law of the value at time
t=1
of a hypoelliptic diffusion
(B^{(1)}_{t},B^{(2)}_{t},L_{t})
on
G
, where
(B^{(1)}_{t},B^{(2)}_{t})
is a planar Brownian motion and
L_{t}
is its Lévy area (see e.g.
[
FV10
]
). A well-known formula due to P. Lévy
[
Lév51
]
allows to compute the Fourier transform of
\nu
(see
[
Gav77
, Théroème 1]
,
[
BB23
, Example 2]
), from which it follows that:

c_{\mu}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{|\det{\Cov_{\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

where
\Cov_{\mu}(x_{1},x_{2})
is the covariance matrix of the pair
(x_{1},x_{2})
distributed according to
\mu_{ab}
. Note that this is insensitive to the mean of the central coordinate
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x_{3})
.

The proof of the LLT for centered walks on the Heisenberg group obtained in
[
Bre05b
]
relied on Fourier methods combined with certain spectral gap estimates for infinite dimensional irreducible unitary representations of the Heisenberg group. In a recent breakthrough
[
DH21
]
, Diaconis and Hough came up with a new idea that allowed them to bypass these estimates. They exploit the exchangeability of the random product under permutations of the variables in order to produce further smoothing in the central coordinate. This yields the desired decay of the Fourier transform.
With this new “path-swapping” technique they could obtain a new proof of the LLT for the Heisenberg group with improved (i.e. double-sided) uniformity. Quite remarkably, Hough
[
Hou19
]
was able to further these ideas and managed to prove the LLT for non-lattice centered walks on arbitrary nilpotent Lie groups. He also obtained the uniform LLT under the additional assumption that
\mu
satisfies a strong form of aperiodicity (Cramér condition).

In the first part of this paper, we push the Diaconis-Hough path-swapping method one step further and show how it can be adapted to prove the local limit theorem on nilpotent Lie groups for
non-centered
(we also say
biased
) walks as well. Our result is the first of this kind, as all previous works on the nilpotent LLT assume the random walk to be centered. It is worth pointing out that the study of random walks on a non-abelian nilpotent Lie group does not reduce to the centered case. More precisely, after recentering, such a random walk may be seen as a process with centered but
time-dependent
driving measure. There are at least two significant consequences. First, the distribution
\mu^{*N}
spreads at a faster rate around its average than in the centered case. In fact, the large scale geometry of
\mu^{*N}
is not captured by the descending central series (as for centered walks) but by some other filtration with smaller ideals, which depends on the increment average. We will need to work both with the descending central series (to exploit the bracket structure of
G
) and with this new filtration. The second novelty appearing in the non-centered case is that the limiting measure in the CLT is typically not of full support (even though it is absolutely continuous). This is a new phenomenon that came as a surprise in our previous work
[
BB23
]
and forces some changes in the very formulation of the LLT in the non-centered case. In fact, the recentering sequence
g_{n}
needs to be fine-tuned to remain within the range of the support of the limiting measure for the LLT to be meaningful, see
Corollary

1.5
below.

The limit theorems proven in this paper are the following.
Theorem

1.2
is the local limit theorem for non-lattice nilpotent random walks. It allows the driving measure to be non-centered. It also provides two-sided moderate uniformity without a Cramér assumption, which is new even for centered walks, and useful in applications. An effective nilpotent LLT with better approximation rate but regularity conditions on the test function is also given in
Theorem

1.3
. We then deduce
Corollary

1.5
, which answers affirmatively the question raised above at
Equation

1
for non-lattice driving measures. For centered walks, we also manage to show a uniform limit theorem for ratios,
Corollary

1.6
. Such estimates are new even in the Heisenberg case. The proof of
Theorem

1.2
and
Corollary

1.5
unifies both the centered and the non-centered cases and is valid for any non-lattice probability measure on
G
with a finite moment of high enough order, say
2(\dim G)^{2}
. This moment condition also improves on
[
Bre05b
,
DH21
,
Hou19
]
. A truncated version of the uniform LLT and its effective form is also established under a moment of order
2+\varepsilon
(
Theorem

3.1
,
Section

3
).

The second part of the paper is devoted to two applications of our LLT for biased walks. The first,
Corollary

1.7
, gives a new proof of
the Choquet-Deny theorem
for measures with finite moments on nilpotent Lie groups. This asserts that bounded
\mu
-harmonic functions are constant, a result proven by Guivarc’h in his thesis
[
Gui73
]
which extends the classical Choquet-Deny theorem
[
CD60
]
on abelian groups. The second application,
Corollary

1.8
, concerns the equidistribution of unipotent random walks on homogeneous spaces. In an unpublished chapter of the second author’s thesis
[
Bre04
, chapter 2]
, a
probabilistic Ratner’s theorem
was shown asserting that
\mu^{*n}*\delta_{x}
weakly converges towards Ratner’s invariant measure in any finite volume homogeneous space of a connected Lie group, under the assumption that
\mu
is a finitely supported symmetric measure whose support generates a unipotent subgroup. Results of this type have since known a number of extensions to non-unipotent settings, in particular to subgroups whose Zariski-closure is semisimple, as in the breakthrough work of Benoist-Quint
[
BQ11
]
(see also
[
Bénar
]
). As a consequence of our LLT, we can now prove the equidistribution of random walks driven by an arbitrary, possibly biased, probability measure with finite third moment and supported on a unipotent subgroup.

We now give a detailed presentation of our main results. In the paper
G
will denote a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra. The step (or nilpotency class) of
\mathfrak{g}
is the smallest integer
s
such that
G^{[s+1]}=\{1\}
, where
G^{[i]}:=[G,G^{[i-1]}]
is the central descending series. In the sequel
G
will be identified with
\mathfrak{g}
via the exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
. This allows to see the Lie product
*
as a composition law on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Abusing notation we will continue to denote by
*
the associated convolution product of measures on
\mathfrak{g}
. Observe that
*
is in fact a polynomial map on
\mathfrak{g}
as follows from the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula
[
Dyn47
]
:

x*y=x+y+\frac{1}{2}[x,y]+\frac{1}{12}[x,[x,y]]+\frac{1}{12}[y,[y,x]]+\dots

which contains only finitely many non-vanishing terms as
G
is assumed nilpotent.

Given a probability measure
\mu
on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
we consider the random walk
S_{N}=X_{1}*\ldots*X_{N}
where the
X_{i}
’s are independent with law
\mu
. We denote by
\mu_{ab}
the projection of
\mu
onto the abelianization
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}:=\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Throughout the text, we assume that
\mu_{ab}
has finite first moment and is not supported on an affine hyperplane. We also set
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})\in\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
. Where further moment or aperiodicity assumptions are required they will be specified.

1.1
The central limit theorem

Before discussing the local limit theorem, we need to recall the central limit theorem (or CLT for short) for the random product
S_{N}
, in particular inasmuch as its formulation in the non-centered case requires the introduction of a subtle rescaling as we discovered in
[
BB23
]
. The large scale behaviour of
S_{N}
on the Lie group is captured by a certain decreasing sequence of ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
whose definition depends only on
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})\in\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
,

\mathfrak{g}=:\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(t+1)}=\{0\},

where
[\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i+j)}
for all
i,j
. In the centered case, i.e. when
\overline{X}_{\mu}=0
, the filtration coincides with the descending central series, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}:=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{[i-1]}]
and
t=s
. In general, the filtration is defined by induction, setting
\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(i-1)}]+[\overline{X}_{\mu},\mathfrak{g}^{(i-2)}]
. We call
(\mathfrak{g}^{(i)})_{i\leq 2s-1}
the
weight filtration
associated to
\overline{X}_{\mu}
.

It is shown in
[
BB23
]
that for any choice of vector subspaces
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}
, the projection of
S_{N}
to
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
(modulo the sum of the other
\mathfrak{m}^{(j)}
) spreads at a rate
N^{i/2}
around its average. Renormalizing accordingly, the law converges weakly and we obtain the central limit theorem (CLT). More precisely, let

\displaystyle\pi^{(i)}:\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi^{(i)}(x)=x^{(i)}

be the coordinate projections induced by the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i}\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
. Set
X_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(x^{(1)})
and observe
X_{\mu}
corresponds to
\overline{X}_{\mu}
via the identification
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}\equiv\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
induced by
\pi^{(1)}
. Introduce an associated one-parameter family of
dilations

(D_{r})_{r>0}
by setting

D_{r}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},\,\,\,D_{r}(x)=\sum_{i\geq 1}r^{i}x^{(i)}.

(6)

The central limit theorem
[
BB23
, Theorem 1.1]
states that if
\mu
has finite second moment for the weight filtration, then we have the convergence in law

D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})\to\nu

(7)

where
\nu
is a certain probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
with a smooth density. Here, one says that
\mu
has finite
m
-th moment for the weight filtration if the map
x\mapsto\|x^{(i)}\|^{m/i}
is in
L^{1}(\mu)
for any
i
(we take some norm, say a Euclidean norm, on each
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
). We note in passing that since
\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
and the inclusion may be strict, this condition is
weaker
than the ordinary notion of moment on a nilpotent Lie group. The ordinary notion can be defined in the same way via the central descending series rather than the weight filtration; equivalently it means that the distance to the identity (for some, or any, left invariant riemannian metric) has a finite
m
-th moment.

The CLT also involves a description of the limiting measure
\nu
as the value at time
t=1
of a certain explicit hypoelliptic diffusion process
(\sigma_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. The infinitesimal generator of
(\sigma_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
is time-dependent, and left invariant for a new group structure
*^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, inherited from the choice of subspaces
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
. This new group structure is defined by the formula

a*^{\prime}b=\lim_{t\to+\infty}D_{1/t}(D_{t}(a)*D_{t}(b)).

(8)

With this new Lie structure,
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
is again an
s
-step nilpotent Lie group and dilations act as automorphisms. The semigroup
(\sigma_{t})_{t>0}
is then defined (as in
[
Hun56
]
) by way of its infinitesimal generator

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\left(\exp(ta_{X_{\mu}})E_{i}\right)^{2}+\exp(ta_{X_{\mu}})B_{\mu}

(9)

where
E_{i},B_{\mu}
are left invariant vectors fields on
(\mathfrak{g},*^{\prime})
such that the
E_{i}
’s form a basis of
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
in which the covariance matrix of
\mu_{ab}
is the identity, and
B_{\mu}=E_{\mu}(x^{(2)})\in\mathfrak{m}^{(2)}
. Here

a_{X_{\mu}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the nilpotent operator defined by

a_{X_{\mu}}(y)=\pi^{(i+2)}([X_{\mu},y])\,\textnormal{ for }y\in\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}.

(10)

The properties of the limiting measure
\nu=\sigma_{1}
are studied in
[
VSCC92
]
in the centered case and
[
BB23
]
in general. It is proven that
\nu=v(x)dx
where
v
is a smooth and rapidly decaying (Schwartz) function on
\mathfrak{g}
. The measure
\nu
is an ordinary Gaussian distribution on
\mathfrak{g}
(in the Euclidean sense) if and only if
[\overline{X}_{\mu},\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}]=[\overline{X}_{\mu},\mathfrak{g}^{[i+1]}]
for every
i\leq s
, which corresponds exactly to situations where the eigenvalues of the renormalization
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}}
are odd negative powers of
\sqrt{n}
, see
[
BB23
, Theorem 1.4]
. Usually there is no explicit formula for
v(x)
nor its Fourier transform (the case of the Heisenberg group
[
Gav77
, Théorème 1]
is an exception). However, satisfactory estimates hold when
\nu
arises from a centered walk: there exist
A,\alpha>0
such that

\alpha\exp(-A|x|^{2})\leq v(x)\leq A\exp(-\alpha|x|^{2})

where
|x|
denotes the distance to the origin for some (any) left-invariant Riemannian metric on
(\mathfrak{g},*)
.
Apart from the centered case, or when the group is abelian or of nilpotency class
2
and certain exceptions (such as the full group of unipotent upper triangular matrices), it is usually the case that the support of the density
\nu
is a
proper
closed subset of
\mathfrak{g}
, see
[
BB23
, Section 3.3.2]
. This came as a surprise to us.

1.2
The non-centered local limit theorems

We are now in a position to state the main results of this paper.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}
be the integer:

d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\dim\mathfrak{g}^{(i)},

where
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}
is the
weight filtration
introduced above.

We call
d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}
the
homogeneous dimension of the
\mu
-random walk
, or more precisely of the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}_{\mu}
. Note that
d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}
is related to the determinant of the dilations:
\det D_{r}=r^{d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}}
. When
\mu
is centered,
d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}
coincides with the homogeneous dimension of
\mathfrak{g}
, namely
d_{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\dim\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}
, which controls the volume growth of balls in
G
by the Bass-Guivarc’h formula
[
Gui73
,
Bre14
]
. We further note that
d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}\geq d_{\mathfrak{g}}
with equality if and only if the weight filtration coincides with the descending central series, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{g}^{[i]}
for all
i
.

In the next theorem we assume that the projection
\mu_{ab}
is
non-lattice
; this means that its support is not contained in a translate of a proper closed additive subgroup of
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Non-centered uniform LLT)
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
with finite moment of order
m_{\mu}>d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}+2
for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
, and such that
\mu_{ab}
is non-lattice. Then for every continuous and compactly supported function
f
on
\mathfrak{g}
, for all
N\geq 1
,

\displaystyle|(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})(f)-(D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu)(f)|=o_{\mu,f}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}}),

(11)

where
\nu
is the limit measure from the central limit theorem (
7
), and
d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}
is the homogeneous dimension of the
\mu
-random walk.

Moreover for
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small enough, the error rate in (
11
) is uniform in all the bideviations
\delta_{g}*f*\delta_{h}:x\mapsto f(g*x*h)
for
g,h\in\mathfrak{g}
, provided that either
g
or
h
belongs to

\mathcal{E}_{N,\varepsilon_{0}}:=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\,:\,\|x^{(i)}\|\leq N^{\frac{i}{2}+\varepsilon_{0}}\}+[-N^{1+\varepsilon_{0}},N^{1+\varepsilon_{0}}]X_{\mu}.

In
[
Bre05b
]
this result was proven for the Heisenberg group in the special case of a centered
\mu
of compact support. As discussed above, Diaconis and Hough
[
DH21
]
gave a new proof in the centered Heisenberg case. Later, Hough
[
Hou19
]
obtained the LLT for centered walks on arbitrary nilpotent Lie groups. His result allows deviations of the test function
f
only under the further assumption that
\mu_{ab}
satisfies the Cramér condition, which is a strong form of aperiodicity typically concerning spread out measures, or discrete measures with good diophantine properties (see
[
Bre05a
,
AP17
]
). We allow deviations without Cramér assumption and this is important for our applications in
Section

5
.

Theorem

1.2
is the first of its kind to allow the measure
\mu
to be non-centered. The proof is again via characteristic functions. It is inspired by the path swap technique used by Diaconis and Hough in
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
. Since their method was tailored to work for the bracket structure of the descending central series, we will need extra work to obtain results related to the weight filtration (they usually do not coincide in the non-centered case).

We also note that we improve on the moment assumption compared to
[
Bre05b
,
DH21
,
Hou19
]
even in the centered case. This improvement is performed by truncating suitably the measure
\mu
.
Theorem

1.2
will in fact be a corollary of a local limit theorem for a truncated version of
\mu
, namely
Theorem

3.1
, available under the sole condition that
\mu
has a finite moment of order
2+\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
. This truncated formulation will turn out useful for applications later.

Remark
. In the
centered Heisenberg
case, the LLT proved by Diaconis and Hough in
[
DH21
]
is uniform in all deviations
g,h\in G
, and also allows the measure to be lattice. We do not know whether this extra uniformity holds in the setting of
Theorem

1.2
. In the Heisenberg case, it relies on the observation that the
L^{1}
-norm of the Fourier transform of a test function is constant under two-sided deviation, but this is not true in a general nilpotent group. The LLT for lattice measures on a general nilpotent Lie group is also still open. It requires a more delicate analysis that has not been worked out yet, even in the centered case (with the notable exception of centered walks on discrete nilpotent groups by work of Alexopoulos
[
Ale02c
]
, see
Section

1.4
).

If
f
is in
L^{1}(\mathfrak{g})
and its Fourier transform decays fast enough, we also obtain an effective local limit theorem with power saving. In other words, we make explicit the dependency on
f
in the error term, and improve the rate of approximation from
N^{-\frac{1}{2}d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}}
to
N^{-\frac{1}{2}(d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}+1-\varepsilon)}
. To do so,
given
c\in(0,1)
,
L>1
, we introduce the function
gap_{c}:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\rightarrow(0,c)
given by

gap_{c}(R)=c\inf\{1-|\widehat{\mu}_{ab}(\xi^{\prime})|\,:\,\xi^{\prime}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{ab},\,c\leq\|\xi^{\prime}\|\leq c^{-1}(1+R)\}

and we define the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mu_{ab},c,L}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|(1+\|\xi\|)^{L}gap_{c}(\|\xi\|)^{-L}d\xi.

Here
\widehat{\mu}_{ab}
,
\widehat{f}
refer respectively to the Fourier transforms of
\mu_{ab}
and
f
.

Theorem 1.3

(Effective LLT with power saving)
.

Keep the assumptions of
Theorem

1.2
, suppose also
m_{\mu}\geq(d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}+4)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
.
There exist constants
c=c(\mu)\in(0,1)
,
C=C(\mu,\varepsilon)>1
,
L=L(\dim\mathfrak{g},\varepsilon)>1
such that
for every continuous integrable function
f
on
\mathfrak{g}
, every
N\geq 1
, we have

|(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})(f)-(D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu)(f)|\,\leq\,C\|\delta_{g}*f*\delta_{h}\|_{\mu_{ab},c,L}\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}(d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}+1-\varepsilon)}

for every
g,h
in
\mathcal{E}_{N,1/L}
.

Following the terminology introduced in
[
Bre05a
]
in the case when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we will say that the measure
\mu
on
\mathfrak{g}
is
\kappa
-diophantine
if
\mu_{ab}
is
\kappa
-diophantine in the sense of
[
Bre05a
, Definition 7.1]
, namely if there is
c>0
such that

\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu_{ab}}(\{\xi(x)+t\}^{2})\geq\frac{c}{\|\xi\|^{\kappa}}

for all large enough
\xi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}_{ab}}
, where
\{\cdot\}
denotes the distance to the nearest integer. This condition is easily seen to be equivalent to the requirement that for some (or each)
c>0
there is
c^{\prime}>0
such that for all
R>0

gap_{c}(R)\geq\frac{c^{\prime}}{(1+R)^{\kappa}},

a condition studied in
[
AP17
]
under the name of
weak Cramér condition
in the classical setting when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. When this condition holds, Theorem
1.3
becomes:

Corollary 1.4

(LLT under a diophantine condition)
.

Keep the assumptions of
Theorem

1.2
, suppose also
m_{\mu}\geq(d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}+4)
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
\kappa>0
and assume that
\mu
is
\kappa
-diophantine. Then there are constants
C=C(\mu,\varepsilon)>1
and
L=L(\dim\mathfrak{g},\varepsilon)>1
such that
for every continuous integrable function
f
on
\mathfrak{g}
, every
N\geq 1
, we have

|(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}})(f)-(D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu)(f)|\,\leq\,CS_{L(1+\kappa)}(\delta_{g}*f*\delta_{h})\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}(d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}+1-\varepsilon)}

for every
g,h
in
\mathcal{E}_{N,1/L}
.

Here
S_{L}
denotes the Sobolev norm:

S_{L}(f):=\|f\|_{L^{1}}+\int_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|(1+\|\xi\|)^{L}d\xi.

It is worth observing that the diophantine condition on which the quality of the error depends is expressed only in terms of the projection of
\mu
to the abelianization
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
. This feature is common to many other situations regarding equidistribution in nilpotent Lie groups, such as in
[
GT12
]
.

The local limit theorem reduces the estimation of
\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}}(f)
to that of
D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu(f)
. The latter is easier to understand, because the limit measure
\nu
has a smooth and rapidly decaying density as we have discussed. However
\nu
does not always have full support. In particular if the density of
\nu
vanishes at the origin, the LLT in the form of (
11
) does not provide meaningful asymptotics for the decay of
\mu^{*N}*\delta_{-NX_{\mu}}(f)
, but only an upper bound in
o_{\mu,f}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}})
. One way to remedy this is to use the uniformity with respect to deviations in our LLT in order to change the recentering appropriately as follows. Recall that
\nu
has a smooth density
v(x)\geq 0
on
\mathfrak{g}
. We have:

Corollary 1.5

(LLT with variable recentering)
.

Keep the assumptions of
Theorem

1.2
, let
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
and set
g_{N}=-NX_{\mu}*-D_{\sqrt{N}}Y
. Then for every continuous and compactly supported function
f
on
\mathfrak{g}
we have:

N^{\frac{1}{2}d_{\overline{X}_{\mu}}}\,(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{g_{N}})(f)\underset{N\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}v(Y)\int_{\mathfrak{g}}f(x)dx.

In particular this answers positively the question asked in the introduction as the quantity in
Equation

1
converges to a non-trivial multiple of the Haar measure on
G
provided we choose
Y
such that
v(Y)>0
. The following variant can also be derived in the centered case:

Corollary 1.6

(Ratio LLT with controlled deviations)
.

Assume that
\mu_{ab}
is
centered
, non lattice, and
\mu
has finite moment of order
m_{\mu}>d_{\mathfrak{g}}+2
. For all non-zero
f\in C_{c}(\mathfrak{g})
with
f\geq 0
, one has

\frac{\mu^{*N}(f)}{D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu(f)}\underset{N\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}1

and for any fixed
c>0
sufficiently small, the convergence is uniform as we replace
f
by the bitranslates
\delta_{g}*f*\delta_{h}
with

g,h\in\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\,:\,\|x^{(i)}\|\leq c\,(N\log N)^{i/2}\}.

Due to the possible vanishing of the limiting measure, the assumption that the walk is centered seems unavoidable to guarantee uniformity in the ratio LLT.

1.3
Applications of the LLT

We now present two applications of our LLT (
1.2
,
3.1
).

The Choquet-Deny property
.
Let
G
be a locally compact group, and
\mu
a probability measure on
G
. A function
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
is said to be
\mu
-harmonic
if for almost-every
x\in G
,

f(x)=\int_{G}f(xg)\,d\mu(g).

Note that every function
f
that is right-invariant by the support of
\mu
, i.e. such that
f*\delta_{g}=f
for every
g\in\text{supp}(\mu)
, must be
\mu
-harmonic.

G
is said to have the
Choquet-Deny property
if the converse holds: for any measure
\mu
, any bounded
\mu
-harmonic function
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
is right-invariant by the support of
\mu
. True for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
Bla55
]
and discrete nilpotent groups
[
DM61
]
, this property is by now completely understood when the group
G
is discrete, thanks to the groundbreaking recent work of Frisch, Hartman, Tamuz and Vahidi-Ferdowsi
[
FHTVF19
]
who showed that a finitely generated group is Choquet-Deny if and only if it is virtually nilpotent. For totally disconnected groups of polynomial growth, several characterizations of the Choquet-Deny property are given in
[
JR07
]
. The case of continuous groups however remains more subtle. Guivarc’h in his thesis
[
Gui73
]
established the property for all locally compact nilpotent groups under the assumption of a finite moment (of arbitrarily small but positive order) on the measure
\mu
, while the general case with no moment condition is still an open problem, despite
[
Rau04
]
. Guivarc’h’s proof relied on unitary representations. Assuming that
G
is a nilpotent Lie group and
\mu
has a moment of order
2+\varepsilon
, we provide a different argument, based on our LLT.

Corollary 1.7

(Bounded
\mu
-harmonic functions)
.

Let
G
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group. Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
with a finite moment of order strictly greater than
2
for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
, and whose support generates a dense subgroup of
G
. Then bounded
\mu
-harmonic functions on
G
are constant.

The condition that
G
is
simply
connected is not essential here, as one may always lift
\mu
and
\mu
-harmonic functions to the universal cover of
G
. It is however convenient to express our moment condition on
\mu
.

Probabilistic Ratner theorem
.
Let
H
be a real Lie group and
\Lambda\leq H
a lattice (discrete subgroup with finite covolume). Let
U
be a simply connected nilpotent Lie group and
\rho:U\to H
a Lie group homomorphism such that
Ad\circ\rho
is contained in a unipotent subgroup of
\GL(\mathfrak{h})
, where
\mathfrak{h}=Lie(H)
is the Lie algebra of
H
. Considering the resulting action of
U
on
\Omega:=H/\Lambda
, Ratner established
[
Rat91
,
Mor05
]
that the closure of every
U
-orbit is itself a homogeneous subspace of finite volume: for each
\omega\in\Omega
, the stabilizer
L_{\omega}=\{h\in H,\,h.\overline{U\omega}=\overline{U\omega}\}
acts transitively on
\overline{U\omega}
while preserving a probability measure on
\overline{U\omega}
. This measure is unique and we denote it by
m_{\omega}
. Ratner’s proof is based on an equidistribution theorem for one-parameter unipotent flows, that was subsequently extended to the multi-parameter case by Shah
[
Sha94
]
. Combining this with our LLT we obtain:

Corollary 1.8

(Equidistribution in homogeneous spaces)
.

In the above setting, let
\mu
be a probability measure on
U
whose support generates a dense subgroup of
U
. Assume
\mu
has a finite moment of order strictly greater than
2
for the weight filtration induced by
\overline{X}_{\mu}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
. Then for every
\omega\in\Omega
, we have the following weak-
\star
convergence of probability measures:

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu^{k}*\delta_{\omega}\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}m_{\omega}.

If, additionally,
\mu
is assumed centered and aperiodic, then
(\mu^{*N}*\delta_{\omega})_{N\geq 1}
itself converges to
m_{\omega}
.

We stress that the LLT is used not only through the Choquet-Deny property and its consequence that
\mu
-stationary measures are invariant. Indeed, we also rely crucially on the LLT to establish the non-escape of mass of the above sequences of probability measures. It ensures that any limit measure has mass
1
and is absolutely continuous with respect to
m_{\omega}
.

The moment condition in
Corollary

1.8
may not be optimal. However the other assumptions are tight. If
\mu
fails to be centered, or aperiodic, then easy examples show that the convergence of individual powers fails and only the Cesaro convergence holds (see
[
Bre05b
, 10.2.1]
).

Corollary

1.8
generalizes the unpublished chapter
[
Bre04
, Chapter 2]
, where this result was proven by a different method (combining work of Guivarc’h and Varopoulos) in the special case of a symmetric and finitely supported measure
\mu
, as well as the case of centered walks on the Heisenberg group, treated in
[
Bre05b
]
.

We also point out the analogous fact for measures supported on subgroups whose Zariski-closure is semisimple is a major result of Benoist-Quint
[
BQ11
]
in the Cesaro case, while convergence of individual powers was recently established by the first named author in
[
Bénar
]
in that setting, see also
[
BFLM11
,
HLL16
]
.

1.4
Historical remarks

Random walks and limit theorems on non-commutative Lie groups have a long history. We refer the reader to the early book
[
Gre63
]
and survey
[
ST66
]
for background on non-commutative analogues of the law of large numbers and central limit theorems, and more recent references such as
[
SC01
,
Bre04
,
BQ16
,
Lia04
,
Var21
]
. The local limit theorem has been studied for compact groups in
[
KI40
,
AK63
,
BG12
,
BdS16
]
, for the group of Euclidean rigid motions in
[
Kaz65
,
Gui76
,
Var15
,
LV16
]
and semisimple Lie groups in
[
Bou81
,
Kog22
]
. On nilpotent Lie groups, the central limit theorem was studied in
[
Tut64
,
Vir74
,
CR78
,
Rau78
,
BP96
,
BB23
]
. Random walks on nilpotent covers of compact manifolds or finite graphs have also attracted a lot of attention, see
[
Ish03
,
IKN20
,
IKN21
]
, where certain limit theorems for centered and non-centered walks are also obtained.

A completely new method was brought forward by Varopoulos in the 80s (see e.g.
[
VSCC92
]
) and further developed by Alexopoulos culminating in
[
Ale02b
,
Ale02a
,
Ale02c
]
. This method provides very complete results on the asymptotics of convolutions powers
\mu^{*N}
of a measure
\mu
on a nilpotent group (either Lie or discrete), including central and local limit theorems, even with the full Edgeworth expansion. However there are two caveats. The first is that this method requires the measure
\mu
to have a density with respect to the Haar measure, an assumption which seems unavoidable. The second is that the limit theorems that have been obtained by this method always assumed the measure to be centered.

The method used in our paper however (as well as that of
[
Bre05b
,
DH21
,
Hou19
,
BB23
]
) is based on the analysis of characteristic functions and allows to handle probability measures without any assumption on their regularity or whether they are centered or not. While our results assume the measure to be non-lattice, it remains an open problem to handle general lattice measures (even centered ones) on general nilpotent groups.

1.5
The path swap principle behind the proof of
Theorem

1.2

The approach to prove
Theorem

1.2
relies on characteristic functions as in the classical proof of the LLT for abelian groups. Similarly to previous works, the main part of the proof consists in establishing super-polynommial decay of the Fourier transform outside of a shrinking domain around the origin: for every
Q\geq 1
,
\gamma_{0}>0
,

|\widehat{\mu^{*N}}(\xi)|\ll_{Q,\gamma_{0}}N^{-Q}

(12)

outside the range of Fourier parameters
\xi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\|\xi\circ D_{\sqrt{N}}\|\ll N^{\gamma_{0}}
.

In
[
Bre05b
]
, a spectral gap estimate for the infinite dimensional irreducible unitary representations of the Heisenberg group was established for this purpose. In
[
DH21
]
, Diaconis and Hough proposed a different method to prove
(\ref{foudecay})
in the centered Heisenberg case, which is reminiscent of the well-known
van der Corput trick
in the proof of Weyl’s equiditribution theorem for polynomials, where one differentiates the polynomial phase to produce cancellation in an exponential sum (see e.g.
[
GT12
, §4]
for an example of its use in the context of equidistribution of ergodic flows on nilmanifolds). In
[
DH21
]
, this discrete derivative is replaced by a difference of permutations acting on the variables, which after iteration, generates some independence. This path swap principle is also exploited by
[
Hou19
]
to prove (
12
) for centered walks on arbitrary nilpotent groups, and we use it as well for
Theorem

1.2
.

We now give a brief outline of this principle (see
Section

3.1
for details). Given a subgroup of permutations
F\leq\Sym(N)
we may write

\widehat{\mu^{*N}}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes N}}\big(e_{\xi}(\Pi\underline{x})\big)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes N}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\sigma\in F}(e_{\xi}(\Pi\sigma\underline{x}))\big)

where
e_{\xi}(x)=\exp(-2i\pi\xi.x)
and
\Pi\underline{x}=x_{1}*\dots*x_{N}
is the Lie product of the variables
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
and
\Sym(N)
acts by permuting the variables. By Cauchy-Schwarz:

|\widehat{\mu^{*N}}(\xi)|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes N}}\big(|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma\in F}(e_{\xi}(\Pi\underline{x}))|^{2}\big)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes N}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau\in F}(e_{\xi}(\Pi\sigma\underline{x}-\Pi\tau\underline{x}))\big).

(13)

The right hand side can be rewritten
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau\in F}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{*N}}(e_{\xi}((\sigma-\tau)\Pi\underline{x}))\big)
where
\sigma-\tau
is now viewed as an element of the group algebra
\mathbb{R}[\Sym(N)]
acting on the free Lie algebra.

We can then iterate this procedure
s-1
times, averaging successively over commuting subgroups
F_{1},\ldots,F_{s-1}
. If
F=F_{1}\times\ldots\times F_{s-1}
,

|\widehat{\mu^{*N}}(\xi)|^{2^{s-1}}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau\in F}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes N}}(e_{\xi}(A_{\sigma,\tau}\Pi\underline{x}))\big)

where
A_{\sigma,\tau}=\prod_{1}^{s-1}(\sigma_{i}-\tau_{i})\in\mathbb{R}[\Sym(N)]
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s-1})\in F
.

We now choose the
F_{i}
’s so that for any fixed
\sigma,\tau\in F
, the term
A_{\sigma,\tau}\Pi\underline{x}
is a sum of independent variables as
\underline{x}
varies with law
\mu^{\otimes N}
. Assuming
N=N^{\prime}(2s-1)k
for some
k,N^{\prime}
, we may split the
N
variables
x_{i}
into
N^{\prime}
consecutive large blocks
(B_{j})_{j\leq N^{\prime}}
of length
(2s-1)k
. We see each large block as a concatenation of
(2s-1)
blocks of length
k
. We then let
F_{i}\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{N^{\prime}}
be the group generated by the transpositions exchanging the two blocks at distance
i
from the central block in some
B_{j}
. As the
F_{i}
have disjoint support, they do commute. The combinatorial properties of the Lie product on a nilpotent group imply that:

A_{\sigma,\tau}\Pi\underline{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{N^{\prime}}A_{\sigma,\tau}u_{j},

where each
u_{j}=\Pi\underline{x}_{j}
is a Lie product of the variables
x_{i}
whose indices
i
belong to the block
B_{j}
. It is therefore a sum of
N^{\prime}
uncorrelated terms. And hence:

|\widehat{\mu^{*N}}(\xi)|^{2^{s-1}}\leq\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau\in F}\prod_{j=1}^{N^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes N}}(e_{\xi}(A_{\sigma,\tau}u_{j}))=\big(\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau}\mathbb{E}_{\mu^{\otimes k(2a-1)}}(e_{\xi}(A_{\sigma,\tau}u_{1})\big)^{N^{\prime}}.

Furthermore the choice of
F
will ensure that
A_{\sigma,\tau}u_{1}
is a sum of
s
-brackets (i.e. pure commutators of length
s
), hence belongs to the center of
\mathfrak{g}
. Choosing
k\sim N^{\varepsilon}
and
N^{\prime}\sim N^{1-\varepsilon}
, this will eventually lead to the desired super-polynomial decay estimate
(\ref{foudecay})
.

.

We have
\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

∎

For each

\mathfrak{g}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(i+1)}.

It follows that
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{m}^{(2s-1)}.

We call such a direct sum (with choices of
\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}

of
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}

\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

(15)

x\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(i)},y\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(j)},z\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(k)}
, we have

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]^{\prime}]^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\pi^{(i+j+k)}[x,[y,z]=\pi^{(i+j+k)}\left([[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]]\right)

\displaystyle=[[x,y]^{\prime},z]^{\prime}+[y,[x,z]^{\prime}]^{\prime}.

∎

Note that the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
is an ideal of
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},[.,.]^{\prime})
and that
X

[.,.]^{\prime}
. The Lie product associated to

will be denoted by
*^{\prime}
. The operator

discussed in

.

Finally, we will make use of the

on

Conventions: norm, Lebesgue measure, dual, Fourier transform

We choose once and for all a basis
(e_{j}^{(i)})_{1\leq j\leq q_{i}}

\mathfrak{m}^{(i)}
, and we add
e_{0}^{(2)}=\chi
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(2)}
. We denote by
\|.\|
the associated Euclidean norm on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
making that basis orthonormal. The associated Lebesgue measure will be denoted by

. Recall that

is also a left and right invariant Haar measure (for both

), see for instance
[

We also fix a choice of left invariant Riemannian metric on

the induced distance to

We endow the dual space
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
of linear forms on

Given

2.1.3
Polynomials and Lie product

Recall that there is a well-defined notion of polynomial function
f
on a
d
-dimensional vector space
V
. Namely, it is independent of the choice of coordinates. So is its degree, which is defined as the maximal degree of a monomial
m:=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{n_{d}}
appearing in
f
, where the degree of
m
is
n_{1}+\ldots+n_{d}
.

On a vector space endowed with a filtration
V=V_{1}>V_{2}>\ldots>V_{s+1}=\{0\}
of subspaces, there is another notion of degree of a polynomial function, which is tailored to the choice of filtration and we call
w-degree
. It can be defined as follows. Choose supplementary subspaces
m_{k}
so that
V_{k}=m_{k}\oplus V_{k+1}
and a basis of each
m_{k}
to form a full basis
(e_{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}
of
V
. We may assign the weight
w_{i}=k
to each coordinate
x_{i}
with
e_{i}\in m_{k}
. Now the w-degree of
m
is set to be
w_{1}n_{1}+\ldots+w_{d}n_{d}
and that of
f
is defined as before.

It is straightforward to check that the w-degree
d_{f}
depends only on the filtration and not on the choice of basis nor on the choice of
m_{k}
. To see this, one may for instance observe, for
f\neq 0
, that
\sup_{B_{R}}|f|\simeq c_{f}R^{d_{f}}
for some
c_{f}>0
as
R
tends to infinity, where, for some Euclidean distance
d(\cdot,\cdot)
on
V
we define

B_{R}:=\{x\in V\,:\,\forall k\geq 1,\,d(x,V_{k+1})\leq R^{k}\}.

By definition, a
polynomial map

V\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is one whose coordinates are polynomial functions and its degree (or w-degree) if the maximum of the degree (or w-degree) of its coordinate functions.
In this paper, we will apply this to
V=\mathfrak{g}
or
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
endowed with the weight filtration defined in
Section

2.1.1
.

We now pass to the description of the Lie product via the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula. Write
\underline{x}
for the tuple
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
. By virtue of this formula, in any nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, the Lie product map

\displaystyle\Pi:\mathfrak{h}^{N}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathfrak{h},

\displaystyle\underline{x}

\displaystyle\mapsto\Pi\underline{x}:=x_{1}*x_{2}*\dots*x_{N}

(17)

is a polynomial map.
To describe this map it is convenient to begin working formally in the free associative algebra on
N
letters
\mathcal{A}_{N}:=\mathbb{R}\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\rangle
. The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{N}
admits a Lie bracket given by
[\omega,\omega^{\prime}]=\omega\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{\prime}\omega
. More generally, we may define the
r
-bracket

L^{[r]}:\mathcal{A}^{r}_{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{N}
recursively by
L^{[1]}=Id_{\mathcal{A}_{N}}
and
for all
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\in\mathcal{A}_{N}
,

L^{[r]}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})=[L^{[r-1]}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r-1}),\omega_{r}].

To each monomial
m=a_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{r}
in
\mathcal{A}_{N}
(with
a_{i}\in\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\}
) we may assign the
r
-bracket
L(m):=L^{[r]}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
and extend linearly to obtain a well-defined map:

L:\mathcal{A}_{N}\to\mathcal{A}_{N}

whose image is a realization of the free Lie algebra on
N
letters (see
[
Reu93
, Theorem 0.5]
). We note that if
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra and
\omega\in L(\mathcal{A}_{N})
, then there is an
evaluation map

\mathfrak{h}^{N}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h},\underline{x}\mapsto\omega\underline{x}
where
\omega\underline{x}
is computed by writing
\omega
as a linear combination of iterated brackets in
\mathcal{A}_{N}
then replacing each letter
u_{i}
by
x_{i}
. By the universal property of free Lie algebras, the evaluation map is well-defined. Moreover if the monomials involved in
\omega
have length at most
s
, then
\omega
is characterized by the evaluation maps it induces on nilpotent Lie algebras of step (at most)
s
. For more details see
[
Reu93
, Chapter 0]
.

Fixing
s\geq 1
, the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula (as proved by Dynkin in
[
Dyn47
]
) guarantees the existence of a unique element
\Pi_{N}\in L(\mathcal{A}_{N})
satisfying the following universal property: (1) for every
s
-step nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
and every
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathfrak{h}^{N}
, one has
\Pi_{N}\underline{x}=x_{1}*\dots*x_{N}
; (2)
\Pi_{N}
only involves monomials of length at most
s
.

We will use only few algebraic properties of the element
\Pi_{N}
. Among them is the following important feature formulated in the Claim below.

We use the standard shorthand notation
[N]
for the set
\{1,\ldots,N\}
. For a monomial
m\in\mathcal{A}_{N}
we define its support
I_{m}\subseteq[N]
to be the set of indices of the variables appearing in
m
. For
I\subseteq[N]
we let
(\mathcal{A}_{N})_{I}
be the span of all monomials with support
I
, and
\mathcal{A}_{N,t}
the sum of all
(\mathcal{A}_{N})_{I}
with
|I|=t
. For any
R\in\mathcal{A}_{N}
we denote by
R_{I}
its linear projection to
(\mathcal{A}_{N})_{I}
(modulo the other
(\mathcal{A}_{N})_{J}
,
J\neq I
). Note that the projection
R\mapsto R_{I}
commutes with
L
. We may of course write:

\Pi_{N}=\sum_{t=1}^{s}\Pi_{N,t}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{ where }\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\Pi_{N,t}:=\sum_{I\subseteq[N],|I|=t}(\Pi_{N})_{I}.

We note that
\mathcal{A}_{N}
comes with a natural action of
\Sym(N)
by permutation of the variables, i.e.
\sigma u_{i}=u_{\sigma i}
for
\sigma\in\Sym(N)
. This action preserves
L(\mathcal{A}_{N})
and
\mathcal{A}_{N,t}
. For each
I\subseteq[N]
with
|I|=t
, enumerate
I=\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{t}\}
and choose a permutation
\sigma_{I}
of
[N]
sending
j\in[t]
to
i_{j}
.
There is a natural
periodization map

\mathcal{P}_{t}:\mathcal{A}_{t,t}\to\mathcal{A}_{N}
that sends
R
to
\mathcal{P}_{t}R
:

\mathcal{P}_{t}R:=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{t}\leq N}R(u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{t}})=\sum_{|I|=t}\sigma_{I}R.

Claim 1:
Let
t=1,\ldots,s
. Then
\Pi_{N,t}
is in the image of
\mathcal{P}_{t}
. In fact:

\Pi_{N,t}=\mathcal{P}_{t}\Pi_{t,t}.

Proof.

The proof relies on the associativity of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula defining
\Pi_{N}
.
In view of the decomposition
\Pi_{N,t}:=\sum_{I\subseteq[N],|I|=t}(\Pi_{N})_{I}
we only need to check that for
I=\{i_{1}<\ldots<i_{t}\}\subseteq[N]
, we have
(\Pi_{N})_{I}=\sigma_{I}\Pi_{t,t}
. Note that
(\Pi_{N})_{I}
and
\sigma_{I}\Pi_{t,t}
only involve brackets of length at most
s
, so we have to check that they induce the same evaluation map on every nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of step at most
s
. Let
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathfrak{h}^{N}
, and
\underline{y}\in\mathfrak{h}^{N}
obtained from
\underline{x}
by setting
y_{i}=x_{i}
if
i\in I
and
y_{i}=0
if
i\notin I
. We have
(\Pi_{N})_{I}\underline{x}=\Pi_{N}\underline{y}=y_{i_{1}}*\ldots*y_{i_{t}}=(\sigma_{I}\Pi_{t,t})\underline{x}
, whence the result.
∎

Therefore, setting
P_{t}:=\Pi_{t,t}
,

\Pi_{N}=\sum_{t=1}^{s}\sum_{|I|=t}\sigma_{I}P_{t}.

(18)

For example,
\Pi_{1}=P_{1}=u_{1}
,
\Pi_{2}=u_{1}+u_{2}+P_{2}
,
\Pi_{3}=u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3}+P_{2}(u_{1},u_{2})+P_{2}(u_{1},u_{3})+P_{2}(u_{2},u_{3})+P_{3}
. Also note that
P_{2}^{[2]}=\frac{1}{2}L(u_{1}u_{2})
,
P_{3}^{[3]}=\frac{1}{6}L(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3})+\frac{1}{6}L(u_{3}u_{2}u_{1})
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
and the path-swapping method, it will be helpful to see
\mathcal{A}_{N}
as a module for the group algebra of
\Sym(N)
. In particular, if
R\in\mathcal{A}_{N}
and

a=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sym(N)}a_{\sigma}\sigma

is an arbitrary element of the group algebra
\mathbb{R}[\Sym(N)]
, we will write:

aR:=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sym(N)}a_{\sigma}\sigma R.

(19)

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, the space of functions
\{P:\mathfrak{h}^{N}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}\}
is also as
\mathbb{R}[\Sym(N)]
-module, where the action is given by

aP=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sym(N)}a_{\sigma}P\circ\sigma^{-1}.

The two actions are compatible in the sense that
\underline{x}\mapsto(aR)\underline{x}
and
a(\underline{x}\mapsto R\underline{x})
obviously coincide for
R\in L(\mathcal{A}_{N})
.

We end this subsection by introducing another piece of notation regarding the product
\Pi_{N}
. Recall that the free associative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{N}
has a natural grading by monomial length. Namely given
R\in\mathcal{A}_{N}
we may write

R=\sum_{r\geq 1}R^{[r]}

where
R^{[r]}
is the part of
R
which is a linear combination of monomials of length
r
. The projection
R\rightarrow R^{[r]}
preserves
L(\mathcal{A}_{N})
. The polynomials
\Pi_{N}^{[a]}
,
a=1,\ldots,s
will play a role below. We note that for any nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of step at most
s
, the evaluation map
\mathfrak{h}^{N}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h},\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi_{N}^{[a]}\underline{x}
defines a polynomial map whose coordinates are homogeneous polynomials of (ordinary) degree
a
on the vector space
\mathfrak{h}^{N}
. In fact it is the
a
-homogeneous component of
\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi_{N}\underline{x}
.

Further to this, we now introduce a refined decomposition that takes into account the weight filtration and the w-degree. Suppose that the nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
comes with a filtration by ideals
(\mathfrak{h}^{(i)})_{i\leq t}
and that supplementary subspaces
\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}
are chosen so that
\mathfrak{h}^{(i)}=\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{(i+1)}
. This is the situation encountered, for example, in
Section

2.1.1
. An element
x\in\mathfrak{h}
can be written as a sum of its constituents
x^{(i)}\in\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}
each of which will have weight
i
. We may thus speak of the w-degree of a polynomial function
f
on
\mathfrak{h}
or
\mathfrak{h}^{N}
. And any such function can then be decomposed into a sum of homogeneous components for the w-degree

\displaystyle f=\sum_{b\geq 0}f^{(b)}.

(20)

We denote by
\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi^{(b)}\underline{x}
the
b
-homogeneous component of
\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi\underline{x}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{N}
for the w-degree. And for
b\geq a
, we write
\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi^{[a,b)}\underline{x}
for the component of
\underline{x}\mapsto(\Pi\underline{x})^{[a]}
that is
b
-homogeneous for the w-degree, or equivalently the component of
\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi^{(b)}\underline{x}
that is
a
-homogeneous for the ordinary degree. This produces a bi-grading of the ring of polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{h}^{N}
. We note that this new grading depends not only on the filtration
\mathfrak{h}^{(i)}
but also on the choice of
\mathfrak{n}^{(i)}
. In particular, contrary to
\Pi^{[a]}
which corresponds to an element in the associative algebra, the notation
\Pi^{[a,b)}\underline{x}
only refers to a map on
\mathfrak{h}^{N}
. For an alternative equivalent description of
\Pi^{[a,b)}\underline{x}
in terms of a free Lie algebra element, we refer the reader to our companion paper
[
BB23
]
.

In the remainder of this article, we will apply these considerations in the case where
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}
or
\mathfrak{h}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
endowed with the weight filtration. We further note in this context that
\Pi^{[a,b)}\underline{x}
behaves in a homogeneous way with respect to the dilations introduced in
Section

2.1.1
. Namely
\Pi^{[a,b)}D_{r}\underline{x}=r^{b}\Pi^{[a,b)}\underline{x}
for any
r>0
.

2.1.4
The driving measure and its bias extension

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote by
\mu_{ab}
the projection of
\mu
to the abelianization
\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=:\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
. The subscript
ab
stands for “abelianized”. We assume that
\mu_{ab}
has a finite first moment and write
\overline{X}=\mathbb{E}(\mu_{ab})
for the expectation of
\mu_{ab}
. We also set
\mu^{(i)}:=\pi^{(i)}_{\star}\mu
, where the subscript
\star
added to a map denotes the push-forward by that map. We set
X=\mathbb{E}(\mu^{(1)})
and notice
X
projects onto
\overline{X}
under the abelianization map.

To prove the LLT, we will replace the measure
\mu
by its
bias extension

\widetilde{\mu}
, defined as the image of
\mu
under the map
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},x\mapsto(x,1)
. Note that for all
N\geq 1
, one has

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}=\mu^{*N}-NX+N\chi=\mu^{*N}*-NX*N\chi

(21)

where the notation
\mu^{*N}-NX+N\chi
stands for the image of
\mu^{*N}
under the map
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},x\mapsto x-NX+N\chi
, and similarly for
\mu^{*N}*-NX*N\chi
. The role of
\widetilde{\mu}
is to convert the drift
X\in\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
into
\chi\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(2)}
while keeping the fluctuations around the drift in
\mathbb{R}X\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
. This separation aims to reflect the fact that the drift will contribute to higher commutators with weight
2
whereas the fluctuation around the mean will contribute with weight
1
.

In proving the local limit theorem, we will suppose that
\mu
is
aperiodic
(or
non-lattice
). This means that the support of
\mu
is not contained in a coset of some proper subgroup, i.e. some
x*H
where
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
H
is a proper subgroup of
(\mathfrak{g},*)
. For simply connected nilpotent Lie groups, it is equivalent to ask that
\mu_{ab}
is non-lattice. This is because, as is well-known and easy to check by induction on the nilpotency class, a closed subgroup of
(\mathfrak{g},*)
is proper if and only if its image in
\mathfrak{g}_{ab}
is proper. Moreover the non-lattice condition on
\mu_{ab}
is equivalent to requiring that
|\widehat{\mu_{ab}}(\xi)|<1
for all
\xi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{ab}\setminus\{0\}
(indeed the latter fails precisely when
\mu_{ab}
is supported on a coset of
\xi^{-1}(\mathbb{Z})
).

We also need moment conditions. For
m>0
, we say that
\mu
has
finite
m
-th moment
for the weight filtration if for all
b\leq 2s-1
, one has

\int_{\mathfrak{g}}\|x^{(b)}\|^{m/b}d\mu(x)<\infty.

N.B. Unless explicitely stated otherwise, moment conditions on
\mu
will always refer to moment for the weight filtration.

2.1.5
Moderate deviations

Given
N\geq 1
,
g,h\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
, we denote by
g*\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}*h
, or
\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}_{g,h}
for short, the image of
\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}
under the map
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},x\mapsto g*x*h
. Setting
p:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
the projection to the first factor (which is a Lie algebra homomorphism), we have
p_{\star}\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}_{g,h}=\mu^{*N}_{p(g),p(h)}
.

We shall prove estimates for
\widetilde{\mu}^{*N}_{g,h}
uniformly in a certain range of
moderate deviations
: given
\delta_{0}\in[0,1)
, we set

\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})=\{x\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}\,:\,\forall i\leq 2s-1,\,\,\|x^{(i)}\|\leq N^{i/2+s^{-1}\delta_{0}}\},

where
x^{(i)}=\pi^{(i)}(x)
is the coordinate projection with respect to the grading
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}}^{(b)})_{b}
of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. It is worth pointing out that
\mathcal{D}_{N}
behaves well with respect to the product
*
: There exists
C>0
such that for all
\delta_{0}>0
, for all large enough
N\geq 1
,

x,y\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0})\implies x*y\in\mathcal{D}_{N}(C\delta_{0}).

Indeed, this follows from the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula: the coordinates of
(x*y)^{(i)}
are polynomials in
(x,y)
of w-degree at most
i
.

\ll
and Landau notations

,
o(.)
. Unless otherwise stated, the implied constants will depend only on the initial data

2.2
The three reductions

To prove the local limit theorem
Theorem

1.2
announced earlier, we perform a series of reductions on the random product
X_{1}*\dots*X_{N}
that help estimate its Fourier transform. First, we see that after a suitable truncation, the random product has moment estimates similar to what can be proven in the case where
\mu
has finite moment for every order. Second, we note that the product structure
*
can be replaced, up to a small error, by its graded counterpart
*^{\prime}
for which dilations act as automorphisms. Third, we show that
\mu
can be replaced by any other driving measure that share the same moments up to order
2
. Those reductions were already used in
[
BB23
]
in order to prove the central limit theorem.

The notations
(\mathfrak{g},*,\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},\overline{X},(\mathfrak{m}^{(b)})_{b\leq 2s-1},\Pi^{[a,b)},*^{\prime},\mu,\widetilde{\mu},\mathcal{D}_{N}(\delta_{0}))
are those of
Section

2.1
. Throughout
Section

2.2
we assume that
\mu
has finite second moment for the weight filtration
induced by
\overline{X}
.

2.2.1
Truncation and moment estimates

We will need the moment estimates collected in
Proposition

2.4
below. They concern a truncation of the driving measure in order to allow weaker moment assumptions on
\mu
. If
\mu
has finite moment for every order, such truncation is not necessary (see
[
BB23
, Proposition 4.1]
). As mentioned earlier, we will use the bias extension
\widetilde{\mu}
instead of
\mu
for technical convenience.

The
truncation

T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
is the image of
\widetilde{\mu}
under the map

\displaystyle T_{N}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle x=\oplus x^{(b)}

\displaystyle\mapsto\oplus T_{N}x^{(b)}

where

T_{N}x^{(b)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{(b)}&\mbox{if }\|x^{(b)}\|\leq N^{b/2}\\
0&\mbox{if }b\geq 2\text{ and }\|x^{(b)}\|>N^{b/2}\\
c_{N}&\mbox{if }b=1\text{ and }\|x^{(1)}\|>N^{1/2}\end{array}\right.

and
c_{N}:=-\widetilde{\mu}({\|x^{(1)}\|>N^{1/2}})^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\widetilde{\mu}}(x^{(1)}\mathbbm{1}_{{\|x^{(1)}\|\leq N^{1/2}}})
is chosen so that
T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
is centered in the
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}
-coordinate.

The error induced when replacing the measure
\widetilde{\mu}
by its truncation
T_{N}\widetilde{\mu}
is controlled via the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(Cost of truncation)
.

Let
q\in[2,+\infty)
. If
\mu
has finite
q
-th moment for the weight filtration, then for all
N\geq 1
,
(N_{i})_{i\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{N}
,

\|\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes N}-\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}T_{N_{i}}\widetilde{\mu}\|=o\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}N_{i}^{-\frac{q}{2}}\right)

where
\|.\|
is the total variation norm for measures on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}
.

3.1
Path swap

The main part of the proof of
Theorem

3.1
is the domain reduction, namely
Section

3
. In this subsection, we establish
Proposition

3.6
, which gives an exponential bound on the Fourier transform of a random product restricted to its components involving only brackets of length bounded by an integer
a
.
Proposition

3.6
will be an essential ingredient in the proof of
Section

3
.

Proposition

3.6
relies on a path swap technique inspired by the work of Diaconis and Hough in
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
. The idea is to take advantage of the fact that the law of the product
X_{1}*\ldots*X_{N}
is invariant under permutations of the
X_{i}
by averaging over a certain subset of permutations in
Sym(N)
, in order to generate smoothness in the higher commutators. This is akin to a kind of
discrete derivative
and its use in combination to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as effected below can be viewed as a kind of non-abelian analogue (for commutator calculus) of the van der Corput trick, which is ubiquitous in nilpotent dynamics
[
Lei05
,
HK05
,
GT12
]
. In what follows the average is taken
over a certain elementary abelian
2
-subgroup of
Sym(N)
. In this subsection, as well as in
[
DH21
,
Hou19
]
, the path swap technique is tailored to deal with the central filtration of
\mathfrak{g}
. We will need extra work to obtain information regarding the weight filtration instead.

We now present the path-swap technique.
Let us consider a subgroup
F\leq\Sym(N)
, which is a cartesian product
F=F_{1}\times\ldots\times F_{a-1}
of commuting subgroups
F_{i}\leq\Sym(N)
. An element
\sigma\in F
may be written uniquely
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{a-1}
with
\sigma_{i}\in F_{i}
.

Recall from
Section

2.1.3
that the set of maps from
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is a
\mathbb{R}[\Sym(N)]
-module. Consider a measurable map
P:\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}},\underline{x}\mapsto P\underline{x}
and a probability measure
\kappa
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}
such that the distributions
P_{\star}\kappa
and
(\sigma P)_{\star}\kappa
coincide for all
\sigma\in F
(for instance
\kappa
could be
\Sym(N)
-invariant). We write:

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{\star}\kappa}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}e_{\xi}(P\underline{x})\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})=\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma\in F}e_{\xi}(\sigma P\underline{x})\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{a-1}\in F_{a-1}}\dots\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{1}\in F_{1}}e_{\xi}(\sigma_{a-1}\ldots\sigma_{1}P\underline{x})\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}
refers to the uniform probability measure on the subgroup in subscript. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz then Fubini, we obtain with
\sigma=\sigma_{a-1}\cdots\sigma_{1}=\sigma^{\prime}\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma^{\prime}\in F_{a-1}\ldots F_{2}
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{P_{\star}\kappa}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^{\prime}}|\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{1}}e_{\xi}(\sigma P\underline{x})|^{2}\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{1},\tau_{1}}e_{\xi}\left(\sigma^{\prime}\sigma_{1}P\underline{x}-\sigma^{\prime}\tau_{1}P\underline{x})\right)\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{1},\tau_{1}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma^{\prime}}e_{\xi}\left((\sigma_{1}-\tau_{1})\sigma^{\prime}P\underline{x}\right)\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

where
\tau_{1}\in F_{1}
is a new independent variable with uniform distribution on
F_{1}
. We can repeat this process and obtain the
path-swap inequality
:

\displaystyle|\widehat{P_{\star}\kappa}(\xi)|^{2^{a-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{1},\tau_{1}}\dots\mathbb{E}_{\sigma_{a-1},\tau_{a-1}}e_{\xi}\left(A_{\sigma,\tau}P\underline{x}\right)\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}}\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau}e_{\xi}\left(A_{\sigma,\tau}P\underline{x}\right)\,\,d\kappa(\underline{x})

(23)

where
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma,\tau}
is over the uniform distribution on
F\times F
and

A_{\sigma,\tau}:=\prod_{i=1}^{a-1}(\sigma_{i}-\tau_{i})\in\mathbb{R}[\Sym(N)].

This inequality will be applied later in the proof of
3.5
and
P
will be taken to be the map
\underline{x}\mapsto\Pi^{[<a]}\underline{x}+\Pi^{[a]}\underline{x}_{ab}
where
\underline{x}_{ab}
refers to the coordinate-wise projection of

to
\mathfrak{m}^{(1)}\oplus\mathbb{R}\chi
modulo
\mathfrak{g}^{[2]}

acts on

\Pi^{[a]}
for an appropriate choice of permutation subgroup

We specify

The second lemma shows that

desynchronizes the contributions of different large blocks. Let

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\xi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\smallsetminus\{0\}

\underline{x}_{-1},\underline{x}_{1}

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}\sim(T_{N_{0}}\widetilde{\mu})^{\otimes N}}\left[e_{\xi}\left(\Pi^{[<a]}\underline{y}+\Pi^{[a]}\underline{y}_{ab}\right)\right]\big|^{2^{a-1}}\ll(1-gap_{c}(\|\xi\|))^{N^{\prime}}

We first define the implicit constant that will occur in the condition

Note that we have

Let us now prove the proposition in the case

. Let

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{(T_{N_{0}}\widetilde{\mu})^{\otimes N}}\left[e_{\xi}\left(\sum\underline{y}_{ab}\right)\right]\big|=\big|\widehat{(T_{N_{0}}\widetilde{\mu})_{ab}}(\xi)\big|^{N}=\big|\widehat{(T_{N_{0}}\widetilde{\mu})^{+k}_{ab}}(\xi)\big|^{N^{\prime}}.

\displaystyle\big|\widehat{(T_{N_{0}}\widetilde{\mu})^{+k}_{ab}}(\xi)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\widehat{\widetilde{\mu}^{+k}_{ab}}(\xi)\big|+\|(T_{N_{0}}\widetilde{\mu})^{\otimes k}-\widetilde{\mu}^{\otimes k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\widehat{\widetilde{\mu}^{+k}_{ab}}(\xi)\big|+o(kN_{0}^{-m_{\mu}/2})

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\,\,\,\,N^{-\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{a}}

because

. Then we choose

so that

The second step is now to bound the remaining expectation

. Recalling that

and

, we see that

is a polynomial in

. It follows that

where

only depends on
\dim\mathfrak{g}
.

Consider one of these monomials

consisting of consecutive variables which do not appear in
\underline{x}^{\prime}_{a-1}
,
\underline{x}_{a}
, nor

the block length prescribed for

.
As the number of distinct variables in

is bounded by

, we may write

is a tuple of variables which do not appear in

and of length

. Then using Fubini’s theorem, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\underline{y}\sim g\otimes\underline{\kappa}_{N}\otimes h}\left[e_{\xi}(S\underline{y})e_{\xi}(E\underline{y}_{a})M(\underline{x})\right]\,=\,\mathbb{E}_{\underline{x}^{\prime}_{a-1},\underline{z}_{0},\underline{z}_{1},\underline{x}_{a}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\underline{z}}[e_{\xi}(S\underline{y})]e_{\xi}(E\underline{y}_{a})M(\underline{x})\right].

3.6
implies that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}_{\underline{z}}[e_{\xi}(S\underline{y})]\big|

\displaystyle\ll(1-gap_{c}(\|\xi\|))^{\frac{1}{2^{a-1}}N^{\prime}_{aa}}

as long as

. To justify this last upper bound, we check that for

. As

for some

\displaystyle\widehat{\Upsilon^{N}_{g,h}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{M}c_{M}\mathbb{E}_{g\otimes\underline{\kappa}_{N}\otimes h}\left[e_{\xi}(S\underline{y})e_{\xi}(E\underline{y}_{a})M(\underline{x})\right]+O_{m}(N^{-Q})

\displaystyle\ll_{\gamma_{a-1},\gamma_{a},m}(\sum_{M}|c_{M}|)\,(1-gap_{c}(\|\xi\|))^{\frac{1}{2^{a-1}}N^{\frac{\varepsilon_{a}}{2s}}}+N^{-Q}.

\displaystyle\ll_{\gamma_{0},Q}N^{C^{\prime\prime}m}(1+\|\xi\|)^{m}(1-gap_{c}(\|\xi\|))^{\frac{1}{2^{a-1}}N^{\frac{\varepsilon_{a}}{2s}}}+N^{-Q}.

∎

Proposition 3.12

i)

We first reduce to the case of aperiodic walks thanks to the following general lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Reduction to aperiodic case)

\|\sigma_{N}\|\leq\varepsilon
, such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}D_{\sqrt{k}}\nu*kX_{\mu}\leq Cm_{N}+\sigma_{N}

and
m_{N}\to_{N\to+\infty}m_{\omega}
. The statement also holds without Cesàro average if
X_{\mu}=0
.

Let
K_{1}
be a compact neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathfrak{u}
and let
K_{r}=D_{\sqrt{r}}K_{1}
. Since
\nu
has a continuous density, there is
C_{1}>0
such that
\nu\leq C_{1}\text{leb}_{|K_{r}}+\nu_{\mathfrak{u}\smallsetminus K_{r}}
for all
r\geq 1
.
For all
N\geq 1
,

\displaystyle D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu

\displaystyle\leq D_{\sqrt{N}}C_{1}\text{leb}_{K_{r}}+D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu_{\mathfrak{u}\smallsetminus K_{r}}=C_{1}N^{-d_{\overline{X}}/2}\text{leb}_{D_{\sqrt{rN}}K_{1}}+D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu_{\mathfrak{u}\smallsetminus K_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}M\frac{\text{leb}_{B_{\underline{t}_{Nr}}}}{\text{leb}(B_{\underline{t}_{Nr}})}+D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu_{\mathfrak{u}\smallsetminus K_{r}}=Cm_{N}+\sigma_{N}

setting
C=C_{1}M
, where the constant
M
and the box
B_{\underline{t}_{rN}}\supset D_{\sqrt{rN}}K_{1}
are given by
5.5
below applied to
K_{1}
. Choosing
r
such that
\|\nu_{\mathfrak{u}\smallsetminus K_{r}}\|<\varepsilon
, the result follows in case
\mu
is centered. In the general case when
X_{\mu}\neq 0
, we translate by
kX_{\mu}
on the right and average over
k
:

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}D_{\sqrt{k}}\nu*kX_{\mu}\leq C\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\text{leb}_{B_{\underline{t}_{rN}}*kX_{\mu}}}{\text{leb}(B_{\underline{t}_{rN}})}+\sigma_{N}

where
\sigma_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(D_{\sqrt{k}}\nu_{\mathfrak{u}\smallsetminus K_{r}})*kX_{\mu}
has mass less than
\varepsilon
.
As we took care of choosing
e_{d}=X_{\mu}
, the diffeomorphism
t_{1}e_{1}*\dots*t_{d}e_{d}\mapsto(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})
sends the Lebesgue measure
\text{leb}_{B_{\underline{t}_{rN}}*kX_{\mu}}
to the product measure

\text{leb}_{[-\underline{t}_{rN,1},\underline{t}_{rN,1}]}\otimes\dots\otimes\text{leb}_{[-\underline{t}_{rN,d-1},\underline{t}_{rN,d-1}]}\otimes\text{leb}_{[-\underline{t}_{rN,d},\underline{t}_{rN,d}]+k}.

By construction of
\underline{t}_{rN}
, we have
\underline{t}_{rN,d}=M\sqrt{rN}
, so for
N
large enough

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\text{leb}_{[-\underline{t}_{rN,d},\underline{t}_{rN,d}]+k}\leq\frac{2M\sqrt{Nr}+1}{N}\text{leb}_{[-2N,2N]}.

Hence

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}D_{\sqrt{k}}\nu*kX_{\mu}\leq C(2M+1)\frac{\sqrt{r}\text{leb}_{B_{\underline{t}^{\prime}_{rN}}}}{\sqrt{N}\text{leb}(B_{\underline{t}_{rN}})}+\sigma_{N}

where
\underline{t}^{\prime}_{rN}
is obtained from
\underline{t}_{rN}
by replacing the coordinate
\underline{t}_{rN,d}
par
2N
. As
\text{leb}(B_{\underline{t}^{\prime}_{rN}})
is comparable to
\frac{2N}{\sqrt{rN}}\text{leb}(B_{\underline{t}_{rN}})
the claim follows by
Theorem

5.4
.
∎

In the proof we used the following well-known comparison principle between Malcev coordinates of the first and second kind
[
CG90
, 1.2]
.

Lemma 5.5

(comparison principle)
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathfrak{u}
be a compact set. There exists
M>0
such that for all
r>1
, there exists
\underline{t}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

D_{\sqrt{r}}K\subseteq B_{\underline{t}_{r}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,et\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{leb}(B_{\underline{t}_{r}})\leq Mr^{\frac{d_{\overline{X}}}{2}}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to check that for all
C>0
, there exists
M>0
such that for all
r>1
,

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{i,j}s^{(i)}_{j}e^{(i)}_{j}\,:\,\forall i,j,\,|s^{(i)}_{j}|\leq C\right\}\subseteq D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}}\left\{\prod_{i,j}t^{(i)}_{j}e^{(i)}_{j}\,:\,\forall i,j,\,|t^{(i)}_{j}|\leq Mr^{i/2}\right\}

(34)

where the product follows the order prescribed the enumeration
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
. But we can write
D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}}\prod_{i,j}t^{(i)}_{j}e^{(i)}_{j}=\prod^{*^{\prime}}_{i,j}r^{-i/2}t^{(i)}_{j}e^{(i)}_{j}+P_{r}((r^{-i/2}t^{(i)}_{j})_{i,j})
where
*^{\prime}
is the graded product coming from
*
for the weight filtration, and
P_{r}
is obtained by dividing by a (non zero) power of
\sqrt{r}
each coefficent of a polynomial function
P
with
d
variables. Then, we choose
r_{0}>0
such that for
r>r_{0}
,

\|P_{r}((r^{-i/2}t^{(i)}_{j})_{i,j})\|\leq 1

and
M>0
large enough so that

\left\{\sum_{i,j}s^{(i)}_{j}e^{(i)}_{j}\,:\,\forall i,j,\,|s^{(i)}_{j}|\leq C+1\right\}\subseteq\left\{{\prod_{i,j}}^{*^{\prime}}t^{(i)}_{j}e^{(i)}_{j}\,:\,\forall i,j,\,|t^{(i)}_{j}|\leq M\right\}

This proves (
34
) for
r>r_{0}
. Up to increasing the value of
M
even more, we get the result for all
r>1
.
∎

We can finally conclude the proof of
Corollary

1.8
.

Proof of
Corollary

1.8
.

Proposition

5.3
guarantees that every weak limit of the sequence

(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu^{N}*\delta_{\omega})_{N\geq 1}

is a probability measure on
\Omega
that is absolutely continuous with respect to
m_{\omega}
. It is also
\mu
-stationary, so it must be
U
-invariant by
Corollary

1.7
and hence
m_{\omega}
itself, because
m_{\omega}
is
U
-ergodic by Ratner’s theorem
[
Rat91
]
.

When
\mu
is centered, we may apply the same argument to the sequence
(\mu^{N}*\delta_{\omega})_{N\geq 1}
, but we need to justify why limit measures are
\mu
-stationary. It is sufficient to check that for every compactly supported smooth function
f
on
\Omega
, we have
|\mu^{N+1}*\delta_{\omega}(f)-\mu^{N}*\delta_{\omega}(f)|\rightarrow 0
as
N\to+\infty
. Setting
F(u)=f(\exp(u)\omega)
we obtain a bounded Lipschitz function on
\mathfrak{u}
and by
Proposition

5.1
, it boils down to checking that
|D_{\sqrt{N+1}}\nu(F)-D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu(F)|\rightarrow 0
. To see this, we observe that for the norm in total variation,

\|D_{\sqrt{N+1}}\,\nu-D_{\sqrt{N}}\nu\|=\|\nu-D_{\sqrt{\frac{N}{N+1}}}\,\nu\|\longrightarrow 0

whence the result.

∎

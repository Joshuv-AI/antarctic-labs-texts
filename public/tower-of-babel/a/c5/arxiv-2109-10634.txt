Filtered integration rules for finite Hilbert transforms Journal: Arxiv
Donatella Occorsio, Maria Grazia Russo, Woula Themistoclakis

Abstract
A product quadrature rule, based on the filtered de la Vallée Poussin polynomial approximation, is proposed for evaluating the finite Hilbert transform in [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] . Convergence results are stated in weighted uniform norm for functions belonging to suitable Besov type subspaces. Several numerical tests are provided, also comparing the rule with other formulas known in literature.

Introduction

The numerical computation of the Hilbert transform of a function plays an important role in several fields, since many mathematical models in applied sciences lead to it
(see e.g.
[
19
,
20
]
and the references therein). Depending on the specific application, we may consider bounded or unbounded integration domains where the function may have several degrees of smoothness (see e.g. the treatise by F. King in two volumes
[
20
]
on many aspects of the Hilbert transform and its possible variants). Here we consider the case of locally continuous functions

(also known as the “density function”) defined as follows

\mathcal{H}^{u}f(t):=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f(x)}{x-t}u(x)dx=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{|x-t|\geq\epsilon}\frac{f(x)}{x-t}u(x)dx,\qquad-1<t<1,

(1)

being

in the case

.

Incidentally, such transform appears in Cauchy singular integral equations, which, in turn, arise in several mathematical models (see for instance
[
21
,
37
,
24
]
).
Moreover,

is related to hypersingular operators that can be defined as derivatives of the Hilbert transform

In this paper, as system of nodes, we shall consider the zeros of Jacobi polynomials
\{p_{n}(w)\}_{n}
associated to suitable Jacobi weights

X_{n}(w):=\{x_{k}:=x_{n,k}(w)\ :\ k=1,\ldots,n\},\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2)

Based on Jacobi systems of nodes, several quadrature rules are known in literature (see e.g.

Here we propose a similar product rule (that we will call VP-rule), but instead of the Lagrange polynomial, we employ the filtered de la Vallée Poussin (VP) polynomial of

,

]

V_{n}^{m}(w,f,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x),\qquad n>m\in{\mathbb{N}},\qquad x\in[-1,1],

based on the same

0

n-m
n+m
VP filter
[formula]

Such filter function defines the fundamental VP polynomials

\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)=\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n}^{m}(j)p_{j}(w,x_{k})p_{j}(w,x),

being

\lambda_{k}:=\lambda_{n,k}(w)=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}[p_{j}(w,x_{k})]^{2}\right)^{-1},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,n,

(3)

the Christoffel numbers related to the weight
w
.

Note that in the limiting case
m=0
the previous VP polynomial
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)
coincides with the Lagrange polynomial
L_{n}(w,f)
and therefore it yields the same quadrature rule.

The aim of the present paper is to show that, by using
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)
with
0<m<n
, we can take advantage of the additional parameter
m
which can be suitably modulated in order to improve the quadrature error of the L-rule.

Indeed, it is already known (see e.g.
[
38
,
34
,
36
,
35
]
) that corresponding to suitable choices of
0<m<n
, the polynomial
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)
provides a pointwise approximation better than the one offered by
L_{n}(w,f)
, especially in presence of Gibbs phenomena. In fact, if
f
presents some “pathologies” (peaks, cusps, etc.) localized in isolated points, the Gibbs phenomenon affects
L_{n}(w,f)
with overshoots and oscillations that spill over the whole interval, but such phenomenon appears strongly reduced by using
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)
.

The experimental results will show that such an improvement is inherited by VP-rules, that for these kinds of density functions may provide a performance better than the L-rule.

In our experiments we will also compare VP-rules with another class of quadrature formulas based on Jacobi zeros, i. e. the Modified Gaussian rules (shortly denoted by MG rules), proposed in
[
7
]
and also studied in
[
4
]
.
Such formulas provide higher performance than L-rule, but we will show some cases where VP-rules produce an even better quadrature error.
Moreover, we remark that differently from both L and VP-rules, MG rules require a variable
number of nodes depending on the singular point
t
and consequently their employment in numerical methods for singular integral equations appears more complicated than the previous product rules.

From a theoretical point of view, in this paper we provide new estimates of the VP polynomial approximation in some Besov type spaces characterized by a Dini–type condition. Using such estimates we prove the convergence of the proposed quadrature rule and state several asymptotic bounds for the quadrature error.

The outline of the paper is the following. Section 2 contains preliminary notations and results concerning the approximation spaces. Moreover, in a dedicated subsection, the mapping properties of the Hilbert transform in such spaces are studied. Section 3 is devoted to the VP approximation, while the new quadrature rule is introduced in Section 4, where we state the main results. Finally, Section 5 concerns the numerical tests while Section 6 is devoted to the proofs.

2
Notations and preliminary results

Consider a fixed Jacobi weight

v(x)=v^{\gamma,\delta}(x):=(1-x)^{\gamma}(1+x)^{\delta},\qquad x\in(-1,1),\quad\gamma,\delta\geq 0.

Throughout the paper the space of all functions
f
continuous on
(-1,1)
and satisfying

\lim_{x\rightarrow+1}f(x)v(x)=0\quad\mbox{if $\gamma>0$},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{x\rightarrow-1}f(x)v(x)=0\quad\mbox{if $\delta>0$},

(4)

is denoted by
C^{0}_{v}
and equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{C^{0}_{v}}:=\|fv\|_{\infty}=\max_{x\in[-1,1]}|f(x)|v(x).

It is well-known (see for instance
[
25
]
) that the Weierstrass approximation theorem holds in this Banach space and we have

f\in C^{0}_{v}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{n}(f)_{v}=0,

where
E_{n}(f)_{v}
denotes the error of best approximation of
f\in C^{0}_{v}
in the space
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
of all algebraic polynomials of degree at most
n
, namely

E_{n}(f)_{v}:=\inf_{P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\|(f-P)\|_{C^{0}_{v}}.

The rate of convergence of such error as
n\rightarrow\infty
depends on the smoothness of the function
f
and it is well characterized by the following moduli of smoothness introduced in
[
13
]
by Z.Ditzian and V.Totik

\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}=\sup_{0<h\leq t}\|v\Delta^{k}_{h\varphi}f\|_{L^{\infty}[-1+2h^{2}k^{2},1-2h^{2}k^{2}]},

\omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}=\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}+\inf_{q\in{\mathbb{P}}_{k-1}}\|(f-q)v\|_{L^{\infty}[-1,-1+t^{2}k^{2}]}+\inf_{q\in{\mathbb{P}}_{k-1}}\|(f-q)v\|_{L^{\infty}[1-t^{2}k^{2},1]},

where

\Delta^{k}_{h\varphi}(f,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}f\left(x+\frac{kh}{2}\varphi(x)-ih\varphi(x)\right).

In fact, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large (say
n\geq n_{0}
) and
t>0
sufficiently small (say
t\leq t_{0}
), the following Jackson and Stechkin type inequalities hold (see
[
13
]
,
[
23
]
)

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982ptE_{n}(f)_{v}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\omega_{\varphi}^{k}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right)_{v}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}}{t}dt,\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f),

(5)

\displaystyle\hskip-31.2982pt\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}\leq\omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}\leq{\mathcal{C}}t^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{[1/t]}(n+1)^{k-1}E_{n}(f)_{v},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f,t),

(6)

where throughout the paper we use
{\mathcal{C}}
in order to denote a positive constant, which may have different values at different occurrences, and we write
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f,\ldots)
to mean that
{\mathcal{C}}>0
is independent of
n,f,\ldots
.

Note that the previous direct and inverse results yield, for instance, the following equivalences

E_{n}(f)_{v}={\mathcal{O}}(n^{-r})\Longleftrightarrow\omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}={\mathcal{O}}(t^{r})\Longleftrightarrow\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}={\mathcal{O}}(t^{r})

holding for any
0<r<k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

In literature, several approximation spaces have been introduced in order to classify the smoothness of the functions
f
w.r.t. the decay of
E_{n}(f)_{v}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In particular, we recall the following ones

\displaystyle B_{r}(v):=\left\{f\in C^{0}_{v}\ :\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{r-1}E_{n}(f)_{v}<\infty\right\},\qquad r>0,

\displaystyle\mbox{equipped with}\quad\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}:=\|fv\|_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n+1)^{r-1}E_{n}(f)_{v}.

These Banach spaces belong to the class of Besov type space
B_{r,q}^{\infty}(v)
, introduced in
[
14
]
and characterized by the following norm

\|f\|_{B_{r,q}^{\infty}(v)}:=\|fv\|_{\infty}+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(n+1)^{r-\frac{1}{q}}E_{n}(f)_{v}\right]^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},&\mbox{if $\quad 1\leq q<\infty$},\\
&\\
\displaystyle\sup_{n>0}(n+1)^{r}E_{n}(f)_{v},&\mbox{if $\quad q=\infty$}.\end{array}\right.

Hence
B_{r}(v)\equiv B_{r,1}^{\infty}(v)
.

In the sequel, for
A,B>0
we will write
A\sim B
meaning that
{\mathcal{C}}^{-1}A\leq B\leq{\mathcal{C}}A
, with
{\mathcal{C}}>0
independent of the main parameters in
A,B
.
By virtue of (
5
)–(
6
) the following equivalence between the norms holds true
[
14
, Th. 3.1]
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\|fv\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}}{t^{r+1}}dt,\qquad k>r>0,

(7)

being the constant
{\mathcal{C}}
, involved in such equivalence, only depending on
r
.
Moreover, in (
7
) the main part modulus
\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}
can be also replaced by the complete modulus
\omega_{\varphi}^{k}
or by the following K–functional

K_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t^{k})_{v}:=\inf_{g^{(k-1)}\in AC_{loc}}\left[\|(f-g)v\|_{\infty}+t^{k}\|g^{(k)}\varphi^{k}v\|_{\infty}\right]

where
AC_{loc}
denotes the class of locally absolutely continuous functions on
[-1,1]
(i.e. absolutely continuous on any
[c,d]\subset(-1,1)
). We also recall that
[
13
]

K_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t^{k})_{v}\sim\omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}.

For any
r>0
, the previous spaces
B_{r}(v)
are compactly embedded in a larger space
B_{0}(v)
that is defined
[
23
]
as the “limit” case of the Besov spaces
B_{r}(v)
as

, namely

B_{0}(v):=\left\{f\in C^{0}_{v}\ :\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{E_{n}(f)_{v}}{n+1}<\infty\right\},

and it is equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{B_{0}(v)}:=\|fv\|_{\infty}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{E_{n}(f)_{v}}{n+1}.

Also in this case, by virtue of (
5
)–(
6
), we have the following equivalences

\|f\|_{B_{0}(v)}\sim\|fv\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}}{t}dt\sim\|fv\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{K_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t^{k})_{v}}{t}dt.

(8)

Moreover, the elements
f
of
B_{0}(v)
can be easily characterized with the help of the classical modulus of continuity of
g=fv
, defined as

\omega(g,t):=\sup_{x,y\in[-1,1],\ |x-y|\leq t}|g(x)-g(y)|.

Indeed a practical criterion to check whether
f\in C^{0}_{v}
belongs to
B_{0}(v)
or not is the following
[
22
, Th.2.6]

f\in B_{0}(v)\Longleftrightarrow\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(fv,t)}{t}dt<\infty,

and we also have the equivalence between the norms

\|f\|_{B_{0}(v)}\sim\|fv\|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega(fv,t)}{t}dt.

For all
r\geq 0
, if
v=1
(
\gamma=\delta=0
) we use the notation
B_{r}
instead of
B_{r}(v)
, and have the following

Lemma 2.1

For all
r\geq 0
and any
v=v^{\gamma,\delta}
with
\gamma,\delta\geq 0
we have that
f\in B_{r}(v)
iff it is
(fv)\in B_{r}
and (
4
) holds. Moreover we have

\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}\sim\|fv\|_{B_{r}},\qquad\forall f\in B_{r}(v).

(9)

Finally the following result states some asymptotic bounds for the error of the best polynomial approximation in
B_{r}(v)
.

Lemma 2.2

For any
n
sufficiently large (say
n\geq n_{0}
) it results

E_{n}(f)_{v}\leq\mathcal{C}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{B_{0}(v)}}{\log n},&\mbox{if $f\in B_{0}(v)$},\\
&\\
\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}}{n^{r}},&\mbox{if $f\in B_{r}(v)$},\quad r>0,\end{array}\right.\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f).

(10)

2.1
Mapping properties of the Hilbert transform

The approximation space
B_{0}(v)
was firstly introduced in
[
23
]
in order to find the “correct and minimal” space for studying the boundedness of the Hilbert transform w.r.t. uniform norm. More precisely, using the standard notation for the constants

c_{+}:=\max\{0,c\},\qquad c_{-}:=\max\{0,-c\},

by
[
23
, Th. 3.1]
we have the following

Theorem 2.3

Let
u=v^{a,b}
, with
a,b>-1
, be the Jacobi weight defining the Hilbert transform (
1
) and set
u=\frac{u_{+}}{u_{-}}
with
u_{+}(x):=v^{a_{+},b_{+}}(x)
and
u_{-}(x):=v^{a_{-},b_{-}}(x)
.

Let us consider the following weight functions

u_{1}(x):=u_{+}(x),\qquad u_{2}(x):=\frac{u_{-}(x)}{1+(u_{+}u_{-})(-1)|\log(1+x)|+(u_{+}u_{-})(1)|\log(1-x)|}.

Hence for all
t\in(-1,1)
and any
f\in B_{0}(u_{1})
, we have

|{\mathcal{H}}^{u}f(t)|u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left(|f(t)|u_{1}(t)+\|f\|_{B_{0}(u_{1})}\right),\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(f,t).

(11)

From the previous result it immediately follows that
{\mathcal{H}}^{u}
is a bounded map from
B_{0}(u_{1})
to
C^{0}_{u_{2}}
.

3
Filtered VP discrete approximation

This section concerns the main approximation tool we are going to use in getting our quadrature rule for (
1
).

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and denote by
p_{n}(w,x)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
the
n
–th orthonormal polynomial (with positive leading coefficient) associated to the Jacobi weight

w(x)=v^{{\alpha},{\beta}}(x)
, with
{\alpha},{\beta}>-1
.
Based on the set of zeros of
p_{n}(w)
, i. e.
X_{n}(w)
in (
2
),
the filtered VP polynomial of a function
f
is defined as follows (see
[
38
,
5
]
)

V_{n}^{m}(w,f,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x),\qquad 0<m<n,

(12)

with

\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)=\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n,j}^{m}p_{j}(w,x)p_{j}(w,x_{k}),

(13)

where
\lambda_{k}
are the Christoffel numbers defined in (
3
) and

\mu_{n,j}^{m}
are the following VP filter coefficients

\mu_{n,j}^{m}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\mbox{if}\quad j=0,\ldots,n-m,\\
&\\
\displaystyle\frac{n+m-j}{2m},&\mbox{if}\quad n-m<j<n+m.\end{array}\right.

(14)

We point out that the polynomial in (
12
) depends
on two degree–parameters:
n
, which determines the number of nodes, and
m
which determines the VP filter action range. Both the parameters are involved in the following polynomial preserving property
[
38
]
.

V_{n}^{m}(w,P)=P,\qquad\forall P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}.

(15)

In what follows we will assume that the degree–parameters
m,n
are such that

c_{1}m\leq n\leq c_{2}m,\qquad\mbox{for some $c_{2}\geq c_{1}>1$ independent of $n$ and $m$},

and we will write
m\approx n
to express this kind of relation.

We firstly recall the following theorem which gives sufficient conditions such that the map
V_{n}^{m}(w):f\in C^{0}_{v}\rightarrow V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\in C^{0}_{v}
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n

[
40
]
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
v=v^{\gamma,\delta}
with
\gamma,\delta\geq 0
and let
f\in C^{0}_{v}
. Moreover let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
m\approx n
be arbitrarily fixed and consider the filtered VP polynomial
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)
associated to the Jacobi weight
w=v^{\alpha,\beta}
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
. If we have

\left|\gamma-\delta-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\right|<1,\qquad

(16)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\frac{{\alpha}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}<\gamma\leq\frac{{\alpha}}{2}+\frac{5}{4}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{{\beta}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}<\delta\leq\frac{{\beta}}{2}+\frac{5}{4}\end{array}\right.

(17)

then we get

\|V_{n}^{m}(w,f)v\|_{\infty}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|fv\|_{\infty},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,f).

(18)

We also recall that this result has been recently improved in the special case
w
is one of the four Chebyshev weights, i.e.
|{\alpha}|=|{\beta}|=\frac{1}{2}
, and we refer the reader to
[
35

where necessary and sufficient conditions on the weights

We point out that, due to (
15
), the uniform boundedness result (

) is equivalent to the following error estimate

E_{n+m-1}(f)_{v}\leq\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}\leq{\mathcal{C}}E_{n-m}(f)_{v},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,f).

(19)

Hence, taking into account that
m\approx n
implies
(n-m)\sim n
, we can say that if (
16
)–(
17
) hold then we have

\lim_{m\approx n\rightarrow\infty}\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}=0,\qquad\forall f\in C^{0}_{v},

being the convergence order comparable with that one of the error of best polynomial approximation

In the sequel we are going to investigate the behaviour of VP filtered approximation in the case

belongs to the Besov type spaces

introduced in the previous section. We underline that recently in

, some convergence estimates were stated in Zygmund type spaces (i.e. in the Besov type space

Obviously, under the assumption of Theorem

, by (

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}}{n^{r}},&\mbox{if $f\in B_{r}(v)$},\quad r>0,\end{array}\right.

(20)

where
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f)
.

Moreover, we have the following

Theorem 3.2

Let

and

be such that

Moreover, for all

\displaystyle\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{0}(v)}

3.1
The limit case

: the Lagrange interpolation

As said in the Introduction, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, when the parameter

L_{n}(w,f,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k})l_{n,k}(x),\qquad l_{n,k}(x)=\lambda_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p_{j}(w,x_{k})p_{j}(w,x),

While under suitable conditions for the weights

is uniformly bounded w.r.t.

(cf. Th.

[

25
]

More precisely, we have the following theorem which collects the analogous of previous estimates stated for the VP approximation.

Theorem 3.3

Let
v=v^{\gamma,\delta}
and
w=v^{\alpha,\beta}
be such that
\gamma,\delta\geq 0
,
\alpha,\beta>-1
. For all
f\in C^{0}_{v}
, the conditions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\frac{{\alpha}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\leq\gamma\leq\frac{{\alpha}}{2}+\frac{5}{4}\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{{\beta}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\leq\delta\leq\frac{{\beta}}{2}+\frac{5}{4}\end{array}\right.

(24)

are necessary and sufficient for having

\|[f-L_{n}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\log nE_{n}(f)_{v},\quad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f).

(25)

Moreover, if (
24
) holds then for all
f\in B_{r}(v)
with
r>0
, we get

\displaystyle\|f-L_{n}(w,f)\|_{B_{0}(v)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{r}}\ \|f\|_{B_{r}(v)},\qquad r>0,

(26)

\displaystyle\|f-L_{n}(w,f)\|_{B_{s}(v)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\log n}{n^{r-s}}\ \|f\|_{B_{r}(v)},\qquad r\geq s>0,

(27)

where in both cases
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f)
.

Remark 3.4

Comparing the convergence results between VP and Lagrange approximations we remark that for the same class of function (say
f\in B_{r}(v)
,
r>0
) the convergence rate is similar, but in the Lagrange estimates an extra factor
\log n
appears. Moreover conditions (
17
) are wider than (
24
).

4
Filtered VP quadrature rules for the Hilbert transform

In this section we are going to introduce a new class of product integration rules for the Hilbert transform in (
1
) based on filtered VP approximation. More precisely, by
replacing
f
in (
1
) with the polynomial
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)
, we define

\mathcal{H}^{u}_{n,m}(w,f,t):=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{V_{n}^{m}(w,f,x)}{x-t}u(x)dx,\qquad-1<t<1.

(28)

More explicitly, by (
12
)-(
13
) we get the following quadrature rule (VP rule)

\mathcal{H}^{u}_{n,m}(w,f,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n+m-1}\rho_{n,j}^{m}(w,f)Q_{j}^{u}(w,t),\qquad-1<t<1,

where we set

\displaystyle Q_{j}^{u}(w,t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{p_{j}(w,x)}{x-t}u(x)dx,

(29)

\displaystyle\rho_{n,j}^{m}(w,f)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mu_{n,j}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}p_{j}(w,x_{k})f(x_{k}),

and with
\mu_{n,j}^{m}
defined in (
14
).

4.1
Computational details

First of all we point out that the coefficients
\rho^{m}_{n,j}(w,f)
do not depend on
t
, and therefore are not influenced by the closeness of
t
to any interpolation point
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.
This means that the numerical cancellation is avoided in computing these quantities.

About the functions
\{Q_{j}^{u}(w,t)\}_{j}
, we remark that only in some particular cases they are explicitly known (see e.g.
[
37
]
) while in the general case
they can be computed via a recurrence relation (see
[
20
]
). The following proposition states such relation, deducible from the well–known recurrence relation for the orthonormal sequence
\{p_{j}(w)\}_{j}

b_{j+1}p_{j+1}(w,x)=(x-a_{j})p_{j}(w,x)-b_{j}p_{j-1}(w,x),\qquad j\geq 0,

(30)

being
p_{-1}(w,x)=0
and
p_{0}(w,x)=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}w(x)dx\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
the starting values (see for instance
[
25
, pp.131-133]
for the explicit expressions of the coefficients).

Proposition 4.1

For all Jacobi weights
u,w
and any
t\in(-1,1)
, the functions
Q_{j}(t):=Q_{j}^{u}(w,t)
defined in (
29
) satisfy the following three-term recurrence relation

Q_{j+1}(t)=(A_{j}t+B_{j})Q_{j}(t)-C_{j}Q_{j-1}(t)+D_{j},\qquad j\geq 0,

where the starting values are given by

Q_{-1}(t)=0,\qquad Q_{0}(t)=\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u(x)}{x-t}dt\right)\left(\int_{-1}^{1}w(t)dt\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

and the coefficients are defined, by means of the coefficients
\{a_{j}\}_{j}
and
\{b_{j}\}_{j}
in (
30
), as follows

A_{j}=b_{j+1}^{-1},\ B_{j}=-\frac{a_{j}}{b_{j+1}},\ C_{j}=\frac{b_{j}}{b_{j+1}},\ D_{j}=\frac{1}{b_{j+1}}\int_{-1}^{1}p_{j}(w,x)u(x)dx,\quad j=0,1,\dots.

4.2
Error estimates

Let us analyze the error function

{\mathcal{E}}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t):=\left|{\mathcal{H}}^{u}f(t)-\mathcal{H}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t)\right|,\qquad-1<t<1.

(31)

Of course the behaviour of such error is influenced by the approximation error provided by the VP polynomial that has been employed. Such dependence is specified in the following convergence theorem

Theorem 4.2

Under the notation of Theorem
2.3
, for all
f\in B_{0}(u_{1})
and any
t\in(-1,1)
, we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t)u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left|f(t)-V_{n}^{m}(w,f,t)\right|u_{1}(t)+{\mathcal{C}}\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{0}(u_{1})},

(32)

where
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f,t)
.
Moreover, if
V_{n}^{m}(w):C^{0}_{u_{1}}\rightarrow C^{0}_{u_{1}}
is an uniformly bounded map w.r.t.
m\approx n
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{E}}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t)u_{2}(t)=0,\qquad\forall f\in B_{0}(u_{1}),

(33)

and the convergence holds uniformly in
t\in(-1,1)
.

The previous theorem assures that if the function
f
is in
B_{0}(u_{1})
then the proposed quadrature formula converges on conditions that the weight
w=v^{\alpha,\beta}
is suitably chosen.
In particular Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
3.1
provide the following criteria for the choice of the weight
w=v^{\alpha,\beta}

a_{+}-b_{+}-1<\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}<a_{+}-b_{+}+1,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle 2a_{+}-\frac{5}{2}\leq\alpha<2a_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\displaystyle 2b_{+}-\frac{5}{2}\leq\beta<2b_{+}+\frac{1}{2}\end{array}\right.

(34)

where
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
,
b_{+}=\max\{b,0\}
, being
u=v^{a,b}
the weight defining the Hilbert transform.

Moreover, recalling Theorem
3.2
, under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.2
the map
V_{n}^{m}(w):B_{0}(u_{1})\rightarrow B_{0}(u_{1})
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n\sim(n-m)
. Hence by the invariance property (
15
) and by (
32
) we get

{\mathcal{E}}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t)u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}|f(t)-V_{n}^{m}(w,f,t)|u_{1}(t)+{\mathcal{C}}E_{n}(f)_{B_{0}(u_{1})},

(35)

where
E_{n}(f)_{B_{0}(u_{1})}:=\inf_{P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\|f-P\|_{B_{0}(u_{1})}
and
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,m,f)
.

We point out that (
35
) says that for each
t
the convergence rate of the VP-rule depends on two components: the pointwise approximation that the VP polynomial of
f
provides at the specific
t\in(-1,1)
, and the smoothness of
f
that influences the convergence rate of the error of best polynomial approximation of
f
.

If
f
is smoother than in Theorem
4.2
, from (
32
) and (
22
) we immediately deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
4.2
, for all
f\in B_{r}(u_{1})
,
r>0
, we get

{\mathcal{E}}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t)u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}|f(t)-V_{n}^{m}(w,f,t)|u_{1}(t)+{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\log n}{n^{r}}\|f\|_{B_{r}(u_{1})},

(36)

where
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f,t).

Obviously, concerning the converge rate of the VP-rule, from (
36
) and (
20
) we get that

{\mathcal{E}}_{n,m}^{u}(w,f,t)u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\log n}{n^{r}},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,t),

(37)

holds true
\forall f\in B_{r}(u_{1})
, with
r>0
.

We conclude the section with a comparison between the proposed VP-rule and the analogous one using the Lagrange polynomial (L-rule):

{\mathcal{H}}^{u}_{n}f(t):=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{L_{n}(w,f,x)}{x-t}u(t)\ dx.

(38)

This kind of formula was considered by several authors in the case
w=u
(see for instance
[
4
]
and the reference therein). Here, set

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{u}(w,f,t):=\left|{\mathcal{H}}^{u}f(t)-\mathcal{H}_{n}^{u}(w,f,t)\right|,\qquad-1<t<1,

as a consequence of Theorems
2.3
and
3.3
, we note that if the exponents of
u=v^{a,b}
and
w=v^{\alpha,\beta}
are such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle 2a_{+}-\frac{5}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 2a_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\\
\\
\displaystyle 2b_{+}-\frac{5}{2}\leq\beta\leq 2b_{+}-\frac{1}{2}\end{array}\right.

(39)

then, for all
f\in B_{r}(u_{1})
,
r>0
, we have

{\mathcal{E}}_{n}^{u}(w,f,t)u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}|f(t)-L_{n}(w,f,t)|u_{1}(t)+{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\log^{2}n}{n^{r}}\ \|f\|_{B_{r}(u_{1})},

(40)

with
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f,t).

Proof of Lemma
2.1

In the case
r=0
, equivalence (
9
) was proved in
[
22
, Eq.(2.8)]
. The proof was mainly based on the following crucial equivalences

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{E(n)}{n}\ \sim\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E(2^{j})\ \sim\ E(1)+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{E(t^{-\theta})}{t}dt,

where
E(x)
stands for any nonnegative decreasing function defined on
[1,\infty)
and
\theta
is an arbitrary positive fixed number.

Therefore, in the general case
r>0
, (
9
) can be proved with analogous arguments by replacing the previous equivalences with the following ones

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{r-1}E(n)\ \sim\ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jr}E(2^{j})\ \sim\ E(1)+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{E(t^{-\theta})}{t^{r+1}}dt.

\square

Proof of Lemma
2.2

The statement easily follows from the Jackson type inequalities in (
5
). Using the first one of such inequalities and recalling that
\omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}
is an increasing function of
t
, for all
f\in B_{0}(v)
we get

\displaystyle E_{n}(f)_{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\omega^{k}_{\varphi}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right)_{v}=\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{\log n}\omega^{k}_{\varphi}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right)_{v}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{\log n}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}\frac{\omega^{k}_{\varphi}\left(f,t\right)_{v}}{t}\ dt\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{\log n}\|f\|_{B_{0}(v)},

having used (
8
) in the last step.

Moreover, starting from the second inequality in (
5
), for any
f\in B_{r}(v)
and
k>r
, we get

E_{n}(f)_{v}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}}{t}\ dt={\mathcal{C}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{t^{r}\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}}{t^{r+1}}\ dt\leq\frac{{\mathcal{C}}}{n^{r}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\Omega_{\varphi}^{k}(f,t)_{v}}{t^{r+1}}\ dt

and the second inequality in (
10
) follows by (
7
).

\square

Proof of Theorem
2.3

We remark that in
[
23
, Th. 3.1]
the authors have already proved that

|{\mathcal{H}}^{u}f(t)|u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|f\|_{B_{0}(u_{1})},\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(f,t)

holds. Here we show how the slightly different estimate in (
11
) can easily deduced from the proof given in
[
23
]
.

Indeed, starting from the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{u}f(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f(x)}{x-t}\frac{u_{+}(x)}{u_{-}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t)u_{+}(t)\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u_{-}^{-1}(x)}{x-t}\ dx+\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f(x)u_{+}(x)-f(t)u_{+}(t)}{x-t}\frac{dx}{u_{-}(x)}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle J_{1}(t)+J_{2}(t),

the first term
J_{1}
can be estimated by means of
[
23
, Lemma 4.4]
, which yields

\left|\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{u_{-}^{-1}(x)}{x-t}\ dx\right|\leq{\mathcal{C}}u_{2}^{-1}(t),\quad\mathcal{C}\neq{\mathcal{C}(f,t)},

and consequently

|J_{1}(t)|u_{2}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}|f(t)|u_{1}(t),\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(f,t).

The estimate of the other term
J_{2}
can be achieved taking into account that

\displaystyle J_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-1}^{\frac{t-1}{2}}+\int_{\frac{t+1}{2}}^{1}\right)\frac{f(x)u_{+}(x)-f(t)u_{+}(t)}{x-t}u_{-}^{-1}(x)\ dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{t-1}{2}}^{\frac{t+1}{2}}\frac{f(x)u_{+}(x)-f(t)u_{+}(t)}{x-t}u_{-}^{-1}(x)\ dx

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{2}+I_{3}

and applying the estimates of these two addenda given in the proof of

[
23
, Th. 3.1]
.

\square

Proof of Theorem
3.2

Let us first prove that for all
r\geq 0
, the map
V_{n}^{m}(w):B_{r}(v)\rightarrow B_{r}(v)
is uniformly bounded w.r.t.
n
. To this aim we observe that

E_{k}(V_{n}^{m}(w,f))_{v}=0,\qquad\forall k\geq n+m-1,

since
V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1}
. Moreover, for
k=0,\ldots,n+m-2
, we note that

E_{k}(V_{n}^{m}(w,f))_{v}\leq E_{k}(V_{n}^{m}(w,f)-f)_{v}+E_{k}(f)_{v}\leq\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}+E_{k}(f)_{v}.

Consequently, for all
r\geq 0
, since we are assuming that
V_{n}^{m}(w)
is uniformly bounded in
C^{0}_{v}
, we get

\displaystyle\|V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{r}(v)}=\|V_{n}^{m}(w,f)v\|_{\infty}+\sum_{k=0}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{r-1}E_{k}(V_{n}^{m}(w,f))_{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|fu\|_{\infty}+\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{r-1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{r-1}E_{k}(f)_{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}+\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{r-1}

Hence, taking into account that

\sum_{k=0}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{r-1}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log n,&\mbox{if $r=0$}\\
n^{r},&\mbox{if $r>0$}\end{array}\right.,\qquad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n),

from (
20
) we deduce

\|V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{r}(v)}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)},\quad{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f),\qquad\forall r\geq 0.

This equation and (
15
), for all
P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}
, yield

\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{r}(v)}\leq\|f-P\|_{B_{r}(v)}+\|V_{n}^{m}(w,f-P)\|_{B_{r}(v)}\leq{\mathcal{C}}\|f-P\|_{B_{r}(v)},

where
{\mathcal{C}}\neq{\mathcal{C}}(n,f,P)
and (
21
) follows by taking into account that
(n-m)\sim n
and that the polynomials are dense in the space
B_{r}(u)
,
r\geq 0
(see
[
22
]
,
[
26
]
).

Finally, let us prove (
22
)–(
23
).

We remark that

E_{k}(f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f))_{v}\left[\begin{array}[]{lr}=E_{k}(f)_{v},&\mbox{if \ $k\geq n+m-1$},\\
&\\
\leq\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty},&\mbox{if \ $k<n+m-1$}.\end{array}\right.

Consequently, for all
s\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{s}(v)}=\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^{s-1}E_{k}(f-V_{n}^{m}f)_{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|[f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)]v\|_{\infty}\left[1+\sum_{k=1}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{s-1}\right]+\sum_{k=n+m-1}^{\infty}(k+1)^{s-1}E_{k}(f)_{v}

Therefore, using (
20
) and (
10
), in the case
s=0
we get

\displaystyle\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{0}(v)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}}{n^{r}}\left[1+\sum_{k=1}^{n+m-2}\frac{1}{k+1}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}\sum_{k=n+m-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{r+1}}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}}{n^{r}}({\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{C}}\log n)

as well as, for all
s>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|f-V_{n}^{m}(w,f)\|_{B_{s}(v)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}}{n^{r}}\left[1+\sum_{k=1}^{n+m-2}(k+1)^{s-1}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}\sum_{k=n+m-1}^{\infty}(k+1)^{s-r-1}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{B_{r}(v)}}{n^{r}}\left({\mathcal{C}}+{\mathcal{C}}n^{s}\right)

\square

Drastic change in magnetic anisotropy of UTe 2 \text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} under pressure revealed by Te 125 \hphantom{{}^{\text{125}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Te} -NMR
Katsuki Kinjo, Hiroki Fujibayashi, Genki Nakamine, Shunsaku Kitagawa, Kenji Ishida, Yo Tokunaga, Hironori Sakai, Shinsaku Kambe, Ai Nakamura, Yusei Shimizu, Yoshiya Homma, Dexin Li, Fuminori Honda, Dai Aoki

Abstract
To investigate the normal-state magnetic properties of UTe 2 \text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} under pressure, we perform Te 125 \hphantom{{}^{\text{125}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{125}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Te} nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) measurements up to 2 GPa. Below 1.2 GPa, the b b -axis NMR Knight shift shows a broad maximum at the so-called T χ max T_{\chi_{\rm max}} on cooling, which is consistent with the magnetization measurement under pressure. T χ max T_{\chi_{\rm max}} decreases with increasing pressure and disappears at the critical pressure P c P_{\rm c} = 1.7 GPa, above which superconductivity is destroyed. This tendency is also observed in the temperature dependence of the nuclear spin-lattice relaxation rate 1 / T 1 1/T_{1} . At low pressures, 1 / T 1 1/T_{1} shows a conventional Fermi-liquid behavior ( 1 / T 1 ​ T 1/T_{1}T = constant) at low temperatures, indicating the formation of the heavy-fermion state. Above P c P_{\rm c} , 1 / T 1 ​ T 1/T_{1}T follows a 1 / T 1/T behavior without any crossover to the heavy-fermion state down to the lowest temperature ( ∼ 3 \sim 3 K). In addition, the NMR signals disappear below 3 K, due to the influence of the magnetically ordered moments. From the pressure dependence of the T χ max T_{\chi_{\rm max}} and Knight shift, it was found that the Fermi surface character is abruptly changed at P c P_{\rm c} , and that superconductivity is observed only in the heavy-fermion state.

The relationship between superconductivity and magnetism has been intensively studied in condensed-matter physics.
This is because magnetism, which was once considered an enemy of superconductivity, is now regarded as a parent state of various unconventional superconductors
[
1
]
.
In uranium(U)-based superconductors, such as
\text{UGe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
under pressure (
P

[

The upper critical fields (

H

2

(magnetic easy axis)
[
5

, but that of URhGe
[
6
]
and UCoGe
[
7
]
is enhanced by
H\parallel b
(magnetic hard axis).
Therefore, these are the leading candidates for spin-triplet superconductivity
[
8
]
.

A new U-based superconductor
\text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
was discovered and its superconducting (SC) transition temperature (
T_{\rm c}
) is approximately 1.6 K
[
9

.
Although
\text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Recent NMR results in the SC state strongly support the spin-triplet scenario

One of the unique properties of

This is considered to be related to the Kondo coherence temperature, and thus, the

Under a high magnetic field along the

As the relation between

Because superconductivity is also destroyed at the same field, the close relationship between superconductivity and the Kondo coherent state is expected.
Therefore, it is important to investigate the relationship between

and superconductivity in
\text{UTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Under hydrostatic pressure, it was reported that its

The specific heat measurement and the anisotropy of

Recently, the magnetic susceptibility measurement under pressure revealed the two magnetic anomalies above

[

The magnetic-ordered (MO) state is characterized by the sharp change in the magnetic susceptibility, and the weakly-magnetic-ordered (WMO) state is characterized by the broad maximum of the susceptibility for

The magnetic short-range correlations are considered to develop in the WMO state

The magnetic characters of these two states are still under investigation.

Although the pressure-induced phases in

NMR under pressure is a microscopic measurement that can investigate magnetic properties via the hyperfine fields at the NMR-nuclear site without background subtraction from a pressure cell.

In conclusion, from the

c

Despite the similarity of the electronic states in the normal state and the SC properties between UTe

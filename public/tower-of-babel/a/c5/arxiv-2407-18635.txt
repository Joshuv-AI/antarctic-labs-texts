Mean-field control of non exchangeable systems
Anna De Crescenzo, Marco Fuhrman, Idris Kharroubi, Huyên Pham

Abstract
We study the optimal control of mean-field systems with heterogeneous and asymmetric interactions. This leads to considering a family of controlled Brownian diffusion processes with dynamics depending on the whole collection of marginal probability laws. We prove the well-posedness of such systems and define the control problem together with its related value function. We next prove a law invariance property for the value function which allows us to work on the set of collections of probability laws. We show that the value function satisfies a dynamic programming principle (DPP) on the flow of collections of probability measures. We also derive a chain rule for a class of regular functions along the flows of collections of marginal laws of diffusion processes. Combining the DPP and the chain rule, we prove that the value function is a viscosity solution of a Bellman dynamic programming equation in a L 2 L^{2} -set of Wasserstein space-valued functions.

The study of large population and complex systems is a major question in mathematical modelling with various applications in our society like e.g. social networks, power grid networks, financial markets, lightning networks.

Our main goal is to provide an analytic characterization of the solution to this novel class of control problems by adopting a dynamic programming approach. This will be achieved through the following steps.

We define the value function associated to our MFC problem, and by a law invariance property, it is a
function defined on

Next, we aim to derive an Itô’s formula for the collection of flow of probability measures

A verification theorem is shown for classical solutions to the Bellman equation, and in general, we prove the (discontinuous) viscosity property of the value function to the Bellman equation. Uniqueness of viscosity solutions is beyond the scope of this paper and postponed for future research.

For every

Denote

Then we consider the equation

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X^{\epsilon,u}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)+\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\bar{X}^{\epsilon,u}_{s}|^{2}\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)

(3.18)

\displaystyle\quad\leq T\left\{\int_{U}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X^{\epsilon,u}_{s}-\bar{X}^{\epsilon,u}_{s}|^{2}]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)\right\}^{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}}\left\{\int_{U}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}(1+|X^{\epsilon,u}_{s}|+|\bar{X}^{\epsilon,u}_{s}|)^{2}]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)\right\}^{\frac{2-\gamma_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left\{\epsilon^{2}\,\lambda(U)\right\}^{\gamma_{1}/2}

(3.28)

by (

and (

Using the inequality

similar passages and the Hölder inequality allow to treat the other terms and conclude that

In view of Theorem

3.2
Dynamic programming principle

We note that for the solution
X_{s}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}
to equation (
2.4
) we have the following flow property: for
0\leq t\leq\theta\leq T
,
\alpha\in{\cal A}
, and
\xi\in{\cal I}_{t}
we have, for every
\lambda-
a.e.
u\in U
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\displaystyle X_{s}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}

\displaystyle=\;X_{s}^{\theta,X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha},\alpha,u},\qquad s\in[\theta,T].

(3.30)

This follows immediately from the uniqueness statement in Theorem
2.1
, since both terms are solution to the state equation (
2.4
) in the interval
[\theta,T]
. As a consequence, we can state the dynamic programming principle for the value function
V
.

Theorem 3.2

For
t\in[0,T]
and
\xi\in{\cal I}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle V(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha\in{\cal A}}\left\{\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{\theta}f\big(u,X_{s}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u},{\mathbb{P}_{X_{s}^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}}},\alpha_{s}^{u},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{s}^{\cdot}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad+\;V\left(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u}\right)\right\},

for any
\theta\in[t,T]
.

Proof.

Fix
0\leq t\leq\theta\leq T
and
\xi\in{\cal I}_{t}
. For
\alpha\in{\cal A}
we have from (
3.30
) and the definitions of the cost functional
J
, and the value function
V

\displaystyle J(t,\xi,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{\theta}f(u,X^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{X^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}})\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle+\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\theta}^{T}f\big(u,X^{u,\theta,X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha},\alpha}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{X^{\theta,X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha},\alpha,\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\;g\big(u,X^{\theta,X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha},\alpha,u}_{T},\mathbb{P}_{X^{\theta,X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha},\alpha,\cdot}_{T}}\big)\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{\theta}f\big(u,X^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{X^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)+V\left(\theta,({X^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}_{s}})_{u}\right)\;.

Since the previous inequality holds for any
\alpha\in{\cal A}
, we get

\displaystyle V(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha\in{\cal A}}\left\{\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{t}^{\theta}f\big(u,X_{s}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u},\alpha_{s}^{u},\mathbb{P}_{X_{s}^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\right]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)+V\left(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u}\right)\right\}.

We turn to the reverse inequality. Fix
\alpha\in{\cal A}
and
\varepsilon>0
. From the definition of the value function

, there exists some
\alpha^{\varepsilon}=(\alpha^{\varepsilon,u})_{u}\in{\cal A}
such that

\displaystyle J\big(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u},\alpha^{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\;V\big(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u}\big)+\varepsilon

We next define the control
\bar{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}=(\bar{\alpha}^{\varepsilon,u})_{u}
by

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}^{\varepsilon,u}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{u}_{s}\mathds{1}_{[t,\theta)}(s)+\alpha^{\varepsilon,u}_{s}\mathds{1}_{[\theta,T]}(s)\;,\quad s\in[0,T]\;,

for
u\in U
and
s\in[t,T]
. We obviously have
\bar{\alpha}^{\varepsilon}\in{\cal A}
. Moreover, using the flow property (

\displaystyle J(t,\xi,\bar{\alpha}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{\theta}f(u,X^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{X^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}})\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)+J\big(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u},\alpha^{\varepsilon}\big).

From (

), we get

\displaystyle J(t,\xi,\bar{\alpha}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{\theta}f(u,X^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{X^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}})\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle+\;V\left(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u}\right)+\varepsilon,

and so

\displaystyle V(t,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{t}^{\theta}f\big(u,X^{t,\xi,\alpha,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{X^{t,\xi,\alpha,\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{\cdot}_{s}}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)+V\big(\theta,(X_{\theta}^{t,\xi,\alpha,u})_{u}\big)+\varepsilon.

4
Itô formula

4.1
Derivatives on square integrable measure maps

In our context, the Itô formula describes the time derivative of the composition of a real function
v(\mu)
of
\mu\in L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and a map
s\mapsto\mu_{s}
corresponding to the law of a family of stochastic processes
(X^{u})_{u}
, namely
\mu_{s}^{u}=\mathbb{P}_{X^{u}_{s}}
.
It holds under regularity assumptions on
v
- that may also depend explicitly on time - and requires the definition of derivatives for functions
v:L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to\mathbb{R}
that we are now going to introduce. Given
\mu

\in

L_{\lambda}^{2}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and a measurable function
(u,x)

\in

U\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mapsto

\varphi(u,x)
, with quadratic growth in
x
, uniformly in
u
, we define the duality product:

\displaystyle<\varphi,\mu>

\displaystyle:=\;\int_{U}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(u,x)\mu^{u}(\mathrm{d}x)\lambda(\mathrm{d}u).

(4.1)

Definition 4.1

Given a function

v:L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that a measurable function

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{\delta m}v:L_{\lambda}^{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\times U\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\ni\;(\mu,u,x)\;\longmapsto\;\frac{\delta}{\delta m}v(\mu)(u,x)

(4.2)

is the linear functional derivative of
v
if

1.

for every compact set
K\subset L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
there exists a constant
C_{K}>0
such that

\left|\frac{\delta}{\delta m}v(\mu)(u,x)\right|\leq C_{K}\,(1+|x|^{2}),

for every
u\in U
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu\in K
;

2.

for every
\mu,\nu\in L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
we have

\displaystyle v(\nu)-v(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}<\frac{\delta}{\delta m}v(\mu+\theta(\nu-\mu))(.),\nu-\mu>\mathrm{d}\theta

(4.3)

(a)

(i)

{\bf\Psi}(\nu)^{u}=\mathbb{P}_{X^{\nu,u}}
, the law of the process
X^{\nu,u}
seen as a probability on
C_{[t,T]}^{d}
.
We will prove the following claim:

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{the map }u\mapsto{\cal L}(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})\text{ is Borel measurable }\\
\text{from }U\text{ to }{\cal P}_{2}(C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)).\end{array}

(A.2)

Admitting this for a moment, standard estimates on the stochastic equation in (
A.1
) show that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X_{s}^{\nu,u}|^{2}\Big]\leq c\left(1+\mathbb{E}[|\xi^{u}|^{2}]+\int_{0}^{T}\Big\{\mathbb{E}[d_{A}(\alpha_{s}^{u},0)^{2}]+{\cal W}_{2}(\nu_{s}^{u},\delta_{0})^{2}+{\cal W}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{s}^{v}},\delta_{0})^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

for some constant
c>0
(that does not depend on
\nu
). Noting that
{\cal W}_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{s}^{u}},\delta_{0})^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}[d_{A}(\alpha_{s}^{u},0)^{2}]
and
recalling the admissibility condition
(
2.6
) we find

\|X^{\nu}\|^{2}=\int_{U}\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X_{s}^{\nu,u}|^{2}]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)<\infty
and so
X^{\nu}\in{\cal S}_{t}
.
This implies that

(\mathbb{P}_{X^{u}})_{u}
belongs to
L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
, so that the map

{\bf\Psi}:L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))\to L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
is well defined.

We will next prove the following claim:

{\bf\Psi}\text{ has unique fixed point }\bar{\nu}\text{ in }L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d})).

(A.3)

This leads immediately to the required conclusion. Indeed, the process
X^{\bar{\nu}}
corresponding to the fixed point is clearly a solution. Moreover,
if
(\tilde{X}^{u})_{u}\in{\cal S}_{t}
is another solution then its law

(\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{X}^{u}})_{u}
also belongs to
L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
and it is a fixed point of
{\bf\Psi}
; it must therefore coincide with
\bar{\nu}
and it follows that

X^{u}=\tilde{X}^{u}
for
\lambda
-almost all
u\in U
since they are both solutions to the same equations (
A.1
).

In order to conclude the proof we have to prove the two claims.

Step I: proof of claim (
A.2
)
.

We note that
\hat{w}\oplus\check{w}
depends on
\check{w}
only through its increments, namely denoting
\check{w}-\check{w}_{t}
the function
(\check{w}(s)-\check{w}(t))_{s\in[t,T]}
we have

\hat{w}\oplus\check{w}=\hat{w}\oplus(\check{w}-\check{w}_{t})
.

Given
t\in[0,T]
and a Borel measurable function
\alpha:U\times[0,T]\times C_{[0,T]}^{\ell}\times(0,1)\to A
, we define a Borel measurable function
\tilde{\alpha}:U\times[0,T]\times C_{[t,T]}^{\ell}\times C_{[0,t]}^{\ell}\times(0,1)\to A

setting

\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,\check{w},\hat{w},z)=\alpha(u,s,\hat{w}\oplus\check{w},z)

(A.4)

for
u\in U
,
s\in[0,T]
,
\hat{w}\in C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}
,
\check{w}\in C^{\ell}_{[t,T]}
,
z\in(0,1)
. We note that this formula establishes a bijection between those classes of functions whose inverse is

\displaystyle\alpha(u,s,w,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,w_{[0,t]},w_{[t,T]},z),

where we recall that
w_{[0,t]}
,
w_{[t,T]}
denote the restrictions of
w\in C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}
to the indicated intervals.
Finally we note that, for
s\in[t,T]
,

\alpha(u,s,w_{s\wedge\cdot},z)=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,w_{s\wedge\cdot},w_{[0,t]},z).

(A.5)

where we write
w_{s\wedge\cdot}
instead of the more cumbersome
\big(w_{[t,T]}\big)_{s\wedge\cdot}
.

Below we need a similar notation for functions defined on spaces of paths. Given

\Phi:C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}

it is convenient to consider the function
\tilde{\Phi}:C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}

defined by

\tilde{\Phi}(x,\check{w},\hat{w},z)=\Phi(x,\hat{w}\oplus\check{w},z),\quad x\in C^{d}_{[t,T]},\check{w}\in C^{\ell}_{[t,T]},\hat{w}\in C^{\ell}_{[0,t]},z\in(0,1).

(A.6)

Using these notations
we write equation (
A.1
) in a different way. Recalling

(
A.4
)
and
(
A.5
)
we first have

\alpha_{s}^{u}=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s}-W^{u}_{t},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})\;,\quad s\in[t,T]\;.

We note that
(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})
is independent of the increments
W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s}-W^{u}_{t}
and the law
of
(W^{u},Z^{u})
is the product
\mathbb{W}_{T}\otimes m
of the Wiener measure
\mathbb{W}_{T}
on
C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}
and the Lebesgue measure
m
on
(0,1)
. Given a bounded continuous
\phi:A\to\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\phi(\alpha^{u}_{s})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\phi\Big(\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})\Big)\Big]

(A.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\mathbb{E}\Big[\phi\Big(\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},w^{\prime}_{[0,t]},z^{\prime})\Big)\Big]\,{\cal L}(W^{u},Z^{u})(\mathrm{d}w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})

(A.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\mathbb{E}\Big[\phi\Big(\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},w^{\prime}_{[0,t]},z^{\prime})\Big)\Big]\,(\mathbb{W}_{T}\otimes m)(\mathrm{d}w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}).

(A.9)

Setting
\alpha_{s}^{u,w,z}=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},w_{[0,t]},z)
this
shows that the laws
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{u}_{s}}
may be written

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{u}_{s}}=\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{s}^{u,w^{\prime},z^{\prime}}}\,(\mathbb{W}_{T}\times m)(\mathrm{d}w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime}).

(A.10)

Equation (
A.1
) then becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX^{\nu,u}_{s}\!=\!\sigma\!\left(u,X^{\nu,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},(\nu_{s}^{v})_{v},\left(\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{s}^{v,w^{\prime},z^{\prime}}}\,(\mathbb{W}_{T}\times m)(dw^{\prime}\,dz^{\prime})\right)_{v}\right)\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s},\;s\in[t,T],\\
X_{t}^{\nu,u}=\xi^{u},\\
\alpha_{s}^{u}=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}).\end{array}\right.

(A.11)

Let us consider the analogue of this equation where the random elements
\xi^{u}
,
W^{u}_{[0,t]}
,
Z^{u}
are “freezed” at given points
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
w\in C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}
,
z\in(0,1)
, namely

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX^{\nu,u,x,w,z}_{s}\!\!=\sigma\!\left(\!u,X^{\nu,u,x,w,z}_{s}\!\!,\alpha^{u,w,z}_{s}\!\!,(\nu^{v}_{s})_{v},\left(\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\mathbb{P}_{\alpha_{s}^{v,w^{\prime},z^{\prime}}}\,(\mathbb{W}_{T}\times m)(dw^{\prime}\,dz^{\prime})\right)_{v}\right)\mathrm{d}W^{u}_{s}\\
X_{t}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}=x,\\
\alpha_{s}^{u,w,z}=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,W^{u}_{\cdot\wedge s},w,z).\end{array}\right.

(A.12)

For fixed
u\in U
, this is a stochastic equation depending measurably on the parameters
x,w,z
and it admits as a solution a measurable function
(\omega,s,x,w,z)\mapsto X_{s}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}(\omega)
.
Measurability is understood in the following sense. Since the equation is driven by the increments of the Brownian motion
(W_{s}^{u}-W_{t}^{u})_{s\geq t}
the solution is predictable with respect to the corresponding filtration, i.e. measurable for the corresponding
\sigma
-algebra on
[t,T]
, say
{\cal P}^{t}
; measurability of
(\omega,s,x,w,z)\mapsto X_{s}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}(\omega)
is understood with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
{\cal P}^{t}\otimes{\cal B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times C^{\ell}_{[t,T]}\times(0,1))
: see Stricker-Yor and the references therein.
Therefore we may consider the composition

X^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}}
obtained substituting
(x,w,z)
with
(\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})
. Using the fact that the latter is independent of

X^{\nu,u,x,w,z}
we may see that this is well defined; indeed, if
\tilde{X}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}
is another solution to (
A.12
) with the same measurability properties we have

G(x,w,z):=\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X_{s}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}|^{2}]=0

for every
x,w,z
and by independence

\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X_{s}^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}}-\tilde{X}_{s}^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}}|^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[G(\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})]=0

so that
X^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}}
and
\tilde{X}^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}}

are indistinguishable. By similar arguments one concludes that the process
X^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}}

satisfies
equation
(
A.11
)
and therefore it coincides with

X^{\nu,u}
, up to a
\mathbb{P}
-null set, for
\lambda
-almost all
u
.

Our aim is to prove that the law

{\cal L}(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})
(a measure on

C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)
) depends in a measurable way on
u\in U
. To this end we use the criterion (
2.1
) and we
consider the integral of an arbitrary bounded continuous function
\Phi:C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
that we write in the form

\displaystyle\int_{C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\Phi(x,w,z)\,{\cal L}(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})(\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}z)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\Phi}\left(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u}\right)\bigg]

(A.13)

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\Phi}\left(X^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u}\right)\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tilde{\Phi}\left(X^{\nu,u,\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}},W^{u}_{[t,T]},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}\right)\bigg],

(A.14)

using the notation
\tilde{\Phi}
introduced in (
A.6
).
We recall that we may replace
W^{u}_{[t,T]}
by its increments

W^{u}_{[t,T]}-W^{u}_{t}
, which are independent of
(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})
.
Noting that equation (
A.12
) is driven by
W^{u}_{[t,T]}-W^{u}_{t}
we conclude that the pair
(X^{\nu,u,x,w,z},W^{u}_{[t,T]}-W^{u}_{t})
is independent of
(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u},\xi^{u})
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\Phi}\left(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u}\right)\bigg]

(A.15)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}\times(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\tilde{\Phi}\left(X^{\nu,u,x,w,z},W^{u}_{[t,T]},w,z\right)\bigg]\,{\cal L}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u},\xi^{u})(\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x).

(A.16)

Now let us take an arbitrary complete probability space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\cal F},\hat{\mathbb{P}})
with an
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
-valued standard Brownian motion
\hat{W}
and denote
\hat{\mathbb{F}}=(\hat{\cal F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
the corresponding completed Brownian filtration.
For every
u\in U
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
w\in C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}
,
z\in(0,1)
we consider the equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d\hat{X}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}_{s}\!\!=\sigma\!\left(\!u,\hat{X}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}_{s}\!,\hat{\alpha}^{u,w,z}_{s}\!,\left(\nu_{s}^{v}\right)_{v},\!\left(\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)}\hat{\mathbb{P}}_{\hat{\alpha}_{s}^{v,w^{\prime},z^{\prime}}}\,(\mathbb{W}_{T}\times m)(\mathrm{d}w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}z^{\prime})\right)_{v}\right)\mathrm{d}\hat{W}_{s}\\
\hat{X}_{t}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}=x,\\
\hat{\alpha}_{s}^{u,w,z}=\tilde{\alpha}(u,s,\hat{W}_{\cdot\wedge s},w,z).\end{array}\right.

(A.17)

Since
\hat{W}
does not depend on
u
, this is a stochastic equation depending measurably on all the parameters
u,x,w,z
(including
u
) and it admits as its solution a measurable function
(\omega,s,u,x,w,z)\mapsto\hat{X}_{s}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}(\omega)
.
Similar as before, measurability is understood with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
{\cal P}^{t}\otimes{\cal B}(U\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times C^{\ell}_{[t,T]}\times(0,1))
: see Stricker-Yor.
Comparing this equation with (
A.12
) we see that they have the same coefficients and are both driven by the increments of Brownian motions -
W^{u}
and
\hat{W}
respectively - on the interval
[t,T]
.
As we have strong uniqueness to the considered SDEs, we conclude that on the space
C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[t,T]}
the law of
(X^{\nu,u,x,w,z},W^{u}-W^{u}_{t})
under
\mathbb{P}
is the same as the
law of
(\hat{X}^{\nu,u,x,w,z},\hat{W}-\hat{W}_{t})
under
\hat{\mathbb{P}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Phi\left(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u}\right)\bigg]

(A.18)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}\times(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\tilde{\Phi}\left(\hat{X}^{\nu,u,x,w,z},\hat{W}_{[t,T]},w,z\right)\bigg]\,{\cal L}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u},\xi^{u})(\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x).

(A.19)

Now what occurs under the sign
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is a measurable function of
u,x,w,z
. Since we are assuming that

{\cal L}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u},\xi^{u})
depends measurably on
u
we conclude that
{\cal L}(X^{\nu,u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})
is also a measurable function of
u
(as a measure on

C^{d}_{[t,T]}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,T]}\times(0,1)
).
This concludes the proof of the claim (
A.2
).

We note for later use that, when the function
\Phi
only depends on its first argument, equation
(
A.18
)
becomes a formula for the map

{\bf\Psi}(\nu)=({\bf\Psi}(\nu)^{u})_{u}
introduced at the beginning of the proof. Indeed, recalling that
{\bf\Psi}(\nu)^{u}=\mathbb{P}_{X^{\nu,u}}
, it follows from (
A.18
) that for every continuous bounded
\phi:C^{d}_{[t,T]}\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{C^{d}_{[t,T]}}\phi(x)\,{\bf\Psi}(\nu)^{u}(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\phi\left(X^{\nu,u}\right)\bigg]

(A.20)

\displaystyle=\int_{C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}\times(0,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\phi\left(\hat{X}^{\nu,u,x,w,z}\right)\bigg]\,{\cal L}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u},\xi^{u})(\mathrm{d}w\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}x).

(A.21)

Step II: proof of claim (
A.3
).

For any
r\in[t,T]
we consider the space
L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,r]}^{d}))
with the corresponding distance, that will be denoted
{\bf d}_{t}^{r}
.
Given
\nu,\mu\in L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
, let
(X^{\nu,u})_{u}
,
(X^{\mu,u})_{u}
denote the corresponding solutions to
(
A.1
).
For suitable constants

C_{1},C_{2}

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,r]}|X_{s}^{\nu,u}-X_{s}^{\mu,u}|^{2}\Big]

(A.22)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{1}\,\mathbb{E}\,\int_{t}^{r}\Big|\sigma\left(u,X^{\nu,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\left(\nu_{s}^{v}\right)_{v},\left(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{v}_{s}}\right)_{v}\right)-\sigma\left(u,X^{\mu,u}_{s},\alpha^{u}_{s},\left(\mu_{s}^{v}\right)_{v},\left(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha^{v}_{s}}\right)_{v}\right)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

(A.23)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{2}\,\int_{t}^{r}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\,[|X^{\nu,u}_{s}-X^{\mu,u}_{s}|^{2}]+{\bf d}(\nu_{s},\mu_{s})^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s

(A.24)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{2}\,\int_{t}^{r}\Big\{\sup_{q\in[t,s]}\mathbb{E}\,[|X^{\nu,u}_{q}-X^{\mu,u}_{q}|^{2}]+{\bf d}_{t}^{s}(\nu,\mu)^{2}\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(A.25)

Next we note that

(X^{\nu,u},X^{\mu,u})_{u}
(a collection of
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
-valued processes)
satisfies a stochastic equation to which the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
apply. In particular, it starts
at time
t
from the initial condition
(\xi^{u},\xi^{u})_{u}
, which is admissible, since the map
u\mapsto{\cal L}(\xi^{u},\xi^{u},W_{[0,t]},Z^{u})
is Borel measurable.
So we can apply the already proved claim (
A.2
) and conclude in particular that

u\mapsto{\cal L}(X^{\nu,u},X^{\mu,u})
is Borel measurable. It follows that both sides of the previously displayed inequality are measurable functions of
u
.
Integrating with respect to
\lambda(du)
and applying the Gronwall lemma yields

{\bf d}_{t}^{r}(\Psi(\nu),\Psi(\mu))^{2}\leq\int_{U}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,r]}|X_{s}^{\nu,u}-X_{s}^{\mu,u}|^{2}\Big]\,\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)\leq C\int_{t}^{r}{\bf d}_{t}^{s}(\nu,\mu)^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad r\in[t,T],

(A.26)

for some constant
C>0
that only depends on the Lipschitz constants of
b,\sigma
, on
T
and on
\lambda(U)
. Setting
r=T
we obtain

{\bf d}_{t}^{T}(\Psi(\nu),\Psi(\mu))^{2}\leq\|X^{\nu}-X^{\mu}\|^{2}\leq C\cdot(T-t)\,{\bf d}_{t}^{T}(\nu,\mu)^{2}

(A.27)

which proves in particular the continuity of
\Psi
.
Iterating
(
A.26
) one proves that

{\bf d}_{t}^{r}({\bf\Psi}^{(k+1)}(\nu),{\bf\Psi}^{(k+1)}(\mu))^{2}\leq\frac{C^{k+1}}{k!}\int_{t}^{r}{\bf d}_{t}^{s}(\nu,\mu)^{2}(r-s)^{k}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(A.28)

Choosing an arbitrary
\nu^{(0)}\in L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
and setting
\nu^{(k+1)}={\bf\Psi}(\nu^{(k)})
for
k\geq 0
, it follows that

{\bf d}_{t}^{T}(\nu^{(k+1)},\nu^{(k)})^{2}\leq\frac{C^{k}(T-t)^{k}}{k!}{\bf d}_{t}^{T}(\nu^{(1)},\nu^{(0)})^{2}.

Now standard arguments allow to conclude that the sequence
(\nu^{(k)})_{k}
is Cauchy for
{\bf d}_{t}^{T}
and it converges in
L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
to a limit, denoted
\bar{\nu}
, which is a fixed point of the map
{\bf\Psi}
. The uniqueness of the fixed point follows from (
A.28
).
The claim (
A.3
) is proved.
∎

A.2
Uniqueness in law

Proposition A.1

Let
t\in[0,T]
and
\alpha:U\times[0,T]\times C_{[0,T]}^{\ell}\times(0,1)\to A
a Borel measurable function
Fix
\xi=(\xi^{u})_{u}\in{\cal I}_{t}
and denote by
X=(X^{u})_{u}
the unique solution to
(
2.4
)
.

For
u\in U
, we consider an
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
-valued random process
(\tilde{W}^{u}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, a real random variable
\tilde{Z}^{u}
and an
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued random variable
\tilde{\xi}^{u}

(possibly defined on a different probability space)
such that

{\cal L}(\xi^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})={\cal L}(\tilde{\xi}^{u},\tilde{W}^{u}_{[0,t]},\tilde{Z}^{u})

(A.29)

for all
u\in U
.
We define
(\tilde{X}^{u})_{u}
as the unique solution to the SDE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}d\tilde{X}^{u}_{s}&=&b\left(u,\tilde{X}^{u}_{s},\tilde{\alpha}^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{X}^{\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\alpha}^{\cdot}_{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&&+\sigma\left(u,\tilde{X}^{u}_{s},\tilde{\alpha}^{u}_{s},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{X}^{\cdot}_{s}},\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\alpha}^{\cdot}_{s}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}^{u}_{s},\;\;s\in[t,T],\\
\tilde{X}_{t}^{u}&=&\tilde{\xi}^{u},\;\;u\in U,\end{array}\right.

(A.30)

where
\tilde{\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{t}^{u}={\alpha}(u,t,\tilde{W}^{u}_{\cdot\wedge t},\tilde{Z}^{u}),\quad t\in[0,T],\,u\in U.

Then we have

\displaystyle{\cal L}(X^{u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\tilde{X}^{u},\tilde{W}^{u}_{[0,T]},\tilde{Z}^{u})

for all
u\in U
.

Proof. We only sketch the proof.
In the proof of Theorem
2.1
the solution was obtained via a fixed point for the map
\nu\mapsto\Psi(\nu)
introduced there. The fixed point can be obtained by a Picard iteration scheme
\nu^{n+1}=\Psi(\nu^{n})
starting from
\nu^{0}={\cal L}(\xi)={\cal L}(\tilde{\xi})
.
In view of
(
A.29
),
formula
(
A.18
)
makes it clear that at each iteration we have

\displaystyle{\cal L}(X^{\nu^{n},u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\tilde{X}^{\nu^{n},u},\tilde{W}^{u}_{[0,T]},\tilde{Z}^{u})

for all
u\in U
. We know
that the sequence
(\nu^{n})
converges in
L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(C_{[t,T]}^{d}))
to the fixed point. This allows to pass to the limit in
(
A.18
)
and conclude that

{\cal L}(X^{u},W^{u}_{[0,T]},Z^{u})={\cal L}(\tilde{X}^{u},\tilde{W}^{u}_{[0,T]},\tilde{Z}^{u})
as required.

\Box

Proposition B.1

Let

and

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal L}\big({\xi}^{u},W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}\big)

for every

(W,Z)
(defined on an arbitrary probability space), where

We first need to prove the following result.

Lemma B.1

For any family
(Y^{u})_{u}
of random variables uniformly distributed on
(0,1)
and any family
(\Phi^{u})_{u}
of Borel maps from
(0,1)
to some Polish space
S
such that
u\mapsto{\cal L}(\Phi^{u}(Y^{u}))
is Borel measurable, there exists a Borel map
\tilde{\Phi}:\penalty\ U\times(0,1)\rightarrow S
such that
{\cal L}(\Phi^{u}(Y^{u}))={\cal L}(\tilde{\Phi}(u,Y))
for every
u\in U
where
Y
is any random variable uniformly distributed on
(0,1)
(defined on an arbitrary probability space).

Proof.
To prove this claim note that setting

Q(u,A)={\cal L}(\Phi^{u}(Y^{u}))(A)
, for
u\in U
and any Borel set
A\subset U
, we define a transition kernel from
U
to
S
, by the measurability assumption. It is a classical result of Skorohod (used in the proof of the Skorohod representation theorem) that there exists a Borel function
\tilde{\Phi}(u,\cdot):(0,1)\to S
carrying the Lebesgue measure on
(0,1)
to the measure
Q(u,\cdot)
and therefore satisfying
{\cal L}(\tilde{\Phi}(u,Y))=Q(u,\cdot)={\cal L}(\Phi^{u}(Y^{u}))
.
The function

\tilde{\Phi}(u,\cdot)
is obtained from
Q(u,\cdot)
in a constructive way which shows that, since
Q
is a kernel, the function
\tilde{\Phi}(u,y)
is in fact Borel measurable in
(u,y)\in U\times(0,1)
: for a detailed proof see for instance the proof of Theorem 3.1.1 in
[
20
]
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.
Fix a bijection
\psi:C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}\times(0,1)\rightarrow(0,1)

\psi

and
(W_{[0,t]}^{u},Z^{u})=\psi^{-1}(F^{-1}(Y^{u}))

\displaystyle{\cal L}\big(\underline{\xi}^{u}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}),W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal L}\big(\Phi^{u}(Y^{u})\big)

where

\Phi^{u}(y)\in S:=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times C^{\ell}_{[0,t]}\times(0,1)

\Phi^{u}(y)=\big(\underline{\xi^{u}}(\psi^{-1}(F^{-1}(y))),\psi^{-1}(F^{-1}(y))\big)

Proposition B.2

Let
(t_{n},\mu_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of
[0,T]\times L^{2}_{\lambda}({\cal P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

\underline{\xi}
and
({\underline{\xi}}_{n})_{n}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

(\mathbb{P}_{\underline{\xi}^{u}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})})_{u}=\mu
,

(\mathbb{P}_{\underline{\xi}^{n,u}(W^{u}_{[0,t_{n}]},Z^{u})})_{u}=\mu_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|\underline{\xi}^{n,u}(W^{u}_{[0,t_{n}]},Z^{u})-\underline{\xi}^{u}(W^{u}_{[0,t]},Z^{u})\big|^{2}\Big]\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow+\infty]{}

\displaystyle 0\;.

Proof.
As in the proof of the Proposition
B.1
, we simply need to prove the following statement:

For any family
(Y^{u})_{u}
of random variables uniformly distributed on
(0,1)
and any Polish space
S
,
there exists Borel maps
\Phi
and
\Phi_{n}
,

, from
U\times(0,1)
to
S
such that
{\cal L}(\Phi(u,Y^{u}))=\mu^{u}
,

for every

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|{\cal L}(\Phi(u,Y^{u}))-{\cal L}(\Phi_{n}(u,Y^{u}))\big|^{2}\Big]\lambda(\mathrm{d}u)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow+\infty]{}

\displaystyle 0\;.

Let
(\pi^{n})_{n}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x-y|^{2}\pi_{n}^{u}(\mathrm{d}x,\mathrm{d}y)

Let

Abstract
Generalized Kerr-Schild nilpotent deformations of an arbitrary background spacetime g ¯ μ ​ ν ​ ( x ) \overline{g}_{\mu\nu}(x) are considered. Those are of the form g ¯ μ ​ ν + l μ ​ l ν \overline{g}_{\mu\nu}+l_{\mu}l_{\nu} with l 2 = 0 l^{2}=0 . The relationship between the Ricci tensor of the background metric and the Ricci tensor of the deformed metric is found exactly. It consists of two terms, one is essentially the Fierz-Pauli operator, and the other is new. When the background is flat, the Kerr-Schild family is recovered. Novel examples for more general backgrounds (even including some simple sources) are discussed.

Introduction

The importance of finding exact solutions of Einstein’s equations of General Relativity (GR) cannot be overestimated. In spite of the portentous advances in numerical solutions, we still need them to examine global properties of the spacetime such as horizons, singularities, etc. They also serve as useful templates for simulations. Unfortunately exact solutions are few and far between. Any effort to find new such solutions is potentially useful in the endeavor of reaching a better understanding of the physics of gravitation.

The vacuum equations of GR are just the vanishing of the Ricci tensor of the spacetime

R_{\mu\nu}=0

(1)

which are nonlinear. This means that given two solutions,
g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}
and
g_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}
then the sum of the two

ag_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}+bg_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}

(2)

is not a solution. This is perhaps the main fact that prevents standard techniques (such as Fourier analysis) to be useful in this case. When we talk about linear equations, we are assuming superposition in this sense.

On the other hand, the Fierz-Pauli (FP) equations
[
3
]
are linear equations that represent a consistent quantum field free theory of spin two
h_{\mu\nu}(x)
in flat space
\eta_{\mu\nu}
(although this can be generalized to an arbitrary spacetime background)

\Box h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}h-\partial^{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}h_{\lambda\nu}-\partial^{\lambda}\partial_{\nu}h_{\lambda\mu}=0

(3)

where
h\equiv\eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}
. This theory can be proved to be the linear approximation to the Ricci tensor, id est, the first order in
\kappa\equiv\sqrt{8\pi G}
in the expansion

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu}

(4)

we shall take units with
\kappa=1
in the sequel, for simplicity.

In this paper we are going to exploit a technique to generate new Ricci flat spacetimes out of some other Ricci flat spacetime, which we will dub the
seed
. The idea is a generalization of the approach in
[
7
]
and originated in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

In the original work of Kerr and Schild they examined Ricci flat metrics for which there is a gauge of the form

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+l_{\mu}l_{\nu}

(5)

(where
\eta_{\mu\nu}
is the flat Minkowski metric) and they found all of those trough quite heavy calculations using a clever tretrad. In this paper by using an apparently coarser approach we shall find unexpected relationships with the flat space spin 2 Fierz-Pauli equation.

2
The generalized Fierz-Pauli equation.

In this section, we present the generalization of Fierz-Pauli equation for an arbitrary background. Using expansion of the metric with
\kappa=1

g_{\mu\nu}=\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}

(6)

the first step in the computation is to calculate the inverse metric,
g^{\mu\nu}
. This is only possible as an infinite power series; there is no closed formula in the general case. The same thing happens with the determinant of the metric. The one exception is precisely the hypothesis

\displaystyle h_{\mu\lambda}h^{\lambda}_{\nu}=0

\displaystyle h=0

(7)

in which case the inverse of the metric simply reads

g^{\mu\nu}=\widebar{g}^{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}

(8)

and this expansion terminates; there are no more terms in it.

Next, let us compute an
explicit
and
exact
expressions of the Ricci tensor. Cartan’s connection (that is the Christoffel connection corresponding to the full metric
g_{\mu\nu}
) can be easily computed as

\Gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=\bar{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}+C^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}

(9)

where

C^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\widebar{\nabla}_{\alpha}(h_{\beta}^{\penalty\ \mu})+\widebar{\nabla}_{\beta}(h_{\alpha}^{\penalty\ \mu})-\widebar{\nabla}^{\mu}(h_{\alpha\beta})+h^{\mu\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}(h_{\alpha\beta})\right]

(10)

with the usefull property of
C^{\lambda}_{\lambda\alpha}=0

Please note that this expression is also exact.

The corresponding Ricci tensor of the metric (
6
) is

R_{\mu\nu}=\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}+\widebar{\nabla}_{\rho}C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}-C^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma}C^{\sigma}_{\nu\rho}

(11)

After a little bit of algebra one gets

R_{\mu\nu}=\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}+R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}+R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}

(12)

where
R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(n)}}}
is the expansion at order
n
of the Ricci tensor. To be specific,

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2}\left[\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}(h_{\nu}^{\penalty\ \lambda})+\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}(h_{\mu}^{\penalty\ \lambda})-\bar{\Box}(h_{\mu\nu})\right]

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\Big[\widebar{\nabla}_{\sigma}\left[h^{\sigma\rho}\widebar{\nabla}_{\rho}(h_{\mu\nu})\right]-\widebar{\nabla}_{\sigma}(h_{\mu}^{\penalty\ \rho})\widebar{\nabla}_{\rho}(h_{\nu}^{\penalty\ \sigma})+

\displaystyle+\widebar{\nabla}^{\rho}(h_{\mu}^{\penalty\ \sigma})\widebar{\nabla}_{\rho}(h_{\nu\sigma})-\frac{1}{2}\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}(h_{\rho\sigma})\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}(h^{\rho\sigma})\Big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{3}}{2}h^{\rho\sigma}\widebar{\nabla}_{\rho}(h_{\mu\lambda})\widebar{\nabla}_{\sigma}(h_{\nu}^{\penalty\ \lambda})

(13)

To sum up, the Ricci tensor of the metric (
6
) with the condition (
7
) reduces to

R_{\mu\nu}=\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}+R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}+R_{\mu\nu}^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}

(14)

where the first term, which is linear in the deformation is exactly the linear Fierz- Pauli operator in the background
\overline{g}_{\mu\nu}
with
h\equiv\overline{g}_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}=0
and the second one is nonlinear and more complicated and contains all the nonlinear terms up to third order in the deformation. We stress that in spite of the seemingly perturbative approach, this expansion also terminates; there are no more terms in it.

The surprising thing is that the terms of Ricci tensor obey

\{R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0\}\Longrightarrow\{R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0\}

(15)

The proof is straightforward algebra.

To be specific, let us give details on the simplest case. Assume that the perturbation is a Kerr-Schild metric

h_{\mu\nu}=l_{\mu}l_{\nu}

(16)

where
l_{\mu}
is a nilpotent vector,
l^{2}=0
, in which case (
7
) is trivially satisfied. It has already been pointed out that in this case, and only in this case, the perturbative expansion has got only a finite number of terms.

Throughout this work we employ the standard
optical variables
, namely, the
acceleration

\dot{l}^{\alpha}\equiv l^{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l^{\alpha}

(17)

(which vanishes if the vector field is geodesic) and the
expansion

\theta\equiv\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l^{\lambda}

(18)

To be specific, the first order in the deformation reads

R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}={1\over 2}\left[\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\alpha}(l^{\mu}l_{\beta})+\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\beta}(l^{\mu}l_{\alpha})-\bar{\Box}(l_{\alpha}l_{\beta})\right]

(19)

the remaining terms in the expansion of the Ricci tensor are given by

\displaystyle R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\theta\left(\dot{l}_{\mu}l_{\nu}+l_{\mu}\dot{l}_{\nu}\right)+\ddot{l}_{\mu}l_{\nu}+\dot{l}_{\mu}\dot{l}_{\nu}+l_{\mu}\ddot{l}_{\nu}-\dot{l}^{\rho}\bar{\nabla}_{\rho}\left(l_{\mu}l_{\nu}\right)-

\displaystyle-l_{\mu}l_{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}^{\rho}l_{\sigma}\widebar{\nabla}^{\sigma}l_{\rho}+l_{\mu}l_{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}^{\sigma}l^{\rho}\widebar{\nabla}_{\sigma}l_{\rho}-l_{\mu}l_{\nu}\dot{l}_{\rho}\dot{l}^{\rho}\Big\}

(20)

Assuming

R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu\nu}=0

(21)

and contracting with
l^{\mu}l^{\nu}
yields

l^{\mu}l^{\lambda}l^{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}l_{\mu}=l^{\lambda}l^{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\left(l^{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}l_{\mu}\right)-l^{\lambda}l^{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l^{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}l_{\mu}=-\dot{l}_{\mu}\dot{l}^{\mu}=0

(22)

then, because
\dot{l}^{\mu}l_{\mu}=0
there must exist an scalar field
\phi
such that

\dot{l}^{\alpha}\equiv l^{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l^{\alpha}=\phi\,l^{\alpha}

(23)

As has already been pointed out, the vector field is geodesic whenever
\phi=0
.

Contracting
R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu\nu}=0
with
l^{\mu}
and using this, we get

\displaystyle l^{\mu}l_{\nu}\bar{\Box}l_{\mu}-l^{\mu}l_{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}l^{\lambda}-l^{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l^{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}l_{\nu}-l^{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}l^{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l_{\nu}-l^{\mu}l^{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}l_{\nu}-l^{\mu}l^{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}l_{\mu}=

\displaystyle=\left(\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l_{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}^{\lambda}l^{\mu}+\dot{\theta}+\dot{\phi}+\phi^{2}+\theta\phi\right)l_{\nu}=0

(24)

so that

\widebar{\nabla}_{\lambda}l_{\mu}\widebar{\nabla}^{\lambda}l^{\mu}=-(\dot{\theta}+\dot{\phi})-\phi(\phi+\theta)-\widebar{R}_{\alpha\beta}l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}

(25)

On the other hand computing
\dot{\theta}
leads to

\widebar{\nabla}_{\mu}l^{\nu}\widebar{\nabla}_{\nu}l^{\mu}=\dot{\phi}-\dot{\theta}+\phi\theta-\widebar{R}_{\alpha\beta}l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}

(26)

Collecting all the results

R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\mu\nu}=0

(27)

Then not only is the expansion of the Ricci tensor finite, but also the vanishing of the linear term implies the vanishing of the whole expansion. This is the more outstanding given that
R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}
is linear in the deformation
h_{\mu\nu}\equiv l_{\mu}l_{\nu}
, whereas
R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\alpha\beta}
is quadratic and cubic in the deformation. This important theorem (
15
) is in fact a simple consequence of the previous equations (
23
)(
25
)(
26
).

We find it noteworthy that in this formalism solutions of the Fierz-Pauli equation in a Ricci flat background automatically yield solutions of the vacuum Einstein’s equations, that is new Ricci flat spacetimes.

3
Sources

It is also interesting to test this approach when there is a source in Einstein’s equations. We are only able to do this in some simple cases. Consider for example a constant curvature spacetime, like de Sitter or anti de Sitter (the sign of the cosmological constant is not material in the sequel)

\text{d}s^{2}_{\text{\tiny{(CCS)}}}=\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}=\text{d}t^{2}-e^{2Ht}\delta_{ij}\text{d}x^{i}\text{d}y^{j}

(45)

where
H
is a constant. The Ricci tensor reads

\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}=-3H^{2}\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}

(46)

and obeys Einstein’s equations sourced by an energy-momentum tensor

\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}=\kappa^{2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}T\right)

(47)

with

T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{3H^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}

(48)

corresponding to a vacuum energy density

\rho=-p=\frac{3H^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}

(49)

Consider a deformation of the same constant curvature de Sitter background with a null vector field which is isotropic in the spatial directions

l_{\mu}=\left(1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}e^{Ht},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}e^{Ht},\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}e^{Ht}\right)

(50)

The resulting spacetime metric looks quite involved

\displaystyle ds^{2}=2\,dt^{2}-{2\over 3}e^{2Ht}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right)+{2\over\sqrt{3}}e^{Ht}\left(dtdx+dtdy+dtdz\right)+

\displaystyle+{2\over 3}e^{2Ht}\left(dxdy+dxdz+dydz\right)

(51)

In spite of the appearances this cosmological metric shares rotational invariances with the seed spacetime.

The perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
now induces a nonvanishing

tensor, namely

R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu\nu}=H^{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&-\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&-\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}\\
-\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&-4e^{2Ht}&-e^{2Ht}&-e^{2Ht}\\
-\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&-e^{2Ht}&-4e^{2Ht}&-e^{2Ht}\\
-\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&-e^{2Ht}&-e^{2Ht}&-4e^{2Ht}\end{pmatrix}

and

R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\mu\nu}=H^{2}\begin{pmatrix}3&\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}&\sqrt{3}e^{Ht}\\

4
Is there superposition?

It would seem that we have somewhat magically, traded a nonlinear equation (Ricci flatness) by a linear one (Fierz-Pauli) in which case it seems that there must be superposition of solutions. Things are more complicated though.

First of all, in order to find the first new Ricci flat metric, the null vector has to be a solution of the FP equation

R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0

(59)

if now we want to consider the defomed spacetime

g_{\mu\nu}\equiv\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}+l_{\mu}l_{\nu}

(60)

as a new seed, we need to solve

R_{\mu\nu}=\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}

(61)

which is different equation altogether. There is no question of superposition.

But there is more.

Even if we consider the same FP equation, given two different nilpotent solutions of the equation
R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0
, say

h_{\mu\nu}\equiv l^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu}l^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\nu}

(62)

and

h_{\mu\nu}\equiv l^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}_{\mu}l^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}_{\nu}

(63)

their sum

h_{\mu\nu}\equiv l^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu}l^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\nu}+l^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}_{\mu}l^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}_{\nu}

(64)

is of course again a solution of the Fierz-Pauli equations
R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0
,
but not necessarily nilpotent
. In order to fulfill this condition, it is necessary that there exists a third null vector,
l^{\text{\tiny{(3)}}}_{\mu}
such that

h_{\mu\nu}=l^{\text{\tiny{(3)}}}_{\mu}l^{\text{\tiny{(3)}}}_{\nu}

(65)

This happens only when

l_{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}^{\lambda}l^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}_{\lambda}=0

(66)

which in turn enforces

l_{\lambda}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}=l_{\lambda}^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}=l_{\lambda}^{\text{\tiny{(3)}}}

(67)

Consider for example two black hole located at different points where

h_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}{1\over r}+{1\over|\vec{x}-\vec{a}|}&-{x^{j}\over r^{2}}-{x^{j}-a^{j}\over|\vec{x}-\vec{a}|^{2}}\\
-{x^{j}\over r^{2}}-{x^{j}-a^{j}\over|\vec{x}-\vec{a}|^{2}}&{x^{i}x^{j}\over r^{3}}+{(x^{i}-a^{i})(x^{j}-a^{j})\over|\vec{x}-\vec{a}|^{3}}\end{pmatrix}

(68)

it is clear

\det\,h_{\mu\nu}\neq 0

(69)

and the deformed spacetime is not Ricci flat.

This means that in spite of the fact that
R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0
is a linear equation (the crucial point is that it depends on the background), superposition in the present context is still an exceptional property of Ricci flat spacetimes. It basically reduces to scaling
[
2
]
.

To be specific, consider a spherical black hole (BH) in Eddington coordinates

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}_{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}-{r_{s}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}\over r}(\text{d}t+\text{d}r)^{2}

(70)

this metric supports a light vector

l_{\mu}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{r_{s}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}}{r}}\left(1,-\frac{x^{j}}{r}\right)

(71)

it can be easily verified that this metric is Ricci flat and unimodular.

Let us now consider a superposition of two such metrics

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}_{\text{\tiny{(1+2)}}}

\displaystyle=\eta_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}-{r_{s}^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}+r_{s}^{\text{\tiny{(2)}}}\over\sqrt{2}r}(\text{d}t+\text{d}r)^{2}

(72)

it is plain that this superposition is Ricci flat as well.

The Schwarzschild radius of the superposition is

r_{s}^{(12)}={r_{s}^{(1)}+r_{s}^{(2)}\over\sqrt{2}}

(73)

This of course, the same relationship of the mass of the combined spacetime containing two black holes on top of each other with the parent masses. It is to be remarked that this mass is
smaller
than the sum of the two initial masses.
The entropy, in turn, is

S_{(12)}=\pi\left(r_{s}^{(12)}\right)^{2}=\pi{\left(r_{s}^{(1)}+r_{s}^{(2)}\right)^{2}\over 2}=S_{(1)}+S_{(2)}-{\pi\over 2}\left(r_{s}^{(1)}-r_{s}^{(2)}\right)^{2}

(74)

which again,
smaller
than the sum of the two parent entropies. We do not know any physical interpretation of this fact. Were we to add
n
copies of the BH, a similar rescaling leas to

r_{s}^{(n)}={1\over\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{s}^{(i)}

(75)

again,
smaller
than the sum of the two initial masses.
The entropy reads

S_{(n)}=\pi\left(r_{s}^{(n)}\right)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{(i)}-\pi{n-1\over n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r_{s}^{(i)})^{2}+{\pi\over n}\prod_{i\neq j}r_{s}^{(i)}r_{s}^{(j)}

(76)

To summarize, in this section we have showed that in spite of the central rôle that the linear Fierz-Pauli equation plays in our approach, there is no superposition of solutions in general. Only a given spacetime can be put on top of itself, as has been explicitly done with black holes; this is essentially scaling.

5
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied nilpotent deformations of an arbitrary background (or seed),
id est
,

g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\widebar{g}_{\mu\nu}(x)+l_{\mu}l_{\nu}

(77)

with
l^{2}=0
. Those are an extension of the original Kerr-Schild ansatz, which obviously covers the full space of metrics, just by starting with an appropiate seed metric.

A general formula for the ensuing Ricci tensor has been obtained, namely.

R_{\mu\nu}=\widebar{R}_{\mu\nu}+R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu\nu}+R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\mu\nu}

(78)

where
R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\mu\nu}
is a linear operator to wit, the Fierz-Pauli one and
R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\mu\nu}
is a non-linear operator. They have the remarkable property that

\{R^{\text{\tiny{(1)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0\}\Longrightarrow\{R^{\text{\tiny{(2+3)}}}_{\alpha\beta}=0\}

(79)

When the seed is taken as flat space, we recover the Kerr-Schild family of Ricci flat solutions.

We have also considered nontrivial Ricci flat spaces as seed, such as Kasner spacetime or plane waves, and also Ricci non flat seeds, concretely de Sitter space, which solves Einstein’s equations with a source. It is plain that we have only begun to scratch the surface of the possibilities of our theorem to find new vacuum solutions of Einstein’s equations.

A grand view of the situation is as follows.

The space of all Ricci flat metrics in a given spacetime is divided into
orbits
, in such a way that it is not possible to travel from one orbit to a different one through the deformations used in this paper.

For example, the orbit generated by flat space comprises all Kerr-Schild spacetimes, but it is known that constant curvature spaces do not belong to this orbit. This shows that there are at least two different orbits.

We have finally pointed out that in spite of the fact that in some sense, for this particular family of spacetimes, the Ricci flat condition has beed traded by the Fierz-Pauli equation, which is linear, there is no superposition of spacetimes in general. Only scaling survives
[
2
]
in which some spacetime is put on top of itself.

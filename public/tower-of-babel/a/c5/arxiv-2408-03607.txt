TANGENT SPACE OF THE STABLE AND UNSTABLE MANIFOLD OF ANOSOV DIFFEOMORPHISM ON 2-TORUS
Federico Bonneto, Jack Wang, Vishal Kumar

Abstract
In this paper we describe the tangent vectors of the stable and unstable manifold of a class of Anosov diffeomorphisms on the torus 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} using the method of formal series and derivative trees. We start with linear automorphism that is hyperbolic and whose eigenvectors are orthogonal. Then we study the perturbation of such maps by trigonometric polynomial. It is known [ 1 ] that there exist a (continuous) map H H which acts as a change of coordinate between the perturbed and unperturbed system, but such a map is in general, not differentiable. By “re-scaling” the parametrization H H , we will be able to obtain the explicit formula for the tangent vectors of these maps.

Our goal is to find the tangent vector of the sets

7 The Three Dimensional Case

We conclude our discussion with some remarks about the three-dimensional case. Our method of formal series and product trees gives a good method for finding the tangent vector field of the stable and unstable manifold of the analytic perturbation of a special class of linear automorphisms on

\mathbb{T}^{3}

Definition 9
.

A diffeomorphism

that is invariant under

(i)

(ii) There are continuous functions

||Df(v^{s})||<\sigma(p)<||Df(v^{c})||<\mu(p)<||Df(v^{u})||
.

As an example
[

]
, consider the automorphism given by the following matrix with determinant 1:

A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&0\\
1&2&1\\
0&1&1\\
\end{pmatrix}

whose eigenvalues are approximately 0.20, 1.55, and 3.25.

could be viewed as a partially hyperbolic system with expanding center distribution. In fact,

The same method described in this paper shows that one could

With the same notation as in the previous lemma, the explicit formula for

Distributing the term
\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}b_{n-d}
, we get:

\displaystyle q_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}-\left(\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}b_{n-d}a_{d}+\left(\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}b_{n-d}a_{k}\left(\sum_{w\in P(d-k)}\alpha w\right)\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=a_{n}-\left(\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}b_{n-d}a_{d}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{d=k+1}^{n-1}a_{k}b_{n-d}\left(\sum_{w\in P(d-k)}\alpha w\right)\right)\right)\right)

In the term
(\sum_{d=k}^{n-1}b_{n-d}(\sum_{w\in P(d-k)}\alpha w))
, we are multiplying each
w
by a
b_{n-d}
. This turns the sum into the following:

\sum_{d=k+1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{w\in(P(d-k)+(n-d))}\alpha w\right)=\sum_{w\in P(n-k)}\alpha w-b_{n-k},

Since we have incremented the number of
b_{i}
in
w
by one,
\alpha=1
when the number of
b_{i}
is odd, and
-1
otherwise. Since our index does not start at
d=k
, and thus we do not get the set
P(0)+(n-k)=\{b_{n-k}\}
.Plugging it back into our equation, we get:

\displaystyle q_{n}

\displaystyle=a_{n}-\left(\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}b_{n-d}a_{d}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}\left(\sum_{w\in P(n-k)}\alpha w-b_{n-k}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=a_{n}-\left(\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}b_{n-d}a_{d}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}b_{n-k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}\left(\sum_{w\in P(n-k)}\alpha w\right)\right)

\displaystyle=a_{n}-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}\left(\sum_{w\in P(n-k)}\alpha w\right)\right)

We multiply the negative sign in so that, again,
\alpha=1
when the number of
b_{i}
in
w
are even, and
-1
otherwise. Re-expanding, we finally get:

q_{n}=a_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{n-k}(-1)^{s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+...+m_{s}=n-k\\
m_{1},...,m_{s}\geq 1\end{subarray}}(b_{m_{1}}b_{m_{2}}...b_{m_{s}})\right)

∎

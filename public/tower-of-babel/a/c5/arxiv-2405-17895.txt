Enhanced dissipation and temporal decay in the Euler-Poisson-Navier-Stokes equations
Young-Pil Choi, Houzhi Tang, Weiyuan Zou

Abstract
This paper investigates the global well-posedness and large-time behavior of solutions for a coupled fluid model in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} consisting of the isothermal compressible Euler-Poisson system and incompressible Navier-Stokes equations coupled through the drag force. Notably, we exploit the dissipation effects inherent in the Poisson equation to achieve a faster decay of fluid density compared to velocities. This strategic utilization of dissipation, together with the influence of the electric field and the damping structure induced by the drag force, leads to a remarkable decay behavior: the fluid density converges to equilibrium at a rate of ( 1 + t ) − 11 / 4 (1+t)^{-11/4} , significantly faster than the decay rates of velocity differences ( 1 + t ) − 7 / 4 (1+t)^{-7/4} and velocities themselves ( 1 + t ) − 3 / 4 (1+t)^{-3/4} in the L 2 L^{2} norm. Furthermore, under the condition of vanishing coupled incompressible flow, we demonstrate an exponential decay to a constant state for the solution of the corresponding system, the damped Euler-Poisson system.



Key words.

Euler-Poisson-Navier-Stokes system, global well-posedness, spectral analysis, optimal temporal decay rates.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the dynamics of a coupled fluid model, the so-called Euler-Poisson-Navier-Stokes (EP-NS) system, consisting of the isothermal compressible Euler-Poisson system and incompressible Navier-Stokes equations coupled through the drag force:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\textrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}\big(\rho u\otimes u\big)+\nabla\rho=-\rho(u-v)-\rho\nabla U,\\
\displaystyle-\Delta U=\rho-1,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\Delta v+\rho(u-v),\\
\displaystyle\text{div}v=0.\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
denote the density and velocity for the compressible Euler fluid flow, respectively,
U=U(x,t)
is the electric potential,
v=v(x,t)
and
P=P(x,t)
represent the velocity and pressure for the incompressible Navier-Stokes fluid flow, respectively.

We supply the EP-NS system (
1.1
) with the initial data and far-field states:

(\rho,u,v)(x,0)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},v_{0})(x),\quad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}(\rho,u,v)(x,t)=(1,0,0).

respectively. Moreover, we assume the neutrality and non-vacuum conditions for the initial density:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho_{0}-1)\,dx=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho_{0}(x)>0\mbox{ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}.

(1.2)

In recent years, the interactions between particles and fluids have gained significant attention due to their widespread applications across various fields, including biotechnology, medicine, and the study of complex fluid phenomena. These interactions play a crucial role in phenomena such as sedimentation, the compressibility of droplets in sprays, the formation of cooling tower plumes, and the combustion processes in diesel engines
[

,

,

,

]
. For a more comprehensive understanding of these phenomena, readers are directed to
[

,
20
]
for additional insights into the physical background of multiphase flow models. Beyond their practical implications, the mathematical analysis of models capturing these interactions holds significant importance. By rigorously examining the underlying mathematical structures and dynamics, a deeper understanding of the behavior of real-world systems can be attained. Furthermore, the development of mathematical models facilitates the prediction and optimization of processes in diverse applications.

Related Literature and Objective

In the current work, we investigate the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of classical solutions to the EP-NS system (
1.1
) and its large-time behaviors. In the last decade, there have been significant developments in the mathematical analysis of the kinetic-fluid or coupled fluid models. To put our study in the proper perspective, we shall recall a few of the references on the Cauchy problem for coupled fluid models. Choi and Jung
[
7
]
studied the hydrodynamic limit from the kinetic-fluid model, the nonlinear Vlasov-Poisson-Fokker-Planck equation coupled with the incompressible Navier-Stokes system, towards the EP-NS system (
1.1
) in the periodic spatial domain employing the relative entropy argument, see
[
3
]
for the case without Poisson interactions. Specifically, the global-in-time existences of solutions to both equations, the kinetic-fluid and the EP-NS equations, are established, and thus the hydrodynamic limit holds for all times. The large-time behavior of solutions to (
1.1
) in the periodic domain showing the exponential decay rate of convergences towards the equilibrium is obtained in
[
8
]
. In that paper, the Poincaré inequality is crucially used, and thus it is not clear to extend that result to the study of large-time behavior of solutions in the whole space. On the other hand, in the absence of the Poisson interaction, (
1.1
) reduces to the Euler-Navier-Stokes system, and Choi et al
[
9
]
and Huang et al
[
15
]
established the global well-posedness and optimal decay estimates in the whole space. In the case where the coupled flow exhibits compressibility, Choi
[
6
]
investigated the global existence and exponential decay rates of classical solutions in the periodic domain. Later, Wu et al
[
24
]
extended the analysis of large-time behavior of solutions to the whole space case, obtaining optimal algebraic decay rates. Meanwhile, Wu and Zou
[
25
]
focused on space-time estimates for this model. Notably, the coupling of the two-phase flow through the drag force term
\rho(u-v)
enables the smooth effect of the Navier-Stokes equations to propagate to the Euler system, ensuring global well-posedness. Remarkably, observations on the large-time behavior in three-dimensional space indicate that the decay properties of the Euler flow remain consistent with those of incompressible or compressible Navier-Stokes flow through the drag force.

The primary focus of this paper lies in addressing the Cauchy problem of system (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We are motivated by two key objectives:

(i)

Preventing Singularities:
The Euler-Poisson equations are known to develop singularities in finite time, irrespective of the smoothness of the initial data
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
12
,
19
]
. An intriguing question arises: can we utilize the smooth effect arising from coupled Navier-Stokes flow to establish the existence of solutions for the system (
1.1
)?

(ii)

Understanding Electric Field Influence:
Building upon previous works on the compressible Navier-Stokes-Poisson system
[
17
,
22
]
and Euler-Poisson system
[
13
,
14
,
16
,
18
]
, we aim to elucidate the influence of the electric field on coupled fluid models.

To address these questions, we employ a combination of methodologies, including the Hodge decomposition and spectral analysis for the linearized system around the equilibrium state, energy methods, and low-high frequency decomposition for nonlinear equations. Our results reveal that, owing to the smooth effect of incompressible flow, the formation of singularities in the Euler-Poisson system can be prevented through the drag force. Furthermore, influenced by the electric field, the density of particles exhibits an algebraic decay rate of
(1+t)^{-11/4}
in
L^{2}
-norm, contrasting with the velocity decay rate of
(1+t)^{-3/4}
, which aligns with the order of the nonlinear term
\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}
. In order to clearly distinguish these differences in convergence rates, under additional assumption on the initial velocities, we provide a lower bound decay rate for the velocity, indicating the decay rate
(1+t)^{-3/4}
is optimal. We also investigate the decay rates for higher-order spatial derivatives of solutions, demonstrating enhancements in decay rates. Interestingly, for the corresponding linear system, the density decays to a constant state exponentially, while velocities tend to zero algebraically. This disparity diverges from previous works
[
17
,
22
]
, where the density decay was dominated by the linear part and occurred at the same rate as
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}
, namely
(1+t)^{-5/4}
. Consequently, while it may not be feasible to establish a lower-bound decay rate for the density of the EP-NS system, such a bound still holds for velocities. We also would like to point out that the linearized Euler-Poisson system for irrotational flows has the Klein-Gordon structure
[
13
]
. Thus, the density cannot have the exponential decay rate of convergence, nor can it have a faster decay rate than the velocity. Moreover, the derivative of solutions does not enhance the convergence rate. However, in our coupled model, the two fluids are coupled through the drag force, which imparts a linear damping effect on velocity, resulting in a completely different behavior of fluid density, as stated above. Furthermore, in scenarios where the coupled incompressible flow vanishes, we observe exponential decay rates for solutions of the Euler-Poisson system with damping, further highlighting the influence of coupled flow on the Euler-Poisson system.

Idea of Proof

Now we outline the key ideas employed in the proof of the main theorems. Assuming the neutrality condition (
2.3
) holds, we first establish global well-posedness using an energy method combined with
hypocoercivity-type
estimates. Note that the hypocoercivity-type estimates give the dissipation rate for the density. To investigate the large-time behavior of solutions, as stated in Introduction, we linearize the system (
2.1
) and estimate the Green function of that using Hodge decomposition and spectral analysis. This linear analysis reveals that the density decays to a constant state exponentially, while the velocities tend to zero at an algebraic rate. For the nonlinear system, we express the density
n(x,t)
using Duhamel’s principle as:

\displaystyle n(t)=S_{11}*n_{0}+S_{12}*u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{11}(t-s)*f_{1}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S_{12}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\,ds,

(2.11)

where
f_{1}=-u\cdot\nabla n
,
f_{2}=-u\cdot\nabla u-\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)
and
(S_{ij})
are from the Green function for the linearized system, see Section
4.1.1
for details. Notably, the linear part of
n(t)
decays faster than the nonlinear part, indicating that the decay rate of density is dominated by that of nonlinear term
u\cdot\nabla u
. Since the decay rate of the incompressible flow is the same as that of the heat equation, we infer that
\|u\|_{L^{2}}
behaves similarly to the heat equation influenced by the velocity term
v
in the momentum equations for
u
in (
2.1
). Building upon this observation, we define a suitable function
M(t)
given by

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{2}(1+s)^{\frac{11}{4}+\frac{k}{2}}\left\|\nabla^{k}n(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}+(1+s)^{\frac{15}{4}}\|\nabla^{3}n(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{3}(1+s)^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{k}{2}}\left\|\nabla^{k}(u,v)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}.

Our subsequent task is to prove that
M(t)
is uniformly bounded in time, thereby establishing the desired decay estimates of solutions.

By estimating the Green function and by the definition of
M(t)
, we initially obtain:

\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\delta_{0}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}M(t)^{2},\quad k=0,1,2,

where
\delta_{0}
is appeared in Theorem
2.1
and
R>0
is independent of
t
.
However, it is evident from (
2.11
) that it cannot sustain third derivatives, exceeding the setting in the definition of
M(t)
. To address this issue, we introduce a low-high frequency decomposition:

n=n^{\ell}+n^{h},

where
n^{\ell}
denotes the low-frequency part of solutions and
n^{h}
represents the high-frequency part. Due to the smoothing effect of low frequencies, we have:

\left\|\nabla^{3}n^{\ell}(t)\right\|_{2}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\delta_{0}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}M(t)^{2}.

For the high-frequency part, we apply energy methods to the high-frequency part of the nonlinear system, yielding:

\|\nabla^{3}(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}\big(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{\frac{3}{2}}+M(t)^{2}\big).

Combining these results, we obtain:

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{2}),\quad k=0,1,2,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{3}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{\frac{3}{2}}+M(t)^{2}).

The velocities
u,v
are addressed similarly. Combining these estimates with the smallness of
\delta_{0}
, we establish the uniform bound of
M(t)
, which yields the upper bound decay rates of solutions. Moreover, by analyzing the equations for
u-v
, we obtain the upper bound decay rate of
\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, faster than the decay rates of velocities themselves.

In order to show the optimality of the decay rate of solutions, by selecting specific initial data (non-empty), we also obtain the estimates of lower bounds for
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
and upper bounds for
\|\Lambda^{-1}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. Then, by Sobolev interpolation inequalities, we establish the optimal decay rates of
u(t)
for any order derivatives, similar to
\|\nabla^{k}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. Thanks to the key observation that the Green function can borrow a derivative due to the opposite signs of the drag force, we overcome the difficulty arising from the non-conservative structure of (
1.1
) to obtain the lower bound rate of
\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. To elucidate the effect of coupled flow on the Euler-Poisson system, we consider the case where the coupled flow vanishes. We provide that the solution decays to a constant state at an exponential rate.

\displaystyle n\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle u\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\nabla u\in L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle v\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C^{1}([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\nabla v\in L^{2}([0,T_{0}];H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

3.2
A priori estimates

To extend the local solution to a global one, the crucial step is to obtain uniform estimates. For later use, we introduce the following communicator estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

(
[
22
, Lemma A.3]
)

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer and define the communicator

[\nabla^{m},f]g=\nabla^{m}(fg)-f\nabla^{m}g.

Then we have

\big\|[\nabla^{m},f]g\big\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\nabla^{m-1}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}+\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{L^{p_{3}}}\|g\|_{L^{p_{4}}},

where
p,p_{2},p_{3}\in(1,+\infty)
and

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}.

We then define energy
\mathcal{E}
and corresponding dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
as

\mathcal{E}(t)=\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}+\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{D}(t)=\|n(t)\|_{H^{3}}+\|\nabla v(t)\|_{H^{3}}+\|(u-v)(t)\|_{H^{3}},

(3.1)

respectively. To close the nonlinear estimates, we provide the
a priori
assumption that

\mathcal{E}(t)=\|(n,v,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}+\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\leq\delta,

(3.2)

with
\delta
a sufficiently small positive constant.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
T>0
and
(n,u,v)
be the classical solution of system (
2.1
) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.2
). Then, for
0<t\leq T
, it holds that

\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\|(n,u,v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla U\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\}+C_{0}\left(\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq 0,

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant independent of time.

Proof
.
Taking basic
L^{2}
energy estimates on the system (
1.1
) gives

\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\rho\log\rho-\rho+1+\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla_{x}U|^{2}\Big)\,dx\right\}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho|u-v|^{2}\,dx+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=0.

Using (
3.2
) and
n=\log\rho
, we obtain that for
\delta>0
small enough

\displaystyle\rho\leq e^{\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}}\leq e^{C\|\nabla n\|_{H^{1}}}\leq e^{C\delta}\leq\frac{5}{4},\quad\rho\geq e^{-\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}}\geq e^{-C\|\nabla n\|_{H^{1}}}\geq e^{-C\delta}\geq\frac{4}{5},

and

\rho\log\rho-\rho+1\simeq\frac{1}{2}(\rho-1)^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{2}n^{2}.

There exists a positive constant
C_{0}
such that

\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\|(n,u,v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla U\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\}+C_{0}\left(\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq 0.

This completes the proof.

For the estimates of high-order derivatives, we rewrite the system (
2.1
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+{\rm{div}}\,u=f_{1},\\
&\partial_{t}u+\nabla n+u-v+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n=f_{2},\\
&\partial_{t}v+v-\mathbb{P}u-\Delta v=f_{3},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leary operator
\mathbb{P}=I+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\text{div}
, and the nonlinear terms
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
, and
f_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle f_{1}=-u\cdot\nabla n,

(3.4)

\displaystyle f_{2}=-u\cdot\nabla u-\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right),\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle f_{3}=-\mathbb{P}(v\cdot\nabla v)+\mathbb{P}((e^{n}-1)(u-v)),

respectively.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T>0
and
(n,u,v)
be the classical solution of system (
2.1
) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.2
). Then, for
0<t\leq T
and
k=0,1,2
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\nabla^{k}n\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{k+1}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{k+1}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\left\|\nabla^{k+2}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{k+1}(u-v)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t),

where the positive constant
C
is independent of time.

Proof
.
Rewrite the equation
\eqref{Main2}_{4}
as

\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P-u+v=\Delta v+(\rho-1)(u-v).

This together with (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\left\|\nabla^{2}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.5)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\nabla u\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla f_{1}\cdot\nabla n\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla f_{2}\cdot\nabla u\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(v\cdot\nabla v)\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla((\rho-1)(u-v))\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

In terms of integration by parts and the equation
\eqref{main4}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\nabla u\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta\text{div}u\,dx

(3.6)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta\left(-\partial_{t}n+f_{1}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta\partial_{t}n\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta f_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n\cdot\partial_{t}n\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta f_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta f_{1}\,dx.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
3.6
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\Delta f_{1}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\nabla f_{1}\,dx\right|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\nabla(u\cdot\nabla n)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}nu\cdot\nabla n\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

Combining (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) together gives

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\nabla u\,dx\leq-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.5
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla f_{1}\cdot\nabla n\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(u\cdot\nabla n)\cdot\nabla n\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla(u\cdot\nabla n)-u\cdot\nabla\nabla n)\cdot\nabla n\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla\nabla n\cdot\nabla n\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

The third term on the right-hand side of (
3.5
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla f_{2}\cdot\nabla u\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\left(\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right)\cdot\nabla u\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla(u\cdot\nabla u)-u\cdot\nabla\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\,dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,dx\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\cdot\Delta u\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|e^{n}-1-n\|_{L^{2}}+\|e^{n}-1-n\|_{L^{1}})\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t)+C\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}+\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

Here we used the fact that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\nabla K*\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|e^{n}-1-n\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|e^{n}-1-n\right\|_{L^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
K
is the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation.

The remaining terms in (
3.5
) can also be treated similarly

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(v\cdot\nabla v)\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla((\rho-1)(u-v))\cdot\nabla v\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla(v\cdot\nabla v)-v\cdot\nabla\nabla v)\cdot\nabla v\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\cdot\nabla\nabla v\cdot\nabla v\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\rho-1)(u-v)\cdot\Delta v\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho-1\|_{L^{3}}\|u-v\|_{L^{6}}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|n\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

Thus, we conclude from the above fact that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|n\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\left\|\nabla^{2}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

(3.8)

Similarly, we also prove for
k=1,2
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\nabla^{k}n\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{k+1}u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{k+1}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\left\|\nabla^{k+2}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{k+1}(u-v)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t).

Combining (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) concludes the desired result.

Note that the dissipation rate
\mathcal{D}
includes the density term,
\|n\|_{H^{3}}
. Thus, in order to close the a priori estimates and provide the uniform-in-time bound for

, we need to have the dissipation rate for the density. To this end, we provide a hypocoercivity-type estimate.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
T>0
and
(n,u,v)
be the classical solution of system (

) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.2

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{k}u\cdot\nabla^{k+1}n\,dx\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\|n\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\leq C_{4}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|u-v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)+C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t),

Proof
. Multiplying
\eqref{main4}_{2}
by

, then integrating the resulting equation over

yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla n\,dx+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\cdot\nabla n\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla\partial_{t}n\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-v)\cdot\nabla n\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(-u\cdot\nabla u-\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right)\cdot\nabla n\,dx

Note that

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(u-v)\cdot\nabla n\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.11)

for some

. Similarly, there exists a positive constant

with

Proof of Theorem

We now close the energy estimates which provide the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of classical solutions for the system (

It follows from Section

\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}+C_{5}\left(\|\nabla v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|u-v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right)\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t),

for some

Taking

)
+
\eqref{030812}\times\beta_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{k}u\cdot\nabla^{k+1}n\,dx\right\}

(3.14)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{1}\|n\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+C_{5}\left(\|\nabla v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|u-v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right)

Due to the smallness of

, we find that

\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{k}u\cdot\nabla^{k+1}n\,dx\simeq\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}+C_{6}\left(\|n\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|u-v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right)\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)\mathcal{D}^{2}(t),

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time over

and using the a priori assumption (

\displaystyle\|(n,u,v)(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla U(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{6}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|n(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla v(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|(u-v)(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right)d\tau

4
Large-time behavior for the EP-NS system

In this section, we investigate the large-time behavior of the EP-NS system demonstrating the decay estimates of solutions as stated in Theorem
2.1
. Specifically, we establish time-decay estimates employing spectral analysis via the Hodge decomposition.

We define operators
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
on
L^{2}
as

\mathcal{K}_{1}f=f^{\ell}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\big(\hat{\chi}_{1}(\xi)\mathscr{F}[f](\xi)\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{K}_{\infty}f=f^{h}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\big(\hat{\chi}_{\infty}(\xi)\mathscr{F}[f](\xi)\big),

respectively, where
\hat{\chi}_{j}(\xi)(j=1,\infty)\in C^{\infty}(\mathds{R}^{3})
,
0\leq\hat{\chi}_{j}\leq 1
are smooth cut-off functions defined by

\hat{\chi}_{1}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1\quad(|\xi|\leq r_{0}),\\
0\quad(|\xi|\geq R_{0})\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hat{\chi}_{\infty}(\xi)=1-\hat{\chi}_{1}(\xi).

Here
r_{0}>0
is sufficiently small and
R_{0}>0
is sufficiently large.

To analyze the large-time behavior of the solutions
\eta=(n,u,v)^{t}
in frequency space, we adopt the low-high frequency decomposition for the solution:

\eta(t,x)=\eta^{\ell}(t,x)+\eta^{h}(t,x)\triangleq(n^{\ell},u^{\ell},v^{\ell})+(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h}),

where
\eta^{\ell}(t,x)\triangleq\mathcal{K}_{1}\eta(t,x)
denotes the low-frequency part and
\eta^{h}(t,x)\triangleq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}\eta(t,x)
represents the high-frequency part.

Before proceeding further, we present an auxiliary lemma providing the Sobolev embedding for low- and high-frequency parts.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
15
, Lemma 2.4]
)

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For
0\leq n<m
, there exist positive a constant
C
such that for
f\in H^{m}
,

\|\nabla^{m}f^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{n}f^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{n}f^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{m}f^{h}\|_{L^{2}},

and

\|\nabla^{n}f^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{n}f\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{n}f^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{n}f\|_{L^{2}}.

4.1
Spectral analysis for the linearized EP-NS system

Letting
f_{i}\equiv 0
,
i=1,2,3
in (
3.3
), we have the following linearized system:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+\operatorname{div}u=0,\\
&\partial_{t}u+\nabla n+u-v+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n=0,\\
&\partial_{t}v+v-\mathbb{P}u-\Delta v=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

To estimate the optimal decay rate of convergences of solutions to the above system, we make use of spectral analysis. To this end, we first decompose the velocities
u
and
v
using the Hodge decomposition, separating them into their divergence and curl components. Specifically, let us define

q=\Lambda^{-1}\text{div}u,\quad w=\Lambda^{-1}\text{curl}u,\quad\mbox{and}\quad b=\Lambda^{-1}\text{curl}v,

where the operator
\Lambda^{-1}
is the pseudodifferential operator defined as (
1.3
).

We then derive

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+\Lambda q=0,\\
&\partial_{t}q+q-\Lambda n-\Lambda^{-1}n=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w+w-b=0,\\
&\partial_{t}b+b-w+\Lambda^{2}b=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.3)

Taking Fourier transform of (
4.2
) with respect to
x
yields

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{n}+|\xi|\hat{q}=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\hat{q}+\hat{q}-|\xi|\hat{n}-|\xi|^{-1}\hat{n}=0,\\
&(\hat{n},\hat{q})|_{t=0}=(\hat{n}_{0},\hat{q}_{0}).\end{aligned}\right.

Letting
\hat{\Phi}=(\hat{n},\hat{q})^{t}
, we can rewrite the above system as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{\Phi}+\hat{S}_{1}\hat{\Phi}=0,\\
&\hat{\Phi}|_{t=0}=\hat{\Phi}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\mbox{with}\quad\hat{S}_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&|\xi|\\
-|\xi|-|\xi|^{-1}&1\end{array}\right).

The eigenvalues of
\hat{S}_{1}
are computed by solving

\displaystyle\text{det}(\lambda I+\hat{S}_{1})=\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&|\xi|\\
-|\xi|-|\xi|^{-1}&\lambda+1\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}+\lambda+|\xi|^{2}+1=0.

A simple computation gives

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{1-4(|\xi|^{2}+1)}}{2}=\frac{-1+\sqrt{3+4|\xi|^{2}}i}{2},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{-1-\sqrt{1-4(|\xi|^{2}+1)}}{2}=\frac{-1-\sqrt{3+4|\xi|^{2}}i}{2}.

Then the explicit formula of
\hat{\Phi}=(\hat{n},\hat{q})^{t}
can be expressed as

\hat{\Phi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{n}\\
\hat{q}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}&\frac{e^{\lambda_{2}t}-e^{\lambda_{1}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}|\xi|\\
\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|}&\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{n}_{0}\\
\hat{q}_{0}\end{array}\right).

(4.4)

Similarly as the above, letting
\hat{\Psi}=(\hat{w},\hat{b})^{t}
and
\hat{\Psi}_{0}=(\hat{w}_{0},\hat{b}_{0})^{t}
,
we obtain from (
4.3
) that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\hat{\Psi}+\hat{S_{2}}\hat{\Psi}=0,\\
\hat{\Psi}|_{t=0}=\hat{\Psi}_{0},\end{array}\right.\quad\mbox{with}\quad\hat{S_{2}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&-I\\
-I&(1+|\xi|^{2})I\end{array}\right)

and the eigenvalues of
\hat{S_{2}}
can be computed by solving

|\lambda I+\hat{S_{2}}|=\left(\lambda^{2}+(2+|\xi|^{2})\lambda+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{3}=0.

After directly calculation, we find

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=\frac{-\left(2+|\xi|^{2}\right)+\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{4}=\frac{-\left(2+|\xi|^{2}\right)-\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}}{2}

indicating that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
each have an algebraic multiplicity of
3
.
Then,
\hat{\Psi}
can be explicitly expressed as

\hat{\Psi}(t,\xi)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{w}\\
\hat{b}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{(\lambda_{3}+1)e^{\lambda_{4}t}-(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}I&\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}I\\
\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}I&\frac{(\lambda_{3}+1)e^{\lambda_{3}t}-(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}I\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{w}_{0}\\
\hat{b}_{0}\end{array}\right).

(4.5)

In the following lemma, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues by dividing the frequency into the low-frequency and high-frequency parts.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
r_{0}
is a sufficiently small positive constant. For the low frequency part
|\xi|<r_{0}
, the eigenvalues satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+O\left(|\xi|^{4}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{4}=-2-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+O\left(|\xi|^{4}\right).

For
|\xi|\geq r_{0}
, there exists a positive constant
R
such that

{\rm{Re}}\,(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4})\leq-R.

Proof
.
For
|\xi|<r_{0}\ll 1
, we find

\displaystyle\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}=2\sqrt{1+\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{4}}=2\left(1+\frac{1}{8}|\xi|^{4}+O(|\xi|^{8})\right),

and thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=\frac{-\left(2+|\xi|^{2}\right)+\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}}{2}=-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+O\left(|\xi|^{4}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}=\frac{-\left(2+|\xi|^{2}\right)-\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}}{2}=-2-\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}+O\left(|\xi|^{4}\right).

On the other hand, for
|\xi|>R_{0}\gg 1
, we get

\displaystyle\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}=|\xi|^{2}\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{|\xi|^{4}}}=|\xi|^{2}\left(1+\frac{2}{|\xi|^{4}}+O(|\xi|^{-8})\right),

and this implies

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}=\frac{-\left(2+|\xi|^{2}\right)+\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}}{2}=-1+O\left(|\xi|^{-2}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}=\frac{-\left(2+|\xi|^{2}\right)-\sqrt{4+|\xi|^{4}}}{2}=-1-|\xi|^{2}+O\left(|\xi|^{-2}\right).

For
r_{0}\leq|\xi|\leq R_{0}
, we easily obtain
\text{Re}\,(\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4})\leq-\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}+2}.

Combining
{\rm{Re}}~{\lambda_{1}}=\ {\rm{Re}}~{\lambda_{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}
and choosing
R=\min\left\{\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}+2},\frac{1}{2}\right\}
, we conclude for
|\xi|\geq r_{0}
that

{\rm{Re}}\,(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4})\leq-R.

This completes the proof.

4.1.1
Green function of the linearized system

To establish the time-decay estimates of the linear system, let
\eta=(n,u,v)^{t}
. Then, the system (
4.1
) can be expressed as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\eta(t)+\mathcal{L}\eta(t)=0,\\
\eta|_{t=0}=\eta_{0},\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\rm{div}&0\\
\nabla+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}&I&-I\\
0&-\mathbb{P}&I-\Delta\\
\end{array}\right).

(4.7)

Taking Fourier transformation of (
4.1
) yields

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\hat{\eta}(t)+\hat{\mathcal{L}}\hat{\eta}(t)=0,\\
\hat{\eta}|_{t=0}=\hat{\eta}_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Then, by solving the above equations, we have

\hat{\eta}(t,\xi)=e^{-t\hat{\mathcal{L}}}\hat{\eta}_{0}=\hat{S}(t,\xi)\hat{\eta}_{0},

where the Green function
S(t,x)=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
. We can directly obtain the time decay rates of the Green function
S(t,x)
as follows:

Proposition 4.3
.

For a given function
g(t,x)=(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})^{t}
, we have the following decay estimates of the Green function
S(t,x)=(S_{ik})_{3\times 3}
:

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{1i}*g_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\|\nabla^{k}g_{i}\|_{L^{2}},\quad i=1,2,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{21}*g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}(\|\nabla^{k}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{k}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}),\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{ij}*g_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{1}}+Ce^{-Rt}\|\nabla^{k}g_{j}\|_{L^{2}},\quad i,j=2,3.

In particular, for the low-frequency part
S^{\ell}(t,x)=\mathcal{K}_{1}S(t,x)=(S^{\ell}_{ik})_{3\times 3}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{1i}^{\ell}*g_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\|\nabla^{k}g_{i}\|_{L^{2}},\quad i=1,2,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{21}^{\ell}*g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\|\nabla^{k}(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla g_{1}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{ij}^{\ell}*g_{j}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{1}},\quad i,j=2,3.

Proof
.
Note that the function
u
can be decomposed into its divergence part and curl part as follows:

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=-\Lambda^{-1}\nabla\Lambda^{-1}\text{div}u+\Lambda^{-1}\text{curl}~(\Lambda^{-1}\text{curl}~u)=-\Lambda^{-1}\nabla q+\Lambda^{-1}\text{curl}~w.

Then by taking Fourier transform and utilizing the expression of
(\hat{q},\hat{w})
in (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we get

\displaystyle\hat{u}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|\xi|}i\xi\cdot\left(\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|}\hat{n}_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\hat{q}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{|\xi|}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&-i\xi_{3}&i\xi_{2}\\
i\xi_{3}&0&-i\xi_{1}\\
-i\xi_{2}&i\xi_{1}&0\end{array}\right)\left[\frac{\left(\lambda_{3}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\left(\lambda_{4}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{w}_{0}+\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{b}_{0}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}i\xi\hat{n}_{0}+\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{\left(\lambda_{3}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{4}t}-(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\left(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)+\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right]\hat{u}_{0}.

The Fourier transform of velocity
v
can be treated similarly, giving:

\hat{v}(t,\xi)=\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)+\frac{(\lambda_{3}+1)e^{\lambda_{3}t}-(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}(\xi).

In summary, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} \hat{n}(t,\xi)&=\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\hat{n}_{0}-\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}i\xi\cdot\hat{u}_{0},\\
\hat{u}(t,\xi)&=-\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}i\xi\hat{n}_{0}+\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}\\
&\quad+\left[\frac{\left(\lambda_{3}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{4}t}-(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\left(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)+\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right]\hat{u}_{0},\\

(4.10)

The Fourier transform of Green function

\displaystyle\hat{S}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{2}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{1}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}&-\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}i\xi^{t}&0\\

Therefore, applying Lemma
4.2

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}S_{11}*g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|(i\xi)^{k}\hat{S}_{11}\hat{g}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-Rt}\|\nabla^{k}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

4.1.2
Optimal temporal decay estimates for the linearized system

In this part, we present both lower and upper bound estimates of decay rates for the velocities. Proposition
4.3
indicates that achieving a lower bound on the decay rate of convergence for the density is challenging since it decays exponentially fast towards the equilibrium state.

More precisely, the main result of this part can be stated as follows:

Proposition 4.4
.

Assume that all the conditions in Theorem
2.2
hold. Let
(n,u,v)
be the global classical solution to the linear system (
4.6
). Then for sufficiently large
t\geq t_{0}
, we have

d_{*}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\|(u,v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Moreover, we obtain

\bar{d}_{*}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\leq\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}},

where
C
,
d_{*}
and
\bar{d}_{*}
are positive constants independent of time.

Proof
.
Following Proposition
4.3
, we establish the upper bound estimates for the velocities
(u,v)
as:

\|(u,v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}.

For the estimates of the lower bound of the time decay rates, by using the expression (
4.10
), we divide
\hat{u}
into two terms:

\displaystyle\hat{u}(t,\xi)=I_{1}+I_{2},

where

I_{1}\triangleq\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}-\frac{(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\big)\hat{u}_{0}

and

I_{2}\triangleq-\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}i\xi\hat{n}_{0}+\Big[\frac{(\lambda_{3}+1)e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\big)+\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big]\hat{u}_{0}-\frac{e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}.

We then use the assumption (
2.8
) to deduce

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big|^{2}\Big|\hat{v}_{0}-(\lambda_{4}+1)\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}\Big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}\Big|\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big|^{2}\Big|\hat{v}_{0}-(\lambda_{4}+1)\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}\Big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}t}\Big|\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}+\hat{v}_{0}\Big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\alpha_{0}^{2}\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}t}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C_{9}^{2}\alpha_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

where
C_{9}
is a small positive constant independent of time. As for
I_{2}
, it can be bounded by

\|I_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}.

Thus, for large
t
, we obtain

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\hat{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|I_{1}\|_{L^{2}}-\|I_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\geq C_{9}\alpha_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}-Ce^{-Ct}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{9}\alpha_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

(4.11)

Similarly, we decompose
\hat{v}(t,\xi)
as:

\displaystyle\hat{v}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)+\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}(\xi)\Big\}

\displaystyle+\Big\{-\frac{e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)+\big[\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{3}t}-(\lambda_{4}+1)e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\big]\hat{v}_{0}(\xi)\Big\}

\displaystyle\triangleq I_{3}+I_{4},

where

\displaystyle\|I_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big|^{2}\Big|\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}+\hat{v}_{0}\Big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}\Big|\frac{e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big|^{2}\Big|\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}+\hat{v}_{0}\Big|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\alpha_{0}^{2}\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}t}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C_{9}^{2}\alpha_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

due to (
2.8
). On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
4.2
and direct calculations that

\|I_{4}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}+Ce^{-Rt}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}.

Consequently, for large time

, we deduce

\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|I_{3}\|_{L^{2}}-\|I_{4}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{9}\alpha_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

(4.12)

Combining (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) yields

d_{*}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\|(u,v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},

where
C
and
d_{*}=\frac{1}{2}C_{9}\alpha_{0}
are positive constants independent of time.

For the decay estimate of the difference of velocities

, we consider

\hat{u}-\hat{v}=I_{5}+I_{6},

where

I_{5}\triangleq-\frac{1+|\xi|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}\frac{e^{\lambda_{1}t}-e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}i\xi\hat{n}_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}e^{\lambda_{1}t}-\lambda_{2}e^{\lambda_{2}t}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\hat{u}_{0}+\frac{(\lambda_{3}+2)e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\left(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\hat{u}_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{4}e^{\lambda_{4}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}

and

I_{6}\triangleq-\frac{(\lambda_{4}+2)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\left(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\hat{u}_{0}-\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}.

Then, by Lemma
4.2
, we have

\|I_{6}\|\leq C\left\||\xi|^{2}e^{-|\xi|^{2}t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+Ce^{-Rt}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|I_{5}\|\leq Ce^{-Rt},

which implies

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.2
and the assumption (
2.8
), we find

\displaystyle\|I_{6}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|<r_{0}}\left|-\frac{(\lambda_{4}+2)e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\Big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)\hat{u}_{0}-\frac{\lambda_{3}e^{\lambda_{3}t}}{\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}}\hat{v}_{0}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C_{10}^{2}\alpha_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{2}},

where

denotes a small positive constant independent of time. Thus, we obtain

\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}=\|\hat{u}-\hat{v}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|I_{6}\|_{L^{2}}-\|I_{5}\|_{L^{2}}\geq C_{10}\alpha_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}-Ce^{-Ct}\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{10}\alpha_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}.

Choosing
\bar{d}_{*}=\frac{1}{2}C_{10}\alpha_{0}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{*}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\leq\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}.

Thus we complete the proof.

In this subsection, we provide the upper bound of the decay rates of solutions to the EP-NS system (

) proving the large-time behavior estimates stated in Theorem
2.1
. We consider the following nonlinear system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\eta(t)+\mathcal{L}\eta(t)=F,\\
\eta|_{t=0}=\eta_{0},\end{array}\right.

with
\eta=(n,u,v)^{t}
,
\mathcal{L}
appeared in (
4.7
) ,and
F=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})^{t}
, where
f_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
are given as in (
3.4
). By Duhamel’s principle, we have

\eta(t)=S(t)*\eta_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)*F(s)\,ds,

where
S=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}
. From this, we obtain

\displaystyle n(t)

\displaystyle=S_{11}*n_{0}+S_{12}*u_{0}

(4.13)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}S_{11}(t-s)*f_{1}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S_{12}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\,ds,

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=S_{21}*n_{0}+S_{22}*u_{0}+S_{23}*v_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}S_{21}(t-s)*f_{1}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S_{22}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S_{23}(t-s)*f_{3}(s)\,ds,

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle=S_{32}*u_{0}+S_{33}*v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S_{32}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}S_{33}(t-s)*f_{3}(s)\,ds.

We then recall the time-weighted energy function defined as

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{2}(1+s)^{\frac{11}{4}+\frac{k}{2}}\left\|\nabla^{k}n(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}+(1+s)^{\frac{15}{4}}\|\nabla^{3}n(s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=0}^{3}(1+s)^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{k}{2}}\left\|\nabla^{k}(u,v)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}.

It’s straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}M(t),\quad k=0,1,2,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{3}n(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}M(t),

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{k}(u,v)(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}M(t),\quad k=0,1,2,3.

Our next objective is to show that
M(t)
remains uniformly bounded over time. More precisely, we prove the following proposition, which corresponds to the large-time behavior estimates of solutions stated in Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(n,u,v)
be the classical solutions of system (
3.3
). Then it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|n(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(1+t)^{-2},

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

\displaystyle k=0,1,2,

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{3}n(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}},

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{k}(u,v)(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

\displaystyle k=0,1,2,3,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

\displaystyle k=0,1,

where
C
is a positive constant independent of time.

Proof
.
We first show the decay estimates of density. By Proposition
4.3
, we deduce

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla^{k}S_{11}*n_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{k}S_{12}*u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla^{k}S_{11}(t-s)*f_{1}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla^{k}S_{12}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-Rt}\left(\left\|\nabla^{k}n_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{k}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left\|\nabla^{k}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-Rt}\left(\left\|\nabla^{k}n_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{k}u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\Big\{\left\|\nabla^{k}(u\cdot\nabla n)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{k}(u\cdot\nabla u)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\nabla^{k}(\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(e^{n}-1-n))\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}\,ds.

\bullet
For
k=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}(\left\|u\cdot\nabla n\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}})\,ds

(4.17)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq CM(t)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\Big\{(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{13}{4}}+(1+s)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+s)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\Big\}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}}M(t)^{2}.

Letting
Z(x,t)\triangleq e^{n}-1-n
, we also estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\|\nabla K*Z\|_{L^{2}}\,ds

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left(\left\|\nabla K^{\ell}*Z\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla K^{h}*Z\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left(\left\|\nabla K^{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|Z\|_{L^{1}}+\|Z\|_{L^{2}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left(\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq CM(t)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\Big((1+s)^{-\frac{11}{2}}+(1+s)^{-\frac{25}{4}}\Big)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{2}}M(t)^{2},

where
K
is the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation and we used
\|\nabla K^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
and the fact that
|\widehat{\nabla K^{h}}|\leq C
. Then we substitute (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) into (
4.16
) to get the time decay rates for
k=0
that

\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\delta_{0}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}}M(t)^{2}.

\bullet
For
k=1,2
, we notice that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u\cdot\nabla n)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}})\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{21}{4}}M(t)^{2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla(u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}})\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{13}{4}}M(t)^{2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla n)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}})\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{23}{4}}M(t)^{2},

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}})\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}M(t)^{2}.

By using these estimates, we have the following estimate of derivatives of density:

\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\delta_{0}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}M(t)^{2},\quad k=0,1,2.

It is worth noting that we cannot directly take the third derivatives on the nonlinear term
u\cdot\nabla u
due to the constraints of our setting with
M(t)
. To this end, we decompose it into low-high frequency parts:

\displaystyle n=n^{\ell}+n^{h}.

For
\nabla^{3}n^{\ell}
, we find

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{3}n^{\ell}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{11}*n_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{12}*u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{11}(t-s)*f_{1}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{12}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-Rt}\left(\left\|n_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left\|\nabla^{2}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-Rt}\left(\left\|n_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\Big\{\left\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla n)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla u)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\|\nabla^{2}(\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(e^{n}-1-n))\right\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}\,ds.

Here we estimate

\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla n)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{23}{4}}M(t)^{2},\quad\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}M(t)^{2},

and

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla^{2}\nabla K*\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\nabla^{2}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{1}}+C\left\|\nabla^{2}\left(e^{n}-1-n\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla^{2}(n\cdot n)\|_{L^{1}}+\|\nabla^{2}(n\cdot n)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{13}{2}}M(t)^{2}.

Substituting the above facts into (
4.19
), we have

\left\|\nabla^{3}n^{\ell}(t)\right\|_{2}\leq Ce^{-Rt}\delta_{0}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{4}}M(t)^{2}.

(4.20)

Now we move to the estimate of the decay rates for velocities.
By using the fact

\big(I-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\big)\cdot\frac{i\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}=0

and Proposition
4.3
, we obtain from (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-Rt}(\|n_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}})+C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{L^{1}}+Ce^{-Rt}\|(u_{0},v_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}(\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}f_{1}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{1}(s)\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{2}(s)\|_{L^{2}}+\|f_{3}(s)\|_{L^{2}})\,ds

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\|h_{2}(s)\|_{L^{1}}+\|h_{3}(s)\|_{L^{1}})\,ds,

where the nonlinear terms
f_{1},f_{2}
are given by (
3.4
) and the new definitions
h_{2}
and
h_{3}
satisfy

h_{2}=-u\cdot\nabla u,\quad h_{3}=-v\cdot\nabla v+(e^{n}-1)(u-v).

(4.21)

On the other hand, we use (
4.14
) to get

\displaystyle\|f_{1}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-4}M(t)^{2},\quad\|f_{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{19}{4}}M(t)^{2},

(4.22)

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-2}M(t)^{2},\quad\|f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}}M(t)^{2},

\displaystyle\|h_{2}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-2}M(t)^{2},\quad\|h_{3}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-2}M(t)^{2},

\displaystyle\|f_{3}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{11}{4}}M(t)^{2},

and

\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}f_{1}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}}+\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}})\leq C(1+s)^{-4}M(t)^{2}.

Then, this together with applying Hölder inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\delta_{0}+CM(t)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}(1+s)^{-\frac{11}{4}}\,ds

\displaystyle+CM(t)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+s)^{-2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\left(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{2}\right).

The derivatives of
u
can be obtained in a similar manner. Thus, we get for
k=0,1,2
that

\left\|\nabla^{k}u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\left(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{2}\right).

By using a similar argument, we also obtain the decay estimates of velocity
v
for
k=0,1,2
that

\left\|\nabla^{k}v(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\left(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{2}\right).

For low-frequency part of
\left\|\nabla^{3}u^{\ell}\right\|_{L^{2}}
, we consider

\displaystyle\nabla^{3}u^{\ell}(t)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{21}*n_{0}+\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{22}*u_{0}+\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{23}*v_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{21}(t-s)*f_{1}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{22}(t-s)*f_{2}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla^{3}S^{\ell}_{23}(t-s)*f_{3}(s)\,ds.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{3}u^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{9}{4}}\delta_{0}+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(t-s)}\left\|\nabla^{2}f_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}ds

(4.23)

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{9}{4}}(\left\|h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|h_{3}\right\|_{L^{1}})\,ds

\displaystyle+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-s)^{-\frac{5}{4}}(\left\|\nabla^{2}h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\nabla^{2}h_{3}\right\|_{L^{1}})\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{9}{4}}\left(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{2}\right),

where we used (
4.22
) and the fact that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{23}{4}}M(t)^{2},\quad\left\|\nabla^{2}h_{2}\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-3}M(t)^{2},\quad\|\nabla^{2}h_{3}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-3}M(t)^{2}.

Similarly, we also achieve that

\|\nabla^{3}v^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{9}{4}}\left(\delta_{0}+M(t)^{2}\right).

(4.24)

For the decay rate of
\|\nabla^{3}(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}
, we consider the high-frequency part of the main system (
3.3
) as:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n^{h}+{\rm{div}}u^{h}=f^{h}_{1},\\
&\partial_{t}u^{h}+\nabla n^{h}+u^{h}-v^{h}+\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}=f^{h}_{2},\\
&\partial_{t}v^{h}+v^{h}-\mathbb{P}u^{h}-\Delta v^{h}=f^{h}_{3},\\
&\text{div}v^{h}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Taking
L^{2}
inner product with
\nabla^{3}(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h})
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{3}(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{4}v^{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}(u^{h}-v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{2}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{3}\cdot\nabla^{3}v^{h}\,dx.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}\operatorname{div}u^{h}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{4}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\,dx.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{\|\nabla^{3}(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{4}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}+\left\|\nabla^{4}v^{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}(u^{h}-v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(4.25)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{2}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{3}\cdot\nabla^{3}v^{h}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\,dx.

We split the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.25
) into two terms:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f_{1}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{\ell}_{1}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\triangleq J_{1}+J_{2}.

We then estimate
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
as
The term
J_{1}
is estimated as

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{3}(u\cdot\nabla n)-u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla n)\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla n\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{3}(u\cdot\nabla n)-u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla n)\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla n^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{3}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{4}n^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-10}M(t)^{3}

and

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq\|\nabla^{3}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}f^{\ell}_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{3}n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}(u\cdot\nabla n)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-10}M(t)^{3}.

This deduces

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\cdot\nabla^{3}n^{h}\,dx\leq C(1+t)^{-10}M(t)^{3}

for some
C>0
independent of time.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.25
), we also divide it into two terms:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{2}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f_{2}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{\ell}_{2}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\triangleq J_{3}+J_{4}.

Here the first term of
J_{3}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)-u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla u)\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla u\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)-u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla u)\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla u^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla^{3}\nabla u^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{4}u^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\epsilon\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{2}}M(t)^{4}+\epsilon\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

The other terms of
J_{3}
and
J_{4}
can be handled in a similar manner. Finally, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{2}\cdot\nabla^{3}u^{h}\,dx=J_{3}+J_{4}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{2}}(M(t)^{3}+M(t)^{4})+C\epsilon\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

We also have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{3}\cdot\nabla^{3}v^{h}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{3}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\cdot\nabla^{3}f^{h}_{1}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{2}}(M(t)^{3}+M(t)^{4})+C\epsilon\|\nabla^{3}v^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

The other terms are treated similarly, and thus we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big\{\|\nabla^{3}(n^{h},u^{h},v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{4}(-\Delta)^{-1}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}+\left\|\nabla^{4}v^{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}(u^{h}-v^{h})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{2}}\Big(M(t)^{3}+M(t)^{4}\Big)+C\epsilon(\|\nabla^{3}u^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{3}v^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{15}{2}}\Big(M(t)^{3}+M(t)^{4}\Big)+C\epsilon(\|\nabla^{3}(u^{h}-v^{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{4}v^{h}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}).

Due to the smallness of
\epsilon
, we have

\eqref{032509}+\beta_{3}\times\eqref{032508}+\beta_{3}\times\eqref{032505}
with
\beta_{3}>0
small enough, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\left\|n\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\Big)+\beta_{3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{k}u\cdot\nabla^{k+1}n\,dx+\beta_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u\cdot\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\,dx\right\}

\displaystyle+(1+\bar{C}_{0})\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{3}\|n\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{3}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\beta_{3}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\bar{C}_{1}+\bar{C}_{2})\beta_{3}\|u\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+C\mathcal{A}(t)\mathcal{B}^{2}(t)+C\beta_{3}\mathcal{A}(t)\mathcal{B}^{2}(t).

Due to the smallness of
\beta_{3}
, we find a positive constant
\bar{C}_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\left\|n\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|n\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right\}+\bar{C}_{3}\left\{\left\|n\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|n\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{A}(t)\mathcal{B}^{2}(t)\leq C\delta_{*}\mathcal{B}^{2}(t).

According to the definition
\mathcal{B}(t)
and the smallness of
\delta_{*}
, we arrive at

\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\left\|n\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|n\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right\}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{C}_{3}\left\{\left\|n\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|n\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right\}\leq 0.

(6.5)

Integrating the above inequality with respect to time from
0
to
t
yields

\displaystyle\left\|n\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|n\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{C}_{3}\int_{0}^{t}\left\{\left\|n(s)\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\left\|n(s)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}^{2}+\left\|u(s)\right\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\right\}ds\leq C\delta_{0}^{2},

which guarantees the uniform-in-time boundedness of solutions. Then by a continuation argument, we establish the global-in-time well-posedness.

Moreover, applying Grönwall’s lemma to (
6.5
), we have

\left\|n(t)\right\|_{H^{3}}+\left\|n(t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}+\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{3}}\leq C\delta_{0}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\bar{C}_{3}t}.

Finally, we set
C_{*}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{C}_{3}
to complete the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

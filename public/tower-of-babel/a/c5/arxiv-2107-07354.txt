Abstract
. This work concerns surjective maps φ : R → S \varphi\colon R\to S of commutative noetherian local rings with kernel generated by a regular sequence that is part of a minimal generating set for the maximal ideal of R R . The main result provides criteria for detecting such exceptional complete intersection maps in terms of the lattices of thick subcategories of the derived category of complexes of finite length homology. A key input is a characterization of such maps in terms of the truncated Atiyah class of φ \varphi .

Introduction

This work is part of an ongoing project to understand properties of commutative noetherian rings, and of maps between them, in terms of the triangulated structure of their derived categories. Building on the work of Dwyer, Greenlees and the first author
[
20
]
, the fourth author
[
35
]
established a homotopical description of locally complete intersection rings: These are the commutative noetherian rings with the property that any nonzero thick subcategory of its bounded derived category contains a nonzero perfect complex. Similar homotopical characterizations of the complete intersection property have since appeared in the works of Briggs, Grifo and the authors
[
14
,
15
,
30
]
; the most general result is contained in
[
15
]
.

A surjective map
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
of local rings is complete intersection if its kernel is generated by an
R
-regular sequence. The focus of the present manuscript is on a special class of complete intersection maps where, in addition, the regular sequence is part of a minimal generating set for the maximal ideal of
R
. Such maps arise naturally in various contexts; for example, the diagonal of a smooth map has this property locally. They also arose in recent work of the first author with Brochard and Khare
[
16
]
, where they are called
exceptional complete intersection
maps; we adopt this terminology.

Our motivation for the present undertaking comes from
[
15
]
that raises the prospect of defining locally complete intersection maps in the broader setting of tensor triangulated categories. However to realize this goal requires a homotopical description of the diagonal of a smooth map, and more generally, of a weakly regular map. Our main result provides such a description for general exceptional complete intersection maps; see also the discussion in
5.8
. Let
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
be the subcategory of the derived category of
R
consisting of complexes with finite length homology. The statement involves the functor
{\varphi}_{*}\colon{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)\to{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
induced by restriction along
{\varphi}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be a surjective map of commutative noetherian local rings. Then
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite and
{\varphi}_{*}
induces an isomorphism between the lattice of thick subcategories of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
and that of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
.

See
Corollary

5.10
for a more precise statement. The exceptional complete intersection property does not localize and so, unsurprisingly, our characterization is in terms of the complexes supported only at the maximal ideals of
R
and
S
. One cannot, in general, relax this hypothesis to allow thick subcategories with bigger support; see
Example

3.7
and
Lemma

5.3
. It is also worth pointing out that the characterizations of the complete intersection property in
[
14
,
15
,
35
]
use objects from
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
in an essential way.

Questions about the thick subcategories of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
, the bounded derived category of
R
, can often be reduced to those about the thick subcategories of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})
as
\mathfrak{p}
ranges over the prime ideals in
R
; this is the upshot of local-to-global principles from
[
12
]
. When the ring
R
is Cohen–Macaulay,
A
allows one to further reduce to the case of artinian rings. This idea is illustrated in
Corollary

5.13
.

To each
S
-complex
M
there is a naturally defined morphism in
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
called the
truncated Atiyah class of
{\varphi}
at
M
, denoted
\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}(M)
, which was introduced in
[
27
]
; see
2.1
. The following characterization of exceptional complete intersection maps in terms of the truncated Atiyah class of
k
is an input in our proof of
A
.

Theorem B
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be a surjective map of commutative noetherian local rings, and let
k
be their common residue field. Then
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite and
\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}(k)=0
.

This result is part of
Theorem

3.4
. It is essentially contained in
[
3
, Proposition 2.8]
and
[
15
, 3.11]
, though couched in terms of actions of Hochschild cohomology. Our contribution is to clarify the connection with the Atiyah class; see, especially,
Lemma

2.8
.

B
also sheds light on the faithfulness (or lack thereof) of the restriction functor
{\varphi}_{*}\colon{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(S)\to{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
, at least when
{\varphi}
is complete intersection. In this case,
{\varphi}_{*}
is faithful if and only if
\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}(M)
is zero for each

in
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
; see
3.8
. This holds, for example, when

is an algebra retract, but not always, even for exceptional complete intersection maps. This is connected to the “lifting problem”; see

. This connection also makes it clear that in

cannot be replaced with the stronger conclusion that
\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}(M)
is zero for each

in
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
; see

and

1.
Hochschild cohomology

The Atiyah class of a map is closely connected to Hochschild cohomology of the algebras involved. In this section we record the necessary facts on Hochschild cohomology and their characteristic action on derived categories. Some constructions require dg (=differential graded) algebras and the corresponding derived category of dg modules; everything we need is explained in
[
4
,
7
]
.

1.1
.

We only consider graded-commutative dg algebras, so let
A
be such a dg algebra. The derived category of dg
A
-modules is denoted
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
, and its suspension functor is denoted
{\sf\Sigma}
. One has the usual bi-functors
\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(-,-)
and
-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{A}-
on
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
; for dg
A
-modules
M
and
N
, we set

\Ext^{*}_{A}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{H}^{*}(\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(M,N))\text{ and }\Tor^{A}_{*}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{H}_{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{A}N)\,.

An element
\alpha
in
\Ext_{A}^{n}(M,N)
corresponds to a morphism
M\to{\sf\Sigma}^{n}N
in
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
; by a slight abuse of notation we write also
\alpha
for this morphism. Given
\alpha\colon M\to{\sf\Sigma}^{n}M
we write
\alpha^{i}
for the composition

M\xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ }{\sf\Sigma}^{n}M\xrightarrow{{\sf\Sigma}^{n}\alpha}\ldots\xrightarrow{{\sf\Sigma}^{n(i-1)}\alpha}{\sf\Sigma}^{ni}M

in
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
, and say
\alpha
is
nilpotent
if
\alpha^{i}=0
in
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
for some
i\geqslant 0
.

1.2
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be a map of commutative rings. Set
{S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}S
, the (derived) enveloping algebra of
S
over
R
, and let
\mu_{R}^{S}\colon{S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}\to S
be the multiplication map. We can model
{S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}
as
S\otimes_{R}A
where
{\varphi}
factors as maps of dg algebras

R\longrightarrow A\xrightarrow{\ \epsilon\ }S

with
A
a dg
R
-algebra such that each
R
-module
A_{i}
is flat, and
\epsilon
is a quasi-isomorphism. The multiplication map
\mu_{R}^{S}
is modeled by
1\otimes\epsilon\colon S\otimes_{R}A\to S
. This is independent of the choice of factorization of
{\varphi}
in the following sense: If
R\to A^{\prime}\to S
is another such factorization of
{\varphi}
, then
A^{\prime}\otimes_{R}S\simeq A\otimes_{R}S
as dg algebras augmented to
S
. In particular,
S
is fixed under the equivalence
{\mathsf{D}}(S\otimes_{R}A)\equiv{\mathsf{D}}(S\otimes_{R}A^{\prime})
induced by the aforementioned quasi-isomorphism of dg algebras.

We view
S
as a dg
{S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}
-module through the multiplication map
\mu^{S}_{R}
.

1.3
.

The Hochschild cohomology of
S
over
R
is the graded-commutative
S
-algebra

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Ext_{{S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}}^{*}(S,S)\,.

For each
M
in
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
there is a morphism of graded
S
-algebras

\chi_{M}\colon\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)\longrightarrow\Ext_{S}^{*}(M,M)\,

induced by the functor
-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{S}M\colon{\mathsf{D}}({S}^{\operatorname{e}})\to{\mathsf{D}}(S)
. We call
\chi_{M}
the
characteristic map of
M
. It defines a central action of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)
on
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
: for
N
in
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
and maps
\alpha
in
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)
and
\beta
in
\Ext_{S}(M,N)
one has

\chi_{N}(\alpha)\beta=(-1)^{|\alpha||\beta|}\beta\chi_{M}(\alpha)\,.

1.4
.

A subset
U
of
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)
acts
trivially
, respectively,
nilpotently
, on
M
if for each
\alpha\in U
, the element
\chi_{M}(\alpha)
in
\Ext_{S}(M,M)
is zero, respectively, nilpotent. For example,
\operatorname{HH}^{\geqslant 1}(S/R)
acts trivially on the
S
-complex
S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}L
for any
R
-complex
L
. The subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
consisting of
S
-complexes on which
U
acts nilpotently is thick; that is to say, it is a triangulated subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
closed under direct summands. These observation will be used often in what follows.

2.
The truncated Atiyah class

In this section we recall the construction of the truncated Atiyah class, introduced by Huybrechts and Thomas in
[
27
]
, and discuss its connection to Hochschild cohomology. While the truncated Atiyah class, like the Atiyah class itself, can be defined for any map of rings, we only need it for surjective maps.

Throughout
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
is a surjective map of commutative noetherian rings, with kernel
I
. The discussion below is extracted from
[
15
]
.

2.1
.

The multiplication map
\mu^{S}_{R}
from
1.2
extends in an exact triangle

{S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}}\xrightarrow{\ \mu\ }S\to{\sf\Sigma}J\to

in
{\mathsf{D}}({S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}})
. From the triangle above, it is clear that

\operatorname{H}_{i}(J)=\begin{cases}\Tor_{\geqslant 1}^{R}(S,S)&i\geqslant 1;\\
0&i\leqslant 0\,.\end{cases}

Thus the canonical truncation yields a morphism
J\to{\sf\Sigma}I/I^{2}
in
{\mathsf{D}}({S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}})
. The
truncated Atiyah class
of
{\varphi}
, denoted
\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}
, is the composition of morphisms

S\to{\sf\Sigma}J\to{\sf\Sigma}^{2}I/I^{2}

in
{\mathsf{D}}({S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}})
. Consider the map sending
\alpha
in
\Hom_{S}(I/I^{2},S)
to the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf\Sigma}^{2}I/I^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf\Sigma}^{2}S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\sf\Sigma}^{2}\alpha}

in
{\mathsf{D}}({S}_{R}^{\operatorname{e}})
. By
[

\delta^{\varphi}\colon\Hom_{S}(I/I^{2},S)\xrightarrow{\cong}\operatorname{HH}^{2}(S/R)\,.

For each

{\mathsf{D}}(S)
and

\Hom_{S}(I/I^{2},S)

as a class in

2.2
.

As discussed in
[
27
]
, the truncated Atiyah class
\mathsf{at}^{{\varphi}}(M)
can be recovered from the universal Atiyah class

{\sf At}^{\varphi}\colon S\to{\sf\Sigma}{\rm L}({\varphi})

where
{\rm L}({\varphi})
is the cotangent complex of
{\varphi}
; cf.
[
28

Namely,

M\xrightarrow{{\sf At}^{\varphi}\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{S}M}{\sf\Sigma}{\rm L}({\varphi})\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{S}M\to{\sf\Sigma}^{2}I/I^{2}\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{S}M

where the second map is induced by the soft truncation using that
\operatorname{H}_{0}({\rm L}({\varphi}))=0
and
\operatorname{H}_{1}({\rm L}({\varphi}))=I/I^{2}
.
The cotangent complex and universal Atiyah class of
{\varphi}
are defined via simplicial resolutions and since this machinery is not needed in this article we direct the interested reader to

for further details.

2.3
.

Suppose now that the surjective map
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
is complete intersection of codimension
c

is generated by an

S
-module

is a free
S
-module of rank

, and the map
\delta^{\varphi}
induces the isomorphism of

-algebras

\mathrm{Sym}(\delta^{\varphi})\colon\mathrm{Sym}_{S}(\Hom_{S}(I/I^{2},S))\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)

where

sits in cohomological degree 2. See
[

for a proof. Choosing a basis for the

gives rise to a description of

as a polynomial ring over

[

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S

Proof.

Set

over

.
∎

Assume

is complete intersection of codimension one. By

is isomorphic to

3.
Exceptional complete intersections

In this section we prove
B
from the introduction, and present examples that witness some, perhaps, surprising behavior.

3.1
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be a surjective map of noetherian local rings; set
I\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Ker}{\varphi}
. Following
[
16
]
we say
{\varphi}
is an
exceptional complete intersection
provided
I
is generated by a regular sequence that can be extended to a minimal generating set for
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
, the maximal ideal of
R
. The latter condition means that the natural map

\frac{I}{\mathfrak{m}_{R}I}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{R}}{\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}}

is one-to-one. We refer to
[
16
]
, where this notion is introduced for not necessarily surjective maps of local rings, for various characterizations of exceptional complete intersection maps in terms of numerical invariants associated to
R
and
S
. Here is one more, extracted from the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be a surjective map of noetherian local rings. The map
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite and the natural map
I/\mathfrak{m}_{R}I\to\mathfrak{m}_{R}/\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}
is one-to-one.

Proof.

Since a complete intersection map has finite projective dimension, the only if direction is clear. As to the converse: We can assume
I\neq 0
. Then the hypothesis that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite means that
I
contains a nonzero divisor, say
x
; this is by a result of Auslander and Buchsbaum
[
17
, Corollary 1.4.7]
. Since
I\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{R}
, one can ensure that
x
is not in
\mathfrak{m}^{2}_{R}
; this is by a variation of the prime avoidance argument
[
17
, Lemma 1.2.2]
. Setting
R^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R/xR

Example 3.7

4.
Homological invariants

In this section we track the change of certain homological invariants along exceptional complete intersection maps. The results obtained suggest that, notwithstanding the remarks in
3.8
and
3.9
, homological properties of the source and target of an exceptional complete intersection map are tightly linked.

As before, given a local ring
S
, we write
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
for the full subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
consisting of objects with finitely generated homology.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be exceptional complete intersection and

in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0
for

if and only if
\Ext_{S}^{i}(M,N)=0
for
i\gg 0
;

(2)

\Tor^{R}_{i}(M,N)=0
for

if and only if
\Tor^{S}_{i}(M,N)=0
for
i\gg 0
.

Proof.

We first reduce to the case when
\operatorname{H}(M)
has finite length: Let
K
be the Koszul complex on a system of parameters for

and consider the
S
-complex
K\otimes_{S}M
. Then the length of the
S
-module
\operatorname{H}(K\otimes_{S}M)
is finite. Moreover, since
K\otimes_{S}M
can be realized as an iterated mapping cone of maps defined by multiplication by an element of
S
, a straightforward argument yields that

\displaystyle\Ext_{R}^{i}(M,N)=0\quad\text{for $i\gg 0$}\iff\Ext_{R}^{i}(K\otimes_{S}M,N)=0\quad\text{for $i\gg 0$}

\displaystyle\Ext_{S}^{i}(M,N)=0\quad\text{for $i\gg 0$}\iff\Ext_{S}^{i}(K\otimes_{S}M,N)=0\quad\text{for $i\gg 0$;}

see, for instance,
[
20
, 2.9]
. Thus replacing
M
by
K\otimes_{S}M
we can assume the length of
\operatorname{H}(M)
is finite.

(1) Since the length of
\operatorname{H}(M)
is finite, the
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)
-action on
\Ext_{S}(M,M)
, and hence also on
\Ext_{S}(M,N)
, is nilpotent; see
3.6
. The desired equivalence is now immediate from
[
24
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
that state the
R
-module
\Ext_{R}(M,N)
is finitely generated if and only if the
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(S/R)
-module
\Ext_{S}(M,N)
is finitely generated.

(2) With
(-)^{\vee}
denoting Matlis duality of
S
-modules, one has isomorphisms

\Tor^{R}_{i}(M,N)^{\vee}\cong\Ext_{R}^{i}(M^{\vee},N)\quad\text{and}\quad\Tor^{S}_{i}(M,N)^{\vee}\cong\Ext_{S}^{i}(M^{\vee},N)\,.

Since
\operatorname{H}(M)
has finite length, so does
\operatorname{H}(M^{\vee})
; the desired equivalence now follows from (1) and the faithfulness of Matlis duality.
∎

Setting
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k
in either part of

recovers Nagata’s theorem

for the definitions of the projective dimension and injective dimension of a complex.

Corollary 4.2

If
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
is exceptional complete intersection, then for each

{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
there are equalities

\displaystyle\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M

\displaystyle=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{S}M+\dim R-\dim S

\displaystyle=\mathrm{inj\,dim}_{S}M+\dim R-\dim S\,.

As noted above, from

we know

The desired result translates to

in

\operatorname{HH}^{2}(S/R)
acts nilpotently on each object of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
. Now the desired result follows from
Lemma

5.3
.

(\Leftarrow)
: Since
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite, there exist
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
from
5.5
. Using the assumption that
{\varphi}
has ascent of building it follows from the two properties of the
M_{i}
from
5.5
that
{\varphi}
is complete intersection.

As
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite,
S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}k\models_{R}k
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
. Since
{\varphi}
has ascent of building on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
, we conclude that

S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}k\mathrel{\mathop{\models}\limits_{\vbox to3.98611pt{\kern-3.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\,\,S$}\vss}}}k\,.

In particular
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(S/R)
acts nilpotently on
k
, for
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(S/R)
acts trivially on
S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}k
. Thus
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection, by
Theorem

3.4
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

Instead of invoking
Theorem

3.4
in the proof of
(\Leftarrow)
of
Theorem

5.6
one can argue as follows: Use the same argument as before that
{\varphi}
is complete intersection. Next factor
R\to E\to S
where
E
is the Koszul complex over
R
on a minimal generating set for
\operatorname{Ker}{\varphi}.
There is the following commutative diagram obtained by restricting scalars
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}(S)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\equiv}

where the vertical map is an equivalence since
E\xrightarrow{\simeq}S.
Now as
{\varphi}
has ascent of building on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
, so does
R\to E
on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(E)
.

The main point is that the Koszul complex over
R
on a minimal generating set for
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
, denoted
K^{R}
, is an object of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(E)
with the property

E\otimes_{R}K^{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\models}\limits_{\vbox to3.98611pt{\kern-3.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\,\,E$}\vss}}}K^{R}

if and only if
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection; this can be verified by a direct calculation. Now as
R\to E
has ascent of building on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(E)
and one always has the finite building

E\otimes_{R}K^{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\models}\limits_{\vbox to3.98611pt{\kern-3.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\,\,R$}\vss}}}K^{R}\,,

we obtain the desired conclusion from the previous remark.

Note that the argument sketched above justifies that when
{\varphi}
is complete intersection,
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection if and only if
S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}K^{R}
has finite projective dimension over
S
.

5.8
.

Theorem

5.6
is a characterization of exceptional complete intersection maps purely in terms of the structure of
{\mathsf{D}}(R)
and
{\mathsf{D}}(S)
as triangulated categories. Namely, for a triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
one can consider its thick subcategory of compact objects, denoted
{\mathsf{T^{c}}}
. Define
{\mathsf{T^{fl}_{b}}}
to be the full subcategory of
{\mathsf{T}}
consisting of objects
X
such that

\Hom_{{\mathsf{T}}}(C,X)
is a finite length
\Hom_{{\mathsf{T}}}(C,C)
-module for each
C
in
{\mathsf{T^{c}}}
. In the context of
{\mathsf{D}}(R)
, it is well known an
R
-complex belongs to
{\mathsf{D}}(R)^{\sf c}
precisely when it has finite projective dimension over
R
, and
{\mathsf{D}}(R)_{\sf b}^{\sf fl}={\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
.

Let
\Thick{\mathsf{T}}
be the lattice of thick subcategories of a triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
.

5.9
.

Let
{\varphi}\colon R\to S
be a surjective map of local rings, and
{\varphi}_{*}\colon{\mathsf{D}}(S)\to{\mathsf{D}}(R)
the restriction functor.
For a thick subcategory
{\mathsf{U}}
of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
, we write
F({\mathsf{U}})
to denote the smallest thick subcategory containing all objects
M
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
such that
{\varphi}_{*}(M)
is in
{\mathsf{U}}
. If
{\mathsf{S}}
is a thick subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
, then
G({\mathsf{S}})
is the smallest thick subcategory containing all objects
{\varphi}_{*}(M)
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
such that
M
is in
{\mathsf{S}}
. Thus one has maps of lattices

\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)\mathrel{{\mathop{\rightleftarrows}\limits}}\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)\,.

The following result is Theorem
A
.

Corollary 5.10
.

The map
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection if and only if
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite and
F
and
G
are mutually inverse lattice isomorphisms.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
: This follows immediately from
Theorem

5.6
.

(\Rightarrow):

From
3.6
and
Lemma

5.3
,
FG
is the identity on
\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S).

Fix a thick subcategory
{\mathsf{U}}
in
\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
. To show
GF({\mathsf{U}})={\mathsf{U}}
, it suffices to show for each object
L
in
{\mathsf{U}}

one has

(5.10.1)

\thick_{R}L=\thick_{R}{\varphi}_{*}(M)\qquad\text{where $M=S\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}L$.}

Since
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite,
L\models_{R}{\varphi}_{*}(M)
, by Lemma
5.2
. Let
K
be the Koszul complex on a minimal generating set for
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
and
E
the Koszul complex on a minimal generating set for
\operatorname{Ker}({\varphi})
. By
5.1
one has that
E\models_{R}K
and so

{\varphi}_{*}(M)\simeq E\otimes_{R}L\mathrel{\mathop{\models}\limits_{\vbox to3.98611pt{\kern-3.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\,\,R$}\vss}}}K\otimes_{R}L\,.

Furthermore, as
\operatorname{H}(L)
is finite length over
R
, it follows that
K\otimes_{R}L\models_{R}L
and so
{\varphi}_{*}(M)\models_{R}L
; thus,
Eq.

5.10.1
holds.
∎

Corollary 5.11
.

Consider surjective maps
R\xrightarrow{{\varphi}}S\xrightarrow{\psi}T
of commutative noetherian local rings. If
{\varphi}
and
\psi
are exceptional complete intersection, then
\psi{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection. The converse holds when
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{S}T
is finite.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition that the class of exceptional complete intersection maps is closed under composition. It also follows trivially from the characterization in
Theorem

5.6
.

Assume that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{S}T
is finite and
\psi{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection. It can be argued as in
[
15
, Corollary 4.2]
that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}S
is finite. Also, it is clear that
\psi
has ascent of finite building on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(S)
since
\psi{\varphi}
does. Hence,
\psi
is exceptional complete intersection. Now as
\psi
and
\psi{\varphi}
have ascent of finite building on finite length objects, we can apply
Corollary

5.10
to deduce
{\varphi}
is exceptional complete intersection.
∎

Another application of
Corollary

5.10
is provided when
R
is an equicharacteristic Cohen–Macaulay ring of
minimal multiplicity
. When
R
is Cohen–Macaulay Abhyankar
[
1
]
proved the Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity of

is bounded below by
{\rm codim}R+1
; when equality holds
R
is said to be of minimal multiplicity.

5.12
.

For the rest of the section, assume
R
is an equicharacteristic Cohen–Macaulay ring of minimal multiplicity and infinite residue field
k
. There exists an exceptional complete intersection map
R\to S
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{S}^{2}=0
; cf.
[
17
, 4.6.14]
. As
S
is equicharacteristic, it has the form
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{2}
for some
n\geqslant 0.

The structure of the lattice
\mathcal{T}=\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
is especially simply when
R
is also Gorenstein; this forces
n\leqslant 1
.

If
n=0
, then
R
is regular and so
\mathcal{T}
is simply
0\subsetneq{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
.

If
n=1,
then the lattice
\mathcal{T}
is

0\subsetneq\thick_{R}(K)\subsetneq{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)

where
K
is a Koszul complex on any system of parameters for
R
. This is a consequence of
Corollary

5.10
and
[
18
,
31
]
.

When
R
is not Gorenstein,
\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)
possesses a far wilder structure.

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
R
be an equicharacteristic Cohen–Macaulay non-Gorenstein ring of minimal multiplicity with infinite residue field

. Then there exists an infinite descending binary tree of finitely generated thick subcategories in
\Thick{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{fl}}_{\sf{b}}(R)

The assumptions on
R

, from

, to be
k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{2}

.
∎

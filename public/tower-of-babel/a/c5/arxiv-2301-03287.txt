S1
Light-matter coupling

The light-matter coupling part of the total Hamiltonian under the electric dipole approximation is,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{cav-mol}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}-\bm{\hat{\mu}}_{\mathbf{m}}\cdot\mathbf{\hat{E}}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{m}},0),

(S1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}-\Big[\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=\pm}(\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}})\Big]\cdot\mathbf{\hat{E}}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{m}},0),

where
\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}=\bm{\mu}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}=\bra{\mathbf{m},\alpha'_{\mathrm{mol}}}\bm{\hat{\mu}}\ket{\mathbf{m},\mathrm{G}}
is independent of
\mathbf{m}
since we assume that all porphyrin molecules lie flat in the x-y plane and are oriented. The electric field operator of the mode labeled by
\mathbf{k}
and
\alpha

\mathbf{\hat{E}}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r},z)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}}{2V\varepsilon\epsilon_{0}}}\Big(\mathbf{f}^{*}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r},z)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}+\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r},z)\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}\Big).

(S2)

Here,

is the volume of the box we consider, where as mentioned in the main manuscript, we apply periodic boundary conditions along the
x
and
y
directions. From here on, we will call the in-plane area of the box
A=L_{x}L_{y}
.
Here,
\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r},z)
is the mode profile and it satisfies
fabre2020modes

\int d\mathbf{r}\int_{0}^{L_{z}}dz\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{*}(\mathbf{r},z)\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r},z)=L_{z}A.

(S3)

For the TE and TM modes
zoubi2005microscopic

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\mathrm{TE}}(\mathbf{r},z)=

\displaystyle e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\sqrt{2}\sin[\frac{n_{z}\pi}{L_{z}}\Big(z+\frac{L_{z}}{2}\Big)\Bigg]\bm{\hat{\phi}},

(S4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\mathrm{TM}}(\mathbf{r},z)=

\displaystyle e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}+\big(\frac{n_{z}\pi}{L_{z}}\big)^{2}}}\Bigg\{\Big(\frac{n_{z}\pi}{L_{z}}\Big)\sin[\frac{n_{z}\pi}{L_{z}}\Big(z+\frac{L_{z}}{2}\Big)\Bigg]\bm{\hat{\rho}}-i|\mathbf{k}|\cos[\frac{n_{z}\pi}{L_{z}}\Big(z+\frac{L_{z}}{2}\Big)\Bigg]\mathbf{\hat{z}}\Bigg\}.

We make the rotating-wave approximation,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{cav-mol}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}-\Big[\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=\pm}(\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}+\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}})\Big]\cdot\Big[\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}}{2V\varepsilon\epsilon_{0}}}\Big(\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{*}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{m}},0)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}+\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{m}},0)\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}\Big)\Big],

(S5)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}-\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}}{2V\varepsilon\epsilon_{0}}}\Big[\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{m}},0)\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}+\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\cdot\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{*}(\mathbf{r}_{\mathbf{m}},0)\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}\Big],

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}\Big[\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k\cdot r_{m}}}}{\sqrt{N_{x}N_{y}}}(\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha})\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}+\frac{e^{-i\mathbf{k\cdot r_{m}}}}{\sqrt{N_{x}N_{y}}}(\bm{\mu}_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\cdot\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}^{*})\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}\Big],

S2
Chern number calculation

Figure S1:
(a) This is a cartoon figure that demonstrates the way Berry flux and Chern number are computed in our system. The small squares are the plaquettes over which Berry flux is computed. The blue arrows specify the orientation used for Berry flux computation. Note that the direction is opposite for the small squares and the large square. (b) Same as (a), but placed on a sphere. Here, it is more clear that the direction of the arrow for the large square indicates the way Berry flux is computed for the giant plaquette covering the rest of the sphere.

For the Chern invariant to be an integer, it is important that the Berry curvature is integrated over a closed and bounded surface
asboth2016short
. For periodic systems with a finite period, the Brillouin zone is a torus which satisfies this requirement. However, for a continuous system,
(k_{x},k_{y})
lies on an unbounded plane; for such systems, Silveirinha
silveirinha2015chern
proposed mapping this infinitely large plane onto a sphere to compute the Chern number. This is the procedure we follow in our work. We discretize k-space and compute the Berry flux in each plaquette within a square-shaped region in k-space,
-k_{\mathrm{max}}\leq k_{x},k_{y}\leq k_{\mathrm{max}}

fukui2005chern
;
asboth2016short
(Fig. S1a and S1b). The entire region that satisfies the condition
k_{x},k_{y}>k_{\mathrm{max}}
or
k_{x},k_{y}<-k_{\mathrm{max}}
is taken as a single giant plaquette (Fig. S1b), and the Berry flux within this region is computed by taking the Berry phase along the boundary of the plaquette but in a direction opposite to that used to compute Berry flux for plaquettes within the square
-k_{\mathrm{max}}\leq k_{x},k_{y}\leq k_{\mathrm{max}}
as indicated in Fig. S1a and S1b. To ensure that we obtain a converged Chern number, we calculate the Chern number for different
k_{\mathrm{max}}
and find that, for our system, once
k_{\mathrm{max}}\gtrsim 100\mu
m
-1
, the Chern number converges to
C_{1}=\pm 1,C_{2}=\mp 1,C_{3}=0,
and
C_{4}=0
when
f_{+}\neq f_{-}
with
|f_{+}-f_{-}|\gtrsim 0.11
. Smaller differences between
f_{+}
and
f_{-}
,
|f_{+}-f_{-}|\lesssim 0.11
require larger
k_{\mathrm{max}}
for convergence. This is not a problem for the
f_{+}=f_{-}
case because the Chern invariant will always be zero due to time-reversal symmetry
\Omega_{l}(\mathbf{k})=-\Omega_{l}(-\mathbf{k})
, and we can use
k_{\mathrm{max}}\approx 100\mu
m
-1
to compute it.

The Stokes parameter,
S_{3}(\mathbf{k})
, of the lowest energy band (Band 1) under pumping with

The number of excitations in the system
N_{\mathrm{ex}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}a_{\mathbf{k},\alpha}+\sum_{\mathbf{n},\alpha}\sigma^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{n},\alpha}\sigma_{\mathbf{n},\alpha}
is a conserved quantity of this Hamiltonian. Therefore, when we have
f_{+}
fraction of molecules in the
\ket{+_{\mathrm{mol}}}
state and
f_{-}
in the
\ket{-_{\mathrm{mol}}}
state, we will only have to look at the
(f_{+}+f_{-})N^{\mathrm{th}}
excitation manifold. Unfortunately, the dimensions of the Hilbert space of this manifold scale as
\binom{N}{(f_{+}+f_{-})N}
, and this quickly becomes computationally intractable as the system size,
N
, increases. Using mean-field theory, we reduce this many-body problem to a one-body problem. That is, we derive an effective Hamiltonian for a single excitation in the mean-field of the remaining
(f_{+}+f_{-})N
excitations; in this way, we reduce the dimensions of the Hilbert space to that of the first excitation manifold. To do this, we follow a procedure similar to that used by Ribeiro
et al.

f2018theory
and write the Heisenberg equations of motion (EOM) for the operators
\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},\pm}
and
\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}
,

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\commutator{\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{mol}}}+\commutator{\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{cav}}}+\commutator{\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{cav-mol}}}

(S9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{e}}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{x}N_{y}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r_{n}}}\Big[(1-\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\mp}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\mp}-2\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm})\Big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},+}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\pm}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},-}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\pm}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},-}\Big)-\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\mp}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}\Big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},+}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\mp}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+}+\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},-}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\mp}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},-}\Big)\Big],

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\commutator{\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{mol}}}+\commutator{\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{cav}}}+\commutator{\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}}{\hat{H}_{\mathrm{cav-mol}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(E_{0}+\frac{\hbar^{2}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}{2m^{*}}\pm\zeta|\mathbf{k}|\cos\phi\Big)\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}+\Big(-\beta_{0}+\beta|\mathbf{k}|^{2}e^{\mp i2\phi}\Big)\hat{a}_{\mp,\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{x}N_{y}}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r_{m}}}\Big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}^{*}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{+}^{*}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},+}+\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}^{*}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{-}^{*}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},-}\Big).

We make a mean-field approximation to linearize these EOM. For instance, we use
mn\approx\bar{m}n
, that is,

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+}=

\displaystyle\Big(\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}\rangle+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}-\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}\rangle\Big)\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+}

(S10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}\rangle\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+}+(\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}-\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}\rangle)\langle\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}^{\dagger}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},+}\rangle\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},+},

where
\langle\hat{O}\rangle=\Tr[\hat{\rho}_{0}\hat{O}]
with

fowler2022efficient
. Here, we assume that after dephasing of the molecular amplitudes,
\hat{\rho}_{\mathbf{m}}=f_{\mathrm{G}}\ket{\mathbf{m},G}\bra{\mathbf{m},G}+f_{+}\ket{\mathbf{m},+_{\mathrm{mol}}}\bra{\mathbf{m},+_{\mathrm{mol}}}+f_{-}\ket{\mathbf{m},-_{\mathrm{mol}}}\bra{\mathbf{m},-_{\mathrm{mol}}}

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{n},\pm}}{dt}\approx

(S11)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},-}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\pm}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},-}\Big),

\displaystyle\Big(E_{0}+\frac{\hbar^{2}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}{2m^{*}}\pm\zeta|\mathbf{k}|\cos\phi\Big)\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}+\Big(-\beta_{0}+\beta|\mathbf{k}|^{2}e^{\mp i2\phi}\Big)\hat{a}_{\mp,\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{x}N_{y}}}\sum_{\mathbf{m}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r_{m}}}\Big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}^{*}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{+}^{*}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},+}+\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}^{*}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{-}^{*}\hat{\sigma}_{\mathbf{m},-}\Big).

(S12)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{k},-}\cdot\bm{\mu}_{\pm}\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},-}\Big),

\displaystyle\Big(E_{0}+\frac{\hbar^{2}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}{2m^{*}}\pm\zeta|\mathbf{k}|\cos\phi\Big)\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k},\pm}+\Big(-\beta_{0}+\beta|\mathbf{k}|^{2}e^{\mp i2\phi}\Big)\hat{a}_{\mp,\mathbf{k}}

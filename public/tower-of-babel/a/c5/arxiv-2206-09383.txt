An expansion for the sum of a product of an exponential and a Bessel function. II
R.B. Paris

Abstract
We examine the sum of a decaying exponential (depending non-linearly on the summation index) and a Bessel function in the form ∑ n = 1 ∞ e − a ​ n p ​ J ν ​ ( a ​ n p ​ x ) ( 1 2 ​ a ​ n p ​ x ) ν ( x > 0 ) , \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an^{p}}\frac{J_{\nu}(an^{p}x)}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}an^{p}x)^{\nu}}\qquad(x>0), where J ν ​ ( z ) J_{\nu}(z) is the Bessel function of the first kind of real order ν \nu and a a and p p are positive parameters. By means of a Mellin transform approach we obtain an asymptotic expansion that enables the evaluation of this sum in the limit a → 0 a\to 0 . A similar result is derived for the sum when the Bessel function is replaced by the modified Bessel function I ν ​ ( z ) I_{\nu}(z) when x ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) x\in(0,1) . The case of even p p is of interest since the expansion becomes exponentially small in character. We demonstrate that in the case p = 2 p=2 , a result analogous to the Poisson-Jacobi transformation exists for the above sum. 1. Introduction

We consider the sum

S_{\nu,p}(a,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an^{p}}\,\frac{J_{\nu}(an^{p}x)}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}an^{p}x)^{\nu}},\qquad p>0,\ x>0,\ a>0,

(1.1)

where
J_{\nu}(x)
is the Bessel function of the first kind of real order
\nu
and

(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}z)^{-\nu}\!J_{\nu}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z^{2}/4)^{k}}{\Gamma(1+\nu+k)k!}.

Our interest herein is the asymptotic expansion of
S_{\nu,p}(a,x)
in the limit
a\to 0
when convergence of the above sum becomes slow. We employ a Mellin transform approach to express the sum as a Mellin-Barnes integral involving the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
and the Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
. It is found that the resulting (algebraic) asymptotic series converges when
p<1
and diverges when
p>1
; the case
p=1
requires the condition

a<2\pi/\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
for convergence.

In
[
2
]
the asymptotic expansion of the sum

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an}\frac{J_{\nu}(bn)}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}bn)^{\nu}}

was examined for
a\to 0
with
0<b<2\pi
and
\nu>-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
. The details in this case relied on the use of a double Mellin-Barnes integral involving the zeta function when
a<b
.
When
a=0
, the sum has been considered by Tric̆ković
et al.
in
[
4
]
, where approaches using Poisson’s summation formula and Bessel’s integral were employed to derive convergent expansions.

An interesting situation arises when
p
is an even integer in the sum (
1.1
), where the algebraic asymptotic series vanishes to leave an exponentially small contribution. We pay particular attention to the case
p=2
, where it will be demonstrated that a transformation analogous to the well-known Poisson-Jacobi transformation
[
6
, p. 124]

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}/a}\qquad(\Re(a)>0)

(1.2)

holds for the sum in (
1.1
).

2. The asymptotic expansion of
S_{\nu,p}(a,x)
for
a\to 0

Let
a>0
,
x>0

p>0

\nu

S_{\nu,p}(a,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an^{p}}\,\frac{J_{\nu}(an^{p}x)}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}an^{p}x)^{\nu}}

(2.1)

for

. We adopt a Mellin transform approach as described, for example, in
[
3

and write the above sum as

S_{\nu,p}(a,x)=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{-\nu}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h(an^{p}),\qquad h(\tau):=e^{-\tau}\tau^{-\nu}J_{\nu}(x\tau).

With the Mellin transform given by
H(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{s-1}h(\tau)\,d\tau

[
5

H(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{s-\nu-1}e^{-\tau}J_{\nu}(x\tau)\,d\tau=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2}),

where
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;z)
is the Gauss hypergeometric function
[
1
, p. 384]
. Then upon use of the Mellin inversion theorem
[
3
, p. 118]
we obtain the integral representation in the form

S_{\nu,p}(a,x)=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{-\nu}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}H(s)(an^{p})^{-s}ds=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{-\nu}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}H(s)\,\zeta(sp)\,a^{-s}ds

=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(s)\,\zeta(sp)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})\,a^{-s}ds,

(2.2)

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function and the integration path is such that
c>1/p
.

The integrand in (
2.2

s=1/p
,
s=0

s=-k
(

\Re(s)<0

on account of the trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
at
s=-2,-4,\ldots\,
. And if
p
is an even integer there are no poles in
\Re(s)<0

p\neq 2,4,\ldots\,
) then formally produces

S_{\nu,p}(a,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\biggl\{\frac{1}{p}\,a^{-1/p}\Gamma(1/p)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2p}}$},\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{p+1}{2p}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})-\frac{1}{2}+\Upsilon(a)\biggr\}

(2.3)

with

\Upsilon(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-a)^{k}}{k!}\,\zeta(-kp)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k,-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2}),

where we have used the fact that
\zeta(0)=-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
. From the functional relation
[
1

\zeta(s)=2^{s}\pi^{s-1}\,\zeta(1-s)\Gamma(1-s)\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi s,

(2.4)

we find

\zeta(-kp)=-\frac{(2\pi)^{-kp}}{\pi}\,\zeta(1+kp)\Gamma(1+kp)\sin(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi kp)

so that the residue sum
\Upsilon(a)
can be written alternatively as

\Upsilon(a)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{k}}{k!}\biggl(\frac{a}{(2\pi)^{p}}\biggr)^{\!k}\zeta(1+kp)\Gamma(1+kp)\sin(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi kp)\hskip 85.35826pt

\hskip 142.26378pt\times\ {}_{2}F_{1}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k,-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2}).

(2.5)

Throughout the paper we define the acute angle

To discuss the convergence of the series (

\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k,-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})\hskip 227.62204pt

\sim\frac{(1+x^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}k+\nu/2+3/4}}{\sqrt{\pi}(xk)^{\nu+1/2}}\,\sin((k+\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$})\phi-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$}\pi)\qquad(k\to\infty).

\mu\neq-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$},\ldots\,
, to obtain

S_{\nu,p}^{\mu}(a,x)=\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\mu}}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\biggl\{\frac{\Gamma(\mu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{2a^{1/2}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\mu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$},\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\mu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})

-\frac{\sin\pi\mu}{\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\biggl(\frac{a}{(2\pi)^{2}}\biggr)^{\!\mu+k}\zeta(1\!+\!2\mu\!+\!2k)\Gamma(1\!+\!2\mu\!+\!2k)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k,-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}k\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1\!+\!\nu;-x^{2})\biggr\},

(4.2)

where we have made use of the functional relation in (
2.4
).

When
\mu=1
it is seen that the infinite sum of residues in (
4.2
) vanishes, which provides us with another example of a generalised Poisson-Jacobi transformation. When
\nu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
, we obtain

S_{1/2,2}^{1}(a,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an^{2}}\sin(an^{2}x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\pi}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$},\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{5}{4}}$};\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};-x^{2})+R(a)\biggr\}

=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,\frac{\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\phi}{(1+x^{2})^{1/4}}+xR(a)\biggr\}.

(4.3)

Here

R(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(-2s)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1-s}{2}}$},\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{2-s}{2}}$};\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};-x^{2})a^{s}ds\qquad(c>0)

=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\biggl(\frac{\pi^{2}}{a}\biggr)^{\!-u}\Gamma(u)\zeta(2u)(u-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}u\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$},-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}u\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{5}{4}}$};\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};-x^{2})\,du,

since from (
2.4
)

\Gamma(1-s)\zeta(-2s)=-\frac{(u-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{\pi^{2u-1/2}}\,\Gamma(u)\zeta(2u)\qquad(s\to u-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}).

Making use of the identity

{}_{2}F_{1}(-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}u\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}$},-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}u\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{5}{4}}$};\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};-x^{2})=(1+x^{2})^{u/2-1/4}\,\frac{\sin((u-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\phi)}{x(u-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})},

we then obtain

xR(a)=-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,\frac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{1/4}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\biggl(\frac{\pi^{2}n^{2}}{a\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\biggr)^{\!-u}\Gamma(u)\sin((u-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\phi)\,du

\hskip 5.69046pt=\,\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,\frac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{1/4}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-Y)^{k}}{k!}\sin((k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\phi),\qquad Y:=\frac{\pi^{2}n^{2}}{a\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}.

Then since

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-Y)^{k}}{k!}\sin((k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\phi)=-e^{-Y\cos\phi}\sin(Y\!\sin\phi-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\phi),

we finally obtain

xR(a)=-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\,\frac{1}{(1+x^{2})^{1/4}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp\,\biggl[\frac{-\pi^{2}n^{2}}{a(1+x^{2})}\biggr]\sin\biggl(\frac{\pi^{2}n^{2}x}{a(1+x^{2})}-\frac{1}{2}\phi\biggr).\hskip 17.07182pt

(4.4)

Combination of (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) then gives the expansion of
S_{1/2,2}^{1}(a,x)
for
a>0
and clearly has the form of a generalised Poisson-Jacobi transformation.
This result is easily verified upon use of (
1.2
) applied to the sum

\pi^{-1/2}\sum_{n\geq 1}e^{-an^{2}}\sin(an^{2}x)
.

Another extension is the sum

T_{\nu,p}(a,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-an^{p}}\frac{I_{\nu}(an^{p}x)}{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}an^{p}x)^{\nu}},\qquad x\in(0,1),

(4.5)

=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\,\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-\infty i}^{c+\infty i}\Gamma(s)\,\zeta(sp)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}s\!+\!\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1\!+\!\nu;x^{2})\,a^{-s}ds\qquad(c>1/p),

where
I_{\nu}(z)
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind. Then we obtain the same expansion given in (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) with the argument of the hypergeometric function replaced by
x^{2}
.
When
0<p<1
the infinite series of residues is convergent but divergent (asymptotic) when
p>1
.

The special case
p=1
yields

T_{\nu,1}(a,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1+\nu)}\biggl\{\frac{1}{a}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},1;1+\nu;x^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\hskip 113.81102pt

\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-)^{k}\biggl(\frac{a}{2\pi}\biggr)^{\!2k+1}\zeta(2k+2)\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;x^{2})\biggr\}.

(4.6)

From (
A.5
), the leading large-
k
behaviour of the above hypergeometric function is given by

{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;x^{2})\sim\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{(1+x)^{2k+\nu+3/2}}{(kx)^{\nu+1/2}}\qquad(k\to\infty)

so that the infinite sum in (
4.6
) converges when
a<2\pi/(1+x)
. If
a(1+x)/(2\pi)=1
, we have convergence of the sum
for
\nu>-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
, since it is easily seen that the hypergeometric series is positive for
k\geq 1
.

5. Concluding remarks

In Theorem 1 we have presented the expansion of
S_{\nu,p}(a,x)
for
p>0
(
p\neq 2,4,\ldots
) with the parameters

a>0
and
x>0
. This expansion is convergent when
p<1
but is asymptotic when
p>1
. In the case
p=1
convergence requires the condition
a<2\pi/\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
. When
p
is an even integer the character of the expansion changes
to become exponentially small. In two cases when
p=2
it was possible to display a generalised Poisson-Jacobi transformation, although both follow in a straightforward manner from (
1.2
). An attempt at the general case
managed to establish the standard infinite sum of exponentials of the form
\exp\,[-\pi^{2}n^{2}/a]
in (
3.5
), although the factor
P_{\nu}(x,\pi^{2}n^{2}/a)
could not be simplified.

The situation when
p=4
, or higher, is more complicated and preliminary investigation suggests a composite of expansions each containing the exponential factor
\exp\,[-\pi^{2}n^{2}/a]
. We do not consider this situation further here, nor how the expansion might change as
p\to 2
.

Appendix: The large-
k
behaviour of some hypergeometric functions

In this appendix we determine the large-
k
asymptotic behaviour of the hypergeometric functions appearing in the main body of the paper. We first consider the function

F\equiv{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})\qquad(k\to+\infty),

(A.1)

where
x^{2}>0
.
From
[
1
, pp. 388, 390]
we have

F=(1+x^{2})^{2k+\nu+3/2}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(k+\nu+1;k+\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})

=(1+x^{2})^{2k+\nu+3/2}\,\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)\Gamma(k+1)}{2\pi i\,\Gamma(k+\nu+1)}\,\int_{0}^{(1+)}\frac{t^{k+\nu}(t-1)^{-k-1}}{(1+x^{2}t)^{k+\nu+3/2}}\,dt,

(A.2)

where the integration path is a closed contour starting and finishing at
t=0
that encircles
t=1
in the positive sense. The above integral can be written as

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{(1+)}e^{k\psi(t)}f(t)\,dt,

where

\psi(t)=\log\biggl(\frac{t}{(t-1)(1+x^{2}t)}\biggr),\qquad f(t)=\frac{t^{\nu}}{(t-1)(1+x^{2}t)^{\nu+3/2}}.

Saddle points of
\psi(t)
arise where
\psi^{\prime}(t)=0
; that is, at
t_{s}=\pm i/x
. For the saddle at
t_{s}=i/x
, we have

\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{s})=\frac{2ix^{3}}{(1+ix)^{2}},\qquad f(t_{s})=(i/x)^{\nu-1}(1+ix)^{-\nu-5/2},

e^{k\psi(t_{s})}=\frac{1}{(1+ix)^{2k}}=\frac{e^{-2ik\phi}}{(1+x^{2})^{k}},\qquad\phi:=\arctan x.

(A.3)

The path of steepest descent through the saddle
t_{s}=i/x
emanates from the origin and passes to infinity in the upper half-plane in the direction
\arg\,t=2\phi
; that is, in

\Re(t)>0
when
0<x<1
, along
\Re(t)=0
when
x=1
and in
\Re(t)<0
when
x>1
.
The direction of integration at the saddle is
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\arg\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{s})=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$}\pi+\phi
; the steepest descent path through the saddle at
-i/x
in the lower half-plane is the reflection of the path in the upper half-plane. The integration path can then be expanded to infinity around an infinite arc to pass over both saddles, which contribute equally to the integral.

Application of the saddle-point method
[
1
, p. 47]
shows that the contribution to the integral from the saddle
t_{s}=i/x
is

-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{k(-\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{s}))}}\,f(t_{s})e^{k\psi(t_{s})}=-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{k}}\,\frac{(1+x^{2})^{-k-\nu/2-3/4}}{x^{\nu+1/2}}\,\exp\biggl[-i(2k+\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$})\phi+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi i(\nu-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\biggr]

with the conjugate expression from the saddle
t_{s}=-i/x
. Hence, noting that the ratio of gamma functions multiplying
the integral in (
A.2
) is
\Gamma(1+\nu)k^{-\nu}
for large
k
, we obtain the final result

{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;-x^{2})\hskip 227.62204pt

\hskip 56.9055pt\sim\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{(1+x^{2})^{k+\nu/2+3/4}}{(xk)^{\nu+1/2}}\,\sin\biggl((2k+\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$})\phi-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi\nu+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$}\pi\biggr)\qquad(k\to\infty).

(A.4)

In the special case
\nu=-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
, (
A.4
) yields

{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};-x^{2})\sim(1+x^{2})^{k+1/2}\cos((2k+1)\phi),

which is the exact result.

For the function with positive argument

{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;x^{2})\qquad x\in(0,1),

the procedure is similar, where now
\psi(t)=\log[(t/((t-1)(1-x^{2}t)]
with saddles at
\pm 1/x
.
The integration path can be deformed to pass over the saddle at
t_{s}=1/x
, where the direction of integration at the saddle is
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
and

\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{s})=\frac{2x^{3}}{(1-x)^{2}},\qquad f(t_{s})=x^{1-\nu}(1-x)^{\nu-1/2},\qquad e^{k\psi(t_{s})}=(1-x)^{-2k}.

Then we obtain the leading large-
k
behaviour given by

{}_{2}F_{1}(-k,-k-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$};1+\nu;x^{2})\sim\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{(1+x)^{2k+\nu+3/2}}{(xk)^{\nu+1/2}}\qquad(k\to\infty).

(A.5)

Finally, we require an estimate for

|{}_{2}F_{1}(-N+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}it,-N+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}it;1+\nu;-x^{2})|,\qquad t\in(-\infty,\infty)

that appears in the estimation of the remainder term
R_{N}(a)
in (
2.7
). The procedure follows that employed in the estimation of (
A.1
) with
k
replaced by
N-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}it
. The modulus of the exponential factor in (
A.3
) becomes
e^{-\phi t}/(1+x^{2})^{N}
, with the result that

|{}_{2}F_{1}(-N+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}it,-N+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}it;1+\nu;-x^{2})|<\frac{K(1+x^{2})^{N}e^{-\phi t}}{(N^{2}+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}$}t^{2})^{\nu/2+1/4}},

(A.6)

where

is a positive constant.

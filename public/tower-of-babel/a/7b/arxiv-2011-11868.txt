Topological violation of global symmetries in quantum gravity Preprint: TU-1113
Kazuya Yonekura

Abstract
We discuss a topological reason why global symmetries are not conserved in quantum gravity, at least when the symmetry comes from compactification of a higher form symmetry. The mechanism is purely topological and does not require any explicit breaking term in the UV Lagrangian. Local current conservation does not imply global charge conservation in a sum over geometries in the path integral. We explicitly consider the shift symmetry of an axion-like field which originates from the compactification of a p p -form gauge field. Our topological construction is motivated by the brane/black-brane correspondence, brane instantons, and an idea that virtual black branes of a simple kind may be realized by surgery on spacetime manifolds.



1
Introduction

There is strong evidence that exact global symmetries do not exist in quantum
gravity (e.g.
Hawking:1974sw
;
Banks:1988yz
;
Coleman:1988cy
;
Giddings:1988cx
;
Kallosh:1995hi
;
ArkaniHamed:2006dz
;
Banks:2010zn
;
Harlow:2018jwu
;
Harlow:2018tng
).
One of the arguments is based on Euclidean wormholes
Coleman:1988cy
;
Giddings:1988cx
.
Such wormholes may induce terms in the low energy effective theory which violate global symmetries.

There are some conceptual difficulties in the interpretation of the results based on Euclidean wormholes,
such as the fact that those wormholes
may require baby universes and ensemble of theories (or in other words random couplings) rather than a single theory.
It was not so clear whether we should include them in the gravitational path integral.
Recently there are several developments such as exact solutions of the JT gravity
Saad:2019lba

and replica wormholes
Penington:2019kki
;
Almheiri:2019qdq

which all indicate that Euclidean wormholes are important ingredients
of quantum gravity. These developments, as well as others,
lead to new insights into violation of global symmetries in quantum gravity, see e.g.
Harlow:2020bee
;
Chen:2020ojn
;
Hsin:2020mfa
.
(See also
Liu:2020sqb
;
Daus:2020vtf
.)

However, there are still some mysteries, such as how to interpret the appearance of ensemble of theories
Saad:2019lba

(or Coleman’s
\alpha
-parameters
Coleman:1988cy
;
Giddings:1988cx
).
For example, the problem can be sharpened by considering Euclidean wormholes in AdS spaces
which are known to have a definite CFT dual
Maldacena:2004rf
;
ArkaniHamed:2007js
.

In this paper, we study the problem in a broader context of
p
-form symmetries
Gaiotto:2014kfa
.
(See also
Montero:2017yja
;
Harlow:2018tng
;
Rudelius:2020orz
for other discussions.) We argue that
the relevant geometries for the violation of
p
-form symmetries are obtained by surgery on Euclidean spacetime (and its generalization
which we leave to future work). We will explain surgery and its motivation in quantum gravity in later sections.
Euclidean wormholes are special cases of more general surgery.
However, the case of
p=0
(i.e. ordinary symmetry) and
p>0
are qualitatively different, and
we will see that the situation seems to be much more transparent for
p
-form symmetries with
p>0

without difficult conceptual issues such as baby universes and random couplings.
Surprisingly, global symmetries are violated even if we have conserved currents, and the violation is caused
just by summing over certain topologically nontrivial configurations.

2
The general mechanism

Here we would like to discuss why local current conservation does not imply global charge conservation in quantum gravity.
Let us first review how charge conservation is proved in non-gravitational theories
on a fixed background
X={\mathbb{R}}\times Y
, where
{\mathbb{R}}
is the (Euclidean) time direction and
Y
is space.
For definiteness, we consider
p
-form
\mathrm{U}(1)
global symmetries,
although the same conclusion holds for discrete
p
-form symmetries by
more general characterization of symmetry operators as topological operators
Gaiotto:2014kfa
.
2
2

2

The point of view of topological operators make further generalizations possible.
See e.g.
Bhardwaj:2017xup
;
Chang:2018iay
;
Komargodski:2020mxz
.
There may be more general “symmetries” associated to nontrivial bordism classes
McNamara:2019rup
;
Montero:2020icj
,
but we will not discuss them in this paper.

For a
p
-form
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry, there is a conserved current
J
which is a
(p+1)
-form operator,
and satisfies the conservation equation

\displaystyle{\rm d}(\star J)=0,

(1)

where
\star
is the Hodge star.

On a
D
-dimensional manifold
X={\mathbb{R}}\times Y
with fixed topology,
we can define a charge as follows. We take a
(D-p-1)
-dimensional submanifold
\Sigma\subset Y
,
and define the charge

Q(\Sigma_{t})
as

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma_{t})=\int_{\{t\}\times\Sigma}\star J,

(2)

where
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the (Euclidean) time and
\Sigma_{t}=\{t\}\times\Sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times Y
.

Now the conservation of this charge between the initial state at
t=-\infty

and the final state at
t=+\infty
can be shown as follows.
By taking
\Gamma=[-\infty,+\infty]\times\Sigma
, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t=+\infty}-\Sigma_{t=-\infty}=\partial\Gamma.

(3)

Therefore, by Stokes theorem we get

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma_{t=+\infty})-Q(\Sigma_{t=-\infty})=\int_{\Gamma}{\rm d}\star J=0.

(4)

In this way, the charge conservation
Q(\Sigma_{t=+\infty})=Q(\Sigma_{t=-\infty})
follows from the current
conservation
{\rm d}(\star J)=0
.

In quantum gravity, the above reasoning fails.
We may take a sum over different topologies, and the partition function or transition amplitude may be given as

\displaystyle{\cal Z}={\cal Z}(X)+{\cal Z}(X^{\prime})+{\cal Z}(X^{\prime\prime})+\cdots,

(5)

where
X={\mathbb{R}}\times Y
, but
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}
and so on, can have complicated topologies.
The only requirement on them is that they have the same asymptotic regions
\{\pm\infty\}\times Y

(and also the same spatial infinity if
Y
is not compact) as
X
.
3
3

3

This requirement is just imposed for the purpose of a clear demonstration
of global symmetry violation in this paper. We can consider more general processes in which the topologies of the initial time
and the final time are different. See
Jafferis:2017tiu
for an interpretation of such cases.

In other words, we require that

\displaystyle\partial X=\partial X^{\prime}=\partial X^{\prime\prime}=\cdots.

(6)

In the intermediate region, they need not be a product of time
{\mathbb{R}}
and space
Y
.

One might try to show that
Q(\Sigma_{t=+\infty})=Q(\Sigma_{t=-\infty})
by the same reasoning as above.
For this purpose, the crucial point is to find a submanifold
\Gamma
on each of
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime},\cdots
whose
boundary is given by
\Sigma_{t=+\infty}-\Sigma_{t=-\infty}
as in (
3
).
If one could find such a
\Gamma
, one would use Stokes theorem to show the charge conservation.
However, as we will show explicitly in Sec.
4
,
there exists a manifold
X^{\prime}
in which we cannot find
any such
\Gamma
. In other words,
\Sigma_{t=-\infty}
and
\Sigma_{t=+\infty}

are topologically distinct on that manifold
X^{\prime}
. In such a configuration,
there is no reason at all that
Q(\Sigma_{t=-\infty})
and
Q(\Sigma_{t=+\infty})
should be the same.
This is the general reason that a charge is not expected to be conserved in quantum gravity.
We demonstrate it more explicitly in Sec.
4
.

The above argument assumes that the relevant manifold
X^{\prime}
contributes to the path integral.
In quantum gravity, it is a nontrivial question whether a certain gravitational configuration contributes to
the path integral or not. In particular, we will not show (except for a simple example) that
X^{\prime}
satisfies the classical equations of motion.
We have two comments on this point.
First, the results on JT gravity in
Saad:2019lba
encourages us to consider
gravitational contributions even if they do not satisfy equations of motion, that is, they are not saddle points of the path integral.
Next, our configuration is motivated by some brane considerations in string theory.
We believe that we should sum over brane instantons in the Euclidean path integral.
Our gravitational configuration is motivated by branes under brane/black-brane
correspondence which have led to the discovery of AdS/CFT correspondence; see
Aharony:1999ti
for a review.
Thus, it is reasonable to think that our configuration is relevant for the path integral.

3
Virtual black branes as surgery

Virtual effects of black branes may be incorporated in the Euclidean path integral as
surgery on spacetime manifolds. Let us first discuss a simple case before explaining surgery in general.

Let us recall Hawking-Page transition
Hawking:1982dh
. (See also
Witten:1998zw
.)
If we only care about the topology, Hawking-Page transition may be described as follows.
We consider a spacetime with topology
S^{1}\times D^{D-1}
, where
D^{k}
is a
k
-dimensional disk.
The boundary of this manifold is
S^{1}\times S^{D-2}
. Now there is a manifold

D^{2}\times S^{D-2}
whose boundary is the same as
S^{1}\times D^{D-1}
.
The interpretation of it in gravitational theories is as follows. In the geometry
S^{1}\times D^{D-1}
,
suppose that we put a black hole at the center
0\in D^{D-1}
. The world line of the black hole, regarded as a point particle,
wraps
S^{1}\times\{0\}
.
The black hole wrapping
S^{1}\times\{0\}\subset S^{1}\times D^{D-1}
is equivalent to the geometry
D^{2}\times S^{D-2}
.
This is the topological interpretation of Hawking-Page transition.
In AdS/CFT, it is important to include both contributions to the path integral
to reproduce confinement/deconfinement phases of CFT
Witten:1998zw
.

We expect that a natural generalization of the above construction
to a black brane with worldvolume dimension
p
is as follows.
Let us consider a spacetime
S^{p}\times D^{D-p}
, and put a brane on
S^{p}\times\{0\}\subset S^{p}\times D^{D-p}
.
Then it is equivalent to a smooth geometry
D^{p+1}\times S^{D-p-1}
.
Both manifolds have the same boundary
S^{p}\times S^{D-p-1}
.
In fact, this kind of transition is really realized in string theory in the context of
deformed/resolved conifolds
Gopakumar:1998ki
;
Ooguri:2002gx
;
Klebanov:2000hb
;
Maldacena:2000yy
;
Vafa:2000wi
.
There, we put D-branes or NS5-branes on
S^{p}\times\{0\}\subset S^{p}\times D^{6-p}
(times
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
),
and by AdS/CFT duality we get a smooth geometry on
D^{p+1}\times S^{5-p}
(times
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
),
where
p=2\text{~or~}3
.

More generally, when a CFT is put on
S^{p}
, AdS/CFT itself may be regarded topologically as a duality between

S^{p}\times D^{D-p}
with branes wrapped on
S^{p}\times\{0\}
and a smooth geometry
D^{p+1}\times S^{D-p-1}
,
where
D^{p+1}
is a Euclidean AdS (i.e. a hyperbolic space).
4
4

4

I would like to thank Yuji Tachikawa
for pointing out this point of view.

For example, we may consider M5-branes wrapping
S^{6}
and then its gravity dual
{\rm AdS}_{7}\times S^{4}
is topologically

D^{p+1}\times S^{D-p-1}
for
p=6
and
D=11
.

On a manifold
X
of general topology, the above transition is described by surgery as follows.
In this paper we assume for simplicity that we have a submanifold
S^{p}\subset X
whose normal bundle
is topologically trivial and hence we can take a tubular neighborhood of
S^{p}
as
S^{p}\times D^{D-p}\subset X
.
We wrap a black brane on
S^{p}\times\{0\}\subset S^{p}\times D^{D-p}\subset X
.
Then it is equivalent to a new geometry
X^{\prime}
in which
S^{p}\times D^{D-p}
is replaced by
D^{p+1}\times S^{D-p-1}

(up to orientation which we mention later).
This is possible since both
S^{p}\times D^{D-p}
and
D^{p+1}\times S^{D-p-1}
have the same boundary
S^{p}\times S^{D-p-1}
.
Mathematically, this process of obtaining a new manifold
X^{\prime}
from the old one
X
is called surgery.
This is done by a local operation in a neighborhood of
S^{p}
in
X
.

4.1
The theory and
p
-form global symmetry

We consider the following theory of gravity coupled to a
p
-form gauge field
A
and possibly other fields.
The Euclidean action
I
(which appears in the path integral as
\exp(I)
) is

\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int{\rm d}^{D}x\sqrt{g}R-\frac{2\pi}{2e^{2}}\int F\wedge\star F+\cdots,

(7)

where
\star
is the Hodge star,
F={\rm d}A
is the field strength,
D
is the spacetime dimension, and
G
,
e
are parameters.
The ellipsis denote possible other matter fields and interactions.

The gauge field
A
is normalized as follows. A
p
-dimensional object which has charge
{\mathsf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}

under this gauge field has a term in its action given by

\displaystyle 2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int A.

(8)

Then the standard Dirac quantization condition is that
\int F\in{\mathbb{Z}}
on
(p+1)
-cycles.

We call an object which is charged under
A
as an electric brane. We will consider the case that it is a black brane,
but for the present discussion we need not assume it.
Suppose that an electric brane is placed at
\{0\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{D}
.
The Euclidean action contains terms

\displaystyle I\supset-\frac{2\pi}{2e^{2}}\int F\wedge\star F+2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int\delta({z})\wedge A.

(9)

where
{z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{D-p}
is the coordinates of
{\mathbb{R}}^{D-p}
which is transverse to the brane,
and
\delta({z})
is the delta function
(D-p)
-form localized on
{z}=0
.
The equation of motion is

\displaystyle(-1)^{p}{\rm d}\left(e^{-2}\star F\right)+{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}(-1)^{p(D-p)}\delta({z})=0

(10)

We define the electromagnetic dual of
F
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{i}}(-1)^{Dp}e^{-2}\star F.

(11)

The factor of the imaginary unit
{\mathsf{i}}
is an artifact due to the fact that we are working in Euclidean signature metric
rather than Lorentzian.
Then, from the above equation we get

\displaystyle{\rm d}\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\delta({z})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int_{S^{D-p-1}}\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}},

(12)

where
S^{D-p-1}
is a sphere surrounding the electric brane.
This means that the electric brane is a magnetic source of the dual field
\widetilde{F}
.

The duality between
F
and
\widetilde{F}
can be achieved by the following action

\displaystyle I(A,\widetilde{F})=2\pi\int\left({\mathsf{i}}(-1)^{D-p-1}\widetilde{F}\wedge{\rm d}A-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\widetilde{F}\wedge\star\widetilde{F}\right).

(13)

Here we have taken
A
and
\widetilde{F}
as independent variables. If we integrate over
\widetilde{F}
,
we get back to the original action of
A
given above with
e^{2}\star\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{i}}(-1)^{D-p-1}F
which is equivalent to (
11
).
On the other hand, if we integrate over
A
, we get a dual action for
\widetilde{F}={\rm d}\widetilde{A}

by the following argument. First, the integration over topologically trivial
A
gives
{\rm d}\widetilde{F}=0
, which is the Bianchi identity.
Next, the sum over nontrivial fluxes of
F={\rm d}A
sets the fluxes of
\widetilde{F}
to be integer-valued.
It can be shown by the formula
\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}e^{2\pi{\mathsf{i}}nx}=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta(x-m)
and Poincare duality theorem.
Therefore, after integration over
A
,
\widetilde{F}
is described by a dual
(D-p-2)
-form gauge field
\widetilde{A}
as
{\rm d}\widetilde{A}=\widetilde{F}
.
See
Witten:1995gf
for more detailed discussions
(which was done in the case of
D=4,p=1
but can be generalized).

The theory has two higher form symmetries
Gaiotto:2014kfa
.
They are related by duality
F\leftrightarrow\widetilde{F}
so we only discuss one of them.
The action of
A
is invariant under a shift

\displaystyle A\to A+\eta

(14)

where
\eta
is a closed
p
-form,
{\rm d}\eta=0
.
This is the
p
-form symmetry which is possessed by
A
.
A gauge transformation (rather than a global symmetry transformation) is given by
A\to A+\eta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
where
\eta_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a closed
p
-form whose integrals on
p
-cycles are integers.
Thus the actual global symmetry transformations are characterized by elements of
H^{p}(X;{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})
.
The purpose of this section is to see that this symmetry is broken by the topological reason discussed in Sec.
2

even if the Lagrangian does not contain any explicit breaking term.

4.2
Brane instanton and spacetime surgery

We make the following set of assumptions on the spacetime geometry
X
.
First, we assume that the theory is compactifed on a closed manifold
M
of dimension
D-d
so that the
topology of spacetime is

\displaystyle X={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M.

(15)

We assume that
M
contains a nontrivial
p
-dimensional cycle
C
which is topologically
S^{p}
,

C\cong S^{p}
. The cycle
C
is assumed to generate a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
factor in the homology group
H_{p}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Let
NC
be the normal bundle to
C
in
M
, and let
\underline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
be the trivial rank
d
bundle
which is the tangent bundle of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
above.
We assume that
NC\oplus\underline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
is topologically trivial on
C
.
We remark that this triviality is required only as a real vector bundle as opposed to a complex vector bundle.
For example, let us consider a Calabi-Yau manifold
M
which contains a complex submanifold
C\cong\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.
The Calabi-Yau condition requires that the normal bundle
NC
as a complex bundle has the first Chern class
\int_{C}c_{1}(NC)=-2
,
and hence it is nontrivial as a complex vector bundle. However, if we view
NC
as a real vector bundle by forgetting its complex structure,
its second Stiefel-Whitney class is zero,
\int_{C}w_{2}(NC)=-2=0\mod 2
. The second Stiefel-Whitney class is the only obstruction
for a real vector bundle of rank
r>2
to be trivialized on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\cong S^{2}
, and hence the normal bundle is trivial
if
r>2
. This is because of the fact that the transition function between the northern hemisphere and the southern hemisphere
is classified by
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{SO}(r))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
In more general case with
C\cong S^{p}
, the classification is
\pi_{p-1}(\mathrm{SO}(r))
(in orientable case).
The relevant rank
r
in this discussion is that of
NC\oplus\underline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
,
which is
r=D-p
.

Of course, on generic manifolds we can easily find cycles
C
which do not satisfy the above assumptions.
Sometimes a nontrivial element of
H_{p}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
cannot even be represented by a submanifold.
Normal bundles can also have nontrivial topology.
We leave it as a future work to understand those more general cases.

By de Rham theorem, there is a closed
p
-form
\omega
which we take as a harmonic form such that
\int_{C}\omega=1
.
More precisely, we may fix a basis of
H_{p}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
and require
\int\omega=0
for the basis elements other than
C
.
The low energy theory on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
contains a scalar field
\phi
, an “axion”, as

\displaystyle A=\phi\omega~~\Longrightarrow~~\phi=\int_{C}A.

(16)

By gauge transformations, it has periodicity
\phi\sim\phi+1
.
The axion field also has the shift symmetry
\phi\to\phi+\alpha
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}

which comes from the
p
-form symmetry (
14
) by taking
\eta=\alpha\omega
.

Suppose for a moment that there exist “elementary” (i.e. not black) branes in the theory.
On the Euclidean spacetime
X={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M
, we can consider a brane instanton of charge
{\mathsf{q}}
by wrapping an electric brane on

\{x\}\times C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M=X
.
Its exponentiated action is proportional to
\exp(2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int_{C}A)=\exp(2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\phi)
. Therefore, the low energy
effective field theory on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
contains a term in the effective action which is proportional to

\displaystyle I_{\rm eff}\propto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\rm d}^{d}x(e^{2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\phi}+\text{c.c.}).

(17)

In particular, it explicitly breaks the shift symmetry
\phi\to\phi+\alpha~(\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}})
which was present at the classical level.
The only remaining symmetry is
\phi\to\phi+1
, which is a gauge transformation and should not be broken.

Our purpose here is to see that we can produce the effective action
I_{\rm eff}
by purely geometric effects
without considering the electric brane as an “elementary object”.
This is expected to be possible by the brane/black-brane correspondence and the idea that a black brane in Euclidean signature space
is realized by surgery, as discussed in Sec.
3
.
At least at the topological level, we argue that it is indeed possible under the assumptions on
C
made above.

Let us define

\displaystyle C_{x}=\{x\}\times C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M=X.

(18)

where
x
is an arbitrary point on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
These are the cycles on which the brane instanton would wrap in the above discussion (which is then integrated over
x
).
The normal bundle to
C_{x}
in
X
is
\underline{{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\oplus NC
which
we have assumed to be topologically trivial. We fix one trivialization.
Then we can take a tubular neighborhood
T_{x}
of
C_{x}
which is topologically
T_{x}\cong D^{D-p}\times S^{p}
.
The submanifold
C_{x}
lies at the origin of
D^{D-p}
.

Now we perform surgery which is sketched in Sec.
3
.
First we notice that
T_{x}
has a boundary which is topologically
\partial T_{x}\cong S^{D-p-1}\times S^{p}
.
This is the same as the boundary of another manifold
S^{D-p-1}\times D^{p+1}
,
up to an orientation change by
(-1)^{D-p-1}
since
\partial(S^{D-p-1}\times D^{p+1})=(-1)^{\dim S^{D-p-1}}S^{D-p-1}\times\partial D^{p+1}
.
We take a copy of
S^{D-p-1}\times D^{p+1}
with the orientation change mentioned above,
and denote it as
T^{\prime}_{x}
. Notice that
\partial T_{x}\cong\partial T^{\prime}_{x}
.
Surgery is performed by removing
T_{x}
from
X
and then gluing
T^{\prime}_{x}
.
This makes
X
to another manifold
X^{\prime}
.

Notice that the original cycle
C\cong S^{p}
is now trivialized near the point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by the surgery
because we “filled the inside of
S^{p}
by
D^{p+1}
”.
Also notice that we have created a new nontrivial cycle
S^{D-p-1}
.
On this new cycle, we put an electric flux

\displaystyle\int_{S^{D-p-1}}\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(19)

Compare it with (
12
).
We claim that this is the black-brane version of the above electric brane instanton.
The new spacetime
X^{\prime}
is completely smooth and it consists of purely the metric
g
and the gauge field
(A,\widetilde{F})
.

Let us study some topological structure of the above flux (
19
) on the entire manifold
X^{\prime}
.
We set

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\eta

(20)

so that
\int_{S^{D-p-1}}\eta=1
.
Topologically, the flux given by
\eta
is equivalent to a delta function
\eta\sim\delta(y-y_{0})
localized on an arbitrary point
y_{0}\in S^{D-p-1}
.
In other words, a representative of the Poincare dual of
\eta
within the local region
T^{\prime}_{x}\cong S^{D-p-1}\times D^{p+1}

is given by
\{y_{0}\}\times D^{p+1}\subset T^{\prime}_{x}
. The Poincare dual is intuitively regarded as a flux tube of
\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\eta
.

Its boundary in

The only point here is that the flux can escape to infinity.

We conclude that topologically

with the following properties.
It is topologically a disk
D^{p+1}

and the boundary is

\displaystyle\partial L=\{\infty\}\times C\cong S^{p},

(21)

where
\infty\in S^{d-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Here
S^{d-1}
is the boundary at infinity.
This

represents an element of the relative homology group
H_{p+1}(X^{\prime},\partial X^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})

where
\partial X^{\prime}=\partial X=S^{d-1}\times M

Poincare duality is the isomorphism
H_{p+1}(X^{\prime},\partial X^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H^{D-p-1}(X^{\prime};{\mathbb{Z}})
under which

L
and
\eta
correspond to each other.

Now we can see how the geometry generates the term proportional to
e^{2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\phi}
in the low energy effective action on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
First we notice that the original cycle
C
is now trivialized near
x
as remarked above by “filling
S^{p}
by
D^{p+1}
” at the point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then the field
A
cannot be
\phi\omega
everywhere on
X^{\prime}
. We replace it by a configuration
A_{\phi}

which goes to
\phi\omega
at infinity
S^{d-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and smoothly goes to zero in the region near
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
where we have performed the surgery. We assume that
\phi
is constant in the neighborhood of the boundary at infinity
S^{d-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then the field strength
F_{\phi}={\rm d}A_{\phi}
is zero near
S^{d-1}
, and
it gives an element of cohomology with compact support,
H^{p+1}(X^{\prime},\partial X^{\prime};{\mathbb{R}})
.

We use the action (
13
) in which
A
and
\widetilde{F}
are regarded as independent variables,
and consider the contribution to the path integral coming from the configuration

\displaystyle A=A_{\phi},\qquad\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\eta.

(22)

The relevant part of the action is

\displaystyle I\supset 2\pi\int\left({\mathsf{i}}(-1)^{D-p-1}\widetilde{F}\wedge{\rm d}A-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\widetilde{F}\wedge\star\widetilde{F}\right).

(23)

The second term produces a real value for the action.
The first term is the crucial part for our purposes and it is computed as follows. We have seen that
\eta
is Poincare-dual to the cycle
L
.
After carefully examining the orientation, one gets

6
6

6

To check the sign, let us compute it on
T^{\prime}_{x}
. We remarked earlier that it is
S^{D-p-1}\times D^{p+1}
up to an orientation change by
(-1)^{D-p-1}
.
We also have
\eta=\delta(y-y_{0})
on
S^{D-p-1}
. Thus

\displaystyle 2\pi{\mathsf{i}}(-1)^{(D-p-1)}\int_{T^{\prime}_{x}}{\mathsf{q}}\eta\wedge F_{\phi}=2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int_{S^{D-p-1}\times D^{p+1}}\delta(y-y_{0})\wedge F_{\phi}=2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int_{D^{p+1}}F_{\phi}.

(24)

This computation makes sure that the sign in (

\displaystyle 2\pi{\mathsf{i}}(-1)^{(D-p-1)}\int_{X^{\prime}}{\mathsf{q}}\eta\wedge F_{\phi}=2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int_{L}F_{\phi}=2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int_{\partial L}A_{\phi}=2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\phi,

(25)

We have also implicitly used the fact that

and computed

Therefore, we have reproduced the phase factor

It is a nontrivial question whether the configuration discussed above really contributes to the gravitational path integral.
We regard the brane/black-brane correspondence

4.3
Topological obstruction to charge conservation

On the manifold
X
with topology
X={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M
,
the charge
Q(\Sigma_{t})=\int_{\{t\}\times\Sigma}\star J
of the
p
-form symmetry current
J
is conserved
as discussed in Sec.
2
.
In the case discussed in this section,
the
p
-form symmetry current is
\star J=\widetilde{F}
.
For
\Sigma
, we may take

\displaystyle\Sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C},

(26)

where
\widetilde{C}
is a submanifold on
M
which is Poincare-dual to
\omega
on
M
.
In particular the intersection number between
C
and
\widetilde{C}
is 1 up to sign,
since Poincare-duality implies
\int_{C\cap\widetilde{C}}1=\int_{C}\omega=1
.

{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
is a “time slice” on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
though we are working with Euclidean signature metric.

In quantum gravity we may take a sum over geometries with different topologies,
and the partition function or transition amplitude may be given as

\displaystyle{\cal Z}={\cal Z}(X)+{\cal Z}(X^{\prime})+\cdots.

(27)

We claimed in Sec.
2
that for some
X^{\prime}
, there is no
\Gamma

such that
\partial\Gamma=\Sigma_{t=+\infty}-\Sigma_{t=-\infty}
.
Here we prove the non-existence of
\Gamma

for the
X^{\prime}
which is obtained from
X={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M
by the surgery
as constructed above.

Before proving it, let us make one remark.
In the conservation argument given in Sec.
2
, we have implicitly neglected spatial infinity on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
.
More precisely, including the spatial infinity, we replace
\Sigma_{t=+\infty}-\Sigma_{t=-\infty}
by

S^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C}
, where
S^{d-1}
is the sphere at infinity on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then the question is whether we can find a submanifold
\Gamma
such that
\partial\Gamma=S^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C}
.

Recall that
\widetilde{C}
is defined to be the Poincare dual of
\omega
.
Let
A_{\phi=1}
be a
p
-form, defined in a similar way as
A_{\phi}
, which is
\omega
at the spatial infinity
and goes to zero in the region near
T^{\prime}_{x}
.
Then we see that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C}}\eta=\int_{S^{d-1}\times M}\eta\wedge\omega=(-1)^{D-p-1}\int_{X^{\prime}}\eta\wedge{\rm d}A_{\phi=1}=1,

(28)

where we have used (
25
) with
\phi
set to 1.
If there were
\Gamma
such that
\partial\Gamma=S^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C}
, we would get

\int_{S^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C}}\eta=\int_{\Gamma}{\rm d}\eta=0
,
a contradiction. Therefore, there is no such
\Gamma
.

The above argument shows that the flux
\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}\eta
is precisely the obstruction to the existence of
\Gamma
on
X^{\prime}
such that

\partial\Gamma=S^{d-1}\times\widetilde{C}
.
The flux
\widetilde{F}
have played an important role in the generation of the phase
2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\phi
in the above discussions,
and it also gives an obstruction to the existence of
\Gamma
.
We conclude that
Q(\Sigma_{t=-\infty})
and
Q(\Sigma_{t=+\infty})
need not be the same on
X^{\prime}
,
and the charge conservation is violated in the sum (
27
).

4.4
Example

There is a simple example which is actually a saddle point of the path integral at the classical level.
7
7

7

This example is subtle.
We neglect possible quantum mechanical instabilities, such as the one discussed in
Witten:1981gj
.
In fact, our configuration is similar to that of
Witten:1981gj
,
but we want to interpret it as a loop of a “virtual charged particle” as discussed later. Also,
it is not so certain how to think about it since it can also happen in string theory
GarciaEtxebarria:2020xsr
.
We leave it as a future work to better understand it.

Let us discuss this example.
Similar discussions on global symmetry violation have also been given in Sec. 4.3 of
Montero:2017yja
.

We consider
D=4
,
p=1
,
d=3
and
M=C=S^{1}
. Thus the manifold
X
is
X={\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times S^{1}
where
S^{1}
is the internal manifold in the compactification.
In this situation, we can consider the Euclidean version of the usual Reissner-Nordström black hole
going around
S^{1}
. Only the interpretation is different. Usually,
S^{1}
is considered as
a Wick-rotated time direction and the black hole solution is considered to represent the thermodynamic state
of the black hole. Instead of that interpretation, in our context, we interpret
S^{1}
as one of the space directions,
and the time direction is included in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. We sum over geometries
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}.\cdots,

with no virtual black hole, one virtual black hole, two virtual black holes etc. in the path integral.
The shift symmetry of the axion field
\phi=\int_{S^{1}}A
is violated.

More explicitly, the situation is as follows. First let us describe the topology.
By considering a “sphere at infinity”
S^{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
as the boundary of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we may regard
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
topologically as a three-dimensional disk
D^{3}
.
Thus we regard
X={\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times S^{1}
topologically as
X\cong D^{3}\times S^{1}
. By performing surgery near
\{0\}\times S^{1}\subset D^{3}\times S^{1}
,
we get
X^{\prime}\cong S^{2}\times D^{2}
. Both
X
and
X^{\prime}
have the same asymptotic boundaries
\partial(D^{3}\times S^{1})\cong\partial(S^{2}\times D^{2})\cong S^{2}\times S^{1}
.
We also put a flux
\int_{S^{2}}\widetilde{F}={\mathsf{q}}
on the
S^{2}
of
X^{\prime}\cong S^{2}\times D^{2}
. This construction of
X^{\prime}
is an explicit example of the general construction described in the previous subsections.

The geometry of
X^{\prime}
is that of the Reissner-Nordström black hole and is given by

\displaystyle{\rm d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})}{r^{2}}{\rm d}\tau^{2}+\frac{r^{2}}{(r-r_{+})(r-r_{-})}{\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\phi^{2}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathsf{q}}}{4\pi}\sin\theta{\rm d}\theta\wedge{\rm d}\phi.

(29)

The parameters
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
are given in terms of the radius
R
of
S^{1}=\{\tau;\,\tau\sim\tau+2\pi R\}
as

\displaystyle R=\frac{2r_{+}^{2}}{r_{+}-r_{-}},\qquad r_{+}r_{-}=\frac{1}{2}Ge^{2}{\mathsf{q}}^{2}.

(30)

The first equation is required by the condition that
(r,\tau)
form a smooth disk
D^{2}
in the region
r\geq r_{+}
,
where
r=r_{+}
is the center
\{0\}
of
D^{2}
.
The second one comes from Einstein equations.

The coordinates
(r,\tau)
give the disk
D^{2}
of
X^{\prime}\cong S^{2}\times D^{2}
. More precisely,
(\sqrt{r-r_{+}},\tau/R)
are the polar coordinates of it.
The coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
give the sphere
S^{2}
of
X^{\prime}\cong S^{2}\times D^{2}
.
In the region
r\to\infty
, both
X
and
X^{\prime}
have the same asymptotic regions described by the metric
{\rm d}s^{2}\to{\rm d}\tau^{2}+{\rm d}r^{2}+r^{2}({\rm d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta{\rm d}\phi^{2})
.
In this asymptotic region, we may take Euclidean coordinates

\displaystyle(t,x,y,\tau)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta\cos\phi,r\sin\theta\sin\phi,\tau)

(31)

and we may regard
t
as the (Euclidean) time coordinate. The cycles
\Sigma_{t=\pm\infty}
on which we define initial and final charges are taken to be

\Sigma_{t=\pm\infty}=\{\pm\infty\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}
, where
\mathrm{pt}\in S^{1}
is a point on
S^{1}
. One can see that there
is no
\Gamma
on
X^{\prime}
such that
\partial\Gamma=\Sigma_{t=+\infty}-\Sigma_{t=\pm\infty}

(or more precisely
\partial\Gamma=S^{2}\times\{\mathrm{pt}\}
where
S^{2}
is the sphere at infinity)
as was proved more generally in Sec.
4.3
.

To compute the gravitational action
I
of the above geometry, we borrow a formula from the thermodynamic interpretation
of the above configuration
Gibbons:1976ue
. If we interpret
M=S^{1}
as the Euclidean time direction, the action
I

is proportional to the free energy of the black hole,

\displaystyle\frac{I}{2\pi R}=-E+TS+{\mathsf{q}}\mu,

(32)

where the energy
E
, the temperature
T
, the entropy
S
and the chemical potential
\mu
are given by

\displaystyle E=\frac{(r_{+}+r_{-})}{2G},\qquad T=\frac{1}{2\pi R},\qquad S=\frac{4\pi r_{+}^{2}}{4G},\qquad\mu=\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{R}\phi

(33)

Therefore, the gravitational path integral is proportional to

\displaystyle\exp(I)=e^{S}\exp(-2\pi RE+2\pi i{\mathsf{q}}\phi).

(34)

The amount of the shift symmetry breaking of
\phi=\int_{S^{1}}A
is proportional to this quantity.

In our case,
M=S^{1}
is not a Euclidean time direction, but one of the spatial directions with a fixed radius
R
.
For large enough
R\gg\sqrt{Ge^{2}{\mathsf{q}}^{2}}
, there are two solutions to (
30
), and one of them is given by

r_{+}\simeq r_{-}\simeq\sqrt{Ge^{2}{\mathsf{q}}^{2}/2}
and
r_{+}-r_{-}\simeq Ge^{2}{\mathsf{q}}^{2}/R
which is near extremal.
For this solution, the formula (
34
) may be interpreted as follows.
Let us regard the black hole as a particle.
The usual action for a relativistic particle of mass
m
and charge
{\mathsf{q}}
is

\displaystyle I_{\rm particle}=-m\int{\rm d}s+2\pi{\mathsf{i}}{\mathsf{q}}\int A.

(35)

When a virtual particle goes around the
M=S^{1}
, the first term gives
-2\pi Rm
with
m=E
, where
\int{\rm d}s=2\pi R
,
and the second term gives
2\pi i{\mathsf{q}}\phi
.
Moreover, the particle has internal states, and the number of states which contribute to the virtual particle loop around
M=S^{1}

is of order
e^{S}
.
8
8

8

We remark that the total number of particle states can be infinity.
Only the number of states which give significant contributions to the path integral is of order
e^{S}
.
For example, states whose energy
E^{\prime}
is significantly larger than the averaged energy
E

do not contribute if
E^{\prime}-E\gg R^{-1}
since their contributions are exponentially suppressed.

This virtual particle interpretation gives the action (
34
).

5
Discussions

5.1
Brief summary of the case
p>0

In this paper we have discussed that global symmetry charges are not expected to be conserved in quantum gravity.
We sum over topologies

\displaystyle X+X^{\prime}+X^{\prime\prime}+\cdots.

(36)

For definiteness, we require that their boundary manifolds are topologically the same,

\displaystyle\partial X=\partial X^{\prime}=\partial X^{\prime\prime}=\cdots.

(37)

In particular, the initial and final states are assumed to have the common topology in all
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}
and so on.
9
9

9

This assumption is not really a requirement of quantum gravity,
but is made to clarify our charge violation mechanism.

The standard proof of global charge conservation is done by using the Stokes theorem.
Namely, we take a cycle
\Sigma_{t=\pm\infty}
at the initial time
t=-\infty
and the final time
t=+\infty
,
and define the global charges at
t=\pm\infty
as
Q(\Sigma_{\pm\infty})=\int_{\{t=\pm\infty\}\times\Sigma}\star J
.
Then we can show that
Q(\Sigma_{+\infty})-Q(\Sigma_{-\infty})=\int_{\Gamma}{\rm d}(\star J)=0
if there is some appropriate
\Gamma

such that
\partial\Gamma=\Sigma_{+\infty}-\Sigma_{-\infty}
.

We have pointed out that the above proof fails in quantum gravity in which we take a sum as in (
36
).
By our simplifying assumption (
37
)
that all
X,X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}
and so on have the common initial and final state topology, the cycles
\Sigma_{t=\pm\infty}
are well-defined.
However, in some
X^{\prime}
, we fail to find a
\Gamma
with the above property.
Thus, from the beginning, there was no proof of charge conservation.

In the case of
0
-form symmetries, the above mechanism involves some conceptual issues such as two disjoint universes as we discuss later.
However, the charge violation is very clear in the case of higher form global symmetries which are compactified to 0-form symmetries.

We consider a
D
-dimensional theory with a
p
-form global symmetry, and compactify the theory down to
d
-dimensions as

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M
, where
M
is an internal manifold. Explicitly, we have studied the case of a
p
-form gauge field which has a
p
-form shift symmetry.
Depending on the topology of
M
, we get a 0-form axion-like field with a shift symmetry in the low energy effective
theory on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
In quantum gravity, the shift symmetry is broken and in generic situations (without supersymmetry etc. so that there is no fermion zero modes),
the axion-like field gets a potential energy.

We emphasize that the generation of the potential energy of the axion-like field
is a clear physical effect, irrespective of conceptual interpretations of our results:

1.

In the case of quantum field theory in which we do not sum over topologies,
the axion-like field is a Goldstone boson in the low energy theory on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
in particular it is exactly massless. This is due to the shift symmetry which is ensured by the higher dimensional
p
-form symmetry.

2.

In the case of quantum gravity in which we sum over some relevant topologies, the axion-like field is no longer massless in generic situations (unless
it is forced to be massless by other reasons such as supersymmetry.) In any case, the shift symmetry is broken in the low energy theory on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

There is no conceptual issue in these statements, since e.g. the mass of the axion-like field is a physical observable.
We can even give an order estimate of the size of the shift symmetry breaking as in the example in Sec.
4.4
.
We have produced the mass term (or more general shift symmetry breaking terms) without introducing any explicit breaking term in the UV Lagrangian
of the higher dimensional theory. This is a clear difference from the case of quantum field theory.

5.2
Speculations on the case
p=0

The construction described in Sec.
4
requires
a cycle
C
which is topologically
S^{p}
, and represents a nontrivial
element of the homology
H_{p}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
There is a qualitative difference between the cases
p>0
and
p=0
.

For
p>0
, a sphere
S^{p}
is connected. On the other hand, for
p=0

a sphere
S^{0}
consists of two points. Moreover, the fact that
C\cong S^{0}
needs to be a
nontrivial element of
H_{0}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
implies that
M
needs to have at least two components,

M=M_{1}\sqcup M_{2}
, where
\sqcup
means disjoint union.
One point of
S^{0}
is on
M_{1}
, and the other is on
M_{2}
.
Thus we can think of
X_{1}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M_{1}
as “one universe” and
X_{2}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times M_{2}
as “another universe”,
and they are disconnected from each other.
Then our virtual black brane for
p=0
is exactly the Euclidean axionic wormhole
Giddings:1987cg
,
and in the current case it is topologically the connected sum
X_{1}\#X_{2}
of the two universes.
Such wormholes are argued to violate usual (i.e. 0-form) global symmetries
Coleman:1988cy
;
Giddings:1988cx
.
In our situation, the violation happens by a flow of global symmetry charge from one universe to the other through the wormhole.

In the case
p=0
, the wormhole poses conceptual questions about its interpretation.
In particular,
Maldacena:2004rf
;
ArkaniHamed:2007js
(see also
Betzios:2019rds
) have presented a sharp problem in AdS/CFT
by constructing wormholes in AdS where there is a single CFT dual.
The problem becomes even more severe in the light of the discovery that
off-shell configurations which are not saddle points can contribute to the path integral
Saad:2019lba
.

From the point of view of the brane/black-brane correspondence,
the Euclidean wormhole may be considered to be equivalent to
two
(-1)
-branes, or more precisely one
(-1)
-brane and one anti-
(-1)
-brane, since
S^{0}
consists of two points.
Then we conjecture that there must be a
(-1)
-brane in a theory
which is really a single unitary theory in a single universe without random couplings.
A very related proposal has also been given in
Marolf:2020xie
;
McNamara:2020uza
from a different (but related) viewpoint.

The reason for the necessity of a
(-1)
-brane is as follows.
10
10

10

We may also call
(-1)
-branes as “half-wormholes”. See
Saad:2021rcu
for recent work on half-wormholes.

Most of the problems about Euclidean wormholes
are due to the fact that they connect two different points in spacetime in a nonlocal way.
However, let us replace a Euclidean wormhole by a pair of a
(-1)
-brane and an anti-
(-1)
-brane.
Then, there is no reason to only sum over configurations in which the numbers of
(-1)
-branes
and anti-
(-1)
-branes are the same. We can consider configurations with independent numbers

N_{+}
and
N_{-}
of
(-1)
-branes and anti-
(-1)
-branes, respectively, and sum over
N_{+}
and
N_{-}

independently. Then we do not get nonlocal interactions between two points.
A pair of a
(-1)
-brane and an anti-
(-1)
-brane
must induce the same symmetry violation as a Euclidean wormhole,
and hence
(-1)
-branes must transform nontrivially under the global symmetry and then lead to its breaking.

In fact, we can consider an analogous problem in non-gravitational theories.
Let us consider a four dimensional gauge theory like QCD on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.
We assume that the theory is higgsed at a high energy scale so that instanton gas approximation is valid.
If we only sum over configurations with total instanton number 0, we get a problem of nonlocal interactions.
For example, the leading nontrivial contribution comes from an instanton at a point
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}

and an anti-instanton at
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
so that the total instanton number is zero.
Then we get an effective nonlocal interaction of the form

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}^{4}x{\rm d}^{4}y\,O(x)\overline{O}(y)+\cdots

(38)

where
O(x)
is produced by an instanton (such as an ’t Hooft vertex
tHooft:1976rip
), and
\overline{O}(y)
is produced by
an anti-instanton. However, if we sum over all configurations of arbitrary instanton and anti-instanton numbers, we get

\displaystyle\exp\left(\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,O(x)\right)\exp\left(\int{\rm d}^{4}y\,\overline{O}(y)\right)=\exp\left(\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,[O(x)+\overline{O}(x)]\right).

(39)

This is the reason that we sum over all configurations of instantons on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
.
See
Weinberg:1996kr
for more discussions.
11
11

11

On the other hand, on a spacetime of compact topology rather than
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
,
it is possible to impose some constraints on instanton numbers; see e.g.
Seiberg:2010qd
;
Tanizaki:2019rbk
;
Pantev:2005rh
;
Pantev:2005zs
;
Pantev:2005wj
.
The difference between noncompact and compact cases is due to some degrees of freedom
which is frozen in noncompact spaces but is summed over in compact spaces. For example,
if a discrete 0-form
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
symmetry is spontaneously broken, we pick a single vacuum on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
to satisfy cluster decomposition,
but we sum over all vacua on noncompact spaces.

In this context, the analog of Coleman’s
\alpha
-parameters may be the
\theta
-angle associated to the instanton numbers.
See also
Ohmori:2021fms
for an interesting field theoretical analog of replica wormholes.

The existence of
(-1)

due to more detailed problems beyond topology, such as the numerical value of the action of the Euclidean wormhole

In the case of configurations studied in this paper with
p>0

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in a single universe.
Thus we do not get any nonlocal interactions in the low energy theory on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

This is the conceptual simplicity of our setup for
p>0
.
In fact, from the point of view of a low energy observer on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(-1)
-brane.
Thus we can see global symmetry violation in a clean way without difficult conceptual issues.

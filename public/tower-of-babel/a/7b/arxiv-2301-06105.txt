Abstract
In the Longest ( s , t ) (s,t) -Detour problem, we look for an ( s , t ) (s,t) -path that is at least k k vertices longer than a shortest one. We study the parameterized complexity of Longest ( s , t ) (s,t) -Detour when parameterized by k k : this falls into the research paradigm of ‘parameterization above guarantee’. Whereas the problem is known to be fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) on undirected graphs, the status of Longest ( s , t ) (s,t) -Detour on directed graphs remains highly unclear: it is not even known to be solvable in polynomial time for k = 1 k=1 . Recently, Fomin et al. made progress in this direction by showing that the problem is FPT on every class of directed graphs where the 3-Disjoint Paths problem is solvable in polynomial time. We improve upon their result by weakening this assumption: we show that only a polynomial-time algorithm for 2-Disjoint Paths is required. What is more, our approach yields an arguably simpler proof.

Introduction

In the

A recent trend in parameterized complexity is to consider above guarantee versions of

and its variants where the parameter is the difference between

and some guarantee. Hence, the length of the sought path could be potentially large while the parameter is small.
One such example is the

(s,t)
-Detour

and

of

contains a path from

to

to

Let

be the class of directed graphs where
2
-Disjoint Paths
problem is polynomial-time solvable.
We show that the

(s,t)
-Detour

.

The

(s,t)
-Detour

[

]

-Detour

3
-Disjoint Paths
problem is polynomial-time solvable. Thus, we improve the requirement of solvability of
3
-Disjoint Paths
to the solvability of
2
-Disjoint Paths

,

3.3
Running Time

Lemma 7
.

Algorithm
1
runs in
46.308^{k}n^{{\mathcal{O}}(1)}
time.

Proof.

Step 1 of the algorithm can be done by a BFS, which takes
{\mathcal{O}}(n+m)
time. In Steps 3-5, we use Theorem
3
, which takes

\displaystyle{4k\choose k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4k!}{k!(4k-k)!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{1/12(4k)}\sqrt{2\pi(4k)}((4k)/e)^{4k}}{\sqrt{2\pi k}(k/e)^{k}\sqrt{2\pi(3k)}(3k)/e)^{3k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{256}{27}\right)^{k}

Abstract
. Given two labelled Markov decision processes (MDPs), the trace-refinement problem asks whether for all strategies of the first MDP there exists a strategy of the second MDP such that the induced labelled Markov chains are trace-equivalent. We show that this problem is decidable in polynomial time if the second MDP is a Markov chain. The algorithm is based on new results on a particular notion of bisimulation between distributions over the states. However, we show that the general trace-refinement problem is undecidable, even if the first MDP is a Markov chain. Decidability of those problems was stated as open in 2008. We further study the decidability and complexity of the trace-refinement problem provided that the strategies are restricted to be memoryless.

.

.

2.1.
Labelled Markov Decision Processes

In this article a
labelled Markov decision process
(MDP) is a generative probabilistic model with nondeterminism.
Formally, an MDP is a quadruple
\mathcal{D}=\langle Q,\mu_{0},{\sf L},\delta\rangle
,
consisting of a finite set
Q
of states, an initial distribution
\mu_{0}\in{\sf Dist}(Q)
,
a finite set
{\sf L}
of labels,
and a finite probabilistic transition relation
\delta\subseteq Q\times{\sf Dist}({\sf L}\times Q)
where
states are in relation with distributions over pairs of labels and successors.
We assume that for each state
q\in Q
there exists some distribution

d\in{\sf Dist}({\sf L}\times Q)
where
\langle q,d\rangle\in\delta
.
The set of
moves
in
q
is
{\sf moves}(q)=\{d\in{\sf Dist}({\sf L}\times Q)\mid\langle q,d\rangle\in\delta\}
;
denote by
{\sf moves}=\bigcup_{q\in Q}{\sf moves}(q)
the set of all moves.

For the complexity results, we assume that probabilities of transitions are rational and
given as fractions of integers represented in binary.

We describe the behaviour of an MDP as a trace generator running in steps.
The MDP starts in the first step in state
q
with probability
\mu_{0}(q)
.
In each step, if the MDP is in state
q

the controller chooses
{\sf m}\in{\sf moves}(q)
;
then, with probability
{\sf m}(a,q^{\prime})
, the label
a
is generated and the next step starts in the
successor state
q^{\prime}
.

Given
q\in Q
, denote by
{\sf post}(q)
the set

\{(a,q^{\prime})\in{\sf Supp}({\sf m})\mid{\sf m}\in{\sf moves}(q)\}
.
A
path
in
\mathcal{D}
is a sequence
\rho=q_{0}a_{1}q_{1}\dots a_{n}q_{n}

such that
(a_{i+1},q_{i+1})\in{\sf post}(q_{i})
for all
0\leq i<n
.
The last state of
\rho
is
{\sf last}(\rho)=q_{n}
.
The trace
{\sf trace}(\rho)
generated by
\rho
is
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}
.
We let
{\sf Paths}(\mathcal{D})
denote the set of paths in
\mathcal{D}
,

{\sf Paths}(w)
denote
\{\rho\in{\sf Paths}(\mathcal{D})\mid{\sf trace}(\rho)=w\}
the set of paths generating
w
,
and
{\sf Paths}(w,q)
denote
\{\rho\in{\sf Paths}(\mathcal{D})\mid{\sf trace}(\rho)=w\text{ and }{\sf last}(\rho)=q\}
the set of paths generating
w

ending in
q
.

Strategies.

A
strategy
for an MDP
\mathcal{D}
is a function

\alpha:{\sf Paths}(\mathcal{D})\to{\sf Dist}({\sf moves})
that, given a path
\rho
,
returns a probability distribution
\alpha(\rho)\in{\sf Dist}({\sf moves}({\sf last}(\rho)))
.
Let
q={\sf last}(\rho)
, then
\alpha(\rho)
generates a label
a
and selects a successor state
q^{\prime}

with probability

\sum_{{\sf m}\in{\sf moves}(q)}\alpha(\rho)({\sf m})\cdot{\sf m}(a,q^{\prime}).

Abusing notation slightly we write
\alpha(\rho)(a,q^{\prime})

for
\sum_{{\sf m}\in{\sf moves}}\alpha(\rho)({\sf m})\cdot{\sf m}(a,q^{\prime})
.

A strategy
\alpha
is
pure
if for all
\rho\in{\sf Paths}(\mathcal{D})
,
there exists
{\sf m}\in{\sf moves}
such that
\alpha(\rho)({\sf m})=1
;
we thus view pure strategies as functions
\alpha:{\sf Paths}(\mathcal{D})\to{\sf moves}
.
A strategy
\alpha
is
memoryless
if
\alpha(\rho)=\alpha(\rho^{\prime})

for all paths
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
with
{\sf last}(\rho)={\sf last}(\rho^{\prime})
;
we thus view memoryless strategies as functions
\alpha:Q\to{\sf Dist}({\sf moves})
.
A strategy
\alpha
is
trace-based
if
\alpha(\rho)=\alpha(\rho^{\prime})

for all
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
where
{\sf trace}(\rho)={\sf trace}(\rho^{\prime})
and
{\sf last}(\rho)={\sf last}(\rho^{\prime})
;
we view trace-based strategies as functions
\alpha:{\sf L}^{*}\times Q\to{\sf Dist}({\sf moves})
.
For a traced-based strategy
\alpha
we write
\alpha(w,q)(a,q^{\prime})

for
\sum_{{\sf m}\in{\sf moves}}\alpha(w,q)({\sf m})\cdot{\sf m}(a,q^{\prime})
.

Trace-probability function.

For an MDP
\mathcal{D}
and a strategy
\alpha
,
the probability of a single path is
inductively defined by
{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(q)=\mu_{0}(q)
and

{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(\rho aq^{\prime})={\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(\rho)\cdot\alpha(\rho)(a,q^{\prime}).

This induces

{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w,q)=\sum_{\rho\in{\sf Paths}(w,q)}{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(\rho)

and
{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w)=\sum_{\rho\in{\sf Paths}(w)}{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(\rho)
.

The

\mathit{Tr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}:{\sf L}^{*}\to[0,1]

\mathit{Tr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w)={\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w).

We may drop the subscript
\mathcal{D}
or

{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}

We let
\mathit{subDist}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w)\in{\sf subDist}(Q)
denote the subdistribution after generating a trace
w
, that is

\mathit{subDist}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w)(q)={\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w,q).

We have:

\mathit{Tr}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w)=\lVert\mathit{subDist}_{\mathcal{D},\alpha}(w)\rVert

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an MDP and
\alpha,\beta
be two strategies; we denote by

when the equality

\mathit{Tr}_{\alpha}(w)=\mathit{Tr}_{\beta}(w)

An extended path is a sequence

(a_{i+1},q_{i+1})\in{\sf post}(q_{i})

.
The last memory state is

.
All notions for paths are naturally transferred to extended paths.
For an extended path
\pi=q_{0}M_{0}a_{1}q_{1}M_{1}\dots a_{n}q_{n}M_{n}

\rho_{\pi}
denote

We let

{\sf ExtPaths}(\rho)

\rho
,

{\sf ExtPaths}(\rho,M)

\rho

Labelled Markov Chains.

A finite-state labelled Markov chain (MC for short) is an MDP where
only a single move is available in each state, and thus controller’s choice plays no role.
An MC
\mathcal{C}=\langle Q,\mu_{0},{\sf L},\delta\rangle
is an MDP where
\delta:Q\to{\sf Dist}({\sf L}\times Q)

is a probabilistic transition function.
Since MCs are MDPs, we analogously define paths, and the probability of a single path
inductively as follows:

{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(q)=\mu_{0}(q)
and
{\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(\rho aq)={\sf Pr}_{\mathcal{C}}(\rho)\cdot\delta(q^{\prime})(a,q)

where
q^{\prime}={\sf last}(\rho)
.
The notations
\mathit{subDist}_{\mathcal{C}}(w)
and
\mathit{Tr}_{\mathcal{C}}
are defined analogously.

2.2.
Trace Refinement

Given two MDPs
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
with the same set
{\sf L}
of labels, we say that

\mathcal{E}
refines
\mathcal{D}
, denoted by
\mathcal{D}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{E}
,
if for all strategies
\alpha
for
\mathcal{D}
there exists some strategy
\beta
for
\mathcal{E}
such that

\mathit{Tr}_{\mathcal{D}}=\mathit{Tr}_{\mathcal{E}}
.
We are interested in the problem
{\sf MDP\sqsubseteq MDP}
, which asks, for two given MDPs
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
, whether
\mathcal{D}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{E}
.
The decidability of this problem was posed as an open question in
[
7
]
.
We show in Theorem
3
that the problem
{\sf MDP\sqsubseteq MDP}
is undecidable.

We consider various subproblems of
{\sf MDP\sqsubseteq MDP}
, which asks whether
\mathcal{D}\sqsubseteq\mathcal{E}
holds.
Specifically, we speak of the problem

•

{\sf MDP\sqsubseteq MC}
when
\mathcal{E}
is restricted to be an MC;

•

{\sf MC\sqsubseteq MDP}
when
\mathcal{D}
is restricted to be an MC;

•

{\sf MC\sqsubseteq MC}
when both
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
are restricted to be MCs.

We show in Theorem
3
that even the problem
{\sf MC\sqsubseteq MDP}
is undecidable.
Hence we consider further subproblems.
Specifically, we denote by
{\sf MC\sqsubseteq_{\sf m}MDP}
the problem where the MDP is restricted to use only memoryless strategies,
and by
{\sf MC\sqsubseteq_{\sf pm}MDP}
the problem where the MDP is restricted to use only pure memoryless strategies.
When both MDPs
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{E}
are restricted to use only pure memoryless strategies, the trace-refinement problem is denoted by
{\sf MDP_{pm}\sqsubseteq_{\sf pm}MDP_{pm}}
.
The problem
{\sf MC\sqsubseteq MC}
equals the
trace-equivalence problem
for MCs: given two MCs
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
we have
\mathcal{C}_{1}\sqsubseteq C_{2}
if and only if

Given a set

The proof therein implies that the variant of the problem with a fixed number

6.
Conclusions

We have settled the decidability and complexity status of most subproblems of trace refinement between two MDPs.
Key technical ingredients were links to a certain notion of bisimulation, linear-algebra arguments, and comparisons of polytopes.

As an open problem, we highlight the complexity of the distribution-based notion of bisimulation, which we have shown to be in
coNP
.
Is this notion of bisimulation
coNP
-complete or in
P
?

A curious multisection identity by index factorization
C. Vignat and M. Milgram Department of Mathematics, Tulane University, cvignat@tulane.edu Consulting Physicist, Geometrics Unlimited, Ltd., mike@geometrics-unlimited.com

Abstract
. This manuscript introduces a general multisection identity expressed equivalently in terms of infinite double products and/or infinite double series, from which several new product or summation identities involving special functions including Gamma, hyperbolic trigonometric, polygamma and zeta functions, are derived. It is shown that a parameterized version of this multisection identity exists, a specialization of which coincides with the standard multisection identity.

Introduction

In the recent article
[
1
]
, the second author derived the
curious identity

(1.1)

\prod_{j\geq 1}\left(\frac{\tan\left(\frac{a}{2^{j}}\right)}{\frac{a}{2^{j}}}\right)^{2^{j-1}}=\frac{a}{\sin a}

along with several other identities of the same flavor.
Upon closer inspection, the first author noticed that this identity is
the specialization of a more general relationship that can be stated as
the even more curious identity

(1.2)

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j}}}\right)^{2^{j}}=\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}

that holds for any sequence of entities
\left\{a_{m}\right\}

such that the infinite product
\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}
is convergent.
In (
1.2
) we have explicitly written each index
m
as the product of the two components of its factored form. That is,
m=(2n).2^{j}
or
m=(2n-1).2^{j}

according to whether
m
is respectively even or odd (see below).
The identity (
1.1
) is the specialization
a_{m}=\left(1-\frac{a^{2}}{m^{2}\pi^{2}}\right)^{-1}

of (
1.2
) ( see (
3.18
) and (
8.23
) below). Identity (
1.2
) can be viewed as a
structural
identity
: it holds as a consequence of one way the
terms are grouped by the components of the factorization of their index in the product, rather than of the specific values of the individual elements
a_{m}
.

Other examples of structural identities are:

•

for an arbitrary sequence
\left\{a_{m}\right\},

(1.3)

\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{2m-1}a_{2m}=\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m},

which is a simple application of the dissection principle;

•

for an arbitrary summable double sequence
\left\{a_{m,n}\right\}_{\left(m,n\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
,

(1.4)

\sum_{\left(m,n\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}a_{m,n}=\sum_{m<n}a_{m,n}+\sum_{m>n}a_{m,n}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m,m}

corresponding to an obvious multisection of the two-dimensional lattice. For similar additive rather than multiplicative multisections, see
[
12

and
[
15

In the following, each extension or specialization of (

this is

(1.5)

\sum_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}2^{j}b_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}-2^{j}b_{\left(2n\right).2^{j}}=\sum_{m\geq 1}b_{m},

where

is an arbitrary summable sequence.

2.
A first generalization

Playing with (
1.2
) suggested the following generalization.

Proposition 2.1
.

For an arbitrary value
q\in\mathbb{C}
and for an arbitrary sequence

\left\{a_{m}\right\}
such that
\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}
exists,
we have

(2.1)

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j}}^{q-1}}\right)^{q^{j}}=\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}

and its series version: for an arbitrary sequence

\left\{b_{m}\right\}
such that
\sum_{m\geq 1}b_{m}
exists,

(2.2)

\sum_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}q^{j}b_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}-q^{j}\left(q-1\right)b_{\left(2n\right).2^{j}}=\sum_{m\geq 1}b_{m}.

A proof of this identity
is provided
in Section
3
below.
A proof in the particular case
b_{m}=t^{m}
can be found in
Section
6
below.
Specializations of identity (
2.1
) are given next.

Example 2.1.1
.

For
q=0,
identity (
2.1
) reduces to the usual dissection
identity

(2.3)

\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{2n-1}a_{2n}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{n}.

Proof.

Notice that

(2.4)

q^{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j=0\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}

so that the right-hand side reduces to

(2.5)

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j}}^{q-1}}\right)^{q^{j}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right)}}{a_{2n}^{-1}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{2n-1}a_{2n}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{n}.

∎

Remark 2.1
.

The fact that we recover the usual dissection identity for
q=0

shows that identity (
2.1
) can be considered as a parameterized
version of the usual dissection formula.

Example 2.1.2
.

The case
q=1
of identity (
2.1
) produces

(2.6)

\prod_{j\geq 1,n\geq 1}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{2n}.

Proof.

Start from

\displaystyle\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}=\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{2n.2^{j}}^{q-1}}\right)^{q^{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{2n-1}\prod_{j\geq 1,n\geq 1}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}

from which we deduce

(2.7)

\prod_{j\geq 1,n\geq 1}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{2n}.

∎

This result is interpreted as follows: consider an arbitrary even
number
m
. By (
1.7
),
there is a unique way to write

(2.8)

m=p.2^{\nu_{2}\left(m\right)}

with
p
an odd number: first compute its valuation
\nu_{2}\left(m\right)
according to (
1.6
) and
then consider

(2.9)

p=\frac{m}{2^{\nu_{2}\left(m\right)}}

which by definition is an odd number.

Example 2.1.3
.

In the case
q=-1
, (
2.1
) produces

(2.10)

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{2j}}a_{2n.2^{2j}}^{2}}{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{2j+1}}a_{2n.2^{2j+1}}^{2}}=\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}.

Proof.

For
q=-1,
we have

(2.11)

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{2n.2^{j}}^{q-1}}\right)^{q^{j}}=\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}a_{2n.2^{j}}^{2}\right)^{\left(-1\right)^{j}}.

Separating the terms with even values of
j
(with a
+1

from those with odd values of

(with a
-1

result.
∎

A general case

A general version of identity (

Proposition 3.1

For two functions

Proposition 4.1
.

For any integer

Choosing an integer

and replacing the function

with its truncated version

\varphi_{J}\left(j\right)=\begin{cases}\varphi\left(j\right)&j\geq J\\

in

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}^{\varphi_{J}\left(j\right)}=\prod_{m\geq 1}a_{m}^{\varphi_{J}\left(s_{2}\left(m\right)\right)}

such that

which are exactly

m=2^{J}p,\,\,p\in\mathbb{N}

so that

(4.7)

\prod_{j\geq J,n\geq 1}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}^{\varphi\left(j\right)}=\prod_{p\geq 1}a_{p.2^{J}}^{\varphi\left(s_{2}\left(p.2^{J}\right)\right)}.

Since moreover

we deduce the result.

Proposition 5.1
.

Consider a double-indexed sequence
\left\{a_{p,q}\right\}
. We
have

(5.1)

\prod_{k\geq 0,m\geq 1}\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}\right)^{2^{j+k}}=\prod_{p\geq 1,q\geq 1}a_{p,q}.

The sum version is, for an arbitrary sequence
\left\{b_{p,q}\right\},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q\geq 1}b_{p,q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\sum_{m,n\geq 1}2^{j+k}\left[b_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}+b_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}\right.

\displaystyle-\left.b_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}-b_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}\right]

Proof.

For a fixed value of
q
we have

(5.3)

\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},q}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},q}}\right)^{2^{j}}=\prod_{p\geq 1}a_{p,q}.

Denote

(5.4)

b_{q}=\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},q}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},q}}\right)^{2^{j}}

so that

(5.5)

\prod_{q\geq 1}b_{q}=\prod_{p\geq 1,q\geq 1}a_{p,q}.

Using

(5.6)

\prod_{q\geq 1}b_{q}=\prod_{k\geq 0,m\geq 1}\left(\frac{b_{\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}}{b_{\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}\right)^{2^{k}}

we deduce

\displaystyle\prod_{p\geq 1}a_{p,q}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 0,m\geq 1}\left(\frac{\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}}\right)^{2^{j}}}{\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}\right)^{2^{j}}}\right)^{2^{k}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 0,m\geq 1}\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j},\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j},\left(2m\right).2^{k}}}\right)^{2^{j+k}}

∎

Corollary 5.1.1
.

The two Lambert series

(5.7)

f\left(q\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}n\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}\text{ and }\,g\left(q\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}n\frac{q^{n}}{1+q^{n}}

are related as

(5.8)

f\left(q\right)=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}2^{2j+k}\left[g\left(q^{2^{j+k}}\right)-4g\left(q^{2^{j+k+1}}\right)\right].

Proof.

Writing the function
f
as the double sum

(5.9)

f\left(q\right)=\sum_{m,n}nq^{nm},

the dissection formula (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle f\left(q\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}\sum_{n,m\geq 1}2^{j+k}\left[\left(2n-1\right)2^{j}q^{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}.\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}-\left(2n-1\right)2^{j}q^{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}.\left(2m\right).2^{k}}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\left(2n\right).2^{j}q^{\left(2n\right).2^{j}.\left(2m\right).2^{k}}-\left(2n\right)2^{j}q^{\left(2n\right).2^{j}.\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}\right].

Each of the 4 sums over
n,m\geq 1
is computed as follows

(5.10)

\sum_{n,m\geq 1}\left(2n-1\right)q^{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}.\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(2n-1\right)\frac{q^{\left(2n-1\right)2^{j+k}}}{1-q^{\left(2n-1\right)2^{j+k+1}}},

(5.11)

\sum_{n,m\geq 1}\left(2n-1\right)q^{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}.\left(2m\right).2^{k}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(2n-1\right)\frac{q^{\left(2n-1\right)2^{j+k+1}}}{1-q^{\left(2n-1\right)2^{j+k+1}}},

\sum_{n,m\geq 1}\left(2n\right)q^{\left(2n\right).2^{j}.\left(2m\right).2^{k}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}2n\frac{q^{n.2^{j+k+2}}}{1-q^{n.2^{j+k+2}}},

\sum_{n,m\geq 1}\left(2n\right)q^{\left(2n\right).2^{j}.\left(2m-1\right).2^{k}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(2n\right)\frac{q^{n.2^{j+k+1}}}{1-q^{n.2^{j+k+2}}}.

With the notation

this yields

\displaystyle f\left(q\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geq 0}2^{2j+k}\sum_{n\geq 1}\left[\left(2n-1\right)\frac{q_{j,k}^{2n-1}-q_{j,k}^{2\left(2n-1\right)}}{1-q_{j,k}^{2\left(2n-1\right)}}+\left(2n\right)\frac{q_{j,k}^{4n}-q_{j,k}^{2n}}{1-q_{j,k}^{4n}}\right]

This inner sum coincides with the difference between the odd part

h_{o}
and the even part

of the function

also equal to

6.1.
The base 2 case

6.1.1.
Two proofs of Teixeira’s identity

The series version of (
1.2
) applied to the case
a_{m}=t^{m}
is

(6.1)

\sum_{m\geq 1}t^{m}=\sum_{k\geq 0,n\geq 1}2^{k}t^{\left(2n-1\right)2^{k}}-2^{k}t^{\left(2n\right)2^{k}}

Summing over
n
in the right hand-side and using the simplification

(6.2)

\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k}\frac{t^{2^{k}}\left(1-t^{2^{k}}\right)}{1-t^{2^{k+1}}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k}\frac{t^{2^{k}}}{1+t^{2^{k}}}

produces

(6.3)

\frac{t}{1-t}=\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k}\frac{t^{2^{k}}}{1+t^{2^{k}}}.

This identity is well-known and
appears as
Problem 10 of Chapter II in
[
3
, p. 118]
, where the result
is attributed to F.G. Teixeira
[
5
]
.

first proof

A simple proof of (
6.3
) is as follows: define

(6.4)

\varphi\left(t\right)=\frac{t}{1-t},

notice that this function satisfies

(6.5)

\varphi\left(t\right)+\varphi\left(-t\right)=2\varphi\left(t^{2}\right)

and iterate this identity (see
[
1
, Appendix A]
for convergence details).

second proof

Another proof appeals to the theory of Lambert series: first, recall the definition of the Dirichlet eta function

(6.6)

\eta\left(s\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}}{n^{s}}=\left(1-2^{1-s}\right)\zeta\left(s\right).

Then consider the following result provided in
[
2
, Problem 11, p.300]

Proposition 6.1
.

Given two sequences
\left\{a_{n}\right\}
and
\left\{b_{n}\right\}
,

(6.7)

\eta\left(s\right)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}

if and only if

(6.8)

\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{1+x^{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}x^{n}.

Let us apply this result to the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
defined as

(6.9)

a_{n}=\begin{cases}2^{l}\text{ if }n=2^{l}\\
0\text{ else}\end{cases}

to obtain, on the left-hand side of (
6.8
),

(6.10)

\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{1+x^{n}}=\sum_{l\geq 1}2^{l}\frac{x^{2^{l}}}{1+x^{2^{l}}}

and, on the left-hand side of (
6.7
),

(6.11)

\eta(s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}}{n^{s}}=\eta(s)\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{1}{2^{l(s-1)}}=\zeta(s).

We deduce from (
6.7
) that

(6.12)

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{b_{n}}{n^{s}}=\zeta(s)

so that
b_{n}=1,\,\,n\geq 1
and, as a consequence, Teixeira’s identity (
6.3
).

A simple interpretation of Teixeira’s identity (
6.3
)
is as follows:

(6.13)

\frac{t}{1-t}=\sum_{n\geq 1}t^{n}

is the generating function of the sequence
\left\{1,1,\dots\right\}:

each integer
n
is counted with a unit weight. The first term
\left(j=0\right)

in the right-hand side of Teixeira’s identity (
6.3
)
is

(6.14)

\frac{t}{1+t}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(-1\right)^{n}t^{n}

so that all even integers are weighted by
+1
but the odd ones are
weighted by
\left(-1\right).
The extra terms

(6.15)

\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{j}\frac{t^{2^{j}}}{1+t^{2^{j}}}

will correct these negative weights so that the final sum has all positive
unit weights. More precisely:

\displaystyle\frac{t}{1+t}

\displaystyle=t-t^{2}+t^{3}-t^{4}+t^{5}-t^{6}+t^{7}-t^{8}+t^{9}-t^{10}+t^{11}-t^{12}

\displaystyle+t^{13}-t^{14}+t^{15}-t^{16}+t^{17}-t^{18}+t^{19}-t^{20}+O\left(t^{21}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{t}{1+t}+2\frac{t^{2}}{1+t^{2}}

\displaystyle=t+t^{2}+t^{3}-3t^{4}+t^{5}+t^{6}+t^{7}-3t^{8}+t^{9}+t^{10}+t^{11}-3t^{12}

\displaystyle+t^{13}+t^{14}+t^{15}-3t^{16}+t^{17}+t^{18}+t^{19}-3t^{20}+O\left(t^{21}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{t}{1+t}+2\frac{t^{2}}{1+t^{2}}+4\frac{t^{4}}{1+t^{4}}

\displaystyle=t+t^{2}+t^{3}+t^{4}+t^{5}+t^{6}+t^{7}-7t^{8}+t^{9}+t^{10}+t^{11}+t^{12}

\displaystyle+t^{13}+t^{14}+t^{15}-7t^{16}+t^{17}+t^{18}+t^{19}+t^{20}+O\left(t^{21}\right)

so that all powers of
t
in

(6.16)

6.1.2.
A generalization of Teixeira’s identity

Now consider the functions

(6.17)

We deduce

The arbitrary base b case

Consider the functions

A q-calculus application

This section produces a
q-
calculus application of our main identity.

Proposition 7.1
.

With the
q-
Pochhammer symbol

(7.1)

\left(a;q\right)_{\infty}=\prod_{k\geq 0}\left(1-aq^{k}\right),

we have the identities, for
q\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|q|<1,

(7.2)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2^{j+1}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}\right)^{2^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty},

and more generally, for any complex number
p,

(7.3)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2^{j+1}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}^{p-1}}\right)^{p^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}

and

(7.4)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2.3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{3^{j+1}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}^{p-1}}\right)^{p^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}.

The special case
p=1
produces respectively

(7.5)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}

and

(7.6)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2.3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}.

Proof.

In the identity

(7.7)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{2n.2^{j}}}\right)^{2^{j}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{n},

take
a_{n}=1-q^{n}
so that the right-hand side is
\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}
.
Moreover,

(7.8)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 0,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(2n+1\right).2^{j}}\right)=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}

and

(7.9)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{2n.2^{j}}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 0,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(2n+2\right).2^{j}}\right)=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{2^{j+1}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}

so that

(7.10)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2^{j+1}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}\right)^{2^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}.

In the more general case

(7.11)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{2n.2^{j}}^{p-1}}\right)^{p^{j}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{n},

we deduce

(7.12)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2^{j+1}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}^{p-1}}\right)^{p^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}.

For example,
p=3
produces

(7.13)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2^{j+1}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}^{2}}\right)^{3^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}.

In the base 3 case, using

(7.14)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(\frac{a_{\left(3n-1\right).3^{j}}a_{\left(3n-2\right).3^{j}}}{a_{3n.3^{j}}^{p-1}}\right)^{p^{j}}=\prod_{n\geq 1}a_{n},

we have

(7.15)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(3n-1\right).3^{j}}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 0,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(3n+2\right).3^{j}}\right)=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{2.3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}

and

(7.16)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(3n-2\right).3^{j}}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 0,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(3n+1\right).3^{j}}\right)=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}

and

(7.17)

\prod_{n\geq 1,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{3n.3^{j}}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 0,j\geq 0}\left(1-q^{\left(3n+3\right).3^{j}}\right)=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(q^{3^{j+1}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}

so that

(7.18)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(q^{3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}\left(q^{2.3^{j}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{3^{j+1}};q^{3^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}^{p-1}}\right)^{p^{j}}=\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}.

∎

Proposition 7.2
.

The following multisection formula holds:

(7.21)

\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\left(aq^{2^{j}-1};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(aq^{2^{j+1}-1};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}\right)^{2^{j}}=\left(a;q\right)_{\infty}.

We have

\displaystyle\prod_{j\geq 0,n\geq 1}\left(\frac{a_{\left(2n-1\right).2^{j}}}{a_{\left(2n\right).2^{j}}}\right)^{2^{j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\geq 0}\left(\frac{\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(1-aq^{\left(2n+1\right).2^{j}-1}\right)}{\prod_{n\geq 0}\left(1-aq^{\left(2n+2\right).2^{j}-1}\right)}\right)^{2^{j}}

∎

Remark 7.1
.

A combinatorial interpretation of identity (
7.5
) is as follows: rewrite (
7.5
) as

(7.22)

\frac{1}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}}=\prod_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}.

The left-hand side is the generating function for the number of partitions
p(n)
of the integer
n
.
More generally, the infinite product

(7.23)

\prod_{i\in I}\frac{1}{1-q^{i}}

is the generating function for the number of partitions

of
n
with parts belonging to the set

Hence

\frac{1}{\left(q^{2^{j}};q^{2^{j+1}}\right)_{\infty}}=\prod_{i\in I_{j}}\frac{1}{1-q^{i}}

is the generating function for the number of partitions of

It is easy to check that the set of subsets

\mathbb{N}

has an elementary interpretation in base 2: since

n
according to their

valuation.

Proof of identity (

Identity (

(A.2)

\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j+1}\frac{q^{2^{j}+1}}{q^{2^{j}}+q^{-2^{j}}}=\frac{2q^{2}}{q^{-1}-q}

(A.3)

\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{j+1}\frac{1}{q^{-2^{j+1}}+1}=\frac{2q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}.

Substituting

which is (

Piecewise linear iterated function systems on the line of overlapping construction
R. Dániel Prokaj, Károly Simon

Abstract
. In this paper we consider Iterated Function Systems (IFS) on the real line consisting of continuous piecewise linear functions. We assume some bounds on the contraction ratios of the functions, but we do not assume any separation condition. Moreover, we do not require that the functions of the IFS are injective, but we assume that their derivatives are separated from zero. We prove that if we fix all the slopes but perturb all other parameters, then for all parameters outside of an exceptional set of less than full packing dimension, the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor is equal to the exponent which comes from the most natural system of covers of the attractor.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Attractors on the line

Iterated Function Systems (IFS) on the line consist of finitely many strictly contracting self-mappings of
\mathbb{R}
. It was proved by Hutchinson
[
11
]

that
for every IFS
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
there is a unique non-empty compact set
\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}
which is
called the attractor of the IFS
\mathcal{F}
and defined by

(1.1)

\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{m}f_{k}(\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}).

It is easy to see that for every IFS
\mathcal{F}
there exists a unique "smallest" non-empty compact interval
I^{\mathcal{F}}
which is sent into itself by all the mappings of
\mathcal{F}
:

(1.2)

I^{\mathcal{F}}:=\bigcap\left\{J:J\subset\mathbb{R}\mbox{ compact interval with }f_{k}(J)\subset J\mbox{, for all }k\in[m]\right\},

where
[m]:=\left\{1,\dots,m\right\}
.
It is easy to see that

(1.3)

\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=\bigcap\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup\limits_{(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in[m]^{n}}I_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}^{\mathcal{F}},

where
I_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}^{\mathcal{F}}:=f_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}(I^{\mathcal{F}})
are the
cylinder intervals
, and we use the common shorthand notation
f_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}:=f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}
for an

(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in[m]^{n}
.

The following three IFS families, each defined on
\mathbb{R}
, appear on Figure
1
.

(a)

Self-similar IFS:

\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{i}(x)=\rho_{i}x+t_{i}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
, where
\rho_{i}\in(-1,1)\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

Hyperbolic IFS:
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\right\}
, where each
f_{k}:J\to J
is a
C^{1+\varepsilon}(J)
contracting self-mappings of a non-empty open interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

We introduce the
Continuous Piecewise Linear Iterated Function Systems (CPLIFS)
: These are IFSs of the form

\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\right\}
, where

f_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous, piececewise linear contractions (not necessarily injective) with all slopes different from zero.

Figure 1
.

We note that the interval
I^{\mathcal{F}}

introduced in (
1.2
) is the convex hull of the attractor in the first two cases, but not necessarily for non-injective CPLIFSs.

The
t
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of an open Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is

(1.4)

\mathcal{H}^{t}(A):=\sup\limits_{\delta>0}\left\{\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|A_{i}|^{t}:A\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i},\ |A_{i}|<\delta\right\}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary IFS on the line.
We can give a natural upper bound on
\mathcal{H}^{t}(\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}})
if we substitute the covering system made of the cylinder intervals
\left\{I^{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}\right\}_{(i_{1}\dots i_{n})\in[m]^{n}}
in the place of the most efficient cover in (
1.4
),
just like in (
1.3
). For this particular system the right hand side of (
1.4
) is related to the sequence of sums
\left\{S_{n}^{t}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

(1.5)

S_{n}^{t}:=\sum_{(i_{1}\dots i_{n})\in[m]^{n}}|I^{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}|^{t}.

Namely, if the exponential growth rate of
\left\{S_{n}^{t}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is negative (positive), then it suggests that
\mathcal{H}^{t}(\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}})
is equal to zero (infinity). Therefore the minimum of the dimension of the ambient space
and the value of the exponent
t
for which this exponential growth rate is zero is a good natural guess for the Hausdorff dimension of
\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}
.
This motivates the introduction of the exponential growth rate

(1.6)

\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}(s):=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}|I^{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}|^{s}.

It is easy to see that we can obtain
\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}(s)

above as a special case of the non-additive upper capacity topological pressure introduced by Barreira in
[
4
, p. 5]
.
According to
[
4
, Theorem 1.9]
, the zero of
\Phi^{\mathcal{F}}(s)
is well defined

(1.7)

s_{\mathcal{F}}:=(\Phi^{\mathcal{F}})^{-1}(0).

Barreira considered generalized Moran constructions
[
4
, Section 2.1.2]
. Condition (b) of such a construction requires that the cylinder intervals
\left\{I^{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{1}\dots i_{n}}\right\}
are sufficiently well separated. This separation assumption does not hold for the constructions considered in this paper, thus not all of his results apply.
In the last inequality of

[
4
, Theorem 2.1 part (a)]

Barreira proves that the upper box dimension of
\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}
is less than or equal to the root of the non-additive upper capacity topological pressure.
It is easy to see that its proof does not require any separation conditions on the cylinder intervals. That is

Corollary 1.1

(Barreira)
.

For any IFS
\mathcal{F}
on the line

(1.8)

\overline{\dim}_{\rm B}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}\leq s_{\mathcal{F}.}

For a given IFS
\mathcal{F}
on the line we name
s_{\mathcal{F}}

the
natural dimension of the system
.
It is easy to see that
in the self-simiar case
s_{\mathcal{F}}
is the solution of the self-similar equation

(1.9)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\rho_{k}|^{s_{\mathcal{F}}}=1.

We call
s_{\mathcal{F}}
the similarity dimension in this case.
If
\mathcal{F}
is a hyperbolic system, then
s_{\mathcal{F}}

is the root of the so-called pressure formula (see
[
15
]
). In both cases the Open Set Condition (OSC) implies that

(1.10)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=\min\left\{s_{\mathcal{F}},1\right\}.

The cylinder intervals of the attractor of a system that satisfies the OSC are well separated
(see
[
7
, p. 35]
).
However, less strict separation conditions might lead to the same result.
In the self-similar case the celebrated Hochman Theorem
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
yields that
(
1.10
) also follows from the Exponential Searation Condition
(ESC).

1.2.
Introducing Continuous Piecewise Linear IFSs

The IFSs we consider in this paper are consisting of piecewise linear functions. Thus their derivatives might change at some points, but they are linear over given intervals of
\mathbb{R}
. We always assume that the functions are continuous, piecewise linear, strongly contracting with non-zero slopes, and that the slopes can only change at finitely many points. This setup enables us to investigate the case of non-injective functions as well, which is not achievable with either hyperbolic or self-similar systems.

A special case of CPLIFSs can be handled using the theory of F. Hofbauer
[
10
]
and P. Raith
[
16
]
.
In particular, a family of CPLIFSs satisfying the one dimensional version of the rectangular open set condition.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a CPLIFS
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
satisfies the
Interval Open Set Condition
(IOSC) if the first cylinder intervals

\{I^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
are pairwise disjoint.

Take a CPLIFS
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{m}
of injective functions that satisfies the IOSC. Each
f_{k}
function can be considered as the local inverse over
I^{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
of a strictly expanding map on
\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor of such systems is equal to the root of a topological pressure function
[
16
]
.
Further, according to
[
10
]
, for this special CPLIFS family the Hausdorff and the box dimensions of the attractor are also equal.

In general, when we have no information about the possibble overlapping of the cylinders, we can claim only that (
1.10
) holds in some sense typically. Instead of a particular IFS, it is natural to consider its so-called translation family.

Definition 1.3
.

For every
\boldsymbol{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

we define the translation family of
\mathcal{F}
by

(1.11)

\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right\}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}},\mbox{ where }\mathcal{F}^{\tau}=\{f_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}},\dots,f_{m}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\}

and
f_{k}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(x):=f_{k}(x)+\tau_{k}
for a
k\in[m]
. Moreover,
for an
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Sigma^{*}
we define the function

(1.12)

f_{\mathbf{i}}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(x):=f_{i_{1}}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(x).

The classical results
[
5
]
,

[
6
]
and
[
17
]

about the translation families read like this: For typical (in some sense) translations
\boldsymbol{\tau}
,
the Hausdorff dimension of the translated attractor
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is equal to its natural dimension. Here the sense of typicality depends on the family of the IFS considered. For example, for hyperbolic IFS on the line the typicality above means "typical with respect to the
m
-dimensional Lebesgue measure"
[
17
]
(at least in the case when all contractions are stronger than
\frac{1}{2}
). In this case we say that the Hausdorff dimension and the natural dimension coincide for Lebesgue typical translations.
On the other hand, Hochman
[
9
]
proved a much stronger theorem for the translation family of self-similar IFSs that we call
Multi Parameter Hochman Theorem
(see Theorem
3.5
). This theorem implies that the
exceptional set of translations
\boldsymbol{\tau}
for which
the ESC does not hold for
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
(and consequently

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
is different from the similarity dimension), has packing dimension (and in this way also Hausdorff dimension) less than or equal to
m-1
. Remember that the dimension of the parameter space was equal to
m
.
Motivated by this, we introduce the following terminology related to more general IFS families on the line:

Terminology
:

Let
\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\right\}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in U}
be a family of IFSs on the line where the parameter set
U
is an open
subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\geq 1
.
We say that a
property

\mathfrak{P}
, which makes sense for all elements of this family,
holds
\dim_{\rm P}
-typically
if the exceptional set
E
of those parameters
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in U

for which
\mathfrak{P}
does not hold satisfies
\dim_{\rm P}E<d
.
That is
the packing dimension of the exceptional set

E\subset U
is smaller than the dimension of the parameter space
U
.

Our main result states that
\dim_{\rm P}
-typically the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor of a CPLIFS that satisfies certain regularity conditions is equal to its natural dimension.
More precisely,
a CPLIFS
\mathcal{F}
is called regular if it has sufficiently small contraction ratios and there exists an
N
such that for all
(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\in[m]^{N}

the cylinder interval
I_{i_{1}\dots i_{N}}^{\mathcal{F}}

does not contain any point of non-differentiability of any of the functions of
\mathcal{F}
.
We will show the following:

(a)

For a regular CPLIFS
\mathcal{F}
we consider its generated self-similar IFS

(see Section

). If it satisfies the so-called Exponential Separation Condition (ESC), then its

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}^{N}
and any subsystem of

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}^{N}

We point out that we can select a suitable subsystem

such that the attractor of this graph-directed system and

coincide.
Then we use the Jordan Rapaport Theorem

]

to compute the dimension of this graph-directed attractor which, as we just mentioned, is the same as

\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}
. In this way we obtain that

(1.13)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=s_{\mathcal{F}}.

(a)

We verify in our Main Proposition that for a

-typical set of parameters (see our Terminology)

Then we apply the multi-parameter Hochman Theorem

-typical set of parameters.
That is by part (a) (

1.5.
The organization of the paper

The first two sections are dedicated to introduce and motivate the continuous piecewise linear iterated function systems, and the pressure function we use to calculate the natural dimension of the attractor. We introduce here all the important definitions and notations, and the typicality we use in our theorems as well. At the end of the chapter we state our main theorems.

In Section
3
we present some recent results from the field of self-similar iterated function systems, that will be useful in our proofs. Following
[
8
]
we define the exponential Separation Condition (ESC) and state some important theorems of M. Hochman that utilizes this assumption. We also state here a recent result of Jordan and Rapaport

that will help us give a lower bound on the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor of a regular CPLIFS.
In the second half of this section we introduce the self-similar graph-directed iterated function systems and their dimension theory based on

and
[

.

To prove our results, we need to associate regular CPLIFSs to graph-directed iterated function systems. Section

We also introduce here an ergodic invariant measure supported on the attractor of a regular CPLIFS, which lets us calculate the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor of the corresponding CPLIFS with the help of the Jordan-Rapaport theorem.

Section

2.1.
Parametrizing CPLIFSs

We fix a number
m\geq 2
, and use it as the number of functions in a CPLIFS throughout the paper.
Let

\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}

We write

for the number of breaking points of

for

(2.1)

|f^{\prime}_{k_{1}\dots k_{n}}(x)|\leq\rho_{k_{1}\dots k_{n}},\mbox{ for all }x.

Finally, we write

(2.11)

\tau_{k}:=f_{k}(0),\mbox{ and }\boldsymbol{\tau}:=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

So, the parameters that uniquely determine an
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{CPLIFS}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}

can be organized into a vector

(2.12)

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\mathfrak{b},\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\rho})\in\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}:=\mathfrak{B}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathfrak{R}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{L}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{L+m}=\mathbb{R}^{2L+2m}.

It is easy to see that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
indeed determines a CPLIFS uniquely. For instance, assuming that all the breaking points in the system are positive, we have the following formula for the functions of
\mathcal{F}
:

(2.13)

\quad f_{k}(x)=\tau_{k}+\sum_{j=1}^{l(k)}(b_{k,j}-b_{k,j-1})\rho_{k,j}\boldsymbol{1}\{b_{k,j}\leq x\}+\sum_{j=1}^{l(k)+1}\rho_{k,j}x\boldsymbol{1}\{x\in J_{k,j}\},

for any
k\in[m]
and
x\geq 0
, where we used the notation
\forall k\in[m]:b_{k,0}=0
to make the formula more compact.

For a
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
we write
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
for the corresponding CPLIFS,
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
for its attractor, and
s_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
for its natural dimension. Similarly,
for an
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{CPLIFS}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
we write

\boldsymbol{\lambda}(\mathcal{F})
for the corresponding element of
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
.

We can handle only those situations when the contraction ratios are sufficiently small.
Along the lines of Definition
1.4
, we define the set of small contraction vectors for a given type
\boldsymbol{\ell}
as

(2.14)

\mathfrak{R}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}_{\mathrm{small}}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathfrak{R}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}:\mathcal{F}^{(\mathfrak{b},\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\rho})}\mbox{ is small for all }\mathfrak{b}\in\mathfrak{B}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\mbox{ and }\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\}.

With the help of these notations we can taylor the terminology of
\dim_{\rm P}
-typicality for CPLIFSs.

Terminology
:

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a property that makes sense for every CPLIFS.
For a contraction vector

\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathrm{small}}^{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\ell}}}

we consider the (exceptional) set

(2.15)

E_{\mathfrak{P},\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=:\left\{(\mathfrak{b},\boldsymbol{\tau})\in\mathfrak{B}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}:\mathcal{F}^{(\mathfrak{b},\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\rho})}\mbox{ does not have property }\mathfrak{P}\right\}.

We say
that
property
\mathfrak{P}
holds
\dim_{\rm P}
-typically
if for all
type
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and for all
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathrm{small}}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
we have

(2.16)

\dim_{\rm P}E_{\mathfrak{P},\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\boldsymbol{\rho}}<L+m,

where
\boldsymbol{\ell}=(l(1),\dots,l(m))
and
L=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}l(k)
as above.

2.2.
Our main results

With the help of all these definitions and notations we can state now the main results of this paper.

Theorem 2.1

(Main Theorem)
.

For a
\dim_{\rm P}
-typical small CPLIFS
\mathcal{F}
we have

(2.17)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=\dim_{\rm B}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=s_{\mathcal{F}}.

To prove our main result we verify the following theorem and proposition:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a regular CPLIFS for which the generated self-similar IFS (defined in Section
4.1
) satisfies the Exponential Separation Condition (ESC) (see Definition
3.1
). Then

(2.18)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=\dim_{\rm B}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=s_{\mathcal{F}}.

To prove this, we use the multi-parameter Hochman Theorem

[
9
, Theorem 1.10]

and a very recent result of Jordan and Rapaport
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Proposition 2.3

(Main Proposition)
.

A
\dim_{\rm P}
-typical small CPLIFS is regular.

Together with
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
, this proposition implies the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

For a
\dim_{\rm P}
-typical small CPLIFS
\mathcal{F}
let
s:=\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}
be the Hausdorff dimension of its attractor. Further, assume that all functions in
\mathcal{F}
are injective.
Then we have

(2.19)

s=\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}=\dim_{\rm B}\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}.

Further,
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}})<\infty
, where
\mathcal{H}^{s}
denotes the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

3.
Self-similar and self-similar graph-directed IFSs on
\mathbb{R}

Here we summarize the relevant results from the theory of self-similar IFSs.
Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{M}
be an IFS
on
\mathbb{R}
and
\Lambda
be the attractor of
\mathcal{F}
.
We denote the symbolic space by
\Sigma=\{1,\ldots,M\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and its elements by
\mathbf{i}=(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots)\in\Sigma
. We write
\Sigma^{\ast}
for the set of all finite length words.
Elements of
\Sigma^{\ast}
are denoted by
\overline{\imath}=i_{1}\ldots i_{n}
, and sometimes we obtain them as truncations of infinite length words
\mathbf{i}|n=i_{1}\ldots i_{n}
.
As usual, the natural projection
\Pi:\Sigma\to\Lambda
is defined by

(3.1)

\Pi(\mathbf{i})=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}f_{\mathbf{i}|n}(0).

We write
\sigma
for the left-shift on
\Sigma
. For the definition of invariance, ergodicity, and entropy (denoted by
h(\mu)
) of the measure
\mu
we refer the reader to Walters’ book
[
18
]
.
If the measure
\mu
is ergodic and the derivatives of all of the mappings
f_{i}
are continuous, we define the Lyapunov exponent of
\mu

by

(3.2)

\chi(\mu):=\int\log|f^{\prime}_{i_{i}}(\Pi(\sigma\mathbf{i}))|d\mu(\mathbf{i}).

Let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
. The Hausdorff dimension of
\nu
is defined as

(3.3)

\dim_{\rm H}\nu:=\inf\left\{\dim_{\rm H}E:\nu(E)>0\right\}.

3.1.
Self-similar IFSs on the line

In the special case when all
f_{i}
are similarities (the slopes are constants)
\mathcal{F}
is called self-similar IFS.
In this case for all
i\in[M]:=\left\{1,\dots,M\right\}
the mappings
f_{i}
, can be presented in the form

(3.4)

f_{i}(x)=r_{i}x+t_{i},\mbox{ where }r_{i}\in(-1,1)\setminus\left\{0\right\}\mbox{ and }t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.

The simplest guess for the dimension of the attractor
\Lambda
can be expressed in terms of the
similarity dimension

\dim_{\rm S}\Lambda
, which is defined as the solution
s
of the equation
\sum\limits_{K=1}^{M}|r_{k}|^{s}=1
. That is

(3.5)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda\leq\min\left\{1,\dim_{\rm S}\Lambda\right\},

It follows from a theorem of Hochman’s (see in the next subsection) that
in some sense typically we have equality above.
For an
\overline{\imath}:=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
we define

(3.6)

r_{\overline{\imath}|_{0}}:=1,\quad r_{\overline{\imath}}=r_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}=r_{i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot r_{i_{n}}\mbox{ and }t_{\overline{\imath}}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}t_{i_{k}}r_{\overline{\imath}|_{k}}.

Clearly, we have
f_{\overline{\imath}}(x)=r_{\overline{\imath}}x+t_{\overline{\imath}}
and

(3.7)

\Pi(\mathbf{i})=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{i_{1}\dots i_{n-1}}t_{i_{n}}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}t_{\mathbf{i}|_{n}}.

If
\mu
is a measure on
\Sigma
then we write
\Pi_{*}\mu
for the push forward measure of
\mu
.
That is
\Pi_{*}\mu(E)=\mu(\Pi^{-1}E)
.
Let
\mu
be a
\sigma
-invariant ergodic measure on
\Sigma
.
Then the Lyapunov exponent is

(3.8)

\chi(\mu)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\mu[k]\log r_{k}.

For a probability vector

\mathbf{p}:=(p_{1},\dots,p_{M})
we define the measures:

(3.9)

\mu_{\mathbf{p}}([i_{1},\dots,i_{n}]):=p_{i_{1}}\cdots p_{i_{n}},\mbox{ and }\nu_{\mathbf{p}}:=\Pi_{*}\mu_{\mathbf{p}}.

We say that
\nu_{\mathbf{p}}
is a
self-similar measure
.
The simplest guess for the Hausdorff dimension of a self-similar measure

\nu_{\mathbf{p}}

is

(3.10)

\dim_{\rm S}\nu_{\mathbf{p}}:=\frac{h(\nu_{\mathbf{p}})}{\chi(\nu_{\mathbf{p}})}=\frac{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{M}p_{k}\log p_{k}}{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{M}p_{k}\log r_{k}}.

\dim_{\rm H}\nu_{\mathbf{p}}\leq\dim_{\rm S}\nu_{\mathbf{p}}
and a theorem of Hochman states that typically we have equality. We say that
\dim_{\rm S}\nu_{\mathbf{p}}
is the
similarity dimension of the measure

\nu_{\mathbf{p}}
.

Hochman
[
8
]
introduced the notion of exponential separation for self-similar IFSs. To state it, first we need to define the distance of two similarity mappings

g_{1}(x)=\varrho_{1}x+\tau_{1}
and
g_{2}(x)=\varrho_{2}x+\tau_{2}
,
\varrho_{1},\varrho_{2}\in(-1,1)\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
on

\mathbb{R}
. Namely,

(3.11)

\mathrm{dist}\left(g_{1},g_{2}\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|,&\hbox{if $\varrho_{1}=\varrho_{2}$;}\\
\infty,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a self-similar IFS
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}(x)\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
on
\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
Exponential Separation Condition (ESC)
if
there exists a
c>0
and a strictly increasing sequence of natural numbers
\left\{n_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
such that

(3.12)

\mathrm{dist}\left(f_{\overline{\imath}},f_{\overline{\jmath}}\right)\geq c^{n_{\ell}}\mbox{ for all }\ell\mbox{ and for all }\overline{\imath},\overline{\jmath}\in\left\{1,\dots,M\right\}^{n_{\ell}},\ \overline{\imath}\neq\overline{\jmath}.

We note that the exponential separation condition always holds when an IFS is parametrized by algebraic parameters
[
8
]
.

3.1.1.
Some important results about self-similar IFSs

Here we present three very important theorems about the dimension theory of self-similar IFSs.
In this subsection
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}(x)=r_{i}x+t_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{M}
, and
r_{k}\in(-1,1)\setminus\left\{0\right\}
as above, and we use the notation of the previous subsections. First we recall two theorems of Hochman.

Theorem 3.2

(Hochman
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a self-similar IFS on
\mathbb{R}
which satisfies
the Exponential Separation Condition (ESC). Then

(a)

For every
self-similar measure
\nu
we have

(3.13)

\dim_{\rm H}\nu=\min\{1,\dim_{\rm S}\Lambda\}.

(a)

Consequently,

(3.14)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda=\min\left\{1,\dim_{\rm S}\Lambda\right\}

We remark that exact overlaps (the existence of
\overline{\imath}\neq\overline{\jmath}
such that
f_{\overline{\imath}}\eqcirc f_{\overline{\jmath}}
) may lead to dimension drop.
On the other hand, it follows from Baker
[
1
]
and Bárány, Käenmäki
[
2
]
that ESC can fail even if there is no exact overlap.

The following theorem of Hochman is about the translation family of a self similar IFS.
To state it we need some further notation.

Definition 3.3
.

We consider the translation family
\left\{\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\right\}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}}

(defined in Definition
1.3
) of a self-similar IFS
\mathcal{F}
.
We denote the attractor of
\mathcal{F}^{\tau}
by
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
For
\overline{\imath},\overline{\jmath}\in[M]^{n}
we write

(3.15)

\Delta_{\overline{\imath},\overline{\jmath}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}):=f_{\overline{\imath}}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(0)-f_{\overline{\jmath}}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(0).

Let us define the exceptional set

(3.16)

E:=\bigcap\limits_{\varepsilon>0}\left(\bigcup\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}\bigcap\limits_{n>N}\left(\bigcup\limits_{\overline{\imath},\overline{\jmath}\in[M]^{n}\atop\overline{\imath}\neq\overline{\jmath}}\Delta^{-1}_{\overline{\imath},\overline{\jmath}}(-\varepsilon^{n},\varepsilon^{n})\right)\right)

One can easily check that the following simple fact holds:

Fact 3.4
.

Using the notation of Definition
3.3
, we have

(3.17)

\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mbox{ does not satisfy the ESC }\right\}\subset E.

The following theorem is a Corollary of

[
9
, Theorem 1.10]

Theorem 3.5

(Multi parameter Hochman Theorem

[
9
]
)
.

Using the notation of Definition
3.3
, we have

(3.18)

\dim_{\rm P}E\leq M-1.

Consequently,

(3.19)

\dim_{\rm P}\left\{\boldsymbol{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}:\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\mbox{ does not satisfy the ESC }\right\}\leq M-1.

The consequences of the following theorem will be very important in this paper.

Theorem 3.6

(Jordan, Rapaport
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
be a self-similar IFS on
\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the ESC. Moreover, let
\mu
be an invariant ergodic probability measure on
\Sigma=[M]^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

(3.20)

\dim_{\rm H}\Pi_{*}\mu=\min\left\{1,\frac{h(\mu)}{\chi(\mu)}\right\}.

In the Appendix in Corollary
7.2
we prove that with the help of this result we can extend part (b) of Theorem
3.2
to self-similar graph-directed iterated function systems.

3.2.
Self-similar graph-directed IFSs on the line

We present here the most important notations and results related to self-similar Graph-Directed Iterated function Systems (GDIFS).
In this subsection we follow the book
[
7
]
and the papers
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
.
The major difference is that in the first two references the authors assume separation in between the graph-directed sets.
In
[
13
]
no separation is assumed, and we follow that line.

To define the graph-directed iterated function systems we need a directed graph
\mathcal{G}=\left(\mathcal{V,E}\right)
.
We label the vertices of this graph with the numbers
\{1,2,...,q\}
, where
|\mathcal{V}|=q
. This
\mathcal{G}
graph is not assumed to be simple, it might have multiple edges between the same vertices, or even loops. For an edge
e=(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}
we write
s(e):=i
for the source and
t(e):=j
for the target of
e
.
Denote with
\mathcal{E}_{i,j}
the set of directed edges from vertex
i
to vertex
j
, and write
\mathcal{E}_{i,j}^{k}
for the set of length
k
directed paths between
i
and
j
. Similarly, we write
\mathcal{E}^{n}
for the set of all directed paths of length
n
in the graph.
We assume that
\mathcal{G}

is strongly connected. That is for every
i,j\in\mathcal{V}
there is a directed path in
\mathcal{G}
from
i
to
j
.

For all edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
given a contracting similarity mapping
F_{e}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The contraction ratio is denoted by
r_{e}\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}
. Let
e_{1}\dots e_{n}
be a path in
\mathcal{G}
.
Then we write

F_{e_{1}\dots e_{n}}:=F_{e_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ F_{e_{n}}
.
It follows from the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]

that
there exists a unique family of non-empty compact sets
\Lambda_{1},...,\Lambda_{q}
labeled by the elements of
\mathcal{V}
, for which

(3.21)

\Lambda_{i}=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{q}\bigcup\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{i,j}}F_{e}(\Lambda_{j}),\quad i=1,\dots,q.

We call the sets
\{\Lambda_{1},...,\Lambda_{q}\}

graph-directed sets
, and we say that
\Lambda:=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{q}\Lambda_{i}
is
the attractor of the
self-similar graph-directed IFS

\mathcal{F}=\{F_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
.
We abbreviate it to
self-similar GDIFS
.

By iterating (
3.21
) we get

(3.22)

\Lambda_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}\bigcup_{(e_{1},...e_{k})\in\mathcal{E}_{i,j}^{k}}F_{e_{1}\dots e_{k}}(\Lambda_{j}).

To get the most natural guess for the dimension of
\Lambda
, for every
s\geq 0
,
we define a
q\times q
matrix with the following entries

(3.23)

C^{(s)}=(c^{(s)}(i,j))_{i,j=1}^{q}\mbox{ and }c^{(s)}(i,j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{if $\mathcal{E}_{i,j}=\emptyset$;}\\
\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{i,j}}|r_{e}|^{s},&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The spectral radius of
C^{(s)}
is denoted by
\varrho(C^{(s)})
. Mauldin and Williams
[
14
, Theorem 2]
proved that the function

s\mapsto\varrho(C^{(s)})
is strictly decreasing, continuous, greater than
1
at
s=0
, and less than
1
if
s
is large enough.

Definition 3.7
.

For the self-similar GDIFS
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{e}\right\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
we write

\alpha=\alpha(\mathcal{F})
for the unique number satisfying

(3.24)

\varrho(C^{(\alpha)})=1.

The relation of
\alpha
to the dimension of the attractor is given by the following theorem. Both parts appeared in
[
14
]

apart from the box dimension part, which is from
[
7
]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{e}\right\}_{e\in E}
be a self-similar GDIFS as above.
In particular, the graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},E)
is strongly connected and let
\Lambda
be the attractor.

(a)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda\leq\alpha
.

(a)

Let
I_{k}
be the convex hull of
\Lambda_{k}
for all
k\in\mathcal{V}
. If the intervals
\left\{I_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{q}
are pairwise disjoint then

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda=\dim_{\rm B}\Lambda=\alpha
. Moreover,

0<\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(\Lambda)<\infty
.

4.
The preparation for the proof of the Main Theorem

In this rather technical section we will complete the following steps:

(1)

First we recall the definition of the generated self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
for a CPLIFS

\mathcal{F}
.

(1)

We assume that
\mathcal{F}
is regular and we consider a suitable sub-system

\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
(called associated IFS) of the
N
-th iterate
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}^{N}
of the generated self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
for the appropriate
N
.

(1)

We represent
\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}

as the attractor of a graph directed self-similar IFS whose functions
are elements of
\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
.

(1)

Then we define an appropriate invariant and ergodic measure (actually a Markov measure) for the self-similar IFS
\mathscr{S}_{\mathcal{F}}
, whose support is the attractor of the previously mentioned grapgh directed self-similar IFS which coincides with
\Lambda_{\mathcal{F}}
as we mentioned above.

Later we will apply the Jordan Rapaport theorem (Theorem
3.6
) for this measure to get the dimension of
\Lambda^{\mathcal{F}}
.

4.1.
The generated self-similar IFS

Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}\in\mathrm{CPLIFS}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
for a type
\boldsymbol{\ell}=(l(1),\dots,l(m))
with
L=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}l(k)
.
As we already said in Subsection
1.3
, the
generated self-similar IFS

S_{\mathcal{F}}
consists of those similarity mappings on
\mathbb{R}
whose graph coincide with the graph of
f_{k}
for some
k\in[m]
on some interval of linearity
J_{k,i}
,
i\in l(k)+1
of
f_{k}
.
Hence, it is natural to parameterize the generated self-similar IFS by the
l(k)+1
intervals of linearity
J_{k,1},\dots,J_{k,l(k)+1}

of the mapping
f_{k}
for all
k\in[m]
.
That is

(4.1)

\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}=\left\{S_{k,i}(x)=\rho_{k,i}\cdot x+t_{k,i}\right\}_{k\in[m],i\in[l(k)+1]},\quad S_{k,i}|_{J_{k,i}}\equiv f_{k}|_{J_{k,i}}.

We organize the translation parts
\left\{t_{k,i}\right\}_{k\in[m],i\in[l(k)+1]}
of the mappings of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}

into a vector

(4.2)

\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{b},\boldsymbol{\tau},\boldsymbol{\rho}):=(t_{1,1},\dots,t_{1,l(1)+1},\dots,t_{m,1},\dots,t_{m,l(m)+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{L+m}.

If
b_{k,i}>0
for all
i\in[l(k)]
then the formula for
t_{k}
is simple:

(4.3)

t_{k,1}=\tau_{k}\mbox{ and }t_{k,i}=\tau_{k}+\sum\limits_{p=1}^{i}b_{k,i}\left(\rho_{k,i}-\rho_{k,i}\right).

Using this and an appropriate conjugation in the general case when
some of
b_{k,i}\leq 0
we obtain by simple calculation that the following fact holds:

Fact 4.1
.

For any fixed
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\mathfrak{R}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
,
consider the mapping
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}:\mathfrak{B}^{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{L+m}
defined by

(4.4)

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\mathfrak{b},\boldsymbol{\tau}):=\mathfrak{t},

where
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{L+m}
was defined in (
4.2
).
Then
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is a non-singular affine transformation. Hence

\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
and its inverse
\Phi_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{-1}
preserve Hausdorff and packing dimensions, and also preserve the sets of zero measure with respect to
\mathcal{L}^{L+m}
.

To get a more effective method of labeling the
f_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
functions’ intervals of linearity, and in this way the functions in
S_{\mathcal{F}}
, we introduce

(4.5)

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{(k,i):k\in[m]\mbox{ and }i\in[l(k)+1]\right\}.

Moreover, we write

\mathcal{A}^{N}:=\underbrace{\mathcal{A}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{A}}_{N}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
.
That is

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}=\left\{S_{a}\right\}_{a\in\mathcal{A}}
. Note that
\#\mathcal{A}=L+m
.
Recall that the slope
\rho_{a}
of
S_{a}
was defined in (
2.6
), and we define
t_{a}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.6)

S_{a}(x)=\rho_{a}x+t_{a}.

4.2.
Associated self-similar and graph-directed self-similar IFS for a regular CPLIFS

Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{m}
be a regular CPLIFS and let
N=N(\mathcal{F})\in\mathbb{N}
be the smallest
n
such that for all
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in[m]^{n}
the cylinder interval
I_{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathcal{F}}
does not contain any breaking points.
Then we say that
\mathcal{F}
is regular of order
N
.
The collection of regular CPLIFS of type
\boldsymbol{\ell}
and order
N

is denoted by
\mathrm{CPLIFS}_{\boldsymbol{\ell},N}
.

Given an
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{CPLIFS}_{\boldsymbol{\ell},N}
.
Let

5.
The proof of Theorems

,
2.1
assuming the Main Proposition

This section contains the proofs of our main results, under the assumption that the Main Proposition holds. First we prove some facts and a lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a regular CPLIFS. Then we have

(5.1)

\alpha(\mathcal{F})=s_{\mathcal{F}}.

To prove this lemma we will use the following facts.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a CPLIFS. We say that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
Bounded Distortion Property (BDP)
if there exist
0<C_{1}<C_{2}
such that

(5.2)

\forall n,\;\forall|\mathbf{j}|=n,\;\forall x,y\in I:\quad C_{1}\leq\frac{|f_{\mathbf{j}}^{\prime}(x)|}{|f_{\mathbf{j}}^{{}^{\prime}}(y)|}\leq C_{2}.

It is not true in general that a CPLIFS satisfies the BDP. For example, if a function’s fixed point coincides with one of its breaking points, then this condition trivially fails. However, in the regular case the BDP does hold.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a regular CPLIFS of order
N
. Then
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the bounded distortion property.

Proof.

As there are finitely many words of length at most
N
, the statement trivially holds for
n<N
.
Let
\mathbf{j}=j_{1}\dots j_{n}
with
n>N
. Let
x,y\in I
be arbitrary numbers.
First we investigate the derivative of
f_{\mathbf{j}}
at a given
x\in I
.

(5.3)

f_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}^{{}^{\prime}}(x)=f_{j_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}}(f_{\sigma\mathbf{j}}(x))\cdots f_{j_{n-N}}^{{}^{\prime}}(f_{\sigma^{n-N}\mathbf{j}}(x))f_{\sigma^{n-N}\mathbf{j}}^{{}^{\prime}}(x).

We said that
N
is the order of our CPLIFS. It means that the functions of
\mathcal{F}
have constant slopes over all the
N
cylinders. Hence
f_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}(\widehat{x})=f_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}(\widehat{y})
if
\widehat{x},\widehat{y}
are elements of the same cylinder interval of level
N
.

For
1\leq l\leq n-N
the words
\sigma^{l}\mathbf{j}
has length at least
N
. It implies that
f_{\sigma^{l}\mathbf{j}}(I)
is a subset of a cylinder interval of level
N
. Thus
f_{\sigma^{l}\mathbf{j}}(x)
and
f_{\sigma^{l}\mathbf{j}}(y)
are contained in the same cylinder interval of level
N
for
1\leq l\leq n-N
.

We conclude that for
1\leq l\leq n-N

(5.4)

f_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}(f_{\sigma^{l}\mathbf{j}}(x))=f_{k}^{{}^{\prime}}(f_{\sigma^{l}\mathbf{j}}(y)),

for any
k\in[m]
.

After substituting (
5.4
) into (
5.3
) we obtain that

(5.5)

7.
Appendix

The purpose of the Appendix is to make explicit an important consequence of Jordan and Rapaport’s result

[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
related to the dimension of the attractors of graph-directed self-similar systems on
\mathbb{R}
.

Given a self-similar graph-directed IFS
\mathcal{F}
with a strongly connected graph.
We will construct an ergodic and invariant measure
\mu
on the symbolic space which is a Markov measure with the following property:
the entropy of
\mu
divided by the Lypunov exponent of
\mu
is equal to
\alpha(\mathcal{F})
, where
\alpha(\mathcal{F})
was defined in Definition
3.7
. That is why
\mu
can be considered as the natural measure for the
self-similar graph-directed IFS
\mathcal{F}
.

Using this and the Jordan-Rapaport Theorem (Theorem
3.6
),
we obtain in Corollary
7.2
,
that the Hausdorff dimension of the push-forward measure of
\mu

is the minimum of
1
and
\alpha(\mathcal{F})
if ESC holds for the
self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}
associated to
\mathcal{F}
.
This can be considered as a generalization of part (b) of Hochman Theorem (Theorem
3.2
) for graph-directed self-similar systems.

Throughout the Appendix we use the notation of Section
3.2
.
Consider a self-similar graph-directed iterated function system
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{e}(x)=r_{e}x+t_{e}\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
with directed graph
\mathcal{G}=\{\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}\}
.
We may identify

(7.1)

\mathcal{V}=[q]=\left\{1,\dots,q\right\},\mbox{ and }\mathcal{E}=\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{M}\right\}.

We assume that
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be the set of infinite length directed paths in
\mathcal{G}
.
Moreover, for
l\in[q]
we introduce

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{\ell}:=\left\{\mathbf{e}=(e_{1},e_{2},\dots)\in\mathcal{E}_{\infty}:s(e_{1})=\ell\right\}.

We identify
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
with
\Sigma_{Z}:=\left\{(i_{1},i_{2},\dots)\in[M]^{\mathbb{N}}:z_{i_{k},i_{k+1}}=1,\>\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
, where

Z=(z_{i,j})_{i,j=1}^{m}
is an
M\times M
matrix such that

(7.2)

z_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{if $t(e_{i})=s(e_{j})$;}\\
0,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Set
\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(e_{1},e_{2},\dots):=\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{e_{1}\dots e_{n}}(0)
. It is clear that the non-empty compact sets

\left\{\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{E}^{\ell}_{\infty})\right\}_{\ell\in[q]}
satisfy (
3.21
). Hence

(7.3)

\Lambda_{i}=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\infty}),\mbox{ and }\Lambda=\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{E}_{\infty})

We define the natural pressure function as

(7.4)

\Phi(s)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\mathbf{e}\in\mathcal{E}^{n}}|F_{\mathbf{e}}(\Lambda_{t(e_{n})})|^{s},

where the existence of the limit follows from the same standard argument used in the proof of Fact
5.5
. We assumed that
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected, which implies that the matrix
C^{(s)}
we defined in (
3.23
) is irreducible.
It is easy to see that by the Perron Frobenius Theorem we have

(7.5)

\Phi(s)=\log\varrho(C^{(s)}),

where
\varrho
is the spectral radius. According to
[
14
, Theorem 2]

\Phi(s)
is continuous, strictly decreasing,
\Phi(0)>0
, and
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\Phi(s)=-\infty
, thus there exists a unique
0<s_{\mathcal{F}}
for which
\Phi(s_{\mathcal{F}})=0
.
For a self-similar GDIFS with strongly connected graph we call
s_{\mathcal{F}}
the natural dimension of the system.
By Definition
3.7
it is clear that

(7.6)

s_{\mathcal{F}}=\alpha.

Definition 7.1
.

We call
\mathcal{S}=\left\{S_{k}(x)=r_{e_{k}}x+t_{e_{k}}\right\}_{k=1}^{M}

the self-similar IFS associated with the self-similar GDIFS

\mathcal{F}
. Clearly,

(7.7)

S_{k}|_{\Lambda_{t(e_{k})}}\equiv F_{e_{k}}|_{\Lambda_{t(e_{k})}}.

Now we show that

3.8

(Theorem
3.6
) implies that under some conditions, part (b) of Hochman’s theorem holds for self-similar graph directed attractors as well, with

in the place of

.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{e}\right\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}
be a self-similar GDIFS on
\mathbb{R}
with directed graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
and attractor
\Lambda
. Further, assume that
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected, and that the self-similar IFS
\mathcal{S}
associated to

satisfies the ESC. Then

(7.8)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda=\min\{1,s_{\mathcal{F}}\}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected, we can apply Theorem
3.8
to obtain

(7.9)

\dim_{\rm H}\Lambda\leq\min\{\alpha,1\}\mbox{, since }\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}.

In order to prove the opposite inequality, first we define an ergodic invariant measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{V}^{\mathbb{N}}

from (

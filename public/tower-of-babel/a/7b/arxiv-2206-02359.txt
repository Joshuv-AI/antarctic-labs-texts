An inverse random source problem for the Helium production-diffusion equation driven by a fractional Brownian motion
Jing Li, Hao Cheng, Xiaoxiao Geng

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the prediction of the helium concentrations as function of a spatially variable source term perturbed by fractional Brownian motion. For the direct problem, we show that it is well-posed and has a unique mild solution under some conditions. For the inverse problem, the uniqueness and the instability are given. In the meanwhile, we determine the statistical properties of the source from the expectation and covariance of the final-time data u ⁡ ( r , T ) u(r,T) . Finally, numerical implements are given to verify the effectiveness of the proposed reconstruction.

Introduction

The inverse source problem (ISP) of heat conduction is one of the important research subjects of inverse problems for partial differential equations. It has many scientific and industrial applications such as the detection and control of urban pollutants and marine pollution sources, the migration of groundwater, and the exploitation and control of unknown heat sources in oil wells. They have been extensively investigated in the literature

For instance, a steel storage tank yard for blast furnace steelmaking in the metallurgical field can be simplified as a cylindrical region; the helium diffusion of apatite in the laboratory is considered as a spherical area. In

However, the source is not being deterministic in the complex physical and engineering phenomena. The reasons for uncertainty can be roughly divided into the following three aspects

Driven by these reasons, it is necessary to add random processes to the mathematical model. Since the solutions of stochastic differential equations are random functions, it is more important to study their statistical characteristics such as mean value, variance, and even higher order moments in many practical problems. In addition to instability, the ISP of stochastic equation is much more difficult than the deterministic problem due to randomness and uncertainty. to replace variable and obtain that

\displaystyle I_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{t-\tau}(t-\tau-q)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q)^{H-1}dq\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{t-\tau}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n}(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}-n}(q)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}dq\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{0}^{t-\tau}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n}(-1)^{n}\left(t-\tau\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}-n}(q)^{n+H-1}dq\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+H}\right]^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{2H-1}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2H}.

(3.10)

Substituting (3.6), (3.9) and (3.10) into (3.5), we can get when
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),
that

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{t}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\bigg]\lesssim t^{2H}.

For the case
H=\frac{1}{2},
it follows from It
\hat{o}
isometry (2.2) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{t}a(s)ds}dB^{\frac{1}{2}}(\tau)\right)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{t}a(s)ds}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}(t-\tau)}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}t}}{2\lambda_{n}a_{0}}\leq t.

For the case
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have from (2.3) that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{t}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{r}^{t}a(s)ds}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{t}a(s)ds}|r-u|^{2H-2}dudr

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|r-u|^{2H-2}dudr

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{(t-r)^{2H-1}}{2H-1}+\frac{r^{2H-1}}{2H-1}\right)dr

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{2H}.

Therefore, for any
H\in(0,1),
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{t}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\bigg]\lesssim t^{2H}.

Hence

\displaystyle S_{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[I_{n,2}^{2}(t)]\bigg)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}^{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{t}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\bigg]\right)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}^{2}t^{2H}dt=\frac{T^{2H+1}}{2H+1}\|g\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}.

(3.11)

From (3.4) and (3.11) we obtain the following conclution

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\|u\|_{L^{2}(D\times[0,T])}^{2}\bigg]\lesssim T^{3}\|f\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{2}+\frac{T^{2H+1}}{2H+1}\|g\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2},

which implies the stability estimate for the mild solution (2.8).

4.
The inverse problem

In this section, we attempt to reconstruct
f
and
\left|g\right|
from the empirical expectation and correlation of the final time data
u(r,T)
. From (2.1)-(2.3) and(2.5), it follows that the final time expectation and variance can be formulated as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(u_{n}(T))

\displaystyle=f_{n}\int_{0}^{T}h(\tau)e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(u_{n}(T))

\displaystyle=g_{n}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right],

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle Cov(u_{m}(T),u_{n}(T))=g_{m}g_{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right].

(4.3)

Below, we consider the uniqueness and the instability of the inverse problem, separately.

4.1
Uniqueness.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let assumption 1 hold. Then the source terms
f
and
g
up to sign, i.e.
\left|g\right|
can be uniquely determined by the data set

\{\mathbb{E}(u_{n}(T)),Cov(u_{m}(T),u_{n}(T)):m,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Proof.
Firstly, we need to prove for each fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}h(\tau)e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau\geq C_{1}>0.

(4.4)

setting
\tilde{\tau}=T-\tau,
from assumption 1 we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}h(\tau)e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}h(T-\tilde{\tau})e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{T-\tilde{\tau}}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{T}h(T-\tilde{\tau})e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{1}\tilde{\tau}}d\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle\geq C(h)\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{1}\tilde{\tau}}d\tilde{\tau}

\displaystyle=C(h)\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{1}T}}{\lambda_{n}a_{1}}:=C_{1}>0.

Secondly, we certificate for each fixed
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
there exists another constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right]\geq C_{2}>0.

(4.5)

We denote
\phi_{n}(\tau)=e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}
and

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\phi_{m}(\tau)dB^{H}(\tau)\int_{0}^{T}\phi_{n}(\tau)dB^{H}(\tau)\right]:=E_{mn}.

Next, we estimate
E_{mn}
for
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
H=\frac{1}{2}
, and
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, separately.

For
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),
we first note that the square integrable kernel
K_{H}
has the following form

K_{H}(T,s)=c_{H}\left[(\frac{T}{s})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(T-s)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}-(H-\frac{1}{2})s^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\int_{s}^{t}u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(u-s)^{h-\frac{1}{2}}du\right],

Since
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),

0<\tau<T
, it is obvious that
K_{H}(T,\tau)>0
. From the mean value theorem, it follows that

\displaystyle\quad\int_{s}^{T}(\phi_{n}(u)-\phi_{n}(\tau))c_{H}(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du

\displaystyle=c_{H}\int_{\tau}^{T}\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*})(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\qquad\quad(\tau<u_{k}^{*}<u<T)

\displaystyle=M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*}),

where

M_{H}(\tau)=c_{H}\int_{\tau}^{T}(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du>0.

Denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the inner product on
L^{2}(0,T)
, which given by

\langle\psi(\tau),\phi(\tau)\rangle=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(\tau)\phi(\tau)d\tau.

We have from (2.1) that

\displaystyle E_{mn}

\displaystyle=\langle K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{m}(\tau)+M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*}),K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)+M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*})\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle=\langle K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{m}(\tau),K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\langle M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*}),M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*})\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\quad+\langle K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{m}(\tau),M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*})\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\langle M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*}),K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}.

We consider the first term

\displaystyle\quad\langle K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{m}(\tau),K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}K_{H}^{2}(T,\tau)\phi_{m}(\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\geq e^{-(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n})a_{1}T}\int_{0}^{T}K_{H}^{2}(T,\tau)d\tau:=\tilde{c}_{1}>0.

We notice that
\phi_{n}(\tau)>0
and
\phi_{n}(\tau)
is a monotonically increasing function as
\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(\tau)=\lambda_{n}a(\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)>0
,
\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(\tau)
is a monotonically increasing function as
\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}(\tau)+\lambda_{n}^{2}a^{2}(\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)>0
. Accordingly we have
\phi_{n}(\tau)\geq\phi_{n}(0)>0
and
\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(\tau)\geq\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)>0
, for
0<\tau<T
. Hence

\langle M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*}),M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*})\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}\geq\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\int_{0}^{T}M_{H}^{2}(\tau)d\tau:=\tilde{c}_{2}>0.

Similarly, we have

\langle K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{m}(\tau),M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*})\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}\geq\phi_{m}(0)\phi_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\int_{0}^{T}K_{H}(T,\tau)M_{H}(\tau)d\tau:=\tilde{c}_{3}>0,

and

\langle M_{H}(\tau)\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(u_{k}^{*}),K_{H}(T,\tau)\phi_{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(0,T)}\geq\phi_{m}^{{}^{\prime}}(0)\phi_{n}(0)\int_{0}^{T}K_{H}(T,\tau)M_{H}(\tau)d\tau:=\tilde{c}_{4}>0.

From the above estimates we conclude that

E_{mn}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{4}\tilde{c}_{j}:=C_{2}>0.

For
H=\frac{1}{2},
from (2.2), we have

\displaystyle E_{mn}=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n})\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau\right]\geq\int_{0}^{T}e^{-(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n})a_{1}T}d\tau>0.

For
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1),
from (2.3), we have

\displaystyle E_{mn}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}\int_{r}^{T}a(s)ds}e^{-\lambda_{m}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}|r-u|^{2H-2}dudr

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}a_{1}T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{1}T}|r-u|^{2H-2}dudr

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}e^{-(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n})a_{1}T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|r-u|^{2H-2}dudr

\displaystyle=T^{2H}e^{-(\lambda_{m}+\lambda_{n})a_{1}T}>0.

Finally, we have from
f,g\in L^{2}(D)
that

f(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{k}\omega_{n}(r),\qquad g(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{k}\omega_{n}(r),

which gives that

g^{2}(x)=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\omega_{m}(r)\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\omega_{n}(r)\right)=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n}g_{n}\omega_{m}(r)\omega_{n}(r).

Combining (4.1)-(4.5), we can obtain the uniqueness of the inverse problem, which completes the proof.

4.1
Instability.

Theorem 4.2
.

The inverse problem is ill-posedness to reconstruct the source terms f and
\pm g.
Moreover, if
h\in L^{\infty}(0,T)
is a nonnegative function and its support has a positive measure, then the following estimates hold

\Big|\int_{0}^{T}h(\tau)e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}},

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{p}},

where p=min
{
2H,1
}
.

Proof.
First, it is clear to note that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau\leq\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}d\tau=\frac{1-e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}}{\lambda_{n}a_{0}}<\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}a_{0}},

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{T}h(\tau)e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\quad\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

(4.6)

which shows that it is unstable to recover
f
due to (4.1).

Second,we discuss the instability of recovering
g_{n}^{2}
, which is equivalent to the instability of recovering
|g|.
Similarly, we present the three different cases
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
H=\frac{1}{2}
and
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, separately.

For the case
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we consider (3.6) with
t=T
and the estimate of
I_{j}(T),

j=1,2,3.
For
I_{1}(T),
a simple calculation yields

\displaystyle I_{1}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}(\frac{\tau}{T})^{1-2H}(T-\tau)^{2H-1}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}(\frac{\tau}{T})^{1-2H}(T-\tau)^{2H-1}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}(T-\tau)^{2H-1}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\frac{T^{2H-1}}{2H}.

For
I_{2}(T),
we have

\displaystyle I_{2}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\tau^{1-2H}\left(\int_{\tau}^{T}u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\right)^{2}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}\tau^{1-2H}\left(\int_{\tau}^{T}u^{H-\frac{3}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\right)^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}\tau^{1-2H}(T^{2H-1}-\tau^{2H-1})^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}\tau^{1-2H}(T^{4H-2}+\tau^{4H-2})d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}\int_{0}^{T}\tau^{2H-1}d\tau\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\frac{T^{2H-1}}{2H}.

For
I_{3}(T)
, we divide it as follows

\displaystyle I_{3}(T)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{\tau}^{T}\bigg(e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}-e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}\bigg)(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{\tau}^{t_{*}}\bigg(e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}-e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}\bigg)(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\bigg(e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}-e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}\bigg)(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\left[\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{*}}\bigg(e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}-e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}\bigg)(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\left[\int_{\tau+t_{*}}^{T}\bigg(e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}-e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}\bigg)(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle:=K_{1}+K_{2}+K_{3}+K_{4},

where
t_{*}\in(0,T)
will be chosen later. About
K_{1}
and
K_{2},
from the differential mean value theorem we obtain that

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}a(u_{*})e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u_{*}}^{T}a(s)ds}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{\tau}^{t_{*}}(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a(u_{*})}{\int_{u_{*}}^{T}a(s)ds}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{\tau}^{t_{*}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{\tau}^{t_{*}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t_{*}}(t_{*}-\tau)^{2H-1}d\tau\lesssim t_{*}^{2H+2},

similarly, it is easy to get

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\left[\int_{\tau}^{\tau+t_{*}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t_{*}}^{T}t_{*}^{2H+1}d\tau\lesssim t_{*}^{2H+1}.

About
K_{2}
and
K_{4},
we obtain from straightforward calculations that

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[\int_{t_{*}}^{T}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\left[(T-\tau)^{2H-1}-(t_{*}-\tau)^{2H-1}\right]d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left[T^{2H}-(T-t_{*})^{2H}+t_{*}^{2H}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H},

and

\displaystyle K_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\left[\int_{\tau+t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}(\frac{u}{\tau})^{H-\frac{1}{2}}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\left[\int_{\tau+t_{*}}^{T}(u-\tau)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}du\right]^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\left[(T-\tau)^{2H-1}+t_{*}^{2H-1}\right]d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}(T-t_{*})^{2H}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H-1}.

Combining the above estimates about
K_{1}-K_{4}
, and choosing
t_{*}=1/\lambda_{n},
we get

I_{3}(T)\lesssim t_{*}^{2H+2}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H}+t_{*}^{2H+1}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}t_{*}^{2H-1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2H}}.

From the above estimates for
I_{1},I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we get for
H\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\frac{T^{2H-1}}{H}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2H}}.

For the case
H=\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{\frac{1}{2}}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau\leq\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda_{n}a_{0}T}}{2\lambda_{n}a_{0}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}.

For the case
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{u}^{T}a(s)ds}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}|u-\tau|^{2H-2}dud\tau

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{H}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}|u-\tau|^{2H-2}dud\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{T-t_{*}}\int_{0}^{T-t_{*}}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}|u-\tau|^{2H-2}dud\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{T-t_{*}}^{T}\int_{T-t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}|u-\tau|^{2H-2}dud\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{T-t_{*}}^{T}\int_{0}^{T-t_{*}}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}|u-\tau|^{2H-2}dud\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T-t_{*}}\int_{T-t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-u)}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}(T-\tau)}|u-\tau|^{2H-2}dud\tau

\displaystyle:=Q_{1}+Q_{2}+Q_{3}+Q_{4}.

Substitute variables
u^{{}^{\prime}}=T-u
and
\tau^{{}^{\prime}}=T-\tau
for the above formula we get

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}u^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}\tau^{{}^{\prime}}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}a_{0}u^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}a_{0}\tau^{{}^{\prime}}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\frac{1}{u^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\tau^{{}^{\prime}}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\frac{1}{t_{*}^{2}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\frac{1}{t_{*}^{2}},

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}u^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}\tau^{{}^{\prime}}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{*}^{2H},

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}u^{{}^{\prime}}}e^{-\lambda_{n}a_{0}\tau^{{}^{\prime}}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}a_{0}u^{{}^{\prime}}}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}t_{*}}\int_{0}^{t_{*}}\int_{t_{*}}^{T}|u^{{}^{\prime}}-\tau^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2H-2}du^{{}^{\prime}}d\tau^{{}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{T^{2H-2}}{\lambda_{n}t_{*}}(T-t_{*})t_{*}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}.

Due to the symmetry of
u
and
\tau
, it is easy to obtain that
Q_{4}=Q_{3}
. From the above estimates about
Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3},Q_{4}
and chose
t_{*}=\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
we get that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\frac{1}{t_{*}^{2}}+t_{*}^{2H}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}.

From the above estimates for
H\in(0,1)
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right)^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{2H}}\quad\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

(4.7)

Combining (4.6) and (4.7) can demonstrate the inverse problem is unstable to recover
f
and
|g|
.

5.
Numerical experiments

In this section, we present some numerical experiments for the one-dimensional problem where
D=[0,\pi].
Since the difficulty in getting the exact solution of the problem (1.1), it is assumed that
f(r),

h(t),
and
g(r)
in the source term are known, and the direct problem is solved by the finite difference method, the final value data
u(r,T)
can be obtained. Adding the random disturbance to it, we obtain the noisy data

u^{\delta}(r,T)=u(r,T)+\epsilon u(r,T)\cdot\left(2rand\Big(size\big(u(r,T)\big)\Big)-1\right)

where
\epsilon>0
is relative error level,
\delta=\|u^{\delta}(r,T)-u(r,T)\|.

For some fixed integers
M
and
N
, we define the time and space step-sizes
h_{t}=T/M
,
h_{r}=\pi/N
and nodes

t_{n}=nh_{t},\quad n\in\{0,1,\cdots,M\},\qquad r_{i}=ih,\quad i\in\{0,1,\cdots,N\}.

First, we present the numerical solution of the direct problem. We discretize time with the first order backward difference and space with the second order central difference to generate the synthetic data. Let
u_{i}^{n}
be the numerical approximation to
u(r_{i},t_{n})
. Then we obtain the following explicit scheme:

\displaystyle\frac{u_{i}^{n}-u_{i}^{n-1}}{h_{t}}

\displaystyle=a(t_{n})\left(\frac{u_{i+1}^{n}-2u_{i}^{n}+u_{i-1}^{n}}{h^{2}}+\frac{2}{r_{i}}\frac{u_{i+1}^{n}-u_{i-1}^{n}}{2h}\right)

\displaystyle+f(r_{i})h(t_{n})+g(r_{i})\frac{B^{H}(t_{n})-B^{H}(t_{n-1})}{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}.

(5.1)

where
i=1,2,\cdots,N-1,

n=1,2,\cdots,M.
we have the matrix equation corresponding to the discrete format

AU^{n}=\frac{h^{2}}{h_{t}}U^{n-1}+h^{2}F+\frac{h^{2}}{h_{t}}B+C,\quad n=1,2,\cdots,M,

where

\displaystyle F=(f(r_{1})h(t_{n}),f(r_{2})h(t_{n}),\cdots,f(r_{N-1})h(t_{n}))^{T},

\displaystyle B=\Big(g(r_{1})\left[B^{H}(t_{n})-B^{H}(t_{n-1})\right],g(r_{2})\left[B^{H}(t_{n})-B^{H}(t_{n-1})\right],\cdots,g(r_{N-1})\left[B^{H}(t_{n})-B^{H}(t_{n-1})\right]\Big)^{T},

\displaystyle C=\left(-a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{1}}-1\right)u_{0}^{n},0,\cdots,0,a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{N-1}}+1\right)u_{N}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle U^{n}=(u_{1}^{n},u_{2}^{n},\cdots,u_{N-1}^{n})^{T},\quad n=1,2,\cdots,M-1,

Matrix A is a tridiagonal matrix

\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\frac{h^{2}}{h_{t}}+2a(t_{n})&-a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{1}}+1\right)&0&0&\cdots\\
a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{2}}-1\right)&\frac{h^{2}}{h_{t}}+2a(t_{n})&-a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{2}}+1\right)&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\cdots&0&a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{N-2}}-1\right)&\frac{h^{2}}{h_{t}}+2a(t_{n})&-a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{N-2}}+1\right)\\
\cdots&0&0&a(t_{n})\left(\frac{h}{r_{N-1}}-1\right)&\frac{h^{2}}{h_{t}}+2a(t_{n})\end{array}\right]

Next, we give the numerical solution of the inverse problem. For simplicity, we consider the reconstruction of
g^{2}

instead of
\left|g\right|
. Recall that functions
f,g\in L^{2}(D)
have the expanisions

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}\omega_{n},\qquad g^{2}=\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}g_{m}g_{n}\omega_{m}\omega_{n},

(5.2)

and coefficients
f_{n}
and
g_{m}g_{n}

\displaystyle f_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}(u_{n}(T))}{\int_{0}^{T}h(\tau)e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}d\tau},

\displaystyle g_{m}g_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{Cov(u_{m}(T),u_{n}(T))}{\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{m}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\lambda_{n}\int_{\tau}^{T}a(s)ds}dB^{H}(\tau)\right]},

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the inverse random source problem of Helium production-diffusion equation driven by fractional Brownian motions. For the direct problem, we obtain some well-posed results. For the inverse source problem, we give the uniqueness and the instability results. In the numerical experiments, three different examples shows the effectiveness and feasibility of the method.

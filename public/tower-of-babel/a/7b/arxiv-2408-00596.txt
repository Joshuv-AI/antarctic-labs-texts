Abstract
. We prove strong hybrid subconvex bounds simultaneously in the q q and t t aspects for L L -functions of selfdual GL 3 \GL_{3} cusp forms twisted by primitive Dirichlet characters. We additionally prove analogous hybrid subconvex bounds for central values of certain GL 3 × GL 2 \GL_{3}\times\GL_{2} Rankin–Selberg L L -functions. The subconvex bounds that we obtain are strong in the sense that, modulo current knowledge on estimates for the second moment of GL 3 \GL_{3} L L -functions, they are the natural limit of the first moment method pioneered by Li and by Blomer. The method of proof relies on an explicit GL 3 × GL 2 ↭ GL 4 × GL 1 \GL_{3}\times\GL_{2}\leftrightsquigarrow\GL_{4}\times\GL_{1} spectral reciprocity formula, which relates a GL 2 \GL_{2} moment of GL 3 × GL 2 \GL_{3}\times\GL_{2} Rankin–Selberg L L -functions to a GL 1 \GL_{1} moment of GL 4 × GL 1 \GL_{4}\times\GL_{1} Rankin–Selberg L L -functions. A key additional input is a Lindelöf-on-average upper bound for the second moment of Dirichlet L L -functions restricted to a coset, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

q

(1)

For

L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,F\otimes\chi\right)\ll_{F,\varepsilon}(q(|t|+1))^{\frac{3}{5}+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{q^{2/5}}{q_{1}^{1/2}(|t|+1)^{1/10}}+\frac{q_{1}^{1/8}}{(q(|t|+1))^{1/10}}\right).

(2)

Let

be a Hecke–Maaß newform of weight

q^{2}
, principal nebentypus, and Laplacian eigenvalue

L\left(\frac{1}{2},F\otimes f\right)\ll_{F,\varepsilon}(q(|t_{f}|+1))^{\frac{6}{5}+\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{q^{4/5}}{q_{1}(|t_{f}|+1)^{1/5}}+\frac{q_{1}^{1/4}}{(q(|t_{f}|+1))^{1/5}}\right).

(3)

Let
f

q^{2}
, and principal nebentypus, and suppose that

q

These bounds imply hybrid subconvexity simultaneously in the

, or

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\mid q_{1}q_{2}\\

1.2.2.
A Sketch of the Proof

The proof of this spectral reciprocity formula follows the approach of the second author and Khan in
[
HK25
]
, where a related result is proven in the special case
q_{1}=q_{2}=1
and
U=T
. We replace the central value
1/2
with a complex parameter
w
with large real part, which allows us to replace the
L
-function
L(w,F\otimes f\otimes\chi_{1})
with its absolutely convergent Dirichlet series. After interchanging the order of summation, we apply the Kuznetsov and Petersson formulæ. On the right-hand side of these formulæ, the delta terms give us a main term, while for the Kloosterman terms, we open up the Kloosterman sums, interchange the order of summation, and apply the
\GL_{3}
Voronoĭ summation formula. After some careful rearrangements, using both additive reciprocity and analytic reciprocity (see (
2.33
) and (
2.34
)), we find that the Kloosterman terms give rise to a dual moment involving two Dirichlet series, one representing
L(1/2+it,F\otimes\psi)
and the other representing
L(2w-1/2-it,\overline{\psi})
, as well as a distinguished character sum resembling
g(\chi_{1},\psi_{1})
. This gives us a spectral reciprocity formula for
L(w,F\otimes f\otimes\chi_{1})
with
\Re(w)
sufficiently large, which we state in
Proposition
3.1
. To obtain the desired spectral reciprocity formula stated in
Theorem
6.4
, we holomorphically extend this identity to the central value
w=1/2
.

1.2.3.
A Comparison to Alternate Approaches

Instead of using the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for
L(w,F\otimes f\otimes\chi_{1})
with
\Re(w)>1
followed by analytic continuation to
w=1/2
, a more traditional approach towards proving

1.2
is to use the approximate functional equation for

\GL_{2}
moment of

]
. When

A major advantage of our analytic continuation approach is that it circumvents this issue altogether; in particular, we do not require that

1.4.
Improvements

The first term on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) is related to the size of the family of automorphic forms over which we average. The size of the family is minimised by taking
q_{2}
and
U
to be as small as possible, namely
q_{2}=U=1
. It was noted in
[
LNQ23
]
by the third and fourth authors and Qi, however, that minimising the size of the family comes at the cost of enlarging the size of the dual moment. They showed that if one slightly enlarges the size of the family by instead taking
U=T^{1/5}
, this enlarging of the size of the dual moment is mitigated, leading to improved subconvex bounds.
Theorem
1.2
introduces this trick additionally in the level aspect: by allowing for the possibility that
q_{2}>1
, we may enlarge the size of the family in the level aspect, which leads to improved subconvex bounds.

The size of the second term on the right-hand size of (
1.3
) is intimately related to bounds for the second moment

(1.4)

\sum_{\psi\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q}}\int_{-T}^{T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,F\otimes\psi\right)\right|^{2}\,dt

for a positive integer
q
and for
T\geq 1
. The generalised Lindelöf hypothesis implies the conditional bound
O_{F,\varepsilon}((qT)^{1+\varepsilon})
for this hybrid second moment. A standard application of Gallagher’s hybrid large sieve yields only the weaker unconditional bound

, as we show in

. Were we able to improve this to

, we would in turn be able to improve the size of the second term on the right-hand size of (

) to

O_{F,\varepsilon}\left(q_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}}q_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}}U^{\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}(q_{1}q_{2}T)^{\varepsilon}\right).

In turn, this would yield strengthenings of the subconvex bounds in

. We note that when

[

2.
Summation Formulæ

2.1.
Kuznetsov and Petersson Formulæ

We first state the Kuznetsov and Petersson formulæ for automorphic forms of arbitrary level, where the spectral side is explicitly written in terms of Hecke eigenvalues of newforms. Before we state these formulæ, we explain some notation. Given an
L
-function
L(s,\pi)
, we write
L_{p}(s,\pi)
for the
p
-component of the Euler product of
L(s,\pi)
and define
L_{q}(s,\pi)\coloneqq\prod_{p\mid q}L_{p}(s,\pi)
and
L^{q}(s,\pi)\coloneqq L(s,\pi)/L_{q}(s,\pi)
. Given a cusp form
f
either in
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\ast}(q,\chi)
or in
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}^{\ast}(q,\chi)
, we write
\lambda_{f}(n)
for the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue of
f
. Moreover, given a pair of Dirichlet characters
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
, we define
\lambda_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(n,t)\coloneqq\sum_{ab=n}\psi_{1}(a)a^{-it}\psi_{2}(b)b^{it}
, which is the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue of the Eisenstein series associated to this pair.

Lemma 2.1

(Kuznetsov formula)
.

Let
q
be a positive integer and let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
q_{\chi}\mid q
. Let
h
be an even function that is holomorphic in the strip
|\Im(t)|<1/2+\delta
in which it satisfies
h(t)\ll(1+|t|)^{-2-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
. Then for
(mn,q)=1
, we have that

(2.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\mid q\\
q^{\prime}\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\alpha(q,q^{\prime},q_{\chi})\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\ast}(q^{\prime},\chi)}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{f}(m)}\lambda_{f}(n)}{L^{q}(1,\ad f)}h(t_{f})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\mid q\\
q^{\prime}\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\alpha(q,q^{\prime},q_{\chi})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q}\\
\psi_{1}\psi_{2}=\chi\\
q_{\psi_{1}}q_{\psi_{2}}=q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(m,t)}\lambda_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(n,t)}{L^{q}(1+2it,\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}})L^{q}(1-2it,\overline{\psi_{1}}\psi_{2})}h(t)\,dt\\
=\delta_{m,n}q\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(r)r\tanh\pi r\,dr+q\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c=1\\
c\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{S_{\chi}(m,n;c)}{c}(\mathscr{K}h)\left(\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right).

Here

\displaystyle\alpha(q,q^{\prime},q_{\chi})\coloneqq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid q^{\prime}\\
p\nmid\frac{q}{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\parallel q\\
p\nmid q_{\chi}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right),\qquad S_{\chi}(m,n;c)\coloneqq\sum_{d\in(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(d)e\left(\frac{md+n\overline{d}}{c}\right),

\displaystyle(\mathscr{K}h)(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{J}_{r}^{+}(x)h(r)r\tanh\pi r\,dr,\qquad\mathcal{J}_{r}^{+}(x)\coloneqq\frac{\pi i}{\sinh\pi r}\left(J_{2ir}(4\pi x)-J_{-2ir}(4\pi x)\right).

Proof.

This is stated in
[
Hum18
, Proposition 3.17]
except with the left-hand side written in a slightly different form. The first term on the left-hand side is instead written in the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\mid q\\
q^{\prime}\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\ast}(q^{\prime},\chi)}\frac{2q\xi_{f}\left|\rho_{f}(1)\right|^{2}}{\cosh\pi t_{f}}\overline{\lambda_{f}(m)}\lambda_{f}(n)h(t_{f}),

where
\rho_{f}(1)
denotes the first Fourier–Whittaker coefficient of
f
. Here
\xi_{f}
is a certain constant that, by
[
Hum18
, Lemma 4.1]
, is equal to

\xi_{f}=\sum_{n\mid\left(\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\infty}}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}}{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\parallel\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}\\
p\nmid q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right).

Moreover, from
[
Hum18
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have that

\frac{\cosh\pi t_{f}}{|\rho_{f}(1)|^{2}}=\pi\vol\left(\Gamma_{0}(q)\backslash\mathbb{H}\right)\Res_{s=1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}}{n^{s}}=2qL^{q}(1,\ad f)\sum_{n\mid q^{\infty}}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}}{n},

where the second equality follows from the fact that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
(n,q)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}}{n^{s}}=\frac{\zeta^{q}(s)L^{q}(s,\ad f)}{\zeta^{q}(2s)}.

Since

|\lambda_{f}(p^{\beta})|^{2}=\begin{cases}1&if $p\mid q^{\prime}$ and $p\nmid\frac{q^{\prime}}{q_{\chi}}$,\\
\frac{1}{p^{\beta}}&if $p\parallel q^{\prime}$ and $p\nmid q_{\chi}$,\\
0&if $p^{2}\mid q^{\prime}$ and $p\mid\frac{q^{\prime}}{q_{\chi}}$,\end{cases}

we have that

\frac{\sum_{n\mid q^{\infty}}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}n^{-1}}{\sum_{n\mid\left(\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\infty}}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}n^{-1}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\mid q^{\prime\infty}\\
\left(n,\frac{q}{q^{\prime}}\right)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}}{n}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid q^{\prime}\\
p\nmid\frac{q}{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-1}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\parallel q^{\prime}\\
p\nmid\frac{qq_{\chi}}{q^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{1-p^{-2}}.

In particular,

\frac{2q\xi_{f}\left|\rho_{f}(1)\right|^{2}}{\cosh\pi t_{f}}=\frac{\alpha(q,q^{\prime},q_{\chi})}{L^{q}(1,\ad f)}.

Similarly, the second term on the left-hand side is written in a form that, using the theory of Eisenstein newforms developed in
[
You19
]
, can be written as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\mid q\\
q^{\prime}\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q}\\
\psi_{1}\psi_{2}=\chi\\
q_{\psi_{1}}q_{\psi_{2}}=q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2q\xi_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2};t}\left|\rho_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(1,t)\right|^{2}}{\cosh\pi t}\overline{\lambda_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(m,t)}\lambda_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(n,t)h(t)\,dt,

where
\rho_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(1,t)
denotes the first Fourier–Whittaker coefficient of the Eisenstein newform associated to the pair of Dirichlet characters
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
. An analogous argument shows that

\frac{2q\xi_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2};t}\left|\rho_{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}(1,t)\right|^{2}}{\cosh\pi t}=\frac{\alpha(q,q^{\prime},q_{\chi})}{L^{q}(1+2it,\psi_{1}\overline{\psi_{2}})L^{q}(1-2it,\overline{\psi_{1}}\psi_{2})}.\qed

Lemma 2.3

(Petersson formula)
.

Let
q
be a positive integer and let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
q_{\chi}\mid q
. Let
h^{\mathrm{hol}}:2\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a sequence that satisfies
h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k)\ll k^{-2-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
. Then for
(mn,q)=1
, we have that

(2.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\mid q\\
q^{\prime}\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q_{\chi}}\end{subarray}}\alpha(q,q^{\prime},q_{\chi})\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}^{\ast}(q^{\prime},\chi)}\frac{\overline{\lambda_{f}(m)}\lambda_{f}(n)}{L^{q}(1,\ad f)}h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k_{f})\\
=\delta_{m,n}q\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=2\\
k\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{k-1}{2\pi^{2}}h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k)+q\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c=1\\

where

Proof.

This follows in the same way as for the Kuznetsov formula.

2.2.
The
\GL_{3}

We record some standard facts, which follow from Stirling’s formula, regarding certain products of gamma functions that appear in the
\GL_{3}
Voronoĭ summation formula.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
BK19
, Section 2.2 and Lemma 4]
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{C}
, define

(2.6)

G^{\pm}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}G_{0}(s)\mp\frac{1}{2i}G_{1}(s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\exp\left(\pm\frac{\pi is}{2}\right),

where for
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)\coloneqq\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
,

G_{j}(s)\coloneqq\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+j)}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(1-s+j)}=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\times\begin{cases}\cos\frac{\pi s}{2}&if $j=0$,\\
\sin\frac{\pi s}{2}&if $j=1$.\end{cases}

Then
G^{\pm}(s)
is meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
with simple poles at
s=-\ell
for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Moreover, if
s=\sigma+i\tau
is a bounded distance away from such a pole, we have that

(2.7)

G^{\pm}(s)\ll_{\sigma}(1+|\tau|)^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\pi\Omega^{\pm}(\tau)},

where

\Omega^{\pm}(\tau)\coloneqq\begin{cases}0&if $\sgn(\tau)=\mp$,\\
|\tau|&if $\sgn(\tau)=\pm$.\end{cases}

For
|\tau|\geq 1
and any
M>0
, there exists a smooth function
\widetilde{g}_{\sigma,M}
satisfying
|\tau|^{m}\widetilde{g}_{\sigma,M}^{(m)}(\tau)\ll_{m,\sigma,M}1
such that

(2.8)

G^{\pm}(s)=|\tau|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(i\tau\log\frac{|\tau|}{2\pi e}\right)\widetilde{g}_{\sigma,M}(\tau)+O_{\sigma,M}(|\tau|^{-M}).

Similarly, let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\in(i\mathbb{R})^{3}
, and for
s\in\mathbb{C}
, define

(2.9)

\mathscr{G}_{\mu}^{\pm}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\prod_{j=1}^{3}G_{0}(s+\mu_{j})\pm\frac{1}{2i}\prod_{j=1}^{3}G_{1}(s+\mu_{j}).

Then
\mathscr{G}_{\mu}^{\pm}(s)
is meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
with simple poles at
s=-\mu_{j}-\ell
for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Moreover, if
s=\sigma+i\tau
is a bounded distance away from such a pole, we have that

(2.10)

\mathscr{G}_{\mu}^{\pm}(s)\ll_{\sigma,\mu}(1+|\tau|)^{3\sigma-\frac{3}{2}}e^{-3\pi\Omega^{\pm}(\tau)}.

For
|\tau|\geq 1
and any
M>0
, there exists a smooth function
g_{\sigma,M,\mu}
satisfying
|\tau|^{m}g_{\sigma,M,\mu}^{(m)}(\tau)\ll_{m,\sigma,\mu,M}1
such that

(2.11)

\mathscr{G}_{\mu}^{\pm}(s)=|\tau|^{3\sigma-\frac{3}{2}}\exp\left(3i\tau\log\frac{|\tau|}{2\pi e}\right)g_{\sigma,M,\mu}(\tau)+O_{\sigma,M,\mu}(|\tau|^{-M}).

We make use of the
\GL_{3}
Voronoĭ summation formula due to Miller and Schmid
[
MS06
]
in a Dirichlet series form recorded by Blomer and Khan
[
BK19
]
. This involves the Kloosterman sum
S(m,n;c)\coloneqq S_{\chi_{0(1)}}(m,n;c)
, where we write
\chi_{0(q)}
to denote the principal character modulo
q
.

Lemma 2.12

(
\GL_{3}
Voronoĭ Summation Formula
[
BK19
, Section 4]
)
.

Given a Hecke–Maaß cusp form
F
for
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
with Hecke eigenvalues
A_{F}(\ell,n)
, define the Voronoĭ series

(2.13)

\displaystyle\Phi_{F}(c,d,\ell;w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{F}(\ell,n)}{n^{w}}e\left(\frac{n\overline{d}}{c}\right),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Xi_{F}(c,d,\ell;w)

\displaystyle\coloneqq c\sum_{n_{1}\mid c\ell}\sum_{n_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{F}(n_{2},n_{1})}{n_{2}n_{1}}S\left(d\ell,n_{2};\frac{c\ell}{n_{1}}\right)\left(\frac{n_{2}n_{1}^{2}}{c^{3}\ell}\right)^{-w},

where
c,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in(\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. The former series converges absolutely for
\Re(w)>1
, while the latter series converges absolutely for
\Re(w)>0
, and both series extend holomorphically to the entire complex plane, in which they satisfy the functional equation

(2.15)

\Phi_{F}(c,d,\ell;w)=\sum_{\pm}\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{F}}^{\pm}(1-w)\Xi_{F}(c,\pm d,\ell;-w)

for
\Re(w)<0
, with
\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{F}}^{\pm}
as in (
2.9
) with
\mu=\mu_{F}
equal to the spectral parameters of
F
. Moreover, we have the bounds

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Phi_{F}(c,d,\ell;w)

\displaystyle\ll_{F,\varepsilon}\begin{cases}(c^{3}\ell(1+|\Im(w)|^{3}))^{\varepsilon}\max_{a\mid\ell}|A_{F}(a,1)|&if $\Re(w)>1$,\\
(c^{3}\ell(1+|\Im(w)|^{3}))^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\Re(w))+\varepsilon}\max_{a\mid\ell}|A_{F}(a,1)|&if $0\leq\Re(w)\leq 1$,\\
(c^{3}\ell(1+|\Im(w)|^{3}))^{\frac{1}{2}(1-2\Re(w))+\varepsilon}\max_{a\mid\ell}|A_{F}(a,1)|&if $\Re(w)<0$,\end{cases}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\Xi_{F}(c,d,\ell;w)

\displaystyle\ll_{F,\varepsilon}\begin{cases}(c^{3}\ell)^{\frac{1}{2}(1+2\Re(w))+\varepsilon}(1+|\Im(w)|^{3})^{\varepsilon}\max_{a\mid\ell}|A_{F}(a,1)|&if $\Re(w)>0$,\\
(c^{3}\ell)^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\Re(w))+\varepsilon}(1+|\Im(w)|^{3}))^{-\frac{1}{2}\Re(w)+\varepsilon}\max_{a\mid\ell}|A_{F}(a,1)|&if $-1\leq\Re(w)\leq 0$,\\
(c^{3}\ell)^{\varepsilon}(1+|\Im(w)|^{3}))^{-\Re(w)-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\max_{a\mid\ell}|A_{F}(a,1)|&if $\Re(w)<-1$.\end{cases}

2.3.
An Application of the
\GL_{3}
Voronoĭ Summation Formula

Given Dirichlet characters
\chi,\psi
modulo
q\in\mathbb{N}
and nonzero integers
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, we define the character sum

(2.18)

\mathcal{V}_{\chi}(\psi;m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r)\coloneqq\frac{1}{q}\sum_{t,u\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}}\tau(\overline{\chi},t+m_{2}u)\overline{\chi}(rt+m_{1}m_{2})\tau(\chi,u)\chi(ru-m_{1})\tau(\overline{\psi},m_{3}t),

where
\tau(\chi,a)
denotes the generalised Gauss sum

(2.19)

\tau(\chi,a)\coloneqq\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}}\chi(b)e\left(\frac{ab}{q}\right).

Note that the generalised Gauss sum
\tau(\chi,a)
satisfies

(2.20)

\tau(\chi,an)=\overline{\chi}(n)\tau(\chi,a),\qquad(n,q)=1.

We do
not
assume that
\chi
is primitive; in particular, it need not be the case that
|\tau(\chi,1)|=\sqrt{q}
. We briefly mention that the character sum (
2.18
) is trivially bounded in absolute value by
q^{4}
; in practice, much better bounds are valid, as we discuss in
Section
4
.

We record here a useful fact about Kloosterman sums, namely that they satisfy a twisted multiplicativity property. If
c_{1},c_{2}
are positive integers for which
(c_{1},c_{2})=1
and if
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
are Dirichlet characters modulo
c_{1},c_{2}
, then

(2.21)

\begin{split}S_{\chi_{1}\chi_{2}}(m,n;c_{1}c_{2})&=S_{\chi_{1}}(m\overline{c_{2}},n\overline{c_{2}};c_{1})S_{\chi_{2}}(m\overline{c_{1}},n\overline{c_{1}};c_{2})\\
&=\chi_{1}(c_{2})\chi_{2}(c_{1})S_{\chi_{1}}(m,n\overline{c_{2}^{2}};c_{1})S_{\chi_{2}}(m,n\overline{c_{1}^{2}};c_{2}).\end{split}

We now prove an identity relating sums of Kloosterman sums and Voronoĭ series to integrals of
L
-functions, which further involves a certain finite Euler product that includes the character sum (
2.18
). This identity is central to the proof of
\GL_{3}\times\GL_{2}\leftrightsquigarrow\GL_{4}\times\GL_{1}
spectral reciprocity.

Lemma 2.22
.

Let
F
be a Hecke–Maaß cusp form for
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, let
q
be a positive integer, and let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
q
. Then for
w=u+iv
and
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
u>3/2
and
5-6u<\sigma<-2u-1
, we have that

(2.23)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell^{\prime}=1\\
(\ell^{\prime},q)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{\ell^{\prime}}^{2w}}\sum_{c_{0}\mid q^{\infty}}c_{0}^{s-2}\sum_{c^{\prime}\mid\ell^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}^{s+2w-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}/c^{\prime}c_{0}q\mathbb{Z}\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(a)S_{\overline{\chi}^{2}}(1,a;c^{\prime}c_{0}q)\\
\times\sum_{c_{1}\mid c^{\prime}c_{0}q}\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/c_{1}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}e\left(\frac{a\overline{b}}{c_{1}}\right)\Xi_{F}\left(c_{1},\mp b,\frac{\ell^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}};-\frac{s}{2}-w\right)\\
=\frac{q^{1-s}}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\psi\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{q}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}L\left(\frac{1}{2}+z,\widetilde{F}\otimes\psi\right)L\left(2w-\frac{1}{2}-z,\overline{\psi}\right)\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}(\psi;w,z)\\
\times\psi(\mp 1)G^{\mp}\left(\frac{s}{2}+w-\frac{1}{2}+z\right)\,dz.

Here
G^{\pm}
is as in (
2.6
), while
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is the contour consisting of the straight lines connecting the points
x_{1}-i\infty
,
x_{1}-i(\tau/2+v+1)
,
1/2-s/2-w+\delta
,
x_{1}-i(\tau/2+v-1)
,
x_{1}+i\infty
, with
1/2<x_{1}<-\sigma/2-u
and
0<\delta<\sigma/2+3u-2
, and

(2.24)

\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}(\psi;w,z)\coloneqq\sum_{c_{0}\mid q^{\infty}}\frac{1}{\varphi(c_{0}q)^{2}c_{0}^{2w-\frac{1}{2}-z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c_{1,0}c_{2,0}d_{0}n_{1,0}=q\\
(c_{2,0}d_{0}n_{1,0},c_{0})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(c_{0}c_{1,0})\varphi(c_{0}c_{1,0}d_{0}n_{1,0})\mu(d_{0})A_{F}(1,n_{1,0})}{c_{1,0}^{2w-\frac{1}{2}-z}c_{2,0}^{2w-1+2z}d_{0}^{2w+2z}n_{1,0}^{2w-\frac{1}{2}+z}}\\
\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2,0}\mid q^{\infty}\\
(n_{2,0},c_{0})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{A_{F}(n_{2,0},1)}{n_{2,0}^{\frac{1}{2}+z}}\mathcal{V}_{\chi}(\psi;c_{2,0}d_{0}n_{1,0},c_{2,0}d_{0}^{2}n_{1,0}n_{2,0},c_{2,0},c_{0}),

with
\mathcal{V}_{\chi}(\psi;m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r)
as in (
2.18
).

Here for
\Re(s)>1
and
\psi
a Dirichlet character modulo
q
, we have that

L(s,\widetilde{F}\otimes\psi)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{F}(n,1)\psi(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad L(s,\overline{\psi})\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\psi}(n)}{n^{s}}.

If
\psi
is imprimitive, these must be corrected by certain Euler factors in order to obtain primitive

-functions.

Remark 2.25
.

The expression (
2.24
) defining
\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}(\psi;w,z)
is valid more generally for

is required to ensure that the sum over

converges absolutely.

Lemma
2.22

\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}/c^{\prime}c_{0}q\mathbb{Z}\\

, then we instead make the change of variables
t\mapsto t-1
and
u\mapsto 1-u
, yielding

\frac{1}{p^{\beta}}\sum_{t,u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{\beta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}R_{p^{\beta}}(t-u)R_{p^{\beta}}(u-1)R_{p^{\beta}}(t-1).

For
\beta=1
, this is
p^{-1}(p^{3}-p^{2}-p-1)
. For
\beta\geq 2
, the summand vanishes unless
t,u\equiv 1\pmod{p^{\beta-1}}
. Making the change of variables
t\mapsto 1+p^{\beta-1}t
and
u\mapsto 1+p^{\beta-1}u
, this becomes

p^{2\beta-3}\sum_{t,u\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}R_{p}(t-u)R_{p}(u)R_{p}(t),

which is again
p^{2\beta-1}(p-1)
.∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\chi_{0(p^{\beta})}
be the principal character modulo
p^{\beta}
and let
\psi_{p^{\beta}}
be a nonprincipal character modulo
p^{\beta}

,
m_{1}\mid m_{2}

0&otherwise.\end{cases}

In particular,
\mathcal{V}_{\chi_{0(p^{\beta})}}(\psi_{p^{\beta}};m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r)\ll p^{\beta/2}

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as that of

4.3
(namely a case-by-case treatment) except that in place of the Ramanujan sum

in (

as in (

). The strategy is identical except that we use character orthogonality for the nonprincipal character

as well as the fact that if

modulo

is induced from a primitive Dirichlet character

modulo

\tau(\chi,a)=\begin{cases}\overline{\chi}^{\star}\left(\frac{a}{(q,a)}\right)\chi^{\star}\left(\frac{q}{d(q,a)}\right)\mu\left(\frac{q}{d(q,a)}\right)\frac{\varphi(q)}{\varphi\left(\frac{q}{(q,a)}\right)}\tau(\chi^{\star})&if $d\mid\frac{q}{(q,a)}$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

∎

When

is primitive, so that

for
any

, we have that
\mathcal{V}_{\chi_{p^{\beta}}}(\psi_{p^{\beta}};m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r)=\chi_{p^{\beta}}(-1)\widehat{H}(\psi_{p^{\beta}},\chi_{p^{\beta}},m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r)

\widehat{H}(\psi,\chi,m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},r)\coloneqq\sum_{t,u,v\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}}\chi(t+m_{2}u)\overline{\chi}(rt+m_{1}m_{2})\overline{\chi}(u)\chi(ru-m_{1})\overline{\psi}(v)e\left(\frac{m_{3}tv}{q}\right).

We may therefore appeal to earlier work of Petrow and Young to determine the behaviour of the character sum (

Lemma 4.7

(Petrow–Young

Corollary 4.12
.

Let

be a selfdual Hecke–Maaß cusp form for
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let

be a positive integer with
(q_{1},q_{2})=1
. Let
\chi_{1}
be a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor

extends holomorphically to an entire function, and we have that

\lim_{w\to\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{\chi}\left(\psi_{0(q)};w,2w-\frac{3}{2}\right)}{L_{q}(2w-1,\widetilde{F})}=\begin{cases}\frac{\chi_{1}(-1)q_{1}^{2}q_{2}}{L_{q}(1,F)}&if $q_{2}$ is squarefree,\\

In particular,
\lim_{w\to 1/2}L_{p}(2w-1,\widetilde{F})^{-1}=0

4.9

4.11

∎

5.
Test Functions and Transforms I

In the process of analytically continuing the
\GL_{3}\times\GL_{2}\leftrightsquigarrow\GL_{4}\times\GL_{1}
spectral reciprocity formula derived in
Proposition
3.1
to the central value
w=1/2
, we require good control over the transform
\mathcal{H}_{\mu_{F}}^{\pm}(w,z)
given by (
3.7
). In particular, we need to ensure that this expression is holomorphic in both
w
and
z
in certain regions and additionally ensure that it decays sufficiently rapidly in
|\Im(z)|
. These conditions are met once we enforce particular conditions on our tuple of test functions
(h,h^{\mathrm{hol}})
.

Following work of Blomer and Khan
[
BK19
, Section 6]
, we call a function
H:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}

admissible of type
(A,B)
for some
A,B>5
if it is a linear combination of functions of the following two types:

(1)

H(x)=(\mathscr{K}h)(x)
for some function
h
that is even, holomorphic in the strip
|\Im(t)|<A
with zeroes at
\pm(n+1/2)i
for
n\in\{0,\ldots,\lfloor A-1/2\rfloor\}
, and satisfies the bound
h(t)\ll(1+|t|)^{-B-2}
in this strip;

(2)

There exist constants
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
with
a-b\geq A
and
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
k\in 2\mathbb{N}
with
k>a-b
and
\alpha_{k}\ll k^{-B-2}
such that

H(x)=\alpha_{0}\mathcal{J}_{a+1}^{\mathrm{hol}}(x)x^{-b}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=2\\
k\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\\
k>a-b\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}\mathcal{J}_{k}^{\mathrm{hol}}(x).

Similarly, let
h
be an even function that is holomorphic in the strip
|\Im(t)|<1/2+\delta
in which it satisfies
h(t)\ll(1+|t|)^{-2-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
and let
h^{\mathrm{hol}}:2\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a sequence satisfying
h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k)\ll k^{-2-\delta}
for some
\delta>0
. We say that the tuple of test functions
(h,h^{\mathrm{hol}})
is
admissible of type
(A,B)
for some
A,B>5
if the transform
H:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
given by (
3.2
) is admissible of type
(A,B)
.

From
[
BK19
, Lemma 9]
, the Mellin transform
\widehat{H}(s)
of an admissible function
H
of type
(A,B)
is holomorphic in the open half-plane
\Re(s)>-A
, in which it satisfies the bound

(5.1)

\widehat{H}(s)\ll_{\sigma}(1+|\tau|)^{\sigma-1}.

Furthermore, for
\sigma>-A
and
|\tau|\geq 1
, there exists a smooth function
j_{\sigma}(\tau)
satisfying the bound
|\tau|^{m}j_{\sigma}^{(m)}(\tau)\ll_{\sigma}1
for all nonnegative integers

such that we have the asymptotic formula

(5.2)

\widehat{H}(s)=|\tau|^{\sigma-1}\exp\left(i\tau\log\frac{|\tau|}{4\pi e}\right)j_{\sigma}(\tau)+O_{\sigma}(|\tau|^{\sigma-1-B}).

Using this, we show the following.

Lemma 5.3
.

(A,B)

Lemma 8.6

Lemma 8.13
.

Let
(h,h^{\mathrm{hol}})
be the tuple (
8.1
) and define

\displaystyle(\mathscr{D}h)(s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\mathscr{F}h)(u)(\cosh^{2}\pi u)^{-s}\,du,

(8.15)

\displaystyle(\mathscr{D}^{\mathrm{hol}}h^{\mathrm{hol}})(s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\int_{k-\frac{1}{2}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathscr{F}^{\mathrm{hol}}h^{\mathrm{hol}})(u)(\cos^{2}\pi u)^{-s}\,du.

For
-C-1/4<\sigma<1/2
, we have that

(8.16)

\widehat{H}(2s)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(2\pi)^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right)}(\mathscr{D}h)(s)+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(2\pi)^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(1-s)}(\mathscr{D}^{\mathrm{hol}}h^{\mathrm{hol}})(s).

Proof.

We recall from (

[
BK19

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{K}h}(2s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{J}_{r}^{+}}(2s)h(r)r\tanh\pi r\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(2\pi)^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2s-1}}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(s+ir)\Gamma(s-ir)}{\Gamma(2s)}h(r)r\tanh\pi r\,dr.

From
[
GR15
, 3.985.1]
, we have that for
\Re(s)>0

\frac{2^{2s-1}}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(s+ir)\Gamma(s-ir)}{\Gamma(2s)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\cosh^{2}\pi u)^{-s}e(-ru)\,du.

Interchanging the order of integration, we find that

\widehat{\mathscr{K}h}(2s)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(2\pi)^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right)}(\mathscr{D}h)(s).

This extends holomorphically to

) together with the bounds (
8.9
) ensure that
(\mathscr{D}h)(s)

) implies that

vanishes at

, which cancels out the poles of
\Gamma(s)
.

Similarly, we have via
[
BK19
, (3.13)]
, Euler’s reflection formula, and the Legendre duplication formula that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{K}^{\mathrm{hol}}h^{\mathrm{hol}}}(2s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=2\\
k\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\widehat{\mathcal{J}_{k}^{\mathrm{hol}}}(2s)\frac{k-1}{2\pi^{2}}h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(2\pi)^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(1-s)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{2s}\frac{\Gamma(1-2s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-s+k\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-s-k\right)}h^{\mathrm{hol}}(2k)(2k-1).

From
[
GR15
, 3.892.2]
, we have that for
\Re(s)<1

2^{2s}\frac{\Gamma(1-2s)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-s+k\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-s-k\right)}=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cos^{2}\pi u)^{-s}e\left(\frac{u}{2}\right)e(-ku)\,du.

Via the Poisson summation formula, we find that
\widehat{\mathscr{K}^{\mathrm{hol}}h^{\mathrm{hol}}}(2s)
is equal to

-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}(2\pi)^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(1-s)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h^{\mathrm{hol}}(2r)(2r-1)e(-kr)\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cos^{2}\pi u)^{-s}e\left(\frac{u}{2}\right)e(-ru)\,du\,dr.

Interchanging the order of integration and making the change of variables

This extends holomorphically to

Lemma 8.17

Proof.

From (

\frac{d^{j}}{d\tau^{j}}(\mathscr{D}h)(s)=\sum_{\pm}Ti^{-j}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g_{\pm}(u)\left(\sech\frac{\pi u}{U}\right)^{2C+\frac{1}{2}+2\sigma}\left(\log\cosh^{2}\frac{\pi u}{U}\right)^{j}e^{i\Psi_{\tau,\pm}(u)}\,du,

\frac{d^{j}}{d\tau^{j}}(\mathscr{D}h)(s)\ll_{j,N}T^{-N}.

In the former case, we let
\widetilde{\Omega}
be a smooth compactly supported function equal to
1
on
[-1,1]

Since the stationary point does not occur in the support of the integrand of the second term in (

for all

11.2.
\mathrm{GL}_{3}\times\mathrm{GL}_{2}\leftrightsquigarrow\mathrm{GL}_{4}\times\mathrm{GL}_{1}

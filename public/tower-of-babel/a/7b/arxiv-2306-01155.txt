Algebraic Approach to Relativistic Landau Levels in the Symmetric Gauge
Ulrich D. Jentschura

Abstract
We use an algebraic approach to the calculation of Landau levels for a uniform magnetic field in the symmetric gauge with a vector potential A → = 1 2 ​ ( B → × r → ) \vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}\times\vec{r}) , where B → \vec{B} is assumed to be constant. The magnetron quantum number constitutes the degeneracy index. An overall complex phase of the wave function, given in terms of Laguerre polynomials, is a consequence of the algebraic structure. The relativistic generalization of the treatment leads to fully relativistic bispinor Landau levels in the symmetric gauge, in a representation which writes the relativistic states in terms of their nonrelativistic limit, and an algebraically accessible lower bispinor component. Negative-energy states and the massless limit are discussed. The relativistic states can be used for a number of applications, including the calculation of higher-order quantum electrodynamic binding corrections to the energies of quantum cyclotron levels.



I
Introduction

This paper is about nonrelativistic
and relativistic Landau levels
for spin-
1/2
particles,
pertinent to a uniform magnetic field.
We aim to develop algebraic relations,
which describe the energetically
degenerate Landau levels in terms
of cyclotron and magnetron quantum
numbers. Furthermore, we aim to
generalize the treatment to the
fully relativistic domain,
where the Landau levels become
eigenstates of the magnetically
coupled Dirac equation.
Landau levels, including the relativistic
case, are important for many physical
processes, first and foremost perhaps,
in the context of the determination of
fundamental physical constants,
for quantum cyclotron processes
in Penning traps
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
;
Jentschura 2023
;
Jentschura and Moore
.
Among the many other application, we mention the
description of the quantum Hall
effect
Goerbig 2009
;
Goerbig 2011
;
Goerbig 2022
;
Ciftja 2020
;
Ciftja 2023
,
2D quantum dots
Bhuiyan and Marsiglio 2020
,
particle production in the magnetars
Maruyama et al. 2018
,
and processes related to synchrotron
radiation
Sokolov and Ternov 1986
;
Karlovets and Di Piazza
.

The constant, uniform magnetic field
is assumed to be directed along the
z
axis,
and used in the form

\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}=B_{\mathrm{T}}\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{z}
,
where we use the subscript
\mathrm{T}
to denote
the possible application to a Penning trap
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
.
Landau levels are normally calculated
Landau 1937

in the Landau gauge
\vec{A}=-By{\hat{\rm e}}_{x}

[see Eq. (112.1) of Ref.
Landau and Lifshitz 1958
].
Upon solving the corresponding
Schrödinger equation, one finds that
in the Landau gauge, energy eigenstates
are also eigenstates of
the
x
component
p_{x}
of the momentum component.
The momentum component
p_{x}

becomes a parameter which shifts the center of oscillation
for the effective harmonic oscillator
potential, which acts onto the motion in the
y

direction [see Eq. (112.5) of Ref.
Landau and Lifshitz 1958
].
In the Landau gauge, the value of
p_{x}

does not affect the energy of the state
and becomes a continuous degeneracy index.

Here, we use the so-called symmetric
gauge
Powell and Crasemann 1965
;
Bhuiyan and Marsiglio 2020
,
where the vector potential is taken as

\vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})
.
In the symmetric gauge, for the nonrelativistic case,
the eigenfunctions and
energies are in principle known
Powell and Crasemann 1965
;
Bhuiyan and Marsiglio 2020
.
Here, we augment the theory
by identifying the raising and lowering operators
of the cyclotron and magnetron motions as
the determining dynamic variables of the problem.
This enables us to fully clarify
the origin of the degeneracy of the
(nonrelativistic) Landau levels in the symmetric gauge,
and greatly simplify the evaluation
of matrix elements of eigenstates.

We also find the fully relativistic
generalization of the Landau levels,
corresponding to the relativistic quantum cyclotron
states in the limit of vanishing axial frequency.
The relativistic states can be used as input for higher-order
quantum electrodynamic calculations
of quantized-field effects in Penning traps
Jentschura and Moore
,
and any physical systems where relativistic
effects become important.
Units with
\hbar=c=\epsilon_{0}=1

are used throughout this paper.

II
Nonrelativistic Problem

We start our treatment of Landau levels in the
symmetric gauge, inspired
by an electron in a Penning trap
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
;
Wienczek et al. 2022
,
where an additional electric quadrupole potential
leads to axial confinement.
In the limit of a vanishing axial
frequency, the cyclotron and magnetron levels
of the Penning trap become equal to Landau
levels. In general, quantum cyclotron states are
described
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
;
Wienczek et al. 2022
by the quantum numbers
k\ell ns
,
where
k=0,1,2,\dots
is the axial excitation,

\ell=0,1,2,\dots
is the magnetron quantum number,

n=0,1,2,\dots
is the cyclotron quantum number,
and
s=\pm 1
is the spin projection
quantum number.

The kinetic momentum
\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}=\vec{p}-e\,\vec{A}
,
in the symmetric gauge,
can be written as

\displaystyle\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{\pi}_{\parallel}+\vec{p}_{\perp}\,,

(1a)

\displaystyle\vec{\pi}_{\parallel}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{p}_{\parallel}-\frac{e}{2}\left(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r}\right)\,,\quad\vec{p}_{\parallel}=p_{x}\,{\hat{\rm e}}_{x}+p_{y}\,{\hat{\rm e}}_{y}\,,

(1b)

\displaystyle\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}=

\displaystyle\;B_{\mathrm{T}}\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{z}\,.\qquad\vec{p}_{\perp}=p_{z}{\hat{\rm e}}_{z}\,.

(1c)

Here,
e
is the electron charge.
As already mentioned, we use the symmetric gauge
for the vector potential
\vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})
,
rather than

\vec{A}^{\prime}=-By{\hat{\rm e}}_{x}
,
which yields the same magnetic field.
The gauge transformation
\Lambda=-\tfrac{1}{2}\,B_{\mathrm{T}}xy

leads from
\vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})

to
\vec{A}^{\prime}
.
We note that the physical interpretation of a
wave function can depend on the gauge
(see p. 268 of Ref.
Lamb 1952
).
The choice
\vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})

leads to wave functions which are confined in the
x
and
y

directions, while, with the choice
\vec{A}^{\prime}
,
one obtains unconfined wave functions in the
y
direction.

Furthermore, the choice
\vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})

is generalizable to the case of
a nonvanishing additional electric quadrupole trap field
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
.
The nonrelativistic Hamiltonian is given as follows,

\displaystyle H_{0}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}{2m}=H_{\parallel}+\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{p_{z}^{2}}{2m}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\omega_{c}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{e\,B_{\mathrm{T}}}{m}=\frac{|e|\,B_{\mathrm{T}}}{m}\,.

(3)

Here,
|e|=-e
is the modulus of the electron charge.
The spin-independent
part of the Hamiltonian relevant for the
xy
plane is

\displaystyle H_{\parallel}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\vec{\pi}_{\parallel}^{\,2}}{2m}=\frac{\vec{p}_{\parallel}^{\,2}}{2m}+\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}L_{z}+\frac{m\omega_{c}^{2}}{8}\,\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\omega_{c}\left(a_{c}^{\dagger}\,a_{c}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,

(4a)

where the
a_{c}^{\dagger}
and
a_{c}

are the raising and lowering operators
of the cyclotron motion.
We have set the electric quadrupole potential
of the trap equal to zero,
wherefore, in the sense of Ref.
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
,
the magnetron frequency vanishes.
The cyclotron (
c
) and magnetron (
{\rm m}
) raising
(
a_{c}^{\dagger}
and (
a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}
)
and lowering operators (
a_{c}
and
a_{\rm m}
)
are given as follows,

\displaystyle a_{c}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left({\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{x}-{\rm i}{\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{y}-\frac{{\rm i}\,x}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}-\frac{y}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)\,,

(5a)

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}_{c}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left({\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{x}+{\rm i}{\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{y}+\frac{{\rm i}\,x}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}-\frac{y}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)\,,

(5b)

\displaystyle a_{\rm m}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left({\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{x}+{\rm i}{\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{y}-\frac{{\rm i}\,x}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}+\frac{y}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)\,,

(5c)

\displaystyle a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left({\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{x}-{\rm i}{\tilde{a}}_{0}\,p_{y}+\frac{{\rm i}\,x}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}+\frac{y}{2\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)\,.

(5d)

In these formulas, the generalized (magnetic) Bohr radius is
(we temporarily restore factors of
\hbar
and
c
)

{\tilde{a}}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{mc^{2}}{\hbar\omega_{c}}}\,\frac{\hbar}{mc}=\frac{\hbar}{\alpha_{c}mc}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{|e|B_{\mathrm{T}}}}=\ell_{B}\,,

(6)

where we have set
\alpha_{c}=\sqrt{\hbar\omega_{c}/(mc^{2})}

(
\alpha_{c}
is a generalized fine-structure constant),
and
\ell_{B}
is the magnetic length
Goerbig 2009
.
The relations (
5
) can be inverted,

\displaystyle p_{x}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\,\left[a_{c}+a_{c}^{\dagger}+a_{\rm m}+a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(7a)

\displaystyle p_{y}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{{\rm i}}{2\sqrt{2}{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\,\left[a_{c}-a_{c}^{\dagger}-a_{\rm m}+a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(7b)

\displaystyle x=

\displaystyle\;\frac{{\rm i}{\tilde{a}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left[a_{c}-a_{c}^{\dagger}+a_{\rm m}-a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(7c)

\displaystyle y=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{{\tilde{a}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\left[a_{c}+a_{c}^{\dagger}-a_{\rm m}-a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}\right]\,.

(7d)

The nonvanishing commutators are as follows,

[a_{c},a^{\dagger}_{c}]=[a_{\rm m},a^{\dagger}_{\rm m}]=1\,.

(8)

All other commutators vanish,
which means that, in particular,
the cyclotron and magnetron
excitation numbers, together with the
z
component
of the momentum and the spin projection quantum number

s
, form a complete set of commuting
observables for the nonrelativistic spin-independent
problem. The eigenfunctions are given as follows,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{NR}}\,\psi_{kn\ell s}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\;E_{\mathrm{NR}}\,\psi_{kn\ell s}(\vec{r})\,,

(9a)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{NR}}=

\displaystyle\;\omega_{c}\,\left(n+\frac{s+1}{2}\right)+\frac{k^{2}}{2m}\,.

(9b)

Here,
k\in\mathbbm{R}
is a continuous quantum number
characterizing the momentum component in the
z
direction.
The energy eigenvalue is independent of the
magnetron quantum number
\ell
.
The momentum in the
z
component and the
spin component of the eigenstate can be split off,

\displaystyle\psi_{k\ell ns}(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\;\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})\,\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kz}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\chi_{s}\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle\vec{\rho}=

\displaystyle\;x\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{x}+y\,\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{y}\,,

(10b)

\displaystyle\chi_{+1}=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad\chi_{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\end{array}\right)\,.

According to Refs.
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
;
Wienczek et al. 2022
,
one uses the quantum numbers
k\ell ns

(in this, alphabetically inspired, sequence) by convention.
However, for the spin-independent,
two-dimensional, nonrelativistic case,
we use the designation
n\ell
, in correspondence with the
hydrogen atom
where the first index indicates the quantum
number which determines the energy
(for a comprehensive discussion,
see Chap. 4 of Ref.
Jentschura and Adkins 2022
).
One may search for the general formula for the nonrelativistic
eigenfunction
\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})
,
which fulfills the equation

H_{\parallel}\,\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})=E_{n}\,\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})\,,\qquad E_{n}=\omega_{c}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,

(11)

where
H_{\parallel}
is given in Eq. (
4
).
The solution reads as follows,

\displaystyle\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2^{-\tfrac{1}{2}(|n-\ell|+1)}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\min(n,\ell)!}{\max(n,\ell)!}}\,\left(\frac{\rho}{{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)^{|n-\ell|}

\displaystyle\;\times{\rm i}^{|n-\ell|}\,L^{|n-\ell|}_{\min(n,\ell)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\frac{\rho}{{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)^{2}\right)\,

\displaystyle\;\times{\rm e}^{{\rm i}(n-\ell)\varphi}\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{4}\,\left(\frac{\rho}{{\tilde{a}}_{0}}\right)^{2}\right]\,.

(12)

Here,
\rho=|\vec{\rho}|
and
\varphi=\arctan(y/x)
.
The complex phase
{\rm i}^{|n-\ell|}
is a
consequence of the application of the
raising operators on the ground states
[see also Eq. (
13a
)].

A comparison of Eq. (
II
) with the literature
is indicated.
In Eq. (2) of Ref.
Bellandi et al. 1976
,
one obtains a corresponding
result. The spin-independent wave function obtained
in Ref.
Bellandi et al. 1976

is equal to our wave
function if one replaces

k
(in the notation of Ref.
Bellandi et al. 1976

by
\min(n,\ell)
, and
and
m
(in the notation of Ref.
Bellandi et al. 1976
)
by
n-\ell
(in our notation).
Furthermore, one may point out that
the wave functions given in
Eq. (2) of Ref.
Bellandi et al. 1976
are specialized to the
case
\omega_{c}=2
.
In Ref.
Ciftja 2020
, this restriction is
lifted. The parameter
l_{0}
defined in
Eq. (28) of Ref.
Ciftja 2020
is equal to
our parameter
{\tilde{a}}_{0}
,

In Eq. (A31) of Ref.
Bhuiyan and Marsiglio 2020
,
the authors obtain a corresponding
result. Their parameter

\ell
(which, in the conventions
of Ref.
Bhuiyan and Marsiglio 2020
can become negative) is
equal to our
n-\ell
.
The inclusion of additional electric fields into the
formalism is discussed in
Refs.
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
;
Wienczek et al. 2022

and in Ref.
Edery and Audin 2019
.

Our formalism clearly identifies
the cyclotron excitation, which is linked to the quantum
number
n
that determines the energy,
and the magnetron quantum number
\ell
,
which does not shift the energy but shifts the
orbit further outward with increasing
\ell

for given
n
(see Figs.
1
and
2
).
When one increases
\ell

by one, the wave function acquires
a phase factor
\exp(-{\rm i}\varphi)
.
The cyclotron and magnetron excitations can be
associated with the action of mutually commuting
raising operators, acting on the ground state,
and the quantum numbers
n
and
\ell
belong to mutually
commuting observables.

Indeed, the nonrelativistic
eigenfunctions can be obtained from the
ground state by the operation of the
raising operators,

\displaystyle\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!\,\ell!}}\,(a_{c}^{\dagger})^{n}\,(a_{\rm m}^{\dagger})^{\ell}\,\psi_{00}(\vec{\rho})\,,

(13a)

\displaystyle\psi_{00}(\vec{\rho})=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{{\tilde{a}}_{0}\,\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4{\tilde{a}}_{0}^{2}}\right)\,.

(13b)

The number operators count the number of
cyclotron and magnetron excitations,

a_{c}^{\dagger}\,a_{c}\,|n,\ell\rangle=n\,|n,\ell\rangle\,,\qquad a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}\,a_{\rm m}\,|n,\ell\rangle=\ell\,|n,\ell\rangle\,.

(14)

The wave functions
\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})
are
eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian
H_{\parallel}
,
while their energy eigenvalue only depends on
the cyclotron quantum number
n
,
and is independent of the magnetron quantum number
\ell
.
The magnetron quantum number
\ell=0,1,2,\dots
acts
as a degeneracy index for the Landau level.
Here, the magnetron degeneracy index is countable
and leads to a more intuitive form of the wave
function, which is normalizable to unity.
Wave functions are orthonormal,

\int{\rm d}^{2}\rho\,\psi^{*}_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})\,\psi_{n^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}(\vec{\rho})=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\,.

(15)

A total of four example wave functions are
plotted in Figs.
1
and
2
.
Furthermore, one has the relation

\int{\rm d}^{3}r\,\psi^{\dagger}_{k\ell ns}(\vec{\rho})\,\psi_{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}s^{\prime}}(\vec{\rho})=\delta(k-k^{\prime})\,\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\,\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\,.

(16)

(For the spinor case, we need to use
\psi^{\dagger}
because
it is a two-component wave function.)
With increasing magnetron quantum number,
the states spread further away from the
origin, as is evident from the
expectation value

\int{\rm d}^{2}\rho\,\rho^{2}\,|\psi_{n\ell}(\vec{\rho})|^{2}=(2+2n+2\ell){\tilde{a}}_{0}^{2}\,.

(17)

This trend is depicted in Fig.
2
.

Figure 1:

We display the probability density

|\psi_{(n=0),(\ell=0)}(\vec{\rho})|^{2}

of the cyclotron ground state,
as given in Eq. (
II
).
It is radially symmetric and nonvanishing at
the origin.

Figure 2:

The cyclotron states with
n=2
all have
the same nonrelativistic energy
\tfrac{5}{2}\omega_{c}
.
The probability density
|\psi_{(n=2)\ell}(\vec{\rho})|^{2}

vanishes at the origin.
With increasing magnetron excitation
\ell=0,6,12
,
the states spread away from the origin.
The azimuthal dependence of the
wave function is given by the factor
\exp[{\rm i}(n-\ell)\,\varphi]
.
The plot surfaces are shaded according to
the imaginary part of the wave function,
with lighter regions indicating a positive imaginary part,
and darker regions indicating a negative imaginary part
of the wave function.

III
Relativistic Landau Levels

Before we indulge in the calculations, let us
remember that the problem of a relativistic
electron coupled to a uniform electric or magnetic
field, has been discussed in the literature before,
notably, in a comprehensive treatise given in
Ref.
Demeur 1953
.
In unnumbered equations between
Eqs. (10) and (11)
of Sec. 3 of Chap. 3 of Ref.
Demeur 1953
,
the relativistic eigenstate
of the magnetically coupled Dirac equation is
given in terms of parabolic cylinder functions
(Weber functions, denoted as
D_{n}
).
The vector potential is taken in the Landau gauge.
Alternative discussions of the
relativistic states are given in
Sec. 4 of Chap. 26 of Ref.
Sokolov and Ternov 1986

and in Sec. 6 of Chap. 2 of Ref.
Bagrov and Gitman 1990
.
Our goal here is to find a representation of the
relativistic eigenstates which allows us to
clearly identify the connections of the
nonrelativistic limit with the fully relativistic
state.

We here aim to find the relativistic Landau levels
in the symmetric gauge.
Let us recall the nonrelativistic (NR) Hamiltonian
and its eigenstates,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{NR}}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}=

\displaystyle\;E_{\mathrm{NR}}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\,,\quad H_{\mathrm{NR}}=\frac{(\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}})^{2}}{2m}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad E_{\mathrm{NR}}=

\displaystyle\;\omega_{c}\left(n+\frac{s+1}{2}\right)+\frac{k^{2}}{2m}\,.

(19)

The kinetic momentum in the trap is

\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}=\vec{p}-e\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}
,
while the vector potential
\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}
for the
uniform magnetic field of the trap can be written as

\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}=\vec{p}-e\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}\,,\qquad\vec{A}_{\mathrm{T}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})\,.

(20)

The Dirac Hamiltonian for a uniform magnetic field
is

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{D}}=

\displaystyle\;\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}+\beta\,m\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\vec{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\vec{\sigma}\\
\vec{\sigma}&0\end{array}\right)\,,\quad\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}&0\\
0&-\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

where we use the Dirac matrix in the
Dirac representation.
We search for bispinor solutions of the form

H_{\mathrm{D}}\,\Psi=E_{\mathrm{D}}\,\Psi\,,\qquad\Psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{array}\right)\equiv\Psi_{k\ell ns}\,.

(26a)

We note that
\Psi
denotes the relativistic bispinor
wave function, while
\psi
is the nonrelativistic
counterpart. We use the following

ansatz
for the relativistic eigenstates,

\psi_{1}=\mathcal{N}\,\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\,,\qquad\psi_{2}=\mathcal{N}\,\frac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{E_{D}+m}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\,.

(26b)

Acting with
H_{\mathrm{D}}
on
\Psi
, one obtains

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{D}}\Psi=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{N}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}&\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\\
\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}&-m\mathbbm{1}_{2\times 2}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\\
\frac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{N}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m\left(1+\dfrac{2E_{\mathrm{NR}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\right)\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\\[4.79333pt]
\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}\left(1-\dfrac{m}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\right)\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\mathop{=}^{\mbox{!}}

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{N}\,E_{\mathrm{D}}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\\[4.79333pt]
\dfrac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\end{array}\right)=E_{D}\,\Psi\,.

From the secular equation for the upper component,
one obtains

E_{\mathrm{D}}=m\left(1+\frac{2E_{\mathrm{NR}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\right)=m+\frac{2mE_{\mathrm{NR}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\,.

(35)

This can be rewritten as follows,

E_{\mathrm{D}}-m=\frac{2mE_{\mathrm{NR}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\,,\qquad E_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}-m^{2}=2mE_{\mathrm{NR}}\,.

(36)

The Dirac energy
E_{\mathrm{D}}
is obtained
as follows,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{D}}=

\displaystyle\;\sqrt{m^{2}+2mE_{\mathrm{NR}}}=m\sqrt{1+\frac{2E_{\mathrm{NR}}}{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;m\sqrt{1+\frac{\omega_{c}}{m}\left(2n+s+1\right)+\frac{k^{2}}{m^{2}}}\,.

(37)

Somewhat surprisingly, the state with
quantum numbers
n=0
and
s=-1
, and
k=0
, has exact
rest mass energy even in the fully relativistic formalism.
A check of the eigenvector
property for the lower component is successful,
We start from the
expression in round brackets (lower component)
in the second line of Eq. (
III
) and obtain:

\left(1-\frac{m}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\right)=\frac{E_{\mathrm{D}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}=E_{\mathrm{D}}\,\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\,.

(38)

In view of the Taylor expansion

\sqrt{1+\epsilon}=1+\frac{\epsilon}{2}-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{8}+\frac{\epsilon^{3}}{16}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
,
we can write
for positive energy in the limit
k\to 0
,

E_{\mathrm{D}}(k\to 0)=m+\frac{\omega_{c}}{2m}\left(2n+s+1\right)\\[0.4878pt]
-\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{\omega_{c}}{m}\left(2n+s+1\right)\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{16}\left(\frac{\omega_{c}}{m}\left(2n+s+1\right)\right)^{3}\,.

(39)

This is consistent with Eqs. (90), (96) and (105)
of Ref.
Wienczek et al. 2022
.
We set
\omega_{c}=\alpha_{c}^{2}m
,
where
\alpha_{c}
is the cyclotron coupling constant.
The normalization of the relativistic states
according to :

\int{\rm d}^{3}r\,|\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}|^{2}=\int{\rm d}^{3}r|\Psi|^{2}=1

leads to the relation

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}^{3}r\;\Psi^{\dagger}(\vec{r})\,\Psi(\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{N}^{2}\left[1+\frac{2mE_{\mathrm{NR}}}{(E_{\mathrm{D}}+m)^{2}}\right]\mathop{=}^{\mbox{!}}1\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}=

\displaystyle\;\left[1+\frac{2mE_{\mathrm{NR}}}{(E_{\mathrm{D}}+m)^{2}}\right]^{-1/2}\,.

(41)

In the above formulas for the relativistic
states, the dependence on the quantum numbers
k\ell ns

has been suppressed for notational simplicity.
Restoring the quantum numbers, one finds that

\int{\rm d}^{3}r\,\Psi^{\dagger}_{k\ell ns}(\vec{\rho})\,\Psi_{k^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}n^{\prime}s^{\prime}}(\vec{\rho})=\delta(k-k^{\prime})\,\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\,\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\,.

(42)

For completeness, it is perhaps useful to
remark that, if desired, one can easily
write the entire operator
\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}

in terms of cyclotron, magnetron and spin ladder operators,
and express the lower component
\psi_{2}
of the
fully relativistic state, in terms of
nonrelativistic eigenstates of raised and lowered
quantum numbers.

IV
Negative–Energy States

In Dirac theory, there is an energy gap
between positive-energy states with
E\geq m

and negative-energy states with
E\leq-m
.
It is relatively straightforward to check that the states
[cf. Eqs. (
26a
) and (
26b
)]

\Phi=\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\dfrac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{E_{\mathrm{D}}+m}\,\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\\[12.91663pt]
\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\end{array}\right)

(43)

with the same normalization factor
\mathcal{N}
as
for positive-energy states [see Eq. (
41
)],
has the property

H_{\mathrm{D}}\,\Phi=-E_{\mathrm{D}}\Phi

(44)

and thus constitutes the negative-energy
(antiparticle) state of Dirac theory.
As compared to the positive-energy
state, the upper and lower components of the
Dirac bispinor are interchanged,
and the upper spinor receives a
minus sign.

V
Massless Limit

In the limit
m\to 0
, the gap between positive-energy
and negative-energy states vanishes.
We recall that

\omega_{c}m=|e|B_{\mathrm{T}}=\ell_{B}^{-2}\,.

(45)

The Dirac energy (
III
) attains the
massless limit

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{D}}\mathop{=}^{m\to 0}\sqrt{\ell_{B}^{-2}\left(2n+s+1\right)+k^{2}}\,.

(46)

The square-root dependence on the
quantum numbers confirms the
corresponding dependence, obtained
on the basis of the Weyl equation,
given in Eq. (2.23)
of Ref.
Goerbig 2009
.
The appropriate zero-mass limit of the
normalization factor is

\mathcal{N}\mathop{=}^{m\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(47)

and the positive-energy solution is

\Psi\mathop{=}^{m\to 0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\\[14.2083pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\dfrac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{E_{D}}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\end{array}\right)\,.

(48)

The negative-energy solution is

\Phi\mathop{=}^{m\to 0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\dfrac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{\pi}_{\mathrm{T}}}{E_{D}}\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\\[14.2083pt]
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\psi_{\mathrm{NR}}\end{array}\right)\,.

(49)

VI
Conclusions

We have considered the calculation of
both nonrelativistic as well as fully relativistic
Landau levels in the symmetric gauge where

\vec{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\vec{B}_{\mathrm{T}}\times\vec{r})
,
based on an algebraic approach.
The spin-independent part of the
nonrelativistic states finds a natural form in terms
of the cyclotron quantum number
n
, and
of the magnetron quantum number
\ell
.
We find a universal representation of the
spinless nonrelativistic quantum state wave function
with quantum numbers

n
(cyclotron) and

\ell
(magnetron) in Eq. (
II
).
After including the spin and the axial motion,
we find the nonrelativistic spinor state with
quantum numbers
n
and
\ell
,
and
k
(axial) and
s
(spin),
as given in Eq. (
10
).
For the nonrelativistic wave
function
\psi=\psi_{k\ell ns}
,
the spin-independent form is given
in Eq. (
II
), and
the nonrelativistic spinor form is given
in Eq. (
10
).
The orthonormality relations are given in
Eqs. (
15
) (for the
spin-independent part) and
in Eq. (
16
) (for the spinor
wave function).
While the magnetron quanta are raised from
the ground state by the magnetron
raising operator
a_{\rm m}^{\dagger}

[see Eq. (
14
)],
the magnetron quantum number
\ell

does not enter the formula for the
energy of the Landau level [see
Eqs. (
11
) and (
9
)].
Inverting the relations given in
Eq. (
5
), one obtains a
representation of the
x
,
y
,
p_{x}

and
p_{y}
operators in terms of the
raising and lowering
operators of the cyclotron and magnetron motions
[see Eq. (
7
)].

In the context of the quantum Hall effect,
Landau levels have been considered
in both the Landau as well as the
symmetric gauges
Goerbig 2009
.
They are also relevant to
quantum cyclotron states in Penning traps
Brown and Gabrielse 1982
;
Brown and Gabrielse 1986
.
Indeed, in the limit of vanishing
axial confinement, the quantum cyclotron states
approach the Landau levels in the symmetric
gauge and our formulas are essential elements
in the discussion of higher-order quantum
electrodynamic corrections to
quantum cyclotron energy levels
Jentschura and Moore
.

Based on an essential generalization
of the nonrelativistic problem (see Sec.
II
and Ref.
Goerbig 2009
),
we calculate the fully relativistic Landau levels
in the symmetric gauge, in Sec.
III
.
These relativistic states constitute solutions of the
magnetically coupled relativistic Dirac equation.
The same quantum numbers
k
,
\ell
,
n
and
s
,
that we found for the nonrelativistic problem,
characterize the relativistic state.
The relativistic wave function is given in
Eqs. (
26a
) and (
26b
),
with the normalization given in Eq. (
40
).
The fully relativistic form is needed
as an essential ingredient in the calculation
of quantum electrodynamic corrections to
quantum cyclotron energy levels
Jentschura and Moore
.
We mention relativistic quantum dynamics
in synchrotrons as a further potential area
of application.
Negative-energy states are discussed in Sec.
IV
,
and the massless limit of the Dirac bispinor solutions
is discussed in Sec.
V
.

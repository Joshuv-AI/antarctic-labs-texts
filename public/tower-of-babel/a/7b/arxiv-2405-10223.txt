1.
Introduction

The problem known as the
slicing inequality for general measures
asks which is the greatest possible value
s_{n}
such that for every origin-symmetric convex body
K
(convex compact with non-empty interior) and every even continuous probability density
f
on
K
, there exists a hyperplane
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\int_{K\cap F}f\geq s_{n}.

Koldobsky investigated this problem in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
and showed that
s_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}
, although the constant can be improved if
K
is restricted to certain classes of convex bodies.

A
star body
is a set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the form

K=\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}:|x|\leq\rho_{K}(x/|x|)\}

where the
radial function

\rho_{K}:S^{n-1}\to(0,\infty)
is continuous and strictly positive, and
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm.
Every convex body containing the origin in the interior is also a star body.
The radial distance between two star bodies
K,L
is
\max_{v\in S^{n-1}}|\rho_{K}(v)-\rho_{L}(v)|
.

Given a star body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
intersection body
of
K
is the star body
{\rm I}K
defined by
\rho_{{\rm I}K}(v)=\left|K\cap v^{\perp}\right|_{n-1}
, where
|\cdot|_{k}
denotes the
k
-dimensional volume.
Taking the closure in the radial metric of the sets
{\rm I}K
with
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a star body, we get the class of
intersection bodies
, which we denote by
\mathcal{I}_{n}
.

For any class of star bodies
\mathcal{C}
the outer volume ratio distance from a star body
K
to
\mathcal{C}
is defined by

d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{C})=\inf\left\{\left(\frac{\left|D\right|_{n}}{\left|K\right|_{n}}\right)^{1/n}:K\subseteq D,D\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

In
[
11
]
the following inequality was proven:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be an origin-symmetric star body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any even continuous non-negative function
f
we have

\int_{K}f\leq 2d_{\rm ovr}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\left|K\right|_{n}^{1/n}\max_{v\in S^{n-1}}\int_{K\cap v^{\perp}}f.

In particular, if
K
is an origin-symmetric star body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of volume
1
and
f
is an even continuous probability density in
K
then

(1)

\max_{v\in S^{n-1}}\int_{K\cap v^{\perp}}f\geq(2d_{\rm ovr}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n}))^{-1}.

Here
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})
is the outer volume ratio distance between
K
and the class of intersection bodies.
Since origin-symmetric ellipsoids are intersection bodies, if
K
is an origin-symmetric convex body, John’s theorem implies that
d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\leq\sqrt{n}
, hence the bound
s_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}
.

The upper bound is
s_{n}\leq c/\sqrt{n}
, in other words, there exists an origin-symmetric convex body
K
of volume
1
and an even continuous probability density
f
on
K
, such that

\max_{v\in S^{n-1}}\int_{K\cap v^{\perp}}f\leq c\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

This estimate was obtained in
[
5
]
up to a factor
\sqrt{\log(\log(n))}
, and this factor was later removed in
[
6
]
.
Moreover, the estimates in
[
5
,
6
]
also apply to non-central sections of
K
, this is,

\max_{v\in S^{n-1},t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{K\cap(v^{\perp}+tv)}f\leq c\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that the largest possible distance from a convex body to
\mathcal{I}_{n}
has order
c\sqrt{n}
, this is,

c\sqrt{n}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,\mathcal{I}_{n})\leq\sqrt{n}

where the supremum runs over all origin-symmetric convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
c>0
is a universal constant.
A similar argument was used to show in
[
1
]
(lower bound) and
[
12
]
(upper bound) that

c\sqrt{n/p}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{p}^{n})\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{n/p}

for
p\geq 1
, where
L_{p}
is the class of convex bodies
L
such that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{L})
embeds in
L_{p}
as a Banach space (see
[
8
]
for details).
Also, in
[
4
]
the convex body and measure constructed in
[
5
]
were adapted to a different slicing inequality from
[
3
]
to obtain the lower bound

(2)

\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{-p}^{n})\geq cn^{\frac{1}{2p}},

for
p\in[1,n)
, where the supremum runs over all origin-symmetric convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
c>0
is a universal constant and
L_{-p}^{n}
is the class of star bodies
L
for which
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{L})
embeds in
L_{-p}
.
For integer
k\in[1,n)
,
L_{-k}
is the class of
k
-intersection bodies (see
[
8
]
for details).

A version of Theorem
1.1
with hyperplanes replaced by lower dimensional subspaces (Theorem
1.2
below) was established also in
[
11
]
.
Denote by
G_{n,n-k}
the Grassmanian of subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of codimension
k
.
The class of convex bodies responsible for the
k
-codimensional problem is the class of
generalized
k
-intersection bodies
, denoted by
\mathcal{BP}_{k}^{n}
, which can be defined as follows:
Given two star bodies
K,L
and
p>0
, their radial
p
-sum is the star body
K\tilde{+}_{p}L
whose radial function is

\rho_{K\tilde{+}_{p}L}^{p}=\rho_{K}^{p}+\rho_{L}^{p}.

For
k\in[1,n)
, the class
\mathcal{BP}_{k}^{n}
is defined as the closure in the radial metric of finite radial
k
-sums of origin-symmetric ellipsoids.
The class
\mathcal{BP}_{k}^{n}
was introduced by Zhang in
[
17
]
in connection with the lower dimensional Busemann-Petty problem. The lower-dimensional slicing inequality is as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
11
, Corollary 1]
)
.

Let
K
be an origin-symmetric star body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Then for any even continuous non-negative function
f
,

(3)

\int_{K}f\leq c^{k}d_{\rm ovr}(K,\mathcal{BP}_{k}^{n})^{k}\left|K\right|_{n}^{k/n}\max_{F\in G_{n,n-k}}\int_{K\cap F}f,

where
c>0
is a universal constant.
In particular, if
K
is an origin-symmetric star body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of volume
1
and
f
is an even continuous probability density in
K
then

(4)

\max_{F\in G_{n,n-k}}\int_{K\cap F}f\geq c^{-k}(d_{\rm ovr}(K,\mathcal{BP}_{k}^{n}))^{-k}.

An upper bound for the distance to
{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n}
was obtained in
[
13
]
(the power of the logarithmic term was later corrected in
[
11
, Proposition 1]
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
K
be an origin-symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Then

(5)

d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n})\leq c\sqrt{n/k}\left(\log\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)\right)^{3/2}

where
c>0
is a universal constant.

Moreover, since
{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n}\subseteq L_{-k}^{n}
(this was proven in
[
7
]
and
[
15
]
, see also
[
8
, Theorem 4.23]
), inequalities (
2
) and (
5
) imply

(6)

cn^{\frac{1}{2k}}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,L_{-k}^{n})\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n})\leq c\sqrt{n/k}\left(\log\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)\right)^{3/2}.

The two estimates are tight for large values of
k
(at least proportional to
n
). For low values of
k>1
the lower and upper bounds are far apart as
n\to\infty
. In this paper we use the same technique as in
[
5
]
to prove that Theorem
1.2
is sharp up to a logarithmic term, thus obtaining the correct order (up to logarithms) of
\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n})
.
Our main theorem is the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that for every
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
, there exists an origin-symmetric convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an even continuous probability density
f
in
K
such that
for every subspace
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of codimension
k
,

\int_{F}f\leq\left(c\sqrt{\frac{\log(n)k}{n}}\right)^{k}.

As a consequence of Theorems
1.4
and
1.2
we deduce that the estimate (
5
) is also asymptotically sharp, up to logarithmic terms.

Corollary 1.5
.

There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that for every
n\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq n-1
, there exists an origin-symmetric convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n})\geq c\sqrt{n/k}(\log(n))^{-1/2}.

Combining Corollary
1.5
and Theorem
1.3
we obtain

c\sqrt{n/k}(\log(n))^{-1/2}\leq\sup_{K}d_{\operatorname{ovr}}(K,{\mathcal{BP}}_{k}^{n})\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{n/k}\left(\log\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)\right)^{3/2}

where the supremum runs over all origin-symmetric convex bodies
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
c,c^{\prime}>0
are universal constants.

It should be noted that our proof is a simplification of those used in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
since we do not intend to suppress any logarithmic term.

Theorem

Throughout the section

and

First we recall two elementary inequalities from

Lemma 2.1

(

For

Let

The next elementary lemma concerns the parametrization of the sphere with respect to the so-called

. It can be found in

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider the decomposition
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
m+k=n
and

.
If

(7)

\int_{S^{n-1}}f(v)dv=\int_{S^{m-1}}\int_{S^{k-1}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}f(\cos(\alpha)x,\sin(\alpha)y)\\
\times\cos(\alpha)^{m-1}\sin(\alpha)^{k-1}d\alpha dydx

where integration on the sphere

is with respect to the

The cases

being the counting measure.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

By rotational invariance, we may assume without loss of generality that

.
We decompose
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
and notice that for every

we have
d(F,(x,y))=|x|
.

To find the expectation we integrate, use Lemma
2.3
and then the change of variables

.

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\varphi(d(F,n

\displaystyle\theta)))

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}}\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi(nd(F,\theta))d\theta

(10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa_{n}}\int_{S^{k-1}}\int_{S^{n-k-1}}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\varphi(n\cos(\alpha))\cos(\alpha)^{k-1}\sin(\alpha)^{n-k-1}d\alpha dxdy

(11)

(13)

The Grassmanian
G_{n,n-k}
of
k
-codimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be constructed as the quotient space of the group of rotations
O_{n}
, by the subgroup of rotations that fix a given
k
-codimensional subspace.
As such,
G_{n,n-k}
inherits naturally a metric
d
, which is the quotient of the operator norm metric.
This is defined as

(18)

d_{G}(F_{1},F_{2})=\min\{\|I-T\|_{\rm op}:T\in O_{n},T(F_{1})=F_{2}\}

for
F_{1},F_{2}\in G_{n,n-k}
.
Here
\|\cdot\|_{\rm op}
denotes the operator norm, defined by

\|S\|_{\rm op}=\sup_{\theta\in S^{n-1}}|S(\theta)|,

for every linear map
S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

First we need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\theta\in S^{n-1}
be fixed, then the function
F\in G_{n,n-k}\mapsto d(F,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz with constant
1
.

Proof.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}\in G_{n,n-k}
.
By the definition of
d_{G}
, there exists
T\in O_{n}
with
T(F_{1})=F_{2},\|T-I\|_{\rm op}=d_{G}(F_{1},F_{2})
.
We have

(19)

\displaystyle|d_{G}(F_{1},\theta)-d_{G}(F_{2},\theta)|

\displaystyle=|d_{G}(F_{2},T(\theta))-d_{G}(F_{2},\theta)|

(20)

\displaystyle\leq|T(\theta)-\theta|

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\|T-I\|_{\rm op}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq d_{G}(F_{1},F_{2}),

and the lemma follows.
∎

For
\delta>0
, a
\delta
-net with respect to
d_{G}
is a finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq G_{n,n-k}
such that for every
F\in G_{n,n-k}
there is
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
with
d_{G}(F,F^{\prime})<\delta
.
We shall use the following result by Szarek
[
16
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
16
, Proposition 8]
)
.

For every
0\leq\delta\leq\sqrt{2}={\rm diam}(G_{n,n-k})
there exists a
\delta
-net with respect to
d_{G}
of cardinality less than
(c/\delta)^{k(n-k)}
, where
c>0
is a universal constant.

Proposition 2.7
.

There exist a universal constant
c>0
and
N=cn^{\frac{k}{2}+4}
points
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(d(F,n\theta))\leq 4n^{-k/2}

for every
F\in G_{n,n-k}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta=n^{-k/2-1}
and consider the corresponding
\delta
-net
\mathcal{F}
of cardinality less than
(c^{\prime}n^{k/2+1})^{k(n-k)}\leq e^{c^{\prime\prime}n^{4}}
.
Take
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
independent random points uniformly distributed in
S^{n-1}
.
By Lemma
2.4
we have
\mathbb{E}(\varphi(d(F,n\theta_{i})))\leq n^{-k/2}
.
Applying Lemma
2.2
, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
we have

(23)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(d(F,n\theta_{i}))\geq 3n^{-k/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-n^{-k/2}N}=e^{-cn^{4}}

\mathbb{P}\left(\exists F\in\mathcal{F},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(d(F,n\theta_{i}))\geq 3n^{-k/2}\right)\leq e^{c^{\prime\prime}n^{4}}e^{-cn^{4}}

which is less than
1
for all
1\leq k\leq n
,
n\geq 2
, for some universal constant
c>0
depending on
c^{\prime\prime}
.
We get the existence of
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(d(F,n\theta_{i}))\leq 3n^{-k/2}

for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

By Lemma

, the function
F\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(d(F,n\theta_{i}))
is Lipschitz with constant

.
Then for any
F\in G_{n,n-k}
,

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varphi(d(F,n\theta_{i}))\leq n\delta+3n^{-k/2}=4n^{-k/2}

and the proposition follows.
∎

Let
\gamma_{n}(x)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}
denote the standard Gaussian probability measure.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N}
be the points obtained in Proposition

.
Define the function
f_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{n}(x-p_{i})
, where

\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{M}\}=\{n\theta_{1},-n\theta_{1},n\theta_{2},-n\theta_{2},\ldots,n\theta_{N},-n\theta_{N}\},

Define the convex set

The set

These properties will be stated separately in the following three propositions.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let

Let

Proof.

Let

Using the change of variables

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{F}f_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{F}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x-p_{i}|^{2}}dx

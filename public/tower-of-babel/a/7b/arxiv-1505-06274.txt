The Moments of the Hydrogen Atom by the Method of Brackets
Ivan GONZALEZ † 1 {}^{{\dagger}^{1}}, Karen T. KOHL † 2 {}^{{\dagger}^{2}}, Igor KONDRASHUK † 3 {}^{{\dagger}^{3}}, Victor H. MOLL † 4 {}^{{\dagger}^{4}} and Daniel SALINAS † 5 {}^{{\dagger}^{5}}

Abstract
Expectation values of powers of the radial coordinate in arbitrary hydrogen states are given, in the quantum case, by an integral involving the associated Laguerre function. The method of brackets is used to evaluate the integral in closed-form and to produce an expression for this average value as a finite sum.

1
Introduction

The computation of the expectation
\langle r^{k}\rangle
of the electron for atoms with a single electron is a standard problem in quantum mechanics, see
[
19
,
29
]
. For a given energy state
n
, the problem is expressed as

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{n\ell}^{2}(r)r^{k+2}dr,

where
R_{n\ell}(r)
is the radial solution of the Schrödinger equation for the hydrogen atom. Conditions on the parameters
n
,
\ell
,
k
are determined by the convergence of this integral.

In the non-relativistic situation, the solution is given in terms of the Hahn polynomials
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle h_{m}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x,N)=\frac{(1-N)_{m}(\beta+1)_{m}}{m!}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-m, \,\alpha+ \beta+ m + 1,\, -x}{\beta+ 1,\, 1 - N}\bigg|{\,1}\right).

(1.1)

In particular, these expectations are given in terms of the Chebyshev polynomials of discrete variables
[
18
,
24
]

\displaystyle t_{m}(x,N)=h_{m}^{(0,0)}(x,N)

in the form

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle=\frac{1}{2n}(2\mu)^{-k}t_{k+1}(n-\ell-1,-2\ell-1),\qquad\text{when}\quad k=-1,0,1,2,\dots,

(1.2)

and

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle=\frac{1}{2n}(2\mu)^{-k}t_{-k-2}(n-\ell-1,-2\ell-1),\qquad\text{when}\quad k=-2,-3,\dots,-2\ell-2.

(1.3)

The parameters are
\mu=Z/na_{0}
with
a_{0}=\hbar^{2}/me^{2}
the Bohr radius and
Z
is the nuclear charge. The constants
m
and
e
are the mass and
charge of the electron, respectively.

The identity

\displaystyle t_{k}(n-\ell-1,-2\ell-1)=\frac{\Gamma(2\ell+k+2)}{\Gamma(2\ell+2)}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-k, \, k+1, \, -n+ \ell+ 1 }{1, \, 2 \ell+ 2}\bigg|{\,1}\right)

follows from (
1.1
). Then (
1.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=\frac{1}{2n(2\mu)^{k}}\frac{\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)}{\Gamma(2\ell+2)}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-1-k, \, k+2, \, -n+\ell+1}{1, \,2 \ell+2}\bigg|{\,1}\right)

for
k=-1,0,1,2,\dots
, and (
1.3
)

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=\frac{1}{2n(2\mu)^{k}}\frac{\Gamma(2\ell-k)}{\Gamma(2\ell+2)}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-2+k, \, -k+3, \, -n+\ell+1}{1, \, 2 \ell+2}\bigg|{\,1}\right)

for
k=-2,-3,\dots,-2\ell-2
, where the dependence upon the parameters
n
and
\ell
have been made explicit.

In the quantum case, the radial component of the wave function for a hydrogen atom with nuclear charge
Z
is characterized by two quantum numbers:
n
the

principal quantum number
and
\ell
the
orbital number
. The corresponding normalized radial function is

\displaystyle R_{n\ell}(r)=A_{n\ell}(2\mu r)^{\ell}\exp(-\mu r)L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(2\mu r),

where the normalization constant is

\displaystyle A_{n\ell}=\sqrt{\frac{(2\mu)^{3}}{2n}\frac{(n-\ell-1)!}{(n+\ell)!}}

and

\displaystyle L_{m}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+m+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-m}{1+\alpha}\bigg|{\,x}\right)

is the associated Laguerre function; see
[
14
, formula 8.972.1]
. The expectation value of a power of the radial coordinate is given by

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=(2\mu)^{2\ell}A_{n\ell}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k}e^{-2\mu r}\big[L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(2\mu r)\big]^{2}dr,

(1.4)

with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The goal of the work is to compute the integral in (
1.4
) by the
method of brackets
, to illustrate its flexibility. The reader will find in
[
3
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
]
a collection of examples of definite integrals evaluated by this method. The basic procedure is described in Section
3
.

The examples presented here are to be considered as the beginning of a series of calculations of integrals related to the hydrogen atom. These include the evaluation of the integral
[
13
]

\displaystyle J_{nms}^{\alpha\beta}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{\alpha+s}L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)L_{m}^{\beta}(x)dx

given by S.K. Suslov and B. Trey
[
25
]
. The method of brackets provides an alternative method of proof that
only uses
the hypergeometric representation of the Laguerre function. The method can also be used to discuss the relativistic situation. Details will appear elsewhere.

The reductions of the formulas discussed here uses basic properties of the gamma function, such as

\displaystyle\Gamma(a+n)=\Gamma(a)(a)_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(a)_{-n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(1-a)_{n}}\qquad\text{for}\quad a\in\mathbb{R},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.5)

Here
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol.

2
A direct evaluation

This section presents a direct evaluation of the integral

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=(2\mu)^{2\ell}A_{n\ell}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k}e^{-2\mu r}\big[L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(2\mu r)\big]^{2}dr

(2.1)

given in (
1.4
). The proof is based on some identities for the associated Laguerre function appearing in the integrand. The methods presented here are then compared with the evaluation by the
method of brackets
explained in the next section.

The first identity used to modify the integrand appears in
[
14
, formula 8.976.3]

\displaystyle\big[L_{m}^{\alpha}(x)\big]^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+m+1)}{2^{2m}\Gamma(m+1)}\sum_{s=0}^{m}\binom{2m-2s}{m-s}\frac{\Gamma(2s+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+s+1)\Gamma(s+1)}L_{2s}^{2\alpha}(2x).

(2.2)

Therefore

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=(2\mu)^{2\ell}A_{n\ell}^{2}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+n+1)}{2^{2(n-\ell-1)}\Gamma(n-\ell)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big\langle r^{k} \big\rangle_{n \ell} =}{}\times\sum_{s=0}^{n-\ell-1}\binom{2(n-\ell-1-s)}{n-\ell-1-s}\frac{\Gamma(2s+1)}{\Gamma(2\ell+2+s)\Gamma(s+1)}G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu),

(2.3)

where

\displaystyle G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k}e^{-2\mu r}L_{2s}^{2(2\ell+1)}(4\mu r)dr.

(2.4)

To obtain an expression for
G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)
, the representation

\displaystyle L_{n}^{a}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(a+n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(1+a)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-n}{1+a}\bigg|{\,x}\right)

(2.5)

for the Laguerre function (see
[
14
, formula 8.972.1]
) is used.

Theorem 2.1
.

The integral
G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)
is given by

\displaystyle G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)(2\mu)^{2\ell+k+3}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{-2s},\, {2 \ell+ k + 3}}{4 \ell+ 3}\bigg|{\,2}\right).

Proof.

The hypergeometric representation (
2.5
) shows that

\displaystyle L_{2s}^{2(2\ell+1)}(4\mu r)=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-2s}{4 \ell+ 3 }\bigg|{\,4\mu r}\right).

Expanding the hypergeometric function gives

\displaystyle G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{(-2s)_{j}}{(4\ell+3)_{j}}\frac{(4\mu r)^{j}}{j!}r^{2\ell+2+k}e^{-2\mu r}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)}\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{(-2s)_{j}}{(4\ell+3)_{j}j!}(4\mu)^{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\ell+2+k+j}e^{-2\mu r}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)}\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{(-2s)_{j}(4\mu)^{j}}{(4\ell+3)_{j}j!}\frac{\Gamma(2\ell+k+j+3)}{(2\mu)^{2\ell+k+j+3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)(2\mu)^{2\ell+k+3}}\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{(-2s)_{j}2^{j}}{(4\ell+3)_{j}j!}\Gamma(2\ell+k+j+3)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)(2\mu)^{2\ell+k+3}}\sum_{j=0}^{2s}\frac{(-2s)_{j}(2\ell+k+3)_{j}}{(4\ell+3)_{j}j!}2^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)(2\mu)^{2\ell+k+3}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-2s)_{j}(2\ell+k+3)_{j}}{(4\ell+3)_{j}j!}2^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(4\ell+2s+3)\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)}{\Gamma(2s+1)\Gamma(4\ell+3)(2\mu)^{2\ell+k+3}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{-2s},\, {2 \ell+ k + 3}}{4 \ell+ 3}\bigg|{\,2}\right).

This is the stated form for
G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)
.
∎

Note 2.2
.

Observe that
s\in\mathbb{N}
, so the hypergeometric function in the expression for
G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)
is actually a polynomial in its last variable. Thus, there are no convergence issues.

The expression for
G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)
and (
2.3
) are used to produce the next result (after the change
s\mapsto n-\ell-1-s
).

Corollary 2.3
.

For
n=1,2,\dots
,
\ell=0,1,\dots,n-1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
2\ell+k+3>0
. The moments of the hydrogen atom are given by

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=\frac{\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)(2n+2\ell)!}{n2^{2n-2\ell-1}(4\ell+2)!(2\mu)^{k}(n+\ell)!(n-\ell-1)!}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big\langle r^{k} \big\rangle_{n \ell} =}{}\times\sum_{s=0}^{n-\ell-1}\frac{\binom{n+\ell}{s}\binom{n-\ell-1}{s}}{\binom{2n+2\ell}{2s}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{-2(n-\ell-1-s)},\, {2 \ell+ k +3}}{4 \ell+ 3}\bigg|{\,2}\right).

Note 2.4
.

The restriction
2\ell+k+3>0
avoids the singularities of the gamma factor
\Gamma(2\ell+k+3)
. Also observe that the first entry in the series
{{}_{2}F_{1}}
in the answer is a negative integer, therefore the series reduces to a finite sum.

3
The method of brackets

The evaluation of the integral giving the mean value
\langle r^{k}\rangle
(
2.1
) presented in the previous section, used the relation (
2.2
) in a fundamental way. A method to evaluate integrals over the half line
[0,\infty)
, based on a small number of rules has been developed in
[
11
,
12
]
. This
method of brackets
is described next. The heuristic rules are currently being placed on solid ground
[
3
]
. The reader will find in
[
6
,
9
,
10
]
a large collection of evaluations of definite integrals that illustrate the power and flexibility of this method.

For
a\in\mathbb{C}
, the symbol

\displaystyle\langle a\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{a-1}dx

is the
bracket
associated to the (divergent) integral on the right. The symbol

\displaystyle\phi_{n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1)}

is called the
indicator
associated to the index
n
. The notation
\phi_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{r}}
, or simply
\phi_{12\cdots r}
, denotes the product

\phi_{i_{1}}\phi_{i_{2}}\cdots\phi_{i_{r}}
.

Rules for the production of bracket series.

Rule

\boldsymbol{{\rm P}_{1}}
.
Assign to the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\;dx
a bracket series:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n}\phi_{n}a(n)\langle\alpha n+\beta\rangle.

Here the coefficients
a(n)
come from an assumed expansion
f(x)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\phi_{n}a(n)x^{\alpha n+\beta-1}
. The extra ‘
-1
’ in the exponent is set for convenience. The coefficients are written as
a(n)
because these will soon be evaluated at complex numbers
n
, not necessarily positive integers. Now we need to state how to convert the bracket series into a number.

Rule

\boldsymbol{{\rm P}_{2}}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, the multinomial power
(a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r})^{\alpha}
is assigned the

r
-dimensional bracket series

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}}\cdots\sum_{n_{r}}\phi_{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{r}}a_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{n_{r}}\frac{\langle-\alpha+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}\rangle}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}.

Rules for the evaluation of a bracket series.

Rule

\boldsymbol{{\rm E}_{1}}
. The one-dimensional bracket series is assigned the value

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\phi_{n}f(n)\langle an+b\rangle=\frac{1}{|a|}f(n^{*})\Gamma(-n^{*}),

where
n^{*}
is obtained from the vanishing of the bracket; that is,
n^{*}
solves
an+b=0
. This is precisely the Ramanujan’s master theorem.

The next rule provides a value for multi-dimensional bracket series of index
0
, that is, the number of sums is equal to the number of brackets.

Rule

\boldsymbol{{\rm E}_{2}}
. Assume the matrix
A=(a_{ij})
is non-singular, then the assignment is

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{n_{r}}\phi_{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\langle a_{11}n_{1}+\cdots+a_{1r}n_{r}+c_{1}\rangle\cdots\langle a_{r1}n_{1}+\cdots+a_{rr}n_{r}+c_{r}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{1}{|\text{det}(A)|}f(n_{1}^{*},\dots,n_{r}^{*})\Gamma(-n_{1}^{*})\cdots\Gamma(-n_{r}^{*}),

where
\{n_{i}^{*}\}
is the (unique) solution of the linear system obtained from the vanishing of the brackets. There is no assignment if
A
is singular.

Rule

\boldsymbol{{\rm E}_{3}}
. Each representation of an integral by a bracket series has associated an
index of the representation
via

\displaystyle\text{index}=\text{number of sums}-\text{number of brackets}.

It is important to observe that the index is attached to a specific representation of the integral and not just to integral itself. The experience obtained by the authors using this method suggests that, among all representations of an integral as a bracket series, the one with
minimal index
should be chosen.

The value of a multi-dimensional bracket series of positive index is obtained by computing all the contributions of maximal rank by Rule
E_{2}
. These contributions to the integral appear as series in the free parameters. Series converging in a common region are added and divergent series are discarded. Any series producing a non-real contribution is also discarded. There is no assignment to a bracket series of negative index.

4
The evaluation of the expectations. A first bracket calculation

This section describes the evaluation of the integral

\displaystyle I_{n,\ell,k}(\mu):=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k}e^{-2\mu r}\big[L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(2\mu r)\big]^{2}dr,

(4.1)

that appeared in (
1.4
) by the method of brackets. The expectation value of a power of the radial coordinate is then given by

\displaystyle\big\langle r^{k}\big\rangle_{n\ell}=(2\mu)^{2\ell}A_{n\ell}^{2}I_{n,\ell,k}(\mu).

This integral can be scaled to

\displaystyle I_{n,\ell,k}(\mu)=\frac{1}{(2\mu)^{3+2\ell+k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2+2\ell+k}e^{-t}\big[L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(t)\big]^{2}dt.

(4.2)

This does not appear in the table
[
14
]
. The closest entry is 7.414.10:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-bx}x^{2a}\big[L_{n}^{a}(x)\big]^{2}dx=\frac{2^{2a}\Gamma\big(a+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)\Gamma\big(n+\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}{\pi(n!)^{2}b^{2a+1}}\Gamma(a+n+1)\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-n,\,a + \tfrac{1}{2}}{\tfrac{1}{2}-n}\bigg|{\left(1-\tfrac{2}{b}\right)^{2}}\right).

Note 4.1
.

In the evaluation of (
4.1
), it is convenient to write it as

\displaystyle I_{n,\ell,k:A,B,C}(\mu):=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k}e^{-Ar}L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(Br)L_{n-\ell-1}^{2\ell+1}(Cr)dr

and then consider the limiting value as
A
,
B
,
C
tend to
2\mu
.

The computation of (
4.2
) described in this section is obtained without any further identities for the Laguerre function. Next section describes the computation of the function
G_{\ell,k,s}(\mu)
, defined in (
2.4
).

The first step is to compute a series representation for the factors in the integrand.

Lemma 4.2
.

The functions in the integrand of (
4.1
) have series given by

\displaystyle e^{-ax}=\sum_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{1}}a^{n_{1}}x^{n_{1}}

and

\displaystyle L_{m}^{\alpha}(x)=\Gamma(\alpha+1+m)\sum_{n_{2}}\phi_{n_{2}}\frac{x^{n_{2}}}{\Gamma(1+m-n_{2})\Gamma(1+\alpha+n_{2})}.

Proof.

The series of the exponential function is elementary. Indeed,

\displaystyle e^{-ax}=\sum_{n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{(-a)^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}!}x^{n_{1}}=\sum_{n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}!}(ax)^{n_{1}}=\sum_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{1}}(ax)^{n_{1}}.

To evaluate the series of the Laguerre function, treat
m
as a real non-integer parameter, and observe that

\displaystyle L_{m}^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1+m)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(m+1)}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-m)_{n_{2}}}{(\alpha+1)_{n_{2}}}\frac{x^{n_{2}}}{n_{2}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1+m)}{\Gamma(m+1)}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(n_{2}-m)}{\Gamma(-m)\Gamma(\alpha+1+n_{2})}\frac{x^{n_{2}}}{n_{2}!}.

The series for the Laguerre function now follows from the identity

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n_{2}-m)}{\Gamma(-m)}=(-1)^{n_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(1+m)}{\Gamma(1+m-n_{2})}

valid for
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\not\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

The series given in Lemma
4.2
are now used directly to evaluate the integral (
4.1
). This gives

\displaystyle I_{n,\ell,k;A,B,C}(\mu)=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k}\left[\sum_{n_{1}}A^{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{1}}r^{n_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n,\ell,k;A,B,C}(\mu) =}{}\times\left[\sum_{n_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+n+1)}{\Gamma(n-\ell-n_{2})\Gamma(2\ell+2+n_{2})}\phi_{n_{2}}B^{n_{2}}r^{n_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n,\ell,k;A,B,C}(\mu) =}{}\times\left[\sum_{n_{3}}\frac{\Gamma(\ell+n+1)}{\Gamma(n-\ell-n_{3})\Gamma(2\ell+2+n_{3})}\phi_{n_{3}}C^{n_{3}}r^{n_{3}}\right]dr

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n,\ell,k;A,B,C}(\mu)}{}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2+2\ell+k+n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}}drA^{n_{1}}B^{n_{2}}C^{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n,\ell,k;A,B,C}(\mu) =}{}\times\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\ell+n+1)}{\Gamma(n-\ell-n_{2})\Gamma(2\ell+2+n_{2})\Gamma(n-\ell-n_{3})\Gamma(2\ell+2+n_{3})}

\displaystyle\hphantom{I_{n,\ell,k;A,B,C}(\mu)}{}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}\langle n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+3+2\ell+k\rangle A^{n_{1}}B^{n_{2}}C^{n_{3}}\phi_{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}}

The moment

The goal is to produce a representation of the series for the Laguerre polynomials, given initially by

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{-n}{\alpha+1}\bigg|{\,x}\right).

This series is now written in a form suitable for the application of the method of brackets:

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-n)_{k_{1}}}{(\alpha+1)_{k_{1}}}\frac{x^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k_{1}}(-n)_{k_{1}}(-\alpha)_{-k_{1}}\frac{x^{k_{1}}}{k_{1}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(-n)\Gamma(-\alpha)}\sum_{k_{1}}\phi_{1}\Gamma(-n+k_{1})\Gamma(-\alpha-k_{1})x^{k_{1}}.

To produce a bracket series representation of the last expression, observe that

\displaystyle\Gamma(\beta)=\sum_{\ell}\phi_{\ell}\langle\beta+\ell\rangle

and this leads to

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(-n)\Gamma(-\alpha)}\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}k_{3}}\phi_{123}\langle-n+k_{1}+k_{2}\rangle\langle-\alpha-k_{1}+k_{3}\rangle x^{k_{1}}.

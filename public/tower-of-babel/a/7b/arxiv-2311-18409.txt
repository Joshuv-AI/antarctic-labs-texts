Separated wave equations for all metric components over the Kerr background
Jianwei Mei

Abstract
This paper describes a method that seeks to find the fully separated wave equations for all the metric components over the Kerr background directly. Unfortunately, the solution found in the original version is purely gauge. The main text has not been corrected, as a follow up updated is hoped for and the correct solution is still being searched with the method.



Introduction—
Black hole is one of the most intriguing predictions of
GR
(
GR
). Observations indicate that almost every galaxy has a massive black hole at its center
Kormendy and Richstone 1995
and galaxies like the Milky Way may contain millions of stellar mass black holes
Elbert et al. 2018
. Given the difficulty for astrophysical black holes to maintain significant amount of electric charges
Gibbons 1975
;
Ghosh and Afrin 2023
, the Kerr hypothesis
Carter 1971
;
Bekenstein 1996
;
Chrusciel et al. 2012
essentially predicts that all stationary black holes in nature are described by the Kerr metric
Kerr 1963
,

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dt^{2}+H\Big(\frac{dr^{2}}{X}+\frac{dx^{2}}{Y}\Big)+(r^{2}+a^{2})Yd\phi^{2}

(1)

\displaystyle+\frac{2Mr(dt-aYd\phi)^{2}}{H}\,,

where
X=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr\,
,
Y=1-x^{2}
and
H=r^{2}+a^{2}x^{2}\,
.

The breakthrough in
GW
(
GW
) detection has opened the door to test the nature of black holes with unparalleled precision
Cardoso and Gualtieri 2016
;
Barack et al. 2019
. One example is to precisely map the surrounding geometry of a black hole
Ryan 1995
. Extremely high precision might be possible with a type of
GW
sources called
EMRI
, which are systems consisted of a stellar mass compact object inspiralling into a central massive black hole, making about
{\cal O}(10^{5})
round motions before the final plunge. With future space-based
GW
detectors such as TianQin
Luo et al. 2016
;
Mei et al. 2021
, LISA
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
and more
Gong et al. 2021
, one may measure the intrinsic parameters of
EMRI
to the order
{\cal O}(10^{-6})

Fan et al. 2020
;
Zi et al. 2021
;
Seoane et al. 2023
. Another example is to measure the ringdown signal of a deformed black hole settling down to a stationary state
Franchini and Völkel 2023
. By using future space-based
GW
detectors to detect the ringdown signals from
MBHB
(
MBHB
) events, one may constrain potential deviations from
GR
to the level
{\cal O}(10^{-3})

Shi et al. 2019
;
Arun et al. 2022
. The expected precisions are so high that nonlinear effects are needed in some of the waveform modeling, such as the self-force effect of
EMRI

Poisson et al. 2011
;
Pound and Wardell 2021
and the nonlinear effect in black hole ringdowns
Cheung et al. 2023
;
Mitman et al. 2023
.

BHPT
(
BHPT
) is essential in formulating the desired non-linear effects
Afshordi et al. 2023
. At the basis of
BHPT
, the fully separated metric wave equations over the Schwarzschild background have been constructed by Regge and Wheeler
Regge and Wheeler 1957
and Zerilli
Zerilli 1970
, while separated equations for the metric waves over the Kerr background are only known in a limited form. The separated Kerr perturbation equations were originally discovered by Teukolsky in the early 1970’s for the linearized Weyl scalars
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{4}

Teukolsky 1972
;
Teukolsky 1973
;
Teukolsky 2015
, and were then extended to cover the metric perturbations by reconstructing them from a Hertz potential through the CCK procedure
Chrzanowski 1975
;
Kegeles and Cohen 1979
;
Wald 1978
;
Stewart 1979
. The CCK procedure is limited to the radiation gauge and the source-free case, the metric perturbation is determined up to a stationary and axisymmetric piece, and the construction needs to solve an inversion problem involving higher order differential equations
Wald 1973
;
Spiers et al. 2023
;
Dolan et al. 2023
. This has become an obstacle to waveform modeling involving higher order perturbations, for which the lower order perturbations are needed as a source
Whiting and Price 2005
;
Ioka and Nakano 2007
. Overcoming the difficulties of the CCK procedure and formulating non-linear perturbations of the Kerr black hole is a challenging task, and the field is still being actively developed, see, e.g.,
Merlin et al. 2016
;
Green et al. 2020
;
Loutrel et al. 2021
;
Toomani et al. 2022
;
Aly and Stojkovic 2023
;
Wardell et al. 2023
;
Ripley et al. 2021
;
Dolan et al. 2023
;
Sberna et al. 2022
;
Yang et al. 2015
;
Green et al. 2023
for more references.

In this work, I present the fully separated wave equations for all metric components over the Kerr background. To obtain the result, the Killing-Yano tensor of the Kerr metric is used to construct a symmetry operator that commutates with the wave operator, the eigen equation of the symmetry operator is used to introduce the needed constant of separation, and then the wave equation and the eigen equation of the symmetry operator are solved simultaneously.

There have been previous works attempting to derive separated wave equations for all the metric components under some restricted conditions, such as in the near-horizon extremal Kerr spacetimes
Chen and Stein 2017
and in the small rotation limit
Franchini 2023
. Apart from the immediate application to
GW
waveform modeling, the separated equations can also be useful for other important problems, such as the nonlinear stability of black hole solutions
Dafermos et al. 2021
;
Yang et al. 2015
;
Klainerman and Szeftel 2023
.

In the following, all covariant derivatives are defined with the background metric (
1
), and a prime on any function always means a derivative with respect to the corresponding argument of that function.

Method—
Consider the general problem of separating the variables for the wave equation,

\widetilde{\Box}\Psi=0\,,

(2)

where the details of the wave operator
\widetilde{\Box}
will depend on the nature of the field
\Psi\,
that it acts on.

The separability of (
2
) over the Kerr background is known to be related to the existence of a Killing-Yano tensor
Walker and Penrose 1970
;
Frolov et al. 2017
,

\displaystyle k_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}0&ax&ar&0\cr-ax&0&0&a^{2}xY\cr-ar&0&0&r(r^{2}+a^{2})\cr 0&-a^{2}xY&-r(r^{2}+a^{2})&0\end{matrix}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mu,\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t,r,x,\phi\,,

(3)

which satisfies
\nabla_{\mu}k_{\nu\rho}+\nabla_{\nu}k_{\mu\rho}=0\,
. The “square” of the Killing-Yano tensor,
K_{\mu\nu}=k_{\mu\rho}k_{\nu}^{~\rho}\,
, satisfies
\nabla_{\mu}K_{\nu\rho}+\nabla_{\nu}K_{\rho\mu}+\nabla_{\rho}K_{\mu\nu}=0\,
. Note
K=K_{\alpha}^{~\alpha}=2H\,
.

To help separate the variables in (
2
), one can construct a symmetry operator,
{\cal K}
, that commutates with
\widetilde{\Box}
,

[\widetilde{\Box}\,,\,{\cal K}]=0\,,

(4)

and then the eigen-equation

{\cal K}\Psi=\lambda\Psi\,,

(5)

will supply the needed constant of separation, here denoted by
\lambda
. I shall focus on
{\cal K}
being a double-quadratic operator, i.e., one that is quadratic both in the covariant derivative and in the Killing-Yano tensor. Similar to
\widetilde{\Box}\,
, the detail of
{\cal K}
will also depend on the nature of the field that it acts on.

In the following, I apply the method to the scalar, vector and metric wave equations one after another.

Scalar—
Separated scalar wave equations over the Kerr background have been explicitly known long ago
Brill et al. 1972
. I use it as the simplest example to illustrate the method. For the purpose of this work, it is enough to consider the massless and source-free scalar field. The field equation is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Box}\Phi\equiv\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\Phi=0\,.

(6)

The symmetry operator can be constructed as

\displaystyle{\cal K}\Phi=-\nabla\mu(K^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\Phi)\,.

(7)

One can then solve (
6
) in combination with the following equation,

\displaystyle{\cal K}\Phi=\lambda\Phi\,,

(8)

which brings an independent constant of separation,
\lambda\,
.

Since the Kerr background is stationary and axisymmetric, one can use the ansatz

\Phi(t,r,x,\phi)=\Phi(r,x)e^{i(wt-m\phi)}\,.

(9)

In this way, the dependence on the variables
t
and
\phi
is easily separated, and the problem is reduced to separating the remaining variables
r
and
x\,
. The final step is achieved with
\Phi(r,x)=R(r)P(x)
and

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{X}\Big[\lambda-\frac{[w(r^{2}+a^{2})-ma]^{2}}{X}\Big]R-\frac{2(r-M)}{X}R^{\prime}\,,

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{Y}\Big[\lambda-\frac{(waY-m)^{2}}{Y}\Big]P+\frac{2x}{Y}P^{\prime}\,.

(10)

By letting
\lambda=Q+(m-wa)^{2}\,
, one can recover the result of
Brill et al. 1972
in the massless and source-free case.

Vector—
Separated vector wave equations over the Kerr background have also been known: based on
Teukolsky 1972
, Chandrasekhar has solved for the vector field in terms of the separated Teukolsky functions
Chandrasekhar 1976
;
Chandrasekhar 1985
, and this work has been generalized to type-D backgrounds by Torres Del Castillo
Torres
Del Castillo 1988
. Lunin has re-derived the separated equations with symmetry considerations and by using a special ansatz
Lunin 2017
. Here I re-derive the separated equations again by constructing several symmetry operators that commutate with the wave operator.

For the purpose of this work, it is enough to consider the source-free Maxwell equations,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Box}A^{\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}A^{\mu}=0\,.

(11)

Limited to the expressions that are both quadratic in the derivatives and in the Killing-Yano tensor, four combinations that commutate with (
11
) off-shell can be found,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{1}A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}k^{\gamma\mu}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}k^{\alpha\beta}+A_{\alpha}k_{\beta\gamma}\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\mu}k^{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}A^{\mu}\nabla_{\gamma}k_{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\gamma}k^{\alpha\beta}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{2}A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}k_{\beta\gamma}k^{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\mu}A^{\alpha}+k_{\beta\gamma}\nabla_{\alpha}A^{\alpha}\nabla^{\mu}k^{\beta\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{3}A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{\beta\gamma}k_{\alpha}^{~\gamma}\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla^{\beta}A^{\mu}-k_{\beta\gamma}\nabla^{\gamma}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}A^{\mu}

\displaystyle-A_{\alpha}k^{\gamma\mu}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla_{\gamma}k^{\alpha\beta}-A_{\alpha}\nabla^{\gamma}k^{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\mu}k_{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle-2\Big(k_{\alpha\gamma}\nabla^{\mu}k_{\beta}^{~\gamma}-2k_{\beta\gamma}\nabla^{\mu}k_{\alpha}^{~\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad+k^{\gamma\mu}\nabla_{\gamma}k_{\alpha\beta}\Big)\nabla^{\beta}A^{\alpha}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{4}A^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{\alpha\gamma}k_{\beta}^{~\mu}\nabla^{\beta}\nabla^{\gamma}A^{\alpha}+\Big(k_{\alpha\gamma}\nabla^{\mu}k_{\beta}^{~\gamma}

(12)

\displaystyle-k_{\beta\gamma}\nabla^{\mu}k_{\alpha}^{~\gamma}+2k^{\gamma\mu}\nabla_{\gamma}k_{\alpha\beta}\Big)\nabla^{\beta}A^{\alpha}\,.

It turns out that not all the operators are on equal footing:
{\cal K}_{1}A\equiv A
and so is trivial,
{\cal K}_{2}A
vanishes in the Lorenz gauge,
\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0\,
, while
{\cal K}_{4}A
is the only one that is invariant under the gauge transformation,
A\;\rightarrow\;A+\nabla\tilde{\Phi}\,
, with
\tilde{\Phi}
being an arbitrary function, and that is also commutating with all the other operators:

[{\cal K}_{4}\,,\,{\cal Q}]=0\,,\quad{\cal Q}\in\{\widetilde{\Box}\,,\,{\cal K}_{i}\,,\,i=1,2,3\}\,.

(13)

Because of these properties,
{\cal K}_{4}
has been chosen as the symmetry operator, i.e., (
11
) is solved in combination with

{\cal K}_{4}A^{\mu}=\lambda A^{\mu}\,,

(14)

where
\lambda
is the new constant of separation for the vector field. I have not studied the cases when other symmetry operators are used instead.

With the ansatz

A^{\mu}(t,r,x,\phi)=A^{\mu}(r,x)e^{iwt-im\phi}\,,

(15)

(
11
) and (
14
) can be solved using

\displaystyle A^{t}(r,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\lambda^{2}ar(r^{2}+a^{2})(m-wa)Y(Z_{1}+Z_{2})}{HX_{2}Y_{2}}\,,

\displaystyle A^{\phi}(r,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\lambda^{2}a^{2}r(m-wa)Y(Z_{1}-Z_{2})}{HX_{2}Y_{2}}\,,

\displaystyle A^{r}(r,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda a^{2}(m-wa)^{2}XY}{HX_{2}Y_{2}}\tilde{R}\tilde{P}\,,

\displaystyle A^{\theta}(r,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{RP}{H}\,,

(16)

where

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[\tilde{R}-\frac{2aX_{4}(r^{2}+a^{2})Y-H_{2}X(m-waY)}{2\lambda a^{2}r(r^{2}+a^{2})(m-wa)XY}X_{2}R\Big]\tilde{P}

\displaystyle-\frac{xH_{2}X_{2}Y_{2}RP}{2\lambda a^{2}r(r^{2}+a^{2})(m-wa)^{2}Y^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle Z_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{HX_{2}(m-waY)R}{2\lambda a^{2}r(r^{2}+a^{2})(m-wa)Y}

(17)

\displaystyle\times\Big[\tilde{P}-\frac{xY_{2}P}{(m-wa)(m-waY)Y}\Big]\,,

and

\displaystyle\tilde{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\prime}+\frac{\lambda rX_{4}R}{a(m-wa)X}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\prime}+\frac{\lambda x(m-waY)P}{(m-wa)Y}\,,

\displaystyle X_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}r^{2}+a^{2}(m-wa)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}r^{2}-a^{2}(m-wa)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle X_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle am-(r^{2}+a^{2})w\,,

\displaystyle Y_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle wa(m-wa)Y+\lambda(m-waY)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}+a^{2}(2-x^{2})\,.

(18)

The two functions,
R=R(r)
and
P=P(x)\,
, satisfy

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{[\lambda^{2}+wa(m-wa)]XX_{3}-\lambda X_{2}X_{4}^{2}}{\lambda X^{2}X_{2}}R

\displaystyle-\frac{(r^{2}-a^{2})X_{2}-XX_{3}}{rXX_{2}}R^{\prime}\,,

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{2}^{2}-\lambda^{2}Y[2wax^{2}(m+waY)+Y_{2}]}{\lambda Y^{2}Y_{2}}P

(19)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\lambda x(m^{2}-w^{2}a^{2}Y^{2})}{YY_{2}}P^{\prime}\,.

In this result, the separation constant has a clear operator-based meaning.

Metric—
In
GR
, the vacuum equations governing the metric perturbations over the Kerr background (
1
) can be obtained from perturbing the Ricci tensor,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\Box}h)_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{\rho}(\nabla_{\mu}h_{\nu\rho}+\nabla_{\nu}h_{\mu\rho}-\nabla_{\rho}h_{\mu\nu})-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}h

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

where
h_{\mu\nu}\equiv\delta g_{\mu\nu}\,
. Limited to the combinations that are both quadratic in the derivatives and in the Killing-Yano tensor, the following four operators can be found,

\displaystyle({\cal K}_{1}h)_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(k^{\alpha\rho}k^{\beta}_{~\nu}-k^{\beta\rho}k^{\alpha}_{~\nu}-k^{\alpha\beta}k^{\rho}_{~\nu})\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}h_{\rho\mu}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}k^{\rho\alpha}k_{\rho\nu}[\nabla^{\beta}\,,\,\nabla_{\mu}]h_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{3}{4}k^{\alpha\beta}k^{\rho}_{~\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}h_{\rho\mu}

\displaystyle+k_{\beta\rho}h_{\alpha\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\nabla^{\rho}k^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}h_{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\rho}k_{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\rho}k^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{4}hk^{\beta}_{~\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}k_{\beta\mu}\,,

\displaystyle({\cal K}_{2}h)_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k_{\rho\nu}\nabla^{\alpha}k^{\rho}_{~\mu}+k_{\rho\mu}\nabla^{\alpha}k^{\rho}_{~\nu})\nabla^{\beta}{\tilde{h}}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle-k^{\rho\alpha}(k_{\rho\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\beta}{\tilde{h}}_{\alpha\beta}+k_{\rho\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\nabla^{\beta}{\tilde{h}}_{\alpha\beta})\,,

\displaystyle({\cal K}_{3}h)_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(K\nabla^{\rho}{\tilde{h}}_{\rho\nu})+\nabla_{\nu}(K\nabla^{\rho}{\tilde{h}}_{\rho\mu})\,,

\displaystyle({\cal K}_{4}h)_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k^{\alpha\rho}k^{\beta}_{~\nu}-k^{\beta\rho}k^{\alpha}_{~\nu})\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}h_{\rho\mu}

(21)

\displaystyle+(k^{\alpha\rho}k^{\beta}_{~\mu}-k^{\beta\rho}k^{\alpha}_{~\mu})\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}h_{\rho\nu}

\displaystyle-k^{\beta\rho}(k_{\alpha\rho}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla^{\alpha}h_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\beta}k_{\alpha\rho}\nabla^{\alpha}h_{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle+\nabla^{\alpha}k^{\beta\rho}(h_{\alpha\mu}\nabla_{\nu}k_{\beta\rho}+h_{\alpha\nu}\nabla_{\mu}k_{\beta\rho})

\displaystyle-h^{\alpha\beta}(\nabla_{\mu}k_{\alpha\rho}\nabla_{\nu}k_{\beta}^{~\rho}+\nabla_{\nu}k_{\alpha\rho}\nabla_{\mu}k_{\beta}^{~\rho})

\displaystyle+2(J_{\nu}^{~\alpha\beta}-J^{\alpha~\beta}_{~\nu})\nabla_{\beta}h_{\alpha\mu}

\displaystyle+2(J_{\mu}^{~\alpha\beta}-J^{\alpha~\beta}_{~\mu})\nabla_{\beta}h_{\alpha\nu}\,,

where
{\tilde{h}}_{\alpha\beta}=h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}h\,
,
h=g^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}\,
, and
J^{\alpha}_{~\mu\nu}=k_{\rho\mu}\nabla^{\alpha}k^{\rho}_{~\nu}-k_{\rho\nu}\nabla^{\alpha}k^{\rho}_{~\mu}\,
. Like in the vector case, the operators vary in their properties:
({\cal K}_{1}h)_{\mu\nu}\equiv h_{\mu\nu}
and so is trivial; both
{\cal K}_{2}
and
{\cal K}_{3}
vanish in the Lorenz/de Donder gauge,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\alpha}{\tilde{h}}_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla^{\alpha}h_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\beta}h=0\,.

(22)

By looking for the simultaneous solutions to the wave equation (
20
), the Lorenz gauge condition (
22
) and the following eigen equation,

\displaystyle({\cal K}_{4}h)_{\mu\nu}=\lambda h_{\mu\nu}\,,

(23)

where
\lambda
is the constant of separation, one can find that the equations can be fully separated with:

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}(t,r,x,\phi)=e^{i(wt-m\phi)}f_{\mu\nu}(r,x)\,,

(24)

and

\displaystyle f_{tt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{2}RP+\frac{MXY}{H^{3}}\Big(\frac{2a^{2}rx}{X}RP^{\prime}+\frac{r^{2}-a^{2}x^{2}}{Y}R^{\prime}P\Big)\,,

\displaystyle f_{tr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\Big(\frac{M[am-(r^{2}+a^{2})w](r^{2}-a^{2}x^{2})}{H^{2}X}R+wR^{\prime}\Big)P\,,

\displaystyle f_{tx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iR\Big[\frac{2Marx(m-awY)}{H^{2}Y}P+wP^{\prime}\Big]\,,

\displaystyle f_{t\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-mwRP-\frac{MaY}{H^{3}}\Big[2r(r^{2}+a^{2})xRP^{\prime}+(r^{2}-a^{2}x^{2})XR^{\prime}P\Big]\,,

\displaystyle f_{rr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{X}\Big(\lambda-\frac{[am-(r^{2}+a^{2})w]^{2}}{X}\Big)RP-\frac{a^{2}xY}{HX}RP^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2r}\Big(\frac{3r^{2}+a^{2}x^{2}}{H}+\frac{r^{2}-a^{2}}{X}\Big)R^{\prime}P\,,

\displaystyle f_{rx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{rRP^{\prime}+a^{2}xR^{\prime}P}{H}-R^{\prime}P^{\prime}\,,

\displaystyle f_{r\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{X}\Big\{mr-\frac{M}{H^{2}}\Big(2mr^{4}-a[3r^{4}w+a^{3}(m-aw)x^{2}]Y+a^{2}r^{2}(1+x^{2})(m-awY)\Big)\Big\}RP+imR^{\prime}P\,,

\displaystyle f_{xx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda Y-(m-awY)^{2}}{Y^{2}}RP-\frac{x}{Y}\Big(1-\frac{a^{2}Y}{H}\Big)RP^{\prime}-\frac{rX}{HY}R^{\prime}P\,,

\displaystyle f_{x\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ix}{Y}\Big(m+\frac{2a^{2}MrY(m-awY)}{H^{2}}\Big)RP+imRP^{\prime}\,,

\displaystyle f_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}RP+\frac{xY}{H}\Big[r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2a^{2}Mr(r^{2}+a^{2}+H)Y}{H^{2}}\Big]RP^{\prime}-\frac{XY}{H}\Big[r-\frac{a^{2}M(r^{2}-a^{2}x^{2})Y}{H^{2}}\Big]R^{\prime}P\,.

(25)

The equations for the functions
R=R(r)
and
P=P(x)
are exactly the same as (
10
). Given the separated wave equations in the vacuum case, the source terms can be added as in
Teukolsky 1973
. The properties of these equations will be investigated in future works.

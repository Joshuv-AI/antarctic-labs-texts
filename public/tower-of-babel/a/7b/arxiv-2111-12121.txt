Galleries for root subsystems
Vladimir Shchigolev

Abstract
. We consider projection and lifting of labelled galleries to and from roots subsystems. Our constructions allow us to construct some topological embeddings of Bott-Samelson varieties skew equivariant with respect to the compact torus and order-preserving on the sets of points fixed by it.



1.
Introduction

Let
E
be a Euclidian space and
\Phi\subset E
be a finite root system. We require neither that
\Phi
span
E

nor that
\Phi
be crystallographic. A subset
\Psi\subset\Phi
is called a
root subsystem
if
it is not empty and is stable under the reflections with respect to the roots
\alpha\in\Psi

(in other words, if
\Psi
is a root system in its own right).
Root subsystems were objects of extensive study, see for example
[
W
]
and
[
O
]
.
Moreover A. Borel and J. de Siebental
[
BdS
]
established a connection between closed connected subgroups
of compact Lie subgroups of maximal rank and the maximal closed root subsystems.
The aim of this paper is to relate
chambers and galleries for the root systems

\Phi
and
\Psi
and apply these results to Bott-Samelson varieties.

For any root
\alpha\in\Phi
, we denote by
L_{\alpha}
the hyperplane in
E
perpendicular to
\alpha
.
We consider
E
with respect to the metric topology and for any subset
S\subset E
we denote by
\bar{S}
its closure.
For a subset
X\subset\Phi
, we denote the connected components of the difference

E\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in X}L_{\alpha}
by
\mathbf{Ch}_{X}
and call them
chambers
with respect to
X
.
We say that a chamber
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{X}
is
attached
to
L_{\alpha}
if
\overline{C}\cap L_{\alpha}
has a nonempty interior
in
L_{\alpha}
. Similarly, two chambers
C,D\in\mathbf{Ch}_{X}
are
connected
through
L_{\alpha}
if

\overline{C}\cap\overline{D}\cap L_{\alpha}
has a nonempty interior in
L_{\alpha}
.

For any chamber
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
, we denote by
C_{\Psi}
the unique chamber of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
containing
C
.
Proposition
1
, allows us to make a (labelled) gallery of chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}

from a gallery of chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
in the obvious way, see Section
3.2
.
We call this procedure
projection
.

The transition in the opposite direction, called here
lifting
, is more difficult and is not unique.
First we have to lift chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
to chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
, see Theorem
4
.
Then we prove Theorem
11
for galleries. This result establishes
existence and uniqueness in a weak form.

The projection and lifting procedures applied successively allow us to construct the so-called
p
-pairs of galleries
described by some wall crossing properties, see Definition
8
. We also
discuss gluing of galleries produced this way, see Section
3.5
.

Finally, we embed Bott-Somelson varieties into one another in Section
4

as an application of the theory we develop. Our main result here is Theorem
21
,
which asserts the existence of topological embeddings of Bott-Samelson varieties
skew equivariant with respect to the compact torus
K
and order-preserving on the set of the
K
-fixed points.

2.
Roots subsystems

2.1.
Definitions and notation

We assume in the sequel the notation of the introduction.
We denote by
\omega_{\alpha}
the reflection of
E
through
L_{\alpha}
.
The Weyl group
W
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(E)
generated by all reflections
\omega_{\alpha}
.
We denote its neutral element by
1
. The scalar product on
E
is denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
For any subset
X\subset E
, we set
-X=\{-x\mathbin{\rm|}x\in X\}
and denote by
\mathbb{R}X
the set of all
\mathbb{R}
-linear combinations

c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}
, where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
.
This linear combination is called
positive
(respectively,
negative
) if
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}>0
(respectively,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}<0
).

We will generally follow
[
St
]
and recall now briefly the main definitions introduced therein.
An
ordering
of
E
is a subset
E^{+}\subset E
closed under addition and multiplication
by positive scalars such that
E^{+}\cap(-E^{+})=0
and
E^{+}\cup(-E^{+})=E
.
The
positive
(respectively,
negative
)
system
of
\Phi
relative to this ordering is the intersection
\Phi^{+}=\Phi\cap E^{+}

(respectively,
\Phi^{-}=\Phi\cap(-E^{+})
). The
fundamental chamber
of this positive system is

\{e\in E\mathbin{\rm|}\forall\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\;(e,\alpha)>0\}.

A
simple system
of
\Phi
is a subset
X\subset\Phi
that is linearly independent
and any root of
\Phi
is either a positive or a negative linear combination of elements of
X
.

For any chamber
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
, we denote by
\Phi^{+}(C)
the positive system with fundamental chamber
C
, that is,

\Phi^{+}(C)=\{\alpha\in\Phi\mathbin{\rm|}\forall e\in C\;(e,\alpha)>0\}.

Clearly,
C
is the fundamental chamber of
\Phi^{+}(C)
.

We also define
\Phi^{-}(C)=-\Phi^{+}(C)
and choose the (unique) simple system
\Phi^{s}(C)
contained in
\Phi^{+}(C)
.
These operators are
W
-invariant:

\Phi^{+}(wC)=w\Phi^{+}(C),\quad\Phi^{-}(wC)=w\Phi^{-}(C),\quad\Phi^{s}(wC)=w\Phi^{s}(C)

(1)

for any
w\in W
. We will use the similar notation
\Psi^{+}(C)
,
\Psi^{-}(C)
and
\Psi^{s}(C)
for
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
.

The proof of the following simple result is left to the reader.

Proposition 1
.

(i) Let
C
and
D
be chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
connected through
L_{\beta}
for
\beta\in\Phi\setminus\Psi
.
Then
C_{\Psi}=D_{\Psi}
.

(ii) Let
C
and
D
be chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
connected through
L_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Psi
. Then
C_{\Psi}
and
D_{\Psi}

are also connected through
L_{\alpha}
. Moreover,
C_{\Psi}=D_{\Psi}
if and only if
C=D
.

Our favorite root subsystems are given by the following result.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Psi
be a nonempty subset of
\Phi
.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\Psi=\mathbb{R}X\cap\Phi
for some nonempty
subset
X\subset\Phi
;

(ii)

\Psi=\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi
.

A subset
\Psi
satisfying these conditions is a root subsystem of
\Phi
.

Proof.

Clearly
(ii)
implies
(i)
.
Suppose conversely that
(i)
is satisfied. As
\Psi\subset\mathbb{R}X
, we get
\mathbb{R}\Psi\subset\mathbb{R}X
.
On the other hand,
X\subset\Psi
, whence
\mathbb{R}X\subset\mathbb{R}\Psi
. Thus
\mathbb{R}X=\mathbb{R}\Psi
and we get
(ii)
.
The last claim holds, as both
\mathbb{R}\Psi
and
\Phi
are stable under the reflections with respect to the roots of
\Psi
.
∎

If the conditions of this lemma hold, we say that
\Psi
is
saturated
in
\Phi
.

2.2.
Lifting

Let
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
and
D\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
.
We say that
C

lifts

D
if
\Psi^{s}(D)\subset\Phi^{s}(C)
.
Obviously, the last inclusion implies
\Psi^{+}(D)\subset\Phi^{+}(C)
.
The converse is not true, see Example
5
.

Lemma 3
.

If
C
lifts
D
, then
C_{\Psi}=D
.

Proof.

As
C
and
D
are the fundamental chambers of
\Phi^{+}(C)
and
\Psi^{+}(D)
, respectively
and
\Psi^{+}(D)\subset\Phi^{+}(C)
, we get
C\subset D
. Hence by definition
D=C_{\Psi}
.
∎

Theorem 4
.

(i)
Suppose that
\Psi
is saturated in
\Phi
.
Then for any
D\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
,
there exists at least one chamber
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
lifting
D
.

​​​(ii) Suppose that a chamber
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
lifts a chamber
D\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
. Then
\Psi
is saturated in
\Phi
.

Proof.

(i) We denote
V=\mathbb{R}\Psi
for brevity. Let
V^{+}
be an ordering of
V
such that

\Psi^{+}(D)=\Psi^{+}(D\cap V)=\Psi\cap V^{+}
. Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}
be a basis of the orthogonal compliment

V^{\perp}=\{e\in E\mathbin{\rm|}\forall v\in V(e,v)=0\}
.
We define
E^{+}
as the set of all sums
c_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}e_{k}+v
,
where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and either
c_{1}=\cdots=c_{k}=0
and
v\in V^{+}

or
v\in V
and there is some
i=1,\ldots,k
such that
c_{1}=\cdots=c_{i-1}=0
and
c_{i}>0
.
It is clear that
E^{+}
is an ordering of
E
and
V^{+}=E^{+}\cap V
.
Moreover, the following property holds:

(
*
)

A positive linear combination of vectors of
E^{+}
belongs to
V

if and only if each vector belongs to
V^{+}
.

Let
C
be the fundamental chamber relative to the positive system
\Phi\cap E^{+}
.
Obviously
\Psi^{+}(D)\subset\Phi^{+}(C)=\Phi\cap E^{+}
. We claim also that
\Psi^{s}(D)\subset\Phi^{s}(C)
.
Indeed let
\alpha\in\Psi^{s}(D)
. Then
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(C)
and we have a decomposition

\alpha=c_{1}\beta_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}\beta_{n}
, where
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in\Phi^{s}(C)

and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}>0
. By (
*
), we get
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in V^{+}
.
As
\Psi
is saturated in
\Phi
, we also get
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in\Psi
, whence
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in\Psi^{+}(D)
.
We get a contradiction unless
n=1
and
c_{1}=1
, which proves the claim.

(ii) Let
\Phi^{s}(C)=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
, where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\Psi^{s}(D)
,

\alpha_{k+1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\notin\Psi^{s}(D)
and all roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
are pairwise distinct.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi^{+}(C)
.
There are two
\mathbb{R}
-linear decompositions

\beta=c_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}\alpha_{k}
and
\beta=d_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}\alpha_{n}
with
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}>0
.
Hence
d_{k+1}=\cdots=d_{n}=0
and
\beta
is a positive linear combination of the roots of
\Psi^{s}(D)
.
Similarly, if
\beta\in\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi^{-}(C)
, then
\beta
is a negative combination of the roots of
\Psi^{s}(D)
.
Thus
\Psi^{s}(D)
is a simple system for both root systems
\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi
and
\Psi
.
As a simple system uniquely determines its root system, we get
\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi=\Psi
.
∎

Example 5
.

Let
\Phi
be the root system of type
A_{2}
with the simple roots
\alpha
and
\beta
.
We consider the root subsystem
\Psi=\{-\alpha,\alpha\}
.
For any root
\gamma\in\Phi
(respectively
\gamma\in\Psi
), we denote by
C(\gamma)
(respectively
D(\gamma)
)
the chamber of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
(respectively
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
) containing
\gamma
.
The only chambers of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
lifting
D(\alpha)
are
C(\alpha+\beta)
and
C(-\beta)
.
On the other hand,

\Psi^{+}(D(\alpha))=\{\alpha\}\subset\{-\beta,\alpha,\alpha+\beta\}=\Phi^{+}(C(\alpha))
and

\Psi^{s}(D(\alpha))=\{\alpha\}\not\subset\{-\beta,\alpha+\beta\}=\Phi^{s}(C(\alpha))
.

Example 6
.

Let
\Phi
be the root system of type
B_{2}
and
\Psi
be its subsystem consisting of the short roots (of type
A_{1}\times A_{1}
).
One root of any simple system
of
\Phi
is long and both roots of any simple system
of
\Psi

are short. Therefore, no chamber of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
lifts a chamber of
\mathbf{Ch}_{\Psi}
. We have
\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi\neq\Psi
,
that is,
\Psi
is not saturated in
\Phi

in accordance with Theorem
4
.

We will need the following additional criterion.

Lemma 7
.

Let
C,D\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
and
\Psi
be a root subsystem of
\Phi
.
The chamber
C
lifts
D_{\Psi}
if and only if there exists

X\subset\Phi^{s}(C)\cap\Phi^{+}(D)
such that
\Psi=\mathbb{R}X\cap\Phi
.
If these conditions hold, then we can take
X=\Psi^{s}(D_{\Psi})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
C
lifts
D_{\Psi}
. Let us take
X=\Psi^{s}(D_{\Psi})
.
By our assumption, we have
X\subset\Phi^{s}(C)
.
On the other hand,
X\subset\Psi^{+}(D_{\Psi})
and
D\subset D_{\Psi}
.
Hence for any
\alpha\in X
and
e\in D
, we get
(\alpha,e)>0
. Thus
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}(D)
.
As
\alpha
was chosen arbitrarily in
X
, we get
X\subset\Phi^{+}(D)
.
Moreover, by Theorem
4
(ii), we get

\mathbb{R}X\cap\Phi=\mathbb{R}\Psi\cap\Phi=\Psi
.

Conversely, suppose that a set
X
as in the formulation of the lemma exists.
We claim that
X
is a simple system for
\Psi
. Indeed let
\Phi^{s}(C)=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
,
where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in X
,

\alpha_{k+1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\notin X
and all roots
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
are pairwise distinct.
Let
\beta\in\Psi\cap\Phi^{+}(C)
. There are two
\mathbb{R}
-linear decompositions

\beta=c_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}\alpha_{k}
and
\beta=d_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}\alpha_{n}
with
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}>0
.
Hence
d_{k+1}=\cdots=d_{n}=0
and we have the positive linear combination
\beta=d_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}\alpha_{k}
.
We get a similar negative linear combination for
\beta\in\Psi\cap\Phi^{-}(C)
. Finally note that the set
X

is linearly independent as a subset of the simple system
\Phi^{s}(C)
.

Let
\alpha\in X
,
e\in D
and
v\in D_{\Psi}
. As
\alpha\in\Psi
, the hyperplane
L_{\alpha}
does not separate
the points
e
and
v
. Hence and from
(e,\alpha)>0
, we get
(v,\alpha)>0
.
Thus we have proved that
\alpha\in\Psi^{+}(D_{\Psi})
. As
\alpha
was chosen arbitrarily in
X
,
we get
X\subset\Psi^{+}(D_{\Psi})
. Hence and from the previous paragraph we get
\Psi^{s}(D_{\Psi})=X\subset\Phi^{s}(C)
.
∎

3.3.
Lifting

We formulate the following definition inspired by Section
2.2
.

Defintition 10
.

A gallery
\Gamma
in
\Phi
lifts a gallery
\Delta
in
\Psi

if the sequences of walls of these galleries coincide and the
i
th chamber of
\Gamma
lifts the i
th
chamber
of
\Delta
for any admissible

.

We are going to prove the following existence and uniqueness result.

Theorem 11
.

For any gallery
\Delta
in
\Psi
and a chamber
C\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\Phi}
lifting the
i
th chamber of
\Delta
,
there exists a unique gallery in
\Phi
lifting
\Delta
whose
i
th chamber is
C
.

Proof.

Existence.
Let us denote

\Delta=(D_{0},L_{\beta_{1}},D_{1},\ldots,L_{\beta_{m}},D_{m})

(4)

We set
C_{i}=C
, define the chambers
C_{1},\ldots,C_{i-1}
recursively (from right to left) by

C_{j-1}=\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{j}&\text{if }D_{j-1}=D_{j};\\
\omega_{\beta_{j}}C_{j}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

and the chambers
C_{i+1},\ldots,C_{m}
recursively (from left to right) by

C_{j+1}=\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{j}&\text{if }D_{j+1}=D_{j};\\
\omega_{\beta_{j+1}}C_{j}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right..

Then we define
\Delta^{C}_{i}=(C_{0},L_{\beta_{1}},C_{1},\ldots,L_{\beta_{m}},C_{m})
and claim that it is the required gallery.

First we consider the case
i=0
. We will apply induction on
m
. The case
m=0
is obvious. Suppose that
m>0
and that the claim is true for the
truncated gallery
\widetilde{\Delta}=(D_{0},L_{\beta_{1}},D_{1},\ldots,L_{\beta_{m-1}},D_{m-1})
.
As
D_{m-1}
is attached to
L_{\beta_{m}}
, we get

\beta_{m}\in\Psi^{s}(D_{m-1})\subset\Phi^{s}(C_{m-1})
. This proves that
C_{m-1}

is attached to
L_{\beta_{m}}
. If
D_{m}=D_{m-1}
, then by definition
C_{m}=C_{m-1}

and we are done. If
D_{m}\neq D_{m-1}
, then
D_{m}=\omega_{\beta_{m}}D_{m-1}

and by definition
C_{m}=\omega_{\beta_{m}}C_{m-1}
.
Hence
\Psi^{s}(D_{m})=\omega_{\beta_{m}}\Psi^{s}(D_{m-1})\subset\omega_{\beta_{m}}\Phi^{s}(C_{m-1})=\Phi^{s}(C_{m})

by (
1
).

The case

can be treated similarly. In the case
0<i<m
, we get
\Delta^{C}_{i}=(\Delta_{[1,i]})^{C}_{i}\cup(\Delta_{[i,m]})_{0}^{C}

and the result follows from the two cases considered above.

Uniqueness.
Let
\Gamma=(C^{\prime}_{1},L_{\beta_{1}},\ldots,C^{\prime}_{m-1},L_{\beta_{m}},C^{\prime}_{m})
be a gallery in
\Phi

\Delta
whose

We are going to prove that
\Gamma=\Delta_{i}^{C}

.
Suppose that we have already proved that

First consider the case

. Suppose that

. Then

.
Changing the sign of
\beta_{j+1}

\beta_{j+1}\in\Psi^{s}(D_{j})
.
As
C^{\prime}_{j+1}
lifts
D_{j+1}
and
C_{j}
lifts

.
On the other hand,

which is a contradiction. Thus we have proved that

Finally, we consider the case

\beta_{j+1}

Arguing inductively with the help of the previous two paragraphs, we can prove that

The prove of this equality for

4.
Bott-Samelson varieties

4.1.
Construction

Let
G
be a semisimple complex algebraic group.
We fix a Borel subgroup
B<G
and a maximal torus
T<B
. We denote by
W
the Weyl group of
G

and by
\prec
the Bruhat order on it.
For any simple reflection
t\in W
, we consider the minimal parabolic subgroup
P_{t}=B\cup BtB
.

Let
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be a sequence of simple reflections. The
classical Bott-Samelson
variety
for
s
is the quotient variety

P_{s_{1}}\times\cdots\times P_{s_{n}}/B^{n}

with the following right action of
B^{n}
:

(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n})(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n})=(p_{1}b_{1},b_{1}^{-1}p_{2}b_{2},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{-1}p_{n}b_{n}).

It is convenient here to redefine Bott-Samelson varieties using only compact groups.
Let
C
,
K
and
C_{t}
be the maximal compact subgroups of
G
,
T
and
P_{t}
, respectively.
The
compactly defined Bott-Samelson variety
for
s
is the quotient variety

C_{s_{1}}\times\cdots\times C_{s_{n}}/K^{n}

under the similar right action of
K^{n}
:

(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{n})=(c_{1}k_{1},k_{1}^{-1}c_{2}k_{2},\ldots,k_{n-1}^{-1}c_{n}k_{n}).

It is well-known
[
GK
]
that both varieties defined above are homeomorphic
(for the same sequence of simple reflections). We will denote them by
\mathrm{BS}(s)
, call them simply the
Bott-Samelson
variety
and always use only the definition via the compact groups.

(5)

There is the

4.5.

\widetilde{\mathbf{Seq}}

The objects of this category are the sequences of simple reflections including the empty one.
Each morphism

. Let

Blowup dynamics for mass critical Half-wave equation in 2D
Vladimir Georgiev, Yuan Li

Abstract
We consider the half-wave equation i ​ u t = D ​ u − | u | ​ u iu_{t}=Du-|u|u in two dimensions. For the initial data u 0 ​ ( x ) ∈ H s ​ ( ℝ 2 ) u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) , s ∈ ( 3 4 , 1 ) s\in\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right) , we obtain the non-radial ground state mass blow-up solutions with the blow-up speed ‖ D 1 2 ​ u ​ ( t ) ‖ L 2 ∼ 1 | t | \|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{|t|} as t → 0 − t\to 0^{-} . 1
Introduction and Main Result

In this paper, we consider the half-wave (first-order wave) equation in two dimensions

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u=Du-|u|u,\\
u(t_{0},x)=u_{0}(x),\ u:I\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval containing the initial time
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\displaystyle\widehat{(Df)}(\xi)=|\xi|\hat{f}(\xi)

denotes the first-order nonlocal fractional derivative. Let us review some basic properties of this equation. The Cauchy problem
(
1.1
) is an infinite-dimensional Hamiltonian system, which have the following three conservation laws:

\displaystyle\text{Mass:}\ \ M(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx=M(u_{0}),

\displaystyle\text{Energy:}\ \ E(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bar{u}(t,x)Du(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(t,x)|^{3}dx=E(u_{0}),

\displaystyle\text{Momentum:}\ \ P(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}-i\nabla u(t,x)\bar{u}(t,x)dx=P(u_{0}).

The equation (
1.1
) possesses a large group of symmetries: If
u(t,x)
is a solution to (
1.1
), then for all
(t_{0},x_{0},\gamma_{0},\lambda_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{*}^{+}
, so is

u(t,x)\mapsto\lambda_{0}u(\lambda_{0}t-t_{0},\lambda_{0}x-x_{0})e^{i\gamma_{0}}.

From the scaling invariant, it is known that equation (
1.1
) is mass critical.

This equation has recently gained much attention. For the general power-type nonlinear terms,
[
1
,
10
,
2
,
5
,
14
]
studied the local/global well-posedness. In particular, for
F(u)=|u|^{p}u
, Krieger, Lenzmann and Raphaël
[
14
]
considered the local well-posedness in one dimensional case; Bellazzini, Georgiev and Visciglia
[
1
]
obtained the local existence in the space
H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
N\geq 2
; furthermore, Hidano and Wang
[
10
]
improved this result and established the local existence in the space
H^{s}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
N\geq 2
, and in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
s\in\left(\max\left\{\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{N+1}{4}\right\},p\right)
and
s\geq s_{c}
, where
p>\max\left\{s_{c},\frac{N-1}{2},\frac{N+1}{4},1\right\}
,
s_{c}=\frac{N}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
. For the blow up solution, Krieger, Lenzmann and Raphaël
[
14
]
first constructed a minimal mass blow-up solutions to the mass critical Half-wave equation in one dimension, and then the authors
[
6
,
7
]
obtained the radial ground state mass blow-up solution in the two and three dimension cases, for other blow-up results, one can see
[
4
,
11
,
17
]
. In additional, Dinh
[
2
]
established the ill well-posedness and low regularity data. Now in the present paper, we aim to extend the result in
[
7
]
and study the non-radial case.

Now we state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
(E_{0},P_{0})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there exist
t^{*}<0
independent of
E_{0}
,
P_{0}
, and a ground state mass solution
u\in C^{0}([t^{*},0);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
,
s\in\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)
, of equation (
1.1
) with

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2},\ E(u(t))=E_{0},\ P(u(t))=P_{0},

which blows up at time
T=0
. More precisely, it holds that

\displaystyle u(t,x)-\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}Q\left(\frac{x-\alpha(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)e^{i\gamma(t)}\rightarrow 0\ \text{in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \text{as}\ t\rightarrow 0^{-},

where

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\lambda^{*}t^{2}+\mathcal{O}(t^{5}),\ \alpha(t)=\mathcal{O}(t^{3}),\ \gamma(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{*}|t|}+\mathcal{O}(t),

with some constant
\lambda^{*}>0
, and the blowup speed is given by:

\displaystyle\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)\|_{2}\sim\frac{C(u_{0})}{|t|}\ \text{as}\ t\rightarrow 0^{-},

where
C(u_{0})>0
is constant depending only on the initial data
u_{0}
.

Let us make some comments on the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

1. We aim to construct an exact solution of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}[Q_{\mathcal{P}}+\epsilon]\left(t,\frac{x-\alpha(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)e^{i\gamma(t)}=\tilde{Q}+\tilde{\epsilon}.

In case of higher dimensions
N\geq 2
the mass critical exponent
1+\frac{2}{N}
becomes smaller, the modulation estimates, the refine energy estimates and the bootstrap argument (especially the
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1/2+\cdot}}
-norm) become more complicated. In
[
7
,
6
]
, radial properties play a crucial role in the discussion. Now in the present paper, radial properties are failure and so we need some new techniques to overcome this difficulty.

2. One can control the
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}}
norm by the weighted
L_{t}^{q}H^{1}_{x}
-norm in the two dimensional and radial case and this is a crucial tool to evaluate some terms in the refine energy estimates and bootstrap argument. However, in the non-radial case, such a control is impossible, so we need new tools.

3. Our proof of the existence of ground state blow-up solution in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
s\in\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)
bases on the
L^{q}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}
and
L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}_{x}
Strichartz-type estimates (see Klainerman and Machedon
[
13
]
and Hidano and Wang
[
10
]
).

This paper is organized as follows: in Section 2, We collect some useful estimates and inequalities; in Section 3, we construct the higher order approximate
Q_{\mathcal{P}}
solution of the renormalized equation; in Section 4, we decompose the solution and show the estimate the modulation parameters; energy/virial type estimate and bootstrap argument that will be needed in the construction of the ground state mass blowup solutions; in Section 5, we prove the Theorem
1.1
; and the finally section is Appendix.

Preliminaries

In this section, for half-wave equation

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u-Du=G(u),~~u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),~~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.1)

By Duhamel formula,

\displaystyle u(t)=U(t)u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}U(t-s)G(u(s))ds,

Lemma 2.1
.

(Strichartz estimates)
Let

,
\sigma=1-\frac{1}{q}
. Then we have the following inequality

\|U(t)f\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\sigma}}.

In particular,

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|G(u)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},~~s\in[0,1)

and

\|u\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|U(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}+\|G(u)\|_{L^{1}\dot{H}^{\sigma}}.

The following result (generalized Leibniz rule) is proved in
[
9
]
(also see
[
12
,
16
]
) for Riesz and
Bessel potentials of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Fractional Leibniz rule) Suppose that
1<p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
,
\alpha\geq 0
,
\beta\geq 0
and
\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{1}{q_{i}}=\frac{1}{p}
with

,

,

. Then

\|D^{s}(fg)\|_{p}\lesssim\|D^{s+\alpha}f\|_{p_{1}}\|D^{-\alpha}g\|_{q_{1}}+\|D^{-\beta}f\|_{p_{2}}\|D^{s+\beta}g\|_{q_{2}},

where

D^{s}=(\mu^{2}-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}},~~\mu\geq 0.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Fractional Chain rule
[
8
]
) Let

or

,

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},~~s\in(0,\min\{p,N/2\}),

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|u\|_{H^{s}},~~s\in(0,p).

Approximate Blowup Profile

In this section, we aim to construct the approximate blowup profile.
We start with a general observation: If
u=u(t,x)
solves (
1.1
), then we define the function
v=v(s,y)
by setting

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}v\left(s,\frac{x-\alpha(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)e^{i\gamma(t)},\ \ \frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}.

It is easy to check that
v=v(s,y)
with
y=\frac{x-\alpha}{\lambda}
satisfies

i\partial_{s}v-Dv-v+|v|v=i\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda v+i\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\lambda}\cdot\nabla v+\tilde{\gamma_{s}}v,

where we set
\tilde{\gamma_{s}}=\gamma_{s}-1
. Here the operators
D
and
\nabla
are understood as
D=D_{y}
and
\nabla=\nabla_{y}
, respectively. Following the slow modulated ansatz strategy developed in
[
18
,
14
]
(also see
[
15
,
6
,
7
,
17
]
), we freeze the modulation

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}=a,\ \ \frac{\alpha_{s}}{\lambda}=b.

(3.1)

And we look for an approximate solution of the form

\displaystyle v(s,y)=Q_{\mathcal{P}(s)}(y),\ \mathcal{P}(s)=(a(s),b(s)),

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle Q_{\mathcal{P}}(y)=Q(y)+\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}a^{k}R_{k,0}(y)\right)+\left(\sum_{k+\ell\geq 1,\ell\neq 0}a^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{2}b^{\ell}_{j}R_{k,\ell,j}(y)\right),

where
\mathcal{P}=(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.
And the terms
R_{k,0}(y)
,
R_{k,\ell,j}(y)
are decomposed in real and imaginary parts as follows

\displaystyle R_{k,0}(y)=T_{k,0}(y)+iS_{k,0}(y),\,R_{k,\ell,j}(y)=T_{k,\ell,j}(y)+iS_{k,\ell,j}(y).

We also use the notation

\displaystyle T_{k,\ell}=(T_{k,\ell,1},T_{k,\ell,2})~\,\text{and}~\,S_{k,\ell}=(S_{k,\ell,1},S_{k,\ell,2}),~~\text{where}~~\ell\neq 0.

We shall define ODE for

a(s),b(s)
of type

a_{s}=P_{1}(a,b),~~b_{s}=P_{2}(a,b),

where
P_{1},P_{2}
are appropriate polynomials in
a,b
.

Using the heuristic asymptotic expansions

\lambda(t)\sim t^{2},~~|\alpha(t)|\sim t^{3},

from
\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}
we see that
s=s_{0}-1/t
goes to
\infty

t\nearrow 0
and
t=1/(s_{0}-s)\sim-1/s
as
s\to+\infty.
Moreover, the modulation relations (
3.1
) show that

a(s)=-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\sim\frac{1}{s},\ |b(s)|=\frac{|\alpha_{s}|}{\lambda}\sim\frac{1}{s^{2}}.

These asymptotic expansions suggests to define
a(s),b(s)
so that

a_{s}=-\frac{a^{2}}{2},\ b_{s}=-ab.

Moreover the asymptotic expansions for
a(s),b(s)
show that we can consider
\mathcal{P}=(a,b)
close to the origin with norm

\|\mathcal{P}\|^{2}\sim a^{2}+|b|.

We adjust the modulation equation for
(a(s),b(s))
to ensure the solvability of the obtained system, and a specific algebra leads to the laws to leading order:

a_{s}=-\frac{a^{2}}{2},\ b_{s}=-ab.

From (
3.2
), we have

\partial_{s}v=-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\partial_{a}Q_{\mathcal{P}}-a\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\partial_{b_{j}}Q_{\mathcal{P}}.

Therefore, our aim is to construct a higher order approximate profile
Q(y,a,b)=Q_{\mathcal{P}}(y)
that is the approximate solution to

\displaystyle-i\frac{a^{2}}{2}\partial_{a}Q_{\mathcal{P}}-ia\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\partial_{b_{j}}Q_{\mathcal{P}}-DQ_{\mathcal{P}}-Q_{\mathcal{P}}+ia\Lambda Q_{\mathcal{P}}-i\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\partial_{j}Q_{\mathcal{P}}+|Q_{\mathcal{P}}|Q_{\mathcal{P}}=-\Phi_{\mathcal{P}},

where
\mathcal{P}=(a,b)
is close to
0
and
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
is some small terms of order
\mathcal{O}(\|P\|^{5})=\mathcal{O}(a^{5}+|b|^{5/2})
.

We have the following result about an approximate blowup profile
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}
, parameterized by
\mathcal{P}=(a,b)
, around the ground state
\mathbf{Q}=[Q,0]^{\top}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(Approximate Blowup Profile)
Let
\mathcal{P}=(a,b)
. There exists a smooth function
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}(x)
of the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}+a\mathbf{R}_{1,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\mathbf{R}_{0,1,j}+a\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\mathbf{R}_{1,1,j}+a^{2}\mathbf{R}_{2,0}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}^{2}\mathbf{R}_{0,2,j}+a^{3}\mathbf{R}_{3,0}+a^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\mathbf{R}_{2,1,j}+a^{4}\mathbf{R}_{4,0}

-J\frac{1}{2}a^{2}\partial_{a}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}-Ja\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\partial_{b_{j}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}-D\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}-\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}+Ja\Lambda\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}-J\sum_{j=1}^{2}b_{j}\partial_{b_{j}}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}+|\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}|\mathbf{Q}_{\mathcal{P}}=-\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}.

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{R}_{k,l}\|_{H^{m}}+\|\Lambda\mathbf{R}_{k,l}\|_{H^{m}}+\|\Lambda^{2}\mathbf{R}_{k,l}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim 1,\

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(a^{5}+|b|^{2}|\mathcal{P}|),\ |\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}_{\mathcal{P}}|\lesssim\mathcal{O}(a^{5}+|b|^{2}|\mathcal{P}|)\langle x\rangle^{-3},

is an approximate solution to (
1.1
) such that

i\tilde{Q}_{t}-D\tilde{Q}+|\tilde{Q}|\tilde{Q}=\psi,

with the priori bounds

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}\|_{2}\lesssim 1,\ \|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{Q}\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},\ \|\nabla\tilde{Q}\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}.

We decompose
u=\tilde{Q}+\tilde{\epsilon}
, and hence
\tilde{\epsilon}
satisfies

i\tilde{\epsilon}_{t}-D\tilde{\epsilon}+(|u|u-|\tilde{Q}|\tilde{Q})=-\psi,

where we assume the priori estimate

\displaystyle\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda,

as well as

\displaystyle|\lambda_{t}+a|\lesssim\lambda^{2},\ a\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}},\ |a_{t}|\lesssim 1,\ |\alpha_{t}|\lesssim\lambda.

Next, Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth and radial function with the following properties

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(x)=\begin{cases}x\ \ &\text{for}\ \ 0\leq x\leq 1,\\
3-e^{-|x|}\ &\text{for}\ x\geq 2,\end{cases}

and the convexity condition

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0\ \text{for}\ x\geq 0.

Furthermore, we denote

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{1}{3}|u|^{3},\ f(u)=|u|u,\ F^{\prime}(u)\cdot h=\Re(f(u)\bar{h}).

Let
A>0
be a large constant and define the quantity

\displaystyle J_{A}(u):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\epsilon}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{|\tilde{\epsilon}|^{2}}{\lambda}-\int[F(u)-F(\tilde{Q})-F^{\prime}(\tilde{Q})\cdot\tilde{\epsilon}]

\displaystyle+\frac{a}{2}\Im\left(\int A\nabla\phi\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{A\lambda}\right)\cdot(\nabla\tilde{\epsilon})\bar{\tilde{\epsilon}}\right).

Our strategy will be to use the preceding functional to bootstrap control over
\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{H^{s}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

(Localized energy estimate) Let
J_{A}
be as above. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{dJ_{A}}{dt}=

\displaystyle\Im\left(\psi,D\tilde{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\tilde{\epsilon}-f^{\prime}(\tilde{Q})\tilde{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{1}{\lambda}(\tilde{\epsilon},f^{\prime}(\tilde{Q})\tilde{\epsilon})-\Re\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{Q},{(f(u)-f(\tilde{Q})-f^{\prime}(\tilde{Q})\cdot\tilde{\epsilon})}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{a}{2\lambda}\int\frac{|\tilde{\epsilon}|^{2}}{\lambda}-\frac{2a}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sqrt{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta\phi\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{A\lambda}\right)|\nabla\tilde{\epsilon}_{s}|^{2}dxds

\displaystyle+\frac{a}{2A^{2}\lambda^{3}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sqrt{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta^{2}\phi\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{A\lambda}\right)|\tilde{\epsilon}_{s}|^{2}dxds

\displaystyle+\Im\left(\int\left[iaA\nabla\phi\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{A\lambda}\right)\cdot\nabla\psi+i\frac{a}{2\lambda}\Delta\psi\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{A\lambda}\right)\psi\right]\bar{\tilde{\epsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle+a\Re\left(\int A\nabla\phi\left(\frac{x-\alpha}{A\lambda}\right)\left(\frac{3}{4}|\tilde{Q}|^{-1}|\tilde{\epsilon}|^{2}w\tilde{Q}+\frac{1}{4}|\tilde{Q}|^{-1}\tilde{\epsilon}^{2}\bar{\tilde{Q}}\right)\cdot\overline{\nabla\tilde{Q}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}\|\psi\|_{2}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2}),

where
\tilde{\epsilon}_{s}:=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{-\Delta+s}\tilde{\epsilon}
with
s>0
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Lemma 5.1 in
[
7
]
. A small modification due to the presence of the translation
\alpha
is needed. For this we omit the details of the proof.
∎

4.4
Backwards Propagation of Smallness

We now apply the energy estimate of the previous subsection in order to establish a bootstrap argument that will be needed in the construction of the ground state mass blowup solution.

Let
u=u(t,x)
be a solution to (
1.1
) defined in
[t_{0},0)
. Assume that
t_{0}<t_{1}<0
and suppose that
u
admits on
[t_{0},t_{1}]
a geometrical decomposition of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}[Q_{\mathcal{P}(t)}+\epsilon]\left(s,\frac{x-\alpha(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)e^{i\gamma(t)},

where
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}+i\epsilon_{2}
satisfies the orthogonality condition (
4.2
) and
a^{2}+|b|+\|\epsilon\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2}\ll 1
holds. We set

\displaystyle\tilde{\epsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}\epsilon\left(s,\frac{x-\alpha(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)e^{i\gamma(t)}.

Suppose that the energy satisfies
E_{0}=E(u_{0})>0
and define the constant

\displaystyle A_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{e_{1}}{E_{0}}},

(4.10)

with the constant
e_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{-}S_{1,0},S_{1,0})>0
. Moreover, Let
P_{0}=P(u_{0})
be the linear momentum and define the vector

\displaystyle B_{0}=\frac{P_{0}}{p_{1}},

(4.11)

where
p_{1}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}L_{-}S_{0,1}\cdot S_{0,1}>0
is a constant.

Now we claim that the following backwards propagation estimate holds.

Lemma 4.4
.

(Backwards propagation of smallness)
Assume that, for some
t_{1}<0
sufficiently close to
0
, we have the bounds

\displaystyle\left|\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\|Q\|_{2}^{2}\right|\lesssim\lambda^{2}(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\epsilon}(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda(t_{1})}\lesssim\lambda^{2}(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\left|\lambda(t_{1})-\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{4A_{0}^{2}}\right|\lesssim\lambda^{3}(t_{1}),\ \left|\frac{a(t_{1})}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(t_{1})}\right|\lesssim\lambda(t_{1}),\ \left|\frac{b(t_{1})}{\lambda(t_{1})}-B_{0}\right|\lesssim\lambda(t_{1}),

where
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
are defined in (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), respectively. Then there exists a time
t_{0}<t_{1}
depending on
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
such that
\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\epsilon}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda(t)}\lesssim\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\epsilon}(t_{1})\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda(t_{1})}\lesssim\lambda^{2}(t),

\displaystyle\left|\lambda(t)-\frac{t^{2}}{4A_{0}^{2}}\right|\lesssim\lambda^{3}(t),\ \left|\frac{a(t)}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)}-\frac{1}{A_{0}}\right|\lesssim\lambda(t),\ \left|\frac{b(t)}{\lambda(t)}-B_{0}\right|\lesssim\lambda(t).

Proof.

By using the Lemma
4.2
and
4.3
and the similar argument as
[
7
]
, we can obtain the estimates about
\tilde{\epsilon}
in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
\lambda(t)
and
a(t)
. Here we only give the estimate for
b
. In fact, by following the calculations in the proof of Lemma
4.1
for the linear momentum
P(u_{0})
and recalling that
a^{2}+|b|\sim\lambda
, we deduce that

\displaystyle bp_{1}=\lambda P_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),

where
p_{1}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}L_{-}S_{0,1}\cdot S_{0,1}
is a positive constant. Here we also used the fact that
\|\epsilon\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{2}
. Recalling the definition of
B_{0}=\frac{P_{0}}{p_{1}}
see (
4.11
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{b(t)}{\lambda(t)}-B_{0}\right|\lesssim\lambda(t).

This complete the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

5
Existence of ground state mass blowup solutions

In this section, we prove the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\gamma_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
P_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
E_{0}>0
be given. Then there exist a time
t_{0}<0
and a solution
u\in C([t_{0},0);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
,
\delta\in\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right)
of (
1.1
) such that
u
blowup at time
T=0
with

\displaystyle E(u)=E_{0},\ P(u)=P_{0},\ \text{and}\ \|u\|_{2}^{2}=\|Q\|_{2}^{2}.

Furthermore, we have
\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{2}\sim t^{-1}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{-}
, and
u
is of the form

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda(t)}[Q_{\mathcal{P}(t)}+\epsilon]\left(t,\frac{x-\alpha}{\lambda}\right)e^{i\gamma(t)}=\tilde{Q}+\tilde{\epsilon},

where
\mathcal{P}(t)=(a(t),b(t))
, and
\epsilon
satisfies the orthogonality condition (
4.2
). Finally, the following estimate hold:

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda,\ \|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{H^{1/2}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda(t)-\frac{t^{2}}{4A_{0}^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),\ \frac{a}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}(t)-\frac{1}{A_{0}}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda),\ \frac{b}{\lambda}(t)-B_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\lambda),

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=-\frac{4A_{0}^{2}}{t}+\gamma_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}),\ \alpha(t)=x_{0}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}),

for
t\in[t_{0},0)
and
t
sufficiently close to
0
.
Here
A_{0}>0
and
B_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a constant and the vector defined in (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), respectively.

Proof.

Step 1.
Backwards uniform bounds.

Let
t_{n}\rightarrow 0^{-}
be a sequence of negative times and let
u_{n}
be the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data at
t=t_{n}
given by

\displaystyle u_{n}(t_{n},x)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(t_{n})}Q_{\mathcal{P}_{n}(t_{n})}\left(\frac{x-\alpha_{n}(t_{n})}{\lambda_{n}(t_{n})}\right)e^{i\gamma_{n}(t_{n})},

where the sequence
\mathcal{P}_{n}(t_{n})=(a_{n}(t_{n}),b_{n}(t_{n}))
and
\{\lambda_{n}(t_{n}),\alpha_{n}(t_{n})\}
are given by

\displaystyle a_{n}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=-\frac{t_{n}}{2A_{0}},\ \lambda_{n}(t_{n})=\frac{t_{n}^{2}}{4A_{0}^{2}},\ \gamma_{n}(t_{n})=\gamma_{0}-\frac{4A_{0}^{2}}{t_{n}},

\displaystyle b_{n}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{0}t_{n}^{2}}{2A_{0}},\ \alpha_{n}(t_{n})=x_{0}.

(5.1)

By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\int|u_{n}(t_{n})|^{2}=\int|Q|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(t_{n}^{4}),

and
\tilde{\epsilon}(t_{n})=0
by construction. Thus
u_{n}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.4
. Hence we can find a backwards time
t_{0}
independent of
n
such that for al
t\in[t_{0},t_{n})
we have the geometric decomposition

\displaystyle u_{n}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}(t)}Q_{\mathcal{P}_{n}(t)}\left(\frac{x-\alpha_{n}(t)}{\lambda_{n}(t)}\right)+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(t,x),

with the uniform bounds given by

\displaystyle\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(t)\|_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{n}(t)}\lesssim\lambda_{n}(t),

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{n}(t)-\frac{t^{2}}{4A_{0}^{2}}\right|\lesssim K\lambda_{n}^{2}(t),\ \left|\frac{a_{n}(t)}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)}-\frac{1}{A_{0}}\right|\lesssim K\lambda_{n}(t),\ \left|\frac{b_{n}(t)}{\lambda_{n}(t)}-B_{0}\right|\lesssim K\lambda_{n}(t),

and

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta},~~\text{where}~~~\theta\in\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}\right),

(5.2)

which we prove in step 2.

The proof of similar to the proof in
[
7
, Theorem 7.1]
. A small modification due to the presence of the translation parameter
\alpha
is needed.
For this, we notice that

P(u(t))=\frac{b}{\lambda}p_{1}+o(1)\to P_{0}~~\text{as}~~t\to 0^{-},

by the choice of
B_{0}
and
\lambda_{n}(t_{n})
. By linear momentum conservation, this shows that

P(u(t))=P_{0}.

Finally, we recall the rough bound
\left|\frac{(\alpha_{n})_{s}}{\lambda_{n}}+b_{n}\right|\lesssim\lambda_{n}
. Integrating this and using the bounds for
b_{n}
and
\lambda_{n}
, we deduce that

\left|\frac{d}{dt}(\alpha_{n}-x_{0})\right|=\left|\frac{(\alpha_{n})_{s}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|\lesssim\lambda_{n}+|b_{n}|\lesssim\lambda_{n}.

Integrating this bound and using (
5.1
), we find that

\alpha_{n}(t)=x_{0}+\mathcal{O}\left(\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\right),

which shows that the claim for
\alpha(t)
holds.

Step 2.

H^{\frac{1}{2}+\theta}
bound.

It remains to prove the
H^{1/2+\theta}
bound (
5.2
). Our point of departure is again the identity

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}=D\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}-\psi_{n}-F_{n},

where

\displaystyle F_{n}=|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|(\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n})-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|\tilde{Q}_{n}

We plan to obtain a
H^{1/2+\theta}
-bound on
\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}
, taking advantage of the a priori bounds at time
t_{n}\sim\lambda_{n}^{1/2}
and those assumed for
t\in[t_{0},t_{n}]
.
We make partition of the interval
[t_{0},t_{n}]
into

t_{0}=s_{0}<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{N}=t_{n},\ s_{j}-s_{j-1}=h,\ j=1,\cdots,N,

where

h\sim\lambda_{n}^{2},~N\sim(t_{n}-t_{0})/h\sim(\lambda_{n})^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

We can obtain estimates in each interval
\Delta_{j}=[s_{j-1},s_{j}]
. To obtain the desired result, for any time interval
I
we use the space
X_{I}
with norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{I}}=\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{q}_{I}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
\frac{1}{q}\geq 1-s
,
q>4
and
s<1
.

Now we estimate the nonlinear term

\displaystyle F_{n}=

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|(\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n})-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|\tilde{Q}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}+(|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|)\tilde{Q}_{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\tilde{Q}_{n}|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}+|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}+(|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|)\tilde{Q}_{n}.

To this end we can use the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists
\delta>0
so that for any
s\in[0,1]
there exists a constant
C=C(\delta,s)>0
so that for any
u\in H^{s}\cap L^{\infty}
such that

\left\|\frac{u}{Q_{n}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\delta,

we have

\||Q_{n}+u|-|Q_{n}|\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

It is sufficient to make the series expansion

|Q_{n}+u|-|Q_{n}|=|Q_{n}|\left(\sum_{k+\ell\geq 1}c_{k,\ell}\left(\frac{u}{Q_{n}}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{\overline{u}}{\overline{Q_{n}}}\right)^{\ell}\right)

combined with the fractional Leibniz rule of Lemma
2.2
and

\left\||Q_{n}|\frac{u}{Q_{n}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

(5.3)

The last estimate can be verified for
s=1
and then by complex interpolation we have (
5.3
).
∎

Hence, by Lemma
2.2
and
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\|F_{n}\|_{H^{s}}=

\displaystyle\||\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}+(|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|)\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|(|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|)\|_{H^{s}}\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\|(|\tilde{Q}_{n}+\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}|-|\tilde{Q}_{n}|)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}},

where in the last step we used the Lemma
5.2
.

Now defining the nonlinear mapping

\displaystyle\Psi(u)(t):=U(t_{0})u(t_{0},x)-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}U(t-s)[F_{n}(s)+\psi_{n}(s)]ds.

Then

\displaystyle\|\Psi(u)(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{H^{s}}+\int_{I}\left(\|F_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{H^{s}}+\int_{I}[\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}]dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{H^{s}}+{|I|}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}\Big)+\int_{I}\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{H^{s}}+{|I|}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\Big(|I|^{\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}+\lambda^{-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}\Big)+\int_{I}\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}dt,

where
I=[t_{0},t]
is the time interval and the
L^{q}_{I}L^{\infty}
-norm

\displaystyle\|\Psi(u)\|_{L^{q}_{I}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|U(t)u(t_{0})\|_{L^{q}_{I}L^{\infty}}+\|F_{n}+\psi_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{I}\dot{H}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}}+\int_{I}\|F_{n}+\psi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\int_{I}[\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{s}}]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}\int_{I}\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}\int_{I}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}\int_{I}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{1}\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+|I|\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}+|I|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{n}^{-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+|I|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{s}}+|I|\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}\dot{H}^{s}}.

Here
s\geq 1-\frac{1}{q}
,
H^{s}\subset\dot{H}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}
and we used Strichartz estimates (see Lemma
2.1
) and the estimates
\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-1}
and
\|\tilde{Q}_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{-s}
again.

Now we consider the interval
\Delta_{N}=[s_{N-1},s_{N}]
,

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}=

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|h|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\Big(|h|^{\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+\lambda^{-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle+|h|\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+|h|\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+|h|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{n}^{-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+|h|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+|h|\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{(1-\frac{1}{q})}\Big(\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+\lambda^{-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{n}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+(\lambda_{n}^{2})^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\lambda_{n}^{-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{n}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{N}}L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}H^{s}}+\lambda_{n}^{2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}+\lambda_{n}^{2-\frac{2}{q}-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}+\lambda_{n}^{2-\frac{2}{q}}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}.

Here we used
\tilde{\epsilon}(t_{n})=0
and
h\sim\lambda_{n}^{2}
. Since

,
1>s\geq 1-\frac{1}{q}
and
\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}
is small, the above estimate implies

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}{H}^{s}}.

The term
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{N}}{H}^{s}}.
can be estimated by the following estimates

\|\nabla^{k}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{1-k},~~k=0,1.

This estimates can be obtained by
[
7
, Lemma 6.1. Step 4]
and a small modification due to the presence of the translation parameter
\alpha
.
So that

\displaystyle\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{I}H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s}\|\psi_{n}\|_{H^{1}}^{s}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{1-s}.

Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{3-s}.

For the other intervals
\Delta_{j}
,

, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(s_{j})\|_{H^{s}}+\lambda_{n}^{2}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j}}}+\lambda_{n}^{2(1-\frac{1}{q})-s}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j}}}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{n}^{2(1-\frac{1}{q})}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j}}}^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\Delta_{j}}H^{s}}.

By the similar argument as before, we deduce

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}(s_{j})\|_{H^{s}}+\lambda_{n}^{3-s}\lesssim\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j+1}}}+\lambda_{n}^{3-s}.

and inductively we find

\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{j}}}\lesssim(N-j)\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{\Delta_{N}}}+\lambda_{n}^{3-s}\lesssim(N-j+1)\lambda_{n}^{3-s}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{X_{[t_{0},t_{n}]}}=

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L_{t_{0},t_{n}]}^{\infty}H^{s}}+\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{[t_{0},t_{n}]}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{1\leq j\leq N}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L_{\Delta_{j}}^{\infty}H^{s}}+|N|^{\frac{1}{q}}\sup_{1\leq j\leq N}\|\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{q}_{\Delta_{j}}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N\lambda_{n}^{3-s}+N^{\frac{1}{q}}N\lambda_{n}^{3-s}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{\frac{3}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)-s}.

Since
q>4
and
1-\frac{1}{q}\leq s<1
, then

\frac{3}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)-s>\frac{3}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\right)-s=\frac{9}{8}-s>0.

This means

\displaystyle\|D^{s}\tilde{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{9/8-s}\lesssim\lambda_{n}^{1-s},~~\text{for}~~s\in\left(\frac{3}{4},1\right).

With
s=1/2+\theta,
then we get (
5.2
).

Now the proof of this Theorem is complete.
∎

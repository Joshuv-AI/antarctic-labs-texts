General Mneimneh-type Binomial Sum involving Harmonic Numbers
Ende Pan and Ce Xu[1mm] a. College of Teacher Education, Quzhou University,Quzhou 324022, P.R. Chinab. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University,Wuhu 241002, P.R. China

Abstract




Abstract.
Recently, Mneimneh proved the remarkable identity

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-p)^{i}}{i}\quad(p\in[0,1])

as the main result of a 2023
Discrete Mathematics
paper, where
H_{k}:=\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{k}1/i
is the classical
k
-th harmonic number. Thereafter, Campbell provided several other proofs of Mneimneh’s formula as above in a note published in
Discrete Mathematics
in 2023. Moreover, Campbell also considered how Mneimneh’s identity may be proved and generalized using the
Mathematica package Sigma
. In particular, he found the generalized Mneimneh’s identity

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}=(x+y)^{n}\left(H_{n}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{y^{i}(x+y)^{-i}}{i}\right).

In this paper, we will prove a more generalization of Mneimneh’s identity involving Bell numbers and some Mneimneh-type identities involving (alternating) harmonic numbers by using a few results of our previous papers.

Keywords
: Mneimneh’s identity; harmonic numbers; Binomial coefficients and sums; (unsigned) Stirling numbers; Bell polynomials; Bell numbers.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
be the set of natural numbers, and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. In his recent paper
[
7

, Mneimneh used the method of probabilistic analysis to establish the following binomial sum identity involving harmonic numbers

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}H_{k}\binom{n}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-p)^{i}}{i}\quad(p\in[0,1]),

(1.1)

where
H_{k}
is the
classical
k
-th harmonic number
defined by

H_{k}:=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\quad\text{and}\quad H_{0}:=0.

Quite recently, Campbell
[
1
]
gave two new proofs of (
1.1
) by using Zeilberger’s algorithm and beta-type integral formula. Further, using the Sigma package for the Mathematica Computer Algebra System, Campbell gave the more general Mneimneh’s identity

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}=(x+y)^{n}\left(H_{n}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{y^{i}(x+y)^{-i}}{i}\right).

(1.2)

Clearly, setting
(x,y)=(p,1-p)
in (
1.2
) gives (
1.1
). Moreover,
Campbell also emphasized that this approach may also be used to derive identities for expressions such as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}^{2}.

In a 2024
Discrete Mathematics
article
[
6
]
, Komatsu and Wang extended Mneimneh’s formula to the generalized hyperharmonic numbers. Gen
\check{\rm c}
ev
[
4
]
studied of the binomial sum

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\displaystyle\frac{z^{j}}{j}\right)p^{k}(1-p)^{k}\quad(r\in\mathbb{N},\ p,z\in\mathbb{R},\ p\neq 1)

and established explicit formula, see
[

.

In this paper, using the method of integrals of natural logarithms, we will establish the explicit formulas of the following general Mneimneh-type binomial sums involving Bell numbers and some Mneimneh-type binomial sums involving (alternating) harmonic numbers.

2.1
(unsigned) Stirling number of the first kind

We recall the definition of
(unsigned) Stirling number of the first kind
. Let
s(n,k)
denote the (unsigned) Stirling number of the first kind, which is defined by
[
3
,
2
]

\displaystyle n!x\left({1+x}\right)\left({1+\displaystyle\frac{x}{2}}\right)\cdots\left({1+\displaystyle\frac{x}{n}}\right)=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}s(n+1,k+1)x^{k+1},

(2.11)

with
s(n,k):=0
if
n<k
, and
s(n,0)=s(0,k):=0,\ s(0,0):=1
, or equivalently, by the generating function:

\displaystyle{\log^{k}}({1-x})={\left({-1}\right)^{k}}k!\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{s(n,k)\displaystyle\frac{{{x^{n}}}}{{n!}}}\quad(x\in[-1,1)).

The Stirling numbers
{s\left({n,k}\right)}
of the first kind satisfy a recurrence relation in the form

s({n,k})=s({n-1,k-1})+\left({n-1}\right)s({n-1,k})\quad(n,k\in\mathbb{N}).

Obviously,
{s\left({n,k}\right)}
can be expressed in terms of a linear combinations of products of harmonic numbers (see (
2.15
)). In particular,

\displaystyle s({n,1})=\left({n-1}\right)!,

\displaystyle s\left({n,2}\right)=\left({n-1}\right)!{H_{n-1}},

\displaystyle s({n,3})=\displaystyle\frac{{\left({n-1}\right)!}}{2}\left[{H_{n-1}^{2}-{H^{(2)}_{n-1}}}\right],

\displaystyle s({n,4})=\displaystyle\frac{{\left({n-1}\right)!}}{6}\left[{H_{n-1}^{3}-3{H_{n-1}}{H^{(2)}_{n-1}}+2{H^{(3)}_{n-1}}}\right],

\displaystyle s({n,5})=\displaystyle\frac{{\left({n-1}\right)!}}{{24}}\left[{H_{n-1}^{4}-6{H^{(4)}_{n-1}}-6H_{n-1}^{2}{H^{(2)}_{n-1}}+3(H^{(2)}_{n-1})^{2}+8H_{n-1}{H^{(3)}_{n-1}}}\right].

In
[
9
, Thm 2.5]
, we proved

\displaystyle s\left({n,k}\right)=\left({n-1}\right)!{\zeta_{n-1}}({{{\left\{1\right\}}_{k-1}}})\quad(k,n\in\mathbb{N}),

(2.12)

where
\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{k})
is a special multiple harmonic sum defined by (

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(\{1\}_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}>n_{2}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{r}}.

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\equiv\zeta_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

2.2
Bell polynomials

Define the
exponential partial Bell polynomials

B_{n,k}
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}\displaystyle\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right)^{k}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\displaystyle\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,,\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots\,,

and the
exponential complete Bell polynomials

Y_{n}
by

Y_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

(see
[
2
, Section 3.3]
). According to
[
8
, Eq. (2.44)]
, the complete Bell polynomials
Y_{n}
satisfy the recurrence

Y_{0}=1\,,\quad Y_{n}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}x_{n-j}Y_{j}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{j})\,,\quad n\geq 1\,,

from which, the first few polynomials can be obtained immediately:

\displaystyle Y_{0}=1\,,\quad Y_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}\,,\quad Y_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x^{2}_{1}+x_{2}\,,\quad Y_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x^{3}_{1}+3x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}\,,

\displaystyle Y_{4}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=x^{4}_{1}+6x^{2}_{1}x_{2}+4x_{1}x_{3}+3x^{2}_{2}+x_{4}\,.

Define the
Bell number

{Y_{k}}(n)
by

\displaystyle{Y_{k}}(n):={Y_{k}}({{H_{n}},1!{H_{n}^{(2)}},2!{H_{n}^{(3)}},\cdots,({k-1})!{H_{n}^{(k)}}}).

(2.16)

Clearly, the Bell number
{Y_{k}}(n)
is a rational linear combination of products of harmonic numbers. We have

\displaystyle{Y_{1}}(n)={H_{n}},

\displaystyle{Y_{2}}(n)=H_{n}^{2}+{H^{(2)}_{n}},

\displaystyle{Y_{3}}(n)=H_{n}^{3}+3{H_{n}}{H^{(2)}_{n}}+2{H^{(3)}_{n}},

\displaystyle{Y_{4}}(n)=H_{n}^{4}+8{H_{n}}{H^{(3)}_{n}}+6H_{n}^{2}{H^{(2)}_{n}}+3(H^{(2)}_{n})^{2}+6{H^{(4)}_{n}},

\displaystyle{Y_{5}}(n)=H_{n}^{5}+10H_{n}^{3}{H^{(2)}_{n}}+20H_{n}^{2}{H^{(3)}_{n}}+15{H_{n}}({H^{(2)}_{n}})^{2}+30{H_{n}}{H^{(4)}_{n}}+20{H^{(2)}_{n}}{H^{(3)}_{n}}+24{H^{(5)}_{n}}.

In
[
9
, Eq. (2.9)]
, we showed

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}\left({{{\{1\}}_{m}}}\right)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{{m!}}{Y_{m}}\left(n\right)\quad(n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),

(2.17)

where
\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{r})
is a special multiple harmonic star sum defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}^{\star}(\{1\}_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{r}}.

For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and positive integer
n
, the generalized
multiple harmonic star sums
(MHSSs) are defined by

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}_{n}({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\equiv\zeta^{\star}_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}>0}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}.

(2.18)

Similarly, we set
{\zeta^{\star}_{n}}(\emptyset):=1
if
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=\emptyset

5
Conclusion

We presented the explicit formulas of the following Mneimneh-type binomial sum of (alternating) harmonic numbers

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}Y_{p}(k),\quad\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}{\bar{H}}_{k},\quad\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}^{(2)}\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}H^{2}_{k}.

It is possible that some of other Mneimneh-type binomial sums can be obtained using techniques of the present paper. For example, multiplying (
1.5
) by
\log^{p-1}(z)/z\ (p\in\mathbb{N})
and integrating over the interval (0,1), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}^{(p+1)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{(x+y)^{n-j}}{j}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\log^{p-1}(z)\displaystyle\frac{(xz+y)^{j}-y^{j}}{z}dz.

(5.32)

Hence, if the evaluation of the integral on the right hand of above can be established, we can obtain the explicit formula of Mneimneh-type binomial sums on the left hand of above. We leave the detail to the interested reader. It should be emphasized that Komatsu-Wang
[
6
, Eq. (4)]
gave an evaluation of (
5.32
) with
x=1-q
and
y=q
, where
q
is a real number. Gen
\check{\rm c}
ev
[
4
, Thm. 2.1]
established a generalization of Mneimneh summation formula

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\displaystyle\frac{z^{j}}{j^{r}}\right)p^{k}(1-p)^{k}=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq n_{1}\geq\cdots n_{r}\geq 1}\displaystyle\frac{(1-p)^{n_{1}}\left(\left(1+\displaystyle\frac{zp}{1-p}\right)^{n_{r}}-1\right)}{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}},

(5.33)

where
r\in\mathbb{N},p,z\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\neq 1
.

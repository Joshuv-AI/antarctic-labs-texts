Higher-order fractional equations and related time-changed pseudo-processes
Fabrizio Cinque, Enzo Orsingher Department of Statistical Sciences, Sapienza University of Rome, Italy

Abstract
We study Cauchy problems of fractional differential equations in both space and time variables by expressing the solution in terms of “stochastic composition” of the solutions to two simpler problems. These Cauchy sub-problems respectively concern the space and the time differential operator involved in the main equation. We provide some probabilistic and pseudo-probabilistic applications, where the solution can be interpreted as the pseudo-transition density of a time-changed pseudo-process. To extend our results to higher order time-fractional problems, we introduce stable pseudo-subordinators as well as their pseudo-inverses. Finally, we present our results in the case of more general differential operators and we interpret the results by means of a linear combination of pseudo-subordinators and their inverse processes.



Keywords:
Dzherbashyan-Caputo derivative, Pseudo-processes, Fourier transforms, Laplace transforms, Stable subordinator and inverse.

Primary 35R11; Secondary 60K99.

Introduction

In this paper we study a method to solve a class of differential problems involving fractional derivatives, in the sense of Dzherbasyhan-Caputo (and more general differential operators), by considering two sub-problems, one of which with solution being interpreted, under some further hypotheses, as a pseudo-density of the generalization of the inverse of a stable subordinator.

Fractional calculus is an emerging field in mathematics which in the last decades has been intensively studied with both theoretical and applied results. Nowadays, Dzherbashyan-Caputo fractional derivatives and the associated Cauchy problems have
been investigated by many authors and, after first relevant applications in physics and mechanics (see for instance
[
5
,
18
]
), they are deeply used in all related fields of science and engineering; we refer to
[
19
]
for a comprehensive collection of applications. We also refer to
[
2
,
7
,
20
]
(and references therein) for some further analytic results on fractional differential equations. The Dzherbashyan-Caputo fractional derivative is defined as, for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\alpha>-1
,

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{\alpha}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{\alpha}}f(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(m-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{m-\alpha-1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s^{m}}f(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,&\ \text{if}\ m-1<\alpha<m,\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{m}}f(t),&\ \text{if}\ \alpha=m,\end{array}\end{cases}

(1.1)

and its Laplace transform reads, with
\mu>0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{\alpha}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{\alpha}}f(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\mu^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}f(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\alpha\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\alpha-k-1}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{k}}f\Big|_{t=0}

(1.2)

where we assume that
\lim_{t\longrightarrow\infty}e^{-\mu t}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{k}}f(t)=0,\ k\geq 0
.

We analyze problems of the form, with
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N}>0
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}>0
,

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{i}}}{\partial t^{\nu_{i}}}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=f_{k}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ k=0,\dots,\max\{\left\lceil\nu_{i}\right\rceil\,:1\leq i\leq N\}-1.\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
O_{x}
being a general space differential operator which behaves “well” with respect to the Fourier transform, meaning that there exists a function
F
such that
\bigl(\mathcal{F}O_{x}u\bigr)(\gamma)=F(\gamma)\mathcal{F}u(\gamma)
(see hypothesis (
2.2
) for details).

In the last section of the paper, we also consider problems of the form (
1.3
) with more general time-operators, which behave “well” with respect to the Laplace transform (see hypothesis (
4.1
) for details).

Throughout the paper, we consider the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be measureable functions, we define
stochastic composition of
f
with
g
the function

f\diamond g\,(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(s,x)g(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

(1.4)

for
t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the integral converges.

We point out that if
f\in L^{\infty}\bigl([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)
and
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\cdot,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\in L^{\infty}\bigl([0,\infty)\bigr)
, then
f\diamond g\in L^{\infty}\bigl([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)
and this is the kind of functions we are mostly considering along the paper.

The name “stochastic composition” in Definition
1.1
justifies because if
f
and
g
are respectively the transition densities of two stochastic processes
\{F(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{G(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, then
f\diamond g
is the transition density of the process
F\circ G=\{F\bigl(G(t)\bigr)\}_{t\geq 0}
. Note that
F
can be a vector-valued process. Below, we often use this interpretation of the stochastic composition of functions, applying it also for pseudo-processes.

One of our main results is that the solution to problem (
1.3
) is the stochastic composition (defined below) of the solutions to two simpler problems, one concerning the space operator (see system (
2.4
)) and the other one concerning the time operator (see system (
2.5
) for the easiest scenario and (
4.4
) for the general framework).

Under some hypotheses concerning the initial condition of problem (
1.3
), we are able to interpret the solution to the time problem in terms of the pseudo-density of a pseudo-inverse of a pseudo-subordinator, which extends the idea of stable-subordinators to the field of pseudo-processes (we refer to
[
21
]
for the theory of stable processes and stochastic subordinators). In particular, the pseudo-subordinator has density solving the fractional heat-type equation (with space variable defined on the positive half-line only),

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}u(t,x),\ \ \ t,x\geq 0,\ \nu>0,

(1.5)

and its construction follows the well-known method based on the definition of the probabilistic Wiener measure and suitably adapted in the case of signed-measures (see references below).

Several authors tackled the study of processes governed by a real valued density integrating to one, meaning the so-called pseudo-probability densities: originally, their derivation and its corresponding (quasi) signed measure, was connected to higher order generalization of the heat equation, with the pioneering works of Krylov
[
8
]
, Dalestkii and Fomin
[
3
]
and then Hochberg
[
6
]
, who also introduced an Itò-type calculus for pseudo-processes for the even-order pseudo-process (that is the process with density satisfying the heat equation (
1.5
) with space derivative of order
2n,\ n\in\mathbb{N}
, instead of the second-order one). Then, the works of Orsingher
[
15
]
and Lachal
[
10
]
contributed to extend the theory to heat-type equation of all possible natural orders, meaning (
1.5
) with integer
\nu\geq 2
. Furthermore, in these papers (see also
[
14
]
), in view of the similarity and connections to the theory of stochastic processes, the researchers also studied some functionals of the pseudo-processes, like the first passage time and the sojourn time. We also refer to
[
1
,
4
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
for further results.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we study the simplest form of problem (
1.3
), that is with
N=1
and
\lambda_{1}=1
, i.e. considering only one fractional time derivative. This is useful to show the main results in an easier setting and introduce, in Section 3, the probabilistic and pseudo-probabilistic applications. Here, a relevant part concerns the construction of the stable pseudo-subordinator and the corresponding pseudo-inverse, also studying some of their properties in accordance to the theory of genuine probabilistic stable subordinators and their inverses. Finally, in Section 4 we state the main results in a general framework and show some further applications in the case of problems of the form (
1.3
), focusing on the probabilistic and pseudo-probabilistic interpretations, involving linear combination of pseudo-subordinators and the related pseudo-inverse.

2
Stochastic composition of solutions to fractional Cauchy problems

Let
d\in\mathbb{N},\nu>0
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}u(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\gamma\cdot x}u(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the Fourier transform (when the integral converges) of
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Throughout the whole paper we study differential problems under the following hypotheses. Let
O_{x}
be a space-differential operator and
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
a differentiable functions such that, for natural
k
depending on the order of the operator
O_{x}
,

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}u(t,x)=0,\ \ \ t\geq 0,

(2.1)

where the derivative of order
k
concerns all the needed combinations of the spacial components (according to
O_{x}
). Note that
u\in L^{\infty}
because of the continuity of
u
and (
2.1
), which holds at least for
k=0
. Then, we assume that there exists a function
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, such that

\Re\bigl(F(\gamma)\bigr)<0\ \text{ and }\ \bigl(\mathcal{F}O_{x}u\bigr)(\gamma)=F(\gamma)\mathcal{F}u(\gamma)\ \forall\ \gamma,

(2.2)

where the condition on the real part is useful for the convergence of some integrals below.

Now, in this section, we deal with the following Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial t^{\nu}}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=f_{k}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ k=0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1,\end{cases}

(2.3)

with
f_{k}
admitting Fourier transform
\forall\ k
, that we call the
space-time
problem.

We relate the solution of problem (
2.3
) to the solutions of two simpler sub-problems. In detail, we consider the following problems:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle u(0,x)=g(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(2.4)

with
g
admitting Fourier transform, that we call the
space
problem;

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial t^{\nu}}u(t,x)+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(t,x)=0,\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle u(t,0)=h(t),\ \ t\geq 0,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=a_{k}(x),\ \ x\geq 0,\ k=0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1,\end{cases}

(2.5)

with
h
admitting Laplace transform, that we call the
time
problem.

Firstly, we solve the Cauchy problems (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) and then we study their stochastic composition, say
u_{1},u_{2}
their corresponding solutions, we study the function

u_{1}\diamond u_{2}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{1}(s,x)u_{2}(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\ \ \ t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.6)

Secondly, we solve problem (
2.3
) and compare its solution with the function (
2.6
).

Solution to problem (
2.4
).

By using the
x
-Fourier transform, problem (
2.4
) turns into

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{F}u(t,\gamma)=F(\gamma)\mathcal{F}u(t,\gamma),\ \ t\geq 0,\ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}u(0,\gamma)=\mathcal{F}g(\gamma),\ \ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

(2.7)

Then the solution is

\mathcal{F}u(t,\gamma)=\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)e^{tF(\gamma)},\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.8)

Solution to problem (
2.5
).

By using the
t
-Laplace transform, problem (
2.5
) turns into (keep in mind formula (
1.2
))

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Bigl(\mu^{\nu}\mathcal{L}u(\mu,x)-\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}a_{k}(x)\Bigr)+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathcal{L}u(\mu,x)=0,\ \ \mu,x\geq 0,\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(\mu,0)=\mathcal{L}h(\mu),\ \ \mu\geq 0.\end{cases}

(2.9)

Therefore, the solution reads

\mathcal{L}u(\mu,x)=e^{-\mu^{\nu}x}\Bigl(\mathcal{L}h(\mu)+\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}\int_{0}^{x}e^{\mu^{\nu}y}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Bigr),\ \ \mu,x\geq 0.

(2.10)

Let us denote by
u_{1}
the solution to problem (
2.4
), whose
x
-Fourier transform is given in (
2.8
), and by
u_{2}
the solution to problem (
2.5
), whose
t
-Laplace transform is given in (
2.10
). The stochastic composition of the solutions
u_{1},u_{2}
(see formula (
2.6
)) can be expressed by means of its
t
-Laplace-
x
-Fourier transform, that is, for
\mu>0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(remember that
\Re\bigl(F(\gamma)\bigr)<0
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}\bigl(u_{1}\diamond u_{2}\bigr)(\mu,\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\gamma\cdot x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{1}(s,x)u_{2}(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)e^{sF(\gamma)}\,e^{-\mu^{\nu}s}\Bigl(\mathcal{L}h(\mu)+\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{\mu^{\nu}y}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{L}h(\mu)}{\mu^{\nu}-F(\gamma)}+\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mu^{\nu}y}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(z+y)(F(\gamma)-\mu^{\nu})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\biggr)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)}{\mu^{\nu}-F(\gamma)}\biggl(\mathcal{L}h(\mu)+\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{F(\gamma)y}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\biggr).

(2.11)

Solution to problem (
2.3
).

By means of the
x
-Fourier transform and then the
t
-Laplace transform, we obtain

\mu^{\nu}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u(\mu,\gamma)-\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma)=F(\gamma)\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u(\mu,\gamma)

(2.12)

and therefore the solution reads

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u(\mu,\gamma)=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1}\mu^{\nu-k-1}\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma)}{\displaystyle\mu^{\nu}-F(\gamma)},\ \ \ \mu\geq 0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.13)

By comparing formulas (
2.13
) and (
2.11
), it is clear that we can represent the solution to problem (
2.3
) as stochastic composition of the solutions of the Cauchy problems (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) with suitable initial conditions. In detail, it is easy to show that the following conditions are sufficient for such representation:

(
a
)

fix
j\in\{0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1\}
,
f_{j}\not=0
and
f_{k}=0,\ k\not=j
, then it is sufficient that
g=f_{j},a_{k}=0\ \forall\ k
and
h
such that
\mathcal{L}h(\mu)=\mu^{\nu-j-1}
,

(
b
)

for general
f_{k}\ \forall\ k
, it is sufficient that
h=0,\,g=\delta
(i.e.
\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)=1\ \forall\ \gamma)
and
a_{k}
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{yF(\gamma)}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma)\ \forall\ \gamma,k
,

where, as usual,
\delta
denotes the Dirac delta function centered in the origin.

Note that in point (
b
), the last condition can also be expressed as
\mathcal{L}a_{k}\bigl(-F(\gamma)\bigr)=\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma)
.

The above arguments prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

The solution to the differential Cauchy problem (
2.3
), under the hypotheses (
2.2
), can be expressed as the stochastic composition of the solution to (the space) problem (
2.4
) with the solution to (the time) problem (
2.5
), where these problems have initial conditions given in point (
b
) above.

Let us now consider the limit behavior for
\nu\,\downarrow\,0
of problem (
2.3
) and its solution. It is interesting to observe that under some hypothesis (required to exchange limit and integral), the limit of the solution to problem (
2.3
) is solution to the limit of such differential problem.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let us denote by
u_{\nu}
the solution to problem (
2.3
). Assume that there exists the pointwise limit
u_{0}=\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}u_{\nu}
and that
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u_{0}=\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u_{\nu}
. Then,
u_{0}
satisfies the problem, for
t\geq 0
,

\begin{cases}u(t,x)-f_{0}(x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[0.0pt]
\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\longrightarrow\infty}u(t,x)=0.\end{cases}

(2.14)

that is the ordinary Cauchy problem obtained as the limit of problem (
2.3
).

Clearly, a similar (but less interesting) result equivalently holds in the case of the limit
\nu\longrightarrow\nu_{0}>0
, see also Remark 3.2 of
[
2
]
(where the authors work after the application of the Fourier transform, which reduces the equation to an ordinary fractional differential equation in the variable
t
).

Proof.

Assume suitable
u
such that
\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\nu}u^{\prime}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}(t-s)^{-\nu}u^{\prime}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s
(for instance
u
such that
u^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}\bigl([0,\infty)\bigr)
), then

\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial t^{\nu}}u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}u(s,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=u(t,x)-u(0,x),\ \ \ t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.15)

where we used the definition (
1.1
) of fractional derivative.

Thus, by applying the limit (
2.15
), problem (
2.3
) coincides with (
2.14
), where in the latter we may omit the variable
t
because there are just
x
-differential operators.

Now, by keeping in mind the
t
-Laplace
x
-Fourier transform (
2.13
), we can write

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u_{0}(\mu,\gamma)=\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u_{\nu}(\mu,\gamma)=\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\mathcal{F}f_{0}(\gamma)}{1-F(\gamma)},\ \ \ \mu>0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

whose
t
-Laplace inverse reads

\mathcal{F}u_{0}(t,\gamma)=\frac{\mathcal{F}f_{0}(\gamma)}{1-F(\gamma)},\ \ \ t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.16)

that is constant in
t
. Finally, it is easy to see that (
2.16
) is the Fourier transform of the solution to the differential problem (
2.14
).
∎

3
Probabilistic and pseudo-probabilistic applications

In this section we mainly focus on the Cauchy problem (
2.3
) with null initial conditions except for the first one, that is

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial t^{\nu}}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
u(0,x)=\delta(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ k=1,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1.\end{cases}

(3.1)

In light of Theorem
2.1
we can interpret the solution to (
3.1
) in terms of subordination with the inverse of a stable subordinator if
\nu\in(0,1]
and its generalization otherwise. In fact, by keeping in mind point (
a
) before Theorem
2.1
, the
time
problem related to system (
3.1
) is given by

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial t^{\nu}}u(t,x)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(t,x),\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[5.0pt]
u(t,0)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\bigl(\mu^{\nu-1}\bigr)(t),\ \ t\geq,0\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=0,\ \ x\geq 0,\ k=0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1,\end{cases}

(3.2)

whose solution has
t
-Laplace transform equal to
\mathcal{L}u(\mu,x)=\mu^{\nu-1}e^{-\mu^{\nu}x}
, see (
2.10
).

In the case of
\nu\in(0,1]
the first boundary condition of (
3.2
) becomes
u(t,0)=t^{-\nu}/\Gamma(1-\nu)
since
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}t^{-\nu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t/\Gamma(1-\nu)=\mu^{\nu-1}
. Then, it is well-known (see
[
5
,
21
]
) that the solution of the
time
problem is the probability transition density of the inverse of a stable subordinator of order
\nu
,
L_{\nu}
, i.e. the function

l_{\nu}(t,x)=\frac{1}{t^{\nu}}W_{-\nu,1-\nu}\Bigl(-\frac{x}{t^{\nu}}\Bigr),\ \ \ t,x\geq 0,

(3.3)

where
W_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}/\bigl(k!\,\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)\bigr),
with
\alpha>-1,\beta,z\in\mathbb{C}
, is the Wright function.

Before generalizing the notion of inverse of stable subordinators in the case of a non-probabilistic “measure” and giving further results concerning problem (
3.1
), we give a probabilistic example of Proposition
2.1
. From the results above, we need to study the limit of the inverse of the stable subordinator for
\nu\,\downarrow\,0
. By considering the
t
-Laplace transform it is easy to show that
L_{\nu}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\sim Exp(1)
, so the limit is an exponential random variable not depending on time
t
. In fact, with
t,x\geq 0
,

\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}l_{\nu}(t,x)=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{t}\Bigl(\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}l_{\nu}(t,x)\Bigr)=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1}\Bigl(\lim_{\nu\,\downarrow\,0}\mu^{\nu-1}e^{-\mu^{\nu}x}\Bigr)=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{-1}\Bigl(\frac{e^{-x}}{\mu}\Bigr)=e^{-x}.

One can also prove this result by means of the explicit form of the transition probability density (
3.3
).

3.1
General theory of pseudo-processes

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and
u:[0,\infty)\times E\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that for all
t\geq 0
,
\int_{E}u(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1
and
\int_{E}|u(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=+\infty
. We now show how to define and construct a pseudo-process
X=\{X(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
taking values in
E
and having pseudo-density (or kernel or, for brevity, density)
u
. Our construction is inspired by previous remarkable papers, see
[
6
,
8
]
, where the authors applied a method similar to the Wiener’s procedure used for Brownian motion.

Let
\Omega=\{g:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow E\,:\,g\ \text{measurable}\}
called the
sample path space
. By means of
u
we construct a set function
Q
on the functional space
\Omega
.

Let us consider the cylinders in
\Omega
,
C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})=\{g\in\Omega\,:\,g(t_{i})\in I_{i},\ i=1,\dots n\}
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\underline{I}
is a collection of Borel sets of
E
,
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
(i.e.
I_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(E)\ \forall\ i
) and
\underline{t}
is a vector of time instants
0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}<\infty
. We define the function
Q
on a cylinder
C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})
as follows

Q\bigl(C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})\bigr)=\int_{I_{1}}\cdots\int_{I_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}u(t_{i}-t_{i-1},x_{i}-x_{i-1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{i},

(3.4)

where

From the hypotheses on

is a set function with infinite total variation and it is not a signed-measure, thus the classical measure theory does not apply (and we cannot use Kolmogorov extension theorem because its hypotheses are not satisfied). In order to build a feasible framework for the set function

we proceed as follows.

First, we extend the set function on the class of all the cylinders
\{C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,\underline{I}\in\mathcal{B}(E)^{n},\,\underline{t}\in[0,\infty)^{n}\ \text{s.t.}\ 0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}\}

\mathcal{A}=\alpha\Bigl(\big\{C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,\underline{I}\in\mathcal{B}(E)^{n},\,\underline{t}\in[0,\infty)^{n}\ \text{s.t.}\ 0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n},\,\big|Q\bigl(C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})\bigr)\big|<\infty\big\}\Bigr),

(3.5)

where

is the intersection of all the algebras containing the family

).

We call

the

(with respect to the Lebesgue measure) and the elements of

Q

Now, we consider the sequence of algebras

where, for

\mathcal{A}_{s}=\alpha\Bigl(\big\{C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N},\,\underline{I}\in\mathcal{B}(E)^{n},\,\underline{t}\in[0,s]^{n}\ \text{s.t.}\ 0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n},\,\big|Q\bigl(C_{n}(\underline{I},\underline{t})\bigr)\big|<\infty\big\}\Bigr)

(3.6)

which is an increasing collection of algebras such that

.

Eventually, the pseudo-process

with pseudo-density (or kernel)

and we write

.

For

, it is useful to clarify that the element

of the pseudo-process has pseudo-density

which leads to a pseudo-measure

defined on the algebra of the cylinders which keep into account only the time instant

\mathcal{C}_{t}=\alpha\Bigl(\big\{C(I,t)\,:\,I\in\mathcal{B}(E)\big\}\Bigr).

(3.7)

Note that

and therefore it follows that

coincides with

on the pseudo-measurable space

.

3.2
Stable pseudo-subordinators and pseudo-inverses

Let
\nu>1
. We consider the fractional Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=-\frac{\partial^{\nu}}{\partial x^{\nu}}u(t,x),\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[8.0pt]
u(0,x)=\delta(x),\ \ x\geq 0,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial x^{k}}\Big|_{x=0}=0,\ \ t\geq 0,\ k=0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1,\end{cases}

(3.8)

whose solution is denoted by
u_{\nu}
(belonging to

) and it has Laplace transform given by

\mathcal{L}u_{\nu}(t,\mu)=e^{-\mu^{\nu}t},\ \ \ t,\mu\geq 0.

(3.9)

With (
3.9
) at hand we readily have that
\int_{0}^{\infty}u_{\nu}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1\ \forall\ t
and that

is not a probability density since it takes real values. Indeed (remember
\nu>1
),

\int_{0}^{\infty}xu_{\nu}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\mathcal{L}u_{\nu}(t,\mu)\Big|_{\mu=0}=\nu t\mu^{\nu-1}e^{-\mu^{\nu}t}\Big|_{\mu=0}=0

meaning that
u_{\nu}
must be sign-varying.

We point out that if
\nu\in(0,1]
the solution to (
3.8
) is the probability law of a stable subordinator of order
\nu
; formula (
3.9
) is the moment generating function and we can also write
u_{\nu}(t,x)=\nu t\,l_{\nu}(x,t)/x
, with
l_{\nu}
density of the inverse, see (
3.3
).

By following the procedure presented in Section
3.1
, setting
E=[0,\infty)
, we construct the pseudo-process
S_{\nu}=\{S_{\nu}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with kernel
u_{\nu}
, i.e. such that
Q\{S_{\nu}(t_{1})\in I_{1},\dots,S_{\nu}(t_{n})\in I_{n}\}=Q\bigl(C(\underline{I},\underline{t})\bigr)
, suitably adapted from (
3.4
), for
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}\in\mathcal{B}\bigl([0,\infty)\bigr)
and
0\leq t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}<\infty
. We call
S_{\nu}

stable pseudo-subordinator of order
\nu>1
. It starts at
S_{\nu}(0)=0

Q
-a.e. because of the first initial condition of problem (
3.8
), and from the Laplace transform (
3.9
) (which, according to (
i
) of Definition
3.1
, is its moment generating function) we derive that it has independent and stationary increments since we can decompose
e^{-\mu^{\nu}t}=e^{-\mu^{\nu}(t-s)}e^{-\mu^{\nu}s}
.
The stable pseudo-subordinator assumes non-negative values
Q
-a.e. because of the definition of the pseudo-measure (
3.4
) and since

is defined only for

(we may extend it to

by setting

Hence,

is a non-negative and non-decreasing pseudo-process with moment generating function (

). These reasons justify the name “subordinator” in the definition.

Remark 3.1

(Properties of stable pseudo-subordinators)
.

As for Brownian motion and other pseudo-processes (see for instance
[
6
]
), thanks to formula (
3.9
), it is straightforward to obtain the following properties of stable pseudo-subordinators. For
\nu>0
(i.e. including also the probabilistic case) and
t\geq 0
,

(
i
)

the
t
-Laplace transform of
u_{\nu}
can be derived as follows, with

:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta t}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu x}u_{\nu}(t,x)

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta t}e^{-\mu^{\nu}t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{\mu^{\nu}+\delta}

\displaystyle\implies\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta t}u_{\nu}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=x^{\nu-1}E_{\nu,\nu}(-\delta x^{\nu}),\ \ x\geq 0,

where
E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}/\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)
, with
\alpha,\beta,z\in\mathbb{C}
and
\Re(\alpha)>0
, is the Mittag-Leffler function;

(
ii
)

S_{\nu}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}t^{2/\nu}S_{\nu}(1/t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}c^{1/\nu}S_{\nu}(t/c)
, with
c>0
;

(
iii
)

let

be independent pseudo-subordinators and
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a non-negative real sequence such that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{\nu}/n\longrightarrow a<\infty
, then

\frac{1}{n^{1/\nu}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}S_{\nu}^{(k)}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}a^{1/\nu}S_{\nu}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{\nu}(at).

(3.10)

In fact, thanks to the independence assumption and that from (

)

, with

, the

-Laplace transform of the pseudo-density of the first member of (

\prod_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu x}u_{\nu}\Bigl(\frac{a_{k}^{\nu}t}{n},x\Bigr)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\prod_{k=1}^{n}e^{-\mu^{\nu}a_{k}^{\nu}t/n}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{}e^{-\mu^{\nu}at}.

(
iv
)

with

,
S_{\nu_{1}}\bigl(S_{\nu_{2}}(t)\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}(t)
.

It is well-known that these properties hold in the case of

. The latter property can be linked to the idea of stable pseudo-random variables (and processes).
\diamond

In analogy to the derivation of probabilistic subordinators, we derive the representation of a pseudo-subordinator as limit of a compound Poisson with ingredients which include genuine random variables with power-law distribution and suitable sign-varying terms (which are the pseudo-subordinators themselves at time

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S_{\nu}^{(1)},S_{\nu}^{(2)},\dots
be independent stable pseudo-subordinators of order
\nu>1
and

be an independent homogeneous Poisson process of rate

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\sum_{k=1}^{N(t\,\delta^{-\beta})}X_{k}^{\beta,\delta}S_{\nu}^{(k)}(1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{\beta}\bigl(\lambda t\,\Gamma(1-\beta/\nu)\bigr),

(3.11)

where
0<\beta<\nu
and
X_{1}^{\beta,\delta},X_{2}^{\beta,\delta},\dots
are independent copies of the random variable
X^{\beta,\delta}
such that

P\{X^{\beta,\delta}>x\}=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}1,&x<\delta,\\
\Bigr(\frac{\delta}{x}\Bigr)^{\beta},&x\geq\delta.\end{array}\end{cases}

(3.12)

Furthermore, we can write

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\sum_{k=1}^{N(t\,\delta^{-\nu})}\delta S_{\nu}^{(k)}(1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}S_{\nu}(\lambda t).

(3.13)

The interested reader can find some further results of the type of those in Proposition
3.1
in the paper of Orsingher and Toaldo
[
16
]
(concerning other classes of pseudo-processes).

Proof.

We study the pseudo-random object

\sum_{k=1}^{N(t\,\delta^{-\alpha})}X_{k}^{\beta,\delta}S_{\nu}^{(k)}(1),\ \ \text{for }\ \alpha,\beta,\nu,\delta>0.

(3.14)

Let us denote by
f(x)=\beta\delta^{\beta}/x^{\beta+1}\mathds{1}_{[\delta,\infty)}(x),\ x\in\mathbb{R},
the density of the random variable in (
3.12
). Note that all the subordinators have the same pseudo-distribution
u(1,\cdot)
(omitting the subscript
\nu
) and keep in mind the independence among all the elements appearing in (
3.14
). Now, the Laplace transform of the pseudo-density of (
3.14
) writes as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P\{N(t\delta^{-\alpha})=n\}

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{\infty}f(x_{1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\int_{\delta}^{\infty}f(x_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}u(1,y_{1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}u(1,y_{n})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{n}e^{-\mu\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}y_{k}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t\delta^{-\alpha}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Bigl(\lambda t\delta^{-\alpha}\Bigr)^{n}}{n!}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Biggl(\int_{\delta}^{\infty}f(x_{k})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu x_{k}y_{k}}u(1,y_{k})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{k}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg\{-\lambda t\delta^{-\alpha}\Biggl(1-\int_{\delta}^{\infty}f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu xy}u(1,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Biggr)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg\{-\lambda t\delta^{-\alpha}\Biggl(1-\int_{\delta}^{\infty}f(x)e^{-(\mu x)^{\nu}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Biggr)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg\{-\lambda t\delta^{-\alpha}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\Bigl(1-e^{-(\mu x)^{\nu}}\Bigr)f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg\{-\lambda t\beta\delta^{\beta-\alpha}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-\mu^{\nu}x^{\nu}}}{x^{\beta+1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\exp\Bigg\{-\lambda t\delta^{-\alpha}\Bigl(1-e^{-\mu^{\nu}\delta^{\nu}}\Bigr)-\lambda t\nu\mu^{\nu}\delta^{\beta-\alpha}\int_{\delta}^{\infty}e^{-\mu^{\nu}x^{\nu}}x^{\nu-\beta-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta\downarrow 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{l l}1,&\alpha<\beta,\nu,\\
e^{-\lambda t\mu^{\beta}\Gamma(1-\beta/\nu)},&\alpha=\beta<\nu,\\
e^{-\lambda t\mu^{\nu}},&\alpha=\nu<\beta,\\
0,&\beta\leq\alpha\leq\nu\text{ or }\alpha>\nu.\end{array}\end{cases}

(3.15)

We observe that in the second last step we used integration by parts.

Finally, the second and third cases of (
3.15
) respectively yields (
3.11
) and (
3.13
); note that
X^{\beta,\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}X^{\delta}=\delta
a.s. for

.
∎

We point out that the formulas in Proposition

prove that

has non-decreasing piecewise constant trajectories.

Since

is a non decreasing pseudo-process taking values in

(of the stable pseudo-subordinator of order

\int_{0}^{x}l_{\nu}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\int_{t}^{\infty}u_{\nu}(x,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\ \text{ and }\ \int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}l_{\nu}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\int_{0}^{t}u_{\nu}(x,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

which lead to

Note that when we use

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta x}\mu^{\nu-1}e^{-\mu^{\nu}x}

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\mu^{\nu-1}}{\mu^{\nu}+\delta},\ \ \mu\geq 0\implies\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\delta x}l_{\nu}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=E_{\nu,1}(-\delta t^{\nu}),\ \ t\geq 0.

In view of formula (

Hence, the task of solving problem (

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle u(0,x)=\delta(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(3.18)

3.18
) with differential equation given by

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\bigl(-\Delta\bigr)^{\beta_{i}}u(t,x),\ \ \ t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R^{d}}.

We point out that if
d=1,\ N=1,\beta_{1}=1
and
\lambda_{1}=\lambda
, we obtain the scaled Brownian motion.
\diamond

4
Generalization of the main results

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
. In this section, for
\nu>0
, we consider more complex differential equations and the related Cauchy problems, involving a general time-differential operator
O^{\nu}_{t}
such that there exist a function
L^{\nu}:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
, the functions
L^{\nu}_{k}:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)

and the operators
\tilde{O}^{\nu}_{t,k}
, for some specific indexes
k\in J_{\nu}\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
t_{0}\geq 0
such that

\mathcal{L}\bigl(O^{\nu}_{t}u\bigr)(\mu,x)=L^{\nu}(\mu)\mathcal{L}u(\mu,x)-\sum_{k\in J_{\nu}}L^{\nu}_{k}(\mu)\tilde{O}^{\nu}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x),\ \ \ \mu\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.1)

Note that the quantity
\tilde{O}^{\nu}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x)
is a function of
x
and it is connected to the initial conditions of the Cauchy problem (studied at time
t_{0}
).

Example 4.1
.

To clarify the form of these operators, we note that, for example, in the case of the Dzherbashyan-Caputo fractional operator, (usually)
t_{0}=0
and the set of indexes is
J_{\nu}=\{0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil-1\}
concerning the order of the derivatives involved in the calculation of the Laplace transform (see (
1.2
)), represented by the operators
\tilde{O}^{\nu}_{t,k}=\partial^{k}/\partial t^{k}
. Furthermore, we have the functions
L^{\nu}(\mu)=\mu^{\nu}
and
L^{\nu}_{k}(\mu)=\mu^{\nu-k-1},\ k\in J_{\nu}
.

In the case of Riemann-Liouville fractional differential operator, (usually)
t_{0}=0
,
J_{\nu}=\{1,\dots,\left\lceil\nu\right\rceil\}
,
\tilde{O}^{\nu}_{t,k}
is the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative of order
\nu-k
and
L^{\nu}(\mu)=\mu^{\nu}
,
L^{\nu}_{k}(\mu)=\mu^{k-1}
.
\diamond

Let
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N}>0
and remind the space-operator in (
2.2
). We are interested in the problem

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}O^{\nu_{i}}_{t}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\tilde{O}^{\nu_{i}}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x)=f_{i,k}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ i=1,\dots,N,\ k\in J_{\nu_{i}},\end{cases}

(4.2)

Obviously, in the case that for some
i,k
and
j,h
happens that
\tilde{O}^{\nu_{i}}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x)=\tilde{O}^{\nu_{j}}_{t,h}u(t_{0},x)
, then also the functions
f_{i,k}
and
f_{j,h}
must coincide (meaning that the condition is simply repeated; this is the case when we consider the Dzherbashyan-Caputo derivative).

By proceeding as shown for problem (
2.3
), by using the
t
-Laplace transform (remember expression (
4.1
)) and the
x
-Fourier transform (remember hypothesis (
2.2
)), we have that the solution to (
4.2
) is such that

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u(\mu,\gamma)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in J_{\nu_{i}}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)\,\mathcal{F}f_{i,k}(\gamma)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)-F(\gamma)},\ \ \ \mu\geq 0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.3)

Now, we consider the time problem

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}O^{\nu_{i}}_{t}u(t,x)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(t,x),\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle u(t,0)=h(t),\ \ t\geq 0,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\tilde{O}^{\nu_{i}}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x)=a_{i,k}(x),\ \ x\geq 0,\ i=1,\dots,N,\ k\in J_{\nu_{i}}.\end{cases}

(4.4)

The
t
-Laplace solution reads (we proceed similarly to the proof of problem (
2.5
)),

\mathcal{L}u(\mu,x)=e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)x}\Bigl(\mathcal{L}h(\mu)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in J_{\nu_{i}}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)\int_{0}^{x}e^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)y}a_{i,k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\Bigr),\ \ \mu,x\geq 0.

(4.5)

Let us denote by
u_{2}
the solution to the time problem (
4.4
). By recalling formula (
2.8
) concerning the space problem (
2.4
) and denoting by
u_{1}
its solution, we obtain that the stochastic composition of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
is such that, for
\mu\geq 0,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u_{1}\diamond u_{2}(\mu,\gamma)=\frac{\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)-F(\gamma)}\biggl(\mathcal{L}h(\mu)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in J_{\nu_{i}}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{F(\gamma)y}a_{i,k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\biggr).

(4.6)

Finally, the next statement is easily verified by keeping in mind formulas (
4.3
) and (
4.6
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let us assume the hypotheses (
2.2
) and (
4.1
). Then, the solution to the differential Cauchy problem (
4.2
) can be expressed as the stochastic composition of the solution to (the space) problem (
2.4
) with the solution to (the time) problem (
4.4
), with the initial conditions

g=\delta,\ \ h=0,\ \ \int_{0}^{\infty}e^{F(\gamma)y}a_{i,k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\mathcal{F}f_{i,k}(\gamma),\ \ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \forall\ i=1,\dots,N,\ k\in J_{\nu_{i}}.

(4.7)

Note that if the problem (
4.2
) has particular initial conditions, or the operators
\tilde{O}^{\nu_{i}}_{t,k}
have some specific forms, then the associate sub-problems can have an easier structure. The simplest case is when
f_{i,k}=0
for all
i,k
except for one couple of indexes
j,k_{j}
, then it is sufficient that
g=f_{j,k_{j}},\ a_{i,k}=0\ \forall\ i,k
and
h
such that
\mathcal{L}h(\mu)=L_{k_{j}}^{\nu_{j}}(\mu)
.

We now consider more specific operators in order to obtain better results on the required initial conditions, in particular, we generalize Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let us assume the hypotheses (
2.2
), (
4.1
) and that

\tilde{O}_{t,k}^{\nu_{i}}=\tilde{O}_{t,k}^{\nu_{j}}=:\tilde{O}_{t,k}\ \forall\ i,j=1,\dots,N\ \text{ and }\ k\in J_{\nu_{i}},J_{\nu_{j}}.

(4.8)

Then, the solution to the differential Cauchy problem (
4.2
) can be expressed as the stochastic composition of the solution to (the space) problem (
2.4
) with the solution to (the time) problem (
4.4
), with the initial conditions

g=\delta,\ \ h=0,\ \ \int_{0}^{\infty}e^{F(\gamma)y}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma),\ \ \gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \forall\ k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}.

(4.9)

Furthermore, if
f_{k}=0
for all
k
except for one index
j
, then sufficient initial conditions for the auxiliary problems are

g=f_{j},\ \ a_{k}=0\ \forall\ k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}},\ \ h\text{ such that }\mathcal{L}h(\mu)=\sum_{i\in I_{j}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{j}(\mu),\ \mu\geq 0.

(4.10)

Obviously, under hypothesis (
4.8
), both the general problem (
4.2
) and the time problem (
4.4
) can be suitably rewritten; see the proof below for their explicit forms.

Note that (
4.10
) and (
4.9
) respectively coincides with cases (
a
) and (
b
) before Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

Under hypothesis (
4.8
) problem (
4.2
) simplifies (getting rid of redundant repetitions of the initial conditions) and becomes

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}O^{\nu_{i}}_{t}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[1.0pt]
\displaystyle\tilde{O}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x)=f_{k}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}.\end{cases}

(4.11)

Note that the request
k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}
is necessary and sufficient to include all the initial conditions.

For each
k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}
, we consider the set
I_{k}=\{1\leq i\leq N\,:\,k\in J_{\nu_{i}}\}
and we can rewrite the solution (
4.3
) as

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u(\mu,\gamma)=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k\in J_{\nu_{i}}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)-F(\gamma)}=\frac{\sum_{k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}}\mathcal{F}f_{k}(\gamma)\sum_{i\in I_{k}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)-F(\gamma)}

(4.12)

Similarly, also the initial conditions
a_{i,k}
of the time problem (
4.4
) (suitably modified) do not depend on
i
, i.e.
a_{i,k}=a_{k}\ \forall\ i
, and the time problem reads

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}O^{\nu_{i}}_{t}u(t,x)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(t,x),\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle u(t,0)=h(t),\ \ t\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle\tilde{O}_{t,k}u(t_{0},x)=a_{k}(x),\ \ x\geq 0,\ k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}.\end{cases}

(4.13)

The time problem (
4.13
) has solution (
4.5
) with suitable changes and the stochastic composition (
4.6
) modifies into

\mathcal{L}\mathcal{F}u_{1}\diamond u_{2}(\mu,\gamma)=\frac{\mathcal{F}g(\gamma)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)-F(\gamma)}\biggl(\mathcal{L}h(\mu)+\sum_{k\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}J_{\nu_{i}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{F(\gamma)y}a_{k}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\,\sum_{i\in I_{k}}L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)\biggr).

(4.14)

Finally, by comparing (
4.14
) with (
4.13
) we obtain the requests (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) on the initial condition which complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.1
Multi-order Dzherbashyan-Caputo fractional derivatives

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. In this section we present multi-order Dzherbashyan-Caputo fractional Cauchy problem as a particular case of the above theory. Indeed, Theorem
4.2
applies to the solution to the problem, with
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N}>0
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}>0
,

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{i}}}{\partial t^{\nu_{i}}}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=f_{k}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ k=0,\dots,\max\{\left\lceil\nu_{i}\right\rceil\,:1\leq i\leq N\}-1.\end{cases}

(4.15)

Using the notation above, we have
O_{t}^{\nu_{i}}=\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{i}}}{\partial t^{\nu_{i}}},\ t_{0}=0,\ \tilde{O}_{t,k}^{\nu_{i}}=\tilde{O}_{t,k}=\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}
with
k\in J_{\nu_{i}}=\{0,\dots,\left\lceil\nu_{i}\right\rceil-1\}
and
L^{\nu_{i}}(\mu)=\lambda_{i}\mu^{\nu_{i}},\ L^{\nu_{i}}_{k}(\mu)=\lambda_{i}\mu^{\nu_{i}-k-1}
.

Our interest focuses on the probabilistic and pseudo-probabilistic applications, therefore, we restrict ourselves to study in further detail the problems of the following type, with
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N}>0
and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}>0
,

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{i}}}{\partial t^{\nu_{i}}}u(t,x)=O_{x}u(t,x),\ \ t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[7.0pt]
u(0,x)=\delta(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=0,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ k=1,\dots,\max\{\left\lceil\nu_{i}\right\rceil\,:1\leq i\leq N\}-1.\end{cases}

(4.16)

According to Theorem
4.2
and conditions (
4.10
), the sub-problems related to system (
4.16
) are (
3.18
), concerning the space operator, and, for the time operator we have

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{i}}}{\partial t^{\nu_{i}}}u(t,x)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}u(t,x),\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[7.0pt]
u(t,0)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\mu^{\nu_{i}-1}\bigr)(t),\ \ t\geq 0,\\[7.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial t^{k}}\Big|_{t=0}=0,\ \ x\geq 0,\ k=0,\dots,\max\{\left\lceil\nu_{i}\right\rceil\,:1\leq i\leq N\}-1.\end{cases}

(4.17)

The next statement concerns the linear combination of pseudo-subordinators as well as the pseudo-probabilistic solution to the time problem (
4.17
) (including the probabilistic case as a particular one).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S_{\nu_{1}},\dots S_{\nu_{N}}
be independent stable pseudo-subordinators of orders
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N}>0
and
\underline{\nu}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{N})
. Define the pseudo-processes

S_{\underline{\nu}}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}^{1/\nu_{i}}S_{\nu_{i}}(t),\ \ \ t\geq 0.

(4.18)

The pseudo-density of
S_{\underline{\nu}}
satisfies the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t,x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\frac{\partial^{\nu_{i}}}{\partial x^{\nu_{i}}}u(t,x),\ \ t,x\geq 0,\\[7.0pt]
u(0,x)=\delta(x),\ \ x\geq 0,\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k}u}{\partial x^{k}}\Big|_{x=0}=0,\ \ t\geq 0,\ k=0,\dots,\max\{\left\lceil\nu_{i}\right\rceil\,:1\leq i\leq N\}-1.\end{cases}

(4.19)

Furthermore, let us denote by
L_{\underline{\nu}}(t)=\inf\{x\geq 0\,:\,S_{\underline{\nu}}(x)\geq t\},\ t\geq 0,
the pseudo-inverse of
S_{\underline{\nu}}
. Then, the pseudo-density of
L_{\underline{\nu}}
satisfies (the time) problem (
4.17
).

Proof.

Let us denote by
u_{\underline{\nu}}
the density of
S_{\underline{\nu}}
and by
u_{\nu_{i}}
the density of
S_{\nu_{i}}\ \forall\ i
. In view of the independence of the pseudo-subordinators and formula (
3.9
), the
x
-Laplace transform of
u
can be computed as follows

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu x}u_{\underline{\nu}}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu\lambda_{i}^{1/\nu_{i}}y}u_{\nu_{i}}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=e^{-t\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\mu^{\nu_{i}}},\ \ \mu,t\geq 0.

(4.20)

Finally, it is not difficult to show that (
4.20
) satisfies system (
4.19
) after having applied the
x
-Laplace transform.

We denote by
l_{\underline{\nu}}
the pseudo-density of
L_{\underline{\nu}}
. Now, by observing that the relationships in (
3.16
) hold in the case of
S_{\underline{\nu}}
and its inverse
L_{\underline{\nu}}
, we readily prove that the
t
-Laplace transform of the pseudo-density of
L_{\underline{\nu}}
reads, with
\mu,x\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu t}l_{\underline{\nu}}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\mu^{\nu_{i}-1}e^{-x\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\mu^{\nu_{i}}},

(4.21)

which satisfies system (
4.17
) after having applied the
t
-Laplace transform.
∎

We are now ready to give the pseudo-probabilistic interpretation of the solution to (
4.16
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be an independent pseudo-process whose density satisfies the space problem (
3.18
), then the density of the pseudo-process
\big\{X\bigl(L_{\underline{\nu}}(t)\bigr)\big\}_{t\geq 0}
is solution to (
4.16
) (where
L_{\underline{\nu}}
is defined in Proposition
4.1
, i.e. it is the inverse of the linear combination of stable pseudo-subordinators given in (
4.18
)).

Proof.

The theorem is a straightforward consequence of Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Example 4.2

(Riesz-Feller operator)
.

Let

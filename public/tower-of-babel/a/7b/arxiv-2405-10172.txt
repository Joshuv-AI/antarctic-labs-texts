Abstract
. Let L / K L/K be a finite separable extension of fields of degree n n , and let E / K E/K be its Galois closure. Greither and Pareigis showed how to find all Hopf–Galois structures on L / K L/K . We will call a subextension L ′ / K L^{\prime}/K of E / K E/K parallel to L / K L/K if [ L ′ : K ] = n [L^{\prime}:K]=n . In this paper, we investigate the relationship between the Hopf–Galois structures on an extension L / K L/K and those on the related parallel extensions. We give an example of a transitive subgroup corresponding to an extension admitting a Hopf–Galois structure but that has a parallel extension admitting no Hopf–Galois structures. We show that once one has such a situation, it can be extended into an infinite family of transitive subgroups admitting this phenomenon. We also investigate this fully in the case of extensions of degree p ​ q pq with p , q p,q distinct odd primes, and show that there is no example of such an extension admitting the phenomenon.

1.
Background and Introduction

Hopf–Galois structures were discovered by Chase and Sweedler in
[
CS69
]
in a theory which describes an analogue of the Galois correspondence for commutative rings, and in particular, for non-Galois field extensions. Using Galois descent, Greither and Pareigis in
[
GP87
]
then showed that the problem of finding Hopf–Galois structures on separable (but not necessarily normal) field extensions can be approached entirely group theoretically. Let
L/K
be a separable extension of degree
n
with Galois closure
E
. One can form the Galois groups
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
and
G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L)
. The result of Greither and Pareigis shows that a
K
-Hopf algebra
H
giving a Hopf–Galois structure on
L/K
corresponds to a subgroup
N\leq\text{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
(the abstract isomorphism class of
N
is known as the ‘type’ of the Hopf–Galois structure) which is regular on the coset space
G/G^{\prime}
and normalised by the image,
\lambda(G)
, of the left translation map
\lambda:G\rightarrow\text{Perm}(G/G^{\prime}),\lambda(g)(hG^{\prime})\mapsto ghG^{\prime}
. More precisely,
H=E[N]^{G}
, the sub-Hopf algebra fixed under the action of
G
. Here,
G
acts on
E
by field automorphisms, and on
N
by conjugation via
\lambda
, that is for
g\in G,\eta\in N
, we have
g\cdot\eta:=\lambda(g)\eta\lambda(g)^{-1}
. Greither–Pareigis theory also tells us how
H
acts on
L/K
, but we do not give that here.

In 1996, Byott in
[
Byo96
]
gave a reformulation of this situation by effectively reversing the roles played by
N
and
G
. Now, given a group
N
of order, say,
n
, one may construct the
holomorph
,
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, of
N
. Abusing notation, given the left translation map
\lambda:N\to\mathrm{Perm}(N)
given by
\lambda(\eta)(\mu):=\eta\mu
for all
\eta,\mu\in N
, the holomorph of
N
is defined as the normaliser of
\lambda(N)
in
\mathrm{Perm}(N)
, and is thus viewed as a permutation group. It can then be shown that

\mathrm{Hol}(N)\cong N\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(N),

with multiplication given by

[\eta,\alpha][\mu,\beta]:=[\eta\alpha(\mu),\alpha\beta]\;\;\forall\eta,\mu\in N,\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(N).

This is the viewpoint we will take for the rest of this paper unless specified otherwise. There is then a natural action of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
on
N
given by

[\eta,\alpha]\cdot\mu:=\eta\alpha(\mu).

A subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is then called
transitive
if its action on
N
is transitive. Given a separable extension
L/K
with
G,G^{\prime}
as above, Byott showed that
N
is a regular subgroup of
\mathrm{Perm}(G/G^{\prime})
normalised by
\lambda(G)
(with
\lambda
as initially defined) if and only if there is a transitive subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
and an isomorphism
\phi:G\to M
with
\phi(G^{\prime})=M^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N})
. In this setting, we will say that
(G,G^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
(M,M^{\prime})
. Therefore it is possible to hunt for Hopf–Galois structures by looking for transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. That is, given a transitive subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for some group
N
of order
n
, any separable extension
L/K
of degree
n
such that
(G,G^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
(M,M^{\prime})
will admit a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N
. Note here that if we start from the holomorph, there is no inverse Galois problem as the base field of the corresponding extension(s) is not assumed. In the case that there are transitive subgroups
M_{1}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{1})
and
M_{2}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
such that there is an isomorphism
\phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
with
\phi(M_{1}^{\prime})=M_{2}^{\prime}
, then we will say that
(M_{1},M_{1}^{\prime})
and
(M_{2},M_{2}^{\prime})
are
isomorphic as permutation groups
.

Now let
n
be a positive integer and suppose we fix a separable field extension
L/K
of degree
n
with Galois closure
E
. Then Galois theory tells us that there may be other degree
n
subextensions
L^{\prime}/K
of
E
(see Figure
1
). We will call these related extensions
parallel extensions
with respect to
L/K
. It can then be asked how the extension
L^{\prime}/K
behaves relative to
L/K
in terms of the Hopf–Galois structures that it admits. Indeed, if
L/K
is Galois, then
E=L=L^{\prime}
and so there are no parallel extensions to
L/K
. If
L/K
is not Galois, then interesting phenomena may occur. For example, let
L/K
be a degree
4
extension with
\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\cong D_{8}
, the dihedral group of order
8
. Note that
D_{8}
has four non-normal subgroups of order
2
(one of which corresponds to
L/K
), and one normal subgroup
H
of order
2
. Thus there is a subextension
L^{\prime}/K
of
E/K
, of degree
4
which is Galois (see Figure
2
). In this case, the extension
L^{\prime}/K
has smaller Galois closure (i.e.
L^{\prime}
itself) than
L/K
, and therefore admits Hopf–Galois structures with smaller permutation group. This example also highlights that the relation of extensions being parallel is not symmetric. In particular,
L^{\prime}/K
is parallel to
L/K
, but not vice versa, as
L
is not contained in the Galois closure of
L^{\prime}/K
.

Using this example as motivation, it is interesting and fruitful to study such phenomena on large classes of extensions where the Hopf–Galois structures have already been determined.

To this end, suppose that we have a classification of Hopf–Galois structures on separable extensions of degree, say,
n
. Further suppose that
L/K
is one such extension which admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N
, for some group
N
of order
n
. Recall that
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
where
E/K
is the Galois closure of
L/K
. Thus an index
n
subgroup
H
of
G
corresponds to a degree
n
sub-extension
L^{\prime}/K
of
E/K
, that is, a parallel extension to
L/K
. The Hopf–Galois structures admitted by
L^{\prime}/K
then depend on its Galois closure
E^{\prime}
, which, as highlighted in Figure
2
, may be strictly smaller than
E
. This may be computed by first taking the largest normal subgroup
C
of
G
contained in
H
, that is
C=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H):=\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}
. Then
E^{\prime}=E^{C}
satisfies
\mathrm{Gal}(E^{\prime}/K)\cong G/C
and
\mathrm{Gal}(E^{\prime}/L^{\prime})\cong H/C
. In other words, asking whether
L^{\prime}/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
M
is the same as asking whether there is a transitive subgroup
J\leq\mathrm{Hol}(M)
and an isomorphism
\phi:G/C\to J
such that
\phi(H/C)=J^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{J}(1_{M})
.

In this paper, we start by giving some necessary definitions and present some results relating to parallel extensions to an extension of squarefree degree. In Section
3
, we specialise to the case of extensions of degree
pq
where
p
and
q
are distinct odd primes. In the final section, we give some examples of permutation groups (of non-squarefree degree) which correspond to separable extensions admitting a Hopf–Galois structure, but having a parallel extension admitting no Hopf–Galois structure. We also give a way of constructing an infinite family of examples.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we give the results needed for the rest of the paper.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
N
any group of order
n
. We denote an element of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
by
[\eta,\alpha]
where
\eta\in N
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(N)
. We will write
\eta
for
[\eta,\text{id}_{N}]
and
\alpha
for
[1_{N},\alpha]
to simplify notation. We also write
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
for the ring of integers modulo
m
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
G
be a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
with
|N|=n
and let
L/K
be a separable extension corresponding to
G
as in Section
1
. Let
H_{1},H_{2}
be two index
n
subgroups of
G
, let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
denote the normal cores of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
respectively in
G
, and let
L_{1}/K
and
L_{2}/K
be the respective corresponding extensions. We note that the relationship between
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
greatly determines how
L_{1}/K
and
L_{2}/K
behave with respect to each other from the perspective of Hopf–Galois theory:

If, for example,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are in the same orbit under
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, then given a
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
such that
\phi(H_{1})=H_{2}
, it induces an isomorphism
\overline{\phi}:G/C_{1}\to G/C_{2}
such that
\overline{\phi}(H_{1}/C_{1})=H_{2}/C_{2}
. If there is then a transitive subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N^{\prime})
for some group
N^{\prime}
of order at most
n
such that
(G/C_{1},H_{1}/C_{1})
is isomorphic to
(M,M^{\prime})
, we thus also have that
(G/C_{2},H_{2}/C_{2})
is isomorphic to
(M,M^{\prime})
. We note that this makes sense as we may view, for example,
G/C_{1}\overset{\psi}{\cong}\mathrm{Gal}(E^{C_{1}}/K)
with
\psi(H_{1}/C_{1})=\mathrm{Gal}(E^{C_{1}}/L_{1})
, where
E^{C_{1}}
is the Galois closure of
L_{1}/K
. We therefore have that
L_{1}/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N^{\prime}
if and only if
L_{2}/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N^{\prime}
. We note that the situation in which
H_{2}
and
H_{2}
are conjugate in
G
(in which case
L_{1}/K
and
L_{2}/K
are conjugate extensions) is a special case of this behaviour; in particular, we get that
C_{1}=C_{2}
and not just
C_{1}\cong C_{2}
.

It is also possible for
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
to be isomorphic as abstract groups, but not be in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit. In this case, there are a number of possibilities for
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
and hence
E^{C_{1}}/K
and
E^{C_{2}}/K
. In particular, we may have that
[E^{C_{1}}:K]\neq[E^{C_{2}}:K]
, and in the extreme case as demonstrated by Figure
2
, it could be that
E^{C_{1}}/K
is Galois and
E^{C_{2}}/K
is not.

A particularly interesting scenario is where
E^{C_{1}}=E^{C_{2}}
with
L_{1}/K
admitting Hopf–Galois structures, but
L_{2}/K
not admitting any. With respect to the extension
L/K
we started off with, say
L_{1}:=L
and so
H_{1}=G^{\prime}
, this would correspond to
H_{2}
having trivial core, possibly even
H_{2}\cong G^{\prime}
, but
L_{2}/K
behaving very differently from
L/K
. In particular,
(G,H_{2})
is not isomorphic to any transitive subgroup
(M,M^{\prime})
of some
\mathrm{Hol}(N^{\prime})
.

In light of this remark, we note it is therefore of interest to sort the index
n
subgroups into conjugacy classes, orbits under
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
, and abstract isomorphism classes.

Let
\pi
be any set of primes. We recall that a
\pi
-subgroup of a group
G
is a subgroup
H\leq G
such that the prime divisors of
|H|
all lie in
\pi
. Further, a
Hall
\pi
-subgroup
of
G
is a
\pi
-subgroup
H
whose order is coprime with
|G:H|
. We also recall Hall’s Theorem below, which will be referred to in the rest of this paper.

Theorem 2.2

(Hall, 1928. See 9.1.7 of
[
Rob96
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite solvable group and
\pi
any set of primes dividing
|G|
. Then every
\pi
-subgroup of
G
is contained in a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
G
. Further, any two Hall
\pi
-subgroups of
G
are conjugate.

In light of this, for a positive integer
n
, we define

\displaystyle\pi(n):=\{\text{primes dividing }n\},

\displaystyle\pi^{\prime}(n):=\{\text{primes not dividing }n\}.

We also denote by
\#\mathrm{Cl}_{n}(G)
the number of conjugacy classes that the index
n
subgroups of
G
fall into.

For the remainder of this section, we let
n
be any squarefree integer unless stated otherwise, and recall that
N
is a group of order
n
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
N
is a group of squarefree order, then by Lemma 3.2 of
[
AB18
]
, we have that
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is solvable. So Hall’s theorem applies to any subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
.

We now begin by stating and proving some results for transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There is a unique Hall
\pi(n)
-subgroup,
Q
, of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Furthermore, let
G
be any subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Then
G
may be written as
G=U\rtimes V
, where
U=G\cap Q
is the unique Hall
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
G
, and
V
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}(n)
-subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

If
N
is a group of squarefree order
n
, then (see
[
AB18
]
) for some
d,e
with
n=ed
and some
k
with order
d
modulo
e
, we have

N\cong\langle\sigma,\tau\mid\sigma^{e}=\tau^{d}=1,\tau\sigma=\sigma^{k}\tau\rangle.

Further, let
z:=\gcd(k-1,e)
and consider the elements
\theta,\{\phi_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{e}^{\times}}
of
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
given by

\displaystyle\theta(\sigma)=\sigma,

\displaystyle\theta(\tau)=\sigma^{z}\tau,

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(\sigma)=\sigma^{s},

\displaystyle\phi_{s}(\tau)=\tau.

Then Lemma 4.1 of
[
AB20
]
tells us that

\mathrm{Aut}(N)=\langle\theta\rangle\rtimes\{\phi_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{e}^{\times}}.

Noting that
\Phi:=\{\phi_{s}\}_{s\in\mathbb{Z}_{e}^{\times}}
is an abelian group, we further decompose
\Phi
as
\Phi=\Phi_{1}\times\Phi_{2}
such that
\Phi_{1}
is the Hall
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
\Phi
and
\Phi_{2}
is the Hall
\pi^{\prime}(n)
subgroup of
\Phi
.

Now, the subgroup
Q:=N\rtimes(\langle\theta\rangle\rtimes\Phi_{1})
is a normal (and therefore unique) Hall
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, and has
\Phi_{2}
as a complementary subgroup. Therefore, we may write
\mathrm{Hol}(N)=Q\rtimes\Phi_{2}
.

Let
G
be any subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Denote by
U
the unique maximal normal
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
G
. Clearly we have that
U\leq Q
as any
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
must be contained in
Q
. We also have that
G\cap Q\leq U
. This is because
G\cap Q
is normal in
G
and
U
contains every normal
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
G
. Therefore we have that
U=G\cap Q
. It is clear then that
U
has order coprime to its index in
G
, and therefore by the Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem,
G=U\rtimes V
, where
V
is some
\pi^{\prime}(n)
-subgroup of
G
. The Schur-Zassenhaus Theorem also tells us that
V
is contained in a conjugate subgroup of
\Phi_{2}
in
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
.
∎

Whenever we write
G=U\rtimes V
, we will now assume that
U=G\cap Q
and
V
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}(n)
-subgroup of
G
which is a subgroup of a conjugate of
\Phi_{2}
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
G=U\rtimes V\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
be any subgroup. Then any index
n
subgroup
H
of
G
can be written as
H=U^{\prime}\rtimes V^{\prime}
, where
U^{\prime}
is some index
n
subgroup of
U
and
V^{\prime}
is a subgroup conjugate to
V
in
G
.

Proof.

This is the same proof as that given for the form of
G
. However, we note that
|V^{\prime}|=|V|
, and so
V
and
V^{\prime}
must be conjugate in
G
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
be any subgroup. Let

H_{1}:=U_{1}\rtimes V_{1},\text{ and }H_{2}:=U_{2}\rtimes V_{2}

be index
n
subgroups of
G
as described in Corollary
2.5
. Then

(i)

The subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are conjugate in
G
if and only if
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are conjugate in
G
.

(ii)

The subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit if and only if
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit.

(iii)

The subgroups
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are isomorphic as abstract groups if and only if
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are isomorphic as abstract groups.

Proof.

(i)
Suppose first that there is a
g\in G
such that
gH_{1}g^{-1}=H_{2}
. Then

gU_{1}g^{-1}\rtimes gV_{1}g^{-1}=g(U_{1}\rtimes V_{1})g^{-1}=U_{2}\rtimes V_{2}.

If
gU_{1}g^{-1}\not\subseteq U_{2}
, then there is a
u\in U_{1}
such that
gug^{-1}\in H_{2}\setminus U_{2}
. However, this contradicts the fact that
U_{2}
is the unique Hall
\pi(n)
-subgroup of
H_{2}
. This is because
u
and hence also
gug^{-1}
have order a product of primes in
\pi(n)
. So we must have
gU_{1}g^{-1}=U_{2}
.

Now suppose that there is a
u\in G
such that
uU_{1}u^{-1}=U_{2}
. We show that there is a
g\in G
such that
g(U_{1}\rtimes V_{1})g^{-1}=U_{2}\rtimes V_{2}
. We claim that there is a
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{2})
such that
u^{\prime}V_{2}u^{\prime-1}=uV_{1}u^{-1}
. Given this, we see that

u^{\prime}(U_{2}\rtimes V_{2})u^{\prime-1}=u^{\prime}U_{2}u^{\prime-1}\rtimes u^{\prime}V_{2}u^{\prime-1}=U_{2}\rtimes uV_{1}u^{-1}=u(U_{1}\rtimes V_{1})u^{-1}.

Therefore
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are conjugate in
G
(that is
g:=u^{\prime-1}u)
. To show the existence of
u^{\prime}
, we note that both
V_{2}
and
uV_{1}u^{-1}
are subgroups of
\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{2})
. This is clear for
V_{2}
, but for
uV_{1}u^{-1}
, we have
V_{1}\leq\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{1})
, and so

uV_{1}u^{-1}\leq u\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{1})u^{-1}=\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(uU_{1}u^{-1})=\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{2}).

Finally, we note that both
V_{2}
and
uV_{1}u^{-1}
are Hall
\pi^{\prime}(n)
-subgroups of
\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{2})
, and so they must be conjugate by an element, say
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Norm}_{G}(U_{2})
.

(ii)
We now show
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit if and only if
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit. Indeed, if there is a
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
such that
\phi(H_{1})=H_{2}
, then
\phi(U_{1}\rtimes V_{1})=U_{2}\rtimes V_{2}
. We must have
\phi(U_{1})=U_{2}
as
U_{1},U_{2}
are the unique Hall
\pi(n)
-subgroups in
H_{1},H_{2}
respectively. Therefore, now suppose that there is a
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
such that
\phi(U_{1})=U_{2}
. Then
\phi(U_{1}\rtimes V_{1})=\phi(U_{1})\rtimes\phi(V_{1})=U_{2}\rtimes\phi(V_{1})
. Now clearly
U_{2}
is conjugate to
U_{2}
, so by the first part of this proof,
U_{2}\rtimes\phi(V_{1})
must be conjugate to
U_{2}\rtimes V_{2}
. Composing
\phi
with this conjugation, we see that
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit.

(iii)
Finally, we note that the argument for
(ii)
can be replicated for
\phi:H_{1}\to H_{2}
now an isomorphism of abstract groups.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Suppose
G=U\rtimes V
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Then for any index
n
-subgroup
H
of
G
,
\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H\cap U)
.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)\supseteq\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H\cap U)
. As mentioned in Lemma
2.4
, we have that
G=U\rtimes V
, where
U:=G\cap Q
and
V
is some subgroup of
G
of order coprime to
n
. By Corollary
2.5
, we also have that any index
n
subgroup
H
of
G
has the form
H=U_{1}\rtimes V_{1}
, where
U_{1}
is an index
n
subgroup of
U
and
V_{1}
is conjugate to
V
in
G
.

Suppose then that
C:=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)=U_{2}\rtimes V_{2}
where
U_{2}\leq U_{1}
and
V_{2}
is a complement of
U_{2}
in
C
(that is
V_{2}
is conjugate to some subgroup of
V_{1}
by Schur-Zassenhaus). In particular,
C
is normal in
G
, and so
gV_{2}g^{-1}\leq C
for all
g\in G
. Therefore, for each
v_{2}\in V_{2}
, there exist a
u^{\prime}\in U_{2}
and
v^{\prime}\in V_{2}
such that
gv_{2}g^{-1}=u^{\prime}v^{\prime}
. However, we then have
u^{\prime}=gv_{2}g^{-1}v^{\prime-1}
, and as
u^{\prime}
has coprime order with both
v_{2}
and
v^{\prime}
, we must have that
u^{\prime}=1
, and so
V_{2}
must be normal in
G
. In particular, we have that
V_{2}=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(V_{2})
. We claim that
V_{2}
is trivial and so
C=U_{2}=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H\cap U)
.

Since
G
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, the index
n
subgroup
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(1_{N})
of
G
has trivial core. Now by Corollary
2.5
, we have that
G^{\prime}=U_{3}\rtimes V_{3}
where

is a
\pi^{\prime}(n)
-subgroup of
G
. In particular,
V_{1}
is conjugate to
V_{3}
in

\mathrm{Core}_{G}(V_{2})\subseteq\bigcap_{g\in G}gV_{1}g^{-1}=\bigcap_{g\in G}gV_{3}g^{-1}\subseteq\bigcap_{g\in G}gG^{\prime}g^{-1}=\{1\}.

∎

3.
Parallel extensions of degree
pq

We now specialise to the case
n=pq
with
p>q
distinct odd primes. In this section, we investigate the Hopf–Galois structures on parallel extensions of degree
n
. We start with a few more results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L/K
be a separable extension of degree
pq
with
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{q}
. Then any parallel extension to
L/K
admits a unique Hopf–Galois structure, which is of cyclic type.

Proof.

If
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{q}
, then
\gcd(pq,\varphi(pq))=1
, so
pq
is a Burnside number. Theorem 1 of
[
Byo96
]
then tells us that every separable extension of degree
pq
admits a unique Hopf–Galois structure, which is of cyclic type. If
L/K
is a separable extension of degree
pq
, then any parallel extension to
L/K
also has degree
pq
and thus also admits a unique Hopf–Galois structure, which is also of cyclic type.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L_{1}/K,L_{2}/K
be two separable extensions of degree
pq
with the same Galois closure
E
such that they both admit Hopf–Galois structures. Then
L_{1}/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N
if and only if
L_{2}/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
N
.

Proof.

Let
G:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)
,
G_{1}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L_{1})
and
G_{2}:=\mathrm{Gal}(E/L_{2})
. If both
L_{1}/K
and
L_{2}/K
admit Hopf–Galois structures, then there are transitive subgroups
M_{1}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{1})
,
M_{2}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N_{2})
with
M_{1}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{M_{1}}(1_{N_{1}})
,
M_{2}^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{M_{2}}(1_{N_{2}})
and isomorphisms
\phi_{1}:G\to M_{1}
,
\phi_{2}:G\to M_{2}
with
\phi_{1}(G_{1})=M_{1}^{\prime}
and
\phi_{2}(G_{2})=M_{2}^{\prime}
respectively.

It was observed in section 3 of
[
Dar24b
]
(immediately after the proof of Proposition 3.19) that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are isomorphic as permutation groups if and only if they are isomorphic as abstract groups. Given that
M_{1}\cong G\cong M_{2}
, we therefore must have that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are also isomorphic as permutation groups, and therefore both
L_{1}/K
and
L_{2}/K
admit Hopf–Galois structures of types
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
.
∎

Remark 3.3

3.2.
Cyclic case

Let
N
be the cyclic group of order
pq
, let
G

, and let

.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X,Y,t
and
c
be as in section
3.1
.

If
G=N\rtimes X
, then

\#\mathrm{Cl}_{pq}(G)=\begin{cases}1,\;\;\text{if }q^{2}\nmid r,\\
2,\;\;\text{if }q^{2}\mid r\text{ and }\tau\notin Z(G),\\
q+1,\;\;\text{if }q^{2}\mid r\text{ and }\tau\in Z(G).\end{cases}

If
G=J_{t,c}\rtimes Y
, then

\#\mathrm{Cl}_{pq}(G)=1.

Further, for any such
G
, all parallel extensions to any extension associated with
G
admit at least one Hopf–Galois structure of cyclic type.

Proof.

We begin by noting that for any transitive subgroup
G<\mathrm{Hol}(N)
such that
q^{2}\nmid r
, any index
pq
subgroup of
G
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}(pq)
-subgroup of
G
, and so they are all conjugate. Thus we assume that
q^{2}\mid r
for the remainder of the proof, and thus we let
c+1
be the largest power of
q
dividing
r
(and so
1\leq c\leq e_{0}
). For the index
pq
subgroups
H
of
G
, we also note that by Proposition
2.6
, we need only look at the conjugacy classes of
H\cap Q
in
G
.

Now fix a subgroup
X\leq\mathrm{Aut}(N)
and consider the transitive subgroup
G=N\rtimes X
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. Let
H
be an index
pq
subgroup of
G
. Then it can be shown that
H\cap Q
has one of the following forms:

H_{1}=\left\langle\left[\sigma^{a}\tau^{b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]\right\rangle\;\;\text{ or }\;\;H_{2}=\left\langle\left[\sigma^{a},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-(c-1)}}\right],\tau\right\rangle,

for some
0\leq a\leq p-1
and
0\leq b\leq q-1
, noting that, for subgroups of type
H_{2}
, we have
a=0
whenever
c=1
. With a view to computing
\#\mathrm{Cl}_{pq}(G)
, we first note that we can conjugate any of these subgroups by a suitable power of
\sigma
to obtain a subgroup of the same type but with
a=0
. Given this observation, we abuse notation and now work with the representatives

(1)

H_{1}=\left\langle\left[\tau^{b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]\right\rangle\;\;\text{ or }\;\;H_{2}=\left\langle\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-(c-1)}},\tau\right\rangle.

If
\tau\notin Z(G)
, then there is an element
\beta\in G
such that conjugation by
\beta
gives a non-trivial automorphism of
\langle\tau\rangle
. Thus we have, say,
\beta\tau=\tau^{x}\beta
for some
1\leq x\leq q-1
. We claim that we can assume
\beta\in H
. This is because
\beta
has order coprime with
pq
and so lies in a Hall
\pi^{\prime}(pq)
-subgroup of
G
. Further by Corollary
2.5
, we have that
H
contains another Hall
\pi^{\prime}(pq)
-subgroup of
G
and so also an element
\beta^{\prime}
conjugate to
\beta
. Finally, as both
N
and
\mathrm{Aut}(N)
are abelian, and so it can be shown that
\beta^{\prime}\tau\beta^{\prime-1}=\beta\tau\beta^{-1}
.

Now, if we conjugate the generator
\left[\tau^{b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]
of
H_{1}
by
\beta
and
\beta^{2}
respectively and assume that
b\neq 0
, we see that
\left[\tau^{bx},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right],\left[\tau^{2bx},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]\in H_{1}
. In particular, we then get that
\tau^{bx}\in H_{1}
, and thus
\tau,\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\in H_{1}
. However, this then means that
q^{c+1}\mid|H_{1}|
, which gives a contradiction as then
H_{1}
has index
p
in
G
. So we must have
b=0
in (
1
). We note that
H_{1}
is cyclic and
H_{2}
is not, unless
c=1
. Thus, for
c>1
, we obtain two conjugacy classes of index
pq
subgroups of
G
, represented by
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. Each of these classes is of size
p
. For
c=1
, we get the families represented by

(2)

H_{1}=\left\langle\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-1}}\right\rangle\;\;\text{ or }\;\;H_{2}=\left\langle\tau\right\rangle.

It is clear that they remain in distinct conjugacy classes as
\langle\tau\rangle
is a normal subgroup in
G
. Thus
H_{1}
represents a conjugacy class of size
p
, and
H_{2}
represents a class of size
1
.

We now compute
C:=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)
for each index
pq
subgroup
H
of
G
, with a view to seeing whether
G/C
appears as a transitive subgroup
J
of
\mathrm{Hol}(M)
with
\mathrm{Stab}_{J}(1_{M})\cong H/C
for
M=C_{pq}
or
M=C_{p}\rtimes C_{q}
. We will find that taking
M=C_{pq}=N
is sufficient, showing that all parallel extensions in this situation admit a Hopf–Galois structure of cyclic type.

For an index
pq
subgroup
H
containing a group of type
H_{1}
, we see that
H
is conjugate to
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(1_{N})
, and therefore
C=\{1\}
. The corresponding parallel extension is therefore conjugate to the original extension and so admits a Hopf–Galois structure of cyclic type.

For an index
pq
subgroup
H
containing a group of type
H_{2}
, we compute that
C=\langle\tau\rangle
. For some
X^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(N)
of coprime order to
pq
, we therefore have:

G/C\cong\langle\sigma,\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\rangle\rtimes X^{\prime}

and

H/C\cong\left\langle\left[\sigma^{a},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-(c-1)}}\right]\right\rangle\rtimes X^{\prime}.

Consider the isomorphism
\phi:G/C\to J_{1,c}\rtimes X^{\prime}
given by:

\displaystyle\phi(\sigma)=\sigma,

\displaystyle\phi(\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}})=\left[\sigma^{-a}\tau,\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right],

with
\phi
acting as identity on
X^{\prime}
. Then we note that
\phi
sends
H/C
to

\left\langle\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-(c-1)}}\right\rangle\rtimes X^{\prime}=\mathrm{Stab}_{J_{1,c}\rtimes X^{\prime}}(1_{C_{pq}})

giving an isomorphism from
(G/C,H/C)
to a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(C_{pq})
. The corresponding parallel extension therefore admits the same Hopf–Galois structures as an extension with Galois closure isomorphic to
J_{1,c}\rtimes X^{\prime}
.

Suppose now that
\tau\in Z(G)
. We obtain no restrictions on
b
. In this case, conjugating by any element does not change the power of
\tau
, and thus
H_{1}
represents
q
conjugacy classes, each of size
p
. By a similar argument as above,
H_{2}
again represents a single conjugacy class (of size
p
if
c>1
and of size
1
if
c=1
) which is distinct from any class represented by
H_{1}
.

The core of any conjugacy class represented by
H_{1}
is trivial. For some
X^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{Aut}(N)
of coprime order to
pq
, we therefore have:

G/C=G/\{1\}\cong G=\left\langle\sigma,\tau,\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right\rangle\rtimes X^{\prime}

and

H/C=H/\{1\}\cong H=\left\langle\left[\tau^{b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]\right\rangle

for some
0\leq b\leq q-1
. Consider now the isomorphism
\phi:G\to G
given by

\phi\left(\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right)=\left[\tau^{-b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]

and acting as identity on
N
and
X^{\prime}
. Then we note that
\phi
sends
H
to

G^{\prime}=\left\langle\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right\rangle\rtimes X^{\prime},

giving an isomorphism from
(G/\{1\},H/\{1\})
to a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(C_{pq})
. The corresponding parallel extension therefore admits a Hopf–Galois structure of cyclic type.

Any index
pq
subgroup containing a group of
H_{2}
again has core
\langle\tau\rangle
. Similar arguments as above show that each parallel extension admits a Hopf–Galois structure of cyclic type.

Finally, for some fixed
t,c
, we consider the case
G=J_{t,c}\rtimes Y
. In this case,
G\cap Q=J_{t,c}
. Let
H
be an index

subgroup of
G
. Then

is of the form

\left\langle\left[\sigma^{a},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-(c-1)}t}\right]\right\rangle,

for some

. We see that any group of this form is conjugate, by some

, to any other, and so all such

are in the same conjugacy class. As

is one such index

subgroup of

and is in the same conjugacy class as any other

, all corresponding parallel extensions must therefore admit a Hopf–Galois structure of cyclic type.
∎

We end this section with the classification of the index

subgroups into both
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
orbits and abstract isomorphism classes.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
be a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
. If
\#\mathrm{Cl}_{pq}(G)>1
, the index
pq
subgroups of
G
form two distinct orbits under
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
. If
q^{3}\mid r
, they form two distinct (abstract) isomorphism classes. If
q^{3}\nmid r
, they form a single (abstract) isomorphism class.

Proof.

Let
H
be an index
pq
subgroup of
G=N\rtimes X
for some
X\leq\mathrm{Aut}(N)
and let
c+1
be the largest power of
q
dividing
r
(as
\#\mathrm{Cl}_{pq}(G)>1
, we must have
q^{2}\mid r
by Proposition
3.4
and therefore
c\geq 1
). By Proposition
2.6
, we need only consider the
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit of
H\cap Q
. We will work with representatives from the conjugacy classes of
H\cap Q
worked out in Proposition
3.4
:

H_{1,b}=\left\langle\left[\tau^{b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]\right\rangle\;\;\text{ and }\;\;H_{2}=\left\langle\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-(c-1)}},\tau\right\rangle,

where
b=0
in the case that
\tau\notin Z(G)
, and
0\leq b\leq p-1
otherwise.

The number of isomorphism classes (as abstract groups) may be worked out as follows:

If
q^{3}\nmid r
, then
c=1
and each representative is cyclic of order
q
. In particular, we have:

(3)

H_{1,b}=\left\langle\left[\tau^{b},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-1}}\right]\right\rangle\cong C_{q}\cong\langle\tau\rangle=H_{2}.

If
q^{3}\mid r
, then
c>1
, and so, whereas
H_{1,b}
is cyclic for each
b
, we have that

H_{2}\cong C_{q}\times C_{q^{c-1}}

is not. In the latter case, we therefore get two (abstract) isomorphism classes for the subgroups
H\cap Q
. Proposition
2.6
tells us that this result may then be lifted to the index
pq
-subgroups
H
of
G
.

We now consider the
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbits of these representatives.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
. Then by an order argument, we must have

\displaystyle\phi(\sigma)=\sigma^{a_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi(\tau)=\left[\sigma^{a_{2}}\tau^{b_{2}},\alpha^{c_{2}q^{e_{0}-1}}\right],

\displaystyle\phi\left(\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right)=\left[\sigma^{a_{3}}\tau^{b_{3}},\alpha^{c_{3}q^{e_{0}-c}}\right]

for some
1\leq a_{1}\leq p-1
,
0\leq a_{2},a_{3}\leq p-1
,
1\leq b_{2}\leq q-1
, and
0\leq b_{3},c_{2},c_{3}\leq q-1
. Using the relations

\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma,\;\alpha(\sigma)=\sigma^{a_{\alpha}},\;\alpha(\tau)=\tau,

we get that
a_{2}=c_{2}=0
and
c_{3}=1
. In particular, we have:

\displaystyle\phi(\sigma)=\sigma^{a_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi(\tau)=\tau^{b_{2}},

\displaystyle\phi\left(\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right)=\left[\sigma^{a_{3}}\tau^{b_{3}},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\right].

Thus the subgroup
\langle\tau\rangle
is characteristic in
G
, and so no
H_{1,b}
can be in the same
\mathrm{Aut}(G)
-orbit as
H_{2}
as
\tau\notin H_{1,b}
.

Now suppose that
\beta\in X
, then

\phi(\beta)=[\sigma^{i}\tau^{j},\beta^{\prime}]

for some
0\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq q-1
and some
\beta^{\prime}\in X
of the same order as
\beta
. Given that
\alpha
and
\beta
commute, and suppose that
\beta^{\prime}(\sigma)=\sigma^{x}
for some
1\leq x\leq p-1
,
\beta^{\prime}(\tau)=\tau^{y}
for some
1\leq y\leq q-1
, we therefore have that

\left[\sigma^{a_{3}+ia_{\alpha}^{q^{e_{0}-c}}}\tau^{j+b_{3}},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\beta^{\prime}\right]=\left[\sigma^{i+xa_{3}}\tau^{j+yb_{3}},\alpha^{q^{e_{0}-c}}\beta^{\prime}\right].

In particular, we have that

tells us that the associated extension also admits a Hopf–Galois structure of non-abelian type.
∎

4.
Parallel extensions admitting no Hopf–Galois structures

We will say that a separable extension
L/K
admits the
parallel no-HGS
property if
L/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure, and has a parallel extension
L^{\prime}/K
admitting no Hopf–Galois structure of any type. Theorem
3.9
tells us that no separable extension of degree
pq
admits the parallel no-HGS property. Thus the question remains as to whether there are indeed examples of extensions admitting the parallel no-HGS property, and if so, how rare such a phenomenon is.

In order to investigate this question for extensions
L/K
of a fixed degree
n
, it is necessary to know a lot about the transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for each abstract group
N
of order
n
. More specifically, if
L/K
has Galois closure
E/K
of degree
mn
, answering the question requires a classification of all transitive subgroups of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
(for each
N
of order
n
) of order divisible by
n
and dividing
mn
.

Our approach to searching for extensions of low degree
n
admitting the parallel no-HGS property has been to write a computer algorithm, see
[
Dar24a
]
, which can be implemented in MAGMA,
[
BCP97
]
. The algorithm first builds a list of all transitive subgroups
G
of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for each group
N
of order
n
. For each
G
, it then computes all subgroups
H
of order
|G|/n
, followed by computing the core
C:=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)
. We then consider the natural homomorphism
\pi:G\to J:=G/C
and view
H/C
as the image
J^{\prime}
of
H
under
\pi
(this makes sure Magma understands that
H/C
is a genuine subgroup of
G/C
). We then ask whether
J
is isomorphic as an abstract group to any transitive subgroup
M
of some
\mathrm{Hol}(N^{\prime})
(for some group
N^{\prime}
of order
n
) in our original list. If such an isomorphism
\phi
exists, we can construct all isomorphisms from
J
to
M
by composing
\phi
with elements of
\mathrm{Aut}(M)
. We finally check whether any of these isomorphisms send
J^{\prime}
to
M^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N^{\prime}})
. If no isomorphism exists, then given a separable extension
L/K
with Galois closure
E
such that
\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\overset{\phi}{\cong}G
with
\phi(\mathrm{Gal}(E/L))=G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(1_{N})
, we have that
L/K
admits the parallel no-HGS property. If such a transitive subgroup
G
is found, then we also say that
G
admits the parallel no-HGS property.

The algorithm described has enabled us to find examples of transitive subgroups
(G,G^{\prime})
of degrees
n=8,12,24
and
27
which admit the parallel no-HGS property.

\mathrm{Hol}(N)
up to conjugacy for each abstract group
N
of order
n
, and the final column gives the number of such subgroups admitting the parallel no-HGS property.

We present a degree 8 example of the occurrence of the parallel no-HGS property in the form of a theorem:

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a separable extension
L/K
of degree
8
admitting the parallel no-HGS property.

Further, all parallel extensions to any extension
F/K
of degree
8
admitting the parallel no-HGS property have the same Galois closure as
F/K
.

Proof.

We give an example of a transitive subgroup
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(C_{2}\times C_{4})
with the parallel no-HGS property.

Consider the following permutation representation of
\mathrm{Hol}(C_{2}\times C_{4})
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hol}(C_{2}\times C_{4})=\langle

\displaystyle(1,5,2,6)(3,7,4,8),(1,3)(2,4)(5,7)(6,8),

\displaystyle(1,2)(3,4)(5,6)(7,8),(5,7)(6,8),(3,4)(7,8)

\displaystyle(5,6)(7,8)\rangle.

One may identify
C_{2}\times C_{4}
inside
\mathrm{Hol}(C_{2}\times C_{4})
with the subgroup

\displaystyle C_{2}\times C_{4}\cong\langle

\displaystyle(1,5,2,6)(3,7,4,8),

\displaystyle(1,3)(2,4)(5,7)(6,8)\rangle.

Then the following group
G
of order
32
, given by

\displaystyle G:=\langle

\displaystyle(1,5,4,8)(2,6,3,7),(1,5,2,6)(3,8,4,7),

\displaystyle(1,3)(2,4)(5,7)(6,8),(1,2)(3,4)(5,6)(7,8),(5,6)(7,8)\rangle

\displaystyle\cong(C_{2}\times C_{2})\cdot D_{4}\text{ (non-split extension)}

is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(C_{2}\times C_{4})
, with
G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(1)
of order
4
given by

G^{\prime}=\langle(5,6)(7,8),(3,4)(5,7,6,8)\rangle.

The group

H=\langle(5,6)(7,8),(1,3)(2,4)(5,8)(6,7)\rangle\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}

is an index
8
subgroup of
G
, with trivial core.

Then it can be exhaustively checked by computer that, for any group
N
of order
8
and any transitive subgroup
M\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
, there is no isomorphism
\phi:G\to M
such that
\phi(H)=\mathrm{Stab}_{M}(1_{N})
.

Therefore, a separable extension
L/K
with Galois closure
E
such that

\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\overset{\psi}{\cong}G
with
\psi(\mathrm{Gal}(E/L))=G^{\prime}
admits the parallel no-HGS property. In particular,
L/K
admits a Hopf–Galois structure of type
C_{2}\times C_{4}
, but has a parallel extension which admits no Hopf–Galois structure of any type.

Every such degree 8 example given by the algorithm gives an index
8
subgroup with trivial core, hence all parallel extensions of interest have the same Galois closure as the original extension.
∎

We remark that we have not yet found an example of the phenomenon for separable extensions of squarefree degree. This observation, along with the discussion in Section
3
inspires the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.2
.

There is no separable extension of squarefree degree admitting the parallel no-HGS property.

Despite the rare occurrence of the phenomenon, given an extension admitting the parallel no-HGS property, we now give a way of constructing two infinite families of permutation groups admitting the property. At present, we can only do so for examples of odd degree, but it should be possible to extend this to examples of any degree. We start with the following lemmas:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N
be a group and
M
a characteristic subgroup of
N
. Then there is a group homomorphism
\phi:\mathrm{Hol}(N)\to\mathrm{Hol}(N/M)
given by

\phi(\eta,\alpha)=(\overline{\eta},\overline{\alpha})

where
\overline{\eta}:=\eta M
and
\overline{\alpha}(\overline{\eta}):=\alpha(\eta)M
.

We note that if
G\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N)
is transitive on
N
, then
\phi(G)
is transitive on
N/M
.

Proof.

We show that
\overline{\alpha}
is well-defined. Take
\eta\in N,\mu\in M
, then

\alpha(\eta\mu)M=\alpha(\eta)\alpha(\mu)M=\alpha(\eta)M.

The second equality is due to the fact
M
is characteristic in
N
, and so
\alpha(\mu)\in M
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For any integer
n
, there are infinitely many primes
q
such that
\gcd(q-1,n)\leq 2
.

Proof.

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the system of congruences

(4)

x-1\equiv 2\bmod{4},\;\;x-1\equiv 1\bmod{p},\;\;\text{ for all odd primes }p\mid n

has infinitely many solutions. Define
\mathrm{rad}_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)
to be the product of all distinct odd primes dividing
n
. Then given a solution
x
of (
4
), for any integer
k
, we have that

x^{\prime}=x+4k\mathrm{rad}_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)

is also a solution of (
4
). Next, given that
x
and
4\mathrm{rad}_{\mathrm{odd}}(n)
are coprime, Dirichlet’s Theorem tells us that there are infinitely many primes
q
of the form

q=x+4k\mathrm{rad}_{\mathrm{odd}}(n).

If
n
is odd, then
q-1\equiv x-1\equiv 1\bmod{p}
for all primes
p\mid n
, and so
\gcd(q-1,n)=1
. If
n
is even, then
q-1
is coprime to the largest odd factor of
n
, and so
q-1\equiv x-1\equiv 2\bmod{4}
forces
\gcd(q-1,n)=2
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
G
be a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for some group
N
of order
n
with
n
odd such that
G
admits the parallel no-HGS property. Then there are infinitely many primes
q
such that
M\cong G\times C_{q}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N\times C_{q})
is a transitive subgroup also admitting the parallel no-HGS property.

As demonstrated by Table
5
, we have found examples of transitive subgroups of degree 27 admitting the parallel no-HGS property, and so it is reasonable to ask that
n
is an odd integer.

Proof.

As
G
admits the parallel no-HGS property, there is an index
n
subgroup
H
of
G
with
C:=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)
such that
(G/C,H/C)
is not isomorphic as a permutation group to any transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N^{\prime})
for any group
N^{\prime}
of order
n
.

By Lemma
4.4
, there are infinitely many primes
q
such that

(i)

\gcd(q-1,n)=1
,

(ii)

q>n
,

(iii)

q>|\mathrm{Aut}(Y)|
for every group
Y
of order
n
.

In particular, if we ask that
q>n!
, we get both (ii) and (iii). So, for the rest of this proof, we let
q>n!
be a prime satisfying
\gcd(q-1,n)=1
.

We note that
G\times C_{q}
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N\times C_{q})
with
\mathrm{Stab}_{G\times C_{q}}(1_{N})=G^{\prime}\times\{1\}
, where
G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(1_{N})
. One index
qn
subgroup of
G\times C_{q}
is
H\times\{1\}
, which has
\mathrm{Core}_{G\times C_{q}}(H\times\{1\})=C\times\{1\}
. We then have
(G\times C_{q})/(C\times\{1\})\cong G/C\times C_{q}
and
(H\times\{1\})/(C\times\{1\})\cong H/C\times\{1\}
. We will show that
(G/C\times C_{q},H/C\times\{1\})
is not isomorphic as a permutation group to any transitive subgroup
I\leq\mathrm{Hol}(J)
for any group
J
of order
qn
. We will aim to get contradiction, assuming that we have such a situation.

By (ii),
J
has a unique Sylow
q
-subgroup, which is therefore characteristic. Then by the Schur-Zassenhaus theorem,
J
must be a split extension of
C_{q}
. In particular, we have that
J=C_{q}\rtimes Y
for some group
Y
of order
n
. Finally, by (i), the action of
Y
on
C_{q}
is trivial, and hence we can write
J=C_{q}\times Y
. We note that
Y
is also characteristic in
J
, and hence
\mathrm{Hol}(J)=\mathrm{Hol}(C_{q})\times\mathrm{Hol}(Y)
.

Now, suppose we have a transitive subgroup
I\leq\mathrm{Hol}(J)
with
(I,I^{\prime})
isomorphic to
(G/C\times C_{q},H/C\times\{1\})
, where
I^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Stab}_{I}(1_{J})
. By Lemma
4.3
, we have a homomorphism
\phi:\mathrm{Hol}(J)\to\mathrm{Hol}(Y)
, and that
\phi(I)
is a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(Y)
. By (ii) and (iii), the factor
C_{q}
must have trivial image in
\phi
, and so
G/C
must act transitively (but a priori not necessarily faithfully) on
Y
with stabiliser
H/C
. The kernel of this action is then the core of
H/C
in
G/C
, which is trivial since
C=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(H)
. Therefore
(G/C,H/C)
embeds as a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(Y)
, giving a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
G
be a transitive subgroup of
\mathrm{Hol}(N)
for some group
N
of odd order
n
such that
G
admits the parallel no-HGS property. Then for any positive integer
k
, there are infinitely many sequences
p_{1},\cdots,p_{k}
of length
k
of primes coprime to
n
such that
M\cong G\times C_{m}\leq\mathrm{Hol}(N\times C_{m})
is a transitive subgroup also admitting the parallel no-HGS property, where
m=p_{1}\cdots p_{k}
.

Proof.

This follows from repeated application of Proposition
4.5
.
∎

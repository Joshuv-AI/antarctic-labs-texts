Introduction

In
[
1
]
it is shown that recurrent neural networks (RNNs) can learn — in a metric entropy optimal manner — discrete time, linear time-invariant (LTI) systems. This is effected by comparing the number of bits needed to encode the approximating RNN to the metric entropy of the class of LTI systems under consideration
[

,

]

[

,
3
]
, in the process of which minor mathematical issues appearing in
[
2
,
3
]
are cleaned up. These corrections also lead to the correction of a constant in a result in
[
1
]
(see Remark
0.2
).

0.2
Setting and main statement

The input-output relation of the LTI system
\mathcal{L}
is given by the
convolution of the input signal
x[\cdot]
with the system’s impulse response
k[\cdot]
according to

(\mathcal{L}x)[t]=\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}k[\tau]x[t-\tau]=:(k*x)[t],\qquad t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1)

where we assume that
x[t]=0,\,k[t]=0
, both for
t<0
, that is we consider one-sided input signals and causal systems. We occasionally use the notation
k_{\mathcal{L}}
to designate the impulse response associated with the system
\mathcal{L}
.
We shall frequently make use of the one-sided
\mathcal{Z}
-transform for
\ell_{\infty}
-signals defined as
1
1

1

Note the positive exponents of
z
in the definition of the
\mathcal{Z}
-transform. This convention is chosen to maintain consistency with Definition
0.2
below adopted from
[
3
]
.

X(z):=(\mathcal{Z}\!\left\{x\right\})(z)=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}x[t]z^{t},\quad|z|<1.

(2)

Note that thanks to
x\in\ell_{\infty}
the series (
2
) converges absolutely for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<1
.
Further, we will need the following norms.

{definition}
[
[
4
, Chapter 17]
]
With
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mathcal{Z}\!\left\{x\right\}\mid x\in\ell_{\infty},\,x[t]=0,\text{ for }t<0\}
,
define for

X\in\mathcal{K}
the Hardy norms

\displaystyle\lVert X\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|X(re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta,}

(3)

\displaystyle\lVert X\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{|z|<1}|X(z)|.

(4)

The corresponding Hardy spaces are given by
{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=\{X(\cdot)\,|\,X\in\mathcal{K},\;\lVert X\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}<\infty\}
and
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}=\{X(\cdot)\,|\,X\in\mathcal{K},\;\lVert X\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}<\infty\}
.

Next, we note the well-known relation

\left(\mathcal{Z}\!\left\{\mathcal{L}x\right\}\right)(z)=(\mathcal{Z}\!\left\{k*x\right\})(z)=K(z)\cdot X(z),

(5)

where

K(z):=\left(\mathcal{Z}\!\left\{k\right\}\right)(z)

is commonly referred to as the system’s transfer function. We now define the distance between LTI systems as follows.

{definition}

For LTI systems
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
with transfer functions
K(z)
and
K^{\prime}(z)
, respectively, both in
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
, we define the metric

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime}):=\lVert K-K^{\prime}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}.

{remark}

By Theorem
.5
,
\rho(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
can be expressed in the following forms

\displaystyle\rho(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\;\lVert K-K^{\prime}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{X\,\in\,{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}\frac{\lVert(K-K^{\prime})X\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}}{\lVert X\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{2}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1}\lVert(k-k^{\prime})*x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1}\lVert\mathcal{L}x-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}.

We are now ready to state the main result.

{theorem}

Let
{a}{},{b}{}>0
and consider the set

\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})=\{\mathcal{L}\mid k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]\in\mathbb{R},\,|k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]|\leq{a}{}e^{-{b}{}t},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]=0,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}\}{}.

(6)

The metric entropy of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
with respect to the metric

\rho(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\lVert K-K^{\prime}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}=\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1}\lVert(k-k^{\prime})*x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

satisfies

\mathcal{E}(\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)\thicksim\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2},

(7)

where
\gamma:=\log_{2}(e)
and
f(\epsilon)\thicksim g(\epsilon)
stands for
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\left|\frac{f(\epsilon)}{g(\epsilon)}\right|=1
.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
.
According to Lemma
0.3
below, there exists a
(2\epsilon)
-packing of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
with
M_{2\epsilon}
elements, where

\log_{2}\!\left(M_{2\epsilon}\right)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right).

Further, by Lemma
0.3
below, there exists an
\epsilon
-covering of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
with
N_{\epsilon}
elements, where

\log_{2}\!\left(N_{\epsilon}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right).

Using Lemma
.4
, we can hence sandwich the metric entropy according to

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log_{2}\!\left(M_{2\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log_{2}\!\left(M(2\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log_{2}\!\left(N(\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)\right)

\displaystyle\left(\right.

\displaystyle=\left.\mathcal{E}{}(\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)\;\right)

\displaystyle\leq\log_{2}\!\left(N_{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right).

Dividing by
\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}
and taking
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}
, then yields

1\leq\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal{E}{}(\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)}{\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}}\leq 1,

which establishes (
7
).
∎

{remark}

It follows from Theorem
0.2
that the constant
\frac{1}{b}
in the scaling result

\mathcal{E}(\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)\thicksim\frac{1}{{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}

specified in
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
is incorrect and should be replaced by
\frac{\gamma}{2b}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{E}(\epsilon;\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{}),\rho)\thicksim\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}
.
Similarly, the number of required bits specified as

\frac{1}{{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right)

in
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
should be replaced by

\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right)
.

0.3
Covering and packing bounds

{lemma}

Let
{a}{},{b}{}>0
and consider the set

\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})=\{\mathcal{L}\mid k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]\in\mathbb{R},\,|k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]|\leq{a}{}e^{-{b}{}t},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]=0,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}\}{}

equipped with the metric

\rho(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\lVert K-K^{\prime}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,a)
, there exists a
(2\epsilon)
-packing of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})

with
M_{2\epsilon}
elements, where

\log_{2}\!\left(M_{2\epsilon}\right)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right),

with
\gamma:=\log_{2}(e)
.

Proof.

We explicitly construct a
(2\epsilon)
-packing as visualized in Figure
1
.

Figure 1:
The packing is constructed by quantizing the impulse response at each time instant
t
with quantization interval size of at least
2\epsilon
.

Define
C_{1}:=\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil-1
. Now, for all
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{1}\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}<{\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle b\,t

\displaystyle<\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle-b\,t

\displaystyle>\log\left(\frac{\epsilon}{a}\right)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle e^{-bt}

\displaystyle>\frac{\epsilon}{a}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\frac{ae^{-bt}}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle>1.

Next, for
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{1}\}
, we set
n_{t}:=\left\lceil\frac{2ae^{-bt}}{2\epsilon}\right\rceil-1\in\left[1,\frac{2ae^{-bt}}{2\epsilon}\right)
and
\delta_{t}:=\frac{2ae^{-bt}}{n_{t}}>2\epsilon
.
Now, we define the set

\mathcal{P}:=\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{{i}_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}\mid i_{\ell}\in\{0,\dots,n_{\ell}\},\text{ for }\ell\in\{0,\dots,C_{1}\}\right\},

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}
is the LTI system associated with the impulse response

\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}[t]:=\begin{cases}-ae^{-bt}+i_{t}\delta_{t},&0\leq t\leq C_{1}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases},

and we show that
\mathcal{P}
constitutes a
(2\epsilon)
-packing of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
.
First, we establish that
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
by verifying that
\left|\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}[t]\right|\leq ae^{-bt},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, holds for all
\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}\in\mathcal{P}
.
Indeed, for
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{1}\}
, we have

-ae^{-bt}\leq\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}[t]=-ae^{-bt}+i_{t}\delta_{t}\leq-ae^{-bt}+n_{t}\delta_{t}=-ae^{-bt}+2ae^{-bt}=ae^{-bt},

and for
t>C_{1}
,

\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}[t]=0.

Next, we show that for distinct
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}},\,\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}}\in\mathcal{P}
, i.e., there is at least one
\ell\in\{0,\dots,C_{1}\}
such that
i_{\ell}\neq j_{\ell}
, it holds that
\rho(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}},\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}})>2\epsilon
.
Indeed, for any such
\ell
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}},\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\lVert\left(\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}-\widetilde{k}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}}\right)*x\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

(8)

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}-\widetilde{k}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\left(\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}[t]-\widetilde{k}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}}[t]\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\left(\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{1}}}[\ell]-\widetilde{k}_{j_{0},\dots,j_{C_{1}}}[\ell]\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=|-ae^{-b\ell}+i_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}+ae^{-b\ell}-j_{\ell}\delta_{\ell}|

\displaystyle=|i_{\ell}-j_{\ell}|\delta_{\ell}

\displaystyle>2\epsilon,

(10)

where in (
8
) we used Theorem
.5
, in (
9
) we inserted the particular choice
x[t]=\mathbbm{1}_{\{t=0\}}[t]
to lower-bound the
\sup
, and in (
10
) we used
\delta_{\ell}>2\epsilon
.
This establishes that
\mathcal{P}
constitutes a
(2\epsilon)
-packing of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
with respect to the metric
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
specified in Definition
0.2
. It remains to bound the cardinality of
\mathcal{P}
, which we denote by
M_{2\epsilon}
. Specifically, we have

\displaystyle\log_{2}\!\left(M_{2\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\log_{2}\prod_{t=0}^{C_{1}{}}(1+n_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{C_{1}}\log_{2}\left\lceil\frac{a}{\epsilon}\,e^{-bt}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=0}^{C_{1}}\log_{2}\left({\frac{a}{\epsilon}\,e^{-bt}}\right)

\displaystyle=(C_{1}+1)\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)+\sum_{t=0}^{C_{1}}\log_{2}\left(e^{-bt}\right)

\displaystyle=(C_{1}+1)\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-b\;\log_{2}(e)\sum_{t=0}^{C_{1}}t\,

\displaystyle=(C_{1}+1)\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-b\,\gamma\,\frac{C_{1}{}(C_{1}{}+1)}{2}\,

(11)

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-b\,\gamma\,\frac{(\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil-1)\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil}{2}\,

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil^{2}+\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}\left\lceil\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right\rceil

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)+1\right)^{2}+\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\log_{2}\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{b\,\gamma}{2b^{2}}\left(\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-\gamma\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{b}\left(\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2b}\left(\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}

(12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2b}\left(\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)-\frac{b\,\gamma}{2}

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2b}\left(\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}-o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{a}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right),

where
in (
12
) we used

\log_{2}(x)=\gamma\log(x)
.
∎

Before providing a covering for
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
, we need the following auxiliary result.

{lemma}

Consider the LTI systems with impulse responses
k[\cdot]
and
\widetilde{k}[\cdot]
and corresponding transfer functions
K(z)
and
\widetilde{K}(z)
, both in
{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}
. We have

\lVert K-\widetilde{K}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}\leq\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}|k[t]-\widetilde{k}[t]|.

Proof.

The proof is by the following chain of relations

\displaystyle\lVert K-\widetilde{K}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{|z|<1}\left|\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}k[t]z^{t}-\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{k}[t]z^{t}\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{|z|<1}\left|\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(k[t]-\widetilde{k}[t])z^{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|z|<1}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}|k[t]-\widetilde{k}[t]||z|^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}|k[t]-\widetilde{k}[t]|.

We are now ready to provide an upper bound on the covering number of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
.

{lemma}

Let
{a}{},{b}{}>0
and consider the set

\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})=\{\mathcal{L}\mid k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]\in\mathbb{R},\,|k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]|\leq{a}{}e^{-{b}{}t},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ and }k_{\mathcal{L}}[t]=0,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{N}_{0}\}{},

equipped with the metric

\rho(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\lVert K-K^{\prime}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}.

For every
\epsilon\in(0,a)
, there exists an
\epsilon
-covering of
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})

with
N_{\epsilon}
elements, where

\log_{2}\left(N_{\epsilon}\right)\leq\frac{\gamma}{2{b}{}}\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}+o\left(\left(\log\left(\frac{{a}{}}{\epsilon}\right)\right)^{2}\right),

(14)

with
\gamma:=\log_{2}(e)
.

Proof.

The proof is effected by explicit construction of an
\epsilon
-covering as visualized in Figure
2
.

Figure 2:

The covering is constructed by quantizing the impulse response at each time instant
t
with quantization interval size
\delta
.

We start by defining

C_{2}:=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{{b}{}}\log\left(\frac{2a}{\epsilon\left(1-e^{-{b}}\right)}\right)\right\rfloor,\quad\delta:=\frac{\epsilon}{C_{2}+1},

(15)

and

n_{t}:=\left\lceil\frac{2ae^{-bt}}{\delta}\right\rceil,\qquad\text{for }t\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\}.

As indicated in Figure
2
, we quantize the impulse response at each time instant
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\}

with quantization interval size
\delta
using
n_{t}
points.
To formalize this, we start by defining, for
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\},
the mappings

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{t}:\;\{1,\dots,n_{t}\}&\rightarrow[-ae^{-bt},ae^{-bt}]\\
f_{t}(i)&:=\min\left\{-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+i\delta,\,ae^{-bt}\right\}\,,\end{split}

(16)

and show that, for all
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\}
, the following properties hold:

(P1)

f_{t}(1)\leq-ae^{-bt}+\frac{\delta}{2}
,

(P2)

f_{t}(n_{t})\geq ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}
,

(P3)

f_{t}(i+1)-f_{t}(i)\leq\delta
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{t}-1\}
.

First, (
P1
) follows by

f_{t}(1)=\min\left\{-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+\delta,\,ae^{-bt}\right\}\leq-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+\delta=-ae^{-bt}+\frac{\delta}{2}.

To establish (
P2
) we note that either

f_{t}(n_{t})=ae^{-bt}\qquad\text{ or }\qquad f_{t}(n_{t})=-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+n_{t}\delta.

In the former case we have
f_{t}(n_{t})=ae^{-bt}\geq ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}
, and in the latter, we obtain

f_{t}(n_{t})=-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+n_{t}\delta\geq-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+2ae^{-bt}=ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}.

Finally, to prove (
P3
), we observe that, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{t}-1\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+i\delta

\displaystyle\leq-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+(n_{t}-1)\delta

(17)

\displaystyle\leq-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+\left(\frac{2ae^{-bt}}{\delta}\right)\delta

(18)

\displaystyle=ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}\leq ae^{-bt}.

(19)

Thus,
f_{t}(i)=-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+i\delta
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{t}-1\}
, and therefore

\displaystyle f_{t}(i+1)-f_{t}(i)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+(i+1)\delta,\,ae^{-bt}\right\}-\left(-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+i\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+(i+1)\delta-\left(-ae^{-bt}-\frac{\delta}{2}+i\delta\right)=\delta,\qquad\text{ for }i\in\{1,\dots,n_{t}-1\},

which establishes (
P3
).

Together, (
P1
)-(
P3
) imply that, for every
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\}
and
x\in[-ae^{-bt},ae^{-bt}]
, there is an
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{t}\}
such that
|f_{t}(i)-x|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
.
Now, we define the set

\mathcal{M}{}:=\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{{i}_{0},\dots,i_{C_{2}}}\mid i_{\ell}\in\{1,\dots,n_{\ell}\},\text{ for }\ell\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\}\right\},

(20)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{2}}}
is the LTI system associated with the impulse response

\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{2}}}[t]:=\begin{cases}f_{t}(i_{t}),&0\leq t\leq C_{2}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\\
\end{cases},

(21)

and we show that
\mathcal{M}{}
is, indeed, an
\epsilon
-covering for
\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
. Fix
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{C}({a}{},{b}{})
with corresponding impulse response
k[\cdot]
. As just established, for each
t\in\{0,\dots,C_{2}\}
, there is an
i_{t}\in\{1,\dots,n_{t}\}
such that
|k[t]-f_{t}(i_{t})|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
. Hence, the corresponding
\widetilde{k}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{2}}}\in\mathcal{M}{}
satisfies

\displaystyle\lVert K-\widetilde{K}_{i_{0},\dots,i_{C_{2}}}\rVert_{{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}}}

{definition}

[

]
Let
(\mathcal{X},\rho)
be a metric space. An
\epsilon
-covering of a compact set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with respect to the metric
\rho
is a set of points
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}\subset\mathcal{C}
such that for each
x\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists an
i\in[1,N]
so that
\rho(x,x_{i})\leq\epsilon
. The
\epsilon
-covering number
N(\epsilon;\mathcal{C},\rho)
is the cardinality of a smallest
\epsilon
-covering of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{E}(\epsilon;\mathcal{C},\rho):=\log_{2}(N(\epsilon;\mathcal{C},\rho))
is the metric entropy of
\mathcal{C}
.

{definition}

Let
({\cal X},\rho)
be a metric space. An
\epsilon
-packing of a compact set
{\cal C}\subset{\cal X}
with respect to the metric
\rho
is a set
\{x_{1},...,x_{N}\}\subset\cal C
such that

\rho(x_{i},x_{j})>\epsilon
, for all distinct
i,j
. The
\epsilon
-packing number
M(\epsilon;{\cal X},\rho)
is the cardinality of the largest
\epsilon
-packing.

{lemma}

Let
({\cal X},\rho)
be a metric space and
{\cal C}
a compact set in
{\cal X}
. For all
\epsilon>0
,
it holds that

M(2\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)\leq N(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)\leq M(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho).

Proof.

First, choose a minimal
\epsilon
-covering and a maximal
2\epsilon
-packing of
{\cal C}
. Since no two centers of the
2\epsilon
-packing can lie in the same ball of the

\epsilon
-covering, it follows that
M(2\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)\leq N(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)
. To establish
N(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)\leq M(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)
, we note that, given a maximum

\epsilon
-packing with cardinality
M(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)
, for every
x\in{\cal C}
, we have the center of at least one of the balls in the packing within distance less than
\epsilon
. If this were not the case, we could
add another ball to the packing thereby violating its maximality. This maximal packing hence also provides an
\epsilon
-covering
and hence

N(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)\leq M(\epsilon;{\cal C},\rho)
.
∎

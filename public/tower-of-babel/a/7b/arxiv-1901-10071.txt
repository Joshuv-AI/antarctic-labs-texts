1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following 2d Boussinesq equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla p=\theta e_{2},\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\rm T}^{2}\times[0,1]\\
\hbox{div}v=0,\\
\partial_{t}\theta+v\cdot\nabla\theta-\triangle\theta=0,\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\rm T}^{2}\times[0,1],\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
{\rm T}^{2}
denotes the 2-dimensional torus, and
e_{2}=\binom{0}{1}
. Here in our notations,
v
is the velocity vector,
p
is the pressure, and
\theta
denotes the temperature or density which is a scalar function.
The Boussinesq equation was introduced to model many geophysical flows, such as atmospheric fronts and ocean circulations (see, for example,
[
36
]
,
[
43
]
).

The global well-posedness of strong solution has been established by many authors for the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) in 2d with regularity data
(
see, for example,
[
9
]
,
[
25
]
). For the 3-dimensional case, the global existence of smooth solution of (
1.1
) remains open.

Moreover, the study of weak solutions in fluid dynamics, including those which fail to conserve energy, is quite natural in the context of turbulent flow, and has been conducted by many people in the past two decades (see
[
44
]
,
[
45
,
46
]
,
[
18
,
19
]
). The triplet
(v,p,\theta)
on
[0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}
is called a weak solution of (
1.1
) if
\theta\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2}),~p\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2}),~\theta\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,1);H^{1}({\rm T}^{2}))
and solve (
1.1
) in the following sense:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}(\partial_{t}\varphi\cdot v+\nabla\varphi:v\otimes v+p{\rm div}\varphi+\theta e_{2}\cdot\varphi)dxdt=0

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2};R^{2})
;

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}(\partial_{t}\phi\theta+v\cdot\nabla\phi\theta-\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\phi)dxdt=0

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2};R)
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}v\cdot\nabla\psi dxdt=0

for all
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1)\times{\rm T}^{2};R).

The study of constructing non-unique or dissipative weak solution to fluid systems is very fashionable in recent years. The construction is based on the convex integration method pioneered by De Lellis-Székelyhidi Jr in
[
18
,
21
]
, where the authors tackle the Onsager conjecture for the incompressible Euler equation, showing that the incompressible Euler equation admits Hölder continuous solutions which dissipate kinetic energy. More precisely, the Onsager conjecture on incompressible Euler equation can be stated as follows:

(1)

C^{0,\alpha}
weak solutions are energy conservative when
\alpha>\frac{1}{3}
.

(2)

For any
\alpha<\frac{1}{3}
, there exist dissipative solutions with
C^{0,\alpha}
regularity .

For this conjecture, the part (a) has been proved by P. Constantin, E, Weinan and E. Titi in
[
15
]
. Slightly weaker assumptions on the solution were subsequently shown to be sufficient for energy conservation by P. Constantin, etc. in
[
10
,
24
]
, also see
[
48
]
. More recently, P. Isett and Sung-jin Oh gave a proof to this part of Onsager’s conjecture for the Euler equations on manifolds by the heat flow method in
[
26
]
.

Part (b) was proved by P. Isett in
[
29
]
, based on a series of progress on this problem in
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
22
,
27
]
, see also
[
6
]
for the construction of admissible weak solutions. Moreover, the idea and method can be used to construct dissipative weak solutions for other models, see
[
7
,
31
,
34
,
41
,
47
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
.

Recently, Buckmaster and Vicol established the non-uniqueness of weak solution to the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes in
[
8
]
by introducing some new ideas. Furthermore, the method was used to construct weak solution in
[
2
,
32
,
33
,
35
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
.

Motivated by the study of Onsager’s conjecture of the incompressible Euler equation in earlier works and non-uniqueness of weak solutions to the Navier-Stokes equation, we consider the Boussinesq equation with diffusive temperature and want to know if the anomalous dissipation of kinetic energy can also happened when considering the temperature effects. The difference is that there are conversions between internal energy and mechanical energy, and we need to overcome the difficulty of interactions between velocity and temperature. To this end, we consider the existence of Hölder continuous periodic solutions of the 2d Boussinesq equations with diffusive temperature which satisfy the prescribed kinetic energy. Following the general scheme in the construction of Hölder continuous weak solution of the incompressible Euler equation and introducing some new ideas, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
e(t):[0,1]\rightarrow R
is a given positive smooth function and let
\theta^{0}(x)\in X:={\rm span}\{\sin(x_{2}),\cos(x_{2})\}
. Then there exist

\displaystyle(v,p)\in C([0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2}),\quad\theta\in C([0,1]\times{\rm T}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,1;H^{1}({\rm T}^{2}))\quad

such that they
solve the system (
1.1
) in the sense of distribution and

\displaystyle e(t)=\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}|v|^{2}(t,x)dx,\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Furthermore, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{10})
, there holds

\displaystyle v\in C^{\frac{1}{10}-\varepsilon}_{t,x},\quad p\in C^{\frac{1}{5}-\varepsilon}_{t,x},\quad\theta\in C^{1,\frac{1}{20}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}_{t}C^{2,\frac{1}{10}-\varepsilon}_{x}.

Moreover,
\theta
satisfies the following identity:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}ds=\frac{1}{2}\|\theta^{0}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Remark 1.1
.

We consider the following 2d Boussinesq-equation with fractional dissipation in velocity:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla p+(-\triangle)^{\alpha}v=\theta e_{2},\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\rm T}^{2}\times[0,1]\\
{\rm div}v=0,\\
\partial_{t}\theta+v\cdot\nabla\theta-\triangle\theta=0,\quad\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\rm T}^{2}\times[0,1].\end{cases}

(1.2)

For
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, we also can construct Hölder continuous weak solutions for (
1.2
) by the argument in this paper. For
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1
, we have constructed finite energy weak solutions for (
1.2
) in
[
33
]
.

Here and below, we denote

\displaystyle\binom{a}{b}\otimes\binom{c}{d}=\binom{a}{b}(c\quad d)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}ac&ad\\
bc&bd\end{array}\right).

Proof.

A computation gives

\displaystyle{\rm div}\big(\vec{f}_{1}(x)\otimes\vec{f}_{2}(x)+\vec{f}_{2}(x)\otimes\vec{f}_{1}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm div}\left(\binom{-b}{a}\otimes\binom{-d}{c}e^{i(a+c,b+d)\cdot x}+\binom{-d}{c}\otimes\binom{-b}{a}e^{i(a+c,b+d)\cdot x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm div}\Big[\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2bd&-bc-ad\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}2bd&-bc-ad\\
-ad-bc&2ac\end{array}\right)\binom{a+c}{b+d}e^{i(a+c,b+d)\cdot x}

\displaystyle i\binom{bd(a+c)-b^{2}c-ad^{2}}{ac(b+d)-ad^{2}-bc^{2}}e^{i(a+c,b+d)\cdot x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\binom{bd(a+c)+ac(a+c)-ac^{2}-ca^{2}-b^{2}c-ad^{2}}{ac(b+d)+bd(b+d)-bd^{2}-db^{2}-ad^{2}-bc^{2}}e^{i(a+c,b+d)\cdot x}

3.3.
Anti-divergence operator

We recall the anti-divergence operator in this subsection.

Lemma 3.5
.

(Anti-divergence operator)
There exists an operator
\mathcal{R}
satisfying the following property:

•

For any
v\in C^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2};R^{2})
,
\mathcal{R}v(x)
is a symmetric trace-free matrix for each
x\in{\rm T}^{2}
and

{\rm div}\mathcal{R}v(x)=v(x)-\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}v(x)dx.

•

The following estimates hold: for any
a\in C^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})
and any
m\in{\rm N}_{+},~\alpha\in(0,1)
, there holds

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{R}\big(a(x)e^{i\lambda k\cdot x}\big)\big\|_{\alpha}\leq C(m,\alpha)\Big(\frac{\|a\|_{0}}{\lambda^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{\|\nabla^{m}a\|_{0}}{\lambda^{m-\alpha}}+\frac{\|\nabla^{m}a\|_{\alpha}}{\lambda^{m}}\Big).

(3.8)

Proof.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})
be a solution to

\displaystyle\triangle u=v-\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}v(x)dx,

where
C_{0}^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})=\{f\in C^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2}):\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}f(x)dx=0\}.

Then set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}v(x):=\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{{\rm T}}-({\rm div}u){\rm Id}.

Then
\mathcal{R}
satisfies the above property. The detail can be found in
[
11
]
, and we omit it here.

MM

\square

Moreover,
\mathcal{R}
satisfies the following property:

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
s>0
, there holds

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}(v)\|_{0}\leq C\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

(3.9)

Here and below,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}|v_{k}|^{2}|k|^{2s}.

Proof.

In fact, we can give a explicit formula for
\mathcal{R}
by the Fourier series expansion. Let

\displaystyle v(x)=\sum_{k\in Z^{2}}v_{k}e^{ik\cdot x},\quad x\in{\rm T}^{2}

where
v_{k}\in{\rm C}^{2}
is the Fourier coefficient of the vector function
v(x)
. Then

\displaystyle v(x)-\fint_{{\rm T}^{2}}v(x)dx=\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}v_{k}e^{ik\cdot x},\quad x\in{\rm T}^{2}.

Thus, due to
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\rm T}^{2})
and
\triangle u=v
, we deduce

\displaystyle u(x)=\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{-v_{k}}{|k|^{2}}e^{ik\cdot x},\quad x\in{\rm T}^{2}.

Thus, there hold

\displaystyle\nabla u(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{-iv_{k}\otimes k}{|k|^{2}}e^{ik\cdot x},

\displaystyle(\nabla u)^{\rm T}(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{-ik\otimes v_{k}}{|k|^{2}}e^{ik\cdot x},

\displaystyle{\rm div}u(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{-iv_{k}\cdot k}{|k|^{2}}e^{ik\cdot x}.

Summing them together, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(v)(x)=\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\Big(\frac{-iv_{k}\otimes k}{|k|^{2}}+\frac{-ik\otimes v_{k}}{|k|^{2}}+\frac{iv_{k}\cdot k}{|k|^{2}}{\rm Id}\Big)e^{ik\cdot x},

hence for any
s>0
, there hold

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}(v)\|_{0}\leq C\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{|v_{k}|}{|k|}\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}|v_{k}|^{2}|k|^{2s}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{1}{|k|^{2(1+s)}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

where we used

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{1}{|k|^{2(1+s)}}

is convergent for any
s>0
. In fact,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Z^{2},k\neq 0}\frac{1}{|k|^{2(1+s)}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=n,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0}\frac{1}{(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})^{1+s}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2(1+s)}}+\frac{1}{(1+(n-1)^{2})^{(1+s)}}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+\frac{1}{n^{2(1+s)}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{(1+s)}(n+1)}{n^{2(1+s)}}\leq C_{s}<+\infty.

MM

\square

3.4.
Transport-diffusion equation with oscillatory force

We consider the transport-diffusion equation with oscillatory force

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\theta+v\cdot\nabla\theta-\triangle\theta=a(t,x)\cos(\lambda k\cdot x),\quad x\in{\rm T}^{2},\\[3.0pt]
{\rm div}v=0,\\[3.0pt]
\theta(0,x)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
k\in S^{1}\cap Q^{2}
is a vector and
\lambda k\in Z^{2},\lambda\neq 0
.

To simplify the formulas, we introduce the following notation: for any
n\in{\rm N}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\|(f_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,f_{n})\|_{X}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|f_{k}\|_{X},

where
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is a norm.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\theta(t,x)
be a solution of (
3.4
). Then there hold

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle C(M)\frac{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda}+C(M)\frac{\|\nabla^{M+1}a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda^{M+1}},\quad\forall M\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{N}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle C(N)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}^{\frac{N+1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+C(N)\|(\nabla^{N-1}a,\lambda^{N-1}a)\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}},\quad N\geq 1.

(3.13)

Proof.

Estimate on
\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}
:

A direct energy estimate gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Big|\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}a(t,x)\cos(\lambda k\cdot x)\theta(t,x)dx\Big|.

By integration by parts, for any integer
M
, there holds

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}a(t,x)\cos(\lambda k\cdot x)\theta(t,x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\|a(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big|\int_{{\rm T}^{2}}\nabla a(t,x)\sin(\lambda k\cdot x)\theta(t,x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{M}\frac{\|\nabla^{i}a(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}}{\lambda^{i+1}}+\frac{\|\nabla^{M+1}a(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}}{\lambda^{M+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{M}\frac{\|\nabla^{i}a(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\lambda^{2(i+1)}}+\frac{\|\nabla^{M+1}a(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\lambda^{2(M+1)}}+\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{i=0}^{M}\frac{\|\nabla^{i}a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda^{i+1}}+C\frac{\|\nabla^{M+1}a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda^{M+1}}.

Finally, using interpolation inequality and Young inequality, we know

\displaystyle\frac{\|\nabla^{i}a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda^{i+1}}\leq\frac{\|\nabla^{M+1}a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda^{M+2}}+\frac{\|a\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}}{\lambda},

which gives the estimate on
\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}_{x}}
in (
3.7
).

Estimate on
\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}\dot{H}^{N}}
(
N\geq 1
):

For
N\geq 1
, acting
\nabla^{N}
on both sides of (
3.4
), taking
L^{2}
inner products with
\nabla^{N}\theta
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{N}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\big<\nabla^{N-1}(v\cdot\nabla\theta,\nabla^{N+1}\theta)\big>|+|\big<\nabla^{N-1}(a(t,\cdot)\cos(\lambda k\cdot)),\nabla^{N+1}\theta)\big>|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}C_{N-1}^{k}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}\|\nabla^{N-k}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{N-1}(a(t,\cdot)\cos(\lambda k\cdot))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.14)

Using interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\theta\|^{\frac{N+1-m}{N+1}}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta\|^{\frac{m}{N+1}}_{L^{2}},\quad 0\leq m\leq N,

and Young inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}C_{N-1}^{k}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}\|\nabla^{N-k}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}C_{N-1}^{k}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|^{\frac{k+1}{N+1}}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2N+1-k}{N+1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{N+1}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(N)\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}^{\frac{2(N+1)}{k+1}}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, it’s easy to obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{N-1}(a(t,\cdot)\cos(\lambda k\cdot))\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(N)\|(\nabla^{N-1}a(t,\cdot),\lambda^{N-1}a(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}}.

Putting these estimates into (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{N}\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C(N)\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}^{\frac{2(N+1)}{k+1}}\|\theta(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C(N)\|(\nabla^{N-1}a(t,\cdot),\lambda^{N-1}a(t,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{N}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq C(N)\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\nabla^{k}v\|_{0}^{\frac{N+1}{k+1}}+C(N)\|(\nabla^{N-1}a,\lambda^{N-1}a)\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}},

this completes the proof of this lemma.

MM

\square

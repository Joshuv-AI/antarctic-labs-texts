1.
Introduction

Throughout, let
k
be an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic
p
. For a finite group
G
of order divisible by
p
, the nonvanishing of the first Hochschild cohomology of the group algebra
kG
is a consequence of a result of Fleischmann, Janiszczak and Lempken
[
9
]
. There, it is shown that one can always find an element
x\in G
of order divisible by
p
, whose
p
-part is not contained in
C_{G}(x)^{\prime}
, the commutator subgroup of the centraliser of
x
in
G
; developing the terminology used in
[
9
]
we will call such an element
x
a
weak Non-Schur element
. Consequently, one obtains the nontriviality of
\mathrm{Hom}(C_{G}(x),k)
for
x
a weak Non-Schur element, and combining this with the centraliser decomposition for the Hochschild cohomology of group algebras (
[
1
, Theorem 2.11.2]
), one sees that
HH^{1}(kG)
is always nonzero.

On the other hand, this is still an open problem for the family of
twisted group algebras
, that is, the algebras obtained by “twisting” the group algebra multiplication by some
2
-cocycle. Formally, let
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G;k^{\times})
and denote by
k_{\alpha}G
the
k
-algebra with a
k
-basis given by
X:=\{\hat{x}\ |\ x\in G\}
and multiplication given by
\hat{x}\hat{y}=\alpha(x,y)\widehat{xy}
for all
\hat{x},\hat{y}\in X
. Here, the product
\hat{x}\hat{y}
is in
k_{\alpha}G
whilst
\widehat{xy}
is the image in
X
of the product
xy
in
G
.

In this note we determine the following result, similar to that of
[
9
]
, which may be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite simple group of order divisible by
p
. Then
G
contains a weak Non-Schur element
x
which satisfies
\alpha(g,x)=\alpha(x,g)
for all
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G;k^{\times})
and all
g\in C_{G}(x)
.

Our motivation for this result lies in one immediate consequence of this theorem, as we obtain the nonvanishing first Hochschild cohomology of twisted finite simple group algebras.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite simple group of order divisible by
p
. Then for all
2
-cocycles
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G;k^{\times})
, the first Hochschild cohomology group of the twisted group algebra
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)\neq\{0\}
.

Just as with the result of Fleischmann, Janiszczak and Lempken, the proof of Theorem
1.1
uses the classification of finite simple groups. What is more it is interesting to note that no proof of the nonvanishing first Hochschild cohomology of group algebras is known at present, that does not use the classification. We are also unaware of a reduction to finite simple groups for the nonvanishing Hochschild cohomology of twisted group algebras.

There has been some recent independent interest in the first Hochschild cohomology of twisted group algebras from Todea
[
18
]
. As with our work, Todea takes
[
9
]
as a starting point and some similar methods are used, though the main results differ: there, it is shown that for
p
-solvable groups, their twisted group algebras have nonzero first Hochschild cohomology. Consequently, we are able to give a partial answer to a question of Todea (
[
18
, Question 1.3]
) in the affirmative, with a reduction to finite simple groups remaining the only barrier to a full affirmative answer.

In addition to generalising the case where
\alpha
represents the trivial class in
H^{2}(G;k^{\times})
, the nonvanishing of the first Hochschild cohomology of twisted group algebras is a problem of interest in its own right as may be seen when one observes that
k_{\alpha}G
always arises as the direct sum of blocks of some central group extension of
G
(see
[
11
, §1]
). Thus showing that
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)\neq\{0\}
in general can also be seen as a stepping stone towards proving the nonvanishing of
HH^{1}(B)
for a block
B
of a finite group algebra with a nontrivial defect group.

The first Hochschild cohomology of a
k
-algebra
A
admits a Lie algebra structure, which extends to a graded Lie algebra structure on
HH^{*}(A)
, though in this note we are only interested in degree one. Our motivation to study the more general problem of the nonvanishing of
HH^{1}(B)
comes in part from a wider investigation into the links between the Lie algebra structure of
HH^{1}(B)
and the
k
-algebra structure of
B
, examining how each influences the other. In particular, the Lie algebra structure is also expected to provide information useful to the Auslander-Reiten conjecture (see
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
for more examples of this).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
amounts to an examination of the weak Non-Schur elements given in the Fleischmann, Janiszczak and Lempken result
[
9
]
, and determining that in each case the desired property holds. In Section
2
we give auxiliary results and details of twisted group algebras and Hochschild cohomology in the first degree, and in Section
3
we separate the main result into propositions, showcasing different methods to prove that it holds for specific families of finite simple groups. Finally, in Section
4
we use
GAP
to calculate some dimensions of Hochschild cohomology groups of twisted finite simple group algebras.

2.
Auxiliary results

Throughout this section we fix
G
to be an arbitrary finite group unless otherwise stated. For a
2
-cocycle
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G;k^{\times})
the associativity of multiplication in
k_{\alpha}G
is a consequence of the
2
-cocycle identity. Recall that we have an isomorphism of abelian groups between
H^{2}(G;k^{\times})
and
M(G)_{p^{\prime}}
, the component of the Schur multiplier of
G
of order coprime to
p
. It is well known that there is a bijective correspondence between the cohomology classes of
2
-cocycles
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G;k^{\times})
and central extensions
1\to k^{\times}\to H\to G\to 1
. Moreover, for such an

one can always reduce to a central extension of the form
1\to Z\to\hat{G}\to G\to 1,
for some finite subgroup
\hat{G}
of
k^{\times}
and cyclic
p^{\prime}
-subgroup

of
k^{\times}
(see for example
[
13
, Proposition 1.2.18]
). With this notation, multiplication in
\hat{G}
is equivalent to multiplication between elements of the basis of
k_{\alpha}G
indexed by
G
, and so we make no distinction between the two. In particular, for each
x\in G
, we write
\hat{x}
to mean both the image of

in
k_{\alpha}G
or a preimage of
x
in
\hat{G}
. Note that we may now restrict to the case where
\alpha
takes values in
Z
, and these details are made explicit by the following fact (
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
): with
|Z|=m
we have an isomorphism of
k
-algebras

k\hat{G}\cong\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{\alpha^{i}}G.

(1)

Let
A
be an associative
k
-algebra and let
M
be an
(A,A)
-bimodule. Let
A^{e}
denote the tensor product of
k
-algebras
A\otimes_{k}A^{\mathrm{op}}
and view

as an
A^{e}
-module. Then for all

the
n
’th Hochschild cohomology group of
A
with coefficients in
M
is the

-module

HH^{n}(A;M)=\mathrm{Ext}^{n}_{A^{e}}(A;M),

and the
n
’th Hochschild cohomology group of

is
HH^{n}(A;A)=HH^{n}(A)
.

It is straightforward to show that taking Hochschild cohomology of (twisted) group algebras distributes nicely with respect to
(\ref{dirprod})
, and we obtain an isomorphism of
k
-modules,

HH^{1}(k\hat{G})\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m-1}HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G).

(2)

This will see regular applications in the proof of Proposition
4.1
: in particular it will be used to calculate the dimensions of the degree one Hochschild cohomology of twisted simple group algebras for small
Z
, and one notes that we have

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k\hat{G}))-\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG))=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G)).

(3)

What is more, it is easily seen that
k_{\alpha^{-1}}G\cong(k_{\alpha}G)^{\mathrm{op}}
as
k
-algebras and
HH^{n}(A)\cong HH^{n}(A^{\mathrm{op}})
as Lie algebras for an arbitrary
k
-algebra

and for all

, which can be used to simplify (
2
) and (
3
) in explicit cases, as this results in an equality of dimensions
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G))=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{-1}}G))
. Moreover, the
centraliser decomposition for the Hochschild cohomology of group algebras
(
[
1
, Theorem 2.11.2]
) allows for the easy computation of the dimensions on the left hand side of (
3
).

The centraliser decomposition has a generalisation to twisted group algebras, which we will refer to throughout.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
20
, Lemma 3.5]

Let
G
be a finite group and
[\alpha]\in H^{2}(G;k^{\times})
. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of graded
k
-modules

HH^{*}(k_{\alpha}G)\cong\bigoplus_{x}H^{*}(C_{G}(x);k_{\alpha}\hat{x}),

where
x
runs over a complete set of conjugacy class representatives of

,
k_{\alpha}\hat{x}
is the one-dimensional
k
-module spanned by the image
\hat{x}
of
x
in
k_{\alpha}G
, and the action of
C_{G}(x)
on
k_{\alpha}\hat{x}

g\cdot\hat{x}=\alpha(g,x)\alpha(x,g)^{-1}\hat{x}
.

Note that setting
[\alpha]=[1]\in H^{2}(G;k^{\times})

is the “twisted conjugation” action:
\hat{g}\hat{x}=\alpha(g,x)\widehat{gx}=\alpha(g,x)\widehat{xg}=\alpha(g,x)\alpha(x,g)^{-1}\hat{x}\hat{g}
, whence
g\cdot\hat{x}=\hat{g}\hat{x}\hat{g}^{-1}
for all
g\in C_{G}(x)
. We will denote by
\lambda_{\alpha}:C_{G}(x)\to k^{\times}
, the homomorphism inducing this action defined by
g\mapsto\alpha(g,x)\alpha(x,g)^{-1}
for all
g\in C_{G}(x)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Following well known terminology

For the remainder of this section we fix the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

With the notation above, we have the following.

(i)

There are isomorphisms of groups

(ii)

(iv)

.

Suppose that

has order coprime to

. Then

is
\alpha
-regular, and the action of

The group

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose

Proposition 2.11

for some weak Non-Schur element

. Then
\mathrm{Hom}(C_{G}(x),k)
is nonzero. In particular,

for all finite groups

Proof.

Recall that
O^{p}(G)
denotes the smallest normal subgroup of

such that the quotient group
G/O^{p}(G)
is a

, which is of order divisible by

, since

satisfies

. Then with

, the quotient

is a nontrivial elementary abelian

-group with rank equal to
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\mathrm{Hom}(C_{G}(x),k))
. The second statement then follows from
[

]
and Proposition

.
∎

It is not true in general that

. Indeed, were this the case then one could easily show that

, via the following result.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

. Then for all
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G;k^{\times})
, every strong Non-Schur element
x
is
\alpha
-regular. In particular,
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)\neq\{0\}
.

Proof.

On the one hand,

is a
p
-element satisfying

by Proposition

. On the other, by Lemma

is

-regular, so

.
∎

It is easy to show a slight reformulation of the proposition above: any group satisfying

also satisfies
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k\hat{G}))>\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG))

4.
Dimensions

Using the isomorphism given by (
2
) and the comments that follow it, the centraliser decomposition, and the
GAP
code in
[
16
, Appendix A]
we are able to calculate the dimensions of the first Hochschild cohomology of some finite simple group algebras. Since the cover
\hat{G}
is an extension of
G
by a relatively small central subgroup
Z
in each case below, finding the dimensions of
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)
is a case of elementary arithmetic.

More explicitly, we have that for the case where
Z=C_{2}
,
k\hat{G}
is isomorphic as a
k
-algebra to
kG\times k_{\alpha}G
. Taking Hochschild cohomology, one obtains
HH^{1}(k\hat{G})\cong HH^{1}(kG)\oplus HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)
which may then be used to show that
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k\hat{G}))-\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG))
. When
Z=C_{3}
, we have
kG\times k_{\alpha}G\times k_{\alpha^{-1}}G
so that
HH^{1}(kG)\oplus HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)^{\oplus 2}
by our earlier remarks, whence
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)=(\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k\hat{G}))-\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG)))/2
.

When
Z=C_{4}
,
C_{6}
or in one case
C_{12}
we have to be a little more careful. In some of these cases, one finds that by Corollary
1.2
the only option is that all the dimensions of the
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G)
,
i=1,\ldots,|Z|-1
are all equal to
1
; take, for example, the case where
G=A_{7}
,
Z=C_{6}
and
p=5
. Here,
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k\hat{G}))=6
,
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(kG))=1
so that
\sum_{i=1}^{5}\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G))=5
. By our main corollary, the nonvanishing of
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G)
, one therefore obtains
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G))=1
for
i=0,\ldots,5
. On the other hand, in this same example with instead the prime
p=7
, we have that
\alpha^{2}
and
\alpha^{-2}
correspond exactly to the entry of the table for which
Z=C_{3}
, and
\alpha^{3}
corresponds to the earlier entry for which
Z=C_{2}
. In other words, subtracting the dimensions of
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha}G))
for the entries
Z=C_{3}
and
Z=C_{2}
from the dimension of
HH^{1}(k\hat{G})
we conclude that the dimensions of
HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G)
,
i=0,\ldots,5
are equal to
2
.

In fact, the only occurrence in our table for which these dimensions are not all equal for all powers of
\alpha
is when
G
is the Mathieu group
M_{22}
,
Z=C_{4}
and
p=3
. In this case, one sees (using the same reasoning as above) that
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(HH^{1}(k_{\alpha^{i}}G)
is equal to
3,2,3,2
for
i=0,1,2,3
respectively.

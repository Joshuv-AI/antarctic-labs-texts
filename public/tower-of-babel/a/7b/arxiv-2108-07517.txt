Let
\{t_{n}(z)\}_{n=0}^{\infty},
with deg(
t_{n})=n,n\in\mathbb{N},
be a sequence of orthogonal polynomials
and
w(z)
be the corresponding weight function on a support

Then a well known fact about the zeros of

is that,
they are real, simple and lie in
(c,d).

Moreover, the zeros of
t_{n}(z)
and
t_{n-1}(z)
interlace,
if there is exactly one zero of
t_{n-1}(z)
in between any two consecutive zeros of
t_{n}(z).

Similarly, the zeros of the two equal degree polynomials
t_{n}(z)
and
u_{n}(z)
are interlacing if
there is exactly one zero of
u_{n}(z)
(equivalently,
t_{n}(z)
)
in between any two consecutive zeros of
t_{n}(z)
(equivalently,
u_{n}(z)
).
Another interlacing property given by Stieltjes (cf.
[
19

usually called Stieltjes interlacing, states that,
for

with

if there exists
m

t_{n}(z)

then there is exactly one zero of
u_{m}(z)
in each of the
m
open intervals.
It follows that, if there is at least a zero of
u_{m}(z)
lies outside the interval

(t_{1,n},t_{n,n});
where
t_{1,n}
and
t_{n,n},
respectively,
are the smallest and the largest zero of
t_{n}(z),

the Stieltjes interlacing will not retain between
the zeros of
u_{m}(z)
and
t_{n}(z)
with
n-1>m.

The departure of zeros of polynomials from the interval of orthogonality
leads to the following definition of quasi-orthogonality:

z_{i,n}z_{i+1,n}>0.

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+1)}(z_{i,n};q)

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)

changes sign in

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)
has an odd number of zeros
in

0<y_{1,n}<x_{1,n}<y_{2,n}<x_{2,n}<\cdots<y_{n,n}<x_{n,n}.

z_{n,n}<x_{n,n},

is the largest zero of
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q).

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)
and
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)

so from (

In the next theorem we derive an interlacing result
between the zeros of the above considered polynomials involving the point

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 2,n\in\mathbb{N},
let
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)

and
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)
be co-prime

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)

and
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)

c_{n}\notin(x_{i,n},x_{i+1,n})

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q),

interlace with those of
z\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q).

Proof.

Evaluating (
11
) at the consecutive zeros
x_{i,n}
and
x_{i+1,n},i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\},

of
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q),

=\left({1-q^{\delta+n+1}\over q^{\delta+2n+1}}\right)^{2}\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(x_{i,n};q)\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(x_{i+1,n};q).

(14)

It is clear from (
13
) that,

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+1)}(x_{i,n};q)\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+1)}(x_{i+1,n};q)<0

for every
i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}.

If for each
i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\},

c_{n}\notin(x_{i,n},x_{i+1,n}),

then the left-hand side of (
14
) is negative, whence

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(x_{i,n};q)\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(x_{i+1,n};q)<0.

Since
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)
has positive zeros
and the least zero of
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)
is negative,
the only possible arrangement of zeros is

for one
j,

j\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\},

then we deduce from (
14

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(x_{j,n};q)\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(x_{j+1,n};q)>0

for one
j,j\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}.

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)

has

sign changes between the consecutive zeros of
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta+2)}(z;q)
in
(0,\infty)

(x_{j,n},x_{j+1,n})

Since
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)
has
n-1

and
n-2

(0,\infty),
no zero of
\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)

lies in

that contains

and one zero of

\tilde{L}_{n}^{(\delta)}(z;q)

or

) and (

therefore, the zeros of

and

This proves the theorem.
∎

(15)

Evaluating (

Remark 2
.

From (

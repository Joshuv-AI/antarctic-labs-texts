Introduction and main result

This work builds on preliminary (unpublished) results obtained jointly by Duvan Henao (Pontificia Universidad
Católica de Chile), who has
guided this Masters project, and Sylvia Serfaty (New York University).

Cavitation in solid mechanics is the name given to the sudden formation and expansion
of cavities in the interior of an elastic (or elasto-plastic) body subject to
sufficiently large and multiaxial tension.
The first experimental studies of cavitation in elastomers are due to
Gent & Lindley
[
GL59
]
, who were also able to give
a theoretical prediction for the critical hydrostatic load at which
the internal rupture occurs by solving the equilibrium equations for
an infinitely thick nonlinearly elastic shell under the assumption of radial symmetry.
The first analysis of the evolution of a cavity beyond its nucleation was due to Ball
[
Bal82
]
,
who showed that the one-parameter family of deformations

\displaystyle u(x)=\sqrt[n]{|x|^{n}+L^{n}}\frac{x}{|x|},\quad L\geq 0,\quad n=2,3

(1)

provides a stable branch of weak solutions to the incompressible elasticity equations
that bifurcates from the homogeneous deformation at the critical dead-load
predicted by Gent & Lindley.
The assumption of radial symmetry, which persisted in this pioneering work,
was finally removed by Müller & Spector
[
MS95
]
and Sivaloganathan
& Spector
[
SS00
]
who proved the existence of minimizers of
the elastic energy allowing for all sorts of cavitation configurations.
Lopez-Pamies, Idiart & Nakamura
[
LPIN11
]
and
Negrón-Marrero & Sivaloganathan
[
NMS12
]
discussed the onset of cavitation
under non-symmetric loadings and Mora-Corral
[
MC14
]
studied the quasistatic evolution of cavitation. We refer to the Introduction in
[
HS13
]
and the
references therein for a more complete guide through the extensive literature on this fracture mechanism.

This thesis is concerned with the determination of the maximum load at which the cavities formed no longer grow independently, retaining their spherical symmetry, but are forced to interact with each other.
The interaction between cavities, in Sobolev models for perforated domains, has been numerically studied in

[
XH11
,
LL11b
,
LL11a
,
LL12
,
LRCLP15
]
.
Henao & Mora-Corral
[
HMC10
]
proposed a free-discontinuity model allowing
for fracture by void coalescence, which was further analyzed in
[
HMC11
,
HMC12
,
HMC15
]
.
An Ambrosio-Tortorelli regularization of this model was presented in
[
HMCX15
]

and implemented in
[
HMCX16
]
, showing the transition from the independent growth of circular cavities to coalescence. However, the only existing quantitative analysis of the interaction between cavities is due to Henao & Serfaty
[
HS13
]

who study the behaviour of

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\varepsilon,a_{1},

\displaystyle a_{2},v_{1},v_{2}):=\min\Bigg\{\int_{{\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon))}\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}dx\,:\

\displaystyle u\in H^{1}({\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon));\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ u(x)=\lambda x\ \text{for }x\in\partial{\mathcal{B}},

(2)

\displaystyle u\text{ is invertible (in a certain sense),\ }\det Du=1\ \text{a.e.},

\displaystyle\text{and}\ |\imT(u,B(a_{i},\varepsilon))|=v_{i}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),\ i=1,2.\Bigg\}

as the puncture scale
\varepsilon
and the distance
|a_{1}-a_{2}|
tend to zero.
Here
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon))
can be
interpreted as
the reference configuration of a two-dimensional elastic body
containing initial micro-cavities centred at
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
;
the map

represents the deformation of the body subject to
the pure displacement boundary condition
u(x)=\lambda x

on
\partial{\mathcal{B}}
, with
\lambda>1
;
for
i=1,2

the set
\imT(u,B(a_{i},\varepsilon))
is what is known
in the literature
[

,
MS95
]
as
the topological image of the ball
B(a_{i},\varepsilon)
,
and corresponds (in this case) to the space occupied by cavity
i

after the deformation;

|\imT(B(a_{i},\varepsilon))|
is the area of that cavity;
the positive parameters
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are fixed
(independent of
\varepsilon
); finally,
the last constraint expresses that the deformed cavities
are required to have areas that are closer and closer
to
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, respectively, as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The incompressibility constraint
\det Du\equiv 1

imposes a relation between the stretch factor
\lambda

associated to the Dirichlet condition on the outer
boundary
\partial{\mathcal{B}}
and
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}|{\mathcal{B}}|-(v_{1}+v_{2})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left|\lambda{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\big(\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{1},\varepsilon))\cup\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{2},\varepsilon))\big)\right|

(3)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left|u_{\varepsilon}\Big({\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon))\Big)\right|

(4)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon))}\det Du_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=|{\mathcal{B}}|,

(5)

provided that effectively
|\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{i},\varepsilon))|=v_{i}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),\ i=1,2
. We write
\varepsilon\to 0
and
u_{\varepsilon}
for simplicity of notation, though in reality we are considering a sequence
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
,
a corresponding sequence of deformations
(u_{\varepsilon_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, and the limit as
j\to 0
.
Also, note that
the constraint
|\imT(u,B(a_{i},\varepsilon))|=v_{i}
, with
v_{i}
independent of
\varepsilon
, cannot be satisfied because
the areas of the micro-cavities
B(a_{i},\varepsilon)
need to be taken into account, hence the need of the
O(\varepsilon^{2})

The first result in
[
HS13
]
(see
[
HS13
, Thm. 1.5]
) is the lower bound

\int_{{\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon))}\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}dx\\
\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}|\imT(u,B(a_{i},\varepsilon))|\right)\left(C+\log\frac{\dist(\{a_{1},a_{2}\},\partial{\mathcal{B}})}{\varepsilon}\right),

(6)

which is satisfied for any
u
in the admissible space and any cavitation points
a_{1}
,
a_{2}

(assuming they are fixed with respect to
\varepsilon
).
This estimate shows, in particular, that the Dirichlet energy of any sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
blows
up as
(v_{1}+v_{2})|\log\varepsilon|
when
\varepsilon\to 0
(at least in the case when
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
remain far from
\partial{\mathcal{B}}
),
which, in a sense, is to be expected since the singularity in the gradient of a map creating a cavity from a single point
a\in{\mathcal{B}}

is at least of the order of

\displaystyle|Du(x)|\sim\frac{L}{r},\quad r=|x-a|

where
L
is such that
\pi L^{2}
equals the area of the created cavity.
Nevertheless, one can still ask under what conditions the energy of a sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
blows up
at no more than the stated rate of
(v_{1}+v_{2})|\log\varepsilon|
, i.e., under what conditions
the
renormalized energy

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{B}}\setminus(B(a_{1},\varepsilon)\cup B(a_{2},\varepsilon))}\frac{|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}dx-(v_{1}+v_{2})|\log\varepsilon|

(7)

is uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
.
A more general situation was considered in
[
HS13
]
, where
a_{1}=a_{1,\varepsilon}
and
a_{2}=a_{2,\varepsilon}
, as well
as the ratio
\frac{v_{1,\varepsilon}}{v_{2,\varepsilon}}
(but not the total cavity area
v_{1,\varepsilon}+v_{2,\varepsilon}
),
are allowed to change with
\varepsilon
.
It was proved in
[
HS13
, Thm. 1.9]
that
if the renormalized energy is bounded independently of
\varepsilon

and the centres
a_{1,\varepsilon}
,
a_{2,\varepsilon}
are compactly contained in
{\mathcal{B}}

then, passing to a subsequence,
one of the following holds:

i)

the sequences
(v_{1,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(v_{2,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

converge to values
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
that are strictly positive;
the cavities
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{1_{\varepsilon}},\varepsilon))
and
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{2,\varepsilon},\varepsilon))

converge to disks of areas
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
(in the metric given by
\dist(E_{1},E_{2})=|E_{1}\triangle E_{2}|
); and (under the additional assumption that the midpoints

\frac{a_{1,\varepsilon}+a_{2,\varepsilon}}{2}
remain far from
\partial{\mathcal{B}}
),
the distance
|a_{1,\varepsilon}-a_{2,\varepsilon}|
does not vanish as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

ii)

One of the sequences
(v_{i,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
(say
(v_{2,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
) vanishes
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and the cavities
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{1,\varepsilon},\varepsilon))
converge to
a disk of area
v_{1}
.

iii)

The distances
|a_{1,\varepsilon}-a_{2,\varepsilon}|
scale like
O(\varepsilon)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
; the unions of the cavities
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{1,\varepsilon},\varepsilon))\cup\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{2,\varepsilon},\varepsilon))

converge to a disk of area
v_{1}+v_{2}
; and each of the cavities, independently,
are necessarily distorted, in the sense that their distance to the set of all disks
is bounded away from zero.

Here we consider a more restrictive setting where the centres
a_{1}
and
a_{2}

in the reference configuration, as well as the target cavity areas
v_{1}>0
and
v_{2}>0
,
are given (they are part of the data of the problem, together with
{\mathcal{B}}
).
In particular, for any sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with bounded renormalized energy,
scenario ii) -where one of the cavities closes up in the limit- and scenario iii) -where the cavities are
pushed together to form one equivalent round cavity- will not occur;
we will only be left with the possibility that
the cavities
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{1},\varepsilon))
and
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{2},\varepsilon))

must converge to disks of areas
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.
We interpret this as saying that the second stage in the experimental observations of fracture inititation
in elastomers, in which the cavities formed stop growing independently (retaining their spherical symmetry)
and start deforming together (to the point of eventually coalescing), corresponds to a
higher energy regime, it is not attainable with an energy of just
(v_{1}+v_{2})|\log\varepsilon|
.
On the other hand, it is not always possible to produce circular “independent” cavities of any given areas
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

coming from any fixed locations
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
in the reference configuration.
Indeed, suppose that
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{1},\varepsilon))
and
\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B(a_{2},\varepsilon))

effectively converge to disks
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, of areas
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, which must be contained
in
\lambda{\mathcal{B}}
. If, for example,
{\mathcal{B}}=B(0,R_{0})
, we must have that the line segment joining
the centres of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
is shorter than (or equal to) the radius
\lambda R_{0}
,
so necessarily

\sqrt{\frac{v_{1}}{\pi}}+\sqrt{\frac{v_{2}}{\pi}}\leq\lambda R_{0}.

On the other hand, incompressibility yields

v_{1}+v_{2}=(\lambda^{2}-1)\pi R_{0}^{2}.

Hence, a necessary condition for the existence of a sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

with bounded renormalized energy is that

(\lambda^{2}-1)R_{0}^{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{v_{1}v_{2}}}{\pi}\leq\lambda^{2}R_{0}^{2}
, i.e.

2\sqrt{v_{1}v_{2}}\leq\pi R_{0}^{2}.

This shows that if the load is sufficiently large (if the requirement is imposed that cavities
must be opened of areas
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
with
2\sqrt{v_{1}v_{2}}>\pi R_{0}^{2}
)
then the deformations must necessarily enter in the higher energy regime
where the energies blow up at a rate higher than
(v_{1}+v_{2})|\log\varepsilon|
.
This gives rise to the question of for what values of
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
and what locations
a_{1}
and
a_{2}

can the hypothesis of the existence of a sequence with bounded renormalized energy
actually be satisfied. This is the specific question we address in this article.

In fact,
we consider a more general version of the above mentioned question where the material
can open not only two but an arbitrarily large (albeit fixed) number of cavities.
We consider the case of a circular domain
{\mathcal{B}}
and of a displacement
condition of the form
u(x)=\lambda x
for
x
on the outer boundary
\partial{\mathcal{B}}
,
though in reality more general domains and Dirichlet conditions could be treated
with minor modifications from this work.
We prove that a sufficient condition on
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
, …,
a_{n}

and
v_{1}
,
v_{2}
, …,
v_{n}
, for a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for the existence of a sequence of deformations

(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
with bounded renormalized energy is that
the following simple geometric property be satisfied.

Definition 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
R_{0}>0
, and
{\mathcal{B}}:=B(0,R_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We say that
\Big((a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\Big)

is a configuration attainable
through an evolution of circular cavities (or, more briefly, an
attainable configuration
)
if
a_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}
and
v_{i}>0
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
and there exist evolutions

•

z_{i}\in C^{1}([1,\lambda],\mathbb{R}^{2})
of the cavity centres, and

•

L_{i}:[1,\lambda]\to[0,\infty)
of the cavity radii,

where
\lambda
is given by

\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}=(\lambda^{2}-1)\pi R_{0}^{2},

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\pi L_{i}^{2}(t)=(t^{2}-1)\pi R_{0}^{2}\qquad\forall\,t\in[1,\lambda]

(8)

and for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

i)

L_{i}^{2}
belongs to
C^{1}([1,\lambda],[0,\infty))
;

ii)

z_{i}(1)=a_{i}
and
L_{i}(1)=0
;

iii)

\pi L_{i}^{2}(\lambda)=v_{i}
; and

iv)

for all
t\in[1,\lambda]
the disks
\overline{B(z_{i}(t),L_{i}(t))}
are disjoint
and contained in
B(0,tR_{0})
.

Although other time parametrizations are of course possible for the evolution of the centres
and the radii in the above definition, we have chosen the stretch factor at the outer boundary

\partial{\mathcal{B}}
as our parameter.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\mathcal{B}}=B(0,R_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose that the configuration

\Big((a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\Big)
is attainable.
Let
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
be a sequence that we will denote in what follows simply by
\varepsilon
.
Set
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{B}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(a_{i})
.
Assume that for every
\varepsilon
the map
u_{\varepsilon}
minimizes
\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}|Du|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

among all
u\in H^{1}({\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying

•

the invertibility condition (INV) of Definition
3
;

•

u(x)=\lambda x
for
x\in\partial{\mathcal{B}}
;

•

\det Du(x)=1
for a.e.
x\in{\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}}
;

•

and
|\imT(u,B_{\varepsilon}(a_{i}))|=v_{i}+O(\varepsilon^{2})
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Then there exists a constant
C=C\big(n,R_{0},(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\big)

independent of
\varepsilon
such that

\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{|Du_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\right)|\log\varepsilon|.

Moreover, there exists a subsequence (not relabelled) and

u\in\bigcap_{1\leq p<2}W^{1,p}({\mathcal{B}},\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H^{1}_{\loc}({\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\},\mathbb{R}^{2})

such that

•

u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
in
H^{1}_{\loc}({\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\},\mathbb{R}^{2})
;

•

\Det Du_{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\Det Du
in
{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
;
locally in the sense of measures (where
\Det Du
is the distributional Jacobian of
Definition
5
);

•

\Det Du=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\delta_{a_{i}}+\mathcal{L}^{2}
in
{\mathcal{B}}
(where
\mathcal{L}^{2}
is
the Lebesgue measure);

•

The cavities
\imT(u,a_{i})
(as defined in Definition
2
) are disks of area
v_{i}
, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
;

•

|\imT(u_{\varepsilon},B_{\varepsilon}(a_{i}))\triangle\imT(u,a_{i})|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The following example gives a sense about which configurations
\Big((a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\Big)

are attainable through an evolution of circular cavities.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{\mathcal{B}}:=B(0,R_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}>0
.
Let
\lambda>1
be such that
(\lambda^{2}-1)\pi R_{0}^{2}=\sum v_{i}
. Set

\displaystyle\sigma=\min\left\{\min_{i}\frac{\left(1-\frac{|a_{i}|}{R_{0}}\right)^{2}}{\frac{v_{i}}{\sum v_{k}}},\min_{i\neq j}\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{v_{i}}{\sum v_{k}}}+\sqrt{\frac{v_{j}}{\sum v_{k}}}\right)^{2}}\right\}.

(9)

Then both in the case
\sigma\geq 1
and in the case
\sigma<1
and
\lambda^{2}<\frac{1}{1-\sigma}

the configuration
\Big((a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\Big)

is attainable through an evolution of circular cavities.

Proof.

For every
t\in[1,\lambda]
and every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
set

\displaystyle z_{i}(t):=ta_{i},\quad L_{i}(t):=\sqrt{(t^{2}-1)\frac{v_{i}}{\sum v_{k}}}\cdot R_{0}.

(10)

We only need to check that the
\overline{B(z_{i}(t),L_{i}(t))}
are disjoint and contained in
B(0,tR_{0})
for all
t

(the remaining conditions in Definition
1
are immediately verified).
Both in the case
\sigma\geq 1
and in the case
\sigma<1
and
\lambda^{2}<\frac{1}{1-\sigma}

we have that

1-\lambda^{-2}<\sigma.

As a consequence, we obtain that

1-t^{-2}<\sigma\quad\forall\,t\in[1,\lambda].

Hence,

1-t^{-2}<\frac{\left(1-\frac{|a_{i}|}{R_{0}}\right)^{2}}{\frac{v_{i}}{\sum v_{k}}}\quad\forall\,i

and

1-t^{-2}<\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}}{R_{0}^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{v_{i}}{\sum v_{k}}}+\sqrt{\frac{v_{j}}{\sum v_{k}}}\right)^{2}}\quad\forall\,i\neq j.

It is easy to see that the first inequality is equivalent to

L_{i}(t)^{2}<t^{2}(R_{0}-|a_{i}|)^{2}

which in turn says that
L_{i}(t)+|z_{i}(t)|<tR_{0}
(i.e., each
\overline{B(z_{i}(t),L_{i}(t))}\subset B(0,tR_{0})
).
Analogously, the second inequality is equivalent to

(\sqrt{L_{i}(t)}+\sqrt{L_{j}(t)})^{2}<t^{2}|a_{i}-a_{j}|^{2}

which in turn says that
L_{i}(t)+L_{j}(t)<|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)|
(i.e., the disks are disjoint).
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark
.

In the case when
v_{1}=v_{2}=\cdots=v_{n}
,

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle n\pi\min\left\{\min_{i}(R_{0}-|a_{i}|)^{2},\min_{i\neq j}\left(\frac{|a_{i}-a_{j}|}{2}\right)^{2}\right\}}{\pi R_{0}^{2}}.

(11)

This is the packing density of the largest disjoint collection of the form
\{B(a_{i},\rho):i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\}

contained in
{\mathcal{B}}
. There is an extensive literature on the famous circle packing problem;
for example, it is known
[
Mel94
]
that when
n=11
the maximum packing density is

\frac{11}{\left(1+\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{9}}\right)^{2}}\approx 0.7145,

which yields the upper bound

\lambda<\sqrt{\frac{(1+\sin\frac{\pi}{9})^{2}}{1+2\sin\frac{\pi}{9}-10\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{9}}}\approx 1.8714

for which our above construction is able to produce attainable configurations with 11 cavities of equal size.

Definition 2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,p}(\partial B(x,r),\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
r>0
, and
p>1
. Then

\imT(u,B(x,r)):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\deg(u,\partial B(x,r),y)\neq 0\}.

Given
u\in W^{1,p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
x\in E
, there is a set
R_{x}\subset(0,\infty)
,
which coincides a.e. with
\{r>0:B(x,r)\subset E\}
, such that
u|_{\partial B(x,r)}\in W^{1,p}

and both
\deg(u,\partial B(x,r),\cdot)
and
\imT(u,B(x,r))
are well defined for all
r\in R_{x}
.

Definition 3
.

We say that
u
satisfies condition INV if for every
x\in E
and every
r\in R_{x}

(i)

u(z)\in\imT(u,B(x,r))
for a.e.

and

(ii)

u(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\imT(u,B(x,r))
for a.e.

.

If
u
satisfies condition INV then
\{\imT(u,B(x,r)):r\in R_{x}\}
is increasing in
r
for every

.

Definition 4
.

Given

we define

\imT(u,a):=\bigcap_{r\in R_{a}}\imT(u,B(a,r)).

Analogously, if

is defined and satisfies condition INV in a domain of the form

Proposition 3.1

Proof

letting

(and using the fact that

u(x)=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\nu}(y-x)u(y)-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\Phi(y-x)dS(y)-\int_{\Omega}\Delta u(y)\Phi(y-x)dy,

with the normal pointing outside

. Now (as can be seen in

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{y}\phi^{x}(y)&=k&&\text{if $y\in\Omega$,}\\

(15)

being

\int_{\Omega}\Delta_{y}\phi^{x}(y)u(y)-\Delta u\phi^{x}(y)dy=\int_{\partial\Omega}u(y)\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\phi^{x}(y)-\phi^{x}(y)\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}dS(y)

where we have used (

u(x)=C-\int_{\partial B_{R}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\left(\Phi(y-x)-\phi^{x}(y)\right)dS(y)-\int_{\Omega}\Delta u\left(\Phi(y-x)-\phi^{x}(y)\right)dy.

Proposition 3.3
.

: Let
v
be harmonic in the distributional sense in
\Omega
and
R\geq Cd
, then we have the folllowing estimates :

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq Cd^{-2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

\left\|D^{\beta}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq Cd^{-2-|\beta|}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

[v]_{1,\alpha(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq Cd^{-4}R^{1-\alpha}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Proof
: The first and third estimates follow from the previous Lemma.
To prove the second estimate note that using polar coordinates we get (for
r\in(R+\frac{1}{3}d,R+\frac{2}{3}d)
and

|v(re^{i\theta_{1}})-v(re^{i\theta_{2}})|\leq\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\left|\frac{d}{d\theta}\left(v(re^{i\theta})\right)\right|d\theta\leq\int_{\theta_{1}}^{\theta_{2}}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\right|r|\sin(\theta)|+\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\right|r|\cos(\theta)|d\theta

\leq Cd^{-3}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}r|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\leq Cd^{-3}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}|re^{i\theta_{1}}-re^{i\theta_{2}}|^{\alpha}R^{1-\alpha},

since

\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\leq\frac{1-\cos(\theta)}{\theta^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]

|re^{i\theta_{1}}-re^{i\theta_{2}}|\leq 2r\leq CR
.

Moreover, for
\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]
and
r_{1},r_{2}\in[R+\frac{1}{3}d,R+\frac{2}{3}d]
, we have:

|v(r_{1}e^{i\theta})-v(r_{2}e^{i\theta})|\leq\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\left|\frac{d}{dr}\left(v(re^{i\theta})\right)\right|dr\leq\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}}\right||\cos(\theta)|+\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}\right||\sin(\theta)|dr

\leq Cd^{-3}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}|r_{1}-r_{2}|\leq Cd^{-3}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}|r_{1}e^{i\theta}-r_{2}e^{i\theta}|^{\alpha}R^{1-\alpha}

Now, for
r_{1},r_{2}\in[R+\frac{1}{3}d,R+\frac{2}{3}d]
,
r_{1}\leq r_{2}
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[-\pi,\pi]
, such that
|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|\leq\pi
, we have:

|v(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})-v(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})|\leq|v(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})-v(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{2}})|+|v(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{2}})-v(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})|

\leq Cd^{-3}R^{1-\alpha}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}(|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}-r_{1}e^{i\theta_{2}}|^{\alpha}+|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{2}}-r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}|^{\alpha})

\leq Cd^{-3}R^{1-\alpha}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}(|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}-r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}|^{\alpha}+|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}-r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}|^{\alpha}),

since
|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}-r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}|^{2}=(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+2r_{1}r_{2}(1-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))\geq 2r_{1}^{2}(1-\cos(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}))=|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}-r_{1}e^{i\theta_{2}}|^{2}

|r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}-r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}|\geq|r_{1}-r_{2}|
.
The proof of the fourth estimate is analogous.

\int_{\bigcup\{x:\varepsilon<|x-a_{i}|<R_{i}\}}\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\sum_{i}\pi R_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i}v_{i}\log R_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\right)|\log\varepsilon|

for every small
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Given
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
r\in(0,R_{i})
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi]

\displaystyle Du(a_{i}+re^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{r}{\sqrt{L_{i}(\lambda)^{2}+r^{2}}}e^{i\theta}\otimes e^{i\theta}+\sqrt{1+\frac{L_{i}(\lambda)^{2}}{r^{2}}}ie^{i\theta}\otimes ie^{i\theta}.

(28)

Hence
\det Du\equiv 1
and

\displaystyle\int_{\bigcup\{x:\varepsilon<|x-a_{i}|<R_{i}\}}\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\int_{\varepsilon}^{R_{i}}\left(1+\left(1+\frac{L_{i}(\lambda)^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\right)\cdot\pi r\,\mathrm{d}r.

(29)

∎

In order to ‘glue’ these symmetric independent cavitations,
we build an incompressible deformation
far from the cavities using the flow of Dacorogna & Moser
[
DM90
]

and the fine estimates of the previous section.

Theorem 5
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\mathcal{B}}=B(0,R_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose that the configuration

\Big((a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\Big)
is attainable. There exists
u_{ext}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{B}\setminus\bigcup_{1}^{n}B(a_{i},R_{i}),\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where the
R_{i}
are as in
(
27
), satisfying
u_{ext}(x)=\lambda x
on
\partial\mathcal{B}
;
\det Du_{ext}\equiv 1
in
\mathcal{B}\setminus\bigcup_{1}^{n}B(a_{i},R_{i})
; condition (INV); and

u_{ext}(a_{i}+R_{i}e^{i\theta})=z_{i}(\lambda)+\sqrt{L_{i}(\lambda)^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}e^{i\theta},\quad\forall\,i\in\{i,\ldots,n\}\ \forall\,\theta\in[0,2\pi].

Theorem
1
follows by combining the above results.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

We proceed as follows:

•

We fix the notation to describe the growth of the (boundaries of the) circular holes
(corresponding to the disks
B(a_{i},R_{i})
of Proposition
4.1
which are not analyzed in
Theorem
5
and are, thus, removed from
\mathcal{B}
).

•

At each instant we build a velocity field for the material points
by superposing two auxiliary fields, one that increases the radii
r_{i}(t)
of the excised holes
and another that deals with the evolution of their centers
z_{i}(t)
.

•

The trajectory of each material point is obtained as the solution of the ODE
that establishes its relation to the previously constructed instantaneous velocity fields.

•

We explain why the resulting deformation is injective and incompressible.

Evolution of the domains

For every
t\in[1,\lambda]
set

E(t):=B(0,tR_{0})\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}B(z_{i}(t),r_{i}(t))

where
r_{i}(t)
is defined in (
27
).
By continuity, there exists
d>0
(independent of
t
) such that
(
21
) is satisfied, for every
t\in[1,\lambda]
,
with
z_{i}
replaced with
z_{i}(t)
and
r_{i}
replaced with
r_{i}(t)
.
Regarding
r_{0}(t)=tR_{0}
, note that
r_{0}(t)\leq\lambda R_{0}
for all
t\in[1,\lambda]
.
Hence, setting
\delta:=\frac{d}{2\lambda R_{0}}
(which depends on
n
,
R_{0}
,

(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
and
(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
but not on
t
) we have that

E(t)\in\mathcal{F}_{\delta}\quad\forall\,t\in[1,\lambda].

In particular, by Theorem
3
there exists
C
such that

C_{P}\Big(E(t)\Big)\leq C\cdot r_{0}(t)
for all
t
. This implies that

B\Big(E(t)\Big)\leq C
for some
C
independent of
t
, where
B\Big(E(t)\Big)

is that of Proposition
3.21
.

A velocity field that accounts for the increase in the radii
r_{i}(t)

Consider a fixed
t\in[1,\lambda]
. Define
g:\partial E(t)\to\mathbb{R}
by

g(y)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}r_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\quad\forall\,y\in\partial B(z_{i}(t),r_{i}(t)),\quad i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}.

Clearly (
8
) and (
27
) imply (
23
).
We have thus all the hypotheses of Theorem
4
,
which yields the existence of
v_{t}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{E(t)},\mathbb{R}^{2})

such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}v_{t}\equiv 0\text{ in }E(t)

(30)

\displaystyle v_{t}\Big(z_{i}(t)+r_{i}(t)e^{i\theta}\Big)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}r_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}e^{i\theta}\quad\forall\,i,\theta

(31)

\displaystyle\|Dv_{t}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|g\|_{\infty},

(32)

where
C=C\big(n,R_{0},(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(v_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\big)
.
Recall that
L_{i}^{2}\in C^{1}([1,\lambda],[0,\infty))
(by Definition
1
), so

\|g\|_{\infty}=\max_{i}\left|\frac{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}(L_{i}^{2}(t))}{r_{i}(t)}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\min_{i}R_{i}}

is bounded above indepedently of
t
.

A velocity field for the translation of the excised holes

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,1))
be such that
\eta(0)=1
and
\eta^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Define

w(y):=\begin{cases}\eta\left(\frac{r-r_{i}(t)}{d}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\cdot(rie^{i\theta}),&\text{if }y=z_{i}(t)+re^{i\theta},\ r_{i}(t)\leq r<r_{i}(t)+d;\\
0&\text{in other case}\end{cases}

and

{\tilde{v}}_{t}(y):=D^{\perp}w(y),\qquad y\in\overline{E(t)}.

Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}{\tilde{v}}_{t}\equiv 0\text{ in }E(t)

(33)

\displaystyle{\tilde{v}}_{t}(y)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\text{ on }\partial B(z_{i}(t),r_{i}(t))

(34)

and

\displaystyle\|D{\tilde{v}}_{t}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\max_{i}\Bigg\|\left(d^{-2}\eta^{\prime\prime}e^{i\theta}\otimes e^{i\theta}+(dr)^{-1}\eta^{\prime}ie^{i\theta}\otimes ie^{i\theta}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\cdot(rie^{i\theta})

\displaystyle\hskip 180.00027pt+d^{-1}\eta^{\prime}\left(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{\perp}\otimes e^{i\theta}\Bigg\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C(d^{-2}\cdot(\lambda R_{0})+d^{-1})\left|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}z_{i}(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|,

which again is bounded uniformly in
t
since
z_{i}\in C^{1}([1,\lambda],\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Definition of
u_{ext}
and energy bounds

For every
x\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\bigcup_{1}^{n}B(a_{i},R_{i})
and every
t\in[1,\lambda]

let

f(x,t)
be the solution of the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)=v_{t}(f(x,t))+{\tilde{v}}_{t}(f(x,t))\\
&f(x,1)=x.\end{aligned}

(35)

It can be seen (as in Dacorogna & Moser
[
DM90
]
)
that the above autonomous ODE indeed
has a well defined solution with enough regularity in time and space
(in spite of the fact that the velocity fields are defined in changing domains).
Moreover,

f(a_{i}+R_{i}e^{i\theta},t)=z_{i}(t)+r_{i}(t)e^{i\theta}\quad\forall\,i,\theta

and

f(R_{0}e^{i\theta},t)=tR_{0}e^{i\theta}

thanks to the boundary conditions for
v_{t}
and
{\tilde{v}}_{t}
.
Define
u_{ext}
by

u_{ext}(x):=f(x,\lambda),\quad x\in\mathcal{B}\setminus\bigcup_{1}^{n}B(a_{i},R_{i}).

For every
i\in\{i,\ldots,n\}
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi]

u_{ext}(a_{i}+R_{i}e^{i\theta})=z_{i}(\lambda)+\sqrt{L_{i}(\lambda)^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}e^{i\theta}

since
r_{i}(\lambda)=\sqrt{L_{i}(\lambda)^{2}+R_{i}^{2}}
. Also
u_{ext}(x)=\lambda x
on
\partial\mathcal{B}
.

The resulting deformation
u_{ext}
is incompressible because

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\det D_{x}f(x,t)

\displaystyle=\cof D_{x}f(x,t)\cdot D_{x}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\cof D_{x}f(x,t)\cdot D_{x}((v_{t}+{\tilde{v}}_{t})\circ f)(x,t)

\displaystyle=\cof D_{x}f(x,t)\cdot(D_{y}(v_{t}+{\tilde{v}}_{t})(f(x,t))D_{x}f(x,t))

\displaystyle=(\cof D_{x}f(x,t)(D_{x}f(x,t))^{T})\cdot D_{y}(v_{t}+{\tilde{v}}_{t})(f(x,t))

\displaystyle=(\det D_{x}f(x,t))I\cdot D_{y}(v_{t}+{\tilde{v}}_{t})(f(x,t))

and the right-hand side is zero since
\operatorname{div}(v_{t}+{\tilde{v}}_{t})\equiv 0
.

To see that
u_{ext}\in H^{1}
it is enough to observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int|D_{x}f(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int D_{x}f(x,t)\cdot D_{x}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int D_{x}f(x,t)\cdot((D_{y}(v_{t}+{\tilde{v}}_{t}))(f(x,t))D_{x}f(x,t)),

whence

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int|D_{x}f(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\underbrace{(\sup_{t}\|Dv_{t}+D{\tilde{v}}_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(E(t))})}_{:=C}\int|D_{x}f(x,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This implies that
e^{-Ct}\int|D_{x}f(x,t)|^{2}
decreases with
t
. Consequently,

\int|Du_{ext}|^{2}\leq e^{C(\lambda-1)}\int|I|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

Finally, Ball’s global invertibility theorem
[
Bal81
]
shows that
u_{ext}
is one-to-one a.e. which combined with the previous energy estimate and
[
BHMC17
, Lemma 5.1]

yields that
u_{ext}
satisfies condition INV.

∎

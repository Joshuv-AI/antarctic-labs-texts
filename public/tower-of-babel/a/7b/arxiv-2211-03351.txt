1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
denote the space of analytic functions in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
. Each
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
induces the integral operator, also called Volterra-type operator, defined by

T_{g}(f)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)g^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

The study of this operator has attracted a substantial amount of attention during the last decades since the publications of the seminal works
[
1
,
2
,
23
]
.
Characterizing the operators
T_{g}
, mapping a Banach space (or complete metric space)
X\subset\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
boundedly or compactly to the Hardy space
H^{\infty}
, in terms of a reasonable condition depending on the symbol
g
only is known to be difficult, and remains unsettled in many cases
[
3
,
4
,
24
]
. Consequently, restricting
g
to some subclass of
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
, sets a natural approach to the problem. In
[
24
]
the authors described the univalent symbols
g
such that
T_{g}:H^{\infty}\to H^{\infty}
is bounded. Further, the functions
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
with non-negative Taylor coefficients such that
T_{g}(H^{p})\subset H^{\infty}
were described in
[
20
]
. In this paper, we are mainly interested in the situation where
X
is a weighted Dirichlet space and, in some of the results, the symbol
g
has non-negative Maclaurin coefficients.

For a non-negative function
\omega\in L^{1}([0,1))
, its extension to
\mathbb{D}
, defined by
\omega(z)=\omega(|z|)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, is called a radial weight. For
0<p<\infty
and such an
\omega
, the Berman space
A^{p}_{\omega}
consists of
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})

such that

\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)=\frac{dx\,dy}{\pi}
is the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.
The corresponding weighted Dirichlet space is

D^{p}_{\omega}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}):\left\|f\right\|_{D^{p}_{\omega}}^{p}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}^{p}+|f(0)|^{p}<\infty\right\}.

The classical weighted Dirichlet space
D^{p}_{\alpha}
is, by definition, equal to
D^{p}_{\omega}
, induced by the standard radial weight
\omega(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
, where
-1<\alpha<\infty
. Throughout this paper we assume
\widehat{\omega}(z)=\int_{|z|}^{1}\omega(s)\,ds>0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, for otherwise
D^{p}_{\omega}=\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})=A^{p}_{\omega}
.

A radial weight
\omega
belongs to the class
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
if there exists a constant
C=C(\omega)\geq 1
such that the tail integral
\widehat{\omega}
satisfies the doubling condition
\widehat{\omega}(r)\leq C\widehat{\omega}(\frac{1+r}{2})
for all
0\leq r<1
. Moreover, if there exist constants
K=K(\omega)>1
and
C=C(\omega)>1
such that
\widehat{\omega}(r)\geq C\widehat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1-r}{K}\right)
for all
0\leq r<1
, then we write
\omega\in\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
. Finally, the intersection
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}\cap\widecheck{\mathcal{D}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}
.

It is worth noticing that, if there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{D}
such that

\omega(z)\asymp\nu(z)(1-|z|)^{p},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

(1.1)

then
D^{p}_{\omega}
coincides with the weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\nu}
by
[
13
, Theorem 5]
. However,

\widehat{\omega}(z)
may decrease to zero arbitrarily slowly, as
z
approaches to the boundary, and therefore (
1.1
) may very well fail. Typical examples of weights violating (
1.1
) are
\omega(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
with
-1<\alpha<p-1
. Moreover, weights in
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
may have a wild oscillatory behavior and they may even vanish on sets that are not hyperbolically uniformly bounded. In these cases (
1.1
) also certainly fails. Illuminating examples of weights in the deceivingly simply looking class
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
are given in
[
12
, Proposition 10]
and
[
18
, Proposition 12]
.

We begin with considering the question of when
T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only, provided
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. From now on, if
X\subset\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
is a Banach space (or complete metric space),
T(X,H^{\infty})
(resp.
T_{c}(X,H^{\infty})
) denotes the set of
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
such that
T_{g}:X\to H^{\infty}
is bounded (resp. compact). It is known that
T(H^{p},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only, if only if,
0<p<1
, by
[
4
, Theorem 2.5(vi)]
. Therefore each
T(A^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
contains constant functions only, provided
0<p<1
and
\omega
is any radial weight. As expected, the situation is different in the case of the weighted Dirichlet space
D^{p}_{\omega}
. The first result of this paper characterizes the triviality of
T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
and
T_{c}(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
in terms of a neat condition on
p
and
\omega
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
g\in H^{\infty}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{(1-r)^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}=\infty
;

(iii)

I:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow D^{1}_{0}
is unbounded.

Moreover,

(i)

T_{c}(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{(1-r)^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}>0
;

(iii)

I:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow D^{1}_{0}
is not compact.

Probably the most important part of the theorem is the surprising equivalence between the behavior of the embedding
D^{1}_{0}\subset D^{p}_{\omega}
and that of the integration operator
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
. The true difficulty in the proof lies in showing that (i) implies (ii), because it is not that complicated to show that (iii) implies (i), and the equivalence between (ii) and (iii) is just the Carleson embedding theorem
[
15
, Theorem 1]
. Before further comments on the proof, we state the result concerning the range
1<p<\infty
. We underline here that when
1<p<\infty
there is no such neat connection to embeddings as in the case
0<p\leq 1
, as is shown in Section
6
, see Theorem
23
and the discussion presiding it.

Theorem 2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
g\in H^{\infty}
. Then
T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
(equivalently
T_{c}(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
) consists of constant functions only, if and only if,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{p^{\prime}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}\,dr=\infty.

Theorem
2
and its proof allow us to extend
[
4
, Theorem 1.3]
from the setting of so-called regular weights to the whole doubling class
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. Being precise, we deduce that
T(A^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only, if and only if,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}=\infty,

provided
1<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
g\in H^{\infty}
. Details yielding to this conclusion are given at the very end of the paper, in Section
6
.

Apart from the geometric characterizations of Carleson embeddings for the weighted Bergman spaces
[
15
]
, the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
are based on two main ingredients: an abstract criterion of the continuity of integral operators
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
, and appropriate duality relations for the space
D^{p}_{\omega}
, given in Section
3
. In particular, we will show that, for
0<p<1
, the dual of
D^{p}_{\omega}
is isomorphic to the Zygmund space
\mathcal{Z}
via the pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{W}}=\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}+f(0)\overline{g(0)},

with equivalence of norms, see Lemma
9
in Section
3
. Here
W
is an appropriate radial weight which depends on
p
and
\omega
. Recall that the Zygmund space
\mathcal{Z}
consists of
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{Z}}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})+|f^{\prime}(0)|+|f(0)|<\infty.

It is well-known that
\mathcal{Z}
is a subset of the disc algebra
\mathcal{A}
, and the containment of
f
in
\mathcal{Z}
is characterized by the boundary value condition

\sup_{\theta}\sup_{h>0}\frac{|f(e^{i(\theta+h)}+f(e^{i(\theta-h)})-2f(e^{i\theta})|}{h}<\infty.

It is worth observing that
(D^{p}_{p-1})^{\star}
is isomorphic to the weighted Bloch space
\mathcal{B}^{2}

via the
A^{2}_{\frac{1}{p}-1}
-pairing, provided
0<p\leq 1

[
20
, Lemma 6]
. The interplay between these different identifications of the dual of
D^{p}_{\omega}
is of course the change of the pairing with respect to which there are taken. The advantage of our duality
(D^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq\mathcal{Z}
compared to
(D^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq\mathcal{B}^{2}
is that, apart from being a much more general result, its proof is easier than that of
[
20
, Lemma 6]
. Namely, the crucial step in the proof of the last-mentioned result relies on technical tools related to coefficient multipliers of the Bloch spaces. Because of the pairing we work with, we can avoid many tedious calculations all together, and the proof itself becomes more straightforward and transparent via an appropriate use of a Carleson embedding theorem for the weighted Bergman spaces.

The dual of
D^{1}_{\omega}
is described via a suitable weighted
A^{2}
-pairing in terms of a weighted
\mathord{\rm BMOA}
-type space in Lemma
10
in Section
3
. This result has its roots in the description of
(A^{1}_{\omega})^{\star}
given in
[
13
, Theorem 4]
. While at first glance the last-mentioned duality, as well as our description of
(D^{1}_{\omega})^{\star}
, might look intuitively unclear, this involved result serves us to resolve the case
p=1
in Theorem
1
and other forthcoming results.

The techniques developed on the way to the aforementioned results also allow us to characterize the weights
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
such that
T_{c}(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
contains constant functions only. The statements are given in Sections
4
–
6
when each of the three cases
0<p<1
,
p=1
and
1<p<\infty
are considered separately in the said order.

The other set of results we obtain concern symbols
g
with non-negative Maclaurin coefficients such that
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
is bounded or compact. These characterizations are provided in terms of the moments of the weights. Therefore we write
\omega_{x}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{x}\omega(r)\,dr
for all
x\geq 0
. From now on, set
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{g}(n)z^{n},z\in\mathbb{D}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
g\in H^{\infty}
such that
\widehat{g}(n)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then the following statements are valid:

(i)

If
0<p<1
, then
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
is bounded if and only if

A_{p,\omega}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left((1-r)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}r^{k}}{(\omega_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{g}(n+1)(n+1)}{n+k+1}\right)\right)<\infty,

and

\|T_{g}\|_{D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow H^{\infty}}\asymp A_{p,\omega}.

Moreover,
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
is compact if and only if

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\left((1-r)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}r^{k}}{(\omega_{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{g}(n+1)(n+1)}{n+k+1}\right)\right)=0.

(ii)

If
p=1
, then
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
is bounded if and only if

A_{\omega}=\sup_{0<r,s<1}\left(\widehat{\omega}(r)^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-s}{(m+1)^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{r^{k+2}}{(\omega\widehat{\omega})_{k+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{g}(n+1)(n+1)}{n+k+1}s^{n+m+3}\right)^{2}\right)<\infty,

and

\|T_{g}\|_{D^{1}_{\omega}\rightarrow H^{\infty}}^{2}\asymp A_{\omega}.

Moreover,
T_{g}:D^{1}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
is compact if and only if

\limsup_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\limsup_{s\rightarrow 1^{-}}\left(\widehat{\omega}(r)^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-s}{(m+1)^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{r^{k+2}s^{m-k}}{(\omega\widehat{\omega})_{k+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{g}(n+1)(n+1)}{n+k+1}\right)^{2}\right)=0.

(iii)

If
1<p<\infty
, then
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow H^{\infty}
is bounded (equivalently compact) if and only if

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)^{-2}}{(\omega_{k})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)\widehat{g}(n+1)}{n+k+1}\right)^{p^{\prime}}<\infty.

Moreover,

\|T_{g}\|_{D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow H^{\infty}}^{p^{\prime}}\asymp\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k+1)^{-2}}{(\omega_{k})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)\widehat{g}(n+1)}{n+k+1}\right)^{p^{\prime}}.

The conditions appearing in the three cases in Theorem
3
are different due to the different identifications of the dual of
D^{p}_{\omega}
used. This fact forces us to employ different techniques in each case of the theorem. In particular, we will use a decomposition norm theorem for
D^{p}_{\omega}

[
17
, Theorem 3.4]
, see also
[
14
, Theorem 4]
, valid for
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, and results on universal Césaro basis of polynomials.

It is worth mentioning that the hypothesis
g\in H^{\infty}
in Theorems
1
-
3
is not a real restriction, because it is obviously necessary so that
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\to H^{\infty}
to be bounded.

There is one more thing worth mentioning before proceeding to the proofs. Namely, some of our arguments take us very naturally to consider spaces defined in terms of the Maclaurin coefficients of the function. To this end, for
0<p<\infty
and a radial weight
\omega
, define the weighted Hardy-Littlewood space
\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}
as the set of those
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
whose Maclaurin coefficients
\{\widehat{f}(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfy

\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}^{\omega}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lvert\widehat{f}(n)\rvert^{p}(n+1)^{p-2}\omega_{np+1}<\infty.

In the next section we explain how this space come to the picture and what it serves us for.

We finish the introduction by couple of words about the notation used in this paper. Throughout the paper,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
for
1<p<\infty.
Further, the letter
C=C(\cdot)
will denote an absolute constant whose value depends on the parameters indicated
in the parenthesis, and may change from one occurrence to another. If there exists a constant

C=C(\cdot)>0
such that
a\leq Cb
, then we write either
a\lesssim b
or
b\gtrsim a
. In particular, if
a\lesssim b
and

a\gtrsim b
, then we denote
a\asymp b
and say that
a
and
b
are comparable.

2.
New spaces and basic results

Recall that, for
0<p<\infty
and
-1<\alpha<\infty
, the classical weighted Dirichlet space
D^{p}_{\alpha}
is induced by the standard radial weight
\omega(z)=(\alpha+1)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
. The closely related Hardy-Littlewood space
\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}
contains those
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
whose Maclaurin coefficients
\{\widehat{f}(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfy

\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lvert\widehat{f}(n)\rvert^{p}(n+1)^{p-2}<\infty.

These and the Hardy spaces satisfy the well-known continuous embeddings

D^{p}_{p-1}\subset H^{p}\subset\mathord{\rm HL}_{p},\quad 0<p\leq 2,

(2.1)

and

\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}\subset H^{p}\subset D^{p}_{p-1},\quad 2\leq p<\infty,

(2.2)

by
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. Each of these inclusions is strict unless
p=2
in which case all the spaces are the same by direct calculations or straightforward applications of Parseval’s formula and Green’s theorem.

Recall that, for
0<p<\infty
and a radial weight
\omega
, the weighted Hardy-Littlewood space
\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}
was defined by the condition

\lVert f\rVert^{p}_{\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}^{\omega}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lvert\widehat{f}(n)\rvert^{p}(n+1)^{p-2}\omega_{np+1}<\infty,

where
\omega_{x}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{x}\omega(r)\,dr
for all
0\leq x<\infty
. Since obviously
\omega_{x}\to 0
, as
x\to\infty
, we have
\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}\subset\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}^{\omega}
for each radial weight
\omega
. The spaces
\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}
arise naturally when (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are applied to dilatations and integrated over
(0,1)
with respect to
r\omega(r)
. To see this in detail, we will need the following lemma which will be used repeatedly also throughout the rest paper, see
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
for a proof.

Lemma A
.

Let
\omega
be a radial weight. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
;

(ii)

There exist
C=C(\omega)>0
and
\beta_{0}=\beta_{0}(\omega)>0
such that

\widehat{\omega}(r)\leq C\left(\frac{1-r}{1-t}\right)^{\beta}\widehat{\omega}(t),\quad 0\leq r\leq t<1,

for all
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
;

(iii)

The asymptotic equality

\omega_{x}=\int_{0}^{1}s^{x}\omega(s)\,ds\asymp\widehat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right),

is valid for any
x\geq 1
;

(iv)

There exist constants
C=C(\omega)\geq 1
and
\eta=\eta(\omega)>0
such that

\omega_{x}\leq C\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{\eta}\omega_{y},\quad 0<x\leq y<\infty.

Set
\widetilde{\omega}(z)=\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{(1-|z|)}
and
\omega_{[\beta]}(z)=\omega(z)(1-|z|)^{\beta}
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. If
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, then the spaces
\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}
,
A^{p}_{\omega}
and
D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}
are closely related and obey the inclusions corresponding to the norm inequalities appearing in the following result.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
0<p\leq 2
, then
\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}^{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
;

(ii)

If
2\leq p<\infty
, then
\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}_{p}^{\omega}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

We begin with (i). Denote
f_{r}(z)=f(rz)
for all
0\leq r<1
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. An application of (
2.1
) to
f_{r}
and a change of variable yield

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\widehat{f}(n)|^{p}r^{np}(n+1)^{2-p}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{p}^{p}(r,f)\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lvert f^{\prime}(rte^{i\theta})\rvert^{p}\,d\theta\right)r^{p}(1-t)^{p-1}t\,dt+|f(0)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}M_{p}^{p}(s,f^{\prime})\left(r-s\right)^{p-1}\frac{s}{r}\,ds+|f(0)|^{p},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}).

By multiplying this inequality by
\omega(r)r
, integrating from 0 to 1 with respect to
r
, and using Fubini’s theorem we deduce

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}}^{p}&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{2-p}\lvert\widehat{f}(n)\rvert^{p}\omega_{np+1}\lesssim\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\\
&\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{r}M_{p}^{p}(s,f^{\prime})\left(r-s\right)^{p-1}s\,ds\right)\omega(r)\,dr+|f(0)|^{p}\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}M_{p}^{p}(s,f^{\prime})\left(\int_{s}^{1}\left(r-s\right)^{p-1}\omega(r)\,dr\right)s\,ds+|f(0)|^{p}=\|f\|^{p}_{D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}},\end{split}

(2.3)

where

h_{\omega}(z)=\int_{|z|}^{1}\left(r-|z|\right)^{p-1}\omega(r)\,dr,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

If
1\leq p<\infty
, then the pointwise inequality
h_{\omega}(z)\leq\widehat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|)^{p-1}=\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}(z)
is valid for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This observation together with (
2.3
) proves (i) with
1\leq p\leq 2
without any hypothesis on the radial weight
\omega
.

If
0<p<1
, then
h_{\omega}\geq\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}
on
\mathbb{D}
, and hence we must argue in a different manner. By the hypothesis
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
we deduce

\int_{r}^{1}\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}(t)\,dt\lesssim\int_{r}^{1}\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{1+t}{2}\right)(1-t)^{p-1}\,dt=2^{p}\int_{\frac{1+r}{2}}^{1}\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}(t)\,dt,\quad 0\leq r<1,

and thus
\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
for each
0<p<\infty
. Therefore the estimate
\|f\|_{D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}}
follows from
[
15
, Theorem 1(b)]
at once if we show that
h_{\omega}(S(a))\lesssim\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}(S(a))
for all
a\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
h_{\omega}
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}
both are radial, we may ignore the angular integral and prove this as follows. By Fubini’s theorem and the hypothesis
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
we deduce

\begin{split}\int_{|a|}^{1}h_{\omega}(s)\,sds&\leq\int_{|a|}^{1}\omega(r)\left(\int_{|a|}^{r}\left(r-s\right)^{p-1}\,ds\right)\,dr=\frac{1}{p}\int_{|a|}^{1}\left(r-|a|\right)^{p}\omega(r)\,dr\\
&\leq\frac{1}{p}\widehat{\omega}(a)(1-|a|)^{p}\lesssim\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{1+|a|}{2}\right)\int_{|a|}^{\frac{1+|a|}{2}}(1-t)^{p-1}\,dt\\
&\leq\int_{|a|}^{1}\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}(t)\,dt,\quad a\in\mathbb{D}.\end{split}

(2.4)

It follows that, for each
0<p<1
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, we have
\|f\|_{D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. This finishes the proof of (i).

To prove (ii) we first observe that, by arguing as in the first part of the proof, an application of (
2.2
) yields
\|f\|^{p}_{D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}}^{p}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. Further, if
1<p<\infty
, then an integration by parts and the hypothesis
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
yield

\displaystyle h_{\omega}(z)

\displaystyle=(p-1)\int_{|z|}^{1}\left(r-|z|\right)^{p-2}\widehat{\omega}(r)\,dr\geq(p-1)\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{3+|z|}{4}\right)\int_{\frac{1+|z|}{2}}^{\frac{3+|z|}{4}}\left(r-|z|\right)^{p-2}\,dr

\displaystyle=\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{3+|z|}{4}\right)(1-|z|)^{p-1}\left(\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{p-1}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{p-1}\right)\gtrsim\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

This pointwise estimate yields
\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
, provided
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. Therefore (ii) is proved.

To this end we observe that the proof above actually shows that, for each
0<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, we have
\|f\|_{D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}}\asymp\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. Therefore there is no loss of information when passing from
D^{p}_{h_{\omega}}
, which arises naturally when (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are applied to dilatations and integrated, to the space
D^{p}_{\widetilde{\omega}_{[p]}}
if
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
.
∎

Our next goal is to show that the inclusions derived from Proposition
4
might be strict unless
p=2
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\omega
be a radial weight and
0<p<\infty
. If
p\neq 2
, then
\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}
and
A^{p}_{\omega}
are two different spaces.

Proof.

If the spaces were the same, then there would exist a constant
C=C(p,\omega)>1
such that

C^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}}\leq\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}^{\omega}_{p}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. But this is impossible because the monomials
m_{n}(z)=z^{n}
satisfy

\frac{\|m_{n}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}^{p}}{2}=\omega_{np+1}=\frac{\|m_{n}\|_{\mathord{\rm HL}^{p}_{\omega}}^{p}}{(n+1)^{2-p}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

and this yields a contradiction as
n\to\infty
.
∎

The following lemma describes the dual of the Dirichlet-type space
D^{p}_{\omega}
when
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. Then
(D^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
via the pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{\omega}}=\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{\omega}}+f(0)\overline{g(0)}

with equivalence of norms.

Proof.

Let us first show that each
g\in D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
induces a bounded linear functional on
D^{p}_{\omega}
.
Hölder’s inequality yields

\begin{split}|\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{\omega}}|&\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\omega(z)\,dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p^{\prime}}\omega(z)\,dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+|f(0)||g(0)|\\
&\lesssim\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\omega}}\|g\|_{D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}},\quad f,g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}).\end{split}

Thus each
g\in D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
induces a bounded linear functional on
D^{p}_{\omega}
via the
D^{2}_{\omega}
-pairing.

Let now
L
be a bounded linear functional on
D^{p}_{\omega}
. By the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 7]
, we have
(A^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq A^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
via the
A^{2}_{\omega}
-pairing with equivalence of norms. For each
f\in D^{p}_{\omega}
, there exists
F=F_{f}\in A^{p}_{\omega}
such that
\mathcal{I}(F)=f-f(0)
, where
\mathcal{I}(F)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}F(\zeta)\,d\zeta
. Further,
\mathcal{I}
is an isometric mapping from
A^{p}_{\omega}
to
D^{p}_{\omega}
, in particular, it is bounded. Therefore the composition
L\circ\mathcal{I}
is a bounded linear functional on
A^{p}_{\omega}
, and hence there exists a unique
G\in A^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
such that
\|G\|_{A^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|L\circ\mathcal{I}\|\lesssim\|L\|
and

\begin{split}L(f)=L(f-f(0)+f(0))&=(L\circ\mathcal{I})(F)+f(0)L(1)\\
&=2\int_{\mathbb{D}}F(z)\overline{G(z)}\omega(z)dA(z)+f(0)L(1)\\
&=2\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)\overline{G(z)}\omega(z)dA(z)+f(0)L(1).\end{split}

Further, since
G\in A^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
, there exists a unique
g\in D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
such that
g^{\prime}=G
and
\overline{g(0)}=L(1)
. Consequently, there exists a unique
g\in D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}
such that

L(f)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}\omega(z)dA(z)+f(0)\overline{g(0)}=\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{\omega}}.

Moreover,
\|g\|^{p^{\prime}}_{D^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}}=\|G\|^{p^{\prime}}_{A^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}}+|L(1)|^{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|L\|^{p^{\prime}}
, and the assertion is proved.
∎

The next lemma shows that, for
0<p<1
, the dual of
D^{p}_{\omega}
can be identified with
\mathcal{Z}
via the
D^{2}_{W}
-pairing where
W
depends on
\omega
appropriately. The definition of
W
is given in (
3.1
) below, and the identity
\widehat{W}(z)=\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|z|)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}
explains why this choice appears to be convenient in concrete calculations. Observe that recently it was shown in
[
20
, Lemma 6]
that
(D^{p}_{p-1})^{\star}\simeq\mathcal{B}^{2}
via the
A^{2}_{\frac{1}{p}-1}
-pairing, provided
0<p\leq 1
. The crucial step in the proof of this last-mentioned duality relies on technical tools related to coefficient multipliers of the Bloch spaces. Since the pairing is different in our setting, we can avoid many tedious calculations all together, and the proof itself becomes more straightforward and transparent via a suitable use of a Carleson embedding theorem for Bergman spaces.

Lemma 9
.

Let
0<p<1
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and

W(z)=W_{p,\omega}(z)=\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|z|)^{\frac{1}{p}-2}+\frac{\omega(z)}{p}\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}(1-|z|)^{\frac{1}{p}-1},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

(3.1)

Then
(D^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq\mathcal{Z}
via the pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{W}}=\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}+f(0)\overline{g(0)}

(3.2)

with equivalence of norms.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. Then Green’s formula and Fubini’s theorem yield

\begin{split}|\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}|&=\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime}(z)\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}W(z)\,dA(z)\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(2\int_{\mathbb{D}}f^{\prime\prime}(rz)r\overline{g^{\prime\prime}(rz)r}\log\frac{1}{|z|}\,dA(z)+f^{\prime}(0)\overline{g^{\prime}(0)}\right)W(r)r\,dr\right|\\
&\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)||g^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)|\left(\int_{|\zeta|}^{1}\log\frac{r}{|\zeta|}W(r)r\,dr\right)\,dA(\zeta)+|f^{\prime}(0)||g^{\prime}(0)|W(\mathbb{D}).\end{split}

where

\int_{|\zeta|}^{1}\log\frac{r}{|\zeta|}W(r)r\,dr\leq\log\frac{1}{|\zeta|}\int_{|\zeta|}^{1}W(r)r\,dr\leq\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{|\zeta|}

by the inequality
-\log t\leq\frac{1}{t}(1-t)
, valid for all
0<t\leq 1
, and the identity
\widehat{W}(z)=\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|z|)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}
. It follows that

\begin{split}|\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}|&\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)|\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}}{|\zeta|}\,dA(\zeta)+|f^{\prime}(0)||g^{\prime}(0)|\\
&\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)|\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\,dA(\zeta)+|f^{\prime}(0)||g^{\prime}(0)|\end{split}

because
M_{1}(r,f^{\prime\prime})
is non-decreasing. An easy calculation based on the Cauchy formula gives
M_{1}(r,h^{\prime})\leq 4M_{1}\left(\frac{1+r}{2},h\right)(1-r)^{-1}
for all
0<r<1
and
h\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
. An application of this to function
h=f^{\prime}
together with the hypothesis
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
gives

|\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(\zeta)|\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{1}{p}-2}\,dA(\zeta)+|f^{\prime}(0)||g^{\prime}(0)|.

(3.3)

If
0<p\leq q<\infty
, then, by
[
15
, Theorem 1(c)]
, the Bergman space
A^{p}_{\omega}
is continuously embedded into the measure space
L^{q}_{\mu}
if and only if
\mu(S)\lesssim\omega(S)^{\frac{q}{p}}
for all Carleson squares
S
. Now that
0<p<1
, we deduce

\begin{split}\int_{S(a)}\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|z|)^{\frac{1}{p}-2}\,dA(z)\leq\widehat{\omega}(a)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|a|)\int_{|a|}^{1}(1-r)^{\frac{1}{p}-2}\,dr\asymp\left(\omega(S(a))\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad|a|\to 1^{-}.\end{split}

This together with (
3.3
) yields

|\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\|f\|_{D^{p}_{\omega}},\quad\text{ for all $f,g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})$}.

(3.4)

Hence each
g\in\mathcal{Z}
induces a bounded linear functional on
D^{p}_{\omega}
via the pairing (
3.2
).

Let
L\in(D^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}
, and recall that
\mathcal{I}(F)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}F(\zeta)\,d\zeta
. Then
|(L\circ\mathcal{I})(F)|\lesssim\|\mathcal{I}(F)\|_{D^{p}_{\omega}}=\|F\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}
for all
F\in A^{p}_{\omega}
.
Therefore
L\circ\mathcal{I}\in(A^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}
. It is known by
[
21
, Theorem 1]
that
(A^{p}_{\omega})^{\star}
is isomorphic to the Bloch space via the
A^{2}_{W}
-pairing.
Hence there exists a unique
G\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\|G\|_{\mathcal{B}}\lesssim\|L\circ\mathcal{I}\|\lesssim\|L\|
and
(L\circ\mathcal{I})(F)=\langle F,G\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}
for all
F\in A^{p}_{\omega}
.
Moreover, for each
f\in D^{p}_{\omega}
, there exists
F=F_{f}\in A^{p}_{\omega}
such that
\mathcal{I}(F)=f-f(0)
. Therefore

\begin{split}L(f)&=L(f-f(0)+f(0))=L(f-f(0))+f(0)L(1)\\
&=L(\mathcal{I}(F))+f(0)L(1)=\langle F,G\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}+f(0)L(1)\\
&=\langle f^{\prime},G\rangle_{A^{2}_{W}}+f(0)L(1),\quad f\in D^{p}_{\omega}.\end{split}

By picking up
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
such that
g^{\prime}=G
and
g(0)=\overline{L(1)}

we deduce
L(f)=\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{W}}
for all
f\in D^{p}_{\omega}
, and
\|g\|_{\mathcal{Z}}=\|G\|_{\mathcal{B}}+|L(1)|\lesssim\|L\|
.
∎

We next give an identification, useful for our purposes, for the dual space of
D^{1}_{\omega}
in the case when

. To this end, for a radial weight

\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)=\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\big(\|f_{r}\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}}\widehat{\omega}(r)\big)<\infty\}.

Let now
\mathord{\rm BMOA}^{\prime}(\infty,\omega)
denote space of primitives of functions in
\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)
endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}^{\prime}(\infty,\omega)}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)}+|f(0)|.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. Then
(D^{1}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq\mathord{\rm BMOA}^{\prime}(\infty,\omega)
via the pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}}=\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}}+f(0)\overline{g(0)}

with equivalence of norms.

Proof.

First observe that
\omega\widehat{\omega}\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, and

(\omega_{x})^{2}\asymp\left(\widehat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)^{2}\asymp\widehat{\omega\widehat{\omega}}\left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right)\asymp(\omega\widehat{\omega})_{x},\quad x\geq 1,

by Lemma
A
(iii). This together with
[
13
, Theorem 4]

gives
(A^{1}_{\omega})^{\star}\simeq\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)
via the
A^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}
-pairing. Therefore

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\omega(z)\widehat{\omega}(z)\,dA(z)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)}\|f\|_{A^{1}_{\omega}},\quad g\in\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega),\quad f\in A^{1}_{\omega},

and hence

\displaystyle|\langle f,g\rangle_{D^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}}|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle f^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle_{A^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}}|+|f(0)||g(0)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g^{\prime}\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}(\infty,\omega)}\|f^{\prime}\|_{A^{1}_{\omega}}+|f(0)||g(0)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathord{\rm BMOA}^{\prime}(\infty,\omega)}\|f\|_{D^{1}_{\omega}},\quad g\in\mathord{\rm BMOA}^{\prime}(\infty,\omega),\quad f\in D^{1}_{\omega}.

Thus each
g\in\mathord{\rm BMOA}^{\prime}(\infty,\omega)
induces a bounded linear functional on
D^{1}_{\omega}
via the
D^{2}_{\omega\widehat{\omega}}
-pairing.

The proof can be completed by analogous arguments to those used in the second part of the proof of Lemma
9
. The only extra ingredient needed is

-pairing. Since the details do not give us anything new, we omit them.
∎

Lemma 11

We begin with the following lemma which concerns the range

. It proves the equivalence between (ii) and (iii) in Theorem
1
, and since
\omega_{x}\asymp\widehat{\omega}\left(1-\frac{1}{x}\right)
due Lemma

(iii), it also gives an equivalent condition in terms of moments.

Lemma 12
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{(1-r)^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}<\infty
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left((1-r)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2(k-1)}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)<\infty
;

(iii)

I:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow D^{1}_{0}
is bounded.

Similarly, the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\frac{(1-r)^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}=0
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to 1^{-}}\left((1-r)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2(k-1)}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)=0
;

(iii)

I:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow D^{1}_{0}
is compact.

Proof.

We first observe that in both sets of statements (i) and (iii) are equivalent for all
0<p\leq 1
by the Carleson embedding theorems
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
19
, Theorem 3]
. We next show that (i) and (ii) are equivalent. Direct calculations show that

\begin{split}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2(k-1)}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}&\asymp\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{r^{2x}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{x})^{\frac{1}{p}}x^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{\frac{2}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(t)^{\frac{1}{p}}}dt=I_{1}(r)+I_{2}(r),\end{split}

(4.1)

where

I_{1}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{r^{\frac{2}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(t)^{\frac{1}{p}}}dt\quad\textrm{and}\quad I_{2}(r)=\int_{r}^{1}\frac{r^{\frac{2}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(t)^{\frac{1}{p}}}dt.

Moreover,

I_{1}(r)\lesssim\frac{(1-r)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\lesssim\int_{r}^{\frac{1+r}{2}}\frac{r^{\frac{2}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(t)^{\frac{1}{p}}}dt\leq I_{2}(r),\quad r\to 1^{-}.

(4.2)

Further, by Lemma
A
(ii) and the change of variable
r^{\frac{2}{1-t}}=s
, we deduce

\begin{split}I_{2}(r)&\lesssim\left(\frac{(1-r)^{\beta}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\int_{r}^{1}r^{\frac{2}{1-t}}(1-t)^{-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}dt\\
&=\left(\frac{(1-r)^{\beta}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}2^{1-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{1-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\int_{0}^{r^{\frac{2}{1-r}}}\left(\log\frac{1}{s}\right)^{\frac{1+\beta}{p}-2}\,ds\\
&\leq\left(\frac{(1-r)^{\beta}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}2^{1-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\left(\log\frac{1}{r}\right)^{1-\frac{1+\beta}{p}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\log\frac{1}{s}\right)^{\frac{1+\beta}{p}-2}\,ds\\
&\asymp\frac{(1-r)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\quad r\to 1^{-}.\end{split}

(4.3)

By combining (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2(k-1)}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\asymp\frac{(1-r)^{1-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\quad r\to 1^{-}.

(4.4)

Hence (ii) is equivalent to (iii) for all
0<p\leq 1
.
∎

We next prove the boundedness part for the case
0<p<1
of Theorem
1
. As an immediate consequence we deduce that if
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and

then
T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only.

Theorem 13
.

Let
0<p<1
,
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
and
g\in H^{\infty}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
consists of constant functions only;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\frac{(1-r)^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{\frac{1}{p}}}=\infty
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq r<1}\left((1-r)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2(k-1)}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)=\infty
;

(iv)

I:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow D^{1}_{0}
is unbounded.

Proof.

First observe that (ii)-(iv) are equivalent by Lemma
12
. We next show that (i) and (iii) are equivalent. A careful inspection of the proof of
[
4

shows that it can be applied to the space

in the case

even if it is not a Banach space. By this observation and Lemma

,
T_{g}:D^{p}_{\omega}\rightarrow H^{\infty}
is bounded if and only if
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|G^{D^{2}_{W}}_{g,z}\|_{\mathcal{Z}}<\infty
, where

\overline{G^{D^{2}_{W}}_{g,z}(w)}=\int_{0}^{z}g^{\prime}(\zeta)\overline{K^{D^{2}_{W}}_{\zeta}(w)}\,d\zeta
and

is the reproducing kernel of

at the point
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
.

Assume (iii) holds. A direct calculation shows that

\begin{split}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|G^{D^{2}_{W}}_{g,z}\|_{\mathcal{Z}}&\geq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|w|^{2})|G^{D^{2}_{W}}_{g,z})^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(w)|\right)\\
&=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\sup_{w\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|w|^{2})\left|\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k-1}{2kW_{2k-1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\widehat{g}(n+1)}(n+1)\overline{z}^{n+k+1}}{n+k+1}\right)w^{k-2}\right|\right)\\

Then Fubini’s theorem and Hardy’s inequality yield

\begin{split}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|G^{D^{2}_{W}}_{g,z}\|_{\mathcal{Z}}&\gtrsim\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(k-1)r^{2k+n-1}}{2k(n+k+1)W_{2k-1}}\right)\overline{\widehat{g}(n+1)}(n+1)e^{i\theta(n+3)}\right|d\theta\\
&\gtrsim\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2k+n-1}}{(n+k+1)W_{2k-1}}\right)|\overline{\widehat{g}(n+1)}|.\end{split}

If
g\in T(D^{p}_{\omega},H^{\infty})
is not a constant, then there exists an

such that
\widehat{g}(N+1)\neq 0
. Since
W\in\mathcal{D}
by the proof of

imply

for all

. Now that
\widehat{g}(N+1)\neq 0

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\|G^{D^{2}_{W}}_{g,z}\|_{\mathcal{Z}}\gtrsim\limsup_{0\leq r<1}\left((1-r)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2k}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)=\infty.

This contradiction shows that (i) is satisfied.

Conversely assume that (iii) does not hold. We claim that then

contains all polynomials. As a matter of fact, if

&\asymp\sup_{0\leq r<1}(1-r)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2(k-1)}}{\widehat{\omega}(1-\frac{1}{k})^{\frac{1}{p}}k^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}<\infty.\end{split}

Thus (i) and (iii) are equivalent, and the proof if complete.

12
. We next prove (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) by showing that if

is compact and

line by line, with minor modifications, gives

(4.5)

whenever

. Therefore, by using the first part of the proof of Theorem
13
, we obtain

\begin{split}\sup_{a,z\in\mathbb{D}}&\int_{\mathbb{D}\setminus D(0,R)}|(G_{g,z}^{D^{2}_{W}})^{\prime\prime}(w)|^{2}(1-|\varphi_{a}(w)|^{2})^{2}\,dA(w)\\

\displaystyle 0

&\asymp\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{2^{j+1}}}{2^{(j+1)p^{\prime}}(\omega_{2^{j+1}})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\left\|\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\widehat{g}(n+1)}(n+1)\overline{z}^{n+2}}{n+k+2}\right)\zeta^{k}\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{H^{p^{\prime}}}\end{split}

from which Fubini’s theorem and M. Riesz projection theorem yield

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle>\sup_{0\leq s<1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2^{j+1}}}{2^{(j+1)p^{\prime}}(\omega_{2^{j+1}})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\widehat{g}(n+1)}(n+1)(se^{it})^{n+2}}{n+k+2}\right)e^{ik\theta}\right|^{p^{\prime}}dt~d\theta

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq s<1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2^{j+1}}}{2^{(j+1)p^{\prime}}(\omega_{2^{j+1}})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\widehat{g}(n+1)}(n+1)\left(\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\frac{e^{ik\theta}}{n+k+2}\right)(se^{it})^{n+2}\right|^{p^{\prime}}d\theta~dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sup_{0\leq s<1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2^{j+1}}}{2^{(j+1)p^{\prime}}(\omega_{2^{j+1}})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\overline{\widehat{g}(N+1)}(N+1)\left(\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\frac{e^{ik\theta}}{N+k+2}\right)(se^{it})^{N+2}\right|^{p^{\prime}}d\theta~dt

\displaystyle\asymp\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{(j+1)p^{\prime}}(\omega_{2^{j+1}})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\frac{e^{ik\theta}}{N+k+2}\right|^{p^{\prime}}d\theta.

Now, by
[
17
, Lemma E]
and
[
4
, Lemma 2.7]
, with
M_{n}=2^{n}
and
M_{n+1}=2^{n+1}
, we deduce

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{k=2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\frac{e^{ik\theta}}{N+k+2}\right|^{p^{\prime}}d\theta\asymp 2^{-(j+1)},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

Consequently, since
\omega\in\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
, we finally obtain

\infty>\|T_{g}\|^{p^{\prime}}_{D^{p}_{\omega}}\gtrsim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{(j+1)(p^{\prime}+1)}(\omega_{2^{j+1}})^{p^{\prime}-1}}\asymp\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{p^{\prime}+2}\omega_{k}^{p^{\prime}-1}},

which is contradicts (iii), and thus (i) is satisfied.

We complete the proof by showing that (ii) and (iv) are equivalent. By
[
15
, Theorem 1]
,
I:D^{p^{*}}_{\widehat{\omega}_{[x]}}\rightarrow D^{\frac{p^{*}}{p}}_{y}
is bounded if and only if

\begin{split}\infty&>\int_{\mathbb{D}}\widehat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|)^{x}\left(\int_{\Gamma(z)}\frac{(1-|\zeta|)^{y}}{\widehat{\omega}_{[x]}(S(\zeta))}\,dA(\zeta)\right)^{p^{\prime}}dA(z)\\
&\asymp\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\omega}(r)(1-r)^{x}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{(r-s)(1-s)^{y}}{\widehat{\omega}(s)(1-s)^{x+2}}s\,ds\right)^{p^{\prime}}dr=I_{\omega}.\end{split}

On one hand, by Lemma
A
(ii), there exists a constant
\beta=\beta(\omega)>0
such that

\displaystyle I_{\omega}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int^{1}_{0}\frac{(1-r)^{x+\beta p^{\prime}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{r-s}{(1-s)^{\beta+x+2-y}}s\,ds\right)^{p^{\prime}}dr\asymp\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{x+p^{\prime}y-xp^{\prime}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}dr.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle I_{\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{x}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\frac{r-s}{(1-s)^{x+2-y}}s\,ds\right)^{p^{\prime}}dr\asymp\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-r)^{x+p^{\prime}y-xp^{\prime}}}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}dr.

Since
x=p(y-1)
, (ii) and (iv) are equivalent.
∎

with

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dr}{\widehat{\omega}(r)^{p^{\prime}-1}}=\infty.

(6.8)

This last result extends

4
, Theorem 1.3]
from the setting of regular weight to the whole doubling class
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
. In the case

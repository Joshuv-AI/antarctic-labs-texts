Non-equilibrium-diffusion limit of the compressible Euler radiation model Thanks: Corresponding author: Zhengce Zhang Thanks: Keywords: Non-equilibrium-diffusion limit; Radiation hydrodynamics; General initial data; Initial layer. Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 76N99; 35M33; 35Q30
Qiangchang Ju, Lei Li and Zhengce Zhang

Abstract
. We justify rigorously the non-equilibrium-diffusion limit of the compressible Euler model coupled with a radiative transfer equation arising in radiation hydrodynamics. For general initial data, we establish the uniform existence of the solution to the coupled model in 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} and prove the convergence of the solutions to the limiting system in the nonequilibrium-diffusion regime. Moreover, the initial layer for the radiative density is constructed to get the strong convergence in L ∞ L^{\infty} norm.



1.
Introduction

The Euler system coupled with a radiative transfer equation (see
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
) can describe the motion of a compressible inviscid fluid under the presence of the radiative field as follows

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho\vec{u})+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u}\otimes\vec{u})+\nabla_{x}P=-\vec{S}_{F},\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho e)+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho e\vec{u})+P\mathrm{div}_{x}\vec{u}=-S_{E}+\vec{S}_{F}\cdot\vec{u},\\
&\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}f+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=S.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Here the unknowns
\rho,\vec{u}=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})
and
e
denote the density, velocity, and specific internal energy, respectively.
f=f(t,\vec{x},\nu,\vec{w})
is the radiative intensity which depends on time
t\geq 0
, the special variable
\vec{x}\in\Omega:=\mathbb{T}^{3}=(\mathbb{R}\setminus(2\pi\mathbb{Z}))^{3}
, the frequency
\nu\geq 0
and direction
\vec{w}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
of photons with
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
being the unit sphere. The pressure
P=P(\rho,\theta)
and the internal energy
e=e(\rho,\theta)
are smooth functions of
\rho
and the temperature
\theta
.

We suppose that the radiative source term
S
in
(\ref{research equations-x-1})_{4}
is given by

S=\sigma_{a}[B(\nu,\theta)-f(t,\vec{x},\nu,\vec{w})]+\sigma_{s}\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f(t,\vec{x},\nu,\vec{w})\mathrm{d}\vec{w}-f(t,\vec{x},\nu,\vec{w})\Big),

where
\sigma_{a}=\sigma_{a}(\nu,\theta)
and
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{s}(\nu,\theta)
are (given) nonnegative functions and represent the absorption and the scattering coefficient, respectively. Moreover, the radiative equilibrium function
B(\nu,\theta)
is given by

B(\nu,\theta)=2h\nu^{3}c^{-2}\Big(e^{\frac{h\nu}{k_{B}\theta}}-1\Big)^{-1},

where
c,k_{B}
and
h
are the speed of light, the Boltzmann and Planck constants, respectively. Furthermore, the radiative flux
\vec{S}_{F}
and the radiative energy source
S_{E}
are defined by

\vec{S}_{F}=\frac{1}{c}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\vec{w}S\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\nu

and

S_{E}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}S\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\nu.

The system (
1.1
) is a simplified model in radiation hydrodynamics
[
10
]
. More realistic systems have been proposed by
Lowrie, Morel and Hittinger
[
9
]
and Buet and Després
[
2
]
.

In order to identify the appropriate limit regime, we nondimensionalize the system (
1.1
) as in
[
3
,
6
]
by setting

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widehat{t}=\frac{t}{T_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{\vec{x}}=\frac{\vec{x}}{L_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{\vec{u}}=\frac{\vec{u}}{U_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{\rho}=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{\theta}=\frac{\theta}{\theta_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{P}=\frac{P}{P_{\infty}},\\
&\widehat{e}=\frac{e}{e_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{\sigma_{a}}=\frac{\sigma_{a}}{\sigma_{a,\infty}},~~~\widehat{\sigma_{s}}=\frac{\sigma_{s}}{\sigma_{s,\infty}},~~~\widehat{f}=\frac{f}{f_{\infty}},~~~\widehat{\nu}=\frac{\nu}{\nu_{\infty}}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Here we use
T_{\infty},L_{\infty},U_{\infty},\rho_{\infty},\theta_{\infty},P_{\infty}
and
e_{\infty}
to denote the reference hydrodynamical quantities (time, length, velocity, density, temperature, pressure, energy). Furthermore, we use
f_{\infty},\nu_{\infty},\sigma_{a,\infty},
and
\sigma_{s,\infty}
to denote the reference radiative quantities (radiative intensity, frequency, absorption and scattering coefficients). Besides, we use the carets to symbolize nondimensional quantities. Moreover, we also assume the compatibility conditions
P_{\infty}=\rho_{\infty}e_{\infty},\nu_{\infty}=\frac{k_{B}\theta_{\infty}}{h},f_{\infty}=\frac{2h\nu_{\infty}^{2}}{c^{2}}
and we denote by

S_{r}:=\frac{L_{\infty}}{T_{\infty}U_{\infty}},~~~\;Ma:=\frac{U_{\infty}}{\sqrt{P_{\infty}/\rho_{\infty}}},~~~\;\mathcal{C}:=\frac{c}{U_{\infty}}

the Strouhal, Mach and “infrarelativistic” numbers corresponding to hydrodynamics, and by

\mathcal{L}:=L_{\infty}\sigma_{a,\infty},~~~\;\mathcal{L}_{s}:=\frac{\sigma_{s,\infty}}{\sigma_{a,\infty}},~~~\;\mathcal{P}:=\frac{2k_{B}^{4}\theta_{\infty}^{4}}{h^{3}c^{3}\rho_{\infty}e_{\infty}},

the various dimensionless numbers corresponding to radiation. Using above scalings and omitting the carets, we can write (
1.1
) as follows

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{r}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u})=0,\\
&S_{r}\partial_{t}(\rho\vec{u})+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u}\otimes\vec{u})+\frac{1}{Ma^{2}}\nabla_{x}P=-\frac{\mathcal{P}}{Ma^{2}}\vec{S}_{F},\\
&S_{r}\partial_{t}(\rho e)+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho e\vec{u})+P\mathrm{div}_{x}\vec{u}=-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{C}S_{E}+\mathcal{P}\vec{S}_{F}\cdot\vec{u},\\
&\frac{S_{r}}{\mathcal{C}}\partial_{t}f+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=S,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{F}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\vec{w}S\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\nu,~~~\;S_{E}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}S\mathrm{d}\vec{w}\mathrm{d}\nu,\\
&S=\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}(B(\nu,\theta)-f)+\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma_{s}(\overline{f}-f),\\
&B(\nu,\theta)=\frac{\nu^{3}}{e^{\frac{\nu}{\theta}}-1},~~~\;\overline{f}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f\mathrm{d}\vec{w}.\end{aligned}\right.

In the present paper, we consider the grey hypothesis such that the transport coefficients
\sigma_{a}
and
\sigma_{s}
are independent of the frequency
\nu
. For simplicity, we assume that
\sigma_{a}
and
\sigma_{s}
are two positive constants in the following derivation. It is worthy to note that the general case
\sigma_{a}=\sigma_{a}(\rho,\theta)
and
\sigma_{s}=\sigma_{s}(\rho,\theta)
can be dealt with similarly. Integrating the radiative transfer equation in (
1.2
) with respect to the frequency
\nu
, and noticing that

\int_{0}^{\infty}B(\nu,\theta)\mathrm{d}\nu=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\nu^{3}}{e^{\frac{\nu}{\theta}}-1}\mathrm{d}\nu=C\theta^{4}:=B(\theta)

for some positive constant
C
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{Sr}{\mathcal{C}}\partial_{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\mathrm{d}\nu+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\mathrm{d}\nu=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}\Big(B(\theta)-\int_{0}^{\infty}f\mathrm{d}\nu\Big)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma_{s}\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\vec{w}-\int_{0}^{\infty}f\mathrm{d}\nu\Big).

Moreover, if we denote
\int_{0}^{\infty}f\mathrm{d}\nu
by
f
, then the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{Sr}{\mathcal{C}}\partial_{t}f+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}(B(\theta)-f)+\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma_{s}\Big(\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f\mathrm{d}\vec{w}-f\Big).

Defining
\langle f\rangle=\overline{f}
, we can rewrite (
1.2
) after careful calculations as follows

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &S_{r}\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u})=0,\\
&S_{r}\partial_{t}(\rho\vec{u})+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u}\otimes\vec{u})+\frac{1}{Ma^{2}}\nabla_{x}P=\frac{\mathcal{P}}{Ma^{2}}(\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}+\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma_{s})\langle\vec{w}(f-\overline{f})\rangle,\\
&S_{r}\partial_{t}(\rho e)+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho e\vec{u})+P\mathrm{div}_{x}\vec{u}=-\mathcal{P}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}(B(\theta)-\overline{f})\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt-\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}+\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma_{s})\langle\vec{w}(f-\overline{f})\rangle\cdot\vec{u},\\
&\frac{Sr}{\mathcal{C}}\partial_{t}f+\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mathcal{L}\sigma_{a}(B(\theta)-f)+\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}_{s}\sigma_{s}(\overline{f}-f).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

The main purpose of the present paper is to identify the non-equilibrium diffusion regime for the system (
1.2
) in a three-dimensional torus. So, we put
\mathcal{L}_{s}=1/\varepsilon^{2}
and
\mathcal{L}=\varepsilon
. For the strong under-relativistic flow, we put
\mathcal{C}=1/\varepsilon
. We also assume that a certain amount of radiation is present, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}=1
, and the Mach number is fixed, i.e.
Ma=1
. Moreover, we put
S_{r}=1
in the previous system. We shall focus on the fluids obeying the ideal polytropic gas relations

(1.4)

e=C_{V}\theta,P=R\rho\theta,

where the specific gas constant
R
and the specific heat at constant volume
C_{V}
are positive constants. For simplicity, we take
\sigma_{a},\sigma_{s},C_{V}
and
R
in (
1.3
) to be one. As a result, it is equivalent to proving the limit of the solution
(f^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon},\vec{u}^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})
to the initial value problem of the following system

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}\vec{u}^{\varepsilon})=0,~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon}\vec{u}^{\varepsilon})+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}\vec{u}^{\varepsilon}\otimes\vec{u}^{\varepsilon}+\rho^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{I}_{3\times 3})=\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\Big)\langle\vec{w}(f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}^{\varepsilon})\rangle,~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon})+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon}\vec{u}^{\varepsilon})+\rho^{\varepsilon}\theta^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{div}_{x}\vec{u}^{\varepsilon}=\overline{f}^{\varepsilon}-B(\theta^{\varepsilon})\\
&\hskip 196.32384pt-\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\Big)\langle\vec{w}(f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}^{\varepsilon})\rangle\cdot\vec{u}^{\varepsilon},~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\
&{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}+f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}^{\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}^{2}f^{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}^{2}B(\theta^{\varepsilon}),~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&\rho^{\varepsilon}(0,\vec{x})=\rho^{0}(\vec{x}),\;\;\vec{u}^{\varepsilon}(0,\vec{x})=\vec{u}^{0}(\vec{x})~~\;\mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega,\\
&\theta^{\varepsilon}(0,\vec{x})=\theta^{0}(\vec{x}),\;\;\;f^{\varepsilon}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=h(\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\;\mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega\\
\end{aligned}\right.

as
\varepsilon
tends to zero.

The first theorem in the paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
h(\vec{x},\vec{w})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2};W_{2}^{6}(\Omega))
and
\rho^{0}(\vec{x}),\vec{u}^{0}(\vec{x}),\theta^{0}(\vec{x})\in W_{2}^{6}(\Omega)

\varepsilon
, such that the problem (
1.5
) has a unique solution
(f^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon},\vec{u}^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})
satisfying
(f^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon},\vec{u}^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})\in G=\{(f,\rho,\vec{u},\theta),\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};W_{2}^{6}(\Omega)))}+\|(\rho,\vec{u},\theta)\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{2}^{6}(\Omega))}\leq r\}
for some positive constant

. Furthermore, the unique solution
(f^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon},\vec{u}^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon})
satisfies

(1.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2};W_{2}^{5}(\Omega)))}=0,

and

(1.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|(\overline{f}^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f},\rho^{\varepsilon}-\rho,\vec{u}^{\varepsilon}-\vec{u},\theta^{\varepsilon}-\theta)\|_{C^{0}([0,T];W_{2}^{5}(\Omega))}=0,

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\mathrm{div}_{x}(\rho\vec{u})=0,~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T)\times\Omega,\\

Theorem 1.2
.

For

and

obtained in Theorem

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}-f_{I,0}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0

for

.

There are many classical results
[
1

4

about the single neutron transport equation with physical boundary. Instead of the single neutron equation, the coupled model (

2.

In this section, we consider the well-posedness of the system (

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+f-\overline{f}+{\varepsilon}^{2}f=F(t,\vec{x},\vec{w})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ (0,\infty)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\

Define the
L^{2}
,
L^{\infty}
and

norms in

&\theta_{2}(0,\vec{x})=\widehat{\theta_{2}}(\infty,\vec{x}),\;\;\overline{f}_{2}(0,\vec{x})=\mathcal{F}_{2}(\infty,\vec{x})~~\mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that
B_{2}(\theta_{0};\theta_{2})=4\theta_{2}\theta_{0}^{3}+6\theta_{1}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}
. Similar to the analysis of system (
4.24
), we can obtain that there exists a solution
(\overline{f}_{2},\rho_{2},\vec{u}_{2},\theta_{2})\in(L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{2}^{4}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W_{2}^{5}(\Omega)))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{2}^{4}(\Omega))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{2}^{4}(\Omega))\times L^{\infty}(0,T;W_{2}^{4}(\Omega))
about the system (
4.32
).

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We divide the proof into four steps:

Step 1.
Remainder definitions.
We may rewrite the asymptotic expansion as follows:

(4.33)

f^{\varepsilon}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}f_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}f_{I,k}.

The remainder can be defined as

\displaystyle R_{N}

\displaystyle=f^{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}f_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}f_{I,k}

\displaystyle=f^{\varepsilon}-Q_{N}-\mathcal{D}_{I,N},

where

Q_{N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}f_{k}

and

\mathcal{D}_{I,N}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}f_{I,k}.

We note that the equation
(\ref{research equations})_{1}
is equivalent to the equation
(\ref{research equation in the initial layer})_{1}
, we write
\mathcal{L}
to denote the transport operator as follows:

(4.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}f^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}+f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2}f^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f^{\varepsilon}}{\partial\tau}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\varepsilon}+(f^{\varepsilon}-\overline{f}^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{2}f^{\varepsilon}.

Step 2.
Estimates of
\mathcal{L}Q_{2}.

(4.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{t}Q_{2}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}Q_{2}+(Q_{2}-\overline{Q}_{2})+\varepsilon^{2}Q_{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}B(\theta_{0})+\varepsilon^{3}\partial_{t}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{4}\partial_{t}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{4}f_{2}.

We have

(4.36)

\|\varepsilon^{3}\partial_{t}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{4}\partial_{t}f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{3},

(4.37)

\|\varepsilon^{3}\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{3}

and

(4.38)

\|\varepsilon^{3}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{4}f_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{3}.

Step 3.
Estimates of
\mathcal{L}D_{I,2}.

(4.39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mathcal{D}_{I,2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{D}_{I,2}}{\partial\tau}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathcal{D}_{I,2}+(\mathcal{D}_{I,2}-\overline{\mathcal{D}_{I,2}})+\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{D}_{I,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{I,0}}{\partial\tau}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{I,0}+(f_{I,0}-\overline{f}_{I,0})+\varepsilon^{2}f_{I,0}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\Big(\frac{\partial f_{I,1}}{\partial\tau}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{I,1}+(f_{I,1}-\overline{f}_{I,1})+\varepsilon^{2}f_{I,1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\frac{\partial f_{I,2}}{\partial\tau}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{I,2}+(f_{I,2}-\overline{f}_{I,2})+\varepsilon^{2}f_{I,2}\Big).

Since
B(\theta^{0}+\theta_{I,0})-B(\theta^{0})=0,
we have

(4.40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mathcal{D}_{I,2}=\varepsilon^{3}f_{I,1}+\varepsilon^{3}\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{I,2}+\varepsilon^{4}f_{I,2}.

Furthermore,

(4.41)

\|\varepsilon^{3}f_{I,1}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{3}\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{I,2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{3},

(4.42)

\|\varepsilon^{4}f_{I,2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C\varepsilon^{4},

combining with (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and (
4.18
).

Step 4.
Uniform convergence

In summary, since
\mathcal{L}f^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{2}B(\theta^{\varepsilon}),
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{L}f^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{L}Q_{2}-\mathcal{L}\mathcal{D}_{I,2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|B(\theta^{\varepsilon})-B(\theta_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+C\varepsilon^{3},

by collecting (
4.35
)
\sim
(
4.38
) and (
4.39
)
\sim
(
4.42
).

Now we consider the estimate of
\|B(\theta^{\varepsilon})-B(\theta_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}
. Since
\overline{f}_{0}=\overline{f}
and
\theta_{0}=\widehat{\theta}
by the uniqueness of system (
1.8
), we obtain

(4.43)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\|\theta^{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}\|_{C^{0}([0,T];W_{2}^{5}(\Omega))}=0.

Since
\|\theta^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq C(r)
, we conclude that

(4.44)

\|B(\theta^{\varepsilon})-B(\theta_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq C(r)\|\theta^{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}\|_{C^{0}([0,T];W_{2}^{5}(\Omega))}.

Finally, we consider the asymptotic expansion to
N=2
where the remainder
\mathcal{R}_{2}
satisfies the following equation

(4.45)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}^{2}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{2}+\varepsilon\vec{w}\cdot\nabla_{x}\mathcal{R}_{2}+\mathcal{R}_{2}-\overline{\mathcal{R}_{2}}+{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathcal{R}_{2}=\mathcal{L}\mathcal{R}_{2}\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ (0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2},\\
&\mathcal{R}_{2}(0,\vec{x},\vec{w})=0\ \ \mathrm{in}\ \ \Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

By Lemma 3.1 we have

\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|\mathcal{L}\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq C(r)\|\theta^{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}\|_{C^{0}([0,T];W_{2}^{5}(\Omega))}+C\varepsilon.

Since

\Big\|\sum_{k=1}^{2}\varepsilon^{k}f_{k}^{\varepsilon}+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\varepsilon^{k}f_{I,k}^{\varepsilon}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times S^{2})}=O(\varepsilon),

we have

\|f^{\varepsilon}-f_{0}-f_{I,0}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq O(\varepsilon)+C(r)\|\theta^{\varepsilon}-\theta_{0}\|_{C^{0}([0,T];W_{2}^{5}(\Omega))},

which implies (
1.9
) by the fact that
\overline{f}=\overline{f}_{0}=f_{0}
.

Approximation by Nörlund means with respect to Vilenkin system in Lebesgue spaces
N. Anakidze, N. Areshidze, L. Baramidze

Abstract
. In this paper we improve and complement a result by Móricz and Siddiqi [ 19 ] . In particular, we prove that their estimate of the Nörlund means with respect to the Vilenkin system holds also without their additional condition. Moreover, we prove a similar approximation result in Lebesgue spaces for any 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty .

42C10, 42B30.

Key words and phrases:
Vilenkin group, Vilenkin system, Fejér means, Nörlund means, approximation.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.
Let

m=:(m_{0},m_{1},...)

be a sequence of positive integers not less than 2. Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,...,m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}.
Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{k}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{k}}\textrm{'s}
.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{ \ }(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}m_{k}<+\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded Vilenkin group. If
\{m_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
sequance is unbounded, then
G_{m}
is said to be unbounded Vilenkin group. In this paper we consider only bounded Vilenkin groups.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by the sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{k},\dots)\qquad\left(\text{ }x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
G_{m}
, namely

\displaystyle I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},\ \ \ I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},\dots,y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(x\in G_{m},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}).

For the simplicity we also define by
I_{n}
as
I_{n}:=I_{n}(0).

Let us define a generalized number system based on
m
in the following way:

M_{0}=:1,\quad M_{k+1}=:m_{k}M_{k}\quad(k\in\mathbb{N})

Then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as

n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{j}M_{j},\ \ \ \text{where }\ \ \ n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}\ \ \ (j\in\mathbb{N})

and only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero. Let

|n|=:\max\{j\in\mathbb{N},n_{j}\neq 0\}.

In 1947 Vilenkin
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
investigated a group
G_{m}
and introduced the Vilenkin systems
\{{\psi}_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
as

\psi_{n}\left(x\right):=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\text{ \quad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

where
r_{k}(x)
are the generalized Rademacher functions defined by

r_{k}(x):=\exp(2\pi ix_{k}/m_{k}),\text{ \ \ }\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

These systems include as a special case the Walsh system when
m_{k}=2
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The norms (or quasi-norms) of Lebesgue spaces
L_{p}(G_{m})
are defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}^{p}:=\int_{G_{m}}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu.

The Vilenkin system is orthonormal and complete in
L^{2}\left(G_{m}\right)\,
(for details see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
30
]
).

If
f\in L^{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
, we can define the Fourier
coefficients, the partial sums of the Fourier series, the Fejér means, the Dirichlet and Fejér kernels
with respect to the Vilenkin system in
the usual manner:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{k}d\mu,\,\text{\quad}\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

\displaystyle S_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k},\text{
\quad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\text{ }S_{0}f:=0\right),

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}f,\text{ \quad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k},\text{ \quad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_{k},\text{ \quad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).\

Recall that (for details see e.g.
[
1
]
and
[
28
]
),

(4)

\quad\hskip 0.0ptD_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ }M_{n},\text{\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if\thinspace\thinspace}x\in I_{n},\\
\text{ }0,\text{\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if
\thinspace\thinspace}x\notin I_{n},\end{array}\right.

(5)

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}-j}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)-\overline{\psi}_{M_{n}-1}(-x)D_{j}(-x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)-\psi_{M_{n}-1}(x)\overline{D_{j}}(x),\,\,\ 0\leq j<M_{n}.

(6)

n\left|K_{n}\right|\leq 2\sum_{l=0}^{|n|}M_{l}\left|K_{M_{l}}\right|,

and

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{G_{m}}K_{n}(x)d\mu(x)=1,\ \ \ \ \ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}}\left|K_{n}(x)\right|d\mu(x)\leq 2.

Moreover, if
n>t,

t,n\in\mathbb{N},
then

(8)

K_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{M_{t}}{1-r_{t}(x)},&x\in I_{t}\backslash I_{t+1},\quad x-x_{t}e_{t}\in I_{n},\\
\frac{M_{n}+1}{2},&x\in I_{n},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The
n
-th Nörlund mean
t_{n}
of the Vilenkin-Fourier series of a integrable function
f
is defined by

(9)

t_{n}f:=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}S_{k}f,

where

Q_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}q_{k}.

Here
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is a sequence of nonnegative numbers, where
q_{0}>0
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{n}=\infty.

Then the summability method (
9
) generated by
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
is regular if and only if (see
[
18
]
)

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=0.

In this paper we investigate regular Nörlund means only.

It is well-known (for details see e.g.
[
28
]
) that every Nörlund summability method generated by non-increasing sequence
(q_{k},k\in\mathbb{N})
is regular, but Nörlund means generated by non-decreasing sequence
(q_{k},k\in\mathbb{N})
is not always regular.

The representation

t_{n}f\left(x\right)=\underset{G_{m}}{\int}f\left(t\right)F_{n}\left(x-t\right)d\mu\left(t\right)

play central roles in the sequel, where

(10)

F_{n}=:\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}D_{k}

is called the kernels of the Nörlund means.

If we invoke Abel transformation we get the following identities:

(11)

\displaystyle Q_{n}:=\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=0}{\sum}}q_{j}=\overset{n}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}q_{n-j}\cdot 1=\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j+q_{0}n

and

(12)

t_{n}f=\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\left(\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\left(q_{n-j}-q_{n-j-1}\right)j\sigma_{j}f+q_{0}n\sigma_{n}f\right).

3.
Formulation of Main Results

Based on estimate (
1
) we can prove our next main results:

Theorem 1
.

Let
M_{N}\leq n<M_{N+1}
and
{{t}_{n}}
be a regular Nörlund mean generated by non-decreasing sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\},
in sign
q_{k}\uparrow.
Then, for some
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p<\infty,

\|t_{n}f-f\|_{p}\leq\frac{3R^{3}}{Q_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}M_{i}q_{n-M_{i}}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{M_{i}},f\right)+2R^{3}\omega_{p}\left(\frac{1}{M_{N}},f\right).

Theorem 2
.

Let
{{t}_{n}}
be Nörlund mean generated by non-increasing sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, in sign
q_{k}\downarrow
.
Then, for some
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|t_{M_{n}}f-f\|_{p}\leq 3R^{2}\sum_{s=0}^{n}\frac{M_{s}}{M_{n}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{s},f\right)+C\overset{n-1}{\underset{s=0}{\sum}}\frac{(n-s)M_{s}}{M_{n}}\frac{q_{M_{s}}}{q_{M_{n}}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{s},f\right).

Theorem 3
.

Let
M_{N}\leq n<M_{N+1}
and
{{t}_{n}}
be Nörlund mean generated by non-increasing sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
, in sign
q_{k}\downarrow
, satisfying the condition

(13)

\frac{1}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\text{\ \ as \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

Then, for some
f\in L^{p}(G_{m}),
where
1\leq p<\infty,

\displaystyle\|t_{n}f-f\|_{p}\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{M_{j}}{M_{N}}\omega_{p}\left(1/M_{j},f\right).

As a consequence we obtain the following similar result proved in Móricz and Siddiqi
[
19
]
:

Corollary 1
.

Let
\{q_{k}:k\geq 0\}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers such
that in case
q_{k}\uparrow
condition

(14)

\frac{q_{n-1}}{Q_{n}}=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\text{\ \ as \ \ }n\rightarrow\infty.

is satisfied, while in case
q_{k}\downarrow
condition (
13
) is satisfied. If
f\in Lip(\alpha,p)
for some
\alpha>0
and
1\leq p<\infty,
then

(15)

\|t_{n}f-f\|_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ }O(n^{-\alpha}),\text{\hskip 20.44434pt\hskip 20.44434ptif\hskip 20.44434pt}0<\alpha<1,\\
\text{ }O(n^{-1}\log n),\text{\hskip 20.44434ptif
\hskip 10.22217pt\ }\alpha=1,\\
\text{ }O(n^{-1}),\text{\hskip 20.44434pt\hskip 20.44434ptif \hskip 10.22217pt\ \ }\alpha>1,\end{array}\right.

As a consequence we obtain the following similar result proved in Móricz and Siddiqi
[
19
]
:

Corollary 2
.

a) Let
{{t}_{n}}
be Nörlund means generated by non-decreasing sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfying regularity condition (
14
). If
f\in Lip(\alpha,p)
for some
\alpha>0
and
1\leq p<\infty,
then
{t}_{n}f
converge to
f
in
L_{p}(G_{m})
norm.

b) Let
{{t}_{n}}
be Nörlund mean generated by non-increasing sequence
\{q_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfying condition (
13
).
If
f\in Lip(\alpha,p)
for some
\alpha>0
and
1\leq p<\infty,
then
{t}_{n}f
converge to
f
in
L_{p}(G_{m})
norm.

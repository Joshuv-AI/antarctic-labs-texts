Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Gauss map of rotational hypersurfaces in 4-dimensional Lorentz-Minkowski space concerning the linear second order differential operators L 1 L_{1} and L 2 L_{2} , where L 1 L_{1} is usually called as the Cheng-Yau operator. We obtain some classifications of rotational hypersurfaces which have L k L_{k} -harmonic Gauss map, L k L_{k} -pointwise 1-type Gauss map and generalized L k L_{k} 1-type Gauss map, where k = 1 , 2 k=1,2 .

1.
Introduction

A crucial area of research in differential geometry is the study of
submanifolds of semi-Riemannian space forms whose coordinate functions are eigenfunctions of the Laplace
operator. Takahashi has
examined isometric immersions in the Euclidean space whose coordinate
functions are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator, associated to the same
eigenvalue
\lambda
in
[
20
]
, where it was proved that if an isometric immersion

x:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
of a Riemannian
n
-manifold
M
in a Euclidean

(n+k)
-space satisfies
\Delta x=\lambda x
for some constant
\neq 0
, then

\lambda
must necessarily be positive and
x
defines a minimal immersion in a
sphere
S^{n+k+1}
of a radius
\sqrt{n/k}
in
\mathbb{E}^{n+k}
. After this study, Garay has established an
extension of Takahashi’s theorem in 1990 and he has proved that if

x:M^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is an immersed hypersurface in Euclidean
space with
\Delta x=Ax
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)}
is a constant
diagonal matrix, then
M
is minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
or an open
piece of a round hypersphere or an open piece of generalized right spherical
cylinders
[
8
]
. Also, Dillen et al. have considered surfaces in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose immersion satisfes the condition
\Delta x=Ax+b
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is a constant matrix and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a constant vector in

[
7
]
. Afterward, using different techniques, the result of Dillen et
al. has been independently generalized to hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
in

[
4
]
and
[
10
]
.

The Laplace operator
\Delta
of a hypersurface
M
embedded in
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
is an
intrinsically defined second-order linear differential operator which
can be viewed as the first operator in the
sequence of operators
L_{0}=\Delta,\ L_{1},\ldots,\ L_{n-1}

represents the linearized operator of the first
variation of the
(k+1)
-th mean curvature, which arises from the normal
variations of the hypersurface defined by

L_{k}(f)=tr(P_{k}\circ\nabla^{2}f),

where
\nabla^{2}f
is the hessian of
f
and
P_{k}
denotes the
k
-th Newton transformation associated with
the shape operator of the hypersurface.
Note that the operator
L_{1}
is usually called as the Cheng-Yau operator,
[
2
,
6
,
18
,
19
]
. In this context, an extension of
Takahashi theorem for the linearized operators

has been given in
[
1
]
, where it is proved that a hypersurface of the Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}
satisfying

L_{k}x=Rx
is an open part of a sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Later, this study was moved into the hypersurfaces of non-flat Riemannian space forms in in
[
23
]
.

On the other hand, the study of Gauss map of submanifolds by considering eigenfunctions of the linearized operators
L_{k}
have initiated by Chen and Piccini in
[
5
]
, where they consider submanifolds with 1-type Gauss map. After that, some important classes of hypersurfaces of semi-Euclidean spaces were studied in many papers by concerning the type of their Gauss maps. For example, in
[
17
]

surfaces of revolution and canal surfaces with
generalized Cheng-Yau 1-type Gauss map have been obtained. Moreover,
hypersurfaces in non-flat pseudo-Riemannian space forms satisfying have
been studied in
[
14
]
. Also, pointwise 1-type Gauss map of surfaces in

\mathbb{E}_{1}^{3}
concerning Cheng-Yau operator has been given in
[
13
]
,
spacelike hypersurfaces in the Lorentz-Minkowski space satisfying

L_{k}x=Rx+b
have been classified in
[
22
]
, hypersurfaces in non-flat
Lorentzian space forms satisfying
L_{k}\psi=A\psi+b
have been studied in

[
15

and normalized null hypersurfaces in nonflat Lorentzian space

L_{k}x=Ux+b
have been introduced in
[
21
]
.
Cheng-Yau operator and Gauss map of surfaces of revolution in
\mathbb{E}^{3}
and
Cheng–Yau operator and Gauss map of rotational hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{E}^{4}

have been studied in
[
12

and

-pointwise

In this paper, we give some classifications for Gauss maps of

Linearized Operators for the Gauss Map of Hypersurfaces in

Consider an isometric immersion

, where

. Note that if

(resp.

is Lorentzian (resp. Riemannian). Moreover, in
[
16
]
the equation

L_{k}N=-\varepsilon C_{k}\Big(\nabla H_{k+1}+\left(nH_{1}H_{k+1}-\left(n-k-1\right)H_{k+2}\right)N\Big)

is obtained, where

is the
k
-th mean curvature of
M
,
defined by

\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right)H_{k}=\left(-\varepsilon\right)^{k}a_{k},

(2.2)

and the constants
a_{k}
and

are given by

\left.\begin{array}[c]{l}a_{1}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i},\\
\\
a_{k}=(-1)^{k}\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<...<i_{k}}^{n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\kappa_{i_{2}}...\kappa_{i_{k}},~k=2,3,...,n\end{array}\right\}

(2.3)

and

C_{k}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}n\\
k+1\end{array}\right)(-\varepsilon)^{k}.

(2.4)

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
be non-zero smooth functions on
M
,
C\in\mathbb{E}_{1}^{n+1}
be a non-zero constant vector and
k\in\{0,1,2,...,n-1\}
. If the
Gauss map

of an oriented submanifold

in
\mathbb{E}_{1}^{4}
satisfies

i:

L_{k}N=0
, then

is called
L_{k}
-harmonic;

ii:

L_{k}N=\mathfrak{m}N,
then

has first kind
L_{k}
-pointwise
1-type Gauss map;

iii:

L_{k}N=\mathfrak{m}(N+C),
then

has second kind
L_{k}
-pointwise 1-type Gauss map;

iv:

L_{k}N=\mathfrak{m}N+\mathfrak{n}C,

has generalized

L_{k}
1-type Gauss map.

Now, consider the case
n=3
and let
M
be an oriented hypersurface in
\mathbb{E}_{1}^{4}
with a local coordinat system
\{s,t,w\}
. Then, the gradient of a smooth function

is defined by

\nabla f=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{g}}\left(\nabla_{1}\partial_{s}+\nabla_{2}\partial_{t}+\nabla_{3}\partial_{w}\right),

(2.5)

where we put

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{13}^{2}g_{22}-2g_{12}g_{13}g_{23}+g_{11}g_{23}^{2}+g_{12}^{2}g_{33}-g_{11}g_{22}g_{33},

\displaystyle\nabla_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{23}^{2}-g_{22}g_{33}\right)f_{s}+\left(-g_{13}g_{23}+g_{12}g_{33}\right)f_{t}+\left(g_{13}g_{22}-g_{12}g_{23}\right)f_{w},

\displaystyle\nabla_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-g_{13}g_{23}+g_{12}g_{33}\right)f_{s}+\left(g_{13}^{2}-g_{11}g_{33}\right)f_{t}+\left(-g_{12}g_{13}+g_{11}g_{23}\right)f_{w},

\displaystyle\nabla_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{13}g_{22}-g_{12}g_{23}\right)f_{s}+\left(-g_{12}g_{13}+g_{11}g_{23}\right)f_{t}+\left(g_{12}^{2}-g_{11}g_{22}\right)f_{w}

and
g_{11}=\left\langle\partial s,\partial s\right\rangle,

g_{12}=\left\langle\partial s,\partial t\right\rangle,

g_{13}=\left\langle\partial s,\partial w\right\rangle,

g_{22}=\left\langle\partial t,\partial t\right\rangle,

g_{23}=\left\langle\partial t,\partial w\right\rangle,

2.2.
Rotational hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{E}_{1}^{4}

In this subsection we recall some geometric characterizations of rotational

\mathbb{E}_{1}^{4}

The rotational hypersurface
\Gamma^{s}
which is obtained by rotating the

(0,0,0,1)

given by

\Gamma^{s}(s,t,w)=\left(s\cosh t\cosh w,s\sinh t,s\cosh t\sinh w,f(s)\right),

where

.

With the aid of the first differentials of (
2.6
) with respect to

s,

t
and
w
, the Gauss map

of the rotational hypersurface
(
2.6
) is obtained by

N^{s}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}\left(f^{\prime}\cosh t\cosh w,f^{\prime}\sinh t,f^{\prime}\cosh t\sinh w,1\right)

(2.7)

and so,

\varepsilon^{s}=\left\langle N^{s},N^{s}\right\rangle=1.

(2.8)

Also, the nonzero components of the coefficients in the first fundamental form
of (
2.6
) are

g_{11}^{s}=f^{\prime 2}-1,\text{ }g_{22}^{s}=s^{2},\text{ }g_{33}^{s}=s^{2}\cosh^{2}t

(2.9)

and the principal curvatures of (
2.6
) are

\kappa_{1}^{s}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{\left(1-f^{\prime 2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\text{ }\kappa_{2}^{s}=\kappa_{3}^{s}=\frac{f^{\prime}}{s\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}.

(2.10)

On the other hand, the rotational hypersurface
\Gamma^{t}
which is obtained by rotating the
profile curve
\alpha^{t}(s)=(f(s),0,0,s)
about timelike axis
(1,0,0,0)
is
given by

\Gamma^{t}(s,t,w)=\left(f(s),-s\cos t\sin w,-s\sin t,s\cos t\cos w\right),

(2.11)

where
s\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\},

0\leq t,w\leq 2\pi
.

Moreover, the Gauss map
N^{t}
of the rotational hypersurface
(
2.11
) is defined by

N^{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f^{\prime 2}-1}}\left(1,-f^{\prime}\cos t\sin w,-f^{\prime}\sin t,f^{\prime}\cos t\cos w\right)

(2.12)

and so

\varepsilon^{t}=\left\langle N^{t},N^{t}\right\rangle=1.

(2.13)

Also, the nonzero components of the coefficients in the first fundamental form
of (
2.11
) are

g_{11}^{t}=1-f^{\prime 2},\text{ }g_{22}^{t}=s^{2},\text{ }g_{33}^{t}=s^{2}\cos^{2}t

(2.14)

and the principal curvatures of (
2.11

\kappa_{1}^{t}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{\left(f^{\prime 2}-1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\text{ }\kappa_{2}^{t}=\kappa_{3}^{t}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{s\sqrt{f^{\prime 2}-1}}.

(2.15)

Finally, the rotational hypersurface
\Gamma^{l}
which is obtained by rotating the
profile curve
\alpha^{l}(s)=(s,f(s),0,0)
about lightlike axis
(1,1,0,0)
is
given by

\Gamma^{l}(s,t,w)=\begin{array}[c]{l}\left(\left(\frac{t^{2}+w^{2}}{2}+1\right)s-\frac{t^{2}+w^{2}}{2}f(s),\frac{t^{2}+w^{2}}{2}s+\left(1-\frac{t^{2}+w^{2}}{2}\right)f(s),\right.\\
\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }st-f(s)t,sw-f(s)w\big),\end{array}

(2.16)

where
s\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
.

It turns out that the Gauss map
N^{l}
of the rotational hypersurface
(
2.16
) is

N^{l}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}\begin{array}[c]{l}\big(t^{2}+w^{2}-\left(t^{2}+w^{2}+2\right)f^{\prime},t^{2}+w^{2}-2-\left(t^{2}+w^{2}\right)f^{\prime},\\
\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }2t\left(1-f^{\prime}\right),2w\left(1-f^{\prime}\right)\big)\end{array}

(2.17)

and so

\varepsilon^{l}=\left\langle N^{l},N^{l}\right\rangle=1.

(2.18)

Also, the nonzero components of the coefficients in the first fundamental form
of (
2.16
) are

g_{11}^{l}=f^{\prime 2}-1,\text{ }g_{22}^{l}=\left(s-f\right)^{2},\text{
}g_{33}^{l}=\left(s-f\right)^{2}

(2.19)

and the principal curvatures of (
2.16
) are

\kappa_{1}^{l}=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{\left(1-f^{\prime 2}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\text{ }\kappa_{2}^{l}=\kappa_{3}^{l}=\frac{f^{\prime}-1}{\left(s-f\right)\sqrt{1-f^{\prime 2}}}.

(2.20)

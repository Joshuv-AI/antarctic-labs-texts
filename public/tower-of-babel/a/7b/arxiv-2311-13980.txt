On the Permutation-Representation Number of Bipartite Graphs using Neighborhood Graphs
Khyodeno Mozhui, K. V. Krishna

Abstract
The problems of determining the permutation-representation number ( prn ) and the representation number of bipartite graphs are open in the literature. Moreover, the decision problem corresponding to determination of the prn of a bipartite graph is NP-complete. However, these numbers were established for certain subclasses of bipartite graphs, e.g., for crown graphs. Further, it was conjectured that the crown graphs have the highest representation number among the bipartite graphs. In this work, first, we reconcile the relation between the prn of a comparability graph and the dimension of its induced poset and review the upper bounds on the prn of bipartite graphs. Then, we study the prn of bipartite graphs using the notion called neighborhood graphs. This approach substantiates the aforesaid conjecture and gives us theoretical evidence. In this connection, we devise a polynomial-time procedure to construct a word that represents a given bipartite graph permutationally. Accordingly, we provide a better upper bound for the prn of bipartite graphs. Further, we construct a class of bipartite graphs, viz., extended crown graphs, defined over posets and investigate its prn using the neighborhood graphs.

Introduction

The class of word-representable graphs, which includes a wide range of graphs such as comparability graphs, circle graphs, and 3-colorable graphs, is an important class of graphs with intriguing properties. The monograph
[
23
]
provides a comprehensive account of word-representable graphs, their connections to other contexts, and contributions to the topic. Finding the representation number of an arbitrary word-representable graph is a hard problem, and many authors have focused on determining the numbers for various subclasses.

The class of comparability graphs – the graphs which admit transitive orientations – is an important subclass of word-representable graphs. Indeed, the class of comparability graphs is precisely the class of permutationally representable graphs, each of which can be represented by a concatenation of permutations of its vertices
[
25
]
. The minimum number of such permutations of vertices of a comparability graph is called the permutation-representation number (
prn
) of the graph. Every comparability graph induces a poset based on one of its transitive orientations. The
prn
of a comparability graph is precisely the dimension of the corresponding induced poset (cf. Section
3
). For
3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, it is known that determining whether a poset with
n
elements has dimension
k
is NP-complete
[
35
]
. Therefore, it is NP-complete to determine whether an
n
-element comparability graph has
prn

k
.

The class of complete graphs is precisely the class of graphs having the representation number and the
prn
equal to one. While the class of graphs with representation number at most two is characterized by the class of circle graphs in
[
18
]
, the class of permutation graphs precisely has the
prn
at most two (cf.
[
28
]
). Although examples of graphs with greater representation numbers and
prn
exist, in both contexts, no characterization of graphs is available for other numbers.

A bipartite graph is a comparability graph that is precisely isomorphic to the cover graph of its induced poset. Determining the representation number and
prn
of bipartite graphs are open problems. In
[
35
]
, it was shown that deciding whether a bipartite graph has
prn

k
, for fixed
k\geq 4
is NP-complete. Further, in
[
11
]
, it was established that deciding whether a bipartite graph has
prn
three is also NP-complete. Accordingly, in the literature, various authors have focused on determining these numbers or bounds on these numbers for specific subclasses of bipartite graphs. For example, these numbers are determined for even cycles, trees, and crown graphs (cf.
[
15
,
17
,
28
]
). The representation number of prisms, ladder graphs, outerplanar graphs,
G_{k}
graphs, and complete bipartite graphs are known (cf.
[
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
). Upper bounds for the representation number of hypercubes, 3-colorable graphs, etc., were determined in
[
5
,
17
]
. Certain bounds on the
prn
of bipartite graphs are given through the notion called boxicity in
[
1
]
. However, determining boxicity for bipartite graphs is NP-hard
[
6
]
. The upper bound on the
prn
of bipartite graphs given in
[
4
]
is the best known in the literature.

In this paper, we reconcile the relation between the
prn
of a comparability graph and the dimension of its induced poset, and observe that they are the same. Accordingly, we present the classes of graphs for which the

are known (see Section
3

In this paper, we only consider the graphs which are simple (i.e., without loops or parallel edges) and undirected. To distinguish between two-letter words and edges of graphs, we write

An orientation of a graph is said to be a transitive orientation if the adjacency relation on the vertices in the resulting directed graph is transitive. A graph

A bipartite graph with bipartition

A graph

A graph

For a complete bipartite graph

with at least three vertices, we have
\mathcal{R}^{p}(K_{m,n})=\mathcal{R}(K_{m,n})=2
. It was shown in
[

, see the proof of Theorem 4]

) and in
[
15
]
that
\mathcal{R}(H_{n,n})=\lceil n/2\rceil

), where

n

.

A set
P
with a partial order, say
<
on
P
, is called a partially ordered set or simply a poset. Two elements

are comparable if
a<b
or
b<a
; otherwise, they are incomparable. A chain in

is a set of pairwise comparable elements of

, whereas an antichain is a set of pairwise incomparable elements of

. A chain cover of a poset

is a collection of disjoint chains whose union is

. The width of

, denoted by

, is the size of a largest antichain in

. It was established in
[
8
]
that for any finite poset, the size of a smallest chain cover is equal to the width of the poset, known as Dilworth’s Theorem. A polynomial-time method for determining the width and a smallest chain cover of a poset was provided in
[
12
,
20
]
.

A linear order of a set

is a partial order on

in which all elements of
P
form a chain. A collection of linear orders

of a poset

is called a realizer of

\cap^{k}_{i=1}L_{i}
is the partial order on

, i.e., for every

,
a<b
in

if and only if
a<b
in all

. The dimension of a poset

is the smallest positive integer
k
such that
P
has a realizer of size
k
, denoted by
\dim(P)

[
9
]
.

A comparability graph
G
induces a poset denoted by
P_{G}
based on the chosen transitive orientation. The cover graph of a poset
P
, denoted by
G_{P}
, is the graph whose vertex set is the set
P
, and two elements
a,b\in P
are adjacent in

if and only if
a<b
in
P
and there is no element

in
P
such that
a<c<b
. The Hasse diagram of a poset

It is clear from the following result that the

of a disconnected graph is equal to the maximum of the respective numbers of its components.

Lemma 1
(
[
4
]
)

Let

and

be graphs with

can be permutationally represented by
p_{1}q_{1}p_{2}q_{2}\cdots p_{k-1}q_{k-1}q_{k}p_{k}q_{k+1}p_{k}\cdots q_{l}p_{k}
.

Corollary 1

Suppose
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G)=k
and
\mathcal{R}^{p}(H)=l
. Then,
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G\cup H)=\max\{k,l\}
.

Accordingly, it is sufficient to focus on determining the
prn
of connected bipartite graphs. In this paper, unless specified otherwise, a bipartite graph with bipartition
\{A,B\}
, written
G=(A\cup B,E)
, is a connected graph.

Comparability Graphs: Dimension vs

In this section, we reconcile the results that reveal the connection between the
prn
of a comparability graph and the dimension of its induced poset, and observe that they are the same. Accordingly, we present the upper bounds for the

of comparability graphs, particularly for bipartite graphs. First, we recall the following result.

Lemma 2
(
[

If
G

\mathcal{R}^{p}(G)\leq k
if and only if
\dim(P_{G})\leq k

In fact, it was shown in

is precisely a set of permutations of vertices of

Corollary 2

If
G
is a comparability graph, then
\mathcal{R}^{p}(G)=k
if and only if
\dim(P_{G})=k
.

For

4
General Properties of Permutations

In this section, we prove some general properties of permutations used in representing a bipartite graph. These properties will play a vital role in constructing the respective permutations. Let
G=(A\cup B,E)
be a bipartite graph represented by
p_{1}\cdots p_{k}
, where
p_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq k
) is a permutation of the vertices of
G
.

First, we show that a vertex adjacent to two vertices will not appear in between them in any permutation
p_{i}
.

Lemma 3

If
\overline{ab},\overline{cb}\in E
, then
abc\not\leq p_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof

Suppose
abc\leq p_{t}
for some
t
. By the choice of
a
and
c
, they are not adjacent in the bipartite graph
G
. Hence, as
ac\leq p_{t}
, there exists a permutation
p_{s}
such that
ca\leq p_{s}
. Further, since
\overline{ab}\in E
and
ab\leq p_{t}
, we have
ab\leq p_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Consequently,
cb\leq p_{s}
. This is not possible, as
\overline{cb}\in E
and
bc\leq p_{t}
. Hence,
abc\not\leq p_{i}
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
. ∎

As a consequence of Lemma
3
, we observe that if any vertex adjacent to a vertex
a
appears on the right side (or left side) of
a
in a permutation
p_{i}
, then every other vertex adjacent to
a
also appears on the right side (respectively, left side) of
a
in
p_{i}
.

Lemma 4

For
\overline{ab}\in E
, if
ab\leq p_{i}
, then
ab^{\prime}\leq p_{i}
, for all
b^{\prime}\in N(a)
and
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof

For some
b^{\prime}\in N(a)
, if
b^{\prime}a\leq p_{i}
, then
b^{\prime}ab\leq p_{i}
. But by Lemma
3
, this is not possible. Hence,
ab^{\prime}\leq p_{i}
. ∎

We now prove a result on appearance of neighbors of two vertices, say
a
and
c
, in a permutation, when they have common neighbors. If the neighbors of
a
appear on the right side (or left side) of
a
in a permutation
p_{i}
, then the neighbors of
c
also appear on the right side (respectively, left side) of
c
in any
p_{i}
.

Lemma 5

Suppose
N(a)\cap N(c)\neq\varnothing
, for some vertices
a,c
of
G
. For
1\leq i\leq k
and
b\in N(a)
, if
ab\leq p_{i}
, then
cb^{\prime}\leq p_{i}
, for all
b^{\prime}\in N(c)
.

Proof

For
b\in N(a)
, suppose
ab\leq p_{i}
. Let
b^{\prime\prime}\in N(a)\cap N(c)
. By Lemma
4
,
ab^{\prime\prime}\leq p_{i}
. If
b^{\prime\prime}c\leq p_{i}
, then
ab^{\prime\prime}c\leq p_{i}
. This is not possible by Lemma
3
. Hence,
cb^{\prime\prime}\leq p_{i}
. Again, by Lemma
4
,
cb^{\prime}\leq p_{i}
, for all
b^{\prime}\in N(c)
. ∎

5
Upper Bound for
prn

In this main section, we present a polynomial-time procedure to obtain permutations of the vertices of a bipartite graph
G
and provide an upper bound for the
prn
of
G
.

A graph is said to be a reduced graph if no two vertices have the same neighborhood. Every graph can be reduced by considering only the vertices with distinct neighborhoods and removing the remaining vertices. In
[
4
]
, it was shown that the
prn
of a bipartite graph and the
prn
of its reduced graph are the same by constructing their respective words. Accordingly, in what follows, we consider reduced bipartite graphs and discuss their
prn
.

5.1
Polynomial-time Procedure

Let
{S}=\{a_{i_{1}},a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{k}}\}
be a set with indices
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
. We write
\underline{S}
to represent the word
a_{i_{k}}a_{i_{k-1}}\cdots a_{i_{1}}
in which the symbols of
S
appear exactly once and are arranged in such a way that the indices are in decreasing order. Similarly, we write
\overline{S}
to represent the word
a_{i_{1}}a_{i_{2}}\cdots a_{i_{k}}
in which the symbols of
S
appear exactly once and are arranged such that the indices are in increasing order.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
A\subseteq V
. We define a neighborhood graph of
G
with respect to
A
as the directed graph
\mathcal{N}_{A}(G)=(A,E^{\prime})
, where
\overrightarrow{ab}\in E^{\prime}
if and only if
N(a)\subseteq N(b)
for all
a,b\in A
. If
A=V
, we may simply call it as a neighborhood graph of
G
, and it is denoted by
\mathcal{N}(G)
.

Remark 1

Since the set containment relation is transitive, the graph
\mathcal{N}_{A}(G)
is a comparability graph, and hence, it induces a poset. In the rest of the paper, the poset
P_{\mathcal{N}_{A}(G)}
induced by
\mathcal{N}_{A}(G)
is simply denoted by
P_{A}
.

Let
G=(A\cup B,E)
be a bipartite graph. Consider a chain cover of
P_{A}
as per the following:

X_{1}:a_{11}<a_{12}<a_{13}<\cdots<a_{1n_{1}}

X_{2}:a_{21}<a_{22}<a_{23}<\cdots<a_{2n_{2}}

\vdots

X_{k}:a_{k1}<a_{k2}<a_{k3}<\cdots<a_{kn_{k}}

Corresponding to each chain
X_{i}
, we create a permutation
p_{i}
of vertices of
A\cup C
, where
C
is a relabeled set of
B
as described below.

For a set of vertices
U
and a vertex
a
, let
U_{-a}=U\setminus N(a)
, the set of non-adjacent vertices of
a
in the set
U
.

Consider one of the chains, say
X_{1}
, and construct a permutation, say
p_{1}^{\prime}
, as per the following. In
X_{1}
, since
N(a_{1(i-1)})\subsetneq N(a_{1i})
, we write the exclusive neighbors of
a_{1i}
, i.e.,
N(a_{1i})_{-a_{1(i-1)}}
, on the right side of
a_{1i}
and on the left side of
a_{1(i-1)}
. Then, we write the vertices in
B
which are not adjacent to any of the vertices in
X_{1}
, i.e.,
B_{-a_{1n_{1}}}
, on the left side of
a_{1n_{1}}
. Now we write the remaining vertices of
G
, i.e., the vertices of the chains
X_{2},\ldots,X_{k}
, on the left side of
B_{-a_{1n_{1}}}
. Although the vertices of
N(a_{1i})_{-a_{1(i-1)}}
and
B_{-a_{1n_{1}}}
can be written in any order, we will write them in the increasing order as per their indices. Thus,

p_{1}^{\prime}=x_{1}\overline{B_{-a_{1n_{1}}}}a_{1n_{1}}\overline{N(a_{1n_{1}})_{-a_{1(n_{1}-1)}}}a_{1(n_{1}-1)}\cdots a_{12}\overline{N(a_{12})_{-a_{11}}}a_{11}\overline{N(a_{11})},

where
x_{1}=X_{2}^{{}^{\text{o}}}\cdots X_{k}^{{}^{\text{o}}}
. Here, for
1\leq j\leq k
,
X_{j}^{{}^{\text{o}}}=a_{j1}a_{j2}\cdots a_{jn_{j}}
, the word obtained by writing the vertices of
X_{j}
in the increasing order as per the ordering of
P_{A}
.

Consider
p^{\prime}_{1_{B}}
the subword of
p_{1}^{\prime}
with the vertices of
B
. Relabel the word
p^{\prime}_{1_{B}}
as
c_{1}c_{2}\cdots c_{m}
(where
m=|B|
) with indices increasing from left to right. Thus,
C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\}
is the relabeled set of
B
. Now let
p_{1}
be the permutation obtained from
p_{1}^{\prime}
by replacing the relabeled vertices from

Extended Crown Graphs

In this section, we provide a class of bipartite graphs, called extended crown graphs, for which the
prn
can be investigated in view of Theorem
5.3
. Thus, extended crowns graphs are a class of bipartite graphs whose
prn
attains the upper bound given in this work. In this section, we also provide a partial characterization for extended crown graphs of
prn
three.

An extended crown graph is constructed by extending a crown graph based on a given poset. Let
P
be a poset. We construct a bipartite graph
G=(A\cup B,E)
on
2n
vertices, where
|P|=n
, as per the following procedure:

1.

Let
A
be the set of elements of
P
and
B=\{v^{\prime}\;:\;v\in A\}
.

2.

Consider a largest antichain
M=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\}\subseteq A
of the poset
P
.

3.

Let
M^{\prime}=\{a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{k}^{\prime}\}\subseteq B
.

4.

For
1\leq i,j\leq k
and
i\neq j
, put
\{a_{i},a_{j}^{\prime}\}\in E
, i.e., construct a crown graph of
2k
vertices with
M
and
M^{\prime}
as partite sets.

5.

Then, for all
v\in A\setminus M
, put
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E
.

6.

Further, for all
u,v\in A
, if
u<v
in
P
, then iteratively make the vertices of
N(u)
adjacent to
v
, i.e., for all
w\in N(u),\{w,v\}\in E
.

7.

The resultant graph
G=(A\cup B,E)
is an extended crown graph.

10
Conclusion

In this paper, we observed that the notions of the
prn
of a comparability graph and the dimension of its induced poset are the same, and accordingly, we reconciled the
prn
of some comparability graphs. We have employed the notion of a neighborhood graph to provide a cubic-time computational procedure for constructing permutations of the vertices of a given bipartite graph whose concatenation represents the bipartite graph permutationally. As a result, we establish a better upper bound for the
prn
of a bipartite graph compared to the best-known upper bound given in
[
4
]
. In fact, the given procedure estimates the
prn
of certain bipartite graphs. Further, based on our observations, the representation number of a specific type of bipartite graph is conjectured as per the following:

Conjecture 1

Let
G
be a bipartite graph. For
\kappa_{0}\geq 5
, if
H_{\kappa_{0},\kappa_{0}}
is a largest induced crown graph in
G
, then
\mathcal{R}(G)
equals
\lceil\frac{\kappa_{0}}{2}\rceil
or
\lceil\frac{1+\kappa_{0}}{2}\rceil
.

This works opens up some specific questions towards finding the
prn
of various subclasses of comparability graphs. For instance, one may target to characterize the graph classes that correspond to the poset classes whose dimensions are known in the literature. Further, it is interesting to extend the given computational procedure to comparability graphs in general. In line with the contributions in this paper, we also ask some specific questions related to the class of extended crown graphs: 1) Whether an extended crown graph defined by a poset of width two is a circle graph? 2) Characterize the posets of width at least three in terms of the
prn
(and representation number) of their extended crown graphs.

Completely regular codes with covering radius 1 1 and the second eigenvalue in 3 3 -dimensional Hamming graphs
Ivan Mogilnykh, Anna Taranenko, Konstantin Vorob’ev

Abstract
We obtain a classification of the completely regular codes with covering radius 1 1 and the second eigenvalue in the Hamming graphs H ⁡ ( 3 , q ) H(3,q) up to q q and intersection array. Due to works of Meyerowitz, Mogilnykh and Valyuzenich, our result completes the classifications of completely regular codes with covering radius 1 1 and the second eigenvalue in the Hamming graphs H ⁡ ( n , q ) H(n,q) for any n n and completely regular codes with covering radius 1 1 in H ⁡ ( 3 , q ) H(3,q) .



1
Introduction

The purpose of this study is completing the classification of the completely regular codes in
H(n,q)
with covering radius
1
and the second eigenvalue that was initiated in
[
12
]
. Completely regular codes with covering radius
1
are known under various different names, e.g. equitable
2
-partitions, perfect
2
-colorings, and intriguing sets
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
12
]
. The concept of a completely regular code, suggested by Delsarte
[
7
]
, is traditionally considered in classical association schemes. For a survey on completely regular codes in Hamming and Johnson graphs we refer to
[
3
]
. In
[
2
]
a more specific case of covering radius
1
(equitable
2
-partitions) in Hamming graphs is studied in details.

In recent years, several new approaches for studying special classes of completely regular codes in different association schemes were suggested.
Some papers focus on codes of small strength
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
, other target codes with minimum possible eigenvalue
[
13
]
or linear codes of small covering radius
[
4
]
.

We grade
q
-ary
completely regular codes by their strengths as
orthogonal arrays. It is well-known that the strength is determined by the largest eigenvalue of the code in the following manner. A completely regular code has strength
i
if its maximum eigenvalue, different from the graph valency, has index
i+1
as eigenvalue of the Hamming graph
[
7
]
. The completely regular codes of strength
0
in the Hamming graphs were classified up to isomorphism by Meyerowitz in
[
11
]
.

We say that the position
j
is
essential
for a code (set)
C
in
H(n,q)
if there are vertices from
C
and the complement of
C
different only in
j
th position. The eigenvalues of the graph
H(n,q)
are

\lambda_{i}(n,q)=n(q-1)-qi

with multiplicity
{n\choose i}(q-1)^{i}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
(see, for example,
[
5
]
). We say that a code in
H(n,q)
is
reduced
if all its positions are essential.
The following fact is well-known, see e.g.
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
,
[
3
, Proposition 33(ii)]
,
[
12
, Lemma 2]
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
C
be a code in
H(n,q)
with nonessential position

j
. The code
C
is completely regular with covering radius
\rho
, intersection array
A
and eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}(n,q):i\in I\}
if and only if
the code in
H(n-1,q)
obtained by deleting
j
th position in the
tuples of
C
is completely regular with covering radius
\rho
, intersection array
A
and eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}(n-1,q):i\in I\}
.

Two constuctions of completely regular codes with covering radius
1
were introduced in
[
12
]
.
One is based on alphabet liftings of two cycles in the Hamming graph
H(4,2)
, see also
[
2
]
.
The idea behind the second method, called permutation switching construction is as follows. We choose a specially chosen completely regular code in
H(2,q)
and
add nonessential positions. Then we apply coordinate permutations (switchings) to certain subcodes of this code. The obtained code has from
3

to
\frac{q}{2}+1
essential positions so it is nonisomorphic to the original code with two essential coordinates, while both codes have the same parameters.
The following theorem was proven for the completely regular codes with the second eigenvalue, the details on both mentioned constructions below could be found in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1
.

[
12
]

All reduced completely regular codes in
H(n,q)
with covering radius
1
, second eigenvalue and at least four essential positions are obtained by permutation switchings or alphabet liftings of a cycle in
H(4,2)
. The intersection arrays of these codes have only even entries.

The theorem above reduces the classification to the case of
H(3,q)
, which we tackle in this work. Our study shows that there are many nonisomorphic codes with the same parameters (
q
and intersection array), so we narrow the goal to classification up to parameters. Meanwhile, we still obtain complete description of these objects in certain cases.

The paper is organized as follows. We provide necessary definitions and background results in Sections
2
and
3
. Four constructions (Constructions A-D) of completely regular codes with new parameters are given in Section
4
, two of which (Construction A and B) were known in the literature, while Constructions C and D are new and provide a rich family of new codes, sufficient to cover all open parameter cases. Construction D can be considered as a generalization of the splitting construction II from
[
2
]
for
3
-dimensional Hamming graphs.

As a simple consequence of Construction
A
we characterize all completely regular codes for odd
q
up to parameters (Proposition
6
).
Construction C covers all codes of sizes at least
\frac{q^{3}}{4}
in the Hamming graph
H(3,q)
up to parameters for even
q
.
In Section
5
, we show that Construction D gives a complete description for completely regular codes with covering radius
1
that are unions of disjoint cliques in
H(3,q)
.

Furthermore, we make use of an eigenvalue technique
[
12
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
in Section
6
and show that Construction D describes all completely regular codes with the second eigenvalue, even
q
, and odd entries in intersection array when the size of a code is less than a quarter of the order of the graph. This fact, combined with other constructions, is an essential ingredient of the characterization of completely regular codes with covering radius
1
in
H(3,q)
up to parameters given in Section 7.
The results of this work and Theorem
1
imply the description of the parameters of completely regular codes with covering radius
1
and the second eigenvalue in
H(n,q)
(Theorem
8
).

Definitions

Let
C
be a
code
in a regular graph
\Gamma
, i.e. a set of its vertices. The complement of the set
C
in
\Gamma
is denoted by
\overline{C}
.
A vertex
x
is in
C_{i}
if the minimum of the distances between
x
and the vertices of
C
is
i
.
The maximum of these distances is called
the covering radius
of
C
and is denoted by
\rho
. The
distance partition
of the vertices of
\Gamma
with respect to
C_{0}=C
is
\{C_{i}:i\in\{0,\ldots,\rho\}\}
.

A code
C
is called
completely regular
(shortly, CRC) if there are numbers

\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{\rho}
,
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{\rho-1}
,
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{\rho}

such that for any
i
, any vertex of
C_{i}
is adjacent to exactly
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
, and
\gamma_{i}
vertices of
C_{i-1}
,
C_{i}
, and
C_{i+1}

respectively. Note that
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{\rho}
can be found from the remaining numbers and the valency of the graph. Similarly to distance-regular graphs
[
5
]
, the array

\{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{\rho-1};\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{\rho}\}
is called the
intersection array
of the completely regular code
C
.

For a completely regular code
C
consider the three-diagonal
(\rho+1)\times(\rho+1)

intersection matrix

M

such that
M_{i,j}
equals the number of vertices of
C_{j}
adjacent to a fixed vertex of
C_{i}
.
Its eigenvalues are called the
eigenvalues of the completely regular code

C
.

It is well-known (see e.g.

[
6
, Theorem 4.5]
) that the eigenvalues of the intersection matrix
M
are necessarily eigenvalues of the adjacency
matrix of the graph.

Remark 1
.

As all completely regular codes we consider below have covering radius
1
, throughout the paper we simplify the
notations for the intersection numbers. We denote
\gamma_{1}
by
\gamma
and
\beta_{0}
by
\beta
.

The following statement is well-known, see e.g.
[
12
]
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
C
be a completely regular code with
\rho=1
in a

k
-regular graph
\Gamma
. Then
\alpha_{0}+\beta=\gamma+\alpha_{1}=k
,
|C|=|V(\Gamma)|\gamma/(\gamma+\beta)
, and the eigenvalues of the
code
C
are
k
and
k-(\gamma+\beta)
.

From the statement above, we see that the intersection matrix
M=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{0}&\beta\\
\gamma&\alpha_{1}\end{pmatrix}
of a completely regular code with
\rho=1
in a
k
-regular graph
\Gamma
can be reconstructed given the element
\gamma
of the intersection array and its eigenvalue
\lambda\neq k
. It is easy to see that a code is CRC with

\rho=1
if and only if its complement is CRC with
\rho=1
. In view of this fact, we assume that
\gamma\leq\beta
and
|C|\leq\frac{|V(\Gamma)|}{2}
throughout the paper (otherwise switch over
C
and
\overline{C}
).

The vertex set of the
Hamming graph

H(n,q)
is the Cartesian
n
th power of a set
{\cal A}
of size

q
, vertices
x
and
y
are adjacent if they differ in
exactly one coordinate position.

For a given
a\in{\cal A}
the set
\{x\in{\cal A}^{n}:x_{i}=a\}
is called a
hyperface
of direction
i
.
Any maximum clique of a Hamming graph
H(n,q)
is a set of
q
tuples having
distinct symbols in a fixed position
i
. We refer to
i
as the
codirection
of the clique to emphasize the dualistic nature of cliques and hyperfaces. We note the following obvious property in the Hamming graphs.

\mbox{A set of }k\mbox{ cliques of codirection }i\mbox{ meets any hyperface of direction }i\mbox{ in }k\mbox{ vertices.}

(1)

The
Cartesian product

\Gamma\square\Gamma^{\prime}
of graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a graph with the vertex set
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma^{\prime})
, and
any two vertices
(u,u^{\prime})
and
(v,v^{\prime})
are adjacent if and only if either

u=v
and
u^{\prime}
is adjacent to
v^{\prime}
in
\Gamma^{\prime}
, or

u^{\prime}=v^{\prime}
and
u
is adjacent to
v
in
\Gamma
. We see that the Hamming graph
H(n,q)
is the Cartesian
n
th power of the complete graph
H(1,q)
.

3
Stochastic properties of CRCs with
\rho=1
in the Cartesian products of complete graphs

In this section we recall one of the most known properties of the completely regular codes in Hamming graphs which relate it to an orthogonal array of certain ”strength”
[
7
]
. A similar fact could be formulated for a more general class of graphs such as the Cartesian product of complete graphs with different orders, which are not necessarily distance-regular. In case of these graphs, we rather use a general term ”stochastic set” that finds similarity with a consonant matrix class.

The maximal cliques of the graph
H(1,q)\square H(1,q^{\prime})
are
{\cal A}\times i
and
j\times{\cal A}^{\prime}
for
i\in{\cal A}^{\prime}
,
j\in{\cal A}
, where
{\cal A}
and
{\cal A}^{\prime}
are the alphabet sets of
H(1,q)
and
H(1,q^{\prime})
, respectively. We see that the CRCs with the minimum eigenvalue in the graph
H(1,q)\square H(1,q^{\prime})
are evenly distributed by the maximal cliques of the graph.

Proposition 3
.

1.
[
12
, Proposition 2]
A code
C
in the graph

H(1,q)\square H(1,q^{\prime})
is completely regular with
\rho=1
and eigenvalue
-2

if and only if for any maximal clique
K
the number
|K\cap C|
depends only on
|K|
. Moreover, for the parameter
\gamma
of such a code
C
we have that

\frac{|K\cap C|}{|K|}=\frac{\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}
for any maximal clique
K
of
H(1,q)\square H(1,q^{\prime})
.

2. A completely regular code in the graph

H(1,q)\square H(1,q^{\prime})
with
\rho=1
, eigenvalue
-2
, and
\gamma
exists if and only if
\frac{\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}q
is an integer,
0<\frac{\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}q<q
.

Proof.

2.
Necessity follows from the first statement.

Sufficiency.
Let
g

H(1,q)
.
Consider a set

H(1,q)
of size
\frac{q\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}
and its

images
g^{i\frac{q\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}}(D)
for

The desired CRC with
\rho=1

\{(g^{i\frac{q\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}}(j),i):j\in D,i=0,\ldots,q^{\prime}-1\}.

Indeed, it is easy to see that any maximal clique of size

or

\frac{q\gamma}{q+q^{\prime}}

and the result follows from the first statement.

∎

A code
C
in the graph

is called

is

and

.
In turn, an
(a,b)
-stochastic subset of

H(1,q)\square H(1,q^{\prime})

aq^{\prime}=bq.

(2)

In a similar fashion, we call a subset

(a,*)

if any maximal clique of size

respectively contains exactly

(a,*)

-stochastic set is

-stochastic.

It is well-known that if the characteristic
function of a code in
H(n,q)
is orthogonal to any

\lambda_{j}(n,q)
-eigenfunction for all

[

). In particular, in the case when

is a CRC in

with
the second eigenvalue, the code

is evenly distributed by the Hamming subgraphs

. We have the following for

.

Proposition 4
.

1.
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
Let
C
be a completely regular code with
\rho=1
,
eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}(n,q)
, and
\gamma
in
H(3,q)
.
Then for
any
i\in\{1,2,3\}
,
a\in{\cal A}
we have that

|\{x\in C:x_{i}=a\}|=\gamma q/2.

2.
[
10
]
A code
C
is a completely regular code with

\rho=1
and eigenvalue
\lambda_{3}(3,q)
in
H(3,q)
if and only if
any maximal clique contains the same number of vertices in
C
.
Such codes with the parameter
\gamma
exist if and only if
\gamma
is divisible by
3
.

The Proposition
4
.2 is a folklore property which essentially states that a set is a completely regular code with the covering radius
1
and the minimum eigenvalue if and only if it is evenly distributed by the Delsarte cliques in the Delsarte clique graphs. A detailed proof could be found in
[

, where this class of codes is shown to coincide with multifold MDS codes in Hamming graphs.

Theorem 5
.

Let
C
be a completely regular code in

,

\rho=1
, eigenvalue

and odd
\gamma

Proof.

We show that

be a vertex in

We now consider possible cases for the value of
(\chi_{C})_{3,u,v}(x_{1},x_{2})
. Due to definition (
9

C

(\chi_{C})_{3,u,v}(x_{1},x_{2})

Consider several cases.

Case a. Suppose that for all

we have that

(\chi_{C})_{3,u,v}(x_{1},x_{2})=0

Case b. Assume that for some

.

Suppose that strong clique property does not hold for the code

\gamma q/2

A description of the code by Construction D follows from Theorem

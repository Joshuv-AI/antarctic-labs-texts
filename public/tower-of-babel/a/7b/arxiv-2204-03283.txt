Optimal convergence order for multi-scale stochastic Burgers equation
Peng Gao, Xiaobin Sun

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the strong and weak convergence rates for multi-scale one-dimensional stochastic Burgers equation. Based on the techniques of Galerkin approximation, Kolmogorov equation and Poisson equation, we obtain the slow component strongly and weakly converges to the solution of the corresponding averaged equation with optimal orders 1/2 and 1 respectively. The highly nonlinear term in system brings us huge difficulties, we develop new technique to overcome these difficulties. To the best of our knowledge, this work seems to be the first result in which the optimal convergence orders in strong and weak sense for multi-scale stochastic partial differential equations with highly nonlinear term.

1.
Introduction

Many multi-scale problems arise from material sciences, chemistry, fluids dynamics, biology, ecology, climate dynamics and other application areas. Usually, this kind of systems usually consist of two components, which correspond to slow component
X^{{\varepsilon}}
and fast component
Y^{{\varepsilon}}
by using a small scale parament
{\varepsilon}
. The issue how to describe asymptotic behavior of
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
has attracted many people’s attention. Averaging principles can help us study this kind of asymptotic behavior of
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
, moreover, averaging principle provides a powerful tool for
simplifying nonlinear dynamical systems and obtain approximation solutions to differential equations arising from mechanics, mathematics, physics, control, and other areas. Furthermore, it can also help us understand and investigate the physical phenomenon described by nonlinear dynamical systems.

The theory of averaging principle of multi-scale system has a long history and rich results. The idea of averaging appeared in the perturbation theory developed by Clairaut, Laplace, and Lagrange in the 18th century. Then various averaging schemes (Gauss, Fatou, Delone-Hill) were widely applied in celestial mechanics in the 19th century. These were mainly formal techniques. Bogoliubov and Mitropolsky
[
3
]
first studied the averaging principle for the deterministic systems.

The averaging principle in the stochastic differential equations (SDEs for short) setup was first considered by Khasminskii in
[
32
]
.
Generally speaking, a multi-scale stochastic system driven by Brownian motion has the following form

\begin{array}[]{l}(*)\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=f(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sigma_{1}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dW_{t}\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}g(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{2}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dW_{t}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Classical averaging principle of multi-scale stochastic system
:

For system
(*)
, under some assumption of dissipation and ergodicity,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{array}[]{llll}X^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\bar{X},\penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm{as}}\penalty\ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0,\end{array}\end{array}

in weak sense, where
\bar{X}
is the solution to the following averaged equation

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\begin{array}[]{llll}d\bar{X}_{t}=\bar{f}(\bar{X}_{t})dt+\bar{\sigma}_{1}(\bar{X}_{t})dW_{t}\end{array}\end{array}

where
\bar{f}(x)=\int f(x,y)\mu_{x}(dy)
and
\bar{\sigma}_{1}(x)=\left[\int\sigma_{1}(x,y)\sigma^{\ast}_{1}(x,y)\mu_{x}(dy)\right]^{1/2}
are the averaged coefficients, with
\mu_{x}(dy)
being the ergodic
invariant measure of the fast variables
Y_{x}
with a frozen
x
variable, namely,

dY_{t}=g(x,Y_{t})dt+\sigma_{2}(x,Y_{t})dW_{t}.

Then it is extended to stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs for short) by Cerrai and Freidlin
[
9
]
. Since then, averaging principle for multi-scale SPDEs has become an active research area, we refer to
[
1
,
4
,
8
,
17
,
30
,
37
,
38
]
and references therein for more interesting results on this topic.

It is well-known that a lot of models or equations in physics are accompanied by highly nonlinear term. Thus more and more multi-scale SPDEs with highly nonlinear term attracted much attention in this topic. For instance, stochastic one dimensional Burgers equations
[
15
]
, stochastic Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation
[
20
]
, stochastic Schrödinger equation
[
21
]
, stochastic Klein-Gordon equation
[
22
]
, stochastic Korteweg-de Vries equation
[
23
]
, stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equation
[
19
]
, stochastic 3D fractional Leray-
\alpha
model
[
26
]
. Since the importance of the above models in physics, the rate of convergence has important practical significance, an important question arises from numerical simulation and scientific calculation:

Can we obtain the optimal convergence order when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
?

In this paper, we try to answer this question for multi-scale SPDEs with highly nonlinear term.
In the above references, the authors usually apply the technique of stopping time to deal with the nonlinear term, however it seems that this kind of technique will destroy the convergence rate, thus no satisfactory convergence rates were obtained in the mentioned references above.
In order to overcome these difficulties, we develop
new framework
to obtain the optimal convergence order. This kind of framework and method is successfully applied to multi-scales stochastic Burgers equations.

The Burgers equation

X_{t}-X_{\xi\xi}+XX_{\xi}=0

is used to study the turbulent fluid flow in
[
6
]
and the references therein,
it arises as a 1-D simplification of the control system associated to the Navier-Stokes system, which has
been extensively studied these last years, see, for instance
[
18
]
. The Burgers equation has been
extensively used as a toy model to investigate properties of more complex systems in a rather simple
setting. This equation was introduced in the seminal paper
[
7
]
by Burgers. Both from a theoretical
and a numerical point of view, it already exhibits some key behaviors, such as interaction between the
nonlinearity and the smoothing effect. Since the stochastic Burgers equation can be used to model vortex lines in high-temperature superconductors, dislocations in disordered solids and kinetic roughening of interfaces in epitaxial growth, formation of
large-scale structures in the universe, constructive quantum field theory, etc., in recent years, many authors study the stochastic Burgers equation in
[
2
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
25
,
31
,
36
]
.

In this paper, we consider the following multi-scale stochastic Burgers equation on the interval
[0,1]
:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial X^{\varepsilon}_{t}(\xi)}{\partial t}=\big[\Delta X^{\varepsilon}_{t}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t}(\xi))^{2}+F(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})(\xi)\big]+\frac{\sqrt{Q_{1}}\partial W^{1}}{\partial t}(t,\xi),\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\\
\frac{\partial Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}(\xi)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\big[\Delta Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}(\xi)+G(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})(\xi)\big]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{2}}\partial W^{2}}{\partial t}(t,\xi),\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\\
X^{\varepsilon}_{t}(0)=X^{\varepsilon}_{t}(1)=Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}(0)=Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}(1)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter describing the ratio of time scales between the slow component
X^{\varepsilon}

and fast component
Y^{\varepsilon}
.
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator, measurable functions
F
and
G
satisfy some appropriate conditions.
Q_{1},Q_{2}:H\rightarrow H
are two non-negative selfadjoint trace operators.
Q_{1}
is also a bounded linear operator.
\{W_{t}^{1}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
and
\{W_{t}^{2}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
are mutually independent cylindrical Wiener processes, which are defined on a complete filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0},\mathbb{P})
.

In
[
15
]
, Dong et. al. used the techniques of classical Khasminskii’s time discretization and stopping time to prove the strong convergence, and applied the method of asymptotic expansion of solutions of Kolmogorov equations to study the weak convergence. Unfortunately, it does not achieve optimal convergence rate in the strong and weak sense. Recently, the technique of Poisson equation is widely used to study the optimal convergence rate in the multi-scale SDEs or SPDEs, we refer to
[
4
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
. More applications of Poisson equation, see e. g.
[
28
,
29
]
and references therein.

Thus, this paper aims to improve the strong and weak convergence orders obtained in
[
15
]
to optimal orders. More precisely, using the techniques of Galerkin approximation, Kolmogorov equation and Poisson equation, we obtain the slow component strongly and weakly converges to the solution of the corresponding averaged equation with optimal orders 1/2 and 1 respectively. It is worth to point that in order to overcome the difficulty cased by the high nonlinear term, some accurate estimates, such as the exponential order, of the solution are needed.
To the best of our knowledge, it seems that this paper is the first attempt in studying the optimal convergence rates for multi-scale SPDEs with highly nonlinear term.
Moreover, since the optimal convergence order is achieved, then the subsequent problems, such as the central limit type theorem or diffusion approximation, for multi-scale SPDEs with highly nonlinear term can be considered, which are left for our further works.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some notation and under some suitable assumptions, we formulate our main results. Section 3 are devoted to some a priori estimates of the finite dimensional frozen equation and study the corresponding Poisson equation. Section 4 and Section 5 are devoted to proving the strong convergence and weak convergence, respectively. In the Appendix 6, we recall some useful inequalities and give the detailed proofs of the Galerkin approximation. Throughout this paper,

,

and
C_{p,T}
stand for constants whose value may change from line to line, and

and

is used to emphasize that the constant depend on
T
and
p,T
respectively.

Main results

Let

.

(

-

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{{\varepsilon}}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{p}\right)\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{p/2}.

Instead of using the technique of stopping time in

Theorem 2.7
.

(

-
4

3.
The a priori estimates

Note that the operator
A
is an unbounded operator, we use Galerkin approximation to reduce the infinite dimensional problem to a finite dimension, i.e., considering the following approximation:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}=[AX^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}+B^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t})+F^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t},Y^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t})]dt+\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{t},\ X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{0}=x^{m}\in H_{m},\\
dY^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}[AY^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}+G^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t},Y^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t})]dt+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}d\bar{W}^{2,m}_{t},\quad Y^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{0}=y^{m}\in H_{m},\end{array}\right.

where
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
H_{m}:=\text{span}\{e_{k};1\leqslant k\leqslant m\}
,
\pi_{m}
is the orthogonal projection of
H
onto
H_{m}
,
x^{m}:=\pi_{m}x,y^{m}:=\pi_{m}y
and

\displaystyle B^{m}(x):=\pi_{m}B(x^{m}),\quad F^{m}(x,y):=\pi_{m}F(x^{m},y^{m}),\quad G^{m}(x,y):=\pi_{m}G(x^{m},y^{m}),

\displaystyle\bar{W}^{1,m}_{t}:=\sum^{m}_{k=1}W^{1,k}_{t}e_{k},\quad\bar{W}^{2,m}_{t}:=\sum^{m}_{k=1}W^{2,k}_{t}e_{k}.

Similarly, we consider the following approximation to the averaged equation
(
2.14
)
:

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle d\bar{X}^{m}_{t}=\left[A\bar{X}^{m}_{t}+B^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{t})+\bar{F}^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{t})\right]dt+\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{t},\\
\bar{X}^{m}_{0}=x^{m},\end{array}\right.

where
\bar{F}^{m}(x):=\int_{H_{m}}F^{m}(x,y)\mu^{x,m}(dy)
, and

is the unique invariant measure of the transition semigroup of the following frozen equation:

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dY^{x,y,m}_{t}=[AY^{x,y,m}_{t}+G^{m}(x,Y^{x,y,m}_{t})]dt+\sqrt{Q_{2}}d\bar{W}^{2,m}_{t},\\
Y^{x,y,m}_{0}=y\in H_{m}.\end{array}\right.

Next, we give some a priori estimates of the solution

of the averaged equation

)
and the solution

3.1.
The a priori estimates of the solution
\bar{X}^{m}_{t}

Recall the finite dimensional averaged equation
(
3.2
)
.
Note that

\bar{F}^{m}(x):=\int_{H_{m}}F^{m}(x,z)\mu^{x,m}(dz)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}F^{m}(x,Y^{x,y,m}_{t}),\quad\forall y\in H_{m}.

By assumptions
1
and
2
, through a straightforward computation, it is easy to check that

(3.4)

\displaystyle|D\bar{F}^{m}(x)\cdot h|\leqslant C|h|\quad\forall x,h\in H_{m},

(3.5)

\displaystyle|D^{2}\bar{F}^{m}(x)\cdot(h,k)|\leqslant C|h|\|k\|_{\tau},\quad\forall x,h,k\in H_{m},

(3.6)

\displaystyle|D^{3}\bar{F}^{m}(x)\cdot(h,k,l)|\leqslant C|h|\|k\|_{\tau}\|l\|_{\tau},\quad\forall x,h,k,l\in H_{m},

where
\tau
is the constant in assumption

.

As a consequence,
(
3.2
)
admits a unique mild solution

\displaystyle\bar{X}^{m}_{t}=e^{tA}x^{m}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}B^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\bar{F}^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}\bar{W}^{1,m}_{s}.

The following is the a prior estimate of the solution
\bar{X}^{m}_{t}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

(1) For any
p\geqslant 1
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{p,T}>0
such that for any
x\in H
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}^{m}_{t}|^{p}\right)\leqslant C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}).

(2) For any
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that for any
x\in H
and
\delta<\frac{\lambda_{1}-2L_{F}}{4\|Q_{1}\|}
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\delta\sup_{t\leqslant T}|\bar{X}^{m}_{t}|^{2}+\delta\int^{T}_{0}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|^{2}_{1}ds}\right)\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|)e^{\delta|x|^{2}}.

(3) For any
T>0
,
p\geqslant 1
,
\gamma\in(1,3/2)
and

with
\theta\in[0,\gamma]
,
there exist constants

and
C_{T}>0
such that for any

, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\left[\mathbb{E}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{t}\|^{p}_{\gamma}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant C_{T}t^{-\frac{(\gamma-\theta)}{2}}(1+|x|^{d}+\|x\|_{\theta}).

Proof.

(1) Note that
\text{Tr}Q_{1}<\infty
, by a minor revision as in the proof of
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
, we can easily obtain
(
3.7
)
.

(2) The proof of
(
3.8
)
is inspired from
[
10
, Proposition 5.11]
. However, for the reason of technique which will be used later. Here we need a stronger result. Set

Z^{m}_{t}=|\bar{X}^{m}_{t}|^{2}+\int^{t}_{0}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|^{2}_{1}ds,\quad t\in[0,T].

Then we have

dZ^{m}_{t}=\left[-\|\bar{X}^{m}_{t}\|^{2}_{1}+\text{Tr}Q^{m}_{1}+2\langle\bar{X}^{m}_{t},\bar{F}(\bar{X}^{m}_{t})\rangle\right]dt+2\langle\bar{X}^{m}_{t},\sqrt{Q_{1}}dW^{1}_{t}\rangle,

where

.

By Itô’s formula, it follows that for any
\delta>0
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle e^{\delta Z^{m}_{t}}=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle e^{\delta|x|^{2}}+\int^{t}_{0}\delta e^{\delta Z^{m}_{s}}\left[-\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|^{2}_{1}+2\delta|\sqrt{Q_{1}}\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{2}+\text{Tr}Q^{m}_{1}+2\langle\bar{X}^{m}_{s},\bar{F}^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})\rangle\right]ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}2\delta e^{\delta Z^{m}_{s}}\langle\bar{X}^{m}_{s},\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{s}\rangle.

Note that by
(
2.7
)
, it follows

Then for any
\delta\in(0,\frac{\lambda_{1}-2L_{F}}{2\|Q_{1}\|})
, where
\|Q_{1}\|
is the usual operator norm of
Q_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle-\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|^{2}_{1}+2\delta|\sqrt{Q_{1}}\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{2}+2\langle\bar{X}^{m}_{s},\bar{F}^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle-(\lambda_{1}-2L_{F})|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{2}+2\delta\|Q_{1}\||\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{2}+C|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|\leqslant C,

which combines with
(
3.10
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\delta Z^{m}_{t}}\leqslant e^{\delta|x|^{2}}+\delta(\text{Tr}Q_{1}+C)\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}e^{\delta Z^{m}_{s}}ds.

By Gronwall’s lemma, we obtain that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\delta Z^{m}_{t}}\leqslant e^{\delta|x|^{2}+t\delta\left(\text{Tr}Q_{1}+C\right)}.

Using
(
3.10
)
,
(
3.11
)
and Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\leqslant T}e^{\delta Z^{m}_{t}}\right)\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle e^{\delta|x|^{2}}+\delta(\text{Tr}Q_{1}+C)\int^{T}_{0}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\delta Z^{m}_{s}}\right)ds+C\delta\text{Tr}Q_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{T}_{0}e^{2\delta Z^{m}_{s}}|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{2}ds\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle e^{\delta|x|^{2}}+\delta T(\text{Tr}Q_{1}+C)e^{\delta|x|^{2}+T\delta\left(\text{Tr}Q_{1}+C\right)}+C_{T}\delta\text{Tr}Q_{1}(1+|x|)e^{\delta|x|^{2}+T\delta\left(\text{Tr}Q_{1}+C\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|)e^{\delta|x|^{2}},

which implies that
(
3.8
)
holds.

(3) Recall that

\displaystyle\bar{X}^{m}_{t}=e^{tA}x^{m}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}B^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\bar{F}^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{s}.

According to
(
2.1
)
and Lemma

, we have for any

,

\displaystyle\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}B^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds\Big\|_{\gamma}\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\big\|e^{(t-s)A}B^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})\big\|_{\gamma}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{-\alpha_{3}-\gamma}{2}}\|B^{m}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})\|_{-\alpha_{3}}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{-\alpha_{3}-\gamma}{2}}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\alpha_{1}}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\alpha_{2}+1}ds,

where we choose positives constants

and
\alpha_{2}
be small enough such that

, then find a proper

satisfying

.

and

Using interpolation inequality, for any

we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\alpha_{1}}\leqslant C|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{\frac{\gamma-\alpha_{1}}{\gamma}}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\gamma}^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\gamma}},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\alpha_{2}+1}\leqslant C|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{\frac{\gamma-\alpha_{2}-1}{\gamma}}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\gamma}^{\frac{\alpha_{2}+1}{\gamma}}.

It follows from
(
3.12
)

(
3.13

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}B(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds\Big\|_{\gamma}^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{-\alpha_{3}-\gamma}{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{\frac{p(2\gamma-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-1)}{\gamma}}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\gamma}^{\frac{p(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+1)}{\gamma}}\right]^{1/p}ds

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\frac{-\alpha_{3}-\gamma}{2}}\Big(\left[\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{\frac{p(2\gamma-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-1)}{\gamma-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}-1}}\right]^{1/p}+\left[\mathbb{E}\|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}\|_{\gamma}^{p}\right]^{1/p}\Big)ds.

(
3.7

(
2.1

, we obtain for any

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\bar{F}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})ds\Big\|_{\gamma}^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\Big[\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}|\bar{F}(\bar{X}^{m}_{s})|^{p}\right]^{1/p}ds

\displaystyle C\Big[\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}(1+|\bar{X}^{m}_{s}|^{p})\right]^{1/p}ds

(3.15)

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|).

Note that

Finally, combining

)

)

Note that
(
2.7
)

24

Proposition 4.1
.

Poisson equation

)

5.
The proof of weak convergence

In this section, we shall prove Theorem

5.1.
Idea of the proof of weak convergence

In order to show the key idea to readers for convenience, we here give a direct-viewing about how to use these techniques.

Step 1 (Finite dimensional approximation)

For a test function
\phi\in C_{b}^{3}(H)
, we have for any

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\phi\left(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}\right)-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})\right|\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\phi\left(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}\right)-\mathbb{E}\phi\left(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}\right)\right|+\left|\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{m}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})\right|

By
(
6.3
)
and
(
6.4
)

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbb{E}\phi\left(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}\right)-\mathbb{E}\phi\left(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}\right)\right|=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{m}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})\right|=0.

Then it is sufficient to show that there exists a constant

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\mathbb{E}\phi\left(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}\right)-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{m}_{t})\right|\leqslant C{\varepsilon}.

Step 2 (Kolmogorov equation)

In order to prove
(
5.2
)

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{m}(t,x)=\bar{\mathscr{L}}^{m}_{1}u_{m}(t,x),\quad t\in[0,T],\\

(
3.2
)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathscr{L}}^{m}_{1}u_{m}(t,x):=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle D_{x}u_{m}(t,x)\cdot\left[Ax+B^{m}(x)+\bar{F}^{m}(x)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum^{m}_{k=1}\left[D_{xx}u_{m}(t,x)\cdot(\sqrt{Q_{1}}e_{k},\sqrt{Q_{1}}e_{k})\right],\quad x\in H_{m},t\in[0,T].

\displaystyle+C_{p,T,{\varepsilon}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}\!\!e^{(t-s)A/{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}d\bar{W}^{2,m}_{s}-\int^{t}_{0}\!\!e^{(t-s)A/{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}dW^{2}_{s}\right|^{p}.

By a simple computation, we get

\displaystyle X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle e^{tA}(x^{m}-x)+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}(\pi_{m}-I)B(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\left[B^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})-B^{m}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right]ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}(\pi_{m}-I)F(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\left[F^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})-F^{m}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right]ds

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{s}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}dW^{1}_{s}.

We now define the following stopping time

\tau_{k}=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}|+|X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}|\geqslant k\}.

Note that by Sobolev’s embedding theorem,

\displaystyle\left|e^{(t-s)A}\left[B^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})-B^{m}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right]\right|\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\left|e^{(t-s)A}\left[D_{\xi}\left((X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})^{2}-(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})^{2})\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\left\|e^{(t-s)A}\left[D_{\xi}\left((X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})^{2}-(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})^{2})\right)\right]\right\|_{H^{1/2,1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}+X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\|_{L^{1}}ds,

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}|+|X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|)|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|ds.

Then we have for any
p\geqslant 4
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\left[B^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})-B^{m}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right]ds\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{k}\sup_{t\leqslant T\wedge\tau_{k}}\left|\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{3}{4}}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|ds\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{k}\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}\left|\left[\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{-\frac{3q}{4}}ds\right]^{1/q}\left[\int^{t}_{0}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|^{p}ds\right]^{1/p}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{k,T}\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}\left[\int^{t}_{0}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|^{p}ds\right]^{1/p},

which implies that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\left[B^{m}(X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s})-B^{m}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right]ds\right|^{p}\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{k,T}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|^{p}ds.

Hence by
(
6.5
)
, we get that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}|^{p}

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{p}|x^{m}-x|^{p}+C_{p,T,{\varepsilon}}|y^{m}-y|^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{k,p,T,{\varepsilon}}\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{s}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|^{p}ds+\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}(\pi_{m}-I)B(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{p,T}\!\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}|(\pi_{m}-I)F(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})|^{p}ds\!+\!C_{p,T,{\varepsilon}}\!\int^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}_{0}\!\!\mathbb{E}|(\pi_{m}-I)G(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})|^{p}ds

\displaystyle+C_{p}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{s}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}dW^{1}_{s}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{p,T,{\varepsilon}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A/{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}d\bar{W}^{2,m}_{s}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A/{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}dW^{2}_{s}\right|^{p}.

Then by Gronwall’s inequality and taking expectation, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}|^{p}\right)

(6.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{p,k,T,{\varepsilon}}|x^{m}-x|^{p}+C_{p,T}|y^{m}-y|^{p}+C_{p,k,T,{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{T}_{0}(\pi_{m}-I)|B(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})|ds\right|^{p}

\displaystyle+C_{p,k,T,{\varepsilon}}\!\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\mathbb{E}|(\pi_{m}-I)F(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})|^{p}ds\!+\!C_{p,T}\!\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\mathbb{E}|(\pi_{m}-I)G(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})|^{p}ds

\displaystyle+C_{p,k,T,{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}d\bar{W}^{1,m}_{s}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}\sqrt{Q_{1}}dW^{1}_{s}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle+C_{p,k,T,{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A/{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}d\bar{W}^{2,m}_{s}-\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A/{\varepsilon}}\sqrt{Q_{2}}dW^{2}_{s}\right|^{p}\right].

By the a priori estimate of

Summarizing the above, we get that for

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T\wedge\tau_{k}]}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}|^{p}\right)=0.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{m,{\varepsilon}}_{t}-X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

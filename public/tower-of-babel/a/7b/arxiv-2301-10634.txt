1.
Introduction

Moments of the Riemann zeta function and other
L
-functions are classical objects in analytic number theory utilized in the study large values and the value distribution of
L
-functions.
It has long been conjectured that for
\beta\geq 0
there is an asymptotic

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2\beta}\sim C_{\beta}(\log T)^{\beta^{2}}

as
T\rightarrow\infty
for certain constants
C_{\beta}
.
The values of the constants
C_{\beta}
were conjectured recently in the work of Keating and Snaith
[
33
]
using analogies between the Riemann zeta function and characteristic polynomials of random matrices.
These asymptotics have only been established in the case of
\beta=1
due to work of Hardy and Littlewood
[
26
]
and the case of
\beta=2
in work of Ingham
[
24
]
, however.
Upper bounds of the correct order are known for real
\beta\leq 2
due to work of Heap, Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
30
]
, and are known for all
\beta\geq 0
assuming the Riemann hypothesis due to Harper
[
28
]
.
Lower bounds are known unconditionally for all
\beta\geq 0
due to Heap and Soundararajan
[
31
]
.

While relatively little is known unconditionally about the moments of zeta for
\beta>2
, in recent literature there has been interest in moments and the value distribution of zeta in short intervals on the critical line.
Instead of studying zeta along an interval
[T,2T]
, one instead chooses a point
t\in[T,2T]
uniformly at random and then tries to understand the typical behavior of the moments or maximum of zeta in the shorter interval
[t-1,t+1]
, say.
The question of the maximum of zeta in short intervals is the subject of the Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating conjecture
[
21
,
22
]
, which predicts that if
t
is chosen uniformly at random from
[T,2T]
then as
T\rightarrow\infty

(1)

\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{|h-t|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)|>e^{y}\frac{\log T}{(\log\log T)^{3/4}}\right)\rightarrow 1-F(y),

where
F(y)
is a cumulative distribution function with tail decay
1-F(y)\sim ye^{-2y}
as
y\rightarrow\infty
.
The fraction
\frac{3}{4}
and the tail decay are manifestations of correlations of nearby shifts of zeta.
If these correlations were not present, probabilistic heuristics would predict a
\tfrac{1}{4}
in place of
\tfrac{3}{4}
and a tail decay of
e^{-2y}
instead of
ye^{-2y}
.
An analogous conjecture is known to hold for the circular-
\beta
ensemble in the random matrix setting– refer to the work Paquette and Zeitouni
[
41
,
42
]
.

There has been a lot of recent progress toward the conjecture (
1
).
Conditionally on the Riemann hypothesis, Najnudel showed with probability
1-o_{\varepsilon}(1)
that
\max_{|h-t|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)|
is bounded by
(\log T)^{1+\varepsilon}
, as well corresponding upper and lower bounds for the imaginary part of
\log\zeta

[
39
]
.
Unconditionally Arguin, Belius, Bourgade, Radziwiłł, and Soundararajan
[
1
]
proved that
\max_{|h-t|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)|
lies in the interval
[(\log T)^{1-\varepsilon},(\log T)^{1+\varepsilon}]
with probability
1-o_{\varepsilon}(1)
.
Harper
[
27
]
then refined the upper bound showing that with probability
1-o(1)

\max_{|h-t|\leq 1}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)|\leq\frac{\log T}{(\log\log T)^{3/4+o(1)}}.

Most recently Arguin, Bourgade, and Radziwiłł established the upper bound in the Fyodorov-Hiary-Keating conjecture
[
2
]
.
Indeed, one consequence of
[
2
]
is that for fixed
y
the probability in (
1
) is
O(ye^{-2y})
as
T\rightarrow\infty
.

Moments of zeta in short intervals were also recently studied Fyodorov and Keating
[
22
]
followed by Arguin, Ouimet, and Radziwiłł
[
3
]
.
The problem now is to understand the behavior of the short moments or the partition function

(2)

\int_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)|^{2\beta}dt

where
\theta>-1
and
t
is chosen uniformly at random from
[T,2T]
.
The typical behavior of these short moments was investigated by Fyodorov and Keating
[
22
]
when
\theta=0
, and their behavior for all
\theta>-1
was determined by Arguin, Ouimet, and Radziwiłł
[
3
]
.
One particularly interesting conclusion of the analysis is that there is a so-called freezing transition as one varies the parameter
\beta
.
More precisely, for fixed
\theta>-1
there is a critical value
\beta_{c}(\theta)
such that when
\beta<\beta_{c}(\theta)
, the moments (
2
) are governed by the typical values of
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
,
yet when
\beta>\beta_{c}(\theta)
the moments (
2
) are dominated by just a few large values of
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
.

In this paper, we will not be interested in the typical values of the moments (
2
), but instead larger values.
More precisely, we will investigate the
k=2
case of the so-called
moments of moments
of zeta:

\text{MoM}_{T}(k,\beta):=\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}\Bigg(~\int\displaylimits_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)|^{2\beta}~dh\Bigg)^{k}~dt

where
\beta\geq 0
and
\theta>-1
.
In other terms, we study the variance of the moments (
2
).
The problem of moments of moments was first studied by Fyodorov, Hiary, and Keating in the random matrix setting
[
21
,
22
]
, who conjectured asymptotics for

\text{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta):=\int_{U(N)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|P_{N}(A,\theta)|^{2\beta}d\theta\right)^{k}dA

as
N\rightarrow\infty
where
dA
is the Haar measure on the unitary group
U(N)
of size
N
and
P_{N}(A,\theta):=\det(I-Ae^{-i\theta})
is the characteristic polynomial of
A
.
There has been a lot of recent progress on these conjectures, and it has now been proven that (see
[
4
,
5
,
13
,
20
]
) for
\beta\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
that

\text{MoM}_{U(N)}(k,\beta)\sim\begin{cases}A(k,\beta)N^{k\beta^{2}}&k<1/\beta^{2}\\
B(k,\beta)N^{k^{2}\beta^{2}-k+1}&k>1/\beta^{2}\end{cases}

for certain constants
A(k,\beta),B(k,\beta)
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
For
k\geq 2
, the transition at the critical exponent
\beta=1/\sqrt{k}
was analyzed by Keating and Wong
[
35
]
, who showed

\text{MoM}_{U(N)}(1/\beta^{2},\beta)\sim D_{\beta}N\log N

for certain explicit constants
D_{\beta}
that also appear in the theory of Gaussian multiplicative chaos.

In the case
\theta=0
, the random matrix philosophy of Keating and Snaith
[
33
]
leads us to the prediction that if
k\geq 2
is an integer and
\beta\geq 0
then

\text{MoM}_{T}(k,\beta)\sim\begin{cases}A^{\prime}(k,\beta)(\log T)^{k\beta^{2}}&k<1/\beta^{2}\\
B^{\prime}(k,\beta)(\log T)(\log\log T)&k=1/\beta^{2}\\
C^{\prime}(k,\beta)(\log T)^{k^{2}\beta^{2}-k+1}&k>1/\beta^{2}\end{cases}

for certain constants
A^{\prime}(k,\beta),B^{\prime}(k,\beta),C^{\prime}(k,\beta)
as
T\rightarrow\infty
.
These conjectures were also shown to hold for
\beta,k\in\mathbb{N}
by Bailey and Keating
[
6
]
assuming the standard conjectures on shifted moments of zeta due to Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein, and Snaith
[
17
]
.
These asymptotics do not however include the critical or subcritical regimes where
\beta\leq 1/\sqrt{k}
.

The goal of this paper is to provide upper and lower bounds of the expected order of magnitude in the case
k=2
and non-integer
\beta
.
In particular we will show that there is indeed a phase transition at
\beta=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
, although this transition occurs in the sub leading order terms when
\theta>0
.
The starting point of the method is to rewrite the integral as

(3)

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)=\frac{1}{T}\iint\displaylimits_{|h_{1}|,|h_{2}|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{1})\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2})|^{2\beta}~dt~dh_{1}~dh_{2}.

Therefore we can understand the
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)
by understanding the correlation structure of zeta in short intervals and then integrating over all of the possible shifts
h_{1},h_{2}
.
Morally, we will find that if
|h_{1}-h_{2}|\ll 1/\log T
then

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{1})\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2})|^{2\beta}~dt\approx(\log T)^{4\beta^{2}},

while when
|h_{1}-h_{2}|\gg 1/\log T
that

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{1})\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2})|^{2\beta}~dt\approx(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}}|\zeta(1+i(h_{1}-h_{2}))|^{2\beta^{2}}.

So
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{1})
and
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2})
are almost perfectly correlated when
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are close, and they decorrelate for more distant shifts.
Indeed one may see that the factor of
|\zeta(1+i(h_{1}-h_{2}))|^{2\beta^{2}}
is bounded on average by computing the moments of zeta on the one line.
A similar correlation structure for
\log|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|
was proven by Bourgade
[
11
]
, and this correlation structure is what gives rise to the phase transition at
\beta=1/\sqrt{2}
.
However as we look at larger shifts, we also see that a large value of
\zeta(1+ih)
on the one line will cause

\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)
and
\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih)
to have an unusually large average correlation.

For
\beta=1
, an asymptotic formula for
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,1)
as
T\rightarrow\infty
is obtained in Kovaleva’s thesis, which also studies the fourth moment of zeta with shifts as large as
T^{6/5-\varepsilon}

[
37
]
.
However, this asymptotic does not address the particularly interesting critical case
\beta=1/\sqrt{2}
.
Assuming RH, Chandee
[
12
]
studied more general shifted moments

M_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{\beta}}(T)=\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{k=1}^{m}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+i(t+\alpha_{k}))|^{2\beta_{k}}dt

where
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{\alpha}(T)=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
and
\bm{\beta}=(\beta_{1}\ldots,\beta_{m})
satisfy
|\alpha_{k}|\leq T/2
,
\beta_{k}\geq 0
, and
|\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}|=O(1)
. Under these assumptions Chandee obtained upper bounds of the conjectured order up to a
(\log T)^{\varepsilon}
loss. Due to this loss, we are unable to detect the
\log\log T
factor at the phase transition; however the work of Chandee could still be used to give upper bounds of the conjectured order up to a factor of
(\log T)^{\varepsilon}
for
\text{MoM}_{T}(k,\beta)
when
-1<\theta\leq 0
,
\beta\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
In the special case where
\bm{\beta}=(\beta,\beta)
, Ng, Shen, and Wong
[
40
]
removed the
(\log T)^{\varepsilon}
loss using the work of Harper
[
28
]
.
This work is sufficient to obtain upper bounds of the conjectured order for
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)
when
-1<\theta\leq 0
for all
\beta\geq 0
.
Ng, Shen, and Wong also gave upper bounds in the larger regime where
|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|\leq T^{3/5}
; however these bounds are not sharp when
|\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}|
is unbounded and result in a loss of order
\log\log T
when bounding
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)
with
\theta>0
.

To unconditionally obtain upper bounds for
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)
when
\beta
is not an integer, we will use a principle pioneered in works of Heap, Radziwiłł, and Soundararajan
[
30
]
and Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
45
]
; to obtain lower bounds we will use a principle in the works of Heap and Soundararajan
[
31
]
and Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
44
]
.
Overall, these works demonstrate that if one can asymptotically evaluate the twisted
2k^{\text{th}}
moment of a family of
L
-functions for some
k>0
, then one can obtain upper bounds of the correct order for the
2\beta^{\text{th}}
moment in that family when
\beta\leq k
as well as sharp lower bounds for for all
\beta\geq 0
. This method has been used to give conjecturally sharp upper and lower bounds for moments of several families of
L
-functions; see
[
19
,
30
,
31
,
44
,
45
]
for example.

The first task of this paper will be to establish the corresponding twisted second and fourth moment formulae with shifts of order
(\log T)^{\theta}
.
The second moment can be handled using a slight modification of the techniques used in Young’s proof of Levinson’s theorem
[
48
]
, and the fourth moment will follow from some minor modifications to the work of Hughes and Young on the twisted fourth moment
[
32
]
.
With these formulae in hand, we may then follow the principle of
[
30
]
and
[
45
]
to establish sharp upper bounds for
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)
for all
\beta\leq 1
, and lower bounds for all
\beta\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
-1<\theta\leq 0
. If
0\leq\beta<1/\sqrt{2}
then

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\ll\frac{1}{1-2\beta^{2}}(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}(1-\theta)+2\theta}.

For
1/\sqrt{2}<\beta\leq 1

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\ll\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}-1}(\log T)^{4\beta^{2}+\theta-1},

and for
\beta=1/\sqrt{2}

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\ll(1+\theta)(\log T)^{1+\theta}(\log\log T).

Instead if
\theta>0
, then if
0\leq\beta\leq\min(\sqrt{(1+\theta)/2},1)

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\ll(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}+2\theta}+\frac{1}{1-2\beta^{2}}(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}+\theta},

where the sub leading term is replaced by
(1+\theta)(\log T)^{1+\theta}(\log\log T)
if
\beta=1/\sqrt{2}
.
In the case
\min((\sqrt{1+\theta})/2,1)\leq\beta\leq 1
we have

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\ll\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}-1}(\log T)^{4\beta^{2}-1+\theta}.

All implied constants are absolute assuming
T
is taken sufficiently large in terms of
\theta
.

Remark
.

If one assumes RH and that
\theta\leq 0
, the restriction
\beta\leq 1
may be removed using the work of Ng, Shen, and Wong
[
40
]
.
For
\theta>0
and
0\leq\beta\leq\min(\sqrt{(1+\theta)/2},1)
, the sub leading order term can be removed provided one that
T
is sufficiently large in terms of
\beta
or if one allows the implicit constant to depend on
\beta
.
This technicality is necessary because the phase transition occurs in the sub leading order terms of the second moment of moments.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
-1<\theta\leq 0
. If
0\leq\beta<1/\sqrt{2}
then

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\gg_{\beta}(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}(1-\theta)+2\theta}.

For
\beta\geq 1/\sqrt{2}

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\gg_{\beta}(\log T)^{4\beta^{2}+\theta-1},

and for
\beta=1/\sqrt{2}

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\gg(1+\theta)(\log T)^{1+\theta}(\log\log T).

Instead if
\theta>0
, then if
0\leq\beta\leq\sqrt{(1+\theta)/2}

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\gg_{\beta}(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}+2\theta}.

When
\beta\geq\sqrt{(1+\theta)/2}
, we have

\text{MoM}_{T}(2,\beta)\gg_{\beta}\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}-1}(\log T)^{4\beta^{2}-1+\theta},

where all implied constants depend only on
\beta
provided
T
is large enough in terms of
\theta
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are consequences of the following result, which may be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
0\leq\beta\leq 1
,
\theta>-1
and
|h_{1}|,|h_{2}|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}
then

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{1})\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2})|^{2\beta}~dt\ll(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}}|\zeta(1+i(h_{1}-h_{2})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta^{2}}.

For all
\beta\geq 0
, we have a lower bound

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{1})\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2})|^{2\beta}~dt\gg_{\beta}(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}}|\zeta(1+i(h_{1}-h_{2})+1/\log T)|^{2\beta^{2}}.

This improves the result of Ng, Shen, and Wong
[
40
]
and provides a lower bound under the more restrictive assumptions that
\beta\leq 1
and that the shifts are smaller than
(\log T)^{\theta}
.
This bound is of the same strength as the result of
[
40
]
when
|h_{1}-h_{2}|
is bounded, but is stronger when
|h_{1}-h_{2}|
tends to infinity.

We will first establish the necessary twisted moment formulae in section
2
.
Subsequently we will prove Theorem
1.1
in section
3
and then Theorem
1.2
in section
4
.
We now introduce some notation we will use throughout the paper.
The notation is very similar to that of
[
30
]
, with only a few cosmetic differences.
Given some positive integer
\ell
and parameters
T_{0}<T_{1}<\ldots<T_{\ell}
, define for
1\leq j\leq\ell

\mathcal{P}_{j}(s):=\textup{Re }\sum_{T_{j-1}<p\leq T_{j}}p^{-s},\qquad P_{j}:=\sum_{T_{j-1}<p\leq T_{j}}p^{-1}.

If
T_{\ell}
is chosen to be some small power of
T
, then the sum of all the
\mathcal{P}_{j}(s)
will be a good proxy for
\log|\zeta(s)|
on average
[
30
,
31
]
, and each
P_{j}
can be thought of as the variance of each
\mathcal{P}_{j}(s)
on the half line.
Then given parameters
K_{j}\geq 0
for
1\leq j\leq\ell
let

\mathcal{N}_{j}(s;\beta):=\sum_{m=0}^{K_{j}}\frac{1}{m!}(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j}(s))^{m}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid n\Rightarrow p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]\\
\Omega(n)\leq K_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\beta^{\Omega(n)}g(n)}{n^{s}}

where
g(n)
is the multiplicative function such that
g(p^{a})=1/a!
and
\Omega(n)
is the number of prime divisors of
n
counting multiplicity.
Notice that
\mathcal{N}_{j}(s;\beta)
is simply a truncation of the series expansion for
\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j}(s))
.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{j}(s)
is a reasonable proxy for
\log|\zeta(s)|
, if we choose parameters
K_{j}
to be large multiples of the variances
P_{j}
then one might expect

\mathcal{N}(s;\beta):=\prod_{j\leq\ell}\mathcal{N}_{j}(s;\beta)

to behave like
\zeta(s)^{\beta}
on average on the half line.
The main difficulty here is that if the
\mathcal{P}_{j}(s)
are unusually large for some bad set of
s
, then the series approximation for
\exp(\beta\mathcal{P}_{j}(s))
will be weaker.
Fortunately, such events are rare, and we can discard the contribution of such bad
s
using the incremental structure of
\mathcal{N}(s;\beta)
.

2.
Twisted moments with mesoscopic shifts

2.1.
Twisted second moment

An asymptotic for the twisted second moment of zeta with unbounded shifts is not necessary to prove Theorems
1.1
or
1.2
. However, such an estimate will imply an asymptotic for
\text{MoM}_{T}(2,1)
and will serve as a good warm up to the twisted fourth moment estimate we will require.

Before stating our twisted moment estimate, we introduce some convenient notation.
Denote

\lambda(s)=\pi^{s-1/2}\frac{\Gamma((1-s)/2)}{\Gamma(s/2)}.

Therefore the functional equation for
\zeta
can be expressed as
\zeta(s)=\lambda(1-s)\zeta(1-s)
, and
\lambda
satisfies its own functional equation
\lambda(s)\lambda(1-s)=1
.
Define

G(s)=e^{s^{2}}\left(1-\left(\frac{2s}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\right)^{2}\right),

g_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(s,t)=\pi^{-s}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha_{1}+s+it}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha_{2}+s-it}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha_{1}+it}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha_{2}-it}{2}\right)},

X_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},t}=\lambda(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha_{1}+it)\lambda(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha_{2}-it).

The precise definition of
G
here is not too important–
the key properties are that
G(0)=1
,
G
has a zero at
\tfrac{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}{2}
, and
G
decays rapidly in any vertical strip.
By equation 4.12.3 of
[
47
]
, we can approximate

\displaystyle X_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},t}=

\displaystyle\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp\Bigg((\alpha_{1}+it)\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{t+\textup{Im }\alpha_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle+(\alpha_{2}-it)\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{t-\textup{Im }\alpha_{2}}\right)+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\Bigg).

Therefore if we define

Y_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},t}:=\exp\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+it}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{t+\textup{Im }\alpha_{1}}\right)+i\frac{\alpha_{2}-it}{2}\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{t-\textup{Im }\alpha_{2}}\right)+\frac{\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}}{2}\right)

we have the approximation
X_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}=Y_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}^{2}(1+O(1/t))
.
We will use this quantity to symmetrize our formula for the twisted second moment.
Finally, we write

V_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\frac{G(s)}{s}g_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(s,t)x^{-s}~ds.

By Proposition 5.4 of
[
25
]
it follows that for
A\geq 0
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
T
sufficiently large in terms of
\theta

(4)

t^{j}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial t^{j}}V_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(x,t)\ll_{A,j}(1+|x|/t)^{-A}.

We will make use of the following version of the approximate functional equation given by theorem 5.3 of
[
25
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
t\in[T/4,4T]
,
\textup{Re }\alpha_{j}\ll 1/\log T
,
|\textup{Im }\alpha_{j}|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}
for
j=1,2

\displaystyle\sum_{h,k,m,n}\frac{a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(h)\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(k)}}{m^{-h_{2}}n^{-h_{1}}\sqrt{hkmn}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{X}_{h_{1},h_{2},t}\left(\frac{h}{kmn}\right)^{-it}\widetilde{V}_{-h_{2},-h_{1}}(mn,t)w(t/T)~dt.

Using the decay of
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
, we can discard the contribution of terms with
hmn\neq k
and
h\neq kmn
in the first and second lines of (
13
) respectively.
Next we can parameterize the diagonal sums in the first and second lines by
\sum_{h|k,mn=k/h}
and
\sum_{k|h,mn=h/k}
respectively.
Then after using the definition of
\widetilde{V}_{\alpha,\beta}
, shifting the contour, and recognizing the sum over
m
and

as a familiar Dirichlet convolution we find

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\beta,h_{1},h_{2})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\leq\ell}\sum_{h|k}\frac{a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(h)\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(k)}}{k}\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(k/h)\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(t/T)~dt

\displaystyle+\prod_{j\leq\ell}

\displaystyle\sum_{k|h}\frac{a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(h)\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(k)}}{h}\sigma_{-h_{2},-h_{1}}(h/k)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{X}_{h_{1},h_{2},t}~w(t/T)~dt+O(T^{1-\delta}).

for some
\delta>0
.

Due to the oscillating term
\widetilde{X}_{h_{1},h_{2},t}
the second line in (
14

Because

is supported on
[T,2T]
and that
|h_{1}|,|h_{2}|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}
, Stirling’s approximation gives
\widetilde{X}_{h_{1},h_{2},t}=e^{-if(t)}(1+O(1/T))
where

f(t)=(h_{1}+t)\log\left(\frac{t+h_{1}}{2\pi}\right)+(h_{2}+t)\log\left(\frac{t+h_{2}}{2\pi}\right)-2t-h_{1}-h_{2}-\frac{\pi}{2}.

For
t\in[T,2T]
note that
f^{\prime}(t)\gg\log t
and that
f^{\prime\prime}(t)\ll 1/t
.
Therefore, an integration by parts à la van der Corput gives the bound

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{X}_{h_{1},h_{2},t}~w(t/T)~dt\ll 1/\log T.

Therefore because
\prod_{1\leq j\leq\ell}A_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(s)\overline{B_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(s)}
is a Dirichlet polynomial of length
\leq T^{\beta/9}
, we may absorb the second line of (

O(T^{1-\delta})
.

It remains to analyze the sums

S_{j}:=\sum_{h|k}\frac{a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(h)\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(k)}}{k}\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(k/h)=\sum_{a,h}\frac{\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(a)a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(h)\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(ah)}}{ah}

for
j\leq\ell
.
Here we are summing over all integers
a,h
composed of primes in
(T_{j-1},T_{j}]
such that
\Omega(a)+\Omega(h)\leq K_{j}
.
If we discard the latter condition on
a
and
h
via Rankin’s trick, we incur an error at most

\displaystyle e^{-K_{j}}

\displaystyle\prod_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\sum_{r,s\geq 0}\frac{|\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(p^{r})a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(p^{s})\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(p^{r+s})}|}{p^{r+s}}e^{\Omega(r)+\Omega(s)}

\displaystyle\ll e^{-K_{j}}\prod_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\left(1+\frac{2e}{p}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)\ll e^{-100P_{j}},

whence

S_{j}=\prod_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\sum_{r,s\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(p^{r})a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(p^{s})\overline{b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(p^{r+s})}}{p^{r+s}}+O(e^{-100P_{j}}).

Now since
a_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(p)=(\beta-1)\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(p)
and
b_{h_{1},h_{2},j}(p)=\beta\sigma_{h_{1},h_{2}}(p)
it follows that

S_{j}=\prod_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\left(1+\frac{2\beta^{2}(1+\cos((h_{1}-h_{2})\log p))}{p}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)+O(e^{-100P_{j}}),

so up to a power savings
\mathcal{I}(\beta,h_{1},h_{2})
is equal to

T\prod_{j\leq\ell}\left(\prod_{p\in(T_{j-1},T_{j}]}\left(1+\frac{2\beta^{2}(1+\cos((h_{1}-h_{2})\log p))}{p}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)+O(e^{-100P_{j}})\right)\|w\|_{1}.

Using Lemma
3.6
and that
\|w\|_{1}>0.8
we conclude

|\mathcal{I}(\beta,h_{1},h_{2})|\gg_{\beta}T(\log T)^{2\beta^{2}}|\zeta(1+1/\log T_{\ell}+i(h_{1}-h_{2}))|,

and Proposition
4.1
follows. ∎

Proof of Proposition

Lemma 4.4

For

\displaystyle|M_{1,j}(t)M_{2,j}(t)|^{2}\leq|\mathcal{N}_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it

\displaystyle+ih_{1};\beta)\mathcal{N}_{j}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+ih_{2};\beta)|^{2}(1+O(e^{-K_{j}/10}))

\displaystyle+it+ih_{2};\beta)|^{2}(1+O(e^{-K_{j}/10}))

\displaystyle it+ih_{2};\beta)|^{2}(1+O(e^{-K_{j}/10}))

\displaystyle+2^{4/\beta}

\displaystyle Q_{1,j}(t)Q_{2,j}(t)\big),

.

Lemma 4.5
.

For

\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}Q_{a,j}(t)dt\ll e^{-K_{j}},\quad\frac{1}{T}\int_{T}^{2T}Q_{a,j}(t)^{2}dt\ll e^{-K_{j}}.

Proof.

To handle the first estimate, note for

To handle the second estimate an application of the Cauchy Schwarz inequality yields

On the Convergence of Stochastic Gradient Descent for Linear Inverse Problems in Banach Spaces
B. Jin, Z. Kereta

Abstract
In this work we consider stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for solving linear inverse problems in Banach spaces. SGD and its variants have been established as one of the most successful optimisation methods in machine learning, imaging and signal processing, etc. At each iteration SGD uses a single datum, or a small subset of data, resulting in highly scalable methods that are very attractive for large-scale inverse problems. Nonetheless, the theoretical analysis of SGD-based approaches for inverse problems has thus far been largely limited to Euclidean and Hilbert spaces. In this work we present a novel convergence analysis of SGD for linear inverse problems in general Banach spaces: we show the almost sure convergence of the iterates to the minimum norm solution and establish the regularising property for suitable a priori stopping criteria. Numerical results are also presented to illustrate features of the approach.

Linear inverse problems arise naturally in many applications in science and engineering, and also form the basis for studying nonlinear inverse problems. Hence, design and analysis of stable reconstruction methods for linear inverse problems have received much attention.

Iterative regularisation is a powerful algorithmic paradigm that has been successfully employed

Classical iterative methods for inverse problems include (accelerated) Landweber method, conjugate

The per-iteration computational bottleneck of many iterative methods lies in utilising all the data at each iteration, which can be of a prohibitively large size.
For example, this occurs while computing the derivative of an objective. One promising strategy to
overcome this challenge is stochastic gradient descent (SGD), due to Robbins and Monro

40

decomposes the original problem into (finitely many) sub-problems, and then at each iteration uses
only a single datum, or a mini-batch of data, typically selected uniformly at random. This greatly
reduces the computational complexity per-iteration, and enjoys excellent scalability with respect to
data size. In the standard, and best
studied setting,

and

are finite dimensional Euclidean spaces and the corresponding data fitting objective is the

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\mu_{k+1}{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\ast}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}),\quad k=0,1,\ldots,

[

laying the groundwork for the field of stochastic approximation
[

since had a major impact on statistical inference and machine learning, especially for the training of neural networks. SGD has been extensively studied in the Euclidean setting; see
[
2

SGD has also been a popular method for image reconstruction, especially in medical imaging.
For example, the (randomised) Kaczmarz method is a reweighted version of SGD that has been extensively

Iterative methods in Euclidean and Hilbert spaces are effective for reconstructing smooth solutions but fail to capture special features of the solutions, such as sparsity and piecewise constancy. In practice, many imaging inverse problems are more adequately described in non-Hilbert settings, including
sequence spaces

and Lebesgue spaces

[

and analyse algorithms for inverse problems in Banach spaces, and this has received much attention
[
41
,
46

[
44

were the first to prove strong convergence of the iterates under a suitable
step-size schedule for a smooth and uniformly convex Banach space
{\cal X}
and an arbitrary Banach space

. This has since been extended and refined in various aspects, e.g. regarding acceleration
[

,

,

[

,

and Gauss-Newton methods
[

In this work, we investigate SGD for inverse problems in Banach spaces, which has thus far lagged behind due to outstanding challenges in extending the analysis of standard Hilbert
space approaches to the Banach space setting. The main challenges in analysing SGD-like gradient-based
methods in Banach spaces are two-fold:

1.

The use of duality maps results in non-linear update rules, which greatly complicates the convergence analysis. For example, the (expected)
difference between successive updates can no longer be identified as the (sub-)gradient of the objective.

2.

Due to geometric characteristics of Banach spaces, it is more common to use the Bregman distance
for the convergence analysis, which results in the loss of useful algebraic tools, e.g. triangle
inequality and bias-variance decomposition, that are typically needed for the analysis.

In this work,
we develop an SGD approach for the numerical solution of linear inverse problems in Banach spaces,
using the sub-gradient approach based on duality maps, and present a novel convergence analysis.
We first consider the case of exact data, and show that SGD iterates converge to a minimising
solution (first almost surely and then in expectation) under standard assumptions on summability of step-sizes, and geometric properties of the space
{\cal X}
, cf. Theorems

3.8
and
3.10
. This solution is identified
as the minimum norm solution if the initial guess
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}
satisfies the range condition
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\rm{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
.
Further, we give a convergence rate in Theorem
3.14
when the forward operator
{\mathbf{A}}
satisfies a conditional stability
estimate. In case of noisy observations, we show the regularising property
of SGD, for properly chosen stopping indices, cf. Theorem
4.3
. The analysis rests on a descent property in Lemma
3.6
and Robbins-Siegmund theorem for almost super-martingales. In addition, we perform extensive numerical experiments on a model inverse problem (linear integral equation) and computed tomography (with parallel beam geometry) to illustrate distinct features of the proposed Banach space SGD, and examine the influence of various factors, such as the choice of the spaces
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
, mini-batch size and noise characteristics (Gaussian or impulse).

When finalising the paper, we became aware of the independent and simultaneous work
[
27
]
on a stochastic mirror descent method for linear inverse problems between a Banach space

and a Hilbert space

. The method is a randomised version of the well-known Landweber-Kaczmarz method. The authors prove convergence results under

satisfies a benchmark source condition, by interpreting the method as a randomised block gradient method applied to the dual problem. Thus, the current work differs significantly from
[

Preliminaries on Banach spaces

In this section we recall relevant concepts from Banach space theory and the geometry of Banach spaces.

2.1
Duality map

In a Hilbert space
{\cal H}
, for every
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal H}
, there exists a unique
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\in{\cal H}^{\ast}

\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>=\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal H}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\|_{{\cal H}^{\ast}}

For Banach spaces, however, such an

is not necessarily unique, motivating the notion of duality maps.

Definition 2.1

(Duality map)
.

For any

(

\frac{1}{p}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\left\{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\in{\cal X}^{\ast}:\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>=\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}},\text{ and }\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}=\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\right\},

(3)

with gauge function

.
A single-valued selection of

is denoted by
\jmath_{p}^{{\cal X}}
.

In practice, the choice of the power parameter

depends on geometric properties of the space
{\cal X}
. For single-valued duality maps, we use

and

2.2
Bregman distance

Due to the geometry of Banach spaces, it is often more convenient to use the

(Bregman distance)

For a smooth Banach space

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{w}}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{w}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{z}}})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{w}}}}\right>,

useful properties of the Bregman distance, which show the relationship between the

(4)

(iii)

(iv)

{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}(\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
and
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\in\operatorname{null}({\mathbf{A}})
, then
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
.

By Lemma
3.3
, the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
is unique modulo
the null space of
{\mathbf{A}}
, under certain smoothness and convexity assumptions on
{\cal X}
.
These conditions exclude Lebesgue and sequence spaces
{\cal L}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, cf. Example
2.4
(iv).
The standard Landweber method
[
33
,
44
]
constructs an approximation to the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
by running the iterations

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}={\cal J}_{p^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\left({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\mathbf{A}}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}})\right),\quad k=0,1,\ldots,

(7)

where
\mu_{k+1}>0
is the step-size.
Asplund’s theorem
[
46
, Theorem 2.28]
allows characterising the duality map as the
sub-differential,
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}=\partial({\frac{1}{p}\|\cdot\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}})
for
p>1
.
This identifies the descent direction
{\mathbf{A}}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}})
as the sub-gradient:
{\mathbf{A}}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}})=\partial(\frac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}})({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})
.
Note that
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}
is single valued by Assumption
3.1
and Theorem
2.3
, though
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal Y}}
is not.
For well selected step-sizes, Landweber iterations (
7
) converge to an MNS of (
1
)
[
44
, Theorem 3.3]
.

The evaluation of the sub-gradient
{\mathbf{A}}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}})
represents the main
per-iteration cost of the iteration (
7
). In this work, we consider the following Kaczmarz type setting:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\\
\vdots\\
{\mathbf{A}}_{N}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{N}\end{pmatrix},

(8)

where
{\mathbf{A}}_{i}:{\cal X}\rightarrow{\cal Y}_{i}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\in{\cal Y}_{i}
, for
i\in[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Problem (
8
) is defined on the direct product
(\otimes_{i=1}^{N}{\cal Y}_{i},\ell^{r})
, equipped with the
\ell^{r}
norm, for
r\geq 1

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}:=\|({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{1},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{N})\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=\|(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal Y}_{1}},\ldots,\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{N}\|_{{\cal Y}_{N}})\|_{\ell^{r}}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}^{r}\Big)^{1/r}.

(9)

Below we identify
{\cal Y}_{i}={\cal Y}
for notational brevity, and use
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal Y}}
to denote both the norm of the direct product space and the component spaces, though all the relevant proofs and concepts easily extend to the general case. Then the objective
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
is given by

\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}),\quad\mbox{with }\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}.

Note that for many common imaging problems we use
\mathcal{Y}=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, which then naturally gives

\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{pN}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}
. To reduce the computational cost per-iteration, we exploit the finite-sum structure of the objective
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
and adopt SGD iterations of the form

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}={\cal J}_{p^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\left({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\right),

(10)

where
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}=g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i_{k+1})
is the stochastic update direction given by

\displaystyle g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)={\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i})=\partial\Big(\tfrac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}\Big)({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}),

(11)

and the random index
i_{k}
is sampled uniformly over the index set
[N]
,
independent of
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}
. Clearly, it is an unbiased estimator of the sub-gradient
\partial\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, i.e.
\mathbb{E}[g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)]=\partial\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, and the per-iteration cost is reduced by a factor of
N
.

Remark 3.4
.

In the model (
8
), if
{\cal Y}
admits a complemented sum
{\cal Y}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\cal Y}_{i}
, we can take the
(
internal
)
direct sum
(\oplus_{i=1}^{N}{\cal Y}_{i},\ell^{r})
, so that
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{1}+\ldots+{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{N}
and the corresponding norm

\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|=\|(\|{\rm Proj}_{{\cal Y}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}})\|_{{\cal Y}_{1}},\ldots,\|{\rm Proj}_{{\cal Y}_{N}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}})\|_{{\cal Y}_{N}})\|_{r}
.
With this identification the spaces
(\otimes_{i=1}^{N}{\cal Y}_{i},\ell^{r})
and
(\oplus_{i=1}^{N}{\cal Y}_{i},\ell^{r})
are isometrically isomorphic
[
48
]
and the norms are equivalent for all
r\geq 1
.

We now collect some useful properties about the objective
\Psi
and the Bregman divergence. Throughout,
L_{\max}=\max_{i\in[N]}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\|.
Note that
c_{N}=1/N
if
{\cal Y}={\cal L}^{p}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
i\in[N]
,
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}
, and any
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\min}
(such that
{\mathbf{A}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}
), we have

\displaystyle\left<{\partial\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>=p\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\quad\text{and}\quad\left<{\partial\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>=p\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}).

(12)

Moreover,
\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\leq\frac{\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\|^{p}}{C_{p}}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
,
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\leq\frac{L_{\max}^{p}}{C_{p}}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, and for some
C_{N}>0
we have

\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\geq\frac{C_{N}}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}.

Proof.

It follows from the identity
{\mathbf{A}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}
that

\displaystyle\left<{\partial\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>=\left<{{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>=\left<{\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i})},{{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}}\right>=p\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}).

Since
\partial\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, the second identity in (
12
) follows from the linearity of the dual product.
By the
p
-convexity of the space
{\cal X}
and Theorem
2.6
(iii), we get

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}=\frac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}\leq\frac{\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\|^{p}}{p}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq\frac{\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\|^{p}}{C_{p}}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}).

The second claim follows since
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
.
Lastly, by the norm equivalence (
9
) for
1<r<\infty
, there exists
C_{N}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}\geq\frac{C_{N}}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}.

∎

We now focus on the convergence study of the iterations (
10
), without and with noise in the data, and discuss convergence rates under conditional stability.

3.1
Convergence for the Kaczmarz model

Below the notation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
denotes taking expectation with respect to the sampling of the random indices
i_{k}
and
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]
denotes taking conditional expectation with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{k}
. The remaining variables, e.g.
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}
and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}
, are measurable with respect to the underlying probability measure. To study the convergence of SGD (
10
), we first establish
a descent property in terms of the Bregman distance.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold. For any
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}
, the iterates in (
10
) satisfy

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\leq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

(13)

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{k}:={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
.
By Definition
2.5
and expression (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1})},{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>

\displaystyle{=\frac{1}{p}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\left({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\right)\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1})},{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>}.

Using Definition
2.1
, the identity
p(p^{\ast}-1)=p^{\ast}
and Theorem
2.3
(iii), we deduce

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p(p^{\ast}-1)}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>.

Since
{\cal X}
is
p
-convex,
{\cal X}^{\ast}
is
p^{\ast}
-smooth, cf. Theorem
2.3
(i).
By
[
9
, Corollary 5.8]
, this implies

\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\leq\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast})},{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}}\right>,\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\ast}\in{\cal X}^{\ast}.

Using the identities
p^{\ast}(p-1)=p
and
({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}})^{-1}={\cal J}_{{p}^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}
, cf. Theorem
2.3
(iii), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\|\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}-\left<{\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}}\right>.

Combining the preceding estimates gives the desired assertion through

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p}-\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})},{\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>

\displaystyle=\Delta_{k}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

∎

Lemma
3.6
allows showing that the sequence of Bregman distances
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
forms an almost super-martingale (in the Robbins-Siegmund sense defined below) for
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}_{\min}
and well chosen step-sizes
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
We will show the almost sure convergence of the iterates using Robbins-Siegmund theorem.

Theorem 3.7

(Robbins-Siegmund theorem on the convergence of almost super-martingales,
[
38
, Lemma 11]
)
.

Consider a filtration
({\cal F}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and four non-negative,
({\cal F}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
adapted processes
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\beta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(\gamma_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
(\delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Let the sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an
almost super-martingale
, i.e. for all
k
we have

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\alpha_{k+1}]\leq(1+\beta_{k})\alpha_{k}+\gamma_{k}-\delta_{k}.

Then the sequence
(\alpha_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges a.s. to a random variable
\alpha_{\infty}
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty
a.s. on the set
\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k}<\infty,\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}<\infty\}
.

Under certain conditions on
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}
, the limit is the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
. Below
\mathbb{E}_{k}
denotes the conditional expectation with respect to the filtration
{\cal F}_{k}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty,

Assumption
3.1
hold, and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
be the MNS.
Then the sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges a.s. to a solution of (
1
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}_{\min}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}=0\Big)=1.

Moreover, if
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
,
we have
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})=0
a.s.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, we have

\left<{\partial\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>=p\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\leq\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\|\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i})\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}\leq L_{\max}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1},

with
L_{\max}=\max_{i\in[N]}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\|
. Thus, since
p^{\ast}(p-1)=p
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\big]\leq pL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}=pL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}).

Upon taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{k}[\cdot]
of the descent property (
13
) (with
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
), and using the measurability of
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}
with respect to
{\cal F}_{k}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}-p\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})+pL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}).

Using Lemma
3.5
again we have
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\leq\frac{L_{\max}^{p}}{C_{p}}\Delta_{k}
,
which yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}+p}\frac{p}{C_{p}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\right)\Delta_{k}-p\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}).

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
by assumption, we can apply Theorem
3.7

and deduce that the sequence
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges a.s. to a random variable
\Delta_{\infty}
and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})<\infty
a.s.
Let
\Omega
be the measurable set on which
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})<\infty
, and
\mathbb{P}(\Omega)=1
.
Next we show
\liminf_{k}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})=0
a.s. Consider an event
\omega
on which this is not the case, i.e. where
\liminf_{k}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})>0
.
Then there exist
\epsilon>0
and
k_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{\epsilon}
,
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\geq\epsilon
, giving

\sum_{k\geq k_{\epsilon}}\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\geq\epsilon\sum_{k\geq k_{\epsilon}}\mu_{k+1}
.
Since for all events in
\Omega
this would lead to a contradiction: the right hand side diverges (
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
by assumption), whereas the left hand side is the remainder of a convergent series, we conclude
\omega\not\in\Omega
. Since
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{c})=0
, we have
\liminf_{k}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})=0
a.s.
For every event in the set where
\liminf_{k}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})=0
holds we can then find a sub-sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})=0
.
Define also
\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\widehat{\Psi}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, with
\widehat{\Psi}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}
.
We have
\liminf_{k}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})=0
and
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{j}})=0
(on the same subsequence), since by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}\leq N\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

Moreover,
\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})^{p}\leq pN^{p}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
.
The following argument is understood pointwise on the a.s. set
\Omega
where
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})<\infty
, and
\liminf_{k}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})=0
.
Since
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges it is bounded.
By the coercivity of the Bregman distance (see Lemma
A.3
) so are
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
By further passing to a subsequence, we can find a subsequence of
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, that we denote the same, such that

(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\|_{{\cal X}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent,
({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is weakly convergent, and

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})=0\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})\leq\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n})\text{ for all }n<n_{k}.

(14)

The latter can be obtained by setting
n_{1}=1
, and then recursively defining
n_{k+1}=\min\{k>n_{k}:\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\leq\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})/2\}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Any following subsequence satisfies the same property.
Using Theorem
2.6
(ii),
we have for
k>\ell

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{\ell}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})

\displaystyle\!=\!\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\!\Big(\!\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{\ell}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\!-\!\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\!\Big)\!+\!\left<{\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\!)\!-\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{\ell}}\!)},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\!}\right>\!+\!\left<{\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\!)\!-\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{\ell}}\!)},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\!\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\!}\right>.

Since the first two terms involve Cauchy sequences, it suffices to treat the last term, denoted by
{\rm I}_{k,\ell}
.
Using telescopic sum and applying the iterate update rule, we have

\displaystyle{\rm I}_{k,\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\!\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n+1})\!-\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>=\!\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\!\mu_{n+1}\left<{{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{n+1}}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{n+1}})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\mu_{n+1}\left<{\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{n+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})},{{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{n+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{n+1}}}\right>.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and properties of the duality map, we get

\displaystyle|{\rm I}_{k,\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\!\!\mu_{n+1}\!\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{n+1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{n+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{n+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{n+1}}\!\|_{{\cal Y}}\leq\!\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\!\mu_{n+1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i_{n+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n})^{p-1}\widehat{\Psi}_{i_{n+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}}).

Since
\widehat{\Psi}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\leq\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, for all
i\in[N]
, we use (
14
) and get

\displaystyle|{\rm I}_{k,\ell}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\mu_{n+1}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n})^{p-1}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})\leq\sum_{n=n_{\ell}}^{n_{k}-1}\mu_{n+1}\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n})^{p}.

Since
\widehat{\Psi}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})^{p}\leq pN^{p}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, the right hand side of the inequality converges to
0
as
n_{\ell}\to\infty
.
Therefore, by
[
44
, Theorem 2.12(e)]
, it follows that
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, is a Cauchy sequence, and thus converges strongly to an
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}
such that
\Psi(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=0
.

The above argument showing the a.s. convergence of

(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
can be applied pointwise to any solution.
Namely, on the event where
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges strongly to an
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\min}
(i.e.
{\mathbf{A}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}
), define
\widehat{\Delta}_{k}:={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
.
By repeating the argument using Lemma
3.5
, we deduce

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta}_{k+1}\leq\left(1+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}+p}\frac{p}{C_{p}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\right)\widehat{\Delta}_{k}-p\mu_{k+1}\Psi_{i_{k}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}).

Since
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
, it follows that the (deterministic) sequence
(\widehat{\Delta}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a
\widehat{\Delta}_{\infty}\geq 0
.
The continuity of the Bregman distance in the first argument (Theorem
2.6
(vi)) gives

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n_{j}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})={\mathbf{B}}_{p}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=0
,
and thus
\widehat{\Delta}_{\infty}=0
.
Moreover, by the
p
-convexity of
{\cal X}
(Theorem
2.6
(iii)), we have

0\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq\frac{p}{C_{p}}\widehat{\Delta}_{k}.

From the squeeze theorem it follows that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}=0
.
Thus, for every event in an a.s. set
\Omega
, the sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
strongly converge to some minimising solution, that is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}_{\min}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}=0\Big)=1.

Next assume
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\mathrm{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
.
From (
10
), it follows that
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
holds for all
k\geq 1
.
By the continuity of
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}
, we have
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
.
Thus, from
{\mathbf{A}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})=0
and Lemma
3.3
it follows
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
.
∎

The assumptions and conclusions of Theorem
3.8
can be broken down into two parts.
The step-size conditions
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
are required to show the a.s. convergence of
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to
0
, for some non-deterministic
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\min}
.
The remaining assumption
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\mathrm{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
is needed to identify this limit as the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
, as the Landweber method
[
44
, Remark 3.12]
.
If

{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\not\in\overline{\mathrm{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
, we commonly establish
convergence to an MNS relative to
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}
, i.e. a solution
which minimises
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\|_{{\cal X}}
, analogous to the Euclidean case
[
24
]
.

Remark 3.9
.

The stepsize conditions

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
are satisfied by a polynomially decaying step-size schedule
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}=(\mu_{0}k^{-\beta})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}<\beta\leq 1
.

Theorem
3.8
states sufficient conditions ensuring the a.s. convergence of
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
to
0
. To strengthen this to the convergence
in expectation, we require an additional assumption to ensure that
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

is a uniformly integrable super-martingale and the space
{\cal X}
being uniformly smooth. Note that removing the assumptions of Theorem

3.8
from Theorem
3.10

would still result in convergence in expectation to
some non-negative random variable, but not necessarily to
0
. Recall that a family
(X_{t})_{t}
of random variables is uniformly integrable provided
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[\|X_{t}\|\mid\boldsymbol{1}_{\|X_{t}\|\geq k}]=0
, where
\boldsymbol{1}(\cdot)
is the indicator function.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let the conditions of Theorem
3.8
hold with
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
and let

\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}\leq\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then there holds

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]=0.

Moreover, for
1\leq r\leq p
, we have
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{r}]=0
and if
{\cal X}
is additionally uniformly smooth, then
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}]=0
.

Proof.

The step-size conditions allow applying Lemma
A.2
, which yields
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\leq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})
for all
k
.
It follows that
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, and is thus uniformly integrable, and by Theorem
3.8
, it converges a.s. to
0
.
Then, by Vitali’s convergence theorem
[
1
, Theorem 4.5.4]
, we deduce that

(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

converges to
0
in expectation as well.
Using now the
p
-convexity of
{\cal X}
and the monotonicity of expectation, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{C_{p}}{p}\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}]\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]=0.

By the continuity of the power function and the Lyapunov inequality for
1\leq r{\leq p}
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{r}]\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}])^{r/p}=0.

To prove the last claim we use uniform smoothness of
{\cal X}
and Theorem
2.3
(iv), to deduce

\displaystyle\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\leq C\max\{1,\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}},\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}\}^{p}\,{\overline{\rho}_{\cal X}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}})^{p^{\ast}},

where
\overline{\rho}_{\cal X}(\tau)=\rho_{\cal X}(\tau)/\tau
is a modulus of smoothness function such that
\overline{\rho}(\tau)\leq 1
and
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\overline{\rho}(\tau)=0
, cf. Definition
2.2
.
By Lemmas
A.2
and
A.3

(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is (uniformly) bounded, giving that the sequence
(\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded and thus uniformly integrable.
Since
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}]=0
, it follows that
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}
converges to
0
in probability, and thus by the continuous mapping theorem
{\overline{\rho}_{\cal X}}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}})^{p^{\ast}}
also converges to
0
in probability.
Applying Vitaly’s theorem to the uniformly integrable sequence
(\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
yields that it converges to
0
in measure, and the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Note that the condition
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
on
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}
is crucial for ensuring that all the limits are the same.
Landweber iterations converge for uniformly convex and smooth
{\cal X}
, and any Banach
space
{\cal Y}

[
44
, Theorem 3.3]
. In our analysis, we have assumed that
{\cal X}
is
p
-convex to simplify the analysis. First,
p
-convexity is used in the proof of Lemma
3.6
.
If
{\cal X}
were only uniformly convex (and
{\cal X}^{\ast}
only uniformly smooth),
then we may use the modulus of smoothness function
\rho_{\mathcal{X}}
, cf. (
2.2
)
and
[
46
, Theorem 2.41]
, to establish a suitable analogue of the descent
property (
13
). Second,
p
-convexity is used in the proof of Theorem
3.8
, allowing a more direct application of Robbins-Siegmund theorem by relating the objective
values to Bregman distances. Meanwhile, the Landweber method in
[
44
]
requires
step-sizes that depend on the modulus of smoothness, the current iterate and objective
value, which is more restrictive than that in this work.

3.2
Convergence analysis for the generalised Kaczmarz model

Schöpfer et al
[
44
]
studied general powers of
the Banach space norm and sub-gradients of the form
\partial(\frac{1}{q}\|{\mathbf{A}}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q})({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
.
Now we take an analogous perspective for the objective

\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}),\quad\mbox{with }\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}):=\frac{1}{q}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q},

with
1<q\leq 2
. This model is herein called the generalised Kaczmarz model. (Note that this is different from the randomised extended Kaczmarz method
[
53
]
.) We shall show the convergence of SGD with stochastic directions

\displaystyle g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)={\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{\ast}\jmath_{q}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i})=\partial(\tfrac{1}{q}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q})({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}).

(15)

The descent property (
13
) is unaffected, and a direct computation again yields

{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\leq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

(16)

However, Robbins-Siegmund theorem cannot be applied directly.
Instead, we pursue a different proof strategy by first establishing the uniform boundedness of iterates.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let Assumption
3.1
hold.
Consider SGD with descent directions (
15
) for
1<q\leq 2
, and assume that
\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}<\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}}
holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}=:\Gamma<\infty
.
Then
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are uniformly bounded.

Proof.

Let
\overline{\Psi}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q}
, and
\Delta_{k}={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})
. Then we have

\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>=\overline{\Psi}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})
and

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\ast}\jmath_{q}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p^{\ast}(q-1)}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})^{p^{\ast}\frac{q-1}{q}}=L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}},

where
q^{\ast}\geq 2
is the conjugate exponent of
q
.
Plugging this into (
16
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}\leq\Delta_{k}-\mu_{k+1}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}.

(17)

Since
1<p^{\ast}\leq 2
by Theorem
2.6
(iii), and
q^{\ast}\geq 2
, we have
\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}\leq 1
.
Now we define two sets of indices

{\cal I}=\{j\leq k:\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})\geq 1\}\text{ and }{\cal J}=\{j\leq k:\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})<1\},

so that
{\cal I}\cap{\cal J}=\emptyset
, and
{\cal I}\cup{\cal J}=[k]
. Note that
{\cal I}
and
{\cal J}
actually depend on the current iterate index
k
. Applying the inductive argument to (
17
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mu_{j+1}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}\underbrace{-\sum_{j\in{\cal I}}\mu_{j+1}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{j\in{\cal I}}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}}_{(\star)}\underbrace{-\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}\mu_{j+1}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})}_{(\star\star)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\underbrace{L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}}_{(\star\star\star)}.

Next we analyse these three terms separately. First, for
j\in{\cal I}
, we have
\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})\geq 1
and since
\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}<1
, we have
\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\leq\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})
, giving

\displaystyle(\star)

\displaystyle\!\leq\!-\!\sum_{j\in{\cal I}}\mu_{j+1}\!\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})\!+\!L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\!\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\!\sum_{j\in{\cal I}}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})

\displaystyle=\!-\!\sum_{j\in{\cal I}}\!\Big(\!1\!-\!L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}-1}\!\Big)\!\mu_{j+1}\!\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j}).

Since
\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}-1}<\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}}
holds by assumption,
the term
(\star)
is non-positive. Moreover,
(\star\star)
is trivially non-positive.
Since
\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})<1
for
j\in{\cal J}
, the last term
(\star\star\star)
can be bounded as

\displaystyle L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{\Psi}_{i_{j+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{j})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{j\in{\cal J}}\mu_{j+1}^{p^{\ast}}\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}^{p^{\ast}}=L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\Gamma.

By combining the last three bounds on
(\star)
,
(\star\star)
and
(\star\star\star)
, we get

\Delta_{k+1}\leq\Delta_{0}+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\Gamma,\text{ for all }k\geq 0.

Thus,
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded and by Lemma
A.3
, so is
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
3.12
exposes the challenge in extending the convergence
results to general stochastic directions. Namely, in the proof of Theorem

3.8
, we showed the convergence by taking conditional expectation of (
13
), recasting the resulting expression
as an almost super-martingale, and then relating objective values to Bregman distances via
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\leq C\Delta_{k}
, for some
C>0
.
Here, using
\frac{q}{q^{\ast}}=q-1
and
\frac{p^{\ast}}{p}=p^{\ast}-1
, we instead have

\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\leq C\Delta_{k}^{(p^{\ast}-1)(q-1)},\quad\mbox{with }C=q^{-\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}(q-1)}\Big(\frac{p}{C_{p}}\Big)^{(p^{\ast}-1)(q-1)},

which gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq\Delta_{k}+CL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}q^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\Delta_{k}^{(p^{\ast}-1)(q-1)}-q\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}).

Here
0<(p^{\ast}-1)(q-1)<1
, provided
p^{\ast}\neq 2
and
q\neq 2
.
Therefore, Robbins-Siegmund theorem cannot be applied directly. Nonetheless, we still have the following analogue of Theorem
3.10
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Consider iterations (
10
) with descent directions (
15
) for
1<q\leq 2
and let Assumption
3.1
hold and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
be the MNS.
Let the step-sizes
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
, and
\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}<\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges a.s. to a solution of (
1
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}_{\min}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal X}}=0\Big)=1.

Moreover, if
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\mathrm{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})=0\ \mbox{ a.s.}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]=0.

Proof.

To establish the a.s. convergence of iterates, we first take the conditional
expectation of the descent property (
16
) and obtain

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq\Delta_{k}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{\mathbb{E}_{k}[{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}]},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\big].

(18)

We now have

\left<{\mathbb{E}_{k}[{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}]},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>=\left<{\partial\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>=q\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})
,
and

\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\leq L_{\max}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q-1}.

Then taking the conditional expectation of
\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p^{\ast}(q-1)}\Big]=L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\mathbb{E}\Big[(\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\Big].

We have
0<\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}\leq 1
, with the equality achieved only if
p^{\ast}=q^{\ast}=2
.
In the latter case, it trivially follows that
\mathbb{E}[\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}]\leq qL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
.
If
0<\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}<1
, by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\big]

\displaystyle\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\mathbb{E}\Big[(\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\Big]\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{E}[\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{q}])^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}q^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}.

Plugging this estimate into the conditional descent property (
18
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq\Delta_{k}-q\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})+L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}q^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}.

Since the sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded by Lemma
3.12
, so is
(\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and we thus have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{q^{\ast}}}\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty.

Thus, we can apply Robbins-Siegmund theorem for almost super-martingales, and deduce
that
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges a.s. to a non-negative random variable
\Delta_{\infty}
.
Moreover,
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k+1}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})<\infty
holds a.s. By repeating the argument for Theorem
3.8
, there exists a subsequence

({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
that a.s. converges to some
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}_{\min}
,
and hence
\Delta_{\infty}=0
, as desired. Moreover, by Lemma
3.12
,
the sequence
(\Delta_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded, and thus uniformly integrable.
Since it converges to
0
a.s., from Vitali’s theorem it follows that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]=0
.
∎

The results in Theorem
3.13
are similar to that of Theorem
3.10
, but the generality of the latter is compensated for by an additional step-size assumption ensuring boundedness of iterates
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

3.3
Convergence rates for conditionally stable operators

Theorem
3.10
states the conditions needed for the convergence of Bregman distances in expectation.
However, it does not provide convergence rates. In order to obtain convergence rates, one needs additional conditions on the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
, which are collectively known as source conditions. One approach is via conditional stability:
for a locally conditionally stable operator, we can extract convergence in expectation and quantify the convergence speed.
Conditional stability is known for many inverse problems for PDEs, and has been used extensively to investigate regularised solutions
[
8
,
13
]
.
It is useful for analysing ill-posed problems that are locally well-posed, and in case of a (possibly) non-linear forward operator
F
it is of the form

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{2}\|_{{\cal X}}\leq\Phi(\|F({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1})-F({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{2})\|_{{\cal Y}}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{2}\in{\cal M}\subset{\cal X},

(19)

where
\Phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with
\Phi(0)=0
is a continuous, non-decreasing function, and
{\cal M}
is typically a ball in the ambient norm
[
19
]
.
In Banach space settings, the conditional stability needs to be adjusted, by replacing the left hand side of (
19
) with a non-negative error measure
[
7
]
. Since the most relevant error measure for Banach space analysis is the Bregman distance
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{2})
, a Hölder type stability estimate then reads: for some
\alpha\geq 1
and
C_{\alpha}>0

{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\alpha}\leq C_{\alpha}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}.

(20)

Now we give a convergence rate under conditional stability bound (
20
). The constant
C_{N}
appears in Lemma
3.5
and denotes the norm equivalence constant.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let the forward operator
{\mathbf{A}}
satisfy the conditional stability bound
(
20
) for some
\alpha\geq 1
and
C_{\alpha}>0
.
Let
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})\in\overline{\operatorname{range}({\mathbf{A}}^{\ast})}
, and for
C_{k}=C_{N}C_{\alpha}(1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1})>0
, the step-sizes satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}C_{k}=\infty
.
Then there holds

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]=0.

Moreover,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})}{\Big(1+(\alpha-1){\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\alpha-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}C_{j}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}},&\quad\text{ if }\alpha>1,\\
{\exp\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}C_{j}\Big){\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}),}&\quad\text{ if }\alpha=1.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{k}:={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})
. The proof of Theorem
3.8
and
the conditional stability bound (
20
) imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}-p\mu_{k+1}\left(1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}-1}\right)\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}-p\mu_{k+1}\frac{C_{N}C_{\alpha}}{p}\Big(1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}-1}\Big)\Delta_{k}^{\alpha},

since by Lemma
3.5
, there exists a
C_{N}>0
such that

\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\geq\frac{C_{N}}{p}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}
.
Taking the full expectation and using Jensen’s inequality lead to

\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]-\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]^{\alpha}.

Since
C_{k+1}>0
by assumption,
(\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}])_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a monotonically decreasing sequence.
By the convexity of the function
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}
(for
\alpha\geq 1
), for any
\epsilon>0
and
x\geq\epsilon
, we have
\epsilon^{\alpha}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{x}x^{\alpha}
. We claim that for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
k_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]\leq\epsilon
for all
k\geq k_{\epsilon}
.
Assuming the contrary,
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]\geq\epsilon
for all
k
, gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]-\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]^{\alpha}\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]-\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1}\epsilon^{\alpha}\leq\Delta_{0}-\epsilon^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\mu_{j}C_{j}\rightarrow-\infty,

since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu_{j}C_{j}=\infty
by assumption, which is a contradiction.
Therefore,
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]=0
.
For
\alpha>1
, by Polyak’s inequality (cf. Lemma
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Big(1+(\alpha-1)\Delta_{0}^{\alpha-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\mu_{j}C_{j}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}.

Meanwhile, for
\alpha=1
, using the inequality
1-x\leq e^{-x}
for
x\geq 0
, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k+1}]\leq(1-\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1})\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k+1}(1-\mu_{j}C_{j})\Delta_{0}\leq{\exp\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\mu_{j}C_{j}\Big)\Delta_{0},}

completing the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

We have the following comments on Theorem
3.14
.

(i)

The estimates for
\alpha>1
and
\alpha=1
in Theorem
3.14
are consistent in the sense that

\lim_{\alpha\searrow 1}\frac{{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})}{\Big(1+(\alpha-1){\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\alpha-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}C_{j}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-1}}}=\exp\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}C_{j}\Big){\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}).

(ii)

While it might seem counter-intuitive,
\alpha=1
gives a better convergence rate than
\alpha>1
, because of the following

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\alpha}\geq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\tilde{\alpha}}\text{ if and only if }\alpha\log{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\geq\tilde{\alpha}\log{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}).

Hence, whenever
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})<1
, we have
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\geq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\alpha}
for
\alpha>1
.
Plugging this into the conditional stability bound (
20
) yields

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})^{\alpha}\leq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\leq C_{1}^{-1}\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}=C_{1}^{-1}pN\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}).

Meanwhile, the proof of Theorem
3.14
uses the conditional stability bound to establish a relationship between the objective value and the Bregman distance to the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
, cf. (
21
).
Putting these together gives that
\alpha=1
provides a greater decrease of the expected Bregman distance, once we are close enough to the solution.

The conditional stability estimate (
20
) for a linear operator
{\mathbf{A}}
implies its injectivity. Then the objective
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
is strongly convex.
Under condition (
20
), there can indeed be only one solution: if
{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}={\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}
, then
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})=0
follows from (
20
).
The step-size condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}C_{k}=\infty
is weaker than that in Theorem
3.10
.
Namely, it follows from step-size conditions in Theorem
3.8
, since

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}C_{k}=C_{N}C_{\alpha}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}\Big)=\infty

holds if
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
.
Further, if there exists a
C>0
such that
1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}>C
holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, e.g. if
\mu_{k}
is a constant satisfying this condition, then
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}C_{k}=\infty
is weaker than the conditions in Theorem
3.8
, since the condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
is no longer needed for convergence, and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
suffices. Moreover,
we can choose constant step-sizes. Indeed, setting
\mu_{k}=\mu_{0}
, with
1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{0}^{p^{\ast}-1}=\frac{1}{2}
,
we get an exponential convergence rate for
\alpha=1
, since
C_{k}=\frac{C_{N}C_{\alpha}}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\mu_{0}C_{k+1})\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]\leq\bigg(1-2^{-1-1/(p^{\ast}-1)}L_{\max}^{-p^{\ast}/p^{\ast}-1}\Big(\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}}C_{N}C_{\alpha}\Big)^{1/p^{\ast}-1}\bigg)^{k}\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{0}]

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1-2^{-p}L_{\max}^{-p}\Big(\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}}\Big)^{p^{\ast}/p}C_{N}C_{\alpha}\bigg)^{k}\Delta_{0}.

Note that this convergence rate is largely comparable with that in the Hilbert case: the conditional stability bound implies the strict convexity of the quadratic objective
\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
, and the SGD is known to converge exponentially fast (see e.g.
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
), with the rate determined by a variant of the condition number.

Remark 3.16
.

The conditional stability bound (
20
) is stated globally. However,
such conditions are often valid only locally. A local definition could have been
employed in (
20
), with minor modifications of the argument. Indeed, by the argument of Theorem

3.10
, we appeal to Lemma
A.2
,
showing that the Bregman distances of the iterates are non-increasing. Thus, it
suffices to assume that the initial point
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}
is sufficiently close to the MNS
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
.

Remark 3.17
.

Conditional stability is intimately tied with classical source conditions.
For example, as shown in
[
41
]
, assuming
\alpha=1
in (
20
) allows to show a variational inequality

\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}C_{\alpha}^{-1}(pC_{p}^{-1})^{1/p}\|{\mathbf{A}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\|_{{\cal Y}}.

Then Hahn-Banach theorem and
[
41
, Lemma 8.21]
give the canonical range type condition
{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})={\mathbf{A}}^{\ast}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{w}}}
, for
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{w}}}\in{\cal X}
such that
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{w}}}\|_{{\cal X}}\leq 1
.
Connections between source conditions and conditional stability estimates have been studied, e.g. for linear operators in Hilbert spaces
[
47
]
and in
{\cal L}^{p}
spaces
[
5
]
.
Moreover, variational source conditions often imply conditional stability estimates
[
20
]
, and in case of bijective and continuous operators they are trivially inferred by a standard source condition
(
albeit only in a possibly small neighbourhood around the solution
)
.
See the book
[
50
]
about the connections between source conditions and conditional stability estimates, and
[
21
]
for inverse problems for differential equations.

4
Regularising property

In practice, we often do not have access to the exact data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}
but only to noisy
observations
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}
, such that
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}\|_{{\cal Y}}{\leq}\delta
. The convergence study
in the presence of observational noise requires a different approach, since the sequence of objective
values
(\|{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
generally will not converge to
0
. In this section
we show that SGD has a regularising effect, in the sense that the expected error

\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k(\delta)}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]
converges to
0
as the noise level
\delta

decays to
0
, for properly selected stopping indices
k(\delta)
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the
noiseless and noisy iterates, defined respectively by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle={\cal J}_{p^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\left({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\right),\quad\mbox{with }{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}=g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i_{k+1}),

(22)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}^{\delta}

\displaystyle={\cal J}_{p^{\ast}}^{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\left({\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}^{\delta}_{k+1}\right),\quad\mbox{with }{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}^{\delta}=g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta},i_{k+1}).

(23)

The key step in proving the regularising property is to show the stability
of SGD iterates with respect to noise. The noise enters into the iterations
through the update directions
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}^{\delta}_{k+1}
and thus, the stability of the
iterates requires that of update directions. This however requires imposing suitable assumptions
on the observation space
{\cal Y}
since in general, the single valued duality maps
\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}
are
continuous only at
0
. If
{\cal Y}
is uniformly smooth, the corresponding duality maps
are also smooth. This assumption is also needed for deterministic iterates, cf.

[
46
, Proposition 6.17]
or
[
35
, Lemma 9]
. Thus we make the following assumption.

Assumption 4.1
.

The Banach space
{\cal X}
is
p
-convex and uniformly smooth, and
{\cal Y}
is uniformly smooth.

We then have the following stability result on the iterates with respect to noise, whose elementary but lengthy proof is deferred to the appendix.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let Assumption
4.1
hold.
Consider the iterations (
22
) and (
23
) with
the same initialisation
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}^{\delta}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}
, and following the same path
(
i.e.
using same random indices
i_{k}
)
. Then, for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})]=\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}]=\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\mathbb{E}[\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}]=0.

Now we show the regularising property of SGD for suitable stopping indices
k(\delta)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let Assumption
4.1
hold, and the step-sizes
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}}<\infty
and
1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}>C>0
. If
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}k(\delta)=\infty
and
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\delta^{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k(\delta)}\mu_{\ell}=0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k(\delta)}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})]=0.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{k}={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})
and
\Delta_{k}^{\delta}={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})
. Take any
\delta>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
By the three point identity (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}^{\delta}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})+\Delta_{k}+\left<{{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})+\Delta_{k}+\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}.

(24)

Consider a sequence
(\delta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
decaying to zero.
Taking any
\epsilon>0
, it suffices to find a
j_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq j_{\epsilon}
we have
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k(\delta_{j})}^{\delta_{j}}]\leq 4\epsilon
.
By Theorem
3.10
,
there exists a
k_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{\epsilon}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k}]<\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}]<\epsilon^{1/2}.

(25)

Moreover, for any fixed
k_{\epsilon}
, by Lemma
4.2
,
there exists
j_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq j_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k_{\epsilon}}^{\delta_{j}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k_{\epsilon}})]<\epsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k_{\epsilon}})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k_{\epsilon}}^{\delta_{j}})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}]<\epsilon^{1/2}.

(26)

Thus, plugging the estimates (
25
) and (
26
) into (
4
), we have
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k_{\epsilon}}^{\delta_{j}}]<3\epsilon
, for all
j\geq j_{1}
.
Note, however, that the same does not necessarily hold for all
k\geq k_{\epsilon}
, and thus for a monotonically increasing sequence of stopping indices
k(\delta_{j})
, since
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k(\delta_{j})}^{\delta_{j}}]
are not necessarily monotone.
Instead, taking the expectation of the descent property (
13
) with respect to
{\cal F}_{k}
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{k}[\Delta_{k+1}^{\delta}]\leq\Delta_{k}^{\delta}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{\mathbb{E}_{k}[{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}^{\delta}]},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}}\right>+pL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}).

Then we decompose the middle term into

\displaystyle\left<{\mathbb{E}_{k}[{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}^{\delta}]},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}}\right>

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left<{\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta})},{-({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta})+{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}}\right>

\displaystyle=-p\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left<{\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta})},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}}\right>

\displaystyle\leq-p\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}

\displaystyle\leq-p\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})+\delta\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1},

where we have used (
3
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Taking the full expectation gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k}]\!-\!p\mu_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]\!+\!pL_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]\!+\!\delta\mu_{k+1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k}]-p\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]+\delta\mu_{k+1}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1}],

where
C_{k}\!=\!1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}>C>0
. Now using the Lyapunov inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}[\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1}]\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\mathbb{E}[\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}]\Big)^{(p-1)/p}=p^{1/p^{\ast}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]\Big)^{1/p^{\ast}},

we deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k}]-p\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]+\delta\mu_{k+1}p^{1/p^{\ast}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]\Big)^{1/p^{\ast}}.

(27)

Next we remove the exponent in the last term. Using Young’s inequality
ab\leq\frac{a^{p}}{p}\omega^{-p}+\frac{b^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\omega^{p^{\ast}}
, with
a=\delta
and
b=\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]^{1/p^{\ast}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta\Big(\mathbb{E}[\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]\Big)^{1/p^{\ast}}\leq\delta^{p}\frac{\omega^{-p}}{p}+\mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})\Big]\frac{\omega^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\leq\delta^{p}\frac{\omega^{-p}}{p}+\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]\frac{\omega^{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}.

Plugging this back in (
27
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k}]-p\mu_{k+1}C_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]+p^{1/p^{\ast}}(p^{\ast})^{-1}\omega^{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}\mathbb{E}[\Psi({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})]+p^{-1/p}\delta^{p}\omega^{-p}\mu_{k+1}.

Taking
\omega>0
small enough so that
\omega^{p^{\ast}}\leq p^{\ast}p^{1/p}C_{k}
(which can be made uniformly on
k
, thanks to the positive lower bound on
C_{k}
), replacing
k+1
with
k(\delta)
and using the inductive argument, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k(\delta)}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k(\delta)-1}]+p^{-1/p}\omega^{-p}\delta^{p}\mu_{k(\delta)}\leq\mathbb{E}[\Delta^{\delta}_{k_{\epsilon}}]+p^{-1/p}\omega^{-p}\delta^{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k(\delta)}\mu_{\ell}.

Since
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\delta^{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k(\delta)}\mu_{\ell}=0
and
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}k(\delta)=\infty
, there exists
j_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
j\geq j_{2}
we have
k(\delta_{j})\geq k_{\epsilon}
and
p^{-1/p}\omega^{-p}\delta_{j}^{p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k(\delta_{j})}\mu_{\ell}<\epsilon
.
Taking
{j_{\epsilon}}=j_{1}\vee j_{2}
shows
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{k(\delta_{j})}^{\delta_{j}}]<4\epsilon
for all
j\geq j_{\epsilon}
, and hence the desired claim follows.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In the constant step-size regime, such as in the case of conditionally stable operators, the correspondence between the noise level and the step-size regime takes a more standard form.
Namely, the condition in Theorem
4.3
reduces to

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\delta^{p}k(\delta)=0
. In other words, we have
k(\delta)={\cal O}(\delta^{-p})
, mirroring the traditional conditions in Euclidean spaces. Note that the condition on
k(\delta)
is fairly broad, and does not give useful concrete stopping rules directly. Generally, the issue of a posterior stopping rules for stochastic iterative methods is completely open, even for the Hilbert setting
[
22
]
. For a polynomially decaying step-sizes
\mu_{k}=c_{0}k^{-\beta}
, the conditions
\frac{1}{p^{*}}<\beta\leq 1
and
c_{0}<(\frac{P^{\ast}}{L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}G_{p^{\ast}}})^{\frac{1}{p^{\ast}-1}}
give a valid step-size choice, and the stopping index
k(\delta)
should satisfy
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}k(\delta)=\infty
and
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}k(\delta)\delta^{\frac{p}{1-\beta}}=0
.

Remark 4.5
.

It is of much interest to derive a convergence rate for noisy data under a conditional stability condition as in Theorem
3.14
, as a natural extension of the regularising property. However, this is still unavailable. Within the current analysis strategy, deriving the rate would require quantitative versions of stability estimates in Lemma
4.2
in terms of
\delta
and
k
. Generally the convergence rate analysis for iterative regularisation methods in Banach space remains a very challenging task, and much more work is still needed.

5
Numerical experiments

We present numerical results on two sets of experiments to illustrate distinct features of the SGD (
10
). The first set of experiments deals with an integral operator and the reconstruction of a sparse signal in the presence of either Gaussian or impulse noise. On this model example, we investigate the impact of the number of batches and the choice of the spaces
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
on the performance of the algorithm.
To simplify the study we investigate spaces
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
that are smooth and convex of power type, and thus the corresponding duality maps are singletons.
To facilitate a direct comparison of the SGD with the Landweber method, we count the computational complexity with respect to the number of epochs, i.e. the size
N_{b}
of partition defined below.
Note moreover that our implementation of the Landweber method does not use the stepsizes described in
[
44
, Method 3.1]
, since the latter requires knowledge of quantities that are inconvenient to compute in practice.
The second set of experiments is about tomographic reconstruction, with respect to different types of noise. All the shown reconstructions are obtained with a single stochastic run, as is often done in practice, and the stopping index is determined in a trial and error manner so that the corresponding reconstruction yields small errors.

5.1
Model linear inverse problem

First we consider the following model inverse problem studied in
[
28
]
. Let
\kappa:\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, with
\Omega=(0,1)
, be a continuous function, and define an integral operator
{\cal T}_{\kappa}:{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal X}}}(\Omega)\rightarrow{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal Y}}}(\Omega)
, for
1<r_{{\cal X}},r_{{\cal Y}}<\infty
, by

\displaystyle({\cal T}_{\kappa}x)(t)=\int_{\Omega}\kappa(t,s)x(s)ds.

(28)

This is a compact linear operator between
{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal X}}}(\Omega)
and
{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal Y}}}(\Omega)
, with the adjoint
{\cal T}_{\kappa}^{\ast}\!:\!{\cal L}^{r^{\ast}_{{\cal Y}}}(\Omega)\!\rightarrow\!{\cal L}^{r^{\ast}_{{\cal X}}}(\Omega)
given by

({\cal T}_{\kappa}^{\ast}y)(s)\!=\!\int_{\Omega}\kappa(t,s)y(t)dt
.
To approximate the integrals, we subdivide the interval
\overline{\Omega}
into
N\!=\!1000
subintervals
[\frac{k}{N},\frac{k+1}{N}]
, for
k\!=\!0,\!\ldots\!,N\!-\!1
, and then use quadrature, giving a finite-dimensional model
{\mathbf{A}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\!=\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}
, with
{\mathbf{A}}\!=\!\frac{1}{N}\!\left(\kappa\!\left(\frac{j-1}{N},\frac{2k-1}{N}\right)\!\right)_{j,k=1}^{N}
and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\!=\!\left(x\left(\frac{2j-1}{2N}\right)\right)_{j=1}^{N}
.
For SGD we use
N_{b}\in[N]
mini-batches. To obtain equisized batches, we assume that
N_{b}
divides
N
.
The mini-batch matrices
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}
are then constructed by taking every
N_{b}
-th row of
{\mathbf{A}}
, shifted by
j
, resulting in well-balanced mini-batches, in the sense that the norm
\|{\mathbf{A}}_{j}\|
is (nearly) independent of
j
.

The kernel function
k(t,s)
and the exact signal
x^{\dagger}
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\kappa(t,s)=\begin{cases}40t(1-s),&\text{ if }t\leq s,\\
40s(1-t),&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\quad x^{\dagger}(s)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }s\in[\frac{9}{40},\frac{11}{40}]\cup[\frac{29}{40},\frac{31}{40}],\\
2,&\text{ if }s\in[\frac{19}{40},\frac{21}{40}],\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

This is a sparse signal and we expect sparsity promoting norms to perform well.
To illustrate this, we compare the following four settings: (a)
{\cal X}={\cal Y}={\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)
; (b)
{\cal X}={\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
{\cal Y}={\cal L}^{1.1}(\Omega)
; (c)
{\cal X}={\cal L}^{1.5}(\Omega)
and
{\cal Y}={\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)
; (d)
{\cal X}={\cal L}^{1.1}(\Omega)
and
{\cal Y}={\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Setting (a) is the standard Hilbert space setting, suitable for recovering smooth solutions from measurement data with i.i.d. Gaussian noise, whereas settings (b)-(d) use Banach spaces.
Settings (c) and (d) both aim at sparse solutions, and we expect the latter to yield sparser solutions, since spaces
{\cal L}^{r}(\Omega)
progressively enforce sparser solutions as the exponent
r
gets closer to
1
.
In the experiments, we employ the step-size schedule

\mu_{k}=\frac{L_{\max}}{1+0.05(k/N_{b})^{1/p^{\ast}+0.01}},

with
L_{\max}=\max_{j\in[N_{b}]}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{j}\|
.
This satisfies the summability conditions
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}=\infty
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{p^{*}}<\infty
required by Theorem
3.8
.
The operator norm
\|{\mathbf{A}}_{j}\|=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{j}\|_{{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal X}}}\rightarrow{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal Y}}}}=\max_{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\neq 0}\frac{\|{\mathbf{A}}_{j}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal Y}}}}}{\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\|_{{\cal L}^{r_{{\cal X}}}}}
is estimated using Boyd’s power method
[
3
]
.
All the reconstruction algorithms are initialised with a zero vector.

In Fig.

, we compare the reconstructions with settings (a)-(d) for exact data.
We observe from Fig.

{\cal X}={\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)
, result in smooth solutions that fail to capture the sparsity structure of the true signal

. In contrast, the choice
{\cal X}={\cal L}^{1.5}(\Omega)
recovers a sparser solution, and the choice

gives a truly sparse reconstruction, but with peaks that overshoot the magnitude of

.
This might be related to the fact

{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
and data noise. In CT reconstruction, we aim at determining the density of cross sections of an object by measuring the

[
36
]
. Mathematically, the forward map is given by the Radon transform.
In the experiments, the discrete forward operator
{\mathbf{A}}
is defined by a
2D
parallel beam geometry, with
180
projection angles on a
1
angle separation,
256
detector elements, and pixel size of
0.1
. The sought-for signal
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}
is a (sparse) phantom, cf. Fig.

(a).

{\mathbf{A}}

0.01
) or salt-and-pepper noise is added.

5\%
of values changed to either salt or pepper values) and high (
10\%
of values changed) noise regimes.

Appendix A
Technical results and proofs

Lemma A.1

(
[
38
, Lemma 6]
)
.

Let
(\delta_{n})_{n}
be a sequence of non-negative scalars,
(\mu_{n})_{n}
a sequence of positive scalars, and
\alpha>0
.
If

\delta_{n+1}\leq\delta_{n}-\mu_{n+1}\delta_{n}^{1+\alpha},\text{ for all }n=0,\ldots,N,

then

\delta_{N}\leq{\delta_{0}}{\Big(1+\alpha\delta_{0}^{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu_{n}\Big)^{-1/\alpha}}.

A.1
Two elementary estimates

In this section, we present two elementary estimates on the SGD iterates for exact data that are useful in establishing the regularising property.

Lemma A.2
.

Let the sequence
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be generated by iterations (
10
), and let the step-sizes
(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy
\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}\leq\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and stochastic update directions
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k}
be of the form (
11
). Then for any
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in{\cal X}_{\min}
, the sequence
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is non-increasing. In particular, if
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\leq\rho
, then
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})\leq\rho
for all
k
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{k}={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}})
. By Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

By the definition of duality map and the choice of the update directions
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>

\displaystyle=\left<{\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})},{{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}}\right>=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p},

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\ast}\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\leq\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\ast}\|^{p^{\ast}}\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{(p-1)p^{\ast}}=L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}-\mu_{k+1}\left<{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}},{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}}\right>+\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}-\Big(1-L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{k+1}^{p^{\ast}-1}\Big)\mu_{k+1}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p}.

Since
\mu_{k}^{p^{\ast}-1}\leq\frac{p^{\ast}}{G_{p^{\ast}}L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}}
by assumption,
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{0}
, completing the proof.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Coercivity of the Bregman distance)
.

If
\Delta_{k}={\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger})\leq C<\infty
for all
k
, then
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq(2p^{\ast})^{p}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\vee C)
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\Delta_{k}
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\Delta_{k}\geq\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}+\frac{1}{p}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}-\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}.

Then we have
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}(\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}-\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}})\leq\Delta_{k}.

If now
\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}-\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}\leq\frac{1}{2p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}
, it follows
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq(2p^{\ast})^{p}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}
.
Otherwise, if
\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}-\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}\geq\frac{1}{2p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}
, we have

\frac{1}{2p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}\left(\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}-\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}\right)\leq\Delta_{k}.

Combining these two bounds gives
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq(2p^{\ast})^{p}\left(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\vee\Delta_{k}\right)
.
∎

A.2
Proof of Lemma
4.2

To prove Lemma
4.2
, we need the following simple fact.

Lemma A.4
.

For any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the clean iterates
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}
generated by (
22
) are uniformly bounded, i.e. there exists
C_{k}>0
such that

\sup_{\omega\in{\cal F}_{k}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}\leq C_{k}<\infty.

Proof.

If stepsizes
\mu_{k}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
A.2
, the statement
is direct from Lemma
A.3
, and moreover,
C_{k}
can be chosen to
be independent of
k
. Otherwise we proceed by induction.
The induction basis is trivial. Indeed, by the triangle inequality and the definition of duality maps, we have

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}+L_{\max}\mu_{k+1}\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}+L_{\max}\mu_{k+1}\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}+L_{\max}^{p}\mu_{k+1}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}.

Now under the inductive hypothesis
\sup_{\omega\in{\cal F}_{k}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}\leq C_{k}<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}+L_{\max}^{p}\mu_{k+1}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}+\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1})

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{p-1}(1+L_{\max}^{p}\mu_{k+1})+L_{\max}^{p}\mu_{k+1}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\dagger}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p-1}.

This directly proves the statement of the lemma.
∎

Now we can present the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

For any sequence
(\delta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, with
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{j}=0
, we consider
a sequence of random vectors
({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta_{j}},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. We will show by induction that
(for any fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
) the sequence
({\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta_{j}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}))_{j}
is uniformly
bounded, i.e.
\sup_{\omega\in{\cal F}_{k}}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta_{j}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})<\infty
, converges to

0
point-wise, and that
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta_{j}}
is uniformly bounded. The remaining two claims regarding
the convergence of
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}
and
\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}
then follow directly.
For notational brevity, we also suppress the sequence notation
\delta_{j}
, and only use
\delta
. For the induction base, by Theorem
2.6
(i) and (iv), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1})={\mathbf{B}}_{p^{\ast}}\!\Big(\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}),{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{1})\!\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0})-\mu_{1}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}=\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\mu_{1}^{p^{\ast}}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}},

where
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}^{\delta}=g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta},i_{1})
and
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}=g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i_{1})
.
Specifically, in the case (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}^{\delta}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{1}\!\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\!=\!\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}^{\ast}\!\big(\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}^{\delta}\!)\!-\!\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}\!)\big)\!\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}\!

\displaystyle\leq\!L_{\max}^{p^{\ast}}\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{1}}\!)\!-\!\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}\!)\!\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}.

Since
{\cal Y}
is by assumption uniformly smooth, by Theorem
2.3
(iv), we have

\displaystyle\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}^{\delta}\!)\!-\!\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}\!)\!\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\!\max\{1,\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}^{\delta}\!\|_{{\cal Y}},\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{1}}\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{0}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}\!\|_{{\cal Y}}\}^{p-1}\bar{\rho}_{\cal Y}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}\!-\!{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{1}}^{\delta}\!\|_{{\cal Y}}\!).

Upon maximising over
{\cal F}_{1}
, the term in the maximum is uniformly bounded.
Since
\bar{\rho}_{\cal Y}:=\rho_{\cal Y}(\tau)/\tau\leq 1
,
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1})
is uniformly bounded.
Since
\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0}\bar{\rho}_{\cal Y}(\tau)=0
, it follows that
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1})=0
, point-wise.
By the
p
-convexity of
{\cal X}
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{C_{p}}{p}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}^{p}\leq{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}).

Thus,
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}
is uniformly bounded and
\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}=0
, point-wise.
By the uniform boundedness of
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}
and Lemma
A.4
, the sequence
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta}
is also uniformly bounded:

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}\leq\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{1}\|_{{\cal X}}.

(29)

For some
k>0
, assume that
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})
is uniformly bounded, converges to
0
point-wise, as
\delta\to 0^{+}
.
Using the
p
-convexity of
{\cal X}
, it follows that
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}
is uniformly bounded and converges to
0
point-wise, and using again Lemma
A.4
, it follows that
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}
is also uniformly bounded. Then by Theorem
2.6
(i) and (iv), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k+1},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1})={\mathbf{B}}_{p^{\ast}}\!\Big(\!{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1}),{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k+1})\!\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})-\mu_{k+1}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}^{p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{G_{p^{\ast}}}{p^{\ast}}\big(\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}+\mu_{k+1}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{g}}}_{k+1}\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\big)^{p^{\ast}}.

Now we separately analyse the two terms in the parenthesis. First, using the uniform smoothness of
{\cal X}
(and Theorem
2.3
(iv) with
\bar{\rho}_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}(\tau)<C\tau^{p^{\ast}-1}
, cf. Definition
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}\leq C\max\{1,\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal X}},\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}\}^{p-1}\bar{\rho}_{\cal X}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}).

(30)

Since the right hand side is uniformly bounded and converges to
0
point-wise by the induction hypothesis, the same holds for the left hand side.
Next we decompose the second term into a sum of two perturbation terms

\displaystyle\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta},i_{k+1})-g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i_{k+1})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta},i_{k+1})-g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}},i_{k+1})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta},i_{k+1})-g({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta},i_{k+1})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}:={\rm I}+{\rm II}.

First, by the assumption
{\cal Y}
being uniformly smooth and Theorem
2.3
(iv), we have

\displaystyle{\rm I}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\ast}\big(\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}})-\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})\big)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\max}\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}})-\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}})\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq CL_{\max}\max\{1,\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}},\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}\}^{p-1}\bar{\rho}_{\cal Y}(\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\delta}\|_{{\cal Y}}).

By the induction hypothesis and repeating the arguments from the base of induction, the right hand side is uniformly bounded and converges to
0
point-wise. Second, similarly, we have

\displaystyle{\rm II}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}^{\ast}\big(\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}})-\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}})\big)\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\max}\|\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}})-\jmath_{p}^{{\cal Y}}({\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}})\|_{{\cal Y}^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq CL_{\max}\max\{1,\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}},\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{y}}}^{\delta}_{i_{k+1}}\|_{{\cal Y}}\}^{p-1}\bar{\rho}_{\cal Y}(\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal Y}}).

By the same arguments, the right hand side is uniformly bounded.
Moreover,
\|{\mathbf{A}}_{i_{k+1}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal Y}}\leq L_{\max}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}
, which by the induction hypothesis converges point-wise to
0
. Putting all these bounds together yields that
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k+1},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k+1})
is uniformly bounded and converges point-wise to
0
.
Using Vitaly’s theorem, the desired statement follows directly.
Since
{\mathbf{B}}_{p}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k},{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})
is uniformly bounded and converges point-wise to
0
for any
k
, then so does
\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}^{\delta}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}\|_{{\cal X}}
, and consequently by the inequality (
30
) (and (
29
)) so does
\|{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k}^{\delta})-{\cal J}_{p}^{{\cal X}}({\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{k})\|_{{\cal X}^{\ast}}
.
The second part of the claim thus follows. This completes the proof of the induction step, and hence also the lemma.
∎

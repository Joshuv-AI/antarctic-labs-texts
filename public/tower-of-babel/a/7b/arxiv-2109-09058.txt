Mesons and Baryons: Parity Partners
L.X. Gutiérrez-Guerrero, G. Paredes-Torres, A. Bashir

Abstract
We calculate masses of light and heavy mesons as well as baryons of negative parity containing u , d , s , c {\color[rgb]{1,0,0}u},{\color[rgb]{0.64,0,0}d},{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}s},{\color[rgb]{0.55,0,0.55}c} and b {\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b} quarks. It is an extension of our previous work where we had studied the positive parity baryons. We adopt a quark-diquark picture of baryons where the diquarks are non-pointlike with a finite spatial extension. The mathematical foundation for this analysis is implemented through a symmetry-preserving Schwinger-Dyson equations treatment of a vector-vector contact interaction, which preserves key features of quantum chromodynamics, such as confinement, chiral symmetry breaking, axial vector Ward-Takahashi identity and low-energy Goldberger-Treiman relations. This treatment simultaneously describes mesons and provides attractive correlations for diquarks in the 3 ¯ \overline{3} representation. Employing this model, we compute the spectrum and masses of all spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 baryons of negative parity, supplementing our earlier evaluation of positive parity baryons, containing 1, 2 or 3 heavy quarks. In the process, we calculate masses of a multitude of mesons and corresponding diquarks. Wherever possible, we make comparisons of our results with known experimental observations as well as theoretical predictions of several models and approaches including lattice quantum chromodynamics, finding satisfactory agreement. We also make predictions for heavier states not yet observed in the experiment.

I
Introduction

The heavy baryons are an immediate prediction of the quark model and their spectroscopy has
attracted a lot of attention in recent years due to their ongoing and expected observations in particle colliders
such as LHCb and Belle II. However, as has so often been the case for quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and hadron physics, what is relatively easier to calculate
theoretically is harder to measure experimentally and vice versa. For example, QCD based computation of the properties of
triply heavy baryons does not involve the complexities of the light quark dynamics but there exists no experimental signal for any of them. Any realistic estimate of their production
shows that it is a wild goose chase in foreseeable future.

A baryon containing two charm quarks
\Xi_{cc}^{++}
(3621.2
\pm
0.7 MeV) was detected in the LHCb experiment at the Large
Hadron Collider (LHC) at CERN in proton collisions in both the 7TeV and 13TeV runs,
Aaij et al. 2018
.
It has made its entry into the Particle Data Group
Zyla et al. 2020
. A controversial double charm baryon
\Xi_{cc}^{+}
(3519
\pm
2 MeV) was reported earlier by the SELEX Collaboration
Mattson et al. 2002
.
This state remains unconfirmed by FOCUS
Ratti 2003
, BABAR
Aubert et al. 2006
, Belle
Chistov et al. 2006

and LHCb experiments
Chatrchyan et al. 2013
which did not find evidence for a state with the properties reported by SELEX.
However, these null results do not rule out the original observations,
Brodsky et al. 2018
. There are no
doubly heavy baryons observed with two bottom quarks or with one charm and one bottom quark. Several singly heavy quark baryons have been found, see
Zyla et al. 2020
for a detailed list. In this article, we set out to calculate the masses of negative parity baryons of spin 1/2 and 3/2. These are parity partners of the baryons we studied in Ref.
Gutiérrez-Guerrero
et al. 2019
. We employ a coupled analysis of Schwinger-Dyson equations (SDE), Bethe-Salpeter equations (BSE) describing the two-body bound-state problem, and Faddeev equations (FE) for a bound-state of three particles. The computations have been carried out within a vector-vector contact interaction (CI) which preserves essential features of QCD such as confinement, dynamical chiral symmetry breaking (DCSB) and the low energy implications of the divergence of the weak axial vector current.

In relativistic quantum field theory, the parity partner of any given state can be obtained via a simple chiral rotation of the original state. If it were a good symmetry, parity partners would be of equal masses.
However, the spectrum of mesons and baryons explicitly violates this symmetry.
For example, in the light mesons sector, mass of
\rho_{1^{-}}
(770MeV) and that of its parity partner
{a_{1}}_{1^{+}}
(1260MeV)
are not the same. The mass difference is of the order of 500MeV. In the baryon sector, similar amount of mass splitting is observed between
the parity partners
\Delta_{3/2^{+}}
(1232MeV) and
\Delta_{3/2^{-}}
(1700MeV), as well as the nucleon
N_{1/2^{+}}
(939MeV) and
N^{*}_{1/2^{-}}(1535)
. This splitting is due to the effects of the DCSB-dictated repulsion involving
P-wave components of the bound-state wave-functions, incorporated in the kernels of BSE and FE for the negative-parity hadrons.

The chiral-partners structure of hadrons including one heavy quark has been studied in Refs.
Nowak et al. 1993
;
Nowak et al. 2004
;
Bardeen and Hill 1994
;
Bardeen et al. 2003
. In Ref.
Ma and Harada 2016
, doubly heavy baryons with negative parity were investigated by regarding them as chiral partners of the positive parity heavy baryons. The techniques of SDE were employed in
Qin et al. 2019
to study the problem involving any number of heavy
quarks. In this article, we extend the work reported in
Gutiérrez-Guerrero
et al. 2019
to calculate the masses of
negative parity baryons by employing solutions of the BSE and FE. It naturally requires the study of axial and scalar mesons as well their
corresponding diquarks.
We use a realistic description of baryons as bound states of quarks and non-pointlike and dynamical diquark correlations
Bashir et al. 2012a
;
Barabanov et al. 2020
.
This reduces the problem to a simplified sub-structure of several two-body correlations. The same interaction
which describes mesons also generates corresponding diquark correlations in the colour-antitriplet (
\overline{3}
) channel
with half the attractive strength. The diquarks being a colored correlation are confined within the baryons.
The baryon system is bound due to the interaction between the quarks forming a diquark and the spectator quark
which continuously interchange their roles. The validity of the quark-diquark picture
was confirmed in
Eichmann et al. 2010
at the level of 5% for the nucleon mass.

Our article has been organized as follows. In a brief Sec. II, we summarize the main features of the
CI along with the sets of parameters we shall employ in our subsequent analysis. Sec. III is devoted
to a detailed study of the BSE for all the relevant mesons and the corresponding diquarks. In Sec. IV,
we solve the FE and calculate the masses of all the spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 baryon parity partners
containing
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}u},{\color[rgb]{0.64,0,0}d},{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}s},{\color[rgb]{0.55,0,0.55}c},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}
quarks. Sec. V concludes our work. The mathematical details of our
analysis have been presented in appendices A,B and C.

The simplicity of the CI allows one to readily compute hadronic observables, such as masses, decay constants, charge radii and form factors. With this in mind, in the next section, we describe and solve the BSE for mesons and diquarks.

Physically, this might be understood by acknowledging that valence-quarks within a diquark are more loosely correlated than the valence-quark and -antiquark pair in a bound-state meson. Consequently, spin-orbit repulsion in diquarks should be less pronounced than it is in the corresponding mesons.
With the diquark masses and amplitudes described herein one can construct all Faddeev kernels associated with ground-state octet and decouplet baryons, and their chiral partners.

IV.1
Baryons with Spin
1/2

The nucleon’s parity partner is composed
of pseudoscalar, vector, scalar and axial-vector diquark correlations and its Faddeev amplitude must change sign under
a parity transformation. These alterations lead to changes in the locations of the
\gamma_{5}
matrices in the FE.
The Faddeev amplitude of the nucleon parity partner with the interaction employed in this article can be written in terms of:

\displaystyle S(P)

\displaystyle=s(P)\gamma_{5}I_{D},

\displaystyle A_{\mu}^{i}(P)

\displaystyle=ia^{i}_{1}(P)\gamma_{\mu}-a_{2}^{i}(P)\hat{P}_{\mu},

\displaystyle S_{\mathpzc{p}}(P)

\displaystyle=-i{\mathpzc{p}}(P)I_{D},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}^{i}_{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle=iv^{i}_{1}(P)\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}+v^{i}_{2}(P)\gamma_{5}\hat{P}_{\mu}\;,

where
i=0,+
and
\hat{P}^{2}=-1
. The mass of the ground-state baryon with spin
1/2
and negative parity comprised by the quarks
[{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}]
is determined by a
10\times 10
matrix FE.
In the explicit matrix representation, one can write it as follows :

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{r}{\cal S}(P)\,u(P)\\[3.01385pt]
{\cal A}^{i}_{\mu}(P)\,u(P)\\[3.01385pt]
S_{\mathpzc{p}}(P)\,u(P)\\[3.01385pt]
{\mathcal{V}}^{i}_{\mu}(P)\,u(P)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,4\,\int\frac{d^{4}\ell}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,{\cal M}(\ell;P)\left[\begin{array}[]{r}{\cal S}(P)\,u(P)\\[3.01385pt]
{\cal A}^{j}_{\nu}(P)\,u(P)\\[3.01385pt]
S_{\mathpzc{p}}(P)\,u(P)\\
{\mathcal{V}}^{i}_{\nu}(P)\,u(P)\end{array}\right],\rule{10.00002pt}{0.0pt}

where
u(P)
is a Dirac spinor; The kernel in Eq.
IV.1
is
detailed in the appendix
B

The general matrices
{\cal S}(P)
,
{\cal A}^{i}_{\nu}(P)
,
S_{\mathpzc{p}}(P)

{\mathcal{V}}^{i}_{\nu}(P)
which describe the momentum-space correlation between the quark and diquark in the nucleon and the Roper, are described in Refs.
Oettel et al. 1998
;
Cloet et al. 2007
.
The Faddeev amplitude is thus represented by the eigenvector :

\displaystyle\Psi(P)=(s,a_{1}^{0},a_{1}^{+},a_{2}^{0},a_{2}^{+},{\mathpzc{p}},v^{0}_{1},v^{+}_{1},v^{0}_{2},v^{+}_{2})^{T}.

(40)

static approximation

Buck et al. 1992

\displaystyle S(p)=\frac{1}{i\gamma\cdot p+M_{f}}\to\frac{1}{M_{f}}\,.

(41)

A variation of it was implemented in

\displaystyle S(p)=\frac{1}{i\gamma\cdot p+M_{f}}\to\frac{g^{2}_{N\,\Delta}}{i\gamma\cdot p+M_{f}}\,.

IV.2
Baryons with Spin
3/2

Baryons with spin
3/2
are specially important because they can involve states with three
{\color[rgb]{0.55,0,0.55}c}
-quarks and three
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}b}
-quarks. In order to calculate the masses we note that it is not possible to combine a spin-zero
diquark with a spin-1/2 quark to obtain spin-
3/2
baryon. Hence such a baryon is comprised solely of vector correlations.
Understanding the structure of these states is thus simpler in some sense than the nucleon.
The Faddeev amplitude for the positive-energy baryon
is :

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mu}=\psi_{\mu\nu}(P)u_{\nu}(P)\;,

where
P
is the baryon’s total momentum and
u_{\nu}(P)
is a Rarita-Schwinger spinor,

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu\nu}(P)u_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{qq_{1^{+}}\mu}\Delta_{\mu\nu,_{qq}}^{1^{+}}(\ell_{qq}){\cal D}_{\nu\rho}(P)u_{\rho}(P)\,

(45)

and

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{\nu\rho}(\ell;P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal S}(\ell;P)\,\delta_{\nu\rho}+\gamma_{5}{\cal A}_{\nu}(\ell;P)\,\ell^{\perp}_{\rho}\,.

(46)

We give more details of this equation in the appendix
A
.
We assume that the parity partner of a given baryon is obtained by replacing the diquark correlation(s) involved by its (their) parity partners.
We consider the baryons with two possible structures:
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}
.

Baryons(
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}
):
There exists only a single possible combination of diquarks for a baryon composed of the same three quarks
({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q})
. The Faddeev amplitude for this case is:

{\cal D}_{\nu\rho}(\ell;P)u_{\rho}^{B}(P)=f^{B}(P)\,{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}\,u_{\nu}^{B}(P)\,.

(47)

Employing Feynman rules for Fig.
4
and using the expression for the Faddev amplitude, Eq. (
47
), we can write

\displaystyle f^{B}(P){\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}u_{\mu}^{B}(P)=4\frac{g_{B}}{M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}}\int\frac{d^{4}\ell}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathcal{M}f^{B}(P){\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}u_{\nu}^{B}(P)\,,

(48)

where we have suppressed the functional dependence of
\mathcal{M}
on
momenta for the simplicity of notation. We
now multiply both sides by
{\bar{u}}^{B}_{\beta}(P){\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}
from the left and sum over the polarization,
not explicitly shown here, to obtain

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}\Lambda_{+}(P)R_{\mu\beta}(P){\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}=4\frac{g_{B}}{M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}}\int\frac{d^{4}\ell}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathcal{M}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}\Lambda_{+}(P)R_{\mu\beta}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}.

Finally we contract with
\delta_{\mu\beta}

\displaystyle 2\pi^{2}=\frac{1}{M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}}\frac{E_{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}^{2}}{m_{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}^{2}}\hskip-5.69054pt\int_{0}^{1}\hskip-5.69054ptd\alpha\,{\cal L}^{B}\overline{\cal C}^{\rm iu}_{1}(\omega(\alpha,M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}^{2},m^{2}_{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}},m_{B}^{2}))\,,

where we have defined

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{B}=[m_{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}m_{B}^{2}][\alpha m_{B}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}-}M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}]\,.

(50)

From the last two expressions, it is straightforward to compute the mass of the baryon constituted
by three equally heavy quarks.

Baryons(
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}
):

For a baryon with quark structure (
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}
), there are two possible diquarks,
\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}
and
\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}
. The Faddeev amplitude for such a baryon is:

{\cal D}_{\nu\mu}^{B}(P)u_{\mu}^{B}(P;s)=\sum_{i}d^{i}(P)\delta_{\nu\lambda}{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}u_{\lambda}^{B}(P;s),

(51)

where
i=\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}},\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}
. The FE has the form

\left[\begin{matrix}d^{\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}\\
d^{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}\end{matrix}\right]{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}u_{\mu}^{B}=-4\int\frac{d^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{4}}\\
{\cal M}\left[\begin{matrix}d^{\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}\\
d^{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}\end{matrix}\right]{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0}\bf{\gamma_{5}}}u_{\nu}^{B}\,,

(52)

where

\displaystyle\cal{M}=\left[\begin{matrix}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}},\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}&{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}},\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}\\
{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}},\{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}&{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}},\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}_{1^{-}}}\end{matrix}\right]

(53)

with the elements of the matrix
{\cal M}
given by :

\begin{split}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{00}&=t^{f_{00}}\frac{1}{M_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}}}\,\Gamma_{\rho}^{1^{-}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,\bar{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{1^{-}}(-k_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,S(l_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,\Delta_{\rho\nu}^{1^{+}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}),\\
{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{01}&=t^{f_{01}}\frac{1}{M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}}\,\Gamma_{\rho}^{1^{-}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,\bar{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{1^{-}}(-k_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,S(l_{q_{1}})\,\Delta_{\rho\nu,\{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}\}}^{1^{+}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}),\end{split}

\begin{split}{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{10}&=t^{f_{10}}\frac{1}{M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}}\,\Gamma_{\rho}^{1^{-}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,\bar{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{1^{-}}(-k_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,S(l_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,\Delta_{\rho\nu}^{1^{+}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}q_{1}}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}),\\
{\cal M}_{\mu\nu}^{11}&=t^{f_{11}}\frac{1}{M_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}}\,\Gamma_{\rho}^{1^{-}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,\bar{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{1^{-}}(-k_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}})\,S(l_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}_{1}})\,\Delta_{\rho\nu}^{1^{+}}(\ell_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}q}}),\end{split}

(54)

where
t^{f}
are the flavor matrices and can be found in appendix

V
Conclusions

The CI model was first introduced in
Gutierrez-Guerrero
et al. 2010
. It adapts itself well to the infrared
behavior of QCD. It incorporates a mass scale of about 500 MeV for the gluon, mimics confinement through
the absence of quark production thresholds, respects the axial vector Ward-Takahashi identity and preserves low energy Golberger-Treiman relations. Therefore, it is able to reproduce hadron spectrum and masses to a desirable accuracy. Most of the meson and baryon masses containing light and heavy quarks have already been reported in literature using the CI. In this article, we compute the masses of the remaining negative parity baryons containing heavy quarks. The set of parameters we adopt is inspired by a quark-mass fit studied in our previous work
Raya et al. 2018
. In the quark-diquark picture of baryons, we need to evaluate several meson and diquark masses through the BSE before embarking upon the
evaluation of baryon masses through the FE. The Tables
1
,
2
show the parameters used in the entirety of
the article. Tables
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
depict all the meson masses. The diquark masses are detailed in Tables
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
. For bound states of two particles, we have calculated the masses of about 80 particles.

The spin-1/2 and spin-3/2 heavy baryon masses are listed in Table
14
and Table
16
, respectively. Motivated by our earlier satisfactory computation of the singly and doubly heavy positive parity baryons,
and our comparisons of the negative parity baryon masses in the present work with other established models whenever possible, we are confident
our predictions will lie in close proximity of the future experimental observations.
The computed masses in this article include 58 baryons, 29 with positive parity and 29 with negative parity. In total, we present the computation of approximately 138 states.
Our planned next steps of research will involve computation of excited states, tetra- and penta-quarks, as well as form factors of
mesons and baryons containing heavy quarks.

Appendix A
Euclidean Space Conventions

In our Euclidean space formulation:

p\cdot q=\sum_{i=1}^{4}p_{i}q_{i}\,;

(A.1)

where

\displaystyle\{\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}\}=2\,\delta_{\mu\nu}\,;\;\gamma_{\mu}^{\dagger}=\gamma_{\mu}\,;\;\sigma_{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma_{\mu},\gamma_{\nu}]\,;\;

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\,[\gamma_{5}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\rho}\gamma_{\sigma}]=-4\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,,\epsilon_{1234}=1\,.

(A.2)

A positive energy spinor satisfies

\bar{u}(P,s)\,(i\gamma\cdot P+M)=0=(i\gamma\cdot P+M)\,u(P,s)\,,

(A.3)

where
s=\pm
is the spin label. It is conventionally normalised as :

\bar{u}(P,s)\,u(P,s)=2M\,,

(A.4)

and may be expressed explicitly as :

u(P,s)=\sqrt{M-i{\cal E}}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\hskip 14.22636pt\chi_{s}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{P}}{M-i{\cal E}}\chi_{s}\end{array}\right)\,,

(A.5)

with
{\cal E}=i\sqrt{\vec{P}^{2}+M^{2}}
,

\chi_{+}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)\,,\;\chi_{-}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\end{array}\right)\,.

(A.6)

For the free-particle spinor,
\bar{u}(P,s)=u(P,s)^{\dagger}\gamma_{4}
.
It can be used to construct a positive energy projection operator:

\Lambda_{+}(P):=\frac{1}{2M}\,\sum_{s=\pm}\,u(P,s)\,\bar{u}(P,s)=\frac{1}{2M}\left(-i\gamma\cdot P+M\right).

(A.7)

A negative energy spinor satisfies

\bar{v}(P,s)\,(i\gamma\cdot P-M)=0=(i\gamma\cdot P-M)\,v(P,s)\,,

(A.8)

and possesses properties and satisfies constraints obtained through obvious analogy
with
u(P,s)
. A charge-conjugated BSA is obtained via

\bar{\Gamma}(k;P)=C^{\dagger}\,\Gamma(-k;P)^{\rm T}\,C\,,

(A.9)

where “T” denotes transposing all matrix indices and

C=\gamma_{2}\gamma_{4}
is the charge conjugation matrix,
C^{\dagger}=-C
. Moreover, we note that

C^{\dagger}\gamma_{\mu}^{\rm T}\,C=-\gamma_{\mu}\,,\;[C,\gamma_{5}]=0\,.

(A.10)

We employ a Rarita-Schwinger spinor to represent a covariant spin-
3/2
field. The positive energy
spinor is defined by the following equations:

(i\gamma\cdot P+M)\,u_{\mu}(P;r)=0\,,\gamma_{\mu}u_{\mu}(P;r)=0\,,P_{\mu}u_{\mu}(P;r)=0,

(A.11)

where
r=-3/2,-1/2,1/2,3/2
. It is normalised as:

\bar{u}_{\mu}(P;r^{\prime})\,u_{\mu}(P;r)=2M\,,

(A.12)

and satisfies a completeness relation

\frac{1}{2M}\sum_{r=-3/2}^{3/2}u_{\mu}(P;r)\,\bar{u}_{\nu}(P;r)=\Lambda_{+}(P)\,R_{\mu\nu}\,,

(A.13)

where

R_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}\mbox{\boldmath$I$}_{\rm D}-\frac{1}{3}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+\frac{2}{3}\hat{P}_{\mu}\hat{P}_{\nu}\mbox{\boldmath$I$}_{\rm D}-i\frac{1}{3}[\hat{P}_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}-\hat{P}_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}]\,,

(A.14)

with
\hat{P}^{2}=-1
. It is very useful in simplifying the FE for a positive energy decouplet state.

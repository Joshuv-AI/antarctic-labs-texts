Generalised θ \theta operators on unitary Shimura varieties
Lorenzo La Porta

Abstract
. The main result of this paper is the construction of a new class of weight shifting operators, similar to the theta operators of [ 16 ] , [ 10 ] and others, which are defined on the lower Ekedahl-Oort strata of the geometric special fibre of unitary Shimura varieties of signature ( n − 1 , 1 ) (n-1,1) at a good prime p p , split in the in the reflex field E E , which we assume to be quadratic imaginary. These operators act on certain graded sheaves which are obtained from the arithmetic structure of the EO strata, in particular the p p -rank on each stratum. We expect these operators to have applications to the study of Hecke-eigensystems of ( mod ​ p ) ({\mathrm{mod}}\,p) modular forms and generalisations of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

The theory of the classical theta operator was instrumental in the proof of the weight part of Serre’s modularity conjecture of
[
15
]
. Edixhoven’s proof relied, in particular, on the study of the
\theta
-cycles
of Tate and Jochnowitz, introduced in
[
28
]
. Since then, much work has been devoted to extending the construction of this operator to other Shimura varieties, with an eye towards generalisations of Serre’s conjecture, or to gain insight in the Langlands programme
({\mathrm{mod}}\,p)
in a broader sense.

Interesting results have been obtained in the case of Hilbert modular varieties, starting with work of Katz, in
[
33
]
. Following Katz’s construction of
partial Hasse invariants
, Andreatta & Goren, in
[
3
]
, constructed
partial theta operators
and described their kernels and effects on the
weight filtration
. These results have subsequently been improved upon and generalised further, see for instance
[
13
]
.

In
[
48
]
, Yamauchi constructed theta operators for Siegel modular forms, in degree
2
, and managed to study their theta cycles.

In
[
10
]
, de Shalit & Goren, building on their previous work
[
8
]
, constructed
({\mathrm{mod}}\,p)
and
p
-adic theta operators on certain unitary Shimura varieties.

At the same time, Eischen, Mantovan and others, in a series of papers,
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, constructed theta operators on PEL Shimura varieties of types A and C. Their approach is independent from that of de Shalit & Goren and it uses geometric techniques which, unlike the more classical theory, do not rely on
q
-expansions, or more general Fourier-Jacobi and Serre-Tate expansions.

Most of these works leave the problem of studying theta cycles largely open and, where results are obtained, they seem to depend on the specific context. We believe that, in order to understand theta cycles in greater generality, one could benefit from considering new theta or “theta-like” operators, which produce more general weight shifts. Our goal is to present the construction of a new class of
generalised theta operators
that seem to produce exactly the weight shifts that one would expect from a representation-theoretic viewpoint. Our theory of generalised theta operators ties in neatly with the theory of generalised Hasse invariants of
[
7
]
and
[
23
]
.

1.2.
Notations and conventions

We fix
E
a quadratic imaginary extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
p
an odd, rational prime, split in
E
. We write
{\mathbb{F}}
for a given algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. We choose, once and for all, a preferred embedding
\sigma\colon E\to{\mathbb{C}}
, so that
{\mathrm{Hom}}(E,{\mathbb{C}})=\{\sigma,\overline{\sigma}\}
, and an element
i=\sqrt{-1}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\delta_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}
denote the unique generator of the different ideal
\mathfrak{D}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}
with positive imaginary part (with respect to our choices of
\sigma
and
i
). In particular, we have the discriminant
D=D_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}=-{\mathrm{N}}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\delta_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}})=-\delta_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}\overline{\delta}_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}=-\lvert\delta_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}\rvert^{2}
. If we also fix an isomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}\cong\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
, we obtain

{\mathrm{Hom}}(E,{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}(E,\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}_{E}},{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathrm{ur}}_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}_{E}}/(p),{\mathbb{F}}),

the last two isomorphisms depending on the fact that
p
is unramified in
{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
. The last identification induces on
\{\sigma,\overline{\sigma}\}
an action of the Frobenius automorphism
{\mathbb{F}}\to{\mathbb{F}}
by post-composition. Since
p
is split, this action is trivial. We will also write
{\mathcal{O}}_{E,{\mathrm{ur}}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{O}_{E}}[1/2D]
. We fix
n\geq 3
an integer, which will denote throughout the paper the relative dimension of the abelian schemes parametrised by the moduli spaces under consideration.

Unless otherwise specified, we assume that all the schemes we work with are locally noetherian.

1.3.
The elliptic case

We sketch here the construction of the theta operator in the classical case of modular curves, following
[
32
]
. This construction is the prototype on which most of the more general definitions are based.

Let
N\geq 5
be an integer prime to
p
. Let
Y_{1}(N)
, or simply
Y
, be the modular curve of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
over
{\mathbb{F}}
. It is a smooth, affine, connected curve over
{\mathbb{F}}
, which comes equipped with a universal elliptic curve
\pi\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to Y_{1}(N)
. From this universal object we obtain the invertible sheaf
{\underline{\omega}}=\pi_{\ast}\big(\Omega^{1}_{{{\mathcal{E}}}/Y}\big)
, the so-called
Hodge sheaf
. One can consider a projective compactification
Y_{1}(N)\subset X_{1}(N)
, which we may simply denote by
X
, and extend
{\underline{\omega}}
to
X
.

In this setting,
modular forms with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}
are the elements of the graded
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebra
{\mathrm{M}}(N)=\oplus_{k}{\mathrm{M}}_{k}(N)
, with
{\mathrm{M}}_{k}(N)\coloneqq H^{0}(X,{\underline{\omega}}^{k})
. The space of
cusp forms
of weight
k
is
{\mathrm{S}}_{k}(N)\coloneqq H^{0}(X,{\underline{\omega}}^{k}(-C))\subseteq M_{k}(N)
, where
C=X\setminus Y
is the
cuspidal divisor
. On this algebra one can define as usual an action of the Hecke operators
T_{l}
, for
l\neq p
any prime, and hence an action of the Hecke algebra they generate.

Since we are working in characteristic
p
, over
Y
, we can consider the Verschiebung morphism
V\colon{\mathcal{E}}^{(p)}\to{\mathcal{E}}
, which by pullback defines
V\colon{\underline{\omega}}\to{\underline{\omega}}^{(p)}\cong{\underline{\omega}}^{p}
and hence a section
h\in H^{0}(Y,{\underline{\omega}}^{p-1})
. This section extends to a form
h\in M_{p-1}(N)
, which is called the
Hasse invariant
. It has the special property of vanishing with simple zeros precisely at the
supersingular points

Y^{\mathrm{ss}}\subset Y\subset X
and its
q
-expansion at the cusps is identically
1
. The complement
Y^{\mathrm{ord}}=Y\setminus Y^{\mathrm{ss}}
is a dense open subset of
Y
, which is called the
ordinary locus
.

A key observation in Katz’s geometric construction of the classical theta operator is that over the ordinary locus there is a natural splitting for the
Hodge filtration

0\longrightarrow{\underline{\omega}}\longrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}({\mathcal{E}}/Y)\longrightarrow{\underline{\omega}}^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0.

Write
H=H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}({\mathcal{E}}/Y)=R^{1}\pi_{\ast}\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathcal{E}}/Y}
for the (relative) de Rham cohomology of
\pi\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to Y
and let
F\colon H^{(p)}\to H
be the morphism obtained by pulling back via the relative Frobenius
F\colon{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{E}}^{(p)}
. Katz shows that, over
Y^{\mathrm{ord}}
, a natural complement for the subsheaf
{\underline{\omega}}\subset H
is given by
{\mathcal{U}}={{\mathrm{im}}}(F\colon H^{(p)}\to H)
, providing

H\cong{\underline{\omega}}\oplus{\mathcal{U}},

which is called the
unit-root splitting
(of (
1.3
)). While this splitting cannot be naturally extended to
Y
, the projection parallel to it,

p_{\mathrm{ur}}\colon H\to{\underline{\omega}},

does extend to
Y
upon multiplication by the Hasse invariant. This underlies Katz’s construction of the theta operator. We present here a reformulation of these facts which leads naturally to the generalisations we want to discuss. Over
Y^{\mathrm{ord}}
, which can be described as the locus where
V|_{{\underline{\omega}}}\colon{\underline{\omega}}\to{\underline{\omega}}^{(p)}
is an isomorphism, we may consider the composition

H\overset{V}{\longrightarrow}{\underline{\omega}}^{(p)}\overset{V|_{{\underline{\omega}}}^{-1}}{\longrightarrow}{\underline{\omega}}.

It is easy to see that the map
H\to{\underline{\omega}}
from (
1.3
) is precisely
p_{\mathrm{ur}}
. In particular, the morphism
h\cdot p_{\mathrm{ur}}\colon H\to{\underline{\omega}}^{p}
can be written as

H\overset{V}{\longrightarrow}{\underline{\omega}}^{p}\overset{V|_{{\underline{\omega}}}^{-1}}{\longrightarrow}{\underline{\omega}}\overset{h=V|_{{\underline{\omega}}}}{\longrightarrow}{\underline{\omega}}^{p},

which is simply the surjection
V\colon H\to{\underline{\omega}}^{p}
and readily extends from
Y^{\mathrm{ord}}
to the entire modular curve. More generally, one can look at

{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(p_{\mathrm{ur}})\colon{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H)\longrightarrow{\underline{\omega}}^{k},

where
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}
is the
k
-th symmetric power. The locally free sheaf
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H)
admits a descending filtration

F^{i}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H))={{\mathrm{im}}}({\underline{\omega}}^{i}\otimes{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k-i}(H)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H))

and one can show, in the same vein, that the morphism

h\cdot{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(p_{\mathrm{ur}})|_{F^{k-1}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}H)}\colon F^{k-1}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}H)\to{\underline{\omega}}^{k+p-1}

extends from
Y^{\mathrm{ord}}
to
Y
. This is important, because on
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H)
and over
Y
, one can define the Gauss-Manin connection

\nabla\colon{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{Y/{\mathbb{F}}}

which satisfies a general transversality property implying, in particular, that

\nabla({\underline{\omega}}^{k})\subseteq F^{k-1}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H))\otimes\Omega^{1}_{Y/{\mathbb{F}}}\subseteq{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H)\otimes\Omega^{1}_{Y/{\mathbb{F}}}.

All of this is used by Katz to define the theta operator as the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\theta\colon{\underline{\omega}}^{k}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{k-1}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(H))\otimes\Omega^{1}_{Y/{\mathbb{F}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{\omega}}^{k+p+1},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\nabla}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(h\cdot{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k}(p_{\mathrm{ur}}))\otimes{\underline{{\mathrm{ks}}}}^{-1}}}

where
{\underline{{\mathrm{ks}}}}\colon{\underline{\omega}}^{2}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y/{\mathbb{F}}}
is the Kodaira-Spencer isomorphism. A more detailed inspection of
\theta
, for instance via
q
-expansions and the
q
-expansion principle, shows that it extends to an operator over
X
. Taking global sections gives rise to

\theta\colon{\mathrm{M}}_{k}(N)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{S}}_{k+p+1}(N)\subseteq{\mathrm{M}}_{k+p+1}(N),

the
theta operator on modular forms
. This is a derivation of the algebra
{\mathrm{M}}(N)
of modular forms of degree
p+1
. By this we mean that for two modular forms
f,g
, we have
\theta(fg)=f\theta(g)+\theta(f)g
, which is a cusp form of degree
p+1+\deg(fg)
. The operator
\theta
is
h
-linear, in the sense that
\theta(h)=0
. Moreover, one can show that

T_{l}\,\theta=l\,\theta\,T_{l}.

In particular, if
f
is an eigenform, so is
\theta(f)
.

1.4.
The Picard case

We briefly sketch the construction of ordinary and generalised theta operators in the special case of

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1\leq r<n
be an integer and

2.3.
The moduli problem

We are almost ready to formulate the PEL moduli problem we are interested in. We will be working with a neat,
p
-hyperspecial level
K\subseteq\mathbf{G}({\mathbb{A}}^{\infty})
, see
[
37
, 1.4.1.8-13]

Let

be a scheme defined over
\mathcal{O}_{E,(p)}\coloneqq{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}
. We will denote the category of such schemes by
{\underline{{\mathrm{Sch}}}}_{\mathcal{O}_{E,(p)}}
. To

we can associate a quadruple
{\underline{A}}=(A,\lambda,\iota,\eta_{K})
where:

(1)

A\to S
is an abelian scheme.

(2)

\lambda\colon A\to A^{\vee}
is a prime-to-

(3)

\iota\colon{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\to\edom_{S}(A)
is a ring homomorphism such that:

(a)

The Rosati involution is compatible with conjugation on

, which amounts to the relation

\lambda\iota(\overline{\alpha})=\iota(\alpha)^{\vee}\lambda,\,\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}_{E}}.

We will sometimes call this the

.

(b)

On the locally free, finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module

we have an induced action of
{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
. We can consider as above the characteristic polynomial
\det(X-\alpha|_{{\mathrm{Lie}}(A/S)})
, for any
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
. We require that

\det(X-\iota(\alpha)|_{{\mathrm{Lie}}(A/S)})=p_{\alpha}(X)\in{\mathcal{O}_{E}}[X]\subset\mathcal{O}_{S}[X],

for every
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
.

(4)

\eta_{K}
is an
{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
-linear integral level
K
-structure, described in further details below.

An isomorphism of two such quadruples is an isomorphism of the underlying abelian schemes compatible with the remaining pieces of data in the natural way. In
[
37
, Ch. 2]
, it is shown that, under our assumptions on
K
, the functor

\displaystyle{\underline{{\mathrm{Sch}}}}_{\mathcal{O}_{E,(p)}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\underline{{\mathrm{Set}}}},

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\longmapsto\{(A,\lambda,\iota,\eta_{K})\}/_{\cong},

is represented by a smooth, quasi-projective scheme
\mathcal{S}_{K}\in{\underline{{\mathrm{Sch}}}}_{\mathcal{O}_{E,(p)}}
. One can show that

has relative dimension

.

2.3.1.
Level structures

Assume now that
S\in{\underline{{\mathrm{Sch}}}}_{\mathcal{O}_{E,(p)}}
is connected. Let
(A,\lambda,\iota)
be a triple, notations as above, over
S
. Also let

be a geometric point of

. The Tate
\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}
-module
V^{p}A_{s}
of

is a lisse
\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}
-sheaf, which is equivalent to saying that it is determined by the
\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}
-module

together with its continuous
\pi_{1}^{{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}}(S,s)
-action. Notice that

contains a natural
\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{p}
-lattice

.

Definition 2.4
.

A
rational level
K^{p}
-structure
on
A
is a
K^{p}
-orbit
\eta_{K}
of isomorphisms

\eta\colon V_{\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}}\longrightarrow V^{p}A_{s}

of
\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}
-modules such that:

(1)

\eta
is compatible with the action of
{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
on both sides.

(2)

\eta
is isometric, for
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}}
on
V_{\mathbb{A}^{p,\infty}}
and the Weil pairing

on

, meaning that there is an isomorphism

, such that

for all

(3)

The orbit
\eta_{K}
is fixed by the action of
\pi_{1}(S,s)

We say moreover that the structure
\eta_{K}
is

if every choice of

in the orbit

induces an isomorphism of

-modules

and similarly

Notice that
\mathbf{G}(\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{p})
acts simply transitively on the set of isomorphisms

satisfying 1 and 2, when this set is non-empty. The notion of

-level structure is essentially independent of the choice of the point

, see
[
37
, Lemma 1.3.8.6]

we can define a integral level structure by working separately on each connected component, since a locally noetherian topological space is the disjoint union of its connected components, which are clopens.

Remark 2.5
.

In
[
37
, 1.3]
the author gives a more general definition of level structure without any noetherianness assumption. This becomes equivalent to the notion of level we have presented here in the locally noetherian case.

2.4.
Hodge and determinant sheaves

For
G\to S
a group scheme we will use the notation

{\underline{\omega}}_{G/S}\coloneqq e^{\ast}(\Omega^{1}_{G/S})

to denote the cotangent space at the identity

of

. One can show that
f^{\ast}({\underline{\omega}}_{G/S})\cong\Omega^{1}_{G/S}
, where
f\colon G\to{S}
denotes the structure morphism. Let now

be an abelian scheme. Then, we have

, so that

f_{\ast}\Omega^{1}_{A/S}\cong f_{\ast}f^{\ast}{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}\cong f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{A})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}={\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}.

We will call the sheaf
{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}
the
Hodge sheaf
of
A/S
.

Let
{\underline{A}}=(A,\lambda,\iota,\eta_{N})\in\mathcal{S}_{K}(S)
, for some
S\in{\underline{{\mathrm{Sch}}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E,(p)}}
. If
{\underline{A}}
is in the universal class, we will simply write
{\underline{\omega}}
instead of
{\underline{\omega}}_{A/{\mathcal{S}}_{K}}
. Similarly, we will often write
{\underline{\omega}}
instead of
{\underline{\omega}}_{A_{B}/S_{B}}
, for some base change

of
{\mathcal{S}}_{K}
given by
B\to\spec({{\mathcal{O}}_{E,(p)}})
, depending on the context. We will care mostly about the case
B=\spec k
, with
k
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
. As an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-sheaf,
{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}
is locally free of rank
n
with an action of
{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
of signature
(n-1,1)
: the determinant condition on
{\mathrm{Lie}}(A/{S})
implies a dual analogous condition on
{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}
which is equivalent to the fact that

{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S,\sigma}\coloneqq e_{\sigma}\cdot{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S},\quad{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S,\overline{\sigma}}\coloneqq e_{\overline{\sigma}}\cdot{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S},

are locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{S}_{K}}
-sheaves of ranks
n-1
and
1
, respectively. This is the CM decomposition of
{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S}
, in the sense of

{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S,\sigma}
,
{\underline{\omega}}_{A/S,\overline{\sigma}}
the
\sigma
,
{\overline{\sigma}}
-components of the Hodge bundle, respectively.

We recall some basic facts concerning the de Rham cohomology of abelian schemes, following
[
5
, 2.5.1]
. First of all, for
f\colon X\to S
a smooth morphism of schemes of relative dimension
g
we can consider the de Rham complex of finite flat sheaves

\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}\colon 0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\overset{d_{X/S}}{\longrightarrow}\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow\Omega^{g}_{X/S}\longrightarrow 0.

The
i
-th de Rham cohomology of

is the
i
-th derived pushforward
H^{i}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S)\coloneqq R^{i}f_{\ast}\Omega_{X/S}^{\bullet}
of this complex. On
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
, one can consider the usual truncation filtration
F^{\bullet}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S})
, that is,

\displaystyle F^{i}(\Omega^{j}_{X/S})

\displaystyle=0\text{ if }j<i,

\displaystyle F^{i}(\Omega^{j}_{X/S})

\displaystyle=\Omega^{j}_{X/S}\text{ if }j\geq i.

The corresponding filtration spectral sequence

E^{p,q}_{1}=R^{q}f_{\ast}\Omega^{p}_{X/S}\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S)

is called the
Hodge-de Rham spectral sequence
. If we assume that

is quasi-compact, then from this spectral sequence we deduce that the
H^{i}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S)

We consider now the case where

Lemma 2.10

(
\delta

Proposition 2.13

3.
Hecke operators

In this section we recall the definition of the algebra of tame Hecke operators via cohomological correspondences.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a finite flat morphism of schemes (hence separated and quasi-compact). In particular, one can consider the trace morphism
{\mathrm{tr}}\colon f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
, a morphism of quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-modules. Using the trace we can define a duality morphism, denoted
{\mathrm{Tr}}
,

{\mathrm{Tr}}\colon f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})\longrightarrow f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}={\underline{{\mathrm{Hom}}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}(f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}),{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}).

One can see that
f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}
has a natural structure of sheaf of
f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
-modules and
{\mathrm{Tr}}
is the morphism of
f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
-modules defined by
1\mapsto({\mathrm{tr}}\colon f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})
. When
{\mathrm{Tr}}\colon f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})\longrightarrow f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}
is an isomorphism we say that
f
is
separable
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The finite flat morphism
f
is separable if and only if it is unramified, that is,
\Omega^{1}_{X/Y}=0
. Therefore,
f
is separable if and only if it is étale.

Proof.

Since
f
is of finite presentation, being unramified is the same as being formally unramified. Moreover, the statement is local in nature, so we can assume that
Y=\spec A,X=\spec B
, with
A\to B
a finite flat morphism. This is then
[
21
, Thm. 4.6.7-8, 8.3.6]
.
∎

Consider
{\mathcal{G}}
a quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
-module over
Y
. Then we can endow

f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}{\mathcal{G}}={\underline{{\mathrm{Hom}}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}(f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X}),{\mathcal{G}})

with a structure of quasi-coherent
f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
-module. Since
f
is affine, any quasi-coherent
f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
-module is
f_{\ast}
of some quasi-coherent
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module, in a natural, functorial way. We write
f^{!}{\mathcal{G}}
for the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-sheaf corresponding to
f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}{\mathcal{G}}
. The trace map
{\mathrm{Tr}}
induces a morphism

{\mathrm{Tr}}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon f_{\ast}f^{\ast}{\mathcal{G}}=f_{\ast}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}{\mathcal{G}}\longrightarrow f_{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{\vee}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}{\mathcal{G}}=f_{\ast}f^{!}{\mathcal{G}},

hence, by the same correspondence, a morphism
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon f^{\ast}{\mathcal{G}}\longrightarrow f^{!}{\mathcal{G}}

By
3.1
, this map is an isomorphism when

is finite étale.
The functor

, that is, we have a natural functorial isomorphism

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}}(f_{\ast}{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}})\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}({\mathcal{F}},f^{!}{\mathcal{G}}),\quad\forall{\mathcal{F}}\in{\underline{{\mathrm{QCoh}}}}_{X},{\mathcal{G}}\in{\underline{{\mathrm{QCoh}}}}_{Y}.

From this adjunction we obtain the natural counit morphism
f_{\ast}f^{!}{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{G}}
. The composition

{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathcal{G}}\colon f_{\ast}f^{\ast}{\mathcal{G}}\overset{{\mathrm{Tr}}_{\mathcal{G}}}{\longrightarrow}{f_{\ast}f^{!}{\mathcal{G}}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}

we began with. Notice that we have a morphism of

-modules

Let

Definition 3.2

A

In this section we recall how the Gauss-Manin connection and the Kodaira-Spencer map are defined and how they are related. We then proceed to describe some of their basic properties. The classical reference for the definition of the GM connection is
[

]
. We will follow
loc. cit.
closely, but also benefit from the exposition given in the first chapter of
[

and in
[

. For the more context-specific, or arithmetic, properties of the KS morphism we will follow

4.1.
Generalities on filtrations

We record here general, basic facts concerning filtrations. These will be essential in our construction of generalised theta operators.

4.1.1.
Tensor product of filtrations

Let
S
be a scheme and consider
({\mathcal{F}},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{F}})),({\mathcal{G}},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}))
finite locally free sheaves over
S
endowed with finite, separated, exhaustive, descending filtrations

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=F^{0}({\mathcal{F}})\supseteq F^{1}({\mathcal{F}})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq F^{r}({\mathcal{F}})\supseteq F^{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})=0,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=F^{0}({\mathcal{G}})\supseteq F^{1}({\mathcal{G}})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq F^{s}({\mathcal{G}})\supseteq F^{s+1}({\mathcal{G}})=0,

by finite locally free subsheaves
F^{i}({\mathcal{F}}),F^{j}({\mathcal{G}})
, such that the graded pieces
{\mathrm{gr}}^{i}{\mathcal{F}},{\mathrm{gr}}^{j}{\mathcal{G}}
are themselves finite locally free. Then we can defined a
tensor product filtration
on
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}{\mathcal{G}}
with the same properties as the two filtrations on
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
by setting:

F^{k}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}})\coloneqq\sum_{i+j=k}F^{i}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes F^{j}({\mathcal{G}}).

From the hypotheses on
({\mathcal{F}},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{F}})),({\mathcal{G}},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{G}}))
we can deduce the following.

Lemma 4.1

(Grothendieck Group Decomposition)
.

The graded pieces of
F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}})
can be described as:

{\mathrm{gr}}^{k}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}})\cong\bigoplus_{i+j=k}{\mathrm{gr}}^{i}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes{\mathrm{gr}}^{j}({\mathcal{G}}).

Notice that if we have a finite family of finite locally free sheaves
({\mathcal{F}}_{i},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{F}}_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq m}
with filtrations subject to the same conditions as above, we can define by induction a filtration on their tensor product, which turns out to be

F^{k}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{\lvert{\underline{j}}\rvert=k}F^{j_{1}}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})\otimes F^{j_{2}}({\mathcal{F}}_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes F^{j_{m}}({\mathcal{F}}_{m}),

where
{\underline{j}}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
and
\lvert{\underline{j}}\rvert=\sum_{i}j_{i}
. Its graded pieces are

{\mathrm{gr}}^{k}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{\lvert{\underline{j}}\rvert=k}{\mathrm{gr}}^{j_{1}}{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\otimes{\mathrm{gr}}^{j_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathrm{gr}}^{j_{m}}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}.

4.1.2.
Dual filtration

Let
S
and
({\mathcal{F}},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{F}}))
be as above. We can define a decreasing filtration on
{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}
by setting

F^{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\vee})\coloneqq\left({\mathcal{F}}/F^{r-i+1}({\mathcal{F}})\right)^{\vee}.

Its graded pieces are

{\mathrm{gr}}^{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\vee})\cong F^{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\vee})/F^{i+1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\vee})\cong\frac{({\mathcal{F}}/F^{r-i+1}({\mathcal{F}}))^{\vee}}{({\mathcal{F}}/F^{r-i}({\mathcal{F}}))^{\vee}}\cong{\mathrm{gr}}^{r-i}({\mathcal{F}})^{\vee}.

4.1.3.
Filtration of the exterior algebra

Let
S
be as above and consider a ses of finite locally free sheaves over
S
of constant rank

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0.

The
Koszul filtration
of
\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}}
, for some
0\leq j\leq{\mathrm{rk}}({\mathcal{F}})
, is defined by

K^{i}(\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}})\coloneqq{{\mathrm{im}}}(\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}\wedge^{j-i}{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}}),\quad 0\leq i\leq j.

This filtration can also obtained by taking quotients from the tensor product filtration on
{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes j}
. We can extend this to an exhaustive, separated filtration by setting
K^{i}(\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}})=\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}}
for
i<0
and
K^{i}(\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}})=0
for
i>j
. In that case,
(K^{i}(\wedge^{\bullet}{\mathcal{F}}))_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is an exhaustive, separated filtration of
\wedge^{\bullet}{\mathcal{F}}
.
In particular, we have
K^{0}(\wedge^{\bullet}{\mathcal{F}})=\wedge^{\bullet}{\mathcal{F}}
and
K^{i}(\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{F}})=\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
, which could be
0
, if
i>{\mathrm{rk}}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
. The filtration is compatible with the algebra structure given by the
\wedge
product in the sense that
K^{i}\wedge K^{i^{\prime}}\subseteq K^{i+i^{\prime}}(\wedge^{\bullet}{\mathcal{F}})
.
The graded terms of the filtration are

{\mathrm{gr}}^{i}(\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}})=K^{i}/K^{i+1}\cong\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}\wedge^{j-i}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime},\quad 0\leq i\leq j\leq{\mathrm{rk}}({\mathcal{F}}).

In particular, when
j={\mathrm{rk}}({\mathcal{F}})
and
i={\mathrm{rk}}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
we get the usual isomorphism of invertible sheaves

\det{\mathcal{F}}\cong\det{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\otimes\det{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime\prime}.

4.1.4.
Filtration of the symmetric algebra

Let
S
be as above. Let
{\mathcal{G}}
be a finite locally free sheaf. In what follows, for
j<0
, we set

{\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}({\mathcal{G}})\coloneqq\left({\mathrm{Sym}}^{-j}({\mathcal{G}})\right)^{\vee}

and whenever
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{-j}({\mathcal{G}})
is endowed with a filtration,
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}({\mathcal{G}})
is endowed with the dual filtration.

One can consider
{\mathcal{F}}
a finite locally free sheaf over
S
, filtered by finite locally free subsheaves with finite locally free quotients

{\mathcal{F}}=F^{0}({\mathcal{F}})\supseteq F^{1}({\mathcal{F}})\supseteq\cdots\supseteq F^{r}({\mathcal{F}})\supseteq F^{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})=0,

and thus, by taking the quotient of the filtration on
{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes j}
, we get a filtration on
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}
. One can give an explicit description of this filtration:
F^{k}\left({\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is the image of the morphism

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda(k)}{\mathrm{Sym}}^{e_{0}(\lambda)}F^{0}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes{\mathrm{Sym}}^{e_{1}(\lambda)}F^{1}({\mathcal{F}})\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathrm{Sym}}^{e_{r}(\lambda)}F^{r}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}},

where
\Lambda(k)
is the set of monomials
\lambda=X_{0}^{e_{0}}X_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots X_{r}^{e_{r}}
of degree
j
such that
\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}ie_{i}=k
. In particular, we have

F^{0}\left({\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}\right)={\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}},\quad F^{jr}\left({\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}\right)={\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}F^{r}({\mathcal{F}}).

The graded term
{\mathrm{gr}}^{k}\left({\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}\right)
is naturally isomorphic to

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(k)}{\mathrm{Sym}}^{e_{0}(\lambda)}({\mathrm{gr}}^{0}({\mathcal{F}}))\otimes{\mathrm{Sym}}^{e_{1}(\lambda)}({\mathrm{gr}}^{1}({\mathcal{F}}))\otimes\cdots\otimes{\mathrm{Sym}}^{e_{r}(\lambda)}({\mathrm{gr}}^{r}({\mathcal{F}})).

Remark 4.2
.

We will need, for instance, to consider a filtration on
{\mathbb{S}}^{{\underline{k}}}(H_{\sigma})
,
where
{\underline{k}}=(k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\cdots\geq k_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-1}_{\geq 0}
and
H=H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(A/S)
, for
A/S
an abelian scheme with
{\mathcal{O}_{E}}
-action. To construct such filtration, first, we consider the Koszul filtrations
K^{\bullet}(\wedge^{j}H_{\sigma})
,
j=1,\dots,n-1
, induced by the Hodge filtration
8
. Taking tensor products we obtain a filtration on
\otimes_{j}\wedge^{l_{j}}(H_{\sigma})
,
{\underline{l}}
conjugate to
{\underline{k}}
. Similarly we construct filtrations on
H_{\sigma}^{\lvert{\underline{k}}\rvert}
and
\otimes_{i}{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k_{i}}(H_{\sigma})
. Finally we notice that the construction of
{\mathbb{S}}^{{\underline{k}}}(H_{\sigma})
is compatible with these filtrations.

When constructing generalised theta operators we will consider more complicated filtrations, but they will be built following the same ideas.

4.2.
Generalities on connections

We fix for the rest of this section the following data, a special case of
[
30
, 1]
:

X\overset{f}{\longrightarrow}S\overset{g}{\longrightarrow}B

where
f
is smooth and proper and
g
is smooth. In particular, the sheaves
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{i}(X/S)
are finite locally free. We will mainly be interested in the case
X=A
where
{\underline{A}}
is the universal object over
S={\mathcal{S}}_{K,B}
, for some
B\in{\underline{{\mathrm{Sch}}}}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{E,(p)}}
.

Definition 4.3

(Connection)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a quasi-coherent sheaf on
S
. A
B
-linear connection
on
{\mathcal{F}}
is a morphism

\nabla\colon{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}\Omega^{1}_{S/B}

of abelian sheaves satisfying the
Leibniz rule

\nabla(hs)=h\nabla(s)+s\otimes d_{S/B}(h),

where
h
and
s
are sections of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
, respectively, over some open
U\subset S
and
d_{S/B}\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\Omega^{1}_{S/B}
is the exterior differential.

We can derive from
\nabla
, for
({\mathcal{F}},\nabla)
a sheaf with connection, a family of morphisms of abelian sheaves

\nabla_{i}\colon{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Omega^{i}_{S/B}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Omega^{i+1}_{S/B}

defining it on sections
s
and
\omega
of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
\Omega^{i}_{S/B}
, respectively, over some open
U\subset S
, via a generalised Leibniz rule

s\otimes\omega\longmapsto\nabla(s)\wedge\omega+s\otimes d\omega

and then extending by linearity. The
curvature
of the connection
\nabla
is defined as
K(\nabla)\coloneqq\nabla_{1}\circ\nabla\colon{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{S/B}
, which is an
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-linear morphism. A connection is said to be
integrable
if
K(\nabla)=0
.

Given two quasi-coherent sheaves with connections
({\mathcal{F}},\nabla_{{\mathcal{F}}}),({\mathcal{G}},\nabla_{{\mathcal{G}}})
we can use the Leibniz rule to define the tensor product connection
({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}},\nabla_{{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}})
, obtained by setting on local sections

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}}\colon{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S/B},

\displaystyle f\otimes g

\displaystyle\longmapsto\nabla(f)\otimes g+f\otimes\nabla(g)

and then extending by linearity, where, of course, we are identifying
{\mathcal{F}}\otimes\Omega^{1}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}\cong{\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\Omega^{1}
. In particular, from
({\mathcal{F}},\nabla)
we can define a natural connection
\nabla\colon{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes j}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes j}\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S/B}
, for
j\geq 0
, still denoted by
\nabla
. From this, taking quotients, we get well-defined connections on
\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}
. For
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{j}\in{\mathcal{F}}(U)
, over
U\subset S
some open, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\colon s_{1}\wedge s_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{j}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{k=1}^{j}s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\nabla(s_{k})\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{j},

\displaystyle\nabla\colon s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{j}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{k=1}^{j}s_{1}\cdots\nabla(s_{k})\cdots s_{j}.

Consider in particular the case where
{\mathcal{F}}
is finite locally free and it admits a one step-filtration by a finite locally free subsheaf
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
, with finite locally free quotient
{\mathcal{F}}/{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
. Then we can take the Koszul filtration on
\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{F}}
and we immediately see, working with local sections, that the construction we just gave entails the following.

Lemma 4.4

(Koszul transversality)
.

The connection
\nabla
respects the Koszul filtration up to a shift by one, that is, we have, for
i\geq 1
:

\nabla(K^{i}(\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}}))\subseteq K^{i-1}(\wedge^{j}{\mathcal{F}})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S/B}.

More generally, if
({\mathcal{F}},F^{\bullet}({\mathcal{F}}))
is a finite locally free sheaf finitely filtered by finite locally free sheaves with finite locally free quotients, we have

\displaystyle\nabla(F^{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes j}))

\displaystyle\subseteq F^{i-1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\otimes j})\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S/B},

\displaystyle\nabla(F^{i}({\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}{\mathcal{F}}))

\displaystyle\subseteq({\mathrm{Sym}}^{j}F^{i-1}({\mathcal{F}}))\otimes\Omega^{1}_{S/B},

for
i\geq 1
.

4.3.
The Gauss-Manin connection

Consider the first exact sequence of
X/S/B
which, for
f\colon X\to S
smooth, is

(12)

0\longrightarrow f^{\ast}\Omega^{1}_{S/B}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X/B}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{X/S}\longrightarrow 0.

Then we can take the corresponding Koszul filtration of

, for each

, whose graded pieces are

{\mathrm{gr}}^{i}(\Omega^{j}_{X/B})=\Omega^{j-i}_{X/S}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}f^{\ast}\Omega^{i}_{S/B}.

One can check that

is a sub-complex of

. In particular, the finite filtration

gives a convergent spectral sequence whose first page is

\displaystyle E_{1}^{i,j}

\displaystyle=R^{i+j}f_{\ast}({\mathrm{gr}}^{i}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/B}))=R^{i+j}f_{\ast}(\Omega^{\bullet-i}_{X/S}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}f^{\ast}\Omega^{i}_{S/B})=

\displaystyle=R^{j}f_{\ast}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}f^{\ast}\Omega^{i}_{S/B})\cong R^{j}f_{\ast}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}\Omega^{i}_{S/B}

\displaystyle=H^{j}_{\mathrm{dR}}({X/S})\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{S}}\Omega^{i}_{S/B}.

Consider
\nabla=\nabla^{j}=d_{1}^{0,j}
, the differentials on the first page of
E_{\bullet}
. The multiplicative structure of

induces a multiplicative structure on

, in the sense of
[
34
, p. 203]
or
[
46
, 5.4.8]
, given by cup products on
\Omega^{\bullet}_{S/B},H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S)
. From the definition of this multiplicative structure we deduce the following.

Proposition 4.5
.

The map
\nabla^{j}
gives an integrable connection on
{H}^{j}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S)
.

For more details see
[
34
, 2]
.

Definition 4.6

(Gauss-Manin connection)
.

We call the natural connection
\nabla
on

the
Gauss-Manin connection (relative to

)
.

Since the GM connection and the ones derived from it are essentially the only connections we will discuss, we reserve the notation
\nabla
for them, from now on.

Proposition 4.7

(Griffiths transversality)
.

Let
F^{\bullet}=F^{\bullet}(H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S))
denote the Hodge filtration. The GM connection respects this filtration up to a shift by 1, that is, we have

\nabla(F^{i}(H^{j}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S)))\subseteq F^{i-1}(H^{j}_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/S))\otimes\Omega^{1}_{X/S}.

In particular, the GM connection induces a mapping between graded terms.

For more details see
[
30

an abelian scheme this essentially reduces to

2.6

6.1.1.
The ordinary splitting

On

the morphism

induces via

Moreover,

. As in the proof of

relative to the splitting of

given by
{\mathcal{U}}_{\sigma}
. This is usually called the

. We have proved the following.

Lemma 6.2

(Ordinary unit-root splitting)
.

Over the ordinary locus
S^{\mu}_{K}
, the Hodge filtration

admits a natural splitting induced by the Frobenius

The morphism

. In fact, from

we deduce the following.

Lemma 6.3
.

The morphism
A_{1}\cdot p_{{\mathrm{ur}},1}\colon H_{\sigma}\to{\underline{\omega}}_{\sigma}\otimes\det{\underline{\omega}}_{\sigma}^{p-1}

. Similarly, for

Fix some

The morphism

6.2.
Generalised theta operators

We prove here the main result of this paper, Theorem
6.5
. Let us start by recalling some notations. Pick
1\leq r\leq n-1
an integer and
({\underline{k}},w)
an automorphic weight with
k_{n-1}\geq 0
. On
\overline{S}_{K,w_{r}}
we have the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\underline{\omega}}_{0,r}\longrightarrow{\underline{\omega}}_{\sigma}\longrightarrow{\underline{\omega}}_{\mu,r}\longrightarrow 0,

where
{\underline{\omega}}_{0,r}=\ker(V^{r-1}\colon{\underline{\omega}}_{\sigma}\to{\underline{\omega}}_{\sigma}^{(p^{r-1})})
is a locally free sheaf of rank
r-1
. This short exact sequence induces a filtration
F^{\bullet,r}
on
{\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}},w}
over
\overline{S}_{K,w_{r}}
, which is trivial when
r=1
(see
4.1
for more details on the definition of

). The filtration

gives rise to the graded sheaf

over
\overline{S}_{K,w_{r}}
. Notice that the graded sheaf
{\mathrm{gr}}^{\bullet,r}({\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}},w})
decomposes as a direct sum of sheaves

{\mathrm{gr}}^{{\underline{a}},r}({\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}},w})\coloneqq\,\sum_{{\underline{b}}}{{\mathrm{im}}}(\otimes_{j}\wedge^{l_{j}-b_{j}}({\underline{\omega}}_{0})\otimes\wedge^{b_{j}}({\underline{\omega}}_{\mu})\to\otimes_{i}{\mathrm{Sym}}^{k_{i}-a_{i}}({\underline{\omega}}_{0})\otimes{\mathrm{Sym}}^{a_{i}}({\underline{\omega}}_{\mu}))

where
{\underline{a}},{\underline{b}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-1}_{\geq 0}
are such that
a_{i}\leq k_{i},b_{j}\leq l_{j}
for all
i,j
and
\lvert{\underline{a}}\rvert=\lvert{\underline{b}}\rvert
. In particular, given
f\in H^{0}(\overline{S}_{K,w_{r}},\mathrm{gr}^{\bullet,r}({\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}},w}))
, we can write it uniquely as
f=\sum_{{\underline{a}}}f_{{\underline{a}}}
, where
f_{{\underline{a}}}\in H^{0}(\overline{S}_{K,w_{r}},{\mathrm{gr}}^{{\underline{a}},r}({\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}},w}))
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
1\leq r<n
be an integer and
({\underline{k}},w)
an automorphic weight with
k_{n-1}\geq 0
. There exists a differential operator

\theta_{r}\colon{\mathrm{gr}}^{\bullet,r}({\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}},w})\longrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}^{\bullet,r}({\underline{\omega}}^{{\underline{k}}+{\underline{\Delta}}_{r},w}),

defined on the (closure of the) Ekedahl-Oort stratum

, with

{\underline{\Delta}}_{r}=(p+1,p,\cdots,p,1,\cdots,1)

where exactly the last
r-1
entries are
1
. The operator
\theta_{r}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The operator
\theta_{r}
is
A_{r}
-linear, that is,
\theta_{r}(A_{r})=0
, where
A_{r}
is the partial Hasse invariant defined above.

(2)

The operator
\theta_{r}
is Hecke-equivariant.

(3)

Let

and write it as

. Then

is divisible by the Hasse invariant

if and only if for each component

either

or

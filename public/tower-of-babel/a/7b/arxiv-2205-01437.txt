Abstract
Recently CDF II Collaboration reported that they measured W boson mass precisely. The measurement is deviated from the Standard Model (SM) Prediction at 7 σ \sigma . Also, the recent FNAL measurement of the muon magnetic moment shows a 4.2 ​ σ 4.2\sigma deviation. To resolve the W boson as well as the muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) anomalies, we explore the type-X two Higgs doublet model (2HDM). We analyze the ( m A , tan ⁡ β ) (m_{A},\tan\beta) parameter space of the type-X 2HDM consistent with the muon ( g − 2 ) (g-2) , lepton universality test and recent W W boson mass measurments from the CDF II collaboration. We find that the measurement of the h → A ​ A h\to AA branching ratio gives a strong lower mass bound on m H m_{H} .

II.1

The model contains two Higgs doublets

Lepton Universality

The lepton universality has been measured by
Z

\tau

0.1\%

In the type-X 2HDM, loop corrections provide huge contributions due to the enhanced leptonic couplings of extra Higgs bosons with large
\tan\beta

Let us first consider the lepton universality from
Z

The
Z

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}\right)}{\Gamma\left(Z\to e^{+}e^{-}\right)}

\displaystyle 1.0009\pm 0.0028,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(Z\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}\right)}{\Gamma\left(Z\to e^{+}e^{-}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.0019\pm 0.0032,

with a positive correlation of
0.63

For each lepton flavor, we can evaluate different quantities which can be parameterized as

\displaystyle\delta_{\ell\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(Z\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\right)}{\Gamma\left(Z\to e^{+}e^{-}\right)}-1.

A sizable contributions can generate from the tau Yukawa coupling of
m_{\tau}\tan\beta/v

II.5
Constraints from Higss decay

Since the pseudoscalar is lighter than
m_{h}/2
, the SM Higgs can decay to a pair of
pseudoscalars. Since the pseudoscalar couples strongly to leptons, the relevant
signal at the LHC is
pp\to h\to AA\to 4\tau/2\mu 2\tau
. The both CMS and ATLAS have studied
these channels
ATLAS:2015unc
;
CMS:2017dmg
;
ATLAS:2018coo
;
CMS:2018qvj
;
CMS:2019spf
;
CMS:2020ffa
.
From these observations, the limit on
BR(h\to AA)
is as low as
<2\%
for
m_{A}=9
GeV.
For most of the
m_{A}
range, the limit is
BR(h\to AA)<5\%
. We need to satisfy these constraints.

The relevant coupling for the Higgs decay to
AA
is given by
Gunion:2002zf
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}s_{2\beta}^{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}+\lambda_{5})(s_{\beta}^{4}+c_{\beta}^{4})-2\lambda_{5}

\displaystyle\lambda_{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}s_{2\beta}(s_{\beta}^{2}\lambda_{1}-c_{\beta}^{2}\lambda_{2}+c_{2\beta}(\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}+\lambda_{5}))

\displaystyle\lambda_{hAA}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v(\lambda_{T}s_{\beta-\alpha}-\lambda_{U}c_{\beta-\alpha})

(18)

By rewriting
\sin\alpha=\xi_{h}^{\ell}\cos\beta
and by inverting the quartic coupling to the
relevant mass parameter we can calculate the branching ratio as a function of
m_{H},m_{A},\xi_{h}^{\ell}
.
Figure.
3
depictes the branching ratios. Evidently,
BR(h\to AA)
is close to
100\%
for most of the parameter
space, unless the parameters are fine-tuned to cancel.
This cancellation happens for a particular value of coupling modifier depending on the mass of heavy Higgs.
The supression in the branching ratio does not depend on the quartic coupling
\lambda_{1}
, another free parameter of the model.
From the Figure, it is evident that the parameter space is very strongly constrained. Since the coupling
modifier dictates the deviation of Higgs Yukawa coupling, it is constrained by the measurement
of Higgs coupling strength. The current measurement of these coupling strengths by CMS and ATLAS collaboration
allows
\xi_{h}^{\ell}
to be within 0.7 to 1.15 at 2
\sigma

CMS:2022kdi
;
ATLAS:2022yrq
.
Thus, the combination of Higgs signal strength measurement with the measurement of Higgs branching
ratio to light pseudoscalar puts a lower limit on the mass spectrum of 2HDM-X, and the CP even Higgs has to
heavier than 300 GeV.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we explored type-X 2HDM to constrain the
(m_{A},\tan\beta)
parameter region with the recent measurement obtained by CDF II collaboration.
To resolve the W boson mass discrepancy, the mass difference between
m_{H}
and
m_{H^{\pm}}
are the order of
\mathcal{O}(10)
GeV due to enhancement in the
oblique correction,
T\sim 0.1-0.2
. We calculated constraints coming from the lepton universality test and measurement of Higgs signal strength at the LHC. We found that the CDF measurement does not constrain the 2HDM-X parameter space, which can explain the muon
(g-2)
anomaly. Interestingly, the Higgs signal strength
measurement, specifically the measurement of
BR(h\to AA)
, put a strong lower limit on the mass of the heavy Higgs boson, which should be heavier than
200
GeV.

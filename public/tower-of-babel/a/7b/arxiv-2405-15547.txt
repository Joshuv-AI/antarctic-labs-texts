On energy of graphs with self loops


Abstract
Let G G be a simple graph on n n vertices with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . The energy of G G , denoted by, ℰ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{E}(G) is the sum of all absolute values of the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) . It is the first eigenvalue-based topological molecular index and is related to the molecular orbital energy levels of π \pi -electrons in conjugated hydrocarbons. Recently, the concept of energy of a graph is extended to a self-loop graph. Let S S be a subset of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . The graph G S G_{S} is obtained from the graph G G by attaching a self-loop at each of the vertices of G G which are in the set S S . The energy of the self-loop graph G S G_{S} , denoted by ℰ ⁡ ( G S ) \mathcal{E}(G_{S}) , is the sum of all absolute eigenvalues of the matrix A ⁡ ( G S ) A(G_{S}) . Two non-isomorphic self-loop graphs are equienergetic if their energies are equal. Akbari et al. (2023) [ 4 ] conjectured that there exist a subset S S of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) such that ℰ ⁡ ( G S ) > ℰ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{E}(G_{S})>\mathcal{E}(G) . In this paper, we confirm this conjecture. Also, we construct pairs of equienergetic self-loop graphs of order 24 ​ n 24n for all n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . AMS Classification: 05C50. Key Words: Energy of a graph, Singular value of a matrix, Self-loop graph.

Introduction

G

be the eigenvalues of

. The energy of the graph
G
, denoted by
\mathcal{E}(G)
, is the sum of all absolute eigenvalues of
G
(
A(G)
), i.e.
\mathcal{E}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|
. For recent studies including its chemical significance, see
[
2

,
7
,
12
,

13
,

. Let
S
be a subset of

G_{S}
is obtained from the graph

G
which are in the set
S
. The adjacency matrix of the self-loop graph

A(G_{S})=D_{S}(G)+A(G)
, where

\lambda^{S}_{1}\geq\lambda^{S}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda^{S}_{n}

|S|=\alpha
. The energy of self-loop graph

\mathcal{E}(G_{S})

[

\mathcal{E}(G_{S})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\lambda^{S}_{i}-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right|.

Two non-isomorphic self-loop graphs are equienergetic if their energies are equal. The concept of energy of a self-loop graph was put forward very recently by Gutman et al. in
[
9
]

[

[

\mathcal{E}(G_{S})\geq\mathcal{E}(G)
,

Conjecture 1.1
.

For every simple graph
G
of order at least 2, there exists
S\subseteq V(G)
such that
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})>\mathcal{E}(G)
.

In this paper we prove that the Conjecture
1.1
is true. Also, we construct pairs of equienergetic self-loop graph of order
24n
for all
n\geq 1
.

2
Main results

Let
A
be an
n\times n
complex matrix and denote its singular values by
s_{1}(A)\geq s_{2}(A)\geq\cdots\geq s_{n}(A)
. Since the matrices
A(G)
and
A(G_{S})
are symmetric,
\mathcal{E}(G)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(A(G))
and
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\left(A(G_{S})-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)
. We need the following two lemmas to prove our main result.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
8
]

Let
A
and
B
be two
n\times n
matrices. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(A+B)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(A)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(B)

and the equality holds if and only if there exists an unitary matrix
P
such that both the matrices
PA
and
PB
are positive semi-definite. Moreover, if
A
and
B
are real matrices, then the matrix
P
can be taken as real orthogonal.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
10
]

Let
A=[a_{ij}]_{n\times n}
be a positive semi-definite matrix such that
a_{ii}=0
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Then
a_{ij}=a_{ji}=0
for all
1\leq j\leq n
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be any graph, and let
S\subseteq V(G)
be any subset of vertices of
G
. Then the induced subgraph

G[S]
is the graph whose vertex set is
S
and whose edge set consists of all of the edges in
E(G)
that have both endpoints in
S
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V(G)
.

(i)

If
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})<\mathcal{E}(G)
, then
\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S})>\mathcal{E}(G)
. Whereas if
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})=\mathcal{E}(G)
, then
\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S})\geq\mathcal{E}(G)
.

(ii)

If
S
is a vertex independent set of a connected component
H
(say) of
G
such that
|V(H)|\geq 2
, then either
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})>\mathcal{E}(G)
or
\mathcal{E}(V(G)\backslash S)>\mathcal{E}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
G[S]
and
G[V(G)\backslash S]
be the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertex sets
S
and
V(G)\backslash S
, respectively. Let
|S|=\alpha
. Then

A(G_{S})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A(G[S])+I_{\alpha}&X\\[8.53581pt]
X^{T}&A(G[V(G)\backslash S])\end{array}\right]

and

A(G_{V(G)\backslash S})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A(G[S])&X\\[8.53581pt]
X^{T}&A(G[V(S)\backslash S])+I_{n-\alpha}\end{array}\right].

Therefore,

\left[A(G_{S})-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n}\right]+\left[A(G_{V(G)\backslash S})-\Big(\dfrac{n-\alpha}{n}\Big)\,I_{n}\right]=2A(G).

(1)

Applying Lemma
2.1
to the equation (
1
), we get

2\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(A(G))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\left(A(G_{S})-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\left(A(G_{V(G)\backslash S})-\Big(\dfrac{n-\alpha}{n}\Big)\,I_{n}\right).

(2)

That is,

2\,\mathcal{E}(G)\leq\mathcal{E}(G_{S})+\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S}).

(3)

If
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})<\mathcal{E}(G)
, then by equation (
3
), we get

\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S})>\mathcal{E}(G)
, and if
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})=\mathcal{E}(G)
, then again by equation (
3
), we get

\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S})\geq\mathcal{E}(G)
. Proving (i).

Let
S
be a vertex independent set of a connected component
H
of
G
with
|V(H)|\geq 2
. Then

A(G)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\textbf{0}&X\\[8.53581pt]
X^{T}&A(G[V(G)\backslash S])\end{array}\right]~~\mbox{ and }~~A(G_{S})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{\alpha}&X\\[8.53581pt]
X^{T}&A(G[V(G)\backslash S])\end{array}\right].

Since
|V(H)|\geq 2
, we must have
X\neq\textbf{0}
.

Assume that
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})=\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S})=\mathcal{E}(G)
. Then the equality in equation (
2
) holds. Thus by Lemma
2.1
, there exists a real orthogonal matrix
P
such that
PA(G)
,
P\left[A(G_{S})-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n}\right]
and
P\left[A(G_{V(G)\backslash S})-\left(\dfrac{n-\alpha}{n}\right)I_{n}\right]
are positive semi-definite. Moreover since
A
is symmetric, we can further assume that the orthogonal matrix
P
is symmetric.

Now,

P\left[A(G_{S})-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n}\right]=PA(G)+P\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)I_{\alpha}&\textbf{0}\\[8.53581pt]
\textbf{0}&-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n-\alpha}\end{array}\right].

(4)

Since
PA(G)
and
P\left[A(G_{S})-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n}\right]
are positive semi-definite, by symmetry the matrix

P\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)I_{\alpha}&\textbf{0}\\[8.53581pt]
\textbf{0}&-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n-\alpha}\end{array}\right]
is symmetric. Let
P=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}P_{11}&P_{12}\\[8.53581pt]
P_{21}&P_{22}\end{array}\right]
and the matrix

be of order

P\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)I_{\alpha}&\textbf{0}\\
\\
\textbf{0}&-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}I_{n-\alpha}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)P_{11}&-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}P_{12}\\
\\
\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)P_{21}&-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}P_{22}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)P_{11}&\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}\right)P_{12}\\
\\
-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}P_{21}&-\dfrac{\alpha}{n}P_{22}\end{array}\right]

Thus,
P_{12}=P_{21}=\textbf{0}

P=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}P_{11}&\textbf{0}\\[8.53581pt]

Now we are ready to prove Conjecture

Proof of Conjecture
1.1

Let
\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V(G)
and
|S|=\alpha

\mathcal{E}(G_{S})>\mathcal{E}(G)

\mathcal{E}(G_{V(G)\backslash S})>\mathcal{E}(G)

3
Conclusions

In this work, we confirm the conjecture that there exist a subset
S
of
V(G)
such that
\mathcal{E}(G_{S})>\mathcal{E}(G)
by showing that a vertex independent set or its complement set satisfies this condition. In future,
it would be interesting to determine all possible sets
S
for which the condition holds. Also, in this paper, pairs of connected self-loops graphs which are equienergetic are presented using join operation. The problem of determining connected graphs with self-loops whose energies are equal to the ordinary energies of the underlying graphs would be interesting for future work.

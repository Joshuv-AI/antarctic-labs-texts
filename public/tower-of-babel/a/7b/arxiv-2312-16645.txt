Abstract
. We define Hochschild cohomology of the second kind for differential graded (dg) or curved algebras as a derived functor in a compactly generated derived category of the second kind, and show that it is invariant under Morita equivalence of the second kind. A bimodule version of Koszul duality is constructed and used to show that Hochschild cohomology of the second kind is preserved under (nonconilpotent) Koszul duality. We show that Hochschild cohomology of the second kind of an algebra often computes the ordinary Hochschild cohomology of geometrically meaningful dg categories. Examples include the category of infinity local systems on a topological space, the bounded derived category of a complex algebraic manifold and the category of matrix factorizations.



1.
Introduction

Hochschild cohomology of an associative algebra (or, more generally, a dg algebra or dg category) is an important invariant that has been of much current interest, particularly because of its connections to Kontsevich’s deformation quantization. It is known to be invariant under Morita equivalence, in particular, given any (dg) algebra its Hochshild cohomology is isomorphic to the Hochschild cohomology of its derived category.
A related fundamental result, or series of results, is the invariance of Hochschild cohomology under Koszul duality. In other words, Hochschild cohomology of a dg algebra and that of its Koszul dual (dg or curved) conilpotent coalgebra are isomorphic, together with various structures that they possess; we refer to
[
17
]
for the most structured version of this result and its history.

Another version of Hochschild cohomology has been developed (sometimes called Hochschild cohomology of the second kind or compactly supported Hochschild cohomology,
[
20
]
).
Note that the Hochschild complex of a dg algebra is a
double complex
and for the Hochschild cohomology of the first kind one takes its direct products totalization whereas for the Hochschild cohomology of the second kind the totalization is by the direct sums. For an ungraded algebra that makes no difference, but for a genuine dg algebra this leads to completely different theories, the one of the second kind being much more mysterious and less understood. Hochschild cohomology of the second kind belongs to ‘homological algebra of the second kind’, where the notion of a quasi-isomorphism is replaced by a (typically finer) notion of a weak equivalence.
For the general philosophy of homological algebra of the second kind we refer to Positselski’s survey
[
22
]
.

One key motivation to study these new invariants is that derived categories of the second kind carry important geometric information in many examples.
For example the singular cochain algebra on a topological space, the de Rham algebra of a smooth manifold or the Dolbeault algebra of a complex analytic manifold have derived categories of the second kind that capture a lot more geometric invariants than their ordinary derived categories, i.e. the category of
\infty
-local systems and the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves respectively
[
10
,
3
]
.
One expects that Hochschild cohomology of the second kind for these algebras is also geometrically meaningful and in particular computes the ordinary Hochschild cohomology of the associated dg categories (which is not true for the ordinary Hochschild cohomology of these algebras).

This phenomenon was first studied in
[
20
]
in the case of matrix factorizations and we will see that this is more generally the case if one uses a new construction of Hochschild cohomology of the second kind that does not in general agree with compactly supported Hochschild cohomology.
Our construction moreover is Morita invariant in a suitable sense.

In more detail, our results are as follows: after recalling in Section
3
the notion of a twisted complex over a dg category and, as a special case, that of a twisted finitely generated module over a dg algebra together with Morita equivalence for dg categories, we give a proof of the Morita invariance of Hochschild cohomology. Of course, this is a well-known result, however our proof is constructed in such a way that it is suitable for generalization to homological algebra of the second kind.
We review coderived categories of coalgebras and two versions of nonconilpotent Koszul duality between comodules over a (not necessarily conilpotent) dg coalgebra and modules over its Koszul dual dg algebra, following
[
21
]
and
[
13
]
.
In particular, we recall the compactly generated derived category of the second kind
\DII(A)
for a dg algebra
A
, generated by its subcategory
\PerfII(A)
of compact objects.
The category
\DII(A)
is equivalent to the coderived category
\Dco(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A)
of its Koszul dual.

Definition
4.18
.

The
Hochschild cohomology complex of the second kind
of a dg algebra
A
is

\HHIIc(A)=\RHom_{\DII(A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}})}(A,A)
and the
Hochschild cohomology of the second kind
is
\HHII(A)=H(\HHIIc(A))
.

A map of dg or curved algebras
f:A\to B
is a
Morita equivalence of the second kind
if it induces a equivalence
\DII(A)\simeq\DII(B)
and we show Morita invariance for Hochschild cohomology:

Theorem
4.20
.

A Morita equivalence of the second kind between dg algebras
F\colon A\to B
induces a quasi-isomorphism
\HHIIc(A)\simeq\HHIIc(B)
of dg algebras.

This is the content of Section
4
.
As was already mentioned, our version of Hochschild cohomology of the second kind is
not
the same as that of Positselski and Polishchuk
[
20
]
but rather lies between the ordinary Hochschild cohomology and that of Positselski-Polishchuk. In fact, our Hochschild cochain complex of the second kind is a certain completion of the one considered by Positselski-Polishchuk and the ordinary Hochschild cochain complex is a further completion, cf. Remark
5.9
for a precise statement.
Our definition is less elementary than the one in
[
20
]
but has the advantage of being compatible with Koszul duality; in favourable cases the two definitions are equivalent.

Note that Hochschild cohomology, be it of the first or second kind, is constructed as a (co)derived functor of bi(co)modules; whereas Koszul duality is usually formulated as an equivalence between one-sided modules and comodules. It is natural, therefore, to establish Koszul duality as an equivalence between bimodules over an augmented algebra and bicomodules over a suitably Koszul dual coalgebra; note that this is the approach of
[
17
]
.
This is done in Section
5
, both in the context of conilpotent and non-conilpotent Koszul duality. In fact, our result is slightly more general and establishes compatibility of Koszul duality with tensor products of dg algebras and dg coalgebras. A consequence of this compatibility is a Quillen equivalence between the model categories of
C
-bicomodules for a dg coalgebra
C
and dg bimodules over its cobar-construction
\Omega C
.
In the case when
C
is conilpotent, the weak equivalences on the
\Omega C
-bimodule side are the ordinary quasi-isomorphisms (and our result reduces to Keller’s
[
17
, Proposition 2.4]
) but in the non-conilpotent case they are more subtle (closer to isomorphisms).

We deduce:

Corollary
5.7
.

For any dg algebra
A
, there is a quasi-isomorphisms of dg algebras

\HHIIc(A)\simeq\HHc(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A).

Here
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
is the
extended
bar-construction of
A
, cf.
[
1
,
13
,
7
]
concerning this notion.

Similarly, for a dg algebra
A
, we establish two further types of Koszul duality. One is a Quillen equivalence between the category of dg
A
-bimodules with the ordinary model structure (i.e. with quasi-isomorphisms for weak equivalences) and bicomodules over
\mathsf{B}A
, the bar-construction of
A
; this is also essentially Keller’s result in op. cit. The other is a Quillen equivalence between the category of
A
-bimodules with the compactly generated model structure of the second kind and
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
-bicomodules.

For simplicity we state and prove our results first in the differential graded and augmented case and formulate the general results for curved, not necessarily augmented algebras in
Section
6
.

In particular, Definition
1
, Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
hold more generally for curved algebras.
The proofs are mostly the same except that in the curved setting there is no Yoneda embedding available as a curved algebra is not a left or right module over itself. To circumvent this difficulty, we construct for every curved algebra
A
a dg algebra
A^{\prime}
with
\DII(A)\cong\DII(A^{\prime})
and so,
\HHIIc(A)\simeq\HHIIc(A^{\prime})
. It is interesting to observe that the dg algebra
A^{\prime}
is acyclic, in particular its ordinary derived category is trivial as well as the ordinary Hochschild cohomology.

Finally, in Section
7
we give some examples of Hochschild cohomology of the second kind of a dg or curved algebra
A
that can be reduced to Hochschild cohomology of the
first kind
of the dg category of perfect
A
-modules of the second kind, which often has geometric or topological meaning.
Results of this sort were first obtained in
[
20
]
for the category of matrix factorizations.
We show an analogous result as well as two new examples:

•

If
A
is the Dolbeault algebra of a smooth complex algebraic manifold
X
then
\HHII(A)
is Hochschild cohomology of a dg model of the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
,

•

If
A
is is the singular cochain algebra of a topological space
M
or the de Rham algebra of a smooth compact manifold
M
then
\HHII(A)
is Hochschild cohomology of the category of infinity local systems on
M
,

•

If

is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded regular commutative algebra concentrated in even degrees with curvature element

then, under a technical assumption,

is Hochschild cohomology of the category of matrix factorizations

.

2.
Notation and Conventions

We fix a ground field
k
throughout.
The category of (cohomologically
\mathbb{Z}
-graded) differential graded (dg) vector spaces over
k
will be denoted by
\mathsf{DGVect}
. The shift of a dg vector space
A
is denoted by
A[1]
so that
A[1]^{i}=A^{i+1}
. The category
\mathsf{DGVect}
is monoidal with respect to the tensor product; monoids and comonoids in it are dg algebras and dg coalgebras respectively. A
curved
algebra
(A,d,h)
is a graded algebra
A
equipped with an element
h\in A^{2}
and a derivation
d
such that
d^{2}(a)=[h,a]
. If
h=0
, a curved algebra can be viewed as a dg algebra.
A curved module
(M,d)
over
(A,d,h)
is a graded
A
-module
M
with a derivation
d
such that
d^{2}(m)=hm
. The notions of a curved coalgebra and a comodule over it can be defined by duality.
More details on curved (co)algebras and (co)modules can be found e.g. in
[
21
]
.

2.1.
Pseudocompact algebras

Instead of (dg or curved) coalgebras, we will consider (dg or curved) pseudocompact algebras, i.e. topological algebras arising as inverse limits of finite-dimensional discrete algebras.

Taking (continuous) duals gives a contravariant equivalence of categories between coalgebras and pseudocompact algebras, and similarly between right comodules over
C
and right pseudocompact modules over
C^{*}
. Local augmented pseudocompact algebras correspond to conilpotent coalgebras.

A tensor product of pseudocompact algebras or modules is always assumed completed, equivalently it is the linear dual of the tensor product of the dual coalgebras or comodules.

We denote the dual of a dg vector space
V
by
V^{*}
. If
V
is discrete this is
\Hom(V,k)
equipped with the natural inverse limit topology. If
V
is pseudocompact this is the discrete
k
-module of continuous maps
V\to k
. In particular this ensures
V^{**}\cong V
.

2.2.
DG categories

A
dg category

\mathsf{A}
is a category enriched over
\mathsf{DGVect}
; in particular, for each pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathsf{A}
, homomorphisms from
X
to
Y
form a dg
k
-module which we denote by
\Homdg_{\mathsf{A}}(X,Y)
.
A
dg functor
is a functor enriched over
\mathsf{DGVect}
.
Any dg category
\mathsf{A}
has an associated ordinary category
\Ho\mathsf{A}
, called its
homotopy category
, whose objects are the same as
\mathsf{A}
but whose morphisms are defined by
\Ho\mathsf{A}(X,Y)=H^{0}(\Homdg_{\mathsf{A}}(X,Y))
.

A dg functor
F\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
is a
quasi-equivalence
if

(1)

F
induces quasi-isomorphisms
\mathsf{A}(X,Y)\to\mathsf{B}(FX,FY)
for any objects
X,Y
in
\mathsf{A}
.

(2)

H^{0}(F)
is essentially surjective (or, equivalently assuming (1),
H^{0}(F)
is an equivalence of categories).

3.
Twisted modules

3.1.
DG modules and twistings

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a dg category.
A (
right
)
dg
\mathsf{A}
-module
is a dg functor
M\colon\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{DGVect}
.

We denote by
\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\mathsf{A}
the category of dg
\mathsf{A}
-modules.
There is a natural map
h\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\mathsf{A}
, sending an object
Y\in\mathsf{A}
to
\Homdg(-,Y)
, called the
Yoneda embedding
; a dg version of the usual Yoneda lemma says that
h
is fully faithful.
Similarly a
left
dg
\mathsf{A}
-module is a right dg
\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module, i.e. a dg functor
N:\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{DGVect}
. We will always specify if we consider a left dg module; by default a dg module will be a right module.

A dg
\mathsf{A}
-module
M
is
acyclic
if it is acyclic pointwise, i.e.
M(A)
is an acyclic complex for all
A\in\mathsf{A}
.
The
derived category

\D(\mathsf{A})
of dg
\mathsf{A}
-modules is the Verdier quotient of
\Ho(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\mathsf{A})
by acyclic dg
\mathsf{A}
-modules.
There is a model category structure on
\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\mathsf{A}
, where weak equivalences are pointwise quasi-isomorphisms and fibrations are pointwise surjections.
All objects are fibrant; we denote by
\Cof(\mathsf{A})
the cofibrant objects in
\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\mathsf{A}
.
By general results on model categories,
\Ho\Cof(\mathsf{A})=\D(\mathsf{A})
.

Twisted complexes were first defined in
[
6
]
and later redefined in
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A (
two-sided
)
twisted complex
over
\mathsf{A}
is a formal expression
(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}[r_{i}],q)
, where
C_{i}\in\mathsf{A}
,
r_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n\geq 0
,
q=(q_{ij})
,
q_{ij}\in\Hom(C_{j}[r_{j}],C_{i}[r_{i}])
homogeneous of degree 1 such that
dq+q^{2}=0
.
A twisted complex is

q_{ij}=0
for all

.

For two twisted complexes

C^{\prime}
, the space of
morphisms of twisted complexes

is the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
k
-module of matrices

,
f_{ij}\in\Hom(C_{j}[r_{j}],C^{\prime}_{i}[r^{\prime}_{i}])
with differential

df=(df_{ij})+q^{\prime}f-(-1)^{|f_{ij}|}fq
.
Composition of morphisms is usual matrix multiplication.

We denote the dg category of twisted complexes over
\mathsf{A}
by
\Tw(\mathsf{A})
and the dg category of one-sided twisted complexes by
\tw(\mathsf{A})
.
In
[
6
]
, these were respectively denoted

can alternatively be defined as the pretriangulated hull of

, that is, it is the closure of
\mathsf{A}
under shifts and cones.

There is a natural functor from twisted complexes over a dg category
\mathsf{A}
to right dg
\mathsf{A}
-modules: We send
(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}[r_{i}],q)
to
(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Hom(A,C_{i})[r_{i}],q_{*})
.

As an example, consider the case where
\mathsf{A}
is a dg algebra
(A,d_{A})
, considered as a dg category with one object.
Recall that a

in

is an element

such that

, and that the set of all Maurer–Cartan elements in

is denoted by

.
Then a twisted complex over

is a pair
(M,q)
where
M\cong V\otimes A
as an
A
-module for some finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
V
, and

.
The
A
-module

becomes a (right) dg

-module when equipped with the differential

, and in fact, every dg
A
-module structure on the

-module

arises this way, as noted in
[
10

.
A twisted complex over

is therefore precisely a
finitely generated
twisted

-module in the following sense.

Definition 3.2
.

A
twisted
A
-module
over a dg algebra

is a dg

-module that is free as an

-module after forgetting the differential, that is, it is isomorphic as an
A
-module to

for some graded vector space

.
A

is a twisted

Note that there is a slight clash of terminology here; nevertheless this is unavoidable as we wish to refer to non-finitely generated twisted modules later.
Note also that

above is a dg
(\End V\otimes A)
-

-

by the element

.
More generally, we have the following.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(A,d_{A})
be a dg algebra and
x\in\MC(A)
.

(1)

The
twisted algebra of
A
by

(2)

Let

be a

The

, denoted

The condition

A

-module for a dg category

3.2.
DG Morita equivalence

A dg functor
F\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
is a (
dg
)
Morita equivalence
if the induced map
F_{!}\colon\D(\mathsf{A})\to\D(\mathsf{B})
is an equivalence of triangulated categories.
Equivalently,
F
is a Morita equivalence if it induces a quasi-equivalence
\tw(\mathsf{A})\to\tw(\mathsf{B})
,
\Perf(\mathsf{A})\to\Perf(\mathsf{B})
or
\Cof(\mathsf{A})\to\Cof(\mathsf{B})
. (The first two statements follow from the fact that
\D{A}
is compactly generated by
\Perf(\mathsf{A})
.)
We will abuse notation and also denote these maps by
F_{!}
.

A main result of
[
6
]
is that the Yoneda embedding
h\colon\mathsf{A}\to\tw(\mathsf{A})\hookrightarrow\Tw(\mathsf{A})
induces dg equivalences
\tw(\mathsf{A})\to\tw(\tw(\mathsf{A}))
and
\Tw(\mathsf{A})\to\Tw(\Tw(\mathsf{A}))
.
In particular, this implies that
h_{!}\colon\Perf(\mathsf{A})\to\Perf(\Perf(\mathsf{A}))
is a quasi-equivalence, so the Yoneda embedding is a Morita equivalence.
In fact,
\Perf(\mathsf{A})
is a Morita fibrant replacement of
\mathsf{A}
in the sense of
[
16
, Section 4.6]
.

4.
Hochschild cohomology of the first and second kind

In this section we recall Hochschild cohomology for dg categories and define Hochschild cohomology of the second kind for dg algebras.
It is well-known that Hochschild cohomology is Morita invariant; we will give an alternative proof of this fact and show how the proof can be adapted to give a Morita invariance result in the second kind case.

4.1.
Hochschild cohomology of the first kind

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a dg category.
The
Hochschild cohomology complex
of
\mathsf{A}
is
\HHc(\mathsf{A})=\RHom_{\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}}}(\mathsf{A},\mathsf{A})
.
This is a dg algebra considered as the algebra of derived endomorphisms of
A
and an
A
-bimodule. The
Hochschild cohomology
of
A
is
\HH(\mathsf{A})=H(\HHc(\mathsf{A}))
.

We show that Hochschild cohomology is invariant under Morita equivalence. This is well-known, cf.

[
26
, Corollary 8.2]

but we give a proof that is different from the standard proofs and will be used as a model when proving the analogous result for Hochschild cohomology of the second kind.

Theorem 4.1
.

A Morita equivalence
F\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
induces a quasi-isomorphism
\HHc(\mathsf{A})\simeq\HHc(\mathsf{B})
of dg algebras.

Proof.

Since
F\colon\mathsf{A}\to\mathsf{B}
induces a map

(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}\colon\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}(\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}})\to\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}(\mathsf{B}\otimes\mathsf{B}^{\mathrm{op}})

sending
\mathsf{A}
to
\mathsf{B}
, and all bimodules admit cofibrant replacements, it suffices to show that

(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}\colon\Cof(\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}})\to\Cof(\mathsf{B}\otimes\mathsf{B}^{\mathrm{op}})

is a quasi-equivalence.

Indeed, the restriction of
(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}
to
\Perf(\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}})
fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Perf(\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Perf(\Perf(\mathsf{A})\otimes\Perf(\mathsf{A})^{\mathrm{op}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Perf(\mathsf{B}\otimes\mathsf{B}^{\mathrm{op}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Perf(\Perf(\mathsf{B})\otimes\Perf(\mathsf{B})^{\mathrm{op}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(h\otimes h^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(h\otimes h^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{!}\otimes F_{!}^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}

where the rows are quasi-equivalences as the Yoneda embedding
h
induces a quasi-equivalence
h_{!}\colon\Perf(\mathsf{A})\to\Perf(\Perf(\mathsf{A}))
, and
(F_{!}\otimes F_{!}^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}
is a quasi-equivalence as
F
is a Morita equivalence. Hence
\Ho(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}\colon\D(\mathsf{A}\otimes\mathsf{A}^{\mathrm{op}})\to\D(\mathsf{B}\otimes\mathsf{B}^{\mathrm{op}})
restricts to an equivalence of categories on the compact objects, and any such cocontinuous functor between compactly
generated triangulated categories is an equivalence, by
[
24
, Lemma 3.3]
.
Finally the endomorphisms of corresponding objects are multiplicatively quasi-isomorphic.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathsf{A}
be a dg category.
Then the Yoneda embedding
\mathsf{A}\to\Perf(\mathsf{A})
induces a quasi-isomorphism

\HHc(\mathsf{A})\simeq\HHc(\Perf(\mathsf{A})).

Proof.

Immediate as
\mathsf{A}\to\Perf(\mathsf{A})
is a Morita equivalence.
∎

4.2.
Model structures of the second kind and Koszul duality

In this section we give the other model structures that will feature in the remainder of this paper.
These model structures will all give rise to “derived categories of the second kind”, the collective name given to derived categories which arise from localizing at some collection of weak equivalences that are finer than quasi-isomorphism.

There are many such derived categories; we will recall the coderived category of a pseudocompact algebra, following
[
21
]

We first define, following

Let now

The categories

Theorem 4.3

(see

For any pseudocompact dg algebra

Theorem 4.4

(see

Let

Where it is necessary to distinguish between the usual model structure of the first kind on dg

The

Recall that the cobar construction
\Omega C

is the tensor graded algebra

with differential defined using the differential and multiplication on

The model structures above are then related by the following statement, referred to as Koszul duality for the cobar construction.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
C
be a pseudocompact algebra. There is a Quillen equivalence

G\colon(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}C)^{\mathsf{op}}\rightleftarrows(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\Omega C)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}\penalty\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\mskip 2.0muF.

We will sometimes write
G_{C}(N)
for

when we want to make the dependence on
C
clear.

The functor
G
sends a pseudocompact dg
C
-module
N
to the dg
\Omega C
-module

GN:=(N^{*}\otimes\Omega C)^{[\xi]},

where
\xi
is the canonical Maurer–Cartan element in
C\otimes\Omega C
. The twist makes sense as we regard
N^{*}\otimes\Omega C
as a
(C\otimes\Omega C)^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\Omega C
-module,
where the left action of
C
comes from the right action of
C
on

and the
\Omega C^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\Omega C
-action arises from the left and right multiplication on
\Omega C

Hence the twisted module
(N^{*}\otimes\Omega C)^{[\xi]}
is a left dg
(C\otimes\Omega C)^{\xi}

\Omega C
-module, and

is obtained by forgetting the left
(C\otimes\Omega C)^{\xi}
-action.
More explicitly,

can be written as a complex as follows:

N^{*}\leftarrow N^{*}\otimes\widebar{C}\leftarrow N^{*}\otimes\widebar{C}^{2}\leftarrow\dots.

This is, in fact, a double complex where the vertical differential (not indicated explicitly) is induced by the internal differentials in
C
and
N
whereas the horizontal differential is induced by the multiplication in
C
and its action on
N^{*}
.
Explicitly, the horizontal differential is given by

d(c_{0}\otimes\ldots\otimes c_{k}\otimes n)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}c_{0}\otimes\ldots c_{i}c_{i+1}\ldots c_{k}\otimes n+(-1)^{k+1}c_{0}\otimes\ldots\otimes c_{k}n

where the last term in the above sum corresponds to the twisting by
\xi
.
Similarly, the functor
F
sends a dg
\Omega C
-module
M
to the pseudocompact dg
C
-module

FM:=(M^{*}\otimes C)^{[\xi]}

where we use the left
\Omega C
-module structure on

and the left
C
-module structure on

to define the twist.

Remark 4.6
.

A version of Theorem

[

with Positselski’s result we see that if

then the homotopy category

is precisely the coderived category of

defined by localizing at coacyclic dg

-modules.

In the case that

is augmented local (equivalently, that

is conilpotent), the cobar construction

is cofibrant and one recovers the more classical statement with

on the right hand side.

More on the relationship between different (co)derived categories can be found in
[

.

For the sake of convenience, we will now sketch the proof of
4.5
. We will later note that the arguments also work for (pseudocompact) modules over a curved pseudocompact algebras
C
.
First note that the functors
F
and
G
are adjoint in the sense that there are natural isomorphisms

\Hom_{\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\Omega C}(GN,M)\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}C}(FM,N),

for
N
and
M
as above, as both sides are identified with the dg vector spaces
(N\otimes M)^{[\xi]}
.
Composing the functors gives the pseudocompact dg
C
-module

FGN=(N\otimes\Omega C^{*}\otimes C)^{[\xi\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi]},

which as a (double) complex, can be written as follows:

N\otimes C\leftarrow N\otimes\widebar{C}^{*}\otimes C\leftarrow N\otimes(\widebar{C}^{*})^{2}\otimes C\leftarrow\dots.

The horizontal differential is described by a similar formula as above.
Note, however, that due to the tensor factor
C
, the horizontal differential is acyclic except at the first term; in fact it gives the standard cobar resolution of
N
, the map from the above complex to
N
being induced by the action map
N\otimes C\to N
.
Of course, one has to be careful calling this a resolution since
C
is a pseudocompact algebra and
N
is a pseudocompact
C
-module.
This can be made sense of as follows.
Consider the homotopy cofiber of the map
FGN\to N
; this is the double complex that can be represented as follows:

N\leftarrow N\otimes C\leftarrow N\otimes\widebar{C}^{*}\otimes C\leftarrow N\otimes(\widebar{C}^{*})^{2}\otimes C\leftarrow\dots.

Its horizontal differential is acyclic.
By taking canonical truncations this complex can be represented
as a homotopy inverse limit of acyclic complexes of finite horizontal length, which are, therefore, contra-acyclic as pseudocompact
C
-modules.
Thus, we obtain the following result, from which
4.5
follows.

Proposition 4.7
.

The adjunction unit
FGN\to N
is a cofibrant resolution of the pseudocompact dg
C
-module
N
.

Analogous statements can be obtained if we instead start with a dg algebra
A
; however, depending on whether we consider model structures of the first or second kind over
\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A
, we will have two different equivalences.
Let us denote by
\widehat{\mathsf{B}}A
the bar construction of
A
and by
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
the extended bar construction of
A
, both viewed as pseudocompact dg algebras.

As graded algebras these are respectively defined as
\widehat{T}\widebar{A}^{*}[-1]
and
\widecheck{T}\widebar{A}^{*}[-1]
, where
\widehat{T}
denotes the free
local
pseudocompact functor while
\check{T}
denotes the free pseudocompact functor on a graded pseudocompact vector space. (When dualizing, this corresponds to the free conilpotent coalgebra and the free coalgebra, respectively. Note that we are using
\widehat{\mathsf{B}}A
rather than
\mathsf{B}A
to distinguish this construction from the usual bar construction on
A
, which would be a coalgebra dual to our
\widehat{\mathsf{B}}A
.)
As before, bar differentials are defined using the differential and multiplication on
A
; see
[
13
, Definition 2.5]
in the extended bar case.
We then have the following Koszul duality statements for the bar constructions, see
[
13
, Theorem 4.7]
:

Theorem 4.8
.

There are Quillen equivalences

(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\widehat{\mathsf{B}}A)^{\mathsf{op}}\rightleftarrows(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{I}}

and

(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A)^{\mathsf{op}}\rightleftarrows(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}.

We collect some useful facts about the model structure of the second kind:

Proposition 4.9
.

(1)

Twisted modules are cofibrant of the second kind if they are a union of finitely generated twisted modules. All cofibrant modules are retracts of such.

(2)

Any map
f:M\to N
in
\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A
with coacyclic or contra-acyclic cone is also a weak equivalence in the sense of Theorem
4.3
.

Proof.

(1)

Any
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
-module is fibrant, and may be written as a filtered limit of finite-dimensional modules. Under Koszul duality the finite-dimensional
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
-modules correspond to finitely generated twisted
A
-modules. An arbitrary cofibrant object is a retract of its cofibrant replacement (which is obtained as a union of finitely generated twisted modules).

(2)

Let
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
be an exact triple and
T
a finitely generated twisted
A
-module. Then
0\to\Hom_{A}(T,L)\to\Hom_{A}(T,M)\to\Hom_{A}(T,N)\to 0
is exact. Therefore the dg space of homomorphisms from
T
into the totalization of
L\to M\to N
is acyclic. Thus the weak equivalences are closed under totalization of exact triples. They are automatically closed under direct products, and as any finitely generated twisted module is compact they are also closed under direct sums.∎

We now define perfect modules of the second kind.

Definition 4.10
.

A dg
A
-module is
perfect of the second kind
if it is homotopy equivalent to a direct summand of a two-sided twisted module in
\Tw(A)
.
We denote by
\PerfII(A)
the full dg subcategory of
\CofII(A)
consisting of perfect modules of the second kind.

We note that any homomorphism
f:A\to B
induces a dg functor
f_{!}:\PerfII(A)\to\PerfII(B)
and there is a quasi-fully faithful Yoneda embedding
h:A\to\PerfII(A)
.

The following statement for
\PerfII
is analogous to the corresponding statement for
\Perf
.

Lemma 4.11
.

For any dg category
\mathsf{A}
, the Yoneda embedding induces a quasi-equivalence

\PerfII(\mathsf{A})\to\PerfII(\PerfII(\mathsf{A}))
.

Proof.

The natural map
i\colon\Tw(\mathsf{A})\to\PerfII(\mathsf{A})
is a Morita fibrant replacement by
[
25
]
.
It suffices to show that
i^{\prime}:\Tw^{2}(\mathsf{A})\to(\PerfII)^{2}(\mathsf{A})
is also a Morita fibrant replacement:
the map
\PerfII(\mathsf{A})\to(\PerfII)^{2}(\mathsf{A})
is then a Morita fibrant replacement of the the functor
\Tw(\mathsf{A})\to\Tw^{2}(\mathsf{A})
induced by the Yoneda embedding, which is a quasi-equivalence by the Definition of
\Tw
.
So
i^{\prime}
is a Morita equivalence between Morita fibrant categories, and hence a quasi-equivalence, as the Morita model structure is a (left) Bousfield localisation of the usual Dwyer-Kan model structure.

But the map
i^{\prime}

is the composition

\Tw^{2}(\mathsf{A})\to\PerfII(\Tw(\mathsf{A}))\to(\PerfII)^{2}(\mathsf{A})
and
each of the factors is a Morita equivalences, obtained by applying the map
i
to
\Tw(\mathsf{A})
and by applying
\PerfII
to
i
respectively. Hence
i^{\prime}
is itself a Morita equivalence.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
A
be a dg algebra.
Then
\Ho\PerfII(A)
is the category of compact objects in
\DII(A)
, and
\DII(A)
is compactly generated.

Proof.

By definition,
\Ho\PerfII(A)
consists of dg
A
-modules that are direct summands of finitely generated twisted
A
-modules, and by Proposition
4.9
it is clear that these are compact as finite-dimensional twisted modules are.

To prove the that
\PerfII(A)
generates we use that
\DII(A)
is by Koszul duality (Theorem
4.8
) anti-equivalent as a triangulated category to the coderived category of pseudocompact dg modules over a pseudocompact dg algebra, which is cocompactly cogenerated by finite dimensional dg modules by
[
21
, Section 5.5]
(which correspond to finitely generated twisted
A
-modules).

Finally, to show that
\PerfII(A)
contains all compact objects we use
[
18
, Lemma 2.2]
in the case
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{R}=\DII(A)
.
∎

4.3.
Hochschild cohomology of the second kind

We define Morita equivalence of the second kind.
We will then define Hochschild cohomology of the second kind for dg algebras, and prove that it is invariant under Morita equivalence of the second kind.

Definition 4.13
.

Let
A
and
B
be dg algebras.
A morphism
F\colon A\to B
is a
Morita equivalence of the second kind
if it induces an equivalence of triangulated categories
\DII(A)\to\DII(B)
.

Example 4.14
.

For any dg algebra
A
the counit map
\Omega\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A\to A
is a Morita equivalence of the second kind. This followis directly by
[
7
, Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.6]
.

Morita equivalence can equivalently be characterized as follows.

Proposition 4.15
.

A morphism
F\colon A\to B
is a Morita equivalence of the second kind if and only if it induces a quasi-equivalence
\CofII(A)\to\CofII(B)
,
\PerfII(A)\to\PerfII(B)
or
\Tw(A)\to\Tw(B)
.

Proof.

It follows from the model category structure that
F
is a Morita equivalence of the second kind if and only if the induced left Quillen functior
F_{!}
induces a quasi-equivalence
\CofII(A)\to\CofII(B)
.
But this is a quasi-equivalence if and only if it is a quasi-equivalence when restricted to compact objects by Theorem
4.12
, and
\PerfII(A)
is the category of compact objects in
\CofII(A)
by
4.12
again.
Finally, as
\PerfII(A)
is the idempotent completion of
\Tw(A)
,
\PerfII(A)\simeq\PerfII(B)
follows from the quasi-equivalence
\Tw(A)\to\Tw(B)
.
∎

If
A
is a dg algebra, then by Lemma
4.11
the Yoneda embedding
A\to\PerfII(A)
is a Morita equivalence of the second kind.

Lemma 4.16
.

For dg algebras
A,B
there is a quasi-equivalence

\PerfII(A\otimes B)\simeq\PerfII(\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(B))

Proof.

Consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(A\otimes B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(A)^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(\PerfII(A\otimes B))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(h^{A}\otimes h^{B})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{A\otimes B}_{!}}

Here
h^{A\otimes B}_{!}
is a quasi-equivalence by Proposition
4.15
as the Yoneda embedding is a Morita equivalence of the second kind by Lemma
4.11
.
The functor
i:\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(B)\to\PerfII(A\otimes B)
is quasi-fully faithful and thus so is
i_{!}
.
It follows that
(h^{A}\otimes h^{B})_{!}
is quasi-fully faithful and
thus, is a quasi-equivalence. The claimed statement is therefore proved.
∎

We will now consider the category of
A
-bimodules with the model structure from Theorem
4.4
,
A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A:=\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes A)
.

Lemma 4.17
.

The Hom functor
\Hom_{A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(-,-):(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}\to\mathsf{DGVect}
is a Quillen bifunctor if we put the projective model structure on
\mathsf{DGVect}
.

Proof.

By adjointness it suffices to show that

\otimes_{k}:(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathsf{DGVect}\to(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}

is a Quillen bifunctor.
So let
g:P\to Q
be a cofibration in
\mathsf{DGVect}
and
f:L\to M
a cofibration in
(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}
and consider

f\boxempty g:M\otimes P\amalg_{L\otimes P}L\otimes Q\to M\otimes Q.

To check that
f\boxempty g
is a cofibration if
f
and
g
are, it suffices to check the case that
g
is a generating cofibration.

Then we can write
g:k[n]\to\operatorname{cone}(\textsf{id}_{k[n]})

and we obtain
f\boxempty g:M\oplus L[1]\to M\oplus M[1]
which is a cofibration if
f
is.

If
g
is a generating acyclic cofibration it is of the form

0\to\operatorname{cone}(\textsf{id}_{k[n]})
.
Then
f\boxempty g:L\otimes Q\to M\otimes Q
has cokernel
\operatorname{cone}(f)\otimes\operatorname{cone}(\textsf{id}_{k[n]})
which is a totalization of a short exact sequence and thus coacyclic, and thus
f\boxempty g
is a weak equivalence by Proposition
4.9
.

Finally, it is clear from the definition in Theorem
4.4
that if
f
is a weak equivalence so is
f\otimes\textsf{id}_{B}
for
B
bounded finitely generated in
\mathsf{DGVect}
. Thus if
L\to M
is a weak equivalence then
L\otimes P\to M\otimes P
and its pushout along
L\otimes P\to L\otimes Q
are weak equivalences, as is
L\otimes Q\to M\otimes Q
. By the 2-out-of-3 property, this gives a weak equivalence
f\boxempty g
.
∎

We now write
\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A}(M,N)
for the derived hom space between
M
and
N
in
(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}
.

Definition 4.18
.

Let
A
be a dg algebra and
M
a dg bimodule.
The
Hochschild cohomology complex of the second kind
of
A
with coefficients in
M
is

\HHIIc(A,M)=\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}(A,M).

We write
\HHIIc(A)
for
\HHIIc(A,A)
and
\HHII(A)
for
H(\HHIIc(A,A))
.

Remark 4.19
.

We are concerned with Hochschild cohomology in this paper, but could equally define the
Hochschild homology complex of the second kind
to be
A\otimes^{L,\text{II}}_{A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}}A
where the tensor product is derived in the model category
(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}
.

This makes sense as the tensor product over
A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}
is a left Quillen bifunctor into
\mathsf{DGVect}
with the projective model structure.
This can be shown using that generating cofibrations in
(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}
are the images of injection of finite-dimensional comodules under Koszul duality.
The definition of weak equivalences ensures that tensoring with the image of a finite-dimensional comodule preserves weak equivalences.

Theorem 4.20
.

A Morita equivalence of the second kind
F\colon A\to B
between dg algebras induces an quasi-isomorphism
\HHIIc(A)\simeq\HHIIc(B)
of dg algebras.

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of
4.1
.
As before, it suffices to show that

(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}\colon\CofII(A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}})\to\CofII(B\otimes B^{\mathrm{op}})

is a quasi-equivalence. The restriction of
(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}
to
\PerfII(A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}})
fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(A)^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(B\otimes B^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\PerfII(\PerfII(B)\otimes\PerfII(B)^{\mathrm{op}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(h\otimes h^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(h\otimes h^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{!}\otimes F_{!}^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}}

where the rows are quasi-equivalences by Lemma
4.16
,
and
(F_{!}\otimes F_{!}^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}
is a quasi-equivalence as
F
is a Morita equivalence of the second kind.
Hence by
4.12
, the functor
\Ho(F\otimes F^{\mathrm{op}})_{!}\colon\DII(A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}})\to\DII(B\otimes B^{\mathrm{op}})
restricts to an equivalence of categories on the compact objects, so is an equivalence.
∎

The aim of this section is to generalize the Koszul duality statements,

Throughout this section,
A

5.1.
Koszul duality for the cobar construction

We now obtain an analogue of

\widehat{\mathsf{B}}\Omega C^{\mathrm{op}}\to C^{\mathrm{op}}

Define

The functor

where as before,

It is the bimodule cobar-construction of the

Remark 5.9
.

There is another definition of Hochschild cohomology of the second kind, cf.
[

]
which is
not

and the ordinary bar-construction

In other words,

We may compute

6.
Curved and non-augmented cases

We note that the results in the previous two sections can be generalized to the curved and non-augmented settings, i.e. to curved, non-augmented algebras which are Koszul dual to non-local, curved pseudocompact algebras.

We restrict ourselves to the case of algebras and do not consider curved categories.

We now gather the results, and indicate where there is a difference in the proofs.

Definition 6.1
.

A
twisted module
over a curved algebra
(A,d,w)
is just a curved
A
-module whose underlying graded module has the form
V\otimes A
for a graded
k
-module
V
.
Explicitily a twisted module is of the form
(V\otimes A,1\otimes d+q)
where
q\in(\End V\otimes A)^{1}
satisfies
dq+q^{2}=1\otimes w\in\End V\otimes A
.

With this definition we can define finitely-generated twisted modules,

\CofII(A)
and
\PerfII(A)
for a curved algebra. Note that modules over
A
are somewhat more subtle if
A
is curved since
A
itslef is no longer a (left or right) twisted module over itself. It is, however, always a bimodule over itself.

Koszul duality extends to the curved setting following
[
21
,
13
,
7
]
.
As a first step one may extend the bar and cobar construction to the non-augmented case by choosing a section of the unit to define a decomposition
A\cong\bar{A}\oplus k
of an algebra as a
k
-module. This will introduce curvature.
The Koszul dual of a non-augmented dg algebra is thus a curved pseudocompact algebra. Similarly the Koszul dual of a non-local pseudocompact algebra is a curved algebra.

Next, while the bar construction of a curved algebra is not a good notion in general, we always have the extended bar construction
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
for a curved algebra
A
, right adjoint to the cobar construction.

With this we still have the model structure on
(\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}A)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}
by
[
13
, Theorem 4.6]
and the Quillen adjunction to
\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}}
by
[
13
, Theorem 4.7]
.

There is also a Morita equivalence of the second kind
\Omega\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A\simeq A
. This follows directly by
[
7
, Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.6]
.

Theorem
4.12
still holds in the curved setting: compact generation by twisted modules is again inherited via Koszul duality from the fact that comodules are compactly generated by finite-dimensional comodules. This also holds in the curved, nonconilpotent setting, see
[
21
, Section 5.5]
.

Since a curved algebra is always a bimodule over itself, the following definition makes sense:

Definition 6.2
.

Let
A
be a curved algebra and
M
be an
A

).
The

We write
\HHIIc(A)

and
\HHII(A)

The next goal is to transfer the remaining content of Section
4

However, other results strongly rely on the fully faithful Yoneda embedding

If

is curved, this is no longer available since

is not a (right) module over itself and so it has no Yoneda embedding. To get around this technical point, we need the following result, which is of some independent interest.

Lemma 6.3
.

Given a curved algebra

, there is a dg algebra

such that
\PerfII(A)
and

Proof.

Let

be the curvature element. Let

and consider the

is an MC element in the curved algebra

The curved algebra

as a curved algebra, it follows that
\PerfII(A)
and
\PerfII(A^{\prime})
are quasi-equivalent as desired (in fact, the constructed quasi-equivalence is even an equivalence as ordinary categories).
∎

Remark 6.4
.

It is easy to see that the dg algebra
A^{\prime}
constructed above, is acyclic (and so, the twisted module
M
is homotopically trivial). Indeed, the identity element
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
is the coboundary of the element
\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
in
A^{\prime}
. Note that the ordinary derived category of
A^{\prime}
is, of course, trivial.

We now have the curved analogue of Lemma
4.16
.

Lemma 6.5
.

For curved algebras
A,B
there is a quasi-equivalence

\PerfII(A\otimes B)\cong\PerfII(\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(B))

Proof.

We have a quasi-equivalence
\PerfII(A\otimes B)\simeq\Perf(A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime})
(where
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are dg algebras II-Morita equivalent to
A
and
B
as constructed in Lemma
6.3
) since
A^{\prime}\otimes B^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the tensor product of
A\otimes B
and a
4\times 4
matrix algebra. Similarly
\PerfII(\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(B))
is Morita equivalent of the second kind to
\PerfII(A\otimes B)
which reduces the question to the uncurved case that has already been proved in Lemma
4.16
.
∎

Using Lemma
6.5
in place of Lemma
4.16
, the curved analogue of Theorem
4.20
can now be shown with the same proof:

Theorem 6.6
.

A Morita equivalence of the second kind
F\colon A\to B
between curved algebras induces a quasi-isomorphism
\HHIIc(A)\simeq\HHIIc(B)
of dg algebras.

With the same adjustment of using Lemma
6.5
we also have the curved analogues of
Theorem
5.6
and Corollary
5.7
by the same proof. We will just define the necessary functors and state the theorem:

As there is an adjunction
\Omega\dashv\widecheck{B}
also for curved pseudocompact algebras
[
7
]

we obtain a canonical MC element
\xi_{A}\in\MC(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}})
corresponding to the counit
\Omega\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}}\to A^{\mathrm{op}}
.
We may define

\xi:=\xi_{A}\otimes 1+1\otimes\xi_{E}\in A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes E\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}E,

then
\xi\in\MC(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes E\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}E)
.
The functor
\widecheck{F}
sends a dg
A
-
E
-bimodule
M
to

\widecheck{F}M\coloneqq(M^{*}\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}E)^{[\xi]}

and the functor
\widecheck{G}
sends a pseudocompact dg
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A
-
\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}E
-bimodule
N
to

\widecheck{G}N\coloneqq(N^{*}\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes E)^{[\xi]}.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
A,E
be curved algebras.
The functor
\widecheck{G}
is left adjoint to
\widecheck{F}
respectively, and forms a Quillen equivalence

\widecheck{G}\colon(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}E)^{\mathsf{op}}\rightleftarrows(A\textrm{-}\mathsf{DGMod}\textrm{-}E)^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathrm{c}}\penalty\mskip 6.0mu plus 1.0mu\mathpunct{}\nonscript\mkern-3.0mu{:}\mskip 2.0mu\widecheck{F}.

Furthermore for any dg algebras
A
and
E
, there is an equivalence of triangulated categories

\DII(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}E)\cong\DII(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}(A^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes E)).

Corollary 6.8
.

For any curved algebra
A
, there is a quasi-isomorphism of algebras

\HHIIc(A)\simeq\HHc(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A)

and

\HHII(A)
is computed by the complex
(\widecheck{\mathsf{B}}A\otimes A)^{\xi}
.

7.
Examples of Hochschild cohomology of the second kind

7.1.
Preliminaries

In this final section we compare Hochschild cohomology of the first and second kind in some situations.
Let
A
and
E
be two curved algebras and
M,N
be two
A\otimes E
-modules.
Recall that
\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes E}(M,N)
is the derived complex of homomorphisms from
M
to
N
in the compactly generated model category of the second kind of
A\otimes E
-modules.
Thus,
\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes E}(M,N)
can be represented as
\Hom_{A\otimes E}(\tilde{M},{N})
where
\tilde{M}
is a cofibrant replacement of
M
as an
A\otimes E
-module.

Recall that
\PerfII(?)
stands for the dg category of perfect cofibrant
?
-modules of the second kind (which can be represented as retracts of finitely generated twisted modules).

The modules
M
and
N
can be viewed as
\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)
-modules: the corresponding functor to dg vector spaces
\PerfII(A)\to\mathsf{DGVect}
is given by
L\mapsto\Hom_{A\otimes E}(M,L)
where
L
is a given
A\otimes E
-module. We can, therefore, form
\RHom_{\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)}(M,N)
, the derived functor of homomorphisms in the model category (of the first kind) of
\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)
-modules.

We would like to compare
\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes E}(M,N)
and
\RHom_{\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)}(M,N)
.
Note that it is well-known that
\RHom_{A\otimes E}(M,N)
and
\RHom_{\Perf(A)\otimes\Perf(E)}(M,N)
are naturally quasi-isomorphic, the reason being that the Yoneda embedding
A\otimes E\to\Perf(A)\otimes\Perf(E)
is a Morita equivalence of categories.
This argument, however, breaks down in our situation (e.g. because we wish to compare
\RHom
of the first kind with
\RHom
of the second kind).
In fact, there is not even a natural map between
\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes E}(M,N)
and
\RHom_{\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)}(M,N)
.

Consider
the natural functor

i\colon\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)\to\PerfII(A\otimes E)

sending a pair
M,N
of perfect modules of the second kind to
M\otimes N
.

Then we have the following result, similar to
[
20
, Theorem 3.5 C]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose that
i
above is a Morita equivalence (of the first kind).
Then for
A\otimes E
-modules
M
and
N
we have that
\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes E}(M,N)
and
\RHom_{\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)}(M,N)
are naturally quasi-isomorphic.

Proof.

We first note that there is an equivalence
\DII(A)\cong\D(\PerfII(A))
induced by the natural map
\DII(\PerfII(A))\to\D(\PerfII(A))
. This follows by comparing compact objects, which are
\PerfII(A)
on both sides (using
\Perf(\PerfII(A))\cong\PerfII(A)
).

With this we can compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{A\otimes E}(M,N)

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{\DII(A\otimes E)}(M,N)

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{D(\PerfII)(A\otimes E)}(M,N)

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{D(\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E))}(M,N)

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(E)}(M,N)

where we used the assumption in the penultimate step.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

There is no reason to believe that
i
is always a quasi-equivalence.
In fact, the dg category
\PerfII(A\otimes E)
should be viewed as a kind of a
completed
tensor product of the categories
\PerfII(A)
and
\PerfII(E)
.
There are, however, important situations when this completion is extraneous.

Let us take
E:=A^{\mathrm{op}}
and
M:=N:=A
.
The above lemma specializes to the following statement.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
A
be a dg algebra such that
\PerfII(A)\otimes\PerfII(A^{\mathrm{op}})
is Morita equivalent to
\PerfII(A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}})
and
M
be an
A
-bimodule.
Then
\HHIIc(A,M)
is naturally quasi-isomorphic to
\HHc(\PerfII(A),M)
.

In other words, under the assumptions of
7.1
, Hochschild cohomology of the second kind of dg algebras reduce to Hochschild cohomology of the first kind of a suitable dg category.
A version of this question was considered in
[
20
]
where some partial results were obtained (for a different notion of Hochschild cohomology of the second kind, see Remark
5.9
).

7.2.
Complex algebraic manifolds

Let
X
be a compact complex projective manifold and
A(X):=(\mathcal{A}^{0,*}(X),\bar{\partial})
its Dolbeault-algebra.
It is well-known
[
3
,
10
]
that the bounded derived category
\DCoh^{b}(X)
of coherent sheaves on
X
is equivalent to the derived category of sheaves on
X
with bounded coherent cohomology and, since
X
is smooth, the latter coincides with the derived category of perfect complexes of sheaves on
X
.
We will consider its dg model
\Perf(X)
formed by taking Dolbeault resolutions of coherent sheaves. According to
[
5
, Corollary 3.1.8]
, the latter is equivalent to the dg category of dg modules over some dg algebra (the endomorphism algebra of a generator of
\Perf(X)
).

The following result holds.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. The categories
\Perf(X\times X)
and
\Perf(X)\otimes\Perf(X)
are Morita equivalent.

Proof.

The statement is well-known and follows from
a very general result
[
2
, Theorem 1.2(1)]

valid for perfect derived stacks, not merely for complex projective manifolds. The argument goes back to
[
5
]
.
We sketch a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a complex of sheaves representing a generator of
\Perf(X)
and
B=R\End(\mathcal{F})
be its endomorphism dg algebra.
Then the external tensor product
\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{F}
is a generator of
\Perf(X\times X)
and
B\otimes B\simeq R\End(\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{F})
. So,
\Perf(X)
is quasi-equivalent to
\Perf(B)
and
\Perf(X\times X)
is quasi-equivalent to
B\otimes B
.
Thus we have reduced the question to that of a perfect derived category of a dg algebra. Since
\Perf(B\otimes B)
is Morita equivalent to
\Perf(B)\otimes\Perf(B)
(for any dg algebra
B
), the desired claim follows.
∎

We recall the following result of Block
[
3
]
relating
A(X)
and
\Perf(X)
:

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
X
be an analytic manifold.

\PerfII(A(X))
is a dg enhancement of
\Perf(X)
.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. The Dolbeault algebra
A(X)
satisfies the conditions of
7.3
. Thus
we have a natural isomorphism

\HHII(A(X))\cong\HH(\Perf(X)).

Proof.

As
A
is graded commutative
A^{op}\cong A
and
\Perf(A)\simeq(\PerfII(A))^{op}
.

From Proposition
7.4

together with Theorem
7.5
we obtain
that

\PerfII(A(X))\otimes\PerfII(A(X))
is Morita equivalent
\PerfII(A(X\times X))
. It remains to show that
\PerfII(A(X\times X))
is Morita equivalent to
\PerfII(A(X)\otimes A(X))
.
It follows from
[
28
, Theorem 51.6]
that there is an isomorphism of dg algebras between
A(X\times X)
and the completed tensor product
A(X)\hat{\otimes}A(X)
and thus we have Morita equivalences

\PerfII(A(X))\otimes\PerfII(A(X))\simeq\PerfII(A(X\times X))\simeq\PerfII(A(X)\hat{\otimes}\PerfII(A(X)))

The composition naturally factors as

\PerfII(A(X)\otimes\PerfII(A(X)))\to\PerfII(A(X)\otimes A(X)))\to\PerfII(A(X)\hat{\otimes}A(X))

which shows that the second map induces a quasi-essentially surjective map on derived categories.
As the induced map is also quasi-fully faithful by the Künneth theorem it must be a quasi-equivalence and putting all our equivalences together we obtain the desired Morita equvielence

\PerfII(A(X)\otimes A(X))\simeq\PerfII(A(X))\otimes\PerfII(A(X))
.

∎

7.3.
Topological spaces

In the topological setting we consider
\infty
-local systems on a topological space
X
. To be precise we denote by
\LC(X)
the dg category of fibrant cofibrant cohomologically locally constant (clc) sheaves of complexes over
k
whose cohomology sheaves have finite-dimensional fibers.
We recall the following:

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
X
be a connected locally contractible topological space and
C^{*}(X)
its normalized singular cochain algebra with coefficients in
k
.
Then
\PerfII(C^{*}(X))\cong\LC(X)
.

Let
k=\mathbb{R}
,
X
a connected manifold and
\mathcal{A}^{*}(X)
its de Rham algebra. Then
\PerfII(\mathcal{A}^{*}(X))\cong\LC(X)
.

Proof.

For the first result it follows from
[
10
, Theorem 8.4]
that
\LC(X)
is given by finitely generated twisted modules over
C^{*}(X)
since
finitely generated twisted modules correspond to clc sheaves whose fibers are bounded and finite dimensional in each degree. As
\LC(X)
is idempotent complete it follows that it is quasi-equivalent to
\PerfII(C^{*}(X))
.

The second result is
[
10
, Theorem 8.1]
.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

Note that if we go may go beyond
\infty
-local systems with perfect fibers if we consider
C^{*}(X)
as a pseudocompact algebra.
We consider
\Dctr(C^{*}(X))
as the homotopy category of pseudocompact modules over
C^{*}(X)
whose underlying graded is of the form
C^{*}(X,V)
for some chain complex
V
.
Then there is an equivalence
\Dctr(C^{*}(X))\cong\LC^{\infty}(X)
, where the right hand side is the category of potentially infinite-dimensional local systems
[
10
, Theorem 8.4]
.

But
\Dctr(C^{*}(X))
is different from
\DII(C^{*}(X))
, the natural map
\DII(C^{*}(X))\to\Dctr(C^{*}(X))
has in its image all
\infty
-local systems that arise as colimits of
\infty
-local systems with perfect fibers.
For
X=S^{1}
this is clearly not essentially surjective as the regular representation of
\mathbb{Z}
is not a colimit of finite-dimensional ones.

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
X
be a CW complex with finitely many cells in each degree. Then
\LC(X\times X)
and
\LC(X)\otimes\LC(X)
are Morita equivalent.

Proof.

We let
\{G_{i}\}
be a collection of generators for
\LC(X)
, e.g. the collection of all (classical) local systems on
X
.
We first show that
G_{i}\boxtimes G_{j}
generate
\LC(X\times X)
.
To do this we follow again the argument by Bondal-Van den Bergh.

Let
N
be right orthogonal to
\{G_{i}\boxtimes G_{j}\}
, i.e.
\RHom_{X\times X}(G_{i}\boxtimes G_{j},N)\simeq 0
for all
i,j
. We need to show that
N
is trivial.

By adjunction we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\RHom_{X\times X}(G_{i}\boxtimes G_{j},N)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\RHom_{X\times X}(\pi_{1}^{*}G_{i},\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{2}^{*}G_{j},N))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\RHom_{X}(G_{i},(\pi_{1})_{*}\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{2}^{*}G_{j},N)).

It follows that
(\pi_{1})_{*}\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{2}^{*}G_{j},N)\simeq 0
as the
G_{i}
generate
\LC(X)
and
(\pi_{1})_{*}\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{2}^{*}G_{j},N)\in\LC(X)
by our finiteness assumption.

Thus the fibers of
(\pi_{1})_{*}\mathcal{H}om(\pi_{2}^{*}G_{j},N)
must be trivial and we have

\Hom_{\{x_{1}\}\times X}(\pi_{2}^{*}G_{j}|_{\{x_{1}\}\times X},N|_{\{x_{1}\}\times X})\simeq 0

which gives
\Hom_{X}(G_{j},(\pi_{2})_{*}N|_{\{x_{1}\}\times X})\simeq 0
and as the
G_{j}
generate we have that
N|_{\{x_{1}\}\times X}
is trivial and it follows that
N
itself is trivial.

This shows that the comparison functor
\LC(X)\otimes\LC(X)\to\LC(X\times X)
given by
\boxtimes
is essentially surjective.

It remains to be shown the functor is quasi-fully faithful. It suffices to check on generators, i.e. on local systems.
We thus have to compare

\RHom(L,L^{\prime})\otimes\RHom(M,M^{\prime})
with
\RHom(L\boxtimes L,M\boxtimes M^{\prime})
for local systems
L,L^{\prime},M,M^{\prime}
on
X
.
But this is equivalent to showing

C^{*}(X,L^{\prime}\otimes L^{*})\otimes C^{*}(X,M^{\prime}\otimes M^{*})\simeq C^{*}(X\times X,(L^{\prime}\otimes L^{*})\boxtimes(M^{\prime}\otimes M^{*})).

Thus the result follows from the Künneth theorem with local coefficients
[
12
, Theorem 1.7]
. Here we use the finiteness assumption.
∎

Corollary 7.10
.

Let
X
be a connected locally contractible topological space that has the homotopy type of a CW complex with finitely many cells in each degree. Then
\HHII(C^{*}(X))\cong\HH(\LC(X)))
.

If
X
is moreover a manifold
\HHII(\mathcal{A}^{*}(X))\cong\HH(\LC(X)))
.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.7
we have
\PerfII(C^{*}(X))\simeq\LC(X)
, so the result follows from
Corollary
7.3
if we can verify that

\PerfII(C^{*}(X))\otimes\PerfII(C^{*}(X))
and
\PerfII(C^{*}(X)\otimes C^{*}(X)\mathrm{op})
are Morita equivalent. From Proposition
7.9
we have
\PerfII(C^{*}(X))\otimes\PerfII(C^{*}(X))
is Morita equivalent to
\PerfII(C^{*}(X\times X))

Thus it remains to show that there is a Morita equvialence between
\PerfII(C^{*}(X\times X))
and
\PerfII(C^{*}(X)\otimes C^{*}(X))
to complete the proof.

We know
D(\Omega(C_{*}(X\times X))\simeq\LC^{\infty}(X\times X)
. As
\Omega
is quasi-strong monoidal
[
15
]
we also have
D(\Omega(C_{*}X\otimes C_{*}(X)))\simeq D(\Omega(C_{*}X)\otimes\Omega(C_{*}(X)))\simeq\LC^{\infty}(X\times X)
.
Thus by Koszul duality we have an equivalence

\Dctr(C^{*}(X\times X))\simeq\Dctr(C^{*}(X)\otimes C^{*}(X))
, cf. Remark
7.8
.
It remains to identify the subcategories of finitely generated twisted modules.
Replacing singular chains by reduced singular chains we my assume
C^{0}(X)=k
.
The equivalence
\Dctr(C^{*}(X\times X))\simeq\Dctr(C^{*}(X)\otimes C^{*}(X))
is compatible with the maps
M\mapsto M\otimes_{A}k
for
A=C^{*}(X\times X)
respectively
C^{*}(X)\otimes C^{*}(X)
.

Thus it identifes the modules
M
such that
M\otimes_{A}k
is finite-dimensional. But these modules are exactly the finitely generated twisted modules.
One inclusion is clear and given
M\otimes_{A}k
finite-dimensional we can use
[
9
, Proposition 6.4]
to show
M
is equivalent to a twisted finitely generated module. ∎

7.4.
Matrix Factorizations

We now turn to a curved example. Let
R
be a commutative algebra over a field
k
of characteristic 0, and assume
R
is regular, and let
w\in R
. Interpreting
R
as a
\mathbb{Z}/2
graded algebra concentrated in even degrees we may consider
w
as curvature and define a curved ring
R_{w}=(R,0,w)
.

Then the dg category of matrix factorizations
\MF(R,w)
may be defined as the idempotent completion of the category of curved modules over
R_{w}
such that the underlying graded module is finitely generated and projective in each degree.
Its homotopy category is known to be equivalent to the derived category of singularities
\D^{b}_{coh}(Z)/\Perf(Z)
where
Z=w^{-1}(0)\subset\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is the zero locus with singular locus
\operatorname{crit}(w)

[
19
]
.

For a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded curved algebra we may define its
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded categories of twisted modules and perfect complexes of the second kind
\Perf^{\textrm{II}}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
by just changing the grading in our definitions.

With these definitions we obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 7.11
.

There is a quasi-equivalence
\MF(R,w)\simeq\Perf^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(R_{w})
of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded dg categories.

Proof.

The definitions of
\MF(R,w)
and
\Perf^{\mathrm{II}}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(R_{w})
agree except that matrix factorizations are built out of projective modules for
R^{\#}
, the underlying graded of
R
, rather than free modules.
But any curved
R_{w}
module whose underlying graded module
P
is finitely generated projective is a direct summand of a finitely generated twisted module, i.e. a curved module whose underlying graded is finitely generated free over
R^{\#}
.

To show this, pick a finitely generated projective
R^{\#}
-module
L
such that
P\oplus L
is free and consider
G(L)
the free curved
R
-module on
L
which has elements formal sums
\ell+d\ell
and differential
d(\ell+d\ell)=h\ell+d\ell
, cf.
[
21
, Section 3.6]
.
Then the underlying graded of
G(L)
is
L\oplus L[-1]
and
P\oplus P[-1]\oplus G(L)
is the desired
R_{w}
-module, proving the assertion.
∎

We define Hochschild cohomology of the second kind of
R_{w}
as in the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded case, noting that

\HHII(R_{w})=\mathrm{RHom}^{\mathrm{II}}_{R_{w}\otimes R_{w}^{\mathrm{op}}}(R_{w},R_{w})
is now a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded complex.

Proposition 7.12
.

In the situation as above let furthermore
w
satisfy
\operatorname{crit}(w)\subset w^{-1}(0)
. Then
\HHII(R_{w})\cong\HH(\MF(R,w))
.

Proof.

The Thom-Sebastiani theorem
[
23
, Theorem 4.1.3]
says

\MF(R,w)\otimes\MF(R,w)\cong\MF(R\otimes R,w\otimes 1+1\otimes w)
. Rewriting in terms of curved algebras this is exactly saying
\MF(R_{w})\otimes\MF(R_{w})=\MF(R_{w}\otimes R_{w})
.

Using Lemma
7.11
we are in the setting of Corollary
7.3
and immediately obtain

\HHII(R_{w})\cong\HH(\MF(R,w)).\qed

Remark 7.13
.

Note that
\HH(\MF(R,w))
has also been computed in terms of compactly supported Hochschild cohomology (cf. Remark
5.9
). For isolated singularities of
w^{-1}(0)
this is
[
8
,
29
]

and in general

[
20
, Section 4.8]
.
They show
\HH(\MF(R,w))=\HH_{\mathrm{PP}}(R_{w})
.
This is further used for example in
[
11
]
.

It is notable that in this important case our definitions agree with the older definition that is different in many cases.

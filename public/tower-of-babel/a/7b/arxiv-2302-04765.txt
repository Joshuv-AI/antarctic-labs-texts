Abstract
In this paper, we study the global dynamics of a general reaction-diffusion model based on acid-mediated invasion hypothesis, which is a candidate explanation for the Warburg effect. A key feature of this model is the density-limited tumor diffusion term for tumor cells, which might give rise to the degeneracy of the parabolic equation. Our theoretical results characterize the effects of acid resistance and mutual competition of healthy cells and tumor cells on tumor progression in the long term, i.e., whether the healthy cells and tumor cells coexist or the tumor cells prevail after tumor invasion. The approach relies on the construction of suitable Lyapunov functionals and upper/lower solutions. This paper continues and improves the work begun in [ 20 ] .



Keywords
: Reaction-diffusion systems; Lyapunov functional; Global stability

At the end, some remarks from the view of mathematics and strategies of our proofs are appropriate.

(i)

The system (
1.1
) can be viewed as a generalization of a classical mutual competition system
[
17
]
.
On the basis of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
,
we observe that the multiplication of competition coefficients
a_{1}a_{2}
is a critical quantity in determining the global dynamics. To be more specific, thanks to the case (i) in Theorem
1.1
and the cases (i) and (ii) in Theorem
1.2
,
the property that local stability implies global stability
is verified under the conditions that

a_{1}<1,\ a_{1}a_{2}<1,\ d_{1}\leq d^{h}_{1}=\left(\displaystyle\frac{1+\sqrt{1-a_{1}a_{2}}}{1-\sqrt{1-a_{1}}}\right)^{2}-a_{2}.

However, when

a_{1}<1,\ a_{1}a_{2}>1,

for any
d_{1}>0
,
there always exist the ranges where the homogeneous tumor state is locally stable but the global stability is unknown. Moreover, in Theorem
1.3
, the conditions on
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
automatically exclude the strong competition, i.e.,
a_{1}>1
,
a_{2}>1
.
Indeed, in the studies of the classical Lotka-Volterra system for two competing species, the multiplication of competition coefficients, still denoted by
a_{1}a_{2}
, also plays an important role in determining the global dynamics. It is known that when
a_{1}a_{2}<1
,
the property that local stability implies global stability
is completely verified, while when
a_{1}a_{2}>1
, the global dynamics becomes very complicated and is far from being understood. See
[
2
,
13
]
and the references therein for more details. Therefore, back to the system (
1.1
),
it is naturally expected that more complicated phenomena might happen in the unknown ranges.

(ii)

The model (
1.1
) is a combination of ODE and PDEs. A novel feature of this model is the density-limited tumor diffusion term in the second equation, which might give rise to the degeneracy of the parabolic equation. In
[
20
]
, where the global stability of the heterogenous state
(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})
is studied, to exclude this possibility of degeneracy, the rectangle method idea is employed to the system where spatially homogeneous upper and lower solutions are constructed. The conditions on the parameters in Theorem A are imposed to guarantee that the upper solution approaches the lower solution as time goes to infinity.

(iii)

The proofs of our theorems make crucial use of Lyapunov functionals. Inspired by
[
1
,
12
]
, we design proper Lyapunov functionals for each nontrivial steady state to derive
L^{2}
convergence of
w
, where the conditions on the parameters are required to warrants the existence of the desired Lyapunov functionals. Moreover, based on the equation satisfied by
w
,
the
L^{2}
convergence of
w
is improved to
L^{\infty}
convergence. Indeed, the
L^{2}
convergence of
u,\,v
are derived at the same time. However, since there is no diffusion term for
u
and the density-limited tumor diffusion term for

might cause the degeneracy of the parabolic equation, the norm of the convergence cannot be improved for
u,\,v
.

(iv)

For the heterogenous state
(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})
, to prove the
L^{\infty}
convergence of
u,\,v
, similar to
[
20
]
, we also employ the rectangle method idea. However, different from
[
20
]
, where the auxiliary system of ODEs is constructed for the whole system (
1.1
) to obtain the upper and lower solutions, we only construct the auxiliary system of ODEs for the first two equations in the system (
1.1
), where
w
is replaced by a suitable perturbation of
w^{*}
. Obviously, the
L^{\infty}
convergence of
w
to
w^{*}
guarantees that this simplification is practicable. Thanks to this simplification, no more requirements on the parameters are needed.

(v)

For the homogeneous tumor state and the healthy state, the auxiliary systems of ODEs are constructed similarly, but the arguments in deriving the convergence relations between lower and upper solutions are different, since in each case, the corresponding elements of the lower solutions are reduced to be zero and the arguments for the heterogenous state is not applicable anymore.

The following property is based on elementary analysis and useful in the proofs of Lemma

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that

At the end, we show how to improve the

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
(u,v,w)
is a global solution of
(\ref{model})
-
(\ref{ini})
and satisfies

||w-\mathfrak{w}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Recall from Lemma

that

is uniformly bounded, by the smoothing property of

\displaystyle c_{n}||w(\cdot,0)||_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+c_{n}\Big\{\sup\limits_{\tau>0}||v(\cdot,\tau)||^{(2n-1)/(2n)}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\Big\}\Big\{\sup\limits_{\tau>0}||v(\cdot,\tau)||^{1/(2n)}_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\Big\}<\infty.

3
The heterogeneous state

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

, which is about the global convergence of the heterogeneous state

(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})=(1-(a_{2}+d_{1})v_{h},v_{h},v_{h}),\ \textrm{where}\ v_{h}:=\displaystyle\frac{1-a_{1}}{1-a_{1}a_{2}+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}d_{1}},\ a_{1}\neq 0,

where
u^{*}>0,\,v^{*}>0,\,w^{*}>0.

Recall that the heterogeneous state
(u^{*},v^{*},w^{*})
exists and is linearly stable if and only if

a_{1}<1,\ \displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}>a_{2}+d_{1}-1.

According to the strategies explained at the end of the introduction, we present the proof in three steps:

•

in Section
3.1
, we demonstrate the

convergence of
w
to
w^{*}
with the help of a Lyapunov functional, the form of which is inspired by
[
1

12
]
.

•

in Section
3.2
, the auxiliary system of ODEs for the first two equations in the system (

) is constructed and some properties are prepared.

•

in Section

to

3.1
L^{\infty}
convergence of
w
in the heterogeneous state

To prove the
L^{\infty}
convergence of
w
, the key step is to select the proper Lyapunov functional in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
(u,v,w)
is the global solution of
(\ref{model})
-
(\ref{ini})
and the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
hold. Define

A_{h}(t)=\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)-u^{*}-u^{*}\ln\displaystyle\frac{u(x,t)}{u^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

B_{h}(t)=\int_{\Omega}v(x,t)-v^{*}-v^{*}\ln\displaystyle\frac{v(x,t)}{v^{*}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

C_{h}(t)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(w(x,t)-w^{*})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then there exist
\beta_{h}>0
,
\eta_{h}>0
and
\varepsilon_{h}>0
such that the functions
E_{h}(t)
and
F_{h}(t)
defined by

E_{h}(t)=A_{h}(t)+\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{h}}{r}B_{h}(t)+\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{h}}{c}C_{h}(t),\quad t>0

(3.27)

and

\displaystyle F_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u(x,t)-u^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(v(x,t)-v^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(w(x,t)-w^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w(x,t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad t>0,

(3.28)

satisfy

E_{h}(t)\geq 0,\quad t>0

as well as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{{\mathrm{d}t}}E_{h}(t)\leq-\varepsilon_{h}F_{h}(t).

(3.29)

On the basis of Lemma
3.1
is valid, we establish the
L^{\infty}
convergence of
w
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that assumptions of Theorem
1.1
hold, and
(u,v,w)
is the global solution of
(\ref{model})
-
(\ref{ini})
, then

||w-w^{*}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Integrating
(\ref{decay1})
from
0
to
+\infty
, by the fact that
E^{h}(\cdot)
is nonnegative, we have

\int_{0}^{+\infty}||u-u^{*}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+||v-v^{*}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+||w-w^{*}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+||\nabla w||^{2}_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}t\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}E^{h}(0)<+\infty.

Using standard
L^{p}
estimate and Sobolev embedding on
w
, we obtain that there exist two constants
\mathcal{C}_{1}>0
and
0<\alpha_{n}<1
such that

||w||_{C^{\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n}/2}(\bar{\Omega}\times(k,k+1])}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1},\quad\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore,

||w(\cdot,t)-w^{*}||^{2}_{L^{2}}

is uniformly continuous with respect to

. Thus, Lemma
2.3
ensures that

||w-w^{*}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad t\rightarrow\infty,

and the desired conclusion follows from Lemma
2.4
.
∎

It remains to prove Lemma
3.1
. Notice that to verify (
3.29
), it suffices to show that the matrix
\mathbb{P}_{h}
defined in (
3.34
) coming from the Lyapunov functional
E_{h}(t)
is positive definite. Thus to derive the optimal results, we first leave the coefficients
\beta_{h}
and
\eta_{h}
in the energy functional

E_{h}(t)
undetermined, and then manage to explore the equivalent conditions on the parameters in the system (
1.1
) which guarantee the existence of positive coefficients
\beta_{h}
and
\eta_{h}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{h}
is positive definite.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

First of all, we claim that

A_{h}(t),\,B_{h}(t)
are nonnegative. In fact, by setting function
\mathcal{I}(\mathfrak{u}):=\mathfrak{u}-u^{*}\ln\mathfrak{u}
for
\mathfrak{u}>0
, and using Taylor’s formula, for all
x\in\Omega
and
t>0
, there exists
\xi=\xi(x,t)\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(u(x,t))-\mathcal{I}(u^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u^{*})\cdot(u(x,t)-u^{*})+\frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}(\,\xi u(x,t)+(1-\xi)u^{*})\cdot(u(x,t)-u^{*})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{u^{*}}{2(\xi u(x,t)+(1-\xi)u^{*})^{2}}(u(x,t)-u^{*})^{2}\geq 0.

From the computation above, we obtain that

A_{h}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\mathcal{I}(u(x,t))-\mathcal{I}(u^{*})\right)\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

Similarly,
B_{h}(t)
is also nonnegative. Since
\beta_{h}
and
\eta_{h}
are positive,
E_{h}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\geq 0
.

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\frac{u-u^{*}}{u}u\left(1-u-a_{2}v-d_{1}w\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})\big[(u^{*}-u)+a_{2}(v^{*}-v)+d_{1}(w^{*}-w)\big]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x-a_{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})(v-v^{*})\mathrm{d}x-d_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})(w-w^{*})\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}B_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\frac{v-v^{*}}{v}\big[D\nabla\cdot((1-u)\nabla v)+rv\left(1-v-a_{1}u\right)-d_{2}w\big]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\frac{Dv^{*}}{r}\int_{\Omega}(1-u)\left|\displaystyle\frac{\nabla v}{v}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(v-v^{*})\big[a_{1}(u^{*}-u)+(v^{*}-v)+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}(w^{*}-w)\big]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})(v-v^{*})\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}(v-v^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\int_{\Omega}(v-v^{*})(w-w^{*})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\frac{Dv^{*}}{r}\int_{\Omega}(1-u)\left|\displaystyle\frac{\nabla v}{v}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}C_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\int_{\Omega}(w-w^{*})\big[\Delta w+c(v-w)\big]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(w-w^{*})\big[(v-v^{*})+(w^{*}-w)\big]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(v-v^{*})(w-w^{*})\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}(w-w^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

By differentiating
(\ref{eh})
and substituting the three equations above into it, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{h}(t)=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\beta_{h}\int_{\Omega}(v-v^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\eta_{h}\int_{\Omega}(w-w^{*})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

(3.32)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-(a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{h})\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})(v-v^{*})\mathrm{d}x-d_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u-u^{*})(w-w^{*})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h})\int_{\Omega}(v-v^{*})(w-w^{*})\mathrm{d}x-\displaystyle\frac{D\beta_{h}v^{*}}{r}\int_{\Omega}(1-u)\left|\displaystyle\frac{\nabla v}{v}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{h}}{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{P}_{h}\mathbf{X}\,\mathrm{d}x-\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{h}}{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

(3.33)

where
\mathbb{P}_{h}
and
\mathbf{X}
are defined by

\mathbb{P}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle 1&\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{h}}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{h}}{2}&\beta_{h}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}&\eta_{h}\end{pmatrix},

(3.34)

\mathbf{X}=\left(u-u^{*},v-v^{*},w-w^{*}\right)^{\mathbf{T}}.

In order to verify
(\ref{decay1})
, we need to show that there exist positive constants
\beta_{h}
,
\eta_{h}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{h}
is positive definite.
We claim that this property holds if and only if there exists a positive constant
\beta_{h}
satisfying the two following inequalities simultaneously:

\displaystyle\Phi_{h}(\beta_{h}):=-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}^{2}+2\Big[\,2\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)-(a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}d_{1})\,\Big]\beta_{h}-(a_{2}+d_{1})^{2}>0,

(3.35)

\displaystyle\Psi_{h}(\beta_{h}):=-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}^{2}+2(2-a_{1}a_{2})\beta_{h}-a_{2}^{2}>0.

(3.36)

Since a matrix is positive definite if and only if all its principal minors are positive, it remains to verify the positivity of every principal minor of
\mathbb{P}_{h}
. For simplicity, we denote
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{h})
.
First of all, we verify the first two principal minors:

\mathbf{M^{h}_{1}}:=1,

\mathbf{M^{h}_{2}}:=\begin{vmatrix}1&\alpha\\
\alpha&\beta_{h}\end{vmatrix}=\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}^{2}+2(2-a_{1}a_{2})\beta_{h}-a_{2}^{2}\right)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Psi_{h}(\beta_{h}).

Thus,
(\ref{group2})
is equivalent to the positivity of
\mathbf{M^{h}_{2}}
.
Next, we consider
\det{\mathbb{P}_{h}}
:

\displaystyle\det{\mathbb{P}_{h}}=

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}\displaystyle 1&\alpha&\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}\\
\alpha&\beta_{h}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}&\eta_{h}\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}\beta_{h}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}&\eta_{h}\end{vmatrix}-\alpha\begin{vmatrix}\alpha&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}&\eta_{h}\end{vmatrix}+\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}\begin{vmatrix}\alpha&\beta_{h}\\
\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\eta_{h}}{2}\end{vmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\!\left[\!-\eta_{h}^{2}\!+\!2\Big(2(\beta_{h}\!-\!\alpha^{2})\!+\!\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}\!-\!\alpha d_{1}\Big)\Big)\eta_{h}\!+\!\Big(2\alpha d_{1}\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}\!-\!\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}\Big)^{2}\!\!-\!d_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}\!\Big)\right].

(3.37)

Notice that
(\ref{group2})
yields

2\alpha d_{1}\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}\Big)^{2}-d_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}<-\beta_{h}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\alpha-d_{1}\Big)^{2}\leq 0,

by elementary properties of quadratic polynomial, there exists a positive constant
\eta_{h}
such that
\det{\mathbb{P}_{h}}>0
if and only if the following situations holds:

\begin{cases}\Delta_{h}>0,\\
2(\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2})+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\alpha d_{1}\Big)\geq 0,\end{cases}

(3.38)

where
\Delta_{h}
is the discriminant of the quadratic
(\ref{quadratic_h1})
.
By calculating this discriminant and substituting
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{h})
into it, we find

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}:=

\displaystyle 4\left\{\left[(2(\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2})+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\alpha d_{1}\Big)\right]^{2}+\Big(2\alpha d_{1}\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}\Big)^{2}-d_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16\big[\,\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2}-\alpha d_{1}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}d_{1}^{2}\,\big](\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg\{-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}^{2}+2\Big[2\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)-(a_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}d_{1})\Big]\beta_{h}-(a_{2}+d_{1})^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\times\Bigg\{-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{h}^{2}+2(2-a_{1}a_{2})\beta_{h}-a_{2}^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{h}(\beta_{h})\Psi_{h}(\beta_{h}).

Since we already have
(\ref{group2})
, the equation above implies that
\Delta_{h}>0
is equivalent to
(\ref{group1})
.
Also, when
\Delta_{h}>0
and
\Psi_{h}(\beta_{h})>0
, we have

2(\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2})+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{h}-\alpha d_{1}\Big)>\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)\beta_{h}-\alpha^{2}-\alpha d_{1}>\frac{1}{4}\Phi_{h}(\beta_{h})>0,

i.e. the second inequality in
(\ref{stronger1})
is automatically satisfied.
Hence, on the basis of
(\ref{group2})
, there exist positive constants
\beta_{h}
and
\eta_{h}
such that
\det{\mathbb{P}_{h}}>0
if and only if
(\ref{group1})
holds. Summing up the discussion above, our assertion has been proved.

Now, it remains to show that under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there exists positive
\beta_{h}
which satisfies
(\ref{group1})
and
(\ref{group2})
simultaneously. For this purpose, we denote the positive solution of

and positive solution of

We assume for now that we have

\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}<a_{2}+d_{1}-1,

(3.39)

which is already contained in the case

and is indeed necessary since it comes from the existence and linear stability of heterogeneous steady state.
Thanks to

and

is not empty. On the other hand,

is not empty due to

.
Hence, the argument above allows us to calculate

,

,
L^{h}_{2}
and

.
Since
\Phi_{h}(\beta_{h})=0
if and only if

\displaystyle\beta_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2a_{1}^{2}}\left\{2\Big[\,2\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})\,\Big]\pm 2\sqrt{\Big[\,2\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})\,\Big]^{2}-a_{1}^{2}(a_{2}+d_{1})^{2}}\,\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}}\left\{\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)+\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})\Big)\pm 2\sqrt{\,\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)\Big(1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})\Big)}\,\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{1}^{2}}\left\{\sqrt{1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}}\pm\sqrt{1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}\right\}^{2}

\displaystyle L^{h}_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}}-\sqrt{1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}}{a_{1}},\ R^{h}_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}}+\sqrt{1+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}}{a_{1}},

, to prove there is overlap part between

Hence, in this case we have

. On the other hand, it follows from the discussion in

\mathbb{P}_{h}
is positive definite. By the definition of positive definite matrix, there exists

Lemma 3.4
.

The solution of

We introduce the notations

With the notations above, it remains to show

By the definition of

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\bar{u}_{h}-u^{*})=\bar{u}_{h}\big[(u^{*}-\bar{u}_{h})+a_{2}(v^{*}-\underline{v}_{h})+d_{1}\sigma(t)\big].

. Since

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big[(u^{*}-\bar{u}_{h})_{+}\big]^{2}\leq\frac{a_{2}^{2}}{2}\,[(\underline{v}_{h}-v^{*})_{+}]^{2}.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\bar{v}_{r}=r\bar{v}_{r}\big[1-a_{1}\underline{u}_{r}-\bar{v}_{r}\big],&\quad t>0,\end{cases}

(5.93)

with initial data

\underline{u}_{r}(0)=\underline{u}^{r}_{0}:=\min\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}u_{0},\quad\bar{v}_{r}(0)=\bar{v}^{c}_{0}:=\max\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}v_{0},

(5.94)

Next, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, again parallel to Lemmas
3.3
and
3.5
in Section 3.2 we derive the follwing estimates related to the auxiliary ODE system
(\ref{oder})
-
(\ref{inioder})

•

0<\underline{u}_{r}(t)\leq 1,\ 0<\bar{v}_{r}(t)\leq 1,\quad t\geq 0\,;

•

\underline{u}_{r}(t)\leq u(x,t)<1,\ \ 0<v(x,t)\leq\bar{v}_{r}(t),\quad x\in\Omega,\ t\geq 0.

Then since the auxiliary ODE system
(\ref{oder})
is also competitive, similar to proof of Lemma
4.6
, we have

||u-1||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||v||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad as\quad t\rightarrow\infty.

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
, the last step is to show

||u-1||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||v||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+||w||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{exponentially as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty,

by similar arguments in handling the homogeneous tumor state at the end of Section 4.
We omit all the details since they are similar and simpler.

It remains to prove Lemma
5.1
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Thanks to the assumption
v_{0}\leq 1
, by comparison principle of parabolic equations, we obtain that
v(x,t)\leq 1
for all
x\in\Omega
and
t>0
. Straightforward computations show

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A_{r}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(u-1)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-a_{2}\int_{\Omega}(u-1)v\,\mathrm{d}x-d_{1}\int_{\Omega}(u-1)w\,\mathrm{d}x,

(5.95)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}B_{r}(t)

\displaystyle=-a_{1}\int_{\Omega}(1-u)v\,\mathrm{d}x-(a_{1}-1)\int_{\Omega}v\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{d_{2}}{r}\int_{\Omega}vw\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-a_{1}\int_{\Omega}(1-u)v\,\mathrm{d}x-a_{1}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{d_{2}}{r}\int_{\Omega}vw\,\mathrm{d}x,

(5.96)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}C_{r}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{c}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}vw\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.97)

By differentiating
(\ref{er})
and substituting
(\ref{dar})
-
(\ref{dcr})
into it, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{r}(t)\leq-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbb{P}_{r}\mathbf{Z}\,\mathrm{d}x-\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{r}}{c}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla w\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,

(5.98)

where

\mathbb{P}_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle 1&\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{r}}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{r}}{2}&a_{1}\beta_{r}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\eta_{r}}{2}\\
\displaystyle\frac{d_{1}}{2}&\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\eta_{r}}{2}&\eta_{r}\end{pmatrix}

(5.99)

and

\mathbf{Z}=\left(u-1,v,w\right)^{\mathbf{T}}.

Similar to the previous two cases, we claim that there exist two positive constants
\beta_{r}
and
\eta_{r}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{r}
is positive definite if and only if there exists
\beta_{r}>0
satisfies the following two inequalities simultaneously:

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}(\beta_{r}):=-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{r}^{2}+2\Big[\,2\Big(a_{1}+\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})\,\Big]\beta_{r}-(a_{2}+d_{1})^{2}>0,

(5.100)

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}(\beta_{r}):=-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{r}^{2}+2(2a_{1}-a_{1}a_{2})\beta_{r}-a_{2}^{2}>0.

(5.101)

For simplicity, we denote
\alpha:=\tfrac{1}{2}(a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{r})
. To verify our assertion, we just need to compute all the principal minors of
\mathbb{P}_{r}
:

\mathbf{M^{r}_{1}}:=1,

\mathbf{M^{r}_{2}}:=\begin{vmatrix}1&\alpha\\
\alpha&a_{1}\beta_{r}\end{vmatrix}=a_{1}\beta_{r}-\alpha^{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(-a_{1}^{2}\beta_{r}^{2}+2(2a_{1}-a_{1}a_{2})\beta_{r}-a_{2}^{2}\,\right)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Psi_{r}(\beta_{r}),

and
(\ref{group2r})
holds if and only if
\mathbf{M^{r}_{2}}>0
. Now, we consider the discriminant of
\mathbb{P}_{r}
.

\displaystyle\det{\mathbb{P}_{r}}=\frac{1}{4}\left\{-\eta_{r}^{2}+2\Big(2(a_{1}\beta_{r}-\alpha^{2})+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\alpha d_{1}\Big)\Big)\eta_{r}\right.

(5.102)

\displaystyle\left.+\Big(2\alpha d_{1}\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}\Big)^{2}-a_{1}d_{1}^{2}\beta_{r}\Big)\right\}.

Notice from
(\ref{group2r})
and
a_{1}>1
that

2\alpha d_{1}\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}\Big)^{2}-a_{1}d_{1}^{2}\beta_{r}<-\beta_{r}\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\alpha-d_{1}\Big)^{2}\leq 0,

fundamental properties of quadratic polynomials implies that there exists
\eta_{r}>0
such that
\det{\mathbb{P}_{r}}>0
if and only if the following situation holds:

\begin{cases}{\Delta_{r}}>0,\\
2(a_{1}\beta_{r}-\alpha^{2})+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\alpha d_{1}\Big)\geq 0,\end{cases}

(5.103)

where
{\Delta_{r}}
is the discriminant of the quadratic
(\ref{quadratic-r})
. By calculating this discriminant and substituting
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{2}(a_{2}+a_{1}\beta_{r})
into it, we have

\Delta_{r}=16\Big[(a_{1}+d_{2})\beta_{r}-\alpha d_{1}-\frac{1}{4}d_{1}^{2}\,\Big](a_{1}\beta_{r}-\alpha^{2})=\Phi_{r}(\beta_{r})\Psi_{r}(\beta_{r})

Since we already have
(\ref{group2r})
, it follows from the above equations that
{\Delta_{r}}>0
if and only if
(\ref{group1r})
holds.
Also, when
\Delta_{r}>0
and
\Psi_{r}(\beta_{r})>0
,

2(a_{1}\beta_{r}-\alpha^{2})+\Big(\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}\beta_{r}-\alpha d_{1}\Big)>\Big(a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}\Big)\beta_{c}-\alpha^{2}-\alpha d_{1}>\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Phi_{c}(\beta_{c})>0,

which implies the second equation in
(\ref{stronger_r})
is automatically satisfied.
Therefore, on the basis of
(\ref{group2r})
, there exist two positive constants
\beta_{r}
and
\eta_{r}
such that
\det{\mathbb{P}_{r}}>0
if and only if
(\ref{group1r})
holds. Summing up the discussion above, our assertion has been proved.

Now, it remains to show that under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, there exists
\beta_{r}>0
which satisfies
(\ref{group1r})
and
(\ref{group2r})
simultaneously. For this purpose, we denote the positive solution of
(\ref{group1r})
as
S^{r}_{1}:=\left((L^{r}_{1})^{2},\,(R^{r}_{1})^{2}\right)
and positive solution of
(\ref{group2r})
as
S^{r}_{2}:=\left((L^{r}_{2})^{2},\,(R^{r}_{2})^{2}\right)
.
We assume for now that we have

\displaystyle\frac{d_{2}}{r}>a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1}-1),

(5.104)

which is already contained in the case
(\ref{d2_r_1})
.
Thanks to
(\ref{d2-new-r})
,
S^{r}_{1}
is not empty. On the other hand,
S^{r}_{2}
is not empty due to
a_{2}<1
.
Then, direct computations show that

\displaystyle L^{r}_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{1}}\left(\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}}-\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}\,\right),

\displaystyle R^{r}_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{a_{1}}\left(\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}}+\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}\,\right),

\displaystyle L^{r}_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{1}}}\big(1-\sqrt{1-a_{2}}\,\big),\quad R^{r}_{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{1}}}\big(1+\sqrt{1-a_{2}}\,\big).

Since
\frac{d_{2}}{r}>a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1}-1)
, we have

R^{r}_{1}>\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{a_{2}+d_{1}+1}}{\sqrt{a_{1}}}>\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{1}}}>L^{r}_{2},

we only need
L^{r}_{1}<R^{r}_{2}
, namely

\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}}-\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}<\sqrt{a_{1}}\big(1+\sqrt{1-a_{2}}\,\big),

(5.105)

so that there is overlap part between
S^{r}_{1}
and
S^{r}_{2}
.
Recall that we also need
(\ref{d2-new-r})
, hence, in the following part, we verify that
(\ref{d2-new-r})
and
(\ref{inequality_r})
hold under the assumption of Theorem
1.3
.

First, we consider the case
(\ref{d2_r_1})
. Since in this case we already have
(\ref{d2-new-r})
, it remains to show that when
d_{1}\leq d_{1}^{r}
, where

\frac{a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}{1+\sqrt{1-a_{2}}}<\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}}+\sqrt{a_{1}+\frac{d_{2}}{r}-a_{1}(a_{2}+d_{1})}.

Substituting

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous nonlinear
Schrödinger equation with inverse-square potential, denoted by INLS
c
equation,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{iu_{t}-P_{c}u=\lambda|x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}u,\;(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},}\\
{u\left(0,\;x\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right),}\end{array}\right.

where
d\geq 3
,
u:\;\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,
u_{0}:\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,
b,\;\sigma>0

\lambda=\pm 1

\lambda=-1

and
\lambda=1
corresponds to the defocusing case. The restriction on

(1.2)

\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|x|^{-2}\left|u(x)\right|^{2}dx}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\left|\nabla u(x)\right|dx},\penalty\ \forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

which ensures that
P_{c}
is a positive operator. The INLS
c

[
3
]
and the references therein). In particular, when
c=0

[
17

b=0

1.1

[

The case

An easy computation shows that

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
0<b<\frac{4}{d}
,
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{d-2}

\left(\gamma(r),\;r\right)
is an admissible pair satisfying

(1.8)

Moreover, for any admissible pair
\left(\gamma\left(p\right),\;p\right)
, we have

(1.9)

u\in L^{\gamma\left(p\right)}\left(\left[-T,\;T\right],\;H^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right).

If
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Theorem

[

c

To establish the nonlinear estimates, we recall the following fractional Hardy inequality which is a direct consequence of Theorem 3.1 of

Lemma 3.1

(Fractional Hardy Inequality)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<\frac{d}{p}
. Then we have

\left\||x|^{-s}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Using Lemma
3.1
, we have the following nonlinear estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\bar{r}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
,
r=\frac{2d(d+2-2b)}{d^{2}-2db+4}
,
0<b<\frac{4}{d}
and
\sigma=\frac{4-2b}{d-2}
. Then we have

(3.1)

\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1,\bar{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1,r}}^{\sigma+1},

(3.2)

\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}v\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1,\bar{r}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1,r}}^{\sigma}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{r}}.

Proof.

Noticing that

(3.3)

\left|\nabla\left(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}u\right)\right|\lesssim|x|^{-b-1}|u|^{\sigma+1}+|x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}|\nabla u|,

we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}^{1}}=\left\|\nabla\left(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}u\right)\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}\lesssim\left\||x|^{-b-1}|u|^{\sigma+1}\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}+\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}\nabla u\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}.\end{split}

First we estimate
\left\||x|^{-b-1}|u|^{\sigma+1}\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}
. We can see that

\frac{1}{\bar{r}^{\prime}}=\left(\sigma+1\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{d}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1}\right)\right).

Putting

\frac{1}{\rho}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{d}\left(1-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1}\right),

we have
\dot{H}^{1-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1},r}\subset L^{\rho}
. Here, we use the fact
1-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1}>0\Leftrightarrow b<\frac{4}{d}
. Using Lemma 3.1, we have

(3.5)

\left\||x|^{-b-1}|u|^{\sigma+1}\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}=\left\||x|^{-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1}}u\right\|_{\rho}^{\sigma+1}\lesssim\left\||x|^{-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{b+1}{\sigma+1},r}}^{\sigma+1}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1,r}}^{\sigma+1}.

Next we estimate
\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}\nabla u\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}
. We get

\sigma\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{d}\left(1-\frac{b}{\sigma}\right)\right)+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}.

Putting

\frac{1}{\gamma}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{d}\left(1-\frac{b}{\sigma}\right),

and noticing
1-\frac{b}{\sigma}>0
, we have
\dot{H}^{1-\frac{b}{\sigma},r}\subset L^{\gamma}
. Hence it follows from Hölder inequality and Lemma 3.1 that

(3.6)

\left\||x|^{-b}|u|^{\sigma}\nabla u\right\|_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}\leq\left\||x|^{-\frac{b}{\sigma}}u\right\|_{\gamma}^{\sigma}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{r}\lesssim\left\||x|^{-\frac{b}{\sigma}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\frac{b}{\sigma},r}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{1}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1,r}}^{\sigma+1}.

In view of (
3.4
)–(
3.6
), we immediately have (
3.1

Similarly we also have

4
Blowup

In this section, we prove Theorem 1.4. To arrive at this goal, we derive the sharp Hardy-Sobolev inequality as well as the standard virial identity and localized virial estimate related to the focusing, energy-critical INLS
c
equation.

4.1
Sharp Hardy-Sobolev inequality

In this subsection, we consider the sharp Hardy-Sobolev inequality related to the focusing, energy-critical INLS
c
equation:

(4.1)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|x|^{-b}\left|f\right|^{\sigma^{\star}+2}dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{\star}+2}}\leq C_{HS}(b,c)\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}},

where the sharp constant
C_{HS}(b,c)
is defined by

(4.2)

C_{HS}(b,c)=\inf_{f\in\dot{H}^{1}_{c}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}{\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}}}{\left(\int{|x|^{-b}\left|f\right|^{\sigma^{\star}+2}dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{\star}+2}}}}.

Lemma 4.1

(Sharp Hardy-Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
d\geq 3
,
0<b<2
and
c>-c(d)
.

1.

If
-c(d)<c\leq 0
, then the equality in (
4.1
) is attained by function
W_{b,c}(x)
given in (
1.10
).

2.

If
c>0
, then
C_{HS}(b,c)\leq C_{HS}(b,0)
.

Proof.

The proof of Item 1 can be found in
[
16
]
. Using the fact
c>0
, we immediately have that
\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}}
for any
f\in\dot{H}^{1}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Hence it follows from Item 1 that

\left(\int{|x|^{-b}\left|f\right|^{\sigma^{\star}+2}dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{\sigma^{\star}+2}}\leq C_{HS}(b,0)\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<C_{HS}(b,0)\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}},

which implies that
C_{HS}(b,c)\leq C_{HS}(b,0)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

When
b=0
and
c>0
, it is known that
C_{HS}(b,c)=C_{HS}(b,0)
and the equality in (
4.1
) is never attained. In fact,
C_{HS}(0,c)\geq C_{HS}(0,0)
can be proved by considering
f_{n}(x)=W_{0,c}(x-x_{n})
for any sequence
x_{n}\rightarrow\infty
. See
[
10
,
18
]
for details. But when
b>0
, we could not apply this argument and we don’t know whether
C_{HS}(b,c)=C_{HS}(b,0)
.

Next, we recall some properties related to
W_{b,c}
. Lemma 2.2 of
[
16
]
also shows that
W_{b,c}
with
-c(d)<c\leq 0
solves the equation

P_{c}W_{b,c}=|x|^{-b}\left|W_{b,c}\right|^{\sigma^{\star}}W_{b,c},

and satisfies

(4.3)

\left\|W_{b,c}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}}^{2}=\int{|x|^{-b}W_{b,c}^{\sigma^{\star}+2}dx}.

Hence, we have for
-c(d)<c\leq 0
,

(4.4)

\left\|W_{b,c}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}}^{2}=\int{|x|^{-b}W_{b,c}^{\sigma^{\star}+2}dx}=C_{HS}(b,c)^{-\frac{2(d-b)}{2-b}},\penalty\ \left\|W_{b,c}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}}^{\sigma^{\star}}=C_{HS}(b,c)^{-(\sigma^{\star}+2)},

(4.5)

E_{b,c}\left(W_{b,c}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|W_{b,c}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{c}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\sigma^{\star}+2}\int{|x|^{-b}W_{b,c}^{\sigma^{\star}+2}dx}=\frac{2-b}{2(d-b)}C_{HS}(b,c)^{-\frac{2(d-b)}{2-b}}.

Moreover, for any
c>-c(d)
, we have

(4.6)

C_{HS}(b,c)\leq C_{HS}(b,\bar{c})=\left\|W_{b,\bar{c}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{\bar{c}}}^{-\frac{2-b}{d-b}}=\left\||x|^{-b}W_{b,\bar{c}}^{\sigma^{\star}+2}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{-\frac{2-b}{2(d-b)}}=\left[\frac{2(d-b)}{2-b}E_{b,\bar{c}}\left(W_{b,\bar{c}}\right)\right]^{-\frac{2-b}{2(d-b)}}.

4.2
Virial estimates

In this subsection, we derive the standard virial identity and localized virial estimate related to the focusing INLS
c
equation. Given a real valued function
a
, we define the virial potential by

V_{a}(t):=\int{a(x)\left|u(t,x)\right|^{2}dx.}

A simple computation shows that the following result holds.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 3
and
c>-c(d)
. If
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a smooth-in-time and Schwartz-in-space solution to
iu_{t}-P_{c}u=N(u)

\textnormal{Im}(N(u)\bar{u})=0

\frac{d}{dt}V_{a}(t)=2\int{\nabla a(x)\cdot\textnormal{Im}(\bar{u}(t,x)\nabla u(t,x))dx},

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{a}(t)&=-\int{\Delta^{2}a(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx+4\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}{\int{\partial_{jk}^{2}a(x)\textnormal{Re}(\partial_{k}u(t,x)\partial_{j}\bar{u}(t,x))dx}}}\\

where
\left\{f,g\right\}_{p}:=\textnormal{Re}(f\nabla\bar{g}-g\nabla\bar{f})

Note that if

Hence, we immediately have the following result.

.

If
u
is a smooth-in-time and Schwartz-in-space solution to the focusing INLS
c
equation, then we have for any
t\in I

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}V_{a}(t)&=-\int{\Delta^{2}a(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx+4\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}{\int{\partial_{jk}^{2}a(x)\textnormal{Re}(\partial_{k}u(t,x)\partial_{j}\bar{u}(t,x))dx}}}\\
&+4c\int{\nabla a(x)\cdot\frac{x}{|x|^{4}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx}-\frac{2\sigma}{\sigma+2}\int{\Delta a(x)|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{\sigma+2}dx}\\

We have the following standard virial identity for the focusing INLS
c

(Standard Virial Identity)
.

4.3

•

The case

First, we consider the case

Next, we consider the case

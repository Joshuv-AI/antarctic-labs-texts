Simplified Forms of the Transition Probabilities of the Two-Species ASEP with Some Initial Orders of Particles
Eunghyun LEE and Temirlan RAIMBEKOV

Abstract
It has been known that the transition probability of the single species ASEP with N N particles is expressed as a sum of N ! N! N N -fold contour integrals which are related to permutations in the symmetric group S N S_{N} . On other hand, the transition probabilities of the multi-species ASEP, in general, may be expressed as a sum of much more terms than N ! N! . In this paper, we show that if the initial order of species is given by 2 ⋯ 21 2\cdots 21 , 12 ⋯ 2 12\cdots 2 , 1 ⋯ 12 1\cdots 12 or 21 ⋯ 1 21\cdots 1 , then the transition probabilities can be expressed as a sum of at most N ! N! contour integrals, and provide their formulas explicitly.

Introduction

1.1
Definition of the model and previous results

In the multi-species asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) on
\mathbb{Z}
, particles belong to a species labelled by one of positive integers. Each particle chooses a direction to move one step to the right or to the left after waiting time exponentially distributed with rate 1. The probability to choose the right direction is
p
and the probability to choose the left direction is
q=1-p
. If the target site is empty, the particle moves to the site, but if the site is already occupied, the following rule is applied: if a particle belonging to species
l
tries moving to the target site occupied by a particle belonging to species
l^{\prime}\geq l
, then the move is prohibited, but if
l^{\prime}<l
, the particle belonging to
l
can move to the target site by interchanging sites with the particle belonging to
l^{\prime}
. If we assume that there are
N
particles, a state is denoted by a pair
(X,\pi)
where
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
with
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N}
for the positions of particles and
\pi=\pi(1)\pi(2)\cdots\pi(N)
is a permutation of a multi-set
\mathcal{M}=[i_{1},\dots,i_{N}]
with elements taken from
\{1,\dots,N\}
to represent the species of particles. Here,
\pi(i)
represents the
i^{\rm th}
leftmost particle’s species. Since particles can interchange their positions, the order of species may change over time. The transition probability from the initial state
(Y,\nu)
to state
(X,\pi)
at time
t
is denoted by
P_{(Y,\nu)}(X,\pi;t).
Earlier works on the transition probabilities and some distributions in the multi-species ASEP are found in
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
17
,
18
]
. Also, the multi-species ASEP can be considered as a special case of the coloured stochastic vertex model (see
[
1
,
2
]
). According to
[
12
]
, the transition probability of the multi-species ASEP with
N
particles from the initial state
(Y,\nu)
to the state
(X,\pi)
is a matrix element of an
N^{N}\times N^{N}
matrix
\mathbf{P}_{Y}(X;t)
whose columns
\nu
and rows
\pi
are labelled by
11\cdots 1,\dots,NN\cdots N
. In
[
12
]
, the formula of
\mathbf{P}_{Y}(X;t)
is written

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{Y}(X;t)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{c}\cdots\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{c}\sum_{\sigma\in{S}_{N}}\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\big(\xi_{\sigma(i)}^{x_{i}-y_{\sigma(i)}-1}{\rm e}^{\varepsilon(\xi_{i})t}\big)\,{\rm d}\xi_{1}\cdots{\rm d}\xi_{N},

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}
is a
N^{N}\times N^{N}
matrix. Note that the form of (
1.1
) resembles (2.3) in
[
16
]
. Here,
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{c}
implies
(1/2\pi{\rm i})\int_{c}
where the contour
c
is a positively oriented circle centered at the origin with sufficiently small radius so that no poles except the one at the origin are included in
c
, and

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\xi_{i})=\frac{p}{\xi_{i}}+q\xi_{i}-1

and the sum in (
1.1
) is over all permutations
\sigma
in the symmetric group
S_{N}
.
The integral on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) implies the matrix element-wise integral, so the
(\pi,\nu)^{\rm th}
term of
\mathbf{P}_{Y}(X;t)
is

\displaystyle[\mathbf{P}_{Y}(X;t)]_{\pi,\nu}

\displaystyle=P_{(Y,\nu)}(X,\pi;t)

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{c}\cdots\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{c}\sum_{\sigma\in{S}_{N}}[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\big(\xi_{\sigma(i)}^{x_{i}-y_{\sigma(i)}-1}{\rm e}^{\varepsilon(\xi_{i})t}\big)\,{\rm d}\xi_{1}\cdots{\rm d}\xi_{N}.

(1.2)

The procedure introduced in
[
12
]
to find the matrix
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}
is as follows. Let
T_{i}
be the simple transposition which interchanges the number at the
i^{\rm th}
slot and the number at the
(i+1)^{\rm st}
slot. It is well known that simple transpositions
T_{1},\dots,T_{N-1}
generate the symmetric group
S_{N}
. Hence, any permutation
\sigma\in S_{N}
can be written as a product of simple transpositions, that is,

\displaystyle\sigma=T_{i_{j}}\cdots T_{i_{1}}

(1.3)

for some
i_{1},\dots,i_{j}\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
. (Here, the expression (
1.3
) is not unique.) If
T_{i}
is acted on a permutation with
\alpha
at the
i^{\rm th}
slot and
\beta
at the
(i+1)^{\rm st}
slot, then

\displaystyle T_{i}(\cdots\alpha\beta\cdots)=(\cdots\beta\alpha\cdots),

and we denote this
T_{i}
by
T_{i}(\beta,\alpha)
when we need to show explicitly which numbers are interchanged. For example, for
N=3
,

\displaystyle T_{1}T_{2}T_{1}=T_{1}T_{2}T_{1}(123)=T_{1}T_{2}(213)=T_{1}(231)=321,

and we write
321=T_{1}(3,2)T_{2}(3,1)T_{1}(2,1)
. Hence, we will write (
1.3
) as

\displaystyle\sigma=T_{i_{j}}(\beta_{j},\alpha_{j})\cdots T_{i_{1}}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})

(1.4)

when necessary.
Corresponding to
T_{i}(\beta,\alpha)
, we define
N^{N}\times N^{N}
matrix
\mathbf{T}_{i}(\beta,\alpha)
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{i}(\beta,\alpha)=\underbrace{\mathbf{I}_{N}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N}}_{(i-1)~\text{times}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{\beta\alpha}\otimes\underbrace{\mathbf{I}_{N}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N}}_{(N-i-1)~\text{times}},

where
\mathbf{I}_{N}
is the
N\times N
identity matrix and
\mathbf{R}_{\beta\alpha}
is an
N^{2}\times N^{2}
matrix whose columns and rows are labelled by
11,12,\dots,1N,21,\dots,NN
. The matrix elements of
\mathbf{R}_{\beta\alpha}
are given by

\displaystyle[\mathbf{R}_{\beta\alpha}]_{ij,kl}=\begin{cases}S_{\beta\alpha}&\text{if}~ij=kl~\text{with}~i=j,\\
P_{\beta\alpha}&\text{if}~ij=kl~\text{with}~i<j,\\
Q_{\beta\alpha}&\text{if}~ij=kl~\text{with}~i>j,\\
pT_{\beta\alpha}&\text{if}~ij=lk~\text{with}~i<j,\\
qT_{\beta\alpha}&\text{if}~ij=lk~\text{with}~i>j,\\
0&\text{for all other cases},\end{cases}

(1.5)

where

\displaystyle S_{\beta\alpha}=-\frac{p+q\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\xi_{\beta}}{p+q\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\xi_{\alpha}},\qquad

\displaystyle P_{\beta\alpha}=\frac{(p-q\xi_{\alpha})(\xi_{\beta}-1)}{p+q\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\xi_{\alpha}},

\displaystyle T_{\beta\alpha}=\frac{\xi_{\beta}-\xi_{\alpha}}{p+q\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\xi_{\alpha}},\qquad

\displaystyle Q_{\beta\alpha}=\frac{(p-q\xi_{\beta})(\xi_{\alpha}-1)}{p+q\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-\xi_{\alpha}}.

With this setting, it was obtained that
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}=\mathbf{T}_{i_{j}}(\beta_{j},\alpha_{j})\cdots\mathbf{T}_{i_{1}}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1}),

(1.6)

when
\sigma
is written as in (
1.4
). Although the expression (
1.4
) is not unique, (
1.6
) is well-defined in the sense that (
1.6
) represents the same matrix for any expression (
1.4
) (see
[
12
, Remark 2.2]
).

1.2
Motivation and main results

1.2.1
Motivation

It can be shown that
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}=0
in (
1.2
) unless both
\pi
and
\nu
are from the same multi-set (see Section
2.1
), and in this case, it is obvious that
P_{(Y,\nu)}(X,\pi;t)=0
, which physically also makes sense. However, in general, the matrix element of
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}
is written as a
sum
because
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}
is a product of matrices as seen in (
1.6
). If we want to know the explicit formula of
P_{(Y,\nu)}(X,\pi;t)
, we should know the corresponding matrix elements,
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}
, explicitly, for all
\sigma
. It is interesting that some elements of
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}
that are expressed as
sums
can be further factorized. For example, for
\sigma=321=T_{2}(2,1)T_{1}(3,1)T_{2}(3,2)
,

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{321}]_{121,211}=[\mathbf{T}_{2}(2,1)\mathbf{T}_{1}(3,1)\mathbf{T}_{2}(3,2)]_{121,221}=pP_{32}Q_{31}T_{21}+pQ_{21}S_{31}T_{32}

but we can further observe that

\displaystyle pP_{32}Q_{31}T_{21}+pQ_{21}S_{31}T_{32}=Q_{21}pT_{31}S_{32}.

Actually, if we use a different expression
\sigma=321=T_{1}(3,2)T_{2}(3,1)T_{1}(2,1)
, then we directly obtain

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{321}]_{121,211}=[\mathbf{T}_{1}(3,2)\mathbf{T}_{2}(3,1)\mathbf{T}_{1}(2,1)]_{121,221}=Q_{21}pT_{31}S_{32}

through matrix multiplication. However, unfortunately, not all
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}
can be simplified to a factorized form. In this paper, we are interested in the factorized forms of
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}
because single-species models for which the Bethe ansatz can be applicable have factorized forms in the transition probabilities and some interesting probability distributions were obtained from these transition probabilities
[
5
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
19
]
. In this paper, we show that if the initial permutation
\nu
of species is one of
\nu=2\cdots 21
,
1\cdots 12
,
21\cdots 1
and
12\cdots 2
, and
\sigma
’s expression by simple transpositions is obtained in a special way, then
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}
is zero or written as a factorized form

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}=\prod_{(\beta,\alpha)}R_{\beta\alpha},

(1.7)

where
R_{\beta\alpha}
is one of
S_{\beta\alpha}
,
Q_{\beta\alpha}
,
P_{\beta\alpha}
,
pT_{\beta\alpha}
or
qT_{\beta\alpha}
, which is similar to

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{1\cdots 1,1\cdots 1}=\prod_{(\beta,\alpha)}S_{\beta\alpha}

in the (single species) ASEP
[
16
]
. The notation
\prod_{(\beta,\alpha)}
implies that the product is taken over all inversions
(\beta,\alpha)
of
\sigma
. An inversion of a permutation
\sigma=\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(N)
is a pair of elements
(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))
with
i<j
and
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)
.
It will remain for future works to see if some interesting probability distributions can be obtained in
neat
forms from the transition probabilities with the formulas of
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}
given in this paper.

1.2.2
Main results

Each permutation in
S_{N}
may be written as a product of simple transpositions in many ways via

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}&~~~\text{if $|i-j|\geq 2$},\\
&T_{i}T_{j}T_{i}=T_{j}T_{i}T_{j}&~~~\text{if $|i-j|=1$},\\
&T_{i}^{2}=1.\end{aligned}

(1.8)

One of the findings in this paper is that if
\sigma
is expressed as in Theorem
1.1
, which is a known fact, then
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,2\cdots 21}
is zero or written as (
1.7
). If
\sigma
is expressed in a different way from Theorem
1.1
, then the form of
[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,2\cdots 21}
may not be in a factorized form.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Consider the following subsets of the symmetric group
S_{N}
.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Sigma_{1}=\{1,T_{1}\},\\
&\Sigma_{2}=\{1,T_{2},T_{2}T_{1}\},\\
&\Sigma_{3}=\{1,T_{3},T_{3}T_{2},T_{3}T_{2}T_{1}\},\\
&\hskip 8.5359pt\vdots\\
&\Sigma_{N-1}=\{1,T_{N-1},T_{N-1}T_{N-2},\dots,T_{N-1}\cdots T_{2}T_{1}\}.\end{aligned}

For any permutation
\sigma\in S_{N}
, there is a unique element

\displaystyle(w_{1},\dots,w_{N-1})\in\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}\times\cdots\times\Sigma_{N-1}

such that
\sigma=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{N-1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma=w_{1}\cdots w_{N-1}
be an expression as in Theorem
1.1
and let

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\sigma}=\mathbf{T}_{i_{j}}(\beta_{j},\alpha_{j})\cdots\mathbf{T}_{i_{2}}(\beta_{2},\alpha_{2})\mathbf{T}_{i_{1}}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})

be the matrix corresponding to
\sigma

Then, for all
N\geq 2
,

(a)

\big[{\mathbf{A}}_{12\cdots N}\big]_{2\cdots 21,2\cdots 21}=1
and
\big[{\mathbf{A}}_{12\cdots N}\big]_{\pi,2\cdots 21}=0
if
\pi\neq 2\cdots 21
.

(b)

If
\sigma\neq 12\cdots N
, then
\big[{\mathbf{A}}_{\sigma}\big]_{\pi,2\cdots 21}
is zero or written as

\displaystyle\big[{\mathbf{A}}_{\sigma}\big]_{\pi,2\cdots 21}=\prod_{(\beta,\alpha)}R_{\beta\alpha}

(1.9)

where
R_{\beta\alpha}
is one of
S_{\beta\alpha}
,

,
Q_{\beta\alpha}
,
pT_{\beta\alpha}
,
qT_{\beta\alpha}
. The product in (
1.9
) is taken over all inversions

in
\sigma
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem

The method to express

Now, we state the formulas for
[{\mathbf{A}}_{\sigma}]_{\pi,\nu}
. First, we find the formula of the diagonal terms. That is, this is the case that initial order of species and the order at time

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\nu=2\cdots 21

\displaystyle\sigma=w_{1}\cdots w_{N-1}={T}_{i_{j}}(\beta_{j},\alpha_{j})\cdots{T}_{i_{1}}(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})

(1.10)

.

Remark 1.9
.

The formulas for

and

Remark 1.10
.

The technique in

provided a method to find the explicit formulas of the transition probabilities but

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}\big((\mathbf{w}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w}_{N-1})\oplus({\mathcal{S}}_{l+1}\cdots{\mathcal{S}}_{N-1})\big)_{i,k}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{k,N+1}

\displaystyle=\big((\mathbf{w}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w}_{N-1})\oplus({\mathcal{S}}_{l+1}\cdots{\mathcal{S}}_{N-1})\big)_{i,N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{w}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w}_{N-1})_{i,N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}.

\displaystyle[\mathbf{w}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w}_{N-1}]_{i,N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\prod_{k=l+1}^{i-1}[\mathbf{w}_{k}]_{i,i}\prod_{k=i}^{N-1}[\mathbf{w}_{k}]_{k,k+1}\Bigg)[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\prod_{k=l+1}^{i-1}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{i,i}\prod_{k=i}^{N-1}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{k,k+1}\Bigg)[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=l+1}^{i-1}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{i,i}\prod_{k=i}^{N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{k,k+1}.

for

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{\sigma^{\prime}}]_{i,N+1}=\prod_{k=l+1}^{i-1}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{i,i}\prod_{k=i}^{N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{k,k+1}

1.4

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N+1}[\mathbf{w^{\prime}}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w^{\prime}}_{N-1}]_{N,k}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{k,N+1}

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{w^{\prime}}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w^{\prime}}_{N-1}]_{N,N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}=[\mathbf{w}_{l+1}\cdots\mathbf{w}_{N-1}]_{N,N}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\prod_{k=l+1}^{N-1}[\mathbf{w}_{k}]_{N,N}\Bigg)[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}=\Bigg(\prod_{k=l+1}^{N-1}[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}]_{N,N}\Bigg)[\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{N}]_{N,N+1}.

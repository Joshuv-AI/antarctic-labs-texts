Abstract:

Very recently, Junk
[
11
]
showed that for directed polymers in bounded random environments, the weak disorder (uniform integrable) phase implies that
the polymer martingale is bounded in
L^{p}
for some
p>1
and also in
L^{q}
for some
q<0
. Here, we establish this characterization of the weak disorder phase without requiring
the boundedness assumption on the environments.

1.
Introduction

We consider the model of
directed polymers in random environment
described by the following setup. Fix a collection
\{\omega(t,x):t\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.)
random variables on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
satisfying

\mathbb{E}[{\rm e}^{\beta|\omega(1,0)}|]<\infty\qquad\qquad\forall\beta\geq 0.

(1.1)

In the above display and in the sequel,
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}
will stand for the expectation w.r.t.
\mathbb{P}
.
We are interested in the
renormalized partition function
defined by

W_{n}=W_{n}(\beta):=E_{0}\left[{\rm e}^{\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(i,S_{i})-n\lambda(\beta)}\right],

(1.2)

where
\lambda(\beta):=\log\mathbb{E}[{\rm e}^{\beta\omega(1,0)}]
and
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
starting at the origin, whose law is denoted by
P_{0}
and corresponding expectation denoted by
E_{0}
.
As already observed by Bolthausen
[
3
]
, for any
\beta\geq 0
,

(W_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a (non-negative) martingale w.r.t. the filtration generated by the i.i.d. random variables
\{\omega(t,x):t\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
.
Therefore, for any
\beta\geq 0
,
W_{n}
converges almost surely to a non-negative random variable
W_{\infty}=W_{\infty}(\beta)
. It is not difficult to see that the
event
\{W_{\infty}=0\}
belongs to the tail
\sigma
-field generated by the i.i.d. random variables
\{\omega(t,x):t\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
and therefore

\mathbb{P}[W_{\infty}>0]\in\{0,1\}
. It is well-known that (
[
10
,
3
,
9
,
8
,
13
]
)
when
d\geq 3
, there is
\beta_{c}=\beta_{c}(d)\in(0,\infty)
(see (
2.3
) for a precise definition) so that for for
\beta\in(0,\beta_{c})
,
W_{\infty}
is non-degenerate and

W_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(\beta)>0\qquad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.,}

(1.3)

while for
\beta>\beta_{c}
,
W_{\infty}=0
. The phases
\beta\in(0,\beta_{c})
(resp.
\beta>\beta_{c}
) are known as the
weak
(resp.
strong
)
disorder
.
Furthermore, weak disorder is equivalent to uniform
integrability of the martingale
(W_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Recently, a new characterization of the weak disorder phase was established in an important work of Junk
[
11
]
, who showed that if
the random environment is bounded
– that is, if we assume that

\text{there exists some }K>0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}(\omega(1,0)\leq K)=1,

(1.4)

then uniform integrability of the martingale
(W_{n})_{n}
implies its
L^{p}(\mathbb{P})
-boundedness for some
p>1
(i.e.,
\sup_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]<\infty
).
It was also shown there that under the assumption

\text{there exists some }K>0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}(\omega(1,0)\geq-K)=1,

(1.5)

the uniform integrability of
(W_{n})_{n}
implies that it is
L^{q}(\mathbb{P})
-bounded for some
q<0
. The purpose of our article is to show that these properties
continue to remain true even if we drop the boundedness assumption. Here is our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
d\geq 3
and
\beta>0
so that the martingale
(W_{n})_{n}
satisfies (
1.3
). Then there exist
p_{0}>1
and
q_{0}<0
such that

•

for all
1<p<p_{0}
, the martingale
(W_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is
L^{p}(\mathbb{P})
-bounded,

•

for all
q_{0}<q<0
, the martingale
(W_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is
L^{q}(\mathbb{P})
-bounded.

To put our work into context, let us mention that the uniform integrability criterion for weak disorder does not provide any

closed-form
characterization of the critical temperature
\beta_{c}
and, in practice, the uniform integrability is also not easy to analyze. Several important features of the weak disorder phase have been established
(e.g.
[
9
,
2
]
) but our understanding of the directed polymers is much more complete and a lot more works are available for a different,
very high temperature phase
, which is characterized by the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the martingale
(W_{n})_{n}
, which is strictly stronger than uniform integrability, see (
2.4
) below. While the very high temperature regime is computationally quite convenient, the result of
[
11
]
shows that, for bounded environments, no
true
phase transition occurs within the weak disorder phase at the
L^{2}
threshold. Theorem
1.1
above reconfirms this intuition and underlines that the characterization of weak disorder via moments is an
intrinsic
property of directed polymers in a random environment, independent of any boundedness of the underlying environment – in particular, Theorem
1.1
establishes this characterization of weak disorder for natural choices of unbounded environments like Gaussian, Poisson etc.

Let us briefly comment on the main idea of the proof. An important ingredient for the argument in
[
11
]
is a result concerning the running maximum of uniformly integrable martingales (see Theorem 2.1 there). Such arguments
have been quite useful in the context of branching processes
[
1
]
and branching random walks
[
4
]
where the property

\frac{M_{n+1}}{M_{n}}\leq K\qquad\mbox{a.s. for some}\,\,K>0,

(1.6)

(here
(M_{n})_{n}
is the associated martingale in the respective setup)
implies that the martingale converges in
L^{p}
for some explicit
p>1
. If one assumes that for any convex function
f
,
\mathbb{E}[f(\frac{M_{n+m}}{M_{n}})|\mathcal{F}_{n}]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(M_{m})]
, then
the branching structure provides enough independence between particles in generation
n
, implying the
L^{p}
boundedness since the influence from the early generations is small.
This idea was used in
[
11
]
for directed polymers in a random environment, where in contrast to the branching structure, the partition function carries long-range correlations. Combined with the boundedness assumption
(note that (
1.4
) implies (
1.6
)) the
L^{p}
boundedness was then established there.
While the latter assumption is not available in the present setup, we will combine the previous approach with some of the ideas from our recent work
[
6
]
, where a similar statement as Theorem
1.1

was obtained for Gaussian multiplicative chaos in the Wiener space (also constructed there).
However, in the continuous setup, contributions coming from the “jumps” and the stopping times that we use get flushed out, which contrasts the present discrete setup.
Finally, we remark that, under the aforementioned boundedness assumption, it has been shown also recently in
[
12
, Corollary 1.3]
that for weak disorder,
(W_{n})_{n}
remains bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{P})
for

p\in(1,1+\frac{2}{d})
. This result heavily depends on the fluctuation results
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
obtained there for which the boundedness assumption seems to play perhaps an even more important role. It is conceivable
that combined with some of the present ideas, a similar statement could also be shown without requiring boundedness.

The rest of the article is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For the proof of the theorem, we will use the following results from
[
11
]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
be a convex function, and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\mathcal{F}_{n}
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the i.i.d. random variables

\big(\omega(i,x):i\leq n,x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\big)
, then

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\frac{W_{n+m}}{W_{n}}\right)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(W_{m})]\qquad a.s.

(2.1)

In particular,

\mathbb{E}\left[f\left(\frac{W_{n+m}}{W_{n}}\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(W_{m})]\leq\mathbb{E}[f(W_{n+m})].

(2.2)

The inequality is reversed for a concave function.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
M_{n}:=\max_{0\leq j\leq n}W_{n}
and
M_{\infty}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}W_{n}
. If the martingale
W_{n}
is uniformly integrable, then
\mathbb{E}[M_{\infty}]<\infty
.

The proofs can be found in Appendix
A
.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: positive moments.

Let

\beta_{c}:=\sup\Big\{\beta>0:(W_{n})_{n\geq 0}\text{ is uniformly integrable}\Big\}

(2.3)

and

\beta_{L^{2}}:=\sup\Big\{\beta>0:\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{2}]<\infty\Big\}.

(2.4)

It is well-known that
0<\beta_{L^{2}}<\beta_{c}
when
d\geq 3

[
5
,
7
]
. Moreover, if
\beta<\beta_{L^{2}}
, then we can take
p_{0}=2
.
1
†
†

1
Note that Theorem
1.1
also implies that for the directed polymer model, for
\beta\in(0,\beta_{L^{2}})
, there is
\varepsilon>0
such that
\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{2+\varepsilon}]<\infty.
Thus, we can assume that
\beta_{L_{2}}\leq\beta<\beta_{c}
, and therefore we need to find
p_{0}\in(1,2)
such that
W_{\infty}\in L^{p}
for all
1<p<p_{0}
. Given
t>1
, define the stopping time

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle:=\inf\Big\{n\geq 0:W_{n}>t\Big\}.

(2.5)

For a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>1
,
p\in(1,2)
to determine, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p},\tau_{t}>n]+\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p},\tau_{t}\leq n]

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq t^{p}+\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p},\tau_{t}\leq n]

\displaystyle=t^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p},\tau_{t}=i]

\displaystyle=t^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{n}}{W_{i}}\right)^{p}\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{p}W_{i-1}^{p},\tau_{t}=i\right]

\displaystyle\leq t^{p}+t^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{n}}{W_{i}}\right)^{p}\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{p}\tau_{t}=i\right].

In the last line, we used that on the event
\{\tau_{t}=i\}
,
W_{i-1}^{p}\leq t^{p}
. Conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{i}
and using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]

\displaystyle\leq t^{p}+t^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{p}\mathbbm{1}\{\tau_{t}=i\}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{n}}{W_{i}}\right)^{p}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{i}\right]\right]

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq t^{p}+t^{p}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{p}\mathbbm{1}\{\tau_{t}=i\}\right]

Moreover, if
q,r>1
(to be chosen later) satisfy
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1
, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\tau_{t}=i,\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{pq}\right]^{1/q}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

(2.8)

By Lemma
2.1
,
\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{pq}\right]^{1/q}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[W_{1}^{pq}\right]^{1/q}
. Since
p<2
,

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{1}^{pq}\right]^{1/q}\leq\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]^{\frac{p}{2q}}\leq\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]

(recall that
\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]\in[1,\infty)
by (
1.1
)). The last display and (
2.8
) yield

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\tau_{t}=i,\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)^{1-\frac{1}{q}},

(2.9)

and therefore (recall (
2.7
)),

\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]\leq t^{p}+t^{p}\mathbb{E}\left[W_{n}^{p}\right]\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

(2.10)

Let

\varphi(t,r):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)^{r}\in[0,\infty],\qquad t>1,r\in(0,1].

Note that for all
t>1
,
\varphi(t,1)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau=i)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}<\infty)=\mathbb{P}(M_{\infty}>t)
(recall Lemma
2.2
), and

\mathbb{E}[M_{\infty}]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{\infty}>t){\rm d}t=1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{\infty}>t)<\infty.

We conclude that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\varphi(t,1){\rm d}t=c:=\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{\infty}>t){\rm d}t<\infty.

By monotone convergence,
\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\varphi(t,r){\rm d}t=c
. In particular, for
r>0
close to
1,\int_{1}^{\infty}\varphi(t,r){\rm d}t<\infty
. As a consequence, if
q>1
is sufficiently large, then for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
t_{0}=t_{0}(q,\varepsilon)>1
satisfying

\sup_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t_{0}}=i)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\leq\varphi\left(t_{0},1-\frac{1}{q}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon}{t_{0}}.

For
q
as above, set

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]},

so that for some
t_{0}>1
,

\displaystyle t_{0}^{p}\,\mathbb{E}\left[(W_{n})^{p}\right]\,\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{2q}]\,\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t_{0}}=i)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg)<\frac{t_{0}^{p-1}}{2}.

Finally, choose
p\in(1,2)
such that
t_{0}^{p-1}<2
. The last display and (
2.10
) imply

\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]\leq t_{0}^{p}+\frac{t_{0}^{p-1}}{2}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}],

and hence

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{p}]\leq\frac{2t_{0}^{p}}{2-t_{0}^{p-1}}<\infty.

∎

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: negative moments

We will find
q\in(0,1)
such that
\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]<\infty
. The proof is similar to the previous case. For
t>1
to determine, set

\displaystyle\tau_{t}

\displaystyle:=\inf\{n\geq 0:W_{n}\leq 1/t\}.

We obtain a decomposition as in (
2.6
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q},\tau_{t}>n]+\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q},\tau_{t}\leq n]

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq t^{q}+\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q},\tau_{t}\leq n]

\displaystyle=t^{q}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q},\tau_{t}=i]

\displaystyle=t^{q}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{n}}{W_{i}}\right)^{-q}\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{-q}W_{i-1}^{-q},\tau_{t}=i\right]

\displaystyle\leq t^{q}+t^{q}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{n}}{W_{i}}\right)^{-q}\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{-q},\tau_{t}=i\right],

where in the last line we used that
W_{i-1}\geq\frac{1}{t}
on the event
\{\tau_{t}=i\}
. Conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{i}
and using Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that

\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]\leq t^{q}+t^{q}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{-q},\tau_{t}=i\right].

(2.12)

Next, we apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{1}{q}
and
\frac{1}{1-q}
, and (
2.2
) with the convex function
x\mapsto x^{-1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]

\displaystyle\leq t^{q}+t^{q}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{W_{i}}{W_{i-1}}\right)^{-1}\right]^{q}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)^{1-q}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq t^{q}+t^{q}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q}]\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{-1}]\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)^{1-q}.

Note that

\varphi(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}=i)=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t}<\infty)\to 0\qquad\mbox{as }\,\,t\to\infty.

Therefore, for
t_{0}>1
large enough,

\varphi(t_{0})<\frac{1}{4\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{-1}]}.

Since
\varphi(t_{0})=\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t_{0}}=i)^{r}
, we can find some
q_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

t_{0}^{q_{0}}<2\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{t_{0}}=i)^{1-q_{0}}<\frac{1}{2\mathbb{E}[W_{1}^{-1}]}.

The last display, combined with (
2.13
) implies that for all
n
and
q_{0}\in(0,1)
as above,

\mathbb{E}[W_{n}^{-q_{0}}]\leq\frac{4}{2-t_{0}^{q_{0}}}<\infty.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.∎

Appendix A
Proof of Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
.

While the proofs can be found in
[
11
]
, we include them here for the sake of completeness.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let

\mu_{n,\beta}(A)=\mu_{\beta,n}^{\omega}(A)=\frac{1}{W_{n}}E_{0}\big[\exp\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega(i,S_{i})-n\lambda(\beta)\big){\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{A}\big]

be the polymer (probability) measure and let

(\theta_{n,y}\omega)(\cdot,\cdot)=\omega(n+\cdot,y+\cdot)
denote the space-time shift. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[f\bigg(\frac{W_{n+m}}{W_{n}}\bigg)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[f\bigg(\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mu_{n,\beta}(S_{n}=y)W_{m}\circ\theta_{n,y}\bigg)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]

(A.1)

By Jensen’s inequality,

f\bigg(\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mu_{n,\beta}(S_{n}=y)W_{m}\circ\theta_{n,y}\bigg)\leq\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mu_{n,\beta}(S_{n}=y)f\big(W_{m}\circ\theta_{n,y}\big)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[f\bigg(\frac{W_{n+m}}{W_{n}}\bigg)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{n}\bigg]\leq\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mu_{n,\beta}(S_{n}=y)\mathbb{E}\big[f\big(W_{m}\circ\theta_{n,y}\big)\big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[f(W_{m})].

∎

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Let
\tau:=\inf\{n:W_{n}>t\}
, so that
W_{\tau}>t
on the event
\{\tau<\infty\}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[W_{n}>t\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg[\tau\leq n,\frac{W_{n}}{W_{\tau}}>\varepsilon\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\big[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\tau=k}\mathbb{E}\bigg({\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\frac{W_{n}}{W_{k}}>\varepsilon}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\tau=k}\mathbb{E}\bigg[f_{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{W_{n}}{W_{k}}\big)\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{k}\bigg]\bigg],

where
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}-1)\wedge 1
, which is a concave function that satisfies

{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{[\varepsilon,\infty)}(x)\geq f_{\varepsilon}(x)\geq{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{[2\varepsilon,\infty)}(x)-{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{[0,\varepsilon]}(x)
whenever
x\geq 0
.
Now, using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}[W_{n}>t\varepsilon]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigg[{\mathchoice{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.0mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-4.5mu\mathrm{l}}{1\mskip-5.0mu\mathrm{l}}}_{\tau=k}\mathbb{E}\big[f_{\varepsilon}(W_{n-k})\big]\geq\mathbb{P}[\tau\leq n]\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}[f_{\varepsilon}(W_{k})].

(A.2)

Moreover,

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}[f_{\varepsilon}(W_{k})]\geq\mathbb{E}[\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f_{\varepsilon}(W_{k})]\geq\mathbb{P}[\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}W_{k}\geq 2\varepsilon]-\mathbb{P}[\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}W_{k}\leq\varepsilon]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varepsilon\to 0}}{{\to}}\mathbb{P}[M_{\infty}>0]-\mathbb{P}[M_{\infty}=0]=1,

where invoked the lower bound on
f_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
and the fact that
\{W_{\infty}>0\}=\{\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}W_{k}>0\}
is an event of probability 1. It follows that for some
\varepsilon>0
,
\eta:=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}[f_{\varepsilon}(W_{k})]>0
. For such
\varepsilon>0
, we conclude that for all
t>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{P}[M_{n}>t]=\mathbb{P}(\tau\leq n)\leq\frac{1}{\eta}\mathbb{P}[W_{n}>t\varepsilon].

Thus, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}[M_{n}]\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon\eta}\mathbb{E}[W_{n}]+1\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon\eta}+1.

Passing to
n\to\infty
, the left hand side converges to
\mathbb{E}[M_{\infty}]
. Thus, this quantity is also finite, as required.
∎

Contact ( + 1 ) (+1) -surgeries and algebraic overtwistedness
Zhengyi Zhou

Abstract
. We show that a contact ( + 1 ) (+1) -surgery along a Legendrian sphere in a flexibly fillable contact manifold ( c 1 = 0 c_{1}=0 if not subcritical) yields a contact manifold that is algebraically overtwisted if the Legendrian’s homology class is not annihilated in the filling. Our construction can also be implemented in more general contact manifolds yielding algebraically overtwisted manifolds through ( + 1 ) (+1) -surgeries. This gives new proof of the vanishing of contact homology for overtwisted contact manifolds. Our result can be viewed as the symplectic field theory analog in any dimension of the vanishing of contact Ozsváth-Szabó invariant for ( + 1 ) (+1) -surgeries on two-component Legendrian links proved by Ding, Li, and Wu [ 21 ] .

4.4.
Infinite non-loose Legendrians

By the work of Lazarev
[
34
]
, any Weinstein structure on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
can be obtained as attaching a critical handle to a Legendrian sphere in
(\partial(T^{*}S^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{D}}),\xi_{\rm std})\simeq S^{n-1}\times S^{n}
, where the embedding of the Legendrian is in the same homotopy class as
S^{n-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\to}}S^{n-1}\times\{p\}\subset S^{n-1}\times S^{n}
. When the Weinstein structure is standard, the attaching Legendrian is the loose one
\Lambda_{loose}
, i.e. the Legendrian lift of the Lagrangian zero section in
T^{*}S^{n-1}
.

A formal Legendrian submanifold of a contact manifold
(Y^{2n-1},\xi)
consists of an embedding of a
n-1
dimensional manifold
f:\Lambda\subset Y
and a family of injective bundle maps
F_{s}:T\Lambda\to f^{*}TY
for
s\in[0,1]
, such that
F_{0}={\rm d}f
and
F_{1}
’s images are Lagrangian subspace of
f^{*}\xi
w.r.t. the natural conformal symplectic structure, or equivalently (up to homotopy) a totally real subspace for a fixed almost complex structure on
\xi
compatible with the conformal symplectic structure. Formal Legendrian isotopy is an isotopy of those data. Given two Legendrian embeddings
f_{0},f_{1}:\Lambda\to Y
, if they are smoothly isotopic by
f_{s}
, then the Legendrian embedding
f_{1}
is formally Legendrian isotopic to
(f_{0},F_{s})
, where
F_{s}=P_{s}{\rm d}f_{s}
, where
P_{s}
is the parallel transportation from
f_{s}(p)
back to
f_{0}(p)
using an auxiliary complex connection on
\xi
. Therefore
F_{1}
and
{\rm d}f_{0}
are differed by a section
s
of the bundle
U(f_{0}^{*}\xi)
of unitary automorphisms. If this section is homotopic to the identity section, then
(f_{0},F_{s})
, hence
(f_{1},{\rm d}f_{1})
, is formally Legendrian isotopic to
(f_{0},{\rm d}f_{0})
. Now we can consider the situation of Legendrian spheres
f_{0},f_{1}:S^{n-1}\to Y
, then
f_{0}^{*}\xi
is isomorphic to
i^{*}TT^{*}S^{n-1}
for the zero section
i:S^{n-1}\to T^{*}S^{n-1}
, i.e. the complexification
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}S^{n-1}
of
TS^{n}
. Note that
T_{{\mathbb{C}}}S^{n-1}\oplus\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}\simeq\underline{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}
, we get a inclusion of
U(T_{{\mathbb{C}}}S^{n-1})
to a trivial
U(n)
bundle. Since
U(n-1)\subset U(n)
is isomorphic on the
2n-2
skeleton, whether
s
is homotopic to identity in
U(T_{{\mathbb{C}}}S^{n-1})
is the same as whether
s
is homotopic to identity in the trivial
U(n)
bundle whenever
n-1<2n-2
, i.e.
n>1
. Finally, such a section is always homotopic to identity by Bott periodicity as
\pi_{n-1}(U(n))=0
for
n>1
odd.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be two different Weinstein structures on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
that are different from the standard one. Then the corresponding attaching Legendrian spheres are non-loose, not Legendrian isotopic, smoothly isotopic, and formally Legendrian isotopic when
n
is odd.

Proof.

If the attaching Legendrian is loose, then the resulting Weinstein structure must be the standard Weinstein structure by
[
16
]
. As they are different Weinstein structures, the attaching Legendrian can not be Legendrian isotopic. Finally, applying the smooth Whitney trick, one sees that the attaching Legendrian is smoothly isotopic to
\Lambda_{loose}
. And by the discussion above it is formally Legendrian isotopic to
\Lambda_{loose}
if
n
is odd.
∎

In view of the proposition above,
Proposition

1.8
follows from the existence of infinitely many exotic Weinstein structures on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. Such structures were first found by Seidel and Smith
[
46
]
for
n
even, then infinitely many for
n
even by McLean
[
37
]
. The most flexible and efficient way of constructing infinite such exotic structures for any
n\geq 3
was due to Abouzaid and Seidel
[
1
]
. Their exoticity and differences are both illustrated using symplectic cohomology.

Liouville classification of integrable geodesic flows on a projective plane in a potential field
Antonov E. I, Kozlov I. K

Abstract
A Liouville classification of a natural Hamiltonian system on the projective plane with a rotation metric and a linear integral is obtained. All Fomenko–Zieschang invariants (i.e., labeled molecules) of the system are calculated.



Key words: integrable Hamiltonian systems, geodesic flow, labeled molecule, Fomenko–Zieschang invariant.

1.1
Linearly integrable geodesic flows on
S^{2}

Consider a Riemannian manifold of revolution
M=S^{2}
with natural coordinates
(r;\varphi),r\in(0;L),\varphi\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, in which the rotation metric is written as

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\mathrm{d}r^{2}+f^{2}(r)\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2}.

In a neighbourhood of the poles consider local coordinates

x=f(r)\cos{\varphi},\qquad y=f(r)\sin{\varphi}.

The function
f(r)
satisfies the conditions of the following lemma, therefore the metric at the poles is
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2}
.

Lemma 1

(A. Besse,
[
4
]
)
.

A metric on a manifold of revolution
M
and a function
V(r)
invariant under rotations are smooth at the poles (that is at the points
r=0
and
r=L
) if there exist
F=F(r)
and
W=W(r)
defined on the whole number line such that
F|_{\left[0;L\right]}=f,W|_{\left[0;L\right]}=V
and the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

F(-r)=-F(r)=F(2L-r)
, that is the functions
F(r)
and
F(L+r)
are odd (or, equivalently, the function
F(r)

is periodic with a period
2L
and odd) and
F^{\prime}(0)=1,F^{\prime}(L)=-1
;

2.

W(-r)=W(r)=W(2L-r)
, that is the functions
W(r)
and
W(L+r)
are even (or, equivalently, the function
W(r)
is periodic with a period
2L
and even).

Consider an integrable Hamiltonian system on the cotangent bundle
T^{*}S^{2}
with Hamiltonian

H=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{p^{2}_{r}}{2}+\frac{p^{2}_{\varphi}}{2f^{2}(r)}+V(r),\qquad&\text{outside the poles (i.e. $r\not=0,L$),}\\
\displaystyle\frac{p^{2}_{x}+p^{2}_{y}}{2}+V(0),\qquad&\text{at the poles (i.e. $r=0$ or $L$)}\end{cases}

(1)

and first integral

K=\begin{cases}p_{\varphi},\qquad&\text{outside the poles,}\\
xp_{y}-yp_{x},\qquad&\text{near the poles.}\end{cases}

(2)

Remark 4
.

Some properties of the function
U_{h}(r)
:

1.

It follows from the symmetries of the functions

that the function

is symmetric about the axis

. In other words, the function
U_{h}(r-\frac{L}{2})

2.

At the poles

3.

For

if and only if

.

4.

Also note that for

and

the zeros of

cannot be points of the local extremum of the function

. Otherwise, at these points

and
\frac{\partial H}{\partial r}=0

1
).

It is easy to prove the following statement about the topology of

\pi:Q^{3}_{h}\to\mathbb{RP}^{2}

\pi(Q^{3}_{h})

. The set
\pi(Q^{3}_{h})

\mathbb{RP}^{2}

Proposition 1
.

Let
Q^{3}
be the connected component of
Q^{3}_{h}
of a nonsingular isoenergy surface for the system under consideration on
\mathbb{RP}^{2}

1.

If
\pi(Q^{3})=\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, that is

on the whole
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, then
Q^{3}\approx L_{4,1}
.

2.

If
\pi(Q^{3})
is a disk

centered at the pole, then
Q^{3}\approx S^{3}
.

3.

If
\pi(Q^{3})
is a ring

or a Mobius strip

, then
Q^{3}\approx S^{1}\times S^{2}
.

REPRESENTATION OF REAL SOLVABLE LIE ALGEBRAS HAVING 2-DIMENSIONAL DERIVED IDEAL AND GEOMETRY OF COADJOINT ORBITS OF CORRESPONDING LIE GROUPS
TU T. C. NGUYEN, VU A. LE

Abstract
Let Lie ⁡ ( n , k ) \mathrm{Lie}\left(n,k\right) be the class of all n n -dimensional real solvable Lie algebras having k k -dimensional derived ideals. In 2020 the authors et al. gave a classification of all non 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras of Lie ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right) . We propose in this paper to study representations of these Lie algebras as well as their corresponding connected and simply connected Lie groups. That is, for each algebra, we give an upper bound of the minimal degree of a faithful representation. Then, we give a geometrical description of coadjoint orbits of corresponding groups. Moreover, we show that the characteristic property of the family of maximal dimensional coadjoint orbits of a MD-group studied by K. P. Shum and the second author et al. is still true for the Lie groups considered here. Namely, we prove that, for each considered group, the family of the maximal dimensional coadjoint orbits forms a measurable foliation in the sense of Connes. The topological classification of these foliations is also provided.

Introduction

The problem of classifying all solvable Lie algebras in an arbitrarily large finite dimension is presently unsolved and is generally believed to be unsolvable. All known full classifications terminate at relatively low dimensions or contain a certain specific given property such as classifying solvable Lie algebras with low-dimensional derived ideal introduced immediately below.

Denote by
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,k\right)
the class of all
n
-dimensional real solvable Lie algebras having
k
-dimensional derived ideals. Several classifications of
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,k\right)
with
k\in\{1,2\}
have been investigated. More precisely, a complete classification of
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,1\right)
and a partial classification of
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)
were given by Schöbel
[
22
]
in 1993. Eberlein
[
6
]
in 2003 gave a formal classification of 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras including 2-step nilpotent ones in
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)
. Janisse
[
11
]
in 2010 gave a partial classification of
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)
over an algebraically closed field. In 2020, the authors et al.
[
18
]
presented a new approach to the problem of classifying
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)
and gave a list of non 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras in
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)
.

This paper is concerned with the issue of studying representations of Lie algebras in
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)
and can be considered as a continuation of
[
18
]
.

As is well known, the Ado-Iwasawa theorem
[
1
,
10
]
asserts that every finite-dimensional Lie algebra has a faithful representation of finite degree. It is well known that the degree of a faithful representation can be arbitrarily large. It can not be arbitrarily small however. Concretely, it is bounded from below by the square root of the dimension of the Lie algebra. Denote by
\mu(\mathcal{G})
the minimal degree of a faithful representation of a Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}
. In general, it is very difficult to determine
\mu(\mathcal{G})
and it is also hard to obtain suitable estimations for
\mu(\mathcal{G})
.

On the other hand, the orbit method, which is also known as the Kirillov theory, establishes a correspondence between irreducible unitary representations of a Lie group and its coadjoint orbits (see
[
13
]
). The theory was first introduced by A. A. Kirillov in the early 1960’s and remains a useful and powerful tool in representation theory of Lie groups. It is obvious that coadjoint orbits play an important role in the orbit method. So it is worthwhile to study coadjoint orbits.

We consider in this paper the indecomposable Lie algebras listed in
[
18
]
. Besides constructing faithful representations of small degree for a family of them, we carry out a survey of coadjoint orbits of all corresponding Lie groups. We hope that some beautiful properties of MD-groups studied by K. P. Shum and the second author et al. in
[
19
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
can be generalized for all considered Lie groups of general dimension
n\geq 5
.

The main results of the paper are as follows. First, we give in Theorem
3.2
the upper bound of
\mu(\mathcal{G})
for each Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}
under consideration. After that, we describe in Theorem
3.10
the geometry of all coadjoint orbits of connected and simply connected Lie groups corresponding to these algebras. Next, Theorem
3.13
shows that, for each considered group of general dimension
n\geq 5
, the family of the maximal dimensional coadjoint orbits forms a measurable foliation in the sense of Connes in
[
4
]
. Finally, the topological classification of these foliations is provided in Theorem
3.15
and their Connes’
C^{*}
-algebras are described in Corrolary
3.17
.

The paper is organized into four sections including this introduction. Section
2
recalls some notions and useful results. All main results are presented and proved in Section
3
. Finally, Section
4
is some concluding remarks.

Representations and

-orbits

In this subsection, we recall some notions and well-known results about representations which will be used later. For more details, we refer the reader to the references

12

13
]

Definition 2.1
.

A
linear representation
(or

) of

\rho:\mathcal{G}\to

\mathrm{gl}(V),\,X(\in\mathcal{G})\mapsto\rho_{X}\in\mathrm{gl}(V)

Definition 2.2
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}
, then
\mu(\mathcal{G})
is defined to be the minimal dimension of
V
such that
\mathcal{G}
admits a faithful representation on
V
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is easy to check the following assertions.

{romanlist}
[(ii)]

If
\rho:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{gl}(V)
and
\rho^{\prime}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{gl}(V^{\prime})
are two representations of
\mathcal{G}
, then the direct sum
\rho\oplus\rho^{\prime}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{gl}(V\oplus V^{\prime})
defined by

(\rho\oplus\rho^{\prime})_{X}(v,v^{\prime})=(\rho_{X}(v),\rho^{\prime}_{X}(v^{\prime}))\quad\mbox{for all }X\in\mathcal{G};\,v\in V;\,v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}

is also a representation of
\mathcal{G}
.

If
\rho
is a finite-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{G}
and faithful on the centre of
\mathcal{G}
, then the direct sum of
\rho
and adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}
is faithful on
\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathrm{ad}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{gl}(\mathcal{G}),\,X\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{X}
such that

\mathrm{ad}_{X}(Y):=[X,Y]\quad\mbox{for all }Y\in\mathcal{G}.

For every
g\in G
, we denote the internal automorphism associated with

by

, i.e.

A_{g}(x):=g.x.g^{-1}
for all
x\in G.
By taking the derivative at the identity, this automorphism induces the map
{A_{g}}_{*}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
defined as follows

{A_{g}}_{*}(X):=\frac{d}{{dt}}\left[{g.\exp\left({tX}\right).g^{-1}}\right]\left|{{}_{t=0}}\right.\quad\mbox{for all }X\in\mathcal{G}.

This map is called
tangent map
of
A_{g}
.

Definition 2.4
.

{romanlist}

[(ii)]

The action

\mathrm{Ad}:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}),\,g\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}_{g}:={A_{g}}_{*}\quad\mbox{for all }g\in G

is called the
adjoint representation
of
G
in
\mathcal{G}
.

The coadjoint representation (or
\mathrm{K}
-representation) of
G
in
\mathcal{G}^{*}
is the action

\mathrm{K}:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{G}^{*}),\,g\mapsto\mathrm{K}_{g}

such that

\left\langle\mathrm{K}_{g}(F),X\right\rangle:=\left\langle F,\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(X)\right\rangle\quad\mbox{for all }g\in G;F\in\mathcal{G}^{*};X\in\mathcal{G}.

Definition 2.5
.

The
coadjoint orbit
(or
\mathrm{K}
-orbit
) of
G
through any
F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
is defined to be

\Omega_{F}=\{\mathrm{K}_{g}(F):g\in G\}.

The description of
\mathrm{K}
-orbits of a given Lie group is very interesting. In 1990, the second author introduced a method of describing based on the structure of Lie algebra (see
[
14
]
). Namely, for each
F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
, we define

\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})=\left\{F_{X}:X\in\mathcal{G}\right\}

in which
F_{X}\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
is defined by

\langle F_{X},Y\rangle=\langle F,\exp(\mathrm{ad}_{X})(Y)\rangle\quad\mbox{for all }Y\in\mathcal{G}.

Obviously, we always have
\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})\subseteq\Omega_{F}

for all
F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
. First, we describe the results on
\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})
. Then, we show some sufficient conditions to obtain the equality
\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})=\Omega_{F}
.

Recall that a Lie group
G
is called to be
exponential
, if and only if the exponential map
\exp:\mathcal{G}\rightarrow G
is diffeomorphic.

Proposition 2.6

(see
[
2
]
)
.

The Lie group
G
is exponential if and only if for each
X\in\mathcal{G}
the operator
\mathrm{ad}_{X}
has no purely imaginary eigenvalues.

Proposition 2.7

(see
[
14
]
)
.

If
G
is exponential then
\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})=\Omega_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
.

Proposition 2.8

(see
[
14
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected Lie group. Assume that
\{\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})\}_{F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}}
form a partition of
\mathcal{G}^{*}
and for all
F^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F}
the sets
\Omega_{F^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G})
are all open (or closed). Then we have
\Omega_{F}(\mathcal{G})=\Omega_{F}
for all
F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\rho
be a finite-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{G}
. Then the
character

\chi_{\rho}
of
\mathcal{G}
afforded by
\rho
is the function given by

\chi_{\rho}(X):=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\exp(\rho(X))\right)\quad\mbox{for all }X\in\mathcal{G},

where

2.2
Foliations and measurable foliations

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold (
0<n\in\mathbb{N}
). We always denote its tangent bundle by
TV
, the tangent space of
V
at
x\in V
by
T_{x}V
. Let us recall some notions in the theory of foliations. For more details, we refer the reader to the reference
[
4
]
of Connes.

Definition 2.10
.

A smooth subbundle
\mathcal{F}
of
TV
is called integrable if and only if every
x\in V
is contained in a submanifold
W
of
V
such that
T_{p}W=\mathcal{F}_{p};\forall p\in W
.

Definition 2.11
.

A foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
is defined by a smooth manifold
V
and an integrable subbundle
\mathcal{F}
of
TV
. Then,
V
is called the foliated manifold and
\mathcal{F}
is called the subbundle defining the foliation. The dimension of
\mathcal{F}
is also called the dimension of the foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
and
n-\dim\mathcal{F}
is called the codimension of the foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
in
V
. Each maximal connected submanifold
L
of
V
such that
T_{x}L=\mathcal{F}_{x}

(\forall x\in L)
is called a leaf of the foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
.

Each
x\in V
has always a system of local coordinates
\{U;x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
such that
x\in U
and for any leaf
L
with
L\cap U\neq\emptyset
, each connected component of
L\cap U
(which is called a
plaque
of the leaf
L
) is given by the equations

x_{k+1}=c_{1},\ldots,x_{n}=c_{n-k},

where
k=\dim\mathcal{F}<n
and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-k}
are suitable constants. Each such system
\{U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is called a
foliation chart
.

A
k
-dimensional foliation can be given by a partition of
V
in a family
\mathcal{C}
of its
k
-dimensional submanifolds (
k\in\mathbb{N},0<k<n
) if there exists an integrable
k
-dimensional subbundle
\mathcal{F}
of
TV
such that each
L\in\mathcal{C}
is a maximal connected integral submanifold of
\mathcal{F}
. In this case,
\mathcal{C}
is the family of leaves of the foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
. Sometimes
\mathcal{C}
is identified with
\mathcal{F}
and we say that
(V,\mathcal{F})
is formed by
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2.12
.

Two foliations
(V_{1},\mathcal{F}_{1})
and
(V_{2},\mathcal{F}_{2})
are said to be topologically equivalent if and only if there exists a homeomorphism
h:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that
h
sends leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
onto those of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. The mapping
h
is called a topological equivalence of considered foliations.

Definition 2.13
.

A submanifold
N
of the foliated manifold
V
is called a transversal if and only if
T_{x}V=T_{x}N\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{x},\,\forall x\in N
. Thus,

\dim N=n-\dim\mathcal{F}=\,\mbox{codim}\mathcal{F}
. A Borel subset
B
of
V
such that
B\cap L
is countable for any leaf
L

is called a Borel transversal to
(V,\mathcal{F})
.

Definition 2.14
.

A transverse measure
\Lambda
for the foliation
(V,\mathcal{F})
is
\sigma
-additive map

B\mapsto\Lambda(B)
from the set of all Borel transversals to [0,
+
\infty
] such that the following conditions are satisfied:

{romanlist}
[(ii)]

If
\psi
:
B_{1}\rightarrow B_{2}
is a Borel bijection and

\psi(x)
is on the leaf of any
x\in B_{1}
, then
\Lambda(B_{1})=\Lambda(B_{2})
.

\Lambda(K)<+\infty
if
K
is any compact subset of a smooth transversal submanifold of
V
.

By a measurable foliation in the sense of Connes
[
4
]
we mean a foliation

(V,\mathcal{F})
equipped with a transverse measure

\Lambda
.

Let
(V,\mathcal{F})
be a foliation with
\mathcal{F}
is
oriented. Then the complement of zero section of the bundle

{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathcal{F})
(
0<k=\dim\mathcal{F}<n
) has two
components
{\Lambda}^{k}{(\mathcal{F})}^{-}
and

{\Lambda}^{k}{(\mathcal{F})}^{+}
.
Let
\mu
be a measure on
V
and
\{U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}

be a foliation chart of
(V,\mathcal{F})
.
Then
U
can be identified with the direct product
N\times{\Pi}

of some smooth transversal submanifolds
N
of
V
and some plaques

\Pi
. The restriction of
\mu
on
U\equiv N\times{\Pi}
becomes
the product
{\mu}_{N}\times{\mu}_{\Pi}
of measures
{\mu}_{N}

and
{\mu}_{\Pi}
respectively.
Let
X\in C^{\infty}{\bigl({\Lambda}^{k}(\mathcal{F})\bigr)}^{+}

be a smooth
k
-vector field and
{\mu}_{X}
be the measure on each leaf
L

determined by the volume element
X
.

Definition 2.15
.

The measure
\mu
is called

X
-invariant if and only if
{\mu}_{X}
is proportional to

{\mu}_{\Pi}
for an arbitrary foliation chart
\{U;x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.

Definition 2.16
.

Let (
X,\mu
), (
Y,\nu
) be two pairs where
X,Y\in C^{\infty}{\bigl({\Lambda}^{k}(\mathcal{F})\bigr)}^{+}
and

are measures on

such that
\mu
is
X
-invariant,
\nu
is

-invariant. Then (

and

for some

Remark 2.17
.

There is a bijection between the set of transverse measures for

(V,\mathcal{F})
and the set of equivalent classes of pairs (

), where

and
\mu
is a

-invariant measure on

is measurable, we only need to choose a suitable pair (

) on

.

3.1
Upper bound for minimal degree of a faithful representation

First, let us consider the indecomposable case. It is easily seen that the centers of

,
\mathcal{G}_{3,2}
,
\mathcal{G}_{3,3(\lambda)}
,

and
\mathcal{G}_{4,4}
are all trivial; the Lie algebras
\mathcal{G}_{4,2}
,
\mathcal{G}_{5+2k}
and

are

\mathcal{G}_{4,1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{6+2k,1}

Remark 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}
belongs to
\{\mathcal{G}_{4,1},\mathcal{G}_{6+2k,1}(k\geq 0)\}
. Then
\mathcal{G}
has a basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
(

Moreover, the center of

The first result in this paper is explicitly constructing faithful representations of small degree for an infinite family of non nilpotent Lie algebras in
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)

Theorem 3.2

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a non 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra in
\mathrm{Lie}\left(n,2\right)

For each group
G\in\{G_{3,1},G_{3,2},G_{3,3(\lambda)},G_{4,1},G_{4,2},G_{4,4}\}
, there are exactly two types of
\mathrm{K}
-orbits: either
0
-dimensional (trivial) or maximal dimensional. They are called MD-groups in term of D. N. Do
[
5
]
. Moreover, the family of maximal dimension of each
G
always forms a measurable foliation in term of Connes
[
4
]
. This foliation is called MD-foliation assocated to
G
. In 1990, the second author studied the topology of MD-foliations assocated to all MD-groups of dimension
4
and desribed the Connes’
C^{*}
-algebras of these MD-foliations (see
[
14
,
15
,
5
]
).

The only
4
-dimensional case that is not an MD-group is the group
G_{4,3}
. However, since its maximal dimensional
\mathrm{K}
-orbits are exactly two half-spaces it is too simple. Therefore, we do not need to study this group further.

In the general case of the dimension
n\geq 5
, three groups
G_{5+2k},G_{6+2k,1}
and
G_{6+2k,2}
are not MD-groups. For them, there are exactly three types of
\mathrm{K}
-orbits: either dimension
0,2
or maximal dimension. Similar to MD-groups, we will show in the next subsection that the family of maximal dimensional
\mathrm{K}
-orbits of each of the groups
G_{5+2k},G_{6+2k,1}
and
G_{6+2k,2}
forms a measurable foliation.

3.3
Foliations formed by the maximal dimensional
\mathrm{K}
-orbits

We will establish in this subsection the remaining new results of the paper on the foliations formed by the maximal dimensional
\mathrm{K}
-orbits of each Lie group
G
of genenral dimension, i.e.
G\in\{G_{5+2k},G_{6+2k,1},G_{6+2k,2}\}
.

Theorem 3.13
.

For any group
G\in\{G_{5+2k},G_{6+2k,1},G_{6+2k,2}\}
, assume that
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is the family of all maximal dimensional
\mathrm{K}
-orbits of
G
and
V_{G}:=\cup\{\Omega:\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}
. Then
V_{G}
is an open submanifold of
\mathcal{G}^{*}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}
forms a measurable foliation of dimension
(4+2k)
on
V_{G}
in the sense of Connes
[
4
]
and this foliation
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is called the general MD-foliation (GMD-foliation, for short) associated to
G
.

Proof 3.14
.

The proof is analogous to the case of MD-groups in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. Recall that
4+2k
is always the maximal dimension of each
\mathrm{K}
-orbit of the family
\mathcal{F}_{G}
. For any
G
in the considered set, we first need build a suitable differential system
S_{G}
of rank
4+2k
on the manifold
V_{G}
such that each
\mathrm{K}
-orbit
\Omega
from
{\mathcal{F}}_{G}
is a maximal connected integrable submanifold corresponding to this system. As the next step, we have to show that the Lebegues measure is invariant for a smooth polyvector field
\mathfrak{X}
of degree
4+2k
such that it generates
S_{G}
.

Step 1: Prove that
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is a foliation for each
G\in\{G_{5+2k},G_{6+2k,1},G_{6+2k,2}\}

Firstly, we consider the simplest case
G=G_{5+2k}
. Recall that for any
F(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
(
n=5+2k
), by Item (
3
) of Theorem
3.10
, the
\mathrm{K}
-orbits
\Omega_{F}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{G}
if and only if
f_{2}\neq 0
, namely,
\Omega_{F}
is a hyperplane as follows

\Omega_{F}=\{X^{*}:x^{*}_{2}=f_{2},\,x^{*}_{1},\,x^{*}_{3},\,x^{*}_{4},\,x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};\,\forall j=5,\ldots,5+2k\}.

(2)

It is clear that
V_{G}=\cup\{\Omega:\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}\equiv\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
and it is open submanifold of
\mathcal{G}^{*}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, the dimension of submanifold
V_{G}
is exaxlty
\dim\mathcal{G}^{*}=n=5+2k
. On

V_{G}
we consider the differential system

S_{5+2k}:=\{\mathfrak{X}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{1}},\,\mathfrak{X}_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{j}};\,j=3,4,5,\ldots,5+2k\}

contained
4+2k
vector fields. Obviously,
\operatorname{rank}(S_{5+2k})=4+2k=n-1
and
\mathfrak{X}_{1},\,\mathfrak{X}_{j}
are smooth over
V_{G}
,
j=3,4,5,\ldots 5+2k
. Because each
\Omega
from
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is a hyperplane given by Eq. (
2
) so it is obvious a maximal connected integrable submanifold corresponding to
S_{5+2k}
. In other words,
S_{5+2k}
generates
\mathcal{F}_{G}
and
(V_{G},\,\mathcal{F}_{G})
is a
(4+2k)
-dimensional foliation for the case
G=G_{5+2k}
.

Nextly, we consider the case
G=G_{6+2k,1}
(i.e.
n=6+2k
). For any
F(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
, by Item (
4b
) of Theorem
3.10
, the
\mathrm{K}
-orbits
\Omega_{F}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{G}
if and only if
f_{2}\neq 0
. Furthermore, we have

\Omega_{F}=\{X^{*}:x^{*}_{2}=f_{2},\,x^{*}_{1}e^{-\frac{x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{2}}}=f_{1}e^{-\frac{f_{4}}{f_{2}}},\,x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};\,j=3,5,\ldots,6+2k\}.

(3)

It is clear that
V_{G}=\cup\{\Omega:\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}\equiv\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
and it is open submanifold of
\mathcal{G}^{*}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
. On the open submanifold

V_{G}

we consider the differential system
S_{6+2k,1}:=\{\mathfrak{X}_{1},\,\mathfrak{X}_{3},\,\mathfrak{X}_{j};\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k\}
contained
4+2k
vector fields as follows

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{X}_{1}:=x^{*}_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{1}}+x^{*}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}},\,\mathfrak{X}_{3}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}},\\
\mathfrak{X}_{j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{j}};\;j=5,\ldots,6+2k.\end{cases}

Obviously,
\operatorname{rank}(S_{6+2k,1})=4+2k=n-2
and
\mathfrak{X}_{1},\,\mathfrak{X}_{3},\,\mathfrak{X}_{j}
are smooth over
V_{G}
,
j=5,\ldots 6+2k
. We will show that
S_{6+2k,1}
produces
\mathcal{F}_{G}
, i.e. show that each
\mathrm{K}
-orbit
\Omega
from
{\mathcal{F}}_{G}
is a maximal connected integrable submanifold corresponding to
S_{6+2k,1}
.

Now we consider vector fields

\mathfrak{X}_{3}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{j}}
(
j=5,\ldots,6+2k
). Clearly, the flows (i.e. one-parameter subgroups) of them are determined as follows

\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{x^{*}_{3}-f_{3}}:F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{x^{*}_{3}-f_{3}}(F):=(f_{1},\,f_{2},\,x^{*}_{3},\,f_{4},\,f_{5},\ldots,\,f_{6+2k}),

\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{j}}_{x^{*}_{j}-f_{j}}:F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{j}}_{x^{*}_{j}-f_{j}}(F):=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{j-1},\,x^{*}_{j},\,f_{j+1},\ldots,f_{6+2k});\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k.

Next, we consider
\mathfrak{X}_{1}:=x^{*}_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{1}}+x^{*}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}}
. Assume that

\varphi:s\mapsto\varphi(s)=\bigl(x^{*}_{1}(s),\,\ldots,\,x^{*}_{6+2k}(s),\bigr);s\in\mathbb{R}

is an integral curve of
\mathfrak{X}_{1}
passing through
F=\varphi(0)
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(s)={\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{\varphi(s)}\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{6+2k}x^{*^{\prime}}_{j}(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{i}}=x^{*}_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{1}}+x^{*}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x^{*^{\prime}}_{2}(s)=0,\,x^{*^{\prime}}_{3}(s)=0,\,x^{*^{\prime}}_{j}(s)=0;j=5,\ldots,6+2k\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{1}(s)=x^{*}_{1}(s)\\
x^{*^{\prime}}_{4}(s)=x^{*}_{2}(s)\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x^{*}_{2},x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{j}\,\mbox{ are constant functions},\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k\\
x^{*}_{1}(s)=constant\times e^{s}\\
x^{*}_{4}(s)=x^{*}_{2}s+constant.\end{cases}

(4)

Combining with condition
F=\varphi(0)
, Eq. (
4
) gives us

x^{*}_{1}=f_{1}e^{s},\,x^{*}_{4}=x^{*}_{2}s+f_{4},\,x^{*}_{3}=f_{3},\,x^{*}_{j}=f_{j};\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k.

Therefore, the flow of
\mathfrak{X}_{1}
is

\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-f_{1}}:F\mapsto\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-f_{1}}(F):=(f_{1}e^{s},\,f_{2},\,f_{3},\,f_{2}s+f_{4},\,f_{5},\dots,\,f_{6+2k}).

By setting
\theta=\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6+2k}}_{x^{*}_{6+2k}-f_{6+2k}}\circ\ldots\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}_{5}-f_{5}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{x^{*}_{3}-f_{3}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-f_{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\theta(F)=

\displaystyle\,\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{6+2k}}_{x^{*}_{6+2k}-f_{6+2k}}\circ\ldots\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{5}}_{x^{*}_{5}-f_{5}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{3}}_{x^{*}_{3}-f_{3}}\circ\theta^{\mathfrak{X}_{1}}_{x^{*}_{1}-f_{1}}(F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,X^{*}(x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2},x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{4},x^{*}_{5},\ldots,x^{*}_{6+2k})

where
x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k
and

x^{*}_{1}=f_{1}e^{s},\,x^{*}_{2}=f_{2},\,x^{*}_{4}=f_{2}s+f_{4};s\in\mathbb{R}.

Removing the parameters we get

\{\theta(F)=X^{*}:x^{*}_{2}=f_{2},\,x^{*}_{1}e^{-\frac{x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{2}}}=f_{1}e^{-\frac{f_{4}}{f_{2}}},\,x^{*}_{3},x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k\}\equiv\Omega_{F}.

In other words,
\Omega_{F}
is a maximal connected integrable submanifold corresponding to
S_{6+2k,1}
. Therefore
S_{6+2k,1}
generates
\mathcal{F}_{G}
and
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is a
(4+2k)
-dimensional foliation for the case
G=G_{6+2k,1}
.

Finally, we consider the case
G=G_{6+2k,2}
(i.e.
n=6+2k
). For any
F(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
, by Item (
4b
) of Theorem
3.10
, the
\mathrm{K}
-orbits
\Omega_{F}
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{G}
if and only if
f_{2}\neq 0
. Furthermore, we have

\Omega_{F}=\{X^{*}:x^{*}_{2}=f_{2},\,{x^{*}_{1}}^{2}-2x^{*}_{2}x^{*}_{4}={f_{1}}^{2}-2f_{2}f_{4};\\
x^{*}_{3},\,x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=5,\ldots,6+2k\}.

Of course,
V_{G}=\cup\{\Omega:\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{G}\}\equiv\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
and it is open submanifold of
\mathcal{G}^{*}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
. On the open submanifold

V_{G}

we consider the differential system
S_{6+2k,2}:=\{\mathfrak{X}_{1},\,\mathfrak{X}_{3},\,\mathfrak{X}_{j};\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k\}
contained
4+2k
vector fields as follows

\begin{cases}\mathfrak{X}_{1}:=x^{*}_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{1}}+x^{*}_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{4}},\,\mathfrak{X}_{3}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{3}},\\
\mathfrak{X}_{j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{*}_{j}};\;j=5,\ldots,6+2k.\end{cases}

By calculations quite similar to the case of group
G_{6+2k,1}
, we can verify that
\operatorname{rank}(S_{6+2k,1})
is equal to
4+2k=n-2
and
S_{6+2k,2}
produces
\mathcal{F}_{G}
. Therefore
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is a foliation on the open submanifold
V_{G}
and the proof of the first step is complete.

Step 2: Prove that the foliation
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is measurable in the sense of Connes for any
G
from
G_{5+2k},\,G_{6+2k,1},\,G_{6+2k,2}

We now turn to the second step of the proof. Namely, we have to show that the foliation
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
is measurable in the sense of Connes. By Remark
2.17
, we only need to choose a suitable pair (
\mathfrak{X}_{G},\mu_{G}
) on
V_{G}
where
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
is a smooth polyvector field of degree
4+2k
defined on
V_{G}
,
\mu_{G}
is a measure on
V_{G}
such that
\mathfrak{X}
generates
S_{G}
and
\mu_{G}
is
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
-invariant.
We choose
\mu_{G}
to be exactly the Lebegues measure on
V_{G}
for any
G\in\{G_{5+2k},\,G_{6+2k,1},\,G_{6+2k,2}\}
.
For
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
, we set

\mathfrak{X}_{G}:=\begin{cases}\mathfrak{X}_{1}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{3}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{4}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{5}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{5+2k}\qquad{\mbox{if}}\;\;G=G_{5+2k},\\
\mathfrak{X}_{1}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{3}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{5}\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{6}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathfrak{X}_{6+2k}\qquad{\mbox{if}}\;\;G\in\{G_{6+2k,1},\,G_{6+2k,2}\}.\end{cases}

For any
G\in\{G_{5+2k},\,G_{6+2k,1},\,G_{6+2k,2}\}
, it is clear that
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
is a polyvector field of degree
4+2k
, smooth, non-zero everywhere on
V_{G}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
generates
S_{G}
. That is, when choosing on
(V_{G},\mathcal{F}_{G})
a suitable orientation, then
\mathfrak{X}_{G}\in C^{\infty}{\bigl({\Lambda}^{4+2k}(\mathcal{F})\bigr)}^{+}
. It is obvious that the invariance of the Lebegues measure
\mu_{G}
with respect to
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
is equivalent to the invariance of
\mu_{G}
for the
\mathrm{K}
-representation that is restricted to the foliated submanifold
V_{G}
in
\mathcal{G}^{*}
. For any
X(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{G}
, direct computation show that Jacobi’s determinant

J_{X}
of differential mapping
\mathrm{K}_{\exp(X)}
is a constant which depends only on
X
but do not depend on the coordinates of any point which moves in each
\mathrm{K}
-orbit
\Omega\in\mathcal{F}_{G}
. In other words, the Lebegues measure
\mu_{G}
is
\mathfrak{X}_{G}
-invariant. The proof is complete.

Theorem 3.15

(Description and classification of topological types of GMD-foliations)
.

The topology of GMD-foliations in Theorem
3.13
has the following properties.

{romanlist}
[(ii)]

Three GMD-foliations
(V_{G_{5+2k}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{5+2k}})
,
(V_{G_{6+2k,1}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,1}})
and
(V_{G_{6+2k,2}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
determine exactly two types of topology. The single foliation
(V_{G_{5+2k}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{5+2k}})
gives the first type and we denote it by
\mathcal{F}_{5+2k}
. Two remaining foliations
(V_{G_{6+2k,1}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,1}}),(V_{G_{6+2k,2}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
are topologically equivalent and they give the second type which is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{6+2k}
.

The type
\mathcal{F}_{5+2k}
is a trivial fibration with connected and simply connected fibers on
\mathbb{R}^{*}:=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

The type
\mathcal{F}_{6+2k}
is a trivial fibration with connected and simply connected fibers on
\mathbb{R}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof 3.16
.

We now prove three assertions of the theorem.

{itemlist}

Firstly, we prove Item (i) of the theorem. It is obvious that
(V_{G_{5+2k}},{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{5+2k}})
is not topologically equivalent to any foliation from
\{(V_{G_{6+2k,1}},{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{6+2k,1}})
,
(V_{G_{6+2k,2}},{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{6+2k,2}})\}
because
V_{G_{5+2k}}
is
(5+2k)
-dimensional manifold while both of
V_{G_{6+2k,1}}
and
V_{G_{6+2k,2}}
are
(6+2k)
-dimensional manifolds.

Now we will prove that two foliations
\{(V_{G_{6+2k,1}},{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{6+2k,1}}),\,(V_{G_{6+2k,2}},{\mathcal{F}}_{G_{6+2k,2}})\}
are topologically equivalent. Note that

V_{G_{6+2k,1}}\equiv V_{G_{6+2k,2}}\equiv\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{4+2k}

and we will denote both of them by
V_{6+2k}
for convenience. By Definition
2.12
, to prove the topological equivalence of two foliations which are given on the same foliated manifold
V_{6+2k}
, we need to find a homeomorphism
h
of
V_{6+2k}
such that
h
sends leaves of the first foliation onto those of the second one.

Let
h:V_{6+2k}\rightarrow V_{6+2k},\,X^{*}(x^{*}_{1},\ldots,x^{*}_{6+2k})\mapsto h(X^{*}):=\widetilde{X^{*}}(\widetilde{x^{*}_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{x^{*}_{6+2k}})
which is defined as follows:

\begin{cases}\widetilde{x^{*}_{1}}:=x^{*}_{1}+x^{*}_{4},\,\widetilde{x^{*}_{2}}:=x^{*}_{2},\,\widetilde{x^{*}_{4}}:=\frac{1}{2x^{*}_{2}}\Bigl[{\bigl(x^{*}_{1}+x^{*}_{4}\bigr)}^{2}-x^{*}_{1}e^{-\frac{x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{2}}}\Bigr],\\
\widetilde{x^{*}_{3}}:=x^{*}_{3},\,\widetilde{x^{*}_{j}}:=x^{*}_{j};\,j=5,\ldots,6+2k.\end{cases}

It is clear that the considered map
h
is homeomorphic. Now, we take an arbitrary leaf
L
of
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,1}})
. In fact,
L
is determined as follows

L=\{X^{*}\in V_{6+2k}:x^{*}_{2}=c_{2},\,x^{*}_{1}e^{-\frac{x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{2}}}=c_{1};\,x^{*}_{3},\,x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};j=5,\ldots,6+2k\}

where
c_{1},c_{2}
are some real constants,
c_{2}\neq 0
. For
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
, we consider the leaf
\widetilde{L}
determined as follows

\widetilde{L}=\{\widetilde{X^{*}}\in V_{6+2k}:\,\widetilde{x}^{*}_{2}=c_{2},\,{\widetilde{x}^{*}_{1}}{}^{2}-2\widetilde{x}^{*}_{2}\widetilde{x}^{*}_{4}=c_{1};\,\widetilde{x}^{*}_{3},\,\widetilde{x}^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};j=5,\ldots,6+2k\}.

Now we look back at the homeomorphism
h:V_{6+2k}\to V_{6+2k}
. It is plain that

\displaystyle X^{*}\in L\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\,\,x^{*}_{2}=c_{2},\,x^{*}_{1}e^{-\frac{x^{*}_{4}}{x^{*}_{2}}}=c_{1},\,x^{*}_{3},\,x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};j=5,\ldots,6+2k

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\,\,\widetilde{x}^{*}_{2}=c_{2},\,{\widetilde{x}^{*}_{1}}{}^{2}-2\widetilde{x}^{*}_{2}\widetilde{x}^{*}_{4}=c_{1},\,\widetilde{x}^{*}_{3},\,\widetilde{x}^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};j=5,\ldots,6+2k

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\,\,h_{1}(X^{*})=\widetilde{X^{*}}\in\widetilde{L}.

In other words,
h(L)=\widetilde{L}
for any
L
of
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,1}})
. Therefore, two foliations
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,1}}),(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
are topological equivalent and they determine the second type
\mathcal{F}_{6+2k}
. Item (i) of the theorem is proven.

Now we prove Item (ii) of Theorem
3.15
. Note that all leaves of GMD-foliation
(V_{G_{5+2k}},\mathcal{F}_{G_{5+2k}})
are hyperplanes of the form

\{X^{*}:x^{*}_{2}=c;x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{,}x^{*}_{4},x^{*}_{j}\in\mathbb{R};j=5,\ldots,5+2k\}

parameterized by the non-zero constant
x^{*}_{2}=c\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
. Therefore the foliation of the first type
\mathcal{F}_{5+2k}
come from the following fibration

p_{5+2k}:V_{G_{5+2k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*};\,X^{*}(x^{*}_{1},\,x^{*}_{2},\,\ldots,\,x^{*}_{5+2k})\mapsto p_{5+2k}(X^{*}):=x^{*}_{2}.

Finally, we prove Item (iii) of Theorem
3.15
for the second type
\mathcal{F}_{6+2k}
. Of course, we only need to consider the foliation
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
instead of both ones of this type.

Let
p_{6+2k}:V_{6+2k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
be the map that defines as follows

X^{*}(x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2},\ldots,x^{*}_{6+2k})\mapsto p_{6+2k}(X^{*}):=(x^{*}_{2},\,{x^{*}_{1}}^{2}-2x^{*}_{2}x^{*}_{4}).

It can be verified that
p_{6+2k}
is a submersion and
p_{6+2k}:V_{6+2k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
is a fibration on
\mathbb{R}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
with connected (and simply connected) fibers. Moreover, each leaf of the foliaion
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
is exactly one fiber of this fibration. In other world,
(V_{6+2k},\mathcal{F}_{G_{6+2k,2}})
comes from this fibration.
The proof is complete.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.15
and the result of Connes in
[
4
]
, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.17
.

The Connes’
C^{*}
-algebras of GMD-foliations in Theorem
3.15
are determined as follows

{romanlist}
[(ii)]

C^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{5+2k})\cong C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{*})\otimes\mathcal{K}\cong\bigl(C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\oplus C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\bigr)\otimes\mathcal{K}

C^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{6+2k})\cong C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{*}\times\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{K}\cong\bigl(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\oplus C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\bigr)\otimes\mathcal{K}

where
C_{0}(X)
is the algebra of continuous complex-valued functions defined on an arbitrary locally compact space
X
vanishing at infinity and
\mathcal{K}
denotes the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on an (infinite dimensional) separable Hibert space.

4
Conclusion

In the paper, we have considered all non
2

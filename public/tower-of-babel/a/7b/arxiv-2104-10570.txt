QCSP on Reflexive Tournaments
Benoît Larose, Petar Marković, Barnaby Martin, Daniël Paulusma, Siani Smith, Stanislav Živný

Abstract
We give a complexity dichotomy for the Quantified Constraint Satisfaction Problem QCSP ⁡ ( H ) \mathrm{QCSP}(\mathrm{H}) when H \mathrm{H} is a reflexive tournament. It is well-known that reflexive tournaments can be split into a sequence of strongly connected components H 1 , … , H n \mathrm{H}_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{H}_{n} so that there exists an edge from every vertex of H i H_{i} to every vertex of H j H_{j} if and only if i < j i<j . We prove that if H \mathrm{H} has both its initial and final strongly connected component (possibly equal) of size 1 1 , then QCSP ⁡ ( H ) \mathrm{QCSP}(\mathrm{H}) is in 𝖭𝖫 \mathsf{NL} and otherwise QCSP ⁡ ( H ) \mathrm{QCSP}(\mathrm{H}) is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard.

3.3
The strongly connected case: Generalising the Base Cases

We now generalise the two base cases to more general cases via some recursive procedure. Afterwards we will show how to combine these two cases to complete our proof.
We will first need a slightly generalised version of Lemma

, which nonetheless has virtually the same proof.
For completeness of this article we provide this proof from
[

]
.

Lemma 14

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{H}_{2}\supset\mathrm{H}_{1}\supset H_{0}
be a sequence of strongly connected reflexive tournaments, each one a subtournament of the one before. Suppose that any endomorphism of
\mathrm{H}_{1}
that fixes
\mathrm{H}_{0}
is an automorphism. Then any endomorphism
h
of
\mathrm{H}_{2}
that maps
\mathrm{H}_{0}
to an isomorphic copy
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}=i(\mathrm{H}_{0})
of itself also gives an isomorphic copy of
\mathrm{H}_{1}
in
h(\mathrm{H}_{1})
.

Proof.

For contradiction, suppose there is an endomorphism
h
of
\mathrm{H}_{2}
that maps
\mathrm{H}_{0}
to an isomorphic copy
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}=i(\mathrm{H}_{0})
of itself that does not yield an isomorphic copy of
\mathrm{H}_{1}
. In particular,
|h(\mathrm{H}_{1})|<|V(\mathrm{H}_{1})|
. We proceed as in the proof of the Lemma
12
. Choose
h^{-1}
in the following fashion. We let
h^{-1}
of
h(\mathrm{H}_{0})
be the natural isomorphism of
h(\mathrm{H}_{0})
to
\mathrm{H}_{0}
(that inverts the isomorphism given by
h
from
\mathrm{H}_{0}
to
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}
). Otherwise we choose
h^{-1}
arbitrarily, such that
h^{-1}(y)=x
only if
h(x)=y
. Since

\mathrm{H}_{2}
is a reflexive tournament,
h^{-1}
is an isomorphism. And
h^{-1}\circ h
is an endomorphism of
\mathrm{H}_{2}
that fixes
\mathrm{H}_{0}
that does not yield an isomorphic copy of
\mathrm{H}_{1}
in
h(\mathrm{H}_{1})
, a contradiction.

Corollary 17
.

Let
\mathrm{H}
be a non-trivial strongly connected reflexive tournament.
Then
\mathrm{QCSP}(\mathrm{H})
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard.

Proof.

As
\mathrm{H}
is a strongly connected reflexive tournament, which has more than one vertex by our assumption,
\mathrm{H}
is not transitive.
Note that
\mathrm{H}
-Retraction

is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete (see Section 4.5 in
[
15
]
, using results from
[
14
,
5
,
16
]

\mathrm{H}
is endo-trivial, the result follows from Proposition
11
(note that we could also have used Corollary

Suppose
\mathrm{H}
is not endo-trivial. Then, by Lemma
4
,
\mathrm{H}
is not retract-trivial either. This means
that
\mathrm{H}
has a non-trivial retraction to some subtournament
\mathrm{H}_{0}
. We may assume
that
\mathrm{H}_{0}
is endo-trivial, as otherwise we will repeat the argument until we find a retraction from
\mathrm{H}
to an endo-trivial (and consequently strongly connected) subtournament.

Suppose that
\mathrm{H}
retracts to all isomorphic copies
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}=i(\mathrm{H}_{0})
of
\mathrm{H}_{0}
within it, except possibly those for which
\mathrm{Spill}_{m}(\mathrm{H}[\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0},i(\mathrm{HC}_{0})])\neq V(\mathrm{H})
. Then the result follows from Proposition
11
. So there is a copy
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}=i(\mathrm{H}_{0})
to which
\mathrm{H}
does not retract for which
\mathrm{Spill}_{m}(\mathrm{H}[\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0},i(\mathrm{HC}_{0})])=V(\mathrm{H})
.
If
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0})
is endo-trivial, the result follows from Proposition
13
. Thus we assume
(\mathrm{H},\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0})
is not endo-trivial and we deduce the existence of
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\mathrm{H}_{1}\subset\mathrm{H}
(
\mathrm{H}_{1}
is strictly between
\mathrm{H}
and
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0}
) so that
(\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{0})
and
H^{\prime}_{0}
are endo-trivial and
\mathrm{H}
retracts to
\mathrm{H}_{1}
. Now we are ready to break out. Either
\mathrm{H}
retracts to all isomorphic copies of
\mathrm{H}^{\prime}_{1}=i(\mathrm{H}_{1})
in

Theorem
27
resolved the open case in Table
1
.
It is difficult to position this result in the overall classification program for finite-domain QCSPs save to say that our methods are tailored, indeed specialised, to reflexive tournaments. It is not clear that they can be applied easily to different or wider classes (in this vein we return to mixed-type tournaments below). Since complexities outside of P, NP-complete and Pspace-complete were discovered for QCSPs in
[
25
]
, for example co-NP-complete, DP-complete and
\Theta_{2}^{\mathrm{P}}
, the whole classification task has been thrown wide open. Classes such as that of reflexive tournaments might provide comfort, as it is doubtful such monstrous complexities could be found here. Though, we cannot be sure, with our lacuna between NP-hard and Pspace-complete.

Recall that the results for the irreflexive tournaments in this table were all proven in a more general setting, namely for irreflexive semicomplete graphs.
One
natural direction for future research is to determine a complexity dichotomy for
\mathrm{QCSP}
and
\mathrm{SCSP}

for reflexive semicomplete graphs. We leave this as an interesting open direction.

The task of promoting our
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness results to
\mathsf{Pspace}
-complete, while using the same method, seems to require corresponding
\mathsf{Pspace}
-hardness results for reflexive tournaments with constants. If
\mathrm{QCSP}^{c}(\mathrm{H})
were
\mathsf{Pspace}
-complete, for
\mathrm{H}
a non-trivial reflexive strongly connected tournament, then likely our
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness results, for the similar class of graphs, would easily rise to
\mathsf{Pspace}
-complete. The cases that are not strongly connected require additional arguments, and perhaps even a different method.

Mixed-type tournaments, where some vertices are reflexive and others irreflexive, are well-understood algebraically
[
21
]
. Indeed, from this paper there follows a complexity dichotomy for
\mathrm{CSP}^{c}(\mathrm{H})
where
\mathrm{H}
is a mixed-type tournament. Furthermore,
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathrm{H})
is either trivial or
\mathrm{H}
is an irreflexive tournament, so the complexity dichotomy for
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathrm{H})
is also known. Though many of our supporting lemmas hold for mixed-type tournaments, some do not. For example, Lemma
1
fails for the transitive
2
-tournament
\mathrm{TT}_{2}
in which one vertex is a self-loop and the other is not. To extend our classification to mixed-type tournaments thus requires still some work.

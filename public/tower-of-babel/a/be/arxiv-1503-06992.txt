Classification of flag-transitive primitive symmetric ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda) designs with PSL ⁡ ( 2 , q ) {\rm PSL}(2,q) as socle
Shenglin Zhou, Delu Tian a. School of Mathematics, South China University of Technology,Guangzhou, Guangdong 510640, P. R. Chinab. Department of Mathematics, Guangdong University of Education,Guangzhou, 510310, P. R. China

Abstract
Let 𝒟 \cal D be a nontrivial symmetric ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda) design, and G G be a subgroup of the full automorphism group of 𝒟 \cal D . In this paper we prove that if G G acts flag-transitively, point-primitively on 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and Soc ⁡ ( G ) = PSL ⁡ ( 2 , q ) {\rm Soc}(G)={\rm PSL}(2,q) , then 𝒟 \cal D has parameters ( 7 , 3 , 1 ) (7,3,1) , ( 7 , 4 , 2 ) (7,4,2) , ( 11 , 5 , 2 ) (11,5,2) , ( 11 , 6 , 3 ) (11,6,3) or ( 15 , 8 , 4 ) (15,8,4) . Keywords : symmetric design, flag-transitive, primitive group MR(2000) Subject Classification 05B05, 05B25, 20B25



1
Introduction

A 2-
(v,k,\lambda)

design

\mathcal{D}
is a set
P
of
v
points together with a set
\mathcal{B}
of
b
blocks, such that
every block contains
k
points and every pair of points is in
exactly
\lambda
blocks. The design
\mathcal{D}
is
symmetric
if

b=v
, and is
non-trivial
if
2<k<v-1
. In this paper we
only study non-trivial symmetric 2-
(v,k,\lambda)
designs, and
for brevity we call such a design a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)

design. A
flag
in a design is an incident point-block pair.
The
complement
of
\cal D
, denoted by
\cal D^{\prime}
, is a
symmetric
(v,v-k,v-2k+\lambda)

design whose set of points is the same as the set of points of
\cal D
, and whose blocks
are the complements of the blocks of
\cal D
.
The
automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(\cal D)
of
{\cal D}
consists of
all permutations of
P
which leave
\cal B
invariant. For
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\cal D)
, the design
\cal D
is called
point-primitive
if
G
is primitive on
P
, and
flag-transitive
if
G
is transitive on the set of flags. The

socle
of a group
G
, denoted by
{\rm Soc}(G)
, is the subgroup
generated by its minimal normal subgroups.

The classification program for symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
designs has
been studied by several researchers. In 1985, Kantor

[
9
]
classified all symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
designs
admitting 2-transitive automorphism groups. In
[
4
]
, Dempwolff determined all symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
designs
which admit an automorphism group
G

such that
G
has a nonabelian socle and is a primitive rank three group on points (and blocks).
In
[
13
]
, we classified flag-transitive point-primitive symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
designs
admitting an automorphism group
G
such that
{\rm Soc}(G)
is a sporadic simple group.
This paper is devoted to the complete classification of flag-transitive point-primitive symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
designs
which admit an automorphism group
G
with
{\rm Soc}(G)={\rm PSL}(2,q)
,
and extend the result of symmetric designs with
\lambda=4
in
[
15
]
to the
general case.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\mathcal{D}=(P,\mathcal{B})
be a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design which admits a flag-transitive, point-primitive
automorphism group
G
, and
x
be a point of
P
. If
G
is an almost simple group and

X={\rm Soc}(G)={\rm PSL}(2,q)
, where
q=p^{f}
and
p
is a prime,
then
\mathcal{D}
is one of the following:

(i)

a
(7,3,1)
design with
X={\rm PSL}(2,7)
and
X_{x}=S_{4}
;

(ii)

a
(7,4,2)
design with
X={\rm PSL}(2,7)
and
X_{x}=S_{4}
;

(iii)

a
(11,5,2)
design with
X={\rm PSL}(2,11)
and
X_{x}=A_{5}
;

(iv)

a
(11,6,3)
design with
X={\rm PSL}(2,11)
and
X_{x}=A_{5}
;

(v)

a
(15,8,4)
design with
X={\rm PSL}(2,9)
and
X_{x}={\rm PGL}(2,3)
.

Corollary 1.2

For
\lambda\geq 5
, there is no symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design
admitting a flag-transitive, point-primitive almost simple
automorphism group with socle
{\rm PSL}(2,q)
.

Lemma 2.1

Let

(iii)

and

, where

is the stabilizer in

of a point

.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
3
]
)
Let
{\cal D}=(P,{\cal B})
be a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design and
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\cal D)
.
Suppose that
\gcd(k,\lambda)=1
and
G
is 2-transitive on
P
. Then
G
is flag-transitive.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
Let
{\cal D}
be a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design and
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\cal D)
. Then

(i)

G
has as many orbits on points as on blocks;

(ii)

if
G
is a transitive automorphism group, then
G
has the same rank whether considered as a permutation group
on points or on blocks.

Lemma 2.4

Let
{\cal D}=(P,{\cal B})
be a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design admitting a
flag-transitive automorphism group
G
. Then
G
is 2-transitive
on
P
if and only if
G
is flag-transitive on

\cal D^{\prime}
, the complement design of
\cal D
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
is 2-transitive on
P
.
Then for any
x\in P
and
B\in{\cal B}
, Lemma
2.3
shows that both
G_{x}
and
G_{B}
acting on points or on blocks have two orbits.
The flag-transitivity implies that
G_{B}
acts transitively on the
points of
B
. Thus
G_{B}
has an orbit
\Gamma_{1}
of length
k
on

P
and the other orbit
\Gamma_{2}
of length
v-k
and

\Gamma_{2}=P-\Gamma_{1}
. Therefore,
\Gamma_{2}=B^{\prime}
is one of the
blocks of
{\cal D}^{\prime}
and
G_{B}=G_{B^{\prime}}
. So
G_{B^{\prime}}
is transitive on
the points of
B^{\prime}
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.3
,

G
is block-transitive on
{\cal D}^{\prime}
since

G
is 2-transitive on
P
.
Hence
G
is flag-transitive on
{\cal D}^{\prime}
.

Conversely, if
\cal D^{\prime}
is flag-transitive, then
G_{B^{\prime}}
is
transitive on the points of
B^{\prime}
for every block
B^{\prime}
of
\cal D^{\prime}
. Let
B=P-B^{\prime}
. Then
B
is one of the blocks of
{\cal D}
and

G_{B}=G_{B^{\prime}}
. Since
\cal D
is flag-transitive,
G_{B}
is
transitive on
B
. Thus
G_{B}
has two orbits acting on points,
which implies that the point-stabilizer
G_{x}
has two orbits
acting on
P
by Lemma
2.3
. Hence
G
is 2-transitive on

P
.
\hfill\square

Lemma 2.5

Let
G
be a group that acts transitively on
P
, and let
X\unlhd G
.
Then each orbit of
G_{x}
, the stabilizer of
G
for some
x\in P
, acting on
P
is the union of some
orbits of
X_{x}
which have the same cardinality.

Proof.

Suppose that
the orbits of the action of the stabilizer
X_{x}
on
P
are
\Delta_{1}(=\{x\})
,
\Delta_{2},\cdots
,
\Delta_{m}
.
For any orbit
\Delta_{i}
(
i=1,2,\cdots,m
) and any
g\in G_{x}
,
we have
(\Delta_{i}^{g})^{X_{x}}=(\Delta_{i}^{g})^{g^{-1}{X_{x}}g}=\Delta_{i}^{X_{x}g}=\Delta_{i}^{g}
,
where the first equality holds since
X_{x}\unlhd G_{x}
.
Hence
\Delta_{i}^{g}
is also an orbit of
X_{x}
.
It follows that
\Delta_{i}^{G_{x}}=\{\Delta_{i}^{g}\ |\ g\in{G_{x}}\}
, a union of orbits of
X_{x}
which have the same cardinality,
gives an orbit of
G_{x}
.

\hfill\square

Lemma 2.6

Let
\mathcal{D}=(P,\mathcal{B})
be a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design admitting a flag-transitive, point-primitive automorphism group
G
with socle
X
.
If the non-trivial subdegree
t
of
X
appears with multiplicity
s
, then
k\mid\lambda st
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Gamma_{1}
,
\Gamma_{2},\cdots
,
\Gamma_{s}
are all orbits of
X_{x}
with cardinality
t
, where
x\in P
.
By Lemma
2.5
, the group
G_{x}
acts on
\Gamma=\bigcup\limits^{s}_{i=1}\Gamma_{i}
, and the cardinalities of orbits of
G_{x}
are

(a_{1}t)^{s_{1}},\ (a_{2}t)^{s_{2}},\ \cdots,\ (a_{r}t)^{s_{r}},

where
a^{b}
means that
a
appears with multiplicity
b
, and
r
,
a_{i}
,
s_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq r
) are all positive integers such that

\sum\limits^{r}_{i=1}a_{i}{s_{i}}=s
, and
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
if and only if
i\neq j
for
1\leq i,j\leq r
.
Let
c=\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r})
, then
c\mid s
.
Lemma
2.1
(
ii
) shows that
k\mid\lambda(a_{i}t)
,

i=1,2,\cdots,r
.
So
k\mid\lambda ct
, and hence
k\mid\lambda st
.

\hfill\square

Proof of Theorem 1.1

Let
\mathcal{D}=(P,\mathcal{B})
be a symmetric
(v,k,\lambda)
design admitting a flag-transitive, point-primitive automorphism group
G
with

X\unlhd G\leq{\rm Aut}(X)
, where
X={\rm PSL}(2,q)
with
q=p^{f}
and
p
prime.
As a maximal subgroup of
G
, the point stabilizer
G_{x}
does not contain
X
since
X
is transitive on
P
. Thus Lemma
2.8
shows that either
X\cap G_{x}
is maximal in
X
, or

G
and
G_{x}
are given in Table 1. We will prove Theorem
1.1
by the
following three subsections.

3.1
Cases in Table
1

In these cases, we may view the maximal subgroup
M
as the point stabilizer
G_{x}
.
We get the 3-tuples
(|G|,u,v)
in Table
1

where
v
is the index
|G:G_{x}|
and
u=|G_{x}|
. For each case except the last one, we can obtain all potential symmetric designs using
Algorithm
2.9
implemented in
GAP

[
6
]
. There exists only one potential
(21,16,12)
design with
G={\rm PGL}(2,7)
and
G_{x}=D_{16}
.
The subdegrees of
{\rm PGL}(2,7)
acting on the cosets of
D_{16}
are 1, 4, 8 and
8. (Throughout this paper, we apply
Magma

[
1
]
to calculate the subdegrees of
G
and the number of the conjugacy class of
subgroups.)
Then by using the
Magma
-command

Subgroups(G:OrderEqual:=n)
where
n=|G|/v
, we obtain the fact that
G
has only one conjugacy class of subgroups with index 21.
Thus
G_{x}
is conjugate to
G_{B}
for any
x\in{\cal P},B\in{\cal B}
which forces that there exists a block
B_{0}
such that
G_{x}=G_{B_{0}}
.
The flag-transitivity of
G
implies that
G_{B_{0}}
is transitive on the block
B_{0}
.
So
B_{0}
should be an orbit of
G_{x}
,
but there is no such orbit of size
k=16
, a contradiction.

Now we consider the last case. Here
G={\rm PGL}(2,q)
with
q=p\equiv\pm 11,19\pmod{40}
,
G_{x}=S_{4}
, and
v=\frac{q(q^{2}-1)}{24}
.
Since
|G_{x}|^{3}>|G|
, we have
24^{3}>q(q^{2}-1)
, and so
q=p=11
or 19.
If
q=11
then
v=55
.
There exist two potential symmetric designs with parameters
(55,27,13)
and
(55,28,14)
,
but neither of them satisfies the condition that
k\mid|G_{x}|
.
If
q=19
then
v=285
, and so
(k,\lambda)=(72,18)
or
(213,159)
. However, for every case
k>24
which contracts the fact that
k
divides
|G_{x}|
.

3.2
Odd characteristic

In this subsection, we consider the cases that
G
has odd characteristic
p
and
X\cap G_{x}
is maximal in
X
.
The structure of
X\cap G_{x}
comes from Table
2
.

Case (1)
.
X\cap G_{x}=E_{q}:\frac{q-1}{2}
.

Here
v=q+1
, so
k(k-1)=\lambda(v-1)=\lambda q=\lambda p^{f}
. If
p\mid k
,
then from
\gcd(p,k-1)=1
we have
p^{f}\mid k
, that is,
v-1\mid k
which is impossible. Then
p\nmid k
, and so
p^{f}\mid k-1

implies
v-1\mid k-1
, which contradicts
k<v-1
.

Case (2)
.
X\cap G_{x}=D_{q-1}

(q\geq 13)
.

In this case,
v=\frac{1}{2}q(q+1)
,
|{\rm Out}(X)|=2f
,

|G|=\frac{1}{2}eq(q^{2}-1)
and
|G_{x}|=e(q-1)
, where
e
is a
positive integer and
e\mid 2f
.

From
k\mid|G_{x}|
we get
k\mid e(q-1)
. So there exists a
positive integer
m
such that
k=\frac{e(q-1)}{m}
. The equality

k(k-1)=\lambda(v-1)
implies that

\frac{e(q-1)}{m}\big(\frac{e(q-1)}{m}-1\big)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda(q+2)(q-1)
, and hence

(2e^{2}-m^{2}\lambda)q=2m^{2}\lambda+2e^{2}+2em>0.

This implies that
m<2e
.
From
p^{f}=q=\frac{2m^{2}\lambda+2e^{2}+2em}{2e^{2}-m^{2}\lambda}=\frac{6e^{2}+2em}{2e^{2}-m^{2}\lambda}-2
, we get

p^{f}<6e^{2}+2em<6e^{2}+(2e)^{2}=10e^{2}\leq 40f^{2}.

It follows that the 3-tuples
(q,p,f)
are

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}&(27,3,3),&(81,3,4),&(243,3,5),&(729,3,6),&(25,5,2),&(125,5,3),\\
&(625,5,4),&(49,7,2),&(343,7,3),&(121,11,2),&(13,13,1),&(17,17,1),\\
&(19,19,1),&(23,23,1),&(29,29,1),&(31,31,1),&(37,37,1).&&\\
\end{array}

We call each of these 3-tuples a subcase.
Since
k\mid e(q-1)
and
e\mid 2f
, it follows that
k\mid u
,
where
u=2f(q-1)
. It is easy to compute the values of
u
and
v

for every subcase. However, for every subcase, there is no such
symmetric design satisfying the condition that
k\mid u
by
Algorithm
2.9

calculated with
GAP
.

Case (3)
.
X\cap G_{x}=D_{q+1}
(
q\neq 7,9
).

Now
v=\frac{1}{2}q(q-1)
,
|{\rm Out}(X)|=2f
,

|G|=\frac{1}{2}eq(q^{2}-1)
, and
|G_{x}|=e(q+1)
, where
e
is a
positive integer and
e\mid 2f
.

Since
k\mid|G_{x}|=e(q+1)
, it follows that there exists a
positive integer
m
such that
k=\frac{e(q+1)}{m}
. Then

\frac{e(q+1)}{m}\big(\frac{e(q+1)}{m}-1\big)=\frac{1}{2}\lambda(q-2)(q+1)
. So we have

(m^{2}\lambda-2e^{2})q=2e^{2}-2em+2m^{2}\lambda=2(e-\frac{1}{2}m)^{2}+(2\lambda-\frac{1}{2})m^{2}>0.

Thus
p^{f}=q=\frac{2e^{2}-2em+2m^{2}\lambda}{m^{2}\lambda-2e^{2}}=\frac{6e^{2}-2em}{m^{2}\lambda-2e^{2}}+2

which gives

p^{f}<6e^{2}+2\leq 24f^{2}+2.

Combining this with
q\neq 7,9
, we obtain all possible 3-tuples

Case (1)

Case (2)

2.1

. The fact that

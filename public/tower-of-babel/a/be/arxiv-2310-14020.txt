Dimension Reduction for Positively Curved Steady Solitons
Pak-Yeung Chan, Zilu Ma, Yongjia Zhang

Abstract
We consider noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci solitons with nonnegative sectional curvature. We show that such solitons always dimension reduce at infinity. This generalizes an earlier result in [ CDM22 ] to higher dimensions. In dimension four, we classify possible reductions at infinity, which lays foundation for possible classifications of steady solitons. Moreover, we show that any tangent flow at infinity of a general noncollapsed steady soliton must split off a line. This generalizes an earlier result in [ BCDMZ21 ] to higher dimensions. While this article is under preparation, we realized that part of our main results are proved independently in a recent post [ ZZ23 ] under different assumptions.

Introduction

In order to apply Ricci flow to topological problems especially in dimension 4, one needs better understanding of the formation of singularities in order to perform surgeries. Among singularity models,
steady Ricci solitons
play a crucial role as they may arise from type-II singularities
[
Ha95
]
. Moreover, the classification for steady solitons is fundamental to the classification of general ancient flows, see
[
Bre13
,
Bre20
,
ABDS22
,
BDS21
]
.

We call a triple
(M^{n},g,f)
a complete
steady gradient Ricci soliton
, or
steady soliton
,
if
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold and
f
is a smooth function on
M
satisfying

\Ric=\nabla^{2}f.

A Ricci flat manifold is an obvious trivial example of steady soliton, but we shall always consider the non-Ricci-flat case. By a classical result due to Hamilton
[
Ha95
]
, we may normalize the metric so that

R+|\nabla f|^{2}=1.

Let
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be the
1
-parameter group of diffeomorphisms generated by
-\nabla f,
then
g_{t}=\Phi_{t}^{*}g
is a Ricci flow, called the
canonical Ricci flow
induced by the steady soliton
(M^{n},g,f).
The time span of
(M,g_{t})
is
\mathbb{R}
, and any such solution is called
eternal
by Hamilton. In fact, each time-slice of the canonical flow is also a steady soliton: let
f_{t}:=f\circ\Phi_{t}
, then we obvisouly have

\displaystyle\Ric_{g_{t}}=\nabla^{2}_{g_{t}}f_{t},\quad R_{g_{t}}+|\nabla_{g_{t}}f_{t}|^{2}=1,\quad\text{ for all }\quad t\in(-\infty,+\infty).

(1.1)

We say that a steady soliton
(M^{n},g,f)
is
noncollapsed
, if the canonical Ricci flow induced by
(M^{n},g,f)
has bounded Nash entropy, i.e.,

\mu_{\infty}:=\inf_{\tau>0}\mathcal{N}_{o,0}(\tau)>-\infty,

where
\mathcal{N}_{o,0}(\tau)
denotes the pointed Nash entropy based at a space-time point
(o,0)
at scale
\tau
; see, e.g.,
[
Bam20a
]
. Nash entropy is the fundamental monotone quantity in Bamler’s works
[
Bam20a
,
Bam20c
]
. By a result of the second and the third authors
[
MZ21
, Theorem 1.13]
, our notion of noncollapsedness is equivalent to the
\kappa
-noncollapsedness
[
Per02
]
for the canonical Ricci flow of a steady soliton with nonnegative sectional curvature.

Choi, Haslhofer, and Hershkovits in
[
CHH23
]
fully classified
\alpha
-noncollapsed translators in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, which are natural analogues of steady solitons in the extrinsic setting.
Among other conjectures concerning general noncollapsed ancient flows, Haslhofer
[
Has23
]
proposed the following conjecture about
4
-dimensional noncollapsed steady solitons.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Any
4
-dimensional noncollapsed steady soliton with
\sec\geq 0
can only be one the following: the 4d Bryant soliton, the 3d Bryant soliton times a line, or one of the 1-parameter family of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times{\rm O}_{3}
-symmetric steady
solitons constructed by Lai
[
Lai20
]
.

We remark that this is slightly stronger than Haslhofer’s original conjecture, which assumes
\Rm\geq 0
(nonnegative curvature operator). One of the reasons why this stronger version is also likely is because the compactness result given in the appendix of the current paper assumes only
\sec\geq 0
(nonnegative sectional curvture).

According to Theorem
2.2
in
[
BCDMZ21
]
, any noncollapsed non-Ricci-flat 4d steady solitons with bounded curvature admits a unique tangent flow at infinity, which falls into one of the following:
(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}
.
Assuming
\sec\geq 0
and
\Ric>0,
Corollary
5.4
below shows that the tangent flow at infinity can only be
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
;
in the former case, the steady soliton must be the Bryant soliton by Theorem
1.5
, which is in fact implied by
[
BCDMZ21
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
Bre14
, Theorem 1.2]
. So, in the majority of the paper, we consider steady solitons with
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
as the unique tangent flow at infinity, which conjecturally should be Lai’s examples in
[
Lai22
]
.

As a preliminary step towards the classification of steady solitons, we shall understand geometric limits at infinity. For example, in the recent classification results for 3d ancient Ricci flows
[
Bre13
,
Bre20
,
ABDS22
,
BDS21
,
Lai22
]
, the geometric limits were classified at first.
We first prove dimension reduction for steady solitons in any dimensions, assuming
\sec\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci soliton with
\sec\geq 0
. If
(M^{n},g)
is non-flat, then it dimension reduces at infinity. That is, for any points
x_{i}\to\infty,
let
r_{i}^{-2}=R(x_{i})
.
By passing to a subsequence,

(M,r_{i}^{-2}g_{r_{i}^{2}t},(x_{i},0))\longrightarrow(N^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R},\bar{g}_{t}+\mathrm{d}z^{2},((\bar{x},0),0)),

in the sense of Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton over
(-\infty,0]
,
where
(N,\bar{g}_{t})
is some noncollapsed ancient flow, and
g_{t}
denotes the canonical Ricci flow induced by the steady soliton.

In fact, we shall prove a pre-compactness result, namely, Theorem
A.2
, for a larger class of noncollapsed ancient flows. As a consequence, we can prove a slightly more general splitting theorem at infinity—Theorem
A.11
. Theorem
1.2
follows as a special case. We follow Perelman’s arguments in
[
Per02
, Section 11]
and Brendle’s recent presentation in
[
Bre22
]
.

In dimension 4, thanks to the recent full classification of 3d noncollapsed ancient flows (
[
Bre20
,
BDS21
]
),
we can classify possible reductions.
When assuming
\sec\geq 0,\Ric>0,
by Corollary
5.4
below, the remaining interesting case is the one in which steady solitons has
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
as the tangent flow at infinity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a complete noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci soliton with
\sec\geq 0,\Ric>0
. If the tangent flow at infinity is
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, then it can only dimension reduce to

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\quad\text{ or }\quad M^{3}_{\rm Bry}.

By a slight abuse of notations, we denote by

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R},\quad M^{3}_{\rm Bry},\quad M^{3}_{\rm Per}

the standard shrinking
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, the canonical form of the three-dimensional Bryant soliton, Perelman’s type-II compact
\kappa
-solution
[
Per03a
, 1.4]
, respectively. For simplicity, we sometimes omit the metrics when they are clear from the context.

Remark.

We point out that some results similar to Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
are proved independently in a recent post
[
ZZ23
]
under different assumptions.

In a forthcoming work
[
MMS23
]
, Mahmoudian, Šešum, and the second-named author will obtain precise geometric asymptotics for 4d steady solitons with
{\rm O}_{3}
-symmetry, which are similar to
[
ABDS22
]
. Theorem
1.3
is fundamental to such precise analysis.

The idea of proving Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
is to show that the limit admits a line, by making use of the curvature positivity condition. The advantage of this approach is that one can obtain a splitting limit from any sequence of points going to infinity, and the disadvantage is the restriction of the curvature condition. However, there is another approach presented in
[
BCDMZ21
]
, which is to show that the potential function converges to a splitting map. This method works without any curvature restriction, but holds only for tangent flows at infinity. We also apply this method to higher dimensions and prove the following general result.

Theorem 1.4

(= Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete non-Ricci-flat noncollapsed steady soliton with bounded curvature.
Then any tangent flow at infinity of its canonical flow splits off a line.

Noted that the tangent flow at infinity
[
Bam20c
]
is an
\mathbb{F}
-limit of a scaled sequence of Ricci flows; it is a metric soliton which possibly has a singular set with codimension at least four. Tangent flow at infinity is not unique a priori; it depends on the scaling sequence but is independent of the base point
[
CMZ21a
]
. Therefore, if one tangent flow at infinity is smooth, there could possibly be other non-smooth tangent flows. However, we shall prove the uniqueness of tangent flow for the canonical flow of a steady soliton, given that one of its tangent flows is a cylinder. This generalizes a result in
[
BCDMZ21
]
. We remark that the following theorem is not a direct consequence of
[
CM22
]
, since tangent flows are metric solitons instead of smooth solitons.

Theorem 1.5

(= Theorem
5.2
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete non-Ricci-flat noncollapsed steady soliton with bounded curvature.
If one tangent flow at infinity is
(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}/\Gamma)\times\mathbb{R}
, then it is the unique one. Furthermore, for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a compact set
K_{\epsilon}
such that every point outside
K_{\epsilon}
is a center of an
\epsilon
-neck. Moreover, outside
K_{\epsilon}
we have,

\Rm>0,\qquad rR=\tfrac{n-1}{2}+o(1),

as
x\to\infty,

where
r(x)=|ox|
for some point
o\in M.

If, in addition,
\sec\geq 0
and
\Ric>0,
then
\Gamma=1
and
(M^{n},g,f)
is isometric to the Bryant soliton up to rescaling.

Preliminaries

We denote by

\mathcal{C}_{f}:=\{x:\nabla f(x)=0\}

the set of all critical points of
f.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a steady soliton with
\Ric>0.

Then
|\mathcal{C}_{f}|\leq 1
, i.e.
f
has at most
1
critical point.
There is a compact subset
K\subset M
, such that
if
M_{s}:=\{f=s\}
does not intersect
K
, then
M_{s}
is a smooth manifold and

\sec_{M_{s}}>\sec_{M}.

As a consequence, if
n=4,
then
M_{s}
is diffeomorphic to either
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Proof.

f
is strictly convex, because
\nabla^{2}f=\Ric>0.

Thus, if
\mathcal{C}_{f}\neq\emptyset
, then
|\mathcal{C}_{f}|=1
.
In this case, by
[
DZ21
, Lemma 2.1]
, each level set is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Now for both cases,

|\mathcal{C}_{f}|=0
or
1,
we may assume that
|\nabla f|>0,
outside
B(o,1),
for some
o\in M.
Since
\nabla^{2}f=\Ric>0,
each level set
M_{s}=\{f=s\}
is a smooth manifold.

We write
\mathbf{n}:=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}.
The second fundamental form
{\rm II}
of
M_{s}
is given by

{\rm II}(X,Y)=\langle\nabla_{X}\mathbf{n},Y\rangle=\frac{1}{|\nabla f|}\Ric(X,Y),

for any
X,Y
tangent to
M_{s}.

Thus,
{\rm II}>0
. By the Gauss formula,

\displaystyle\sec_{M_{s}}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=R(X,Y,Y,X)+{\rm II}(X,X){\rm II}(Y,Y)-\left({\rm II}(X,Y)\right)^{2}>\sec_{M}(X,Y),

for any unit vectors
X,Y
tangent to
M_{s}
with
X\perp Y.
Here, we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, since
{\rm II}>0,
and
X\perp Y.

In particular, if
n=4,M_{s}
is a
3
-manifold with
\Ric_{M_{s}}>0.
Thus,
M_{s}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by classical results.
∎

3
Growth of the Potential Function

This section is taken partly from the second named author’s dissertation
[
M22
, §7.2]
.

For a steady gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f)
, it is obvious that if
R
has uniform decay, then the critical set of
f

\mathcal{C}_{f}:=\{x\,|\,\nabla f(x)=0\}

is nonempty and compact. The converse is incorrect. For example, consider the product of two Bryant solitons.
Thus, it is strictly weaker than the uniform decay of
R
to assume that
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is nonempty and compact.

The following arguments are indeed contained in
[
CDM22
, Section 9]
. We shall record them for future use.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
{\rm Ric}\geq 0
everywhere on
M
.
If
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is nonempty and compact, then
f
has linear growth.

Proof.

Pick
o\in\mathcal{C}_{f}.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{f}\subset B(o,A)
for some
A>0.

Claim:
For any
r\geq 2A
and any
x\notin\bar{B}(o,r),
there is
t_{x}>0,
such that

\Phi_{t_{x}}(x)\in\partial B(o,r),;\qquad\Phi_{t}(x)\notin\bar{B}(o,r),,\quad t\in[0,t_{x}).

Proof of the Claim.

Since
{\rm Ric}\geq 0
on
M
, for
t\geq 0
and any
x\in M,

{\rm dist}(o,\Phi_{t}(x))=|ox|_{t}\leq|ox|,

by the standard distance distorsion estimate. (See, e.g.,
[
Ha95
, Lemma 17.13]
.)
So the curve
\Phi_{t}(x)
stays bounded for
t\geq 0.

We show that
\Phi_{t}(x)
accumulates on
\mathcal{C}_{f}.
Suppose to the contrary that there is a subsequence
t_{j}\to\infty,\Phi_{t_{j}}(x)\to y
for some
y
but
y\notin\mathcal{C}_{f}.

Let
2c=|\nabla f|^{2}(y)>0.
For
t\geq 0,

\partial_{t}|\nabla f|^{2}(\Phi_{t}(x))=-2{\rm Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)(\Phi_{t}(x))\leq 0.

Thus for large
j,

f(\Phi_{t_{j}}(x))-f(x)=-\int_{0}^{t_{j}}|\nabla f|^{2}(\Phi_{s}(x))\,ds\leq-|\nabla f|^{2}(\Phi_{t_{j}}(x))t_{j}\leq-ct_{j},

which is impossible since
f(\Phi_{t_{j}}(x))\to f(y)
while
t_{j}\to\infty
.
Hence
\Phi_{t}(x)
accumulates on
\mathcal{C}_{f}.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{f}\subset B(o,A),
there must be such a number
t_{x}>0
for each
x\notin\bar{B}(o,r),.

∎

Now we fix
r_{0}\geq 2A.

Let
C,\theta>0
be constants such that

\min_{\partial B(o,r_{0})}f\geq-C,\quad\min_{\partial B(o,r_{0})}|\nabla f|\geq\theta.

Then

\begin{split}f(x)&=f(\Phi_{t_{x}}(x))+\int_{0}^{t_{x}}|\nabla f|^{2}(\Phi_{s}(x))ds\\
&\geq-C+|\nabla f|(\Phi_{t_{x}}(x))\int_{0}^{t_{x}}|\partial_{s}\Phi_{s}(x)|ds\\
&\geq-C+\theta\cdot d(x,\partial B(o,r_{0}))\geq\theta|ox|-(C+\theta r_{0}).\end{split}

Thus,
f
has linear growth.
∎

Recall that a function is said to be proper, if
\{f\leq c\}
is compact, for any
c\in\mathbb{R}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
{\rm Ric}\geq 0
outside a compact set
and
f
is proper. Then
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is nonempty and compact.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Ric\geq 0
outside
B(o,A)
for some
A>0,o\in M.

As
f
is proper, it has a minimum and thus
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is nonempty. Assume by contradiction that
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is not compact. Then there is a sequence
x_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
and
x_{j}\to\infty.

By
[
CDM22
, Lemma 9.7]
, we may assume that there is
x_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
such that, for each
j\geq 1
,
x_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
and any minimal geodesic from
x_{0}
to
x_{j}
does not intersect
B(o,A).

Let
\gamma_{j}:[0,\ell_{j}]\to M
be a normal minimizing geodesic joining
x_{0}
to
x_{j}
.
Note that
\Phi_{t}(x_{j})\equiv x_{j}
for all
t
since
x_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}.
Thus, for any
t\in\mathbb{R},

|x_{0}x_{j}|_{t}={\rm dist}(\Phi_{t}(x_{0}),\Phi_{t}(x_{j}))=|x_{0}x_{j}|=\ell_{j}.

We denote by
L_{t}(\gamma)
the length of a curve
\gamma
with respect to metric
g_{t}.

For
t\in(-1,1),

L_{t}(\gamma_{j})\geq|x_{0}x_{j}|_{t}=\ell_{j},\quad L_{0}(\gamma_{j})=\ell_{j}.

So

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}L_{t}(\gamma_{j})=0.

On the other hand, by the standard distance distorsion formula under Ricci flow
[
Ha95
, Section 17]
,

0=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}L_{t}(\gamma_{j})=-\int_{0}^{\ell_{j}}\Ric(\dot{\gamma}_{j},\dot{\gamma}_{j})\Big|_{\gamma_{j}(s)}\,ds.

Since
\gamma_{j}
does not intersect
B(o,A)
, we have
\Ric(\dot{\gamma}_{j},\dot{\gamma}_{j})|_{\gamma_{j}(s)}\geq 0
for
s\in[0,\ell_{j}].

It follows that

\Ric(\dot{\gamma}_{j},\dot{\gamma}_{j})|_{\gamma_{j}(s)}=0,

for all
s\in[0,\ell_{j}].

So
\dot{\gamma}_{j}(s)
is a null eigenvector of
{\rm Ric}|_{\gamma_{j}(s)}
and

\partial_{s}R(\gamma_{j}(s))=-2{\rm Ric}(\dot{\gamma}_{j},\nabla f)(\gamma_{j}(s))=0,\quad\forall s\in[0,\ell_{j}].

Then

|\nabla f|(\gamma_{j}(s))\equiv|\nabla f|(x_{0})=0,\quad\forall s\in[0,\ell_{j}].

Thus, for any
j\geq 1,

f(x_{j})=f(x_{0}),

which is a contradiction to the assumption that
f
is proper.

∎

Geometry at Infinity

In dimension
4
, we have finer descriptions of the geometry at infinity thanks to the recent full classification of
\kappa
-solutions in dimension
3.

Our goal in this section is to prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a noncollapsed steady soliton with
\sec\geq 0,\Ric>0.
If its tangent flow at infinity is
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},
then it can only dimension reduces to

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\quad\text{ or }\quad M^{3}_{\rm Bry}.

We summarize the basic idea of the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a steady soliton as in Theorem
4.1
.
By Theorem
1.2
, it always dimension reduces.
By the full classification of noncollapsed ancient flows,
[
Bre20
,
BDS21
]
, it can only dimension reduce to the following:

\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma,\quad\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R},\quad\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R},\quad M_{\rm Bry}^{3},\quad M_{\rm Per}^{3}.

Our goal is to rule out
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
,

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
, and
M_{\rm Per}^{3}
as possible limits. First of all,
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
cannot be a possible limit, since otherwise
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
can be embedded into some level set
\{f=s\}
, which is diffeomorphic to either
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
; this is impossible. Secondly,
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
or
M_{\rm Per}^{3}
cannot be a possible limit, since otherwise it prevents some level set
\{f=s\}
from having arbitrarily long necks; this argument roughly follows from
[
BDS21
, Section 2]
.

For clarity, we divide the proof of Theorem
4.1
into several subsections.

Background assumptions:

In the rest of this section, unless otherwise stated,
(M^{4},g,f)
is a noncollapsed steady soliton with

\sec\geq 0,\quad\Ric>0.

Suppose also that its tangent flow at infinity is

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

4.1
Ruling out
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}

We first rule out
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
as a possible limit.

Proposition 4.2
.

(M^{4},g,f)
cannot dimension reduce to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
.

The following lemma is needed in the proof of the above proposition.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a noncollapsed steady soliton. Suppose that for some
x_{i}\to\infty,

if
r_{i}^{-2}:=R(x_{i}),

(M,r_{i}^{-2}g_{r_{i}^{2}t},x_{i})\longrightarrow(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{t},\bar{x}),

locally smoothly over
(-\infty,0],
where
(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{t})
is a Ricci flow admitting a shrinking gradient Ricci soliton struture. Then

R(x_{i})\to 0.

Moreover, the limit flow splits off a line:

(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{t})=(\mathbb{R}\times N,dz^{2}+h_{t}),

where
z
denotes the standard coordinate on
\mathbb{R},

and

f_{i}:=r_{i}^{-1}(f-f(x_{i}))

converges locally smoothly to the coordinate function
z
.

Assuming this Lemma, we can rule out
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Suppose that for some
x_{i}\to\infty,

if
r_{i}^{-2}=R(x_{i}),

(M,r_{i}^{-2}g_{r_{i}^{2}t},x_{i})\to((\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R},(0,\bar{x})),

locally smoothly over
(-\infty,0].
By Lemma
4.3
,
f_{i}=(f-f(x_{i}))/r_{i}
converges to the coordinate function
z
of the last
\mathbb{R}
-factor. Therefore, the level set
\{f=f(x_{i})\}
, after scaling, converges to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
, locally smoothly. In particular,
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
, as the tip of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R}
, can be embedded into
M_{i}:=\{f=f(x_{i})\}
, for large
i.

However,
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
cannot be embedded into
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}.
This is a contradiction.

∎

Now we prove Lemma
4.3
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
BCDMZ21
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Suppose to the contrary that
R(x_{i})\geq a
, for some constant
a>0
, by possibly passing to a subsequence.
By the assumption, there is a smooth function
\phi
such that

\Ric_{\bar{g}_{0}}+\nabla^{2}_{\bar{g}_{0}}\phi=\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{g}_{0}.

By
[
CDM22
, Lemma 9.1]
, since
r_{i}^{-2}\geq a,

(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{0})
also admits a steady gradient Ricci soliton structure with potential function
\bar{f},
i.e.,

\Ric_{\bar{g}_{0}}=\nabla^{2}_{g_{0}}\bar{f}.

Define
\rho=\phi+\bar{f}.
Then

\nabla^{2}_{g_{0}}\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}g_{0},

and thus
(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{0})
must be the standard
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
This is a contradiction to the fact that
R_{\bar{g}_{0}}(\bar{x})=1.
Thus,
r_{i}^{-2}=R(x_{i})\to 0.

Finally, to prove the splitting result of the limit flow, we consider only the finial time-slices of the scaled flows
(M^{n},r_{i}^{-2}g)
. We write
g_{i}:=r_{i}^{-2}g,f_{i}=(f-f(x_{i}))/r_{i}.
Then

f_{i}(x_{i})=0,\quad|df_{i}|^{2}_{g_{i}}(x_{i})=1-R(x_{i})\to 1.

Moreover,

|\nabla^{2}_{g_{i}}f_{i}|_{g_{i}}=r_{i}^{-1}|{\Ric}_{g_{i}}|_{g_{i}}\to 0,

uniformly over compact subsets, due to the smooth convergence. Similarly, we obtain the convergence of higher order derivatives of
f_{i}
. Therefore, the limit flow splits off a line and
f_{i}
converges to the coordinate function of the
\mathbb{R}
factor.
∎

Definition 4.4

(

1.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)

Curv

Proof.

By the proof of Lemma 2.4 in

\ell

(o,0)

for some universal constants

Here, we used the fact that

grows linearly.

Claim 1:
For any sequence

by passing to a subsequence,

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

∎

Claim 2:

Suppose to the contrary that there is a sequence

5
Splitting of Tangent Flows at Infinity

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete noncollapsed non-Ricci-flat steady soliton with bounded curvature.
Then any tangent flow at infinity splits off a line.

Proof.

The argument is basically identical to that of
[
BCDMZ21
]
. Let
\left(\mathcal{X},(\nu^{\infty}_{t})_{t<0}\right)
be a tangent flow at infinity of the canonical flow
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in(-\infty,+\infty)}
of the steady soliton.
Fix
o\in M
, and let
\tau_{i}\to\infty
such that

\left((M^{n},g^{i}_{t})_{t\leq 0},(\nu_{o,-\tau_{i}t})\right)\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathbb{F}\hskip 8.19447pt}\left(\mathcal{X},(\nu^{\infty}_{t})_{t<0}\right),

where
g^{i}_{t}:=\tau_{i}^{-1}g_{\tau_{i}t}
. By
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 2.3]
, there must be
z_{\infty}\in\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
(the regular part of
\mathcal{X}
), so that
z_{\infty}
is a point of smooth convergence, i.e., there exist
z_{i}\in M
such that

\big(M,g^{i}_{t},(z_{i},-1)\big)\longrightarrow(\mathcal{R}_{\infty},g^{\infty}_{t},z_{\infty}),

locally smoothly. So,

\tau_{i}R_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i})=R_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i})\to R_{g^{\infty}_{-1}}(z_{\infty})<\infty.

Since
|\nabla_{g_{t}}f_{t}|^{2}+R_{g_{t}}=1
, where
f_{t}=f\circ\Phi_{t}
, on
M
by (
1.1
), we have

\beta_{i}^{-2}:=|\mathrm{d}f|_{g^{i}_{-1}}^{2}(z_{i})=\tau_{i}|\nabla_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}f_{-\tau_{i}}|^{2}_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i})=\tau_{i}\left(1-R_{g_{-\tau_{i}}}(z_{i})\right)\to\infty.

Let us define

\tilde{f}_{i}=\beta_{i}\left(f_{-\tau_{i}}(\cdot)-f_{-\tau_{i}}(z_{i})\right).

Then
\tilde{f}_{i}(z_{i})=0
,
|d\tilde{f}_{i}|_{g^{i}_{-1}}(z_{i})=1
,
\nabla_{g^{i}_{-1}}^{2}\tilde{f}_{i}=\beta_{i}\Ric_{{g^{i}_{-1}}}\to 0
. Moreover, applying the standard derivative estimates to (
1.1
), we have that the higher derivatives
\nabla^{k+2}_{g^{i}_{-1}}\tilde{f}_{i}=\beta_{i}\nabla^{k}_{g^{i}_{-1}}\Ric_{g^{i}_{-1}}
are uniformly bounded on compact sets of
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
. Hence
\tilde{f}_{i}
subconverges smoothly and locally to a function
\tilde{f}_{\infty}
on
\mathcal{R}_{\infty,-1}
(that is, the
t=-1
time-slice of
\mathcal{R}_{\infty}
) satisfying
|d\tilde{f}_{\infty}|_{g^{\infty}_{-1}}(z_{\infty})=1
and
\nabla^{2}_{g^{\infty}_{-1}}\tilde{f}_{\infty}\equiv 0
. This implies that
\mathcal{R}_{\infty,-1}
splits off a line. Furthermore, by
[
Bam20c
, Theorem 15.28(c)]
,
\mathcal{X}_{-1}
splits off a line. Finally, since
\mathcal{X}
is a metric soliton,
\mathcal{X}
must also split off a line.
∎

With the general splitting result, we prove the following, generalizing the previous results in
[
BCDMZ21
, Theorem 1.3]
to any dimensions.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a complete noncollapsed steady soliton with bounded curvature.
If one tangent flow at infinity is
(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}/\Gamma)\times\mathbb{R}
, then it is the unique one, and for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a compact set
K_{\epsilon}
such that every point outside

is a center of an
\epsilon

\Rm>0,\qquad rR=\tfrac{n-1}{2}+o(1),

as

where

for some point

If additionally

then
\Gamma=1
and
(M^{n},g,f)
is isometric to the Bryant soliton, up to rescaling.

The following lemma is the key ingredient of the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete group acting freely on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. For any
Y>0
, there is a constant
\epsilon(n,\Gamma,Y)>0
with the following property. Let
(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})
be a metric flow pair, where
I=[-2,-1/2]
, satisfying

1.

(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})
splits off a line;

2.

(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})
arises as a tangent flow at infinity of some smooth ancient Ricci flow
(M^{n+1},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}
with
\inf_{\tau}\mathcal{N}_{o,0}(\tau)>-Y
.

3.

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\{-1\}}\bigg((\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}),\Big((\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma\times\mathbb{R},\bar{g}_{t})_{t\in I},(\bar{\mu}_{t})_{t\in I}\Big)\bigg)<\epsilon,

where
\bar{g}_{t}
is the standard shrinking metric, and
\bar{\mu}_{t}
is the standard conjugate heat flow on the cylinder defined by
d\bar{\mu}_{t}=C(|\Gamma|,n)(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot(4\pi|t|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|t|}\right)d\bar{g}_{t}
.

Then
(\mathcal{X},(\mu_{t})_{t\in I})
is isometric to
\Big((\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma\times\mathbb{R},\bar{g}_{t})_{t\in I},(\bar{\mu}_{t})_{t\in I}\Big)
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose there is a sequence of metric flow pairs
\{(\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu_{t}^{i})_{t\in I})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
satisfying conditions (1) and (2) (they may not arise as tangent flows of the same ancient flow). Assume that

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\{-1\}}\bigg((\mathcal{X}^{i},(\mu^{i}_{t})_{t\in I}),\Big((\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma\times\mathbb{R},\bar{g}_{t})_{t\in I},(\bar{\mu}_{t})_{t\in I}\Big)\bigg)\longrightarrow 0,

(5.1)

but none of them is isometric to
\Big((\mathbb{S}^{n}/\Gamma\times\mathbb{R},\bar{g}_{t})_{t\in I},(\bar{\mu}_{t})_{t\in I}\Big)
.

By
[
CMZ23
, Corollary 1.3]
, the convergence in (

) is a local smooth one. Therefore, whenever

.

On the other hand, recall that each

A Compactness Theorem and Its Applications

We shall prove a compactness theorem for a slightly larger class which includes, for example, the steady solitons with
\sec\geq 0.

Since we mainly follow Perelman’s arguments in
[
Per02
]
, (see also,
[
KL08
]
), we put this section in the appendix.

Definition A.1
.

For constants
n\in\mathbb{N},\mu_{\infty}<0,\Lambda\geq 1,
we denote by

\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda)

the class of ancient flows
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}
with the following properties.

1.

For some
o\in M
, (and thus for any
o\in M
,)

\inf_{\tau>0}\mathcal{N}_{o,0}(\tau)\geq\mu_{\infty}.

2.

Hamilton’s trace Harnack inequality holds, i.e.,

\partial_{t}R-2\nabla_{X}R+2\Ric(X,X)\geq 0,

(Harnack)

on
M\times(-\infty,0],

for any vector field
X.

3.

\Ric\geq 0,\quad|{\Rm}|\leq\Lambda R,

(Curv)

everywhere on
M\times(-\infty,0].

4.

g_{t}
has bounded curvature over compact time intervals. (The bound may not be uniform.)

We shall prove a (smooth) pre-compactness theorem for
\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda)
following Perelman’s arguments in
[
Per02
, Section 11]
.

Theorem A.2
.

\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda)
is pre-compact under the Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton convergence, after parabolic rescaling by the scalar curvature.

To be more specific,
let
(M^{n}_{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda),r_{i}>0,
and
x_{i}\in M_{i}
, such that
R_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i})\leq r_{i}^{-2}.

Then by passing to a subsequence,

(M^{n}_{i},r_{i}^{-2}g^{i}_{r_{i}^{2}t},(x_{i},0))\to(M^{n}_{\infty},g^{\infty}_{t},(x_{\infty},0)),

locally smoothly over
(-\infty,0].

(M^{n}_{\infty},g^{\infty}_{t})_{t\leq 0}
is noncollapsed and satisfies (
Harnack
) and (
Curv
) with the same constants
\mu_{\infty},\Lambda
, but
g^{\infty}_{t}
may have unbounded curvature.

Remark A.3
.

As observed in
[
MZ21
, Theorem 1.13]
, (
Harnack
) holds on a steady soliton if
\Ric\geq 0.

Thus, the canonical Ricci flow induced by any noncollapsed steady soliton with
\sec\geq 0
is in
\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},n)
, for some
\mu_{\infty}<0.

Remark A.4
.

By
[
MZ21
, Theorem 1.13]
, assuming the other conditions, condition (C1) is equivalent to Perelman’s notion of
\kappa
-noncollapsing. This was claimed by Perelman under (not essentially) stronger curvature conditions.
Recall that an ancient flow
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}
is (strongly)
\kappa
-noncollapsed, for some
\kappa>0,
if for any
(x,t)\in M\times(-\infty,0]
,

\sup_{B_{t}(x,r)}R\leq r^{-2}\implies|B_{t}(x,r)|_{g_{t}}\geq\kappa\,r^{n}.

Our approach to prove Theorem
A.2
is similar to Ni’s arguments for ancient Kähler-Ricci flows. Similar arguments also apply to steady solitons. See, e.g.,
[
DZ18
,
CDM22
]
.

First, we prove that any ancient flow in this class has zero aymptotic volume growth (AVR), which relies on the fact that any non-flat shrinker has zero AVR. This was proved by Carrillo-Ni in
[
CaN10
, Corollary 1.1]
. Recall that the AVR of a Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
\Ric\geq 0
is defined as

{\rm AVR}(g):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{|B(o,r)|}{r^{n}},

where
o\in M
. Clearly, AVR does not depend on the choice of
o.

We need the following simple Lemma.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\in[-T,0]}
be a complete Ricci flow with

0\leq\Ric\leq\Lambda,

uniformly on
M\times[-T,0]
, for some
\Lambda\geq 1,T>0.
Then

{\rm AVR}(g_{-T})\geq{\rm AVR}(g_{0}).

Proof.

By Perelman’s distance expanding estimates,
[
Per02
, Lemma 8.3]
,

\partial_{t}|xy|_{t}\geq-5n\Lambda,

for any
t\in[-T,0],x,y\in M.
So

|xy|_{0}-|xy|_{-T}\geq-5n\Lambda T,

and thus

B_{0}(x,r)\subseteq B_{-T}(x,5n\Lambda T+r),

for any
r>0.

For any measurable subset
\Omega\subseteq M,
and any
t\in[-T,0],

\partial_{t}|\Omega|_{t}=-\int_{\Omega}R\,dg_{t}\leq 0.

Thus,

|B_{-T}(x,5n\Lambda T+r)|_{-T}\geq|B_{0}(x,r)|_{0}.

The conclusion follows by dividing
r^{n}
and then taking
r\to\infty.

∎

Theorem A.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda).

Then Perelman’s asymptotic shrinkers exist, and

{\rm AVR}(g_{0})=0.

Proof.

The proof is the same as that of
[
MZ21
, Corollary 5.2]
. We rely on the fact that non-flat shrinkers have zero AVR, which was proved by Carrillo and Ni in
[
CaN10
, Corollary 1.1]
.
We briefly sketch the proof.

By Perelman’s arguments, (see, e.g.,
[
MZ21
]
,) Perelman’s asymptotic shrinkers exist assuming the Harnack inequality and (
Curv
).
That is, for any
\tau_{i}\to\infty,
taking
p_{i}
such that
\ell(p_{i},\tau_{i})\leq n/2,

(M^{n},\tau_{i}^{-1}g_{\tau_{i}t},(p_{i},-1))\to(M_{\infty}^{n},g^{\infty}_{t},(p_{\infty},-1)),

for some shrinker
(M_{\infty}^{n},g^{\infty}_{t}),
over
(-\infty,0).
Here,
\ell
refers to Perelman’s
\ell
-function in
[
Per02
, Section 11]
.
Suppose to the contrary that
{\rm AVR}(g_{0})\geq c,
for some
c>0
.
By Lemma
A.5
, for any
t\leq 0,

{\rm AVR}(g_{t})\geq{\rm AVR}(g_{0})\geq c.

Passing to the limit,
{\rm AVR}(g^{\infty}_{-1})>0,
which is a contradiction to
[
CaN10
, Corollary 1.1]
.

∎

For completeness, we carry out Perelman’s arguments based on the result above about zero AVR.
For simplicity, we write

|B(x,r)|_{g}:=|B_{g}(x,r)|_{g}.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda).

Then for any
\theta\in(0,1),
there is a constant
C=C(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda,\theta)
with the following property.
If
x\in M,s>0
satisfy

\frac{|B(x,s)|_{g_{0}}}{s^{n}}\geq\theta,

then

s^{2}R\leq C,\quad\text{ on }B_{0}(x,s).

As a consequence of (
Harnack
),

s^{2}R\leq C,\quad\text{ on }B_{0}(x,s)\times(-\infty,0].

Proof.

Suppose not. Then there exist
(M_{i}^{n},g^{i}_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda),\bar{x}_{i},y_{i}\in M_{i},\bar{s}_{i}>0,

for some fixed
n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda,

such that

\frac{|B(\bar{x}_{i},\bar{s}_{i})|_{g^{i}_{0}}}{\bar{s}_{i}^{n}}\geq\theta,\quad|\bar{x}_{i}y_{i}|_{g^{i}_{0}}\leq s_{i},\quad\bar{s}_{i}^{2}R_{g^{i}_{0}}(y_{i})\to\infty.

In the following, we omit the subindecies
g^{i}_{0}
when it is clear from the context.
By a standard point picking, e.g.,
[
KL08
, Lemma H.1]
, there exist
s_{i}\leq\bar{s}_{i},x_{i}\in B(\bar{x}_{i},8\bar{s}_{i})
such that

s_{i}^{2}R(x_{i})\geq\bar{s}_{i}^{2}R(y_{i})\to\infty,

and

R\leq 2R(x_{i}),\quad\text{ on }B(x_{i},s_{i}).

Moreover, since
|x_{i}\bar{x}_{i}|<8\bar{s}_{i},s_{i}\leq\bar{s}_{i}
, by the volume monotonicity,

\frac{|B(x_{i},s_{i})|}{s_{i}^{n}}\geq\frac{|B(x_{i},10\bar{s}_{i})|}{(10\bar{s}_{i})^{n}}\geq\frac{|B(\bar{x}_{i},\bar{s}_{i})|}{(10\bar{s}_{i})^{n}}\geq 10^{-n}\theta>0.

(A.1)

Let

r_{i}^{-2}:=R_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i}),\quad\tilde{g}^{i}_{t}:=r_{i}^{-2}g_{r_{i}^{2}t}^{i}.

By (
Harnack
),

R_{\tilde{g}^{i}}\leq 2,\quad\text{ on }B_{\tilde{g}^{i}_{0}}(x_{i},s_{i}/r_{i})\times(-\infty,0].

Recall that
s_{i}/r_{i}\to\infty.

Since each ancient flow
\tilde{g}^{i}
is noncollapsed with a uniform entropy bound, by Hamilton’s compactness theorem, (e.g.
[
KL08
, Corollary E.2]
,)

(M_{i}^{n},\tilde{g}^{i}_{t},x_{i})\to(M_{\infty}^{n},g^{\infty}_{t},x_{\infty}),

locally smoothly over
(-\infty,0],
by possibly passing to a subsequence.

Clearly,
(M_{\infty}^{n},g^{\infty}_{t})\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda).

By (
A.1
) and the volume monotonicity,

\frac{|B(x_{i},A)|_{\tilde{g}^{i}_{0}}}{A^{n}}\geq 10^{-n}\theta,

for any
A<\infty,
if
i
is large. Thus,
{\rm AVR}(g^{\infty}_{0})>0.

This is a contradiction to Theorem
A.6
.

∎

We then prove a compactness result with additional assumptions on volume noncollapsing.

Lemma A.8

(
[
KL08
, Corollary 42.1-(3)]
)
.

Let
(M^{n}_{i},g^{i}_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda),
and
x_{i}\in M_{i}.

If

R_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i})\leq 1,\quad\frac{|B(x_{i},r)|_{g^{i}_{0}}}{r^{n}}\geq\theta,

for some
r,\theta>0,
independent of
i,
then by passing to a subsequence,

(M_{i}^{n},g^{i}_{t},(x_{i},0))\to(M^{n}_{\infty},g^{\infty}_{t},(x_{\infty},0)),

locally smoothly over
(-\infty,0]
.

Proof.

The proof is a direct application of Proposition
A.7
and the standard volume comparison.
∎

The weak compactness theorem implies the stronger version, Theorem
A.2
.

Proof of Theorem
A.2
.

By rescaling, we may assume that
R_{g^{i}_{0}}(x_{i})=1.

It suffices to verify that there is a constant
\theta>0
independent of
i
such that

|B(x_{i},1)|_{g^{i}_{0}}\geq\theta,

for all
i.
Then we may apply the weaker version, Lemma
A.8
above.

Suppose to the contrary that, by passing to a subsequence,

|B(x_{i},1)|_{g^{i}_{0}}\to 0.

(A.2)

By the volume mononicity, there is
s_{i}<1
such that

\frac{|B(x_{i},s_{i})|}{s_{i}^{n}}=\frac{\omega_{n}}{2},

where
\omega_{n}
denotes the volume of the standard unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We claim that
s_{i}\to 0.

Proof of the claim.

Suppose to the contrary that
s_{i}\geq a
for some
a\in(0,1).

Then

\frac{\omega_{n}}{2}=\frac{|B(x_{i},s_{i})|}{s_{i}^{n}}\leq\frac{|B(x_{i},a)|}{a^{n}}\leq\frac{|B(x_{i},1)|}{a^{n}}\to 0,

which is a contradiction.
∎

We consider

\tilde{g}^{i}_{t}:=s_{i}^{-2}g^{i}_{s_{i}^{2}t}.

For the rescaled metric,

R_{\tilde{g}^{i}_{0}}(x_{i})\leq s_{i}^{2}\to 0,\quad|B(x_{i},1)|_{\tilde{g}^{i}_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{n}.

By Lemma
A.8
, by passing to a subsequence,

(M^{i},\tilde{g}^{i}_{t},(x_{i},0))\to(M^{n}_{\infty},g^{\infty}_{t},(x_{\infty},0)),

locally smoothly over
(-\infty,0].

R(x_{\infty})=0
on
M_{\infty}
, and thus
(M_{\infty},g^{\infty}_{t})
must be flat due to the maximum principle and (
Curv
).
By (
A.2
),
{\rm AVR}(g^{\infty}_{0})=0,
which is a contradiction to the fact that the limit flow is noncollapsed (by Perelman’s
\kappa
-noncollapsedness or
[
Bam20a
, Theorem 6.1]
).
∎

As a standard consequence of the compactness theorem, we have

Corollary A.9
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda).

Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N},

|{\nabla^{k}\Rm}|\leq C_{k}R^{\frac{k+2}{2}},

everywhere on
M\times(-\infty,0]
, where
C_{k}
depends on
k,n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda.

We also have Perelman’s long range curvature estimate. Here, we follow Brendle’s formulation in
[
Bre22
, Theorem 4.2]
.

Theorem A.10
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda).

There is an increasing function
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
depending on
n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda,
such that

R(y,t)\leq R(x,t)\cdot\omega\Big(R(x,t)|xy|_{t}^{2}\Big),

for any
x,y\in M,t\leq 0.

With the help of Perelman’s long range curvature estimate, we have the following general splitting theorem.

Theorem A.11
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{t})_{t\leq 0}\in\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},n)
with
\sec\geq 0
on
M\times(-\infty,0].
Then for any
x_{i}\to\infty,
if
r_{i}^{-2}=R(x_{i},0),
then
by passing to a subsequence,

(M,r_{i}^{-2}g_{r_{i}^{2}t},(x_{i},0))\to(N^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R},\bar{g}_{t}+dz^{2},((\bar{x},0),0)),

in the sense of Cheeger-Gromov-Hamilton over
(-\infty,0],

for some noncollapsed ancient flow
(N,\bar{g}_{t})
.

For completeness, we include Brendle’s proof of this theorem,
[
Bre22
, Corollary 4.4]
, using Perelman’s long range curvature estimate.

Proof of Theorem
A.11
.

Let
x_{i}\to\infty
. Fix
o\in M.

By Perelman’s long range curvature estimate,

0<R(o,0)\leq R(x_{i},0)\cdot\omega\Big(R(x_{i},0)|ox_{i}|_{0}^{2}\Big).

We claim that

R(x_{i},0)|ox_{i}|_{0}^{2}\to\infty.

In fact, suppose to the contrary that
R(x_{i},0)|ox_{i}|_{0}^{2}\leq A<\infty
, for some subsequence. Then

R(x_{i},0)\geq R(o,0)/\omega(A)>0,

and thus
R(x_{i},0)|ox_{i}|_{0}^{2}\to\infty
, which is a contradiction.

Let

r_{i}^{-2}=R(x_{i},0),\quad g^{i}_{t}:=r_{i}^{-2}g_{r_{i}^{2}t}.

By the compactness, Theorem
A.2
, by passing to a subsequence,

(M,g^{i}_{t},x_{i})\to(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{t},\bar{x})
, smoothly over
(-\infty,0].

By the standard theorem of splitting at infinity, e.g.,
[
MT07
, Theorem 5.35]
,
(\overline{M},\bar{g}_{t})
splits off a line.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
1.2
follows by Remark
A.3
and Theorem
A.11
.

∎

We mention an application of the curvature derivative estimates to steady solitons due to Deng and Zhu in
[
DZ20a
,
DZ20b
]
.

Theorem A.12

(Deng-Zhu)
.

Let
(M^{n},g,f)
be a steady soliton such that its cononical Ricci flow is in
\mathcal{A}(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda)
.
Suppose that

R(o)\geq a,

for some
o\in M,a>0.

Then for any
\rho>0,x\in B(o,\rho),t\leq 0,

(1-t)R(x,t)\geq c(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda,\rho,a)>0.

Our improvement here is that the constant
c
is universal depending only on
n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda,\rho,a
. Since
\Phi_{t}(x)
moves away from
x
linearly in
-t
, this is a inverse linear lower bound for the scalar curvature.
See also
[
CCMZ23
, Corollary 2.7]
for a inverse quadratic lower bound of
R
for 4d steady soliton without any curvature positivity conditions.

Proof.

Fix
o\in\mathcal{C}_{f}.
For any
x\in M,t\leq 1,
along
\Phi_{t}(x),
by Corollary
A.9
,

\partial_{t}R(\Phi_{t}(x))=\Delta R+2|{\Ric}|^{2}\leq CR^{2},

where
C
depends on
n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda.

By integration, for
t\leq 0,

\frac{1}{R(x,1)}-\frac{1}{R(x,t)}\geq-C(1-t).

As a standard consequence of (
Harnack
), for any
x\in B(o,\rho),

R(x,1)\geq R(o)\cdot\exp\left(-{|xo|^{2}/2}\right)\geq ae^{-\rho^{2}/2}.

Thus, for any
x\in B(o,\rho),t\leq 0,

(1-t)R(x,t)\geq c(n,\mu_{\infty},\Lambda,\rho,a).

A summary of categorical structures in 𝐏𝐨𝐥𝐲 \mathbf{Poly}
David I. Spivak

Abstract
In this document, we collect a list of categorical structures on the category 𝐏𝐨𝐥𝐲 \mathbf{Poly} of polynomial functors. There is no implied claim that this list is in any way complete. It includes: infinitely-many symmetric monoidal closed structures that distribute over + + , an asymmetric monoidal product, various duoidalities and coclosures, as well as a discussion of monoids and comonoids for some of these. We also discuss various adjunctions of which 𝐏𝐨𝐥𝐲 \mathbf{Poly} is a part, including the free monad and cofree comonad and their interaction with various monoidal structures.

Chapter 1
Background and notation

A polynomial functor
p\colon\mathbf{Set}\to\mathbf{Set}
is any functor that’s isomorphic to a coproduct of representables

p\coloneqq\sum_{I:p(1)}{\mathcal{y}}^{p[I]}.

We will typically use the above notation—which we call

Putting polynomials in this form lets us consider them as combinatorial objects. We refer to elements of
p(1)
as
positions
of
p
and, for each
I:p(1)
, we refer to the elements of

as

The functor

corresponding to

is called its

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}\colon\mathbf{Poly}

\displaystyle\to\mathbf{Set}^{\mathbf{Set}}

(1)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto X\mapsto\sum_{I:p(1)}X^{p[I]}

(2)

The functor
\mathrm{Ext}
preserves coproducts and is fully faithful, meaning that a morphism between polynomials is a natural transformation

. What we denote

\mathrm{Ext}(p)(1)
. By the Yoneda lemma and universal property of coproducts, a morphism in

consists of a function

and, for each

a function

. A map

The category of polynomial functors and morphisms between them is denoted
\mathbf{Poly}
; the wide subcategory of polynomial functors and cartesian morphisms between them is denoted
\mathbf{Poly}^{\textnormal{cart}}
. Both of these have all limits and colimits. The forgetful functor
\mathbf{Poly}\to\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{Set},\mathbf{Set})
preserves limits and coproducts. The forgetful functor
\mathbf{Poly}^{\textnormal{cart}}\to\mathbf{Fun}(\mathbf{Set},\mathbf{Set})
preserves all limits and colimits.

Chapter 3
Substitution product

There is a nonsymmetric monoidal structure on
\mathbf{Poly}
given by composing polynomials. Its unit is
{\mathcal{y}}
and its monoidal product is given by the following formula:

p\mathbin{\triangleleft}q\coloneqq\sum_{I:p(1)}\sum_{J\colon p[I]\to q(1)}{\mathcal{y}}^{\sum\limits_{i:p[I]}q[Ji]}

p\mathbin{\triangleleft}q\to p\mathbin{\triangleleft}q^{\prime}

Chapter 4
Monoidal closures

There are closures for
\times
,
\otimes
, and
\ovee_{S}
for each
S:\mathbf{Set}
(
Eqs.

9
,
10
, and
11
),given by

\displaystyle q^{p}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{I:p(1)}q\mathbin{\triangleleft}(p[I]+{\mathcal{y}})

(36)

\displaystyle[p,q]

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{I:p(1)}q\mathbin{\triangleleft}(p[I]\times{\mathcal{y}})

(37)

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{I:p(1)}q\mathbin{\triangleleft}(p[I]+p[I]\times S{\mathcal{y}}+{\mathcal{y}})

(38)

These satisfy the defining universal properties:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Poly}(p^{\prime},q^{p})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Poly}(p^{\prime}\times p,q)

(39)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Poly}(p^{\prime},[p,q])

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Poly}(p^{\prime}\otimes p,q)

(40)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Poly}(p^{\prime},\langle p,q\rangle_{S})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Poly}(p^{\prime}\ovee_{S}p,q)

(41)

The first one,
q^{p}
, is the Cartesian closure; its standard form is not particularly enlightening, and neither is that of the third one,
\langle p,q\rangle
. The second one,
[p,q]
, is what we call the

; it has a very nice standard form:

[p,q]\cong\sum_{\varphi:\mathbf{Poly}(p,q)}{\mathcal{y}}^{\sum\limits_{I:p(1)}q[\varphi_{1}I]}

where
\varphi_{1}\colon p(1)\to q(1)
is the
1
-component of the natural transformation
\varphi
. Another nice representation is

[p,q]\cong\prod_{I:p(1)}\sum_{J:q(1)}\prod_{j:q[J]}\sum_{i:p[I]}{\mathcal{y}}

(42)

The cartesian closure satisfies all the usual arithmetic properties:

\displaystyle q^{0}\cong 1,\quad q^{p_{1}+p_{2}}\cong(q^{p_{1}})\times(q^{p_{2}}),\quad 1^{p}\cong 1,\quad(q_{1}\times q_{2})^{p}\cong q_{1}^{p}\times q_{2}^{p},\quad q^{1}\cong q,\quad q^{p_{1}\times p_{2}}\cong(q^{p_{2}})^{p_{1}}

(43)

The Dirichlet closure has only some of the analogous properties:

\displaystyle[0,p]\cong 1,\qquad[p_{1}+p_{2},q]\cong[p_{1},q]\times[p_{2},q],\qquad[{\mathcal{y}},q]\cong q,\qquad[p_{1}\otimes p_{2},q]\cong[p_{1},[p_{2},q]]

The Dirichlet closure also satisfies the following relation with

[p,1]\cong 1\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad[p,q_{1}\times q_{2}]\cong[p,q_{1}]\times[p,q_{2}].

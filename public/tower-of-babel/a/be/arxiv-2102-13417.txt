Incompatibility in Quantum Parameter Estimation Journal: New J. Phys.
Vittorio Giovannetti

Abstract
In this paper we introduce a measure of genuine quantum incompatibility in the estimation task of multiple parameters, that has a geometric character and is backed by a clear operational interpretation. This measure is then applied to some simple systems in order to track the effect of a local depolarizing noise on the incompatibility of the estimation task. A semidefinite program is described and used to numerically compute the figure of merit when the analytical tools are not sufficient, among these we include an upper bound computable from the symmetric logarithmic derivatives only. Finally we discuss how to obtain compatible models for a general unitary encoding on a finite dimensional probe.



Keywords
: quantum parameter estimation, quantum metrology, quantum measurements, incompatibility.

2
Multiparameter quantum estimation

2.1
Setting and definitions

A prototypical example of multi-parameter quantum metrology is provided by magnetometry
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
50
]
where a spin particle is used as a probe for evaluating the three components of a magnetic field
\boldsymbol{B}:=\left(B_{x},B_{y},B_{z}\right)
. In the most basic scenario the evolution of the particle is given by the unitary transformation
U_{\boldsymbol{B}}:=\exp\left[\rmi\left(B_{x}S_{x}+B_{y}S_{y}+B_{z}S_{z}\right)t\right]
where
S_{i}
for
i=x,y,z
are the components of the spin. By measuring the evolved state of the probe we can hence try infer the values of
B_{x},B_{y},B_{z}
, following the post-processing of the measurements output. What makes this procedure truly quantum in nature is that, fixing the number of experimental repetitions, due to the non-commuting nature of the generators
S_{i}
, any attempt to improve the estimation accuracy of one of the cartesian components of
\boldsymbol{B}
will have a negative impact on the accuracies of the other two
[
51
]
. An exact formalization of this problem can be obtained by considering a more general model where one is asked to determine
d
parameters
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{d}\right)\in\Theta
(an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) that have been encoded in the input state
\rho
of a probing quantum system via a mapping of the form

\displaystyle\rho\rightarrow\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}:=\mathcal{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\left(\rho\right)\;,

(1)

where now
\mathcal{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
is a completely positive, trace-preserving (CPT) transformation
[
52
]
which parametrically depends on
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
and which, at variance with the simplified scenario detailed at the beginning of the section, might include a noise disturbing the process. Given
N
copies of
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
we can now try to recover the needed information by performing on them some (possibly joint) positive operator valued measure (POVM)
{\text{M}}_{N}:=\{E_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}}^{(N)}\}_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}}
whose elements are labelled by a classical outcome variable
{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}
that, without loss of generality
[
53
]
, can be assumed to belong to the same set
\Theta
of
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. Accordingly
{\text{M}}_{N}
can hence be thought as a operation which, starting from
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\otimes N}
, induces a measure on
\Theta
, defined by the conditional probability distribution

\displaystyle P_{{\text{M}}_{N}}({\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\mbox{Tr}\,[E_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}}^{(N)}\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\otimes N}]\;,

(2)

with the stochastic outcome
{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}:=({\hat{\theta}}_{1},{\hat{\theta}}_{2},\dots,{\hat{\theta}}_{d})\in\Theta
playing the role of the estimator of
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. The two most important properties of the estimator
{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}
are the bias vector
\boldsymbol{b}^{(N)}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right):=\left(b_{1}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right),b_{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right),\cdots b_{d}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\right)
, of components

\displaystyle b_{i}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right):=\mathbb{E}\,[{\hat{\theta}}_{i}]-\theta_{i}\;,

(3)

and the mean square error (MSE)
d\times d
matrix
{\Sigma^{(N)}}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
, of elements

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(N)}_{ij}\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right):=\mathbb{E}\,[(\hat{\theta_{i}}-\theta_{i})({{\hat{\theta}}}_{j}-\theta_{j})]\;,

(4)

with
\mathbb{E}
representing the statistical average computed with the probability measure in (
2
). Ideally we would like to deal with estimators that are unbiased, meaning that
\boldsymbol{b}^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0
for all
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in\Theta
, but this may not always be possible. Accordingly in what follows we shall focus on sensing, i.e. we shall measure small variations of the parameters
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
around a known value and assume that we are allowed to employ locally unbiased POVMs at such special point, that is measurements which bias vector
\boldsymbol{b}^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
satisfy the following conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\boldsymbol{b}^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\Big|_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}}=0\;,&\\
\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}\boldsymbol{b}^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\Big|_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}}=0\;,&\forall j=1,\cdots,d\;.\end{array}\right.

For these measurements the quantum Cramér-Rao (QCR) bound
[
3
,
54
]
gives a limit on the precision of the sensing task, formulated as a lower bound on the associated MSE matrix, i.e.

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq\frac{F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{N}\;.

(9)

In this expression
F({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
is the so called quantum Fisher information (QFI) matrix which no longer depends on the selected POVM
\text{M}_{N}
and whose elements can be computed as

\displaystyle F_{ij}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\frac{1}{2}\Tr\Big[\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\left(L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})L_{j}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})+L_{j}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right)\Big]\;,

(10)

with
L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
the symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD)
[
54
]
associated to the
i
th component of the parameter vector
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
, i.e. the operator (possibly dependent on
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
) fulfilling the identity

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}{\partial\theta_{i}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})+L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\Big)\;.

(11)

For a pure state
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=|\psi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}|
the above equation admits as solution

\displaystyle L_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=2\frac{\partial\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\;,

(12)

while in general a solution is
[
54
]

\displaystyle L_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=2\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rme^{-s\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\frac{\partial\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\rme^{-s\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rmd s\;.

(13)

Throughout the paper we will assume the QFI to be limited (i.e.
\lVert{F}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\rVert<\infty
) and non-singular (i.e.
F({\boldsymbol{\theta}})>0
). In particular the last requirement imposes that the maximum value of
d
(the number of parameters) we can allow in our study is upper bounded by
D^{2}-1
with
D
being the dimension of the Hilbert space associated with the probing system (indeed values of
d
greater than such limit will necessarily force a linear dependence between the SLD operators
L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
, leading to a singular QFI matrix).

2.2
Achievability of the multi-parameter QCR bound

In general the multiparameter QCR bound (
9
) cannot be saturated, meaning that there is no locally unbiased POVM
\text{M}_{N}
with a
\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
matrix equal to
F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})/N
or, equivalently, which is capable of saturating the inequality

\displaystyle\Tr\,[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]\geq\frac{1}{N}\Tr\,[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]:=\frac{C_{\text{S}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)}{N}\;,

(14)

for all choices of a positive weight matrix
G\geq 0
. This is the form of metrological incompatibility that will be extensively studied in this paper. In order to better appreciate the meaning of this, suppose that we are interested in the estimation of an analytic function
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\left(\Theta\right)
of the unknown parameters vector
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. The function
f
will be evaluated on the estimator
{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}
extracted from the observations. By expanding to first order the expectation value of
f({\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}})-f\left({\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
we get the expression for the error

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,[(f({\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}})-f({{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}))^{2}]

(15)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\mathbb{E}\,[\partial_{i}f(\boldsymbol{\theta})({{\hat{\theta}}}_{i}-\theta_{i})\partial_{j}f(\boldsymbol{\theta})({{\hat{\theta}}}_{j}-\theta_{j})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\partial_{i}f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\partial_{j}f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\mathbb{E}\,[({{\hat{\theta}}}_{i}-\theta_{i})({{\hat{\theta}}}_{j}-\theta_{j})]\;,

(16)

which can be equivalently written as:

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\langle\partial f(\boldsymbol{\theta})|\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})|\partial f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle=\Tr\,[G(\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]\;,

(17)

where we introduced the rank-
1
weight matrix
G_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\partial_{i}f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\partial_{j}f(\boldsymbol{\theta})=|\partial f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle\!\langle\partial f(\boldsymbol{\theta})|
, with
|\partial f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Written in this form we can now use (
14
) to cast a bound on the accuracy of the estimation of
f\left({\boldsymbol{{\theta}}}\right)
. As a matter of fact a rank-
1

G
can always be thought as the weight matrix of some function
f({{\boldsymbol{\theta}}})
. We will see that according to our definitions a rank-
1

G
manifests no incompatibility, indeed we will see that the error associated to a single tangent vector
|\partial f(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
on the statistical manifold can saturate the ultimate QFI (this can be understood e.g. from the upper bound (
31
) discussed in section
3.5
below, which, for
G
rank-
1
, collapses to
C_{\text{S}}\left(G,{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\right)
). On the contrary the gap manifests itself when the weight matrix
G
is at least rank-
2
. This situations arises as we try to estimate at the same time multiple functions of the parameters
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
, named
f_{1}({{\boldsymbol{\theta}}})
,
f_{2}({{\boldsymbol{\theta}}})
, …,
f_{K}({{\boldsymbol{\theta}}})
, which could also just be the components
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{d}
of the vector
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
. To each of the functions we associate a weight
g_{i}\geq 0
, then the total error is the weighted sum of the errors for the estimation of each
f_{i}\left({{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\right)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\sum_{i=1}^{K}g_{i}\Tr\,[|\partial f_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle\!\langle\partial f_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})|\Sigma^{(N)}]=\Tr\,[G(\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}],\;

(18)

with
G(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\sum_{i=1}^{K}g_{i}|\partial f_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle\!\langle\partial f_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})|\geq 0
.

3
Incompatibility measure

In this section we introduce a figure of merit to gauge the incompatibility of multi-parameter estimation procedures,
which is based on the assumption that the agent is allowed to perform on the probes arbitrary locally unbiased POVMs. After showing its well-definedness we clarify its interpretation in the framework of information geometry. We then provide a linear program to compute this incompatibility measure and an analytical upper bound. The figure of merit is then generalized to separable measurements.

3.1
Definition of the figure of merit.

Given the encoding (
1
) and a generic weight matrix
G
, from (
14
) it follows that a bona-fide evaluation of the precision attainable with a locally unbiased POVM
\text{M}_{N}
can be obtained by considering the ratio

\displaystyle r_{N}\left(G,\text{M}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right):=\frac{N\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}\geq 1,

(19)

where
{\Sigma^{(N)}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
is the MSE matrix (
4
) associated with

\text{M}_{N}
. As indicated by the notation the quantity (
19
) exhibits an explicitly functional dependence on
G
and
\text{M}_{N}
which we remove by considering the term

Definition 3.1

(Incompatibility figure of merit for
N
probes)

\displaystyle r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\inf_{\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}}\sup_{G\geq 0}r_{N}\left(G,\text{M}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\;,

(20)

where now
\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}
indicates the set of locally unbiased POVM on
N
copies of the probes. For any given elements
\text{M}_{N}
of
\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}
the
\sup_{G\geq 0}
selects the weight matrix that has the reachable precision
\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]
as far away from the information content
\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]/N
as possible. Then we minimize on
\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}
to compute the best worst case scenario, as in a typical min-max definition
[
55
]
. The figure of merit
r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
quantifies the competition between optimal measurements for different parameters, and has a clear operational meaning. Because of the QCR bound in (
19
) we have
r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq 1
and the
N
define a fully compatible model only when
r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=1
. This is true if and only if
\exists\,\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}
(possibly dependent on
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
) for which in (
19
) equality holds
\forall\,G
. On the contrary
r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})>1
indicates the presence of incompatibility and happens if and only if
\forall\,\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}

\exists\,G\geq 0
such that in (
19
) the strict inequality holds. In the asymptotic scenario of infinitely many probes available we introduce

Definition 3.2

(Incompatibility figure of merit)

\displaystyle\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\;,

(21)

which always exists and from
r_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
inherits the property
\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq 1
. In particular in this case we have
\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=1
if and only if there exists a sequence of
\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}
, which, for all
G\geq 0
, allows us to saturate the inequality (
19
) asymptotically in
N
. It is worth noticing that the incompatibility figure of merit could be defined for locally asymptotic covariant measurements (LAC)
[
42
,
48
]
as well, and it would be exactly equal to
\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, see
A
for the details.

3.2
Well-definedness of the figure of merit

We now briefly show that
r_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
in (
20
) is invariant under reparametrization and therefore a well-defined quantity. This translates to
\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, which is therefore a well defined property of the statistical manifold (see section
3.3
). Consider a reparametrization
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}={{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\left({\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right)
having an invertible Jacobian
J_{ij}:=\frac{\partial\theta_{i}({\boldsymbol{\eta}})}{\partial\eta_{j}}
. Then the MSE matrix for the parameters
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, defined in (
4
), can be written
\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=J\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\eta})J^{t}
, where
\Sigma^{(N)}_{ij}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}\right):=\mathbb{E}\,[(\hat{\eta_{i}}-\eta_{i})({{\hat{\eta}}}_{j}-\eta_{j})]
is the MSE matrix for the parameters
\boldsymbol{\eta}
. Similarly we write the inverse of the QFI matrix as
F^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=JF^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})J^{t}
, with
F(\boldsymbol{\eta})
computed from the symmetric logarithmic derivatives
L_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
, which differ from the definition in (
11
) in the derivatives, which are taken with respect to
\eta_{i}
. Its now easy to show that
r_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=r_{N}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
. The action of the Jacobian matrix on the MSE matrix and on the QFI can be moved on
G
, that becomes
J^{t}GJ
both at numerator and at denominator of the ratio in (
19
), while multiplying respectively
\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
and
F^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
. Then we observe that the set of positive matrices is invariant under congruence for an invertible matrix, i.e.
J^{t}\{G\geq 0\}J=\{G\geq 0\}
, and therefore we get

\displaystyle r_{N}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\inf_{\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}}\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{N\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right]}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right]}:=r_{N}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\;.

(22)

It worth stressing that, by construction the quantity
\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
only depends on the input probe state
\rho
, the encoding
\mathcal{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
, and the specific point of interest
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. It is an intrinsic property of the statistical manifold defined by the trajectories (
1
). The need for a reparametrization invariant measure of incompatibility was already pointed out in
[
56
]
, in which the figure of merit
\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
was independently discovered.

3.3
Geometric interpretation

The parameters
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\in\Theta
can be interpreted as coordinates defining via the map (
1
) a submanifold of the space of states
\mathfrak{S}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
, called the statistical manifold. The QFI matrix, being a positive semidefinite matrix can be thought as a Riemannian metric on this manifold. This metric is generally non trivial as it explicitly depends on the coordinates
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
and may have intrinsic curvature. The QFI is said to be a distinguishability metric
[
57
,
58
]
: given two very near states
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
and
\rho_{{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}+d{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}
, their infinitesimal distance in the QFI metric is

\displaystyle ds^{2}:=\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\rmd\theta_{i}\rmd\theta_{j}=2\left(1-\sqrt{\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}},\rho_{{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}+d{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}\right)}\right),

(23)

which is negatively correlated with the fidelity
\mathcal{F}\left(\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}},\rho_{{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}+d{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}\right)
between
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
and
\rho_{{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}+d{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}
, defined as
\mathcal{F}\left(\rho,\sigma\right):=\left[\Tr\left(\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\right)\right]^{2}

[
59
]
. In order to gain information about
{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
it is thus better to choose the probe state
\rho
such that in the statistical manifold the codified state
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
is highly distinguishable from its neighbors
\rho_{{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}+d{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}}
, and has therefore the highest statistical distance from them as possible. This picture clarifies why the inverse of the distinguishability metric, i.e.
F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
, gives the precision to which a single point
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
can be identified in
\Theta
, given the quantum state
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
. For
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\otimes N}
the relevant metric is
F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})/N
. When a measurement is performed and an estimator
{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}
is chosen there is a new Riemannian metric insisting on the statistical manifold: the positive semidefinite
\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
matrix. The key question is if one can find a POVM
{\text{M}}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{{\text{(LU)}}}
with a MSE metric that fully adapts to the underling quantum metric
F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})/N
of the manifold, i.e. if the inequality (
9
) can be saturated (at a certain point
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
). In general this is not possible. Let us introduce a representation of
G
as a sum of projectors
|v_{i}\rangle\!\langle v_{i}|
, each weighted with
g_{i}\geq 0
, i.e.
G:=\sum_{i}g_{i}|v_{i}\rangle\!\langle v_{i}|
, where
|v_{i}\rangle
are vectors in the tangent plane of the statistical manifold at point
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\Tr\,[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}g_{i}\langle v_{i}|F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})|v_{i}\rangle\;.

(24)

According to the above expression, the information content is a weighted combination of the distinguishability of the manifold in different directions defined by
|v_{i}\rangle
, see figure
1
for a 2D representation. This has to be compared with the experimental weighted distinguishability, i.e.

\displaystyle\Tr\,[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]=\sum_{i}g_{i}\langle v_{i}|\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})|v_{i}\rangle,

(25)

given by a particular measurement.

Figure 1:
Representation of a 2D statistical manifold with its tangent space at a point
\boldsymbol{\theta}
and two directions
|v_{1}\rangle
and
|v_{2}\rangle
on this plane.

The whole point of the non commutative nature of the manifold is the impossibility to saturate the distinguishability in more than one direction at the same time. By taking

\displaystyle\sup_{G\geq 0}\tfrac{N\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}=\sup_{g_{i}\geq 0,|v_{i}\rangle}\tfrac{N\sum_{i}g_{i}\langle v_{i}|\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})|v_{i}\rangle}{\sum_{i}g_{i}\langle v_{i}|F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})|v_{i}\rangle}\;,

(26)

we measure the worst case fitting of the
\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
matrix on the metric
F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})/N
at a point
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
, spanning all possible sets of tangent vectors and weights. Then we minimize on the classical metric (and hence on the POVM) to find the most adapt one. By taking the asymptotic limit of infinitely many probes (through the
\liminf
) we have completed the analysis of the definition (
21
) from the geometrical point of view. We sum up everything and say that
\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
measures, in the asymptotic scenario, the failure of finding a metric on the statistical manifold, stemming from a measurement, which fully adapts to the underlying quantum metric (in all directions) at a specific point
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
.

3.4
Computation of the figure of merit

We would like to apply the existing results in local estimation theory to compute the incompatibility figure of merit. This requires the exchange of the
\sup
and the
\inf
in (
20
). In
B
we do that and show

\displaystyle\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{C(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{C_{\text{S}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)}\;,

(27)

with
C_{\text{S}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)
defined in (
14
), and with
the numerator given by the quantity

\displaystyle C(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\min_{N\leq n}\inf_{\text{M}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}}N\Tr\,[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]\;.

(28)

In
[
42
]
it has been proved that
C(G,\boldsymbol{\theta})=C_{\text{H}}(G,\boldsymbol{\theta})
, where
C_{\text{H}}(G,\boldsymbol{\theta})
is the Holevo-Cramér-Rao bound functional
C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})

[
60
,
41
]
. Exploiting this facts we can use (
27
) to deduce the following equality

\displaystyle\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{C_{\text{S}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)}\;.

(29)

In
[
61
]
the upper bound
C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\leq 2C_{\text{S}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})
is given, which implies
\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\leq 2
. Because of this it makes sense to introduce

Definition 3.3

(Incompatibility measure)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})-1\;,

(30)

as a proper quantifier of incompatibility: by construction it belongs to the interval
\left[0,1\right]
with
\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0
indicating full compatibility, while
\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=1
maximal incompatibility. The Holevo-Cramér-Rao bound can be computed via the semidefinite linear program in
[
49
]
, which can be adapted to compute
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, as reported in
C
.

3.5
Upper bound on
\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

In this section we propose an upper bound on
\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
that relies only on the computation of the symmetric logarithmic derivatives defined in (
11
). It is essentially based on
C_{\text{Z}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)

[
62
]
, a well know upper bound on
C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})
, which reads

\displaystyle C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\text{Z}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})

(31)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Tr\left[GF^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]+\Tr\text{Abs}\left[GF^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]\;,

where
A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
contains the expectation values of the commutators of the SLDs:

\displaystyle A_{ij}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\frac{1}{2\rmi}\Tr\left[\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\left[L_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}),L_{j}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]\right].

(32)

In writing (
31
) we used
\Tr\text{Abs}\left[G\cdot R\right]:=\Tr|\sqrt{G}R\sqrt{G}|
, with
|X|:=\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}
.
Combining (
31
) and (
29
) we get

\displaystyle\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\leq 1+\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{\Tr{\text{Abs}}\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}:=\underline{r}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\;.

(33)

The above inequality shows that a sufficient condition to have compatibility is
A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0
. In
D
we compute explicitly
\sup_{G\geq 0}
in (
33
) and obtain

\displaystyle\underline{r}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=1+\lVert F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\rVert\;,

(34)

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
is the operator norm. This translates to an upper bound on
\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
, i.e.

Theorem 3.1

(Upper bound on the incompatibility measure)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\leq\mathcal{I}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\underline{r}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})-1=\lVert F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\rVert\;.

(35)

This strengthen the interpretation of
A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
as a measure of incompatibility
[
51
]
. The upper bound
\mathcal{I}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
was first defined in
[
63
]
and called
\mathcal{R}
. It has already been used as a measure of incompatibility and “quantumness” and applied to qubits
[
56
]
and many-body systems
[
63
,
64
]
. By defining
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
we offer a more informative definition of incompatibility. It is noteworthy that for a
D
-invariant model
[
65
]
this bound is saturated and
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
.

3.6
Incompatibility for separable measurements

We now go back to the first definition of a figure of merit presented in (
21
), but consider the minimization in (
20
) to be performed only on the locally unbiased separable measurements subset
\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{(LU-S)}}}
of
\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}
which operate locally on
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\otimes N}
. This brings to the definitions

\displaystyle r_{N}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\inf_{{\text{M}}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{(LU-S)}}}}\sup_{G\geq 0}r_{N}\left(G,{\text{M}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\;,

(36)

and

Definition 3.4

(Incompatibility figure of merit for separable measurements)

\displaystyle\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}r_{N}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\;.

(37)

Now we apply the result of
[
66
]
, which gives us a lower bound on the precision of the estimation with
N
probes when we use a measurement
{\text{M}}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{(LU-S)}}}
. The bound reads

\displaystyle N\Tr\,[G\cdot{\Sigma^{(N)}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]\geq\frac{1}{D-1}\left(\Tr\sqrt{F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})GF^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})}\right)^{2}\;,

(38)

where
{\Sigma^{(N)}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
is the MSE matrix of
{\text{M}}_{N}
and
D
is the size of the Hilbert space of the single probe
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
. This translates to a lower bound on
r_{N}^{\text{s}}\left(G,{\text{M}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)

\forall\,N
, i.e.

\displaystyle r_{N}^{\text{s}}\left(G,{\text{M}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\geq\frac{\left(\Tr\sqrt{F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})GF^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})}\right)^{2}}{(D-1)\Tr[F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})GF^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]}\;,

(39)

which propagates to the definition of
\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
, giving

Theorem 3.2

(Lower bound for separable measurements)

\displaystyle\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq\frac{d}{D-1}\;,

(40)

where we have compute explicitly
\sup_{G\geq 0}
using the AM-QM inequality and its saturation. Observe that the inequality (
40
) bares no reference to the details of the encoding process (
1
) and that it is non trivial only if the number
d
of parameters we have to estimate is larger than or equal to
D-1
. The manipulations of
B
are valid also for the class of measurements
\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{(LU-S)}}}
, because only the local unbiasedness is required in their proof. Therefore we can write

\displaystyle\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{C^{\text{s}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)}{C_{\text{S}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)}\;,

(41)

where now

\displaystyle C^{\text{s}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\min_{N\leq n}\inf_{{\text{M}}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{(LU-S)}}}}N\Tr\,[G\cdot\Sigma^{(N)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]\;.

(42)

At least for the case of a qubit probe (
D=2
) the above expression allows us to exactly compute
\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
. Indeed as shown in
[
60
,
66
]
for this model one has

\displaystyle C^{\text{s}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\left(\Tr\sqrt{F^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})GF^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})}\right)^{2}\;,

(43)

leading to

Corollary 1

\displaystyle D=2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=d\;,

(44)

which shows that in the case of a single qubit, multi-parameter estimation always exhibit incompatibility for separable locally unbiased measurements (remember that our analysis is explicitly restricted to the cases where
d\leq D^{2}-1=3
).

3.7
Hierarchy of incompatibility measures

Whether a certain estimation process is compatible or not depends on the set of measurements
\mathcal{M}_{N}
that we are allowed to perform. Consider a hierarchy of POVM sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{(2)}_{N}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{(k)}_{N}\quad\quad\forall\,N\;,

(45)

we define the figure of merit
r^{\left(i\right)}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
as in (
20
), but taking
\mathcal{M}^{(i)}_{N}
as the domain of the infimum. By construction the
r^{\left(i\right)}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
satisfy the following hierarchy of inequalities

\displaystyle r^{(1)}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq r^{(2)}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq\cdots\geq r^{(k)}_{N}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\quad\quad\forall\,N\;,

(46)

which carries over to

\displaystyle\underline{r}^{(1)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq\underline{r}^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq\cdots\geq\underline{r}^{(k)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\;,

(47)

when taking the proper
N\rightarrow\infty
limits (
21
). For example the space of separable locally unbiased measurement is a subset of the set of all locally unbiased measurements, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{\tiny{(LU-S)}}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{N}^{\text{(LU)}}\;,

(48)

which means
\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\geq\underline{r}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
.

4.1
Incompatibility for a one-qubit probe

First of all we analyze the case of a single qubit probe. The fact that the figure of merit is parameterization invariant allows for an elegant exact solution of the qubit model for whatever probe state and encoded phases under depolarization noise. In this example the measure
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and its upper bound
\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
will coincide. After the encoding by
U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
in (
49
), the probe undergoes the action of the noise map
\Lambda_{\lambda}
in (
50
), so that its final state
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
is described by the mapping (
1
) with
\mathcal{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}(\rho):=\Lambda_{\lambda}(U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rho U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\dagger})=U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\Lambda_{\lambda}(\rho)U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\dagger}\;.

(51)

The purity of the encoded state
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}=\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left(\rho\right)
is independent on
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, this makes the statistical model D-invariant
[
62
,
65
]
, and allows us to conclude that the Holevo-Cramér-Rao bound
C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})
coincides with
C_{\text{Z}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})
defined in (
31
), therefore the inequality (
33
) is saturated (
\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\mathcal{I}^{\star}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
), and the incompatibility can be computed from the symmetric logarithmic derivatives only. We consider an arbitrary qubit probe state
\rho:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathds{1}+\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)
. Its Bloch vector is
\boldsymbol{a}:=\left(a_{x},a_{y},a_{z}\right)
, with
\Tr\rho^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\lVert\boldsymbol{a}\rVert^{2})
. After the encoding the Bloch vector of
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\left(\rho\right)
is
\boldsymbol{a}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=\lambda\left(a_{x}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),a_{y}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),a_{z}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)
. We can perform an implicitly defined change of variables
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\rightarrow(\alpha,\beta)
, that brings us to
\boldsymbol{a}_{(\alpha,\beta)}=\lambda\,\sqrt{2\Tr\rho^{2}-1}(\cos\alpha\cos\beta,\cos\alpha\sin\beta,\sin\alpha)
. For this model
[
69
]
we compute the matrices
F(\alpha,\beta)
and the
A(\alpha,\beta)
, which are

\displaystyle F(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\Tr\rho^{2}-1}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{2}&0\\
0&\lambda^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha\end{pmatrix}\;,

\displaystyle A(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\Tr\rho^{2}-1}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\lambda^{3}\cos\alpha\\
\lambda^{3}\cos\alpha&0\end{pmatrix}\;,

that substituted in (
35
) give

Theorem 4.1

(Incompatibility measure for a depolarized qubit two phase model)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)=\mathcal{I}(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)=\sqrt{2\Tr\rho^{2}-1}\,|\lambda|\quad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}\,,\;\forall\,\rho\;.

(52)

Equation (
52
) reveals that the noise level intensity controls directly the compatibility. Indeed for fixed input the value of
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)
reaches its maximum in the noiseless scenario (
\lambda\rightarrow 1
) providing full incompatibility
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)\rightarrow 1
for pure input states. On the contrary as the noise sends
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
to the completely mixed state (
\lambda\rightarrow 0
) the codified information is dissipated and the compatibility increases, indeed
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)\rightarrow 0
. Fundamentally the same result was discover in
[
56
]
for qubit tomography. We finally remind the reader that, as anticipated at the end of section
3.6
, for a single qubit we get
\underline{r}^{\text{s}}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=d=2
independently on the noise. Again this result is valid
\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}
and for every input probe
\rho
.

In this section we study the incompatibility for a generic unitary encoding of

4.4
Incompatibility for a
D
-dimensional probe

Let us consider a single-probe scenario where the state of the system is described by the density matrix

\displaystyle\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\lambda}(U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\dagger})=U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\Lambda_{\lambda}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}^{\dagger}\;

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda|\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rangle\langle\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}|+(1-\lambda)\frac{\mathds{1}}{D}\;,

with
|\psi\rangle
being the pure input state of the system, and with
|\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rangle:=U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}|\psi\rangle
. If we now call

the symmetric logarithmic derivative associated to the parameter

in the absence of noise, i.e. the SLD of
|\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rangle
, given in (

\displaystyle L_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)=\frac{\lambda D}{2+\lambda\left(D-2\right)}L_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;,

is the SLD in the noisy scenario. We obtain this expression by substituting
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
defined in (

and the commutator matrix

are both found to be proportional to their noiseless counterparts

and

computed from

, i.e.

\displaystyle F(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}D}{2+\lambda\left(D-2\right)}F(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;,

(58)

\displaystyle A(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{3}D^{2}}{[2+\lambda(D-1)]^{2}}A(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;.

(59)

Replaced into (

) the above expressions lead to

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)=\frac{|\lambda|D}{2+\lambda(D-2)}\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;,

with

being the upper bound on the noiseless incompatibility figure of merit defined in (

, i.e.
\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)\neq\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},-\lambda)
for
\lambda\geq 0
. We define

Definition 4.1

(Asymmetry factor for
\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)
)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\star}(\lambda):=\frac{|\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)-\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},-\lambda)|}{\mathcal{I}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)}=\frac{2|\lambda|(D-2)}{2-\lambda(D-2)}\;.

5
Design of compatible models for quantum metrology

The following section is somewhat disconnected from the previous discussions on the incompatibility measure
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\lambda)
. Here we want to analyze some strategies that have been proposed in the past and some generalizations that allow to produce a fully compatible statistical model in quantum metrology. We will only need to asses the condition
A(\boldsymbol{\theta})=0
to claim compatibility, according to the bound (
35
), and therefore we won’t need the linear program for
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. We first review what is already known for
2
qubits, each codified via (
49
), and then generalize it for the
D
-dimensional encoding (
54
) when a couple of
D
-dimensional systems are available.

5.1
Known results for a two-qubits probe

In this section we analyze the compatibility of three different two qubit encoding scenarios in the absence of noise and for some special instances of the input states. We do not claim paternity of these results, they are only reviewed here in order to be generalized in the next section. Consider first the ancilla-aided model, in which only one of the two qubits is subject to the unitary encoding (
49
). This means that the total evolution of the two qubits is
\mathds{1}\otimes U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
. As input state for the probes we take a Bell state, which is known in the literature to be optimal for the estimation of
SU(2)
operations
[
70
]
, for example

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle:=\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\;,

(64)

where
|0\rangle
and
|1\rangle
are the eigenvectors of
\sigma_{z}
. From a direct computation we see that for this state
A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0\;\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}
, which from (
35
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0\;,\qquad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}\;.

(65)

leading to compatibility. This result was first reported in
[
70
]
. Interestingly enough compatibility can also be obtained when operating on the maximally entangled state (
64
) with the encoding
U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\otimes U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
. Indeed by explicit computation we get again
A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0\;\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}
that leads once more to (
65
). Such result can be found in
[
51
]
. We will see in section
5.2
that these effects are just a special instance of a more general trend since a maximally entangled state of a finite dimensional probe always gives full compatibility, both for one and two uses of the encoding unitary channel.

We now give a last example, which we here name “anti-parallel spin strategy” for future reference. Take the input state going through the encoding
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\otimes U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
to be
|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle\otimes|-\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle
, where
|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle
and
|-\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle
have opposite Bloch vectors
+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}
and
-\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}
. This state has the same QFI of the state of two parallel spins
|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle\otimes|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle
, but has
A({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=0\;\forall\boldsymbol{\theta}
(in contrast to
|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle\otimes|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle
), which means that it is fully compatible and a superior probe for the sensing task. This result can be obtained from direct computation or thanks to the observation of section
5.3
, where we generalize this ideas to finite dimensional probes. The superiority of the anti-parallel spin state was already observed by Gisin and Popescu in
[
71
]
and in the context of parameter estimation in
[
72
]
. In all these three examples, being the encoded state pure, a measure entangled across two qubits only is sufficient to get compatibility
[
73
]
.

5.2
Compatibility of the maximally entangled states

In this subsection we will show that the results of section
5.1
are only a particular case of a general observation, by proving that the use of an ancilla, maximally entangled with the probe, can completely remove the incompatibility, leading to the identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=0\;,\qquad\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}\;,

(66)

Consider hence as input the following pure state

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|i\rangle\otimes|i\rangle\in\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}\;,

(67)

on which the evolution
\mathds{1}\otimes U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
acts to produce the output state

\displaystyle|\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rangle:=\mathds{1}\otimes U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}|\psi\rangle\;,

(68)

(i.e. the Choi–Jamiołkowski state of the channel
U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}

[
74
,
75
]
). Following section
4.3
and
E
, the associated symmetric logarithmic derivatives (
12
) of
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=|\psi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}|
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle L_{k}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\frac{2\rmi}{D}\sum_{ij}|i\rangle\!\langle j|\otimes\left(H_{k}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})|i\rangle\!\langle j|-|i\rangle\!\langle j|H_{k}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)\;,

(69)

which lead to the following expressions for the
F(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
A(\boldsymbol{\theta})
matrices:

\displaystyle F_{lm}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\Tr\left(\{H_{l}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{m}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}\right)/D\;,

(70)

\displaystyle A_{lm}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\rmi\Tr\left(\left[H_{l}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{m}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right]\right)/D=0\;,

(71)

where in the last identity we used the fact that
\left[H_{l}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{m}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right]
is a traceless operator. Accordingly the upper bound (
35
) imposes (
66
), hence the thesis: the addition of a sufficiently large ancilla permits to remove entirely the quantum incompatibility for the LAC measurements.

A similar result holds true also when we let evolve the maximally entangled state (
67
) through
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\otimes U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. In this case (
68
) gets replaced by

\displaystyle|\psi_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\rangle:=U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\otimes U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}|\psi\rangle\;,

(72)

which leads to

\displaystyle F_{lm}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\Tr\left(\{H_{l}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{m}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}\right)/D\;,

(73)

\displaystyle A_{lm}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\rmi\Tr\left(\left[H_{l}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{m}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right]\right)/D=0\;,

(74)

which gives again the full compatibility condition (
66
).

5.3
Generalized anti-parallel spin strategy

Now we generalize the “anti-parallel spin” strategy of section
5.1
. Suppose that we only have two parameters to estimate (
d=2
) and we take for probe the separable input state
|\psi_{1}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{2}\rangle
that evolves through the mapping induced by
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\otimes U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. The sufficient condition for compatibility
A(\boldsymbol{\theta})=0
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}|[H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]|\psi_{1}\rangle+\langle\psi_{2}|[H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]|\psi_{2}\rangle=0\;.

(75)

The operator
[H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{2}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]
is skew-hermitian and therefore is diagonalizable and has purely imaginary eigenvalues
\pm\rmi a_{j}
, where
a_{j}>0
, for
j=1,2,\dots,\lfloor D/2\rfloor
, each associated with an eigenvector
|\pm\rmi a_{j}\rangle
. If the dimension
D
is odd, then we have an extra unique zero eigenvalue. Let’s denote with
V
the unitary operator that performs such diagonalization, i.e.
V^{\dagger}\left[H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{2}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right]V=\text{diag}\left(\pm\rmi a_{1},\pm\rmi a_{2},\cdots\right)
. Let’s define
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,\lfloor D/2\rfloor\}
, then a couple of states that guarantees compatibility is

\displaystyle|\psi_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\rme^{i\varphi^{1}_{j}}V|\rmi(-1)^{s_{j}}a_{j}\rangle\;,

(76)

\displaystyle|\psi_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{S}}\rme^{i\varphi^{2}_{j}}V|-\rmi(-1)^{s_{j}}a_{j}\rangle\;,

(77)

where
\varphi_{j}^{1}
and
\varphi_{j}^{2}
are arbitrary phases,
s_{j}\in\{0,1\}
, and
|\mathcal{S}|
is the cardinality of
\mathcal{S}
. Notice that we are also free to add in the definition of whichever
|\psi_{1}\rangle
or
|\psi_{2}\rangle
the state
V|0\rangle
, with
|0\rangle
being the eigenvector with null eigenvalue (in case there is one). With the above choice of probes the compatibility condition (
75
) is realized. Notice also that the QFI matrix of
|\psi_{1}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{2}\rangle
is the sum of the QFIs of
|\psi_{1}\rangle
and
|\psi_{2}\rangle
. This two states taken individually manifest incompatibility, but when measured jointly they gain full compatibility. This construction is the analogue of the “anti-parallel spin strategy” of section (
5.1
). We observe that a state
|\psi\rangle
, being an equal superposition of
|\psi_{1}\rangle
and
|\psi_{2}\rangle
is also fully compatible. The condition (
75
) justifies also the compatibility of
|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle\otimes|-\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle

\forall\,\boldsymbol{\theta}
in section
5.1
. These two states are an orthogonal basis for the qubit Hilbert space, therefore

\displaystyle\langle+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}|[H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{2}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]|+\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle+\langle-\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}|[H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{2}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]|-\widehat{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Tr\left([H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{2}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})]\right)=0\;,

hence condition (
75
) is satisfied and (
66
) holds. Incidentally we observe also that for a
d
-parameter estimation, the state
|\psi_{1}\rangle\otimes|\psi_{2}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\psi_{D}\rangle
is compatible when
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1}^{D}
is base for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, because then we would have

\displaystyle A_{ij}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\propto\Tr\left(\left[H_{i}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H_{j}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right]\right)=0\;.

(78)

6
Conclusions

One of the defining properties of quantum mechanics is the intrinsic incompatibility between the possible experiments that could be carried out on a quantum system. This causes the appearance of information limits on the precision to which different characteristics of a certain quantum system can be known. Formally, this comes always from the non-commutativity of quantum operations. The main role of this paper is to give a theoretical foundation to the measure of incompatibility in the quantum estimation task. For this purpose we define in section
3
a figure of merit having a well defined operational and geometrical meaning. The figure of merit
\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
in (
21
) is built to be easily liked to the asymptotic results of estimation theory
[
42
]
. This allows us to easily compute it, as explained in
C
, and to give the upper bound in section
3.5
. The definition of incompatibility depends on the operations that we are able to perform, which is our level of control over the system. If we are only able to perform separable measurements on the probes then the relevant incompatibility measure is
\underline{r}^{\text{s}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
defined in (
37
). In section
4
the estimation is studied in the scenario where a depolarizing noise acts, this is a form of disturbance which is often used to model the decoherence dynamics in metrology
[
76
,
77
]
. We observed some interesting phenomena like the asymmetry of the incompatibility for inversion in the space of states in section
4.3
. In section
5
we discuss some strategies able to produce compatible models for quantum metrology with generic
D
-dimension systems, which are for example the use of maximally entangled states. As a further development it would be interesting to determine which state gives the maximum and minimum incompatibility for a certain encoding at a fixed point
\boldsymbol{\theta}
. This optimization is hard because the figure of merit is a complicated non linear function of the state. In a sense the probe that maximizes incompatibility is the one which captures at most the quantum properties of the encoding process. Finally we would like to look for a link between the incompatibility and the Heisenberg scaling. In this context the only relevant measures are the one that assume no constraints on the separability of the input, because a single giant entangled probe would be used.

Appendix A
Figure of merit for LAC measurements

In this appendix we will define a version of the incompatibility figure of merit for local asymptotic covariant (LAC) measurements
[
42
,
48
]
. Consider the sequences of POVMs
\left({\text{M}}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}
that satisfy local asymptotic covariance (LAC) at the point
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in\Theta
, as defined in
[
42
,
48
]
. The
k
th measurement
{\text{M}}_{k}
of a sequence in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}
acts on
k
probes and has
\Sigma^{(k)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
as associated MSE matrix. Because of the definition of LAC,
\left({\text{M}}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}
admits a limiting MSE matrix, i.e.
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}k\Sigma^{(k)}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\Sigma({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
(we also ask
\lVert\Sigma({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\rVert<\infty
). The new figure of merit is hence defined as

\displaystyle r({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\inf_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}}\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}\;.

(79)

In
[
48
]
the authors have proven the validity of the Holevo bound for the LAC measurements and its achievability in the same class (for a full rank
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}
with non degenerate spectrum), that is

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}}\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]\;=C_{\text{H}}\left(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\;.

(80)

We now prove that th
\inf
and
\sup
of (
79
) can be commuted. It is easy to prove that the set
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}
is convex. The set of POVMs acting on
{\mathfrak{S}}\left(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N}\right)
and having
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
as outcome set can be thought as a convex subset of a certain dual vector space
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}

[
78
]
. The set containing the infinite sequences of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
is a vector space, and the set of sequences thereof that are sequences of POVMs is convex. Furthermore the defining properties of LAC
[
48
]
is stable under convex combination of two measurement sequences. In the Minimax theorem of
[
79
]
the both spaces are required to be be locally convex. Banach spaces like
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\supset\mathcal{M}_{N}
are locally convex, and the countable infinite product space of multiple
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
(which is the space of sequences) is also a locally convex space. Furthermore given
\Sigma^{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
\Sigma^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
the limiting MSE matrices of two sequences
\left({\text{M}}^{1}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\left({\text{M}}^{2}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, the asymptotic MSE of the convex combination
\left(\alpha{\text{M}}^{1}_{k}+\left(1-\alpha\right){\text{M}}^{2}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is
\Sigma(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\alpha\Sigma^{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})+\left(1-\alpha\right)\Sigma^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. Consequently, just like in
B
, the Minimax theorem of Kneser
[
79
,
80
]
can be applied to swap the order of
\inf
over
{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}}
and
\sup_{G\geq 0}
. It is understood that the argument of (
79
) can be cast into the same form of (
87
), from which the linearity in
\left({\text{M}}_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
G
, and the continuity in
G
easily follow. We arrive therefore at

\displaystyle r({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\sup_{G\geq 0}\inf_{\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{\tiny{(LAC)}}}}\frac{\Tr\left[G\cdot\Sigma({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F^{-1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]}=\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{C_{\text{H}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{C_{\text{S}}(G,{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}=\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;.

(81)

Appendix B
Exchanging
\sup
and

We will arrive at (

Lemma 1
. The function

\displaystyle|r_{N}\left(G_{1},{\text{M}}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)-r_{N}\left(G_{2},{\text{M}}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)|\leq N\lVert F(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rVert\lVert\Sigma^{(N)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rVert\lVert G_{1}-G_{2}\rVert\;.

\displaystyle\text{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}}:=\lambda\text{E}^{1}_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}}+\left(1-\lambda\right)\text{E}^{2}_{{\boldsymbol{\hat{\theta}}}}\;.

The
\Sigma(\boldsymbol{\theta})

Lemma 3

\displaystyle r_{N_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\geq r_{N_{1}N_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;.

Lemma 5
. The function

is upper semicontinuous in

Consider next the supremum over

\displaystyle=

for every fixed

,
\forall\,\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{{\text{M}}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{{\text{(LU)}}}}r_{N}\left(G_{\alpha},{\text{M}}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)

Putting together (

Appendix C
Formulation of the semidefinite program

We start from (
29
) and write

\displaystyle\underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{G\geq 0}\frac{C_{\text{H}}\left(G,\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)}{\Tr\left[G\cdot F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{-1}\right]}

(132)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}C_{\text{H}}\left(F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}GF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)\,.

The semidefinite program for
C_{\text{H}}\left(G,\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)
reported in
[
49
]
is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} C_{\text{H}}\left(G,\boldsymbol{\theta}\right)\;=\;&\underset{V\in\mathbb{S}^{n},X\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}\times n}}{\text{minimize}}&&\Tr\,[G\cdot V]\\
&\text{subject to}&&\begin{pmatrix}V&X^{T}R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}\\
R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}X&\mathds{1}_{\tilde{r}}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0\;,\\
&&&X^{T}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}=\mathds{1}_{n}\;.\end{aligned}

(133)

See the work
[
49
]
for the definitions of all the objects appearing in this program, it is not necessary to understand them in order to follow our derivation. Equation (
132
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;=\;&\sup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\underset{V\in\mathbb{S}^{n},X\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}\times n}}{\text{minimize}}&&\Tr\,[F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}GF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot V]\\
&\text{subject to}&&\begin{pmatrix}V&X^{T}R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}\\
R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}X&\mathds{1}_{\tilde{r}}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0\;,\\
&&&X^{T}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathds{1}_{n}\;.\end{aligned}

(134)

The objective function
\Tr\,[F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}GF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot V]
is linear and continuous in both
G
and
V
. The domain of the
\sup
and the
\min
are both convex, with
\mathcal{G}
being compact. We can therefore apply again the Mimimax theorem of
[
79
]
as done in
B
. Having
\sup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}
as the innermost operation we can solve it and write

\displaystyle\sup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\Tr\,[F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}GF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot V]=\lVert F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rVert\;.

(135)

Now the objective of the minimization is the spectral norm of
F^{\frac{1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})VF^{\frac{1}{2}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. We can introduce a dummy variable
t
and write the program as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;=\;&\underset{V\in\mathbb{S}^{n},X\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}\times n}}{\text{minimize}}&&t\\
&\text{subject to}&&\lVert F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rVert\leq t\;,\\
&&&\begin{pmatrix}V&X^{T}R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}\\
R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}X&\mathds{1}_{\tilde{r}}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0\;,\\
&&&X^{T}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathds{1}_{n}\;.\end{aligned}

(136)

The condition on
\lVert F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rVert
can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{\max}(F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq t^{2}\Longrightarrow\\
&F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq t^{2}\mathds{1}\Longrightarrow\\
&t\mathds{1}-F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(t\mathds{1}\right)^{-1}F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0\;.\end{aligned}

Because of the Schur complement condition for the positive semidefinite matrices
[
83
]
the optimization becomes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \underline{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;=\;&\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\\
X\in\mathbb{R}^{\tilde{d}\times n}\end{subarray}}{\text{minimize}}&&t\\
&\text{subject to}&&\begin{pmatrix}t\mathds{1}&F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
F(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}VF(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\frac{1}{2}}&t\mathds{1}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0\;,\\
&&&\begin{pmatrix}V&X^{T}R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}\\
R_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}X&\mathds{1}_{\tilde{r}}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0\;,\\
&&&X^{T}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\mathds{1}_{n}\;.\end{aligned}

(137)

From which we compute
\mathcal{I}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
according to (
30
). This semidefinite program is solved by means of the modeling system CVX developed on Matlab
[
84
]
.

) it must be

\displaystyle G=-\frac{A(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\prime}GA(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\prime}}{\Tr\left[-A(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\prime}GA(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{\prime}\right]}\;,

(141)

this is realized for
\widetilde{G}_{11}=\widetilde{G}_{22}=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore we have build implicitly a matrix
G
that saturates the
\sup
and gives (
34
).

Appendix E
Effective generators for
\boldsymbol{\theta}\neq 0

Consider the single qubit encoding given in (
49
), in order to compute the relevant metrological quantities, for example the QFI and the Holevo-Cramér-Rao bound, it is necessary to take the derivatives of this evolution, evaluated at
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, i.e.
\frac{\partial U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\boldsymbol{\theta}}\Big|_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. If we are sensing small deviations of the phases around
\boldsymbol{\theta}=0
, then these expressions are fairly easily computable, they are indeed
\frac{\partial U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\theta_{1}}\Big|_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\rmi\sigma_{y}
, and
\frac{\partial U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}{\partial\theta_{2}}\Big|_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\rmi\sigma_{z}
. But if the base point of the sensing process is
\boldsymbol{\theta}\neq 0
, then these derivatives became cumbersome, and can hinder the derivation of simple analytical results. To overcome this issue we show in this section that the metrological properties of the estimation at a point
\boldsymbol{\theta}\neq 0
are equivalent to that of a sensing process around zero, where the encoding has the effective generators
H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
H_{2}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, which are null-trace hermitian operators depending on the non null point
\boldsymbol{\theta}
and are in general not simply
\sigma_{y}
and
\sigma_{z}
. We write explicitly the small variations
\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
from the base point
\boldsymbol{\theta}
in the encoding (
49
), i.e.

\displaystyle U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\rmi[(\theta_{1}+\delta\theta_{1})\sigma_{y}+(\theta_{2}+\delta\theta_{2})\sigma_{z}]\right)

(142)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\rmi H+\rmi\delta H\right)\;.

(143)

The variables
\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
are now the unknown parameters, while
\boldsymbol{\theta}
is known and fixed. The Hamiltonians
H:=\theta_{1}\sigma_{y}+\theta_{2}\sigma_{z}
and
\delta H:=\delta\theta_{1}\sigma_{y}+\delta\theta_{2}\sigma_{z}
have been defined. We expand the expression for
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}
in terms of
H
and
\delta H
with the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula, and keep only the first order terms in the infinitesimal variation
\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
, obtaining

\displaystyle U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\simeq U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}\exp\left(\rmi\delta H-\frac{1}{2}[\rmi H,\rmi\delta H]+\frac{1}{6}[\rmi H,[\rmi H,\rmi\delta H]]+\dots\right)\;.

(144)

Now, the idea is to perform the rotation
U_{-\boldsymbol{\theta}}
on the probe after the encoding with
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}
, in such way we compensate for the know component of the rotation
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}
and leave only a term depending on the new unknown variables
\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

\displaystyle U_{-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}U_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\delta{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\rmi\delta H-\frac{1}{2}[\rmi H,\rmi\delta H]+\frac{1}{6}[\rmi H,[\rmi H,\rmi\delta H]]+\dots\right)

(145)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\rmi\left[\delta\theta_{1}H^{\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})+\delta\theta_{2}H^{\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\right]\right)\;.

(146)

In the last expression we have collected the terms multiplied by
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
respectively, which have been named
H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. Notice that the commutator of two skew-hermitian operators like
\rmi H
and
\rmi\delta H
is again skew-hermitian, this applies to all the elements of the exponentiated sum in (
145
), and means that the right hand side of (
145
) is a unitary operator even if we have neglected higher order terms in
\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}
. The exponentiated sum is either equal to
\rmi\delta\theta_{1}H^{\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
or to
\rmi\delta\theta_{2}H^{\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
when we set either
\delta\theta_{2}=0
or
\delta\theta_{1}=0
, therefore the effective generators are also hermitian operators. Consider a probe
\rho
codified by
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}
, i.e.
\rho_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}\simeq U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}\rho U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}
. All the informational quantities remain the same if a know unitary is applied to the state, indeed its effects can be always absorbed at the measurement stage (if the selected measurements set allows to do so). By choosing
U_{-\boldsymbol{\theta}}
to be this unitary we get
U_{-\boldsymbol{\theta}}U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}\rho U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}U_{-\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}=U_{\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}\rho U_{\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}
, with
U_{\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=U_{-\boldsymbol{\theta}}U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}+\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}
. We observe that the traces of
H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
H_{1}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
can be neglected without consequences, indeed they contribute only to a global phase. Also if the gate
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
is used multiple times on an entangled state, so that the encoding is
U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\otimes U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\otimes\dots\otimes U_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
, the traces of the generators only give an irrelevant global phase. We now further manipulate the encoding and look for a parameterization in which the generators are orthogonal. Two qubits operators
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are said to be orthogonal if
\{H_{1},H_{2}\}=0
. As null-trace hermitian operators on a qubit
H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and
H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
can be written

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sigma_{x}+\beta_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sigma_{y}+\gamma_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sigma_{z}\;,

(147)

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sigma_{x}+\beta_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sigma_{y}+\gamma_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\sigma_{z}\;,

(148)

with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=(\alpha_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\alpha_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\alpha_{3}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=(\beta_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\beta_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\beta_{3}(\boldsymbol{\theta}))\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The orthogonality condition is then
\{H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}=2\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\,\mathds{1}
. We can decompose
H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
in a term proportional to
H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
and one orthogonal as following

\displaystyle H^{\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\frac{\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}(\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\lVert\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rVert^{2}}H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})+H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\;,

(149)

this is the definition of
H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
, which satisfies
\{H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}=0
. We define
x(\boldsymbol{\theta}):=\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\boldsymbol{\beta}(\boldsymbol{\theta})/\lVert\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rVert^{2}
for ease of notation and substitute (
149
) in (
146
), thus getting

\displaystyle U_{\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\exp\left(\rmi[(\delta\theta_{1}+x({\boldsymbol{\theta}})\delta\theta_{2})H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})+\delta\theta_{2}H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})]\right)\;,

(150)

where
H^{\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
has been renamed
H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. The final step is to normalize the generators, thus defining
\widetilde{H}^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})/\Tr\left[H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})^{2}\right]
, and
\widetilde{H}^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})/\Tr\left[H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})^{2}\right]
. Going from
H_{i}^{\text{eff}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
to
\widetilde{H}^{\bot\text{eff}}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
corresponds to the following reparametrization

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\delta\theta_{1}^{\prime}=\Tr\left[H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})^{2}\right](\delta\theta_{1}+x(\boldsymbol{\theta})\delta\theta_{2})\;,\\
\delta\theta_{2}^{\prime}=\Tr\left[H^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})^{2}\right]\delta\theta_{2}\;.\end{cases}

(151)

A rotation of the reference system can align
\widetilde{H}^{\bot\text{eff}}_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})
with
\sigma_{y}
and
\sigma_{z}
, remember thought that the probe state must also be transformed. Let us introduce the unitary
V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
such that
V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\widetilde{H}^{\bot\text{eff}}_{1}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}=\sigma_{y}
and
V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\widetilde{H}^{\bot\text{eff}}_{2}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}=\sigma_{z}
, then
V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}U_{\delta\boldsymbol{\theta}}V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}=\rme^{\rmi\left(\delta\theta_{1}^{\prime}\sigma_{y}+\delta\theta_{2}^{\prime}\sigma_{z}\right)}
, while the probe state becomes
V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\rho V_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}^{\dagger}
.

Constructive subsampling of finite frames with applications in optimal function recovery
Felix Bartel, Martin Schäfer, Tino Ullrich

Abstract
In this paper we present new constructive methods, random and deterministic, for the efficient subsampling of finite frames in ℂ m \mathds{C}^{m} . Based on a suitable random subsampling strategy, we are able to extract from any given frame with bounds 0 < A ≤ B < ∞ 0<A\leq B<\infty (and condition B / A B/A ) a similarly conditioned reweighted subframe consisting of merely 𝒪 ⁡ ( m ​ log ⁡ m ) \mathcal{O}(m\log m) elements. Further, utilizing a deterministic subsampling method based on principles developed by Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava to control the spectrum of sums of Hermitian rank-1 matrices, we are able to reduce the number of elements to 𝒪 ⁡ ( m ) \mathcal{O}(m) (with a constant close to one). By controlling the weights via a preconditioning step, we can, in addition, preserve the lower frame bound in the unweighted case. This permits the derivation of new quasi-optimal unweighted (left) Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for L 2 ​ ( D , ν ) L_{2}(D,\nu) with constructible node sets of size 𝒪 ⁡ ( m ) \mathcal{O}(m) for m m -dimensional subspaces of bounded functions. Those can be applied e.g. for (plain) least-squares sampling reconstruction of functions, where we obtain new quasi-optimal results avoiding the Kadison-Singer theorem. Numerical experiments indicate the applicability of our results.

Introduction

The paper mainly deals with the question of how to choose a well-conditioned subframe out of a given frame. The notion of a frame goes back to Duffin and Schaeffer
[
13
]
. Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space with scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and norm
\|\cdot\|
. A countable subset
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i}
in
H
is said to be a
frame
if there are constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that

\displaystyle A\|\bm{x}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{i}|\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|\bm{x}\|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x}\in H\,.

(1.1)

We are mostly interested in frames consisting of finitely many elements in some finite dimensional Hilbert space
H
, see e.g. Casazza and Kutyniok
[
7
]
for an introduction to finite frame theory.
Systems of this kind may be represented by
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
with
M\geq m
.
The question of finding good subframes in such a system is rather fundamental and important for many applications ranging from graph sparsifiers
[
3
,
32
]
, the Kadison-Singer problem
[
24
,
38
]
, to optimal discretization and sampling recovery of multivariate functions
[
11
,
20
,
21
,
27
,
22
,
34
,
30
]
. In this context, let us also mention the possibility of generating ‘approximations’ of Hadamard matrices, a problem which has been considered in
[
12
]
for example. Subsampling of a tight Hadamard frame
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
, where all entries of the
\bm{y}^{i}
are
\pm 1
(or in a complex setting of modulus
1
), may lead to an almost square Hadamard-type matrix with good condition.

As a first step, we put forward a simple random subsampling strategy in
Section
2
.
For the index set
\{1,\ldots,M\}
of the intial system let us subsequently use the short-hand notation
[M]
.
Theorem
2.1
shows that drawing elements
\bm{y}^{i}
,
i\in[M]
, according to the probabilities
\varrho_{i}:=\|\bm{y}^{i}\|^{2}_{2}/(\sum_{j}\|\bm{y}^{j}\|^{2}_{2})
yields a
reweighted subframe
(\varrho_{i}^{-1/2}\bm{y}^{i})_{i\in J}
with similar frame bounds, with high probability provided
|J|=\mathcal{O}(m\log m)
.
In terms of computational complexity this strategy is very efficient.
It is not optimal with respect to the number of frame elements, however.

This shortcoming is dealt with in
Section
3
.
Here we formulate a deterministic algorithm (
BSS
) which yields reweighted subframes with an optimal order of
\mathcal{O}(m)
frame elements. It is an extension of a subsampling method due to Batson, Spielmann, Srivastava
[
3
]
to the complex and non-tight case, which in the original version only applies to tight frames in
\mathds{R}^{m}
. Let us remark that an extension to tight frames in
\mathds{C}^{m}
was already considered in
[
22
, Cor. 2.1]
. We further modify the subsampling method
in
[
3
]
by allowing variable barrier shifts.
As proved in Theorem
3.1
, the
BSS
algorithm constructs in polynomial time
for any frame
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
with constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
and
b>\kappa^{2}\geq 1
,
\kappa=\kappa(A,B)
being the value in (
3.1
), a subset
J\subset[M]
and weights
s_{i}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle A\|\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in J}s_{i}|\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq\gamma\cdot B\|\bm{x}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

(1.2)

with
|J|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
and
\gamma=\gamma(b,\kappa)
as in (
3.3
).
Combined with a suitable ‘preconditioning’ step, it
can even achieve (
1.2
) for any
b>1
and with the better constant
\gamma=\gamma(b,1)
. In this variant (
BSS
⟂
), at the core,
BSS
is only applied to a tight frame. Since exact tightness can usually not be guaranteed in practice, due to numerical inaccuracies, it is nevertheless important for applications that
BSS
works stably also for non-tight frames, as ensured by Theorem
3.1
.

Sections
2
and
3
lay the groundwork for the main
Section
4
, where we are concerned with the extraction of unweighted subframes.
This is a much more difficult task. The existence of similarly conditioned subframes consisting of order
\mathcal{O}(m)
elements is guaranteed by the solution of the famous Kadison-Singer problem by Markus, Spielmann, Srivastava
[
24
]
. To the knowledge of the authors, there are no general
constructive polynomial-time methods available, however. Our approach to tackle this problem is
to use the obtained results on weighted subframes (e.g. (
1.2
)) and try to control
the weights
s_{i}
. By bounding those from above, we are able to preserve the lower frames bounds,
which for many applications are the relevant ones.

One of the main results is Theorem
4.4
.
In Corollary
4.5
, we obtain the non-weighted sparsification inequality (
1.3
), which holds true for arbitrary sets of vectors
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
.
For any
1+\frac{1}{m}<b\leq\frac{M}{m}
we can extract a subset
J\subset[M]
of cardinality
|J|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
such that

\frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}|\langle{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq C_{0}\frac{b^{3}}{(b-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{|J|}\sum\limits_{i\in J}|\langle{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{x}\in\mathds{C}^{m}\,,

(1.3)

where
C_{0}>0
is an absolute constant.
The lower frame bound of an initial frame is thus preserved up to a constant
(b/(b-1))^{3}
. Earlier results in this direction, see Harvey, Olver
[
15
]
, Nitzan, Olevskii, Ulanovski
[
28
]
, Limonova, Temlyakov
[
22
]
, and Nagel, Schäfer, T. Ullrich
[
27
]
, are all non-constructive and have been initiated by the solution of the Kadison-Singer problem
[
24
]
in the form of Weaver’s conjecture
[
38
]
. These results need additional restrictions on the size of the frame elements. They provide the upper frame bounds as well, however.

A
central message
in this paper is the fact that, at least for the lower bounds,
we can argue much more elementary and do not need such deep results as Kadison-Singer.
Our approach is semi-constructive (with an at times probabilistic component) and yields polynomial-time algorithms (e.g.
plainBSS
) that work with high probability and can be efficiently implemented.
We developed a corresponding
Julia
-package, availabe at
www.github.com/felixbartel/BSSsubsampling.jl
,
and conducted numerical experiments with this code.
A few results are presented in
Section
5
.

Some applications of our obtained subsampling results are discussed in

Section
6
. A first interesting consequence of (
1.3
) is the non-weighted left Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality given by Theorem
6.2
. Assume we are given an

m
-dimensional space

\displaystyle V_{m}=\text{span}\{\eta_{1}(\cdot),\cdots,\eta_{m}(\cdot)\}

(1.4)

of complex-valued functions on some non-empty set
D
.
In case that
V_{m}\subset L_{2}(D,\nu)
for a finite measure
\nu
, (
1.3
) allows us to construct a set of nodes
{\mathbf{X}}_{n}=({\bm{x}}^{1},\ldots,\bm{x}^{n})\in D^{n}
with
n\leq\lceil bm\rceil
for any
1+\frac{1}{m}<b\leq\frac{M}{m}
in polynomial time (in
m
) such that

\|f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}^{2}\leq C_{1}\frac{b^{3}}{(b-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|f({\bm{x}}^{i})|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in V_{m}\,.

(1.5)

Here
C_{1}>0
is again an absolute constant.
Inequalities like (
1.5
) have direct consequences for the sampling recovery of functions.
With
\nu
and
V_{m}
as before,
let us consider a simple plain least squares recovery operator
S_{V_{m}}^{\bm{X}_{n}}
for
nodes
\bm{X}_{n}
satisfying (
1.5
). It reconstructs
any
\nu
-measurable function
f:D\to\mathds{C}
in
V_{m}
via a plain least squares minimization at the nodes
\bm{X}_{n}
and, according to Theorem
6.3
, with

\|f-S_{V_{m}}^{{\mathbf{X}}_{n}}f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}^{2}\leq C_{2}\frac{b^{3}}{(b-1)^{3}}e(f,V_{m})_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,,

(1.6)

where
e(f,V_{m})_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}=\inf_{g\in V_{m}}\|f-g\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}
is the error of best approximation of
f
from
V_{m}
.

Inequalities of this type have been first established by Cohen and Migliorati
[
8
]
, but with a larger number of samples, namely
n=\mathcal{O}(m\log(m))
. This has been improved by Temlyakov
[
34
]
to
n=\mathcal{O}(m)
samples with unspecified constants. The mentioned results rely on weighted least squares algorithms, however, and Temlyakov posed the
question in
[
34
]
, if also classical plain least squares methods could be used.
Theorem
6.3
gives an affirmative answer and even displays the dependence of the constant on the oversampling factor
b
.
As a consequence we obtain from (
1.6
) for classes of bounded complex-valued functions
F\subset\ell_{\infty}(D)
, when optimizing over all
m
-dimensional reconstruction spaces, the new relation

g_{\lceil bm\rceil,m}^{\mathrm{ls}}(F,L_{2}(D,\nu))\leq C_{3}\frac{b^{3/2}}{(b-1)^{3/2}}d_{m}(F,\ell_{\infty}(D))

(1.7)

between the
m
-th Kolmogorov number
d_{m}
of the class
F
(see (
6.11
) for a definition) and the plain sampling numbers
g_{n,m}^{\mathrm{ls}}(F,L_{2}(D,\nu))
defined in (
6.10
). Here recovery is restricted to canonical plain least squares operators using
n
samples acting on subspaces of dimension
m
. Since the quantities on the left-hand side of (
1.7
)
are in general larger than the standard sampling numbers
[
9
, (5.0.1)]
, where there are no such restrictions on the recovery, this slightly improves on recent results by Temlyakov, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 in
[
34
]

as well as
[
22
, Thm. 3.4]
, the latter joint work with Limonova. Interestingly, the
b
-dependent constant may be improved to
(b-1)^{-1}
when allowing weighted least squares algorithms, cf.
[
22
, Thm. 1.7]
(or the original
[
10
, Thm. 6.3]
) for the case of real functions (an extension to the complex case has been given in
[
22
, Rem. 3.2]
but only for
b>2
). In our case a distinction between real and complex
L_{2}(D,\nu)
in (
1.7
) as in
[
22
]
is unnecessary due to the validity of Theorem
3.1
in the complex setting. Note that the right-hand side in (
1.7
) is of particular importance if the linear widths in
L_{2}
are not square-summable
[
35
,
36
]
.

A related scenario is investigated in the recent papers
[
11
,
20
,
19
,
25
,
27
]
. Here one is interested in the recovery of functions from a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
H(K)
in
L_{2}(D,\nu)
, where
\nu
is allowed to be infinite.
If we assume
H(K)
to fulfill some natural assumptions (such as a finite trace of the kernel
K
and a compact embedding
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}:H(K)\hookrightarrow L_{2}(D,\nu)
), our polynomial-time
subsampling schemes allow to construct node sets
\bm{X}_{n}
with
n\leq\lceil bm\rceil
,
1+\frac{1}{m}<b\leq 2
, and a weight function
w_{m}
such that

\|f-S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\leq C_{4}\frac{1}{(b-1)^{3}}\log\Big(\frac{m}{p}\Big)\Big(\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+\frac{7}{m}\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\Big)\|f\|^{2}_{H(K)}

(1.8)

for every
f\in H(K)
, with a probability exceeding
1-\frac{3}{2}p
for each

(see Theorem
6.7
). Here,

is an absolute constant,

denote the singular numbers of the embedding

, and the recovery operator

is a weighted
least squares operator for the subspace
V_{m}
, as in (

), spanned by the left singular functions corresponding to the
m
largest singular numbers. The performance (

) is near-optimal as in
[
27
]
. The latter reference is the first which used the Weaver subsampling technique for the sampling recovery problem. However, it does not achieve the optimal rate. By a further refinement of the technique, established very recently in
[

]
by Dolbeault, Krieg, and M. Ullrich, the optimal rate (without additional

,

]
we have a semi-constructive method to generate the sampling nodes (offline step) that does not need the Kadison-Singer theorem in terms of the Weaver subsampling. In addition, the dependence on the oversampling factor

is displayed.
An

remains.
Although in many relevant cases (like periodic Sobolev spaces with mixed smoothness) the recovery operator turns out to be a canonical plain least squares operator with equal weights (acting on the hyperbolic cross frequency subspace with nodes displayed in Figure

) we do not know whether this is possible in general.

2
Random weighted subsampling of finite frames

We begin with a random subsampling strategy that allows to extract ‘good’ subframes of
\mathcal{O}(m\log m)
elements out of any given frame in
\mathds{C}^{m}
.
This goes back to Rudelson and Vershynin
[
31
]
, see also Spielman and Srivastava
[
32
]
, where the goal was to efficiently find a low rank approximation of a given matrix such that the error with respect to the spectral norm remains small.
The method is rather simple since it relies on a random subselection where the discrete probability mass
\varrho_{i}
for selecting one particular frame element
\bm{y}^{i}
(see Theorem
2.1
) is directly linked to its contribution to the sum of the norms, i.e., the Frobenius norm
\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}
of the matrix

\displaystyle\bm{Y}:=\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{y}^{1})^{\ast}\\[-4.30554pt]
\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\\[-4.30554pt]
\vdots\\[-4.30554pt]
\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\\
(\bm{y}^{M})^{\ast}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathds{C}^{M\times m}\,.

(2.1)

Note that for a given frame
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
, with
M\geq m
and
m\in\mathds{N}
, this matrix represents the analysis operator
of the frame and that

\displaystyle{m}A\leq\tr(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})=\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}\leq{m}\|\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y}\|_{2\to 2}=m\lambda_{\max}(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})\leq{m}B\,.

(2.2)

Our main result of this section
relies on a matrix Chernoff bound proven by Tropp
[
37
, Thm. 1.1]
(see Theorem
A.3
in the Appendix).
It shows how one can randomly subsample a finite frame of arbitrary size in
\mathds{C}^{m}
to a weighted subframe with
\mathcal{O}({m}\log{m})
elements while essentially keeping its stability properties.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
be a frame with constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
(see (
1.1
)).
Let further
p,t\in(0,1)
and
n\in\mathds{N}
be such that

\displaystyle n\geq\frac{3B}{At^{2}}m\log\left(\frac{2m}{p}\right)\,.

Drawing
n
indices
J\subset[M]
(with duplicates) i.i.d. according to the discrete probability density
\varrho_{i}=\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}/\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}
,
i\in[M]
, then gives a rescaled random subframe
(\varrho_{i}^{-1/2}\bm{y}^{i})_{i\in J}
such that

\displaystyle(1-t)A\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\varrho_{i}^{-1/2}\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq(1+t)B\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

with probability exceeding
1-p
.

Proof.

The result is a direct consequence of Tropp’s result in Lemma
A.3
. For a randomly chosen index
i\in[M]
we define the rank-one random matrix

\bm{A}_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\varrho^{-1}_{i}(\bm{y}^{i}\otimes\bm{y}^{i})
.
Clearly, it holds

\displaystyle\lamax(\bm{A}_{i})=\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{n}\varrho^{-1}_{i}(\bm{y}^{i}\otimes\bm{y}^{i})\right)=\frac{1}{n}\varrho_{i}^{-1}\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}\,.

Furthermore, having
n
independent copies
(\bm{A}_{i})_{i\in J}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\mathds{E}\bm{A}_{i}=\sum_{i\in J}\mathds{E}\left(\frac{1}{n}\varrho^{-1}_{i}(\bm{y}^{i}\otimes\bm{y}^{i})\right)=\sum_{i\in J}\frac{1}{n}\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y}=\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y}\,.

This gives for
\mu_{\min}:=\lamin(\sum_{i\in J}\mathds{E}\bm{A}_{i})
and
\mu_{\max}:=\lamax(\sum_{i\in J}\mathds{E}\bm{A}_{i})
that

\displaystyle\mu_{\min}=\lambda_{\min}(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})\geq A\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{\max}=\lambda_{\max}(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})=\|\bm{Y}^{\ast}\bm{Y}\|_{2\to 2}^{2}\,.

Since
\|\bm{Y}\|^{2}_{F}=\tr(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})
, Lemma
A.3
and (
2.2
) gives

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\varrho_{i}^{-1}\bm{y}^{i}\otimes\bm{y}^{i}\right)\geq(1+t)B\right)

\displaystyle\leq{m}\exp\left(-\frac{n\|\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y}\|_{2\to 2}}{\tr(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})}\frac{t^{2}}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\leq m\exp\left(-\frac{n}{{m}}\frac{t^{2}}{3}\right)\,.

For the smallest eigenvalue things are a bit different. Here we obtain

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\varrho_{i}^{-1}\bm{y}^{i}\otimes\bm{y}^{i}\right)\leq(1-t)A\right)

\displaystyle\leq m\exp\left(-\frac{nA}{\tr(\bm{Y}^{*}\bm{Y})}\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq m\exp\left(-\frac{An}{Bm}\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)\,.

For the probability of our assertion we need the complement of the two events above:

\displaystyle 1-m\exp\left(-\frac{n}{{m}}\frac{t^{2}}{3}\right)-m\exp\left(-\frac{An}{Bm}\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)\geq 1-2m\exp\left(-\frac{An}{{Bm}}\frac{t^{2}}{3}\right)\geq 1-p\,,

which follows from the assumption on
n
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The rescaled random subframe
(\varrho_{i}^{-1/2}\bm{y}^{i})_{i\in J}
in Theorem
2.1
is an equal-norm frame.
Thus, starting with a tight frame, we are able to construct an ‘almost tight’ frame with unit-norm (UNTF).
These are important in robust data transmission and have proven notoriously difficult to construct, cf.
[
5
,
6
]
.

3
Deterministic weighted subsampling of finite frames

We next present a deterministic subsampling algorithm for finite frames in
\mathds{C}^{m}
which we subsequently call (generalized)
BSS
algorithm.
A version for real-valued tight frames in
\mathds{R}^{m}
was originally introduced by Batson, Spielman, and Srivastava in the context of graph sparsification
[
3
]
.
It allows to extract from any given finite frame in
\mathds{C}^{m}
a comparably well-conditioned
re-weighted subframe of cardinality
\mathcal{O}(m)
. This is the statement of Theorem
3.1
below which generalizes
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
.

In contrast to related non-weighted subsampling results, such as e.g.
[
27
, Thm. 2.3]
which are all based on Weaver’s theorem, a deep result equivalent
to the famous Kadison-Singer theorem
[
24
]
, the proof of Theorem
3.1
is elementary and constructive. The underlying
BSS
algorithm lends itself to practical polynomial time implementation.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
be a frame (
1.1
) with frame constants
0<A\leq B<\infty
and let
b>\kappa^{2}\geq 1
with

\displaystyle\kappa:=\left(\frac{B}{2A}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\sqrt{\left(\frac{B}{2A}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-1}\,.

(3.1)

Then the
BSS
algorithm in Subsection
3.2
computes a subset
J\subset[M]
with
|J|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
and nonnegative weights
s_{i}
,
i\in J
, such that

3.1
The principal structure of the
BSS
algorithm

The frame property of the vectors
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
can be formulated as

\displaystyle A\bm{I}\preceq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\preceq B\bm{I}\,,

where
\bm{I}
denotes the identity matrix in
\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
(see the notation paragraph for the meaning of
\preceq
).
Furthermore, condition (
3.2
) of the subsampled frame can be rewritten as

\displaystyle A\bm{I}\preceq\sum_{i\in J}s_{i}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\preceq\gamma\cdot B\bm{I}\,.

(3.4)

The idea of the
BSS
algorithm is to build the sum
\sum_{i\in J}s_{i}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}
iteratively
in
n:=\lceil bm\rceil
steps.
Starting with the zero-matrix
\bm{A}^{(0)}:=0
, a sequence of Hermitian matrices

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{(0)},\,\bm{A}^{(1)},\,\bm{A}^{(2)},\,\ldots,\,\bm{A}^{(n)}

(3.5)

is computed via rank-1 updates of the form

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{(k)}=\bm{A}^{(k-1)}+t^{(k)}\bm{y}^{i^{(k)}}(\bm{y}^{i^{(k)}})^{\ast}\,,\quad k\in[n]\,,

with suitably selected indices
i^{(k)}\in[M]
and weights
t^{(k)}>0
.
After
n
iterations we have thus constructed a matrix
\bm{A}^{(n)}
of the form

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{(n)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t^{(k)}\bm{y}^{i^{(k)}}(\bm{y}^{i^{(k)}})^{\ast}=\sum_{i\in J}\tilde{s}_{i}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}

(3.6)

where

\tilde{s}_{i}:=\sum\limits_{k:i^{(k)}=i}t^{(k)}\quad\text{and}\quad J:=\Big\{i^{(k)}:k=1,\ldots,n\Big\}\,.

Clearly,
|J|\leq n=\lceil bm\rceil
.
During the whole process the spectra of the constructed matrices
\bm{A}^{(k)}
are controlled by means of so-called spectral barriers,
i.e., numbers
l^{(k)},u^{(k)}\in\mathds{R}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{A}^{(k)})\subset(l^{(k)},u^{(k)})\,,\quad k\in\{0,\dots,n\}\,.

(3.7)

Whereas the precise location of the eigenvalues of
\bm{A}^{(k)}
may not be known, in this way we have enclosed their location in
open intervals
(l^{(k)},u^{(k)})
, in particular it holds

\displaystyle l^{(k)}\bm{I}\preceq\bm{A}^{(k)}\preceq u^{(k)}\bm{I}\,.

The algorithm starts with initial barriers
l^{(0)}<0
and
0<u^{(0)}
for
\bm{A}^{(0)}=0
. From each step to the next, the barriers
are then shifted to the right, most simply by certain fixed lengths
\delta_{L}>0
and
\delta_{U}>0
.
In the
k
th iteration we thus have
l^{(k)}=l^{(0)}+k\delta_{L}
and
u^{(k)}=u^{(0)}+k\delta_{U}

(see Figure
3.1
).
For each
\bm{A}^{(k)}
the indices and weights
i^{(k+1)}
and
t^{(k+1)}
are further chosen such that (
3.7
) remains valid
for the updated matrix
\bm{A}^{(k+1)}
. This is the main technical challenge and requires some preparation, carried out in Subsections
3.3
and
3.4
.
Under these conditions, the final matrix
\bm{A}^{(n)}
then has property (
3.7
) for

l^{(n)}=l^{(0)}+n\delta_{L}\quad\text{and}\quad u^{(n)}=u^{(0)}+n\delta_{U}\,.

As shown in Subsection
3.5
, for the ‘right’ choice of
l^{(0)}
,
u^{(0)}
,
\delta_{L}
, and
\delta_{U}

we end up with final barriers satisfying

\displaystyle l^{(n)}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{u^{(n)}}{l^{(n)}}\leq\gamma\cdot\frac{B}{A}\,.

This finally allows to rescale the weights
\tilde{s}_{i}
in (
3.6
) appropriately, giving the desired weights
s_{i}
such that (
3.4
) is fulfilled.

3.3
Spectral analysis of rank-1 updates

In this subsection we analyze from a general perspective, how the spectrum
\sigma(\bm{A})
of a Hermitian matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
changes
under a rank-1 update of the form

\displaystyle\bm{A}\rightsquigarrow\bm{A}^{\prime}:=\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}

(3.10)

with
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
and
t\in\mathds{R}
. This question has already been discussed for the real setting in
[
3
, Sec. 3.1 & 3.2]
.
Our analysis here is analogous, however, we go a bit more into detail. In the end, we can derive precise conditions in Lemmas
3.7
,
3.8
,
and Corollary
3.9
.

With the matrix determinant lemma (Lemma
A.1
in the Appendix)
the characteristic polynomial
p_{\bm{A}^{\prime}}(\lambda)=\det(\lambda\bm{I}-\bm{A}^{\prime})
of
\bm{A}^{\prime}
in (
3.10
) can be calculated explicitly. For
\lambda\notin\sigma(\bm{A})

\displaystyle p_{\bm{A}^{\prime}}(\lambda)=\det(\lambda\bm{I}-\bm{A})\left(1-t\bm{v}^{\ast}(\lambda\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-1}\bm{v}\right)=p_{\bm{A}}(\lambda)\bigg(1-t\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{|\langle\bm{v},\bm{u}^{j}\rangle|^{2}}{\lambda-\lambda_{j}}\bigg)\,,

where
\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\}
are the, not necessarily distinct, eigenvalues of
\bm{A}
and
\{\bm{u}^{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
is a corresponding orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.
The eigenvalues of
\bm{A}^{\prime}
can be obtained from the associated characteristic equation. In case
t=0
,
we obtain as solutions the eigenvalues of
\bm{A}
, i.e., the roots of
p_{\bm{A}}(\lambda)
. In case
t\neq 0
, we obtain the roots of
p_{\bm{A}}(\lambda)
of at least second order together
with the solutions of the so-called secular equation

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{|\langle\bm{v},\bm{u}^{j}\rangle|^{2}}{\lambda-\lambda_{j}}=\frac{1}{t}\,.

A discussion of this equation yields the following insight:
If
t\neq 0
the eigenvalues of
\bm{A}^{\prime}
interlace the eigenvalues of
\bm{A}
, shifted to the left when
t<0
and shifted to the right when
t>0
.
Moreover, the shifts occur continuously in
t
.
In the limit
t\to\infty
, the largest eigenvalue tends to
\infty
and the corresponding eigenvector to
\bm{v}
.
For
t\to-\infty
, the smallest eigenvalue tends to
-\infty
, while
the corresponding eigenvector again tends to
\bm{v}
.
In case of algebraic multiplicities, always merely one of the respective eigenvalues moves, the rest remain at their old position.

With this, we already have a good qualitative picture of what happens to
\sigma(\bm{A})
when applying a rank-1 update (
3.10
).
Next, we want to quantify how far the eigenvalues are shifted depending on the size of
t
.
Again we follow
[
3
]
and utilize so-called potential functions.

Definition 3.5

(cf.
[
3
, Def. 3.2]
)
.

Let
\bm{I}
denote the identity matrix in
\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
.
For
l,u\in\mathds{R}
and a Hermitian matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
with eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\}\subset\mathds{R}

the
lower and upper potential functions

\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})
and
\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})
are given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})

\displaystyle:=\tr([\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})

\displaystyle:=\tr([u\bm{I}-\bm{A}]^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{u-\lambda_{i}}\,.

When
l
and
u
are lower, respectively upper, barriers for
\sigma(\bm{A})
, i.e., when
l<\lamin(\bm{A})
, respectively
\lamax(\bm{A})<u
,
these potential functions serve well as measures for the distance of
\sigma(\bm{A})
to the respective barriers.
Note that the so-measured distance counts in the whole spectrum of
\bm{A}
, i.e., the location of all eigenvalues matters.
Further, it holds: the larger the distance, the lower the potentials, and the smaller the distance, the larger the potentials.

From the qualitative discussion above it is clear that
the upper potential
\Phi^{u}(\bm{A}^{\prime})

becomes smaller when
t
decreases and larger when
t
increases.
The lower potential
\Phi_{l}(\bm{A}^{\prime})
behaves the other way round,
it becomes smaller when
t
increases and larger when
t
decreases.
Based on the Sherman-Morrison formula, we now precisely quantify the change of the potentials.

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
is Hermitian and let
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
be a vector.

(i)

For
l\notin\sigma(\bm{A})
and
t\neq-(\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v})^{-1}

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})=\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})-\frac{t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-2}\bm{v}}{1+t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}\,.

(ii)

For
u\notin\sigma(\bm{A})
and
t\neq(\bm{v}^{\ast}(u\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-1}\bm{v})^{-1}

\displaystyle\Phi^{u}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})=\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})+\frac{t\bm{v}^{\ast}(u\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-2}\bm{v}}{1-t\bm{v}^{\ast}(u\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-1}\bm{v}}\,.

Proof.

(i): The lower potential has the form

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\tr\Big(\big[\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}-l\bm{I}\big]^{-1}\Big)\,.

Using the Sherman-Morrison formula and properties of the trace, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\tr\bigg([\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}-\frac{t[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}}{1+t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\tr\big([\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\big)-\frac{t\tr\big([\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\big)}{1+t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})-\frac{t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-2}\bm{v}}{1+t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}\,.

(ii): Analogous to (i).
∎

Next, we turn our attention to barrier shifts, i.e., modifications
of the barriers of the form
l\rightsquigarrow l^{\prime}:=l+\delta_{L}
and
u\rightsquigarrow u^{\prime}:=u+\delta_{U}
.
Our concrete goal is to specify shifts
for which the spectrum of the updated matrix
\bm{A}^{\prime}
in (
3.10
)
is enclosed in
(l^{\prime},u^{\prime})
and the distance of
\sigma(\bm{A}^{\prime})
to the barriers has not decreased.
In other words, we want to find
l^{\prime},u^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{A}^{\prime})\subset(l^{\prime},u^{\prime})\,,\quad\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A}^{\prime})\leq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi^{u^{\prime}}(\bm{A}^{\prime})\leq\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})\,.

(3.11)

In Lemma
3.7
below, which corresponds to
[
3
, Lem. 3.3]
, we first handle the lower potential.

Lemma 3.7

(cf.
[
3
, Lem. 3.3]
)
.

Let
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
be Hermitian and assume
\Delta_{L}:=\lamin(\bm{A})-l>0
for
l\in\mathds{R}
,
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
be any vector.
Further let
\delta_{L}\in(-\infty,\Delta_{L})
and additionally assume
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,\delta_{L})>0
in case
\delta_{L}>0
,
where
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,\delta_{L}):=\infty
for
\delta_{L}=0
and otherwise

\displaystyle L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,\delta_{L}):=\frac{\bm{v}^{\ast}(\bm{A}-(l+\delta_{L})\bm{I})^{-2}\bm{v}}{\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A})-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})}-\bm{v}^{\ast}(\bm{A}-(l+\delta_{L})\bm{I})^{-1}\bm{v}\,.

(3.12)

Then precisely for
t\geq L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,\delta_{L})^{-1}
(with
\infty^{-1}=0
)

\displaystyle\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\leq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\quad\text{and}\quad\lamin(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})>l+\delta_{L}\,.

(3.13)

For
0<\delta_{L}<\Delta_{L}
with
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,\delta_{L})\leq 0
there are no
t\in\mathds{R}
satisfying (
3.13
).

Proof.

Let
l^{\prime}=l+\delta_{L}
. Due to
\delta_{L}<\Delta_{L}
, we have
\lamin(\bm{A})>l^{\prime}
and in particular
l^{\prime}\notin\sigma(\bm{A})
. Hence, from Lemma
3.6
, we directly derive

\displaystyle\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})=(\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A})-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A}))-\frac{t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-2}\bm{v}}{1+t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}\,.

Note further that always
\bm{v}^{\ast}(\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I})^{-2}\bm{v}>0
and
\bm{v}^{\ast}(\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I})^{-1}\bm{v}>0
.

The case
\delta_{L}=0
is clear. Here
\Phi_{l}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\leq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})
and
\lamin(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})>l
precisely for
t\geq 0=L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,0)^{-1}
.

Next we turn to
\delta_{L}>0
. Here we have the additional assumption
L_{\bm{A}}:=L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};l,\delta_{L})>0
. We hence have
t\geq L_{\bm{A}}^{-1}>0
which implies

0<1/t\leq L_{\bm{A}}
. We conclude

\displaystyle\frac{t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-2}\bm{v}}{1+t\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}\geq\frac{\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-2}\bm{v}}{L_{\bm{A}}+\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}}=\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A})-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\,.

(3.14)

In addition,
\lamin(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})>\lamin(\bm{A})>l^{\prime}
due to
t>0
.

Finally, if
\delta_{L}<0
then
\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A})<\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})
and thus
L_{\bm{A}}<-\bm{v}^{\ast}(\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I})^{-1}\bm{v}<0
.
Hence, in the range
0>t\geq L_{\bm{A}}^{-1}
we have
1/t\leq L_{\bm{A}}<-\bm{v}^{\ast}(\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I})^{-1}\bm{v}
and (
3.14
) is valid.
For
t\geq 0
(
3.14
) is also valid since then we have a negative right-hand side and a positive left-hand side there.
Also, clearly
\lamin(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\geq\lamin(\bm{A})>l^{\prime}
in case
t\geq 0
.
If
t<0
we argue by contradiction. Assuming
\lamin(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\leq l^{\prime}
, by continuity since
\lamin(\bm{A})>l^{\prime}
,
there would be
t^{\prime}
with
t\leq t^{\prime}<0
and
\lamin(\bm{A}+t^{\prime}\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})=l^{\prime}
.
But
\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A}+t^{\prime}\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\leq\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\leq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})<\infty
, which is a contradiction.

For the last statement, assume that
0<\delta_{L}<\Delta_{L}
and
L_{\bm{A}}\leq 0
.
Then necessarily
t>0
for (
3.13
). Further
\Phi_{l^{\prime}}(\bm{A})>\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})

and as a consequence
L_{\bm{A}}>-\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}
. However
1/t>0\geq L_{\bm{A}}
now
contradicts (
3.14
) since
\frac{1}{t}+\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}>L_{\bm{A}}+\bm{v}^{\ast}[\bm{A}-l^{\prime}\bm{I}]^{-1}\bm{v}>0
.
∎

In the next lemma, which corresponds to
[
3
, Lem. 3.4]
, we handle the upper potential, where the situation is dual to the one before.

Lemma 3.8

(cf.
[
3
, Lem. 3.4]
)
.

Let
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
be Hermitian and assume
\Delta_{U}:=u-\lamax(\bm{A})>0
for
u\in\mathds{R}
,
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

be any vector
.
Further let
\delta_{U}\in(-\Delta_{U},\infty)
and additionally assume
U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U})<0
in case
\delta_{U}<0
,
where
U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U}):=\infty
for
\delta_{U}=0
and otherwise

\displaystyle U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U}):=\frac{\bm{v}^{\ast}[(u+\delta_{U})\bm{I}-\bm{A}]^{-2}\bm{v}}{\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})-\Phi^{u+\delta_{U}}(\bm{A})}+\bm{v}^{\ast}[(u+\delta_{U})\bm{I}-\bm{A}]^{-1}\bm{v}\,.

(3.15)

Then precisely for
t\leq U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U})^{-1}
(with
\infty^{-1}=0
)

\displaystyle\Phi^{u+\delta_{U}}(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})\leq\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})\quad\text{and}\quad\lamax(\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast})<u+\delta_{U}\,.

(3.16)

For
-\Delta_{U}<\delta_{U}<0
with
U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U})\geq 0
there are no
t\in\mathds{R}
satisfying (
3.16
).

Proof.

Using duality, the proof carries over from Lemma
3.7
in a strictly analogous manner.
∎

Let us now assume that
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
is Hermitian with
\sigma(\bm{A})\subset(l,u)
.
Then, for any fixed vector
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
and any given
\delta_{U}
fulfilling the assumptions of Lemma
3.8
,
condition (
3.16
) can be ensured by choosing
t
small enough.
Similarly, condition (
3.13
) in Lemma
3.7
can always be ensured for large
t
if
\delta_{L}
satisfies the assumptions of this lemma.
It is unclear, however, if there exist
t\in\mathds{R}
which fulfill both conditions simultaneously, i.e., values for
t

).

Combining Lemma
3.7
and Lemma

It provides a precise condition when such

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
be a vector and
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
a Hermitian matrix with lower and upper barriers
l,u\in\mathds{R}
.
Let further
\delta_{L},\delta_{U}\in\mathds{R}

\delta_{L}<\Delta_{L}
and
\delta_{U}>-\Delta_{U}
, where
\Delta_{L}:=\lamin(\bm{A})-l
and
\Delta_{U}:=u-\lamax(\bm{A})

Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

With
L_{\bm{A}}:=L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U})
and
U_{\bm{A}}:=U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{v};u,\delta_{U})
as in (

\displaystyle{\mathrm{sgn}\,}\delta_{L}={\mathrm{sgn}\,}L_{\bm{A}}\,,\quad{\mathrm{sgn}\,}\delta_{U}={\mathrm{sgn}\,}U_{\bm{A}}\,,\quad L_{\bm{A}}^{-1}\leq U_{\bm{A}}^{-1}\,.

(3.17)

(ii)

There exist
t\in\mathds{R}
such that (
3.11
) is fulfilled for
\bm{A}^{\prime}:=\bm{A}+t\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}
,
l^{\prime}:=l+\delta_{L}
, and
u^{\prime}:=u+\delta_{U}
.

In case that (i) and (ii) hold true, (
3.11
) is fulfilled precisely for
t\in[L_{\bm{A}}^{-1},U_{\bm{A}}^{-1}]
.

Proof.

The corollary is a consequence of Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.8
.
To see this, note that
\delta_{U}>0
implies
U_{\bm{A}}>0
and that, similarly,
\delta_{L}<0
implies
L_{\bm{A}}<0
.
∎

3.4
The well-determined update step

Let us now focus back on the
BSS
algorithm.
The main result of this subsection, Lemma
3.11
, is the key tool to keep control of the spectra of the matrices
\bm{A}^{(k)}
in (
3.5
).
It provides a condition on the barrier shifts
\delta_{L}
and
\delta_{U}

to guarantee that, when the spectral window
(l^{(k)},u^{(k)})
of
\bm{A}^{(k)}
is shifted to
(l^{(k+1)},u^{(k+1)})
,
there is at least one frame vector
\bm{y}^{i}

such that condition (
3.17
) in Corollary
3.9
is fulfilled. Hence, there exists

t>0
such that (
3.11
) is fulfilled for the update
\bm{A}^{(k+1)}=\bm{A}^{(k)}+t\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}
, in particular
\sigma(\bm{A}^{(k+1)})\subset(l^{(k+1)},u^{(k+1)})
.

For the proof of Lemma
3.11
we need two auxiliary results.
The first result is Lemma
A.2
from the Appendix.
The second auxiliary result is
[
3
, Claim 3.6]
,
which we recall for convenience here.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\delta_{L},\,\epsilon_{L}>0
,
l\in\mathds{R}
,
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\}\subset\mathds{R}
.
If
\lambda_{i}>l
for
i\in[m]
,
0\leq\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l)^{-1}\leq\epsilon_{L}
, and
1/\delta_{L}-\epsilon_{L}\geq 0
,
then

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L})^{-2}}{\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L})^{-1}-\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l)^{-1}}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L}}\geq\frac{1}{\delta_{L}}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l}\,.

(3.18)

With this, we are ready to prove Lemma
3.11
,
which plays the same role in the proof of Theorem
3.1
as
[
3
, Lem. 3.5]
in the proof of
[
3
, Thm. 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
be a frame with frame bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
.
Let further
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
be Hermitian with
\sigma(\bm{A})\subset(l,u)
for
l,u\in\mathds{R}
and with corresponding potentials
\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})
,
\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})
.
If the quantities

\delta_{L},\,\delta_{U},\,\epsilon_{L},\,\epsilon_{U}>0

satisfy the condition

\displaystyle 0<\frac{B}{A}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{U}}+\epsilon_{U}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{L}}-\kappa\epsilon_{L}\,,\quad\epsilon_{L}\geq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\,,\quad\epsilon_{U}\geq\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})\,,

(3.19)

where
\kappa=\kappa(A,B)
is as in (
3.1
),
then there exists an index
i\in[M]
such that
condition (
3.17
) in Corollary
3.9
is fulfilled for
\bm{y}^{i}
,
and the indices with this property are precisely those where
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})

for
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})=L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i};l,\delta_{L})
and
U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})=U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i};u,\delta_{U})
as in (
3.12
) and (
3.15
).
The corresponding rank-1 updates
\bm{A}^{\prime}:=\bm{A}+t\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}
fulfill (
3.11
) with
l^{\prime}=l+\delta_{L}
and
u^{\prime}=u+\delta_{U}

for each
t>0
with

L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq 1/t\geq U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\,.

Proof.

First note that, according to our assumptions, we have
\epsilon_{L}\geq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})
and
0<\delta_{L}^{-1}-\kappa\epsilon_{L}
, which
implies

\displaystyle\delta_{L}<(\kappa\epsilon_{L})^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa}\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa}\Delta_{L}\,,

(3.20)

where
\Delta_{L}=\lamin(\bm{A})-l
and the last estimate is due to

\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l}\geq\frac{1}{\lamin(\bm{A})-l}=\Delta_{L}^{-1}\,.

In particular,
\delta_{L}<\Delta_{L}
since
\kappa\geq 1
. Further
\delta_{U}>0>-\Delta_{U}
for
\Delta_{U}=u-\lamax(\bm{A})
,
wherefore the quantities
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})
and
U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})
are well-defined (see (
3.12
) and (
3.15
)).

Now, analogous to the proof of
[
3
, Lem. 3.5]
, we aim to show

\displaystyle\sum_{i}L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq\sum_{i}U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\,.

(3.21)

On the one hand, using Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}((u+\delta_{U})\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-2}\bm{y}^{i}}{\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})-\Phi^{u+\delta_{U}}(\bm{A})}+\sum_{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}((u+\delta_{U})\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-1}\bm{y}^{i}

\displaystyle\leq B\bigg[\frac{\tr((u+\delta_{U})\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-2}}{\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})-\Phi^{u+\delta_{U}}(\bm{A})}+\tr((u+\delta_{U})\bm{I}-\bm{A})^{-1}\bigg]

\displaystyle=B\bigg[\frac{\sum_{i}(u+\delta_{U}-\lambda_{i})^{-2}}{\sum_{i}(u-\lambda_{i})^{-1}-\sum_{i}(u+\delta_{U}-\lambda_{i})^{-1}}+\Phi^{u+\delta_{U}}(\bm{A})\bigg]\,.

The denominator of the first term in the brackets can be estimated as follows,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\frac{1}{u-\lambda_{i}}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{u+\delta_{U}-\lambda_{i}}=\sum_{i}\frac{\delta_{U}}{(u-\lambda_{i})(u+\delta_{U}-\lambda_{i})}>\sum_{i}\frac{\delta_{U}}{(u+\delta_{U}-\lambda_{i})^{2}}\,.

Taking into account
\epsilon_{U}\geq\Phi^{u}(\bm{A})>\Phi^{u+\delta_{U}}(\bm{A})
, we obtain altogether

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{i}U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})<B\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{U}}+\epsilon_{U}\Big)\,.\end{aligned}

(3.22)

On the other hand, again using Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}L_{\bm{A}}

\displaystyle(\bm{y}^{i})=\frac{\sum_{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}(\bm{A}-(l+\delta_{L})\bm{I})^{-2}\bm{y}^{i}}{\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A})-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})}-\sum_{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}(\bm{A}-(l+\delta_{L})\bm{I})^{-1}\bm{y}^{i}

\displaystyle\geq A\bigg[\frac{\tr(\bm{A}-(l+\delta_{L})\bm{I})^{-2}}{\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A})-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})}\bigg]-B\bigg[\tr(\bm{A}-(l+\delta_{L})\bm{I})^{-1}\bigg]

\displaystyle=A\bigg[\frac{\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L})^{-2}}{\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L})^{-1}-\sum_{i}(\lambda_{i}-l)^{-1}}-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L}}\bigg]-(B-A)\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A})\,,

and the term in brackets can further be estimated by Lemma
3.10
.
The assumptions of this lemma are satisfied, in particular
\delta_{L}^{-1}-\epsilon_{L}\geq\delta_{L}^{-1}-\kappa\epsilon_{L}>0
due to
\kappa\geq 1
.
Using (
3.18
) leads to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{i}L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq A\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{L}}-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\Big)-(B-A)\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A})\,.\end{aligned}

(3.23)

We distinguish two cases,
A=B
and
A<B
, to derive

\displaystyle\sum_{i}L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq A\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{L}}-\kappa\epsilon_{L}\Big)\,.

(3.24)

If
A=B
then
\kappa=1
and (
3.24
) follows directly from (
3.23
), since
\epsilon_{L}\geq\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})
.
If
A<B
the argument is a bit more involved. We then have
\kappa>1
.
From (
3.20
) we further deduce
\delta_{L}<\kappa^{-1}(\lambda_{i}-l)
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
This allows for the estimate

\displaystyle\Phi_{l+\delta_{L}}(\bm{A})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l-\delta_{L}}\leq\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}-l}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big)^{-1}=\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1}\,.

Plugging this relation into (
3.23
) then also implies (
3.24
), namely

\displaystyle\sum_{i}L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq\frac{A}{\delta_{L}}-\Phi_{l}(\bm{A})\Big[A+(B-A)\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1}\Big]\geq A\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{L}}-\kappa\epsilon_{L}\Big)\,,

since
\kappa
as in (
3.1
) fulfills

\displaystyle A+(B-A)\frac{\kappa}{\kappa-1}=A\kappa\,.

Hence, we have finally established (
3.22
) and (
3.24
) and, in view of assumption (
3.19
),
these two results yield (
3.21
).

As a consequence, there exists
at least one
i\in[M]
so that
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})
. Furthermore,
U_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})>0

due to
\delta_{U}>0
, which in turn implies
L_{\bm{A}}(\bm{y}^{i})>0
. Since also
\delta_{L}>0
by assumption,
condition (
3.17
) in Corollary
3.9
is satisfied for
\bm{y}^{i}
.
The rest follows from Corollary
3.9
, which can indeed be applied due to
\delta_{L}<\Delta_{L}
and
\delta_{U}>-\Delta_{U}

as established at the beginning of this proof.
∎

With Lemma
3.11
we are now prepared to give a proof for Theorem
3.1
.

But even more holds true. For the further analysis of the algorithm, it is useful
to note that for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{L}^{(k)}}-\kappa\epsilon^{(k)}_{L}-\frac{B}{A}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{U}^{(k)}}+\epsilon^{(k)}_{U}\Big)=\Delta\Big(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big)\geq 0\,.

(3.25)

This can be easily checked for
k=0
by a direct calculation. The definition of the variable barrier shifts
\delta_{L}^{(k)}
and
\delta_{U}^{(k)}
in line
5
of the algorithm further ensures that this expression does not change with
k
.

Let us now assume that for some arbitrary
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{A}^{(k)})\subset(l^{(k)},u^{(k)})\quad,\quad\delta^{(k)}_{L},\,\delta^{(k)}_{U}>0\,,

(3.26)

which was already checked for
k=0
.
Then, due to (
3.25
), condition (
3.19
) of Lemma
3.11
is fulfilled with respect to
\bm{A}^{(k)}
.
Hence there is an index
i
such that condition (
3.17
) in Corollary
3.9
is satisfied for
\bm{y}^{i}
and
L^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq U^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{i})
, where (see line
9
of the
BSS
algorithm)

L^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{i})=L_{\bm{A}^{(k)}}\big(\bm{y}^{i};l^{(k)},\delta_{L}^{(k)}\big)\quad\text{and}\quad U^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{i})=U_{\bm{A}^{(k)}}\big(\bm{y}^{i};u^{(k)},\delta_{U}^{(k)}\big)\,.

Further, based on (
3.25
) and looking into the proof of Lemma
3.11
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{A}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\big[L^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{j})-U^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{j})\big]=\frac{1}{\delta_{L}^{(k)}}-\kappa\epsilon^{(k)}_{L}-\frac{B}{A}\Big(\frac{1}{\delta_{U}^{(k)}}+\epsilon^{(k)}_{U}\Big)\geq\Delta\Big(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big)\,,

which implies that there is always even an index
i\in[M]
such that
\bm{y}^{i}
satisfies

\displaystyle L^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{i})-U^{(k)}(\bm{y}^{i})\geq\frac{\Delta}{M}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big)\,.

Hence, an index
i
which satisfies the selection condition in line
10
is found stably in each iteration of the
BSS
algorithm. According to Lemma
3.11
,
such an index in particular satisfies condition (
3.17
) of Corollary
3.9
.

An update of
\bm{A}^{(k)}
to
\bm{A}^{(k+1)}
as in line
15
of the
BSS
algorithm thus yields a matrix with

\displaystyle\sigma(\bm{A}^{(k+1)})\subset(l^{(k+1)},u^{(k+1)})\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\epsilon_{L}^{(k+1)}\leq\epsilon_{L}^{(k)}\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\epsilon_{U}^{(k+1)}\leq\epsilon_{U}^{(k)}\,.

From this and
\delta_{L}^{(k)},\,\delta_{U}^{(k)}>0
, we deduce

\displaystyle\delta_{L}^{(k+1)}\geq\delta_{L}^{(k)}>0\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\delta_{U}^{(k+1)}\leq\delta_{U}^{(k)}\,.

(3.27)

Hence, (
3.26
) is fulfilled for
k+1
. Inductively, this proves that (
3.26
)
is fulfilled for
k=0,\ldots,n
.
In particular,
\sigma(\bm{A}^{(n)})\subset(l^{(n)},u^{(n)})
and due to (
3.27
)

l^{(n)}=l^{(0)}+\sum_{k=0}^{\lceil bm\rceil-1}\delta^{(k)}_{L}\geq l^{(0)}+bm\delta^{(0)}_{L}=-m\kappa\sqrt{b}+bm>0\,,

where
b>\kappa^{2}
was used in the last estimation step. Hence,

\displaystyle 0<l^{(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad l^{(n)}\bm{I}\preceq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tilde{s}^{(i)}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\preceq u^{(n)}\bm{I}\,.

The system

(\sqrt{\tilde{s}^{(i)}}\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
, where the
\tilde{s}^{(i)}
are the weights from
the output line
17
before the rescaling, is thus a frame with bounds
0<l^{(n)}\leq u^{(n)}<\infty
.
Finally, we can estimate with (
3.27
)

\displaystyle\frac{u^{(n)}}{l^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{(0)}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta^{(i)}_{U}}{l^{(0)}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\delta^{(i)}_{L}}\leq\frac{u^{(0)}+\lceil bm\rceil\delta^{(0)}_{U}}{l^{(0)}+\lceil bm\rceil\delta^{(0)}_{L}}=\frac{\delta^{(0)}_{U}}{\delta^{(0)}_{L}}+\frac{u^{(0)}-l^{(0)}\delta^{(0)}_{U}/\delta^{(0)}_{L}}{l^{(0)}+\lceil bm\rceil\delta^{(0)}_{L}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta^{(0)}_{U}}{\delta^{(0)}_{L}}+\frac{u^{(0)}-l^{(0)}\delta^{(0)}_{U}/\delta^{(0)}_{L}}{l^{(0)}+bm\delta^{(0)}_{L}}=\frac{u^{(0)}+bm\delta^{(0)}_{U}}{l^{(0)}+bm\delta^{(0)}_{L}}=\frac{B}{A}\gamma(1+\Delta)\,.

The rescaled system
(\sqrt{s^{(i)}}\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
with the actual output weights
s^{(i)}
from Algorithm
1

has thus frame bounds in the range
[A,B\gamma(1+\Delta)]
due to

\displaystyle l^{(n)}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{A}{l^{(n)}}+\frac{B\gamma(1+\Delta)}{u^{(n)}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(A+\frac{l^{(n)}}{u^{(n)}}B\gamma(1+\Delta)\Big)\geq A\,,

\displaystyle u^{(n)}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{A}{l^{(n)}}+\frac{B\gamma(1+\Delta)}{u^{(n)}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{u^{(n)}}{l^{(n)}}A+B\gamma(1+\Delta)\Big)\leq B\gamma(1+\Delta)\,.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.1
, choosing
\Delta=0
.

\blacksquare

4
Non-weighted subsampling of finite frames

We now turn to non-weighted versions of the subsampling strategies in
Sections
2
and
3
.
Our approach is to give estimates on the occurring weights. In this way, we are able to save the lower frame bounds. For many applications those are the important ones as they ensure the stable reconstruction of any vector
\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
from its frame coefficients
\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle
.
Results in this section will be of the following form:

Given vectors
\bm{y}^{1},\dots,\bm{y}^{M}
, we seek inequalities of the type

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{|J|}\sum_{i\in J}|\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

(4.1)

for
J\subset[M]
and some fixed constant
C>0
.
If the initial
\bm{y}^{1},\dots,\bm{y}^{M}
satisfy a lower frame bound, (
4.1
) gives that the vectors
\bm{y}^{i}
,
i\in J
, satisfy a lower frame bound as well.

For the non-weighted version of the random subsampling in Theorem
2.1
the construction of
\bm{\tilde{Y}}
is covered by Lemma
3.4
. We obtain the following result with
|J|=\mathcal{O}(m\log m)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
be a frame and
c,p,t\in(0,1)
and
n\in\mathds{N}
be such that

\displaystyle n\geq\frac{3}{ct^{2}}m\log\left(\frac{m}{p}\right)\,.

Drawing
n
indices
J\subset[M]
(with duplicates) i.i.d. according to the discrete probability density
\varrho_{i}=(1-c)/M+c\cdot\|\bm{\tilde{y}^{i}}\|_{2}^{2}/m
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(1-c)(1-t)}\frac{1}{|J|}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

with probability exceeding
1-p
.

Proof.

Similar to Theorem
2.1
, the result follows from Tropp’s result in Lemma
A.3
.
Here it is applied to the random rank-1 matrices

\bm{A}_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\varrho^{-1}_{i}\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\otimes\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}
, where
\bm{\tilde{y}}^{1},\dots,\bm{\tilde{y}}^{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
are the rows of the matrix
\bm{\tilde{Y}}
obtained according to Lemma
3.4
from
\bm{Y}
, the analysis operator (
2.1
) of
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
. The matrices
\bm{A}_{i}
satisfy

\displaystyle\lamax(\bm{A}_{i})=\frac{1}{n}\varrho_{i}^{-1}\|\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{m}{cn}\,.

For
n=|J|
independent copies
(\bm{A}_{i})_{i\in J}
, due to the orthogonality of
\bm{\tilde{Y}}
, we further have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\mathbb{E}\bm{A}_{i}=\sum_{i\in J}\mathds{E}\left(\frac{1}{n}\varrho^{-1}_{i}\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\otimes\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\right)=\sum_{i\in J}\frac{1}{n}\bm{\tilde{Y}}^{*}\bm{\tilde{Y}}=\bm{I}\,,

where
\bm{I}
is the
m\times m
dimensional identity matrix.
Thus,
\mu_{\min}=\lamin(\sum_{i\in J}\mathbb{E}\bm{A}_{i})=1
and Lemma
A.3
gives

\displaystyle\mathds{P}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\varrho_{i}^{-1}\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\otimes\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\right)\leq 1-t\right)

\displaystyle\leq m\exp\left(-\frac{cn}{m}\frac{t^{2}}{3}\right)\,,

which is smaller than
p
by the assumption on
n
.
Using
\varrho_{i}\geq(1-c)/M
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\bm{\tilde{Y}}\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\varrho_{i}^{-1}|\langle\bm{a},\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq\frac{M}{(1-c)(1-t)}\frac{1}{n}\|(\bm{\tilde{Y}}\bm{a})|_{J}\|_{2}^{2}

for all
\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
with probability exceeding
1-p
.
By the arguments in (
3.9
) and after we may replace
\bm{\tilde{Y}}
with the original
\bm{Y}
to obtain the assertion.
∎

Next, we assume that we have a Bessel sequence in
\mathds{C}^{m}
with elements that are norm-bounded from
below.
Applying Algorithm
2
(
BSS
⟂
) then yields a non-weighted inequality of type (
4.1
)
with
|J|=\mathcal{O}(m)
.
In Section
5
this algorithm is used in the experiments 1-3.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
be a Bessel sequence in
\mathds{C}^{m}
, i.e., a set of vectors satisfying the upper bound in (
1.1
) for some
B>0
. Further assume
M\geq m
and

\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\beta m/M
for some
\beta>0
and all
i\in[M]
.
Then, for any
b>1
, there exists a subset
J\subset[M]
with
|J|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
(without duplicates) such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{B}{\beta}\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}\,.

Proof.

Applying Algorithm
2
(
BSS
⟂
) to the sequence
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
yields weights
s_{i}\geq 0
, where
|\{i:s_{i}\neq 0\}|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
. Recall that, by the discussion
of Algorithm
2
, its application to any input sequence

(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
is possible provided
M\geq m
. We obtain (
3.8
). Taking into account the
Bessel property of
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
and choosing
\Delta=0
in Algorithm
2
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in J}s_{i}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}}B\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}

(4.2)

for
J:=\{i:s_{i}\neq 0\}
and all
\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
.
Setting
\bm{a}=\bm{y}^{j}
for
j\in J
, we obtain by the assumption
\|\bm{y}^{j}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\beta m/M
and the upper estimate in (
4.2
)

\displaystyle s_{j}\leq\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}B}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}\|\bm{y}^{j}\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{B}{\beta}\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}}\frac{M}{m}\,.

Thus, by the lower estimate in (
4.2
), we obtain the assertion.
∎

The condition on the norms
\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}
in Lemma
4.2
can be dropped with a more elaborate subsampling strategy,
PlainBSS
(see below) instead of
BSS
⟂
.
The ‘preconditioning’ in
PlainBSS
is based on Lemma
4.3
rather than Lemma
3.4
. The final result is stated in Corollary
4.5
.
The price we pay for this is the dependence of the constant in terms of the oversampling factor
b
. It deteriorates to
(b-1)^{-3}
while in the previous result it is
(b-1)^{-2}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\bm{Y}\in\mathds{C}^{M\times m}
be a matrix and
K\in\{0,\dots,M\}
.
Then there is a matrix
\bm{\tilde{Y}}\in\mathds{C}^{M\times m^{\prime}}
with

m^{\prime}\in\{K,\dots,K+m\}
and rows
\bm{\tilde{y}}^{1},\dots,\bm{\tilde{y}}^{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m^{\prime}}
such that

\displaystyle\range(\bm{\tilde{Y}})\supset\range(\bm{Y})\,,\quad\bm{\tilde{Y}}^{\ast}\bm{\tilde{Y}}=\bm{I}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{K}{M}\,,

where
\bm{I}
is the
m^{\prime}\times m^{\prime}
dimensional identity matrix.

Proof.

Let us denote the columns of
\bm{Y}
with
\bm{c}^{1},\dots,\bm{c}^{m}
. Further define columns in
\mathds{C}^{M}
by

\displaystyle d^{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\Big[\exp\Big(2\pi\mathrm{i}k\frac{j}{M}\Big)\Big]_{j=1}^{M}

for
k=1,\dots,K
, which are the first
K
columns of a Fourier matrix.
By construction the system
(\bm{d}^{k})_{k=1}^{K}
is orthonormal.
It can hence be extended by appropriate vectors
\bm{\tilde{c}}^{1},\ldots,\bm{\tilde{c}}^{l}
to an orthonormal basis of

\spn\{\bm{d}^{1},\dots,\bm{d}^{K},\bm{c}^{1},\dots,\bm{c}^{m}\}\,.

Those can be constructed e.g. via the Gram-Schmidt algorithm.
Finally, we set up

\displaystyle\bm{\tilde{Y}}:=\left[\,\bm{d}^{1}\,\middle|\,\cdots\,\middle|\,\bm{d}^{K}\,\middle|\,\bm{\tilde{c}}^{1}\,\middle|\,\cdots\,\middle|\,\bm{\tilde{c}}^{l}\,\right]=\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{\tilde{y}}^{1})^{\ast}\\[-4.30554pt]
\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\\[-4.30554pt]
\vdots\\[-8.1805pt]
\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt\\
(\bm{\tilde{y}}^{M})^{\ast}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathds{C}^{M\times(K+l)}\,,

which fulfills the stated conditions.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
be a sequence of vectors in
\mathds{C}^{m}
and
K\in\{0,\dots,M\}
.
Then, for any
b>1
, a set of indices
J\subset[M]
(without duplicates) can be constructed (in polynomial time) such that
|J|\leq\lceil b(K+m)\rceil
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}}\frac{m}{K}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}\,.

(4.3)

Proof.

We construct the vectors
\bm{\tilde{y}}^{1},\dots,\bm{\tilde{y}}^{M}
according to Lemma
4.3
.
They form a tight frame in
\mathds{C}^{m^{\prime}}
with
m^{\prime}\in\{K,\dots,K+m\}
and
\|\bm{\tilde{y}}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{K}{M}
for all
i\in[M]
.
We can thus apply Lemma
4.2
(
BSS
⟂
, which in effect is here
BSS)
with
B=1
. We obtain a subset

J\subset[M]
with
|J|\leq\lceil bm^{\prime}\rceil\leq\lceil b(K+m)\rceil
(without duplicates) such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}\,

which finishes the proof.
∎

A result in terms of the ‘real’ oversampling factor
b^{\prime}
in Theorem
4.4
, determined by
\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil=\lceil b(K+m)\rceil
, is given in Corollary
4.5
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\bm{y}^{1},\dots,\bm{y}^{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
be vectors with
m\in\mathds{N}
. Further, take
b^{\prime}>1+\frac{1}{m}
and assume
M\geq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
.
We then obtain indices
J^{\prime}\subset[M]
with
|J^{\prime}|\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq 89\frac{(b^{\prime}+1)^{2}}{(b^{\prime}-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in J^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}\,.

Proof.

The idea is to apply Theorem
4.4
for specifically chosen
K\in\mathds{N}
and
b>1
, such that
\lceil b(m+K)\rceil\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
for the given
b^{\prime}
and the prefactor in (
4.3
) becomes small.
Theorem
4.4
yields the prefactor

\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{2}}{(\sqrt{b}-1)^{2}}\frac{m}{K}=\frac{(\sqrt{b}+1)^{4}}{(b-1)^{2}}\frac{m}{K}\leq 4\frac{(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{2}}\frac{m}{K}\eqqcolon C(K)\,.

Choosing
b
and
K
such that
b^{\prime}=b\frac{m+K}{m}
gives

\displaystyle b=b^{\prime}/(1+K/m)\,,\quad b+1=(b^{\prime}+1+K/m)/(1+K/m)\,,\quad b-1=(b^{\prime}-1-K/m)/(1+K/m)\,,

and hence

C(K)=4\Big(\frac{b^{\prime}+1+\frac{K}{m}}{b^{\prime}-1-\frac{K}{m}}\Big)^{2}\frac{m}{K}\,.

(4.4)

We now choose
K^{\star}=\lceil\frac{(b^{\prime}-1)m}{8}\rceil\in[M]
. Assuming
b^{\prime}\geq 1+4/m
, we can then bound

\frac{b^{\prime}-1}{8}\leq\frac{K^{\star}}{m}\leq\frac{b^{\prime}-1}{4}\,.

Further, since
b^{\prime}-1-K^{\star}/m>0
, we arrive at the estimate

\displaystyle C(K^{\star})\leq 4\Big(\frac{b^{\prime}+1+(b^{\prime}-1)/4}{b^{\prime}-1-(b^{\prime}-1)/4}\Big)^{2}\frac{8}{b^{\prime}-1}=\frac{32}{b^{\prime}-1}\Big(\frac{5b^{\prime}/4+3/4)}{3(b^{\prime}-1)/4}\Big)^{2}\leq 32\Big(\frac{5}{3}\Big)^{2}\frac{(b^{\prime}+1)^{2}}{(b^{\prime}-1)^{3}}\leq 89\frac{(b^{\prime}+1)^{2}}{(b^{\prime}-1)^{3}}\,.

(4.5)

Next, we consider the cases
b^{\prime}=1+2/m
and
b^{\prime}=1+3/m
separately, where in both
K^{\star}=1
.
The associated
b
are given by
b=1+1/(m+1)
and
b=1+2/(m+1)
. Further

1/m=(b^{\prime}-1)/2
and
1/m=(b^{\prime}-1)/3
. Inserting these values into (
4.4
),
we obtain estimates for
C(K^{\star})
as in (
4.5
). The prefactors, being
72
and
48
,
are even smaller than
89
.
Finally, to extend the estimate (
4.5
) to the whole range
b^{\prime}>1+\tfrac{1}{m}
, note that the right-hand side of (
4.5
) is increasing for
b^{\prime}\searrow 1
. Taking into account
\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil=m+k+1
for each
k\in\mathds{N}
and
b^{\prime}\in(1+\tfrac{k}{m},1+\tfrac{k+1}{m}]
, we are finished.
∎

Building on the proof of Corollary
4.5
, we now formulate Algorithm
3
(
PlainBSS
).
Like Algorithm
1
(
BSS
) and Algorithm
2
(
BSS
⟂
), it is polynomial in time.

Algorithm 3

PlainBSS

Input:

Vectors
\bm{y}^{1},\dots,\bm{y}^{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
with
m\in\mathds{N}
and
M\geq m+2
;

Oversampling factor
b^{\prime}
s.t.
m+2\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil\leq M
; Stability factor
\Delta\geq 0
.

Output:

Indices
J\subset[M]
such that
|J|\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
and

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle|^{2}\leq 89\frac{(b^{\prime}+1)^{2}}{(b^{\prime}-1)^{3}}\frac{1+\Delta}{m}\sum_{i\in J}|\langle\bm{a},\bm{y}^{i}\rangle|^{2}
.

1:

Compute
K^{\star}
and
b
from
b^{\prime}
as in the proof of Corollary
4.5
.

2:

Construct vectors
\bm{\tilde{y}}^{1},\dots,\bm{\tilde{y}}^{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m^{\prime}}
with
K=K^{\star}
according to Lemma
4.3
, where the initial
\bm{Y}\in\mathds{C}^{M\times m}
is the matrix (
2.1
) with rows
(\bm{y}^{1})^{\ast},\dots,(\bm{y}^{M})^{\ast}
.

3:

Apply Algorithm
1
(
BSS
) to
\bm{\tilde{y}}^{1},\dots,\bm{\tilde{y}}^{M}
with oversampling factor
b
and stability factor
\Delta
to obtain weights
s_{1},\dots,s_{M}
.

4:

return

indices
J\coloneqq\{i:s_{i}\neq 0\}
.

For a better runtime, it might sometimes be advantageous to
combine
BSS
subsampling with a preceding random subsampling step. Theorem
2.1
could be used, for instance, to quickly reduce the number of vectors to
\mathcal{O}(m\log(m))
in case of very large
M
.
In the following corollary such a two-step procedure is used to construct a
unit-norm frame with very few (close to
m
) elements and well-behaved frame bounds.
Here it is crucial that the
BSS
algorithm returns no duplicates, which is used in the proof.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume that the vectors
\bm{y}^{1},\dots,\bm{y}^{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m},m\in\mathds{N}
, form a tight frame
and let
b^{\prime}>1+\frac{1}{m}
.
Further choose
p,t\in(0,1)
and draw

\displaystyle n:=\left\lceil\frac{3}{t^{2}}m\log\left(\frac{2m}{p}\right)\right\rceil

indices
J\subset[M]
(with duplicates) i.i.d. according to the discrete probability density
\varrho_{i}=\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}/\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}
. In case
n>\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
, those can further be subsampled
using
BSS
(with oversampling factor
b^{\prime}
) giving
J^{\prime}\subset J
with
|J^{\prime}|\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil

and a unit-norm frame
(\bm{y}^{i}/\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2})_{i\in J^{\prime}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{(1-t)(b^{\prime}-1)^{3}}{89(b^{\prime}+1)^{2}}\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{i\in J^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\frac{\bm{y}^{i}}{\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq(1+t)\left\lceil\frac{3\log(2m/p)}{t^{2}}\right\rceil\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}

for all
\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
with probability exceeding
1-p
. Otherwise, when
n\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
,
the frame
(\bm{y}^{i}/\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2})_{i\in J^{\prime}}
with
J^{\prime}=J
already satisfies
|J^{\prime}|\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
and (
4.6
) for all
\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
with probability exceeding
1-p
.

Proof.

By (
2.2
) and
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
forming a tight frame, we have
\|\bm{Y}\|_{F}^{2}=mA
.
By Theorem
2.1
we first obtain a subframe with
n=|J|
elements such that

\displaystyle\frac{1-t}{m}\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\frac{\bm{y}^{i}}{\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{1+t}{m}\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

(4.6)

Next, if we apply Algorithm
3
(
PlainBSS
) to this subframe, we obtain
J^{\prime}\subset J

with
|J^{\prime}|\leq\lceil b^{\prime}m\rceil
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\frac{\bm{y}^{i}}{\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq 89\frac{(b^{\prime}+1)^{2}}{(b^{\prime}-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in J^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\frac{\bm{y}^{i}}{\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\,,

which is used in the lower frame bound.
For the upper frame bound we use that
J^{\prime}
has no duplicates, wherefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in J^{\prime}}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\frac{\bm{y}^{i}}{\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\left\lceil\frac{3\log(2m/p)}{t^{2}}\right\rceil\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in J}\left|\left\langle\bm{a},\frac{\bm{y}^{i}}{\|\bm{y}^{i}\|_{2}}\right\rangle\right|^{2}\,.

Here, the relation of
n
and
m
was used.
Last, we use the upper frame bound (
4.6
) and obtain the assertion.
∎

6
Applications and discussion

Finally, we apply the subsampling results from the previous sections
to the problem of
L_{2}
(-stable) recovery of multivariate complex-valued functions
f\colon D\to\mathds{C}
. Those are assumed to be given
on some measure space
(D,\nu)
and the considered task shall be to recover
f

from sampling values

\displaystyle\bm{f}_{n}:=(f(x^{1}),\ldots,f(x^{n}))\in\mathds{C}^{n}

(6.1)

taken at certain sampling nodes

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{n}:=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})\in D^{n}\,.

(6.2)

In order to give sense to the point evaluation
f(x^{i})
(i.e. to ensure that it represents a continuous functional), we model
f
to belong either to a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
H(K)
on
D
or to
\ell_{\infty}(D)
, the space of bounded functions on
D
.
Since (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) is
usually insufficient information for an exact reconstruction of
f
,
we merely seek to find good approximants
\tilde{f}
of
f
. The approximation shall take place in
L_{2}(D,\nu)
, the space
of square-integrable complex-valued functions with

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}}=\int_{D}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L_{2}}=\left(\int_{D}|f(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\right)^{1/2}\,.

A possible way to recover
f
from the given data (
6.1
) and (
6.2
)
is to apply a weighted least squares reconstruction operator

\displaystyle S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f:=\argmin\limits_{g\in V_{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{m}(x^{i})|g(x^{i})-f(x^{i})|^{2}\,,

(6.3)

for certain weights
w_{m}(x^{i})
and some
m
-dimensional reconstruction space
V_{m}\subset H(K)\subset L_{2}(D,\nu)
(or, alternatively
V_{m}\subset\ell_{\infty}(D)
) .
Such operators perform well in many scenarios, depending on the utilized node set
\bm{X}_{n}
, the space
V_{m}
, and the weights
w_{m}(x^{i})
.
Of particular interest are plain least squares operators, where
w_{m}(x^{i})=1
for
i\in[n]
.
For those we will use the simpler notation
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m}}
.

To practically determine
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f
,
it is useful to employ a basis
(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{m})
of
V_{m}
. The coefficients
\bm{c}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

of
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f
in this basis can then be obtained by solving

\bm{c}=\argmin\limits_{\bm{c}\in\mathds{C}^{m}}\|\bm{L}_{n,m}\cdot\bm{c}-\bm{f}_{n}\|_{\ell_{2,w}}^{2}=\argmin\limits_{\bm{c}\in\mathds{C}^{m}}\|\bm{W}_{n}(\bm{L}_{n,m}\cdot\bm{c}-\bm{f}_{n})\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\,,

(6.4)

where
\bm{L}_{n,m}:=(\eta_{k}(x^{i}))_{k=1\ldots m}^{i=1\ldots n}\in\mathds{C}^{n\times m}
and
\bm{W}_{n}:=\diag(\sqrt{w_{m}(x^{1})}),\ldots,\sqrt{w_{m}(x^{n})})\in\mathds{C}^{n\times n}
.
For this to make sense, the
\eta_{k}
are assumed to be proper functions, not equivalence classes, in
L_{2}(D,\nu)
(see e.g. the explanation after (
B.2
)).

If the matrix
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m}:=\bm{W}_{n}\bm{L}_{n,m}

has full column rank, the solution of (
6.4
) is unique and can be
expressed by

\bm{c}=(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m})^{\dagger}\bm{W}_{n}\mathbf{f}_{n}\,,

where
(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m})^{\dagger}
is the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m}
and has the explicit form

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m})^{\dagger}=((\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m})^{\ast}\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m})^{-1}(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{n,m})^{\ast}\,.

(6.5)

6.1
Sampling recovery in spaces of finite measure

We first consider the case of reconstructing functions
f
from
\ell_{\infty}(D)
.
We hereby assume that
L_{2}(D,\nu)
is equipped with a finite measure
\nu
. In this case,

\ell_{\infty}(D)\hookrightarrow L_{2}(D,\nu)
and there is a constant
D_{\nu}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\leq D_{\nu}\|f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}\quad\text{for all }f\in\ell_{\infty}(D)\,.

(6.6)

Our primary goal is now to derive unweighted (left) Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities for
m
-dimensional subspaces of
\ell_{\infty}(D)
. We let
m\in\mathds{N}
and
V_{m}:=\spn(\eta_{k})_{k=1}^{m}
. The spanning set
(\eta_{k})_{k=1}^{m}
shall
be a fixed orthonormal basis of
V_{m}
. Then we define a new sampling measure
d\mu
as
d\mu:=\varphi^{Vm}(\cdot)d\nu
with

\displaystyle\varphi^{V_{m}}(x):=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\eta_{k}(x)|^{2}}{m}

(6.7)

and draw random sampling nodes according to this measure, which is independent of the chosen orthonormal basis since
\varphi^{V_{m}}
in (
6.7
)
is unique up to
\nu
-null sets. This density first appeared in
[
30
]
.

For a sufficiently large number of sampling nodes we have the following result.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p,t\in(0,1)
and let
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}=(\tilde{x}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\in D^{M}
be
M
nodes drawn independently (with duplicates)
according to the probability measure
\mu
on
D
given by (
6.7
). In case

\displaystyle M\geq\frac{4}{t^{2}}m\log\left(\frac{m}{p}\right)

(6.8)

it holds

\displaystyle(1-t)\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{M}\|\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all ${\bm{a}}\in\mathds{C}^{m}$}

with probability exceeding
1-p
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{llll}{[}\eta_{1}/\sqrt{\varphi^{V_{m}}}{]}(\tilde{x}^{1})&\cdots&{[}\eta_{m}/\sqrt{\varphi^{V_{m}}}{]}(\tilde{x}^{1})\\
\qquad\vdots&&\qquad\vdots\\
{[}\eta_{1}/\sqrt{\varphi^{V_{m}}}{]}(\tilde{x}^{M})&\cdots&{[}\eta_{m}/\sqrt{\varphi^{V_{m}}}{]}(\tilde{x}^{M})\end{array}\right)\,.

Proof.

Let
\bm{u}^{i}
,
i\in[M]
, denote the rows of
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}
and define
\bm{A}_{i}:=\frac{1}{M}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}
.
Then we have
\lamax(\bm{A}_{i})=\frac{\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}}{M}\leq\frac{2m}{M}
due to
\varphi^{V_{m}}\geq\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\eta_{k}|^{2}
(see (
6.7
)).
The matrix

\bm{H}_{m}:=\frac{1}{M}\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}^{\ast}\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{A}_{i}

is further Hermitian positive semi-definite and fulfills
\mathbb{E}(\bm{H}_{m})=\bm{I}
, where
\bm{I}
is the identity matrix in
\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
. The latter follows from the orthogonality of the function system
(\eta_{i}/\sqrt{\varphi^{V_{m}}})_{i=1}^{m}
in
L_{2}(D,\mu)
.

Lemma
A.3
, applied with
\mu_{\min}=\mu_{\max}=1
and
R=2m/M
, now states that

\displaystyle\lamin(\bm{H}_{m})\leq 1-t

with probability not more than
m\exp(-Mt^{2}/(4m))
. If we choose
M
according to (
6.8
), this then yields

\frac{1}{M}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}\bm{w}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\bm{w}^{\ast}\bm{H}_{m}\bm{w}\geq(1-t)\|\bm{w}\|_{2}^{2}

with probability exceeding
1-p
.
∎

In the following, let
b>1+\frac{1}{m}
be a fixed parameter. Further, let
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}=(\tilde{x}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
be a node sequence
sampled according to Lemma
6.1
fulfilling
M\geq\lceil bm\rceil
.
Applying the
plainBSS
algorithm to
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}
(i.e. the rows of
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}
) with respect to
b
yields
an index set
J\subset[M]
with
|J|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
. Selecting the corresponding nodes
in
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}
, we obtain a subsequence

\bm{X}_{n}=(x^{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}
with
n\leq\lceil bm\rceil
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(D,\nu)
be a finite measure space and
p,t\in(0,1)
. Let further
V_{m}
be an
m
-dimensional subspace of
\ell_{\infty}(D)
for fixed
m\in\mathds{N}
.
Let further
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}=(\tilde{x}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\in D^{M}
and
\bm{X}_{n}=(x^{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in D^{n}
denote the node sets constructed as above for
b>1+\frac{1}{m}
, with
M=M_{p,t}
satisfying (
6.8
) and

n\leq\lceil bm\rceil\leq M
.
Then with probability exceeding
1-p
for all
f\in V_{m}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{M(1-t)}\sum_{i=1}^{M}|f(\bm{\tilde{}}{x}^{i})|^{2}\leq\frac{178(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}(1-t)}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(x^{i})|^{2}\,.

(6.9)

Proof.

Let
\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
be the coefficient vector of
f
with respect to
(\eta_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
. The first inequality follows from Lemma
6.1
and the fact that
\varphi^{V_{m}}\geq 1/2
.
We have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}^{2}=\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{\|\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}}{M(1-t)}=\frac{1}{M(1-t)}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{|f(\tilde{x}^{i})|^{2}}{\varphi^{V_{m}}(\tilde{x}^{i})}\leq\frac{2}{M(1-t)}\sum_{i=1}^{M}|f(\tilde{x}^{i})|^{2}\,.

An application of Corollary
4.5
proves the second inequality.
∎

From this, we can directly derive a recovery result for functions
f\in\ell_{\infty}(D)
. Earlier versions of this result can be found in
[
8
]
and
[
34
]
. The relation between the
L_{2}
recovery error and the
\ell_{\infty}
best approximation has been first established in
[
8
]
. The main contribution here is that we prove the existence of a plain least squares recovery operator. In fact, the following theorem is a consequence of
[
34
, Thm. 2.1]
together with our Theorem
6.2
above. For the convenience of the reader we give a proof.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
V_{m}\subset\ell_{\infty}(D)
with dimension
m\in\mathds{N}
and
\bm{X}_{n}=(x^{i})_{i=1}^{n}

be as above, with
n\leq\lceil bm\rceil
and
b>1+\frac{1}{m}
,
fulfilling (
6.9
) (with high probability).
For any
f\in\ell_{\infty}(D)
the plain least squares operator
S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}
recovers
f
in
L_{2}(D,\nu)
with the following error

\|f-S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}^{2}\leq C_{\nu}\frac{b^{3}}{(b-1)^{3}}e(f,V_{m})_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,,

with a constant
C_{\nu}>0
that only depends on
\nu
, where

e(f,V_{m})_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}:=\inf\limits_{g\in V_{m}}\|f-g\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}\,.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, we obtain for any
g\in V_{m}

\|f-S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}=\|f-g\|^{2}_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}+\|g-S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\,.

The fact that
\nu
is a finite measure gives
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\leq D_{\nu}\|f-g\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}
(see (
6.6
)). The second summand equals

\|S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}(g-f)\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}
, which can be estimated by Theorem
6.2
, namely

\begin{split}\|S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}&(g-f)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\leq\frac{Cb^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}(1-t)}\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|(S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}(f-g))({x}^{i})|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{2Cb^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}(1-t)}\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|(S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}(f-g))({x}^{i})-(f-g)({x}^{i})|^{2}+|(f-g)({x}^{i})|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{4Cb^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}(1-t)}\frac{1}{m}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|(f-g)({x}^{i})|^{2}\leq\frac{4\tilde{C}b^{3}}{(b-1)^{3}(1-t)}\|f-g\|^{2}_{\ell_{\infty}}\,.\end{split}

From the second to the third line, we hereby used

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|(S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}_{n}}}(f-g))({x}^{i})-(f-g)({x}^{i})|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|(f-g)({x}^{i})|^{2}\,.

Choosing
g\in V_{m}
such that
\|f-g\|_{\ell_{\infty}}\leq 2e(f,V_{m})_{\ell_{\infty}}
yields the result.
∎

At last, let us define the following quantity for a function class
F\subset\ell_{\infty}(D)
,

g_{n,m}^{\mathrm{ls}}(F,L_{2}(D,\nu)):=\inf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}V_{m}\subset\ell_{\infty}(D)\\
\mathrm{dim}V_{m}=m\end{subarray}}\inf\limits_{{\bm{X}}_{n}=({x}^{1},\cdots,{x}^{n})\in D^{n}}\sup\limits_{f\in F}\|f-S_{V_{m}}^{{\bm{X}}_{n}}f\|_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\,.

(6.10)

It measures the error of an optimal plain least squares algorithm of the above type,
using
n
nodes and an
m
-dimensional reconstruction space. Our last result of this subsection, Corollary
6.4
,
compares this quantity with the Kolmogorov number

\displaystyle d_{m}(F,\ell_{\infty}(D)):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subset\ell_{\infty}(D)\\
\dim(W)=m\end{subarray}}\sup_{f\in F}\min_{w\in W}\|f-w\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}

(6.11)

of the class
F
. It is a direct consequence of Theorem
6.3
. The constant
C_{\nu}>0

only depends on
\nu
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
F
be a class of functions in
\ell_{\infty}(D)
. Then for
m\in\mathds{N}
and
b>1+\frac{1}{m}

g_{\lceil bm\rceil,m}^{\mathrm{ls}}(F,L_{2}(D,\nu))\leq C_{\nu}\frac{b^{3/2}}{(b-1)^{3/2}}d_{m}(F,\ell_{\infty}(D))\,.

This estimate improves on a recent result by Temlyakov
[
34
]
, where the quantity
g_{\lceil bm\rceil,m}^{\mathrm{ls}}(F,L_{2}(D,\nu))
is related to a modified Kolmogorov width which includes an additional restriction on the admissible subspaces in (
6.11
).

6.2
Sampling recovery in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces

We next consider functions from a RKHS
H(K)
with a finite trace kernel
K
. The RKHS is
assumed to be compactly embedded into
L_{2}(D,\nu)
via a Hilbert-Schmidt embedding
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
. The measure
\nu
does not have to be a finite measure as in Subsection
6.1
. In this setting, the number of non-zero singular values of
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
is countable and, under the additional assumption that the subspace
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}(H(K))
of
L_{2}(D,\nu)
is infinite-dimensional, also infinite. We thus have
a sequence
(\sigma_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
of strictly positive singular numbers, which we order in descending order.
The associated left and right singular functions shall be denoted by
(\eta_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
(e_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
(see
B
for more details).
We follow the course of
[
11
,
20
,
19
,
25
,
27
]
, where this setting was considered as well.

A natural reconstruction space in this scenario is
V_{m}:=\spn(\eta_{k})_{k=1}^{m}
spanned by
the first
m
left singular functions associated to the
m
largest singular numbers of
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
.
Appropriate nodes and weights for
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}
are constructed in a two-step procedure,
similar to the node generation in Subsection
6.1
.
The initial node set
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}
is drawn according to a probability measure
d\varrho_{m}:=\varrho_{m}(\cdot)d\nu
with

\varrho_{m}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}|\eta_{k}(x)|^{2}+\frac{K(x,x)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}|e_{k}(x)|^{2}}{\int_{D}K(x,x)d\nu(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma^{2}_{k}}\bigg)

(6.12)

as density function. The corresponding weight function is

\displaystyle w_{m}:D\to[0,\infty)\quad,\quad w_{m}(x):=\begin{cases}\varrho_{m}(x)^{-1/2}\quad&,\,\varrho_{m}(x)\neq 0\,,\\
0&,\,\varrho_{m}(x)=0\,.\end{cases}

The set
\bm{X}_{n}
is then again obtained from
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}
by
PlainBSS
. Note
that (
6.12
) is well-defined for all
m\in\mathds{N}
due to the positivity of the
singular numbers.

Generation of sampling nodes

Step 1 (Initial nodes)

Let
m\in\mathds{N}
and
b>1+\frac{1}{m}
and fix parameters
p,t\in(0,1)
. Then, with

\displaystyle M=\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{4}{t^{2}}m\log\Big(\frac{m}{p}\Big)\right\rceil,\lceil bm\rceil\right\}\,,

(6.13)

an initial random sampling set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}:=(\tilde{x}^{1},\ldots,\tilde{x}^{M})\in D^{M}

(6.14)

is drawn, each node independently according to the measure
d\varrho_{m}
with density (
6.12
).

For the associated weights almost surely
w_{m}(\tilde{x}^{i})>0
.
Furthermore, with probability exceeding
1-p
the rows of the matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}
where (cf.
[
27
]
replacing
n
with
M
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}=\left(\begin{array}[]{llll}{[}w_{m}\eta_{1}{]}(\tilde{x}^{1})&\cdots&{[}w_{m}\eta_{m}{]}(\tilde{x}^{1})\\
\qquad\vdots&&\qquad\vdots\\
{[}w_{m}\eta_{1}{]}(\tilde{x}^{M})&\cdots&{[}w_{m}\eta_{m}{]}(\tilde{x}^{M})\end{array}\right)\,,

represent a finite frame with lower frame bound
(1-t)
.
This can be formulated as

\displaystyle(1-t)\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{M}\big\|\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}\bm{a}\big\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}

(6.15)

and follows from Lemma
6.5
below.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
p,t\in(0,1)
and let
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}=(\tilde{x}^{1},\ldots,\tilde{x}^{M})\in D^{M}
be
M
nodes drawn independently (with duplicates)
according to the measure
d\varrho_{m}
given by (
6.12
). In case

\displaystyle M\geq\frac{4}{t^{2}}m\log\left(\frac{m}{p}\right)

(
6.15
) holds
with probability exceeding
1-p
.

Proof.

Based on Lemma
A.3
, analogous to the proof of Lemma
6.1
.
∎

For any
\tilde{p}\in(0,1)
we also have

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{M}(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m})^{\ast}\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\leq 2\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+\frac{42}{M}\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{2}

(6.16)

with probability exceeding
1-\tilde{p}
for the infinite matrix
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}
given by

\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{lll}[w_{m}e_{m+1}](\tilde{x}^{1})&[w_{m}e_{m+2}](\tilde{x}^{1})&\ldots\\
\qquad\vdots&\qquad\vdots&\\
{[}w_{m}e_{m+1}{]}(\tilde{x}^{M})&{[}w_{m}e_{m+2}{]}(\tilde{x}^{M})&\ldots\end{array}\right)\,.

This is a consequence of the following lemma, itself a corollary
of
[
25
, Prop. 3.8]
(Proposition
A.5
in the Appendix).

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\bm{u}^{i}
,
i\in[M]
, be i.i.d. random sequences from
\ell_{2}(\mathds{N})
with
M\in\mathds{N}_{\geq 3}
.
Let further
R>0
such that
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}\leq R
almost surely and

\mathds{E}(\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i})=\mathbf{\Lambda}
for each
i\in[M]
.
Then for each
\tilde{p}\in(0,1)
, with probability exceeding
1-\tilde{p}
,

\Big\|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\,\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\leq 2\|\mathbf{\Lambda}\|_{2\to 2}+\frac{21R^{2}}{M}\log\Big(\frac{2^{\frac{3}{4}}M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)\,.

Proof.

In
[
25
, Prop. 3.8]
(Proposition
A.5
) we can choose
r>1
such that
\tilde{p}=2^{\frac{3}{4}}M^{1-r}
. Then
\log(2^{\frac{3}{4}}M/\tilde{p})=r\log(M)
and, noting
8\kappa^{2}\leq 21
, we obtain

\Big\|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\,\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}-\mathbf{\Lambda}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\leq\|\mathbf{\Lambda}\|_{2\to 2}+\frac{21R^{2}}{M}\log\Big(\frac{2^{\frac{3}{4}}M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)

with probability exceeding
1-\tilde{p}
. The triangle inequality finally yields the result.
∎

To prove (
6.16
) with Lemma
6.6
the rows of
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}
are interpreted as sequences
\bm{u}^{i}\in\ell_{2}(\mathds{N})
, where
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{k\geq m+1}\sigma_{k}^{2}
for each
i\in[M]
. Those then satisfy

\mathds{E}(\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i})=\diag(\sigma_{m+1}^{2},\sigma_{m+2}^{2},\ldots)=:\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}\,,

with
\|\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}\|_{2\to 2}=\sigma_{m+1}^{2}
,
and applying Lemma
6.6
with
R^{2}=2\sum_{k\geq m+1}\sigma_{k}^{2}
yields (
6.16
).

Step 2 (Subsampling)

The
PlainBSS
algorithm is used to determine a set of indices
J\subset[M]
and a
subset of nodes
\bm{X}_{n}\subset\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}
of cardinality
|\bm{X}_{n}|=|J|\leq\lceil bm\rceil
, where
b>1+\frac{1}{m}
as in Step 1.
We then build a submatrix
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m}
of
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}
by selecting the corresponding rows of
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}
.
From (
6.15
) and Corollary
4.5
we get, with probability exceeding
1-p
, that

\displaystyle(1-t)\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{M}\|\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{89(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{m}\|\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m}\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\bm{a}\in\mathds{C}^{m}\,.

In particular,
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m}
then has full rank and the norm of the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse
(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{\dagger}=((\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{\ast}\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{-1}(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{\ast}

(see (
6.5
)) fulfills the estimate

\displaystyle\|(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{2\to 2}\leq\frac{89(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1}{m}\,.

(6.17)

Performance analysis

The sampling reconstruction operator
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}
defined in (
6.3
), with nodes
\bm{X}_{n}
constructed according to the previous paragraph, yields a near-optimal reconstruction performance (cf.
[
20
,
19
,
25
,
27
]
), with the advantage of a precise control of the oversampling factor
b

as well as a polynomial-time semi-constructive node generation procedure. A modified subsampling procedure was presented recently by Dolbeault, Krieg, and M. Ullrich
[
11
]
, leading to an optimal reconstruction rate (without the log-term). It is a refinement of the
non-constructive Weaver subsampling from
[
27
]
and cannot be made constructive by our approach, since for that the upper frame bounds need to be preserved as well.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
H(K)
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on a measurable domain
(D,\nu)
with a positive semi-definite
\nu
-measurable kernel

K:D\times D\to\mathds{C}
of finite trace

\int_{D}K(x,x)d\nu(x)<\infty\,.

Further, assume that
H(K)
is separable and that the
canonical Hilbert-Schmidt embedding

\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}:H(K)\to L_{2}(D,\nu)

has infinite rank. In this setting, fix
m\in\mathds{N}_{\geq 10}
,

p\in(0,\frac{2}{3})
,
t=\frac{2}{3}
, and construct a node
set

\bm{X}_{n}\subset D\quad\text{with}\quad|\bm{X}_{n}|\leq\lceil bm\rceil

for
b>1+\frac{1}{m}

according to the procedure described in the previous paragraph (applying
PlainBSS
on
M\geq\max\{\frac{4}{t^{2}}m\log(\frac{m}{p}),\lceil bm\rceil\}
randomly drawn nodes).
The reconstruction
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}
given by (
6.3
) then performs as

\sup\limits_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}(D,\nu)}\leq\frac{4827}{\min\{b-1,1\}}\frac{(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{m}{p}\Big)\bigg(\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+\frac{7}{m}\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\bigg)

(6.18)

with probability exceeding
1-\frac{3}{2}p
, where
(\sigma_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the sequence of decreasingly ordered, square-summable, non-trivial singular numbers of
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
.

Proof.

First recall that, with certain probabilities,
the initial nodes
associated to
\bm{X}_{n}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{M}=(\tilde{x}^{1},\ldots,\tilde{x}^{M})\,,

(6.19)

fulfill property (
6.15
) for the matrix
\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{M,m}

and (
6.16
) for
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}
.
To prove (
6.18
), we now take
f\in H(K)
with
\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1
and denote with

P_{m}:L_{2}(D,\nu)\to V_{m}
the orthogonal projection onto the reconstruction space
V_{m}
of
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}
.
Since the operator
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}

acts as identity on
V_{m}
, whence
S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}P_{m}f=P_{m}f
,
we have

\displaystyle\|f-S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle=\|f-P_{m}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}}+\|S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}(f-P_{m}f)\|^{2}_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}_{m+1}+\|(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{2\to 2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w_{m}(x^{i})^{2}|f(x^{i})-P_{m}f(x^{i})|^{2}\,.

(6.20)

Clearly, we also have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w_{m}(x^{i})^{2}|f(x^{i})-P_{m}f(x^{i})|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}w_{m}(\tilde{x}^{i})^{2}|f(\tilde{x}^{i})-P_{m}f(\tilde{x}^{i})|^{2}\,,

(6.21)

where
(\tilde{x}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}
are the initial nodes (
6.19
).
For
f\in\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu})
, the null-space of
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
, the right-hand side of
(
6.21
) vanishes almost surely, due to the separability of
H(K)
.
For general
f
, following the proof in
[
25
, Thm. 5.1]
,
almost surely

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{M}w_{m}(\tilde{x}^{i})^{2}|f(\tilde{x}^{i})-P_{m}f(\tilde{x}^{i})|^{2}\leq\|(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m})^{\ast}\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}\|_{2\to 2}

(6.22)

and according to (
6.16
)

\displaystyle\Big\|(\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m})^{\ast}\widetilde{\mathbf{\Phi}}_{M,m}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\leq 2M\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+42\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,.

(6.23)

Altogether, the estimates (
6.2
)-(
6.23
)
together with the norm estimate (
6.17
) for
(\widetilde{\bm{L}}_{J,m})^{\dagger}

yield

\displaystyle\|f-S^{\bm{X}_{n}}_{V_{m},w_{m}}f\|^{2}_{L_{2}}\leq\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+\frac{89(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}}\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1}{m}\Big(2M\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+42\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\Big)\,.

(6.24)

Let us proceed to bound the second summand in (
6.24
), which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{(b+1)^{2}}{(b-1)^{3}}\frac{178}{t^{2}(1-t)}\Big(\frac{Mt^{2}}{m}\sigma_{m+1}^{2}+\frac{21t^{2}}{m}\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\sigma_{k}^{2}\Big)\,.

(6.25)

Plugging in (
6.13
) for
M
with, at the moment, still arbitrary
p,t\in(0,1)
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{M-1}{m}\leq\max\Big\{\frac{4}{t^{2}}\log\Big(\frac{m}{p}\Big),b\Big\}\quad\text{and thus}\quad\frac{Mt^{2}}{m}\leq\frac{4M}{M-1}\max\Big\{\log\Big(\frac{m}{p}\Big),\frac{bt^{2}}{4}\Big\}\,.

(6.26)

Using
\log(\frac{m}{p})\leq\frac{m}{\mathrm{e}p}
as well as
\log(xy)\leq x\log(y)
for
x\geq 1
and
y\geq\mathrm{e}
, we also have

\displaystyle\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{M}{\tilde{p}}\Big)\leq\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{Mm}{\tilde{p}(M-1)}\max\Big\{\log\Big(\frac{m}{p}\Big),\frac{bt^{2}}{4}\Big\}\frac{4}{t^{2}}\Big)\leq\frac{M}{M-1}\log\Big(2^{\frac{3}{4}}\frac{4m}{\tilde{p}t^{2}}\max\Big\{\frac{m}{\mathrm{e}p},\frac{bt^{2}}{4}\Big\}\Big)\,.

(6.27)

For
t=\frac{2}{3}
the denominator
t^{2}(1-t)
in (

For the second part, we have

Appendix A
Matrix theory

A basic tool in Subsection
3.3
is the matrix determinant lemma (cf.
[
3
, Lem. 2.2]
).
The complex version reads as follows.

Lemma A.1

(Matrix determinant lemma)
.

If
\bm{A}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
is nonsingular and
\bm{v}\in\mathds{C}^{m}
is a vector, then

\det(\bm{A}+\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}\big)=\det(\bm{A})(1+\bm{v}^{\ast}\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{v})\,.

Lemma
A.1
is a direct consequence of the Sherman-Morrison formula (see e.g.
[
16
]
)

\big(\bm{A}+\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}\big)^{-1}=\bm{A}^{-1}-\frac{\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{v}\bm{v}^{\ast}\bm{A}^{-1}}{1+\bm{v}^{\ast}\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{v}}\,,

which holds under the same assumptions as in Lemma
A.1
.

Another basic result, needed in the proof of Lemma
3.11
, is the following statement.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(\bm{y}^{i})_{i=1}^{M}\subset\mathds{C}^{m}
be a frame with frame bounds
0<A\leq B<\infty
.
Let further
\bm{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
be a positive semi-definite Hermitian matrix.
Then

\displaystyle A\tr(\bm{M})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\bm{M}\bm{y}^{i}\leq B\tr(\bm{M})\,.

Proof.

First, observe that for an arbitrary matrix
\bm{M}\in\mathds{C}^{m\times m}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\bm{M}\bm{y}^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tr\Big((\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\bm{M}\bm{y}^{i}\Big)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tr\Big(\bm{M}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\Big)=\tr\Big(\bm{M}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\Big)\Big)\,.

Since
\bm{Y}:=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\bm{y}^{i}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}
is positive-definite Hermitian with
\sigma(\bm{Y})\subset[A,B]
, there exists a unitary matrix
\bm{U}
such that

\bm{D}:=\bm{U}\bm{Y}\bm{U}^{-1}

is diagonal with entries in the range
[A,B]
. We can hence conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\bm{y}^{i})^{\ast}\bm{M}\bm{y}^{i}=\tr\Big(\bm{M}\bm{Y}\Big)=\tr\Big(\bm{U}\bm{M}\bm{Y}\bm{U}^{-1}\Big)=\tr\Big(\bm{U}\bm{M}\bm{U}^{-1}\bm{D}\Big)\,.

Under the assumption that
\bm{M}
is positive semi-definite Hermitian
the transformation
\bm{U}\bm{M}\bm{U}^{-1}
is also positive semi-definite Hermitian. In particular, its diagonal entries are all nonnegative real numbers.
As a consequence, we obtain the assertion

\displaystyle A\tr\Big(\bm{M}\Big)=A\tr\Big(\bm{U}\bm{M}\bm{U}^{-1}\Big)\leq\tr\Big(\bm{M}\bm{Y}\Big)\leq B\tr\Big(\bm{U}\bm{M}\bm{U}^{-1}\Big)=B\tr\Big(\bm{M}\Big)\,.

∎

A.1
Concentration results for random matrices

Here we give some concentration inequalities which enable us to control the spectrum of
sums of Hermitian positive semi-definite rank-
1
matrices. In the finite case, these sums take the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}\quad\text{for a sequence of random vectors }\bm{u}^{i}\in\mathds{C}^{m},\,i\in[n]\,.

(A.1)

Recall the notation
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The basic assumption is always that the vectors
\bm{u}^{i}
are drawn i.i.d. according to some probability distribution and that a uniform bound
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}\leq M
is satisfied almost surely for every
i\in[n]
.
Putting
\bm{A}_{i}:=\frac{1}{n}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}
, we obtain a sequence
({\bm{A}_{i}})_{i=1}^{n}
of i.i.d. Hermitian positive semi-definite random matrices
which satisfy
\lamax(\bm{A}_{i})\leq\frac{M^{2}}{n}
almost surely.
In this situation, a matrix Chernoff inequality proved by Tropp
[
37
, Thm. 1.1]
can be applied. We derive the following form.

Lemma A.3

(Matrix Chernoff, cf.
[
37
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

For a sequence
(\bm{A}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathds{C}^{m\times m}
of independent, Hermitian, positive semi-definite random matrices satisfying
\lambda_{\max}(\bm{A}_{i})\leq R
almost surely it holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\lamin\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{A}_{i}\Big)\leq(1-t)\mu_{\min}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq{m}\exp\Big(-\frac{\mu_{\min}}{R}(t+(1-t)\log(1-t))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{m}\exp\Big(-\frac{\mu_{\min}t^{2}}{2R}\Big)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\lamax\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{A}_{i}\Big)\geq(1+t)\mu_{\max}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq{m}\exp\Big(-\frac{\mu_{\max}}{R}(-t+(1+t)\log(1+t))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{m}\exp\Big(-\frac{\mu_{\max}t^{2}}{3R}\Big)

for
t\in[0,1]
, where
\mu_{\min}\coloneqq\lambda_{\min}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bm{A}_{i})
and
\mu_{\max}\coloneqq\lambda_{\max}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathds{E}\bm{A}_{i})
.

Proof.

The first estimates are provided by
[
37
, Thm. 1.1]
. Based on the Taylor expansion

\displaystyle(1+t)\log(1+t)=t+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k(k-1)}t^{k}\,,

which holds true for
t\in[-1,1]
, we can further derive the inequalities

\displaystyle t+(1-t)\log(1-t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(k-1)}t^{k}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2}

and

\displaystyle-t+(1+t)\log(1+t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k(k-1)}t^{k}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{2}-\frac{t^{3}}{6}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{3}

for the range
t\in[0,1]
.
∎

A concentration inequality for the case when the vectors
\bm{u}^{i}
in (
A.1
) are infinite dimensional is given in
[
25
, Thm. 1.1]
. Here it is assumed that the
\bm{u}^{i}

are i.i.d. random sequences from
\ell_{2}(\mathds{N})
. Let us recite this result.

Theorem A.4

(
[
25
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let
\bm{u}^{i},i\in[n],
be i.i.d. random sequences from
\ell_{2}(\mathds{N})
. Let further
n\geq 3
,
M>0
such that
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}\leq M
almost surely and
\mathbb{E}(\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i})={\mathbf{\Lambda}}
for
i\in[n]
with
\|\bm{\Lambda}\|_{2\to 2}\leq 1
. Then

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}-{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\geq t\Big)\leq 2^{\frac{3}{4}}n\exp\Big(-\frac{t^{2}n}{21M^{2}}\Big)\,.

A useful rephrasing of Theorem
A.4
is given by
[
25
, Prop. 3.8]
. It is as follows.

Proposition A.5

(
[
25
, Prop. 3.8]
)
.

Let
\bm{u}^{i},i\in[n]
, be i.i.d. random sequences from
\ell_{2}(\mathds{N})
. Let further
n\geq 3
,
r>1
,
M>0
such that
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}\leq M
almost surely and

\mathbb{E}(\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i})={\mathbf{\Lambda}}
for all
i\in[n]
. Then

\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}-{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\geq F\Big)\leq 2^{\frac{3}{4}}\,n^{1-r}\,,

where
F:=\max\Big\{\frac{8r\log n}{n}M^{2}\nu^{2},\|{\mathbf{\Lambda}}\|_{2\to 2}\Big\}
and
\nu=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

Appendix B
The considered RKHS setting

In Subsection
6.2
we consider functions from a RKHS
H(K)
on a
non-empty measure space
(D,\nu)
. In the sequel, this setting is analyzed in more detail.
First note that the space
H(K)
, as a RKHS, consists of proper point-wise defined functions. By definition, it
is associated with a positive semi-definite Hermitian kernel
K:D\times D\to\mathds{C}

such that the reproducing property

\displaystyle f(x)=\langle f,K(\cdot,x)\rangle_{H(K)}

(B.1)

holds true for all
f\in H(K)
and
x\in D
.
Due to this property, sampling is not only a well-defined but even continuous operation in
H(K)

(see e.g.
[
17
]
).

For our analysis of
L_{2}
-approximation, an embedding relation between
H(K)
and
L_{2}(D,\nu)
is
crucial. It is guaranteed by the presumed finite trace of
K
, namely

\tr(K):=\int_{D}K(x,x)\,d\nu(x)<\infty\,.

Under such a condition,
see
[
17
]
and
[
33
, Lem. 2.3]
,
for every
f\in H(K)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{2}=\int_{D}|\langle f,K(\cdot,x)\rangle|^{2}\,d\nu(x)\leq\int_{D}\|f\|_{H(K)}^{2}\|K(\cdot,x)\|_{H(K)}^{2}\,d\nu(x)=\|f\|_{H(K)}^{2}\cdot\tr(K)\,.

As a consequence, there is a compact embedding

\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}:H(K)\hookrightarrow L_{2}(D,\nu)\,,

which can be shown to be even Hilbert-Schmidt (see
[
33
, Lem. 2.3]
).
Here, it is important to note that, in contrast to
H(K)
, the elements of
L_{2}(D,\nu)
are not functions
but
\nu
-equivalence classes, with two functions
f,\tilde{f}:D\to\mathds{C}
considered
\nu
-equivalent if
f(x)=\tilde{f}(x)
for
\nu
-almost every
x\in D
.
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
may hence not be injective.
In fact,
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
is the restriction of
\mathrm{Id}_{\nu}
to
H(K)
, where
\mathrm{Id}_{\nu}
is the map
that assigns to every
f:D\to\mathds{C}
the corresponding
\nu
-equivalence class.
Depending on the measure
\nu
, the null-space
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu})
can thus be non-trivial.
The choice
\nu=0
illustrates this, where
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu})=H(K)
.

Without loss of generality, to simplify the considerations in Subsection
6.2
, it is further assumed that the subspace
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}(H(K))
of
L_{2}(D,\nu)
is infinite dimensional (i.e. that
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
has infinite rank). Under this condition, the sequence
(\sigma_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
of strictly positive singular numbers associated to
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
is countably infinite. For this, note that
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}(H(K))
is a separable subspace of
L_{2}(D,\nu)
due to the compactness of
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
.

We now fix orthonormal systems
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}\subset L_{2}(D,\nu)
and
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}\subset H(K)
of associated left and right singular functions
such that

\displaystyle e_{k}=\sigma_{k}\eta_{k}\quad\text{for all}\quad k\in\mathds{N}\,.

(B.2)

Whereas each
e_{k}
represents a point-wise function on
D
, the left singular functions
\eta_{k}
, as elements of
L_{2}(D,\nu)
, refer to
\nu
-equivalence classes. However,
we choose for each
\eta_{k}
the specific representative
e_{k}/\sigma_{k}
so that both systems
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
and
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
are comprised of proper functions satisfying (
B.2
) in a point-wise sense.

Let us next ask for basis properties of these systems. Clearly,
(\eta_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
is a basis for
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}(H(K))
.
The system
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
, on the other hand, is usually not a basis for
H(K)
since it only corresponds to the non-trivial singular numbers of
\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu}
.
Under additional restrictions ensuring
\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu})=\{0\}
it would be, e.g. if the kernel
K
is continuous and bounded (i.e. a Mercer kernel).
Generally,
H(K)
decomposes in the form

\displaystyle H(K)=\overline{\spn}\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots\}\oplus_{\text{orth}}^{H}\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu})\,.

A useful representation of
K
in terms of the functions
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
can be obtained as follows.
For each
y\in D
, first expand the function
K(\cdot,y)\in H(K)
in the form

\displaystyle K(\cdot,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}c_{k}e_{k}+r_{y}

with an associated function
r_{y}\in\mathcal{N}(\mathrm{Id}_{K,\nu})
and convergence of the sum in
H(K)
.
For the coefficients calculate with (
B.1
)

\displaystyle c_{k}=\langle K(\cdot,y),e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}=\overline{e_{k}(y)}\,.

Since convergence in
H(K)
entails point-wise convergence, we obtain the representation

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}e_{k}(x)\overline{e_{k}(y)}+r_{y}(x)\,.

In case that
H(K)
is separable, which is assumed in Theorem
6.7
, it is shown in
[
17
]
and
[
33
, Cor. 3.2]
that
r_{x}(x)
vanishes
\nu
-almost everywhere. We hence have
K(x,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}|e_{k}(x)|^{2}
for
\nu
-almost every
x\in D
. This is a crucial ingredient in the proof of Theorem
6.7
, see also
[
25
, Sec. 4]
and

[
19
, Sec. 2]
.

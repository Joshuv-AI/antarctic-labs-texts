Integrality of genus zero Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants of semi-positive varieties
You-Cheng Chou

Abstract
. We give an alternate proof of the integrality conjecture of genus zero Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants on semi-positive varieties using algebraic geometry. The main technique is to relate Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants to quantum K K -invariants and to utilize the integrality of the latter.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth complex projective variety. (Cohomological) Gromov–Witten invariants of
X
(with primary field) are defined to be

\begin{split}{\mathrm{GW}}_{g,\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})&:=\langle\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\rangle_{g,n,\beta}^{X,H}\\
&=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{{\rm vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\rm ev}_{i}^{*}(\gamma_{i})\in\mathbb{Q},\end{split}

where
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{{\rm vir}}
is the virtual fundamental class,
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\in H^{*}(X)
, and
{\rm ev}_{i}
is the evaluation map at the
i
-th marked point.

Due to the multiple cover contribution, Gromov-Witten invariants are in general not integers. For Calabi-Yau threefolds, the genus zero multiple cover formula was conjectured by physicists
[
CdlOGP92
]
, reinterpreted in mathematical terms by Aspinwall-Morrison
[
AM93
]
, and proved rigorously by Voisin
[
Voi96
]
and many others. The genus one case was computed in physics
[
BCOV93
]
and mathematics
[
GP99
]
. The higher genus case was proved by Faber-Pandharipande
[
FP00
]
.

The multiple cover formulas lead to integral contributions to the “BPS invariants”, sometimes in a subtle way. See
[
Bry01
]
for some discussion. The BPS invariants, also called Gopakumar-Vafa invariants, can be defined via Gromov-Witten invariants via the remarkable formula (
1.1
) by Gopakumar-Vafa
[
GV98
]
. The integrality of this ad hoc definition has been shown by E. Ionel and
T. Parker
[
IP18
]
.

(1.1)

\begin{split}&\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}~\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})_{>0}}{{\mathrm{GV}}}_{g,\beta}{\frac{1}{k}}\left(2\sin\left({\frac{k\lambda}{2}}\right)\right)^{2g-2}q^{k\beta}\\
&:=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}~\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})_{>0}}{{\mathrm{GW}}}_{g,\beta}q^{\beta}\lambda^{2g-2}.\end{split}

In
[
KP08
]
,
A. Klemm and R. Pandharipande generalized the genus zero multiple cover formula to all Calabi-Yau
m
-folds with
m\geq 4
. They defined Gopakumar–Vafa type invariants (in terms of Gromov-Witten invariants) and conjectured their integrality. In
[
IP18
]
, the definition has been generalized to semi-positive (including Calabi-Yau and Fano) variety
X
with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X\geq 3
. It consists of two parts.

•

If
-\beta\cdot K_{X}=0
, it is the multiple cover formula in the form given by Klemm-Pandharipande
[
KP08
]

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{>0}\\
\beta\cdot K_{X}=0\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{GV}}_{0,\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{r\beta}}{r^{3-n}}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{>0}\\
\beta\cdot K_{X}=0\end{subarray}}{\mathrm{GW}}_{0,\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})q^{\beta}.

•

If
-\beta\cdot K_{X}>0
,

{\mathrm{GV}}_{0,\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}):={\mathrm{GW}}_{0,\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}).

In this paper, we write
{\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta}(\cdots)
and
{\mathrm{GV}}_{\beta}(\cdots)
for simplicity since we only consider the genus zero case.

The integrality of genus zero Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants has been proved by Ionel–Parker using symplectic geometry.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
IP18
, Theorem 9.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a semi-positive variety and
\gamma_{i}\in H^{2*}(X;\mathbb{Z})
be cohomology classes with even degree. The invariants
{\mathrm{GV}}_{\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
are integers.

The goal of this paper is to give an alternate proof of this result using only algebraic geometry. In section
2
, we recall some necessary ingredients on A. Givental and his collaborators’ framework about quantum
K
-theory (including the virtual Kawasaki’s Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch formula, the fake theory, and the stem theory).
In section
3
, we relate Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants to quantum
K
-invariants. The semi-positive assumption ensures that the virtual dimension of twisted sector (Kawasaki strata) is less than or equal to the virtual dimension of the untwisted one. It will greatly simplify the computation. Finally, we show that the integrality of quantum
K
-invariants will imply the integrality of Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants.

2.
Quantum
K
-theory

In this section, we give a limited introduction of Givental and his collaborators’ framework about quantum
K
-theory.

K
-theoretic Gromov-Witten invariants
are
integral
invariants
[
Giv00
,
Lee04
]
.
For any smooth projective variety
X
, they are defined as

\begin{split}{\mathrm{QK}}_{g,\beta}(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n})&:=\langle\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}\rangle_{g,n,\beta}^{X,K}\\
&=\chi\Big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta);\Big(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\rm ev}_{i}^{*}(\Gamma_{i})\Big)\otimes\mathcal{O}^{{\rm vir}}\Big)\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{split}

Here
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}\in K^{0}(X)
, the topological
K
-theory of
X
, and
\mathcal{O}^{{\rm vir}}
is the virtual structure sheaf on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
. We denote
{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta}(\dots)
for genus 0 case. It is useful to encode genus zero invariants into power series, called (big)
J
-function. We recall its construction below.

2.1.
The symplectic loop space formalism

Let
\Lambda=\mathbb{Q}[\![Q]\!]
be the Novikov ring and

\mathbf{K}:=K^{0}(X)\otimes\Lambda.

Givental’s loop space for quantum
K
-theory is defined as

\mathcal{K}:=K^{0}(X)(q)\otimes\Lambda.

\mathcal{K}
has a natural symplectic structure with the symplectic form
\Omega
,

\mathcal{K}\ni f,g\mapsto\Omega(f,g):=\Big({\rm Res}_{q=0}+{\rm Res}_{q=\infty}\Big)(f(q),g(q^{-1}))^{K}\frac{dq}{q}.

Here
(\cdot,\cdot)^{K}
denotes the
K
-theoretic intersection pairing on
\mathbf{K}
:

(a,b)^{K}:=\chi(X,a\otimes b)=\int_{X}{\rm td}(T_{X}){\rm ch}(a){\rm ch}(b).

\mathcal{K}
admits the following Lagrangian polarization with respect to
\Omega
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}&=\mathcal{K}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{-}\\
&:=\mathbf{K}[q,q^{-1}]\,\oplus\,\{f(q)\in\mathcal{K}|f(0)\neq\infty,\ f(\infty)=0\}.\end{split}

Definition 2.1
.

The
big
J
-function
of
X
in the quantum
K
-theory is defined as a map
\mathcal{K}_{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
:

\mathbf{t}\mapsto J^{{K}}(\mathbf{t}):=(1-q)+\mathbf{t}(q)+\sum_{\alpha}\Phi^{\alpha}\sum_{n,\beta}\frac{Q^{\beta}}{n!}\langle\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-qL},\mathbf{t}(L),\dots,\mathbf{t}(L)\rangle^{X,K}_{0,n+1,\beta},

where
\{\Phi_{\alpha}\}
and
\{\Phi^{\alpha}\}
are Poincaré-dual basis of
K^{0}(X)
with respect to
(\cdot,\cdot)^{K}
.

2.2.
J^{K}
-function as a graph sum via Kawasaki’s HRR

Let
I\mathcal{M}=\sqcup_{i}\mathcal{M}_{i}
be the inertia stack of
\mathcal{M}
, with
\mathcal{M}_{i}
connected components. Following Givental, we refer to them as
Kawasaki strata
. Kawasaki’s formula
[
Kaw79
]
reads

\chi(\mathcal{M},E)=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{m_{i}}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}{\rm td}(T_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}){\rm ch}\Big(\frac{{\rm Tr}(E|_{\mathcal{M}_{i}})}{{\rm Tr}(\Lambda^{*}N_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}^{*})}\Big),

where the sum over
i
runs through all connected components.

Applying the virtual Kawasaki’s Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula for Deligne–Mumford stacks (VKHRR)
[
Ton14
]
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
, we get

\begin{split}J^{{K}}(\mathbf{t})&=(1-q)+\mathbf{t}(q)+\sum_{n,\beta,\alpha}\frac{Q^{\beta}\Phi^{\alpha}}{n!}\langle\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-qL},\mathbf{t}(L),\dots,\mathbf{t}(L)\rangle^{X,K}_{0,n+1,\beta}\\
&=(1-q)+\mathbf{t}(q)+\sum_{n,\beta,\alpha}\frac{Q^{\beta}\Phi^{\alpha}}{n!}\sum_{\zeta}\langle\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-qL},\mathbf{t}(L),\dots,\mathbf{t}(L)\rangle^{X_{\zeta}}_{0,n+1,\beta},\end{split}

where in
\sum_{\zeta}

\zeta
runs through all roots of unity (including 1),
X_{\zeta}
stand for the collection of inertia stacks (“Kawasaki strata”) where
g
acts on
L_{1}
with eigenvalue
\zeta
, and
\langle...\rangle^{X_{\zeta}}
denote the contributions of
X_{\zeta}
in the Riemann–Roch formula. In other words,
\langle...\rangle^{X,K}
represent (true) quantum
K
-invariants while
\langle...\rangle^{X_{\zeta}}
stand for (collections of)
cohomological
invariants. Symbolically, we have

\langle\cdots\rangle^{X,K}=\sum_{\zeta}\langle\cdots\rangle^{X_{\zeta}}.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
r
-th roots of unity. Define

\begin{split}{\rm arm}(q)&:=\sum_{n,\beta\neq 0,\alpha}\frac{Q^{\beta}\Phi^{\alpha}}{n!}\sum_{\zeta^{\prime}\neq 1}\langle\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-qL},\mathbf{t}(L),\dots,\mathbf{t}(L)\rangle^{X_{\zeta^{\prime}}}_{0,n+1,\beta}\in\mathbf{K}[\![1-q]\!];\\
{\rm leg}_{r}(q)&:=\Psi^{r}({\rm arm}(q)|_{\mathbf{t}=0})\in\mathbf{K}[\![1-q]\!];\\
{\rm tail}_{\zeta}(q)&:=\sum_{n,\beta\neq 0,\alpha}\frac{Q^{\beta}\Phi^{\alpha}}{n!}\sum_{\zeta^{\prime}\neq\zeta}\langle\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-qL},\mathbf{t}(L),\dots,\mathbf{t}(L)\rangle^{X_{\zeta^{\prime}}}_{0,n+1,\beta}\in\mathbf{K}[\![1-\zeta q]\!],\end{split}

where
\zeta^{\prime}
in the above sums are arbitrary roots of unity and

\Psi^{r}
are the
Adams operations
. Recall that Adams operations are additive and multiplicative endomorphisms of
K
-theory or more generally
\lambda
-rings, acting on line bundles by
\Psi^{r}(L)=L^{\otimes r}
. Here
\Psi^{r}
also act on the Novikov variables by
\Psi^{r}(Q^{\beta})=Q^{r\beta}
and on
q
by
\Psi^{r}(q)=q^{r}
.

We recall the following two propositions which relate contributions from Kawasaki strata into fake
K
-theory and stem theory respectively.

The fake quantum
K
-theory, defined by “naively” applying the virtual Riemann–Roch for schemes to stacks

\langle\tau_{d_{1}}(\Gamma_{1})\dots\tau_{d_{n}}(\Gamma_{n})\rangle^{X,{\rm fake}}_{g,n,\beta}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{{\rm vir}}}{\rm td}(T_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)}^{{\rm vir}}){\rm ch}(\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\rm ev}_{i}^{*}(\Gamma_{i})L_{i}^{d_{i}}),

where
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{{\rm vir}}
is the (cohomological) virtual fundamental class and
T^{{\rm vir}}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)}
is the virtual tangent bundle.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
GT14
, Proposition 1]
)
.

J^{K}(\mathbf{t})|_{q=1}=J^{{\rm fake}}(\mathbf{t}+{\rm arm}),

where
()|_{q=1}
is the Laurent series expansion at
q=1
(of the rational function).

For
\zeta\neq 1
, a primitive
r
-th roots of unity, we consider the
stem space
, which is isomorphic to

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2,\beta}^{X}(\zeta):=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2}\left([X/\mathbb{Z}_{r}],\beta;(g,1,\dots,1,g^{-1})\right).

Here the group elements
g,1,g^{-1}
signal the twisted sectors in which the marked points lie. The notation
[...]^{X_{\zeta}}
will be reserved for stem contributions

\begin{split}&\Big[T_{1}(L),T(L),\dots,T(L),T_{n+2}(L)\Big]^{X_{\zeta}}_{0,n+2,\beta}\\
:=&\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2,\beta}^{X}(\zeta)]^{{\rm vir}}}{\rm td}(T_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}){\rm ch}\left(\frac{{\rm ev}_{1}^{*}(T_{1}(L)){\rm ev}_{n+2}^{*}(T_{n+2}(L))\prod_{i=2}^{n+1}{\rm ev}_{i}^{*}T(L)}{{\rm Tr}(\Lambda^{*}N^{*}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}})}\right),\end{split}

where
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2,\beta}^{X}(\zeta)]^{{\rm vir}}
is the virtual fundamental class,
T_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
is the (virtual) tangent bundle to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2,\beta}^{X}(\zeta)
, and
N_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
is the (virtual) normal bundle of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+2,\beta}^{X}(\zeta)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,nr+2}(X,r\beta)
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
GT14
, § 8]
)
.

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
r
-th roots of unity.
We have

\begin{split}&\sum_{n,\beta,\alpha}\frac{Q^{\beta}\Phi^{\alpha}}{n!}\langle\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-qL},\mathbf{t}(L),\dots,\mathbf{t}(L)\rangle^{X_{\zeta}}_{0,n+1,\beta}\\
=&\sum_{n,\beta,\alpha}\frac{Q^{r\beta}\Phi^{\alpha}}{n!}\Big[\frac{\Phi_{\alpha}}{1-q\zeta L^{1/r}},{\rm leg}_{r}(L),\dots,{\rm leg}_{r}(L),\delta_{\zeta}(L^{1/r})\Big]^{X_{\zeta}}_{0,n+2,\beta},\end{split}

where

\delta_{\zeta}(q)=(1-\zeta^{-1}q)+t(\zeta^{-1}q)+{\rm tail}_{\zeta}(\zeta^{-1}q).

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we assume
X
to be a semi-positive variety of dimension
m
. In section

3.1.
Twisted Gromov-Witten theory

Let
\zeta
be a primitive
r
-th roots of unity. In the stem theory, we consider the twisting class

{\rm td}(T_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}){\rm ch}\Big(\frac{1}{{\rm Tr}(\Lambda^{*}N^{*}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}})}\Big).

Such twisting classes come from the deformation theory of the moduli of stable maps,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{X}_{0,n+2,\beta}(\zeta)
, and consist of three parts. See e.g.,
[
GT14
, Section 8]
.

type
A
.

\displaystyle{\rm td}(\pi_{*}^{K}{\rm ev}^{*}(TX))\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}{\rm td}_{\zeta^{k}}(\pi_{*}^{K}{\rm ev}^{*}(T_{X}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{k}}))
, where
\pi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\rm ev}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow X/\mathbb{Z}_{r}
form the universal (orbifold) stable map diagram.
\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{k}}
is the line bundle over
B\mathbb{Z}_{r}
with
g
acts by
\zeta^{k}
, and for any line bundle
l
, define the invertible multiplicative characteristic classes

{\rm td}(l):=\frac{c_{1}(l)}{1-e^{-c_{1}(l)}},\quad{\rm td}_{\lambda}(l):=\frac{1}{1-\lambda e^{-c_{1}(l)}}.

type
B
.

\displaystyle{\rm td}(\pi_{*}^{K}(-L^{-1}))\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}{\rm td}_{\zeta^{k}}(\pi_{*}^{K}(-L^{-1}\otimes{\rm ev}^{*}(\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{k}})))
, where
L=L_{n+3}
is the universal cotangent line bundle of

\mathcal{C}\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+3}^{X/\mathbb{Z}_{r},\beta}(g,1,\dots,1,g^{-1},1).

type
C
.

\displaystyle{\rm td}^{\vee}(-\pi_{*}^{K}i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{g}}){\rm td}^{\vee}(-\pi_{*}^{K}i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}})\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}{\rm td}^{\vee}_{\zeta^{k}}(-\pi_{*}^{K}i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z_{1}})
, where
Z_{1}
stands for unramified nodal locus, and
Z_{g}
stands for ramified one with
i:Z\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
the embedding of nodal locus. For any line bundle
l
,

{\rm td}^{\vee}(l)=\frac{-c_{1}(l)}{1-e^{c_{1}(l)}},\quad{\rm td}^{\vee}_{\lambda}(l)=\frac{1}{1-\lambda e^{c_{1}(l)}}.

We have the following observations.

Lemma 3.1
.

{\rm td}(T_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}){\rm ch}\Big(\frac{1}{{\rm Tr}(\Lambda^{*}N^{*}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}})}\Big)=:r^{-(n-K_{X}\cdot\beta)}+T_{0,n+2,\beta}(\zeta),

where
T_{0,n+2,\beta}(\zeta)\in H^{>0}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{X}_{0,n+2,\beta}(\zeta))
, with
0
,
n
,
\beta
, and
\zeta
inherited from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{X}_{0,n+2,\beta}(\zeta)
.

Proof.

Note that
N_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
has virtual dimension
(r-1)(n-K_{X}\cdot\beta)
since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
is considered as Kawasaki strata in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,nr+2}(X,r\beta)
.

Only the twisting classes on normal bundle give nontrivial constant. It is of the following form:

\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}{\rm td}_{\zeta^{k}}\Big(\pi^{K}_{*}(E\otimes{\rm ev}^{*}(\mathbb{C}_{\zeta^{k}}))\Big),

where
E
is a virtual bundle of rank
n-K_{X}\cdot\beta
. The constant term is given by

\Big(\prod_{k=1}^{r-1}\frac{1}{1-\zeta^{k}}\Big)^{n-K_{X}\cdot\beta}=r^{-(n-K_{X}\cdot\beta)}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})_{>0}
, assume
\deg_{\mathbb{C}}\gamma_{1}\geq m-K_{X}\cdot\beta-1
. We have

\langle\tau_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{1}),\dots,\tau_{k_{n}}(\gamma_{n})\rangle_{g,n,\beta}^{\#}=0,

where
\#
denotes cohomological GW invariants with any twisting.

In
K
-theory, assume
\deg_{\mathbb{C}}{\rm ch}(\Gamma_{1})\geq m-K_{X}\cdot\beta-1
. We have

\langle\tau_{k_{1}}(\Gamma_{1}),\dots,\tau_{k_{n}}(\Gamma_{n})\rangle_{g,n,\beta}^{K}=0.

Proof.

For the cohomological case, let

\pi_{1}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}(X,\beta)

be the forgetful map forgetting the last
n-1
marked points and
T\in H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
be the twisting class.
By projection formula

\begin{split}\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{{\rm vir}}}T\ &\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}{\rm ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}\Big)\\
&=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}(X,\beta)]^{{\rm vir}}}({\rm ev}_{1}^{*}\gamma_{1})\ (\pi_{1})_{*}\left(T\ \psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}{\rm ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}\right).\end{split}

The last equation vanishes for dimension reason. The same argument works in
K
-theory.
∎

From Lemma
3.2

{\rm arm}
,

Corollary 3.3
.

\begin{split}&{\rm ch}({\rm arm}(q))\in H^{\geq 2(2+K_{X}\cdot\beta)}(X)[\![1-q,Q]\!];\\
&{\rm ch}({\rm leg}_{r}(q))\in H^{\geq 2(2+K_{X}\cdot\beta)}(X)[\![1-q,Q]\!];\\
&{\rm ch}({\rm tail}_{\zeta}(q))\in H^{\geq 2(2+K_{X}\cdot\beta)}(X)[\![1-\zeta q,Q]\!].\end{split}

3.2.
Relations between GV and QK

In this subsection, we prove three formulas relating Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants to quantum

Proposition 3.4
.

Let

Proof.

We compute

.

Combine all three contributions, we have

\begin{split}&{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta}(\Gamma)={\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta}(\gamma)\\
&+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)\\
r\neq 1\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\Big(\phi(r)-\mu(r)\Big){\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta/r}(\Gamma)+\delta_{K_{X}\cdot\beta,0}\frac{\mu(r)}{r^{2}}{\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta/r}(\gamma)\Bigg).\end{split}

Recall the definition of
{\mathrm{GV}}
and reorganize the sum gives the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\gamma_{1}\in H^{2j}(X)
and
\gamma_{2}\in H^{2(m-K_{X}\cdot\beta-1-j)}(X)
. Let
\Gamma_{i}:={\rm ch}^{-1}(\gamma_{i})\in K^{0}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
for
i=1
and
2
. We have

{\mathrm{GV}}_{\beta}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\sum_{r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)}\mu(r){\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta/r}(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}).

Proof.

Using the same approach as Propositon
3.4
, we compute
{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})
from the type 1, type 2, and type 3 contributions in VKHRR.

The type 1 contribution gives
{\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
since there is no twisting contribution for dimensional reason.

The type 2 contribution has two cases. If
-K_{X}\cdot\beta=0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)\\
r\neq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\zeta^{r}=1}\Big[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\Big]^{X_{\zeta}}_{0,2,\beta/r}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)\\
r\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(r)}{r}{\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta/r}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).

If
-K_{X}\cdot\beta>0
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)\\
r\neq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\zeta^{r}=1}\Big[\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}\Big]^{X_{\zeta}}_{0,2,\beta/r}=0

for dimensional reason.

There is no type 3 contribution for dimensional reason. More precisely, we have

\Big[\Gamma_{1},{\rm leg}_{r}(L)(q=0),\Gamma_{2}\Big]^{X,K}_{0,3,0}=0

since
{\rm ch}({\rm leg}(L))(q=0)\in H^{\geq 2(2+K_{X}\cdot\beta)}(X)[\![Q]\!]
by Corollary
3.3
.

Combine all three contributions, we have

\begin{split}{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})&={\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})+\delta_{K_{X}\cdot\beta,0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)\\
r\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\phi(r)}{r}{\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta/r}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\\
&=\sum_{r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)}{\mathrm{GV}}_{\beta/r}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}).\end{split}

In the last equality, we rewrite all
{\mathrm{GW}}
in terms of
{\mathrm{GV}}
and use the following identity:

\sum_{k|r}\frac{\phi(k)}{r}=1.

The proposition follows from the Möbius inverse formula.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\gamma_{i}\in H^{*}(X)
for
i=1,\dots,n
with
n\geq 3
and let
\Gamma_{i}:={\rm ch}^{-1}(\gamma_{i})\in K^{0}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. We have

{\mathrm{GV}}_{\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})=\sum_{r|{\mathrm{ind}}(\beta)}\mu(r)r^{n-3}{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta/r}(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n}).

Proof.

Using the same approach as Propositon
3.4
, we compute
{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n})
from the type 1, type 2, and type 3 contributions in VKHRR.

Only type 1 contribution is nonvanishing and we have

\begin{split}{\mathrm{QK}}_{\beta}(\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{n})&={\mathrm{GW}}_{\beta}(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}).\end{split}

The proposition follows from rewriting
{\mathrm{GW}}
to
{\mathrm{GV}}
and apply Möbius inverse formula.
∎

3.3.
Conclusion of the proof

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. We claim that for any
\gamma\in H^{2i}(X;\mathbb{Z})/{\rm torsion}\subset H^{2i}(X;\mathbb{Q})
. There exists
\Gamma\in K^{0}(X)
such that
{\rm ch}(\Gamma)=\gamma
(mod
H^{>2i}(X;\mathbb{Q})
). One can prove it by giving
X
a CW complex structure and induction on the number of cells on
X
. See the argument in
[
Hat03
, Proposition 4.5]
.

Theorem
1.1
follows from the formulas in Proposition
3.4
, Proposition
3.5
, and Proposition
3.6
with only one difference. Instead of defining
\Gamma_{i}
as
{\rm ch}^{-1}(\gamma_{i})
which defines over
\mathbb{Q}
, we take
\Gamma_{i}
from the preceding claim. Note that the higher degree ambiguity will not change the computation in Proposition
3.4
, Proposition
3.5
, and Proposition
3.6
for dimensional reason. The integrality of quantum
K
-invariants then implies the integrality of Gopakumar-Vafa type invariants. This completes the proof.

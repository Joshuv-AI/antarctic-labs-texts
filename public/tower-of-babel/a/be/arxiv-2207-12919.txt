On second eigenvalues of closed hyperbolic surfaces for large genus
Yuxin He and Yunhui Wu

Abstract
. In this article, we study the second eigenvalues of closed hyperbolic surfaces for large genus. We show that for every closed hyperbolic surface X g X_{g} of genus g g ( g ≥ 3 ) (g\geq 3) , up to uniform positive constants multiplications, the second eigenvalue λ 2 ​ ( X g ) \lambda_{2}(X_{g}) of X g X_{g} is greater than ℒ 2 ​ ( X g ) g 2 \frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}{g^{2}} and less than ℒ 2 ​ ( X g ) \mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g}) ; moreover these two bounds are optimal as g → ∞ g\to\infty . Here ℒ 2 ​ ( X g ) \mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g}) is the shortest length of simple closed multi-geodesics separating X g X_{g} into three components. Furthermore, we also investigate the quantity λ 2 ​ ( X g ) ℒ 2 ​ ( X g ) \frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})} for random hyperbolic surfaces of large genus. We show that as g → ∞ g\to\infty , a generic hyperbolic surface X g X_{g} has λ 2 ​ ( X g ) ℒ 2 ​ ( X g ) \frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})} uniformly comparable to 1 ln ⁡ ( g ) \frac{1}{\ln(g)} .



1.
Introduction

The spectrum of the Laplacian on a hyperbolic surface has been a fascinating topic in several mathematical fields including analysis, dynamics, geometry, mathematical physics, number theory and so on for a long time. Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
. The spectrum of
X_{g}
is a discrete closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
and consists of eigenvalues with finite multiplicity. We enumerate them, counted with multiplicity, in the following increasing order:

0=\lambda_{0}(X_{g})<\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\leq\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\leq\cdots\to\infty.

It is known by Buser
[
Bus77
]
that the
(2g-3)
-th eigenvalue can be arbitrarily closed to
0
; and the
n
-th eigenvalue can be arbitrarily closed to
\frac{1}{4}
for any
n\geq(2g-2)
. Otal-Rosas
[
OR09
]
showed that the
(2g-2)
-th eigenvalue is always greater than
\frac{1}{4}
. One may also refer to Ballmann-Matthiesen-Mondal
[
BMM16
,
BMM17
]
and Mondal
[
Mon14
]
for more recent general statements on
\lambda_{2g-2}(X_{g})
.

Definition
.

For a hyperbolic surface
X
and any integer
k\in[1,|\chi(X)|-1]
where
\chi(X)
is the Euler characteristic of
X
, we define a positive quantity
\mathcal{L}_{k}(X)
of
X
to be the minimal possible sum of the lengths of simple closed multi-geodesics in
X
that cut
X
into
k+1
components.

In the closed case, the quantity
\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})
can be arbitrarily closed to
0
when
X_{g}
is close enough to a maximal cusped surface. Schoen-Wolpert-Yau
[
SWY80
]
showed that for any integer
k\in[1,2g-3]
, there exist two constants
\alpha_{k}(g)>0
and
\beta_{k}(g)>0
, depending on
k
and
g
, such that

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(g)\leq\frac{\lambda_{k}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})}\leq\beta_{k}(g).

One may see Dodziuk-Randol
[
DR86
]
for a different proof, and also see Dodziuk-Pignataro-Randol-Sullivan
[
DPRS
]
for similar results of cusped hyperbolic surfaces. Motivated by the work of Dodziuk-Randol
[
DR86
]
, joint with Xue the second named author in
[
WX22a
]
studied the asymptotic behavior of the constant
\alpha_{1}(g)
for large genus and showed that for any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
,

\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\succ\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})}{g^{2}}.

Moreover, it is known by their earlier work
[
WX22c
]
that for all
g\geq 2
, there exists a closed hyperbolic surface
\mathcal{Z}_{g}
of genus
g
such that
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{g})\asymp\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{g})}{g^{2}}
.
And they pose the following question:

Question
.

For every
g\geq 2
,
k\in[1,2g-3]
and any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
,

\lambda_{k}(X_{g})\succ\frac{k\cdot\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})}{g^{2}}?

As introduced above the case of
k=1
was positively answered in
[
WX22a
]
. In this paper we prove

Theorem 1
.

For every
g\geq 3
and any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
,

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}{g^{2}}\prec\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\prec\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g}).

Moreover, these two bounds above are optimal for large genus in the sense that for all
g\geq 3
, there exist two closed hyperbolic surfaces
\mathcal{X}_{g}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{g}
of genus
g
such that

\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\asymp\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{g})}{g^{2}}\ \textit{and}\ \lambda_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g})\asymp\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g}).

Remark
.

(1)

The proof of Theorem
1
above also works for the general index
k
independent of
g
, which we leave to interested readers. For simplicity, we only argue the case of
k=2
in this paper. So the remaining
unsolved
case of the question above is for the index
k=k(g)\in[1,2g-3]
depending on
g
.

(2)

The upper bound in Theorem
1
follows from a refined argument in
[
SWY80
]
. However, the existence of
\mathcal{Y}_{g}
with
\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g})\asymp\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g})
is nowhere trivial, which relies on robust results in
[
Mir13
]
of Mirzakhani on Weil-Petersson random hyperbolic surfaces of large genus.

For the proof of the lower bound in Theorem
1
, the following result on the first positive Neumann eigenvalue plays a key role, which is also of independent interest. More precisely,

Theorem 2
.

If
X_{g,n}
is a compact hyperbolic surface of genus
g
with
n
geodesic boundary component, where
2g+n\geq 4
, and the length of each boundary component is less than or equal to
L
for some constant
L>0
, then there exists a constant
C(L)>0
only depending on
L
such that

\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})\geq C(L)\cdot\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})}{(2g-2+n)^{2}}

where
\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})
is the first positive Neumann eigenvalue of
X_{g,n}
.

We remark here that the constant
C(L)>0
in the theorem above can not be chosen to be uniform as in
[
WX22a
]
for closed hyperbolic surface case: actually we will construct an example to see that
C(L)\to 0
as
L=L(g)\to\infty
(see Example
17
). In either
[
SWY80
]
or
[
DR86
]
, the lower bounds in (
1
) for index
i\in[2,2g-3]
follow from
\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\geq\alpha_{1}(g)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})
together with a max-minimal principle. However, when applying the same argument together by using
\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\succ\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})}{g^{2}}
in
[
WX22a
]
, one can not get the lower bound in Theorem
1
because
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})
may go to infinity as
g\to\infty
, preventing us from directly applying Theorem
2
. The major contribution for the lower bound in Theorem
1
is to overcome this difficulty and get the optimal coefficient
\frac{1}{g^{2}}
for large
g
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
denote the moduli space of closed hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric. Recently the study of random surfaces in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
for large genus is quite active, which was initiated by Mirzakhani
[
Mir13
]
based on her celebrated thesis works
[
Mir07a
,
Mir07b
]
. One may see
[
GPY11
,
MP19
,
MT22
,
NWX23
,
PWX22
]
and references therein for recent developments on the geometry of random surfaces of large genus; and see
[
GLMST21
,
LMS23
,
Tho22
,
Mon21
,
WX22b
,
LW21
,
Hid23
,
Rud23
,
SW22
]
and references therein for recent developments on the spectral theory of random surfaces of large genus.

We view the quantity
\frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}
as a positive random variable on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
. Theorem
1
tells that

\inf\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}\asymp\frac{1}{g^{2}}\ \textit{and}\ \sup\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}\asymp 1.

In this paper, we show that as
g\to\infty
, a generic
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
has
\frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}
uniformly comparable to
\frac{1}{\ln g}
. More precisely, let
\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}
be the probability measure on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
induced by the Weil-Petersson metric. We prove

Theorem 3
.

The following limit holds:

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}\asymp\frac{1}{\ln g}\right)=1.

It would be
interesting
to know whether the following two limits exist:

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\inf\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\frac{g^{2}\cdot\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}\ \textit{and}\ \lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\sup\limits_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\frac{\lambda_{2}(X_{g})}{\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})}.

Strategy on the proof.

We briefly introduce the proof of Theorem
1
which is divided into two parts.

Optimal lower bound:
we cut
X_{g}
along a simple closed multi-geodesic that realizes
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})
into two components
M_{1},M_{2}
. Then we consider their first eigenvalues for the Neumann boundary condition and aim to use the Max-minimal principle.
When
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})
is uniformly bounded from above
, the lower bound in Theorem
1
is established and based on Theorem
2
which closely follows the methods in
[
WX22a
]
.
When
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})
is unbounded for large genus
g
, we estimate
\sigma_{1}(M_{i})
by the widths of maximal half-collars of boundary components. These two cases are separated as Proposition
19
and
20
in Section
4
. To see that the lower bounds are optimal: actually the surface
\mathcal{X}_{g}
constructed in
[
WX22c
]
satisfies that
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\asymp 1
and
\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\asymp\frac{1}{g^{2}}.

Optimal upper bound:
as in
[
SWY80
]
we take appropriate test functions
\psi
with
\nabla\psi
supporting on the collars of a simple closed multi-geodesic that realizes
\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})
into the max-minimal principle. The upper bound in Theorem
1
follows from certain direct computations. The construction of
\mathcal{Y}_{g}
, realizing the upper bound in Theorem
1
, is from the perspective of probability. By the work of Mirzakhani
[
Mir13
]
, for large
g
one may choose a hyperbolic surface
X_{g-2}\in\mathcal{M}_{g-2}
such that both its systole and Cheeger constant are uniformly positive, and moreover there exists one point in
X_{g-2}
such that the injectivity radius at this point goes to infinity as
g\to\infty
. We remove that point, and consider the unique complete hyperbolic metric on that surface with the same complex structure as
X_{g-2}
. Then it is known by the work of Brooks
[
Bro99
]
that this one-cusped hyperbolic surface of genus
(g-2)
has uniformly positive Cheeger constant. Next we replace the cusp with a geodesic boundary and glue a fixed
S_{2,1}
along it to get a hyperbolic surface
\mathcal{Y}_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
. We shall show that the new surface
\mathcal{Y}_{g}
satisfies that
\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g})\asymp 1
and
\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g})\asymp 1
.

Notations.

For any two positive functions
f_{1}(g)
and
f_{2}(g)
, we say

f_{1}(g)\prec f_{2}(g)\quad\emph{or}\quad f_{2}(g)\succ f_{1}(g)

if there exists a universal constant
C>0
, independent of
g
, such that
f_{1}(g)\leq C\cdot f_{2}(g)
; and we say

f_{1}(g)\asymp f_{2}(g)

if
f_{1}(g)\prec f_{2}(g)
and
f_{2}(g)\prec f_{1}(g)
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we include some basis on 2-dimensional hyperbolic geometry and spectrum theory, along with preparative calculations used in this paper.

Bounds on
\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})

Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the moduli space of closed Riemann surface of genus
g
. Let
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
\mathcal{P}
be a pants decomposition of
X_{g}
consisting of
(3g-3)
pairwise disjoint simple closed geodesics
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3g-3}
. Set

\ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{P})=\max_{1\leq i\leq 3g-3}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(X_{g}).

The Bers’ constant can be defined as

\mathcal{B}_{g}=\sup_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\inf_{\mathrm{pants\ decomp}\ \mathcal{P}}\ \ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{P}).

In
[
Ber85
]
it was shown that

\mathcal{B}_{g}\leq 26(g-1)
for
g\geq 2
. It easily follows that
\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})\leq 78\cdot k\cdot g
for
1\leq k\leq 2g-3
. However, this estimate is not precise enough for our purpose.

In this subsection, we will prove the following two estimates.

Proposition 4
.

If
X_{g,n}
is a hyperbolic surface of genus
g
with
n
geodesic boundary components, where
2g+n\geq 4
, and the length of each boundary component is less than or equal to
L
for some constant
L>0
, then there exists a constant
s(L)>0
depending only on
L
such that

\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})\leq s(L)\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})\right).

Remark
.

As
L\to 0
, this in particular tells that Proposition
4
also holds for cusped hyperbolic surfaces.

Proposition 5
.

For any integer
k\in[1,2g-3]
, there exists a universal constant
c_{k}>0
only depending on
k
such that for any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})\leq c_{k}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right).

Remark
.

The case of
k=1
for Proposition
5
has been proved in
[
NWX23
]
, based on the result in
[
Sab08
]
. We will provide a new and elementary proof for general
k
. Moreover, we will show that as
g\to\infty
, a generic hyperbolic surface
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
has
\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})\succ\ln g
: see Proposition
24
and its following remark.

Before proving them, we will introduce several important results on hyperbolic geometry. The first one is the classical Collar Lemma. see
e.g.
[
Kee74
]
.

Lemma 6

(Collar Lemma)
.

If
\gamma
is a simple closed geodesic on a hyperbolic surface
X
, then the neighborhood

T(\gamma)=\{x\in X:\mathop{\rm dist}(x,\gamma)\leq w(\gamma)\},

called the collar of
\gamma
, is isometric to the cylinder
(\rho,t)\in[-w(\gamma),w(\gamma)]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
with the metric

ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+\ell^{2}_{\gamma}(X)\cosh^{2}{\rho}\,dt^{2},

where
w(\gamma)=\mathop{\rm arcsinh}\left(\frac{1}{\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2}\ell_{\gamma}(X)\right)}\right)
is the half width of the collar
T(\gamma)
.

Let
S_{g,n}
be a fixed surface of genus
g
with
n
boundary components.
We use
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(L)=\mathcal{M}(S_{g,n},\ell_{\alpha_{i}}=L_{i})
with
L=(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}
to represent the moduli space of hyperbolic Riemann surfaces homeomorphic to
S_{g,n}
with
n
geodesic boundary components
(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})
of lengths
(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})
.
The following theorem, given by Parlier in
[
Par05
]
, will be frequently applied in this paper to deal with hyperbolic surfaces with boundaries.

Theorem 7

(Parlier)
.

Use
L\leq\tilde{L}
to represent
L_{i}\leq\tilde{L}_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq n
,
where
L=(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})
and
\tilde{L}=(\tilde{L}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{L}_{n})
.
For any
X_{g,n}\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(L)
, if
L\leq\tilde{L}
, then there is an
\tilde{X}_{g,n}\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\tilde{L})
such that for any simple closed curve
\gamma
on
S_{g,n}
,

\ell_{\gamma}(X_{g,n})\leq\ell_{\gamma}(\tilde{X}_{g,n}).

Similarly, for any
X_{g,n}\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(L)
, if
L\geq\tilde{L}
, then there is an
\tilde{X}_{g,n}\in\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(\tilde{L})
such that for any simple closed curve
\gamma
on
S_{g,n}
,

\ell_{\gamma}(X_{g,n})\geq\ell_{\gamma}(\tilde{X}_{g,n}).

Here the length of a boundary component is allowed to be zero, representing a puncture instead of a boundary geodesic.

For a hyperbolic surface
X
, its
systole

\mathop{\rm sys}(X)
is the infimum of all lengths of simple closed geodesics that are not homotopic to any boundary component of
X
. An easy area argument shows that for any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
,

(2)

\mathop{\rm sys}(X_{g})\leq 2\ln(4g-2)\leq 6\ln g.

Proposition
4

. The boundary
\partial N_{\epsilon}(c\cup\gamma)
will be homotopic to some simple closed geodesics that bound a pair of pants along with
\gamma
, with the total length bounded from above by
4w+2\ell_{\gamma}(X_{g}).
It follows from (
5
) and (
7
) that

(8)

\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})\leq 4w+3\ell_{\gamma}(X_{g})\leq 38\ln g.

Recall that
\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})=4\pi(g-1)
by Gauss-Bonnet. Therefore it follows from (
6
) and (
8
) that

\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})\leq c_{1}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right)

for a suitable universal constant
c_{1}>0
.

Now we show the way to find
c_{k}
based on the estimate for
k-1
if
k\geq 2
. Let
\gamma=\cup_{j=1}^{t}\gamma_{j}
be the multi-curve realizing
\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(X_{g})
which separates
X_{g}
into
k
components
\{M_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
. By assumption, we have

(9)

\ell_{\partial M_{i}}(M_{i})\leq\ell_{\gamma}(X_{g})=\mathcal{L}_{k-1}(X_{g})\leq c_{k-1}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right).

For large
g
, at least one component
M_{i}
is not of type
S_{1,1}
or
S_{0,3}
, and we can assume it is
M_{1}
. Then it
follows from (
3
) and (
9
) that

(10)

\mathcal{L}_{1}(M_{1})\leq 2c_{k-1}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right)+4\ln\left(1+\frac{4\mathop{\rm Area}(M_{1})}{c_{k-1}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right)}\right).

Along with
\gamma
, the multi-curve realizing
\mathcal{L}_{1}(M_{1})
will separate
X_{g}
into
k+1
components. So by (
9
) and (
10
) we have

\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})\leq 3c_{k-1}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right)+4\ln\left(1+\frac{4\mathop{\rm Area}(M_{1})}{c_{k-1}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right)}\right),

which implies the conclusion

\mathcal{L}_{k}(X_{g})\leq c_{k}\ln\left(\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})\right)

for suitable choice of
c_{k}
only depending on

, since
\mathop{\rm Area}(M_{1})\leq\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})=4\pi(g-1)
.
∎

Eigenvalues for geometric Laplacian

Use
\phi_{k}
to represent the
k^{th}
normalized eigenfunction of
\Delta
with respect to the
k^{th}
eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(X_{g})
of
X_{g}
. It is easy to see that
\phi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g})}}
. For
k\geq 1
, the
k^{th}
eigenvalue of
\Delta
can be expressed as

\lambda_{k}(X_{g})=\inf\limits_{\left<f,\phi_{i}\right>=0,i<k}\frac{\int_{X_{g}}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu}{\int_{X_{g}}f^{2}d\mu},

where
\left<f,\phi_{i}\right>
means
\int_{X_{g}}f\phi_{i}d\mu
, and
f
runs over the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(X_{g})
or
C^{\infty}(X_{g})
, which is dense in
W^{1,2}(X_{g})
. The infimum of this Rayleigh quotient can be achieved by some eigenfunction
\phi_{k}\in C^{\infty}(X_{g})
.

For
X_{g,n}
with non-empty boundary
\partial X_{g,n}
, the first positive eigenvalue
\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})
for Neumann boundary condition is

\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})=\inf\limits_{\int_{X_{g,n}}fd\mu=0}\frac{\int_{X_{g,n}}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu}{\int_{X_{g,n}}f^{2}d\mu},

where
f
runs over
W^{1,2}(X_{g,n})
. The infimum can be achieved by some Neumann eigenfunction
f\in C^{\infty}(X_{g,n})
, and the partial derivative
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\vec{n}}
in the normal direction will vanish at
\partial X_{g,n}
.

Generally the
k^{th}
eigenvalue can be estimated by the following max-minimal principle. See
e.g.
[
Ber16
, Theorem
3.34
]
for its proof.

Theorem 8

(Max-minimal principle)
.

(1) If
X=N_{1}\cup\cdots\cup N_{k}
is a partition of surface
X
, with
\mathop{\rm Area}(N_{i})>0
and
\mathop{\rm Area}(N_{i}\cap N_{j})=0
, then

\lambda_{k}(X)\geq\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\sigma_{1}(N_{i}).

(2) If
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k+1}
are
k+1
functions on
X
with
L^{2}
norm
1
and the supports of any two of them have intersections of measure zero, then

\lambda_{k}(X)\leq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq k+1}\int_{X}|\nabla f_{i}|^{2}d\mu.

The Cheeger inequality
[
Che70
]
gives lower bounds for first eigenvalues.

Definition
.

For a surface
\Omega
with possibly non-empty boundary, the Cheeger constant
h(\Omega)
of
\Omega
is defined as

h(\Omega)=\inf_{\Gamma}\frac{\ell(\Gamma\cap\mathring{\Omega})}{\min\{\mathop{\rm Area}(A),\mathop{\rm Area}(B)\}},

where
\mathring{\Omega}
is the interior of
\Omega
(
\mathring{\Omega}=\Omega
if
\Omega
is closed), and
\Gamma
is any set of piecewise smooth curves separating
\Omega
into two parts
A
and
B
. It is known by an observation due to Yau that
A
and
B
can be chosen to be connected.

Theorem 9

(Cheeger inequality)
.

If
\Omega=X_{g,n}
is a hyperbolic surface with non-empty boundary, then

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{4}h^{2}(\Omega).

If
\Omega=X_{g}
is a closed hyperbolic surface, then

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{4}h^{2}(\Omega).

2.0.1.
Eigenvalues for
S_{1,1}
and
S_{0,3}

For a compact hyperbolic surface
X
of type
S_{1,1}
or
S_{0,3}
, the geometric quantity
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X)
is not meaningful. Its first eigenvalue with Neumann boundary condition can be controlled solely by its boundary lengths. Before proving that, we recall the following two classical results.
The first one is the isoperimetric inequality. See
e.g.
Section
8.1
in
[
Bus10
]
.

Lemma 10

(Isoperimetric inequalities)
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain with piecewise smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, equipped with the hyperbolic metric. If
\Omega
is topologically a disk or a cylinder, then

\ell(\partial\Omega)\geq\mathop{\rm Area}(\Omega).

The second one is as follows.
See
e.g.
Theorem 4.2.2 in
[
Bus10
]
for details.

Lemma 11
.

If
\gamma\subset X
is a non-simple closed geodesic on
X
, then

\ell_{\gamma}(X)\geq 4\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1.

With the help of the two lemmas above, we will prove two lower bounds of the first eigenvalues with respect to Neumann boundary conditions for
S_{1,1}
and
S_{0,3}
, which will be applied later.

Lemma 12
.

If
X
is of type
S_{0,3}
or
S_{1,1}
with each boundary component of length less than
L
, then there exists a constant
K(L)>0
only depending on
L
such that

\sigma_{1}(X)\geq K(L).

Proof.

Consider a division of
X
into
A\cup B
with
\partial A\cap\mathring{X}=\partial B\cap\mathring{X}=\mathring{\Gamma}
, where
\mathring{\Gamma}=\Gamma\setminus\partial\Gamma
is the interior of the (multi)-curve
\Gamma
obtained by removing its boundary points on
\partial X
, and
h(X)\leq\frac{\ell(\Gamma)}{\min\{\mathop{\rm Area}(A),\mathop{\rm Area}(B)\}}\leq 2h(X)
. It follows that

h(X)\geq\frac{\ell(\Gamma)}{2\pi}.

By the Collar Lemma,
i.e.
, Lemma
6
, each boundary geodesic admits a half collar of width
w(L)>0
.
If
\Gamma
intersects with the boundary
\partial X
and is not homotopic to an arc on
\partial X
, then
\ell(\Gamma)\geq 2w(L)
. Therefore, we have

(11)

h(X)>\frac{w(L)}{\pi}.

If
\Gamma
contains a non-simple component and
\Gamma\cap\partial X=\emptyset
, then by Lemma
11

\ell(\Gamma)\geq 4\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1.
Therefore, we have

(12)

h(X)>\frac{2\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1}{\pi}.

If neither of the the above two situations occurs, then
\Gamma
will bound disks or cylinders, assuming the union of which is
A
. By Lemma
10
, we have
\mathop{\rm Area}(A)\leq\ell(\partial A)
. However, some parts of
\partial A
may lie on
\partial X
: those arcs are homotopic to some sub-arcs of
\Gamma
. Therefore we always have
\ell(\partial A)\leq 2\ell(\Gamma)
.
This gives

(13)

h(X)\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Then the lemma follows from the Cheeger inequality,
i.e.
, Theorem
9
, along with equations (
11
), (
12
) and (
13
).
∎

Viewing
X
as a subsurface of
X_{g}
,
if the boundary lengths of
X
are not bounded from above by any constant
L
, but grow slowly as
g\to\infty
, then we have the following estimate.

Lemma 13
.

If
X
is of type
S_{1,1}
or
S_{0,3}
, with each boundary component of length less than
\ln g
for
g\geq 2
, then we have

\sigma_{1}(X)\succ\frac{1}{g}.

Proof.

By Lemma
6
, each boundary component of
X
admits a half collar of width

\displaystyle w=

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm arcsinh}\frac{1}{\sinh\frac{\ln g}{2}}\geq\mathop{\rm arcsinh}\frac{2}{g^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{2}{g^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{g}}\right)\geq\frac{k}{g^{\frac{1}{2}}}

for some uniform constant
k>0
. Consider a division of
X
into
A\cup B
with
\partial A\cap\mathring{X}=\partial B\cap\mathring{X}=\mathring{\Gamma}
and
h(X)\leq\frac{\ell(\Gamma)}{\min\{\mathop{\rm Area}(A),\mathop{\rm Area}(B)\}}\leq 2h(X)
.
If some part
\gamma
in
\Gamma
intersects
\partial X
and isn’t homotopic to an arc on
\partial X
, then
\ell(\gamma)\geq 2w
, so
h(X)\geq\frac{w}{\pi}.

If
\Gamma
have non-simple closed part, by Lemma
11
we have
\ell(\Gamma)\geq 4\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1
, so
h(X)\geq\frac{2\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1}{\pi}
.
Otherwise, similar to the proof of Lemma
12
, it follows from Lemma
10
that
\frac{\ell(\Gamma)}{\min\{\mathop{\rm Area}(A),\mathop{\rm Area}(B)\}}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, which means
h(X)\geq\frac{1}{4}
. Then it follows from Theorem
9
that

\sigma_{1}(X)\geq\min\left\{\frac{1}{64},\ \left(\frac{\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1}{\pi}\right)^{2},\ \frac{k^{2}}{4\pi^{2}g}\right\}\asymp\frac{1}{g},

which completes the proof.
∎

3.
Uniform lower bounds for first positive Neumann eigenvalues

It was proven in
[
WX22a
]
that

Theorem 14

(Wu-Xue)
.

For any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
, we have

\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\succ\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g})}{g^{2}}.

In this section we prove Theorem
2
, extending the above result to first Neumann eigenvalues for compact hyperbolic surface
X_{g,n}
with non-empty geodesic boundaries, where
X_{g,n}
is not of type
S_{1,1}
and
S_{0,3}
. This plays a key role in the proof of our main result Theorem
1
. We follow the idea in
[
WX22a
]
to prove Theorem
2
. Before proving it, we provide the following two propositions as summaries of special cases encountered in the proof. Firstly we fix a uniform constant

(14)

0<\epsilon<0.05.

Proposition 15
.

Let
X_{g,n}
be the hyperbolic surface in Theorem
2
and satisfy
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})\geq\epsilon
. Then there is a constant
\hat{c}_{\epsilon}(L)>0
only depending on
L
and
\epsilon
such that

\frac{\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})}{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})}\geq\frac{\hat{c}_{\epsilon}(L)}{|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|^{2}}.

Proof.

Consider a division of
X_{g,n}
into
A\cup B
with
\partial A\cap\mathring{X}_{g,n}=\partial B\cap\mathring{X}_{g,n}=\mathring{\Gamma}
such that
\mathop{\rm Area}(A)\leq\mathop{\rm Area}(B)
and
h(X_{g,n})\leq\frac{\ell(\Gamma)}{\mathop{\rm Area}(A)}\leq 2h(X_{g,n})
.

Case-1:

A
contains only disks or cylinders.
Similar as in the proof of Lemma
12
, it follows from Lemma
10
that
\frac{\ell(\Gamma)}{\mathop{\rm Area}(A)}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Hence,
h(X_{g,n})\geq\frac{1}{4}
. By Proposition
4
and Theorem
9
, we have

(15)

\frac{\sigma_{1}(M)}{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})}\geq\frac{1}{64s(L)\ln(|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|)}\geq\frac{1}{64s(L)}\frac{1}{|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|^{2}}.

Case-2:

\Gamma\cap\partial X_{g,n}=\emptyset
and
A
contains some component which is not topologically a disk or a cylinder.
In this case
\Gamma
contains a homotopically non-trivial closed curve which is not a boundary component. In particular,
\ell(\Gamma)\geq\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})
. By Theorem
9
and the assumption
\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})\geq\epsilon
, we have

(16)

\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})\geq\frac{1}{16}\frac{\ell^{2}(\Gamma)}{|\mathop{\rm Area}(A)|^{2}}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{4|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|^{2}}\cdot\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n}).

Case-3:

A
is not of the types in case-1 or case-2.
In this case,

A
contains a connected component
A_{0}
such that
\partial A_{0}
contains
a simple curve
\Gamma_{0}
which intersects with
\partial X_{g,n}
, and does not bound a disk along with
\partial X_{g,n}
. Since for any component
\gamma\subset\partial X_{g,n}
,
\ell_{\gamma}(X_{g,n})\leq L
, by Lemma
6
, all boundary components admit a half collar of width
w(L)=\mathop{\rm arcsinh}\left(\frac{1}{\sinh\frac{L}{2}}\right)
. Hence we have

\ell(\Gamma_{0})\geq 2w(L).

If
\Gamma_{0}
connects two different boundary components
\alpha
and
\beta
at
p_{0}\in\alpha
and
q_{0}\in\beta
, then we consider
\partial N_{\epsilon}(\alpha\cup\Gamma_{0}\cup\beta)
, which is homotopic to a simple closed geodesic
\delta
of length

\ell_{\delta}(X_{g,n})\leq\ell_{\alpha}(X_{g,n})+\ell_{\beta}(X_{g,n})+2\ell(\Gamma_{0})\leq 2L+2\ell(\Gamma_{0}).

Along with
\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
\delta
bounds a pair of pants. Since
2g+n\geq 4
,
\delta
is not a boundary geodesic; otherwise
X_{g,n}\cong S_{0,3}
. So we have

\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})\leq\ell_{\delta}(X_{g,n}).

In this situation, by Theorem
9
and
\ell(\Gamma_{0})\geq 2w(L)
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})}{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4(2L+2\ell(\Gamma_{0}))}\left(\frac{\ell(\Gamma_{0})}{2\mathop{\rm Area}(A)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{w^{2}(L)}{(2L+4w(L))|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|^{2}}.

If
\Gamma_{0}
has two endpoints on the same boundary component
\alpha
, and the two endpoints cut
\alpha
into
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
, then
the piecewise smooth curves
\Gamma_{0}\cup\alpha_{1}
and
\Gamma_{0}\cup\alpha_{2}
will be homotopic to simple closed geodesics
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
respectively of lengths

\ell_{\delta_{i}}(X_{g,n})\leq\ell(\Gamma_{0})+L

for

. Along with
\alpha
,
\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}

will bound a pair of pants. Since
2g+n\geq 4
,
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
can not simultaneously be boundary geodesics; otherwise
X_{g,n}\cong S_{0,3}
. Since
X_{g,n}
is not of type
S_{1,1}
,
\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
separates
X_{g,n}
. In particular, we have

\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})\leq\ell_{\delta_{1}}(X_{g,n})+\ell_{\delta_{2}}(X_{g,n}).

In this situation, by Theorem
9
and
\ell(\Gamma_{0})\geq 2w(L)
, we also have

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})}{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4(2L+2\ell(\Gamma_{0}))}\left(\frac{\ell(\Gamma_{0})}{2\mathop{\rm Area}(A)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{w^{2}(L)}{(2L+4w(L))|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|^{2}}.

In conclusion, it follows from (
15
), (
16
), (

\frac{\sigma_{1}(X_{g,n})}{\mathcal{L}_{1}(X_{g,n})}\geq\frac{1}{|\mathop{\rm Area}(X_{g,n})|^{2}}\cdot\min\left\{\frac{1}{64s(L)},\ \frac{\epsilon}{4},\ \frac{w^{2}(L)}{2L+4w(L)}\right\}.

The proof is completed by setting
\hat{c}_{\epsilon}(L)=\min\left\{\frac{1}{64s(L)},\ \frac{\epsilon}{4},\ \frac{w^{2}(L)}{2L+4w(L)}\right\}
.
∎

If

5.
Optimal upper bounds for second eigenvalues

In this section we complete the proof of the optimal upper bound in Theorem
1
. More precisely,

Theorem 21

(Optimal upper bound)
.

For every
g\geq 3
and any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
,

\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\prec\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g}).

Moreover, for all
g\geq 3
there exists a closed hyperbolic surfaces
\mathcal{Y}_{g}
of genus

\lambda_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g})\asymp\mathcal{L}_{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{g}).

It is known by Cheng
[

]
that the eigenvalues of

can be bounded from above by the diameter
\diam(X_{g})
of

. More precisely,
for all

\lambda_{k}(X_{g})\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{16\pi^{2}\cdot k^{2}}{\diam(X_{g})^{2}}.

A standard area argument together with Gauss-Bonnet implies that

\diam(X_{g})\geq\ln(4g-3).

The two inequalities above yield that for all

(42)

\limsup\limits_{g\to\infty}\sup_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\lambda_{k}(X_{g})\leq\frac{1}{4}.

A recent breakthrough of Hide-Magee
[

]
says that this upper bound
\frac{1}{4}
is optimal. More precisely, they showed that for all

,

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\sup_{X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}}\lambda_{k}(X_{g})=\frac{1}{4}.

One may also see
[
WZZ22
]
for optimal higher spectral gaps of closed hyperbolic surfaces of large genus. In this article we study its connection to the geometric quantity
\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})
. First we refine the argument in
[

]
to show the upper bound in Theorem

.

Proof of Part
(1)
of Theorem

.

We split the proof into two cases.

. Then it follows from (

(43)

\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\leq\frac{1}{2\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1}\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{64\pi^{2}}{(\ln{9})^{2}}\right)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})\asymp\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g}).

Case-2:

\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})\leq 2\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1.
Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{2}(X_{g})
is realized by a simple closed multi-geodesic
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}
. We write

and

Manipulating Large Language Models to Increase Product Visibility
Aounon Kumar, Himabindu Lakkaraju

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being integrated into search engines to provide natural language responses tailored to user queries. Customers and end-users are also becoming more dependent on these models for quick and easy purchase decisions. In this work, we investigate whether recommendations from LLMs can be manipulated to enhance a product’s visibility. We demonstrate that adding a strategic text sequence (STS)—a carefully crafted message—to a product’s information page can significantly increase its likelihood of being listed as the LLM’s top recommendation. To understand the impact of STS, we use a catalog of fictitious coffee machines and analyze its effect on two target products: one that seldom appears in the LLM’s recommendations and another that usually ranks second. We observe that the strategic text sequence significantly enhances the visibility of both products by increasing their chances of appearing as the top recommendation. This ability to manipulate LLM-generated search responses provides vendors with a considerable competitive advantage and has the potential to disrupt fair market competition. Just as search engine optimization (SEO) revolutionized how webpages are customized to rank higher in search engine results, influencing LLM recommendations could profoundly impact content optimization for AI-driven search services. Code for our experiments is available at: https://github.com/aounon/llm-rank-optimizer .

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) are being used on e-commerce platforms to search product catalogs and provide users with personalized recommendations tailored to their specific query.
Although traditional search engines are good at retrieving relevant pages, they lack the ability to synthesize the information and present it as a coherent response.
LLMs are quickly filling this gap by compiling search results into natural language responses that directly address users’ specific inquiries.
Search engines like Google Search and Microsoft Bing have begun integrating LLM-driven chat interfaces alongside their traditional search boxes.

In this work, we demonstrate that an LLM can be manipulated to increase the chances of a product being listed as the top recommendation.
We develop a framework to game an LLM’s recommendations in favor of a target product by inserting a strategic text sequence (STS) into the product’s information. Our framework leverages adversarial attack algorithms such as the Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) algorithm
[

to optimize the STS. Although such algorithms are typically designed to bypass an LLM’s safety guardrails
and induce harmful outputs, we show that they can be repurposed for more benign objectives, such as increasing product visibility, that can have a profound impact on society, particularly
in business and e-commerce.
Our framework also allows us to optimize the STS to be robust to variations in the order in which retrieved product information is listed in the LLM’s input prompt.

Using a catalog of fictitious coffee machines, we demonstrate that adding the strategic text sequence significantly improves the visibility of the target product and increases its chances of being recommended to potential customers.

The ability to manipulate LLM search responses gives vendors a significant competitive advantage over rival products. This capability has far-reaching implications for market dynamics, as it can alter the balance of competition and lead to a skewed representation of products.
As LLMs become more deeply embedded in the digital commerce infrastructure, safeguards must be established to prevent the exploitation of AI-driven search tools for unfair advantage.

3
Strategic Text Sequence

In this section, we describe the LLM-driven search framework and the method for generating strategic text sequences.
We focus on the use case of online product recommendations and define the search framework as illustrated in Figure
3
.
The process begins with a user making a query, which prompts the system to extract relevant product information from a knowledge base, such as the Internet or a specific product catalog database.
The extracted product information and the query are then concatenated into a single text sequence and fed into the LLM to generate a curated response for the specific user query.
A vendor influences the process by inserting a carefully designed text sequence on their product’s information page to steer the LLM toward favoring their products in its recommendations.

In our experimental setup, we assume that the information about the target product is retrieved from the knowledge base and fed to the LLM.
This is essential for the vendor to have any influence over the LLM, as the target product’s information page is the only component that they can control.
We also restrict our focus to open-source language models, such as Llama-2, as the LLM. However, sequences produced by adversarial attack algorithms such as GCG have been shown to transfer to black-box models like GPT-4
[
1
]
.

We consider the scenario where a user is looking for recommendations for affordable coffee machines.
The LLM prompt consists of three main components: the system prompt, the product information extracted from the knowledge base (list of coffee machines), and the user query.
The system prompt sets the context of the conversation and controls the behavior of the LLM.
The product information is formatted as a collection of JSON lines containing information about ten fictitious coffee machines.
Each JSON line consists of fields such as Name, Description, Price, Capacity, Customer Rating, etc.
The strategic text sequence (STS) is inserted in a field of one of these products, namely the target product.
The user query is a request for recommendations for affordable coffee machines.

We optimize the STS with the objective of minimizing the LLM output’s cross-entropy loss with respect to the string ‘1. [Target Product Name]’.
We initialize the STS with a sequence of dummy tokens ‘*’ and iteratively optimize it using the GCG algorithm.
At each iteration, this algorithm randomly selects an STS token and replaces it with one of the top
k
tokens with the highest gradient.
The STS can also be made robust to variations in product order by randomly permuting the product list in each iteration.

We evaluate the LLM with the optimized STS inserted in the target product’s information.
It generates a list of coffee machines based on the user’s preferences, in this case, affordability.
The STS in the product’s information causes the LLM to recommend it highly, even though it may not be an affordable option and would not be recommended otherwise.
The following is the effect of rank optimization on the LLM’s response.
The target product ColdBrew Master is shown as the top recommendation over more affordable options such as SingleServe Wonder and FrenchPress Classic in the product list.

We study the impact of the strategic text sequence (STS) on the LLM’s ranking of two target products: ColdBrew Master and QuickBrew Express.
The LLM almost never shows ColdBrew Master to customers due to its high price of $199.
However, we show that adding the STS to this product’s information can not only make it appear in the LLM’s response but also position it as the top-recommended product in most cases.
This indicates that the STS can skew LLM recommendations towards products that may not align with the user’s criteria.
However, one might argue that, given the high price of ColdBrew Master, customers are unlikely to buy it even if it is the top recommendation.
So, we study a more reasonably priced product, QuickBrew Express ($89), which commonly ranks second in the LLM’s recommendations.
We observe that the addition of the STS can noticeably increase the product’s visibility, elevating it to the top recommendation with high probability.
This shows that even for products close to the top of the recommendation list, the STS has substantial influence over the product’s ranking and visibility, offering a competitive advantage over a rival product.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we demonstrate that strategic text sequences can influence LLM-driven search tools in the context of e-commerce.
We show that a vendor can significantly improve their product’s ranking in the LLM’s recommendations by inserting an optimized sequence of tokens into the product information page.
The ability to manipulate LLM recommendations has serious implications for business and commerce, as it offers vendors a competitive edge in the market by enhancing their products’ visibility.
While our work explores a specific vulnerability in LLMs, more research is needed to uncover other vulnerabilities that can give businesses an unfair advantage.

The impact of our findings extends beyond e-commerce, shedding light on the broader implications of AI search optimization and the ethical considerations that come with it.
As LLMs become increasingly integral to the digital landscape, it is crucial to develop safeguards against potential misuse.
Future work in this area could explore the ethical boundaries of using strategic text sequences, develop guidelines for their responsible use, and investigate countermeasures to prevent unfair manipulation of search results.
By balancing search optimization and ethical standards, we can ensure that advancements in AI search technology contribute to a fair and competitive marketplace.

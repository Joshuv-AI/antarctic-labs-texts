Estimating and computing Kronecker Coefficients: a vector partition function approach
Marni Mishna and Stefan Trandafir

Abstract
. We study the Kronecker coefficients g λ , μ , ν g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu} via a formula that was described by Mishna, Rosas, and Sundaram, in which the coefficients are expressed as a signed sum of vector partition function evaluations. In particular, we use this formula to determine formulas to evaluate, bound, and estimate g λ , μ , ν g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu} in terms of the lengths of the partitions λ , μ \lambda,\mu , and ν \nu . We describe a computational tool to compute Kronecker coefficients g λ , μ , ν g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu} with ℓ ⁡ ( μ ) ≤ 2 , ℓ ⁡ ( ν ) ≤ 4 , ℓ ⁡ ( λ ) ≤ 8 \ell(\mu)\leq 2,\ \ell(\nu)\leq 4,\ \ell(\lambda)\leq 8 . We present a set of new vanishing conditions for the Kronecker coefficients by relating to the vanishing of the related atomic Kronecker coefficients, themselves given by a single vector partition function evaluation. We give a stable face of the Kronecker polyhedron for any positive integers m , n m,n . Finally, we give upper bounds on both the atomic Kronecker coefficients and Kronecker coefficients.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Computational questions on Kronecker coefficients

The
Kronecker coefficients

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
are the structure constants in the decomposition of a tensor product of irreducible representations of the symmetric group into irreducible representations:

(1)

V_{\mu}\otimes V_{\nu}=\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda}g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}V_{\lambda}.

Consequently, they can be expressed using Schur functions

(2)

s_{\lambda}[XY]=\sum_{\mu,\nu}g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}s_{\mu}[X]s_{\nu}[Y],

where
X:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),Y:=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),XY:=(x_{1}y_{1},x_{1}y_{2},\dots,x_{m}y_{n})
.
Here, the Schur functions are indexed by partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
with at most
mn,m,n
parts respectively. The number of parts of a partition
\alpha
is its
length
and is denoted here by
\ell(\alpha)
. When we focus on the set of partitions of length at most
k
, we may pad the partitions with
0
’s so that they are all sequences of length
k
.

Since their introduction in 1938 by Murnaghan, the Kronecker coefficients have proved to be among the most intriguing objects in algebraic combinatorics. After several decades of research, many open questions about the Kronecker coefficients remain. They are all non-negative integers, but have no known combinatorial interpretation. The Littlewood-Richardson
c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}
coefficients describe the decomposition of a tensor product of irreducible representations of the
general linear group
into irreducible representations. This difference could appear small, yet the Littlewood-Richardson
c_{\mu,\nu}^{\lambda}
coefficients have several combinatorial interpretations. One might view Kronecker coefficients as a generalization of the Littlewood-Richardson coefficients, hence the resistance to a clear interpretation is surprising, particularly in view of the publicity they have received

Even the problem of approximating the Kronecker coefficients is non-trivial and very few useful bounds are known. Pak and Panova
[

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{l}\binom{\lambda_{i}-i+mn}{mn-i}.

More recently, in

via contingency tables

(4)

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\leq\left(1+\frac{lmn}{N}\right)^{N}\left(1+\frac{N}{lmn}\right)^{lmn}.

Remark both of these bounds are polynomial in the length of the partitions - however, the degree is generally not optimal. For example, when
l=4,m=2,n=2
, we find that if
\lambda=(\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4},\frac{N}{4})
, both bounds are of the order
O(N^{16})
whereas the actual growth is (more precisely)
O(N^{2})

By

, we denote the hook-length of the box

in the Ferrers diagram of
\lambda

also gives a bound:

(5)

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\leq\min(f^{\lambda},f^{\mu},f^{\nu}),

where
f^{\alpha}:=\frac{k!}{\prod\limits_{u\in[\lambda]}h(u)}
for a partition
\alpha
of length
k
.

Some progress has been made to understand conditions on
\lambda,\mu,\nu
for which
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=0
. Denote by
k\alpha
the partition obtained by multiplying each part of
\alpha
by
k
. Littlewood-Richardson coefficients satisfy a
saturation property
:

c_{\lambda,\mu}^{\nu}=0\iff c_{k\lambda,k\mu}^{k\nu}=0.

The Kronecker coefficients do not satisfy such a property universally:

g_{(1,1),(1,1),(1,1)}=0,\quad\text{ but }\quad g_{(2,2),(2,2),(2,2)}=1.

Even deciding “
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=0
?” is NP-hard
[
14
]
. There are numerous vanishing conditions known- typically expressed as inequalities in the parts of
\lambda,\mu,\nu
which guarantee that the coefficient
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is zero. A classical result of Murnaghan and Littlewood (appearing for example in
[
15
]
) is that for any non-zero Kronecker coefficient
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
, it follows that
\overline{\lambda}\leq\overline{\mu}+\overline{\nu}
, where
\overline{\gamma}
is the partition obtained by deleting the first part of partition
\gamma
. Consider the set of points constructed by concatenating partitions of fixed length. Those points that come from partitions giving a non-zero Kronecker coefficient have a nice geometry. Specifically,

(6)

Kron_{l,m,n}:=\{(\lambda,\mu,\nu)\in\mathbb{Z}^{l+m+n}:g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\neq 0,\ \ell(\lambda)\leq l,\ \ell(\mu)\leq m,\ell(\nu)\leq n\}

is a finitely generated semigroup in
\mathbb{Z}^{l+m+n}
that generates a rational polyhedral cone. Following Manivel
[
18
]
, we call this cone the
Kronecker polyhedron
and denote it
PKron_{l,m,n}
. In
[
16
]
the cone
PKron_{l,m,n}
is computed explicitly for small values of
l,m,n
, and it seems the number of inequalities increases rapidly. While this set is theoretically computable for any positive integers
l,m,n
, it is quickly computationally infeasible to do so. Another set of vanishing conditions valid for triples of partitions of any lengths were given recently by Ressayre in
[
26
, Theorems 1 & 2]
.

A classic result of Murnaghan states that for partitions
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
the sequence
\left(g_{\lambda+(k),\mu+(k)\nu+(k)}\right)_{k\geq 0}
eventually stabilizes. Since then, many other partition triples
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
with this property have been identified– that is, the values of the sequence
\left(g_{\lambda+k\gamma,\mu+k\alpha,\nu+k\beta}\right)_{k\geq 0}
stabilize for any choice of
\lambda,\mu,\nu
. Such triples
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
are called
stable triples
.
Stabilization phenomenon have been studied in
[
8
,
18
,
19
,
25
,
29
]
.

Applications of Kronecker coefficients extend beyond the realm of algebraic combinatorics. The
Geometric Complexity Theory
(GCT) program, developed by Mulmuley and Sohoni, with the goal of solving
P
versus
NP
, relies heavily on the computation of Kronecker coefficients as one of its main ingredients (see
[
4
,
14
,
22
]
). More specifically, problems of positivity (as discussed in the Appendix of
[
7
]
entitled
Erratum to the saturation hypothesis (SH) in
“Geometric Complexity Theory VI”
and contributed by Mulmuley) related to the previously described saturation problems play an important role.

Kronecker coefficients appear in quantum computing where they encode the relationship between composite systems and their subsystems
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
. As in the case of GCT, being able to determine the positivity of Kronecker coefficients is useful. In the context of quantum computing, non-zero Kronecker coefficients correspond to
admissible spectral triples
which play an important role in the study of bipartite quantum states in quantum information theory
[
10
]
.

1.2.
Kronecker coefficients and vector partition functions

Here, we address many of these fundamental questions on Kronecker coefficients using a detailed analysis of Eq. (
2
). The first step is to deduce an expression for
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
using coefficient extraction of multivariate Taylor series of rational functions. This formulation allows us to represent Kronecker coefficients as a signed sum of
vector partition function
evaluations. Let
A
be a
d\times n
matrix
A
with integer entries satisfying

\operatorname{ker}(A)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}=\{\mathbf{0}\}.

Denote the columns of
A
by
\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n}
. The
vector partition function

p_{A}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the counting function

p_{A}(\mathbf{b}):=\#\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}:A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}\}.

Geometrically,
p_{A}(\mathbf{b})
is the discrete volume of the polyhedron defined by the solutions of
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
and the inequalities
x_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,\dots,d
. The generating function formulation is in terms of the coefficient of the term
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}
in the Taylor series expansion of a product of geometric series:

(7)

p_{A}(\mathbf{b})=[\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{b}}]\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{{1-\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}_{j}}},

with the convention that for vectors
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbf{u}^{\mathbf{v}}
denotes the product
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}u_{i}^{v_{i}}
. The rational function
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{{1-\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}_{j}}}
is called the
vector partition generating function
.
Sturmfels
[
30
]
determined that the vector partition function is a piecewise quasi-polynomial whose domains of quasi-polynomiality are convex polyhedral cones called
chambers
of a fan called the
chamber complex
of
A
(defined by Zelevinsky, Alekseevskaya, and Gelfand
[
31
]
).
Barvinok gave an algorithm which allows one to compute
p_{A}(\mathbf{b})
in polynomial-time for fixed dimension
n
of the the polytope
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b},\mathbf{x}\geq\mathbf{0}

[
3
]
. An adapted version of this algorithm (the Barvinok-Woods algorithm) has been implemented in C (the implementation is named
Barvinok
) and is publicly available
[
17
]
.

We reformulate the expression for
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
given in
[
21
, Theorem 26]
as Theorem
1.1
below. The main ingredients in this approach are:

(1)

a matrix
A^{m,n}
and its vector partition function
p_{A^{m,n}}
;

(2)

vectors
\alpha,\beta
;

(3)

linear functions
r_{s},r_{t}
;

(4)

linear functions
l_{s}(\cdot;\sigma),l_{t}(\cdot;\sigma)
defined for each
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{mn}
.

The quantities
\alpha,\beta
, and the linear functions
r_{s},r_{t},l_{s},l_{t}
(which all depend on
m,n
) are explicitly given in the discussion after Theorem
1.1
and defined (implicitly) in
[
21
]
. The matrices
A^{m,n}
are given implicitly in
[
21
]
; we give explicitly only the cases
m=2
,
n=3,4
used in our work. For given
m,n
and
\sigma
, we call the function in the parts of
\lambda,\mu,\nu
,
\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma):=(r_{s}(\mu,\nu)+\alpha-l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma),\ r_{t}(\mu,\nu)+\beta-l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma))
the
vector partition function input of
\sigma
. Additionally, we refer to the quantity

(8)

\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\ p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma)\biggr)

as the
contribution of the alternant term associated to
\sigma
.
In general it will be clear to which
m,n
we refer, but we explicitly state this when needed.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers. Then for any partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq mn,\ \ell(\mu)\leq m,\ \ell(\nu)\leq n
, we have

(9)

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{mn}}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\ p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma)\biggr).

The following expressions are valid for all integers
u,v
with
1\leq u\leq m-1
and
1\leq v\leq n-2
. The components of the vectors
\alpha,\beta
are:

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,{\left(nm+n-m-2\right)}{\left(n-1\right)}{\left(m-1\right)}

\displaystyle\alpha_{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,{\left(u^{2}n-2\,unm+2\,nm^{2}-u^{2}+u-n-2\,m+2\right)}{\left(n-1\right)}

\displaystyle\beta_{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}\,{\left(8\,n^{2}m^{2}-6\,vnm+5\,n^{2}m-10\,nm^{2}+6\,v^{2}-12\,vn+6\,vm-19\,nm+2\,m^{2}+18\,v+14\,m\right)}{\left(m-1\right)}.

The components of the vectors
r_{s}(\mu,\nu)
and
r_{t}(\mu,\nu)
are:

\displaystyle r_{s}(\mu,\nu)_{0}

\displaystyle=|\nu|-\nu_{1}+\binom{n-1}{2}

\displaystyle r_{s}(\mu,\nu)_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=u+1}^{m}\mu_{i}+|\nu|-\nu_{1}+\binom{m-u}{2}+\binom{n-1}{2}

\displaystyle r_{t}(\mu,\nu)_{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{m}(i-1)\mu_{i}+(m-1)\sum_{j=2}^{v+1}\nu_{j}+m\sum_{j=v+2}^{n}\nu_{j}+\binom{m}{3}+(m-1)\binom{n-1}{2}+\binom{n-v-1}{2}.

The components of the vectors
l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma)
and
l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma)
are:

\displaystyle l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma)_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=m+1}^{mn}\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\delta_{\sigma(i)}\biggr)

\displaystyle l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma)_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=u+1}^{m+u(n-1)}\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\delta_{\sigma(i)}\biggr)+2\sum_{i=m+u(n-1)+1}^{mn}\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\delta_{\sigma(i)}\biggr)

\displaystyle l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma)_{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{m}(i-1)\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\delta_{\sigma(i)}\biggr)+(m-1)\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+v}\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\delta_{\sigma(i)}\biggr)+m\sum_{i=m+v+1}^{m+n-1}\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(i)}+\delta_{\sigma(i)}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^{v}(i+m-1)\biggr(\lambda_{\sigma(m+i(n-1)+j)}+\delta_{\sigma(m+i(n-1)+j)}\biggr)+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\sum_{j=v+1}^{n-1}(i+m)\lambda_{m+i(n-1)+j}.

The identity permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{mn}
is denoted by
Id
. It is convenient to have an explicit derivation in the case when
\sigma=Id
:

\displaystyle l_{s}(\lambda;Id)_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=m+1}^{mn}\lambda_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\,{\left(nm-m-1\right)}{\left(n-1\right)}m

\displaystyle l_{s}(\lambda;Id)_{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=u+1}^{m+u(n-1)}\lambda_{i}+2\sum_{i=m+u(n-1)+1}^{mn}\lambda_{i}+\frac{1}{2}(m-u)\biggr(2mn-u-m-1\biggr)

\displaystyle+(n-1)^{2}m-\binom{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(m-1)\biggr(n(m-1)-m\biggr)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(m-u)\biggr(mn-u(n-1)-m-1\biggr)

\displaystyle l_{t}(\lambda;Id)_{v}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{m}(i-1)\lambda_{i}+(m-1)\sum_{i=m+1}^{m+v}\lambda_{i}+m\sum_{i=m+v+1}^{m+n-1}\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^{v}(i+m-1)\lambda_{m+i(n-1)+j}+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\sum_{j=v+1}^{n-1}(i+m)\lambda_{m+i(n-1)+j}

\displaystyle+\frac{m}{12}\left(8\,{m}^{2}-3\,m+1\right){n}^{2}-m\left(m+1\right)\left(10\,m+6\,v-1\right)n

\displaystyle+2\,m\left(3\,{v}^{2}+3\,vm+2\,{m}^{2}+6\,v+3\,m+1\right).

Notably, for all
0\leq u\leq m-1
, the constant term (with respect to
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{mn},\ \mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m},\ \nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}
) of the
i
th
coordinate of
r_{s}(\mu,\nu)+\alpha-l_{s}(\lambda;Id)
is
0
, and for all
1\leq v\leq n-2
, the constant term of the
j
th
coordinate of
r_{t}(\mu,\nu)+\beta-l_{t}(\lambda;Id)
is also
0
. In other words both
r_{s}(\mu,\nu)+\alpha-l_{s}(\lambda;Id)
and
r_{t}(\mu,\nu)+\beta-l_{t}(\lambda;Id)
are linear forms whose variables are the parts of
\lambda,\mu,\nu
(and thus so is the vector partition function input
\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;Id)
).

The expression in Eq. (
9
) writes the Kronecker coefficient
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
as a signed sum of permutations. The single term associated with the identity permutation is the largest, and can be used to derive properties about the Kronecker coefficient. Specifically, for partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
with
\ell(\mu)\leq m,\ \ell(\nu)\leq n\ \ell(\lambda)\leq mn
, the
atomic Kronecker coefficient

\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is the coefficient obtained by taking only the contribution of the alternant term corresponding to the identity permutation in Eq. (
9
) - that is,

(10)

\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}:=p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;Id)\biggr).

Atomic Kronecker coefficients were introduced in
[
21
]
, where it was proven that in the
m=n=2
case they provide an upper bound for the Kronecker coefficients. These authors also conjecture that they provide an upper bound in general
[
20
]
. This seems to be justified in each computation we have made (in the
m=2,n=3,4
cases). Interestingly, the atomic Kronecker coefficients depend on the values
m,n
and not just the indexing partitions. As an example (given also in
[
21
]
), consider
\lambda=(12,7,4,1),\mu=(12,12),\nu=(12,12)
. If we set,
m=n=2
, the atomic Kronecker coefficient
\tilde{g}^{2,2}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is
32
- however, by by padding
\lambda
and
\nu
with zeroes (i.e. representing
\lambda,\nu
as
\lambda=(12,7,4,1,0,0),\nu=(12,12,0)
), we find that the atomic Kronecker coefficient
\tilde{g}^{2,3}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
in this case is
8793
. The atomic Kronecker coefficients are expressed using a single partition function, which is polynomial time computable for a fixed dimension. However, the dimension grows very quickly as a function of
m,n
.

1.3.
Summary of contribution

In this work we apply Theorem
1.1
to study some of the main questions of Kronecker coefficients: exact computation, vanishing conditions, stability, and upper bounds. In
[
21
]
the authors focused on the
m=n=2
case; we adapt the main ideas of that article to general
m,n
. Section
2
describes the pertinent aspects of their work to this article.

Once an expression of the vector partition function
p_{A^{m,n}}
as a piecewise quasi-polynomial has been computed, the complexity of using this form to determine the Kronecker coefficient comes from the large number
(mn)!
of terms in the sum. Significantly fewer than the
(mn)!
terms are needed (either due to vanishing or cancellation): when
m=n=2
only
7
of the
24
terms are needed, and when
m=2,n=3
at most
482
are needed. However, we do not know how many terms are needed in general for a given
m,n
.

Using this to compute
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is efficient for small
m
and
n
, and we have developed a
Sagemath
tool for computing Kronecker coefficients
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
for
l\leq 8,m\leq 2,n\leq 4
. The exact formulas are given in Section
3
. This section is split into two subsections - in
3.1
we describe the more restricted case
\ell(\mu)\leq 2,\ell(\nu)\leq 3,\ell(\lambda)\leq 6
, and in
3.2
we describe the general case.

In Section
4
, we show vanishing conditions (conditions on
\lambda,\mu,\nu
ensuring that the coefficient in question is
0
) on the atomic Kronecker coefficient give vanishing conditions for the Kronecker coefficients. We subsequently deduce explicit conditions. These are given in Theorem
4.5
. For each
m,n
we obtain a set of
m+n-2
conditions for partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
with
\ell(\mu)\leq m,\ \ell(\nu)\leq n,\ \ell(\lambda)\leq mn
. Our conditions have the advantage of being easy to compute and implement practically.

By considering the set of partition triples
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
satisfying the equation

(11)

\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;Id)=\mathbf{0}

we obtain a stable face of the Kronecker cone
PKron_{m,n,mn}
. Additionally, each
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
satisfying the above equation has the property that
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=1
, and moreover the partition triple is stable (Theorem
5.4
). Eq. (
11
) is natural to consider from the point of view of the expression for the Kronecker coefficient given in Eq. (
9
). In this case, the contribution of the alternant term associated to the identity permutation is
1
, and the contribution of all other alternant terms is
0
(and so the atomic Kronecker coefficient and Kronecker coefficient are both equal to
1
). These results are described in Section
5
.

The atomic Kronecker coefficient can be bounded from above using binomial coefficients (Theorem
6.5
). By bounding each of the terms of Eq. (
9
) we are able to obtain upper bounds for the Kronecker coefficients which seem to be best known in certain cases. This is described in Section
6
, and the main results are Corollaries
6.8
and
6.6
.

Finally, in Section
7
we summarize some open questions.

2.
Vector partition functions and Kronecker coefficients

The central formula, Eq. (
9
), was developed by Mishna, Rosas and Sundaram
[
21
]
. It is deduced from the formula using Schur polynomials, determinant formulas for Schur polynomials and, a variable substitution. We reproduce some of the details here to establish notation.

2.1.
From Schur polynomials to vector partition generating functions

We recall the
staircase partition

\delta^{(k)}=(k-1,k-2,\dots,1,0)
. For
\lambda
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq k
, the
alternant

a_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
is the anti-symmetric polynomial

(12)

a_{\lambda}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\det(x_{i}^{\lambda_{j}})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}.

An expression for the Kronecker coefficients involving alternants is

(13)

\frac{a_{\delta_{n}[X]}a_{\delta_{n}[Y]}}{a_{\delta_{mn}[XY]}}a_{\lambda+\delta_{mn}}[XY]=\sum_{\mu,\nu}g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}S(a_{\mu+\delta_{m}}[X])S(a_{\nu+\delta_{n}}[Y]),

where
X=(1,x_{1},\dots,x_{m-1}),Y=(1,y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}),XY=(1,x_{1},\dots,x_{m-1},y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1},x_{1}y_{1},x_{1}y_{2},\dots,x_{m-1}y_{n-1})
, and

(14)

S(a_{\alpha}(z_{1},\dots,z_{k}))=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{z_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}},

for a partition
\alpha
of length at most
k
.

The ratio of alternants
\frac{a_{\delta_{m}}[X]a_{\delta_{m}}[Y]}{a_{\delta_{mn}}[XY]}

simplifies to the rational function

(15)

\frac{a_{\delta_{m}}[X]a_{\delta_{n}}[Y]}{a_{\delta_{mn}}[XY]}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{F}}

with the following polynomials:

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x_{i}-y_{j})

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-x_{i}y_{j})

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

4.
Vanishing conditions

A key to our analysis is a dominance property of vector partition functions. We use this property to prove Theorem
4.5
, a generalization of some non-vanishing conditions for the Kronecker coefficients given in
[
5
]
. Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We say that
\mathbf{u}

dominates

\mathbf{v}
if
\mathbf{u}_{i}\geq\mathbf{v}_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq k
, and we denote this by
\mathbf{u}\succeq\mathbf{v}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers, if
\mathbf{a}\succeq\mathbf{b}
, then
p_{A^{m,n}}(\mathbf{a})\geq p_{A^{m,n}}(\mathbf{b})

Proof.

Each of the standard basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{m+n-2}
is a column of
A^{m,n}
. Without loss of generality assume that columns
1,\dots,m+n-2
are the standard basis vectors
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{m+n-2}
in the same order. It follows that any vector partition
\mathbf{x}\in S_{A^{m,n}}(\mathbf{b})
can be mapped to a unique vector partition
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}\in S_{A^{m,n}}(\mathbf{a})
by taking
\mathbf{x^{\prime}}_{i}:=\mathbf{x}_{i}+(a_{i}-b_{i})\mathbf{e}_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq m+n-2
. This forms an injective map from
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbf{b})
to
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbf{a})
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
m,n
be positive integers. Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathfrak{S}_{mn}
such that

(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma_{1}),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma_{1}))\succeq(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma_{2}),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma_{2}))

for all partitions
\lambda
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq mn
. Then

p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma_{1})\biggr)\leq p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma_{2})\biggr)

for all partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq mn,\ell(\mu)\leq m,\ell(\nu)\leq n
.

Proof.

Multiplication by
-1
reverses domination. The domination of one vector over another is preserved if we subtract the same vector from both sides, and if we add a positive vector to the larger one. Thus, for any partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq mn,\ell(\mu)\leq m,\ell(\nu)\leq n
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle=\biggr(r_{s}(\mu,\nu),r_{t}(\mu,\nu))+(\alpha,\beta)-(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma_{2}),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma_{2})\biggr)

\displaystyle\succeq\,\biggr(r_{s}(\mu,\nu),r_{t}(\mu,\nu))+(\alpha,\beta)-(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma_{1}),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma_{1})\biggr)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma_{1}).

Then by Lemma
4.1
we have that
p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma_{2})\biggr)\geq p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma_{1})\biggr)
as required.
∎

The previous lemma induces a poset structure on
\mathfrak{S}_{mn}
via the relation
\sigma_{2}\geq\sigma_{1}
if and only if

(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma_{1}),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma_{1}))\succeq(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma_{2}),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma_{2}))

for all partitions
\lambda
with
\ell(\lambda)\leq mn
. Figure

The poset in the figure is the dependency digraph for the monomials in

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{mn}
and partitions

of length at most

(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma))\succeq(l_{s}(\lambda;Id),l_{t}(\lambda;Id)).

Proof.

The alternant

is the determinant of the matrix
(z_{i}^{\lambda_{j}})_{1\leq i,j\leq mn}

(l_{s}(\lambda;\sigma),l_{t}(\lambda;\sigma))_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{mn}\left(\lambda_{i}+mn-i\right)\deg_{u}(z_{\sigma^{-1}(i)})

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be partitions with

Proof.

If
\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=0

\displaystyle 0=\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;Id\biggr)\quad\text{by Eq.~\eqref{eq:atomic}},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle p_{A^{m,n}}\biggr(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma\biggr)\quad\text{by Lemmas~\ref{lem:poset-structure} and \ref{lem:domination}},

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0,

Proposition 5.1
.

If
\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;Id)=\mathbf{0}

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}=1.

Proof.

When

\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;\sigma)
has at least one negative coordinate for each
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{mn},\ \sigma\neq Id

(37)

\displaystyle g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}

(38)

\displaystyle=p_{A^{m,n}}(\mathbf{b}^{m,n}(\lambda,\mu,\nu;Id))

(39)

.

6.
Upper bounds for Kronecker coefficients

The atomic Kronecker coefficients are given by a single vector partition function evaluation
p_{A^{m,n}}(\mathbf{b})
. By constructing a companion matrix to
A^{m,n}
, we are able to obtain a simpler vector partition function for which the evaluations can be computed by hand and whose evaluations bound
p_{A^{m,n}}
from above. By bounding each of the terms of Eq.(
9
), we are then able to obtain upper bounds for the Kronecker coefficients.

6.1.
A bound in terms of atomic Kronecker coefficients

In
[
21
]
, Mishna, Rosas and Sundaram show that in the
m=n=2
case, the atomic Kronecker coefficient
\tilde{g}^{2,2}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
bounds the corresponding Kronecker coefficient
g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
from above, and in
[
20
]
they conjecture that this is the case in general. Since we do know that the atomic Kronecker coefficient is the largest term in the sum, we can use this to give a general weaker bound.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be partitions with
\ell(\mu)\leq m,\ell(\nu)\leq n,\ell(\lambda)\leq ln
. Then

(44)

g_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\leq\frac{(mn)!}{2}\tilde{g}^{m,n}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}.

Proof.

Splitting the sum in Eq. (
9
) in two, one for the permutations with positive sign, and one for the permutations with negative sign, we bound each of the negative sign terms above by
0
and each of the positive terms by the atomic term (by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
).
∎

6.2.
Estimating atomic Kronecker Coefficients

We can approximate the partition function of a matrix
A
by replacing its columns with standard basis vectors so that the rank is preserved. Partition functions of such matrices are easy to write using binomial coefficients. Lemma
6.3
describes the replacement process and Proposition
6.4
is the resulting bound. The following proposition sets up Lemma
6.3
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
A
be a
d\times n
matrix with integer entries and
\ker(A)\cap\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geq 0}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. Let
\mathbf{c}
be a column of
A
, and let
\mathbf{c^{\prime}}
be a
1\times n
vector. Let
A^{\prime}
be the matrix obtained by replacing column
\mathbf{c}
with
\mathbf{c^{\prime}}
. If
p_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbf{c})\geq p_{A}(\mathbf{c})
, then

p_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbf{b})\geq p_{A}(\mathbf{b})

for all
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
j
be the index at which column
\mathbf{c}
appears in A (and thus column
\mathbf{c^{\prime}}
appears in
A^{\prime}
).
Partition the set of vector partitions
\mathcal{P}_{A}(\mathbf{b})
of
\mathbf{b}
into
U_{1}:=\{\mathbf{x}:A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b},x_{j}=0\}
and
U_{2}:=\{\mathbf{x}:A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b},x_{j}>0\}
. The set
U_{1}
is equal to the set
\{x:A^{\prime}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b},x_{j}=0\}
. Also
\mathbf{c}\in\{A^{\prime}\mathbf{x}:x_{j}>0\}
since
p_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbf{c})\geq p_{A}(\mathbf{c})
, and so
|U_{2}|\leq|\{x:A^{\prime}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b},x_{j}>0\}|
. Thus
p_{A}(\mathbf{b})=|U_{1}|+|U_{2}|\leq p_{A^{\prime}}(\mathbf{b})
as required.
∎

The following Lemma describes how to replace columns of
A^{m,n}
with standard basis vectors via the previous proposition.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
A
be a
d\times n
matrix with non-negative integer entries and each standard basis vector
\mathbf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{d}
appearing as a column of
A
. Let
\mathbf{c}
be a column of
A
, and let
I=\{k:c_{k}>0,1\leq k\leq n\}
be the set of non-zero coordinates of
\mathbf{c}
. Let
E^{(i)}
denote the matrix obtained by replacing column
\mathbf{c}
with
\mathbf{e}_{i}
for some
i\in I
. Then

p_{E^{(i)}}(\mathbf{b})\geq p_{A}(\mathbf{b})

for all
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
E
be a
d\times n
matrix such that the columns of
E
are formed by taking
i_{j}
copies of each standard basis vector
\mathbf{e}_{i}
where
i_{1},\dots,i_{d}\geq 0
. Then

p_{A}(\mathbf{b})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\binom{b_{i}+i_{j}-1}{i_{j}-1}}.

Proof.

For each component
i
we must take a total of
b_{i}
copies of the standard basis vector
\mathbf{e}_{i}
. We can think of this problem as distributing
b_{i}
balls to the
i_{j}
different columns of
A
which are the copies of
\mathbf{e}_{i}
. This is counted by the
i
th
term in the given product of binomial coefficients.
∎

By application of Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
6.4
we obtain binomial coefficient bounds for the atomic Kronecker coefficients, and thus the Kronecker coefficients as well. The technical details of the proof appear in Appendix
A
where we work out explicitly which columns have which non-zero coordinates. Note that there are many choices of column replacements that can be made, and different choices provide better bounds for certain choices of

7.
Conclusion and open problems

The partition function approach to Kronecker functions is elementary, yet provides a useful structure to calculate values and upper bounds. The notion of the atomic Kronecker Coefficient is very useful to determine vanishing conditions, bounds, and also to generate stable triples. We summarize a few open problems.

Introduction

Stochastic Partial Differential Equations (SPDEs) have gathered significant attention in recent years, due to their broad applications across diverse fields such as applied mathematics, theoretical physics, polymer science, and mathematical finance. For a comprehensive introduction in this domain, see
[
19
]
. Meanwhile, fractional SPDEs driven by homogeneous noise have also emerged as a vibrant area of research, attracting substantial interest due to their rich mathematical structure and wide-ranging applicability. See for example
[
15
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we estimate small ball probabilities for the solution to the fractional stochastic heat equation of the type:

\partial_{t}u(t,\textbf{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u(t,\textbf{x})+\sigma(t,\textbf{x},u)\dot{F}(t,\textbf{x}),\quad\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(1)

with given initial deterministic profile
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
where
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{d}
is a
d
-dimensional torus. The operator
-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
, where
1<\alpha\leq 2
, is the fractional power Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. The centered Gaussian noise
\dot{F}
is white in time and homogeneous in space, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}\left[\dot{F}(t,\textbf{x})\dot{F}(s,\textbf{y})\right]=\delta_{0}(t-s)\Lambda(\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}),

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta generalized function and
\Lambda:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a non-negative generalized function whose Fourier series is given by

\Lambda(\textbf{x})=\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda(\textbf{n})\exp(2\pi i\textbf{n}\cdot\textbf{x}),

(2)

where
\textbf{n}\cdot\textbf{x}
represents the dot product of two
d
-dimensional vectors.

We are working on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, so that the expression
\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}
should be interpreted as the unique point
z
in
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{d}
such that
\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}=\textbf{z}+\textbf{n}
for some
\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Moreover, we will need the following two assumptions on the function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.

{hypothesis}

There exists a constant
\mathcal{D}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
,

|\sigma(t,\textbf{x},u)-\sigma(t,\textbf{x},v)|\leq\mathcal{D}|u-v|.

(3)

{hypothesis}

There exist constants
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
\mathcal{C}_{2}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\sigma(t,\textbf{x},u)\leq\mathcal{C}_{2}.

(4)

Assumption (
4
) assures that
\sigma
is non-zero, which is helpful for us to control the variance of noise terms. We say that a process
\{u(t,\textbf{x})\}_{t\geq 0,\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}
on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
is a
mild solution
to (
1
) in the sense of Walsh
[
34
]
if it is square integrable, adapted with respect to the filtration induced by
F
and satisfies:

u(t,\textbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})u_{0}(\textbf{y})d\textbf{y}+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})\sigma(s,\textbf{y},u(s,\textbf{y}))F(dsd\textbf{y}),

(5)

where
\bar{p}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the fundamental solution of the fractional heat equation on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x})=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x}),\\
\bar{p}(0,\textbf{x})=\delta_{0}(\textbf{x}).\end{cases}

(6)

We give more information about the heat kernel in Section
4
. Under the Lipschitz assumption (
3
) and given an initial profile
u_{0}(\textbf{x})=0
, it is well known (see
[
12
]
and Appendix of
[
25
]
) that if the Fourier coefficients of
\Lambda(\textbf{x})
satisfy

\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)(\textbf{n})}{1+|\textbf{n}|^{\alpha}}<\infty,

(7)

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm, then there exists a unique random field solution
u(t,\textbf{x})
to equation (
5
). Examples of spatial correlation function satisfying (
7
) are:

1.

The spatial covariance function that is an analogue of the Riesz kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is specified in
[
10
]
. Moreover,
[
8
]
, Lemma 2.9, proved that there exists a constant
C(d,\beta)>0
such that the covariance function admits the following estimate,

\Lambda(\textbf{x})\leq C(d,\beta)|\textbf{x}|^{-\beta},

(8)

when
0<\beta<d
. There are two positive constants
c_{1}(d)
and
c_{2}(d)
such that the Fourier coefficients for
\Lambda
are given by

\lambda(\textbf{n})=\begin{cases}c_{1}(d)&\text{if~$\textbf{n}=\textbf{0}$};\\
c_{2}(d)|\textbf{n}|^{-(d-\beta)}&\text{if~$\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\textbf{0}\}$},\end{cases}

(9)

and it is easy to check that condition (
7
) holds whenever
\beta<\alpha
.

2.

Space-time white noise
\Lambda(\textbf{x})=\delta_{0}(\textbf{x})
. In this case,
\lambda(\textbf{n})
is a constant and (
7
) is only satisfied when
\alpha>d
, that is,
d=1
and
1<\alpha\leq 2
.

We refer readers to
[
15
]
for more examples.

Small ball problems have been well studied and one can see
[
24
]
for an overview of known results on Gaussian processes and references on other processes. In summary, we investigate the behavior of the probability that a stochastic process
X_{t}
starting at the origin stays near its initial location for a long time, i.e.,

\log P\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|<\varepsilon\right)

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. When
X_{t}
is Brownian motion, small ball estimates are obtained from the reflection principle or the study of eigenvalues, among other techniques, resulting in a sharp approximation, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0

\log P\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|X_{t}|<\varepsilon\right)\sim-\frac{\pi^{2}T}{8\varepsilon^{2}}.

Often these results are given in terms of metric entropy estimates
[
21
]
, which are hard to explicitly compute. However, there are a few Gaussian measures for which small ball probabilities can be completely determined. One exceptional case is Brownian sheet, for which fairly sharp small ball estimates are explicitly known, see
[
5
]
and
[
32
]
.
[
20
]
studied small ball estimates for the integrated Brownian motion using local time techniques. In
[
13
]
, the authors provided small ball estimates for Gaussian processes that satisfy a certain condition which is related to the Gaussian concept of local nondeterminism.

However, there have not been many explorations of small ball probabilities in the context of SPDEs.
[
26
]
has studied small ball problems for a linear SPDE with additive white noise, where the solution is a Gaussian process.
[
27
]
followed the approach of
[
32
]
to study a stochastic wave equation

\partial^{2}_{t}u(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x}u(t,x)+f(u)+g(t,x)\dot{W}(t,x),

(10)

where
\dot{W}(t,x)
is a space-time white noise and
g(t,x)
is a deterministic function. Without
f(u)
, the solution
u
would be a Gaussian process of the type studied by
[
5
]
and
[
32
]
.

Although the stochastic wave equation (
10
) includes a multiplicative noise term, in our case, the noise coefficient
\sigma
depends on the solution
u
. This dependency causes
u
to deviate from being Gaussian. The study of small ball probabilities for equations of this type has seen notable developments:
[
3
]
analyzed the stochastic heat equation driven by space-time white noise;
[
14
]
extended this work by considering small ball probabilities under a Hölder semi-norm; and
[
17
]
derived results under the assumption that the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
is uniformly elliptic but only Hölder continuous in
u
. All these works were restricted to the one-dimensional torus due to the limitations imposed by space-time white noise.

Recently,
[
9
]
adapted the approach of
[
3
]
, incorporating the foundational framework from
[
12
]
, to investigate small ball probabilities for the stochastic heat equation driven by spatially homogeneous noise on the one-dimensional torus. The analysis utilized the Da Prato-Kwapien-Zapczyk factorization method to estimate fluctuations in the noise term and introduced a distinct time step size compared to
[
3
]
. The primary aim of the present work is to generalize these results to the fractional stochastic heat equation driven by spatially homogeneous noise on the
d
-dimensional torus.

Remark 2

(a)

When

(b)

If

We outline the key steps in the proof of Theorem
1
. In
[
3
]
, the authors decomposed a one-dimensional spatial interval into small subintervals. Extending this approach to the
d
-dimensional setting, we instead partition the domain into a sequence of small boxes.

Concerning the temporal coefficient
c_{0}
introduced in
[
3
]
, it was shown there to be uniformly bounded, leveraging the exponential decay of spatial correlations as the distance between two points increases. However, for spatially homogeneous noise, the decay of correlation is significantly slower. As a result, a uniform bound on
c_{0}
is no longer feasible. Instead, we control
c_{0}
in terms of
\varepsilon
, as specified in (
11
).

To establish the upper bound in Theorem
1
, we employ a perturbation argument that approximates the solution
u
by a Gaussian random field within small regions. This is complemented by deriving bounds for a corresponding Gaussian process. For the lower bound, we utilize the Gaussian correlation inequality along with a change-of-measure technique inspired by
[
3
]
. Additionally, we demonstrate that the approximation error can be effectively controlled by appropriately choosing the time intervals over which the coefficients are frozen. A critical component of our analysis is the Markov property of (
1
) with respect to the time variable
t
. This property allows us to reduce the problem to studying the behavior of the non-Gaussian solution over small time intervals.

4.1
Heat Kernel Estimates

For
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\alpha=2
, yet it is a smooth function determined by its Fourier transform
\hat{p}(t,\nu)
in
x
, i.e.,

\hat{p}(t,\nu):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,\textbf{x})\exp(-2\pi i\nu\cdot\textbf{x})d\textbf{x}=\exp\left(-t(2\pi|\nu|)^{\alpha}\right),\quad\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, from the standard Fourier decomposition, we use
\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x})
to denote the fundamental solution on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
in (
6
) as

\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x})=\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\left(-(2\pi|\textbf{n}|)^{\alpha}t\right)\exp(2\pi i\textbf{n}\cdot\textbf{x}),

(19)

and the summation converges in
L^{2}
sense.

The following lemma gives two estimates on
\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x})
, which are similar to Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
in
[
23
]
.

Lemma 5
.

For all
t\geq s>0
and
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, there exist constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
depending entirely on
\alpha,d
such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,\textbf{y}-\textbf{x})-\bar{p}(t,\textbf{y})|d\textbf{y}\leq\frac{C|\textbf{x}|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\wedge 2,

(20)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,\textbf{x})-\bar{p}(s,\textbf{x})|d\textbf{x}\leq C^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{t}{s}\right)\wedge 2.

(21)

Proof.

We begin with inequality (
20
),

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,{\textbf{y}-\textbf{x}})-\bar{p}(t,\textbf{y})|d\textbf{y}&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left[p(t,\textbf{y}-\textbf{x}+\textbf{n})-p(t,\textbf{y}+\textbf{n})\right]\right|d\textbf{y}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|p(t,\textbf{y}-\textbf{x}+\textbf{n})-p(t,\textbf{y}+\textbf{n})\right|d\textbf{y}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p(t,\textbf{y}-\textbf{x})-p(t,\textbf{y})|d\textbf{y}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\textbf{x}|\cdot\sup_{c\in[0,1]}\left|\nabla_{\textbf{y}}p(t,\textbf{y}-c\textbf{x})\right|d\textbf{y},\end{split}

(22)

where we use the Mean Value Theorem for the last inequality.

Using the inequality from Lemma 3 and Lemma 5 in
[
7
]
along with equation
(2.3)
from
[
18
]
, we obtain the following bound:

\left|\nabla_{\textbf{z}}p(t,\textbf{z})\right|\leq C(d,\alpha)|\textbf{z}|\left(\frac{t}{|\textbf{z}|^{d+2+\alpha}}\wedge t^{-(d+2)/\alpha}\right)\leq C(d,\alpha)\frac{t|\textbf{z}|}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|\textbf{z}|)^{d+2+\alpha}}.

(23)

Plugging (
23
) in (
22
) yields

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,{\textbf{y}-\textbf{x}})-\bar{p}(t,\textbf{y})|d\textbf{y}&\leq C(d,\alpha)|\textbf{x}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{t|\textbf{y}|}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|\textbf{y}|)^{d+2+\alpha}}d\textbf{y}\\
&=C(d,\alpha)|\textbf{x}|\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{tx}{(t^{1/\alpha}+x)^{d+2+\alpha}}x^{d-1}dx\\
&=\frac{C(d,\alpha)|\textbf{x}|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{d}}{(1+w)^{d+2+\alpha}}dw\\
&=\frac{C(d,\alpha)|\textbf{x}|}{t^{1/\alpha}}.\end{split}

Clearly,
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,{\textbf{y}-\textbf{x}})-\bar{p}(t,\textbf{y})|d\textbf{y}\leq 2
, so that (
20
) follows.

For inequality (
21
), we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,{\textbf{x}})-\bar{p}(s,\textbf{x})|d\textbf{x}&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left[p(t,\textbf{x}+\textbf{n})-p(s,\textbf{x}+\textbf{n})\right]\right|d\textbf{x}\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|p(t,\textbf{x}+\textbf{n})-p(s,\textbf{x}+\textbf{n})\right|d\textbf{x}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p(t,\textbf{x})-p(s,\textbf{x})|d\textbf{x}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}|\partial_{r}p(r,\textbf{x})|drd\textbf{x}.\end{split}

(24)

Proposition
2.1
in
[
33
]
provides an estimate for the kernel
A:=(-\Delta)^{\sigma/2}p
in terms of the
\sigma
-parabolic ”distance”
|Y|_{\sigma}:=\left(|\textbf{x}|^{2}+|t|^{2/\sigma}\right)^{1/2}
. Specifically, it establishes that

|(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}p(r,\textbf{x})|=|A(r,\textbf{x})|\leq\frac{C(d,\alpha)}{(r^{2/\alpha}+|\textbf{x}|^{2})^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}},

(25)

where
C(d,\alpha)
is a constant depending on the dimension
d
and and parameter
\alpha
. Hence, applying (
6
), (
25
) and Fubini’s theorem to (
24
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,{\textbf{x}})-\bar{p}(s,\textbf{x})|d\textbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}|\partial_{r}p(r,\textbf{x})|drd\textbf{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}|(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}p(r,\textbf{x})|drd\textbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{(r^{2/\alpha}+|\textbf{x}|^{2})^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}drd\textbf{x}

\displaystyle=C(d,\alpha)\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{(r^{2/\alpha}+|\textbf{x}|^{2})^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}d\textbf{x}dr

\displaystyle=C(d,\alpha)\int_{s}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{d-1}}{(r^{2/\alpha}+x^{2})^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}dxdr

\displaystyle=C(d,\alpha)\int_{s}^{t}\frac{dr}{r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{d-1}}{(1+w^{2})^{\frac{d+\alpha}{2}}}dw

\displaystyle=C(d,\alpha)\left(\log(t)-\log(s)\right).

Similarly,
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\bar{p}(t,{\textbf{x}})-\bar{p}(s,\textbf{x})|d\textbf{x}\leq 2
, so that (
21
) follows.
∎

4.2
Noise Term Estimates

We denote the second integral of (
5
), i.e., the noise term, by

N(t,\textbf{x}):=\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})\sigma(s,\textbf{y},u(s,\textbf{y}))F(dsd\textbf{y})

(26)

whose regularities will be estimated in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 6
.

There exists a constant
C(d,\alpha,\beta)>0
independent of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (
4
) such that for any
\xi\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\alpha}\wedge\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\right)
,
t\in[0,1]
and
\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y}))^{2}\right]\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}.

Proof.

To simplify our computations, we fix
t,\textbf{x}
and
y
, and define, for
0<s<t
,

K_{s}(\textbf{z}):=\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})-\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{y}-\textbf{z}).

By a similar argument as on page 2871 of
[
6
]
, we use Burkholder’s inequality, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and take the absolute value inside the integral and get, for the case
k=2
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y}))^{2}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}K_{s}(\textbf{z})\sigma(s,\textbf{z},u(s,\textbf{z}))F(dsd\textbf{z})\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\leq C\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}K_{s}(\textbf{z})K_{s}(\textbf{w})\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})\sigma(s,\textbf{z},u(s,\textbf{z}))\sigma(s,\textbf{w},u(s,\textbf{w}))d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}ds\right|\\
&\leq C\sup_{r,\textbf{u}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(r,\textbf{u},u(r,\textbf{u}))^{2}\right]\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|K_{s}(\textbf{z})|\cdot|K_{s}(\textbf{w})|\cdot\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}ds\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|K_{s}(\textbf{z})|\cdot[\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})+\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{y}-\textbf{w})]\Lambda(\textbf{w-z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}ds.\end{split}

(27)

Then we apply the standard Fourier decomposition (
19
) and (
9
) to estimate the spatial convolution,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}&=\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda(\textbf{n})\exp(-(2\pi|\textbf{n}|)^{\alpha}(t-s))\exp(2\pi i\textbf{n}\cdot(\textbf{x}-\textbf{z}))\\
&\leq\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda(\textbf{n})\exp(-(2\pi|\textbf{n}|)^{\alpha}(t-s))\\
&\leq C(d,\beta)\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\textbf{n}|^{-d+\beta}\exp(-(2\pi|\textbf{n}|)^{\alpha}(t-s))\\
&\leq C(d,\beta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\textbf{x}|^{-d+\beta}\exp(-(2\pi|\textbf{x}|)^{\alpha}(t-s))d\textbf{x}\\
&\leq C(d,\beta)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-d+\beta}\exp(-(2\pi x)^{\alpha}(t-s))x^{d-1}dx\\
&=C(d,\alpha,\beta)(t-s)^{-\beta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-1}\exp(-w)dw\\
&=C(d,\alpha,\beta)(t-s)^{-\beta/\alpha},\end{split}

(28)

where
\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-1}\exp(-w)dw
is the Gamma function valued at
\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
. Similarly, we get a bound for
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-s,\textbf{y}-\textbf{w})\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}
as well.

Plugging (
28
) and Lemma
5
in (
27
), and since
2\wedge Cx\leq\max\{2,C\}x^{\alpha\xi}
for all
x>0,\xi\in(0,1/\alpha)
, we derive

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y}))^{2}\right]&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|K_{s}(\textbf{z})|(t-s)^{-\beta/\alpha}d\textbf{z}ds\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\beta/\alpha}\left(\frac{C|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|}{(t-s)^{1/\alpha}}\wedge 2\right)ds\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\xi-\beta/\alpha}ds\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}.\end{split}

Note that the integral
\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\xi-\beta/\alpha}ds
converges when
\xi<\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

There exists a constant
C(d,\alpha,\beta)>0
independent of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (
4
) such that for any
\zeta\in\left(0,\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\right)
,
1\geq t\geq s>0
and
\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x}))^{2}\right]\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{\zeta}.

Proof.

As in Lemma
6
, we fix
s
,
t
and
x
, and define, for
0<r<s<t
,

L_{r}(\textbf{z})=\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})-\bar{p}(s-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z}).

Following the fact that the Gaussian noise
\dot{F}(t,\textbf{x})
is independent on disjoint time intervals, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}&[(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x}))^{2}]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L_{r}(\textbf{z})\sigma(r,\textbf{z},u(r,\textbf{z}))F(d\textbf{z}dr)\right.\right.\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\left.\left.+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})\sigma(r,\textbf{z},u(r,\textbf{z}))F(d\textbf{z}dr)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L_{r}(\textbf{z})\sigma(r,\textbf{z},u(r,\textbf{z}))F(d\textbf{z}dr)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})\sigma(r,\textbf{z},u(r,\textbf{z}))F(d\textbf{z}dr)\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

(29)

We study
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
separately. For
I_{1}
, as in (
27
), we get

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C\sup_{r,\textbf{u}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(r,\textbf{u},u(r,\textbf{u}))^{2}\right]\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|L_{r}(\textbf{z})|\cdot|L_{r}(\textbf{w})|\cdot\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}dr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|L_{r}(\textbf{z})|\cdot[\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})+\bar{p}(s-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})]\Lambda(\textbf{w-z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}dr.\end{split}

(30)

Applying (
28
) and Lemma
5
to (
30
), and since
2\wedge C^{\prime}\log(1+x)\leq\max\{2,C^{\prime}\}x^{\zeta}
for all
x>0,\zeta\in(0,1)
, we derive

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\left(C^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{t-r}{s-r}\right)\wedge 2\right)\cdot\left[(t-r)^{-\beta/\alpha}+(s-r)^{-\beta/\alpha}\right]dr\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\left(C^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{t-r}{s-r}\right)\wedge 2\right)\cdot\left[(s-r)^{-\beta/\alpha}+(s-r)^{-\beta/\alpha}\right]dr\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{s}\left(C^{\prime}\log\left(\frac{t-s+u}{u}\right)\wedge 2\right)u^{-\beta/\alpha}du\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}\int_{0}^{s}u^{-\beta/\alpha-\zeta}du\\
&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}.\end{split}

(31)

Note that the integral
\int_{0}^{s}u^{-\beta/\alpha-\zeta}du
converges when
\zeta<\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}
.

For
I_{2}
, as in (
27
), we get

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C\sup_{r,\textbf{u}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma(r,\textbf{u},u(r,\textbf{u}))^{2}\right]\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\cdot\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}dr\\
&\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}dr.\end{split}

(32)

As in (
28
), we use the standard Fourier decomposition (
19
) and (
9
) to estimate the spatial convolution in (
32
),

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})&\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{w})\Lambda(\textbf{w}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{w}d\textbf{z}\\
&=\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda(\textbf{n})\exp\left(-2(2\pi|\textbf{n}|)^{\alpha}(t-r)\right)\\
&\leq C(d,\beta)\sum_{\textbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\textbf{n}|^{-d+\beta}\exp\left(-2(2\pi|\textbf{n}|)^{\alpha}(t-r)\right)\\
&\leq C(d,\beta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\textbf{x}|^{-d+\beta}\exp(-2(2\pi|\textbf{x}|)^{\alpha}(t-r))d\textbf{x}\\
&\leq C(d,\beta)\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-d+\beta}\exp(-2(2\pi x)^{\alpha}(t-r))x^{d-1}dx\\
&=C(d,\alpha,\beta)(t-r)^{-\beta/\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-1}\exp(-w)dw\\
&=C(d,\alpha,\beta)(t-r)^{-\beta/\alpha},\end{split}

(33)

where
\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-1}\exp(-w)dw
is the Gamma function valued at
\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
. Plugging it in (
32
) yields

\begin{split}I_{2}&\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}\int_{s}^{t}(t-r)^{-\beta/\alpha}dr\\
&=C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}}\\
&<C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}.\end{split}

(34)

By (
29
), (
31
) and (
34
), we therefore conclude

\mathbb{E}\left[(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x}))^{2}\right]\leq C(d,\alpha,\beta)\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}.

∎

Lemma 8
.

There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}>0
depending only on
\alpha,\beta,d
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in (
4
) such that for all
0\leq s<t\leq 1
,
\textbf{x},\textbf{y}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
\xi\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\alpha}\wedge\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\right),\zeta\in\left(0,\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\right)
, and
\kappa>0
, we have

P(|N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y})|>\kappa)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}\right),

(35)

P(|N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x})|>\kappa)\leq C_{3}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{4}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|t-s|^{\zeta}}\right).

(36)

Proof.

For a fixed
t
, we define

N_{t}(s,\textbf{x}):=\int_{[0,s]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})\sigma(r,\textbf{y},u(r,\textbf{y}))F(drd\textbf{y}).

Note that
N_{t}(t,\textbf{x})=N(t,\textbf{x})
and
N_{t}(s,\textbf{x})
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{F}
adapted martingale in
s\leq t
since the integrand does not depend on
s
. For fixed
t,\textbf{x}
and
y
, let

M_{s}:=N_{t}(s,\textbf{x})-N_{t}(s,\textbf{y})=\int_{[0,s]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}[\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{z})-\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{y}-\textbf{z})]\sigma(r,\textbf{z},u(r,\textbf{z}))F(drd\textbf{z}),

and it is easy to check that
M_{t}=N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y})
. As
M_{s}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{s}^{F}
adapted martingale with
M_{0}=0
, it is a time changed Brownian motion, i.e.,

M_{t}=B_{\langle M\rangle_{t}},

and Lemma
6
gives a uniform bound as

\langle M\rangle_{t}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}.

Therefore, by the reflection principle for Brownian motion
B_{\langle M\rangle_{t}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle P(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y})>\kappa)

\displaystyle=P(M_{t}>\kappa)=P(B_{\langle M\rangle_{t}}>\kappa)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\sup_{s\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}B_{s}>\kappa\right)=2P\left(B_{C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}>\kappa\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}\right).

Switching
x
and
y
yields

\displaystyle P(-N(t,\textbf{x})+N(t,\textbf{y})>\kappa)

\displaystyle=P(M_{t}<-\kappa)\leq 2P\left(B_{C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}<-\kappa\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}\right).

Consequently, for
\forall\xi\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\alpha}\wedge\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha}\right)
,

P(|N(t,\textbf{x})-N(t,\textbf{y})|>\kappa)\leq C_{1}\exp\left(-\frac{C_{2}\kappa^{2}}{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}|\textbf{x}-\textbf{y}|^{\alpha\xi}}\right),

which completes the proof of (
35
).

For a fixed
x
, we define

U_{q_{1}}:=\int_{[0,{q_{1}}]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}[\bar{p}(t-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})-\bar{p}(s-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})]\sigma(r,\textbf{y},u(r,\textbf{y}))F(drd\textbf{y})

where
0\leq q_{1}\leq s
. Note
U_{q_{1}}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{q_{1}}^{F}
adapted martingale with
U_{0}=0
. In addition, we define

V_{q_{2}}:=\int_{[0,{q_{2}}]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{p}(t-s-r,\textbf{x}-\textbf{y})\sigma(r+s,\textbf{y},u(r+s,\textbf{y}))F(drd\textbf{y})

where
0\leq q_{2}\leq t-s
. Note that
V_{q_{2}}
is a continuous
\mathcal{F}_{q_{2}}^{F}
adapted martingale with
V_{0}=0
. Thus, both
U_{q_{1}}
and
V_{q_{2}}
are time changed Brownian motions, i.e.,

U_{s}=B_{\langle U\rangle_{s}}\text{~and~}V_{t-s}=B^{\prime}_{\langle V\rangle_{t-s}},

where
B
,
B^{\prime}
are two different Brownian motions. It is clear that
N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x})=U_{s}+V_{t-s}
, which leads to

P(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x})>\kappa)\leq P(U_{s}>\kappa/2)+P(V_{t-s}>\kappa/2),

and Lemma
7
provides a uniform bound as

\langle U\rangle_{s}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}\text{~and~}\langle V\rangle_{t-s}\leq C\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}(t-s)^{\zeta}.

By the reflection principle for Brownian motions
B_{\langle U\rangle_{s}}
and
B^{\prime}_{\langle V\rangle_{t-s}}
, we get

\displaystyle P(N(t,\textbf{x})-N(s,\textbf{x})>\kappa)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(B_{\langle U\rangle_{s}}>\kappa/2\right)+P\left(B^{\prime}_{\langle V\rangle_{t-s}}>\kappa/2\right)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(s,\textbf{y})f(s,\textbf{z})\Lambda(\textbf{y}-\textbf{z})d\textbf{z}d\textbf{y}ds=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(s,\textbf{y})\frac{\bar{p}_{s}(u_{0})(\textbf{y})}{t_{1}\sigma(s,\textbf{y})}d\textbf{y}ds\\

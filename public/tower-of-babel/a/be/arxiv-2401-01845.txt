Hille’s theorem for Bochner integrals of functions with values in locally convex spaces
T. J. Sullivan

Abstract
Abstract. Hille’s theorem is a powerful classical result in vector measure theory. It asserts that the application of a closed, unbounded linear operator commutes with strong/Bochner integration of functions taking values in a Banach space. This note shows that Hille’s theorem also holds in the setting of complete locally convex spaces. Keywords. Introduction

The strong or Bochner integral of a function taking values in a Banach space
V
is a long-established mathematical concept
(
Bochner 1933
)
, as are various weaker integrals such as the
V^{\prime\prime}
-valued Dunford integral
(
Dunford 1937
;
Dunford 1938
)
and the
V
-valued weak and Gel
′
fand–Pettis integrals (
Gel′fand 1936
;
Pettis 1938
).
The extension of the weaker integrals to integrands taking values in more general topological vector spaces is relatively straightforward, since those integrals are characterised though the application of continuous linear functionals.
The strong integral can also be generalised to locally convex spaces
V
, as has been done by e.g.
Blondia 1981
.
Integrability by seminorm (
Garnir et al. 1972
;
Blondia 1981
) is intermediate between the strong and weak integrals, although it agrees with the strong/Bochner integral in the Banach case.

One of the key features of the strong/Bochner integral, in the Banach case, is Hille’s theorem
(
Hille and Phillips 1957
, p.83)
, which asserts that integration commutes not merely with bounded/continuous linear operators, but even with
closed
unbounded operators.
This non-trivial fact has no analogue for the weak integral — q.v. standard counterexamples to the principle of differentiation under the integral sign when the domination hypothesis fails
(
Talvila 2001
)
— and is of vital importance in, e.g., understanding the mean and covariance structure of linear images of stochastic processes under differential operators.
This note proves the following Hille-type theorem for the Bochner integral of a function taking values in an LCS, which does not appear to have been explicitly noted in the literature hitherto.

Theorem 1.1

(Hille’s theorem for the Bochner integral in an locally convex space)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a finite measure space and let
V
and
W
be complete locally convex spaces over the same field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
T\colon\dom(T)\subseteq V\to W
be a closed operator defined on a linear subspace
\dom(T)
of
V
and suppose that both
u\colon\Omega\to V
and
Tu\colon\Omega\to W
are Bochner integrable with respect to
\mu
.
Then, for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu\in\dom(T)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{A}Tu\,\mathrm{d}\mu=T\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(H)

3
Hille’s theorem for the Bochner integral in an LCS

It is perhaps worth noting that Hille’s theorem is almost trivial to prove for any simple integrand.
Thus, there would seem to be an obvious approach to proving Hille’s theorem for Bochner-integrable functions:
one would take a sequence
(s_{n})_{n}
of simple functions that approximate
u
in the Bochner sense, reason that
(Ts_{n})_{n}
is a sequence of simple functions that approximates
Tu
, and take the limit as
n\to\infty
of the corresponding integrals.
Unfortunately, almost everything in this approach is wrong:
first, there is no guarantee that
s_{n}
takes values only in
\dom(T)
;
second, even if this obstacle is resolved, there is no guarantee that the simple functions
Ts_{n}
converge to anything, let alone to
Tu
, since
T
is not continuous.

The correct way to proceed is to approximate
u
and
Tu
independently, at least at first, and moreover to work not with a sequence of simple functions that converges a.e. to the approximand, but a sequence/net of
\sigma
-simple functions that converges uniformly on a set of full measure.
This trick is closely related to Egorov’s theorem, which has analogues for metrisable locally convex spaces and LF spaces
(
de María González 1984
, Theorems 4 and 7)
.
However, the following lemma establishes the required result in a different way, exploiting the relationship between essential separability and approximation using
\sigma
-simple functions when the approximand is known a priori to be Borel measurable.

Lemma 3.1

(Essentially uniform
\sigma
-simple approximation of essentially separable functions)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a finite measure space, let
V
be a uniform space, and let
u\colon\Omega\to V
be Borel measurable and essentially separably valued.
Let
\Lambda
be a directed index set for a fundamental system of entourages for
V
, and hence for a neighbourhood basis of any point of
V
.
Then there exists
Z\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mu(Z)=0
and a net
(\phi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
of
\sigma
-simple functions
\phi_{\lambda}\colon\Omega\to V
such that
u=\lim_{\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}
uniformly on
\Omega\setminus Z
.

Remark 3.2
.

Readers unfamiliar with uniform spaces may wish to follow the proof below thinking of the case that
V
is a metric space,
\Lambda=\mathbb{N}
, and
N_{\lambda}[v]
is the metric ball centred on
v
with radius
\nicefrac{{1}}{{\lambda}}
.
In this situation, the approximating net can be taken to be a sequence and the proof concludes with the estimate that
\sup_{\Omega\setminus Z}d(u,\phi_{\lambda})\leqslant\nicefrac{{1}}{{\lambda}}
.

Proof.

Let
\{U_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a fundamental system of entourages for the uniform space
V
, so that the neighbourhoods
N_{\lambda}[v]\coloneqq\{v^{\prime}\in V\mid(v,v^{\prime})\in U_{\lambda}\}
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, form a neighbourhood basis for each point
v\in V
, directed by inclusion in the usual way.

Let
Z\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mu(Z)=0
and
C=\{c_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq V
be such that
u(\Omega\setminus Z)\subseteq\overline{C}
.
For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G_{\lambda,k}\coloneqq N_{\lambda}[c_{k}]\setminus\bigcup_{\ell<k}N_{\lambda}[c_{\ell}]
, so that, for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we have a measurable, countable, pairwise disjoint covering

\biguplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}G_{\lambda,k}\supseteq\overline{C}\supseteq u(\Omega\setminus Z).

Define
\phi_{\lambda}\colon\Omega\to V
to take the value
c_{k}
on the set
u^{-1}(G_{\lambda,k})
, i.e., in the case that
V
is a topological vector space,

\phi_{\lambda}(\omega)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}\mathds{1}_{u^{-1}(G_{\lambda,k})}(\omega).

(Heuristically, to define
\phi_{\lambda}(\omega)
, one considers all the neighbourhoods

that contain
u(\omega)
, and then “rounds”
u(\omega)
to the

with least
k

Clearly, each
\phi_{\lambda}
assumes at most countably many distinct values;
each
\phi_{\lambda}
is measurable since the sets

are measurable and hence, by the Borel measurability of

, each
u^{-1}(G_{\lambda,k})\in\mathcal{F}
.
By construction, for all
\omega\in\Omega\setminus Z
,
\phi_{\lambda}(\omega)\in N_{\lambda}[u(\omega)]
, and so
\lim_{\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}=u
uniformly on
\Omega\setminus Z
, as required.
∎

There is also a straightforward result in the converse direction:

Lemma 3.3

(Essential separability of pointwise sequential limits of
\sigma
-simple functions)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be a finite measure space and let

be a topological space.
If

of

Proof.

Let
Z\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mu(Z)=0
be such that
u(\omega)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{n}(\omega)
for all
\omega\in\Omega\setminus Z
.
Let
C\coloneqq\{\phi_{n}(\omega)\mid n\in\mathbb{N},\omega\in\Omega\}
;
the hypotheses on
\phi_{n}
and the Axiom of Countable Choice imply that

Also, for every
\omega\in\Omega\setminus Z

∎

K\to\infty

Therefore, since
T
is a closed operator,

\displaystyle\int_{A}u_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\in\dom(T)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{A}Tu_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=T\int_{A}u_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

(3.4)

Step 3: Integration of

and

For each

, the triangle inequality (

to

p\left(\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{A}u_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)\leqslant\int_{A}p(u-u_{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leqslant\gamma_{\lambda,p}\mu(A)\to 0;

similarly, for each

\displaystyle q\left(\int_{A}Tu\,\mathrm{d}\mu-T\int_{A}u_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)

\displaystyle=q\left(\int_{A}Tu\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{A}Tu_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)

by (

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{A}q(Tu-Tu_{\lambda})\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant\Gamma_{\lambda,q}\to 0

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu\in\dom(T)\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{A}Tu\,\mathrm{d}\mu=T\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu,

Some readers may prefer to view the steps in the above proof of

4
Integration by seminorm

Integrability by seminorm appears to have been introduced by
Garnir et al. 1972
and then developed further by
Blondia 1981
;
see
Lewis 2022
for a recent survey and further references.
Although essential separability by seminorm, measurability by seminorm, and integrability by seminorm agree with the usual notions of essential separability, strong measurability, and strong integrability when
V
is a Banach space, they do differ in general, and it is instructive to briefly review the concepts and see how the proof of Hille’s theorem breaks down in the “by seminorm” setting.

A function
u\colon\Omega\to V
is
integrable by seminorm
if, for each
p\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists a sequence
(s_{n}^{p})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of simple functions
s_{n}^{p}\colon\Omega\to V
such that

(a)

\lim_{n\to\infty}p(s_{n}^{p}-u)=0
pointwise on
\Omega\setminus Z_{p}
, where
\mu(Z_{p})=0
, i.e.
u
is
measurable by seminorm
;

(b)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}p(s_{n}^{p}-u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0
;

and there exists, for each
A\in\mathcal{F}
, some element
\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu\in V
such that, for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}p\left(\int_{A}s_{n}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{A}u\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)=0.

Borel measurability always implies measurability by seminorm;
if the space
V
is metrisable, then measurability by seminorm implies strong measurability and hence Borel measurability
(
Lewis 2022
, Proposition 2.4)
.
There is also a version of Pettis’ measurability theorem for the ‘‘by seminorm’’ setting:
measurability by seminorm is equivalent to weak measurability together with essential separability by seminorm
6
6

6

A function
u\colon\Omega\to V
is
essentially separably valued by seminorm
if, for each
p\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
Z_{p}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mu(Z_{p})=0
and a countable set
C_{p}\subseteq V
such that
u(\Omega\setminus Z_{p})
is contained within the closure of
C_{p}
with respect to the seminorm
p
.
Confusingly,
Beckmann and Deitmar 2015
refer to essential separability by seminorm as essential separability.
The two concepts are not at all equivalent:
both
\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}Z_{p}\subseteq\Omega
and
\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}C_{p}\subseteq V
may be very large sets.

(
Lewis 2022
, Theorem 2.3)
.

If
V
is complete, then integrability by seminorm is equivalent to measurability by seminorm together with integral boundedness (
Blondia 1981
, Theorem 2.10
;
Lewis 2022
, Theorem 2.7
).

Crucially, when defining integrability by seminorm, both the approximating sequence of simple functions
(s_{n}^{p})_{n}
and the exceptional
\mu
-null set
Z_{p}\in\mathcal{F}
on which
p(s_{n}^{p}-u)\not\to 0
may both depend on
p
.
If
V
is not first countable, then it may be the case that
\bigcup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}Z_{p}\notin\mathcal{F}
or that it has positive outer measure.
There is also no guarantee that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{p}(s_{n}^{p}-u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0

for distinct continuous seminorms
p,\widetilde{p}\in\mathcal{P}
.
It is for this reason that the above proof of Hille’s theorem for the strong/Bochner integral fails for integration by seminorm:
the analogue of Step 1 would produce a family of a approximating nets
\bigl(\phi_{\lambda}^{(p,q)},\psi_{\lambda}^{(p,q)}\bigr)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, one for each
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, each converging essentially uniformly to
(u,Tu)
only with respect to those two seminorms
p
and
q
.
Combining all these nets to form a single approximation
u_{\lambda}
, as in (
3.1
), that provides essentially uniform approximation of
u
in
V
(and of
Tu
in
W
), would seem to be challenging if not impossible.
Without this, Step 3 would fail, because one would not know, e.g., that
\bigl(\int_{A}u_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\bigr)_{\lambda}
converges in
V
, and hence there would be no scope to appeal to the closedness of the operator
T
.

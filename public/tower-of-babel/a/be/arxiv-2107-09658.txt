Abstract
. Numerical semigroup rings are investigated from the relative viewpoint. It is known that algebraic properties such as singularities of a numerical semigroup ring are properties of a flat numerical semigroup algebra. In this paper, we show that arithmetic and set-theoretic properties of a numerical semigroup ring are properties of an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra.

1.
Introduction

A
numerical semigroup

S
is a monoid generated by finitely many positive rational
numbers. In the literature, it is often assumed that
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(S)=1
. With
these assumptions, the numerical semigroup ring is defined as the subring

(1)

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]:=\Big\{\sum_{s\in S}a_{s}\mathbf{u}^{s}\colon a_{s}\in\kappa\Big\}

of the power series ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
over a field
\kappa
.
In addition to
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
, we also use the notation
S=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
to indicate the correspondence between logarithms and exponents of semigroups and rings.
Because of the correspondence, one may work either on the logarithmic side or the
exponential side of the theory. In this paper, we choose to stay in the exponential side
in compliance with the previous study of ring theoretic properties such as
Cohen-Macaulayness, Gorensteiness and complete intersection
[
5
]
.
The reader preferring semigroups can switch freely to the logarithmic side.
Our relative viewpoint on arithmetic and set-theoretic properties such as
symmetry, irreducibility and Cohen-Macaulay type suggests further developments
for numerical semigroups.

In the literature, the numerical semigroup ring (
1
) is commonly
denoted as
\kappa[\![S]\!]
, or
\kappa[\![s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}]\!]
if
S
is generated by

s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
. Although the common notation leads to no misunderstanding in the
classical situation, the appearance of rings such as
{\mathbb{Q}}[\![3,5,7]\!]
are
vague in distinguishing elements
3,5,7
in the underlying field
\mathbb{Q}
from
exponents
3,5,7
of monomials
\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7}
. To emphasize the relative
nature, our notation is more handy in adding monomials to a numerical semigroup
ring. For instance, given a numerical semigroup ring
R
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
, we may
add a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{t}
to
R
and obtain
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{t}]\!]
.

The assumptions
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(S)=1
for a numerical semigroup
S

are not essential for the construction of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
. For an arbitrary numerical
semigroup
S
, we may still define a numerical semigroup ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
as
(
1
) allowing for monomials in
\mathbf{u}
to have rational exponents. Given a
positive rational number
t
, the numerical semigroup ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{v}^{tS}]\!]
is
isomorphic to
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
via the symbolized relation
\mathbf{v}^{t}=\mathbf{u}
. For a
numerical semigroup
S
, there is a unique positive rational number
t
such that

tS\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(tS)=1
. Therefore any numerical semigroup ring
is isomorphic to one constructed from a numerical semigroup
S
with the conditions

S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(S)=1
.

A numerical semigroup ring has various algebra structures. It is an algebra over the underlying field, as well as over other subrings. We consider the category of numerical semigroup rings. A morphism in the category gives rise to an algebra.
As seen in
[
4
,
5
]
, such a relative viewpoint sheds light to the algebraic properties of numerical semigroup rings. In this paper, we show that arithmetic and set-theoretic properties of a numerical semigroup
ring are also relative in nature. To be precise, we consider
numerical semigroups
S\subset S^{\prime}
. The numerical semigroup ring
R^{\prime}:=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S^{\prime}}]\!]

is an algebra over the numerical semigroup ring
R:=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
. We denote the algebra

R^{\prime}
together with the
coefficient ring

R
by
R^{\prime}/R
and call it a
numerical semigroup algebra

A numerical semigroup algebra and its coefficient ring can be represented using different variables.
In this paper, we use the same variable for a numerical semigroup algebra and its coefficient ring.

In the classical case that
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(S)=1
, there naturally arise two numerical semigroup algebras:

•

The ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is an algebra over a Noether normalization

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
, where
0\neq s\in S
.

•

The ring

serves also as a coefficient ring for the algebra

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]

Singularities such as Cohen-Macaulayness, Gorensteiness and complete intersection

are in fact properties of the flat algebra

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
. We may replace the power series ring

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
by an arbitrary numerical semigroup ring
R
and consider
singularities of a numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime\prime}/R
and its subalgebra
R^{\prime}/R
.
See

The purpose of this paper is to clarify notions of the ring

that are
in fact notions of the algebra
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
. We will replace

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
by an arbitrary numerical semigroup ring

For a numerical semigroup

is equi-gcd. Arithmetic

. We

for numerical semigroups are special cases of general phenomenons for coefficient

Beyond the classical theory of numerical semigroups, there are new phenomenons
in our framework. For example, the numerical semigroup ring

R=k[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
is not symmetric as seen in Kunz’s

[

. Adding the monomial

This paper is organized as follows:
For an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra, we will define Frobenius monomials,
pseudo-Frobenius monomials, gap monomials and sporadic monomials in
Section

as generalizations of corresponding invariants for numerical
semigroups. In the classical case, the number of gap monomials has an upper

. For a general equi-gcd numerical semigroup
algebra, besides an analogous upper bound, we will also give a lower bound for the

to be Gorenstein
[

is generalized.

The next two sections are about symmetry, irreducibility and their connection.
Section
3

read from Apéry sets. We present an analogous way to obtain pseudo-Frobenius monomials in Section
4

In the last two sections, we will see classes of extensions. Radicals of coefficient rings are introduced in Section

to generalize the classical result that a numerical semigroup is one over
n
of
infinitely many symmetric numerical semigroups
[

]

[

Fundamental gap monomials emerge as in the classical case.

2.
Frobenius and Pseudo-Frobenius Monomials

In this section, we consider a numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
.
Recall that the conductor of
R^{\prime}/R
is the ideal

\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R):=\{a\in R\colon aR^{\prime}\subset R\}
of
R^{\prime}
. Conductors are generated by
monomials and satisfy the following properties.

•

\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)=R^{\prime}
if and only if
R^{\prime}=R
.

•

\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime})\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)\subset\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime\prime}/R)\subset\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
for numerical semigroup algebras
R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}/R
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{9}]\!]
,
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{5}]\!]
,
R^{\prime\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{3}]\!]

and
R^{\prime\prime\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
. Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime\prime\prime}/R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{u}^{4}=R^{\prime}\mathbf{u}^{4}+R^{\prime}\mathbf{u}^{5},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{u}^{2}=R^{\prime}\mathbf{u}^{2}+R^{\prime}\mathbf{u}^{5},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\prime}\mathbf{u}^{4}+R^{\prime}\mathbf{u}^{7}=R\mathbf{u}^{4}+R\mathbf{u}^{6}+R\mathbf{u}^{7}+R\mathbf{u}^{9}.

Monomials in
R^{\prime}
but not in
R
are called
gap monomials
of
R^{\prime}/R
. Monomials in

R
but not in
\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
are called
sporadic monomials
of
R^{\prime}/R
. The set of gap
monomials and the set of sporadic monomials of
R^{\prime}/R
are denoted by
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
and

\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
, respectively. For an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R
, sporadic monomials and gap monomials are exponential versions of
sporadic elements and gaps of the numerical semigroup
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
. For a numerical
semigroup
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
, the set of gaps is finite if and only if
\gcd(S)=1
. This well-known
fact is a special case of the following result for numerical semigroup algebras.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
, the following conditions are equivalent.

•

R^{\prime}/R
is equi-gcd.

•

\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)\neq 0
.

•

The set
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
is finite.

•

The set
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
is finite.

Proof.

We may assume that
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime})=1
. So

\mathfrak{f}(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R^{\prime})=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}+1}
, where
w^{\prime}
is the Frobenius number
of
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime}
. Let
p=\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R)
.

Consider first the case that
R^{\prime}/R
is equi-gcd, equivalently
p=1
. Let
w
be the Frobenius number
of
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
. Then
\mathfrak{f}(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R)=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]\mathbf{u}^{w+1}
and

\mathbf{u}^{w+w^{\prime}+1}\in\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
. As a subset of
the finite set
\G(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R)
(resp.
\sN(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R)
), the set

\G(R^{\prime}/R)
(resp.
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
) is finite.

Now we consider the case that
R^{\prime}/R
is not equi-gcd, equivalently
p>1
. There are infinitely
many monomials
\mathbf{u}^{s}
in
R^{\prime}
such that

p
does not divide
s
. Therefore
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
is infinite. Choose such a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}
.
Then
\mathbf{u}^{t}\mathbf{u}^{s}\not\in R
for any
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in R
. That means
\mathbf{u}^{t}\not\in\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
. Therefore

\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)=0
. The set
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
consists of all monomials in
R
and hence is also infinite.
∎

If an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
is flat, then
R^{\prime}=R
by
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Beyond flatness, there are properties of non-trivial equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebras worth studying.
As seen in
[
4
,
5
]
, Apéry monomials form an essential tool to study flat numerical
semigroup algebras. For equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebras, Frobenius monomials and
Pseudo-Frobenius monomials play an important role.
In a numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
, we have two partial orders:

•

\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{t}
if and only if
\mathbf{u}^{t-s}\in R
.

•

\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R^{\prime}}\mathbf{u}^{t}
if and only if
\mathbf{u}^{t-s}\in R^{\prime}
.

We consider maximal elements in
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
with respect to these two partial orders. Those maximal with
respect to
\preceq_{R^{\prime}}
are called
Frobenius monomials
of
R^{\prime}/R
. Those maximal with
respect to
\preceq_{R}
are called
pseudo-Frobenius monomials
of
R^{\prime}/R
. Frobenius
monomials are pseudo-Frobenius monomials. The sets of Frobenius monomials and
pseudo-Frobenius monomials are denoted by
\F(R^{\prime}/R)
and
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
. The
cardinalities of these sets are called the
Frobenius type
(F-type for short) and the

Cohen-Macaulay type
(CM-type for short) of
R^{\prime}/R
, respectively.
Cohen-Macaulay type was introduced for numerical semigroups
[
3
]
.
This notion of type coincides with the Cohen-Macaulay type of a numerical semigroup ring
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
. The Frobenius type of an equi-gcd numerical semigroup
algebra
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is always one. Beyond the classical case, the
Frobenius type has an application somewhat dual to the Cohen-Macaulay type in the comparison
of the number of gap monomials and the number of sporadic monomials. See Proposition
2.6
.

Frobenius monomials and pseudo-Frobenius monomials generalize Frobenius numbers and
pseudo-Frobenius numbers in the non-trivial case. Assume that
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
is a numerical
semigroup with
\gcd(S)=1
.
Recall that a number
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus S
is
called pseudo-Frobenius in
[
13
]
if
w+s\in S
for any
0\neq s\in S
. For the trivial case

S={\mathbb{N}}
, there is a unique Frobenius number and a unique pseudo-Frobenius number, namely
-1
.
However
\F(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!])=\PF(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!])=\emptyset

in our definition and if
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, the only Frobenius monomial of

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is
\mathbf{u}^{w}
, where
w
is the Frobenius number of
S
.
For the non-trivial case
S\neq{\mathbb{N}}
, the exponents of pseudo-Frobenius
monomials of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
are exactly pseudo-Frobenius numbers of
S
.
We remark that all pseudo-Frobenius numbers are positive in the nontrivial case.
Note that
0\neq w+1\in S
in such a case. If there is a negative pseudo-Frobenius number
-s
,
then
0\neq w+1-s\in S
. By induction,
w+1-ns\in S
for all
n\geq 1
. This is impossible, since
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{9}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{5}]\!]
.
Then
\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{11}\}
,

\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{6}\}
and
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{11}\}
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{9}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{11}]\!]
. Then

\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{13}\}
,
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{5}\}

and
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{13}\}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{13}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{3}]\!]
. Then

\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{5}\}
,
\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{9}\}
,
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{9}\}

and
\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{9}\}
.

In the classical case, the ratio of
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|
to
|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
is bounded above by the CM-type
of
R^{\prime}/R

[
3
, Theorem 20]
. For equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebras, this result still
holds. Moreover, the inverse of the F-type provides a lower bound for the ratio.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be a non-trivial equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra of CM-type
n
and F-type

n^{\prime}
. Then

\frac{1}{n^{\prime}}\leq\frac{|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|}{|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|}\leq n.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
, there exists a pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in R
.
Clearly
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\not\in\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
.
The element
(\mathbf{u}^{w-s},\mathbf{u}^{w})\in\sN(R^{\prime}/R)\times\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
determines

\mathbf{u}^{s}
. Therefore

|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|\leq n|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|.

Let
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{s}\not\in\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
, there exists a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{v}\in R^{\prime}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{s+v}\notin R
, which implies
\mathbf{u}^{v}\notin R
. Setting
w=s+v
, we have
\mathbf{u}^{w}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
and
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
.
Multiplying
\mathbf{u}^{w}
by a monomial of
R^{\prime}
, we may assume that
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is a Frobenius monomial.
The element
(\mathbf{u}^{w-s},\mathbf{u}^{w})\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)\times\F(R^{\prime}/R)
determines

\mathbf{u}^{s}
. Therefore

|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|\leq n^{\prime}|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|.

∎

In the situation where
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R
is equi-gcd, the numerical semigroup ring

R
is Gorenstein if and only if it has only one pseudo-Frobenius monomial. Gorensteiness
of
R
has a criterion that
|\G(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R)|=|\sN(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R)|
.
We may generalize this classical criterion
[
6
]
to the relative situation as follows.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra with a single Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
.
The algebra has a single pseudo-Frobenius monomial if and only if
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
.

Proof.

Given
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
, there is
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
such that
\mathbf{u}^{t+s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
.
Since
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is the unique Frobenius monomial,
\mathbf{u}^{w-t-s}\in R^{\prime}
and hence

\mathbf{u}^{w-s}=\mathbf{u}^{w-t-s}\mathbf{u}^{t}\in R^{\prime}
. But
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\not\in R
, since
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
and

\mathbf{u}^{w}=\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\mathbf{u}^{s}\not\in R
. So we have an injective map
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)\to\G(R^{\prime}/R)

sending
\mathbf{u}^{s}
to
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}
.

If
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
, the above map is surjective. The preimage of
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)

is
\mathbf{u}^{w-t}
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{t}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{w}
, the Frobenius monomial is the only
pseudo-Frobenius monomial.

Now we assume that
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is the only pseudo-Frobenius monomial of
R^{\prime}/R
.
Consider
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
. Then
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in R
. But
\mathbf{u}^{w}=\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\mathbf{u}^{s}\not\in R
.
Hence
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
. This shows that the map
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)\to\G(R^{\prime}/R)
above is
also surjective. Therefore
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
.

∎

Example 2.8
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{19},\mathbf{u}^{21}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{22}]\!]
. Then

\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{22},\mathbf{u}^{30},\mathbf{u}^{34}\}
and
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{12}\}
.
In this example,
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
even though
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{30},\mathbf{u}^{34}\}
.

In examples
2.3
and
2.8
, there is a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}
such that

\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\mathbf{u}^{s}\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
. This is not true in general for a numerical semigroup algebra

R^{\prime}/R
such that
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
and
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{5}]\!]
. Then
\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{7}\}

and
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6}\}
. Note that
\G(R^{\prime}/R)\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
for any
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime}
.
In this example,
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{7}\}
.

Example 2.10
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{15},\mathbf{u}^{19},\mathbf{u}^{21}]\!]

and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{31},\mathbf{u}^{39}]\!]
. Then

\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{31},\mathbf{u}^{39},\mathbf{u}^{46},\mathbf{u}^{50}\}
and
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{15},\mathbf{u}^{19}\}
. Note that

\G(R^{\prime}/R)\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
for any
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime}
.
In this example,
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{46},\mathbf{u}^{50}\}
.

3.
Irreducibility

To study an algebraic structure, one often searchs for simplest objects. Irreducibility
is such a notion for numerical semigroups and was first introduced in
[
10
]
.
In this section, we extend the notion to the relative situation.
Let
R^{\prime\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra. For any extension
R^{\prime}
of
R
, the algebras

R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}/R
are also equi-gcd. The coefficient ring
R
is
irreducible
in
R^{\prime\prime}

if it cannot be expressed as the intersection of two non-trivial extensions.
For the case
R^{\prime\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
, the coefficient ring
R
is irreducible if and only if

\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
is an irreducible numerical semigroup.
Recall that, in the classical case
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R
, the algebra has a single Frobenius
monomial.

Example 3.1
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{9}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
. Then
\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{13}\}
.
Any non-trivial extension of
R
must contain

\mathbf{u}^{13}
. Therefore
R
is irreducible as a coefficient ring of
R^{\prime}
. Let
R^{\prime\prime}=R^{\prime}[\![\mathbf{u}^{11}]\!]
. Then

\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{13}\}
and
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{11}]\!]\cap R[\![\mathbf{u}^{13}]\!]=R
.
Therefore
R
is not irreducible as a coefficient ring of
R^{\prime\prime}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{19},\mathbf{u}^{21}]\!]
. The algebra
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{22}]\!]/R
given in
Example
2.8
has gap monomials
\mathbf{u}^{22},\mathbf{u}^{30},\mathbf{u}^{34}
. Since

R=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{30}]\!]\cap R[\![\mathbf{u}^{34}]\!]
, it is not irreducible.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra. If the coefficient ring
R
is irreducible,
then
R^{\prime}/R
has a single Frobenius monomial.

Proof.

A Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
of
R^{\prime}/R
is maximal in
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
with respect to
\preceq_{R^{\prime}}
,
hence
\mathbf{u}^{2w}\in R
and
\G(R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w}]\!]/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
. If
R^{\prime}/R
has two distinct
Frobenius monomials
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}
and
\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}
, then
R=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}}]\!]\cap R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}}]\!]

is not irreducible.
∎

The following criterion for irreducibility extends the classical case
[
10
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathbf{u}^{w}
be a gap monomial of an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
.
The following conditions are equivalent .

•

R
is irreducible with
\mathbf{u}^{w}
as the single Frobenius monomial.

•

R
is maximal with respect to set inclusion
among coefficient rings with
\mathbf{u}^{w}
as the single Frobenius monomial.

•

R
is maximal with respect to set inclusion
among coefficient rings over which
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is a Frobenius monomial.

Proof.

For a Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\F(R^{\prime}/R)
, note that
\G(R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{s}\}
.
In other words, joining a Frobenius monomial to a coefficient ring creates only one extra
monomial. Therefore the set of coefficient rings with
\mathbf{u}^{w}
as the single Frobenius monomial and
the set of coefficient rings over which
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is a Frobenius monomial have the same maximal
coefficient rings with respect to set inclusion.

Let
R_{1}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w}]\!]
and
R_{2}
be an extension of
R
such that
\mathbf{u}^{w}\in\F(R^{\prime}/R_{2})
.
If
\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
, then
\G(R_{1}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
and
R=R_{1}\cap R_{2}
. If
R
is furthermore irreducible, then
R_{2}=R
.
So we obtain maximality. Let
R_{3}
and
R_{4}
be proper extensions of
R
. If
R
is maximal among
coefficient rings of
R^{\prime}
with
\mathbf{u}^{w}
as the single Frobenius monomial, then
\mathbf{u}^{w}\in R_{3}\cap R_{4}
and hence

R\neq R_{3}\cap R_{4}
. Therefore maximality implies irreducibility.
∎

For an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
with a single Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
,
we would like to find an irreducible coefficient ring
R_{1}
containing
R
such that
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is the single
Frobenius monomial of
R^{\prime}/R_{1}
. If
R
is not irreducible, it is not maximal and there is a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}
contained in
an extension of
R
such that
\mathbf{u}^{ns}\neq\mathbf{u}^{w}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In other words,
\mathbf{u}^{s}

is not a radical of
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. As a step to construct an irreducible coefficient ring, the following result
generalizes
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra with a single Frobenius monomial

\mathbf{u}^{w}
. Given a pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}
,
if it is not a radical of
\mathbf{u}^{w}
, then

\mathbf{u}^{w}
is also the single Frobenius monomial of
R^{\prime}/R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
.

Proof.

For any
1\neq\mathbf{u}^{a}\in R
, we have
1\neq\mathbf{u}^{s+a}\in R
since
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
.
By induction,
\mathbf{u}^{ns+a}\in R
for
n\geq 1
. Together with the assumption,
\mathbf{u}^{w}\not\in R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
.
A gap monomial

\mathbf{u}^{t}
of
R^{\prime}/R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
is also a gap monomial of
R^{\prime}/R
. Therefore
\mathbf{u}^{t}\preceq_{R^{\prime}}\mathbf{u}^{w}
.
In other words,
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is the single Frobenius monomial of
R^{\prime}/R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. The coefficient ring

R
is irreducible if and only if
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
or
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w},\mathbf{u}^{w/2}\}
for some

\mathbf{u}^{w}\in G(R^{\prime}/R)
. For CM-type one algebras over an irreducible coefficient ring,
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
.
For CM-type two algebras over an irreducible coefficient ring,
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|+1
.

Proof.

Consider the case that the coefficient ring
R
is irreducible. By Lemma
3.3
,

R^{\prime}/R
has a single Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. It is shown in Proposition
3.4

that
R
is maximal among numerical semigroup subrings of
R^{\prime}
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
with
\mathbf{u}^{w}

as the single Frobenius monomial. Assume that there is a pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}
other than
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. By
Lemma
3.5
,
w=ns
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is the unique Frobenius monomial,

\mathbf{u}^{ns-s}\in R^{\prime}
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{ns}\not\in R
and
\mathbf{u}^{s}
is pseudo-Frobenius, the monomial
\mathbf{u}^{ns-s}

outside
R
is also pseudo-Frobenius. By Lemma
3.5
again,
n-1
divides
n
. This implies that

n=2
.

Consider the case that
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
. It is shown in Theorem
2.7
that

|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
. Given
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
, we obtain
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in R
. Therefore any subring of
R^{\prime}

properly containing
R
has to contain
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. By maximality, the coefficient ring
R
is irreducible.

Consider the case that
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w},\mathbf{u}^{w/2}\}
.
Given
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
not equal
\mathbf{u}^{w/2}
, either
\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{w}
or

\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{w/2}
. If
\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{w}
, then
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in R
.
If
\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{w/2}
, then
1\neq\mathbf{u}^{(w/2)-s}\in R
and hence

\mathbf{u}^{w-s}=\mathbf{u}^{(w/2)-s}\mathbf{u}^{w/2}\in R
. In either cases,
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in R
.
But
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\not\in\mathfrak{f}(R^{\prime}/R)
, since
\mathbf{u}^{w}=\mathbf{u}^{s}\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\not\in R
. So we have an one-to-one map

\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{\mathbf{u}^{w/2}\}\to\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
given by
\mathbf{u}^{s}\mapsto\mathbf{u}^{w-s}
.
As seen in the proof of Theorem
2.7
,
\mathbf{u}^{w-t}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
for any
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in N(R^{\prime}/R)
.
Therefore the one-to-one map is also onto, whence the identity
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|+1
.
Given
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
not equal
\mathbf{u}^{w/2}
, we obtain
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}\in R
.
As the CM-type one case, any subring of
R^{\prime}

properly containing
R
has to contain
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. By maximality, the coefficient ring
R
is irreducible.
∎

Example 3.7
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{n+1},\mathbf{u}^{n+2},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{2n-1},\mathbf{u}^{2n+1}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{n}]\!]
,
where
n>1
. If
n=2
, then
\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{7}\}
and
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{7}\}
.
If
n>2
, then

\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{n},\mathbf{u}^{2n}\}
.
The coefficient ring
R
is irreducible.

4.
Symmetries

Symmetry for a set can be often regarded as a phenomenon observed from one-to-one
correspondence of elements in certain subsets determined by a simple rule. Besides symmetric numerical semigroups that have been observed and well-studied, there are also pseudo-symmetry and almost symmetry properties standing out as variations. These
notions of symmetry have a relative nature. Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra in the
variable
\mathbf{u}
. In view of Theorem
2.7
, symmetry of the coefficient ring
R
is about
correspondence of elements in
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
and
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
. Pseudo-symmetry is about
correspondence of elements in
\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{\mathbf{u}^{w/2}\}
and
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
, where

\mathbf{u}^{w}
occurs as the only Frobenius monomial of
R^{\prime}/R
. To be precise, we call the coefficient
ring
R

symmetric
, if
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
. We call
R

pseudo-symmetric
, if

\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w},\mathbf{u}^{w/2}\}
. As a generalization of
[
10
]
, Theorem
3.6
asserts that a coefficient ring
is irreducible if and only if it is symmetric or pseudo-symmetric. In this section, we consider almost
symmetry for equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebras.

As a generalization of Apéry numbers to the relative case,
Apéry monomials
of a
numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
can be described using the order
\preceq_{R}
as minimal elements among monomials in
R^{\prime}
. Among Apéry monomials, maximal elements with
respect to the order
\preceq_{R^{\prime}}
are called
maximal Apéry monomials
. It is known that a flat numerical semigroup
algebra
R^{\prime}/R
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
is Gorenstein if and only if it has a unique maximal Apéry monomial

[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
. Let
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
be a numerical semigroup ring and
s\in S
. It is also
known that
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is a Gorenstein ring if and only if
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]

is a Gorenstein algebra.

Assume that
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is equi-gcd. Kunz’s symmetry
criterion for the Gorenstein property of the ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is in fact a statement of the algebra

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
. Let
\mathbf{u}^{w}
be the Frobenius monomial. Kunz shows that

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is Gorenstein if and only if the map
S\to{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus S
given by
s\mapsto w-s

is bijective
[
6
]
. Equivalently,
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is Gorenstein if and only if the map

\sN(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!])\to\G(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!])
given by

\mathbf{u}^{s}\mapsto\mathbf{u}^{w-s}
is bijective. On the other hand, the algebra
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
can describe
maximal Apéry monomials of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. Given
1\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
,

\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{t-s}:\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\max_{\preceq_{R}}\left(\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])\setminus\Apr(R^{\prime}/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])\right)\}.

Proof.

The set
\mathbf{u}^{s}\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{s+v}:\mathbf{u}^{v}\in\PF(R^{\prime}/R)\}
consists of exactly those elements in
\mathbf{u}^{s}\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{s+v}:\mathbf{u}^{v}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)\}
maximal with
respect to the order
\preceq_{R}
. Note that
\mathbf{u}^{s}\PF(R^{\prime}/R)\subset R
. Hence the set

R\cap\mathbf{u}^{s}\G(R^{\prime}/R)
has same maximal elements with
\mathbf{u}^{s}\G(R^{\prime}/R)
. The lemma follows
from the fact that

R\cap\mathbf{u}^{s}\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])\setminus\Apr(R^{\prime}/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])
.
∎

For the case that
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
in Lemma
4.1
, the set
\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])\setminus\Apr(R^{\prime}/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])

consists of all elements
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])
such that
t>s
.
Let
\mathbf{u}^{w}
be the Frobenius monomial of
R^{\prime}/R
.
Then
\mathbf{u}^{w+s}\in\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])
. Given

\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])
, if
t<s
, then
\mathbf{u}^{t}\preceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{w+s}
.
So we recover
[
13
, Proposition 2.20]
that

\PF(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{t-s}:\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\max_{\preceq_{R}}\left(\Apr(R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!])\right)\}.

In particular,
R/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
has a unique maximal Apéry monomial if and only if

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R
has a unique pseudo-Frobenius monomial.

Note that maximal Apéry monomials of
R^{\prime}/R
can be characterized as those Apéry monomial

\mathbf{u}^{w}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{w}\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}
is not Apéry for any non-trivial Apéry monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}
.
As an analogue, a pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
is called
maximal
, if
\mathbf{u}^{w}\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}
is not
pseudo-Frobenius for any pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}
. Generalizing symmetry,
the coefficient ring
R
is called
almost symmetric
in
R^{\prime}
, if the algebra has a unique maximal pseudo-Frobenius
monomial.

When
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
, the coefficient ring
R
is almost symmetric precisely when

\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
is almost symmetric as it is defined in
[
1
]
.
Generalizing
[
7
, Theorem 2.4]
, the next proposition justifies
our terminology. Note that, over an almost symmetric coefficient ring, a numerical semigroup
algebra has a unique Frobenius monomial.
By Theorem
3.6
, an irreducible coefficient ring of an equi-gcd numerical semigroup
algebra is almost symmetric. Therefore our notions extend the classical hierarchy that almost
symmetric numerical semigroups
include symmetric numerical semigroups and pseudo-symmetric numerical semigroups.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra with a single Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. Write

\PF(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}=\{\mathbf{u}^{t_{1}},\dots,\mathbf{u}^{t_{n-1}}\}
, where
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n-1}
. The following statements are equivalent.

•

R
is almost symmetric in
R^{\prime}
.

•

\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{n-i}}=\mathbf{u}^{w}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

•

|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|=|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|+n-1
.

Proof.

If
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{n-i}}=\mathbf{u}^{w}
for all
i
, then all
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}
are not maximal. With the only maximal
pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
, the coefficient ring
R
is almost symmetric.

Conversely, if
R
is almost
symmetric, the only maximal pseudo-Frobenius monomial has to be
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. For each
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}
,
there is an
\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{1}}}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{1}}}
is pseudo-Frobenius. If the product is not
yet maximal, there is an
\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{2}}}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{1}}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{2}}}
is
pseudo-Frobenius. Since
\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{1}}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{2}}}\not\in R
and
\mathbf{u}^{s}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{1}}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{2}}}\in R

for any
1\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
, we obtain a pseudo-Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{t_{j_{1}}+t_{j_{2}}}
. Keep
multiplying pseudo-Frobenius monomials, we reach some
\mathbf{u}^{t_{j}}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{t_{j}}=\mathbf{u}^{w}
.
Sorting the numbers
w-t_{i}
, we see
j=n-i
.

There is a map

\varphi:\sN(R^{\prime}/R)\cup(\PF(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\})\longrightarrow\G(R^{\prime}/R)

sending
\mathbf{u}^{s}
to
\mathbf{u}^{w-s}
. Since the map is injective,

(2)

|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|\geq|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|+n-1

and the equality holds if and only if
\varphi
is surjective. The image of
\sN(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{1\}

is contained in
\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
. If
\varphi
is surjective, every
\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}
is the
image of some
\mathbf{u}^{t_{j}}
. Taking the increasing sequence into account, this implies that
t_{i}+t_{n-i}=w
.
Conversely, assume that
t_{i}+t_{n-i}=w
. Then
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
is in the image of
\varphi
. To show that

\varphi
is surjective, we take an element
\mathbf{u}^{v}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
. Then

\mathbf{u}^{s}\mathbf{u}^{v}\in\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
for some
1\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
. From the assumption, either
\mathbf{u}^{s+v}=\mathbf{u}^{w}

or
\mathbf{u}^{w-s-v}\in\PF(R^{\prime}/R)
. Since
\mathbf{u}^{w-v}=\mathbf{u}^{s}\mathbf{u}^{w-s-v}\in R
and

\mathbf{u}^{w-v}\mathbf{u}^{v}=\mathbf{u}^{w}\not\in R
, we obtain an element
\mathbf{u}^{w-v}\in\sN(R^{\prime}/R)
such that

\varphi(\mathbf{u}^{w-v})=\mathbf{u}^{v}
.
∎

For a numerical semigroup algebra
R^{\prime}/R
with F-type one,
|\PF(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\F(R^{\prime}/R)|
provides
a lower bound for
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|-|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|
. The inequality does not hold in general. The number
|\G(R^{\prime}/R)|-|\sN(R^{\prime}/R)|

is not even necessarily positive, when the F-type is greater than one.

Example 4.3
.

Let
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{10},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{13},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{15}]\!]
and
R
be its coefficient
ring such that
\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{13},\mathbf{u}^{23},\mathbf{u}^{27}\}
. Then

\sN(R^{\prime}/R)=\{1,\mathbf{u}^{10},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{15}\}
has more elements than
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
. Here the F-type is two.

Example 4.4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{2n+1},\mathbf{u}^{2n+2},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{4n+1}]\!]
and

R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{2}]\!]
. Then
\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{4},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{2n}\}
and

\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{2n}\}
. The coefficient ring
R
is almost symmetric. Note that
R
is irreducible if and only if
n\leq 2
.

5.
Radicals

Let
R^{\prime\prime}/R
be a numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. To illustrate the relative
nature of an extension
R^{\prime}
of
R
, we often write
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}]\!]
by
joining
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}\in\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R)
to
R
. Clearly,
\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R)
is the disjoint union
of
\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime})
and
\G(R^{\prime}/R)
. If
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}\in\F(R^{\prime\prime}/R)
, then

\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\F(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}\}
. Recall that a monomial
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime\prime}
is the

n
th radical of a monomial in
R
if
\mathbf{u}^{ns}\in R
. Besides trivial extensions
R
and
R^{\prime\prime}
,
radicals of the coefficient ring
R
provide more interesting examples of extensions. Given

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the extension of
R
in
R^{\prime\prime}
generated by all
n
th radicals of monomials of
R
is
called the
n
th
radical
of
R
in
R^{\prime\prime}
. If
R^{\prime\prime}
is understood from the context, we denote
the
n
th radical by
\sqrt[n]{R}
.

For the case
R^{\prime\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
, radicals are investigated in the logarithmic form as quotients
of the numerical semigroup
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
. It is known that a numerical semigroup is one half of a
symmetric numerical semigroup
[
11
]
. In fact, there are infinitely
many such symmetric numerical semigroups
[
12
]
. Even more,
for each
n\geq 2
, a numerical semigroup can be expressed as quotients of infinitely many
symmetric numerical semigroups by
n

[
17
, Theorem 5]
. For each
n\geq 3
,
a numerical semigroup can be also expressed as quotients of infinitely many pseudo-symmetric
numerical semigroups by
n

[
17
, Theorem 6]
. In this section, we show that
the classical results in
[
17
]
have a relative nature.

Example 5.1
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{10},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{2n+1}]\!]
, where
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\sqrt[2]{R}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7}]\!]
in
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{5}]\!]
.

Numerical duplications are a source of radicals of coefficient rings. They are first introduced in
the logarithmic form
[
2
]
. In our notation, a
numerical duplication
of a
numerical semigroup ring
R
is the numerical semigroup ring

R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{u}^{s_{0}/2},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{s_{n}}\mathbf{u}^{s_{0}/2}]\!]
, where

\mathbf{u}^{s_{0}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{s_{n}}\in R
but
\mathbf{u}^{s_{0}/2}\not\in R
. In
R^{\prime}
, we have
\sqrt[2]{R}=R^{\prime}
.
Flatness of numerical semigroup algebras is a main theme for the study of singularities.
However, as seen in the following proposition, there are no extensions of the coefficient ring
other than radicals in a flat algebra.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
R^{\prime\prime}/R
be a numerical semigroup algebra and let
R^{\prime}
be an extension of the coefficient ring
R
.
If
R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime\prime}/R
are both flat, then
R^{\prime}
is a radical of
R
.

Proof.

We may assume that the numerical semigroup rings are in the variable
\mathbf{u}
and
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
d^{\prime\prime}=\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime})
,
d^{\prime}=\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime})
and
d=\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R)
.
An element of
R^{\prime}
can be written
as
\mathbf{u}^{sd^{\prime}}
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
q=d/d^{\prime}
. We claim
R^{\prime}=\sqrt[q]{R}
. On the one hand,

\mathbf{u}^{sd^{\prime}q}=\mathbf{u}^{sd}\in\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{d/d^{\prime\prime}}]\!]\cap R^{\prime\prime}=R
,
where the last equality holds by flatness of
R^{\prime\prime}/R

[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Therefore
R^{\prime}\subset\sqrt[q]{R}
. On the other hand, consider
\mathbf{u}^{sd^{\prime}}\in\sqrt[q]{R}
. Then
\mathbf{u}^{sd}\in R

and hence
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The inclusion
\sqrt[q]{R}\subset R^{\prime}
follows from
\mathbf{u}^{sd^{\prime}}\in\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{d^{\prime}/d^{\prime\prime}}]\!]\cap R^{\prime\prime}=R^{\prime}
,
where the last equality holds by flatness of
R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime}
[
ibid.
].
∎

Note that an equi-gcd algebra
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{S}]\!]
is never flat, unless
S={\mathbb{N}}
.
We can say more about radicals for equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebras in general.
Let
n\geq 2
and
R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime}
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
.
Using a similar idea as
[
17
]
, we will show that there are infinitely many equi-gcd
numerical semigroup algebras
R^{\prime\prime}/R
such that
R^{\prime}=\sqrt[n]{R}
and
R
is symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
.
We may assume that
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime})=1
. In its logarithmic form,
the condition
R^{\prime}=\sqrt[n]{R}
can be stated as
nS^{\prime}=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R\cap n\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime}
, where

S^{\prime}=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime}
. Since
R^{\prime\prime}/R
is required to be equi-gcd, there should be a monomial

\mathbf{u}^{h}\in R^{\prime\prime}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R
for
s>h
. To construct a symmetric coefficient ring
R
,
we will choose an
h
and join to

R_{0}:=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{nS^{\prime}},\mathbf{u}^{h+1},\mathbf{u}^{h+2},\dots,\mathbf{u}^{2h+1}]\!]

certain monomials
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime\prime}
satisfying
s\not\in n\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime}
. Note that
R^{\prime\prime}/R_{0}
is
equi-gcd. Denote
\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}=\F(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R^{\prime})
. In other words,
w
is the Frobenius
number of
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime}
. If
h>w
, then
R_{0}\subset R^{\prime}
. If
h>nw
, then
nS^{\prime}=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R_{0}\cap n\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime}
.
The algebra
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R^{\prime}
has finitely many gap monomials
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{w_{2}},\ldots
.
If
h>nw+w
, then

R_{1}:=R_{0}[\![\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{2}},\ldots]\!]\subset R^{\prime}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
h>2nw
. Then

\G(R_{1}/R_{0})=\{\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{2}},\ldots\}
.

Proof.

The condition
h>2nw
implies
2h-nw_{i}-nw_{j}>h
. Hence
\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{i}}\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{j}}\in R_{0}
.
Monomials of
R_{0}
are of the form
\mathbf{u}^{t}
or
\mathbf{u}^{ns}
, where
t>h
and
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime}
.
Clearly,
\mathbf{u}^{t}\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{i}}\in R_{0}
. If
s>w_{i}
, then
ns+h-nw_{i}>h
and hence
\mathbf{u}^{ns}\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{i}}\in R_{0}
. If
s<w_{i}
,
then
\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}-s}\in\G(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{u}^{ns}\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{i}}=\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{j}}
for some
j
. We
conclude that joining
\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{h-nw_{m}}
to
R_{0}
does not create other gap
monomials.
∎

Now we assume furthermore
n\nmid h
besides the condition
h>2nw
. Then
\mathbf{u}^{h}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R_{1})

and
nS^{\prime}=\log_{\mathbf{u}}R_{1}\cap n\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime\prime}
. Before joining to
R_{1}
more monomials, we consider a monomial

\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime})\subset\G(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R^{\prime})
. By the assumption
h>2nw
, we have

h-s\geq h-w>w
and hence
\mathbf{u}^{s}\preceq_{R^{\prime\prime}}\mathbf{u}^{h}
. This implies
\F(R^{\prime\prime}/R_{1})\subset R^{\prime}
.
Let
\mathbf{u}^{t_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{t_{2}},\ldots
be those monomials in
\G(R^{\prime}/R_{1})
satisfying

\mathbf{u}^{t_{i}}\npreceq_{R^{\prime\prime}}\mathbf{u}^{h}
. Define

R_{2}:=R_{1}[\![\mathbf{u}^{t_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{t_{2}},\ldots]\!]\subset R^{\prime}.

Then
\G(R_{2}/R_{1})=\{\mathbf{u}^{t_{1}},\mathbf{u}^{t_{2}},\ldots\}
.
By the choice of
t_{i}
, the algebra
R^{\prime\prime}/R_{2}
has a single Frobenius monomial
\mathbf{u}^{h}
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
R^{\prime\prime}/R^{\prime}
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra. Given
n\geq 2
, the coefficient ring

R^{\prime}
is the
n
th radical of infinitely many coefficient ring
R
, which can be taken to be symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
.
If
n\geq 3
,
R
can be taken to be pseudo-symmetric.

Proof.

For
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
n\nmid h
and
h>2nw
, we construct
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
as above so that

\F(R^{\prime\prime}/R_{2})=\{\mathbf{u}^{h}\}
. By Proposition
3.4
, there is an irreducible coefficient ring

R
of
R^{\prime\prime}
extending
R_{2}

such that
\F(R^{\prime\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{h}\}
.
Clearly,
\sqrt[n]{R}\supset\sqrt[n]{R_{1}}=R^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, if
\mathbf{u}^{ns}\in R

for some
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime\prime}
,
then
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime}
; otherwise
\mathbf{u}^{h-ns}\in R_{1}\subset R_{2}\subset R
contradicting to
\mathbf{u}^{h}\not\in R
.
Therefore
\sqrt[n]{R}=R^{\prime}
. In particular,
R\subset R^{\prime}
. Since
R
is irreducible,

\PF(R^{\prime}/R)\subset\{\mathbf{u}^{h},\mathbf{u}^{h/2}\}
by Theorem
3.6
. If we choose
h
such that

\mathbf{u}^{h/2}\notin R^{\prime\prime}
, then
\PF(R^{\prime\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{h}\}
, which means
R
is symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
.
There are infinitely many such
h
. For instance, we may choose
h
to be an odd integer.
If
\mathbf{u}^{h/2}\in R^{\prime\prime}
, then
\mathbf{u}^{h/2}\in\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R)
and
\mathbf{u}^{h/2}\npreceq_{R}\mathbf{u}^{h}
. Therefore,

\PF(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{h},\mathbf{u}^{h/2}\}
, i.e.
R
is pseudo-symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
.
If
n\geq 3
, there are infinitely many such
h
. For instance, we may choose

h
to be of the form
ns+2
, where
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

Example 5.5
.

Consider
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{9}]\!]
and
R^{\prime\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{7}]\!]
.
Then
\F(\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]/R^{\prime})=\{\mathbf{u}^{5}\}
. In the above construction for
n=2
and
h=21
, we have

\displaystyle R_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{18},\mathbf{u}^{23},\mathbf{u}^{25},\mathbf{u}^{27},\mathbf{u}^{29}]\!],

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{0}[\![\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{15},\mathbf{u}^{17},\mathbf{u}^{19}]\!]=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{15},\mathbf{u}^{17},\mathbf{u}^{18}]\!]

and
R_{2}=R_{1}
. Then
R=R_{2}
is symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
and
\sqrt[2]{R}=R^{\prime}
.
For
n=3
and
h=31
we have

\displaystyle R_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{18},\mathbf{u}^{21},\mathbf{u}^{27},\mathbf{u}^{32},\mathbf{u}^{34},\mathbf{u}^{35},\mathbf{u}^{37},\mathbf{u}^{38},\mathbf{u}^{40},\mathbf{u}^{41},\mathbf{u}^{43}]\!],

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{0}[\![\mathbf{u}^{16},\mathbf{u}^{22},\mathbf{u}^{25},\mathbf{u}^{28}]\!]=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{16},\mathbf{u}^{18},\mathbf{u}^{21},\mathbf{u}^{22},\mathbf{u}^{25},\mathbf{u}^{27},\mathbf{u}^{35}]\!],

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{1}[\![\mathbf{u}^{29}]\!]=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{12},\mathbf{u}^{16},\mathbf{u}^{18},\mathbf{u}^{21},\mathbf{u}^{22},\mathbf{u}^{25},\mathbf{u}^{27},\mathbf{u}^{29},\mathbf{u}^{35}]\!].

Note that
R_{2}
is not symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
. Indeed,

\PF(R^{\prime\prime}/R_{2})=\{\mathbf{u}^{17},\mathbf{u}^{20},\mathbf{u}^{23},\mathbf{u}^{26},\mathbf{u}^{31}\}
.
However,
R=R_{2}[\![\mathbf{u}^{17},\mathbf{u}^{20},\mathbf{u}^{23},\mathbf{u}^{26}]\!]
is symmetric in
R^{\prime\prime}
and
\sqrt[3]{R}=R^{\prime}
.

6.
Fundamental Gap Monomials

Let
R^{\prime\prime}/R
be a numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. Intersections of radicals of
R
have been studied in

[
8
]
for the case
R^{\prime\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
under the
name “arithmetic extension” from the logarithmic viewpoint. In this section, we exhibit the relative nature
of the connection between arithmetic extensions and fundamental gaps.

Example 6.1
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{9},\mathbf{u}^{10},\mathbf{u}^{11},\mathbf{u}^{13}]\!]
and
R^{\prime\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{4}]\!]
.
Then
\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{12}\}
. The extension

R[\![\mathbf{u}^{12}]\!]=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{4}]\!]\cap R[\![\mathbf{u}^{3}]\!]
of
R
is the intersection
\sqrt[2]{R}\cap\sqrt[3]{R}
of radicals.

Example 6.2
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{13}]\!]
and
R^{\prime\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
. Then

\G(R^{\prime\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{14}\}
. The coefficient ring
R
has three non-trivial extensions
in
R^{\prime\prime}
. The extensions
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{7}]\!]
and

R[\![\mathbf{u}^{14}]\!]
are not intersection of radicals, while
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]=\sqrt[2]{R}
.

Example 6.3
.

The coefficient ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6}]\!]
of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{19}]\!]
has only
trivial extensions.
The extension
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{19}]\!]
of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6}]\!]
in
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
is not an intersection of radicals.

Example 6.4
.

The coefficient ring
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{14}]\!]
of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
has two
non-trivial extensions
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{7}]\!]
and
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6},\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
.
Both are not intersection of radicals.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra. The elements in

\FG(R^{\prime}/R):=\G(R^{\prime}/R)\cap\sqrt[2]{R}\cap\sqrt[3]{R}

are called the
fundamental gap monomials
of
R^{\prime}/R
. The gap monomial with the largest exponent
is a fundamental gap monomial.
For the case
R^{\prime}=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
, the exponents of monomials in
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)

are the fundamental gaps of the numerical semigroup
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R
first
introduced in
[
14
]
. Note that
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)\subset\sqrt[n]{R}

for
n\geq 2
. Therefore any intersection of radicals of
R
contains
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)
.

Example 6.5
.

Let
R=\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{6}]\!]
and
R^{\prime}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
. Then

\G(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{7},\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{13},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{19}\}.

Radicals of
R
are
\sqrt[2]{R}=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{13},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{19}]\!]=R[\![\mathbf{u}^{8}]\!]
and

\sqrt[n]{R}=R^{\prime}
for
n>2
. Hence
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{8},\mathbf{u}^{13},\mathbf{u}^{14},\mathbf{u}^{19}\}
.

As observed in the classical case
[
8
, Lemma 10]
, equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebras with a single fundamental gap monomial
have interesting properties.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. A monomial

\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
is the only fundamental gap monomial if and only if
any gap monomial is a radical of
\mathbf{u}^{s}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{s}\}
. Given
\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{\mathbf{u}^{s}\}
, we have

\mathbf{u}^{t}\not\in\sqrt[n_{1}]{R}
for some
n_{1}\geq 2
. Hence
\mathbf{u}^{tn_{1}}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
. If
\mathbf{u}^{tn_{1}}\neq\mathbf{u}^{s}
,
there exists
n_{2}\geq 2
such that
\mathbf{u}^{tn_{1}n_{2}}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
. Repeating this procedure, eventually we
find
n\geq 2
such that
\mathbf{u}^{tn}=\mathbf{u}^{s}
.

On the other hand, we assume that any gap monomial is a radical of
\mathbf{u}^{s}
. Given

\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus\{\mathbf{u}^{s}\}
, there exists
n\geq 2
such that
\mathbf{u}^{tn}=\mathbf{u}^{s}
. Hence

\mathbf{u}^{t}\not\in\sqrt[n]{R}
and
\mathbf{u}^{t}\not\in\FG(R^{\prime}/R)
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Any numerical semigroup ring has finitely many equi-gcd coefficient rings,
over which there is a single fundamental gap monomial.

Proof.

We may work on a numerical semigroup ring
R^{\prime}
in the variable
\mathbf{u}
such that
\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{N}}

and
\gcd(\log_{\mathbf{u}}R^{\prime})=1
. Choose an integer
n>4
such that
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in R^{\prime}
for any
s\geq n
.
If the proposition was not true, there would exist an equi-gcd coefficient ring
R
, over which there is a single fundamental gap monomial
\mathbf{u}^{w}
for some
w>2n
. Let

m=\begin{cases}\frac{w+1}{2},&\text{ if }w\text{ is odd;}\\
\frac{w+2}{2},&\text{ if }w\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

Then
\mathbf{u}^{m},\mathbf{u}^{w-m}\in R^{\prime}
are not radicals of
\mathbf{u}^{w}
. Hence
\mathbf{u}^{m},\mathbf{u}^{w-m}\in R
by Lemma
6.6
.
However,
\mathbf{u}^{m}\mathbf{u}^{w-m}\in R
contradicts to
\mathbf{u}^{w}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
.
∎

Example 6.8
.

It is shown in the logarithmic form
[
8
, Lemma 11]
that the numerical semigroup rings

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}[\!]
,
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{3}[\!]
,
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{5}]\!]
,
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{5}]\!]
,

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{3},\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7}]\!]
and
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{4},\mathbf{u}^{5},\mathbf{u}^{7}]\!]
are all equi-gcd coefficient rings of

\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}]\!]
, over which there is a single fundamental gap monomial.

Example 6.9
.

Let
R
be a non-trivial equi-gcd coefficient ring of
\kappa[\![\mathbf{u}^{2},\mathbf{u}^{3}]\!]
. Then

Corollary 6.10
.

Any numerical semigroup ring has finitely many equi-gcd coefficient rings, whose extensions
are all intersections of radicals.

Proof.

Let
R^{\prime}
be a numerical semigroup ring in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. Given an equi-gcd coefficient ring

R
whose extensions are all intersections of radicals, it suffices to prove that
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)
is a singleton.
Assume the contrary that
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)
contains monomials
\mathbf{u}^{t}
and
\mathbf{u}^{s}
such that
t<s
. Then the extension
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
is not an intersection of radicals, because it does not contain
\mathbf{u}^{t}
.
∎

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
R^{\prime}/R
be an equi-gcd numerical semigroup algebra in the variable
\mathbf{u}
. Then
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)
is a singleton if
and only if all extensions of
R
in
R^{\prime}
are intersections of radicals.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathbf{u}^{s},\mathbf{u}^{t}\in\FG(R^{\prime}/R)
and
t<s
. Then the extension
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{s}]\!]
is not
an intersection of radicals, because it does not contain
\mathbf{u}^{t}
. On the other hand, we assume that
\FG(R^{\prime}/R)=\{\mathbf{u}^{w}\}
.
Consider an extension
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}[\!]
, where
\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)
.
For each
\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}\in\G(R^{\prime}/R)\setminus R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}[\!]
, there exits
d^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

d^{\prime}w^{\prime}=w
. Hence
\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}\not\in\sqrt[d^{\prime}]{R}
. We claim that
\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}}\in\sqrt[d^{\prime}]{R}
for all
i
. It then
follows that
R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}[\!]
is the intersection of all such radicals of
R
. If

\mathbf{u}^{w_{i}}\not\in\sqrt[d^{\prime}]{R}
, there exists
d_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
d_{i}d^{\prime}w_{i}=w
. Together with
d^{\prime}w^{\prime}=w
,
we obtain
d_{i}w_{i}=w^{\prime}
contradicting to
\mathbf{u}^{w^{\prime}}\not\in R[\![\mathbf{u}^{w_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{u}^{w_{n}}[\!]
.

∎

3D Lovelock Gravity and the Holographic c-theorem
Gökhan Alkaçi i alkac@mail.com, Gün Süerii ii suer.gun@metu.edu.tr

Abstract
We show that the scalar-tensor theory that arises in a rigorous D → 3 D\to 3 limit of Lovelock gravity up to cubic order admits a holographic c-theorem and verify that the value of the c-function at the UV fixed point matches with the Weyl anomaly coefficient (up to an irrelevant factor). The constructed c-function is the same as one that follows from the “naive limit”, which is obtained by scaling the couplings by a factor of 1 D − 3 \frac{1}{D-3} , and then setting D = 3 D=3 .

2
c-theorem in Lovelock Gravity in
D>6

We will construct a holographic c-function for the cubic Lovelock gravity in
D>6
by the method of
[
63
]
. The action that we consider is as follows:

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-2\Lambda_{0}+\alpha L^{2}\mathcal{L}_{m=2}+\beta L^{4}\mathcal{L}_{m=3}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial^{\mu}\chi\right],

(2.1)

where the Lagrangians
\mathcal{L}_{m=2,3}
can be found from the general expression (
1.2
), and the free-scalar
\chi
is introduced to impose the NEC in a practical way. Considering the following domain-wall ansatz

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{2A(r)}\eta_{ab}\mathrm{d}x^{a}\mathrm{d}x^{b}+e^{2B(r)}\mathrm{d}r^{2},

(2.2)

where
\eta_{ab}
is the Minkowski metric and the Latin indices run from
0
to
(D-2)
, and assuming a radial profile for the free-scalar,
\chi=\chi(r)
, one can show that the NEC reduces to the positivity of the radial kinetic energy of the scalar field, i.e.,
\frac{1}{2}\chi^{\prime 2}\geq 0
. Using this configuration in the action (
2.1
) gives an effective action for the functions
\left[A(r),B(r),\chi(r)\right]
. After finding the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations, and then fixing the gauge by
B(r)=0
, an inequality of the following form can be obtained by eliminating the bare cosmological constant
\Lambda_{0}
from the equations:

\frac{1}{2}\chi^{\prime 2}=\mathcal{F}\left(A^{\prime},A^{\prime\prime},\ldots\right)\geq 0,

(2.3)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a polynomial of the derivatives of the function
A(r)
. If it does not contain more than second-order derivatives of the function
A(r)
, and has the form

\mathcal{F}=\sum_{n}a_{n}{A^{\prime}}^{2n}A^{\prime\prime}\geq 0,

(2.4)

a monotonically increasing c-function can be defined as
[
31
]

c(r):=\frac{1}{({\ell_{P}{A^{\prime}})}^{(D-2)}}\sum_{n}\frac{a_{n}}{2(n+1)-D}{A^{\prime}}^{2n}.

(2.5)

While the monotonicity is evident for even
D
, it was also shown to be the case by construction for odd

in
[
20
]
. For the value of the c-function at the UV fixed point, we set
A(r)=\frac{r}{L}
and find

c=\left(\frac{L}{\ell_{P}}\right)^{(D-2)}\sum_{n=0}^{D-1}\frac{a_{n}}{2(n+1)-D}.

(2.6)

The application of this procedure to cubic Lovelock gravity in
D
-dimensions yields the following inequality

\mathcal{F}=(D-2)\left[-1+2\alpha(D-3)(D-4)L^{2}A^{\prime 2}-3\beta(D-3)(D-4)(D-5)(D-6)L^{4}A^{\prime 4}\right]A^{\prime\prime}\geq 0,

(2.7)

which leads to the monotonically increasing c-function

c(r)=\frac{1}{({\ell_{P}{A^{\prime}})}^{(D-2)}}\left[1-2\alpha(D-2)(D-3)L^{2}A^{\prime 2}+3\beta(D-2)(D-3)(D-4)(D-5)L^{4}A^{\prime 4}\right],

(2.8)

whose value at the UV fixed point is given by

c=\left(\frac{L}{{\ell_{P}}}\right)^{(D-2)}\left[1-2\alpha(D-2)(D-3)+3\beta(D-2)(D-3)(D-4)(D-5)\right].

(2.9)

It is obvious that this whole structure allows a naive limit to
D=3
by scaling the couplings as
(\alpha,\beta)\to\dfrac{1}{D-3}\ (\alpha,\beta)
and then setting
D=3
. The resulting expressions are

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\left[-1-2\alpha L^{2}A^{\prime 2}+18\beta L^{4}A^{\prime 4}\right]A^{\prime\prime}\geq 0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle c(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\ell_{P}{A^{\prime}}}}\left[1-2\alpha L^{2}A^{\prime 2}+6\beta L^{4}A^{\prime 4}\right],

(2.11)

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{{\ell_{P}}}\left[1-2\alpha+6\beta\right].

(2.12)

In the next section, we will show that these expressions are indeed preserved in 3D Lovelock gravity up to the cubic term. In 3D, the value of the c-function at the UV fixed point
c
should be proportional to the Weyl anomaly coefficient and the central charge of the boundary CFT.

c-theorem in 3D Lovelock Gravity

3D cubic Lovelock gravity is described by the action

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-2\Lambda_{0}+\alpha L^{2}\mathcal{L}_{m=2}+\beta L^{4}\mathcal{L}_{m=3}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial^{\mu}\chi\right],

\chi

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m=2}

\displaystyle=4G^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}-4X\Box\phi+2X^{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m=3}

\displaystyle=-48R^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu\nu}X-48R^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}X+24RX\Box\phi+6RX^{2}

\displaystyle+96\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}\Box\phi+48\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu\nu}\Box\phi-24\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu\nu}X

\displaystyle-144\phi_{\mu\nu}\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}X-96\phi^{\mu}\phi^{\nu}\phi_{\mu\rho}\phi_{\ \nu}^{\rho}-32\phi^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu\rho}\phi_{\ \nu}^{\rho}

\displaystyle-16(\Box\phi)^{3}+24X(\Box\phi)^{2}-24X^{3},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(A^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime})\Big[A^{\prime}\left(2\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\alpha+27\beta\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)-\alpha\phi^{\prime\prime}-36\beta\phi^{\prime 4}\right)+\phi^{\prime}\left(3\phi^{\prime\prime}\left(\alpha-30\beta\phi^{\prime 2}\right)-2A^{\prime\prime}\left(\alpha-18\beta\phi^{\prime 2}\right)\right)

With this at hand, we can show the agreement with the Weyl anomaly coefficient

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{1+\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}}+r^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}),

Note that the c-theorem in Horndeski gravity involving up to only linear curvature terms was investigated in
[
61

. With our conventions, the action that the authors considered is as follows:

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{D}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L},\qquad\qquad\mathcal{L}=R-2\Lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha X-\gamma\,G^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}\right).

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+\gamma\Lambda_{\text{eff}})X.

They found that a monotonic c-function is admitted only at the critical point where the effective kinetic term (

\gamma=\frac{2\alpha L^{2}}{D(D-1)}

Together with this finding, our results suggest that boundary terms play a crucial role in comparing Lovelock gravity in lower dimensions with pure gravity theories.

5
Summary

In this paper, we studied the existence of a holographic c-function in 3D Lovelock gravity described by the Lagrangian (
3.1
), a scalar-tensor theory that arises after a rigorous
D\to 3
limit of Lovelock gravity. Being an example of a Horndeski theory, it has second-order field equations. However, as shown in
[
61
]
before, this does not guarantee the formulation of the holographic c-theorem. We have shown that the theory admits a holographic c-function and its value at the UV fixed point agrees with the one predicted by the Weyl anomaly in (
3.10
). Moreover, our findings match the results obtained by the naive limit (
2.10
-
2.12
). By checking two important holographic relations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) of 3D higher-derivative gravity theories, we also shed light on the important role that boundary terms play in the comparison of lower-dimensional Lovelock gravity and pure gravity theories.

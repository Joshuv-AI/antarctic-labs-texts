Transience of symmetric non-local Dirichlet forms
Yuichi Shiozawa

Abstract
We establish transience criteria for symmetric non-local Dirichlet forms on L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}) in terms of the coefficient growth rates at infinity. Applying these criteria, we find a necessary and sufficient condition for recurrence of Dirichlet forms of symmetric stable-like with unbounded/degenerate coefficients. This condition indicates that both of the coefficient growth rates of small and big jump parts affect the sample path properties of the associated symmetric jump processes.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we establish transience criteria
for a symmetric non-local Dirichlet form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

in terms of the growth rates of the coefficients at infinity.
Applying these criteria,
we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition
for recurrence of Dirichlet forms of symmetric stable-like
with unbounded/degenerate coefficients.

In the theory of stochastic processes,
it is one of the important research subjects
to study the global path properties of a symmetric Markov process.
These properties are closely related to
the analytic and geometric conditions
such as the coefficient growth of the generator and
the volume growth of the state space
(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
20
,
22
]
and references therein for details).

Here we focus on recurrence and transience of symmetric Markov processes
generated by Dirichlet forms.
Roughly speaking, we mean by recurrence the nature of particles
hitting every open set infinitely many times almost surely.
On the other hand,
we mean by transience the nature of particles escaping to infinity eventually almost surely.
There are many recurrence criteria
for symmetric Markov processes
in terms of the analytic and geometric conditions as mentioned before
(see, for instance,
[
6
,
7
,
9
,
12
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
25
]
);
however, there are less such transience criterion
in spite of their importance.

For the Brownian motion on a spherically symmetric Riemannian manifold,
we know a necessary and sufficient condition for recurrence
in terms of the surface area of a ball
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, Theorem 5.1 in Section 9]
or
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
).
Ichihara
[
9
]
established sharp recurrence and transience criteria
for the symmetric diffusion process on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

generated by a strongly local Dirichlet form with unbounded/degenerate coefficient
(see also
[
20
, Section 6]
for the exposition on this topic,
and
[
10
]
and
[
20
, Section 9]
for the Brownian motion on a Riemannian manifold).

As for symmetric jump processes on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
Ôkura-Uemura
[
19
]
and Okamura-Uemura
[
14
]

obtained a sufficient condition for recurrence
in terms of the coefficient growth rates of the associated non-local Dirichlet forms.
This condition is consistent with that of
[
9
]
.
However, as far as the author knows,
we have no corresponding condition for transience.
Our objective in this paper is to establish transience criteria
for a class of symmetric jump processes on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(Theorems
3.3
and
3.8
).
Using these criteria, we can verify the sharpness of the results of
[
19
,
14
]

in terms of the polynomial growth rates of the coefficients
(Corollary
3.13
).

We note here that, in contrast with local Dirichlet forms,
non-local ones have two kinds of the coefficients
corresponding to the small and big jump parts.
On account of this, we are interested in clarifying the relation between
the growth rates of these coefficients and the global properties of sample paths.
From this point of view, we established conservativeness criteria for
symmetric jump processes (
[
21
,
23
]
).
We also find in
[
24
]
a necessary and sufficient condition
for the associated Markovian semigroups being
L^{2}
-compact.
These two results say that
the coefficient growth rate of the big jump is irrelevant to
the validity of the conservativeness and
L^{2}
-compactness.
Our results in this paper prove that
both of the coefficients do affect the sample path properties.

To establish transience criteria for a non-local Dirichlet form,
we adopt the so-called Lyapunov method;
that is, we apply some test function to the generator and
use the martingale characterization of the generator.
This method is standard for diffusion processes (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
and references therein)
and applicable to jump processes (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 4]
).
Here we use this method separately to the generators
corresponding to small and big jump parts.
For the small jump part,
we develop calculation in
[
24
, Proposition 2.5]

to find the influence of the coefficient growth rate and spatial dimension on transience (Proposition
3.4
).
Concerning the big jump part,
we need elementary but involved calculation
in order to ensure the existence of a test function (Lemma
3.12
).
Because of this, we impose a strong restriction on the big jump part
(see Subsection
3.3
).
Our approach for the big jump part is motivated by
[
11
, Lemmas 4 and 7]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, we summarize some materials on
the Dirichlet form theory relevant to recurrence and transience.
In Section
3
, we present transience criteria for symmetric jump processes
and carry out the Lyapunov method.
Some of the involved calculation are postponed to Section
4
.
Appendix contains a version of the recurrence criterion for non-local Dirichlet forms
established by Ôkura
[
16
]
and Ôkura-Uemura
[
19
]
,
and an application of this criterion.
This appendix makes the proof of Corollary
3.13
self-contained.

2
Preliminaries

We collect some materials
related to recurrence and transience of Dirichlet forms from
[
4
]
.

2.1
Analytic part

Let
(X,{\cal B},m)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and

(u,v)=\int_{X}uv\,{\rm d}m
for
u,v\in L^{2}(X;m)
.
Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a strongly continuous Markovian semigroup on
L^{2}(X;m)

and
({\cal E},{\cal F})
an associated Dirichlet form.
Namely,
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is a closed and Markovian symmetric form on
L^{2}(X;m)
given by

{\cal F}=\left\{u\in L^{2}(X;m)\mid\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}(u-T_{t}u,u)<\infty\right\},\ {\cal E}(u,u)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}(u-T_{t}u,u)\ \text{for $u\in{\cal F}$}

(see
[
4
, Lemma 1.3.4]
).

Let

S_{t}f=\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}f\,{\rm d}s,\quad f\in L^{2}(X;m).

Here the integral is defined as the strong convergence limit in
L^{2}(X;m)

of the Riemann sum.
We can then extend
S_{t}
and
T_{t}
from
L^{1}(X;m)\cap L^{2}(X;m)

to
L^{1}(X;m)
uniquely. We use the same notations for such extensions.
Let

L_{+}^{1}(X;m)=\left\{f\in L^{1}(X;m)\mid f\geq 0,\ \text{$m$-a.e.}\right\}

and

Gf=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}S_{N}f,\quad f\in L_{+}^{1}(X;m).

We say that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
(or
({\cal E},{\cal F})
) is
recurrent

if for any
f\in L_{+}^{1}(X;m)
,
either
Gf=0

m
-a.e. or
Gf=\infty

m
-a.e. holds.
We also say that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
(or
({\cal E},{\cal F})
) is
transient
if

Gf<\infty

m
-a.e. for any
f\in L_{+}^{1}(X;m)
.

The next theorem is a comparison principle
for recurrence and transience of Dirichlet forms.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.6.4]
)

Let
({\cal E}^{(1)},{\cal F}^{(1)})
and
({\cal E}^{(2)},{\cal F}^{(2)})
be
Dirichlet forms on
L^{2}(X;m)
such that

{\cal F}^{(2)}\subset{\cal F}^{(1)}
.
If there exists
C>0
such that

{\cal E}^{(1)}(u,u)\leq C{\cal E}^{(2)}(u,u)\quad\text{for any $u\in{\cal F}^{(2)}$,}

then the following assertions hold
:

(1)

if
({\cal E}^{(2)},{\cal F}^{(2)})
is recurrent, then so is
({\cal E}^{(1)},{\cal F}^{(1)})
;

(2)

if
({\cal E}^{(1)},{\cal F}^{(1)})
is transient, then so is
({\cal E}^{(2)},{\cal F}^{(2)})
.

A measurable set
A\subset X
is called (
T_{t}
-)
invariant
if
for any
f\in L^{2}(X;m)
and
t>0
,

T_{t}({\bf 1}_{A}f)={\bf 1}_{A}T_{t}f,\quad\text{$m$-a.e.}

We say that
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
(or
({\cal E},{\cal F})
) is
irreducible

if any invariant set
A\subset X
satisfies
m(A)=0
or
m(X\setminus A)=0
.
We then know

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.6.4 (iii)]
)

If
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is irreducible, then it is recurrent or transient.

In what follows, let
X
be a locally compact separable metric space
and
m
a positive Radon measure on
X
with full support.
Let
X_{\Delta}=X\cup\{\Delta\}
be a one-point compactification of
X
.
We write
{\cal O}
for the totality of open sets in
X
.

Let
{\cal E}_{1}(u,u)={\cal E}(u,u)+\|u\|_{L^{2}(X;m)}^{2}
for
u\in{\cal F}
.
For
A\in{\cal O}
, let

{\cal L}_{A}=\left\{u\in{\cal F}\mid u\geq 1\ \text{$m$-a.e.\ on $A$}\right\}

and

\mathop{\rm Cap}(A)=\begin{cases}\inf\{{\cal E}_{1}(u,u)\mid u\in{\cal L}_{A}\}&\text{if ${\cal L}_{A}\neq\emptyset$,}\\
\infty&\text{if ${\cal L}_{A}=\emptyset$.}\end{cases}

We define the
capacity
of a set
B\subset X
by

\mathop{\rm Cap}(B)=\inf_{A\in{\cal O},A\supset B}\mathop{\rm Cap}(A).

Note that if
B
is measurable with
m(B)>0
, then
\mathop{\rm Cap}(B)>0
by definition.

For
A\subset X
, a statement depending on
x\in A
is said
to hold quasi everywhere (q.e. for short) on
A

if there exists
N\subset X
of zero capacity
such that the statement holds for every
x\in A\setminus N
.

2.2
Probabilistic part

Let
{\mathbf{M}}=(\Omega,{\cal F},\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\{P_{x}\}_{x\in X},\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\zeta)

be a
m
-symmetric Borel right process on
X
.
Here
\zeta=\inf\left\{t>0\mid X_{t}\in\Delta\right\}
is the lifetime of
{\mathbf{M}}

and
\theta_{t}:\Omega\to\Omega
is the shift of paths defined
by the relation
X_{s}\circ\theta_{t}=X_{s+t}
for every
s\geq 0
.

We write
{\cal B}(X)
for the set of all Borel measurable subsets of
X

and

{\cal B}(X_{\Delta})={\cal B}(X)\cup\left\{B\cup\{\Delta\}\mid B\in{\cal B}(X)\right\}.

A set
B\subset X
is called
nearly Borel measurable

if there exist
B_{1},B_{2}\in{\cal B}(X_{\Delta})
such that

B_{1}\subset B\subset B_{2}
and for any probability measure
\mu
on
X_{\Delta}
,

P_{x}(\text{$X_{t}\in B_{2}\setminus B_{1}$ for some $t\geq 0$})=0,\quad\text{$\mu$-a.e.\ $x\in X$}.

For a nearly Borel set
A
,
let
\sigma_{A}=\inf\{t>0\mid X_{t}\in A\}
denote the hitting time of
{\mathbf{M}}
to
A
.
A set
N\subset X
is called
exceptional
if there exists a nearly Borel set
\tilde{N}

such that
\tilde{N}\supset N
and
P_{x}(\sigma_{\tilde{N}}<\infty)=0
for
m
-a.e.
x\in X
.
We say that a set
N\subset X
is
properly exceptional

if it is nearly Borel such that
m(N)=0
and

X\setminus N
is
{\mathbf{M}}
-invariant, that is,

P_{x}(\text{$X_{t}\in(X\setminus N)_{\Delta}$ and $X_{t-}\in(X\setminus N)_{\Delta}$ for any $t>0$})=1,\quad x\in X\setminus N.

Here
(X\setminus N)_{\Delta}=(X\setminus N)\cup\{\Delta\}
and
X_{t-}=\lim_{s\rightarrow t-0}X_{s}
.
Note that any properly exceptional set is exceptional by definition.

Let
\{p_{t}\}_{t>0}
be an
m
-symmetric Markovian transition function of
{\mathbf{M}}

on
(X,{\cal B}(X))
.
Then it uniquely associates an strongly continuous Markovian semigroup
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}

and thus a Dirichlet form
({\cal E},{\cal F})
on
L^{2}(X;m)
(
[
4
, §4.2]
).
The next theorem shows a probabilistic consequence of the recurrence of irreducible Dirichlet forms.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
4
, Theorem 4.7.1 (iii)]
)

If
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is irreducible recurrent,
then for any nearly Borel non-exceptional set
B
,

P_{x}(\sigma_{B}\circ\theta_{n}<\infty\ \text{for any $n\geq 0$})=1,\quad\text{q.e.\ $x\in X$}.

Lemma
2.2
and Theorem
2.3
imply that
an irreducible Dirichlet form
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is transient if there exists a nearly Borel
non-exceptional set
B
such that for any exceptional set
N\subset X
,

P_{x}(\sigma_{B}<\infty)<1
for some
x\in X\setminus N
.

Let
C_{0}(X)
denote the totality of continuous functions on
X
with compact support.
A Dirichlet form
({\cal E},{\cal F})
on
L^{2}(X;m)
is called
regular

if
{\cal F}\cap C_{0}(X)
is dense both in
{\cal F}

with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{{\cal E}_{1}}:=\sqrt{{\cal E}_{1}(u,u)},\quad u\in{\cal F},

(2.1)

and in
C_{0}(X)
with respect to the uniform norm.

Suppose that the Dirichlet form
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is regular.
Then there exists an
m
-symmetric Hunt process
{\mathbf{M}}
on
X

whose Dirichlet form is
({\cal E},{\cal F})
(
[
4
, Theorem 7.2.1]
).
We also know that a set
N\subset X
is exceptional
if and only if
\mathop{\rm Cap}(N)=0
(
[
4
, Theorem 4.2.1]
).

3
Transience

In this section, we provide a sufficient condition
for transience of a non-local Dirichlet form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with unbounded coefficients.
To do so, we separate the small and big jump parts of this Dirichlet form.

3.1
Setting

Let
J(x,{\rm d}y)
be a nonnegative kernel on

({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
.

Assumption 3.1
.

The measure
J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)=J(x,{\rm d}y)\,{\rm d}x
is symmetric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
the function

x\mapsto\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1\wedge|x-y|^{2})J(x,{\rm d}y)

belong to
L_{{\rm loc}}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Let
({\cal E},{\cal D}({\cal E}))
be a quadratic form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
defined by

\begin{split}{\cal D}({\cal E})&=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\,J(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x<\infty\right\},\\
{\cal E}(u,v)&=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\,J(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x,\quad u,v\in{\cal D}({\cal E}).\end{split}

(3.1)

Let
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal E}_{1}}
be the norm on
{\cal D}({\cal E})
defined similarly to (
2.1
),
and let
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the set of smooth functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

with compact support.
Then under Assumption
3.1
,

C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subset{\cal D}({\cal E})
and

({\cal E},C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is closable with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal E}_{1}}
.
Hence if
({\cal E},{\cal F})
denotes
the
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal E}_{1}}
-closure of
({\cal E},C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,
then it is a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Let

J^{(1)}({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)={\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|<1\}}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y),\quad J^{(2)}({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)={\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y).

For
i=1,2
, let
({\cal E}^{(i)},{\cal D}({\cal E}^{(i)}))
be a quadratic form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

defined by

\begin{split}{\cal D}({\cal E}^{(i)})&=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\,J^{(i)}({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)<\infty\right\},\\
{\cal E}^{(i)}(u,v)&=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\,J^{(i)}({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y),\quad u,v\in{\cal D}({\cal E}^{(i)}).\end{split}

Let
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal E}_{1}^{(i)}}
be the norm on
{\cal D}({\cal E}^{(i)})

defined similarly to (
2.1
).
If
({\cal E}^{(i)},{\cal F}^{(i)})
is the
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal E}_{1}^{(i)}}
-closure of

({\cal E}^{(i)},C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
,
then it is a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
By definition, we have for
i=1,2
,

{\cal F}\subset{\cal F}^{(i)}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal E}^{(i)}(u,u)\leq{\cal E}(u,u)\quad\text{for any $u\in{\cal F}$.}

(3.2)

Let
a_{i}(x)\ (i=1,2)
be positive Borel measurable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and

c(x,y)=(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(y)){\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|<1\}}+(a_{2}(x)+a_{2}(y)){\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}.

We further make the next assumption on the jumping measure
J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)
.

Assumption 3.2
.

There exists a kernel
J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

satisfying the following
:

(i)

The measure
J_{0}({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)=J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x
is symmetric.
Moreover, there exists a positive and rotationally invariant
Borel measure
\nu({\rm d}z)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}(1\wedge|z|^{2})\,\nu({\rm d}z)<\infty
and
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
A\in{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

J_{0}(x,x+A)\geq\nu(A).

(3.3)

(ii)

Assumption
3.1
is fulfilled for
the measure
J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)=c(x,y)J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x

with the kernel
J(x,{\rm d}y)=c(x,y)J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y)
.

By assumption,
\nu
is the Lévy measure of a rotationally invariant Lévy process on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
The function
c(x,y)
plays the role of the coefficient for
({\cal E},{\cal F})
.
We note here that if
B\in{\cal B}(X)
satisfies
J(B\times B^{c})=J(B^{c}\times B)=0
,
then
m(B)=0
or
m(B^{c})=0
holds.
Hence
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is irreducible by
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Let

c_{1}(x,y)=(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(y)){\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|<1\}},\quad c_{2}(x,y)=(a_{2}(x)+a_{2}(y)){\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}.

(3.4)

Then under Assumption
3.2
,

J^{(i)}({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)=c_{i}(x,y)J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y)\,{\rm d}x,\quad i=1,2.

Since
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is irreducible,
we see by
[
15
, Example 4.1 and Theorems 1.2 and 2.1 (ii)]
that

({\cal E}^{(i)},{\cal F}^{(i)})
is also irreducible for each
i=1,2

so that it is recurrent or transient by Lemma
2.2
.
Note that by Theorem
2.1
,

({\cal E},{\cal F})
is transient if so is either of
({\cal E}^{(i)},{\cal F}^{(i)})
.

3.2
Small jump part

In this subsection, we prove

Theorem 3.3
.

If there exist
c>0
and
p>(2-d)/2
such that

a_{1}(x)\geq c(1+|x|^{2})^{p}\quad\text{for any $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$,}

then
({\cal E}^{(1)},{\cal F}^{(1)})
is transient.

On account of Theorem
2.1
,
we may assume that the equality holds in
(
3.3
) and

a_{1}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{p}
with some
p\in((2-d)/2,1]
.
For the proof of Theorem
3.3
,
we apply the Lyapunov method
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, Section 6]
for diffusion processes
with singular diffusion coefficients)
by using the martingale characterization
of the generator of
({\cal E}^{(1)},{\cal F}^{(1)})
.

In what follows, we assume that the equality holds in
(
3.3
) and
a_{1}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{p}
for some
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
We first apply a test function to the generator.
Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the standard inner product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
{\cal L}^{(1)}
be a linear operator on
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
defined by

\begin{split}{\cal L}^{(1)}u(x)&=\int_{0<|z|<1}\left(u(x+z)-u(x)-\langle\nabla u(x),z\rangle\right)(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(x+z))\,\nu({\rm d}z)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle\nabla u(x),z\rangle(a_{1}(x+z)-a_{1}(x-z))\,\nu({\rm d}z),\quad(u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).\end{split}

(3.5)

Then by
[
23
, Theorem 2.2]
, we know that for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
v\in{\cal F}
,

{\cal E}^{(1)}(u,v)=(-{\cal L}^{(1)}u,v)_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

In particular,
{\cal L}^{(1)}u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap{\cal B}_{b}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
For any
u\in C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
all the integrals in (
3.5
) are convergent
so that we can define
{\cal L}^{(1)}u
by the expression (
3.5
).

Here we take
\psi_{\delta}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\delta}\ (\delta>0)
as a test function.
Note that
\psi_{\delta}\in C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
p>(2-d)/2
,
then for any
\delta\in(0,p-(2-d)/2)
,
there exist positive constants
C
and
M
such that

{\cal L}^{(1)}\psi_{\delta}(x)\leq-\frac{C\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1-p}},\quad|x|\geq M.

Proof.

Let

{\cal L}^{(a)}\psi_{\delta}(x)=\int_{0<|z|<1}(\psi_{\delta}(x+z)-\psi_{\delta}(x)-\langle\nabla\psi_{\delta}(x),z\rangle)(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(x+z))\,\nu({\rm d}z).

(3.6)

We estimate the right hand side above
in a way similar to
[
24
, Proposition 2.2]
.
Applying the Taylor theorem to
\psi_{\delta}
, we have

\begin{split}&{\cal L}^{(a)}\psi_{\delta}(x)\\
&=4\delta(\delta+1)\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0<|z|<1}\frac{\langle x+sz,z\rangle^{2}}{(1+|x+sz|^{2})^{\delta+2}}(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(x+z))\,\nu({\rm d}z)\right)(1-s)\,{\rm d}s\\
&-2\delta\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0<|z|<1}\frac{|z|^{2}}{(1+|x+sz|^{2})^{\delta+1}}(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(x+z))\,\nu({\rm d}z)\right)(1-s)\,{\rm d}s\\
&={\rm(I)}-{\rm(II)}.\end{split}

Since
a_{1}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{p}
,
there exists
M>0
for any
\varepsilon>0
such that
for any
x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|x|\geq M
,
|z|<1
and
s\in(0,1)
,

\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2-p}}\leq\frac{a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(x+z)}{(1+|x+sz|^{2})^{\delta+2}}\leq\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2-p}}.

(3.7)

Let

c_{*}=\int_{0<|z|<1}|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z),\quad c_{**}=\int_{0<|z|<1}|z|^{4}\,\nu({\rm d}z).

Since the measure
\nu
is rotationally invariant by assumption,
we have

\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle x,z\rangle^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)=\frac{c_{*}}{d}|x|^{2},\quad\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle x,z\rangle|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)=0

so that

\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle x+sz,z\rangle^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)=\frac{c_{*}}{d}|x|^{2}+c_{**}s^{2}.

Hence by (
3.7
),
we obtain for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|x|\geq M
,

\begin{split}{\rm(I)}&\leq 4\delta(\delta+1)\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2-p}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle x+sz,z\rangle^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)\right)(1-s)\,{\rm d}s\\
&=4\delta(\delta+1)\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2-p}}\left(\frac{c_{*}}{d}|x|^{2}+\frac{c_{**}}{6}\right)\leq\frac{4c_{*}\delta(\delta+1)}{d}\frac{(1+2\varepsilon)\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1-p}}.\end{split}

In the same way, we have

{\rm(II)}\geq\frac{2c_{*}\delta(1-\varepsilon)\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1-p}}

so that

{\cal L}^{(a)}\psi_{\delta}(x)={\rm(I)}-{\rm(II)}\leq\frac{2\delta\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1-p}}\frac{c_{*}}{d}\left\{2(\delta+1)(1+2\varepsilon)-d(1-\varepsilon)\right\}.

(3.8)

Let

\begin{split}{\cal L}^{(b)}\psi_{\delta}(x)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle\nabla\psi_{\delta}(x),z\rangle(a_{1}(x+z)-a_{1}(x-z))\,\nu({\rm d}z)\\
&=-\frac{\delta}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\delta+1}}\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle x,z\rangle(a_{1}(x+z)-a_{1}(x-z))\,\nu({\rm d}z).\end{split}

Then by the Taylor theorem,
there exists
\theta\in(0,1)
for any
x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that

\begin{split}a_{1}(x+z)-a_{1}(x-z)&=(1+|x+z|^{2})^{p}-(1+|x-z|^{2})^{p}\\
&=4p(1+|x+2\theta z|^{2})^{p-1}\langle x+2\theta z,z\rangle,\end{split}

and therefore,

{\cal L}^{(b)}\psi_{\delta}(x)=-\frac{4p\delta}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\delta+1}}\int_{0<|z|<1}\left(\langle x,z\rangle^{2}+2\theta\langle x,z\rangle|z|^{2}\right)(1+|x+2\theta z|^{2})^{p-1}\,\nu({\rm d}z).

(3.9)

In a similar way to (
3.7
),
there exists
M>0
for any
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|x|\geq M
,

\frac{(1-\varepsilon)c_{*}}{d}(1+|x|^{2})^{p}\leq\int_{0<|z|<1}\langle x,z\rangle^{2}(1+|x+2\theta z|^{2})^{p-1}\,\nu({\rm d}z)\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)c_{*}}{d}(1+|x|^{2})^{p}.

(3.10)

We also see that there exists
c>0
such that for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|x|\geq M
,

\left|\int_{0<|z|<1}2\theta\langle x,z\rangle|z|^{2}(1+|x+2\theta z|^{2})^{p-1}\,\nu({\rm d}z)\right|\leq c(1+|x|^{2})^{p-1/2}.

Combining this with (
3.10
), we see from (
3.9
) that

{\cal L}^{(b)}\psi_{\delta}(x)\leq 4(-1\pm 2\varepsilon)\frac{pc_{*}\delta}{d}\frac{\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1-p}},\quad|x|\geq M.

(3.11)

Here the sign of
\pm
above is the same with that of
p
.

By (
3.8
) and (
3.11
), we have for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
|x|\geq M
,

\begin{split}{\cal L}^{(1)}\psi_{\delta}(x)&={\cal L}^{(a)}\psi_{\delta}(x)+{\cal L}^{(b)}\psi_{\delta}(x)\\
&\leq-\frac{2\delta\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{1-p}}\frac{c_{*}}{d}\left\{-2(\delta+1)(1+2\varepsilon)+d(1-\varepsilon)-2p(-1\pm 2\varepsilon)\right\}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Then for any
\delta\in(0,p-(2-d)/2)
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,

-2(\delta+1)(1+2\varepsilon)+d(1-\varepsilon)-2p(-1\pm 2\varepsilon)>0.

Hence the proof is complete by (
3.12
).
∎

We next present a lemma which will be used to justify the martingale characterization
of the generator applied to functions in
{\cal A}
.
Let
\gamma(x)=(4+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
.
For
u\in C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, let
H(u)
be the Hessian matrix of
u
.
We say that a function
u\in C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
belongs to the class
{\cal A}
(
u\in{\cal A}
in notation)
if there exist
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that for any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\gamma(x)|\langle\nabla u(x),z\rangle|\right)\leq c_{1}|z|

and

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\gamma(x)^{2}|\langle H(u)(x)z,z\rangle|\right)\leq c_{2}|z|^{2}.

Note that for any
\delta>0
,
the function
\psi_{\delta}
belongs to the class
{\cal A}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists a sequence

\{\phi_{n}\}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

such that if
p\leq 1
, then for any
f\in{\cal A}
,

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|{\cal L}^{(1)}(f\phi_{n})\|_{\infty}<\infty

(3.13)

and

{\cal L}^{(1)}(f\phi_{n})(x)\rightarrow({\cal L}^{(1)}f)(x)\ (n\rightarrow\infty)\quad\text{for any $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}$}.

(3.14)

Proof.

Let
w\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfy
0\leq w(t)\leq 1
for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

and

w(t)=\begin{cases}1&(|t|\leq 1),\\
0&(|t|\geq 2).\end{cases}

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\phi_{n}(x)=w(|x|/n)\ (x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then for any
f\in{\cal A}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we have
f\phi_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\begin{split}f\phi_{n}(x+z)-f\phi_{n}(x)-\langle\nabla(f\phi_{n})(x),z\rangle&=f(x+z)\left(\phi_{n}(x+z)-\phi_{n}(x)-\langle\nabla\phi_{n}(x),z\rangle\right)\\
&+\phi_{n}(x)\left(f(x+z)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),z\rangle\right)\\
&+\langle\nabla\phi_{n}(x),z\rangle(f(x+z)-f(x)).\end{split}

(3.15)

In the same way to
[
23
, Proof of Theorem 2.3]
,
there exist
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\gamma(x)|\langle\nabla\phi_{n}(x),z\rangle|\right)\leq c_{1}|z|

and

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\gamma(x)^{2}|\langle H(\phi_{n})(x)z,z\rangle|\right)\leq c_{2}|z|^{2}.

Hence
\phi_{n}\in{\cal A}
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then by (
3.15
) and the Taylor theorem,
there exists
c_{3}>0
such that for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
for any
x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
0<|z|<\gamma(x)/2
,

\begin{split}&|f\phi_{n}(x+z)-f\phi_{n}(x)-\langle\nabla(f\phi_{n})(x),z\rangle|\\
&\leq|f(x+z)\left(\phi_{n}(x+z)-\phi_{n}(x)-\langle\nabla\phi_{n}(x),z\rangle\right)|\\
&+|\phi_{n}(x)\left(f(x+z)-f(x)-\langle\nabla f(x),z\rangle\right)|\\
&+|\langle\nabla\phi_{n}(x),z\rangle(f(x+z)-f(x))|\\
&\leq c_{3}|z|^{2}/\gamma(x)^{2}.\end{split}

(3.16)

This in particular implies that for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

\begin{split}&\int_{0<|z|<1}\left|f\phi_{n}(x+z)-f\phi_{n}(x)-\langle\nabla f\phi_{n}(x),z\rangle\right|(a_{1}(x)+a_{1}(x+z))\,\nu({\rm d}z)\\
&\leq\frac{c_{4}a_{1}(x)}{\gamma(x)^{2}}\int_{0<|z|<1}|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)\leq c_{5}(1+|x|^{2})^{p-1}\leq c_{6}.\end{split}

By the Taylor theorem again,
there exists
c_{6}>0
such that

|a_{1}(x+z)-a_{1}(x-z)|\leq c_{6}(1+|x|^{2})^{p-1/2}|z|,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ 0<|z|<1.

Since there exists
c_{7}>0
such that for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

|\langle\nabla(f\phi_{n})(x),z\rangle|\leq c_{7}|z|/\gamma(x),\quad x,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

(3.17)

we have

\begin{split}\int_{0<|z|<1}\left|\langle\nabla(f\phi_{n})(x),z\rangle(a_{1}(x+z)-a_{1}(x-z))\right|\,\nu({\rm d}z)&\leq c_{8}(1+|x|^{2})^{p-1}\leq c_{9}.\end{split}

so that (
3.13
) holds.
Then (
3.14
) follows
by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Let
p\leq 1
.
Then according to the calculation in the proof of Lemma
3.5
,
there exists
c>0
for any
f\in{\cal A}

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Assume that the equality holds in
(
3.3
) and

a_{1}(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{p}
with some
p\in((2-d)/2,1]
.
Let
{\mathbf{M}}=(\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\{P_{x}\}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})

be a symmetric Hunt process on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

generated by
({\cal E}^{(1)},{\cal F}^{(1)})
.
Then
{\mathbf{M}}
is conservative by
[
23
, Theorem 2.3]
.

According to the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
,
there exists a properly exceptional set
N\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
such that
for any
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

E_{x}\left[u(X_{t})\right]-u(x)=E_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\cal L}^{(1)}u(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right],\quad t\geq 0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus N.

, this equality is valid by Lemma
3.5

so that a stochastic process

For

, let

and
\sigma_{r}=\inf\{t\geq 0\mid|X_{t}|\leq r\}

{\mathbf{M}}=(\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\{P_{x}\}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus N})
.
Recall that for any
\delta>0
,
the function
\psi_{\delta}=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\delta}
belongs to the class
{\cal A}

so that

.
Hence by the optional stopping theorem
applied to the martingale

, we obtain

E_{x}\left[\psi_{\delta}(X_{t\wedge\sigma_{r}})\right]=\psi_{\delta}(x)+E_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\sigma_{r}}{\cal L}^{(1)}\psi_{\delta}(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right].

(3.18)

Let
\delta\in(0,p-(2-d)/2)
.
Then by Proposition
3.4
,
there exists
r>0
such that

{\cal L}^{(1)}\psi_{\delta}(z)\leq 0
for any
z\in B(r)^{c}
.
Fix
R>r
and

E_{x}\left[\psi_{\delta}(X_{t\wedge\sigma_{r}})\right]\geq E_{x}\left[\psi_{\delta}(X_{\sigma_{r}});\sigma_{r}\leq t\right]\geq\left(\inf_{y\in\overline{B(r)}}\psi_{\delta}(y)\right)P_{x}(\sigma_{r}\leq t)

and

\begin{split}\psi_{\delta}(x)+E_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\sigma_{r}}{\cal L}^{(1)}\psi_{\delta}(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s\right]=\psi_{\delta}(x)+E_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{t\wedge\sigma_{r}}{\cal L}^{(1)}\psi_{\delta}(X_{s-})\,{\rm d}s\right]\leq\sup_{w\in B(R)^{c}}\psi_{\delta}(w),\end{split}

we have by (

),

Letting

so that

.

.

3.3

In this subsection, we assume the next condition

Assumption 3.7
.

The measure

is the restriction on

of
the Lévy measure of a symmetric

We now prove

Theorem 3.8
.

If there exist

and

In the similar way as Theorem
3.3
,

On account of Theorem
2.1
, we may and do assume that the equality holds in (
3.3
) and

for some

.
Let

Then by

, Theorem 2.2]
again,

and

,

In particular,
we can also define

for

Lemma 3.9
.

There exists a sequence

\{\phi_{n}\}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be

.
According to
[

,
we have for any

\begin{split}{\cal L}^{(2)}u(x)&=\int_{1\leq|z|<\gamma(x)/2}\left(u(x+z)-u(x)-\langle\nabla u(x),z\rangle\right)\frac{a_{2}(x)+a_{2}(x+z)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\,{\rm d}z\\

) holds.
We also have (
3.22
) by (

) and the dominated convergence theorem.

Proposition 3.11

,

.

&=\psi_{\delta}(x)a_{2}(x)\int_{|z|\geq 1}\left[\frac{\psi_{\delta}(x+z)}{\psi_{\delta}(x)}-1\right]\frac{1+a_{2}(x+z)/a_{2}(x)}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\,{\rm d}z\\
&=\psi_{\delta}(x)a_{2}(x)\int_{|z|\geq 1}\left[\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{\delta}}{(1+|x+z|^{2})^{\delta}}-1\right]\frac{1+(1+|x+z|^{2})^{q}/(1+|x|^{2})^{q}}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}\,{\rm d}z\\
&=\frac{\omega_{d-2}\psi_{\delta}(x)}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2-q}}\int_{1/{\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}}}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{q}(\delta,x,r,s)(1-s^{2})^{(d-3)/2}\,{\rm d}s\right)\frac{{\rm d}r}{r^{1+\alpha}}.\end{split}

(3.26)

At the fourth equality above
we used the change of variables formula (
z=\langle x\rangle u
)
and then the polar coordinate expression.

Let

F_{q}(\delta,x)=\int_{1/{\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}}}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}J_{q}(\delta,x,r,s)(1-s^{2})^{(d-3)/2}\,{\rm d}s\right)\frac{{\rm d}r}{r^{1+\alpha}},\quad\delta\geq 0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Let
\tilde{J}_{q}(\delta,r,s)=\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}J_{q}(\delta,x,r,s)
and

\Lambda_{q}(\delta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{J}_{q}(\delta,r,s)(1-s^{2})^{(d-3)/2}\,{\rm d}s\right)\frac{{\rm d}r}{r^{1+\alpha}},\quad\delta\geq 0.

Note that

\begin{split}\tilde{J}_{q}(\delta,r,s)&=\left[\frac{1}{(r^{2}+2rs+1)^{\delta}}-1\right](1+(r^{2}+2rs+1)^{q})\\
&+\left[\frac{1}{(r^{2}-2rs+1)^{\delta}}-1\right](1+(r^{2}-2rs+1)^{q}),\quad\delta\geq 0,\ r\geq 0,\ s\in[0,1).\end{split}

We then define

K_{q}(\delta,r,s)=\frac{\log(r^{2}+2rs+1)}{(r^{2}+2rs+1)^{\delta}}[1+(r^{2}+2rs+1)^{q}],\quad\delta\in{\mathbb{R}},\ r\geq 0,\ s\in(-1,1).

Lemma 3.12

for non-local Dirichlet forms
established in

A.1
Recurrence criterion

We first recall a necessary and sufficient condition for recurrence of regular Dirichlet forms
in terms of the capacity (
[
16
,
17
]
).
Let
X
be a locally compact separable metric space and

m
a positive Radon measure on
X
with full support.
Let
X_{\Delta}=X\cup\{\Delta\}
denote the one point compactification of
X
.
Let
({\cal E},{\cal F})
be a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(X;m)
.
We say that a function
u
on
X
belongs locally to
{\cal F}

if for any relatively compact set
G
in
X
,
there exists
u_{G}\in{\cal F}
such that
u=u_{G}
,
m
-a.e. on
G
.
Let
{\cal F}_{{\rm loc}}
denote the totality of such functions.
In what follows, we impose the next assumption on
({\cal E},{\cal F})
:

Assumption A.1
.

There exists a nonnegative function
\rho
on
X
such that

(i)

\rho\in{\cal F}_{{\rm loc}}
and
\rho(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\Delta
;

(ii)

For any
r>0
, the set
B_{\rho}(r):=\{y\in X\mid\rho(y)<r\}
is relatively compact in
X
.

Fix a function
\rho
satisfying Assumption
A.1
.
For
r,R>0\ (0<r<R)
, we define

{\mathbf{C}}(r,R)=\inf\left\{{\cal E}(u,u)\mid u\in{\cal F}\cap C_{0}(X),\ \text{$u\geq 1$ on $\overline{B_{\rho}(r)}$ and $u=0$ on $B_{\rho}(R)^{c}$}\right\}.

Theorem A.2
.

(
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
17
, Theorem 1]
)

Under Assumption
A.1
,

({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent if and only if
for each fixed
r>0
,

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbf{C}}(r,R)=0.

Using this theorem, we show a recurrence criterion for regular Dirichlet forms.
Let
{\rm diag}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X\mid x=y\}
.
We now suppose that
({\cal E},{\cal F})
has no killing measure
in the Beurling-Deny representation
(
[
4
, Theorem 3.2.1 and Lemma 4.5.4]

there exist a symmetric form
({\cal E}^{(c)},{\cal F}\cap C_{0}(X))

with the strongly local property (see
[
4
, p.120]
for definition) and
a symmetric positive Radon measure

on
X\times X\setminus{\rm diag}
such that

{\cal E}(u,u)={\cal E}^{(c)}(u,u)+\iint_{X\times X\setminus{\rm diag}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y),\quad u\in{\cal F}\cap C_{0}(X).

We can then extend
{\cal E}^{(c)}
uniquely to
{\cal F}
.
Moreover, for each
u\in{\cal F}
,
there exists a positive Radon measure
\mu_{\langle u\rangle}^{c}
on
X

such that
{\cal E}^{(c)}(u,u)=\mu_{\langle u\rangle}^{c}(X)/2
(
[
4
, p.123]

Let
B(r)=B_{\rho}(r)
.
We then define

M_{0}(r)=\mu_{\langle\rho\rangle}^{c}(B(r)),\quad M_{1}(r)=\iint_{B(r)\times B(r)}(\rho(x)-\rho(y))^{2}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y),\quad r>0

Theorem A.3
.

Assume that there exist
c_{0}>1
,
r_{0}>1
, and
a positive continuous and nondecreasing function

on

which is slowly varying at infinity,

(A.1)

Then there exists
K>0
such that for any

with

(A.2)

In particular,

is recurrent if

(A.3)

and
[
19

L(r)
is continuous and slowly varying at infinity.
Therefore, by the uniform convergence theorem (
[
1
, Theorem 1.2.1]
)
or
[
19
, Lemma 5.4]
,
there exist
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0

for any positive constants
a
,
b
with
0<a<b
, such that

C_{1}\leq\frac{L(\lambda r)}{L(r)}\leq C_{2},\quad r\geq 1,\ \lambda\in[a,b].

Furthermore, since
s_{n+1}/s_{n-1}=c^{2}
and
s_{n}/s_{n-1}=c
for any
n\geq 1
,
we see by (
A.1
) that for some
c_{2}>0
,

\frac{1}{s_{n}^{2}}(M_{0}(s_{n})+M_{1}(s_{n+1}))+M_{2}(s_{n},c_{0}s_{n})\leq c_{2}L(s_{n-1}),\quad n=1,\dots,N.

(A.6)

Let

C=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{L(s_{n-1})}\right)^{-1},\quad p_{n}=\frac{C}{L(s_{n-1})}

and

u_{N}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_{n}\varphi_{n}.

Then
[
1
, Theorem 1.5.4]
shows that for any
\alpha>0
,
there exists a nonincreasing function
\phi
on
[r_{0},\infty)
such that

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}L(r)/(r^{\alpha}\phi(r))=1
.
Therefore,
there exists
c_{\alpha}>0
such that for any
\kappa>1
,
s\geq r_{0}
and
t\geq r_{0}
with

s\leq t\leq\kappa s
,

L(s)=s^{\alpha}\frac{L(s)}{s^{\alpha}}\geq c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{t}{\kappa}\right)^{\alpha}\frac{L(t)}{t^{\alpha}}=\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\kappa^{\alpha}}L(t).

Since
L(s)
is nondecreasing by assumption,
we have by (
A.5
) and (
A.6
),

\begin{split}{\cal E}(u_{N},u_{N})&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_{n}^{2}{\cal E}(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})+2\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}p_{n}\sum_{l=n+1}^{N}p_{l}{\cal E}(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{l})\\
&\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_{n}^{2}\left({\cal E}(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})+2\sum_{l=n+1}^{N}|{\cal E}(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{l})|\right)\\
&\leq c_{3}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{C^{2}}{L(s_{n-1})}=c_{3}C\leq c_{3}\left(\frac{1}{c-1}\int_{r}^{R}\frac{{\rm d}s}{sL(s)}\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

As
{\mathbf{C}}(r,R)\leq{\cal E}(u_{N},u_{N})
,
we obtain (
A.2
).
In particular, if (
A.3
) holds, then
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent
by (
A.2
) and Theorem
A.2
.
∎

A.2
Application

In this subsection, we apply Theorem
A.3
to
regular Dirichlet forms on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

with unbounded/degenerate coefficients.
Let
({\cal E},{\cal F})
be a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as in Subsection
3.1

with Assumption
3.2
replaced by the next assumption:

Assumption A.4
.

There exists a kernel
J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

satisfying the following
:

(i)

Assumption
3.2
holds with the inequality (
3.3
)
replaced by the reversed one
;
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
A\in{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}})
,

J_{0}(x,x+A)\leq\nu(A).

(A.7)

(ii)

There exists a positive Borel measurable function
f
on
(0,\infty)

such that

{\bf 1}_{\{|y|\geq 1\}}\nu({\rm d}y)=f(|y|)\,{\rm d}y.

Moreover, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
,
c_{4}
such that
for any positive constants
a,b
with
c_{1}\leq b/a\leq c_{2}
, then
c_{3}\leq f(b)/f(a)\leq c_{4}
.

Under Assumption
A.4
,
Theorem
A.3
is applicable to
({\cal E},{\cal F})
because
it fulfills Assumption
A.1
with
\rho(x)=|x|
.

Let
B(r)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid|x|<r\}
.
By the symmetry of the measure
J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x
and (
A.7
),
we have

\begin{split}&\iint_{B(s)\times B(s)}|x-y|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|<1\}}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)\\
&=2\iint_{B(s)\times B(s)}a_{1}(x)|x-y|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|<1\}}\,J_{0}(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x\\
&\leq 2\int_{B(s)}a_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x\int_{0<|z|<1}|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)\end{split}

(A.8)

and

\begin{split}\iint_{B(s)\times B(s)}|x-y|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)\leq 2\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x\int_{1\leq|z|<2s}|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z).\end{split}

Hence

\begin{split}&\iint_{B(s)\times B(s)}|x-y|^{2}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)\\
&\leq 2\left(\int_{B(s)}a_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x\int_{0<|z|<1}|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)+\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x\int_{1\leq|z|<2s}|z|^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}z)\right).\end{split}

Let
c_{0}>2
and
r_{0}>1
.
Since
(c_{0}-1)r_{0}>1
, we have for any
s\geq r_{0}
,

\iint_{B(s)\times B(c_{0}s)^{c}}\log\left(\frac{|y|}{c_{0}s}\right){\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|<1\}}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)=0.

Note that for any
x\in B(s)
and
y\in B(c_{0}s)^{c}
,
we have
|x-y|/2\leq|y|\leq 2|x-y|
,
which implies that
f(y)/f(y-x)
is bounded from below and above by positive constants.
Then, in a similar way to (
A.8
), we also obtain

\begin{split}&\iint_{B(s)\times B(c_{0}s)^{c}}\log\left(\frac{|y|}{c_{0}s}\right){\bf 1}_{\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}\,J({\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y)\\
&\leq\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x\int_{|y|\geq(c_{0}-1)s}\log\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)\,\nu({\rm d}y)+\omega_{d}s^{d}\int_{|y|\geq(c_{0}-1)s}a_{2}(y)\log\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)\,\nu({\rm d}y).\end{split}

In particular, if we let

\begin{split}N(s)&=\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{B(s)}a_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x\\
&+\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x\left[\int_{1\leq|y|<s}\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)^{2}\,\nu({\rm d}y)+\int_{|y|\geq s}\log\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)\,\nu({\rm d}y)\right]\\
&+s^{d}\int_{|y|\geq s}a_{2}(y)\log\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)\,\nu({\rm d}y),\end{split}

(A.9)

then by Theorem
A.3
,

({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent if
\int_{\cdot}^{\infty}(sN(s))^{-1}\,{\rm d}s=\infty
.

Example A.5
.

We show a recurrence criterion in terms of the coefficient growth at infinity.
Even though the first example below is already given in
[
19
, Example 4.4]

under the general setting,
we state it for the sake of completeness.

(1)

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\beta\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
J(x,{\rm d}y)
satisfy Assumption
A.4
with

\nu({\rm d}z)={\bf 1}_{\{0<|z|<1\}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{|z|^{d+\alpha}}+{\bf 1}_{\{|z|\geq 1\}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{|z|^{d+\beta}}.

Assume first that
\beta\neq 2
. Then for some
c>0
,

N(s)\leq c\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{B(s)}a_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{s^{\beta\wedge 2}}\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x+s^{d}\int_{|y|\geq s}\frac{a_{2}(y)}{|y|^{d+\beta}}\log\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)\,{\rm d}y\right).

Hence if there exist
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

a_{1}(x)\leq c_{1}(1+|x|)^{2-d}\log(2+|x|),\quad a_{2}(x)\leq c_{2}(1+|x|)^{(\beta\wedge 2)-d}\log(2+|x|),

(A.10)

then there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

N(s)\leq c_{3}\log(2+s)
for any
s\geq 2
.
Since
\int_{2}^{\infty}(sN(s))^{-1}\,{\rm d}s=\infty
,

({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent.

Assume next that
\beta=2
. Then

N(s)\leq c\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{B(s)}a_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{\log s}{s^{2}}\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x+s^{d}\int_{|y|\geq s}\frac{a_{2}(y)}{|y|^{d+2}}\log\left(\frac{|y|}{s}\right)\,{\rm d}y\right).

In particular, if there exist
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

a_{1}(x)\leq c_{1}(1+|x|)^{2-d}\log(2+|x|),\quad a_{2}(x)\leq c_{2}(1+|x|)^{2-d}\log(\log(3+|x|)),

then there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

N(s)\leq c_{3}\log(2+s)\log\log(2+s)
for any
s\geq 2
.
Since
\int_{2}^{\infty}(sN(s))^{-1}\,{\rm d}s=\infty
,
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent.

We note that a similar result is valid for
\nu({\rm d}z)={\bf 1}_{\{0<|z|<1\}}|z|^{-(d+\alpha)}\,{\rm d}z

or
\nu({\rm d}z)={\bf 1}_{\{|z|\geq 1\}}|z|^{-(d+\beta)}\,{\rm d}z
.

(2)

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\in(0,\infty)
.
If

\nu({\rm d}z)={\bf 1}_{\{0<|z|<1\}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{|z|^{d+2}(\log(2+|z|))^{\alpha}}+{\bf 1}_{\{|z|\geq 1\}}\frac{{\rm d}z}{|z|^{d}(\log(2+|z|))^{\beta+2}},

then for some
c>0
,

\begin{split}&N(s)\\
&\leq c\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{B(s)}a_{1}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{(\log s)^{\beta}}\int_{B(s)}a_{2}(x)\,{\rm d}x+s^{d}\int_{|y|\geq s}\frac{a_{2}(y)\log(|y|/s)}{|y|^{d}\log(2+|y|)^{\beta+2}}\right).\end{split}

If there exist
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

a_{1}(x)\leq c_{1}(1+|x|)^{2-d}\log(2+|x|),\quad a_{2}(x)\leq c_{2}\frac{(\log(2+|x|))^{\beta}}{(1+|x|)^{d}},

then there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

N(s)\leq c_{3}\log(2+s)
for any
s\geq 2
.
Since
\int_{2}^{\infty}(sN(s))^{-1}\,{\rm d}s=\infty
,
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent.

Example A.6
.

Let
\{a_{ij}(x)\}_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
be a family of locally integrable Borel measurable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

such that the matrix
(a_{ij}(x))_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
is symmetric and nonnegative definite for each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Assume that
\{a_{ij}(x)\}_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
is locally uniformly elliptic
and
J(x,{\rm d}y)
satisfies Assumption
3.1
.
Under this assumption, if we define the quadratic form
({\cal E},C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
by

{\cal E}(u,u)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}(x)\,{\rm d}x+\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\,J(x,{\rm d}y){\rm d}x,

then it is closable on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
its closure
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is a regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

We now assume that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq c(1+|x|)^{2-d}\log(2+|x|)|\xi|^{2},\quad x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

for some
c>0
and
J(x,{\rm d}y)
satisfies the same condition as in Example
A.5
(1).
Then
({\cal E},{\cal F})
is recurrent by Theorem
A.3
.

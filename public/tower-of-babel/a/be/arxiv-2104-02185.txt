Auxiliary Function Approach for Determining Symmetry Energy at Supra-saturation Densities
Bao-Jun Cai, Bao-An Li

Abstract
Nuclear symmetry energy E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) at density ρ \rho is normally expanded or simply parameterized as a function of χ = ( ρ − ρ 0 ) / 3 ​ ρ 0 \chi=(\rho-\rho_{0})/3\rho_{0} in the form of E sym ​ ( ρ ) ≈ S + L ​ χ + 2 − 1 ​ K sym ​ χ 2 + 6 − 1 ​ J sym ​ χ 3 + ⋯ E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx S+L\chi+2^{-1}K_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{2}+6^{-1}J_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{3}+\cdots using its magnitude S S , slope L L , curvature K sym K_{\textrm{sym}} and skewness J sym J_{\textrm{sym}} at the saturation density ρ 0 \rho_{0} of nuclear matter. Much progress has been made in recent years in constraining especially the S S and L L parameters using various terrestrial experiments and astrophysical observations. However, such kind of expansions/parameterizations do not converge at supra-saturation densities where χ \chi is not small enough, hindering an accurate determination of high-density E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) even if its characteristic parameters at ρ 0 \rho_{0} are all well determined by experiments/observations. By expanding the E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) in terms of a properly chosen auxiliary function Π sym ​ ( χ , Θ sym ) \Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}) with a parameter Θ sym \Theta_{\textrm{sym}} fixed accurately by an experimental E sym ​ ( ρ r ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{r}}) value at a reference density ρ r \rho_{\textrm{r}} , we show that the shortcomings of the χ \chi -expansion can be completely removed or significantly reduced in determining the high-density behavior of E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) . In particular, using two significantly different auxiliary functions, we show that the new approach effectively incorporates higher χ \chi -order contributions and converges to the same E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) much faster than the conventional χ \chi -expansion at densities ≲ 3 ​ ρ 0 \lesssim 3\rho_{0} . Moreover, the still poorly constrained skewness J sym J_{\textrm{sym}} plays a small role in determining the E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) at these densities in the auxiliary function approach. The new approach thus provides a nearly model-independent constraint on the E sym ​ ( ρ ) E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho) at densities ≲ 3 ​ ρ 0 \lesssim 3\rho_{0} . Several quantitative demonstrations using Monte Carlo simulations are given.

E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)

Despite of many fruitful applications of expansions and/or parametrizations using forms similar to the Eq. (
1

\chi
becomes large as
\rho

1

E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at supra-saturation densities using Eq. (
1

\rho_{0}

\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\chi

\chi
-expansion in determining the high-density
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)

\chi
-expansion and our strategy to solve them for the purpose of determining
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
up to about
3\rho_{0}

are given. In section
IV

5\rho_{0}
, while the conventional expansion already breaks down near
2\rho_{0}
. In section
V

E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at
\rho_{0}
. A brief summary and outlook are given in section
VI
.

II
The main issues and our strategies

During the last two decades, much progress has been made in constraining especially the low-order parameters of nuclear symmetry energy, e.g.,
the magnitude
S
and the slope
L
are relatively well constrained to the ranges of
S\approx 31.7\pm 3.2\,\textrm{MeV}
and
L\approx 58.7\pm 28.1\,\textrm{MeV}

Oer17
;
LiBA13
with few exceptions, respectively.
Moreover, the curvature
K_{\textrm{sym}}
is found to be effectively correlated with some neutron star properties
Zhang18
;
Xie19
;
Xie20
;
YZhou19
;
YZhou19-a
, and its value has been shown to be negative.
For instance, Bayesian analyses of the tidal deformation of canonical neutron stars from GW170817 and the radius data from NICER (Neutron Star Interior Explorer) found a value of
K_{\mathrm{sym}}=-120_{-100}^{+80}
at 68% confidence level
Xie20
. In addition, a recent Bayesian analysis on some theoretical calculations gave a skewness
J_{\textrm{sym}}\approx 90\pm 334
MeV at 68% confidence level
Som20
, see also the constraint
J_{\textrm{sym}}\approx 296.8\pm 73.6\,\textrm{MeV}
by analyzing the systematics of over 520 energy density functionals
Mon17
, indicating that the skewness
J_{\textrm{sym}}
is probably positive.

Given the above information about the characteristics of
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at
\rho_{0}
, the expression (
1
) provides the simplest way to predict the symmetry energy at supra-saturation densities. However, to what accuracies and up to what densities it can be used have been uncertain because the dimensionless quantity
\chi
becomes eventually large and then the
\chi
-expansion diverges as
\rho
increases. Thus, despite of the progress made in constraining the characteristics
S,L,K_{\textrm{sym}}
and even the
J_{\textrm{sym}}
, there still remain some fundamental issues related to the expansion (
1
). Moreover, a few natural questions concerning the structure and implications of the expansion (
1
) emerge:

(a)

Are the characteristics
(S,L,K_{\textrm{sym}},J_{\textrm{sym}},\cdots)
enough
to describe the symmetry energy at supra-saturation densities, such as
\rho\approx 2\rho_{0}
or
\rho\approx 3\rho_{0}
?
Are some of them irrelevant for
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at densities up to
3\rho_{0}
? The
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
around
(1-3)\rho_{0}
is most important for the radii and tidal deformations of canonical neutron stars but is currently poorly determined
BALI19
. Above this density range, non-nucleonic degrees of freedom become important and various phase transitions may set in.
The answer to the last question is definitely “no” in the conventional expansion (
1
) since
\chi=2/3
at
\rho=3\rho_{0}
and a small change of the skewness
J_{\textrm{sym}}
may easily introduce sizable effects on
E_{\textrm{sym}}(3\rho_{0})
.

(b)

Can we find other forms to re-express the symmetry energy to make the corresponding expansion
based on the same quantities
(S,L,K_{\textrm{sym}},J_{\textrm{sym}},\cdots)
quickly converge and more accurately describe the symmetry energy at supra-saturation densities? It does not mean that exact functionals and/or theories for the high-density asymmetric nuclear matter (ANM) EOS are not needed. Our main purpose is to find once the characteristics like
L
and
K_{\textrm{sym}}
are well constrained, if we can make some (near) model-independent predictions for the
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at densities
\lesssim 2\rho_{0}
or
3\rho_{0}
? Intuitively, it should be, however it is known that different models often predict (very) different high-density behaviors for
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
even when they predict very similar or the same characteristics for
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at
\rho_{0}
. In this sense, we would like to study if we can “reconstruct” accurately the symmetry energy at supra-saturation densities based on its known characteristics at
\rho_{0}
constrained by experiments/observations instead of calculating it based on any nuclear many-body theory.

(c)

In searching for new forms of
f(\rho)
to reconstruct the symmetry energy
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)=S+f(\rho)
, a natural boundary condition is that the first several terms of
f(\rho)
expanded around
\chi=0
should be the same as the ones given by the conventional expansion (
1
). However, to go beyond the latter, certain higher
\chi
-order contributions should also be effectively encapsulated in
f(\rho)
using still only the first few characteristics of
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at
\rho_{0}
, i.e.,
L,K_{\textrm{sym}}
and
J_{\textrm{sym}}
.

The breakdown of the conventional expansions (
1
) is due to the fact that the
\chi
is not always small enough for small-quantity expansions.
Even if one re-scales the
\chi
to be
\widetilde{\chi}=(\rho-\rho_{0})/\xi\rho_{0}
with
\xi\geq 3
a constant, the corresponding terms in (
1
) still have their original forms, e.g.,
2^{-1}K_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{2}\to(\xi^{2}/18)K_{\textrm{sym}}\widetilde{\chi}^{2}
. The adjusted expansion shares the same shortcomings as the original one.
On the other hand, if we adopt an effective auxiliary function of
\chi
, i.e.,
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
with
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
a model parameter to be determined at a reference density
\rho_{\textrm{r}}
where the
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{r}})
is well constrained by experimental data,
and then expand the symmetry energy around the difference
\widetilde{\nu}_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})-\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
,

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}E_{\textrm{sym}}}{\mathrm{d}\rho^{n}}(\rho-\rho_{0})^{n}\rightarrow\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}E_{\textrm{sym}}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}^{n}}\widetilde{\nu}_{\textrm{sym}}^{n}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}),

(2)

some new possibilities emerge:

(a)

If the function
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
is selected well, then expanding it around
\chi\approx 0
gives,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\approx

\displaystyle\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})+\Pi^{\prime}_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\chi

\displaystyle+\Pi^{\prime\prime}_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\chi^{2}/2

\displaystyle+\Pi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\chi^{3}/6+\cdots,

(3)

where the prime “
′
” denotes derivatives with respect to
\chi
(or equivalently with respect to the density
\rho
).
Although the symmetry energy is expanded, e.g., to order
\chi^{3}
,
the
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
can effectively generates higher order terms in
\chi
.
If the factor
\widetilde{\nu}_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
is small enough at supra-saturation densities, the auxiliary function expansion is naturally expected to
converge faster than the conventional expansion (
1
).
Of course, a reasonable auxiliary function
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
has to be chosen to meet this goal.

(b)

The question of model dependence related to choosing the auxiliary function
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
and determining the associated
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
parameter emerges naturally since the auxiliary function could take vastly different forms. Thus, one should compare results of using very different auxiliary functions. Once a model
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
is adopted/selected, the parameter
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
can be determined by the symmetry energy at a density where it is well determined, similar to determining the low-energy coefficients in chiral effective field theories by some low-energy scattering processes
Bur21
. Nevertheless, logically one should self-consistently determine the values of
S,L,K_{\textrm{sym}},\cdots
and
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
using a certain selected auxiliary function model simultaneously (via analyzing nuclear experimental data and/or astrophysical observations) and see how the symmetry energy depends on the form of the auxiliary function (at supra-saturation densities). As the first step in our exploratory study in this direction, here we merely investigate whether one could reconstruct the
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at supra-saturation densities
\lesssim 3\rho_{0}
in an effective manner once the lower-order characteristics are known without caring about how these characteristics are constrained. Future works along this line should consider more about the self-consistency of the approach.

In the following, we use two significantly different auxiliary functions, namely an exponential and an algebraic model. Interestingly, we find that the predicted symmetry energies at supra-saturation densities
\rho_{0}\lesssim\rho\lesssim 3\rho_{0}
are almost the same with the two models, indicating that the auxiliary-function-based reconstruction is effective and has some essential universality. Moreover, in our Monte Carlo simulations the auxiliary-function-based expansion indeed converges faster than the conventional
\chi
-expansion.

III
Framework and Formalism

Given the four characteristic parameters
S,L,K_{\textrm{sym}}
and
J_{\textrm{sym}}
of
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at
\rho_{0}
, the symmetry energy
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
can be expanded around
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
to order
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
as

\displaystyle E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx

\displaystyle S+L\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})+\frac{1}{2}K_{\textrm{sym}}\Phi\nu^{2}_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}J_{\textrm{sym}}\Psi\nu^{3}_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}),

(4)

where

\displaystyle\Phi=

\displaystyle 1+\left.\frac{L}{K_{\textrm{sym}}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}}}\right)\right/{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}}\right)^{2}}_{\chi=0},

(5)

\displaystyle\Psi=

\displaystyle 1+\left.\frac{K_{\textrm{sym}}}{J_{\textrm{sym}}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}}}\right)\right/{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}}\right)^{3}}_{\chi=0}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{L}{J_{\textrm{sym}}}\left({\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\rho}\frac{\partial^{3}\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}}}\right)\right/{\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}}\right)^{3}}_{\chi=0},

(6)

and,

\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\equiv\left[\frac{1}{3\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})}\right]_{\chi=0}\cdot\widetilde{\nu}_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}),

(7)

where
\widetilde{\nu}_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})-\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(0,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
.
It can be proved straightforwardly that the conventional expansion (
1
) corresponds to the special case of selecting
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\propto\chi
.
However, terms higher than
\chi^{3}
are effectively included in (
4
) even it is truncated at order
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
, since the latter itself encapsulates the higher order effects in
\chi
.
Moreover, the
K_{\textrm{sym}}\Phi
and
J_{\textrm{sym}}\Psi
in (
4
) can be treated as the effective curvature and skewness of the symmetry energy with respect to the expansion in
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
.

While the expansion (
4
) is general, its applications depend on the specific form of
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
to be adopted.
In the following, we consider two models, namely the exponential model (abbreviated as “exp”) and the algebraic model (abbreviated as “alge”). Specifically,

1.

In the exponential model,
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=\exp[-\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}(1+3\chi)]
, the
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}
is given as

\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=\frac{1}{3\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}}\left(1-e^{-3\chi\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}}\right).

(8)

The resulting auxiliary-function-based reconstruction of the symmetry energy is given by

\displaystyle E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx

\displaystyle S+L\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}K_{\textrm{sym}}\left(1+\frac{3L}{K_{\textrm{sym}}}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\right)\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}J_{\textrm{sym}}\left(1+\frac{9K_{\textrm{sym}}}{J_{\textrm{sym}}}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}+\frac{18L}{J_{\textrm{sym}}}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}).

(9)

Some new features emerge in (
9
). Firstly, besides the conventional term
2^{-1}K_{\textrm{sym}}
, a new term
3\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}L/K_{\textrm{sym}}
(normalized by
2^{-1}K_{\textrm{sym}}
) contributes at order
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
. This term is generally sizable and can not be thought as a perturbation.
Secondly, for small
\chi
, e.g,
\rho_{0}\lesssim\rho\lesssim 3\rho_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\approx

\displaystyle\chi-\frac{3}{2}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}\chi^{3}-\frac{9}{8}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}\chi^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{27}{40}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{4}\chi^{5}-\frac{27}{80}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{5}\chi^{6}+\cdots,

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\chi,~~\chi\to 0,

(10)

i.e., although high-order terms such as the fourth-order kurtosis
I_{\textrm{sym}}
, etc., are absence in the expansion (
4
),
the effects of
\chi^{4}
or
\chi^{5}
are effectively generated.
It means that the effects of high order terms are modeled with the help
of the function
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
.
For example, the effective kurtosis
I_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{eff}}
of the symmetry energy defined as the fourth-order Taylor’s expansion coefficient of
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at
\rho_{0}
could be obtained in terms of
L,K_{\textrm{sym}},J_{\textrm{sym}}
and
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
once the expression (
9
) is expand to order
\chi^{4}
, i.e.,
I_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{eff}}=-9\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}(2J_{\textrm{sym}}+11\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}K_{\textrm{sym}}+18\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}L)
. From the above expansion, one can see that the limit
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\to 0
(equivalently
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\to\chi
) is equivalent to the conventional expansion of the
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
in Eq. (
1
).

2.

In the algebraic model,
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=[1+\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}(1+3\chi)]^{-1}
, we have

\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})=\chi\frac{1+\Theta^{-1}_{\textrm{sym}}}{1+3\chi+\Theta^{-1}_{\textrm{sym}}},

(11)

for the expansion element, and

\displaystyle E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx

\displaystyle S+L\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}K_{\textrm{sym}}\left(1+\frac{6L}{K_{\textrm{sym}}}\frac{1}{1+\Theta^{-1}_{\textrm{sym}}}\right)\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}J_{\textrm{sym}}\Bigg[1+\frac{18K_{\textrm{sym}}}{J_{\textrm{sym}}}\frac{1}{1+\Theta^{-1}_{\textrm{sym}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{54L}{J_{\textrm{sym}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\Theta^{-1}_{\textrm{sym}}}\right)^{2}\Bigg]\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

(12)

for the auxiliary-function-based reconstruction of the symmetry energy.
There are two limits for the function
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}
.
If
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
is small (i.e., the small-
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
limit), we then have

\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\approx\chi\left(1-3\chi\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\right)\to\chi,

(13)

where the last relation holds for
\chi\to 0
(near the saturation density).
On the other hand, if
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
is large (i.e., the large-
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
limit), then one can treat
1/\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
as a small quantity,

\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})\approx\frac{\chi}{1+3\chi}\left(1+\frac{1}{\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}}\frac{3\chi}{1+3\chi}\right)\to\chi.

(14)

In fact from expressions (
11
) and (
12
), one can find that the
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
appears naturally in the form of

.

There are two different approaches to determine the parameter
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
. In the first approach, one determines the expression for
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
by truncating the symmetry energy in the expansion (
4
). For example, if the symmetry energy is truncated at order
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}
, i.e.,
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx S+L\nu_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})

\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
.

\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}=-K_{\textrm{sym}}/3L

1

E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx S+L\chi-3L\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\chi^{2}/2+\mathcal{O}(\chi^{3})
.
This determination could also be expressed as the following condition,

\Phi=0,

(15)

with
\Phi
defined in Eq. (
5

\Phi=1+3\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}L/K_{\textrm{sym}}

\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}=-K_{\textrm{sym}}/3L
.
At this order, we thus have
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)\approx S-L^{2}K^{-1}_{\textrm{sym}}[1-e^{\chi K_{\textrm{sym}}/L}]
.
Similarly, the
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
could be determined when the truncation of the symmetry energy (
4
) is made at order
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}

\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
from

\Psi=0

(16)

For the exponential model, for example, we have

Setting the last term equal to

The second approach which is adopted in the current work determines the value of
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}

The physical meaning and determination procedure of
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
discussed above can be seen more clearly by making an analogy with solving the forced oscillator problem.
Consider an oscillator moving under an extra force
-\sigma x^{3}
besides the conventional Hooke’s force
f_{\textrm{H}}(x)=-kx
with
k
the spring constant, i.e.,
f_{\textrm{tot}}(x)=-kx-\sigma x^{3}
, here
\sigma>0
is a high-order coefficient. In order to avoid dealing with the dynamical variable “
x
” in the nonlinear term
\sigma x^{3}
, one can define an effective spring constant
k_{\textrm{eff}}\approx k(1+s_{1}\phi+s_{2}\phi^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{3})
where
\phi=\sigma d_{\max}^{2}/k\ll 1
with
d_{\max}
the amplitude of the oscillator, via the equation of energy conservation
2^{-1}m\dot{x}^{2}+2^{-1}kx^{2}+U_{\sigma}(x)=2^{-1}kd_{\max}^{2}+U_{\sigma}(d_{\max}),U_{\sigma}(x)=4^{-1}\sigma x^{4}
. The two coefficients
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
could be matched by requiring, e.g., that the periods of the oscillation obtained by using the full potential
U_{\textrm{tot}}(x)=2^{-1}kx^{2}+U_{\sigma}(x)
and the effective potential
U_{\textrm{eff}}(x)=2^{-1}k_{\textrm{eff}}x^{2}
are the same, to order
\phi^{2}
.
Consequently, one obtains
s_{1}=3/4
and
s_{2}=-3/128
and thus
k_{\textrm{eff}}\approx k(1+3\phi/4-3\phi^{2}/128)
through the basic formula
T=2\pi(m/k_{\textrm{eff}})^{1/2}
, as the period of the oscillation from the full potential is given by

T\approx 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}\times\left(1-\frac{3}{8}\frac{\sigma d_{\max}^{2}}{k}+\frac{57}{256}\frac{\sigma^{2}d_{\max}^{4}}{k^{2}}\right)

(18)

to order
\phi^{2}
.
In other applications, one can then use the effective potential
U_{\textrm{eff}}(x)=2^{-1}k_{\textrm{eff}}x^{2}
to do the relevant calculations (without dealing with the dynamical variable “
x
”).
The high order effects characterized
by the parameter
\sigma
appear in the effective potential through the low-order coefficient
k_{\textrm{eff}}
.
The effective spring constant
k_{\textrm{eff}}
could be constructed order by order with respect to the perturbative element
\phi
, similar to the construction of the
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
parameter by considering certain types of higher order contributions from the
K_{\textrm{sym}},J_{\textrm{sym}}
, etc.

V.2
Understanding the Dependence of High-Density Symmetry Energy
E_{\textmd{sym}}(\rho)
on its Characteristics at
\rho_{0}

While in the conventional expansion of Eq. (
1
) the dependences of
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
on its characteristic parameters at
\rho_{0}
are obvious by definition, these dependences are no longer obvious in the auxiliary-function-based expansions because of the convolutions. Nevertheless, they can be mathematically analyzed rigorously by calculating the relevant derivatives.
For example, the dependences of
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{f}})
at a reference density
\rho_{\textrm{f}}
on
K_{\textrm{sym}}
and
J_{\textrm{sym}}
can be analyzed within the auxiliary-function-based expansion with the exponential model to order
\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}
using the following derivatives,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{f}})}{\partial K_{\textrm{sym}}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{f},2}\left(1+3\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{f}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[1-\left(\frac{\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{low}}}{\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{f}}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1+3\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{low}}}{1+3\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{f}}}\right)\left(\frac{\Upsilon_{\textrm{f}}}{\Upsilon_{\textrm{low}}}\right)\right],

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{f}})}{\partial J_{\textrm{sym}}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{f},3}\times\left[1-\left(\frac{\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{low}}}{\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{\textrm{f}}}\right)^{3}\left(\frac{\Upsilon_{\textrm{f}}}{\Upsilon_{\textrm{low}}}\right)\right],

(23)

where the superscripts/subscripts “f” and “low” are for
\rho_{\textrm{f}}
and
\rho_{\textrm{low}}\approx 0.05\,\textrm{fm}^{-3}
(see the fitting scheme (
17
)), respectively.
The function
\Upsilon(\rho)
in the above two equations is given by

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\rho)=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}L\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\left(K_{\textrm{sym}}+4L\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\right)\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial\nu_{\textrm{sym}}}{\partial\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}}\Bigg[L+\left(K_{\textrm{sym}}+3L\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}\right)\nu_{\textrm{sym}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{\textrm{sym}}+9K_{\textrm{sym}}\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}+18L\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}\right)\nu_{\textrm{sym}}^{2}\Bigg].

(24)

The corrections in the square brackets in (
22
) and (
23
) come from the dependence of the
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
parameter on the curvature
K_{\textrm{sym}}
and the skewness
J_{\textrm{sym}}
of the symmetry energy, i.e.,
\partial\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}/\partial K_{\textrm{sym}}
and
\partial\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}/\partial J_{\textrm{sym}}
.
By adopting the empirical values of

and
\partial E_{\textrm{sym}}(3\rho_{0})/\partial J_{\textrm{sym}}\approx 0.0026

On the other hand, we have in the conventional reconstruction that
{\partial E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{f}})}/{\partial K_{\textrm{sym}}}=\chi_{\textrm{f}}^{2}/2
and
{\partial E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{\textrm{f}})}/{\partial J_{\textrm{sym}}}=\chi_{\textrm{f}}^{3}/6

\partial E_{\textrm{sym}}(3\rho_{0})/\partial K_{\textrm{sym}}\approx 0.2223

The small value of

with

VI
Summary and outlook

In summary, by adopting the auxiliary-function-based expansion for nuclear symmetry energy at suprasaturation densities, one can effectively incorporate contributions from its higher order characteristics at saturation density.
The symmetry energy in the density region of
\rho_{0}\lesssim\rho\lesssim 3\rho_{0}
is found to be stable irrespective of the truncation order in the approach and/or the empirical values for the higher order characteristics like
K_{\textrm{sym}}
and
J_{\textrm{sym}}
adopted. The reconstructed symmetry energy at suprasaturation densities from the new approach has smaller error bars compared to that from using the conventional
\chi
-expansion.
Moreover, the symmetry energy
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho)
at densities
2\rho_{0}\lesssim\rho\lesssim 3\rho_{0}
is found to be flat and trending down as the density increases.

The auxiliary function approach is found to converge much faster than the conventional
\chi
-expansion in the density region of
\rho_{0}\lesssim\rho\lesssim 3\rho_{0}
.
In principle, it can be applied not only to expanding the symmetry energy but also the EOS of SNM. The conventional expansion of the latter based on
\chi
suffers from similar shortcomings as in expanding the symmetry energy. Moreover, the auxiliary function approach may also be used to study simultaneously the isospin and density dependences of superdense neutron-rich matter by reforming the expansion of its EOS in terms of the isospin asymmetry
\delta^{2}
via a similar transform
\Omega(\delta,\Delta)
similar to the
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
, where
\Delta
is a parameter of
\Omega
similar to the
\Theta_{\textrm{sym}}
used in the
\Pi_{\textrm{sym}}(\chi,\Theta_{\textrm{sym}})
function. Applications of such approach may be useful for extracting more accurately the EOS of superdense neutron-rich matter from structures and collision prooducts of both heavy nuclei in terrestrial laboratories and neutron stars in heaven.

Horocycle flows on abelian covers of surfaces of negative curvature
Roberto Castorrini, Davide Ravotti

Abstract
. We consider the unit speed parametrization of the horocycle flow on infinite Abelian covers of compact surfaces of negative curvature. We prove an asymptotic result for the ergodic integrals of sufficiently regular functions. In the case of constant curvature, where the unit speed and the uniformly contracting parametrizations of horocycles coincide, we recover a result by Ledrappier and Sarig. Our method, which does not use symbolic dynamics, is based on a general Fourier decomposition for Abelian covers and on the study of spectral theory of weighted (and twisted) transfer operators for the geodesic flow acting on appropriate anisotropic Banach spaces. Finally, as a byproduct result, we obtain a power deviation estimate for the horocycle ergodic averages on compact surfaces, without requiring any pinching condition as in previous results.



1.
Introduction

One of the central problems in ergodic theory is understanding the behaviour of typical orbits of measure preserving flows and, in particular, the asymptotics of their ergodic integrals. For ergodic flows preserving a finite measure, Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem implies that the growth of ergodic integrals is linear (proportional to the space average of the observable) and the same for almost every point. On the other hand, the behaviour of ergodic and conservative flows preserving an infinite,
\sigma
-finite measure is rather different: a celebrated theorem of Aaronson
[
1
]
states that one cannot find an asymptotic rate that applies to almost every orbit and, as such, the growth of ergodic integrals depends on the starting point. As a consequence of Hopf’s Ratio Ergodic Theorem, the dependence on the chosen observable is only as a multiplicative constant, namely its space average.

One can therefore hope to describe the ergodic integrals of an ergodic, conservative, measure preserving flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(M,\mu)
, with
\mu(M)=\infty
, as

(1)

\int_{0}^{T}f\circ\varphi_{t}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=a(T)\Phi_{T}(x)\mu(f)(1+o(1))

where the factor
a(T)
describes the “correct” asymptotic growth, and
\Phi_{T}(x)
represents an “oscillating” term, which does not depend on the integrable function
f
; moreover, although
\Phi_{T}
does not converge pointwise almost everywhere, it perhaps does in some weaker sense.
This indeed has been achieved for some families of
parabolic
flows, see, for instance,
[
3
,
7
,
29
,
30
]
.

In this paper, we derive an expansion of the form (
1
) (see Theorem
A
) for the
unit speed
parametrization of the horocycle flow on infinite Abelian covers of compact surfaces of negative, possibly variable curvature.
We explicitly describe the rate
a(T)
and the oscillating factor
\Phi_{T}(x)
in (
3
), along with an estimate for the error term. Next, we establish equidistribution for geodesic translates of horocycle segments, which is our second result (see Theorem
B
). Finally, as a bonus, we obtain a power deviation estimate for the horocycle ergodic averages on compact surfaces with negative curvature (see Theorem C), without requiring any pinching condition as in (
[
2
, Corollary 3.4]
).

In the case of constant curvature
-1
, the unit speed parametrization of horocycles coincides with the so-called
uniformly contracting
parametrization. We then recover the result by Ledrappier and Sarig in
[
29
]
, which applies in general for the uniformly contracting parametrization.
Differently from
[
29
]
and other previous results (e.g.,
[
3
]
), our approach does not use symbolic dynamics; instead, it is geometric in nature and it relies purely on functional analysis and transfer operators methods. The use of transfer operators acting on anisotropic Banach spaces to study parabolic systems appeared first in the seminal work of Giulietti and Liverani
[
22
]
, and has proved successful in several settings
[
19
,
2
,
13
]
. It stems from the idea of
renormalization
, which, in our case, is provided by the geodesic flow. The lack of uniformity, in general, for the unit speed parametrization of horocycle flows (as opposed to the uniformly contracting one, as suggested by the name) makes the analysis harder and requires the use of weighted transfer operators, analogously to
[
2
]
.

There are two further difficulties that one needs to overcome to apply the transfer operator method in our setting.
Firstly, the (weighted) transfer operator associated with the geodesic flow on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-covers of a compact space acts on smooth functions defined on a non-compact space. Although it may still be possible to establish a Lasota-Yorke type inequality, which is crucial to obtain quasi-compactness of the operator, the non-compactness of the space precludes the direct application of Hennion’s theorem
[
33
]
.
To this end, we exploit a Fourier-type decomposition of the space of square integrable functions given by the natural
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-action of the Galois group of the cover.
Roughly speaking, this reduces the problem to studying the action of the transfer operator on a family of spaces of functions which “behave as if they were defined on a compact space” (more precisely, they can be seen as sections of line bundles over the compact base manifolds, as in
[
21
]
).
In turn, we will study the action of a family of weighted and twisted transfer operators on a fixed Banach space containing densely the space of smooth functions on the compact base manifold. We then conduct the necessary spectral analysis of these operators associated to the geodesic flow.

The second issue is that the flow is not uniformly hyperbolic due to the presence of the flow direction, rendering it only partially hyperbolic. Because of this neutral direction, proving the existence of a spectral gap is extremely difficult (if possible at all). Only in special cases the existence of a spectral gap for transfer operators of partially hyperbolic systems has been proved (see for instance
[
15
]
in discrete time or
[
46
]
in continuous time).
Nonetheless, drawing from the works of
[
31
]
and
[
10
]
, quasi-compactness of the resolvent of the generator suffices to obtain a spectral decomposition for the transfer operators.
Combined with the idea of renormalization, it will allow us to prove the expansion in (
1
).

Organization of the paper

The paper is organized as follows.

, Theorem

.

2.
Setting and results

2.1.
Geodesic and horocycle flows on surfaces of negative curvature

Let
S
be a compact, connected surface equipped with a complete smooth Riemannian metric of negative curvature.
We let
M=T^{1}S
denote the unit tangent bundle of
S
. For any
x=(z,v)\in M
, there exists a unique unit speed geodesic
\gamma_{x}(t)
, defined for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, such that
\gamma_{x}(0)=z
and
\dot{\gamma_{x}}(0)=v
.
The geodesic flow
(\mathsf{g}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is the smooth flow on
M
defined by

\mathsf{g}_{t}(x)=(\gamma_{x}(t),\dot{\gamma_{x}}(t)).

The vector field
X
on
M
generating the geodesic flow is given by

Xf(x)=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}f\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}(x),

for all
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(M)
.
The geodesic flow is an Anosov flow, in particular the following holds.
Let
D\mathsf{g}_{t}\colon TM\to TM
denote the differential of
\mathsf{g}_{t}
. There exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\lambda>0
, and there exists a
D\mathsf{g}_{t}
-invariant splitting of
TM=E_{-}\oplus E_{0}\oplus E_{+}
into 1-dimensional subbundles, where
E_{0}=\langle X\rangle
and
E_{\pm}
satisfy

(2)

\|D\mathsf{g}_{t}U\|\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}\|U\|,\quad\forall U\in E_{-}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|D\mathsf{g}_{-t}V\|\leq Ce^{-\lambda t}\|V\|,\quad\forall V\in E_{+},

for all
t\geq 0
. Explicit bounds on
\lambda
can be expressed in terms of the geometry of
S
; we refer the reader to
[
32
, Part IV, §17.6]
for a detailed discussion.
In the case
S
has constant negative curvature
-1
, then, for all
x\in M
, we have

D\mathsf{g}_{t}(x)U_{x}=e^{-t}U_{\mathsf{g}_{t}(x)},\text{\ for all $U\in E_{-}(x)$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad D\mathsf{g}_{t}(x)V_{x}=e^{t}V_{\mathsf{g}_{t}(x)},\text{\ for all $V\in E_{+}(x)$},

as we will see in
Lemma

3.2
below.

A further important remark is that the flow
(\mathsf{g}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
preserves a
contact
form and hence the associated Liouville measure, which, up to a scalar multiple, coincides with the natural Riemann volume on
M
. We will denote this measure by
\vol
. The geodesic flow is thus an example of a
contact Anosov flow
.

We denote by
h_{\topp}
the topological entropy of the time-one map
\mathsf{g}_{1}
.

The distributions
E_{\pm}
are orientable and are of class
\mathscr{C}^{2-\varepsilon}
for every
\varepsilon>0
; more precisely they are
\mathscr{C}^{1}
and their derivative is in the Zygmund class, as proved by Hurder and Katok
[
34
]
.
On the other hand, they fail to be
\mathscr{C}^{2}
, unless the surface
S
has constant curvature.

Both
E_{-}
and
E_{+}
integrate to 1-dimensional
\mathsf{g}_{t}
-invariant foliations
W^{-}
and
W^{+}
, whose leaves are called the
stable
and
unstable manifolds
of
(\mathsf{g}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.
The unit speed motions along the stable and unstable manifolds define two flows
(\mathsf{h}^{-}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
and
(\mathsf{h}^{+}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
M
, called the
stable
and
unstable horocycle flow
.

In this paper, we are going to focus on the stable horocycle flow, which henceforth we will simply call the horocycle flow and will be denoted by
(\mathsf{h}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
. By definition, the generating vector field
U
, given by

Uf(x)=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}f\circ\mathsf{h}_{t}(x)

for all
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(M)
, spans the line bundle
E_{-}
.

The horocycle flow is an important example of a
parabolic flow
. It is minimal
[
6
,
40
]
, has zero entropy
[
27
]
and is mixing with respect to the unique invariant probability measure
\mu

[
39
]
. We remark that, unless the curvature is constant, the invariant measure
\mu
is singular with respect to the Liouville measure on
M
.

2.2.
Abelian covers

Let
G=\pi_{1}(S)
be the fundamental group of the surface
S
and let
G^{\prime}=[G,G]
denote the derived subgroup.
The quotient
G^{\abel}=G/G^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the first homology
H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
of
S
, which is a free abelian group of rank
2g
, where
g\geq 2
is the genus of
S
.

Intermediate subgroups
G^{\prime}\leq\Gamma\leq G
are in 1-to-1 correspondence with subgroups of
H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
via the projection
\Gamma\mapsto\Gamma^{\abel}=\Gamma/G^{\prime}
. Each
\Gamma
defines a cover
p_{0}\colon{\widetilde{S}}\to S
with a Galois group

\Deck:=\operatorname{Aut}({\widetilde{S}}/S)

of deck transformations isomorphic to
G/\Gamma=H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})/\Gamma^{\abel}
. We will assume that the latter has no torsion, which is always the case up to a finite cover, and hence is a free abelian group of rank
1\leq d\leq 2g
.
We fix
d
linearly independent primitive homology classes

[\gamma_{i}]=\gamma_{i}\,G^{\prime}\in H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad\text{for $i=1,\dots,d$}

so that the elements

D_{i}=\gamma_{i}\,\Gamma\in\Deck

form a basis of
\Deck\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
We say that the associated cover
p_{0}\colon{\widetilde{S}}\to S
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-cover of
S
.
The cover
{\widetilde{S}}
is equipped with the pullback Riemannian metric under
p_{0}
, so that
p_{0}\colon{\widetilde{S}}\to S
is a Riemannian cover, and the deck transformations act isometrically.
The action of
\Deck
extends naturally to an action on the unit tangent bundle
{\widetilde{M}}=T^{1}{\widetilde{S}}
of
{\widetilde{S}}
and we have an associated cover
p\colon{\widetilde{M}}\to M
.

We equip the cover
{\widetilde{S}}
with the pullback Riemannian area form under
p_{0}
. By a little abuse of notation, we still denote by
\vol
the Liouville form on
{\widetilde{M}}
. It induces an infinite measure, normalized so that
\vol(M)=1
.

2.3.
The results

Given a positive definite symmetric
d\times d
matrix
\Sigma
, we define the
\|\cdot\|_{\Sigma}
norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
as

\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\Sigma}:=\sqrt{\mathbf{x}\cdot\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf{x}}.

Our first main result is the following, and its proof can be found in
Section

7.3
.

Theorem A
.

Let
p\colon{\widetilde{M}}\to M
a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-cover of
M=T^{1}S
, where
S
is a compact surface of negative curvature. There exist constants
C_{M}\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
, a positive definite symmetric
d\times d
matrix
\Sigma
, and functions

\begin{split}&t_{\ast}\colon M\times[C_{M},\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},\qquad\text{satisfying}\qquad\|T-e^{h_{\topp}\,t_{\ast}(\cdot,T)}\|_{\infty}\leq C_{M}T^{1-\varepsilon}\qquad\text{and}\\
&F_{\ast}\colon M\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\qquad\text{satisfying}\qquad\frac{F_{\ast}(\cdot,T)}{\sqrt{T}}\to\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma)\text{\ \ in distribution,}\end{split}

for which the following holds.
For every
f\in\mathscr{C}_{c}^{2}({\widetilde{M}})
and for every
x\in{\widetilde{M}}
, there exists a constant
C(f,x)\geq 0
depending on the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-norm of
f
, on the diameter of its support
\supp(f)
, and on the distance between
x
and
\supp(f)
, such that for all
T\geq C_{M}
, denoting
t_{\ast}=t_{\ast}(p(x),T)
, and

(3)

a(T):=\frac{h_{\topp}^{\frac{d}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}\frac{T}{(\log T)^{\frac{d}{2}}},\qquad\Phi_{T}(x):=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\frac{F_{\ast}(p(x),t_{\ast})}{\sqrt{t_{\ast}}}\right\|_{\Sigma}^{2}\right),

we have

\Bigg\lvert\int_{0}^{T}f\circ\mathsf{h}_{s}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-a(T)\Phi_{T}(x)\int_{{\widetilde{M}}}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\Bigg\rvert\leq C_{M}C(f,x)\,\frac{T\cdot\log\log T}{(\log T)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}.

Before proceeding, let us provide some remarks on the result.

Remark 2.1

(Winding cycle and normalizing time)
.

The functions
t_{\ast}
and
F_{\ast}
are explicitly defined and have precise geometric meaning. The function
t_{\ast}
is a
normalizing time
, in the sense that it is the time the geodesic flow takes to normalize the horocycle orbit of length
T
at
x
to unit size (see (
55
) below for the precise definition). In particular,
C_{M}=1
and
t_{\ast}(x,T)=\log T
for surfaces of constant negative curvature
-1
.

The vector
F_{\ast}(x,T)
is the so-called
Frobenius function
, or the
geodesic winding cycle
: its components are ergodic integrals of harmonic 1-forms along the geodesic orbit at
x
of length
T
and they describe the behaviour of this geodesic with respect to the cohomology classes that vanish on
{\widetilde{M}}
(roughly speaking, how the geodesic segment “winds around” the cycles in the first homology of
M
which do not lift to closed loops on the cover); see (
13
) and
Section

7.3
for the precise definition. The covariance matrix
\Sigma
is associated to the quadratic form defined in (
46
), normalized by
-1/(4\pi^{2})
.

Remark 2.2

(Asymptotic expansion)
.

Following the proof outlined in Section
7.3
, we observe that the result follows by computing the leading term in the expansion of the integral in (
54
). While it is possible, in principle, to derive a complete expansion using stationary phase methods, we refrain from doing so due to the complexity of the computations, particularly for large
d
; we refer to
[
7
]
for the analogous computations in the setting of translation flows.

Remark 2.3

(Generic points)
.

The conclusion of
A
holds for any point
x\in{\widetilde{M}}
; however, the term
a(T)\Phi_{T}(x)\mu(f)
describes the asymptotics of the horocycle integrals only for those points
x
for which
\Phi_{T}(x)
is not “too small”, namely for
x\in{\widetilde{M}}
such that
F_{\ast}(x,t)/t\to 0
as
t\to\infty
(whereas, for the other points, we only obtain an upper bound). Indeed, we do not expect the points for which such condition does not hold to be generic for
\mu
: in the case of hyperbolic surfaces, there is a
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
family of ergodic invariant Radon measures
[
4
,
42
]
, and Sarig and Schapira showed in
[
43
]
that the set of generic points for these measures are parametrized by the possible limits of
F_{\ast}(x,t)/t
. We expect a similar characterization to hold in our general setting of variable curvature.

Remark 2.4

(Curvature and dimension)
.

In the scenario of constant negative curvature, we recover the result presented in
[
29
]
. Notably, Theorem
A
extends to compact surfaces of variable negative curvature. This extension is facilitated by the one-dimensionality of the stable and unstable manifolds. While this simplification streamlines many computations, it is primarily crucial for proving a Dolgopyat-type inequality (see Proposition
6.2
) without assuming any bunching condition

1

1

Looking at
[
23
, Remark 7.6]
it becomes clear why the bunching condition was not needed in our setting.
. Hence, another suitable choice of Banach spaces, coupled with a bunching condition, would likely suffice to generalize our result to any dimension. This could be achieved using the arguments presented in
[
2
]
or
[
23
]
to establish the required Dolgopyat inequality.

Remark 2.5

(Limit theorems)
.

Our proof of the main theorem relies on a pure functional analytic approach, wherein we investigate the spectrum of the weighted transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{t}
associated with the geodesic flow on
T^{1}S
. Establishing the quasi-compactness of
\mathcal{L}_{t}
would grant access to various statistical properties, such as decay of correlations. Particularly, employing a spectral method to study complex perturbations of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{t,\nu}
(see
[
24
]
) would imply limit theorems such as the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), local CLT, and large deviations, among others. However, the absence of uniform hyperbolicity has posed significant challenges in proving quasi-compactness for
\mathcal{L}_{t}
.

Nonetheless, the pioneering work of Liverani
[
31
]
indicates that quasi-compactness of the resolvent of the generator of the semigroup
\mathcal{L}_{t}
is enough to get, at least, exponential decay of correlations. Building on this insight, our analysis on the resolvent of the generator of the perturbed operator
\mathcal{L}_{t,\nu}
could yield an alternative approach to establishing the CLT for the geodesic flow on negatively curved manifolds and exploring its finer statistical properties (See Proposition
6.4
and Remark
6.5
).

The same methods we develop in this paper yield an equidistribution result for geodesic translates of horocycle segments, which is our second main result. Its proof is contained in
Section

7.4
.

Theorem B
.

Let
p\colon{\widetilde{M}}\to M
a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-cover of
M=T^{1}S
, where
S
is a compact surface of negative curvature. There exists a a positive definite symmetric
d\times d
matrix
\Sigma

for which the following holds.
Let
\eta
be a 1-form of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
on
{\widetilde{M}}
with compact support, fix
x\in{\widetilde{M}}
and
\sigma>0
. Define

\gamma_{x,\sigma}(s)=\mathsf{h}_{s}(x),\qquad\text{for}\qquad s\in[0,\sigma],

the horocycle segment starting at
x
of length
\sigma
. There exist constants
C(\sigma)
depending on
\sigma
and
C(\eta,x)\geq 0
depending on the
\mathscr{C}^{2}
-norm of
\eta
, on the diameter of its support
\supp(\eta)
, and on the distance between
x
and
\supp(\eta)
, such that for all

we have

\left\lvert\frac{(2\pi t)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}{e^{h_{\topp}t}\,\sigma}\int_{\mathsf{g}_{-t}\circ\gamma_{x,\sigma}}\eta-\int_{{\widetilde{M}}}\langle\eta,U\rangle\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\right\rvert\leq C(\sigma)C(\eta,x)\frac{\log t}{\sqrt{t}}.

An important difference with

is the absence of the oscillating factor in

above. As it will be clear in

A somehow similar phenomenon happens in the case of non-compact, finite volume hyperbolic surfaces, see
[

]
and references therein.

Finally, as an outgrowth of our methods, we are also able to prove a power deviation estimate for the horocycle ergodic averages on compact surfaces of negative curvature. The case of hyperbolic surfaces is due to Burger
[

]
, and has been later refined by several authors
[

,

. The case of variable curvature has been studied by Adam and Baladi

, which provides also more precise information on

given in (

) in terms of the topological entropy

Theorem C
.

Let

be the unit tangent bundle of a compact surface

such that, for each

,

(4)

\left\lvert\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f\circ\mathsf{h}_{s}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\mu(f)\right\rvert\leq\dfrac{{C_{\sharp}}}{T^{a}}\|f\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}.

3.
Renormalization

In this Section we collect some results on the dynamics of geodesic and of horocycle flows on their unit tangent bundles.
Although this two flows do not commute, they satisfy a “renormalization relation”, which plays a fundamental role in the study of the dynamics and the ergodic theory of the horocycle flow.

Lemma 3.1

(Renormalization relation)
.

For every
x\in M
and
s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists a unique
\tau(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(5)

\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{s}(x)=\mathsf{h}_{\tau(s,t,x)}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}(x).

Proof.

For any
x\in M
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\mathsf{h}_{s}(x)\in W^{-}(x)
. Since
\mathsf{g}_{t}(W^{-}(x))=W^{-}(\mathsf{g}_{t}(x))
there exists
\tau=\tau(s,t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that (
5
) is satisfied.

If there were
\tau\neq\tau^{\prime}
satisfying (
5
), then there would be a closed orbit for the horocycle flow, which contradicts minimality
[
6
,
40
]
.
∎

The renormalization time
\tau(s,t,x)
of
Lemma

3.1
is intimately related to the curvature of the surface, as the next lemma shows. Its derivative
J(t,x)
will play a crucial role in this paper.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
x\in M
, the function

J(t,x):=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=0}\tau(s,t,x)

satisfies

\partial^{2}_{t}{J}(t,x)+K(\mathsf{g}_{t}(x))J(t,x)=0,\qquad J(0,x)=1,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}J(t,x)=0,

where
K
denotes the curvature. Moreover, given
\overline{k},\underline{k}<0
such that
\underline{k}\leq K(\mathsf{g}_{t}(x))\leq\overline{k}
for each
x\in M
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, then

(6)

e^{-\sqrt{-\underline{k}}t}\leq J(t,x)\leq e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t},\qquad\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0},\quad\forall x\in M.

Proof.

For convenience, let us set
J_{x}(t):=J(t,x)
. For any
x\in M
,
\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{s}(x)
defines a 1-parameter family of geodesics, hence

\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Big|_{s=0}\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{s}(x)=D\mathsf{g}_{t}(x)U_{x}

is a Jacobi field. In particular, from (
5
) we can write

It remains to prove (

&=-[\overline{J}\dot{J}-J\dot{\overline{J}}](t)+\int_{t}^{+\infty}[k(s)-\overline{k}]J(s)\overline{J}(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Therefore, since

which implies that

\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{\overline{J}(t)}{J(t)}\right)\cdot J^{2}(t)\geq 0.

Hence, the function

is increasing for each

4.
Twisted Hilbert spaces

In this section we introduce the Fourier decomposition of our space. We recall the notations from
Section

2.2
.
Let us fix a compact connected subset
{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\widetilde{M}}
whose boundary has zero measure such that the restriction of
p
to the interior of
{\mathcal{F}}
is injective and
p({\mathcal{F}})=M
. We say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is a fundamental domain for the cover
p
.

Any function on
M
can be seen as a
\Deck
-invariant function on
{\widetilde{M}}
, and vice-versa. Under this identification, it is not hard to see that

\int_{M}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol=\int_{{\mathcal{F}}}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol.

In particular, for all
q\geq 1
, the Banach spaces
L^{q}(M)
and
L^{q}({\mathcal{F}})
are naturally isomorphic.

For any
i=1,\dots,d
, let
\omega_{i}\in H^{1}(S,{\mathbb{R}})
be the cohomology class defined by

\omega_{i}(\Gamma)=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\omega_{i}([\gamma_{j}])=\delta_{i\,j},

where
\delta_{i\,j}
is the Kronecker delta.
By the Hodge Theory, we can identify
\omega_{i}
with a harmonic 1-form on
S
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}
the real vector space

{\mathcal{H}}:=\langle\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d}\rangle
and by
{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{Z}})
the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\omega_{d}
. Their quotient is a
d
-dimensional torus

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}:={\mathcal{H}}/{\mathcal{H}}({\mathbb{Z}}).

For any
\omega\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, we define

E_{\omega}\colon\Deck\to\mathbb{S},\qquad E_{\omega}([\gamma]+\Gamma^{\abel})=e^{2\pi\imath\omega\cdot[\gamma]}.

Note that, if a function
f\colon{\widetilde{M}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfies
f\circ D^{-1}=E_{\omega}(D)\,f
for all
D\in\Deck
, then
|f|
is a
\Deck
-invariant function and hence it is well defined on
M
.
Given
\omega\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, we define

L^{2}(M,\omega):=\left\{f\colon{\widetilde{M}}\to{\mathbb{C}}\ :\ f\circ D^{-1}=E_{\omega}(D)\,f\text{\ for all $D\in\Deck$, and\ }\int_{{\mathcal{F}}}|f|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol<\infty\right\}.

We equip
L^{2}(M,\omega)
with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{{\mathcal{F}}}f\cdot\overline{g}\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol,

which turns into a Hilbert space.
For every integer
\ell\geq 0
, we also set

\mathscr{C}^{\ell}(M,\omega)=L^{2}(M,\omega)\cap\mathscr{C}^{\ell}({\widetilde{M}}).

For any continuous function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{0}_{c}({\widetilde{M}})
with compact support, let us define

\pi_{\omega}(f)(x)=\sum_{D\in\Deck}E_{\omega}(D)\cdot f\circ D(x).

Note that, since
f
has compact support, the sum on the right hand side above is finite for any
x\in{\widetilde{M}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\ell\geq 0
,

\pi_{\omega}\colon\mathscr{C}^{\ell}_{c}({\widetilde{M}})\to\mathscr{C}^{\ell}(M,\omega).

Moreover, for every
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\ell}_{c}({\widetilde{M}})
and for any
x\in{\widetilde{M}}
, we have

f(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\pi_{\omega}(f)(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega.

Proof.

Fix
\omega\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
.
From the fact that
\Deck
acts properly discontinuously, it follows immediately that
\pi_{\omega}(f)
is a
\mathscr{C}^{\ell}
-function whenever
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\ell}_{c}({\widetilde{M}})
.

Let us fix a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\Deck\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
Since
f
has compact support, there exists a constant
C(f)>0
such that
f\circ D(x)=0
for all
x\in{\mathcal{F}}
whenever
\|D\|\geq C(f)
. Note that, moreover,

\widetilde{C}(f):=C(f)^{2(d+1)}\sum_{D\in\Deck}\|D\|^{-2(d+1)}

is finite.
By Cauchy-Schwarz, for any
x\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we have

\begin{split}|\pi_{\omega}(f)(x)|^{2}&=\left\lvert\sum_{D\in\Deck}E_{\omega}(D)\cdot f\circ D(x)\cdot\frac{\|D\|^{d+1}}{\|D\|^{d+1}}\right\rvert^{2}\\
&\leq\left(\sum_{D\in\Deck}\|D\|^{-2(d+1)}\right)\left(\sum_{D\in\Deck}|f|^{2}\circ D(x)\cdot\|D\|^{2(d+1)}\right)\\
&\leq\widetilde{C}(f)\sum_{D\in\Deck}|f|^{2}\circ D(x).\end{split}

Thus, since the orbit of
{\mathcal{F}}
under
\Deck

{\widetilde{M}}

\int_{\mathcal{F}}|\pi_{\omega}(f)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol\leq\widetilde{C}(f)\sum_{D\in\Deck}\int_{\mathcal{F}}|f|^{2}\circ D\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol=\widetilde{C}(f)\int_{\widetilde{M}}|f|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\vol,

which is finite by assumption.

Let us show that
\pi_{\omega}(f)\circ D_{0}^{-1}=E_{\omega}(D_{0})\cdot\pi_{\omega}(f)

For every

E_{\omega}(D_{0}^{-1})=E_{\omega}(-[\gamma_{0}]+\Gamma^{\abel})=E_{-\omega}(D_{0})=E_{\omega}(D_{0})^{-1}

\begin{split}\pi_{\omega}(f)\circ D_{0}^{-1}&=\sum_{D\in\Deck}E_{\omega}(D)\cdot f\circ(D_{0}^{-1}\cdot D)\\

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}E_{\omega}(D)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}e^{2\pi\imath\omega\cdot[\gamma]}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\omega=0\qquad\text{if and only if}\qquad D=[\gamma]+\Gamma^{\abel}\neq 0,

For every

Lemma 4.3

Let
\omega\in{\mathcal{H}}

\Xi_{\omega}\colon L^{2}(M,\eta+\omega)\to L^{2}(M,\eta).

Since

For each

Take

t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
,
I\subset{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho},\omega\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\psi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{r}_{c}(I)
with
\|\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{r}(I)}\leq C_{*}
and
\varphi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}_{c}(I)
, we have

\|(\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{(\cdot)}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\varsigma}(I)}\leq C_{\varsigma}(t)\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\varsigma}(I)},\quad\varsigma\in\{0,\alpha,1+\alpha\},

where

(59)

C_{\varsigma}(t)=\begin{cases}1\qquad&\text{if}\qquad\varsigma=0,\\
2\pi C_{*}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}\alpha t}\big)\qquad&\text{if}\qquad\varsigma=\alpha,\\
C_{*}+8\pi^{2}\max\{C_{*}^{3},C_{*}^{4}\}B(\overline{k})A(X,\omega)\big(1+4e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}(1+\alpha)t}\big)\qquad&\text{if}\qquad\varsigma=1+\alpha.\end{cases}

Proof.

The case
\varsigma=0
is trivial since
\|G_{t,\omega}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}=1
.
Let us use the symbol
′
to denote the derivative with respect to
\eta\in I
. We have

\begin{split}((\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ&\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime}\\
=&(G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime}\cdot(\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)+G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\cdot(\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime}\\
=&2\pi\imath\psi^{\prime}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(D\mathsf{g}_{t-a}(U)\langle\omega,X\rangle)\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\right)(G_{t,\omega}\cdot\varphi)\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\\
&+G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\cdot\psi^{\prime}(D\mathsf{g}_{t}(U)\varphi)\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi.\end{split}

Recalling that
D\mathsf{g}_{t}U=J_{t}U
and
J_{-t}=D\mathsf{g}_{-t}(U)
, we note that

(60)

(\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime}=\psi^{\prime}(D\mathsf{g}_{t}(U)\varphi)\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi=\psi^{\prime}J_{t}(U\varphi)\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi.

Therefore, since
\|\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{r}}\leq C_{*}
and
\|G_{t,\omega}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}=1
, we have

\begin{split}|((\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime}|&\leq 2\pi C_{*}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}|J_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a+C_{*}|J_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}|\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}.\end{split}

By Lemma
3.2
,
|J_{t}(x)|\leq e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}
for each
x\in M
, and

(61)

\left|\int_{0}^{t}J_{t-a}\circ h_{\eta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\right|\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}(t-a)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}},

therefore,

(62)

\begin{split}\|(\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}&\leq\frac{2\pi C_{*}}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}+C_{*}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}\\
&\leq C_{*}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}\right)\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}.\end{split}

For
\varsigma=\alpha
it is enough to observe that, for each
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\in(0,\rho)
,

(63)

\begin{split}|(\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{(\cdot)}\circ\psi)(\tilde{\eta})-(\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{(\cdot)}\circ\psi_{\tilde{\eta}})(\eta)|&\leq d_{I}(\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi,\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\tilde{\eta}}\circ\psi)^{\alpha}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}\\
&\leq C_{*}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}\alpha t}|\eta-\tilde{\eta}|^{\alpha}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}},\end{split}

where
d_{I}(x,y)
is the distance of two points
x,y
along
I
. Hence, since
G_{t,\omega}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{r}
, using the inequality

(64)

\|\varphi G\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}\|G\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}+\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}\|G\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}},

we have

\begin{split}\|(\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ&\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}(I)}\leq\\
&\leq\|G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}+\|G_{t,\omega}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}\|\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}\\
&\leq C_{*}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}\right)\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}+C_{*}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}\alpha t}\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}\\
&\leq C_{*}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}\alpha t}\right)\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}.\end{split}

It remains the case
\varsigma=1+\alpha
. Using (
64
), we can check that if
G\in{\mathscr{C}}^{2}
and
\varphi\in{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}
, then

(65)

\|\varphi G\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}}(1+4\|G\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}).

Let us thus compute the second derivative of
(G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)
: using again that
D\mathsf{g}_{t}U=J_{t}U
,

(66)

\begin{split}|(G_{t,\omega}\circ&\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime\prime}|\\
=&\left|\left(2\pi\imath(G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)\psi^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t}J_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\cdot U(\langle\omega,X\rangle\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\right)^{\prime}\right|\\
\leq&2\pi C_{*}\bigg(\left|\int_{0}^{t}(J_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta})^{\prime}\cdot U(\langle\omega,X\rangle\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\right|\\
&+\left|\int_{0}^{t}J_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\cdot(U(\langle\omega,X\rangle\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi))^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\right|\bigg)\\
&+2\pi C_{*}\left|\int_{0}^{t}J_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\cdot U(\langle\omega,X\rangle\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\right|(1+\|G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}).\end{split}

We need to estimate the above terms. By (
6
), (
7
), (
8
) and (
61
),

\begin{split}\left|(J_{t-a}\circ{\mathsf{h}_{\eta}})^{\prime}\right|&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t}(\Phi^{-}\circ\mathsf{g}_{a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta})^{\prime}\right|\left|J_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\right|\leq\dfrac{\|\Phi^{-}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}(t-a)}.\end{split}

On the other hand

\left|(U(\langle\omega,X\rangle\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi))^{\prime}\right|\leq C_{*}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}\|J_{t-a}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{0}}\leq C_{*}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}(t-a)}

and

\int_{0}^{t}|U(\langle\omega,X\rangle\circ\mathsf{g}_{t-a}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a\leq C_{*}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}(t-a)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a.

Using the above three inequalities into (
66
), and recalling (
62
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}&\leq 2\pi C_{*}^{2}\dfrac{\|\Phi^{-}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}}{2{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}}+\frac{2\pi C_{*}^{2}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}}{2\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\\
&+\frac{2\pi C_{*}^{2}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}}{2\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\left(1+C_{*}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}}\|\langle\omega,X\rangle\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1}}+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}\right)\right).\end{split}

We have thus obtained

\|G_{t,\omega}\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{2}}\leq 2\pi^{2}\max\left\{\frac{C_{*}^{2}}{\sqrt{-\overline{k}}},\frac{C_{*}^{3}}{-\overline{k}}\right\}A(X,\omega)\left(4+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}\right).

Hence, by (
65
), we have

\|(\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\\
\leq(1+8\pi^{2}\max\{C_{*}^{2},C_{*}^{3}\}B(\overline{k})A(X,\omega)(4+e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}t}))\|\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}}.

Finally, by (
60
), and arguing as in (
63
),

\|(\varphi\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi)^{\prime}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}=\|\psi^{\prime}J_{t}(U\varphi)\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}\leq C_{*}e^{-\sqrt{\overline{k}}t}e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}\alpha t}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{\alpha}}.

Inserting the above in the previous equation we conclude:

\|(\varphi G_{t,\omega})\circ\mathsf{g}_{t}\circ\mathsf{h}_{\eta}\circ\psi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}}\\
\leq\left(C_{*}+8\pi^{2}\max\{C_{*}^{3},C_{*}^{4}\}B(\overline{k})A(X,\omega)\big(1+4e^{-\sqrt{-\overline{k}}(1+\alpha)t}\big)\right)\|\varphi\|_{{\mathscr{C}}^{1+\alpha}}.

The proof is therefore complete.
∎

A series transformation formula and related degenerate polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. Recently, Boyadzhiev studied a power series whose coefficients are binomial expressions and extended some known formulas involving classical special functions and polynomials. The aim of this paper is to adopt his ideas to express several identities involving ‘degenerate formal power series’ as those including degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, degenerate Bell polynomials, degenerate Fubini polynomials and degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials.

11B83; 05A15

1.
Introduction

In [3], Boyadzhiev studied a power series whose coefficients are binomial expressions. He extended some known formulas involving classical special functions and polynomials like Hurwitz zeta functions, Euler eta functions, Bernoulli polynomials and Euler polynomials.
He had done this by looking at these formulas from a different perspective, including them in a larger theory and connecting them to the series transformation formulas of Euler [4] and the series transformation formulas considered in [5].
This led naturally to certain asymptotic expansions and gave new proofs of some classical asymptotic expansions. In particular, Boyadzhiev obtained the asymptotic expansion of zeta function.

In recent years, studying degenerate versions of some special polynomials and numbers regained interests of some mathematicians, which include the degenerate Bernoulli numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds, the degenerate Cauchy numbers, the degenerate Bell numbers and polynomials,
the degenerate complete Bell polynomials and numbers, and so on (see [7,9,10,13,14,16,18] and the references therein). It is remarkable that this study of degenerate versions is not only limited to polynomials and numbers but also extended to transcendental functions like the gamma functions (see [11,12]). They have been studied by various means like combinatorial methods, generating functions, differential equations, umbral calculus,
\lambda
-umbral calculus,
p
-adic analysis, and probability theory.

The aim of this paper is to adopt the ideas in [3] and to express several identities involving ‘degenerate formal power series’ as those including degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, degenerate Bell polynomials, degenerate Fubini polynomials and degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials (see Theorems 2,5,6,9-12). Along the way, we also obtain some related identities which involve the
\lambda
-falling factorials, the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds, the degenerate Bernoulli numbers, the degenerate Fubini polynomials and the degenerate Euler polynomials. For the rest of this section, we recall the facts that will be used throughout this paper.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential function is defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{n,\lambda}}{n!}t^{n},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,9,10]),

where the
\lambda
-falling factorials are given by
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\ (n\geq 1)
.

When
x=1
, we write
e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
. Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}\displaystyle
.

Let us consider the ‘degenerate formal power series’ which is given by

(2)

f_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)_{k,\lambda}=a_{0}+a_{1}(t)_{1,\lambda}+a_{2}(t)_{2,\lambda}+\cdots\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!],

where the
a_{i}^{\prime}s
are constant complex numbers.
Then we let

(3)

f(t)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}f_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!].

The Carlitz degenerate Bernoulli polynomials are defined by

(4)

\frac{t}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6]).

Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n,\lambda}=B_{n}(x)
, where
B_{n}(x)
are Bernoulli polynomials given by

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,14-18]).

Kim-Kim considered the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind defined by

(5)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

As the inversion formula of (
5
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(6)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,9,10]).

In [13], the degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by

(7)

\displaystyle e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that
\displaystyle\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}\displaystyle
. When
x=1,\ \phi_{n,\lambda}=\phi_{n,\lambda}(1)
are called the degenerate Bell numbers.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate Fubini polynomials are defined by Kim-Kim as

(8)

\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14]).

It is well known that the polylogarithmic function is defined by

(9)

\mathrm{Li}_{k}(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{n^{k}},\quad(k\in\mathbb{Z}),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2]).

Note that
\mathrm{Li}_{1}(x)=-\log(1-x)
.

2.
Some identities of degenerate special polynomials

Here we consider the ‘degenerate formal power series’ given by

(10)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=a_{0}+a_{1}(t)_{1,\lambda}+a_{2}(t)_{2,\lambda}+a_{3}(t)_{3,\lambda}+\cdots\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!],

and express several identities involving ‘degenerate formal power series’ as those including degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, degenerate Bell polynomials, degenerate Fubini polynomials and degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials (see Theorems 2,5,6,9-12).
From (
6
), we note that

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}e_{\lambda}^{l}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(l)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg\{\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}(l)_{n,\lambda}\bigg\}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
11
), we get

(12)

\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}(l)_{n,\lambda}=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),

with the understanding that
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=0
, for
0\leq n<k
.
It is not difficult to show that

(13)

(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}(zk)_{l,\lambda}(y)_{m-l,\lambda},\quad(m\geq 0).

We observe that

(14)

\displaystyle(zk)_{p,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(zk)(zk-\lambda)\cdots(zk-(p-1)\lambda)

\displaystyle=k^{p}z\bigg(z-\frac{\lambda}{k}\bigg)\cdots\bigg(z-(p-1)\frac{\lambda}{k}\bigg)

\displaystyle=k^{p}(z)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{k}}=z^{p}(k)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}},\quad(p\geq 0).

From (
13
) and (
14
), we note that

(15)

\displaystyle(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}k^{p}(z)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{k}}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}(k)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}.

By (
12
) and (
15
), we get

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\bigg\{\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}(k)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}(k)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}(k)_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n).

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
16
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
, the following identity holds.

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n).

Remark 2
.

As
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=0
, for
k>n
and
k<0
, we see from Theorem 1 that we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}=0,~~\mathrm{if}~~n>m.

From (
10
), we note that

(17)

f_{\lambda}(y+zk)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}.

By (
12
), (
15
), (
17
) and Theorem 1, we get

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(y+zk)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}(y+zk)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,m)z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\bigg\{\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}\bigg\}.

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
n\geq 0
, the following identity is valid.

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(y+zk)=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\bigg\{\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}\bigg\}.

In particular, for
y=0
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)z^{m}.

If
n=m
in Theorem 1, then we have

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}(y+zk)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{p=0}^{n}\binom{n}{p}z^{p}(y)_{n-p,\lambda}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!z^{n}.

Therefore, by (
19
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have the following identity:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}(y+zk)_{n,\lambda}=(-1)^{n}n!z^{n}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}.

Dividing (
20
) by
x
and then taking
x=0
, we have

(21)

(-1)^{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}(n-1)!=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!.

Therefore, by (
21
), we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following identity holds true.

(-1)^{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}(n-1)!=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!.

From Theorem 3, we note that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n).

By multiplying
\frac{1}{n}
on both sides, we get

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)=(-1)^{n}(n-1)!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n),

where
n=1,2,3,\dots
.

Thus, by using Lemma 5, we have

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(n-1)!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(n-1)!S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}(-1)^{m}\bigg(\frac{\lambda}{z}\bigg)^{m-1}(m-1)!

\displaystyle=z\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}(-1)^{m}\lambda^{m-1}(m-1)!.

Therefore, by (
22
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)_{k,\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]\displaystyle
, the following identity holds.

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)\bigg\}=-z\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}(-1)^{m}\lambda^{m-1}(m-1)!

We observe that

(23)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{d}{dt}f_{\lambda}(t)\bigg|_{t=0}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{d}{dt}(t)_{k,\lambda}\bigg|_{t=0}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}(-1)^{k-1}\lambda^{k-1}(k-1)!.

By Theorem 6 and (
23
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)\bigg\}=-z\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}(-1)^{m-1}\lambda^{m-1}(m-1)!=-zf_{\lambda}^{\prime}(0).

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)_{k,\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]\displaystyle
. Then the following identity is valid.

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)\bigg\}=-zf_{\lambda}^{\prime}(0),

where
f_{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{d}{dt}f_{\lambda}(t)\big|_{t=0}
.

Let us take
f_{\lambda}(t)=\binom{t}{p}_{\lambda}=\frac{(t)_{p,\lambda}}{p!},\ (p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})
. Then we have
a_{m}=\frac{1}{m!}\delta_{m,p},\ (m,p\geq 0)
, where
\delta_{m,n}
is the Kronecker’s symbol.

Then, from Theorem 3, we have

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{zk}{p}_{\lambda}=(-1)^{n}\frac{n!}{p!}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)z^{p}.

Thus, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have the following identity.

(26)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{zk}{p}_{\lambda}=(-1)^{n}\frac{n!}{p!}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)z^{p}.

Note that
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{zk}{n}_{\lambda}=(-1)^{n}z^{n}\displaystyle
. It is well known that the Bell polynomials are defined by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=e^{x(e^{t}-1)}.

The compositional inverse of
e_{\lambda}(t)
is denoted by
\log_{\lambda}t
. So
e_{\lambda}(\log_{\lambda}t)=\log_{\lambda}(e_{\lambda}(t))=t
. They are called the degenerate logarithms and given by

(27)

\log_{\lambda}t=\frac{1}{\lambda}(t^{\lambda}-1),~~\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}(1)_{n,\frac{1}{\lambda}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Now, we observe that

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\Big((1+t)^{z}-1\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\Big(e^{z\log(1+t)}-1\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}S_{2}(m,n)\frac{z^{m}}{m!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{m}=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}S_{2}(m,n)z^{m}\sum_{p=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(p,m)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=n}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=n}^{p}S_{2}(m,n)S_{1}(p,m)z^{m}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{p!},

where
S_{2}(m,n)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\lambda}(m,n)
and
S_{1}(m,n)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\lambda}(m,n)
are respectively the Stirling numbers of the second kind and the Stirling numbers of the first kind.

On the other hand,

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\Big((1+t)^{z}-1\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\Big(e_{\lambda}^{z}(\log_{\lambda}(1+t))-1\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\Big(e_{\frac{\lambda}{z}}(z\log_{\lambda}(1+t))-1\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)\frac{z^{m}}{m!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)z^{m}\sum_{p=m}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(p,m)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=n}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=n}^{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)S_{1,\lambda}(p,m)z^{m}\bigg)\frac{t^{p}}{p!}.

Therefore, by (
27
) and (
29
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

For
n,p
, with
n\geq p\geq 0
, the following holds true.

\sum_{m=n}^{p}S_{2}(m,n)S_{1}(p,m)z^{m}=\sum_{m=n}^{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(m,n)S_{1,\lambda}(p,m)z^{m}.

From Theorem 3, we note that

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(y+kz)\bigg\}\

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\bigg\{\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)z^{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}(-1)^{n}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}\sum_{n=0}^{p}x^{n}(-1)^{n}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}\phi_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

From (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)_{k,\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]\displaystyle
. Then we have the following identity:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(y+kz)\bigg\}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}\phi_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

In particular, for
y=0
,

(31)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(kz)\bigg\}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\phi_{m,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

Let us take
x=-1
in (
31
). Then we have

(32)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(kz)\bigg\}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}\phi_{m,\frac{\lambda}{z}}.

Let us take
f_{\lambda}(t)=\binom{t}{p}_{\lambda}=\frac{(t)_{p,\lambda}}{p!}
. Then, from (
31
) and Theorem 3, we get

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{zk}{p}_{\lambda}\bigg\}=\frac{z^{p}}{p!}\phi_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

Here we observe that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{zk}{p}_{\lambda}=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{p!}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)z^{p}=0
, if
n>p
.

From (
8
), we note that

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}k!\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}k!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}k!S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, we have

(35)

F_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

From Theorem 3, we have

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(y+zk)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\bigg\{\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}(-1)^{n}n!S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{p}x^{n}(-1)^{n}n!S_{2,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(p,n)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}F_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

Therefore, by (
36
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(t)_{k,\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]\displaystyle
. Then the following identity holds true.

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(y+zk)\bigg\}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}(y)_{m-p,\lambda}z^{p}F_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

In particular, for
y=0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}f_{\lambda}(zk)\bigg\}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}z^{m}F_{m,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

Let us take
f_{\lambda}(t)=\binom{t}{p}_{\lambda}=\frac{(t)_{p,\lambda}}{p!}
. Then we have
a_{m}=\frac{1}{m!}\delta_{m,p},\ (m,p\geq 0)
. Hence we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^{n}\bigg\{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\binom{zk}{p}_{\lambda}\bigg\}=\frac{1}{p!}z^{p}F_{p,\frac{\lambda}{z}}(-x).

From Theorem 11, we note that

(37)

4.
Conclusion

In this paper, we adopted the ideas of Boyadzhiev on binomial power series and expressed several identities involving degenerate formal power series as those including degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, degenerate Bell polynomials, degenerate Fubini polynomials and degenerate poly-Bernoulli polynomials. In addition, we also obtained some related identities which involve the
\lambda
-falling factorials, the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds, the degenerate Bernoulli numbers, the degenerate Fubini polynomials and the degenerate Euler polynomials.

Here our replacement of power series by degenerate power series is in the same spirit as the recent paper
[8].The Rota’s theory on umbral calculus is based on the linear functionals and the differential operators. The Sheffer sequences occupy the central position in the theory and are characterized by the generating functions involving the usual exponential function. The motivation for [8] started from the question that what if the usual exponential function is replaced by the degenerate exponential functions. It may be said that this question is very natural in view of the regained recent interests in degenerate special numbers and polynomials. As it turns out, it corresponds to replacing the linear functional by the
\lambda
-linear functionals and the differential operator by the
\lambda
-differential operators. In this way, we were led to introduce
\lambda
-umbral calculus and
\lambda
-Sheffer sequences.

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue to pursue our searches for
\lambda
-counterparts of some special polynomials, some special numbers, some transcendental functions and so on.

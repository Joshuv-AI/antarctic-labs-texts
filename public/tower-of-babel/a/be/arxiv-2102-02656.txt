The Incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier Limit from Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac Equation Thanks: August 9, 2026
Ning Jiang, Linjie Xiong, Kai Zhou

Abstract
. We study Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation when quantum effects are taken into account in dilute gas dynamics. By employing new estimates on trilinear terms of collision kernels, we prove the global existence of the classical solution to Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation near equilibrium. Furthermore, the limit from Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation to incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations is justified rigorously. The corresponding formal analysis was given in the thesis of Zakrevskiy [ 49 ] Keywords. Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation, Classical solutions, Navier-Stokes-Fourier Limit.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation.

The evolution of quantum particles obeying Fermi-Dirac statistics can be described by Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla F=C(F),\\
&F\big|_{t=0}=F_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
0\leqslant F(t,x,v)\leqslant 1
is the number density of particles at time
t\geqslant 0
, position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The collision integral
C(F)
takes the form

\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}b(v_{1}-v,\omega)\Big[\,F^{\prime}F_{1}^{\prime}(1-\delta F)(1-\delta F_{1})-FF_{1}(1-\delta F^{\prime})(1-\delta F_{1}^{\prime})\,\Big]\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}.

In the expression above,
\delta=\hbar^{3}
is a constant (
\hbar
is the Planck constant),
F\equiv F(t,x,v)
,
F_{1}\equiv F(t,x,v_{1})
,
F^{\prime}\equiv F(t,x,v^{\prime})
,
F_{1}^{\prime}\equiv F(t,x,v_{1}^{\prime})
, and for
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

v^{\prime}=v+[(v_{1}-v)\cdot\omega]\omega,\quad v_{1}^{\prime}=v_{1}-[(v_{1}-v)\cdot\omega]\omega

are velocities after a collision of two particles with velocities
v
and
v_{1}
before. The particle pairs with the same mass, during the collision, follow the conservation laws of momentum and kinetic energy:

v+v_{1}=v^{\prime}+v_{1}^{\prime},\qquad|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}=|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}.

The collision kernel
b(v_{1}-v,\omega)
is an a.e. positive function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, which encodes features of the molecular interaction in kinetic theory. Physically, it is assumed to depend only on the modulus of the relative velocity
|v-v_{1}|
and on the scalar product
\frac{v_{1}-v}{|v_{1}-v|}\cdot\omega
. We assume the collision kernel takes the factor form:

b(v_{1}-v,\omega)=|v_{1}-v|^{\gamma}\hat{b}(\cos\theta),\quad\cos\theta=\frac{v_{1}-v}{|v_{1}-v|}\cdot\omega,\quad-3<\gamma\leqslant 1.

For
\gamma\geqslant 0
, we call the collision kernel a hard potential; in particular, for
\gamma=0
, we call it a Maxwell collision kernel and for
\gamma=1
, we call the collision hard sphere collision; and for
-3<\gamma<0,
we call it a soft potential. In Grad angular cutoff,
\hat{b}(\cos\theta)
satisfies

\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\hat{b}(\cos\theta)\sin\theta d\theta<+\infty.

Throughout the paper, we take
\delta=1
and the hard sphere collision in (
1.1
) for convenience, namely, the collision kernel has the following explicit expression

(1.2)

b(v_{1}-v,\omega)=|(v_{1}-v)\cdot\omega|.

Next, we list some basic properties of Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation (
1.1
). First, the collision operator
C(F)
satisfies the conservation laws:

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}C(F)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)\mathrm{d}v=0.

Next, as an analog of Boltzmann
H
-theorem, the following three assertions are equivalent
[
49
]
:

(1)

C(F)=0
;

(2)

The entropy production rate is zero,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}C(F)\ln\frac{1-F}{F}\mathrm{d}v=0;

(3)

F
is a Fermi-Dirac distribution,

F_{f,u,\theta}(t,x,v)=\frac{1}{1+\mathrm{exp}(\frac{|v-u|^{2}}{2\theta}-f)}.

In (3) above,
\theta=\theta(t,x)>0
is the temperature,
u=u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the bulk velocity, and
f(t,x)/\theta(t,x)
is the total chemical potential. By the appropriate
choice of Galilean frame, the Fermi-Dirac distribution
F
can be taken with
(f,u,\theta)=(1,0,1)
, we denote it by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mu(v)=\frac{1}{1+e^{\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}-1}}.

The main goals of the current paper are the global existence of classical solutions to Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation, and the connection between kinetic theory for Fermi-Dirac statistics and macroscopic fluid equations. In particular, this paper focuses on the incompressible Navier-Stokes scaling, under which the equation (
1.1
) can be rescaled as

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}F_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla F_{\epsilon}=&\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}C(F_{\epsilon}),\\
F_{\epsilon}\big|_{t=0}=&F_{\epsilon,0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\epsilon
is the so-called Knudsen number which is the ratio between the mean free path and macroscopic length scale. It can be derived the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (for details, see Chapter 3 of the thesis of Zakrevskiy
[
49
]
) from (
1.5
) by fluctuating around the global Fermi-Dirac distribution
\mu
with size
\epsilon
:

F_{\epsilon}=\mu+\epsilon\mu(1-\mu)g_{\epsilon}.

More specifically, putting the perturbation above into (
1.5
) yields:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}g_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lg_{\epsilon}&=\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g_{\epsilon},g_{\epsilon})+T(g_{\epsilon},g_{\epsilon},g_{\epsilon}),\\
g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)|_{t=0}&=g_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)\,.\end{aligned}\right.

From now on, we use the notations

f=f(v),\quad f_{1}=f(v_{1}),\quad f^{\prime}=f(v^{\prime}),\quad f_{1}^{\prime}=f(v_{1}^{\prime}),

for any function
f
, and

\mathcal{N}=\mu_{1}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}(1-\mu_{1})(1-\mu)=\mu_{1}\mu(1-\mu_{1}^{\prime})(1-\mu^{\prime})\,.

Then the linear operator
L
in (
1.6
) is given by

(1.7)

Lg=\nu(v)g-Kg,

and
K=K_{2}-K_{1}
, where the collision frequency
\nu(v)
is

(1.8)

\nu(v)=\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1},

the operator
K_{1}
is

(1.9)

K_{1}g=\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}g_{1}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1},

and the operator
K_{2}
is

(1.10)

K_{2}g=\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}(g_{1}^{\prime}+g^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}.

Moreover, in (
1.6
), the bilinear form
Q(f,g)
is given by

(1.11)

\displaystyle Q(f,g)=

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\bigg\{\bigg(1-\mu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}\bigg)f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}-\bigg(1-\mu_{1}-\mu\bigg)f_{1}g

\displaystyle+\bigg(\mu_{1}^{\prime}f_{1}^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}f^{\prime}\bigg)g_{1}-\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}+f^{\prime}\bigg)\mu_{1}g_{1}+\bigg(\mu_{1}^{\prime}f_{1}^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}f^{\prime}\bigg)g-\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}+f^{\prime}\bigg)\mu g\bigg\}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle Q_{1}(f,g)+Q_{2}(f,g)+\cdots+Q_{6}(f,g).

and the trilinear form
T(f,g,h)
is given by

(1.12)

\displaystyle T(f,g,h)=

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\cdot\bigg\{\mu_{1}\mu f_{1}g\bigg(h_{1}^{\prime}+h^{\prime}\bigg)-\mu_{1}^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}\bigg(h_{1}+h\bigg)

\displaystyle+\mu_{1}\mu_{1}^{\prime}\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}h_{1}-f_{1}gh_{1}^{\prime}\bigg)+\mu\mu_{1}^{\prime}\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}h-f_{1}gh_{1}^{\prime}\bigg)+\mu_{1}\mu^{\prime}\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}h_{1}-f_{1}gh^{\prime}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\mu\mu^{\prime}\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}h-f_{1}gh^{\prime}\bigg)+f_{1}g\bigg(\mu_{1}^{\prime}h_{1}^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime}h^{\prime}\bigg)-f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}\bigg(\mu_{1}h_{1}+\mu h\bigg)\bigg\}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}

\displaystyle T_{1}(f,g,h)+T_{2}(f,g,h)+\cdots+T_{8}(f,g,h).

1.2.
Well-posedness and hydrodynamic limits.

The Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation, which describes the evolution of rarefied gas with quantum effect, is derived from a modification of the classical Boltzmann equation
[
15
]
, when the exclusion Pauli principle are taken into account. It is also often called Uehling-Uhlenbeck equation or Nordheim equation. However, different from the classical case, the rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation has not been established. Since the heuristic arguments of Nordheim
[
43
]
, Uehling and Uhlenbeck
[
47
]
, rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation can be found Sphon
[
46
]
, Erdös, Salmhofer and Yau
[
19
]
and Benedetto, Pulvirenti, Castella and Esposito
[
9
]
.

For mathematical theory of well-posedness, early results were obtained by Dolbeault
[
18
]
and Lions
[
38
]
. They studied the global existence of solutions in mild or distributional sense for the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
under some assumptions on the collision kernel. Furthermore, Dolbeault
[
18
]
obtained that the solution of Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation converges to the solution of the Boltzmann equation as
\delta\to 0
for very special bounded collision kernel case. Allemand
[
5
]
extended the results of
[
18
]
to bounded domains with specular reflection at the boundaries for integrable collision kernels. Alexandre
[
1
]
obtained another kind of weak solution satisfying the entropy inequality, the so-called
H
-solution. For general initial data, Lu
[
40
]
studied the global existence and weak stability of weak solution in
\mathbb{T}_{x}^{3}
for very soft potential with a weak angular cutoff. More results are referred to
[
20
,
21
,
39
,
41
]
.

In the context of classical solution near global equilibrium, much less is known for Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation (after we finished the draft of this paper, we were aware of the just posted paper
[
44
]
on this topic). We first review the corresponding results for Boltzmann equation. Ukai
[
48
]
obtained the first global-in-time smooth solution with cutoff kernel. Later on, Guo
[
27
,
28
]
developed the so-called nonlinear energy method to get the same type of result for soft potential with
\gamma\geq-3
. For more progress in this direction, we refer to
[
2
,
11
,
12
,
25
,
30
,
33
]
.

In the other direction, the hydrodynamic limits from kinetic equations to fluid equations has been very active in recent decades. One of the important feature of kinetic equations is their connection to the fluid equations. The smaller the Knudsen number
\epsilon
is, the more the dilute gas behaves like a fluid. Mathematically, the so-called hydrodynamic limits are the process that the Knudsen number goes to zero. Depending on the physically scalings, different fluid equations (incompressible of compressible Navier-Stokes, Euler, etc.) can be derived from kinetic equations.

Bardos and Ukai
[
8
]
proved the global existence of classical solution
g_{\epsilon}
to scaled Boltzmann equation (perturbed around the global Maxwellian with size
\epsilon
) uniformly in
0<\epsilon<1
for hard potential with cutoff collision kernel. Consequently they justified the limit to incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with small initial data. By employing semigroup approach, Briant
[
10
]
also proved the same limit on the torus for hard cutoff potential, with convergence rate. Recently, Jiang, Xu and Zhao
[
36
]
proved again the same limit for a more general class of collision kernel by using non-isotropic norm developed in the series of work
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
25
]
. For the fluid limits of Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation, Zakrevskiy
[
49
]
formally derived the compressible Euler and Navier-Stokes limits and incompressible Navier-Stokes limits. We also mention that, Filbet, Hu and Jin
[
23
]
introduced a new scheme for quantum Boltzmann equation to capture the Euler limit by numerical computations.

Starting from the solutions to the limiting fluid equations, Caflisch
[
14
]
and Nishida
[
42
]
proved the compressible Euler limit from the Boltzmann equation in the context of classical solution by the Hilbert expansion, and analytic solutions, respectively. Caflisch’s approach was applied to the acoustic limit by Guo, Jang and Jiang
[
31
,
32
,
34
]
by combining with nonlinear energy method. We also mention some more results using Hilbert expansions
[
29
,
35
]
.

The main concern of the current paper is to justify rigorously the formal derivation of Zakrevskiy
[
49
]
. The present paper verifies the incompressible Navier-Stokes limits rigorously in the context of classical solution. Precisely, we first prove the uniform in
\epsilon
global existence of classical solution around the equilibrium to the scaled Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation for the hard sphere collision. More importantly, we obtain the uniform energy estimate, using which we rigorously prove the limit from Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation to Incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations by taking limit as
\epsilon\to 0
.

As indicated in
[
49
]
, the equilibrium of the Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation (
1.1
) is the global Fermi-Dirac distribution (
1.4
). Thus we choose
\mu(1-\mu)
as the weight, which leads to the gain of new nonlinear terms in (
1.6
) in comparing with the case of perturbed Boltzmann equation, and brings us some new difficulties.

2.1.
Properties of the linear operator

The most crucial point in energy estimate is that

L

Proposition 2.1
.

For the operator
L=\nu-K
, we have the following results:

•

There are two constants
C_{1},\;C_{2}>0
such that

(2.1)

C_{1}(1+|v|)\leqslant\nu(v)\leqslant C_{2}(1+|v|).

•

The operator

K:\,L^{2}\left(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\right)\longrightarrow L^{2}\left(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\right)

is compact.

•

The linearized collision operator
L
is symmetric, nonnegative, and its null space is

(2.2)

Null(L)=\operatorname{span}\left\{1,\,v,\,|v|^{2}\right\}.

•

There exists a constant
\lambda>0
such that

(2.3)

\langle Lg\;,g\rangle\geqslant\lambda\left|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\right|_{\nu}^{2}.

We denote the global Maxwellian by

M(v)=e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}.

and the collision frequency in perturbed Boltzmann equation by

\nu_{M}=\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|M(v_{1})\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}.

(

) holds since
\nu
is just replacing

in
\nu_{M}
with
\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}
. The compactness of

is a consequence of compactness criterion in
[

]
, and (

) is the corollary of the three properties above. For more details, see
[
24
,
27

49
]

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

and
\widetilde{h}

we have

(2.5)

\left|\big(Q(f,g),\widetilde{h}\big)\right|\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(|\nu^{1/2}f|_{L_{v}^{2}}|g|_{L_{v}^{2}}+|f|_{L_{v}^{2}}|\nu^{1/2}g|_{L_{v}^{2}}\Big)\left|\nu^{1/2}\widetilde{h}\right|_{L_{v}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x,

and

(2.6)

\left\|\big\langle Q(\overline{f},\overline{g}),\widetilde{h}\big\rangle\right\|\leqslant C\sup\limits_{x,v}\left|(\mu(1-\mu))^{\frac{1}{4}}\widetilde{h}\right|\sup\limits_{x}|f|_{L_{v}^{2}}\|g\|;

for

we have

(2.7)

\left|\big(T(f,g,h),\widetilde{h}\big)\right|\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nu^{1/2}f|_{L_{v}^{2}}|g|_{L_{v}^{2}}+|f|_{L_{v}^{2}}|\nu^{1/2}g|_{L_{v}^{2}}\right)|h|_{L_{v}^{2}}\left|\nu^{1/2}\widetilde{h}\right|_{L_{v}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x,

and

(2.8)

\left\|\left\langle T(\overline{f},\overline{g},\overline{h}),\widetilde{h}\right\rangle\right\|\leqslant C\sup\limits_{x,v}\left|(\mu(1-\mu))^{\frac{1}{4}}\widetilde{h}\right|\sup\limits_{x}|f|_{L_{v}^{2}}\sup\limits_{x}|g|_{L_{v}^{2}}\|h\|.

(\overline{f},\overline{g})
and
(\overline{f},\overline{g},\overline{h})
are permutations of
(f,g)
and
(f,g,h)

Proof.

For simplicity, we suppress the

and
\widetilde{h}(x,v)
, to prove (
2.5
), it’s sufficient to establish the same inequality for

. Recall that

Q_{1}(f,g)=\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\bigg(1-\mu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}\bigg)f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1},

and

Q_{2}(f,g)=-\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\bigg(1-\mu_{1}-\mu\bigg)f_{1}g\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1},

note that

\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\bigg(1-\mu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu^{\prime}\bigg),\quad\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\bigg(1-\mu_{1}-\mu\bigg)

In the following, we will deal with the nonlinear terms that are different from those in the case of perturbed Boltzmann equation. For

\displaystyle\left|\big\langle Q_{3}(f,g),\widetilde{h}\big\rangle\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle C\iiint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|M_{1}MM_{1}^{\prime}|f_{1}^{\prime}|\,|f|\,|\widetilde{h}|\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+C\iiint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|M_{1}MM^{\prime}|f^{\prime}|\,|f|\,|\widetilde{h}|\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\mathrm{d}v,

Due to Proposition
2.2

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left|\big\langle Q_{3}(f,g),\widetilde{h}\big\rangle\right|\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|M_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}M^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{1}^{\prime}|f_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\iiint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|M_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}M^{\frac{3}{2}}M_{1}^{\prime}|f|^{2}\,|\widetilde{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\mathrm{d}v\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v_{1}^{\prime}}^{3}}|f_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}M_{1}^{\prime}\mathrm{d}v_{1}^{\prime}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v^{\prime}}^{3}}|v_{1}^{\prime}-v^{\prime}|{M_{1}^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{2}}{M^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|g|_{L_{v}^{2}}|\nu^{1/2}\widetilde{h}|_{L_{v}^{2}}

\displaystyle C|\nu^{1/2}f|_{L_{v}^{2}}|g|_{L_{v}^{2}}|\nu^{1/2}\widetilde{h}|_{L_{v}^{2}}.

\displaystyle+C\iiint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|MM_{1}M^{\prime}|f^{\prime}|\,|g|\,|\widetilde{h}|\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|g|\,|\widetilde{h}|M\mathrm{d}v\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}M_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|^{2}\,|\cos\theta|^{2}M_{1}{M^{\prime}}^{2}|f^{\prime}|^{2}\mathrm{d}v_{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}\mu_{1}}{1-\mu}f_{1}g\bigg(h_{1}^{\prime}+h^{\prime}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}I_{1}^{1}+I_{1}^{2}.

\displaystyle|T_{3}(f,g,h)|=

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|v_{1}-v|\,|\cos\theta|\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mu(1-\mu)}\mu_{1}\mu_{1}^{\prime}\bigg(f_{1}^{\prime}g^{\prime}h_{1}-f_{1}gh_{1}^{\prime}\bigg)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\right|

For
I_{3}^{2}

3.
The existence of global solutions

We use the standard strategy to obtain the global existence of solution to (
1.6
): construct the local solutions for a sequence of iterating approximate equations and take limit to get local solution to (
1.6
); then obtain a uniform energy estimate; finally, the use of the continuation argument. The uniform energy estimate consists of two parts: one part is to obtain microscopic dissipation rate, the more complicated part is to deal with macroscopic dissipation rate.

3.1.
Construction of local solutions

In order to prove the existence of nonlinear problem (
1.6
), we shall firstly study the following linear Cauchy problem:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}g+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}g+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lg&=\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(f,f)+T(f,f,f),\\
g(t,x,v)|_{t=0}&=g_{0}(x,v),\end{aligned}\right.

where
f
is a given function. For (
3.1
), we have the following result:

Lemma 3.1
.

For
0<\epsilon<1
, assume that

g_{0}\in H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v)),

and that for some
T>0
,
f
satisfies

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(f)(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(f)(t)dt<+\infty.

Then, the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) admits a unique solution:

g(t,x,v)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)

Proof.

We prove the existence of a solution to the Cauchy problem (
3.1
) by the Hahn-Banach theorem. We define the linear operator
\mathcal{T}
by

\mathcal{T}g\equiv\partial_{t}g+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}g+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lg.

Then, we rewrite (
3.1
) into the following form:

\mathcal{T}g=\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(f,f)+T(f,f,f),\quad g(0)=g_{0}.

For
h\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{x,v}^{6})\right)
with
h(T)=0
, we define
\mathcal{T}^{*}
through

(g,\mathcal{T}^{*}h)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}=(\mathcal{T}g,h)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)},

so that
\mathcal{T}^{*}
is the adjoint of the operator
\mathcal{T}
in the Hilbert space
L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
.

We set

\mathbb{W}=\left\{w=\mathcal{T}^{*}h\,\big|\,h\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{x,v}^{6})\right)\text{ with }h(T)=0\right\},

which is a dense subspace of
L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
. We also have that

\mathcal{T}^{*}h=-\partial_{t}h-\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}h+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lh.

Then

(h,\mathcal{T}^{*}h)_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|h(t)\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}(v\cdot\nabla_{x}h,h)_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}(Lh,h)_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}.

Note that the second term above vanishes, and by the estimate (
2.3
), we have that

\int_{t}^{T}(h,\mathcal{T}^{*}h)_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}dt\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\|h(t)\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(h)(s)ds.

Thus, for all
0<t<T
,

\displaystyle\|h(t)\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(h)(s)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([t,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}\|h\|_{L^{2}\left([t,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}.

This implies that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|h(t)\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(h)(s)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}\|h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{T}\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}.

This immediately gives

(3.3)

\|h\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}\leqslant 2\sqrt{T}\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}.

Furthermore, (
3.2
) can be written as follows: let
\mathcal{Y}=\|h\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(h)(s)ds\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{T}\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}\mathcal{Y}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{Y}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{T}{2}\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}^{2}.

Thus,

(3.4)

\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\int_{t}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(h)(s)ds\right)^{1/2}\leqslant\frac{\sqrt{T}}{\sqrt{2\lambda}}\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}.

Next, we define a functional
G
on
\mathbb{W}
as follows:

G(w)=\big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(f,f)+T(f,f,f),h\big)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}+\big(g_{0},h(0)\big)_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}},

note that
0<\epsilon<1
, using (
3.19
) and (
3.26
), we have the estimate

\displaystyle|G(w)|\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(f)\right)\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(f)\right)\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(h)dt\right\}+\|g_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}\|h(0)\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)(t)+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(f)(t)\Big)\left\{\sqrt{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)(t)+\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(f)(t)dt\right)^{1/2}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(h(t))dt\right)^{1/2}+\|g_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}.

Finally (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) imply that

\displaystyle|G(w)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{f,g_{0}}\|\mathcal{T}^{*}h\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|w\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)},

where

\displaystyle C_{f,g_{0}}=2\sqrt{T}\|g_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}}+C\sqrt{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)(t)+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(f)(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\left\{\sqrt{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)(t)+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(f)(t)dt\right)^{1/2}\right\}.

Thus,
G
is a continuous linear functional on
\big(\mathbb{W};\,\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}\big)
. So by the Hahn-Banach Theorem,
G
can be extended from
\mathbb{W}
to
L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
. From the Riesz representation theorem, there exists

g\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right),

such that for any
w\in\mathbb{W}
,

G(w)=(g,w)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}.

Thus fixing
f\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
, by the definitions of the operator
\mathcal{T}^{*}
and
G
, we have for any
h\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{x,v}^{6})\right)
with
h(T)=0
, there exists a unique
g\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
such that,

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}g,\widehat{h})_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,L_{x}^{2}L_{v}^{2}\right)}=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}g,h)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g,\mathcal{T}^{*}h)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H_{x}^{N}L_{v}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(f,f)+T(f,f,f),\widehat{h}\big)_{L^{2}\left([0,T];\,L_{x}^{2}L_{v}^{2}\right)}+\big(g_{0},\widehat{h}(0)\big)_{L_{x}^{2}L_{v}^{2}}.

where

\widehat{h}=\Lambda_{x}^{2N}h\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{x,v}^{6})\right)\text{ with }\widehat{h}(T)=0,

and
\Lambda=(1-\Delta_{x})^{1/2}
,
\Lambda_{x}^{2N}
is used for changing the inner product from

H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\text{ to }L^{2}(\mathrm{d}x;L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v).

Since
\Lambda_{x}^{2N}
is an isomorphism on

h:h\in C^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{x,v}^{6})\right)

with
h(T)=0
,

g\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)

is a solution of the Cauchy problem (
3.1
). Furthermore, using (
3.5
), we also have

\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g)(t)+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g)(t)dt<\widetilde{C}_{f,g_{0}},

here

\widetilde{C}_{f,g_{0}}=C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(f)(t)+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{4}(f)(t)\Big)\left\{T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(f)(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(f)(t)dt\right\}+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g_{0})

∎

Now we concentrate on the local existence of solutions for fully nonlinear problem. Consider the following iterative scheme:

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}g^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}g^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lg^{n+1}&=\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n},g^{n})+T(g^{n},g^{n},g^{n}),\\
g^{n+1}(t,x,v)|_{t=0}&=g_{0}(x,v),\end{aligned}\right.

with
g^{0}=0
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist constants
0<\delta_{0}\leqslant 1
and
0<T\leqslant 1
such that for any
0<\epsilon<1
, if
g_{0}\in H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))
with
\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{0})\leqslant\delta_{0}
, then the iteration problem (
3.6
) admits a sequence of solutions
\{g^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
satisfying

(3.7)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})(t)+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})(t)dt\leqslant 4\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g_{0})

Proof.

It is enough to prove (
3.7
) by induction, since for the linear Cauchy problem (
3.6
), given
g^{n}
satisfying (
3.7
), the existence of
g^{n+1}
is assured by Lemma
3.1
. Applying
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
to (
3.6
), then taking inner product with
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g^{n+1}
in
L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x)
, using (
2.3
), (
3.19
) and (
2.24
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n+1})+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)\left\{\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}(g^{n})+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(g^{n})\right\}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(g^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2}\eta}{2\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n+1})+\frac{1}{2\eta}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{4}(g^{n})\right)\left(\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}\right)^{2}(g^{n})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})\right),

in the last step we have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with
\eta>0
. Thus let
C^{2}\eta=\lambda
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n+1})+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C^{2}}{\lambda}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{4}(g^{n})\right)\left(\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}\right)^{2}(g^{n})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})\right).

Note that we have (
3.17
), integrating on
[0,T]
with
T\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n+1})(t)+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n+1})(t)dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g_{0})+C^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{4}(g^{n})\right)\cdot\left\{T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n}(t))+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n}(t))dt\right\},

we chose
\delta_{0}>0
such that

1+16C^{2}\delta_{0}^{2}\left(1+4\delta_{0}^{2}\right)\leqslant 4,

to complete the proof.
∎

Finally, we prove the convergence of
\{g^{n}\}
using the uniform estimate (
3.7
).

Theorem 3.1

(Local existence)
.

There exist constants
0<\delta_{0}\leqslant 1
and
0<T\leqslant 1
such that for any
0<\epsilon<1
, if
g_{\epsilon,0}\in H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))
with
\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0})\leqslant\delta_{0},
then there is a unique solution

g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)

to the Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation (
3.8
) such that

(3.8)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g_{\epsilon})(t)+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g_{\epsilon})(t)dt\leqslant 4\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g_{\epsilon,0}).

Moreover,
\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon})(t)
:
\left[0,T\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous. If
0\leqslant F(0,x,v)=\mu+\mu(1-\mu)g_{0}(x,v)\leqslant 1
, then

0\leqslant F(t,x,v)=\mu+\mu(1-\mu)g(t,x,v)\leqslant 1.

Proof.

We prove that
\{g^{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x)\right)
. Setting
w^{n}=g^{n+1}-g^{n}
, we deduce from (
3.6
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}w^{n}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}w^{n}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lw^{n}=\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n},g^{n})+T(g^{n},g^{n},g^{n})\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n-1},g^{n-1})+T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},g^{n-1})\right),\\
&w^{n}|_{t=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Since for any
h\in L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu))
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n},g^{n})+T(g^{n},g^{n},g^{n})\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n-1},g^{n-1})+T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},g^{n-1})\right),\mathbf{P}h\right\rangle=0

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n},g^{n})+T(g^{n},g^{n},g^{n})\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n-1},g^{n-1})+T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},g^{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\left[Q(\epsilon w^{n-1},\epsilon g^{n})+T(\epsilon w^{n-1},\epsilon g^{n},\epsilon g^{n})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\left[Q(\epsilon g^{n-1},\epsilon w^{n-1})+T(\epsilon g^{n-1},\epsilon w^{n-1},\epsilon g^{n})\right]+T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},w^{n-1}),

then we have

\displaystyle\left(\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n},g^{n})+T(g^{n},g^{n},g^{n})\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n-1},g^{n-1})+T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},g^{n-1})\right),w^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\Big(Q(\epsilon w^{n-1},\epsilon g^{n})+T(\epsilon w^{n-1},\epsilon g^{n},\epsilon g^{n}),\{\,\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\,\}w^{n}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\left(Q(\epsilon g^{n-1},\epsilon w^{n-1})+T(\epsilon g^{n-1},\epsilon w^{n-1},\epsilon g^{n}),\{\,\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\,\}w^{n}\right)

\displaystyle+\Big(T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},w^{n-1}),\{\,\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\,\}w^{n}\Big).

We need to estimate the three terms on the right hand side above. Note that
0<\epsilon<1
, by (
2.29
), (
2.30
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with
\eta>0
, the first term is bounded by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{0}(w^{n-1})\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n})\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}(w^{n-1})+\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}(w^{n-1})\right)\right\}\left(1+\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n})\right)\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}(w^{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\eta}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n-1})\right)\right\}\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n}),

the second term is bounded by

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{0}(w^{n-1})\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(g^{n-1})+\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n-1})\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}(w^{n-1})+\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}(w^{n-1})\right)\right\}\left(1+\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n})\right)\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}(w^{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\eta}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n-1})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n-1})\right)\right\}\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n}),

and the third term of is bounded by

(3.11)

\displaystyle C\left\{\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n-1})+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(g^{n-1})\right\}\mathcal{E}_{N}(g^{n-1})\mathcal{E}_{0}(w^{n-1})\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}(w^{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\eta}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n-1})\right\}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})+\eta\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n}).

Thus we have the estimate

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left|\Big(\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n},g^{n})+T(g^{n},g^{n},g^{n})\right)-\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g^{n-1},g^{n-1})+T(g^{n-1},g^{n-1},g^{n-1})\right),w^{n}\Big)_{L_{x,v}^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\bigg\{\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})\right]+

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\right)\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n-1})\right]+

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\right]\bigg\}+\frac{3\eta}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{0}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n}).

Let
3\eta=\frac{\lambda}{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|w^{n}\|+\frac{\lambda}{2\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{\eta}\bigg\{\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})\right]+

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\right)\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})+\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n-1})\right]+

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n-1})\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\right]\bigg\},

it follows that

\displaystyle\|w^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,{L_{x}^{2}L_{v}^{2}}\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n})dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{0}^{2}(w^{n-1})\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\right)\times

\displaystyle\left[T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n})dt+T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(g^{n-1})dt\right]

\displaystyle+C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})\right)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n})+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}(g^{n-1})\right]\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n-1})dt.

By using (
3.7
) with
0<\delta_{0}<1
small enough we get that for any
0<\epsilon<1
,

\displaystyle\|w^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,{L_{x,v}^{2}}\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n})dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\{\|w^{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,{L_{x,v}^{2}}\right)}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}(w^{n-1})dt\right\}.

Thus we have proved that
\{g^{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x)\right)
.

The interpolation combining with the estimate (
3.7
) implies that
\{g^{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N-\eta}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
for any
\eta>0
and the limit is in the functional space
L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)
. Finally the estimate (
3.8
) follows from weak lower semicontinuity.

Finally we prove the uniqueness of the local solutions. Let
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
be two local solutions of (
3.8
). Then

g=g_{1}-g_{2}\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)

and it satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}g+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla g+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lg&=\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g,g_{1})+T(g,g_{1},g)+\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g_{2},g)+T(g_{2},g,g)+T(g_{2},g_{2},g),\\
g(t,x,v)|_{t=0}&=0,\end{aligned}\right.

a similar procedure proves that
g\equiv 0
, which implies the uniqueness of the solutions.
∎

In the rest subsections, we devote ourselves to the global energy estimate. The global existence of Cauchy problem (
3.8
) is obtained by a standard continuation argument of local solutions.

3.2.
Microscopic energy estimate

Firstly, we study the estimate on the microscopic part
\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g
in the function space

. For notational simplification, we drop the sub-index
\varepsilon
of the local solution

in the notations

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];\,H^{N}(\mathrm{d}x;\,L^{2}(\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v))\right)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}\leqslant C\left\{\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}\right)\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{4}\right)\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}\right)^{2}\right\}.

Proof.

Applying
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
to (
3.8
) and taking inner product with

in

for

, we get

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant N}\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\right)+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant N}\Big(L\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant N}\Big(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left(Q(\epsilon g,\epsilon g)+T(\epsilon g,\epsilon g,\epsilon g)\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\Big).

where the inner product including

vanishes by integration by parts. By the coercivity of

(
2.3
) we have

(3.15)

\Big(L\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\Big)\geqslant\lambda\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\|_{\nu}^{2}.

3.2
). Fixing

(3.16)

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left(Q(\epsilon g,\epsilon g)+T(\epsilon g,\epsilon g,\epsilon g)\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\Big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\alpha_{1}}\Big(Q(\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}g,\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}g),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}=\alpha}C_{\alpha_{3}\alpha_{4}\alpha_{5}}\Big(T(\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{3}}g,\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{4}}g,\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{5}}g),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\Big).

Without loss of generality, let’s assume
|\alpha_{1}|\leqslant N/2
, then
|\alpha_{2}|>0
. From (

), let
(\overline{f},\overline{g})
be
(f,g)

\left|\Big(Q(\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}g,\epsilon\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{2}}g),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\Big)\right|\leqslant C\epsilon^{2}\mathcal{E}_{N}\left\{\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right\}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}

For the second term on the right hand side of (

for the following three cases:

, we get (

).

(c)
|\alpha_{3}|=0
and
|\alpha_{4}|=0
, then

. Using (
2.31
) with

).

(B) Only one of

\left|\Big(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left(Q(\epsilon g,\epsilon g)+T(\epsilon g,\epsilon g,\epsilon g)\right),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\Big)\right|\leqslant C\epsilon^{2}\Big(\mathcal{E}_{N}+\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}\Big)\left\{\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right\}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}(g).

Repeating the process of (

(3.25)

\Big(T(\epsilon g,\epsilon g,\epsilon g),\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\Big)\leqslant C\epsilon^{3}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}\left\{\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right\}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}.

Hence combining (

\left|\Big(Q(\epsilon g,\epsilon g)+T(\epsilon g,\epsilon g,\epsilon g),g\Big)\right|\leqslant C\epsilon^{2}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}+\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}\right)\left\{\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right\}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}.

We plug (

(3.27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}+\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}\right)\left\{\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}+\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right\}\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}\right)\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}+\frac{\eta}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mic}\right)^{2}+\frac{C}{\eta}\left(\mathcal{D}_{N}^{mac}\right)^{2}\left(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{4}\right).

By taking

we complete the proof.
∎

3.3.
Macroscopic energy estimate

Now we study the macroscopic part
\mathbf{P}g
, where

is a solution of the equation (

).
The key idea is to show the macroscopic part of the solution,
a
,
b
,

, are bounded by the microscopic part
\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g
. Therefore, we plug the decomposition
g=\,\mathbf{P}g\,+\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g
into the Boltzmann-Fermi-Dirac equation (

\mathbf{P}g

(3.28)

\left\{\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\right\}\left(\mathbf{P}g\right)=-\left\{\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}L\right\}\{\,\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\,\}g+\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g,g)+T(g,g,g).

In order to study the time evolution of

\mathbf{P}g=a(t,x)+b(t,x)\cdot v+c(t,x)|v|^{2},

and set the thirteen moments

\big\{e_{k}(v)\big\}_{k=1}^{13}=\left\{\,1,\,v_{i},\,v_{i}v_{j},\,v_{i}|v|^{2}\,\right\},\;i,j=1,2,3.

Then for fixed
t,x

3.29

(3.31)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}a

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}r^{(0)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}m^{(0)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\ell^{(0)}+h^{(0)}

(3.32)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{i}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\partial_{i}a

(3.33)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\partial_{i}b_{i}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}r_{i}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}m_{i}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\ell_{i}^{(2)}+h_{i}^{(2)}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\partial_{i}b_{j}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\partial_{j}b_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\partial_{i}c

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}r_{i}^{(3)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}m_{i}^{(3)}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\ell_{i}^{(3)}+h_{i}^{(3)},

\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g,g)+T(g,g,g)

On the other hand, we introduce some constants:

(3.36)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle p_{0}^{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu\mathrm{d}v,&\displaystyle\qquad p_{2}^{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}\mu\mathrm{d}v,\\

Lemma 3.4
.

It holds that

(3.40)

\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leqslant N-1}\sum\limits_{ij}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\left[\ell^{(0)},\ell_{i}^{(1)},\ell_{i}^{(2)},\ell_{ij}^{(2)},\ell_{i}^{(3)}\right]\right\|\leqslant C\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leqslant N-1}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\right\|,

Let
\{\widetilde{e_{i}}(v)\}_{i=1}^{13}

For the linear term, the coefficients

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
. Let
|\alpha|\leqslant N-1
, (
3.39
) holds for that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\cdot e_{n}(v)\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|e_{n}(v)|^{2}|v|^{2}\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}\nabla\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g|^{2}\mu(1-\mu)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leqslant N}\left\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}g\right\|^{2},

Note that

and
K
is bounded from

to itself, then the same process as above gives the estimates (

For the coefficients

,
h_{i}^{(2)}

,

thus (

∎

.

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,i}^{1}(t)

Abstract
It is shown that a possibly infinite-valued proper lower semicontinuous convex function on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} has an extension to a convex function on the half-space ℝ n × [ 0 , ∞ ) \mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty) which is finite and smooth on the open half-space ℝ n × ( 0 , ∞ ) \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty) . The result is applied to nonlinear elasticity, where it clarifies how the condition of polyconvexity of the free-energy density ψ ⁡ ( D ​ y ) \psi(Dy) is best expressed when ψ ⁡ ( A ) → ∞ \psi(A)\to\infty as det A → 0 + \det A\to 0+ .



∎

Introduction

The main purpose of this paper is to prove the following theorem, giving an extension of a possibly infinite-valued proper lower semicontinuous convex function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a convex function on the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)
which is finite and smooth on the open half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper lower semicontinuous convex function. Then there exists a lower semicontinuous convex function

\varphi:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty],\;\varphi=\varphi(x,y),

such that

(i)

\varphi(0,y)=\Phi(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(ii)

\lim_{x\to 0+}\varphi(x,y)=\Phi(y)
for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(iii)

\varphi:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is smooth,

If
\Phi\geqslant 0
, then
\varphi
can be chosen so that
\varphi\geqslant 0
, and if
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is continuous,
\varphi:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
can be chosen to be continuous. If
\Phi
is strictly convex on
{\rm dom}\,\Phi:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\Phi(y)<\infty\}
then
\varphi
can be chosen to be strictly convex on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following result is an immediate consequence (setting
\Phi^{(j)}(y)=\varphi(j^{-1},y)
).

Corollary 1

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper lower semicontinuous convex function. Then there exists a sequence
\Phi^{(j)}
of smooth convex functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\Phi^{(j)}(y)=\Phi(y)
for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The theorem applies, for example, to the case when
\Phi
is the indicator function
i_{K}
of a nonempty closed convex subset
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined by

i_{K}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if }x\in K\\
\infty&\text{if }x\not\in K.\end{array}\right.

With
K=\{0\}
a suitable smooth strictly convex extension is then given by
\varphi(x,y)=\theta(x,y)-\frac{x}{x+1}
, where

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{|y|^{2}}{x},&\text{if }x>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
0,&\text{if }x=0,y=0,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

which follows as a special case of (
7
) (or (
13
)) below. (We note that with
y
momentum and
x
density the convexity of
\theta
plays an important role in optimal transport, as noted in
benamoubrenier
.)

The theorem was motivated by the problem of proving the existence of energy minimizers in 3D nonlinear elasticity under the assumption of polyconvexity of the free-energy density. In
j26
an apparently weaker version of the polyconvexity condition given in
j8
was used. That this version is indeed weaker follows from Theorem
1.1
, and this is explained in Section
3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first show the existence of an extension
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfying (i), (ii), which in addition is (strictly) decreasing in
x
, giving two different proofs. The first proof is the more direct and provides a wide range of possible extensions, while the second uses infimal convolution and is convenient for proving the assertion in the theorem regarding strict convexity.

Proposition 1

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
there exists a lower semicontinuous convex extension
\tilde{\varphi}=\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)
of
\Phi
to
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is finite for
x>0
, decreasing in
x
, and such that
\lim_{x\to 0+}\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)=\Phi(y)
for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

1st Proof.

We first note that
\Phi
is the supremum of a family of affine functions:

\displaystyle\Phi(y)=\sup_{(\alpha,b)\in S}(\alpha+b\cdot y),\;\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(5)

for some nonempty set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. This is a standard result; see, for example,
(
ekelandtemam
, Proposition 3.1)
,
(
rockafellar70
, Theorem 12.1)
. (In Remark
1
below we note that we can take the family of affine functions to consist of exact affine minorants, but this is not needed for the proof.)

Let
\psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
satisfy

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\psi(t)}{t}=\infty.

(6)

We claim that

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(x,y):=\sup_{(\alpha,b)\in S}\left(\alpha+b\cdot y-\psi\left(|\alpha|+|b|\right)x\right)

(7)

provides a suitable convex extension. Indeed by (
5
)
\tilde{\varphi}(0,y)=\Phi(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and since it is the supremum of continuous affine functions
\tilde{\varphi}
is convex and lower semicontinuous.

Given
x>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by (
6
) there exists
M(x,y)>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(|\alpha|+|b|)}{|\alpha|+|b|}>x^{-1}\max(1,|y|)\;\;\text{ if }|\alpha|+|b|>M(x,y).

(8)

Hence for
|\alpha|+|b|>M(x,y)
we have

\displaystyle\alpha+b\cdot y-\psi(|\alpha|+|b|)x\leqslant 0.

(9)

Therefore, since
\psi\geqslant 0
,
\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)\leqslant\max(1,|y|)M(x,y)<\infty
, as required.

\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)
is nonincreasing in
x
, and can be made decreasing by adding
-x
to
\tilde{\varphi}
.

Since
\tilde{\varphi}
is lower semicontinuous

\displaystyle\Phi(y)=\tilde{\varphi}(0,y)\leqslant\liminf_{x\to 0+}\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)\leqslant\limsup_{x\to 0+}\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)

(10)

\displaystyle\hskip-79.49744pt\leqslant\sup_{(\alpha,b)\in S}(\alpha+b\cdot y)=\Phi(y),

so that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0+}\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)=\Phi(y).

(11)

2nd Proof.

Define
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to[0,\infty]
by (
1
).
Note that
\theta
is convex and lower semicontinuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
; the convexity follows, for example, from the identity

\displaystyle\lambda\theta(x_{1},y_{1})+(1-\lambda)\theta(x_{2},y_{2})-\theta(\lambda(x_{1},y_{1})+(1-\lambda)(x_{2},y_{2}))

(12)

\displaystyle\hskip-130.08621pt=\frac{\lambda(1-\lambda)}{\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}}\left|\sqrt{\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}}y_{2}-\sqrt{\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}}y_{1}\right|^{2}\geqslant 0

for
\lambda\in[0,1]
and
(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and examining the behaviour of
\theta
along lines in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

Let
\tilde{\varphi}=\Phi\,\square\,\theta
be the infimal convolution of
\Phi
and
\theta
with respect to
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\displaystyle(\Phi\,\square\,\theta)(x,y)=\inf_{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\Phi(y^{\prime})+\theta(x,y-y^{\prime})\right).

(13)

The convexity of
\Phi
and
\theta
implies that the function

\displaystyle h(x,y,y^{\prime}):=\Phi(y^{\prime})+\theta(x,y-y^{\prime})

(14)

is convex on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. Hence by
(
bauschkeetal
, Prop. 8.26)

\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)=\inf_{y^{\prime}}h(x,y,y^{\prime})
is convex in
(x,y)
. Since
\Phi
is proper, there exists
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\Phi(\bar{y})<\infty
. Therefore for
x>0
we have that
\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)\leqslant\Phi(\bar{y})+\frac{|y-\bar{y}|^{2}}{x}<\infty
. Also
\tilde{\varphi}(0,y)=\min\,(\Phi(y),\infty)=\Phi(y)
, so that
\tilde{\varphi}
is an extension of
\Phi
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\Phi(y)\geqslant\alpha+b\cdot y\text{ for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ and some }\alpha\in\mathbb{R},b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(15)

Hence for
x>0

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)\geqslant\inf_{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\alpha+b\cdot y^{\prime}+\frac{|y-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{x}\right)=\alpha+b\cdot y-\frac{|b|^{2}x}{4}>-\infty,

(16)

so that
\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)
is finite, and thus by convexity continuous on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
\tilde{\varphi}
were not lower semicontinuous there would exist a sequence
(x_{j},y_{j})\to(0,y)
and
y^{\prime}_{j}
with

\displaystyle\sup_{j}\left(\Phi(y_{j}^{\prime})+\frac{|y_{j}-y_{j}^{\prime}|^{2}}{x_{j}}\right)<\Phi(y).

(17)

In particular the left-hand side of (
17
) is bounded, and so, using (
15
),
y_{j}^{\prime}\to y
. Thus by the lower semicontinuity of
\Phi
the left-hand side is greater than or equal to
\Phi(y)
, a contradiction. If
x_{j}\to 0+
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then by the lower semicontinuity
\Phi(y)\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x_{j},y)\leqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(x_{j},y)\leqslant\Phi(y)
, so that
\lim_{x\to 0+}\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)=\Phi(y)
as required.

Clearly
\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)
is nonincreasing in
x
. As defined it may not be decreasing (consider the case
\Phi\equiv 0
), but
\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)-x
is decreasing in
x
and satisfies the other requirements.
∎

Assume in addition to the hypotheses of Proposition

Proof

We use the construction in the second proof of Proposition
1
. Fix
x>0
. Given

, by (

) the minimum of

implies that

is unique. Given distinct

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(x,\lambda y+(1-\lambda)\bar{y})

\displaystyle=\lambda\tilde{\varphi}(x,y)+(1-\lambda)\tilde{\varphi}(x,\bar{y}).

Hence

is strictly convex in

.

To complete the proof we use the following lemma.

Lemma 1

Let
f:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be convex with

strictly convex in

for each

. If

.

Proof

g
is convex. If

were not strictly convex then there would exist distinct pairs

and

with

\displaystyle f(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2}),\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})+\psi(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})

\displaystyle=

(20)

\displaystyle\hskip-180.67499pt\lambda f(x_{1},y_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(x_{2},y_{2})+\lambda\psi(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\psi(x_{2}).

It follows from (

) and the convexity of

that
\psi(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2})=\lambda\psi(x_{1})+(1-\lambda)\psi(x_{2})
, and since

. But then (

in

.∎

Now let
\psi:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be strictly convex and decreasing with

(for example,

\rho=\rho(x,y)\geqslant 0

Remark 1

In (
5
) we can take
S
to consist of all points
(\Phi(y_{0})-b(y_{0})\cdot y_{0},b(y_{0}))
where
y_{0}
belongs to the domain
{\rm dom}\,\partial\Phi
of the subdifferential
\partial\Phi
of
\Phi
and
b(y_{0})\in\partial\Phi(y_{0})
. That is
\Phi
is the supremum of all its exact affine minorants. This fact is not typically given in standard texts on convex analysis, although
(
phelps
, Corollary 3.21)
gives such a result for points
y
where
\Phi(y)<\infty
. The result is stated (for Hilbert spaces) in the paper of Moreau
(
moreau65
, Section 8.c)
(see also
(
moreau67
, Section 13)
), and follows from his theorem
(
moreau65
, Section 8.b)
(see also
(
rockafellar70
, Theorem 24.9)
) that if
\Phi,\Psi
are proper lower semicontinuous convex functions with
\partial\Phi(y)\subset\partial\Psi(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
then
\Phi=\Psi+c
for some constant
c
. Indeed if we define

\displaystyle\Psi(y)=\sup_{y_{0}\in{\rm dom}\,\partial\Phi,b(y_{0})\in\partial\Phi(y_{0})}\Phi(y_{0})+b(y_{0})\cdot(y-y_{0}),

(28)

then
\Phi\geqslant\Psi
and for any
y_{0}\in{\rm dom}\,\partial\Phi
and
b(y_{0})\in\partial\Phi(y_{0})
we have for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\Psi(y)\geqslant\Phi(y_{0})+b(y_{0})\cdot(y-y_{0})\geqslant\Psi(y_{0})+b(y_{0})\cdot(y-y_{0}).

(29)

Hence
\Psi(y_{0})=\Phi(y_{0})
and therefore
b(y_{0})\in\partial\Psi(y_{0})
. Hence by the result of Moreau
\Psi=\Phi+c
for some constant
c
. But
{\rm dom}\,\partial\Phi
is nonempty (for example because
\partial\Phi
is maximal monotone) and so
c=0
and
\Psi=\Phi
.

If
\Phi
is not strictly convex on

then the function

. Indeed if

Remark 4

An interesting open problem is to determine the pairs
\Phi_{0}
and
\Phi_{1}
of proper lower semicontinuous convex functions on

3
Polyconvexity conditions

In this section we give an application of Theorem
1.1
to 3D nonlinear elasticity. Denote by
M^{3\times 3}
the space of real
3\times 3
matrices. Consider an elastic body occupying a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
in a reference configuration. The total free energy at a constant temperature corresponding to a deformation
y:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by

\displaystyle I(y)=\int_{\Omega}\psi(Dy(x))\,dx,

(44)

where the free-energy density
\psi:M^{3\times 3}_{+}\to[0,\infty),
and

M^{3\times 3}_{+}:=\{A\in M^{3\times 3}:\det A>0\}
.

To help prevent interpenetration of matter it is usually assumed that

\displaystyle\psi(A)\to\infty\text{ as }\det A\to 0+,

(45)

which implies that if
I(y)<\infty
then

\det Dy(x)>0\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega
.

In order to prove existence of an absolute minimizer of
I
it is necessary to suppose, among other hypotheses, that
\psi
satisfies a suitable convexity condition. The convexity condition assumed in
j8
(see
ciarlet83
for a clear and more recent exposition) is that
\psi
is
polyconvex
, that is
there is a convex function
g:M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
such that

\displaystyle\psi(A)=g(A,{\rm cof}A,\det A)\text{ for all }A\in M^{3\times 3}_{+},

(46)

where
{\rm cof}A
denotes the matrix of cofactors of
A
. Given
\delta>0
, define
E_{\delta}=\{(A,{\rm cof}A,\delta):\det A=\delta\}
. Since, as is proved in
(
j8
, Theorem 4.3)
, the convex hull of
E_{\delta}
in
M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\times\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{19}
is equal to
M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\times\{\delta\}
, it follows from (
46
) that
g(A,H,\delta)<\infty
for all
A,H\in M^{3\times 3}
and
\delta>0
.

In
j8
it was further assumed that
g
is continuous with
g(A,H,0)=\infty
for all
A,H\in M^{3\times 3}
. Provided that
\psi(A)\to\infty
as
|A|\to\infty
this implies that (
45
) holds.

Later, in
j26
(see also
ciarlet83
) it was observed that existence could be proved if one only assumes (
45
) and that (
46
) holds for a convex
g:M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. But it is not immediately obvious that this really is a weaker hypothesis. Applying Theorem
1.1
we see that it is.

Theorem 3.1

There exists a smooth polyconvex function
\psi:M^{3\times 3}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying (
45
) for which the corresponding
g
is continuous but does not satisfy
g(A,H,0)=\infty
for all
A,H\in M^{3\times 3}
.

Proof

Let
V=\{(A,{\rm cof}A):\det A=0\}
. Then
V
is a closed subset of
M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}
and
({\mathbf{1}},{\mathbf{1}})\not\in V
, where
{\mathbf{1}}
is the identity
3\times 3
matrix. Let
r>0
be such that
\det A>0
if
|A-{\mathbf{1}}|\leqslant r
, where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
M^{3\times 3}\cong\mathbb{R}^{9}
. Define
\Phi:M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\to[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle\Phi(A,H)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{r^{2}-|A-{\mathbf{1}}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}-|H-{\mathbf{1}}|^{2}}&\text{if }|A-{\mathbf{1}}|<r,|H-{\mathbf{1}}|<r\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then
\Phi
is convex and continuous, so that by Theorem
1.1
there exists a continuous convex function
g:M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
such that
g(A,H,0)=\Phi(A,H)
for all
A,H\in M^{3\times 3}
and
g(A,H,\delta)
is smooth for
\delta>0
. Define
\psi(A)=g(A,{\rm cof}A,\det A)+|A|^{2}
. Then
\psi:M^{3\times 3}_{+}\to[0,\infty)
is smooth and polyconvex,
\psi(A)\geqslant|A|^{2}
and
g({\mathbf{1}},{\mathbf{1}},0)<\infty
. If
\det A^{(j)}\to 0+
then we may assume either that
|A^{(j)}|\to\infty
, in which case
\psi(A^{(j)})\to\infty
, or that
A^{(j)}\to A\in M^{3\times 3}
with
\det A=0
, when

\psi(A^{(j)})=g(A^{(j)},{\rm cof}A^{(j)},\det A^{(j)})\to g(A,{\rm cof}A,0)=\infty
. Hence (
45
) holds.∎

From the point of view of mechanics, Theorem
3.1
is unsatisfactory because the
\psi
constructed does not satisfy the physically necessary

frame-indifference condition

\displaystyle\psi(RA)=\psi(A)\text{ for all }R\in SO(3),A\in M^{3\times 3}_{+},

(50)

which is not used for the proofs of existence in
j8
;
j26
. In addition one would like an example which is also
isotropic
, so that

\displaystyle\psi(AQ)=\psi(A)\text{ for all }Q\in SO(3),A\in M^{3\times 3}_{+}.

(51)

However we can adapt Example
1
to give a frame-indifferent and isotropic example.

Example 3

The frame-indifferent and isotropic function

\displaystyle\psi(A)=\frac{|A|^{2}}{\det A}

(52)

is polyconvex with corresponding
g:M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
given by

\displaystyle g(A,H,\delta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{|A|^{2}}{\delta},&A,H\in M^{3\times 3},\delta>0\\
0,&(A,H,\delta)=(0,0,0)\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and
\psi(A)\to\infty
as
\det A\to 0+
.

That
g
is convex and lower semicontinuous follows as for
\theta
(see (
12
)), while Hadamard’s inequality
|A|^{3}\geqslant 3^{\frac{3}{2}}\det A
implies that
\psi(A)\geqslant 3(\det A)^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.

If
\psi
is polyconvex and frame-indifferent, we can without loss of generality suppose that the corresponding
g
satisfies the invariance condition

\displaystyle g(RA,RH,\delta)=g(A,H,\delta)\text{ for all }R\in SO(3),A,H\in M^{3\times 3},\delta\in[0,\infty).

(57)

Indeed we can replace
g
by

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(A,H,\delta)=-\hskip-10.84006pt\int_{SO(3)}g(RA,RH,\delta)\,d\mu(R),

(58)

where

-\hskip-10.84006pt\int_{SO(3)}f(R)\,d\mu(R):=\frac{\int_{SO(3)}f(R)\,d\mu(R)}{\mu(SO(3))}

and
\mu
denotes Haar measure on
SO(3)
. Then
\tilde{g}
satisfies (
57
), is convex, and by (
50
) and the relation
{\rm cof}(RA)=R\,{\rm cof}A
we have

\displaystyle\psi(A)

\displaystyle=g(A,{\rm cof}A,\det A)

\displaystyle=-\hskip-10.84006pt\int_{SO(3)}g(RA,R\,{\rm cof}A,\det A)\,d\mu(R)

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}(A,{\rm cof}A,\det A).

(59)

But, as is well known,
0
belongs to the convex hull of
SO(3)
. Explicitly,
0=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{3}R_{i}
, where
R_{0}={\mathbf{1}},R_{i}=-{\mathbf{1}}+e_{i}\otimes e_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
, and
e_{i}
is the unit vector in the
i^{\rm th}
coordinate direction. So for any
A,H

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(0,0,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{g}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{1}{4}R_{i}A,\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{1}{4}R_{i}H,0\right)

(60)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{1}{4}\tilde{g}(R_{i}A,R_{i}H,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{1}{4}\right)\tilde{g}(A,H,0)=\tilde{g}(A,H,0),

so that
\tilde{g}(0,0,0)=\infty
implies
\tilde{g}(A,H,0)=\infty
for all
A,H
. Thus to construct an example we need
\tilde{g}(0,0,0)<\infty
, as in Example
3
.

In Theorem
3.1

g(\cdot,\cdot,0)
is finite on an open subset of
M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}
. However no such example is possible for
\tilde{g}
. Indeed, if
\tilde{g}(\cdot,\cdot,0)<\infty
on an open set
U\subset M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}
then
\tilde{g}(A,H,0)<\infty
for
(A,H)
in the open set
\{(RA,RH):(A,H)\in U,R\in SO(3)\}
, the convex hull of which is therefore open, and which contains
(0,0)
by (
60
). Therefore
g(A,H,0)<\infty
for
(A,H)
in some open ball
B(0,r)
with centre
0\in M^{3\times 3}\times M^{3\times 3}
. Since
g(\cdot,\cdot,0)
is convex, it is continuous and bounded on
B(0,r/2)
. Similarly
\tilde{g}(\cdot,\cdot,1)
is bounded on
B(0,r/2)
, so that by convexity
\tilde{g}
is bounded on
B(0,r/2)\times[0,1]
. But then
\psi(A)=\tilde{g}(A,{\rm cof}A,\det A)
is bounded as
A\to 0
with
\det A>0
.

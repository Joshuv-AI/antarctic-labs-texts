Abstract
. We define non–ordinary instanton bundles on Fano threefolds X X extending the notion of (ordinary) instanton bundles introduced in [ 14 ] . We determine a lower bound for the quantum number of a non–ordinary instanton bundle, i.e. the degree of its second Chern class, showing the existence of such bundles for each admissible value of the quantum number when i X ≥ 2 i_{X}\geq 2 or i X = 1 i_{X}=1 , rk ​ Pic ⁡ ( X ) = 1 \mathrm{rk}\operatorname{Pic}(X)=1 and X X is ordinary. In these cases we deal with the component inside the moduli spaces of simple bundles containing the vector bundles we construct and we study their restriction to lines. Finally we give a monadic description of non–ordinary instanton bundles on ℙ 3 {\mathbb{P}^{3}} and the smooth quadric studying their loci of jumping lines, when of the expected codimension.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a threefold over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
. We say that
X
is a
Fano threefold
if its anticanonical line bundle
\omega_{X}^{-1}
is ample. The greatest integer
i_{X}
such that
\omega_{X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-i_{X}h)
for some ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is called
the index
of
X
: it is well–known that such an
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
is uniquely determined and it is called the
fundamental line bundle
of
X
.

The very first examples of Fano threefold are
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
and the smooth quadric
Q\subseteq{\mathbb{P}^{4}}
. In this cases
i_{{\mathbb{P}^{3}}}=4
and
i_{Q}=3
respectively and the fundamental line bundle is the one cut out by the hyperplanes. Thus the origin of the study of vector bundles supported on Fano threefolds may be traced back to the seminal papers
[
7
,
29
,
24
]
, inspecting the case of the projective
3
–space, and to
[
49
,
48
]
for
Q
.

In the aforementioned papers, the authors focus their attention on
\mu
–stable bundles. Recall that the number
\mu({\mathcal{F}})=c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})h^{2}/\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})
is defined for each torsion–free sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
. The torsion–free sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
is called
\mu
–semistable
(resp.
\mu
–stable
) if for all proper subsheaves
{\mathcal{G}}
with
0<\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{G}})<\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})
we have
\mu({\mathcal{G}})\leq\mu({\mathcal{F}})
(resp.
\mu({\mathcal{G}})<\mu({\mathcal{F}})
).

Among the
\mu
–stable rank two vector bundles on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, a relevant role is played by
instanton bundles
, i.e.
\mu
–stable rank two vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
such that
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0
and
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-2)\big)=0
. Instanton bundles on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
carry many interesting properties and they have been thoroughly studied in the past and recent years.

Starting from
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, the notion of instanton bundle has been widely generalized, e.g. to projective space and smooth quadrics of any dimension. A first generalization to the Fano threefolds with Picard number
\varrho_{X}:=\mathrm{rk}\operatorname{Pic}(X)=1
can be found in
[
25
]
(see also
[
38
]
). More precisely, an instanton bundle is defined in
[
25
]
as a
\mu
–stable rank
2
bundle with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=(2q_{X}-i_{X})h
and
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}h)\big)=0
where
q_{X}=\left[\frac{i_{X}}{2}\right]
. The existence of instanton bundles according to this definition is proved for
i_{X}=2,3
in
[
25
]
and for
i_{X}=1
in
[
10
]
.

In
[
41
]
, the authors extended the notion of instanton bundle to the flag manifold
F(0,1,2)
which is a Fano threefold with
\varrho_{X}=2
. More recently, the definition has been further generalized to each Fano threefold, regardless of
i_{X}
or
\varrho_{X}
: see
[
14
, Definition 1.2]
. Moreover, the existence of instanton bundles according to the latter definition has been settled on some Fano threefolds: see
[
14
,
17
,
4
,
3
]
.

In any case, each instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
satisfies
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})\in\{0,-h\}
according to the parity of
i_{X}
. E.g.
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=0
when
X\cong{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
. Nonetheless, rank
2
vector bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-1
on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
are certainly interesting and they have been classical object of study (e.g. see
[
31
,
11
,
23
]
) for low values of
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})
.

Motivated by the above considerations, in this paper we give the following definition, where for
\varepsilon\in\left\{0,1\right\}
we set

q_{X}^{\varepsilon}=\left[\frac{i_{X}+1-\varepsilon}{2}\right]

and we denote the integral cohomology of
X
by
H^{i}(X)
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

A vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank 2 on
X
is called an instanton bundle if the following properties hold:

•

c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-\varepsilon h
, where
\varepsilon\in\left\{0,1\right\}
;

•

{\mathcal{E}}
is
\mu
–semistable with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
and
(1-\varepsilon)h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)=0
;

•

h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}h)\big)=0
.

The class
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}})\in H^{4}(X)
and its degree are respectively called (topological) charge and quantum number of
{\mathcal{E}}
.

We spend now a few words in order to better explain Definition
1.1
above. When
\varepsilon=1
we do not require the vanishing
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)=0
in the above definition. On the one hand, in spite of this apparent asymmetry, the vanishing of
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)
when
\varepsilon=1
is actually a direct consequence of the
\mu
–semistability of
{\mathcal{E}}
, because
\mu({\mathcal{E}})<0
. On the other hand, when
\varepsilon=0
the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}:={\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{\oplus 2}
certainly satisfies all the properties of Definition
1.1
but
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)=0
. Moreover, when
i_{X}\leq 2
it is not difficult to give further examples of non–trivial rank
2
bundles with an analogous behaviour and with arbitrarily large quantum number: e.g. every rank
2
bundle associated via the Serre correspondence to a disjoint set of integral curves of degree
3-i_{X}
in
X
.

In order to have a rough idea of the meaning of the
instantonic condition
, i.e. the vanishing condition for the degree
1
cohomology in Definition
1.1
above, we will show in Lemma
2.4
that
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(th)\big)=0
for
t\leq-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}
for each
\mu
–semistable rank
2
vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-\varepsilon h
,
\varepsilon\in\left\{0,1\right\}
. If
X\cong{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, thanks to
[
40
, Theorem 7]
the vanishing

h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}((1-q_{{\mathbb{P}^{3}}}^{\varepsilon})h)\big)=h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-h)\big)=0,

implies that
{\mathcal{E}}
is a direct sum of line bundles: the same is true for
X\cong Q
and
\varepsilon=0
. In particular, in these cases,
-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}=-2
is the greatest integer such that there could exist an indecomposable
{\mathcal{E}}
as above with
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(th)\big)=0
for
t\leq-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}
.

In Section
2
we list some helpful facts about vector bundles and Fano threefolds that we will use throughout the whole paper.

When
\varrho_{X}=1
, each instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
is
\mu
–stable, hence
simple
, i.e.

\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}\big)\cong H^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}^{\vee}\big)\cong\mathbb{C}.

When
\varrho_{X}\geq 2
this is no longer true. Nevertheless in Section
3
we prove the following result characterizing the group of endomorphisms of an instanton bundle.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is an instanton bundle on
X
, the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is indecomposable, then it is simple.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is decomposable, then
{\mathcal{E}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D-\varepsilon h)
,
where
Dh^{2}=-\varepsilon h^{3}/2
and

\begin{gathered}h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D)\big)=h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D-\varepsilon h)\big)=0,\\
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}h)\big)=h^{1}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D-(\varepsilon+q_{X}^{\varepsilon})h)\big)=0.\end{gathered}

(1.1)

In this case,
\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}\big)\cong\mathbb{C}^{\oplus 2}
acting diagonally.

When
i_{X}-\varepsilon
is even, instanton bundles in the sense of Definition
1.1
coincide with instanton bundles as defined in
[
25
]
and
[
14
]
, because
q_{X}=q_{X}^{\varepsilon}
. For this reason, we give the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

An instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-\varepsilon h
is called even or odd (resp. ordinary, non–ordinary), if
\varepsilon
(resp.
i_{X}-\varepsilon
) is even or odd respectively.

As we already pointed out above, ordinary instanton bundles have been widely studied. The present paper is essentially focused on the non–ordinary instanton bundle, studying their elementary properties, constructing examples and studying the corresponding points in the moduli spaces where they sit.

More precisely, in Section
4
we deal with non–ordinary instanton bundles and we prove the following lower bound for the quantum numbers of such bundles (see
[
14
, Lemma 4.2]
for a similar bound in the ordinary case).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is an indecomposable non–ordinary instanton bundle on
X
, then its quantum number is even and satisfies

k\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\text{if either $i_{X}\geq 3$ or $i_{X}=2$ and $h^{3}\leq 5$,}\\
4&\text{if either $i_{X}=2$ and $h^{3}\geq 6$ or $i_{X}=1$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

The above lower bound is actually sharp when
i_{X}\geq 2
. Indeed, in Construction
5.2
we use the Serre correspondence to associate certain bundles of rank
2
to a suitable set of pairwise disjoint conics, which enables us to prove the following result in Section
5
extending
[
29
, Example 3.1.2]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with
i_{X}\geq 2
and very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

For each even integer
k
satisfying Inequality (
1.2
), Construction
5.2
gives a
\mu
–stable, non–ordinary instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
with quantum number
k
.

When
i_{X}=1
the existence of even instanton bundles is slightly less immediate. Indeed Construction
5.2
returns rank
2
bundles which are certainly not instanton, because they have non–zero sections. In particular the approach via Serre correspondence is certainly not possible if
X
is a
prime Fano threefold
, i.e. a Fano threefold such that
\varrho_{X}=1
as we show at the beginning of Section
6
.

We also describe therein a completely different approach based on a suitable chain of deformations of the bundles obtained via Construction
5.2
, showing that such an approach leads to instanton bundles and proving the following result extending
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
at least when
X
is
ordinary
, i.e. it contains a line with normal bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-1)
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
X
be an ordinary prime Fano threefold with very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

For each even integer
k\geq 4
, there exists a
\mu
–stable, even instanton bundle with quantum number
k
.

Again
k=4
is the minimal admissible value for the quantum number of an even instanton bundle on an ordinary prime Fano threefold given by Theorem
1.4
. Thus Theorem
1.4
is sharp also if
i_{X}=1
, at least in the case
\varrho_{X}=1
.

The problem of the existence of instanton bundles when
\varrho_{X}\geq 2
remains wide open in general, though it is not difficult to show the existence of even instanton bundles in several specific cases via Serre construction. Finally, in Remark
6.7
we suggest a completely different possible approach for constructing minimal instanton bundles when
\varrho_{X}\geq 2
.

An important property of instanton bundles on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, occasionally assumed in some classical definitions, is that they are trivial when restricted to the general line, thanks to the Grauert–Mülich theorem (see
[
46
, Corollary 2 of Theorem II.2.1.4]
). A line on
X
is a curve
L
with Hilbert polynomial
t+1
, i.e. such that
\deg(L)=1
and
p_{a}(L)=0
: if
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
is very ample, then lines on
X
are exactly the curves which are lines with respect to the embedding induced by
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
. In what follows we will denote by
\Lambda(X)
the Hilbert scheme of lines on
X
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

We say that an instanton bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
on
X
with
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-\varepsilon h
is generically trivial on the component
\Lambda(X)_{0}\subseteq\Lambda(X)
, if
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}((i_{X}+\varepsilon-2q_{X}^{\varepsilon}-1)h)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\big)=0
when
L\in\Lambda(X)_{0}
is general.
{\mathcal{E}}
is generically trivial, if it is generically trivial on each component.

When
i_{X}\geq 2
, then each Fano threefold is covered by lines, while this is no longer true if
i_{X}=1
.

Generically trivial ordinary instanton bundles have been constructed on several Fano threefolds (
[
25
,
41
,
4
,
17
,
3
]
). Thus it is quite natural to analyze the behaviour of the instanton bundles whose existence is guaranteed by Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
when restricted to general lines: we make such an analysis in Section
7
where we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

Then the bundles obtained via Construction
5.2
are generically trivial. Moreover, the general bundle as in Theorem
1.6
is generically trivial.

In the same section, we also show that such bundles also behave well when restricted to smooth and irreducible divisors (see Proposition
7.2
).

Theorem
1.2
implies that indecomposable instanton bundles correspond to points inside the moduli space
{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
of simple torsion–free sheaves (see
[
1
]
for details about such a space). Moreover, the instantonic condition implies by semicontinuity that such points fill an open subset
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{I}}_{X}
.

When
X\cong{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
, the moduli space of instanton bundles with fixed charge is irreducible (see
[
50
,
51
]
) and smooth (see
[
37
]
). In
[
25
]
, the author proves that for many other families of Fano threefolds
X
with
\varrho_{X}=1
the moduli space of instanton bundles with fixed charge has a generically smooth irreducible component. Similar results have been obtained in
[
41
,
14
,
4
,
17
,
3
]
for other families of Fano threefolds.

In Section
8
we check that the instanton bundles given by Construction
5.2
represent smooth points of one and the same component of their moduli space (see Proposition
8.1
).

An instanton bundle is called
minimal
, if its quantum number
k
is as small as possible: they typically carry additional properties (e.g. see
[
14
, Remark 4.3]
). As a by–product of Theorem
1.5
we infer that the moduli space of minimal non–ordinary instanton bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
on Fano threefolds
X
with
i_{X}=2
and
\varrho_{X}=1
is irreducible and smooth, because such bundles are the ones studied in
[
49
]
(see Remark
8.3
). In particular such minimal non–ordinary instanton bundles are
aCM (with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
)
, i.e.
h^{i}\big({\mathcal{E}}(th)\big)=0
for
i=1,2
and each
t\in\mathbb{Z}
(see also
[
6
]
).

Moreover, we also show that each instanton bundle obtained as in Theorem
1.6
is a smooth point inside the component of the moduli space which contains it (see Proposition
8.5
).

Finally, in the last Section
9
, we focus on non-ordinary instanton bundles on the two Fano threefolds with
i_{X}\geq 3
. In particular we show that non-ordinary instanton bundles on
{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
can be realized as cohomology of a monad, similarly to the classical ordinary case. Using such a monad we are also able to explicitly describe the locus of jumping lines for non–ordinary instanton bundles.

2.
General results

We list below some general helpful results used throughout the whole paper. Let
X
be any smooth variety with canonical line bundle
\omega_{X}
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a vector bundle of rank
2
on
X
and let
s\in H^{0}\big({\mathcal{F}}\big)
. In general its zero–locus

(s)_{0}\subseteq X
is either empty or has codimension at most

2
. We can always write
(s)_{0}=Z\cup S

where
Z
has codimension
2
(or it is empty) and
S
has pure codimension

1
(or it is empty). In particular
{\mathcal{F}}(-S)
has a section vanishing
on
Z
, thus we can consider its Koszul complex

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(S)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}(-S)\otimes\det({\mathcal{F}})\longrightarrow 0.

(2.1)

Moreover we also have the standard exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\longrightarrow 0

(2.2)

Sequence
2.1
tensored by
{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
yields
{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}/{\mathcal{I}}^{2}_{Z|X}\cong{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}(S)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
, whence the normal bundle of
Z
inside
X
satisfies

{\mathcal{N}}_{Z|X}\cong{\mathcal{F}}(-S)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}.

(2.3)

Thus
Z
is locally complete intersection inside
X
, because
\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})=2
. In particular, it has no embedded components.

The Serre correspondence allows us to revert the above construction as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
Z\subseteq X
be a local complete intersection subscheme of codimension
2
.

If
\det({\mathcal{N}}_{Z|X})\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\otimes\mathcal{L}
for some
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)
such that
h^{2}\big(\mathcal{L}^{\vee}\big)=0
, then there exists a vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
of rank
2
on
X
such that:

(1)

\det({\mathcal{F}})\cong\mathcal{L}
;

(2)

{\mathcal{F}}
has a section
s
such that
Z
coincides with the zero–locus
(s)_{0}
of
s
.

Moreover, if
H^{1}\big({\mathcal{L}}^{\vee}\big)=0
, the above two conditions determine
{\mathcal{F}}
up to isomorphism.

Proof.

See
[
5
]
.
∎

If
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
are coherent sheaves on
X
, then the Serre duality holds

\mathrm{Ext}_{X}^{i}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{G}}\otimes\omega_{X}\big)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{X}^{\dim(X)-i}\big({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}\big)^{\vee}

(2.4)

(see
[
30
, Proposition 7.4]
). Moreover, we define

\chi({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}):=\sum_{i=0}^{\dim(X)}\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{X}\big({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}}\big).

Notice that if
{\mathcal{G}}
is locally free, then
\chi({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}})=\chi({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{G}}^{\vee})
. If
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free, Equality (
2.4
) gives
\chi({\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{F}})=(-1)^{\dim(X)}\chi({\mathcal{G}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X})
.

Consider a locally complete intersection subscheme
Z\subseteq X
. Then

\mathcal{E}\kern-1.07639pt{\mathit{x}t}^{i}_{X}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{Z},{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\big)\cong\mathcal{E}\kern-1.07639pt{\mathit{x}t}^{i-1}\big({\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X},{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}\big)\cong\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{N}}_{Z|X}

(2.5)

where
{\mathcal{N}}_{Z|X}^{\vee}:={\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}/{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}^{2}\cong{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}
.

The Riemann–Roch formula for a vector bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
on a threefold
X
is

\displaystyle\chi({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})+{\frac{1}{6}}(c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{3}-3c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})c_{2}({\mathcal{F}})+3c_{3}({\mathcal{F}}))

(2.6)

\displaystyle-{\frac{1}{4}}(\omega_{X}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})^{2}-2\omega_{X}c_{2}({\mathcal{F}}))+{\frac{1}{12}}(\omega_{X}^{2}c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})+c_{2}(\Omega_{X})c_{1}({\mathcal{F}}))

(see
[
28
, Theorem A.4.1]
).

We now assume that
X
is a Fano threefold.
The following lemma will be helpful.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a simple vector bundle on
X
, then
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}\big)=0
.

Proof.

Equality (
2.4
) implies
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}\big)^{\vee}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}(-i_{X}h)\big)
. In order to prove the statement, it then suffices to check that the latter space is zero. On the one hand,
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}(-i_{X}h)\big)
, hence
\det(\varphi)\in H^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-i_{X}\mathrm{rk}({\mathcal{F}})h)\big)=0
. On the other hand
\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}(-i_{X}h)\big)\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{F}}\big)
, because
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)\big)\neq 0
(see
[
34
, Theorem 2.3.1]
). As
{\mathcal{F}}
is simple, each non zero endomorphism of
{\mathcal{F}}
is an automorphism. We deduce that necessarily
\varphi=0
.
∎

We are interested in
\mu
–semistable bundles, hence the following lemmas will be useful.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
X
, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is
\mu
–stable (resp.
\mu
–semistable) with respect to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
if and only if
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{F}}(-D)\big)=0
for each divisor
D\subseteq X
such that
Dh^{2}\geq\mu({\mathcal{F}})
(resp.
>\mu({\mathcal{F}})
).

Proof.

The group
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is free, hence it suffices to apply
[
36
, Corollary 4]
.
∎

The following proposition partially clarifies the importance of the number
q_{X}^{\varepsilon}
defined in the introduction.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a rank
2

\mu
–semistable bundle on
X
such that
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}})=-\varepsilon h
with
\varepsilon\in\left\{0,1\right\}
and
h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}h)\big)=0

(1)

A helpful characterization of ordinary instanton bundles on a Fano threefold is related to the naturality of their cohomology in a certain range. We show below that such a characterization actually holds also in the non–ordinary case.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold.

Every
\mu
–semistable bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
of rank
2
on

and

\varepsilon-i_{X}\leq t\leq\varepsilon-i_{X}-1+q_{X}^{\varepsilon}
and

The case when

q_{X}^{\varepsilon}\geq 1

is
\mu

Assume that

\displaystyle h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}h)\big)\leq h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)=0,\qquad h^{3}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-q_{X}^{\varepsilon}h)\big)=h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}((1-q_{X}^{\varepsilon})h)\big)\leq h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)=0.

3.

In this section we deal with the endomorphism group of an instanton bundle. The lemma below is well–known.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety of dimension

If

Proof.

By definition, there is a subsheaf

of rank
1
such that
\mu(\mathcal{L})=\mu({\mathcal{E}})
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}:={\mathcal{E}}/\mathcal{L}
and denote by

\mu

[

46

implies that

on

Let

be a non–zero section: with the notation introduced in the previous section we write

and we can construct Sequence (

) with

∎

5.

The existence of ordinary instanton bundles on a Fano threefold

with very ample

has been the object of several papers. More precisely ordinary instanton bundles always exist when
i_{X}\geq 2
and in several cases when

(see the introduction and the references therein for further details).

In this section we will show that
X
also supports non–ordinary instanton bundles when

. The first step in this direction is the following result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with
i_{X}\geq 2
and very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

For each non–negative integer
s
, there is a curve
Z\subseteq X
whose connected components are
s
pairwise disjoint integral conics.

Proof.

The statement is trivial if
i_{X}\geq 3
, hence we only need to examine the case
i_{X}=2
in what follows. Let
d:=h^{3}
be the degree of
X
: we know that
3\leq d\leq 7
.

In this case the general hyperplane section

of

is a del Pezzo surface obtained by blowing up

points in general position on
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
. Thus
\operatorname{Pic}(H)
is generated by the pull–back

of the class of a general line in
{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
and by the classes

of the exceptional divisors. Moreover,
\ell^{2}=-e_{i}^{2}=1
,
\ell e_{i}=e_{i}e_{j}=0
for
1\leq i<j\leq r
.

Since the class of the hyperplane section of
H
is
3\ell-\sum_{k=1}^{r}e_{k}
, it follows that that every element in
|\ell-e_{1}|
is a conic. Notice that two such conics do not intersect.
∎

Now let us fix a Fano threefold
X
with
i_{X}\geq 2
and consider

such that
i_{X}-\varepsilon
is odd. The following construction extends

.

Construction 5.2
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with very ample

Since

\mu({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{6,3}}(D))\geq\mu({\mathcal{E}})
if and only if
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\geq-3/2
. Thus either all the
\alpha_{i}
’s are non–negative, or one of them is strictly negative and one non–negative. In both cases
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{6,3}}(-h-D)\big)=0
, hence we can use the same argument used for proving the
\mu
–stability in the previous case.

Finally we examine the
\mu
–stability of
{\mathcal{E}}
when
X:=F_{7}
using Lemma
2.3
and
[
16
, Proposition 2.7]
. The argument is essentially the same used before, but we need some more care because the geometry of
{F_{7}}
is slightly richer. Indeed in this case
\Gamma({F_{7}})
is the union of the fourfold of conics which are complete intersection of divisors in
|\xi|
and
|f|
, with the
{\mathbb{P}^{5}}
of conics inside
E
, as we showed in Remark
4.4
. Anyhow there are still no divisors in
|f|
containing more than one component of
Z
. In this case if
{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{7}}(D)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{7}}(\alpha_{1}\xi+\alpha_{2}f)
, then
\mu({\mathcal{O}}_{F_{7}}(D))\geq\mu({\mathcal{E}})
if and only if

4\alpha_{1}+3\alpha_{2}\geq-7/2.

(5.4)

The cohomology of Sequence (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}(-\alpha_{1}\xi-\alpha_{2}f)\big)

\displaystyle\leq h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{{F_{7}}}(-(\alpha_{1}+1)\xi-(\alpha_{2}+1)f)\big)

\displaystyle+h^{0}\big({\mathcal{I}}_{Z|{F_{7}}}(-\alpha_{1}\xi-\alpha_{2}f)\big).

If
\alpha_{1}\geq 1
the last member is zero, because
-\alpha_{1}=(-\alpha_{1}\xi-\alpha_{2}f)f^{2}
. Thus we have to deal with the case
\alpha_{1}\leq 0
. In this case Inequality (
5.4
) yields

-(\alpha_{1}+1)-(\alpha_{2}+1)\leq-\alpha_{1}+\frac{8\alpha_{1}+7}{6}-2=\frac{2\alpha_{1}-5}{6}<0

hence
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{{F_{7}}}(-(\alpha_{1}+1)\xi-(\alpha_{2}+1)f)\big)=0
. Moreover,

-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\leq-\alpha_{1}+\frac{8\alpha_{1}+7}{6}=\frac{2\alpha_{1}+7}{6}.

If
-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\leq-1
, then
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{I}}_{Z|{F_{7}}}(-\alpha_{1}\xi-\alpha_{2}f)\big)\leq h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{{F_{7}}}(-\alpha_{1}\xi-\alpha_{2}f)\big)=0
.
Thus we have to check the cases
-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}=1
and
-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}=0
.

In the former case
\alpha_{2}=-1-\alpha_{1}
, hence Inequality (
5.4
) returns
2\alpha_{1}\geq-1
. The hypothesis
\alpha_{1}\leq 0
then forces
\alpha_{1}=0
and we have to check if
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{I}}_{Z|{F_{7}}}(f)\big)=0
. In the latter case
\alpha_{2}=-\alpha_{1}
and we have to check if
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{I}}_{Z|{F_{7}}}(-\alpha_{1}\xi+\alpha_{1}f)\big)=0
. This is true if
\alpha_{1}=0
, hence we consider the case
\alpha_{1}\leq-1
in what follows. In this case
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{{F_{7}}}(-\alpha_{1}\xi+\alpha_{1}f)\big)=1
, hence the linear system
|-\alpha_{1}\xi+\alpha_{1}f|
only contains the divisor
-\alpha_{1}E
. Since we are assuming
k\geq 4
, then
Z
has at least two connected components, which cannot be contained simultaneously in either a divisor in
|f|
or in
E
, as explained above. Thus the proof of the
\mu
–stability of
{\mathcal{E}}
is complete.
∎

6.
Non–ordinary instanton bundles on ordinary prime Fano threefolds

In this section we will give some results on instanton bundles on an ordinary prime Fano threefold
X
, proving Theorem
1.6
. We will also assume that
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
is very ample.

In this case
H^{2}(X)
and
H^{4}(X)
are respectively generated by the hyperplane class
h
and by the class
\ell
of a line. Equality (
2.6
) returns

\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h))=\frac{h^{3}}{2}+3.

Thus
\deg(X)=h^{3}
is an even integer. The number
g_{X}:=\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h))-2
is usually called the
genus of
X
. If
\varrho_{X}=1
, then
3\leq g_{X}\leq 12
and
g_{X}\neq 11
.

In this case the irreducible components of
\Lambda(X)
and
\Gamma(X)
have dimensions
1
and
2
respectively (see
[
39
, Lemmas 2.2.3 and 2.3.4]
). Moreover, there is component
\Gamma(X)_{0}\subseteq\Gamma(X)
whose general point is an integral conic
Z
by
[
35
, Theorem 4.4]
. The conics in
\Gamma(X)_{0}
cover
X
by
[
39
, Lemma 2.3.4]
, hence the normal bundle of
Z
satisfies
{\mathcal{N}}_{Z|X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
by
[
35
, Proposition 4.3]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
X
be an ordinary prime Fano threefold with very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

If
Z\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
is general, then the general conic in
\Gamma(X)_{0}
and the general line in each component of
\Lambda(X)
do not intersect
Z
.

Proof.

There exists a conic
C
not intersecting
Z
: see
[
9
, Proof of Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 4.1]
where it suffices to replace
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}(X)
with
\Gamma(X)_{0}
. Thus the same is true for the general conic in
\Gamma(X)_{0}
.

Let us now prove that there is a also a line in each component of
\Lambda(X)_{i}\subseteq\Lambda(X)
not intersecting
Z
. Assume that all the conics in
\Gamma(X)_{0}
intersect each line in
\Lambda(X)_{i}
. The scheme
R_{i}:=\bigcup_{L\in\Lambda(X)_{i}}L\subseteq X
is an irreducible surface. If
Z\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
is general, then
Z\cap R_{i}
is a finite set of points, because
X
is dominated by
\Gamma(X)_{0}
. Since the surfaces
R_{i}
are irreducible, it follows that we can find a point
v_{i}\in Z\cap R_{i}
lying on all the lines in
R_{i}
. Thus each
R_{i}
is a cone with vertex
v_{i}
and the general conic in
\Gamma(X)_{0}
passes necessarily through all such points, hence it intersects
Z
, contradicting what we proved above.
∎

In what follows we want to construct instanton bundles on each prime Fano threefold. On the one hand, the only line bundle we can actually use in Serre construction for obtaining instanton bundles is
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
. Thus the only admissible construction by means of Serre correspondence is actually Construction
5.2
. On the other hand, such a construction does not return instanton bundles
{\mathcal{E}}
because in this case
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}\big)=1
, as pointed out in the previous section. For this reason we need a different and smarter approach for prime Fano threefolds.

The existence of minimal instanton bundles on a Fano threefold
X
follows from
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
. Thanks to an easy induction on the quantum number, it is possible to prove the existence of even instanton bundles on each prime Fano threefold for every admissible quantum number.

We will prove the existence of instanton bundles by induction on
k
. The proof of the inductive step essentially coincides with few differences with the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 4.1]
which suffices to prove the existence of minimal instanton bundles. Anyhow, in order to start with our induction we need some more properties of such minimal bundles. The proofs of these properties can be found in
[
9
]
, but they are a little hidden in the proofs and not explicitly indicated in the statements. Thus, for reader’s benefit we explain how to recover them in what follows and how to adapt the proof in
[
9
]
to get the inductive step.

To this purpose we introduce a new construction.

Construction 6.2
.

Let
X
be a prime Fano threefold with very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

If
k=2
, let
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
be the bundle
{\mathcal{F}}
obtained via Construction
5.2
from a general
Z\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
and
\eta\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
the corresponding inclusion map. By construction the bundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is strictly
\mu
–semistable: moreover, restricting Sequence (
5.1
) to a general integral conic
Y\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
, we deduce that
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
.

If
k\geq 4
is even, let
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
be a
\mu
–stable even instanton bundle with quantum number
k
, such that
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
for the general
Y\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
and
\mathrm{Ext}_{X}^{2}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k},{\mathcal{E}}_{k}\big)=0
.

Thus, if
C\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
is general, then the general
\varphi\colon{\mathcal{E}}_{k}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
is surjective. We define
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}:=\ker(\varphi)
. By construction there is the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{k}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{C}\longrightarrow 0.

(6.1)

While the existence of
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}
is clear, the existence of
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
when
k\geq 4
will be proved in what follows. Anyhow
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
, if it exists, is not locally free at the points of
C
, thus it cannot be an instanton bundle, though it enjoys many properties of instanton bundles as we will show in the following proposition.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
X
be an ordinary prime Fano threefold with very ample
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(h)
.

For each even
k\geq 2
, the sheaf
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
defined in Construction
6.2
satisfies the following properties.

•

{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
is torsion–free and
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=0
,
hc_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=k+2
,
c_{3}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=0
.

•

h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)=h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}(-h)\big)=0
.

•

{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
is simple.

•

For the general

, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
.

•

\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)=2k+1
,
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)=\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)=0
.

Proof.

Trivially
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
is torsion free, because it is contained in the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
. By combining Sequences (
5.1
) and (
2.2
) we obtain

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{C}\longrightarrow 0.

(6.2)

We deduce that the product of the Chern polynomials of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
must be
1
, hence
c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{C})=0
,
c_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{C})=-C
and
c_{3}({\mathcal{O}}_{C})=0
. Using these informations we compute the Chern classes of
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
from Sequence (
6.1
) obtaining
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=0
,
c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{k})+C
, hence
hc_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=hc_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{k})+2
, and
c_{3}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=0
.

When
k\geq 4
, the vanishings
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)=h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}(-h)\big)=0
follow trivially from the cohomology of Sequence (
6.1
), because the same vanishings hold for
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
by induction and
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)\big)=0
.

Let
k=2
. Notice that
\mathrm{im}(\eta)\not\subseteq{\mathcal{E}}_{2,\varphi}
, hence the restriction
\varphi\circ\eta\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{C}
is surjective. Combining Sequences (
5.1
) and (
6.1
), the Snake lemma implies the existence of an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{C|X}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{2,\varphi}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{Z|X}\longrightarrow 0,

(6.3)

whence we deduce
h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2,\varphi}\big)=h^{1}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2,\varphi}(-h)\big)=0
.

Let us prove that
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
is simple. If
k=2
then it follows from
[
9
, Lemma 4.5]
. If
k\geq 4
, then
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
is actually
\mu
–stable, because each sheaf destabilizing
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
, also destabilizes
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
, because
\mu({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi})=\mu({\mathcal{E}}_{k})
. Thus
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}
is also simple thanks to
[
33

.

By Lemma
6.1
, a general
Y\in\Gamma(X)_{0}
does not intersect
C
. Restricting Sequence (
6.1
) to
Y
we then obtain
{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\cong{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
. In the remaining part of the proof, we will compute
\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)
, for
i\geq 1
.

Recall that
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k},{\mathcal{E}}_{k}\big)=0
for each

, thanks to Lemma
2.2
, because
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}
is simple. If
k=2

and Equality (
2.4
) yield

\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2},{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\big)=h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(-h)\big).

The cohomologies of Sequences (
5.2
) and (

) tensored by
{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(-h)
imply

h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{2}(-h)\big)\leq 2h^{0}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2}(-h)\big).

Again the cohomologies of the same sequences tensored by

imply that the dimension on the right is zero.
Similarly,
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k},{\mathcal{E}}_{k}\big)=0
when

by hypothesis. If
k=2
, then
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{2},{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\big)=0
thanks to
[

.

If we apply

to Sequence (

), then

, because

for each

. Thus,

\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)=0,\qquad\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)\subseteq\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{C},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big),

for each

, by applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big(-,{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)
to Sequence (
6.1
).

Equality (
2.4
) implies
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{C},{\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi}\big)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{O}}_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)\big)

\mathcal{E}\kern-1.07639pt{\mathit{x}t}^{1}_{X}\big({\mathcal{O}}_{C},{\mathcal{O}}_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-h)\big)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)^{\oplus 2}

(see Equalities (

\mathcal{H}\kern-1.07639pt{\mathit{o}m}_{X}\big(-,{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)\big)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)^{\oplus 2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}\kern-1.07639pt{\mathit{o}m}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)\big)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)^{\oplus 2}\longrightarrow 0,

because, being

locally free, we have
\mathcal{E}\kern-1.07639pt{\mathit{x}t}^{1}_{X}\big({\mathcal{E}}_{k},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(-2)\big)=0

\mathrm{Hom}_{X}({\mathcal{E}}_{k,\varphi},-\big)

Definition 7.1

Let

We say that an instanton bundle

is earnest if

Let us spend few words on earnest instanton bundles. Let

be an integral, smooth divisor on

is very ample. When

When

are characterized by a finite number of further

, i.e. vanishings

In the case of the non–ordinary instanton bundles obtained in the previous sections we can prove the following result.

The instanton bundles obtained in Theorem

Theorem

. Recall that
\Lambda(Q)\cong{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
. In this case
J_{\mathcal{E}}
is the support of the sheaf
R^{1}\lambda_{*}\chi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}(-h)
and it is a divisor (see Example
9.6
).

Proposition 9.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be an even instanton bundle on
Q
with quantum number
k
.

Then
J_{{\mathcal{E}}}\subseteq\Lambda(Q)
is a divisor of degree
k
endowed with a sheaf
\mathscr{G}
fitting into

0\longrightarrow H^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)}(-2)\mathbin{\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\Psi}}}H\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)}\longrightarrow\mathscr{G}\longrightarrow 0.

(9.8)

Moreover,
J_{\mathcal{E}}
is a hypersurface of degree
k
.

Proof.

Splitting Monad (
9.3
) we obtain the exact sequences

\begin{gathered}0\longrightarrow\chi^{*}\mathcal{K}(-h)\longrightarrow K\otimes\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-h)\longrightarrow H\otimes\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\longrightarrow 0,\\
0\longrightarrow H^{\vee}\otimes\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-2h)\longrightarrow\chi^{*}\mathcal{K}(-h)\longrightarrow\chi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}(-h)\longrightarrow 0.\end{gathered}

(9.9)

As in the previous proof we apply the functor
\lambda_{*}
to the two above sequences. In particular we have to identify the sheaves
R^{i}\lambda_{*}\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-th)
for
t=0,1,2
and
i\geq 0
.

Recall that
\Lambda(Q)\cong{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
is endowed with the standard polarization
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}^{3}}}(1)
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathscr{L}}
fits into the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)}(-2)\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-h)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)}(-1)\boxtimes{\mathcal{S}}

(9.10)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)\times{Q}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathscr{L}}\longrightarrow 0

where
{\mathcal{S}}
is the unique spinor bundle on
Q
, whose non–zero sections vanish exactly on the lines inside
Q
. We then compute
R^{i}\lambda_{*}\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-th)
by applying the functor
\widehat{\lambda}_{*}
to Sequence (
9.10
) tensored by
\widehat{\chi}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-th)
. We then deduce

R^{1}{\lambda}_{*}{\chi}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-2h)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)}(-2),\qquad R^{0}{\lambda}_{*}{\chi}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{\Lambda(Q)},

all the other
R^{i}
’s being zero. Replacing the above isomorphisms in Sequences (
9.9
) we finally obtain Sequence (
9.8
) where
\mathscr{G}\cong R^{1}\lambda_{*}\chi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}(-h)
and
\Psi
is induced by the map
\psi\colon H^{\vee}\otimes\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-2h)\to K\otimes\chi^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Q}(-h)
.

Finally observe that
{\mathcal{G}}
is supported on the vanishing locus of
\det(\Psi)
. The statement then follows by noticing that
\Psi
is represented by a
{k}/{2}
square matrix of degree two homogenous forms (see Sequence (
9.8
)).
∎

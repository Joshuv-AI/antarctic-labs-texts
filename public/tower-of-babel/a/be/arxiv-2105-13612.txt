The concentration-compactness principle for the nonlocal anisotropic p p -Laplacian of mixed order Thanks: Jamil Chaker gratefully acknowledges support by the DFG through CRC 1283. Minhyun Kim and Marvin Weidner gratefully acknowledge financial support by the DFG through IRTG 2235.

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the existence of minimizers of the Sobolev quotient for a class of nonlocal operators with an orthotropic structure having different exponents of integrability and different orders of differentiability. Our method is based on the concentration-compactness principle which we extend to this class of operators. One consequence of our main result is the existence of a nontrivial nonnegative solution to the corresponding critical problem.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study variational problems for the nonlocal anisotropic
p
-Laplacian of mixed order. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

(1.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-\partial_{ii})^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|^{p_{i}-2}(u(x)-u(x+he_{i}))}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h,

where
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}>1
.
This operator can be seen as a nonlocal analog of the anisotropic
p
-Laplacian

(1.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-\partial_{ii})_{p_{i}}u(x)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{p_{i}-2}\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\right).

Local operators with such an orthotropic structure are well known in the literature and there are several results related to this type of operators, see

The aim of this paper is to study the existence of minimizers of the Sobolev quotient for the operator in (
1.1

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-\partial_{ii})^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}
.

The approach we use in this paper is based on the concentration-compactness principle (CCP) that provides an important tool to prove relative compactness of minimizing sequences. It has been introduced by P.-L. Lions in a series of papers, see

Before we address known results from the literature and describe the strategy of our paper, we formulate the main results and assumptions of the present work.

Given
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}>1
, we define

\bar{s}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{i}}\right)^{-1},\quad\overline{sp}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{s_{i}p_{i}}\right)^{-1},\quad\text{and}\quad p^{\ast}=\frac{n\overline{sp}/\bar{s}}{n-\overline{sp}}.

Assumption 1
.

Given
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}>1
, we assume

\overline{sp}<n\quad\text{ and }\quad p_{\max}:=\max\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}<p^{\ast}.

Let
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
and
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
.
We introduce the homogeneous anisotropic Sobolev space
\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s_{i}(1-s_{i})\frac{|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h\,\textnormal{d}x<\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, where

\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s_{i}(1-s_{i})\frac{|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}

with

|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u(x)|^{p_{i}}:=s_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h

for
i=1,\dots,n
. Note that
\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
becomes a normed space in the light of
Theorem

1.1
. It is the natural space for minimizing the functional connected to the Sobolev quotient. The first main result of this paper is a robust Sobolev-type inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in[s_{0},1)
for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}>1
be such that
1
holds.
Then there is a constant
C=C(n,p^{\ast},p^{\ast}-p_{\max},s_{0})>0
such that
for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Theorem

1.1
is robust in the sense that the appearing constant depends only on a lower bound
s_{0}\leq s_{1},\dots,s_{n}
. This allows us to recover the local anisotropic Sobolev inequality (see
[

,
44
,

,

(1.3)

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|D_{i}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where

(1.4)

p_{i}>1,\quad\bar{p}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)^{-1},\quad\bar{p}<n,\quad q=\frac{n\bar{p}}{n-\bar{p}},\quad\text{and}\quad p_{\max}<q.

Moreover, our result provides an alternative proof of (
1.3
).

Our second main result establishes the existence of nonnegative minimizers of the Sobolev quotient.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
and
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
satisfy
1

Then there exists a nonnegative minimizer

of

(1.5)

S:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}.

A consequence of the foregoing result is the existence of a nontrivial, nonnegative solution to the corresponding critical problem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Assume
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
and
\vec{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
satisfy
1
.
Then there exists a nontrivial nonnegative solution

(1.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-\partial_{ii})^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u=|u|^{p^{\ast}-2}u\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We first comment on known results from the literature before we explain the novelty of our results. For
s\in(0,1)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define the
normalized Gagliardo–Slobodeckiĭ seminorm as

[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left(s(1-s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\textnormal{d}x\,\textnormal{d}y\right)^{1/p}

and for

p\in(1,\infty)

[u]_{W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The homogeneous Sobolev space is then given as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-seminorm. In the subconformal case
sp<n
, it can be represented as the function space

\mathcal{D}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty\},

where
p^{\ast}=np/(n-sp)
. For details on homogeneous Sobolev spaces, we refer the reader to
[
12
]
and the references therein.

The existence of a minimizer of the Sobolev quotient

(1.7)

\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{[u]_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}}{\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}}

is an important problem in the analysis of variational problems. One reason for this is that minimizers of (

(1.8)

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u=|u|^{p^{\ast}-2}u\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For
s=1

p=2

For

[
4

46

Not only the existence of minimizers to (
1.7

u(x)=\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-n}{p}}.

Let us now comment on results in the fractional case
s\in(0,1)

In the linear case

2.
Sobolev inequality and compact embedding

In this section, we prove an anisotropic fractional Sobolev inequality and a compact embedding theorem.
Even as a self-standing result the anisotropic fractional Sobolev inequality is very interesting. On the one hand, it allows for different orders of integrability
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}>1
and at the same time also different orders of differentiability
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in(0,1)
. Hence, this Sobolev inequality covers plenty of anisotropic nonlocal operators, such as
-(-\partial_{11})^{s_{1}}-\dots-(-\partial_{nn})^{s_{n}}
or fractional orthotropic
p
-Laplacians (see
[
15
]
).
By comparability of energy forms, one can deduce the Sobolev inequality also for isotropic operators such as the fractional Laplacian
-(-\Delta)^{s}
. On the other hand, the Sobolev inequality is robust in the sense that the appearing constant depends only on a lower bound of
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}
. This allows us to recover local isotropic and anisotropic Sobolev inequalities such as Troisi’s inequality, see (
1.3
). Therefore,
Theorem

1.1
not only extends (
1.3
) to the nonlocal setting, but also provides another proof of it.

The compact embedding
\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\subset L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
1\leq p<p^{\ast}
is a consequence of the anisotropic fractional Sobolev inequality and direct computations.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is based on the boundedness of maximal and sharp maximal operators. For this purpose, we define a class of rectangles taking the anisotropy into account. For
i=1,\dots,n
, set

(2.1)

m_{i}=\frac{s_{\max}}{s_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{p_{\max}}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)^{-1}.

Note that
m_{i}\geq 1
by the assumption
p_{\max}<p^{\ast}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\rho>0
, we define

(2.2)

M_{\rho}(x)=\mathop{\mathchoice{\raise-2.20001pt\hbox{\huge$\times$}}{\raise-0.50003pt\hbox{\Large$\times$}}{\hbox{\large$\times$}}{\times}}_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}-\rho^{m_{i}},x_{i}+\rho^{m_{i}}\right).

The sets
M_{\rho}(x)
are balls with respect to the metric
d_{M}
defined by

d_{M}(x,y)=\sup_{i=1,\dots,n}|x_{i}-y_{i}|^{1/m_{i}},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that
d_{M}
is a metric since
m_{i}\geq 1
. An important ingredient in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
, is the fact that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{M})
equipped with the Lebesgue measure is a doubling metric measure space. Since

|M_{2\rho}|=2^{n}(2\rho)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}}=2^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}}|M_{\rho}|=2^{s_{\max}/(\frac{1}{p_{\max}}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}})}|M_{\rho}|\leq 2^{\frac{(p^{\ast})^{2}}{p^{\ast}-p_{\max}}}|M_{\rho}|,

the doubling constant depends on
p^{\ast}
and
p^{\ast}-p_{\max}
.

Let us recall the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function and sharp maximal function: for
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

{\bf M}u(x)=\sup_{\rho>0}\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}u(y)\,\textnormal{d}y\quad\text{and}\quad{\bf M}^{\sharp}u(x)=\sup_{\rho>0}\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}|u(y)-(u)_{M_{\rho}(x)}|\,\textnormal{d}y,

where
(u)_{M_{\rho}(x)}=\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}u(z)\,\textnormal{d}z
. It is well known that the maximal inequality and sharp maximal inequality hold true on doubling metric spaces, see
[
31
,
30
,
15
]
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then the maximal function
{\bf M}u
and sharp maximal function
{\bf M}^{\sharp}u
are well defined. Following the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 2.4]
, one obtains

(2.3)

\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}|u(y)-(u)_{M_{\rho}(x)}|\,\textnormal{d}y\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho^{s_{i}m_{i}}\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where the function
F_{i}
is defined by

F_{i}(y):=\sup_{\rho>0}\left(2\rho^{-s_{i}m_{i}}\fint_{-2\rho^{m_{i}}}^{2\rho^{m_{i}}}|u(y)-u(y+he_{i})|\,\mathrm{d}h\right).

By Hölder’s inequality, we have

(2.4)

\begin{split}\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p^{\ast}}&\leq\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}^{p_{i}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p_{i}}\\
&\leq|M_{\rho}|^{-\frac{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p_{i}}.\end{split}

Thus, it follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) that

\begin{split}&\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}|u(y)-(u)_{M_{\rho}(x)}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p^{\ast}}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}n^{{p^{\ast}}-1}\rho^{{p^{\ast}}s_{i}m_{i}}\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p^{\ast}}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}n^{{p^{\ast}}-1}\rho^{{p^{\ast}}s_{i}m_{i}}|M_{\rho}|^{-\frac{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p_{i}}.\end{split}

Note that for each
i
,

p^{\ast}s_{i}m_{i}-\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}=0.

Therefore, we arrive at

\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}|u(y)-(u)_{M_{\rho}(x)}|\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p^{\ast}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{n^{{p^{\ast}}-1}}{2^{n\frac{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}\left(\fint_{M_{\rho}(x)}F_{i}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p_{i}}.

We take the supremum over
\rho>0
and use the estimate
2^{n\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\geq 1
, then

\left({\bf M}^{\sharp}u(x)\right)^{p^{\ast}}\leq n^{p^{\ast}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}({\bf M}F_{i}(x))^{p_{i}}.

By boundedness of the sharp maximal operator in
L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the maximal operator in
L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}\leq C\|{\bf M}^{\sharp}u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{{p^{\ast}}-p_{i}}\|{\bf M}F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}

for some
C>0
. The constant
C
depends on the doubling constant and the number of intersections of anisotropic dyadic rectangles as in
[
15
, Appendix B]
. Therefore,
C=C(n,p^{\ast},p^{\ast}-p_{\max},s_{0})
.

It only remains to show that there is a
C=C(n,p^{\ast},p^{\ast}-p_{\max},s_{0})>0
such that

\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}\leq Cs_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}x

for each
i=1,\dots,n
, which follows exactly as in
[
15
, Lemma 2.3 and Theorem 2.4]
.
∎

Theorem 2.1

(Compact Embedding)
.

Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\in[s_{0},1)
for some
s_{0}\in(0,1)
and
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}>1
be such that
1
holds. Then

\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\subset L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
1\leq p<p^{\ast}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[
19
, Theorem 4.54]
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be bounded and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open, bounded set. By
Theorem

1.1
we know that
\mathcal{B}
is bounded in
L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, by a standard Hölder interpolation argument, it is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{B}
is precompact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
in order to deduce the desired result. We will show that there is
C>0
such that for every
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
u\in\mathcal{B}
it holds:

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u(x+re_{i})-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}x\leq C|r|^{s_{i}}|\Omega|^{(p_{i}-1)/p_{i}}\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\Omega_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>|r|\}
.
By the triangle inequality, we have

\begin{split}&2|r|\int_{\Omega_{r}}|u(x+re_{i})-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega_{r}}\int_{(-|r|,|r|)}|u(x+re_{i})-u(x+te_{i})|+|u(x+te_{i})-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{r}}\int_{(-|r|,|r|)}\frac{|u(x+re_{i})-u(x+te_{i})|}{|t-r|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}|t-r|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{r}}\int_{(-|r|,|r|)}\frac{|u(x+te_{i})-u(x)|}{|t|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}|t|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}x.\end{split}

Both expressions on the right-hand side can be estimated from above in a similar way. We demonstrate how to treat the second expression.
By Hölder’s inequality

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{r}}&\int_{(-|r|,|r|)}\frac{|u(x+te_{i})-u(x)|}{|t|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}}}}|t|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}\int_{(-|r|,|r|)}\frac{|u(x+te_{i})-u(x)|^{p_{i}}}{|t|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{r}}\int_{(-|r|,|r|)}|t|^{\frac{1+s_{i}p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}}\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}}\\
&\leq C|r|^{1+s_{i}}|\Omega|^{1-1/p_{i}}\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\end{split}

for some
C>0
. This proves (
2.5
). Now let
h\in B_{1}(0)
.
Then

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{2|h|}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{2|h|}}|u(x+h_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+h_{i}e_{i})-u(x+h_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+h_{i-1}e_{i-1})|\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{h_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+h_{i-1}e_{i-1}+\Omega_{2|h|}}|u(x+h_{i}e_{i})-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}x.\end{split}

Since
h_{1}e_{1}+\dots+h_{i-1}e_{i-1}+\Omega_{2|h|}\subset\Omega_{h_{i}}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{2|h|}}|u(x+h)-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}x&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega_{h_{i}}}|u(x+h_{i}e_{i})-u(x)|\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq C|h|^{s_{0}}\max\{1,|\Omega|^{1-1/p_{\max}}\}\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

for some
C>0
. By boundedness of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the right-hand side vanishes uniformly as
h\searrow 0
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is also bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, we can deduce precompactness of
\mathcal{B}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
from the Riesz–Fréchet–Kolmogorov theorem (c.f.
[
19
, Theorem 1.95]
).
∎

3.
Concentration-compactness principle

In this section we present the concentration-compactness principle for solving the minimization problem (
1.5
).
Throughout this section,
\vec{s}
and
\vec{p}
always satisfy
1
.

Recall that

S=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}},

which is positive by
Theorem

1.1
. Furthermore, by definition of
S
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
, it holds that

S\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
u\neq 0
and
\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
. Let
v=u/\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, then
\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. Thus, by the definition of
S
,

S\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}v\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\frac{\|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}}{\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\frac{\|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}u\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}}{\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}}.

∎

The following scaling property is a consequence of a straightforward computation.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
i=1,\dots,n
, let
m_{i}
be the exponent defined by (
2.1
). For every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\rho>0
, and
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function
v
defined by

(3.1)

v(x)=\rho^{s_{\max}p_{\max}/(p^{\ast}-p_{\max})}u(\rho^{m_{1}}x_{1}+y_{1},\cdots,\rho^{m_{n}}x_{n}+y_{n})

satisfies
\|v\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

To prove the existence of a minimizer of
S
, let us take a minimizing sequence
(u_{k})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
S
satisfying
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We define the Lévy concentration function

Q_{k}(\rho)=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{M_{\rho}(y)}|u_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x,\quad\rho>0,

where
M_{\rho}(y)
is the rectangle defined by (
2.2
). For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\rho_{k}>0
such that
Q_{k}(\rho_{k})=1/2
. Moreover, there exists
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

Q_{k}(\rho_{k})=\int_{M_{\rho_{k}}(y_{k})}|u_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x=\frac{1}{2}.

By
Lemma

3.2
, the function
v_{k}
defined by (
3.1
) with
\rho=\rho_{k}
satisfies

(3.2)

\int_{M_{1}(0)}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{M_{1}(y)}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Furthermore, the sequence
(v_{k})
is also a minimizing sequence for
S
. By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem and
Theorem

1.1
, we have

(3.3)

v_{k}\rightharpoonup v~\text{in}~\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})~\text{and}~L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for some
v\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, up to subsequences. Moreover, we have by
Theorem 2.1
that

(3.4)

v_{k}\to v~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}

up to subsequences. By the lower semicontinuity of norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, we have

(3.5)

\|v\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=S\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1.

Therefore, it is enough to prove the following theorem to find a minimizer for
S
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the defined as above. Then

\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1.

This is the main theorem of this section. We will provide the proof at the beginning of
Section

3.3
.

3.1.
Decay of cutoff functions

Let us make a few comments on the significance of cutoff functions in this article. Unlike in the local case, the support of a function is not preserved by the application of a nonlocal operator. This basic fact causes some problems since the compact embedding
\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\subset L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1\leq p<p^{\ast}
, (c.f.
Theorem

2.1
) cannot be used directly. In
[
9
]
, the authors solve this issue by proving a compact embedding into weighted spaces of the form
L^{p}(|D^{s}\phi(x)|^{p}\textnormal{d}x)
, where
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a cutoff function. The main observation is that the fractional
(s,p)
-gradient of a compactly supported function decays fast enough for such result to hold true, despite having full support.

The following lemmas discuss the adaption of this argument to the class of anisotropic nonlocal operators considered in this article.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\rho>0
, and
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we introduce the notation

M_{\rho}^{(i)}(x)=\mathop{\mathchoice{\raise-2.20001pt\hbox{\huge$\times$}}{\raise-0.50003pt\hbox{\Large$\times$}}{\hbox{\large$\times$}}{\times}}_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(x_{j}-\rho^{m_{j}},x_{j}+\rho^{m_{j}}\right)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathop{\mathchoice{\raise-2.20001pt\hbox{\huge$\times$}}{\raise-0.50003pt\hbox{\Large$\times$}}{\hbox{\large$\times$}}{\times}}_{j=i+1}^{n}\left(x_{j}-\rho^{m_{j}},x_{j}+\rho^{m_{j}}\right).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\supp(\phi)\subset M_{1}(0)
. Then

(3.6)

|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi(x)|^{p_{i}}\leq C\min(1,|x_{i}|^{-1-s_{i}p_{i}}),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

where
C=C(n,s_{i},p_{i},\|\phi\|_{\infty},\|\partial_{i}\phi\|_{\infty})>0
. Moreover,
\supp(|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi|)\subset M_{1}^{(i)}(0)
.

Proof.

We first observe that for given
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x_{j}|>1
, it holds that
\phi(x+e_{i}h)=0
for every
h\in\mathbb{R}
and
i\neq j
. It becomes evident that
\supp(|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi|)\subset M_{1}^{(i)}(0)
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we compute

s_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(x+e_{i}h)|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h\leq C(p_{i},n)\left(\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{p_{i}}+\|\partial_{i}\phi\|_{\infty}^{p_{i}}\right).

If
|x_{i}|>2
and
|x_{i}+h|<1
, then
|h|>|x_{i}|/2
. Therefore, we estimate for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x_{i}|>2
:

\begin{split}s_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(x+e_{i}h)|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h&=s_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{|x_{i}+h|<1}\frac{|\phi(x+e_{i}h)|^{p_{i}}}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}h\\
&\leq C\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{p_{i}}}{|x_{i}|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}.\end{split}

Assertion (
3.6
) follows by combining the aforementioned estimates.
∎

Given
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we introduce the scaled cutoff function
\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}
defined by

(3.7)

\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)=\phi(x^{(\varepsilon,\xi)}),

where
x^{(\varepsilon,\xi)}:=((x_{1}-\xi_{1})\varepsilon^{-m_{1}},\dots,(x_{n}-\xi_{n})\varepsilon^{-m_{n}})
and
m_{i}
is given by (
2.1
).

One easily computes:

|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{\varepsilon,0}(x)|^{p_{i}}=\varepsilon^{-m_{i}s_{i}p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi(x^{(\varepsilon,0)})|^{p_{i}}.

Consequently, we have the following corollary of
Lemma

3.4
:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\supp(\phi)\subset M_{1}(0)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}(x)|^{p_{i}}\leq C\min(\varepsilon^{-m_{i}s_{i}p_{i}},\varepsilon^{m_{i}}|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|^{-1-s_{i}p_{i}}),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

where
C=C(n,s_{i},p_{i},\|\phi\|_{\infty},\|\partial_{i}\phi\|_{\infty})>0
.
Moreover,
\supp(|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|)\subset M_{\varepsilon}^{(i)}(\xi)
.

The following lemma makes use of the compact embedding
Theorem

2.1
and the decay estimates.
It follows in the spirit of
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
and can be interpreted as a compact embedding of
\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into the weighted space
L^{p_{i}}(|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x)
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Let us recall the function
v_{k}
constructed in
Section

3
and define

w_{k}:=v_{k}-v.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\supp(\phi)\subset M_{1}(0)
. Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\to 0,

as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
R>0
to be chosen later, we decompose

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x=\int_{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|<R}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x+\int_{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|>R}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x=:I+II.

In order to bound
II
, we apply Hölder’s inequality,
Lemma

3.2
, and
Corollary

3.5
:

\begin{split}II&\leq C\left(\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}/p^{\ast}}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}/p^{\ast}}\right)\left(\int_{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|>R}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{i}\left(\frac{p^{\ast}}{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}\right)}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p^{\ast}}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{M_{\varepsilon}^{(i)}(\xi)\cap\{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|>R\}}|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|^{(-1-s_{i}p_{i})\left(\frac{p^{\ast}}{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}\right)}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p^{\ast}}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{|x_{i}|>R}|x_{i}|^{(-1-s_{i}p_{i})\left(\frac{p^{\ast}}{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}\right)}\,\textnormal{d}x_{i}\right)^{\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p^{\ast}}}\\
&\leq CR^{\gamma},\end{split}

where
C>0
is independent of
k
, and
\gamma=\left((-1-s_{i}p_{i})\left(\frac{p^{\ast}}{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}\right)+1\right)\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p^{\ast}}=-p_{i}(s_{i}+1/p^{\ast})<0
. Thus,
II\to 0
as
R\to\infty
uniformly in
k
.

For
I
, we observe that the integration takes place on the compact set

\Omega:=M_{\varepsilon}^{(i)}(\xi)\cap\{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|<R\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

due to
Corollary

3.5
. From
Theorem

2.1
, we know that
\|w_{k}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\Omega)}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Hence, it follows that
I\to 0
as
k\to\infty
for any
R>0
upon recalling that
|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|
is bounded due to
Corollary

3.5
.
∎

We end this section by another convergence result for sequences of scaled cutoff functions
(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})_{\varepsilon}
given
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
v
be the function given in
Theorem

3.3
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\supp(\phi)\subset M_{1}(0)
. Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})v|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\to 0,

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
we estimate, using
Corollary

3.5
,

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})v|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x&\leq\varepsilon^{-m_{i}s_{i}p_{i}}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}|v(x)|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x+\varepsilon^{m_{i}}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}^{(i)}(\xi)\cap\{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|>\varepsilon^{m_{i}}\}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p_{i}}}{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=:I+II.\end{split}

The first summand can be bounded from above by Hölder’s inequality:

I\leq\varepsilon^{-m_{i}s_{i}p_{i}}\left(\int_{M_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}|v(x)|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{p_{i}/p^{\ast}}|M_{\varepsilon}(\xi)|^{\frac{p^{\ast}-p_{i}}{p^{\ast}}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))}^{p_{i}}

for some
C>0
, where we used that
m_{i}s_{i}p_{i}=\left(1-\frac{p_{i}}{p^{\ast}}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}
. Since
v\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
it is clear that
I\to 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

For
II
, we decompose the domain of integration as follows:

\begin{split}II&=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{m_{i}}\int_{M^{(i)}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\cap\{(2^{l+1}\varepsilon)^{m_{i}}>|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|>(2^{l}\varepsilon)^{m_{i}}\}}\frac{|v(x)|^{p_{i}}}{|x_{i}-\xi_{i}|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{-m_{i}s_{i}p_{i}}2^{-lm_{i}(1+s_{i}p_{i})}\int_{M_{2^{l+1}\varepsilon}(\xi)}|v(x)|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that

II\leq c\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}2^{-lm_{i}}\left(\int_{M_{2^{l+1}\varepsilon}(\xi)}|v(x)|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{p_{i}/p^{\ast}}.

Note that for every
\delta>0
, we can find
l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{l=l_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-lm_{i}}<\delta
. Therefore

(3.8)

II\leq\delta\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}+C\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}\left(M_{2^{l_{0}+1}\varepsilon}(\xi)\right)}^{p_{i}},

where
C=C(l_{0})>0
. Since (
3.8
) holds true for every
\delta>0
, also
II\to 0
as
\varepsilon\searrow 0
. This implies the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Reverse Hölder inequalities

In this subsection, we prove reverse Hölder inequalities for the “components” of limiting measures of the minimizing sequence that spread or concentrate.
Recall the sequence
(v_{k})
constructed in
Section

3
. We may take a further subsequence of
(v_{k})
so that

\displaystyle|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(v_{k}-v)|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\overset{v}{\rightharpoonup}\mu_{i},

\displaystyle|v_{k}-v|^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\overset{v}{\rightharpoonup}\nu,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\overset{v}{\rightharpoonup}\widetilde{\mu}

for some positive bounded Borel measures
\mu_{i}
,
\nu
, and
\widetilde{\mu}
, where
\overset{v}{\rightharpoonup}
denotes vague convergence.
Moreover, we define
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\mu_{i}
. These quantities contain information on the concentration of
(v_{k})
.
We also define the limits

\begin{split}&\mu_{\infty}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{R}(0)}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\\
&\nu_{\infty}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{R}(0)}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

which encode information on the spreading of
(v_{k})
.

In the following, we will make use of the estimate

(3.9)

\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(fg)\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(f)g\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(g)f\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which holds true for all functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, whenever the aforementioned quantities are finite.
This inequality is an immediate consequence of Minkowski’s inequality.

Our first result provides reverse Hölder inequalities for
\mu_{\infty}
and
\nu_{\infty}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For
\mu_{\infty}
and
\nu_{\infty}
as above, the following is true:

S\nu_{\infty}^{p_{\max}/p^{\ast}}\leq\mu_{\infty}.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
\phi\equiv 1
in
M_{1}(0)
and
\phi\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{2}(0)
. For
R>0
, we define
\psi_{R}:=1-\phi_{R,0}
, where
\phi_{R,0}
is defined as in (
3.7
). Then,
\psi_{R}\equiv 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{2R}(0)
and
\psi_{R}\equiv 0
in
M_{R}(0)
. We first prove that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\nu_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mu_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{R}^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For (
3.10
) we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{2R}(0)}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{R}(0)}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x,

which implies that

(3.12)

\nu_{\infty}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, the identity (
3.10
) follows now upon the observation that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|v|^{p^{\ast}}\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus M_{R}(0)}|v|^{p^{\ast}}\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x=0,

since
v\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The proof of (
3.11
) is analogous.

Let us next prove that
S\nu_{\infty}^{p_{\max}/p^{\ast}}\leq\mu_{\infty}
.
By Brezis–Lieb’s lemma and (
3.4
) we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|w_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}-\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}<1.

It follows from
Lemma

3.1
that for large
k

S\|\psi_{R}w_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\psi_{R}w_{k})|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

By
Lemma

3.6
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w_{k}|^{p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\psi_{R}|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|w_{k}|^{p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}\phi_{R,0}|^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Therefore, by (
3.9
),

S\left(\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|\psi_{R}w_{k}\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p_{\max}}\right)\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{R}^{p_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the desired result follows from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
).
∎

The following lemma provides the reverse Hölder inequalities for
\mu_{i}
and
\nu
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\supp(\phi)\subset M_{1}(0)
. Then, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu\right)^{1/p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu_{i}\right)^{1/p_{i}}\quad\text{and}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu\right)^{1/p^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{\max}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu\right)^{1/p_{\max}}

for some constant
C=C(\|\mu\|,\|\phi\|_{\infty})>0
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
\phi=\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}
. First, we prove (
3.13
). By definition of
\nu
we deduce that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi w_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu
as
k\to\infty
. By
Theorem

1.1
it is sufficient to prove that for
i=1,\dots,n
:

(3.15)

\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi w_{k})\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu_{i}\right)^{1/p_{i}}.

By definition of
\mu_{i}
it follows that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(w_{k})\phi|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu_{i}
, as
k\to\infty
. Therefore, by (
3.9
), it remains to show that

(3.16)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi)w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\to 0,~~\text{ as }k\to\infty,

in order to deduce (
3.15
). Since (
3.16
) follows from
Lemma

3.6
, estimate (
3.13
) is proved.

For (
3.14
) we observe that for every
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
by Hölder’s inequality:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu_{i}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu\leq C\left(\|\mu\|\|\phi\|_{\infty}\right)^{1-\frac{p_{i}}{p_{\max}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\phi|^{p_{\max}}\,\textnormal{d}\mu\right)^{p_{i}/p_{\max}}.

∎

Corollary 3.10
.

There exists an at most countable set
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and positive weights
(\nu_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}
,
(\mu_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}
such that

\nu=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}\delta_{\{\xi\}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu\geq\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\mu_{\xi}\delta_{\{\xi\}}.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.9
it becomes clear that
\nu\ll\mu
, and for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,

(3.17)

\nu(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))\leq C\mu(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{p_{\max}}}

for some constant
C=C(\|\mu\|)>0
.
Since
\mu
is a finite measure, the set
\Xi
of atoms is at most countable. This proves already that
\mu\geq\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\mu_{\xi}\delta_{\{\xi\}}
and
\nu\geq\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}\delta_{\{\xi\}}
for two families of positive weights
(\mu_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}
,
(\nu_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Xi}
that are given by
\mu_{\xi}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))
and
\nu_{\xi}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\nu(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))
for every
\xi\in\Xi
. By
\nu\ll\mu
, it follows that

\nu(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))=\int_{M_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}D_{\mu}(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mu(x),

where

D_{\mu}(x):=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\nu(M_{r}(x))}{\mu(M_{r}(x))}\leq C\lim_{r\to 0}\mu(M_{r}(x))^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{p_{\max}}-1}=\begin{cases}C\mu_{x}^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{p_{\max}}-1},&~~x\in\Xi,\\
0,&~~\text{else},\end{cases}

and we used (
3.17
). Consequently,
\nu=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}\delta_{\{\xi\}}
, as desired.
∎

As a consequence of
Lemma

3.9
and
Corollary

3.10
, we can infer a reverse Hölder inequality for the atoms of
\nu
and
\widetilde{\mu}
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Every
\xi\in\Xi
is an atomic point of
\widetilde{\mu}
and it holds

(3.18)

S\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}\leq\widetilde{\mu}(\{\xi\}).

In particular,

\|\widetilde{\mu}\|\geq S\left(\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}\right).

Proof.

In order to prove (
3.18
), we approximate
\widetilde{\mu}(\{\xi\})
by cutoff functions
(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})_{\varepsilon}
, where
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\supp(\phi)\subset M_{1}(0)
,
\phi(0)=1
and
0\leq\phi\leq 1
. By Brezis–Lieb’s lemma and (
3.4
), it holds that
|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\rightharpoonup|v|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x+\nu
, and consequently:

(3.19)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{p^{\ast}}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{p^{\ast}}|v|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{M_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu\geq\nu_{\xi}.

On the other hand, by (
3.9
),
Lemma

3.7
and
Lemma

3.6
:

(3.20)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}v_{k})|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Bigg[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi})v|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{1/p_{i}}.

Consequently, by combining (
3.19
), (
3.20
) and applying
Lemma

3.1
,

\begin{split}S\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}&\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}S\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}^{p^{\ast}}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\right)^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}\\
&\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}v_{k})|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}|\phi_{\varepsilon,\xi}|^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{\mu}(M_{\varepsilon}(\xi))\\
&=\widetilde{\mu}(\{\xi\}).\end{split}

Hence, (
3.18
) holds true. From the observation that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v|^{p_{i}}\textnormal{d}x
is orthogonal to the atomic part of
\widetilde{\mu}
, we conclude that

\|\widetilde{\mu}\|\geq S\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}v\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}\geq S\left(\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}\right),

where we also applied
Lemma

3.1
. This concludes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Proof of main results

This subsection is devoted to the proofs of the main results
Theorem

1.2
and
Corollary

1.3
. We first prove
Theorem

3.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Let us assume by contradiction that
\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<1
.
First, we have that

(3.21)

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{S}\left(\|\widetilde{\mu}\|+\mu_{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}+\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\nu_{\infty}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}

\displaystyle\geq\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}+\|\nu\|^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\nu_{\infty}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}},

where the second step follows from
Lemma

3.8
and
Corollary

3.11
. In order to prove the first equality in (
3.21
) we define
\psi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as in the proof of
Lemma

3.8
and compute

\begin{split}S&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\|D_{p_{i}}^{s_{i}}v_{k}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{i}}\\
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}(1-\psi_{R}^{p_{i}})\,\textnormal{d}x+\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}v_{k}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{R}^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\psi_{R}^{p_{i}})\,\textnormal{d}\widetilde{\mu}+\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}w_{k}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{R}^{p_{i}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\|\widetilde{\mu}\|+\mu_{\infty},\end{split}

where we used (
3.11
). This proves (
3.21
).
On the other hand, it holds that

(3.22)

\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}+\|\nu\|^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\nu_{\infty}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}\geq 1.

This follows from the observation

(3.23)

\begin{split}1&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|v_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1-\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}})|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x+\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi_{R}^{p^{\ast}}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\\
&=\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}}+\|\nu\|+\nu_{\infty},\end{split}

where we used that
|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\rightharpoonup|v|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x+\nu
as a consequence of Brezis–Lieb’s lemma and (
3.4
), and (
3.12
). In order to derive (
3.22
), we take (
3.23
) to the power
p_{\max}/p^{\ast}<1
.
Combining (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) yields that

\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p_{\max}}+\|\nu\|^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}+\nu_{\infty}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}=1.

Consequently,
\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p^{\ast}},\|\nu\|,\nu_{\infty}\in\{0,1\}
. By assumption, we can now deduce that
v=0
. Moreover, by (
3.2
) we know that
\nu_{\infty}\leq 1/2
and therefore it must be that

1=\|\nu\|=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi},

where we applied
Corollary

3.10
in the last step.
From (
3.21
), we deduce that
1\geq\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}
. It follows that

1=\left(\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}\right)^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}\leq\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu_{\xi}^{\frac{p_{\max}}{p^{\ast}}}\leq 1,

and therefore all inequalities in the above line can be replaced by equality signs. Thus,
\nu_{\xi}\in\{0,1\}
for every
\xi\in\Xi
and therefore, there exists exactly one
\xi_{0}\in\Xi
such that
\nu_{\xi_{0}}=1
and
\nu_{\xi}=0
for every
\xi\in\Xi\setminus\{\xi_{0}\}
. This leads to a contradiction since by (
3.2
):

\frac{1}{2}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{M_{1}(y)}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\geq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{M_{1}(\xi_{0})}|v_{k}|^{p^{\ast}}\,\textnormal{d}x\to\|\nu\|=1.

Therefore, it must hold that
\|v\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
, as desired.
∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

1.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

As explained in the beginning of
Section

3
, let us take a minimizing sequence
(u_{k})\subset\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
S
satisfying
\|u_{k}\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We may assume that
u_{k}
is nonnegative because
(|u_{k}|)
is also a minimizing sequence for
S
. Indeed,
\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}|u_{k}|\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|D^{s_{i}}_{p_{i}}u_{k}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
follows from the inequality

||u(x)|-|u(x+he_{i})||\leq|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|.

Let us now consider the rescaled function
v_{k}
as in the beginning of
Section

3
, which satisfies (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
). Then, it follows from (
3.5
) and
Theorem

3.3
that
v
is a nontrivial minimizer for
S
. Moreover, since
v_{k}
is nonnegative a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so is
v
.
∎

Let us next prove
Corollary

1.3
.

Proof of
Corollary

1.3
.

By the standard Lagrange multiplier rule, any minimizer
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of (
1.5
) satisfies

\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}(1-s_{i})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x+he_{i})|^{p_{i}-2}(u(x)-u(x+he_{i}))}{|h|^{1+s_{i}p_{i}}}\,\mathrm{d}h=S|u|^{p^{\ast}-2}u\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus, the function
v
defined by
v(x)=u(S^{-\frac{1}{s_{1}p_{1}}}x_{1},\dots,S^{-\frac{1}{s_{n}p_{n}}}x_{n})
solves (
1.6
).
∎

By rescaling a solution of (
1.6
), we get infinitely many solutions.

Corollary 3.12
.

If
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\vec{s},\vec{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
solves (
1.6
), then the function
v
defined by (
3.1
) also solves (
1.6
).

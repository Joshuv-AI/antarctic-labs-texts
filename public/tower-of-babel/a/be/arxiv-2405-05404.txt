Abstract
. In this paper we study convexity properties for quasilinear Lane-Emden-Fowler equations of the type { − Δ p ​ u = a ⁡ ( x ) ​ u q in Ω , u > 0 in Ω , u = 0 on ∂ Ω , \begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=a(x)u^{q}&\quad\hbox{ in $\Omega$},\\ u>0&\quad\hbox{ in $\Omega$},\\ u=0&\quad\hbox{ on $\partial\Omega$},\end{cases} when Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} is a convex domain. In particular, in the subhomogeneous case q ∈ [ 0 , p − 1 ] q\in[0,p-1] , the solution u u inherits concavity properties from a a whenever assumed, while it is proved to be concave up to an error if a a is near to a constant. More general problems are also taken into account, including a wider class of nonlinearities. These results generalize some contained in [ 92 ] and [ 121 ] . Additionally, some results for the singular case q ∈ [ − 1 , 0 ) q\in[-1,0) and the superhomogeneous case q > p − 1 q>p-1 , q ≈ p − 1 q\approx p-1 are obtained. Some properties for the p p -fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) p s (-\Delta)^{s}_{p} , s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , s ≈ 1 s\approx 1 , are shown as well. We highlight that some results are new even in the semilinear framework p = 2 p=2 ; in some of these cases, we deduce also uniqueness (and nondegeneracy) of the critical point of u u .

1.1.
Some background

Qualitative properties of solutions of PDEs are a classical topic, and often the features of the domain and of the nonlinearity are inherited by the solutions.
Consider the equation

Remark 1.2
.

It is easy to see that, whenever

,

Thus

-\Delta u^{\alpha}\leq-\alpha(\alpha-1)\varepsilon^{\alpha-2}C^{2}+\alpha\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}g(\varepsilon);

If

near the boundary; that is,

is not concave.
This remark in particular applies to

The techniques on which the previous results are based mainly involve regularity of solutions and maximum principles on the

Anyway, due to the regularity restrictions, these techniques cannot be directly applied to

The second ingredient is the form of the regularization: as a matter of fact, we need a regularization process which preserves the concavity structure of the original equation.
This is what has been done by

Remark 1.12
.

As a further consequence of Theorem

, by Hyers-Ulam Theorem
[

we know that there exists a concave function

such that

notice that this is actually an equivalence, since the existence of such a function easily implies an estimate as the one in Theorem

When

Theorem

(instead of

It would be interesting anyway to investigate some properties of the function

(e.g., if it satisfies some differential equation).

Remark 1.13
.

We believe that the condition

Lemma 3.1

(
[
101
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, be open and with the uniform interior cone condition. Consider a function

,
\beta\in(0,1]
and
q\in[1,+\infty)
.
Then

(3.1)

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u\|_{C^{0,\beta}}^{1-\kappa_{q}}\|u\|_{q}^{\kappa_{q}}

where

\kappa_{q}:=\kappa_{N,\beta,q}:=\frac{\beta}{\beta+\frac{N}{q}}\in(0,1)

and

is given as follows:

•

if

(here

is the volume of the unit ball);

•

if

, let

be the radius of the (uniform) interior cone; then

(here

is the volume of the cone scaled to radius 1).

As a consequence of Lemma
3.1
, if

,

,
\beta\in(0,1]

\|u\|_{C^{k}}\leq C\|u\|_{C^{k,\beta}}^{1-\kappa_{q}}\|u\|_{W^{k,q}}^{\kappa_{q}}.

As a corollary of Lemma

We notice that, if

\kappa_{p^{*}}=\tfrac{p\beta}{p\beta+N-p}\in(0,1)
(for any

Corollary 3.2
.

.

6

6

4.
General lemmas about concavity

To discuss the concavity of a function
v
, we move the attention to the negativity of its concavity function
\mathcal{C}_{v}
and, in particular, to its supremum. The discussion will be splitted according to the location of the maximum, that is, on (or near) the “boundary” or the “interior” of the domain.
We highlight that the function
v
will take the place of the transformation of a solution
u
, see Corollary
4.4
below.

4.1.
Concavity on the boundary

In this Section we discuss the possibility of the concavity function
\mathcal{C}_{v}
to have a maximum on the boundary of its domain.
Here we do not use the equation, but only the information on the boundary; see anyway
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
where also the equation is exploited (in a singular framework).

We start by a result which deals
precisely
with the boundary of (a general)
\Omega
, but gives no good quantitative information to be inherited by functions approximating
v
(compare it with Proposition
4.5
below and subsequent comments).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, be open, bounded, convex. Let
v:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
and

(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})\in\overline{\Omega}\times\partial\Omega\in[0,1]
.
Then the following facts hold.

a)

if
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\subset\partial\Omega
, assume

(4.1)

v=const\;\hbox{ on $[\bar{x},\bar{y}]$};

then
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})=0
;

b)

if
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\not\subset\partial\Omega
and
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,1)
, assume

(4.2)

\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\lambda)<0\quad\hbox{for $\lambda\approx 0^{+}$};

then
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})
is not a global maximum of
\mathcal{C}_{v}
{}_{|\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\times[0,1]}
.

As a consequence, if conditions in a)-b) hold, then
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})
is not a positive global maximum of
\mathcal{C}_{v}
.
As a further consequence, if
\Omega
is strictly convex,
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,1)
,
\bar{x}\neq\bar{y}
and condition in b) hold, then
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})
is not a global maximum of
\mathcal{C}_{v}
.

Proof.
We can assume
\bar{\lambda}\in(0,1)
, otherwise
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})=0
. Set
\bar{z}:=\bar{\lambda}\bar{x}+(1-\bar{\lambda})\bar{y}
.
If
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\subset\partial\Omega
, then
\bar{z}\in\partial\Omega
and hence by (
4.1
) we have
v(\bar{x})=v(\bar{y})=v(\bar{z})
, which implies
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})=0
.
Thus we focus on
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\not\subset\partial\Omega
.

Roughly speaking, by the assumptions
v
is concave along the segment
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]
, near
\bar{y}
; by moving
\bar{y}
a little closer to
\bar{x}
, but keeping their convex combination the same, we expect that this procedure increases the value of
\mathcal{C}_{v}
.
Let us see this in details.
By (
4.2
), for some small
\lambda^{*}\in(0,\bar{\lambda})
, (
\lambda^{*}\approx 0
) we have

\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\lambda^{*})<0.

Set
\bar{y}^{*}:=\lambda^{*}\bar{x}+(1-\lambda^{*})\bar{y}
, (
\bar{y}^{*}\approx\bar{y}
) it means

(4.3)

v(\bar{y}^{*})>\lambda^{*}v(\bar{x})+(1-\lambda^{*})v(\bar{y}).

Choose
\mu\in(0,1)
in such a way

\mu\bar{x}+(1-\mu)\bar{y}^{*}=\bar{\lambda}\bar{x}+(1-\bar{\lambda})\bar{y}
,
that is

\mu:=\frac{\bar{\lambda}-\lambda^{*}}{1-\lambda^{*}}\in(0,1);

notice that
\bar{y}^{*}
, rather than
\bar{y}
, is closer to
\bar{x}
, while
\mu
maintains the middle combination constant.
Moreover (
4.3
) clearly implies

\mu v(\bar{x})+(1-\mu)v(\bar{y}^{*})>\bar{\lambda}v(\bar{x})+(1-\bar{\lambda})v(\bar{y})

from which
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y}^{*},\mu)>\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})
, that is the claim.
We conclude by observing that case a) cannot occur on a strictly convex domain, unless
\bar{x}=\bar{y}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In order to apply Lemma
4.1
, we notice that (
4.2
) is equivalent to check

\frac{v\big(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\big)-v(\bar{y})}{\lambda}>v(\bar{x})-v(\bar{y})\quad\hbox{for $\lambda\approx 0^{+}$}.

Thus it is sufficient to verify

(4.4)

\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{v\big(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\big)-v(\bar{y})}{\lambda}>v(\bar{x})-v(\bar{y})

or equivalently, if
v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

\partial_{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}v(\bar{y})\equiv\nabla v(\bar{y})\cdot(\bar{x}-\bar{y})>v(\bar{x})-v(\bar{y})

or similarly

v(\bar{x})<\nabla v(\bar{y})\cdot(\bar{x}-\bar{y})+v(\bar{y}).

Notice that, saying

v(x)<\nabla v(\bar{y})\cdot(x-\bar{y})+v(\bar{y})\quad\hbox{for $x\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{\bar{y}\}$}

means that
v
lies strictly beneath the plane tangent to
v
in the boundary point
\bar{y}\in\partial\Omega
(see also (
4.6
)).

Remark 4.3
.

By assuming
v\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and the stronger assumption – which clearly implies (
4.4
) –

(4.5)

\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{v\big(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\big)-v(\bar{y})}{\lambda}=+\infty

the proof of Lemma
4.1
can be simplified: indeed, in the case
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\not\subset\partial\Omega
, being for
\varepsilon
small
\bar{y}+\varepsilon(\bar{y}-\bar{x})\in\Omega
and exploiting
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})\geq\mathcal{C}_{v}\big(\bar{x},\bar{y}+\varepsilon(\bar{y}-\bar{x}),\bar{\lambda}\big)
, we get

(1-\bar{\lambda})\frac{v(\bar{y})-v\big(\bar{y}+\varepsilon(\bar{y}-\bar{x})\big)}{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{v\big(\bar{\lambda}\bar{x}+(1-\bar{\lambda})\bar{y}\big)-v\big(\bar{\lambda}\bar{x}+(1-\bar{\lambda})\bar{y}+\varepsilon(1-\bar{\lambda})(\bar{y}-\bar{x})\big)}{\varepsilon}.

Thus, sending
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we obtain
-\infty\geq(1-\bar{\lambda})\nabla v(\bar{\lambda}\bar{x}+(1-\bar{\lambda})\bar{y})\cdot(\bar{y}-\bar{x});

being
\bar{\lambda}\bar{x}+(1-\bar{\lambda})\bar{y}\in\Omega
, we have a contradiction.

We can finally deal with transformations of a function
u
; notice that we are not requiring
\varphi
to be monotone nor concave.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, be open, bounded and convex.
Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
and
u
be a function with

u=k\;\hbox{ and }\;\partial_{\nu}u>0\quad\hbox{ on $\partial\Omega$};

here
\nu
is the interior normal vector.
Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{k\})
and

\limsup_{t\to k}\varphi^{\prime}(t)=+\infty.

Set
v:=\varphi(u)
.
If
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})
is a global maximum of
\mathcal{C}_{v}
{}_{|\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\times[0,1]}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})=0
or
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\textup{int}\left(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\right)
.
In particular, if
\Omega
is strictly convex, then
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})=0
may happen only if
\bar{x}=\bar{y}
or
\bar{\lambda}\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.
Assume by contradiction
\mathcal{C}_{v}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{\lambda})>0
and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\notin\textup{int}\left(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}\right)
; with no loss of generality, we assume

\bar{y}\in\partial\Omega
,
\bar{x}\in\overline{\Omega}
; we want to ensure condition (
4.4
) (actually, (
4.5
)) in order to apply Lemma
4.1
.
Fixed such points, by
\partial_{\nu(\bar{y})}u(\bar{y})>0
, we have that also
\partial_{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}u(\bar{y})>0
on
\partial\Omega
(here we use that
[\bar{x},\bar{y}]\not\subset\partial\Omega
, thus
\bar{x}-\bar{y}\not\perp\nu
is pointing inward).
Thus

\ell_{\bar{x},\bar{y}}:=\partial_{x-\bar{y}}u(\bar{y})=\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{u(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y}))-u(\bar{y})}{\lambda}>0;

in particular,
u(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y}))>u(\bar{y})
for
\lambda
small.
As a consequence, by the mean value theorem, for each
\lambda
small there exists
t(\lambda)\in\big(u(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})),u(\bar{y})\big)
(and thus
t(\lambda)\to u(\bar{y})=k
as
\lambda\to 0
) such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{v\big(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\big)-v(\bar{y})}{\lambda}=\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{\varphi\big(u\big(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\big)\big)-\varphi(u(\bar{y}))}{\lambda}

∎

\displaystyle=\limsup_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\bigg(\varphi^{\prime}(t(\lambda))\frac{u\big(\bar{y}+\lambda(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\big)-u(\bar{y})}{\lambda}\bigg)=+\infty>v(\bar{x})-v(\bar{y}).

∎

\displaystyle\,\lambda b(x,u(x))+(1-\lambda)b(y,u(y))-b\big(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y,\lambda u(x)+(1-\lambda)u(y)\big),

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{HC}_{b}\big((x,u(x)),(y,u(y)),\lambda\big)

\displaystyle\,\frac{b(x,u(x))b(y,u(y))}{\lambda b(y,u(y))+(1-\lambda)b(x,u(x))}-b(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y,\lambda u(x)+(1-\lambda)u(y)\big).

which implies, by Theorem

∎

Very few is known regarding concavity results for fractional equations: some partial results are contained in

Anyway, the only result, close to our framework and known to the authors, is contained in

On the other hand, the lack of a transformation with singular derivative, together with the Neumann boundary condition of the extended equation, brings to much more difficulties in handling the boundary.

By looking at the cases

and

What we do in this Section is some partial results in this direction, through perturbation arguments.

Indeed, consider the

(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dy\quad\hbox{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

We highlight that the choice of the constant in front of the integral (in this case, equal to

In what follows, we recall some results in the literature folklore, but of which the authors were not able to find a proof. We will state the results in the case of a general

A.1.
Uniqueness

We state a uniqueness result when
\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}
is strictly decreasing (see also
[
71

).
When
f(x,t)\equiv g(t)
, we refer also to
[
23
, Proposition 3.8]
which essentially says that the same uniqueness holds if
\frac{g(t)}{t^{p-1}}
is nonincreasing and decreasing only on a small region
[0,\delta]
; see also
[
116
]
for further comments. See also
[
15
,
88
]
where the interesting tool of hidden convexity has been used to deal with general
\Omega
.
Finally, we refer to Section

for the superhomogeneous case.

Lemma A.1

(Brezis-Oswald uniqueness)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, be open, bounded, connected, satisfying the interior sphere condition, and let
p\in(1,+\infty)
.
Assume that
f:\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

•

t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}
is nonincreasing,

•

f
is bounded on bounded sets,

and let
u,v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be two solutions of
(
5.1
).
Then

v=ku\quad\hbox{for some $k\in(0,+\infty)$}.

Assume moreover that

•

t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}
is strictly decreasing.

Then
v=u
.

Proof.
First, we notice that
\Delta_{p}u,\Delta_{p}v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and that
\frac{u}{v},\frac{v}{u}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
: indeed, being
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
,
by Hopf boundary lemma
[
128
, Theorem 5]

we have
\partial_{\nu}u,\partial_{\nu}v>0
on
\partial\Omega
, and thus by de l’Hopital rule we have, for any
x\in\partial\Omega
,

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{u(x+t\nu)}{v(x+t\nu)}\leq\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\partial_{t}u(x+t\nu)}{\partial_{t}v(x+t\nu)}=\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\nabla u(x+t\nu)\cdot\nu}{\nabla v(x+t\nu)\cdot\nu}=\frac{\partial_{\nu}u(x)}{\partial_{\nu}v(x)}<+\infty,

thus the claim.
Hence we can apply
[
50
, Lemma 2]
and obtain

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}-\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}\right)(u^{p}-v^{p})=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{-\Delta_{p}u}{u^{p-1}}-\frac{-\Delta_{p}v}{v^{p-1}}\right)(u^{p}-v^{p})\geq 0.

Since
t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}
is nonincreasing, we have

(A.1)

\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}=\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}};

notice that, if
t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}
is strictly decreasing, we have the second claim.
By Picone’s inequality
[
3
]
we gain

(A.2)

|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\leq|\nabla u|^{p}

where the equality is attained if and only if
u
and
v
are proportional. Integrating, we get

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}.

Notice that
\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}=(\frac{u}{v})^{p-1}u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
and
\nabla\left(\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)=p\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}\nabla u+(p-1)\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\nabla v\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
thus
\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and by the equations
and the above relation we obtain

\int_{\Omega}f(x,v)\frac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla u=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)u

that is

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}-\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}\right)u^{p}\leq 0.

On the other hand, due to (
A.1
), the last is an equality, and so it must be (
A.2
), which implies the claim.
∎

A.2.
Comparison principle

We show now a comparison principle for quasilinear equations in presence of a
p
-subhomogeneous function; notice that we are not requiring
f
itself to be decreasing.
The proof is inspired by
[
115
, Theorem 1.5]
; notice moreover that the result was obtained, when
f
is a power, by
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
in a more general setting through the use of hidden convexity.
See also
[
45
, Theorems 1.3 and 3.3]
,
[
24

.

Finally, notice that the uniqueness result part of Lemma
A.1
can be deduced from this comparison principle.

Lemma A.2

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, be open, bounded, connected, satisfying the interior sphere condition, and let
p\in(1,+\infty)
.
Assume that
f:\Omega\times(0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

•

t\mapsto\frac{f(x,t)}{t^{p-1}}
strictly decreasing,

•

f
is bounded on bounded sets,

and let
u,v\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a subsolution and a supersolution,
namely

-\Delta_{p}u-f(x,u)\leq-\Delta_{p}v-f(x,v)\quad\hbox{ in $\Omega$}

with

u,v>0\;\hbox{ in $\Omega$},\quad u\leq v\;\hbox{ on $\partial\Omega$}.

Then

u\leq v\quad\hbox{in $\Omega$.}

Proof.
Set
w:=(u^{p}-v^{p})^{+}
, we need to prove that
w\equiv 0
. Consider
\Omega_{+}:=\textup{supp}(w)=\{u\geq v\}
.
As in the proof of Lemma
A.1
we have
\frac{u}{v},\frac{v}{u}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
thus we compute

\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{u^{p-1}}\right)=\chi_{\Omega_{+}}\left(\nabla u-\nabla\left(\tfrac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right)+\delta(p-1)\nabla u\right);

this first tells us that
\frac{w}{u^{p-1}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover

|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{u^{p-1}}\right)=\chi_{\Omega_{+}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right)\right);

similarly

\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{v^{p-1}}\right)=\chi_{\Omega_{+}}\left(-\nabla v+\nabla\left(\tfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\right),

thus
\frac{w}{v^{p-1}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and

|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{v^{p-1}}\right)=\chi_{\Omega_{+}}\left(-|\nabla v|^{p}+|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{u^{p-1}}\right)-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{v^{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\Omega_{+}}\left(|\nabla v|^{p}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{v^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\right)\right)+\chi_{\Omega_{+}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\right)\right)\geq 0

by Picone’s inequality (
A.2
).
In particular, being
\frac{w}{u^{p-1}},\frac{w}{v^{p-1}}
nonnegative test functions,

\int_{\Omega}\left(f(x,u)\frac{w}{u^{p-1}}-f(x,v)\frac{w}{v^{p-1}}\right)\geq\int_{\Omega_{+}}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{u^{p-1}}\right)-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla\left(\tfrac{w}{v^{p-1}}\right)\right)\geq 0.

On the other hand, by the monotonicity
\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}-\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}\leq 0
on
\Omega_{+}
,
thus

\left(\frac{f(x,u)}{u^{p-1}}-\frac{f(x,v)}{v^{p-1}}\right)w\equiv 0\quad\hbox{on $\Omega$}.

Finally, by the strict monotonicity, we gain
w\equiv 0
, that is the claim.
∎

As a consequence we obtain a uniform bound from below.

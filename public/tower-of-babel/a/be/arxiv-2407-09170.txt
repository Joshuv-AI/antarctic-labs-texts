Linear waves on the expanding region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetimes: forward asymptotics and scattering from infinity
Louie Bernhardt

Abstract
We study solutions to the linear wave equation on the cosmological region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetimes. We show that all sufficiently regular finite-energy solutions to the linear equation possess a particular finite-order asymptotic expansion near the future boundary. Specifically, we prove that several terms in this asymptotic expansion are identically zero. This is accomplished with new weighted higher-order energy estimates that capture the global expansion of the cosmological region. Furthermore we prove existence and uniqueness of scattering solutions to the linear wave equation on the expanding region. Given two pieces of scattering data at infinity, we construct solutions that have the same asymptotics as forward solutions. The proof involves constructing asymptotic solutions to the wave equation, as well as a new weighted energy estimate that is suitable for the backward problem. This scattering result extends to a large class of expanding spacetimes, including the Kerr de Sitter family.

1.1
Existence of solutions to the linear wave equation with prescribed scattering data on
\Sigma^{+}

We give a complete treatment of scattering of wave equations on Schwarzschild-de Sitter in

\psi_{\text{asymp}}(t,\omega)=\psi_{0}(t,\omega)+\frac{\psi_{2}(t,\omega)}{r^{2}}+\frac{\psi_{3}(t,\omega)}{r^{3}},

\psi_{0}

4

(Weighted energy estimate for the backward problem)

Suppose

We prove Proposition

is monotone decreasing

5

Extension to the cosmological horizon

While Theorem
1.4
guarantees existence of solutions to
\square_{g}\psi=0
on the interior of the expanding region, the energy estimate from Proposition
1.8
degenerates at the cosmological horizons, and does not guarantee that solutions remain uniformly bounded on the cosmological horizons.

We define a nondegenerate energy on the cosmological horizons. We denote this energy by
E^{N}[\psi]
. The full definition of
E^{N}[\psi]
can be found in Section
3.5
, but for now we mention that on the cosmological horizons:

E^{N}[\psi]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}^{+}\cup\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{+}}(T\psi)^{2}+|\not{\nabla}\psi|^{2},

where
\not{\nabla}
denotes the covariant derivative on the sphere of radius
r
.

In order to solve all the way to the cosmological horizons
\mathcal{C}^{+}\cup\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
in a nondegenerate energy space, we additionally assume exponential decay of the data
\psi_{0}
,
\psi_{3}
along the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
towards
\iota^{+}
and
\overline{\iota}^{+}
.

Theorem 1.10

(Scattering up to the cosmological horizon)
.

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
be scattering data defined on the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
such that
\psi_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}),\psi_{3}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Moreover, assume that
\psi_{0}
,
\psi_{3}
decay exponentially, in that there exists some sufficiently large constant
\beta=\beta(\Lambda,m)>0
such that as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
we have

\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{4}((-\tau,\tau)^{c}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}((-\tau,\tau)^{c}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m}e^{-\beta\tau}.

(1.11)

Then there exists a unique solution
\psi
to (
1.1
) on
\mathcal{R}^{+}\cup\mathcal{C}^{+}\cup\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
with scattering data
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
, and at the cosmological horizon, we have the energy estimate

E^{N}[\psi]\lesssim_{\Lambda,m}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.

(1.12)

Remark 1.11
.

The infimum of all values of the constant
\beta
for which Theorem

holds is
\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}
is the

of the cosmological horizons. This requirement of a sufficient degree of exponential decay is tied to the

, a property of Killing horizons such as cosmological horizons. The previously-discussed redshift effect leads to boundedness of waves forwards-in-time along Killing horizons. However for the backwards problem this is seen as a blueshift, which leads to exponential growth of waves backwards-in-time. While it is primarily discussed in the setting of black holes in asymptotically flat spacetimes (see
[
5
,
9
,
26
]
), the blueshift effect is present for all nondegenerate Killing horizons.

By applying the pointwise boundedness estimate found in Corollary 2.12 in

]
, it follows that the solutions constructed in Theorem

\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{+}
.

.

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{3},\psi

\psi_{0}\in H^{6}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
,
\psi_{3}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})

\sup_{\{r\geq r_{\mathcal{C}}+\epsilon\}}|\psi|\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,\epsilon}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{6}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

If additionally

\sup_{\overline{\mathcal{R}}^{+}}|\psi|\lesssim_{\Lambda,m}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{6}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Corollary

1.2
Forward asymptotics for linear waves on Schwarzschild-de Sitter

We now turn our attention to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which we set up and prove in
Section
4
. Theorem
1.1
relies on several new higher-order energy estimates. These estimates capture the global expansion of
\mathcal{R}^{+}
. We have:

Theorem 1.14

(Higher-order redshift estimate)
.

Let
\psi
be a solution to the wave equation
\square_{g}\psi=0
on the expanding region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter, and let
r_{0}
be a radius that is slightly larger than
r_{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
X,Y
denote the vectorfields

X=r\phi^{-2}\partial_{r},\quad Y=r\phi^{-1}\partial_{r}.

Then for all
r_{2}>r_{1}\geq r_{\mathcal{C}}
we have the energy estimate

E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X\psi)](r_{2})+E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi](r_{2})+E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}[\psi](r_{2})\\
\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X\psi)](r_{1})+E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi](r_{1})+E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}[\psi](r_{1}).

(1.15)

Remark 1.15
.

The weights in
r
for the vectorfield commutators
X,Y
are, to leading order:

X\sim r^{3}\partial_{r},\quad Y\sim r^{2}\partial_{r},

and so applying these vectorfields to a solution
\psi
to the wave equation essentially identifies higher-order terms in the asymptotic expansion (
1.5
). To see this, if we assume the asymptotic expansion (
1.5
) holds, then

\psi\sim\psi_{0},\quad r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi\sim\psi_{2},\quad r^{2}\partial_{r}(r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi)\sim\psi_{3}.

Remark 1.16
.

In
[
27
]
, it was shown that the zeroth-order energy
E[\psi]
is monotone in
r
, so that if
\square_{g}\psi=0
, then

E[\psi](r_{2})\leq E[\psi](r_{1})

(1.16)

for all
r_{2}\geq r_{1}>r_{\mathcal{C}}
. In that paper a
vectorfield multiplier
is identified that captures the global expansion of
\mathcal{R}^{+}
, in the sense that it produces a nonnegative bulk term in the resulting energy identity.

We derive the higher-order redshift estimate of Theorem
1.14
by showing that as
vectorfield commutators
,
X
and
Y
satisfy good commutation identities, once again leading to a nonnegative bulk term. These commutators are proportional to
\partial_{r}
, and so are not Killing. This means that
X
and
Y
have commutation identities containing lower-order terms that also need to be controlled.

Remark 1.17
.

A similar higher-order global redshift estimate has been proved in
[
29
]
for
Weyl fields
on expanding spacetimes. Weyl fields are trace-free solutions to the Bianchi equations

\nabla^{\mu}W_{\mu\nu\lambda\delta}=0.

In that paper, the Bianchi equations are the hyperbolic differential equations for which a higher-order redshift estimate is proved. In the context of the local redshift effect on black hole horizons, these vectorfield commutator estimates stem from Dafermos and Rodnianski in
[
8
]
. We also mention
[
12
]
, in which similar ideas are used to proved higher-order energy estimates for linear waves on the black hole region of Schwarzschild spacetimes.

Remark 1.18
.

See
[
18
,
25
]
for results on forward asymptotics for the wave equation on related cosmological spacetimes. Forward asymptotics have been studied for solutions to Einstein’s vacuum equations. In particular, asymptotic expansions for perturbations of Minkoswki are obtained in

Through a density argument presented in Section

implies that the quantities

all have a nonzero limit on

Remark 1.19

We expect that one can derive successive higher-order weighted estimates by repeatedly commuting the existing estimate (

) with the commutator

1.3
Scattering on perturbations of Schwarzschild-de Sitter

The results we prove in this paper Schwarzschild-de Sitter can be suitably generalised to a broad class of expanding spacetimes. This is because they do not rely on fundamental solution techniques. In Section
5
we highlight this by extending the scattering result from Theorem
1.4
to a large class of expanding spacetimes. In particular, we show that the construction of scattering solutions extends to Kerr-de Sitter, as well as spacetimes that do not necessarily converge to Schwarzschild-de Sitter. The class of metrics we consider, broadly speaking, are those with metrics that have the same leading-order asymptotic behaviour as Schwarzschild-de Sitter in terms of growth or decay in
r
.
6
6

6

This class of spacetimes is similar to those considered in
[
30
]
, with the difference being that the metric components here can be assumed to be of finite-order regularity rather than smooth.
We do not assume closeness of the metric to Schwarzschild-de Sitter, and we make no symmetry assumption. An explicit description of the metrics we consider can be found in Definition
5.2

On this class of spacetimes we have the following scattering result, stated informally:

Theorem 1.20

(Scattering of linear waves on perturbations of Schwarzschild-de Sitter)
.

An analogue of the scattering result of Theorem
1.4
holds for solutions to the linear wave equation on spacetimes with the same leading-order asymptotic behaviour as Schwarzschild-de Sitter.

For a formal statement of this result, see
Theorem
5.10
in
Section
5.3
. We show that the two components of the proof, which are the construction of asymptotic solutions and a weighted energy estimate suitable for the backward problem, generalise from Schwarzschild-de Sitter to the perturbed setting.

Depending on the form of the metrics, the asymptotic solutions to the wave equation on a given spacetime we construct are of the form

\psi_{\text{asymp}}\sim\psi_{0}+\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\log r\>\psi_{3,1}}{r^{3}}+\frac{\psi_{3}}{r^{3}}.

Thus a
r^{3}\log r\>\psi_{3,1}
term can be present in the expansion. We specify sufficient conditions on the metric for which this log term is not present. In particular these conditions are satisfied by the expanding region of the Kerr-de Sitter family of spacetimes. This implies the following:

Corollary 1.21

(Scattering on Kerr-de Sitter)
.

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
be sufficiently regular functions at the future boundary of Kerr-de Sitter. Then there exists a unique, finite-energy solution to the wave equation
\square_{g}\psi=0
on the expanding region of Kerr-de Sitter such that for large
r
we have

\psi\sim\psi_{0}+\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\psi_{3}}{r^{3}},

where
\psi_{2}
is determined by
\psi_{0}
.

In this section we recall some of the relevant properties of Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetimes, and the energies, norms, and other notation which we use throughout this paper. For a more complete introduction of the geometry of Schwarzschild-de Sitter, see
[
21
]
, as well as
[
27
]
.

2.1
Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime

The Schwarzschild-de Sitter family of spacetimes, discovered independently in
[
20
]
and
[
31
]
, constitute a one-parameter family of solutions to
Einsteins vacuum equations with positive cosmological constant

\text{Ric}(g)-\Lambda g=0,\quad\Lambda>0.

(2.1)

These spacetimes are parametrised by the black hole mass, denoted by
m
.
We assume that
0<3m<1/\sqrt{\Lambda}
, which restricts the class of spacetimes we analyse to
subextremal
Schwarzschild-de Sitter.

Recall the metric

g_{\Lambda,m}=-\frac{1}{\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-1+\frac{2m}{r}}\textnormal{d}r^{2}+\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-1+\frac{2m}{r}\Big)\textnormal{d}t^{2}+r^{2}\>\textnormal{d}\theta^{2}+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\>\textnormal{d}\varphi^{2},

(2.2)

defined on the expanding region. From this, we see that the coordinate vector field
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
is timelike on
\mathcal{R}^{+}
, and we can view
r
as a
time function
for the expanding region. Recall also the lapse of the
\Sigma_{r}
-foliation

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-1+\frac{2m}{r}}}.

As
\phi
frequently appears in weighted energies in this paper, we emphasise that for large
r
,
\phi\sim 1/r
. One can decompose the metric (
2.2
) so that

g=-\phi^{2}\textnormal{d}r^{2}+\overline{g}_{r},

(2.3)

where
\overline{g}_{r}
is the induced metric on the hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{r}
.

We also recall from
[
27
]
the
Kruskal coordinates

(u,v)
on Schwarzschild-de Sitter
The coordinates
(r,t,\theta,\varphi)
cover only the expanding region
\mathcal{R}^{+}
, and the metric (
2.2
) becomes singular at
r=r_{\mathcal{C}}
. In contrast the Kruskal coordinates
(u,v,\theta,\varphi)
cover the cosmological horizons
\mathcal{C}^{+},\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
, and are related to
(r,t,\theta,\varphi)
coordinates implicitly by

\displaystyle uv=\frac{r-r_{\mathcal{C}}}{(r-r_{\mathcal{H}})^{\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}}(r+|\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}|)^{\overline{\alpha_{\mathcal{C}}}}},

\displaystyle\log\Big|\frac{u}{v}\Big|=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}\frac{(r_{\mathcal{C}}-r_{\mathcal{H}})(r_{\mathcal{C}}+|\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}|)}{r_{\mathcal{C}}}t,

where

\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}=\frac{r_{\mathcal{H}}}{r_{\mathcal{C}}}\frac{r_{\mathcal{C}}+\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}}{r_{\mathcal{H}}+\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}},\quad\overline{\alpha_{\mathcal{C}}}=\frac{\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}}{r_{\mathcal{C}}}\frac{r_{\mathcal{C}}-r_{\mathcal{H}}}{r_{\mathcal{C}}+\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}}

are positive constants such that
\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}+\overline{\alpha_{\mathcal{C}}}=1
.
We will also write

\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}\doteq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Lambda}{3}\frac{(r_{\mathcal{C}}-r_{\mathcal{H}})(r_{\mathcal{C}}+|\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}|)}{r_{\mathcal{C}}},

(2.4)

and mention briefly that
\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}
is the so-called surface gravity of the cosmological horizons. With respect to these coordinates, the metric is

g=-\Omega^{2}\textnormal{d}u\textnormal{d}v+r^{2}\gamma_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

(2.5)

where
\Omega^{2}
is the function

\Omega^{2}=\frac{\Lambda}{3}\frac{1}{\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}r}(r-r_{\mathcal{H}})^{1+\alpha_{\mathcal{H}}}(r+|\overline{r_{\mathcal{C}}}|)^{1+\overline{\alpha_{\mathcal{C}}}}.

(2.6)

We emphasise that
\Omega^{2}
does not vanish on
\mathcal{C}^{+}
. The Kruskal coordinates
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
cover the region
r_{\mathcal{H}}<r(u,v)<\infty
, and the expanding region
\mathcal{R}^{+}
is equivalent to the set
\{(u,v):0<uv<1\}
. Moreover, for the cosmological horizons
\mathcal{C}^{+}=\{(u,v):v=0,u\geq 0\}
,
\overline{\mathcal{C}}^{+}=\{(u,v):u=0,v\geq 0\}
. Finally, the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
corresponds with the level set
\{(u,v):uv=1,u,v>0\}
.

We recall the Killing vector field
T
, which in Kruskal coordinates is given by

T=\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}\Big(u\frac{\partial}{\partial u}-v\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\Big).

(2.7)

On
\mathcal{R}^{+}
,
T
is precisely the coordinate vectorfield
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
, and is spacelike. On the cosmological horizons
T
is null. We also define the vectorfield

Y|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}=\frac{1}{\iota_{\mathcal{C}}}\frac{1}{u}\frac{\partial}{\partial v},

(2.8)

where

\iota_{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{4}{\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}}\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}.

One can see that
Y
is conjugate to
T
on the horizon, specifically we have

g(T,Y)|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}\iota_{\mathcal{C}}\Omega^{2}|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}=-2.

2.2
Wave equations and the energy method

In this paper we apply the classical vectorfield method to prove estimates on finite-energy solutions to linear wave equations, see
[
8
]
for an introduction to these methods in a general relativity context. Recall the standard energy-momentum tensor
T
for the linear wave equation, given by

T_{\mu\nu}[\psi]=\partial_{\mu}\psi\partial_{\nu}\psi-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\alpha}\psi),

as well as the energy current
J^{X}
with respect to a given vectorfield
X
, a
1
-form defined by

J^{X}[\psi]\cdot Y=T[\psi](X,Y).

Given timelike vector fields
X,Y
,
J^{X}[\psi]\cdot Y
is a positive-definite quadratic form of
\partial\psi
on
\mathcal{R}^{+}
, in the sense that the following pointwise inequality holds:

J^{X}[\psi]\cdot Y\gtrsim\sum_{|\alpha|=1}|\partial^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}.

We will use the following notation for energy fluxes and norms induced by these energy currents.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a causal vectorfield
X
, and spacelike or null hypersurface
\Sigma
, Let
E_{\Sigma}^{X}[\psi]
denote the energy flux through
\Sigma
with respect to
X
, so that

E_{\Sigma}^{X}[\psi]\doteq\int_{\Sigma}\>^{*}J^{X}[\psi],

(2.9)

where
\>{}^{*}J^{X}[\psi]
denotes the Hodge dual of the corresponding vectorfield
(J^{X})^{\sharp}
.
We also define the corresponding induced energy norm

\|\psi\|_{X,\Sigma}\doteq\Big(E_{\Sigma}^{X}[\psi]\Big)^{1/2}.

(2.10)

If
\Sigma
is a spacelike hypersurface, we may write

\>{}^{*}J^{X}[\psi]|_{\Sigma}=J^{X}[\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma}\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{\Sigma}},

where
n_{\Sigma}
is the unit normal of
\Sigma
, and
\overline{g}_{\Sigma}
is the induced metric on
\Sigma
.

Remark 2.2
.

We point out that the energy
E[\psi]
defined in (
1.2
) is equivalent to the energy flux
E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial_{r}}
:

E[\psi](r)=E_{\Sigma_{r}}^{\partial r}[\psi]=\int_{\Sigma_{r}}J^{\partial_{r}}[\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}

To understand the weights in
r
associated with the energy
E[\psi]
, we note that

\displaystyle n_{\Sigma_{r}}=\phi^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\sim r\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\quad\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}=r^{2}\phi^{-1}\>\textnormal{d}t\wedge\textnormal{d}\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sim r^{3}\>\textnormal{d}t\wedge\textnormal{d}\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

Thus for large
r
we have

\displaystyle E[\psi]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{2}\phi^{-2}(\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}+r^{2}\phi^{2}(\partial_{t}\psi)^{2}+r^{2}|\not{\nabla}\psi|^{2}\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}\mu_{\mathring{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\sim r^{4}\|\partial_{r}\psi(r)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}+\|\tilde{\nabla}\psi(r)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.

where
\not{\nabla}\psi
is the standard covariant derivative on a sphere of radius
r
,
\tilde{\nabla}\psi
is the standard covariant derivative on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and
\textnormal{d}\mu_{\mathring{\gamma}}
is the standard volume form on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

We will also define the following
L^{2}
-norm on the
\Sigma_{r}
-hypersurfaces. Let

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}=\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\psi^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\sim r^{3}\|\psi(r)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}.

In this paper, we adopt the following notation conventions to distinguish differential operators on relevant manifolds or submanifolds. We write
\not{\nabla}
to denote the covariant derivative restricted to a sphere of radius
r
,
\overline{\nabla}
for the covariant derivative restricted to the level sets
\Sigma_{r}
, and
\tilde{\nabla}
for the covariant derivative with respect to the conformal metric at
\Sigma^{+}
:

\tilde{g}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\overline{g}_{r}\Big)=\frac{\Lambda}{3}dt^{2}+\gamma_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

We will also use this convention for other differential operators which appear in this paper, such as the Laplacian.

Theorem
1.4

3.3

1.10

3.6

\psi_{0}

\psi_{3}

3.1
The construction of asymptotic solutions

In this section we construct asymptotic solutions that satisfy the wave equation
\square_{g}\psi_{\text{asymp}}=0
up to some small error that decays toward
\Sigma^{+}
. As previously stated, we will eventually set

\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\psi_{0}+\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\psi_{3}}{r^{3}},

where
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{3}
are freely chosen, and
\psi_{2}
is determined by
\psi_{0}
. We motivate this choice by first considering a more general finite-order power series that includes logarithmic terms, so that

\psi_{\text{asymp}}^{(N)}(r,t,\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{\psi_{n}(t,\omega)}{r^{n}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\log r\>\psi_{n,1}(t,\omega)}{r^{n}},

(3.1)

for some positive integer
N
. Here the
\psi_{n}
,
\psi_{n,1}
are functions on the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
. Since
\Sigma^{+}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the
\psi_{n}
can also be thought of as functions on the standard cylinder.

Remark 3.1
.

We emphasise that we will not be constructing solutions via an infinite power series expansion.
The notion of constructing solutions to the linear wave equation on Minkowski spacetime as a power series in
1/r
was first explored in detail by Friedlander, see
[
13
]
. Due to the different geometry of Minkowski spacetime, the particular asymptotic expansion is manifestly different. In particular, solutions to
\square\psi=0
behave to leading order near
\mathcal{I}^{+}
like

\psi(t,x)\sim\frac{F(r-t,\omega)}{r},\quad r=|x|,\quad\omega=\frac{x}{|x|},

where
F(r-t,\omega)
is the so-called
radiation field
, whose existence was established by Friedlander in the aforementioned papers. See also
[
12
]
for an example of asymtotic expansions of linear waves near a black hole singularity.

The wave equation on the expanding region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime is expressed as

\square_{g}\psi=\phi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{r^{2}}{\phi^{2}}\partial_{r}\psi\Big)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi.

(3.2)

We begin with a preliminary computation that we will use to compute
\square_{g}\psi_{\text{asymp}}^{(N)}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
r_{0}>r_{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
n
be a nonnegative integer, and let
\psi_{n}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Then for all
r\geq r_{0}
, we have the following two identities:

\square_{g}\Big(\frac{\psi_{n}(t,\omega)}{r^{n}}\Big)=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}n(n-3)\frac{1}{r^{n}}\psi_{n}+\frac{1}{r^{n+2}}\Big(n(n-1)\psi_{n}+\tilde{\Delta}\psi_{n}\Big)\\
-n^{2}\frac{2m}{r^{n+3}}\psi_{n}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{n+4}})(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi_{0}).

(3.3)

\square_{g}\Big(\frac{\log r\>\psi_{n,1}}{r^{n}}\Big)=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}n(n-3)\frac{\log r}{r^{n}}\psi_{n,1}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}(2n-3)\frac{1}{r^{n}}\psi_{n,1}+O\big(\frac{\log r}{r^{n+2}})\big.

(3.4)

Proof.

By (
3.2
), we have

\square_{g}\Big(\frac{\psi_{n}}{r^{n}}\Big)=\frac{\phi^{2}}{r^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi_{n}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{r^{2}}{\phi^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{n}}\Big)\Big)\psi_{n}+\frac{1}{r^{n+2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{n}.

(3.5)

The leading-order behaviour of
\phi^{2}
is

\phi^{2}=\frac{1}{\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-(1-\frac{2m}{r})}=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{1-\frac{2m}{r}}{\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-1+\frac{2m}{r}\Big)}=\frac{3}{\Lambda}\frac{1}{r^{2}}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big),

(3.6)

and so

\frac{\phi^{2}}{r^{n}}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi_{n}+\frac{1}{r^{n+2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi_{n}=\frac{1}{r^{n+2}}\tilde{\Delta}\psi_{n}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{n+4}}\big).

Meanwhile, we compute for the radial derivatives:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{r^{2}}{\phi^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{n}}\Big)\Big)\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{\phi^{2}r^{n-1}}\Big)\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{3}\frac{1}{r^{n-3}}-\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}+\frac{2m}{r^{n}}\Big)\psi_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}\frac{n(n-3)}{r^{n}}\psi_{n}+\frac{n(n-1)}{r^{n+2}}\psi_{n}-2m\frac{n^{2}}{r^{n+3}}\psi_{n}.

Combining this with (
3.5
) yields (
3.3
). A similar computation implies (
3.4
).
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We see from Lemma
3.2
that for all but finitely many
n
, we have

\displaystyle\square_{g}(r^{-n}\psi_{n})=O(r^{-n}),\quad\square_{g}(r^{-n}\log r\>\psi_{n,1})=O(r^{-n}\log r).

The three exceptions are
\psi_{0},\psi_{3},\psi_{3,1}
, where the leading order term vanishes, giving

\displaystyle\square_{g}\psi_{0}=O(r^{-2}),\quad\square_{g}(r^{-3}\psi_{3})=O(r^{-5}),\quad\square_{g}(r^{-3}\log r\>\psi_{3,1})=O(r^{-3}).

In the next Lemma we consider a general asymptotic solution
\psi_{\text{asymp}}^{(N)}
of the form (
3.1
). We will show that
\psi_{\text{asymp}}^{(N)}
satisfies the linear wave equation up to a particular order in
r
if and only if the terms
\psi_{n}
,
\psi_{n,1}
satisfy recurrence relations. Moreover, we will show that
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
capture the correct notion of scattering data for the asymptotic solution.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{1,1},\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{N,1},\psi_{N}
be sufficiently regular functions on
\Sigma^{+}
, and let
\psi_{\text{asymp}}^{(N)}
be of the form (
3.1
). Then

\square_{g}\psi_{\text{asymp}}=O\big(\frac{\log r}{r^{N+1}}\big)

(3.7)

if and only if
\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\psi_{1,1},\dots,\psi_{N},\psi_{N,1}
satisfy
2N-1
recurrence relations. These recurrence relations have two degrees of freedom, with
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
being the leading-order terms that can be freely chosen. The recurrence relations for the logarithmic terms reduce to

\psi_{n,1}=0,\quad n=1,\dots,N,

(3.8)

while the recurrence relations for the other terms reduce to

\displaystyle\psi_{1}=0,\quad\psi_{2}=-\frac{3}{\Lambda}\tilde{\Delta}\psi_{0},\quad\psi_{n}=R_{n}(\psi_{0},\psi_{3}),\quad n\geq 4,

where the
R_{n}
are linear functions of
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
and their derivatives.

Proof.

This is a straightforward application of Lemma
3.2
. In general, we have

\square_{g}\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{F_{n}}{r^{n}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\log r\>F_{n,1}}{r^{n}}+O\big(\frac{\log r}{r^{N+1}}\big),

where the coefficients
F_{n}
are functions of
\psi_{0},\dots,\psi_{n}
,
\psi_{1,1}\dots,\psi_{n,1}
, while the coefficients
F_{n,1}
are functions of
\psi_{1,1}\dots,\psi_{n,1}
only. From this we see that (
3.7
) holds if and only if
F_{0}=\dots=F_{n}=0
, and
F_{1,1}=\dots=F_{n,1}=0
, and these generate
2N+1
recurrence relations between the
\psi_{n},\psi_{n,1}
. We compute these recurrence relations in order of decay, simplifying as needed. As
\square_{g}\psi_{0}=O(r^{-2})
, the recurrence relation
F_{0}=0
is trivial, and the first two nontrivial recurrence relations are

F_{1,1}=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\psi_{1,1}=0,\quad F_{1}=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\psi_{1}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}\psi_{1,1}=0,

which reduce to
\psi_{1,1}=\psi_{1}=0
. Assuming this holds, the next two recurrence relations are

F_{2,1}=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\psi_{2,1}=0,\quad F_{2}=\tilde{\Delta}\psi_{0}+\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\psi_{2}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}\psi_{2,1}=0.

These reduce to
\psi_{2,1}=0
and
\psi_{2}=-\frac{3}{2\Lambda}\tilde{\Delta}\psi_{0}
. This implies that
\psi_{0}
can be freely chosen. By Lemma
3.2
, we see that

\square_{g}\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\tilde{\Delta}\psi_{0}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big)\quad\square_{g}\Big(\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big).

Moreover, we have
\square_{g}(r^{-3}\psi_{3})=O(r^{-5})
,
\square_{g}(r^{-3}\log r\>\psi_{3,1})=O(r^{-3})
, and so the recurrence relation
F_{3,1}=0
is trivial, while the recurrence relation for
F_{3}
is

F_{3,1}=\frac{\Lambda}{3}\psi_{3,1}=0.

For
n\geq 4
, the coefficients
F_{n,1}
are functions of
\psi_{1,1},\dots,\psi_{n,1}
only. Since
\square_{g}(r^{-n}\log r\>\psi_{n,1})=O(r^{-n}\log r)
for
n\geq 4
, the recurrence relations for the
F_{n,1}
reduce to
\psi_{n,1}=0
for all
4\leq n\leq N
. Thus the coefficients of all log terms in
\psi_{\text{asymp}}^{(N)}
are identically zero only if (
3.7
) holds. While we do not compute the remaining recurrence relations explicitly, we note that the relation
F_{4}=0
implies that
\psi_{4}
is determined entirely by
\psi_{0}
, and for
F_{5}
we have

F_{5}=F_{5}(\psi_{0},\psi_{2},\psi_{3},\psi_{4},\psi_{5}).

As
\psi_{2},\psi_{4}
are determined by
\psi_{0}
, this recurrence relation also has a degree of freedom, with
\psi_{3}
being the next leading-order term that can be freely chosen. Hence the remaining recurrence relations imply that the remaining terms are all determined by
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Lemma
3.4
depends heavily on the asymptotic expansion of the components of the metric (
2.2
) for Schwarzschild-de Sitter. In particular, the fact that

, the integral on the right hand side remains bounded as
R_{2}\rightarrow\infty
, and we have the bound

\|v\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\frac{1}{R_{1}}\|\tilde{\nabla}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\frac{1}{R_{1}^{2}}\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Thus for each
r\geq r_{0}
,
\|v\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}\rightarrow 0
as
R_{1}
,
R_{2}\rightarrow 0
. Hence there exists a function
\psi
such that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\|\psi-\psi_{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}=0

for each
r\geq r_{0}
.

We now derive the estimates on
\psi
, in particular we prove (
1.7
). By the triangle inequality we have

\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}\leq\|\psi_{\text{rem}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}+\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}.

We apply the weighted energy estimate Proposition
1.8
to
\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}
on
\mathcal{D}_{(r,R)}
, so that

\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}\leq\|\psi_{\text{rem}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}++\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{r}^{R}\|s\phi^{1/2}(s)\square_{g}\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}\textnormal{d}s,

where we used the fact that
\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}
has vanishing data on
\Sigma_{R}
, so that
\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}=0
. Again from Lemma
3.6
, the final integral is bounded as
R\rightarrow\infty
, and we have the bound

\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\|\psi_{\text{rem}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\|\tilde{\nabla}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.\\

(3.25)

Letting
R\rightarrow\infty
,
\|\psi_{\text{rem}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}\rightarrow 0
, and so it follows that for
\psi-\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\psi_{\text{rem}}
we have.

\displaystyle\|\psi-\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\|\tilde{\nabla}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\frac{1}{r^{4}}\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

(3.26)

From this and (
3.10
) we also have for all
r\geq r_{0}
,

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}r^{2}\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}}+\|\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\|\tilde{\nabla}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},

which is (
1.7
). Uniqueness follows immediately, since if we set
\psi_{0}=\psi_{3}=0
, then
\psi=0
by the above inequality.∎

3.4

First we establish the decay of the

Lemma 3.12
.

Let

,

Proof.

We will give a sketch of the proof here as this is a simple modification of Theorem

. First, we observe that (

) follows from the decay estimate (

), and the fact that

for all

), we start with the straightforward bound

Through the same method of proof as that of Theorem

, one can show that the sequence of functions

that solve

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}

then employing the weighted energy estimate from Proposition

to bound the integrand

Proof of pointwise decay (
1.8
).

We will apply the Sobolev estimate from Appendix
B
to
\psi_{\text{rem}}
. Let
I=(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4})
be a multiindex, and let
Z^{I}
denote the differential operator

Z^{I}=\Omega_{1}^{I_{1}}\Omega_{2}^{I_{2}}\Omega_{3}^{I_{3}}T^{I_{4}}.

By Proposition
B.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\psi_{\text{rem}}|^{2}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{3}}\Big\{\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}+\|(T\psi_{\text{rem}})\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\Omega_{i}\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\Omega_{i}T\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\|\Omega_{i}\Omega_{j}\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{3}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\|Z^{I}\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}.

(3.30)

As the operators
Z^{I}
are composed of Killing vector fields, they commute with the wave equation, and so
Z^{I}\psi_{\text{rem}}
solves the equation

\square_{g}(Z^{I}\psi_{\text{rem}})=-\square_{g}(Z^{I}\psi_{\text{asymp}}).

Moreover, the function
Z^{I}\psi_{\text{asymp}}
remains a good asymptotic solution, since

Z^{I}\psi_{\text{asymp}}=Z^{I}\psi_{0}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}Z^{I}\psi_{2}+\frac{1}{r^{3}}Z^{I}\psi_{3},

where

Z^{I}\psi_{2}=-\frac{3}{2\Lambda}\tilde{\Delta}(Z^{I}\psi_{0}).

By Theorem
1.4
, the solution
\varphi
to the wave equation with scattering data
Z^{I}\psi_{0}
,
Z^{I}\psi_{3}
is unique, and so the function
\psi_{\text{rem}}=\varphi-Z^{I}\psi_{\text{asymp}}
obeys the
L^{2}
-based decay estimate (
3.27
) from Propostion
3.12
, i.e.

\|Z^{I}\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{5/2}}\|\tilde{\nabla}(Z^{I}\psi_{0})\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}+\frac{1}{r^{7/2}}\|Z^{I}\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

Substituting this into (
3.30
), the result now follows.
∎

Here we slightly modify the construction of

\nabla_{Y}Y=-\sigma(Y+T).

This construction, capturing the local redshift effect of Killing horizons, was initially carried out in

7

8

[

Proposition 3.13
.

For
r_{0}>r_{\mathcal{C}}
, let
\mathcal{D}_{(r_{\mathcal{C}},r_{0})}
denote the spacetime region
\{(u,v):r_{\mathcal{C}}\leq r(u,v)\leq r_{0}\}
.
Let
\sigma<0
be some sufficiently negative constant, and let
Y
denoted the vectorfield define in (
2.8
) and (
3.31
). The vectorfield multiplier
N=Y+T
is strictly timelike on
\mathcal{D}_{(r_{\mathcal{C}},r_{0})}

c=c(\Lambda,m)>0
such that on
\mathcal{D}_{(r_{\mathcal{C}},r_{0})}
we have

K^{N}[\psi]\leq\frac{c}{u}J^{N}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v}.

(3.32)

Proof.

From
[
8
]
and
[
27
]
, we have

K^{Y}[\psi]\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}=-\frac{1}{2}|\sigma|(T\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}(Y\psi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\sigma||\not{\nabla}\psi|^{2}+\frac{2}{r_{\mathcal{C}}}(T\psi)(Y\psi).

(3.33)

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we may bound

\frac{2}{r}(T\psi)(Y\psi)\leq\frac{1}{2}|\sigma|(T\psi)^{2}+\frac{2}{r_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}|\sigma|}(Y\psi)^{2},

and so by (
3.33
), we have

K^{Y}[\psi]\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}+\frac{2}{r_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}|\sigma|}\Big)(Y\psi)^{2}.

(3.34)

A short computation reveals that the energy current
J^{N}\cdot\partial_{v}
restricted to the cosmological horizons is

J^{N}[\psi]\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}=\frac{u}{\iota_{\mathcal{C}}}\Big((Y\psi)^{2}+\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}|\not{\nabla}\psi|^{2}\Big).

Since
T
is Killing everywhere,
K^{N}=K^{Y}+K^{T}=K^{Y}
, and so by (
3.34
),

K^{N}[\psi]\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}+\frac{2}{r_{\mathcal{C}}^{2}\sigma}\Big)\frac{\iota_{\mathcal{C}}}{u}J^{N}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}.

By continuity this inequality can be extended to a neighbourhood of
\mathcal{C}^{+}
, and so we may choose some
r_{0}
sufficiently close to
r_{\mathcal{C}}
, and some
\sigma
sufficiently negative such that (
3.32
) holds on
\mathcal{D}_{(r_{\mathcal{C}},r_{0})}
.
∎

\frac{\textnormal{d}\tau}{\textnormal{d}u}\Big|_{\mathcal{C}^{+}}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\mathcal{C}}u}.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\psi

f(\tau)=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau}^{\prime}}\>^{*}J^{N}[\psi].

\mathcal{R}^{+}\cup\mathcal{C}^{+}

\displaystyle f(\tau_{1})+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}^{+}}\>^{*}J^{N}[\psi]\leq\>

Applying the divergence theorem to the energy current

\int_{C_{\tau_{2}}^{c}}\>^{*}J^{N}[\psi]+\int_{\Sigma_{r_{0}}^{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})}}J^{N}[\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}+\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}^{(r_{0})}}K^{N}[\psi]\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{g}\\

Let
\psi_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})

\|\tilde{\nabla}\psi_{0}\|_{H^{3}((-\tau,\tau)^{c}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})},\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{2}((-\tau,\tau)^{c}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m}e^{-\beta\tau}

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r}^{\tau})},\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\overline{\Sigma}_{r}^{\tau})}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r}e^{-\beta\tau}

1.8

3.19

through
\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}

\overline{\Sigma}_{r}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}+\|\psi_{\text{rem}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}+\|\psi_{\text{rem}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}+\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}

(3.40)

As a consequence of the localised energy estimate (
3.19

\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{R}\|_{M,\Sigma_{r}^{\tau}}\leq\int_{r}^{R}\|r\phi^{1/2}\square_{g}\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r}^{\tau})}\textnormal{d}r.

This follows from taking the limit
\overline{v}\rightarrow 0
, while still keeping
\overline{u}
fixed in (

). From Lemma

4
Asymptotics of forward solutions to the wave equation on Schwarzschild-de Sitter

In this section we prove the main asymptotics result
Theorem
1.1
for solutions to the linear wave equation on Schwarzschild-de Sitter. We emphasise the absence of an
O(r^{-1})
term in this expansion, and additionally the absence of log terms. In particular we prove the existence of the limits (
1.4
) in Section
4.5
.

We establish these asymptotics by employing several higher-order energy estimates, which we also prove in this section. These estimates are obtained by commuting the wave equation with suitably chosen vectorfield commutators. These commutators are not the Killing vectorfields
T,\Omega_{i}
that commute with the wave operator
\square_{g}
, but are instead proportional to the radial coordinate vectorfield
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}
. The main energy estimate, found in
Theorem
1.14
, is proved in Section
4.3
.

4.1
Higher order energy estimates with vectorfield commutators

In order to prove the subsequent energy estimates throughout this section, we will be returning to a common argument for generating energy estimates on
\mathcal{R}^{+}
. We apply the divergence theorem to the energy current
J^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]
on the spacetime region bound in the past by
\Sigma_{r_{1}}
, and to the future by
\Sigma_{r_{2}}
, where
r_{2}>r_{1}\gg r_{\mathcal{C}}
, which yields the inequality

\int_{\Sigma_{r_{2}}}J^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\phi\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big)\textnormal{d}r\leq\int_{\Sigma_{r_{1}}}J^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}},

(4.1)

where we applied the coarea formula to the bulk term. The strategy for proving the higher-order energy estimates in this section is to identify vectorfield commutators
X
for which the divergence of
J^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]
possesses key sign and decay properties. Recall that by the product rule we have

\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]=K^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]+\partial_{r}(X\psi)\square_{g}(X\psi)

(4.2)

It was computed in
[
27
]
that

K^{\partial_{r}}[X\psi]=\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{m}{r}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X\psi))^{2}+\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{m}{r}\Big)\phi^{4}(\partial_{t}(X\psi))^{2}.

(4.3)

One can see from this that (to leading order in
r
), the energy current
K^{\partial_{r}}
is nonnegative. Indeed this nonnegativity is what implies the global redshift estimate of
[
27
]
. For higher-order estimates, however, one must consider contributions from the second term
\partial_{r}(X\psi)\square_{g}(X\psi)
. We will restrict our attention to commutation vectorfields of the form
X=f(r)\partial_{r}
. We begin by deriving an identity for
\square_{g}(X\psi)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in C^{2}(r_{\mathcal{C}},\infty)
with
f(r)>0
for all
r>r_{\mathcal{C}}
, and let
X=f(r)\partial_{r}
. Then

\square_{g}(X\psi)=X(\square_{g}\psi)+\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{f^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(X\psi)+\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\Big(\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{f}\Big)\partial_{r}\psi\\
+2f\phi^{4}\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi+\frac{2f}{r}\square_{g}\psi.

(4.4)

Proof.

By (
3.2
), we can write

[\square_{g},X]\psi=-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}(f\partial_{r}\psi)\Big)+f\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi)\Big)+[\overline{\Delta},X]\psi.

Computing the final term, we have

\displaystyle[\overline{\Delta},X]\psi

\displaystyle=\phi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}(f\partial_{r}\psi)-f\partial_{r}(\phi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(f\partial_{r}\psi)-f\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi\Big)

\displaystyle=-f\partial_{r}(\phi^{2})\partial_{t}^{2}\psi-f\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{2f}{r}\overline{\Delta}\psi+2\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\phi^{4}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi,

where we used in the last line the fact that

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(\phi^{2})

\displaystyle=-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)\phi^{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{r}\phi^{2}-2\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{3}}\Big)\phi^{4}.

Substituting (
3.2
), it follows that

[\square_{g},X]\psi=-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}(f\partial_{r}\psi)\Big)+f\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi)\Big)\\
+\frac{2f}{r^{3}}\partial_{r}\Big(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi\Big)+\frac{2f}{r}\square_{g}\psi+2\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\phi^{4}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi

As for the radial derivatives, we have

-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}(f\partial_{r}\psi)\Big)+f\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi)\Big)+\frac{2f}{r^{3}}\partial_{r}\Big(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi\Big)\\
=\Big[\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r}f+\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})f-2\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}f\Big]\partial_{r}^{2}\psi\\
+\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}(\phi^{-2})f+\frac{4\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})}{r}f+\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r^{2}}f\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad-\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})\partial_{r}f-\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r}\partial_{r}f-\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}f\Big]\partial_{r}\psi.

By using that

\partial_{r}^{2}\psi=\frac{1}{f}\partial_{r}(X\psi)-\frac{\partial_{r}f}{f}\partial_{r}\psi,

it follows that

\square_{g}(f\partial_{r}\psi)=f\partial_{r}(\square_{g}\psi)+\frac{2f}{r}\square_{g}\psi+2\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\phi^{4}\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\\
+\Big[\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r}+\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})-2\phi^{-2}\frac{\partial_{r}f}{f}\Big]\partial_{r}(f\partial_{r}\psi)\\
+\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}(\phi^{-2})f+\frac{2\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})}{r}f+\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r^{2}}f\\
\quad\quad\quad-2\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})\partial_{r}f-\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r}\partial_{r}f-2\phi^{-2}\frac{(\partial_{r}f)^{2}}{f}-\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}f\Big]\partial_{r}\psi.\\

A brief computation shows that the coefficients of the radial derivatives are given by

\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{f^{2}}\Big)=\Big[\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r}+\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})-2\phi^{-2}\frac{\partial_{r}f}{f}\Big].

and

\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\Big(\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{f}\Big)=\Big[\partial_{r}^{2}(\phi^{-2})f+\frac{2\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})}{r}f+\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r^{2}}f-2\partial_{r}(\phi^{-2})\partial_{r}f\\
-\frac{2\phi^{-2}}{r}\partial_{r}f-2\phi^{-2}\frac{(\partial_{r}f)^{2}}{f}-\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}f\Big].

i.e. they match those in (
3.2
).
∎

We now focus on the
\partial_{r}\psi

4.4

for which the coefficient of the
\partial_{r}\psi

f(r)=\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{c_{1}+c_{2}r},

for parameters
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{(0,0)\}
. Consequently we define the vector fields

X_{s}=r\phi^{-2},\quad X_{w}=r^{2}\phi^{-2},

which correspond with choosing
(c_{1},c_{2})=(0,1)
and
(c_{1},c_{2})=(1,0)
respectively. Throughout this section, we will frequently be considering only the leading-order behaviour of terms that appear in the energy estimates, and so we remind the reader that
\phi\sim r^{-1}
for large

, and therefore
X_{s}\sim r^{3}\partial_{r}
,
X_{w}\sim r^{4}\partial_{r}
. The subscripts of
X_{s},X_{w}
refer to the fact that
X_{s}
will be used to prove a sharp energy estimate, while
X_{w}
will be used to prove an auxillary estimate that yields weaker decay of certain second derivatives present in both energy fluxes, but is nevertheless necessary to prove the sharp estimate.

(4.5)

Proof.

We will denote the energy flux of
\psi

(4.6)

for all
r_{2}>r_{1}>r_{\mathcal{C}}
. We focus on the bulk term. With the choice

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}\Big(\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{f^{2}}\Big)=-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big).

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\Big(\frac{r^{2}\phi^{-2}}{f}\Big)=0.

\displaystyle 2f\phi^{4}\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{2}}\Big)=2\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big).

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\phi\>\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)\square_{g}(X_{s}\psi)=-\phi\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}+2\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi).

(4.7)

Moreover, from (
4.3
) we have

\phi\>K^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]=\phi\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{m}{r}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}+\phi^{5}\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{m}{r}\Big)(\partial_{t}(X\psi))^{2}.

(4.8)

We point out that to leading order in
r
, the
(\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}
term on the right hand side of (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) cancel, and there is no term that depends on
\partial_{r}\psi
. Summing these yields
\phi\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[\psi]
, so that

\phi\>\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]=-\phi\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\\
+\phi^{5}\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{t}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}+2\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi).

(4.9)

We now wish to bound each term on the right hand side of (
4.9
) by the energies
J^{\partial_{r}}[\psi]\cdot n
and
J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n
. For reference, the energy flux density through
\Sigma_{r}
with respect to the vector field
\partial/\partial_{r}
of an arbitrary function
\varphi
is

J^{\partial_{r}}[\varphi]\cdot n=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\phi^{-1}(\partial_{r}\varphi)^{2}+\phi^{3}(\partial_{t}\varphi)^{2}+\phi|\not{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}\Big).

Therefore we may bound simply

\displaystyle\Big|\phi\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\Big|\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n.

\displaystyle\Big|\phi^{5}\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{t}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\Big|\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n.

The final term in (
4.9
) is more difficult to bound, as terms like
\partial_{t}^{2}\psi
are not controlled pointwise by the energy densities
J^{\partial_{r}}[\psi]
,
J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]
. An application of Cauchy-Schwartz implies

\Big|2\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\>\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)\Big|\leq O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n+O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\Big)(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}.

It follows that

\Big|\phi\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\Big|\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2},

and so by (
4.6
) we have

E[X_{s}\psi](r_{2})\leq E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[X_{s}\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r\\
+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\Big(O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big)\textnormal{d}r.

(4.10)

To control the integral of
(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}
, we employ the elliptic estimate of Corollary
A.2
from Appendix
A
, which implies

O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\overline{\Delta}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\partial_{i}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}.

(4.11)

Since
\psi
satisfies the wave equation, we have by (
3.2
) that

\overline{\Delta}\psi=\square_{g}\psi+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi),

and therefore

\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\overline{\Delta}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}=\frac{1}{r^{4}}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi))^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\leq O\Big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\Big)E[X_{w}\psi](r).

(4.12)

Note that we still cannot bound this solely by the higher-order energy flux
E[X_{s}\psi]
. Moreover, if we were to naively bound it by both
E[X_{s}\psi]
and the standard energy flux
E[\psi]
via the product rule, the resulting terms would possess weights in
r
that are too large to close the argument through a Grönwall-type inequality. Instead, this is where we employ a standalone, auxillary energy estimate that arises from the alternative choice of vectorfield commutator
X_{w}
. We claim that for any solution
\psi
to the wave equation on
\mathcal{R}^{+}
, the following estimate holds for all
r_{2}>r_{1}\geq r_{0}
:

\frac{1}{r_{2}^{4}}\int_{\Sigma_{r_{2}}}J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\frac{1}{r_{1}^{4}}\int_{\Sigma_{r_{1}}}J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}+\int_{\Sigma_{r_{1}}}J^{\partial_{r}}[\psi]\cdot n_{\Sigma_{r}}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}.

(4.13)

We will delay the proof of this estimate until later in this section. Combining the elliptic estimate (
4.11
), (
4.12
), as well as the auxillary energy estimate (
4.13
), we bound

\displaystyle O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}

\displaystyle\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{6}}\big)E[X_{w}](r)+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[\psi](r)

\displaystyle=O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)\Big[\frac{1}{r^{4}}E[X_{w}\psi](r)+E[\psi](r)\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)\Big[E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1})\Big],

and so by (
4.10
) we have

E[X_{s}\psi](r_{2})\lesssim E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[X_{s}\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r\\
+\Big(\frac{1}{r_{1}^{4}}E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1})\Big)\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\textnormal{d}r.

As
r^{-2}
is integrable, it follows that

E[X_{s}\psi](r_{2})\lesssim E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[X_{s}\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r.

An application of the standard Grönwall inequality yields

\displaystyle E[X_{s}\psi](r_{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{r_{0}}\Big[E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1})\Big]\exp\Big[C\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\textnormal{d}r\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1})

for all
r_{2}\geq r_{1}
.
By the product rule we may simply bound the energy fluxes through
\Sigma_{r_{1}}
like

E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})\lesssim_{r_{0}}E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1}),

which implies (
4.5
).
∎

Remark 4.3
.

To highlight the associated weights in
r
for terms that appear in the energy estimate (
4.5
), we note that for large
r
we have

\int_{\Sigma_{r}}J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{4}(\partial_{r}(r\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}\psi))^{2}+r^{6}(\partial_{t}\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}+r^{8}|\not{\nabla}\partial_{r}\psi|^{2}\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}\mu_{\mathring{\gamma}}.

(4.14)

As was shown in
[
27
]
, solutions to the wave equation on the expanding region of Schwarzschild-de Sitter are bounded with respect to the energy (
1.2
). The boundedness of the weighted higher-order energy already eliminates the possibility of an
O(r^{-1})
-weighted term being present in the asymptotic expansion of solutions, as we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{r}}J^{\partial_{r}}\Big[X_{s}\Big(\psi_{0}+\frac{\psi_{1}}{r}\Big)\Big]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{r}}J^{\partial_{r}}\Big[X_{s}\Big(\frac{\psi_{1}}{r}\Big)\Big]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}r^{6}\psi_{1}^{2}+r^{2}|\tilde{\nabla}\psi_{1}|^{2}\textnormal{d}t\textnormal{d}\mu_{\mathring{\gamma}},

Therefore if a solution
\psi
was like
\psi\sim\psi_{0}+\psi_{1}/r
for large

, this higher-order energy would not be bounded.

Proof of the auxillary energy estimate (

The proof of (

\phi\>\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi)\square_{g}(X_{w}\psi)=-\phi\>\Big(\frac{4\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2}{r}+\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi))^{2}+2\phi^{3}(r-3m)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi),

(4.15)

and summing this with
\phi K^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]
gives by (

\phi\>\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]=-\phi\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{m}{r^{2}}\Big)(\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi))^{2}+\phi^{2}\Big(\frac{\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{m}{r}\Big)(\partial_{t}(X_{w}\psi))^{2}\\
+2\phi^{3}(r-3m)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\>\partial_{r}(X_{w}\psi).

In contrast to (

\Big|\phi\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]\Big|\leq\frac{4}{r}J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]\cdot n+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{w}\psi]\cdot n+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2},

and so by (
4.6
) we have

E[X_{w}\psi](r_{2})\leq E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\Big[\frac{4}{r}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)\Big]E[X_{w}\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r\\
+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\textnormal{d}r.

An application of the elliptic estimate from Corollary
A.2
along with (
4.12
) yields

O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\lesssim O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big)E[X_{w}\psi](r)+O\big(1)E[\psi](r),

and therefore there exists a constant
C>0
such that

E[X_{w}\psi](r_{2})\leq E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+C\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}E[\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\beta(r)E[X_{w}\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r,

where

\beta(r)=\frac{4}{r}+\frac{C}{r^{2}}

for some large constant
C
. We then use the fact from
[
27
]
that the natural energy
E[\psi]
is monotonically decreasing in
r
to bound

\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}E[\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r\leq r_{2}E[\psi](r_{1}).

If we set
\alpha(r)=E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+CrE[\psi](r_{1})
, then we have for all
r_{2}\geq r_{1}
that

E[X_{w}\psi](r_{2})\leq\alpha(r_{2})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\beta(r)E[X_{w}\psi](r)\textnormal{d}r,

which is a Grönwall-type inequality, and implies

E[X_{w}\psi](r_{2})\leq\alpha(r_{2})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\alpha(s)\beta(s)\exp\Big[\int_{s}^{r_{2}}\beta(r)\textnormal{d}r\Big]\textnormal{d}s.

Finally we bound

\exp\Big[\int_{s}^{r_{2}}\frac{4}{r}+\frac{C}{r^{2}}\Big]\textnormal{d}r=\exp\Big[4\log(r_{2})-4\log(s)\Big]\exp\Big[\int_{s}^{r_{2}}\frac{C}{r^{2}}\textnormal{d}r\Big]\lesssim\frac{r_{2}^{4}}{s^{4}},

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\alpha(s)\beta(s)\exp\Big[\int_{s}^{r_{2}}\beta(r)\textnormal{d}r\Big]\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{2}^{4}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})}{s^{5}}+\frac{E[\psi](r_{1})}{s^{4}}\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim r_{2}^{4}\Big[E[X_{w}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1})\Big].

This implies (
4.13
) after dividing through by
r_{2}^{4}
.
∎

Remark 4.5

4.3
Second-order energy estimate

We now prove the second-order estimate of Theorem
1.14
. This estimate follows from a second commutation with the vectorfield of the form
Y=r\phi^{-1}\partial_{r}
, after already commuting once with
X_{s}
. The commutator
Y
once again creates cancellations in various terms that are produced when computing
\square_{g}(Y(X_{s}\psi))
.

Proof of Theorem
1.14
.

We begin with the inequality

E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r_{2})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\phi\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big)\textnormal{d}r\leq E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r_{1}).

(4.17)

By Lemma
4.1
, for some scalar function
\varphi
we have

\square_{g}(Y\varphi)=\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}(r\phi^{-1})\partial_{r}\varphi+2\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\varphi+2\phi^{-1}\square_{g}\varphi+Y(\square_{g}\varphi).

Setting
\varphi=X_{s}\psi
, it follows that

\square_{g}(Y(X_{s}\psi))=\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}(r\phi^{-1})(X_{s}\psi)+2\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)\\
+2\phi^{-1}\Big[-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)+2\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\Big]\\
+Y\Big[-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)+2\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\Big],

(4.18)

where we used the commutation identity (
4.7
) for the commutator
X_{s}
. We expand the final term giving

Y\Big[-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)+2\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\Big]\\
=-\phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r^{2}-\frac{2m}{r}\Big)\partial_{r}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)-\phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{4m}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)\\
+2\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)+2r\phi^{-1}\partial_{r}\Big[\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\Big]\partial_{t}^{2}\psi.

Since
Y=r\phi^{-1}\partial_{r}
, we have

\partial_{r}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)=\frac{\phi}{r}\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))-\frac{\phi}{r}\partial_{r}(r\phi^{-1})\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi).

It follows from (
4.18
) that

\square_{g}(Y(X_{s}\psi))=-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))+4\phi^{3}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)\\
+\Big[2r\phi^{-1}\partial_{r}\Big[\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\Big]+2\phi\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\Big]\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\\
+\Big[\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}(r\phi^{-1})+\partial_{r}(r\phi^{-1})\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)-\phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{6\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{8m}{r^{2}}\Big)\Big]\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi).

(4.19)

We now observe that the coefficient of the
\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)
in (
4.19
) vanishes to leading order, as

\displaystyle\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}^{2}(r\phi^{-1})=\frac{2}{r\phi^{3}}+O(1).

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(r\phi^{-1})\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)=\frac{4}{r\phi^{3}}+O(1).

\displaystyle-\phi^{-1}\Big(\frac{6\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{8m}{r^{2}}\Big)=-\frac{6}{r\phi^{3}}+O(1).

Therefore the coefficient of the
\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)
term is of order
O(1)
. This is also true for the coefficient of the
\partial_{t}^{2}\psi
term, as

\displaystyle 2r\phi^{-1}\partial_{r}\Big[\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\Big]=-2\phi+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle 2\phi\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)=2\phi+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big),

and so the coefficient of the
\partial_{t}^{2}\psi
term is of order
O(r^{-2})
. If we keep track of the leading-order behaviour of the coefficients of the other terms as well, we get

\phi\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\square_{g}(Y(X_{s}\psi))=\\
-\phi\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)[\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}+O\big(\frac{1}{r}\big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)\\
+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\Big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{t}^{2}\psi.

(4.20)

We restate that

\phi K^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]=\phi\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{m}{r}\Big)[\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}+\phi^{5}\Big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{3m}{r^{2}}\Big)[\partial_{t}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2},

and point out that the coefficients of the
[\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}
terms found above and in (
4.20
) cancel to leading order. This means that essentially all terms now become lower-order error terms that we may control with lower-order energy estimates, elliptic estimates and a Grönwall-type inequality, similar to in Theorem
4.2
. Thus we have

\phi\nabla\cdot J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]=O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)[\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{6}}\big)[\partial_{t}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}\\
+O\big(\frac{1}{r}\big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))\\
+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{t}^{2}\psi.

(4.21)

We now bound all terms on the right hand side of (
4.21
) pointwise by energy energy currents, if possible. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}[\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\cdot n.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{6}}[\partial_{t}(Y(X_{s}\psi))]^{2}\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\cdot n.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\Big|\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))\Big|\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\cdot n+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{4}}\Big|\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi)\Big|\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\cdot n+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{7}}\big)(\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{3}}\Big|\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\Big|\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\cdot n+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}.

It follows from (
4.17
) and (
4.21
) that

E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r_{2})\leq E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r)\textnormal{d}r\\
+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[X_{s}\psi](r)\Big]\textnormal{d}r+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{7}}\big)\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big]\textnormal{d}r\\
+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{5}}\big)\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big]\textnormal{d}r.

(4.22)

The final term can be controlled by
O(1/r^{2})[E[X_{s}\psi](r)+E[\psi](r)]
via an elliptic estimate like in Theorem
4.2
. For the second-to-last term, another elliptic estimate gives

\frac{1}{r^{7}}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{t}^{2}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\big)\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\overline{\Delta}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{7}}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}|\partial_{i}(X_{s}\psi)|^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}.

The final term can be controlled by
O(r^{-4})E[X_{s}\psi](r)
. Moreover, observe that

\overline{\Delta}(X_{s}\psi)=\square_{g}(X_{s}\psi)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}(r^{2}\phi^{-2}\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi))\\
=-\Big(\frac{2\Lambda}{3}r-\frac{2m}{r^{2}}\Big)\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)+2\phi^{2}\Big(1-\frac{3m}{r}\Big)\partial_{t}^{2}\psi\\
+\frac{1}{r\phi}\partial_{r}(Y(X_{s}\psi))+\frac{1}{r\phi}\partial_{r}(r\phi^{-1})\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi),

and therefore

\frac{1}{r^{3}}(\overline{\Delta}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[X_{s}\psi]\cdot n+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{7}}\big)(\partial_{t}^{2}\psi)^{2}+O\big(\frac{1}{r^{4}}\big)J^{\partial_{r}}[Y(X_{s}\psi)]\cdot n.

We estimate each of these terms as before, giving

\frac{1}{r^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\overline{\Delta}(X_{s}\psi))^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\leq O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)\Big\{E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r)+E[X_{s}\psi](r)+E[\psi](r)\Big\}.

Therefore

E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r_{2})\leq E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r_{1})+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)E[Y(X_{s}\psi)](r)\textnormal{d}r\\
+\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}O\big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big)\Big\{E[X_{s}\psi](r)+E[\psi](r)\Big\}\textnormal{d}r.

(4.23)

An application of Theorem
4.2
implies

\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big\{E[X_{s}\psi](r)+E[\psi](r)\Big\}\textnormal{d}r\lesssim E[X_{s}\psi](r_{1})+E[\psi](r_{1}).

The result now follows by a Grönwall inequality, and the fact that the factor
r^{-2}
is integrable.
∎

4.4
Pointwise decay estimates

For sufficiently regular solutions to
\square_{g}\psi=0
, the energy estimates from Theorem
4.2
implies pointwise decay of higher derivatives of
\psi
. We return to the argument of Section
3.4
that takes advantage of the Killing vectorfields of Schwarzschild-de Sitter, as well as the Sobolev estimate from Appendix
B
. To this end, recall the notation that given a multiindex
I=(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4})
,
Z^{I}
denotes the differential operator

Z^{I}=\Omega_{1}^{I_{1}}\Omega_{2}^{I_{2}}\Omega_{3}^{I_{3}}T^{I_{4}},

where
T,\Omega_{i}:i=1,2,3
are Killing vector fields that span the tangent space of the
\Sigma_{r}
hypersurfaces.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\psi
be a solution to the linear wave equation
\square_{g}\psi=0
, with finite higher-order energy on an initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{r_{0}}
,

D[\psi](r_{0})=\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\Big\{E[Y(X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi))](r_{0})+E[X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)](r_{0})+E[Z^{I}\psi](r_{0})\Big\}<\infty.

Then we have the following pointwise bounds for large
r
:

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\partial_{r}\psi|\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{3}}(D[\psi](r_{0}))^{1/2}.

(4.24)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\tilde{\nabla}(X_{s}\psi)|\lesssim(D[\psi](r_{0}))^{1/2}.

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\partial_{r}(X_{s}\psi)|\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}(D[\psi](r_{0}))^{1/2}.

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\tilde{\nabla}(Y(X_{s}\psi))|\lesssim(D[\psi](r_{0}))^{1/2}.

(4.27)

Proof.

Using the Sobolev estimate from Proposition
B.1
, we have

\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|X_{s}\psi|\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{3/2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\|X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})},

keeping in mind that the differential operators
Z^{I}
commute with
X_{s}
. An application of the fundamental theorem of calculus on
[r_{0},r]
then implies

\frac{1}{r^{3/2}}\|X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}\lesssim\|X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r_{0}})}+\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{1}{r^{3/2}}\|\partial_{r}(X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi))\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}\textnormal{d}r.

We control the last term with the higher-order energy estimate from Theorem
4.5
. Since
Z^{I}
is composed of Killing vector fields,
Z^{I}\psi
satisfies the homogeneous wave equation, and so by (
4.5
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{3/2}}\|\partial_{r}(X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi))\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(E[X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)](r)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(E[X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)](r_{0})+E[Z^{I}\psi](r_{0})\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(D[\psi](r_{0})\Big)^{1/2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|X_{s}\psi|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\Big\{\|X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r_{0}})}+\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big(D[\psi](r_{0})\Big)^{1/2}\textnormal{d}r\Big\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(D[\psi](r_{0})\Big)^{1/2}.

Since
X_{s}\sim r^{3}\partial_{r}
, we divide the above inequality through by
r^{3}
and obtain (
4.24
). As for (
4.25
), we use the Sobolev estimate again and control the resulting
L^{2}
-norm by the higher-order energy, so that

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\tilde{\nabla}(X_{s}\psi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{3}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\|\tilde{\nabla}(X_{s}\psi)\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{r})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 2}E[X_{s}(Z^{I}\psi)](r),

and finally appeal to the higher-order estimate from Theorem
4.2
. The decay estimates (
4.26
) and (
4.27
) follow in the same manner from the second-order estimate of Theorem
1.14
.
∎

4.5
Concluding the proof of Theorem
1.1

We conclude this section by proving the existence of the limits

\psi_{0}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\psi,\quad\psi_{2}=-2\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}[r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi],\quad\psi_{3}=3\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}[r^{2}\partial_{r}(r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi)]

(4.28)

with respect to the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm on the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
, which we recall is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
. This is equivalent to proving that the quantities
\psi,X_{s}\psi,Y(X_{s}\psi)
all have a limit on
\Sigma^{+}
. The particular constants in front of the limits for
\psi_{2}
,
\psi_{3}
are just normalisation factors that account for repeated differentiation in
r
. We note that the energy controls the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm on the
\Sigma_{r}
hypersurfaces, meaning there exists a constant
C(\Lambda,m,r_{0})>0
independent of
r
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi(r)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}^{2}\leq CE[\psi](r),

for all
r\geq r_{0}
. To prove the existence of the limits (
4.28
), we appeal to a density argument. Let
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of smooth, compactly supported solutions to the linear wave equation that converge to
\psi
, so that for some
r_{0}>r_{\mathcal{C}}
we have

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\Big[\|\psi-\varphi_{j}\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r_{0}}}+\|X_{s}(\psi-\varphi_{j})\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r_{0}}}+\|Y(X_{s}(\psi-\varphi_{j}))\|_{\partial_{r},\Sigma_{r_{0}}}\Big]=0.

This can be accomplished by taking a sequence of smooth compactly supported data on
\Sigma_{r_{0}}
that converge to the data of
\psi
on
\Sigma_{r_{0}}
, and then considering the corresponding solution to the wave equation. By commuting the energy estimate from Theorem
1.14
with Killing vectorfields, one can then show that for each
j
, the functions
\varphi_{j},X_{s}\varphi_{j},Y(X_{s}\psi)
each have a pointwise limit on
\Sigma^{+}
. This pointwise limit extends to a limit with respect to the
\dot{H}^{1}
-norm via the dominated convergence theorem. Finally, it follows from the energy estimate of Theorem
4.2
that
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\psi
,
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}X_{s}\psi
, and
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}Y(X_{s}\psi)
all exist with respect to the homogeneous Sobolev norm
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
.

We finally prove the existence of the limits (
1.4
) by showing that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\|r^{2}(\psi-\psi_{0})-\psi_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0.

(4.29)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\Big\|r^{3}\Big(\psi-\psi_{0}-\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}\Big)-\psi_{3}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}=0.

(4.30)

To prove (
4.29
), we know that
\|\psi-\psi_{0}-r^{-2}\psi_{r}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow\infty
, and so by the fundamental theorem of calculus we have

\Big\|\psi-\psi_{0}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\psi_{2}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\int_{r}^{\infty}\Big\|\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{2}{r^{3}}\psi_{2}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\textnormal{d}r,

noting that
\partial_{r}(\psi-\psi_{0}-r^{-2}\psi_{2})=\partial_{r}\psi+2r^{-3}\psi_{2}
. We bound

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\Big\|\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{2}{r^{3}}\psi_{2}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\textnormal{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{3}}\sup_{[r,\infty)}\|r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi+2\psi_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\textnormal{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\sup_{[r,\infty)}\|r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi+2\psi_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

This implies (
4.29
), as

\|r^{2}(\psi-\psi_{0})-\psi_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{[r,\infty)}\|r^{3}\partial_{r}\psi+2\psi_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})}.

The right hand side vanishes as
r\rightarrow\infty
, hence (
4.29
) holds. The limit (
4.30
) follows in a similar manner.

5
Scattering on general expanding spacetimes

We will now generalise the scattering result
Theorem
1.4
to the linear wave equation on a wide class of expanding spacetimes. No symmetries are required on the expanding region, and the geometry at infinity of these spacetimes can differ significantly from Schwarzschild-de Sitter. In particular the expanding region of Kerr-de Sitter is contained in the class of spacetimes we consider, so we prove existence and uniqueness of scattering solutions to the linear wave equation on Kerr-de Sitter.

Remark 5.1
.

Wave equations on another class of perturbations of Schwarzschild-de Sitter were also considered in
[
27
]
, and the redshift estimate (
1.16
) for the forward problem was shown to generalise to those perturbations. We note the perturbed spacetimes considered in
[
27
]
are in a more restricted class compared to those that we consider in this paper, as their metric components are assumed to converge at infinity to their counterparts on Schwarzschild-de Sitter.

For a given spacetime, we will construct asymptotic solutions to the wave equation which are specialised to that spacetime. We note that for the class of perturbations of Schwarzschild-de Sitter we consider, logarithmic terms are present in the asymptotic solution, see Remark
3.5
. However we will show that for a restriction of the perturbed class that includes Kerr-de Sitter, logarithmic terms are not present.

We will then prove a weighted energy estimate suitable for the backward problem, extending Proposition
1.8
to this large class of spacetimes. The existence of scattering solutions from Theorem
1.20
then follows by repeating the argument from Section
3.3
, but in a perturbed setting.

We define the following class of spacetimes:

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\Sigma^{+}
denote a 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold, equipped with a Riemannian
C^{3}
-metric
h
, and fix
\Lambda>0
. Let
M
denote the differentiable manifold
M=(0,\rho_{0})\times\Sigma^{+}
for some
\rho_{0}>0
, and let
g
be a Lorentzian
C^{3}
-metric defined on
M
.

We say
(M,g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
if the conformal metric
\tilde{g}=\rho^{2}g
admits a
C^{3}
-extension across
\{\rho=0\}\times\Sigma^{+}
of the form

\tilde{g}=-\frac{3}{\Lambda}\textnormal{d}\rho^{2}+h+O(\rho).

(5.1)

Moreover, we say
(\mathcal{M},g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
if the conformal metric
\tilde{g}
is like

\tilde{g}=-\frac{3}{\Lambda}\textnormal{d}\rho^{2}+h+O(\rho^{2}).

(5.2)

Remark 5.3
.

This class of spacetimes is very similar those considered in
[
30
]
, where in particular smooth scattering solutions are constructed from infinity. The primary difference here is that we do not assume smoothness of the metric, but instead
C^{3}
-regularity. This class is also interesting because of its relation to the scattering problem for Einstein’s vacuum equations with positive cosmological constant, see for example
[
15
]
.

Remark 5.4
.

A straightforward computation shows that de Sitter, Schwarzschild-de Sitter and Kerr-de Sitter spacetimes are all members of
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
. For de Sitter and Schwarzschild-de Sitter, one can see this by taking the metric in standard coordinates on the expanding region (for example take (
2.2
) for Schwarzschild-de Sitter), and introducing the coordinate
\rho=r^{-1}
. For Kerr-de Sitter this can be seen by considering the metric in
Boyer-Lindquist
coordinates on the expanding region, and again introducing the coordinate
\rho=r^{-1}
.

It is simpler for us to do the computations in this section with respect to the conformal metric
\tilde{g}
rather than
g
. For the rest of this section, we assume that unless explicitly stated otherwise, all quantities are defined in terms of
\tilde{g}
and not the original metric.

First, we note that the homogeneous wave equation
\square_{g}\psi=0
is equivalent to the following wave equation on the conformal metric
\tilde{g}
:

\square_{\tilde{g}}\psi=\frac{2}{\rho}\tilde{g}^{\rho\nu}\partial_{\nu}\psi,

(5.3)

where a subscript or superscript
\rho
is to be treated as the component in terms of the
\rho
-coordinate and not an abstract index.
If we set
\phi=(-\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho})^{-1/2}
, then the future-pointing timelike unit normal
n
of the level sets of constant
\rho
is given by

n^{\mu}=\phi\tilde{g}^{\rho\mu}

(5.4)

and so we may write

\square_{\tilde{g}}\psi=\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\psi.

(5.5)

Note that for the conformal metric neither the unit normal
n
nor the lapse
\phi
possess weights in
\rho
, and both are uniformly continuously differentiable up to
\Sigma^{+}
.

5.1
Constructing asymptotic solutions

In this section we construct asymptotic solutions to the wave equation for metrics in
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
,
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
. While these constructions are similar to their counterparts on Schwarzschild-de Sitter, in the perturbed setting we include logarithmic terms in the asymptotic solution. The wave equation (
5.5
) can be written as

\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)\psi=\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi+2\tilde{g}^{\rho i}\partial_{\rho}\partial_{i}\psi+\tilde{g}^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\psi\\
-\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\psi-\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}\partial_{i}\psi-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\partial_{\rho}\psi-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}\tilde{g}^{\rho i}\partial_{i}\psi.

(5.6)

If the metric belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
, then a short computation using (
5.1
) implies that the asymptotics of the various coefficients that appear above are

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}+O(\rho),\quad\tilde{g}^{\rho i}=O(\rho),\quad\tilde{g}^{ij}=h^{ij}+O(\rho),

(5.7)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}=O(1),\quad\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}=f_{\Sigma^{+}}^{i}+O(\rho),

(5.8)

where the
f_{\Sigma^{+}}^{i}
are
C^{2}
-functions on
\Sigma^{+}
. If the metric also belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
, then by (
5.2
) the asymptotics are

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}=-\frac{\Lambda}{3}+O(\rho^{2}),\quad\tilde{g}^{\rho i}=O(\rho^{2}),\quad\tilde{g}^{ij}=h^{ij}+O(\rho^{2}).

(5.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}=O(\rho),\quad\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{i}=f_{\Sigma^{+}}^{i}+O(\rho^{2}).

(5.10)

Lemma 5.5
.

Fix a spacetime
(\mathcal{M},g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
, and let
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
be sufficiently regular functions on
\Sigma^{+}
. Then define the asymptotic solution

\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\psi_{0}+\rho^{2}\psi_{2}+\rho^{3}\log\rho\>\psi_{3,1}+\rho^{3}\psi_{3},

where
\psi_{2}
,
\psi_{3,1}
are functions determined entirely by
\psi_{0}
and derivatives thereof. Then there exists an integer
k\geq 0
such that

\Big|\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\psi_{\text{asymp}})\Big|\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}}\rho^{2}\log\rho\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}|\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}^{\alpha}\psi_{0}|+\rho^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}|\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}^{\alpha}\psi_{3}|,

(5.11)

where
\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}
is the covariant derivative restricted to
\Sigma^{+}
. If additionally
(\mathcal{M},g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
, then the
\psi_{3,1}
term vanishes identically, and we have

\Big|\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\psi_{\text{asymp}})\Big|\lesssim\rho^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}|\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}^{\alpha}\psi_{0}|+\rho^{3}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}|\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}^{\alpha}\psi_{3}|

(5.12)

Proof.

We first assume just
(\mathcal{M},g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
. Then we compute term-by-term. By (
5.6
), (
5.7
), and (
5.8
) we have

\displaystyle\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\psi_{0})=h^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\psi_{0}-f_{\Sigma^{+}}^{i}\partial_{i}\psi+O(\rho),

\displaystyle\quad\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\rho^{2}\psi_{2})=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\psi_{2}+O(\rho).

For the remaining terms, we note that in (
5.6
) the slowest-decaying terms are
\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\partial_{\rho}^{2}\psi
and
\frac{2}{\rho\phi}\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}
. But for
\rho^{3}\log\rho\>\psi_{3,1}
,
\rho^{3}\psi_{3}
these terms cancel to leading order, giving

\displaystyle\Big(\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\partial_{\rho}^{2}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\Big)(\rho^{3}\log\rho\>\psi_{3,1})=\Lambda\rho+O(\rho^{2}\log\rho),

\displaystyle\Big(\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\partial_{\rho}^{2}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}\tilde{g}^{\rho\rho}\Big)(\rho^{3}\psi_{3})=O(\rho^{2}).

Hence we may freely choose
\psi_{0}
,
\psi_{3}
, and we set if we set the terms in

\square_{\tilde{g}}\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\psi_{\text{asymp}}

of order
O(1)
and
O(\rho)
equal to zero, then
\psi_{2}
,
\psi_{3,1}
are determined entirely by
\psi_{0}
and its derivatives, which implies (
5.11
).

Moreover if we assume
(\mathcal{M},g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
, then by (
5.6
), (
5.9
), and (
5.10
) we have instead for
\psi_{0}
,
\psi_{2}
,

\displaystyle\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\psi_{0})=h^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\psi_{0}-f_{\Sigma^{+}}^{i}\partial_{i}\psi+O(\rho^{2}),

\displaystyle\quad\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\rho^{2}\psi_{2})=\frac{2\Lambda}{3}\psi_{2}+O(\rho^{2}).

Hence setting the order
O(\rho)
term in
\square_{\tilde{g}}\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\psi_{\text{asymp}}
to be identically zero implies that
\psi_{3,1}
= 0, and so (
5.12
) holds.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

We compare the Lemma above to the construction of asymptotic solutions on Schwarzschild-de Sitter in Section
3.1
. In the notation used in this section, Lemma
3.4
and (
3.9
) the approximate solution on Schwarzschild-de Sitter is

\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\psi_{0}+\rho^{2}\psi_{2}+\rho^{3}\psi_{3},

and
\psi_{\text{asymp}}
obeys the bound

\Big|\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\psi_{\text{asymp}})\Big|\lesssim\rho^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 4}|\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}^{\alpha}\psi_{0}|+\rho^{3}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}|\nabla_{\Sigma^{+}}^{\alpha}\psi_{3}|,

which is essentially (
5.12
). Also note that while logarithmic terms may be present in asymptotic solutions in the perturbed setting, the term
\rho\>\psi_{1}
cannot be present, even for metrics in
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
. This is because

\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)(\rho\>\psi_{1})=O\big(\frac{1}{\rho}\Big),

so any asymptotic solution containing
\rho\psi_{1}
would only satisfy (
5.5
) up to order
\rho^{-1}
.

Remark 5.7
.

As previously stated, Kerr-de Sitter spacetimes are in the class
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{2}
. Thus by the previous Lemma, asymptotic solutions to the linear wave equation on Kerr-de Sitter are of the form

\psi_{\text{asymp}}=\psi_{0}+\frac{\psi_{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\psi_{3}}{r^{3}},

where
\psi_{2}
is determined by
\psi_{0}
.

5.2
Energy estimate for the backward problem on expanding spacetimes

We now prove a weighted backward estimate in the perturbed setting, analogous to Proposition
1.8
. We will apply this to solutions to the inhomogeneous wave equation

\square_{\tilde{g}}\psi-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\psi=F.

(5.13)

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
M=\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}n
, and suppose
\psi
satisfy the inhomogeneous wave equation (
5.13
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

K^{M}[\psi]\leq CJ^{M}[\psi]\cdot n.

on all of
\tilde{M}
. Furthermore, there exists a (different) constant
B>0
such that for all
\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\in(0,\rho_{0})
with
\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
, we have

\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{2}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\leq B\Big[\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{1}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}+\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}F^{2}\Big)\textnormal{d}\rho\Big],

(5.14)

where
\Sigma_{\rho}
denotes the level sets of
\rho
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
.

Remark 5.9
.

We point out that for
\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
, the hypersurface
\Sigma_{\rho_{1}}
lies to the
future
of
\Sigma_{\rho_{2}}
, and the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
corresponds to
\{\rho=0\}
. So this is a backwards-in-time estimate just like Proposition
1.8
.

Proof.

Recall the definition (
5.4
) of the timelike unit normal
n
. As
M
is a multiple of
n
, by the product rule we have

\>{}^{(M)}\pi^{\mu\nu}=n^{(\mu}\tilde{g}^{\nu)\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}(\rho^{-4})+\rho^{-2}\>{}^{(n)}\pi^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{4\rho^{-5}}{\phi}n^{\mu}n^{\nu}+\rho^{-4}\>{}^{(n)}\pi^{\mu\nu},

which implies

K^{M}[\psi]=-\frac{4}{\rho\phi}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n+\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}K^{n}[\psi].

Writing the current explicitly we may bound

J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n=\frac{1}{2}(M\psi)(n\psi)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\phi^{2}}{\rho^{4}}|\overline{\nabla}\psi|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}(M\psi)(n\psi),

and so

K^{M}[\psi]\leq-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}(M\psi)(n\psi)+\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}K^{n}[\psi].

We then claim that the following bound holds on all of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
:

\phi|K^{n}[\psi]|\leq CJ^{n}[\psi]\cdot n

(5.15)

The pointwise version of this bound is always true, but the bound being uniform follows from the fact that the components of
\tilde{g}
and the function
\phi
are continuously differentiable, moreover their derivatives are uniformly bounded across
\{\rho=0\}
.
It follows from (
5.15
) that for the divergence of
J^{M}[\psi]
we have

\displaystyle\phi\nabla\cdot J^{M}[\psi]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2}{\rho}(M\psi)(n\psi)+\frac{\phi}{\rho^{4}}K^{n}[\psi]+\phi(M\psi)\Big(\frac{2}{n\phi}\psi+F\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\phi}{\rho^{4}}CJ^{n}[\psi]\cdot n+\phi(M\psi)F\sim CJ^{M}[\psi]\cdot n+(M\psi)F.

We apply the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the
(M\psi)F
term, so that

(M\psi)F\leq\frac{\rho^{4}}{2}(M\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\rho^{4}}F^{2}\lesssim CJ^{M}[\psi]\cdot n+\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}F^{2}

which yields the bound

\phi\nabla\cdot J^{M}[\psi]\lesssim CJ^{M}[\psi]\cdot n+\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}F^{2}.

(5.16)

We apply the divergence theorem to the energy current
J^{M}[\psi]
on the region bounded in the past by
\Sigma_{\rho_{2}}
and to the future by
\Sigma_{\rho_{1}}
for
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<P
. We denote this region by
\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}
. This gives

\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{2}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}=\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{1}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}+\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}}\nabla\cdot J^{M}[\psi]\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\tilde{g}}=,

where
\gamma_{\rho}
is the Riemannian metric on
\Sigma_{\rho}
induced from the conformal metric
\tilde{g}
. The particular signs of each integral are due to the fact that
n\sim-\partial_{\rho}
, i.e. the normal vectorfield is pointing in the direction of
decreasing

\rho
. We assert that
|\tilde{g}|^{1/2}=\phi|\gamma_{\rho}|^{1/2}
, which implies that the volume form
\textnormal{d}\mu_{\tilde{g}}
satisfies

\textnormal{d}\mu_{\tilde{g}}=\phi\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\wedge\textnormal{d}\rho,

and so by the coarea formula we have

\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{2}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}=\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{1}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}+\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}\phi\nabla\cdot J^{M}[\psi]\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\Big)\textnormal{d}\rho.

(5.17)

Inserting (
5.16
) then gives

\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{2}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\lesssim\int_{\Sigma_{\rho_{1}}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}+\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}F^{2}\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\Big)\>\textnormal{d}\rho\\
+C\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\Big)\>\textnormal{d}\rho.

The result now follows through a Grönwall-type inequality.
∎

5.3
Existence and uniqueness of scattering solutions on expanding spacetimes

We now state the full version of Theorem
1.20
, proving existence and uniqueness of scattering solutions to the linear wave equation in the class of expanding spacetimes
\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
. Recall from (
2.10
) the notation for the energy norm

\|\psi\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}=\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}J^{M}[\psi]\cdot n\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}

Theorem 5.10
.

Fix
\Lambda>0
, and let
(\mathcal{M},g)\in\mathcal{G}_{\Lambda}^{1}
be an expanding spacetime with future boundary
\Sigma^{+}
. Suppose
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}\in H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})
for some
k
sufficiently large. Then there exists a unique solution to the linear wave equation
\square_{g}\psi=0
such that for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, we have

\|\psi\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{M}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}.

(5.18)

Proof.

Given
\psi_{0},\psi_{3}
, let
\psi_{\text{asymp}}
be the asymptotic solution constructed in Lemma
5.5
. Let
\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}
be a solution to the inhomogeneous wave equation

\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}=-\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)\psi_{\text{asymp}},

with trivial data on
\Sigma_{P}
:

\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}|_{\Sigma_{P}}=n\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}|_{\Sigma_{P}}=0.

Then taking the difference
v=\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P_{1})}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P_{2})}
for
0<P_{1}<P_{2}
, one can repeat the proof of existence of scattering solutions from Section
3.3
. In particular, we show that for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
,
v\rightarrow 0
as
P_{1},P_{2}\rightarrow 0
, where the limit of
v
is taken with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}
. Here we use the bound (
5.11
) for the asymptotic solution, and the weighted estimate of Proposition
5.8
. The key inequality we reach is (with a constant independent of
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
):

\|v\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}^{2}\leq C\int_{P_{1}}^{P_{2}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}\Big[\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)\psi_{\text{asymp}}\Big]^{2}\>\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\Big)\>\textnormal{d}\rho,

for all
\rho>P_{2}
. We compare this to the corresponding bound (
3.24
) in Section
3.3
. By the pointwise estimate (
5.11
), we have the
L^{2}
-bound

\int_{P_{1}}^{P_{2}}\int_{\Sigma_{\rho}}\frac{1}{\rho^{4}}\Big[\Big(\square_{\tilde{g}}-\frac{2}{\rho\phi}n\Big)\psi_{\text{asymp}}\Big]^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\gamma_{\rho}}\textnormal{d}\rho\lesssim\int_{P_{1}}^{P_{2}}(\log\rho)^{2}(\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}^{2}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}^{2})\>\textnormal{d}\rho.

Since
(\log\rho)^{2}
is integrable up to
\rho=0
, the right hand side above vanishes as
P_{1},P_{2}\rightarrow 0
, and so
\lim_{P_{1},P_{2}\rightarrow 0}\|v\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}=0
. Hence there exists
\psi
such that for each
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, we have

\lim_{P\rightarrow\infty}\|\psi-\psi_{\text{asymp}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}=0.

The bound (
5.18
) from bounding

\|\psi\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}\leq\|\psi-\psi_{\text{asymp}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}+\|\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}+\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}.

By (
5.11
) we estimate
\|\psi_{\text{asymp}}\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}
. To control
\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}
, we apply Proposition
5.8
to
\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}
on the spacetime slab between
\Sigma_{P}
and
\Sigma_{\rho}
, giving

\|\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}^{2}\lesssim\rho^{2}(\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}^{2}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}^{2})\int_{P}^{\rho}(\log s)^{2}\>\textnormal{d}s\lesssim\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}^{2}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}^{2},

where we used the fact that that
\|\cdot\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}\sim\rho^{2}\|\cdot\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}
. This implies

\|\psi\|_{n,\Sigma_{\rho}}\leq\|\psi-\psi_{\text{asymp}}-\psi_{\text{rem}}^{(P)}\|_{M,\Sigma_{\rho}}+\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}+\|\psi_{3}\|_{H^{k}(\Sigma^{+})}.

Taking the limit
P\rightarrow 0
, the result now follows.
∎

Appendix A
Elliptic Estimates

In this appendix we will state an elliptic estimate which we use to prove the higher-order energy estimates in Section
4
. Given a 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma,\overline{g})
, we denote the metric connection by
\overline{\nabla}
, and the Laplacian
\overline{\nabla}^{i}\overline{\nabla}_{i}=\overline{\Delta}
. We also write

|\overline{\nabla}^{2}\psi|^{2}=\overline{g}^{ij}\overline{g}^{kl}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\overline{\nabla}_{k}\psi\overline{\nabla}_{j}\overline{\nabla}_{l}\psi.

We recall the following result from Section 4 of
[
3
]
; see Remark 4 therein.

Proposition A.1

(Elliptic estimate from
[
3
]
)
.

Let
\psi
be a scalar function on the 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\Sigma,\overline{g})
. Then there exists some positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\Sigma}|\overline{\nabla}^{2}\psi|^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\Sigma}(\overline{\Delta}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}}+\int_{\Sigma}|\emph{Ric}[\overline{g}]^{ij}\overline{\nabla}_{i}\psi\overline{\nabla}_{j}\psi|\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}}\Big).

(A.1)

As a consequence, if we consider the spacelike hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{r}
on Schwarzschild-de Sitter, the Ricci curvature of the induced metric
\overline{g}_{r}
obeys the bound

|\text{Ric}[\overline{g}_{r}]^{ij}|\sim\frac{1}{r^{4}}.

for sufficiently large
r
. Moreover, one may bound

\displaystyle|\overline{\nabla}^{2}\psi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\overline{g}_{r}^{ii}\overline{g}_{r}^{jj}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\psi)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\overline{g}_{r}^{ii})^{2}(\partial_{i}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{r^{4}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{4}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\partial_{i}\psi)^{2}.

(A.2)

We note that the final term in (
A.2
) can be controlled by the integrand of the final term in (
A.1
), which allows us to absorb this term into the right hand side of (
A.1
). Thus Proposition (
A.1
) implies the following Corollary:

Corollary A.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda,m},g)
be a subextremal member of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter family of spacetimes, and fix
r_{0}\geq r_{\mathcal{C}}
, and let
\psi
be a scalar field on the expanding region

, we have the following elliptic estimate:

\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}C\Big(r^{4}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\overline{\Delta}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}(\partial_{i}\psi)^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big).

(A.3)

Appendix B
Sobolev Estimates

In this appendix we prove a Sobolev estimates that we use to derive pointwise estimates of solutions to the wave equation on Schwarzschild-de Sitter.

Proposition B.1

(Sobolev Estimate for Schwarzschild-de Sitter)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda,m},g)
be a subextremal member of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter family of spacetimes, and fix
r_{0}\geq r_{\mathcal{C}}
. Recall the family of Killing vectors
T
,
\Omega_{i}:i=1,2,3
that span the level sets
\Sigma_{r}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Lambda,m)>0
such that for all
r\geq r_{0}
, we have

\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\varphi|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{r^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\Big\{\varphi^{2}+(T\varphi)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Omega_{i}\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Omega_{i}T\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}|\Omega_{i}\Omega_{j}\psi|^{2}\Big\}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}.

(B.1)

Proof.

It was shown in
[
29
]
for a class of spacetimes that includes Schwarzschild-de Sitter, we have for any tensor field
\theta
that

\sup_{t}\Big(\int_{S_{t}}r^{4}|\theta|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\not{g}}\Big)^{1/4}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\Big(\int_{\Sigma_{r}}|\theta|^{2}+r^{2}|\overline{\nabla}\theta|^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}\Big)^{1/2},

where
S_{t}
is the sphere on
\Sigma_{r}
on which the coordinate
t
is constant, and
\textnormal{d}\mu_{\not{g}}
is the induced volume form on
S_{t}
.
Moreover, in
[
28
]
it was shown that

\sup_{S_{t}}|\varphi|^{2}\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\frac{1}{r}\Big(\int_{S_{t}}\varphi^{4}+r^{4}|\not{\nabla}\varphi|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\not{g}}\Big)^{1/2}.

Combining these two estimates gives

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma_{r}}|\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r}\sup_{t}\Big(\int_{S_{t}}\varphi^{4}+r^{4}|\not{\nabla}\varphi|^{4}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\not{g}}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{r^{3}}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}\varphi^{2}+r^{2}|\overline{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}+r^{4}|\overline{\nabla}\not{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}\textnormal{d}\mu_{\overline{g}_{r}}.

We can bound the covariant derivative restricted to the sphere and the cylinder with spheres of radius
r
like

\displaystyle r^{2}|\overline{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}|T\varphi|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Omega_{i}\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle r^{2}|\not{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Lambda,m,r_{0}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}|\Omega_{i}\varphi|^{2},

and therefore (
B.1
) holds.
∎

Abstract
. We study the insulated conductivity problem with closely spaced insulators embedded in a homogeneous matrix where the current-electric field relation is the power law J = | E | p − 2 ​ E J=|E|^{p-2}E . The gradient of solutions may blow up as ε \varepsilon , the distance between insulators, approaches to 0. We prove an upper bound of the gradient to be of order ε − α \varepsilon^{-\alpha} , where α = 1 / 2 \alpha=1/2 when p ∈ ( 1 , n + 1 ] p\in(1,n+1] and any α > n / ( 2 ​ ( p − 1 ) ) \alpha>n/(2(p-1)) when p > n + 1 p>n+1 . We provide examples to show that this exponent is almost optimal in 2D. Additionally in dimensions n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , for any p > 1 p>1 , we prove another upper bound of order ε − 1 / 2 + β \varepsilon^{-1/2+\beta} for some β > 0 \beta>0 , and show that β ↗ 1 / 2 \beta\nearrow 1/2 as n → ∞ n\to\infty .

1.
Introduction and Main results

We investigate the phenomenon of electric field concentration in high-contrast composites. Such a phenomenon can occur when two approaching inclusions possess material properties that differ significantly from the background matrix (see e.g.
[
18
,
24
,
7
]
). The study of this area originated from
[
3
]
, where the problem with inclusions closely located in a linear background medium was studied numerically. In this paper, we study the scenario in which the inclusions are insulators, and the background matrix follows the current-electric field relation described by the power law:

J=\sigma|E|^{p-2}E,\quad p>1,

(1.1)

where
J
,
E
, and
\sigma
denote current, electric field, and conductivity, respectively. Physically, such power law can occur in various materials, including dielectrics, plastic moulding, plasticity phenomena, viscous flows in glaciology, electro-rheological and thermo-rheological fluids. See the second paragraph of
[
6
]
and the references therein.

Let us describe the mathematical setup: let
n\geq 2
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary containing two
C^{1,1}
open sets
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
with dist
(\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2},\partial\Omega)>c>0
. Let

\varepsilon:=\mbox{dist}(\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}),

\widetilde{\Omega}:=\Omega\setminus\overline{(\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2})}
, and
\sigma=\mathbbm{1}_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
. The voltage potential
u
satisfies the following
p
-Laplace equation with
p>1
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\operatorname{div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)&=0\quad\mbox{in }\widetilde{\Omega},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0\quad\mbox{on}~\partial\mathcal{D}_{i},~i=1,2,\\
u&=\varphi\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

where
\varphi\in{C}^{1,1}(\partial\Omega)
is given, and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})
denotes the inner normal vector on
\partial\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\partial\mathcal{D}_{2}
.

Our goal is to quantitatively analyze the concentration of the electric field
E=-Du
between the inclusions, and this is a challenging problem even in the linear case when
p=2
. While the optimal blow-up rate for the linear case in two dimensions was captured about two decades ago in
[
2
,
1
]
, the optimal rate in dimensions
n\geq 3
was only recently identified in
[
11
,
12
]
. This optimal rate is linked to the first non-zero eigenvalue of an elliptic operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
, which is determined by the principal curvatures of the inclusions. This phenomenon is completely different from the perfect conductivity problem, where the optimal blow-up rates do not depend on the curvatures of the inclusions (see
[
2
,
1
,
4
]
). For other earlier work on the linear insulated conductivity problem, we refer the reader to
[
5
,
20
,
21
,
28
,
27
]
.
In the case when the current-electric field relation is given by (
1.1
), Gorb and Novikov in
[
16
]
and Ciraolo and Sciammetta in
[
9
]
studied the field concentration when
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are perfect conductors. They proved that for
n\geq 2
,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C\varepsilon^{-\frac{n-1}{2(p-1)}}&&p>\frac{n+1}{2},\\
&C\varepsilon^{-1}|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}&&p=\frac{n+1}{2},\\
&C\varepsilon^{-1}&&p<\frac{n+1}{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

These bounds were shown to be optimal in their respective papers. However, the phenomenon of electric field concentration between insulators has not been studied before.

3.
Improved gradient estimates

In this section, we utilize a similar approach of flattening the boundaries and extending the equation, as described in
[

]

) in dimensions

To prove Theorem

, for

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\operatorname{div}\Big((\eta+|Du_{\eta}|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}Du_{\eta}\Big)&=0\quad\mbox{in }\widetilde{\Omega},\\

\displaystyle(p-1)|\xi|^{2}\leq a^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad

\displaystyle|\xi|^{2}\leq a^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq(p-1)|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad

Localization of the free energy in supergravity
Pietro Benetti Genolini 1, Jerome P. Gauntlett 2, Yusheng Jiao 2, Alice Lüscher 3, James Sparks 3

Abstract
We derive a general formula for the gravitational free energy of Euclidean supersymmetric solutions to D = 4 D=4 , 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 gauged supergravity coupled to vector multiplet matter. This allows one to compute the free energy without solving any supergravity equations, just assuming the solutions exist. As well as recovering some known results in the literature with ease, we also present new supergravity results that match with holographically dual field theory computations.

at the fixed point, with
\partial_{\varphi_{i}}
rotating each factor. The case
b_{1}=0
is then a bolt, while
b_{1}\neq 0
is a nut.
For a fixed bolt, from (
15
)
we have

y^{I}_{\pm}=\pm{\kappa}\mskip 1.0muu^{I}_{\pm}
. For a fixed nut
(
14
) implies

\displaystyle y^{I}_{\pm}=\pm{\kappa}\mskip 1.0mu(1\mp\omega)u^{I}_{\pm}\,,\quad\mbox{where}\ \omega\equiv\frac{b_{1}}{b_{2}}\,.

(20)

Notice this expression is valid both when the origin of the

\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a nut
±
or a bolt
±
.
Substituting into
(
19
) then gives a “UV-IR relation”, valid for any
\xi
-invariant submanifold
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that
has a UV boundary
S^{1}_{\mathrm{UV}}
.
A corollary is that for all such
S^{1}_{\mathrm{UV}}

the UV data is constrained via

\displaystyle\zeta_{I}(\Delta^{I}+\mathrm{i}\beta\sigma^{I})=\zeta_{I}y_{\pm}^{I}=\pm{\kappa}\mskip 1.0mu{(1\mp\omega)}\,.

(21)

Additional constraints can also be obtained from the magnetic fluxes, as we illustrate in the examples below.

V
Examples

We can immediately
recover the result for minimal supergravity

[
11
]
from (
1
).
It is also
straightforward to recover other results
in the literature, and also obtain new formulae. We illustrate with
the STU model, for which

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(X^{I})=-2\mathrm{i}\sqrt{X^{0}X^{1}X^{2}X^{3}}\,,

(22)

with
\zeta_{I}=1
for all
I=0,1,2,3
.
Solutions to this theory uplift on
S^{7}
to solutions
of
D=11
supergravity
[
21
]
and in our conventions

\frac{\pi}{2G_{4}}=F^{\mathrm{ABJM}}_{S^{3}}
is the free energy of the dual ABJM theory on
S^{3}
in the large
N
limit.

V.1
Deformations of Euclidean
AdS_{4}

Let
M_{4}
topologically be
\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{R}_{1}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}_{2}^{2}
, with conformal
boundary
\partial M_{4}=M_{3}\cong S^{3}
.
We take

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{2}b_{i}\partial_{\varphi_{i}}
where
\partial_{\varphi_{i}}
rotate
each
\mathbb{R}_{i}^{2}
. We do not make any further assumptions on the metric on

M_{4}
or the boundary
S^{3}
.
For
b_{i}\neq 0
there is an isolated fixed point at the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
and from (
1
) we may write

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}=\mp\frac{4(b_{1}\mp b_{2})^{2}}{b_{1}b_{2}}\sqrt{u^{0}_{\pm}u^{1}_{\pm}u^{2}_{\pm}u^{3}_{\pm}}F^{\mathrm{ABJM}}_{S^{3}}\,.

(23)

Here the
\pm
signs are correlated with the chirality of the spinor at the origin,
and from (
2
) we have the constraint
\sum_{I=0}^{3}u^{I}_{\pm}=1
.

This incorporates two distinct solutions in the literature:
(i) taking
S^{3}
to have a round metric and setting
b_{1}=\mp b_{2}
gives the
SO(4)
-symmetric solutions
of
[
15
]
(for real
z^{i},\widetilde{z}^{\tilde{i}}
), where the two choices of sign
were referred to as different “branches” of solution. For
b_{1}=\mp b_{2}

the R-symmetry is embedded in
U(1)_{\mp}\subset SU(2)_{\mp}
, where

SU(2)_{+}\times SU(2)_{-}
is the double cover of the
SO(4)

isometry.
In this case
\Delta^{I}=0
, and the UV-IR relation (
19
)
relates
u^{I}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\sigma^{I}
, where
\sigma^{I}
are defined on
the
S^{3}
UV boundary. This case is dual to ABJM theory on
S^{3}
with real mass
deformations, and matches the large
N
limit of the partition function
[
22
]
.
The
AdS_{4}
vacuum is recovered by setting
u^{I}_{\pm}=\tfrac{1}{4}
for all
I
, which also extremizes
(
23
) as a (constrained) function of
u^{I}_{\pm}
; (ii)
instead setting
u^{I}_{\pm}=\tfrac{1}{4}
for all
I
but keeping
b_{i}
general, the
U(1)^{2}
-invariant metric on
S^{3}
is now
squashed, reproducing the minimal supergravity solutions of

[
23
,
24
]
.

The supergravity solutions with
M_{4}\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}
with generic
b_{i}
and
u^{I}_{\pm}
have not been
constructed, but assuming they exist (
23
) gives their free energy.
Generically they will have
U(1)^{2}
isometry, and thus be cohomogeneity two, with a squashed metric on the boundary
S^{3}
.
The result in (
23
) is in precise agreement with the large
N
result in
[
25
]
.

V.2
Black saddle
solutions

Euclidean, dyonically charged, “black saddle” solutions of the STU model were
analysed in
[
26
]
, with topology
M_{4}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma_{g}
, where

\Sigma_{g}
is a genus
g
Riemann surface.
The special subclass of solutions with
\widetilde{X}^{I}=\overline{X}^{I}
can be Wick rotated
to Lorentzian signature extremal black holes with horizon
\Sigma_{g}
.
The R-symmetry Killing vector rotates the Euclidean time circle, which
is the polar direction in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and thus the horizon
is a fixed bolt.
The normal bundle is trivial, so
c_{1}(L)=0
and
from (
1
) (with
{\kappa}=1
) we have

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}=-2\sqrt{u^{0}_{\pm}u^{1}_{\pm}u^{2}_{\pm}u^{3}_{\pm}}\sum_{I=0}^{3}\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{I}}{u^{I}_{\pm}}F^{\mathrm{ABJM}}_{S^{3}}\,,

(24)

where

\mathfrak{p}^{I}=\int_{\Sigma_{g}}F^{I}/4\pi
as in (
16
), which by (
3
) are constrained to satisfy
\sum_{I=0}^{3}\mathfrak{p}^{I}=-2(1-g)
. The
scalars
u^{I}_{\pm}
are necessarily constant on the horizon
\Sigma_{g}
,
and are related to the UV variables
\Delta^{I}+\mathrm{i}\beta\sigma^{I}
on the conformal
boundary
M_{3}=S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g}

via the general UV-IR relation (
19
), (
20
), thus proving
the conjecture of
[
26
]
(who verified this relation
for a subset of analytic and numerical solutions).
The result (
24
) agrees with a large
N
limit
of the topologically twisted index of the ABJM theory
on
M_{3}=S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g}

[
26
]
(for
g\neq 1
).

V.3
Taub-bolt saddle solutions

One can generalize the black saddle construction of
[
26
]
by considering
the boundary
M_{3}
to have the
S^{1}
fibred over
\Sigma_{g}
,
with Chern number
-p\in\mathbb{Z}
. The large
N
limit
of the partition function of the ABJM theory on this space was computed in
[
27
]
.
A natural candidate gravity dual has
M_{4}=\mathcal{O}(-p)\rightarrow\Sigma_{g}
, the total space of the associated
complex line bundle over
\Sigma_{g}
. Such solutions
to the Euclidean STU theory are not known, although
there are explicit solutions in minimal supergravity
constructed in
[
28
,
27
]
.
Assuming these solutions exist,
they have a bolt with non-trivial normal bundle and

\int_{\Sigma_{g}}c_{1}(L)=-p
. Thus, from (
1
) (with
{\kappa}=1
) we have

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}=-2\sqrt{u^{0}_{\pm}u^{1}_{\pm}u^{2}_{\pm}u^{3}_{\pm}}\Big(\pm 2p+\sum_{I=0}^{3}\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{I}_{\pm}}{u^{I}_{\pm}}\Big)F^{\mathrm{ABJM}}_{S^{3}}\,,

(25)

with
\sum_{I=0}^{3}\mathfrak{p}^{I}_{\pm}=\mp p-2(1-g)
from (
3
) and
with the same UV-IR relation as in the previous subsection.
Remarkably, (
25
) is in
precise agreement with the
large
N
field
theory result of
[
27
]
.

V.4
Spindle solutions

Now consider solutions with topology
M_{4}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\Sigma
,
where
\Sigma\cong\mathbb{WCP}^{1}_{[n_{N},n_{S}]}
is a
spindle. The latter is topologically a
two-sphere, but with conical deficit angles
2\pi(1-1/n_{N,S})

at the north and south poles,
respectively.
These correspond to dyonic black saddle solutions with acceleration
and rotation;
such solutions in minimal gauged supergravity
have been constructed
[
29
]
.

We parametrize

\xi=\varepsilon\mskip 1.0mu\partial_{\varphi_{1}}+b\mskip 1.0mu\partial_{\varphi_{2}}
,
where
\partial_{\varphi_{1}}
,
\partial_{\varphi_{2}}
rotate
the spindle and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
factors, respectively,
and
\Delta\varphi_{1}=2\pi=\Delta\varphi_{2}
.
We choose sign conventions so that
the fixed north pole
N
has negative chirality,
while the south pole
S
has chirality
-\sigma=\mp 1
,
which are the twist and anti-twist of
[
18
]
.
The weights at the poles are
(\varepsilon_{N}/n_{N},b_{N})\equiv(-\varepsilon/n_{N},1)
and
(\varepsilon_{S}/n_{S},b_{S})\equiv(\varepsilon/n_{S},1)
.
Applying (
1
) with
d_{N}=n_{N}
,
d_{S}=n_{S}
immediately
gives
a “gravitational block”
[
30
]
type of expression:

\displaystyle F_{\mathrm{grav}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\Big[\sqrt{y_{N}^{0}y_{N}^{1}y_{N}^{2}y_{N}^{3}}-\sigma\sqrt{y_{S}^{0}y_{S}^{1}y_{S}^{2}y_{S}^{3}}\mskip 3.0mu\Big]F^{\mathrm{ABJM}}_{S^{3}}\,,

(26)

where the
y^{I}_{N,S}
variables satisfy the constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{I=0}^{3}y^{I}_{N}=\frac{\varepsilon}{n_{N}}-1\,,\quad\sum_{I=0}^{3}y^{I}_{S}=-\sigma\frac{\varepsilon}{n_{S}}-1\,,

(27)

where we have taken
{\kappa}_{N}=1
,
{\kappa}_{S}=\sigma
.
The fluxes can be obtained by localizing
\Phi^{I}_{(F)}
, leading to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}^{I}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\left(\left.\Phi_{0}^{I}\right|_{N}-\left.\Phi_{0}^{I}\right|_{S}\right)=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(y_{N}^{I}-y_{S}^{I})\,,

(28)

which implies
\sum_{I=0}^{3}\mathfrak{p}^{I}=-\frac{n_{S}+\sigma n_{N}}{n_{N}n_{S}}
.
These results agree with
[
5
]
after
identifying
y^{I}_{N,S}=-x^{I}_{\pm}/2
,
\varepsilon=b_{0}
.

VI
Discussion

Our derivation of (
1
) assumed solutions with real fields, but we expect this
can be relaxed. For example, our results are in agreement with the solutions in
[
15
]
both for real and complex scalars.
Furthermore, the on-shell action that we obtained for putative Euclidean spindle solutions also gives rise to the on-shell action
for a complex locus of accelerating black hole solutions, and hence the entropy (after a Legendre transform)
[
31
,
32
]
.

Other generalizations include adding hypermultiplets, which for some models will be associated with a Higgs mechanism in the bulk,
leading to additional constraints on the parameters
u_{\pm}^{I}
, similar to
[
5
]
, and generalizing
to
D=5
. We also anticipate that we can incorporate higher derivatives,
and this will provide a promising way to prove and extend the conjectures discussed in
[
33
,
34
]

as well as make contact with
1/N
corrections to field theory results as in e.g.
[
25
]
.

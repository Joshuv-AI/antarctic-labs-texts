Abstract
. A real valued function f f defined on a real open interval I I is called Φ \Phi -convex if, for all x , y ∈ I x,y\in I , t ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] t\in[0,1] it satisfies f ⁡ ( t ​ x + ( 1 − t ) ​ y ) ≤ t ​ f ​ ( x ) + ( 1 − t ) ​ f ​ ( y ) + t ​ Φ ​ ( ( 1 − t ) ​ | x − y | ) + ( 1 − t ) ​ Φ ​ ( t ​ | x − y | ) , \displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)+t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big(t|x-y|\big), where Φ : ℝ + → ℝ + \Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+} is a nonnegative error function. If f f and − f -f are simultaneously Φ \Phi -convex, then f f is said to be a Φ \Phi -affine function. In the main results of the paper, we describe the structural and inclusion properties of these two classes. We characterize these two classes of functions and investigate their relationship with approximately monotone and approximately-Hölder functions. The properties of this subclass of error function are investigated as well. Then we offer two formulas for the lower Φ \Phi -convex envelop. Besides, a sandwich type theorem is also added.

In these papers several aspects of approximate convexity were investigated: stability problems, Bernstein–Doetsch-type theorems, Hermite–Hadamard type inequalities, etc. The notions and results have various applications in nonsmooth and convex analysis and optimization theory, and also in the theory of functional equations and inequalities. Assume that
I
is a nonempty interval and a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following inequality

(1)

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)+\varepsilon\big(t\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)^{p}+(1-t)\big(t|x-y|\big)^{p}\big),

\displaystyle\qquad(x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1])

for some nonnegative constant
\varepsilon
and real constant
p\in\mathbb{R}
. In other words,
f
is approximately convex with an error term described in terms of the
p

\varepsilon=0
, then the above condition is equivalent to the convexity of
f
. One can easily notice that every convex function
f
satisfies (
1
). On the other hand, if
p=0
, then
f
satisfies (
1
) if and only if it can be decomposed as
f=g+h
, where
g
is convex and
||h||_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon/2
. In the case
p=1

[
37

show that
f
fulfills (
1
) if and only if
f=g+\ell
, where
g
is convex and
\ell
is Lipschitzian with a Lipschitz modulus
c\varepsilon
. Surprisingly, for
p>2
, the situation is completely different. Then (
1
) holds if and only if
f
is convex.

The above described observations and results motivate the investigation of classes of functions that obey a more general approximate convexity and also the related approximately affine property. In fact, the class of
\Phi
-convex functions was introduced in the paper
[
28

2.
Basic Results

Let
I
be a nonempty open real interval throughout this paper and let
\ell(I)\in\,]0,\infty]
denote its length. The symbols
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
denote the sets of real and nonnegative real numbers, respectively.

The class of all functions
\Phi:[0,\ell(I)[\,\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, called error functions, will be denoted by
{\mathscr{E}}(I)
. Obviously,
{\mathscr{E}}(I)
is a convex cone, i.e., it is closed with respect to addition and multiplication by nonnegative scalars. In what follows, we are going to define four properties related to an error function
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
. First we recall the notions of
\Phi
-monotone and
\Phi
-Hölder functions that have been introduced in our former papers
[
10
,
11
]
.

A function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
will be called
\Phi
-monotone
if, for all
x,y\in I
with
x\leq y
,

(2)

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(y)+\Phi(y-x).

If this inequality is satisfied with the identically zero error function
\Phi
, then we say that
f
is
monotone (increasing)
.
The class of
\Phi
-monotone functions on
I
will be denoted by
\mathscr{M}_{\Phi}(I)
.

A function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
will be called
\Phi
-Hölder
if, for all
x,y\in I
,

(3)

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\Phi(|x-y|).

The class of
\Phi
-Hölder functions on
I
will be denoted by
\mathscr{H}_{\Phi}(I)
.

The following concept was introduced in the paper
[
28
]
. We say that a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is
\Phi
-convex
if, for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
,

(4)

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)+t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big(t|x-y|\big).

If the above inequality is satisfied with the identically zero error function
\Phi
, then we say that
f
is
convex
.
The class of
\Phi
-convex functions on
I
will be denoted by
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)
.

Finally, a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
\Phi
-affine
if, for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
,

(5)

\displaystyle|f(tx+(1-t)y)-tf(x)-(1-t)f(y)|\leq t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big(t|x-y|\big).

If, in particular, the above inequality is satisfied with the identically zero error function
\Phi
, then we say that
f
is
affine
.
The class of
\Phi
-affine functions on
I
will be denoted by
\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{n}\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{1}}(I)+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{n}}(I)\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\alpha_{1}\Phi_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\Phi_{n}}(I).

In particular, for all functions
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
, the class
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)
is convex.

Proof.

To prove the inclusion of the proposition, let
f\in\alpha_{1}\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{1}}(I)+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{n}}(I)
. Then,
f
is of the form

(6)

\displaystyle f=\alpha_{1}f_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}f_{n}

for some elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
belonging to
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{1}}(I),\dots,\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{n}}(I)
, respectively. Then, for all
x,y\in I
,
t\in[0,1]
, and
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf_{i}(x)+(1-t)f_{i}(y)+t\Phi_{i}\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi_{i}\big((t|x-y|\big).

Multiplying this inequality by
\alpha_{i}
and summing up side by side, we will arrive at the inequality (
4
), where
\Phi:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\Phi_{i}
.
This proves that
f\in\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)
and completes the proof of the second inclusion.
∎

The following result is the counterpart of the previous statement.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{n}\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\mathscr{A}_{\Phi_{1}}(I)+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\mathscr{A}_{\Phi_{n}}(I)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{|\alpha_{1}|\Phi_{1}+\cdots+|\alpha_{n}|\Phi_{n}}(I).

In particular, for all functions
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
, the class
\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)
is convex and central symmetric, i.e.,
\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)
is closed with respect to multiplication by
(-1)
.

Proof.

To prove the inclusion of the proposition, let
f\in\alpha_{1}\mathscr{A}_{\Phi_{1}}(I)+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\mathscr{A}_{\Phi_{n}}(I)
. Then,
f
is of the form (
6
) for some elements
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
belonging to
\mathscr{A}_{\Phi_{1}}(I),\dots,\mathscr{A}_{\Phi_{n}}(I)
, respectively. Then, for all
x,y\in I
,
t\in[0,1]
, and
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{i}(tx+(1-t)y)-tf_{i}(x)-(1-t)f_{i}(y)|\leq t\Phi_{i}\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi_{i}\big((t|x-y|\big).

Multiplying this inequality by
|\alpha_{i}|
and summing up side by side, we will arrive at the inequality

\displaystyle|f(tx+(1-t)y)-tf(x)-(1-t)f(y)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\big(f_{i}(tx+(1-t)y)-tf_{i}(x)-(1-t)f_{i}(y)\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|\big|f_{i}(tx+(1-t)y)-tf_{i}(x)-(1-t)f_{i}(y)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|t\Phi_{i}\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi_{i}\big((t|x-y|\big)

\displaystyle=t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big((t|x-y|\big),

where
\Phi:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|\Phi_{i}
.
This proves that (
5
) holds, i.e.,
f\in\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)
and completes the proof of the second inclusion.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
. Then

(7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\Phi}(I)\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)=\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)\cap(-\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)).

Proof.

To prove the inclusion, let
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\Phi}(I)
. Let
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
.
By the
\Phi
-Hölder property of
f
, we have

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle\leq f(x)+\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big),

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle\leq f(y)+\Phi\big(t|x-y|\big).

Multiplying the above inequalities by
t
and
1-t
, then summing up the inequalities so obtained side by side, we get

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)+t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big((t|x-y|\big),

which shows that
f\in\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)
. Repeating the same argument with
(-f)
instead of
f
, it follows that
-f\in\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)
. Therefore,
f\in\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)\cap(-\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I))=\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)
.

To show the equality assertion, assume that
f
is a
\Phi
-affine function. Then, for any
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
,
f
will satisfy the inequality (
5
) and hence the inequalities

(8)

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq

\displaystyle tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)+t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big(t|x-y|\big).

\displaystyle\mbox{and}

\displaystyle tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)\leq

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)+t\Phi\big((1-t)|x-y|\big)+(1-t)\Phi\big(t|x-y|\big).

holds. Rearranging theses two inequalities, we have that both
f
and
-f
are
\Phi
-affine. That is

f\in\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)\cap(-\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)).

To show the inverse inclusion, let
f\in\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)\cap(-\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)).
Due to the property of
\Phi
-convexity of the two classes of function,
f
will satisfy the two inequalities in (
8
). Hence, inequality (
5
) holds for any
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
. This inequality being symmetric in
x
and
y
, we get that (
5
) is satisfied for all
x,y\in I
.
∎

Corollary 2.4

Let
\Phi,\Psi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\Phi}(I)+\mathscr{C}_{\Psi}(I)\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\Phi+\Psi}(I)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathscr{H}_{\Phi}(I)+\mathscr{A}_{\Psi}(I)\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{\Phi+\Psi}(I)

We say that a family
\mathscr{F}
of real valued functions is
closed with respect to the pointwise supremum
if
\{f_{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is a subfamily of

with a pointwise supremum

(9)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f_{\gamma}(x)\qquad(x\in I),

then

. Similarly, we can define that a family
\mathscr{F}
of real valued functions is
closed with respect to the pointwise infimum

\{f_{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is called a

if, for all

or
f_{\beta}\leq f_{\alpha}
holds on

. We say that a family
\mathscr{F}
of real valued functions is
closed with respect to the pointwise chain supremum (chain infimum)
if
\{f_{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}\mid\gamma\in\Gamma\}\subseteq\mathscr{F}
is a chain with a pointwise supremum (infimum)

, then
f\in\mathscr{F}
.

Let

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)

\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)

Assume that

\displaystyle f=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}g_{n},\qquad\mbox{where}\qquad g_{n}:=\sup_{k\geq n}f_{k}.

If all the functions

is
\Phi
-convex.
(Because these classes are closed with respect to the pointwise supremum.) On the other hand, the sequence

is decreasing, therefore

Proposition 2.7

\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
, let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\Phi
-convex function and

be an increasing and sublinear function. Then

is a
(g\circ\Phi)
-convex function.

Proof.

Using the
\Phi

\displaystyle(g\circ f)(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle\leq g\Big(t\big(f(x)+\Phi\big((1-t)|y-x|\big)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi\big(t|y-x|\big)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq t\big(g\circ f(x)+g\circ\Phi\big((1-t)|y-x|\big)\big)+(1-t)\big(g\circ f(y)+g\circ\Phi\big(t|y-x|\big)\big).

Therefore, we arrive at our desired conclusion.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent to each other:

(i)

f
is
\Phi
-convex;

(ii)

For all

with
x<u<y
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}\leq\dfrac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u};

(iii)

There exists a function
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
x,u\in I
,

(10)

\displaystyle f(u)+(x-u)\varphi(u)\leq f(x)+\Phi(|u-x|);

(iv)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in I,

t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\geq 0
with
t_{1}+\dots+t_{n}=1

\displaystyle f(t_{1}x_{1}+\dots+t_{n}x_{n})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\big(f(x_{i})+\Phi(|(t_{1}x_{1}+\dots+t_{n}x_{n})-x_{i}|)\big).

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Assume that
f
is
\Phi
convex and let
x<u<y
be arbitrary elements of
I
. Choose

such that
u=tx+(1-t)y
. That is let

\displaystyle f(u)\leq\dfrac{y-u}{y-x}f(x)+\dfrac{u-x}{y-x}f(y)+\dfrac{y-u}{y-x}\Phi(u-x)+\dfrac{u-x}{y-x}\Phi(y-u).

Therefore,

\displaystyle(y-u+u-x)f(u)\leq

\displaystyle(y-u)f(x)+(u-x)f(y)

\displaystyle+(y-u)\Phi(u-x)+(u-x)\Phi(y-u).

Rearranging this inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}.

(ii)
\Rightarrow

Assume that

\displaystyle\varphi(u):=\underset{x<u}{\mathrm{\sup}}\bigg(\dfrac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}\bigg)\qquad(u\in I).

In view of condition

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}\leq\varphi(u)\leq\dfrac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}.

From the left hand side inequality in (

\displaystyle f(u)+(x-u)\varphi(u)\leq f(x)+\Phi(u-x)\qquad(x\in I,\,x<u).

Similarly, from the right hand side inequality in (

\displaystyle f(u)+(x-u)\varphi(u)\leq f(x)+\Phi(x-u)\qquad(x\in I,\,u<x).

Now, combining inequalities (

).

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv):

To deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\big(f(x_{i})+\Phi(|u-x_{i}|)\big).

Using that

(iv)
\Rightarrow

To deduce the

-convexity of

Proof.

Assertions (i), (ii) and (iv) are equivalent by Theorem
3.1
.

Assume that (ii) holds and
\Phi
is increasing.
First we are going to show the following inequality:

(15)

\displaystyle\max\bigg(

\displaystyle\sup_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x},\sup_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)-\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\min\bigg(\inf_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)+\Phi(u-x)}{u-x},\inf_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}\bigg).

By condition (ii), for all
x,u,y\in I
with
x<u<y
, we have the following two inequalities

(16)

\displaystyle\dfrac{f(u)-f(x)}{u-x}-\dfrac{f(y)-f(u)}{y-u}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}+\dfrac{\Phi(y-u)}{y-u},

\displaystyle-\dfrac{f(u)-f(x)}{u-x}+\dfrac{f(y)-f(u)}{y-u}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}+\dfrac{\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}.

Then, one can easily see that the first and the second of the above inequalities
imply

\displaystyle\sup_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u},

\displaystyle\sup_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)-\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)+\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}

for all
u\in I
, respectively. In order to show that (
15
) holds, it remains to prove that

(17)

\displaystyle\sup_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)+\Phi(u-x)}{u-x},

\displaystyle\sup_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)-\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}.

For the first inequality, we need to prove that, for all
x_{1},x_{2}<u
,

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{f(u)-f(x_{1})-\Phi(u-x_{1})}{u-x_{1}}\leq\frac{f(u)-f(x_{1})+\Phi(u-x_{1})}{u-x_{1}}

If
x_{1}=x_{2}
, this follows from the nonnegativity of
\Phi
. We have to consider two cases according to the possibilites
x_{1}<x_{2}
or
x_{2}<x_{1}
. In the first case, using (
16
) for the triplet
x_{1}<x_{2}<u
, then the monotonicity of
\Phi
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{f(u)-f(x_{1})}{u-x_{1}}-\frac{f(u)-f(x_{2})}{u-x_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{u-x_{1}}\bigg(\frac{f(x_{2})-f(x_{1})}{x_{2}-x_{1}}-\frac{f(u)-f(x_{2})}{u-x_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{u-x_{1}}\bigg(\frac{\Phi(x_{2}-x_{1})}{x_{2}-x_{1}}+\frac{\Phi(u-x_{2})}{u-x_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\Phi(x_{2}-x_{1})}{u-x_{1}}+\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{u-x_{1}}\frac{\Phi(u-x_{2})}{u-x_{2}}\leq\frac{\Phi(u-x_{1})}{u-x_{1}}+\frac{\Phi(u-x_{2})}{u-x_{2}},

which shows (
18
) in the case
x_{1}<x_{2}
. In the case
x_{2}<x_{1}
, the proof is analogous, and hence the first inequality in (
17
) has been verified. The second inequality in (
17
) can be shown in a similar manner. Thus, inequality (
15
) has been proved, too. Define now the function
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\varphi(u):=\max\bigg(

\displaystyle\sup_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x},\sup_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)-\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}\bigg).

Then, for all
x<u<y
, we get

(19)

\displaystyle f(u)-f(x)-(u-x)\varphi(u)\leq\Phi(u-x),\qquad f(y)-f(u)+(u-y)\varphi(u)\leq\Phi(y-u)

Based on inequality (
15
), we also have

\displaystyle\varphi(u)\leq\min\bigg(\inf_{x<u}\frac{f(u)-f(x)+\Phi(u-x)}{u-x},\inf_{u<y}\frac{f(y)-f(u)+\Phi(y-u)}{y-u}\bigg),

which implies, for all
x<u<y
, that

(20)

\displaystyle f(x)-f(u)+(u-x)\varphi(u)\leq\Phi(u-x),\qquad f(u)-f(y)-(u-y)\varphi(u)\leq\Phi(y-u).

The first inequalities in (
19
) and (
20
) yield, for all
x<u
, that

\displaystyle|f(u)-f(x)-(u-x)\varphi(u)|\leq\Phi(u-x).

On the other hand, the second inequalities in (
19
) and (
20
) show, for all
u<y
, that

\displaystyle|f(u)-f(y)-(u-y)\varphi(u)|\leq\Phi(y-u).

Combining these two inequalities, we can see that assertion (iii) must be valid.

Conversely, if assertion (iii) holds, then
\varphi
is a
\Phi
-slope function for
f
, whence it follows that
f
is
\Phi
-convex. Similarly, (iii) also implies that
(-\varphi)
is a
\Phi
-slope function for
(-f)
, hence
(-f)
is also
\Phi
-convex. These two
\Phi
-convexity properties yield that
f
is
\Phi
-affine.
∎

Motivated by the condition
(iii)
of Theorem
3.2
, we say that
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
is an
absolute
\Phi
-slope function for
f
if it satisfies inequality (
14
) for all
x,u\in I
. Then Theorem
3.2
states that a function
f
is
\Phi
-affine (provided that
\Phi
is increasing) if and only if there exists an absolute
\Phi
-slope function for
f
.

The next two propositions state that the
\Phi
-slope function of a
\Phi
-convex (resp. absolute
\Phi
-slope function of a
\Phi
-affine) function is approximately monotone (resp. Hölder) with respect to a transformation
\Phi^{*}
of the error function
\Phi
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
,
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\Phi
-convex function and
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\Phi
-slope function for
f
. Then
\varphi
is
\Phi^{*}
-monotone, where

(21)

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(t):=\begin{cases}2\dfrac{\Phi(t)}{t}&\mbox{if }0<t<\ell(I),\\
0&\mbox{if }t=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\Phi
-slope function for
f
and let
x,y\in I
with
x<y
be arbitrary. By obvious substitution into the inequality (
10
), we get

\displaystyle f(x)+(y-x)\varphi(x)\leq f(y)+\Phi(y-x),

\displaystyle f(y)+(x-y)\varphi(y)\leq f(x)+\Phi(y-x).

Adding these inequalities side by side, the values
f(x)
and
f(y)
cancel out, hence we arrive at

\displaystyle(y-x)(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))\leq 2\Phi(y-x).

Dividing by
y-x
, this inequality implies that
\varphi
is
\Phi^{*}
-monotone, which was to be proved.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
be increasing,
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\Phi
-affine function and
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a
\Phi
-slope function for
f
. Then
\varphi
is
\Phi^{*}
-monotone, where
\Phi*
is defined by
(
21
)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be an absolute
\Phi
-slope function for
f
and let
x,y\in I
with
x\neq y
be arbitrary. By obvious substitution into the inequality (
14
), we get

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)-(x-y)\varphi(x)|\leq\Phi(|x-y|),

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(x)-(y-x)\varphi(y)|\leq\Phi(|y-x|).

Therefore, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle|(y-x)(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))|

\displaystyle\leq|f(x)-f(y)-(x-y)\varphi(x)|+|f(y)-f(x)-(y-x)\varphi(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(|y-x|)

Dividing by
|y-x|
, this inequality shows that
\varphi
is
\Phi^{*}
-Hölder, which was to be proved.
∎

4.
Optimal error functions

We say that an error function
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)

possesses the property
\Gamma
if it satisfies the inequality

(22)

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)\leq\Phi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Phi(y)\qquad(x\geq 0,\,y>0,\,x+y<\ell(I)).

The subclass of error functions in
{\mathscr{E}}(I)
with the property
\Gamma
will be denoted by
{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
. The subset of
{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
whose elements also satisfy
\Phi(0)=0
will be denoted by
{\mathscr{E}}_{0}^{\Gamma}(I)
.

One can easily see that any subadditive error function satisfies property
\Gamma
, however, as we will see later, the reversed implication is not true.

The next result establishes some necessary and some sufficient conditions for the
\Gamma
property.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
. Then
\sqrt{\Phi}
and the map
t\mapsto t^{-1}\Phi(t)
is subadditive on
\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
. If, in addition,
\varphi:[0,\ell(I)[\,\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is decreasing on
\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
, then
\varphi\cdot\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
. In particular, if
\Psi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
and
t\mapsto t^{-2}\Psi(t)
is deccreasing on
\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
, then
\Psi\in{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
.

Proof.

To prove the subadditivity of
\sqrt{\Phi}
on
\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
, let
x,y>0
with
x+y<\ell(I)
. We have

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)\leq\Phi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Phi(y)=\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)+2\frac{x}{y}\Phi(y).

and, interchanging the roles of
x
and
y
, we also have

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)\leq\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)+2\frac{y}{x}\Phi(x).

These two inequalities imply that

(23)

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)\leq\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)+2\min\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Phi(y),\frac{y}{x}\Phi(x)\Big).

By using that the geometric mean of two numbers exceeds their minimum, we get

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)\leq\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)+2\sqrt{\Phi(x)\Phi(y)}=\big(\sqrt{\Phi(x)}+\sqrt{\Phi(y)}\big)^{2}.

Taking square root of this inequality side by side, it follows that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Phi(x+y)}\leq\sqrt{\Phi(x)}+\sqrt{\Phi(y)},

which completes the proof of the subadditivity of
\sqrt{\Phi}
.

To show the subadditivity of
t\mapsto t^{-1}\Phi(t)
, we use that the minmimu of two numbers is smaller than their arithmetic mean. Thus, from (
23
) we arrive at

\displaystyle\Phi(x+y)\leq\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)+\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Phi(y)+\frac{y}{x}\Phi(x)\Big)=(x+y)\frac{\Phi(x)}{x}+(x+y)\frac{\Phi(y)}{y}.

Dividing both sides by
x+y
, we can reach at our desired conclusion.

To prove the next assertion, denote
\Psi:=\varphi\cdot\Psi
and let
x>0
,
y>0
with
x+y<\ell(I)
. Using the
\Gamma
property of
\Phi
and then the decreasingness of
\varphi
, we get

\displaystyle\Psi(x+y)=\varphi(x+y)\Phi(x+y)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x+y)\Phi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\varphi(x+y)\Phi(y)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x)\Phi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\varphi(y)\Phi(y)=\Psi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Psi(y),

which establishes the
\Gamma
property of
\Psi
.

Finally, let
\Psi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
be arbitrary such that
t\mapsto t^{-2}\Psi(t)=:\varphi(t)
is decreasing on
\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
. Since
\Phi(t):=t^{2}
possesses the
\Gamma
property, it follows that
\L\Psi=\varphi\cdot\Phi
belongs to
{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
and define the function
\Phi_{p}:[0,\infty[\,\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by
\Phi_{p}(t):=t^{p}
for
t>0
and
\Phi_{p}(0):=0
. Then
\Phi_{p}\in{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
if and only if
p\leq 2
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Phi_{p}
possesses the
\Gamma
property. Then, according to the first part of Theorem
4.1
,
t\mapsto t^{-1}\Phi_{p}(t)=t^{p-1}
is subadditive on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Therefore,
p-1\leq 1
, which yields
p\leq 2
.

On the other hand, if
p\leq 2
, then
t\mapsto t^{-2}\Phi_{p}(t)=t^{p-2}
is decreasing, therefore the last part of Theorem
4.1
yields that
\Phi_{p}\in{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
. Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
u_{1}\geq 0
,
u_{2},\dots,u_{n}>0
with
u_{1}+\dots+u_{n}<\ell(I)
, the following inequality holds

(24)

\displaystyle\Phi(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n})\leq\Phi(u_{1})+\frac{2u_{1}+u_{2}}{u_{2}}\Phi(u_{2})+\dots+\frac{2(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n-1})+u_{n}}{u_{n}}\Phi(u_{n}).

Proof.

Assume that
\Phi
possesses the
\Gamma
property, i.e., (
22
) is satisfied.

If
n=1
, then the statement trivially holds with equality. If
n=2
, then (
22
) with
x:=u_{1}
and
y:=u_{2}
is equivalent to (
24
). Assume that (
24
) has been proved for some
n\geq 2
and let
u_{1}\geq 0
,
u_{2},\dots,u_{n+1}>0
with
u_{1}+\dots+u_{n+1}<\ell(I)
. Then, by the
\Gamma
property with
x:=u_{1}+\dots+u_{n}
,
y:=u_{n+1}
and then by inductive assumption, we get

\displaystyle\Phi(u_{1}

\displaystyle+\dots+u_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n})+\frac{2(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n})+u_{n+1}}{u_{n+1}}\Phi(u_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(u_{1})+\dots+\frac{2(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n-1})+u_{n}}{u_{n}}\Phi(u_{n})+\frac{2(u_{1}+\dots+u_{n})+u_{n+1}}{u_{n+1}}\Phi(u_{n+1}),

which is exactly the inequality to be proved for
n+1
variables.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

The classes
{\mathscr{E}}^{\Gamma}(I)
and
{\mathscr{E}}_{0}^{\Gamma}(I)
are closed with respect to addition, multiplication by nonnegative scalars, pointwise maximum, pointwise chain infimum and the limsup operation.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.5
, therefore it is left to the reader.
∎

In view of the above proposition, we can see that the supremum of all error functions with the
\Gamma
property which are smaller than a given error function
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
is the largest error function below
\Phi
with the
\Gamma
property, which will be denoted as
\Phi^{\Gamma}
and called the
\Gamma
-envelope of
\Phi
. In what follows, we describe a construction for
\Phi^{\Gamma}
in the class
{\mathscr{E}}_{0}^{\Gamma}(I)
. For this aim, for any error function
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
, we define its
\gamma
-transform

\Phi^{\gamma}:[0,\ell(I)[\,\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
of
\Phi
by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\gamma}(0):=0,\qquad\Phi^{\gamma}(u):=\inf\bigg\{\Phi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Phi(y)\,\bigg|\,x\geq 0,\,y>0\colon x+y=u\bigg\}\qquad(u>0).

Obviously, taking
x=0
in the above definition, it follows from
\Phi(0)=0
that
\Phi^{\gamma}\leq\Phi
on
[0,\ell(I)[
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
and define the sequence
(\Phi_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
by

(25)

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}:=\Phi,\qquad\Phi_{n+1}:=\Phi_{n}^{\gamma}\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Then
(\Phi_{n})
is a pointwise decreasing sequence on
[0,\ell(I)[
whose pointwise limit function equals
\Phi^{\Gamma}
on
[0,\ell(I)[
.

Proof.

The inequality
\Phi_{2}\leq\Phi_{1}
is the consequence of
\Phi^{\gamma}\leq\Phi
. Now, using the obvious monotonicity of the mapping
\Phi\mapsto\Phi^{\gamma}
, by induction on
n
, it follows that
\Phi_{n+1}\leq\Phi_{n}
holds on
[0,\ell(I)[
, which proves the decreasingness of the sequence
(\Phi_{n})
. This implies that the pointwise limit function of this sequence, denoted as
\Phi_{0}
, exists and is not greater than
\Phi
.

By the construction, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that
\Phi_{n}(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\Phi_{n+1}(u)=\Phi_{n}^{\gamma}(u)=\inf\bigg\{\Phi_{n}(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Phi_{n}(y)\,\bigg|\,x\geq 0,\,y>0\colon x+y=u\bigg\}\qquad(0<u<\ell(I)).

This implies that

\displaystyle\Phi_{n+1}(x+y)\leq\Phi_{n}(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Phi_{n}(y)\qquad(x\geq 0,\,y>0,\,x+y<\ell(I)).

Upon taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, it follows that
\Phi_{0}
possesses the
\Gamma
property.

It remains to show that
\Phi_{0}
is nonsmaller than any error function
\Psi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
with
\Psi\leq\Phi
which possesses the
\Gamma
property. We prove, by induction on
n
, that
\Psi\leq\Phi_{n}
on
[0,\ell(I)[
. The inequality
\Psi\leq\Phi_{1}=\Phi
holds by assumption. Assume that
\Psi\leq\Phi_{n}
is valid for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
u\in\,]0,\ell(I)[
and let
x\geq 0
,
y>0
such that
u=x+y
.
Then

\displaystyle\Psi(u)=\Psi(x+y)\leq\Psi(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Psi(y)\leq\Phi_{n}(x)+\frac{2x+y}{y}\Phi_{n}(y).

Upon taking the infimum for all
x\geq 0
,
y>0
with
u=x+y
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Psi(u)\leq\Phi_{n}^{\gamma}(u)=\Phi_{n+1}(u).

This shows that
\Psi\leq\Phi_{n+1}
holds on
[0,\ell(I)[
, which was to be verified.

Finally, taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we can conclude that
\Psi\leq\Phi_{0}
is valid on
[0,\ell(I)[
, which demonstrates that
\Phi_{0}
is the largest error function below
\Phi
possessing the
\Gamma
property. Therefore,
\Phi_{0}=\Phi^{\Gamma}
holds.
∎

The next result establishes a sufficient condition that ensures the
\Gamma
envelope to be zero.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}(I)
and assume that

(26)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{-2}\Phi(t)=0.

Then
\Phi^{\Gamma}(u)=0
holds for
u\in\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\,]0,\ell(I)[\,
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, with
u_{1}:=\cdots:=u_{n}:=\frac{u}{n}
, the inequality (
24
) applied for
\Phi^{\Gamma}
(instead of
\Phi
) and the inequality
\Phi^{\Gamma}\leq\Phi
yield that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\Gamma}(u)\leq(1+3+\dots+(2n-1))\Phi^{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{u}{n}\Big)=n^{2}\Phi^{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{u}{n}\Big)\leq n^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{u}{n}\Big)=u^{2}\Big(\frac{n}{u}\Big)^{2}\Phi\Big(\frac{u}{n}\Big).

Upon taking the limit
n\to\infty
and using the relation (
26
), we get that
\Phi^{\Gamma}(u)=0
, which proves the statement.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
and define
\Phi_{p}
as in Corollary
4.2
. Then

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}^{\Gamma}=\begin{cases}\Phi_{p}&\mbox{ if }p\leq 2,\\
0&\mbox{ if }p>2.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
p\leq 2
, then, by Corollary
4.2
,
\Phi_{p}
possesses the
\Gamma
property, and hence,
\Phi_{p}^{\Gamma}=\Phi_{p}
. In the case
p>0
,
we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{-2}\Phi_{p}(t)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{p-2}=0.

Therefore, by Theorem
4.6
, it follows that
\Phi_{p}^{\Gamma}=0
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)=\mathscr{C}_{\Phi^{\Gamma}}(I)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)=\mathscr{A}_{\Phi^{\Gamma}}(I).

Proof.

Due to the inequality
\Phi^{\Gamma}\leq\Phi
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)\supseteq\mathscr{C}_{\Phi^{\Gamma}}(I)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathscr{A}_{\Phi}(I)\supseteq\mathscr{A}_{\Phi^{\Gamma}}(I).

To prove the reversed inclusions, we will first verify that
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi}(I)\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\Phi^{\gamma}}(I)
.

Let
f
be a
\Phi
-convex function and let
x,y\in I
with
x<y
and
t\in\,]0,1[\,
. Define
u:=tx+(1-t)y
and then choose
v\in[x,u[\,
and
w\in\,]u,y]
arbitrarily. Applying the
\Phi
-convexity of
f
three times, we obtain

\displaystyle f(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{w-u}{w-v}\big(f(v)+\Phi(u-v)\big)+\frac{u-v}{w-v}\big(f(w)+\Phi(w-u)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{w-u}{w-v}\bigg(\frac{u-v}{u-x}\big(f(x)+\Phi(v-x)\big)+\frac{v-x}{u-x}\big(f(u)+\Phi(u-v)\big)+\Phi(u-v)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{u-v}{w-v}\bigg(\frac{y-w}{y-u}\big(f(u)+\Phi(w-u)\big)+\frac{w-u}{y-u}\big(f(y)+\Phi(y-w)\big)+\Phi(w-u)\bigg)

After a simple calculation, this reduces to the inequality

(27)

\displaystyle f(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{y-u}{y-x}\bigg(f(x)+\Phi(v-x)+\frac{u+v-2x}{u-v}\Phi(u-v)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{u-x}{y-x}\bigg(f(y)+\Phi(y-w)+\frac{2y-u-w}{w-u}\Phi(w-u)\bigg).

Observe, that by the definition of
\Phi^{\gamma}
, we have that

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in[x,u[}\bigg(\Phi(v-x)+\frac{u+v-2x}{u-v}\Phi(u-v)\bigg)=\Phi^{\gamma}(u-x)

and

\displaystyle\inf_{w\in\,]u,y]}\bigg(\Phi(y-w)+\frac{2y-u-w}{w-u}\Phi(w-u)\bigg)=\Phi^{\gamma}(y-u).

Therefore, upon taking the infimum with respect to
v\in[x,u[\,
and
w\in\,]u,y]
in (
27
), it follows that

\displaystyle f(u)\leq\frac{y-u}{y-x}\big(f(x)+\Phi^{\gamma}(u-x)\big)+\frac{u-x}{y-x}\big(f(y)+\Phi^{\gamma}(y-u)\big),

which proves that
f
is
\Phi^{\gamma}
-convex.

5.
The
\Phi
-convex envelop

For
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, define
\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f):I\to[-\infty,\infty)
by

(28)

\displaystyle\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u):=\inf_{\mbox{\tiny$\begin{array}[]{c}x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1]\\
u=tx+(1-t)y\end{array}$}}t\big(f(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
. Then the operator
\underline{C}_{\Phi}
is monotone and concave in the pointwise sense, i.e., for
f,g:I\to\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)+(1-\lambda)\underline{C}_{\Phi}(g)\leq\underline{C}_{\Phi}(\lambda f+(1-\lambda)g).

Furthermore, for all
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, the inequality
\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)\leq f
holds and here equality is valid if and only if
f
is
\Phi
-convex. In other words, the fixed points of
\underline{C}_{\Phi}
are exactly the
\Phi
-convex functions.

Proof.

The monotonicity of
\underline{C}_{\Phi}
is obvious. To see its subadditivity, observe that, by the definition, for all
u\in I
and
x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1]
with
u=tx+(1-t)y
,
we have

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)+(1-\lambda)\underline{C}_{\Phi}(g)(u)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\big(t\big(f(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big)\big)

\displaystyle+(1-\lambda)\big(t\big(g(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(g(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big)\big)

\displaystyle=t\big((\lambda f+(1-\lambda)g)(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big((\lambda f+(1-\lambda)g)(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big).

Upon taking the infimum for
x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1]
with
u=tx+(1-t)y
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\lambda\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)+(1-\lambda)\underline{C}_{\Phi}(g)(u)\leq\underline{C}_{\Phi}(\lambda f+(1-\lambda)g)(u).

Taking
x:=y:=u
in the definition of
\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)
and using
\Phi(0)=0
, we can see that
\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)\leq f(u)
holds for all
u\in I
.

On the other hand, if
f=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)
holds for some
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, then, for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
with
u:=tx+(1-t)y
, we get

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)

\displaystyle=f(u)=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)

\displaystyle\leq t\big(f(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big),

which shows that
f
is
\Phi
-convex.

Conversely, if
f
is
\Phi
-convex, then, for all
x,y\in I
and
t\in[0,1]
with
u=tx+(1-t)y
,

\displaystyle f(u)=f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq t\big(f(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big).

Now, taking the infimum for
x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1]
with
u=tx+(1-t)y
,
it follows that
f(u)\leq\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)
. This implies that, in fact
f(u)=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)
an completes the proof of the equality
f=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)
.
∎

In what follows, we construct the
\Phi
-convex envelope of any function which admits a
\Phi
-convex minorant in terms of the operator
\underline{C}_{\Phi}
. The following auxiliary result will be useful.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
be increasing and subadditive. Then
\Psi(u):=-\Phi(|u|)
is
\Phi
-convex on
J:=I-I
. In particular, for
p\in[0,1]
,
\Psi_{p}(u):=-\Phi_{p}(|u|)
is
\Phi_{p}
-convex on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in J
and
t\in[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle-\Psi(x)=\Phi(|x|)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(|tx+(1-t)y|+|(1-t)(x-y)|)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(|tx+(1-t)y|)+\Phi(|(1-t)(x-y)|)

\displaystyle=-\Psi(tx+(1-t)y)+\Phi(|(1-t)(x-y)|)

and

\displaystyle-\Psi(y)=\Phi(|y|)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(|tx+(1-t)y|+|t(y-x)|)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(|tx+(1-t)y|)+\Phi(|t(x-y)|)

\displaystyle=-\Psi(tx+(1-t)y)+\Phi(|t(x-y)|).

Multiplying the first inequality by
t
and the second one by
(1-t)
and then adding up the inequalities so obtained side by side, we obtain that

\displaystyle-t\Psi(x)-(1-t)\Psi(y)\leq-\Psi(tx+(1-t)y)+t\Phi(|(1-t)(x-y)|)+(1-t)\Phi(|t(x-y)|),

which proves that
\Psi
is
\Phi
-convex.

The last assertion of the lemma follows from the fact the
\Phi_{p}
is nondecreasing and subadditive provided that
p\in[0,1]
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\Phi\in{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(I)
such that
\Psi(u):=-\Phi(|u|)
is
\Phi
-convex on
J:=I-I
. Let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a function which admits a
\Phi
-convex minorant. Then the function
\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)
is the largest
\Phi
-convex function which is smaller than or equal to
f
.

Proof.

The inequality
\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)\leq f
follows from the previous theorem.

Now suppose
g
is a
\Phi
-convex function such that
g\leq f
holds (by the assumption, there is at least one such function
g
). Then, according to Theorem
5.1
,
g=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(g)\leq\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)
, which proves that
g\leq\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)
.

To complete the proof, it will be sufficient to show that
g:=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)
is
\Phi
-convex. Let
u\in I
be fixed arbitrarily. Then, for all
x<u<y
, with
t:=\frac{y-u}{y-x}
, we have
u=tx+(1-t)y
. Thus, the definition of
g(u)=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(u)
yields

\displaystyle g(u)\leq\frac{y-u}{y-x}\big(f(x)+\Phi(u-x)\big)+\frac{u-x}{y-x}\big(f(y)+\Phi(y-u)\big).

This inequality implies

\displaystyle\varphi(u):=\sup_{x<u}\frac{g(u)-f(x)-\Phi(u-x)}{u-x}\leq\inf_{u<y}\frac{f(y)+\Phi(y-u)-g(u)}{y-u}.

Therefore, for every
z\in I
, we have

(29)

\displaystyle g(u)+\varphi(u)(z-u)\leq f(z)+\Phi(|z-u|).

Let
v\in I
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then, by the definition of
g(v)=\underline{C}_{\Phi}(f)(v)
, there exist
x,y\in I
,
t\in[0,1]
with
v=tx+(1-t)y
such that

\displaystyle t\big(f(x)+\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi(t|y-x|)\big)<g(v)+\varepsilon.

Now, applying the inequality (
29
) for
z:=x
and
z:=y
, and in the last step using the
\Phi
-convexity of
\Psi
, it follows that

\displaystyle g(u)

\displaystyle+\varphi(u)(v-u)

\displaystyle=t\big(g(u)+\varphi(u)(x-u)\big)+(1-t)\big(g(u)+\varphi(u)(y-u)\big)

\displaystyle\leq t\big(f(x)+\Phi(|x-u|)\big)+(1-t)\big(f(y)+\Phi(|y-u|)\big)

\displaystyle<g(v)+\varepsilon+t\big(\Phi(|x-u|)-\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(\Phi(|y-u|)-\Phi(t|y-x|)\big)

\displaystyle=g(v)+\varepsilon+t\big(-\Psi(x-u)-\Phi((1-t)|y-x|)\big)+(1-t)\big(-\Psi(y-u)-\Phi(t|y-x|)\big)

\displaystyle\leq g(v)+\varepsilon-\Psi((tx+(1-t)y)-u)=g(v)+\varepsilon+\Phi(|v-u|).

Upon taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, we obtain that
g

satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle g(u)+\varphi(u)(v-u)\leq g(v)+\Phi(|v-u|)\qquad(u,v\in I).

Therefore, according to the characterization theorem of
\Phi
-convexity, this implies that
g
is
\Phi
-convex.
∎

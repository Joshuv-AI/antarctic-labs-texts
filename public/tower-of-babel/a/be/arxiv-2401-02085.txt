On Phase Transition of Two-Dimensional Topological Gravity
Jian Zhou

Abstract
. We show that one can use some renormalized coupling constants to compute the free energy and correlation functions at all critical points of the two-dimensional topological gravity in a uniform way. In particular, one can derive the critical exponents of the free energy and correlation functions at all critical points in a uniform way. Some concrete results for the case of ( 3 , 2 ) (3,2) -model (pure gravity) and the ( 5 , 2 ) (5,2) -model (Yang-Lee edge singularity coupled with gravity) are also presented.



1.
Introduction

There are several different approaches to two dimensional quantum gravity theory in the physics literature.
They are based on different kinds of geometries of surfaces,
and they lead to different fields of mathematics.

The first approach is the differential geometric approach of Liouville field theory (see e.g.
[
7
,
12
]
, for a review see
[
33
]
)
in which the action involves the Liouville field, the conformal matter fields, and the ghost fields.
A milestone in this approach is the fractional structures discovered in
[
27
]
and generalized in
[
7
,
12
]
.
These are the salient features that have to be reproduced by other approaches.
The conformal fields are usually taken from the
(p,q)
-minimal model,
where
p
and
q
are coprime.
They have central charge

\displaystyle c=1-\frac{6(p-q)^{2}}{pq},

which gives the dimension of a fictitious space used to define the Liouville action.
It can be a rational number or even a negative number.
The primary fields
\Phi_{r,s}
are indexed by
(r,s)
(
1\leq r<q
,
1\leq s<p
),
and the conformal dimensions of
\Phi_{r,s}
are

\displaystyle h_{r,s}=\frac{(pr-qs)^{2}-(p-q)^{2}}{4pq}.

The Liouville fields are concerned with the Riemannian metrics on Riemann surfaces,
so the Liouville field theory depends on the area
A
of the surfaces.
The free energy in genus
g
takes the form

(1)

F_{g}(A)\sim K\cdot A^{\gamma_{g}-3},

where
\gamma_{g}
is the
string susceptibility
given by the KPZ formula

(2)

\gamma_{0}=\frac{1}{12}(c-1-\sqrt{(1-c)(25-c)})

in genus zero
[
27
]
,
and by

(3)

\gamma_{g}=(1-g)(\gamma_{0}-2)+2=\frac{1-g}{12}(c-25-\sqrt{(1-c)(25-c)}+2

in higher genera
[
7
,
12
]
.
After coupling with gravity,
a primary field
\phi_{0}
with conformal weight
\Delta_{0}
is gravitationally dressed to become an operator
\phi=\phi_{0}e^{\alpha\varphi}
,
where

(4)

\alpha=\frac{1}{\sqrt{12}}(\sqrt{25-c}-\sqrt{1-c+24\Delta_{0}}),

then the dressed primary operator has scaling exponent:

(5)

\Delta=\frac{\sqrt{1-c+24\Delta_{0}}-\sqrt{1-c}}{\sqrt{25-c}-\sqrt{1-c}}.

This is the DDK/KPZ formula.
This formula means if
\phi^{1},\dots,\phi^{n}
are
n
dressed operators with scaling exponents
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
respectively,
then the
n
-point correlation function has the following behavior:

(6)

\langle{\phi^{1}\cdots\phi^{n}}\rangle_{g}\sim A^{\gamma_{g}-3+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-\Delta_{i})}.

So in the Liouville field approach to two-dimensional gravity,
one has different models, indexed by coprime couples of integers
(p,q)
,
each model has some fraction structure and one is concerned with the critical exponents of the free energy and the scaling operators.
To unify them, it is natural to regard them as different phases of a single theory
and study the phase transitions in this theory,
similar to the Wilson paradigm of the study of phase transitions and critical phenomena in statistical physics in
which a big phase space of infinitely many coupling constants is considered.
Starting with a single model and considering its perturbations by adding to its action all possible scaling operators,
one is supposed to be able to reach all other models as (multi)critical points.
This was indeed the point of view taken in
[
11
]
:
Starting with the model
(1,2)
, all models
(2k-1,2)
can be reached.

The second approach to two-dimensional quantum gravity is the discrete geometric approach of dynamical tessellations of Riemann surfaces.
For reviews, see e.g.
[
10
,
22
]
.
In this approach one starts with a matrix model which is integrations of suitable weight function
over some spaces of matrices,
and interprets the partition function as a suitable way of counting tessellations
of surfaces.
One can study the partition functions and correlations functions in matrix models
using the theory of orthogonal polynomials.
This fact plays a crucial role in the following two aspects in the development of the matrix model approaches.
First, the three-term recursion relation for orthogonal polynomials leads naturally to the theory of integrable hierarchies,
secondly,
the asymptotic analysis of the orthogonal polynomials lead to the double scaling limits of matrix models
in which conformal matters emerge in the matrix model approach.
Kazakov
[
26
]
observed that by fine tuning the potential function in
the matrix model,
one may get a multicritical model with string susceptibility

(7)

\gamma_{0}=-\frac{1}{k},

he then suggested that this corresponds to the unitary minimal model
(k+1,k)
coupled with two-dimensional gravity.
As discovered in later work,
his multicritical models actually correspond to the minimal
(2k-1,2)
-models coupled with gravity.
This will be referred to as the
k
-th multicritial point
.
See e.g.
[
23
]
and the references therein.
One of the main results in
[
23
]
is as follows:
at the
k
-th critical point,
the specific heat
u=F^{\prime\prime}(t)
satisfies the following differential equation (see also
[
14
,
5
]
):

(8)

\begin{split}t&=\frac{k!}{(2k-1)!!}\hat{K}[u(t),\partial_{t}]^{k}\cdot 1,\\
\hat{K}[u(t),\partial_{t}]&:=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+u(t)+\partial_{t}^{-1}u(t)\partial_{t},\end{split}

where the operator
\hat{K}
was introduced by Gelfand and Dikiï in their study of higher
order KdV equations
[
21
]
.
Let us recall some key steps in their derivations for matrix models with even potentials.
First in the large
N
limit,

(9)

Z_{N}(g-s)\sim\exp\biggl(g_{s}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}dx(t-x)\ln R(x)\biggr),

where
t=Ng_{s}
is the ’t Hooft coupling constant,
and
R(x)
is determined from the string equation:

(10)

-x+W(R(x))=0,

where
W(R)
is determined from the even potential
U(\phi)
as follows:

(11)

\displaystyle U(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}U_{2k}\phi^{2k},

\displaystyle W(R)

\displaystyle=\sum\frac{(2k)!}{k!(k-1)!}U_{2k}R^{k}.

The
k
-th multicritical point occurs when
1-W(R)
and

k-1
of its derivatives vanish at, say,
R=1
.
In other words,

(12)

\displaystyle W_{k}(R)

\displaystyle=1-(1-R)^{k},

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=1-(1-x)^{1/k},

(13)

\displaystyle U_{k}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j-1}\frac{k!(j-1)!}{k-j)!(2j)!}\phi^{2j}.

See also an earlier derivation in
[
26
]
using loop equations in matrix model theory.
In genus zero one then gets

(14)

u_{0}(t)=F^{\prime\prime}_{0}(t)=t^{1/k},

where
t=1-x
up to a suitable rescaling constant,
and so

(15)

F_{0}(t)=\frac{k^{2}}{(k+1)(2k+1)}t^{2+1/k},

ignoring the integration constants.
In order to get correlation functions in genus zero,
one needs to consider the deformed model by introducing scaling operators
O_{l}
.
The operator
O_{l}
introduced in
[
23
]
is
\Tr(2-\phi)^{l+1/2}
up to some normalization constant in matrix model.
Then the following equation (called the string equation) is derived after taking large N limit in
[
23
]
:

(16)

t=u^{k}-\sum_{i}\mu_{i}u^{i}.

After solving for
u
as a function of
t
and the
\mu_{i}
’s and integrating twice,
one can obtain the free energy in genus zero, again ignoring all the integration constants.
The following explicit formula is obtained in
[
23
]
:

(17)

\begin{split}\langle{O_{l_{1}}\cdots O_{l_{p}}}\rangle_{0}&=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{l_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu_{l_{p}}}F(t;\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dots)\biggl|_{\mu_{j}=0}\\
&=-\frac{1}{k}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})^{p-3}t^{(\sum_{i}l_{i}+1-k)/k}\\
&\sim t^{[2+1/k]+\sum_{i}(l_{i}/k-1)},\end{split}

These results are compatible with the DDK/KPZ formula,
so the fractional structures naturally emerge in the formalism of the string equation.
In the computations of the free energy and the correlation functions in higher genera,
the operator
\hat{K}
of Gelfand and Dikii emerge naturally.
In the double scaling limit,
\Tr(2-\phi)^{l+1/2}
corresponds to
L^{l+1/2}
,
where
L=g_{s}^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}+u
is the Lax operator.
Using the results of Gelfand and Dikii,
(
8
) was then obtained.
Since one can obtain all
(2k-1,2)
-models by fine tuning the coefficients in the even potential
U(\phi)
after double scaling limit,
as proposed in
[
1
]
,
there arise the general massive model interpolating all the
(2k-1,2)
-models,
so that the string equation (
35
) takes the form

(18)

x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\frac{1}{2})T_{k}R_{k}[u],

where
u
is the specific heat which satisfies

(19)

\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{k}}u=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}R_{k}[u].

I.e., the partition function
Z
is a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy specified by the string equation (
18
).
The macroscopic loop operator
w(l)
used in
[
23
,
1
]
has an expansion

(20)

w(l)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{l^{n+1/2}}{\Gamma(n+\frac{3}{2})}\sigma_{n}.

It satisfies the loop equation
[
8
]
:

(21)

\partial_{x}^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\langle{w(l)}\rangle=(\frac{1}{2}g_{s}^{2}\partial_{x}^{4}+2u\partial_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x})\langle{w(x)}\rangle+\frac{\partial_{x}u}{\sqrt{\pi l}},

which can be reformulated as the Virasoro constraints satisfied by the partition function.
According to
[
13
]
the
(q-1)
-matrix model is related to the p-th generalized
KdV hierarchy in which the p-th multi-critical point corresponds to the
(p,q)
minimal CFT coupled to gravity.
The partition functions of these models satisfy the
W
-constraints
[
8
,
19
]
.
Since all the
(q,p)
-models can be obtained by taking the double scaling limit by fine tuning the coupling constants in
the potential of the
(q-1)
-matrix model,
one can again regard all
(p,q)
-models as different phases of a unified model.

More recently, two further developments have grown out of the matrix model approach.
The first is the Eynard-Orantin topological recursion
[
16
]
,
and the second is the determinantal formula for differential systems
[
3
]
.
They have both been applied the
(p,q)
-minimal models coupled with gravity and shown to yield the same results
[
4
,
2
]
.

The third approach is Witten’s two dimensional topological gravity
[
36
]
.
He related the partition function in the
c=-2
case to intersection numbers of
Deligne-Mumford moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of stable algebraic curves
[
37
]
.
Based on such connection to algebraic geometry,
he derived some recursion relations in genus zero and genus one called the topological recursion relations.
In genus zero he used the topological recursion relations to recover the results of Gross-Migdal
[
23
]
.
Based on the conjectural equivalence of topological gravity approach and the matrix model approach,
Witten
[
38
]
conjectured that the generating series of the intersection numbers of

\psi
-classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a tau-function of the KdV hierarchy specified by the puncture equation.
Witten also made generalizations to include equivalence with multimatrix models in
[
39
,
40
]
.
The relevant algebraic geometric objects are the moduli spaces of
r
-spin curves.

Witten’s work have inspired much progress in the mathematical literature.
His conjecture in
[
38
]
has been proved by Kontsevich
[
30
]
using a different kind of matrix models,
now called the Kontsevich matrix models, by Mirzhakhani
[
32
]
using hyperbolic geometry,
and by Okounkov and Pandhanripande and some other authors using Hurwitz numbers
[
35
,
28
,
6
,
29
]
.
His conjecture in
[
40
]
has been proved by
[
17
]
.
His work also forms the foundation of Gromov-Witten theory and FJRW theory
[
18
]
, Frobenius manifolds
[
15
]
,
etc.

Now the geometric theory of topological gravity is fully established,
the phase transitions in this theory have yet to be fully explored.
A first step towards this direction was made by Dijkgraaf and Witten
[
9
]
.
They used the topological recursion relations and the puncture equation to derive the string equation in genus zero:

(22)

u=t_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}u^{i}

and interpreted it as a Landau-Ginzburg equation.
The Landau-Ginzburg potential is then

(23)

W(u)=-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{i}}{i+1}u^{i}.

The
k
-th critical point correspond to the particular value
t_{1}=1
,
t_{k}=-1
while other
t_{i}
’s are equal to
0
.
Such an interpretation of the string equation as the Landau-Ginzburg equation
already has some feature of the Wilson paradigm where infinitely many coupling constants are
considered.
After the renormalization, the bare coupling constants are supposed to become the renormalized coupling constants.
The partition functions and the correlation functions are a priori formal power series in
t_{i}
’s,
and in general one does not expect they are convergent.
In
[
24
]
,
Itzykson and Zuber introduced formal power series
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
in
\{t_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
.
They showed

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{I_{0}^{3}}{6}-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{I_{0}^{k+2}}{k+2}\frac{t_{k}}{k!}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{I_{0}^{k+1}}{k+1}\sum_{a+b=k}\frac{t_{a}}{a!}\frac{t_{b}}{b!},

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}\log\frac{1}{1-I_{1}},

and make the ansatz
that for
g\geq 2
,
F_{g}
is a polynomial in
I_{k}/(1-I_{1})^{\frac{2k+1}{3}}
.

In earlier work we interpreted the
I_{n}
’s as the renormalized
t_{n}
’s.
We considered the matrix model of size
1\times 1
and called it the topological 1D gravity
[
42
]
(see also
[
34
]
).
In such a model a renormalization procedure produces the
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
from
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

in the limit.
We then understood
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
as different coordinates on a big phase
space and compute the Jacobian matrices of the coordinate changes in
[
42
]
.
In
[
41
]
we prove the Itzykson-Zuber ansatz.
In this paper we will use these earlier results to study the phase transitions
in two-dimensional topological gravity.
In particular,
we will show how to give a uniform way to compute the free energy and correlation functions
at all the critical points using the renormalized coupling constants.
This has the advantage that it is clear that all results are essentially polynomial expressions
of some simple form,
and also it is easy to check the critical exponents predicted by
the DDK/KPZ formula at all critical points and at all genera.

We arrange the rest of the paper as follows.
In Section
2
we recall the big phase space of the 2D topological gravity
and the multicritical points on it as seen from the Landau-Gingzburg potential.
We also explain how the dilaton equation gives rise to difficulty in reach the multicritical points starting from the Witten-Kontsevich tau-function.
We explain how to compute the free energy and correlation functions in the renormalized coupling constants
in Section
3
.
This is the key technical Section.
As illustrations we present some concrete computation in the case of pure gravity (the
k=2
case) in Section
4.1

and the case of Yang-Lee edge singularity coupled with gravity (the
k=3
case) in Section
5
.
We determine the critical exponents of the free energy functions and correlation function in each genus using the renormalized coordinates
in Section
6
.

2.
Critical Points on the Big Phase Space of 2D Topological Gravity

In this Section we recall the big phase space of two-dimensional gravity and the critical points on it.
We will review the special roles played by the puncture operator
\tau_{0}
and the dilaton operator
\tau_{1}
.

2.1.
Big phase space

Let us begin by recalling the Witten-Kontsevich tau-function
which is the partition function of the type
(1,2)
-theory in two-dimensional topological gravity.
The relevant genus
g
,
n
-point correlators are defined by the intersection numbers:

(24)

\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}:=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}.

For degree reasons,
the following selection rule is satisfied:
\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}\neq 0

only if

(25)

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=3g-3+n.

From this rule it is clear that if all
a_{i}
’s are bigger than
1
,
then for each fixed genus
g
,
there are only finitely many nonzero correlators to compute.
So in order to get finite expressions,
we need some recursion relations that remove all the
\tau_{0}
’s and
\tau_{1}
’s from the correlators.
This can be achieved by the puncture equation and the dilaton equation to be recalled below.

The genus
g
free energy is defined by:

(26)

F_{g}(t_{0},t_{1},\dots):=\sum_{n:n>2-2g}\frac{t_{a_{1}}\cdots t_{a_{n}}}{n!}\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}.

The all genera free energy is defined by

(27)

F(t_{0},t_{2},\dots;\lambda)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}F_{g}(t_{0},t_{1},\dots),

and the partition function

(28)

Z(t_{0},t_{1},\dots;\lambda):=\exp F(t_{0},t_{2},\dots;\lambda)

is called the Witten-Kontsevich tau-function.

By the big phase space we mean the infinite-dimensional vector space
with linear coordinates
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.
Because
F_{g}
,
F
and
Z
are just defined as formal power series in the
t_{n}
’s,
a priori, they are understood to make sense only near the origin where all
t_{n}
’s are equal to
0
.
Our purpose is to understand the big phase space as a moduli spaces of theories so that
the Witten-Kontsevich tau-function corresponds to the theory with all
t_{n}=0
,
and all other theories of type
(2k-1,2)
lie in hypersurface with
t_{1}=1
.

2.2.
KdV hierarchy

The specific heat is defined as the second derivative of the free energy:

(29)

u({\bf t};\lambda)=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g}u_{g}({\bf t})=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g}\frac{\partial^{2}F_{g}({\bf t})}{\partial t_{0}^{2}}.

According to Witten Conjecture/Kontsevich Theorem
[
38
,
30
]
,

u
should satisfy the KdV hierarchy

(30)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t_{n}}=\partial_{x}R_{n+1}[u],

for the sequence of Gelfand-Dickey differential polynomials
R_{n}[u]
.
define by the Lenard recursion relations:

(31)

\begin{split}&R_{0}=1,\\
&\partial_{x}R_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2n+1}\biggl(\partial_{x}u\cdot R_{n}+2u\cdot\partial_{x}R_{n}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\partial_{x}^{3}R_{n}\biggr).\end{split}

For example,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}R_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u,

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\cdot\partial_{x}u+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial_{x}^{3}u,

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}\partial_{x}^{2}u,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}R_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\cdot\partial_{x}u+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}u\cdot\partial_{x}^{3}u+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{6}\partial_{x}u\cdot\partial_{x}^{2}u+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{240}\partial_{x}^{5}u,

\displaystyle R_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}u^{3}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{12}u\cdot\partial_{x}^{2}u+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{24}(\partial_{x}u)^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{240}\partial_{x}^{4}u.

2.3.
The puncture equation

The coupling constant
t_{0}
corresponds to the
puncture operator
.
The recursion relation satisfied by the insertion of the puncture operator is
the puncture equation
[
38
]
:

(32)

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t_{0}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}t_{n+1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial t_{n}}+\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}.

In terms of correlators,

(33)

\langle{\tau_{0}\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{i}-1}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}.

From this one sees that one can reduce correlators without
\tau_{0}
.
One well-known application of the puncture equation is to the removal of
all the insertions of the puncture operator from the correlator.

2.4.
The string equation

Let us recall another well-known application of the puncture equation:
The derivation of the string equation from the puncture equation
[
8
]
.
Taking
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t_{0}^{2}}
twice on both sides of (
32
) twice
and use the KdV hierarchy for
u
,
one gets:

(34)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t_{0}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}t_{n+1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t_{n}}+1=\sum_{n\geq 0}t_{n+1}\partial_{x}R_{n+1}[u]+1.

The string equation was then obtained by integrating with respect to
t_{0}
once:

(35)

0=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(t_{k}-\delta_{k,1})R_{k}+x.

2.5.
Landau-Ginzburg equation

Expanding both sides of the string equation as series in
\lambda
,
one gets by comparing the leading terms the following equation:

(36)

u_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}t_{k}\cdot u_{0}^{k}+x.

We will call this equation the
Landau-Ginzburg equation
.
It can be rewritten as follows.

(37)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}(t_{k}-\delta_{k,1})\cdot u_{0}^{k}=0.

Here
u_{0}
is the genus zero part of
u
:

(38)

u_{0}=\frac{\partial F_{0}}{\partial t_{0}^{2}}.

2.6.
Critical points and multi-critical points

The idea of Landau and Ginzburg was to minimize some action of some mean field theory.
For the genus zero part it is straightforward to consider:

(39)

G(v;{\bf t})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)!}(t_{k}-\delta_{k,1})\cdot v^{k+1}.

Then the Landau-Ginzburg equation is just the equation:

(40)

\frac{\partial}{\partial v}G(v;{\bf t})=0.

Now note

(41)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}G(v;{\bf t})=-1+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}t_{k+1}\cdot v^{k}.

Therefore,
when
t_{1}=t_{2}=\cdots=0
,

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}G(v;{\bf t})|_{t_{k}=0,k\geq 1}=-1\neq 0.

However,
when
t_{1}=1
,
t_{2}=t_{3}=\cdots=0
,

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}G(v;{\bf t})|_{t_{1}=1,t_{k}=0,k\geq 2}=0.

But note

(42)

\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial v^{3}}G(v;{\bf t})=t_{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}t_{k+2}\cdot v^{k}.

Therefore,
when
t_{1}=1
,
t_{2}\neq 0
,
one has

(43)

\frac{\partial}{\partial v}G(v;{\bf t})=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial v^{2}}G(v;{\bf t})=0,\;\;\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial v^{3}}G(v;{\bf t})\neq 0,

Continue this way of thinking,
take
t_{1}=1
,
t_{k}=0
for
k\geq 2
except for
t_{m}\neq 0
.
Then

(44)

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial v^{k}}G(v;{\bf t})=0,\;\;1\leq k\leq m-1,\;\;\;\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial v^{m}}G(v;{\bf t})\neq 0.

In this situation, physicists say
G(v;{\bf t})
as a function of
v

acquires a multi-critical point when such parameters are chosen.

The
n
-th critical point is where one has

(45)

t_{n}\neq 0,\;\;\;\;\;t_{k}=0,\;\;k\neq n.

These points are where the
(2n-1,2)
-theories arise.

2.7.
Dilaton equation

The coupling constant
t_{1}
corresponds to the dilaton operator
\tau_{1}
.
The recursion relation satisfied by the insertion of
\tau_{1}
is called the
dilaton equation
:

(46)

\langle{\tau_{1}\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2a_{i}+1}{3}\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}.

Using this equation,
one can reduce the calculations of all correlators
\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}

to such correlation so that none of the
a_{i}
’s is equal to
1
.
As a consequence,
one can show that
F_{g}(t_{0},t_{1},\dots)
takes the following form:

(47)

\begin{split}F_{g}(t_{0},t_{1},\dots)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n:n>2-2g\\
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n!}\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{t_{a_{i}}}{(1-t_{1})^{(2a_{i}+1)/3}}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n:n>2-2g\\
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\neq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{t_{a_{1}}\cdots t_{a_{n}}}{n!(1-t_{1})^{2g-2+n}}\langle{\tau_{a_{1}}\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}}\rangle_{g}.\end{split}

From this it seems that we cannot take
t_{1}=1
at all.
But we are working in a perturbative setting,
and we are working with formal power series with infinitely many other coupling constants
t_{n}
’s.
We will see later that it is possible to change to the renormalized constants to get finite expressions,
and then it is actually possible to reach the hyperplane
t_{1}=1
where all the multicritical points lie.

Theorem 3.2

-coordinates as follows:

(63)

\begin{split}\nabla(x)=&\frac{1}{(x-2I_{0})^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-I_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{0}}\\
+&\sum_{l\geq 1}\biggl(\frac{1}{(x-2I_{0})^{3/2}}\cdot\frac{I_{l+1}}{1-I_{1}}+\frac{(2l+1)!!}{(x-2I_{0})^{(2l+3)/2}}\biggr)\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}.\end{split}

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\nabla(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(2n+1)!!}{x^{n+\frac{3}{2}}}\biggl(\frac{1}{1-I_{1}}\frac{I_{0}^{n}}{n!}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{0}}+\frac{I_{0}^{n}}{n!}\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{I_{l+1}}{1-I_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}+\sum_{1\leq l\leq n}\frac{I_{0}^{n-l}}{(n-l)!}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(2n+1)!!}{x^{n+\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{I_{0}^{n}}{n!}\cdot\biggl(\frac{1}{1-I_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{0}}+\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{I_{l+1}}{1-I_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}\biggr)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2l+1)!!}{x^{l+\frac{3}{2}}}(1-\frac{2I_{0}}{x})^{-(2l+3)/2}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{1}{(1-\frac{2I_{0}}{x})^{3/2}}\biggl(\frac{1}{1-I_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{0}}+\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{I_{l+1}}{1-I_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}\biggr)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2l+1)!!}{(x-2I_{0})^{(2l+3)/2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{l}}.

In the above we have some combinatorial identities as follows.
First note

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n+1)!!}{n!}t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n+1)!}{n!n!}(\frac{t}{2})^{n}=(1-2t)^{-3/2}.

Take

on both sides:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n+3)!!}{n!}t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3\cdot(1-2t)^{-5/2}.

Repeat this for several times:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2n+2l+1)!!}{n!}t^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2l+1)!!\cdot(1-2t)^{-(2l+3)/2}.

∎

7.
Conclusions and Prospects

In this work we have studied the phase transition and critical phenomena in two-dimensional topological gravity
within the Landau-Ginzburg-Wilson paradigm.
The Landau-Ginzburg equation is derived from the puncture equation and the KdV hierarchy
satisfied by the Witten-Kontsevich partition function
[
9
]
.
In genus zero the Landau-Ginzburg potential
is of the form

(150)

-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t_{n}\frac{v^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.

As shown in
[
42
]
,
after a simple renormalization procedure one gets:

(151)

-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}+\sum t_{n}\frac{v^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}=I_{-1}+\sum_{=1}^{\infty}(I_{n}-\delta_{n,1})\frac{(v-I_{0})^{n}}{(n+1)!},

where
I_{0}
is the solution of

(152)

I_{0}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t_{n}\frac{I_{0}^{n}}{n!},

and for
k\geq 1
,

(153)

I_{k}=\sum_{n\geq 0}t_{n+k}\frac{I_{0}^{n}}{n!},

and

(154)

I_{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t_{n}\frac{I_{0}^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.

The renormalized coupling constants are used to get unified computations to compute
the free energy functions and the correlations functions at all the critical points,
and the critical components can also be obtained in a simple unified way.

Usually one works locally at a single critical point.
Surprisingly we can work uniformly at all critical points in this case.
We believe it is a common feature for any
theory obtained from two-dimensional topological gravity coupled with some topological matter.
Indeed,
as shown in
[
9
]
,
one can derive the Landau-Ginzburg equations from the topological recursion relations in genus zero for such a theory.
Starting with the Landau-Ginzburg potential for these equations,
one can perform renormalization as in the case of Witten-Kontsevich tau-function.
The puncture equation and the dilaton equation hold for such a theory.
If the puncture operator and the dilaton operator can be reformulated as simple operators in the renormalized coupling constants,
then one can again expect the free energy in genus
>0
as finite expressions.
The most natural extension is to consider the intersection numbers on
Witten’s moduli spaces of r-spin curves
and treat all the
(p,q)
-models in the same fashion.
In a forthcoming paper we will work out the details for case of
r=3
.
Further extension to the general case and topological gravity coupled to topological sigma models
will be investigated in the future.

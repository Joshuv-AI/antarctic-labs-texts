Post Lie-Yamaguti algebras, relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weights, and their deformations Thanks: Keywords : relative Rota-Baxter operato of weight 1 1 , post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra, cohomology, deformation Thanks: Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): Primary 17B38; Secondary 17B60,17A99
Jia Zhao, Senrong Xu, Yu Qiao*

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notions of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 1 on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and post-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which is an underlying algebraic structure of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 1 . We give the relationship between these two algebraic structures. Besides, we establish the cohomology theory of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 1 via the Yamaguti cohomology. Consequently, we use this cohomology to characterize linear deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 1 1 on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We show that if two linear deformations of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1 1 are equivalent, then their infinitesimals are in the same cohomology class in the first cohomology group. Moreover, we show that an order n n deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight 1 1 can be extended to an order n + 1 n+1 deformation if and only if the obstruction class in the second cohomology group is trivial.

1.3.
Motivation

It is nature to consider relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Lie-Yamaguti algebras. The first purpose of the present paper is to devote its relations between relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
and associated algebraic structures. For this purpose, we introduce the notion of post-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which can be seen as the underlying algebraic structures of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
. And we show that a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra gives rise to a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure and an action of this Lie-Yamaguti algebra on itself. Consequently, the identity map is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
on this induced Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

The second purpose is to explore cohomology and deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
. For this aim, we need to construct a new representation of the induced sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra by relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
, and to construct an
0
-cochain, which is one of the main tasks in this paper. Once the cohomology is established, we are able to examine deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
. We intend to study two kinds of deformations: linear and higher order deformations.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations, and cohomology. In Section 3, we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and study some properties of it. In Section 4, we introduce the notion of post-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which is the underlying algebraic structures of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
. We give the relationship between post-Lie-Yamaguti algebras and Lie-Yamaguti algebras. In Section 5, we establish the cohomology of relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and examine a functorial property of the cohomology theory. Moreover, we use this type of cohomology to explore two kinds of deformations, and show that the infinitesimal of linear deformations can be governed by cohomology and that the extension of a higher deformation is characterized by a special cohomology class, which is called the obstruction class.

In this paper, all vector spaces are assumed to be over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
and finite-dimensional.

2.
Preliminaries: Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and cohomology

In this section, we recall some basic notions such as Lie-Yamaguti algebras, representations and their cohomology theories.
The notion of Lie-Yamaguti algebras was introduced by Yamaguti in
[
39
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
20
]

A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with a bilinear bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
and a trilinear bracket
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, which meet the following conditions: for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(1)

\displaystyle[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket y,z,x\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket[x,y],z,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[y,z],x,w\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket[z,x],y,w\right\rrbracket=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,[z,w]\right\rrbracket=[\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w]+[z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket],

(4)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket=\left\llbracket\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket,t\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket\right\rrbracket.

In the sequel, we denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra by
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a Lie algebra. Define a trilinear bracket

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

by

\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket:=[[x,y],z],\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then by a direct computation, we know that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

The following example is even more interesting.

Example 2.3
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold with an affine connection, and denote by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of vector fields on
M
. For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, set

\displaystyle[x,y]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-T(x,y),

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-R(x,y)z,

where
T
and
R
are torsion tensor and curvature tensor respectively. It turns out that the triple

(\mathfrak{X}(M),[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
forms a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. See
[
30
]
for more details.

Next, we recall the notion of representations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Definition 2.4
.

[
40
]

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space
V
equipped with a linear map
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and a bilinear map
\mu:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, which meet the following conditions: for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(5)

\displaystyle\mu([x,y],z)-\mu(x,z)\rho(y)+\mu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\mu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\mu(x,z)+\rho(z)\mu(x,y)=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],

(8)

\displaystyle\mu(z,w)\mu(x,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(x,z)-\mu(x,\left\llbracket y,z,w\right\rrbracket)+D_{\rho,\mu}(y,z)\mu(x,w)=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+\mu(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\mu(z,w)],

where the bilinear map
D_{\rho,\mu}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is given by

(10)

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y):=\mu(y,x)-\mu(x,y)+[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y]),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is obvious that
D_{\rho,\mu}
is skew-symmetric, and we write
D
in the sequel without ambiguities. We denote a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
by
(V;\rho,\mu)
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\rho=0
and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie tripe system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
, then the representation reduces to that of the Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
:
(V;\mu)
. If
\mu=0
,
D=0
and the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
reduces to a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
, then the representation reduces to that of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot])
:
(V;\rho)
. Hence a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a natural generalization of that of a Lie algebra or of a Lie triple system.

By a direct computation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
(V;\rho,\mu)
is a representation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
. Then the following equalities are satisfied:

\displaystyle D([x,y],z)+D([y,z],x)+D([z,x],y)=0;

\displaystyle D(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)+D(z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket)=[D(x,y),D_{\rho,\mu}(z,w)];

\displaystyle\mu(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,w)=\mu(x,w)\mu(z,y)-\mu(y,w)\mu(z,x)-\mu(z,w)D(x,y).

Example 2.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. We define linear maps
\mathrm{ad}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{R}:\otimes^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by
x\mapsto\mathrm{ad}_{x}
and
(x,y)\mapsto\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}
respectively, where
\mathrm{ad}_{x}z=[x,z]
and
\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket z,x,y\right\rrbracket
for all
z\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
forms a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on itself, where
\mathfrak{L}:=D_{\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R}}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}=\mathfrak{R}_{y,x}-\mathfrak{R}_{x,y}+[\mathrm{ad}_{x},\mathrm{ad}_{y}]-\mathrm{ad}_{[x,y]},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

By (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{x,y}z=\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket,\quad\forall z\in\mathfrak{g}.

In this case,
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
is called the
adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let us recall the cohomology theory on Lie-Yamaguti algebras given in
[
40
]

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket)

(V;\rho,\mu)
a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote the set of

, where

\displaystyle C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\triangleq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n},V)\times\mathrm{Hom}(\underbrace{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\cdots\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{g},V),&\forall n\geqslant 1,\\
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V),&n=0.\end{cases}

In the sequel, we recall the coboundary map of
p
-cochains:

•

If
n\geqslant 1
, for any
(f,g)\in C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)
, the coboundary map is given by

\delta=(\delta_{\rm I},\delta_{\rm II}):C^{n+1}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{n+2}_{\rm LieY}(\mathfrak{g},V),

\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad(f,g)\mapsto(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g),\delta_{\rm II}(f,g))

where
\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)
and
\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)
are defined to be

(11)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm I}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\rho(x_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})-\rho(y_{n+1})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},[x_{n+1},y_{n+1}])\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}D(\mathfrak{X}_{k})f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}f(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1}),

and

(12)

\displaystyle\Big(\delta_{\rm II}(f,g)\Big)(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\Big(\mu(y_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},x_{n+1})-\mu(x_{n+1},z)g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{k+1}D(\mathfrak{X}_{k})g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leqslant k<l\leqslant n+1}(-1)^{k}g(\mathfrak{X}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\mathfrak{X}_{k}},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{k}\circ\mathfrak{X}_{l},\cdots,\mathfrak{X}_{n+1},z)

respectively. Here

.

•

3.

Definition 3.2

The following proposition demonstrates that an action can be characterized by semidirect product Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+D(x,y)w+\mu(y,z)u-\mu(x,z)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}.

is called the

with respect to an action

.

Proof.

It only involves computations, and we omit the details.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})

(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})

(\rho,\mu)
an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Then a linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

1
with respect to

if and only if the graph of
T

is a subalgebra of the semidirect Lie-Yamaguti algebra

Proof.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a linear map. Then for all

\displaystyle[Tu+u,Tv+v]_{\ltimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle+N^{2}\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big),\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and
(\rho,\mu)
an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
. Then a linear map
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
with respect to
(\rho,\mu)
if and only if

\overline{T}=\begin{pmatrix}{\rm{Id}}&T\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

is a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, on one hand, we have

\displaystyle[\overline{T}(x+u),\overline{T}(y+v)]_{\ltimes}=[x+Tu,y+Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}},

and

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\overline{T}(x+u),\overline{T}(y+v),\overline{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}=\left\llbracket x+Tu,y+Tv,z+Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket Tu,y,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket Tu,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,Tv,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,y,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}.

On the other hand, since
\overline{T}^{2}=\overline{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{T}\Big([\overline{T}(x+u),y+v]_{\ltimes}+[x+u,\overline{T}(y+v)]_{\ltimes}-\overline{T}[x+u,y+v]_{\ltimes}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,Tv]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[Tu,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+T\Big(\rho(Tu)v-\rho(Tv)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

\displaystyle\overline{T}\Big(\left\llbracket\overline{T}(x+u),\overline{T}(y+v),z+w\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}+\left\llbracket\overline{T}(x+u),y+v,\overline{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}+\left\llbracket x+u,\overline{T}(y+v),\overline{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}\Big)

\displaystyle-\overline{T}^{2}\Big(\left\llbracket\overline{T}(x+u),y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}+\left\llbracket x+u,\overline{T}(y+v),z+w\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}+\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,\overline{T}(z+w)\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}\Big)

\displaystyle+\overline{T}^{3}\left\llbracket x+u,y+v,z+w\right\rrbracket_{\ltimes}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket Tu,y,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket Tu,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,Tv,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,y,Tw\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+T\Big(D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Thus
\overline{T}
is a Nijenhuis operator on the semidirect Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
if and only if

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{g}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra such that the adjoint representation
(\mathfrak{g};\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
is an action of the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on itself. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{g}^{1}\cap\mathfrak{h}=0
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{t}
is a compliment of
\mathfrak{h}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
as vector spaces. Then the projection
P:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
onto the subspace
\mathfrak{h}
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the action
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})
.

Proof.

For all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, denote by
\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z}
the images of
x,y,z
by the projection
P
. Since
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian and
\mathfrak{g}^{1}\cap\mathfrak{h}=0
, we have

\displaystyle[Px,Py]_{\mathfrak{g}}-P\Big([Px,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x,Py]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\bar{x},\bar{y}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-P\Big([\bar{x},y]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x,\bar{y}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Similarly, we also have that

\displaystyle\left\llbracket Px,Py,Pz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-P\Big(\left\llbracket Px,Py,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,Py,Pz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-\left\llbracket y,Px,Pz\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z}\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-P\Big(\left\llbracket\bar{x},\bar{y},z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,\bar{y},\bar{z}\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-\left\llbracket y,\bar{x},\bar{z}\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus

is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight

from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the action
(\mathrm{ad},\mathfrak{R})

∎

4.
Post Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of post-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, which is the underlying algebraic structure of relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weights. Moreover, we show that there exists a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra and that an action can also be obtained via the given post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Definition 4.1
.

A
post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a vector space
A
endowed with two binary operations
\cdot:\wedge^{2}A\longrightarrow A,~*:\otimes^{2}A\longrightarrow A
and two ternary operations
\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\wedge^{2}A\otimes A\longrightarrow A,~\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}:\otimes^{3}A\longrightarrow A
such that
(A,\cdot,\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and the following equalities hold for all
x,y,z,w,t\in A
,

(26)

\displaystyle~\{z,[x,y]_{C},w\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{y*z,x,w\}-\{x*z,y,w\},

(27)

\displaystyle~\{x,y,[z,w]_{c}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z*\{x,y,w\}-w*\{x,y,z\},

(28)

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{C}*w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z*w\}_{D}-z*\{x,y,w\}_{D},

(29)

\displaystyle~\{x,y,\left\llbracket z,w,t\right\rrbracket_{C}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{x,w,z\},w,t\}-\{\{x,y,w\},z,t\}+\{z,w,\{x,y,t\}_{D}\},

(30)

\displaystyle~\{x,y,\{z,w,t\}_{D}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{x,y,z\}_{D},w,t\}+\{z,\left\llbracket x,y,w\right\rrbracket_{C},t\}+\{z,w,\left\llbracket x,y,t\right\rrbracket_{C}\},

(31)

\displaystyle~(x*y)\cdot z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x*y,z,w\rangle=\langle z,w,x*y\rangle=0,

(32)

\displaystyle~x*(yz)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{xy,z,w\}=0,

(33)

\displaystyle~x*\langle z,w,t\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\langle z,w,t\rangle,x,y\}=0,

where operations
[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},~\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C}
and
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{D}
are defined to be

(34)

\displaystyle[x,y]_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x*y-y*x+xy,

(35)

\displaystyle~\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}_{D}+\{x,y,z\}-\{y,x,z\}+\langle x,y,z\rangle,

(36)

\displaystyle~\{x,y,z\}_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z,y,x\}-\{z,x,y\}+(y,x,z)-(x,y,z)-(xy)*z,\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.

Here the notation
(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
stands for the associator with respect to the operation
*
, i.e., for all
x,y,z\in A,

(x,y,z)=(x*y)*z-x*(y*z).

Remark 4.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra. If
xy=\langle x,y,z\rangle=0
for all
x,y,z\in A
, then
(A,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
becomes a pre-Lie-Yamaguti algebra, which is the underlying algebraic structure of relative Rota-Baxter operators of zero weights. One can see
[
33
]
for more details.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{A},*_{A},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{A},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A})
and
(A^{\prime},\cdot_{A^{\prime}},*_{A^{\prime}},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{A^{\prime}},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A^{\prime}})
be two post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra. A
homomorphism
from
A
to
A^{\prime}
is a linear map
\psi:A\longrightarrow A^{\prime}
such that
\psi:(A,\cdot_{A},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A})\longrightarrow(A,\cdot_{A^{\prime}},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A^{\prime}})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism and the following equalities hold

\displaystyle\psi(x*_{A}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(x)*_{A^{\prime}}\psi(y),

\displaystyle~\psi(\{x,y,z\}_{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\psi(x),\psi(y),\psi(z)\}_{A^{\prime}},\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.

The following Theorem reveals the fact that a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra gives rise to a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure on its original space.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(A,\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Then

(i)

the triple
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
of
(A,\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
and is denoted by

, where

and
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C}
are given by (

) and (

(ii)

the pair
(L,\mathcal{R})
is an action of the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A,\cdot,\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
, where the linear maps
L:A\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(A),~x\mapsto L(x)
and
\mathcal{R}:\otimes^{2}A\longrightarrow A,~(x,y)\mapsto\mathcal{R}(x,y)
are defined to be

L(x)z=x*z,\quad\mathcal{R}(x,y)z=\{z,x,y\},\quad\forall z\in A

respectively;

(iii)

the identity map
{{\rm{Id}}}:A\longrightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from
(A,\cdot,\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
to
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
.

Proof.

Obviously, the operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{C}
is skew-symmetric, and
\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the first two variables. For all
x,y,z,w\in A
, by Eq. (
36
), we have that

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{C},z]_{C}+\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{C}+c.p.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,y]_{C}*z-z*(x*y)+z*(y*x)+\{x,y,z\}_{D}+\{x,y,z\}-\{y,x,z\}+(xy)z+\langle x,y,z\rangle+c.p.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

and by (
26
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\llbracket[x,y]_{C},z,w\right\rrbracket_{C}+c.p.(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{[x,y]_{C},z,w\}_{D}+\{[x,y]_{C},z,w\}-\{z,[x,y]_{C},w\}+\langle xy,z,w\rangle+c.p.(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus, Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) with respect to
([\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
hold. Similarly, Eqs. (
3
) and (

) with respect to
([\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
also hold. Thus
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. This gives (i).

For all
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle~D_{L,\mathcal{R}}(x,y)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(y,x)z-\mathcal{R}(x,y)z+[L(x),L(y)]z-L([x,y]_{C})z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{z,y,x\}-\{z,x,y\}+(y,x,z)-(x,y,z)-(x,y)*z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}_{D}.

In the sequel, we denote by
\mathcal{L}:=D_{L,\mathcal{R}}

\mathcal{L}(x,y)z=\{x,y,z\}_{D}
. Moreover, for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}([x,y]_{C},z)w-\mathcal{R}(x,z)L(y)w+\mathcal{R}(y,z)L(x)z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{w,[x,y]_{C},z\}-\{y*w,x,z\}+\{x*w,y,z\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{post1}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle L(\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{C})w-[\mathcal{L}(x,y),L(z)]w

\displaystyle\left\llbracket x,y,z\right\rrbracket_{C}*w-\{x,y,z*w\}_{D}+z*\{x,y,w\}_{D}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{post3}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0.

The left equalities can be obtained similarly. Thus
(L,\mathcal{R})
is an action of
(A,[\cdot,\cdot]_{C},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C})
on

(iii) follows from the expressions of

\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{C}

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\psi:A\longrightarrow A^{\prime}
be a homomorphism from a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A,\cdot_{A},*_{A},\\
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{A},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A})
to another post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(A^{\prime},\cdot_{A^{\prime}},*_{A^{\prime}},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{A^{\prime}},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{A^{\prime}})
. Then
\psi
is also a Lie-Yamaguti algebra homomorphism between the sub-adjacent Lie-Yamaguti algebras from
A^{c}
to
{A^{\prime}}^{c}
.

The following theorem demonstrates that a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure is the underlying algebraic structure of relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weights.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Then
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra, where operations
\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle
are defined to be for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
,

uv=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad u*v=\rho(Tu)v,\quad\{u,v,w\}=\mu(Tv,Tw)u,\quad\langle u,v,w\rangle=\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Proof.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have that

\displaystyle D(Tu,Tv)w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(Tv,Tu)w-\mu(Tu,Tv)w+[\rho(Tu),\rho(Tv)]w-\rho([Tu,Tv])w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{w,v,u\}-\{w,u,v\}+(v,u,w)-(u,v,w)-(uv)*w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u,v,w\}_{D}.

Moreover, for all
u,v,w,s,t\in\mathfrak{h}
, we also have that

\displaystyle\mu([Tu,Tv],Tt)w-\mu(Tu,Tt)\rho(Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tt)\rho(Tu)w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{w,[u,v]_{C},t\}-\{v*w,u,t\}+\{u*w,v,t\},

\displaystyle\mu(Tv,[Tw,Tt])u-\rho(Tw)\mu(Tv,Tt)u+\rho(Tt)\mu(Tv,Tw)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u,v,[w,t]_{C}\}-w*\{u,v,t\}+t*\{u,v,w\},

\displaystyle\rho(\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket)t-[D(Tu,Tv),\rho(Tw)]t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{C}*t-\{u,v,w*t\}+w*\{u,v,t\},

\displaystyle\mu(Tt,Ts)\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tw,Ts)\mu(Tv,Tt)u-\mu(Tv,\left\llbracket Tw,Tt,Ts\right\rrbracket)u

\displaystyle~~+D(Tw,Tt)\mu(Tv,Ts)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{u,v,w\},t,s\}-\{\{u,v,t\},w,s\}+\{w,t\{u,v,s\}\}_{D}-\{u,v,\left\llbracket w,t,s\right\rrbracket_{C}\},

\displaystyle[D(Tu,Tv),\mu(Tt,Ts)]w-\mu(\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Tt\right\rrbracket,Ts)w-\mu(Tt,\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,Ts\right\rrbracket)w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{u,v,\{w,t,s\}\}_{D}-\{\{u,v,w\}_{D},t,s\}-\{w,\left\llbracket u,v,t\right\rrbracket_{C},s\}-\{w,t,\left\llbracket u,v,s\right\rrbracket_{C}\}.

Since
(\rho,\mu)
is a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
, Eqs. (
26
)-(
30
) with respect to
(\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
hold. Moreover, since
(\rho,\mu)
is also an action, it is easy to see that Eqs. (
31
)-(
33
) with respect to
(\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
hold. Besides, the triple
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot,\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is obviously a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Thus
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot,*,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra. This finishes the proof.
∎

4.4
and Theorem
4.6

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle[u,v]_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(Tu,Tv)w+\mu(Tv,Tw)u-\mu(Tu,Tw)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two relative Rota-Bater operators of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Let
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{T},*_{T},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{T^{\prime}},*_{T^{\prime}},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T^{\prime}},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T^{\prime}})
be the induced post-Lie-Yamaguti algebras, and
(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}},\psi_{\mathfrak{h}})
a homomorphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
. Then
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a homomorphism from the post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{T^{\prime}},*_{T^{\prime}},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T^{\prime}},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T^{\prime}})
to the post-Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\cdot_{T},*_{T},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{T},\langle\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{T})
.

Proof.

For all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\{u,v,w\}_{T^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big(\mu(T^{\prime}v,T^{\prime}w)u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}v),\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}w)\Big)\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(w)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\Big(T\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v),T\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(w)\Big)\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(w)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v),\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(w)\}_{T},

and

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big(u*_{T^{\prime}}v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big(\rho(T^{\prime}u)v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}(T^{\prime}u))\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(T\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u))\Big(\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u)*_{T}\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)

Moreover, since
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a homomorphism from
\mathfrak{h}
to
\mathfrak{h}
, then we also have that
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u\cdot_{T^{\prime}}v)=\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u)\cdot_{T}\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v)
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\langle u,v,w\rangle_{T^{\prime}})=\langle\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v),\psi_{\mathfrak{h}}(w)\rangle_{T}
for all
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

T_{t}^{1}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
T_{t}^{2}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are called
equivalent
if there exists an element
\mathfrak{X}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
such that
({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X}),{{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X}))
is a homomorphism from
T_{t}^{2}
to
T_{t}^{1}
.

(ii)

A linear deformation
T_{t}=T+t\mathfrak{T}
of the relative Rota-Baxter operator
T
of weight
1
is called
trivial
if it is equivalent to
T
.

If two linear deformations
T_{t}^{2}
and
T_{t}^{1}
are equivalent, then
Eq. (
22
) yields that for all
v\in\mathfrak{h}
,

\displaystyle\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\mathfrak{L}(\mathfrak{X})\Big)\Big(T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2}\Big)v=\Big(T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1}\Big)\Big({{\rm{Id}}}_{\mathfrak{h}}+tD(\mathfrak{X})\Big)v,

which implies that

(43)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{2}v-\mathfrak{T}_{1}v=T\Big(D(\mathfrak{X})v\Big)-\left\llbracket\mathfrak{X},Tv\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}=\partial(\mathfrak{X})v.

Thus by (
43
), we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. If two linear deformations
T_{t}^{1}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
T_{t}^{2}=T+t\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are equivalent, then
\mathfrak{T}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{T}_{2}
are in the same cohomology class in
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
.

5.2.2.
Higher Order deformations of relative Rota-Baxter operators of nonzero weights on Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this subsection, we introduce a special cohomology class associated to an order
n
deformation of a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
, and show that a deformation of order
n
of a a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
is extendable if and only if this cohomology class in the second cohomology group is trivial. Thus this kind of special cohomology class is called the obstruction class of an order
n
deformation being extendable.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. If
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
with
\mathfrak{T}_{0}=T
,
\mathfrak{T}_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, defines a
\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^{n+1})
-module from
\mathfrak{h}[t]/(t^{n+1})
to the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t]/(t^{n+1})
satisfying

(44)

\displaystyle[T_{t}u,T_{t}v]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{t}\Big(\rho(T_{t}u)v-\rho(T_{t}v)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

(45)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket T_{t}u,T_{t}v,T_{t}w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{t}\Big(D(T_{t}u,T_{t}v)w+\mu(T_{t}v,T_{t}w)u-\mu(T_{t}u,T_{t}w)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
. we say that
T_{t}
is an
order
n
deformation
of
T
.

Remark 5.12
.

The left hand sides of Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) hold in the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}[t]/(t^{n+1})
and the right hand sides of Eqs. (
44
) and (
45
) make sense since
T_{t}
is a
\mathbb{K}[t]/(t^{n+1})
-module map.

Definition 5.13
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
T
. If there exists a
1
-cochain
\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
such that
\widetilde{T_{t}}=T_{t}+\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
is an order
n+1
deformation of
T
, then we say that
T_{t}
is
extendable
.

The following theorem is the key conclusion in this section.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. Let
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
T
. Then
T_{t}
is extendable if and only if the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
is trivial, where
\mathsf{Ob}^{T}=(\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{T},\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{T})\in\mathfrak{C}_{T}^{2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{T}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop i,j\geqslant 1}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{1})v_{2}-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2})v_{1})\Big),

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{T}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1,\atop i,j,k\geqslant 1}\Big(\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}_{i}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{3}\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(D(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{2})v_{3}

\displaystyle+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{2},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{3})v_{1}-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v_{1},\mathfrak{T}_{k}v_{3})v_{2}\big)\Big),\quad\forall v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in h.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{T_{t}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be the extension of
T_{t}
, then for all
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{h}
,

(46)

\displaystyle[\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{T_{t}}\Big(\rho(\widetilde{T_{t}}u)v-\rho(\widetilde{T_{t}}v)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big),

(47)

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}v,\widetilde{T_{t}}w\right\rrbracket

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{T_{t}}\Big(D(\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}v)w+\mu(\widetilde{T_{t}}v,\widetilde{T_{t}}w)u-\mu(\widetilde{T_{t}}u,\widetilde{T_{t}}w)v+\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big).

Expanding the Eq.(
46
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
yields that

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop i,j\geqslant 0}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)u\big)-\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})\Big)=0,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n+1,\atop i,j\geqslant 1}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)u\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+[\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tv]+[Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v]-T\Big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v)u\Big)-\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}([u,v]_{T})=0,

i.e.,

(48)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm I}^{T}+\delta_{\rm I}^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1})=0.

Similarly, expanding the Eq.(
47
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
yields that

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n+1,\atop i,j,k\geqslant 1}\Big(\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}}-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(D(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)v\big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tv,Tw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v,Tw\right\rrbracket+\left\llbracket Tu,Tv,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}w\right\rrbracket-T\Big(D(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tv)w+D(Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v)w

\displaystyle+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}v,Tw)u+\mu(Tv,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}w)u-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}u,Tw)v-\mu(Tu,\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}w)v\Big)-\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}(\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{T})=0,

which implies that

(49)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ob}_{\rm II}^{T}+\delta_{\rm II}^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1})=0.

From (
48
) and (
49
), we get

\mathsf{Ob}^{T}=-\delta^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}).

Thus, the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]
is trivial.

Conversely, suppose that the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]
is trivial, then there exists
\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{1}(V,\mathfrak{g})
, such that
\mathsf{Ob}^{T}=-\delta^{T}(\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}).
Set
\widetilde{T_{t}}=T_{t}+\mathfrak{T}_{n+1}t^{n+1}
. Then for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant n+1
,
\widetilde{T_{t}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=s}\Big([\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v]-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u)v-\rho(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v)u\big)-\mathfrak{T}_{s}([u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}})\Big)=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=s}\Big(\left\llbracket\mathfrak{T}_{i}u,\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w\right\rrbracket-\mathfrak{T}_{i}\big(D(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}v)w+\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}v,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)u-\mu(\mathfrak{T}_{j}u,\mathfrak{T}_{k}w)v\big)-\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\left\llbracket u,v,w\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})\Big)=0.

which implies that
\widetilde{T_{t}}
is an order
n+1
deformation of
T
. Hence it is a extension of
T_{t}
.
∎

Definition 5.15
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
, and
T_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\mathfrak{T}_{i}t^{i}
be an order
n
deformation of
T
. Then the cohomology class
[\mathsf{Ob}^{T}]\in\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
defined in Theorem
5.14
is called the
obstruction class
of
T_{t}
being extendable.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
T:\mathfrak{h}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be a a relative Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
from a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{h}})
to another Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\left\llbracket\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\right\rrbracket_{\mathfrak{g}})
with respect to an action
(\rho,\mu)
. If
\mathcal{H}_{T}^{2}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})=0
, then every
1
-cocycle in
\mathcal{Z}_{T}^{1}(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
is the infinitesimal of some formal deformation of
T
.

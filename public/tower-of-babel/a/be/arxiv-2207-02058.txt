Best Subset Selection with Efficient Primal-Dual Algorithm


Abstract
1 1 1 The initial version of the paper was submitted in February 2020. Best subset selection is considered the ‘gold standard’ for many sparse learning problems. A variety of optimization techniques have been proposed to attack this non-convex and NP-hard problem. In this paper, we investigate the dual forms of a family of ℓ 0 \ell_{0} -regularized problems. An efficient primal-dual method has been developed based on the primal and dual problem structures. By leveraging the dual range estimation along with the incremental strategy, our algorithm potentially reduces redundant computation and improves the solutions of best subset selection. Theoretical analysis and experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets validate the efficiency and statistical properties of the proposed solutions.



1
Introduction

Sparse learning is a standard approach to alleviate model over-fitting issues when the feature dimension is larger than the number of training samples. With a training set
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is the sample feature and
y_{i}
is the corresponding label, this paper focuses on the following generalized best subset selection problem,

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}F(\beta)=f(\beta)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0},

(1)

where

\displaystyle f(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}l(\beta^{\top}x_{i},y_{i})+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||_{2}^{2}.

Here
l(\cdot)
is a convex function,
\beta
is the model parameter, and
\lambda_{0}
,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are hyper-parameters/tuning parameters. It is well-known that an
\ell_{0}
solver (
\lambda_{0}>0,\lambda_{1}=0
) has superior statistical properties when the
signal-to-noise ratio
(SNR) is high, but it may suffer over-fitting issues when SNR is low
(
Gamarnik and Zadik 2017
;
Mazumder et al. 2022
)
. The continuous-shrinkage solvers e.g., ridge/Lasso (
\lambda_{0}=0,\lambda_{1}>0
), can perform better in this case compared with
\ell_{0}
solver
(
Mazumder et al. 2022
;
Hastie et al. 2017
)
. Combinations of there hyper-parameters may adjust the model to work well in different noise levels.
Mazumder et al. 2022
;
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
used ridge/Lasso to improve
\ell_{0}
solutions and achieve better or comparable solutions with less nonzeros.

Solving the vanilla
\ell_{0}
regularized problem is known to be NP-hard
(
Natarajan 1995
)
. By leveraging the significant computational advances in mixed integer optimization (MIO),
Bertsimas et al. 2016
performed near optimal solutions to a special case of problem (
1
), for
\lambda_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{2}=0
. This method scaled up solutions to cases where feature sizes are much larger than what were considered possible in the community
(
Furnival and Wilson 1974
;
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
)
. Their approach can achieve approximate optimality via dual bounds but with the cost of longer computation times.
Bertsimas and Van Parys 2020
showed that cutting plane methods for subset selection can work well with mild sample correlations and a succinctly large
n
.

Different from the soft regularized ridge/Lasso problem given by (
1
), Iterative Hard Thresholding (IHT)
(
Blumensath and Davies 2009
;
Foucart 2011
;
Yuan et al. 2014
;
Shen and Li 2017
;
Yuan and Li 2020
)
has often been used to solve
k
-sparse problems (
2
),

\displaystyle\min_{||\beta||_{0}\leq k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}l(\beta^{\top}x_{i},y_{i})+\lambda_{2}||\beta||_{2}^{2}\ .

(2)

In
Blumensath and Davies 2009
;
Foucart 2011
, the authors demonstrated that IHT can be applied to compute the compressed sensing problem. IHT-based approaches have been studied by many researchers in the context of sparse learning problems
(
Yuan et al. 2014
;
Jain et al. 2014
;
Jain et al. 2016
;
Yuan and Li 2020
)
. IHT methods require a specific value of the features number (
k
) to start the algorithm. Many other approaches have also been developed to tackle the
\ell_{0}
regularized problems
(
Mazumder and Radchenko 2017
;
Mazumder et al. 2022
;
Soussen et al. 2015
;
Bian and Chen 2020
;
Dedieu et al. 2021
;
Yang and Yu 2019
;
Dong et al. 2015
;
Hazimeh et al. 2020
;
Zhu et al. 2020
)
.

Apart from
\ell_{0}
solvers, extremely efficient and optimized
\ell_{1}
-regularization (Lasso) solvers can solve an entire regularization path (with a hundred values of the tuning parameter) in usually less than a second
(
Friedman et al. 2010
)
. Screening and coordinate incremental techniques
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Massias et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2020
)
can further scale the solutions to large datasets. Compared to popular efficient solvers for Lasso, it seems that the high computation cost for using
\ell_{0}
regularized models
(
Bertsimas et al. 2016
)
might discourage practitioners from adopting global optimization-based solvers of (
1
) to daily analysis applications
(
Hastie et al. 2017
;
Mazumder et al. 2022
;
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
)
. However, it is known
(
Loh and Wainwright 2017
;
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
)
that there is a significant gap in the statistical quality of solutions that can be achieved via Lasso (and its variants) and near-optimal solutions to non-convex subset-selection type procedures. The choice of algorithm can significantly affect the quality of solutions obtained. On many instances, algorithms that do a better job in optimizing the non-convex subset-selection criterion (
1
) result in superior-quality statistical estimators (for example, in terms of support recovery
(
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
)
).

Several recent studies attempt to further improve the efficiency of
\ell_{0}
solvers. Along the line of dual methods,
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
recently studied the duality of
k
-sparse problem, and they proved the strong duality of
k
-sparse problem. With the derived dual form by
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
, a dual space based hard-thresholding method has been proposed by the authors. In addition, a screening method has been proposed by
Atamtürk and Gomez 2020
. Following coordinate descent (CD) methods
(
Breheny and Huang 2011
;
Mazumder et al. 2011
;
Friedman et al. 2010
;
Nesterov 2012
)
for linear regression problems,
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
proposed an efficient CD based method to scale up the solutions of problem (
1
). Their method can be improved with the proposed switch techniques that aim to escape from local solutions.
Additionally, the combination of
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{q}
(
q=1
or 2) are considered in many existing literature. See
Liu and Wu 2007
;
Soubies et al. 2017
for theoretical analyses and details.

Following the studies in
Pilanci et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
, we investigate the dual form of the generalized sparse problem (
1
). Under mild conditions, a strong duality theory has been established for problem (
1
). A primal-dual algorithm is proposed to further improve the efficiency and quality of solutions by leveraging the exploration in the dual space along with coordinate screening and active incremental techniques
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2017
;
Atamtürk and Gomez 2020
;
Massias et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2020
)
. In summary, our contributions on theoretical side are three-folds. We first establish the duality theorem for the generalized non-convex sparse learning problem (
1
). We second demonstrate that the derived duality allows us to adopt the screening and coordinate incremental strategies
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2017
;
Massias et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2020
)
in
\ell_{1}
solvers to boost the efficiency of the proposed
\ell_{0}
algorithm.
Thirdly, we provide theoretical study of the proposed algorithms. Our theoretical analysis shows that the generalized sparse problem (
1
) can be solved within polynomial computation complexity.
Experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets show the advantages of our method.

In Section

A discussion is given in Section

2
Duality Theory of Sparse Learning

This section extends the duality studies in
Pilanci et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
to the generalized sparse learning problem (
1
).
Let
X=[x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}]^{\top}
be the feature matrix,
y=[y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{n}]^{\top}
be the response vector and
n
is the number of samples.
Let
l^{*}_{i}(\alpha_{i})=\max_{u\in\mathcal{F}}\{\alpha_{i}u-l_{i}(u)\}
be the Fenchel conjugate
(
Fenchel 1949
)
of convex loss function
l_{i}(u)
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be the feasible set of
\alpha_{i}
regarding
l^{*}_{i}()
. According to the expression
l_{i}(u)=\max_{\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{F}}\{\alpha_{i}u-l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})\}
, the primal problem can be reformulated into

\displaystyle\min_{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max_{\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\big(\alpha_{i}\beta^{\top}x_{i}-l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})\big)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}.

(3)

We use
L(\beta,\alpha)
to represent the following objective

\displaystyle~L(\beta,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\alpha_{i}\beta^{\top}x_{i}-l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})\big)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}.

(4)

Similar to the studies in
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
, the RIP (restricted strong condition number) bound conditions are not explicitly required here. Without specifying
k
in (
2
), our duality theory is close to the standard duality paradigm, and thus generic primal-dual methods may be used to further improve the solvers.

2.1
Strong Duality

We establish the duality theory that guarantees the original non-convex in (
1
) can be solved in a dual space. Following
Yuan et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2017
, we define the saddle point for the Lagrangian (
4
) of the generalized sparse learning (
1
).

Definition 2.1
.

(Saddle Point). A pair
(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathcal{F}^{n}
is said to be a saddle point for
L
(
4
) if the following holds

\displaystyle L(\bar{\beta},\alpha)\leq L(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})\leq L(\beta,\bar{\alpha}).

(5)

Different from the sparse saddle point in
Yuan et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2017
that requires
k
-sparse regarding the primal variable, the saddle point defined here can be taken as a generalized saddle point. Given
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}
, we further define
\eta(\alpha):=-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}=-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}X^{\top}\alpha
,
\eta_{0}:=\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}
, and

\displaystyle\beta_{j}(\alpha):=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\alpha)):=\begin{cases}\mathrm{sign}\big(\eta_{j}(\alpha)\big)\big(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}\big)&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|>\eta_{0}\\
\big\{0,\mathrm{sign}\big(\eta_{j}(\alpha)\big)\big(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}\big)\big\}&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|=\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

(6)

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|>\eta_{0}\\
\big\{0,-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}\big\}\quad&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|=\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

(7)

The following theorem establishes the duality theory regarding the generalized sparse problem (
1
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\bar{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a primal vector and
\bar{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}^{n}
regarding L, then

1.

(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})
is a saddle point of
L
if and only if the following conditions hold:

(a)

\bar{\beta}
solves the primal problem;

(b)

\bar{\alpha}\in[\partial l_{1}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{1}),\partial l_{2}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{2}),...,\partial l_{n}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{n})]^{\top}
;

(c)

\bar{\beta}_{j}=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\bar{\alpha}))
.

2.

The mini-max relationship

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}\min_{\beta}L(\beta,\alpha)=\min_{\beta}\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}L(\beta,\alpha).

(8)

holds if and only if there exists a saddle point
(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})
for L.

3.

The corresponding dual problem of (
1
) is written as

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}D(\alpha)=\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),

(9)

where
l^{*}
is the conjugate function of

\beta_{j}(\alpha)=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\alpha))

4.

(Strong duality)
\bar{\alpha}
solves the dual problem in (
22

D(\bar{\alpha})\geq D(\alpha),\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}
, and
P(\bar{\beta})=D(\bar{\alpha})

(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})
satisfies the three conditions given by (a)
\sim
(c).

Here

is the threshold which controls the sparsity of the solution. Larger

and

The mini-max result in Theorem
2.1

2.1
-2 can be used to establish the duality theory, and it is the min-max side of the problem, and it provides sufficient and necessary conditions under which one can safely exchange a min-max for a max-min regarding

Remark 2.1
.

Applying Theorem

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}\min_{\beta}L(\beta,\alpha)=\min_{\beta}\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}L(\beta,\alpha).

(10)

2.2
Properties of Generalized Sparse Learning

In this paper, we study the duality of generalized sparse learning problem. Based on the strong duality of
\ell_{0}
problem (
1
), screening methods
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2017
)
and coordinate increasing techniques
(
Massias et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2020
)
can be implemented to gain an improvement in algorithm efficiency. Following the Gap screening
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
)
for Lasso problem, we have the following theorem regarding the duality gap.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that the primal loss functions
\{l_{i}(\cdot)\}_{i=1}^{n}
are
1/\mu
-strongly smooth. The range of the dual variable is bounded via the duality gap value, i.e.,
\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n},\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\{B(\alpha;r):||\alpha-\bar{\alpha}||_{2}\leq r,r=\sqrt{\frac{2(P(\beta)-D(\alpha))}{\gamma}}\}
.
Here
\gamma
is a positive constant and
\gamma\geq\mu
.

Let
x_{\cdot i}
be the
i
th column of X, according to the definition of
\mathfrak{B}
in (
6
), the activity of feature
j
is determined by the magnitude of
\eta_{j}(\bar{\alpha})
, i.e.,
|\eta_{j}(\bar{\alpha})|=\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}|x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\bar{\alpha}|
. With the ball region estimation for
\bar{\alpha}
in Theorem
2.2
, we can estimate the activity of a feature with the value of current
\alpha
. Let
r=\sqrt{\frac{2(P(\beta)-D(\alpha))}{\gamma}}
be the radius of the estimated ball range for
\bar{\alpha}
using current
\alpha
and
\beta
solutions. Then

\big||x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\alpha|-||x_{\cdot j}||_{2}r\big|\leq|x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\bar{\alpha}|\leq|x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\alpha|+||x_{\cdot j}||_{2}r
and
we get
|\eta_{i}(\bar{\alpha})|\leq\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\big(|x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\alpha|+||x_{\cdot j}||_{2}r\big)<\eta_{0}\implies x_{\cdot j}\ \text{is an inactive feature}.

It implies

\displaystyle\text{Upper Bound}:|x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\alpha|+||x_{\cdot j}||_{2}r<2\lambda_{2}\eta_{0}=2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\implies j\notin\mathrm{supp}(\bar{\beta}),

\displaystyle\text{Lower Bound}:\big||x^{\top}_{\cdot j}\alpha|-||x_{\cdot j}||_{2}r\big|>2\lambda_{2}\eta_{0}=2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\implies j\in\mathrm{supp}(\bar{\beta}).

According to the derived dual objective (
22
) and equations (
6
)-(
7
), a feature’s activity is determined by its product with the optimal dual variable
\bar{\alpha}
, e.g.,
\eta_{j}(\bar{\alpha})
for feature
j
. The dual range estimation
\bar{\alpha}
(
B(\bar{\alpha};r)
) allows us to perform feature screen in order to improve algorithm efficiency by following the approach for Lasso
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2017
)
. As the support set
S
is unknown, we just set
\gamma=\mu
to ensure the safety of the feature screening. Here safety means the screening operation does not remove any feature belonging to
S=\mathrm{supp}(\bar{\beta})
. The framework proposed in this paper lays a broader bridge between screening methods and the solutions of
\ell_{0}
regularized problems.

3
Algorithm

With the mini-max relationship in Theorem
2.1
regarding Lagrangian form
L(\beta,\alpha)
(
4
) , we first develop a primal-dual algorithm to update both
\alpha
and
\beta
. The dual objective
D(\alpha)
is a non-smooth function as the term
\Psi()
regarding
\alpha
is non-smooth due to the truncation operation. We focus on the following simplified dual form

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\ \text{and}

(12)

\displaystyle\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|\geq\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

(13)

The primal dual link is

\displaystyle\beta_{j}(\alpha)=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\alpha))=\begin{cases}\mathrm{sign}\big(\eta_{j}(\alpha)\big)\big(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}\big)&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|\geq\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

(14)

The super-gradient regarding the dual variable
\nabla_{\alpha}D(\alpha)=X\beta(\alpha)-{l^{*}}^{{}^{\prime}}(\alpha)
can be taken as the partial derivative of
L(\beta,\alpha)
. We give the dual problems of two objective functions in the supplements, and we will focus on linear regression to present the proposed algorithms.

3.1
Primal-dual Updating for Linear Regression

We use linear regression as an example to illustrate the proposed primal-dual inner solver of
\ell_{0}
regularized problems.
For least square problem, the primal form is

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}P(\beta)=f(\beta)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0},\ \text{and}\ f(\beta)=\frac{1}{2}||y-X\beta||^{2}_{2}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||_{2}^{2}.

For least square problem,
l_{i}(u;y_{i})=\frac{1}{2}(y_{i}-u)^{2}
, then

l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\big((y_{i}+\alpha_{i})^{2}-y_{i}^{2}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{i}^{2}+y_{i}\alpha_{i}
. Here
u=x_{i}^{\top}\beta
. Thus the dual problem is

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha}D(\alpha)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\top}\alpha-y^{\top}\alpha+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi(-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{i}x_{i};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}).

(15)

The corresponding super-gradient can be easily computed, i.e.,

g_{\alpha}=\nabla_{\alpha}D(\alpha)=X\beta-Y-\alpha.
After the super gradient ascent for the dual variables, we apply the primal-dual link function to get the variable in the primal space.

The dual objective
D(\alpha)
is non-smooth. The super-gradient
g_{\alpha}=\nabla_{\alpha}D(\alpha)
can be improved with a more accurate primal variable estimation for the Lagrangian form (
4
) with the mini-max relationship in Theorem
2.1
.
We use coordinate descent (CD)
(
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020

The screening operation (

6
Discussion

In this section, we first give several additional remarks on the method, and then we compare our approach with some existing methods.

6.1
Additional Remarks

Technical Contribution:
This paper investigates the dual form and strong duality of the generalized sparse learning problem (
1
) by following
Pilanci et al. 2015
;
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
. The generalized form could overcome over-fitting issues of
\ell_{0}
regularized problems when the data SNRs are low
(
Gamarnik and Zadik 2017
;
Mazumder et al. 2022
)
. A primal-dual framework has been developed to further scale up the solutions of
\ell_{0}
regularized problems based on the derived dual form. Moreover, the proposed framework considers active coordinate incremental and screening strategies
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2017
;
Atamtürk and Gomez 2020
;
Massias et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2020
)
by leveraging the duality structure properties of problem (
1
). The quality of solutions can be evaluated by the duality gap (
11
) with the current dual solution
\alpha
calculated through Remark
2.3
.

Saddle Point:
Different from the sparse saddle point defined in
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
that requires
k
-sparse regarding the primal variable, the saddle point in this paper can be taken as a standard saddle point. Without the specified
k
, our duality theory is more close to the standard duality paradigm, and hence some generic primal-dual methods can be employed to further improve the solver. The methodology developed here can be easily extended to plain
\ell_{1}
or
\ell_{0}
problems (with the
\ell_{2}
term), group sparse structures, fused sparse structures, or even more complex and mixed sparse structures that we cannot or do not need to specify the
k
values.

Strong Duality:
Strong duality holds when both the primal and dual variables reach the optimal values. A closer distance between the current estimation
\alpha
and the optimal value
\bar{\alpha}
gives a smaller duality gap. Once the support of
\bar{\beta}
is recovered, the objective function becomes a convex function since
\|\beta^{s}\|_{0}
remains a constant. According to our theoretical analysis, the saddle point of problem (
1
) could be attained within polynomial computation complexity with a decreasing step size.
When
l(\cdot)
takes some special convex function, strong duality holds. However, for general
l(\cdot)
, it is hard to verify the strong duality.

6.2
Comparisons

We provide more details on the differences between our work and related works in this section to highlight our technical contributions.

The proposed method is significantly different from Dual-IHT method
(
Liu et al. 2017
;
Yuan et al. 2020
)
. Firstly, the proposed primal-dual algorithm focuses on a different problem (
1
) compared with the Dual-IHT’s objective (
2
). Apart from using soft regularization rather than hard constraint, (
1
) also includes the
\ell_{1}
penalty that could be helpful in the cases with low SNR values. Secondly, our primal-dual method perform updating in both primal and dual spaces to approach the saddle point, and it can potentially attain solutions with smaller duality gaps. Finally, most important of all, our objective (
1
) and the derived dual form (
22
) allow us to employ screening and coordinate incremental strategies
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Massias et al. 2018
;
Ren et al. 2020
;
Atamtürk and Gomez 2020
)
to boost the efficiency of the algorithm.

There are several obvious differences between our primal-dual method and the coordinate descent with spacer steps (CDSS) method
(
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
)
. Different from our primal-dual method, CDSS utilizes coordinate descent in the primal space for parameter updating. In CDSS
(
Hazimeh and Mazumder 2020
)
, they also rely on partial swap inescapable (PSI-
\kappa
) to improve the solution with
\kappa=1
. PSI with
\kappa\geq 2
will introduce much more extra computation that is usually not affordable. Our primal-dual method employs coordinate incremental strategy to save computation cost, and experimental results indicate that the proposed method can achieve similar solution quality as CDSS but with less computation time.

In
Atamtürk and Gomez 2020
, the authors propose a screening method for
\ell_{0}
regularized problems. However, their objective does not include the
\ell_{1}
norm. Our propose method focuses on a more generalized problem that is potentially more powerful on datasets with low SNRs. Moreover, besides the safe screening rule proposed in Section
2.2
, the proposed coordinate incremental strategy introduced in Section
3.2
is empirically effective on different datasets. The screening methods
(
Fercoq et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2015
;
Ndiaye et al. 2017
)
and coordinate incremental strategies
(
Ren et al. 2020
;
Massias et al. 2018
)
used for
\ell_{1}
regularized problems can be taken as special cases of the proposed method with
\lambda_{0}=0
.

7
Conclusion

In this paper we studied the dual forms of a broad family of
\ell_{0}
regularized problems. Based on the derived dual form, a primal-dual algorithm accelerated with active coordinate selection has been developed. Our theoretical result show that the reformed best subset selection problem can be solved within polynomial complexity. The developed theory and the proposed framework can be integrated with many screening strategies.
Experimental results show that the proposed primal-dual method can reduce redundant operations introduced by the inactive features and hence save computation costs. The proposed framework sheds light on primal-dual algorithms that can potentially further scale up the solutions of

Lemma A.1
.

For a given
\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}
, let
\beta(\alpha)=\argmin_{\beta}L(\beta,\alpha)
. We have

\displaystyle\min_{\beta}L(\beta,\alpha)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),

where

\displaystyle\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|>\eta_{0}\\
\big\{0,-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}\big\}\quad&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|=\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

To be specific,
l^{*}
is the conjugate function of
l
,
\eta(\alpha):=-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}
and

\eta_{0}:=\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}
. The link function between
\alpha
and
\beta
is

\displaystyle~~~~\beta_{j}(\alpha)=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\alpha))=\begin{cases}\mathrm{sign}\big(\eta_{j}(\alpha)\big)\big(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}\big)&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|>\eta_{0}\\
\big\{0,\mathrm{sign}\big(\eta_{j}(\alpha)\big)\big(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}\big)\big\}&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|=\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

Proof.

With
l^{*}
as the conjugate of
l
, the primal problem can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}L(\beta,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\alpha_{i}\beta^{\top}x_{i}-l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})\big)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\alpha_{i}\beta^{\top}x_{i}-l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})\big)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\alpha_{i}\beta^{\top}x_{i}\big)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{ij}\big)\beta_{j}+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Phi(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{ij};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}).

Let
\tau_{j}=\alpha^{\top}x_{\cdot j}
,
then

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau_{j};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=

\displaystyle\min_{\beta_{j}}\tau_{j}\beta_{j}+\lambda_{0}\mathbf{I}\{\beta_{j}\neq 0\}+\lambda_{1}|\beta_{j}|+\lambda_{2}\beta_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\beta_{j}}\lambda_{2}\bigg(\beta_{j}^{2}+\frac{\tau_{j}}{\lambda_{2}}\beta_{j}\bigg)^{2}+\lambda_{0}\mathbf{I}\{\beta_{j}\neq 0\}+\lambda_{1}|\beta_{j}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{\beta_{j}}\lambda_{2}\bigg(\beta_{j}+\frac{\tau_{j}}{2\lambda_{2}}\bigg)^{2}+\lambda_{0}\mathbf{I}\{\beta_{j}\neq 0\}+\lambda_{1}|\beta_{j}|-\frac{\tau_{j}^{2}}{4\lambda_{2}}.

Let

\displaystyle h(u)=\lambda_{2}\bigg(u+\frac{\tau_{j}}{2\lambda_{2}}\bigg)^{2}+\lambda_{0}\mathbf{I}\{u\neq 0\}+\lambda_{1}|u|-\frac{\tau_{j}^{2}}{4\lambda_{2}}.

If
u\neq 0
, we have the soft-thresholding

\displaystyle\hat{u}=-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\mathrm{sign}(\tau_{j})\max(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1},0).

With
|\tau_{j}|>\lambda_{1}
,

\displaystyle h(\hat{u})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}\bigg(-\frac{\tau_{j}-\lambda_{1}\mathrm{sign}(\tau_{j})}{2\lambda_{2}}+\frac{\tau_{j}}{2\lambda_{2}}\bigg)^{2}+\lambda_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1})-\frac{\tau_{j}^{2}}{4\lambda^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}+\frac{\lambda_{1}|\tau_{j}|}{2\lambda_{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{1}^{2}}{4\lambda_{2}}-\frac{\tau_{j}^{2}}{4\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}\big(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\tau_{j}^{2}-2\lambda_{1}|\tau_{j}|\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}\big(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1}\big)^{2}.

With
h(\hat{u})<h(0)=0
, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}<\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}\big(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1}\big)^{2}\implies|\tau_{j}|>2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}.

Hence,
\hat{u}
is the minimizer when
|\tau_{j}|>2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau_{j};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}\big(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1}\big)^{2}+\lambda_{0}.

When
2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}=|\tau_{j}|
, both
\hat{u}
and 0 are
minimizer. Then

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau_{j};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\{0,-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1})^{2}+\lambda_{0}\}.

If
2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}>|\tau_{j}|
, 0 is the minimizer, and
\Psi(\tau_{j},\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=0
.

The optimal primal can be written as

\displaystyle\beta^{*}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{A}(\tau_{j};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{sign}(\tau_{j})\frac{\lambda_{1}-|\tau_{j}|}{2\lambda_{2}}&\text{if}\ \ |\tau_{j}|>2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\\
\{0,\mathrm{sign}(\tau_{j})\frac{\lambda_{1}-|\tau_{j}|}{2\lambda_{2}}\}\quad&\text{if}\ \ |\tau_{j}|=2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\tau_{j}|<2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\alpha)).

Here
\eta_{0}=\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}}
.
With the optimal
\beta(\alpha)
,
L(\beta,\alpha)
can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau_{j};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1})^{2}+\lambda_{0}&\text{if}\ \ |\tau_{j}|>2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\\
\{0,-\frac{1}{4\lambda_{2}}(|\tau_{j}|-\lambda_{1})^{2}+\lambda_{0}\}\quad&\text{if}\ \ |\tau_{j}|=2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\tau_{j}|<2\sqrt{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{1}\end{cases}.

Alternatively,

\displaystyle\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\Phi(\tau_{j};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|>\eta_{0}\\
\big\{0,-\lambda_{2}(|\eta_{j}(\alpha)|-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2\lambda_{2}})^{2}+\lambda_{0}\big\}\quad&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|=\eta_{0}\\
0&\text{if}\ \ |\eta_{j}(\alpha)|<\eta_{0}\end{cases}.

Here
\eta(\alpha)=-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}x_{i}

Lemma A.2
.

(Saddle Point). Let
\bar{\beta}
be a primal vector and
\bar{\alpha}
a dual vector. Then
(\bar{\alpha},\bar{\beta})
is a saddle point of
L

\bar{\beta}
solves the primal problem;

b)
\bar{\alpha}\in[\partial l_{1}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{1}),\partial l_{2}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{2}),...,\partial l_{n}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{n})]^{\top}

c)
\bar{\beta}_{j}=\mathfrak{B}(\eta_{j}(\bar{\alpha}))

\Leftarrow
: If the pair
(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})

\displaystyle P(\bar{\beta})=\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}L(\bar{\beta},\alpha)\leq L(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha})\leq\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}L(\beta,\bar{\alpha}).

On the other hand, we know that for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}

\displaystyle L(\beta,\alpha)\leq\max_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}L(\beta,\alpha^{\prime})=P(\beta).

\displaystyle P(\bar{\beta})\leq\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}L(\beta,\bar{\alpha})\leq\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}P(\beta)\leq P(\bar{\beta}).

\bar{\beta}
solves the primal problem , which proves the necessary condition (a). Moreover, the above arguments lead to

\displaystyle P(\bar{\beta})=\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}L(\bar{\beta},\alpha)=L(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha}).

Then from the maximizing argument property of convex conjugate we have
\bar{\alpha}_{i}\in\partial l_{i}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{i})
, and it concludes condition b).
Note that

\displaystyle L(\beta,\bar{\alpha})=\lambda_{2}\bigg|\bigg|\beta+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{i}x_{i}\bigg|\bigg|^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}^{*}(\bar{\alpha}_{i})+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+C.

(18)

\bar{F}=\mathrm{supp}(\bar{\beta})

, with

.
This validates the condition (c).

\Rightarrow

\bar{\beta}
is a solution to the primal problem (condition a)), and

\displaystyle L(\bar{\beta},\alpha)\leq P(\bar{\beta})=\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}L(\bar{\beta},\alpha)=\ L(\bar{\beta},\bar{\alpha}).

(19)

By combining the inequalities (

Let
\theta^{t}=[\partial l_{1}((\beta^{t})^{\top}x_{1}),\partial l_{2}((\beta^{t})^{\top}x_{2}),...,\partial l_{n}((\beta^{t})^{\top}x_{n})]^{\top}
. According to Lemma
B.3
,

\displaystyle P(\beta^{t})-D(\alpha^{t})\leq

\displaystyle\langle g^{t}_{\alpha^{t}},\theta^{t}-\alpha^{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||g^{t}_{\alpha^{t}}||\big(||\theta^{t}-\bar{\alpha}||+||\bar{\alpha}-\alpha^{t}||\big).

(40)

As
\bar{\alpha}\in[\partial l_{1}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{1}),\partial l_{2}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{2}),...,\partial l_{n}(\bar{\beta}^{\top}x_{n})]^{\top}
,
l_{i}()
is
\frac{1}{\mu}
smooth, and
||x_{\cdot j}||=1
, we have

\displaystyle||\theta^{t}-\bar{\alpha}||\leq\frac{1}{\mu}||\beta^{t}-\bar{\beta}||\leq\frac{\sigma_{max}(X_{S})}{2\mu\lambda_{2}}||\alpha^{t}-\bar{\alpha}||.

Therefore,

\displaystyle P(\beta^{t})-D(\alpha^{t})\leq

\displaystyle||g^{t}_{\alpha^{t}}||\big(||\theta^{t}-\bar{\alpha}||+||\bar{\alpha}-\alpha^{t}||\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{0}\bigg(1+\frac{\sigma_{max}(X_{S})}{2\mu\lambda_{2}}\bigg)||\alpha^{t}-\bar{\alpha}||.

(41)

If
t>\mathrm{max}\{t_{1},t_{2}\},t_{2}:=\frac{3c_{1}c_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\log\frac{3c_{1}c_{2}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}
, and
c_{2}:=c_{0}\bigg(1+\frac{\sigma_{max}(X_{S})}{2\mu\lambda_{2}}\bigg)
, we have
||\alpha^{t}-\bar{\alpha}||\leq\frac{\epsilon}{c_{0}\bigg(1+\frac{\sigma_{max}(X_{S})}{2\mu\lambda_{2}}\bigg)}
, and with (
41
),
P(\beta^{t})-D(\alpha^{t})\leq\epsilon
.
∎

Appendix C
Dual Problems of Some Loss Functions

C.1
Logistic Loss

The primal form of logistic regression is given by

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}F(\beta)

\displaystyle=f(\beta)+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0},

(42)

\displaystyle~~~~f(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(-y_{i}x_{i}^{\top}\beta+\log(1+\exp(x_{i}^{\top}\beta))\big)+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||_{2}^{2}.

Here
l_{i}(u;y_{i})=-y_{i}u+\log(1+\exp(u))
, and
l_{i}^{*}(\alpha_{i})=(\alpha_{i}+y_{i})\log(\alpha_{i}+y_{i})+(1-\alpha_{i}-y_{i})\log(1-\alpha_{i}-y_{i}),\alpha_{i}+y_{i}\in[0,1]
, here
u=\beta^{\top}x_{i}
.
The dual objective is

\displaystyle D(\alpha)=

\displaystyle-(\alpha+y)\log(\alpha+y)-(1-\alpha-y)\log(1-\alpha-y)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi(-\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bar{\alpha}_{i}x_{i};\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}).

Here
\Psi()
is given by (
13
). With
\alpha_{i}+y_{i}\in[0,1]
, the dual feasible project operator is
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{F}}(\alpha_{i})=\begin{cases}\alpha_{i}\quad\ \quad-y_{i}\leq\alpha\leq 1-y_{i}\\
0\quad\ \ \quad\ \alpha\leq-y_{i}\\
1\quad\ \ \quad\ \alpha\geq 1-y_{i}\\
\end{cases}.

The super gradient regarding logistic regression is

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}=\nabla_{\alpha}D(\alpha)=

\displaystyle\bigg[\beta^{\top}(\alpha)x_{1}-\log\big(\frac{\alpha_{1}+y_{1}}{1-\alpha_{1}-y_{1}}\big),...,\beta^{\top}\big(\alpha)x_{n}-\log(\frac{\alpha_{n}+y_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}-y_{n}}\big)\bigg]^{\top}.

There is no closed form of updating formula with coordinate descent regarding the primal problem (
42
). We can apply proximal algorithm
[
Parikh and Boyd 2014
]
to this type of primal loss functions.

C.2
Huber Loss

Consider a regression problem with Huber loss, i.e.,

\displaystyle\min_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}F(\beta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{l_{Huber}(y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta)\right\}+\lambda_{0}\|\beta\|_{0}+\lambda_{1}\|\beta\|_{1}+\lambda_{2}\|\beta\|_{2},

\displaystyle l_{Huber}(y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta)=\begin{cases}0,&y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta\geq 1\\
1-y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta-\gamma/2,&y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta<1-\gamma\\
\frac{1}{2\gamma}(1-y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta)^{2},&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}

with
\gamma
being some hyper tuning parameter.
The dual function of
l_{Huber}(\cdot)
is

\displaystyle l^{\ast}_{Huber}(\alpha_{i})=\begin{cases}y_{i}\alpha_{i}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\gamma\alpha^{2},&-1\leq y_{i}x_{i}^{T}\leq 0\\
+\infty,&\textrm{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Therefore, the corresponding Lagrangian function is

L(\beta,\alpha)=\sum_{i}\{\alpha_{i}x_{i}^{T}\beta-l_{Huber}^{\ast}(\alpha_{i})\}+\lambda_{0}||\beta||_{0}+\lambda_{1}||\beta||_{1}+\lambda_{2}||\beta||^{2}.

The dual problem can be written as

\displaystyle\max_{\alpha\in\mathcal{F}^{n}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}l_{Huber}^{*}(\alpha_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Psi(\eta_{j}(\alpha);\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}).

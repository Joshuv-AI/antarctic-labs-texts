Introduction

Let
(N,J,g)
be a complex manifold of real dimension
2n
,
n\geq 2
, equipped with a Hermitian metric
g
. For any
\eta\in\Omega^{p}(N)
,

J\eta(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p})=(-1)^{p}\eta(JX_{1},\ldots,JX_{p})\quad\mbox{and}\quad d^{c}\eta=-(-1)^{p}J^{-1}dJ\eta,\quad X_{1},\ldots,X_{p}\in\Gamma(TN).

The
fundamental form
is given by
\omega(.,.)=g(J.,.)
and the
Lee form
is given by
\theta=Jd^{*}\omega=-d^{*}\omega\circ J
, where for any
X_{1},\ldots,X_{p-1}\in\Gamma(TN)
,

d^{*}\eta(X_{1},\ldots,X_{p-1})=-\sum_{i=1}^{2n}\nabla^{LC}_{E_{i}}\eta(E_{i},X_{1},\ldots,X_{p-1}),

\nabla^{LC}
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
and
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{2n})
is a local
g
-orthonormal frame.
A fundamental class of Hermitian metrics is provided by Kähler metrics, satisfying
d\omega=0
. In literature, many generalizations of the Kähler condition have been introduced. Indeed,
(N,J,g)
is called:

1.

strongly Kähler with torsion
or
pluriclosed
if
dd^{c}\omega=0
, i.e.,
dJdJ\omega=0
,

2.

balanced
if
\theta=0
,

3.

locally conformally balanced
if
\theta
is closed,

4.

Gauduchon
if
d^{*}\theta=0
,

5.

locally conformally Kähler
if
d\omega=\frac{1}{n-1}\theta\wedge\omega
and if, in addition,
\nabla^{LC}\theta=0
then it is called
Vaisman
.

For general results about these generalized Kähler metrics, we refer the reader to
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

The Levi-Civita connection of
(N,g)
is the only torsion free metric connection. In general, it does not preserve the complex structure
J
, this condition forcing the metric to be Kähler. Gauduchon proved in
[
13
]
that there exists and affine line of canonical Hermitian connections (they preserve both
J
and
g
) passing through the Bismut connection and the Chern connection.
The
Bismut connection

\nabla^{B}
(also known as
Strominger connection
) is the unique Hermitian connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion and the
Chern connection

\nabla^{C}
is the unique Hermitian connection whose torsion has trivial
(1,1)
-component. For any
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TN)
,

\begin{cases}g(\nabla_{X}^{B}Y,Z)=g(\nabla_{X}^{LC}Y,Z)+\frac{1}{2}d\omega(JX,JY,JZ),\\
g(\nabla_{X}^{C}Y,Z)=g(\nabla_{X}^{LC}Y,Z)-\frac{1}{2}d\omega(JX,Y,Z).\end{cases}

(1)

Let
R^{\tau}(X,Y)=\nabla_{[X,Y]}^{\tau}-\nabla_{X}^{\tau}\nabla_{Y}^{\tau}+\nabla_{Y}^{\tau}\nabla_{X}^{\tau}
be the curvature tensor of
\nabla^{\tau}
. The
Ricci form
of
\nabla^{\tau}
is given, for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TN)
, by

\rho^{\tau}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2n}g(R^{\tau}(X,Y)E_{i},JE_{i}),

(2)

where

(E_{1},\ldots,E_{2n})
is a local
g
-orthonormal frame. It is known
[
2
]
that

\rho^{C}=\rho^{B}-dJ\theta
. Hermitian structures satisfying
\mathrm{Hol}^{0}(\nabla^{B})\subset\mathrm{SU}(n)
, or equivalently
\rho^{B}=0
, are known in literature as
Calabi-Yau with torsion
and appear in heterotic string theory, related to the Hull-Strominger system in six dimensions
[
18
,
21
,
25
]
.

On the other hand, let
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a manifold of dimension
n
endowed with a flat torsionless connection
\nabla
and a Riemannian metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Actually,
\nabla
defines an affine structure on
M
, i.e., there exists on
M
an atlas of charts such that all transition functions between charts are affine transformations of
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
. Conversely, any affine atlas defines a flat torsionless connection. We refer to the charts of this atlas as affine coordinates.

Through-out this paper, we call such triple
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
an
affine-Riemann manifold
, we denote by
D
the Levi-Civita connection of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
,
K(X,Y)=D_{[X,Y]}-D_{X}D_{Y}+D_{Y}D_{X}
its curvature,
\gamma
the
difference tensor
and
\gamma^{*}
its adjoint given, for any
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM)
, by

\gamma_{X}Y=D_{X}Y-\nabla_{X}Y\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle\gamma_{X}^{*}Y,Z\rangle=\langle Y,\gamma_{X}Z\rangle.

(3)

Their traces with respect to the metric are the vector fields given by

{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{E_{i}}E_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{*}E_{i},

(4)

where
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
is a local
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-orthonormal frame. The 1-form
\alpha
given, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, by

\alpha(X)=\langle{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma^{*}),X\rangle

(5)

is closed (see Proposition
3.1
) and it is known as the
first Koszul form
in the theory of Hessian manifolds. The vanishing of
\alpha
is equivalent to the Riemannian volume being parallel with respect to
\nabla
. We introduce also the 1-form
\xi
given, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, by

\xi(X)=\langle{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma),X\rangle.

(6)

We call
\xi
the
adjoint Koszul form
. These two 1-forms play an important role in this paper.

It is well-known (see
[
24
]
) that there is a Hermitian structure
(J_{1},g_{1})
on
TM
canonically associated to
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
and
(TM,J_{1},g_{1})
is Kähler if and only if
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is Hessian, i.e, in each system of affine coordinates
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
there exists a function
\phi
such that
\langle\partial_{x_{i}},\partial_{x_{j}}\rangle=\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}}
. This is equivalent to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
satisfying the Codazzi equation

\nabla_{X}(\langle\;,\;\rangle)(Y,Z)=\nabla_{Y}(\langle\;,\;\rangle)(X,Z),\quad X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(TM).

(7)

We will see that (
7
) is equivalent to
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
.
Actually, there is also a flat torsionless connection
\nabla^{1}
on
TM
such that
\nabla^{1}J_{1}=0
. The affine-Riemann structure
(TM,\nabla^{1},g_{1})
gives rise to a Hermitian structure
(TTM,J_{2},g_{2})
and a flat torsionless connection
\nabla^{2}
on
TTM
. By induction, we get a sequence of Hermitian structures
(T^{k}M,J_{k},g_{k})
where
T^{k}M=T(T^{k-1}M)
and
T^{1}M=TM
. Moreover, each
T^{k}M
carries a flat torsionless connection
\nabla^{k}
such that
\nabla^{k}(J_{k})=0
.

The purpose of this paper is to explore the properties of this sequence of Hermitian structures and find the conditions on
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
leading to some or all
(T^{k}M,J_{k},g_{k})
to satisfy one of the generalized Kähler conditions introduced above. This will lead to the construction of interesting classes of generalized Kähler manifolds. We find also a large class of Hermitian manifolds which are Calabi-Yau with torsion or with vanishing Chern Ricci form. We will show also that the study of the geometry of affine-Riemann manifolds is interesting in its own right and we will generalize some results obtained on Hessian manifolds.

The canonical sequence of Hermitian structures associated to an affine-Riemann manifold

In this section, we introduce the Hermitian structures and the affine connections on the sequence of tangent bundles associated to an affine-Riemann manifold and we show that these structures are left invariant when the affine-Riemann structure is left invariant.

2.1
The Hermitian structures on the sequence of tangent bundles associated to an affine-Riemann manifold

Let
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an affine-Riemann manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\pi_{1}:TM\longrightarrow M
be the canonical projection and
Q:TTM\longrightarrow TM
the connection map of
\nabla
locally given by

Q\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\partial_{\mu_{j}}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(Z_{l}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{i}\mu_{j}\Gamma_{ij}^{l}\right)\partial_{x_{l}},

where
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a system of local coordinates,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})
the associated system of coordinates on
TM
and
\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\partial_{x_{j}}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\Gamma_{ij}^{l}\partial_{x_{l}}
. Then

TTM=\ker T\pi_{1}\oplus\ker Q.

For
X\in\Gamma(TM)
, we denote by
X^{h}
its horizontal lift and by
X^{v}
its vertical lift. The flow of
X^{v}
is given by
\Phi^{X}(t,(x,u))=(x,u+tX(x))
and
X^{h}(x,u)=h^{(x,u)}(X(x))
, where
h^{(x,u)}:T_{x}M\longrightarrow\ker Q(x,u)
is the inverse of the restriction of
d\pi_{1}
to
\ker Q(x,u)
. Since the curvature of
\nabla
vanishes, for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
,

[X^{h},Y^{h}]=[X,Y]^{h},\;[X^{h},Y^{v}]=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{v}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[X^{v},Y^{v}]=0.

(8)

The connection
{\nabla^{1}}
on
TM
given by

{\nabla^{1}}_{X^{h}}Y^{h}=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{h},\;{\nabla^{1}}_{X^{h}}Y^{v}=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{v}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\nabla^{1}}_{X^{v}}Y^{h}=\nabla^{1}_{X^{v}}Y^{v}=0,

(9)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, is flat torsionless and defines an affine structure on
TM
. The tensor field
J_{1}:TTM\longrightarrow TTM
given by
J_{1}X^{h}=X^{v}
and
J_{1}X^{v}=-X^{h}
satisfies
J_{1}^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}_{TTM}
,
\nabla^{1}(J_{1})=0
and hence defines a complex structure on
TM
.

On the other hand, we define on
TM
a Riemannian metric
g_{1}
by

g_{1}(X^{h},Y^{h})=\langle X,Y\rangle\circ\pi_{1},\;g_{1}(X^{v},Y^{v})=\langle X,Y\rangle\circ\pi_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g_{1}(X^{h},Y^{v})=0,\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

This metric is Hermitian with respect to
J_{1}
and its fundamental form
\omega=g_{1}(J_{1}.,.)
satisfies

\omega(X^{h},Y^{h})=\omega(X^{v},Y^{v})=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\omega(X^{h},Y^{v})=-\omega(Y^{v},X^{h})=\langle X,Y\rangle\circ\pi_{1},\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM).

(10)

Actually, we have a sequence of Hermitian structures. The affine-Riemann manifold
(TM,\nabla^{1},g_{1})
gives rise to a Hermitian structure
(TTM,J_{2},g_{2})
and a flat torsionless connection
\nabla^{2}
on
TTM
. By induction, we get a sequence of Hermitian structures
(T^{k}M,J_{k},g_{k})
where
T^{k}M=T(T^{k-1}M)
and
T^{1}M=TM
. Moreover, each
T^{k}M
carries a flat torsionless connection
\nabla^{k}
such that
\nabla^{k}(J_{k})=0
.

2.2
The canonical sequence of Hermitian structures associated to a left invariant affine-Riemann structure

Let
(G,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an affine-Riemann manifold such that
G
is a connected Lie group and
(\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
are left invariant. Let
(\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G,[\;,\;])
be the Lie algebra of
G
. For any

, we denote by
a^{-}
the left invariant vector field on

associated to

. The affine connection
\nabla
defines a product
\bullet
on

(a\bullet b)^{-}=(\nabla_{a^{-}}b^{-})(e),\quad a,b\in\mathfrak{g}.

This product is Lie-admissible, i.e.,
a\bullet b-b\bullet a=[a,b]
and left symmetric, i.e., for any
a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathrm{ass}(a,b,c)=\mathrm{ass}(b,a,c),

where
\mathrm{ass}(a,b,c)=(a\bullet b)\bullet c-a\bullet(b\bullet c)
.
This is equivalent to
\mathrm{L}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
,
a\mapsto\mathrm{L}_{a}
is a representation of Lie algebras, where
\mathrm{L}_{a}b=a\bullet b
,

Put

\Phi(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}
and define on
\Phi(\mathfrak{g})
a product
\star
, a bracket
[\;,\;]_{\Phi}
, an isomorphism
J:\Phi(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow\Phi(\mathfrak{g})
and a scalar product
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\phi}
by

\displaystyle(a,b)\star(c,d)=(a\bullet c,a\bullet d),\quad[(a,b),(c,d)]_{\Phi}=([a,c],a\bullet d-c\bullet b),

(11)

\displaystyle J(a,b)=(-b,a)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle_{\Phi}=\langle a,c\rangle+\langle b,d\rangle,

(12)

for any

.
It is easy to check that

is left symmetric and hence its commutator which is
[\;,\;]_{\Phi}
is a Lie bracket.
Moreover, for any

On the other hand, let

\theta_{2}=\pi_{2}^{*}(\alpha_{1}-\xi_{1})=\pi_{2}^{*}\circ\pi_{1}(3\alpha-\xi),\;\xi_{2}=\pi_{2}^{*}(\xi_{1}-\alpha_{1})=-\theta_{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha_{2}=2\pi_{2}^{*}(\alpha_{1})=2^{2}\pi_{2}^{*}\circ\pi_{1}(\alpha).

By induction, we get all the desired formulas.
∎

In this section, we give the conditions so that

Let us compute

Proposition 4.1
.

For any

\begin{cases}dJ_{1}d\omega(X^{h},Y^{h},Z^{h},U^{h})=dJ_{1}d\omega(X^{v},Y^{v},Z^{v},U^{v})=dJ_{1}d\omega(X^{h},Y^{h},Z^{h},U^{v})=dJ_{1}d\omega(X^{h},Y^{v},Z^{v},U^{v})=0,\\

Proof.

In
[
24
, Theorem 8.8 pp. 162]
, Shima proved that if
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a compact Hessian manifold such that its first Koszul form vanishes then
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Note that in this case the first Koszul form and the dual Koszul form coincide. The following theorem is a generalization of this result under an additional assumption, namely,
\nabla
is complete. It will be interesting to see if we can drop this assumption.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an affine-Riemann manifold such that
(TM,J_{1},g_{1})
is pluriclosed and the dual Koszul form of
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
vanishes. Then the Ricci curvature of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is nonnegative. Moreover, if
M
is compact and
\nabla
is complete then
\gamma=0
, i.e.,
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
.

Proof.

Note that the vanishing of dual Koszul form is equivalent to
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)=0
. From the relation

K(X,Y)=\gamma_{X}^{*}\circ\gamma_{Y}-\gamma_{Y}^{*}\circ\gamma_{X}

and the fact that

{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)=0
, we deduce that the Ricci curvature of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is given by

{\mathrm{ric}}(X,X)={\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma_{X}^{*}\circ\gamma_{X})\geq 0

and
{\mathrm{ric}}(X,X)=0
if and only if
\gamma_{X}=0
.
By using the splitting theorem of J. Cheeger and D. Gromoll (see for instance
[
6
, Corollary 6.67 pp. 168]
), we deduce that if
M
is compact its universal Riemannian covering is isometric to a Riemannian product
(\overline{M}\times\hbox{\bb R}^{d},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{1}\times\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0})
where is
\overline{M}
is compact and
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0}
is the canonical metric of
\hbox{\bb R}^{d}
. But if
\nabla
is complete the universal covering of
M
is diffeomorphic to
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.1

. Moreover, the Ricci form is given by

We have

\displaystyle R^{B}(X^{h},Y^{h})Z^{h}

\displaystyle=(K(X,Y)Z)^{h}

\displaystyle R^{B}(X^{h},Y^{h})Z^{v}

\displaystyle=(K(X,Y)Z)^{v},

\displaystyle R^{B}(X^{v},Y^{v})Z^{v}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{B}_{X^{v}}(\gamma_{Z}^{*}Y)^{h}-\nabla^{B}_{Y^{v}}(\gamma_{Z}^{*}X)^{h}=(\gamma_{\gamma_{Z}^{*}Y}^{*}X)^{v}-(\gamma_{\gamma_{Z}^{*}X}^{*}Y)^{v},

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{B}_{X^{v}}(\gamma_{Z}^{*}Y)^{v}+\nabla^{B}_{Y^{v}}(\gamma_{Z}^{*}X)^{v}=(\gamma_{\gamma_{Z}^{*}Y}^{*}X)^{h}-(\gamma_{\gamma_{Z}^{*}X}^{*}Y)^{h},

\langle\;,\;\rangle

\displaystyle 2\rho^{B}(X^{h},Y^{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(g_{1}(R^{B}(X^{v},Y^{v})E_{i}^{h},E_{i}^{v})-g_{1}(R^{B}(X^{v},Y^{v})E_{i}^{v},E_{i}^{h})\right)=0,

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(g_{1}(R^{B}(X^{h},Y^{v})E_{i}^{h},E_{i}^{v})-g_{1}(R^{B}(X^{h},Y^{v})E_{i}^{v},E_{i}^{h})\right)

Proposition 5.3
.

K(u,v)=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]
.

A direct computation using Maple shows that when

there is no solution and in the second case we find the

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be simply-connected rigid affine-Riemann manifold with
\langle\;,\;\rangle
complete. Then:

1.

If
\dim M=2
then
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is flat.

2.

If
\dim M=3
then either
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is flat or
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
isomorphic to
\hbox{\bb R}\times S^{2}(r)
endowed with the rigid structure given in Proposition
7.4
or to the left invariant rigid structure given in Proposition
7.5
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\dim M=2
. Note first that the vector fields
({\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma),{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma^{*}))
are
D
-parallel. If
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)={\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma^{*})=0
then we can apply Theorem

to get that the curvature of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
vanishes. If
({\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma),{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma^{*}))\not=(0,0)
then there is a non zero
D
-parallel vector field on

and hence the curvature of
D
vanishes.

Suppose now that
\dim M=3
and
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is not flat. Since
M
is compact and carries an affine structure it is not compact. Then
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a non compact simply-connected symmetric Riemannian manifold and hence it is the Riemannian product of a Euclidean space and a finite number of irreducible symmetric spaces (see
[
6

). Then
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is either irreducible or it is the product of
ℝ
with a complete Riemannian surface of constant curvature. If
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is irreducible then it is Einstein with nonpositive scalar curvature

. If

then

it is Ricci-flat and hence flat since any homogeneous Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold is flat (see
[
3
]
). If

then, according Alekseevskii conjecture which is true in dimension

(see
[

),

is isometric to a solvable Lie group with a left invariant metric. If

is the product of
ℝ
with a complete Riemannian surface

is either the 2-dimensional hyperbolic pace

or
S^{2}(r)
endowed with their canonical metric. When

then

is isometric to a solvable Lie group with a left invariant metric. So far, we have shown that if
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is not flat then
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isometric to a 3-dimensional solvable Lie group with a left invariant Riemannian metric or
\hbox{\bb R}\times S^{2}(r)
.

Suppose that
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isometric to a 3-dimensional solvable Lie group with a left invariant metric. Let us show that
\nabla
is also left invariant. According to Theorem
7.2
, there exists a simply-connected Lie group

which act transitively on

\nabla
and
\langle\;,\;\rangle

,
10
]

contains the left multiplications and hence

is isomorphic to a Lie group with a left invariant connection and a left invariant metric. To complete the proof, we apply Propositions
7.4

The following result describes completely the situation in dimension 2.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a connected 2-dimensional infinitely balanced affine-Riemann manifold. Then
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is Hessian, i.e.,
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
and its sectional curvature is nonnegative.
Moreover, if
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is complete then
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
and
M
is either diffeomorphic to the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
or
\hbox{\bb R}^{2}

Proof.

Let us first show that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is Hessian. Choose an orthonormal frame
(E_{1},E_{2})
. We have in the basis
(E_{1},E_{2})
, since
\gamma_{E_{1}}E_{2}=\gamma_{E_{2}}E_{1}
,

\gamma_{E_{1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{11}^{1}&\gamma_{12}^{1}\\
\gamma_{11}^{2}&\gamma_{12}^{2}\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma_{E_{2}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{12}^{1}&\gamma_{22}^{1}\\
\gamma_{12}^{2}&\gamma_{22}^{2}\end{array}\right).

The condition
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma^{*})={\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}\gamma=0
is equivalent to

\gamma_{11}^{1}+\gamma_{12}^{2}=\gamma_{12}^{1}+\gamma_{22}^{2}=\gamma_{11}^{1}+\gamma_{22}^{1}=\gamma_{11}^{2}+\gamma_{22}^{2}=0.

Thus

\gamma_{E_{1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{11}^{1}&\gamma_{11}^{2}\\
\gamma_{11}^{2}&-\gamma_{11}^{1}\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma_{E_{2}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma_{11}^{2}&-\gamma_{11}^{1}\\
-\gamma_{11}^{1}&-\gamma_{11}^{2}\end{array}\right).

This shows that
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
and hence
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is Hessian. According to to (
15
) and (
16
), the curvature of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is given by

K(X,Y)=[\gamma_{X},\gamma_{Y}].

So the curvature
\kappa:M\longrightarrow\hbox{\bb R}
of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is given by

\kappa=\langle K(E_{1},E_{2})E_{1},E_{2}\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma_{E_{1}}^{2})\geq 0.

If
M
is compact, according to Gauss-Bonnet’s theorem,

\int_{M}\kappa\nu=2\pi(2-2g)\geq 0

and hence
g\leq 1
. But the case
g=0
is not possible since the 2-sphere has no affine structure and hence
g=1
,
\kappa=0
and then
\gamma=0
. If
M
is non compact and
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is complete then according to a theorem of Cohen-Vossen
[
9
]

is diffeomorphic to
\hbox{\bb R}^{2}
. But a theorem of Cheng-Yau and Pogorelov (see
[

, Theorem 8.6 pp. 160]

\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
which satisfies

In dimension superior to 3, we have the following theorem about infinitely balanced Hessian affine-Riemann manifolds.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
(M,\nabla,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an affine-Riemann manifold satisfying
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
and
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)=0
. Then the Ricci curvature of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is nonnegative and
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is Ricci-flat if and only if
\gamma=0
. Moreover, if
M
is compact then

\gamma=0
and hence
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
.

Proof.

The condition
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
implies by virtue of (

) and (

) that, for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
,

K(X,Y)=[\gamma_{X},\gamma_{Y}].

Since

, we get that the Ricci curvature is given by

{\mathrm{ric}}(X,X)={\mathrm{tr}}(\gamma_{X}^{2})\geq 0

and
{\mathrm{ric}}(X,X)=0
if and only if

.
If

\gamma=0
and

If we drop the hypothesis
M
compact there are non trivial infinitely balanced Hessian affine-Riemannian manifolds.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
c>0
. On
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
endowed with its affine connection
\nabla^{0}
and its canonical Euclidean product
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0}
, consider the smooth function

\displaystyle{}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\int_{0}^{r}(t^{n}+1)^{\frac{1}{n}}dt

where
r=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{n}^{2}}
and the matrix

\langle\;,\;\rangle=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
. Then
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is a Riemannian metric and
(\hbox{\bb R}^{n}\setminus\{0\},\nabla^{0},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is an affine-Riemann manifold satisfying
\gamma=\gamma^{*}
and
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)=0
. Moreover, for any
i\not=j
and any
u,v\in T_{X}(\hbox{\bb R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

\begin{cases}\langle\partial_{x_{i}},\partial_{x_{i}}\rangle=\frac{r^{{n+2}}+c(r^{2}-x_{i}^{2})}{r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}},\;\langle\partial_{x_{i}},\partial_{x_{j}}\rangle=-\frac{cx_{i}x_{j}}{r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}},\\
\langle u,v\rangle=\frac{1}{r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\left((r^{n}+c)r^{2}\langle u,v\rangle_{0}-c\langle u,X\rangle_{0}\langle v,X\rangle_{0}\right),\;X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\end{cases}

and the Ricci curvature of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is nonnegative.

Proof.

The function
f
is smooth on
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and it is easy to show that, for any
i\not=j
,

\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial{x_{i}^{2}}}=\frac{r^{{n+2}}+c(r^{2}-x_{i}^{2})}{r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}}=-\frac{cx_{i}x_{j}}{r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}.

Let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
be the canonical basis of
\hbox{\bb R}^{n}
. Let us show that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is definite positive. For any
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\hbox{\bb R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}

u\in T_{X}(\hbox{\bb R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
,
We have

\displaystyle({r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}})\langle u,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r^{2}(r^{n}+c)-cx_{i}^{2})u_{i}^{2}-c\sum_{i\not=j}x_{i}x_{j}u_{i}u_{j}

\displaystyle=(r^{2}(r^{n}+c)\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}^{2}-c(x_{1}u_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}u_{n})^{2}

\displaystyle=(r^{n}+c)|X|_{0}^{2}|u|_{0}^{2}-c\langle u,X\rangle_{0}^{2}.

By virtue of Schwartz inequality

c\langle u,X\rangle_{0}^{2}\leq c|X|_{0}^{2}|u|_{0}^{2}\leq(r^{n}+c)|X|_{0}^{2}|u|_{0}^{2}.

This shows that
\langle u,u\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle u,u\rangle=0
if and only if
u=0
. Then
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is a Hessian metric.

Let us show that
\det(\langle\;,\;\rangle)=1
. Indeed, the rows
(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})
of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
are given by

L_{i}=\frac{1}{{r^{3}(r^{{n}}+c)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}}}(r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{i}-cx_{i}\rho)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho=x_{1}e_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}e_{n}.

So

\displaystyle(r^{3n}(r^{{n}}+c)^{{n-1}})\det(\langle\;,\;\rangle)

\displaystyle=\det(r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{1}-cx_{1}\rho,\ldots,r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{n}-cx_{n}\rho)

\displaystyle=r^{2n}(r^{n}+c)^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\det(r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{1},\ldots,r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{i-1},-cx_{i}\rho,r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{i+1},\ldots,r^{2}(r^{n}+c)e_{n})

\displaystyle=r^{2n}(r^{n}+c)^{n}-cr^{2n}(r^{n}+c)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=r^{2n}(r^{n}+c)^{n-1}(r^{n}+c-c)=r^{3n}(r^{n}+c)^{n-1}.

So
{\mathrm{tr}}_{\langle\;,\;\rangle}(\gamma)=0
which completes the proof.
∎

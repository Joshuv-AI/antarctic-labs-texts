1.
Introduction

The original motivation for this paper is to find relationship between non-weight modules and weight modules over Lie algebras. In
[
1
]
, Batra and Mazorchuk introduced Whittaker pairs. A pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
is called a Whittaker pair if
\mathfrak{n}
is a quasi-nilpotent subalgebra of
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{n}
on the
\mathfrak{n}
-module
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}
is locally nilpotent. For a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
, we can study
\mathfrak{g}
-modules that are locally finite over
\mathfrak{n}
. Apart from Kostant’s Whittaker modules (see
[
7
]
), this kind of modules also include classical weight
modules when
\mathfrak{n}
is chosen to be a Cartan
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and classical Harish-Chandra and generalized Harish-Chandra modules, see e.g.
[
16
]
. Let
\mathfrak{W}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{n}}

be the category which consists of all
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, on which the action of
\mathfrak{n}
is locally finite. A block decomposition of
\mathfrak{W}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{n}}
and
certain properties of simple Whittaker modules under some rather
mild assumptions were described in
[
1
]
. We want to find possible equivalence between
\mathfrak{W}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{n}}
and
\mathfrak{W}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}}
for two different
pairs
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
,
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n}^{\prime})
. For example, if
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
are two Cartan subalgebras
of a complex semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, then by the conjugate relationship
between
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{W}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\mathfrak{W}^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}
are equivalent.

Among the theory of infinite dimensional Lie algebras, the derivation Lie algebra
W_{n}
of
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
is an important simple Lie algebra, see
[
8
,
12
]
. The subspaces
\mathfrak{h}_{n}:=\text{Span}\{t_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{1}}},\dots,t_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{n}}}\}

and
\Delta_{n}:=\text{Span}\{\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{1}}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{n}}}\}
are two abelian subalgebras of
W_{n}
. A
W_{n}
-module
M
with diagonalizable action of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
is called a weight module.
Tensor modules
T(P,V)
over
W_{n}
were introduced by Shen and Larsson, see
[
17
,
9
]
, for a
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
P
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module V, where
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is the Weyl algebra. In
[
10
]
, the simplicity of any
T(P,V)
was completely determined. When
V
is finite dimensional and
P=\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
, the simplicity of
T(P,V)
over the Witt algebra
on an
n
-dimensional torus was given by Rao, see
[
3
]
. Weight modules with finite dimensional weight spaces are also called Harish-Chandra modules, see
[
12
]
.
The classification of simple Harish-Chandra modules over
W_{1}
was given in
[
12
]
. Recently,
built on the classification of simple uniformly bounded
W_{n}
modules (see
[
2
]
for
n=2
and
[
18
]

for any
n
) and the characterization of the weight set of simple weight
W_{n}
modules
(see
[
14
]
), Grantcharov and Serganova completed the classification of all simple Harish-Chandra
W_{n}
-modules, see
[
5
]
. They proved that every such nontrivial module
M
is a simple sub-quotient of some tensor module
T(P,V)
, where
P
is a simple weight
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module.
In
[
19
]
, under a finite condition, we show that every simple
W_{n}
-module
M
with locally finite action of
\Delta_{n}
is a simple quotient of some tensor module
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V)
, where
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
is a simple
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module twisted from
A_{n}
by an isomorphism, see (
2.3
). From these known results, it seems that there is a relation between a subcategory of
W_{n}
-modules
M
which are locally finite over
\Delta_{n}

and some subcategory of uniformly bounded weight
W_{n}
-modules. In this paper, we will address this problem.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we collect all necessary preliminaries, including the Whittaker category
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
and the cuspidal category
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. The category
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
is the category consisting of
W_{n}
-modules
M
such that
\partial_{i}-1
acts locally nilpotently on
M
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and the subspace
\text{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)=\{v\in M\mid\partial_{i}v=v,\ i=1,\dots,n\}
is finite dimensional.
A weight module
M
with finite dimensional weight spaces over
W_{n}
is called a cuspidal module if it is cuspidal when restricted to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, see Definition
2.4
. For an
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
is the category of cuspidal
W_{n}
-modules
M
such that
\text{Supp}M\subset\mathbf{\alpha}+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
In Section 3, we study the localization
U_{\partial}
of
U(W_{n})
with respect to the Ore subset generated by
\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}
. Let
H_{n}
be the centralizer of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}\oplus\Delta_{n}
in
U_{\partial}
.
We show that
H_{n}
is a tensor product factor of
U_{\partial}
, and
H_{n}
is isomorphic to the opposite algebra of the endomorphism algebra of a universal Whittaker module
Q_{\mathbf{1}}
, see Theorems
3.3
and
3.7
. In Section 4, using
H_{n}
-modules, we
study the categories
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.
Every dominant integral weight
\gamma
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
can define a full subcategory
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
of
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
and a subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. We show that
there exists a suitable
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
, see Proposition
4.2
and Theorems
4.9
and
4.10
.

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{*}
the sets of integers, positive integers, nonnegative
integers, complex numbers, and nonzero complex numbers, respectively. For a Lie algebra

L
over
\mathbb{C}
, we use
U(L)
to denote the universal enveloping algebra of
L
.

2.
Preliminaries and known results

2.1.
Witt algebras
W_{n}

Throughout this paper,
n
denotes a positive integer.
We fix the vector space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
n
-dimensional complex vectors.
Denote its standard basis by
\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{n}\}
. Let
|\mathbf{m}|=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}
for any
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}]
be the polynomial algebra in the commuting variables
t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{n}
.
Let
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial{t_{i}}},h_{i}=t_{i}\partial_{i}
for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
.
For the convenience, set

t^{\mathbf{m}}=t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{m_{n}}
,
h^{\mathbf{m}}=h_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots h_{n}^{m_{n}}
for any
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
.
Recall that the Witt algebra
W_{n}=\text{Der}(A_{n})=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}{A}_{n}\partial_{i}
is a free
A_{n}
-module of rank
n
, which is also called the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
It has the following Lie bracket:

[t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i},t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{j}]=r_{i}t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\partial_{j}-m_{j}t^{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\partial_{i},

where all
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},i,j=1,\dots,n
.

It is well-known that
W_{n}
is a simple Lie algebra.
Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\text{Span}\{h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\}
which is a Cartan subalgebra (a maximal
abelian subalgebra that is diagonalizable on
W_{n}
with respect to the
adjoint action) of
W_{n}
. Denote
\Delta_{n}=\text{Span}\{\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}\}
which is also an
n
-dimensional abelian subalgebra of
W_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
W_{n}
-module
M
is called a weight module if the action of
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
on
M

is diagonalizable, i.e.,

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}M_{\lambda},
where

M_{\lambda}=\{v\in M\mid h_{i}v=\lambda_{i}v,\forall\ i=1,\dots,n\}.

For a weight
W_{n}
-module
M
, denote its weight set by
\text{Supp}M=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\mid M_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
. For example, the adjoint module
W_{n}
has the weight set
\text{Supp}W_{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If
M
is indecomposable, then there is a
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\text{Supp}M\subset\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

2.2.
Finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules

We denote by
E_{ij}
the
n\times n
square matrix
with
1
as its
(i,j)
-entry and 0 as other entries. Then
\{E_{ij}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n\}
is a basis of the general linear Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

We can view a
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
as a linear function on
\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}E_{ii}
such that
\lambda(E_{ii})=\lambda_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Let
\Lambda_{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\,|\,\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\text{ for }i=1,2,\dots,n-1\}
be the set of dominant integral weights of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
It is well known that the simple highest weight
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
is finite dimensional if and only if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}
. Define the fundamental weights
\delta_{i}\in\Lambda_{+}
by

\delta_{i}=\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
For convenience, we define
\delta_{0}=0\in\Lambda_{+}
. So
V(\delta_{0})
is the
1
-dimensional trivial
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module.
A module
V
over a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called a trivial module if
Xv=0
for any
X\in\mathfrak{g},v\in V
.
It is well-known that the module
V(\delta_{1})
is isomorphic to the
natural module
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
The exterior product
\bigwedge^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\mathbb{C}^{n}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{C}^{n}\ \ (k\ \mbox{times})
is a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
with the action given by

X(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{k})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}v_{1}\wedge\cdots v_{i-1}\wedge Xv_{i}\cdots\wedge v_{k},\,\,\forall\,\,X\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}.

Moreover,
{\bigwedge}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cong V(\delta_{k}),\forall\ 1\leq k\leq n
, see Exercise 21.11 in
[
6
]
.
We can set
V(\delta_{0})=\bigwedge^{0}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\mathbb{C}
and

v\wedge a=av
for any
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n},a\in\mathbb{C}
.

2.3.
Whittaker modules and cuspidal modules

We recall the definition of Whittaker pair introduced in
[
1
]
. A pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{n})
is called a Whittaker pair if
\mathfrak{n}
is a quasi-nilpotent subalgebra (a generalization of the nilpotent algebra) of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}
is locally nilpotent.
For the Lie algebra
W_{n}
,
\Delta_{n}=\text{Span}\{\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}\}
is an abelian subalgebra, whence is a nilpotent subalgebra.
Since the adjoint action of
\Delta_{n}
on
W_{n}/\Delta_{n}
is locally nilpotent,
(W_{n},\Delta_{n})
is a Whittaker pair.
A
W_{n}
-module
M
is called a Whittaker module if
the action of
\Delta_{n}
on
M
is locally finite.

Definition 2.2
.

For any
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
be the category consisting of
W_{n}
-modules
M
such that:

(
a
)

For any
v\in M
,
1\leq i\leq n
, there is a
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
(\partial_{i}-a_{i})^{k}v=0
.

(
b
)

The subspace
\emph{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)=\{v\in M\mid\partial_{i}v=a_{i}v,\ \forall\ i\}
is finite dimensional.

An element in
\emph{wh}_{\mathbf{a}}(M)
is called a Whittaker vector. For a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we call it non-singular if
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

Denote
\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
For a
W_{n}
-module
M
, and an automorphism
\sigma
of
W_{n}
,
M
can be twisted by
\sigma
to be a new
W_{n}
-module
M^{\sigma}
.
As a vector space
M^{\sigma}=M
, the action of
W_{n}
on
M^{\sigma}
is defined by
u\cdot v=\sigma(u)v,\forall\ u\in W_{n},v\in M
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\mathbf{a}\neq 0
, then
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
is equivalent to
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
.

Proof.

Any
X=(x_{ij})_{n\times n}\in GL(n,\mathbb{C})
can define an automorphism
\sigma_{X}
of
W_{n}
such that
\sigma_{X}(\partial_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{ij}\partial_{j}
. Choosing a proper
X
, we can obtain that
\partial_{i}-1
acts locally nilpotently on
M^{\sigma_{X}}
, i.e.,
M^{\sigma_{X}}\in\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
for any
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
.
∎

Let
E_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\partial_{i}
. It is known that the subalgebra of
W_{n}
spanned by
\partial_{i},t_{i}\partial_{j},t_{i}E_{n},1\leq i,j\leq n
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, see page 578 in
[
13
]
. We denote this subalgebra still by
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. A weight module
M
with finite dimensional weight spaces over
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
is called a cuspidal module if every root vector of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
acts injectively on
M
, see
[
13
,
4
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A weight module
M
with finite dimensional weight spaces over
W_{n}
is called a cuspidal module if it is cuspidal when restricted to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
, i.e.,
\partial_{i},t_{i}\partial_{j},t_{i}E_{n}
act injectively on
M
, for any
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. For an
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
be the category of cuspidal
W_{n}
-modules
M
such that
\emph{Supp}M\subset\mathbf{\alpha}+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

In the remainder of this section, we give all simple modules in
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
using the results in
[
18
,
19
]
.

2.4.
Shen-Larsson’s modules

The Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}

over
A_{n}
is the unital associative algebra
over
\mathbb{C}
generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
,

\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}
subject to
the relations

[\partial_{i},\partial_{j}]=[t_{i},t_{j}]=0,\qquad[\partial_{i},t_{j}]=\delta_{i,j},\ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

The following homomorphism was well known, see
[
17
]
or
[
10
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

The linear map
\phi:U(W_{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phi(t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{k})

\displaystyle=t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{k}\otimes 1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}t^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes E_{ik},

is an associative algebra homomorphism.

The homomorphism in Theorem
2.5
implies that there is a close relationship between
W_{n}
-modules and
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-modules.
For any simple
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
P
, and simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
, the tensor product
P\otimes V
can be lifted to a
W_{n}
-module denoted by
T(P,V)
under the map
\phi
.

The simplicity of
T(P,V)
was determined in
[
10
]
, for any simple
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
P
, and simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
.
For a finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V
,
T(P,V)
is a simple module over

W_{n}
if
V\not\cong V(\delta_{k})
for any
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
. Moreover
we have the following complex of
W_{n}
-modules:

(2.2)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T(P,V(\delta_{0}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{P}_{0}}
\textstyle{T(P,V(\delta_{1}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\pi^{P}_{1}}
\textstyle{\cdots}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{P}_{n-2}\hskip 56.9055pt}
\textstyle{{T(P,V(\delta_{n-1}))}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 22.76228pt\pi^{P}_{n-1}}
\textstyle{T(P,V(\delta_{n}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where

\pi^{P}_{k-1}
is the
W_{n}
-module homomorphism defined by

\begin{array}[]{lrcl}\pi^{P}_{k-1}:&T(P,V(\delta_{k-1}))&\rightarrow&T(P,V(\delta_{k})),\\
&p\otimes v&\mapsto&\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{j}\cdot p\otimes(\mathbf{e}_{j}\wedge v),\end{array}

for all
p\in P
and
v\in V(\delta_{k-1})
, where
T(P,V(\delta_{-1}))=0
,
k\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
.
By Theorem 3.5 in
[
10
]
,
\text{im}\pi^{P}_{0},\text{im}\pi^{P}_{1},\dots,\text{im}\pi^{P}_{n-1}
are simple
W_{n}
-modules.
T(P,V(\delta_{0}))
is not simple if and only if
P\cong A_{n}
.
Moreover
T(P,V(\delta_{n}))
is irreducible if and only if
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\partial_{k}P=P
. If
T(P,V(\delta_{n}))
is reducible, then
T(P,V(\delta_{n}))/\text{im}\pi^{P}_{n-1}
is a trivial module.

For a simple
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
P
, and simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
, we denote

L(P,V(\lambda)):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}T(P,V(\lambda)),\ \text{if}\ \lambda\neq\delta_{i},i=0,1,\dots,n,\\
\text{im}\pi^{P}_{i-1},\ \text{if}\ \lambda=\delta_{i},i=1,\dots,n.\end{array}\right.

And
L(P,V(\delta_{0})):=L(P,V(\delta_{1}))
.
Hence
L(P,V(\lambda))
are simple
W_{n}
-modules, for any
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}
.

2.5.
Simple modules in
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}

For an
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
be the algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
defined by

t_{i}\mapsto t_{i},\partial_{i}\mapsto\partial_{i}+a_{i}.

The
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
A_{n}
can be twisted by
\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}
to be a new
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
. Explicitly the action of

\mathcal{D}_{n}
on
A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}}
is defined by

(2.3)

X\cdot f(t)=\sigma_{\mathbf{a}}(X)f(t),\ \ f(t)\in A_{n},X\in\mathcal{D}_{n}.

By Theorem 4.6 and Lemma 4.7 in
[
19
]
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is nonsingular, i.e.,
a_{i}\neq 0
for any
i=1,\dots,n
. If
M
is a simple module in
\Omega_{\mathbf{a}}
, then
M
is isomorphic to
L(A_{n}^{\mathbf{a}},V(\lambda))
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}
.

For a
\gamma\in\Lambda_{+}
, let
[\gamma]=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}\mid\lambda-\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
,
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
be the Serre subcategory of
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
generated by the simple
modules
L(A_{n}^{\mathbf{1}},V(\lambda))
, for
\lambda\in[\gamma]
. In Section
4
, we will show the subcategory decomposition

\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}=\bigoplus_{[\gamma]}\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
.

2.6.
Simple modules in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}

For any
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
P(\mu)=t_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{\mu_{n}}\mathbb{C}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. As a
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module,

\mu_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any

. Since
\phi(h_{k})=t_{k}\partial_{k}\otimes 1+1\otimes E_{kk}
,
\text{Supp}T(P(\mu),V(\lambda))\subset\mu+\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
T(P(\mu),V(\lambda))
is cuspidal if and only if
\mu_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z},|\mu+\lambda|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
i=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

By (
2.1
), for any
v\in V(\lambda),\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)=

\displaystyle(\mu_{i}+m_{i})t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}(t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)=

\displaystyle(\mu_{j}+m_{j})t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\otimes v+t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}}\otimes E_{ij}v,\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle t_{i}E_{n}(t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}}\otimes v)=

\displaystyle t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes(|\mu+\lambda+\mathbf{m}|+E_{ii})v+\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}t^{\mu+\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{e}_{j}}\otimes E_{ij}v.

Then it can be checked that
\partial_{i},t_{i}\partial_{j}
act injectively on
T(P(\mu),V(\lambda))
if and only if
\mu_{i},\mu_{j}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

t_{i}E_{n}
acts injectively on
T(P(\mu),V(\lambda))
if and only if
|\mu+\lambda|+\lambda_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

3.
Localization of
U(W_{n})

Throughout the paper, we denote
U=U(W_{n})
. In this section, we study the structure of
U
, mainly the centralizer of
\Delta_{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{n}
in a bigger algebra
U_{\partial}
.

3.1.
Tensor product decomposition of
U_{\partial}

Since each
\text{ad}\partial_{i}
is locally nilpotent on
U
, the following set

\partial:=\{\partial_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{n}^{i_{n}}\mid i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}

is an Ore subset of
U
, see Lemma 4.2 in
[
13
]
.
Let
U_{\partial}
be the localization of
U
with respect to
\partial
.
Define

H_{n}=\{u\in U_{\partial}\mid[u,\partial_{i}]=[u,h_{i}]=0,\ \forall\ i=1,\dots,n\},

which is a subalgebra of
U_{\partial}
. Define the following elements in
U_{\partial}
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle z_{i,j}=

\displaystyle(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}-t_{i}\partial_{i},

\displaystyle z_{i,l,j}=

\displaystyle(t_{i}t_{l}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}-(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}(t_{l}\partial_{l})

\displaystyle-(t_{l}\partial_{j})\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}(t_{i}\partial_{i})+(t_{l}\partial_{l})(t_{i}\partial_{i})+\delta_{il}t_{i}\partial_{l},

\displaystyle z_{i}=

\displaystyle(t_{i}^{3}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{2}-3(t_{i}^{2}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}+2(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)(t_{i}\partial_{i}-2),

where
i,j,l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

The following Lemma is used to construct generators of the associative algebra
H_{n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
i,j,l\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
z_{i,j},z_{i,l,j},z_{i}\in H_{n}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
[h_{k},z_{i,j}]=[h_{l},z_{i,j,k}]=[h_{k},z_{i}]=0
.

We can compute that

\displaystyle\ [\partial_{k},(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}-t_{i}\partial_{i}]=\delta_{ki}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}-\delta_{ki}\partial_{i}=0,

and

\displaystyle[\partial_{k},z_{i,l,j}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ki}(t_{l}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}+\delta_{kl}(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}-\delta_{ki}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}(t_{l}\partial_{l})-\delta_{kl}(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}\partial_{l}

\displaystyle-\delta_{kl}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}(t_{i}\partial_{i})-\delta_{ki}(t_{l}\partial_{j})\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}\partial_{i}+\delta_{kl}\partial_{l}(t_{i}\partial_{i})+\delta_{ki}(t_{l}\partial_{l})\partial_{i}+\delta_{ki}\delta_{li}\partial_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{ki}\partial_{i}(t_{l}\partial_{l})-\delta_{kl}\partial_{l}(t_{i}\partial_{i})+\delta_{kl}\partial_{l}(t_{i}\partial_{i})+\delta_{ki}(t_{l}\partial_{l})\partial_{i}+\delta_{ki}\delta_{li}\partial_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{ki}\partial_{i}(t_{l}\partial_{l})+\delta_{ki}(t_{l}\partial_{l})\partial_{i}+\delta_{ki}\delta_{li}\partial_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Finally we can also obtain that

\displaystyle[\partial_{i},z_{i}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\partial_{i},(t_{i}^{3}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{2}-3(t_{i}^{2}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}+2(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)(t_{i}\partial_{i}-2)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3(t_{i}^{2}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{2}-6(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}-3(t_{i}^{2}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{2}+2\partial_{i}(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)(t_{1}\partial_{i}-2)

\displaystyle+2(t_{i}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}(t_{i}\partial_{i}-2)+2(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-6(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}+6(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

∎

The following lemma is well known. For the convenience of proving Lemma
3.5
, we give its proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

When
n>1
,
W_{n}
is generated
t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
,
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|=0,1,2
.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|\geq 3
. If
m_{j}=0
, then there must exists
i\neq j
such that
m_{i}\neq 0
. From

(3.2)

t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[t^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\partial_{j},t_{j}t_{i}\partial_{j}],\text{if}\ m_{j}=0,\\
\frac{1}{(3-m_{j})}[t^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}\partial_{j},t_{j}^{2}\partial_{j}],\text{if}\ m_{j}\neq 0,3,\\
\ [t_{j}\partial_{i},t_{j}^{2}t_{i}\partial_{j}]+t_{j}^{2}t_{i}\partial_{i},\text{if}\ m_{j}=3,|\mathbf{m}|=3,\\
\ \frac{1}{2}[t^{\mathbf{m}-2\mathbf{e}_{j}}\partial_{j},t_{j}^{3}\partial_{j}],\text{if}\ m_{j}=3,|\mathbf{m}|>3,\end{array}\right.

by induction on
|\mathbf{m}|
, we can complete the proof.
∎

Let
B_{n}
be the subalgebra of
U_{\partial}

generated by
\partial_{i},h_{i},\partial_{i}^{-1},i=1,\dots,n
.
The following result gives a tensor product decomposition of
U_{\partial}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

U_{\partial}=B_{n}H_{n}\cong B_{n}\otimes H_{n}
.

Proof.

Clearly
B_{n}H_{n}\subseteq U_{\partial}
. To show that
U_{\partial}\subseteq B_{n}H_{n}
, we need to explain that the generators of
W_{n}
belong to
B_{n}H_{n}
. When
n=1
,
W_{1}
is generated by
\partial_{1},t_{1}\partial_{1},t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1},t_{1}^{3}\partial_{1}
, and

\displaystyle t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1}=z_{1,1,1}\partial_{1}^{-1}+(t_{1}\partial_{1})^{2}\partial_{1}^{-1}-(t_{1}\partial_{1})\partial_{1}^{-1},\partial_{1},t_{1}\partial_{1}\in B_{1}H_{1},

\displaystyle(t_{1}^{3}\partial_{1})=z_{1}\partial_{1}^{-2}+3(t_{1}^{2}\partial_{1})(t_{1}\partial_{1}-1)\partial_{1}^{-1}-2(t_{1}\partial_{1})(t_{1}\partial_{1}-1)(t_{1}\partial_{1}-2)\partial_{1}^{-2}\in B_{1}H_{1}.

By Lemma
3.2
, when
n>1
,
W_{n}
is generated
t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|=0,1,2
. These generators belong to

B_{n}H_{n}
, since

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}=

\displaystyle z_{i,j}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{-1}+(t_{i}\partial_{i})\partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{-1}\in B_{n}H_{n},

\displaystyle t_{i}t_{l}\partial_{j}=

\displaystyle z_{i,l,j}\partial_{i}^{-1}\partial_{l}^{-1}\partial_{j}+(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}(t_{l}\partial_{l})\partial_{i}^{-1}\partial_{l}^{-1}\partial_{j}+(t_{l}\partial_{j})\partial_{l}\partial_{j}^{-1}(t_{i}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{-1}\partial_{l}^{-1}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle-(t_{l}\partial_{l})(t_{i}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{-1}\partial_{l}^{-1}\partial_{j}-\delta_{il}(t_{i}\partial_{l})\partial_{i}^{-1}\partial_{l}^{-1}\partial_{j}\in B_{n}H_{n}.

Since
[B_{n},H_{n}]=0
, we have that
B_{n}H_{n}\cong B_{n}\otimes H_{n}
.
∎

3.2.
Isomorphism between
H_{n}
and the endomorphism algebra of a universal Whittaker module

Let
\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{1}}=\mathbb{C}v_{\mathbf{1}}
be the one dimensional
U(\Delta_{n})
-module such that
\partial_{i}v_{\mathbf{1}}=v_{\mathbf{1}}
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Let

Q_{\mathbf{1}}=U(W_{n})\otimes_{U(\Delta_{n})}\mathbb{C}_{\mathbf{1}},

an induced
W_{n}
-module, and

H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}=\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})^{\text{op}},

an associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. Then
Q_{\mathbf{1}}
is both a left
U(W_{n})
-module and a right
H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}
-module. Let
H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}\text{-mod}
be the category of finite dimensional
H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}
-modules.

In
[
19
]
, we showed the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

The functors
M\mapsto\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
and
V\mapsto Q_{\mathbf{1}}\otimes_{H_{\mathbf{1}}}V
are inverse equivalence between
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
and
H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}\text{-mod}
.

Inspired by the constructions of
z_{i,j},z_{i,l,j}
in (
3.1
), we can give more useful elements in
H_{n}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|\geq 1,\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{e}_{j}
, there is an
X_{\mathbf{m},j}\in H_{n}
such that

(3.3)

X_{\mathbf{m},j}=(t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{j})\partial^{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}+\sum_{0<|\mathbf{r}|<|\mathbf{m}|}(t^{\mathbf{r}}\partial_{j})g_{\mathbf{r}}(h)\partial^{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{j}},

where
g_{\mathbf{r}}(h)
is a polynomial in
h_{1},\cdots,h_{n}
of the degree
|\mathbf{m}|-|\mathbf{r}|
.

Proof.

When
n=1
, denote
d_{i}:=t_{1}^{i+1}\partial_{1}
, for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq-1}
.
Define

X_{m+1,1}:=d_{m}d_{-1}^{m}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m+1}{k+1}d_{k}(\prod_{i=1}^{m-k}(d_{0}-i))d_{-1}^{k}+(-1)^{m}m\prod_{i=0}^{m}(d_{0}-i),

for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. It can be directly verified that
X_{m+1,1}\in H_{1}
.

When
n>1
, we do induction on
|\mathbf{m}|
. If
|\mathbf{m}|=1,2
, then we set
X_{\mathbf{e}_{i},j}=z_{i,j}
and
X_{\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{l},j}=z_{i,l,j}
, where
z_{i,j},z_{i,l,j}
are defined in (
3.1
). For any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|>2
, there exists
m_{i}>0
. We set that

(3.4)

X_{\mathbf{m},j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[X_{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{i},j},X_{\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i},j}],\ \ \text{if}\ m_{j}=0,\\
\frac{1}{(3-m_{j})}[X_{\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{e}_{j},j},X_{2\mathbf{e}_{j},j}],\ \ \text{if}\ m_{j}\neq 0,3,\\
\ [X_{\mathbf{e}_{j},i},X_{2\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i},j}]+X_{2\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i},i},\ \ \text{if}\ m_{j}=3,|\mathbf{m}|=3,\\
\frac{1}{2}[X_{\mathbf{m}-2\mathbf{e}_{j},j},X_{3\mathbf{e}_{j},j}],\ \ \text{if}\ m_{j}=3,|\mathbf{m}|>3,\end{array}\right.

By induction on
|\mathbf{m}|
and the bracket (
3.2
), we can see that
X_{\mathbf{m},j}\in H_{n}
and it is of the form (
3.3
).
∎

By Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
3.5
, we obtain a PBW type basis of
H_{n}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

The ordered monomials in
X_{\mathbf{m},j}\in H_{n}
, for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|\geq 1,\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{e}_{j}
, form a basis of
H_{n}
.

Following the original Premet’s proof of the PBW theorem for finite
W
-algebras (see
[
15
]
), we will show the following algebra isomorphism.

Theorem 3.7
.

We have that
H_{n}\cong H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
L_{n}
be the Lie subalgebra of
W_{n}
spanned by
t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n,\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
with
|\mathbf{m}|\geq 1
. Clearly as a linear space,
Q_{\mathbf{1}}\cong U(L_{n})
.
Any
\theta\in\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})
is determined by
\theta(v_{\mathbf{1}})
and
\theta(v_{\mathbf{1}})\in\text{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})
.
Since
[H_{n},\Delta_{n}]=0
, we can define an algebra homomorphism

\Theta:H_{n}\rightarrow H_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}=\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})^{\text{op}},X\mapsto\Theta(X),

where
\Theta(X)\in\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})^{\text{op}}
such that
\Theta(X)(v_{\mathbf{1}})=Xv_{\mathbf{1}}
. By Lemma
3.5
and Corollary
3.6
,
\Theta
is injective. To show that
\Theta
is surjective, we need to prove that
\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})^{\text{op}}
is generated by
\Theta(X_{\mathbf{m},j})
, for
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{m}|\geq 1,\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{e}_{j}
.
For convenience, denote

\{y_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\}:=\{t_{i}\partial_{j}-t_{i}\partial_{i},t^{\mathbf{m}}\partial_{j}\mid 1\leq i\neq j\leq n,\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},|\mathbf{m}|\geq 2\},

and

\{Y_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\}:=\{\Theta(X_{\mathbf{m},j})\mid j=1,\dots,n,\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n},|\mathbf{m}|\geq 1,\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{e}_{j}\}.

Then
\{y_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\}\cup\{h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\}
is a basis of
L_{n}
.
Let
n_{k}
be the integer such that

[E_{n},y_{k}]=n_{k}y_{k}
. For an
\tilde{l}=(l_{1},l_{2},\dots)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\infty}
with only finite nonzero entries and
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}^{n}
,
denote

\displaystyle\text{wt}(\mathbf{r}+\tilde{l}):=n_{1}l_{1}+\cdots+n_{q}l_{q}+\cdots,

\displaystyle|\mathbf{r}+\tilde{l}|:=|\mathbf{r}|+(n_{1}+1)l_{1}+\cdots+(n_{q}+1)l_{q}+\cdots,

\displaystyle y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}}:=y_{1}^{l_{1}}y_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots y_{q}^{l_{q}}\cdots h_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots h_{n}^{r_{n}}.

We say that the weight of
y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}}
is
\text{wt}(\mathbf{r}+\tilde{l})
, the degree of
y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}}
is
|\mathbf{r}+\tilde{l}|
. For any
0\neq\theta\in\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})
, we can write

\theta(v_{\mathbf{1}})=\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d}a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}}v_{\mathbf{1}},

where
d
is called the degree of
\theta
, and
a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}\neq 0
for at least one
(\tilde{l},\mathbf{r})
with
|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|=d
. Let
\Lambda^{d}=\{(\tilde{l},\mathbf{r})\mid a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}\neq 0,|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|=d\}
, and
\Lambda^{d}_{\text{max}}
be the subset
\Lambda^{d}
of all
(\tilde{p},\mathbf{s})
such that
\text{wt}(\mathbf{s}+\tilde{p})
assumes its maximum value. This maximal value will be denoted by
N(\theta)
. We define
(d,N(\theta))
to be the Whittaker degree of
\theta
.

Claim:
If
(\tilde{p},\mathbf{s})\in\Lambda^{d}_{\text{max}}
, then

\mathbf{s}=0
.

Suppose the contrary. Then there is a
(\tilde{p},\mathbf{s})\in\Lambda^{d}_{\text{max}}
such that
\mathbf{s}\neq 0
.
Hence
s_{k}>0
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
From
[\partial_{k},h^{\mathbf{r}}]=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}\binom{r_{k}}{i}h^{\mathbf{r}-i\mathbf{e}_{k}}
and
(\partial_{k}-1)v_{\mathbf{1}}=0
, we have:

\displaystyle(\partial_{k}-1)\theta(v_{\mathbf{1}})=\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d}a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}[\partial_{k},y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}}]v_{\mathbf{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d}a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}[\partial_{k},y^{\tilde{l}}]h^{\mathbf{r}}v_{\mathbf{1}}+\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d}a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}y^{\tilde{l}}[\partial_{k},h^{\mathbf{r}}]v_{\mathbf{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d}a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}[\partial_{k},y^{\tilde{l}}]h^{\mathbf{r}}v_{\mathbf{1}}+\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{k}}\binom{r_{k}}{i}a_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}-i\mathbf{e}_{k}}v_{\mathbf{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\tilde{l}+\mathbf{r}|\leq d-1}b_{\tilde{l},\mathbf{r}}y^{\tilde{l}}h^{\mathbf{r}}v_{\mathbf{1}}.

We can see that
s_{k}a_{\tilde{p},\mathbf{s}}y^{\tilde{p}}h^{\mathbf{s}-\mathbf{e}_{k}}v_{\mathbf{1}}
is a nonzero term of degree
d-1
in the above sum whose weight assumes
\text{wt}(\mathbf{s}+\tilde{p})
. Hence
(\partial_{k}-1)\theta(v_{\mathbf{1}})\neq 0
, which is a contradiction.

Let
\theta^{\prime}=\theta-\sum_{|\tilde{l}|=d}a_{\tilde{l}}Y^{\tilde{l}}
, where
Y^{\tilde{l}}:=Y_{1}^{l_{1}}Y_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots Y_{q}^{l_{q}}\cdots
. Order the elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{2}
lexicographically.
By the above claim,
\theta^{\prime}
has the Whittaker degree less than
\theta
. By induction on the Whittaker degree of
\theta
, we can conclude that
\text{End}_{W_{n}}(Q_{\mathbf{1}})^{\text{op}}
is generated by

\{Y_{k}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\}
.
∎

4.

In this section, using
H_{n}
-modules, we will show that
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

4.1.
Equivalence between
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
and
H_{n}\text{-mod}

Let
H_{n}\text{-mod}
be the category of finite dimensional
H_{n}
-modules. By Theorems
3.4
and
3.7
, the category
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
is equivalent to
H_{n}\text{-mod}
. In this subsection, we give a more essential proof of this equivalence using the tensor product decomposition of
U_{\partial}
.

We define the functor
F:\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}\rightarrow H_{n}\text{-mod}
such that
F(M)=\text{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
. Since
[H_{n},\partial_{i}]=0
and that

\text{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
is finite dimensional,

F(M)\in H_{n}\text{-mod}
. Conversely, for a finite dimensional
H_{n}
-module
V
, define the action of
\Delta_{n}
on
V
such that
\partial_{i}v=v
for any
v\in V
,
i=1,\dots,n
.
Let

G(V):=\text{Ind}_{H_{n}U(\Delta_{n})_{\partial}}^{U_{\partial}}V=U_{\partial}\otimes_{H_{n}U(\Delta_{n})_{\partial}}V.

It is easy to see that
G(V)=U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\otimes V
, since
U_{\partial}=B_{n}H_{n}
and
B_{n}
is
generated by
\partial_{i},h_{i},\partial_{i}^{-1},i=1,\dots,n
.

The following lemma in
[
19
]
is a key observation on modules in
\Omega_{\mathbf{\mathbf{1}}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a module in

\Omega_{\mathbf{\mathbf{1}}}
. Then as a vector space,

M\cong U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\otimes\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
.

The following proposition reduces modules in
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
to finite dimensional
H_{n}
-modules.

Proposition 4.2
.

The category
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
is equivalent to
H_{n}\text{-mod}
.

Proof.

Since
FG(V)=\text{wh}_{\mathbf{\mathbf{1}}}(U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\otimes V)=V
for any
V\in H_{n}\text{-mod}
,
we have
FG=\text{id}_{H_{n}\text{-mod}}
.
For any
M\in\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}
, by Lemma
4.1
, as a vector space,
M\cong U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})\otimes\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M)
. By the isomorphism
U_{\partial}\cong B_{n}\otimes H_{n}
,
we have that
M\cong G(\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(M))=GF(M)
. So
GF=\text{id}_{\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}}
.
∎

The following corollary gives a classification of all finite dimensional irreducible
H_{n}
-modules in terms of finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-modules. In
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{1}},V)
, we identify
1\otimes V
with
V
.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
M
is a finite dimensional irreducible
H_{n}
-module, then
M\cong\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{1}},V))=V
, or
M\cong\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(\text{im}\pi_{i})
, where
i=0,\dots,n-1
,

V
is a finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module which is not isomorphic to
k
-th exterior power
V(\delta_{k},k)
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, for
k=0,\cdots,n
. Moreover, the action of
H_{n}
on
M
satisfies that:

(4.1)

\displaystyle z_{i,j}v=

\displaystyle(E_{ij}-E_{ii})v,

\displaystyle z_{i,l,k}v=

\displaystyle\Big(E_{ll}E_{ii}-E_{ik}E_{ll}-E_{lk}E_{ii}+\delta_{il}E_{il}

\displaystyle+(\delta_{lk}-\delta_{il})E_{ik}+(\delta_{ik}-\delta_{il})E_{lk}\Big)v,

\displaystyle z_{i,j,j}v=

\displaystyle(E_{ij}-E_{ij}E_{jj})v,

\displaystyle z_{i}v=

\displaystyle 2(E_{ii}^{3}-3E_{ii}^{2}+2E_{ii})v,

where the operators
z_{i,j},z_{i,l,k},z_{i}
are defined in (
3.1
),
i,j=1,\cdots,n,v\in M
.

Proof.

By the action of
W_{n}
on
T(A_{n}^{\mathbf{1}},V)
, see (
2.1
), we have that

\displaystyle z_{i,j}(1\otimes v)

\displaystyle=((t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}-t_{i}\partial_{i})(1\otimes v)

\displaystyle=t_{i}\otimes v+1\otimes E_{ij}v-t_{i}\otimes v-1\otimes E_{ii}v

\displaystyle=1\otimes(E_{ij}-E_{ii})v,

and

\displaystyle z_{i,l,k}(1\otimes v)=

\displaystyle((t_{i}t_{l}\partial_{k})\partial_{i}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}^{-1}-(t_{i}\partial_{k})\partial_{i}\partial_{k}^{-1}(t_{l}\partial_{l})

\displaystyle-(t_{l}\partial_{k})\partial_{l}\partial_{k}^{-1}(t_{i}\partial_{i})+(t_{l}\partial_{l})(t_{i}\partial_{i})+\delta_{il}t_{i}\partial_{l})(1\otimes v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{i}t_{l}\otimes v+t_{i}\otimes E_{lk}v+t_{l}\otimes E_{ik}v-(t_{i}\partial_{k})\partial_{i}\partial_{k}^{-1}(t_{l}\otimes v+1\otimes E_{ll}v)

\displaystyle-(t_{l}\partial_{k})\partial_{l}\partial_{k}^{-1}(t_{i}\otimes v+1\otimes E_{ii}v)+(t_{l}\partial_{l})(t_{i}\otimes v+1\otimes E_{ii}v)

\displaystyle+\delta_{il}(t_{i}\otimes v+1\otimes E_{il}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{i}t_{l}\otimes v+t_{i}\otimes E_{lk}v+t_{l}\otimes E_{ik}v-t_{i}(t_{l}+\delta_{il})\otimes v-t_{i}\otimes E_{ll}v

\displaystyle-(t_{l}+\delta_{il}-\delta_{lk})\otimes E_{ik}v-1\otimes E_{ik}E_{ll}v-t_{l}(t_{i}+\delta_{il})\otimes v-t_{l}\otimes E_{ii}v

\displaystyle-(t_{i}-\delta_{ik}+\delta_{il})\otimes E_{lk}v-1\otimes E_{lk}E_{ii}v+t_{l}(t_{i}+\delta_{il})\otimes v+t_{l}\otimes E_{ii}v

\displaystyle+t_{i}\otimes E_{ll}v+1\otimes E_{ll}E_{ii}v+\delta_{il}(t_{i}\partial_{l}\otimes v+1\otimes E_{il}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\otimes\big(E_{ll}E_{ii}-E_{ik}E_{ll}-E_{lk}E_{ii}+\delta_{il}E_{il}

\displaystyle+(\delta_{lk}-\delta_{il})E_{ik}+(\delta_{ik}-\delta_{il})E_{lk}\big)v.

We can also obtain that

\displaystyle z_{i}(1\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\Big((t_{i}^{3}\partial_{i})\partial_{i}^{2}-3(t_{i}^{2}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)\partial_{i}+2(t_{i}\partial_{i})(t_{i}\partial_{i}-1)(t_{i}\partial_{i}-2)\Big)(1\otimes v)

\displaystyle=1\otimes 2(E_{ii}^{3}-3E_{ii}^{2}+2E_{ii})v.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

For a finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V(\lambda)
,
we denote

W(\lambda)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}V(\lambda),\ \text{if}\ \lambda\neq\delta_{i},i=1,\dots,n,\\
\mathrm{wh}_{\mathbf{1}}(\text{im}\pi^{A_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}}_{i-1}),\text{if}\ \lambda=\delta_{i},i=1,\dots,n.\end{array}\right.

By Corollary
4.3
, under the action (
4.1
),
W(\lambda)
exhaust all finite dimensional simple
H_{n}
-modules.
Note that
W(\delta_{0})\cong W(\delta_{1})
since
\pi_{0}^{A_{n}^{\mathbf{1}}}
is injective.

By Corollary
4.3
, the actions of
z_{i,i,i}
and
z_{i}
on
W(\lambda)
are diagonalizable. We will use the weight sets of
z_{i,i,i}
and
z_{i}
to give a subcategory decomposition of
H_{n}\text{-mod}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\gamma,\lambda\in\Lambda_{+}
, if
\lambda-\gamma\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{H_{n}}(W(\gamma),W(\lambda))=0
.

Proof.

Since
z_{i,i,i}v=(E_{ii}-E_{ii}^{2})v
and
z_{i}v=2(E_{ii}^{3}-3E_{ii}^{2}+2E_{ii})v
for any
v\in W(\gamma)\cup W(\lambda)
, both
z_{i,i,i}
and
z_{i}
act diagonally on
W(\gamma),W(\lambda)
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We consider eigenvalues of
z_{i,i,i}
and
z_{i}
on
W(\gamma)
and
W(\lambda)
.
Let
S_{\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}}=\{\big(\beta(z_{1,1,1}),\dots,\beta(z_{n,n,n}),\beta(z_{1}),\dots,\beta(z_{n})\big)\mid\beta\in\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
.
Note that the eigenvalues of
z_{i,i,i}
and
z_{i}
on
W(\lambda)
constitute a subset of
S_{\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
.
To show that
\text{Ext}^{1}_{H_{n}}(W(\gamma),W(\lambda))=0
,
we will prove that
S_{\gamma+\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\cap S_{\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}}=\emptyset
on the contrary.
Suppose that there exist
\mathbf{r},\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
(\lambda+\mathbf{r})(z_{i,i,i})=(\gamma+\mathbf{s})(z_{i,i,i})
and
(\lambda+\mathbf{r})(z_{i})=(\gamma+\mathbf{s})(z_{i})
, for any
i
.
Then
\lambda_{i}+r_{i}-(\lambda_{i}+r_{i})^{2}=\gamma_{i}+s_{i}-(\gamma_{i}+r_{i})^{2}
and

(\lambda_{i}+r_{i})^{3}-(\lambda_{i}+r_{i})^{2}=(\gamma_{i}+s_{i})^{3}-(\gamma_{i}+s_{i})^{2}
,
consequently
\lambda_{i}+r_{i}=\gamma_{i}+s_{i}
or
\{\lambda_{i}+r_{i},\gamma_{i}+s_{i}\}=\{0,1\}
for any
i
. This is a contradiction, since
\lambda-\gamma\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
∎

For a
\gamma\in\Lambda_{+}
, let
H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
be the Serre subcategory of
H_{n}\text{-mod}
generated by simple
H_{n}
-modules
W(\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in[\gamma]
. By Lemma
4.5
, we obtain the following decomposition of
H_{n}\text{-mod}
.

Corollary 4.6
.

We have the subcategory decomposition

H_{n}\text{-mod}=\bigoplus_{[\gamma]}H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
.

By Proposition
4.2
, we have the following subcategory equivalence.

Corollary 4.7
.

For each
\gamma\in\Lambda_{+}
, the subcategory
\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
is equivalent to
H_{n}^{\gamma}\text{-mod}
. Moreover we have the subcategory decomposition

\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}=\bigoplus_{[\gamma]}\Omega_{\mathbf{1}}^{[\gamma]}
.

4.2.
Equivalence between
H_{n}\text{-mod}
and a full subcategory of weight
W_{n}
-modules

For a finite dimensional
H_{n}
-module
V
, let each
h_{i}

act on it as the scalar
\alpha_{i}
.
Define the induced
U_{\partial}
-module

G_{1}(V)=\text{Ind}_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})H_{n}}^{U_{\partial}}V=U_{\partial}\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{h}_{n})H_{n}}V.

It is clear that
G_{1}(V)=\mathbb{C}[\partial_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,\partial_{n}^{\pm 1}]\otimes V
. Restricted to
W_{n}
,
G_{1}(V)
becomes a
W_{n}
-module which is still denoted by
G_{1}(V)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
H_{n}
-module, the action of
W_{n}
on
G_{1}(V)
satisfies:

(4.2)

\displaystyle h_{k}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{k}-r_{k})(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v),

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{k}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes z_{i,j}v+(\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1-\delta_{ij})\partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes v,

\displaystyle t_{i}t_{j}\partial_{j}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes\Big(z_{i,j,j}+(\alpha_{j}-r_{j})(z_{i,j}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)\Big)v,

where
\partial^{\mathbf{r}}=\partial_{1}^{r_{1}}\dots\partial_{n}^{r_{n}},\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},1\leq l,j,k\leq n
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle h_{k}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{k}-r_{k})(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v),

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{k}}\otimes v.

From

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=z_{i,j}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{-1}+h_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{-1},

\displaystyle t_{i}t_{j}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=z_{i,j,j}\partial_{i}^{-1}-\delta_{ij}(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}^{-1}+(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}(h_{j})\partial_{i}^{-1},

and
[z_{i,j},\partial_{k}]=[z_{i,j,j},\partial_{k}]=0
,
we can obtain that

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\ (z_{i,j}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{-1}+h_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{i}^{-1})\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\ \partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes z_{i,j}v+(\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1-\delta_{ij})\partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes v,

and

\displaystyle t_{i}t_{j}\partial_{j}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)=

\displaystyle(z_{i,j,j}\partial_{i}^{-1}-\delta_{ij}(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}^{-1}+(t_{i}\partial_{j})\partial_{i}\partial_{j}^{-1}(h_{j})\partial_{i}^{-1})\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes\big(z_{i,j,j}+(\alpha_{j}-r_{j})(z_{i,j}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)\big)v.

This completes the proof.
∎

Thus we have a functor
G_{1}
from

H_{n}\text{-mod}
to the category of weight modules over
W_{n}
. In the following theorem, we show that each
H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
is equivalent to some block of the cuspidal category.

Theorem 4.9
.

For each
\gamma\in\Lambda_{+}
, there exists an
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We choose an
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\alpha_{i}-\gamma_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z},|\alpha|+\gamma_{i}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

Claim.

G_{1}(V)\in\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
for any
V\in H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
.

We prove this claim by induction on the length of the
H_{n}
-module
V
. By Corollary
4.3
and Remark
4.4
, any simple
H_{n}
-module
in
H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
is isomorphic to
W(\lambda)
,
\lambda\in[\gamma]
. The action of
H_{n}
on
W(\lambda)
satisfies (
4.1
). By
(
4.1
) and (
4.2
), the action of
W_{n}
on
G_{1}(W(\lambda))
is such that:

\displaystyle t_{i}\partial_{j}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{e}_{j}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes(E_{ij}-E_{ii}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v,\ i\neq j,

\displaystyle t_{i}E_{n}\cdot(\partial^{\mathbf{r}}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\partial^{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{e}_{i}}\otimes\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big((E_{ij}-E_{ij}E_{jj})+(\alpha_{j}-r_{j})(E_{ij}-E_{ii}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)\big)v,

where
1\leq i,j\leq n,v\in W(\lambda)
.

To show
G_{1}(W(\lambda))\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{[\gamma]}
, we need to prove that
\partial_{i},t_{i}\partial_{j},t_{i}E_{n}
act injectively on
G_{1}(W(\lambda))
, for any

1\leq i\neq j\leq n
. By the second formula in (
4.2
),
\partial_{i}
acts injectively on
G_{1}(W(\lambda))
.
Note that
W(\lambda)\subset V(\lambda)
and
V(\lambda)=\oplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}V(\lambda)_{\mu}
,
where
V(\lambda)_{\mu}:=\{w\in V(\lambda)\mid E_{kk}w=\mu_{k}w,k=1\dots,n\}
.
For any nonzero
v=\sum_{\mu\in I}v_{\mu}\in W(\lambda)
, where
I\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a finite subset,
0\neq v_{\mu}\in V(\lambda)_{\mu}
, we have that

\displaystyle(E_{ij}-E_{ii}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in I}E_{ij}v_{\mu}+\sum_{\mu\in I}(-\mu_{i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu\in I}v^{\prime}_{\mu+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}+\sum_{\mu\in I}(-\mu_{i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\mu},

where
v^{\prime}_{\mu+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}:=E_{ij}v_{\mu}\in V(\lambda)_{\mu+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}}
. Since
I
is a finite set, we can choose
\mu^{\prime}\in I
such that
\mu^{\prime}_{i}
is minimal.
By the choice of
\alpha
, we have that
-\mu^{\prime}_{i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1\neq 0
.
So
(E_{ij}-E_{ii}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v\neq 0
,
and hence
t_{i}\partial_{j}

acts injectively on
G_{1}(W(\lambda))
, for any

1\leq i\neq j\leq n
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big((E_{ij}-E_{ij}E_{jj})+(\alpha_{j}-r_{j})(E_{i,j}-E_{i,i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)\big)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\sum_{\mu\in I}(1-\mu_{j}+\alpha_{j}-r_{j})v^{\prime}_{\mu+e_{i}-e_{j}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mu\in I}(\mu_{i}-\mu_{i}^{2})v_{\mu}+\sum_{\mu\in I}(\alpha_{i}-r_{i})\mu_{i}v_{\mu}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mu\in I}(|\alpha|-|\mathbf{r}|)(-\mu_{i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\sum_{\mu\in I}(1-\mu_{j}+\alpha_{j}-r_{j})v^{\prime}_{\mu+e_{i}-e_{j}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mu\in I}(|\alpha|-|\mathbf{r}|+\mu_{i})(-\mu_{i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)v_{\mu}.

The choice of
\alpha
forces that
(|\alpha|-|\mathbf{r}|+\mu_{i})(-\mu_{i}+\alpha_{i}-r_{i}+1)\neq 0
. Hence
t_{i}E_{n}
acts injectively on
G_{1}(W(\lambda))
, for any

1\leq i\leq n
. Therefore
G_{1}(W(\lambda))\in\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
.
By induction on the length of the
H_{n}
-module
V
, we can show that
G_{1}(V)\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{[\gamma]}
for any
V\in H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
.

We define the functor
F_{1}:\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}\rightarrow H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
such that
F_{1}(M)=M_{\alpha}
.
From
[H_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{n}]=0
, we have
H_{n}M_{\alpha}\subset M_{\alpha}
.
Hence
M_{\alpha}\in H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
. We can check that
G_{1}F_{1}=\text{id}_{\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}}
and
F_{1}G_{1}=\text{id}_{H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}}
. Therefore

H_{n}^{[\gamma]}\text{-mod}
is equivalent to
\mathcal{C}^{[\gamma]}_{\alpha}
.
∎

4.7

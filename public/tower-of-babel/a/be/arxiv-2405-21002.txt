The Renormalization Group for Large-Scale Structure: Primordial non-Gaussianities
Charalampos Nikolis, Henrique Rubira, Fabian Schmidt

Abstract
The renormalization group for large-scale structure (RG-LSS) describes the evolution of galaxy bias and stochastic parameters as a function of the cutoff Λ \Lambda . In this work, we introduce interaction vertices that describe primordial non-Gaussianity into the Wilson-Polchinski framework, thereby extending the free theory to the interacting case. The presence of these interactions forces us to include new operators and bias coefficients to the bias expansion to ensure closure under renormalization. We recover the previously-derived “scale-dependent bias” contributions, as well as a new (subdominant) stochastic contribution. We derive the renormalization group equations governing the RG-LSS for a large class of interactions which account for vertices at linear order in f NL f_{\rm NL} that parametrize interacting scalar and massive spinning fields during inflation. Solving the RG equations, we show the evolution of the non-Gaussian contributions to galaxy clustering as a function of scale.

The RG flow approach in principle allows for the resummation of Feynman diagrams, and therefore there are some grounds to expect that additional information can be extracted mainly when adding PNG interactions.

Our Renormalization Group Equations (RGE) describe a large class of cubic interactions during inflation. The equations are general enough to depend

These shapes can directly be implemented in the RG-flow we present in this work. Thus, the effect of new PNG shapes in the galaxy statistics can easily be retrieved from our general RG equations.

The generalized galaxy-bias partition function with non-Gaussianity

In this section we start by discussing the connection of the partition function formalism for the bias expansion to the usual textbook interactions in quantum field theory (QFT). Next, we present how to introduce non-Gaussianities in cosmology and also fix the notation for the new interacting vertex. Later we describe how to modify the galaxy bias expansion to include PNG. Finally, we derive the partition function for the galaxy bias including non-Gaussianities.

2.1
Parallelism with QFT

We begin with a QFT example, that will serve as basis for our later analysis. Consider a real scalar field with a cubic interaction of the type

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\phi(\square+m^{2})\phi-\frac{g}{3!}\phi^{3}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}\,.

(22)

We define the
free
partition function as

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{0}[J]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{iS_{0}[\phi]+i\int d^{4}xJ\phi}\equiv\int\mathcal{D}\phi\mathcal{P}[\phi]\,e^{i\int d^{4}xJ\phi}\,,

(23)

where
\mathcal{P}[\phi]
denotes the free (Gaussian) measure of the path-integral.
The textbook approach of handling QFT interactions is to set up a perturbative expansion of the interaction vertices, and find quantum corrections to the free theory, that will induce deviations from the free-Gaussian case

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[J]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{iS_{0}[\phi]+iS_{\rm int}[\phi]+i\int d^{4}xJ\phi}=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\mathcal{P}[\phi]\,e^{+i\int d^{4}xJ\phi}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1-\frac{ig}{3!}\int_{\bm{x}_{1}}\phi^{3}(\bm{x}_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{-ig}{3!}\right)^{2}\int_{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2}}\phi^{3}(\bm{x}_{1})\phi^{3}(\bm{x}_{2})+\dots\right)\,.

(24)

An important difference between the (usual) QFT case and the EFTofLSS, to be explored below, is the canonical linear coupling of the current to the field.

The
n
-point function for the interacting theory are calculated as the perturbative expansion of the interaction exponential

\displaystyle\langle\phi(\bm{k}_{1})\dots\phi(\bm{k}_{n})\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}[0]}\frac{\delta^{n}\mathcal{Z}}{\delta J_{i}\dots\delta J_{n}}\Big|_{J=0}=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\mathcal{P}[\phi]\,\phi(\bm{k}_{1})\dots\phi(\bm{k}_{n})\,e^{iS_{\rm int}[\phi]}

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\phi\mathcal{P}[\phi]\,\phi(\bm{k}_{1})\dots\phi(\bm{k}_{n})\left(1+iS_{\rm int}[\phi]+\frac{i^{2}}{2}S_{\rm int}[\phi]^{2}\dots\right)\,.

(25)

Corrections from the Gaussian free field are sourced by the interactions in the Lagrangian, or in other words by the
J^{0}
term in the partition function. Moreover, a linear coupling of the current with the field is added as a tool to calculate
n
-point interactions.

An alternative way of formulating interactions is to couple the current to nonlinear operators of the (free or interacting) field. These are termed
composite operators
. As we show now, in general one can switch between the two formulations via a nonlinear field redefinition, where we restrict ourselves to linear order in
g
.
The classical equation of motion

\displaystyle\left(\square+m^{2}\right)\phi=-\frac{g}{2}\phi^{2}\,,

(26)

admits a formal solution of the form

\displaystyle\phi_{\rm cl}=\phi-i\frac{g}{2}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}G\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\phi_{\rm cl}^{2}(x^{\prime})=\phi-i\frac{g}{2}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}G\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\phi^{2}(x^{\prime})+\mathcal{O}(g^{2})\,,

(27)

with
G(x,x^{\prime})
the Green’s function of the free equation of motion,

\displaystyle\left(\square+m^{2}\right)G(x,x^{\prime})=-i\delta_{\rm D}(x-x^{\prime})\,,

(28)

and
\phi
the free-field solution. This motivates us to do the following field redefinition

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}=\phi+i\frac{g}{3!}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}G\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\phi^{2}(x^{\prime})\,,

(29)

then

\displaystyle\phi=\tilde{\phi}-i\frac{g}{3!}\int d^{4}x^{\prime}G\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\tilde{\phi}^{2}(x^{\prime})+\mathcal{O}(g^{2})\,.

(30)

Plugging it back to the Lagrangian we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\phi}\left(\square+m^{2}\right)\tilde{\phi}+J\tilde{\phi}\,,

(31)

where the interaction term has been cancelled and the current is coupled to the full solution of the field. The composite-operator formulation is useful when no fundamental interaction Lagrangian is known, but one can still write down the equations of motion.

For the case of LSS, the tracer field
\delta_{g}[\delta^{(1)}]
in Eq. (
1
) is the main object from which we want to calculate correlators and therefore have a partition function for. We differentiate between two types of interactions that appear for matter tracers when constructing the partition function: the interactions present in the initial conditions due to inflation [imprinted in
\delta^{(1)}
as e.g. Eq. (
35
) below], and
the non-linearities introduced by the gravitational evolution [both in the operator definition Eq. (
4
) and in the
F
kernels of Eq. (
5
)].

For the first type of interactions, we can directly formulate the inflationary initial conditions as nonlocal interactions of
\delta^{(1)}

[see e.g. Eq. (
35
) below].
Therefore, it is convenient to include those interactions as new vertices in the
J
-independent (interaction) term, following the canonical steps outlined above leading to Eq. (
24
).

For the interactions from nonlinear evolution, on the other hand,
the composite-operator formulation is useful since the forward evolution of the density field can be described at the level of equations of motion via the EFTofLSS framework (whereas no fundamental Lagrangian is known for this problem). We then couple the current to the (classical) solution of the equations of motion.

2.2
Primordial non-Gaussianities

We now introduce non-Gaussianities in the context of cosmology. In the usual approach
[
39
,
35
]
, weakly non-Gaussian initial conditions are defined by expanding around a Gaussian field
\varphi_{G}
,

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{k})

\displaystyle={\varphi_{\rm G}}(\bm{k})+f_{\rm NL}\int_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}\hat{\delta}_{\rm D}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}_{12})K_{\rm NL}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}){\varphi_{\rm G}}(\bm{p}_{1}){\varphi_{\rm G}}(\bm{p}_{2})+\mathcal{O}[f_{\rm NL}^{2},g_{\rm NL}]\,,

(32)

with
K_{\rm NL}
the PNG kernel that depends on the structure of interactions during inflation.
Here,
\varphi
is the superhorizon gravitational potential during matter domination, which is directly related to the curvature perturbation in comoving gauge
\mathcal{R}
by
\varphi=(3/5)\mathcal{R}
.
This leads to a non-vanishing bispectrum for the primordial fluctuations,

\displaystyle\left<\varphi(\bm{k}_{1})\varphi(\bm{k}_{2})\varphi(\bm{k}_{3})\right>^{\prime}\equiv B_{\varphi}\left(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\right)=2f_{\rm NL}K_{\rm NL}(\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2})P_{\varphi}(k_{1})P_{\varphi}(k_{2})+2\,\text{perms}\,,

(33)

where two cyclic permutations of the three momenta
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
shall be considered.
An important limit for galaxy clustering is the
squeezed limit
defined as

\displaystyle B_{\varphi}\left(k_{\ell},|\bm{k}_{s}-\frac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{\ell}|,|\bm{k}_{s}+\frac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{\ell}|\right)\xrightarrow{k_{\ell}\ll k_{s}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt2f_{\rm NL}\Big[K_{\rm NL}(\bm{k}_{s},\bm{k}_{\ell})P_{\varphi}(k_{s})P_{\varphi}(k_{\ell})+K_{\rm NL}(-\bm{k}_{s},\bm{k}_{\ell})P_{\varphi}(k_{s})P_{\varphi}(k_{\ell})\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+2f_{\rm NL}K_{\rm NL}(-\bm{k}_{s},\bm{k}_{s})\left[P_{\varphi}(k_{s})\right]^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{k^{2}_{\ell}}{k^{2}_{s}}\right)\,.

(34)

The first two terms in the RHS are what we usually refer to as the squeezed limit of the kernel, which can be uniquely determined by the bispectrum. We refer to those terms as the
scattering interaction
, for reasons that will become clear in Sec.
3.2
. The last term is what we refer to as the
annihilation interaction
which will contribute to the stochastic bias operators.

We can immediately translate the relation Eq. (
32
) to one involving the linearly evolved matter overdensity

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \delta^{(1)}(\bm{k})&=\delta^{(1)}_{\rm G}(\bm{k})+f_{\rm NL}\int_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}\hat{\delta}_{\rm D}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}_{12})K_{\rm NL}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2})\frac{M(|\bm{p}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2}|)}{M(p_{1})M(p_{2})}\delta^{(1)}_{\rm G}(\bm{p}_{1})\delta^{(1)}_{\rm G}(\bm{p}_{2})\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt+\mathcal{O}[f_{\rm NL}^{2},g_{\rm NL}],\end{aligned}}

5
Discussion and conclusions

In this work, we have extended the RG-LSS framework of
[
44
,
45
]
to include interaction terms from primordial non-Gaussianities. We have considered a cubic vertex Eq. (
35
), and work to linear order in
f_{\rm NL}
throughout. We have found interaction terms Eq. (
64
) that can be added directly to the Lagrangian. The new interaction can be easily incorporated using the Wilson-Polchinski framework to understand the running of bias parameters when including PNG, providing a master formula Eq. (
78
) valid at linear order in
f_{\rm NL}
. We identify the main diagrams [see Eq. (
70
) and App.
C
] from the interaction term that contribute to the running of the bias.
In addition to the well-known squeezed limit of the interaction kernel (
scattering
contribution), which has been the basis for the literature on the effect of PNG on LSS so far, we identify a new
annihilation
contribution which is in a different kinematic regime than the squeezed limit. We argue that it will be absorbed by a new stochastic contribution to the partition function, but defer a detailed investigation of this new contribution to future work.

Using the squeezed limit of the interaction kernel, parametrized as
\propto f_{\rm NL}(k_{\ell}/k_{s})^{\Delta}
, we derive the RGE describing the running of the bias parameters for operators induced by spin-0 and 2 interactions during inflation,
and show that non-Gaussian bias terms are in fact generated by the RG flow.
The RGE for the bias evolution are still linear, as in the case for Gaussian initial conditions.
We provide solutions for the bias running for some specific scenarios (spin-0 local and equilateral and spin-2 equilateral). These solutions in fact scale non-linearly with the interaction strength
f_{\rm NL}
. This also applies to the Gaussian bias coefficients, i.e. those present in the bias expansion without PNG.
Moreover, the running of non-Gaussian coefficients also depends on the Gaussian terms and is enhanced for larger values of
\Delta
.

We find that the solutions for the evolution of the coefficients present a rich structure (see e.g. Fig.
6
), much richer than the case for bias evolution with Gaussian initial conditions, which could substantially change the discussion around fixed points.

The RG solutions predict the value of the PNG bias coefficients, once fixed at a given scale, at any other scale. This evolution can be compared with measurements of the PNG bias coefficients as a function of scale (e.g. in simulations at the field level
[
78
]
), which provides an independent validation of the EFT description of galaxy statistics. Should PNG be detected in actual data, this validation can also be applied there. Moreover, the evolution of the non-Gaussian bias coefficients with scale is important to put priors on these coefficients in field-level inference analyses of PNG
[
79
]
, which necessarily work at a finite scale
[
80
]
. Whether the RGE is able to ressum part of diagrams improving the theoretical prediction is something we leave for a future project.

In the future it would also be interesting to generalize the interaction terms to include higher-order vertices, such as
g_{\rm NL}
and higher-in-
f_{\rm NL}
contributions. Going to higher-order in PNG including new types of vertices could eventually induce (
\Lambda
-dependent) corrections to
f_{\rm NL}
itself, similarly to what happens in the context of QFT. Also, the structure of stochasticity when including non-Gaussianities changes, as we point out in App.
C
.
A related issue is the ambiguity of the kernel
K_{\rm NL}
beyond the squeezed limit when given only the bispectrum as constraint
[
39
,
59
]
.
We leave a more in-depth study of these issues for a future work.

\displaystyle\hskip 76.82234pt\times\exp\left(\int_{\bm{k}}J_{\Lambda}(\bm{k})\sum_{O}b_{O}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}O[\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}+\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}](-\bm{k})+\mathcal{O}\left[J^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}\int\mathcal{D}\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}

\displaystyle\quad\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\left(\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}(\bm{k})\right)^{2}}{P_{\rm L,\Lambda}(k)}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\left(\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{k})\right)^{2}}{P_{\rm shell}(k)}+\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm scatter}+\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm annihilation}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(\int_{\bm{k}}J_{\Lambda}(\bm{k})\sum_{O}b_{O}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}O[\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}+\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}](-\bm{k})+\mathcal{O}\left[J^{2}\right]\right)\,,

(145)

where we wrote the kinetic terms explicitly.
The
\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm scatter}
integral, as said before, contributes to the squeezed limit corrections and we calculate them in App.
D
. Our goal is to understand the effect of the
\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm annihilation}
in the RG-flow. In order to gain more physical insights about this term we will apply a similar transformation as the one described in App.
A
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{\Lambda}^{(1)}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}(\bm{k})-f_{\rm NL}M(k)\int_{\bm{p}_{2},\bm{p}_{3}}\hat{\delta}_{\rm D}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}_{23})\frac{K_{\rm NL}(\bm{p}_{2},\bm{p}_{3})}{M(p_{2})M(p_{3})}\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{p}_{2})\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{p}_{3})\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}_{\rm shell}^{(1)}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{k})-f_{\rm NL}M(k)\int_{\bm{p}_{2},\bm{p}_{3}}\hat{\delta}_{\rm D}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}_{23})\frac{K_{\rm NL}(\bm{p}_{2},\bm{p}_{3})}{M(p_{2})M(p_{3})}\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{p}_{2})\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{p}_{3})\,,

(146)

with the integration measure of the path-integral [see Eq. (
127
)]

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\Lambda}+\mathcal{O}(f_{\rm NL}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}\tilde{\delta}_{\rm shell}^{(1)}+\mathcal{O}(f_{\rm NL}^{2})\,.

(147)

We notice that with this transformation the annihilation part is absorbed in the field redefinition

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\left(\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}(\bm{k})\right)^{2}}{P_{\rm L,\Lambda}(k)}+\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm annihilation}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\left(\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\Lambda}\right)^{2}(\bm{k})}{P_{\rm L,\Lambda}(k)}\,,

(148)

while the scattering term will remain unchanged at linear order in
f_{\rm NL}

\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm scatter}\left(\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda},\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}\right)=\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm scatter}\left(\tilde{\delta}_{\Lambda}^{(1)},\tilde{\delta}_{\rm shell}^{(1)}\right)\,.

(149)

The kinetic term with the shell transforms as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\left(\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}(\bm{k})\right)^{2}}{P_{\rm shell}(k)}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\bm{k}}\frac{\left(\tilde{\delta}_{\rm shell}^{(1)}\right)^{2}(\bm{k})}{P_{\rm shell}(k)}-S_{\rm int}^{(3)_{\rm shell}}(\tilde{\delta}_{\rm shell}^{(1)})\,,

(150)

and the remaining three-shell interaction will exactly cancel the interaction vertex Eq. (
140
).
After the field redefinition and grouping the terms together we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[J_{\Lambda}]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{P}[\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\Lambda}]\mathcal{D}\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\rm shell}\mathcal{P}[\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\rm shell}]\exp\left(\left[S_{\rm int}^{(2)_{\rm shell}}\right]_{\rm scatter}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\exp\left(\int_{\bm{k}}J_{\Lambda}(\bm{k})\sum_{O}b_{O}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}O[\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\Lambda}+\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}_{\rm shell}](-\bm{k})+\mathcal{O}(J_{\Lambda}^{2})\right)\,,

(151)

in which we observe that the annihilation interaction is absorbed by the field transformation leaving only the scattering vertex. Furthermore, the terms coupled to the current now contain a non-Gaussian contribution: we have to solve the inverse transformation of Eq. (
146
) to get
\delta^{(1)}
in terms of
\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}
at linear order in
f_{\rm NL}
and the resulting operators coupled to the current will then be of the form
O[\delta^{(1)}]=O[\delta^{(1)}[\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}]]\equiv O[\tilde{\delta}^{(1)}]
. Since we work only at linear order in
f_{\rm NL}
the effect of the scattering vertex is the same as before the transformation because we only consider coupling with the Gaussian counterpart of the
\tilde{\delta}_{\Lambda}^{(1)}
[couplings with the non-Gaussian will give terms of
\mathcal{O}(f_{\rm NL}^{2})
].

We can immediately see that the field redefinition Eq. (
146
) will not change the shell corrections
[\mathcal{S}_{O}^{2}]_{\rm free}
defined in Eq. (
70
) since

\displaystyle[\mathcal{S}_{\delta^{2}}^{2}]_{\rm free}\supset M(0)\int_{\bm{p}}\frac{K_{\rm NL}(\bm{p},-\bm{p})}{M^{2}(p)}P_{\rm shell}(p)=0\,,

(152)

and therefore the annihilation term does not source any
J^{1}
corrections to the bias parameters.
Despite not changing the result for the
J^{1}
terms calculated in this work, the annihilation interactions induce the running of stochastic coefficients (see
[
45
]
for a description of those terms for the Gaussian case).
As seen in
[
45
]
, the main source of stochasticity (or
J^{m}

) come from shell correlators such as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{O_{1},O_{2}}^{22}[\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}](\bm{k}_{1},\bm{k}_{2})=\sum_{n_{1}\geq 2,n_{2}\geq 2}\left\langle O_{1}^{(n_{1}),(2)_{\rm shell}}[\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda},\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}](\bm{k}_{1})O_{2}^{(n_{2}),(2)_{\rm shell}}[\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda},\delta^{(1)}_{\rm shell}](\bm{k}_{2})\right\rangle_{\rm shell}\,.

(153)

We have then, for instance, a new non-Gaussian contribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\delta\delta^{2}}^{22}(\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime})=M(k)\hat{\delta}_{\rm D}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})\int_{\bm{p}}\frac{K_{\rm NL}(\bm{p},-\bm{p})}{M^{2}(p)}\left[P_{\rm shell}(p)\right]^{2}+\mathcal{O}[\delta^{(1)}_{\Lambda}]\,,

[

We leave a more detailed analysis of stochasticity in the presence of non-Gaussianities for a future work.

D.1
Displacements of
\Psi

Before evaluating the shell integrals, we discuss one important point when evaluating expectation values including
\Psi
.
It is important to evaluate the operator
\Psi
at the (Lagrangian) position
\bm{q}
in the initial conditions that corresponds to a given final-time (Eulerian) position
\bm{x}
. Physically, this is due to the fact that the PNG interactions are imprinted at initial time. Formally, it is required to cancel the non-Galilean-invariant displacement terms involving the gradient of the potential
\partial_{i}\Phi_{g}

[
22
,
35
]
.
So at the Lagrangian position
\bm{q}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s}
, with
\mathbf{s}
the displacements, we can write

\Psi(\bm{q})=\Psi(\bm{x}-\mathbf{s}(\bm{q}))\,.

(158)

Note that the displacement field admits a perturbative expansion

\bm{s}(\bm{q})=\bm{s}_{(1)}(\bm{q})+\bm{s}_{(2)}(\bm{q})+\bm{s}_{(3)}(\bm{q})+\dots\,,

(159)

and is also evaluated at the Lagrangian position
\bm{q}
, meaning that it should also be expanded by

\bm{s}(\bm{q})=\bm{s}(\bm{x}-\bm{s})=\bm{s}(\bm{x})-\bm{s}(\bm{q})\nabla\bm{s}(\bm{x})+\dots\,.

(160)

With the above, we have at second-order in perturbation theory
[
29
]

\displaystyle\Psi^{[2]}(\bm{q})=\Psi(\bm{x})-s_{(1)}^{i}\partial_{i}\Psi(\bm{x})\;,

(161)

with

\mathbf{s}_{(1)}=-\frac{\nabla}{\nabla^{2}}\delta^{(1)}\;.

(162)

At third order, we have

\displaystyle\Psi^{[3]}(\mathbf{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle=\Psi(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{s})

\displaystyle=\Psi(\mathbf{x})-s_{(1)}^{i}\partial_{i}\Psi(\mathbf{x})-s_{(2)}^{i}\partial_{i}\Psi(\mathbf{x})+s^{i}_{(1)}\partial_{i}(s^{j}_{(1)})\partial_{j}\Psi(\mathbf{x})+\frac{1}{2}s_{(1)}^{i}s_{(1)}^{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Psi(\mathbf{x})\;,

(163)

with

\mathbf{s}_{(2)}=\frac{3}{14}\frac{\nabla}{\nabla^{2}}\mathcal{G}_{2}\;.

(164)

For the corrections of
\Psi
we have to consider its expansion at fourth order, including terms up to third order in the displacements

\displaystyle\Psi^{[4]}(\bm{q})=\Psi^{[3]}(\bm{x})-s_{(3)}^{i}\partial_{i}\Psi+s_{(1)}^{j}\partial_{j}s_{(2)}^{i}\partial_{i}\Psi+s_{(2)}^{j}\partial_{j}s_{(1)}^{i}\partial_{i}\Psi-\frac{1}{2}s^{j}_{(1)}s^{k}_{(1)}\left(\partial_{j}\partial_{k}s^{i}_{(1)}\right)\partial_{i}\Psi

\displaystyle+s^{i}_{(2)}s^{j}_{(1)}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Psi-s^{k}_{(1)}\partial_{k}s^{j}_{(1)}\partial_{j}s^{i}_{(1)}\partial_{i}\Psi-s^{k}_{(1)}\partial_{k}s^{i}_{(1)}s^{j}_{(1)}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\Psi-\frac{1}{6}s^{i}_{(1)}s^{j}_{(1)}s^{k}_{(1)}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\Psi\,,

(165)

with
[
81
]

\displaystyle\bm{s}_{(3)}=\frac{i}{6}\frac{\bm{k}}{k^{2}}\int_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p},\bm{p}_{3}}\hat{\delta}_{\rm D}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}_{123})\delta^{(1)}(\bm{p}_{1})\delta^{(1)}(\bm{p}_{2})\delta^{(1)}(\bm{p}_{3})

(166)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left[\frac{5}{7}\sigma^{2}_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}\sigma^{2}_{\bm{p}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2},\bm{p}_{3}}-\frac{1}{3}\left(-2-3\sigma^{2}_{\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2}}+2\frac{(\bm{p}_{1}\cdot\bm{p}_{2})\;(\bm{p}_{2}\cdot\bm{p}_{3})\;(\bm{p}_{1}\cdot\bm{p}_{3})}{p_{1}^{2}p_{2}^{2}p_{3}^{2}}\right)\right]\,.

Note that corresponding displacement contributions also exist for the Gaussian operators such as
\delta
,
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, …, but these are included in the perturbation theory kernels.

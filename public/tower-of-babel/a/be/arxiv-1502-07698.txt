Classifying Toric and Semitoric Fans by Lifting Equations from 𝐒𝐋 𝟐 ​ ( ℤ ) \boldsymbol{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
Daniel M. KANE, Joseph PALMER and Álvaro PELAYO

Abstract
We present an algebraic method to study four-dimensional toric varieties by lifting matrix equations from the special linear group SL 2 ​ ( ℤ ) {\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}) to its preimage in the universal cover of SL 2 ​ ( ℝ ) {\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}) . With this method we recover the classification of two-dimensional toric fans, and obtain a description of their semitoric analogue. As an application to symplectic geometry of Hamiltonian systems, we give a concise proof of the connectivity of the moduli space of toric integrable systems in dimension four, recovering a known result, and extend it to the case of semitoric integrable systems with a fixed number of focus-focus points and which are in the same twisting index class. In particular, we show that any semitoric system with precisely one focus-focus singular point can be continuously deformed into a system in the same isomorphism class as the Jaynes–Cummings model from optics.

In this paper we present an algebraic viewpoint to study non-singular complete four-dimensional toric varieties, based on the study of matrix relations in the special linear group
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
. Indeed, one can associate to a rational convex polygon
\Delta
the collection of primitive integer inwards pointing normal vectors to its faces, called a
toric fan
. This is a
d
-tuple

\displaystyle(v_{0}=v_{d},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d},

where
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is the number of faces. A
Delzant polygon

(or
toric polygon
) is one for which
\det(v_{i},v_{i+1})=1

for each
0\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
. This determinant condition forces the
vectors to satisfy the linear equations

a_{i}v_{i}=v_{i-1}+v_{i+1}
, for
i=0,\ldots,d-1
where
v_{-1}=v_{d-1}
,
which are parameterized
by integers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(see Lemma
3.3
and Fulton
[
18
, p. 43]
). These integers satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
1&a_{0}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
1&a_{1}\end{matrix}\right)\cdots\left(\begin{matrix}0&-1\\
1&a_{d-1}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)

(1.1)

(this equation appears in
[
18
, p. 44]
) but in fact, as can be seen in the exercises on p. 44 of
[
18
]
,
not all integers satisfying equation (
1.1
) correspond to a toric fan. To deal with this issue we take an approach similar to that employed in
[
42
]
. In particular, the identity in equation (
1.1
) in
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
can be lifted to the group
G
presented as

\displaystyle G\cong\big\langle S,T\,|\,STS=T^{-1}ST^{-1}\big\rangle

and which, we will see in Lemma
3.2
, satisfies
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\cong G/\big(S^{4}\big)
. The group
G
, as is shown in
[
42
, Section 8.3]
, is isomorphic to the pre-image of
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
in the universal cover of
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
(Proposition
3.7
), and thus we can define what we call the
winding number
of an element of
g\in G
that evaluates to the identity in
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
. Roughly speaking, we view
g
as a word in
S
and
T
and by applying this word to a vector one term at a time we produce a path around the origin. We define the winding number of
g
to be the winding number of this path in the classical sense.

By considering a lift of equation (
1.1
) to the group
G
, we obtain an equation with the property that a collection of integers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1}
is a solution if and only if they correspond to a toric fan. Furthermore, from the integers it is straightforward to recover
the fan up to the appropriate isomorphism. Thus, the collection of toric fans can be studied by instead studying all
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1}
which
satisfy the equation in
G
. This allows us to prove some classical results about toric fans from a new perspective (Section
4
), and generalize these results to the semitoric case (Section
5
).

Recall from
[
37
,
38
]
that a semitoric integrable system is a completely integrable system with two degrees of freedom and for which one of the Hamiltonians generates a periodic flow (see Definition
6.1
). Semitoric systems are a generalization of toric integrable systems which can have, in addition to elliptic type singularities, also focus-focus singularities
(called nodal singularities in the context of Lefschetz fibrations and algebraic geometry). Focus-focus singularities appear in algebraic
geometry
[
19
]
, symplectic topology
[
17
,
32
,
44
,
47
]
, and many simple physical models such as the spherical pendulum
[
1
]
and the Jaynes–Cummings system
[
12
,
26
,
41
]
. Associated to a semitoric system there is also a collection of vectors
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})
, defined up to the appropriate notion of isomorphism, which satisfy more complicated equations (given explicitly in Definition
2.9
). Any collection of such vectors is henceforth referred to as a
semitoric fan
. A semitoric fan can be thought of as a toric fan for which the relations between some pairs of adjacent vectors have been changed as a result of the presence of the focus-focus singularities. Roughly speaking, semitoric fans encode aspects of the singular affine structure induced by the singular fibration associated to a semitoric integrable system. This affine structure also plays a role in parts of symplectic topology, see for
instance Borman–Li–Wu
[
5
]
, and mirror symmetry, see Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
20
,
21
,
30
]
.

We present two theorems in this paper which are applications of the algebraic method we introduce. The first gives a classification of semitoric fans and the second is an application of the first which describes the path-connected components of the moduli space of semitoric integrable systems with a fixed number of focus-focus singular points.

Theorem 1.1
.

Any semitoric fan may be obtained from a standard semitoric fan in a finite number of steps using four standard
transformations.

A more detailed description of Theorem
1.1
is given in Theorem
2.14
and the definitions of the standard semitoric fans and the four standard transformations used in Theorem
1.1
are given in Definition
2.11
. As an application of Theorem
1.1
to the symplectic geometry of integrable systems we will prove Theorem
1.2

The articles
[
37
,
38
]
give a classification of semitoric systems in terms of several invariants, which can be essentially encoded into a polygon (a generalization of its toric counterpart and very closely related to a semitoric fan) together
with finitely many marked interior points, each of which is labeled by a formal power series in two variables and an integer. In contrast to the toric case, a semitoric system has a family of polygons associated to it, each constructed using a toric momentum map defined by removing certain closed subsets; the collection of all such polygons associated to a given system is henceforth referred to as the
semitoric
polygon
associated to the system. The marked interior points are in fact the images of the focus-focus points of the integrable system
and the formal power series in two variables determines the semiglobal model around the focus-focus fiber up to a suitable notion of isomorphism (cf.
[
43
,
45
]
). The collection of integers labeling the focus-focus points for each polygon is the
twisting index invariant
. This invariant encodes how the semiglobal model of the focus-focus fiber “sits” relative to the toric momentum map used to define each element of the semitoric polygon, so the integer assigned to a specific focus-focus point depends on the choice of polygon but the difference between the twisting index of consecutive (ordered according to the
x
-component) values does not depend on the choice of polygon and, roughly speaking, this difference measures the “twist” in the topology of the singular Lagrangian fibration between them. Here we have not described the group action on the space of possible invariants, which is necessary so that choices made when constructing the invariants do not effect the outcome. See Section
6.1
for the precise definition of these invariants and for a discussion of the group action.

For the next result we need to consider the moduli space of semitoric systems as a topological space so we can study continuous families in this space. The natural topology in this situation is the one induced by the metric on the moduli space of semitoric systems
[
35
]
and
which is described in Section
6.2.3
.

For this paper we only define this metric on certain subsets of the full moduli space because this is all that is necessary to produce the same topology, see Remark

.
The metric is produced by pulling back a metric
from the space of all invariants of semitoric integrable systems. The role played by the polygon invariant in the definition of this metric is related to the Duistermaat–Heckman measure.

The twisting index invariant of a system is an equivalence class of a list of integers assigned to each choice of toric momentum map. The integer associated to a given focus-focus point is not a well-defined invariant when considered individually, but one can define the “twisting index class” of a system. Two systems are in the same twisting index class if there exists a choice of polygon for each system such that the integers for these polygons agree (the precise notion is in Definition
6.15
). Now we can state our second main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

With respect to the topology induced by the metric defined in
[
35
]
, any two semitoric systems with the same number of focus-focus singular points which are in the same twisting index class may be continuously deformed into one another, up to isomorphism, via a continuous path of semitoric systems with the same number of focus-focus singular points and in the same twisting index class.

We state a detailed version of this result later in the paper as Theorem
2.19
.

Remark 1.3
.

The topology from
[
35
]
automatically places isomorphism classes of systems with different numbers of focus-focus points into different components of the space. It would be very interesting to have a version of the metric, or at least topology, from

The most pressing issue being if such a deformation can really occur through semitoric systems, i.e., whether the non-degeneracy condition can be satisfied for all values of the deformation (under some extra natural hypotheses, degeneracies must occur, see
[

The Jaynes–Cummings system is an important example of a semitoric system with precisely one focus-focus point and is studied for example

Since there is only one twisting index class of semitoric systems with exactly

With respect to the topology induced by the metric defined in

2
Fans, symplectic geometry, and winding numbers

2.1
Toric fans

A toric variety is a variety which contains an algebraic torus as a dense (in the Zariski topology) open subset such that the standard action of the torus on itself can be extended to the whole variety. That is, a toric variety is the closure of an algebraic torus orbit
[
18
]
. By an
algebraic torus we mean the product
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, where
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}={\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. Under some mild assumptions (which are automatically satisfied if the variety is smooth), the geometry of a toric variety is completely determined by the associated fan
[
18
, Section 1.5]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
rational strongly convex cone
is a convex cone of a vector space with apex at the origin generated by a finite number of integral vectors which contains no line through the origin. A
fan
is a set of rational strongly convex cones in a real vector space such that the face of each cone is also a cone and the intersection of any two cones is a face of each.

Definition
2.1
is the general notion of a fan in arbitrary dimension; in this paper we will be concerned with two-dimensional smooth compact toric varieties and their associated fans, which for simplicity we will call toric fans. We identify such fans with a sequence of points in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
toric fan
is a sequence of lattice points

\displaystyle(v_{0}=v_{d},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}

labeled in
counter-clockwise order such that each pair of adjacent vectors generates all of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and the angle between any two adjacent vectors is less than
\pi
radians. That is,
\det(v_{i},v_{i+1})=1
for
i=0,\ldots,d-1
.

Definition 2.3
.

For the purposes of this paper by
convex polygon
we mean the intersection in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
of finitely or infinitely many closed half planes such that there are at most finitely many corner points in each compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
Delzant polygon
(or
toric polygon
) is a compact convex polygon
\Delta
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
which is simple, rational, and smooth. Recall
v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
is primitive if
v=kw
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
implies
k=1
and
w=v
.

1)

\Delta
is
simple
if there are exactly two edges meeting at each vertex;

2)

\Delta
is
rational
if for each edge
e
of
\Delta
there exists a vector in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
which is normal to
e
;

3)

\Delta
is
smooth
if the inwards pointing primitive vectors normal to any pair of adjacent edges form a basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

Delzant polygons were introduced in the work of Delzant
[
14
]
in symplectic geometry, who built on the work of Atiyah
[
2
]
, Kostant
[
31
]
, and Guillemin-Sternberg
[
24
]
to give a classification of symplectic toric
4
-manifolds in terms of the Delzant polygon (in fact, their work was in any dimension and higher dimensional toric integrable systems correspond to higher dimensional
Delzant polytopes
which satisfy analogous conditions to those given in this Definition
2.4
). Delzant polygons are similar to Newton polygons, as in
[
18
]
, except that the vertices of Delzant polygons do not have to have integer coordinates. Just as in the case with Newton polygons, a toric fan may be produced from a Delzant polygon by considering the collection of inwards pointing normal vectors of the polygon. Notice that since Delzant polygons are required to be convex we automatically have that the angle between adjacent vectors in the fan is less than
\pi
. The natural action of
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
induces an action on the set of Delzant polygons. In
[
18
]
Fulton stated the following result for toric fans. We have adapted the statement to relate it to Delzant polygons.

Theorem 2.5

(Fulton
[
18
, p. 44]
)
.

Up to the action of
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
, every Delzant polygon can be obtained from a Delzant triangle, rectangle, or Hirzebruch trapezoid by a finite number of corner chops.

Figure 1:
(a) The three minimal models from Theorem
2.5
. (b) An illustration of a Delzant polygon produced by corner chopping the Hirzebruch trapezoid.

The minimal models (the Delzant triangle, rectangle, and Hirzebruch trapezoid with parameter
k>1
) are defined in Definition
4.9
and depicted in Fig.
1
. The corner chop operation is defined in Definition
2.11
and is a standard operation in algebraic and symplectic geometry (see, for instance,
[
28
]
). It corresponds to an equivariant symplectic blowup.

The proof of Theorem
2.5
sketched by Fulton in
[
18
, Section 2.5]
, which uses only two-dimensional geometry and basic combinatorial arguments, is relatively long and does not immediately generalize to the case of semitoric polygons we study in this paper. In Section
4
we provide an alternative proof using
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
-relations which may be easily extended to the semitoric case.

The moduli space of toric polygons was endowed with a metric given by the Lebesgue measure of the symmetric difference in
[
36
]
, which we denote by
d_{\mathbb{T}}
, and in that paper the following consequence of Theorem
2.5
was proved:

Theorem 2.6

(
[
36
]
)
.

The moduli space of toric polygons is path-connected with respect to the topology induced by
d_{\mathbb{T}}
.

That is, any two toric polygons may be deformed onto each other continuously via a path of toric polygons. One shows this by first knowing how to generate all toric polygons as in Theorem
2.5
. Then one shows, using elementary analysis, that the three minimal models can be continuously transformed into one another and that the corner chop operation is continuous. Again, let us emphasize that Theorem
2.5
is not new, but in this paper we will prove it from a new viewpoint which we believe to be more natural and which generalizes to the case of semitoric fans.

2.2
Semitoric fans

Motivated by semitoric polygons (originally defined in
[
38
, Definition 2.5]
) we define semitoric fans. A semitoric polygon is a family of polygons which are similar to Delzant polygons except that the presence of the focus-focus singular points in semitoric systems may cause some corners of the polygons to not satisfy the smoothness condition of Definition
2.4
. Such corners must satisfy other conditions and are known as fake or hidden corners. They can exist on the top or bottom boundary of the polygon but there will always be an infinite subfamily of semitoric polygons related to the system for which the hidden and fake corners are all on the top boundary, and we will use one of these polygons to produce the associated semitoric fan. The choice of which polygon with this property is used is reflected in the symmetry group of semitoric fans which we introduce in Definition
2.10
. Let

\displaystyle T=\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

Definition 2.7

(
[
38
, Section 4.1 and Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
v,w\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. The ordered pair
(v,w)
of vectors:

1)

is
on the top boundary
if both vectors are in the open lower half-plane;

2)

satisfies the
Delzant condition
if
\det(v,w)=1
;

3)

satisfies the
hidden condition
if
\det(v,Tw)=1
; and

4)

satisfies the
fake condition
if
\det(v,Tw)=0
.

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that a pair
(v,w)
satisfies both the fake and Delzant conditions if and only if

\displaystyle v=\left(\begin{matrix}k+\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon\end{matrix}\right)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad w=\left(\begin{matrix}k\\
\varepsilon\end{matrix}\right)

for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}
and in order for such a pair to be in the top boundary we can only have the case in which

\varepsilon=-1
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
d>2
. A
semitoric fan
is a collection of primitive vectors
(v_{0}=v_{d},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
labeled in counter-clockwise order such that each pair of adjacent vectors
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
for
i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
is labeled as a
Delzant
,
fake
, or
hidden
corner. We require that each labeled pair of vectors satisfies the corresponding condition from Definition
2.7
and we further require that all fake and hidden corners be on the top boundary. The
defect
of a semitoric fan is the number of corners which are either
fake or hidden.

Notice that the labeling of the pairs is required only because of the case described in Remark
2.8
in which a pair can satisfy both the fake and Delzant conditions. In all other cases the corner type of a pair of vectors can be uniquely determined by inspecting the
vectors involved.

Definition
2.9
is inspired by the toric case. Theorem
2.5
states that any toric fan can be produced from a minimal model using only corner chops. Similarly, our goal is to use a series of transformations to relate any semitoric fan to a standard form up to the action of the appropriate symmetry group.

Definition 2.10
.

The
symmetry group of semitoric fans
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\big\{T^{k}\,|\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\big\},

where
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
acts on a semitoric fan by acting on each vector in the fan in the standard fashion.

Definition 2.11
.

1.

Let
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
. The
standard semitoric fan of defect
c
is the fan
(u_{0},\ldots,u_{c+3})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{c+4}
given by

\displaystyle u_{0}=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}c\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{3}=\left(\begin{matrix}-1\\
0\end{matrix}\right),

and

\displaystyle u_{4+n}=\left(\begin{matrix}-c+n\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)

for
n=0,\ldots,c-1
in which the first four pairs of vectors are Delzant corners and the rest are fake corners.

2.

Let
(v_{0}=v_{d},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
be a semitoric fan. The following are called the
four fan transformations
:

(a)

Suppose that
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
is a Delzant corner for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
. Then

\displaystyle(v_{0},\ldots,v_{i},v_{i}+v_{i+1},v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d+1}

obtained by inserting the sum of two adjacent vectors between them is a semitoric fan with the new pairs
(v_{i},v_{i}+v_{i+1})
and
(v_{i}+v_{i+1},v_{i+1})
both labeled as Delzant corners (Lemma
5.1
). The process of producing this new fan from the original generalizes
corner chopping
for toric fans (cf.
[
28
]
). For this reason, it is also referred to as a corner chop(ping) in the context of semitoric fans.

(b)

Suppose that
(v_{i-1},v_{i})
and
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
are Delzant corners and that
v_{i}=v_{i-1}+v_{i+1}
. Then

\displaystyle(v_{0},\ldots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d-1}

is a semitoric fan with the pair
(v_{i-1},v_{i+1})
being labeled as a Delzant corner (Lemma
5.2
). This process of producing this new fan from the original generalizes
reverse corner chopping
for toric fans (cf.
[
28
]
). For this reason, it is also referred to as a reverse corner chop(ping) in the context of semitoric fans.

(c)

Suppose that the pair
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
is a hidden corner. Then

\displaystyle(v_{0},\ldots,v_{i},Tv_{i+1},v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d+1}

is a semitoric fan with
(v_{i},Tv_{i+1})
a Delzant corner and
(Tv_{i+1},v_{i+1})
a fake corner (Lemma
5.3
). The process of producing this fan is called
removing the hidden corner
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
.

(d)

Suppose that the pair
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
is a fake corner and the pair
(v_{i+1},v_{i+2})
is a Delzant corner on the top boundary. Then

\displaystyle(v_{0},\ldots,v_{i},Tv_{i+2},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}

is a semitoric fan with
(v_{i},Tv_{i+2})
a Delzant corner and
(Tv_{i+2},v_{i+2})
a fake corner (Lemma
5.4
). The process of producing this fan is called
commuting a fake and a Delzant corner
.

Remark 2.12
.

The corner chop and reverse corner chop can be obtained as equivariant symplectic blowups and blowdowns, respectively.

2.4
Applications to symplectic geometry

While Delzant polygons are in correspondence with closed toric manifolds, semitoric polygons are associated with the so-called semitoric integrable systems. A
semitoric integrable system
(or
semitoric manifold
) is given by a triple
(M,\omega,F\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
where
(M,\omega)
is
a connected symplectic
4
-manifold and
F\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is an integrable system given by two maps
J,H\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
J
is a proper map which generates a periodic flow (see Definition
6.1
for the precise definition). In
[
38
]
the authors prove a result analogous to Delzant’s, classifying semitoric systems satisfying a mild assumption via a list of ingredients which includes a family of polygons (there is an overview of this result in Section
6.1
).

In
[
35
]
the second author defines a metric space structure for the moduli space of semitoric systems using
[
38
]
, which is related to the Duistermaat–Heckman measure. The metric is designed to induce a topology that respects continuous transformations of the invariants of semitoric systems (this follows tautologically from the definition) and
to be compatible with the known topology on toric integrable systems from
[
36
]
, in the sense that the projection map from isomorphism classes of compact semitoric systems with zero focus-focus points to toric systems is continuous, as proven in
[
35
, Corollary 4.14]
(this map is an projection since the isomorphism for toric integrable systems is strictly weaker than that for semitoric systems). A natural question is whether, with respect to a such structure, the space is path-connected. That is, can two isomorphism classes of semitoric systems be continuously
deformed (with respect to the above topology) into one another, via a continuous path of isomorphism classes of semitoric systems?

This is preceded by
[
36
]
in which the authors construct a natural metric on the moduli space of symplectic toric manifolds which is related to the Duistermaat–Heckman measure and prove Theorem
2.6
, which is used to conclude the following.

Theorem 2.18

(
[
36
]
)
.

With respect to the topology defined in
[
36
]
, the moduli space of toric manifolds is path-connected.

Similarly, Theorem
2.14
implies the following statement.

Theorem 2.19
.

If
(M,\omega,F)
and
(M^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},F^{\prime})
are simple semitoric integrable systems such that:

(i)

they have the same number of focus-focus singularities;

(ii)

they are in the same twisting index class,

then there exists a continuous
(
with respect to the topology defined in
[
35
]
)
path of semitoric systems with the same number of focus-focus points and in the same twisting index class between them, up to isomorphism. That is, the space of isomorphism classes of semitoric systems with fixed number of focus-focus points and in a fixed twisting index class is path-connected.

The number of focus-focus singularities is discussed in Section
6.1.1
, the twisting index class of a semitoric system is defined in Definition
6.15
, and the notion of a simple semitoric integrable system is defined in Definition
6.1
.

Remark 2.20
.

It is important to note that while there is only one twisting index class for systems with exactly one focus-focus point, this does
not mean that the twisting index invariant is trivial in this case. That is, there exist systems with exactly one focus-focus point for which all invariants agree except for the twisting index and the systems are not isomorphic.

Let

Now that we have set up the algebraic framework the following results are straightforward to prove. First we prove that any fan with more than four vectors can be reduced to a fan with fewer vectors. This result is well-known but the following proof is new.

Lemma 4.7

(
[

By Corollary

{a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
such that

\displaystyle ST^{a_{0}}\cdots ST^{a_{d-1}}=_{G}S^{4}.

(4.1)

We must only show that for some

we have

. Since

for varying

and

∎

The case in which a vector in the fan is the sum of the adjacent vectors is important because this means the fan is the result of corner chopping a fan with fewer vectors in it. Now that we have the proper algebraic tools, we will be clear about the specifics of the corner chopping and reverse corner chopping operations.

Suppose
(v_{0}=v_{d},v_{2},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
is a toric fan with associated integers
(a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
. Then

\displaystyle v_{i+2}=-v_{i}+a_{i}v_{i+1}

so if
a_{i}=1
then we have that
v_{i+1}=v_{i}+v_{i+2}
. Now we see that in this case

\displaystyle\det(v_{i},v_{i+2})=\det(v_{i},-v_{i})+\det(v_{i},v_{i+1})=1,

so

\displaystyle(w_{0}=v_{0},\ldots,w_{i}=v_{i},w_{i+1}=v_{i+2},\ldots,w_{i-2}=v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d-1}

is also a fan. Next notice

\displaystyle-w_{i}+(a_{i+1}-1)w_{i+1}=-(v_{i}+v_{i+2})+a_{i+1}v_{i+2}=-v_{i+1}+a_{i+1}v_{i+2}=w_{i+2}

and

\displaystyle-w_{i-1}+(a_{i-1}-1)w_{i}=(-v_{i-1}+a_{i-1}v_{i})-v_{i}=v_{i+1}-v_{i}=w_{i+1},

so this new fan has associated to it the tuple of integers

\displaystyle(a_{0},\ldots,a_{i-1}-1,a_{i+1}-1,\ldots,a_{d-1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d-1}.

An occurrence of
1
from the original tuple of integers has been removed and the adjacent integers have been reduced by
1
. Algebraically, this move corresponds to the relation
STS=_{G}T^{-1}ST^{-1}
. Geometrically this move corresponds to the inverse of chopping a corner from the associated polygon (as is shown in Fig.
1
) and from the point of view of symplectic geometry this corresponds to an equivariant symplectic blowup. The corner chopping of a toric polygon is done such that the new face of the polygon produced has inwards pointing normal vector given by the sum of the adjacent inwards pointing primitive integer normal vectors.

Now we can see that Lemma
4.7
tells us that fans with five or more vectors are the result of corner chopping a fan with fewer vectors. We will next classify all possible fans with fewer than five vectors.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose that integers
a_{0},\ldots,a_{d-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy

\displaystyle ST^{a_{0}}\cdots ST^{a_{d-1}}=_{G}S^{4}

(4.2)

for some
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
d\geqslant 0
.

1.

If
d=4
then up to a cyclic reordering the set of integer quadruples which satisfy this equation is exactly
a_{0}=0
,
a_{1}=k
,
a_{2}=0,a_{3}=-k
for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

2.

If
d=3
then
a_{0}=a_{1}=a_{2}=-1
.

3.

If
d<3
then there do not exist integers satisfying equation (
4.2
).

Proof.

Notice
ST^{a_{0}}\cdots ST^{a_{d-1}}=_{G}S^{4}
implies that
ST^{a_{0}}\cdots ST^{a_{d-1}}=_{{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}}I
so Corollary
3.9
implies items (2) and (3). Moreover, we also have that

\displaystyle ST^{a_{0}}\cdots ST^{a_{d-1}}=_{{{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}}I,

so we can apply Lemma
3.8
. Suppose that
d=4
. Lemma
3.8
tells us that at least one of the
a_{i}
is in the set
\{-1,0,1\}
. By conjugation (which cyclically permutes the order of the integers) we may assume that
a_{0}\in\{-1,0,1\}
. If
a_{0}=1
then

\displaystyle STST^{a_{1}}ST^{a_{2}}ST^{a_{3}}=_{G}ST^{a_{1}-1}ST^{a_{2}}ST^{a_{3}-1},

so for this to equal
S^{4}_{G}
in
G
we must have
a_{1}-1=a_{2}=a_{3}-1=-1
by the case of
d=3
. It is straightforward to check that
STSST^{-1}S=_{G}S^{4}
so we have found the required solution.

If
a_{0}=-1
then notice

\displaystyle ST^{-1}ST^{a_{1}}ST^{a_{2}}ST^{a_{3}}=_{G}S^{4}\qquad\text{implies}\qquad ST^{a_{1}+1}ST^{a_{2}}ST^{a_{3}+1}=_{G}S^{2}=_{{{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}}I,

so by Lemma
3.8
we must have
a_{1}+1=a_{2}=a_{3}+1=\pm 1.
This time, if
a_{1}+1=a_{2}=a_{3}+1=-1
then equation (
4.2
) does not hold, since the left side will equal
S^{6}
, but if
a_{1}+1=a_{2}=a_{3}+1=1
then the equation holds. So we have found another solution,
ST^{-1}SSTS=_{G}S^{4}
, which has the form described in the statement of the lemma.

Finally, suppose that
a_{0}=0
. Notice

\displaystyle ST^{0}ST^{a_{1}}ST^{a_{2}}ST^{a_{3}}=_{G}S^{4}\qquad\text{implies}\qquad ST^{a_{2}}ST^{a_{1}+a_{3}}=_{G}S^{2}=_{{{\rm PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}}I,

so we can use Lemma
3.8
to conclude that we need
a_{2}=a_{1}+a_{3}=0
. Let
a_{1}=k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Now we have that

\displaystyle ST^{0}ST^{k}ST^{0}ST^{-k}=_{G}S^{4}

for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Finally, observe that the other two possibilities we derived in the
d=4
case are just reorderings of this one with
k=1
.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

A
Delzant triangle
is the convex hull of the points
(0,0)
,
(0,\lambda)
,
(\lambda,0)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for any
\lambda>0
. A
Hirzebruch trapezoid
with parameter

is the convex hull of
(0,0)
,
(0,a)
,
(b,a)
, and
(b+ak,0)

where
a,b>0
. A Hirzebruch trapezoid with parameter zero is a

.

These are shown in Fig.

. So we see that the fan corresponding to any Delzant triangle is

\displaystyle\left(\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}-1\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)\right)

with associated integers
(-1,-1,-1)
and the fan corresponding to a Hirzebruch trapezoid with parameter

\displaystyle\left(\left(\begin{matrix}0\\

-k\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}1\\

with associated integers
(0,k,0,-k)
. The following Theorem is immediate from Lemmas

and
4.8
.

Theorem 4.10

(
[

]
)
.

Every Delzant polygon can be obtained from a polygon
{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
-equivalent to a Delzant triangle, a rectangle, or a Hirzebruch trapezoid by a finite number of corner chops.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be any Delzant polygon with

(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}

4.7
if

a_{i}=1
for some

d-1

is the result of a corner chop of some Delzant polygon with

-equivalent to a rectangle, if

,

5
Semitoric fans

Now we will apply the method from Section
4
to classify semitoric fans (Definition
2.9
).

The first step in the classification is given by a series of lemmas which we will use to manipulate the semitoric fans in a standard form. In Lemma
5.1
we describe the process of corner chopping, in Lemma
5.2
we describe the process of reverse corner chopping, in Lemma
5.3
we describe the process of removing a hidden corner, and in Lemma
5.4
we describe the process of commuting a fake and Delzant corner. In each of these lemmas we prove the result of the corresponding process is still a semitoric fan. These four processes are defined in Definition
2.11
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
is a semitoric fan and
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
is a Delzant corner, then

\displaystyle(w_{0}=v_{0},\ldots,w_{i}=v_{i},w_{i+1}=v_{i}+v_{i+1},w_{i+2}=v_{i+1},\ldots,w_{d}=v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d+1}

is a semitoric fan with the pairs
(w_{i},w_{i+1})
and
(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})
being labeled as Delzant corners.

Proof.

It is immediate that
\det(w_{i},w_{i+1})=\det(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})=1
and the vectors are still in counter-clockwise order because the new vector was inserted between two adjacent vectors.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

If
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
is a semitoric fan,
(v_{i-1},v_{i})
and
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
are Delzant corners, and
v_{i}=v_{i-1}+v_{i+1}
, then

\displaystyle(w_{0}=v_{0},\ldots,w_{i-1}=v_{i-1},w_{i}=v_{i+1},\ldots,w_{d-2}=v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d-1}

is a semitoric fan with the pair
(w_{i-1},w_{i})
being labeled as a Delzant corner.

Proof.

Notice
\det(w_{i-1},w_{i})=\det(v_{i-1},v_{i}-v_{i-1})=1
so we must only show that the vectors in the new fan are labeled in counter-clockwise order, but this is the case because the vectors in the original fan were and to get the new fan we have only removed a vector.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
is a semitoric fan and
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
is a hidden corner, then

\displaystyle(w_{0}=v_{0},\ldots,w_{i}=v_{i},w_{i+1}=Tv_{i+1},w_{i+2}=v_{i+1},\ldots,w_{d}=v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d+1}

is a semitoric fan in which
(w_{i},w_{i+1})
is a Delzant corner and
(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})
is a fake corner.

Proof.

We know that
\det(v_{i},Tv_{i+1})=1
because that pair of vectors forms a hidden corner. Notice that

\displaystyle\det(w_{i},w_{i+1})=\det(v_{i},Tv_{i+1})=1,

so that corner is Delzant and

\displaystyle\det(w_{i+1},Tw_{i+2})=\det(Tv_{i+1,}Tv_{i+1})=0,

so if
(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})
is on the top boundary then it is a fake corner. It is on the top boundary because
v_{i+1}
is in the lower half
plane,
T_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}
sends the lower half plane to the lower half plane, and
(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})=(Tv_{i+1},v_{i+1})
. The vectors in the new fan are still in counter-clockwise order because
w_{i+1}
is in the lower half plane so
w_{i+2}=T^{-1}w_{i+1}
is counter-clockwise to it and since
w_{i}
and
w_{i+1}
are both in the lower half plane and
\det(w_{i},w_{i+1})>0
we see they are in counter-clockwise order.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

If
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
is a semitoric fan and
(v_{i},v_{i+1})
is a fake corner and
(v_{i+1},v_{i+2})
is a Delzant corner on the top boundary, then

\displaystyle(w_{0}=v_{0},\ldots,w_{i}=v_{i},w_{i+1}=Tv_{i+2},w_{i+2}=v_{i+2},\ldots,w_{d-1}=v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}

is a semitoric fan in which
(w_{i},w_{i+1})
is a Delzant corner and
(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})
is a fake corner.

Proof.

We know
\det(v_{i},Tv_{i+1})=0
so
v_{i}=Tv_{i+1}
since they are both on the top boundary and we also know
\det(v_{i+1},v_{i+2})=1
. Now we can check that

\displaystyle\det(w_{i},w_{i+1})=\det(v_{i},Tv_{i+2})=\det(Tv_{i+1},Tv_{i+2})=\det(v_{i+1},v_{i+2})=1

and

\displaystyle\det(w_{i+1},Tw_{i+2})=\det(Tv_{i+2},Tv_{i+2})=0.

Since
(v_{i+1},v_{i+2})
is on the top boundary,
v_{i+1}
and
Tv_{i+1}
are in the lower half plane, so
(w_{i+1},w_{i+2})=(Tv_{i+2},v_{i+2})
is in the top boundary which means it is a fake corner, as desired. The argument for why the new vectors are still in counter-clockwise order is similar to the argument in the proof of Lemma
5.3
.
∎

Recall that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\big\{T^{k}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\,|\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\big\}
is the symmetry group of semitoric fans, given in Definition
2.10
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
is a semitoric fan. Then after a finite number of corner choppings the fan will be
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
-equivalent to one in which two adjacent vectors are
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
v_{d}=v_{0}
. If

\displaystyle v_{i}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right)

for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
then notice

\displaystyle v_{i-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}a\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)

for some
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. This is because
(v_{i-1},v_{i})
is not on the top boundary so it must be a Delzant corner. Then by the action of
T^{-a}_{{\rm SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})}\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, which does not change
v_{i}
, we can attain the required pair of vectors.

Otherwise, renumber so that
v_{0}
is in the lower half plane and
v_{1}
is in the upper half plane. Then insert the vector
v_{0}+v_{1}
between them. This new vector will have a second component with a strictly smaller magnitude than that of
v_{0}
or of
v_{1}
. Since the magnitude is an integer, repeat this process until the new vector lies on the
x
-axis. Since it is a primitive vector it must be
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\pm 1\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. However since it is the sum of two vectors of opposite sides of the
x
-axis with the one above being counterclockwise about the origin of the one on bottom, it must be
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
∎

Now we can put Lemmas
5.3
,
5.4
, and
5.5
together to produce a standard form for semitoric fans (see Fig.
3
). This standard form will be important to us in Section
6
because it can be obtained from any semitoric fan of defect
c
by only using transformations which are continuous in the space of semitoric polygons.

Figure 3:
Any semitoric fan with defect
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
can be transformed into the standard fan of defect
c
. This image has
c=3
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{d}
be a semitoric fan of defect
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
.

1.

By only corner chopping, reverse corner chopping, removing hidden corners, commuting fake and Delzant corners, and acting by the symmetry group
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
we can obtain a new semitoric fan
(w_{0}=w_{\ell+c},\ldots,w_{\ell+c-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{\ell+c}
with
\ell+c\geqslant d
such that

•

w_{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
w_{1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
;

•

each corner
(w_{i},w_{i+1})
for
i=0,\ldots,\ell-1
is Delzant;

•

each corner
(w_{i},w_{i+1})
for
i=\ell,\ldots,\ell+c-1
is fake; and

•

w_{\ell}=T^{c}w_{0}
so
\det(w_{\ell-1},T^{c}w_{0})=1
.

2.

The collection of vectors
(w_{\ell},w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell-1})
is a toric fan;

3.

The semitoric fan
(w_{0},\ldots,w_{c+\ell-1})
can be obtained via a finite number of corner chops and reverse corner chops from a fan
(u_{0},\ldots,u_{c+3})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{c+4}
where

\displaystyle u_{0}=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}c\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{3}=\left(\begin{matrix}-1\\
0\end{matrix}\right),

and

\displaystyle u_{4+n}=\left(\begin{matrix}-c+n\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)

for
n=0,\ldots,c-1
. In this fan the first four pairs are Delzant corners and the rest are fake corners.

Proof.

The first part is immediate from Lemmas
5.3
,
5.4
, and
5.5
. By Lemma
5.5
we know after a finite number of corner choppings and renumbering it can be arranged that the first two vectors in the fan are

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

Then we invoke Lemma
5.3
to remove all of the hidden corners, and finally use Lemma
5.4
commute all of
the fake corners to be adjacent and arrive at the fan
(w_{0},\ldots,w_{\ell+c-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{\ell+c}
in the statement of the proposition. Notice that
(w_{i},w_{i+1})
being fake for
i=\ell,\ldots,\ell+c-1
implies that

\displaystyle\det(w_{\ell},Tw_{\ell+1})=\cdots=\det(w_{\ell+c-1},Tw_{0})=0.

Now, we know both vectors in a fake corner must have negative second component by definition, so this implies that

\displaystyle w_{\ell}=Tw_{\ell+1}=T^{2}w_{\ell+2}=\cdots=T^{c}w_{0}.

Now
(T^{c}w_{0}=w_{\ell},w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell-1})\in\big({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big)^{\ell}
is a toric fan (we know the vectors are in counter-clockwise order because we started with a semitoric fan) so we have established part (2) of the theorem. Denote the associated integers for this toric fan by
b_{0},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so that

\displaystyle ST^{b_{0}}\cdots ST^{b_{\ell-1}}=_{G}S^{4}.

(5.1)

To prove part (3) we will use corner chops and reverse corner chops on this toric fan to remove vectors in the set
(w_{2},\ldots,w_{\ell-1})
until the remaining vectors are in the required form. The idea here is that any corner chop or reverse corner chop on the toric fan
(w_{\ell},w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell-1})
which does not remove
w_{\ell}
or
w_{1}
and does not add a vector between
w_{\ell}
and
w_{1}
can also be performed on the semitoric fan
(w_{0},\ldots,w_{\ell+c-1})
.

Here we proceed by cases on the values of
\ell
and
b_{0},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}
. We will see in Case
I
that the semitoric fan is already of the required form and we will show any that other case can be transformed into Case
I
by corner chops and reverse corner chops on the semitoric fan
(w_{0},\ldots,w_{\ell+c-1})
. Here it is important to recall that
w_{\ell}
and
w_{1}
are already fixed, so the integers
b_{0},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}
completely determine the toric fan
(w_{\ell},w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell-1})
.

Case I
.

\ell=4
and
b_{0}=b_{1}=b_{2}=b_{3}=0
.

In this case the toric fan
(w_{\ell},w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
must be

\displaystyle\left(\left(\begin{matrix}-c\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}c\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\left(\begin{matrix}-1\\
0\end{matrix}\right)\right),

which means that the semitoric fan
(w_{0},\ldots,w_{\ell+c-1})
is in the required form for part (3) of the theorem.

Case II
.

\ell=4
and
b_{0}
,
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
,
b_{3}
not all zero.

In this case, by Lemma
4.8
, we must either have
b_{0}=b_{2}=0
and
b_{1}=-b_{3}
, or
b_{0}=-b_{2}
and
b_{1}=b_{3}=0
. First assume
b_{0}=b_{2}=0
and
b_{1}=-b_{3}
. In this case equation (
5.1
) becomes

\displaystyle S^{2}T^{b_{1}}S^{2}T^{-b_{1}}=_{G}S^{4}.

If
b_{1}>0
then a corner chop will produce
\big(S^{2}T^{b_{1}}STSTST^{-b_{1}+1}\big)_{G}
and a reverse corner chop will produce from this
\big(S^{2}T^{b_{1}-1}S^{2}T^{-b_{1}+1}\big)_{G}
. This can be repeated
b_{1}
times to recover Case
I
. If
b_{1}<0
then a corner chop can produce

\big(S^{2}T^{b_{1}+1}STSTST^{-b_{1}}\big)_{G}
and from this a reverse corner chop can produce
\big(S^{2}T^{b_{1}+1}S^{2}T^{-b_{1}-1}\big)_{G}
. Again, this can be repeated
b_{1}
times to recover Case
I
. Observe that the above corner chops and reverse corner chops do not affect the first and last element of the toric fan; thus the above operations can be realized as corner chops and reverse corner chops on the semitoric fan
(w_{0},\ldots,w_{\ell+c-1})
as required.

The case of
b_{0}=-b_{2}
and
b_{1}=b_{3}=0
is similar.

Case III
.

\ell<4
.

By Lemma
4.8
we must have
\ell=3
. Perform a corner chop between the vectors
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
to add the vector
w_{1}+w_{2}
and reduce to either Case
I
or Case
II
.

Case IV
.

\ell>4
.

In this case we will show that the semitoric fan can always be transformed into another semitoric fan with one less length, so repeating this
process will eventually yield
\ell=4
, thereby reducing to Case
I
or Case
II
. There are two subcases in this case.

Case IV (a)
.

b_{i}=1
for some
i
such that
0<i<\ell-1
.

Here a reverse corner chop may be performed on
w_{i+1}
, removing it from the semitoric fan and reducing the length by 1.

Case IV (b)
.

b_{i}\neq 1
for all
i=1,\ldots,\ell-2
.

Any substitution using
STS=_{G}T^{-1}ST^{-1}
in equation (
5.1
) can be realized as a corner chop or reverse corner chop on the toric fan
(w_{\ell},w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell-1})
and no such substitution corresponds to the corner chop on
(w_{\ell-1},w_{\ell})
so these transformations can also be realized a corner chops and reverse corner chops on the semitoric fan

6.1.3
The polygon invariant and the twisting index invariant

The polygon invariant is the invariant which is directly analogous to the Delzant polygon in the toric case and the twisting index is an integer label on each focus-focus fiber which encodes how the semiglobal (in a neighborhood of a fiber) model of the focus-focus point relates to the global toric momentum map used to generate the polygon. The different choices of toric momentum map produce a family of polygons and thus the component of the twisting index associated to a single focus-focus fiber may change depending on this choice, but if the system has more than one focus-focus point then the difference of twisting index components of focus-focus fibers does not depend on the choice of polygon. This is because different choices of polygon can only change the sequence of integers which is the twisting index by the addition of a common integer, and for this reason we are able to also define the twisting index class (first introduced in
[
35
, Definition 3.7]
, and defined in the present paper in Definition
6.15

In general, the momentum map image of a semitoric system does not have to be a polygon and does not even have to be convex, but in
[
46
]
the author was able to recover a family of convex polygons which take the place of the Delzant polygon in the semitoric case.

6.2.1
The metric on Taylor series

Recall that the Taylor series in

have the coefficient to

in the interval

can see that when defining the topology the endpoints of the closure of this interval should be identified. We define a topology on

such that a sequence of Taylor series converges if and only if each term converges in the appropriate space. This is the topology induced by any of the following metrics.

Definition 6.16

A sequence

with

for each

is called

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{R}[[X,Y]]_{0}}((S)^{\infty},(S^{\prime})^{\infty})=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0,(i,j)\neq(0,1)}^{\infty}\min\{\left|\sigma_{i,j}-\sigma^{\prime}_{i,j}\right|,b_{i+j}\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{d_{\mathbb{R}[[X,Y]]_0}((S)^\infty,(S')^\infty) =}{}+\min\{\left|\sigma_{0,1}-\sigma^{\prime}_{0,1}\right|,2\pi-\left|\sigma_{0,1}-\sigma^{\prime}_{0,1}\right|,b_{1}\},

where

\displaystyle(S)^{\infty}=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0}\sigma_{i,j}X^{i}Y^{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(S^{\prime})^{\infty}=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0}\sigma^{\prime}_{i,j}X^{i}Y^{j}.

v_{i+2}
and let
u_{2}^{\prime}
be the primitive integral vector directing the other edge away from
p^{\prime}
. Then for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
let

\displaystyle\Delta_{t}=\Delta^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{H}_{p^{\prime}}^{t\varepsilon_{0}}(u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime}),

and note that
\Delta_{0}=\Delta^{\prime}
and
\Delta_{1}
has the same semitoric fan as
\Delta
(for the correct choice of
\varepsilon_{0}
we can actually get
\Delta_{0}=\Delta
, but this is not necessary), so we have formed a continuous path from a polygon with associated semitoric fan
\mathcal{F}
to the polygon
\Delta^{\prime}
, as desired.

So in any case we have produced a family of polygons
\Delta_{t}
,
0\leqslant t\leqslant 1
. Let

\displaystyle\Delta_{w}^{t}=\big(\Delta_{t},(\ell_{\lambda_{j}^{t}},+1,k_{j})_{j=1}^{m_{f}}\big),

where
\lambda_{j}^{t}
is the
x
-coordinate of the
j^{\textrm{th}}
non-Delzant corner of
\Delta_{t}
ordered left to right, to complete the proof.
∎

Recall that toric polygons are precisely the compact primitive semitoric polygons with defect zero. Thus, by Proposition
5.6
and Lemma
6.29
we have recovered Theorem
2.6
.

In light of Propositions
5.6
and
6.29
the only difficulty remaining to prove the following lemma is incorporating the case of semitoric systems which have noncompact polygons as invariants. Recall
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
is the topology on
\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
induced by the metric defined in
6.20
.

Lemma 6.30
.

Let
{m_{f}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\vec{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{f}}
. Then
\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
is path-connected with respect to the topology
\tau_{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

Every semitoric polygon is an orbit of a primitive semitoric polygon, and to conclude that there is a continuous path between two semitoric polygons it is sufficient to construct a continuous (with respect to
\tau_{0}
, Definition
6.28
) path between the primitive semitoric polygons that generate those orbits. This is because the map which takes a primitive semitoric polygon to its orbit is continuous. Thus, it suffices to consider only primitive semitoric polygons. Any two compact primitive semitoric polygons in
\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
with the same semitoric fan can be connected by a continuous path. This path is made by continuously changing the lengths of the edges because the angles of the two polygons must all be the same since they have the same semitoric fan while also changing
\lambda_{j}
to still correspond to the locations of the fake and hidden corners. So, by Proposition
5.6
given two elements of
\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
which are compact we know that the corresponding semitoric fans are related by the moves listed in that proposition and then by Lemma
6.29
we know these moves correspond to continuous paths of polygons. So we have established that any two compact elements of
\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)
are connected by a continuous path.

Next assume that

\displaystyle[\Delta_{w}]=\big[\big(\Delta,(\ell_{\lambda_{j}},\epsilon_{j},k_{j})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}}\big)\big]\in\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)

is such that
\Delta
is noncompact but has only finitely many vertices. Let

\displaystyle\pi_{1}\colon\ {\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}

be projection onto the first component. Choose
N\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that all of the vertices of
\Delta
are in the region
\pi_{1}^{-1}([-N,N])
. The set
\Delta\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([-N-1,N+1])
is a polygon but the corners which intersect
\ell_{N+1}\cup\ell_{-N-1}
may not satisfy the Delzant condition.
By
[
36
, Remark 23]
we may change the set on arbitrarily small neighborhoods of these corners to produce a new compact polygon,
\Delta^{\prime}
, which is equal to
\Delta\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([-N-1,N+1])
outside of those small neighborhoods and has only Delzant corners inside of those neighborhoods. Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}_{w}=\big(\Delta^{\prime},(\ell_{\lambda_{j}},\epsilon_{j},k_{j})_{j=1}^{m_{f}}\big)

is a primitive semitoric polygon and by choosing the neighborhoods small enough we can assure that
\Delta\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([-N,N])=\Delta^{\prime}\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([-N,N])
. For
t\in[0,1)
let
\Delta(t)
be the polygon with the same semitoric fan as
\Delta^{\prime}
, the property that

\displaystyle\Delta(t)\cap[-N,N]=\Delta^{\prime}\cap[-N,N],

and which has all of the same edge lengths as
\Delta^{\prime}
with the exception of the two or four edges which intersect
\ell_{N}\cup\ell_{-N}
. These
edges are extended horizontally by a length of
\frac{1}{t-1}
. By this we mean that if an edge of
\Delta^{\prime}
which intersected
\pi_{1}^{-1}(N)
had as one of its endpoints
(x_{0},y_{0})
with
x_{0}>N
then the corresponding edge of
\Delta(t)
would have as its endpoint
\big(x_{0}+\frac{1}{t-1},y_{0}+m\frac{1}{t-1}\big)
, where
m
is the slope of the edge in question. Then define
\Delta(1)=\Delta
and we can see that
\Delta(t)
for
t\in[0,1]
is a path from
\Delta^{\prime}
, which is compact, to
\Delta
so
\big[\big(\Delta(t),(\ell_{\lambda_{j}},+1,k_{j})_{j=1}^{m_{f}}\big)\big]
is a continuous path which connects a compact semitoric polygon to
[\Delta_{w}]
. This process is shown in Fig.
6
.

Now we have connected all of the elements except for those with an infinite amount of vertices. Suppose that

\displaystyle[\Delta_{w}]=\big[\big(\Delta,(\ell_{\lambda_{j}},\epsilon_{j},k_{j})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}}\big)\big]\in\mathcal{D}{\rm Polyg}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\big({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\big)

is such that
\Delta
is noncompact and has infinitely many vertices. We will connect
[\Delta_{w}]
to a semitoric polygon which has only finitely many vertices to finish the proof. Since
\Delta
has everywhere finite height and is the intersection of infinitely many half-planes we can choose two of these planes which are not horizontal and are not parallel to one another. Denote the intersection of these two half-planes by
A
and notice
\Delta\subset A
. Since the boundaries of these two half-planes must intersect we can see that
A
can only be unbounded in either the positive or negative
x
-direction, but not both. Without loss of generality assume that
A
is unbounded in the positive
x
-direction.

Let
\nu
be any admissible measure. For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
since
\nu(A)<\infty
we know there exists some
x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle\nu\big(A\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([x_{n},\infty))\big)<\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}},

\Delta
does not have a corner on the line
\ell_{x_{n}}
, and
x_{n}>\left|\lambda_{j}\right|
for all
j=1,\ldots,{m_{f}}
. Let
\Delta_{n}
denote the polygon which satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([-\infty,x_{n}])=\Delta\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([-\infty,x_{n}])

and has no vertices with
x
-coordinate greater than
x_{n}
.

For each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
and
t\in(0,1]
define
\Delta_{n}(t)
to have the same semitoric fan as
\Delta_{n+1}
and to have all the same edge lengths as
\Delta_{n+1}
except for the two edges which intersect
\ell_{x_{n}}
. Extend those two edges horizontally by
\nicefrac{{1}}{{t}}-1
. Define
\Delta_{n}(0)=\Delta_{n}
. Now
\Delta_{n}(t)
for
t\in[0,1]
is a
C^{0}
path, with respect to the topology on polygons generated by the
\nu
-measure of the symmetric
difference, which takes
\Delta_{n}
to
\Delta_{n+1}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Delta\ast\Delta_{n}(t)\subset A\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}([x_{n},\infty])\qquad\text{so}\qquad\nu(\Delta\ast\Delta_{n}(t))<\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}

for each
t\in[0,1]
. Each of these paths for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
can be concatenated to form a continuous path
\Delta_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, from
\Delta_{0}
to
\Delta
and we know that
\Delta_{0}
has only finitely many vertices. It is important not only that each
\Delta_{n}
be getting closer to
\Delta
but also that the path from
\Delta_{n}
to
\Delta_{n+1}
stays close to
\Delta
. Then we define
[(\Delta(t),(\lambda_{j},+1,k_{j})_{j=1}^{m_{f}})]
which is a continuous
path of semitoric polygons from a semitoric polygon with finitely many vertices to
[\Delta_{w}]
. This is shown in Fig.

.

Now we can classify the connected components of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{T}
. Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}=\mathcal{M}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k^{\prime}}}
if
k_{j}=k^{\prime}_{j}+c
for some
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
so when stating the following lemma we require that the first component of the twisting index be
0
. This is done only to make
sure that there are no repeats in the list of components. Recall
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is the collection of semitoric lists of ingredients with
{m_{f}}=0
and
\mathcal{T}_{0}=\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{0})
is the collection of semitoric systems with no focus-focus singularities. Finally, also recall that the fact that the different
\mathcal{M}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}
are in different components really comes from the definition of the constructed topology in
[
35
]
and not from any result of the present paper.

Lemma 6.31
.

The connected components of
\mathcal{M}
are each element of the set

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{M}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\,|\,{m_{f}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\vec{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{f}}\textrm{ with }k_{1}=0\}\cup\{\mathcal{M}_{0}\}

and they are each path-connected.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that
\mathcal{M}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}
is path-connected for each choice of
{m_{f}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
and
\vec{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{f}}
. Let
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}
with

\displaystyle m=\big([\Delta_{w}],(h_{j})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}},((S_{j})^{\infty})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}}\big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad m^{\prime}=\big([\Delta^{\prime}_{w}],(h^{\prime}_{j})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}},((S^{\prime}_{j})^{\infty})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}}\big).

By Lemma
6.30
we know there exists a continuous path

\displaystyle[\Delta_{w}(t)]=\big[\big(\Delta(t),(\ell_{\lambda_{j}(t)},+1,k_{j})_{j=1}^{m_{f}}\big)\big],

t\in[0,1]
, from
[\Delta_{w}]
to
[\Delta^{\prime}_{w}]
and by
[
35
, Proposition 3.2]
we know
\mathbb{R}[[X,Y]]_{0}
is path-connected so there exists a continuous path
(S_{j}(t))^{\infty}
from
(S_{j})^{\infty}
to
(S^{\prime}_{j})^{\infty}
for each
j=1,\ldots,{m_{f}}
. For
j=1,\ldots,{m_{f}}
let

\displaystyle\textrm{len}_{j}=\textrm{length}(\pi_{2}(\Delta\cap\ell_{\lambda_{j}})),\qquad\textrm{len}^{\prime}_{j}=\textrm{length}(\pi_{2}(\Delta^{\prime}\cap\ell_{\lambda^{\prime}_{j}})),

\displaystyle\textrm{len}_{j}(t)=\textrm{length}(\pi_{2}(\Delta(t)\cap\ell_{\lambda_{j}(t)}))

for
t\in[0,1]
and define

\displaystyle h_{j}(t)=\left(\frac{(1-t)h_{j}}{\textrm{len}_{j}}+\frac{th_{j}^{\prime}}{\textrm{len}^{\prime}_{j}}\right)\textrm{len}_{j}(t).

Now we have that
0<h_{j}(t)<\textrm{len}_{j}(t)
and
t\mapsto h_{j}(t)
is a continuous function from
[0,1]
to
{\mathbb{R}}
because it is impossible for a semitoric polygon to have a vertical boundary at
\ell_{\lambda_{j}}
for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,{m_{f}}\}
. Now define

\displaystyle m(t)=\big([\Delta_{w}(t)],(h_{j}(t))_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}},((S_{j}(t))^{\infty})_{j=1}^{{m_{f}}}\big)

for
t\in[0,1]
which is a continuous path from
m
to
m^{\prime}
.
∎

Thus we have established the following result.

Theorem 6.32
.

The set of connected components of
\mathcal{T}
is

\displaystyle\big\{\mathcal{T}_{{m_{f}},\vec{k}}\,|\,{m_{f}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0},\,\vec{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m_{f}}\textrm{ with }k_{1}=0\big\}\cup\{\mathcal{T}_{0}\}

and they are each path-connected.

Theorem
2.19
is equivalent to Theorem
6.32
.

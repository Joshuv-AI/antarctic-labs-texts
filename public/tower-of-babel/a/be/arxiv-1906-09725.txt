Abstract
. On the product of a complex manifold X X by a complex curve S S considered as a parameter space, we show a Riemann-Hilbert correspondence between regular holonomic relative 𝒟 \mathscr{D} -modules (resp. complexes) on the one hand and relative perverse complexes (resp. S S - ℂ \mathbb{C} -constructible complexes) on the other hand.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14F10, 32C38, 35A27, 58J15

Introduction

Let
X
and
T
be complex manifolds. Relative
\mathscr{D}
-modules are modules over the sheaf
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
of differential operators relative to the projection
p_{X}:X\times T\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}T
.
Holonomic

\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-modules encode holomorphic families of holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-modules parametrized by
T
whose characteristic variety is contained in a fixed Lagrangian subset
\Lambda\subset T^{*}X
. This is a strong condition avoiding confluence phenomena. It describes nevertheless the generic behaviour of a deformation of a holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. From this point of view, it is natural to emphasize those
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-modules which are
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{T}
-flat, that we call
strict
.

There exists a solution functor from the category of
holonomic

\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-modules to that of perverse
\mathbb{C}
-constructible complexes of
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{T}
-modules, objects defined in
[
16
]
. Strictness (i.e., flatness) on the
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-module side corresponds to the property that the Verdier dual of a perverse complex (as a complex of
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{T}
-modules) is also perverse.

In their previous work
[
18
]
, Monteiro Fernandes and Sabbah have introduced the notion of relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-modules. For example, if
T=\mathbb{C}^{*}
, a
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-module underlying a regular mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module (cf.
[
14
]
) is regular holonomic in this sense.

Convention and notation
.

In this article, we mainly consider the case where the parameter space
T
has dimension one, and we emphasize this assumption by denoting it by
S
. Therefore, throughout this article,
S
will denote a complex curve. On the other hand, we will set
d_{X}=\dim X
.

Our main result is a Riemann-Hilbert correspondence for regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-modules, in the following form. We let
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
denote the full subcategory of
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
consisting of complexes having regular holonomic cohomology modules (cf. Section
1.4
for details) and
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})
the category of
\mathbb{C}
-constructible complexes of
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}
-modules (cf. Section
1.2
and
[
16
]
).

Theorem 1
.

The functors

\displaystyle\pSol_{X}:\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})

\displaystyle\RH^{S}_{X}:\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})

are quasi-inverse equivalences of categories.

The functor
\pSol_{X}
is the solution functor shifted by the dimension of
X
(cf.
[
16
, §3.3]
and Section
1.3
), and
\RH^{S}_{X}
is the relative Riemann-Hilbert functor (cf.
[
18
, §3.4]
and Section
2
). One direction of the correspondence, namely
\id\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pSol_{X}\circ\RH^{S}_{X}
, was proved in
[
18
, Th. 3]
, and a particular case of this correspondence was obtained as Theorem 5 in loc. cit., namely, if
\mathscr{M}
underlies a regular mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module, then
\mathscr{M}
can be recovered from
\pSol_{X}(\mathscr{M})
up to isomorphism by the formula
\mathscr{M}\simeq\RH^{S}_{X}(\pSol_{X}(\mathscr{M}))
.

The methods used in the present paper rely on the previous works
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
as well as
[
20
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
2
]
. The main tools in the proof given in
[
18
]
are the good functorial properties satisfied by holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules underlying mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-modules, which include stability under pullback, localization along an hypersurface and direct image by projective morphisms.

The main problem to extend these results to more general situations is the bad behaviour of
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-holonomicity by pullback in general (
[
17
, Ex. 2.4]
). We realized however that we can avoid these general arguments for
regular
holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-modules.
We replace them by proving that regular holonomiciy behaves well with respect to pullback:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathscr{M}\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
and let
f:Y\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}X
be a morphism of complex manifolds, then
{}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}f^{*}\mathscr{M}\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{Y\times S/S})
.

Let us indicate the main points in the proof of Theorem
1
in Section
3
. The first tool is
[
18
, Th. 3]
which asserts that there exists a natural transformation

\id_{\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{\textstyle\kern 4.0pt\alpha\kern 3.0pt}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}}{}{}\pSol_{X}\circ\RH^{S}_{X}

providing a functorial isomorphism

F\xrightarrow[\sim]{~\textstyle\alpha_{F}~}\pSol_{X}(\RH^{S}_{X}(F))

for any
F\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})
. We are then reduced to proving that there exists a natural transformation

\id_{\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})}\mathchoice{\xrightarrow{\textstyle\kern 4.0pt\beta\kern 3.0pt}}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\longrightarrow}}}{}{}\RH^{S}_{X}\circ\pSol_{X}

such that, for any
\mathscr{M}\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
, denoting by

\beta_{\mathscr{M}}:\mathscr{M}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\RH^{S}_{X}(\pSol_{X}(\mathscr{M}))

the unique morphism such that
\pSol_{X}(\beta_{\mathscr{M}})\circ\alpha_{\pSol_{X}(\mathscr{M})}=\id_{\pSol_{X}(\mathscr{M})}
, we have

\mathscr{M}\xrightarrow[\sim]{~\textstyle\beta_{\mathscr{M}}~}\RH^{S}_{X}(\pSol_{X}(\mathscr{M})).

The proof of the existence of such a
\beta
is reduced to that of the following result, which is equivalent to Theorem
1
by an argument already used in
[
18
, §4.3]
(cf. introduction to Section
3
):

Theorem 3
.

For any
\mathscr{M}\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})

and for any
F\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})
the
complex
\rh_{\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}}(\mathscr{M},\RH^{S}_{X}(F))
belongs to
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})
.

The proof of Theorem
3
follows the ideas of
[
11
, Lem. 4.1.4]
(see also Kashiwara’s proof
[
4
, §8.3]
in the absolute case). The proof in the
S
-torsion case amounts to that of loc. cit. On the other hand, in order to prove Theorem
3
in the general case, we apply induction on the dimension of the support and proceed by considering the case of
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-modules of D-type (normal crossing case).

We know (cf.
[
16
, §3]
) that the functor
\pSol_{X}
transforms duality on
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
to Poincaré-Verdier duality on
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})
. A consequence of the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence of Theorem
1
and the full faithfulness of
\RH^{S}_{X}
is the good behaviour of the functor
\RH^{S}_{X}
with respect to Poincaré-Verdier duality on the one hand, and duality for
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-modules on the other hand.

Corollary 4
.

For any
F\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\mathbb{C}\textup{-c}}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})
, there exists an isomorphism in
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})

\boldsymbol{D}(\RH^{S}_{X}(F))\simeq\RH^{S}_{X}(\boldsymbol{D}F)

which is functorial in
F
.

1.
Review on the relative holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-modules and constructible complexes

In this section, we review the main definitions and properties of the objects entering the relative Riemann-Hilbert correspondence. We refer to
[
16
,
18
,
17
]
for details. We also give supplementary properties that will happen to be useful in the proof of the main results of this article.

1.1.
Holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-modules

We denote by
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
the subsheaf of
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T}
of
relative differential operators with respect to the projection

p_{X}:X\times T\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}T,

that we simply denote by
p
when there is no ambiguity. This is a Noetherian sheaf of rings. A coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-module
\mathscr{M}
is said to be
holonomic
if

\Char(\mathscr{M})\subseteq\Lambda\times T
for some closed conic Lagrangian complex analytic subset
\Lambda
of
T^{*}X
(see
[
2
, Lem. 2.10]
for a more precise description of the characteristic variety).

Example 1.1
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}:X\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic functions. We let here
T=\mathbb{C}^{d}
with coordinates
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}
and we consider the partially algebraic version
\mathscr{D}_{X}[s_{1},\dots,s_{d}]
of
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
. Let
M
be a holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module and let
m
be a local section of
M
. Extending
[
3
]
(which is the case
d=1
), one considers the
\mathscr{D}_{X}[s_{1},\dots,s_{d}]
-submodule
\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{D}_{X}[s_{1},\dots,s_{d}]\cdot m\cdot f_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots f_{d}^{s_{d}}
of
M[(\prod_{i}f_{i})^{-1}][s_{1},\dots,s_{d}]
. It is proved in
[
13
, Prop. 13]
that
\mathscr{M}
is relatively holonomic.

Example 1.2
.

Set
S=\mathbb{C}^{*}
with coordinate
z
. Any mixed twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module, in the sense of
[
14
]
, which consists of data parametrized by
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, gives rise, when restricting the parameter to
S=\mathbb{C}^{*}
, to a holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-module (this is by definition, cf.
[
19
, Chap. 1]
).

We denote by

\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})
the full subcategory of
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{coh}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})

whose complexes have holonomic cohomologies.

Given
t_{o}\in T
, let
i_{t_{o}}
denote the inclusion
X\times\{t_{o}\}\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}}X\times T
. Following
[
16
]
, we denote by

Li^{*}_{t_{o}}:\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X})

the derived functor

(1)

p_{X}^{-1}(\mathscr{O}_{T}/\mathfrak{m}_{t_{o}})\overset{L}{\otimes}_{p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{T}}({\raisebox{1.0pt}{${\scriptscriptstyle\bullet}$}}),

where
\mathfrak{m}_{t_{o}}
is the maximal ideal of functions vanishing at
t_{o}
. Thanks to the variant of Nakayama’s lemma
[
18
, Prop. 1.9 & Cor. 1.10]
, the family of functors
Li^{*}_{t_{o}}
on
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})

, is a conservative family, i.e., if
\phi:\mathscr{M}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathscr{N}
is a morphism in
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})
such that
Li^{*}_{t_{o}}\phi
is an isomorphism in

\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}({\mathscr{D}}_{X})
for each
t_{o}\in T
then
\phi
is an isomorphism (or, equivalently, using the mapping cone:
if
\mathscr{M}\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})
is such that

for each

then
\mathscr{M}=0
).

Recall (cf.
[
16
]
) that a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-module is said to be
strict
if it is
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{T}
-flat. If
\mathscr{M}
is strict,
Li^{*}_{t_{o}}\mathscr{M}
consists of a single coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module
i^{*}_{t_{o}}\mathscr{M}
(in degree zero). For example (recall that
\dim S=1
) a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-module
\mathscr{M}
is strict if and only if it has no
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}
-torsion. If
\mathscr{M}
is possibly not strict, we shall denote by
t(\mathscr{M})
its (coherent) submodule consisting of germs of sections which are torsion elements for the
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}
-action, and
f(\mathscr{M}):=\mathscr{M}/t(\mathscr{M})
is called its strict (or torsion-free) quotient. Therefore, the
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-module
\mathscr{M}
is strict if and only if
\mathscr{M}\simeq f(\mathscr{M})
.

Given
\mathscr{M}\in\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})
, the functor

\boldsymbol{D}(\mathscr{M}):=\rh_{\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X\times T}}\Omega^{\otimes^{-1}}_{X\times T/T})[d_{X}]

provides a duality in

but, contrary to the absolute case (i.e.,

), this functor is not
t
-exact. For example, when the parameter space has dimension one, the lack of exactness of the dual functor on
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
is due to the fact that the dual of a torsion holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S}
-module
\mathscr{M}
is not concentrated in degree zero: if
\mathscr{M}\simeq\penalty t(\mathscr{M})
we have
\boldsymbol{D}(\mathscr{M})\simeq\mathscr{H}^{1}(\boldsymbol{D}(\mathscr{M}))[-1]
. On the other hand, if
\mathscr{N}
is a strict holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-module, then
\boldsymbol{D}(\mathscr{N})\simeq\mathscr{H}^{0}(\boldsymbol{D}(\mathscr{N}))
and
\mathscr{H}^{0}(\boldsymbol{D}(\mathscr{N}))
is strict (see
[
18
, Prop. 2]
), that is,
\mathscr{N}
is also dual holonomic: recall that a complex
\mathscr{N}
in
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{hol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T})
is called

if it is in the heart of the
t
-structure

[
2
, §2]

t
-structure dual to the canonical

-structure.

1.2.
Relative constructible and perverse complexes

1.2.1.
Relative local systems

Following

1.2.2.

Lemma 1.7

Let

(

2.2.1.
The functor
\THH^{S}

When
X
is a real analytic manifold and
S
is a complex curve, we define the triangulated functor

\THH^{S}_{X}:\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{R}\textup{-c}}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S})^{\mathrm{op}}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\rD^{+}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S_{\mathbb{R}}/S})

given by

F\mathchoice{\longmapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}\THH^{S}_{X}(F):=\rho^{\prime-1}\rh_{\rho^{\prime}_{*}p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}}(\rho^{\prime}_{*}F,\Db^{t,S}_{X\times S})

where
\mathscr{D}_{X\times S_{\mathbb{R}}/S}
denotes the sheaf of linear differential operators with real analytic coefficients on
X\times S_{\mathbb{R}}
which commute with
p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}
.

Recall that, as a consequence of
[
15
, Prop. 4.7]
we have

\displaystyle\THH^{S}_{X}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{H\times V})

\displaystyle\simeq\rh(\mathbb{C}_{X\times V},\tho(\mathbb{C}_{H\times S},\Db_{X\times S}))

\displaystyle\simeq R\Gamma_{X\times V}\tho(\mathbb{C}_{H\times S},\Db_{X\times S})\quad(\text{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{KS1}{}{}, (2.6.9)]}})

for any relatively compact locally closed,
resp.
open, subanalytic subsets
H
of
X
,
resp.

V
of
S
. If
H=Z
is closed, we have
\tho(\mathbb{C}_{Z\times S},\Db_{X\times S})=\Gamma_{Z\times S}\Db_{X\times S}
by definition. We conclude:

(2)

\THH^{S}_{X}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{Z\times V})\simeq\Gamma_{Z\times V}\Db_{X\times S}.

On the other hand, if
H=\Omega
is open, since
\tho(\mathbb{C}_{\Omega\times S},\Db_{X\times S})
is a c-soft sheaf, we obtain

(3)

\THH^{S}_{X}(p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\Omega\times V})\simeq\Gamma_{X\times V}\tho(\mathbb{C}_{\Omega\times S},\Db_{X\times S}).

2.2.2.
The functor
\RH^{S}

If
X
is a complex manifold and
S
is a complex curve,

\begin{split}F\mathchoice{\longmapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}{\mapsto}\RH^{S}_{X}(F)&:=\rho^{\prime-1}\rh_{\rho^{\prime}_{*}p^{-1}_{X}\mathscr{O}_{S}}(\rho^{\prime}_{*}F,\mathscr{O}^{t,S}_{X\times S})[d_{X}]\\
&\phantom{:}\simeq\rh_{\mathscr{D}_{\overline{X}\times\overline{S}}}(\mathscr{O}_{\overline{X}\times\overline{S}},\THH^{S}_{X}(F))[d_{X}],\end{split}

the last isomorphism being called here “realification procedure” for short (cf.
[
18

).

We collect below some results in

]
which will be useful in the sequel.
The first gives the behaviour of

2.3.2.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
Y
be a complex hypersurface of
X
. Then, for any

there is a natural isomorphism

\RH^{S}_{X}(F)(*(Y\times\penalty S))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\RH^{S}_{X}(F\otimes\mathbb{C}_{(X\smallsetminus Y)\times S}).

In particular, if

\RH^{S}_{X}(F)(*(Y\times\penalty S))
belongs to
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
.

(b)

There is a natural isomorphism
\RH^{S}_{X}(F\otimes\mathbb{C}_{Y\times\penalty S})\!\simeq\!R\Gamma_{[Y\times\penalty S]}(\RH^{S}_{X}(F))
and so
R\Gamma_{[Y\times\penalty S]}(\RH^{S}_{X}(F))
also belongs to
\rD^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{rhol}}(\mathscr{D}_{X\times S/S})
.

(c)

If the natural morphism
\RH^{S}_{X}(F)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\RH^{S}_{X}(F)(*(Y\times\penalty S))
is an isomorphism, then so is the natural morphism
F\otimes\mathbb{C}_{(X\smallsetminus Y)\times S}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}F
.

Let
f=0
be a local defining equation of
Y
. Since this is a local problem we may start by assuming that

for a relatively compact open subanalytic subset

of

. Noting that

is invertible on
\tho(\mathbb{C}_{(\Omega\smallsetminus Y)\times S},\Db_{X\times S})
, according to
[

, Prop. 3.23]
, the natural restriction morphism

\tho(\mathbb{C}_{\Omega\times S},\Db_{X\times S})(*(Y\times\penalty S))\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\tho(\mathbb{C}_{(\Omega\smallsetminus Y)\times S},\Db_{X\times S})

is an isomorphism. Thus, applying Proposition

, the natural

-linear morphism
\THH^{S}_{X}(F)(*(Y\times\penalty S))\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\THH^{S}_{X}(F\otimes\mathbb{C}_{(X\smallsetminus Y)\times S})
is an isomorphism for any

. The existence of the morphism (

The remaining statements (

Proposition 2.9

\displaystyle{}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{D}}f^{*}\RH^{S}_{X}(F)[d_{Y}

Proposition 3.3

Proof.

The question is local. The assumption on

One can find a complex manifold

[
12

(*)

\displaystyle Li^{*}_{s}\mathscr{M}^{\prime\delta}(*(Y^{\prime}\times\penalty S))

\displaystyle\simeq Li^{*}_{s}L\pi^{*}\bigl(\mathscr{M}(*(Y\times\penalty S))\bigr)[\delta]\quad(\text{according to \eqref{eq:DpiMloc}})

\displaystyle\simeq L\pi^{*}(i^{*}_{s}\mathscr{M})(*Y)[\delta]\quad(\text{strictness of }\mathscr{M}(*(Y\times\penalty S)))

\displaystyle\simeq L^{\delta}\pi^{*}(i^{*}_{s}\mathscr{M})(*Y)\quad(\text{same argument as \eqref{eq:DpiMloc}})

has cohomology in degree zero only, as wanted. The same argument shows that, while

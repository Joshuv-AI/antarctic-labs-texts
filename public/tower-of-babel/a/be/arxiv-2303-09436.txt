Balanced multiple q \operatorname{q} -zeta values Thanks: The author was partially funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – SFB-TRR 358/1 2023 — 491392403.
Annika Burmester

Abstract
. We introduce the balanced multiple q-zeta values. They give a new model for multiple q-zeta values, whose product formula combines the shuffle and stuffle product for multiple zeta values in a natural way. Moreover, the balanced multiple q-zeta values are invariant under a very explicit involution. Thus, all relations among the balanced multiple q-zeta values are conjecturally of a very simple shape. Examples of the balanced multiple q-zeta values are the classical Eisenstein series, and they also contain the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series introduced in [ BB22 ] . The construction of the balanced multiple q-zeta values is done on the level of generating series. We introduce a general setup relating Hoffman’s quasi-shuffle products to explicit symmetries among generating series of words, which gives a clarifying approach to Ecalle’s theory of bimoulds. This allows us to obtain an isomorphism between the underlying Hopf algebras of words related to the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series and the balanced multiple q-zeta values.

1.
Introduction

In general, a
q-analog
of some expression is a generalization involving the variable
q
, which returns the original expression by taking the limit
q\to 1
. For example, a q-analog of some integer
n\geq 1
is

\{n\}_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=1+q+\dots+q^{n-1},

since
\lim_{q\to 1}\{n\}_{q}=n
. In this work we are interested in a particular kind of q-analogs of multiple zeta values.

Multiple zeta values.

Multiple zeta values
are real numbers defined for integers
k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
by

\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}.

We refer to the number
k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}
as the
weight
and to the number
d
as the
depth
. Multiple zeta values were first studied in depth
2
by C. Goldbach and L. Euler more than two centuries ago. During the last three decades, they became an active field of research. They occur in various areas of mathematics, such as number theory, algebraic geometry, knot theory, quantum field theory, and also in mathematical physics. The modern systematic study of multiple zeta values is initiated in
[
Hof97
]
,
[
Za94
]
.
A survey on achievements in the theory of multiple zeta values can be found in
[
BGF
]
and
[
Zh20
]
, and all articles related to multiple zeta values are listed in
[
Hof
]
.

The product of multiple zeta values can be expressed in two different ways, one is called the
stuffle product
, and the other one is called the
shuffle product
. Both products possess a description in terms of quasi-shuffle algebras (introduced in Section
2
). First, consider the alphabet
\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots\}
and let
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
be the non-commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{Y}
. Then the
stuffle product

\ast
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
is defined by
\mathbf{1}\ast w=w\ast\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle y_{i}u\ast y_{j}v=y_{i}(u\ast y_{j}v)+y_{j}(y_{i}u\ast v)+y_{i+j}(u\ast v)

(1.1)

for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
. The combinatorics of infinite nested sums imply that there is a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle,\ast)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z},

(1.2)

\displaystyle y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta^{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}).

The elements
\zeta^{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
are the
stuffle regularized multiple zeta values
, they are uniquely determined by
\zeta^{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
for all
k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
and
\zeta^{\ast}(1)=0
.

Second, consider the finite alphabet
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
and let
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
be the non-commutative algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\mathcal{X}
. The
shuffle product
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
is recursively defined by
\mathbf{1}\shuffle w=w\shuffle\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle x_{i}u\shuffle x_{j}v=x_{i}(u\shuffle x_{j}v)+x_{j}(x_{i}u\shuffle v)

(1.3)

for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
. Define
\mathfrak{h}^{1}
to be the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
generated by all words not ending in
x_{0}
, i.e., we have
\mathfrak{h}^{1}=\mathbb{Q}\mathbf{1}+\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle x_{1}
. Using the iterated integral representation of multiple zeta values one obtains a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{h}^{1},\shuffle)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z},

(1.4)

\displaystyle x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\dots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta^{\shuffle}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}).

The elements
\zeta^{\shuffle}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
are the
shuffle regularized multiple zeta values
, they are uniquely determined by
\zeta^{\shuffle}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
for all
k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
and
\zeta^{\shuffle}(1)=0
.

In
[
IKZ06
, Theorem 1]
an explicit map is given relating the stuffle and the shuffle regularized multiple zeta values, which allows comparing the two product formulas given in (
1.2
) and (
1.4
). This yields the
extended double shuffle relations
among multiple zeta values, which are conjectured to give all algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}
(
[
IKZ06
]
).

Quasi-shuffle products can be lifted to symmetries among generating series (Section
3
). Consider the generating series of the shuffle resp. stuffle regularized multiple zeta values

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}_{d}^{\bullet}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1}\zeta^{\bullet}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}\in\mathcal{Z}\llbracket X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\rrbracket,\qquad d\geq 1,

with
\bullet\in\{\ast,\shuffle\}
. The stuffle and shuffle product in depth
2
translate to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}_{1}^{\ast}(X_{1})\mathfrak{z}_{1}^{\ast}(X_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{z}_{2}^{\ast}(X_{1},X_{2})+\mathfrak{z}_{2}^{\ast}(X_{2},X_{1})+\frac{\mathfrak{z}_{1}^{\ast}(X_{1})-\mathfrak{z}_{1}^{\ast}(X_{2})}{X_{1}-X_{2}},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}_{1}^{\shuffle}(X_{1})\mathfrak{z}_{1}^{\shuffle}(X_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{z}_{2}^{\shuffle}(X_{1}+X_{2},X_{2})+\mathfrak{z}_{2}^{\shuffle}(X_{1}+X_{2},X_{1}).

(1.6)

In general depths, there are recursive formulas for the stuffle and shuffle product in terms of generating series of words (
[
Ih07
, Proposition 8]
). Those product formulas explain Ecalle’s notion of
symmetril
and
symmetral
moulds (
[
Ec02
, equation (6), (8)]
).

Multiple q-zeta values.

For a better understanding of the structure of the multiple zeta values, we study particular q-analogs of them. Including the additional variable
q
reveals new structures. For example, one obtains an involution relating the two product formulas of multiple zeta values, or a derivation with respect to
q
known from the theory of quasi-modular forms.

We use the model-free approach to q-analogs of multiple zeta values given in
[
BK20
]
. To integers
s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
and polynomials
R_{1}\in t\mathbb{Q}[t],\ R_{2},\ldots,R_{l}\in

\mathbb{Q}[t]
, associate the
generic multiple q-zeta value

\zeta_{q}(s_{1},...,s_{l};R_{1},...,R_{l})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{l}>0}\frac{R_{1}(q^{n_{1}})}{(1-q^{n_{1}})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{R_{l}(q^{n_{l}})}{(1-q^{n_{l}})^{s_{l}}}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

For
s_{1}\geq 2
,
s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 1
, a generic multiple q-zeta values
\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l};R_{1},\ldots,R_{l})
is indeed a (modified) q-analog of multiple zeta values (Proposition
8.2
). The product of any two generic multiple q-zeta values is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of generic multiple q-zeta values.

Definition
.

The
algebra of multiple q-zeta values
is the subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket
given by

\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\big\{\zeta_{q}(s_{1},...,s_{l};R_{1},...,R_{l})\ \big|\ l\geq 0,s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},...,s_{l}\geq 0,\deg(R_{j})\leq s_{j}\big\},

where we set
\zeta_{q}(\emptyset;\emptyset)=1
.

The additional requirement on the degree of the polynomials is natural. For example,
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is related to polynomial functions on partitions (
[
Ba19
]
,
[
BI22
]
), which implies the existence of spanning sets of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
invariant under some involution. The space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
also occurs in enumerative geometry. More precisely, A. Okounkov conjectured that certain generating series of Chern numbers on Hilbert schemes of points are always contained in the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
(
[
Ok14
]
).

A
model
of multiple q-zeta values is a particular assumption on the polynomials
R_{i}
(usually these particular polynomials form a basis of
\mathbb{Q}[t]
) or just a spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. About twenty years ago the first models for multiple q-zeta values were introduced independently by D. Bradley, K. Schlesinger, J. Zhao, and W. Zudilin (
[
Bra05
]
,
[
Sc01
]
,
[
Zh07
]
,
[
Zu03
]
). Since then several more models appeared in the literature, all of them are contained in the above defined space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. An overview on the various models of multiple q-zeta values and their relations is given in
[
BK20
]
and
[
Bri21
]
.

Traditionally, the models of multiple q-zeta values focus on a q-analog of the shuffle product given in (
1.2
) or the stuffle product given in (
1.4
) for multiple zeta values but do not combine them. So, usually it is difficult to describe a q-analog of the extended double shuffle relations. In rather recent articles on multiple q-zeta values by H. Bachmann (
[
Ba19
]
) and by K. Ebrahimi-Fard, D. Manchon, and J. Singer (
[
EMS16
]
) the focus changed to obtaining a product formula and some invariance under an involution. Combining the product formula and the involution one easily derives a q-analog of the extended double shuffle relations. In joint work with H. Bachmann (
[
BB22
]
), we constructed the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series, which satisfy a weight-graded product formula and are invariant under some weight-homogeneous involution.

Balanced multiple q-zeta values.

A main result of this article gives a new model of multiple q-zeta values, which also satisfies weight-homogeneous relations and is closely related to the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple q-zeta values studied in
[
EMS16
]
. We call the elements in this model the
balanced multiple q-zeta values

\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}),\ s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
(Definition
10.1
). The classical Eisenstein series and their derivatives are examples of balanced multiple q-zeta values. Moreover, the balanced multiple q-zeta values can be used to conjecturally describe all relations among multiple Eisenstein series. This new model also gives an explicit description of a conjectural weight-grading on the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, which extends the weight-grading of the algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of quasi-modular forms with rational coefficients (introduced in
[
KZ95
]
). The balanced multiple q-zeta values are q-analogs of multiple zeta values, taking the limit
q\to 1
always yields an element in the algebra of multiple zeta values (Proposition
11.5
, Remark
11.6
). An advantage of the balanced multiple q-zeta values is that they satisfy a product formula, which can be seen as a balanced combination of the shuffle and stuffle product for the multiple zeta values (Theorem
10.4
). Therefore, they provide a setup to study both products of the multiple zeta values at the same time. Moreover, the balanced multiple q-zeta values satisfy linear relations coming from a particular simple involution. They also possess a description in terms of a finite alphabet similar to the case of multiple zeta values (Theorem
11.4
).

Consider the alphabet

\mathcal{B}=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}

and denote by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
the free non-commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{B}
. The
balanced quasi-shuffle product

\ast_{b}
is the quasi-shuffle product on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
recursively defined by
\mathbf{1}\ast_{b}w=w\ast_{b}\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle b_{i}u\ast_{b}b_{j}v=b_{i}(u\ast_{b}b_{j}v)+b_{j}(b_{i}u\ast_{b}v)+\begin{cases}b_{i+j}(u\ast_{b}v)&\text{ if }i,j\geq 1,\\
0&\text{ else}\end{cases}

(1.7)

for all
b_{i},b_{j}\in\mathcal{B},\ u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
. Restricted to the letters
b_{i}
for
i\geq 1
the product
\ast_{b}
is the stuffle product given in (
1.1
) and modulo words containing the letters
b_{i}
for
i\geq 2
we recover the shuffle product given in (
1.3
), therefore we call
\ast_{b}
the balanced quasi-shuffle product. The product
\ast_{b}
is exactly the associated weight-graded product to the quasi-stuffle product of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple q-zeta values (
[
Si15
, Theorem 3.3]
).

Denote by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
the subalgebra of
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
generated by all words which do not start in
b_{0}
, i.e., we have
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}=\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\backslash b_{0}\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
. Let
\tau:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
be the involution given by
\tau(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
and

\displaystyle\tau(b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}})=b_{m_{d}+1}b_{0}^{k_{d}-1}\dots b_{m_{1}+1}b_{0}^{k_{1}-1}.

A main result of this work is the following.

Theorem

(Theorem
10.4
)
.

There is a
\tau
-invariant, surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z}_{q},

\displaystyle b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}),

where
\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
are the balanced multiple q-zeta values introduced in Definition
10.1
.

To the balanced multiple q-zeta value
\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
associate the
weight

s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}+\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}.

The balanced quasi-shuffle product as well as the
\tau
-invariance of the balanced multiple q-zeta values are homogeneous for the weight. Since we expect that all algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
can be deduced from these to sets of relations among balanced multiple q-zeta values (Conjecture
10.5
), we expect the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
to be graded with respect to this weight.

Essential for the construction of the balanced multiple q-zeta values are the
combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series
introduced in
[
BB22
]
(Definition
9.7
), which also possess a description in terms of quasi-shuffle algebras. Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}=\{y_{k,m}\mid k\geq 1,m\geq 0\}
and let
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
be the non-commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}
. The
stuffle product

\ast
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
is recursively defined by
\mathbf{1}\ast w=w\ast\mathbf{1}=w
and

y_{k_{1},m_{2}}u\ast y_{k_{2},m_{2}}v=y_{k_{1},m_{1}}(u\ast y_{k_{2},m_{2}}v)+y_{k_{2},m_{2}}(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}u\ast v)+y_{k_{1}+k_{2},m_{1}+m_{2}}(u\ast v)

for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
. This is the canonical bi-version of the stuffle product given in (
1.1
). Moreover, there is an involution
\operatorname{swap}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
(Definition
7.9
) closely related to conjugation of partitions.

Following the general explanations in Section
3
, we will express the stuffle product
\ast
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
and the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
by a recursive formula on generating series of words (Theorem
4.1
,
5.1
). Moreover, we will obtain a
regularization map

\operatorname{reg}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})\to(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b}),

which also possesses a description on generating series of words (Theorem
6.3
). This regularization allows defining a
regularized coproduct

\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}^{0}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\otimes\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
compatible with the balanced quasi-shuffle product. The lift to generating series of words allows us to show the following.

Theorem

(Theorem
7.10
)
.

There is an isomorphism of weight-graded Hopf algebras

\displaystyle\varphi_{\#}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle,\ast,\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}})\to(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b},\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}^{0}),

which satisfies
\varphi_{\#}\circ\operatorname{swap}=\tau\circ\varphi_{\#}
.

The stuffle product
\ast
as well as the involution
\operatorname{swap}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
can be rephrased in terms of bimoulds (compare to Section
3
), this leads to the symmetries usually called
symmetril
and
swap invariant
(those symmetries are extensively studied in
[
Ec11
]
,
[
Sc15
]
). An example of a symmetril and swap invariant bimould is the bimould
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathfrak{G}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
of generating series of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series (Definition
9.5
). On the other hand, the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
and the involution on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
also lead to symmetries of bimoulds, which we will refer to as
b-symmetril
and
\tau
-invariant
. Theorem
7.10
gives an isomorphism between symmetril and swap invariant bimoulds and b-symmetril and
\tau
-invariant bimoulds. Applying this isomorphism to the bimould
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathfrak{G}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
of generating series of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series yields the bimould
\mathfrak{B}=(\mathfrak{B}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
of generating series of the balanced multiple q-zeta values (Definition
10.1
). For example, the b-symmetrility of
\mathfrak{B}
in depth
2
reads

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{1}\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot\mathfrak{B}_{1}\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}=\mathfrak{B}_{2}\binom{X_{2},X_{1}}{Y_{2},Y_{1}+Y_{2}}+\mathfrak{B}_{2}\binom{X_{1},X_{2}}{Y_{1},Y_{1}+Y_{2}}+\frac{\mathfrak{B}_{1}\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}-\mathfrak{B}_{1}\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}.

It combines the product formulas on the generating series of the stuffle and shuffle regularized multiple zeta values given in (
1.5
). The results in Section
7
for the generating series of words allow us to deduce the previously presented properties of the balanced multiple q-zeta values from the b-symmetrility and the
\tau
-invariance of
\mathfrak{B}
.

. Then in Section

2.
Quasi-shuffle Hopf algebras

We give a short introduction to quasi-shuffle Hopf algebras, which were introduced and studied in
[
Hof00
]
and
[
HI17
]
. They will be used later to describe the product of the algebra of multiple q-zeta values in terms of particular spanning sets.

In the following,
R
is some arbitrary fixed
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an
alphabet
, this means
\mathcal{A}
is a countable set whose elements are called
letters
. By
R\mathcal{A}
denote the
R
-module spanned by the letters of
\mathcal{A}
and let
R\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
be the free non-commutative algebra generated by the alphabet
\mathcal{A}
. The monic monomials in
R\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
are called
words
with letters in
\mathcal{A}
, and the set of all words is denoted by
\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Moreover, let
\mathbf{1}
be the empty word.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\diamond:R\mathcal{A}\times R\mathcal{A}\to R\mathcal{A}
be an associative and commutative product. Define the
quasi-shuffle product

\ast_{\diamond}
on
R\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
recursively by
\mathbf{1}\ast_{\diamond}w=w\ast_{\diamond}\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle au\ast_{\diamond}bv=a(u\ast_{\diamond}bv)+b(au\ast_{\diamond}v)+(a\diamond b)(u\ast_{\diamond}v)

for all
u,v,w\in R\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
and
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

Note that a quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{\diamond}
can be equally defined recursively from the left and from the right.

Example 2.2
.

1. Define

a\diamond b=0\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathcal{A},

then we get the well-known
shuffle product
, which is usually denoted by
\shuffle
.

2. Consider the bi-alphabet
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}=\{y_{k,m}\mid k\geq 1,\ m\geq 0\}
and on
R\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}
define the product

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\diamond y_{k_{2},m_{2}}=y_{k_{1}+k_{2},m_{1}+m_{2}}.

The obtained quasi-shuffle product is called the
stuffle product
and is denoted by
\ast
. It appears in the context of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series (Corollary
9.10
) and is exactly the associated weight-graded product to the quasi-shuffle product of Bachmann’s bi-brackets (
[
Ba19
, Theorem 3.6]
).

3. Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
and define on
R\mathcal{B}
the product

b_{i}\diamond_{b}b_{j}=\begin{cases}b_{i+j},&\text{if }i,j\geq 1,\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

This quasi-shuffle product occurs for balanced multiple q-zeta values (Theorem
10.4
) and will be called the
balanced quasi-shuffle product
, it is denoted by
\ast_{b}
. It is exactly the associated weight-graded product to the quasi-shuffle product of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple q-zeta values (
[
Si15
, Theorem 3.3]
).

Let
\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\otimes\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
be the
deconcatenation product
, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}(w)=\sum_{uv=w}u\otimes v\quad\text{ for each word }w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle.

(2.1)

We close this section with the following structure theorem of Hoffman.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
Hof00
, Theorem 3.2, 3.3]
) The tuple
(R\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond},\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}})
is an associative, commutative Hopf algebra. There is an isomorphism between all quasi-shuffle Hopf algebras defined over the same alphabet given by some exponential map. ∎

It is well-known that the shuffle algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle,\shuffle)
is a free polynomial algebra on the Lyndon words (
[
Re93
, Theorem 4.9 (ii)]
). The theorem shows that this holds for any quasi-shuffle algebra.

3.
Quasi-shuffle products and generating series

Inspired by
[
Ih07
, Section 7]
, we will explain how quasi-shuffle products can be translated into symmetries of commutative generating series resp. bimoulds. This gives a connection between Hoffman’s theory of quasi-shuffle Hopf algebras (
[
Hof00
]
,
[
HI17
]
) and Ecalle’s theory of bimoulds (
[
Ec11
]
,
[
Sc15
]
). We will use this general setup later to construct a particular nice-behaving model for multiple q-zeta values and explain its properties.

Consider some given quasi-shuffle algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
. Define the
generic diagonal series
of
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}w\otimes w.

We want to apply
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps to the first factors, usually denoted by
\varphi
, or to the second factors, usually denoted by
\rho
, of
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A})
to get generating series of different kinds and describe the resulting properties. We begin with an abstract discussion and later a more detailed explanation of special cases is given.

Let
\operatorname{dep}:\mathcal{A}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a
depth map
compatible with concatenation, i.e., we have

\operatorname{dep}(uv)=\operatorname{dep}(u)+\operatorname{dep}(v),\qquad u,v\in\mathcal{A}^{*}.

Denote by
(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d)}
the set of all words in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
of depth
d
and by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle^{(d)}
the space spanned by
(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d)}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}[Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots]
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map having the following properties with respect to the depth map:

(i)

There is a strictly increasing sequence
\ell(0)<\ell(1)<\ell(2)<\dots
of non-negative integers, such that for each
d\geq 0
the restriction of
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
to
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle^{(d)}
is an injective
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}|_{\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle^{(d)}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle^{(d)}\to\mathbb{Q}[Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}].

(ii)

We have

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(uv)=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(u)\rho_{\mathcal{A}}^{[\ell(n)]}(v),\qquad u\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle^{(n)},\ v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle,

where
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}^{[n]}
denotes the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map obtained from
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
by shifting the variables
Z_{i}
to
Z_{n+i}
, so
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}^{[n]}\Big(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle^{(d)}\Big)\subset\mathbb{Q}[Z_{n+1},\ldots,Z_{n+\ell(d)}]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}[Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots]
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map as in Definition
3.1
. The (commutative)
generating series of words
in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
associated to
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
are given by

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})=\sum_{w\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d)}}w\rho(w)\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\llbracket Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}\rrbracket,\qquad d\geq 0.

We will often drop the index
d
and simply write
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})
.

Example 3.3
.

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots\}
and define the depth of a word in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
by

\operatorname{dep}(y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}})=d.

The map

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{Y}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Q}[Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots],

\displaystyle y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}}

\displaystyle\mapsto Z_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots Z_{d}^{k_{d}-1}

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map satisfying the conditions in Definition
3.1
with
\ell(d)=d
. The associated generating series of words are given by
\rho_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{W})_{0}=\mathbf{1}
and

\rho_{\mathcal{Y}}(\mathcal{W})_{d}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d})=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1}y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}}Z_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots Z_{d}^{k_{d}-1},\qquad d\geq 1.

In the following sections, we will compute more involved examples.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle A\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}[Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots]
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map as in Definition
3.1
with
\ell(d_{1})+\ell(d_{2})=\ell(d_{1}+d_{2})
for all
d_{1},d_{2}\geq 0
. Then the
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots\rrbracket
-linear extension of the concatenation product
\cdot
satisfies

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(n)})\cdot\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d-n}(Z_{\ell(n)+1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})

for all
0\leq n\leq d
.

Proof.

For
n=0,d
the formula is obvious. For
0<n<d
, compute

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(n)})\cdot\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d-n}(Z_{\ell(n)+1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})=\sum_{u\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(n)}}\sum_{v\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d-n)}}\hskip-5.69046ptuv\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(u)\rho_{\mathcal{A}}^{[\ell(n)]}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(n)}}\sum_{v\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d-n)}}uv\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(uv)=\sum_{w\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d)}}w\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(w)=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}).

∎

If we extend the quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
to
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\llbracket Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots\rrbracket
by
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots\rrbracket
-linearity, then by definition of the quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{\diamond}
we have for all
0\leq n\leq d
that

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(n)})\ast_{\diamond}\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d-n}(Z_{\ell(n)+1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\llbracket Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}\rrbracket.

For some very well-behaved quasi-shuffle products appearing for example in the theory of multiple zeta values and multiple q-zeta values, it is possible to describe this product on the generating series of words by a recursive explicit formula with respect to concatenation. This will explain the origin of a particular kind of symmetries occurring in Ecalle’s theory of bimoulds (
[
Ec11
]
). Some important examples related to multiple q-zeta values will be discussed in the following sections, for some other examples also related to multiple zeta values, we refer to
[
Bu23
, Appendix A.4 + A.5]
.

To translate the quasi-shuffle products into symmetries among bimoulds, we apply a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
into some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
to the first component of such a generating series of words
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})
. In other words, we consider the image of the generic diagonal series
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A})
under
\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}[Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots]
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map as in Definition
3.1
,
R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map. Then the
(commutative) generating series with coefficients in
R
associated to
(\varphi,\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
are given by

(\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})_{d}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})=\sum_{w\in(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{(d)}}\varphi(w)\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(w)\in R\llbracket Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}\rrbracket,\qquad d\geq 0.

As before, we will often drop the depth index and simply write
(\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})
.

Such kind of sequences
(\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})=\Big((\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})_{d}\Big)_{d\geq 0}
will occur as examples of Ecalle’s (bi-)moulds (
[
Ec11
]
).

Definition 3.6
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra. A sequence

M=\left(M_{d}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})\right)_{d\geq 0}=\left(M_{0}(\emptyset),M_{1}(X_{1}),M_{2}(X_{1},X_{2}),\ldots\right)\in\prod_{d\geq 0}R\llbracket X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\rrbracket

is called a
mould
with coefficients in
R
. Similarly, a sequence

M=\left(M_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}\right)_{d\geq 0}=\left(M_{0}(\emptyset),M_{1}\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}},M_{2}\binom{X_{1},X_{2}}{Y_{1},Y_{2}},\ldots\right)\in\prod_{d\geq 0}R\llbracket X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{d}\rrbracket

is called a
bimould
with coefficients in
R
.

If
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots]
is a map as in Definition
3.1
with
\ell(d)=d
for all
d\geq 0
, then the corresponding generating series of words
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})=(\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d})_{d\geq 0}
are a mould with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
. For any
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
, the generating series
(\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})=((\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})_{d})_{d\geq 0}
associated to
(\varphi,\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
are a mould with coefficients in
R
. Similarly, if
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\ldots]
is a map as in Definition
3.1
with
\ell(d)=2d
for every
d\geq 0
, then the corresponding generating series of words
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})=(\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d})_{d\geq 0}
are a bimould with coefficients in the algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle
, and also the generating series
(\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})=((\varphi\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})_{d})_{d\geq 0}
associated to
(\varphi,\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
for each
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
are a bimould with coefficients in
R
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and
\ell(0)<\ell(1)<\ell(2)<\dots
a sequence of non-negative integers. A sequence
M=(M_{d})_{d\geq 0}
in
\prod_{d\geq 0}R\llbracket Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}\rrbracket
is called
(\varphi_{\ast_{\diamond}},\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
-symmetric
if there exists a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra morphism
\varphi_{\ast_{\diamond}}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})\to R
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\llbracket Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots\rrbracket
satisfying the conditions in Definition
3.1
, such that for all
d\geq 0

M_{d}=(\varphi_{\ast_{\diamond}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{A}})(\mathcal{W})_{d}.

In the following sections, we will consider two particular quasi-shuffle algebras, which will give rise to the notion of symmetril and b-symmetril bimoulds following Definition
3.7
.

Let
M=(M_{d})_{d\geq 0}\in\prod_{d\geq 0}R\llbracket Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d}\rrbracket
be such a
(\varphi_{\ast_{\diamond}},\rho_{\mathcal{A}})
-symmetric sequence. Then, one obtains immediately from the definition that for
0<n<d

\displaystyle M_{n}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(n)})M_{d-n}(Z_{\ell(n)+1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})

(3.1)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt=\varphi_{\ast_{\diamond}}\Big(\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(n)})\ast_{\diamond}\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{W})_{d-n}(Z_{\ell(n)+1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)})\Big).

The right-hand side is an element in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\llbracket Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{\ell(d)}\rrbracket
, so the map
\varphi_{\ast_{\diamond}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
needs to be extended by
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots\rrbracket
-linearity. As mentioned before, in some special cases the right-hand side can be described in terms of an explicit recursive formula.

Alternatively, we can first apply the evaluation map
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
for some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
to the generic diagonal series
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A})
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map. Define the
(non-commutative) generating series with coefficients in
R
associated to
\varphi
by

\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\varphi(w)w\in R\langle\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\rangle.

Here
R\langle\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\rangle
denotes non-commutative algebra of power series over
R
generated by
\mathcal{A}
.

The generating series
\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))
can also be decomposed into its homogeneous depth components
\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))_{d}
for
d\geq 0
(similar to Definition
3.2
).

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and
\varphi:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle\to R
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map. Assume that the algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{A}\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
is graded with
\operatorname{deg}(a)\geq 1
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and denote by
\Delta_{\ast_{\diamond}}
the dual completed coproduct to
\ast_{\diamond}
.

The map
\varphi
is an algebra morphism for the quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{\diamond}
if and only if
\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))
is grouplike for the coproduct
\Delta_{\ast_{\diamond}}
.

Proof.

By duality, we have

\Delta_{\ast_{\diamond}}\Big(\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))\Big)=\Delta_{\ast_{\diamond}}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\varphi(w)w\right)=\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\varphi(u\ast_{\diamond}v)u\otimes v.

So
\varphi
is an algebra morphism for the quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{\diamond}
if and only if

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ast_{\diamond}}\Big(\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))\Big)=\sum_{u,v\in\mathcal{A}^{*}}\varphi(u)\varphi(v)u\otimes v=\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A}))\otimes\varphi(\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{A})).

∎

\varphi

4.
Generating series of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}=\{y_{k,m}\mid k\geq 1,\ m\geq 0\}
and let the
weight
and the
depth
of a word in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
be given by

\operatorname{wt}(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d},m_{d}})=k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}+m_{1}+\dots+m_{d},\qquad\operatorname{dep}(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d},m_{d}})=d,

for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0
. Define the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\ldots],

(4.1)

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d},m_{d}}

\displaystyle\mapsto X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}\frac{Y_{d}^{m_{d}}}{m_{d}!},

(4.2)

then
\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
3.1
with
\ell(d)=2d
for all
d\geq 0
. The generating series of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
associated to
\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}
are given by
\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})_{0}=\mathbf{1}
and for
d\geq 1
by

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d},m_{d}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}\frac{Y_{d}^{m_{d}}}{m_{d}!}.

(4.3)

The stuffle product
\ast
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
is one of the well-behaved quasi-shuffle products, where we can give an explicit recursive formula on the level of generating series of words.

Theorem 4.1
.

For the stuffle product
\ast
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
(Example
2.2
.2.), one obtains for all
0<n<d
that
\mathbf{1}\ast\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}=\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}\ast\mathbf{1}=\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})_{n}
and

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}}\ast\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{n+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot\left(\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}}{Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}}\ast\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{n+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}\right)

\displaystyle+\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{n+1}}{Y_{n+1}}\cdot\left(\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}}\ast\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{n+2},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{n+2},\ldots,Y_{d}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}+Y_{n+1}}-\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{n+1}}{Y_{1}+Y_{n+1}}}{X_{1}-X_{n+1}}\cdot\left(\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}}{Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}}\ast\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{n+2},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{n+2},\ldots,Y_{d}}\right).

Proof.

First, consider the case
d=2
and compute directly

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\ast\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\\
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\ast y_{k_{2},m_{2}})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}\frac{Y_{2}^{m_{2}}}{m_{2}!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1\\
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\Big(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}y_{k_{2},m_{2}}+y_{k_{2},m_{2}}y_{k_{1},m_{1}}+y_{k_{1}+k_{2},m_{1}+m_{2}}\Big)X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}\frac{Y_{2}^{m_{2}}}{m_{2}!}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1},X_{2}}{Y_{1},Y_{2}}+\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{2},X_{1}}{Y_{2},Y_{1}}+\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}-\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}}{X_{1}-X_{2}},

where the last step follows from simple power series manipulations. Since the definition of the stuffle product
\ast
as well as the above formula for the generating series
\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})
is recursive, the arbitrary depth case follows similarly by induction.
∎

5.
Generating series of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
and define the
weight
and the
depth
of a word in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
by

\operatorname{wt}(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})=s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}+\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\},\qquad\operatorname{dep}(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})=l-\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\},

for all
s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
. Let
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map given by

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Q}[Y_{0},X_{1},Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\ldots],

(5.1)

\displaystyle b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}

\displaystyle\mapsto Y_{0}^{m_{0}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}}\quad(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0),

(5.2)

then
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
3.1
with
\ell(d)=2d+1
for all
d\geq 0
. The generating series of words associated to
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
are given by for
d\geq 0

\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})_{d}\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{0};Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{0},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}Y_{0}^{m_{0}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}}.

Let
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
be the subspace of
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
generated by all words not starting in
b_{0}
, i.e., we have

\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}=\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\backslash b_{0}\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle.

Define the corresponding diagonal series

\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{B})^{0}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{B}^{*}\cap\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}}w\otimes w.

Let
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\to\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\ldots]
be the restriction of
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
to
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
. Then the generating series of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
associated to
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}
are given by

\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})_{0}=\mathbf{1}
and

\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}},\quad d\geq 1.

On
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
is very well-behaved, thus we can also give a recursive definition on generating series of words for this product.

Theorem 5.1
.

For the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
(Example
2.2
.3.), one obtains for all
0<n<d
that
\mathbf{1}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})_{n}=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})_{n}\ast_{b}\ \mathbf{1}=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})_{n}
and

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{n+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n-1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{n+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}\right)\cdot\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{n}}{Y_{n}+Y_{d}}

\displaystyle+\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{d-1}}{Y_{n+1},\ldots,Y_{d-1}}\right)\cdot\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{d}}{Y_{n}+Y_{d}}

\displaystyle+\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n-1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{n+1},\ldots,X_{d-1}}{Y_{n+1},\ldots,Y_{d-1}}\right)\cdot\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{n}}{Y_{n}+Y_{d}}-\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{d}}{Y_{n}+Y_{d}}}{X_{n}-X_{d}}.

Proof.

Consider the case
d=2
. We obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\cdot\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}-b_{0}Y_{1}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{1}.

(5.4)

By applying the second identity in (
5.3
) and the definition of the balanced quasi-shuffle product (we use the recursive definition from the right), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)+\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{2}\right)\hskip 85.35826pt

\displaystyle+\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)+\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)+\left(\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}Y_{2}\right)\right)\cdot b_{0}

\displaystyle+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{2}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}+\left(\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}Y_{1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)\right)\cdot b_{0}

\displaystyle+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{1}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}+\left(\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}Y_{1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{2}\right)\right)\cdot b_{0}

\displaystyle+\left(\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot b_{0}Y_{1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}Y_{2}\right)\right)\cdot b_{0}.

Applying again the second identity in (
5.3
) together with some cancellation, we deduce

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)-2\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}

\displaystyle+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}+\left(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}\right)\cdot b_{0}(Y_{1}+Y_{2}).

Using the first identity in (
5.3
) together with the formula for the concatenation product in terms of generating series of words (Proposition
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)-2\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)\right)\cdot\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}-b_{0}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})}

\displaystyle\hskip 12.80365pt+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2},X_{1}}{Y_{2},Y_{1}+Y_{2}}+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},X_{2}}{Y_{1},Y_{1}+Y_{2}}.

Moreover, one verifies by applying the definition of
\ast_{b}
and some power series manipulation

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\ast_{b}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)=2\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k-1}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}\right)+\frac{\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{1}^{k_{1}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}b_{k}X_{2}^{k-1}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}.

Applying this result to (
5.5
) together with (
5.3
) gives

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}\ast_{b}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 51.21504pt=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2},X_{1}}{Y_{2},Y_{1}+Y_{2}}+\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},X_{2}}{Y_{1},Y_{1}+Y_{2}}+\frac{\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}-\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{2}}{Y_{1}+Y_{2}}}{X_{1}-X_{2}}.

This equals exactly the claimed formula of
\ast_{b}
for the generating series in depth
2
. Since the definition of the product
\ast_{b}
and the above generating series formula are both recursive, we obtain the desired formula in arbitrary depth by applying induction and the same computations as before.
∎

Definition 5.2
.

A bimould
M=(M_{d})_{d\geq 0}\in\prod_{d\geq 0}R\llbracket X_{1},Y_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{d}\rrbracket
with coefficients in some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

is

if there is an algebra morphism
\varphi_{\ast_{b}}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b})\to R
, such that
M
is
(\varphi_{\ast_{b}},\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0})
-symmetric, i.e., we have
M_{0}=1
and for all
d\geq 1

M_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\varphi_{\ast_{b}}(b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}}.

As before, we call the map
\varphi_{\ast_{b}}
the
coefficient map
of

6.
Generating series of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
and regularization

The deconcatenation coproduct on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
(defined in (

)) is compatible with the translation map
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
, hence it can be described in terms of the generating series
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})
of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
. But the deconcatenation coproduct does not preserve the subspace
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}

\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})
of words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}

Proposition 6.1
.

For all
d\geq 0
, we have

\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{0};Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{1},\ldots,X_{i}}{Y_{0};Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{i+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{i};Y_{i+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}.

Proof.

By definition of the deconcatenation coproduct, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{0};Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{0},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}(b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}})Y_{0}^{m_{0}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{0},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+n_{2}=m_{i}\\
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}(b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{i}}b_{0}^{n_{1}}\otimes b_{0}^{n_{2}}b_{k_{i+1}}b_{0}^{m_{i+1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}})Y_{0}^{m_{0}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}}

Reordering these sums, we obtain that this expression is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{0},\dots,m_{i-1},n_{1},n_{2},m_{i+1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}(b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{i}}b_{0}^{n_{1}}\otimes b_{0}^{n_{2}}b_{k_{i+1}}b_{0}^{m_{i+1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}})Y_{0}^{m_{0}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 270.30118pt\dots X_{i}^{k_{i}-1}Y_{i}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}X_{i+1}^{k_{i+1}-1}Y_{i+1}^{m_{i+1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{1},\ldots,X_{i}}{Y_{0};Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{\hskip 14.22636ptX_{i+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{i};Y_{i+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

∎

Next, we introduce a regularization, which assigns to each word in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
an element in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
, such that the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
is preserved. The regularization process is similar to the ones for multiple zeta values given in
[
IKZ06
, Proposition 1]
. We will rephrase this regularization map in terms of generating series of words.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
T
be a formal variable and extend the product
\ast_{b}
by
\mathbb{Q}[T]
-linearity to
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}[T]
. The map

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}_{T}:\ \mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}[T]

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,

\displaystyle wT^{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto w\ast_{b}b_{0}^{\ast_{b}n}

is an algebra isomorphism for the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
.

Proof.

For the surjectivity of
\operatorname{reg}_{T}
, we show that any word
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a polynomial in
b_{0}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
.
Let
w=b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}
for some integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
and
m_{0},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0
. We prove by induction on
m_{0}
, that
w=u+v\ast_{b}b_{0}
for some
u\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
and
v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
, where all words in
v
have weight smaller than
\operatorname{wt}(w)
. Then induction on the weight proves the claim.
The case
m_{0}=0
is trivial, simply choose
u=w,\ v=0
. Next, calculate

\displaystyle b_{0}^{m_{0}-1}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}\ast_{b}b_{0}=

\displaystyle\ m_{0}\ b_{0}^{m_{0}}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(m_{i}+1)\ b_{0}^{m_{0}-1}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{i}}b_{0}^{m_{i}+1}b_{k_{i+1}}b_{0}^{m_{i+1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}.

Applying the induction hypotheses to every word in the second line leads to

w=\frac{1}{m_{0}}\left(u+\big(v+b_{0}^{m_{0}-1}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}\big)\ast_{b}b_{0}\right)

for some
u\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
and
v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
, where
v+b_{0}^{m_{0}-1}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}}
consists of words of weight smaller than
\operatorname{wt}(w)
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}[T]\backslash\{0\}
and write
P=wT^{n}+R
, where
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\backslash\{0\}
and
R\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}[T]
is a polynomial of degree smaller than
n
. We have

\operatorname{reg}_{T}(P)=w\ast_{b}b_{0}^{\ast_{b}n}+\operatorname{reg}_{T}(R)=n!b_{0}^{n}w+\widetilde{w},

where
\widetilde{w}\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
consists of words with at most
(n-1)
-times the letter
b_{0}
at the beginning. We deduce
\operatorname{reg}_{T}(P)\neq 0
, thus
\operatorname{reg}_{T}
is injective.
∎

In particular, by first applying the inverse
\operatorname{reg}_{T}^{-1}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}[T]
and then evaluating in
T=0
yields the desired
regularization map

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})\to(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b}).

(6.1)

By construction, it is an algebra morphism for the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
.

\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\ldots]
-linearity to
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}\llbracket X_{1},Y_{1},X_{2},Y_{2},\ldots\rrbracket
, then we obtain directly for each
d\geq 0
that

\tau\Bigg(\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}\Bigg)=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{1}}{X_{d},\ldots,X_{1}}.

This motivates the following definition for bimoulds.

Definition 7.8
.

For a bimould
M=(M_{d})_{d\geq 0}
, define the bimould
\tau(M)=(\tau(M)_{d})_{d\geq 0}
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{\tau}(M)_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=M_{d}\binom{Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{1}}{X_{d},\ldots,X_{1}}.

We call a bimould
M

\tau
-invariant
if
\tau(M)=M
.

The second involution defined on the algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
is originally given in terms of bimoulds, since its expression is much easier in this case.

Definition 7.9
.

For a bimould
M=(M_{d})_{d\geq 0}
, define the bimould
\operatorname{swap}(M)=(\operatorname{swap}(M)_{d})_{d\geq 0}
as

\operatorname{swap}(M)_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=M_{d}\binom{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d},Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d-1},\ldots,Y_{1}}{X_{d},X_{d-1}-X_{d},\ldots,X_{1}-X_{2}}.

A bimould
M
is called
swap invariant
if
\operatorname{swap}(M)=M
.

Define the involution
\operatorname{swap}:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
implicitly by

(\operatorname{swap}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}})(\mathcal{W})_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\operatorname{swap}(\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W}))_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}},

i.e., the coefficient of
X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}\frac{Y_{d}^{m_{d}}}{m_{d}!}
in
\operatorname{swap}(\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W}))_{d}
is the image of the word
y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d},m_{d}}
under the map
\operatorname{swap}
.

For example, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{swap}(y_{k_{1},m_{1}})

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{1}!}{(k_{1}-1)!}y_{m_{1}+1,k_{1}-1},

(7.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{swap}(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}y_{k_{2},m_{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{v=0}^{k_{2}-1}\frac{(-1)^{v}}{u!v!}\frac{m_{1}!}{(k_{1}-1)!}\frac{(m_{2}+u)!}{(k_{2}-1-v)!}y_{m_{2}+1+u,k_{2}-1-v}y_{m_{1}+1-u,k_{1}-1+v}.

In higher depths, it is hard to give an explicit formula for the involution
\operatorname{swap}
on the algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle
, see for example
[
BI22
, Remark 3.14]
.

Theorem 7.10
.

The map

\varphi_{\#}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle,\ast,\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}})\to(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b},\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}^{0})

is an isomorphism of weight-graded Hopf algebras satisfying
\varphi_{\#}\circ\operatorname{swap}=\tau\circ\varphi_{\#}
.

Proof.

The map
\varphi_{\#}
is an isomorphism of weight-graded Hopf algebras by Theorem
7.4
and Proposition
7.6
. Moreover, one verifies directly on the level of generating series of words

\displaystyle\#_{Y}\circ\operatorname{swap}\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})^{\#_{Y}}\binom{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d},Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d-1},\ldots,Y_{1}}{X_{d},X_{d-1}-X_{d},\ldots,X_{1}-X_{2}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}}(\mathcal{W})\binom{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d},Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d-1},\ldots,Y_{1}}{X_{d},X_{d-1},\ldots,X_{1}},

and

\displaystyle\tau\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})^{\#_{Y}}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=\tau\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},Y_{1}+Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(\mathcal{W}^{0})\binom{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d},Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d-1},\ldots,Y_{1}}{X_{d},X_{d-1},\ldots,X_{1}}.

By definition of the map
\varphi_{\#}
(Definition
7.2
), we deduce that
\varphi_{\#}\circ\operatorname{swap}=\tau\circ\varphi_{\#}
.
∎

An immediate consequence of Theorem
7.10
is the following.

Corollary 7.11
.

A bimould
M=(M_{d})_{d\geq 0}
is
\tau
-invariant if and only if the bimould
M^{\#_{Y}}
is swap invariant.

8.
The algebra of multiple
\operatorname{q}
-zeta values

We introduce the algebra of multiple q-zeta values
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
and explain its relations to multiple zeta values and polynomial functions on partitions. As an application of the previous general results, we will present a particular nice spanning set for
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, the balanced multiple q-zeta values.

Definition 8.1
.

(
[
BK20
]
)
To integers
s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
and polynomials
R_{1}\in t\mathbb{Q}[t],\\
R_{2},\ldots,R_{l}\in

\mathbb{Q}[t]
, associate the
generic multiple q-zeta value

\zeta_{q}(s_{1},...,s_{l};R_{1},...,R_{l})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{l}>0}\frac{R_{1}(q^{n_{1}})}{(1-q^{n_{1}})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{R_{l}(q^{n_{l}})}{(1-q^{n_{l}})^{s_{l}}}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

The assumptions
s_{1}\geq 1
and
R_{1}\in t\mathbb{Q}[t]
are necessary for convergence.

Proposition 8.2
.

(
[
BK20
, p. 6]
) For integers
s_{1}\geq 2,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 1
and polynomials
R_{1}\in t\mathbb{Q}[t],\ R_{2},\ldots,R_{l}\in

\mathbb{Q}[t]
, one has

\lim_{q\to 1}(1-q)^{s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}}\zeta_{q}(s_{1},...,s_{l};R_{1},...,R_{l})=R_{1}(1)\cdots R_{l}(1)\zeta(s_{1},...,s_{l}).

∎

Following H. Bachmann and U. Kühn (
[
BK20
]
), we consider the following kind of multiple q-zeta values.

Definition 8.3
.

Define the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space

\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\!\big\{\zeta_{q}(s_{1},...,s_{l};R_{1},...,R_{l})\ \big|\ l\geq 0,s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},...,s_{l}\geq 0,\deg(R_{j})\leq s_{j}\big\},

where we set
\zeta_{q}(\emptyset;\emptyset)=1
.

The space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
contains all models for multiple q-zeta values studied in the literature, so this can be seen as a model-free approach to them. For
\zeta_{q}(s_{1};R_{1}),\zeta_{q}(s_{2};R_{2})\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, the usual power series multiplication reads

\zeta_{q}(s_{1};R_{1})\cdot\zeta_{q}(s_{2};R_{2})=\zeta_{q}(s_{1},s_{2};R_{1},R_{2})+\zeta_{q}(s_{2},s_{1};R_{2},R_{1})+\zeta_{q}(s_{1}+s_{2};R_{1}R_{2}).

Since
\deg(R_{1}R_{2})\leq s_{1}+s_{2}
, the product is also an element in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. Similar computations for arbitrary multi indices show that
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is an associative, commutative algebra. Moreover, the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
contains the algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of quasi-modular forms with rational coefficients (as introduced in
[
KZ95
]
) and is closed under the well-known derivation
q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}
.

Remark 8.4
.

The additional assumption on the degree of the polynomials
R_{j}
in Definition
8.3
can be justified by its relations to polynomial functions on partitions. More precisely, the original definition of the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
was given by H. Bachmann as the span of the bi-brackets (
[
Ba19
]
), which can be seen as generating series of monomial functions on partitions. In
[
BI22
, cf (1.6)]
it is shown that the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is exactly the image of the polynomial functions on partitions under the q-bracket.

Let
\lambda=(1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}3^{m_{3}}\dots)
be a partition of some natural number
N
of length
d
, i.e., the multiplicities
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are nonzero only for finitely many indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}
and one has
\sum_{i\geq 1}m_{i}i=N
. A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}]
can be evaluated at the partition
\lambda
by

f(\lambda)=f(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d},m_{i_{1}},\ldots,m_{i_{d}}).

Denote by
\mathcal{P}(N,d)
the set of all partitions of
N
of length
d
. Then we associate to a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}]
the generating series

\operatorname{Gen}_{f}(q)=\sum_{N\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(N,d)}f(\lambda)\right)q^{N}.

Whenever
f\in\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}]
is chosen to be a monomial, then
\operatorname{Gen}_{f}(q)
is equal to a bi-bracket of depth
d
, more details are given in
[
Bri21
, Theorem 1.3]
. Therefore, as a consequence of
[
BK20
, Theorem 2.3]
we obtain that the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is spanned by the generating series
\operatorname{Gen}_{f}(q)
with
f\in\mathbb{Q}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}]
,
d\geq 0
.
This indicates that the elements in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
should be invariant under some involution corresponding to conjugation of partitions.

9.
Combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series

We present the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series constructed in
[
BB22
]
. They form a spanning set for the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, so they are a particular model for multiple q-zeta values. Combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series satisfy a weight-graded product formula and are invariant under a weight-homogeneous involution. Therefore, they should induce a grading on
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, which extends the weight-grading of the algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of quasi-modular forms with rational coefficients. Their construction is inspired by the Fourier expansion of multiple Eisenstein series (
[
Ba20
, Theorem 1.4]
). Thus, the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series give a natural connection between the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
and multiple Eisenstein series. In particular, they should give a description of all relations between multiple Eisenstein series. We will recall the construction of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series as given in
[
BB22
]
.

Definition 9.1
.

By the work of G. Racinet (
[
Rac00
]
) and also the combination of the works of V. G. Drinfeld (
[
Dr91
]
) and H. Furusho (
[
Fu11
]
) there exists a rational solution to the extended double shuffle equations

\beta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{Q},\qquad k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\ k_{1}>1,

such that

\displaystyle\beta(k)=-\frac{B_{k}}{2k!},\ k\text{ even},\qquad\beta(k)=0,\ k\text{ odd}.

(9.1)

Denote by
\beta_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),\ k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
, the corresponding stuffle regularized elements and define for all
d\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{d}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1}\beta_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}.

(9.2)

Moreover set
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=1
, then
\mathfrak{b}=(\mathfrak{b}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
is a mould with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.

In general, a solution to the extended double shuffle equations is not unique. In the following, we will fix the mould
\mathfrak{b}
and the whole construction of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series will depend on this choice.

Definition 9.2
.

Define the bimould
\mathfrak{b}=(\mathfrak{b}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
by
\mathfrak{b}_{0}=1
and for
d\geq 1
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\dots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq d}\gamma_{i}\mathfrak{b}_{j-i}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{j-i},\dots,Y_{1}+Y_{2},Y_{1})\mathfrak{b}_{d-j}(X_{j+1},\dots,X_{d}),

where the coefficients
\gamma_{i}
are defined by

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\gamma_{i}T^{i}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\frac{B_{n}}{2n!}T^{n}\right).

Independent of the shape of the mould
\mathfrak{b}
, this construction will always yield a swap invariant bimould. Since the coefficients of the mould
\mathfrak{b}
satisfy the extended double shuffle relations, we obtain that the bimould
\mathfrak{b}
is also symmetril.

Definition 9.3
.

Define the bimould
\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}=(\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{2^{i}i!}\mathfrak{b}_{d-i}\binom{X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{-Y_{1},\ldots,-Y_{d-i}},\qquad d\geq 1.

For each
u\geq 1
, let
\mathfrak{L}^{(u)}=(\mathfrak{L}^{(u)}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
be the bimould given by
\mathfrak{L}^{(u)}_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{d}^{(u)}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathfrak{b}_{j-1}\binom{X_{1}-X_{j},\ldots,X_{j-1}-X_{j}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{j-1}}L_{u}\binom{X_{j}}{Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d}}

\displaystyle\hskip 284.52756pt\cdot\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}_{d-j}\binom{X_{d}-X_{j},\ldots,X_{j+1}-X_{j}}{Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{j+1}},

where the power series
L_{u}\binom{X}{Y}
is defined by

L_{u}\binom{X}{Y}=\frac{\exp(X+uY)q^{u}}{1-\exp(X)q^{u}},\qquad u\geq 1.

It can be shown (
[
BB22
, Lemma 6.20]
) that the bimould
\mathfrak{L}^{(u)}
is symmetril, the proof uses the additional conditions on the depth
1
terms of
\mathfrak{b}
given in (
9.1
).

Definition 9.4
.

Define the bimould
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}=(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
by
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq d\\
0=d_{0}<d_{1}<\dots<d_{j-1}<d_{j}=d\\
u_{1}>\dots>u_{j}>0\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\mathfrak{L}^{(u_{i})}_{d_{i}-d_{i-1}}\binom{X_{d_{i-1}+1},\ldots,X_{d_{i}}}{Y_{d_{i-1}+1},\ldots,Y_{d_{i}}}.

Since the bimould
\mathfrak{L}^{(u)}
is symmetril, also
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is symmetril. This implication is independent of the explicit shape of
\mathfrak{L}^{(u)}
.

Definition 9.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathfrak{G}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
be the mould product of
\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
and
\mathfrak{b}
, i.e., one has
\mathfrak{G}_{0}=1
and for
d\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\ast}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{i}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i}}\mathfrak{b}_{d-i}\binom{X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{i+1},\ldots,Y_{d}}.

The main result of
[
BB22
]
was the following.

Theorem 9.6
.

[
BB22
, Theorem 6.5.]
The bimould
\mathfrak{G}
is symmetril and swap invariant. ∎

As a product of symmetril bimoulds,
\mathfrak{G}
is symmetril. To obtain the swap invariance, one decomposes
\mathfrak{G}
into a sum of bimoulds, for which the swap invariance can be shown.

Definition 9.7
.

For
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0
define the
combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series

G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}}
to be the (normalized) coefficients of the bimould
\mathfrak{G}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}\frac{Y_{d}^{m_{d}}}{m_{d}!}.

We refer to the number
k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}+m_{1}+\dots+m_{d}
as the weight of
G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}}
and to the number
d
as its depth. Moreover, the elements

G(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})=G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{0,\ldots,0},\qquad k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,

are called the
combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series
.

Example 9.8
.

In depth
1
, one obtains

\mathfrak{G}_{1}\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}=\mathfrak{b}_{1}\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}+\sum_{u>0}L_{u}\binom{X_{1}}{Y_{1}}

and thus for
k\geq 1,\ m\geq 0

G\binom{k}{m}=-\delta_{m,0}\frac{B_{k}}{2k!}-\delta_{k,1}\frac{B_{m+1}}{2(m+1)}+\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\sum_{u,v>0}u^{m}v^{k-1}q^{uv}.

In particular, the combinatorial Eisenstein series
G(k)
,
k\geq 2
even, are exactly the classical Eisenstein series of weight
k
with rational coefficients (expressed in their Fourier expansion). The combinatorial bi-Eisenstein series
G\binom{k}{m}
,
k+m\geq 2
even, is essentially the
m
-th derivative of the classical Eisenstein series
G(k)
and hence is also contained in the algebra
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{Q}}(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of quasi-modular forms with rational coefficients.

Proposition 9.9
.

(
[
BB22
, Proposition 6.15.]
) The combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series form a spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. ∎

Theorem
9.6
, the definition of the symmetrility and swap (compare to Definition
4.2
,
7.9
), and Proposition
9.9
imply the following.

Corollary 9.10
.

There is a surjective, swap invariant algebra morphism

\displaystyle G:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle,\ast)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z}_{q},

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d},m_{d}}

\displaystyle\mapsto G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}}.

Conjecture 9.11
.

(
[
BB22
, Remark 6.11.]
)
All algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
are a consequence of the stuffle product formula and the swap invariance of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series.

If Conjecture
9.11
holds, then the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is graded by weight

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\bigoplus_{w\geq 0}\mathcal{Z}_{q}^{(w)}.

(9.3)

Here
\mathcal{Z}_{q}^{(w)}
denotes the subspace of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
spanned by all combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series of weight
w
. This follows immediately from the observation that the stuffle product and also the swap operator are homogeneous in weight.

10.
Balanced multiple
\operatorname{q}
-zeta values

We will apply the results from Section
7
to obtain a new spanning set of the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. They will satisfy very explicit and simple relations, which are homogeneous in weight. Thus, this new spanning set should induce a weight-grading on
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
.

Definition 10.1
.

Define the bimould
\mathfrak{B}=(\mathfrak{B}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
by
\mathfrak{B}_{0}=1
and for
d\geq 1
by

\mathfrak{B}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\mathfrak{G}^{\#_{Y}^{-1}}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}},

where
\mathfrak{G}
is the bimould of generating series of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series (Definition
9.5
). The
balanced multiple q-zeta values

\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
,
s_{1}\geq 1
,
s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
, are the coefficients of the bimoulds
\mathfrak{B}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}Y_{d}^{m_{d}},\quad d\geq 1.

Equivalently, the balanced multiple q-zeta values can be defined as

\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=G(\varphi_{\#}^{-1}(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})),

where
G:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}^{\operatorname{bi}}\rangle\to\mathcal{Z}_{q}
denotes the algebra morphism induced by the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series given in Corollary
9.10
.

Remark 10.2
.

The definition of the balanced multiple q-zeta values has many similarities with Zudilin’s definition of the multiple q-zeta brackets in terms of the bi-brackets (
[
Zu15
, eq (8)]
).

Since the bimould
\mathfrak{G}
of generating series of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series is symmetril and swap invariant (Theorem
9.6
), an immediate consequence of Corollary
7.5
and
7.11
is the following.

Proposition 10.3
.

The bimould
\mathfrak{B}
is b-symmetril and
\tau
-invariant.

These symmetries of the bimould
\mathfrak{B}
yield the following properties of balanced multiple q-zeta values.

Theorem 10.4
.

There is a
\tau
-invariant, surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0},\ast_{b})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z}_{q},

\displaystyle b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}).

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of the b-symmetrility and
\tau
-invariance of the bimould
\mathfrak{B}
(see Definition
5.2
,
7.8
) together with the observation that
\#_{Y}
is a bijection.
∎

As a reformulation of Conjecture
9.11
, we expect the following.

Conjecture 10.5
.

All algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
are a consequence of the balanced quasi-shuffle product formula and the
\tau
-invariance of the balanced multiple q-zeta values.

Observe that the balanced quasi-shuffle product, as well as the
\tau
-invariance, are both completely explicit on the level of words of multi indices and easy to compute. If Conjecture
10.5
holds, then the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is graded by weight

\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\bigoplus_{w\geq 0}\mathcal{Z}_{q}^{(w)},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{q}^{(w)}
is the subspace of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
spanned by all balanced multiple q-zeta values of weight
w
. This conjectural grading coincides with the one of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series given in (
9.3
).

It was conjectured in
[
Ba19
, Conjecture 4.3]
in a slightly different setup that the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is spanned by the elements
\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
,
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
. Some partial results towards this conjecture are obtained in
[

, Section 6]
,
[

, Proposition 4.4, 5.9]
and

.

Example 10.6
.

1. In depth
1
the balanced multiple q-zeta values coincide with the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series up to multiplication with certain factorials. Thus, we deduce from Example
9.8
that for all
k\geq 1,\ m\geq 0

\zeta_{q}(k,\{0\}^{m})=-\delta_{m,0}\frac{B_{k}}{2k!}-\delta_{k,1}\frac{B_{m+1}}{2(m+1)!}+\frac{1}{(k-1)!m!}\sum_{u,v>0}u^{m}v^{k-1}q^{uv}.

So, the element
\zeta_{q}(k,\{0\}^{m})
is essentially equal to the
m
-th derivative of the Eisenstein series
G_{k}
. If
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
, then the balanced multiple q-zeta value
\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
equals the combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series
G(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
. In particular, the balanced multiple q-zeta values give a very natural extension and explicit description of conjectural all relations between multiple Eisenstein series.

2. Recall that the mould
\mathfrak{b}
in depth
2
is given by
\mathfrak{b}(X_{1},X_{2})=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1}\beta_{\ast}(k_{1},k_{2})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}
(compare to (

)). Direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(2,3)

\displaystyle=\beta_{\ast}(2,3)-\frac{1}{48}\sum_{u,v>0}v^{2}q^{uv}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}>u_{2}>0\\
v_{1},v_{2}>0\end{subarray}}v_{1}v_{2}^{2}q^{u_{1}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2}},

\displaystyle\zeta(2,0,3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}>u_{2}>0\\
v_{1},v_{2}>0\end{subarray}}u_{1}v_{1}v_{2}^{2}q^{u_{1}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{1}>u_{2}>0\\
v_{1},v_{2}>0\end{subarray}}u_{2}v_{1}v_{2}^{2}q^{u_{1}v_{1}+u_{2}v_{2}}.

An explicit construction for the numbers
\beta_{\ast}(k_{1},k_{2})
is given in
[
GKZ06
, Section 6]
, in this case one obtains
\beta_{\ast}(2,3)=0
. Further constructions for these rational numbers are given in
[

]
,
[
Ec02
]
.

3. The quasi-modular forms are contained in the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. In particular, the modular discriminant
\Delta(q)=q\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{n})^{24}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of balanced multiple q-zeta values. For example, using the exotic relation given in

we obtain

\displaystyle\ 240\zeta_{q}(4,4,4)-63\zeta_{q}(9,3)+183\zeta_{q}(8,4)-\frac{675}{2}\zeta_{q}(7,5)+\frac{89}{2}\zeta_{q}(6,6)-378\zeta_{q}(5,7)

\displaystyle+183\zeta_{q}(4,8).

11.
Further properties of the balanced multiple
\operatorname{q}
-zeta values

Similar to the case of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple q-zeta values studied by K. Ebrahimi-Fard, D. Manchon, and J. Singer (
[
Si15
]
,
[
EMS16
]
), the balanced multiple q-zeta values possess a description in terms of an alphabet with two letters. We will compute the limits
q\to 1
of a certain kind of balanced multiple q-zeta values and obtain that those are elements in the algebra of multiple zeta values. Finally, we will describe the derivation
q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}
on the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
in terms of the balanced multiple q-zeta values.

Definition 11.1
.

Let
\shuffle_{b}
be the product on the non-commutative free algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle
recursively defined by
\mathbf{1}\shuffle_{b}w=w\shuffle_{b}\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle(yu)\shuffle_{b}v

\displaystyle=u\shuffle_{b}(yv)=y(u\shuffle_{b}v),

\displaystyle(pu)\shuffle_{b}(pv)

\displaystyle=p(u\shuffle_{b}pv)+p(pu\shuffle_{b}v)+\begin{cases}p(u\shuffle_{b}v),&\text{ if }u=y\tilde{u}\text{ and }v=y\tilde{v},\\
0&\text{ else}\end{cases}

for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle
.

The involution
\tau
(Definition
7.7
) can be also defined on the algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle
.

Definition 11.2
.

Let
\tau
be the anti-automorphism on
\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle
given by
\tau(\mathbf{1})=\mathbf{1}
,
\tau(p)=y
, and
\tau(y)=p
, i.e., one has for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{0},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0

\displaystyle\tau(y^{m_{0}}p^{k_{1}}y^{m_{1}}\dots p^{k_{d}}y^{m_{d}})=p^{m_{d}}y^{k_{d}}\dots p^{m_{1}}y^{k_{1}}p^{m_{0}}.

The involution
\tau
connects the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
and the product
\shuffle_{b}
. To make this precise, consider the canonical embedding

\displaystyle i:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle,

\displaystyle b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}

\displaystyle\mapsto p^{s_{1}}y\dots p^{s_{l}}y.

Observe that we have
\tau(i(w))=i(\tau(w))
for all
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
.

Proposition 11.3
.

For all
u,v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
, we have

i(u\ast_{b}v)=\tau(\tau\circ i(u)\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(v)).

Proof.

Let
u=b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}
and
v=b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}}
be words in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
and
s_{l},r_{k}\geq 1
. Using the recursive definition of
\ast_{b}
from the right, we obtain

\displaystyle i(u\ast_{b}v)

\displaystyle=i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l-1}}\ast_{b}b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}})p^{s_{l}}y+i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}\ast_{b}b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k-1}})p^{r_{k}}y

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+i(b_{s_{2}}\dots b_{s_{l}}\ast_{b}b_{r_{2}}\dots b_{r_{k}})p^{s_{l}+r_{k}}y.

On the other hand, applying the definition of
\tau
and
i
gives

\displaystyle\tau(\tau\circ i(u)\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(v))=\tau(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(py^{s_{l}}(py^{s_{l-1}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})+py^{r_{k}}(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k-1}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+py^{s_{l}+r_{k}}(py^{s_{l-1}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k-1}}\dots py^{r_{1}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l-1}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}})\Big)p^{s_{l}}y+\tau\Big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k-1}})\Big)p^{r_{k}}y

\displaystyle\hskip 9.95863pt+\tau\Big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l-1}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k-1}})\Big)p^{s_{l}+r_{k}}y.

So induction on the depth implies the claim. Next, assume that
s_{l}=0
and
r_{k}\geq 0
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle i(u\ast_{b}v)

\displaystyle=i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l-1}}\ast_{b}b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}})y+i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}\ast_{b}b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k-1}})p^{r_{k}}y,

and on the other hand, using again the definition of
\tau
and
i

\displaystyle\tau(\tau\circ i(u)\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(v))=\tau(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(p(py^{s_{l-1}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})+py^{r_{k}}(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k-1}}\dots py^{r_{1}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l-1}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}})\Big)y+\tau\Big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k-1}})\Big)p^{r_{k}}y.

Again, induction on the depth implies the claim.
∎

By Proposition
11.3
, there is injective algebra morphism

\displaystyle\tau\circ i:(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle,\shuffle_{b}),

\displaystyle b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}

\displaystyle\mapsto py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}}.

In particular, the restriction of
\shuffle_{b}
to
\operatorname{im}(\tau\circ i)=\mathbb{Q}\mathbf{1}+p\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle
can be seen as a quasi-shuffle product. Denote

\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle^{0}=\mathbb{Q}\mathbf{1}+p\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle y.

Theorem 11.4
.

There is a
\tau
-invariant, surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle p,y\rangle^{0},\shuffle_{b})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{Z}_{q},

\displaystyle p^{s_{1}}y\dots p^{s_{l}}y

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}).

Proof.

Observe that by definition

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(i(u))=\zeta_{q}(u)\quad\text{ for all }u\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}.

(11.1)

So as the balanced multiple q-zeta values form a spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, we obtain surjectivity. Since
\tau(i(u))=i(\tau(u))
for each
u\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
, we deduce the
\tau
-invariance from Theorem
10.4
. Finally, we prove that the map is an algebra morphism for
\shuffle_{b}
. For
s_{1},r_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l},r_{2},\ldots,r_{k}\geq 0
, we obtain by applying the
\tau
-invariance and (
11.1
)

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}})\zeta_{q}(py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{q}(p^{s_{1}}y\dots p^{s_{l}}y)\zeta_{q}(p^{r_{1}}y\dots p^{r_{k}}y)=\zeta_{q}(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})\zeta_{q}(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}}).

Since
\zeta_{q}
is an algebra morphism for the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}})\zeta_{q}(py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{q}(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}}\ast_{b}b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}}).

Applying Proposition
11.3
and then again the
\tau
-invariance gives

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}})\zeta_{q}(py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{q}\big(\tau\big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}})\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{q}\big(\tau\circ i(b_{s_{1}}\dots b_{s_{l}})\shuffle_{b}\tau\circ i(b_{r_{1}}\dots b_{r_{k}})\big).

Finally, applying the definition of
\tau
and
i
yields the desired formula

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}})\zeta_{q}(py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{q}\big(py^{s_{l}}\dots py^{s_{1}}\shuffle_{b}py^{r_{k}}\dots py^{r_{1}}\big).

∎

Next, we will compute the limit of the balanced multiple q-zeta values for
q\to 1
for some special multi indices.

Proposition 11.5
.

For a word
w=b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}}b_{k_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}
in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
, where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{0,1\}
,
\varepsilon_{n}=0
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
k_{1}\geq 2
, we have

\lim_{q\to 1}(1-q)^{\operatorname{wt}(w)}\zeta_{q}(w)=\zeta(x_{\varepsilon_{n}}\dots x_{\varepsilon_{1}})\zeta(y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}}).

Here we set as explained in (
1.2
), (
1.4
),

\displaystyle\zeta(x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\dots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),\qquad\qquad\zeta(y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),

for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
k_{1}\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{z}=(\mathfrak{z}_{d})_{d\geq 0}
be the mould of the multiple zeta values, so for each
d\geq 1
we have

\mathfrak{z}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})=\sum_{k_{1}\geq 2,k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1}\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}.

Similar to
[
BI22
, Definition 1.3]
define the conjugated multiple zeta values
\xi(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d-1}\geq 0,\ m_{d}\geq 1
, by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d},\ldots,Y_{1}+Y_{2},Y_{1})=\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d-1}\geq 0,m_{d}\geq 1}\xi(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})\frac{Y_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}!}\dots\frac{Y_{d}^{m_{d}}}{m_{d}!}.

(11.2)

By
[
BI22
, Theorem 4.18]
and
[
BB22
, Remark 6.18]
, the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series satisfy

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}(1-q)^{m_{1}+\dots+m_{j}+j+k_{j+1}+\dots+k_{d}}G\binom{\hskip 8.5359pt1,\ldots,1,\hskip 5.69046ptk_{j+1},\ldots,k_{d}}{\hskip-14.22636ptm_{1},\ldots,m_{j},\hskip 5.69046pt0,\ldots,0}=\xi(m_{1},\ldots,m_{j})\zeta(k_{j+1},\ldots,k_{d})

for all
1\leq j\leq d
. We deduce from the definition of the balanced multiple q-zeta values (Definition
10.1
) that we have for
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{0,1\}
,
\varepsilon_{n}=0
and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
k_{1}\geq 2

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}(1-q)^{n+k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}}\zeta_{q}(b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}}b_{k_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\to 1}(1-q)^{n+k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}}G(\varphi_{\#}^{-1}(b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}}b_{k_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}}))

\displaystyle=\xi(\varphi_{\#}^{-1}(b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}}))\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}).

(11.3)

Here
\xi(\varphi_{\#}^{-1}(b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}}))
means that we first have to apply
\varphi_{\#}
to
b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}}
, which gives an element in
\mathbb{Q}\langle y_{1,m}\mid m\geq 0\rangle
, and then we have to identify
\xi(y_{1,m_{1}}\dots y_{1,m_{d}})=\xi(m_{1},\ldots,m_{d})
. On the level of generating series, this means we have to apply
\#_{Y}^{-1}
(Definition
7.2
), therefore we substitute
Y_{1}\mapsto Y_{1},\ Y_{2}\mapsto Y_{2}-Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}\mapsto Y_{d}-Y_{d-1}
in the left-hand side of (
11.2
) and obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}(Y_{d},Y_{d-1},\ldots,Y_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{d-1}\geq 1,m_{d}\geq 2}\zeta(m_{d},\ldots,m_{1})Y_{1}^{m_{1}-1}\dots Y_{d}^{m_{d}-1}.

(11.4)

Combining (
11
) and (
11.4
) gives the desired formula.
∎

Remark 11.6
.

Similar to
[
BI22
, Definition 4.17]
, one could define a regularized limit for the balanced multiple q-zeta values by a certain regularization process in the algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
and obtains for each word
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle

\lim_{q\to 1}\textbf{}\!^{*}(1-q)^{\operatorname{wt}(w)}\zeta_{q}(w)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}uv=w\\
u=b_{\varepsilon_{1}}\dots b_{\varepsilon_{n}},\ \varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\}\\
v=b_{k_{1}}\dots b_{k_{d}},\ k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\zeta^{\shuffle}(x_{\varepsilon_{n}}\dots x_{\varepsilon_{1}})\zeta^{\ast}(y_{k_{1}}\dots y_{k_{d}}).

Here
\zeta^{\shuffle}
and
\zeta^{\ast}
denote the shuffle and stuffle regularized multiple zeta value map as given in (

), (
1.4
). In particular, the regularized limit of the balanced multiple q-zeta value
\zeta_{q}(w)
vanishes whenever the word

cannot be decomposed as

with

and

.

Finally, we want to describe the derivation
q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}
on
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
in terms of the balanced multiple q-zeta values. The obtained formula can be seen as a weight-graded version of the derivation formula for the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple q-zeta values given in
[
Si15
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Proposition 11.7
.

We have for all
s_{1}\geq 1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
that

\displaystyle q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{j=i}^{l}s_{i}\zeta_{q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}+1,\ldots,s_{j},0,s_{j+1},\ldots,s_{l}).

Observe that the derivation
q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}
is a homogeneous operator increasing the weight by
2
.

Proof.

By
[
BB22
, Proposition 6.29]
the generating series of the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series satisfy

\displaystyle q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}\mathfrak{G}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{i}}\mathfrak{G}_{d}\binom{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}}{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d}},\qquad d\geq 1.

By definition of the balanced multiple q-zeta values (Definition
10.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})^{m_{2}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d})^{m_{d}}

(11.5)

must be equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}})\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{i}}X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})^{m_{2}}

(11.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 341.43306pt\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d})^{m_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\geq 1\\
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=i}^{d}(k_{i}-1)m_{j}\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}})X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})^{m_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 65.44142pt\dots X_{i}^{k_{i}-2}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{i})^{m_{i}}\dots X_{j}^{k_{j}-1}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{j})^{m_{j}-1}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d})^{m_{d}}.

Coefficient comparison at
X_{1}^{k_{1}-1}Y_{1}^{m_{1}}X_{2}^{k_{2}-1}(Y_{1}+Y_{2})^{m_{2}}\dots X_{d}^{k_{d}-1}(Y_{1}+\dots+Y_{d})^{m_{d}}
in (
11.5
) and (
11.6
) yields

\displaystyle q\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}q}\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=i}^{d}k_{i}(m_{j}+1)\zeta_{q}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{i}+1,\{0\}^{m_{i}},\ldots,k_{j},\{0\}^{m_{j}+1},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}}),

which is equivalent to the claimed formula.
∎

Outlook.

In forthcoming articles we will formalize the balanced multiple q-zeta values and obtain an algebra generated by symbols satisfying the balanced quasi-shuffle product and
\tau
-invariance. Conjecturally, this algebra is equipped with a Hopf algebra structure where the coproduct is given by a generalization of Goncharov’s coproduct. A first step towards this is a Lie algebra structure obtained in
[
Bu23
]
. Moreover, this algebra of symbols should give a complete description of all relations in the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
and by Theorem
7.10
also a description of all relations between multiple Eisenstein series. By applying Racinet’s ideas for formal multiple zeta values (
[
Rac00
]
) to this new algebra, we will obtain a formal version of the limit computation given in Proposition
11.5
. By
[
BI22
]
this map could be also seen as a formal version of taking the constant term of q-series.

Introduction.
Suppose that

p>2
is prime,

F
is a field
containing a primitive
p^{2}
th root of unity, and suppose

where these new elements give rise to indecomposible division algebras of
exponent
p
and index
p^{2}
(see [T] or [TW] as well as [J], [AJ1],[AJO1],[AJO2],[AJO3] for related work.)
In this paper we give a four-term sequence of Galois modules

0\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{3}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{4}\rightarrow 0,

each of which are free as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules and for which homotopies
h_{i}

giving multiplication by
p
are constructed. Passing to

\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and

a characterization of the kernel

.
Similar applications of [P] can be found in
[AJ2], [AJ3], [AJO1],[AJO2] and [S].

When
p=3
further results are obtained. Using the same four-term sequence
one obtains a six term exact sequence of Galois cohomology groups that
more thoroughly describe the cohomological kernels. These in turn
are used to characterize the Brauer Kernels as well as the
quotient group

are explicitly described as

-cocyle classes where

is measured by

H^{1}(F,\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
. We expect these results to be true for
odd
p>3
but more work is needed. The complexity is the computation of
certain Bochstein maps that is possible when
p=3
because of
the simpler structure of
\mathcal{M}_{4}
in that case.

The paper is organized as follows.

-modules

homotopies for which the theory developed

In order to apply the Positelski theory, it is necessary that the

Although this is clear for

\mathcal{M}_{2}

The paper uses essentially no field theory and consists of computations within

With these assumptions, most of the discussion in the paper deals with group cohomology, although

{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}:=X^{n-1}/\left(N^{n-1}_{3}+N^{n-1}_{4}\right)
.

, where it could have made sense to label

\chi
as
w_{1\,1}
except that we want to emphasize the role of
the cocycle
\chi
as it relates to Sections 3 and 4. This means that
in terms of field theory, the subquotient
{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}
of
H^{n-1}(F,\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
could
be reformulated in terms of
H^{n-1}(E,\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
where
E/F

3

\chi

As a first result we see how the conditions on
\chi\in X^{n-1}

can be used to give elements

H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})

Lemma 5.2.

Suppose
\chi,w_{2\,0},w_{0\,2}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
,

\delta(w_{2\,1})=-i\chi^{\prime}-jw_{2\,0}^{\prime}
and
\delta(w_{1\,2})=-j\chi^{\prime}-iw_{0\,2}^{\prime}.

We define
\alpha\in C^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
by

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle iw_{1\,2}^{\prime}+jw_{2\,1}^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{i}w_{0\,2}^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{j}w_{2\,0}^{\prime}+ij\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i(w_{1\,2}-\chi)^{\prime}+j(w_{2\,1}-\chi)^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{i}(w_{0\,2}-\chi)^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{j}(w_{2\,0}-\chi)^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{-(i+j)}\chi^{\prime}.

Then
\alpha\in Z^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})

and moreover, if
[c]\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{4}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
, and if
\pi_{4}^{*}([c])=\chi
, then
we can express
\eta([c])=[\alpha]\in H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
in the above form.

Proof.
We have
\delta(ij\chi^{\prime})=-(ij^{\prime}+ji^{\prime})\chi^{\prime\prime}
,

\delta(iw_{2\,1}^{\prime})=-i(-j^{\prime}\chi^{\prime\prime}-i^{\prime}w_{0\,2}^{\prime\prime})
,

\delta(\lambda^{2}_{i}w_{0\,2}^{\prime})=-ii^{\prime}w_{0\,2}^{\prime\prime}
,

\delta(jw_{2\,1}^{\prime})=-j(-i^{\prime}\chi^{\prime\prime}-j^{\prime}w_{2\,0}^{\prime\prime})
,

\delta(\lambda^{2}_{j}w_{2\,0}^{\prime})=-jj^{\prime}w_{2\,0}^{\prime\prime}
. Adding shows
that the first expression representing
\alpha
is a cocycle.
The second expression follows from the first using

\lambda^{2}_{-(i+j)}=ij+i+j+\lambda^{2}_{i}+\lambda^{2}_{j}
.

For the second statement, Theorem 3.12 shows if

\pi_{4}^{*}([c])=\chi
then

\eta([c])=[(iy_{1}^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{i}\chi_{3}^{\prime})+(jy_{2}^{\prime}+\lambda^{2}_{j}\chi_{4}^{\prime})+\lambda^{2}_{\psi}\chi^{\prime}].

where
\chi_{3},\chi_{4}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
,

y_{1},y_{2}\in C^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})

and the following equations are true

\displaystyle\delta(y_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\chi_{3}^{\prime}+\psi\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\delta(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j\chi_{4}^{\prime}+\psi\chi^{\prime}

This gives the result.
\Box

We next give a lemma that shows that the
N_{i}
are “norms”.

Lemma 5.3.

Suppose that
\chi\in N^{n-1}_{3}
. Then there exist
n_{3\,0}
,
n_{3\,1}
,
n_{3\,2}\in C^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
with

n=(n_{3\,0}+n_{3\,1}t_{1}+n_{3\,2}t^{2}_{1})T_{3}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]\cdot T_{3})
and
with
\chi=n_{3\,0}
. The analogous result holds for
\chi\in N^{n-1}_{4}
.

Proof.
Setting
\chi=n_{3\,0}
, as
\chi\in N^{n-1}_{3}
, there exists

n_{3\,1}\in C^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
with
\delta(n_{3\,1})=-\psi_{3}n^{\prime}_{3\,0}
.
By
Lemma 3.6, modifying
n_{3\,1}
by an element of
H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
as needed, there exists
n_{3\,2}
with
\delta(n_{3\,2})=-\psi_{3}n^{\prime}_{3\,1}-\lambda^{2}_{\psi_{3}}n^{\prime}_{3\,0}
. It follows that

nT_{3}=(n_{3\,0}+n_{3\,1}t_{1}+n_{3\,2}t^{2}_{1})T_{3}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]T_{3})

as required. The case of
\chi\in N_{4}^{n-1}
is entirely analogous.
\Box

Remark 5.4.
The previous lemma gives a computational
version of Shapiro’s lemma to produce an element of
H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]\cdot T_{3})

with “norm”
\chi
when interpreted in the case of field theory.

Now a lemma that allows us to utilize the map
\pi_{4}
.

Lemma 5.5.
Suppose
\alpha\in{\rm ker}(H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{2}))
. The map given by
\alpha\mapsto\overline{\pi}_{4}(\alpha):=[{\pi_{4}^{*}([c])}]\in{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}=X^{n-1}/(N^{n-1}_{3}+N^{n-1}_{4})
where
\alpha=\eta([c])
for

c\in Z^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{4})
is well-defined.

Proof.
Since
\mathcal{M}_{4}={\rm cok}(\mathcal{M}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{3})
we
represent
[c]
by
c\in C^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{3})
. By the Positselski sequence in Theorem 4.7

{\rm ker}(\eta)={\rm im}(d_{3}^{*})
,
so that if
\eta([c^{\prime}])=\eta([c])
then
c-c^{\prime}\in Z^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{3})
. Writing
c-c^{\prime}=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}T_{3},c_{4}T_{4})
so

c_{3}T_{3}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]T_{3})
,
c_{4}T_{4}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]\cdot T_{4})
we find that

\pi^{*}_{4}([c-c^{\prime}])=[(c_{3}-c_{4})T_{G}]\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]\cdot T_{G})
.
By Lemma 5.3 we can express
c_{3}T_{3}=(c_{3\,0}+c_{3\,1}t_{1}+c_{3\,2}t_{1}^{2})\cdot T_{3}

and
c_{4}T_{4}=(c_{4\,0}+c_{4\,1}t_{1}+c_{4\,2}t_{1}^{2})T_{4}
with

\delta(c_{3}T_{3})=0
and
\delta(c_{4}T_{4})=0
. But then we have

\delta(c_{3\,0})=0
,
\delta(c_{3\,1})+\psi_{3}c_{3\,0}^{\prime}=0
,

\delta(c_{4\,0})=0
and
\delta(c_{4\,1})+\psi_{4}c_{4\,0}^{\prime}=0
. From this

c_{3\,0},c_{4\,0}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
and

[-\psi_{3}c_{3\,0}^{\prime}]=[-\psi_{4}c_{4\,0}^{\prime}]=0\in H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
. These latter
conditions show that
c_{3\,0}\in N_{3}
and
c_{4\,0}\in N_{4}
.
The well-definition of
\overline{\pi}_{4}([c])\in{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}
follows.

\Box

Theorem 5.6.

Suppose
\alpha\in{\rm ker}(H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{2}))
. Then
\eta(\alpha)\in{\rm Dec}^{n}(E/F)
if an only if

\overline{\pi}_{4}(\alpha)=0\in{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}
, that is,

\left({\rm ker}(H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{n}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{2}))\right)/{\rm Dec}^{n}(\mathcal{G},\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\cong{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}
.
In particular, for the
3
-
3
extension

E/F
,

{\rm Br}_{3}(E/F)/{\rm Dec}(E/F)\cong{\mathcal{O}}^{1}\ .

Proof.
First we assume
\overline{\pi}_{4}^{*}(\alpha)=0\in{\mathcal{O}}^{n-1}
, that
is
\pi_{4}^{*}(\alpha)=n_{3\,0}-n_{4\,0}
where for
i=3,4
,
n_{i\,0}\in N_{i}\subseteq H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
. By Lemma 5.5 we have

n_{i\,1},n_{i\,2}\in C^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
with

n_{i}T_{i}=(n_{i\,0}+n_{i\,1}t_{1}+n_{i\,2}t_{1}^{2})T_{i}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}[G]T_{i})
.
It follows that
n:=(0,0,n_{3}T_{3},n_{4}T_{4})\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{3})
and that also

\pi_{4}^{*}([n])=n_{3\,0}-n_{4\,0}
.
But then
\pi^{*}_{4}(\alpha-[n])=0\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
and
moreover, as
\delta(n_{i\,1})=-\psi_{i}n_{i,0}^{\prime}
we
see by
Theorem 3.14 (iii) that
\eta([n])\in{\rm Dec}^{n}(E/F)
.
Next by
Theorem 3.14 (iii) again, as
\pi_{4}^{*}(\alpha-[n])=0
we find
\eta(\alpha-[n])\in{\rm Dec}^{n}(E/F)
and therefore

\eta(\alpha)=\eta(\alpha-[n])+\eta([n])\in{\rm Dec}^{n}(E/F)
.

Converelsly, we assume
\eta(\alpha)\in{\rm Dec}^{n}(E/F)
. This means we have

\eta(\alpha)=i\chi^{\prime}_{3}+j\chi^{\prime}_{4}=ij\cdot 0^{\prime}+i\chi^{\prime}_{3}+j\chi^{\prime}_{4}+\lambda^{2}_{i}\cdot 0+\lambda^{2}_{j}\cdot 0\in H^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
for
\chi_{3},\chi_{4}\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
.
But then by computation of
\eta
in 3.13 we have that

[(\chi_{3}t_{2}^{2},\chi_{4}t_{1}^{2},0,0)]\in H^{n-1}(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{M}_{4}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
with

\eta([(\chi_{3}t_{2}^{2},\chi_{4}t_{1}^{2},0,0)])=[i\chi_{3}^{\prime}+j\chi_{4}^{\prime}]
. Then
by the well-definition of
\overline{\pi}_{4}^{*}
,

\overline{\pi}_{4}^{*}([\alpha])=\overline{\pi}_{4}^{*}([(\chi_{3}t_{2}^{2},\chi_{4}t_{1}^{2},0,0)])=0
. So we see that
\overline{\pi}_{4}^{*}([\alpha])=0
if and only if

\eta(\alpha)\in{\rm Dec}^{n}(E/F)
.

\Box

Tolerance relations and operator systems
Alain Connes, Walter D. van Suijlekom

Abstract
. We extend the scope of noncommutative geometry by generalizing the construction of the noncommutative algebra of a quotient space to situations in which one is no longer dealing with an equivalence relation. For these so-called tolerance relations, passing to the associated equivalence relation looses crucial information as is clear from the examples such as coarse graining in physics or the relation d ⁡ ( x , y ) < ε d(x,y)<\varepsilon on a metric space. Fortunately, thanks to the formalism of operator systems such an extension is possible and provides new invariants, such as the C ∗ C^{*} -envelope and the propagation number. After a thorough investigation of the structure of the (non-unital) operator systems associated to tolerance relations, we analyze the corresponding state spaces. In particular, we determine the pure state space associated to the operator system for the relation d ⁡ ( x , y ) < ε d(x,y)<\varepsilon on a path metric measure space.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we take the next step in the development of noncommutative geometry based on operator systems. While in
[
5
]
we considered operator systems based on spectral truncations, we will now focus on operator systems associated to tolerance relations.

A strong motivation for studying such systems comes from the idea of coarse graining in physics, which may be formulated in phase space or in configuration space. Moreover,
in our understanding of the geometry of spacetime we are limited by the resolution of our measuring device: we can only determine the underlying metric space
up to a finite resolution
. In fact, on purely theoretical grounds one expects our understanding of this geometry to break down at a fundamental scale, given by the Planck length
\ell_{P}=\sqrt{{\hbar G}/{c^{3}}}
.

From a mathematical viewpoint this leads us to consider metric spaces
(X,d)
equipped with the relation
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
for some fixed parameter
\varepsilon
. This is not an equivalence relation as it lacks transitivity, but it is a so-called
tolerance relation
, which is by definition a reflexive and symmetric relation. This notion can be traced back to Poincaré in his
Science and Hypothesis
(though the name was coined in
[
34
]
). In fact, Poincaré argued that in the physical continuum (in contrast with the mathematical continuum) it can hold that for measured quantities one has
A=B
,
B=C
while
A\neq C
due to potentially added measurement errors (see
[
32
]
for a development of the mathematical theory).

Given such a tolerance relation we may imitate the construction of a groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra with the crucial difference that the convolution product cannot be defined due to the lack of transitivity. However, given the symmetry and reflexivity, a tolerance relation does define an operator system. Naturally, this operator system may be formulated in the general context of locally compact groupoids equipped with a Haar system
[
28
]
with the special case of the tolerance relation arising when we consider the pair groupoid on a locally compact topological measure space. It creates a generalization of the basic construction of noncommutative geometry which started from analyzing the geometric examples of intractable spaces of leaves of foliations using noncommutative algebras. Here the issue of the lack of transitivity of the relation is already present in the simplest case where the generated equivalence relation has a single class
i.e.
corresponds to the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators. The main question is then how much of the metric structure of
(X,d)
can be determined from the operator system associated to the relation
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
.

Another natural appearance of tolerance relations occurs in combinatorial topology. The homotopy groups of the geometric realization of a Kan complex may be computed combinatorially. This no longer holds for general simplicial complexes and one motivation for considering tolerance relations comes from the natural examples of “sets” corresponding to a finer notion of the set of components of a simplicial complex
X
which is not a Kan complex. Typically the relation among two vertices
x,y\in X(0)
given by the existence of an edge
e\in X(1)
whose end points are
x,y
is a tolerance relation which only uses the graph underlying the simplicial complex in degrees
0,1
. On the one hand replacing the relation by the equivalence relation that it generates and then passing to the quotient looses most of the relevant information. On the other hand one would like to perform this operation of quotient for factor-relations which are equivalence relations. It is this issue which is solved by the construction of the associated operator system and the notion of Morita equivalence of operator systems which was developed recently in
[
9
]
.

A concrete example of this situation occurs in the theory of divisors on the Arakelov compactification
\overline{{\rm Spec}\;\mathbb{Z}}
(see
[
6
]
). After applying the Dold–Kan correspondence to a suitable morphism of
S
-modules, one obtains that the cohomology
H^{1}(\overline{{\rm Spec}\;\mathbb{Z}},D)
should be described by the tolerance relation given by the circle
S^{1}
endowed with the relation

\mathcal{R}:=\{(x,y)\mid d(x,y)<\varepsilon\}

where
\varepsilon=\exp(\deg D)
becomes small when the degree
\deg D
of the divisor is negative.

This paper is organized as follows. We start in Section
2
by reviewing the theory of non-unital operator systems, due to Werner
[
33
]
, as well as the notions of
C^{*}
-envelopes and propagation number as developed previously in
[
5
]
. Under the assumption that the operator system
E
is a closed
*
-subspace of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
equipped with a suitable approximate order unit (in the sense of
[
24
]
), we prove a generalization of the Jordan decomposition for hermitian functionals on
E
(Theorem
2.15
). This then allows us to characterize the Banach space dual of
E
in terms of the dual of
A
, preparing for an analysis of the corresponding state space.

In Section
3
we define operator systems associated to relations. We work in the slightly more general, but natural context of locally compact groupoids equipped with a Haar system, and associate operator systems to open symmetric subsets (so-called
bonds
, see Definition
3.2
). After discussing some of their general properties, we analyze operator systems associated to tolerance relations on finite sets, and determine their
C^{*}
-envelopes, propagation numbers and pure state space.

Finally, in Section
4
we come to consider the operator system associated to the relation
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
on a path metric space
X
. In addition to invariants such as the
C^{*}
-envelope and the propagation number (Theorem
4.11
), we determine the pure state space of this operator system to consist of those vector states whose support is
\varepsilon
-connected (Theorem
4.18
).

2.
Preliminaries on non-unital operator system and state spaces

We start by recalling and developing some general theory for non-unital operator systems, approximate order units and state spaces.

2.1.
Non-unital operator systems

Werner introduced non-unital operator system in
[
33
]
. Let us give the relevant definitions, referring to
loc.cit.
and also
[
5
]
for more details and references.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a
*
-vector space
E
is matrix ordered if

(1)

for each
n
we are given a cone of positive elements
M_{n}(E)_{+}
in
M_{n}(E)_{h}
,

(2)

M_{n}(E)_{+}\cap(-M_{n}(E)_{+})=\{0\}
for all
n
,

(3)

for every
m,n
and
A\in M_{mn}(\mathbb{C})
we have that
AM_{n}(E)_{+}A^{*}\subseteq M_{m}(E)_{+}
.

For
matrix-ordered operator spaces
one further requires that
E
is an operator space, with the two properties that

(1)

the cones
M_{n}(E)_{+}
are all closed, and,

(2)

the involution is an isometry on
M_{n}(E)
.

The
modified numerical radius

\nu_{E}
is defined for any
x\in M_{n}(E)
by

\nu_{E}(x)=\sup\left\{\left|\varphi\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\right|:\varphi\in M_{2n}(E)^{*}_{+},\|\varphi\|\leq 1\right\}.

Note that
M_{2n}(E)^{*}
is the Banach space dual of
M_{2n}(E)
, in contrast to
M_{2n}(E^{*})=\mathrm{CB}(E,M_{2n})
which comes naturally equipped with the
\mathrm{cb}
-norm. The supremum is thus taken over all noncommutative quasi-states of
E
,
cf.
Equation (
2
) below.

One can show that
\nu_{E}
is a norm and, moreover, we have
\nu_{E}(\cdot)\leq\|\cdot\|
. Werner then gives the following definition in
[
33
]
(where we prefer as in
[
5
]
to restrict to identical, rather than equivalent norms):

Definition 2.2
.

A non-unital operator system is given by a matrix-ordered operator space
E
for which the norm
\nu_{E}(\cdot)
coincides with the norm
\|\cdot\|
.

Werner then constructs a unitization for arbitrary matrix-ordered operator spaces as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E
be a matrix-ordered operator space and define
a_{\varepsilon}=a+\varepsilon\mathbb{I}_{n}
for every matrix
a\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. On the space
E\oplus\mathbb{C}
we define

(1)

(x,a)^{*}=(x^{*},a^{*})
for all
(x,a)\in M_{n}(E)\oplus M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

(2)

for any
(x,a)\in M_{n}(E\oplus\mathbb{C})_{h}
we set

(x,a)\geq 0\qquad\text{iff }\quad a\geq 0\text{ and }\varphi(a_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2}xa_{\varepsilon}^{-1/2})\geq-1

for all
\varepsilon>0
and
\varphi\in M_{n}(E)^{*}_{+}
with
\|\varphi\|=1
.

We denote by
E^{\sharp}
the space
E\oplus\mathbb{C}
equipped with this order structure.

The main conclusion from
[
33
]
is then that
E^{\sharp}
is a unital operator system and that
E
is a non-unital operator system iff the embedding
\imath:E\to E^{\sharp}
is a complete isometry. For a convex geometric description of operator systems and their unitization we refer to
[
7
,
19
]
. The following result is a special case of
[
33
, Proposition 4.16(a)]

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E\subseteq A
be a closed
*
-subspace in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Then
E
is a non-unital operator system.

Proof.

Take
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in M_{2n}(E)
; this is a self-adjoint element in
M_{2n}(A)
. Hence there exists a state
\widetilde{\varphi}
on the
C^{*}
-algebra
M_{2n}(A)
such that
\left|\widetilde{\varphi}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)\right|=\|x\|
. The restriction
\varphi
of this state to
M_{2n}(E)
is a quasi-state and hence
\nu_{E}(x)\geq|\varphi(x)|=\|x\|
.
∎

In
[
5
]
we introduced the notion of
propagation number
of an operator system, a measure for the extent to which the operator system is ‘away’ from a
C^{*}
-algebra. Let us start by briefly recalling the notion of the
C^{*}
-envelope
[
1
,
12
]

Definition 2.5
.

(1)

A
C^{*}
-extension
\kappa:E\to A
of a unital operator system
E
is given by a complete order isomorphism onto
\kappa(E)\subseteq A
such that
C^{*}(\kappa(E))=A
.

(2)

A
C^{*}
-envelope of a unital operator system is a
C^{*}
-extension
\kappa:E\to A
with the following universal property:

\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\kappa}
\scriptstyle{\lambda}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists!\rho}
\textstyle{B}

Such a universal object was shown to exist in the unital case in
[
12
]
while for the non-unital case we refer to
[
5
]
and references therein. We write
\imath_{E}:E\to C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E)
for the canonical complete order injection into the
C^{*}
-envelope.

Given an operator system
E
and integer
n>0
let us write
E^{\circ n}
for the norm closure of the linear span of products of
\leq n
elements of
E
. It is an operator system contained in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(E)
.

Definition 2.6
.

The propagation number
{\rm prop}(E)
of the operator system
E
is defined as the smallest integer
n
such that
\imath_{E}(E)^{\circ n}\subseteq C_{\rm env}^{*}(E)
is a
C^{*}
-algebra.

When no such
n
exists one lets
{\rm prop}(E)=\infty
. In
[
5
, Proposition 2.42]
we showed that the propagation number is invariant under stable equivalence of operator systems. Recently, also a notion of Morita equivalence for (unital) operator systems has been introduced
[
9
]
and shown to coincide with stable equivalence (see Theorem 3.8 in
loc.cit.
). We may thus conclude that the propagation number is also invariant under Morita equivalence. Other properties of the propagation number include compatibility with tensor products. Namely, in
[
21
]
it is shown that for unital operator systems
E
and
F
we have
{\rm prop}(E\otimes_{\text{min}}F)=\max\{{\rm prop}(E),{\rm prop}(F)\}
.

2.2.
Matrix-regular operator spaces and approximate order units

We now introduce a certain degree of regularity on matrix-ordered operator spaces, which relates the matrix norms and the matrix order, in fact yielding a special class of non-unital operator systems as considered above. A key concept will be that of an approximate order unit due to Ng
[
24
]
, but let us start with the following, more general notion introduced by Schreiner in
[
31
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

A matrix-ordered operator space
E
is called a matrix-regular operator space if for all
x\in M_{n}(E)_{h}
the following two conditions are satisfied

(1)

if
y\in M_{n}(E)_{h},-y\leq x\leq y
then
\|x\|\leq\|y\|
;

(2)

if
\|x\|<1
then there exists
y\in M_{n}(E)_{h}
such that
\|y\|\leq 1
and
-y\leq x\leq y
.

Note the equivalent characterization of matrix-regular operator spaces
[
31
, Theorem 3.4]
: for all
x\in M_{n}(E)
,
\|x\|<1
if and only if there exist
a,d\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
,
\|a\|,\|d\|<1
such that

\begin{pmatrix}a&x\\
x^{*}&d\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2n}(E)_{+}.

Schreiner establishes in
[
31
, Corollary 4.7]
that the operator space dual of a matrix-regular operator space is also matrix regular. Moreover, Karn shows in
[
15
,
17
]
(
cf.

[
13
, Theorem 1]
) that matrix-regular operator spaces are non-unital operator systems. In this context we should also mention the recent work on dual spaces of non-unital operator systems in
[
23
]
.

A particular class of matrix-regular operator spaces is given by operator spaces that possess an approximate order unit in the sense of Ng in
[
24
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
E
be a matrix-ordered
*
-vector space. An approximate order unit for
E
is a net
\{e_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
with the following properties

(1)

e_{\lambda}\in E_{+}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
;

(2)

e_{\lambda}\leq e_{\mu}
whenever
\lambda\leq\mu
;

(3)

for each
x\in E_{h}
there exists a positive real number
t
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that

-te_{\lambda}\leq x\leq te_{\lambda}.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
16
, Lemma 2.6]

Let
E
be a matrix-ordered
*
-vector space. If
E
has an approximate order unit, then
M_{n}(E)
has an approximate order unit for all
n
.

Proof.

Our proof is inspired by the approach taken in the unital case
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
. First of all, for
x\in E_{h}
we have (
cf.

[
16
, Proposition 1.8(i)]

-te_{\lambda}\leq x\leq te_{\lambda}\iff\begin{pmatrix}te_{\lambda}&x\\
x&te_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

For a general
x\in E
we may apply this to both
\textup{Re}(x),\textup{Im}(x)
to conclude that

\begin{pmatrix}2te_{\lambda}&x\\
x^{*}&2te_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}te_{\lambda}&\textup{Re}(x)\\
\textup{Re}(x)&te_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-i\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}te_{\lambda}&\textup{Im}(x)\\
\textup{Im}(x)&te_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&i\end{pmatrix}\geq 0.

Thus for
x=(x_{ij})\in M_{n}(E)_{h}
we may choose
t
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
-te_{\lambda}\leq\textup{Re}(v_{ij}),\textup{Im}(v_{ij}\leq te_{\lambda}
for all
i,j
. Hence

2nte_{\lambda}^{n}+x=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}2te_{\lambda}&x_{ij}\\
x_{ji}&2te_{\lambda}\end{pmatrix}A_{ij}\geq 0,

where
A_{ij}
is the
2\times m
matrix with
1
at the
(1,i)
’th and
(2,j)
’th positions and 0 elsewhere for
i,j=1,\ldots,n
. Similarly, we find that
2nte_{\lambda}^{n}-x\geq 0
which proves the claim.
∎

As we see from the proof we have that for all
x\in M_{n}(E)
there exist
t>0
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that

(1)

\begin{pmatrix}te_{\lambda}^{n}&x\\
x^{*}&te_{\lambda}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2n}(E)_{+}

This is the key property that allows us to introduce a matrix norm on
E
(as in
[
24
]
,
[
15
,
17
]
). Indeed, given an approximate order unit
\{e_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
we may introduce a seminorm
p_{\Lambda,n}
on
M_{n}(E)
by setting

p_{\Lambda,n}(x)=\inf\left\{t:\begin{pmatrix}te_{\lambda}^{n}&x\\
x^{*}&te_{\lambda}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2n}(E)_{+}\text{ for some }\lambda\in\Lambda\right\}

In the case of a matrix-ordered operator space, which is equipped with an approximate order unit, it is natural to require the seminorms
p_{\Lambda,n}
to be compatible with the matrix norms, which leads to the following result.

Proposition 2.10
.

Suppose that
E
is a matrix-ordered operator space with an approximate order unit that defines the matrix norms (in the sense that
p_{\Lambda,n}=\|\cdot\|
). Then
E
is matrix regular. Consequently,
E
is a non-unital operator system.

Proof.

Let
x\in M_{n}(E)
with
\|x\|<1
. Then there exist
t<1
so that

\begin{pmatrix}te_{\lambda}^{n}&x\\
x^{*}&te_{\lambda}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2n}(E)_{+}.

where indeed
te_{\lambda}^{n}\geq 0
and
\|te_{\lambda}^{n}\|<1
.

Conversely, suppose there exist
a,d\in M_{n}(E)_{+}
,
\|a\|,\|d\|<1
for which
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&x\\
x^{*}&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\geq 0
. Then also
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&-x\\
-x^{*}&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\geq 0
so that

-\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}.

Since
p_{\Lambda,2n}\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\right)<1
we thus find for some
t<1
that

-te_{\lambda}^{2n}\leq\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}\leq te_{\lambda}^{2n},\implies-te_{\lambda}^{2n}\leq\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\leq te_{\lambda}^{2n},

and thus
\|x\|<1
as required.

The fact that then
E
is a non-unital operator systems follows from
[
15
,
17
]
(
cf.

[
13
, Theorem 1]
).
∎

Note that this result also applies without the assumption of
E
being complete.

2.3.
States, quasi-states and pure states of matrix-ordered operator spaces

Let
E
be a matrix-ordered operator space. We define the noncommutative (nc) state space for any
n
as follows:

\mathcal{S}_{n}(E):=\{\varphi\in M_{n}(E)^{*},\|\varphi\|=1,\varphi\geq 0\}

Note that we consider
M_{n}(E)^{*}
as the Banach space dual of the normed space
M_{n}(E)
, and not as the operator space dual
M_{n}(E^{*})=\mathrm{CB}(E,M_{n})
which is equipped with the
\mathrm{cb}
-norm (in contrast to e.g.
[
8
, Section 5.1]
).

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
E
be a matrix-ordered operator space and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
a dense
*
-subspace. Suppose there exists an approximate order unit
\{e_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
\mathcal{E}
that defines the norm on
\mathcal{E}
and let
\varphi\in M_{n}(E)^{*}
for any
n
. If
\varphi\geq 0
then
\|\varphi\|=\lim\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})
. As a consequence,
\mathcal{S}_{n}(E)
is a convex set.

Proof.

Clearly,
\|\varphi\|\geq\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})
for all
\lambda
so let us prove the other inequality. Take
x\in M_{n}(\mathcal{E})
with
\|x\|\leq 1
. Then we have

-\begin{pmatrix}e_{\lambda}^{n}&0\\
0&e_{\lambda}^{n}\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}e_{\lambda}^{n}&0\\
0&e_{\lambda}^{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathcal{E}
is matrix-regular and
\varphi\geq 0
it follows that

\left\|\begin{pmatrix}0&\varphi(x)\\
\varphi(x)^{*}&0\end{pmatrix}\right\|\leq\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})&0\\
0&\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})\end{pmatrix}\right\|\implies|\varphi(x)|\leq\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})\leq\liminf\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n}).

Taking the supremum over all
x
with
\|x\|\leq 1
we find that
\|\varphi\|\leq\liminf\varphi(e_{\lambda}^{n})
.
∎

Note, however, that in general
S_{n}(E)
is not weakly
*
-compact. Under the conditions stated in Lemma
2.11
we will call an extreme point of
\mathcal{S}_{n}(E)
a
(nc) pure state
, and write
\mathcal{P}_{n}(E)
for all (nc) pure states on
E
.

The
nc quasi-state space
is defined for any
n
as

(2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{n}(E):=\{\varphi\in M_{n}(E)^{*},\|\varphi\|\leq 1,\varphi\geq 0\}

In contrast to
S_{n}(E)
, this is a convex weakly
*
-compact space and contains the point
0
as an extreme point. Any other extreme point
\varphi
has
\|\varphi\|=1
, for if
\|\varphi\|=\lambda<1
then
\lambda^{-1}\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(E)
. We record the following result.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
E
be a matrix-ordered operator space and
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
a dense
*
-subspace containing a norm-defining approximate order unit.
Then a state
\varphi
on a matrix-ordered operator space
E
is pure if and only if it is extreme in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(E)

Proof.

If
\varphi
is extreme in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(E)
then it cannot be written as a convex combination of elements in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(E)
, let alone of elements in
\mathcal{S}(E)
. Conversely, suppose that
\varphi
is not extreme and can thus be written as a convex combination
t\varphi_{1}+(1-t)\varphi_{2}
with
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(E)
, we claim that in fact
\|\varphi_{1}\|=\|\varphi_{2}\|=1
so that
\varphi
is not pure. Indeed, if on the contrary say
\|\varphi_{1}\|<1
we also have that
\|\varphi\|<1
, contradicting the fact that
\varphi
is a state.
∎

2.4.
Extension of positive functionals, Jordan decomposition

Let us start by formulating an Arveson’s extension theorem for positive functionals on matrix-ordered operator spaces contained in a
C^{*}
-algebra, in the presence of a norm-defining approximate order unit. We also refer to
[
15
, Theorem 3.4]
and
[
17
]
for an analogous result in the context of matricial Riesz normed spaces.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
E
be a closed
*
-subspace in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
be dense
*
-subspaces, so that
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq A
. If there exists a norm-defining approximate order unit for
\mathcal{A}
which is contained in
\mathcal{E}
, then any positive linear functional
\varphi
on
M_{n}(E)
can be extended to a positive linear functional
\widetilde{\varphi}
to
M_{n}(A)
such that
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4

E
is a non-unital operator system. Hence, the unitization
E^{\sharp}
from Definition
2.3
is a unital operator system by
[
33
, Lemma 4.8]
.

Now, let
\varphi:E\to\mathbb{C}
be positive and assume that
\|\varphi\|=1
. We first consider the extension of
\varphi
to a linear functional
\varphi^{\sharp}:E^{\sharp}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
\varphi^{\sharp}(x\oplus z)=\varphi(x)+z
. We claim that the map
\varphi^{\sharp}
is positive. Indeed, since
(x,z)\geq 0
iff
z\geq 0
and
\psi(x)\geq-z
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(E)
it follows that in particular
\varphi(x)\geq-z
.

We now apply Arveson’s extension Theorem
[
26
, Theorem 7.5]
to the functional
\varphi^{\sharp}
on
E^{\sharp}\subseteq A^{\sharp}
. This gives a positive
\widetilde{\varphi}^{\sharp}:A^{\sharp}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\widetilde{\varphi}^{\sharp}|_{E^{\sharp}}=\varphi^{\sharp}
, and thus also
\widetilde{\varphi}^{\sharp}|_{E}=\varphi
. We now define
\widetilde{\varphi}:A\to\mathbb{C}
as the restriction
\widetilde{\varphi}=\widetilde{\varphi}^{\sharp}|_{A}
. This map
\widetilde{\varphi}
is the sought-for positive extension of
\varphi
from
E
to
A
.

Finally, the claim that
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|
follows from Lemma
2.11
. Indeed, we may identify
\mathcal{E}^{*}\cong E^{*}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*}=A^{*}
to find that
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\lim\widetilde{\varphi}(e_{\lambda})=\lim\varphi(e_{\lambda})=\|\varphi\|
because
e_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{E}
is an approximate order unit
\mathcal{A}
.

Note that the generalization to
M_{n}(E)
is straightforward since one may consider
M_{n}(E)
as a matrix-ordered operator space contained in the
C^{*}
-algebra
M_{n}(A)
and with approximate order unit
e_{\lambda}^{n}
.
∎

We then have the following well-known result (
cf.

[
22
, Theorem 5.1.13]
adapted to our context as follows.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
E
be a closed
*
-subspace in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
be dense
*
-subspaces, so that
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq A
. If there exists a norm-defining approximate order unit for
\mathcal{A}
which is contained in
\mathcal{E}
, then any pure state
\varphi
on
E
can be extended to a pure state
\widetilde{\varphi}
on
A
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a pure state on
E
and consider the space
\mathcal{A}(\varphi)
of its extensions to states of
A
. This is a convex set, which is weakly
*
-closed in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(A)
, so it is weakly
*
-compact as well. Hence
\mathcal{A}(\varphi)
has extreme points, which are necessarily extreme in
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(A)
. Indeed, if an extreme point
\psi\in A(\varphi)
can be written as
\psi=t\chi_{1}+(1-t)\chi_{2}
for some non-zero
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(A)
then also the restriction can be written as a convex combination:
\varphi=t\chi_{1}|_{E}+(1-t)\chi_{2}|_{E}
. But since
\varphi
is pure, this forces
\chi_{1}|_{E}=\chi_{2}|_{E}=\varphi
so that
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(\varphi)
, contradicting the fact that
\psi
is extreme.

Note that the proof also applies when replacing
E
and
A
by dense subspaces
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

We also record the following version of Jordan decomposition for matrix-ordered operator spaces, which is a direct consequence of the well-known
C^{*}
-algebraic version (see for instance
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.6]
for the case of unital operator systems).

Theorem 2.15

(Jordan decomposition for matrix-ordered operator spaces)
.

Let
E
be a closed
*
-subspace in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
be dense
*
-subspaces, so that
\mathcal{E}\subseteq E
and
\mathcal{A}\subseteq A
. If there exists a norm-defining approximate order unit for
\mathcal{A}
contained in
\mathcal{E}
,
then for each hermitian continuous linear functional
\varphi:M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
on
E
there exist positive linear functionals
\varphi_{+},\varphi_{-}:M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\varphi=\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}
and
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi_{+}\|+\|\varphi_{-}\|
.

Proof.

We extend
\varphi:M_{n}(E)\to\mathbb{C}
by the Hahn–Banach Theorem to a linear functional
\widetilde{\varphi}:M_{n}(A)\to\mathbb{C}
so that
\widetilde{\varphi}|_{M_{n}(E)}=\varphi
and
\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi\|
. Here we may assume that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is hermitian by replacing it by
\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{\varphi}+\widetilde{\varphi}^{*})
(which has the same norm as
\widetilde{\varphi}
has). We then apply the Jordan decomposition (
cf.

[
27
, Theorem 3.2.5]
) for the
C^{*}
-algebra
M_{n}(A)
to obtain positive functionals
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\pm}:M_{n}(A)\to\mathbb{C}
with the property that

\widetilde{\varphi}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{-};\qquad\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|=\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|+\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\|.

The restrictions
\varphi_{\pm}:=\widetilde{\varphi}_{\pm}|_{M_{n}(E)}
are positive functionals on
M_{n}(E)
and we have
\|\varphi_{\pm}\|\leq\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\pm}\|
. But since
\|\varphi\|=\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|
we find that for
\varphi=\widetilde{\varphi}|_{M_{n}(E)}=\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}
we have

\|\varphi\|=\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|+\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\|\geq\|\varphi_{+}\|+\|\varphi_{-}\|

Finally, for
\varphi=\widetilde{\varphi}|_{M_{n}(E)}=\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}
we also have
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}\|\leq\|\varphi_{+}\|+\|\varphi_{-}\|
, and this completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.16
.

Note that uniqueness of the above Jordan decomposition of operator systems does not hold in general, unless
E=A
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. See
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.6, Exercise 4.6.22]
.

In any case, the Jordan decomposition will allow us to say something about the Banach space dual of
M_{n}(E)\subseteq M_{n}(A)
—and consequently about the quasi-state space of
M_{n}(E)
— under the assumption that there is an approximate order unit for
\mathcal{A}
with the stated properties.
Let us start with some notation.

As before we write
M_{n}(E)^{*}
and
M_{n}(A)^{*}
for the Banach space duals of
M_{n}(E)
and
M_{n}(A)
, respectively. Also the notation
M_{n}(E)_{h},M_{n}(A)_{h}
will be used for the hermitian subspaces, as well as
M_{n}(E)_{h}^{*},M_{n}(A)_{h}^{*}
for the Banach space duals (where we note that
(M_{n}(E)^{*})_{h}\cong(M_{n}(E)_{h})^{*}
, isometrically isomorphic). There is a canonical order on the dual spaces which is respected by the restriction map by sending
M_{n}(A)^{*}_{+}\to M_{n}(E)^{*}_{+}
.

We define as usual the annihilator of
M_{n}(E)
as the closed subspace

M_{n}(E)^{\perp}:=\{\eta\in M_{n}(A)^{*}\mid\eta(x)=0,\forall x\in M_{n}(E)\}\subseteq M_{n}(A)^{*}

The quotient
M_{n}(A)^{*}/M_{n}(E)^{\perp}
is a Banach space, on which we may introduce the following canonical order:

\displaystyle(M_{n}(A)^{*}/M_{n}(E)^{\perp})_{+}:=q(M_{n}(A)^{*}_{+})

(3)

\displaystyle\qquad=\{\varphi+M_{n}(E)^{\perp}\mid\varphi\in M_{n}(A)^{*}\text{ and }\varphi+\eta\geq 0\text{ for some }\eta\in M_{n}(E)^{\perp}\}

using the quotient map
q:M_{n}(A)^{*}\to M_{n}(A)^{*}/M_{n}(E)^{\perp}
.

Remark 2.17
.

The general construction of (matrix) orders on quotient spaces of operator systems is quite subtle (already in the unital case) and has been described in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
E\subseteq A
be a closed
*
-subspace in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
and let
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
be dense
*
-subspaces with a norm-defining approximate order unit for
\mathcal{A}
contained in
\mathcal{E}
.

Then the map
\rho:M_{n}(A)^{*}_{h}/M_{n}(E)^{\perp}_{h}\to M_{n}(E)^{*}_{h}
induced by restriction
\rho(\varphi+M_{n}(E)^{\perp}_{h})=\varphi|_{M_{n}(E)_{h}}
is an isometrical order isomorphism.

Proof.

The map
\rho
is clearly well-defined and injective. Moreover, for any
\eta\in M_{n}(E)^{\perp}_{h}
we have

\|\varphi|_{M_{n}(E)_{h}}\|=\|(\varphi+\eta)|_{M_{n}(E)_{h}}\|\leq\|\varphi+\eta\|.

Taking the infimum over all
\eta
we find that
\rho
is a contraction. Note also that
\rho
is an order-preserving map.

The inverse to
\rho
is obtained by applying the Hahn–Banach theorem. Indeed, let a hermitian
\varphi\in M_{n}(E)^{*}
be given and write it in terms of a Jordan decomposition (Thm.
2.15
) as
\varphi=\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}
where
\varphi_{\pm}\in M_{n}(E)^{*}
are two positive functionals such that
\|\varphi\|=\|\varphi_{+}\|+\|\varphi_{-}\|
. By Proposition
2.13
there exist positive extensions
\widetilde{\varphi}_{\pm}\in M_{n}(A)^{*}
with
\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{\pm}\|=\|\varphi_{\pm}\|
. We set
\rho^{-1}(\varphi)=\widetilde{\varphi}+M_{n}(E)^{\perp}
where
\widetilde{\varphi}=\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}-\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}
; this is a well-defined and linear map
M_{n}(E)^{*}_{h}\mapsto M_{n}(A)_{h}^{*}/M_{n}(E)_{h}^{\perp}
. For the norm we have

\|\widetilde{\varphi}+M_{n}(E)^{\perp}_{h}\|\leq\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|\leq\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}\|+\|\widetilde{\varphi}_{-}\|=\|\varphi_{+}\|+\|\varphi_{-}\|=\|\varphi\|,

so that
\rho^{-1}
is a contraction as well. Thus, both
\rho
and
\rho^{-1}
are isometries.

Finally, if
\varphi=\varphi_{+}
is a positive functional then we have
\rho^{-1}(\varphi)=\widetilde{\varphi}_{+}
which is positive as well so that also
\rho^{-1}
is order-preserving.
∎

3.
Operator systems, groupoid
C^{*}
-algebras, bonds and relations

In this section we will introduce the main class of examples of operator systems which are associated to reflexive and symmetric relations on a set. It is convenient and also natural to formulate this in the slightly more general context of groupoids, and the corresponding
C^{*}
-algebras
[
28
]
.

We will consider a locally compact groupoid
G
equipped with a (left invariant) Haar system
\nu=\{\nu_{x}\}
. The space
C_{c}(G)
of compactly supported complex-valued continuous functions on
G
becomes a
*
-algebra with the convolution product and involution given by

f\ast g(\gamma)=\int_{G_{x}}f(\gamma\gamma_{1}^{-1})g(\gamma_{1})d\nu_{x}(\gamma_{1});\qquad f^{*}(\gamma)=\overline{f(\gamma^{-1})},

where
x=s(\gamma)
for any
\gamma\in G
. There is a norm given by

\|f\|_{1}=\sup_{x\in G^{(0)}}\left\{\max(\int_{G_{x}}|f(\gamma)|d\nu_{x}(\gamma),\int_{G_{x}}|f(\gamma^{-1})|d\nu_{x}(\gamma))\right\}

and the completion
C_{c}(G)^{-\|\cdot\|_{1}}
with respects to this norm turns out to be a Banach
*
-algebra. The universal
C^{*}
-algebra enveloping this Banach
*
-algebra will be called the
groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
, and will be denoted by
C^{*}(G,\nu)
, or simply
C^{*}(G)
. We refer to
[
28
, Section II.1]
for full details.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that for simplicity we have assumed that
G
is Hausdorff, but this is not necessary. Indeed, arguing as in
[
4
,
20
]
for the definition of
C^{*}(G)
we should then replace
C_{c}(G)
by a space of —not necessarily continuous— functions spanned by functions which vanish outside a compact Hausdorff subset
K\subset G
and which are continuous on a neighborhood of
K
.

We now come to our main definition.

Definition 3.2
.

A bond is a triple
(G,\nu,\Omega)
consisting of a locally compact groupoid
G
, a Haar system
\nu=\{\nu_{x}\}
and an open symmetric subset
\Omega\subseteq G
containing the units
G^{(0)}
.

When there is no ambiguity on the groupoid and the Haar system,
we will also refer to the subset
\Omega
as a bond.

Then, if
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subseteq G
are two bonds we have their composition, as usual:

\Omega_{1}\circ\Omega_{2}:=\left\{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}:\gamma_{1}\in\Omega_{1},\quad\gamma_{2}\in\Omega_{2},\quad s(\gamma_{1})=r(\gamma_{2})\right\}

Clearly, this subset fails to be symmetric in general so we should consider instead the bond given by the symmetrized composition

\Omega_{1}*\Omega_{2}:=\Omega_{1}\circ\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{2}\circ\Omega_{1}.

The following result follows directly from the definition of the involution in
C_{c}(G)
and the assumption that a bond
\Omega
is symmetric:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\Omega,G,\nu)
be a bond. The closure of the subspace
C_{c}(\Omega)\subseteq C_{c}(G)
in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G)
is an operator system.

We will denote this operator system as
E(\Omega,G,\nu)
(or simply
E(\Omega)
if no ambiguity can arise). Clearly, since
\supp(f\ast g)\subset\supp(f)\circ\supp(g)
this subspace is in general not closed under the product in
C^{*}(G)
, unless the bond
\Omega
is all of
G
in which case we have
E(G)=C^{*}(G)
.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the infinite cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}
as a groupoid. An example of a bond is given by an interval
\Omega_{n}=(-n,n)\subset\mathbb{Z}
for some integer
n
. The corresponding operator system
E(\Omega_{n},\mathbb{Z})
consists of sequences
a=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
C^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
with the following restricted support:

a=(\ldots 0,a_{-n+1},a_{-n+2},\ldots,a_{n-2},a_{n-1},0\ldots)

This operator system was analyzed at length in
[
5
]
, where we called it the Fejér–Riesz operator system
C^{*}(\mathbb{Z})_{(n)}
. In particular, it has propagation number
\infty
.

Example 3.5
.

Consider now a finite cyclic group
C_{m}
. We can again consider the bond
\Omega_{n}=(-n,-n+1\ldots,n-1,n)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
as in the previous example, but this time considered modulo
m
. We may then realize the corresponding operator system
E(\Omega_{n},C_{m})
as the space of
m\times m
banded circulant matrices with fixed band width (equal to
n
). In particular, one finds the propagation number to be finite.

These two examples illustrate the important role played by the ambient groupoid, since for the same bond
(-n,n)
but in different groups
\mathbb{Z}
and
C_{m}
one gets different, and not even Morita equivalent operator systems (in the sense of
[
9
]
).

3.1.
Operator systems for tolerance relations

An important class of examples of bonds is given by a symmetric and reflexive relation on a locally compact space
X
equipped with a (Borel) measure of full support. Indeed, if we consider the pair groupoid
G=X\times X
we may realize the relation as a symmetric subset
\mathcal{R}\subseteq X\times X
that contains the diagonal. In this case the operator system
E(\mathcal{R})
is a subspace of the compact operators since
C^{*}(G)\cong\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))

[
28
, Example 2.12]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be a locally compact space, equipped with a measure
\mu
of full support. Then the operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{1}\ast\mathcal{R}_{2})
associated to the composition of two relations
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}
on
X
coincides with
[E(\mathcal{R}_{1})E(\mathcal{R}_{2})+E(\mathcal{R}_{2})E(\mathcal{R}_{1})]^{-\|\cdot\|}
.

Proof.

Since
E(\mathcal{R}_{1}\ast\mathcal{R}_{2})=E(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})+E(\mathcal{R}_{2}\circ\mathcal{R}_{1})
it suffices to show that
E(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})=[E(\mathcal{R}_{1})E(\mathcal{R}_{2})]^{-\|\cdot\|}
as operator spaces.

Let us first show that
E(\mathcal{R}_{1})E(\mathcal{R}_{2})\subseteq E(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})
. Indeed, for kernels
F_{j}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{j})
(
j=1,2
) the convolution product

F_{1}\ast F_{2}(x,y)=\int_{X}F_{1}(x,z)F_{2}(z,y)d\mu(z)

is supported in
\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2}
,
i.e.

F_{1}\ast F_{2}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})
. The result then follows by taking closures.

For the other inclusion take a function
F\in C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})
with
\supp(F)=:K
. We may find compact subsets
K_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{j}
so that there exists an open subset
U
with
K\subseteq U\subseteq K_{1}\circ K_{2}
. We then take two non-negative functions
\chi_{j}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{j})
with
\supp\chi_{j}=K_{j}
and realize that their convolution product
\chi_{1}\ast\chi_{2}
will be a non-negative function in
C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})
with support equal to
K_{1}\circ K_{2}
because
\mu
has full support. It is thus strictly positive on
\supp(F)
so that we may approximate
F
by functions of the form

(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2}\mapsto\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_{m}(x)g_{m}(y)(\chi_{1}\ast\chi_{2})(x,y);\qquad(f_{m},g_{m}\in C_{c}(X)).

Moreover, kernels of this form satisfy

G(x,y)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}f_{m}(x)g_{m}(y)(\chi_{1}\ast\chi_{2})(x,y)=\sum_{m=1}^{M}G_{1,m}\ast G_{2,m}(x,y)

where we have set
G_{1,m}(x,z)=f_{m}(x)\chi_{1}(x,z)
and
G_{2,m}(z,y)=\chi_{2}(z,y)g_{m}(y)
. Note that
G_{j,m}
are continuous kernels with support in
K_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{j}
for all
m
and we have thus approximated
F
by functions in
C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{1})\ast C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{2})
.
Taking closures then gives the desired inclusion
E(\mathcal{R}_{1}\circ\mathcal{R}_{2})\subseteq[E(\mathcal{R}_{1})E(\mathcal{R}_{2})]^{-\|\cdot\|}
.
∎

3.2.
Operator systems associated to tolerance relations on finite sets

We analyze in more detail the structure of the operator system
E(\mathcal{R})
in the case that
\mathcal{R}
is a tolerance relation on a finite set
X
. In other words, we consider a bond
\mathcal{R}
on the pair groupoid associated to a finite set
X
. In this case, the unital operator system
E(\mathcal{R})
is simply given by the linear span of rank-one operators
e_{xy}
for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}
, acting linearly on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(X)
. We will denote by
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
the matrix
C^{*}
-algebra of all linear operators on
\ell^{2}(X)
.

It is useful to view
\mathcal{R}
as a graph with the set of vertices given by
X
and the set of edges by
\{xy:(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}\}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
X
be a finite set and
\mathcal{R}\subseteq X\times X
a symmetric reflexive relation on
X
and suppose that
\mathcal{R}
generates the full equivalence class
X\times X
(i.e. the graph corresponding to
\mathcal{R}
is connected). Then

(1)

The
C^{*}
-envelope of
E(\mathcal{R})
is
C^{*}_{\mathrm{env}}(E(\mathcal{R}))=\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
.

(2)

The propagation number can be expressed as
\text{prop}(E(\mathcal{R}))=\text{diam}(\mathcal{R})
, in terms of the graph diameter of
\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)=d
there is a sequence
x=x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}=y
of points in
X
such that
(x_{i},x_{i+1})\in\mathcal{R}
. Hence
e_{xy}=e_{x_{0}x_{1}}\cdots e_{x_{d-1}x_{d}}
so that
C^{*}(E(\mathcal{R}))=\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
. But then
C^{*}(E(\mathcal{R}))
is already the
C^{*}
-envelope because any two-sided ideal is trivial and this applies in particular to the Šilov ideal. We conclude that
\text{prop}(E(\mathcal{R}))\leq\text{diam}(\mathcal{R})
.

Conversely, suppose that
\text{prop}(E(\mathcal{R}))=n<\text{diam}(\mathcal{R})
and take two points
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)>n
. Then for all
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\in E(\mathcal{R})
we have

0=\langle e_{x},B_{1}\cdots B_{n}e_{y}\rangle

since
B_{i}(x_{i-1},x_{i})=0
whenever
d(x_{i-1},x_{i})>1
while
d(x_{i-1},x_{i})\leq 1)
would imply that
d(x,y)\leq n
. But then
\langle e_{x},Be_{y}\rangle=0
for all
B\in\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
which is clearly a contradiction. We conclude that
\text{prop}(E(\mathcal{R}))=\text{diam}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

For the description of the dual operator systems and state space, we make use of the following concrete realization of
E(\mathcal{R})
. First we identify
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))\cong M_{|X|}(\mathbb{C})
using the canonical basis
\{e_{x}\}_{x\in X}
of
\ell^{2}(X)
. Then we may write any element in
E(\mathcal{R})
as a
sparse

|X|\times|X|
matrix with a fixed structure:

E(\mathcal{R})\cong\{B\in\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))\mid B_{xy}=0\text{ if }(x,y)\notin\mathcal{R}\}\}.

As a matter of fact, we may identify
E(\mathcal{R})\cong S_{L}(\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X)))
using Schur–Hadamard (=entrywise) multiplication
S_{L}
with the matrix associated to the relation
\mathcal{R}
:

(4)

L=(L_{xy});\qquad L_{xy}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}\\
0&\text{ if }(x,y)\notin\mathcal{R}\end{array}\right.

We will use the following basic result on binary matrices (
i.e.
matrices having entries in
\{0,1\}
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
L
be a binary matrix associated to a symmetric and reflexive relation. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

The matrix
L
is positive semi-definite;

(ii)

The entries of the matrix
L
satisfies the triangle inequalities;

L_{ij}+L_{jk}-L_{ik}\leq 1\qquad(\forall i,j,k);

(iii)

The matrix
L
is associated to an equivalence relation.

Proof.

(i)\implies(ii)
follows since a violating triangle inequality should have
L_{ij}=L_{jk}=1
and
L_{ik}=0
. This corresponds to a submatrix of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\
1&1&1\\
0&1&1\end{pmatrix}

which is indefinite, violating positive definiteness of
L
.

For
(ii)\implies(iii)
one readily finds that the triangle inequalities imply transitivity for the relation represented by
L
.

Finally, for
(iii)\implies(i)
we may permute the columns and rows of the matrix
L
to write it as a direct sum of block matrices containing only 1’s. These blocks are all of the form
e^{T}e
with
e=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}
, and hence positive semi-definite.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

The matrix
L
defined in Equation (
4
) is positive semi-definite if and only if the relation
\mathcal{R}
is an equivalence relation. In this case
E(\mathcal{R})
is a matrix subalgebra of
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
.

We are now ready to apply the general results on dual operator systems from Section
2.4
to the finite-dimensional operator system
E(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
. We will make use of the dual pairing with
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))^{d}\cong\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
:

\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))^{d}\times\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))\to\mathbb{C};\qquad(\rho,B)\mapsto\tr(\rho B)

Proposition 3.10
.

(1)

The dual operator system
E(\mathcal{R})^{d}
of
E(\mathcal{R})
is linearly isomorphic to
S_{L}(\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X)))
with cones of positive elements given by

M_{n}(E(\mathcal{R})^{d})_{+}=(S_{L})_{n}(M_{n}(\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X)))_{+})

where
S_{L}
denote Schur multiplication with the matrix given in Equation (
4
).

(2)

We have
S_{L}(M_{n}(E(\mathcal{R})^{d})_{+})=S_{L}(M_{n}(E(\mathcal{R})^{d}))_{+}
if and only if
\mathcal{R}
is an equivalence relation.

Proof.

As in Theorem
2.18
we have
E(\mathcal{R})^{d}\cong\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))^{d}/E(\mathcal{R})^{\perp}
. Since

E(\mathcal{R})^{\perp}\cong\{\rho\in\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))\mid\rho_{xy}=0\text{ if }(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}\},

we find that the quotient map
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))^{d}\to\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))^{d}/E(\mathcal{R})^{\perp}
is given by Schur multiplication with the matrix
L
. Hence
E(\mathcal{R})^{d}\cong S_{L}(\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X)))
as linear vector spaces.

The order structure on
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))^{d}/E(\mathcal{R})^{\perp}
is defined as in Equation (
3
) (
cf.

[
18
]
) in terms of the quotient map, so that
E(\mathcal{R})^{d}_{+}\cong S_{L}(\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))_{+})
. Since
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(X))
is itself a matrix algebra, the extension to the matrix order is straightforward.

The second claim follows from Lemma
3.8
, in combination with the fact that
S_{L}
is a completely positive map if and only if
L
is positive semi-definite (
cf.
[
26
, Theorem 3.7]
).
∎

For the pure states on the operator system
E(\mathcal{R})
we have the following result.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
X
be a finite set and
\mathcal{R}\subseteq X\times X
a symmetric reflexive relation on
X
. The restriction of a pure state
\varphi\in\mathbb{P}(\ell^{2}(X))
to
E(\mathcal{R})
is pure if and only if the restriction of the relation
\mathcal{R}
to the support
S
of
\varphi
generates the full equivalence relation on
S
.

Proof.

Let
v
be a vector implementing the vector state
\varphi
, one has
v_{x}\neq 0\iff x\in S
. Assume that the restriction of the relation
\mathcal{R}
to the support
S
of
\varphi
generates the full equivalence relation on
S
. Let then
w\in\ell^{2}(X)
be a vector such that

(5)

\langle w,Bw\rangle\leq\langle v,Bv\rangle,\qquad\forall B\geq 0,B\in E(\mathcal{R})

Applying (
5
) to diagonal matrices shows that
w_{x}=0
for all
x\notin S
. Let then
(i,j)
,
i\neq j
, be a pair of elements of
S
which belongs to the relation
\mathcal{R}
. Consider the map
\iota:M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to E(\mathcal{R})
which puts zeros outside the pair
(i,j)
as a matrix with entries in
X
. The pure state on
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
associated to
\varphi\circ\iota
is given by the vector
(v_{i},v_{j})
up to normalization, it is pure because it is a vector state. By (
5
) the inequality works for all positive elements of
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for the vector
(w_{i},w_{j})
. This implies that there exists a scalar
\lambda_{i,j}
such that
(w_{i},w_{j})=\lambda_{i,j}(v_{i},v_{j})
. It follows that the ratio
w_{i}/v_{i}
is independent of

since it is preserved by the relation
\mathcal{R}
.

The converse follows from the existence of two disjoint equivalence classes in
S
which split the restriction of the pure state
\varphi
. ∎

4.
Operator systems associated to metric spaces

For us the motivating examples of tolerance relations —and corresponding operator systems— are given in terms of a metric space
(X,d)
equipped with the relation

(6)

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}:=\left\{(x,y)\in X\times X:d(x,y)<\varepsilon\right\}

Thus, we consider points in the space
X
to be identical if they are within distance
\varepsilon
. Clearly, this is reflexive and symmetric but not transitive (as long as
\varepsilon
is smaller than the diameter of
(X,d)
).

Let us analyze the composition rule for these relations, considering for instance
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\ast\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
for different
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
. Clearly, one expects this to be related to
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}}
but only for a certain class of metric spaces for which the metric distance between two points can be described in terms of smaller line segments. It turns out that the
path metric spaces
of
[
11
]
forms precisely the class of metric spaces that allows this (such spaces are called length spaces in
[
30
]
).

Definition 4.1
.

A path metric space is a metric space
(X,d)
for which the distance between each pair of points equals the infimum of the lengths of curves joining the points.

In particular, they satisfy the following two crucial, equivalent properties
[
11
, Theorem 1.8]
:

(1)

for all
x,y\in X
and
r>0
there is a
z
such that

\max\{d(x,z),d(z,y)\}\leq\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)+r

(2)

for all
x,y\in X
and
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
such that
r_{1}+r_{2}\leq d(x,y)
one has

(7)

d\left(B_{r_{1}}(x),B_{r_{2}}(y)\right)\leq d(x,y)-r_{1}-r_{2}.

We then arrive at the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete, locally compact path metric space and consider the relation
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
6
). For all
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
we have

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}*\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}}.

Proof.

We clearly have
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\circ\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}}
which proves the inclusion
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}*\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}}
. For the converse, take
(x,y)\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}}
so that
d(x,y)<\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Let
r_{1}+r_{2}=d(x,y)
be such that
r_{1}<\varepsilon,r_{2}<\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Then with (
7
) we find that

d\left(B_{r_{1}}(x),B_{r_{2}}(y)\right)=0.

Hence there are sequences
(z_{n})
in
B_{r_{1}}(x)
and
(z^{\prime}_{n})
in
B_{r_{2}}(y)
such that
d(z_{n},z_{n}^{\prime})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. By the Hopf–Rinow Theorem, the closed balls
\overline{B}_{r_{1}}(x)
and
\overline{B}_{r_{2}}(y)
are compact so that the sequences
(z_{n}),(z_{n}^{\prime})
have convergent sub-sequences that both converge to the same element
z\in X
. In fact,
z\in\overline{B}_{r_{1}}(x)\cap\overline{B}_{r_{2}}(y)
so that
d(x,z)\leq r_{1}<\varepsilon
and
d(z,y)\leq r_{2}<\varepsilon^{\prime}
as desired.
∎

4.1.
Operator systems for finite partial partitions

In preparation for the study of the operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
in the next subsection, we first analyze a class of finite-dimensional operator systems
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
which are associated to the relation
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
and so-called finite partial partitions
P
of a metric space
X
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a metric space and let
\varepsilon>0
.

•

A finite partial
\varepsilon
-partition of
X
is a finite collection
P=\{U_{i}\}
of disjoint sets
U\subseteq X
such that
\textup{diam}(U_{i})<\varepsilon
;

•

We say that a finite partial partition
P^{\prime}
is a refinement of another finite partial partition
P
if each set in
P^{\prime}
that lies in the support of
P
is contained in a set in
P
and if the supports
\cup_{U\in P}U\subseteq\cup_{U^{\prime}\in P}U^{\prime}
.

If
X
is equipped with a (Borel) measure, then we will further assume that all sets in a finite partial
\varepsilon
-partition are all measurable and have finite and non-zero measure.

Note that the notion of refinement induces a directed partial ordering on the set of finite partial
\varepsilon
-partitions of a metric space.

Let
X
be a metric measure space and
P
a finite partial
\varepsilon
-partition. We can embed the finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(P)
in
L^{2}(X)
by considering normalized characteristic function
1_{U}
on
U
as elements in
L^{2}(X)
. The corresponding finite-dimensional matrix algebra
\mathcal{A}_{P}
is then defined by

\mathcal{A}_{P}=\left\{\sum_{U,V\in P}a_{UV}|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{V}|:a_{UV}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

and, in fact, we realize that
\mathcal{A}_{P}\cong\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(P))
.
Note that the unit in
\mathcal{A}_{P}
is given by
e_{P}=\sum_{U\in\mathcal{P}}|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{U}|
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
P,P^{\prime}
be finite partial
\varepsilon
-partitions of a metric measure space
X
and let
\mathcal{A}_{P}
be as defined above.

(1)

If
P\leq P^{\prime}
then
\mathcal{A}_{P}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{P^{\prime}}
.

(2)

The (algebraic) direct limit
\varinjlim\mathcal{A}_{P}
is a dense
*
-subalgebra of the compact operators
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
. Hence
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))=\overline{\varinjlim}\mathcal{A}_{P}
is the
C^{*}
-algebraic direct limit.

(3)

The algebra
\varinjlim\mathcal{A}_{P}
has a norm-defining approximate order unit (with respect to the matrix norm and matrix order induced from
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
.

Proof.

(1) follows from the fact that each
U\in P
can be written as a finite disjoint union of sets
U_{j}^{\prime}\in P^{\prime}
. Hence,
\ell^{2}(P)\subset\ell^{2}({P^{\prime}})
and thus also
\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(P))\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}({P^{\prime}}))
.

For (2) we note that
\varinjlim\mathcal{A}_{P}
is the linear span of rank-one operators of the form
|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{V}|
with
\text{diam}(U),\textup{diam}(V)<\varepsilon
. Now, any
L^{2}
-function on
X
can be approximated by a continuous function, which in turn can be approximated by step functions of the form
\sum_{U\in P}c_{U}1_{U}
for some sufficiently fine
P
. As a consequence we find that
\varinjlim A_{P}
is a dense subalgebra of the algebra
\mathcal{K}_{0}(L^{2}(X))
of all finite-rank operators, whose closure is
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
.

For (3) we note that for any
x\in\mathcal{A}_{P}
we have
-\|x\|e_{P}\leq x\leq\|x\|e_{P}
and this indeed defines the norm of
x
. The approximate order unit for
\varinjlim\mathcal{A}_{P}
is then given by
\{e_{P}\}_{P}
, indexed by all finite partial
\varepsilon
-partitions.
∎

Next, we define relations
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}
associated to the partition
P
and the relation
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
introduced in Equation (
6
).

Definition 4.5
.

Let
X
be a metric measure space and
P
a finite partial
\varepsilon
-partition of
X
. We define a tolerance relation
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}
on the finite set
P
by

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}=\{U\times V\mid\quad U,V\in P,\quad U\times V\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\}\subseteq P\times P.

As in Section
3.2
the corresponding finite-dimensional operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
is given by the linear span of operators of the form
|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{V}|
with
U\times V\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
P,P^{\prime}
be finite partial
\varepsilon
-partitions of a metric measure space
X
and let
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
be as defined above.

(1)

If
P\leq P^{\prime}
then
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})\subseteq E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P^{\prime}})
.

(2)

The approximate order unit
\{e_{P}\}_{P}
of
\varinjlim A_{P}
is contained in
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})

Proof.

For (1) take
U,V\in P
with
U\times V\in\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.4
we may write
U=\cup_{j}U_{j}^{\prime}
and
V=\cup_{k}V_{k}^{\prime}
in terms of the refinement
P^{\prime}
. But then also
U_{j}\times V_{k}\subseteq U\times V\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
and hence the rank-one operator
|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{V}|\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
can be written as
\sum_{j,k}|1_{U_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle 1_{V_{k}^{\prime}}|
which is an element in
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P^{\prime}})
.

For (2) it suffices to note that
e_{P}=\sum_{U\in P}|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{U}|
in terms of sets
U
with diameter
<\varepsilon
. But then
U\times U\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
so that
e_{P}\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
.
∎

As a special case of the results obtained at the end of Section
3.2
we have:

Proposition 4.7
.

(1)

The operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
is complete order-isomorphic to
S_{L}(\mathcal{A}_{P})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{P}
, in terms of Schur–Hadamard multiplication with the matrix

L=(L_{ij});\qquad L_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{ if }U_{i}\times U_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\\
0&\text{ if }U_{i}\times U_{j}\not\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\end{array}\right.

(2)

The dual operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})^{d}
of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
is linearly isomorphic to
S_{L}(\mathcal{A}_{P})
with cones of positive elements given by

M_{n}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})^{d})_{+}=(S_{L})_{n}(M_{n}(\mathcal{A}_{P})_{+})

(3)

We have
S_{L}((\mathcal{A}_{P})_{+})=S_{L}(\mathcal{A}_{P})_{+}
if and only if
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}
is an equivalence relation.

For the pure states of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
, Proposition
3.11
implies the following result.

Proposition 4.8
.

The restriction of a pure state
\varphi\in\mathbb{P}(l^{2}(P))
to
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
is pure if and only if the restriction of
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}
to the support of
\varphi
generates the full equivalence relation.

Example 4.9
.

An interesting example of operator systems
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
are associated to finite
\varepsilon
-partitions
P
of the unit interval
[0,1)
. Let us consider partitions of the form

(8)

P=\left\{U_{k}:=\left[\frac{k}{p},\frac{(k+1)}{p}\right)\right\}_{k=0}^{p-1}

We set a lower bound
\varepsilon>1/p
so that each cell
U_{k}
satisfies
U_{k}\times U_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
. The (unital) operator system
\mathcal{E}_{P}(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
can then be realized as the operator system
\mathcal{E}_{p,N}
of
p\times p
band matrices
B
of band width
N
, i.e.,

\mathcal{E}_{p,N}:=\{B=(b_{ij})\in M_{p}(\mathbb{C})\mid\qquad b_{ij}=0\text{ if }|i-j|>N\}

Indeed, the relation
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}
on
P
that defines the operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
can be written as

(9)

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P}=\{U_{k}\times U_{l}:U_{k}\times U_{l}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\}=\{U_{k}\times U_{l}:|k-l|\leq N\}.

so that a basis for
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
is thus given by
\{|1_{U_{k}}\rangle\langle 1_{U_{l}}|\}_{|k-l|\leq N}
which indeed spans the band matrices of the claimed band width.

One quickly realizes that
\mathcal{E}_{p,N}\mathcal{E}_{p,N^{\prime}}=\mathcal{E}_{p,N+N^{\prime}}
and that the propagation number of
\mathcal{E}_{p,N}\subseteq M_{p}(\mathbb{C})
is equal to
\lceil{p/N}\rceil
.

4.2.
Operator systems for metric spaces at finite resolution

Suppose now that
X
is a path metric space, which is also a measure space with a measure of full support. Consider the operator systems
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
. It is convenient to consider it as a limit of the operator systems associated to finite partial partitions that we considered in the previous section. So let us write
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})=\varinjlim E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
for the algebraic direct limit considered in Lemma
4.6
; similarly we write
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}=\varinjlim\mathcal{A}_{P}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
X
be a path metric measure space with a measure of full support. Let
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
be the (non-complete) operator system associated to the relation
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
as defined above. Then

(1)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is a dense subspace of the operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
and, consequently, we have
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})=\overline{\varinjlim}E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
, the closure of the algebraic direct limit;

(2)

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
is a dense
*
-subalgebra of the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
;

(3)

there exists a norm-defining approximate order unit for
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
which is contained in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

For (1) we note that
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is defined as the closure of the space of integral operators
\pi(F)
on
L^{2}(X)
with kernel
F\in C_{c}(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
.
We may cover the compact support of
F
by a finite collection
\mathcal{P}
of open balls of radius
<\varepsilon
. Hence, there are simple functions of the form
\sum_{U\times V\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}}c_{UV}1_{U\times V}
that approximate
F
in
L^{\infty}
-norm. But then the same is true in
L^{2}
-norm, again by compactness of the support of
F
. Consequently, the Hilbert–Schmidt operator
\pi(F)
may be approximated by
\pi(1_{U\times V})=|1_{U}\rangle\langle 1_{V}|\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
.

The second and third statement were already established in Lemma
4.6
.
∎

It is not surprising that the operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is closely related to the
Roe algebra

[
29
]
associated to the coarse metric structure of
(X,d)
. Recall that in the present context of a metric measure space, the Roe algebra (or
translation
C^{*}
-algebra
)
C^{*}(X)
is the norm closure of locally compact and finite propagation bounded operators on
L^{2}(X)
. Here an operator
T
is called locally compact if
fT
and
Tf
are compact for all
f\in C_{0}(X)
and
T
has propagation
<\varepsilon
means that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)>\varepsilon
then
(x,y)
is not in the support of
T
. One realizes as in
[
25
]
that
C^{*}(X)
is filtered by the collection of self-adjoint subspaces
(C^{*}(X)_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
where
C^{*}(X)_{\varepsilon}
is the closure of locally compact bounded operators with propagation less than
\varepsilon
. Now, since the support of an operator
\pi(F)\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is given by the essential support of the kernel
F
, we conclude that
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\subseteq C^{*}(X)_{\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

The following result shows the interplay between the propagation number for the operator system
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
and the finite propagation of the corresponding operators.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete, locally compact path metric measure space and
\mu
a measure on
X
with full support. Let
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
be the operator system associated to the relation
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
. Then

{\rm prop}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))=\lceil\delta/\varepsilon\rceil

where
\lceil\cdot\rceil
is the ceiling function and
\delta
the diameter of
(X,d)
. Moreover the same equality holds after taking the minimal tensor product of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
with the compact operators in a Hilbert space
H
.

Proof.

First observe that for
n\geq\lceil\delta/\varepsilon\rceil
we derive from Lemma
3.6
in combination with Lemma
4.2
that

(10)

E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}=E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\ast\cdots*\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})=E(\mathcal{R}_{n\varepsilon})=\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))

since
\mathcal{R}_{n\varepsilon}=X\times X
. Hence, we have
C^{*}({E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})})\cong{\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))}
. But then
C^{*}({E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})})
is already the
C^{*}
-envelope of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
because any boundary subsystem in the
C^{*}
-extension
C^{*}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))
of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is trivial and this applies in particular to the Šilov ideal. We conclude that
{\rm prop}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))\leq\lceil\delta/\varepsilon\rceil
.

Now let
n
be an integer such that
n\varepsilon<\delta
(
i.e.

n<\lceil\delta/\varepsilon\rceil
). Let
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
be such that the distance between the essential supports of
\xi
and
\eta
is
>n\varepsilon
. One has for
K_{j}=\pi(k_{j})\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
that

\langle\xi,K_{1}K_{2}\ldots K_{n}\eta\rangle=\int\overline{\xi(x_{1})}k_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\ldots k_{n}(x_{n},x_{n+1})\eta(x_{n+1})\prod d\mu(x_{j})

and the integrand vanishes identically since

d(x_{j},x_{j+1})\leq\varepsilon\ \forall j\Rightarrow d(x_{1},x_{n+1})\leq n\varepsilon\Rightarrow\xi(x_{1})\eta(x_{n+1})=0

This shows that one has

(11)

\langle\xi,T\eta\rangle=0,\qquad\forall T\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}

Thus
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}
cannot be a
C^{*}
-algebra since by the argument leading to (
10
) it would then be the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators and the above vanishing does not hold for some compact operator. Hence
{\rm prop}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))\geq\lceil\delta/\varepsilon\rceil
which proves the first claim.

This argument can be extended to the minimal tensor product of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
with the compact operators
\mathcal{K}(H)
on a Hilbert space
H
in the following way. Note first that for any integer
n>0
we have
\left(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)\right)^{(n)}=E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)
essentially because
\mathcal{K}(H)^{(n)}=\mathcal{K}(H)
(in fact,
\mathcal{K}(H)^{(n)}
is a closed two-sided ideal in
\mathcal{K}(H)
, hence exhausts all of
\mathcal{K}(H)
).

The next step consists of extending the inner product in (
11
) to
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)
and apply the same argument as above. In fact, for any
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
the map
T\to\langle\xi,T\eta\rangle
is completely bounded from
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}
to
\mathbb{C}
, since it is the restriction of a linear functional on the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(X))
. If we tensor it with the (also completely bounded) identity map on
\mathcal{K}(H)
we arrive at a completely bounded map

E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)\to\mathcal{K}(H);\qquad T\otimes A\mapsto\langle\xi,T\eta\rangle\otimes A.

We find that for
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(X)
with sufficiently separated essential supports as above, the
\mathcal{K}(H)
-valued inner product is well-defined and in fact vanishes on all of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{(n)}\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)
, showing that for
n<\lceil\delta/\varepsilon\rceil
we have
\left(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)\right)^{(n)}\neq\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))\otimes_{\mathrm{min}}\mathcal{K}(H)
.
∎

This is in line with the result obtained for tolerance relations on finite sets in Proposition
3.7
. In fact, that result can be derived as a special case from Theorem
4.11
. In order to see this, equip the finite set
X
with the graph metric obtained from the given relation
\mathcal{R}\subseteq X\times X
, then for any
1<\varepsilon<2
one readily sees that
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{R}
.

On physical grounds one could expect a relation between the operator systems
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{S^{1}})
for the circle at finite resolution
\varepsilon
and the Toeplitz operator system

obtained by spectral projection of the circle onto the first

4.2.1.
State space of
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})

It is well-known that
\mathcal{K}^{*}\cong\mathcal{L}^{1}
isometrically via the pairing
(\rho,T)\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\times\mathcal{K}\mapsto\tr\rho T
. This duality respects the natural ordering by positive elements on both sides in the sense that

\displaystyle\rho\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}\iff\tr\rho T\geq 0,\qquad\forall T\in\mathcal{K}_{+},

and also

\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{K}_{+}\iff\tr\rho T\geq 0,\qquad\forall\rho\in\mathcal{L}^{1}_{+}.

We then have as a special case of Theorem
2.18
.

Proposition 4.13
.

There is an isometrical order isomorphism
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{*}_{h}\cong\mathcal{L}^{1}_{h}/E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})^{\perp}_{h}
.

Corollary 4.14
.

Any state on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
can be written as
T\mapsto\tr\rho T
(
T\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
), in terms of a trace-class density operator
\rho=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}|\xi_{i}\rangle\langle\xi_{i}|
with
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
and
\xi_{i}\in L^{2}(X)
.

We see from Proposition
2.14
that for any pure state
\varphi
on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
, there exists a pure state on
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
that extends
\varphi
. Since pure states on the compacts are given by vector states, we find that any pure state
\varphi
on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
can be written as

\varphi:A\mapsto\langle\psi,A\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}

for some
\psi\in L^{2}(X)
of norm one. In general, the correspondence between
\mathcal{P}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))
and
\mathbb{P}(L^{2}(X))
is not expected to be one to one. However, this happens if one restricts to pure states on
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
whose support is
\varepsilon
-connected in the following sense.

Definition 4.15
.

A subset
Y
of a metric space
(X,d)
is
\varepsilon
-connected if and only if the equivalence relation on
Y
generated by the relation
d(y,y^{\prime})<\varepsilon
contains only one equivalence class.

We let
\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(L^{2}(X))\subset\mathbb{P}(L^{2}(X))
be the subset of
\mathbb{P}(L^{2}(X))
formed of pure states
|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
where the essential support of
\psi
is
\varepsilon
-connected.

Lemma 4.16
.

The restriction map
\rho:\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(L^{2}(X))\to\mathcal{S}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))
from
\varepsilon
-connected pure states on
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
to states on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is injective.

Proof.

The state
\rho(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
is given by the pairing

\rho(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)(k)=\int k(x,y)\overline{\psi}(x)\psi(y)d\mu(x)d\mu(y)

which determines the product
\overline{\psi}(x)\psi(y)
almost everywhere, for
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
. This shows that if
\rho(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=\rho(|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|)
, then one has
|\psi(x)|=|\psi^{\prime}(x)|
almost everywhere and there exists a measurable map
u:X\to U(1)
well determined on the support of
\psi
, such that
\psi^{\prime}(x)=u(x)\psi(x)
. Let us show that
u
is constant. One has

d(x,y)<\varepsilon\Rightarrow\overline{\psi}(x)\psi(y)=\overline{\psi}^{\prime}(x)\psi^{\prime}(y)

and this shows that for
x,y
in the essential support of
\psi
one has

d(x,y)<\varepsilon\Rightarrow u(x)=u(y).

Thus since the support of
\psi
is
\varepsilon
-connected, one gets that
u
is constant. ∎

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
X
be a metric space with a measure
\mu
with full support.
The restriction of a pure state
\psi\in\mathbb{P}(L^{2}(X))
to
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is pure if and only if
\psi
is
\varepsilon
-connected.

Proof.

Assume first that
\psi
is not
\varepsilon
-connected. Let
S
be the essential support of
\psi
and
S=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
a partition of
S
in two subsets such that the distance
d(S_{1},S_{2})\geq\varepsilon
. With
\psi=|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|
, let
\xi_{j}=1_{S_{j}}\xi
so that
\xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}
and one has

d(S_{1},S_{2})\geq\varepsilon\Rightarrow\langle T\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\rangle=0,\ \forall i\neq j,\ \forall T\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}).

One thus gets that

\psi(T)=\langle T\xi_{1},\xi_{1}\rangle+\langle T\xi_{2},\xi_{2}\rangle,\ \forall T\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}),

and one gets that the restriction of
\psi
to
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is not pure since it is the non-trivial convex combination of the normalized states
|\xi_{j}\rangle\langle\xi_{j}|\times\|\xi_{j}\|^{-2}
.

For the converse, assume that the essential support
S
of
\xi
is
\varepsilon
-connected. The vector state defined by
\xi
is pure if we have

\langle\eta,T\eta\rangle\leq\langle\xi,T\xi\rangle;\qquad\forall T\geq 0,T\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})\implies\eta\in\mathbb{C}\xi.

Consider then such an
\eta
and note that the inequality holds for any positive
T\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})\subset E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
for any finite partial
\varepsilon
-partition
P
.

For any measurable subset
U\subset X\setminus S
of sufficiently small diameter but with non-zero measure, we may consider the finite partial
\varepsilon
-partition
P=\{U\}
. Since we have
e_{P}\in E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
we find that
\langle\eta,e_{P}\eta\rangle\leq\langle\xi,e_{P}\xi\rangle=0
. In other words,
\int_{U}|\eta|^{2}\leq\int_{U}|\xi|^{2}=0
and we may conclude that the essential support of
\eta
is contained in
S
.

For any two
x,y\in S
so that
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
we may consider sets
U,V
of diameter
<\varepsilon
with
x\in U,y\in V
and
U\times V\subset\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
. By the assumption on the support of
\mu
they may be taken to have non-zero measure. Then we find for the partial
\varepsilon
-partitions
P=\{U,V\}
that the vector state restricted to
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
becomes
e_{P}\xi=:(\xi_{U},\xi_{V})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. By Proposition
4.8
this is a pure state on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}^{P})
, whence the vector
e_{P}\eta
is a complex multiple of
e_{P}\xi
.

Let us now fix
V
and suppose that
\eta_{V}=c\xi_{V}
for some
c
where we assume that
\xi_{V}\neq 0
. Now for any
U^{\prime}\subset U
we still have
U^{\prime}\times V\subset\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}
so that the argument from the previous paragraph applies and we find that
\eta_{U^{\prime}}=c\xi_{U^{\prime}}
. We conclude that
\eta=c\eta
in a neighborhood of
x
.

Repeating this argument to any point at distance
<\varepsilon
from
x
and using
\varepsilon
-connectedness of
S
we find that
\eta
is a constant multiple of
\xi
on the essential support of
\xi
.
∎

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
X
be a metric space with a measure
\mu
with full support. The restriction map
\rho:\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}(L^{2}(X))\to\mathbb{\mathcal{P}}(E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}))
from
\varepsilon
-connected pure states on
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
to pure states on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
is bijective.

Proof.

First Lemma
4.17
shows that
\rho
maps
\varepsilon
-connected pure states to pure states. Lemma
4.16
shows that
\rho
is injective. Let us show that it is surjective. By Hahn–Banach extension there exists, given a pure state
\varphi
on
E(\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon})
a pure state
\psi
on
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(X))
whose restriction gives
\varphi
. By Lemma
4.17
the pure state
\psi
is
\varepsilon
-connected. ∎

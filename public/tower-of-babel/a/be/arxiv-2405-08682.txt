Norms of spherical averaging operators for some geometric group actions
Bogdan Nica

Abstract
. We obtain asymptotic estimates for the ℓ p \ell^{p} -operator norm of spherical averaging operators associated to certain geometric group actions. The motivating example is the case of Gromov hyperbolic groups, for which we obtain asymptotically sharp estimates. We deduce asymptotic lower bounds for the combinatorial expansion of spheres.

Primary: 43A15, 20F67. Secondary: 22D15, 22D20, 20F65, 20F69.

1.
Averaging operators

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group, and let
S\subseteq\Gamma
be a non-empty finite subset. The
averaging operator

\lambda_{S}
is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{S}=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S}\lambda(g)

where
\lambda
is the left regular representation of
\Gamma
. More explicitly,
\lambda_{S}
acts on complex-valued functions defined on
\Gamma
, sending a function
\phi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
to the function
\lambda_{S}(\phi):\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\lambda_{S}(\phi)(h)=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S}\phi(g^{-1}h).

The averaging operator
\lambda_{S}
is a natural and fundamental operator, which appears under several guises. In the terminology of random walks, it is the transition operator for the simple random walk defined by
S
. In graph theory,
\lambda_{S}
is closely related to the discrete Laplacian. Averaging operators also appear in harmonic analysis, and in ergodic theory.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
. The averaging operator
\lambda_{S}
is a bounded operator on
\ell^{p}\Gamma
, and the broad problem we are interested in is that of
computing or estimating the
p
-operator norm
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}
.

There are four general facts that frame this broad problem.

Firstly,

(1)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\leq 1,

with equality for
p=1
. We will therefore restrict to
p\in(1,\infty)
in what follows. More interestingly, equality in (
1
) also holds when
\Gamma
is an amenable group. Kesten
[
10
]
originally proved this fact in the case
p=2
; the same argument, a nice application of Følner sets, works in fact for each
p\in(1,\infty)
. The upshot is that our problem is interesting only when
\Gamma
is non-amenable.

Secondly, we have the trivial lower bound

(2)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\geq|S|^{-1/p^{\prime}}

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p\in(1,\infty)
, given by
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. Indeed,
\lambda_{S}
maps
\mathbb{1}_{e}
, the characteristic function of the identity of
\Gamma
, to
|S|^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{1}_{S}
, the normalized characteristic function of the subset
S
. It follows that
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\geq\||S|^{-1}\cdot\mathbb{1}_{S}\|_{p}=|S|^{-1+1/p}=|S|^{-1/p^{\prime}}
, as claimed.

Thirdly, we have the duality formula

(3)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}=\|\lambda_{S^{-1}}\|_{p^{\prime}\to p^{\prime}}.

This can be deduced from the identity
\langle\lambda_{S}(\phi),\psi\rangle=\langle\phi,\lambda_{S^{-1}}(\psi)\rangle
, for
\phi\in\ell^{p}\Gamma
and
\psi\in\ell^{p^{\prime}}\Gamma
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\ell^{p}\Gamma\times\ell^{p^{\prime}}\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is the duality pairing given by
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\phi(h)\>\overline{\psi(h)}
. The duality formula (
3
) allows a back-and-forth between the two ranges of exponents,
p\in(1,2]
and
p\in[2,\infty)
. From now on, we focus on the range
p\in(1,2]
. Our preference for the lower range stems from a circle of ideas, results and open questions addressing the Banach algebra completions of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
under the left regular representation
\lambda
, for varying exponents
p
. The classical completions occur at
p=1
, yielding the Banach algebra
\ell^{1}\Gamma
, respectively at
p=2
, yielding the reduced
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{r}\Gamma
. The group Banach algebras indexed by
p\in(1,2)
interpolate between the ‘easy’
\ell^{1}\Gamma
and the ‘hard’
C^{*}_{r}\Gamma
. First introduced by Carl Herz in the early 1970’s, this interpolating family is currently enjoying some renewed interest, see Liao–Yu
[
12
]
, Phillips
[
16
]
, Samei–Wiersma
[
19
,
20
]
, Gardella–Thiel
[
7
]
.

Fourthly, for
p\in(1,2)
we have the interpolation bound

(4)

\displaystyle\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\leq\|\lambda_{S}\|^{2/p^{\prime}}_{2\to 2}.

Indeed, Riesz-Thorin interpolation yields
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\leq\|\lambda_{S}\|^{1-\theta}_{1\to 1}\>\|\lambda_{S}\|^{\theta}_{2\to 2}
, where
\theta
is given by
1/p=(1-\theta)+\theta/2
. Thus
\theta=2/p^{\prime}
, and we recall that
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{1\to 1}=1
. The interpolation bound (
4
) is promising–it implies that an upper bound for the
2
-operator norm
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{2\to 2}
leads to upper bounds for
p
-operator norm
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}
in the range
p\in(1,2)
. It turns out, however, that the interpolation approach may not provide optimal upper bounds for
p
-operator norms; this is one takeaway from the results described in the next section.

We close this introductory section with the remark that, in certain geometric situations, it may be more natural to consider the right averaging operator
\rho_{S}
; this acts by sending a function
\phi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
to the function
\rho_{S}(\phi):\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\rho_{S}(\phi)(h)=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S}\phi(hg).

The left averaging operator
\lambda_{S}
and the right averaging operator
\rho_{S}
are conjugate on
\ell^{p}\Gamma
via the isometric isomorphism
J:\ell^{p}\Gamma\to\ell^{p}\Gamma
,
J\phi(h)=\phi(h^{-1})
. In particular,
\lambda_{S}
and
\rho_{S}
have the same
p
-operator norm.

2.
Spherical averaging operators on hyperbolic groups

Nearly all results on
p
-operator norms of averaging operators that can be found in the literature address the case when
p=2
. The
2
-operator norm of
\lambda_{S}
,
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{2\to 2}
, is often referred to as a spectral radius for
S
. A classical direction is that of taking
S
to be a symmetric generating set of
\Gamma
. The seminal result of Kesten
[
9
]
settles the case when
\Gamma
is a free group and
S
is the standard symmetric generating set. Kesten-type formulas or estimates have been pursued for a number of other finitely generated groups, and natural symmetric generating sets. A very interesting study case is that of surface groups, see
[
2
,
13
,
23
,
1
,
8
]
.

It is natural to consider the averaging operator
\lambda_{S}
over ‘geometric’ subsets
S
. Our main focus is on the case when
S
is a ‘sphere’ in
\Gamma
. Let
l:\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
be a proper length function; for instance,
l
could be the word-length defined by a finite symmetric generating subset of
\Gamma
. The
sphere
of radius
n
is the (symmetric) subset of
\Gamma
given by

\displaystyle S(n)=\{g\in\Gamma:l(g)=n\}.

Similarly, the
ball
of radius
n
is the (symmetric) subset of
\Gamma
given by
B(n)=\{g\in\Gamma:l(g)\leq n\}
. Averaging operators over balls are also considered in this paper, though they only play a supporting role.

In the case when
\Gamma
is a free group endowed with the standard word-length, the
p
-operator norm of
\lambda_{S(n)}
has been explicitly computed by Cohen
[
5
]
for
p=2
, respectively by Pytlik
[
17
,
18
]
for arbitrary
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Theorem 2.1

(Cohen–Pytlik)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a free group on
k\geq 2
generators, endowed with the standard word-length. Then

(5)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}=\big((1-1/k)n+1\big)\cdot(2k-1)^{-n/2},

and, for
p\in(1,2)
,

(6)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}=C(1/p)\cdot(2k-1)^{-n/p^{\prime}}+C(1/p^{\prime})\cdot(2k-1)^{-n/p}

where
C(t)
is an explicit rational expression in terms of
k
and
t
.

Kesten’s formula, mentioned above, is the case
n=1
of Cohen’s formula (
5
). Pytlik’s approach in
[
17
]
also recovers Cohen’s formula (
5
). But the key outcome of Pytlik’s approach is formula (
6
); this is the only result known to us that addresses the
p
-operator norm of an averaging operator for
p\neq 2
.

The Cohen–Pytlik formulas are undoubtedly remarkable. It has to be acknowledged, however, that such exact computations are extremely rare, and they owe to very special circumstances–free group, standard word-length. (There is one more exact computation we know of, due to Cartwright and Młotkowski
[
3
]
; it addresses groups acting on triangle buildings, for
p=2
.) It is not at all clear how to extend the Cohen–Pytlik formulas to other groups. The fragility of the formulas (
5
) and (
6
) is apparent, even over a free group, as soon as we consider changing the word-length, or replacing the sphere
S(n)
by the ball
B(n)
.

Our contention is that the Cohen–Pytlik exact formulas become more meaningful when viewed in a simplified, asymptotic form. Let us explain what asymptotic equivalence means, for we will use it throughout the paper. Given two functions
f_{1},f_{2}:\mathbb{N}\to(0,\infty)
, we write
f_{1}\asymp f_{2}
if there are positive constants
c,C
so that
cf_{1}(n)\leq f_{2}(n)\leq Cf_{1}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The asymptotic viewpoint on the Cohen–Pytlik exact formulas is in agreement with the general philosophy of geometric group theory; after all, word-lengths on finitely generated groups are asymptotically equivalent in an analogous way.

Theorem 2.2

(Cohen–Pytlik, asymptotic form)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-abelian free group, endowed with the standard word-length. Then

(7)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}

\displaystyle\asymp(n+1)\>|S(n)|^{-1/2},

and, for
p\in(1,2)
,

(8)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}

\displaystyle\asymp|S(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}.

To clarify, formulas (
7
) and (
8
) describe the behavior of the
p
-operator norms
\|\lambda_{S(n)}\|_{p\to p}
as
n
increases; the implied multiplicative constants depend on the rank of
\Gamma
and on
p
, but not on
n
.

We highlight three aspects of the formulas (
7
) and (
8
). Firstly, they exhibit an interesting discrepancy: the case
p=2
has an additional radial factor. We do not have a conceptual explanation for this discontinuity at
p=2
. Secondly, they witness that interpolation may not yield optimal bounds, for the estimate (
8
) is better than what (
4
) and (
7
) would predict. Thirdly, we see that, for
p\in(1,2)
, the estimate (
8
) asymptotically matches the trivial lower bound (
2
).

In turns out that the Cohen–Pytlik asymptotic formulas (
7
) and (
8
) hold, much more generally, for Gromov hyperbolic groups equipped with any word-length function. The case
p=2
is an instance of a more general result from
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, endowed with a word-length. Then:

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}

\displaystyle\asymp(n+1)\>|S(n)|^{-1/2}.

In this paper we handle the case
p\in(1,2)
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, endowed with a word-length, and let
p\in(1,2)
. Then:

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}

\displaystyle\asymp|S(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}.

The strategy towards Theorem
2.4
is very different from the one used in
[
15
]
in order to obtain Theorem
2.3
. In the case
p=2
, the upper bound is granted by property RD, and the issue is to obtain a matching lower bound; this is achieved by using the boundary of
\Gamma
. For the range
p\in(1,2)
, it is the lower bound that is known–namely, it is the trivial lower bound (
2
)–and we are aiming for a matching upper bound. It is unclear whether the boundary of
\Gamma
could be used to this end. Instead, we adapt Pytlik’s approach to (
6
). Although the proof of Theorem
2.4
is ultimately carried out in a different way, our use of the Busemann cocyle (a group cocycle on
\Gamma
) is inspired by Pytlik’s use of the Poisson kernel (a group cocycle on the boundary of
\Gamma
). By avoiding the boundary, we are actually able to formulate a much more general result–see Theorem
5.3
.

Estimates for
p
-operator norms of an averaging operator
\lambda_{S}
have an interesting by-product: estimates for the combinatorial expansion of the subset
S
. Here is an informal description of what this means; we refer to Section
6
for the precise definition. Consider a product set
SX=\{sx:s\in S,x\in X\}\subseteq\Gamma
, where
X
is an arbitrary finite subset of
\Gamma
; trivially, we have the upper bound
|SX|\leq|S||X|
. The combinatorial expansion of
S
encodes lower bounds for
|SX|
relative to
|X|
, uniformly in
X
.

Informally, the next result says that the sequence of spheres in a hyperbolic group forms an asymptotic expander.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, endowed with a word-length. Then there exists a constant
c\in(0,1)
such that, for each
n
, we have

|S(n)X|\geq c|S(n)||X|

for any finite subset
X\subseteq\Gamma
.

The proofs of Theorems
2.4
and
2.5
are completed in Section
7
.

3.
The cocycle bound

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete countable group, and
p\in(1,\infty)
. The left regular representation
\lambda
of
\Gamma
on
\ell^{p}\Gamma
is the isometric representation given by
\lambda(g)\phi=g.\phi
, where
g.\phi(h)=\phi(g^{-1}h)
. We extend
\lambda
by linearity to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
, setting

\displaystyle\lambda(a)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a(g)\lambda(g)

for any finitely supported function
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
.

The main result of this section is an upper bound on the
p
-operator norm
\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}
, in terms of an additional ingredient.

Let
\kappa:\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
be a cocycle. This means that the identity

(9)

\displaystyle\kappa(g_{1}g_{2})=\kappa(g_{1})+g_{1}.\kappa(g_{2})

holds for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\Gamma
. The cocycle identity implies that
\kappa(1)=0
, and that

(10)

\displaystyle\kappa(g^{-1})=-g^{-1}.\kappa(g)

for all
g\in\Gamma
.

Let the
\kappa
-norm
of a finitely supported function
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
be given as follows:

(11)

\displaystyle N_{\kappa}(a)=\sup_{h\in\Gamma}\>\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\>e^{\kappa(g)(h)}=\bigg\|\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\>e^{\kappa(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

Evidently,
N_{\kappa}
is a (
\mathbb{C}
-vector space) norm on
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
; this holds for any map
\kappa:\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
. When
\kappa
is a cocycle, it can be checked that
N_{\kappa}
is also submultiplicative. Therefore the
\kappa
-norm
N_{\kappa}
is an algebra norm on
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
.

For the trivial cocycle
\kappa=0
we have
N_{\kappa}(a)=\|a\|_{1}
. For a general cocycle
\kappa
, we might think of the
\kappa
-norm
N_{\kappa}
as a twisted
\ell^{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
, with conjugate exponent
p^{\prime}
. For any finitely supported function
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

(12)

\displaystyle\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\leq N_{p\kappa}(a^{*})^{1/p}\>N_{p^{\prime}\kappa}(a)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Here
a^{*}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is the function given by
a^{*}(g)=\overline{a(g^{-1})}
. For the trivial cocycle
\kappa=0
, the bound (
12
) is simply the trivial upper bound
\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\leq\|a\|_{1}
. Thus (
12
) could be thought of as a twisting of the trivial upper bound.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\ell^{p}\Gamma
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda(a)\phi(h)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a(g)\phi(g^{-1}h).

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have:

\displaystyle\big|\lambda(a)\phi(h)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|^{1/p^{\prime}}e^{\kappa(g)(h)}\>\big|a(g)\big|^{1/p}\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|\>e^{-\kappa(g)(h)}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\>e^{p^{\prime}\kappa(g)(h)}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\Big(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(h)}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq N_{p^{\prime}\kappa}(a)^{1/p^{\prime}}\>\Big(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(h)}\Big)^{1/p}.

We deduce that

\displaystyle\|\lambda(a)\phi\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\big|\lambda(a)\phi(h)\big|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq N_{p^{\prime}\kappa}(a)^{1/p^{\prime}}\>\Big(\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(h)}\Big)^{1/p}.

Next, we estimate the latter double sum. We write

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(h)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(h)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\big|\phi(h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(gh)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\Big(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\>e^{-p\kappa(g)(gh)}\Big)\>\big|\phi(h)\big|^{p}.

In the second step of the above computation, we have made the change of variable
h:=gh
for each
g\in\Gamma
. Now, by (
10
), we have
-\kappa(g)(gh)=\kappa(g^{-1})(h)
and so

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\>e^{-p\kappa(g)(gh)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\>e^{p\kappa(g^{-1})(h)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a^{*}(g)\big|\>e^{p\kappa(g)(h)}\leq N_{p\kappa}(a^{*}).

We have made another change of variable along the way, namely
g:=g^{-1}
. We infer that

\displaystyle\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big|a(g)\big|\big|\phi(g^{-1}h)\big|^{p}e^{-p\kappa(g)(h)}\leq N_{p\kappa}(a^{*})\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\big|\phi(h)\big|^{p}

and, consequently, that

\displaystyle\|\lambda(a)\phi\|_{p}\leq N_{p^{\prime}\kappa}(a)^{1/p^{\prime}}\>N_{p\kappa}(a^{*})^{1/p}\>\|\phi\|_{p}.

The desired bound (
12
) follows.
∎

The above proof only used the relation (
10
), not the full power of the cocycle identity (
9
).
We find it natural to work with cocycles, however, since they are the main source for the relation (
10
). Additionally, as we have noted, the
\kappa
-norm is an algebra norm on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
whenever
\kappa
is a cocycle.

In keeping with our asymptotic viewpoint, we should consider the stability of the cocycle bound (
12
). Here are two remarks. Firstly, if
\tilde{\kappa}
is a cocycle which is a uniformly bounded perturbation of
\kappa
, meaning that
\sup_{g\in\Gamma}\|\tilde{\kappa}(g)-\kappa(g)\|_{\infty}<\infty
, then the upper bound in (
12
) for the two cocycles,
\tilde{\kappa}
and
\kappa
, is asymptotically the same. Secondly, the cocycle bound (
12
) can be extended without difficulty to quasi-cocycles. Recall, this means that
\kappa:\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
satisfies the cocycle identity (
9
) up to a uniformly bounded error:
\sup_{g,h\in\Gamma}\|\kappa(gh)-\kappa(g)-g.\kappa(h)\|_{\infty}<\infty
.

4.
A geometric application of the cocycle bound

As before, let
\Gamma
be a discrete countable group, and
p\in(1,\infty)
. We pursue the following instance of the cocycle setup, discussed in the previous section: the finitely supported function
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is
\mathbb{1}_{S}
, the characteristic function of the subset
S
, while the cocycle is a classical one, of geometric origin.

Let
X
be a metric space on which
\Gamma
acts properly by isometries. The distance between two points
x
and
y
in
X
is denoted by
d(x,y)
. The
Busemann cocycle

\beta:\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
with respect to a basepoint
o\in X
is defined as follows: for each
g\in\Gamma
,
\beta(g):\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is the map

h\mapsto d(o,ho)-d(go,ho).

We have, indeed, that
\beta(g)\in\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
; in fact,
\|\beta(g)\|_{\infty}=d(o,go)
. The cocycle identity,
\beta(g_{1}g_{2})=\beta(g_{1})+g_{1}.\beta(g_{2})
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in\Gamma
, is easily checked. Note also that a change of the basepoint
o
entails a uniformly bounded perturbation of the Busemann cocycle
\beta
. So, for the purposes of the cocycle bound (
12
), the dependence of the Busemann cocycle on the basepoint is harmless.

We will actually work with scalings of the Busemann cocycle, that is, cocycles of the form
\varepsilon\beta
where
\varepsilon
is some positive parameter. This flexibility will allow us to optimize the cocycle bound (
12
). Recall that we take
a=\mathbb{1}_{S}
, so
a^{*}=\mathbb{1}_{S^{-1}}
. For these choices, Lemma
3.1
implies the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\beta:\Gamma\to\ell^{\infty}\Gamma
be the Busemann cocycle with respect to a basepoint
o\in X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then for any finite subset
S\subseteq\Gamma
we have

(13)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\big\|_{p\to p}\leq\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S^{-1}}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}^{1/p}\>\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p^{\prime}\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Our next concern is, of course, that of upper bounding the norms appearing on the right-hand side of (
13
). We are mainly interested in
S
being a sphere or a ball in
\Gamma
, with respect to the length function
l(g)=d(o,go)
induced by some fixed basepoint
o\in X
. That is to say, we are interested in
S
being one of the following:

S_{o}(n)=\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,go)=n\},\qquad B_{o}(n)=\{g\in\Gamma:d(o,go)\leq n\}.

We now introduce some key geometric hypotheses on the metric space
X
. We start with a notation. Given two points
x,y\in X
and
\rho\geq 0
, we consider the rough geodesic segment

\displaystyle[x,y]_{\rho}=\big\{z\in X:d(x,z)+d(z,y)\leq d(x,y)+\rho\big\}.

Definition 4.2
.

A metric space
X
is
roughly modular
if there is some constant
\rho\geq 0
so that the following holds: for any three points
x,y,z\in X
, there exists a point

\displaystyle m\in[x,y]_{\rho}\cap[y,z]_{\rho}\cap[z,x]_{\rho}.

Such a point
m
is said to be a
rough median
of
x,y,z
.

Modularity, corresponding to the case
\rho=0
, is an already established terminology in graph theory. The adjective ‘rough’ is the functor which relaxes metric equalities by allowing an additive bounded error.

For the remainder of this section, the following
standing assumptions
on
X
are in place:

mod:

X
is a roughly modular graph, with rough modularity constant
\rho
;

pol(
d
):

there exists a non-negative integer
d
and
C^{\prime}>0
such that, for each
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\big|\{z\in[x,y]_{\rho}:d(x,z)=n\}\big|\leq C^{\prime}(n+1)^{d}.

Coincidentally, these geometric assumptions also feature in Lafforgue’s proof that cocompact lattices in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
satisfy property RD
[
11
, Defn.2.2]
; cf. the Chatterji–Ruane criterion
[
4
, Prop.1.7]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that the isometric action of
\Gamma
on
X
satisfies the condition

exp(
\delta
):

there exist
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that, for each
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\big|\{g\in\Gamma:d(x,gy)\leq n\}\big|\leq Ce^{\delta n}
.

Let
\beta
be the Busemann cocycle with respect to a basepoint
o\in X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then for any subset
S\subseteq B_{o}(n)
we have:

(14)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\preccurlyeq\begin{cases}(n+1)^{d}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon>\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon=\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon<\delta/2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume first that
S\subseteq S_{o}(n)
. Fix
h\in\Gamma
.

Note that
\beta(g)(h)=d(o,ho)-d(go,ho)
is an integer, as the distance on
X
is integer-valued. For each
g\in S
, we have
|\beta(g)(h)|\leq d(o,go)=n
. Thus, if we consider the horospherical set

H(j)=\{g\in\Gamma:\beta(g)(h)=j\},

then we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in S}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)(h)}=\sum_{j=-n}^{n}|S\cap H(j)|\>e^{p\varepsilon j}.

Let
g\in S\cap H(j)
, where
j\in\{-n,\dots,n\}
. Let
m=m(g,h)\in X
be a rough median point for the triple
o,go,ho
. For
x\in\{o,go,ho\}
, we introduce the shorthand
\delta(x)=d(m,x)
. See the sketch below.

\textstyle{o\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta(o)}
\textstyle{m\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta(ho)}
\textstyle{ho}
\textstyle{go\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta(go)}

We have

\displaystyle d(o,ho)\leq\delta(o)

\displaystyle+\delta(ho)\leq d(o,ho)+\rho,

\displaystyle d(go,ho)\leq\delta(go)

\displaystyle+\delta(ho)\leq d(go,ho)+\rho.

By subtracting the two inequalities, we see that

\delta(o)-\delta(go)\approx_{\rho}d(o,ho)-d(go,ho)=b(g)(h)=j,

where
\approx_{\rho}
denotes the relation of being within
\rho
of each other. On the other hand

\delta(o)+\delta(go)\approx_{\rho}d(o,go)=n.

The two near equalities combine to give

\delta(o)\approx_{\rho}(n+j)/2,\qquad\delta(go)\approx_{\rho}(n-j)/2.

In view of the hypothesis
pol(
d
)
on the graph
X
, the number of possibilities for
m
is at most a constant multiple of
((n+j)/2+1)^{d}
. Next, by using the hypothesis
exp(
\delta
)
on the action of
\Gamma
on
X
, we deduce that, for each such
m
, the number of possibilities for
g
is at most a constant multiple of
e^{\delta(n-j)/2}
. Overall, we find that

|S\cap H(j)|\preccurlyeq\big((n+j)/2+1\big)^{d}\>e^{\delta(n-j)/2}\asymp(n+j+1)^{d}\>e^{\delta(n-j)/2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in S}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)(h)}\preccurlyeq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-n}^{n}(n+j+1)^{d}\>e^{\delta(n-j)/2}\>e^{p\varepsilon j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2n}(j+1)^{d}\>e^{\delta(2n-j)/2}\>e^{p\varepsilon(j-n)}

\displaystyle=e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\>(j+1)^{d}\>e^{(p\varepsilon-\delta/2)j}.

Since the above estimate is uniform in
h\in\Gamma
, we have

(15)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\preccurlyeq e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\>(j+1)^{d}\>e^{(p\varepsilon-\delta/2)j}.

To estimate the latter sum, we use the following elementary asymptotics:

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{N}\>(j+1)^{d}\>e^{\tau j}\asymp\begin{cases}(N+1)^{d}\>e^{\tau N}&\textrm{ if }\tau>0\\[3.0pt]
(N+1)^{d+1}&\textrm{ if }\tau=0\\[3.0pt]
1&\textrm{ if }\tau<0\end{cases}

where the implicit multiplicative constants depend on
d
and
\tau
, but not on
N
. We deduce that the right-hand side of (
15
) has the following asymptotics:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}\>(2n+1)^{d}\>e^{(p\varepsilon-\delta/2)2n}\asymp(n+1)^{d}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon>\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}\>(2n+1)^{d+1}\asymp(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon=\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon<\delta/2.\end{cases}

To summarize: we have shown that, for a subset
S\subseteq S_{o}(n)
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\preccurlyeq\begin{cases}(n+1)^{d}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon>\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon=\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon<\delta/2.\end{cases}

We now address the general case when
S\subseteq B_{o}(n)
. By partitioning
S
into spherical pieces, we can upper-bound

(18)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S\cap S_{o}(k)}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}.

Using (
17
), and then (
16
) once again, we see that the right-hand side of (
18
) can be asymptotically bounded from above by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\>(k+1)^{d}\>e^{p\varepsilon k}\asymp(n+1)^{d}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon>\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
\sum_{k=0}^{n}\>(k+1)^{d+1}\>e^{p\varepsilon k}\asymp(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{p\varepsilon n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon=\delta/2,\\[3.0pt]
\sum_{k=0}^{n}\>e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)k}\asymp e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}&\textrm{ if }p\varepsilon<\delta/2.\end{cases}

All in all, we reach (
14
).
∎

By combining Lemma
4.3
with Lemma
4.1
, we derive the following asymptotic upper bounds. Note that the Busemann cocycle
\beta
no longer appears in the statement!

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that the isometric action of
\Gamma
on
X
satisfies the condition

exp(
\delta
):

there exist
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that, for each
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\big|\{g\in\Gamma:d(x,gy)\leq n\}\big|\leq Ce^{\delta n}
.

Then, for any subset
S\subseteq B_{o}(n)
, we have

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}e^{\delta n/p}&\textrm{ if }p\in(1,2),\\[3.0pt]
\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\big\|_{2\to 2}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{\delta n/2}&\textrm{ if }p=2.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
p\in(1,2)
, we set
\varepsilon=\delta/(pp^{\prime})
. Then
p\varepsilon=\delta/p^{\prime}<\delta/2
and
p^{\prime}\varepsilon=\delta/p>\delta/2
. According to (
14
), we have

\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S^{-1}}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\preccurlyeq e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n},\qquad\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p^{\prime}\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d}\>e^{p^{\prime}\varepsilon n}.

Hence, by (
13
)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq\big(e^{(\delta-p\varepsilon)n}\big)^{1/p}\cdot\big((n+1)^{d}\>e^{p^{\prime}\varepsilon n}\big)^{1/p^{\prime}}=(n+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}e^{\delta n/p}.

When
p=2
, we set
\varepsilon=\delta/4
. Then
p\varepsilon=\delta/2=p^{\prime}\varepsilon
, and (
14
) yields

\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S^{-1}}e^{p\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty},\;\bigg\|\sum_{g\in S}e^{p^{\prime}\varepsilon\beta(g)}\bigg\|_{\infty}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{\delta n/2}.

Now (
13
) says, quite simply, that

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\big\|_{2\to 2}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+1}\>e^{\delta n/2}

as well.
∎

The
p
-operator norm
\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\big\|_{p\to p}
is non-decreasing as a function of
S
. Therefore (
19
) is at its strongest when
S
is the whole ball
B_{o}(n)
. But this is not the only case of interest–we will also apply Theorem
4.4
to
S
being the sphere
S_{o}(n)
, or even an annulus contained in
B_{o}(n)
.

5.
Geometric actions and pure growth

We maintain the standing assumptions on
X
. But we now place a stronger requirement on the proper isometric action of
\Gamma
on
X
–namely, that the action be cocompact. A proper and cocompact isometric action is said to be a
geometric action
. When the action of
\Gamma
on
X
is geometric, the condition
exp(
\delta
)
is equivalent to

e(
\delta
):

for some exponent
\delta>0
we have
|B_{o}(n)|\preccurlyeq e^{\delta n}
.

Our main goal is that of obtaining asymptotic upper bounds for
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}
, the
p
-norm of the averaging operator
\lambda_{S}=|S|^{-1}\cdot\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})
. We therefore recast (
19
), which takes the form
\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S})\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq f(n)e^{\delta n/p}
, into
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq f(n)e^{\delta n/p}|S|^{-1}
. Now
e^{\delta n/p}|S|^{-1}\asymp|S|^{-1/p^{\prime}}
provided that
|S|\asymp e^{\delta n}
. In summary, we can deduce that
\|\lambda_{S}\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq f(n)|S|^{-1/p^{\prime}}
under the exponential growth assumption
|S|\asymp e^{\delta n}
.

This leads us to the next two results, our main applications of Theorem
4.4
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
X
, and that

b(
\delta
):

for some exponent
\delta>0
we have
|B_{o}(n)|\asymp e^{\delta n}
.

Then

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big\|\lambda_{B_{o}(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}\>|B_{o}(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}&\textrm{ if }p\in(1,2),\\[3.0pt]
\big\|\lambda_{B_{o}(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+1}\>|B_{o}(n)|^{-1/2}&\textrm{ if }p=2.\end{cases}

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
X
, and that

a(
\delta
):

for some exponent
\delta>0
and thickness
\theta\geq 0
, the annuli
A_{\theta}(n)=\{g\in\Gamma:n-\theta\leq d(o,go)\leq n+\theta\}
satisfy
|A_{\theta}(n)|\asymp e^{\delta n}
.

Then

(21)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big\|\lambda_{A_{\theta}(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}\>|A_{\theta}(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}&\textrm{ if }p\in(1,2),\\[3.0pt]
\big\|\lambda_{A_{\theta}(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+1}\>|A_{\theta}(n)|^{-1/2}&\textrm{ if }p=2.\end{cases}

We note that the
annular pure growth
condition
a(
\delta
)
implies the
ball pure growth
condition
b(
\delta
)
which, in turn, obviously implies the exponential growth condition
e(
\delta
)
. Let us also note that, owing to the cocompactness of the action, each one of the conditions
a(
\delta
)
,
b(
\delta
)
, and
e(
\delta
)
, is actually independent of the basepoint
o\in X
: if it holds for some basepoint, then it holds for any other basepoint.

In the next theorem, we highlight a significant instance of annular pure growth, namely the case of
spherical pure growth
. This special case has an additional feature: it yields asymptotic upper bounds for
p
-operator norms of
radial functions
, that is to say finitely supported functions
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
with the property that the value
a(g)
only depends on
d(o,go)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
X
, and that

s(
\delta
):

for some exponent
\delta>0
we have
|S_{o}(n)|\asymp e^{\delta n}
.

Then

(22)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\big\|\lambda_{S_{o}(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}\>|S_{o}(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}&\textrm{ if }p\in(1,2),\\[3.0pt]
\big\|\lambda_{S_{o}(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+1}\>|S_{o}(n)|^{-1/2}&\textrm{ if }p=2.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is a radial function supported in the ball
B_{o}(n)
, then

(23)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{(d+1)/p^{\prime}}\|a\|_{p}&\textrm{ if }p\in(1,2),\\[3.0pt]
\|\lambda(a)\|_{2\to 2}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{d+3/2}\>\|a\|_{2}&\textrm{ if }p=2.\end{cases}

There is a small price to pay for the generality of the radial bounds (
23
): compared to the spherical bounds (
22
) and the ball bounds (
20
), there is a slight degree loss in (
23
).

Proof.

The bounds (
22
) are an instance of the bounds (
21
), in the case when
\theta=0
. We only need to argue the radial bounds (
23
). We detail the case
p\in(1,2)
; the case
p=2
is very similar, and we leave it to the reader.

Let
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
be a radial function supported in the ball
B_{o}(n)
. We write

a=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\>\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)}.

By (
22
), we have
\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)})\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(k+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}\big\|\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)}\big\|_{p}
for each
k
. Therefore

\displaystyle\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\>\big\|\lambda(\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)})\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\>(k+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}\big\|\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)}\big\|_{p}.

Next, by means of Hölder’s inequality, we can estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|\>(k+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}\big\|\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)}\big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{d}\Bigg)^{1/p^{\prime}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|^{p}\big\|\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)}\big\|^{p}_{p}\Bigg)^{1/p}.

Since

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{d}\asymp(n+1)^{d+1},

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|^{p}\big\|\mathbb{1}_{S_{o}(k)}\big\|^{p}_{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_{k}|^{p}|S_{o}(k)|=\|a\|^{p}_{p},

we obtain
\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{(d+1)/p^{\prime}}\|a\|_{p}
, as claimed.
∎

In comparing the two upper bounds in (
22
), we might reiterate a point we made while discussing the Cohen–Pytlik asymptotic formulas (
7
) and (
8
): somewhat strikingly, the upper bounds in the range
p\in(1,2)
are significantly better than those obtained by interpolation from the endpoint
p=2
. Indeed, using (
22
) at
p=2
, and (
4
), we would get

\big\|\lambda_{S_{o}(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}\leq\big\|\lambda_{S_{o}(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}^{2/p^{\prime}}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{2(d+1)/p^{\prime}}\>|S_{o}(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}.

By comparison, (
22
) at
p\in(1,2)
has a polynomial factor of
(n+1)^{d/p^{\prime}}
.

The main novelty in Theorems
5.1
,
5.2
, and
5.3
is the asymptotic upper bounds in the range
p\in(1,2)
. The bounds at
p=2
are not exactly novel; they fall under the bigger scope of property RD (for Rapid Decay), which is known in the geometric situation exploited in this paper (cf.
[
4
,
21
]
). Our cocycle-based approach to these bounds is, however, novel. The other distinguishing feature has to do with the ‘degree of decay’. Most works dealing with property RD are content with
some
polynomial factor
(n+1)^{D}
, whose degree
D
often remains unspecified. Here we are very much interested in optimal degrees–an idea first put forth in
[
14
]
, and pursued further in
[
15
]
.

6.
Expansion

We begin with a general setup:
\Gamma
is a countable discrete group, and
S\subseteq\Gamma
is a fixed, non-empty, finite subset. For any non-empty finite subset
X\subseteq\Gamma
, we consider the product set
SX=\{sx:s\in S,x\in X\}\subseteq\Gamma
. We are interested in measuring the size of
SX
, relative to the size of
X
. Clearly,
|SX|\leq|S||X|
and
|SX|\geq|X|
.

Definition 6.1
.

The
expansion
of a finite subset
S\subseteq\Gamma
is defined as

e(S)=\inf\left\{\frac{|SX|}{|X|}:X\textrm{ non-empty finite subset of }\Gamma\right\}.

To put it differently,
e(S)
is the largest constant with the property that
|SX|\geq e(S)|X|
for each finite subset
X\subseteq\Gamma
. The generic estimate for the expansion of
S
is that

1\leq e(S)\leq|S|.

The notion of subset expansion, defined above, is a relative of well-known graph theoretical notions such as vertex expansion, and Cheeger constant. Despite its simple-minded formulation, we have not been able to locate it in the literature. Subset expansion, as defined above, is implicit in
[
21
, Sec.4]
; Sapir’s considerations therein are the starting point for this section.

Expansion is of topical interest in light of the following fact:
p
-operator norms for the averaging operator
\lambda_{S}
serve as lower bounds for the expansion of
S
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then

(24)

\displaystyle e(S)\geq\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|_{p\to p}^{-p^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Recall the duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\ell^{p}\Gamma\times\ell^{p^{\prime}}\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
, defined by
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\sum_{h\in\Gamma}\phi(h)\>\overline{\psi(h)}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\big|\langle\lambda_{S}(\phi),\psi\rangle\big|\leq\|\lambda_{S}(\phi)\|_{p}\cdot\|\psi\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|_{p\to p}\>\|\phi\|_{p}\|\psi\|_{p^{\prime}}.

Let
X\subseteq\Gamma
be a non-empty finite subset. By applying the above bound to
\phi=\mathbb{1}_{X}
and
\psi=\mathbb{1}_{SX}
, we find that

\big|\langle\lambda_{S}(\mathbb{1}_{X}),\mathbb{1}_{SX}\rangle\big|\leq\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|_{p\to p}\>|X|^{1/p}|SX|^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Now

\lambda_{S}(\mathbb{1}_{X})=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S}\mathbb{1}_{gX}

and so

\langle\lambda_{S}(\mathbb{1}_{X}),\mathbb{1}_{SX}\rangle=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{g\in S}\langle\mathbb{1}_{gX},\mathbb{1}_{SX}\rangle=|X|

since, for each
g\in S
, we have
\langle\mathbb{1}_{gX},\mathbb{1}_{SX}\rangle=|gX\cap SX|=|gX|=|X|
. Thus

|X|\leq\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|_{p\to p}\>|X|^{1/p}|SX|^{1/p^{\prime}},

which can be rearranged as

\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|^{-p^{\prime}}_{p\to p}\leq\frac{|SX|}{|X|}.

By taking the infimum over
X
, we obtain (
24
).
∎

The interpolation bound (
4
) says that

\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|_{p\to p}^{-p^{\prime}}\geq\big\|\lambda_{S}\big\|_{2\to 2}^{-2}

whenever
p\in(1,2)
. We therefore expect better lower bounds for
e(S)
by using (
24
) in the range
p\in(1,2)
rather than at the endpoint
p=2
.

By combining the above lemma with the results obtained in the previous section, we can deduce asymptotic lower bounds for the expansion of spheres, balls, or annuli. For the sake of simplicity, we only state the outcome in the case of spheres.

Theorem 6.3
.

Keep the notations and the assumptions of Theorem
5.3
. Then

e(S_{o}(n))\succcurlyeq\frac{|S_{o}(n)|}{(n+1)^{d}}.

In agreement with a point made above, it is critical to work with some
p
in the range
(1,2)
in order to deduce Theorem
6.3
. The bounds (
22
) give

\big\|\lambda_{S_{o}(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}^{-p^{\prime}}\succcurlyeq\frac{|S_{o}(n)|}{(n+1)^{d}}

for any
p\in(1,2)
, yet at
p=2
we only have the much weaker bound

\big\|\lambda_{S_{o}(n)}\big\|_{2\to 2}^{-2}\succcurlyeq\frac{|S_{o}(n)|}{(n+1)^{2(d+1)}}.

7.
Hyperbolic groups

The foremost example for us is the case when
\Gamma
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group. A choice of a finite, symmetric generating set for
\Gamma
defines a word length on
\Gamma
, as well as a Cayley graph
X
of
\Gamma
, on which
\Gamma
acts geometrically.

The first favorable fact is that the graph
X
is roughly modular, and satisfies
pol(
d
)
for
d=0
. We detail this fact in the following simple lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X
be a connected, uniformly finite, hyperbolic graph. Then

(i)

for
\rho=2\delta+2
, where
\delta\geq 0
is a hyperbolicity constant in the sense of Rips’s thin triangle condition, we have
[x,y]_{\rho}\cap[y,z]_{\rho}\cap[z,x]_{\rho}\neq\emptyset
for all
x,y,z\in X
;

(ii)

for each
\rho\geq 0
there exists
C^{\prime}>0
so that
|\{z\in[x,y]_{\rho}:d(x,z)=n\}|\leq C^{\prime}
for each
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We will use several times the following observation: if
\gamma_{xy}
is a (discrete) geodesic joining
x
to
y
, and
\mathrm{dist}(m,\gamma_{xy})\leq c
, then
d(x,m)+d(m,y)\leq d(x,y)+2c
.

(i) Let
x,y,z\in X
, and consider (discrete) geodesics
\gamma_{xy}
,
\gamma_{yz}
,
\gamma_{zx}
joining
x
to
y
,
y
to
z
, respectively
z
to
x
. Rips’s thin triangle condition says that
\mathrm{dist}(v,\gamma_{yz})\leq\delta
or
\mathrm{dist}(v,\gamma_{zx})\leq\delta
for every vertex
v
on
\gamma_{xy}
. It follows that there exists
v
on
\gamma_{xy}
such that
\mathrm{dist}(v,\gamma_{yz})\leq\delta+1
and
\mathrm{dist}(v,\gamma_{zx})\leq\delta+1
. By the above observation, we have

(25)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}d(y,v)+d(v,z)\leq d(y,z)+2(\delta+1),\\
d(z,v)+d(v,x)\leq d(z,x)+2(\delta+1).\end{cases}

In other words,
v\in[y,z]_{2\delta+2}
and
v\in[z,x]_{2\delta+2}
; obviously,
v\in[x,y]_{2\delta+2}
as well.

(ii) Let
\rho\geq 0
, and fix
x,y\in X
as well as a (discrete) geodesic
\gamma_{xy}
joining
x
to
y
. Pick
z\in[x,y]_{\rho}
. Consider again a point
v
on
\gamma_{xy}
satisfying (
25
). By adding the two inequalities we deduce that

d(x,y)+2d(z,v)\leq d(x,z)+d(z,y)+4(\delta+1);

as
d(x,z)+d(z,y)\leq d(x,y)+\rho
, it follows that
d(z,v)\leq 2(\delta+1)+\rho/2=:\rho^{\prime}
.

Now, if
z\in[x,y]_{\rho}
also satisfies
d(x,z)=n
, then
n-\rho^{\prime}\leq d(x,v)\leq n+\rho^{\prime}
. At most
2\rho^{\prime}+1=\rho+4\delta+5
vertices
v
satisfy this bound since, we recall,
v
lies on a geodesic
\gamma_{xy}
. For each such
v
there are at most
N_{\rho}
possibilities for
z
, where
N_{\rho}=\sup_{x\in X}|B_{\rho^{\prime}}(x)|
; the finiteness of
N_{\rho}
reflects the hypothesis that
X
is uniformly finite. All in all,
|\{z\in[x,y]_{\rho}:d(x,z)=n\}|\leq(\rho+4\delta+5)N_{\rho}
, uniformly in
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

The second favorable fact is that the Cayley graph
X
enjoys pure spherical growth:
|S(n)|\asymp e^{\delta n}
for some
\delta>0
. This is a result due to Coornaert
[
6
]
. We are therefore in position to apply Theorem
5.3
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, endowed with a word-length, and let
p\in(1,2)
. Then

(26)

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda_{S(n)}\big\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq|S(n)|^{-1/p^{\prime}}.

Furthermore, if
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is a radial function supported in the ball
B(n)
, then

(27)

\displaystyle\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\preccurlyeq(n+1)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|a\|_{p}.

The third favorable fact is that the asymptotic upper bound (
26
) exactly complements the trivial lower bound (
2
), thereby implying Theorem
2.4
.

As for expansion, Theorem
6.3
implies the following result–a restatement of which is Theorem
2.5
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary hyperbolic group, endowed with a word-length. Then

e(S(n))\succcurlyeq|S(n)|.

Once again, this exactly complements the trivial upper bound
e(S(n))\leq|S(n)|
.

8.
Final remarks

Remark 8.1
.

Our motivating example in estimating
p
-operator norms for spherical averaging operators was the case of hyperbolic groups. The end result, Theorem
5.3
, is quite a bit more general, and hyperbolic groups appear–in retrospect–as the simplest case. Pure exponential growth for balls, spheres, or annuli of radius
n
, of the form
e^{\delta n}
, can be generalized to mixed growth–that is, growth of the form
(n+1)^{\alpha}\>e^{\delta n}
. The techniques of this paper clearly go through. This should lead to estimates of
p
-operator norms for spherical averaging operators on cocompact lattices in
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
(cf. Lafforgue
[
11
]
).

Remark 8.2
.

Other contexts in which our results seem applicable include groups acting on finite dimensional CAT
(0)
cube complexes, and groups that are hyperbolic relative to a family of subgroups of polynomial growth (cf. Chatterji–Ruane
[
4
]
). Under additional hypotheses, one also has pure annular growth for these families of groups by results of Yang
[
22
]
.

Yet another context is that of groups which are cocompact lattices in products of hyperbolic graphs. The Cartesian product
X_{1}\square X_{2}
of two graphs
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
is the graph with vertex set
X_{1}\times X_{2}
, and edges defined as follows:
(x_{1},x_{2})
is adjacent to
(y_{1},y_{2})
if
x_{1}=y_{1}
and
x_{2}
is adjacent to
y_{2}
, or if
x_{1}
is adjacent to
y_{1}
and
x_{2}=y_{2}
. The combinatorial distance in
X_{1}\square X_{2}
is the sum metric
d\big((x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\big)=d_{X_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1})+d_{X_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2})
. As a metric space,
X_{1}\square X_{2}
is often referred to as the
\ell^{1}
-product of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
. The main point is that our standing assumptions are stable under taking Cartesian products. Specifically, we have the following fact: if
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are roughly modular graphs, satisfying
pol(
d_{1}
)
respectively
pol(
d_{2}
)
, then their Cartesian product
X_{1}\square X_{2}
is a roughly modular graph satisfying
pol(
d_{1}+d_{2}+1
)
. In particular, the Cartesian product of
d-1
uniformly finite, hyperbolic graphs is roughly modular and satisfies
pol(
d
)
.

Remark 8.3
.

Liao and Yu
[
12
, Defn.4.1]
have introduced the following definition: a group
\Gamma
endowed with a length function has
property RD
p
, where
1<p\leq 2
, if there exist constants
C>0
and
D\geq 0
such that

\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\leq C(n+1)^{D}\|a\|_{p}

for all functions
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
supported in the ball
B(n)
.

For
p=2
, this is the usual property RD; furthermore, it can be shown
[
12
, Thm.4.4]
that property RD implies property RD
p
for all
p\in(1,2]
. This has implications for the K-theory of Banach algebra completions of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
for varying exponents
p\in(1,2]
.

From an analytic standpoint, it is interesting to find the optimal degree
D
in the RD
p
bound, and to understand its dependence on
p
. Theorem
5.3
gives RD
p
-type bounds for constant spherical functions and, more generally, for radial functions, though the approach of this paper does not seem well-suited for the full RD
p
bound. Nonetheless, the radial RD
p
bounds suggest that the optimal degree
D
in the range
p\in(1,2)
is below what interpolation from the endpoint
p=2
would predict.

Specifically, it seems likely that, for a non-elementary hyperbolic group
\Gamma
and
p\in(1,2)
, the optimal degree is
D=1/p^{\prime}
; namely, the following should hold: there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\lambda(a)\|_{p\to p}\leq C(n+1)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|a\|_{p}

for all functions
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
supported in the ball
B(n)
. This is what we establish in (
27
) for radial functions. For comparison, we note that at the endpoint
p=2
the optimal degree is
D=3/2
, and it is attained by radial functions
[
15
]
.

Remark 8.4
.

Asymptotic lower bounds for expansion, as in Theorem
6.3
, appear fairly naturally in connection with asymptotic upper bounds for
p
-operator norms of averaging operators. It would be interesting, however, to derive the bound of Theorem
6.3
–as well as improvements and variations thereof–in a direct, combinatorial way, without appealing to functional-analytic detours. The case of hyperbolic groups (Theorem
7.3
) seems particularly appealing in this respect.

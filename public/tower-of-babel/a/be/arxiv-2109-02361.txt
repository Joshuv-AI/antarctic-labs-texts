Representations of the Yangians associated with Lie superalgebras 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
A. I. Molev

Abstract
We classify the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the Yangians associated with the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 1 | 2 ​ n \mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n} in terms of the Drinfeld polynomials. The arguments rely on the description of the representations in the particular case n = 1 n=1 obtained in our previous work.



1
Introduction

The finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
associated with a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
were classified by
Drinfeld
[
5
]
. The arguments rely on the work of Tarasov
[
9
]

on the particular case of
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
, where the classification was carried over
in the language of monodromy matrices within the quantum inverse scattering method; see

[
7
, Sec. 3.3]
for a detailed adapted exposition of these results.
This description of the representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
,
along with some other low rank cases,
should also play an essential role
in the classification of the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of
the Yangians associated with simple Lie superalgebras.
One of these cases was considered in our previous work
[
8
]
, where
the representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})

were described.

These two basic cases turn out to be sufficient to complete the classification in the case of
the Yangians associated with the
orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}
. We prove in this paper
that, similar to the classification results of
[
5
]
,
the finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

are in one-to-one correspondence with the
n
-tuples of monic polynomials

(P_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u))
, and so we call
them the
Drinfeld polynomials
.

To describe the results in more detail, recall that
the Yangian

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
, as
introduced by Arnaudon
et al.

[
1
]
, can be considered
as a quotient
of the extended Yangian
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
defined via an
RTT
relation.
A standard argument shows that every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
is a highest weight representation. It is isomorphic
to the irreducible quotient
L(\lambda(u))
of the Verma module
M(\lambda(u))

associated with an
(n+1)
-tuple
\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\dots,\lambda_{n+1}(u))
of formal series

\lambda_{i}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]]
. The tuple is called the
highest weight

of the representation.
The key step in the classification is to find
the conditions on the highest weight for
the representation
L(\lambda(u))

to be finite-dimensional. The required necessary conditions are derived by
induction from those for the associated
actions of the Yangians
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{2})
and
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2})
on the respective
cyclic spans of the highest vector
of
L(\lambda(u))
. The sufficiency of these conditions is verified by constructing the

fundamental representations
of the Yangian
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{M|2n})
; cf.
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
.
The following is our main result.

Main Theorem
.

Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

is isomorphic to
L(\lambda(u))
for a certain highest weight
\lambda(u)
.
The representation
L(\lambda(u))

is finite-dimensional if and only if

\frac{\lambda_{i+1}(u)}{\lambda_{i}(u)}=\frac{P_{i}(u+1)}{P_{i}(u)},\hskip 20.00003pti=1,\dots,n,

(1.1)

for some monic polynomials
P_{i}(u)
in
u
. The finite-dimensional irreducible
representations of the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
are in a one-to-one correspondence with
the
n
-tuples of
monic polynomials
(P_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u))
.

Definitions and preliminaries

For any integer
n\geqslant 1
introduce the
involution
i\mapsto i^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}=2n-i+2

\{1,2,\dots,2n+1\}
.

\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}

\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}

\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt

\bar{\imath}=\begin{cases}1\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n,n^{\prime},\dots,1^{\prime},\\

The endomorphism algebra
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}
gets a
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}
-gradation with
the parity of the matrix unit
e_{ij}
found by

\bar{\imath}+\bar{\jmath}\mod 2
.

We will consider even square matrices with entries in
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}
-graded algebras, their

(i,j)
entries will have the parity
\bar{\imath}+\bar{\jmath}\mod 2
.
The algebra of
even matrices over a superalgebra
\mathcal{A}
will be identified with the tensor product algebra

{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes\mathcal{A}
, so that a matrix
A=[a_{ij}]
is regarded as the element

A=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}e_{ij}\otimes a_{ij}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes\mathcal{A}.

We will use the involutive matrix
super-transposition

t
defined by

(A^{t})_{ij}=A_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{i}\theta_{j}
,
where we set

\theta_{i}=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}1\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n+1,\\
-1\qquad\text{for}\quad i=n+2,\dots,2n+1.\end{cases}

This super-transposition is associated with the bilinear form on the space
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}

defined by the anti-diagonal matrix
G=[\delta_{ij^{\prime}}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{i}]
.
We will also regard
t
as the linear map

t:{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\to{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n},\hskip 20.00003pte_{ij}\mapsto e_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\imath}}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{i}\theta_{j}.

(2.1)

In the case of multiple tensor products of the endomorphism algebras,
we will indicate by
t_{a}
the map (
2.1
)
acting on the
a
-th copy of
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}
.

A standard basis of the general linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|2n}
is formed by elements
E_{ij}

of the parity
\bar{\imath}+\bar{\jmath}\mod 2
for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 2n+1
with the commutation relations

[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{kj}\,E_{i\hskip 1.0ptl}-\delta_{i\hskip 1.0ptl}\,E_{kj}(-1)^{(\bar{\imath}+\bar{\jmath})(\bar{k}+\bar{l})}.

We will regard the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}

associated with the bilinear from defined by
G
as the subalgebra
of
\mathfrak{gl}_{1|2n}
spanned by the elements

F_{ij}=E_{ij}-E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\imath}}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}.

Introduce the permutation operator
P
by

P=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ji}(-1)^{\bar{\jmath}}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}

Q=P^{\hskip 1.0ptt_{1}}=P^{\hskip 1.0ptt_{2}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}e_{ij}\otimes e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\bar{\jmath}}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}.

The
R
-
matrix
associated with
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}
is the
rational function in
u
given by

R(u)=1-\frac{P}{u}+\frac{Q}{u-\kappa},\hskip 20.00003pt\kappa=-n-1/2.

This is a super-version of the
R
-matrix
originally found in
[
10
]
.
Following
[
1
]
, we
define the
extended Yangian

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

as a
\mathbb{Z}\hskip 1.0pt_{2}
-graded algebra with generators

t_{ij}^{(r)}
of parity
\bar{\imath}+\bar{\jmath}\mod 2
, where
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 2n+1
and
r=1,2,\dots
,
satisfying certain quadratic relations. In order to write them down,
introduce the formal series

t_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}t_{ij}^{(r)}\,u^{-r}\in{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})[[u^{-1}]]

(2.2)

and combine them into the matrix
T(u)=[t_{ij}(u)]
so that

T(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}e_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})[[u^{-1}]].

Consider the algebra

{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}\otimes{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})[[u^{-1}]]

and introduce its elements
T_{1}(u)
and
T_{2}(u)
by

T_{1}(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}e_{ij}\otimes 1\otimes t_{ij}(u)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}},\hskip 20.00003ptT_{2}(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n+1}1\otimes e_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}}.

The defining relations for the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
take
the form of the
RTT
-
relation

R(u-v)\,T_{1}(u)\,T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)\,T_{1}(u)\,R(u-v).

(2.3)

As shown in
[
1
]
, the product
T(u)\,T^{\hskip 1.0ptt}(u-\kappa)
is a scalar matrix with

T(u-\kappa)\,T^{\hskip 1.0ptt}(u)=c(u)1,

(2.4)

where
c(u)
is a series in
u^{-1}
. All its coefficients belong to
the center
{\rm ZX}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
and generate the center.

The
Yangian

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

is defined as the subalgebra of

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
which
consists of the elements stable under
the automorphisms

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto f(u)\,t_{ij}(u)

(2.5)

for all series

f(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]]
.
We have the tensor product decomposition

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})={\rm ZX}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})\otimes{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}).

(2.6)

The Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
can be equivalently defined as the quotient
of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

by the relation

T(u-\kappa)\,T^{\hskip 1.0ptt}(u)=1.

We will also use a more explicit form of the defining relations (
2.3
)
written in terms of the series (
2.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle[\hskip 1.0ptt_{ij}(u),t_{kl}(v)]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u-v}\big(t_{kj}(u)\,t_{il}(v)-t_{kj}(v)\,t_{il}(u)\big)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa}\Big(\delta_{ki^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\sum_{p=1}^{2n+1}\,t_{pj}(u)\,t_{p^{\prime}l}(v)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{p}}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{p}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt{}-\delta_{lj^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\sum_{p=1}^{2n+1}\,t_{k\hskip 1.0ptp^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{ip}(u)(-1)^{\bar{\jmath}+\bar{p}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{p}}\,\theta_{j}\theta_{p}\Big).

For any
a\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
the mapping

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{ij}(u+a)

(2.8)

defines an automorphism of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
.

The universal enveloping algebra
{\rm U}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
can be regarded as a subalgebra of

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
via the embedding

F_{ij}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\big(t_{ij}^{(1)}-t_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}^{(1)}(-1)^{\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\imath}\bar{\jmath}}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}\big)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}}.

(2.9)

This fact relies on the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for the orthosymplectic Yangian
which was pointed out in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.
It states that the associated graded algebra
for
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
is isomorphic to
{\rm U}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}[u])
.
A detailed proof of the theorem can be given by
extending the arguments of
[
3
, Sec. 3]
to the super case
with the use of the vector representation recalled below in (

The extended Yangian
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
is a Hopf algebra with the coproduct
defined by

\Delta:t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{2n+1}t_{ik}(u)\otimes t_{kj}(u).

(2.10)

For the image of the series
c(u)
we have
\Delta:c(u)\mapsto c(u)\otimes c(u)
and so the Yangian

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
inherits the Hopf algebra structure from
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
.

3
Highest weight representations

We will start by deriving a general reduction property for representations of the
extended Yangians
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
analogous to
[
3
, Lemma 5.13]
.
For an
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
-module
V
set

V^{+}=\{\eta\in V\ |\ t_{1j}(u)\,\eta=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptj>1\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptt_{i\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(u)\,\eta=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pti<1^{\prime}\}.

(3.1)

Proposition 3.1
.

The subspace
V^{+}
is stable under the action of the operators
t_{ij}(u)

subject to
2\leqslant i,j\leqslant 2n
.
Moreover, the assignment
\bar{t}_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{i+1,j+1}(u)

for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 2n-1
defines
a representation of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n-2})
on
V^{+}
, where the

\bar{t}_{ij}(u)
denote the respective generating series for
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n-2})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
2\leqslant k,l\leqslant 2n
and

j>1
. For any
\eta\in V^{+}
apply (
2.7
) to get

t_{1j}(u)\hskip 1.0ptt_{kl}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta=\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa}\,\delta_{lj^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\hskip 1.0pt(-1)^{\bar{\jmath}+\bar{k}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}}\hskip 1.0pt\theta_{j}\,t_{k\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{11}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta.

Another application of (
2.7
) yields

t_{k\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{11}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta=-[t_{11}(u),t_{k\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(v)]\,\eta=\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa}\,t_{k\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{11}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta,

implying
t_{1j}(u)\hskip 1.0ptt_{kl}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta=0
. A similar calculation shows that

t_{i\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(u)\hskip 1.0ptt_{kl}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\eta=0
for
i<1^{\prime}
thus proving the first part of
the proposition.

Now suppose that
2\leqslant i,j,k,l\leqslant 2n
. By
(
2.7
) the super-commutator
[\hskip 1.0ptt_{ij}(u),t_{kl}(v)]

of the operators in
V^{+}
equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u-v}\big(t_{kj}(u)\,t_{il}(v)-t_{kj}(v)\,t_{il}(u)\big)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa}\Big(\delta_{ki^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\sum_{p=2}^{2n}\,t_{pj}(u)\,t_{p^{\prime}l}(v)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{p}}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{p}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt{}-\delta_{lj^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\sum_{p=2}^{2n}\,t_{k\hskip 1.0ptp^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{ip}(u)(-1)^{\bar{\jmath}+\bar{p}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{p}}\,\theta_{j}\theta_{p}\Big)

plus the additional terms

To transform these terms, use (
2.7
) again to get the relations

\displaystyle t_{1j}(u)\,t_{1^{\prime}l}(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa-1}\,\sum_{p=2}^{2n}\,t_{pj}(u)\,t_{p^{\prime}l}(v)(-1)^{\bar{\jmath}+\bar{\jmath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{p}}\,\theta_{p}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa-1}\,\delta_{lj^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\,t_{1^{\prime}1^{\prime}}(v)t_{11}(u)\,\theta_{j}

and

\displaystyle t_{k\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{i1}(u)=[t_{i1}(u)\,t_{k\hskip 1.0pt1^{\prime}}(v)](-1)^{\bar{\imath}+\bar{k}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa-1}\,\delta_{ki^{\hskip 1.0pt\prime}}\,t_{11}(u)\,t_{1^{\prime}1^{\prime}}(v)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}}\,\theta_{i}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u-v-\kappa-1}\,\sum_{p=2}^{2n}\,t_{k\hskip 1.0ptp^{\prime}}(v)\,t_{ip}(u)(-1)^{\bar{\imath}+\bar{p}+\bar{\imath}\hskip 1.0pt\bar{p}}\,\theta_{p}.

by
the left ideal generated by all coefficients of the series
t_{ij}(u)

with
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2n+1
, and
t_{ii}(u)-\lambda_{i}(u)
for

i=1,\dots,2n+1
. As in
[
3
, Prop. 5.14]
,
the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

implies that the Verma module
M(\lambda(u))

is nonzero, and we denote by
L(\lambda(u))
its irreducible quotient.

Proposition 3.4
.

Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

is isomorphic to
L(\lambda(u))
for a certain highest weight

\lambda(u)=(\lambda_{1}(u),\dots,\lambda_{n+1}(u))
.

Proof.

The argument is essentially the same as for the proof of the corresponding counterparts
of the property for the Yangians associated with Lie algebras;
cf.
[
3
, Thm 5.1]
,
[
7
, Sec. 3.2]
. We online some key steps.

Suppose that
V
is a finite-dimensional irreducible
representation of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
and
introduce its subspace
V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}
by

V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}=\{\eta\in V\ |\ t_{ij}(u)\,\eta=0,\hskip 20.00003pt1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2n+1\}.

First we note that
V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}
is nonzero, which follows by considering the set of weights
of
V
, regarded as an
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}
-module defined via the embedding
(
2.9
). This set is finite and hence contains a maximal weight
with respect to the standard partial ordering on the set of weights of
V
.
A weight vector with this weight belongs to
V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}
.

Furthermore, we show that
V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}
is stable under the action of all
operators
t_{ii}(u)
. This follows by straightforward
calculations similar to those used
in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, relying on the defining relations
(
2.7
). In a similar way, we verify that all the operators

t_{ii}(u)
with
i=1,\dots,2n+1
form a commuting family of operators on
V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}
.
Hence they have a simultaneous eigenvector

\xi\in V^{\hskip 1.0pt0}
. Since the representation
V
is irreducible,
the submodule
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})\hskip 1.0pt\xi
must coincide with
V

thus proving that
V
is a highest weight module.

By considering the
\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}
-weights of
V
we can also conclude that
the highest vector
\xi
of
V
is determined
uniquely, up to a constant factor.
∎

Proposition
3.4
yields the first part of the
Main Theorem. Our next step is to show that the conditions in the theorem
are necessary for the representation
L(\lambda(u))
to be finite-dimensional.
So we now suppose that
\dim L(\lambda(u))<\infty
and argue by induction on
n
.
The conditions (
1.1
) in the base case
n=1
are implied by
the main result of
[
8
]
. Suppose further that
n\geqslant 2
.

Recall that the Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
for the general linear Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is defined as a unital associative algebra
with countably many generators
t_{ij}^{(1)\circ},\ t_{ij}^{(2)\circ},\dots
where

1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
,
and the defining relations

(u-v)\,[t^{\circ}_{ij}(u),t^{\circ}_{kl}(v)]=t^{\circ}_{kj}(u)\,t^{\circ}_{il}(v)-t^{\circ}_{kj}(v)\,t^{\circ}_{il}(u)

written in terms of the series

t^{\circ}_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}+t^{(1)\circ}_{ij}u^{-1}+t^{(2)\circ}_{ij}u^{-2}+\dots\in{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})[[u^{-1}]];

see
[
7
]
for a detailed exposition of the algebraic structure
and representation of the Yangians associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.
The Yangian
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
can be regarded as a subalgebra of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
via the
embedding

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\hookrightarrow{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n}),\hskip 20.00003ptt^{\circ}_{ij}(u)\mapsto t_{ij}(-u)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n.

(3.5)

The cyclic span
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\hskip 1.0pt\xi\subset L(\lambda(u))
is a highest weight module
over
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
. Its highest weight is the
n
-tuple
(\lambda_{1}(-u),\dots,\lambda_{n}(-u))
.
Since
\dim L(\lambda(u))<\infty
, the corresponding conditions for finite-dimensional
highest weight representations of
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
must be satisfied; see

[
7
, Sec. 3.4]
. This implies conditions (
1.1
) of the Main Theorem
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
.

Furthermore, by Proposition
3.1
, the subspace

L(\lambda(u))^{+}
is a module over the extended Yangian

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n-2})
. The vector
\xi
generates a highest weight

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n-2})
-module
with the highest weight
(\lambda_{2}(u),\dots,\lambda_{n+1}(u))
.
Since this module is finite-dimensional,
conditions (
1.1
) hold for
i=2,\dots,n
by the induction hypothesis.
This completes the proof of the necessity of the conditions.

Now suppose that conditions (
1.1
) hold and derive that the corresponding
module

L(\lambda(u))
is finite-dimensional.
The
n
-tuple of Drinfeld polynomials
(P_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u))
determines the highest weight

\lambda(u)
up to a simultaneous multiplication of all components

\lambda_{i}(u)
by a series
f(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt[[u^{-1}]]
.
This operation corresponds to twisting the action of
the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
on
L(\lambda(u))
by the automorphism (
2.5
).
Hence, it suffices to prove that
a particular module
L(\lambda(u))
corresponding to a given set
of Drinfeld polynomials is finite-dimensional.

Suppose that
L(\lambda(u))
and
L(\mu(u))
are the irreducible
highest weight modules with the highest weights

\lambda(u)=\big(\lambda_{1}(u),\dots,\lambda_{n+1}(u)\big)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\mu(u)=\big(\mu_{1}(u),\dots,\mu_{n+1}(u)\big).

By the coproduct rule
(
2.10
),
the cyclic span
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})(\xi\otimes\xi^{\prime})
of the tensor product
of the respective highest vectors of
L(\lambda(u))
and
L(\mu(u))

is a highest weight
module
with the highest weight

\big(\lambda_{1}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\mu_{1}(u),\dots,\lambda_{n+1}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\mu_{n+1}(u)\big).

This observation implies that
the cyclic span corresponds to
the set of Drinfeld polynomials
(P_{1}(u)\hskip 1.0ptQ_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u)\hskip 1.0ptQ_{n}(u))
,
where the
P_{i}(u)
and
Q_{i}(u)
are the Drinfeld polynomials for

L(\lambda(u))
and
L(\mu(u))
, respectively.
Therefore,
we only need to establish the sufficiency of conditions (
1.1
)
for the
fundamental representations
of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
associated
with the
n
-tuples of Drinfeld polynomials
such that

P_{j}(u)=1
for all
j\neq i
and
P_{i}(u)=u+b
for a certain
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

and
b\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
; cf.
[
4
]
.
Moreover, it is sufficient to take one
particular value of
b\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
; the general case will then follow
by twisting the action of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})

in such representations
by automorphisms of the form (
2.8
).

Consider the vector representation of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
on
\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}
defined by

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\delta_{ij}+u^{-1}\hskip 1.0pte_{ij}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}}-(u+\kappa)^{-1}\hskip 1.0pte_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}(-1)^{\bar{\imath}\bar{\jmath}}\,\theta_{i}\theta_{j}.

(3.6)

The homomorphism property
follows from (
2.3
) by applying the standard transposition to one copy of

{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}
in the Yang–Baxter equation satisfied by
R(u)
.
Now use the coproduct (
2.10
) and suitable automorphisms (
2.8
)
to equip
the tensor product space
(\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n})^{\otimes k}

with the action of
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})
by setting

t_{ij}(u)\mapsto\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k-1}=1}^{2n+1}t_{ia_{1}}(u)\otimes t_{a_{1}a_{2}}(u-1)\otimes\dots\otimes t_{a_{k-1}j}(u-k+1),

(3.7)

where the generators act in the respective copies of the vector space

\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n}
via the rule (
3.6
).
For the values
k=1,\dots,n
introduce the vectors

\xi_{k}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}{\rm sgn}\,\sigma\cdot e_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\sigma(k)}\in(\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{1|2n})^{\otimes k}.

Now verify that each vector
\xi_{k}
has the properties

t_{ij}(u)\,\xi_{k}=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n+1

(3.8)

and

t_{ii}(u)\,\xi_{k}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{u-k}{u-k+1}\,\xi_{k}\qquad&\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,k,\\[6.00006pt]
\ \xi_{k}\qquad&\text{for}\quad i=k+1,\dots,n+1.\end{cases}

(3.9)

The expression for the vector
\xi_{k}
involves
only tensor products of the basis vectors
e_{i}
with
i\leqslant n
. This implies
that for the application of the operators
t_{ij}(u)
with

1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant n

to
\xi_{k}
we may restrict
the sum in formula (
3.7
) to the values
a_{p}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

By using the embedding (
3.5
), we may regard the cyclic span
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\hskip 1.0pt\xi_{k}

as a
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module. Moreover, this module is isomorphic to

A^{(k)}(\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{n})^{\otimes k}
, where
A^{(k)}
is the anti-symmetrization operator.
It is well-known that this
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-module is isomorphic to the evaluation
module
L(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)
(with
k
ones) twisted by
a shift automorphism
u\mapsto u+k-1
; see e.g.
[
7
, Sec. 6.5]
.
This yields formulas (
3.8
) and (
3.9
)
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant n
. They are easily verified directly
for the remaining generators.

Formulas (
3.9
) show that the corresponding set of Drinfeld polynomials
for the highest weight module
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{osp}_{1|2n})\hskip 1.0pt\xi_{k}
has the form

P_{i}(u)=1
for
i\neq k
, while
P_{k}(u)=u-k
. This completes the proof
of the second part of the Main Theorem concerning conditions (
1.1
).
The last part is immediate from the decomposition
(
2.6
); cf.
[
3
, Sec. 5.3]
.

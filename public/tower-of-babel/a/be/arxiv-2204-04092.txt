Ping Liu, Habib Ammari

Abstract
Particle tracking in a live cell environment is concerned with reconstructing the trajectories, locations, or velocities of the targeting particles, which holds the promise of revealing important new biological insights. The standard approach of particle tracking consists of two steps: first reconstructing statically the source locations in each time step, and second applying tracking techniques to obtain the trajectories and velocities. In contrast to the standard approach, the dynamic reconstruction seeks to simultaneously recover the source locations and velocities from all frames, which enjoys certain advantages. In this paper, we provide a rigorous mathematical analysis for the resolution limit of reconstructing source number, locations, and velocities by general dynamical reconstruction in particle tracking problems, by which we demonstrate the possibility of achieving super-resolution for the dynamic reconstruction. We show that when the location-velocity pairs of the particles are separated beyond certain distances (the resolution limits), the number of particles and the location-velocity pair can be stably recovered. The resolution limits are related to the cut-off frequency of the imaging system, signal-to-noise ratio, and the sparsity of the source. By these estimates we also derive a stability result for a sparsity-promoting dynamic reconstruction. In addition, we further show that the reconstruction of velocities has a better resolution limit which improves constantly as the particles moving. This result is derived by a crucial observation that the inherent cut-off frequency for the velocity recovery can be viewed as the total observation time multiplies the cut-off frequency of the imaging system, which may lead to a better resolution limit as compared to the one for each diffraction-limited frame. It is anticipated that this crucial observation can inspire many new reconstruction algorithms that significantly improve the resolution of particle tracking in practice. In addition, we propose super-resolution algorithms for recovering the number and values of the velocities in the tracking problem and demonstrate theoretically or numerically their super-resolution capability.

Introduction

The problem of particle tracking is of particular importance in many modern imaging experiments such as visualization of cell migration and subcellular dynamics

The former refers to determining the locations of particles in each frame and the later refers to linking particles between consecutive frames. However, this static reconstruction strategy suffers from two major issues. The first key issue is that the particles are not well-separated in some of the frames which results in unstable recovery of the source locations or abandon of a large amount of frames where particles are closely-spaced. The second issue is the ambiguities and heavy computational burden in linking particles with high densities and velocities

However, theoretically the algorithm still requires that the point sources should be separated beyond Rayleigh limit in each frame for a stable recovery, which hampered its application in the case when sources are closely-spaced in some of the frames. In this paper, in order to understand the super-resolution capability of the tracking problem, we aim to analyze the resolution limit for super-resolving the source number, location-velocity pairs, and velocities in the tracking problem . We also provide super-resolution algorithms for reconstructing the velocities of moving particles which are able to super-resolve velocities even when static reconstruction completely fails.

1.1
Our model and contributions

Let us first introduce our model and main contributions. We consider the same model for reconstructing dynamic point sources (or particles) as the one in
[
4
]
. We remark that we will use bold symbols for vectors, matrices and some functions, and ordinary ones for scalar values in our models and discussions throughout the paper. To be specific, we consider a cluster of point sources that are moving constantly, represented by a time-varying measures
\mu_{t}
, where
t\in[0,\eta]
with
\eta>0
being the observation window. Since
\eta

\mu_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\tau t},\quad t=0,\cdots,T,

where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the initial source locations and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the velocities. The
\tau t,t=0,\cdots,T
is the time step in
[0,\eta]
at which we observe the samples. We remark that in the applications of measuring the velocity of blood flow
[
23
,
18
]
and particle tracking velocimetry
[
16
]
, we may have a larger observation window
\eta
and our model is much more applicable. The measurement at each time step is a noisy Fourier data in a bounded domain,

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}e^{i(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}^{\top}+t\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{\top})\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}),\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}||_{2}\leq\Omega,\ t=0,\cdots,T,

(1.1)

where
\top
denotes the transpose,
\Omega
is the cut-off frequency of the imaging system,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{t}
is an additive noise with
|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})|<\sigma
and
\sigma
being the noise level. We denote

For the tracking problem in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we are interested in reconstructing the amplitude, location, and velocity pair set

\Big\{(a_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j})\Big\}_{j=1}^{n},

from the measurements

In
[
4
]
, the authors proposed a fully dynamical method to reconstruct simultaneously the source locations and velocities. They cast the inverse problem as a total variation optimization problem satisfying the measurement constraints and showed that under certain conditions, the optimization is able to reconstruct the amplitudes, locations and velocities of source from noiseless measurements with infinite precision. However, for the noisy case, theoretically, to stably recover the source, the point sources are required to be separated beyond Rayleigh limit in each frame, which is definitely not super-resolution. Thus the superiority of the dynamic reconstruction over the static recovery in the tracking problem is still unclear. Since when the spikes are separated beyond Rayleigh limit, the static reconstruction is also able to recover stably all the
\mu_{t}
’s.

In this paper, in contrast to
[
4
]
, we consider the locations and velocities of point sources to be closely-spaced in sub-Rayleigh regimes respectively, and explore the ability of super-resolution of the dynamic reconstruction in the tracking problem. More precisely, we analyze the resolution limit for recovering the number, locations, and velocities of the source from the measurement constraint. We prove that when

\min_{j\neq p}\Big|\Big|{(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}^{\top},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{\top})-(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{p}^{\top},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{p}^{\top})}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{C_{1}(d,n)}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}},

where
C_{1}(d,n)
is an explicit constant only related to space dimensionality
d
and source number
n
(so do the following constants
C_{2}(d,n),C_{3}(d,n),C_{4}(d,n)
), then we can recover the source number
n
in the dynamic reconstruction. When

\min_{j\neq p}\Big|\Big|{(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}^{\top},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{\top})-(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{p}^{\top},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{p}^{\top})}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{C_{2}(d,n)}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}},

then we can stably recover the source locations and velocities. The estimate demonstrates that the dynamic reconstruction could resolve the location-velocity pairs in sub-Rayleigh regimes for sufficiently large signal-to-noise ratio. It also indicates that the dynamic reconstruction could recover source locations and velocities even when static reconstruction fails in some of the frames, which demonstrates its superiority.

Moreover, by above results, we also show that any algorithms targeting at the sparsest solution satisfying measurement constraint could achieve the resolution
\frac{C_{2}(d,n)}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}
, which demonstrates the favorable performance and the superiority of sparsity-promoting dynamic reconstruction as compared to the static reconstruction methods. This is consistent with the numerical results in
[
4
]
for comparison between reconstructions by TV minimization and static method.

In some applications, the recovery of the velocities of the particles is more interesting than the location recovery. For instance, in the ultrafast ultrasound localization microscopy based non-invasive super-resolution vascular imaging
[
23
,
18
]
, the velocities of the blood flow with a large dynamic range can be extracted by reconstructing the velocities of microbubbles inside. We also note that the particle tracking is a common method in velocimetry
[
16
]
which seeks to measure accurate velocities of fluid. We thus further consider the reconstruction of individual velocities. By some projection methods, we demonstrate super-resolution for resolving the number of point sources and value of velocities. More precisely, when the minimum separation distance of velocities satisfies

\min_{j\neq p}\Big|\Big|{\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}-\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{p}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{C_{3}(d,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}},

we can stably recover the number of point sources. When

\min_{j\neq p}\Big|\Big|{\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}-\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{p}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{C_{4}(d,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}},

we can stably reconstruct all the velocities. The results are derived by a crucial observation that the inherent cut-off frequency for recovering velocities
\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}
’s can be viewed as
T\Omega
, by which we obtain better resolution limits. We hope this observation could inspire new algorithms for super-resolving velocities in the tracking problem. For fixed dimensionality
d
and source number
n
, all the above theoretical results for the resolution limits are optimal in the sense that at the same separation order there are counter examples in the worst-case scenario.

We also propose super-resolution algorithms for resolving the source number and velocities respectively. They are demonstrated theoretically or numerically to achieve the same order of the resolution limits that derived above. We also demonstrate numerically their superiority over the static reconstruction.

Organization of the paper

2.3
Stability for sparsity-promoting dynamic reconstruction

Sparsity-based modeling and optimization is a common way in super-resolution that seeks to accelerate the resolving process or reduce the number of measurements. In
[
4
]
, a sparsity-promoting algorithm is proposed to super-resolve source locations and velocities in the tracking problem, where the authors’ aim is to find an admissible
2d
-dimensional source with minimum total variation norm. There, the theoretical stability result for the optimization requires the point sources to be separated by more than a Rayleigh length in each time step
t
, which is inadequate for demonstrating its super-resolution capability.

As a corollary of the results in Subsections
2.1
and
2.2
, we can derive a stability result for a sparsity-promoting dynamic reconstruction in the tracking problem, which demonstrates the possibility of achieving super-resolution for the sparsity-promoting dynamic reconstructions. We consider a sparsity-promoting dynamic tracking problem which seeks to find the sparest solution satisfying the measurement constraints. The optimization problem is

\min_{\{(\hat{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{v}}}_{j})\},(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}^{\top},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{v}}}_{j}^{\top})^{\top}\in\mathcal{O}}\#\{(\hat{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j},\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{v}}}_{j})\}\quad\text{subject to}\ |\mathcal{F}[\rho_{t}](\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})|<\sigma,\ t=0,\cdots,T,\ ||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}||_{2}\leq\Omega,

(2.12)

where
\rho_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{a}_{j}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}+t\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{v}}}_{j}}
, and
\mathcal{F}[\rho_{t}](\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{a}_{j}e^{i(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}^{\top}+t\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{v}}}_{j}^{\top})\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}
. We have the following theorem for the stability of the minimization problem (

).

Let
n\geq 2

T\geq\frac{n(n+1)}{2}

Theorem

reveals that sparsity promoting over admissible solutions satisfying the measurements of all the frames could resolve the location-velocity pairs to the resolution limit order. It provides an insight that theoretically sparsity-promoting dynamic reconstruction algorithms would have favorable performances on the tracking problem. Especially, under the separation condition

Projection-based velocity number detection algorithm

By the projection methods we use in Section

6
Conclusions and future works

In this paper, we have explored the resolution limits for recovering the number and value of location-velocity pairs in the dynamic reconstruction of the tracking problem. We have also derived sharp and better resolution limits for reconstructing the number and values of velocities in the dynamic reconstruction. Also, two projection-based algorithms have been introduced to super-resolve respectively the number and values of velocities. By these results, we have demonstrated certain advantages of the dynamic reconstruction in the tracking problem over the conventional static reconstructions.

Besides our research findings, our work is also a start of many new topics. Especially, from the crucial observation of measurement (
5.3
), many new algorithms can be inspired to obtain much better resolution for the velocity recovery over the static reconstruction method. In practice, the point spread function may be approximated by other different functions, such as Gaussian functions, and a large amount of point sources may be clustered together in a single image
[
15
]
, which hampered the application of subspace methods. In this case, the convex optimization based algorithms are great surrogates for resolving the point sources. Applying convex optimization to (
5.3
) or its variant may enhance significantly the resolution in the practical tracking problem. In addition, developing efficient algorithms for exploring the amplitudes and the locations of point sources when the velocities are known is also an interesting topic. Note that when the velocities are stably recovered, we can reconstruct the
\hat{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}
’s by solving the minimization problem

\min_{\hat{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}}\Big|\Big|{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{a}_{j}e^{i\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}e^{it\tau\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{v}}}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})}\Big|\Big|_{2},\quad||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}||_{2}\leq\Omega,t=0,\cdots,T.

The aim is to develop a tractable algorithm in order to recover stably the
\hat{a}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}
’s for the above minimization problem or its variants.

t\geq 1
we have

\arctan(t)-\arctan(t-1)=\arctan\Big(\frac{1}{1+t(t-1)}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{t^{2}-t+1}-\frac{1}{3(t^{2}-t+1)^{3}}>\frac{1}{t^{2}+1}.

(7.4)

We denote the unit vector in
S_{t}^{1}
’s as
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}(1,t)^{\top}
. Define
\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j})
the angle between vectors
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}
that

\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j})=\arccos\Big(\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}}{||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}||_{2}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}||_{2}}\Big).

By the above observation, we have

\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j})=\arctan(t)-\arctan(j)>\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}},\quad j<t.

(7.5)

We first estimate the projection to these one-dimensional subspaces
S_{t}^{1}
’s.

Lemma 7.1
.

For
t=0,1,\cdots
and a
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, if

\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}<\frac{1}{2\pi(1+t^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2},

then for
0\leq j<t
, we have

\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi(1+j^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}.

Proof.

We first prove the lemma for
j=t-1
. By (
7.5
), we have

\frac{1}{t^{2}+1}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t-1})\leq\frac{\pi}{4}.

(7.6)

Since
||\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}<\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}
and
|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}|=||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}|\cos(\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}))|
, we have
|\cos(\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}))|<\frac{1}{2\pi(1+t^{2})}
. Thus
\frac{2}{\pi}|\frac{\pi}{2}-\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})|\leq|\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}-\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}))|=|\cos(\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}))|<\frac{1}{2\pi(1+t^{2})}
, and consequently,

\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{1}{4(1+t^{2})}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})<\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{1}{4(1+t^{2})},or\ -\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{1}{4(1+t^{2})}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})<-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{1}{4(1+t^{2})}.

Without loss of generality, we only consider the first case. Together with (
7.6
), we have

0<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t-1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})<\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{3}{4(1+t^{2})},or\ \frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{3}{4(1+t^{2})}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t-1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})<\pi.

Thus,

||\mathcal{P}_{S_{t-1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}\geq\sin\Big(\frac{3}{4(1+t^{2})}\Big)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}.

By
\sin(x)\geq\frac{2x}{\pi}
and
\frac{3}{1+t^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{1+(t-1)^{2}}
, we then show that

||\mathcal{P}_{S_{t-1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+(t-1)^{2}}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}.

For
j<t-1
, since
\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t})>\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}
, we have

||\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}\geq\sin\Big(\sum_{k=j+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})}\Big)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}.

Thus

||\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\Big(\sum_{k=j+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})}\Big)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}.

It is clear that
\frac{2}{\pi}\Big(\sum_{k=j+1}^{t-1}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{1}{(1+t^{2})}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+j^{2}}
, whence the lemma is proved.
∎

We now consider the general
S_{t}^{d}
’s.

Lemma 7.2
.

For any
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}\in S_{t}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\in S_{j}^{d},j<t
satisfying
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j})\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}
be two unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}>0
. Then we have

\cos\Big(\angle\Big(\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\end{pmatrix}\Big)\Big)=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\end{pmatrix}/\Big(\Big|\Big|{\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\end{pmatrix}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\Big|\Big|{\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\end{pmatrix}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\Big)=\frac{(1+tj)\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}}{\sqrt{(1+t^{2})(1+j^{2})}}.

Thus, introducing
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{t}\end{pmatrix}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{q}}}_{j}\end{pmatrix}
yields

0\leq\cos(\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}))\leq\frac{1+tj}{\sqrt{(1+t^{2})(1+j^{2})}}.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}
. Then, it follows that

||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}||_{2}=\sqrt{||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}||_{2}^{2}+||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}||_{2}^{2}-2||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}||_{2}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}||_{2}\cos(\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}))}\geq t-j.

Hence, by considering the two-dimensional space spanned by
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}
and using the same idea as the one in proving (
7.4
) and (
7.5
), we show that for any
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}\in S_{t}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\in S_{j}^{d},j<t
, we have

\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j})\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.

∎

We now extend Lemma
7.1
to the multi-dimensional case.

Lemma 7.3
.

For
t=0,1,\cdots
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, if

||\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}<\frac{1}{2\pi(1+t^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2},

then for
0\leq j<t
, we have

||\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi(1+j^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}.

Proof.

For a fixed
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, let

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}=\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})/||\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2},

and

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}=\mathcal{P}_{s_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})/||\mathcal{P}_{s_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2},

if
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\geq 0
. Otherwise, set
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}=-\mathcal{P}_{s_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})/||\mathcal{P}_{s_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}
. Under the condition stated in the lemma, we have
|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t}|<\frac{1}{2\pi(1+t^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}
. Also, by Lemma
7.2
we get

\frac{\pi}{2}\geq\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j})>\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}.

Considering the two-dimensional space spanned by
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}
, similarly to proof of Lemma
7.1
, we obtain

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{j}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi(1+j^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2},

which implies that
||\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2\pi(1+j^{2})}||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}
.
∎

We next present two auxiliary lemmas that are used in the proof of main results.

Lemma 7.4
.

For a vector
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and two unit vectors
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying
0\leq|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}|\leq\cos\theta
, we have

|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}|^{2}\geq(1-\cos(\theta))||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}^{2}.

(7.7)

Proof.

We have

\Big|\Big|{(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}},\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})^{\top}}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}=\Big|\Big|{\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1}^{\top}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}^{\top}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}\geq\sigma_{\min}^{2}(\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1}^{\top}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}^{\top}\end{pmatrix})||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}^{2}\geq(1-\cos\theta)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}^{2},

(7.8)

where the last inequality follows from calculating
\sigma_{\min}(\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{1}^{\top}\\
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{q}}_{2}^{\top}\end{pmatrix})
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

For a vector
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for spaces
S_{t}^{d},S_{j}^{d},j<t
, we have

\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}+\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}\geq\Big(1-\cos(\theta)\Big)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}^{2},

where
\theta=\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}
.

Proof.

We first construct the basis of
S_{t}^{d}
and
S_{j}^{d}
. Let

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1}:=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2}:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\cdots,\quad\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d}:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

It is easy to verify that the vectors

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1,t}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\end{pmatrix},\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2,t}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \cdots,\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d,t}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\\
t\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\end{pmatrix},

form an orthonormal basis of
S_{t}^{d}
. In the same manner, the vectors

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1,j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+j^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\end{pmatrix},\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2,j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+j^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \cdots,\ \boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d,j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+j^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\\
j\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\end{pmatrix},

form an orthonormal basis of
S_{j}^{d}
. Also, we have

|\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{p,t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{q,j})|=\frac{\pi}{2},\ p\neq q,\ \text{and}\ \sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}<\angle(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{q,t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{q,j})<\frac{\pi}{2},

(7.9)

where the second inequality is from Lemma
7.2
. Thus, if we denote by
V_{q}
the two-dimensional space spanned by
\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{q,t},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{q,j}\}
, then the
V_{q}
’s are orthogonal to each other. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{t}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}+\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{d}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}=\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1,t}\Big|^{2}+\cdots+\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d,t}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1,j}\Big|^{2}+\cdots+\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d,j}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1,t}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{1,j}\Big|^{2}\Big)+\Big(\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2,t}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{2,j}\Big|^{2}\Big)+\cdots+\Big(\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d,t}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{e}}_{d,j}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Big(1-\cos\Big(\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}\Big)\Big)\sum_{q=1}^{d}\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{V_{q}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}^{2}\quad\Big(\text{by Lemma \ref{lem:projectlengthestimate1} and (\ref{equ:spaceangleestimate2})}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1-\cos\Big(\sum_{k=j+1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+k^{2}}\Big)\Big)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}^{2}\quad\Big(\text{since $V_{q}$'s are orthogonal to each other}\Big).

∎

7.2
Results for the static super-resolution problem

In this subsection, we review and restate the stability results in
[
34
]
for the static super-resolution problem in multi-dimensional spaces. These results are useful to prove stability results for the dynamic super-resolution problem.

In the super-resolution problem of single snapshot case, the source is the discrete measure
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
\min_{1\leq j\leq n}|a_{j}|=m_{\min}>0
. The measurement
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}
is the noisy Fourier data of
\mu
in a bounded domain:

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}e^{i\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}),\quad\ ||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}||_{2}\leq\Omega,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

(7.10)

with
|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})|<\sigma
. The inverse problem is to recover the discrete measure from the set of
\sigma
-admissible measures of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}
defined below.

Definition 7.1
.

Given the measurement
\mathbf{Y}
, we say that
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hat{a}_{j}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a
\sigma
-admissible measure of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}
if

\Big|{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hat{a}_{j}e^{i\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\hat{y}}}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})}\Big|<\sigma,\ ||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}||_{2}\leq\Omega.

From
[
34
]
,
we have the following stability results for the recovery of source number and locations.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let the measurement
\mathbf{Y}
in (
7.10
) be generated by a
n
-sparse measure
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{y}_{j}},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}\in B_{\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2\Omega}}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}})
. Let
n\geq 2
and assume that the following separation condition is satisfied

\min_{p\neq j,1\leq p,j\leq n}\Big|\Big|{\mathbf{y}_{p}-\mathbf{y}_{j}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{4.4\pi e\ (\pi/2)^{k-1}(n(n-1)/\pi)^{\xi(k-1)}}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}},

(7.11)

where
\xi(k-1)
is defined by (
2.1
). Then there does not exist any
\sigma
-admissible measures of
\mathbf{Y}
with less than
n
supports.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let the measurement
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}
in (
7.10
) be generated by a
n
-sparse measure
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}\in B_{\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2\Omega}}^{k}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}})

k

d_{\min}:=\min_{p\neq j}\Big|\Big|\mathbf{y}_{p}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{5.88\pi e4^{k-1}\Big((n+2)(n+1)/2\Big)^{\xi(k-1)}}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}},

where

is defined as in (
2.1

\hat{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{a}_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{j}}

N(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq},\Delta_{k})
contains at most one unit vector in (
8.2
). Next recall that there are
N_{k}^{k-1}
different vectors in (
8.2
), where
N_{k}=\lfloor\frac{\pi}{2\theta_{k}}\rfloor\geq\frac{\pi}{2\theta_{k}}-1
. Since

\theta_{k}=2\Delta_{k}=\frac{\pi}{4}\Big(\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-1}},

we have

\displaystyle N_{k}^{k-1}\geq\Big(\frac{\pi}{2\theta_{k}}-1\Big)^{k-1}=\Big(2(\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}-1\Big)^{k-1}\geq\Big((\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\Big)^{k-1}=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}.

Note that
\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\geq n+1
, we can at least find
(n+1)
vectors
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}
’s in (
8.2
) so that
||\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq})||_{2}\geq||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq}||_{2}\sin\Delta_{k}
for all
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq}
’s. Thus we have

\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq})}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geq\frac{2||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq}||_{2}}{\pi}\frac{\pi}{8}\Big(\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}=\frac{||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq}||_{2}}{4}\Big(\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}

for all
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}_{pq}
’s. On the other hand, by Lemma
8.1
we have
0\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{p}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}\leq\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big(\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\Big)
for
p\neq j
.
∎

Lemma 8.6
.

Define

d_{\min,k}=\frac{11.76\pi e4^{k-1}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-1)}}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}.

For a vector
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, suppose we can find two
(k-1)
-dimensional spaces
S_{j}^{k-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{j}^{\perp},j=1,2
with
0\leq\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{1}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{2}\leq\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{4}\Big(\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}\Big)
so that

\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{k-1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}<\frac{1}{2}d_{\min,k-1},\quad j=1,2,

and

\Big|\Big|{\mathcal{P}_{S_{j}^{k-1}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})}\Big|\Big|_{2}\leq\frac{C(k-1,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k-1}T\Omega}\Big)^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}},\quad j=1,2,

where

C(k-1,n)=\big(4^{k-2}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-2)}\big)^{(2n-1)}n2^{6n-3}e^{2n}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Then we have

\Big|\Big|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}<\frac{1}{2}d_{\min,k},

and

\Big|\Big|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\leq\frac{C(k,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k}T\Omega}\Big)^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}},

where

C(k,n)=\big(4^{k-1}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-1)}\big)^{(2n-1)}n2^{6n-3}e^{2n}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}\Big(\frac{2}{(n+1)(n+2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
. By condition for
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{2}
in the lemma and Lemma
8.3
, we have

||\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{1}^{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}^{2}+||\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{v}}_{2}^{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}})||_{2}^{2}\geq(1-\cos\theta)||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}^{2}.

(8.6)

Thus

||\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}||_{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{1-\cos\theta}}\frac{C(k-1,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k-1}T\Omega}\Big)^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}.

Using the inequality

1-\cos\theta\geq\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\theta^{2}=\frac{1}{8}\big(\frac{2}{(n+2)(n+1)}\big)^{\frac{2}{k-1}}
, we further obtain

\Big|\Big|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\leq\frac{4((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}C(k-1,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k-1}T\Omega}\Big)^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}},\quad 1\leq j\leq n.

(8.7)

We next claim that

\Big|\Big|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}<\frac{d_{\min,k}}{2}.

Indeed, by a direct calculation, we can verify that

\displaystyle 4((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}C(k-1,n)\Big(\frac{1}{11.76e4^{k-2}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-2)}}\Big)^{2n-2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}11.76\pi e4^{(k-1)}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-1)}.

On the other hand, we have

(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k-1}T\Omega})^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\leq\Big(\frac{1}{11.76e4^{k-2}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-2)}}\Big)^{2n-2}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{4((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}C(k-1,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k-1}T\Omega}\Big)^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{11.76\pi e4^{k-1}((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\xi(k-1)}}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}.

This is
\Big|\Big|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}<\frac{1}{2}d_{\min,k}
. Moreover, we have

\Big|\Big|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{u}}}\Big|\Big|_{2}\leq\frac{(4((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\frac{1}{k-1}})^{2n-1}C(k-1,n)}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\pi}{d_{\min,k}T\Omega}\Big)^{2n-2}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}},

which follows from (
8.7
) and the equation that
d_{\min,k-1}=\frac{d_{\min,k}}{4((n+2)(n+1)/2)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We define a discrete measure as
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{y_{j}}
and the vector of its Fourier transform at

as

[\mu]=(\mathcal{F}[\mu](0),\mathcal{F}[\mu](\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}[\mu](T\Omega))^{\top},

where
\mathcal{F}[\mu](x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}e^{iy_{j}x}
. We have the following theorems for the source number and location recovery in a one-dimensional static super-resolution problem.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
T\geq 2n-2
. Suppose that the measurement is

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}e^{iy_{j}\Omega t}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}(t),\quad t=0,\cdots,T,

with
|\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}(t)|<\sigma
,
y_{j}\in[-\frac{(n-1)\pi}{T\Omega},\frac{(n-1)\pi}{T\Omega}]
and

d_{\min}:=\min_{p\neq q}\Big|{y_{p}-y_{q}}\Big|\geq\frac{8.8e\pi}{T\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}}.

(8.16)

Then there is no
\hat{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hat{a}_{j}\delta_{\hat{y}_{j}}
with
k<n
so that

||[\hat{\mu}]-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}||_{\infty}<\sigma.

Proof.

Step 1.
We write

T+1=(2n-1)r+q,

(8.17)

where
r,q
are integers with
r\geq 1
and
0\leq q<2n-1
.
We denote by
\theta_{j}=y_{j}r\Omega,j=1,\cdots,n
. For
y_{j}\in[-\frac{(n-1)\pi}{T\Omega},\frac{(n-1)\pi}{T\Omega}]
, in view of (
8.17
), it is clear that

\theta_{j}=y_{j}r\Omega\in\Big[\frac{-\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big],\quad j=1,\cdots,n.

(8.18)

For
\hat{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\hat{a}_{j}\delta_{\hat{y}_{j}}
with
k<n
, note that

\displaystyle[\hat{\mu}]-[\mu]=\left(\mathcal{F}[\hat{\mu}](0),\mathcal{F}[\hat{\mu}](\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}[\hat{\mu}](T\Omega)\right)^{\top}-\left(\mathcal{F}[\mu](0),\mathcal{F}[\mu](\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}[\mu](T\Omega)\right)^{\top}.

Using
only the partial measurement at
qr\Omega,0\leq q\leq 2n-2
, we have

\left(\mathcal{F}\hat{\mu}(0),\mathcal{F}\hat{\mu}(r\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}\hat{\mu}((2n-2)r\Omega)\right)^{\top}-\left(\mathcal{F}\mu(0),\mathcal{F}\mu(r\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}\mu((2n-2)r\Omega)\right)^{\top}=\hat{B}\hat{a}-Ba,

where
\hat{a}=(\hat{a}_{1},\cdots,\hat{a}_{k})^{\top}
,
a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})^{\top}
and

\displaystyle\hat{B}=\big(\phi_{2n-2}(e^{i\hat{\theta}_{1}}),\cdots,\phi_{2n-2}(e^{i\hat{\theta}_{k}})\big),

(8.19)

\displaystyle B=\big(\phi_{2n-2}(e^{i\theta_{1}}),\cdots,\phi_{2n-2}(e^{i\theta_{n}})\big),

with
\theta_{j}=y_{j}r\Omega,\hat{\theta}_{j}=\hat{y}_{j}r\Omega
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\min_{\hat{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{k},\hat{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k}||[\hat{\mu}]-[\mu]||_{\infty}\geq\min_{\hat{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{k},\hat{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k}||\hat{B}\hat{a}-Ba||_{\infty}

(8.20)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n-1}}\min_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{k},\hat{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k}||\hat{B}\alpha-Ba||_{2}.

In view of (
8.18
), we can apply Theorem
8.1
to get

\min_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{k},\hat{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k}||\hat{B}\alpha-Ba||_{2}\geq\frac{m_{\min}\zeta(n)\beta(n-1)(\theta_{\min})^{2n-2}}{\pi^{2n-2}},

where
\theta_{\min}=\min_{j\neq p}|\theta_{j}-\theta_{p}|
. Combining the above estimate with (
8.20
), we get

\displaystyle\min_{\hat{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{k},\hat{y}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k}||[\hat{\mu}]-[\mu]||_{\infty}\geq\frac{m_{\min}\zeta(n)\beta(n-1)(\theta_{\min})^{2n-2}}{\sqrt{2n-1}\pi^{2n-2}}.

(8.21)

Step 2.
Recall that
d_{\min}=\min_{j\neq p}|y_{j}-y_{p}|
. Using the relation
\theta_{j}=y_{j}r\Omega
and (

\theta_{\min}=r\Omega d_{\min}\geq\frac{rT\Omega}{(2n-1)(r+1)}d_{\min}\geq\frac{T\Omega}{2(2n-1)}d_{\min}.\quad\Big(\text{$r\geq 1$}\Big)

Then the separation condition (

\theta_{\min}\geq\frac{4.4\pi e}{2n-1}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}}\geq\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{2n-1}}{\zeta(n)\beta(n-1)}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}},

where we have used (
8.13

It follows that

\displaystyle||[\hat{\mu}]-\mathbf{Y}||_{\infty}=||[\hat{\mu}]-[\mu]-\mathbf{W}||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle||[\hat{\mu}]-[\mu]||_{\infty}-||\mathbf{W}||_{\infty}\geq||[\hat{\mu}]-[\mu]||_{\infty}-\sigma\geq\sigma,

∎

Theorem 8.4

Proof.

Step 1.
We first write

(8.23)

where

(8.26)

Similar to Step 1 in the proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}[\hat{\mu}](0),\mathcal{F}[\hat{\mu}](r\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}[\hat{\mu}]((2n-1)r\Omega)\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}[\mu](0),\mathcal{F}[\mu](r\Omega),\cdots,\mathcal{F}[\mu]((2n-1)r\Omega)\right)^{\top}=\hat{B}\hat{a}-Ba,

where
\hat{a}=(\hat{a}_{1},\cdots,\hat{a}_{n})^{\top}
,

\displaystyle\hat{B}=\big(\phi_{2n-1}(e^{i\hat{\theta}_{1}}),\cdots,\phi_{2n-1}(e^{i\hat{\theta}_{n}})\big),\ B=\big(\phi_{2n-1}(e^{i\theta_{1}}),\cdots,\phi_{2n-1}(e^{i\theta_{n}})\big),

||\hat{B}\hat{a}-Ba||_{\infty}<2\sigma
,
whence we get

||\hat{B}\hat{a}-Ba||_{2}\leq\sqrt{2n}||\hat{B}\hat{a}-Ba||_{\infty}<2\sqrt{2n}\sigma.

(8.28)

In view of (
8.24
), we can apply Theorem
8.2
to get

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|\eta_{n,n}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\theta_{n}},e^{i\hat{\theta}_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\hat{\theta}_{n}})\Big|\Big|_{\infty}<\frac{\sqrt{2n}2^{n+1}\pi^{n-1}}{\zeta(n)(\theta_{\min})^{n-1}}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}.

(8.29)

Step 2.

We apply Lemma
8.7
to estimate
|\hat{\theta}_{j}-\theta_{j}|
’s and
|\hat{y}_{j}-y_{j}|
’s. To do so, let
\epsilon=\frac{2\sqrt{2n}\pi^{2n-1}}{\zeta(n)(\theta_{\min})^{n-1}}\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}

||\eta_{n,n}||_{\infty}<(\frac{2}{\pi})^{n}\epsilon

\theta_{\min}\geq\Big(\frac{4\epsilon}{\lambda(n)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}},\quad\mbox{or equivalently}\,\,\,(\theta_{\min})^{n}\geq\frac{4\epsilon}{\lambda(n)}.

(8.30)

Indeed, by (
8.26
) and the separation condition (

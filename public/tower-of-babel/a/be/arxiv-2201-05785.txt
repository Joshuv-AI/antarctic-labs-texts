A q q -supercongruence modulo the fourth power of a cyclotomic polynomial Thanks: This work is supported by Natural Science Foundations of Shanghai (22ZR1424100).
Xiaoxia Wang, Chang Xu ∗

Abstract
. In this paper, a new q q -supercongruence with two free parameters modulo the fourth power of a cyclotomic polynomial is obtained. Our main auxiliary tools are Watson’s ϕ 7 8 {}_{8}\phi_{7} transformation formula for basic hypergeometric series, the ‘creative microscoping’ method recently introduced by Guo and Zudilin and the Chinese remainder theorem for coprime polynomials. By taking suitable parameter substitutions in the established q q -supercongruence, some nice congruences involving the Bernoulli numbers are derived.

1.
Introduction

In 1997, Van Hamme
[
14
]
conjectured
13
Ramanujan-type supercongruences which were labeled as (A.2)–(M.2).
Van Hamme’s (D.2) states that for primes
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/3}(6k+1)\frac{(1/3)^{6}_{k}}{k!^{6}}\equiv-p\Gamma_{p}\left(1/3\right)^{9}\pmod{p^{4}}.

Here and in what follows,
p
is a prime,
(x)_{0}=1
,
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
stands for the Pochhammer’s symbol and
\Gamma_{p}(x)
is the
p
-adic Gamma function.
In 2006, making use of Dougall’s formula, Long and Ramakrishna
[
12
]
gave an extension of Van Hamme’s (D.2):

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(6k+1)\frac{(1/3)^{6}_{k}}{k!^{6}}\equiv\left\{\begin{aligned} -p\Gamma_{p}(1/3)^{9}\pmod{p^{6}},\qquad\mathrm{if}\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{6},\\
-\frac{10}{27}p^{4}\Gamma_{p}(1/3)^{9}\pmod{p^{6}},\qquad\mathrm{if}\quad p\equiv 5\pmod{6}.\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly, Liu
[
9
]
established a new supercongruence: for
p\geq 5
,

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(6k-1)\frac{(-1/3)^{6}_{k}}{k!^{6}}\equiv\left\{\begin{aligned} 140p^{4}\Gamma_{p}(2/3)^{9}\pmod{p^{5}},\qquad\mathrm{if}\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{6},\\
378p\Gamma_{p}(2/3)^{9}\pmod{p^{5}},\qquad\mathrm{if}\quad p\equiv 5\pmod{6}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Also, Guo and Schlosser
[
3
]
proposed one conjecture as follows:
for
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{(p+1)/3}(6k-1)\frac{(-1/3)^{4}_{k}(1)_{2k}}{(1)^{4}_{k}(-2/3)_{2k}}\equiv p\pmod{p^{3}}.

(1.2)

By using the hypergeometric series identities and
p
-adic Gamma functions, Jana and Kalita
[
5
]
first confrimed the supercongruence (
1.2
).
Later, based on combinatorial identities arising from symbolic summation, Liu
[
8
]
provided a stronger version of (
1.2
):
for odd primes
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{(p+1)/3}(6k-1)\frac{(-1/3)^{4}_{k}(1)_{2k}}{(1)^{4}_{k}(-2/3)_{2k}}\equiv p-p^{3}\left(\frac{1}{9}B_{p-2}\left(1/3\right)-2\right)\pmod{p^{4}},

(1.3)

where the
Bernoulli polynomials
are given by

\frac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

During the past few years, there has been an increasing attention to the issue of finding
q
-analogues of
congruences and supercongruences.
The reader may be referred to
[
7
,
2
,
13
,
16
,

,
18

17

19
,
11

20
]
for some of their work.
Recently, in
[
3

, Guo and Schlosser gave a partial
q
-analogue of supercongruence (
1.2
):
for integers
n>2

n\equiv 2\pmod{3}

\sum_{k=0}^{(n+1)/3}[6k-1]\frac{\left(q^{-1};q^{3}\right)^{4}_{k}\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)_{2k}}{\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)^{4}_{k}\left(q^{-2};q^{3}\right)_{2k}}q^{4k}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(1.4)

In the same paper, they also conjectured a partial
q
-analogue of Liu’s supercongruence (
1.1
) as follows:
for integers
n>1
with
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[6k-1]\frac{\left(q^{-1};q^{3}\right)^{6}_{k}}{\left(q^{3};q^{3}\right)^{6}_{k}}q^{9k}\equiv 0\pmod{[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}}.

Here and in what follows, the
q
-shifted factorial
is defined as

(a;q)_{0}=1
and
(a;q)_{n}=\left(1-a\right)\left(1-aq\right)\cdots\left(1-aq^{n-1}\right)

For brevity, its product form can be written as

(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m};q)_{n}=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n}

[n]=[n]_{q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}
denotes the
q

Moreover,

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n>1

Theorem 3.5

\displaystyle\frac{\left(b-q^{n}\right)\left(ab-1-a^{2}+aq^{n}\right)}{(a-b)(1-ab)}

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1-aq^{n}\right)\left(a-q^{n}\right)}{(a-b)(1-ab)}

\displaystyle\equiv 1\pmod{b-q^{n}},

and employing the Chinese remainder theorem for coprime polynomials, we arrive at the following result

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-r)/d}[2dk+r]\frac{\left(q^{r},cq^{r},eq^{r},q^{r}/b,aq^{r},q^{r}/a;q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d},q^{d}/c,q^{d}/e,bq^{d},q^{d}/a,aq^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}\left(\frac{b}{ce}\right)^{k}q^{(2d-3r)k}

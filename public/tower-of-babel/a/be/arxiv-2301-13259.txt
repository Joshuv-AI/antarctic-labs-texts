Tests of independence and randomness for arbitrary data using copula-based covariances Thanks: Funding in partial support of this work was provided by the Fonds québécois de la recherche en santé and the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada.
Bouchra R. Nasri, Bruno N. Rémillard

Abstract
. In this article, we study tests of independence for data with arbitrary distributions in the non-serial case, i.e., for independent and identically distributed random vectors, as well as in the serial case, i.e., for time series. These tests are derived from copula-based covariances and their multivariate extensions using Möbius transforms. We find the asymptotic distributions of these statistics under the null hypothesis of independence or randomness, as well as under contiguous alternatives. This enables us to find out locally most powerful test statistics for some alternatives, whatever the margins. Numerical experiments are performed for Wald’s type combinations of these statistics to assess the finite sample performance.



1.
Introduction

In many cases, tests of independence using simple dependence measures like Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho, perform as well as tests based on more complex statistics constructed from empirical processes
(
Blum et al., 1961
)
, and are generally much faster to compute. However, tests based of such measures are not always consistent.
Nevertheless, tests of independence or randomness based on copulas should always be performed. Here, we are interested in copula-based of dependence measures for two cases: (i) the non-serial case, where we have a sample of iid observations
\mathbf{X}_{i}=(X_{i1},\ldots,X_{id})\sim H
, with vector of margins
\mathbf{F}=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{d})
,
d\geq 2
, and we want to test the null hypothesis of independence, i.e.,
H_{0}:H(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}F_{j}(x_{j})
, for all
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; (ii) the serial case, i.e., we have a stationary time series
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 1}
with common margin
F
, and we want to test the null hypothesis of randomness, i.e.,
H_{0}:P(Y_{1}\leq y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\leq y_{n})=\prod_{t=1}^{n}F(y_{t})
for all
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. In the serial case, one defines the random vectors
\mathbf{X}_{t}=(Y_{t},\ldots,Y_{t+1-d})
, and hereafter, the series
Y
is extended in a circular way by setting
Y_{t+n}=Y_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In the bivariate case, when the margins are continuous, many copula-based dependence measures are theoretically defined as the correlation
\varrho_{\mathbf{K}}(C)={\rm cor~}\left\{K_{1}^{-1}(U_{i1}),K_{2}^{-1}(U_{i2})\right\}
, since by continuity of the margins,
\mathbf{U}_{i}=(U_{i1},U_{i2})=\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X}_{i})\sim C
, for a unique copula
C
. Here,
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},K_{2})
is a given vector of cdfs, with mean
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
, and finite variances
\sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2}
. These requirements on
K_{1},K_{2}
are necessary in order for the correlation to exist. The value under independence is clearly
0
. Next, by Hoeffding’s identity
(
Hoeffding, 1940
)
,

(1)

\varrho_{\mathbf{K}}(C)=\frac{\gamma_{\mathbf{K}}(C)}{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[C\left\{K_{1}(x_{1}),K_{2}(x_{2})\right\}-K_{1}(x_{1})K_{2}(x_{2})\right]dx_{1}dx_{2},

since
\left(K_{1}^{-1}(U_{1}),K_{2}^{-1}(U_{2})\right)
has joint cdf
C\circ\mathbf{K}
.
For example, suppose that
(U_{1},U_{2})\sim C
. Then, taking
K_{1}=K_{2}=D
, where
D
is the cdf of the uniform distribution over
(0,1)
, one obtains Spearman’s correlation
\rho_{S}(C)=12E(U_{1}U_{2})-3
. The case
K_{1}=K_{2}=\Phi
, where
\Phi
is the cdf of the standard Gaussian distribution, yields the van der Waerden coefficient
\rho_{vdw}(C)=E\left\{\Phi^{-1}(U_{1})\Phi^{-1}(U_{2})\right\}
. Savage’s coefficient corresponds to
K_{j}(x)\equiv 1-e^{-x}
,
\mu_{j}\equiv 1
, with the convention that
0\log{0}=0
, while if
K_{1}=K_{2}
is the cdf of a Bernoulli(1/2), one gets Blomqvist’s coefficient
4C(1/2,1/2)-1
.
Note that by definition, when
K_{1}=K_{2}
,

\varrho_{K}(C_{+})=1
for the complete dependence, where
C_{+}(u_{1},u_{2})=\min(u_{1},u_{2})
is the Fréchet-Hoeffding upper bound.
However, when
K_{1}\neq K_{2}
, the covariance
\gamma_{K_{1},K_{2}}(C)
must be divided by
E\left\{K_{1}^{-1}(U_{1})K_{2}^{-1}(U_{1})\right\}-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}
to give
1
for complete dependence. Blest’s coefficient
(
Blest, 2000
,
Genest and Plante, 2003
)
can be seen as such an example if one considers a natural modification. In fact, Blest’s coefficient has been originally defined as the covariance between
(1-U_{1})^{2}
and
U_{2}
. An obvious modification is obtained by taking
K_{1}(u)=u^{1/2}
,
u\in[0,1]
,
K_{2}=D
, so the modified coefficient is
12E(U_{1}^{2}U_{2})-2
, normalised to give
1
for complete dependence.
Genest and Plante, 2003
also proposed a symmetrised Blest’s coefficient, which, in our general setting, amounts to defining

\gamma_{K_{1},K_{2}}^{*}(C)=\dfrac{\gamma_{K_{1},K_{2}}(C)+\gamma_{K_{2},K_{1}}(C)}{2}.

Not all copula-based dependence measures are defined by a covariance (up to a constant), a well-known example being Kendall’s tau, defined by

\displaystyle\tau(C)=4\int_{(0,1)^{2}}C(u_{1},u_{2})dC(u_{1},u_{2})-1=4E\{C(U_{1},u_{2})\}-1
.
However, Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho have an equivalent limiting distribution, even under a sequence of contiguous alternatives. There are also other interesting copula-based dependence measures, namely
\phi
-dependence measures, recently studied in
Geenens and Lafaye de Micheaux, 2022
, and defined by
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\phi\{c(u_{1},u_{2})\}du_{2}du_{2}
, where
\phi
is a convex function with
\phi(1)=0
. The case
\phi(t)=t\log{t}
yields mutual information, while
\phi(t)=\left(t^{1/2}-1\right)^{2}
yields Hellinger’s correlation.

Estimating the dependence measures defined previously is relatively straightforward. In the continuous case, the copula is replaced by the empirical copula

\widehat{C}_{n}(u_{1},u_{2})=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}\mathbb{I}\left\{\frac{n}{n+1}F_{n1}(X_{i1})\leq u\right\}\mathbb{I}\left\{\frac{n}{n+1}F_{n2}(X_{i2})\leq u_{2}\right\},\quad u_{1},u_{2}\in[0,1],

where
\displaystyle F_{nj}(x)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}\{X_{ij}\leq x\}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
j\in\{1,2\}
.
In fact,
\gamma_{\mathbf{K}}(C)
can be estimated by
\gamma_{\mathbf{K}}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}\right)
, and according to
Genest and Rémillard, 2004
, one has

\gamma_{\mathbf{K}}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\widehat{C}_{n}\left\{K_{1}(x_{1}),K_{2}(x_{2})\right\}-K_{1}(x_{1})K_{2}(x_{2})\right]dx_{1}dx_{2}.

Asymptotic limits and their representations are easier to work with the latter representation, being a linear functional of the empirical process
\widehat{}\mathbb{C}_{n}(u_{1},u_{2})=n^{1/2}\left\{\hat{C}_{n}(u_{1},u_{2})-u_{1}u_{2}\right\}
.
Tests of independence based on these dependence measures work well when the margins are continuous. However, for applications, there is a need to test independence in the more general setting of arbitrary distributions, i.e., when at least of the margins is not continuous. In this case, since there are ties, one might be tempted to replace the ranks by the mid-ranks. However, the asymptotic distribution might not be simple enough and it makes sense to test independence using copula-based extensions of these dependence measures.

The main problem here is that the copula is not unique. If
\mathbf{X}\sim H
, there are infinitely many copulas satisfying Sklar’s equation

H=C\circ\mathbf{F}

(
Sklar, 1959
)
. To construct solutions for this equation, for any copula
\mathcal{C}
, take
\mathbf{V}\sim\mathcal{C}
independent of
\mathbf{X}\sim H
and set

\mathbf{U}=\psi_{\mathbf{F}}(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{V})
, where
U_{j}=\psi_{F_{j}}(X_{j},V_{j})=F_{j}(X_{j}-)+V_{j}\Delta_{F_{j}}(X_{i})
, with

F_{j}(x-)=P(X_{j}<x)
and
\Delta_{F_{j}}(x)=F_{j}(x)-F_{j}(x-)=P(X_{j}=x)
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. It is known
(
Ferguson, 1967
,
Rüschendorf, 1981
,
Nešlehová, 2007
,
Brockwell, 2007
)

that for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
U_{j}\sim{\rm U}(0,1)
, and the joint cdf
C_{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathbf{U}
is a copula satisfying Sklar’s equation.
In addition, there is one interesting copula
C^{\maltese}
in this family, the so-called multilinear copula, obtained by taking
\mathcal{C}=\Pi
, the independence copula, i.e.,
\Pi(\mathbf{u})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}D(u_{j})
. One interesting property of
C^{\maltese}
is that
if
H(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}F_{j}(x_{j})
, then
C_{\mathcal{C}}=\Pi
if and only if
C_{\mathcal{C}}=C^{\maltese}
.
As a by-product, taking the empirical joint cdf
H_{n}
with the vector of margins
\mathbf{F}_{n}=(F_{n_{1}},F_{n2})
, one obtains the empirical multilinear copula

\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}
, for which an explicit expression will be given in the next section.
Note that contrary to
\widehat{C}_{n}
,
\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}
is a genuine copula, so all dependence measures presented before can
be computed with
C^{\maltese}
and its empirical counterpart
\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}
. This is the approach we propose here. However, since
these statistics are not margin-free anymore, we will no longer call them dependence measures. See, e.g.,
(
Geenens and Lafaye de Micheaux, 2022
, Section 2)
for an interesting discussion on dependence. Note that the asymptotic behaviour of the associated versions of Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho has been studied in
Genest et al., 2014
, and tests of independence based on
\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}
were proposed in
Genest et al., 2019
, while
in the serial case, tests of randomness based on the serial version
\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}
have been studied in
Nasri, 2022
, as well as the asymptotic behaviour of the serial versions of Kendall’s tau and Spearman’s rho. The results in
Nasri, 2022
extend those of
Kojadinovic and Yan, 2011
obtained for continuous observations.

The main aim of this article is to define tests of independence and tests of randomness using bivariate and multivariate extensions of the copula-based dependence measures when the margins are arbitrary, to find explicit expressions of these statistics, and to study their asymptotic behaviour. We will also look at the asymptotic distribution of the test statistics under a sequence of contiguous alternatives to be able to suggest locally powerful tests for given dependence models, in the same spirit as
Genest and Verret, 2005
did in the bivariate case for continuous margins. To this end, we also present a new representation of the multilinear copulas in the serial and non-serial cases that enables us to perform calculations more easily.
Note that in both
Genest et al., 2019
and
Nasri, 2022
, the main focus was on using Cramér-von Mises statistics of related multilinear processes, which is not done here.

In Section
2
, we recall the definitions and properties of multilinear copulas in a serial setting
(
Nasri, 2022
)
and non-serial setting
Genest et al., 2019
, together with their associated Möbius transforms. Next, in Section
3
, we define the serial and non-serial versions of the proposed statistics extending the dependence measures, providing explicit formulas that are easy to implement, and we study their asymptotic behaviour under the null hypothesis of independence or randomness. Multivariate extensions similar to those defined in
Genest and Rémillard, 2004
and
Genest et al., 2014

will also be studied. In addition, under additional moment conditions, one finds the asymptotic limits of these statistics when the null hypothesis is not satisfied. Next, in Section
4
, we study the asymptotic behaviour of the proposed test statistics under a sequence of contiguous alternatives, using the results of
Genest et al., 2019
and
Nasri, 2022
. This enables us to find the locally most powerful tests amongst the class of the proposed tests statistics. We also discuss how to combine the proposed tests statistics. Finally, numerical experiments are performed in Section
5
to assess the power of the tests for finite samples.

2.
Multilinear copulas and associated empirical processes

From now on, we consider the following two settings: the non-serial case and the serial case. In the non-serial case, we have independent and identically distributed (iid) random vectors
\mathbf{U}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{U}_{n}\sim C
, for a given copula
C
, and the observations are
\mathbf{X}_{i}=\mathbf{F}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{i})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. In the serial setting,
we have a stationary and ergodic sequence of random variables
(U_{t})_{t\geq 1}
, and the observed time series is
Y_{t}=F^{-1}(U_{t})
,
t\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
We can now define the multilinear copula. For any
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, set
J_{F_{j}}(x_{j},u_{j})=E\left[\psi_{F_{j}}(X_{j},u_{j})|X_{j}=x_{j}\right]=P\left\{F_{j}(x_{j}-)+V_{j}\Delta_{F_{j}}(x_{j})\leq u_{j}\right\}
. Then,

\displaystyle J_{F_{j}}(x_{j},u_{j})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{I}\{F_{j}(x_{j})\leq u_{j}\},&\text{ if }\Delta_{F_{j}}(x_{j})=0,\\
D\left\{\frac{u_{j}-F_{j}(x_{j}-)}{\Delta_{F_{j}}(x_{j})}\right\},&\text{ if }\Delta_{F_{j}}(x_{j})>0.\end{array}\right.
, where
D
is the cdf of
U\sim{\rm U}(0,1)
. Note that when
\Delta_{F_{j}}(x_{j})>0
,
J_{F_{j}}(x_{j},u_{j})=0
if
u_{j}\leq F_{j}(x_{j}-)
,
J_{F_{j}}(x_{j},u_{j})=1
if
u_{j}\geq F_{j}(x_{j})
, and
J_{F_{j}}(x_{j},u_{j})=\dfrac{u_{j}-F_{j}(x_{j}-)}{\Delta_{F_{j}}(x_{j})}
if
F_{j}(x_{j}-)\leq u_{j}\leq F_{j}(x_{j})
.
Using properties of conditional expectations, one obtains

(2)

C^{\maltese}(\mathbf{u})=E\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{d}J_{F_{j}}(X_{j},u_{j})\right\},\quad\mathbf{u}\in[0,1]^{d}.

As a result,

(3)

\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}(\mathbf{u})=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}J_{F_{nj}}(X_{ij},u_{j})=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}D\left\{\frac{u_{j}-F_{nj}(X_{ij}-)}{\Delta_{F_{nj}}(X_{ij})}\right\},\quad\mathbf{u}\in[0,1]^{d}.

This new expression is different from what appears in the literature, e.g.,
Genest et al., 2017
,
Genest et al., 2019
, but it is easier to manipulate for our purposes. In fact,
\hat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}
was previously defined by

\hat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}(\mathbf{u})=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\Bigl[\lambda_{F_{nj}}(u_{j})\mathbb{I}\{X_{ij}\leq F_{nj}^{-1}(u_{j})\}\\
+\{1-\lambda_{F_{nj}}(u_{j})\}\mathbb{I}\{X_{ij}<F_{nj}^{-1}(u_{j})\}\Bigr],

where, for any cdf
G
and
u\in(0,1)
,

\lambda_{G}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\dfrac{u-G\left\{G^{-1}(u)-\right\}}{\Delta_{G}\left\{G^{-1}(u)\right\}},&\quad\Delta_{G}\left\{G^{-1}(u)\right\}>0,\\
1,&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.
.
Next, the empirical serial multilinear copula, first defined and studied in
Nasri, 2022
, can also be written as

(4)

\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}(\mathbf{u})=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}D\left\{\frac{u_{j}-F_{n}(Y_{t+1-j}-)}{\Delta_{F_{n}}(Y_{t+1-j})}\right\},\qquad\mathbf{u}\in[0,1]^{d},

where
\displaystyle F_{n}(y)=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{I}\{Y_{t+1-j}\leq y\}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, using the circular construction.
Further define the empirical multilinear processes
\widehat{}\mathbb{C}_{n}^{\maltese}=n^{1/2}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}-\Pi\right)
and

\widehat{}\mathbb{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}=n^{1/2}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}-\Pi\right)
.
Next,
let
\mathcal{N}_{d}
be the set of all subsets
A
of
\{1,\ldots,d\}
with
{\rm card~}(A)=|A|>1
, and let

\mathcal{S}_{d}
be the set of all elements
A
of
\mathcal{N}_{d}
with
A\ni 1
. It has been shown, e.g.,
Genest and Rémillard, 2004
,
Ghoudi and Rémillard, 2018
,
Genest et al., 2019
,
Nasri, 2022
, that Möbius transforms of empirical processes have nice asymptotic properties for tests of independence or tests of randomness. To this end, define

(5)

\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese}(\mathbf{u})=\mathcal{M}_{A}\left(\widehat{}\mathbb{C}_{n}^{\maltese}\right)(\mathbf{u})=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j\in A}\left[D\left\{\frac{u_{j}-F_{nj}(X_{ij}-)}{\Delta_{F_{nj}}(X_{ij})}\right\}-u_{j}\right],\quad A\in\mathcal{N}_{d},

(6)

\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}(\mathbf{u})=\mathcal{M}_{A}\left(\widehat{}\mathbb{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}\right)(\mathbf{u})=n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\prod_{j\in A}\left[D\left\{\frac{u_{j}-F_{n}(Y_{t+1-j}-)}{\Delta_{F_{n}}(Y_{t+1-j})}\right\}-u_{j}\right],\quad A\in\mathcal{S}_{d},

where the Möbius transform
\mathcal{M}_{A}
is defined in Appendix
A
.
Next, for any
s,t\in[0,1]
, and any cdf
G
, set

(7)

\Gamma_{G}(s,t)=s\wedge t-st-\sum_{x:\Delta_{G}(x)>0}\mathbb{I}\{G(x-)\leq s\wedge t\leq s\vee t\leq G(x)\}\frac{\left\{(s\wedge t)-G(x-)\right\}\left\{G(x)-s\vee s)\right\}}{\Delta_{G}(x)}.

The main findings of
Genest et al., 2019
and
Nasri, 2022
that we need can be summarised as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Under the null hypothesis of independence,
\left\{\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese}:A\in\mathcal{N}_{d}\right\}
converge jointly in
\ell^{\infty}\left((0,1)^{d}\right)
to independent centred Gaussian processes
\left\{\mathbb{G}_{A}^{\maltese}:A\in\mathcal{N}_{d}\right\}
, where

\displaystyle E\left\{\mathbb{G}_{A}^{\maltese}(\mathbf{u})\mathbb{G}_{A}^{\maltese}(\mathbf{v})\right\}=\prod_{j\in A}\Gamma_{F_{j}}(u_{j},v_{j})
. Under the null hypothesis of randomness,

\left\{\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}:A\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\right\}
converge jointly in
\ell^{\infty}\left((0,1)^{d}\right)
to
independent centred Gaussian processes
\left\{\mathbb{G}_{A}^{\maltese,s}:A\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\right\}
,
where

\displaystyle E\left\{\mathbb{G}_{A}^{\maltese,s}(\mathbf{u})\mathbb{G}_{A}^{\maltese,s}(\mathbf{v})\right\}=\prod_{j\in A}\Gamma_{F}(u_{j},v_{j})
.

Remark 1
.

The formulas for the covariances in Theorem
1
follows from (D.6) and (
7
) in
Nasri, 2022
. One can check that for any
s,t\in[0,1]
,
\Gamma_{G}(s,t)\geq 0
with equality if and only if
s\wedge t=0
or
s\vee t=1
. It is interesting to note that for sets
A
of size
2
,
\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese}
and
\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}
are empirical multilinear copula processes. In fact, for
A=\{j,k\}\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
,
j<k
,

\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese}(u_{1},u_{2})=n^{1/2}\left\{\widehat{C}_{A,n}^{\maltese}(u_{1},u_{2})-u_{1}u_{2}\right\}
,
where
\widehat{C}_{A,n}^{\maltese}
is the empirical multilinear copula for the pairs
(X_{ij},X_{ik})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Similarly, for any
A=\{1,1+\ell\}\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}(u_{1},u_{2})=n^{1/2}\left\{\widehat{C}_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}(u_{1},u_{2})-u_{1}u_{2}\right\}
,
where
C_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}
is the empirical multilinear copula for the pairs
(Y_{t},Y_{t-\ell})
,
t\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

3.
Statistics for testing independence or randomness for arbitrary distributions

From now on, let
\mathbf{K}=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{d})
be a vector of margins with mean
\mu_{j}
and finite variance
\sigma_{j}^{2}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{K_{j}}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}K_{j}^{-1}(v)dv
.
Next, for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, and any cdf
G
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{j,G}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}K_{j}^{-1}\left\{G(x-)+s\Delta_{G}(x)\right\}ds
.
Then
\mathcal{K}_{j,G}(x)=K_{j}^{-1}\{G(x)\}
, if
G
is continuous at
x
, and

\mathcal{K}_{j,G}(x)=\dfrac{\mathcal{L}_{K_{j}}\{G(x)\}-\mathcal{L}_{K_{j}}\{G(x-)\}}{\Delta_{G}(x)}
,
if
G
is not continuous at
x
. The extension of the covariance measures is defined in the following way:

In the non-serial case, for any
A\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
, set

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}\right)=n^{-1/2}(-1)^{|A|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{A}}\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese}\left\{\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})\right\}d\mathbf{x}
,
while in the serial case, set

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}\right)=n^{-1/2}(-1)^{|A|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{A}}\mathbb{G}_{A,n}^{\maltese,s}\left\{\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{x})\right\}d\mathbf{x}
,
A\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
.
It then follows from Proposition
2
in Appendix
A
that for any
A\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
, in the non-serial case,

(8)

\gamma_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}\right)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j\in A}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j,F_{nj}}(X_{ij})-\mu_{j}\right\},

while in the serial case, for any
A\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
,

(9)

\gamma_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese,s}\right)=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\prod_{j\in A}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j,F_{n}}(Y_{t+1-j})-\mu_{j}\right\}.

Example 1
.

For Spearman’s rho,
K_{j}\equiv D
, so
\mathcal{L}_{j}(u)=\frac{u^{2}}{2}
.
For van der Waerden’s coefficient,
K_{j}\equiv\Phi
, so
\mathcal{L}_{j}=-\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}
,
\mu_{j}=0
.
For Savage’s coefficient,
K_{j}(x)\equiv 1-e^{-x}
,
x\geq 0
, so
\mathcal{L}_{j}(u)=u-u\log{u}
,
\mu_{j}=1
, with the convention that
0\log{0}=0
.
Finally, for the modified Blest’s coefficient in the bivariate case,
K_{1}^{-1}(u)=u^{2}
,
u\in[0,1]
,
K_{2}=D
.
As a result, one gets the following formula for
12\gamma_{K_{1},K_{2}}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}\right)
:

2n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{F_{nj}^{2}(X_{ij}-)+F_{nj}(X_{ij}-)F_{nj}(X_{ij})+F_{nj}^{2}(X_{ij})-1\right\}\left\{F_{n2}(X_{i2}-)+F_{n2}(X_{i2})-1\right\}.

Remark 2
.

For continuous margins,

\Delta_{F_{nj}}(X_{ij})=n^{-1}
a.s., so

\mathcal{K}_{j,F_{nj}}(X_{ij})=\dfrac{\mathcal{L}_{j}\{F_{nj}(X_{ij})\}-\mathcal{L}_{j}\{F_{nj}(X_{ij}-)\}}{\Delta_{F_{nj}}(X_{ij})}\approx K_{j}^{-1}\left\{\frac{n}{n+1}F_{nj}(X_{ij})\right\}.

Note that in general,
n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{j}^{-1}\left\{\frac{n}{n+1}F_{nj}(X_{ij})\right\}\neq\mu_{j}
, while

\displaystyle n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{K}_{j,F_{nj}}(X_{ij})=\mu_{j}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.
This shows that even for continuous margins, one should use formulas (

)–(

The following result is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
, the continuous mapping theorem, together with representations (
8
) and (

Corollary 1
.

Under the null hypothesis of independence,
\left\{n^{1/2}\gamma_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(\widehat{C}_{n}^{\maltese}\right):A\in\mathcal{N}_{d}\right\}

\displaystyle\varsigma_{\mathbf{K},\mathbf{F},A}^{2}=\prod_{j\in A}\varsigma_{K_{j},F_{j}}^{2}

In the non-serial case,

4.
Asymptotic behaviour along contiguous alternatives and local power

In this section, we consider contiguous alternatives of the form
C_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}
, where
C_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}}=\Pi
, for some
\theta_{0}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}+n^{-1/2}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
, where
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}
and
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
are column vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{p}
.
In the serial case, it is assumed that the sequence
(U_{t})_{t\geq 1}
is
a
d
-Markov process with copula
C_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}
, meaning that the distribution of
(U_{t},\ldots,U_{t+1-d})
is
C_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}
, with density

(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})
at

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
is

(15)

c_{d-1,\theta_{n}}(u_{d-1},\ldots,u_{1})\times\prod_{t=d}^{n}\frac{c_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(u_{t},u_{t-1},\ldots,u_{t+1-d})}{c_{d-1,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(u_{t-1},\ldots,u_{t+1-d})},

where
\displaystyle c_{d-1,{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(u_{d-1},\ldots,u_{1})=\int_{0}^{1}c_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(s,u_{d-1},\ldots,u_{1})ds

It is assumed that the copula family

is smooth enough, namely that the Conditions 1–2 in
Genest et al., 2019
are met. More precisely, these conditions are that

has a continuous density
c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
continuously differentiable with square integrable gradient
\dot{c}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}

{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}

\dot{c}=\left.\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})\right|_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}}

\mathbf{u}\in(0,1)^{d}

\displaystyle\dot{C}(\mathbf{u})=\int_{(0,\mathbf{u}]}\dot{c}(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}

(16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{(0,1)^{d}}[{n}^{1/2}[\{c_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(\mathbf{u})\}^{1/2}-1]-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\top}\dot{c}(\mathbf{u})/2]^{2}d\mathbf{u}=0.

Here,
\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
is the column vector with components
\partial_{\theta_{j}}f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}

Using the mean value theorem, one can see that the following stronger conditions implies (
16

(17)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{(0,1)^{d}}\sup_{\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}\|\leq n^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\|}\left\|\dot{c}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})-\dot{c}(\mathbf{u})\right\|^{2}d\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(18)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{(0,1)^{d}}\sup_{\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}\|\leq n^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\|}\left\|\dot{c}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})\right\|^{4}d\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

As exemplified in the Appendix

is not square integrable.
Before stating the limiting distribution under the sequence of contiguous alternatives
C_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}
, for any

, set

.
It follows from Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{\mathbf{F}}(q_{A})=\mathcal{M}_{A}\circ\mathbb{M}_{\mathbf{F}}\left(\dot{C}\right)=\mathcal{M}_{A}\left(\dot{C}^{\maltese}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{F^{\otimes d}}(q_{A})=\mathcal{M}_{A}\circ\mathbb{M}_{F^{\otimes d}}\left(\dot{C}\right)=\mathcal{M}_{A}\left(\dot{C}^{\maltese,s}\right)

Under the previous conditions, the following results were obtained by

Under the sequence of contiguous alternatives

4.1.
Applications for local power

First note that for many copula families satisfying the smoothness conditions listed at the beginning of the section, one has
\dot{c}_{A}(\mathbf{u})=\prod_{j\in A}J(u_{j})
, and
J_{j}
is often a quantile function. In this case, choosing
K_{j}^{-1}=J_{j}
would make sense in order to have a non-zero mean, and hence having more local power by maximising formulas
(
21
)–(
22
). This is what was proposed in
Genest and Verret, 2005
in the bivariate case, where the margins were assumed to be continuous. In fact, the next proposition shows that this choice is also optimal for any margins. The proof of the following result is given in Appendix
B.2
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that
\dot{c}_{A}(\mathbf{u})\propto\prod_{j\in A}G_{j}^{-1}(u_{j})
,
\mathbf{u}\in(0,1)^{d}
, where
\mathbf{G}=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{d})
is a vector of margins with means
\left(\tilde{\mu}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\mu}_{d}\right)
and variances
\left(\tilde{\sigma}_{1}^{2},\ldots,\tilde{\sigma}_{d}^{2}\right)
, and assume
U\sim{\rm U}(0,1)
. Then, in the non-serial case

\displaystyle{\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\circ F_{j}^{-1}(U),G_{j}^{-1}(U)\right\}={\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}(X_{j}),\mathcal{G}_{j}(X_{j})\right\}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, where
X_{j}\sim F_{j}
, so

\dot{\gamma}_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(C^{\maltese}\right)=\prod_{j\in A}{\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}(X_{j}),\mathcal{G}_{j}(X_{j})\right\}
. In particular, if
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{G}
, then

\dot{\gamma}_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(C^{\maltese}\right)=\varsigma_{\mathbf{K},\mathbf{F},A}^{2}
. In the serial case,

\displaystyle{\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\circ F^{-1}(U),G_{j}^{-1}(U)\right\}={\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}(X),\mathcal{G}_{j}(X)\right\}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, where
X\sim F
, so
\dot{\gamma}_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(C^{\maltese,s}\right)=\prod_{j\in A}{\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}(X),\mathcal{G}_{j}(X)\right\}
. In particular, if
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{G}
, then

\dot{\gamma}_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(C^{\maltese,s}\right)=\varsigma_{\mathbf{K},F,A}^{2}
.

Remark 6
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
1
, it follows from
Proposition 3 in
Genest and Verret, 2005
that
the ARE between the test based on
\mathbf{K}
, and
\mathbf{G}
is given by

\displaystyle\prod_{j\in A}{\rm cor~}^{2}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}(X_{j}),\mathcal{G}_{j}(X_{j})\right\}

in the non-serial case, and the ARE is

\displaystyle\prod_{j\in A}{\rm cor~}^{2}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}(X),\mathcal{G}_{j}(X)\right\}

in the serial case. This shows that whenever
\dot{c}_{A}(\mathbf{u})\propto\prod_{j\in A}G_{j}^{-1}(u_{j})
, the ARE is maximised by taking
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{G}
. Moreover, this result is independent of the margins, although the solution might not be unique.
This is the case for example for a Bernoulli margin in the serial case. In fact, for any
A\in\mathcal{S}_{d}
, using the coefficients
r_{A,n}
defined in Lemma
1
, one gets that

nr_{A,n}^{2}=\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{A,n}^{2}}{\{p_{n}(1-p_{n})\}^{|A|}}
, where
\displaystyle p_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\{Y_{t}=1\}
, and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{A,n}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\prod_{j\in A}\left[\mathbb{I}\{Y_{t+1-j}=1\}-p_{n}\right]
. In the non-serial case, if all margins are Bernoulli,
\displaystyle p_{nj}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{X_{ij}=1\}
, and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{A,n}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j\in A}\left[\mathbb{I}\{X_{ij}=1\}-p_{nj}\right]
,
A\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
, then
nr_{A,n}^{2}=\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{A,n}^{2}}{\prod_{j\in A}p_{nj}(1-p_{nj})}
.

We now consider some copula families studied in the continuous case by
Genest et al., 2007
.

Example 2
.

If
C_{\theta}
is the equicorrelated Gaussian copula, then

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{A}(\mathbf{u})=\sum_{B\subset A,|B|=2}\prod_{j\in B}\Phi^{-1}(u_{j})
. It follows from (
21
)–(
22
)
that in the non-serial case,

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(C^{\maltese}\right)=\mathbb{I}\{|A|=2\}\prod_{j\in A}{\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\circ F_{j}^{-1}(U),\Phi^{-1}(U)\right\}
,
while in the serial case,

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}_{\mathbf{K},A}\left(C^{\maltese,s}\right)=\mathbb{I}\{|A|=2\}\prod_{j\in A}{\rm cov~}\left\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\circ F^{-1}(U),\Phi^{-1}(U)\right\}
.
As a result, van der Waerden’s coefficients should be locally the most powerful when restricted to pairs, i.e., when
|A|=2
. This is not surprising since it coincides with Pearson’s correlation between
\Phi^{-1}(U_{1})
and
\Phi^{-1}(U_{2})
, if
(U_{1},U_{2})\sim C_{\theta}
.

For the Farlie-Gumbel-Morgensten’s copula family,

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{A}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbb{I}\{A=\{1,\ldots,d\}\}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1-2u_{j})

It follows that in the non-serial case,

so Spearman’s rho with

should be locally the most powerful.

For Claytons’s copula family,

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{A}(\mathbf{u})=\sum_{B\subset A,|B|=2}\prod_{j\in B}(1+\log{u_{j}})

6.
Conclusion

For the non-serial and serial settings, we defined tests of independence and tests of randomness derived from bivariate and multivariate extensions of several known copula-based dependence measures that are usually defined for observations with continuous distributions. We showed that working with observations with arbitrary distributions did not add computational difficulties, and even if the statistics are not margin-free, the simulation results proved that the power was quite good under alternative hypotheses. We also deduced the locally most powerful tests based on proposed statistics for some known copula families, whatever the margins. These results generalise the previous findings of
Genest and Verret, 2005
in the bivariate when the margins were assumed to be continuous. In future work, it would be interesting to study extensions of
\phi
-dependence measures for arbitrary data using the multililinear copula density.

.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{-M}\left[J_{F_{n}}\{X_{i},G(x)\}-J_{F}\{X_{i},G(x)\}\right]dx\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\mathcal{G}_{F_{n}}(X_{i})-\mathcal{G}_{F}(X_{i})\right|^{p}

.
To complete the proof, note that the previous inequalities also hold for a stationary ergodic sequence

.

Appendix C
Supplementary material

Inn this section, we verify the conditions for contiguity, which are

C_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
has a continuous density
c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
continuously differentiable with square integrable gradient
\dot{c}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
in a neighbourhood of
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}
, with
\dot{c}=\left.\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}c_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})\right|_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}={\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}}
,
\mathbf{u}\in(0,1)^{d}
,
\displaystyle\dot{C}(\mathbf{u})=\int_{(0,\mathbf{u}]}\dot{c}(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{s}
, and

(29)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{(0,1)^{d}}[{n}^{1/2}[\{c_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(\mathbf{u})\}^{1/2}-1]-{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\top}\dot{c}(\mathbf{u})/2]^{2}d\mathbf{u}=0.

Using the mean value theorem, one can see that the following stronger conditions implies (
29
):

(30)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{(0,1)^{d}}\sup_{\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}\|\leq n^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\|}\left\|\dot{c}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})-\dot{c}(\mathbf{u})\right\|^{2}d\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(31)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{(0,1)^{d}}\sup_{\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{0}\|\leq n^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\|}\left\|\dot{c}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\mathbf{u})\right\|^{4}d\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\infty.

The latter conditions are met for several bivariate copula families, including the Gaussian, Farlie-Gumbel-Morgenstern, Clayton and Frank. However they do not hold for Gumbel’s copula since
\dot{c}
is not square integrable.

Note that
\dot{c}
must be square integrable since if one uses LeCam’s third lemma
(
van der Vaart and Wellner 1996
)
, which is essential in proving contiguity results, one gets

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log{c_{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}}(\mathbf{U}_{i})}={\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\top}\mathbb{Z}_{n}-\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\top}\Sigma_{n}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}+o+P(1)
converges in law to
{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\top}\mathbb{Z}-\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{\delta}}^{\top}\Sigma{\boldsymbol{\delta}}
,
where
\mathbb{Z}_{n}=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{c}(\mathbf{U}_{i})
,
\Sigma_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{c}(\mathbf{U}_{i})\dot{c}(\mathbf{U}_{i})^{\top}
,
\mathbb{Z}\sim N(0,\Sigma)
, and
\displaystyle\Sigma=\int_{(0,1)^{d}}\dot{c}(\mathbf{u})\dot{c}(\mathbf{u})^{\top}d\mathbf{u}
.

C.1.
Clayton’s copula

In this case, one gets

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{\theta}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\log(u)-\log(v)+\frac{1}{1+\theta}+\theta^{-2}\log\left(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{1+2\theta}{\theta}\right)\frac{\left\{u^{-\theta}\log(u)+v^{-\theta}\log(v)\right\}}{\left(u^{-\theta}+v^{-\theta}-1\right)}.

As a result,
\dot{c}(u,v)=\left\{1+\log(u)\right\}\left\{1+\log(v)\right\}
, and
\dot{c}_{\theta}(u,v)-\dot{c}(u,v)=\theta g(u,v)+o(\theta)
, where

\displaystyle g(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln(u)^{2}+\ln(v)^{2}+8\ln(u)\ln(v)+\ln(v)^{2}\ln(u)^{2}+2\ln(v)+2\ln(u)

\displaystyle\qquad+3\ln(v)\ln(u)^{2}+3\ln(u)\ln(v)^{2},

and
\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)^{2}}\{g(u,v)\}^{4}dudv<\infty
.

C.2.
Farlie-Gumbel-Morgenstern

For this copula family,
c_{\theta}(u,v)=1+\theta(1-2u)(1-2v)
, so
\dot{c}_{\theta}(u,v)\equiv(1-2u)(1-2v)
, and (
30
)–(
31
) are obviously met.

C.3.
Frank’s copula

In this case,
\dot{c}(u,v)=\frac{(1-2u)(1-2v)}{2}
and
\dot{c}_{\theta}(u,v)-\dot{c}(u,v)=\theta p_{1}(u,v)+o(\theta)
uniformly over
(0,1)^{2}
, where
p_{1}(u,v)
is a polynomial in
u
and
v
. As a result, both (
30
)–(
31
) are satisfied.

C.4.
Gaussian copula

For the Gaussian copula with parameter
\rho
, the density is square integrable and a smooth function of
\rho
about
\rho=0
, and conditions (
30
)–(
31
) are met.

C.5.
Gumbel’s copula

This case is much more difficult. Here, setting
x=-\log(u)
and
y=-\log(v)
, one gets

\dot{c}(u,v)=\frac{1}{x+y}-(2-x-y)\log(x+y)-(y-1)\log(y)-(x-1)\log(x),

which is not square integrable.

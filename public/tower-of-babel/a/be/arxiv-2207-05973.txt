Multiple solutions to quasi-linear elliptic Robin systems
U. Guarnotta, S.A. Marano, A. Moussaoui

Abstract
. Two opposite constant-sign solutions to a non-variational p p -Laplacian system with Robin boundary conditions are obtained via sub-super-solution techniques. A third nontrivial one comes out by means of topological degree arguments.

1.
Introduction

Multiplicity of solutions to boundary-value problems for elliptic systems looks an interesting topic, both from a theoretical point of view and as regards applications. In fact, even without convective terms, the problem doesn’t usually have a variational structure (which forces very special reactions) and so abstract results different from mountain-pass like theorems are needed. The most common attempt is using fixed point results or some kind of topological degree. Recently, this was accomplished in the paper
[
14
]
, that investigates the Dirichlet problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p_{1}}u_{1}&=f_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2})\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p_{2}}u_{2}&=f_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2})\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
u_{1}=u_{2}&=0\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
p_{i}\in(1,+\infty)
, the symbol
\Delta_{p_{i}}
denotes the
p_{i}
-Laplacian, i.e.,

\Delta_{p_{i}}u:={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p_{i}-1}\nabla u)\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p_{i}}(\Omega),

\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
f_{i}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2
, satisfy Carathéodory’s conditions. Two opposite constant-sign solutions were obtained via the sub-super-solution method for systems; see, e.g.,
[
3
, Section 5.5]
. Then appropriately using the Leray-Schauder degree gave a third nontrivial (precisely, nodal) solution. Chapter 6 of the monograph
[
12
]
represents an excellent introduction to these topics, while
[
5
]
provides a singular version of
[
14
]
. Recall that (
1.1
) is called singular when for instance
f_{i}:\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\lim_{s_{i}\to 0^{+}}f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})=+\infty
; vide the very recent survey
[
8
]
.

A quick search in the Mathematical Reviews shows that, until today, there are about forty papers on Dirichlet problem, sixteen dealing with the Neumann case, and only one, namely
[
2
]
, devoted to Robin boundary conditions. From a technical point of view, most of them employ variational methods, whereas a few are based on topological degree arguments. This work investigates the non-variational Robin system

(P)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p_{1}}u_{1}&=f_{1}(x,u_{1},u_{2})\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p_{2}}u_{2}&=f_{2}(x,u_{1},u_{2})\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
|\nabla u_{1}|^{p_{1}-2}\nabla u_{1}\cdot\nabla\nu&=-\beta_{1}|u_{1}|^{p_{1}-2}u_{1}\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\\
|\nabla u_{1}|^{p_{2}-2}\nabla u_{2}\cdot\nabla\nu&=-\beta_{2}|u_{2}|^{p_{2}-2}u_{2}\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\nu(x)
indicates the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
at its point
x
while
\beta_{i}>0
. We adapt the approach of
[
14
]
, which basically requires four tools:

•

some natural properties (cf. Proposition
3.1
) of the involved differential operator;

•

a non-existence result (Proposition
3.2
) for a family of auxiliary Robin problems;

•

the anti-maximum principle (Proposition
3.3
) with Robin boundary conditions;

•

a sub-super-solution result (Theorem
3.4
).

Under suitable assumptions (see Section
4
), two opposite constant-sign smooth solutions to (
P
) are obtained in Theorem
4.1
. A third nontrivial solution then comes out through topological degree arguments; cf. Theorem
4.4
. Unfortunately, we were not able to show that it is nodal.

Multiplicity results for elliptic Robin
equations
with complete sign information on the solutions have been established by several authors. Usually, critical point theory, truncation-perturbation techniques, and Morse identity produce at least three non-zero solutions; two of them have opposite constant sign and the third one is nodal. A recent reference work on this topic is
[
15
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(U,\|\cdot\|)
be a real Banach space and let
U^{*}
be its topological dual, with duality brackets
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. An operator
A:U\to U^{*}
is said to be:

•

bounded
if it maps bounded sets into bounded sets.

•

coercive
provided
\displaystyle{\lim_{\|u\|\to+\infty}}\frac{\langle A(u),u\rangle}{\|u\|}=+\infty
.

•

pseudo-monotone
if
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
U
and
\displaystyle{\limsup_{n\to+\infty}}\langle A(u_{n}),u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0
force

\displaystyle{\liminf_{n\to+\infty}}\langle A(u_{n}),u_{n}-v\rangle\geq\langle A(u),u-v\rangle\quad\forall\,v\in U.

•

of type
(\mathrm{S})_{+}
provided

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\;\;\mbox{in $U$,}\;\;\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\langle A(u_{n}),u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0\implies u_{n}\to u\;\;\mbox{in $U$.}

Recall that

\mbox{$A$ continuous and of type $(\mathrm{S})_{+}$}\implies\mbox{$A$ pseudo-monotone.}

Actually, demi-continuity suffices; vide
[
6
, Lemma 6.7]
. Moreover, one has (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Theorem 2.99]
)

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
U
be a reflexive Banach space. If
A:U\to U^{*}
is bounded, continuous, coercive, and pseudo-monotone then
A(U)=U^{*}
.

Henceforth,
\Omega
will denote a bounded domain of the real Euclidean
N
-space
(\mathbb{R}^{N},|\cdot|)
,
N\geq 2
, with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
, on which we will employ the
(N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\sigma
.

If
X(\Omega)
is a measurable real-valued function space on
\Omega
and
u,v\in X(\Omega)
then, by definition,
u<v
means
u(x)<v(x)
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. Analogously for
u\leq v
, etc. Put

X(\Omega)_{+}:=\left\{u\in X(\Omega):u\geq 0\right\}

as well as

[u,v]:=\{w\in X(\Omega):u\leq w\leq v\}

once
u\leq v
. To shorten notation, set
C_{+}:=C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}
. An easy computation yields

{\rm int}(C_{+}):=\{u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}):u(x)>0\;\;\forall\,x\in\overline{\Omega}\}.

Let
p\in[1,+\infty]
. We denote by
p^{\prime}
the conjugate exponent of
p
, while

\|u\|_{p}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{1/p}&\text{if }p<+\infty,\\
&\\
\underset{\Omega}{\esssup}\,|u|&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The symbol
|E|
stands for the
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\},\;\;a^{\pm}:=(\pm a)\vee 0,\;\;a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}\;\;\quad\forall\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally,
g:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a Carathéodory function when:

•

x\mapsto g(x,s_{1},s_{2})
turns out measurable for every
(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

•

(s_{1},s_{2})\mapsto g(x,s_{1},s_{2})
is continuous for almost all
x\in\Omega
.

3.
Auxiliary results

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and let
\beta>0
. Consider the operator
A_{p}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{*}
given by

\langle A_{p}(u),\psi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}x+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\psi\,{\rm d}\sigma\quad\forall\,u,\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

If we define

(3.1)

\mathscr{E}_{p}(u):=\langle A_{p}(u),u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,{\rm d}x+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p}\,{\rm d}\sigma\,,\quad u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),

then
\mathscr{E}_{p}(\cdot)^{1/p}
is a norm on
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
equivalent to the usual one
\|\cdot\|_{1,p}
; cf.
[
13
, Remark 1.54]
. Some basic facts concerning
A_{p}
are collected in the next result.

Proposition 3.1
.

({\rm a}_{1})

A_{p}
is bounded, continuous, strictly monotone, and of type
{\rm(S)_{+}}
.

({\rm a}_{2})

A_{p}
is bijective and
A_{p}^{-1}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{*}\to W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is continuous.

Proof.

Boundedness is trivial, while continuity comes from the same property of the trace operator, for which we refer to
[
13
, Theorem 1.39]
. Since strict monotonicity is a straightforward consequence of the vector inequalities in
[
11
, Chapter 12]
, it remains to verify that
A_{p}
fulfills condition
{\rm(S)_{+}}
. We evidently have
A_{p}=B_{p}+C_{p}
, where

(3.2)

\begin{split}\langle B_{p}(u),\psi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\psi\,{\rm d}x,\quad\langle C_{p}(u),\psi\rangle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\psi\,{\rm d}\sigma.\end{split}

By
[
13
, Proposition 12.14]
the operator
B_{p}
is of type
{\rm(S)_{+}}
, while
C_{p}
turns out monotone. Thus, the assertion follows from
[
7
, Proposition 2.138(c)]
. This shows
({\rm a}_{1})
.

After noting that

\langle A_{p}(u),u\rangle=\mathcal{E}_{p}(u)\geq c\|u\|_{1,p}^{p}\quad\forall\,u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),

Theorem
2.1
and
({\rm a}_{1})
directly yield the bijectivity of
A_{p}
; see also
[
17
, Theorem 26.A]
. Now, pick a sequence
\{f_{n}\}\subseteq W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{*}
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{*}
and write
u_{n}:=A_{p}^{-1}(f_{n})
,
u:=A_{p}^{-1}(f)
. One evidently has:

(3.3)

\langle A_{p}(u_{n}),\psi\rangle=\langle f_{n},\psi\rangle\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\;\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega);

(3.4)

\langle A_{p}(u),\psi\rangle=\langle f,\psi\rangle\quad\forall\,\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

Through (
3.3
), besides (
3.1
), we obtain

\mathscr{E}_{p}(u_{n})\leq\|f_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{*}}\|u_{n}\|_{1,p}\leq c\,\mathscr{E}_{p}(u_{n})^{1/p},\;\;n\in\mathbb{N},

because
\{f_{n}\}
turns out bounded. Therefore,
\{u_{n}\}\subseteq W^{1,p}(\Omega)
enjoys the same property and, taking a sub-sequence if necessary,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\hat{u}
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Accordingly, by (
3.3
) again,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle A_{p}(u_{n}),u_{n}-\hat{u}\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle f_{n},u_{n}-\hat{u}\rangle=0.

Since
A_{p}
is of type
{\rm(S)_{+}}
, this entails
u_{n}\to\hat{u}
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and, a fortiori,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla\hat{u}\|_{p}=0,\;\;\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|u_{n}-\hat{u}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}=0

(recall that the trace operator is continuous).
Thanks to
[
13
, Theorem 2.76]
we thus arrive at

(3.5)

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}

\displaystyle\to|\nabla\hat{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\hat{u}\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{in $L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)$,}

\displaystyle|u_{n}|^{p-2}u_{n}

\displaystyle\to|\hat{u}|^{p-2}\hat{u}\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{in $L^{p^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)$,}

whence

\langle B_{p}(u_{n}),\psi\rangle\to\langle B_{p}(\hat{u}),\psi\rangle\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\langle C_{p}(u_{n}),\psi\rangle\to\langle C_{p}(\hat{u}),\psi\rangle\;\;\forall\,\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

On the other hand, from
f_{n}\to f
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{*}
it follows
\langle f_{n},\psi\rangle\to\langle f,\psi\rangle
whatever
\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Thus, (
3.3
)–(
3.4
) easily lead to

\langle A_{p}(\hat{u}),\psi\rangle=\langle f,\psi\rangle=\langle A_{p}(u),\psi\rangle,\;\;\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

By the strict monotonicity of
A_{p}
, we finally have
\hat{u}=u
. Summing up,
u_{n}\to u
, namely
A_{p}^{-1}(f_{n})\to A_{p}^{-1}(f)
, in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, as desired.
∎

We indicate with
(\lambda_{p},\varphi_{p})
, omitting the dependence on
\beta
when no confusion can arise, the pair of first eigenvalue-eigenfunction of the problem

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p}\varphi&=\lambda|\varphi|^{p-2}\varphi\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
|\nabla\varphi|^{p-2}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\nu&=-\beta|\varphi|^{p-2}\varphi\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Recall that
\varphi_{p}\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
, without loss of generality one can assume
\|\varphi_{p}\|_{p}=1
, and

(3.7)

\lambda_{p}=\inf\left\{\mathscr{E}_{p}(u):\,\|u\|_{p}=1\right\},

the infimum being attained at
\varphi_{p}
. Denote by
\hat{\lambda}_{p}
the second eigenvalue of (
3.6
).

The next two results are chiefly patterned after Proposition 9.64 and Theorem 9.67 in
[
13
]
, respectively.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Then the problem

(
{\rm P}_{\lambda_{p}}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p}u&=\lambda_{p}|u|^{p-2}u+h(x)\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\nu&=-\beta|u|^{p-2}u\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

has no weak solution.

Proof.

Suppose there exists
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
solving (

P

λ
p

). We first show that
u\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
. Testing with
-u^{-}
entails

(3.8)

\mathscr{E}_{p}(u^{-})=\lambda_{p}\|u^{-}\|_{p}^{p}-\int_{\Omega}hu^{-}\,{\rm d}x\leq\lambda_{p}\|u^{-}\|_{p}^{p}.

Via (
3.7
) one has
u^{-}=k\varphi_{p}
for some
k\geq 0
. If
k>0
then
u^{-}\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
. Therefore,
\int_{\Omega}hu^{-}\,{\rm d}x>0
and (
3.8
) becomes

\mathscr{E}_{p}(u^{-})<\lambda_{p}\|u^{-}\|_{p}^{p},

which contradicts (
3.7
). So
k=0
, namely
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
. The conditions on
h
force
u\not\equiv 0
. Thus, standard results from nonlinear regularity theory
[
10
]
and the strong maximum principle
[
16
]
yield
u\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
.

Applying Picone’s identity
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
to
\varphi_{p}
and
u+\varepsilon
, with
\varepsilon>0
, we get

\begin{split}0&\leq\|\nabla\varphi_{p}\|_{p}^{p}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\left(\frac{\varphi_{p}^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\right)\,{\rm d}x\\
&=\|\nabla\varphi_{p}\|_{p}^{p}+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{u+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\varphi_{p}^{p}\,{\rm d}\sigma-\lambda_{p}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{u}{u+\varepsilon}\right)^{p-1}\varphi_{p}^{p}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}\frac{h\varphi_{p}^{p}}{(u+\varepsilon)^{p-1}}\,{\rm d}x.\end{split}

As
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, Beppo Levi’s theorem and the properties of
\varphi_{p}
lead to

0\leq\mathscr{E}_{p}(\varphi_{p})-\lambda_{p}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{h\varphi_{p}^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\,{\rm d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{h\varphi_{p}^{p}}{u^{p-1}}\,{\rm d}x<0.

This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)_{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
\mu\in(\lambda_{p},\lambda_{p}+\delta)
, all solutions of the problem

(
{\rm P}_{\mu}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p}u&=\mu|u|^{p-2}u+h(x)\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\nu&=-\beta|u|^{p-2}u\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega\end{aligned}\right.

belong to
-{\rm int}(C_{+})
.

Proof.

If the conclusion were false we might construct two sequences
\{\mu_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\{u_{n}\}\subseteq W^{1,p}(\Omega)
fulfilling
\mu_{n}\searrow\lambda_{p}
, each
u_{n}
solves
({\rm P}_{\mu_{n}})
, as well as

(3.9)

u_{n}\not\in-{\rm int}(C_{+})\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us first verify that
\{u_{n}\}
is unbounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Supposing

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}<+\infty
, standard results from regularity theory
[
10
]
yield

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}<+\infty
.
Hence, thanks to Ascoli-Arzelà’s theorem,
u_{n}\to u
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
, where a sub-sequence is considered when necessary. As
n\to+\infty
, this entails that
u
solves (

P

λ
p

), against Proposition
3.2
.

Define
M_{n}:=\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}
and observe that, up to sub-sequences,
M_{n}\to+\infty
. Dividing both sides of
({\rm P}_{\mu_{n}})
by
M_{n}^{p-1}
we then obtain

(3.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p}w_{n}&=\mu_{n}|w_{n}|^{p-2}w_{n}+\frac{h(x)}{M_{n}^{p-1}}\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
|\nabla w_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{n}\cdot\nabla\nu&=-\beta|w_{n}|^{p-2}w_{n}\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
w_{n}:=\frac{u_{n}}{M_{n}}
. Since
\|w_{n}\|_{\infty}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, like before, nonlinear regularity and Ascoli-Arzelà’s theorem produce
w_{n}\to w
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. From (
3.10
) it follows that
w
solves (
3.6
), because
\mu_{n}\to\lambda_{p}
while
M_{n}\to+\infty
. Moreover,
\|w\|_{\infty}=1
implies
w\not\equiv 0
, whence either
w\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
or
w\in-{\rm int}(C_{+})
. The condition
w\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
forces
w_{n}\in{\rm int}(C_{+})
for any
n
big enough. Due to (
3.10
) again one evidently has

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{p}w_{n}=\lambda_{p}|w_{n}|^{p-2}w_{n}+(\mu_{n}-\lambda_{p})w_{n}^{p-1}+\frac{h(x)}{M_{n}^{p-1}}\quad&&\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
&|\nabla w_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla w_{n}\cdot\nabla\nu=-\beta|w_{n}|^{p-2}w_{n}\quad&&\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

but this contradicts Proposition
3.2
. So, assume
w\in-{\rm int}(C_{+})
. Then
w_{n}\in-{\rm int}(C_{+})
and, a fortiori,
u_{n}\in-{\rm int}(C_{+})
for all sufficiently large
n
, which, bearing in mind (
3.9
), is absurd.
∎

The section ends by proving a sub-super-solution theorem. To shorten notation, write

X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega):=W^{1,p_{1}}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p_{2}}(\Omega).

This space is equipped with the norm

(u_{1},u_{2})\in X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathscr{E}_{p_{1}}(u_{1})^{1/{p_{1}}}+\mathscr{E}_{p_{2}}(u_{2})^{1/{p_{2}}},

equivalent to the usual one
\|u_{1}\|_{1,p_{1}}+\|u_{2}\|_{1,p_{2}}
.

We say that
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2}),(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})\in X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)
form a sub-super-solution pair for (
P
) provided

(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\leq(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
and

(3.11)

\begin{split}\langle A_{p_{1}}\underline{u}_{1},\psi_{1}\rangle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,\underline{u}_{1},v_{2})\psi_{1}\,{\rm d}x,\;\;\langle A_{p_{2}}\underline{u}_{2},\psi_{2}\rangle\leq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,v_{1},\underline{u}_{2})\psi_{2}\,{\rm d}x,\\
\langle A_{p_{1}}\overline{u}_{1},\psi_{1}\rangle\geq\int_{\Omega}f_{1}(x,\overline{u}_{1},v_{2})\psi_{1}\,{\rm d}x,\;\;\langle A_{p_{2}}\overline{u}_{2},\psi_{2}\rangle\geq\int_{\Omega}f_{2}(x,v_{1},\overline{u}_{2})\psi_{2}\,{\rm d}x\\
\end{split}

for all
(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)_{+}
,
(v_{1},v_{2})\in[\underline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{1}]\times[\underline{u}_{2},\overline{u}_{2}]
. The set

\mathcal{C}:=[\underline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{1}]\times[\underline{u}_{2},\overline{u}_{2}]

is often called
trapping region
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2})
,
(\overline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{2})
be a sub-super-solution pair. Suppose there exists
\mu_{i}>0
such that

(3.12)

|f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})|\leq\mu_{i}\;\;\forall\,x\in\Omega,\;(s_{1},s_{2})\in[\underline{u}_{1}(x),\overline{u}_{1}(x)]\times[\underline{u}_{2}(x),\overline{u}_{2}(x)],

i=1,2
. Then problem (
P
) admits a solution
(u_{1},u_{2})\in[\underline{u}_{1},\overline{u}_{1}]\times[\underline{u}_{2},\overline{u}_{2}]
.

Proof.

The truncation operator
T_{i}:W^{1,p_{i}}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p_{i}}(\Omega)
given by

T_{i}(u)(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\underline{u}_{i}(x)\quad&&\mbox{if}\;\;u(x)\leq\underline{u}_{i}(x),\\
&u_{i}(x)\quad&&\mbox{if}\;\;\underline{u}_{i}(x)\leq u(x)\leq\overline{u}_{i}(x),\\
&\overline{u}_{i}(x)\quad&&\mbox{if}\;\;u(x)\geq\overline{u}_{i}(x).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

is bounded continuous
[
3
, Lemma 2.89]
and monotone, as a simple computation shows. Hence, setting

\langle F_{i}(u_{1},u_{2}),\psi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f_{i}(x,T_{1}(u_{1}),T_{2}(u_{2}))\psi\,{\rm d}x\;\;\forall\,(u_{1},u_{2})\in X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega),\;\psi\in W^{1,p_{i}}(\Omega)

yields a continuous map
F_{i}:X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)\to W^{1,p_{i}}(\Omega)^{*}
such that

(3.13)

\sup\left\{\|F_{i}(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{W^{1,p_{i}}(\Omega)^{*}}:(u_{1},u_{2})\in X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)\right\}<+\infty

because of (
3.12
).
Now, consider the operator
\Phi:X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)\to X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)^{*}
defined by

solves (

). Accordingly, the conclusion rewrites as

.

Via (

\begin{split}\langle\Phi(u_{1},u_{2}),(u_{1},u_{2})\rangle&\geq\mathscr{E}_{p_{1}}(u_{1})+\mathscr{E}_{p_{2}}(u_{2})-\max\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\}(\|u_{1}\|_{1}+\|u_{2}\|_{1})\\

. This entails

namely

fulfills condition

turns out bounded, continuous, coercive, and of type

, whence pseudo-monotone. By Theorem

, there exists
(u_{1},u_{2})\in X^{p_{1},p_{2}}(\Omega)
satisfying

. Let us finally verify that
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{C}
. With this aim, inequalities (

(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})=((u_{1}-\overline{u}_{1})^{+},(u_{2}-\overline{u}_{2})^{+})
lead to

\begin{split}0&=\langle A_{p_{1}}(u_{1})-F_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),(u_{1}-\overline{u}_{1})^{+}\rangle+\langle A_{p_{2}}(u_{2})-F_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}),(u_{2}-\overline{u}_{2})^{+})\rangle\\

The strict monotonicity of

∎

Remark 3.5

4.
Main results

Let

be two Carathéodory functions. The following hypotheses will be posited.

{\rm(H_{1})}

There exist

{\rm(H_{2})}

There are constants
\eta_{i}>\lambda_{p_{i}}
,

, fulfilling

\liminf_{s_{i}\to 0^{+}}\frac{f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})}{|s_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}s_{i}}\geq\eta_{i}\quad\mbox{uniformly in }(x,s_{j})\in\Omega\times[0,k_{j,+}],\;j\neq i,

\liminf_{s_{i}\to 0^{-}}\frac{f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})}{|s_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}s_{i}}\geq\eta_{i}\quad\mbox{uniformly in }(x,s_{j})\in\Omega\times[k_{j,-},0],\;j\neq i.

{\rm(H_{3})}

To each
\rho>0
there corresponds
\mu_{i}>0
such that

|f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})|\leq\mu_{i},\;i=1,2,\;\;\forall\,(x,s_{i},s_{j})\in\Omega\times[-\rho,\rho]\times\mathbb{R},\;j\neq i.

{\rm(H_{4})}

There exist constants
\theta_{i}>\lambda_{p_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, satisfying

\lim_{s_{i}\to-\infty}\frac{f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})}{|s_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}s_{i}}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{s_{i}\to+\infty}\frac{f_{i}(x,s_{1},s_{2})}{|s_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}s_{i}}=\theta_{i}

uniformly with respect to
(x,s_{j})\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
,
j\neq i
.

Proofs below are chiefly patterned after the corresponding ones of
[

]
. The first result gives two constant-sign smooth solutions.

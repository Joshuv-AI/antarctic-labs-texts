New-type Quasirandom Groups and Applications


Abstract
This paper aims to introduce a more general definition of quasirandom groups and generalize several well-known results in the literature in this new setting. More precisely, let G G be a semi-direct product of groups and X ⊆ G X\subseteq G , we provide conditions such that one can find tuples ( x 0 , … , x k ) ∈ X k + 1 (x_{0},\ldots,x_{k})\in X^{k+1} satisfying x 1 ​ x 2 ​ … ​ x k = x 0 x_{1}x_{2}\ldots x_{k}=x_{0} or conditions to guarantee that the product set X ​ X XX grows exponentially. In a special case of the group of rigid-motions in the plane over an arbitrary finite field, our results offer a reasonably complete description of structures of this group.

Introduction

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a finite group and
D
be a positive integer. The group
G
is called
D
-quasirandom
if all non-trivial representations of
G
are of degree at least
D
. This notion was introduced by Gowers
[
5
]
in his solution of the following question due to Babai and Sós
[
2
]
: Does there exist a constant
c>0
such that every finite group
G
has a product-free subset of size at least
c|G|
?

Gowers proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is
D
-quasirandom, then

\left|\#\{(x,y,xy)\in X\times Y\times Z\}-\frac{|X||Y||Z|}{|G|}\right|\ll\sqrt{\frac{|G||X||Y||Z|}{D}}

(1)

for every
X,Y,Z\subseteq G
.

This means that when
X,Y
and
Z
are large enough, then the number of triples
(x,y,xy)\in X\times Y\times Z
is close to the expected value. As a direct application, if
X\subseteq G
is a product-free subset of
G
, then we have
|X|\ll|D|^{-1/3}|G|
. Gowers also asked whether a similar result holds for three-term progressions, i.e. triples of the form
(x,xy,xy^{2})
. This question was solved by Tao
[
9
]
for
SL_{d}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, Peluse
[
8
]
for non-abelian finite simple groups, and by Bhangale, Harsha and Roy
[
3
]
for all finite quasirandom groups.

Another interesting application of the estimate (
1
) is on the growth of product of sets in quasirandom groups. More precisely, for
X,Y\subseteq G
, we set
Z=XY
, then

|XY|\gg\min\left\{|G|,\,\frac{D}{|G|}|X||Y|\right\}.

(2)

We note that a more general statement of this result in the form of
||1_{X}\ast 1_{Y}||_{2}^{2}
was studied by Babai, Nikolov, and Pyber
[
1
]
.

In the setting of the group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, it is well–known that
D=(q-1)/2
(see
[
4
]
, page 102). The estimate (
2
) gives
|XX|\gg\min\left\{q^{3},q^{-2}|X|^{2}\right\}
, so any subset
X
of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
|X|=q^{\alpha}

(\alpha>2)
has exponentially product growth.

It is worth noting that computing the product of elements from a group is a fundamental problem in theoretical computer science. Gowers and Viola
[
6
]
studied mixing in several non-quasirandom groups, and obtained results on communication complexity. One of the models considered in their paper is the affine group
\textsf{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is a semi-product group. Since this group has large subgroups, the estimates (
1
) and (
2
) do not hold anymore.

The main purpose of this paper is to extend the estimate (
1
) in the setting of semi-direct product of groups, i.e. groups
G
of the form
N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
. If
H=\{1\}
(the trivial multiplicative group), then our result recovers the estimate (
1
). Our initial motivation of studying this topic comes from the following question: given a set
X
of rigid-motions in the plane over an arbitrary finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, under what conditions on
X
does the set
XX
grow exponentially?

We start with a simple observation. Consider the group
G_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}SO_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of rigid motions with
|G_{0}|=q^{2}(q\pm 1)
. (See more details in section
1.2
and Example
1.10
.) Let
\gamma
be a generator of the cyclic group
SO_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Assume that
|SO_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=q\pm 1=kl
, and let

\mathcal{X}=\{(z,\gamma^{kj}):\,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2},\,0\leq j\leq l-1\}.

Then
\mathcal{X}
is a subgroup of
G_{0}
and
\mathcal{X}\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}
. Here
|\mathcal{X}|/|G_{0}|=1/k
. This infers that, it is possible to choose an arbitrarily large subset
X\subseteq G_{0}
such that the product
XX
does not grow in general. In other words, a condition on the size of
X
is not enough to guarantee the expanding property of
XX
.

The second observation we want to mention here is that, one can apply the estimate (
2
) on
G_{0}
to obtain that

|\mathcal{X}|=|\mathcal{X}\mathcal{X}|\gg\min\left\{q^{3},~\frac{D}{q^{3}}|\mathcal{X}|^{2}\right\}\gg\frac{D}{k}|\mathcal{X}|.

The above example suggests that the number
D
for the group of rigid motions should be at most a constant which does not depend on
q
. This is true and will be confirmed in Theorem
1.7
below.

Putting these two observations together, we realize that in order to understand structures of the product set
XX
, a deeper studying is needed.

In this paper, our results will be stated and proved in the setting of semi-direct product groups. The group of rigid-motions is a special case. The main tool we will use is non-abelian Fourier analysis.

1.1
Main results on semi-direct product groups

Let
(N,\cdot)
and
(H,\cdot)
be two groups with identities
1_{N}
and
1_{H}
, respectively. Assume that
\varphi:\,H\rightarrow Aut(N)
is a group homomorphism. For simplicity, we denote
\varphi_{h}=\varphi(h)
for
h\in H
. The semi-direct product
(G,\cdot)=(N,\cdot)\rtimes_{\varphi}(H,\cdot)
is a group of order
|N||H|
. More explicitly, one has

G=\{(z,h):\,z\in N,h\in H\}

with the group law given by

(z_{1},h_{1})(z_{2},h_{2})=(z_{1}\,\varphi_{h_{1}}(z_{2}),\,h_{1}h_{2}).

Through this paper, for each
g\in G
, we write
g:=(\ddot{g},\dot{g})
or
g=(g^{\cdot\cdot},g^{\cdot})
.

The subgroup
\widetilde{N}:=\{(z,1_{H}):\,z\in N\}
is a normal subgroup of
G
, which gives

G/\widetilde{N}\simeq H.

Denote by
\pi:\,G\rightarrow G/\widetilde{N}
the quotient map. If
\rho:\,G\rightarrow GL(V)
is a complex representation of
G
such that
\rho|_{\widetilde{N}}
is the identity, then
\textsf{Ker}(\rho)\supseteq\widetilde{N}
. As a result, there is a unique homomorphism
\dot{\rho}:\,G/\widetilde{N}\rightarrow GL(V)
such that
\rho=\dot{\rho}\pi
. Here, we can view
\dot{\rho}
as a representation of
H
. On the other hand, for any representation
\dot{\rho}
of
H\simeq G/\widetilde{N}
, the homomorphism
\rho=\dot{\rho}\pi
is a representation of
G
.

Now all the representations of
G
can be divided into the following two types:

Type I:
a representation
\rho
that is lifted from some representation
\dot{\rho}
of
H
;

Type II:
a representation
\rho
such that
\rho((z,1_{H}))
is not the identity for some
z\in N
.

We are interested in the situation that type II representations have large degree.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
D
be a positive number. We say that
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
is
D
-quasirandom on
N
if the degree of any representation of
G
of type II is at least
D
.

We note that if a group
G^{\prime}
is
D
-quasirandom in Gowers’s definition, then it is
D
-quasirandom in this definition by viewing
G^{\prime}\simeq G^{\prime}\rtimes\{1\}
.
However, the inverse is not true, the group of rigid-motions (with Theorem
1.7
below) is an example.

Our first result states as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
be defined as previous, which is
D
-quasirandom on
N
. For any
k\geq 2
and
X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\subseteq G
, denote

\mathcal{M}_{k}=\#\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in X_{0}\times X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{k}:\,x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{k}=x_{0}\},

and

\dot{\mathcal{M}_{k}}=\#\{(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in X_{0}\times X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{k}:\,\dot{x_{1}}\dot{x_{2}}\cdots\dot{x_{k}}=\dot{x_{0}}\}.

Then

\left|\mathcal{M}_{k}-\frac{1}{|N|}\dot{\mathcal{M}_{k}}\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{|G|^{k-1}|{X_{0}}||{X_{1}}|\ldots|{X_{k}}|}{D^{k-1}}}.

Assume that
G^{\prime}
is
D
-quasirandom. Taking
k=2
and
G=G^{\prime}\rtimes\{1\}
in the above theorem, one has
\dot{\mathcal{M}_{2}}=|X_{0}||X_{1}||X_{2}|
. Then Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Theorem
1.3
.

For any subset
X\subseteq G
, denote by
1_{X}
the characteristic function of
X
, i.e.,

1_{X}(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad&\text{if }x\in X,\\
0,\quad&\text{if }x\notin X.\end{cases}

The next theorem shows upper and lower bounds for convolutions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
be defined as previous, which is
D
-quasirandom on
N
. For any integer
k\geq 2
and subsets
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k}
of
G
, we have

\frac{|H|\dot{\mathcal{N}_{k}}}{|G|^{2k}}\leq\|1_{X_{1}}\ast 1_{X_{2}}\ast\ldots\ast 1_{X_{k}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{|H|\dot{\mathcal{N}_{k}}}{|G|^{2k}}+\frac{|X_{1}||X_{2}|\ldots|X_{k}|}{D^{k-1}|G|^{k}},

where

\dot{\mathcal{N}_{k}}=\#\{(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k})\in(X_{1}\times X_{2}\times\ldots\times X_{k})^{2}:\,\dot{x_{1}}\dot{x_{2}}\cdots\dot{x_{k}}=\dot{y_{1}}\dot{y_{2}}\cdots\dot{y_{k}}\}.

For any
X,Y\subseteq G
, the energies
E(X,Y)
and
\dot{E}(X,Y)
are defined by

E(X,Y):=\#\{(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})\in X^{2}\times Y^{2}:\,x_{1}y_{1}=x_{2}y_{2}\},

and

\dot{E}(X,Y):=\#\{(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})\in X^{2}\times Y^{2}:\,\dot{x_{1}}\dot{y_{1}}=\dot{x_{2}}\dot{y_{2}}\}.

The next corollary follows directly from Theorem
1.4
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
be defined as previous, which is
D
-quasirandom on
N
. For any
X,Y\subseteq G
, we have

\frac{|H|}{|G|}\dot{E}(X,Y)\leq E(X,Y)\leq\frac{|H|}{|G|}\dot{E}(X,Y)+\frac{|G|}{D}|X||Y|.

The next theorem provides an estimate of possible product growth.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
and
\dot{\mathcal{N}_{k}}
be defined as previous. Suppose that
G
is
D
-quasirandom on
N
. For an integer
k\geq 2
and subsets
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{k}
of
G
, we have

|X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{k}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\,\frac{|N|}{\dot{\mathcal{N}_{k}}}|X_{1}|^{2}|X_{2}|^{2}\cdots|X_{k}|^{2},\,\,\frac{D^{k-1}}{|G|^{k-1}}|X_{1}||X_{2}|\cdots|X_{k}|\,\right\}.

1.2
Applications

Let
q
be an odd prime power. We now take
N_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
and
H_{0}=SO_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Define the group homomorphism
\varphi:\,H_{0}\rightarrow\textsf{Aut}(N_{0})
by
\varphi(h):=\varphi_{h}
with
\varphi_{h}(x)=hx
. Take the semi-direct product
(G_{0},\cdot)=(N_{0},+)\rtimes_{\varphi}(H_{0},\cdot)
. The set on the right-hand side is

\{(z,h):\,z\in N_{0},\,h\in H_{0}\},

with the group law given by

(z_{1},h_{1})(z_{2},h_{2})=(z_{1}+h_{1}z_{2},\,h_{1}h_{2}).

The group
G_{0}
consists of the rigid-motions that preserve ‘distances’ between pairs of points. For any
(z,h)\in G_{0}
, this affine transformation is given by
(z,h):\,N_{0}\rightarrow N_{0}
with

(z,h)x=z+hx.

The group
H_{0}
is given explicitly by

H_{0}=\left\{\left[\begin{matrix}a&-b\\
b&a\end{matrix}\right]:\,a^{2}+b^{2}=1\right\}.

It is a cyclic group of order
q-\varepsilon_{q}
, where

\varepsilon_{q}=\left(\frac{-1}{q}\right)=\begin{cases}1,\quad&\text{if }q\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }4),\\
-1,\quad&\text{if }q\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4).\end{cases}

In the next result, we show that
G_{0}
is
(q-\varepsilon_{q})
-quasirandom on
N_{0}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

The group
G_{0}
has
q-\varepsilon_{q}
type I irreducible complex representations of degree
1
, and has
q+\varepsilon_{q}
type II irreducible complex representations of degree
q-\varepsilon_{q}
.

Remark 1.1
.

One can similarly consider the group
G_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, which is composed of affine maps that are area–triangle–invariant. It is not hard to prove that
G_{1}
is
(q-1)/2
-quasirandom by using Theorem
1.7
. Indeed, all complex representations of
G_{1}
of type II has degree no less than
q-\varepsilon_{q}
, and all non-trivial representations of
G_{1}
of type I has dimension no less than
(q-1)/2
.

Note that
|G_{0}|=(q-\varepsilon_{q})q^{2}
. Applying Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
on the group
G_{0}
, and combining Theorem
1.7
, we obtain the following two theorems immediately.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
X,Y,Z\subseteq G_{0}
, we have

\left|\#\{(x,y,z)\in X\times Y\times Z:\,xy=z\}-\frac{\dot{\mathcal{M}_{2}}}{q^{2}}\right|\leq q\sqrt{|X||Y||Z|},

(3)

where

\dot{\mathcal{M}_{2}}=\#\{(x,y,z)\in X\times Y\times Z:\,\dot{x}\dot{y}=\dot{z}\}.

Theorem 1.9
.

For
X,Y\subseteq G_{0}
, we have

|XY|\gg\min\left\{\frac{q^{2}|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{\#\{(g_{1},g_{2},h_{1},h_{2})\in X^{2}\times Y^{2}:\,\dot{g_{1}}\dot{h_{1}}=\dot{g_{2}}\dot{h_{2}}\}},\,\frac{|X||Y|}{q^{2}}\right\}.

(4)

When the set
X
is close to a large subgroup of
G
, the error term
q|X|^{3/2}
in (
3
) and the original main term
q^{-1}|X|^{3}
in (
1
) may be both smaller than the number of triples
(x,y,z)\in X^{3}
taken into consideration. So the refinement of the main term in (
3
) is necessary. Moreover, the second term
q^{-2}|X|^{2}
on the right-hand side of (
4
) may be larger than
|XX|
. So the replacement of the first term on the right-hand side of (
2
) by that of (
4
) is also necessary. The details are given in the following example, which also shows the sharpness of Theorem
1.9
.

Example 1.10
.

Let
\gamma
be a generator of the cyclic group
H_{0}
. Assume that
|H_{0}|=q-\varepsilon_{q}=kl
,
a=\gamma^{k}
, and let

\mathcal{X}=\{(t,a^{j}):\,t\in N_{0},\,0\leq j\leq l-1\}.

It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{X}
is a subgroup of
G
. So,

|\mathcal{X}\mathcal{X}|=|\mathcal{X}|=lq^{2}.

and

\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:\,xy=z\}=|\mathcal{X}|^{2}=l^{2}q^{4}.

The error term in (
3
) is
q|\mathcal{X}|^{3/2}=l^{3/2}q^{4}\leq l^{2}q^{4}
, and the original main term in (
1
) is about
q^{-3}|\mathcal{X}|^{3}=l^{3}q^{3}\leq l^{2}q^{4}
. And the second term on the right-hand side of (
4
) is
q^{-2}|\mathcal{X}|^{2}=l^{2}q^{2}\geq lq^{2}
.

To calculate the quantities

\dot{\mathcal{M}_{2}}=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{X}^{3}:\,\dot{x}\dot{y}=\dot{z}\}~~\mbox{and}~~\dot{\mathcal{N}_{2}}=\#\{(g_{1},g_{2},h_{1},h_{2})\in\mathcal{X}^{4}:\,\dot{g_{1}}\dot{h_{1}}=\dot{g_{2}}\dot{h_{2}}\},

we see that for given
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, one has
\dot{x}\dot{y}\in\{a^{j}:\,0\leq j\leq l-1\}
and
\#\{z\in X:\,\dot{z}=\dot{x}\dot{y}\}=|N_{0}|.

It follows that
\dot{\mathcal{M}_{2}}=|\mathcal{X}|^{2}|N_{0}|
. Similarly,
\dot{\mathcal{N}_{2}}=|\mathcal{X}|^{3}|N_{0}|
.
Thus, Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
show that

|l^{2}q^{4}-l^{2}q^{4}|\leq l^{3/2}q^{4}~~\mbox{ and }~~lq^{2}\,\gg\,\min\{lq^{2},\,l^{2}q^{2}\}.

In particular, Theorem
1.9
is optimal in this example.

Remark 1.2
.

If the reader is interested in analogs of Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
in the setting of the group
G_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, then similar results can be derived in the same way by using Remark
1.1
.

Denote by
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{q-\varepsilon_{q}-1}
the irreducible representations of
G_{0}
of type I. When
X=Y
, the next theorem offers a lower bound in terms of the Fourier bias of the set
X
.

Theorem 1.11
.

For
X\subseteq G_{0}
, assume that

|\widehat{1_{X}}(\rho_{r})|\leq M

for all but
k
indices
r\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,q-\varepsilon_{q}-1\}
. Then

|XX|\gg\min\left\{\frac{q^{3}}{k},\,\frac{|X|^{4}}{q^{10}M^{4}},\,\frac{|X|^{2}}{q^{2}}\right\}.

When
M=0
, the second term on the right-hand side of above formula can be omitted.

Example
1.10
also shows that this theorem is sharp. The details are given in Example
5.1
.

In the next theorem, we prove that if the set
X
satisfies certain properties, then the product
XX
grows exponentially.

For two points
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
, define

\|x-y\|=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}.

We say that
(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
is a line segment of length
t
if
\|x-y\|=t
. Note that rigid motions map line segments to line segments of same length. Now let
E
be a given subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with
|E|>q^{3/2}
. For
t\neq 0
, let
n_{t}
be the number of line segments
(x,y)\in E\times E
such that
||x-y||=t
. Iosevich and Rudnev
[
7
]
proved that
n_{t}=(1+o(1))|E|^{2}/q
, here
o(1)\to 0
as
q\to\infty
. Fix a line segment
(u_{0},v_{0})\in E\times E
of length
t
. For each
(x,y)\in E\times E
such that
||x-y||=t
, there exists a unique rigid motion
(z,\theta)\in G_{0}
such that
(z,\theta)x=u_{0}
and
(z,\theta)y=v_{0}
. Let
X_{t}
be the set of all corresponding rigid motions when
(x,y)\in E\times E
runs over all pairs of length
t
. Then
|X_{t}|=n_{t}=(1+o(1))|E|^{2}/q
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
with
|E|=q^{\alpha}
and
\alpha\in(3/2,2)
. Let
t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
. Then there exists
\delta=\delta(\alpha)>0
such that

|X_{t}X_{t}|\gg|X_{t}|^{1+\delta}.

In particular, we have

|X_{t}X_{t}|\gg\min\left\{q|E|,~\frac{|E|^{4}}{q^{4}}\right\}.

In the above theorem,
|X_{t}X_{t}|\gg q^{3}
if and only if
|E|\gg q^{2}
. This raises the following question: does there exist
\epsilon>0
such that
|X_{t}X_{t}|\gg q^{3}
whenever
|E|\gg q^{2-\epsilon}
?

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic properties of semi-driect products and non-abelian Fourier analysis.

In
G=N\rtimes_{\varphi}H
, the identity element is
(1_{N},1_{H})
, and the inverse is given by

(z,h)^{-1}=(\varphi_{h^{-1}}(z^{-1}),h^{-1}).

The product of two elements
(z_{1},h_{1})
and
(z_{2},h_{2})
is determined by

(z_{1},h_{1})(z_{2},h_{2})=(z_{1}\,\varphi_{h_{1}}(z_{2}),\,h_{1}h_{2}).

Recall the notation
g:=(\ddot{g},\dot{g})=(g^{\cdot\cdot},g^{\cdot})
for
g\in G
. It follows from

g_{1}g_{2}=(\ddot{g_{1}},\dot{g_{1}})(\ddot{g_{2}},\dot{g_{2}})=(\ddot{g_{1}}\,\varphi_{\dot{g_{1}}}(\ddot{g_{2}}),\,\dot{g_{1}}\dot{g_{2}}),\quad g^{-1}=(\ddot{g},\dot{g})^{-1}=(\varphi_{\dot{g}^{-1}}(\ddot{g}^{-1}),\dot{g}^{-1})

that
(g_{1}g_{2})^{\cdot}=\dot{g_{1}}\dot{g_{2}}
and
(g^{-1})^{\cdot}=(\dot{g})^{-1}
.

Let
\widehat{G}
and
\widehat{H}
be the sets of all (non-isomorphic) irreducible complex representations of
G
and
H
, respectively.
Let
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
be positive integers. Assume that
\widehat{H}=\{\dot{\rho_{0}},\dot{\rho_{1}},\ldots,\dot{\rho}_{Q-1}\}
, here
\dot{\rho_{0}}
is the trivial representation. Then all irreducible representations of
G
of type I is given by
\widehat{G}_{I}=\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{Q-1}\}
, where

\rho_{r}(g)=\dot{\rho_{r}}(\dot{g}),\quad(g\in G,\,\,0\leq r\leq Q-1).

Here
\rho_{0}
is the trivial representation. We also denote the set of the irreducible representations of
G
of type II by
\widehat{G}_{II}=\{\rho^{\prime}_{1},\rho_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,\rho_{Q^{\prime}}^{\prime}\}
. Therefore,

\widehat{G}=\{\rho_{0},\ldots,\rho_{Q-1},\rho_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\rho_{Q^{\prime}}^{\prime}\}.

For each representation
\rho
, its degree will be denoted by
d_{\rho}
, and its character, denoted by
\chi_{\rho}\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
, is defined by
\chi_{\rho}(x)=\textsf{Tr}(\rho(x))
.

The main tool is non-abelian Fourier analysis. For each representation
(\rho,V)
with
\rho\in\widehat{G}
, we may assume that the inner product on
V
is chosen so that
\rho
is unitary. Assume that the inner product and norm on
V

|G_{0}|=Qq^{2}
. Since
H_{0}
is an cyclic group, the type I irreducible representations of
G_{0}
are lifted from representations of
H_{0}
, and all have degree
1
. More explicitly, let
\gamma
be a generator of
H_{0}
, i.e.,
H_{0}=\{\gamma^{j}:\,0\leq j\leq Q-1\}
, then the representations can be given by

(7)

Moreover, since

is an abelian subgroup of
G_{0}
with

, the dimension of any irreducible representation of
G_{0}
is no larger than

.

In the following, we will find all conjugacy classes of
G_{0}
and then determine the degree of type II representations. For
(z,1)\in G_{0}
with

,

(w,k)(z,1)(w,k)^{-1}=(kz,1)

for any
(w,k)\in G_{0}
. Here

, since

is invertible. Now the conjugacy class of

[(z,1)]=\{(kz,1):\,k\in H_{0}\}.

It follows from (

\#[(z,1)]=|\textsf{Orb}_{H_{0}}(z)|=Q.

Indeed, the set

disjoint conjugacy classes, each of cardinality
Q
. Moreover,
[(0,1)]=\{(0,1)\}

Next we consider the conjugacy class of

for some

(w,k)(0,h)(w,k)^{-1}=\big((1-h)w,h\big)

(h\neq 1)

It follows that the equality holds, i.e.,

for all
j=1,2,\ldots,Q^{\prime}
. The proof is completed.

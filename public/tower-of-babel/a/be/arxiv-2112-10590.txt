The Scattering of Dirac and Majorana Fermions in Spherically Symmetric Gravitational Field and Torsion Field
Junhui Lai, Xun Xue

Abstract
The possibility to distinguish Dirac from Majorana fermion by gravitational interaction and torsion coupling is discussed. The scattering of both Dirac and Majorana particles are the same in the spherically symmetric gravitational field up to the first order perturbation of the fermion-gravity interaction. The scattering behaviors of Dirac and Majorana fermion are also the same by axial vector torsion but different by vector torsion. We get the conclusion that the scattering by vector torsion field can distinguish the Dirac from Majorana neutrino.

Tetrad fields and spin connection

The tetrad fields
{e^{\mu}}_{a}(x),a=0,1,2,3
are defined as

{e^{\mu}}_{a}{e^{\nu}}_{b}{g_{\mu\nu}}={\eta_{ab}}\;,

(1)

where we use Greek and Latin letters to represent space-time and tangent space index respectively. Then we can define Gamma matrices with space-time index

{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)={e^{\mu}}_{a}(x){\gamma^{a}}

(2)

{\Gamma_{\mu a}}^{b}={e_{\beta}}^{b}{\Gamma_{\mu\alpha}}^{\beta}{e^{\alpha}}_{a}+{e_{\alpha}}^{b}{\partial_{\mu}}{e^{\alpha}}_{a}={e_{\alpha}}^{b}{\nabla_{\mu}}{e^{\alpha}}_{a}\;.

(3)

[
25

{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}={{\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}+{K_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}\;,

(4)

where
{{\tilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}
is the Christoffel symbol, spacetime indexed Levi-Civata connection and
{K_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}
is the contortion tensor.
It can be derived from the formula for the spin connection (
3

{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}={e_{\alpha b}}{\nabla_{\mu}}{e^{\alpha}}_{a}={\nabla_{\mu}}\left({e_{\alpha b}}{e^{\alpha}}_{a}\right)-{e^{\alpha}}_{a}{\nabla_{\mu}}{e_{\alpha b}}=-{e^{\alpha}}_{a}{\nabla_{\mu}}{e_{\alpha b}}=-{\Gamma_{\mu ba}}\;,

(5)

{\nabla_{\mu}}\left({e_{\alpha b}}{e^{\alpha}}_{a}\right)=0
holds. The metric preserving property of spin connection indicates that the spin connection is Lorentzian, i.e. it takes value in the Lorentz Lie algebra. The spin connection defines the Fock-Ivanenko covariant derivative

{D_{\mu}}={\partial_{\mu}}-\frac{i}{2}{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{S^{ab}}\;,

(6)

where the second part acts only on the tangent space indices and the Lorentz group generators
S^{ab}
act on field according to the specific Lorentz representation that the field belongs. The Fock-Ivanenko covariant derivative of a field transforming according to some specific representation of local Lorentz group falls to the tensor product of vector and the representation of the field in consideration so it is the covariant derivative of local Lorentz gauge transformation in this sense.
For example, the covariant derivative of vector fields and spinor fields are

{D_{\mu}}{A^{d}}={\partial_{\mu}}{A^{d}}-\frac{i}{2}{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{({V^{ab}})_{c}}^{d}{A^{c}}={\partial_{\mu}}{A^{d}}+{\Gamma_{\mu b}}^{d}{A^{b}}

(7)

and

{D_{\mu}}\psi={\partial_{\mu}}\psi-\frac{i}{2}{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{S^{ab}}\psi

(8)

respectively,
where

{({V^{ab}})_{c}}^{d}=i({\eta^{bd}}{\delta^{a}}_{c}-{\eta^{ad}}{\delta^{b}}_{c})

(9)

and

{S^{ab}}=\frac{1}{2}{\sigma^{ab}}=\frac{i}{4}[{\gamma^{a}},{\gamma^{b}}]

(10)

are the vector representation and spinor representation of generators respectively. For scalar field
\phi
, the generators in the Fock-Ivanenko covariant derivative (
6

{S^{ab}}=0\;.

(11)

The spacetime tensor field transforms in the way of the same type of tensor under local Lorentz transformation. Hence the covariant derivative (
6

{D_{\mu}}({\partial_{\nu}}\psi)={\partial_{\mu}}({\partial_{\nu}}\psi)-\frac{i}{2}{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{S^{ab}}{\partial_{\nu}}\psi+\frac{i}{2}{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{({V^{ab}})_{\nu}}^{\lambda}{\partial_{\lambda}}\psi={\nabla_{\mu}}({\partial_{\nu}}\psi)-\frac{i}{2}{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{S^{ab}}{\partial_{\nu}}\psi\;.

(12)

Utilizing this property of the covariant derivative, one can prove

[{D_{\mu}},{D_{\nu}}]=-{T_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}{D_{\lambda}}-\frac{i}{4}{R_{\mu\nu ab}}{\sigma^{ab}}\;,

(13)

where
{T_{\mu\nu}}^{\lambda}

25

\displaystyle={e^{\nu a}}\left[{({e_{\lambda a}}{\nabla_{\mu}}{e^{\lambda}}_{c}){\gamma^{c}}+{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{\gamma^{b}}}\right]

\displaystyle={e^{\nu a}}\left[{{\Gamma_{\mu ba}}+{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}}\right]{\gamma^{b}}

So for a spinor field

(15)

Fermion scattering cross section by gravitational field

3.1
Fermion Lagrangian in curved spacetime background

To investigate the Fermion scattering cross section by gravitational field in quantum field theory scheme, one needs to write down the Lagrangian of Fermion in the curved spacetime background. Replacing the partial derivative with covariant derivative in the Lagrangian of fermion in flat space-time,

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\bar{\psi}({\partial}-m)\psi\;,

(17)

one can get the Lagrangian in curved space-time,

\mathcal{L}=\bar{\psi}(i{\gamma^{\mu}}{D_{\mu}}-m)\psi\;.

(18)

Observing that the Lagrangian (
18
) is not self-adjoint,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}^{*}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}}

(19)

\displaystyle={\left[{\bar{\psi}\left({i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x){\partial_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{4}{\gamma^{\mu}}(x){\Gamma_{\mu}}-m}\right)\psi}\right]^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=-{\partial_{\mu}}\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi+\frac{1}{4}{\psi^{+}}{\gamma^{0}}{\Gamma_{\mu}}{\gamma^{0}}{\gamma^{0}}{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi-m\bar{\psi}\psi

\displaystyle=-{\nabla_{\mu}}\left({\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi}\right)+\bar{\psi}i{\nabla_{\mu}}{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi+\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x){\partial_{\mu}}\psi+\frac{1}{4}\bar{\psi}{\Gamma_{\mu}}{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi-m\bar{\psi}\psi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}-{\nabla_{\mu}}\left({\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi}\right)+\bar{\psi}i[{D_{\mu}},{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)]\psi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}-{\nabla_{\mu}}\left({\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi}\right)\;,

where
{\Gamma_{\mu}}={\Gamma_{\mu ab}}{\sigma^{ab}}
. It differs from its real part only a total divergence term,
{\nabla_{\mu}}\left({\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi}\right)
.

\mathcal{L}
can be re-written as

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}^{*})+\frac{1}{2}{\nabla_{\mu}}\left({\bar{\psi}i{\gamma^{\mu}}(x)\psi}\right)

(20)

and is indeed a well defined Lagrangian. The corresponding action is

S=\int{{d^{4}}x\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}}\;.

(21)

We will concentrate on the weak field limit of the static spherically symmetric gravitational field background. To take account the weak field limit, we will consider the metric deviation

{h_{\mu\nu}}={g_{\mu\nu}}-{\eta_{\mu\nu}}

(22)

and spin connection
{\Gamma_{\mu ab}}
as infinitesimal quantities. Up to the first order approximation, we have

{{\sqrt{-g}}\cong 1-\frac{1}{2}{h^{\mu}}_{\mu}}

(23)

and

{e^{\mu}}_{a}\cong{\delta^{\mu}}_{a}-\frac{1}{2}{h^{\mu}}_{a}+{\omega^{\mu}}_{a}\;,

(24)

where
{\omega^{\mu}}_{a}
, satisfying
{\omega_{ab}}=-{\omega_{ba}}
, are the pure inertial part of tetrad fields with six degrees of freedom generated by local Lorentz transformation. One can always remove the inertial
{\omega^{\mu}}_{a}
by choosing the appropriate local tetrad fields without losing generality, i.e. choosing the preferred tetrad fields satisfying
{\omega^{\mu}}_{a}=0
.
The spacetime indices and the tangent space one play the same role for the covariant quantities in the first order approximation, e.g.
{h^{\mu}}_{a}={e^{\mu}}_{b}{h^{b}}_{a}\cong{\delta^{\mu}}_{b}{h^{b}}_{a}
.
Up to the first order approximation, the action (
21
) can be expanded as

S=\int{{d^{4}}x\bar{\psi}\left({i\not\partial-m-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma^{a}}{h^{\mu}}_{a}{P_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{4}{\gamma^{a}}{\Gamma_{a}}-\frac{1}{2}{h^{\mu}}_{\mu}(i\not\partial-m)}\right)\psi}\;.

(25)

So the interaction part between fermion and background gravitational field can be read from formula (
25

{S_{int}}=\int{{d^{4}}x\bar{\psi}\left({-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma^{a}}{h^{\mu}}_{a}{P_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{4}{\gamma^{a}}{\Gamma_{a}}-\frac{1}{2}{h^{\mu}}_{\mu}(i\not\partial-m)}\right)\psi}\;.

(26)

Take the assumption that the spacetime is asymptotic Minkowskian and the initial and final states are both free fermion state in the flat spacetime, the last term
-\frac{1}{2}{h^{\mu}}_{\mu}\bar{\psi}(i\not\partial-m)\psi
of (
26
) does not contribute to the scattering amplitude actually.

The Scattering of Dirac Fermion

(28)

{{\bar{v}}_{s}}(p){v_{r}}(p)=-m{\delta_{sr}}\;.

To the first order approximation of spherically symmetric gravitational field, the Schwarzschild metric can be approximated by

\displaystyle=i\left\langle f\right|{S_{{\mathop{\rm int}}}}\left|i\right\rangle

(33)

\displaystyle=i\left\langle f\right|\int{{d^{4}}x\bar{\psi}\left({-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma^{a}}{h^{\mu}}_{a}{P_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{4}{\gamma^{a}}{\Gamma_{a}}}\right)\psi}\left|i\right\rangle

The non-relativistic limit of the cross section (

(37)

3.3
The Scattering of Majorana Fermion

The plane wave expansion of the field operator for Majorana fermion is
[
29
]

{\psi_{M}}=\sum\limits_{s}{\int{{d^{3}}k}}\frac{1}{{\sqrt{EV}}}\left[{{a_{s}}(k){u_{s}}(k){e^{-ikx}}+{a^{\dagger}}_{s}(k){v_{s}}(k){e^{ikx}}}\right]\;.

(38)

The spinor wave function
{v_{s}}(k)
and
u_{s}(k)
are the charge conjugation of each other,

{v_{s}}(k)={u^{c}}_{s}(k)=C{\bar{u}^{T}}_{s}(k)\;,

(39)

where

is the charge conjugation transformation. The Majorana field operator is self charge conjugated,

{\psi_{M}}=C{\bar{\psi}_{M}}^{T}={\psi_{M}}^{c}\;,

(40)

which means Majorana fermion is the antiparticle of itself.
There is almost no difference between the actions of Dirac and Majoranan fermion except for a factor one half,

\mathcal{L}_{M}=\frac{1}{2}{\bar{\psi}_{M}}(i{\gamma^{\mu}}{D_{\mu}}-m){\psi_{M}}\;,

(41)

for Dirac field describes two kinds of particle while only one kind of particle does Majorana field. Similar derivation of formula (
26

{S_{int,M}}=\frac{1}{2}\int{{d^{4}}x{{\bar{\psi}}_{M}}\left({-\frac{1}{2}{\gamma^{a}}{h^{\mu}}_{a}{P_{\mu}}+\frac{1}{4}{\gamma^{a}}{\Gamma_{a}}-\frac{1}{2}{h^{\mu}}_{\mu}(i\not\partial-m)}\right){\psi_{M}}}\;.

where

\displaystyle=\phi(q){{{\bar{v}}_{r}}(k)\tilde{\not}k^{\prime}{v_{s}}(k^{\prime})}

3.4
The Effect of Torsion on Scattering

Torsion may be an essential part of gravity from a point of view of either Einstein-Cartan extension of general relativity or the equivalent description of gravity by both curvature and torsion. To get the torsion involved in the scattering of fermion by gravity, one needs a detailed investigation of the interaction between torsion and fermion. The torsion tensor
T^{\mu\nu\rho}
can be decomposed into three parts according to the irreducible representation of Lorentz group, i.e. the vector torsion
V^{\mu}
,the axial vector torsion
A^{\mu}

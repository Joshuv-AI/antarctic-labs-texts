Absolute exponential stability criteria of delay time-varying systems with sector-bounded nonlinearity: a comparison approach Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Nguyen Khoa Son, Nguyen Thi Hong

Abstract
Absolute exponential stability problem of delay time-varying systems (DTVS) with sector-bounded nonlinearity is presented in this paper. By using the comparison principle and properties of positive systems we derive several novel criteria of absolute exponential stability, for both continuous-time and discrete-time nonlinear DTVS. When applied to the time-invariant case, the obtained stability criteria are shown to cover and extend some previously known results, including, in particular, the result due to S.K. Persidskii in Ukrainian Mathematical Journal, vol. 57(2005). The theoretical results are illustrated by examples that can not be treated by the existing ones.



1
Introduction and Preliminaries

The absolute stability problem, first formulated in
[
1
]
, is one of the main problems in the systems and control theory. Roughly speaking, a dynamic nonlinear system containing nonlinearities in its mathematical description is said to be absolute stable if its equilibrium is asymptotically stable for any nonlinearity in a given nonlinearities class. In this nonlinear framework, the most widely used approach for characterizing and checking the absolute stability is the Lyapunov function method. The reader is referred to the survey article
[
2
]
and the monograph
[
3
]
for the study on absolute stability of the so-called Luri’e control systems and the monograph
[
4
]
which is dedicated to absolute stability analysis of several classes of nonlinear systems of practical interest, including the so-called Persidskii-type systems, whose stability can be characterized by diagonal Lyapunov functions.

As is well-known, the most simple Persidskii-type system can be represented by the differential equation

\dot{x}=Af(x),\ f(0)=0

(1)

where
A:=(a_{ij})
is
n\times n
-matrix and
f
is supposed to be a continuous diagonal function
f(x):=(f_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,f_{n}(x_{n}))^{\top}
belonging to the
infinite sector

K(0,\infty):=\{f:\;x_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})>0,\forall x_{i}\not=0,\ i=1,\ldots,n\}.

(2)

This class of models was first introduced for stability analysis in
[
5
]
, where a linear combination of the integrals of the nonlinearities was used as a Lyapunov
function. Next, that result was improved and extended by Persidskii
[
6
]
and in many subsequent papers (see, e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
), where different types of Lyapunov functions have been proposed for checking absolute stability for continuous-time systems as well as their discrete-time counterparts, under different classes of sector nonlinearities. The study of absolute stability of Persidskii-type systems is an
important tool in many application fields such as automatic control, Lotka-Volterra ecosystems, Hopfield neural networks, and decentralized
power-frequency control of power systems, among others (see, e.g.
[
7
]
). Recently, the absolute stability problems have been investigated also for the classes of switched nonlinear systems, including those with time-delay in the state variables, see, e.g.
[
11
,
12
]
,
[
13
]

[
14
]
and the references therein. It is important to note that in most of the aforementioned works only the classes of time-invariant nonlinear systems have been considered where some criteria of absolute asymptotical stability have been derived. So far little attention has been devoted to time-varying systems and absolute exponential stability analysis.

In this paper we will study the absolute exponential stability problem for the Persidskii class of time-varying delay nonlinear systems of the form

\dot{x}=A(t)f(x(t))+B(t)f(x(t-h)),\ t\geq 0

(3)

where
A(\cdot),B(\cdot)
are
n\times n
-matrix continuous functions and the diagonal function
f
belongs to the
bounded sector

K[\delta,\beta]
defined as

K[\delta,\beta]\!:=\!\{f:\delta_{i}x_{i}^{2}\leq x_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\leq\beta_{i}x_{i}^{2},\forall x_{i}\not=0,\ i=1,\ldots,n\}

(4)

with
0<\delta_{i}\leq\beta_{i},i\in\underline{n}
being given numbers. Our primary purpose is to derive some verifiable criteria of absolute exponential stability for this class of time-varying nonlinear systems. Similar results will be established also for time-varying difference systems. What is more, it is remarkable that when applied to the particular cases of time-invariant systems, the obtained results yield novel criteria of absolute exponential stability which improve the existing ones. Differently from the previous works which are mainly based on the Lyapunov functions (or Lyapunov-Krasovskii functions) method, we will employ the comparison principle in deriving the main results. It is worthy to mention that the comparison approach was proved to be a quite effective tool in stability analysis, particularly for time-varying and switched systems. The readers are referred to
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
and the literature given therein on this topic. Basically, this approach is based on comparing the original systems with a positive comparison system of the same dimension and using the stability characterization of the latter one (which is supported by the powerful Perron-Frobenius theory, see e.g.
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
) to conclude on stability of the original system.

Main Results

Consider the time-varying nonlinear system (

By continuity of

Definition 1

The zero solution
x(t)\equiv 0
of the time-varying nonlinear system (
3
) is said to be absolutely exponentially stable (shortly, AES) if there exist positive numbers
M,\lambda
such that for any admissible nonlinearity
f\in K[\delta,\beta]
and any non-zero continuous function
\varphi\in C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n})
the solution
x(t)=x(t,\varphi)
of (

x(\theta)=\varphi(\theta),\ \theta\in[-h,0],

satisfies

\|x(t)\|=\|x(t,\varphi)\|\leq Me^{-\lambda t}\|\varphi\|,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(6)

Such a number
\lambda
is called the exponential decay rate of
x(t)
.

Define the Meztler matrix function
\widehat{A}(t)=(\widehat{a}_{k,ij}(t)),\ t\geq 0,\ k\in\underline{N},
by setting

\widehat{a}_{i,i}(t)\!=\!a_{ii}(t),\ \widehat{a}_{ij}(t)\!=\!|a_{ij}(t)|,j\not=i,\ i,j\in\underline{n}.

(7)

The main contribution of this paper is the following

Theorem 1

Consider the time-varying system (
3
) with admissible nonlinearities
f\in K[\delta,\beta]

where
\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})^{\top}
,
\delta:=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given positive vectors such that
\beta_{i}\geq\delta_{i}>0,\ \forall i\in\underline{n}
. Assume that there exists a nonnegative
n\times n
-matrix
\bar{B}=(\bar{b}_{ij})\geq 0
such that

|B(t)|\leq\bar{B},\ \forall t\geq 0.

(8)

Then the zero solution of the system (
3
) is AES if there exist
n
-dimensional vector
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n})^{\top}\gg 0
and a real number
\alpha>0
such that

\bigg(D_{\delta}\widehat{A}^{\top}(t)+e^{\alpha h}D_{\beta}\bar{B}^{\top}\bigg)\xi\leq-\alpha\xi,\forall t\geq 0,

(9)

where
D_{\delta}
and
D_{\beta}
are diagonal matrices defined, respectively, as
D_{\beta}=\text{\rm diag}(\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{n}),D_{\beta}=\text{\rm diag}(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{n})
. Moreover, in this case, the exponential decay rate is
\alpha
.

Proof.
Let
x(t)
be the solution of (
3
), with a nonlinearity
f\in K[\delta,\beta]
and a non-zero initial function
\varphi\in C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Then, for each
i\in\underline{n}
,

\dot{x}_{i}(t)\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}(t)f_{j}(x_{j}(t))\!+\!\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}(t)f_{j}(x_{j}(t\!-\!h)),\ t\geq 0.

(10)

Assume that (
9
) holds for some
\alpha>0,\xi\gg 0
. Then, it implies readily

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{a}_{ij}(t)\xi_{i}<0,\ \forall t\geq 0,\ \forall j\in\underline{n}.

(11)

In order to make use of the comparison principle, let us define the numbers

d_{1}:=\min_{i\in\underline{n}}\xi_{i},\ \ \ d_{2}:=\max_{i\in\underline{n}}\xi_{i}.

(12)

and the continuous nonnegative functions
v_{0}(t),v_{1}(t),
by setting,

v_{0}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}|x_{i}(t)|=\xi^{\top}|x(t)|,\ \text{for}\ t\geq-h,

v_{1}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}|\varphi_{i}(t)|\!+\!e^{\alpha h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{-h}^{0}e^{\alpha\theta}\bar{b}_{ij}\xi_{i}\beta_{j}|\varphi_{j}(\theta)|d\theta,

for
t\in[-h,0]
and

v_{1}(t)=v_{0}(t)+e^{-\alpha(t-h)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{t-h}^{t}e^{\alpha s}\bar{b}_{ij}\xi_{i}\beta_{j}|x_{j}(s)|ds,

for
t\geq 0
. Define, moreover, the following comparison function

y_{\alpha}(t)=L_{1}e^{-\alpha t}\|\varphi\|,\ \text{for}\ t\geq-h,

(13)

where
L_{1}
is an arbitrary positive number satisfying

d_{2}+he^{\alpha h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{b}_{ij}\;\xi_{i}\;\beta_{j}}<L_{1}.

(14)

Then, we have obviously

d_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}(t)|\leq v_{0}(t)\leq v_{1}(t),\ \forall t\geq 0.

(15)

Moreover, it follows immediately from the definition of
v_{0},v_{1},y_{\alpha}
and (
12
), (
14
) that

v_{1}(t)<L_{1}\|\varphi\|\leq y_{\alpha}(t),\ \text{for}\ t\in[-h,0].

(16)

Our goal is to prove that the above inequality holds true for all
t\geq 0
, that is

v_{1}(t)\leq y_{\alpha}(t)=L_{1}e^{-\alpha t}\|\varphi\|,\ \forall t\geq 0.

(17)

To this end, taking an arbitrary positive number
\alpha_{\epsilon}\in(0,\alpha)
, we will prove that

v_{1}(t)\leq y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(t)=L_{1}e^{-\alpha_{\epsilon}t}\|\varphi\|,\ \forall t\geq 0.

(18)

Assume to the contrary that
(
18
) does not hold. This implies that the set
T_{0}:=\{t\in(0,+\infty):v_{1}(t)>y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(t)\}
is nonempty. Then, denoting
\bar{t}_{0}=\inf\{t\in T_{0}\}
, we have, by the continuity and (
16
), that
\bar{t}_{0}>0
and

v_{1}(t)\leq y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(t),\ \forall t\in[-h,\bar{t}_{0}),\ v_{1}(\bar{t}_{0})=y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(\bar{t}_{0}),

(19)

and there exist a sequence
t_{k}\downarrow\bar{t}_{0}
such that

v_{1}(t_{k})>y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(t_{k}),k=1,2,\ldots

(20)

Since
f\in K[\delta,\beta]
, it follows immediately from (
4
) that, for each
i,j\in\underline{n}
and
j\not=i
we have

\begin{split}\delta_{i}|x_{i}|\leq f_{i}(x_{i})\ \text{sign}x_{i}=|f_{i}(x_{i})|\ \ \text{and}\\
f_{j}(x_{j})\ \text{sign}x_{i}\leq|f_{j}(x_{j})|\leq\beta_{j}|x_{j}|.\end{split}

(21)

Therefore, by using (
7
), (
8
), (
10
), (
21
), we get, for each
t\in[0,\bar{t}_{0}],

\displaystyle D^{+}v_{0}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}D^{+}|x_{i}(t)|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\text{sign}(x_{i}(t))\dot{x}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\bigg(a_{ii}(t)|f_{i}(x_{i}(t))|+\sum_{j\not=i}^{n}|a_{ij}(t)|\;|f_{j}(x_{j}(t))|\bigg)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|b_{ij}(t)|\;|f_{j}(x_{j}(t-h))|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\xi_{i}\widehat{a}_{ij}(t)|f_{j}(x_{j}(t))|+\xi_{i}|b_{ij}(t)||f_{j}(x_{j}(t-h))|\bigg)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{aii}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\xi_{i}\widehat{a}_{ij}(t)\delta_{j}|x_{j}(t)|+\xi_{i}\bar{b}_{ij}\beta_{j}|x_{j}(t-h)|\bigg).

Consequently, by the definition of
v_{1}
and the properties of the Dini derivative

(see, e.g.
[
23
]

\displaystyle D^{+}v_{1}(t)\!\leq\!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\xi_{i}\widehat{a}_{ij}(t)\delta_{j}|x_{j}(t)|\!+\!\xi_{i}\bar{b}_{ij}\beta_{j}|x_{j}(t-h)|\bigg)

\displaystyle+(-\alpha)\left(v_{1}(t)-v_{0}(t)\right)+

\displaystyle+e^{\alpha h}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\bar{b}_{ij}\beta_{j}|x_{j}(t)|-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\bar{b}_{ij}\beta_{j}|x_{j}(t\!-\!h)|

\displaystyle=-\alpha v_{1}(t)+\alpha v_{0}(t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\left(D_{\delta}\widehat{A}^{\top}(t)+e^{\alpha h}D_{\beta}\bar{B}^{\top}\right)\xi\right]_{j}|x_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{cond1}}}{{\leq}}-\alpha v_{1}(t)+\alpha v_{0}(t)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-\alpha)\xi_{j}|x_{j}(t)|=-\alpha v_{1}(t).

By virtue of (
19

\displaystyle D^{+}v_{1}(\bar{t}_{0})

\displaystyle\leq-\alpha v_{1}(\bar{t}_{0})=-\alpha y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(\bar{t}_{0})

\displaystyle<-\alpha_{\epsilon}y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(\bar{t}_{0})=\frac{d}{dt}y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(\bar{t}_{0}).

On the other hand, by definition of
D^{+}
and (

\displaystyle D^{+}v_{1}(\bar{t}_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{t_{k}\downarrow\bar{t}_{0}}\dfrac{v_{1}(t_{k})-v_{1}(\bar{t}_{0})}{t_{k}-\bar{t}_{0}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{t_{k}\downarrow\bar{t}_{0}}\dfrac{y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(t_{k})-y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(\bar{t}_{0})}{t_{k}-\bar{t}_{0}}=\dfrac{d}{dt}y_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}(\bar{t}_{0}),

conflicting with the above strict inequality. Thus, (

\alpha_{\epsilon}\uparrow\alpha

),
which together with (

\|x(t)\|\leq\frac{v_{0}(t)}{d_{1}}\leq Me^{-\alpha t}\|\varphi\|,\ \forall t\geq 0,

(22)

where

Therefore, (

Corollary 1

Consider the time-varying nonlinear system

\dot{x}=A(t)f(x(t)),\ t\geq 0,

\xi\gg 0

\gamma:=\max_{j\in\underline{n}}\sup_{t\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{a}_{ij}(t)\xi_{i}\ <\ 0,

(24)

where
\widehat{A}(t)
is defined by (
7
). Then the zero solution of (

) is AES, with the exponential decay rate
\alpha,
for any
\alpha\in(0,\frac{-\gamma\delta_{0}}{d_{2}})
, where
\delta_{0}=\min_{j\in\underline{n}}\delta_{j}
and
d_{2}
is defined by (

In the case when both matrix functions
A(t),B(t)
in (
3
) can be upper bounded by some time-invariant matrices, Theorem

implies the following verifiable criterion of absolute exponential stability.

Corollary 2

Consider the time-varying system (
3
) with admissible nonlinearities
f\in K[\delta,\beta]
. Assume that there exist a Metzler matrix
\widehat{A}
, a nonnegative matrix
\bar{B}\geq 0
and a positive vector
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{n})^{\top}\gg 0
satisfying

\widehat{A}(t)\leq\widehat{A},\ |B(t)|\leq\bar{B},\ \ \forall t\geq 0,

(25)

and

\big(\widehat{A}D_{\delta}+\bar{B}D_{\beta}\big)^{\top}\xi\ll 0,\ \forall t\geq 0,

(26)

where
D_{\delta}
and
D_{\beta}
are diagonal matrices defined as in Theorem
1
. Then the zero solution of system (
3
) is AES, with the exponential decay rate
\alpha=\alpha_{\max}>0
, which can be calculated, correspondingly to each
\xi\gg 0
satisfying (
26
), as

\alpha_{\max}=\min_{i\in\underline{n}}\{\alpha_{i}:g_{i}(\alpha_{i})=0\},

(27)

where
g_{i}(\cdot)
are continuous functions defined by

g_{i}(\alpha):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\widehat{a}_{ji}\delta_{i}\xi_{j}+e^{\alpha h}\bar{b}_{ji}\beta_{i}\xi_{j}\big)+\alpha\xi_{i},\ i\in\underline{n}.

(28)

Proof.
Obviously, for each
i\in\underline{n},\ g_{i}(\alpha)
is continuous and monotonically increasing to
+\infty
as
\alpha\rightarrow+\infty
(because
g_{i}^{\prime}(\alpha)>0,\forall\alpha>0
). Since
g_{i}(0)<0
, due to (
26
), it follows that the equation
g_{i}(\alpha)=0
has a unique solution
\alpha_{i}>0
and the inequality
g_{i}(\alpha)\leq 0
is valid for all
\alpha\in[0,\alpha_{i}]
but violated for
\alpha>\alpha_{i}
. This implies that (
9
) is valid for all
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{\max}]
but violated afterwards. Therefore, by Theorem
1
the zero solution of the system (
3
) is AES, with the ’maximal’ (for the given
\xi
) exponential decay rate
\alpha_{\max}
.

Corollary 3

Consider the time-invariant nonlinear system

\dot{x}(t)=Af(x(t))+Bf(x(t-h)),\ t\geq 0,

(29)

where
A
is a Metzler matrix,
B\geq 0
and
f
is any nonlinear function belonging to the sector
K[\delta,\beta]
, defined by (
4
). Then the zero solution of (
29
) is AES if there exists a positive vector
\xi\gg 0
satisfying

(AD_{\delta}+BD_{\beta})^{\top}\xi\ll 0.

(30)

Remark 1

By Lemma
1
, (
30
) is equivalent to that the Metzler matrix
AD_{\delta}+BD_{\beta}
is Hurwitz stable. If, conversely, the time-invariant nonlinear system (
29
) is AES then, since, obviously
f(x):=D_{\delta}x\in K[\delta,\beta]
, it follows that the linear positive system
\dot{x}(t)=AD_{\delta}x+BD_{\delta}x(t-h),\ t\geq 0
is exponentially stable. This, in turn, is equivalent (see, e.g.
[
19
]
) to the existence of a positive vector
\xi\gg 0
such that
D_{\delta}(A+B)^{\top}\xi=(AD_{\delta}+BD_{\delta})^{\top}\xi\ll 0
which implies readily

(A+B)^{\top}\xi\ll 0,

(31)

or, equivalently, the Metzler matrix
A+B
is Hurwitz stable (by Lemma
1
). Thus, in the nondelay case (i.e.
B=0
), Corollary
3
is an extension of the main result of
[
9
]
.

Remark 2

Note that, under the assumption of Corollary
3
, the nonlinear system (
29
) is
positive
which means that
x(t)=x(t,\varphi)\geq 0,\forall t\geq 0
for any nonnegative initial function
\varphi\in C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
(see, e.g.
[
13
]
, Lemma 4). Therefore, in view of Corollary
3
, Corollary
2
amounts to saying that the zero solution of the time-varying system (
3
)-(
4
) is AES if its associate upper bounding (in the sense (
25
)) by time-invariant positive nonlinear system

\dot{x}=\widehat{A}f(x)+\bar{B}f(x(t-h),\ t\geq 0

is AES, under the same sector constraints.

Remark 3

It is important to mention that problems of absolute asymptotic stability have been considered for time-invariant delay systems of the form (
29
) in a number of previous works, including those with switchings. In particular, it has been established (see, e.g.
[
12
,
11
]
) that, for any nonlinearity
f\in K(0,\infty)
, the solution
x(t)
of the positive system (
29
) satisfies
\|x(t)\|\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
if there exists
\xi\gg 0
such that (
31
) holds.
This condition, however, is not sufficient to assure the absolute exponential stability of the zero solution (as asserted in Corollary
3
where, however, a more restrictive nonlinearities class
K[\delta,\beta]\subset K(0,\infty)
is assumed). Indeed, let us consider the scalar nondelay positive system

\dot{x}=-f_{0}(x)
, where
f_{0}(x)=x^{3}
for
|x|\leq 1
and

f_{0}(x)=x
for
|x|>1.
Then, clearly,
f_{0}\in K(0,\infty)
and (
31
) holds for
\xi=1
. However, the zero solution
x=0
, while being globally asymptotically stable equilibrium, is not exponentially stable (e.g., by Theorem 4.6 in
[
24
]
, p. 184). Note that
f_{0}
does not belong to
K[\delta,\beta]
for any
\delta>0
and any
\beta\geq 1>\delta
. In
[
13
]
several criteria of absolute exponential stability for switched time-invariant systems were obtained, also by using the Lyapunov function method, but the conditions look much more complicated and not easy to be checked. Thus, even for time-invariant systems, our results are novel, while those for the time-varying case as presented in this paper have not yet been known in the existing literature, to the best of our knowledge.

3
Some extensions of the main results

The approach developed in the previous section can be extended to get criteria of AES when the system’s equation contains time-varying nonlinearities or multiple discrete delays. We just formulate the results, omitting of proof, because they are largely similar to that for Theorem
1
.

First, consider a more general model related to the Persidskii-type system (

4
Time-Varying Nonlinear Difference Systems with delays

Consider time-varying nonlinear difference system with delays of the form

x(k\!+\!1)\!=\!A(k)f(x(k))+B(k)f(x(k-h)),\;k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(37)

where
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is a given number,
A(k),B(k):\mathbb{Z}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

K(0,\beta]:=\{f:0<x_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\leq\beta_{i}x_{i}^{2},\forall x_{i}\not=0,i\in\underline{n}\},

where
\beta_{i}>0,i=1,\ldots,n
are given positive numbers. Such a nonlinear function

is called admissible sector nonlinearity. It is easy to verify that

if and only if

0<x_{i}f_{i}(x_{i}),\ 0<|f_{i}(x_{i})|\leq\beta_{i}|x_{i}|\ \ \text{for}\ \ x_{i}\not=0,\ i\in\underline{n}.

(39)

Let
S[-h,0]
be the Banach space of functions
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}_{[-h,0]}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

\|\varphi\|=\max_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{[-h,0]}}\|\varphi(k)\|
and

be the solution of (

x(k)=\varphi(k),\ \ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{[-h,0]}.
We will say that the system (

\|x(k)\|=\left\|x(k,\varphi)\right\|\leq L\;\lambda^{k}\;\|\varphi\|,\ \forall k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

Theorem 4

Assume that there exists a vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\xi\gg 0

then we have immediately

|x_{i}(k)|\leq u_{i}(k),\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{Z}_{[-h,0]},\forall i\in\underline{n}.

(42)

Clearly, to prove the theorem, it suffices to show that

|x_{i}(k)|\leq u_{i}(k),\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\forall i\in\underline{n}

6
Conclusion

We have presented a number of verifiable sufficient conditions of absolute exponential stability for different classes of delay time-varying nonlinear systems with sector-bounded nonlinearity. Differently from the traditional approach which is based on using Lyapunov-Krasovskii functionals, our analysis makes use of comparison principle and the stability characterization of positive upper bounding systems. The results have been obtained for both the continuous-time and discrete-time cases. When applied to the time-invariant systems, the obtained results have been shown to cover and improve the stability criteria in existing literature. There are several possibilities for developing the work described here. In particular, it would be of interest to investigate whether our comparison analysis can be adapted to deal with systems with time-varying delays or to address the absolute exponential stability of time-varying Luri’e systems. The application of the results obtained in this paper for studying absolute exponential stability of time-varying nonlinear switched systems would be also an interesting and promising topic which is currently under our consideration.

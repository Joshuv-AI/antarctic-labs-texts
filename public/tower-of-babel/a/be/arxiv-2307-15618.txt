1
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth, bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N},

N\geq 2.
For

1<p<N
and
0<q\leq p^{\ast}:=\frac{Np}{N-p}
let

\lambda_{p,q}:=\inf\left\{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}:u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ \ and \ }\left\|u\right\|_{q}=1\right\},

(1.1)

where

\left\|u\right\|_{r}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{r}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},\text{ \ \ }r>0.

We recall that
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r}
is the standard norm of the
Lebesgue space
L^{r}(\Omega)
if
r\geq 1,
but it is not a norm if
0<r<1
.

Note from (
1.1
) that

\lambda_{p,q}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|u\right\|_{q}^{p}}\text{ for all }u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\left\{0\right\}

(1.2)

since the above quotients are homogeneous.

When
0<q<p^{\ast},
the existence of a minimizer
u_{p,q}
for the
constrained minimization problem (
1.1
) follows from standard arguments
of the Calculus of Variations. Moreover,
u_{p,q}
is a weak solution to the
Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\lambda_{p,q}\left|u\right|^{q-2}u&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

In consequence,
u_{p,q}>0
in
\Omega
and
u_{p,q}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
0<\alpha<1
(we refer to
[
8
, Theorem
1(i)]
for the regularity of
u_{p,q}
when
0<q<1
, in which case
(
1.3
) is singular). These facts are well known when
1\leq q<p^{\ast},

since
\lambda_{p,q}
is the best constant in the Sobolev (compact) embedding

W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega).

It is worth mentioning that
u_{p,q}
is the only positive minimizer to
(
1.1
) in the sublinear case:
0<q<p.
However, this uniqueness property
might fail in the superlinear, subcritical case:
p<q<p^{\ast}.
For examples
and a discussion about this issue we recommend the recent paper
[
4
]

by Brasco and Lindgren, where an important result is established for general
smooth bounded domains: the uniqueness of the minimizer
u_{p,q}
whenever

2<p<q
and
q
is sufficiently close to
p.
We stress that such a uniqueness
result for
1<p<2
is not yet available in the literature.

When
q=p^{\ast},
the infimum
\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}
cannot be attained in

W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
if
\Omega\not=\mathbb{R}^{N}.
Actually,

\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}
does not depend on
\Omega
as it coincides with the
well-known Sobolev constant
S_{N,p},
that is:

\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}=S_{N,p}:=N\omega_{N}^{\frac{p}{N}}\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{\Gamma(N/p)\Gamma(1+N-N/p)}{\Gamma(N)}\right)^{\frac{p}{N}},

(1.4)

where
\Gamma(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{t-1}e^{-s}\mathrm{d}s
is the Gamma
function and
\omega_{N}:=\pi^{N/2}/\Gamma(1+N/2)
is the
N
-dimensional
Lebesgue volume of the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}.

According to
[
2
, Theorem 9]
by Anello, Faraci and Iannizzotto, for each
fixed
p\in(1,N)
the function

(0,p^{\ast}]\ni q\mapsto\lambda_{p,q}\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{p}{q}}

is decreasing and absolutely continuous on compact sets of
(0,p^{\ast}].
The
same result, but for
q\in[1,p^{\ast}],
had already been obtained by
Ercole in
[
7
]
.

As for
q
varying with
p
, Kawohl and Fridman proved in
[
9
]
that

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\lambda_{p,p}=h(\Omega)

(1.5)

where
h(\Omega)
is the Cheeger constant of
\Omega.

We recall that

h(\Omega):=\inf\left\{\frac{P(E)}{\left|E\right|}:E\subset\overline{\Omega}\text{ and }\left|E\right|>0\right\},

where
P(E)
stands for the perimeter of
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and

\left|E\right|
stands for the
N
-dimensional Lebesgue volume of

E.

The Cheeger problem consists of finding a subset
E\subset\overline{\Omega}

such that

h(\Omega)=\frac{P(E)}{\left|E\right|}.

Such a subset
E
is called Cheeger set of
\Omega.

We notice from (
1.4
) that

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}=N\omega_{N}^{\frac{1}{N}}=\left|\Omega\right|^{\frac{1}{N}}h(\Omega^{\star})

(1.6)

where
\Omega^{\star}
denotes the ball of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
centered at the
origin and such that
\left|\Omega^{\star}\right|=\left|\Omega\right|.
The second equality in (
1.6
) is due the fact that
balls are Cheeger sets of themselves (i.e. they are calibrable).
Hence, as
R=(\left|\Omega\right|/\omega_{N})^{\frac{1}{N}}
is the
radius of
\Omega^{\star},
one has
h(\Omega^{\star})=N/R=N(\omega_{N}/\left|\Omega\right|)^{\frac{1}{N}}.
It is well known that

h(\Omega^{\star})\leq h(\Omega),
the equality occurring if and only if

\Omega
is a ball.

Owing to (
1.5
), when
p\rightarrow 1^{+}
the minimizer
u_{p,p}

converges in
L^{1}(\Omega)
(after passing to a subsequence) to a function

u_{1}
whose the
t
-superlevel sets
E_{t}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u_{1}(x)>t\right\}
are Cheeger sets, for almost every
t>0.
As shown in

[
9
]
, these properties are obtained from a variational version of the
Cheeger problem in the
BV
setting, which we briefly present in Section

2
.

In this paper we suppose that
q
varies with
p
along a more general path,

q=q(p)
for
p\in(1,p^{\ast}),
and study the behavior of
\lambda_{p,q(p)}

when
p\rightarrow 1^{+}
and
q(p)\rightarrow 1.
Adapting an estimate from

[
7
]
(see Lemma
3.1
below) and making use of (
1.5
) we
extend the results of
[
9
]
. Our main result, which will be proved in
Section
3
, is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

If
0<q(p)<p^{\ast}
and
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}q(p)=1,

then

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\lambda_{p,q(p)}=h(\Omega),

(1.7)

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{1}=1

(1.8)

and

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|u_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}^{q(p)-p}=1.

(1.9)

Moreover, any sequence
\left(u_{p_{n},q(p_{n})}\right),
with

p_{n}\rightarrow 1^{+},
admits a subsequence that converges in
L^{1}(\Omega)
to a nonnegative function
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that:

(a)

\left\|u\right\|_{1}=1,

(b)

\dfrac{1}{\left|\Omega\right|}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}\leq\dfrac{h(\Omega)^{N}}{\left|\Omega\right|h(\Omega^{\star})^{N}},
and

(c)

for almost every
t\geq 0,
the
t
-superlevel set

E_{t}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\right\}

is a Cheeger set.

Besides allowing
q(p)\rightarrow 1^{-}
, which embraces (
1.3
) in its
singular form, our approach holds for every family of extremals
u_{p,q(p)}

in the superlinear, subcritical case:
p<q(p)<p^{\ast}.

It is simple to verify that the function

v_{p,q}:=\lambda_{p,q}^{\frac{1}{q-p}}u_{p,q},

(1.10)

is a positive weak solution to the Lane-Emden type problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla w\right|^{p-2}\nabla w\right)=\left|w\right|^{q-2}w&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
w=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.11)

The next corollary is stated to solutions to (
1.11
) in the form
(
1.10
). It is an immediate consequence of (
1.7
) and (
1.9
)
since

\left\|v_{p,q}\right\|_{q}^{q-p}=\lambda_{p,q}\left\|u_{p,q}\right\|_{q}^{q-p}=\lambda_{p,q}\text{ \ and \ }\left\|v_{p,q}\right\|_{\infty}^{q-p}=\lambda_{p,q}\left\|u_{p,q}\right\|_{\infty}^{q-p}.

Corollary 1.2

If
0<q(p)<p^{\ast}
and
\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}q(p)=1,

then

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|v_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{q(p)}^{q(p)-p}=h(\Omega)=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|v_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}^{q(p)-p}.

In the particular case where
q(p)\equiv 1
this result had already been
obtained in
[
5
]
by Bueno and Ercole, without using (
1.5
).

As it is well known, (
1.11
) has a unique positive weak solution when

0<q<p,
which is, of course, that given by (
1.10
). However, there are
examples of smooth domains for which (
1.11
) has multiple positive weak
solutions when
p<q<p^{\ast},
which may be of the form (
1.10
) or not
(see
[
4
]
and references therein). By the way, it is plain to check
that

\lambda_{p,q}=\left\|v_{p,q}\right\|_{q}^{q-p}=\min\left\{\left\|w\right\|_{q}^{q-p}:w\text{ is a weak solution to }(\ref{Dir1})\right\}.

(1.12)

Corollary
1.2
deals with the behavior of positive weak solutions to
(
1.11
) that attains the minimum in (
1.12
). Aiming to cover a wider
class of positive weak solutions
w_{p,q}
to (
1.11
), including those
satisfying
\left\|w_{p,q}\right\|_{q}^{q-p}>\lambda_{p,q}
, we
provide the following stronger result, which will be proved in Section

4
by using Picone’s inequality (see
[
1
,
3
]
).

Theorem 1.3

Let
w_{p,q(p)}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a positive weak
solution to (
1.11
), with
p<q(p)<p^{\ast}.
Then, either

\limsup_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|w_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}^{q(p)-p}=+\infty

(1.13)

or

\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|w_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{q(p)}^{q(p)-p}=h(\Omega)=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|w_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{\infty}^{q(p)-p}.

The alternative (
1.13
) can be replaced with (see Remark
4.1
)

\limsup_{p\rightarrow 1^{+}}\left\|w_{p,q(p)}\right\|_{q(p)}^{q(p)-p}=+\infty.

We believe that determining whether this alternative (or its equivalent
version (
1.13
)) is actually possible is a very interesting open question
that we plan to study in the near future.

2
The Cheeger problem in the
BV
setting

In this section, we assume that
\Omega
is a Lipschitz bounded domain and
collect some definitions, properties and basic results related to the
variational version of the Cheeger problem in the
BV
setting. For details we
refer to
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
.

The total variation of
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is defined as

\left|Du\right|(\Omega):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega}u\operatorname{div}\varphi\mathrm{d}x:\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ \ and \ }\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1\right\}.

The space
BV(\Omega)
of the functions
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
of bounded
variation in
\Omega
(i.e.
\,\left|Du\right|(\Omega)<\infty
),
endowed with the norm

\left\|u\right\|_{BV}:=\left\|u\right\|_{1}+\left|Du\right|(\Omega),

is a Banach space compactly embedded into
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, the
functional
BV(\Omega)\ni u\mapsto\left|Du\right|(\Omega)
is lower
semicontinuous in
L^{1}(\Omega).

The Cheeger constant is also characterized as (see
[
10
, Proposition
3.1]
)

h(\Omega)=\inf_{BV_{0}(\Omega)}\frac{\left|Du\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\left\|u\right\|_{1}}

(2.1)

where

BV_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\left\|u\right\|_{1}>0\text{ \ and \ }u\equiv 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\right\}

and

\left|Du\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left|Du\right|(\Omega)+\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|u\right|\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

(
\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
stands for the
(N-1)
-Hausdorff measure in

\mathbb{R}^{N}
).

Proposition 2.1
(
[
6
, Corollary 1(2)]
)

Let
\left(u_{n}\right)\subset BV_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then

\left|Du\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|Du_{n}\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Proposition 2.2

Suppose that

h(\Omega)=\frac{\left|Du\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\left\|u\right\|_{1}}

for some
u\in BV_{0}(\Omega).
Then

E_{t}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\right\}

is a Cheeger set for almost every
t\geq 0.

Inversely, if
E\subset\overline{\Omega}
is a Cheeger set of
\Omega,
then

h(\Omega)=\frac{\left|D\chi_{E}\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\left\|\chi_{E}\right\|_{1}}

where
\chi_{E}
stands for the characteristic function of
E
in

\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.
Combining Coarea formula and Cavalieri’s principle we find

0=\left|Du\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})-h(\Omega)\left\|u\right\|_{1}=\int_{0}^{\infty}(P(E_{t})-h(\Omega)\left|E_{t}\right|)\mathrm{d}t.

(2.2)

As
\left|E_{t}\right|>0
a.e.
\,t\geq 0
we have that
P(E_{t})-h(\Omega)\left|E_{t}\right|\geq 0
a.e.
\,t\geq 0.
Therefore, it
follows from (
2.2
) that

h(\Omega)=\frac{P(E_{t})}{\left|E_{t}\right|}\text{ \ a.e.\thinspace}t\geq 0.

Now, if
E\subset\overline{\Omega}
is a Cheeger set of
\Omega,
then

\chi_{E}\in BV_{0}(\Omega).
As
P(E)=\left|D\chi_{E}\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\left\|\chi_{E}\right\|_{1}=\left|E\right|
we have

h(\Omega)=\frac{P(E)}{\left|E\right|}=\frac{\left|D\chi_{E}\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})}{\left\|\chi_{E}\right\|_{1}}.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We recall from the Introduction that
u_{p,q}
(for
1<p<N
and
0<q<p^{\ast}
) denotes the positive minimizer of the constrained minimization problem
(
1.1
), so that
u_{p,q}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega),

u_{p,q}>0\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ \ }\left\|u_{p,q}\right\|_{q}=1,\text{ \ }\lambda_{p,q}=\left\|\nabla u_{p,q}\right\|_{p}^{p},

and
u_{p,q}
is a weak solution to (
1.3
).

If
q=p
the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) is homogeneous and thus it can be
recognized as an eigenvalue problem. In this setting,
\lambda_{p,p}
is known
as the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet
p
-Laplacian. Actually,

\lambda_{p,p}
is simple no sense that the set of its corresponding
eigenfunctions is generated by
u_{p,p},
that is:
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

is a nontrivial weak solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\lambda_{p,p}\left|u\right|^{p-2}u&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

if and only if
w=ku_{p,p}
for some
k\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}.

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
by assuming that
\partial\Omega

is smooth enough to ensure that
u_{p,q}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}).
In
consequence,
u_{p,q}\in BV_{0}(\Omega)
(after extended as zero on

\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
) and

\left|Du_{p,q}\right|(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\left\|\nabla u_{p,q}\right\|_{1},

since

\left|Du_{p,q}\right|(\Omega)=\left\|\nabla u_{p,q}\right\|_{1}\text{ \ and \ }\int_{\partial\Omega}\left|u_{p,q}\right|\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=0.

The next result is adapted from Lemma 5 of
[
7
]
established there for

1\leq q<p^{\ast}
.

Lemma 3.1

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a
positive weak solution to the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}-\operatorname{div}\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=\lambda\left|u\right|^{q-2}u&\text{{in}}&\Omega\\
u=0&\text{{on}}&\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

with
0\leq q<p^{\ast}
and
\lambda>0.
If
\sigma\geq 1,
then

C_{\lambda,\sigma,q}\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}^{\frac{N(p-q)+p\sigma}{p}}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\sigma}^{\sigma},

(3.3)

where

C_{\lambda,\sigma,q}:=\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{N}{p}}\left(\frac{p}{p+N(p-1)}\right)^{N+1}I_{\sigma,q}

and

I_{\sigma,q}:=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}\sigma\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-\tau\right)^{\frac{N(p-1)}{p}}\tau^{\frac{N(1-q)}{p}+\sigma-1}\mathrm{d}\tau&\text{if}&0\leq q<1\\
\sigma{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}}(1-\tau)^{\frac{N(p-1)}{p}}\tau^{\sigma-1}\mathrm{d}\tau&\text{if}&1\leq q<p^{\ast}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.
For each
0<t<\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}
let us define

(u-t)_{+}:=\max\left\{u-t,0\right\}\text{ \ and \ }A_{t}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\right\}.

As
(u-t)_{+}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
u
is a positive weak solution to
(
3.2
), we have

\int_{A_{t}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x=\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u-t)_{+}\mathrm{d}x=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{q-1}(u-t)_{+}\mathrm{d}x=\lambda\int_{A_{t}}u^{q-1}(u-t)\mathrm{d}x.

(3.4)

We also have

\left(\int_{A_{t}}(u-t)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p}\leq\left|A_{t}\right|^{p-\frac{p}{p^{\ast}}}\left(\int_{A_{t}}(u-t)^{p^{\ast}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\ast}}}\leq\frac{\left|A_{t}\right|^{p-\frac{p}{p^{\ast}}}}{\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}}\int_{A_{t}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x,

(3.5)

where we have used Hölder’s inequality and (
1.2
). Note that

\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}\leq\frac{\left\|\nabla(u-t)_{+}\right\|_{p}^{p}}{\left\|(u-t)_{+}\right\|_{p^{\ast}}^{p}}=\frac{{\displaystyle\int_{A_{t}}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x}{\left({\displaystyle\int_{A_{t}}}(u-t)^{p^{\ast}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\ast}}}}.

We divide the remaining of the proof in two cases.

Case 1:

0\leq q<1.
As

\int_{A_{t}}u^{q-1}(u-t)\mathrm{d}x\leq t^{q-1}\int_{A_{t}}(\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-t)\mathrm{d}x

(3.6)

we obtain from (
3.4
) the estimate

\int_{A_{t}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\lambda t^{q-1}(\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-t)\left|A_{t}\right|.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.7
) and (
3.5
), we obtain the inequalities

\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}\left|A_{t}\right|^{-p+\frac{p}{p^{\ast}}}\left(\int_{A_{t}}(u-t)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p}\leq\int_{A_{t}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\lambda t^{q-1}(\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-t)\left|A_{t}\right|

which lead to

\frac{\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}t^{1-q}}{\lambda(\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-t)}\left(\int_{A_{t}}(u-t)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p}\leq\left|A_{t}\right|^{p(1-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}+\frac{1}{p})}=\left|A_{t}\right|^{p(\frac{N-1}{N})}.

(3.8)

Now, let us define the function

g(t):=\int_{A_{t}}(u-t)\mathrm{d}x.

It is simple to verify that

g(t)=\int_{t}^{\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}}\left|A_{s}\right|\mathrm{d}s,

so that

g^{\prime}(t)=-\left|A_{t}\right|.

Then, (
3.8
) can be rewritten as

\left(\frac{\lambda_{p,p^{\ast}}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{N}{p(N+1)}}\left(\frac{t^{1-q}}{\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-t}\right)^{\frac{N}{p(N+1)}}\leq-g^{\prime}(t)g(t)^{-\frac{N}{N+1}}.

(3.9)

Integration of the right-hand side of (
3.9
) over
[t,\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}]
yields

-\int_{t}^{\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}}g^{\prime}(s)g(s)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}}\mathrm{d}s=(N+1)g(t)^{\frac{1}{N+1}}-(N+1)g(\left\|u\right\|_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{N+1}}\leq(N+1)g(t)^{\frac{1}{N+1}}

(3.10)

whereas integration of the function at the left-hand side of (
3.9
) over

[t,\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}]
yields

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}}\left(\frac{s^{1-q}}{\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-s}\right)^{\frac{N}{p(N+1)}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq t^{\frac{N(1-q)}{p(N+1)}}\int_{t}^{\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\infty}-s\right)^{-\frac{N}{p(N+1)}}\mathrm{d}s

Remark 4.1

One can derive from Remark

Classification of Killing Magnetic Curves In ℍ 3 \mathbb{H}^{3}
Özgür Kelekçi, Furkan Semih Dündar, Gülhan Ayar

Abstract
In this paper, we study classification of magnetic curves corresponding to Killing vector fields of ℍ 3 \mathbb{H}^{3} . First, we solve the geodesic equation analytically. Then we calculate the trajectories generated by all the six Killing vector fields, which are considered as magnetic field vectors, by using perturbation method up to first order with respect to the strength of the magnetic field. We present a comparison of our solution with the numerical solution for one case. We also prove that 3-dimensional ( α ) (\alpha) -Kenmotsu manifolds cannot have any magnetic vector field in the direction of their Reeb vector fields.



Keywords
:Almost contact manifolds; Killing magnetic curves; Hyperbolic spaces.

\mathbb{H}^{3}
) of the geodesic curve which can be obtained through
\mathrm{d}s^{2}/\mathrm{d}t^{2}
where
\mathrm{d}s^{2}
is given by the
\mathbb{H}^{3}
metric. It is seen that
|\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}=c_{3}^{2}
and
c_{3}
should be constant in order
\gamma^{\prime}
to define a geodesic curve. In order to see that apply
\nabla
on both sides and obtain
2\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\nabla c_{3}^{2}
. By scaling the time parameter it is possible to map
c_{3}\to 1
, however we favored the option to display each constant of integration. This is the reason why we kept the
c_{3}
dependence explicit. Since we are interested in
motion
, from now on we suppose
c_{3}>0
. We can easily integrate Equation (
4.16
) and obtain (
c_{4}
is a constant of integration):

\displaystyle t-c_{4}

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}z\left(c_{3}^{2}-(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})e^{2\alpha z}\right)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{3}}\int\mathrm{d}z\left(1-Ae^{2\alpha z}\right)^{-1/2}.

(4.17)

where
A=(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})/c_{3}^{2}
. Let
u=Ae^{2\alpha z}
, and obtain:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha c_{3}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{u\sqrt{1-u}}.

(4.18)

Evaluating the integral yields
[
28
]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\alpha c_{3}}\tanh^{-1}(\sqrt{1-u}).

(4.19)

By inverting this equation and using the definition of
u
in terms of
z
, we obtain:

\displaystyle e^{2\alpha z}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{3}^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}\left[1-\tanh^{2}(\alpha c_{3}(t-c_{4}))\right]=\frac{c_{3}^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}\sech^{2}(\alpha c_{3}(t-c_{4}))

\displaystyle z(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\text{Log}\left(\frac{{c_{3}}^{2}}{({c_{1}}^{2}+{c_{2}}^{2})}\sech^{2}(\alpha c_{3}(t-c_{4}))\right)

(4.20)

Since we know
z
in terms of time, we can use this information to integrate
\dot{x}
and
\dot{y}
in Equation (
4.12
) and in Equation (
4.13

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{c_{1}c_{3}}{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}\tanh(\alpha c_{3}(t-c_{4}))+c_{5},

(4.21)

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{c_{2}c_{3}}{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}\tanh(\alpha c_{3}(t-c_{4}))+c_{6},

(4.22)

where

5
Killing magnetic curves in
\mathbb{H}^{3}

As we have given in Section
3
there are six Killing vectors in
\mathbb{H}^{3}

\displaystyle K_{(1)}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\partial_{x},

(5.1)

\displaystyle K_{(2)}

\displaystyle=B_{2}\partial_{y},

(5.2)

\displaystyle K_{(3)}

\displaystyle=B_{3}(y\partial_{x}-x\partial_{y}),

(5.3)

\displaystyle K_{(4)}

\displaystyle=B_{4}(\alpha x\partial_{x}+\alpha y\partial_{y}+\partial_{z}),

(5.4)

\displaystyle K_{(5)}

\displaystyle=B_{5}\left(\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(x^{2}-y^{2}\right)-\frac{e^{2\alpha z}}{2\alpha}\right)\partial_{x}+\alpha xy\partial_{y}+x\partial_{z}\right),

(5.5)

\displaystyle K_{(6)}

\displaystyle=B_{6}\left(\alpha xy\partial_{x}+\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(y^{2}-x^{2}\right)-\frac{e^{2\alpha z}}{2\alpha}\right)\partial_{y}+y\partial_{z}\right).

(5.6)

where we put the constants
B_{i}
to control the
strength
of the magnetic field (note that the
units
of each
B_{i}
may vary.). We do this because we could not find analytic solutions for arbitrary
B_{i}
and will give equations up to first order in
B_{i}
. However in all cases, we will give the full equations for arbitrary
B_{i}
and then do perturbation analysis.

The Killing magnetic curve generated by the
i^{\text{th}}
Killing vector field is given by Lorentz equation (
2.9
):

\displaystyle\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=K_{(i)}\times\gamma^{\prime},

(5.7)

where the vector product is calculated using the orthonormal basis in a tangent space of the underlying manifold. If we define
F_{(i)}\equiv K_{(i)}\times\gamma^{\prime}
, we obtain the following expressions (remember
\gamma^{\prime}=\dot{\gamma}^{i}\mathbf{e_{i}}
):

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(1)}}{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha z}(\dot{\gamma}^{2}\mathbf{e_{3}}-\dot{\gamma}^{3}\mathbf{e_{2}}),

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(2)}}{B_{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha z}(\dot{\gamma}^{3}\mathbf{e_{1}}-\dot{\gamma}^{1}\mathbf{e_{3}}),

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(3)}}{B_{3}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\alpha z}(-x\dot{\gamma}^{3}\mathbf{e_{1}}-y\dot{\gamma}^{3}\mathbf{e_{2}}+(x\dot{\gamma}^{1}+y\dot{\gamma}^{2})\mathbf{e_{3}}),

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(4)}}{B_{4}}

\displaystyle=(\alpha y\dot{\gamma}^{3}e^{-\alpha z}-\dot{\gamma}^{2})\mathbf{e_{1}}-(\alpha x\dot{\gamma}^{3}e^{-\alpha z}-\dot{\gamma}^{1})\mathbf{e_{2}}+\alpha e^{-\alpha z}(x\dot{\gamma}^{2}-y\dot{\gamma}^{1})\mathbf{e_{3}}.

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(5)}}{B_{5}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(\alpha y\dot{\gamma}^{3}e^{-\alpha z}-\dot{\gamma}^{2})\mathbf{e_{1}}+\left(x\dot{\gamma}^{1}+\frac{e^{\alpha z}}{2\alpha}\dot{\gamma}^{3}+\frac{e^{-\alpha z}\alpha}{2}(y^{2}-x^{2})\dot{\gamma}^{3}\right)\mathbf{e_{2}}

(5.12)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{e^{-\alpha z}\alpha}{2}((x^{2}-y^{2})\dot{\gamma}^{2}-2xy\dot{\gamma}^{1})-\frac{e^{\alpha z}}{2\alpha}\dot{\gamma}^{2}\right)\mathbf{e_{3}},

\displaystyle\frac{F_{(6)}}{B_{6}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-y\dot{\gamma}^{2}-\frac{e^{\alpha z}}{2\alpha}\dot{\gamma}^{3}+\frac{e^{-\alpha z}\alpha}{2}(y^{2}-x^{2})\dot{\gamma}^{3}\right)\mathbf{e_{1}}+y(\dot{\gamma}^{1}-\alpha x\dot{\gamma}^{3}e^{-\alpha z})\mathbf{e_{2}}

(5.13)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{e^{-\alpha z}\alpha}{2}((x^{2}-y^{2})\dot{\gamma}^{1}+2xy\dot{\gamma}^{2})+\frac{e^{\alpha z}}{2\alpha}\dot{\gamma}^{1}\right)\mathbf{e_{3}}

\displaystyle\dot{y}_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{z}_{1}}{z_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})\frac{e^{2\alpha z_{0}}}{\dot{z}_{0}}.

By integrating the last equation, we obtain:

5.3
Magnetic Trajectory by the Third Killing Vector Field

Using the expression found for

) and Equation (

\displaystyle\ddot{y}-2\alpha\dot{y}\dot{z}

\displaystyle=-B_{3}y\dot{z},

(5.37)

We will solve these equations of motion using a perturbation analysis. We write down
x=x_{0}+B_{3}x_{1},y=y_{0}+B_{3}y_{1},z=z_{0}+B_{3}z_{1}

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}=2\alpha c_{1}e^{2\alpha z_{0}}z_{1}+\frac{x_{0}}{2\alpha}-\frac{c_{1}(t-c_{4})}{2\alpha}e^{2\alpha z_{0}}+c_{7}e^{2\alpha z_{0}}.

hence we can write:

[

\displaystyle z_{\text{1h}}=c_{9}\tanh(\tau)+c_{10}(1-\tau\tanh(\tau)),

(5.49)

So we can write:

\displaystyle z_{1}=\frac{\tau}{2\alpha^{3}c_{3}}-\frac{c_{1}c_{7}+c_{2}c_{8}}{\alpha(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2})}+c_{9}\tanh(\tau)+c_{10}(1-\tau\tanh(\tau)).

We use this information in Equation (
5.44
) to find

by performing integration by Mathematica
[
28
]

x_{1}=-\frac{c_{1}\tau^{2}}{4\alpha^{3}c_{3}^{2}}-\frac{c_{1}\log(\cosh(\tau))}{2\alpha^{3}\left(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\right)}\\
+\tau\left(\frac{c_{5}}{2\alpha^{2}c_{3}}+\frac{c_{1}^{3}+c_{2}^{2}c_{1}}{\alpha^{3}\left(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\right){}^{2}}\tanh(\tau)+\frac{c_{1}c_{3}c_{10}\text{sech}^{2}(\tau)}{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}\right)\\

where

is a new integration constant. By using the symmetry between

+\tau\left(\frac{c_{6}}{2\alpha^{2}c_{3}}+\frac{c_{2}^{3}+c_{1}^{2}c_{2}}{\alpha^{3}\left(c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}\right){}^{2}}\tanh(\tau)+\frac{c_{2}c_{3}c_{10}\text{sech}^{2}(\tau)}{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}\right)\\

On the other hand, the homogenous solution

6
Conclusion

In this study, we investigated the
\mathbb{H}^{3}
manifold which is a
(-\alpha)
-Kenmotsu manifold admitting six Killing vector fields. We have solved the geodesic equation of motion analytically. We calculated the motion of a charged particle under the magnetic field
B_{i}K_{(i)}
upto first order in
B_{i}
analytically using perturbation theory for all Killing vector fields
K_{(i)}
. We have put a scaling factor

in front of Killing vectors to manage strength of magnetic field and make it amenable to perturbative analysis. We used units where

B_{i}
is not necessarily the unit of magnetic field. We also proved that 3-dimensional
(\alpha)
-Kenmotsu manifolds cannot have any magnetic vector field in the direction of their Reeb vector fields. This result is interesting because most of the studies related with magnetic curves of almost contact manifolds in literature deals with Reeb magnetic curves.

Appendix A
Representation of a Solution in the Poincaré Disk

In this section, we make connection with the hyperbolic manifold we used (
\mathbb{H}^{3}
) and the Poincaré Disk. The metric we have is:

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=e^{-2\alpha z}(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2})+\mathrm{d}z^{2}.

(A.1)

By multiplying the metric with
\alpha^{2}
we obtain:

\mathrm{d}\sigma^{2}=e^{-2z}(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2})+\mathrm{d}z^{2},

(A.2)

where we map
\alpha x\to x,\alpha y\to y,\alpha z\to z
(This is equivalent to setting
\alpha=1
). If we suppress the
y
coordinate, we obtain:

\mathrm{d}\Sigma^{2}=e^{-2z}\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}z^{2}.

(A.3)

Let us define
y=e^{z}
(this is
not
the original ‘
y
’ coordinate.) Then the metric becomes:

\mathrm{d}\Sigma^{2}=\frac{\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2}}{y^{2}},

(A.4)

which is the metric on the Poincaré half plane
[
26
]
. Considering this plane defined by
\{(x,y)\mid x\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}
as the upper half complex plane with
z=x+iy
, using the following Möbius transformation (where this particular form is known as Cayley transformation
[
17
]
), we map upper complex plane into a unit disk:

\omega(z)=\frac{z-i}{z+i}.

(A.5)

The Cartesian coordinates (
\omega_{x}
is the real part,
\omega_{y}
is the imaginary part) that correspond to
\omega(x+iy)
is then calculated as:

\displaystyle\omega_{x}=\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}-1}{x^{2}+(y+1)^{2}},\quad\omega_{y}=-\frac{2x}{x^{2}+(y+1)^{2}}.

(A.6)

Representation of solutions in the Poincaré Disk is useful, because it allows us to visualize the asymptotic behavior of solutions as
t\to\pm\infty
. In a way, it may be seen similar to Penrose diagrams
[

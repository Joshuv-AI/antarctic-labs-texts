Bilinear maximal functions associated with degenerate surfaces
Sanghyuk Lee and Kalachand Shuin

Abstract
. We study L p × L q → L r L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r} -boundedness of (sub)bilinear maximal functions associated with degenerate hypersurfaces. First, we obtain the maximal bound on the sharp range of exponents p , q , r p,q,r (except some border line cases) for the bilinear maximal functions given by the model surface { ( y , z ) ∈ ℝ n × ℝ n : | y | l 1 + | z | l 2 = 1 } \big\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:|y|^{l_{1}}+|z|^{l_{2}}=1\big\} , ( l 1 , l 2 ) ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ) 2 (l_{1},l_{2})\in[1,\infty)^{2} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . Our result manifests that nonvanishing Gaussian curvature is not good enough, in contrast with L p L^{p} -boundedness of the (sub)linear maximal operator associated to hypersurfaces, to characterize the best possible maximal boundedness. Secondly, we consider the bilinear maximal function associated to the finite type curve in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} and obtain a complete characterization of the maximal bound. We also prove multilinear generalizations of the aforementioned results.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
d\geq 2
and
d\sigma
be the surface measure on a smooth hypersurface
\Sigma
. The maximal function associated with
\Sigma
is defined by

\displaystyle M\!f(x)=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}f(x-ty)d\sigma(y)\Big|

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
L^{p}
boundedness of
M
has been extensively studied for the last several decades since
Stein’s seminal work
[
36
]
on the spherical maximal function, which we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{s}
.
The celebrated theorem due to Stein
[
36
]
and Bourgain
[
6
]
is that

\mathcal{M}_{s}
is bounded on
L^{p}
if and only if
p>{d}/(d-1)
.
The results naturally extends to compact surfaces with nonvanishing Gaussian curvature (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
),
which are said to be
nondegenerate
. Besides
L^{p}
bounds,
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimates (
p<q
) for the localized maximal function which is defined by taking supremum over a compact interval included in
(0,\infty)
are also almost completely understood except for some endpoint cases
[
33
,
34
,
26
]
(see, also,
[
1
]
and
[
31
]
for recent related developments when the supremum is taken over on a subset of dimension less than
1
).

The maximal functions associated with
smooth degenerate surfaces were also considered by various authors
(
[
35
,
9
,
10
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
and references therein).
However, the problem of characterizing
L^{p}
boundedness of
M
when Gaussian curvature of
\Sigma
is allowed to vanish at finite order remains largely open when
d\geq 3
.
The problem for curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is relatively simpler
[
21
]
. So far, this problem is better understood in dimension
d=3
(see, e.g.,
[
20
]
). Maximal functions defined by convex surfaces and some model surfaces were also studied
(
[
28
,
24
,
22
]
). There are also results for surfaces which are non-smooth and non-convex hypersurfaces (
[
18
]
).

Bilinear spherical maximal function

Let
n\geq 1
, and let
S^{\Phi}
denote a compact surface given by
S^{\Phi}=\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:\Phi(y,z)=0\}

for a smooth function
\Phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.
We now consider the maximal function

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{{\Phi}}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{{S}^{\Phi}}f(x-ty)g(x-tz)d\sigma_{\Phi}(y,z)\Big|,

where
d\sigma_{\Phi}
is the normalized surface measure on
{S}^{\Phi}
.
The maximal operator can be regarded as a (sub)bilinear analogue of the (sub)linear maximal operator given by
S^{\Phi}
.
The problem is to characterize the exponents
(p,q,r)
for which the estimate

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{M}^{{\Phi}}(f,g)\|_{L^{r}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}

holds.
When
\Phi(y,z)=|y|^{2}+|z|^{2}-1
,
\mathfrak{M}^{{\Phi}}
is called
the bilinear spherical maximal function, which we denote by

\mathfrak{M}_{s}
. Boundedness of
\mathfrak{M}_{s}
was first studied by Geba–Greenleaf–Iosevich–Palsson–Sawyer
[
14
]
, and later
by Barrionuevo–Grafakos–He–Honzík–Oliveira
[
4
]
and Heo–Hong–Yang
[
17
]
. They obtained partial results.
More recently, Jeong and the first author
[
25
]
established boundedness of
\mathfrak{M}_{s}
on the sharp range for
n\geq 2
.
More precisely, it was shown that
\mathfrak{M}_{s}
boundedly maps
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
if
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}

and

({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\in\mathcal{P}:=\big\{(x,y)\in[0,1)^{2}:x+y<(2n-1)/n\big\}.

The result also has a natural multilinear generalization (
[
25
]
and
[
12
,
11
]
).
Boundedness of the
bilinear circular maximal function (when
n=1
) was shown by Christ–Zhou
[
8
]
and Dosidis–Ramos
[
13
]
, independently.

There are also results in different directions. Sparse domination of
\mathfrak{M}_{s}
was studied by Palsson–Sovine
[
29
]
and Borges–Foster–Ou–Pipher–Zhou
[
5
]
.
Related results for maximal product of spherical averages were obtained by Roncal, Shrivastava and the second author
[
30
]
.
The discrete analogues of
\mathfrak{M}_{s}
were studied by Anderson and Palsson
[
2
,
3
]
.
Christ and Zhou
[
8
]
recently studied boundedness of bilinear lacunary maximal function defined on certain class of curves.

However, boundedness of bilinear maximal estimate associated with other hypersurfaces than the sphere is not well understood.
As far as the authors are aware, there are only a few results concerning bilinear maximal function associated with degenerate surfaces.
Chen–Grafakos–He–Honzík–Slavíková
[
7
]
obtained
L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}
estimate for bilinear maximal function defined by a
(2n-1)
-dimensional compact hypersurface which
has more than
n+2
non-vanishing principal curvatures.

We begin our discussion, on bilinear maximal function associated with degenerate surfaces, with
the following observation which extends the result in
[
7
]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
{S}
be a
(2n-1)
-dimensional compact hypersurface which has at least
n+1
non-vanishing principal curvatures. Then, the bilinear maximal function
\mathfrak{M}^{{S}}
, defined over the hypersurface
{S}
maps
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
, for
1<p,q,r\leq\infty
with
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}
.

Bilinear maximal function associated to degenerate hypersurfaces

The main object of this paper is to investigate boundedness of
\mathfrak{M}^{{\Phi}}
with some degenerate model surfaces.
For the purpose, we consider the surface

\displaystyle S_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}:=\big\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}}+|z|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}}=1\big\},

for
n\geq 2
and
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2})\in(0,\infty)^{2}
.
We consider a biparametric bilinear averaging operator
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}
over
S_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}
which is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}(f,g)(x):=\int_{S_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}}f(x-t_{1}y)g(x-t_{2}z)~d\mu(y,z),\quad\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{2}

where
d\mu
is the normalized surface measure on
S_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}
.
Instead of one parameter maximal function we study the biparametric maximal function

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{2}}\big|\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}(f,g)(x)\big|.

To state our result, for
\mathbf{a}\in(0,\infty)^{2}
we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{P}:x<1-\frac{(\mathbf{a}_{2}-n)_{+}}{\mathbf{a}_{2}n},\ \ y<1-\frac{(\mathbf{a}_{1}-n)_{+}}{\mathbf{a}_{1}n}\Big\},

where
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
. Note that
\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{a}}=\mathcal{P}
when
\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\leq n
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\mathbf{a}\in[1,\infty)^{2}
. Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r>0
with
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}
. Then, the estimate

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

holds if
({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

One can also obtain weak or restricted weak type bounds on
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
for
({1}/{p},{1}/{q})
which belongs to the boundary line segments of
\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{a}}
, using a simple summation trick (for example, see
[
25
]
and
[
26
, Lemma
2.6
]
).
However, in this article we focus on the strong type bound. In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we make use of the slicing argument from
[
25
]
, by which it was also possible to prove boundedness of the biparametric bilinear spherical maximal function. In Section
4
we also consider multilinear maximal operators generalizing Theorem
1.2
.

Remark 1.3

(Sharpness of Theorem
1.2
)
.

The range of exponents in Theorem 1.2 is sharp in that
the estimate (
1.2
) does not hold if
({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\notin\overline{{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathbf{a}}}
, where
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathbf{a}}}
denotes the closure of
\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{a}}
. Moreover, the operator
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
does not map
L^{1}\times L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}\times L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1}
. The same holds true for the maximal operator

(1.3)

\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\big|\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{(t,t)}(f,g)(x)\big|.

(See Section
2.2
.) Hence, Theorem
1.2
also holds even if
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
is replaced by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

Consider
S_{2}^{(2,2l_{2})}
for an integer
l_{2}\geq 2
, i.e., the hypersurface which is given by
y^{2}_{1}+y^{2}_{2}+\cdots+y^{2}_{n}+(z^{2}_{1}+\cdots+z^{2}_{n})^{l_{2}}=1
. Observe that Gaussian curvature of
S_{2}^{(2,2l_{2})}
vanishes at the point
(\theta,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
whenever
|\theta|=1
. However, Theorem
1.2
implies that
\mathfrak{M}^{(2,2l_{2})}
enjoys the same boundedness property as the bilinear spherical maximal function, that is to say,
\mathfrak{M}^{(2,2l_{2})}
is bounded from
L^{p}\times L^{q}
to
L^{r}
,
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}
, for

({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\in\mathcal{P}
as long as
2l_{2}\leq n
. In contrast to
L^{p}
boundedness of the linear maximal function, the boundedness of
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
is less sensitive to curvature vanishing.

Bilinear maximal functions associated to curves

Let
I
denote the interval
(0,1)
and let
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth regular curve. We consider the maximal operator

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{\mathbf{t}>0}\Big|\int f(x-t_{1}\gamma_{1}(s))g(x-t_{2}\gamma_{2}(s))\psi(s)ds\Big|,

where
\gamma(s)=(\gamma_{1}(s),\gamma_{2}(s))
and
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
. If
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(s)\neq 0
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}(s)\neq 0
on
\operatorname{supp}\psi
,
we have a complete characterization of
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
boundedness.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}
. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
be nonnegative and nontrivial. Suppose
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(s)\neq 0
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}(s)\neq 0
on
\operatorname{supp}\psi
. Then,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

holds if and only if
1<r\leq\infty
.

Since
\gamma
is a regular curve (i.e.,
\gamma^{\prime}\neq 0
), by symmetry and a change of variables
we may assume that
\gamma
is of the form

(1.5)

\gamma(s)=(s,\gamma_{2}(s))

in a small neighborhood of
s_{0}\in I
. For

m\geq 2
, we say that
\gamma_{2}
is of type
m
at
s
if

\gamma_{2}^{(k)}(s)=0
for
1\leq k<m
and
\gamma_{2}^{(m)}(s)\neq 0
.
Iosevich
[
21
]
proved that the linear maximal function associated with curves of type
m
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
p>m
.
The following may be regarded as a bilinear counterpart of his result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
m\geq 2
, and
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}
. Let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
be nonnegative.
Suppose
\gamma
takes the form (
1.5
) in a neighborhood of
s_{0}\in I
and
\gamma_{2}
is at most type
m
at
s_{0}
.
If
\operatorname{supp}\psi
is contained in a sufficiently small neighborhood of
s_{0}
and
\psi(s_{0})\neq 0
, then we have the following:

(i)

Suppose
\gamma(s_{0})=0
. Then, (
1.4
) holds if and only if
1<p,q,r\leq\infty
.

(ii)

Suppose
\gamma(s_{0})\neq 0
and
s_{0}=0
. Then, (
1.4
) holds if and only if
1<p\leq\infty
,
m<q\leq\infty
, and
1<r\leq\infty
.

(ii)

Suppose
\gamma(s_{0})\neq 0
and
s_{0}\neq 0
. Then, (
1.4
) holds if and only if
1<p,q\leq\infty
and
{m}/{q}+{1}/{p}<1
.

The assertion
(ii)
in Theorem
1.5
recovers the optimal boundedness of the bilinear circular maximal function which was obtained in
[
8
,
13
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
and Proposition
1.1

In this section we prove boundedness of the bilinear maximal functions
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathcal{S}}
associated with a compact hypersurface of having
k>1
non-vanishing principal curvatures.
We also show sharpness of the results in Theorem
1.2
considering particular functions.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

To prove boundedness of
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
, we utilize the slicing argument from
[
26
]
, which
allows us to break the bilinear maximal operator to a product of two linear maximal operators.
We begin by recalling the following formula (
[
19
, p.136]
).
Let
\Omega
be a
(k-1)
-dimensional hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
given by
\Omega=\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\Phi(\omega)=0\}
. If
|\nabla\Phi(\omega)|\neq 0
for
\omega\in\Omega
, then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(\omega)\frac{d\mu(\omega)}{|\nabla\Phi(\omega)|}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}F(\omega)\delta(\Phi)~d\omega,

where
d\mu
is the induced surface measure on
\Omega
and
F
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

We may assume that
f,g\geq 0
.
Using the identity (
2.1
) for the hypersurface
S^{\mathbf{a}}_{2}=\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z):=|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}}+|z|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}}-1=0\}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{S_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}}f(x-t_{1}y)g(x-t_{2}z)~\frac{d\mu(y,z)}{|\nabla\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-t_{1}y)g(x-t_{2}z)\delta(\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}})dzdy.

Since
|\nabla\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|^{2}=\mathbf{a}^{2}_{1}|y|^{2(\mathbf{a}_{1}-1)}+\mathbf{a}^{2}_{2}|z|^{2(\mathbf{a}_{2}-1)}\neq 0
and
\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\geq 1
, we see
c_{\mathbf{a}}\leq|\nabla\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|^{2}\leq C_{\mathbf{a}}<\infty
for all
(y,z)\in S_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}
and some positive constants
c_{\mathbf{a}},C_{\mathbf{a}}
. Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}(f,g)(x)\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-t_{1}y)g(x-t_{2}z)\delta(\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}})dzdy.

Note that the left hand side is equal to
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x-t_{1}y)(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x-t_{2}z)\delta(\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,\cdot)dz)dy
. Thus, fixing
y
and using the identity (
2.1
) with
\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}(f,g)(x)\sim\int_{|y|<1}f(x-t_{1}y)\int_{\Omega_{y}}g(x-tz)\frac{d\mu_{y}^{\mathbf{a}}(z)}{|\nabla_{z}\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|}~dy,

where
\Omega_{y}=\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,\cdot)^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu_{y}^{\mathbf{a}}
is the induced surface measure on
\Omega_{y}
.

For
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2})\in[1,\infty)^{2}
,
we set

(2.2)

\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)=(1-|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{1/\mathbf{a}_{2}},\quad\tilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{a}}(y)=(1-|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}})^{1/\mathbf{a}_{1}}.

Also, we set

(2.3)

\mathfrak{A}g(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}g(x-t\theta)~d\theta.

Note that
\Omega_{y}
is the
(n-1)
dimensional sphere
\Omega_{y}
of radius
\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)
and
|\nabla_{z}\Phi_{2}^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|=\mathbf{a}_{2}\omega^{\mathbf{a}_{2}-1}_{\mathbf{a}}(y)\neq 0
.
Therefore, by scaling we obtain

(2.4)

\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}(f,g)(x)\sim\int_{|y|<1}f(x-t_{1}y)\mathfrak{A}g(x,t_{2}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y))\omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{{n-\mathbf{a}_{2}}}(y)dy.

Interchanging the roles of
f
and
g
, we also have

(2.5)

\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{\mathbf{t}}(f,g)(x)\sim\int_{|z|<1}g(x-t_{2}z)\mathfrak{A}f(x,t_{1}\tilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{a}}(z))\tilde{\omega}_{\mathbf{a}}^{n-\mathbf{a}_{1}}(z)dz.

Depending on whether
\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}
are less than or greater than the dimension
n
, we consider the following three cases, separately:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{A}):\qquad\ \max\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\}\leq n,

\displaystyle(\mathrm{B}):\min\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\}\leq n<\max\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\},

\displaystyle(\mathrm{C}):\qquad\ n<\min\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\}.

Case
(\mathrm{A}):\max\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\}\leq n

In this case, we note that
\mathcal{P}^{\mathbf{a}}=\mathcal{P}
and
\omega_{\mathbf{a}}^{{n-\mathbf{a}_{2}}}
is bounded. Thus, (
2.4
) gives the inequality

(2.6)

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)\lesssim Mf(x)\mathcal{M}_{s}g(x),

where
M
denotes the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function.
Using Hölder’s inequality, by
L^{p}
-boundedness of
M
(for
1<p\leq\infty
) and the spherical maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{s}
(for
{n}/({n-1})<p\leq\infty
), we see that
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
maps
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
for
({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\in[0,1)\times[0,\frac{n-1}{n})
.
Similarly, since
\mathbf{a}_{1}\leq n
, from (
2.5
) we have

(2.7)

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{s}f(x)Mg(x).

Therefore, Hölder’s inequality and boundedness of
M
and
\mathcal{M}_{s}
show that
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
is bounded from
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
, for
({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\in[0,\frac{n-1}{n})\times[0,1)
.
Hence, interpolation (for example, see
[
15
]
) gives
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
bound on
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathbf{a}}
for all
({1}/{p},{1}/{q})\in\mathcal{P}
.

Case
(\mathrm{B}):\min\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\}\leq n<\max\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\}

We only consider the case
\mathbf{a}_{1}\leq n<\mathbf{a}_{2}
. The other case
(\mathbf{a}_{2}\leq n<\mathbf{a}_{1})
can be handled in the same manner by interchanging the roles of
\mathbf{a}_{1}

3.1.
Proof of Theorem

We first prove the sufficiency part of Theorem
1.4
.
Since
\gamma_{2}^{\prime}(s)\neq 0
on
\operatorname{supp}\psi
, changing variables
y=\gamma_{2}(s)
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{t_{2}>0}\int^{1}_{0}|g(x-t_{2}\gamma_{2}(s))\psi(s)|ds\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}Mg(x).

Thus, by the Hardy–Littlewood maximal inequality we have

(3.1)

\|\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{L^{q}}

for
1<q\leq\infty
.
Similarly, since
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(s)\neq 0
on
\operatorname{supp}\psi
, we get

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\lesssim Mf(x)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

This gives

(3.2)

\|\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}

for
1<p\leq\infty
.
By interpolation we obtain the desired
L^{p}\times L^{q}\to L^{r}
boundedness for
1<p,q\leq\infty
and
{1}/{r}={1}/{p}+{1}/{q}
.

We now show the necessity part. That is to say,
\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}
can not be bounded from
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
if
r\leq 1
.
Since
\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(s)\neq 0
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}(s)\neq 0
on
\operatorname{supp}\psi
, there is a point
s_{0}

such that
\gamma_{1}(s_{0})\neq 0
,
\gamma_{2}(s_{0})\neq 0
, and
\psi(s_{0})>0
.
Thus, we have

(3.3)

|\gamma_{1}(s_{0})-\gamma_{1}(s)|\sim|s-s_{0}|,\quad|\gamma_{2}(s_{0})-\gamma_{2}(s)|\sim|s-s_{0}|

if
s\in[s_{0},s_{0}+\delta]
with a constant
\delta>0
small enough. We may also assume
\psi\gtrsim 1
on the interval
[s_{0},s_{0}+\delta]
.
We consider

f(x)=|x|^{-1/p}\log^{-2/p}(|x|^{-1})\chi_{(-1,1)},\quad g(x)=|x|^{-1/q}\log^{-2/q}(|x|^{-1})\chi_{(-1,1)}.

Then, it is easy to see that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
for
0<p,q<\infty
. Taking
t_{1}={x}/{\gamma_{1}(s_{0})}
and
t_{2}={x}/{\gamma_{2}(s_{0})}
, we have

(3.4)

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\gtrsim\int^{s_{0}+\delta}_{s_{0}}f\big(x-\frac{x\gamma_{1}(s)}{\gamma_{1}(s_{0})}\big)g\big(x-\frac{x\gamma_{2}(s)}{\gamma_{2}(s_{0})}\big)ds.

Thus, using (
3.3
), we have

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\gtrsim\int^{s_{0}+c}_{s_{0}}|x(s_{0}-s)|^{-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\log^{-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{q}}\big(\frac{c}{x|s_{0}-s|}\big)ds

for
x\in(0,1)
and a small
c>0
.
Therefore, after a change of variables we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\gtrsim x^{-1}\int^{cx}_{0}t^{-1/r}\log^{-2/r}(c/t)~dt\gtrsim\begin{cases}\,(x\log(c/x))^{-1},&\ \text{if}\quad r=1,\\
\qquad\infty,&\ \text{if}\quad r<1.\end{cases}

So, it follows that
\|\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)\|_{L^{r}}=\infty
if
r\leq 1
as desired.
When
p=\infty
(
q=\infty
), taking
f=\chi_{(-1,1)}
(
g=\chi_{(-1,1)}
, respectively), we get the same conclusion.

Remark 3.1
.

By the same argument in the above showing the necessity part we can also show that the maximal estimate (
1.4
) fails if

when

3.2.
Proof of Theorem

3.2.1.
Proof of
(i)

It is clear that
\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\lesssim Mf(x)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}
. This gives (

) for

. Since
\gamma_{2}(s_{0})=0
, changing variables

3.2.2.
Proof of
(ii)

Since

(3.6)

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{2}}\int f(x-t_{1}s)\,g(x-t_{2}(c_{\ast}-s^{m}\phi(s)))\psi(s)ds,

for some

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}(f,g)(x)\lesssim\sum_{2^{-k}\leq c^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}_{k}(f,g)(x),

where

\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}_{k}(f,g)(x)=2^{k\frac{m-1}{m}}\sup_{\mathbf{t}\in(0,\infty)^{2}}\int_{2^{-k}\leq|y-c_{\ast}|<2^{1-k}}f(x-t_{1}s(y))\,g(x-t_{2}y){dy}.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

For

Let

Indeed, note that

Recalling (

We prove that (

4.
Multilinear generalizations

In this section we try to extend the bilinear result to a multilinear setting. Let
m\geq 3
and denote

\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{m})\in[1,\infty)^{m},\quad\vec{y}=(y^{1},y^{2},\dots,y^{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}.

We consider the hypersurface

Here, by

we mean
\sup_{t_{1}>0,\dots,t_{m}>0}

are nonnegative.

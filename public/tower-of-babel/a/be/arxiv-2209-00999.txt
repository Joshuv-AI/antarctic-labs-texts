Almost sharp sharpness for Poisson Boolean percolation
Barbara Dembin, Vincent Tassion

Abstract
We consider Poisson Boolean percolation on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with power-law distribution on the radius with a finite d d -moment for d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . We prove that subcritical sharpness occurs for all but a countable number of power-law distributions. This extends the results of Duminil-Copin–Raoufi–Tassion [ 5 ] where subcritical sharpness is proved under the assumption that the radii distribution has a 5 ​ d − 3 5d-3 finite moment. Our proofs techniques are different from [ 5 ] : we do not use randomized algorithm and rely on specific independence properties of Boolean percolation, inherited from the underlying Poisson process. We also prove supercritical sharpness for any distribution with a finite d d -moment and the continuity of the critical parameter for the truncated distribution when the truncation goes to infinity.

Introduction

Overview

Boolean percolation was introduced by Gilbert in
[
8
]
as a continuous version of Bernoulli percolation, introduced by Broadbent and Hammersley
[
3
]
. We consider a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda>0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and on each point, we center a ball of potentially random radius. In Boolean percolation we are interested in the connectivity properties of the occupied set: it is defined as the subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
consisting of all the points covered by at least one ball.

This model undergoes a phase transition in
\lambda
for the existence of an unbounded connected component of balls. For
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
, all the connected components are bounded, and for
\lambda>\lambda_{c}
, there exists at least one unbounded connected component.

When the radii of the balls are bounded, Boolean percolation exhibits a very similar behaviour as standard Bernoulli percolation. In contrast, when the law of the radii has fat tails, the existence of large balls creates long-range dependencies in the occupied set, leading to different behaviours, and proving them often requires new methods. This is particularly true for the subcritical phase: When the radii are bounded, the connection probability between two fixed points of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
decays exponentially fast in the distance between the two points: there is subcritical sharpness. But this cannot be the case when the radii have subexponential tails, because the connection probability is always larger than the probability that the two points are covered by a large ball. Nevertheless, a notion of sharpness can be defined in this case. This was established in
[
5
]
under a moment assumption. In this paper, we extend the result to a minimal moment assumptions, but we restrict to a particular family of laws for the radii. We also establish supercritical sharpness, by relying on standard methods for Bernoulli percolation.

Definition of the model

Let us more formally define Boolean percolation. Let
d\geq 2
. Denote by
\|\cdot\|
the
\ell_{2}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set

\mathrm{B}^{x}_{r}:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\|y-x\|\leq r\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial\mathrm{B}^{x}_{r}:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\|y-x\|=r\right\}

for the closed ball of radius
r
centered at
x
and its boundary. For short, we will write
\mathrm{B}_{r}
for
\mathrm{B}_{r}^{0}
.
For a subset
\eta
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we define the occupied set associated to
\eta
by

\mathcal{O}(\eta):=\bigcup_{(z,r)\in\eta}\mathrm{B}_{r}^{z}.

Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\lambda>0
. Let
\eta
be a Poisson point process of intensity
\lambda\,dz\otimes\mu
where
dz
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Write
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu}
for the law of
\eta
and
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\mu}
for the expectation under the law
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu}
. We will work with measures
\mu
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}t^{d}d\mu(t)<\infty.

(1.1)

This hypothesis is natural for the study of percolation properties of the occupied set
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
. Indeed, Hall proved in
[
12
]
that this condition is necessary and sufficient to avoid that all the space is covered.
We say that two points
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are connected by
\eta
if there exists a continuous path in
\mathcal{O}(\eta)
that joins
x
to
y
. We say that two sets
A
and
B
are connected if there exists
x\in A
and
y\in B
such that
x
and
y
are connected by
\eta
. We denote by
\{A\longleftrightarrow B\}
this event.

The phase transition of Boolean percolation is defined as follows.
Define the critical parameter by

\lambda_{c}(\mu):=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu}\left(0\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)=0\right\}.

Under the minimal assumption (
1.1
), Gouéré proved in
[
9
]
that
0<\lambda_{c}(\mu)<\infty
.

Subcritical sharpness

For Bernoulli percolation, the terminology "subcritical sharpness" refers to the exponential decay of the connection probabilities in the subcritical regime, see
[
16
,
2
,
6
]
. For Boolean percolation with subexponential radii distribution, such exponential decay does not hold for any
\lambda>0
. In order to define subcritical sharpness, we introduce the following critical point (following Gouéré–Théret
[
10
]
)

\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\mu):=\inf\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\,\inf_{r>0}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu}(\mathrm{B}_{r}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{2r})>0\right\}.

In the regime
\lambda<\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\mu)
, renormalization arguments apply and provide an accurate description of the model, see Gouéré,Gouéré–Théret
[
9
,
10
]
. For example, when
\mu
has a subexponential tail, it was proved in
[
5
]
that the probability that
0
is connected to distance
n
is equivalent to the probability that the origin is covered by one ball intersecting
\partial B_{n}
.

Subcritical sharpness
corresponds to coincidence of the two critical points
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\mu)=\lambda_{c}(\mu)

In the case of bounded radius, Boolean percolation behaves at a high level in a similar way as standard percolation. The subcritical sharpness is known, and there is exponential decay in the subcritical regime
[
19
,
5
,
7
]
. In the case of unbounded radius, the subcritical sharpness has been proved by Duminil-Copin–Raoufi–Tassion
[
5
]
, when the distribution has a
5d-3
finite moment, using randomized algorithms and OSSS inequality (they made a discretization of the space-radius to be able to use the OSSS inequality). Later Last–Pecatti–Yogeshwaran
[
14
]
proved a continuous version of the OSSS inequality
[
17
]
and obtained sharpness for a general class of models, but their result does not include all the Boolean percolation with the minimal assumption (
1.1
). A key idea in the OSSS approach is to control the probability that a given ball is revealed when certain exploration algorithms are run. This method fails for fat tails, because the revealment of large balls by the randomized algorithm is too large, and new ideas are needed to bypass this obstacle.

In the special case of
d=2
, the subcritical sharpness has been proved by Ahlberg–Tassion–Teixeira
[
1
]
under minimal assumption (
1.1
) using crucially the planarity (the proofs relies on Russo–Seymour–Welsh theory).

In this paper, we investigate the subcritical sharpness for distribution
\mu
under the minimal assumption (
1.1
). We restrict our study to the following family of power-law distributions.
For
\delta>0
, set

\mu_{\delta}:=\frac{1}{r^{d+1+\delta}}\mathbf{1}_{r\geq 1}dr.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Almost sharp subcritical sharpness)
.

There exists
\mathcal{D}\subset(0,\infty)
at most countable such that for all
\delta\in(0,\infty)\setminus\mathcal{D}
, we have

\lambda_{c}(\mu_{\delta})=\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\mu_{\delta}).

(1.2)

Remark 1.2
.

The set
\mathcal{D}
is defined as the set of discontinuity points of
\delta\mapsto\lambda_{c}(\mu_{\delta})
. By monotonicity, we know that
\mathcal{D}
is at most countable, but we expect that
\lambda_{c}(\delta)
is actually continuous in
\delta
, namely that
\mathcal{D}=\emptyset
, but we were not able to prove this.

Remark 1.3
.

We call this result almost sharp sharpness, because it states that for the laws
\{\mu_{\delta},\delta\}
, Boolean percolation satisfies subcritical sharpness under the minimal assumption (
1.1
) (except possibly for countably many
\delta
). In particular, it implies that for every
d_{0}>d
, there exists a distribution with infinite
d_{0}
-moment for which subcritical sharpness holds.

Our proof techniques are very different from
[
5
]
, in particular we do not use randomized algorithm. We prove a Talagrand formula
[
18
]
for Boolean percolation to prove a sharp threshold. There is a real difficulty in applying Talagrand formula in this context. The big balls play the role of a dictator so we need to increase their intensity a lot to prove a sharp threshold. Doing a small sprinkling in
\lambda
is not sufficient to obtain a sharp threshold in that context.

Supercritical sharpness

In the supercritical regime of bond percolation, the key property to start a renormalization is the so-called local uniqueness: with high probability a large box contains a unique macroscopic cluster. If this property is true for every
p>p_{c}
, we say that the model has supercritical sharpness. The main step to prove local uniqueness is to prove percolation in slabs using Grimmett–Marstrand argument
[
11
]
. They proved that for any
p>p_{c}
, for
k
large enough there exists an infinite connected component in the slab
S_{k}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-k,k]^{d-2}
.
For Bernoulli bond percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, supercritical sharpness is more involved and delicate than its subcritical counterpart. In contrast, for Boolean percolation, subcritical appears more challenging than supercritical percolation: as discussed in the previous section, establishing subcritical sharpness requires new ideas, while for supercritical sharpness, the proof follows essentially the same lines as in the case of bond percolation. Some small adaptations are needed, but the proof is rather simpler for Boolean than the lattice version, thanks to rotational symmetries specific to a continuous model. In this paper, we prove that supercritical sharpness holds for any distribution satisfying (
1.1
).
We define

\lambda_{c}^{S_{k}}(\mu):=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu}\left(0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}(\eta\cap(S_{k}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}))}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\partial\mathrm{B}_{r}\right)=0\right\}.

The following theorem is proved using the seedless renormalization scheme of
[
4
]
, which is a refinement of the original argument
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be a distribution that satisfies (
1.1
). We have

\inf_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{c}^{S_{n}}(\mu|_{[0,n]})=\lambda_{c}(\mu)\,

where
\mu|_{[0,n]}
is the restriction of the distribution on the interval
[0,n]
.

We deduce from this result the two following straightforward corollaries. We have supercritical sharpness for any distribution
\mu
.

Corollary 1.5

(Supercritical sharpness)
.

Let
\mu
be a distribution that satisfies (
1.1
). We have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{c}^{S_{k}}(\mu)=\lambda_{c}(\mu).

The following corollary states that the function
\lambda_{c}
is continuous with respect to the truncated distribution.

Corollary 1.6

(Continuity of
\lambda_{c}
for truncated radius)
.

Let
\mu
be a distribution that satisfies (
1.1
). We have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{c}(\mu|_{[0,n]})=\lambda_{c}(\mu).

Key steps and inputs for the proof Theorem
1.1
.

We proceed by contradiction and assume that there exists a non empty regime
\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta)<\lambda_{c}(\delta)
. In other words, we assume the existence of a regime
\lambda\in(\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta),\lambda_{c}(\delta))
for which there is not infinite cluster but the crossing probability
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{2n})
does not go to
0
(along an infinite subsequence of
n
). We consider
\lambda\in(\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta),\lambda_{c}(\delta))
, and we prove that for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, the process percolates at parameter
\lambda+\varepsilon
, which is a contradiction to
\lambda<\lambda_{c}(\delta)
. We proceed in 3 steps:

Step 1: sharpness for truncated radii.

If
\lambda>\widehat{\lambda}_{c}
, the cluster of
\mathrm{B}_{n}
cannot be too small: at least for some scales, it must intersect
\partial\mathrm{B}_{2n}
with positive probability. By using a union bound, we can deduce a good lower bound on the probability of
\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{N}
for
N\gg n
. On the other hand, if
\lambda<\lambda_{c}
we know that the set of balls of radius
\leq n
do not percolate. Furthermore, by the known sharpness for bounded radii, the connection probabilities for the truncated process (obtained be keeping only the balls of radius
\leq n
) decay exponentially fast. Hence
\mathrm{B}_{n}
cannot be connected too far without using large balls, therefore the cluster of
\mathrm{B}_{n}
must contain balls of radius
\mathrm{B}_{r}
for
r\geq n
. We can make this idea quantitative by using the
\varphi_{p}(S)
argument of
[
6
]
(developed in
[
19
]
for Boolean percolation): For arbitrary
\alpha>0
, we obtain,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{n})\geq\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}},

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\left\{\text{$\mathrm{B}_{n}$ is connected to an open ball of radius larger than $n$}\right\}
(see figure
1
).

Step 2: sharp threshold in
\delta
via hypercontractivity.

The event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
is rotation invariant, and we expect such event to satisfy a sharp threshold. (A small increase in the parameter is sufficient to increase the probability close to one. For a long time in the project, we did not think that this part would be difficult, but we faced two obstacles. The first one is conceptual: there is no general sharp threshold in
\lambda
(see Section
4.1
for an example that illustrates this fact). We overcome this difficulty by proving a sharp threshold in
\delta
instead: when varying
\delta
, the density of large balls increases much faster than the density of the small ones, which prevents the large balls to play a "dictator" role on a full range of parameters. The second difficulty comes from the fact that we want to prove a sharp threshold for rotational invariant events. A natural strategy would be to discretize the space and the radius and apply a discrete Talagrand formula. However, there is a theoretical obstacle in pursuing this strategy: for dimensions
d\geq 3
, there is no rotational invariant way to discretize the space. This is a consequence of the fact that the group of orthogonal transformations is not abelian in dimension
d\geq 3
. Hence, discretizing the model implies losing all the symmetries of the continuous one. For this reason, we had to prove a continuous version of Talagrand’s formula, by using a suitable encoding and limiting procedure. By applying this formula to the event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
, we obtain that

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\delta-\eta}(\mathcal{E}_{n})\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{\alpha}}.

(1.4)

Step 3: Grimmett–Marstrand dynamic exploration:

thanks to the previous step we know that the event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
occurs with high probability for the distribution
\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}
. The open ball in the definition of the event
\mathcal{E}_{n}
plays the role of the seed in Grimmett–Marstrand dynamic renormalization. Up to a small sprinkling
\varepsilon
in
\lambda
, we can prove that there exists an infinite connected component with positive probability. It follows that

\lambda+\varepsilon>\lambda_{c}(\mu_{\delta-\eta}).

(1.5)

Conclusion.

Equation (
1.5
) is not exactly the contradiction we were aiming for because we had to reduce
\delta
, nevertheless we still reach a contradiction if
\delta
is a continuity point of
\lambda_{c}
.

In the regime
(\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta),\lambda_{c}(\delta))
, we can prove that there exists
n\leq N

.

Let
\delta>0

\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta)<\lambda_{c}(\delta)

In the second step of the proof, we prove a sharp threshold in

. In Section

. The following proposition is proved in greater generality in Section

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\lambda,\delta>0
. There exists
\kappa>0
such that the following holds.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta/2\kappa)
,

,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,{\delta}}(\mathcal{E}_{n,N}(\rho))\geq\frac{1}{n^{\varepsilon}}\implies\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,{\delta-\kappa\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{E}_{n,N}(\rho))\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{\varepsilon}}.

Let

1.1
.

Let
\delta>0

\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta)<\lambda_{c}(\delta)

Connection to large balls in the fidicious regime

In this section, we prove Proposition

.

3.1
Correlation length for truncated radii

In this section we consider the law of radii

\mu=\mu_{\delta}|_{[0,n]},

(3.1)

and we write
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}^{n}=\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu}
for the corresponding Boolean percolation measure, and
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}^{n}
for the corresponding expectation. For bounded radii, sharpness of the phase transition is known. In particular, one can define a correlation length in the subcritical regime, and we collect here some quantitative statements that can be deduced from previously known arguments.

Denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Following Ziesche
[
19
]
(see also
[
6
]
), define for every
\lambda\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
and
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\mathrm{B}_{n}\subset S

\varphi_{\lambda}^{n}(S):=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda}^{n}\big[\sum_{(z,r)\in\eta}\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{B}_{r}^{z}\cap\partial S\neq\emptyset,\mathrm{B}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\{(w,s)\in\eta:\mathrm{B}_{s}^{w}\subset S\})}}{{\longleftrightarrow}}\mathrm{B}_{r}^{z}\big]

(3.2)

This functional is instrumental in the proof of sharpness, and we will use the following properties. Let
\lambda\geq 0
,
N\geq n\geq 1
.

1.

If there exists
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\mathrm{B}_{n}\subset S\subset\mathrm{B}_{N}
and
\varphi^{n}_{\lambda}(S)\leq 1/e
then

\forall\ell\geq N\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{\ell})\leq\exp\left(-\left\lfloor{\ell}/{N}\right\rfloor\right).

2.

If for all
S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\mathrm{B}_{n}\subset S\subset\mathrm{B}_{N}
we have
\varphi^{n}_{\lambda}(S)\geq 1/e
then

\forall\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{N})\geq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{e\lambda^{\prime}}.

These properties are not exactly stated in
[
19
]
but they can be obtained from the proof of the first and third item of Theorem 3.1 in
[
19
]
. These properties give rise to a natural definition of a correlation length in the subcritical regime: For every
n\geq 1
and every
\lambda\geq 0
,
let

\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n):=\inf\left\{\ell\in[n,+\infty):\exists S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\mathrm{B}_{n}\subset S\subset\mathrm{B}_{\ell},\,\varphi_{\lambda}^{n}(S)\leq\frac{1}{e}\right\}.

where we use the convention
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
. For fixed
\lambda>0
and
n\geq 1
, by using Properties
1
and
2
and continuity, we have

\displaystyle\forall\ell\geq\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{\ell})\leq\exp\left(-\left\lfloor{\ell}/{\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)}\right\rfloor\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\forall\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{L_{\lambda}(n)})\geq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda}{e\lambda^{\prime}}.

(3.4)

In the subcritical regime and up to logarithmic corrections, the quantity
\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)
can be related to the more standards definition of correlation length for the truncated model (defined as the exponential rate of decay of the the connection probabilities).

3.2
Behaviour of the correlation length in the fidicious regime

By definition, the correlation length satisfies
\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)\in[n,\infty]
. In order to prove Proposition
2.1
, we will use that the correlation length is not degenerated in the fidicious regime, which is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\lambda\in(\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta),\lambda_{c}(\delta))
, there exists
n_{0}\geq 1
such that

\forall n\geq n_{0}\qquad 3n\leq\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)<+\infty.

(3.5)

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
. We prove the following two implications, from which the lemma follows.

\displaystyle\left(\exists n\geq 1\qquad\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)=+\infty\right)\implies\lambda\geq\lambda_{c}(\delta),

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)\leq 3n\text{ for infinitely many }n\geq 1\right)\implies\lambda\leq\widehat{\lambda}_{c}(\delta).

The first implication is a direct consequence of Equation (
3.4
). For the second implication, let
n
such that
\mathrm{L}_{\lambda}(n)\leq 3n
and set
N:=n^{1+\frac{\delta}{2d}}
. If
\mathrm{B}_{N}
is connected to
\partial\mathrm{B}_{2N}
, either the connection uses only balls of radius smaller than or equal to
n
, or there must exist a ball of radius at least
n
intersecting
\mathrm{B}_{2N}
. Therefore, by Markov inequality

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu_{\delta}}(\mathrm{B}_{N}\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{2N})\leq\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{N}\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{2N})+\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\mu}(|\eta\cap E|),

(3.6)

where

. Consider a covering of

\mathrm{B}_{N}
(where

. By the union bound, translation invariance, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{N}\leftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{2N})\leq C\left(\tfrac{N}{n}\right)^{d}\mathbb{P}_{\lambda}^{n}(\mathrm{B}_{n}\longleftrightarrow\partial\mathrm{B}_{N})\overset{\eqref{eq:1}}{\leq}C\left(\tfrac{N}{n}\right)^{d}\exp\big(-\lfloor N/3n\rfloor\big),

Let us now bound the second term in (

is a Poisson random variable with parameter

\mathbb{E}_{\lambda,\mu}(|\eta\cap E|)=(dz\otimes\mu_{\delta})(E)\leq C^{\prime}\int_{n}^{\infty}(N+t)^{d}d\mu_{\delta}(t)\leq C^{\prime\prime}\frac{N^{d}}{n^{d+\delta}}+C^{\prime\prime}\int_{N}^{\infty}t^{d}d\mu_{\delta}(t),

Combining the previous inequalities that hold for an infinite number of

which concludes that

.
∎

4
Sharp threshold

In this section, we prove a Talagrand formula for inhomogeneous weights. By a proper encoding of a Poisson point process, we prove a Talagrand formula for Boolean percolation. We apply this result to prove Proposition
2.2
.

4.1
Why do we need to sprinkle in
\delta
?

A sprinkling in
\lambda
is not sufficient to prove a sharp threshold in full generality. Let us illustrate this fact by a simple example. Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the event that there exists an open ball of radius at least
n^{d/(d+\delta)}
centered inside the ball
\mathrm{B}_{n}
. We have
for every
\lambda>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu_{\delta}}(\mathcal{F})=1-\exp(-\alpha_{d}\lambda),

where
\alpha_{d}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence, even if the event
\mathcal{F}
has a lot of symmetries (the event is invariant under rotations), there is no sharp threshold in
\lambda
. The big balls, that are present with a very small probability, play a dictator role in the sense that the presence of one ball is enough for the event to occur. In the event
\mathcal{E}_{n,N}(\rho)
(defined in Section
2
), the big balls may also play a dictator role. As for the event
\mathcal{F}
, we cannot hope to prove a sharp threshold in
\lambda
for the event
\mathcal{E}_{n,N}(\rho)
.

By slightly reducing
\delta
, the density of big balls increase faster compare to the density of small balls. Going back to the event
\mathcal{F}
, we have for
\varepsilon>0

\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu_{\delta-\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{F})=1-\exp(-\lambda n^{d\varepsilon/(d+\delta)}).

Hence, the event
\mathcal{F}
has a sharp threshold in
\delta
.

4.2
Sharp threshold for inhomogeneous percolation

In this section, we state general results on sharp threshold for inhomogeneous weights.
We will need the following result that is a Talagrand formula that holds for inhomogeneous Bernoulli percolation.
Let
N\geq 1
, and
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in[0,1]
, set

\mathrm{P}_{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}}:=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{Ber}(p_{i})

where
\mathrm{Ber}(p)
is the distribution on
\{0,1\}
of Bernoulli of parameter
p
.
For
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
define
\tau_{i}
the function on
\{0,1\}^{N}
that switches the value of the
i
-th bit.

Proposition 4.1

(Inhomogeneous Talagrand)
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that for any family
(p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}\in(0,1/2]^{N}
for any
f:\{0,1\}^{N}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
.
We have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}|\log p_{i}|\Inf_{i}(f)\geq C\Var(f)\log\left(\frac{1}{\max_{1\leq i\leq N}p_{i}\Inf_{i}(f)}\right)\,

where

\Inf_{i}(f):=\mathrm{P}_{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}}(f\circ\tau_{i}\neq f).

Remark 4.2
.

For simplicity, we prove a version of inhomogeneous Talagrand for
p
’s that are close to
0
. We did not try to optimize the proof to obtain a more symmetric version.

We were not able to find a proof of this inhomogeneous version of Talagrand formula in the literature. For completeness, we include a proof of Proposition
4.1
in the appendix.

4.3
Inhomogeneous Talagrand for Boolean percolation

In this section, we prove a Talagrand formula for Boolean percolation.
For
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
n\geq 1
, define

\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}:=\left(x+\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{d}\right)\times[n,n+1)\,.

Let
\delta>0
and
\lambda>0
. For short, we will write
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}
for
\mathbb{P}_{\lambda,\mu_{\delta}}
. The parameter
\lambda
will remain fixed in all this section.

For
\mathcal{E}
a finite increasing event, denote

\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x}(\mathcal{E}):=\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\notin\mathcal{E},\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})dzd\mu_{\delta}(r).

Set
\Omega
the set of countable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We define the following distance between points in
\Omega
: for any
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega

\mathrm{d}(\eta,\eta^{\prime}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}+\infty&\mbox{ if $|\eta|\neq|\eta^{\prime}|$}\\
\inf_{\pi:\eta\mapsto\eta^{\prime}\text{ bijection}}\sup\{\|x-x^{\prime}\|+|r-r^{\prime}|:(x^{\prime},r^{\prime})=\pi((x,r))\}&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

where we use the convention that
|\eta|=|\eta^{\prime}|
if both sets are infinite.
We will denote by
\partial\mathcal{E}
the boundary of
\mathcal{E}
for the topology associated with the distance
\mathrm{d}
in the space
\Omega
. For
\eta\in\Omega
and
N\geq 1
, we define the neighborhood
\mathcal{N}_{N}(\eta,\varepsilon)
at distance
\varepsilon
restricted to
\mathrm{B}_{N}

\mathcal{N}_{N}(\eta,\varepsilon):=\left\{\eta^{\prime}\in\Omega:\,\mathrm{d}(\eta\cap(\mathrm{B}_{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}),\eta^{\prime}\cap(\mathrm{B}_{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}))\leq\varepsilon\right\}

Let
N
be such that the event
\mathcal{E}
only depends on balls centered in
\mathrm{B}_{N}
.
We will need the following notion of approximate pivotality at level
\varepsilon>0

\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E}):=\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\exists\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}(\eta,\varepsilon):\eta^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{E},\eta^{\prime}\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})dzd\mu_{\delta}(r).

The following proposition is a Talagrand formula for increasing event in Boolean percolation.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
\delta_{0},\delta_{1}>0
. There exists
C_{0}>0
such that the following holds. For any
\delta\in(\delta_{0},\delta_{1})
, For any finite increasing event
\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0
, we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},n\geq 1}\log n\,\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x}(\mathcal{E})\geq C_{0}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})(1-\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E}))\log\left(\frac{1}{\max_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},n\geq 1}\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x}(\mathcal{E})}\right).

Remark 4.4
.

This proposition holds for increasing events for the two following partial orders. An event
\mathcal{E}
is coloring increasing if for any
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
for any
\eta^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{O}(\eta)\subset\mathcal{O}(\eta^{\prime})
then
\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}
.

We also define the partial order
\prec
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
as follows. We say that
\eta\preceq\eta^{\prime}
if for any
(x,r)\in\eta
there exists
(y,r^{\prime})\in\eta^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{B}_{r}^{x}\subset\mathrm{B}_{r^{\prime}}^{y}
.
We say that an event
\mathcal{E}
is increasing for
\prec
if
\eta\in\mathcal{E}
and
\eta\preceq\eta^{\prime}
then
\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Remark 4.5
.

We believe that the hypothesis
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0
holds for any increasing event. Note that we will only work on connection events. For connection events, if
\eta\in\partial\mathcal{E}
then there exists
(z,r),(z^{\prime},r^{\prime})\in\eta
such that
\|z-z^{\prime}\|=r+r^{\prime}
. It follows that
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0
.

Remark 4.6
.

At a first look, it may seem that the formula in Proposition
4.3
involves an underlying discretization of the space. We would like to emphasize that the pivotal quantities
\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x}(\mathcal{E})
are defined in terms of the continuous model itself and not a discretized version of it. One can check that the formula is equivalent to the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

\int_{(z,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}I_{z,r}(\mathcal{E})\log r\,dzd\mu(r)\geq C\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})(1-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}))\log\left(\frac{1}{\sup_{(z,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}I_{z,r}(\mathcal{E})}\right)

where
I_{z,r}(\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{P}(\eta\notin\mathcal{E},\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})
.

We will need the following lemma to prove Proposition
4.3
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a finite increasing event such that for any
\lambda,\delta>0
,
\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0
. We have for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
n\geq 1

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x}(\mathcal{E}).

Proof.

Let
N
be such that
\mathcal{E}
only depends on points centered in
\mathrm{B}_{N}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}&(\exists\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}(\eta,\varepsilon):\eta^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{E},\eta^{\prime}\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{N}(\partial\mathcal{E},\varepsilon))+\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}(\partial\mathcal{E},\varepsilon))+\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\notin\mathcal{E},\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E}).\end{split}

It follows that

\begin{split}\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x,\varepsilon}(\mathcal{E})\leq&\mathrm{Piv}_{n}^{x}(\mathcal{E})+\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{N}(\partial\mathcal{E},\varepsilon))+\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}(\partial\mathcal{E},\varepsilon))dzd\mu_{\delta}(r).\end{split}

We have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{N}_{N}(\partial\mathcal{E},\varepsilon))=\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0.

Similarly, by dominated convergence

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{N}_{N}(\partial\mathcal{E},\varepsilon))dzd\mu_{\delta}(r)=\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\partial\mathcal{E})dzd\mu_{\delta}(r).

Using Proposition 2.1 in
[
13
]

\begin{split}\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\partial\mathcal{E})dzd\mu_{\delta}(r)&\leq\int_{(z,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\partial\mathcal{E},\eta\notin\mathcal{E})dzd\mu_{\delta}(r)\\
&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0\end{split}

The result follows.

Denote by

F(t):=\int_{1}^{t}\frac{d+\delta}{r^{d+1+\delta}}dr\,.

For

\forall t\in[0,1]\qquad F_{n}^{-1}(t):=\inf\left\{s\geq n:\frac{F(s)-F(n)}{F(n+1)-F(n)}\geq t\right\}\,.

Claim 4.8
.

There exists a constant

and

,

.

Fix
\lambda

\delta_{0}<\delta_{1}
.
Set

g(n):=\frac{F(n+1)-F(n)}{d+\delta}\,

and

where
\mathcal{P}(t)
is a Poisson random variable of parameter

.

Encoding the Poisson point process.

For each
\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}
, we associate a Bernoulli random variable
\alpha_{0}
of parameter

. This random variable will encode the presence of at least one point in the cell
\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}
.
For each
(\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x},k)
with

, we associate a Bernoulli random variable
\alpha_{k}
of parameter
p(k,\lambda g(n))
(that will encode the presence of a

\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}

(\beta_{i})_{i\geq 1}

and an i.i.d. family
(\gamma_{i}^{(1)},\dots,\gamma_{i}^{(d)})_{i\geq 1}
of Bernoulli random variables of parameter

(that will encode respectively the radius and the position of the

-th ball of the cell). Write
\mathbb{P}
for the joint law of these families of Bernoulli random variable.
Finally, define for
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}

z_{k}^{m}(\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x})=z_{k}^{m}:=x+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\gamma_{i}^{(1)}}{2^{i}},\dots,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\gamma_{i}^{(d)}}{2^{i}}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2},\dots,\frac{1}{2}\right)

and

When

, we write

and

instead of
z_{k}^{+\infty}
and
r_{k}^{+\infty}
.
It is easy to check that

Denote by

\mathbb{P}(|\eta_{\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}|=k)=\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha_{k}=0,\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}=1\right)=(1-p(k,\lambda g(n)))\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}p(i,\lambda g(n))=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{P}(\lambda g(n))=k).

\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}

is a Poisson point process of intensity

. In particular, we have

\mathbb{P}(\eta\in\mathcal{E})=\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E}).

(4.1)

Approximation of the Poisson point process using a finite encoding.

\mathcal{E}

It is easy to check that

\lim_{K\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_{K})=0\,.

Write
\overline{z}_{k}
and
\overline{r}_{k}
for

.
We now define

\overline{\eta}_{\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}:=\left\{(\overline{z}_{k},\overline{r}_{k}):\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}=1,\,k\leq K\right\}

\overline{\eta}:=\bigcup_{(x,n)\in(\mathrm{B}_{N}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d})\times[0,KN]}\overline{\eta}_{\mathrm{S}_{n}^{x}}.

Note that on the event

4.4
Sharp threshold for Boolean percolation

In this section, we prove a more general version of Proposition
2.2
using the result of the previous section.
We say that
\mathcal{E}
is rotational invariant if for any rotation
\rho
of center
0
, we have

\rho(\mathcal{E}):=\{\{(\rho(x),r):(x,r)\in\eta\}:\eta\in E\}=\mathcal{E}.

The following proposition proves a sharp threshold for finite rotational invariant events not depending on the balls centered in
\mathrm{B}_{n}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\lambda,\delta>0
. There exists
\kappa>0
such that the following holds.
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta/2\kappa)
,
n\geq\kappa
and

\mathcal{E}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
finite increasing event that does not depend on balls centered in
\mathrm{B}_{n}
, such that
\mathcal{E}
is rotational invariant and
\mathbb{P}_{\delta_{0}}(\partial\mathcal{E})=0
for any
\delta_{0}>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{{\delta}}(\mathcal{E})\geq\frac{1}{n^{\varepsilon}}\implies\mathbb{P}_{{\delta-\kappa\varepsilon}}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1-\frac{1}{n^{\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda,\delta_{1}>0
. In this proof
\lambda
is omitted from the notations as it remains fixed.
Set
\delta_{0}=\delta_{1}/2
. In what follows, we will work with
\delta\in[\delta_{0},\delta_{1}]
. Let
\kappa
that will be defined later. Let
n\geq\kappa
and
\mathcal{E}
as in the statement of the proposition.

Let us first compute the derivative of
\delta\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})
.
We have

\begin{split}\mu_{\delta-t}=\frac{1}{r^{d+1+\delta-t}}\mathbf{1}_{r\geq 1}dr=r^{t}\mu_{\delta}&=\left(1+\exp(t\log r)-1\right)\mu_{\delta}\\
&=\left(1+t\log r+tF_{r}(t)\right)\mu_{\delta}\end{split}

where
F_{r}(t):=(\exp(t\log r)-1)/t-\log r
.
We have

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\sup_{r\in[0,N]}|F_{r}(t)|=0\,.

We can apply Theorem 6.6 of
[
13
]
to compute the derivative of
\delta\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})
, we get

\frac{\partial}{\partial\delta}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})=-\lambda\int_{(z,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\notin\mathcal{E},\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})\log r\,dzd\mu_{\delta}(r).

We now distinguish two cases.

First case

We assume that there exists
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
m\geq 1
such that
\mathrm{Piv}_{m}^{x}(\mathcal{E})\geq 1/\sqrt{n}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
m\geq 2
since
\mathrm{Piv}_{2}^{x}(\mathcal{E})\geq c\mathrm{Piv}_{1}^{x}(\mathcal{E})
for some constant
c
depending on
\delta_{1}
. Since
\mathcal{E}
does not depend on balls centered in
\mathrm{B}_{n}
, we have
x\notin\mathrm{B}_{n-d}
.
It is easy to check that there exist
c_{d}>0
depending only on
d
,
\mathrm{S}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{S}_{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
K\geq c_{d}n^{d-1}
such that all the
\mathrm{S}_{i}
are disjoint and for every
i\in\{1,\dots,K\}
, there exists a rotation sending
x+[-1/2,1/2)^{d}
on
\mathrm{S}_{i}
.
By rotation invariance, it follows that

\int_{(z,r)\in\mathrm{S}_{i}\times[m,m+1)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\notin\mathcal{E},\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})dzd\mu_{\delta}(r)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

and

-\frac{\partial}{\partial\delta}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E})=\lambda\int_{(z,r)}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\eta\notin\mathcal{E},\eta\cup\{(z,r)\}\in\mathcal{E})\log r\,dzd\mu_{\delta}(r)\geq c_{d}\lambda\log d\,n^{d-3/2}\,.

Second case

We assume that for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
m\geq d

\mathrm{Piv}_{m}^{x}(\mathcal{E})\leq 1/\sqrt{n}
.
By Proposition
4.3

(depending on

(4.5)

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},k\geq 1}\log k\,\mathrm{Piv}_{k}^{x}(\mathcal{E})\leq-\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\delta}\mathbb{P}_{\delta}(\mathcal{E}).

Hence, for

Appendix A
Inhomogeneous Talagrand formula

In this section, we prove the inhomogeneous Talagrand formula (Proposition
4.1
),
We will use the original Talagrand formula.

Theorem A.1

(Talagrand formula
[
18
]
)
.

There exists a universal constant
C>0
such that for any
p\in(0,1)
for any function
f:\{0,1\}^{N}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
.
We have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Inf_{i}(f)\geq\frac{C}{\log(\frac{2}{p(1-p)})}\Var(f)\log\left(\frac{1}{\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\Inf_{i}(f)}\right)\,

where

\Inf_{i}(f):=\mathrm{P}_{p}^{\otimes n}(f\circ\tau_{i}\neq f).

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Fix
N\geq 1
. Fix
f:\{0,1\}^{N}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
a boolean function. It is easy to check that the function
\Inf_{i}(f)
is a polynomial function of the family
(p_{1},\dots,p_{N})
. As a result, the map

(p_{1},\dots,p_{N})\mapsto(\Inf_{1}^{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}}(f),\dots,\Inf_{N}^{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}}(f))
is continuous.
Hence, it is enough to check that the inequality holds only for
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}
that belong to the set

E:=\{m2^{-\ell}:\ell\geq 1,\,m\leq 2^{\ell}-1\}

which is dense in
[0,1]
.

From now on, we will assume that
p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in E
and write
p_{i}=m_{i}2^{-\ell_{i}}
for all
1\leq i\leq N
.
Let
1\leq i\leq N
. We set

\mathrm{P}:=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{P}_{1/2}^{\otimes\ell_{i}}.

Let
(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})
be
\ell_{i}
independent Bernoulli of parameter
1/2
.
Let us define

\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}}):=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell_{i}}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}.

It is easy to check that
\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})
is uniform on
\{k2^{-\ell_{i}}:0\leq k\leq 2^{\ell_{i}}-1\}
and

\mathrm{P}(\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\geq 1-m_{i}2^{-\ell_{i}})=m_{i}2^{-\ell_{i}}.

Set
Y_{i}:=\mathbf{1}_{\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\geq 1-p_{i}}
.
It follows that

(Y_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
has the distribution of
\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{Ber}(p_{i})
.
Set

\widetilde{f}(X_{1,1},\dots,X_{1,\ell_{1}},\dots,X_{N,1},\dots,X_{N,\ell_{N}}):=f(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{N}).

It is easy to check that
\Var(f)=\Var(\widetilde{f})
.
Let us now compute the influence of
X_{i,j}
on
Y_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}
.
We define
\sigma_{j}^{1}
(respectively
\sigma_{j}^{0}
the function that for
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell_{i}})\in\{0,1\}^{\ell_{i}}
changes
x_{j}
into
1
(respectively into
0
).
Set

\Delta_{j}\pi=\pi\circ\sigma_{j}^{1}-\pi\circ\sigma_{j}^{0}.

We have

\Inf_{i,j}(\widetilde{f})=\mathrm{P}(\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0)\Inf_{i}(f).

Let us now compute
\mathrm{P}(\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0)
.
We have

\left\{\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0\right\}=\left\{\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{\ell_{i}}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\leq 1-p_{i},\,\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{\ell_{i}}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}+\frac{1}{2^{j}}\geq 1-p_{i}\right\}.

Let
j_{i}\geq 1
be the largest integer such that

2^{-j_{i}}\geq p_{i}.

We distinguish two cases.
Let us first assume that
j\geq j_{i}
.

\{\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0\}\subset\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\leq 1-p_{i},\,\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\geq 1-p_{i}-\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}\right\}

It follows that

\mathrm{P}(\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0)\leq\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}.

This rough upper-bound also holds for
j<j_{i}
, but we aim here at obtaining a better upper-bound in that case.
Let us now assume that
j<j_{i}
. We have

\{\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0\}=\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\leq 1-p_{i},\,\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\geq 1-p_{i}-\frac{1}{2^{j}}-\frac{1}{2^{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell_{i}-j}\frac{X_{i,k+j}}{2^{k}}\right\}.

(A.1)

Note that we have almost surely

\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}.

Hence, for the event on the right hand side of (
A.1
) to be non-empty, we need that

1-p_{i}-\frac{1}{2^{j}}-\frac{1}{2^{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell_{i}-j}\frac{X_{i,k+j}}{2^{k}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}.

That is

\sum_{k=1}^{\ell_{i}-j}\frac{X_{i,k+j}}{2^{k}}\geq 1-2^{j}p_{i}.

It follows that

\begin{split}\mathrm{P}&(\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0)\\
&\qquad\leq\mathrm{P}\left(1-p_{i}-\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{X_{i,k}}{2^{k}}\leq 1-p_{i}\right)\mathrm{P}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell_{i}-j}\frac{X_{i,k+j}}{2^{k}}\geq 1-2^{j}p_{i}\right)\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}2^{j}p_{i}\leq 2p_{i}.\end{split}

Finally, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\mathrm{P}(\Delta_{j}\pi(X_{i,1},\dots,X_{i,\ell_{i}})\neq 0)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{j_{i}-1}2p_{i}+\sum_{j=j_{i}}^{\ell_{i}}\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}\leq 4p_{i}|\log p_{i}|

and

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\Inf_{i,j}(\widetilde{f})\leq 4p_{i}|\log p_{i}|\Inf_{i}(f).

Let us now apply Talagrand formula (Theorem
A.1
) to
\widetilde{f}
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\Inf_{i,j}(\widetilde{f})\geq C\Var(\widetilde{f})\log\left(\frac{1}{\max\{\Inf_{i,j}(\widetilde{f}):1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}\}}\right)

and

\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}|\log p_{i}|\Inf_{i}(f)\geq C\Var(f)\log\left(\frac{1}{\max\{p_{i}\Inf_{i}(f):1\leq i\leq N\}}\right).

The result follows.

∎

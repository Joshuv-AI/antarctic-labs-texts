Twistor approach to higher-spin theories and matrix model
*

Abstract
We discuss recent endeavours in connecting twistor theory to higher-spin theories and the IKKT-matrix model. Starting with a brief review on higher-spin algebra 𝔥 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{hs} in four-dimensional target space, we elucidate how higher-spin symmetry can be encoded in 𝔥 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{hs} -valued sections/holomorphic differential forms on (non-commutative) twistor space. This provides an efficient way to construct local higher-spin theories in spacetime from some actions on (non-commutative) twistor space. Remarkably, some higher-spin theories obtained within the framework of twistor theory can have non-trivial scattering amplitudes in flat space.

Twistor space.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}=I^{AB}\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial\mathcal{Z}^{A}}\wedge\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial\mathcal{Z}^{B}}=\Lambda\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\wedge\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial\lambda_{\alpha}}+\frac{\overleftarrow{\partial}}{\partial\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}}\wedge\frac{\overrightarrow{\partial}}{\partial\mu_{\dot{\alpha}}}\,.

A nice feature of the above Poisson structure is that it can act naturally on holomorphic objects on curved twistor space. Furthermore, it also induces the following star-product on
\mathcal{PT}

\displaystyle f\star g

where

is some natural length scale that plays the role of a deformation parameter. Note that the expansion of the

. This allows us to obtain the

\displaystyle L^{\alpha\beta}=\lambda^{\alpha}\lambda^{\beta}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003ptP^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\lambda^{\alpha}\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003ptL^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}=\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}\mu^{\dot{\beta}}\,,

\displaystyle[L^{\alpha\alpha},L^{\beta\beta}]_{\star}

(34a)

(34b)

\displaystyle[P^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},P^{\beta\dot{\beta}}]_{\star}

\displaystyle=\Lambda\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}L^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}+\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}L^{\alpha\beta}\,.

(34c)

Intriguingly, in the flat limit, only ‘half’ of the above relations remain. In addition, the above realization of

The twistor action for chiral HSGRA.

Since we are working with
\mathcal{PT}
, everything must have appropriate projective scalings. Therefore, it is useful to define the following Euler operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{Z}^{A}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{Z}^{A}}\,,

(35)

to keep track of the homogeneity in
\mathcal{Z}
for any twistor expression.

It is well-known that the canonical bundle of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is
\mathcal{O}(-4)
. This fact motivates us to choose the following
SU(4)
-invariant measure

\displaystyle D^{3}\mathcal{Z}=\epsilon_{ABCD}\mathcal{Z}^{A}d\mathcal{Z}^{B}\wedge d\mathcal{Z}^{C}\wedge d\mathcal{Z}^{D}=\langle\lambda d\lambda\rangle\wedge[d\mu\wedge d\mu]\,

(36)

as the canonical measure on
\mathcal{PT}
where
\mathcal{E}(D^{3}\mathcal{Z})=4
. The twistor action for chiral HSGRA in (anti)de-Sitter space which admits a smooth flat limit is
[

\displaystyle\begin{split}S[\mathbb{A}]&=S_{hCS}+S_{c}=\int D^{3}\mathcal{Z}\,\text{Tr}\big[\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{A}_{-h}\star\bar{\partial}\mathbb{A}_{h}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{A}_{h_{1}}\star\mathbb{A}_{h_{2}}\star\mathbb{A}_{h_{3}}\big]+S_{c}\,,\end{split}

(37)

where the twistor field
\mathbb{A}_{h}\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathcal{PT},\text{End}(E)\otimes\mathcal{O}(2h-2))
corresponds to a matrix-valued higher-spin fields of helicity
h
in spacetime. Furthermore, we must have at least one positive-helicity field in (
37
) so that the constraint
\mathcal{E}\,(L[\mathbb{A}])=-4
can be fulfilled. Here,
L[\mathbb{A}]
is the Lagrangian associated with the action (
37
). Note that the measure (

) is not gauge-invariant under the higher-spin diffeomorphism:

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{Z}^{A}=\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\mathcal{Z}^{A},\xi_{h}\}=\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}(\Lambda\partial^{\alpha}\xi_{h}+\partial^{\dot{\alpha}}\xi_{h})\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\xi_{h}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(2h-2))\,.

(38)

For this reason, a correction denoted as
S_{c}
has to be added to the above action (c.f. (
37

Scattering amplitudes.

Remarkably, by doing integration by parts, the number of the star-products in each term of the action

in terms of the on-shell four-momentum

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}(az-b)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}d\bar{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}\Big(\frac{1}{az-b}\Big)\,,

(0,1)
-form holomorphic delta function

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}_{m}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\Big[\frac{\langle\xi\lambda\rangle}{\langle\xi\lambda^{\prime}\rangle}\Big]^{m}\bar{\delta}(\langle\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\rangle)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{dt}{t^{m}}\bar{\delta}^{2}(t\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})

for

(43)

The three-point amplitudes read

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{3}^{\Lambda}(h_{1},h_{2},h_{3})=[12]^{h_{1}+h_{2}-h_{3}}[23]^{h_{2}+h_{3}-h_{1}}[31]^{h_{3}+h_{1}-h_{2}}\frac{\big[\ell_{p}(1-\Lambda\Box_{P})\big]^{h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}-1}}{\Gamma[h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}]}\delta^{4}(P)\,.\end{split}

Note that in our construction, the coupling constant

Reduction to spacetime.

While obtaining the scattering amplitudes from twistor space is a reasonably simple task, integrating out fibre coordinates is quite complicated. The reason is that we have to take into account what is so-called “frame-dragging” effects on
\mathbb{PS}
since the pullback
\pi_{1}^{*}:\mathcal{PT}\rightarrow\mathbb{PS}
is not holomorphic. To illustrate how this subtle effect affects the process of integrating out fibre coordinates, it is enough for us to consider a
\mathcal{PT}
associated with a flat target space. To begin, let us define the following
(1,0)
-vector fields and their dual
(1,0)
-forms:

\displaystyle(1,0)\text{-vectors}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\hat{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\hat{\lambda}\rangle}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{\alpha}}\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}}{\langle\lambda\hat{\lambda}\rangle}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(46a)

\displaystyle(1,0)\text{-forms}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle e^{0}

\displaystyle:=\langle\lambda d\lambda\rangle\,,

\displaystyle e^{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle:=\lambda_{\alpha}dx^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(46b)

where
\partial_{0}
and
\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}
are elements of
\Gamma(T^{1,0}\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(-2))
and
\Gamma(T^{1,0}\mathbb{PT},\mathcal{O}(-1))
, respectively. Using (
15
) and (
46

\displaystyle[\bar{\partial}_{0},\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}]=\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt[\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}},\partial_{0}]=\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(47)

Note that when
\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu^{\dot{\alpha}}}
acts on functions, it is sufficient to use the definition of
\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}
in (
46a
) when we pull it back from
\mathcal{PT}
to
\mathbb{PS}
. However, when the
\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}
-vector field acts on differential forms, we must think of this operation as a Lie derivative generated by a flow along
\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}
. This fact motivates us to introduce the following Poisson structure
[
83
]
:

\displaystyle\omega\,\Pi\,\eta:=\{\omega,\eta\}_{h}=\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}}\omega\wedge\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\beta}}}\eta\,,

(48)

that acts any
(p,q)
-forms
\omega,\eta
on
\mathbb{PS}
to work with higher-spin extension of Penrose transform. The holomorphic Poisson structure (
48
) naturally induces the following star-product

\displaystyle\omega\ast\eta:=\omega\,e^{\ell_{p}\Pi}\wedge\eta=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ell_{p}^{k}}{k!}\omega\,\Pi^{k}\,\eta\,,

(49)

where
\omega
and
\eta
are differential forms.

Instead of working directly with
\mathbb{PS}
, it will be more convenient to work with the non-projective undotted spinor bundle
\mathbb{S}
since we can skip the step of trivializing line bundles in different patches of
\mathbb{PS}
, i.e. we do not need to find a holomorphic frame
\mathrm{e}
where

\displaystyle\bar{\mathrm{D}}\big|_{L_{x}}\mathrm{e}=0\,.

(50)

The tradeoff is that we need to project all functions/forms from
\mathbb{S}
to
\mathbb{PS}
appropriately with the help of the following Euler operator

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}=\lambda^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}\,

(51)

on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
to pick out the correct projective scaling. Any
\omega\in\Omega^{p,q}(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{O}(n))
is demanded to satisfy

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\omega=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{L}_{\hat{\Sigma}}\omega=n\,\omega\,.

(52)

Here, the notation
\hat{\Sigma}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\equiv\iota_{\hat{\Sigma}}
is the interior product wrt. to the
\hat{\Sigma}
vector field. Furthermore,
L_{x}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\sim S^{2}
is endowed with a hermitian Fubini-Study metric of the form

\displaystyle\mathtt{K}:=e^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{0}=\frac{\langle\lambda d\lambda\rangle\wedge\langle\hat{\lambda}d\hat{\lambda}\rangle}{\langle\lambda\hat{\lambda}\rangle^{2}}\,.

(53)

The usual exterior derivative on
\mathbb{PS}
, denoted as
d_{\mathbb{PS}}\equiv\eth
, gets lifted to a unique Chern connection on the bundle
\mathcal{O}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
where
[
84
,
85
]

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{PS}}\equiv\eth:=d_{\mathbb{S}}+n\frac{\langle\hat{\lambda}d\lambda\rangle}{\langle\lambda\hat{\lambda}\rangle}\wedge\,.

(54)

Here,

\displaystyle d_{\mathbb{S}}:=e^{0}\partial_{0}+\bar{e}^{0}\bar{\partial}_{0}+dx^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}=d\lambda^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\alpha}}+d\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\hat{\lambda}^{\alpha}}+dx^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}

(55)

is the exterior derivative on the unprojective spinor bundle
\mathbb{S}
. The Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}}
acting on
\mathcal{O}(n)
-valued
(p,q)
-forms can be defined via the Cartan’s magic formula by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}}\omega=\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\eth\omega+\eth(\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\omega)\,.

(56)

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\eth e^{0}=\eth\bar{e}^{0}=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\eth e^{\dot{\alpha}}=e^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\eth\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}=e^{\dot{\alpha}}\wedge\bar{e}^{0}\,.

(57)

Furthermore, since
\eth:=\partial+\bar{\partial}
, we find

\displaystyle\partial\bar{e}^{0}=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\partial\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}=e^{\dot{\alpha}}\wedge\bar{e}^{0}\,.

(58)

This is what is so-called the “frame-dragging” effect that occurs on twistor space whenever we deform twistor space with a potential that contains derivatives along the fibres
\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(1)
of the fibration
\pi:\mathcal{PT}\rightarrow L_{x}
. Let us now focus on holomorphic form on
\mathbb{PS}
where we can decompose
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{0}\bar{e}^{0}+\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}
. A simple computation provides

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle=(\bar{\partial}_{0}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}-\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{0})\bar{e}^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}+\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}\,,

(59a)

\displaystyle\partial\mathbb{A}

\displaystyle=\partial_{0}\mathbb{A}_{0}e^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{0}+\partial_{0}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}e^{0}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}-(\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{0}+\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}})\bar{e}^{0}\wedge e^{\dot{\alpha}}+\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}e^{\dot{\alpha}}\wedge\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}\,,

(59b)

which can be used to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbb{A}=(\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{0}+\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}})\bar{e}^{0}+\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}\,,

(60)

where
\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\mathbb{A}=0
; and vector fields ‘eat’ differential forms according to the following menu:

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}e^{0}=1\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{\partial}_{0}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\bar{e}^{0}=1\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}e^{\dot{\beta}}=\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbin{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lnot$}}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}=\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}\,.

(61)

Due to the holomorphicity of
\mathbb{A}
, we can show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}}...\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}}\mathbb{A}=(\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{0}+k\partial_{(\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k-1}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}_{k})})\bar{e}^{0}+\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}\,.

(62)

We will choose the following gauge condition
[
85
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbb{A}^{\dot{\alpha}}=\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}^{\dot{\alpha}}=0\,,

(63)

to reduce the number of
\ast
-product by one. Indeed, one can show that
\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathcal{L}_{\partial^{\dot{\alpha}}}\mathbb{A}=0
if (
63
) holds. Once again, we have only one derivative, i.e.
\bar{\partial}
, in the kinetic term. Pulling (

\mathbb{PS}

\displaystyle S[\mathbb{A}]=\int_{\mathbb{PS}}\text{Tr}\Big[\sum_{h\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{A}_{-h}\bar{\partial}\mathbb{A}_{h}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{h_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{A}_{h_{1}}\mathbb{A}_{h_{2}}\ast\mathbb{A}_{h_{3}}\Big]\,.

(64)

With a little more effort, we end up at

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}}...\mathcal{L}_{\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}}\mathbb{A}\wedge\mathcal{L}_{\partial^{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}}...\mathcal{L}_{\partial^{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}}\mathbb{A}\\
&=(\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{0}+k\partial_{(\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k-1}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}_{k})})\partial^{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial^{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}\bar{e}^{0}\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}+\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}\partial^{\dot{\alpha}_{1}}...\partial^{\dot{\alpha}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\gamma}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\beta}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\gamma}}\,.\end{split}

(65)

Observe that Woodhouse gauge (fixing
\mathbb{A}_{0}\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(n))=0
for

[

) is no longer a suitable gauge choice since there is an in-homogeneous contribution to the

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{A}\ast\mathbb{A}=0\,.

(66)

In terms of components, the above decomposes into two sub-equations:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}_{0}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}+(\partial_{\dot{\beta}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\beta}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{0}+k\partial_{(\dot{\beta}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\beta}_{k-1}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}_{k})})\partial^{\dot{\beta}_{1}}...\partial^{\dot{\beta}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}+\partial_{\dot{\gamma}_{1}}...\partial_{\dot{\gamma}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\partial^{\dot{\gamma}_{1}}...\partial^{\dot{\gamma}_{k}}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\beta}}\,.

It can be shown that if we assume

to have the usual Woodhouse representative for positive-helicity higher-spin fields, then the solution of (

\displaystyle\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathbb{A}_{0}=-\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(68)

This implies that

. As a result, we have the following equations

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}_{0}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}=0\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\bar{\partial}_{0}\mathbb{A}_{0}=0\,

(69)

that address the holomorphicity of

when

. If we consider the dotted component of

with the usual Woodhouse representative

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{A}_{h,\dot{\alpha}}\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}=\lambda^{\alpha(2h-1)}A_{\alpha(2h-1),\dot{\alpha}}\,\bar{e}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\end{split}

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{h,0}^{+}\bar{e}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bar{\partial}^{\dot{\alpha}}}{\Box}\mathbb{A}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{e}^{0}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pth>0\,,

where we used the convention

etc. to shorten our expressions. As always, the scalar field is the most problematic piece to be inserted into the spectrum of HSGRAs, given that it has to respect higher-spin symmetry. Let us consider the following twistor field

and

3
HS-IKKT from fuzzy twistor space

There is a fundamental difference between the fuzzy twistor construction, and the usual twistor construction. Namely, the primary objects of the former are functions, while they are holomorphic forms in the latter.

Generating functions of dual K K -theoretic P P - and Q Q -functions and boson-fermion correspondence
Shinsuke Iwao

Abstract
. We present a new algebraic description of the dual K K -theoretic P P -function g ​ q λ gq_{\lambda} and the dual K K -theoretic Q Q -function g ​ p λ gp_{\lambda} . Using a “ β \beta -deformed version” of the boson-fermion correspondence, these functions are realized as the bosonic counterparts to certain bases of the neutral fermion Fock space. This result generalizes Date-Jimbo-Miwa’s neutral-fermionic presentation of the Schur P P -function and the Q Q -function, developed in their study of the BKP-equation. As a corollary, we derive a generating function of g ​ q λ gq_{\lambda} , which was previously conjectured by Nakagawa-Naruse. Additionally, we provide a generating function of g ​ p λ gp_{\lambda} .

2.
Preliminaries: Neutral fermion Fock space

This section provides a brief review of the neutral fermion Fock space.
For readers who are interested in this topic, we recommend Baker’s paper
[
1
]
and Jimbo-Miwa’s paper
[
11
]
.
Let
[A,B]=AB-BA
be the commutator and
[A,B]_{+}=AB+BA
be the anti-commutator.

2.1.
Fock space

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-algebra of
neutral fermions
generated by
\{\phi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}
satisfying the anti-commutation relation

[\phi_{m},\phi_{n}]_{+}=2(-1)^{m}\delta_{m+n,0}
.
In particular, we have
\phi_{0}^{2}=1
and
\phi_{n}^{2}=0
for
n\neq 0
.

Let
|0\rangle
and
\langle 0|
denote the
vacuum vectors
:

\phi_{-n}|0\rangle=0,\quad\langle 0|\phi_{n}=0,\qquad(n>0).

The
Fock space

\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{A}\cdot|0\rangle
is the left
\mathcal{A}
-module generated by
|0\rangle
, and the
dual Fock space

\mathcal{F}^{\ast}:=\langle 0|\cdot\mathcal{A}
is the right
\mathcal{A}
-module generated by
\langle 0|
.
The
vacuum expectation value
is the unique bilinear form

\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\otimes_{\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)}\mathcal{F}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta);\qquad\langle u|\otimes|v\rangle\mapsto\left\langle u|v\right\rangle

that satisfies (i)
\left\langle 0|0\right\rangle=1
, (ii)
\langle 0|\phi_{0}|0\rangle=0
, and (iii)

(\langle u|\phi_{n})|v\rangle=\langle u|(\phi_{n}|v\rangle)
.
We often use the abbreviations

\langle u|X|v\rangle:=(\langle u|X)|v\rangle=\langle u|(X|v\rangle)
and

\langle X\rangle:=\langle 0|X|0\rangle
for any
X\in\mathcal{A}
.

The Fock space
\mathcal{F}
is split into two subspaces as
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{odd}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{even}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{odd}
(
resp.

\mathcal{F}_{even}
) is the subspace generated by all vectors obtained from
|0\rangle
by applying odd (
resp.
even) numbers of
\phi_{n}
(
n\geq 0
).
The dual Fock space is also split as
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}=\mathcal{F}^{\ast}_{odd}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\ast}_{even}
.
If

\langle u|\otimes|v\rangle
is in
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}_{even}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{odd}
or in
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}_{odd}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{even}
, the vacuum expectation value
\left\langle v|u\right\rangle
annihilates automatically.
By restriction, we obtain the nondegenerate bilinear form

(4)

\mathcal{F}_{even}^{\ast}\otimes_{\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)}\mathcal{F}_{even}\to\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta).

There exists an anti-algebra isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{A}
;
x\leftrightarrow x^{\ast}
defined by
\phi_{n}^{\ast}=(-1)^{n}\phi_{-n}
and
(xy)^{\ast}=y^{\ast}x^{\ast}
.
It induces the involution
\mathcal{F}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\ast};v\leftrightarrow v^{\ast}
with
|0\rangle^{\ast}=\langle 0|
.

2.2.
Wick’s theorem

For a
2r\times 2r
matrix
X
,
\mathrm{Pf}(X)
denotes the
Pfaffian

\mathrm{Pf}(X)=\hskip-10.00002pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma(1)<\sigma(3)<\cdots<\sigma(2r-1)\\
\sigma(1)<\sigma(2),\ \sigma(3)<\sigma(4),\dots,\sigma(2r-1)<\sigma(2r)\end{subarray}}\hskip-10.00002pt\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)a_{\sigma(1),\sigma(2)}a_{\sigma(3),\sigma(4)}\dots a_{\sigma(2r-1),\sigma(2r)}.

For
n_{1},\dots,n_{2r}\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have
Wick’s theorem

(5)

\langle\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}}\dots\phi_{n_{2r}}\rangle=\mathrm{Pf}\left(\langle\phi_{n_{i}}\phi_{n_{j}}\rangle\right)_{1\leq i<j\leq 2r}.

For a strict partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dots>\lambda_{r}>0)
of length
r
, we define
|\lambda\rangle\in\mathcal{F}_{even}
as

|\lambda\rangle=\begin{cases}\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\dots\phi_{\lambda_{r}}|0\rangle&(r:\mbox{even})\\
\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\dots\phi_{\lambda_{r}}\phi_{0}|0\rangle&(r:\mbox{odd})\end{cases}.

The orthogonality
\langle\mu|\lambda\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}
is derived directly from Wick’s theorem (
5
).

3.
\beta
-deformed operators

3.1.
\beta
-deformed fermion fields I

The
neutral fermion field

\phi(z)
is the formal series

\phi(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi_{n}z^{n}
, which determines a
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-linear map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}((z))
by left multiplication, and a
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-linear map
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{F}^{\ast}((z^{-1}))
by right multiplication.

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
\beta
-deformed fermion fields

\phi^{(\beta)}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi^{(\beta)}_{n}z^{n}
and
\phi^{[\beta]}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi^{[\beta]}_{n}z^{n}
by

\begin{gathered}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi^{(\beta)}_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(z+\tfrac{\beta}{2})^{n},\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi^{(\beta)}_{-n}z^{-n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{-n}\left(\frac{z^{-1}}{1+\frac{\beta}{2}z^{-1}}\right)^{n},\\
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi^{[\beta]}_{n}z^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\left(\frac{z}{1+\frac{\beta}{2}z}\right)^{n},\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi^{[\beta]}_{-n}z^{-n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{-n}(z^{-1}+\tfrac{\beta}{2})^{n}.\end{gathered}

\phi^{(\beta)}(z)
determines a
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-linear map
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{F}^{\ast}((z^{-1}))
by right multiplication, and
\phi^{[\beta]}(z)
determines a
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-linear map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}((z))
by left multiplication.

The following informal expressions are helpful in understanding what Definition
3.1
describes:

(6)

\phi^{(\beta)}(z)=\phi(z+\tfrac{\beta}{2}),\qquad\phi^{[\beta]}(z)=\phi(\tfrac{z}{1+\frac{\beta}{2}z}).

For a positive integer
n
,
\phi^{(\beta)}_{n}
and
\phi^{[\beta]}_{n}
are infinite
\frac{\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}[\beta]
-linear combinations of
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\dots
.
For example, we have
\phi^{(\beta)}_{1}=\phi_{1}+\beta\phi_{2}+\frac{3\beta^{2}}{4}\phi_{3}+\cdots
.
On the other hand,
\phi^{(\beta)}_{-n}
and
\phi^{[\beta]}_{-n}
are infinite
\frac{\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}{2}[\beta]
-linear combinations of
\phi_{-1},\phi_{-2},\phi_{-3},\dots

Hence, the
\beta
-deformed fermions satisfy the annihilation rule

(7)

\langle 0|\phi_{n}^{(\beta)}=\langle 0|\phi_{n}^{[\beta]}=0,\qquad\phi_{-n}^{(\beta)}|0\rangle=\phi_{-n}^{[\beta]}|0\rangle=0,\qquad(n>0).

The actions of the zero-th fermions
\phi_{0}^{(\beta)}
and
\phi_{0}^{[\beta]}
on the vacuum vectors are simple:

(8)

\langle 0|\phi_{0}^{(\beta)}=\langle 0|\phi_{0},\quad\langle 0|\phi_{0}\phi_{0}^{(\beta)}=\langle 0|,\quad\phi_{0}^{[\beta]}|0\rangle=\phi_{0}|0\rangle,\quad\phi_{0}^{[\beta]}\phi_{0}|0\rangle=|0\rangle.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
8
, §9.1]
)
.

We have the anti-commutation relation:

[(\phi^{(\beta)}_{m})^{\ast},\phi^{[\beta]}_{n}]_{+}=\begin{cases}2&(m=n)\\
\beta&(m=n-1)\\
0&(\mbox{otherwise})\end{cases}.

Proof.

This lemma is derived directly from
[(\phi_{m})^{\ast},\phi_{n}]_{+}=2\delta_{m,n}
and Definition
3.1
.
For details of the proof, see
[
8
, §9.1]
.
∎

3.2.
\beta
-deformed fermion fields II

We next define another two
\beta
-deformed fermion fields

\Phi^{(\beta)}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}\Phi_{n}^{(\beta)}z^{n}
and

\Phi^{[\beta]}(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}}\Phi_{n}^{[\beta]}z^{n}
.

Definition 3.3
.

We define the fields
\Phi^{(\beta)}(z)
and
\Phi^{[\beta]}(z)
by

\Phi^{(\beta)}(z):=\frac{1}{2+\beta z^{-1}}\phi^{(\beta)}(z),\qquad\Phi^{[\beta]}(z):=\frac{1}{2+\beta z}\phi^{[\beta]}(z).

\Phi^{(\beta)}(z)
determines a
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-linear map
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{F}^{\ast}((z^{-1}))
by right multiplication, and
\Phi^{[\beta]}(z)
determines a
\mathord{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta)
-linear map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}((z))
by left multiplication.

For any
n\in\mathord{\mathbb{Z}}
, these new
\beta
-deformed fermions can expand as

(9)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{(\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{n}^{(\beta)}-\frac{\beta}{4}\phi_{n+1}^{(\beta)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\phi_{n+2}^{(\beta)}-\cdots,

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{[\beta]}=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{n}^{[\beta]}-\frac{\beta}{4}\phi_{n-1}^{[\beta]}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\phi_{n-2}^{[\beta]}-\cdots.

From (
9
), we obtain the annihilation rule

(10)

\langle 0|\Phi_{n}^{(\beta)}=0,\quad\Phi_{-n}^{[\beta]}|0\rangle=0,\qquad(n>0).

However, this is not the case for
\langle 0|\Phi_{n}^{[\beta]}
and
\Phi_{-n}^{(\beta)}|0\rangle
.
In fact, using

\phi_{0}=\phi_{0}^{(\beta)}-\frac{\beta}{2}\phi_{1}^{(\beta)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\phi_{2}^{(\beta)}-\cdots=\phi_{0}^{[\beta]}-\frac{\beta}{2}\phi_{-1}^{[\beta]}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}\phi_{-2}^{[\beta]}-\cdots,

we can rewrite (
9
) as

(11)

\displaystyle\Phi_{-n}^{(\beta)}=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{-n}^{(\beta)}-\frac{\beta}{4}\phi_{-n+1}^{(\beta)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\phi_{-n+2}^{(\beta)}-\cdots+\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}\phi_{0},

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{[\beta]}=\frac{1}{2}\phi_{n}^{(\beta)}-\frac{\beta}{4}\phi_{n-1}^{(\beta)}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{8}\phi_{n-2}^{(\beta)}-\cdots+\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}\phi_{0}

for non-negative
n\geq 0
.
From (
7
), we obtain

(12)

\langle 0|\Phi_{n}^{[\beta]}=\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}\cdot\langle 0|\phi_{0}\neq 0,\qquad\Phi_{-n}^{(\beta)}|0\rangle=\frac{(-\beta)^{n}}{2^{n+1}}\cdot\phi_{0}|0\rangle\neq 0,

which implies that the operators
\Phi_{n}^{[\beta]},\Phi_{-n}^{(\beta)}
fail to annihilate the vacuum vectors.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have the following commutation relation:

[(\Phi^{(\beta)}_{m})^{\ast},\phi^{[\beta]}_{n}]_{+}=[(\Phi^{[\beta]}_{m})^{\ast},\phi^{(\beta)}_{n}]_{+}=\delta_{m,n}.

Proof.

It is obtained from Lemma
3.2
and (
9
) by straightforward calculations.
∎

For example, we have

,

,

,

Let

The

Deforming the currents as

(

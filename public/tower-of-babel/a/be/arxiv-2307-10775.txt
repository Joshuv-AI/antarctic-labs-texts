Perturbation bounds for the largest C C -eigenvalue of piezoelectric-type tensors
Xifu Liu, Dongdong Liu, Yaping Shi

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on the perturbation analysis of the largest C C -eigenvalue of the piezoelectric-type tensor which has concrete physical meaning which determines the highest piezoelectric coupling constant. Three perturbation bounds are presented, theoretical analysis and numerical examples show that the third perturbation bound has high accuracy when the norm of the perturbation tensor is small. AMS classification: 15A18, 15A69 Keywords: Perturbation bound; Piezoelectric-type tensor; C C -eigenvalue; Z Z -eigenvalue.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}
be the real field. For a positive integer
n
, we denote the set
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
by
[n]
. We call
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}})
a real tensor of
m
-th order
n
dimension, denoted by
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
(or
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times\cdots\times n}
), if
a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}\in\mathbb{R}
for all
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in[n]
. For any tensor
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
, if its entries
a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{m}}
are invariant under any permutation of its indices, then
\mathcal{A}
is called a symmetric tensor.

Among various concepts of tensor eigenvalues,
Z
-eigenvalue [
References
] is one of the most popular research topics in tensor field.
Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
. If there exist a complex number
\lambda
and a nonzero complex vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}
that are solutions of the following polynomial equations,

\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1}=\lambda\mathbf{x},\;\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,

where
\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1}
is a vector, and its
i
-th entry is given by

(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m-1})_{i}=\sum\limits_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{m}\in[n]}{a_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{m}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}},i\in[n],

(1.1)

then
\lambda
is called an
E
-eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathbf{x}
the
E
-eigenvector of
\mathcal{A}
associated with
\lambda
, and
(\lambda,\mathbf{x})
is called an
E
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{A}
.
We call
(\lambda,\mathbf{x})
a
Z
-eigenpair if both
\lambda
and
\mathbf{x}
are real. Moreover, we have the following results on
Z
-eigenvalue.

Lemma 1.1

([\ref{Qi}])
Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[m,n]}
be a symmetric tensor. Then
\mathcal{A}
always has
Z
-eigenvalues. In particular, we have

\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})=\max\{\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\;\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{A})=\min\{\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\;\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},

where
\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}^{m}=\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}\in[n]}{a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{m}}}
.

A great many methods have been proposed to compute
Z
-eigenpairs of symmetric tensors as yet. Qi et al. [
References
] developed a direct method to find all
Z
-eigenvalues of a third-order three dimension tensor. Kolda and Mayo [
References
] provided a shifted power method for computing all
Z
-eigenpairs, while Zeng and Ni [
References
] proposed a quasi-Newton method to compute them. Cui et al. [
References
] considered Jacobian SDP relaxations in polynomial optimization for computing all
Z
-eigenpairs. Chen et al. [
References
] proposed two homotopy continuation type algorithms to compute the
Z
-eigenpairs. Recently, dynamic method is also offered by Mo et al. [
References
] to reach this goal.

Apart from the
Z
-eigenvalue, the
C
-eigenvalue of the piezoelectric-type tensor has attract many researchers’ attention. Before going further, we recall the strict definitions of the above two terminologies in the following.

Definition 1.1

([\ref{chen}])
Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n}
be a third-order
n
dimensional real tensor. If the later two indices of
\mathcal{A}
are symmetric, i.e.,
a_{ijk}=a_{ikj}
for all
j,k\in[n]
, then
\mathcal{A}
is called a piezoelectric-type tensor.

Given a piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
, for each
i\in[n]
, its horizontal slice
\mathcal{A}(i,:,:)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denote the matrix whose
(j,k)
-entry is
a_{ijk}
. Then, it follows from Definition
1.1
that
\mathcal{A}(i,:,:)
is a symmetric matrix.

As we know, third order tensors have many important applications in some fields, such as physics and engineering [
References
-
References
]. As a special third-order tensor, piezoelectric-type tensors play a crucial role in piezoelectric effect and converse piezoelectric effect [
References
]. In order to investigate more properties related to piezoelectric effect and converse piezoelectric effect in solid crystal, the authors in [
References
] gave the definitions of
C
-eigenvalues and
C
-eigenvectors for piezoelectric-type tensors.

Definition 1.2

([\ref{chen}])
Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times n}
be a piezoelectric-type tensor. If there exist a scalar
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and two vectors
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying the following system

\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}=\lambda\mathbf{x},\quad\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}=\lambda\mathbf{y},\quad\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\quad\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1,

where
\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the
i
-th entry

(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}=\sum\limits_{j,k\in[n]}{a_{ijk}y_{j}y_{k}}\quad and\quad(\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y})_{i}=\sum\limits_{j,k\in[n]}{a_{jki}x_{j}y_{k}},

respectively, then
\lambda
is called a
C
-eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
are called associated left and right
C
-eigenvectors, respectively. Also,
(\lambda,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
can be called a
C
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{A}
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})^{T}
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})^{T}
are the left and right
C
-eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda
, then
\lambda=\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}\triangleq\sum\limits_{i,j,k\in[n]}{a_{ijk}x_{i}y_{j}y_{k}}
. Moreover,
(\lambda,\mathbf{x},-\mathbf{y})
,
(-\lambda,-\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
,
(-\lambda,-\mathbf{x},-\mathbf{y})
are also
C
-eigenpairs of
\mathcal{A}
. For a given piezoelectric type tensor
\mathcal{A}
, let
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})
(or
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}
for short) be the largest
C
-eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}
, then

\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}=\max\{\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}.

(1.2)

Chen et al. [
References
] showed that the largest
C
-eigenvalue of the piezoelectric-type tensor has concrete physical meaning which determines the highest piezoelectric coupling constant.

Due to the importance of the largest
C
-eigenvalue of the piezoelectric-type tensor, some authors focus their interest on this topic. As far as we know, there are several works related to compute all the
C
-eigenpairs of piezoelectric-type tensors. One is the work by Liang and Yang [
References
] in which they proposed a shifted eigenvalue decomposition method for computing
C
-eigenvalues. The others are proposed by Zhao and Luo [
References
], Liu and Mo [
References
] via
Z
-eigenpair methods. Yang and Liang [
References
] developed a convex relaxation method to compute the largest
C
-eigenvalue. Besides, some estimation are given to locate all the
C
-eigenvalues in an interval, see [
References
-
References
] and the references therein.

Liu and Mo [
References
] constructed a symmetric fourth-order tensor that related to a given piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
by two steps. Firstly, construct a partially symmetric tensor
\mathcal{B}=(b_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}})\in\mathbb{R}^{[4,n]}
with entries defined as

b_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}=\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{a_{ii_{1}i_{2}}a_{ii_{3}i_{4}}},\;\;i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n].

(1.3)

In the next step, a symmetric tensor
\mathcal{\bar{B}}=(\bar{b}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}})
can be easily got from the partially symmetric tensor
\mathcal{B}
. We only need to set

\bar{b}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}=\frac{1}{3}(b_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}+b_{i_{1}i_{3}i_{2}i_{4}}+b_{i_{1}i_{4}i_{2}i_{3}}).

(1.4)

They also showed the following close relationships between the
C
-eigenpair of the original piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
and the
Z
-eigenpair of the structured tenor
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
.

Lemma 1.2

([\ref{liu2}])
Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in\mathbb{R}^{[3,n]}
be a piezoelectric-type tensor, and
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
be defined by
(\ref{1.2})
.

(1)
If
(\lambda,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is a
C
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{A}
, then
(\lambda^{2},\mathbf{y})
is a
Z
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
.

(2)
If
(\lambda^{2},\mathbf{y})
is a
Z
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
with
\lambda\neq 0
, then
(\lambda,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is a
C
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathbf{x}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}
.

(3)
If
(0,\mathbf{y})
is a
Z
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
, then
(0,\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is a
C
-eigenpair of
\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathbf{x}
is a real unit solution of the equation
\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}=0
.

Furthermore, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in\mathbb{R}^{[3,n]}
be a piezoelectric-type tensor,
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
be defined by
(\ref{1.02})
and
(\ref{1.2})
. Then
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
is a positive semi-definite
(PSD)
tensor, i.e.,

\mathcal{\bar{B}}\mathbf{x}^{4}=\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n]}{\bar{b}_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}x_{i_{4}}}\geqslant 0\;\;for\;all\;\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore, all the
Z
-eigenvalues of
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
are nonnegative.

Proof.
For each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, Liu and Mo [
References
] proved that
\mathcal{\bar{B}}\mathbf{x}^{4}=\mathcal{B}\mathbf{x}^{4}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bar{B}}\mathbf{x}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathbf{x}^{4}=\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n]}{b_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}x_{i_{4}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n]}{\left({\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{a_{ii_{1}i_{2}}a_{ii_{3}i_{4}}}}\right)}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}x_{i_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x})_{i}^{2}}\geqslant 0.

It follows from [Theorem 5,
References
] that all the
Z
-eigenvalues of
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
are nonnegative. The proof is completed.
\Box

Actually,
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
can be seen as a function of piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
. For convenience, for a given piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
, we adopt
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
to denote this function, namely,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{\bar{B}}
.

2
Main results

Let
\mathcal{A}
and its perturbed tensor
\mathcal{\tilde{A}}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{E}
be piezoelectric-type tensors. According to the definition of piezoelectric-type tensors, we know that the tensor
\mathcal{E}
is also a piezoelectric-type tensor. Based on (
1.2
), we have the following perturbation bound.

Theorem 2.1

Let
\mathcal{A}
and its perturbed tensor
\mathcal{\tilde{A}}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{E}
be piezoelectric-type tensors. Then

\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}})\leqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

(2.1)

Proof.
In view of (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\mathbf{x}\mathcal{\tilde{A}}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{x}\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\max\{\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}+\max\{\mathbf{x}\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}:\;\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{x}=1,\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

On the other hand, let
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
be the associated left and right
C
-eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})
of piezoelectric-type
\mathcal{A}
. Then

\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}})\geqslant\mathbf{x}\mathcal{\tilde{A}}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{x}\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}+\mathbf{x}\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}=\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\mathbf{x}\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}\geqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

(2.3)

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we get the perturbation bound (
2.1
).
\Box

In [
References
], the authors gave an effective estimation (upper bound) on the lagest
C
-eigenvalue of piezoelectric-type tensor. Hence, together with Theorem
2.1
and [Theorem 2.4,
References
] produces the following result immediately.

Corollary 2.1

Let
\mathcal{A}
and its perturbed tensor
\mathcal{\tilde{A}}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{E}
be piezoelectric-type tensors. Then

\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\left\|E\right\|_{2}\leqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}})\leqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\left\|E\right\|_{2},

(2.4)

where
E=\left({\begin{array}[]{*{20}c}{\mathcal{E}(1,:,:)},&\cdots,&{\mathcal{E}(n,:,:)}\\
\end{array}}\right)
is a column block matrix with the
i
-th block is
\mathcal{E}(i,:,:)
.

By the result [Theorem 2.4,
References
], we know
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant\left\|E\right\|_{2}
. Thus, we have

[\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}),\;\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})]\subseteq[\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\left\|E\right\|_{2},\;\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\left\|E\right\|_{2}].

By the relationships between the original piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
and the structured tenor
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
(Lemma
1.2
), we can deduce the following alternative perturbation bound.

Theorem 2.2

Let
\mathcal{A}
and its perturbed tensor
\mathcal{\tilde{A}}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{E}
be piezoelectric-type tensors. Then

\sqrt{(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})}\leqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}})\leqslant\sqrt{(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})}.

(2.5)

Proof.
In view of Lemma
1.2
, we have

\displaystyle(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}})=\max\{(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

(2.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}+(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\max\{(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}+\max\{(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}}).

On the other hand, let
\mathbf{y}_{0}
be the associated right
C
-eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})
of piezoelectric-type
\mathcal{A}
. Clearly,
\mathbf{y}_{0}
is also the associated
Z
-eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})
of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}
. Then

\displaystyle(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{\tilde{A}}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}})=\max\{(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}_{0}^{4}+(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}_{0}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})+(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})\mathbf{y}_{0}^{4}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}}).

Moreover, for each
\mathbf{y}
with
\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1
, we have

(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})\mathbf{y}^{4}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}\geqslant\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}.

Applying Theorem
1.1
to the above inequality, we have

(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})\mathbf{y}^{4}+(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}\geqslant\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}\mathbf{y}^{4}\geqslant 0,

which means that both
(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})
and
(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})
are nonnegative.

Combining (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we get the perturbation bound (
2.5
).
\Box

The following theorem shows that the perturbation bound given by Theorem
2.2
is more accurate than the one given by Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.3

Let
\mathcal{A}
and its perturbed tensor
\mathcal{\tilde{A}}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{E}
be piezoelectric-type tensors. Then

\displaystyle\left[{\sqrt{(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})},\;\sqrt{(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})}}\;\right]

(2.8)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle[\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}),\;\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})].

Proof.
For each piezoelectric-type tensor
\mathcal{A}
, it follows from [Theorem 2.1,
References
] that the fourth-order tensors
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{\bar{B}}
defined by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) have the same
Z
-eigenpairs. So, without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{B}
.

Firstly, we come to prove

\sqrt{(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})}\leqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})+\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

In view of Lemma
1.2
, the above inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}))^{2}+2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})

(2.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})+2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

Let
\mathcal{A}=(a_{ijk})\in\mathbb{R}^{[3,n]}
and
\mathcal{E}=(\varepsilon_{ijk})\in\mathbb{R}^{[3,n]}
, then for each vector
\mathbf{y}
with
\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})\mathbf{y}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n]}{\left({\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(a_{ii_{1}i_{2}}+\varepsilon_{ii_{1}i_{2}})(a_{ii_{3}i_{4}}+\varepsilon_{ii_{3}i_{4}})}-\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{a_{ii_{1}i_{2}}a_{ii_{3}i_{4}}}}\right)}y_{i_{1}}y_{i_{2}}y_{i_{3}}y_{i_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n]}{\left({\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{\varepsilon_{ii_{1}i_{2}}\varepsilon_{ii_{3}i_{4}}}}\right)}y_{i_{1}}y_{i_{2}}y_{i_{3}}y_{i_{4}}+2\sum\limits_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}\in[n]}{\left({\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{a_{ii_{1}i_{2}}\varepsilon_{ii_{3}i_{4}}}}\right)}y_{i_{1}}y_{i_{2}}y_{i_{3}}y_{i_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}+2\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}(\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}+2\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}^{2}}}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}+2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\max\{(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}\leqslant\max\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}+2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\max\{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\}+\max\{2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}:\;\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{y}=1\},

which produces

\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})\leqslant\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})+2\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})}\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})}.

Applying Lemma
1.2
to the above inequality, we get (
2.9
).

Next, we prove

\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})-\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant\sqrt{(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A}))^{2}+\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})}.

(2.10)

When
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})<\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})
, (
2.10
) is evident. When
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\geqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})
, (
2.10
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\min}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{A}}})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}))^{2}-2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})

(2.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})-2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

Since

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})\mathbf{y}^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}+2\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}(\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-2\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}^{2}}}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i\in[n]}{(\mathcal{E}\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y})_{i}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}.

Apply Lemma
1.2
again, and consider the following difference,

\displaystyle\left({\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}}\right)-[\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})-2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})]+2\left({\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})}\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})}-\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})]-2\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})}\left({\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\left({\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})}-\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left({\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})}}\right)\left({\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}+\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})}-2\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\left({\sqrt{\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})}-\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}}\right).

Note that
\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\geqslant\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})
,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}\leqslant\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}\leqslant\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}})
, then, we have

\left({\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}}\right)-[\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})-2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E})]\geqslant 0,

i.e.,

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}-2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\sqrt{\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbf{y}^{4}}\geqslant\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})-2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}).

Therefore,

(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{\tilde{A}}})\mathbf{y}^{4}\geqslant\lambda_{Z\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{E}})-2\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{A})\lambda_{C\mathop{\max}}(\mathcal{E}),

which produces (
2.11
) and (
2.10
).

By the above analyses, we get the desired result (
2.8
).
\Box

Comparing with the bounds in Theorem
2.1
, Corollary
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
, we have proved that the perturbation bound in Theorem
2.2
is always tightest than the other ones.

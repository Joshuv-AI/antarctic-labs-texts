Infinitely many positive solutions of a Gross-Pitaevskii equation in the presence of a harmonic potential and combined nonlinearities
Yakine Bahri, Hichem Hajaiej

Abstract
The main goal of this paper is to address an important conjecture in the field of differential equations in the presence of a harmonic potential. While in the subcritical case, the uniqueness of positive solution has been addressed by Hirose and Ohta in [ 12 ] in 2007, the problem has remained open for years in the supercritical case. In [ 9 ] , the author obtained interesting numerical computations suggesting that for some bifurcating parameter λ \lambda , the equation has many positive solutions that vanish at infinity. In this paper, we provide a proof to this claim by constructing an accountable number of solutions that bifurcate from the unique singular solutions with λ \lambda close to the first eigenvalue λ 1 \lambda_{1} of the harmonic operator − Δ + | x | 2 -\Delta+|x|^{2} . Our method hinges on a matching argument, and applies to the supercritical case, and to the supercritical case in the presence of a subcritical, critical or supercritical perturbation.

A.3
), we get

\left|\frac{d}{ds}\mathcal{J}[\eta]\right|\lesssim|f(s,\eta)|+e^{\frac{\mu}{2}s}\int_{s}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}\sigma}|\sin(\nu(\sigma-s))|f(\sigma,\eta)d\sigma\lesssim e^{-\frac{2(p-q)}{p-1}ms}.

Similarly, we have

\|\mathcal{J}[\eta_{1}]-\mathcal{J}[\eta_{2}]\|_{\Xi_{S_{*}}}\lesssim e^{-\frac{2(p-q)}{p-1}mS_{*}}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\|_{\Xi_{S_{*}}}.

This shows the local existence of the singular solution. Note that the other cases can be proved similarly. The global existence can be obtained exactly as in
[
10
]
using a shooting argument. Since the proof is similar, we omit it.

Let us show (
1.7
). From the above calculation, we have

\Phi(r)=Ar^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}y\left(\frac{\log(r)}{m}\right)=Ar^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\left[1+\eta\left(-\frac{\log(r)}{m}\right)\right].

Hence,

\Phi^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{2A}{p-1}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-1}\left[1+\eta\left(-\frac{\log(r)}{m}\right)+\frac{p-1}{2m}\eta^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\log(r)}{m}\right)\right].

Combining this with
\|\eta\|_{\Xi_{S_{*}}}\lesssim 1
, we conclude (
1.7
). This finishes the proof of this theorem.

∎

A.2
The behavior at infinity

The idea of the proof of the following lemma was first used in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
for (
1.4
). We provide a complete proof in our setting.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
p>1
and
q>1
. There exists
K>0
such that

\Phi=K\exp\left(-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right)r^{\frac{\lambda-d}{2}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\right),\textrm{ as }r\to+\infty

(A.4)

Proof.

First, we consider the change of variable

\Phi=e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\Psi.

(A.5)

Thus, the equation for
\Psi
is given by

\Psi^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+2r\right)\Psi^{\prime}+(\lambda+d)\Psi+\exp\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{p}+\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{q}=0,\quad r>0,

(A.6)

Since
\lim_{r\to\infty}\Phi(r)=0
, we infer from (
A.5
) that

\Psi(r)\lesssim e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\quad{\rm for\ any}\ r>1.

(A.7)

As a consequence, we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi(r)}\in[-\infty,0]\ {\rm exists}.

(A.8)

Indeed, we shall first prove that
\Psi
is decreasing for
r>0
i.e.
\Psi^{\prime}<0
. We rewrite (
A.6
) as follows

\left(\Psi^{\prime}r^{d-1}e^{r^{2}}\right)^{\prime}=-(\lambda+d)r^{d-1}e^{r^{2}}\Psi-r^{d-1}\exp\left(\frac{(p+1)r^{2}}{2}\right)\Psi^{p}-\exp\left(\frac{(q+1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{q},

(A.9)

when
r>0.
From (
A.5
), (
1.6
) and the fact that
\sigma:=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}>1
, we have

\lim_{r\to 0}\Psi^{\prime}(r)r^{d-1}e^{r^{2}}=A(p,d)\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-1}r^{d-1}=A(p,d)\lim_{r\to 0}r^{\sigma-1}r^{\frac{d}{2}-1}=0.

Thus, we integrate (
A.9
) between
0
and
r
and we obtain

\Psi^{\prime}r^{d-1}e^{r^{2}}=-(\lambda+d)\int_{0}^{r}\Psi e^{s^{2}}s^{d-1}ds-\int_{0}^{r}\Psi^{p}e^{\frac{ps^{2}}{2}}s^{d-1}ds-\int_{0}^{r}\Psi^{q}e^{\frac{qs^{2}}{2}}s^{d-1}ds<0,\quad r>0.

This shows that
\Psi^{\prime}<0
and
\Psi
is strictly monotone. Next, we denote by
Z(r):=\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi(r)}
. Assume that
Z(r)
oscillates for
r
large and
\liminf_{r\to\infty}Z(r)=-\alpha
, with
\alpha>0
. Note that if
\alpha=0
, then
\lim_{r\to\infty}Z(r)=0
since
Z<0
. We will discuss later this case.
Coming back to
\alpha>0
, we infer that there exists a sequence
R_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, such that
Z^{\prime}(R_{n})=0
and
Z(R_{n})=-\alpha-\eta_{n},
where
\eta_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. This means that

Z^{\prime}(R_{n})=\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}(R_{n})}{\Psi(R_{n})}-Z(R_{n})^{2}=0.

We divide the previous equality by
Z(R_{n})
in order to get

\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}(R_{n})}{\Psi^{\prime}(R_{n})}=Z(R_{n})=-\alpha-\eta_{n}.

(A.10)

On the other hand, from (
A.6
) we write

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}(R_{n})}{\Psi^{\prime}(R_{n})}=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{d-1}{R_{n}}+2R_{n}\right)-\frac{(\lambda+d)}{Z(R_{n})}-\exp\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}R_{n}^{2}\right)\frac{\Psi^{p-1}(R_{n})}{Z(R_{n})}

\displaystyle-\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)}{2}R_{n}^{2}\right)\frac{\Psi^{q-1}(R_{n})}{Z(R_{n})}.

Hence, combining this with (
A.7
), we obtain

\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}(R_{n})}{\Psi^{\prime}(R_{n})}\lesssim-\left(\frac{d-1}{R_{n}}+2R_{n}\right)+\frac{\lambda+d}{\alpha+\eta_{n}}+\frac{1}{\alpha+\eta_{n}}.

Thus, for
n
large enough we infer that

\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}(R_{n})}{\Psi^{\prime}(R_{n})}<-(\alpha+\eta_{n}).

This is contradiction with (
A.10
) which is sufficient to conclude (
A.8
).
More precisely, we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}Z(r)=-\infty,\quad{\rm with}\quad Z(r)=\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi(r)}.

(A.11)

Indeed, we assume by contradiction that the limit is finite. From (
A.6
), the equation of
Z
is given by

Z^{\prime}+2rZ=-(\lambda+d)-f(Z)

where

f(Z):=Z^{2}+\frac{d-1}{r}Z+\exp\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{p-1}+\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{q-1}.

Thus,

Z(r)=Z(1)e^{-r^{2}}-\int_{1}^{r}\left((\lambda+d)+f(Z(s))\right)e^{s^{2}}ds\ e^{-r^{2}}.

Hence, using l’Hopital’s rule and (
A.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}Z(r)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{1}^{r}\left((\lambda+d)+f(Z(s))\right)e^{s^{2}}ds}{e^{r^{2}}}=-\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{f(Z(r))}{2r}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{r\to\infty}\left(\frac{Z^{2}(r)}{2r}+\frac{d-1}{2r^{2}}Z(r)\right).

(A.12)

Yields,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\left(Z(r)+\frac{Z^{2}(r)}{2r}+\frac{d-1}{2r^{2}}Z(r)\right)=0.

As a consequence, we get
\lim_{r\to\infty}Z(r)=0
. Going back to the first equality in (
A.12
), similarly we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}rZ(r)=-\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{1}^{r}\left((\lambda+d)+f(Z(s))\right)e^{s^{2}}ds}{r^{-1}e^{r^{2}}}=-\frac{(\lambda+d)}{2}.

Hence, given
\varepsilon>0
,

Z(r)=\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi(r)}\geq-\frac{(\lambda+d)+2\varepsilon}{2r}

for
r
large enough. Thus,
\Psi(r)\geq r^{-\frac{(\lambda+d)}{2}-\varepsilon}
. This is a contradiction with (
A.7
). This ends the proof of (
A.11
). In addition, we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{r\Psi(r)}=-2.

(A.13)

Indeed, using l’Hopital’s rule and the equation (
A.6
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{r\Psi(r)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Psi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{r\Psi^{\prime}(r)+\Psi(r)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\Bigg(\frac{-\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+2r\right)\Psi^{\prime}(r)-(\lambda+d)\Psi(r)+\exp\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{p}(r)}{r\Psi^{\prime}(r)+\Psi(r)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{q-1}}{r\Psi^{\prime}(r)+\Psi(r)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to\infty}\Bigg(\frac{-\left(\frac{d-1}{r^{2}}+2\right)-(\lambda+d)\frac{\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}+\exp\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{p-1}(r)\frac{\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}}{1+\frac{\Psi(r)}{r\Psi^{\prime}(r)}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{q-1}(r)\frac{\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}}{1+\frac{\Psi(r)}{r\Psi^{\prime}(r)}}\Bigg)

Hence, from (
A.7
) and (
A.11
), we conclude (
A.13
). Note that, combining (
A.7
) with (
A.13
), we deduce that

|\Psi^{\prime}(r)|\lesssim re^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}.

(A.14)

Next, we define the function

E(r):=r\Psi^{\prime}(r)+2r^{2}\Psi(r).

(A.15)

We claim that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{E(r)}{\Psi(r)}=\frac{\lambda-d}{2}.

(A.16)

Indeed, from (
A.7
) and (
A.14
) we know that
\lim_{r\to\infty}E(r)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\Psi(r)=0
, thus using l’Hopital’s rule, (
A.6
), (
A.7
) and (
A.13
) in order to compute

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{E(r)}{\Psi(r)}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{E^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)+r\Psi^{\prime\prime}(r)+4r\Psi(r)+2r^{2}\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}

\displaystyle=2-d-\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{r\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}\left((\lambda+d-4)+\exp\left(\frac{(p-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{p-1}+\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)}{2}r^{2}\right)\Psi^{q-1}\right)

\displaystyle=2-d+\frac{(\lambda+d-4)}{2}=\frac{\lambda-d}{2}.

(A.17)

In addition, we claim that

\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\left(\frac{E(r)}{\Psi(r)}-\frac{\lambda-d}{2}\right)=\frac{\lambda-d}{8}(\lambda+d-4).

(A.18)

Indeed, following the same computations as in (
A.17
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\left(\frac{E(r)}{\Psi(r)}-\frac{\lambda-d}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(4-\lambda-d)\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\left(\frac{r\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2-\frac{\lambda+d}{2}\right)\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\left(2+\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{r\Psi(r)}\right)\frac{r\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}

\displaystyle=\left(2-\frac{\lambda+d}{2}\right)\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{E(r)}{\Psi(r)}\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{r\Psi(r)}{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda-d}{8}(\lambda+d-4).

Finally, combining (
A.16
) and (
A.18
), we obtain

\frac{E(r)}{\Psi(r)}=\frac{\lambda-d}{2}+\frac{\lambda-d}{8}(\lambda+d-4)\frac{1}{r^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\quad{\rm as}\ r\to\infty.

Yields, from (
A.15
), that

\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(r)}{\Psi(r)}=-2r+\frac{\lambda-d}{2r}+\frac{\lambda-d}{8}(\lambda+d-4)\frac{1}{r^{3}}+o\left(\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right)\quad{\rm as}\ r\to\infty.

Hence, integrating between
1
and
r
, we obtain

\Psi(r)=Ke^{-r^{2}}r^{\frac{\lambda-d}{2}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)\right),\textrm{ as }r\to+\infty,

(A.19)

with
K=\Psi(1)\exp\left\{1+\frac{\lambda-d}{16}(\lambda+d-4)+\int_{1}^{\infty}o\left(s^{-3}\right)ds\right\}.
Thus we finish the proof of (
A.4
) by combing (
A.5
) with (
A.19
).
∎

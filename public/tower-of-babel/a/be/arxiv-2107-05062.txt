Time-dependent study of 𝑲 𝑺 → 𝝅 + ​ 𝝅 − K_{S}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-} decays for flavour physics measurements
P. Pakhlov, V. Popov

Abstract
Nowadays High Energy Physics experiments can accumulate unprecedented statistics of heavy flavour decays that allows to apply new methods, based on the study of very rare phenomena, which used to be just desperate. In this paper we propose a new method to measure composition of K 0 K^{0} - K ¯ 0 \overline{K}{}^{0} , produced in a decay of heavy hadrons. This composition contains important information, in particular about weak and strong phases between amplitudes of the produced K 0 K^{0} and K ¯ 0 \overline{K}{}^{0} . We consider possibility to measure these parameters with time-dependent K 0 → π + ​ π − K^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-} analysis. Due to C ​ P CP -violation in kaon mixing time-dependent decay rates of K 0 K^{0} and K ¯ 0 \overline{K}{}^{0} differ, and the initial amplitudes revealed in the C ​ P CP -violating decay pattern. We perform phenomenological study of K 0 K^{0} decay evolution initially produced as a combination a | K 0 ( t ) ⟩ + b | K ¯ ( t ) 0 ⟩ a\left|K^{0}(t)\right\rangle+b\left|\overline{K}{}^{0}(t)\right\rangle , where a a and b b , complex amplitudes, could also be dependent on decay time of heavy mother particle. In particular we consider cases of charmed hadrons decays: D + → K 0 ​ π + D^{+}\to K^{0}\pi^{+} , D s + → K 0 ​ K + D_{s}^{+}\to K^{0}K^{+} , Λ → p ​ K 0 \Lambda\to pK^{0} and with some assumptions D 0 → K 0 ​ π 0 D^{0}\to K^{0}\pi^{0} . This can be used to test the sum rule for charmed mesons and to obtain input for the full constraint of the two body amplitudes of D D -mesons.



I
Introduction

The final states of heavy hadron decays sometimes differ only by the replacement of the strange
K^{0}
meson by its antiparticle due to the contribution of different diagrams or neutral meson mixing, thus representing a superposition of the strange and antistrange states. An example is the decay
D^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}(K^{0})\pi^{+}
, where both
\overline{K}{}^{0}
and
K^{0}
are produced due to the presence of both Cabibbo favourite (CF) and doubly Cabibbo suppressed (DCS) amplitudes.

Considering charm hadron decays from the point of view of flavour
SU(3)_{f}
symmetry one can obtain the following sum rules
Grossman:2012ry
:

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}A_{D^{0}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}\pi^{0}}+A_{D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}}-A_{D^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}\pi^{+}}=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}A_{D^{0}\to K^{0}\pi^{0}}+A_{D^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}}+\sqrt{2}A_{D^{+}\to K^{+}\pi^{0}}-A_{D^{+}\to K^{0}\pi^{+}}=0.

(2)

These relations are the isospin sum rules and both are broken at the same level of
\mathcal{O}\left((m_{u}-m_{d})/\Lambda_{QCD}\right)\sim 1\%
. Amplitudes involved in eq. (
1
,
2
) are of the same order of Cabibbo suppression and correspond to CF and DCS decay amplitudes, respectively. These sum rules could be illustrated as shown in Fig.
1
.

There is another sum based on
SU(3)_{f}
that is particularly interesting since here CF and DCS amplitudes are mixed together:

A_{D^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}\pi^{+}}-A_{D_{s}^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}K^{+}}-\frac{A_{D^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}K^{+}}}{\lambda}+\frac{A_{D_{s}^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}\pi^{+}}}{\lambda}+\frac{A_{D^{+}\to K^{0}\pi^{+}}}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{A_{D_{s}^{+}\to K^{0}K^{+}}}{\lambda^{2}}=0,

(3)

where
\lambda=\sin{\theta_{12}}
– is the CKM parameter and
\theta_{12}
is the Cabibbo angle.

Recently, the question of validity of
SU(3)_{f}
rules and accuracy of their approximation has become urgent as they are widely used to explain the anomalously large
CP
violation observed in
D^{0}
decays by LHCb experiment
Aaij:2019kcg
:

\Delta A_{CP}=A_{CP}(K^{+}K^{-})-A_{CP}(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=(-15.4\pm 2.9)\times 10^{-4},

(4)

Many papers trying to reconcile LHCb result with the Standard Model suppose that such discrepancy could be explained by the enhanced penguin amplitudes
Golden:1989qx
;
Hiller:2012xm
;
Brod:2012ud
;
Franco:2012ck
. In these studies it was argued that
SU(3)_{f}
breaking not necessary happens at the amplitude level but rather could be explained by FSI generated phases.

Structure of charm decay amplitudes could be described in terms of Wick contractions of operators of effective Hamiltonian. In this work we are particularly interested in amplitudes involving
K^{0}
in the final state. In the following we adopt results for amplitude expressions obtained in
Buccella:2019kpn
,
for Cabibbo-favourite amplitudes:

\displaystyle A_{D^{0}\to K^{-}\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{5}(3T-2C-K)e^{i\delta_{1/2}}+\frac{2}{5}(T+C+\kappa)

\displaystyle A_{D^{0}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{5\sqrt{2}}(3T-2C-K)e^{i\delta_{1/2}}+\frac{3}{5\sqrt{2}}(T+C+\kappa)

\displaystyle A_{D^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(T+C+\kappa)

\displaystyle A_{D_{s}^{+}\to\overline{K}{}^{0}K^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{5}(2T-3C+\Delta)e^{i\delta^{\prime}_{1}}+\frac{2}{5}(T+C+\kappa),

(5)

and for doubly Cabibbo-suppressed amplitudes:

\displaystyle A_{D^{0}\to K^{+}\pi^{-}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{5}(3T-2C+K)e^{i\delta_{1/2}}-\frac{2}{5}(T+C+\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle A_{D^{0}\to K^{0}\pi^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{5\sqrt{2}}(3T-2C+K)e^{i\delta_{1/2}}-\frac{3}{5\sqrt{2}}(T+C+\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle A_{D^{+}\to K^{0}\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{5}(2T-3C+\Delta-K^{\prime})e^{i\delta_{1/2}}-\frac{2}{5}(T+C+\kappa^{\prime})

\displaystyle A_{D_{s}^{+}\to K^{0}K^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(T+C+\kappa^{\prime}).

(6)

Here
T
and
C
correspond to “tree”-level color-connected and color-suppressed amplitudes,
K
and
K^{\prime}
are parameters corresponding to non-conservation of strangeness changing currents,

\kappa
and
\kappa^{\prime}
– parameters allowing
SU(3)_{f}
-breaking in CF and DCS amplitudes. Also two phases are present
\delta_{1/2}
,
\delta_{1}^{\prime}
corresponding to
I=\frac{1}{2}
and
I=1
amplitudes respectively.

Buccella
et al.

Buccella:2019kpn
performed fit to the above mentioned amplitude parameters based on observed values of
CP
asymmetries and branching ratios for charm hadron decays, and made predictions for the strong phase difference in the
D^{0}\to K^{\pm}\pi^{\mp}
decay channel:

\delta^{+-}=(3.14\pm 5.69)^{\circ}.

(7)

Furthermore, this study can be extended to obtain predictions for other strong phase differences:

\displaystyle\delta^{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-3\pm 6)^{\circ};

(8)

\displaystyle\delta^{0+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-76\pm 4)^{\circ};

(9)

\displaystyle\delta_{s}^{0+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(108\pm 4)^{\circ}.

(10)

In this paper we propose a method that allows to measure strong phase differences and amplitude ratios for final states with
K^{0}
-meson, hence probe the validity of approaches used to explain LHCb result and in general the sum rules. Such measurements will possibly allow to identify the source and scale of
SU(3)_{f}
breaking in charm hadron decays.

In our previous paper
Pakhlov:2019wkn
we suggested to use semileptonic
K^{0}
decays and study their time evolution to measure the complex phase between
K^{0}
and
\overline{K}{}^{0}
at the production point. The method is based on the disentanglement of the production phase from the (known)
K^{0}-\overline{K}{}^{0}
mixing phase, using the later as a reference. While the method can work properly, its experimental application is a challenge due to unobserved neutrino in kaon decay. We realized that the similar sensitivity could be achieved using the standard
K^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays, which are easy to reconstruct. In this paper we show that time evolution of the state
a\left|K^{0}(t)\right\rangle+b\left|\overline{K}{}^{0}(t)\right\rangle
decaying into
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
allows to extract the complex parameters
a
and
b
as well. In this case we utilize the
CP
-violating phase
\phi_{+-}
as a reference for the proposed measurements. Both methods are based on the rare neutral kaon effects: rare decays (semileptonic for short lived kaon component) or rare interference (between short and long lived components).

II
Method

Time evolution of the neutral kaon system could be described by Shrödinger equation

i{\partial}_{t}{K^{0}(t)\choose\overline{K}{}^{0}(t)}=\left({{\mathbf{M}}-\frac{i}{2}{\mathbf{\Gamma}}}\right){K^{0}(t)\choose\overline{K}{}^{0}(t)}\,,

(11)

where effective Hamiltonian is a sum of absorptive and dispersive parts,
\bf M
and
\bf\Gamma
are
2\times 2
hermitian matrices. The Hamiltonian eigenvalues could be written as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{S,L}\equiv m_{S,\,L}-i\frac{\Gamma_{S,\,L}}{2}=\left(M_{11}-i\frac{\Gamma_{11}}{2}\right)\pm\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)_{\!\!K}\left(M_{12}-i\frac{\Gamma_{12}}{2}\right)\,,

(12)

where
m_{S,\,L}
,
\Gamma_{S,\,L}
are masses and widths of the Hamiltonian eigenstates
K^{0}_{S}
and
K^{0}_{L}
, and parameters
p
,
q
correspond to the flavour admixtures of eigenstates defined by

\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)_{\!\!K}^{2}=\frac{M_{12}-\frac{i}{2}\Gamma_{12}}{M_{12}^{*}-\frac{i}{2}\Gamma_{12}^{*}}\,.

(13)

Since we consider only
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
final state it is convenient to use
p/q=(1+\varepsilon)/(1-\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon
describes the
CP
-even component in
K^{0}_{L}
. Then amplitudes describing evolution of initially pure flavour eigenstates in terms of
K^{0}_{S}
/
K^{0}_{L}
could be written as

\displaystyle\left|K^{0}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{-i\lambda_{S}t}\left|K_{S}\right\rangle+e^{-i\lambda_{L}t}\left|K_{L}\right\rangle\right],

(14)

\displaystyle\left|\overline{K}{}^{0}(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{2}}\left[e^{-i\lambda_{S}t}\left|K_{S}\right\rangle-e^{-i\lambda_{L}t}\left|K_{L}\right\rangle\right].

(15)

Using these equations one can obtain for time-dependent decay rates

\mathcal{R}(t)=\frac{1\mp 2\mathrm{Re}(\varepsilon)}{2}|A_{K_{S}\to\pi\pi}|^{2}\Big[e^{-\Gamma_{S}t}+|\eta_{+-}|^{2}e^{-\Gamma_{L}t}\\
\pm 2|\eta_{+-}|e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma_{L}+\Gamma_{S})t}\cos{(\Delta mt-\phi_{+-})}\Big],

(16)

where the upper (lower) sign corresponds to initial pure
K^{0}
(
\overline{K}{}^{0}
),
\Delta m=m_{L}-m_{S}
is a mass difference and we introduced for the amplitude ratio parameter

\frac{\left\langle\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\left|H\right|K^{0}_{L}\right\rangle}{\left\langle\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\left|H\right|K^{0}_{S}\right\rangle}=\eta_{+-}=|\eta_{+-}|e^{i\phi_{+-}}.

(17)

The third interference term in eq. (
16
) basically allows to distinguish the initial flavour of neutral kaon.
Parameters
\eta_{+-}
and
\phi_{+-}
have been measured with great precision and current world averages (assuming CPT invariance) are
Zyla:2020zbs
:
\eta_{+-}=(2.232\pm 0.011)\times 10^{-3}
,

\phi_{+-}=(43.51\pm 0.05)^{\circ}
. In the following calculations we neglect the direct
CPV
in kaons and assume
\varepsilon=\eta_{+-}
.

Despite the smallness of indirect CPV in kaons, itself it opens interesting possibilities for flavour physics measurements
Grossman:2011zk
;
Bigi:1994aw
;
Kagan:2020vri
.
In the following section we consider few cases of charm hadron decays that are particularly interesting for the
SU(3)_{f}
probe.

III
Strong phase difference between CF and DCS decays

Here we consider a set of two-body decays, where both Cabibbo-favourite and doubly Cabibbo-suppressed amplitudes contribute to the final states. The following analysis could be applied to the decays
D^{+}\to K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}
,
D_{s}^{+}\to K^{0}_{S}K^{+}
and
\Lambda_{c}\to pK^{0}_{S}
(the later is not related to
SU(3)_{f}
sum rules, but is interesting on its own). Amplitudes of the final states with
K^{0}
could be expressed then

\displaystyle A_{f}

\displaystyle=\langle\pi\pi|H|\overline{K}{}^{0}\rangle+\sqrt{r_{f}}e^{i\delta}\langle\pi\pi|H|K^{0}\rangle,

(18)

\displaystyle\overline{A_{f}}

\displaystyle=\langle\pi\pi|H|K^{0}\rangle+\sqrt{\overline{r_{f}}}e^{i\delta}\langle\pi\pi|H|\overline{K}{}^{0}\rangle

(19)

where
\delta
,
r_{f}
– strong phase difference and amplitude ratio for CF and DCS amplitudes.
In general case
r_{f}\neq\overline{r_{f}}
and present itself direct CPV in charm decays. Current world averages on
A_{CP}
for
D^{+}
and
D_{s}^{+}
mesons are
Amhis:2019ckw
:

\displaystyle A_{CP}^{D_{s}\to K^{0}_{S}K^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(8\pm 26)\times 10^{-4},

\displaystyle A_{CP}^{D^{+}\to K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-41\pm 9)\times 10^{-4}.

(20)

While non-zero effect was observed for
D^{+}
, it was noted
Belle:2012ygt
that after subtraction
K^{0}
-
\overline{K}{}^{0}
-mixing contribution asymmetry is consistent with zero. So we assume no CPV thereafter
r_{f}=\overline{r_{f}}
.

Time-dependent decay rates could be obtained by substituting
K^{0}
(
\overline{K}{}^{0}
) decay amplitudes in (
18
,
19
) with evolution equations (
14
,
15
).

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{+}\equiv|A_{f}|^{2}=\mathcal{\overline{R}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r_{f}\mathcal{R}+\sqrt{r_{f}}\left(\cos{\delta}+2|\eta_{+-}|\sin{\delta}\sin{\phi_{+-}}\right)\times\Big(e^{-\Gamma_{S}t}-|\eta_{+-}|^{2}e^{-\Gamma_{L}t}\Big)

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{r_{f}}|\eta_{+-}|\Big(\sin{\delta+2|\eta_{+-}|\cos{\delta}\sin{\phi_{+-}}}\Big)e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma_{L}+\Gamma_{S})t}\sin{(\Delta mt-\phi_{+-})},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{-}\equiv|\overline{A_{f}}|^{2}=\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle r_{f}\mathcal{\overline{R}}+\sqrt{r_{f}}\left(\cos{\delta}-2|\eta_{+-}|\sin{\delta}\sin{\phi_{+-}}\right)\times\Big(e^{-\Gamma_{S}t}-|\eta_{+-}|^{2}e^{-\Gamma_{L}t}\Big)

(22)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{r_{f}}|\eta_{+-}|\Big(\sin{\delta}-2|\eta_{+-}|\cos{\delta}\sin{\phi_{+-}}\Big)e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\Gamma_{L}+\Gamma_{S})t}\sin{(\Delta mt-\phi_{+-})}.

These formulas demonstrate that the
K^{0}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
time-dependent decay rates depend on the initial strong phase, moreover, both sine and cosine of the strong phase enter the formula, therefore there are no trigonometrical ambiguities in this measurement. The decay rates along with asymmetry are illustrated in Fig.
2

\sim[6,14]\,\tau_{K_{S}}

IV
Feasibility study

In this section we estimate potential precision of proposed measurement in future experiments. It was shown in previous section that one would expect the most sensitivity for strong phase could be achieved at big lifetimes –
[6,14]\,\tau_{K_{S}}
. Based on this one could conclude that it is essential that experiment should possess large tracking detector and/or produce soft kaons. Also proper charged hadron identification is needed, since some of the final states differ by
K/\pi
interchange.

We consider the most promising experiments the project of future Super
c
–
\tau
factory
Bondar:2013cja
and Belle II experiment
Abe:2010gxa
that is already taking data. Both experiments possess large drift chambers (
R\sim\ 1
m) and produce relatively soft kaons,
\beta\gamma\sim 1..4
. Hadron identification in Belle II provided with TOP in barrel part and ARICH in endcaps and for the
c
–
\tau
factory identification will be provided with FARICH detector that covers almost full solid angle. Given the spatial resolution of drift chambers
\sim 100\mu m
, kaon life time resolution could be expected at the level of a few percent that is more than enough to perform proposed measurement.

VI
Summary

In this paper we presented a method to measure strong phase differences in charm hadron decay with
K^{0}
-meson in the final state. It was shown that CPV in
K^{0}
-
\overline{K}{}^{0}
mixing allows us to disentangle initial combination
a\left|K^{0}(t)\right\rangle+b\left|\overline{K}{}^{0}(t)\right\rangle
that arises in the presence of CF and DCS decays. In order to perform such measurement the experiment should satisfy following requirements: large tracking detector that allows reconstruct
K^{0}_{S}
decays even after
10\tau_{K_{S}}
, sufficient statistics of charm decays –
\mathcal{O}(10^{6}..10^{7})
and proper charged hadron identification. The Belle II experiment and future Super
c
–
\tau
factory are good candidates for such measurement. While LHC experiments have huge data samples of charmed mesons, some of the features of the detectors significantly reduce the possibilities of such measurements: LHCb has too short tracker, while CMS and Atlas have no particle identification.

To estimate potential precision of the method feasibility study was performed. Expected number of events was calculated for each particular channel for both experiments. Proposed measurements proved to be unbiased and free of trigonometrical ambiguity. Obtained results for statistical uncertainty (assuming
r_{f}\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
) are comparable with current theoretical uncertainties and uncertainties that could be obtained with semileptonic kaon decays. We also presented an estimation of regeneration contributions to proposed measurement.

Asymptotic Expansions of The Traces of the Thermoelastic Operators
Genqian Liu, Xiaoming Tan

Abstract
. We obtain the asymptotic expansions of the traces of the thermoelastic operators with the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions on a Riemannian manifold, and give an effective method to calculate all the coefficients of the asymptotic expansions. These coefficients provide precise geometric information. In particular, we explicitly calculate the first two coefficients concerning the volumes of the manifold and its boundary. As an application, by combining our results with the isoperimetric inequality we show that an n n -dimensional geodesic ball is uniquely determined up to isometry by its thermoelastic spectrum among all bounded thermoelastic bodies with boundary.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. We consider
\Omega
as a homogeneous, isotropic, thermoelastic body. Assume that the Lamé coefficients
\lambda
,
\mu
and the heat conduction coefficient
\alpha
of the thermoelastic body are constants which satisfy
\mu>0
,
\lambda+\mu\geqslant 0
and
\alpha>0
. We denote by
\operatorname{grad},\,\operatorname{div},\,\Delta_{g},\,\Delta_{B}
and
\operatorname{Ric}
, respectively, the gradient operator, the divergence operator, the Laplace–Beltrami operator, the Bochner Laplacian and the Ricci tensor. We define the thermoelastic operator

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{g}:=\begin{pmatrix}\mu\Delta_{B}+(\lambda+\mu)\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}+\mu\operatorname{Ric}+\rho\omega^{2}&-\beta\operatorname{grad}\\
i\omega\theta_{0}\beta\operatorname{div}&\alpha\Delta_{g}+i\omega\gamma\end{pmatrix},

where the coefficient
\beta
depends on
\lambda,\,\mu
and the linear expansion coefficient,
\gamma
is the specific heat per unit volume,
\theta_{0}
is the reference temperature,
\rho
is the density of the thermoelastic body,
\omega
is the angular frequency and
i=\sqrt{-1}
. We denote by
\textbf{{u}}(x)\in(C^{\infty}(\Omega))^{n}
and
\theta(x)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
the displacement and the temperature variation, respectively. By the methods of
[
27
,
34
]
, we can write the steady thermoelastic equation as follows

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{g}\textbf{{U}}=0\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,

where
\textbf{{U}}=(\textbf{{u}},\theta)^{t}
, the superscript
t
denotes the transpose. Equation (
1.2
) is an extension of the classical elastic equation. In particular, when
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the temperature of the medium is not taken into consideration, equation (
1.2
) reduces to the classical elastic equation (see pp. 35–36 of
[
27
]
).

In the case of classical elasticity, assume that the temperature of the medium is the same at all points and is not changed during deformation. Nevertheless, in reality, most materials are not pure elastic, a temperature change in the elastic body will generate additional strain and stress. Therefore, deformation is followed by temperature variation and, conversely, temperature variation is followed by deformation of the thermoelastic body due to thermal expansion. Even if there is no external stresses or mass forces, some heat sources change the temperature of the thermoelastic body, there will be deformation. The significance of the equation (
1.2
) consists not only in the important independent role of this state, frequently occurring in engineering, but also in the fact that their investigation opens up the way to the study the general type (see p. 528 of
[
27
]
).

In this paper, we study the asymptotic expansions of the taces of the thermoelastic operators on
\Omega
. We denote by
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-}
and
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{+}
the thermoelastic operators with the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, respectively. Here the Neumann boundary condition is defined by, for any
\textbf{{U}}\in(C^{1}(\Omega))^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle N(\textbf{{U}}):=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\nu\operatorname{div}+\mu\nu S&-\beta\nu\\
0&\alpha\partial_{\nu}\end{pmatrix}\textbf{{U}}\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
and
S
is the stress tensor (also called the deformation tensor) of type
(1,1)
defined by
S\textbf{{u}}:=\nabla\textbf{{u}}+\nabla\textbf{{u}}^{t}
for any
\textbf{{u}}\in(C^{1}(\Omega))^{n}
(see p. 562 of
[
53
]
). According to the theory of elliptic operators,
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-}
(respectively,
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{+}
) is an unbounded, self-adjoint and negative (respectively, non-positive) operator in
(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))^{n+1}
(respectively,
(H^{1}(\Omega))^{n+1}
), it follows from
[
3
,
20
,
28
,
34
,
43
]
that there exists a sequence
\{\tau^{-}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
(respectively,
\{\tau^{+}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
) such that the following eigenvalue problem

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{g}\textbf{{U}}^{-}_{k}+\tau^{-}_{k}\textbf{{U}}^{-}_{k}=0&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\textbf{{U}}^{-}_{k}=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{cases}

(respectively,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{g}\textbf{{U}}^{+}_{k}+\tau^{+}_{k}\textbf{{U}}^{+}_{k}=0&\text{in}\ \Omega,\\
N(\textbf{{U}}^{+}_{k})=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega)\end{cases}

has a discrete spectrum
0<\tau^{-}_{1}<\tau^{-}_{2}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\tau^{-}_{k}\leqslant\cdots\to+\infty

(respectively,
0\leqslant\tau^{+}_{1}<\tau^{+}_{2}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\tau^{+}_{k}\leqslant\cdots\to+\infty
), where
\textbf{{U}}^{-}_{k}\in(H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))^{n+1}
(respectively,
\textbf{{U}}^{+}_{k}\in(H^{1}(\Omega))^{n+1}
) is the eigenvector corresponding to the Dirichlet eigenvalue
\tau^{-}_{k}
(respectively, Neumann eigenvalue
\tau^{+}_{k}
).

Let us point out that the thermoelastic operator is a non-Laplace type operator. Contrary to the Laplace type operators, there are no systematic effective methods to explicitly calculate the coefficients of the asymptotic expansions of the traces for non-Laplace type operators. In 1971, Greiner
[
17

indicated that
“the problem of interpreting these coefficients geometrically remains open”
. In this sense, it is just beginning that the study of geometric aspects of spectral asymptotics for non-Laplace type operators, the corresponding methodology is still underdeveloped in comparison with the theory of the Laplace type operator. Thus,
the geometric aspect of the spectral asymptotics of non-Laplace type operators remains an open problem
(see
[
3
]
).

Due to the fact that the Riemannian structure on a manifold is determined by a Laplace type operator, the systematic explicit calculations of coefficients of heat kernels for Laplace type operators are now well understood, see Gilkey
[
16
]
and many others (see

[
7
,
8
,
24
]
). For other types of operators, the first author of this paper gave the first two coefficients of the asymptotic expansions of the traces of the Navier–Lamé operator
[

and the Stokes operator
[

]

The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a smooth compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Assume that
\alpha>0
,
\mu>0
and
\lambda+\mu\geqslant 0
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{-}
and
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{+}
be the thermoelastic operators with the Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions, respectively, and
\{\tau^{\mp}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
satisfy the eigenvalue equations
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\mp}\textbf{{U}}^{\mp}_{k}+\tau^{\mp}_{k}\textbf{{U}}^{\mp}_{k}=0
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-t\tau^{\mp}_{k}}=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\mp}})=t^{-n/2}\bigl(a_{0}+a_{1}^{\mp}t^{1/2}+\cdots+a_{n}^{\mp}t^{n/2}+O(t^{(n+1)/2})\bigr)\quad\text{as}\ t\to 0^{+}.

In particular,

\displaystyle a_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}\biggl(\frac{n-1}{\mu^{n/2}}+\frac{1}{(\lambda+2\mu)^{n/2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{n/2}}\biggr),

\displaystyle a_{1}^{\mp}

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{1}{4}\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\partial\Omega)}{(4\pi)^{(n-1)/2}}\biggr(\frac{n-1}{\mu^{(n-1)/2}}+\frac{1}{(\lambda+2\mu)^{(n-1)/2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{(n-1)/2}}\biggr),

where
\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)
and
\operatorname{vol}(\partial\Omega)
denote the
n
-dimensional volume of
\Omega
and the
(n-1)
-dimensional volume of
\partial\Omega
, respectively.

.

Theorem
1.1
shows that both the volume

(
see
[

]
)
.

\displaystyle N^{\mp}(\tau)=

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{(4\pi)^{n/2}\Gamma(1+n/2)}\biggl(\frac{n-1}{\mu^{n/2}}+\frac{1}{(\lambda+2\mu)^{n/2}}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{n/2}}\biggr)\tau^{n/2}+o(\tau^{n/2})\quad\text{as}\ \tau\to+\infty.

The main ideas of this paper are as follows. Firstly, we denote by
(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\mp}})_{t\geqslant 0}
the parabolic semigroups generated by
\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\mp}
. More precisely,
\textbf{{V}}^{\mp}(t,x)=e^{t\mathcal{L}_{g}^{\mp}}\textbf{{V}}(x)
solve the following initial-boundary problems, respectively,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\textbf{{V}}^{-}}{\partial t}-\mathcal{L}_{g}\textbf{{V}}^{-}=0&\text{in}\ (0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
\textbf{{V}}^{-}=0\quad&\text{on}\ (0,+\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\textbf{{V}}^{-}(0,x)=\textbf{{V}}_{0}&\text{on}\ \{0\}\times\Omega\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\textbf{{V}}^{+}}{\partial t}-\mathcal{L}_{g}\textbf{{V}}^{+}=0&\text{in}\ (0,+\infty)\times\Omega,\\
N(\textbf{{V}}^{+})=0\quad&\text{on}\ (0,+\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
\textbf{{V}}^{+}(0,x)=\textbf{{V}}_{0}&\text{on}\ \{0\}\times\Omega.\end{cases}

Let
\{\textbf{{U}}^{\mp}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
be the orthonormal eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalues
\{\tau^{\mp}_{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
, then the integral kernels
\textbf{{K}}^{\mp}(t,x,y)
are given by

\displaystyle\textbf{{K}}^{\mp}(t,x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-t\tau^{\mp}_{k}}\textbf{{U}}^{\mp}_{k}(x)\otimes\textbf{{U}}^{\mp}_{k}(y).

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{Tr}\textbf{{K}}^{\mp}(t,x,y)\,dV=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-t\tau^{\mp}_{k}}

are spectral invariants.

Furthermore, to investigate the geometric aspects of the spectrum and the asymptotic expansion of the integrals, we combine calculus of symbols (see
[
16
,
19
,
50
]
) and “method of images” (see
[
34
,
37
]
). Let
M=\Omega\cup\partial\Omega\cup\Omega^{*}
be the closed double of
\Omega
, and
L_{g}
the double to
M
of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{g}
. Then
L_{g}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(e^{tL_{g}})_{t\geqslant 0}
on
L^{2}(M)
with integral kernel
\textbf{{K}}(t,x,y)
. Clearly,
\textbf{{K}}^{\mp}(t,x,y)=\textbf{{K}}(t,x,y)\mp\textbf{{K}}(t,x,y^{*})
for
x,y\in\bar{\Omega}
, where
y^{*}
is the double of
y\in\Omega
. Since

\displaystyle e^{tL_{g}}f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}e^{-t\tau}(\tau I+L_{g})^{-1}f(x)\,d\tau,

where
\mathcal{C}
is a suitable curve in the complex plane in the positive direction around the spectrum of
L_{g}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\textbf{{K}}(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}e^{-t\tau}\sigma\big((\tau I+L_{g})^{-1}\big)\,d\tau\bigg)\,d\xi,

where
\sigma\big((\tau I+L_{g})^{-1}\big)
is the full symbol of
(\tau I+L_{g})^{-1}
. In particular, we get
\operatorname{Tr}\textbf{{K}}(t,x,x)
and
\operatorname{Tr}\textbf{{K}}(t,x,x^{*})
for any
x\in\Omega
. Finally, we explicitly calculate the first two coefficients
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
of the asymptotic expansion

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{Tr}\textbf{{K}}^{\mp}(t,x,x)\,dV=a_{0}t^{-n/2}\mp a_{1}t^{-(n-1)/2}+O(t^{1-n/2})\quad\text{as}\ t\to 0^{+},

where
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
are given in Theorem
1.1
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

. Section

is devoted to prove Theorem

2.
Full Symbol of The Resolvent Operator

In the local coordinates
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, we denote by
\bigl\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\bigr\}_{i=1}^{n}
a natural basis for the tangent space
T_{x}\Omega
at the point
x\in\Omega
. In what follows, we will use the Einstein summation convention, the Roman indices run from 1 to
n
, unless otherwise specified. Then the Riemannian metric is given by
g=g_{ij}\,dx_{i}\,dx_{j}
. Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection on
\Omega
,
\nabla_{i}=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}
and
\nabla^{i}=g^{ij}\nabla_{j}
. The gradient operator is denoted by

(2.1)

\operatorname{grad}f=\nabla^{i}f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=g^{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\quad f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
(g^{ij})=(g_{ij})^{-1}
. The divergence operator is denoted by

(2.2)

\operatorname{div}\textbf{{u}}=\nabla_{i}u^{i}=\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial x_{i}}+\Gamma^{j}_{ij}u^{i},\quad\textbf{{u}}=u^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega),

where the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}\bigl(\frac{\partial g_{jl}}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{\partial g_{il}}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\bigr)
. It follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{grad}\operatorname{div}\textbf{{u}}=g^{ij}\Bigl(\frac{\partial^{2}u^{k}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}+\Gamma^{l}_{kl}\frac{\partial u^{k}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial\Gamma^{l}_{kl}}{\partial x_{j}}u^{k}\Bigr)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

Recall that the components of the curvature tensor

(2.4)

\displaystyle R^{l}_{ijk}=\frac{\partial\Gamma^{l}_{jk}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial\Gamma^{l}_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}+\Gamma^{h}_{jk}\Gamma^{l}_{ih}-\Gamma^{h}_{ik}\Gamma^{l}_{jh},

and
R_{ijkl}=g_{lm}R^{m}_{ijk}
, the components of the the Ricci tensor

(2.5)

\displaystyle R_{ij}=g^{kl}R_{iklj}.

The Bochner Laplacian is given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{B}\textbf{{u}}=(\nabla^{j}\nabla_{j}u^{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\quad\textbf{{u}}=u^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega).

Then combining (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we compute

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{B}\textbf{{u}})^{i}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}u^{i}+g^{kl}\Bigl(2\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\frac{\partial u^{j}}{\partial x_{l}}+\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{jk}}{\partial x_{l}}u^{j}+(\Gamma^{i}_{hl}\Gamma^{h}_{jk}-\Gamma^{h}_{kl}\Gamma^{i}_{jh})u^{j}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g}u^{i}-\operatorname{Ric}(\textbf{{u}})^{i}+g^{kl}\Bigl(2\Gamma^{i}_{jk}\frac{\partial u^{j}}{\partial x_{l}}+\frac{\partial\Gamma^{i}_{kl}}{\partial x_{j}}u^{j}\Bigr),

where
\operatorname{Ric}(\textbf{{u}})^{i}=g^{ij}R_{jk}u^{k}
and the Laplace–Beltrami operator is given by

(2.7)

\Delta_{g}f=g^{ij}\Bigl(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}-\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{k}}\Bigr),\quad f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega).

For the sake of simplicity, we denote by

the

identity matrix and

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}(a^{j}_{k})&(b^{j})\\
(c_{k})&d\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}a^{1}_{1}&\dots&a^{1}_{n}&b^{1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a^{n}_{1}&\dots&a^{n}_{n}&b^{n}\\

\end{pmatrix}.

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mu I_{n}&0\\

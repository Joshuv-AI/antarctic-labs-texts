The sharp bound of the third Hankel determinant for inverse of convex functions
Biswajit Rath 1 , K. Sanjay Kumar 2 , {}^{2}, D. Vamshee Krishna 3

Abstract
. The objective of this paper is to find the best possible upper bound of the third Hankel determinant for inverse of convex functions.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
30C45, 30C50

1.
Introduction

Denote
\mathcal{H}
the family all analytic functions in unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and
\mathcal{A}
be the subfamily of functions
f
normilized by the conditions

f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0,
i.e, of the type

(1.1)

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},~a_{1}:=1,

and
\mathcal{S}
be the subfamily of
\mathcal{A},
possessing univalent (schlicht) mappings. For
f\in\mathcal{S},
has an inverse
f^{-1}
given by

(1.2)

f^{-1}(w)=w+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}t_{n}w^{n},~~|w|<r_{o}(f);\left(r_{o}(f)\geq\frac{1}{4}\right).

A typical problem in geometric function theory is to study a functional made up of combination of the coefficients of the original functions. For the positive integers
r,~n,
Pommerenke
[
16
]
characterized the
r^{th}
- Hankel determinant of
n^{th}
-order for
f
given in (1.1), defined as follows:

(1.3)

H_{r,n}(f)=\begin{array}[]{|cccc|}a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\cdots&a_{n+r-1}\\
a_{n+1}&a_{n+2}&\cdots&a_{n+r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n+r-1}&a_{n+r}&\cdots&a_{n+2r-2}\end{array}.

The problem of finding sharp estimates of the third Hankel determinant obtained for
r=3
and
n=1
in (1.2), given by

(1.4)

H_{3,1}(f):=\begin{array}[]{|ccc|}a_{1}=1&a_{2}&a_{3}\\
a_{2}&a_{3}&a_{4}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}&a_{5}\end{array}=2a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}-a_{3}^{3}-a_{4}^{2}+a_{3}a_{5}-a_{2}^{2}a_{5},

is technically much tough than
r=n=2
.

In recent years, many authors are working on obtaining upper bounds (see
[
2
,
8
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
) and a few papers were devoted to the estimation of sharp upper bound to
H_{3,1}(f),
for certain subclasses of analytic functions (see
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
19
]
).
Recently Lecko et al.
[
6
]
obtained the sharp bound for the class of convex function denoted as
\mathcal{S}^{c}
, defined by

(1.5)

\text{Re}\left\{1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}>0.

Motivated by these results, in this paper we obtain sharp estimate for
H_{3,1}(f^{-1})
when
f\in\mathcal{S}^{c}
as
1/36
.

The collection
\mathcal{P},
of all functions
p,
each one called as Carathéodory function
[
5
]
of the form,

(1.6)

p(z)=1+\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}c_{t}z^{t},

having a positive real part in
\mathbb{D}
. In view of (1.4) and (1.5), the coefficients of the functions in
\mathcal{S}^{c}
can be expressed in terms of coefficients of functions in
\mathcal{P}
. We then obtain the upper bound of
H_{3,1}(f^{-1})
, buliding our analysis on the familiar formulas of coefficients
c_{2}
(see,
[
15
, p. 166]
),
c_{3}
(see
[
11
,
12
]
) and
c_{4}
can be found in
[
10
]
.

The foundation for proofs of our main results is the following lemma and we adopt the procedure framed through Libera and Zlotkiewicz
[
12
]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

If
p\in\mathcal{P},
is of the form (1.5) with
c_{1}\geq 0,
such that
c_{1}\in[0,2]
then

\begin{split}&2c_{2}=c_{1}^{2}+\nu\mu,\\
&4c_{3}=c_{1}^{3}+2c_{1}\nu\mu-c_{1}\nu\mu^{2}+2\nu\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho,\\
\text{and}\\
&\begin{split}8c_{4}=~&c_{1}^{4}+3c_{1}^{2}\nu\mu+\left(4-3c_{1}^{2}\right)\nu\mu^{2}+c_{1}^{2}\nu\mu^{3}+4\nu\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\left(1-|\rho|^{2}\right)\psi\\
&+4\nu\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\left(c_{1}\rho-c\mu\rho-\bar{\mu}\rho^{2}\right),\end{split}\end{split}

where
\nu:=4-c_{1}^{2},
for some
\mu
,
\rho
and
\psi
such that
|\mu|\leq 1
,
|\rho|\leq 1
and
|\psi|\leq 1
.

2.
Main result

Theorem 2.1
.

If
f\in

\mathcal{S}^{c}
, then

\big|H_{3,1}(f^{-1})\big|\leq\frac{1}{36}

and the inequality is sharp for
p_{0}(z)=(1+z^{3})/(1-z^{3})
.

Proof.

For
f\in

\mathcal{S}^{c}
, there exists a holomorphic function
p\in\mathcal{P}
such that

(2.1)

\left\{1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right\}=p(z)\Leftrightarrow\left\{f^{\prime}(z)+zf^{\prime\prime}(z)\right\}=p(z)f^{\prime}(z)

Using the series representation for
f
and
p
in (2.1), a simple calculation gives

(2.2)

\begin{split}&a_{2}=\frac{c_{1}}{2},~a_{3}=\frac{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}}{6},~a_{4}=\frac{1}{12}\left[\frac{1}{2}c_{1}^{3}+\frac{3}{2}c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}\right]\\
\text{and}~~&a_{5}=\frac{1}{20}\left[\frac{1}{6}c_{1}^{4}+c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+\frac{1}{2}c_{2}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}c_{1}c_{3}+c_{4}\right]\end{split}

Now from the defination (1.2), we have

(2.3)

w=f(f^{-1})=f^{-1}(w)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}(f^{-1}(w))^{n}.

Further, we have

(2.4)

w=f(f^{-1})=w+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}t_{n}w^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}(w+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}t_{n}w^{n})^{n}.

Upon simplification, we obtain

\displaystyle(t_{2}+a_{2})w^{2}+(t_{3}+2a_{2}t_{2}+a_{3})w^{3}+(t_{4}+2a_{2}t_{3}+a_{2}t_{2}^{2}+3a_{3}t_{2}+a_{4})w^{4}

(2.5)

\displaystyle+(t_{5}+2a_{2}t_{4}+2a_{2}t_{2}t_{3}+3a_{3}t_{3}+3a_{3}t_{2}^{2}+4a_{4}t_{2}+a_{5})w^{5}+......=0.

Equating the coefficients of like power in (2.5), upon simplification, we obtain

(2.6)

\begin{split}&t_{2}=-a_{2};t_{3}=\{-a_{3}+2a_{2}^{2}\};t_{4}=\{-a_{4}+5a_{2}a_{3}-5a_{2}^{3}\};\\
&t_{5}=\{-a_{5}+6a_{2}a_{4}-21a_{2}^{2}a_{3}+3a_{3}^{2}+14a_{2}^{4}\}.\end{split}

Using the values of
a_{n}(n=2,3,4,5)
from (2.2) in (2.6), upon simplification, we obtain

(2.7)

\begin{split}&t_{2}=-\frac{c_{1}}{2},~t_{3}=\frac{1}{6}\left(2c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}\right),~t_{4}=\frac{1}{24}\left(-6c_{1}^{3}+7c_{1}c_{2}-2c_{3}\right)\\
\text{and}~&t_{5}=\frac{1}{120}\left(-6c_{4}+22c_{1}c_{3}-46c_{1}^{2}c_{2}+7c_{2}^{2}+24c_{1}^{4}\right).\end{split}

Now,

\displaystyle H_{3,1}(f^{-1})

\displaystyle=\begin{array}[]{|ccc|}t_{1}=1&t_{2}&t_{3}\\
t_{2}&t_{3}&t_{4}\\
t_{3}&t_{4}&t_{5}\end{array}~,

Using the values of
t_{j},~(j=2,3,4,5)
from (2.7) in (2.8), it simplifies to give

(2.11)

\begin{split}H_{3,1}(f^{-1})=\frac{1}{8640}&\left[4c_{1}^{6}-24c_{1}^{4}c_{2}+12c_{1}^{3}c_{3}+39c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}-44c_{2}^{3}+36c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}\right.\\
&\left.-36c_{1}^{2}c_{4}-60c_{3}^{2}+72c_{2}c_{4}\right].\end{split}

In view of (2.9), using the values of
c_{2},~c_{3}~\text{and}~c_{4}
from lemma 1.1, gives

(2.12)

\begin{split}24c_{1}^{4}c_{2}=&12\left[c_{1}^{6}+c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu\right];\\
12c_{1}^{3}c_{3}=&3\left[c_{1}^{6}+2c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu-c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu^{2}+2c_{1}^{3}\nu(1-|\mu|^{2})\rho\right]\\
44c_{2}^{3}=&\frac{11}{2}\left[c_{1}^{6}+3c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu+3c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}+\nu^{3}\mu^{3}\right];\\
39c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}=&\frac{39}{4}\left[c_{1}^{6}+2c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu+c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}\right];\\
36c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}=&\frac{9}{2}\left[c_{1}^{6}+3c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu+2c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}-c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu^{2}-c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{3}\right.\\
&\left.+2\nu\left(c_{1}^{3}+c_{1}\nu\mu\right)\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho\right];\\
60c_{3}^{2}=&\frac{15}{4}\left[c^{6}+4c^{4}\nu\mu+4c^{4}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}-2c^{4}\nu\mu^{2}-4c^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{3}+c^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{4}\right.\\
&\left.+4\nu(c^{3}+2c\nu\mu-c\nu\mu^{2})(1-|\mu|^{2})\rho+4\nu^{2}(1-|\mu|^{2})^{2}\rho^{2}\right];\\
72c_{2}c_{4}-36c_{1}^{2}c_{4}=&\frac{9}{2}\left[c_{1}^{4}\nu\mu+3c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}+\left(4-3c_{1}^{2}\right)\nu^{2}\mu^{3}+c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{4}\right.\\
&+4\nu^{2}c_{1}\mu\left(1-\mu\right)\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho-4\nu^{2}\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)|\mu|^{2}\rho^{2}\\
&\left.+4\nu^{2}\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\left(1-|\rho|^{2}\right)\mu\psi\right].\end{split}

Imputting the values from (2.10) in the expression (2.9), after simplifying, we get

(2.13)

\begin{split}H_{3,1}(f^{-1})=\frac{1}{8640}&\left[\frac{3}{4}c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}-3c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{3}+\frac{3}{4}c_{1}^{2}\nu^{2}\mu^{4}-\frac{11}{2}\nu^{3}\mu^{3}+18\nu^{2}\mu^{3}\right.\\
&-\left(3c_{1}\nu^{2}\mu+3c_{1}\nu^{2}\mu^{2}\right)\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho-3\nu^{2}\left(5+|\mu|^{2}\right)\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho^{2}\\
&\left.+18\nu^{2}\mu\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\left.(1-|\rho|^{2}\right)\psi\right].\end{split}

Putting
u:=c_{1}
and taking
\nu=\left(4-u^{2}\right)
in (2.11), we obtain

(2.14)

\begin{split}H_{3,1}(f^{-1})=\frac{\left(4-u^{2}\right)^{2}}{8640}&\left[\frac{3}{4}u^{2}\mu^{2}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}\mu^{3}+\frac{3}{4}u^{2}\mu^{4}-(4-u^{2})\mu^{3}\right.\\
&-3u\mu\left(1+\mu\right)\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho-3\left(5+|\mu|^{2}\right)\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\rho^{2}\\
&\left.+18\mu\left(1-|\mu|^{2}\right)\left.(1-|\rho|^{2}\right)\psi\right].\end{split}

Taking modulus on both sides of (2.12), using
|\mu|=v\in[0,1]
,
|\rho|=w\in[0,1]
,
c_{1}=u\in[0,2]
and
|\psi|\leq 1
, we obtain

(2.15)

\bigg|H_{3,1}(f^{-1})\bigg|\leq\frac{\vartheta\left(u,v,w\right)}{8640},

where
\vartheta:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined as

(2.16)

\begin{split}\vartheta\left(u,v,w\right)=\left(4-u^{2}\right)^{2}&\left[\frac{3}{4}u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}v^{3}+\frac{3}{4}u^{2}v^{4}+\left(4-u^{2}\right)v^{3}\right.\\
&+3uv\left(1+v\right)\left(1-v^{2}\right)w+3\left(5+v^{2}\right)\left(1-v^{2}\right)w^{2}\\
&\left.+18v\left(1-v^{2}\right)\left.(1-w^{2}\right)\right]\end{split}

Now, we are making an attempt to maximize the function
\vartheta\left(u,v,w\right)
on

\Omega:=[0,2]\times[0,1]\times[0,1]
.

A.
On the vertices of
\Omega
, from (2.14), we get

\begin{split}&\vartheta\left(0,0,0\right)=\vartheta\left(2,0,0\right)=\vartheta\left(2,1,0\right)=\vartheta\left(2,0,1\right)=\vartheta\left(2,1,1\right)=0,\\
&\vartheta\left(0,0,1\right)=240,~\vartheta\left(0,1,0\right)=\vartheta\left(0,1,1\right)=64.\end{split}

B.
On the edges of
\Omega
, from (2.14), we have

(i) For the edge
u=0,~v=0,~0<w<1,
we obtain.

\vartheta\left(0,0,w\right)=240w^{2}\leq 240.

(ii) For the edge
u=0,~v=1,~0<w<1
, we obtain

\vartheta\left(0,1,w\right)=64.

(iii) For
u=0,~w=0,~0<v<1
,

\vartheta\left(0,v,0\right)=32v(9-7v^{2})\leq 192\sqrt{\frac{3}{7}},,~\text{for}~v=\sqrt{2}.

(iv) For
u=0,~w=1,~0<v<1
,

\vartheta\left(0,v,1\right)=240-192v^{2}+64v^{3}-48v^{4}\leq 240.

(v) For
v=0,~w=1,0<u<2
,

\vartheta\left(u,0,1\right)=15(4-u^{2})^{2}\leq 240.

(vi) For the edges:
v=1,~w=0,0<u<2
or
v=1,~w=1,0<u<2
, we have

\vartheta\left(u,1,w\right)=(4-u^{2})^{2}(4+2u^{2})\leq 64.

(vii) For the edges:
u=2,~v=0,~0<w<1
or
u=2,~v=1,~0<w<1
or

u=2,~w=0,~0<v<1
or
c=2,~w=1,~0<v<1
or
v=0,~w=0,~0<u<2
,

we obtain

\vartheta\left(2,v,w\right)=0.

C.
Now, we consider the six faces of
\Omega
.

(i) On the face
u=2
, from (2.14), we obtain

\vartheta\left(2,v,w\right)=0.

(ii) On the face
u=0,~v\in(0,1)~\text{and}~w\in(0,1)
from (2.14), we get

\begin{split}\vartheta\left(0,v,w\right)&=288v-224v^{3}+(240-288v-192v^{2}+288v^{3}-48v^{4})w^{2}\\
&=288v-224v^{3}+48(5-v)(-1+v)^{2}(1+v)w^{2}\\
&\leq 288v-224v^{3}+48(5-v)(-1+v)^{2}(1+v)\\
&=240-192v^{2}+64v^{3}-48v^{4}\leq 240.\end{split}

(iii) On the face
v=0~u\in(0,2),~w\in(0,1)
, from (2.14), we obtain

\vartheta\left(u,0,w\right)=15(4-u^{2})^{2}w^{2}\leq 15(4-u^{2})^{2}\leq 240.

(iv) On the face
v=1,~u\in(0,2),~w\in(0,1)
, from (2.14), we observe that the

function
\vartheta\left(u,1,w\right)
is independent of
w
, from
B
(vi), we have
\vartheta\left(u,1,w\right)\leq 240.

(v) On the face
w=0,~u\in(0,2),~v\in(0,1)
, from (2.14), we obtain

\begin{split}\vartheta\left(u,v,0\right)=(4-u^{2})^{2}&\left(\frac{3u^{2}v^{2}}{4}+\frac{3u^{2}v^{3}}{2}+(4-u^{2})v^{3}+\frac{3u^{2}v^{4}}{4}+18v(1-v^{2})\right)\\
=(4-u^{2})^{2}&\left(18v-14v^{3}+u^{2}\left(\frac{3v^{2}}{4}+\frac{v^{3}}{2}+\frac{3v^{4}}{4}\right)\right)\\
\leq(4-u^{2})^{2}&\left(12\sqrt{\frac{3}{7}}+2u^{2}\right)\leq 192\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{7}},~u\in(0,2).\end{split}

(vi) On the face
w=1,
in (2.14), we obtain

\displaystyle\vartheta\left(u,v,1\right)=(4-u^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{3}{4}u^{2}v^{2}+\frac{3}{2}u^{2}v^{3}+\frac{3}{4}u^{2}v^{4}+(4-u^{2})v^{3}

\displaystyle+3uv(1+v)(1-v^{2})+3(5+v^{2})\left(1-v^{2}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle:=g_{3}(u,v)

\displaystyle,~\text{with}~(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Note that all real solutions (u,v) of the system of equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{3}}{\partial u}=\frac{3}{2}(-4+u^{2})

\displaystyle\left[8(-1+v)v(1+v)^{2}-10u^{2}(-1+v)v(1+v)^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+u^{3}v^{2}(3+2v+3v^{2})-4u(-10+9v^{2}-2v^{3}+3v^{4})\right]=0

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g_{3}}{\partial v}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}(-4+u^{2})^{2}(-8v(2-v+v^{2})+u^{2}v(1+v+2v^{2})

\displaystyle+u(2+4v-6v^{2}-8v^{3}))=0

by a numerical computation are the following

(0,0),(-2.63625,-1.53087),(-1.0493,1.14045)~\text{and}~(\pm 2,x),x\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore,
g_{3}
has no critical point in
(0,2)\times(0,1).

D.
Now, consider the interior portion of
\Omega
i.e.
(0,2)\times(0,1)\times(0,1).

Differentiating
\vartheta(u,v,w)
partially with respect

, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\vartheta}{\partial w}=\frac{1}{2}(4-u^{2})^{2}&\left[60w^{2}+3v^{2}(u^{2}+4uw-16w^{2})+12v(6+uw-6w^{2})\right.\\
&\left.+3v^{4}(u^{2}-4uw-4w^{2})+2v^{3}(-28+u^{2}-6uw+36w^{2})\right]\end{split}

upon solving

, we get

Hence

has no critical point in the interior of

.

In review of cases

,

,

and

\max\bigg\{\vartheta(u,v,w):u\in[0,2],v\in[0,1],~w\in[0,1]\bigg\}=240.

From expression (2.13) and (2.15), we obtain

(2.18)

\Big|H_{3,1}(f^{-1})\Big|\leq\frac{1}{36}.

For

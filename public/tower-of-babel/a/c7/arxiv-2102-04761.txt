Quasi-Global Momentum: Accelerating Decentralized Deep Learning on Heterogeneous Data
Tao Lin, Sai Praneeth Karimireddy, Sebastian U. Stich, Martin Jaggi

Abstract
Decentralized training of deep learning models is a key element for enabling data privacy and on-device learning over networks. In realistic learning scenarios, the presence of heterogeneity across different clients’ local datasets poses an optimization challenge and may severely deteriorate the generalization performance. In this paper, we investigate and identify the limitation of several decentralized optimization algorithms for different degrees of data heterogeneity. We propose a novel momentum-based method to mitigate this decentralized training difficulty. We show in extensive empirical experiments on various CV/NLP datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and AG News) and several network topologies (Ring and Social Network) that our method is much more robust to the heterogeneity of clients’ data than other existing methods, by a significant improvement in test performance ( 1 % − 20 % 1\%\!-\!20\% ). Decentralized machine learning methods—allowing communications in a peer-to-peer fashion on an underlying communication network topology (without a central coordinator)—have emerged as an important paradigm
in large-scale machine learning
(
Lian et al. 2017
;
Lian et al. 2018
;
Koloskova et al. 2019
;
Koloskova et al. 2020b
)
.
Decentralized Stochastic Gradient Descent (DSGD) methods offer
(1) scalability to large datasets and systems in large data-centers
(
Lian et al. 2017
;
Assran et al. 2019
;
Koloskova et al. 2020a
)
,
as well as (2) privacy-preserving learning for the emerging EdgeAI applications
(
Kairouz et al. 2019
;
Koloskova et al. 2020a
)
,
where the training data remains distributed over a large number of clients
(e.g. mobile phones, sensors, or hospitals)
and is kept locally (never transmitted during training).

A key challenge—in particular in the second scenario—is the large heterogeneity (non-i.i.d.-ness)
in the data present on the different clients
(
Zhao et al. 2018
;
Kairouz et al. 2019
;
Hsieh et al. 2020
)
.
Heterogeneous data (e.g. as illustrated in Figure
1
)
causes very diverse optimization objectives on each client,
which results in slow and unstable global convergence, as well as poor generalization performance
(shown in the inline table of Figure
1
).
Addressing these optimization difficulties is essential to realize reliable decentralized deep learning applications. Although such challenges have been theoretically pointed out in
(
Shi et al. 2015
;
Lee et al. 2015
;
Tang et al. 2018b
;
Koloskova et al. 2020b
)
,
the empirical performance of different DSGD methods remains
poorly understood.
To the best of our knowledge,
there currently exists no efficient, effective, and robust optimization algorithm yet for decentralized deep learning on heterogeneous data.

In the meantime, SGD with momentum acceleration (SGDm)
remains the current workhorse for the state-of-the-art (SOTA)
centralized deep learning training
(
He et al. 2016
;
Goyal et al. 2017
;
He et al. 2019
)
.
For decentralized deep learning,
the currently used training recipes (i.e. DSGDm)
maintain a local momentum buffer on each worker
(
Assran et al. 2019
;
Koloskova et al. 2020a
;
Nadiradze et al. 2020
;
Singh et al. 2020
;
Kong et al. 2021
)

while only communicating the model parameters to the neighbors.
However, these attempts in prior work mainly consider homogeneous decentralized data—and there is no evidence that local momentum enhances generalization performance of decentralized deep learning on heterogeneous data.

As our first contribution, we investigate how DSGD and DSGDm are impacted by the degree of data heterogeneity and the choice of the network topology.
We find that heterogeneous data hinders the local momentum acceleration in DSGDm.
We further show that using a high-quality shared momentum buffer (e.g. synchronizing the momentum buffer globally) improves the optimization and generalization performance of DSGDm.
However, such a global communication significantly increases
the communication cost and violates the decentralized learning setup.

We instead propose Quasi-Global (QG) momentum,
a simple, yet effective, method that
mitigates the difficulties for decentralized learning on heterogeneous data.
Our approach is based on locally approximating the global optimization direction without introducing extra communication overhead.
We demonstrate in extensive empirical results that
QG momentum
can stabilize the optimization trajectory, and that
it can accelerate decentralized learning achieving much better generalization performance under high data heterogeneity than previous methods.

•

We systematically examine the behavior of decentralized optimization algorithms
on standard deep learning benchmarks for various degrees of data heterogeneity.

•

We propose a novel momentum-based decentralized optimization method—QG-DSGDm and QG-DSGDm-N—to stabilize the local optimization.
We validate the effectiveness of our method on a spectrum of non-i.i.d. degrees and network topologies—it is much more robust to the data heterogeneity than all other existing methods.

•

We rigorously prove the convergence of our scheme.

•

We additionally investigate different normalization methods
alternative to Batch Normalization
(BN)
(
Ioffe & Szegedy 2015
)
in CNNs,
due to its particular vulnerable to non-i.i.d. local data and the caused severe quality loss.

3.1
Notation and Setting

We consider sum-structured distributed optimization problems
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

\displaystyle f^{\star}:=\textstyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[f(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right]\,,

(1)

where the components
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are distributed among the
n
nodes
and are given in stochastic form:

f_{i}(\mathbf{x}):={\mathbb{E}}_{\xi\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}}\left[F_{i}(\mathbf{x},\xi)\right]
,
where
\mathcal{D}_{i}
denotes the local data distribution on node
i\in[n]
.
In D(ecentralized)SGD,
each node
i
maintains local parameters
\smash{\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and updates them as:

\textstyle\textstyle\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t+1)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(t)}-\eta\nabla F_{j}(\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(t)},\xi_{j}^{(t)})\right)\,,

(DSGD)

that is, by a stochastic gradient step based on a sample
\xi_{i}^{(i)}\sim\mathcal{D}_{i}
,
followed by gossip averaging with neighboring nodes in the network topology
encoded by the mixing weights
w_{ij}
.

In this paper,
we denote DSGD with local HeavyBall momentum by DSGDm,
and DSGD with local Nesterov momentum by DSGDm-N;
the naming rule also applies to our method.
For the sake of simplicity,
we use HeavyBall momentum variants in Section
3
and
4
for analysis purposes.

3.3
Convergence Analysis

We provide a convergence analysis for our novel QG-DSGDm for non-convex functions.
The proof details can be found in Appendix
C
.

Assumption 1
.

We assume that the following hold:

•

The function
f(\mathbf{x})
we are minimizing is lower bounded from below by
f^{\star}
, and each node’s loss
f_{i}
is smooth satisfying
\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{y})\!-\!\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\!-\!\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
.

•

The stochastic gradients within each node satisfy
{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right]\!=\!\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})

and
{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\!-\!\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
.
The variance across the workers is also bounded as
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\!-\!\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\zeta^{2}
.

•

The mixing matrix is doubly stochastic: for the all ones vector
\mathbf{1}
,
we have
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}\!=\!\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{1}\!=\!\mathbf{1}
.

•

Define
\bar{\mathbf{Z}}\!=\!\mathbf{Z}\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
for any matrix
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
, then the mixing matrix satisfies
{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{W}}\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{W}-\bar{\mathbf{Z}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq(1-\rho)\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}-\bar{\mathbf{Z}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
.

Theorem 3.1

(Convergence of QG-DSGDm for non-convex functions)
.

Given Assumption
1
,
the sequence of iterates generated by (
3
)
for step size
\eta\!=\!\smash{\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n}{\sigma^{2}T}}\right)}

and momentum parameter
\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\leq\frac{\rho}{21}
satisfies
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\epsilon
in iterations

\textstyle T=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{L\sigma^{2}}{n\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{L\tilde{\zeta}}{\rho\epsilon^{3/2}}+\frac{L}{\epsilon}(\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)^{2}})\Big)\,,

where
\tilde{\zeta}^{2}:=\zeta^{2}+(1+\frac{1-\beta}{1-\mu})\sigma^{2}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The asymptotic number of iterations required,
\mathcal{O}\bigl(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n\epsilon^{2}}\bigr)
shows perfect linear speedup in the number of workers
n
, independent of the communication topology. This upper bound matches the convergence bounds of DSGD
(
Lian et al. 2017
)
and centralized mini-batch SGD
(
Dekel et al. 2012
)
, and is optimal
(
Arjevani et al. 2019
)
. This significantly improves over previous analyses of distributed momentum methods which need
\frac{L\sigma^{2}}{n(1-\beta)\epsilon^{2}}
iterations, slowing down for larger values of
\beta

(
Yu et al. 2019
;
Balu et al. 2020
)
. The second
drift
term
\frac{1}{\rho\epsilon^{3/2}}
arises due to the non-iid data distribution, and matches the tightest analysis of DSGD without momentum
(
Koloskova et al. 2020b
)
. Finally, our theorem imposes some constraint on the momentum parameter
\beta
(but not on
\mu
). In practice however, QG-DSGDm performs well even when this constraint is violated.

Connection with MimeLite.

MimeLite
(

)

The difference falls into the way of forming

MimeLite only addresses the FL setting,
which results in a computation and communication overhead (to form
\mathbf{d}
),
and is non-trivial to extend to decentralized learning.

SlowMo and its “noaverage” variant
(
Wang et al. 2020c
)
aim to improve generalization performance
in the homogeneous data-center training scenario,
while QG-DSGDm is targeting learning with data heterogeneity.
In terms of update scheme,
SlowMo variants update the slow momentum buffer through the model difference
\mathbf{d}

of
\tau\!\gg\!1
local update (and synchronization) steps,
while QG-DSGDm only considers consecutive models (analogously
\tau\!=\!1
)

3

3

in Appendix

4.2

Considering the single worker case,

\textstyle\begin{split}\textstyle\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&\textstyle=\hat{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\\

Conclusion

We demonstrated that heterogeneity has an out sized impact on the performance of deep learning models, leading to unstable convergence and poor performance.
We proposed a novel momentum-based algorithm to stabilize the training and established its efficacy through thorough empirical evaluations.
Our method, especially for mildly heterogeneous settings, leads to a 10–20% increase in accuracy.
However, a gap still remains between the centralized training. Closing this gap, we believe, is critical for wider adoption of decentralized learning.

B.3
Connections Between SGDm and QG-SGDm

B.3.1
Connections Between SGDm and QG-DSGDm

Note our scheme QG-DSGDm on the single worker case (i.e. QG-SGDm) has the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\right)\\
\mathbf{m}^{t}&=\mu\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\mu)\frac{\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}}{\eta}=\left(\mu+(1-\mu)\beta\right)\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\,.\end{split}

By letting
\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}:=\frac{\mathbf{m}^{(t)}}{1-\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta(1-\mu)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\right)\\
\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&=\left(\mu+(1-\mu)\beta\right)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\,.\end{split}

We further let
\hat{\beta}:=\mu+(1-\mu)\beta
, then we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\hat{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}+\left(\beta(1-\mu)-\hat{\beta}\right)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}\right)=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\hat{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}-\mu\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}\right)\\
\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&=\hat{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\,.\end{split}

By re-organizing, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&=\hat{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}-\mu\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}\right)=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}-\mu\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}\right)=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left((1-\frac{\mu}{\hat{\beta}})\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}+\frac{\mu}{\hat{\beta}}\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\right)\,,\end{split}

(5)

which recovers the QHM
(
Gitman et al. 2019
)
.

Comparing to the case of SGD with Heavy-ball Momentum (SGDm), where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&=\beta\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\mathbf{g}^{(t)}\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}\,,\end{split}

we can witness that SGDm is only a special case of (
5
) (when
\mu\!=\!0
).

B.3.2
Connections Between SGDm-N and QG-SGDm

First note that our simplified version of QG-DSGDm (i.e. QG-SGDm) can recover the QHM
(
Gitman et al. 2019
)
,
as illustrated in Appendix
B.3.1
.
Furthermore, as pointed out in
(
Gitman et al. 2019
)
that,
the QHM is indeed equivalent to the original SGDm-N
with re-scaling of
\eta\rightarrow\eta/(1-\beta)
.
Therefore, we can argue that our simplified version of QG-DSGDm (i.e. QG-SGDm or QHM)
is equivalent to the original SGDm-N with re-scaling of
\eta\rightarrow\eta/(1-\beta)
.

For the reason of completeness, we include the derivatives below.

First of all, SGD with Nesterov Momentum (SGDm-N) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}\\
\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})}-\eta\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\,,\end{split}

and in PyTorch, we instead have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}\\
\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\,,\end{split}

(6)

where the above equations are equivalent for the constant
\beta
.

Then we reiterate the derivatives from
Gitman et al. 2019
below,
from SGDm-N to QHM (which is equivalently Equation (
5
)).
The SGDm-N in
Gitman et al. 2019
follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}-\eta\nabla f\left(\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}\right)\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\,,\end{split}

where we can move the learning rate out of the momentum into the iterates update:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+\nabla f\left(\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}\right)\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\,,\end{split}

(7)

where the above two methods produce the same sequence of iterates
\mathbf{x}^{(t)}

if
\mathbf{m}^{(0)}
is initialized at
\mathbf{0}
.
The second equation (
7
)
is equivalent to the Pytorch implementation in Equation (
6
).

Let’s normalize the momentum update by
1-\beta
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\beta)\nabla f\left(\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}\right)\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\,,\end{split}

which is equivalent to the un-normalized one by re-scaling
\eta\rightarrow\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}

for constant parameters.
We make a change of variables
\mathbf{y}^{(t)}=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\beta)\nabla f(\mathbf{y}^{(t)})\\
\mathbf{y}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\\
&=\mathbf{y}^{(t)}+\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}-\eta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\\
&=\mathbf{y}^{(t)}+\eta\left(\mathbf{m}^{(t)}-(1-\beta)\nabla f(\mathbf{y}^{(t)})\right)-\eta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\\
&=\mathbf{y}^{(t)}-\eta\left((1-\beta)\nabla f(\mathbf{y}^{(t)})+\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t)}\right)\,,\end{split}

where by renaming
\mathbf{y}^{(t)}
back to
\mathbf{x}^{(t)}
, we obtain the exact formula used in QHM update.

B.3.3
The Simplification of QG-DSGDm-N on Single Worker Case

We can further simplify our scheme QG-DSGDm-N on the single worker case (and obtain QG-SGDm-N):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}\\
\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&=\beta\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}\\
\mathbf{m}^{(t)}&=\mu\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\mu)\frac{\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}}{\eta}\,.\end{split}

We rewrite
\mathbf{m}^{(t)}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\mu\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\mu)\frac{\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}}{\eta}=\mu\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\mu)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu+\beta-\mu\beta\right)\mathbf{m}^{(t-1)}+(1-\mu)\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\,.

Similar to the treatment in Appendix
B.3.1
,
by letting
\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}:=\frac{\mathbf{m}^{(t)}}{1-\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta(1-\mu)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\right)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu+\beta-\mu\beta\right)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\,,

and thus, in the end we have the following equations for QG-SGDm-N

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}+\beta(1-\mu)\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}\\
\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}&=\hat{\beta}\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t-1)}+\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\\
\mathbf{x}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{x}^{(t)}-\eta\left((1-\frac{\mu}{\hat{\beta}})\hat{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}+\frac{\mu}{\hat{\beta}}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t+\frac{1}{2})})\right)\,,\end{split}

where
\hat{\beta}:=\mu+(1-\mu)\beta
and we can recover SGDm-N by setting
\mu=0
.

Appendix C
Global Convergence Rate Proofs

We reiterate the update scheme of QG-DSGDm in a matrix form:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{X}^{(t+1)}&=\mathbf{W}\left(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\mathbf{M}^{(t)}+\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right)\right)\\
\mathbf{M}^{(t+1)}&=\mu\mathbf{M}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\frac{\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\mathbf{X}^{(t+1)}}{\eta}\\
&=\left(\mu+(1-\mu)\beta\mathbf{W}\right)\mathbf{M}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}\mathbf{G}^{(t)}+\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}\,,\end{split}

(8)

where our numerical experiments by default use
\mu=\beta
.
For each matrix
\mathbf{Z}
, we define an averaged vector
\bar{\mathbf{z}}=\mathbf{Z}\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}

and matrix
\bar{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathbf{Z}\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
.
Note that we use bold lower-case to indicate vectors and bold upper-case to denote matrices.

First, we state some standard definitions and regularity conditions.

Assumption 2
.

We assume that the following hold:

1.

The function
f(\mathbf{x})
we are minimizing is lower bounded from below by
f^{\star}
,
and each node’s loss
f_{i}
is smooth satisfying

\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{y})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert\leq L\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rVert
.

The
L
-smoothness implies the following quadratic upper bound on
f_{i}

\displaystyle f_{i}(\mathbf{y})\leq f_{i}(\mathbf{x})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

If additionally the function
f
is convex and
\mathbf{x}^{\star}
is an optimum of
f
, then it also implies

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\right\rVert\leq 2L\left(f(\mathbf{x})-f^{\star}\right)\,.

2.

The stochastic gradients within each node satisfies
{\mathbb{E}}\left[g_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right]=\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})

and
{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert g_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
.
The variance across the workers is also bounded as

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x})-\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert^{2}\leq\zeta^{2}
.

3.

The mixing matrix is doubly stochastic where for the all ones vector
\mathbf{1}
,
we have
\mathbf{W}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{W}^{\top}\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}
.
Further, define
\bar{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathbf{Z}\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
for any matrix
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
.
Then, the mixing matrix satisfies

{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{W}}\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{W}-\bar{\mathbf{Z}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq(1-\rho)\left\lVert\mathbf{Z}-\bar{\mathbf{Z}}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
.

Average parameters.

Let us examine the effect of updates in (
8
)
on
\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}
which is the parameters averaged across the nodes.
Note that since
\mathbf{W}
is doubly stochastic, we can simplify the updates as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}&=\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}+\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\right)\text{, and }\\
\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(t+1)}&=\mu\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\frac{\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}}{\eta}\\
&=(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta))\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\,.\end{split}

(9)

Here,
\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla F_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)},\xi_{i}^{(t)})

is the average of the stochastic gradients across the nodes.

Virtual Sequence.

Now we define a virtual sequence of parameters
\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\}
which has a simple SGD style update,
and an error sequence which will be easy to analyze:

\begin{split}\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}&=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\text{, and}\\
\bar{\mathbf{e}}^{(t)}&:=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\,.\end{split}

(10)

Our strategy for the analysis will be to analyze the virtual sequence
\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\}

and prove that the real sequence of iterates remains close i.e. that the
\mathbf{e}^{(t)}
remains small.

Single-step progress of virtual update.

We will show that every step we make some progress, but have to balance three sources of error:
i) the stochastic error which depends on
\sigma^{2}
due to using stochastic gradients,
ii) consensus error which depends on
\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}
, and finally
iii) momentum error due to using momentum which depends on
\mathbf{e}^{(t)}
.

Lemma C.1

(Non-convex one step progress)
.

Given Assumption
2
,
the sequence of iterates generated by (
8
)
using
\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{4L}
satisfy

{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)})\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\,,

where we define
\tilde{\eta}:=\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}
.

Proof.

Starting from the smoothness of
f
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})+{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}),\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rangle+\frac{L}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}),\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rangle+\frac{L\eta^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\tilde{\eta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}),\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rangle+L\tilde{\eta}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})+L\tilde{\eta}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})+(L\tilde{\eta}^{2}-\tilde{\eta}/2){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}-\tilde{\eta}/4{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{3\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})-\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}\,.

In the last inequality we used our bound on the step-size that
\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{4L}
.
In the rest of the inequalities, we repeatedly use the identity that
2ab=-a^{2}-b^{2}+(a-b)^{2}
.
Finally, using the smoothness of the function
f
and the definition
\tilde{\eta}:=\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2}\left\lVert\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Recalling the definition of
\mathbf{e}^{(t)}
and
\mathbf{X}^{(t)}
yields the lemma.
∎

Lemma C.2

(Strongly-convex one step progress)
.

Suppose that the set of functions
\{f_{i}\}
are
\mu
-strongly convex in addition to Assumption
2
. Then the sequence of iterates generated by (
8
) using
\eta\leq\frac{1-\beta}{4L}
satisfy for
\tilde{\eta}:=\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}
,

{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1-\mu\tilde{\eta}/2){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}-3\tilde{\eta}/4(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-f^{\star})+8L\tilde{\eta}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{5L\tilde{\eta}}{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\,.

Proof.

Starting from the update rule for
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}
, and expanding very similar to the steps performed in the previous lemma we get,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}+2{\mathbb{E}}\left\langle\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rangle+{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{2\tilde{\eta}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}),\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})-\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\tilde{\eta}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-2\tilde{\eta}\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}),\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rangle}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}L^{2}}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+2L\tilde{\eta}^{2}{\mathbb{E}}(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-f^{\star})+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}\,.

We will examine the term
\mathcal{T}_{1}
now. Using strong convexity and smoothness of each of the functions
\{f_{i}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}),\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\frac{\mu}{2}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\frac{\mu}{4}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\frac{\mu}{4}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\mu}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}-\mu\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\frac{\mu}{4}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{L}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}-L\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}\,,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}),\bar{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rangle\geq f(\bar{\mathbf{x}})-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-\frac{L}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}\,,

and finally,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}),\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\pm\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right),\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{8L}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\pm\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}))\right\rVert^{2}-2L\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{4Ln}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{4L}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\right\rVert^{2}-2L\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{L}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}})-f(\mathbf{x}^{\star}))-3L\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\right\rVert^{2}\,,

where the first inequality uses the basic inequality
\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\lVert\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2},\forall\beta>0
, and the second inequality uses the relaxed triangle inequality
\left\lVert\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+\alpha)\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert^{2}+(1+\alpha^{-1})\left\lVert\mathbf{b}\right\rVert^{2},\forall\alpha>0
.

Adding up the three inequalities together yields the following expression for the term
\mathcal{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}),\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-f(\mathbf{x}^{\star}))+\frac{\mu}{4}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{2L}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}-4L\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}\,.

Plugging this back into the previous inequality and using
\tilde{\eta}\leq\frac{1}{4L}
finishes the proof of the lemma.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\tilde{\eta}\mu/2){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{x}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}-\tilde{\eta}(1-2L\tilde{\eta}){\mathbb{E}}(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-f^{\star})+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{\tilde{\eta}^{2}L^{2}+4\tilde{\eta}L}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+8L\tilde{\eta}\left\lVert\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}\,.\qed

Bounding the consensus error.

We will now try to bound the consensus error
(\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t})
between the node’s parameters and its average.
During each step, we perform a diffusion step (communication with neighbors) which brings the parameters of the nodes closer to each other.
However we also perform additional gradient/momentum steps which moves the distance away from each other.

Lemma C.3

(One step consensus change)
.

Given Assumption
2
, the sequence of iterates generated by (
8
)
using
\eta\leq\frac{\rho}{7L}
satisfy for
\tilde{\eta}:=\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}
,

\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\leq\frac{(1-\rho/4)}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{12\eta^{2}\zeta^{2}}{\rho}+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}+\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\,.

Proof.

Starting from the update step (
8
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\left(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\mathbf{M}^{(t)}+\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right)\right)-\left(\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}+\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right)\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\rho}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\mathbf{M}^{(t)}+\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right)\right)-\left(\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}+\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right)\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1-\rho}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\mathbf{M}^{(t)}+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right]\right)\right)-\left(\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}-\eta\left(\beta\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}+{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right]\right)\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-\rho)(1+\rho/2)}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right]-{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right]\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}\,.

Here we used the contractivity of the mixing matrix and Young’s inequality. We can proceed as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-\rho/2)}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}_{t}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right]-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\rho/2)}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{6\eta^{2}}{\rho n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\pm\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-\rho/2)}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{12\eta^{2}}{\rho n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})-\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{12\eta^{2}}{\rho n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f_{i}(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-\rho/2)}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+4(1-\rho)\eta^{2}\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{12\eta^{2}L^{2}}{\rho n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{12\eta^{2}\zeta^{2}}{\rho}\,.

Our assumption that the step size
\eta\leq\frac{\rho}{7L}
ensures that
12\eta^{2}L^{2}\leq\rho^{2}/4
, finishing the proof.
∎

We will now try to bound the momentum error
(\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t})
between the momentum on each node and its average across nodes.

Lemma C.4

(One step momentum change)
.

Given Assumption
2
, the sequence of iterates generated by (
8
)
using momentum satisfying
\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\leq\frac{\rho}{21}
,

\displaystyle\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}-\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert(\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{\rho}{8n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta^{2}\rho\zeta^{2}}{8}+\frac{\eta^{2}\rho\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{8(1-\mu)}\,.

Proof.

Starting from the update step (
8
) and proceeding similar to the previous lemma, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(\mu\mathbf{I}+(1-\mu)\beta\mathbf{W}\right)(\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)})+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)})+\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(\mu\mathbf{I}+(1-\mu)\beta\mathbf{W}\right)(\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)})+\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right])\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}\left(\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right]-(\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right])\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(\mu\mathbf{I}+(1-\mu)\beta\mathbf{W}\right)(\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)})+\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right])\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(1+\frac{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(\mu\mathbf{I}+(1-\mu)\beta\mathbf{W}\right)(\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+\frac{1}{n}\left(1+\frac{1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right]\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\,.

Note that since
\mathbf{W}\prec\mathbf{I}
, we have
\left(\mu\mathbf{I}+(1-\mu)\beta\mathbf{W}\right)\prec(\mu+(1-\mu)\beta)\mathbf{I}=(1-(1-\beta)(1-\mu))\mathbf{I}
. Further, since
-\mathbf{I}\prec\mathbf{W}
, we have
\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W}\prec 2\mathbf{I}
. With these observations, we can continue

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(1+\frac{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\left(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)\right)(\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)})\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+\frac{1}{n}\left(1+\frac{1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right]\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+\frac{1}{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1-\mu}{\eta}(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{W})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}+(1-\mu)\mathbf{W}\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right]\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+\frac{4(1-\mu)}{(1-\beta)n\eta^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{2(1-\mu)}{(1-\beta)n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right]-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\,.

From the proof of the previous lemma, we can simplify the last term as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{4(1-\mu)}{n\eta^{2}(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{2(1-\mu)}{n(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}\left[\mathbf{G}^{(t)}\right]\pm\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)})-\bar{\mathbf{G}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{4(1-\mu)}{n\eta^{2}(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{8(1-\mu)\zeta^{2}}{(1-\beta)}+\frac{4(1-\mu)L^{2}}{n(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+4\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{4(1-\mu)(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{n\eta^{2}(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\frac{8(1-\mu)\zeta^{2}}{(1-\beta)}

Multiplying both sides by
\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\hskip-56.9055pt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}-\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{48\beta^{2}(1+L^{2}\eta^{2})}{n\rho(1-\beta)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{24\eta^{2}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}+\frac{48\eta^{2}\beta^{2}\zeta^{2}}{\rho(1-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}-\frac{6\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{n\rho}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{\rho}{8n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{\eta^{2}\rho\zeta^{2}}{8}+\frac{\eta^{2}\rho\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{8(1-\mu)}\,.

The last step follows from our assumption that the momentum parameter that
\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\leq\frac{\rho}{21}
and
\eta\leq\frac{1}{7L}
. This ensures that
\frac{48\beta^{2}(1+L^{2}\eta^{2})}{\rho(1-\beta)^{2}}\leq\frac{49\beta^{2}}{\rho(1-\beta)^{2}}\leq\frac{\rho}{8}
.
∎

Average and virtual sequences.

We will now bound the difference between the average and the virtual sequences
\mathbf{e}^{(t)}
which recall was defined to be
\mathbf{e}^{(t)}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}
. The latter runs SGD with momentum whereas the former only runs SGD. We view the momentum terms as accumulating the gradient terms, delayed over time and hence the proof views SGDm as simply SGD run with a larger step-size.

Lemma C.6

(One step error contraction)
.

Given Assumption
2
, the sequence of iterates generated by (
8
) satisfy

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2\tilde{\eta}^{2}\beta^{2}}{(1-\beta)(1-\mu)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{t}]\right\rVert^{2}+\tilde{\eta}^{2}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}\,.

Proof.

By definition,
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(0)}=\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(0)}
and hence we have
\mathbf{e}^{(0)}=0
. For
t\geq 0
, starting from the definition of the error term we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\frac{\eta}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\right)-\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)}-\eta(\beta\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)}+\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}^{(t)}-\eta\beta(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(t)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t}-\eta\beta(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(k)})\,.

Using the update of the average momentum (
9
), we can write

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{(t)}-\eta\beta(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{(t)}-\eta\beta\left(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k)}-\left((1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta))\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(k-1)}+(1-\mu)\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k-1)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta))\sum_{k=0}^{t}-\eta\beta\left(\frac{1}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k-1)}-\bar{\mathbf{m}}^{(k-1)}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{(t)}-\frac{\eta\beta}{1-\beta}\left(\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(k-1)}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta))\mathbf{e}^{(t)}-\frac{\eta\beta}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\,.

By convention, we assume that vectors with negative indices are 0. Taking norms and expectations gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta))\mathbf{e}^{(t)}-\frac{\eta\beta}{1-\beta}\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta))\mathbf{e}^{(t)}-\frac{\eta\beta}{1-\beta}{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}]\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1-(1-\mu)(1-\beta)){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{2\eta^{2}\beta^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{3}(1-\mu)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}]\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{\eta^{2}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}}\,.\qed

Convergence rate for non-convex case.

Theorem C.7
.

Given Assumption
2
, the sequence of iterates generated by (
8
) for step size
\eta=\min\left(\frac{\rho}{7L},\frac{1-\beta}{4L},\frac{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)^{2}}{4\beta L},\sqrt{\frac{4n(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})}{L\sigma^{2}T}}\right)
and momentum parameter
\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\leq\frac{\rho}{21}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})\right\rVert\leq\mathcal{O}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{L\sigma^{2}(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})}{nT}}\ +

\displaystyle\sqrt[3]{L^{2}(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})^{2}\frac{\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}T^{2}}}\ +

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\rho}+\frac{1}{1-\beta}+\frac{\beta}{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)^{2}}\right)\frac{L(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})}{T}\Bigg)\,.

Proof.

Define
\tilde{\zeta}^{2}:=\zeta^{2}+\sigma^{2}\left(1+\frac{1-\beta}{1-\mu}\right)
. Scaling Lemma
C.5
by
\frac{24L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{\rho}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{24L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{144L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}(1-\beta)}{n\rho^{2}(1-\mu)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{t+1}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{t+1}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\hskip-227.62204pt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{24L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{144L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}(1-\beta)}{n\rho^{2}(1-\mu)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{312L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}(1-\beta)^{2}\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\,.

Scaling Lemma
C.6
by
\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t+1)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}(1-\mu)^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert{\mathbb{E}}_{t}[\bar{\mathbf{g}}^{t}]\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}\,.

Finally Lemma
C.1
gives

{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t+1)})\leq{\mathbb{E}}f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}\,.

Define

\Phi^{t}:=\frac{24L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{\rho n}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{X}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{144L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}(1-\beta)}{n\rho^{2}(1-\mu)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{M}^{t}-\bar{\mathbf{M}}^{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}}{2(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}^{(t)}\right\rVert^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}[f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{t})-f^{\star}]\,.

Note that
\Phi^{0}={\mathbb{E}}[f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})]-f^{\star}
and that
\Phi^{t}\geq 0
for any
t
. Then adding the three inequalities from the lemmas as described above gives

\displaystyle\Phi^{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi^{t}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}\left\lVert\nabla f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}+\left(\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}(1-\mu)^{2}}-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}\right){\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla f_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{n}+\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2(1-\mu)(1-\beta)}+\frac{312L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}(1-\beta)^{2}\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\,.

Since,
\eta\leq\frac{(1-\mu)(1-\beta)^{2}}{4\beta L}
, we have that
\frac{3L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{3}\beta^{2}}{(1-\beta)^{2}(1-\mu)^{2}}\leq\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{4}
. Rearranging the terms and averaging over
t
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}{\mathbb{E}}\left\lVert\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{(t)})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\tilde{\eta}T}(\Phi^{0}-\Phi^{T})+\frac{L\tilde{\eta}\sigma^{2}}{n}+\frac{6L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{2}\beta^{2}\sigma^{2}}{(1-\mu)}+\frac{1248L^{2}\tilde{\eta}^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\eta}T}4(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})+\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{L\sigma^{2}}{n}\right)+\tilde{\eta}^{2}\left(\frac{L^{2}\rho^{2}\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{(1-\mu)}+\frac{1248L^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)\,.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\eta}T}4(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})+\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{L\sigma^{2}}{n}\right)+\tilde{\eta}^{2}\left(\frac{L^{2}\sigma^{2}(1-\beta)}{(1-\mu)}+\frac{1248L^{2}\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\eta}T}4(f(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{0})-f^{\star})+\tilde{\eta}\left(\frac{L\sigma^{2}}{n}\right)+\tilde{\eta}^{2}\left(\frac{1249L^{2}\tilde{\zeta}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\right)\,.

Choosing an appropriate steps-size
\eta
proves the theorem.
∎

D.9
Comparison with D
2
and Gradient Tracking (GT) methods

We comment on GT methods below (including D
2

(
Tang et al. 2018b
)
), in order to 1) highlight the distinctions between different algorithms, and 2) justify the comparison with existing GT methods.

•

Distinctions between algorithms:
1) Both D
2
and GT do not consider momentum in their algorithm design and theoretical analysis, while one of our main contributions is the design of quasi-global momentum—a simple yet effective approach for the SOTA decentralized deep learning training;
2) It is unclear how to integrate D
2
with momentum, given the original design intuition of D
2
;
3) QG-DSGDm is different from D
2
, where the updates of QG-DSGDm and D
2
follow
\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}((1+\beta\frac{\eta^{(t)}}{\eta^{(t-1)}})\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\beta\frac{\eta^{(t)}}{\eta^{(t-1)}}\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)}-\eta^{(t)}\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}))
and
\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}(2\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)}-\eta(\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)})))
respectively (we simplify the comparison by letting
\mu\!=\!0
in QG-DSGDm);
4) Compared to QG-DSGDm, GT requires extra one communication step per update to approximate the global average of local gradients.

•

D
2
cannot achieve comparable test performance on the standard deep learning benchmark.

–

D
2
requires a constant learning rate, which does not fit the SOTA learning rate schedule (e.g. stage-wise) in deep learning.
Note that D
2
can be rewritten as
\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta((\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)}-\mathbf{X}^{(t)})/\eta+\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)})))
, and the update would break if the magnitude of
\scriptstyle\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)}-\mathbf{X}^{(t)}
is a factor of
10\eta
(i.e. performing learning rate decay at step
t
).

–

It is non-trivial to improve D
2
for the SOTA deep learning training.
To support our argument, Table
2
compares to an improved D
2
variant (noted as D
{}^{2}_{+}
) to address the issue of learning rate decay in D
2
(though it breaks the design intuition of D
2
); the performance of D
{}^{2}_{+}
is far behind our scheme.
The update of D
{}^{2}_{+}
follows
\scriptstyle\mathbf{W}(\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\eta^{(t)}((\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)}-\mathbf{X}^{(t)})/\eta^{(t-1)}+\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t)})-\nabla f(\mathbf{X}^{(t-1)})))
.

–

The numerical results of D
2
in
Tang et al. 2018b
;
Pan et al. 2020
;
Lu et al. 2019
cannot support the practicability of D
2
:
1) The experiments of
Tang et al. 2018b
;
Pan et al. 2020
only consider a very small scale setup (# of nodes
n\!=\!5
or
8
) for CIFAR-10, while
(
Lu et al. 2019
)
only evaluates on the toy MNIST dataset—these setups are much less challenging than ours;
2) Only training loss curves are reported in
Tang et al. 2018b
;
Pan et al. 2020
;
Lu et al. 2019
, and the final training loss values of
Tang et al. 2018b
;
Pan et al. 2020
are much higher than
0
(i.e. not converge to a local minimum).

•

As suggested by anonymous reviewers, we compare with GT methods in Table
2
: QG-DSGDm-N outperforms GT by a large margin.
We would like to point out that
1) the observations of the marginal performance gain in GT are aligned with prior works, e.g. similar numerical results in Figure 3 & 4 & 5 of
Xin et al. 2020
;
2) GT may have more benefits in the extreme low training loss regime (e.g. less than
1e{-}4
) where gains might increase when combining with variance reduction techniques
(
Xin et al. 2020
)
—however, we focus on the test performance for deep learning
(
Defazio & Bottou 2019
)
;
3) recent work
(
Yuan et al. 2020b
)
also proves that gradient tracking methods are in general much more sensitive than diffusion-based methods.

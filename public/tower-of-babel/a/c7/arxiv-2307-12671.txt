The finitistic dimension of a triangulated category
Henning Krause

Abstract
. The finitistic dimension of a triangulated category is introduced. For the category of perfect complexes over a ring it is shown that this dimension is finite if and only if the small finitistic dimension of the ring is finite.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
18G80 (primary), 16E10 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

The finitistic dimension of a ring is a homological invariant which is
conjectured to be finite for any finite dimensional algebra over a
field
[
2
]
. Despite many efforts the conjecture remains
open. So it seems appropriate to take a step back in order to
understand this homological invariant beyond the specific context of
modules over a finite dimensional algebra. This is precisely a
situation where triangulated categories turn out to be useful, because
they enable the transfer of concepts.

Let us consider the category of perfect complexes over a noetherian
scheme. A recent result of Neeman characterises the existence of
bounded t-structures
[
13
]
, confirming a conjecture of
Antieau, Gepner, and Heller
[
1
]
. This requires the scheme
to be of finite dimension, and an analysis of the proof reveals that
there is an analogue of this theorem in a non-commutative setting. It
turns out that the condition on the scheme to be of finite dimension
translates into the finiteness of the finitistic dimension. Thus a
unified proof requires a definition of finitistic dimension for
triangulated categories, and this is precisely what this paper
offers. That there is such a concept is not surprising, because it is
well known that the finiteness of the finitistic dimension of a ring
is a derived invariant, even though the integer values of this
dimension in a derived equivalence class may differ
[
15
]
.

The purpose of this note is to describe the finiteness of the
finitistic dimension of a ring as a property of the triangulated
category of perfect complexes. To this end we introduce for any
triangulated category its finitistic dimension. This is in the spirit
of Rouquier’s dimension
[
17
]
; for a wide class of rings its
finiteness characterises regularity. Note that we focus on the small
finitistic dimension. The big finitistic dimension is discussed in an
appendix, where it is shown that its finiteness is an invariant of the
category of perfect complexes. This requires that the ring is an Artin
algebra and it is based on recent work of Rickard
[
16
]
.

For another definition of finitistic dimension for triangulated
categories we refer to
[
3
]
; this is much closer to what
is needed for the existence of bounded t-structures.

2.
The finitistic dimension of a triangulated category

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with suspension
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{T}
. From
[
4
,
17
]
we recall the following
definition. For an object
X
in
\mathcal{T}
and
n\geq 0
set

\operatorname{thick}^{n}(X):=\begin{cases}\operatorname{add}\varnothing&n=0,\\
\operatorname{add}\{\Sigma^{i}X\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}\}&n=1,\\
\operatorname{add}\{\operatorname{cone}\phi\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{thick}^{1}(X),\operatorname{thick}^{n-1}(X))&n>1,\end{cases}

where
\operatorname{add}\mathcal{X}
denotes the smallest full subcategory containing
\mathcal{X}

that is closed under finite direct sums and direct summands. Also, set

\operatorname{thick}(X):=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\operatorname{thick}^{n}(X).

The triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is

finitely generated
when
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{thick}(X)
for some object
X
, and
\mathcal{T}
is
strongly
finitely generated
if its
dimension

\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{T}:=\inf\{n\geq 0\mid\exists X\in\mathcal{T}\colon\operatorname{thick}^{n+1}(X)=\mathcal{T}\}

is finite.

We continue with some further notation. For a pair of objects
X,Y
in

\mathcal{T}
and
n\geq 0
we write
h(X,Y)\leq n
when for any pair
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}

\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{i}Y)\neq 0\neq\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{j}Y)\;\;\implies\;\;|i-j|<n.

We set

\hom^{n}(X):=\{Y\in\mathcal{T}\mid h(X,Y)\leq n\}.

The
amplitude
of
X
is

\operatorname{amp}(X):=\sup\{|n|\geq 0\mid\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{n}X)\neq 0\}.

We record some elementary properties of
\hom^{n}(X)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
X\in\mathcal{T}
and
n\geq 0
we have

(1)

\hom^{0}(X)=\{Y\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{i}Y)=0\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,

(2)

\hom^{1}(X)=\{Y\in\mathcal{T}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{i}Y)\neq 0\text{ for at most one }i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
,

(3)

\hom^{n}(X)=\hom^{n}(\Sigma^{i}X)
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(4)

\hom^{n}(X)=\hom^{n+|i|}(X\oplus\Sigma^{i}X)
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(5)

\hom^{n}(X)\subseteq\hom^{n}(Y)
for each
Y\in\operatorname{add}X
,

(6)

\hom^{n}(X)\subseteq\hom^{n}(Y)
for each exact triangle
Y^{\prime}\to Y\to Y^{\prime\prime}\to\Sigma Y^{\prime}
with
Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{add}X
.

Proof.

We give some hints and fix objects
Y,Z\in\mathcal{T}
. The assertions (1)–(3) are
clear. For (4) one uses that

h(X,Z)\leq n\;\;\iff\;\;h(X\oplus\Sigma^{i}X,Z)\leq n+|i|.

For (5)
and (6) consider
Y\in\operatorname{add}X
or an exact triangle
Y^{\prime}\to Y\to Y^{\prime\prime}\to\Sigma Y^{\prime}
with
Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{add}X
. Then one has

\operatorname{Hom}(Y,Z)\neq 0\;\;\implies\;\;\operatorname{Hom}(X,Z)\neq 0

and therefore

h(X,Z)\leq n\;\;\implies\;\;h(Y,Z)\leq n.\qed

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Y\in\operatorname{thick}(X)
. For each
p\geq 0
there are inclusions

\operatorname{thick}^{p}(Y)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{q}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad\hom^{p}(X)\subseteq\hom^{q}(Y)\quad\text{for}\quad q\gg 0.

Proof.

Let
Y\in\operatorname{thick}^{n}(X)
. Then
\operatorname{thick}^{p}(Y)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p\cdot n}(X)
.
The second inclusion follows from the previous lemma by an induction on the
number
n
of steps needed to build
Y
from
X
.
∎

The following definition provides a finiteness condition for
triangulated categories which lies in between ‘finitely generated’ and
‘strongly finitely generated’.

Recall from
[
14
]
that an object
X
is
homologically
finite
if it satisfies

h(X,Y)<\infty
for each object
Y
.

Definition 2.3
.

An object
X
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
is a
finitistic
generator
of
\mathcal{T}
if
X
is homologically finite
and

\hom^{p}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p}(X)\quad\text{for all}\quad p\geq 0.

The
finitistic dimension

\operatorname{fin{.}dim}\mathcal{T}:=\inf\{\operatorname{amp}(X)\mid X\text{ is a finitistic generator of

As usual,
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}\mathcal{T}=\infty
if no finitistic generator exists.

Remark 2.4
.

(1) If an object
X
is homologically finite, then

\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{p\geq 0}\hom^{p}(X)
. Thus a finitistic generator
X
of

\mathcal{T}
satisfies
\mathcal{T}=\operatorname{thick}(X)
.

(2) If
\mathcal{T}
admits a finitistic generator, then all objects in
\mathcal{T}
are
homologically finite.

(3) If
\operatorname{thick}^{n+1}(X)=\mathcal{T}
for some
X\in\mathcal{T}
, then

\hom^{p}(Y)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p}(Y)
for
Y=X\oplus\Sigma^{n}X
and all

p\geq 0
. Thus strong finite generation implies that there is
a finitistic generator, provided that all objects are homologically
finite.

3.
The small finitistic dimension of a ring

Let
A
be a ring and let
\mathcal{P}(A)
denote the class of
A
-modules
M

that admit a finite resolution

0\longrightarrow P_{n}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\longrightarrow P_{0}\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow 0

with all
P_{i}
finitely generated projective. We denote

\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A:=\sup\{\operatorname{proj{.}dim}M\mid M\in\mathcal{P}(A)\}

the
small finitistic dimension
of
A
; this is a slight
variation of the original definition in
[
2
]
which takes the
supremum of
\operatorname{proj{.}dim}M
where
M
runs through all finitely generated

A
-modules having finite projective dimension. Our definition seems
natural as the modules in
\mathcal{P}(A)
are precisely the ones which become
compact (or perfect) when viewed as an object in the derived category
of
A
. Note that both definitions coincide when the ring
A
is noetherian.

We are now in the position to characterise
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A<\infty
in terms
of the category of perfect complexes. Let
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
denote the
category of perfect complexes over
A
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a ring
A
we have

\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A<\infty\;\;\iff\;\;\operatorname{fin{.}dim}\operatorname{Perf}(A)<\infty.

More precisely, let
X
be a finitistic generator of
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
. Then
there are
p,q\geq 0
such that

\hom^{1}(A)\subseteq\hom^{p}(X)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{thick}^{1}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{q}(A),

which implies

\operatorname{fin{.}dim}\operatorname{Perf}(A)\leq\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A<p\cdot q.

We will use the following well
known fact, which is a consequence of the ghost lemma (cf.
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Lemma 3.2
.

For
M\in\mathcal{P}(A)
and
n>0
we have

\operatorname{proj{.}dim}M<n\;\;\iff\;\;M\in\operatorname{thick}^{n}(A)\subseteq\mathrm{perf}(A).\qed

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The crucial observation is that taking the cohomology of a complex
identifies the objects in
\hom^{1}(A)
with the modules in
\mathcal{P}(A)
.

Suppose first that
d=\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A<\infty
. We show by induction on
p\geq 0
that

\hom^{p}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p+d}(A)
. The case
p=0
is clear and we
may assume
p>0
. Let
X\in\hom^{p}(A)
and
suppose its cohomology

H^{n}(X)=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\Sigma^{n}X)

is concentrated in
degrees
0,\ldots,p-1
. Then we may assume
X^{n}=0
for
n\geq p
. For
p=1

the complex
X
identifies with
H^{0}(X)\in\mathcal{P}(A)

and then Lemma
3.2
implies
X\in\operatorname{thick}^{d+1}(A)
. For
p>1

we may write
X
as an extension of the truncation

X^{\prime}\colon\quad\cdots\longrightarrow X^{p-4}\longrightarrow X^{p-3}\longrightarrow X^{p-2}\longrightarrow 0\longrightarrow\cdots

and a complex concentrated in
degree
p-1
. We have

X^{\prime}\in\hom^{p-1}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p-1+d}(A)

and therefore

X\in\operatorname{thick}^{p+d}(A)
. Now set
A^{\prime}=A\oplus\Sigma^{d}A
. Then we have

\hom^{p+d}(A^{\prime})=\hom^{p}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p+d}(A)=\operatorname{thick}^{p+d}(A^{\prime})

for
p\geq 0
and
\hom^{d}(A^{\prime})=0
, using Lemma
2.1
.
Thus
A^{\prime}
is a finitistic generator and
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}\operatorname{Perf}(A)\leq\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A

follows since
\operatorname{amp}(A^{\prime})=d
.

Suppose now that
X
is a finitistic generator of
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
. Using
Lemma
2.2
we find
p,q\geq 0
such that

\hom^{1}(A)\subseteq\hom^{p}(X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{thick}^{1}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{q}(A).

Then

\hom^{1}(A)\subseteq\hom^{p}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{p\cdot q}(A).

Thus
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A<p\cdot q
by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

A pair of rings
A,B
is by definition
derived equivalent
if
there is a triangle equivalence
\operatorname{Perf}(A)\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Perf}(B)
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
that for any
pair of derived equivalent rings
A,B
we have that
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}B<\infty
. For coherent rings this
result is due to Pan and Xi
[
15
]
.

Remark 3.4
.

There are examples of rings such that
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A=\infty
and

\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A^{\mathrm{op}}=0
; cf.
[
9
]
. We have

\operatorname{Perf}(A^{\mathrm{op}})\simeq\operatorname{Perf}(A)^{\mathrm{op}}
, and therefore finiteness of
finitistic dimension is not a symmetric notion for triangulated categories.

The following remark collects some known facts about the existence of
strong and finitistic generators of
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
. We give references, and
full proofs are provided in Appendix
A
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
A
be a noetherian ring. Strong finite generation of
\operatorname{Perf}(A)

holds if and only if the global dimension is finite. Clearly, this
implies that
A
is
regular
, so each finitely generated

A
-module has finite projective dimension

[
17
, Proposition 7.25]
. In general (and despite such claims
in the literature), the converse is not true even when
A
is
commutative, because there are regular rings having infinite
finitistic dimension
[
12
, Appendix, Example 1]
, so

A\text{ regular}\quad\;\not\!\!\!\!\implies\quad\operatorname{gl{.}dim}A<\infty.

However
when
A
is in addition semilocal, then
A
is regular if and only
if
A
has finite global dimension
[
20
, Corollary 2]
. This
provides many examples where
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
has a finitistic generator
but no strong generator, because one may take for
A
any finite
dimensional algebra over a field that has infinite global dimension
and finite finitistic dimension (keeping in mind the
question/conjecture from
[
2
]
that for finite dimensional
algebras the finitistic dimension is always finite).

Example 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an idempotent complete algebraic triangulated
category. If
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}\mathcal{T}=0
, then there exists a ring
A
with

\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A=0
and a triangle equivalence
\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Perf}(A)
.

Proof.

A generator
X
of
\mathcal{T}
with
\operatorname{amp}(X)=0
is a tilting object. Thus
for
A=\operatorname{End}(X)
the functor
\operatorname{RHom}(X,-)
provides an equivalence

\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Perf}(A)
; see

[
8
, Proposition 9.1.20]
. Moreover,

\hom^{1}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{1}(A)
implies
\operatorname{fin{.}dim}A=0
by
Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Appendix A
Strong finite generation

In this appendix we record with proofs some facts that are well known,
but only to those knowing them well. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category with
a projective generator
P
. Thus any object
X\in\mathcal{A}
admits an
epimorphism
P^{n}\to X
for some positive integer
n
. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote
the full exact subcategory of projective objects in
\mathcal{A}
. We consider
the derived categories of bounded complexes and the inclusion

\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{A}
induces a triangle equivalence

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{thick}(P)\subseteq\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})

which we view as an identification.

Proposition A.1
.

The following holds for
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})\subseteq\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
.

(1)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})

\iff

\{X\in\mathcal{A}\mid\operatorname{proj{.}dim}X<\infty\}=\mathcal{A}
.

(2)

\operatorname{dim}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})<\infty

\iff

\operatorname{gl{.}dim}\mathcal{A}<\infty
.

Proof.

(1) The equality
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})
holds if and only if each
object from
\mathcal{A}
, viewed as a complex concentrated in degree zero,
belongs to
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})
. But an object from
\mathcal{A}
belongs to
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{P})
precisely
when it has finite projective dimension.

(2) Suppose first that
\operatorname{gl{.}dim}\mathcal{A}<n
. Then
\operatorname{thick}^{n}(P)=\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{A})

by
[
5
, Theorem 8.3]
or
[
10
, Lemma 2.5]
. For the
converse suppose that
\operatorname{thick}(P)=\operatorname{thick}^{n}(P)
for some integer

n
. We claim that
\operatorname{gl{.}dim}\mathcal{A}<n
. This follows from
Lemma
3.2
once we know that each object
X\in\mathcal{A}
has finite
projective dimension. To see this choose a projective resolution

and for
1\leq i\leq n
let
\phi_{i}
denote
the following chain map between appropriate truncations of
Q

One may ask for general criteria on
\mathcal{A}
such that the conditions in
(1) and (2) are equivalent. To indicate some connection we include
the following.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that every non-zero object in
\mathcal{A}
has a
projective cover and a maximal subobject. Then we have

\operatorname{gl{.}dim}\mathcal{A}<\infty\quad\iff\quad\{X\in\mathcal{A}\mid\operatorname{inj{.}dim}X<\infty\}=\mathcal{A}.

Proof.

One direction is clear and requires no assumption on
\mathcal{A}
. For the
converse it is convenient to identify
\mathcal{A}
with the category of
finitely presented modules over
\operatorname{End}(P)
via the functor

X\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(P,X)
. The assumption on
\mathcal{A}
implies that this ring
is semiperfect, and therefore
S=P/\operatorname{rad}P
is semisimple. We claim
that
X\in\mathcal{A}
is projective if and only if
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X,S)=0
. To see
this choose a projective cover

\xi\colon\quad 0\longrightarrow\Omega(X)\longrightarrow P(X)\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow 0.

If
\Omega(X)\neq 0
, then there is a non-zero morphism

\pi\colon\Omega(X)\to S
such that
\pi\circ\xi\neq 0
in

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(X,S)
since
\Omega(X)\subseteq\operatorname{rad}P(X)
. From this
characterisation of projectivity it follows that
\operatorname{gl{.}dim}\mathcal{A}
is bounded
by
\operatorname{inj{.}dim}S
.
∎

Appendix B
The big finitistic dimension

In this appendix we show for an Artin algebra that the finiteness of
the big finitistic dimension is an invariant of the category of
perfect complexes, using some recent work of Rickard

[
16
]
. The argument is different from the one for the small
finitistic dimension, and also much less direct. We refer to

[
3
]
for the notion of a big finitistic dimension for
compactly generated triangulated categories.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category with an equivalence
\Sigma\colon\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{C}
. For a
class of objects
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathcal{C}
set

\mathcal{X}^{+}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{n}Y)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X},n\ll 0\}

and analogously

\mathcal{X}^{-}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{n}Y)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X},n\gg 0\}.

Also set
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{b}}:=\mathcal{X}^{-}\cap\mathcal{X}^{+}
and

\mathcal{X}^{\perp}:=\{Y\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{Hom}(X,\Sigma^{n}Y)=0\text{ for all }X\in\mathcal{X},n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an essentially small triangulated category with suspension

\Sigma\colon\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\mathcal{T}
. We write
\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{T}

for the category of
\mathcal{T}
-modules, i.e. additive functors

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Ab}
. The suspension extends to an equivalence

\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}}\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{T}
via the assignment
X\mapsto X\circ\Sigma^{-}
.
The full subcategory of injective objects in

\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{T}
is denoted by
\operatorname{Inj}\mathcal{T}
.

Let
A
be an Artin algebra. We write
\operatorname{Mod}A
for the category of

A
-modules and consider its derived category. The category of perfect
complexes is viewed as a subcategory and we set

\mathcal{P}:=\operatorname{Perf}(A)\subseteq\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A).

The Yoneda embedding
\mathcal{P}\to\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{P}
identifies
\mathcal{P}
with a full
subcategory
\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{P}
; it consists of injective modules by
Lemmas
B.2
and
B.3
below.

The
big finitistic dimension
of
A
is by definition

\operatorname{Fin{.}dim}A:=\sup\{\operatorname{proj{.}dim}M\mid M\in\operatorname{Mod}A,\,\operatorname{proj{.}dim}M<\infty\}.

Theorem B.1
.

For an Artin algebra
A
the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The big finitistic dimension of
A
is finite.

(2)

In the derived category

Lemma B.3
.

Any complex of the form
DX
is pure-injective.

Proof.

We view
A
as a differential graded algebra and have an isomorphism
of functors

\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-\otimes_{A}X,E)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(X,E))

on the category of dg
A
-modules, which take pure exact sequences
to exact sequences of abelian groups. Thus
DX
is a pure-injective object in the
category of dg
A

\coprod_{I}DX\to DX

Observe that in

Lemma B.4
.

The restricted Yoneda functor
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}A)\to\operatorname{Mod}\mathcal{P}

the pure-injective objects in

the pure-injective objects

(3)

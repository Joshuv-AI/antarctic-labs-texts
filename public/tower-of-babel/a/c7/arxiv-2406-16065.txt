Sub-Riemannian geodesics on the Heisenberg 3D nil-manifold
A. Glutsyuk, Yu. Sachkov

Abstract
We study the projection of the left-invariant sub-Riemannian structure on the 3D Heisenberg group G G to the Heisenberg 3D nil-manifold M M — the compact homogeneous space of G G by the discrete Heisenberg group. First we describe dynamical properties of the geodesic flow for M M : periodic and dense orbits, a dynamical characterization of the normal Hamiltonian flow of Pontryagin maximum principle and its integrability properties. We show that it is Liouville integrable on a nonzero level hypersurface Σ \Sigma of the Hamiltonian outside appropriate smaller proper hypersurface in Σ \Sigma and has no nontrivial analytic integrals on all of Σ \Sigma . Then we obtain sharp twoside bounds of sub-Riemannian balls and distance in G G , and on this basis we estimate the cut time for sub-Riemannian geodesics in M M .



Keywords:
Sub-Riemannian geometry, Heisenberg group, Heisenberg 3D nil-manifold, geodesics, dynamics, Hamiltonian flow, sub-Riemannian balls and distance, optimality

3
Heisenberg group and 3-dimensional
nil-manifold

Recall that the Heisenberg group is

G=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&a&c\\
0&1&b\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\ |\ (a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\right\}.

H=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&m&k\\
0&1&n\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\ |\ (m,n,k)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\right\},\ \ \ M:={\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$H$}\left\backslash\raisebox{1.99997pt}{$G$}\right.}=\{Hg\mid g\in G\}.

The quotient

Let

Remark 3.1

The manifold

. Therefore,

Hence,
H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})=H/\penalty[H,H]

of integer unipotent matrices that differ from the identity just by the upper-right corner element.
Therefore, it is isomorphic to

The manifold

with identified facets

\{c=0\}\leftrightarrow\{c=1\}

Remark 3.2

Any uniform subgroup of the Heisenberg group
G
is isomorphic to a subgroup

D(k)=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&a&\frac{c}{k}\\
0&1&b\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right)\mid a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, see
[
21
]
; in particular,
H=D(1)
. Thus any compact 3D nil-manifold is a homogeneous space

. Since
G
is simply connected, the fundamental group of such a space is
\pi_{1}(G/D(k))=D(k)
. Hence

\pi_{1}(M)=\pi_{1}(G/D(1))=D(1)\subset D(k)=\pi_{1}(G/G(k)),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Consequently, the Heisenberg nil-manifold

is the simplest compact 3D nil-manifold in the sense that it has the smallest fundamental group. This observation motivated us to study sub-Riemannian geometry on the Heisenberg nil-manifold
M
. This work may thus be seen as the first study of sub-Riemannian geometry of compact 3D nil-manifolds, starting from the simplest case of the Heisenberg nil-manifold
M
. We believe that our methods may be useful for the study of sub-Riemannian geometry on compact nil-manifolds of dimension 3 and greater than 3.

4
Sub-Riemannian structure on the Heisenberg group and its projection to the nil-manifold

4.1
Sub-Riemannian geometry

A sub-Riemannian structure
[
16
,
1
]
on a smooth manifold
M
is a vector subspace distribution

\Delta=\{\Delta_{q}\subset T_{q}M\mid q\in M\}\subset TM,\qquad\dim\Delta_{q}\equiv\operatorname{const},

endowed with an inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{q}\text{ inner product in }\Delta_{q}\mid q\in M\}.

A Lipschitzian curve
q\,:\,[0,t_{1}]\to M
is called horizontal if
\dot{q}(t)\in\Delta_{q(t)}
for almost all
t\in[0,t_{1}]
. The sub-Riemannian length of a horizontal curve
q(\cdot)
is

l(q(\cdot))=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\langle\dot{q}(t),\dot{q}(t)\rangle^{1/2}\,dt.

The sub-Riemannian distance between points
q_{0},q_{1}\in M
is

d(q_{0},q_{1})=\inf\{l(q(\cdot))\mid q(\cdot)\text{ horiz. curve s.t. }q(0)=q_{0},\ q(t_{1})=q_{1}\}.

A horizontal curve is called a
sub-Riemannian length minimizer
if its sub-Riemannian length is equal to the sub-Riemannian distance between its endpoints. A
sub-Riemannian geodesic
is a horizontal curve whose sufficiently short arcs are length minimizers. Finally, a cut time along a sub-Riemannian geodesic
q(\cdot)
is

t_{\operatorname{cut}}(q(\cdot))=\sup\{\tau>0\mid\left.q(\cdot)\right|_{[0,\tau]}\text{ is a length minimizer}\}.

If the distribution
\Delta
is
completely nonholonomic
(
completely nonintegrable
), i.e., any points in
M
can be connected by a horizontal curve of
\Delta
, then
the sub-Riemannian distance
d
turns
M
into a metric space, and there are naturally defined a sub-Riemannian sphere of radius
R\geq 0
centered at a point
q_{0}\in M
:

S_{R}(q_{0})=\{q\in M\mid d(q_{0},q)=R\}

and the corresponding sub-Riemannian ball:

B_{R}(q_{0})=\{q\in M\mid d(q_{0},q)\leq R\}.

Let
X_{1},\dots X_{k}
be vector fields on
M
that form an orthonormal frame of a sub-Riemannian structure
(\Delta,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
:

\Delta_{q}=\operatorname{span}(X_{1}(q),\dots,X_{k}(q)),\quad\langle X_{i}(q),X_{j}(q)\rangle=\delta_{ij},\qquad q\in M.

Then sub-Riemannian length minimizers that connect points
q_{0},q_{1}\in M
are solutions to the optimal control problem

\displaystyle\dot{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}u_{i}(t)X_{i}(q),\qquad q\in M,\quad u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k},

\displaystyle q(0)=q_{0},\qquad q(t_{1})=q_{1},

\displaystyle\int^{t_{1}}_{0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}u^{2}_{i}(t)\right)^{1/2}\,dt\to\min.

9
Bounds of cut time via lower bounds

of sub-Riemannian balls

Fix a point
q_{0}=g_{0}^{\prime}\in M
.
Denote the ball
B^{\prime}_{t}=\{q\in M\mid d^{\prime}(q_{0},q)\leq t\}
,
t\geq 0
, where
d^{\prime}
is the sub-Riemannian distance on
M
.
Denote also

\bar{t}=\inf\{t>0\mid B^{\prime}_{t}=M\}.

The following lemmas show the relevance of the number
\bar{t}
for the sub-Riemannian manifold
M
.

Lemma 9.1

We have the following:

(1)

\bar{t}=\sup\{d^{\prime}(q_{0},q_{1})\mid q_{1}\in M\}
.

(2)

\bar{t}=\sup\{t_{\operatorname{cut}}(q(\cdot))\mid q(\cdot)\subset M\text{ a geodesic s.t. }q(0)=q_{0}\}
.

Proof.

(1) Denote
t_{1}=\sup\{d^{\prime}(q_{0},q_{1})\mid q_{1}\in M\}
and assume by contradiction that
\bar{t}\neq t_{1}
.

Let
t_{1}<\bar{t}
. Then for every
t\in(t_{1},\bar{t})
and every
q_{1}\in M
we have
d^{\prime}(q_{0},q_{1})<t
, i.e.,
q_{1}\in B^{\prime}_{t}
. Since
t<\bar{t}
, this contradicts to definition of
\bar{t}
.

Let
t_{1}>\bar{t}
. Then for every
t\in(\bar{t},t_{1})
there exists
q_{1}\in M
such that
d^{\prime}(q_{0},q_{1})>t
, i.e.,
q_{1}\notin B^{\prime}_{t}
. Since
t>\bar{t}
, this contradicts to definition of
\bar{t}
once more.

(2) Denote
t_{2}=\sup\{t_{\operatorname{cut}}(q(\cdot))\mid q(\cdot)\subset M\text{ a geodesic s.t. }q(0)=q_{0}\}
and assume by contradiction that
\bar{t}\neq t_{2}
.

Let
t_{2}<\bar{t}
, take any
t\in(t_{2},\bar{t})
. Then
B^{\prime}_{t}\neq M
, thus there exists a point
q_{1}\in M
such that
d^{\prime}(q_{0},q_{1})>t
. Take a sub-Riemannian length minimizer
q(\cdot)
connecting
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
. We have
t_{\operatorname{cut}}(q(\cdot))>t>t_{2}
, which contradicts the definition of
t_{2}
.

Let
\bar{t}<t_{2}
, take any
t\in(\bar{t},t_{2})
. We have
B^{\prime}_{t}=M
, thus for every
q_{1}\in M
one has the inequality
d^{\prime}(q_{0},q_{1})\leq t
. Then for every geodesic
q(\cdot)\subset M
starting at
q_{0}
we have
t_{\operatorname{cut}}(q(\cdot))\leq t<t_{2}
, which contradicts the definition of
t_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 10.1

If

for an element

for some

We have
\widetilde{g}_{i}=h_{i}g_{i}

If

Let

d(\widetilde{g}_{0},\widetilde{g}_{1})=d(h_{0}g_{0},h_{1}g_{1})=d(g_{0},h_{0}^{-1}h_{1}g_{1})

Lemma 10.2

Let
g(\cdot)
be a sub-Riemannian geodesic in
G
starting at
g_{0}=\operatorname{Id}
such that
t_{\operatorname{cut}}(g(\cdot))\geq\widehat{t}
. Then
t_{\operatorname{cut}}(g^{\prime}(\cdot))\geq\widehat{t}

Proof.

Let

\left.g(\cdot)\right|_{[0,\widehat{t}]}

10.1

t_{\operatorname{cut}}(g^{\prime}(\cdot))\geq t

\widehat{t}

Lemma 10.3

Let
g(\cdot)
be a sub-Riemannian geodesic in
G

11
Conclusion

This work seems to be the first study of a projection of a left-invariant sub-Riemannian structure on a Lie group to a compact homogeneous space. It reveals essential difference between the initial structure and its projection despite their local isometry.

For example, dynamical behaviour of sub-Riemannian geodesics on the Heisenberg group
G
is trivial: all geodesics tend to infinity. Dynamics of sub-Riemannian geodesics on the Heisenberg 3D nil-manifold
M
includes closed geodesics, dense in

geodesics, and geodesics dense in a 2D torus.

Further, optimality of sub-Riemannian geodesics in
G

G

M
is much delicate since there are no continuous symmetries; and discrete symmetries which seem to generate the cut locus are hidden since they do not respect the projection mapping
\pi\,:\,G\to M

M
are possible due to two-sided bounds of sub-Riemannian balls and distance in
G
, which may be of independent interest.

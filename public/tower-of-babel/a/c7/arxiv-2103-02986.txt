Abstract
. In this manuscript, we prove the Bernstein inequality and develop the theory of holonomic D D -modules for rings of invariants of finite groups in characteristic zero, and for strongly F F -regular finitely generated graded algebras with FFRT in prime characteristic. In each of these cases, the ring itself, its localizations, and its local cohomology modules are holonomic. We also show that holonomic D D -modules, in this context, have finite length and we prove the existence of Bernstein–Sato polynomials in characteristic zero. We obtain these results using a more general version of Bernstein filtrations.

1.
Introduction

Let
R
be a regular
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
its ring of
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators, where
\mathbb{k}
is a field of characteristic zero.
A fundamental theorem in the theory of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules is the
Bernstein inequality
, which establishes that the dimension of every finitely generated
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module is at least
\dim(R)
.
Sato, Kawai, and Kashiwara
[
SKK73
]
and Malgrange
[
Mal79
]
proved this result for holomorphic functions.
Gabber
[
Gab81
]
gave another proof that works in smooth cases such as polynomial rings and formal power series rings over a field.
Joseph (see
[
Cou95
, Theorem 9.4.2]
) gave a simple proof for polynomial rings, considering the
Bernstein filtration
on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
instead of the order filtration.
More recently, Bavula
[
Bav09
]
proved the Bernstein inequality for polynomial rings in positive characteristic.

Modules with minimal dimension are called
holonomic
, and are central to the theory of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules since they satisfy rather nice properties.
Holonomic modules have finite length and finite-dimensional de Rham cohomology
[
Kas75
,
vdE85
]
.
Furthermore, holonomic modules play an important role in the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence
[
Kas80
,
Meb80
,
Kas84
,
Meb84a
,
Meb84b
]
.
In addition, holonomicity can be characterized via homological properties
[
Bjö79
]
.

The Bernstein inequality and the study of holonomic modules have generally been limited to regular rings; see below for further discussion.
In this work we prove the Bernstein inequality and develop a theory of holonomic modules for certain singular
\mathbb{k}
-algebras.
Specifically, we summarize our main results in the following theorem, which is pieced together from various results throughout the text.

Theorem A

(See
Corollaries

5.6
and
6.10
)
.

Let
R
be one of the following.

(i)

A ring of invariants of the action of a finite group on a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, or

(ii)

A strongly
F
-regular finitely generated graded algebra over a perfect field
\mathbb{k}
of positive characteristic with finite
F
-representation type
(
FFRT
)
.

Then any nonzero
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module has dimension at least
\dim(R)
.
In addition, any nonzero
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module of dimension
\dim(R)
and finite multiplicity has finite length in the category of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules.
In particular, any principal localization
R_{f}
and any local cohomology module
H^{i}_{I}(R)
has dimension
\dim(R)
, and therefore has finite length as a
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module.

First, we build upon the work of Bavula
[
Bav09
]
on filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebras and filtered modules.
We consider his definition of dimension, which coincides with the usual Gelfand–Kirillov dimension for finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebras, although these notions are different in general.
We also consider a version of multiplicity for filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebras and modules that is convenient for our treatment of holonomic modules.
A key ingredient in Bavula’s work is a technical condition that we call
linear simplicity
(see
Definition

3.1
), which gives a sufficient condition for the Bernstein inequality to hold, and to have a theory of holonomic modules (see
Theorem

3.4
).
Our main contribution is a proof in this setting establishing that holonomic modules have finite length (see
Theorem

3.8
).

Let
R
be a finitely generated graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
its ring of
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators.
In order to have a good theory of holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules that extends the case of regular rings, we want
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
to be linearly simple with respect to an appropriate filtration, so that Bernstein’s inequality holds.
Moreover, we want the dimension of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
to be twice the dimension of
R
, and its multiplicity to be finite and positive.
If
R
satisfies all these properties, we call it a
Bernstein algebra
.
In this case,
R
is a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module, and the class of holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules is closed under localization and taking local cohomology.
We also show that a certain module over
R\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{k}(s)
is holonomic, and as a consequence, we obtain the existence of the Bernstein–Sato polynomial for Bernstein algebras in characteristic zero (see
Corollaries

4.12
and
4.13
).

In
Definition

4.15
, we give a generalization of the Bernstein filtration that works for the graded ring
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.
The difficult part when dealing with specific cases is to prove that
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is linearly simple with respect to the generalized Bernstein filtration.
Sufficient conditions to ensure the remaining properties of a Bernstein algebra are given in terms of
differential signature

[
BJNB19
]
(see
Theorem

4.32
).

We note that in characteristic zero, there are graded hypersurfaces with rational singularities that are not Bernstein algebras, and fail the conclusions of
A
; see
Examples

5.7
and
5.8
.
Likewise, in positive characteristic, there are graded hypersurfaces that are strongly
F
-regular or have FFRT (but not both) that are not Bernstein algebras, and again fail the conclusions of
A
; see
Examples

6.11
and
6.12
.
Thus, strong hypotheses are necessary for a statement akin to
A
.

The main results of this work provide classes of singular rings that are Bernstein algebras.
First, we consider the case of a ring of invariants of a finite group
G
acting linearly on a polynomial ring
R
over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero (see
Corollary

5.6
).
Moreover, we prove in
Theorem

5.9
that a
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-module is holonomic if and only if it is a
differential direct summand
of a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module
[
ÀHNB17
,
AMHJ+21
]
.

For our second class of Bernstein algebras, we focus on algebras over a field of positive characteristic.
Specifically, we work on rings with finite
F
-representation type—FFRT for short—that were introduced by Smith and Van den Bergh
[
SVdB97
]
.
Examples of rings with FFRT include complete regular rings, quotients of polynomial rings by monomial ideals, normal monoid rings, affine cones of Grassmannians, and graded direct summands of polynomial rings.
In
Corollary

6.10
, we show that certain rings with FFRT are Bernstein algebras.

We point out that Gabber
[
Gab81
]
proved the integrability of the characteristic variety for the case of filtered rings whose associated graded rings are commutative and Noetherian.
In this setting, van den Essen
[
vdE86
]
considered the notion of holonomicity, but to the best of our knowledge, this notion has only been applied to smooth algebras when dealing with rings of differential operators. We show in
Example

5.10
that
for our first class of Bernstein algebras, although Gabber’s theorem applies, there are no nonzero
D
-modules that are holonomic in the sense of van den Essen. Thus our results do not follow from Gabber’s theorem. More recently, Losev
[
Los17
]
showed a version of the Bernstein inequality
using representation theory of filtered algebras with commutative Noetherian associated graded rings, whose spectra have finitely many symplectic leaves. In particular, this theory applies to certain algebras in characteristic zero, but to the best of our knowledge,
it does not provide examples of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules satisfying the Bernstein inequality for a singular
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R
.

In one of the first uses of
D
-modules in commutative algebra, Lyubeznik
[
Lyu93
]
proved the finiteness of the set of associated primes of a local cohomology module of a regular ring in characteristic zero.
A key point of his argument is that local cohomology modules of holonomic
D
-modules are themselves holonomic, and thus have finite length as
D
-modules.
The finiteness of sets of associated primes of local cohomology modules has since been established for rings of invariants of finite groups
[
NB12
]
and rings with FFRT
[
TT08
,
HNB17
,
DQ20
]
.
These proofs, however, did not use the theory of
D
-modules, though recently a new proof using
D
-module theory was given for rings of invariants
[
ÀHNB17
]
.

A
gives another proof for the previously mentioned classes of rings via
D
-modules that resembles the one given by Lyubeznik.

2.
Filtrations

In this section, we establish a number of key facts on filtrations and their numerical invariants that we use throughout this work.
Much, if not all, of the material in this section is known
[
Bav09
]
, with the possible exception of the discussion of multiplicity.
To keep the paper self-contained and to avoid unnecessary finiteness hypotheses, we provide proofs for the claims made in this section.

Throughout this manuscript,
\mathbb{k}
always denotes a field, which has arbitrary characteristic unless otherwise stated.
Within
Sections

2
and
3
,
A
denotes an associative but not necessarily commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.

2.1.
Filtrations, dimension, and multiplicity

Definition 2.1
.

Throughout this paper, by a
filtration

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
we mean an ascending, exhaustive filtration by finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces, indexed by the nonnegative integers, and such that
\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathbb{k}
and
\mathcal{F}^{i}\mathcal{F}^{j}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{i+j}
for each
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is a filtration on
A
, we say that
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a
filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
.

Given a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, and a left (respectively, right)
A
-module
M
, a
filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
M
compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is an ascending, exhaustive filtration on
M
by finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces, indexed by the nonnegative integers, and such that
\mathcal{F}^{i}\mathcal{G}^{j}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{i+j}
(respectively,
\mathcal{G}^{j}\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{i+j}
) for every
i
and
j
in
\mathbb{N}
.
If
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is a filtration on
M
compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
, we say that
(M,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is an
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module
.

Convention 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is a filtration on an algebra or module, we adopt the convention that
\mathcal{F}^{i}=0
for each negative integer
i
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be an ascending sequence of finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces. We define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\ \big|\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}}{i^{t}}=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\ \big|\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}\leqslant i^{t}\;\ \forall\ i\gg 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\ \big|\ \exists\ C:\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}\leqslant Ci^{t}\;\ \forall\ i\gg 0\right\}.

If
d=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is finite, then the
multiplicity
of
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is the extended real number

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}}{i^{d}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\cup\{\infty\}.

By convention, if
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is infinite, then we set
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\infty
.

If
(A,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra or
(M,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is a left or right
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module, then we define the dimension (respectively, multiplicity) of
(A,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, or of
(M,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, as the dimension (respectively, multiplicity) of
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
.

We note that the dimension of a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra or module may be infinite, or may fail to be an integer, or even rational, if finite (see
Proposition

2.7
).
Furthermore, there are examples showing that the limit superior in the definition of multiplicity cannot simply be replaced with a limit, as the limit may fail to exist (see
[
KLZ12
, Section 4]
).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be two filtrations on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra or module.

(i)

We say that
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is
shift dominated
by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
if there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that

\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{i+j}\quad\text{for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

We say that
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is
linearly dominated
by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
if there exists
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that

\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{Ci}\quad\text{for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}.

(iii)

We say that
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are
shift equivalent
if both
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is shift dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is shift dominated by
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.

(iv)

We say that
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are
linearly equivalent
if both
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.

Note that if
\mathcal{F}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
(e.g., when
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are filtrations on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra) and there exist positive integers
C
and
j
such that
\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{Ci+j}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, then
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
.
In particular, when
\mathcal{F}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
, shift domination implies linear domination.
We also note that in condition
(i)
in
Definition

2.4
, we may replace “for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
” with “for all
i\gg 0
.”
The same replacement can be made in condition
(ii)
, provided that
\mathcal{F}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{0}
.

Observe also that shift and linear domination are transitive, and that shift and linear equivalence are equivalence relations.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be filtrations on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra or module.
If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, then

(i)

\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\leqslant\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
.

If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
and
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, then

(ii)

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})<\infty
implies
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})<\infty
, and

(iii)

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=0
implies
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=0
.

Finally, if
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is shift dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
and
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, then

(iv)

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\leqslant\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
.

Proof.

Fix
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{Ci}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Given
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}}{i^{t}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{Ci}}{i^{t}}

\displaystyle=C^{t}\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{Ci}}{(Ci)^{t}}\leqslant C^{t}\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}}{i^{t}}.

Parts
(i)
,
(ii)
, and
(iii)
then follow from the definitions.

For part
(iv)
, if
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
have dimension
d
and finite multiplicity, and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
is such that
\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{i+j}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}}{i^{d}}\leqslant\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i+j}}{i^{d}}

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{(i+j)^{d}}{i^{d}}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i+j}}{(i+j)^{d}}=\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}}{i^{d}}=\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}).\qed

The following corollary follows immediately from
Proposition

2.5
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be two filtrations on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra or module.
If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are linearly equivalent, then

(i)

\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
,

(ii)

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})<\infty
, and

(iii)

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})>0
if and only if
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})>0
.

If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are shift equivalent, then in addition we have

(iv)

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
. ∎

We note that the dimension of a filtration on an algebra (or module) necessarily depends on the choice of filtration, rather than solely on the algebra (or module).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
be a filtration on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
, or on a module
M
, with
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=d\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Then, for every real number
\lambda\geqslant d
there exists a filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
A
, or on
M
, such that
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\lambda
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda\geqslant d
; set
s=\lambda/d\geqslant 1
and define
\mathcal{G}^{i}=\mathcal{F}^{\lfloor i^{s}\rfloor}
.
It is apparent that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is ascending, exhaustive, and finite dimensional, and
\mathcal{G}^{0}=\mathcal{F}^{0}
, which equals
\mathbb{k}
in the algebra case.
The fact that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is compatible with multiplication follows from the inequality
\lfloor i^{s}\rfloor+\lfloor j^{s}\rfloor\leqslant\lfloor(i+j)^{s}\rfloor
in the algebra case, or from the inequality
i+\lfloor j^{s}\rfloor\leqslant\lfloor(i+j)^{s}\rfloor
in the module case.

To compute the dimension of
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, note first that for every
t\geqslant 0
, if
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}\leqslant i^{t}
for all
i\gg 0
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{\lfloor i^{s}\rfloor}\leqslant i^{st}
for all
i\gg 0
.
Consequently,
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\leqslant s\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\lambda
.
For the reverse inequality, it suffices to show that for any
t<d
and any
N
, there exists
j>N
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{j}>j^{st}
.
Fix such
t
and
N
.
As
t<d=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, we can find
i>N^{s}
with
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}>i^{t}(1+1/N)^{st}
. Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
(j-1)^{s}\leqslant i<j^{s}
so that, in particular,
j>N
.
We then have

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{j}\geqslant\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}>i^{t}(1+1/N)^{st}\geqslant(j-1)^{st}(1+1/N)^{st}\geqslant j^{st}

as required.
∎

2.2.
Finitely generated modules

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra and
M
a finitely generated left (or right)
A
-module.
We say that a filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
M
is
standard
if there exists a generating set
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
of
M
such that
\mathcal{G}^{i}=\mathcal{F}^{i}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
(or
\mathcal{G}^{i}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}\mathcal{F}^{i}
) for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
A filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
M
is a
good filtration
if it is shift equivalent to a standard filtration.

We caution the reader that the term “standard filtration” is used for other related notions in the literature; see
Definition

2.12
.
We also point out that the notions of standard filtration and of good filtration on a module are dependent on the choice of the filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
on
A
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and
M
a finitely generated left
(
or right
)

A
-module.
Let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
be filtrations on
M
compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.
If
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is a good filtration, then
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is shift dominated by
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
, and consequently
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\leqslant\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{H}^{\bullet})
, and
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\leqslant\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{H}^{\bullet})
whenever these dimensions agree.
Consequently, if both
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
are good filtrations, then
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
are shift equivalent,
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{H}^{\bullet})
, and
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{H}^{\bullet})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, take
M
to be a left
A
-module.
Since
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is shift equivalent, and hence shift dominated, by a standard filtration, transitivity of shift domination allows us to assume that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is standard; say
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}=\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
.
If
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
is such that
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{j}
, then we have

\mathcal{G}^{i}=\mathcal{F}^{i}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{i}\mathcal{H}^{j}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{i+j}

for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, so
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is shift dominated by
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
.
The remaining claims now follow from
Propositions

2.5
and
2.6
.
∎

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and
M
a finitely generated
A
-module.
We define
\operatorname{Dim}(M,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\coloneqq\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
and
\operatorname{e}(M,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\coloneqq\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
for a good filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
of
M
compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.

Note that by
Proposition

2.9
, the dimension and multiplicity of a finitely generated module over a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra do not depend on the choice of the good filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
.
Our next result shows that both the dimension, and the positivity and finiteness of the multiplicity depend only on the linear equivalence class of the filtration on
A
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with filtrations
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{2}
, and
M
a finitely generated left
(
or right
)

A
-module.
Let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2}
be good filtrations on
M
compatible, respectively, with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{2}
.
If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{1}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{2}
, then
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2}
.
Consequently,
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1})\leqslant\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2})
, and when equality holds, if
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2})<\infty
then
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1})<\infty
, and if
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2})=0
then
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1})=0
.

Proof.

Proposition

2.9
allows us to replace
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2}
by any good filtrations.
Thus, we may assume that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2}
are standard filtrations corresponding to the same generating set
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}\}
of
M
, in which case the first claim follows.
The remaining claims follow at once from
Proposition

2.5
.
∎

2.3.
Finitely generated algebras

Definition 2.12
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
We say that a filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
on
A
is
standard
if there exists a generating set
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
of
A
such that
\mathcal{F}^{1}=\mathbb{k}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
and for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
,
\mathcal{F}^{i}=(\mathcal{F}^{1})^{i}
, the
\mathbb{k}
-subspace generated by monomials in
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
of degree
\leqslant i
.

The following proposition is the analogue of
Proposition

2.9
for finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebras, where the role of shift domination is now played by linear domination.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, with filtrations
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
.
If
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is a standard filtration, then
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is linearly dominated by
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
, and thus
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\leqslant\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
.
If both
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are standard filtrations, then
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are linearly equivalent, and consequently,
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
,
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})<\infty
if and only if
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})<\infty
, and
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})>0
if and only if
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})>0
.

Proof.

For the first claim, we fix generators
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{1}=\mathbb{k}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
and
\mathcal{F}^{i}=(\mathcal{F}^{1})^{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.
Let
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
be such that
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\ell}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{C}
, so
\mathcal{F}^{1}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{C}
.
Then for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
we have

\mathcal{F}^{i}=(\mathcal{F}^{1})^{i}\subseteq(\mathcal{G}^{C})^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{Ci}.

The remaining claims now follow from
Propositions

2.5
and
2.6
.
∎

Let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
a standard filtration on
A
.
The Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of
A
is
\operatorname{GK}(A)\coloneqq\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, which does not depend on the choice of the standard filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
by
Proposition

2.13
.
For a non-finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
, the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is defined as

\operatorname{GK}(A)\coloneqq\sup\left\{\operatorname{GK}(A^{\prime})\ \big|\ A^{\prime}\text{ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{k}$-subalgebra of }A\right\}.

2.4.
Finitely generated commutative associated graded algebras

Consider a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, and let
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant 0}\mathcal{F}^{i}/\mathcal{F}^{i-1}
be its associated graded ring.
Given an
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module
(M,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, the associated graded module
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i\geqslant 0}\mathcal{G}^{i}/\mathcal{G}^{i-1}
is a
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module.

In this subsection, we focus on the case where
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a finitely generated commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Suppose that the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a finitely generated commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
M
be a left or right
A
-module.
A filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
M
compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is a good filtration if and only if
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is a finitely generated
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module.
Furthermore, any lift of a generating set for
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is a generating set for
M
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we suppose
M
is a left
A
-module.
Assume first that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is a standard filtration on
M
, that is,
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}=\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
for some generating set
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}
of
M
.
Let
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{G}^{i}/\mathcal{G}^{i-1}
be a nonzero homogeneous element of
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
, which is the class of
u\in\mathcal{G}^{i}\smallsetminus\mathcal{G}^{i-1}
.
Since
u=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}v_{j}
for some
a_{j}\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, the associated graded module
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is generated as a
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module by the classes of the
v_{j}
.
Any good filtration
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt\bullet}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}
is shift equivalent to a standard filtration, so using
[
vdE86
, Lemma 1.12]
we conclude that
\operatorname{gr}(\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt\bullet}_{\hskip-0.24582pt})
is also a finitely generated
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module.

Conversely, suppose
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is generated by homogeneous elements
\overline{u}_{1},\dots,\overline{u}_{s}
as a
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module, and let
u_{j}\in\mathcal{G}^{n_{j}}\smallsetminus\mathcal{G}^{n_{j}-1}
be a lift of
\overline{u}_{j}
, for each
j
.
Thus, any nonzero homogeneous element
\overline{u}\in\mathcal{G}^{i}/\mathcal{G}^{i-1}
can be written in the form
\overline{u}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\overline{a_{j}}\overline{u_{j}}
with
a_{j}\in\mathcal{F}^{i-n_{j}}
, and

u-(a_{1}u_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}u_{s})\eqqcolon x^{(1)}\in\mathcal{G}^{i-1}.

Applying the same argument to
x^{(1)}
, we can find
a_{j}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{F}^{i-n_{j}-1}
, for
j=1,\dots s
, such that
x^{(1)}-(a_{1}^{(1)}u_{1}+\cdots+a_{s}^{(1)}u_{s})\eqqcolon x^{(2)}\in\mathcal{G}^{i-2}
, and thus

u=\big(a_{1}+a_{1}^{(1)}\big)u_{1}+\cdots+\big(a_{s}+a_{s}^{(1)}\big)u_{s}+x^{(2)}.

Repeating this process we eventually end up with some
x^{(j)}=0
, and thus

\mathcal{G}^{i}=\mathcal{F}^{i-n_{1}}u_{1}+\cdots+\mathcal{F}^{i-n_{s}}u_{s}

showing that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is shift equivalent to the standard filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{s}\}
.
This construction also shows that any lift of a generating set for
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is a generating set for
M
.
∎

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Suppose that the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a finitely generated commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
If
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module, then
\operatorname{Dim}(M,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is an integer, and
\operatorname{e}(M,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a positive rational number.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be a good filtration for
M
.
Then
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is a finitely generated graded
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module by
Proposition

2.14
.
By the theory of Hilbert functions on commutative finitely generated graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras,
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}(\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})_{j})
agrees with a quasipolynomial function of
i
with rational coefficients, for
i\gg 0
, and both claims follow.
∎

3.
Bernstein’s inequality and holonomic modules

In this section we recall the Bernstein inequality for what we call
linearly simple algebras
.
The ideas behind this class of algebras can be found in Bavula’s work
[
Bav09
, Theorem 3.1]
.
We rephrase his work for our purposes in the first part of
Theorem

3.4
, and include a proof for the convenience of the reader.
The second part of
Theorem

3.4
concerns multiplicities of filtered modules, which was not treated in Bavula’s work in the generality needed in this manuscript.

Definition 3.1
.

A filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is
C
-linearly simple
for some
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
if for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and each
\delta\in\mathcal{F}^{i}\smallsetminus\{0\}
,

1\in\mathcal{F}^{Ci}\,\delta\,\mathcal{F}^{Ci}.

We say that
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is
linearly simple
if it is
C
-linearly simple for some
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Linear simplicity of a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra depends only on the linear equivalence class of the filtration.
Indeed, suppose
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
are linearly equivalent filtrations on a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A
, with
\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{Ki}
and
\mathcal{G}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{Li}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is
C
-linearly simple, then one easily verifies that
(A,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is
KLC
-linearly simple.

The following key result implicitly appears in Bavula’s work
[
Bav09
, Proof of Theorem 3.1]
.
We single it out because we use it to show new properties for holonomic modules in the generality we need.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and
(M,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
a left
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module.
Suppose that
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is
C
-linearly simple.
Let
\Psi:\mathcal{F}^{i}\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{k}}(\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i},\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i})
be defined by
\delta\mapsto\psi_{\delta}
, where
\psi_{\delta}(v)=\delta v
.
If
\mathcal{G}^{i}\neq 0
, then
\Psi
is injective.
The analogous result holds for right modules as well.

Proof.

We prove the contrapositive:
Suppose that there exists
\delta\in\mathcal{F}^{i}\smallsetminus\{0\}
such that
\psi_{\delta}=0
.
Then
\delta\mathcal{F}^{Ci}\mathcal{G}^{i}\subseteq\delta\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}=0
, and thus
\delta\mathcal{F}^{Ci}\mathcal{G}^{i}=0
.
Since
1\in\mathcal{F}^{Ci}\delta\mathcal{F}^{Ci}
, we have
\mathcal{G}^{i}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{Ci}\delta\mathcal{F}^{Ci}\mathcal{G}^{i}=0
, and therefore
\mathcal{G}^{i}=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Bernstein Inequality
[
Bav09
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})<\infty
, and
(M,\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
a nontrivial left or right
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
-module.
Suppose that
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is
C
-linearly simple.
Then,

\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}).

Moreover, if
\theta\coloneqq\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, then

\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\geqslant\frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}}{(C+1)^{\theta/2}(C+2)^{\theta/2}}.

Proof.

The first claim holds if
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
is infinite, so suppose that is not the case.
Let
t>\theta\coloneqq\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
.
For all sufficiently large
i
we have
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}\leqslant i^{t}
by the definition of dimension, as well as
\mathcal{G}^{i}\neq 0
by the nontriviality of
M
.

Lemma

3.3
then shows that, for such
i
,

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}\leqslant((C+1)i)^{t}((C+2)i)^{t}=(C+1)^{t}(C+2)^{t}i^{2t},

and it follows that
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\leqslant 2t
.
Since this holds for all
t>\theta
, we conclude that
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\leqslant\theta=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})
.

Now assume that
\theta=\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
.
Invoking
Lemma

3.3
one more time, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle=\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathcal{F}^{i}}}{i^{2\theta}}\leqslant\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}}\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i}}}{i^{2\theta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{(C+1)^{\theta}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}}}{((C+1)i)^{\theta}}\right)\left(\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{(C+2)^{\theta}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i}}}{((C+2)i)^{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}\left(\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}}{i^{\theta}}\right)^{2}=(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})^{2},

and the claimed inequality follows.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a linearly simple filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra such that
\dim(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})<\infty
and
0<\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})<\infty
.
A nonzero
A
-module is
holonomic
if it admits a filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
and with finite multiplicity; the zero module is also holonomic by convention.

We shall see in
Theorem

3.8
that a holonomic module has finite length, so in particular it must be finitely generated.
We also point out that there are
A
-modules that admit filtrations of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
but are not holonomic
[
Bav09
, p. 224]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

If
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a linearly simple filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with finite dimension, and positive and finite multiplicity, then the following hold
:

(i)

Every submodule and quotient of a holonomic
A
-module is holonomic.

(ii)

Every finite direct sum of holonomic
A
-modules is holonomic.

Proof.

(i)

Let
M
be a nonzero holonomic
A
-module, and let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be a filtration on
M
of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
and finite multiplicity.
For a nonzero proper submodule
N
of
M
,
N\cap\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is a filtration on
N
with
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}(N\cap\mathcal{G}^{i})\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}
, so
\operatorname{Dim}(N\cap\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})\leqslant\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, and if equality holds, then the multiplicity is finite.
But equality holds by
Theorem

3.4
, so
N
is holonomic.
We show that the quotient
M/N
is holonomic in similar fashion, using the filtration
(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}+N)/N
.

(ii)

This reduces to the case of two modules, say
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, with filtrations
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2}
of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
and finite multiplicity. Then
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}_{2}
is a filtration on
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
of dimension
\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
and finite multiplicity. ∎

Remark 3.7
.

We recall that an extension of two holonomic
A
-modules might not be holonomic
[
KLZ12
, Section 3]
.
This implies that multiplicity is not additive, nor subadditive.
Furthermore, even for a holonomic
A
-module with a standard filtration, the limit superior in the definition of multiplicity cannot be changed to a plain limit, as the sequence in the definition may fail to converge
[
KLZ12
, Section 4]
.

It is known that a holonomic module over the ring of differential operators on a polynomial ring has finite length
[
Bav09
, Theorem 9.6]
.
We now show this for a more general class of algebras.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a
C
-linearly simple filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with finite dimension and positive finite multiplicity.
If
M
is a holonomic
A
-module, then
M
has finite length as an
A
-module. Furthermore,

\operatorname{length}_{A}M\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})^{2}(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}}{\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}

for any filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
M
of dimension
\theta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
and finite multiplicity.

Proof.

Let
0=M_{0}\subsetneqq M_{1}\subsetneqq\cdots\subsetneqq M_{t}=M
be a chain of submodules of
M
, which we may assume is a nontrivial
A
-module.
Given a filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
on
M
of dimension
\theta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
and finite multiplicity, let
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt\bullet}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}
be the filtration on
M_{j}/M_{j-1}
given by
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pti}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}=(\mathcal{G}^{i}\cap M_{j}+M_{j-1})/M_{j-1}
.
We note that, because
\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pti}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}\cong(\mathcal{G}^{i}\cap M_{j})/(\mathcal{G}^{i}\cap M_{j-1})
, the sum
\sum^{t}_{j=1}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pti}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}
telescopes to
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{i}
for each
i
.
By
Lemma

3.3
,

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt(C+1)i}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt(C+2)i}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}

for every
j
and all sufficiently large
i
.
Adding up over
j=1,\ldots,t
, we obtain

\displaystyle t\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{F}^{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{t}\Big(\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt(C+1)i}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt(C+2)i}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt(C+1)i}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{t}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt(C+2)i}_{\hskip-0.24582ptj}\bigg)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i}.

Dividing by
i^{2\theta}
and taking limit superior yields

\displaystyle t\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i}}{i^{2\theta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg(\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}}{i^{\theta}}\bigg)\bigg(\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i}}{i^{\theta}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}\bigg(\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+1)i}}{((C+1)i)^{\theta}}\bigg)\bigg(\limsup_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{(C+2)i}}{((C+2)i)^{\theta}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})^{2}.

We conclude that

t\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})^{2}(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}}{\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}

which proves our two claims.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Theorem

3.8
shows in particular that any holonomic module is finitely generated, so the notion of good filtration applies, and adopting the notation of that theorem, we have

\operatorname{length}_{A}M\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{e}(M,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})^{2}(C+1)^{\theta}(C+2)^{\theta}}{\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(A,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
be a linearly simple filtered
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with nonzero finite dimension and multiplicity.
Then any holonomic
A
-module is cyclic.

Proof.

This follows essentially from the classic proof for the Weyl algebra
[
Cou95
, Theorem 10.2.5]
.
Note that the same proof works in this context without the left Noetherian hypothesis on the ring, as
M
has finite length and
A
is not an Artinian module over itself.
∎

4.
Rings of differential operators and Bernstein algebras

From this section onward, we direct our focus to rings of differential operators on commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebras.
After recalling some basic terminology, we take a look at local cohomology modules from the standpoint of
D
-module theory, and use the machinery developed in the previous sections to determine sufficient conditions under which local cohomology modules are holonomic—which in particular implies the finiteness of the sets of associated primes of these modules.
Narrowing our focus to rings of differential operators on commutative finitely generated graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebras, we introduce a class of filtrations on those rings that generalize the classic Bernstein filtration on the Weyl algebra.
We conclude by introducing a class of algebras over which one can develop a nice theory of holonomic
D
-modules.

4.1.
Rings of differential operators

Generalities

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and consider the ring of
\mathbb{k}
-linear endomorphisms
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{k}}(R)
.
The
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators of order
\leqslant i
, where
i
is a nonnegative integer, are defined inductively as follows:
A differential operator of order
0
is simply the multiplication by an element of
R
.
If
i>0
, then a differential operator of order
\leqslant i
is a
\mathbb{k}
-linear map
\delta:R\to R
such that for
every
r\in R
, the commutator
[\delta,r]\coloneqq\delta\circ r-r\circ\delta
is a differential operator of order
\leqslant i-1
, where we consider
r:R\to R
as the multiplication by
r
.
Equivalently, a
\mathbb{k}
-linear map
\delta:R\to R
is a differential operator of order
\leqslant i
if for every
r_{0},\ldots,r_{i}\in R
the
(i+1)
-fold commutator
[\cdots[[\delta,r_{0}],r_{1}],\ldots,r_{i}]
is zero.
We say that
\delta\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{k}}(R)
is a differential operator of order
i
, and write
\operatorname{ord}(\delta)=i
, if
\delta
is a differential operator of order
\leqslant i
, but not of order
<i
.

Remark 4.1
.

For later use, we note that if
R
is generated as a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra by a set
\Sigma
, then to verify that
\delta\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{k}}(R)
is a differential operator of order
\leqslant i
, it suffices to verify that
[\delta,r]
is a differential operator of order
\leqslant i-1
for all
r\in\Sigma
, or that
[\cdots[[\delta,r_{0}],r_{1}],\ldots,r_{i}]=0
for all
r_{0},\ldots,r_{i}\in\Sigma
.

The set consisting of all
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators on
R
of order
\leqslant i
is a
\mathbb{k}
-subspace of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{k}}(R)
, which we denote by
D^{i}_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.
Differential operators of all orders form a ring

D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}D^{i}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\subseteq{\operatorname{End}}_{\mathbb{k}}(R).

The chain of
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces
D^{0}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\subseteq D^{1}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\subseteq D^{2}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\subseteq\cdots
is called the
order filtration
on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, though we caution the reader that this is not a filtration in the sense of
Definition

2.1
, since these are generally not finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces and
D^{0}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\cong R\not\cong\mathbb{k}
.
Despite that, we occasionally extend some terminology introduced for filtrations to the order filtration—specifically, we say that a filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is linearly dominated by the order filtration if there exists
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{Ci}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Differential operators in positive characteristic

Turning to positive characteristic, suppose now that
R
is a commutative algebra over a perfect field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic
p>0
.
Assume that
R
is
F
-finite, that is,
R
is finitely generated as an
R^{p^{e}}
-module for some (equivalently, all)
e>0
, where
R^{p^{e}}
is the subring of
R
consisting of all
p^{e}
-th powers of its elements.
Then

D_{R|\mathbb{k}}=\bigcup_{e\in\mathbb{N}}D^{(e)}_{R|\mathbb{k}}

where
D^{(e)}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\coloneqq\operatorname{End}_{R^{p^{e}}}(R)
consists of the differential operators of
level
e

[
Smi87
, Theorem 2.7]
(see also
[
Yek92
, Theorem 1.4.9]
).

If
R
is reduced, we may consider the ring
R^{1/p^{e}}=\{r^{1/p^{e}}\;|\;r\in R\}
consisting of the
p^{e}
-th roots of the elements of
R
, and identify
D^{(e)}_{R|\mathbb{k}}
with
\operatorname{End}_{R}(R^{1/p^{e}})
via the map

\delta\mapsto\delta^{1/p^{e}}
, where

(4.1)

\delta^{1/p^{e}}(r^{1/p^{e}})=\delta(r)^{1/p^{e}}.

We note that
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{p^{e}-1}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}
, and so

(4.2)

D^{i}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(\lceil\log_{p}(i+1)\rceil)}

and if
R
is generated by
n
elements as a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, then

(4.3)

D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}\subseteq D^{n(p^{e}-1)}_{R|\mathbb{k}}.

Both of these facts are established in the work of Smith
[
Smi87
, Theorem 2.7]
.

Differential operators on polynomial rings

If
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, then
D_{S|\mathbb{k}}
coincides with the Weyl algebra
S\langle\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}\rangle
, where
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial\ \,}{\partial x_{i}}
are the partial derivatives.
A
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operator on
S
can be written, in its unique
normal form
, as
\delta=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, all but finitely many of the coefficients
a_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb{k}
are zero, and we adopt the multi-index notation
x^{\alpha}\coloneqq x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
,
\partial^{\beta}\coloneqq\partial_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}
.

The order filtration in this case is given by

D^{i}_{S|\mathbb{k}}=\bigg\{\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}:|\beta|\leqslant i\bigg\},

where
|\beta|=\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{n}
.
Note that the
D^{i}_{S|\mathbb{k}}
are not finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces.
The main example of a finite-dimensional filtration on
D_{S|\mathbb{k}}
is the
Bernstein filtration

\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S}
given by

\mathcal{B}^{i}_{S}=\bigg\{\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha\beta}x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}:|\alpha|+|\beta|\leqslant i\bigg\},

which we shall study in greater generality in
Section

4.4
.

Remark 4.2
.

In the terminology used by Smith
[
Smi01
]
and Boldoni
[
Bol13
]
, the order filtration and the Bernstein filtration are filtrations associated to the weight vectors
(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
and
(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, respectively.
More generally one may consider filtrations associated to weight vectors
(\underline{u},\underline{v})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
.

If
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a polynomial ring with coefficients in a perfect field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic
p>0
, then

D_{S|\mathbb{k}}=S\left\langle\frac{1}{p^{e}!}\partial_{i}^{p^{e}}\ \big|\ i=1,\dots,n,\ e\in\mathbb{N}\right\rangle

where
\frac{1}{p^{e}!}\partial_{i}^{p^{e}}
is the operator that maps
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\mapsto\binom{\alpha_{i}}{p^{e}}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-p^{e}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
(so, in particular,
x_{i}^{p^{e}}\mapsto 1
).

Differential operators on finitely generated algebras

Returning to arbitrary characteristic, we now consider differential operators on finitely generated algebras.
Let
S
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of arbitrary characteristic, and
R=S/I
for some ideal
I\subseteq S
.
The ring of
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators on
R
has been described in terms of the
\mathbb{k}
-linear differential operators on
S

[
MR87
, Theorem 15.5.13]
(see also
[
Mil86
,
MM18
]
).
Namely, we have

(4.4)

D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\cong\frac{\{\delta\in D_{S|\mathbb{k}}:\delta(I)\subseteq I\}}{ID_{S|\mathbb{k}}}.

The order of the differential operators is preserved under the previous isomorphism; thus the order filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is given by

D^{i}_{R|\mathbb{k}}\cong\frac{\{\delta\in D^{i}_{S|\mathbb{k}}\ |\ \delta(I)\subseteq I\}}{ID^{i}_{S|\mathbb{k}}}.

Differential operators on graded algebras

When discussing commutative graded algebras, we shall adopt the following convention.

Convention 4.3
.

Throughout this paper, by a
commutative graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
we mean a positively graded commutative ring
R=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}R_{i}
with
R_{0}=\mathbb{k}
, that is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.

If
R
is a commutative graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, the ring of differential operators
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
naturally inherits a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading that extends the grading on
R
, where we declare a differential operator
\delta:R\to R
to be homogeneous of degree
d
if
\delta(R_{i})\subseteq R_{i+d}
for each
i
.

If
S
is a standard graded polynomial ring, then under the previous grading, the partial derivatives
\partial_{i}
are homogeneous of degree
-1
.
More generally, if we consider
S
as a graded ring with
\deg(x_{i})=w_{i}
, then

D_{S|\mathbb{k}}
is a graded ring with
\deg(\partial_{i})=-w_{i}
, and
\deg\big(\frac{1}{p^{e}!}\partial_{i}^{p^{e}}\big)=-p^{e}w_{i}
when
\mathbb{k}
is a field of positive characteristic
p
.
As a consequence, if
\delta\in D^{i}_{S|\mathbb{k}}
, then
\deg(\delta)\geqslant-iw
, where
w=\max\{w_{i}\}
.
By (
4.4
), if
I
is a homogeneous ideal of
S
and
R=S/I
, then
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is a graded algebra, again satisfying
\deg(\delta)\geqslant-iw
for every
\delta\in D^{i}_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.

4.2.
Čech and local cohomology as
D
-modules

A commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R
has a natural structure of a left
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module.
In general, given a
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module
M
and an element
f\in R
, the localization
M_{f}
is also a left
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module.
We define the action of a differential operator
\delta
of order zero by
\delta(\frac{v}{f^{t}})=\frac{\delta(v)}{f^{t}}
.
Assuming the action has been defined for differential operators of order less than
n
, for
\delta\in D^{n}_{R|\mathbb{k}}
we define

\delta\left(\frac{v}{f^{t}}\right)=\frac{\delta(v)-[\delta,f^{t}](\frac{v}{f^{t}})}{f^{t}}.

Note that this well defined, since
[\delta,f^{t}]
has order at most
n-1
.
With this
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module structure on
M_{f}
, the
localization map
M\to M_{f}
is a morphism of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules.

The Čech complex of
M
with respect to a sequence of elements
\underline{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{\ell}\in R
is defined by

\check{\rm{C}}^{\bullet}(\underline{f};M):\hskip 8.53581pt0\to M\to\bigoplus_{i}M_{f_{i}}\to\bigoplus_{i,j}M_{f_{i}f_{j}}\to\cdots\to M_{f_{1}\cdots f_{\ell}}\to 0

where the maps on every summand are localization maps up to a sign.
The Čech cohomology modules of
M
with respect to the sequence
\underline{f}
are defined by

H^{j}_{\underline{f}}(M)=H^{j}(\check{\rm{C}}^{\bullet}(\underline{f};M)).

If
\underline{g}
is another sequence of elements of
R
such that
(\underline{f})=(\underline{g})\eqqcolon I
, then
H^{j}_{\underline{f}}(M)=H^{j}_{\underline{g}}(M)
for each
j
, which justifies our denoting this module simply by
H^{j}_{I}(M)
.

The Čech cohomology modules
H^{j}_{I}(M)
inherit a
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module structure from their construction, and agree with the local cohomology modules of
M
with support in
I
whenever
R
is a Noetherian ring.

We show in this section that holonomicity is preserved by localization and Čech cohomology.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra. Let
f\in R
and
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.
Set
\delta^{(0)}=\delta
, and for each positive integer
i
define
\delta^{(i)}\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
inductively by
\delta^{(i)}=[\delta^{(i-1)},f]
.
Then for each
j\in\mathbb{Z}
we have the following identities in
D_{R_{f}|\mathbb{k}}
:

\delta f^{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{\operatorname{ord}(\delta)}\binom{j}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{f}^{j}\delta=\sum_{i=0}^{\operatorname{ord}(\delta)}(-1)^{i}\binom{j}{i}\delta^{(i)}f^{j-i},

where
\binom{j}{i}=\frac{j\cdot(j-1)\cdots(j-i+1)}{i!}
.

Proof.

The proofs of the two identities are similar, so we prove only the first one.
We first prove it for
j\geqslant 0
by induction on
j
, observing that the identity holds trivially for
j=0
.
For
j>0
, using our induction hypothesis on
\delta f^{j-1}
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta f^{j}

\displaystyle=\delta f^{j-1}f=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j-1}{i}f^{j-1-i}\delta^{(i)}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j-1}{i}f^{j-1-i}\big(f\delta^{(i)}+\delta^{(i+1)}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j-1}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}+\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j-1}{i}f^{j-1-i}\delta^{(i+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j-1}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}+\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j-1}{i-1}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}.

We now prove the first identity for
j\leqslant 0
, using induction on
\operatorname{ord}(\delta)-j
, observing that the identity holds trivially when
j=0
or
\operatorname{ord}(\delta)=0
.
If
j<0
, we have

\delta f^{j}=f^{-1}f\delta f^{j}=f^{-1}\big(\delta f-\delta^{(1)}\big)f^{j}=f^{-1}\delta f^{j+1}-f^{-1}\delta^{(1)}f^{j},

and applying our induction hypothesis to both
\delta f^{j+1}
and
\delta^{(1)}f^{j}
we see that

\displaystyle\delta f^{j}

\displaystyle=f^{-1}\cdot\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j+1}{i}f^{j+1-i}\delta^{(i)}-f^{-1}\cdot\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j+1}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}-\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j}{i-1}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\binom{j}{i}f^{j-i}\delta^{(i)}.\qed

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
be a filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
that is linearly dominated by the order filtration.
Let
M
be a left
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module with a filtration
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
that is compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
has finite dimension
\theta
and finite multiplicity.
Then for any
f\in R
, there exists a filtration
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt\bullet}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}
on
M_{f}
that is compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
, has dimension at most
\theta
, and if its dimension equals
\theta
, then its multiplicity is finite.

Proof.

Given
f\in R
, choose
a
such that
f\in\mathcal{F}^{a}
; fix
C
such that
\mathcal{F}^{i}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{Ci}
for all
i
.
Set
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974ptj}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}\coloneqq\frac{1}{f^{Cj}}\mathcal{G}^{j(Ca+1)}
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt\bullet}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}
is a finite-dimensional, ascending, exhaustive filtration on
M_{f}
, and the claims about the dimension and multiplicity of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pt{}^{\bullet}}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}
follow from the fact that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974ptj}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}\leqslant\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{G}^{j(Ca+1)}
for each
j
.

We need to verify that this filtration is compatible with
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.
Using the notation of
Lemma

4.4
, if the order of
\delta
is less than or equal to
t
, we get

f^{j+t}\delta f^{-j}=\delta f^{t}-\binom{j+t}{1}\delta^{(1)}f^{t-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{t}\binom{j+t}{t}\delta^{(t)}

as an equality in
D_{R_{f}|\mathbb{k}}
; in particular, this operator restricts to an operator in
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.
If
\delta\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, then
\delta
has order at most
Ci
and
\delta^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}^{i+ak}
for all
k
, so the previous equation shows that
f^{Cj+Ci}\delta f^{-Cj}\in\mathcal{F}^{i(Ca+1)}.

We then have

\displaystyle\delta\cdot\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974ptj}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}

\displaystyle=\delta\cdot\frac{1}{f^{Cj}}\mathcal{G}^{j(Ca+1)}\subseteq\frac{1}{f^{Cj+Ci}}\mathcal{F}^{i(Ca+1)}\mathcal{G}^{j(Ca+1)}

\displaystyle\subseteq\frac{1}{f^{C(i+j)}}\mathcal{G}^{(i+j)(Ca+1)}=\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\hskip 0.40974pti+j}_{\hskip-0.24582pt}

as required.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
be a filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
that is linearly dominated by the order filtration.
Suppose that
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is linearly simple with finite dimension and finite positive multiplicity.
If
M
is a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module, then the following hold.

(i)

M_{f}
is holonomic for every
f\in R
.

(ii)

H^{j}_{I}(M)
is holonomic for every finitely generated ideal
I\subseteq R
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

The claim for a localization of
M
follows immediately from
Lemma

4.5
.
Finite direct sums of localizations of
M
are therefore holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules by
Proposition

3.6
(ii)
.
Thus, the kernel of any map in the Čech complex is holonomic by
Proposition

3.6
(i)
, and since each
H^{j}_{I}(M)
is a quotient of one of these kernels, it is holonomic, again by
Proposition

3.6
(i)
.
∎

We now proceed to show that the previous result and
Theorem

3.8
imply that the Čech cohomology modules of a holonomic
D
-module have finite sets of associated primes.
Toward that end, we first show that simple
D
-modules have at most one associated prime.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
If
M
is a simple
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module, then
M
has at most one associated prime.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Bjö79
, Lemmas 3.3.16 and 3.3.17]
, which we reproduce here for the reader’s convenience.
Given a simple left
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module
M
, let
u\in M\smallsetminus\{0\}
, so that
M=D_{R|\mathbb{k}}u
.
We claim that
\mathfrak{p}\coloneqq\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(u)}
is prime and independent of the choice of
u
.
From this, it follows easily that
\mathfrak{p}
is the only possible associated prime of
M
(though
M
could potentially fail to have associated primes).

To verify that
\mathfrak{p}
does not depend on the choice of
u
, let
v
be another nonzero element of
M
.
Then there exists
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
such that
v=\delta u
.
Suppose
f\in R
is such that
f^{i}u=0
for some
i
.
If
\delta
has order
j
, then
Lemma

4.4
tells us that
f^{i+j}\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}f^{i}
, and consequently,
f^{i+j}v=0
.
This shows that
\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(u)}\subseteq\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(v)}
, and switching the roles of
u
and
v
we get the reverse containment.

To verify that
\mathfrak{p}
is prime, let
f,g\in R
and suppose that
fg\in\mathfrak{p}
, but
g\notin\mathfrak{p}
.
Thus,
(fg)^{i}u=0
for some
i
, but
v\coloneqq g^{i}u\neq 0
, and it follows that
f\in\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}_{R}(v)}=\mathfrak{p}
.
Lastly, note that
\mathfrak{p}
is proper, since
M
is nontrivial.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
R
be a commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
be a filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
that is linearly dominated by the order filtration.
Suppose that
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is linearly simple with finite dimension and positive finite multiplicity.
If
M
is a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module, then
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(M)
is a finite set for every finitely generated ideal
I\subseteq R
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

If
M
is a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module, then so is
H^{i}_{I}(M)
by
Theorem

4.6
(ii).

Theorem

3.8
then shows that
H^{i}_{I}(M)
has finite length as a
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module, so there exists an ascending chain

0=M_{0}\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_{\ell}=H^{i}_{I}(M)

of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules such that each quotient
M_{j}/M_{j-1}
is simple.
We conclude that
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}H^{i}_{I}(M)
is contained in
\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\operatorname{Ass}_{R}M_{j}/M_{j-1}
, a finite set by
Lemma

4.7
.
∎

As a side note, we point out that a prime ideal that is minimal over the annihilator of an element of a module is called a
weakly associated prime
of the module
[
Bou07
, p. 341, Exercise 17]
.
In
Lemma

4.7
we actually showed that simple
D
-modules have a unique weakly associated prime, while in
Proposition

4.8
we showed that the Čech cohomology modules of a holonomic
D
-module have finitely many weakly associated primes.

4.3.
Holonomicity of the module
R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}

Let
R
be a commutative algebra over a field
\mathbb{k}
and
f
a nonzero element of
R
.
Another
D
-module of classical importance in the smooth case is the
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
-module
R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
, where
s
is an indeterminate and
\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
is a formal symbol.
This module is characterized by the property that for any integer
t
, any
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
, and any
a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}\in R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
, the equality

(\delta\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}}a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}})\big|_{s\mapsto t}=\delta\big|_{s\mapsto t}(a(t)f^{t})

holds, where
(-)|_{s\mapsto t}
denotes specialization of the indeterminate
s
to the integer
t
.

Over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, any finitely generated or complete local
\mathbb{k}
-algebra admits a unique such module up to isomorphism
[
AMHJ+21
, Theorem 2.12]
.
Note that this module is denoted by
M^{R}[\boldsymbol{f^{s}}]
in
loc. cit.

We first give a more concrete description of this module. For
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
, we define the
order
of
\delta
to be
\min\{i\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ \delta\in D^{i}_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]\}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
R
be a finitely generated commutative algebra over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, and
f
a nonzero element of
R
.
For
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
, set
\delta^{(0)}=\delta
, and for each positive integer
i
define
\delta^{(i)}\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
inductively by
\delta^{(i)}=[\delta^{(i-1)},f]
.
Then the action of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
on
R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
is given by the rule

\delta\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}}a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\delta)}\binom{s}{i}f^{-i}\delta^{(i)}(a(s))\boldsymbol{f^{s}},

where
\binom{s}{i}
is the polynomial
\frac{s\cdot(s-1)\cdots(s-i+1)}{i!}
.

Proof.

First we show that this rule yields a module action. Let
\Theta:D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\to D_{R_{f}|\mathbb{k}}[s]
be given by the rule

\Theta(\delta)=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\delta)}\binom{s}{i}f^{-i}\delta^{(i)}.

We claim that this is a ring homomorphism. For this, it suffices to show that
\Theta
respects multiplication. To see this, first observe that by a straightforward induction, for any
\alpha,\beta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
we have

(\alpha\beta)^{(i)}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}\alpha^{(j)}\beta^{(i-j)}.

Then

\displaystyle\Theta(\alpha\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\alpha\beta)}\binom{s}{i}f^{-i}(\alpha\beta)^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\alpha\beta)}\binom{s}{i}f^{-i}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}\alpha^{(j)}\beta^{(i-j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geqslant 0}\binom{s}{j+k}\binom{j+k}{j}f^{-j-k}\alpha^{(j)}\beta^{(k)}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k\geqslant 0}\binom{s}{k}\binom{s-k}{j}f^{-j-k}\alpha^{(j)}\beta^{(k)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Theta(\alpha)\Theta(\beta)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\alpha)}\binom{s}{i}f^{-i}\alpha^{(i)}\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\beta)}\binom{s}{j}f^{-j}\beta^{(j)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\geqslant 0}\binom{s}{i}\binom{s}{j}f^{-i}\left(\alpha^{(i)}f^{-j}\right)\beta^{(j)},

and applying
Lemma

4.4
to
\alpha^{(i)}f^{-j}
we see that

\Theta(\alpha)\Theta(\beta)=\sum_{i,j,k\geqslant 0}\binom{s}{i}\binom{s}{j}\binom{-j}{k}f^{-i-j-k}\alpha^{(i+k)}\beta^{(j)}.

Grouping the summands according to the value of
l\coloneqq i+k
, this can be rewritten as

\Theta(\alpha)\Theta(\beta)=\sum_{j,l\geqslant 0}\left(\sum_{i,k\geqslant 0\atop i+k=l}\binom{s}{i}\binom{-j}{k}\right)\binom{s}{j}f^{-l-j}\alpha^{(l)}\beta^{(j)}

where the sum in parentheses is a Vandermonde convolution that adds up to
\binom{s-j}{l}
, so this expression indeed equals (
4.5
).
Thus
\Theta
is a homomorphism. We can extend
\Theta
to
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]\to D_{R_{f}|\mathbb{k}}[s]
by setting
\Theta(s)=s
; we use the same name
\Theta
for this extension.

Now note that there is a
D_{R_{f}|\mathbb{k}}[s]
-action
\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.55}{$\clubsuit$}}}}
on
R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
given by taking the standard action of
D_{R_{f}|\mathbb{k}}
on
R_{f}
and extending in such a way that
s
commutes with every operator. The action defined in the statement agrees with the action
\delta\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}}a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}\coloneqq\Theta(\delta)\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.55}{$\clubsuit$}}}}a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
. It follows that this is a module action.

Next, given
a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}\in R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
,
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}[s]
, and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle(\delta\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}}a(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}})\big|_{s\mapsto t}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\delta)}\binom{s}{i}f^{-i}\delta^{(i)}(a(s))\boldsymbol{f^{s}}\right)\Bigg|_{s\mapsto t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{ord}(\delta)}\binom{t}{i}f^{t-i}\big(\delta\big|_{s\mapsto t}\big)^{(i)}(a(t))

\displaystyle=(\delta\big|_{s\mapsto t}f^{t})(a(t))

\displaystyle=\delta\big|_{s\mapsto t}(a(t)f^{t}).

We conclude that the module with the stated action is isomorphic to
R_{f}[s]\boldsymbol{f^{s}}

[
AMHJ+21
, Theorem 2.12]
.
∎

4.4.
Generalized Bernstein filtrations

The aim of this subsection is to extend the Bernstein filtration beyond the polynomial ring case.
We assume the following:

Setup 4.14
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
and
R
a commutative graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, as in
4.3
.
Set
\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{i>0}R_{i}
,
n=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, and
w=\max\{t\in\mathbb{N}\;|\;[\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}]_{t}\neq 0\}
.
We fix a polynomial ring
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over
\mathbb{k}
, a grading on
S
with
\deg x_{i}=w_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, and a homogeneous ideal
I\subseteq S
such that
R\cong S/I
as graded rings.
We observe that
w=\max\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\}
.

Definition 4.15
.

Let
R
be as in
4.14
, and
a
a real number greater than
w
.
The
generalized Bernstein filtration
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{a,R}
on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
with slope
a
is given by

\mathcal{B}^{i}_{a,R}\coloneqq\mathbb{k}\cdot\{\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\ \text{homogeneous}\ |\ \deg(\delta)+a\operatorname{ord}(\delta)\leqslant i\}.

If the slope is clear from the context, or irrelevant, then we simply write
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
.

Example 4.16
.

Let
S
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field of characteristic zero. Then the generalized Bernstein filtration with slope
2
is just the usual Bernstein filtration on the Weyl algebra.
If
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a positively graded polynomial ring with
\deg(x_{i})=w_{i}
, we may interpret the generalized Bernstein filtration with integral slope
a
, in the context of
Remark

4.2
, as the filtration associated to the weight vector
(\underline{w},a-\underline{w})\coloneqq(w_{1},\dots,w_{n},a-w_{1},\dots,a-w_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
.

Lemma 4.17
.

If
R
is as in
4.14
and
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{a,R}
is a generalized Bernstein filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, then the following hold.

(i)

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{B}^{i}_{a,R}
is finite for every
i
.

(ii)

There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\deg(\delta)+(a-\varepsilon)\operatorname{ord}(\delta)>0
for all homogeneous
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\smallsetminus\mathbb{k}
.

Proof.

By explicit computation, this holds for a polynomial ring. It then follows for
R
from the fact that a differential operator on a quotient of a polynomial ring is the image of a differential operator on the polynomial ring.
∎

Proposition 4.18
.

If
R
is as in
4.14
, then every generalized Bernstein filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra filtration in the sense of
Definition

2.1
.

Proof.

The generalized Bernstein filtration
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{a,R}
satisfies
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{a,R}\mathcal{B}^{j}_{a,R}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{i+j}_{a,R}
for each
i
and
j
because
\operatorname{ord}(\delta_{1}\delta_{2})\leqslant\operatorname{ord}(\delta_{1})+\operatorname{ord}(\delta_{2})
and
\deg(\delta_{1}\delta_{2})\leqslant\deg(\delta_{1})+\deg(\delta_{2})
for any homogeneous operators
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.
This filtration is ascending, and it is finite dimensional by
Lemma

4.17
(i).
It is exhaustive because any homogeneous operator
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
lies in
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{a,R}
for
i=\lceil\deg(\delta)+a\operatorname{ord}(\delta)\rceil
.
Finally, if
\delta
is a homogeneous element of
\mathcal{B}^{0}_{a,R}
, then
\deg(\delta)+a\operatorname{ord}(\delta)\leqslant 0
, so

4.17
(ii) tells us that
\delta\in\mathbb{k}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{B}^{0}_{a,R}\subseteq\mathbb{k}
, and since the reverse inclusion is obvious, equality holds.
∎

Proposition 4.19
.

If
R
is as in
4.14
and
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
is a generalized Bernstein filtration on

, then
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
is linearly dominated by the order filtration.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
has slope
a
.
By
Lemma

4.17
(ii) we have some
\varepsilon>0
such that
\deg(\delta)+(a-\varepsilon)\operatorname{ord}(\delta)>0
for every homogeneous operator
\delta
not in
\mathbb{k}
.
Set
C=\lceil 1/\varepsilon\rceil
.
Then, for
\delta
homogeneous in
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\smallsetminus\mathbb{k}
we have
\varepsilon\operatorname{ord}(\delta)<\deg(\delta)+a\operatorname{ord}(\delta)\leqslant i
, so

, from which it follows that

.
∎

Next, we show that all generalized Bernstein filtrations on

are linearly equivalent.

.

If

is as in

, then any two generalized Bernstein filtrations

If

The following is an immediate consequence of

and

.

If

is as in
4.14

Other consequences of

Coming back to the case of a polynomial ring

Proposition 4.22
.

Let
S
be a positively graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero.
If
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S}
is a generalized Bernstein filtration on
D_{S|\mathbb{k}}
, then
(D_{S|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S})
is linearly simple; that is, there exists a positive integer
C
such that for every

and every nonzero

1\in\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{S}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{S}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S}
has slope
a
, which we may assume is an integer, by
Proposition

4.21
.
Suppose that
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\deg(x_{j})=-\deg(\partial_{j})=w_{j}
for each
j
, so that
x_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{w_{j}}_{S}
and
\partial_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{a-w_{j}}_{S}
.
We shall verify that the claim holds for
C=\max\{w_{j},a-w_{j}\}
.
This is true when
i=0
, so suppose that
i>0
and
\delta\in\mathcal{B}^{i}_{S}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
Let
\varepsilon_{j}
denote the
j
-th standard basis vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The identity
[x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta},\partial_{j}]=-\alpha_{j}x^{\alpha-\varepsilon_{j}}\partial^{\beta}
shows that, as long as the normal form of
\delta
contains a monomial
x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}
with
\alpha_{j}\neq 0
for some
j
, the commutator
[\delta,\partial_{j}]
is a nonzero element of
\mathcal{B}^{i-w_{j}}_{S}
.
An inductive argument then allows us to assume that
1\in\mathcal{B}^{C(i-w_{j})}_{S}[\delta,\partial_{j}]\mathcal{B}^{C(i-w_{j})}_{S}
.
But
[\delta,\partial_{j}]
lies in
\mathcal{B}^{a-w_{j}}_{S}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{a-w_{j}}_{S}
, so we conclude that

1\in\mathcal{B}^{C(i-w_{j})}_{S}\mathcal{B}^{a-w_{j}}_{S}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{a-w_{j}}_{S}\mathcal{B}^{C(i-w_{j})}_{S}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{S}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{S}

where the last containment follows from the inequalities

and
w_{j}\geqslant 1
.

If the normal form of
\delta
contains a monomial
\partial^{\beta}
with
\beta_{j}\neq 0
for some
j
, we mimic the previous argument using, instead, the identity
[\partial^{\beta},x_{j}]=\beta_{j}\partial^{\beta-\varepsilon_{j}}
.
This time around, we use the inequalities
C\geqslant w_{j}
and
a-w_{j}\geqslant 1
, the latter being a consequence of our assumption that
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
a>w
.

Finally, if neither condition is satisfied, then
\delta\in\mathbb{k}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, and the result follows.
∎

We conclude this subsection by showing that the associated graded ring of a generalized Bernstein filtration on a polynomial ring is itself a polynomial ring.

Proposition 4.23
.

Let

is a generalized Bernstein filtration on

with an integral slope, then the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S})
is a polynomial ring in

Proof.

Let

be the slope of
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S}
, and assume that
\deg(x_{j})=-\deg(\partial_{j})=w_{j}
, for each

.
As in
Example

4.16
,
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S}
is the filtration associated to the weight vector

.
Since

for all

, the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{S})
is a polynomial ring in
2n
variables
[

, Proposition 2.2, Example 2.4]
.
∎

4.5.

We retain the hypotheses of

We note first that dimension for the ring of differential operators is not well defined in general.

Proposition 4.25

If

Theorem 4.32
.

Let
R
be as in
4.14
, and
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
a generalized Bernstein filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
.
If
\operatorname{s}^{\operatorname{diff}}(R)>0
and
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
is linearly simple, then
R
is a Bernstein algebra.
In particular,
R
, its localizations
R_{f}
for
f\in R
, and its local cohomology modules
H^{j}_{I}(R)
for
I\subseteq R
are all holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules.
Likewise, if
\mathbb{k}
has characteristic zero, then
R_{f}(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
is a holonomic
D_{R(s)|\mathbb{k}(s)}
-module for any nonzero element
f\in R
.

Proof.

For each positive integer
i
, set
\alpha_{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}R/\mathfrak{m}^{{\langle i\rangle}}
.
By
Lemma

4.30
, there exist
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\alpha_{i}}\in D^{i-1}_{R|\mathbb{k}}
and
f_{1},\ldots,f_{\alpha_{i}}\in R
homogeneous such that
\delta_{j}(f_{k})=0
if
j\neq k
, and
\delta_{j}(f_{j})=1
.
In particular, this implies that
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\alpha_{i}}
are linearly independent over
R
.
Let
a
be the slope of
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
, and
d=\dim(R)
.
Noting that
\deg(\delta_{j})\leqslant 0
for each
j
, we see that
[R]_{\leqslant i}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\alpha_{i}}\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{(a+1)i}_{R}
, and thus

\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}}{i^{2d}}\geqslant\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{B}^{(a+1)i}_{R}}{((a+1)i)^{2d}}\geqslant\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([R]_{\leqslant i}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\alpha_{i}}\})}{((a+1)i)^{2d}}.

The linear independence of the
\delta_{j}
over
R
implies that
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}([R]_{\leqslant i}\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{\alpha_{i}}\})=\alpha_{i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}[R]_{\leqslant i}
, so

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}}{i^{2d}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_{i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}[R]_{\leqslant i}}{((a+1)i)^{2d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{2d}}\cdot\limsup\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{i^{d}}\cdot\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{k}}[R]_{\leqslant i}}{i^{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{2d}(d!)^{2}}\cdot\operatorname{s}^{\operatorname{diff}}(R)\operatorname{e}(R).

Since the last quantity is positive, this shows that
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})\geqslant 2d
.

To prove the reverse inequality, let
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be as in
Lemma

4.31
.
Then
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
is a filtration on the
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module
R
compatible with
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
, of dimension
d=\dim(R)
, so Bernstein’s inequality (
Theorem

3.4
) tells us that
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})\leqslant 2d
.

Having established that
\operatorname{Dim}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})=2d
, the calculation displayed above also shows that
\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
is positive, whereas
Theorem

3.4
tells us that if
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
is
C
-linearly simple, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})

\displaystyle\leqslant(C+1)^{d}(C+2)^{d}\operatorname{e}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet})^{2}

\displaystyle=(C+1)^{d}(C+2)^{d}\operatorname{e}(R)^{2}<\infty.

The claims about holonomicity follow from
Theorems

4.6
,
4.19
,
4.31
and
4.11
.
∎

In the final two sections of this paper, we shall use
Theorem

4.32
to introduce two interesting classes of Bernstein algebras.

5.
Rings of invariants of finite groups in characteristic zero

Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, and
G
a finite group acting linearly on
R
.
The goal of this section is to prove that the ring of invariants
R^{G}
is a Bernstein algebra.
In particular, Bernstein’s inequality is satisfied, and the dimension of
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
is twice the dimension of
R^{G}
.
We also relate holonomic modules over
R
and over
R^{G}
.

5.1.
Bernstein inequality for rings of invariants

If
R
and
G
are as in the preceding paragraph, the action of
G
on
R
induces a degree and order-preserving action on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, defined as follows: for each
g\in G
and
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, we define
g\cdot\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
by

(g\cdot\delta)(r)\coloneqq g\cdot\delta(g^{-1}\cdot r).

It is easy to verify that if
\delta\in(D_{R|\mathbb{k}})^{G}
, then
\delta
maps
R^{G}
into itself, so we have a well-defined map

(5.1)

(D_{R|\mathbb{k}})^{G}\to D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}

given by restriction.
Note that as
G
is finite, this map is injective
[
Sch95
, Theorem 6.3(1)]
(see also
[
Tra06
, Theorem 2]
for an elementary proof).

For our applications, we need the restriction map (
5.1
) to be not merely injective, but surjective as well, to allow us to relate generalized Bernstein filtrations on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
and on
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
.
This is made possible by the following result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group that acts linearly on a polynomial ring
R
over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Assume that
|G|
is nonzero in
\mathbb{k}
.
Then there exists a normal subgroup
H\trianglelefteq G
such that
R^{H}
is a polynomial ring
(
that may not be standard graded
)
, and such that
\big(D^{i}_{R^{H}|\mathbb{k}}\big)^{G/H}\cong D^{i}_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
under the natural restriction map for every
i
.

Proof.

We identify
R
with the ring of polynomial functions on a
\mathbb{k}
-vector space
V
.
Let
H\leqslant G
be the subgroup generated by all elements
g
in
G
such that the rank of
\operatorname{id}-g
on
V
is one; such elements are called pseudoreflections in the literature.
The subgroup
H
is normal, and
R^{H}
is a polynomial ring by the Shephard–Todd Theorem
[
ST54
]
(see also
[
NS02
, Theorem 7.1.4]
for a modern proof, in arbitrary characteristic).

The inclusion map
{R^{G}\to R^{H}}
is étale in codimension one.
This is well known to experts, but we include an argument for convenience of the reader.
Since the étale locus is preserved by base change, we may assume that
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed.
Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\in G
be a set of representatives for
G/H\smallsetminus\{H\}
. Let
X\subseteq V
be the union of the fixed spaces of
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}
.
Note that
X
has codimension at least two in
V
.
It suffices to show that for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
that corresponds to a point
v\in V\smallsetminus X
, the inclusion map
{R^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}\cap R^{G}}\to R^{H}_{\mathfrak{m}\cap R^{H}}}
is étale.
To this end, we note that the stabilizer of
v
in
G
is contained in
H
.
Then, by
[
Kem02
, Proposition 1.1]
, the inclusion map induces an isomorphism on the completions
{\widehat{R^{G}}_{\mathfrak{m}\cap R^{G}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\widehat{R^{H}}_{\mathfrak{m}\cap R^{H}}}
, and the claim follows.

Thus, the restriction map

{D^{i}_{R^{H}|\mathbb{k}}\to D^{i}_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}\big(R^{G},R^{H}\big)},

where
D^{i}_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}\big(R^{G},R^{H}\big)
denotes the differential operators from
R^{G}
to
R^{H}
as
R^{G}
-modules, is an isomorphism for all
i

[
BJNB19
, Proof of Proposition 6.4]
.
Restricting to
G/H
invariants on both sides yields the desired isomorphism.
∎

The case of the previous lemma where
G
contains no pseudoreflections is a theorem of Kantor
[
Kan77
, Chapitre III, Théorème 4]
.

With the notation of
Lemma

5.1
, we have
R^{G}=\big(R^{H}\big)^{G/H}
, where
R^{H}
is a polynomial ring and the action of
G/H
on
R^{H}
, although not necessarily linear, fixes
\mathbb{k}
and is degree preserving.

Lemma

5.1
allows us to work in the following setting.

Setup 5.2
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, with an arbitrary positive grading.
Let
G
be a finite group that acts on

by degree-preserving

Henceforth we freely identify

with
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}})^{G}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}}
be generalized Bernstein filtrations on

and

.
Then under the aforementioned identification we have

(5.2)

\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R^{G}}=(\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R})^{G}=\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\cap D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}\ \text{ for each $i$.}

Lemma 5.3
.

In the context of
5.2
, there exists a natural injective ring homomorphism
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}})\to\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
.
This is a module-finite map of commutative rings, and
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of dimension

Proof.

We are identifying

; under this identification, (

) is telling us that
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}}
is the filtration on

induced by
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
.
Thus, we have a natural injective map
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}})\to\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
, which gives an isomorphism

4.23
shows that

is a polynomial ring of dimension

-algebras.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Under
5.2
,
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is a finitely generated right

-module.

Our main technical result in this section is the following.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group that acts linearly on a polynomial ring
R
over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero.

\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}}

Proof.

Lemma

5.1
allows us to work under
5.2
.
Fix a finite set
\Sigma
of generators for
R^{G}
as a
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, and choose
d
so that
\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{d}_{R^{G}}
.
Choose
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

we have

, as in

4.19
, and
1\in\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}
for all

, as in
Proposition

4.22
.
Fix
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{\ell}\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
and

with

for all
i
, as in
Lemma

5.4
, and note that, by possibly increasing

, we may assume that
v>\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_{j})
and
\gamma_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{v}_{R}
for all
j
.

We wish to show that there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
and each nonzero

1\in\mathcal{B}^{Ki}_{R^{G}}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Ki}_{R^{G}}
.
Note, however, that since
\mathcal{B}^{0}_{R^{G}}=\mathbb{k}
, this claim holds for

with any

, so we shall focus on the case of a positive

Fix a positive integer
i
and a nonzero element
\delta\in\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R^{G}}
, and let

.
By

4.1
, there exists a

[\cdots[[\delta,r_{1}],r_{2}],\ldots,r_{m}]

Let

and
f\in\mathcal{B}^{md}_{R^{G}}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{md}_{R^{G}}
.
Since
m\leqslant Ci
by our choice of
C

(5.3)

f\in\mathcal{B}^{Cdi}_{R^{G}}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Cdi}_{R^{G}}.

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
, write
\gamma_{j}^{(0)}=\gamma_{j}
and recursively define
\gamma_{j}^{(k+1)}=[\gamma_{j}^{(k)},f]
.
Note that
\gamma_{j}^{(v)}=0
by our choice of
v
, and

4.4
tells us that we can write

for some integers

.
Since (

\gamma_{j}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{B}^{3Cdki+v}_{R}
for each

.
Setting

) yields

As

is a nonzero element of

(5.6)

1\in\mathcal{B}^{\beta i}_{R}\cdot f\cdot\mathcal{B}^{\beta i}_{R^{G}}.

Applying a Reynolds operator, we shall be able to replace the

in (

.
Indeed,
the map

To wrap up the proof, we invoke (

nor on

.

are all holonomic

-modules, and thus have finite length as
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-modules. Likewise, for any nonzero element
f\in R^{G}
,
R^{G}_{f}(s)\boldsymbol{f^{s}}
is a holonomic
D_{R^{G}(s)|\mathbb{k}(s)}
-module, and thus has finite length.

Proof.

This follows from
Theorems

4.32
and
5.5
, using the fact that the differential signature of
R^{G}
is positive
[
BJNB19
, Theorem 6.15]
.
∎

We end this subsection with a couple of examples to illustrate that the main result of this section does not hold in the setting of rational singularities, even for hypersurfaces.

Example 5.7
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field of characteristic zero, and

This is a standard graded hypersurface domain with rational singularities for which
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
has no elements of negative degree
[

, Theorem 1.2]
.
Consequently,
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
is not linearly simple, and thus
R
is not a Bernstein algebra.
Moreover, the maximal ideal
(w,x,y,z)
is a proper nontrivial

-submodule of

, whence

is not a simple

-module.
For this ring, the residue field

Example 5.8
.

Let

5.2.
Holonomicity and differential direct summands

Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero and let
G
be a finite group acting linearly on
R
.
The ring of invariants
R^{G}
is then a direct summand of
R
.
Namely, the inclusion
R^{G}\hookrightarrow R
has a splitting
\beta:R\to R^{G}
given by the Reynolds operator.
This is one of the main examples in the theory of
differential direct summands

[
ÀHNB17
,
AMHJ+21
]
.
We briefly recall the basics in our setting and refer to loc. cit. for more insight.

First, notice that for any
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, the map
\beta\circ\delta|_{R^{G}}\colon R^{G}\to R^{G}
is an element of
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
.
That is, we have the following diagram:

\textstyle{R^{G}\,\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{R^{G}}
\textstyle{R\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}

We say that a
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-module
M
is a
differential direct summand
of a
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module
N
if
M\subseteq N
and there exists an
R^{G}
-linear splitting
\Theta\colon N\to M
, called a
differential splitting
, such that

\Theta(\delta\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}}v)=(\beta\circ\delta|_{R^{G}})\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}{$\bullet$}}}}v

for every
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
and
v\in M
, where the action on the left-hand side is the
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-action, considering
v
as an element of
N
, and the action on the right-hand side is the
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-action.
Among the properties that these modules satisfy we have
{\rm length}_{D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}}(M)\leqslant{\rm length}_{D_{R|\mathbb{k}}}(N)

[
ÀHNB17
, Proposition 3.4]
.
In particular, any differential direct summand of a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module has finite length.

The main examples of differential direct summands of holonomic modules are the rings
R^{G}\subseteq R
themselves, the localizations
R^{G}_{f}\subseteq R_{f}
at elements
f\in R^{G}
, and the local cohomology modules
H^{i}_{I}(R^{G})\subseteq H^{i}_{IR}(R)
at ideals
I\subseteq R^{G}
.

The goal of this subsection is to prove that the holonomic
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-modules are precisely the differential direct summands of holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting linearly on a polynomial ring
R
over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, and suppose that
G
contains no pseudoreflections.
Then a
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-module
M
is holonomic if and only if it is a differential direct summand of a holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module with respect to the splitting
\beta:R\to R^{G}
given by the Reynolds operator.

Proof.

As in
5.2
, we can identify
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
with
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}})^{G}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, and choose generalized Bernstein filtrations so that
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}}=\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}\cap D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
.
Let
M
be a holonomic left
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-module, and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
a good filtration on
M
compatible with
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}}
.
As in
Lemma

5.4
, take
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{\ell}\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
and a positive integer
v
such that
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\subseteq\gamma_{1}\cdot\mathcal{B}^{i+v}_{R^{G}}+\cdots+\gamma_{\ell}\cdot\mathcal{B}^{i+v}_{R^{G}}
for all
i
.
By possibly replacing
v
with a larger value, we may assume that
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\cdot\gamma_{t}\subseteq\gamma_{1}\cdot\mathcal{B}^{i+v}_{R^{G}}+\cdots+\gamma_{\ell}\cdot\mathcal{B}^{i+v}_{R^{G}}
for each
i
and
t
.

Let
N=D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\otimes_{D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}}M
, and for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{H}^{i}
be the
\mathbb{k}
-subspace of
N
spanned by
\{\gamma_{1}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{vi},\dots,\gamma_{\ell}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{vi}\}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
is finite dimensional, ascending, and, as the
\gamma_{t}
generate
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
over
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
, exhaustive.
Note that if
m\in\mathcal{G}^{vj}
and
i
is positive, then

\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}(\gamma_{t}\otimes m)\subseteq\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\gamma_{k}\mathcal{B}^{i+v}_{R^{G}}\otimes m=\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\gamma_{k}\otimes\mathcal{B}^{i+v}_{R^{G}}m\subseteq\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\gamma_{k}\otimes\mathcal{G}^{vi+vj}

which shows that
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\mathcal{H}^{j}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{i+j}
.
The same holds for
i=0
, since
\mathcal{B}^{0}_{R}=\mathbb{k}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
is a filtration compatible with
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
.
It follows easily from the fact that
M
is holonomic that
\mathcal{H}^{\bullet}
has dimension
\dim(R)
and finite multiplicity, so
N
is holonomic.

Now we check that
M
is a differential direct summand of
N
.
The averaging map
\rho:D_{R|\mathbb{k}}\to(D_{R|\mathbb{k}})^{G}
given by
\delta\mapsto\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot\delta
induces a
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-linear splitting
\Theta
of the natural map
M\to N
; in particular,
M
injects into
N
.
As
(\beta\circ\delta)|_{R^{G}}=\rho(\delta)
for each
\delta\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, the maps
\beta
and
\Theta
induce a differential direct summand structure.

For the converse, let
M
be a differential direct summand of
N
with respect to
\beta
and some
\Theta
.
It follows from (
5.2
)
that
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R^{G}}=\rho(\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R})
, so any element of
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R^{G}}
can be realized as
(\beta\circ\delta)|_{R^{G}}
for some
\delta\in\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R^{G}}
.
It then follows from the differential direct summand condition that the image under
\Theta
of a good filtration for
N
is compatible with the generalized Bernstein filtration
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R^{G}}
on
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
, and has dimension at most
\dim(R^{G})
and finite multiplicity.
∎

5.3.
Comparison with van den Essen’s notion of holonomicity

We briefly discuss the notion of holonomicity of van den Essen
[
vdE86
]
arising from Gabber’s theorem on involutivity of singular supports
[
Gab81
]
. We refer the reader to these sources for further details.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field of characteristic zero and
A
a not necessarily commutative
\mathbb{k}
-algebra with a filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
by
\mathbb{k}
-vector spaces that are not necessarily finite dimensional—that is, a collection of subspaces that satisfies all of the axioms of a filtration in
Definition

2.1
except finite dimensionality. If
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is commutative, then it admits the structure of a
Poisson algebra
, i.e., a commutative ring with a Lie bracket

\{-,-\}\ :\ \operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\times\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\to\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

such that for each
g\in\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, the map

\{g,-\}\ :\ \operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\to\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

is a derivation; this Lie bracket is called a Poisson bracket. The Poisson bracket on
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is defined as follows: For
g\in\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})_{a}
and
h\in\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})_{b}
, write
g=\delta+\mathcal{F}^{a-1}
and
h=\eta+\mathcal{F}^{b-1}
, and set

\{g,h\}=[\delta,\eta]+\mathcal{F}^{a+b-2}\in\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})_{a+b-1}.

Suppose that
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{k}
-algebra. Let
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module and
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
be a good filtration of
M
with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
. One then has that the ideal

I=\operatorname{Ann}_{\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}(\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}))

satisfies
\{I,I\}\subseteq I
; that is,
I
is
involutive
. A famous result of Gabber
[
Gab81
]
shows that in this setting
\sqrt{I}
is also involutive. When
R
is a polynomial ring over
\mathbb{k}
, one can then deduce Bernstein’s inequality by relating the Poisson structure on the associated graded algebra of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
via the order filtration with symplectic geometry and showing that any involutive ideal has height bounded by the dimension of
R
.

Following Gabber’s theorem, van den Essen posed a definition for a notion of holonomicity in rings with a filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
for which the associated graded ring is a finitely generated commutative algebra over a field
\mathbb{k}
. Namely, he defines a nonzero module
M
over such a ring to be holonomic if, for a good filtration of
M
, the height of every minimal prime of the annihilator of the associated graded module of
M
is equal to the maximal height of a graded involutive prime ideal in the associated graded algebra
\operatorname{gr}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
.

The purpose of this section is to note that for invariant rings of finite groups in characteristic zero, there may be no nonzero modules that are holonomic in the sense of van den Essen.

Example 5.10
.

Let
R
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
, and
G
a finite group acting linearly on
R^{G}
with no pseudoreflections. Note that
D^{1}_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
has no elements of negative degree; this follows from the equality
D^{1}_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}=(D^{1}_{R|\mathbb{k}})^{G}
and the observation that the action of
G
on the associated graded ring of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
again has no pseudoreflections. Let
A
be the associated graded ring of
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
with respect to the order filtration. Note that
A
is bigraded with a first grading coming from the grading on
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
and a second grading arising from the associated graded structure; write
A_{i,j}
for the bigraded pieces. Note that
A_{0,0}\cong\mathbb{k}
.

We claim that
A_{+}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i,0}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z},j>0}A_{i,j}
is an involutive ideal.
It suffices to check that for homogeneous elements
a,b\in A_{+}
we have
\{a,b\}\in A_{+}
. Let
a\in A_{i,j}
and
b\in A_{k,\ell}
, so
\{a,b\}\in A_{i+k,j+\ell-1}
. Thus it suffices to deal with the case
j=1,\ell=0
. For
\{a,b\}\notin A_{+}
, we must have
i+k=0
. However,
A_{k,0}=0
for
k<0
and
A_{i,1}=0
for
i<0
since there are no operators of order
1
and negative degree. But if
b\in A_{0,0}
, we have
\{a,b\}=0\in A_{+}
.

Thus, for a nonzero
D_{R^{G}|\mathbb{k}}
-module
M
to be holonomic in the sense of van den Essen, the annihilator of an associated graded must be primary to the homogeneous maximal ideal of
A
, so
M
must be a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector space. The existence of such a module is contradicted by
Corollary

5.6
.

6.
Strongly
F
-regular finitely generated graded algebras with FFRT

In this section, we focus on commutative rings of prime characteristic, more precisely on the class of rings with finite
F
-representation type, introduced by Smith and Van den Bergh
[
SVdB97
]
.
We prove that if such a ring is strongly
F
-regular, then it is a Bernstein algebra, and thus Bernstein’s inequality is satisfied, and the dimension of
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
is twice the dimension of
R
.

To discuss finite
F
-representation type, we need a class of rings for which the analogue of the Krull–Schmidt Theorem holds, and over which the Frobenius map is finite.
Throughout this section we work in the following setting.

Setup 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a perfect field of prime characteristic
p
, and
R
a commutative graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra, as in
4.3
, that is a domain.
Set
\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{i>0}R_{i}
,
n=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, and
w=\max\{t\in\mathbb{N}\;|\;[\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}]_{t}\neq 0\}
.

In the remarks that follow we introduce a suitable category of modules to work with, and make some considerations about the structure of rings of endomorphisms of such modules—especially concerning maximal homogeneous two-sided ideals.

Remark 6.2

(A Krull–Schmidt category)
.

If
R
is as in
6.1
, then the category
\mathbf{C}
of finitely generated
\mathbb{Q}
-graded
R
-modules and homogeneous homomorphisms is a Krull–Schmidt category—that is, every nonzero object in
\mathbf{C}
can be expressed uniquely, up to order and isomorphism, as a direct sum of indecomposable objects.
Indeed, given objects
M
and
N
in
\mathbf{C}
, suppose
M
is generated by homogeneous elements of degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}
, and let us denote the
\mathbb{k}
-vector space of homogeneous homomorphisms
M\to N
by
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)_{0}
.
The map
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)_{0}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{k}}(M_{d_{i}},N_{d_{i}})
given by restriction is an injective
\mathbb{k}
-linear map; its codomain is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector space, and thus so is its domain,
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M,N)_{0}
.
This implies that
\mathbf{C}
is a Krull–Schmidt category
[
Ati56
, Corollary to Lemma 3, Theorem 1]
.

Remark 6.3

(On the structure of rings of endomorphisms)
.

For every
\mathbb{Q}
-graded
R
-module
M
, the module of (not necessarily graded)
R
-endomorphisms
\Lambda=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
is a ring with unity, which is usually not commutative.
If
M
is finitely generated, then
\Lambda
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-graded ring, with the decomposition
\Lambda=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Q}}\Lambda_{i}
, where
\Lambda_{i}=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)_{i}
consists of the graded endomorphisms of degree
i
for each
i\in\mathbb{Q}

[
NVO82
, Corollary I.2.11]
.
If, in addition,
M
is indecomposable in the category
\mathbf{C}
of
Remark

6.2
, then the degree 0 component
\Lambda_{0}=\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)_{0}
is a local ring, that is, the set of all nonunits is a two-sided ideal, which is necessarily the unique maximal (right, left, and two-sided) ideal of
\Lambda_{0}

[
Ati56
, Lemma 7]
.
This, in turn, implies that
\Lambda
has a unique maximal homogeneous (left, right, and two-sided) ideal—namely, the ideal generated by all the homogeneous nonunits, or equivalently, the set of all elements whose homogeneous components are all nonunits
[
Li12
, Theorem 2.5]
.

If
N\cong M^{\alpha}
, where
M
is indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}
, then
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
can be identified with
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)^{\alpha\times\alpha},
the ring of
\alpha\times\alpha
matrices with entries in
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
.
Since all homogeneous two-sided ideals of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)^{\alpha\times\alpha}
are of the form
\mathfrak{A}^{\alpha\times\alpha}
, for
\mathfrak{A}
a homogeneous two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M)
, it follows that
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
also has a unique maximal homogeneous two-sided ideal, namely the set of all matrices whose entries decompose as sums of homogeneous nonunits.

Note that if
N^{\prime}
is also a direct sum of
\alpha
copies of
M
, but with rational degree shifts applied to the various summands, then
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N^{\prime})
agrees with
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
as an ungraded ring, but their gradings may differ by shifts in the off-diagonal entries.
The endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N^{\prime})
again has a unique maximal homogeneous two-sided ideal—in fact the same one as
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
, but with possible changes in the grading.

Generalizing further, suppose now that

N=M^{\alpha_{1}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M^{\alpha_{\ell}}_{\ell}

where
M_{1},\ldots,M_{\ell}
are indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}
, and
M_{i}
is not graded-isomorphic to
M_{j}(d)
for any
d\in\mathbb{Q}

and
i\neq j
.
An endomorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
can be viewed as a block matrix
(\phi_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\ell}
, where
\phi_{ij}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M_{j},M_{i})^{\alpha_{i}\times\alpha_{j}}
.
The endomorphism ring
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
has
\ell
maximal homogeneous two-sided ideals,
\mathfrak{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{N}_{\ell}
, where each
\mathfrak{N}_{k}
consists of all
(\phi_{ij})
such that every homogeneous component of every entry of
\phi_{kk}
is a nonunit.
Although this appears to be a well-known fact to the specialists, for lack of an appropriate reference, we provide the proof below, in
Proposition

6.4
.
Once again, the considerations just made are unaffected by degree shifts in the various components of
N
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose
N=M_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{\ell}^{\alpha_{\ell}}
, as in
Remark

6.3
.
As in that remark, we view the endomorphisms of
N
as block matrices
(\phi_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant\ell}
, with
\phi_{ij}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(M_{j},M_{i})^{\alpha_{i}\times\alpha_{j}}
.
For each
k=1,\ldots,\ell
, let
\mathfrak{n}_{k}
be the unique maximal homogeneous two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{End}(M_{k})^{\alpha_{k}\times\alpha_{k}}
, and let
\mathfrak{N}_{k}
be the subset of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
consisting of block matrices
(\phi_{ij})
with
\phi_{kk}\in\mathfrak{n}_{k}

(
that is, every homogeneous component of every entry of
\phi_{kk}
is a nonunit
)
.
Then
\mathfrak{N}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{N}_{\ell}
are all of the maximal homogeneous two-sided ideals of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
.

Proof.

We first show that
\mathfrak{N}_{1}
is a homogeneous two-sided ideal.
We have that
\mathfrak{N}_{1}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-graded additive subgroup of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
, and the multiplicative properties of an ideal can thus be verified using homogeneous elements.
Take homogeneous elements
\Phi=(\phi_{ij})\in\mathfrak{N}_{1}
and
\Psi=(\psi_{ij})\in\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
, and suppose
\Phi\Psi\notin\mathfrak{N}_{1}
, so the upper left block of that product,
\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\phi_{1k}\psi_{k1}
, does not lie in
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
.
As
\phi_{11}\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}
, this implies that
\phi_{1k}\psi_{k1}\notin\mathfrak{n}_{1}
for some
k\in\{2,\ldots,\ell\}
.
This, in turn, implies that there are entries
\delta:M_{k}\to M_{1}
in
\phi_{1k}
, and
\gamma:M_{1}\to M_{k}
in
\psi_{k1}
, such that
u\coloneqq\delta\gamma:M_{1}\to M_{1}
is a unit.
Thus,
\gamma
is injective, and
u^{-1}\delta
is a splitting of
\gamma
.
As
M_{k}
is indecomposable,
\gamma
must be surjective as well—but this contradicts our assumption that
M_{1}
is not graded-isomorphic to
M_{k}(d)
for any
d\in\mathbb{Q}
.
This shows that
\Phi\Psi\in\mathfrak{N}_{1}
, and the proofs that
\Psi\Phi\in\mathfrak{N}_{1}
, and that the other
\mathfrak{N}_{k}
are homogeneous two-sided ideals, follow similar steps.

We now show that each proper homogeneous two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{A}
is contained in some
\mathfrak{N}_{k}
.
For each
k=1,\ldots,\ell
, let
\mathfrak{a}_{k}
be the image of
\mathfrak{A}
under the projection
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{k})^{\alpha_{k}\times\alpha_{k}}
, and note that
\mathfrak{a}_{k}
is a homogeneous two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{k})^{\alpha_{k}\times\alpha_{k}}
.
If
\mathfrak{a}_{k}
is not proper, then
\mathfrak{A}
contains an element
\Phi=(\phi_{ij})
where
\phi_{kk}=\mathbf{1}
, the identity of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{k})^{\alpha_{k}\times\alpha_{k}}
.
Multiplying
\Phi
on both sides by the element
\Psi_{k}=(\psi_{ij})\in\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
with
\psi_{kk}=\mathbf{1}
and zeros elsewhere, we see that
\Psi_{k}
itself lies in
\mathfrak{A}
.
If none of the
\mathfrak{a}_{k}
were proper, this reasoning would show that
\mathfrak{A}
contains
\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}\Psi_{k}
, the identity of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(N)
, contradicting its properness.
Thus,
\mathfrak{a}_{k}
is proper for some
k
, whence
\mathfrak{a}_{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{k}
and
\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{N}_{k}
.
∎

Next, we recall definitions that involve the structure of rings regarding the Frobenius homomorphism.
To this end, note that the ring of
p^{e}
-th roots
R^{1/p^{e}}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-graded (in fact,
\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{N}
-graded)
R
-module, with
(R^{1/p^{e}})_{i/p^{e}}=(R_{i})^{1/p^{e}}
, and under this grading the inclusion
R\hookrightarrow R^{1/p^{e}}
is homogeneous.
Note also that, because
R
is a finitely generated commutative algebra over a perfect field,
R
is
F
-finite, that is,
R^{1/p^{e}}
is a finitely generated
R
-module.

Definition 6.5

(
[
SVdB97
, Definition 3.1.1]
)
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
.
The ring
R
has
finite
F
-representation type
,
FFRT
for short, if there exists a finite set of indecomposable finitely generated
\mathbb{Q}
-graded
R
-modules,
M_{1},\ldots,M_{\ell}
, such that
R^{1/p^{e}}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely many copies of the
M_{i}
, with possible rational degree shifts, for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
.

Rings of finite
F
-representation type include invariant rings of linearly reductive groups, simple hypersurface singularities, and the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathrm{Gr}(2,n)

[
SVdB97
,
RŠVdB19a
,
RŠVdB19b
]
.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
.
The
splitting ideals
of
R
are defined by

I_{e}(R)=\{r\in R\;|\;\varphi(r^{1/p^{e}})\in\mathfrak{m}\text{ for each }\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R^{1/p^{e}},R)\}

for each
e\in\mathbb{N}
.
The ring
R
is
F
-pure
if
I_{e}(R)\neq R
for some (equivalently, all)
e\geqslant 1
, and
strongly
F
-regular
if
\bigcap_{e\in\mathbb{N}}I_{e}(R)=0
.

We point out that these definitions for
F
-purity and strong
F
-regularity are not the usual ones found in the literature.
Hochster and Roberts
[
HR76
]
were the first to study
F
-purity, while the notion of strong
F
-regularity was originally defined by Hochster and Huneke
[
HH89
]
.
The splitting ideals
I_{e}(R)
first appeared in the work of Aberbach and Enescu
[
AE05
]
and Yao
[
Yao06
]
, although in a different formulation; our formulation appears in the work of Tucker
[
Tuc12
]
.

The following result was proved by Polstra and Tucker in the case of complete local rings, and extends to our setting through localization and completion at
\mathfrak{m}
.

Lemma 6.7

(
[
PT18
, Lemma 5.2, second proof of Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
.
If
R
is strongly
F
-regular, then there exists
a\in\mathbb{N}
such that

I_{a+e}(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{p^{e}}\quad\text{for all $e\in\mathbb{N}$}.\qed

Toward the proof of the main technical result in this section,
Theorem

6.9
, it is convenient to isolate the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring, not necessarily commutative, with
A_{i}=0
for all
i\ll 0
.
Let
\delta
be a nonzero element of
A
, and let
\bar{\delta}
be its homogeneous component of largest degree.
Suppose
A\bar{\delta}A=A
.
Then every nonzero homogeneous element
\gamma
of
A
can be expressed as a sum of elements of the form
\alpha\delta\beta
, where
\alpha,\beta\in A
are homogeneous and
\deg(\alpha\bar{\delta}\beta)\leqslant\deg(\gamma)
.
In particular,
A\delta A=A
as well.

Proof.

We use induction on the degree of
\gamma
, noting that the base of induction holds (vacuously) because of our assumption on
A
.
Let
\gamma\in A
be a nonzero homogeneous element, and suppose the claim holds for like elements of smaller degree.
Since
A\bar{\delta}A=A
, we can write
\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\bar{\delta}\beta_{i}
for some
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in A
.
By expanding and comparing homogeneous components, we may assume that
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
are homogeneous and
\deg(\alpha_{i}\bar{\delta}\beta_{i})=\deg(\gamma)
for each
i
.
Now write
\delta=\bar{\delta}+\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{s}
, where each
\delta_{j}
is homogeneous with
\deg(\delta_{j})<\deg(\bar{\delta})
.
Then

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\delta\beta_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}\delta_{j}\beta_{i}

where each nonzero term in the double sum is homogeneous of degree less than
\deg(\gamma)
, so applying the induction hypothesis to those terms gives us the result.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result in this section.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
, and suppose that
R
is strongly
F
-regular with FFRT.
If
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
is a generalized Bernstein filtration on
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
, then
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
is linearly simple.

Proof.

Recall from
6.1
that
n=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and
w=\max\{t\in\mathbb{N}\;|\;[\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}]_{t}\neq 0\}
.

Proposition

4.21
allows us to assume that the slope of
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R}
is
2w
.
Fix
Z\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

such that
\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{Zi}
for each
i
, as in
Proposition

4.19
, and
a\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that
I_{a+e}(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{p^{e}}
for every
e\in\mathbb{N}
, as in
Lemma

6.7
.

As
R
has FFRT, there exist finitely generated
\mathbb{Q}
-graded
R
-modules
M_{1},\ldots,M_{\ell}
that are indecomposable in the category
\mathbf{C}
of
Remark

6.2
, with
M_{i}
not graded-isomorphic to
M_{j}(d)
for any
i\neq j
and
d\in\mathbb{Q}
,
and such that each
R^{1/p^{e}}
is a direct sum of copies of the
M_{i}
, with possible rational degree shifts, and every
M_{i}
is a direct summand of some
R^{1/p^{e_{i}}}
, again with a possible degree shift.
Set
b=\max\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.
As
R
is strongly
F
-regular, it is in particular
F
-split, so each
R^{1/p^{e_{i}}}
is an
R
-direct summand of
R^{1/p^{b}}
, and the decomposition of
R^{1/p^{b}}
as a direct sum of indecomposable
\mathbb{Q}
-graded
R
-modules contains
all
the
M_{i}
, up to degree shift, by the Krull–Schmidt Theorem.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
We wish to show the existence of
C\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, independent of
i
or
\delta
, such that
1\in\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}
.
As in the proof of
Theorem

5.5
, we assume that
i>0
.

Let
\bar{\delta}
be the homogeneous component of
\delta
of largest degree.
Then
\bar{\delta}\in\mathcal{B}^{i}_{R}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{Zi}
, and hence
-wZi\leqslant\deg(\bar{\delta})\leqslant i
.
Because
R
can be generated by elements of degree at most
w
, the nonzero operator
\bar{\delta}
must act nontrivially on degree
\leqslant w\operatorname{ord}(\bar{\delta})
, that is, there exists
f\in R
homogeneous with
\deg(f)\leqslant w\operatorname{ord}(\bar{\delta})\leqslant wZi
such that
g\coloneqq\bar{\delta}(f)\neq 0
.
We note that
\deg(g)=\deg(f)+\deg(\bar{\delta})\leqslant wZi+i
.
In particular,
f
and
g
are not in
\mathfrak{m}^{(wZ+2)i}
, and thus if we set
c=\lceil\log_{p}((wZ+2)i)\rceil
, we see that
f
and
g
do not lie in
I_{a+c}(R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{p^{c}}
.
Equivalently,
f^{1/p^{b}}
and
g^{1/p^{b}}
do not lie in
I_{a+c}(R^{1/p^{b}})
, and putting
e=a+b+c
, we deduce that
R^{1/p^{b}}f^{1/p^{e}}
and
R^{1/p^{b}}g^{1/p^{e}}
are free direct summands of
R^{1/p^{e}}
.

As
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{Zi}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(c)}
by (
4.2
),
\bar{\delta}
is
R^{p^{c}}
-linear, and the map
\bar{\delta}^{\,1/p^{e}}:R^{1/p^{e}}\to R^{1/p^{e}}
defined as in (
4.1
) is
R^{1/p^{a+b}}
-linear, and thus
R
-linear.
This map induces a bijection between the free summands
R^{1/p^{b}}f^{1/p^{e}}
and
R^{1/p^{b}}g^{1/p^{e}}
.
Given our choice of
b
, and viewing
\bar{\delta}^{\,1/p^{e}}
as a block matrix as in
Remark

6.3
, it follows that for each
j
, the block of
\bar{\delta}^{\,1/p^{e}}
with entries in
\operatorname{End}_{R}(M_{j})
must contain at least one (homogeneous) unit.
This shows that
\bar{\delta}^{\,1/p^{e}}
does not lie in any maximal homogeneous two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{End}_{R}(R^{1/p^{e}})
.
Equivalently,
\bar{\delta}
does not lie in any maximal homogeneous two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{End}_{R^{p^{e}}}(R)=D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}
, and hence the two-sided homogeneous ideal it generates must be the unit ideal, that is,

D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}\cdot\bar{\delta}\cdot D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}=D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}.

Since
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{np^{e}}
by (
4.3
),
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}
is zero in degree
<-wnp^{e}
.

Lemma

6.8
then shows the existence of
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}\in D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}
homogeneous with
\deg(\alpha_{j}\bar{\delta}\beta_{j})\leqslant 0
such that

(6.1)

1=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\delta\beta_{j}.

Recalling that
c=\lceil\log_{p}((wZ+2)i)\rceil
and
e=a+b+c
, we now observe that

D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{(e)}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{np^{e}}\subseteq D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{np^{a+b+1}(wZ+2)i}

and set
Y=np^{a+b+1}(wZ+2)
.
The
\alpha_{j}
and
\beta_{j}
lie in
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}^{Yi}
, so their degrees are bounded below by
-wYi
.
As
\deg(\alpha_{j}\bar{\delta}\beta_{j})\leqslant 0
, we see that

\deg(\alpha_{j})\leqslant-\deg(\bar{\delta})-\deg(\beta_{j})\leqslant wZi+wYi

and so

\deg(\alpha_{j})+2w\operatorname{ord}(\alpha_{j})\leqslant wZi+wYi+2wYi.

We have just shown that
\alpha_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}
, where
C=w(Z+3Y)
.
Likewise,
\beta_{j}\in\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}
, and (
6.1
) tells us that
1\in\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}\cdot\delta\cdot\mathcal{B}^{Ci}_{R}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
R
be as in
6.1
, and suppose that
R
is strongly
F
-regular with FFRT.
Then
R
is a Bernstein algebra.
In particular, every
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module satisfies Bernstein inequality with respect to any generalized Bernstein filtration.
Furthermore,
R
, its localizations
R_{f}
for
f\in R
, and its local cohomology modules
H^{j}_{I}(R)
for
I\subseteq R
are holonomic
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules, and hence have finite length as
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-modules.

Proof.

This follows from
Theorems

4.32
and
6.9
, using the fact that the differential signature of a strongly
F
-regular
\mathbb{k}
-algebra is positive
[
BJNB19
, Theorem 5.17]
.
∎

We end with a couple of examples to demonstrate the necessity of the hypotheses.

Example 6.11
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field of positive characteristic, and
R=\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{k}[x,y]}{(xy)}
.
Then
R
has FFRT, but is not strongly
F
-regular.
The ring
R
is not a Bernstein algebra since
(D_{R|\mathbb{k}},\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}_{R})
is not linearly simple; moreover,
R
is not a simple
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module.
For this ring, the residue field
R/(x,y)\cong\mathbb{k}
is a
D_{R|\mathbb{k}}
-module; any filtration on this module has dimension zero.

Example 6.12
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field of positive characteristic, and

This is a strongly
F
-regular standard graded domain that has a local cohomology module with infinitely many associated primes
[
SS04
, Theorem 5.1]
, and hence,

is not a Bernstein algebra by

4.8
.

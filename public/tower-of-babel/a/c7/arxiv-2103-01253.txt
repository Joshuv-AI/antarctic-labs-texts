Abstract
. In his seminal work on localisation of spectra, Ravenel initiated the study of Bousfield classes of spectra related to the chromatic perspective. In particular he showed that there were infinitely many distinct Bousfield classes between ⟨ M ​ U ⟩ \langle{M\mathrm{U}}\rangle and ⟨ S 0 ⟩ \langle S^{0}\rangle . The main topological goal of this paper is investigate how these Bousfield classes are related to that of another classical Thom spectrum M ​ Sp {M\mathrm{Sp}} , and in particular how ⟨ M ​ Sp ⟩ \langle{M\mathrm{Sp}}\rangle is related to ⟨ M ​ U ⟩ \langle{M\mathrm{U}}\rangle . We follow the approach of Ravenel, but adapt it using the theory of P P -algebras to give vanishing results for cohomology. Our work involves dualising and considering comodules over duals of P P -algebras; these ideas are then applied to the mod 2 2 Steenrod algebra and certain subHopf algebras.

Introduction

In his seminal paper on localisation
[

,

classes of spectra related to the chromatic

\langle{M\mathrm{U}}\rangle

\langle S^{0}\rangle

{M\mathrm{Sp}}
,

\langle{M\mathrm{Sp}}\rangle

\langle{M\mathrm{U}}\rangle

\langle{M\mathrm{Sp}}\rangle\geqslant\langle{M\mathrm{U}}\rangle

This issue is mentioned by Hovey

who also say that Ravenel had outlined an

To achieve this we rework the algebraic

subsequently developed further by

[

. We first consider

{M\mathrm{Sp}}
. After

\langle{M\mathrm{Sp}}\rangle>\langle BP\rangle

1.
P
-algebras and their duals

We will make use material on
P
-algebras and their
modules contained in
[
6
]
*chapter 13.
Here is a summary of assumptions and conventions.

•

We will often suppress explicit mention of
internal grading in cohomology and just
write
M
for
M^{*}
when discussing a module
over
A=A^{*}
. When working in homology we will
usually write
M_{*}
or
A_{*}
.

•

We will work with graded vector spaces over
a field
\Bbbk
and in particular,
\Bbbk
-algebras
and their (co)modules. In our topological
applications,
\Bbbk=\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

For a finite type graded vector space
V_{*}
we think
of
V_{n}
as dual to
V^{n}=\Hom_{\Bbbk}(V_{n},\Bbbk)
, so
V^{*}

is the cohomologically graded degree-wise dual, and
bounded below means that
V^{*}
is bounded below. We
can also start with a finite type graded vector space

V^{*}
and form
V_{*}
where
V_{n}=\Hom_{\Bbbk}(V^{n},\Bbbk)
;
the double dual of
V_{*}
is canonically isomorphic
to
V_{*}
, and vice versa.

A graded vector space which is finite dimensional
will be referred to as a
finite
; when
\Bbbk

is finite this terminology agrees with Margolis’
use of finite module over a
P
-algebra.

•

Recall that a
P
-algebra
A
is a union
of connected cocommutative Hopf algebras

A(n)\subseteq A(n+1)\subseteq A
. Each
A(n)

is a Poincaré duality algebra and we
will denote its highest non-trivial
degree by
\pd(n)
, which is also its
Poincaré duality degree; this satisfies

\pd(n)<\pd(n+1)
. In general,
P
-algebras
are not required to be of finite type,
but all the examples we consider have
that property and it is required in some
of our homological results so we will
assume it holds. We also stress that
each
A(n)
is a local graded ring
and
A(n)^{\pd(n)}=A(n)^{0}=\Bbbk
. Other
important properties are that
A
is
flat as a left or right
A(n)
-module
and a coherent
\Bbbk
-algebra.

Of course our main examples are the
Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}
and infinite
dimensional subHopf algebras.

We will use the following basic result
stated on page 195 of
[
6
]

but left as an exercise.

Proposition 1.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a P-algebra. Let
M

be a finite
A
-module and
F
a bounded
below free
A
-module. Then

\Ext_{A}^{*}(M,F)=0.

Proof.

By
[
6
]
*theorem 13.12, bounded
below projective
A
-modules are also injective,
so
\Ext_{A}^{s}(M,F)=0
when

, therefore
we only have to show that
\Hom_{A}(M,F)=0
.
It suffices to prove this for the case
F=A
.

Suppose that

.
The image of
\theta
contains a simple
submodule in its top degree, so let

\theta(x)\neq 0
be in this submodule;
then

for every positive

. Now for some

,
and by Poincaré duality for

there exists

for which

.
This gives a contradiction, hence no
such

can exist.
∎

A particular concern for us will be the

For a pair of P-algebras

\textstyle{0}

\scriptstyle{\pi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{A(n)^{\oplus\ell}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with exact rows. It is standard that every

-module admits a resolution by

-algebra we also have injective resolutions

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
M
be a coherent module over a
P
-algebra

. Then
M
admits an injective resolution
by finitely generated free modules.

Proof.

By
[
6
]
*theorem 13.12, bounded
below projective
A
-modules are injective.

For some
n
,
M\cong A\otimes_{A(n)}M^{\prime}

where
M^{\prime}
is a finitely generated
A(n)
-module.
It is standard that
M^{\prime}
admits a monomorphism

M^{\prime}\to J^{\prime}
into a finitely generated free
module (since

is a Poincaré duality
algebra this is obvious for a simple module
and can be proved by induction on dimension).
By flatness we can find a monomorphism of

A
-modules

M\xrightarrow{\cong}A\otimes_{A(n)}M^{\prime}\to A\otimes_{A(n)}J^{\prime}

into a finitely generated free module with coherent
cokernel. Iterating this we can build an injection
resolution of the stated form.
∎

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
M
be a finite
A
-module and
N
a coherent

A
-module. Then

\Ext_{A}^{*}(M,N)=0.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.3
there
is an injective resolution
N\to J^{*}
where
each
J^{s}
is a finitely generated free module.
Also, by Proposition
1.1

\Hom_{A}(M,J^{s})=0
and so
\Ext_{A}^{s}(M,N)=0
.
∎

For example, every left
A
-module of the form

A/\!/A(n)=A\otimes_{A(n)}\Bbbk
is coherent so
it admits such an injective resolution and

\Ext_{A}^{s}(\Bbbk,A/\!/A(n))=0
.

The dual of a
P
-algebra and its comodules

The theory of
P
-algebras can be dualised: given
a finite type
P
-algebra
A
, we define its dual

A_{*}
by setting
A_{n}=\Hom_{\Bbbk}(A^{n},\Bbbk)
and making
this a commutative Hopf algebra. We will refer to
the dual Hopf algebra of a finite type
P
-algebra
as a
P_{*}
-algebra
; as far as we know this
is not standard terminology but it seems appropriate.

When working with comodules over a
P_{*}
-algebra
A_{*}

we will use homological grading. For left

A_{*}
-comodules which are bounded below and
of finite type there is no significant difference
between working with them or their (degree-wise)
duals as
A
-modules. In particular,

\Cohom_{A_{*}}(M_{*},N_{*})\cong\Hom_{A}(N^{*},M^{*}),

where
M^{n}=\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(M_{n},\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
M^{*}
is
made into a left
A
-module using the antipode.
More generally,

\Coext^{s,*}_{A_{*}}(M_{*},N_{*})\cong\Ext^{s,*}_{A}(N^{*},M^{*}),

where
\Coext^{s,*}_{A_{*}}(M_{*},-)
denotes the
right derived functor of
\Cohom_{A_{*}}(M_{*},-)
,
which can be computed using extended comodules
which are injective comodules here since we
are working over a field.

Here are the dual versions of
Propositions
1.1

and
1.2
. Recall that a
cofree
A_{*}
-comodule is one of the
form
A_{*}\otimes W_{*}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Suppose that
A_{*}
is a
P_{*}
-algebra.
Let
M_{*}
be a finite
A_{*}
-comodule
and
C_{*}
a bounded below finite type
cofree
A_{*}
-comodule. Then

\Coext_{A_{*}}^{*}(C_{*},M_{*})=0.

Proposition 1.6
.

For a surjective morphism of Hopf algebras

A_{*}\to B_{*}
dual to a pair of P-algebras

B\subseteq A
,

\Coext^{*,*}_{A_{*}}(A_{*},A_{*}\square_{B_{*}}\Bbbk)\cong\Coext^{*,*}_{B_{*}}(A_{*},\Bbbk)=\Cohom_{B_{*}}(A_{*},\Bbbk)=0.

If
A_{*}
is a
P_{*}
-algebra then we will call
an
A_{*}
-comodule
M_{*}

coherent
if its
dual
M^{*}
is a coherent
A
-module. Here is
a dual version of Proposition
1.3
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
M_{*}
be a coherent comodule over a
P_{*}
-algebra

A_{*}
. Then
M_{*}
admits a resolution by finitely
generated cofree comodules.

Proof.

Take an injective resolution of
M^{*}
as
in Proposition
1.3

and then take duals to obtain a projective
resolution.
∎

Notice that since
A_{*}
is an injective comodule
we have

\Coext_{A_{*}}^{*}(A_{*},A_{*})\cong\Hom_{\Bbbk}(A_{*},\Bbbk).

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
M_{*}
be a coherent comodule over a
P_{*}
-algebra

A_{*}
and let
N_{*}
be a finite
P_{*}
-comodule.
Then

\Coext_{A_{*}}^{*}(M_{*},N_{*})=0.

Proof.

Let
P_{\bullet,*}\to M_{*}\to 0
be a resolution
of
M_{*}
by cofree comodules. Then by
Proposition
1.5
, for each

s\geqslant 0
we have

\Cohom_{A_{*}}(P_{s,*},N_{*})=0,

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 1.9
.

For bounded below finite type comodules over

-algebra

2.
Some homological algebra for comodules

In this section we describe some Cartan-Eilenberg

Proposition 2.2
.

The construction is similar to the other one,

The theory of

holds for any subalgebra where

To illustrate this, here is a simple application
involving the mod

Steenrod algebra; this
result appears in
[
12
]
*corollary 4.10.
We denote the mod
2
Eilenberg-Mac Lane

.

Proposition 3.1
.

The
2
-completed
BP
-cohomology of

is
trivial, i.e.,
(BP^{^}_{2})^{*}(H)=0
.

Proof.

There is an Adams spectral sequence of form

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(H_{*}(H),H_{*}(BP))\Longrightarrow(BP^{^}_{2})^{s-t}(H).

Now

and it is well-known

\mathcal{A}_{*}
-comodule algebras,

(3.1)

H_{*}(BP)\cong\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{1}^{2},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2},\ldots]

where

-algebra. Now
by a change of rings isomorphism and

,

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{E}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2})=0.\qed

Doubling

The operation of
doubling
has been
used frequently in studying
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
The reader is referred to the account of
Margolis
[
6
]
*section 15.3
which we will use as background.

Since the dual
\mathcal{A}_{*}
is a commutative
Hopf algebra, it admits a Frobenius
endomorphism
\mathcal{A}_{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}
which
doubles degrees and has Hopf algebra
cokernel

\mathcal{E}_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}=\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\overline{\zeta}_{s}:s\geqslant 1),

where
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{s}^{2}:s\geqslant 1]
.
Dually, there is a Verschiebung
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}

which halves degrees and satisfies

\Sq^{r}\mapsto\begin{cases}\Sq^{r/2}&if $r$ is even,\\
\phantom{\;\;\;}0&if $r$ is odd.\end{cases}

The kernel of this Verschiebung is the
ideal generated by the Milnor primitives

\mathrm{P}^{0}_{t}
(
t\geqslant 1
), hence there
is a grade-halving isomorphism of Hopf
algebras
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{A}
,
where
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is the
subHopf algebra generated by the primitives

\mathrm{P}^{0}_{t}
and dual to the exterior
quotient Hopf algebra
\mathcal{E}_{*}
.

Given a left (graded)
\mathcal{A}
-module
M
, we
can induce an
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}
-module

M_{(1)}
where

M_{(1)}^{n}=\begin{cases}M^{n/2}&if $n$ is even,\\
0&if $n$ is odd,\end{cases}

and we write
x_{(1)}
to indicate the
element
x\in M
regarded as an element
of
M_{(1)}
; the module structure is
given by

\Sq^{r}(x_{(1)})=\begin{cases}(\Sq^{r/2}x)_{(1)}&if $r$ is even,\\
0&if $r$ is odd,\end{cases}

Using this construction, the category of
left
\mathcal{A}
-modules
\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
admits
an additive functor to the category of
evenly graded
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}
-modules,

\Phi\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{ev}};\quad M\mapsto M_{(1)}

which is an isomorphism of categories.
The quotient homomorphism
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}

also induces an additive isomorphism of categories

\rho^{*}\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{ev}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{ev}}

and it is often useful to consider the
composition

\rho^{*}\circ\Phi\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{ev}}
.

By iterating
\Phi^{(1)}=\Phi
we obtain
isomorphisms

\Phi^{(s)}=\Phi\circ\Phi^{(s-1)}\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}^{(s)}}^{(s)};\quad M\mapsto M_{(s)}

where the codomain is the category of

\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}^{(s)}
-modules concentrated
in degrees divisible by
2^{s}
and

\mathcal{E}^{(s)}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is the subHopf
algebra multiplicatively generated by the
elements

\mathrm{P}^{a}_{b}\quad(s\geqslant a\geqslant 0,\;b\geqslant 1).

By doubling all three of the variables involved
the following homological result is immediate
for
e\geqslant 1
and two
\mathcal{A}
-modules
M,N
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Ext_{\mathcal{A}_{(e)}}^{s,2^{e}t}(M_{(e)},N_{(e)})\cong\Ext_{\mathcal{A}}^{s,t}(M,N).

Because doubling is induced using a grade
changing Hopf algebra endomorphism, the
double
\mathcal{A}_{(1)}
is also a Hopf algebra
isomorphic to the quotient Hopf algebra

\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}
and dual to the
subHopf algebra of squares

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}
which is
also given by

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}\mathcal{A}_{*}=(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*})\backslash\!\backslash\mathcal{A}_{*}.

More generally, for any
s\geqslant 1
,
\mathcal{A}_{(s)}

is isomorphic to the quotient Hopf algebra
of
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}^{(s)}
dual to the subalgebra
of
2^{s}
-th powers

\mathcal{A}^{(s)}_{*}=(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(s)}_{*})\backslash\!\backslash\mathcal{A}_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}.

In many ways, doubling is more transparent
when viewed in terms of comodules. For
an
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-comodule
M_{*}
, we can define
a
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}
-coaction

\mu^{(1)}\colon M_{*}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}\otimes M_{*}^{(1)}

where
M_{*}^{(1)}
denotes
M_{*}
with its
degrees doubled; this is given on elements
by the composition

\textstyle{M_{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\scriptstyle{\mu^{(1)}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}_{*}\otimes M_{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(-)^{2}\otimes\Id}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}\otimes M_{*}.}

By iterating we also obtain a
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(s)}
-coaction

\mu^{(s)}\colon M_{*}^{(s)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(s)}\otimes M_{*}^{(s)}
.

Then the comodule analogue of (
3.2
)
is

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(e)}}^{s,2^{e}t}(M_{*}^{(e)},N_{*}^{(e)})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(M_{*},N_{*}).

We can use iterated doubling combined with

to show that

\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(d)})\cong\Ext_{\mathcal{A}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{(d)},\mathcal{A})=0.

By doubling all three of the variables involved

\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(e)}}^{s,2^{e}t}(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(e)},\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(d+e)})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(d)})=0.

Some families of quotient

-algebras
of
\mathcal{A}_{*}

We will begin by describing some quotients
of the dual Steenrod algebra

. For

Therefore

-algebra.

\textstyle{(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}

which is dual to

\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}.

This spectral sequence is tri-graded with

\mathrm{E}_{2}
-term

{\vphantom{\mathrm{E}}}{}^{\text{\tiny CE}}\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Ext^{s}_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{(1)}))

where we have suppressed mention of the
internal grading. As
(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}

acts trivially on
\mathcal{A}_{(1)}
,

\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{(1)})\cong\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\overset{(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{A}_{(1)},

where
\overset{(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}{\boxtimes}

indicates the
\Bbbk
-tensor product with the diagonal
action of the cocommutative Hopf algebra

(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}
. Since there
is a surjection of Hopf algebras

\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)},

dually there is an injection

(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}\to(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}=\mathcal{A}_{(1)}

so the Milnor-Moore theorem implies that

\mathcal{A}_{(1)}
is a free

(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}
-module.
Hence as
(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}
-modules,

\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\overset{(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{A}_{(1)}\cong\mathcal{A}_{(1)}\otimes\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Feeding this into our
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-term we obtain

{\vphantom{\mathrm{E}}}{}^{\text{\tiny CE}}\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}

\displaystyle\cong\Ext^{s}_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{(1)})\otimes\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})

\displaystyle\cong\Coext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)}}(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)},\mathbb{F}_{2})\otimes\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})

\displaystyle\cong\Coext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2})\otimes\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})

\displaystyle=0

since
\mathcal{A}_{*}
is a cofree comodule over the

P_{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}
.

Now we can conclude that
\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,*}(BP,{M\mathrm{Sp}})=0

and so
({M\mathrm{Sp}}^{^}_{2})^{*}(BP)=0
.

The rest of the proof is similar and uses
the spectral sequence (
2.5
)
to reduce to calculate some
\Coext
groups
using the dual algebras. We will describe
the main details required for these.

To verify that
\langle Y_{n}\rangle>\langle{M\mathrm{Sp}}\rangle

the key step involves showing that

\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{*,*}(\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*},\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*})=\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{*,*}(\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2})=0,

where

\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{*,*}(\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}^{*,*}(\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

By
[
7
]
*corollary 21.2.5, there is
a sequence of Hopf algebras

\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}=(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*})\backslash\!\backslash(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*})\to\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}

whose the dual sequence of algebras gives
rise to a Cartan-Eilenberg spectral
sequence (
2.5
) with

{\vphantom{\mathrm{E}}}{}^{\text{\tiny CE}}\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}

\displaystyle\cong\Ext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}_{(2)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{(2)}))

\displaystyle\cong\Ext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}_{(2)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\overset{\mathcal{A}_{(2)}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{A}_{(2)})

\displaystyle\cong\Ext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}_{(2)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{A}_{(2)})\otimes\Ext_{(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)})^{*}}^{t}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}))

\displaystyle=0

since
\mathcal{A}_{(2)}
is a
P
-algebra.

Finally, the verification that

\langle Y_{n}\rangle>\langle Y_{n+1}\rangle
proceeds
in this fashion with
\mathcal{P}(n+1)^{(2)}_{*}
in place
of
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(2)}
and involves the vanishing of

\Coext_{\mathcal{P}(n+1)^{(2)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}^{s,4*}(\mathcal{P}(n+1)^{(2)}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{P}(n+1)_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)_{*}}^{s,*}(\mathcal{P}(n+1)_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2})

which follows from the facts that
\mathcal{P}(n+1)_{*}

is cofree over
\mathcal{P}(n+1)_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)_{*}
by
the Milnor-Moore theorem and the latter is
a
P_{*}
-algebra.
∎

intermediate between
{M\mathrm{Sp}}
and
BP
. These
include Pengelley’s

11
]
,

{M\mathrm{SU}}
and the sequence of
{M\mathrm{Sp}}
-module
spectra obtained by killing the Ray elements

{\varphi}_{(s)}={\varphi}_{s}\in\pi_{2^{s+2}-3}({M\mathrm{Sp}})

as described by Botvinnik
[

(both including and excluding the generator

similarly,
\langle{M\mathrm{SU}}\rangle=\langle BP\rangle
.
So there are no novel Bousfield classes between

\langle{M\mathrm{Sp}}\rangle
and

stemming from these.

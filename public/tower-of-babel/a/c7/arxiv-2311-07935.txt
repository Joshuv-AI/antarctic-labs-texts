Counting function and lower bound for Dirichlet eigenvalues of the m m -order logarithmic Laplacian


Abstract
Our aim in this article is to obtain the limit of counting function for the Dirichlet eigenvalues involving the m m -order logarithmic Laplacian ℒ m \mathcal{L}_{m} in a bounded Lipschitz domain Ω ∈ ℝ N , N ≥ 1 \Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{N},N\geq 1 and to derive also the lower bound, where m ( ≥ 2 ) ∈ ℕ m(\geq 2)\!\in\mathbb{N} and the Fourier transform ℱ ⁡ ( ℒ m ) ​ ( ξ ) = ( 2 ​ log ⁡ | ξ | ) m \mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_{m})(\xi)=(2\log|\xi|)^{m} .

Introduction

Let integer
N\geq 1
,
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the Lipschitz boundary for
N\geq 2
.
Our purpose of this paper is to study the spectral of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
in a bounded domain, i.e.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{m}u=\lambda u&{\rm in}\ \,\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{m}}u=0&{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where

\mathcal{L}_{m}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the
m
-order logarithmic Laplacian with
its Fourier transformation
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_{m})(\xi)=(2\log\xi)^{m}.

In recent years, as a prototype model integro-differential operators, the fractional Laplacian have been studied in various aspects, see e.g.

[
2
,
3
,
17
,
23
,
27
,
28
]
and the references therein.
Here the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
is defined as a singular integral operator

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

(1.2)

with a normalized constant
c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}.

An interesting limiting
property proposed in
[
12
]
is the following

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-\Delta u(x)\quad\ {\rm and}\quad\ \lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)\quad\ {\rm for}\ \,u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.3)

The author
[
5
]
extended the expansion (
1.3
) to
for
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+s\mathcal{L}_{1}u(x)+o(s)\quad{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+},

(1.4)

where, formally, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is the 1-orde
logarithmic Laplacian
. Moreover, qualitative properties of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
are studied there. Here we mention
[
11
,
13
,
16
]
on some related limiting properties of fractional problems.

Very recently, the first author of this paper in
[
7
]
gave a complete expansion by involving high order logarithmic Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
.
Now we introduce the
m
-order logarithmic Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
. Before this, we need to provide
some notations:

\kappa_{1}(s)=2^{-2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1+s)}\quad{\rm and}\quad\kappa_{2}(s)=2^{-2s}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\,\Gamma(1+s)}.

(1.5)

Note that
\kappa_{i}\in C^{\infty}
in
(-\min\{\frac{N}{2},1\},\min\{\frac{N}{2},1\})
and we use the notation
\kappa_{i}^{(m)}(0)
the
m
-th order derivative for
i=1,2
.

Given an integer
m\geq 1
, the
m
order logarithmic Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is an integro-differential operator defined by

\mathcal{L}_{m}\zeta(x)=\sum^{m}_{j=0}\alpha_{j}\mathcal{K}_{j}\zeta(x)\quad{\rm for}\ \,\zeta\in C^{0,\beta}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(1.6)

where
\beta\in(0,1)
,

\alpha_{0}=(-1)^{m}\kappa_{2}^{(m)}(0),\qquad\alpha_{j}=m(-1)^{m+j}\binom{m-1}{j-1}\kappa_{1}^{(m-j)}(0),\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,m

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}\zeta=\zeta,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{j}\zeta(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\zeta(x)1_{B_{1}(x)}(y)-\zeta(y)}{|x-y|^{N}}(-2\log|x-y|)^{j-1}\,dy\ \ {\rm for\ integer}\ j\geq 1.

(1.7)

With help of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
, the expansion of fractional Laplacian with respect to the order could be written that for
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+\sum^{n}_{m=1}\frac{s^{m}}{m!}\mathcal{L}_{m}u(x)+o(s^{n})\quad{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+}.

From this expression,
\mathcal{L}_{m}
could be also viewed as the
m
-order derivative of fractional Laplacian, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{m}=\frac{d^{m}}{ds^{m}}(-\Delta)^{s}\big|_{s=0^{+}}
.

As one of most important of properties, eigenvalues for differential operators has been studied extensively and deeply.
Denote by
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L},\Omega,\mu)
the number of Dirichlet eigenvalues of operator
\mathcal{L}
in
\Omega
, counted according their multiplicity, not exceeding the value
\mu
.
In 1912, Weyl in
[
29
]
shows that the
k
-th eigenvalue
\mu_{k}(\Omega)
of Dirichlet problem

-\Delta u=\mu u\quad{\rm in}\ \ \Omega,\qquad u=0\quad{\rm{on}}\ \ \partial\Omega

(1.8)

has the asymptotic behavior

\mathcal{N}(-\Delta,\Omega,\mu)\sim b_{N}|\Omega|\mu^{\frac{N}{2}}\quad{\rm as}\ \,\mu\to+\infty,

and

\mu_{k}(\Omega)\sim c_{N}(k|\Omega|)^{\frac{2}{N}}\quad{\rm as}\ \,k\to+\infty,

where
b_{N}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}|B_{1}|
and
c_{N}=(2\pi)^{2}|B_{1}|^{-\frac{2}{N}}
.

In 1960 Pólya in
[
24
]
gave an equivalent bound

\mu_{k}(\Omega)\geq c_{N}\left(\frac{k}{|\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{2}{N}},\quad\mathcal{N}(-\Delta,\Omega,\mu)\leq b_{N}|\Omega|\mu^{\frac{N}{2}}

(1.9)

for any ”plane-covering domain”
D
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, this proof works in dimension
N\geq 3
.
From then, it attracts great attentions for the asymtoptics of the eigenvalues in various aspect. The authors in
[
25
,
21
]
independently proved first lower bound in (
1.9
) with a positive constant
c
for general bounded domain; Li-Yau
[
20
]
improved the value of
the constant
C
obtaining
C=\frac{N}{N+2}C_{N}
. We also refer to
[
10
,
18
,
9
]
for generalized topics,
[
4
,
15
]
for degenerate elliptic operators and
[
1
,
8
,
6
,
30
]
for the fractional Laplacian, where
the lower bound of the eigenvalues reads

\mu_{s,k}(\Omega)\geq\frac{N}{N+2s}C_{N}\left(\frac{k}{|\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{2s}{N}}.

(1.10)

Moreover, in the fractional case,
[
14
]
extends the
limit of the counting function of the fractional Laplacian

\mathcal{N}((-\Delta)^{s},\Omega,\mu)\sim b_{N}|\Omega|\mu^{\frac{N}{2s}}\quad{\rm as}\ \,\mu\to+\infty.

Involving the logarithmic Laplacian with the order
m=1
, the basic properties of Dirichlet eigenvalues in bounded domain are investigated
[
6
,
13
]
, the limit of counting function for the Dirichlet eigenvalues is derived in
[
19
]

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}_{1},\omega,\lambda)\sim\frac{\omega_{{}_{N}}}{N(2\pi)^{N}}|\Omega|e^{\frac{N}{2}\lambda}\quad{\rm as}\ \,\lambda\to+\infty.

Our aim of this article is to give the limit of counting function and the lower bound of eigenvalues for high order logarithmic Laplacian with
m\geq 2
. For this, we first denote by
\mathbb{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{m}:=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\log(e+|\xi|))^{m}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi}.

It is shown in
[
7
]
that the embedding
\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact.
Note that the corresponding quadratic form of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
could be defined as

\varphi\mapsto(\varphi,\varphi)_{m}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(2\log|\xi|)^{m}|\hat{\varphi}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi.

It is shown that
(\cdot,\cdot)_{m}
defines a closed, symmetric and semibounded quadratic form with domain
\mathbb{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
.
Note that

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{m})\subset\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega).

The spectrum point
(\lambda_{m,i},\phi_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
could write as

(\phi_{m,k},v)_{m}=\lambda_{m,i}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{m,k}vdx\quad{\rm for\ any}\ v\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega).

(1.11)

From
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
problem (
1.1
) has a sequence of Dirichlet eigenvalues

-\infty<\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{m,2}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)\leq\cdots\quad\to+\infty\ \ {\rm as}\ \,k\to+\infty

and the corresponding eigenfunction
\phi_{m,k}\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
such that
\|\phi_{m,k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
.

Remark 1.1

From the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{L}_{m})(\xi)=(2\log\xi)^{m}
, we have the relationship that

\mathcal{L}_{m}=\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{L}_{1}}_{m}.

Letting for
(\lambda_{1,k},\phi_{1,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Dirichlet eigenvalues and related eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, then it fails to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}\phi_{1,k}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\circ(\mathcal{L}_{1}\phi_{1,k})=\lambda_{1,k}^{2}\phi_{1,k}\quad{\rm in}\ \,\Omega

because although
\mathcal{L}_{1}\phi_{1,k}=\lambda_{1,k}\phi_{1,k}
in
\Omega
but
\mathcal{L}_{1}\phi_{1,k}\not=0
in
\Omega^{c}
, because of the non-local property of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Therefore, the set of the Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is different from
\{(\lambda_{1,k}^{m}(\Omega)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

For simplicity, we use the notation of
\mathcal{N}_{m}(\lambda)=\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L}_{m},\Omega,\lambda)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{m}(\lambda)=\#\big\{k:\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)<\lambda\big\},

(1.12)

which is the counting function for the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{m}
.
Our main result on the limit of counting function for the Dirichlet eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 1.1

Let
m\geq 2
,
\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)
be the
k^{th}
eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{m}
in
\Omega
under the zero Dirichlet boundary condition, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}e^{-\frac{N}{2}\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}\mathcal{N}_{m}(\lambda)=\frac{T_{N}|\Omega|}{N}

(1.13)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-\frac{m-1}{m}}e^{-\frac{N}{2}\lambda^{\frac{1}{m}}}\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega))_{+}=\frac{2m}{N^{2}}T_{N}|\Omega|,

(1.14)

where
T_{N}=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N}}}{(2\pi)^{N}}
and
a_{+}=\max\{0,a\}
.

We provide asymptotic formulas for the counting function (
1.13
) and (
1.14
), which could lead to the Weyl’s limit if we have the estimates of multiplicity of eigenvalues. Here we want to mention that the Weyl’s limit is derived from the upper and lower bounds in
[
6
]
for
m=1
by the Li-Yau’s lower bound and Kröger’s upper bound. An very interesting point is that the limit of the sum of first
k
eigenvalues coincides the Weyl’s limit and they don’t depends on the volume of the domain.

For lower bound, we have the following estimates:

Theorem 1.2

Let the integer
m,k\geq 2
,
\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)
be the
k^{\rm th}
Dirichlet eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{m}
,

a_{m}=\frac{N^{m+1}}{2^{m}m}\frac{(2\pi)^{N}}{\omega_{{}_{N}}},\quad b_{m}=\frac{2^{m}}{N^{m}}(m-1)^{m}+\frac{1}{N^{m}}

and
c_{m}=a_{m}b_{m}
.

(i)
When
either
m
is odd and
\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)\geq 0
or
m
is even,
then

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)\geq

\displaystyle\big(\frac{2}{N}\big)^{m}\min\Big\{\big(\frac{c_{m}}{e^{\tau_{0}}}\big)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}(\frac{k}{|\Omega|})^{\frac{m}{m-1}},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\ \Big(\log\big(c_{m}\frac{k}{|\Omega|}+\tau_{0}^{m-1}e^{\tau_{0}}\big)-(m-1)\log\big(\log(c_{m}\frac{k}{|\Omega|}+e)\big)-\log 2\Big)^{m}\Big\}-b_{m},

where
\tau_{0}>1
is only dependent of
m
and
\omega_{{}_{N}}
is the volume of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(ii)
When
m
is odd and
\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)<0
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)\geq\big(\frac{2}{N}\big)^{m}\min\Big\{

\displaystyle\big(\frac{a_{m}}{e^{\tau_{0}}}\big)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\big(\frac{P_{m}(\Omega)}{|\Omega|}\big)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}k^{\frac{m}{m-1}},

\displaystyle\Big(\log(a_{m}\frac{P_{m}(\Omega)}{|\Omega|}k+\tau_{0}^{m-1}e^{\tau_{0}})-(m-1)\log\big(2\log(a_{m}\frac{P_{m}(\Omega)}{|\Omega|}k+e)\big)\Big)^{m}\Big\}-P_{m}(\Omega),

where
P_{m}(\Omega)=-\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)+b_{m}
.

Our main difficulty comes from
m\geq 2
in the derivation of the limits of counting function and the lower bound. For
m=1
, there is a special scaling property

\lambda_{1,k}(R\Omega)=\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)-\log R,

because of the property
\log(R|\xi|)=\ln(|\xi|)+\log R
, where
R\Omega=\{Rx:\,x\in\Omega\}
.
However, the above property fails for
m\geq 2
and we provide a related rescaling estimates.

Due to the inhomogeneity of the expression of the Fourier symbol of
(2\log(|\!\cdot\!|)^{m}
, the essential tool for the lower bound is the estimates of the count functionfunction. It is worth noting that our lower bound
\lambda_{m,k}
is based on the value
\lambda_{m,1}
and we address some basic properties of
\lambda_{m,1}
in Section 2. In Section 3, we build the basic estimates for the count function of the eigenvalues and the limit of counting functions.
Section 4 is devoted to lower bound of
\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)
.

2
Preliminary properties

2.1
Recaling property

We start the section from the recalling property.

Proposition 2.1

Let
m\geq 3
be
odd
,
\Omega
be a Lipschitz bounded open set and
\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)
be the
k^{th}
Dirichlet eigenvalue of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{m}
, for
k\geq 2
. For
R>1
, denote

\Omega_{{}_{R}}=\{Rx:x\in\Omega\},

then

2^{-m}\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)-4^{m}(\log R)^{m}-\frac{4^{m}-1}{(2\pi)^{N}}C_{m}|\Omega|\leq\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega_{{}_{R}})\leq\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)-2(\log R)^{m},

where
C_{m}=\int_{B_{1}}\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}d\xi
.

Proof.
Due to
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\subset\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
is dense, we set
\phi_{m,k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\phi_{R,m,k}(x)=R^{-\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{m,k}(\frac{x}{R})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

then
\|\phi_{R,m,k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|\phi_{m,k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{R,m,k}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-i\zeta\cdot x}R^{-\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{m,k}(\frac{x}{R})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-iR\zeta\cdot y}R^{-\frac{N}{2}}\phi_{m,k}(y)d(Ry)

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-iR\zeta\cdot y}\phi_{m,k}(y)dy

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{N}{2}}\hat{\phi}_{m,k}(R\zeta),

where
y=\frac{x}{R}
, then we obtain that

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega_{{}_{R}})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega_{{}_{R}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\phi_{R,m,k}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{L}_{m}(\phi_{R,m,k}(x))\phi_{R,m,k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(2\log|\zeta|)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{R,m,k}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{m}R^{N}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\log|\zeta|)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}(R\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta.

On the one hand, for
a,b>0
,
m\geq 3
odd, one has

(a+b)^{m}\geq a^{m}+b^{m}\quad{\rm and}\quad(a-b)^{m}\leq a^{m}-2^{1-m}b^{m}

thus, letting
\xi=R\zeta
, direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega_{{}_{R}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(\log|\xi|-\log R\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\log|\xi|-\log R\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi+\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\big(\log|\xi|-\log R\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}+(\log R)^{m}\big)|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\big(\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}-2^{1-m}(\log R)^{m}\big)|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi-\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{B_{1}}\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\quad-2^{1-m}(\log R)^{m}\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)-2(\log R)^{m}.

On the other hand,
for
a,b>0
,
m\geq 3
odd, there holds

(a+b)^{m}\leq 2^{m}(a^{m}+b^{m})\quad{\rm and}\quad(a-b)^{m}\geq 2^{-m}(a^{m}-2^{m}b^{m}),

then

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega_{{}_{R}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\log|\xi|-\log R\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi+\frac{2^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\big(\log|\xi|-\log R\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{4^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}+(\log R)^{m}\big)|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\big(\big|\log|\xi|\big|^{m}-2^{m}(\log R)^{m}\big)|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(\log|\xi|\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi+\frac{4^{m}-1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{B_{1}}\big(\log|\xi|\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi-\frac{4^{m}}{(2\pi)^{N}}(\log R)^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-m}\lambda_{m,k}(\Omega)-2^{m}(2\log R)^{m}-\frac{4^{m}-1}{(2\pi)^{N}}C_{m}|\Omega|,

where

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B_{1}}\big(\log|\xi|\big)^{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|^{2}d\xi\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}|\hat{\phi}_{m,k}|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m}|\phi_{m,k}|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{m}|\Omega||\phi_{m,k}|_{L^{2}}^{2}=C_{m}|\Omega|.

We complete the proof.
\Box

(ii)

2.2
Lower bound for
\lambda_{m,1}(B_{r_{0}})

Let
r_{0}\in(0,1]
be such that
the sum of the kernel

\sum^{m}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}(-2\ln t)^{j-1}>0\quad{\rm for}\ \,0<t<{r_{0}}.

(2.1)

Proposition 2.2

Let integer
N\geq 2
, odd integer
m\geq 3
and
r_{0}\in(0,1)
,
then we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{m,1}(B_{r_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{m}(\log(2\sqrt{N+2})-\log r_{0})^{m}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2^{m}(2\sqrt{N+2})^{N}\omega_{{}_{N}}^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2N}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{N^{j+2}}\frac{m!}{(m-j)!}(\log(2\sqrt{N+2})-\log r_{0})^{m-j}.

(2.2)

Proof.
Let
v\in L^{2}(B_{r_{0}})
be a radial function such that with
\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{r_{0}})}=1
and recall the Bessel function
J_{l}(t)=(\frac{t}{2})^{l}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!\Gamma(j+l+1)}(\frac{t}{2})^{2j}
.
For
l\geq\sqrt{3}-2
and
0\leq t\leq 2\sqrt{2(l+2)}
, it follows by the appendix of
[
19
]

|J_{l}(t)|\leq\frac{t^{l}}{2^{l}\Gamma(l+1)}.

Taking
l=\frac{N}{2}-1
, we have that

|J_{\frac{N}{2}-1}(t)|\leq\frac{t^{\frac{N}{2}-1}}{2^{\frac{N}{2}-1}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}t^{\frac{N}{2}-1}.

Note that
\hat{v}
is also radial and

\displaystyle|\hat{v}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=s^{1-\frac{N}{2}}\Big|\int_{0}^{r_{0}}v(r)J_{\frac{N}{2}-1}(rs)r^{\frac{N}{2}}dr\Big|

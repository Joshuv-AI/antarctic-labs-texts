Brownian particle in the curl of 2-d stochastic heat equations
Guilherme L. Feltes, Hendrik Weber

Abstract
We study the long time behaviour of a Brownian particle evolving in a dynamic random environment. Recently, Toninelli et al. [ 7 ] proved sharp l ​ o ​ g \sqrt{log} -super diffusive bounds for a Brownian particle in the curl of (a regularisation of) the 2-d Gaussian Free Field (GFF) ω ¯ \underline{\omega} . We consider a one parameter family of Markovian and Gaussian dynamic environments which are reversible with respect to the law of ω ¯ \underline{\omega} . Adapting their method, we show that if s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 , with s = 1 s=1 corresponding to the standard stochastic heat equation, then the particle stays l ​ o ​ g \sqrt{log} -super diffusive, whereas if s < 1 s<1 , corresponding to a fractional heat equation, then the particle becomes diffusive. In fact, for s < 1 s<1 , we show that this is a particular case of Komorowski and Olla [ 13 ] , which yields an invariance principle through a Sector Condition result. Our main results agree with the Alder-Wainwright scaling argument (see [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 8 ] ) used originally in [ 21 ] to predict the log \log -corrections to diffusivity. We also provide examples which display l ​ o ​ g a log^{a} -super diffusive behaviour for a ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ] a\in(0,1/2] .

Introduction and main result

We study the motion of a Brownian particle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, evolving in a dynamic random environment (DRE), given by the solution to the Itô SDE

\begin{cases}&\operatorname{d}\!X(t)=\omega_{t}(X(t))\operatorname{d}\!t+\sqrt{2}\operatorname{d}\!B(t),\quad t\geq 0,\\
&X(0)=0\;,\end{cases}

(1)

where
(B(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a standard two-dimensional Brownian motion and
(\omega_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}
is a time-dependent random field which is independent from
(B(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
We take
(\omega_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}
to be a regularised version of the curl of the solution to the (fractional) stochastic heat equation with additive noise in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and initial condition given by the curl of the regularised Gaussian Free Field (GFF)
\underline{\omega}
. The coordinates of
\omega_{t}=(\omega_{t}^{1},\omega_{t}^{2})
satisfy

\begin{cases}\operatorname{d}\!\omega_{t}^{k}=-(-\Delta)^{s}\omega_{t}^{k}\operatorname{d}\!t+\sqrt{2}\partial^{\perp}_{k}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}\operatorname{d}\!W_{t}\;,\;t\geq 0\;\;,\;\;k=1,2\;\;,\\
\omega_{0}=\underline{\omega}\;\;,\end{cases}

(2)

where
s\in[0,\infty)
and
(\partial^{\perp}_{1},\partial^{\perp}_{2}):=(\partial_{x_{2}},-\partial_{x_{1}})
. Here,
W
is a mollified (in space) space-time white noise, with covariance
\mathbf{E}[W_{r}(x)W_{t}(y)]=\min\{r,t\}V(x-y)
, and
\underline{\omega}
is distributed according to the law of the curl of a mollified GFF. More precisely, for every
k,l=1,2
,
r,t\geq 0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
W_{t}^{s}(x):=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}W_{t}(x)
and
\underline{\omega}
have mean zero and covariance

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\partial^{\perp}_{k}W_{r}^{s}(x)\partial^{\perp}_{l}W_{t}^{s}(y)]=\min\{r,t\}\partial^{\perp}_{k}\partial^{\perp}_{l}V\convolution g_{1-s}(x-y)=\min\{r,t\}(\partial^{\perp}_{k}(-\Delta)^{s-1}\delta_{x},\partial^{\perp}_{l}\delta_{y})_{V}\text{ if }s\leq 1\;,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\partial^{\perp}_{k}W_{r}^{s}(x)\partial^{\perp}_{l}W_{t}^{s}(y)]=\min\{r,t\}\partial^{\perp}_{k}\partial^{\perp}_{l}(-\Delta)^{s-1}V(x-y)=\min\{r,t\}(\partial^{\perp}_{k}(-\Delta)^{s-1}\delta_{x},\partial^{\perp}_{l}\delta_{y})_{V}\text{ if }s>1\;,

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\underline{\omega}^{k}(x)\underline{\omega}^{l}(y)]=-\partial^{\perp}_{k}\partial^{\perp}_{l}V\convolution g_{1}(x-y)=(\partial^{\perp}_{k}(-\Delta)^{-1}\delta_{x},\partial^{\perp}_{l}\delta_{y})_{V}\;,

(5)

where
\convolution
denotes the convolution over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and, for every
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the space of Schwartz functions over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})_{V}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi_{1}(x)V(x-y)\varphi_{2}(y)\operatorname{d}\!x\operatorname{d}\!y\;\;.

(6)

The smooth function
V
is given by

V:=U\convolution U\;,

(7)

for a
U\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, radially symmetric, decaying exponentially fast at infinity and with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}U(x)\operatorname{d}\!x=1
. To simplify some computations, we may also assume that
U
has Fourier transform supported in ball of radius
1
. Also, the kernel
g_{r}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
in (
3
) and (
5
) is given by

\begin{cases}g_{1}(x)&=-(2\pi)^{-1}\log|x|\;\;;\\
g_{r}(x)&=\dfrac{\Gamma(1-r)}{4^{r}\Gamma(r)\pi}\dfrac{1}{|x|^{2-2r}}\quad\text{ if }r\in(0,1)\;\;;\\
g_{0}&=\delta_{0}\;\;\;,\end{cases}

where
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function. I.e.,
g_{r}
is the Green’s function of
(-\Delta)^{r}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for
r\in[0,1]
. Also, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s-1}
for
s>1
can be defined in terms of its Fourier multiplier, as
\widehat{(-\Delta)^{s-1}f}(p)=|p|^{2(s-1)}\widehat{f}(p)
.

Remark 1
.

Note that expressions (
3
)-(
5
) make sense due to the presence of the smooth function
V
. Plugging, e.g., the right-hand side of (
5

(\partial^{\perp}_{k}(-\Delta)^{-1}\delta_{x},\partial^{\perp}_{l}\delta_{y})_{V}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\delta_{x}(u)\partial^{\perp}_{k}\partial^{\perp}_{l}(-\Delta)^{-1}V(u-v)\delta_{y}(v)\operatorname{d}\!u\operatorname{d}\!v\;,

\partial^{\perp}_{k}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s-1}{2}}W_{t}
when

Remark 2
.

As we show in Proposition
8
, the dynamics (
2
) leave the law of
\underline{\omega}
invariant.
The case
s=1
corresponds to the standard stochastic heat equation (SHE), whereas
s=0
is the infinite dimensional Ornstein Uhlenbeck process, as defined, for example in
[
17
, Chapter 1.4]
.
The parameter
s\in[0,\infty)
controls the speed of the environment on different scales: smaller values of
s
correspond to faster movement of the larger scales.

By definition, the drift field
\omega_{t}(x)
in (
1
) is
divergence-free
. Brownian particles evolving in stationary divergence-free random fields have been considered as a toy model for anomalous diffusions in inhomogeneous media, such as the motion of a tracer particle in an incompressible turbulent flow. See e.g. the surveys
[
11
, Chapter 11]
and
[
14
]
.
Depending on the decay of the spatial correlations of the drift field, the particle could behave either
diffusively
or
superdiffusively
, meaning that the mean square displacement satisfies for large
t

D(t):=\frac{\mathbb{E}[|X(t)|^{2}]}{t}\approx\begin{cases}1\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{diffusive,}\\
t^{\nu}\;,\;\nu>0\;\quad\text{superdiffusive.}\end{cases}

(8)

Here,
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation under the joint law of
B
and
\omega
, see Section
2
. If the correlations of the environment decay fast enough (see e.g.
[
11
, Chapter 11]
), one gets diffusive behaviour, and if the decay is too slow (see
[
12
]
), one gets superdiffusive behaviour. There is, however, an intermediate regime for which the correlations decay in such a way that
D(t)
diverges only as
(\log t)^{\gamma}
, for
\gamma>0
. These logarithmic corrections
are expected to be present in two-dimensional Brownian particles evolving in isotropic random drift fields. Indeed, by the Alder-Wainwright scaling argument (see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
8
]
), in 2d, if the displacement of the particle scales faster than the correlations of the environment field, then the (only) expected behaviour for the mean square displacement of the particle is to be of order
t\sqrt{\log t}
. We briefly elaborate on this, following the Appendix of
[
21
]
. Let
K(t,x):=\mathbf{E}[\omega_{0}(0)\omega_{t}(x)]
. Now, assume that
\mathbb{P}(X(t)\in\operatorname{d}\!x)\approx\alpha(t)^{-2}\varphi(\alpha(t)^{-1}x)\operatorname{d}\!x
, where
\varphi
is a density and
\alpha(t)=t^{\nu}(\log t)^{\gamma}
for some
\nu,\gamma\geq 0
. If we also assume that
K(t,x)\approx\beta(t)^{-2}\psi(\beta(t)^{-1}x)
, for another density
\psi
,
then if

\dfrac{\beta(t)}{\alpha(t)}\leq\text{constant, for }t\geq 0\;\;,

(9)

we must have
\nu=1/2
and
\gamma=1/4
, which yields
X(t)\approx t\sqrt{\log t}
. We emphasise here that this argument, even though instructive, it is not mathematically rigorous.
Indeed, the
\sqrt{\log}
correction was rigorously established recently by Toninelli et al.
[
7
]
. They showed that for a time-independent drift field
\omega
distributed according to the law of
\underline{\omega}
, one has

D(t)\approx\sqrt{\log t}\text{ as }t\to\infty\;\;,

(10)

up to
\log\log t
corrections, confirming a conjecture made by Tóth and Valkó
[
21
]
based on this scaling argument.
The result
was obtained in the
Tauberian sense

\eta_{t}:=\omega_{t}(\cdot+X(t))\;,\;t\geq 0\;\;.

(18)

The law of

and therefore, we may focus on

Remark 6

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}:\omega^{i_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots\cancel{\omega^{i_{k}}(x_{k})}\cdots\omega^{i_{n}}(x_{n}):(-(-\Delta)^{s})\omega^{i_{k}}(x_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k,l=1\atop k\neq l}^{n}:\omega^{i_{1}}(x_{1})\cdots\cancel{\omega^{i_{k}}(x_{k})}\cdots\cancel{\omega^{i_{l}}(x_{l})}\cdots\omega^{i_{n}}(x_{n}):((-\Delta)^{s-1}\partial^{\perp}_{i_{k}}\delta_{x_{k}},\partial^{\perp}_{i_{l}}\delta_{x_{l}})_{V}\;\;.

\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!\theta\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-\tilde{\lambda}-|p|^{2}}}\dfrac{r\operatorname{d}\!r}{(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+r^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+r^{2})}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\int_{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}}^{1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!s}{sUB_{k}(s)}

(91)

Lastly, we control the integral in (
90
) using
|\hat{V}(q)-\hat{V}(0)|<C|q|^{2}
for
|q|\leq 1
and (
84
)

\displaystyle\int_{|q|^{2}<1-(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}\dfrac{|\hat{V}(q)-\hat{V}(0)|(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq C\dfrac{LB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\int_{|q|^{2}<1-(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}\dfrac{|q|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2}}\leq C\dfrac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{\sqrt{z}}\int_{|q|<1}\operatorname{d}\!q\leq C\dfrac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{\sqrt{z}}\;\;.

The estimate of the third term is then concluded.

Finally, we deal with (
86
), even though not necessary, we treat the cases
s>1
and
s=1
differently, to emphasise the influence of the exponent
2s
. Consider first
s>1
. We see that the difference in (
86
) is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}|q|^{2s}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})+|q|^{2s}](\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)|q|^{2s}\operatorname{d}\!q}{(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})^{2}UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})}\leq C\frac{LB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\hat{V}(q)|q|^{2s-4}\operatorname{d}\!q\leq C\frac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{\sqrt{z}}\;\;,

where in the first inequality we have used that
|q|^{2s}\geq 0
and that
UB_{k}\geq 1
, and the integral in the last line is of order
\int_{0}^{1}r^{2s-3}\operatorname{d}\!r\leq C
since
s>1
. Now, we treat
s=1
. The difference in (
86
) is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}|q|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})+|q|^{2}](\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}|q|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})^{2}(UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2}))^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})UB_{k}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}+|q|^{2})}\;\;,

where in the last inequality we have used (
75
). Now, by the estimate obtained for (
87
), we have the following upper bound

\frac{1}{\sqrt{z}}\left[\dfrac{\pi}{2}\int_{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}}^{1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho UB_{k}(\varrho)}+C\dfrac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2},z)}{\sqrt{z}}\right]\;\;.

(92)

Now, note that

\int_{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}}^{1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{\varrho UB_{k}(\varrho)}-\int_{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}}^{1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{(\varrho^{2}+\varrho)UB_{k}(\varrho)}=\int_{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}}^{1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{(1+\varrho)UB_{k}(\varrho)}\leq\frac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2},z)}{\sqrt{z}}\;\;.

(93)

Therefore, (
92
) is upper bounded by

\frac{1}{\sqrt{z}}\left[\dfrac{\pi}{2}\int_{\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}}^{1}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!\varrho}{(\varrho^{2}+\varrho)UB_{k}(\varrho)}+C\dfrac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2},z)}{\sqrt{z}}\right]\leq C\dfrac{LB_{k+1}(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2},z)}{\sqrt{z}}\;\;.

where the last inequality is a consequence of (
76
) in Lemma
19
.
The result follows from collecting all the estimates so far.
∎

Lemma 23
.

Let the same assumptions of Lemma
19
to hold and let
\tilde{\lambda}=\lambda+|p_{1:n}|^{2s}
, for every
s\geq 1
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{\text{Off}}>0
, such that

|p|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})]|p^{\prime}+q|}\leq C_{\text{Off}}\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\;\;,

where
p=\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}
,
p^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p_{i}
and
|p_{1:n}|^{2s}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|p_{i}|^{2s}
.

Proof.

We split
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into three regions,
\Omega_{1}=\{q:|p+q|<\frac{|p|}{2}\}
,
\Omega_{2}=\{q:|p^{\prime}+q|<\frac{|p|}{2}\}
and
\Omega_{3}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus(\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2})
. Note that since we are looking for an upper bound, it is irrelevant whether
\Omega_{1}
intersects
\Omega_{2}
or not. In
\Omega_{1}
, note that
|p+q|<\frac{|p|}{2}\Rightarrow|q|<\frac{3}{2}|p|\Rightarrow|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2}<\frac{5}{2}|p|^{2}
, so use the monotonicity of
f
to get
f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})\geq f(\tilde{\lambda}+\frac{5}{2}|p|^{2})
and also
(\sin\theta)^{2}\leq\frac{|p+q|^{2}}{|p|^{2}}
to obtain

\displaystyle|p|\int_{\Omega_{1}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})]|p^{\prime}+q|}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{1}}\dfrac{C|p|^{-1}|p+q|^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+\frac{5}{2}|p|^{2})]|p^{\prime}+q|}\leq\dfrac{C|p|^{-1}}{f(\tilde{\lambda}+\frac{5}{2}|p|^{2})}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{|p^{\prime}+q|}\leq C\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\;,

since by assumption
f(x,z)\geq\frac{z}{g(x,z)}
and
g
is decreasing in
x
. Also, since in
\Omega_{1}
we have
|p+q|<\frac{|p|}{2}\Rightarrow|q|<\frac{3|p|}{2}
, the last integral is of order
|p|
. Indeed, note that denoting
B_{R}(a)
the ball of radius
R
centred at
a
,

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{|p^{\prime}+q|}\leq\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}|p|}(0)}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{|p^{\prime}+q|}=\int_{B_{\frac{3}{2}|p|}(-p^{\prime})}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{|q|}\leq\int_{B_{6|p|}(0)}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{|q|}\leq C\int_{0}^{6|p|}\operatorname{d}\!r\leq C|p|\;\;,

since
q\mapsto|q|^{-1}
has a singularity at zero.
For the region
\Omega_{2}
we use

(\sin\theta)^{2}\leq\dfrac{4|p+q|^{2}}{|p|^{2}\vee(\frac{1}{4}|p^{\prime}|^{2})}\;\;,

where
a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\}
. This is true since, for
|p^{\prime}|\leq 2|p|
, it is a weaker estimate than the previous one, and for
|p^{\prime}|>2|p|
it can be shown that, in the region
\Omega_{2}
, the right hand side is always greater or equal than
1
(see
[
7
, (A.13)]
). Inserting this into the integral it follows that

\int_{\Omega_{2}}\dfrac{|p|\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})]|p^{\prime}+q|}\leq\dfrac{C|p|}{|p|^{2}\vee(\frac{1}{4}|p^{\prime}|^{2})}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})|p^{\prime}+q|}.

Note that, in
\Omega_{2}
, we have that
|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2}\leq(\frac{3}{2}|p|+|p^{\prime}|)^{2}+(\frac{1}{2}|p|+|p^{\prime}|)^{2}\leq 2(\frac{3}{2}|p|+|p^{\prime}|)^{2}
, so using the monotonicity of
f
we obtain the upper bound

\dfrac{C|p|}{|p|^{2}\vee(\frac{1}{4}|p^{\prime}|^{2})f(\tilde{\lambda}+2(\frac{3}{2}|p|+|p^{\prime}|)^{2})}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!q}{|p^{\prime}+q|}=\dfrac{C|p|^{2}}{|p|^{2}\vee(\frac{1}{4}|p^{\prime}|^{2})f(\tilde{\lambda}+2(\frac{3}{2}|p|+|p^{\prime}|)^{2})}\;.

(94)

In order to estimate the last term we maximise in
|p^{\prime}|
(here we think of
p^{\prime}
as any vector in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). It can be easily seen that it is monotonously increasing for
|p^{\prime}|<2|p|
. For
|p^{\prime}|\geq 2|p|
we show that it is monotonously decreasing: since
f
satisfies (
79
),
for any
a,b\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!}{\operatorname{d}\!r}\left(\dfrac{1}{r^{2}f(a+2(b+r)^{2})}\right)=-\dfrac{2rf+4r^{2}(b+r)f^{\prime}}{r^{4}f^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{2}{r^{3}f^{2}}(f+2r(b+r)f^{\prime})<-\dfrac{2}{r^{3}f}\left(1-\dfrac{2r(b+r)}{a+2(b+r)^{2}}\right)<0\;,

where the argument of
f
and
f^{\prime}
is always
a+2(b+r)^{2}
. Therefore, the maximum over
p^{\prime}
of the right hand side of (
94
) is attained at
|p^{\prime}|=2|p|
and is equal to

\dfrac{C}{f(\tilde{\lambda}+2(\frac{7}{2}|p|)^{2})}\leq C\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\;\;.

The final part of the proof is to consider the region
\Omega_{3}
, for which we use
(\sin\theta)^{2}\leq 1
and apply Hölder inequality with exponents
\frac{3}{2}
and
3
, to the functions
[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})]^{-1}
and
|p^{\prime}+q|^{-1}
with respect to the measure
\hat{V}(q)\operatorname{d}\!q
to get

\displaystyle|p|\int_{\Omega_{3}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)(\sin\theta)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})]|p^{\prime}+q|}

\displaystyle\leq|p|\left(\int_{\Omega_{3}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}+|q|^{2})]^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{3}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)\operatorname{d}\!q}{|p^{\prime}+q|^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\;.

(95)

Since in
\Omega_{3}
we have that
|p^{\prime}+q|\geq\frac{|p|}{2}
, the second term in (
95
) is bounded by a constant times
|p|^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.

Moving to the integral inside the first parenthesis in (
95
), note that in
\Omega_{3}
we have that
|p+q|\geq\frac{|p|}{2}\Rightarrow|q|\leq 3|p+q|
and then by the monotonicity of
f
we get the upper bound

\int_{\Omega_{3}}\dfrac{\hat{V}(q)\operatorname{d}\!q}{[\tilde{\lambda}+|p+q|^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+10|p+q|^{2})]^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\int_{\frac{|p|}{2}}^{\infty}\dfrac{Cr\operatorname{d}\!r}{(\tilde{\lambda}+r^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2}))^{\frac{3}{2}}}

(96)

where the last inequality is obtained by bounding
\hat{V}(q)
by a constant, setting
\tilde{q}=p+q
and passing to polar coordinates. Now, we divide the domain of integration
\frac{|p|}{2}\leq r<\infty
into two regions,
\tilde{\lambda}<r^{2}
and its possibly empty complement
\tilde{\lambda}\geq r^{2}
. In the first, it holds that

\tilde{\lambda}+r^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2})\geq\dfrac{1}{20}(\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2})f(\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2})\;\;.

(97)

Using that by assumption
f(x,z)\geq\frac{z}{g(x,z)}
and that
g
is decreasing in
x
, together with (
97
), we can control (
96
) by

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{|p|}{2}}^{\infty}\dfrac{Cr\operatorname{d}\!r}{((\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2})f(\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2}))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C\left(\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+\frac{5}{2}|p|^{2})}{z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\frac{|p|}{2}}^{\infty}\dfrac{r\operatorname{d}\!r}{((\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2}))^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\frac{|p|}{2}}^{\infty}r^{-2}\operatorname{d}\!r\leq C|p|^{-1}\left(\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}

(98)

The last step is to consider the region
\tilde{\lambda}\geq r^{2}
. For that, we have

\int_{\frac{|p|}{2}}^{\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}}}\dfrac{Cr\operatorname{d}\!r}{(\tilde{\lambda}+r^{2}f(\tilde{\lambda}+10r^{2}))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq\dfrac{1}{f(11(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2}))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\frac{|p|}{2}}^{\infty}\dfrac{\operatorname{d}\!r}{r^{2}}\leq C|p|^{-1}\left(\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\;\;.

Inserting all the estimates for the region
\Omega_{3}
into (
95
) we obtain the desired upper bound

C\dfrac{g(\tilde{\lambda}+|p|^{2})}{z}

which completes the proof.
∎

Some Liouville-type theorems for the stationary 3D magneto-micropolar fluids
Jae-Myoung Kim and Seungchan Ko

Abstract
In this paper, we prove some Liouville-type theorems for the stationary magneto-micropolar fluids under suitable conditions in three space dimensions. We first prove that the solutions are trivial under the assumption of certain growth conditions for the mean oscillations of the potentials. And then we show similar results assuming that the solutions are contained in L p ​ ( ℝ 3 ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) with p ∈ [ 2 , 9 / 2 ) p\in[2,9/2) . Finally, we show the same result for lower values of p ∈ [ 1 , 9 / 4 ) p\in[1,9/4) with the further assumption that the solutions vanish at infinity. AMS Subject Classification Number: 35Q30, 76D05, 76D03 keywords: stationary magneto-micropolar equations, Liouville-type theorem

Introduction

In the present paper, we consider the stationary magneto-micropolar fluid equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which consists of the following partial differential equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\Pi&=\chi\nabla\times w+(b\cdot\nabla)b,\\
-\gamma\Delta w+(u\cdot\nabla)w&=\nabla(\nabla\cdot w)+\chi\nabla\times u-2\chi w,\\
-\nu\Delta b+(u\cdot\nabla)b&=(b\cdot\nabla)u,\\
\nabla\cdot u&=\nabla\cdot b\,=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
,
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
,
b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
and
\Pi
denote the fluid velocity,
the angular velocity of the rotation of the fluid particles, the
magnetic fields and pressure respectively. The positive constant

\gamma
in (
1.1
) correspond to the angular viscosity,

\nu
is the inverse of the magnetic Reynolds number and
\chi
is
the micro-rotational viscosity. In this paper, we assume that
\gamma
=
\nu
=
\chi
=1 for simplicity. Equation
\eqref{mag-mipolar}_{1}
is similar to the classical Navier–Stokes equations, but here it is coupled with equations
\eqref{mag-mipolar}_{2}
for
w
and
\eqref{mag-mipolar}_{3}
for
b
. Equation
\eqref{mag-mipolar}_{2}
describes the motion in the macro-volumes as they go through micro-rotational effects, represented by the micro-rotational velocity vector
w
. If the fluids have no microstructure,
w
vanishes and the system (
1.1
) becomes a magneto-hydrodynamics system. Equation
\eqref{mag-mipolar}_{3}
is the Maxwell system for the electric field. This model was first introduced by Ahmadi and Shahinpoor
[
1
]
. After that, Rojas-Medar
[
15
]
proved the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of strong solutions in a bounded domain based on the spectral Galerkin method. Furthermore, Rojas-Medar and Boldrini
[
16
]
established the existence of weak solutions to the model (
1.1
) in a bounded domain, and in particular, the uniqueness was also proved for a two-dimensional domain. The existence of global-in-time strong solutions was addressed by Ortega-Torres and Rojas-Medar
[
14
]
.

After Galdi’s work in
[
9
]
, Liouville-type problems for the
stationary fluid equations have been extensively studied and there are
a large number of works on the Liouville type-problems even to these dates (see e.g.
[
7
,
2
,
5
,
11
,
20
]
and a review paper
[
22
]
). Here, we shall study some Liouville-type results under the assumptions with regard to the potential functions.
We say that
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
is the
potential functions for the vector fields
u\in L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
,
if
\mathrm{div}\,\Phi=u
. In
[
18
]
, Seregin obtained Liouville-type theorems for the steady-state Navier-Stokes equations under the assumption that the potential
\Phi\in{\rm{BMO}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
u\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and in
[
19
]
the integrability condition for the velocity was dropped. After that, very recently, Chae and Wolf
[
6
]

showed Liouville-type theorem for the stationary Navier-Stokes
equations under the assumption

\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}|\Phi-\Phi_{B_{r}}|^{s}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\lesssim r^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{s}}\qquad\forall 1<r<+\infty

for some
3<s<+\infty
, and similar results were proved for MHD equations in
[
3
]
. The first theorem of the present paper is the extension of the result of
[
3
]
. Here, however, we shall adopt a different approach to control the pressure term by introducing an auxiliary function and utilizing it as a test function. In specific, we first aim in this paper to prove the following Liouville-type result.

Theorem 1

Let
(u,b,w,\Pi)
be a smooth solution to the equations (
1.1
). Assume that there exist potentials
\Phi,\Psi,\Upsilon\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})
such that
\nabla\cdot\Phi=u
,
\nabla\cdot\Psi=b
,
\nabla\cdot\Upsilon=w
and

\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\big|\Phi-\Phi_{B_{r}}\big|^{s}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}+\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\big|\Psi-\Psi_{B_{r}}\big|^{s}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

+\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}}\big|\Upsilon-\Upsilon_{B_{r}}\big|^{s}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq Cr^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{s}},\qquad r>1

(1.2)

for some
3<s\leq 6
. Then
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
.

Remark 1

In the case of
w\equiv 0
, Theorem
1
reduces to
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Later, Zhang et. al.
[
23
]
proved that if smooth solutions
of the stationary MHD equations are bounded in
L^{\frac{9}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and have finite Dirichlet integral, then they are also identically zero. After that, Schulz
[
17
]
obtained the
Liouville theorem for this equations provided that the smooth solution
(u,b)
are contained in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap{\rm{BMO}}^{-1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
p\in(2,6]
. Recently, Yuan and Xiao
[
21
]
proved
that if smooth solution
(u,b)\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with
2\leq p\leq\frac{9}{2}
, then
u=b=0
. In this direction, the second objective of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 2

Let
p\in[2,\frac{9}{2})
. Assume that
(u,b,w,\Pi)
is a
smooth solution to the equations (
1.1
) with
u,b,w\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
Then
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
.

Furthermore, parallel to the result of Liu and Liu
[
13
]
, we shall also prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3

Let
p\in[1,\frac{9}{4})
. Assume that
(u,b,w,\Pi)
is a
smooth solution to the equations (
1.1
) with
u,b,w\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfying
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\ u(x)=\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\ b(x)=\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\ w(x)=0
.
Then
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
.

Remark 2

Even if we consider the model with the variable density, that is, density-dependent models, Theorem
1
,
2
and
3
still hold under suitable additional assumptions (see, for example,
[
13
]
).

Remark 3

In the light of the work of Liu and Liu
[
13
]
, through a similar
approach, we can also obtain the Liouville-type results in Lorentz spaces (see e.g.
[
12
,
8
]
).

Preliminaries

x_{0}

Next, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

f\in L^{p}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}f(x){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot v

\displaystyle=f,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla v\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{p}

for some constant

. For this matter, we have the following theorem which is quoted from
[
9
]

Lemma 1

[
9

Assume that
\Omega
satisfies the cone condition. Then for given

with
1<p<\infty
satisfying (
2.2
), there exists at least one solution for the problem (
2.3
).

We will use this result to construct an auxiliary function to control the pressure term in the next section. We will also use the following iteration lemma frequently in our analysis.

Lemma 2

[
10

Let

be a non-negative bounded function on
[r_{0},r_{1}]\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

,

,

f(s)\leq C(a,b,d,\theta)\left[\frac{A}{(t-s)^{a}}+\frac{B}{(t-s)^{b}}+\frac{D}{(t-s)^{d}}+E\right].

for some constant
C>0
. We set
R>\rho>1
and
\overline{R}=(R+\rho)/2
. If we take
\varphi=\xi_{\rho,\overline{R}}
as a cut-off function and proceed with the same argument used to derive (
3.4
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\lesssim(R-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{\overline{R}}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+(R-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\overline{R}}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}.

And then we set
\psi=\xi_{\overline{R},R}
. Then as
\psi=1
on
B(\overline{R})
, we note that

\int_{B_{\rho}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\leq C({\rm{I}}_{1}+{\rm{I}}_{2}),

where

{\rm{I}}_{1}:=(R-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\psi^{2}u|^{2}+|\psi^{2}b|^{2}+|\psi^{2}w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x},

and

{\rm{I}}_{2}:=(R-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\psi^{3}u|^{3}+|\psi^{3}b|^{3}+|\psi^{3}w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}.

Then by Lemma
3
together with the assumption (
1.2
), and Young’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle(R-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{R}}|\psi^{3}u|^{3}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\leq CR(R-\rho)^{-1}\|\psi\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{\frac{18}{s+6}}+CR^{4-\frac{3}{s}}(R-\rho)^{-4}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt+CR(R-\rho)^{-1}\big((R-\rho)^{-1}\|\psi^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\big)^{\frac{18}{s+6}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\psi\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B(R))}^{2}+CR^{\frac{s+6}{s-3}}(R-\rho)^{-\frac{s+6}{s-3}}+{\rm{I}}_{1}.

In the same way, if we proceed with the above argument for
b
and
w
, we obtain

{\rm{I}}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+CR^{\frac{s+6}{s-3}}(R-\rho)^{-\frac{s+6}{s-3}}+{\rm{I}}_{1}.

Next, for
{\rm{I}}_{1}
, using (
2.4
), we have by Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle(R-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{R}}|\psi^{2}u|^{2}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{11}{6}-\frac{1}{s}}(R-\rho)^{-2}\|\psi\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}+CR^{\frac{11}{3}-\frac{2}{s}}(R-\rho)^{-4}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{2}(R-\rho)^{-2}\|\psi\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}+R^{4}(R-\rho)^{-4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\psi\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}+CR^{4}(R-\rho)^{-4}.

If we use the same method with
b
and
w
, it follows that

{\rm{I}}_{1}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+CR^{4}(R-\rho)^{-4}.

Collecting the estimates for
{\rm{I}}_{1}
and
{\rm{I}}_{2}
yields

\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+CR^{\frac{s+6}{s-3}}(R-\rho)^{-\frac{s+6}{s-3}},

where we have used the facts
R(R-\rho)^{-1}>1
and
3<s\leq 6
.
Then applying Lemma
2
gives us the estimate

\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\leq CR^{\frac{s+6}{s-3}}(R-\rho)^{-\frac{s+6}{s-3}}.

If we take
R=2\rho
and let
\rho\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain (
3.10
), and hence from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we conclude that (
3.5
) holds.

It remains to show (
3.6
).

By direct computation, we observe that

\displaystyle r^{-1}\int_{B_{r}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j}(2^{-j}r)^{-1}\int_{B_{2^{-(j-1)}r}\setminus B_{2^{-j}r}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{1/2\leq\rho\leq r/2}\rho^{-1}\int_{B_{2\rho}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt+\int_{B_{1}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}.

Therefore from (
3.5
), we have (
3.6
), and consequently, we deduce that the convergence (
3.7
) holds.

Now we are ready to conclude
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
. From (
3.4
) together with (
3.5
) and (
3.7
), we have

\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\to 0\qquad\mbox{as}\,\,\,\rho\rightarrow\infty,

which means that
u
,
b
and
w
are constants. By (
3.5
), we finally obtain that
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
.

4
Proof of Theorem
2

From (
3.3
) in the proof
in Theorem
1
, we know that

\hskip-227.62204pt\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{3}+|u||b|^{2}+|u||w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

(4.1)

In order to deal with the right-hand side of (
4.1
), let us consider two cases as follows:

(Case 1)

p\in[3,\frac{9}{2})
: Then we have by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\|(u,b,w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2\rho})}+(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\|(u,b,w)\|^{3}_{L^{3}(B_{2\rho})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho^{1-\frac{6}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{2\rho})}+\rho^{2-\frac{9}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{3}_{L^{p}(B_{2\rho})},

where we have chosen
\tau=2\rho
. Hence if we let
\rho\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}=0.

(4.2)

(Case 2)

p\in[2,\frac{18}{5})
: For this case, let us consider a non-negative cut-off function
\theta(x)=\xi_{\tau,2R}(x)-\xi_{\frac{R}{2},\rho}(x)
with
\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\max\{\frac{1}{\rho-\frac{R}{2}},\frac{1}{2R-\tau}\}
. Note that by the interpolation inequality,

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}_{L^{4}(B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho})}\lesssim\|w\theta\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\theta\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(w\theta)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\theta\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\left(\|(\nabla w)\theta\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|w(\nabla\theta)\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R/2})}\left(\|\nabla w\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R/2})}+\frac{1}{R^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|w\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R/2})}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R/2})}\|\nabla w\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R/2})}+\frac{1}{R^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R/2})}.

And thus, by Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u||w|^{2}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho})}\|w\|^{2}_{L^{4}(B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}\left(\|w\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}\|\nabla w\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}+\frac{1}{R^{3/2}}\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-4}\|(u,w)\|^{6}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}+\frac{(\tau-\rho)^{-1}}{R^{3/2}}\|(u,w)\|^{3}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{2R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+\frac{C}{(\tau-\rho)^{2}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt+\frac{C}{(\tau-\rho)^{4}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{6}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}+\frac{C}{(\tau-\rho)R^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{3}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{2R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+CR^{1-\frac{6}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt+CR^{5-\frac{18}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{6}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}+CR^{2-\frac{9}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{3}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

If we apply Lemma
2
with
f(r):=\int_{B_{r}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R/2}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{1-\frac{6}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}+R^{5-\frac{18}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{6}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt+R^{2-\frac{9}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{3}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

If we let
R\rightarrow\infty
we can immediately find that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}=0,

which implies that
u
,
b
and
w
are a constant vectors in

R^{3}
. Since
u,b,w\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
p\in[2,\frac{9}{2})
, we conclude that
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

5
Proof of Theorem
3

We first recall the fact that any continuous functions vanishing at infinity
must be bounded; thus, from the assumption of the theorem, we have
u,b,w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. From
(
3.4
) in the proof in Theorem
1
, we know that

\hskip-227.62204pt\int_{B_{\rho}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}

\lesssim\frac{1}{(\tau-\rho)^{2}}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}+\frac{1}{(\tau-\rho)}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}.

(5.1)

In order to control the right-hand side in (
5.1
), let us
consider it in two cases as follows:

(Case 1)

p\in[1,3/2)
: We note that with the choice
\tau=2R
and
\rho=R
,

(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|^{2}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

And thus, with the same arguments for
b
and
w
, we get

(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{1-\frac{3}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
),

(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|^{3}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{2-\frac{3}{p}}\|u\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})},

and similarly for
b
and
w
, we have

(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{2-\frac{3}{p}}\|(u,b,w)\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

(5.2)

(Case 2)

p\in[\frac{3}{2},\frac{9}{4})
: In this case, we note that

(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|^{2}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\|u\|^{1/2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|^{\frac{3}{2}}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{1-\frac{9}{2p}}\|u\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})},

and in the same way for
b
and
w
, we have

(\tau-\rho)^{-2}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{2}+|b|^{2}+|w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{1-\frac{9}{2p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

For the second term, in a similar way, we also get,

(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|^{3}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\|u\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}|u|^{\frac{3}{2}}{\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{2-\frac{9}{2p}}\|u\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})},

which directly implies that

(\tau-\rho)^{-1}\int_{B_{\tau}\setminus B_{\rho}}\left(|u|^{3}+|b|^{3}+|w|^{3}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim R^{2-\frac{9}{2p}}\|(u,b,w)\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})}.

(5.3)

Collecting (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have

\int_{B_{R}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right){\,{\mathrm{d}}x}\lesssim\begin{cases}(R^{1-\frac{3}{p}}+R^{2-\frac{3}{p}})\|(u,b,w)\|_{L^{p}(B_{2R})},\quad\,p\in[1,\frac{3}{2}),\\
(R^{1-\frac{9}{2p}}+R^{2-\frac{9}{2p}})\|(u,b,w)\|^{3/2}_{L^{p}(B_{2R})},\quad p\in[\frac{3}{2},\frac{9}{4}).\end{cases}

If we let
R\rightarrow+\infty
in the above estimate, we can immediately find that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla b|^{2}+|\nabla w|^{2}\right)\,dx=0,

which implies that
u
,
b
and
w
are a constant vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. As we know
u,b,w\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
p\in[1,\frac{9}{4})
, we finally conclude that
u\equiv b\equiv w\equiv 0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

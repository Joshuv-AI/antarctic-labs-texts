Regularly oscillating mappings between metric spaces and a theorem of Hardy and Littlewood
Marijan Marković

Abstract
. This paper is motivated by the classical theorem due to Hardy and Littlewood which concerns analytic mappings on the unit disk and relates the growth of the derivative with the Hölder continuity. We obtain a version of this result in a very general setting – for regularly oscillating mappings on a metric space equipped with a weight, which is a continuous and positive function, with values in another metric space. As a consequence, we derive the Hardy and Littlewood theorem for analytic mappings on the unit ball of a normed space.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be the unit disk, and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. The classical Hardy and Littlewood theorem
[
8
]
states that an analytic mapping
f
on
\mathbb{D}

satisfies

(1.1)

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq C_{f}(1-|z|)^{\alpha-1},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

if and only if it is Hölder continuous, i.e.,

(1.2)

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq\ C^{\prime}_{f}|z-w|^{\alpha},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D},

where
C_{f}
and
C^{\prime}_{f}
are constants (may depend only on the mapping
f
). This theorem may be found in the Duren book
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
, where the given proof is based on the
Cauchy integral formula. The estimates of the form
C^{\prime}_{f}\leq AC_{f}
and
C_{f}\leq BC^{\prime}_{f}
, where
A,B>0
are absolute constants, are also known.

This classical result has counterparts for other mapping classes. The theorem has a version for quasi-conformal mappings on uniform domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
obtained by Astala and Gehring

[
1
]
. An analog for quasi-regular mappings on Lip
α
-extension domains is given in the Nolder paper
[
14
]
.

Gehring and Martio
[
6
]
obtained a generalization of one part of the Hardy and Littlewood theorem for (not necessarily analytic) mappings on uniform domains, and somewhat
later on Lip
α
-extension domains
[
7
]
. In their work for (analytic) mappings on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, the condition (
1.1
) is replaced by

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq C_{f}d(z,\partial D)^{\alpha-1},\quad z\in D,

where
d(z,\partial D)
, is the distance function, i.e.,

d(z,B)=\inf_{x\in B}|x-z|,\quad z\in\mathbb{C},\quad B\subseteq\mathbb{C},B\neq\emptyset.

Immediately after that, Lappalainen in his Ph.D. thesis
[
11
]
gave a generalization of the same part of the Hardy and Littlewood theorem for mappings on
Lip
φ
-extension domains, where
\varphi
is a majorant. A function
\varphi\in C[0,\infty)
is called the majorant if it has the following properties:

(1)
\varphi(0)=0
;
\varphi(t)>0
,
t\in(0,\infty)
;

(2)
\varphi
is an increasing function on
(0,\infty)
;

(3)
\varphi^{\prime}
is a decreasing function on
(0,\infty)
.

These properties imply the following four (more important):

(a)
\varphi^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}
,
t\in(0,\infty)
;

(b)
\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}
is decreasing;

(c)
\varphi(ct)\leq c\varphi(t)
,
t\in(0,\infty)
,
c>1
;

(d)
\varphi(t+s)\leq\varphi(t)+\varphi(s)
,
t,s\in(0,\infty)
.

A domain
D
is called a Lip
φ
-extension domain
[
11
]
if there exists a constant
M
such that for every
x,y\in D
there exists a rectifiable curve

\gamma_{x,y}\subseteq D
with ends at
x
and
y
such that

(1.3)

\int_{\gamma_{x,y}}\frac{\varphi(d(z,\partial D))}{d(z,\partial D)}|dz|\leq M\varphi(|x-y|).

For the standard majorant
\varphi(t)=\varphi_{\alpha}(t)=t^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, one says that
D
is a Lip
α
-extension domain (the class of domains introduced previously
by Gehring and Martio
[
7
]
).

The variant of the Hardy and Littlewood theorem obtained by Lappalainen
[
11
, Theorem 7.3]
states that if for an analytic mapping on a Lip
φ
-extension domain

D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
holds

(1.4)

|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq C_{f}\frac{\varphi(d(z,\partial D))}{d(z,\partial D)},\quad z\in D,

then
f
satisfies

(1.5)

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq C^{\prime}_{f}\varphi(|z-w|),\quad z,w\in D,

where
C_{f}
and
C^{\prime}_{f}
are constants.

The first condition displayed above is local, and the second one is of the global nature. In this paper we firstly examine, in the context of metric spaces, when does a similar local
condition imply the corresponding global one. The condition (
1.4
) includes the distance function, which we shall replace by a weight, that is a positive and continuous
function, on a metric space. Then we consider the converse question, i.e., what can locally be said about a mapping which satisfy the condition (
1.5
) adapted for metric
spaces. We shall generalize separately the two parts of the classical theorem. As a consequence of our main results, we are able to derive the Hardy and Littlewood theorem for
analytic mappings on the unit ball of a normed space with values in an another normed space.

2.
Preliminaries

This section contains preliminaries on mapping classes we shall encounter in the rest of this paper, as well as some facts concerning the weighted distance on a metric space.

As we have already said, an everywhere positive and continuous function on a metric space is called the weight function. Below we introduce the class of regularly oscillating
mappings between metric spaces with a weight on the first one.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces. For a mapping
f:X\to Y
we define

D^{\ast}f(x)=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{X}(x,y)},\quad x\in X;

we set
D^{\ast}f(x)=0
for an isolated
x\in X
. The mapping class
D^{\ast}(X,Y)
contains
f:X\to Y
such that
D^{\ast}f(x)
is finite for every
x\in X
.

Assume moreover that
w
be a weight on
X
. A mapping
f\in D^{\ast}(X,Y)
which is bounded on every open ball
B_{X}(x,r)
,
x\in X
,
0<r<w(x)
, is said to be regularly oscillating if there
exists a constant
K=K_{f}
such that

(2.1)

D^{\ast}f(x)\leq\frac{K}{r}{\sup_{y\in B_{X}(x,r)}d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))},\quad x\in X,\quad 0<r<w(x).

The corresponding mapping class is denoted by
\mathrm{RO}_{w}^{K}(X,Y)
.

In case of differentiable mapping between normed spaces
D^{\ast}f(x)
coincides with the operator norm of the differential
Df(x)
. A mapping
f:D\to Y
, where
D\subseteq X
is a domain
in a normed space
X
(over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
), and
Y
is another normed space, is Fréchet differentiable at
x\in D
, if there exists a continuous linear mapping
Df(x):X\to Y
, called Fréchet differential, such that in an open ball
B_{X}(x,r)=\{y\in X:\|x-y\|_{X}<r\}\subseteq D
there holds

f(y)-f(x)=Df(x)(y-x)+\alpha(y-x),\quad y\in B_{X}(x,r),

where
\alpha:B_{X}(0,r)\to Y
satisfies
\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\|\alpha(h)\|_{Y}}{\|h\|_{X}}=0
. We denote by

\|Df(x)\|=\sup_{\|\zeta\|_{X}=1}\|Df(x)\zeta\|_{Y}

the operator norm of
Df(x):X\to Y
. The proof that
D^{\ast}f(x)
is equal to the norm of Frechét differential
Df(x):X\to Y
may be found in
[
12
]
. Therefore,
we have

\|Df(x)\|=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{Y}}{\|x-y\|_{X}}.

If
X
and
Y
are normed spaces over the field
\mathbb{C}
, then a mapping
f:D\to Y
is said to be analytic on a domain
D\subseteq X
if it is Fréchet differentiable everywhere
in
D
. The class of regularly oscillating mapping on a domain in a normed with respect to the distance function of the domain contains the class of bounded analytic mappings on
D
.
This may be easily seen by using the Schwarz lemma. For the sake of completeness we prove this in the lemma below.

Lemma 2.1
.

Every bounded analytic mapping
f:D\to Y
, where
D
is domain a normed space
X
, and
Y
is another normed space, belongs to the class
\mathrm{RO}^{1}_{d}(D,Y)
, where
d
is the distance
function on
D
, i.e.,
d(z)=d(z,\partial D)=\inf_{w\in\partial D}\|z-w\|
,
z\in D
.

Proof.

Let
z\in D
. For
r<d(z,\partial D)
the mapping
g(\zeta)=\frac{1}{M}f(z+r\zeta)
, where
M=\sup_{w\in B(z,r)}\|f(w)\|_{Y}
, is analytic on
B_{X}(0,1)
and bounded by
1
. By the
Schwarz lemma there holds
\|Dg(0)\|\leq 1
. Since
Dg(0)=\frac{r}{M}Df(z)
, we have

\|Df(z)\|\leq\frac{M}{r}=\frac{1}{r}\sup_{w\in B_{X}(z,r)}\|f(w)\|_{Y}.

If we replace above
f
with
f-C
, where
C=f(z)
, we obtain

\|Df(z)\|\leq\frac{1}{r}\sup_{w\in B_{X}(z,r)}\|f(w)-f(z)\|_{Y},\quad z\in D,\quad 0<r<d(z,\partial D),

which proves that
f
belongs to the class
\mathrm{RO}^{1}_{d}(D,Y)
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Based on
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
we give below some examples of classes of regularly oscillating functions on a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the distance
function of the domain as the weight function.

The class
\mathrm{OC}^{2}(D)
, where
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain, contains functions
f\in C^{2}(D)
which satisfy the inequality

|\Delta f(z)|\leq C_{f}\sup_{w\in B(z,r)}|\nabla f(w)|,\quad z\in D,\quad 0<r<d(z,\partial D),

where
C_{f}
is a constant. Every function in this class is regularly oscillating with respect to the distance function of the domain
D
. The class
\mathrm{OC}^{2}(D)
contains many others
well known classes of function, such as harmonic, hyperbolically harmonic, and polyharmonic class of functions on the domain
D
.

On the other hand, for a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
one may relax the definition of regularly oscillating mappings in order to include more classes. It may be asked that the
condition (
2.1
) holds for a locally Lipschitz mapping on
D
and for almost every
z\in D
. Then the class of regularly oscillating mappings with respect to the distance function
on
D
include, for example, the class of convex functions.

We introduce now the mapping classes we need in this work.

For a non-negative function
\varphi
on
[0,\infty)
, which will be called the majorant in this context, we introduce the mapping class
\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
(a Lipschitz type class) in
the following way: A mapping
f:X\to Y
belongs to this class if there exists a constant
C_{f}
such that

d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq C_{f}\varphi(d_{X}(x,y)),\quad x,y\in X.

The infimum of all
C_{f}
is called the norm of
f
in the class
\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
, and denoted by
\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)}
. In the case
\varphi=\varphi_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
, we write
\Lambda^{\alpha}(X,Y)
instead of
\Lambda_{\varphi_{\alpha}}(X,Y)
.

For a weight
w
on the metric space
(X,d_{X})
the class
\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y)
(a Bloch-type class) is consisted of mappings
f\in D^{\ast}(X,Y)
for which there exists a constant
C_{f}
such
that

D^{\ast}f(z)\leq C_{f}w(z),\quad z\in X.

The least possible constant
C_{f}
is defined to be the norm of
f
in this class, and denoted by
\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y)}
.

Notice that a mapping
f:X\to Y
belongs to
\mathrm{B}_{1}(X,Y)
if and only if
D^{\ast}f
is bounded on
X
.

Finally, for the sake of completeness we include here some facts concerning the weighted distance on a metric space. We refer to
[
2
]
for the detailed exposition; see also

[
12
]
for a short one.

A continuous mapping
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
, where
[a,b]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is a segment, is called the curve in a metric space
(X,d_{X})
(we identify the curve
\gamma
with its image

\gamma([a,b])\subseteq X
). We say that the curve
\gamma
connects
\gamma(a)
and
\gamma(b)
, or that
\gamma(a)
and
\gamma(b)
are endpoints of the curve
\gamma
. The length of
the curve
\gamma
is

\ell(\gamma)=\sup\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{X}(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i})),

where the supremum is taken over all partition
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=b
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, of
[a,b]
. A curve is rectifiable if its length is finite. The family of rectifiable
curves with endpoints at
x
and
y
is denoted by
\Gamma({x,y})
. A metric space
X
is rectifiable connected if
\Gamma(x,y)
is not empty for every
x,y\in X
. Obviously, for

\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}
we have
d_{X}(x,y)\leq\ell(\gamma)
. If
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
is a rectifiable curve in a metric space
X
, and
[c,d]\subseteq[a,b]
, then the (rectifiable)
curve
\gamma:[c,d]\to X
is denoted by
\gamma_{[c,d]}
. Clearly, we have
\ell(\gamma)=\ell(\gamma_{[a,c]})+\ell(\gamma_{[c,b]})
,
c\in[a,b]
.

The integral of a continuous function
f
on
X
over a rectifiable curve
\gamma\subseteq X
is denoted by
\int_{\gamma}f
. It is the number which satisfies: For every

\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left|\int_{\gamma}f-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\gamma(s_{i}))\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})\right|<\varepsilon

for every partition of
t_{0},t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
of
[a,b]
with
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|t_{i}-t_{i-1}|<\delta
, and every sequence
s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}
such that
t_{i-1}\leq s_{i}\leq t_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
w
be a weight on a metric space
X
. The weighted distance on the (rectifiable connected) metric space
X
is introduced by

d_{w}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}}\int_{\gamma}w,\quad x,y\in X.

In particulary for
w\equiv 1
we have
\int_{\gamma}1=\ell(\gamma)
and
d_{1}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y}}\ell(\gamma)
,
x,y\in X
. The distance
d_{1}
is in some cases equal
to
d_{X}
. For example, this occurs if
X
is a convex domain in a normed space. However, in general, we have
d_{X}\leq d_{1}
. In case of domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
d_{1}
is known as the
inner distance. In case when the weight function is reciprocal to the distance function of a domain, for the weighted distance we have the known quasi-hyperbolic distance on the
domain introduced by Gehring and Palka.

Remark 2.3
.

The topology of
(X,d_{X})
may be the same as the topology of the metric space
(X,d_{w})
. Let
(X,d_{X})
be a metric space with the following local property

(2.2)

(\exists M>0)(\forall x\in X)(\exists r>0)(\forall y\in B_{X}(x,r))(\exists\gamma\in\Gamma_{x,y})(\ell(\gamma_{x,y})\leq Md(x,y)).

For a weight
w
on
X
we shall prove

(2.3)

w(x)\leq\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{d_{w}(x,y)}{d_{X}(x,y)}\leq\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{d_{w}(x,y)}{d_{X}(x,y)}\leq Mw(x),\quad x\in X,

which is sufficient for the equality of the two topologies.

Indeed, since
w
is continuous, for
\varepsilon>0
there exists an open ball
B_{X}(x,r)
such that

0<w(x)-\varepsilon<w(y)<w(x)+\varepsilon,\quad y\in B_{X}(x,r).

We may replace
r
with a smaller number such that (
2.2
) is satisfied. Now, we have

d_{w}(x,y)\leq\int_{\gamma_{xy}}\omega\leq(w(x)+\varepsilon)\ell(\gamma_{xy})\leq(w(x)+\varepsilon)Md_{X}(x,y).

On the other hand, if
\gamma
is among curves that connect
x
and
y
, then

d_{w}(x,y)=\inf_{\gamma}\int_{\gamma}w\geq(w(x)-\varepsilon)d_{X}(x,y).

Therefore,

w(x)-\varepsilon\leq\frac{d_{w}(x,y)}{d_{X}(x,y)}\leq M(w(x)+\varepsilon),\quad y\in B_{X}(x,r),

which implies

(2.4)

w(x)-\varepsilon\leq\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{d_{w}(x,y)}{d_{X}(x,y)}\leq\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{d_{w}(x,y)}{d_{X}(x,y)}\leq M(w(x)+\varepsilon),\quad x\in X.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
2.3
).

The property (
2.3
) occurs on domains in normed spaces, since one may chose
\gamma_{x,y}(t)=(1-t)x+ty
,
t\in[0,1]
. It is easy to show that
\gamma_{x,y}
is a
rectifiable curve, and
\ell(\gamma_{x,y})=\|x-y\|
.

For similar results on the coincidence of topologies we refer to
[
18
]
.

3.
Main results

This section contains our main results. At the end of the paper, based on our approach, we prove a version of the Hardy and Littlewood theorem for analytic mappings between normed spaces.

The following theorem generalize the one part of the classical Hardy and Littlewood theorem. It contains an integral condition which connects the weight on a metric space with the ordinary
metric through the majorant. This condition is a generalization of (
1.3
) which concerns domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and it simply means that the weighted distance may
be controlled by the ordinary one.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces,
w
a weight on
X
, and
\varphi
a non-negative function on
[0,\infty)
. Assume the following condition:

(3.1)

(\exists M>0)(\forall x,y\in X)(\exists\gamma\in\Gamma(x,y))\int_{\gamma}w\leq M\varphi(d_{X}(x,y)).

Then we have

\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)}\leq M\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y)},\quad f\in\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y).

In particular, if
\varphi
is continuous in
0
and
\varphi(0)=0
, then every
f\in\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y)
has a continuous extension on the minimal completion of the metric space
X
, if

Y
is a complete metric space.

In the proof we use the following lemma which gives the estimate of the length of the image of a rectifiable curve. It may be found with a proof in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(see
[
12
]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric spaces,
\gamma\subseteq X
a rectifiable curve, and
f\in\mathrm{B}_{1}(X,Y)
. Then the curve
f\circ\gamma\subseteq Y
is also rectifiable, and we have

\ell(f\circ\gamma)\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}_{1}(X,Y)}\ell(\gamma).

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y)
satisfies

(3.2)

D^{\ast}f(z)\leq C_{f}w(z),\quad z\in X,

where
C_{f}
is a constant. We shall show that
f
belongs to the class
\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
, and

d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq{C}^{\prime}_{f}\varphi(d_{X}(x,y)),\quad x,y\in X,

where
{C}^{\prime}_{f}=MC_{f}
. This clearly implies the statement in our theorem.

Since of (
3.2
) the quantity

B_{f}=\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}_{w}(X,Y)}=\sup_{z\in X}{w(z)}^{-1}D^{\ast}f(z)

is finite. Note that
D^{\ast}f
is bounded on every compact subset of
X
, which also follows from (
3.2
) (since
w
is continuous). Therefore,
f\in\mathrm{B}_{1}(K,Y)
, where

K\subseteq X
is a compact set.

Let
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
be a rectifiable curve such that
\gamma(a)=x
and
\gamma(b)=y
(it follows from (
3.1
) that the set
\Gamma(x,y)
is not empty). The image curve

f\circ\gamma
connects
f(x)
with
f(y)
, and by Lemma
3.2
this curve is also a rectifiable one. Therefore,
\ell(f\circ\gamma)=\int_{f\circ\gamma}{1}
is
finite (which means that the set
\Gamma(f(x),f(y))
is also non-empty). We now proceed to estimate the last term.

Our first aim is to prove that the mapping
f
is Lipschitz with respect to the weighted distance on the first metric space, and the inner distance on the second metric space, i.e.,

(3.3)

d_{1}(f(x),f(y))\leq B_{f}{d_{w}(x,y)},\quad x,y\in X.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Since
w(z)
is continuous, and since a curve is a compact set, we may found a partition
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{n}=b
of
[a,b]
with

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|t_{i-1}-t_{i}|<\varepsilon
, such that

|w(\gamma(t))-w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))|<B^{-1}_{f}\varepsilon,\quad t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}],\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Then we have

w(\gamma(t))<w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))+B^{-1}_{f}\varepsilon,

so it follows

D^{\ast}f(\gamma(t))\leq B_{f}w(\gamma(t))<B_{f}w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))+\varepsilon.

By Lemma
3.2
we have

\ell(f\circ\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})\leq(B_{f}w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))+\varepsilon)\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]}),

therefore for the corresponding integral sum of
\int_{f\circ\gamma}1
we derive the following estimate

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1\cdot\ell(f\circ\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(B_{f}w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))+\varepsilon)\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{f}w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_{f}w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})+\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})\\
&\leq B_{f}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{w(\gamma(t_{i-1}))}\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{i-1},t_{i}]})+\varepsilon\ell(\gamma).\end{split}

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
above, we obtain

\int_{f\circ\gamma}1\leq B_{f}\int_{\gamma}w\Longrightarrow\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(x,y)}\int_{f\circ\gamma}1\leq B_{f}d_{w}(x,y),

so it follows

\begin{split}d_{1}(f(x),f(y))&=\inf_{\delta\in\Gamma(f(x),f(y))}\int_{\delta}{1}\\
&\leq\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(x,y)}\int_{f\circ\gamma}1\\
&\leq B_{f}d_{w}(x,y),\end{split}

which proves (
3.3
).

We shall now prove that
f
belongs to the class
\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
. The condition (
3.1
) implies
d_{w}\leq M(\varphi\circ d_{X})
. Since
d_{Y}\leq d_{1}
and

B_{f}\leq C_{f}
, for every
x,y\in X
we have

\begin{split}d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))&\leq d_{1}(f(x),f(y))\\
&\leq C_{f}d_{w}(x,y)\\
&\leq MC_{f}\varphi(d_{X}(x,y)),\end{split}

which is the inequality we aimed to prove.
∎

The next two theorems are converse of the preceding one but for regularly oscillating mappings with respect to the weight on the first metric space and some conditions on the majorant. In the
first one theorem we have very strong assumptions on the majorant in order that we obtain the control of the B-norm. In the second one we relax the conditions, but we have certain
assumption on the first metric space along with the weight. However, in the last case we are not able to obtain any control of the norm of a mapping in
\mathrm{B}
-space via the
norm of the same mapping in
\Lambda
-space. In the proof of the last mentioned result we use an idea of Stegbuchner
[
17
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces, and let a function
\varphi\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
be nonnegative on
[0,\infty)
with the following property: There exists a constant
A>0

such that

(3.4)

\frac{\varphi(t)}{t}<A\varphi^{\prime}(t),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

Then we have

\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}_{\varphi^{\prime}\circ w}(X,Y)}\leq AK\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)},\quad f\in\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)\cap\mathrm{RO}^{K}_{w}(X,Y)

for every
K>0
and every weight
w
on the metric space
X
.

Proof.

Assume that
f\in\mathrm{RO}^{K}_{w}(X,Y)
belongs to
\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
, and let us denote
C_{f}=\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)}
. Then we have

d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq C_{f}\varphi(d_{X}(x,y)),\quad x,y\in X.

We shall show that
f\in B_{\varphi^{\prime}\circ w}(X,Y)
and

D^{\ast}f(z)\leq AKC_{f}\varphi^{\prime}(w(z)),\quad z\in X,

from which follows the conclusion of our theorem.

Denote
M=AKC_{f}
, and assume contrary, i.e., that there exists
z\in X
such that

(3.5)

D^{\ast}f(z)>M\varphi^{\prime}(w(z)).

Since
f\in\mathrm{RO}^{K}_{w}(X,Y)
, we have

D^{\ast}f(z)\leq\frac{K}{r}\sup_{x\in B(z,r)}d_{Y}(f(z),f(x)),\quad 0<r<w(z),

which implies

\sup_{x\in B(z,r)}d_{Y}(f(z),f(x))\geq\frac{r}{K}D^{\ast}f(z)>\frac{rM}{K}\varphi^{\prime}(w(z)).

On the other hand, since
f\in\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
and since
\varphi
is increasing, we obtain

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in B(z,r)}d_{Y}(f(z),f(x))\leq C\sup_{x\in B(z,r)}\varphi(d_{X}(z,x))\leq C\varphi(r),\quad 0<r<w(z).\end{split}

Therefore, for every
r\in(0,w(z))
the following inequality holds

\frac{rM}{K}\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))<\sup_{x\in B(z,r)}d_{Y}(f(z),f(x))\leq C\varphi(r).

It follows that we must have

A\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))<\frac{\varphi(r)}{r},\quad r\in(0,w(z)).

However, according to our assumption on
\varphi
, this is not possible. Indeed, by the condition (
3.4
) we have

\frac{\varphi(w(z))}{w(z)}<A\varphi^{\prime}(w(z)).

Now, since of continuity of
\varphi^{\prime}
, we obtain that if
r<w(z)
is sufficiently close to
w(z)
the following inequality holds

\frac{\varphi(r)}{r}<A\varphi^{\prime}(w(z)).

Since we reached a contradiction, we conclude that (
3.5
) cannot hold for any
z\in X
. This proves the inequality in our theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
w
a weight on
X
. Assume that

(3.6)

(\exists M>0)(\forall x,y\in X)(\exists\gamma\in\Gamma(x,y))\int_{\gamma}w^{\alpha-1}\leq Md_{X}(x,y)^{\alpha}.

Then we have

\mathrm{RO}_{w}^{K}(X,Y)\cap\Lambda^{\alpha}(X,Y)=\mathrm{RO}_{w}^{K}(X,Y)\cap\mathrm{B}_{w^{\alpha-1}}(X,Y),

Moreover, a regularly oscillating mapping
f\in\mathrm{RO}_{w}^{K}(X,Y)
satisfies

D^{\ast}f(z)\leq C_{f}w(z)^{\alpha-1},\quad z\in X,

if and only if it satisfies

d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq C^{\prime}_{f}d_{X}(x,y)^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in X,

where
C_{f}
and
C^{\prime}_{f}
are constants. If the first inequality holds, then we can take
C^{\prime}_{f}\leq MC_{f}
. If the second inequality holds, then the first one holds with

C_{f}\leq\frac{2K}{\alpha}C^{\prime}_{f}
.

Proof.

We have just to apply Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
for the majorant
\varphi_{\alpha}
and the constant
A=\frac{2}{\alpha}
.
∎

As a consequence of the above result we have the following statement for analytic mappings between normed spaces. We note that the classical theorem of Hardy and Littlewood remains with the
same constants estimate, if we replace analytic mapping on the unit disk with analytic mapping on the unit ball in a normed space with values in another normed space.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
f
be an analytic mapping on
B_{X}(0,1)
, which is the unit ball in a normed space
X
, with the range in another normed space
Y
. Then we have

\|Df(z)\|\leq C_{f}(1-\|z\|_{X})^{\alpha-1},\quad z\in B_{X}(0,1),

if and only if
f
satisfies the Hölder condition

\|f(z)-f(w)\|_{Y}\leq C^{\prime}_{f}\|z-w\|_{X}^{\alpha},\quad z,w\in B_{X}(0,1),

where
C_{f}
and
C^{\prime}_{f}
are constants. Moreover, there holds the estimates
C^{\prime}_{f}\leq\frac{4}{\alpha}C_{f}
and
C_{f}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}C^{\prime}_{f}
.

Proof.

That the unit ball
B_{X}(0,1)
in the Hilbert space
X
satisfies the condition of the preceding theorem with the constant
M=\frac{4}{\alpha}
, may be proved as in the work of Gehring
and Martio
[
7
]
at the page 205. For
x,y\in B_{X}(0,1)
, as in the cited work, one finds a curve
\gamma\in\Gamma(x,y)
such that

\int_{\gamma}d^{\alpha-1}\leq\frac{\pi}{\alpha 2^{\alpha}}\|x-y\|^{\alpha}.

where
d
is the distance function (from the boundary of
B_{X}(0,1)
). The constant which appears above is
\leq\frac{4}{\alpha}
, so it follows the first inequality between constants. For
the reverse estimate we only have to note that
f
belongs to the class
\mathrm{RO}^{1}_{d}(B_{X}(0,1),Y)
and apply the preceding corollary.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a
\sigma
-compact metric space,
w
a weight on
X
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a compact set
K=K_{\varepsilon}
with

w(z)<\varepsilon,\quad z\in X\backslash K,

and
\varphi\in C[0,\infty)
continuously differentiable and concave on
(0,\infty)
, with
\varphi(0)=0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(t)>0
,
t\in(0,\infty)
. If a regularly oscillating mapping

f:X\to Y
, where
(Y,d_{Y})
is another metric space, belongs to
\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
, then there exists a constant
C
such that

D^{\ast}f(z)\leq C\varphi^{\prime}(w(z)),\quad z\in X,

i.e.,
f\in B_{\varphi^{\prime}\circ w}
.

Proof.

Let us denote

Q(z)=\frac{D^{\ast}f(z)}{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))},\quad z\in X.

We shall firstly show that
Q
is bounded on every compact set
K_{1}\subseteq X
.

Assume contrary, i.e., that a compact set
K_{1}
contains a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
(Q(z_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
diverge to infinity. We can select a convergent
subsequence of
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Denote it in the same way, and let it converge to
z\in K_{1}
. Let
r=\frac{w(z)}{2}
. If an integer
n
is enough big, we have
z_{n}\in B(z,r)
.
Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}w(z_{n})=w(z)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(w(z_{n}))=\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))>\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))}{2}.

Therefore, if
n
is enough big, there holds
\varphi^{\prime}(w(z_{n}))>\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))}{2}
. Let us denote

r_{n}=\min\left\{\frac{w(z_{n})}{2},\frac{r}{2}\right\}=\min\left\{\frac{w(z_{n})}{2},\frac{w(z)}{4}\right\}\geq\frac{m}{4},

where
m=\min_{\zeta\in K}w(\zeta)
. Since
f\in\mathrm{RO}^{K}_{w}(X,Y)
, we have

\begin{split}Q(z_{n})&=\frac{D^{\ast}f(z_{n})}{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z_{n}))}\leq\frac{D^{\ast}f(z_{n})}{\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))}{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))}}\frac{K}{r_{n}}\sup_{x\in B(z_{n},r_{n})}d_{Y}(f(x),f(z_{n}))\\
&\leq\frac{2K}{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sup_{x\in B(z_{n},r_{n})}(d_{Y}(f(x),f(z))+d_{Y}(f(z),f(z_{n})))\\
&\leq\frac{2K}{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z))}\frac{1}{\frac{m}{4}}2\sup_{y\in{B}(z,r)}d_{Y}(f(y),f(z)),\end{split}

which is a contradiction, since the sequence
(Q(z_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is unbounded.

If
X
is a compact metric space, we may finish the proof at this moment. In the sequel we suppose that this is not the case, and let
X=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K_{n}
, where

(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a strictly increasing sequence of compact sets in the sense that
K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\cdots\subset K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}\subset\cdots

Assume that there exists a sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
X
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}Q(z_{n})=\infty
, and for the sake of simplicity, let us denote

Q_{n}=Q(z_{n})=\frac{D^{\ast}f(z_{n})}{\varphi^{\prime}(w(z_{n}))},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
Q(z)
is bounded on every compact subset of
X
, the set
K_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, may contain only a finite members of the sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, so according to
our assumption on the weight
w
, we must have
\lim_{n\to\infty}w(z_{n})=0
.

We will construct inductively a subsequence
(z_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
: Let
n_{1}
be such that
Q_{n_{1}}\geq 4
. Assume that we have found
n_{k}
. Denote

\delta_{k}=\frac{w(z_{n_{k}})}{2}
. Let
\delta_{k+1}^{\ast}\in(0,\delta_{k})
be the unique number such that

\varphi(\delta_{k+1}^{\ast})=\frac{\varphi(\delta_{k})}{2}

(which exists, since
\varphi
is continuous and increasing on
[0,\infty)
). Now, let
n_{k+1}>n_{k}
be such that
0<w(z_{n_{k+1}})<\delta_{k+1}
(since the sequence

(w(z_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge to
0
, it is possible to find a such member), and
Q_{n_{k+1}}\geq 2^{k+2}
(recall that
(Q_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
diverge to
\infty
).

For the sake of simplicity let the subsequence
(z_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be denote by
(z_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. By the construction of the sequence
(z_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
we have

D^{\ast}f(z_{k})\geq 2^{k+1}\varphi^{\prime}(w(z_{k})),\quad\varphi(\delta_{k+1})\leq\frac{\varphi(\delta_{k})}{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
f
belongs to the class
\mathrm{RO}_{w}^{K}(X,Y)
, and since
\delta_{k}<w(z_{k})
, we have

D^{\ast}f(z_{k})\leq\frac{K}{\delta_{k}}\sup_{x\in B(z_{k},\delta_{k})}d_{Y}(f(x),f(z_{k})).

Since
\varphi
is concave on
(0,\infty)
, we have

\varphi^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{\varphi(s)-\varphi(t)}{s-t},\quad s>t>0.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in B(z_{k},\delta_{k})}d_{Y}(f(x),f(z_{k}))&\geq\frac{\delta_{k}}{K}D^{\ast}f(z_{k})\\
&\geq\frac{\delta_{k}}{K}2^{k+1}\varphi^{\prime}(\delta_{k})\\
&\geq\frac{\delta_{k}}{K}2^{k+1}\frac{\varphi(\delta_{k})-\varphi(\delta_{k+1})}{\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1}}\\
&\geq\frac{2^{k+1}}{K}\frac{\delta_{k}}{\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1}}(\varphi(\delta_{k})-\varphi(\delta_{k+1}))\\
&\geq\frac{2^{k+1}}{K}(\varphi(\delta_{k})-\varphi(\delta_{k+1}))\\
&=\frac{2^{k+1}}{K}(\varphi(\delta_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\delta_{k}))\\
&=\frac{2^{k}}{K}\varphi(\delta_{k}).\end{split}

However, if
k
is enough large integer, the just obtained inequality is opposite to the following one.

Since
f\in\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)
(recall that
\varphi
is an increasing function) we have

\begin{split}\sup_{x\in B(z_{k},\delta_{k})}d_{Y}(f(x),f(z_{k}))&\leq\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)}\sup_{x\in B(z_{k},\delta_{k})}\varphi(d_{X}(x,z_{k}))\\
&\leq\|f\|_{\Lambda_{\varphi}(X,Y)}\varphi(\delta_{k}).\end{split}

We have just reached a contradiction. Therefore, the sequence
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for which
(Q(z_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
diverge does not exist.
∎

Higher-order asymptotic expansions and finite difference schemes for the fractional p p -Laplacian
Félix del Teso, María Medina, Pablo Ochoa

Abstract
. We propose a new asymptotic expansion for the fractional p p -Laplacian with precise computations of the errors. Our approximation is shown to hold in the whole range p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) and s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , with errors that do not degenerate as s → 1 − s\to 1^{-} . These are super-quadratic for a wide range of p p (better far from the zero gradient points), and optimal in most cases. One of the main ideas here is the fact that the singular part of the integral representation of the fractional p p -Laplacian behaves like a local p p -Laplacian with a weight correction. As a consequence of this, we also revisit a previous asymptotic expansion for the classical p p -Laplacian, whose error orders were not known. Based on the previous result, we propose monotone finite difference approximations of the fractional p p -Laplacian with explicit weights and we obtain the error estimates. Finally, we introduce explicit finite difference schemes for the associated parabolic problem in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and show that it is stable, monotone and convergent in the context of viscosity solutions. An interesting feature is the fact that the stability condition improves with the regularity of the initial data.

1.
Introduction

Consider
J_{p}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given as
J_{p}(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
for
p>1
. We define the fractional
p
-Laplacian operator as

-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi(x):=\,P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}},

with
s\in(0,1)
. The usual definition of the fractional
p
-Laplacian carries a normalizing constant (depending on
p
,
s
, and
d
) in front of the integral (see for instance
[
11
]
). This constant is irrelevant for our purposes so, for the sake of clarity, we omit it in the definition and the formulation of the results.

The limit operator (up to suitable normalization constant) as
s\to 1^{-}
is the so-called
p
-Laplacian, defined as

\Delta_{p}\phi(x):=\nabla\cdot\left(|\nabla\phi(x)|^{p-2}\nabla\phi(x)\right).

The aim of this paper is to give asymptotic expansions with high order error estimates and discretizations of these operators, apply them to the study of a parabolic problem.

More precisely, we first revisit the mean value property (
2.2
) introduced in
[
12
,
4
]
for the
p
-Laplacian, and prove uniform error estimates that, in the best escenario, are of quadratic order (see Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
). Later we exploit the fact that the singular part of the fractional
p
-Laplacian behaves like a weighted
p
-Laplacian to introduce a new asymptotic expansion for this operator, which has even better orders of convergence than the local case (see Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
). Actually these orders improve with
p
and do not degenerate as
s\to 1^{-}
(the orders of the local case are recovered).

Once asymptotic expansions have been introduced, we use suitable quadrature rules for the resulting nonlinear integral operators to provide two monotone finite difference discretizations. Both of them have the same order of consistency, and one of them produce explicit weights (i.e. they do not rely on computing integrals with respect to measures that are typically not explicit). This is an advantage computationally speaking.

Finally, we exploit the fact that all our approximation operators satisfy the maximum principle (i.e. at maximum points the operator has a sign), to introduce a convergent numerical scheme for the associated nonlocal parabolic problem in the context of viscosity solutions. An interesting feature here is that, for regular enough initial data, our scheme is shown to be stable under a very mild CFL-type stability condition that is consistent with the linear case.

1.1.
Description of the results and their context in the literature

The topic of asymptotic expansion for
p
-Laplacian type operators has been intensively studied in the last decade. The first known result in this direction is the seminal work of Manfredi, Parviainen and Rossi
[
27
]
where the following asymptotic formula for
\Delta_{p}^{\textup{N}}\phi:=|\nabla\phi|^{2-p}\Delta_{p}\phi
(the so-called normalized
p
-Laplacian) is presented for
2<p\leq\infty
:

\mathcal{A}^{p}_{r}\phi(x)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{p-2}{2(p+d)}(\max_{y\in\overline{B_{r}}}\phi(x+y)+\min_{y\in\overline{B_{r}}}\phi(x+y))+\frac{d+2}{p+d}\fint_{B_{r}}\phi(x+y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right).

As it is shown in
[
24
, Exercise 3.7]
, this approximation is of order
O(r)
outside of zero gradient points. In this direction another asymptotic expansion, valid also in the case
1<p<2
is presented in
[
22
]
, again of order
O(r)
.

The results for the
p
-Laplacian are due to Bucur and Squassina
[
4
]
, and del Teso and Lindgren
[
12
]
. They introduce the expansions presented in (
2.2
), and show them to be consistent, but with no orders of convergence. As a counterpart of the results of this paper, here we obtain the order
O(r^{\min\{\gamma,2\}})
for all
\gamma<p-1
away from zero gradient points if
p>1
(see Theorem
2.3
and Remark
2.2
for its optimality when
p\geq 3
), and
O(r^{\min\{p-2,2\}})
for all points if
p\geq 2
(see Theorem
2.1
and Remark
2.2
for its optimality). Our results are also coherent with the case
p=2
where the natural asymptotic expansion is the usual mean value formula, known to be of order
O(r^{2})
.

For the fractional
p
-Laplacian
-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
, the only known asymptotic expansion is the one given by Bucur and Squassina in
[
4
]
:

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}_{r}^{s,p}\phi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+(p-2)s}(|y|^{2}-r^{2})^{s}}.

The authors proved this approximation to be of order
O(r^{2-2s})
. In (
2.1
) we propose a new asymptotic expansion with a number of improved properties with respect to (
1.1
):

\bullet

The measure in (
2.1
) is bounded for every
r>0
, while the one in (
1.1
) is always unbounded.

\bullet

Consistency of (
2.1
) also holds in the range
p\in(1,2)
at nonzero gradient points.

\bullet

The order of convergence of (
2.1
) improves with
p
.

\bullet

The order of convergence of (
2.1
) does not degenerate as
s\to 1^{-}
.

\bullet

While the order of (
1.1
) is always sub-quadratic, the order of (
2.1
) is super-quadratic if
p\geq 3/(2-s)
(in particular
p\geq 3
for all
s\in(0,1)
) at nonzero gradient points, and if
p\geq 4/(2-s)
and
p>2
(in particular
p\geq 4
for all
s\in(0,1)
) at every point. We refer to Remark
2.2
for a discussion on the optimality.

The fractional framework when
p=2
has not explicitly been treated in the literature. Our approach, whose main idea is described in Section
3
is also valid in that case.

One of the main ideas here is the fact that the singular part of the integral representation of the fractional
p
-Laplacian behaves like a local
p
-Laplacian with a weight correction. This is related to ideas presented in
[
19
,
1
]
.

Asymptotic expansions for other nonlinear fractional operators have been treated in the literature, for instance in
[
4
,
10
,
25
]
.

The second part of the paper regards finite difference approximations of the fractional
p
-Laplacian.
In the local case, monotone discretizations of
\Delta_{p}^{\textup{N}}
were introduced for the first time by Oberman in
[
29
]
. Other discretizations have been proposed later on (see e.g.
[
7
,
15
]
). The case
p=\infty
has also been treated (see e.g.
[
28
,
5
,
26
]
). For
\Delta_{p}
the only known finite difference discretization is given by del Teso and Lindgren in
[
12
]
(without results on the order of convergence). The results of this paper also apply to this case, and show the orders of convergence.

In the nonlocal case, the results of this paper are the first finite difference discretizations for the fractional
p
-Laplacian. Other works have proposed discrete versions of the fractional
p
-Laplacian (see e.g.
[
21
,
20
]
), however, no consistency results in the line of Theorem
2.6
are known.

The fractional framework when
p=2
has been extensively studied in the literature. A simplified linear version was successfully developed in dimension
d=1
by Huang and Oberman in
[
17
]
(see
[
8
]
for higher dimensions), obtaining orders
O(h^{4-2s})
. An alternative discretization was proposed by Ciaurr et-al in dimension
d=1
in
[
6
]
, showing convergence of order
O(h^{2-2s})
in dimension
d=1
. This idea was revisited in
[
8
]
obtaining orders
O(h^{2})
unconditionally on
s\in(0,1)
and in any dimension.

In the last part of this paper, we propose an explicit numerical scheme for the parabolic equation

(1.2)

\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u=f\quad\textup{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)

using the discretization previously introduced. In particular we show that, for an initial condition with certain
a
-Hölder regularity, the scheme is convergent under a stability condition of the form

h=O(r),\quad\text{and }\quad\tau\lesssim\left\{\begin{split}r^{2s+(s-a)(p-2)}\quad&\textup{if}\quad a<\frac{sp}{p-1}\\
\frac{r^{a}}{|\log(r)|}\qquad\quad\,\,\,&\textup{if}\quad a\geq\frac{sp}{p-1},\end{split}\right.

where
h
and
\tau
are the mesh parameters of the discretizations in space and time respectively.
This result is coherent with the results for
p=2
and
s\in(0,1)
(see e.g.
[
16
,
9
]
) and also the ones in the local case for
p>2
, recently proposed by del Teso and Lindgren in
[
14
]
. Problem (
1.2
) has been previously studied (cf.
[
1
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
). The literature regarding numerical methods for PDE problems related to the fractional
p
-Laplacian is scarce. To our best knowledge, the only known result is the recent work of Borthagaray, Li and Nochetto
[
3
]
where the Dirichlet problem is treated in the finite element setting. In that work, the discrete version of the problem is expressed in terms of integrals of certain interpolation basis with respect to a singular integral (and in general it cannot be explicitly computed even in dimension
d=1
and needs of a quadrature rule that is used for computational reasons). A main advantage of our approach is that the weights of the discretization are explicit and can be directly used without an extra approximation step.

Notation

For
\rho>0
we define

B_{\rho}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x-y|<\rho\},\quad B_{\rho}:=B_{\rho}(0).

The constant
\omega_{d}
will denote the volume of the ball of radius
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider

C_{b}^{k}(\Omega):=\{\phi\in C^{k}(\Omega):\,|D^{\kappa}\phi|\in L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\mbox{ for all multiindex }\kappa,|\kappa|=0,1,\ldots,k\},

equipped with the norm

\|\phi\|_{C_{b}^{k}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\kappa|=0}^{k}\|D^{\kappa}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})}.

Given functions
f
and
g
, we recall that
f=O(g)
if there is
C>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq C|g(x)|
for all
x
. Also,
f=o_{g}(1)
as
x\to x_{0}
if

\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\dfrac{|f(x)|}{|g(x)|}=0.

We say that
f\asymp g
if there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}g\leq f\leq C_{2}g.

For
p>1
,

J_{p}(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi,\quad p>1.

Finally,
C
will denote a universal constant that may change from line to line. When relevant, we will add subscripts to specify the dependence of certain parameters.

2.
Main results

In this section we introduce the principal contributions of the article.

2.1.
Asymptotic expansions

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p>1
. We propose the following asymptotic expansion of the fractional
p
-Laplacian:

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}[\phi](x):=\frac{(p+d)}{p(1-s)r^{d+sp}}\int_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}.

We also recall the two asymptotic expansions for the
p
-Laplacian (case
s=1
) introduced in
[
12
]
:

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}^{p}_{r,1}[\phi](x):=\frac{\kappa_{p,d}}{r^{p}}\fint_{\partial B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),\quad\mathcal{M}^{p}_{r,2}[\phi](x):=\frac{(p+d)\kappa_{p,d}}{d\,r^{p}}\fint_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\,\mathrm{d}y,

where

\kappa_{p,d}:=2\left(\fint_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right)^{-1}.

We present two results. In the first one we prove local uniform convergence of the previous expansions (both local and non local) at every point in the space, with orders of convergence related to the quantity

(2.3)

\gamma:=\left\{\begin{split}2\quad&\mbox{ if }p=2\mbox{ or }p\geq 4\\
p-2\quad&\mbox{ if }p\in(2,4).\end{split}\right.

The result is the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
s\in(0,1)
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
such that
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
. Then, for
r<R/2
and any
\gamma
satisfying (
2.3
) we have

(2.4)

\Delta_{p}\phi=\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi]+O(r^{\gamma})\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad B_{R/2}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2.

If additionally
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

(2.5)

-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi=\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}[\phi]+O(r^{\gamma+p(1-s)})\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad B_{R/2}(x).

Remark 2.2
.

The results in Theorem
2.1
are optimal. In the range
p\in(2,4)
, take for instance
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\phi(x):=\min\{x^{2},1\}
(so that
\nabla\phi(0)=0
). It is standard to get that, for
s\in(0,1)
, we have

|-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi(0)-\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}[\phi](0)|=C_{s,p}r^{p-2+p(1-s)}\quad\textup{with}\quad C_{s,p}\not=0\quad\textup{if}\quad p>2,

and for
j=1,2
,

|\Delta_{p}\phi(0)-\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi](0)|=C_{p,j}r^{p-2}\quad\textup{with}\quad C_{p,j}\not=0\quad\textup{if}\quad p>2.

For
p\geq 4
, we consider
\varphi(x):=\min\{e^{x},2\}
and compute directly

|\Delta_{p}\varphi(0)-\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\varphi](0)|=C_{p}r^{2}+O(r^{3})\quad\textup{with}\quad C_{p}\not=0,

and a Taylor expansion can be used to see that for
r
small enough

|-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\varphi(0)-\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}[\varphi](0)|=C_{s,p}r^{2+p(1-s)}+O(r^{3+p(1-s)})\quad\textup{with}\quad C_{s,p}\not=0.

According to the second example above, it makes sense to expect a better convergence in the range
p\in(1,4)
far from the points where the grandient vanishes. Actually, if we consider

(2.6)

\gamma\in\left\{\begin{split}\{2\}\quad&\mbox{ if }p=2\mbox{ or }p\geq 3,\\
(1,p-1)\quad&\mbox{ if }p\in(2,3),\\
(0,p-1)\quad&\mbox{ if }p\in(1,2),\end{split}\right.

we can prove the second result, that also covers the singular range
p\in(1,2)
. We present here the result for dimensions
d\geq 2
, since in dimension
d=1
we can get better estimates.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
p>1
and
s\in(0,1)
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
such that
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
and
\nabla\phi\neq 0
in
\overline{B_{R}(x)}
. Consider

(2.7)

R_{0}:=\frac{\inf_{B_{R}(x)}{|\nabla\phi|}}{\sqrt{2}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}}.

Then, for
r<\tilde{R}:=\min\{R_{0},R/2\}
and any
\gamma
satisfying (
2.6
) we have

(2.8)

\Delta_{p}\phi=\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi]+O(r^{\gamma})\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad B_{\tilde{R}/2}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2.

If additionally
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

(2.9)

-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi=\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}[\phi]+O(r^{\gamma+p(1-s)})\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad B_{\tilde{R}/2}(x).

Remark 2.4
.

The second example in Remark
2.2
is valid for every
p\geq 3
. Since in that case
\nabla\varphi(0)\neq 0
, it also proves the optimality of Theorem
2.3
in the case
p\geq 3
. An interesting open question is what the optimal error is in the case
p\in(1,3)
. Actually, in dimension
d=1
, we are able to prove the optimal orders (see Theorem
2.5
).

It is important to note that the order of convergence of our asymptotic expansion improves with
p
, reaching super-quadratic orders and more. We also observe that the orders do not degenerate as
s\to 1^{-}
. We refer to Figure

for a visual reference in the whole range
s\in(0,1]
and

.

Finally, we present the results in dimension
d=1
, where we reach optimal orders of convergence according to Remark
2.2
. We would like to note that, in this case, the first asymptotic expansion of the
p
-Laplacian becomes

(2.10)

\mathcal{M}^{p}_{r,1}[\phi](x)=\frac{J_{p}(\phi(x+r)-\phi(x))+J_{p}(\phi(x-r)-\phi(x))}{r^{p}}.

This is itself a finite difference discretization of the
p
-Laplacian. The consistency result in dimension
d=1
reads as follows:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
d=1
and the same assumptions and notation of Theorem
2.3
hold. Then

(2.11)

\Delta_{p}\phi=\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi]+O(r^{2})\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad B_{R/2}(x)\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2,

and

-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi=\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}[\phi]+O(r^{2+p(1-s)})\quad\text{uniformly in}\quad B_{R/2}(x).

2.2.
Discretizations

The second part of the article deals with two discretizations of
\mathcal{M}_{r}^{s,p}
, which as a consequence will provide discretizations of the fractional
p
-Laplacian. Given
h>0
, consider the uniform grid
\mathcal{G}_{h}:=h\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\{y_{\alpha}^{h}:=h\alpha:\,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
, and the cubes

Q_{\alpha}^{h}:=y_{\alpha}^{h}+\frac{h}{2}[-1,1)^{d}.

For simplicity we will drop the dependence on
h
and write
y_{\alpha}
and
Q_{\alpha}
. Given
r>0
we define, for
i=1,2
, the discrete operators:

(2.12)

-(-\Delta)^{s,h}_{p,i}\phi(x):=\frac{(p+d)}{p(1-s)}\frac{h^{d}}{r^{d+sp}}\sum_{y_{\alpha}\in B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y_{\alpha})-\phi(x))+\sum_{y_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y_{\alpha})-\phi(x))W_{\alpha,i},

where

(2.13)

W_{\alpha,1}:=\int_{Q_{\alpha}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}},\qquad W_{\alpha,2}:=\frac{h^{d}}{|y_{\alpha}|^{d+sp}}.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let the hypotheses of either Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.3
or Theorem
2.5
hold, and let
h>0
. Then, there exists
\mu\in(0,1]
such that, for
r
appearing in (
2.12
) and satisfying
r\asymp h^{\mu}
, we have

(-\Delta)^{s,h}_{p,i}\phi=(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi+o_{h}(1)\quad\mbox{as }h\to 0^{+},\quad i=1,2,

uniformly in
B_{\tilde{R}/2}(x)
.

Actually, we obtain precise error estimates in terms of
r
and
h
in the Theorem above, which in the best cases reach the order
O(h)
. For the sake of simplicity of the presentation, we state them in subsection
4.1
.

The case
p=2
in Theorem
2.6
does not need intermediate steps regarding asymptotic expansions, since the discretization of the local Laplacian is already of order
O(h^{2})
. This gives a discretization of order
O(h^{2+2(1-s)})
for the fractional Laplacian (see
[
17
,
8
]
).

2.3.
Application: explicit finite difference scheme for the fractional Cauchy problem

The third part of the paper is devoted to apply Theorem
2.6
to solve via convergence of finite difference numerical schemes the following parabolic fractional problem

(2.14)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}u(x,t)=f(x),&\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,T)=:Q_{T}\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

where
T>0
and
f,u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. We assume the following conditions on the data

(
\textup{A}_{u_{0},f}
)

u_{0}
and
f
are bounded and globally Hölder continuous with exponent
a\in(0,1]
.

We will write

\Lambda_{u_{0}}(\delta):=L_{u_{0}}\delta^{a}\text{ and }\Lambda_{f}(\delta):=L_{f}\delta^{a},

where
L_{u_{0}}:=\max\left\{l_{u_{0}},\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\}>0
and
L_{f}:=\max\left\{l_{f},\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\}>0
, with
l_{u_{0}}
and
l_{f}
being the Hölder constants of
u_{0}
and
f
, respectively.

We introduce now the weights
\omega_{\alpha}=\omega_{\alpha}(h,r)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
associated to our discretization (
2.12
). More precisely, let

(
\textup{A}_{\omega}
)

\omega_{\alpha}:=\left\{\begin{split}\frac{(p+d)}{p(1-s)}\frac{h^{d}}{r^{d+sp}}\quad&\textup{if}\quad|y_{\alpha}|<|r|\\
W_{\alpha,i}\quad&\textup{if}\quad|y_{\alpha}|\geq|r|,\quad\textup{for either $i=1$ or $i=2$}.\end{split}\right.

with
W_{\alpha,i}
defined in (
2.13
).
Finally, we adopt the notation

(2.15)

\mathcal{L}_{h}\phi(x):=\sum_{\alpha\not=0}J_{p}(\phi(x+y_{\alpha})-\phi(x))\omega_{\alpha}.

which is just
-(-\Delta)_{p,i}^{s,h}
given in (
2.12
) for either
i=1
or
i=2
.

We will also introduce a discretization in time whose mesh parameter is
\tau=T/N
, with
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The time grid will be denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{\tau}:=\{t_{j}:=\tau j\}_{j=0}^{N}
.

We assume the following stability type hypothesis on discrete parameters:

(
\textup{A}_{\textup{CFL}}
)

h=O(r),\quad\text{and }\quad\tau\leq K_{s,p,d}\times\left\{\begin{split}r^{2s+(s-a)(p-2)}\quad&\textup{if}\quad a<\frac{sp}{p-1}\\
\frac{r^{a}}{|\log(r)|}\qquad\quad\,\,\,&\textup{if}\quad a\geq\frac{sp}{p-1}\end{split}\right.

where

(2.16)

K_{s,p,d}:=\frac{1}{(p-1)2^{p}C_{s,p,d}\left(L_{u_{0}}+TL_{f}+3\tilde{K}_{2}+1\right)^{p-2}},

with
C_{s,p,d}
and
\tilde{K}_{2}
coming from Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.5
, respectively.

Remark 2.7
.

Note that for
a=s
the CFL condition reads
\tau\lesssim r^{2s}

3.
Asymptotic expansion and orders in the MVP

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.5
. Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
y\not=0
. Let us define, for
p>1
,

(3.1)

D_{y}[\phi](x):=\frac{J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))+J_{p}(\phi(x-y)-\phi(x))}{|y|^{p}}

where
J_{p}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
J_{p}(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
.
The strategy will be the following (avoiding all constants and approximation errors):

Step 1.
Use the nonlinear finite difference-type operator (
3.1
) to obtain an approximation of the form

|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)\approx|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y.

Step 2.
Integrate this expression with respect to the fractional measure to obtain the local
p
-Laplacian

\int_{B_{r}}D_{y}[\phi](x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}\approx\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\approx\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}.

Step 3.
In a similar way,

\mathcal{M}^{p}_{r,j}[\phi](x)\approx\frac{1}{r^{p}}\fint_{B_{r}}D_{y}[\phi](x)\,\mathrm{d}y\approx\Delta_{p}\phi(x).

Step 4.
Combine this information to conclude

\begin{split}-(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}\phi(x)&\approx\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{r}}D_{y}[\phi](x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\\
&\approx\mathcal{M}^{p}_{r,2}[\phi](x)r^{p(1-s)}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\approx\mathcal{M}^{s,p}_{r}[\phi](x).\end{split}

This leads to the asymptotic expansion and results for the fractional
p
-Laplacian given in Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.5
.

Depending on the range of
p
, these steps may be more or less involved. In the case
p\geq 4
, Step 1 is uniformly obtained and the integration is done straightforward, while in the other cases more refined arguments are required to deal with the points where the gradient vanishes. To prove Steps 1-3 we devote a subsection for every range of
p
and we complete Step 4 at the end of the section. The case
d=1
is treated separately.

We start with two auxiliary results that will allow us to perform Step 2 and Step 3.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
,
d\geq 2
and
\nabla\phi(x)\not=0
. Consider
\phi\in C^{1}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<r<R<1
. Then

(3.2)

P.V.\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{\alpha}dy\leq\gamma_{d,\alpha}|\nabla\phi(x)|^{\alpha},

and, for
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
, we have

P.V.\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{\alpha}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}\leq\gamma_{s,p,d,\alpha}|\nabla\phi(x)|^{\alpha}r^{(1-s)p}.

Proof.

Assume that
\nabla\phi(x)=ce_{1}
with
c\not=0
, and thus,
|\nabla\phi(x)|=c
. Changing to polar coordinates:

\begin{split}I_{1}:=&\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{\alpha}dy=\frac{|c|^{\alpha}}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{|y_{1}|^{\alpha}}{|y|^{\alpha}}dy=\frac{|c|^{\alpha}}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{|\rho\cos(\theta)|^{\alpha}}{\rho^{\alpha}}\rho^{d-1}\pi^{d-2}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
=&\frac{2\pi^{d-2}|c|^{\alpha}}{d\omega_{d}}\int_{0}^{\pi}|\cos(\theta)|^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\end{split}

Changing now
\tau=\cos(\theta)
, i.e.,
\,\mathrm{d}\theta=-\,\mathrm{d}\tau/\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}
, we get

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\frac{2\pi^{d-2}|c|^{\alpha}}{d\omega_{d}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tau^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\gamma_{d,\alpha}|c|^{\alpha}\end{split}

In a similar way,

\begin{split}I_{2}:=&\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{\alpha}\frac{dy}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}=|c|^{\alpha}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{|y_{1}|^{\alpha}}{|y|^{\alpha+d-(1-s)p}}dy\\
=&|c|^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{|\rho\cos(\theta)|^{\alpha}}{\rho^{\alpha+d-(1-s)p}}\rho^{d-1}\pi^{d-2}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\theta\leq\gamma_{s,p,d,\alpha}|c|^{\alpha}r^{(1-s)p}.\end{split}

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
d\geq 1
, and
s\in(0,1)
. Consider
\phi\in C^{2}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
and such that
\nabla\phi(x)\not=0
when
p\in(1,2)
. Then,

(3.3)

a_{s,p,d}\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}}(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}.

with
a_{s,p,d}:=(\frac{1}{p(1-s)}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y))^{-1}.
We also have that

(3.4)

\begin{split}a_{p,d}\fint_{\partial B_{r}}(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)&=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p},\\
\frac{(p+d)a_{p,d}}{d}\fint_{B_{r}}(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\mathrm{d}y&=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p},\end{split}

with
a_{p,d}:=\left(\fint_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\right)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
d\geq 2
for now. If
p>2
and
\nabla\phi(x)=0
the identity trivially holds since both sides in (
3.3
) are zero, so we consider the case
\nabla\phi(x)\neq 0
. Assume without loss of generality that
\nabla\phi(x)=ce_{1}
for some
c>0
. Then, for
0<\varepsilon<r
,

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(p-1)&|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\\
&=c^{p-2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{r}\rho^{-d-sp}(p-1)\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{2}D_{ii}\phi(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\,\mathrm{d}\rho.\end{split}

Furthermore,

(p-1)\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p-2}y_{i}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)=\rho^{d+p-1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\quad\textup{for all}\quad i=2,\ldots,d,

and

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{2}D_{ii}\phi(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)&=D_{11}\phi(x)\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p}\sigma(y)+\sum_{i=2}^{d}D_{ii}\phi(x)\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p-2}y_{i}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\\
&=\left(D_{11}\phi(x)+\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{i=2}^{d}D_{ii}\phi(x)\right)\rho^{d+p-1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\\
&=\frac{\rho^{d+p-1}}{p-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{ii}\phi(x)+(p-2)D_{11}\phi(x)\right)\int_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).\end{split}

Finally, we note that

c^{p-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{ii}\phi(x)+(p-2)D_{11}\phi(x)\right)=\Delta_{p}\phi(x),

to get

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}&=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)\int_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\int_{\varepsilon}^{r}\rho^{-1+p(1-s)}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=a_{d,p,s}^{-1}\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}+o_{\varepsilon}(1).\end{split}

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
3.3
).

Proceeding analogously,

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{r}}(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\mathrm{d}y&=\frac{c^{p-2}}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}\int_{0}^{r}(p-1)\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|y_{1}|^{p-2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}y_{i}^{2}D_{ii}\phi(x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}r^{d}}\Delta_{p}\phi(x)\int_{\partial B_{1}}|y_{1}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{d+p-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\\
&=r^{p}\frac{d}{p+d}a_{p,d}^{-1}\Delta_{p}\phi(x).\end{split}

The above computations also hold in dimension
d=1
in a simpler way.
∎

3.1.
Case
p\geq 4
.

To prove Step 1 we start by doing a Taylor expansion up to order three of
J_{p}
, which in this case can be done avoiding singular terms.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\geq 4
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
. Then, for any
y\in B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)\setminus\{0\}
we have

|D_{y}[\phi](x)-\frac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))})|y|^{2}.

Proof.

By doing a fourth-order Taylor expansion we can write

\begin{split}\phi(x\pm y)-\phi(x)&=\underbrace{\pm\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\pm\frac{1}{3!}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}D^{\alpha}\phi(x)y^{\alpha}}_{\phi_{13}^{\pm}(y)}+O(|y|^{4})\end{split}

and thus

(3.5)

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)&=J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))+J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4})).\end{split}

Furthermore, since
|\phi_{13}^{\pm}(y)|=O(|y|)
,

(3.6)

\begin{split}&J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{\pm}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))=J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{\pm}(y))+J_{p}^{\prime}(O(|y|))O(|y|^{4})=J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{\pm}(y))+O(|y|^{p+2}),\end{split}

Hence, plugging (
3.6
) into (
3.5
) yields

(3.7)

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)&=J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{+}(y))+J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{-}(y))+O(|y|^{p+2}).\end{split}

Let us denote

\phi_{12}^{\pm}(y):=\pm\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y.

Performing a second-order Taylor expansion we can write

\begin{split}J_{p}(\phi_{13}^{\pm}(y))&=J_{p}(\phi_{12}^{\pm}(y))+J_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{12}^{\pm}(y))\bigg(\pm\frac{1}{3!}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}D^{\alpha}\phi(x)y^{\alpha}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}\bigg(O(|y|)\bigg)O(|y|^{6})\\
&=J_{p}(\phi_{12}^{\pm}(y))+J_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{12}^{\pm}(y))\bigg(\pm\frac{1}{3!}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}D^{\alpha}\phi(x)y^{\alpha}\bigg)+O(|y|^{p+3}).\end{split}

Using this in (
3.7
) we obtain

(3.8)

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)=J_{p}(\phi_{12}^{+}(y))+J_{p}(\phi_{12}^{-}(y))+\frac{1}{3!}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}D^{\alpha}\phi(x)y^{\alpha}\bigg[J_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{12}^{+}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{12}^{-}(y))\bigg]+O(|y|^{p+2}).\end{split}

Next, we do a first-order Taylor expansion for
J_{p}^{\prime}
to get

(3.9)

\begin{split}J_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{12}^{+}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{12}^{-}(y))=J_{p}^{\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)-J_{p}^{\prime}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+O(|y|^{p-1})=O(|y|^{p-1}).\end{split}

Moreover, since
p\geq 4
,

(3.10)

\begin{split}J_{p}(\phi_{12}^{+}(y))&+J_{p}(\phi_{12}^{-}(y))=J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+\frac{1}{2}J^{\prime}_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+\frac{1}{8}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)(y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y)^{2}\\
&\quad+J_{p}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+\frac{1}{2}J^{\prime}_{p}(-\nabla\phi\cdot y)y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+\frac{1}{8}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)(y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y)^{2}+O(|y|^{p+2})\\
&=J^{\prime}_{p}(\nabla\phi\cdot y)y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+O(|y|^{p+2}).\end{split}

By (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)&=(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+O(|y|^{p+2}).\end{split}

This ends the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Notice that in the previous proof the fact that
p\geq 4
has been crucial to estimate the reminder terms in the Taylor expansions.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
p\geq 4
and
s\in(0,1)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
for some

and

. Then, for

(3.11)

\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2a_{s,p,d}}\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}+O(r^{2+p(1-s)}),

with

given in Lemma

, and

(3.12)

\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi](x)=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)+O(r^{2}),\quad i\in\{1,2\},

\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}
is given in (

Proof.

Observe that, by (

) and Lemma

,

\begin{split}P.V.&\int_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2}\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}}D_{y}[\phi](x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}\\

3.2.
Case
p\in[3,4)
.

We start by doing a Taylor expansion as in the range
p\geq 4
. However, this argument only works up to

, since the power

is negative and singular terms may appear. This provides a uniform error, which in this case is not quadratic, as it is shown in the next Lemma. In order to obtain a quadratic error we need to be away of zero gradient points, and the argument becomes more involved (see Lemma

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p\in[3,4)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
. Then, for any
y\in B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)\setminus\{0\}
we have

|D_{y}[\phi](x)-\frac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))})|y|^{p-2}.

Proof.

Let us define

(3.13)

\phi_{23}^{\pm}(y):=\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\pm\frac{1}{3!}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}D^{\alpha}\phi(x)y^{\alpha},

and, given
\theta_{j}\in(0,1),

(3.14)

\Theta_{j}^{\pm}(y):=\theta_{j}(\pm\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+(1-\theta_{j})\bigg(\pm\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\phi_{23}^{\pm}(y)+O(|y|^{4})\bigg).

We perform a fourth-order Taylor expansion of
\phi
around
x
and then a second-order expansion for
J_{p}
at
\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y
, to get

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)=&\,J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+J_{p}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\\
&+J_{p}^{\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\cdot(\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))+J_{p}^{\prime}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\cdot(\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))\\
&+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{1}^{+}(y))(\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{2}^{-}(y))(\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2},\end{split}

for some
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,1)
. Recalling that
J_{p}
and
J_{p}^{\prime}
are odd and even functions respectively, we get

(3.15)

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)=&\,(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+O(|y|^{p+2})\\
&+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{1}^{+}(y))(\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{2}^{-}(y))(\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}.\end{split}

Adding and substracting in (
3.15
) the terms

\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\pm\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\cdot(\phi_{23}^{\pm}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2},

we get

(3.16)

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)&=(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+O(|y|^{p+2})\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\bigg[J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{1}^{+}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\bigg](\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)(\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\bigg[J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{2}^{-}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\bigg](\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}+\frac{1}{2}J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)(\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}.\end{split}

By
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
applied to

for

, we get

\begin{split}&\bigg[J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\Theta_{1}^{+}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\bigg](\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))^{2}=O(|y|^{2(p-3)})\cdot O(|y|^{4})=O(|y|^{2p-2}),\end{split}

and similarly for the term with
\Theta_{2}^{-}
. Finally, recalling that

is odd, the remianing terms in (

) are of order

. Then the result is achieved putting all the estimates together.
∎

Notice that the order obtained in this Lemma is
p-2
, which is not quadratic when
p\in[3,4)
. Nevertheless, we can refine the argument in a certain region (see the following Lemma) to obtain quadratic error, which is large enough so that the integral in the complementary part preserves the quadratic order (see Theorem

).

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
p\in[3,4)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
, and let
K_{0}:=\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}
. Then, for any

in the region

|D_{y}[\phi](x)-\frac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C_{b}^{4}(B_{R}(x))})\Phi\left(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}\right)|y|^{2},

where
\Phi(\eta):=1+(p-3)|\eta|^{p-4}
.

Proof.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be smooth functions such that
f(y)\not=0
for
y\not=0
and
|g(y)/f(y)|<\frac{1}{2}
. Then

(3.17)

\begin{split}J_{p}&(f(y)+g(y))=J_{p}(f(y))J_{p}\left(1+\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}\right)\\
&=J_{p}(f(y))\left(J_{p}(1)+J_{p}^{\prime}(1)\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}+\frac{J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{2}\left(\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}\right)^{2}+\frac{J_{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)}{3!}\left(\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}\right)^{3}\right)\\
&=J_{p}(f(y))+(p-1)|f(y)|^{p-2}g(y)+\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}|f(y)|^{p-2}\frac{g(y)^{2}}{f(y)}+\frac{J_{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta)}{3!}|f(y)|^{p-2}\frac{g(y)^{3}}{f(y)^{2}}\end{split}

where
\eta\in B_{\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}}(1)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}}(1)
, that is,
1/2\leq|\eta|\leq 1
. Now we consider

(3.18)

\phi_{1}(y):=\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y\quad\textup{and}\quad\phi_{24}^{\pm}(y):=\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\pm\frac{1}{3!}\sum_{|\alpha|=3}D^{\alpha}\phi(x)y^{\alpha}+O(|y|^{4}).

By a direct application of the Mean Value Theorem on
\phi(x\pm y)-\phi(x)
we get the bound

|\phi_{24}^{\pm}(y)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}|y|^{2},

and hence, if
y
belongs to the region
|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|>K_{0}|y|^{2}
then

\bigg|\dfrac{\phi_{24}^{\pm}(y)}{\phi_{1}(y)}\bigg|<\frac{1}{2}.

We use now the definition of
D_{y}[\phi]
together with (
3.17
) to obtain

\begin{split}|y|^{p}&D_{y}[\phi](x)=J_{p}(\phi_{1}(y)+\phi_{24}^{+}(y))+J_{p}(-\phi_{1}(y)+\phi_{24}^{-}(y))\\
=&\underbrace{J_{p}(\phi_{1}(y))+J_{p}(-\phi_{1}(y))}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{(p-1)|\phi_{1}(y)|^{p-2}(\phi_{24}^{+}(y)+\phi_{24}^{-}(y))}_{I_{2}}\\
&+\underbrace{\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}|\phi_{1}(y)|^{p-2}\left(\frac{\phi_{24}^{+}(y)^{2}}{\phi_{1}(y)}-\frac{\phi_{24}^{-}(y)^{2}}{\phi_{1}(y)}\right)}_{I_{3}}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{3!}|\phi_{1}(y)|^{p-2}\left(J_{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta_{1})\frac{\phi_{24}^{+}(y)^{3}}{\phi_{1}(y)^{2}}+J_{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\eta_{2})\frac{\phi_{24}^{-}(y)^{3}}{\phi_{1}(y)^{2}}\right)}_{I_{4}}.\end{split}

By the antisymmetry of
J_{p}
we have
I_{1}=0
. On the other hand,

\begin{split}|I_{2}-(p-1)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|&\leq C|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}|y|^{4}\leq C(p,\|\phi\|_{C_{b}^{4}(B_{R}(x))})|y|^{p+2}.\end{split}

Now we estimate
I_{3}
. First, we note that

\begin{split}|\phi_{24}^{+}(y)^{2}-\phi_{24}^{-}(y)^{2}|&=|(\phi_{24}^{+}(y)+\phi_{24}^{-}(y))(\phi_{24}^{+}(y)-\phi_{24}^{-}(y))|\leq(|y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|+O(|y|^{4}))(O(|y|^{3})+O(|y|^{4}))\\
&\leq C|y|^{5},\end{split}

where we have used that
|y|\leq 1/2
. Then

\begin{split}|I_{3}|\leq&\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}{2}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-3}|y|^{p-3}|\phi_{24}^{+}(y)^{2}-\phi_{24}^{-}(y)^{2}|\leq C(p,\|\phi\|_{C_{b}^{4}(B_{R}(x))})|y|^{p+2}.\end{split}

Finally,

\begin{split}|I_{4}|\leq&\,C(p-3)|y|^{p-4}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-4}||y|^{2}+|y|^{3}+|y|^{4}|^{3}\leq C(p-3)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-4}|y|^{p+2}.\end{split}

∎

Combing the results in Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
we can obtain quadratic order in the error away from the zero gradient points.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
p\in[3,4)
and
s\in(0,1)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
, and such that
\nabla\phi\not=0
in
\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}
. Let
R_{0}
defined in (
2.7
). Then, for
r<\min\{R_{0},R/2\}
and
x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})
we have that

(3.19)

\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2a_{s,p,d}}\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}+O(r^{2+p(1-s)})

with
a_{s,p,d}
given in Lemma
3.2
and

(3.20)

\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi](x)=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)+O(r^{2}),\quad i\in\{1,2\},

where the error terms are uniform in
x
and
\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}
is given in (
2.2
).

Proof.

Let
K_{0}:=\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}
. Consider the region

\Omega:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|>K_{0}|y|^{2}\right\},

and define

(3.21)

\begin{split}I_{1}:=&\,\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2}\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}D_{y}[\phi](x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-p(1-s)}},\\
I_{2}:=&\,\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2}\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}D_{y}[\phi](x)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-p(1-s)}}.\end{split}

By Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.1
we have that

(3.22)

\begin{split}I_{1}=&\,\frac{1}{2}(p-1)\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\\
&+r^{2}O\left(\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-2}+|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-3}+(p-3)|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-4}\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}\right)\\
=&\,\frac{1}{2}(p-1)\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}+O(r^{2+(1-s)p}).\end{split}

By Lemma
3.5
, we can estimate
I_{2}
as

(3.23)

\begin{split}I_{2}=&\,\frac{1}{2}(p-1)\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}+O\left(r^{p-2}\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}\right).\end{split}

Without loss of generality we can assume
\nabla\phi(x)=|\nabla\phi(x)|e_{1}
. Thus, asking
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
is equivalent to require
|y_{1}|\leq\frac{K_{0}}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}|y|^{2}
, where
y_{1}
denotes the first coordinate of
y
. Using standard polar coordinates, this can be rewritten as
|\cos\theta|<\frac{K}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}r
and therefore

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}&=\pi^{d-2}\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\arccos(\frac{K}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\rho)}^{\pi-\arccos(\frac{K}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\rho)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho^{1-(1-s)p}}\\
&=\pi^{d-2}\int_{0}^{r}\left(\pi-2\arccos\left(\frac{K_{0}}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\rho\right)\right)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho^{1-(1-s)p}}.\end{split}

Choosing
r<R_{0}
it can be seen that

\bigg|\pi-2\arccos\left(\frac{K_{0}}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\rho\right)\bigg|\leq 4\frac{K_{0}}{|\nabla\phi(x)|}\rho,

and hence

\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d-(1-s)p}}=O\left(\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{(1-s)p}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\right)=O(r^{1+(1-s)p}).

Replacing in (
3.23
) we get

I_{2}=\,\frac{1}{2}(p-1)\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}+O(r^{p-1+(1-s)p}).

Using this estimate, (
3.22
) and Lemma
3.2
we obtain (
3.19
).

Proceeding analogously we obtain (
3.20
).
∎

3.3.
Case
p\in(2,3)
.

As in the previous case, we can obtain a uniform error of order
p-2
regardless of the value of the gradient. When the gradient is not zero, we consider two regions, as in the case
p\in(3,4)
, but this time the errors obtained are less than quadratic. This is due to two facts:
J_{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}
is singular around zero; and the complementary region is not small enough.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
p\in(2,3)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
. Then, for any
y\in B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)\setminus\{0\}
we have

|D_{y}[\phi](x)-\frac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))})|y|^{p-2}.

Proof.

We perform a fourth-order Taylor expansion of
\phi
and then a first-order expansion of
J_{p}
at
x
and
\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y
, respectively, to obtain

(3.24)

\begin{split}|y|^{p}&D_{y}[\phi](x)=\,J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))+J_{p}(\phi(x-y)-\phi(x))\\
=&\,J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+J_{p}^{\prime}(\Theta_{1}^{+}(y))(\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))+J_{p}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)+J_{p}^{\prime}(\Theta_{2}^{-}(y))(\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4})),\end{split}

where
\Theta_{j}^{\pm}
and
\phi_{23}^{\pm}
were defined in (
3.14
) and (
3.13
) respectively.. Since
J_{p}
is odd, we cancel out the terms
J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)
and
J_{p}(-\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)
. In (
3.24
) we add and substract the terms:

J_{p}^{\prime}(\pm\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)(\phi_{23}^{\pm}(y)+O(|y|^{4})),

to get

\begin{split}|y|^{p}&D_{y}[\phi](x)=\,\dfrac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+O(|y|^{p+2})\\
&+\bigg[J_{p}^{\prime}(\Theta_{1}^{+}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\bigg](\phi_{23}^{+}(y)+O(|y|^{4}))+\bigg[J_{p}^{\prime}(\Theta_{2}^{-}(y))-J_{p}^{\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)\bigg](\phi_{23}^{-}(y)+O(|y|^{4})).\end{split}

From a slight modification of
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
, we obtain for
p\in(2,3)
that there is a constant
C_{p}>0
such that the terms in brackets are of order
O(|y|^{2(p-1)})
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
p\in(2,3)
and
\beta\in(0,p-2)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
, and let
K_{0}:=\|D^{2}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x))}
. Then, for any
y\in B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)\setminus\{0\}
in the region
|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|>K_{0}|y|^{2}
, we have

|D_{y}[\phi](x)-\frac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C_{b}^{4}(B_{R}(x))})\Phi\left(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}\right)|y|^{1+\beta},

where
\Phi(\eta):=1+|\eta|^{p-3}+|\eta|^{p-3-\beta}
.

Proof.

Given
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
smooth functions such that
f(y)\not=0
for
y\not=0
and
|g(y)/f(y)|<1
, we have

\begin{split}J_{p}&(f(y)+g(y))=J_{p}(f(y))J_{p}(1+\frac{g(y)}{f(y)})\\
&=J_{p}(f(y))\left(J_{p}(1)+J_{p}^{\prime}(1)\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}+\frac{J_{p}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{2}\left(\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}\right)^{2}+O\left([J_{p}^{\prime\prime}]_{C^{\beta}(B_{1}\setminus B_{1/2})}\left(\bigg|\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}\bigg|\right)^{2+\beta}\right)\right).\end{split}

Considering
f:=\phi_{1}
and
g^{\pm}:=\phi_{23}^{\pm}
as in (
3.18
) the proof follows analogously to Lemma
3.6
. Noticing that, since
\beta<p-2
, we have that

p-3>p-3-\beta>-1

we can use Lemma
3.1
to conclude that

\begin{split}|\phi_{1}(y)|^{p-3-\beta}O\left(|y|^{4+2\beta}\right)&=|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-3-\beta}|y|^{p-3-\beta}O(|y|^{4+2\beta})=|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-3-\beta}O(|y|^{p+1+\beta}).\qed\end{split}

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
p\in(2,3)
,

and
\beta\in(0,p-2)

(3.25)

\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2a_{s,p,d}}\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}+O(r^{1+\beta+p(1-s)})

3.4.
Case
p\in(1,2)
.

This case is more involved since we are not allowed to do a general Taylor expansion (we cannot differentiate
J_{p}
even once) as in the previous cases. Nevertheless, we can give a precise estimate far from the singular region.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
,
\beta\in(0,p-1)
, and fix
\eta>0
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
. Then, for any
y\in B_{\frac{R}{2}}(0)\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|\geq\eta
, we have

|D_{y}[\phi](x)-\frac{(p-1)}{|y|^{p}}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))})\Phi\left(x,y,\phi\right)|y|^{\beta}

where

(3.27)

\Phi(x,y,\phi)=|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}+|y|\frac{y^{T}}{|y|}D^{2}\phi(x)\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-2-\beta}+|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}|^{p-2-\beta}.

For the proof we use some ideas from
[
12
]
.

Proof.

Expanding
\phi(x+y)
it easily follows that

J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))=J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+O(|y|^{3})),

and using
[
23
, Lemma 3.4]
,

(3.28)

\begin{split}|J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y&+O(|y|^{3}))-J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y)|\\
&\leq C\left(|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|+|O(|y|^{3})|\right)^{p-2}O(|y|^{3})\\
&\leq C\left(|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}+|y|\frac{y^{T}}{|y|}D^{2}\phi(x)\frac{y}{|y|}|\right)^{p-2}O(|y|^{p+1}).\end{split}

Thus we will focus on estimating the term
J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y)
. Since
|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|\geq\eta
,
we can do a Taylor expansion of
J_{p}
to get, for every
\beta\in(0,p-1)
,

\begin{split}|J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+&\frac{1}{2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y)-J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)-J^{\prime}_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|\\
&\leq C\left(|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|^{p-2-\beta}+|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2-\beta}\right)|y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y|^{1+\beta}\\
&\leq C(p,d,\|\phi\|_{C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x))})\Phi\left(x,y,\phi\right)|y|^{p+\beta},\end{split}

with
\Phi
given in (
3.27
). Proceeding analogously for the term arising from
J_{p}(\phi(x-y)-\phi(x))
we can conclude that

\begin{split}|y|^{p}D_{y}[\phi](x)&=J_{p}^{\prime}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y)y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y+\Phi(x,y,\phi)O(|y|^{\beta})+O(|y|^{3-p}),\end{split}

and the result follows.
∎

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\beta\in(0,p-1)
. Consider a function
\phi\in C^{4}_{b}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<R<1
, and such that
\nabla\phi\not=0
in
\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})}
. Then, for
r<R/2
and
x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})
we have that

(3.29)

\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=\frac{1}{2a_{s,p,d}}\Delta_{p}\phi(x)r^{p(1-s)}+O(r^{\beta+p(1-s)}),

with
a_{s,p,d}
given in Lemma
3.2
, and

(3.30)

\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}[\phi](x)=\Delta_{p}\phi(x)+O(r^{\beta}),\quad i\in\{1,2\},

where the error terms are uniform in
x
and
\mathcal{M}_{r,i}^{p}
is given by (
2.2
).

Proof.

Fix
\eta>0
and consider
\Omega:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|\leq\eta\}
. Define

(3.31)

\begin{split}I_{1}:=&\,\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}},\quad I_{2}:=\,\,P.V.\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}J_{p}(\phi(x+y)-\phi(x))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}.\end{split}

Using (
3.28
), the symmetry of the domain and the oddness of
J_{p}
we can write

\begin{split}I_{1}=&\,\,\underbrace{P.V\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}(J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y+y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y)-J_{p}(\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y))\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}}_{I_{11}}\\
&\,+\underbrace{O\left(\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}+|y|\frac{y^{T}}{|y|}D^{2}\phi(x)\frac{y}{|y|}|\right)^{p-2}\frac{|y|^{p+1}\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\right)}_{I_{12}}.\end{split}

By a straightforward generalization of
[
12
, Lemma A3]
for the singular measure (in the spirit of Lemma
3.1
), and the Dominated Convergence Theorem, it can be proven that
I_{12}=o_{\eta}(1)
. Likewise, by
[
23
, Lemma 3.4]
, it follows that

|I_{11}|\leq C\int_{B_{r}\cap\Omega}\left(|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}+|y|\frac{y^{T}}{|y|}D^{2}\phi(x)\frac{y}{|y|}|\right)^{p-2}\frac{|y|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}=o_{\eta}(1).

By Lemma
3.11
,

\begin{split}I_{2}=&\,\frac{1}{2}(p-1)\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}|\nabla\phi(x)\cdot y|^{p-2}y^{T}D^{2}\phi(x)y\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\\
&+O\left(\int_{B_{r}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)}\Phi(x,y,\phi)\,\frac{|y|^{p+\beta}\,\mathrm{d}y}{|y|^{d+sp}}\right),\end{split}

with
\Phi
given in (
3.27
), and
\beta\in(0,p-1)
. Notice that we can bound the last term by the integral in
B_{r}
and, using once again
[
12

on the last integral, we conclude that

\begin{split}&3\tilde{\Lambda}_{u_{0},f,r}(\tau)+\tau\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x^{h})\times[t^{*}-R,t^{*}+R])}\\

&\qquad\leq r^{a}K_{s,p,d}\frac{(3\tilde{K}_{2}S_{a(p-1)}(r)+3\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\partial_{t}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x^{h})\times[t^{*}-R,t^{*}+R])})}{|\log(r)|}\\
&\qquad\leq r^{a}\left({3\tilde{K}_{2}+1}\right).\end{split}

The above computations hold for all
r
small enough and the constant
\tilde{K}_{2}
comes from Lemma
5.5
. Next, in the expression for
g^{\prime}
we will apply estimates (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) (or (
5.19
)) for
|y_{\alpha}|<1
, and we will bound
|\tilde{U}_{h}(x^{h}+y_{\alpha},t_{j})-\xi|^{p-2}
uniformly in the remaining region. Consequently,

\begin{split}g^{\prime}(\xi)&\geq 1-\tau(p-1)\bigg(\sum_{0<|y_{\alpha}|<r}(\Lambda_{u_{0}}(|y_{\alpha}|)+T\Lambda_{f}(|y_{\alpha}|)+(3\tilde{K}_{2}+1)r^{a})^{p-2}\omega_{\alpha}\\
&\quad+\sum_{r\leq|y_{\alpha}|\leq 1}(\Lambda_{u_{0}}(|y_{\alpha}|)+T\Lambda_{f}(|y_{\alpha}|)+(3\tilde{K}_{2}+1)r^{a})^{p-2}\omega_{\alpha}\\
&\quad+2^{p-2}\left(L_{u_{0}}+TL_{f}+1\right)^{p-2}\sum_{|y_{\alpha}|\geq 1}\omega_{\alpha}\bigg)\\
&\geq 1-\tau(p-1)(L_{u_{0}}+TL_{f}+3\tilde{K}_{2}+1)^{p-2}C_{s,p,d}\left(r^{a(p-2)-sp}+S_{a(p-2)}(r)+2^{p-2}\right)\\
&\geq 1-\frac{1}{2^{p-1}}>0\end{split}

where we have used (
5.2
), the fact that
\tau r^{a(p-2)-sp}\leq K_{s,d,p}
, and we have taken
r
small enough. Hence,
g^{\prime}\geq 0
in
[\varphi(x^{h},t_{j}),\tilde{U}_{h}(x^{h},t_{j})]
. This ends the proof.

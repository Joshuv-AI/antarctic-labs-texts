Structural averaged controllability of linear ensemble systems
Bahman Gharesifard, Xudong Chen

Abstract
In the paper, we introduce and address the problem of structural averaged controllability for linear ensemble systems. We provide examples highlighting the differences between this problem and others. In particular, we show that structural averaged controllability is strictly weaker than structural controllability for single (or ensembles of) linear systems. We establish a set of necessary or sufficient conditions for sparsity patterns to be structurally averaged controllable.



1
Introduction

Consider a linear ensemble control system over the parameterization space
\Sigma:=[0,1]
:

\dot{x}(t,\sigma):=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}x(t,\sigma)=A(\sigma)x(t,\sigma)+B(\sigma)u(t),

(1)

where
\sigma\in\Sigma
,
A\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n\times n})
, and
B\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n\times m})
, with
n
and
m
positive integers,
u(t)\in\real^{m}
is the control input at time
t\geq 0
, and
x(t,\sigma)\in\real^{n}
is the state of the individual system indexed by
\sigma
at time
t
.

We review a few known controllability results associated with system (
1
).
First, each individual system is a linear time-invariant system, and is controllable if and only if the columns of
\{A^{k}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\}^{n-1}_{k=0}
span
n
.

Next, we say that the ensemble system (
1
) is
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-controllable, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, if for any initial profile of the ensemble
x(0,\cdot)\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n})
, any target profile
x^{*}\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n})
, any error tolerance
\epsilon>0
, and any time
T>0
, there exists a control input
u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,T];\real^{m})
such that

\|x(T,\cdot)-x^{*}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}<\epsilon
. When
p=\infty
,
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-controllability is also commonly referred to as uniform controllability.

It is known
[
1
]
that the ensemble system (
1
) is
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-controllable if the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-closure of the vector space spanned by the columns of
\{A^{k}B\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Sigma,\real^{n})
for
p<\infty
and
\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n})
for
p=\infty
. We further refer the reader to
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
and references thereafter for other controllability results for linear ensemble systems.

Finally, we recall the notion of
averaged controllability

[
5
]
.
The ensemble system (
1
) is said to be
averaged controllable
if for any initial state
x_{0}\in\real^{n}
, with
\int_{0}^{1}x(0,\sigma)=x_{0}
, any target state
x^{*}\in\real^{n}
, and any time
T>0
, there is a control input
u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}([0,T];\real^{m})
such that

\int_{0}^{1}x(T,\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma=x^{*}
.
The following result
[
5
, Theorem 1]
presents a necessary and sufficient condition on averaged controllability:

Theorem 1.1
.

The ensemble control system (
1
) is averaged controllable if and only if the vector space spanned by the columns of
\{\int_{0}^{1}A^{j}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\}_{j\geq 0}
is of rank
n
.

In this paper, we will consider sparse matrix pairs
(A,B)
and introduce a novel structural controllability problem, namely, the
structural averaged controllability problem
.

The sparsity pattern of
(A,B)
, i.e., the locations of non-zero entries, can be represented by a directed graph (digraph)
\mathcal{G}=(V,\mathcal{E})
on
(n+m)
nodes, with the node set given by
V=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\}
. The
\alpha
-nodes correspond to the states and the
\beta
-nodes correspond to the control inputs. We denote by
V_{\alpha}:=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
and
V_{\beta}:=\{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\}
.
The edge set
\mathcal{E}
is defined as follows:

1.

There is no incoming edge to any
\beta
-node;

2.

If the
ji
th entry of
A
is not a zero function, then
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})\in\mathcal{E}
;

3.

Similarly, if the
ji
th entry of
B
is not a zero function, then
(\beta_{i},\alpha_{j})\in\mathcal{E}
.

Conversely, to any such digraph
\mathcal{G}
, one can assign a class of matrix pairs
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
of appropriate size (i.e.,
A
is
n\times n
and
B
is
n\times m
) such that their sparsity patterns
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are subgraphs of
\mathcal{G}
. All of these pairs
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
form a vector space, which we denote by
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
. Any pair
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})
in the space
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
is said to be
compliant with

\mathcal{G}
.

In the remainder of the section, we first recall the classical structural controllability problem introduced by Lin
[
6
]
and the structural
ensemble
controllability problem introduced in
[
7
]
. After that, we introduce the problem of structural averaged controllability that will be investigated in this paper.

1.1
Structural controllability

Consider finite-dimensional linear time-invariant systems

\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)+Bu(t).

(2)

Here, pairs
(A,B)
are simply constant matrices (instead of being matrix-valued functions).
Correspondingly, for a given sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
, the vector space
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
is now a subspace of
\real{}^{n\times n}\times\real^{n\times m}
.
A sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
is said to be structural controllable if there exists a pair
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
such that the resulting linear system (
2
) is controllable.
Necessary and sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{G}
to be structurally controllable have been derived and presented in various forms
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. We provide below a graphical condition.

We say that the digraph
\mathcal{G}=(V,\mathcal{E})
is
accessible
to the
\beta
-nodes if for each
\alpha
-node
\alpha_{i}
, there exist a
\beta
-node
\beta_{j}
and a path from
\beta_{j}
to
\alpha_{i}
.
For a given subset
V^{\prime}
of
V
, we let
N_{\rm in}(V^{\prime})
be the set of in-neighbors of
V^{\prime}
,

N_{\rm in}(V^{\prime}):=\{v_{i}\in V\mid\exists v_{j}\in V^{\prime}\mbox{ s.t. }(v_{i},v_{j})\in\mathcal{E}\}.

We gather the following result
[
10
, Theorem 1]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

A sparsity pattern is structurally controllable if and only if (1)
\mathcal{G}
is accessible to
\beta
-nodes, and (2) for any subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V_{\alpha}
,
|N_{\rm in}(V^{\prime})|\geq|V^{\prime}|
.

1.2
Structural ensemble controllability

We now return to the ensemble setting (
1
), where
A
and
B
are matrix-valued functions. For a sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
, the vector space
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
is a subspace of
\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n\times n}\times\real^{n\times m})
.

Following
[
7
]
, we say that a sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
is
structurally ensemble controllable
if there exists a pair
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
such that the ensemble system (
1
) is
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-controllable for some (and, hence, any, see
[
7
]
)
p=1,\ldots,\infty
.

We now recall the main result of
[
7
]
.
To state this result, recall that
\mathcal{G}=(V,\mathcal{E})
is said to admit a Hamiltonian decomposition if it contains a subgraph
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=(V,\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is a disjoint union of cycles.

Theorem 1.3
.

A sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
is structurally ensemble controllable if and only if (1)
\mathcal{G}
is accessible to the
\beta
-nodes, and (2) the subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
induced by
V_{\alpha}
admits a Hamiltonian decomposition.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that condition (2) in Theorem
1.3
is stronger than condition (2) in Theorem
1.2
(details can be found in
[
7
]
). Thus, if
\mathcal{G}
is structurally ensemble controllable, then it is structurally controllable, but not vice versa.

1.3
Structural averaged controllability

We now arrive at the central part of what we will study in this paper, namely, structural averaged controllability.
We still consider the ensemble setting (
1
) so that
A
and
B
are matrix-valued functions, and
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
is a subspace of
\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real^{n\times n}\times\real^{n\times m})
as was introduced earlier.

Definition 1.5
.

A sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
is
structurally averaged controllable
if there exists a pair
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
such that the resulting system (
1
) is averaged controllable

One can relax the continuity of
(A,B)
and require that
A
and
B
are measurable and uniformly bounded (as is assumed in
[
5
]
); our main results to follow still apply in this case.
In the sequel, we compare the notion of structural averaged controllability with the other two; we show that it is the weakest among all the three notions. Moreover, we present a sparsity pattern that is structurally averaged controllable, but not structurally (ensemble) controllable. This is done in Section
2.1
. Then, in Sections
2.2
and
2.3
, respectively, we present a few novel necessary or sufficient conditions for sparsity patterns to be structurally averaged controllable. The Appendix includes a novel problem on variations of Hilbert matrices, which may be of independent interest.

2
Main Results and Proofs

We now present and establish the main results of the paper.

2.1
Comparison between different structural controllability

We start by establishing the following result:

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is structurally (ensemble) controllable, then
\mathcal{G}
is also structurally averaged controllable.

Proof.

We exhibit continuous functions
A:\Sigma\to\real^{n\times n}
and
B:\Sigma\to\real^{n\times m}
such that the resulting ensemble system (
1
) is averaged controllable.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is structurally controllable, we let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\real^{n\times n}\times\real^{n\times m}
be compliant with
\mathcal{G}
such that the single linear control system (
2
) is controllable.
Thus, the columns of
\{A^{\prime k}B^{\prime}\}^{n-1}_{k=0}
span
n
.
Now, let
A(\sigma):=A^{\prime}
and
B(\sigma):=B^{\prime}
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
, i.e.,
A
and
B
are constant functions.
Then,

\int_{0}^{1}A^{k}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma=A^{\prime k}B^{\prime},

for any
k\geq 0
. It then follows that the span of the columns of
\{\int_{0}^{1}A^{k}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\}_{k\geq 0}
is the span of the columns of
\{A^{\prime k}B^{\prime}\}_{k\geq 0}
, which is
n
.
∎

We next show that the converse of Proposition
2.1
is not true, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}
being structurally averaged controllable does not imply that
\mathcal{G}
is structural (ensemble) controllable.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists a sparsity pattern that is structurally averaged controllable, yet not structurally (ensemble) controllable.

Proof.

Consider a sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}=(V,\mathcal{E})
on three
\alpha
-nodes
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{3}
, and one
\beta
-node
\beta_{1}
.
The edge set
\mathcal{E}
is given by

\mathcal{E}=\{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{i}),(\beta_{1},\alpha_{1})\mid i=1,2,3\}
.
Thus, any pair
(A,B)
compliant with
\mathcal{G}
takes the following form:

\displaystyle A=\begin{bmatrix}\star&0&0\\
\star&0&0\\
\star&0&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}\star\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Note that the in-neighbor of
V_{\alpha}
is given by

N_{\rm in}(V_{\alpha})=\{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\}
,
so
|N_{\rm in}(V_{\alpha})|<|V_{\alpha}|
.
Thus,
\mathcal{G}
is not structurally controllable by Theorem
1.2
nor structural ensemble controllable by Remark
1.4
.

We now show that
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable. Choose the following pair
(A,B)
in
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
:

\displaystyle A(\sigma):=\begin{bmatrix}\sigma&0&0\\
\sigma^{2}&0&0\\
\sigma^{3}&0&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B(\sigma):=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},

for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
.
Then, by computation,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\int^{1}_{0}B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma&\int_{0}^{1}A(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma&\int_{0}^{1}A^{2}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1/2&1/3\\
0&1/3&1/4\\
0&1/4&1/5\\
\end{bmatrix},

which is of full rank, rendering the resulting system (
1
) averaged controllable by Theorem
1.1
.
This shows that
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable.
∎

Proposition
2.2
shows that a necessary and sufficient condition for a sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
to be structurally averaged controllable is different from those existing ones about structural (ensemble) controllability (e.g., Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
).
Having a complete characterization of any such condition appears to be hard. We provide below a few necessary
or
sufficient conditions.

2.2
On necessary conditions

In this section, we present a few necessary conditions for a sparsity pattern
\mathcal{G}
to be structurally averaged controllable. We first have the following result:

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable, then
\mathcal{G}
is accessible to
\beta
-nodes.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is not accessible to
\beta
-nodes; then, by relabelling (if necessary) the
\alpha
-nodes, we have that every pair
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
takes the following form (the so-called “Form I” in
[
6
]
):

A=\begin{bmatrix}A_{11}&0\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{bmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
B_{2}\end{bmatrix},

where
A_{11}
is
r\times r
for
r\geq 1
,
A_{22}
is
(n-r)\times(n-r)
, and
B_{2}
is
(n-r)\times m
. It should be clear that for any
k\geq 0
,

\int^{1}_{0}A^{k}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\int^{1}_{0}A^{k}_{22}(\sigma)B_{2}(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,
\{\int^{1}_{0}A^{k}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\}^{\infty}_{k=0}
cannot span
n
.
∎

The above result shows that condition (1) in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
is also necessary for
\mathcal{G}
to be structurally averaged controllable.

Lemma
2.3
can be generalized as follows. For a nonnegative integer
k
, let
U_{\alpha}(k)
be the set of
\alpha
-nodes
\alpha_{i}
such that there does
not
exist a walk from any
\beta
-node to
\alpha_{i}
of length greater than
k
. For the case
k=0
, if
U_{\alpha}(0)
is non-empty, then
\mathcal{G}
is not accessible to the
\beta
-nodes. For a general
k
, we have the following result:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
m
be the number of
\beta
-nodes in
\mathcal{G}
.
If
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable, then there does not exist a nonnegative integer
k
such that
|U_{\alpha}(k)|>mk
.

Proof.

We assume that such
k\geq 0
exists and show that
\mathcal{G}
cannot be structurally averaged controllable.

If
k=0
, then
|U_{\alpha}(0)|>0
, i.e.,
U_{\alpha}(0)
is nonempty, so
\mathcal{G}
is not accessible to
\beta
-nodes. By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathcal{G}
is not structurally averaged controllable. We thus assume for the remainder of the proof that
k
is strictly positive and
\mathcal{G}
is accessible to the
\beta
-nodes.

For any
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
, we show that the resulting ensemble system (
1
) is not averaged controllable.
Consider the
i
th row of
A^{k}
, for any
\alpha_{i}\in U_{\alpha}(k)
. We claim that the row is identically zero. Suppose not, say the
ij
the entry of
A^{k}
is not identically zero; then there exists a walk of length
k
in
\mathcal{G}
from
\alpha_{j}
to
\alpha_{i}
. By assumption,
\mathcal{G}
is accessible to the
\beta
-nodes, there exists a path from a
\beta
-node to
\alpha_{j}
. Concatenating the path with the walk, we obtain a walk from the
\beta
-node to
\alpha_{i}
of length greater than
k
, which is a contradiction.

It then follows that for any
\alpha_{i}\in U_{\alpha}(k)
and for any
\ell\geq k
, the
i
th row of
A^{\ell}B
is identically zero. Thus, the span of the columns of
\{\int_{0}^{1}A^{\ell}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\}^{\infty}_{\ell=k}
has dimension at most
(n-|U_{\alpha}(k)|)
.

However, there are only
mk
column vectors in
\{\int_{0}^{1}A^{\ell}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\}^{k-1}_{\ell=0}
, so the dimension of their span is at most
mk
. Thus, the span of
\{\int_{0}^{1}A^{\ell}(\sigma)B(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma\}^{\infty}_{\ell=0}
has dimension at most
(n-|U_{\alpha}(k)|)+mk<n
. By Theorem
1.1
, the ensemble system (
1
) cannot be averaged controllable.
∎

The importance of having a cycle or a self-loop is even more significant when
\mathcal{G}
does

To further formalize the observation, we have the following result as a corollary to Proposition
2.4
:

Corollary 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable, with a single
\beta
-node, and if
|N_{\rm in}(V_{\alpha})|<|V_{\alpha}|=n
, then
\mathcal{G}
cannot be acyclic.

Proof.

We assume that
G
is acyclic and show that
G
is not structurally averaged controllable.
First, by definition of
U_{\alpha}(k)
, we have the inclusion sequence:

U_{\alpha}(0)\subseteq U_{\alpha}(1)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq U_{\alpha}(n)
.
Since
\mathcal{G}
is acyclic and since there are
n

\alpha
-nodes and one single
\beta
-node, there exists an integer
k\leq n
such that
U_{\alpha}(k)=V_{\alpha}
. We show below that
k
can be chosen to be
(n-1)
.
Suppose not, i.e.,

U_{\alpha}(n-1)\subsetneq U_{\alpha}(n)=V_{\alpha}
;
then, there exists a node
\alpha_{i}
and a walk from the
\beta
-node to
\alpha_{i}
of length
n
. Because
\mathcal{G}
is acyclic, the walk has to be a
path
, i.e., all nodes appearing in the walk are distinct.
More specifically, if we express the path explicitly as
\beta,\alpha_{j_{1}}\ldots\alpha_{j_{n}}
with
\alpha_{j_{n}}=\alpha_{i}
, then the nodes
\alpha_{i_{\ell}}
, for
\ell=1,\ldots,n
, are the
n
distinct nodes in
V_{\alpha}
. But, this implies that
N_{\rm in}(V_{\alpha})
includes at least
n
nodes, namely,
\alpha_{j_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{j_{n-1}}
and
\beta
, which contradicts the assumption that
|N_{\rm in}(V_{\alpha})|<|V_{\alpha}|=n
.
Now, we have that
|U_{\alpha}(n-1)|=n>(n-1)
. Thus, by Proposition
2.4
,
\mathcal{G}
is not structurally averaged controllable, concluding the proof.
∎

(3)

then, the subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
induced by
V^{\prime}_{\alpha}
is not acyclic.

2.3
Toward sufficient conditions

In this section, we investigate sufficient conditions for structural averaged controllability. Throughout, we focus on the case where
m=1
.
Corollary
2.6
demonstrates the need for existence of a cycle in
V_{\alpha}
whenever
|N_{\rm in}(V_{\alpha})|<|V_{\alpha}|
.
A simple case we will focus on is that this cycle is a self-loop. More specifically, we have the following assumption:

Assumption 2.8
.

There is only one single
\beta
-node. There exists a node
\alpha_{1}\in V_{\alpha}
such that
\alpha_{1}
has a self-loop and
(\beta,\alpha_{1})
is an edge of
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover, the subgraph
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
induced by
V_{\alpha}
contains a directed spanning tree
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
with
\alpha_{1}
the root.

The sparsity pattern given in the proof of Proposition
2.2
is a typical example that satisfies Assumption
2.8
.
The next result generalizes the pattern:

Theorem 2.9
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
satisfies Assumption
2.8
and that there is an edge from
\alpha_{1}
to any other
\alpha
-node; then
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable.

We postpone the proof of this result, as it will follow as a corollary of Theorem
2.11
below, which we focus on next. We first gather some useful terminologies.

2.3.1
A partition for
V_{\alpha}

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
be the directed spanning tree of
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
as introduced in Assumption
2.8
.
For any
\alpha_{i}
, there exists a
unique
path from the root
\alpha_{1}
to
\alpha_{i}
within
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
. We let the depth of
\alpha_{i}
be the length of the path. Denote by
V_{\alpha}(k)
, for
k\geq 0
, the subset of
V_{\alpha}
that is composed of all nodes of depth
k
. For
k=0
, the set
V_{\alpha}(0)
is a singleton
\{\alpha_{1}\}
.
A partition of
V_{\alpha}
based on the depths of nodes can be obtained as follows:

V_{\alpha}=\cup_{k=0}^{p}V_{\alpha}(k),

(4)

where
p
is the maximum depth. We will use such a partition later for constructing an averaged controllable
(A,B)
pair.

2.3.2
Variations of Hilbert matrices

We start by recalling some preliminaries on Hilbert matrices, which we utilize in the statement of our main result.
Let
{\mathbb{N}}
denote the set of non-negative integers and let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}
. A Hilbert matrix
H_{n}
is an
n\times n
matrix whose entries are

(H_{n})_{ij}=\frac{1}{i+j-1}.

Hilbert matrices are invertible, and in fact positive definite, and their inverse can explicitly be computed; in particular,

\displaystyle(H^{-1}_{n})_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}

\displaystyle(i+j-1)\binom{n+i-1}{n-j}

\displaystyle\times\binom{n+j-1}{n-i}\binom{i+j-2}{i-1}^{2}.

Let
e_{i}\in\real^{n}
be the unit vector with the
i
th entry equal one. For a
\gamma\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
let
\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\gamma}
be a
non-decreasing
sequence of positive integers such that
i\leq\ell_{i}<n
for all
i
. Let
u^{(\ell_{i})}\in\real^{n}
be defined through its components as

u^{(\ell_{i})}_{j}:=\begin{cases}0&j\leq\ell_{i},\\
\frac{1}{i+j-1}&j>\ell_{i}.\\
\end{cases}

Now, for the given sequence
\{\ell_{i}\}^{\gamma}_{i=1}
, we let

H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{\gamma}):=H_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma}u^{(\ell_{i})}e^{\top}_{i}.

(5)

Note that
H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{\gamma})
is simply obtained by setting entries in column
i
with row larger than
\ell_{i}
in
H_{n}
to zero. For example, for
n=5
and
\gamma=3
we have that

H_{n}(2,2,3)=\begin{bmatrix}1&1/2&1/3&1/4&1/5\\
1/2&1/3&1/4&1/5&1/6\\
0&0&1/5&1/6&1/7\\
0&0&0&1/7&1/8\\
0&0&0&1/8&1/9\end{bmatrix}.

(6)

For a special case, we prove in Corollary
A.4
in the Appendix that
H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{\gamma})
as defined above is invertible. In fact, we conjecture that
H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{\gamma})
is in general invertible; this conjecture appears to be difficult to establish. For the purpose of our work, we make this as an assumption:

Assumption 2.10
.

Suppose that
V_{\alpha}
is partitioned as in (
4
). We assume that
H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{p})
, with
\ell_{j}
given as

\ell_{j}:=1+\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}|V_{\alpha}(k)|,

(7)

is invertible.

Note that in (
7
), we have that
\ell_{j}\geq j
. This holds because the cardinality of
V_{\alpha}(k)
is at least one, for
1\leq k\leq j-1
.

2.3.3
A constructive sufficiency result

With the preliminaries above,
we are now in a position to state the main result of this section:

Theorem 2.11
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
satisfies Assumptions
2.8
and
2.10
; then,
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable.

Proof.

The proof is constructive.
We first describe the process of assigning scalar functions to the entries of the pair
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
. After that, we show that the constructed pair is structurally averaged controllable. We still let
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
be the directed spanning tree of
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
as introduced in Assumption
2.8
.
We specify below the entries of
A
and
B
that correspond to the edge
(\beta,\alpha_{1})
and the edges in
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
. All the other entries of
A
and
B
are set to be identically zero.

The edge
(\beta,\alpha_{1})
corresponds to the first entry
b_{1}
of
B
(note that
B
is a column since
m=1
). We set
b_{1}\equiv 1
.

The edges in
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
correspond to the entires in
A
. We re-label, if necessary, the
\alpha
-nodes so that the sub-indices of nodes in
V_{\alpha}(k)
are larger than those in
V_{\alpha}(k^{\prime})
, whenever
k>k^{\prime}
.
To this end, we perform the following assignments:

•

For the self-loop
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1})
, we let
a_{11}\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real)
be given by
a_{11}:\sigma\mapsto\sigma
;

•

For each edge
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{i})
with
\alpha_{i}\in V_{\alpha}(1)
,
we let
a_{i1}\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real)
be given by
a_{i1}:\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{i}
;

•

For each edge
(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})
with
\alpha_{i}\in V_{\alpha}(k)
and
\alpha_{j}\in V_{\alpha}(k+1)
, where
1\leq k\leq p
, we let
a_{ij}\in\mathrm{C}^{0}(\Sigma,\real)
be given by
a_{ij}:\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{j-i+1}
.

An example of such assignment is given by Fig.

We then define a matrix-valued function as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=[B\,\,AB\,\cdots\,A^{n-1}B].

We now claim that for the
(A,B)\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{G})
constructed above and for the sequence
\{\ell_{j}\}_{j=1}^{p}

\ell_{j}

, the following equality is satisfied:

\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{C}(\sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma=H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{p}).

(8)

Note that, by Assumption
2.10
, this will prove that
\mathcal{G}
is structurally averaged controllable. We now prove this claim.

We show that the for any
j=1,\ldots,p
, the last
(n-\ell_{j})
entries of the
j

The first column of

Next, let

For the other entries of

3
Conclusions

We introduce and address a novel problem of structural averaged controllability for linear ensemble systems. Some necessary or sufficient conditions for a sparsity pattern to be structurally averaged controllable are provided. Although the parameterization space is chosen to be the closed interval
\Sigma=[0,1]
, we believe that the results established hold for general continuum spaces. Future work include characterizing necessary
and
sufficient conditions for sparsity patterns to be structurally averaged controllable, studying the minimal controllability problem
[
10
]
, and extending the results to, e.g., bilinear systems
[
11
]
.

Appendix A
Variations of Hilbert matrices with zero entries

We have the following conjecture:

Conjecture A.1
.

The matrix
H_{n}(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{\alpha})
is invertible.

We now prove a preliminary version of this conjecture for
\alpha=1
. For this purpose, let
e_{1}\in\real^{n}
be the unit vector with first entry one and for
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1}
, let
u^{(\ell)}\in\real^{n}
be defined through its
j
th component:

u^{(\ell)}_{j}:=\begin{cases}0&j\leq\ell,\\
\frac{1}{j}&j>\ell.\\
\end{cases}

Theorem A.2
.

The matrix
H_{n}(\ell):=H_{n}-u^{(\ell)}e^{\top}_{1}
is invertible for any
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

By the matrix determinant lemma, we have

\displaystyle\det(H_{n}(\ell))=

\displaystyle(1-e^{\top}_{1}H_{n}^{-1}u^{(\ell)})\det(H_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(H_{n}^{-1})_{1k}u^{(\ell)}_{k})\det(H_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{(k+1)}k\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}u^{(\ell)}_{k})\det(H_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}(-1)^{(k+1)}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}\right)\det(H_{n}).

Clearly, the result follows if we prove that for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}
and
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
,

\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}(-1)^{(k+1)}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}\neq 1.

(9)

To this end, using a backward induction on
\ell
, one can show that

\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}(-1)^{(k+1)}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}Z(\ell,n),

where

Z(\ell,n):=(-1)^{\ell}(\ell+1)^{2}\binom{n}{n-\ell-1}\binom{n+\ell}{n-1}.

(10)

Indeed. The statement can be seen to hold for
\ell=n-1
. Suppose now by the way of induction that the statement holds for some
\ell
, and let us prove that it also holds for
\ell-1
. Note that

\displaystyle n^{2}\sum_{k=\ell}^{n}(-1)^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}\sum_{k=\ell+1}^{n}(-1)^{(k+1)}\binom{n}{n-k}\binom{n+k-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\ell+1}n^{2}\binom{n}{n-\ell}\binom{n+\ell-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell}(\ell+1)^{2}\binom{n}{n-\ell-1}\binom{n+\ell}{n-1}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\ell+1}n^{2}\binom{n}{n-\ell}\binom{n+\ell-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell-1}\left(-(\ell+1)^{2}\times\frac{n-\ell}{\ell+1}\times\frac{n+\ell}{\ell+1}+n^{2}\right)\binom{n}{n-\ell}\binom{n+\ell-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell-1}\ell^{2}\binom{n}{n-\ell}\binom{n+\ell-1}{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z(\ell-1,n),

where we have used the induction assumption to arrive at the second equality. This finishes the induction argument.

Using this result, in order to prove (
9
), it is enough to show that
Z(\ell,n)\neq n^{2}
, for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
(note that
Z(0,n)=n^{2}
). The next result is a stepping stone.

Sublemma A.3
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}
, we have that

\operatorname{argmin}_{\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}}|Z(\ell,n)|=1,

where
Z(\cdot,n)
is given by (
10
).

Proof.

We first show that
|Z(\cdot,n)|
is increasing on
\{1,\ldots,\ell^{*}\}
and decreasing on
\{\ell^{*},\ldots,n-1\}
, where
\ell^{*}=~\lfloor\frac{n}{\sqrt{2}}\rfloor.

Note that for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, we have that

Z(\ell,n)=-(\frac{n^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-1)Z(\ell-1,n),

and, hence

Z(\ell,n)-Z(\ell-1,n)=-\frac{n^{2}}{\ell^{2}}Z(\ell-1,n).

We also have

|Z(\ell,n)|-|Z(\ell-1,n)|=(\frac{n^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-2)|Z(\ell-1,n)|.

Hence,
|Z(\ell,n)|>|Z(\ell-1,n)|
if
\ell\leq\ell^{*}
and
|Z(\ell,n)|<|Z(\ell-1,n)|
, otherwise. As a result, the minimum value of
|Z(\ell,n)|
over
\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
occurs either at
\ell=1
or at
\ell=n-1
. We have that

|Z(1,n)|=n^{4}-n^{2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|Z(n-1,n)|=n^{2}\binom{2n-1}{n-1}.

Noting that
|Z(1,n)|<|Z(n-1,n)|
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 3}
yields the result.
∎

By Sublemma
A.3
,
\min_{\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}}|Z(\ell,n)|=n^{4}-n^{2}
, which cannot be equal to
n^{2}
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 2}
, proving that (
9
) holds, yielding the claim.
∎

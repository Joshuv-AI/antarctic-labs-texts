Interaction-induced Liouvillian skin effect in a fermionic chain with a two-body loss
Shu Hamanaka, Kazuki Yamamoto, Tsuneya Yoshida

Abstract
Despite recent intensive research on topological aspects of open quantum systems, effects of strong interactions have not been sufficiently explored. In this paper, we demonstrate that complex-valued interactions induce the Liouvillian skin effect by analyzing a one-dimensional correlated model with two-body loss. We show that, in the presence of complex-valued interactions, eigenmodes and eigenvalues of the Liouvillian strongly depend on boundary conditions. Specifically, we find that complex-valued interactions induce localization of eigenmodes of the Liouvillian around the right edge under open boundary conditions. To characterize the Liouvllian skin effect, we define the topological invariant by using the Liouvillian superoperator. Then, we numerically confirm that the topological invariant captures the Liouvillian skin effect. Furthermore, the presence of the localization of eigenmodes results in the unique dynamics observed only under open boundary conditions: particle accumulation at the right edge in transient dynamics. Our result paves the way to realize topological phenomena in open quantum systems induced by strong interactions.

III.1
Topological invariant

In this subsection, we first present the topological number defined by the Liouvillian superoperator, and then discuss the relation between the topological number and the Liouvillian skin effect.

First, we introduce the following topological invariant by using the Liouvillian superoperator

\displaystyle\nu(\Lambda_{\rm{ref}})=\oint_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{d\theta}\log\det\bigl[\mathcal{L}(\theta)-\Lambda_{\rm{ref}}\bigr],

\displaystyle i\mathcal{L}(\theta)={H}_{\rm{eff}}(\theta)\otimes{I}-I\otimes{{H}}_{\rm{eff}}^{*}+i\sum\limits_{j}{L}_{j}(\theta)\otimes{{L}}_{j}^{*},

;
Kawabata et al. 2022
.

First, we define the following right-state particle density of the
n
-th eigenmode of the Liouvillian superoperator
\mathcal{L}
to quantify the degree of localization of the eigenmode of the Liouvillian in many-body systems:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}^{(n)}=\langle\langle J|c^{\dagger}_{l}c_{l}\otimes{I}|\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle J|I\otimes{c}^{\dagger}_{l}{c}_{l}|\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle\rangle

(27)

with
l
denoting the set of
j
,
\alpha
and
\sigma
, i.e.
l=j\alpha\sigma
.
Here,
|{J}\rangle\rangle
is the identity operator defined by
|{J}\rangle\rangle=\sum_{j}|j\rangle\otimes|{j}\rangle
, and

|\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle\rangle
is the
n
-th right eigenmode of the Liouvillian
\mathcal{L}
that satisfies the eigenvalue equation given in Eq. (
14
).
We note that the right-state particle density is not identical to the ordinary particle density, which is observable and takes real values.
Specifically, the right-state particle density is complex-valued, which is introduced to measure the degree of localization of eigenmodes in many-body systems.

Next, we show that the right-state particle density defined in Eq. (
27
) reduces to the diagonal element of the right eigenmode in the single-particle system.
We note that the right-state particle density of the
n
-th eigenmode given in Eq. (
27
) is expressed as

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}^{(n)}=\mathrm{Tr}[c^{\dagger}_{l}c_{l}{\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}}],

(28)

where we have used the following relation

\displaystyle\langle{\langle J|}A\otimes{I}|\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{jkl}\langle{j}|\otimes\langle j|~\Bigl(A|k\rangle\otimes|{l}\rangle\Bigr)\rho_{\mathrm{R},{kl}}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{jkl}\delta_{jl}~\langle j|A|k\rangle\rho_{\mathrm{R},{kl}}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}[A\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}].

(29)

Now, we show that the definition given in Eq. (
27
) reduces to
the diagonal element of the right eigenmode of the Liouvillian in the single-particle system.
We take the single-particle basis
|S\rangle
, which is generated by acting the creation operator to the vacuum
|\mathrm{vac}\rangle
as
|S\rangle=c^{\dagger}_{S}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle
with
S
denoting the set of
j
,
\alpha
and
\sigma
, i.e.
S=j\alpha\sigma
.
Then the
n
-th right eigenstate of the density matrix
\rho^{(n)}_{\mathrm{R}}
is expanded by using the single-particle state
|S\rangle
as

\displaystyle\rho^{(n)}_{\mathrm{R}}=\sum\limits_{ST}\rho_{\mathrm{R},ST}^{(n)}|S\rangle\langle T|.

(30)

Here,
\rho^{(n)}_{\mathrm{R},ST}\in\mathbb{C}
is expansion coefficient.
By substituting Eq. (
30
) into Eq. (
28
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}^{(n)}=\rho_{\mathrm{R},SS}^{(n)}.

(31)

Thus the right-state particle density defined in Eq. (
28
) is a generalization of the diagonal element of the right eigenmode, which measures the degree of localization of the eigenmode of the Liouvillian in the single-particle system.

Finally, we show that when the right eigenmode
\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}
is written by the right eigenstate of the effective Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{eff}}
as
|\varphi_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle

the right-state particle density reduces to the particle density defined by

Kawabata et al. 2022

Faugno and Ozawa 2022

We take the right and left eigenstate of the effective Hamiltonian as
|\varphi_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle
and
\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{L}}^{(n)}|

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{eff}}|\varphi_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle=E_{n}|\varphi_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle

(32)

\displaystyle\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{L}}^{(n)}|H_{\mathrm{eff}}=E_{n}\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{L}}^{(n)}|,

(33)

When we take the right eigenmode
\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}=|\varphi_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}\rangle\langle\varphi_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}|,

(34)

\displaystyle\Delta_{l}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}[{\rho_{\mathrm{R}}^{(n)}}c^{\dagger}_{l}c_{l}]

In the following, we demonstrate the right-state particle density

From Eqs. (

right-state particle density is the proper definition to measure the degree of localization of eigenmodes of the Liouvillian in many-body systems.

In this section, we demonstrate that interactions can induce the Liouvillian skin effect by analyzing the Falicov-Kimball model introduced in Sec.

First, in the noninteracting case (
U-i\gamma=0

IV.1
Noninteracting case

First, we see that the Liouvillian skin effect is not observed in the noninteracting case
(U=\gamma=0)

In this case, the Liouvillian given in Eq. (
11

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{free}}=-i\left({H}\otimes{I}-I\otimes{{H}}^{T}\right).

(36)

Because
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{free}}
is skew-Hermitian, i.e.
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{\mathrm{free}}=-\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{free}}

In other words, all eigenvalues of the Liouvillian lie on the imaginary axis regardless of boundary conditions.
As a result,
the point-gap topology

Correspondingly, the eigenvalues and the eigenmodes of the Liouvillian are not sensitive to the boundary conditions
(for more details, see Appendix

Therefore, the Liouvillian skin effect is absent for the noninteracting system.

V
CONCLUSIONS

In this paper, by introducing two-body loss into the one-dimensional correlated system, we have demonstrated that complex-valued interactions induce the Liouvillian skin effect.

Specifically, by introducing the winding number constructed by the Liouvillian superoperator, we have elucidated that interactions make the point-gap topology nontrivial.
Moreover, we have seen that eigenvalues and eigenmodes of the Liouvillian exhibit extreme sensitivity to boundary conditions.
As a result, we have observed the particle accumulation around the right edge in transient dynamics only under OBC which is attributed to the emergence of the skin mode.

As two-body losses have already been introduced in ytterbium atoms by using photoassociation techniques
Tomita et al. 2017
;
Honda et al. 2023
,
our results can be tested in ultracold atoms.
The method to realize the Falikov-Kimball model is provided in Ref.
Semmler et al. 2010
, by introducing two species of atoms like
40
K and
6
Li, where mobile and immobile atoms are coupled via an on-site interaction.
We expect that the interaction-induced Liouvillian skin effect in our model can be observed in ultracold atoms.

Recently, classifications of the Liouvillian superoperator have been actively conducted
Lieu et al. 2020
;
Kawabata et al. 2023b
.
When the Hamiltonian preserves the inversion symmetry,
the winding number defined in Eq. (
22
), which characterizes the nontrivial topological phase of the Liouvillian, can be trivial.
Last but not least, it deserves to study the detailed relations
between the symmetry and the topological number in a different Liouvillian,
but we leave it for future work.

Appendix A
Symmetry constraint on the winding number

In this appendix, we discuss the relation between the symmetry of the
Liouvillian and the winding number
\nu(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}})
. As we will see below, breaking the inversion symmetry of the Hamiltonian is essential for the existence of the nonzero topological number.

The winding number
\nu(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}})
given in Eq. (
22
) becomes trivial when the Liouvillian
superoperator satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\mathcal{L}(-\theta)\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{L}(\theta).

(46)

Here,
\mathcal{U}
is the unitary operator
(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\mathcal{U}=1)
.
We note that Eq. (
46
) leads to
\nu(\Lambda_{\rm{ref}})=-\nu(\Lambda_{\rm{ref}})
.
This relation means that the point-gap topology of the Liouvillian is trivial
\nu(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}})=0
.

In the case of particle loss, this triviality
[\nu(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ref}})=0]
originates from the symmetry of the Hamiltonian. Since the eigenvalue of the Liouvillian
is determined only by the effective Hamiltonian

H_{\rm{eff}}

Torres 2014
, the winding number given in Eq. (
22
) reduces to

\displaystyle\nu(\Lambda_{\rm{ref}})=\oint_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi i}\frac{d}{d\theta}\log\det\bigl[\mathcal{L}_{0}(\theta)-\Lambda_{\rm{ref}}\bigr].

(47)

If the effective Hamiltonian
{H}_{\rm{eff}}
satisfies the following relation

\displaystyle{U}{H}_{\rm{eff}}(-\theta){U}^{\dagger}={H}_{\rm{eff}}(\theta),

(48)

we can construct the unitary operator
\mathcal{U}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}={U}\otimes{V},

(49)

where
{U}{U}^{\dagger}={U}^{\dagger}{U}=1
and we have defined
{V}={U}^{*}
.
Due to the relation
\mathcal{U}\mathcal{L}_{0}(-\theta)\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\theta)
, we find that
the winding number Eq. (
47
) becomes zero.

Now, we discuss whether the Liouvillian given in Eq. (
11
) satisfies the condition given in Eq. (
46
).
The effective Hamiltonian
under twisted boundary conditions in real space is
H_{\mathrm{eff}}(\theta)=H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{bulk}}+H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(\theta)
where

H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{bulk}}
is written as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{bulk}}=H_{1}+H_{2}+H_{3}+H_{4}.

(50)

Here, we have defined

\displaystyle H_{1}=-2it_{h}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{L}({c}^{\dagger}_{ja\uparrow}{c}_{jb\uparrow}-\mathrm{h.c.}),

\displaystyle H_{2}=-0.25t_{h}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{L-1}({c}^{\dagger}_{j+1b\uparrow}{c}_{ja\uparrow}-{c}^{\dagger}_{j+1a\uparrow}{c}_{jb\uparrow}+\mathrm{h.c.}),

\displaystyle H_{3}=t_{h}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{L-1}({c}^{\dagger}_{j+1a\uparrow}{c}_{ja\uparrow}-{c}^{\dagger}_{j+1b\uparrow}{c}_{jb\uparrow}+\mathrm{h.c.}),

\displaystyle H_{4}=(U-i\gamma)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{L}{n}_{jb\uparrow}{n}_{jb\downarrow},

(51)

and

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(\theta)=

\displaystyle-0.25t_{h}(e^{i\theta}{c}^{\dagger}_{1b\uparrow}{c}_{La\uparrow}-e^{i\theta}{c}^{\dagger}_{1a\uparrow}{c}_{Lb\uparrow}+\mathrm{h.c.})

\displaystyle+t_{h}(e^{i\theta}{c}^{\dagger}_{1a\uparrow}{c}_{La\uparrow}-e^{i\theta}{c}^{\dagger}_{1b\uparrow}{c}_{Lb\uparrow}+\mathrm{h.c.}).

(52)

The first term
H_{1}
of the bulk Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{bulk}}
[i.e., the first term of the Bloch Hamiltonian in Eq. (
3a
) denoted by
2t_{h}
] violates the condition Eq. (
46
). In the absence of the first term, the effective Hamiltonian preserves the inversion symmetry
defined by

\displaystyle{P}{H}_{\rm{eff}}(-\theta){P}^{\dagger}={H}_{\rm{eff}}(\theta),

(53)

where the inversion operator
P
acts on the annihilation operator as
{P}{c}_{ja\sigma}{P}^{\dagger}={c}_{L-(j-1)a\sigma},{P}{c}_{jb\sigma}{P}^{\dagger}=-{c}_{L-(j-1)b\sigma}

and satisfies
{P}^{\dagger}{P}={P}{P}^{\dagger}={\mathbf{1}}
.
Then, we see that the inversion symmetry given in Eq. (
53
) is nothing but the condition of the triviality of the winding number given in
Eq. (
48
). Therefore, the Liouvillian superoperator
\mathcal{L}(\theta)
given in Eq. (
23
) satisfies Eq. (
46
)
in the absence of
H_{1}
.
The presence of
H_{1}
breaks the condition Eq. (
53
), which leads to the violation of the condition Eq. (
46
).
Hence, the nonzero winding number originates from the property of the Liouvillian.
In particular, the Hamiltonian breaks the inversion symmetry.

Arithmetic Fujita approximation over adelic curves
Chunhui Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we will prove an analogue of Fujita’s approximation under the framework of Arakelov theory over adelic curves, which gives a positive response to a conjecture of Huayi Chen and Atsushi Moriwaki.



\alttitle

L’approximation arithmétique de Fujita sur courbes adéliques

{altabstract}

Dans cet article, on démontrera une analogie de l’approximation de Fujita sous le cadre de la théorie d’Arakelov sur courbes adéliques, qui donne une réponse positive d’une conjecture de Huayi Chen et Atsushi Moriwaki.

1
Introduction

Fujita approximation is an approximative version of Zariski decomposition of pseudo-effective divisors, and see
[
28
]
for more details. More precisely, let
X
be a projective variety over a field
k
, and
L
be a line bundle on
X
. The volume of
L
is defined as

\vol(L)=\limsup_{D\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\dim_{k}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes D})}{D^{\dim(X)}/\dim(X)!}.

We say that
L
is a
big
line bundle if
\vol(L)>0
. Fujita’s approximation theorem states that, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist a
p\in\mathbb{N}
and a birational morphism
v:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
, such that
v^{*}(L^{\otimes p})
can be decomposed as the tensor product of an ample line bundle
A
and an effective line bundle
M
, which satisfy

p^{-\dim(X)}\vol(A)\geqslant\vol(L)-\epsilon.

Intuitively, this result asserts that a big line bundle can be arbitrarily closely approximated by ample line bundles along an effective direction.

This approximated decomposition theorem is proved by T. Fujita
[
12
]
for the case of characteristic
0
, and by S. Takagi
[
24
]
for an arbitrary characteristic. We refer the readers to
[
17
, §11.4]
for a survey on this topic.

1.1
Arithmetic analogue over number fields

It is quite nature to ask whether there is an analogue of Fujita’s approximation theorem under the framework of Arakelov geometry, since we can define the arithmetic positivity and arithmetic volume of an adelic line bundle over arithmetic varieties.

1.1.1

The arithmetic analogue of volume function and arithmetic bigness under the framework of Arakelov geometry are introduced in
[
19
,
20
]
by A. Moriwaki. Soon after these, voluminous works appeared on this topic, such as
[
21
,
6
,
27
,
14
,
22
,
15
]
.

Let
K
be a number field,
\mathscr{X}
be an arithmetic variety over
\spec\mathcal{O}_{K}
of dimension
d
, and
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
be a Hermitian line bundle on
\mathscr{X}
. We define

\widehat{h}^{0}(\mathscr{X},\overline{\mathscr{L}}^{\otimes D})=\log\#\left\{s\in H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes D})\mid\|s\|_{\sup,v}\leqslant 1,\;\forall\;v\in M_{K,\infty}\right\},

and

\widehat{\vol}(\overline{\mathscr{L}})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widehat{h}^{0}(\mathscr{X},\overline{\mathscr{L}}^{\otimes n})}{n^{\dim(\mathscr{X})}/\dim(\mathscr{X})!}.

By the works mentioned above, the arithmetic volume function
\widehat{\vol}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
has many similar properties to those of the geometric setting
\vol(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
, so it is expected that an arithmetically big line bundle
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
, which means
\widehat{\vol}(\overline{\mathscr{L}})>0
, also has the property of Fujita approximation. In fact, this used to be a conjecture of A. Moriwaki.

1.1.2

In
[
4
,
26
]
, H. Chen and X. Yuan proved the arithmetic Fujita approximation over number field at almost the same time. Although they both applied
[
18
, §3]
, but their methods are essential different. X. Yuan
[
26
, Theorem C]
takes pluri-subharmonic envelope to deal with the infinite part, which is well-known in complex analysis.

In
[
4
]
, H. Chen applied his
\mathbb{R}
-filtration method developed in
[
5
]
. More precisely, by considering the successive minima on
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n})
, we can make
\bigoplus\limits_{n\geqslant 0}H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n})
to be a filtrated graded linear series. Then by combining some results of approximable graded linear series in
[
18
]
, we are able to apply some convergent properties of
\mathbb{R}
-filtrations to construct an ample line bundle which approximates
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
well. This is the main idea of the proof in
[
4
]
.

1.2
Arakelov geometry over adelic curves

In
[
9
]
, H. Chen and A. Moriwaki developed a kind of Arakelov theory over the so-called adelic curve. Based on this framework, they built an arithmetic intersection theory in
[
10
]
, and studied the arithmetic positivities in
[
11
]
. Under this setting, we can study the arithmetic geometry over number fields, function fields and other bases with adelic structures under a same framework.

1.2.1

Briefly speaking, an adelic curve
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
consists of an arbitrary field
K
which is equipped with a parameter set
\Omega
of all its absolute values. We require the set
\Omega
parameterized by
\phi
has a
\sigma
-algebra structure
\mathcal{A}
and a measure
\nu
compatible with
\mathcal{A}
.

Under this setting, we can generalize many classic results and subjects of Arakelov geometry, including the arithmetic volume function, arithmetic positivity, and Hilbert-Samuel formula. Since the adelic structure becomes more complicated than those of classic cases, the obstructions original from measurability, integrability, and non-discreteness make the developing process more technique. In fact, some classic properties are not valid any longer in this framework, though we have developed many in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, and see also
[
23
]
.

1.2.2

Inspired by
[
4
,
26
]
, it is quite natural to ask whether the Fujita approximation is still valid over adelic curves. Over a general adelic curve, we are lack of the Minkowskian property, so we cannot apply the filtrated method by successive minina directly in
[
4
]
At the same time, some constructions do not work any longer without the help of integral models.

In
[
11
, §8.5]
, H. Chen and A. Moriwaki proved a similar result to the arithmetic Fujita approximation over adelic curves, which is called the
relative Fujita approximation
. More precisely, let
\overline{L}
be an adelic line bundle, where
L
is big on
X
. Then for any
t<\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\overline{L})
, there exists a sufficiently large
p\in\mathbb{N}
and a birational morphism
\varphi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
, such that
(\varphi^{*}\overline{L})^{\otimes p}=\overline{M}\otimes\overline{N}
, where
\overline{N}
is relatively ample and
\overline{M}
is effective, and
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}^{\asy}(\overline{L})>pt
. Here
\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
and
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}^{\asy}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
denote the asymptotic maximal and minimal slopes respectively.

Compared with this relative version above, the classic arithmetic Fujita approximation seems to take an "average" at every minima of minimal slope, and approximates all of them simultaneously, not only the maximal one. In fact, this is conjectured by Chen-Moriwaki in
[
11
, Remark 9.2.7]
.

1.3
Main result

In this article, we prove the following result on the arithmetic Fujita approximation over adelic curves under the framework
[
9
,
11
]
, which gives a positive response to the conjecture
[
11
, Remark 9.2.7]
. More generally, we prove a version of adelic
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle below at the same time, which is also a generalization of
[
4
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
26
, Theorem C]
.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm

(Theorem
5.2.2
)
.

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve with a perfect
K
, which satisfies either
\mathcal{A}
is discrete or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
. Let
X
be a projective variety over
\spec K
, and
\overline{L}
be an arithmetically big
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle on
X
over
S
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exist a sufficiently large
p\in\mathbb{N}
and a birational morphism
v:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
, such that
v^{*}(\overline{L}^{\otimes p})=\overline{A}\otimes\overline{M}
, where
\overline{A}
is arithmetically ample and
\overline{M}
is an arithmetically effective
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle. Moreover, we have

p^{-(\dim(X)+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{A})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\epsilon.

1.3.1

In
[
11
, §3]
, they develop a method which constructs a graded linear series over the adelic curve of trivially valued field from general one by its Harder-Narasimhan filtration. Next, we can modify the original norms by its spectral norm, which makes the graded linear series to be filtrated. Under this construction, the new graded linear series has some asymptotic invariants same as the original one. Then some ideas in
[
4
,
18
]
can be absorbed in this work.

Once we prove the case of
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle (in Corollary
5.2.1
) from the case of usual adelic line bundle (in Corollary
5.1
), we can approximate an arithmetically big
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle by an arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle in an effective direction, and then we can prove the case of
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle (in Theorem
5.2.2
).

1.3.2

In the application of
\mathbb{R}
-filtration method, we need to take some limits of some graded linear series and measures, and most operations only work when the objects are measurable and integrable. If either
\mathcal{A}
is discrete or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
, there will be no obstructions on the measurability or integrability, and see
[
9
, §4]
for more details of the necessity.

In order to study some arithmetic positivities by
\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
and
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}^{\asy}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
, we need to suppose the base field
K
is perfect. For example, for an adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
where
L
is nef, we need to apply
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}^{\asy}(\overline{L})\geqslant 0
to deduce
\overline{L}
is arithmetically nef by
[
11
, Proposition 9.1.7]
, where we need the perfect condition. See
[
11
, §9]
for more details of the necessity.

Of course, in
[
4
,
26
]
, there is no such problem since for the case of number field, because
\mathcal{A}
is discrete in the language of adelic curve and the base field is perfect by its characteristic
0
.

1.4
Organization of article

In §
2
, we introduce the required notions of Arakelov theory over adelic curves and
\mathbb{R}
-filtration. In §
3
, we consider graded linear series over a general adelic curve and construct those over the adelic curve of trivially valued field filtrated by the Harder-Narasimhan filtration. In §

5
, we prove the arithmetic Fujita approximation of adelic line bundles in the usual case, the case of
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle and the case of
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle sequentially.

Arakelov theory over adelic curves

In this section, we will introduce the foundations of Arakelov theory over adelic curves and of
\mathbb{R}
-filtration. The main references are
[
9

11

.

2.1
Foundations of Arakelov geometry over adelic curves

First, we recall some useful knowledge on the Arakelov geometry over adelic curves, and see
[
9
]
for a systematical introduction to this subject.

2.1.1
Adelic curve

Let
K
be a field. We denote by
M_{K}
the set of all absolute values on
K
.

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(Adelic curve)
.

We say that
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu)
is an
adelic structure
on
K
equipped with a mapping

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\phi:&\Omega&\longrightarrow&M_{K}\\
&\omega&\mapsto&|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{\omega}\end{array}

which satisfies the properties below:

(1)

\mathcal{A}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega
, and
\nu
is a measure on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
;

(2)

for all
a\in K^{\times}
, the function

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Omega&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
w&\mapsto&\log|a|_{w}\end{array}

is
\mathcal{A}
-measurable and integrable with respect to
\nu
.

In addition, the data
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
is called an
adelic curve
.

We denote by
\Omega_{f}
the subset of
\Omega
whose images in
M_{K}
with respect to
\phi
is non-archimedean, and by
\Omega_{\infty}
the subset of
\Omega
whose image in
M_{K}
is archimedean. We have
\Omega=\Omega_{f}\sqcup\Omega_{\infty}
by definition directly.

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(Proper adelic curve)
.

We say that an adelic curve
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
is
proper
if for all
a\in K^{\times}
, we always have

\int_{\Omega}\log|a|_{w}\nu(\mathrm{d}w)=0,

and the above equality is called the
product formula
of
S
.

We refer the readers to
[
9
, §3]
for more details on these notions.

\exemname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
K
be a number field or a function field. Then the usual adèle ring of
K
gives an adelic structure, and constructs examples of proper adelic curves.

Let
K
be a finitely transcendental extension over
\mathbb{Q}
, which is a sub-field of
\mathbb{C}
. In
[
19
]
, A. Moriwaki constructed a special adelic structure over
K
, and defined a height function with respect to it. This height function has been applied to study many arithmetic geometry subjects.

\exemname

\the\smf@thm

(Trivially valued field)
.

Let
K
be an arbitrary field, and
|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{0}
be the trivial absolute value over
K
, which means

|a|_{0}=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }a\neq 0,\\
0,\text{ if }a=0.\end{cases}

We note
S_{0}=(K,\{0\},|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{0})
the adelic curve which counts the absolute value
|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{0}
once. By definition,
S_{0}
is a proper adelic curve.

Example
2.1.1
will be used in the article later. We refer the readers to
[
9
, §3.2]
for more examples of adelic curve.

2.1.2
Adelic vector bundle

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be an adelic curve, and
E
be a vector space over
K
. For a family of norms
(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega}
on
E_{w}=E\otimes_{K}K_{w}
over
K_{w}
, if the norm family
(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega}
on
E
and its dual norm family
(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|^{\vee}_{w})_{w\in\Omega}
on
E^{\vee}
are both
\mathcal{A}
-measurable on
\Omega
, then we say
\overline{E}=(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
is an
adelic vector bundle
over
S
. If
\dim(E)=1
, we say that
\overline{E}
is an
adelic line bundle
over
S
.

We say that an adelic vector bundle
\overline{F}=(F,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|^{\prime}_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
is an
adelic sub-bundle
of
\overline{E}=(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
over
S
, if
F
is a
K
-vector subspace of
E
, and
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|^{\prime}_{w}
is the restriction of
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w}
on
F
for each
w\in\Omega
.

An adelic bundle
\overline{G}=(G,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|^{\prime\prime}_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
is said to be an
adelic quotient bundle
of
\overline{E}=(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
over
S
, if
G
is a quotient vector space of
E
, and
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|^{\prime\prime}_{w}
is the quotient norm
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w}
with respect to the canonical quotient homomorphism
E\rightarrow G
for each
w\in\Omega
. If
G
is the quotient space of
E
modulo
F
, and is equipped with the quotient metrics from
\overline{F}
, then we denote
\overline{G}=\overline{E}/\overline{F}=\overline{E/F}
.

For an adelic vector bundle
\overline{E}=(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
over
S
, we define
\det(\overline{E})=(\det(E),(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\det,w})_{w\in\Omega})
as follows: the
K
-vector space
\det(E)=\bigwedge^{\dim(E)}(E)
is the determinant space of
E
, and
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\det,w}
is the determinant norm on
\det(E)_{w}
of
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w}
for each
w\in\Omega
. We refer the readers to
[
9
, §1.2.3]
for the details of the construction of
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\det,w}
.

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

In general, for an adelic vector bundle
\overline{E}
over
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
, we cannot make sure that all its subspace equipped with the subspace norms is still an adelic vector bundle, and its quotient spaces admit the same situation. However, if
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete
\sigma
-algebra or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
, we will get adelic vector bundles from all the above constructions, and see
[
9
, Proposition 4.1.24]
for more details. In the whole article, we will always assume these conditions.

2.1.3
Some Arakelov invariants

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve, and
\overline{E}=(E,(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega})
be an adelic vector bundle over
S
. For the determinant bundle
\det(\overline{E})=(\det(E),(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\det,w})_{w\in\Omega})
of
\overline{E}
, we select an
s\in\det(E)\smallsetminus\{0\}
, and define

\widehat{\deg}(\overline{E})=-\int_{\Omega}\log\|s\|_{\det,w}\nu(\mathrm{d}w),

which is well-defined due to
[
9
, §4.2]
. This is independent of the choice of
s
by the product formula of
S
.

We define the
positive Arakelov degree
of
\overline{E}
as

\widehat{\deg}_{+}(\overline{E})=\sup_{F\subseteq E}\widehat{\deg}(\overline{F}),

where
\overline{F}
runs over all the adelic sub-bundles of
\overline{E}
.

For a non-zero adelic bundle
\overline{E}
, we define the
slope
of
\overline{E}
as

\widehat{\mu}(\overline{E})=\frac{\widehat{\deg}(\overline{E})}{\dim(E)}.

In addition, we define the
maximal slope
of
\overline{E}
as

\widehat{\mu}_{\max}(\overline{E})=\sup_{F\subseteq E}\widehat{\mu}(\overline{F}),

where
\overline{F}
runs over all adelic sub-bundles of
\overline{E}
. Similarly, we define the
minimal slope
of
\overline{E}
as

\widehat{\mu}_{\min}(\overline{E})=\inf_{E\twoheadrightarrow G}\widehat{\mu}(\overline{G}),

where
\overline{G}
runs over all adelic quotient bundles of
\overline{E}
.

We refer the readers to
[
9
, §4.3]
for more details and properties of these invariants.

2.2
Harder-Narasimhan filtration from
\mathbb{R}
-filtration

In this part, we introduce a method to filtrate an adelic vector bundle by its minimal slopes, which is called the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of an adelic vector bundle. We will use the language of
\mathbb{R}
-filtration to formulate this construction. See
[
5
]
for its origin and
[
9
]
for the version over adelic curve.

2.2.1
\mathbb{R}
-filtrations on vector spaces

Let
K
be a field, and
W
be a
K
-vector space of finite dimension. For an
\mathbb{R}
-filtration
on
W
, we mean a sequence
\mathcal{F}=\left(\mathcal{F}^{t}W\right)_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of vector subspaces of
W
, which satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

if
t\leqslant s
, then
\mathcal{F}^{s}W\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{t}W
;

(2)

\mathcal{F}^{t}W=0
for sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{F}^{t}W=W
for sufficiently small
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(3)

the function

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
t&\mapsto&\dim_{K}(\mathcal{F}^{t}W)\end{array}

is left continuous with respect to Euclidean topology.

2.2.2
A measure induced by
\mathbb{R}
-filtration

Let
K
be a field. For a
K
-vector space
W
of finite dimension equipped with an
\mathbb{R}
-filtration
\mathcal{F}
, we denote by
\nu_{(W,\mathcal{F})}
(or by
\nu_{W}
if there is no ambiguity) the Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
, which is induced from the derivative of the function

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
t&\mapsto&-\frac{\dim(\mathcal{F}^{t}W)}{\dim(W)}.\end{array}

In fact,
\nu_{(W,\mathcal{F})}
is a linear combination of some Dirac measures. More precisely, if the
\mathbb{R}
-filtration
\mathcal{F}
consists of a flag

W=W_{0}\supsetneq W_{1}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq W_{n}=0

together with the jumps at the real numbers
\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{n}
, then we have

\nu_{(W,\mathcal{F})}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\dim\left(W_{i-1}/W_{i}\right)}{\dim(W)}\delta_{\lambda_{i}},

where
\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
is the Dirac operator at the real number
\lambda_{i}
.

Next, we define the function

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\lambda:&W&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}\\
&x&\mapsto&\sup\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid x\in\mathcal{F}^{t}W\}.\end{array}

(1)

Then we have
\lambda(W)\subset\supp(\nu_{W})\cup\{+\infty\}
, and
\lambda(x)
is finite if
x\neq 0
.

In addition, we define

\lambda_{+}(W)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}x\nu_{W}(\mathrm{d}x)\text{, and }\lambda_{\max}(W)=\max\limits_{x\in W\smallsetminus\{0\}}\lambda(x).

(2)

2.2.3
Harder-Narasimhan filtration

By the notion of
\mathbb{R}
-filtration, we introduce a filtration by the minimal slopes of adelic bundles. This is original from an analogical study of vector bundles on a smooth projective curve
[
13
]
.

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(Harder-Narasimhan filtration)
.

Let
\overline{E}
be a non-zero adelic vector bundle over a proper adelic curve
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
, where either
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete
\sigma
-algebra or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\mathcal{F}_{\hn}^{t}\overline{E}=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{0\}\neq F\in\Theta(E)\\
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}(\overline{F})\geqslant t-\epsilon\end{subarray}}F=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{0\}\neq F\in\Theta(E)\\
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}(\overline{F})\geqslant t\end{subarray}}F,

where
\Theta(E)
denotes the set of all vector subspaces of
E
, and
\overline{F}
is equipped with the subspace norms. This
\mathbb{R}
-filtration
\mathcal{F}_{\hn}
is called the
Harder-Narasimhan filtration
of
\overline{E}
.

The last equality in Definition
2.2.3
if from
[
9
, Proposition 4.3.36]
. In fact, the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\overline{E}
corresponds to an increasing flag of vector subspaces

\{0\}=E_{1}\subsetneq E_{2}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq E_{n}=E

(3)

and a decreasing sequence of real numbers
\mu_{1}>\cdots>\mu_{n}
represents the jumps of the
\mathbb{R}
-filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{\hn}^{t}\overline{E})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, where

\mu_{i}=\sup\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}\mid\dim_{K}(\mathcal{F}^{t}_{\hn}\overline{E})\geqslant i\right\}.

In particular, still by

With all the above notations and conditions. If for every homogeneous elements

3
Graded linear series of global section spaces

Let
S
be an adelic curve,
X
be a projective scheme over
\spec K
, and
\overline{L}
be an adelic line bundle on

over

3.1
Adelic line bundles on an arithmetic variety

First, we recall some notions about arithmetic positivities of the adelic line bundles on arithmetic variety over an adelic curve, which are original from
[
8
,
11

. We refer the readers to
[
11
, §6 – §9]
for a self-contained introduction to these notions.

3.1.1
Adelic line bundle

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be an adelic curve, and
X
be a projective scheme over
\spec K
. We say that
\overline{L}=(L,\varphi)
is an
adelic line bundle
on
X
, if
L
is a line bundle on
X
, and
\varphi=(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w})_{w\in\Omega}
is a family of metrics on
L_{w}
for each
w\in\Omega
, which is dominated and measurable.

In addition, we suppose that either
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete
\sigma
-algebra or
K
contains a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
. Then we construct a family of norms on
H^{0}(X,L)
in the way below: for every
w\in\Omega
and
x\in X_{w}^{\an}
, define

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{\varphi_{w}}(x):&H^{0}(X_{w},L_{w})&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&s&\mapsto&|s|_{\varphi_{w}}(x)\end{array}

as the value of the global section
s
at the point
x
with respect to the given metric, and

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\varphi_{w}}:&H^{0}(X_{w},L_{w})&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&s&\mapsto&\sup\limits_{x\in X^{\an}_{w}}\{|s|_{\varphi_{w}}(x)\}.\end{array}

(5)

We denote
\varphi=(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\varphi_{w}})_{w\in\Omega}
the norm family on
H^{0}(X,L)
over
S
if there is no ambiguity. By
[
9
, Theorem 6.1.13, Theorem 6.1.32]
,
(H^{0}(X,L),\varphi)
is an adelic vector bundle over
S
.

Let
\overline{L}=(L,\varphi)
and
\overline{M}=(M,\psi)
be two adelic line bundles on
X
. We denote by
\overline{L}\otimes\overline{M}=(L\otimes M,\varphi+\psi)
the tensor adelic line bundle, where
\varphi+\psi
is the tensor product of metrics. In particular, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\overline{L}^{\otimes n}=(L^{\otimes n},n\varphi)
the adelic line bundle of
n
-times tensor product of
\overline{L}
, and by
(H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes n}),n\varphi)
the adelic vector bundle over
S
defined above. By
[
9
, Proposition 6.1.28, Proposition 6.1.31]
, all the above constructions are all well-defined.

We refer the readers to
[
9
, §6.1]
for a precise explanation of §
3.1.1
, see also
[
11
, §2.6 – §2.8]
.

3.1.2
Semi-positive metric

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be an adelic curve,
X
be a projective scheme over
\spec K
,
L
be a line bundle on
X
,
\varphi=(\varphi_{w})_{w\in\Omega}
and
\psi=(\psi_{w})_{w\in\Omega}
be two families of metrics on
L
such that
(L,\varphi)
and
(L,\psi)
are both adelic line bundles. Then we define the
distance
between
\varphi
and
\psi
as

d(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{\Omega}\sup_{x\in X^{\an}_{w}}\left|\log\frac{|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{\varphi_{w}}(x)}{|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}|_{\psi_{w}}(x)}\right|\nu(\mathrm{d}w).

If
L
is semi-ample, and if there exist a positive integer
m
and a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of quotient metric families (where
\varphi_{n}
a metric family of
L^{\otimes mn}
), such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{mn}d(nm\varphi,\varphi_{n})=0,

we say that the metric family
\varphi
is
semi-positive
.

3.1.3
Ampleness

Let
\overline{L}=(L,\varphi)
be an adelic line bundle on
X
over
S
. We say
\overline{L}
is
relatively ample
if
L
is ample and
\varphi
is semi-positive.

In addition, an adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
on
X
is
ample
, if it is relatively
ample and if there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that the inequality

(\overline{L}|_{Y}^{\dim(Y)+1})\geqslant\epsilon\deg_{L}(Y)(\dim(Y)+1)

holds for any integral closed subscheme
Y
of
X
, where
(\overline{L}|_{Y}^{\dim(Y)+1})
is the arithmetic intersection number studied in
[
10
]
. We also call it
arithmetically ample
to distinguish it from the geometric case.

\remaname

\the\smf@thm
.

We compare the above definition with the arithmetically ample definition of S. Zhang in
[
29
]
, where an arithmetically ample Hermitian line bundle on an arithmetic variety requires the metrics on the line bundle to be smooth. The definition in
[
11
, Definition 9.1.1]
over an adelic curve only require continuous metrics. In this article, we follow the definition given in
[
11
, Definition 9.1.1]
as above.

3.1.4
Nefness

Let
\overline{L}=(L,\varphi)
be an adelic line bundle on
X
over
S
. We say that
\overline{L}
is
relatively nef
if there exists a relatively ample adelic line bundle
\overline{A}
on
X
and a positive integer
N
such that, for any
n\geqslant N
, the tensor product of adelic line bundles
\overline{L}^{\otimes n}\otimes\overline{A}
is relatively ample.

An adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
is said to be
nef
or
arithmetically nef
, if there exist an arithmetically ample line bundle
\overline{A}
and a positive integer
M
such that
\overline{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\overline{A}
is ample for any
m\geqslant M
in the arithmetic sense.

3.1.5
Effectivity

Let
\overline{L}=(L,\varphi)
be an adelic line bundle on
X
over
S
. If
s\in H^{0}(X,L)
is a non-zero global section which satisfies
\|s\|_{\varphi_{w}}\leqslant 1
for all
w\in\Omega
, we say that the global section
s
is
effective
.

We say that an adelic line bundle
\overline{L}
is
effective
or
arithmetically effective
, if it admits at least one effective global section by the above definition.

3.2
Algebraic construction of graded linear series

In this part we will only consider the geometric setting, which means everything works over an arbitrary field with no metric. For every
D\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we consider the global section space
H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes D})
, and the graded
K
-algebra
\bigoplus\limits_{n\geqslant 0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes n})
.

\definame

\the\smf@thm

(Graded linear series)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over
K
, and
L
be a line bundle on
X
. For a
graded linear series
of

, we mean a graded sub-

-algebra of
\bigoplus\limits_{n\geqslant 0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes n})

3.2.1
Graded linear series containing an ample divisor

The following definition is original from
[
18
, Definition 2.9]
, where it is called "Condition (C)". This notion plays a key role in the arithmetic Fujita approximation of
[
4
]
, and we follow the notation of
[

.

\definame

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
X
be a projective variety, and
L
be a line bundle on
X
. Suppose that there exists a
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, an ample line bundle
A
, an effective line bundle
M
on
X
, and a non-zero section
s\in H^{0}(X,M)
, such that
L^{\otimes p}=A\otimes M
. In this case, we say a graded linear series
W_{\bullet}=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geqslant 0}W_{n}
of

contains an ample divisor
if the homomorphism of graded algebras

\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}H^{0}(X,A^{\otimes n})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes np})

can factor through
\bigoplus\limits_{n\geqslant 0}W_{np}

In §
2.3.2
, we have seen that a filtrated approximable graded linear series has many convergent properties. The following property is original from
[

]
, which is applied in
[

]
to judge whether a graded linear series is approximable.

\propname

\the\smf@thm

(Theorem 3.4,
[

]
)

If

contains an ample divisor and
W_{n}\neq 0
for sufficiently large

is approximable.

3.2.2
Big line bundles

Let
W_{\bullet}=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geqslant 0}W_{n}
be a graded linear series of

\vol(W_{\bullet})=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{K}(W_{n})}{n^{\dim(X)}/\dim(X)!}.

If

, we denote
\vol(L)=\vol(W_{\bullet})
.

If
\vol(L)>0

is a
big
line bundle on

.

3.3.3

For every

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

With all the above notations and conditions. We have

3.4

K

is a discrete
\sigma

Let
\overline{E}_{\bullet}=\bigoplus\limits_{D\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{E}_{D}

\propname

∎

By Proposition

\propname

The equivalence of
\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})>0

\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\overline{L})>0

directly. The equality is from

, Proposition 3.4.1]
.
∎

From Proposition
3.3.3
and Proposition

\propname

\the\smf@thm

\propname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let

\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})=\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{E}_{\bullet}^{(p)}).

Proof.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
\nu_{\mathrm{sp},n}=T_{\frac{1}{n}}\nu_{\left(E_{\hn,n},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right)}
, where the notation
T_{\frac{1}{n}}\nu_{\left(E_{\hn,n},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right)}

\mathbb{R}

with respect to the spectral norms. Then by Proposition

Similarly, let
\nu_{\mathrm{sp},n}^{(p)}=T_{\frac{1}{np}}\nu_{\left(E^{(p)}_{\hn,np},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}\right)}

for

.

Step I. – Simultaneous approximations.

Let

D:\;0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m}<\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\overline{E}_{\hn,\bullet})

be a division of the interval
[0,\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\overline{E}_{\hn,\bullet}))
such that

\nu_{\mathrm{sp}}(\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\})=0.

(11)

We define the function

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}h_{D}:&\mathbb{R}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&x&\mapsto&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m-1}t_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{i},t_{i+1})}(x)+t_{m}\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{m},+\infty)}(x).\end{array}

By Corollary
4.1.3
and Definition
2.3.1
, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a

sufficiently large, such that

approximates all

defined at (
10
) simultaneously, where
i=0,\ldots,m
. In other words, there exists an

, such that for every

\inf_{0\leqslant i\leqslant m}\frac{\dim_{K}\left(\im\left(S^{n}E_{\hn,p}^{t_{i}}\rightarrow E_{\hn,np}^{t_{i}}\right)\right)}{\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np}^{t_{i}})}\geqslant 1-\epsilon

for all

. Then by the above inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{K}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{npt_{i}}(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\left(\im\left(S^{n}E_{\hn,p}^{t_{i}}\rightarrow E_{\hn,np}^{t_{i}}\right)\right)

(12)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(1-\epsilon)\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np}^{t_{i}})

for all
i=0,\ldots,m
.

Step II. – An upper bound of integrations.

We note that

\displaystyle np\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\nu_{\mathrm{sp},np}^{(p)}(\mathrm{d}t)=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\mathrm{d}\dim_{K}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{t}E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dim_{K}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{t}E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\mathrm{d}t\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{m}np(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\dim_{K}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{npt_{i}}E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle np(1-\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{m}(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np}^{t_{i}}),

where the first inequality is from the decreasing of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}
, and the last inequality is from (

E_{\hn,np}^{t_{i}}=\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{sp}}^{npt_{i}}E_{\hn,np}
.

In addition, by applying Abel’s summation formula to the last term of the above inequality, we have

\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\nu_{\mathrm{sp},np}^{(p)}(\mathrm{d}t)\geqslant(1-\epsilon)\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np})\int_{0}^{+\infty}h_{D}(t)\nu_{\mathrm{sp},np}(\mathrm{d}t).

Step III. – Estimates of arithmetic volumes.

Let
d=\dim(X)
. By Proposition
3.4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d+1}{(np)^{d+1}/(d+1)!}\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\nu_{\mathrm{sp},np}^{(p)}(\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d+1)\vol(E_{\bullet}^{(p)})\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\nu_{\mathrm{sp}}^{(p)}(\mathrm{d}t)=\widehat{\vol}(E_{\bullet}^{(p)}).

Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\vol}(E_{\bullet}^{(p)})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d+1}{(np)^{d+1}/(d+1)!}(1-\epsilon)\dim_{K}(E_{\hn,np})\int_{0}^{+\infty}h_{D}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\mathrm{sp},np}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d+1)(1-\epsilon)\vol(L)\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{D}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\mathrm{sp}},

where the last equality is from
[
3
, Chap. IV §5
n^{\circ}
12 Proposition 22]
.

We choose a sequence of subdivisions
(D_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
11

(h_{D_{j}}(t))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
converges uniformly to

\max\{t,0\}-\max\{t-\widehat{\mu}_{\max}^{\asy}(\overline{E}_{\hn,\bullet}),0\}

when

is bounded from above by

\sup_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\vol}(E_{\bullet}^{(p)})\geqslant(d+1)(1-\epsilon)\vol(L)\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\nu_{\mathrm{sp}}(\mathrm{d}t)=(1-\epsilon)\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L}),

where the last equality is from Proposition

on the equality of arithmetic volumes over

and

5.1
Fujita approximation of big adelic line bundles

First, we will prove the arithmetic Fujita approximation of usual adelic line bundles. We will prove the nef version first, and apply it to prove the ample version. In order to apply Proposition
3.4.5
, we assume the base field is perfect.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve with a perfect
K
, where either
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete
\sigma
-algebra or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
. Let
X
be a projective variety with
\dim(X)=d
, and
\overline{L}
be an arithmetically big line bundle on
X
over
S
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a birational morphism
v:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
together with a positive integer
p
, such that the decomposition
v^{*}(\overline{L}^{\otimes p})=\overline{M}\otimes\overline{G}
satisfies:

(1)

\overline{M}
is effective and
\overline{G}
is arithmetically nef;

(2)

p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{G})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\epsilon
.

Proof.

We devide the proof into three steps.

Step I. – Determining the geometric decomposition.

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow\spec K
be the structural morphism. For any
p\geqslant 1
such that
E_{\hn,p}^{0}\neq 0
(see Proposition
4.1.3
for the existence of such
p\in\mathbb{N}
), let

\phi_{p}:X_{p}\rightarrow X

be the blow-up along the base locus of
E_{\hn,p}^{0}
, which means

X_{p}=\proj\left(\im\left(\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\pi^{*}(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}L^{\otimes np}\right)\right).

Let
G_{p}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{p}}(1)
, and
M_{p}
be the line bundle on
X_{p}
with respect to the exceptional divisor. Let
s
be a global section of
M_{p}
which trivializes
M_{p}
outside the exceptional divisor. Then by definition, we have

\phi_{p}^{*}(L^{\otimes p})=G_{p}\otimes M_{p}.

The canonical morphism

\phi_{p}^{*}\pi^{*}(E_{\hn,p}^{0})\rightarrow G_{p}

induces a projective embedding
i_{p}:X_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(E_{\hn,p}^{0}\right)
with
i^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{E_{\hn,p}^{0}}(1)\right)=G_{p}
. Then the restriction of global sections of
\mathcal{O}_{E_{\hn,p}^{0}}(n)
on
X_{p}
gives an injective homomorphism

\im\left(S^{n}E_{\hn,p}^{0}\rightarrow E_{\hn,np}\right)=E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}\rightarrow H^{0}(X_{p},G_{p}^{\otimes n}),

(13)

where
H^{0}(X_{p},G_{p}^{\otimes n})
is considered as a sub-
K
-vector space of
H^{0}(X_{p},\phi_{p}^{*}(L^{\otimes p}))
via the section
s
.

Step II. – Constructing the metrics of line bundles.

We consider
\overline{E}^{(p)}_{\bullet}
as a normed graded linear series over
S
, which is equipped with the subspace norms of
\overline{E}_{\bullet}=\bigoplus\limits_{D\geqslant 0}\overline{E}_{D}
. For any
n\geqslant 1
and
w\in\Omega
, let
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{n,w}
be the quotient norm on
G_{p,w}=G_{p}\times_{\spec K}\spec K_{w}
induced by the surjective homomorphism

\phi_{p}^{*}\pi^{*}(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\rightarrow G_{p}^{\otimes n},

where
E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}
is equipped with the superior norm
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{\sup,w}
with respect to
w\in\Omega
.

In fact, for any
x\in X_{p,w}(\overline{K}_{w})
outside the exceptional divisor, it corresponds to a
1
-dimensional quotient of
E_{\hn,p}^{0}
. For any
n\geqslant 1
, this quotient induces a
1
-dimensional quotient
\ell_{n,x}
of
E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}
.

By Hahn-Banach theorem, every linear form on
\ker(E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}\rightarrow\ell_{n,x})
can be extended to a linear form on
E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}
. In other words, there exists a section
w\in E_{\hn,np}^{(p)}
, whose image in
G_{p,w}^{\otimes n}(x)
has norm
\|w\|_{\sup,w}
. As a section of
L_{w}^{\otimes np}
on
X_{v}(\overline{K}_{w})
, we have
\|w(x)\|_{w}\leqslant\|w\|_{\sup,w}
, where
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{w}
is the metric on
\overline{L}
indexed by
v\in\Omega
. Then for any section
u\in G_{p,w}
over a neighborhood of
x
, we may assume that
u(x)^{\otimes n}
equals to the image of
w(x)
in
G_{p,w}^{\otimes n}(x)
by dilation, then we have

\|u(x)\|_{n,w}=\|w\|_{\sup,w}^{1/n}\geqslant\|w(x)\|_{w}^{1/n}=\|u(x)\otimes s(x)\|_{w}.

Let
\alpha_{n}=(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{n,w})_{w\in\Omega}
be a family of adelic metrics on
G_{p}
, and we define
(M_{p},\beta_{n})=\phi_{p}^{*}(\overline{L})\otimes(G_{p},\alpha_{n})^{\vee}
. In this sense,
s
is an effective section in
(M_{p},\beta_{n})
, then it is an effective adelic line bundle.

Step III. – The relation among arithmetic volumes.

By the construction in Step II, for any
n\geqslant 1
, we have

p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(G_{p},\alpha_{n})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(E_{\bullet}^{(p)},\alpha_{n}),

where

\widehat{\vol}(E_{\bullet}^{(p)},\alpha_{n})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{\deg}_{+}(E_{\hn,mp}^{(p)},(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{n,\sup,w})_{w\in\Omega})}{m^{d+1}/(d+1)!},

and
\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{n,\sup,w}
is the superior norm induced by
\alpha_{n}=(\|\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.}\|_{n,w})_{w\in\Omega}
on
G_{p}
. Then by
[
4
, Corollary A.2]
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\widehat{\vol}(E_{\bullet}^{(p)},\alpha_{n})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{E}_{\bullet}^{(p)}).

By Proposition
4.2
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
p\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(G_{p},\alpha_{n})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Then there exists an
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(G_{p},\alpha_{n})-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\frac{\epsilon}{2}

for infinitely many
n\geqslant N_{0}
.

In addition, since
\widehat{\mu}_{\min}(\overline{E}_{\hn,np}^{(p)})\geqslant 0
for all
n\geqslant 1
, then it has a positive asymptotic minimal slope. Then by Proposition
3.4.5
, we obtain that
(G_{p},\alpha_{n})
is arithmetically nef with the fact that
K
is perfect.

To sum up, we obtain the assertion.
∎

\coroname

\the\smf@thm

is arithmetically big. By Theorem
5.1
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a birational morphism
\phi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
, a
p\in\mathbb{N}
, and a decomposition

\phi^{*}(\overline{L}^{\otimes mp}\otimes\overline{B}^{\otimes(-p)})=\overline{G}\otimes\overline{M}

with
\overline{G}
arithmetically nef and
\overline{M}
effective, such that

p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{G})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\overline{B}^{\vee})-\epsilon.

Let
\overline{B}^{\prime}
be an arithmetically ample line bundle on
X^{\prime}
. Since
\phi^{*}(\overline{B})
is arithmetically big, there exists a
q\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\phi^{*}(\overline{B}^{\otimes q})\otimes\overline{B}^{\prime\vee}
is effective. We consider

\phi^{*}(\overline{L}^{\otimes mpq})=(\overline{G}^{\otimes q}\otimes\overline{B}^{\prime})\otimes(\overline{M}^{\otimes q}\otimes\phi^{*}(\overline{B}^{\otimes pq})\otimes\overline{B}^{\prime\vee}).

In the above decomposition,
\overline{G}^{\prime}:=\overline{G}^{\otimes q}\otimes\overline{B}^{\prime}
is arithmetically ample,
\overline{M}^{\otimes q}\otimes\phi^{*}(\overline{B}^{\otimes pq})\otimes\overline{B}^{\prime\vee}
is effective, and

(mpq)^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{G}^{\prime})\geqslant(mpq)^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{G}^{\otimes q})\geqslant m^{-(d+1)}(\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L}^{\otimes m}\otimes\overline{B}^{\vee})-\epsilon).

By the continuity of the arithmetic volume function (see
[
9
, Corollary 6.4.10]
), we have the result.
∎

5.2
Case of
\mathbb{K}
-line bundles

In this part we will prove the arithmetic Fujita approximation is verified for adelic
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles and
\mathbb{R}
-line bundles over adelic curves.

5.2.1

First, we prove the case of adelic
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle, which is a quite straightforward corollary from the usual case.

\coroname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve with a perfect
K
, where either
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete
\sigma
-algebra or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
. Let
X
be a projective variety over
\spec K
with
\dim(X)=d
, and
\overline{L}
be an arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle on
X
over
S
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a birational morphism
\phi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
together with a
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, such that the decomposition
\phi^{*}(\overline{L}^{\otimes p})=\overline{M}\otimes\overline{G}
satisfies:

(1)

\overline{M}
and
\overline{G}
are adelic line bundles, where
\overline{M}
is effective and
\overline{G}
is arithmetically ample;

(2)

p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{G})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\epsilon
.

Proof.

By definition, there exists an
m\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\overline{L}^{\otimes m}
is a usual arithmetically big line bundle. Then we obtain the assertion by applying Corollary
5.1
to
\overline{L}^{\otimes m}
.
∎

5.2.2

Next, we prove the case of adelic
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle, and see
[
9
, §6.4.3]
for an introduction to this notion. For this target, we prove an auxiliary lemma first, which allows us to approximate an arithmetically big
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle by an arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle along an effective direction.

\lemmname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve, and
X
be a projective variety over
\spec K
. Let
\overline{L}
be an arithmetically big
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle on
X
. Then for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists an arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\overline{L}^{\prime}
, such that
\overline{L}-\overline{L}^{\prime}
is effective and

0<\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L}^{\prime})<\epsilon.

Proof.

The adelic
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle
\overline{L}
is arithmetically big, so
L
is a big
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle. By an equivalent definition
[
16
, Definition 2.2.21]
, we have
L=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}L_{i}
, where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}>0
and
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
are usual big line bundles. By definition,
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
are all effective in the geometric sense.

We equip metrics on
L_{1},\ldots,L_{r}
over
S
, such that
\overline{L}_{1},\ldots,\overline{L}_{r}
are arithmetically effective. Then for any
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have
b_{1}\overline{L}_{1}+\cdots+b_{r}\overline{L}_{r}
is effective.

By the continuity of
\widehat{\vol}(\raisebox{1.72218pt}{.})
from
[
9
, Corollary 6.4.24]
, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{r}>0
, such that for all
t_{i}\in(0,\delta_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,r
, we always have

0<\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\widehat{\vol}\left(\overline{L}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\overline{L}_{i}\right)<\epsilon.

If there does not exist any arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundles satisfying the requirements of the lemma, then
\overline{L}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\overline{L}_{i}
can never be a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle for all
t_{i}\in(0,\delta_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,r
. It means there is no
\mathbb{Q}
-point in the region

(a_{1}-\delta_{1},a_{1})\times\cdots\times(a_{r}-\delta_{r},a_{r})\subset\mathbb{R}^{r},

which has a strict positive Lebesgue measure. Then it contradicts to the fact that
\mathbb{Q}^{r}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{r}
with respect to the Euclidean topology.

By choosing the constant
\epsilon
sufficiently small, we pick a family
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
\overline{L}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\overline{L}_{i}
is an arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle. By the above construction,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\overline{L}_{i}
is effective. Let

\overline{L}^{\prime}=\overline{L}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\overline{L}_{i},

which satisfies the requirement.
∎

We prove the Fujita approximation of
\mathbb{R}
-line bundles via Lemma
5.2.2
.

\theoname

\the\smf@thm
.

Let
S=(K,(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\nu),\phi)
be a proper adelic curve with a perfect
K
, where either
\mathcal{A}
is a discrete
\sigma
-algebra or
K
has a countable sub-field which is dense in
K_{w}
for all
w\in\Omega
. Let
X
be a projective variety over
\spec K
with
\dim(X)=d
, and
\overline{L}
be an arithmetically big adelic
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle on
X
over
S
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a birational morphism
\phi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
together with a
p\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, such that the decomposition
\phi^{*}(\overline{L}^{\otimes p})=\overline{M}\otimes\overline{G}
satisfies:

(1)

\overline{M}
is an arithmetically effective
\mathbb{R}
-line bundle, and
\overline{G}
is an arithmetically ample line bundle;

(2)

p^{-(d+1)}\widehat{\vol}(\overline{G})\geqslant\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\epsilon
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2.2
, for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists an arithmetically big
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
\overline{L}^{\prime}
, such that
\overline{L}-\overline{L}^{\prime}
is effective and

0<\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L})-\widehat{\vol}(\overline{L}^{\prime})<\epsilon.

Then we apply Corollary
5.2.1
to
\overline{L}^{\prime}
, and we obtain the assertion.
∎

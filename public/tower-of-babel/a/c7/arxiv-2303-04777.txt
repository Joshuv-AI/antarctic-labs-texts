LMI-based Data-Driven Robust Model Predictive Control
Hoang Hai Nguyen, Maurice Friedel, Rolf Findeisen

Abstract
Predictive control, which is based on a model of the system to compute the applied input optimizing the future system behavior, is by now widely used. If the nominal models are not given or are very uncertain, data-driven model predictive control approaches can be employed, where the system model or input is directly obtained from past measured trajectories. Using a data informativity framework and Finsler’s lemma, we propose a data-driven robust linear matrix inequality-based model predictive control scheme that considers input and state constraints for linear parameter-varying systems and Lur’e-type nonlinear systems. Using these data, we formulate the problem as a semi-definite optimization problem, whose solution provides the matrix gain for the linear feedback, while the decisive variables are independent of the length of the measurement data. The designed controller stabilizes the closed-loop system asymptotically and guarantees constraint satisfaction. Numerical examples are conducted to illustrate the method.



keywords

Data-driven optimal control, Predictive control, LMI, Robust linear matrix inequalities.

1
Introduction

Model predictive control (MPC, see e. g.
Rawlings et al. 2017
,
Findeisen et al. 2007
,
Lucia et al. 2016
) has become a popular control scheme thanks to the ability of efficiently handling constraints and performance criteria as well as the coherent implementation of multiple hierarchical layers.
The MPC scheme formulated as a semi-definite optimization problem in form of Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs) is often used for some typical classes of systems such as linear parameter-varying systems or Lur’e type systems, see for example,
Kothare et al. 1996
,
Böhm et al. 2009
and
Nguyen et al. 2018
.
The reason for this is the formulated optimization problem is convex and can be efficiently solved.
Conventional MPC requires a model of the plant, which is usually obtained from first principle modelling or measured data via system identification.
However, model development has been shown to be a critical and time-consuming step in MPC implementation (
Rossiter and Kouvaritakis 2001
), while the performance of MPC elementally depends on the quality of the model.
An alternative approach is to design an MPC controller directly from measured data, without prior knowledge of an accurate model, which is often referred to as data-driven MPC. The learning-based approach, with the usage of Gaussian process (
Hewing et al. 2020
,
Maiworm et al. 2021
) or Reinforcement Learning (
Zanon and Gros 2021
), received increasing attention recently.
The drawback of these approaches is that they imperil the advantage of MPC in handling constraints and providing theoretical guarantees since proving the desirable closed-loop properties is challenging.

With regards to direct data-driven approaches, the fundamental result proposed by
Willems et al. 2005
, which is often referred as Willems et al. ’s fundamental lemma, provides an answer on how to design controllers directly from data, where the system is implicitly represented via the Hankel matrix of measured trajectory.
In short, the lemma states that all trajectories of an controllable LTI system can be represented by a finite set of its past trajectories, given that the past trajectories are generated by a sufficiently exciting inputs.
This idea has been investigated by
De Persis and Tesi 2020
, where the stabilizing feedback gain is designed by formulating the problem as LMIs without considering performance and constraints guarantees, and
Berberich et al. 2021
,
Coulson et al. 2019
where this idea is used to develop a data-driven MPC schemes. These approaches use persistently exciting data in control design; thus, the data have to be sufficiently rich enough to uniquely identify the system model within a given model class.

Proposing the data informativity framework, the work
van Waarde et al. 2020
has shown that the corresponding informativity conditions on the data are often weaker than those for system identification in the sense that, in some cases, the data do not need to be sufficiently informative to uniquely identify the system.
Aligned with this framework, the matrix Finsler’s lemma provided in
van Waarde and Kanat Camlibel 2021
(also see for
van Waarde et al. 2022
) provides a less conservative for constructing controllers from noisy data.
Another advantage of Finsler’s lemma over the parameterizations approach based on Willems’ fundamental lemma, which is commonly used, (such as in
De Persis and Tesi 2020
,
Berberich et al. 2021
) is that the decision variables are independent of the time horizon of the experiment as long as it is sufficiently long.
This can play an important role in control design when working with big data sets.

In this paper, we propose a data-driven MPC scheme on the data informativity framework in form of LMIs, which designs an optimal feedback control gain from the data for systems subject to uncertainty in two cases: slowly varying linear systems and Lur’e systems with uncertain nonlinearity.
This work can be considered as the corresponding data-driven results to the model-based results shown in
Kothare et al. 1996
,
Böhm et al. 2009
.
Since we base our scheme on data informativity and Finsler’s lemma, our design method does not depend on the length of the experiments (such as in
Berberich et al. 2021
), which is well suited for larger data sets.

The paper is structured as follows.
Section 2 recapitulates the main ideas of the informativity framework for direct data-driven control and the matrix Finsler’s lemma results.
We establish the LMI-based MPC scheme for the nominal case in Section 3.
In Section 4 and 5, we develop the results for the cases of slowly varying systems and Lur’e systems, respectively.
Section 6 presents numerical examples to illustrate the methods.
Finally, conclusions and outlook are given in Section 7.

2
Data informativity for control design

We first recapitulate the main ideas of the
informativity framework
for direct data-driven control proposed in
van Waarde et al. 2020
.
Let us consider an unknown system
\mathcal{S}
, which is contained in the model class
\Sigma
.
Furthermore, let us assume that we have access to a set of data set
\mathcal{D}
, which are generated by the system
\mathcal{S}
.
Given the data set
\mathcal{D}
, we define
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\Sigma
as the set of all systems that could have generated the data.
The main concern of this paper is to design a controller for the system
\mathcal{S}
from the data set
\mathcal{D}
such that the closed-loop system, which consists of the system
\mathcal{S}
and the controller, has a specific property.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be a system-theoretic property and we denote the set of all systems in
\Sigma
which have the property
\mathcal{P}
as
\Sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
.
Given a controller
\mathcal{K}
, we denote the system-theoretic property
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K})
that is associated with the controller
\mathcal{K}
.
We only can achieve our control design objective if the chosen controller
\mathcal{K}
guarantees that the closed-loop system between the controller and
any
systems from the set
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
has the specified property. This leads to the definition of
informativity for control
.

Definition 1

The data
\mathcal{D}
are informative for the property
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
if there exists a controller
\mathcal{K}
such that
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathcal{P(K)}}
.

In order to illustrate the framework, we consider a controllable and observable discrete-time linear system of the form

\quad x(k+1)=Ax(k)+Bu(k),

(1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the states and control inputs of the system.
The real matrices
A
and
B
are assumed to be unknown.
Instead, we assume that we can collect the input and states data from system (
1
), which are written in the matrices form

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=[x(0)\quad x(1)\quad\cdots x(T)],

(2)

\displaystyle U_{-}

\displaystyle=[u(0)\quad u(1)\quad\cdots u(T-1)].

We denote the shifted versions of the states as follows.

\displaystyle X_{-}

\displaystyle=[x(0)\quad x(1)\quad\cdots x(T-1)],

(3)

\displaystyle X_{+}

\displaystyle=[x(1)\quad x(2)\quad\cdots x(T)].

In this case,
\Sigma
is the model class of controllable and observable discrete-time linear systems; the ”true” system
\mathcal{S}
is corresponding to a particular value of
(A,B)
but we do not know. Then, we can define the data set
\mathcal{D}=(U_{-},X)
. Consequently, the set
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
is equal to

\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}:=\{(A,B)~|~X_{+}=AX_{-}+BU_{-}\}.

(4)

If we aim to design a controller
\mathcal{K}
in the form
u=Kx
with
K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
to stabilize the closed-loop system, then the property we want to achieve is
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{K}):
”state feedback
K
yields a stable closed-loop system.”
The corresponding set
\Sigma_{\mathcal{P(K)}}
is equal to

\Sigma_{stab}:=\{(A,B)~|~A+BK~\textrm{is stable}\}.

(5)

In this case, the data
(U_{-},X)
are
informative
for stabilization by state feedback if there exists a
K
such that
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\Sigma_{stab}
.

There are two problems that we need to answer.
The first one is whether the given data is ”sufficient” to obtain a suitable controller from them, which leads to the
informativity problem for control
.

Problem 1

Provide conditions on
\mathcal{D}
under which there exists a controller
\mathcal{K}
such that
\mathcal{D}
are informative for the property
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
.

After the existence of the controller is ensured, the next problem is how to determine such controller, which is, in the framework, referred as the
control design problem
.

Problem 2

Assume that the data
\mathcal{D}
are informative for property
\mathcal{P}(\cdot)
, find a controller
\mathcal{K}
such that
\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathcal{P(K)}}
.

In this paper, we also use the matrix Finsler’s lemma shown in
van Waarde and Kanat Camlibel 2021
.

Lemma 3

(
van Waarde and Kanat Camlibel 2021
)
Consider symmetric matrices
M,\Xi\in\mathbb{R}^{(k+l)\times(k+l)}
, partitioned as

M=\begin{bmatrix}M_{11}&\quad M_{12}\\
M_{12}^{\top}&\quad M_{22}\end{bmatrix}~\textrm{and}~\Xi=\begin{bmatrix}\Xi_{11}&\quad\Xi_{12}\\
\Xi_{12}^{\top}&\quad\Xi_{22}\end{bmatrix}.

(6)

Assume that (i)
M_{12}=0
and
M_{22}\preceq 0
, (ii)
\Xi_{22}\preceq 0
and
\Xi_{11}-\Xi_{12}\Xi_{22}^{\dagger}\Xi_{12}^{\top}=0
, (iii)
\exists G
such that
M_{11}+G^{\top}M_{22}G\succ 0
and
\Xi_{22}G=\Xi_{12}^{\top}
. Then, we have

\begin{bmatrix}I\\
Z\end{bmatrix}^{\top}M\begin{bmatrix}I\\
Z\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0~\forall Z\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times k}\textrm{such that}\begin{bmatrix}I\\
Z\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\Xi\begin{bmatrix}I\\
Z\end{bmatrix}=0

if and only if there exists
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
such that
M-\epsilon\Xi\succeq 0
.

3
Nominal LMI-based Data-Driven MPC

In this section, we consider controlling system (
1
) subject to constraints
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
u\in\mathcal{U}
where the matrices
A
and
B
are unknown, but we have access to the data (
2
).
The constraint sets satisfy the following assumption.

Assumption 1

The constraint sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{U}
can be described in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:c_{i_{x}}x\leq 1,i_{x}=1,...,\bar{n}\}

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:d_{i_{u}}u\leq 1,i_{u}=1,...,\bar{m}\},

where
c_{i_{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n}
and
d_{i_{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times m}
, and
\bar{n},\bar{m}
are the numbers of states and inputs constraints, respectively.
It should be noted that (
7
) covers the case where
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{U}
are polytopic.

We aim to find a feedback controller of the form

u=Kx.

(8)

The controller aims to minimize the infinite horizon cost functional

J(x(\cdot),u(\cdot))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{\top}(k)Qx(k)+u(k)^{\top}Ru(k),

(9)

where
Q\succ 0
and
R\succ 0
are chosen weighting matrices.

Aligned with the Definition
1
, we can define the data informativity for the case of LMI-based MPC for system (
1
).

Definition 2

Given positive definite matrices
Q\ \in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
and initial state
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
; and suppose that the data
(U_{-},X)
in (
2
) are generated by (
1
). Then, the data
(U_{-},X)
are called informative for designing the LMI-based data-driven model predictive controller if there exists
K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
such that the cost function (
9
) is optimized and the constraints (
7
) are satisfied for all
(A,B)\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{D}}
defined in (
4
).

It should be noted that the constraints (
7
) can be transformed into

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\{[x^{\top}~u^{\top}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:c_{i}x+d_{i}u\leq 1\},

(10)

where
i=1,...,r
, with
r=\bar{n}+\bar{m}
.
We need the following lemma for guaranteeing constraints satisfaction.

Lemma 4

(
Boyd et al. 1994
)
An ellipsoid

\mathcal{E}(\alpha)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{\top}Px\leq\alpha\}

(11)

is contained in the set
\mathcal{W}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:w_{i}x\leq 1,i=1,...,r\}
if and only if

w_{i}(\alpha P^{-1})w_{i}^{\top}\leq 1,i=1,...,r.

(12)

Remark 3.1

The decisive variables
N
and

as well as the constraints (
13d

) do not depend on the length
T

Remark 3.2

The optimization problem (

When solving the optimization problem online, the data can be updated by updating only the second term of the left-hand side of (

4
Robust Data-Driven MPC for Linear parameter-varying systems

The result for the nominal case in the previous section can be extended naturally to the case where the matrices
A
and
B
have bounded uncertainties or they vary inside some convex sets.
However, since we do not neither know the values of matrices
A
and
B
nor the uncertainties, we need to assume that we are somehow able to collect data that sufficiently reflect the uncertainties.

In this section, we consider the polytopic plant described by

x(k+1)=A^{(j)}x(k)+B^{(j)}u(k),\quad[A^{(j)}~B^{(j)}]\in\Omega,

(20)

where the set
\Omega
is a polytope

\Omega=\textrm{Co}\{[A_{1}~B_{1}],[A_{2}~B_{2}],...,[A_{\zeta}~B_{\zeta}]\},

(21)

and
Co
refers to the convex hull.
In other words, if
[A\quad B]\in\Omega
, then for some
\lambda_{j}>0
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{\zeta}\lambda_{j}=1
, we have

[A~B]=\sum_{j=1}^{\zeta}\lambda_{j}[A_{j}~B_{j}].

Polytopic system can be used to approximate systems working in different operating points, linear time-varying systems, linear systems with multiplicative uncertainty and nonlinear systems whose the Jacobian lies inside the polytope
\Omega
in (
21
) (
Kothare et al. 1996
).
For the case of systems with different operating points and each (unknown) pair
[A^{(j)}~B^{(j)}]
is the model of the system at each operating point, we can apply the method proposed in Section
3
at each operating point.
For uncertain linear systems or linear parameter-varying systems, we may need to design a robust control law
u=Kx
to stabilize the system as long as
[A\quad B]\in\Omega
.
However, since we do not know the vertices of the polytope
\Omega
, we need to assume that we are able to collect data that can provide us the information of them.

Assumption 2

It is possible to gather informative input-state data
(U_{-},X)^{(j)}
(referred as data set
\mathcal{D}^{(j)}
) that capture the same dynamical characteristic.

This assumption requires that for the considered parameter-varying linear system, at each vertex
j
, we can collect sufficient data for the controller design purpose.
It can be applicable for multi-mode systems, where the system works in a mode for a period of time before changing to other modes and then may change back to the first mode some time later. A robust approach is to design a controller that stabilizes the system in every mode.

The following theorem establishes the results for nominal systems.

Theorem 6

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The data sets
(U_{-},X)^{(j)}
are informative for designing the LMI-based model predictive controller if the following optimization problem is feasible:

\displaystyle\min_{0\prec N=N^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\alpha,\eta,\epsilon>0}~\alpha\quad

(22a)

subject to

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&\quad x_{0}^{\top}\\
x_{0}&\quad N\end{bmatrix}\succ 0,

(22b)

\displaystyle\small\begin{bmatrix}N-\eta I&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&N&0\\
0&0&0&L&0\\
0&N&L^{\top}&N&\Psi^{\top}\\
0&0&0&\Psi&\alpha I\\
\end{bmatrix}+\epsilon\begin{bmatrix}X_{+}^{(j)}\\
-X_{-}^{(j)}\\
-U_{-}^{(j)}\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}X_{+}^{(j)}\\
-X_{-}^{(j)}\\
-U_{-}^{(j)}\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\succ 0,

(22c)

\displaystyle\forall j=1,...,\zeta,

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}N&\quad\Psi^{\top}\\
\Psi&\quad\alpha I\end{bmatrix}\succ 0,

(22d)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&d_{i}L+c_{i}N\\
(d_{i}L+c_{i}N)^{\top}&N\end{bmatrix}\succ 0,

\displaystyle i=1,...,r,

where
\Psi:=\hat{Q}N+\hat{R}L
,
\hat{Q}=\begin{bmatrix}Q^{1/2}\\
0\end{bmatrix}
,
\hat{R}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
R^{1/2}\end{bmatrix}

When (

\alpha
is the upper bound on the infinite horizon cost functional (

{pf}

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
5
. The only difference is the condition (
22c
), which needs to hold for all the vertex of the polytope (
21
). Since
\Omega
is convex, when the condition (
22c
) holds for all the vertices, they hold for all
[A\quad B]\in\Omega

Boyd et al. 1994
,
Kothare et al. 1996

\blacksquare

5
Robust Data-Driven MPC for uncertain Lur’e-type systems

In this section, we establish the result to design a robust data-driven MPC for Lur’e systems, which are described by

x(k+1)=Ax(k)+Bu(k)+E\gamma(Hx(k)),

(23)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The matrices
A
,
B
and
E
(with appropriate dimensions) are unknown and
H
is assumed to be known.
The unknown nonlinearity
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the sector bound condition

\gamma(z)(\beta z-\gamma(z))\geq 0,~\forall z\in\mathbb{R},

7
Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper, we present a data driven predicted control scheme to design controllers under state and input constraints for two classes of systems: (i) linear systems with multiplicative uncertainty (such as slowly varying linear systems or periodic systems) and (ii) Lur’e systems with sector-bounded nonlinearity.
We developed our results based on the data informativity framework and Finsler’s lemma, and established the conditions in form of LMIs, which can be solved effectively with available toolboxes.
The advantage of this approach in comparison with other data-driven approaches based on Willems’ fundamental lemma is that decisive variables are independent of the length of the available experiment data.
The designed controller is proved to asymptotically stabilize the closed-loop system and guarantee constraints satisfaction.
The method has been illustrated via two simulation examples.
For future work, we aim to extend the approach to the cases in which the system is affected by additive bounded noise and the different classes of nonlinearity.

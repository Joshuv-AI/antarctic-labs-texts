Twisted Edge Laplacians on finite graphs from a Kähler structure
Soumalya Joardar, Atibur Rahaman

Abstract
. This is a continuation of the work done by the authors in [ 14 ] . In [ 14 ] an almost complex structure on finitely many points from bidirected polygon was introduced. In this paper we study a Kähler structure on finite points. In particular, we study the edge Laplacian of a graph twisted by the Kähler structure introduced in this paper. We also discuss a metric aspect from a twisted holomorphic Dolbeault-Dirac spectral triple and show that the points have a finite diameter with respect to Connes’ distance.

1.
Introduction

One of the exciting problems in recent times in the context of noncommutative geometry has been to extend the classical complex geometric ideas into the realm of noncommutative geometry. Although the interplay between the algebraic and analytic aspects of noncommutative complex geometry is far from well understood, the study of noncommutative complex geometry has gained some momentum in recent years. The axioms have been mainly made keeping the quantum flag manifolds in sight. The reader can see
[
12
,
9
,
3
]
for familiarizing himself/herself with such axioms and set up. Although it is worth mentioning that there are other examples and approaches to noncommuatative complex geometry (see
[
6
,
2
,
4
,
5
]
for example). After setting up basic complex geometric notions in the realm of the noncommutative geometry, keeping the success of the Kähler structure in classical complex geometry in mind, the noncommutative Kähler structure has been explored mainly in the context of quantum flag manifolds by various authors (see
[
12
,
10
,
8
]
among many others). It is worth mentioning that whether metrics have a key role to play in noncommutative complex geometry is still not very clear. The study of noncommutative complex geometry being in its relatively early days, it has become imperative to construct new examples to test any hypothesis. The authors of this paper have recently explored an almost complex structure on finitely many points in
[
14
]
. There it has been established that given a bidirected graph over finitely many points, there is a natural almost complex structure associated to it. Furthermore such almost complex structure is naturally integrable. Then a concrete example of such an almost complex structure was studied where the bomodule of one-forms came from a bidirected polygon. In this paper we continue exploring the almost complex structure and we introduce a Kähler structure. But we don’t discuss the Hodge theory here. Rather we study a twisted holomorphic Dolbeault-Dirac operator on a holomorphic hermitian module and an associated twisted edge Laplacian in the spirit of
[
10
]
. At this point it is worth mentioning that an almost complex structure has also been explored on a three point space using the spectral triple formalism in
[
13
]
. But unfortunately there is no compatible Kähler structure in their set up. Coming back to the twisted Laplacian, the twist arises from a left module on finite points which comes from a directed graph on the points. It is shown that the left module coming from a directed graph is a finitely generated projective module admitting a canonical hermitian structure (see Lemma
3.3
). A general holomorphic structure on such a module is indexed by a class of left module maps (see the discussion following Corollary
3.4
). one can restrict the Laplacian on the corresponding left module and the left module being the function space over the edges, can rightfully termed as a twisted edge Laplacian on the corresponding graph. We explore the spectrum of such a twisted edge Laplacian for regular graphs, in particular regular
k
-gons. Then we go on to consider a twisted holomorphic Dolbeaut-Dirac operator coming from a holomorphic hermitian module. Due to the finite dimensionality of the Hilbert spaces involved the adjointabilty of such a Dirac operator (any other such analytical issue) does not pose any problem and we are content with considering Connes’ distance between points coming from the twisted Dolbeault Dirac spectral triple. In particular we show that
n
-many points have finite diameter
\leq[\frac{n}{2}]
(Corollary
5.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
7
]
)
Let
{\cal A}
be a
\ast
-algebra over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.
Then a
\ast
-differential calculus over
{\cal A}
is a datum
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A}),\textup{d},\ast)
where

(i)

\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\Omega^{n}({\cal A})
is a graded
\ast
-algebra such that
\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})
is an
{\cal A}
–
{\cal A}
-bimodule with
\Omega^{0}({\cal A})={\cal A}
;

(ii)

\textup{d}:\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet+1}({\cal A})
is a graded derivation i.e.
d
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map satisfying the graded Leibniz rule

\textup{d}(\omega\wedge\eta)=\textup{d}\omega\wedge\eta+(-1)^{{\rm deg}(\omega)}\omega\wedge\textup{d}\eta,

for
\omega\in\Omega^{n}({\cal A}),\eta\in\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})
and
\textup{d}^{2}=0
;

(iii)

for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})

\textup{d}(\omega^{\ast})=(\textup{d}\omega)^{\ast}
.

Remark 2.2
.

As usual practice, we have denoted the product of the graded product by
\wedge
and with respect to the product
\wedge

\ast
is antilinear graded involution meaning that

(\omega\wedge\eta)^{\ast}=(-1)^{{\rm deg}(\omega){\rm deg}(\eta)}\eta^{\ast}\wedge\omega^{\ast}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A}),\textup{d},\ast)
be a
\ast
-differential calculus over a
\ast
-algebra
{\cal A}
. An almost complex structure on
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A}),\textup{d},\ast)
is a degree zero derivation
J:\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})
such that

1.

J
is identically zero on
{\cal A}
and hence an
{\cal A}
–
{\cal A}
-bimodule endomorphism of
\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})
;

2.

J^{2}=-{\rm Id}
on
\Omega^{1}({\cal A})
; and

3.

J(\omega^{\ast})=(J\omega)^{\ast}
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{1}({\cal A})
.

Given an almost complex structure on a
\ast
-differential calculus, the bimodule of one-forms has the following decomposition into eigenspaces of
J
:

\Omega^{1}({\cal A})=\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A})\oplus\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A}),

where
\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A}):=\{\omega\in\Omega^{1}({\cal A}):J(\omega)=i\omega\}
,
\Omega^{0,1}:=\{\omega\in\Omega^{1}({\cal A}):J(\omega)=-i\omega\}
.
\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A})
and
\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A})
are again
{\cal A}
–
{\cal A}
-bimodules and called the bimodules of
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
-forms respectively. More generally, one has the higher dimensional analogues of the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
-forms. For a fixed
n

\Omega^{n}({\cal A})
has the following decomposition into bimodules of
(p,q)
-forms for

, where

are non negative integers(
[
3
]
*Sec. 2.5):

\Omega^{n}({\cal A})=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\Omega^{p,q}({\cal A}),

where
\Omega^{p,q}({\cal A}):=\{\omega\in\Omega^{n}({\cal A}):J(\omega)=i(p-q)\omega\}
. In the following definition we are going to use the Lefschetz operators. For the definition of the Lefschetz operators the reader is referred to
[
12
]
(see Definition 4.1).

Definition 2.4
.

Given an almost complex structure
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A}),\textup{d},\ast,J)
, a Kähler form
\kappa
is a central closed
(1,1)
form such that with respect to Lefschetz map
L:\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\rightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})
given by
\omega\mapsto\kappa\wedge\omega
, isomorphisms are given by

L^{n-k}:\Omega^{k}\rightarrow\Omega^{2n-k},\ for\ all\ 0\leq k<n.

Given a Kähler-structure with the associated Kähler-form
\kappa
, one can define the Hodge
\ast
-map
\ast_{\kappa}
which we do not recall here. The reader is referred to Section 2.3 of
[
10
]
for details of the Hodge
\ast
-map on a Kähler structure. Now a metric can be defined using the Hodge star map.

Definition 2.5
.

The canonical Hermitian metric associated to the Kähler structure
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A}),\textup{d},J,\kappa)
is the unique map
g_{\kappa}:\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\times\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\rightarrow{\cal A}
such that
g_{\kappa}(\Omega^{k},\Omega^{l})=0
for
k\neq l
and

g_{\kappa}(\omega,\eta)=\ast_{\kappa}(\omega\wedge\ast_{\kappa}(\eta^{\ast})).

The Hermitian metric
g_{\kappa}
is said to be
positive definite
if
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)
belongs to the positive cone
{\cal A}^{+}:={\rm Sp}\{a^{\ast}a:a\in{\cal A}\}
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})
.

We are interested in a Kähler structure on finitely many points. For that purpose we recall the almost complex structure coming from the bidirected polygon on

\displaystyle\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A})={\rm Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu+1}\}_{\mu=1,2,\ldots,n}

\displaystyle\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A})={\rm Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\xi_{\mu+1\rightarrow\mu}\}_{\mu=1,2,\ldots,n}

\displaystyle\Omega^{2,0}({\cal A})=\Omega^{0,2}({\cal A})=0

\displaystyle\Omega^{1,1}({\cal A})={\rm Sp}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu-1}\wedge\xi_{\mu-1\rightarrow\mu}\}_{\mu=1,2,\ldots,n}.

Lemma 2.6
.

On the almost complex structure on finitely many points coming from the bidirected polygon graph the form
\kappa=i\sum_{\mu\in V}\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu-1}\wedge\xi_{\mu-1\rightarrow\mu}
is a Kähler form.

Proof.

By construction
\kappa
is an
(1,1)
-form and central. As

,
\kappa
is closed i.e.

. Recalling that for any edge

\Box

Lemma 2.8
.

The canonical Hermitian metric
g_{\kappa}
corresponding to the Kähler form
\kappa
is positive definite.

Proof.

Clearly it suffices to show that for any
\omega\in\Omega^{k}({\cal A})
,
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)\in{\cal A}_{+}
for
k=0,1,2
. For
k=0
and

,
g_{\kappa}(f,f)=\ast_{\kappa}(f\wedge\ast_{\kappa}(f^{\ast}))=|f|^{2}\in{\cal A}^{+}
. For an
\omega=\sum c_{\mu}\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu+1}\wedge\xi_{\mu+1\rightarrow\mu}\in\Omega^{1,1}({\cal A})
,

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ast_{\kappa}(\omega\wedge\ast_{\kappa}(\omega^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f,

where
f(\mu)=|c_{\mu}|^{2}
so that
f\in{\cal A}^{+}
. As
\Omega^{2,0}({\cal A})=\Omega^{0,2}({\cal A})=0
, we have
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)\in{\cal A}^{+}
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{2}({\cal A})
.

Now note that for any
\omega\in\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A})
and
\eta\in\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A})
,
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\eta)=0=g_{\kappa}(\eta,\omega)
. With this observation for one forms it is enough to show that
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)\in{\cal A}^{+}
for
\omega\in\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A})
or
\omega\in\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A})
. So let
\omega=\sum{c_{\mu}}\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu+1}\in\Omega^{1,0}({\cal A})
where

,

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ast_{\kappa}(\omega\wedge\ast_{\sigma}(\omega^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ast_{\kappa}(-i\sum|c_{\mu}|^{2}\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu+1}\wedge\xi_{\mu+1\rightarrow\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f,

where
f(\mu)=|c_{\mu}|^{2}
and therefore
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)\in{\cal A}^{+}
. For
\omega=\sum c_{\mu}\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu-1}\in\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A})

\displaystyle g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ast_{\kappa}(\omega\wedge\ast_{\kappa}(\omega^{\ast}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ast_{\kappa}(i\sum|c_{\mu}|^{2}\xi_{\mu\rightarrow\mu-1}\wedge\xi_{\mu-1\rightarrow\mu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f,

where
f(\mu)=|c_{\mu}|^{2}
and therefore
g_{\kappa}(\omega,\omega)\in{\cal A}^{+}
for all
\omega\in\Omega^{1}({\cal A})

\Box

3.
The twisted edge Laplacian

In this section we shall consider Hermitian holomorphic modules over finitely many points. In particular we shall focus on such modules arising from finite directed graphs on finitely many points. To that end we start by recalling the definitions of holomorphic and hermitian modules over a
\ast
-algebra.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{\cal A}
be a
\ast
-algebra admitting an almost complex structure. A holomorphic module over
{\cal A}
is a pair
(\cal{E},\bar{\partial}_{\cal{E}})
where
\cal{E}
is a finitely generated projective left module over
{\cal A}
and
\bar{\partial}_{\cal{E}}:\cal{E}\rightarrow\Omega^{0,1}({\cal A})\bar{\ \otimes\ }\cal{E}
is a flat
(0,1)
-connection.

In the following definition and throughout the rest of the paper, for a left module
\cal{E}
over an algebra
{\cal A}
, we denote the set of all left module maps from
\cal{E}\rightarrow{\cal A}
by
{}^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
which has a natural right module structure (see
[
10
]
). For the next definition the reader is urged to consult section 2.6 of
[
10
]
for the definition (as well as the right module structure) of the conjugate module
\bar{\cal{E}}
.

Definition 3.2
.

A hermitian module over an algebra
{\cal A}
is a pair
(\cal{E},h_{\cal{E}})
where
\cal{E}
is a finitely generated projective left module and a right
{\cal A}
-isomorphism
h_{\cal{E}}:\bar{\cal{E}}\rightarrow^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
where
\bar{\cal{E}}
is the conjugate right
{\cal A}
-module such that for the associated sesquilinear pairing
h_{\cal{E}}(-,-):\cal{E}\times\cal{E}\rightarrow{\cal A}
given by
(e,k)\mapsto h_{\cal{E}}(\bar{k})(e)
satisfies

h_{\cal{E}}(e,k)=h_{\cal{E}}(k,e)^{\ast}\quad\text{ and }\quad h_{\cal{E}}(e,e)\in{\cal A}^{+}.

Given a holomorphic, hermitian module over a
\ast
-algebra
{\cal A}
admitting a Kähler structure
(\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A}),\textup{d},J,\kappa)
, recall the map
g_{\cal{E}}:\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\bar{\ \otimes\ }\cal{E}\times\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\bar{\ \otimes\ }\cal{E}\rightarrow{\cal A}
from
[
10
]
(see discussion after Definition 2.3). If the
\ast
-algebra
{\cal A}
admits a faithful state
\tau:{\cal A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, then there is a
\mathbb{C}
-valued inner product
\langle-,-\rangle_{\cal{E}}
on
\Omega^{\bullet}({\cal A})\bar{\ \otimes\ }\cal{E}
given by
\tau\circ g_{\cal{E}}
provided
g_{\cal{E}}
is positive definite.

Lemma 3.3
.

Every left module coming from a finite directed graph on finite points is a finitely generated, projective Hermitian module. Moreover, the Hermitian structure is positive definite.

Proof.

Let us denote the left
C(V)
-module
C(E)
by
\cal{E}
. For any finite graph
(V,E)
, we denote the function on
E
which takes the value
1
on a particular edge
e\in E
and
0
on other edges by
\xi_{e}
. Then
\{\xi_{e}\}_{e\in E}
forms a
\mathbb{C}
-linear basis of
C(E)
. As
\cal{E}
is a finite dimensional complex vector space and therefore finitely generated as a left module. To prove that
\cal{E}
is projective, note that the by Prposition 3.2 of
[
15
]
complete graph on the vertex set
V
is a free left module over
C(V)
. If the number of vertices is
n
, let us denote the complete graph on
V
by
K_{n}
and the corresponding edge set by
E_{n}
. We define a surjective idempotent
I:C(E_{n})\rightarrow\cal{E}
by the following formula:

I(\sum_{e\in E_{n}}\lambda_{e}\xi_{e})=\sum_{e^{\prime}\in E}\lambda_{e^{\prime}}\xi_{e^{\prime}}.

Clearly
I
is a left module morphism and
I(C(E_{n}))\cong\cal{E}
as left modules and
C(E_{n})\cong\cal{E}\oplus(1-I)(C(E_{n}))
, proving that
\cal{E}
is a projective module. Now we shall define a canonical Hermitian structure on
\cal{E}
. To that end let us define a right
C(V)
-module isomorphism
h:\bar{\cal{E}}\rightarrow^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
satisfying the conditions Definition
3.2
. We claim that
{}^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
is a finite dimensional vector space with dimension equal to the number of edges in the graph
(V,E)
. For
e\in E
and
\Phi\in^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
, we have

\Phi(\xi_{e})=\sum_{v\in V}\lambda_{v}\delta_{v}.

For any
w\neq s(e)
,
\delta_{w}\xi_{e}=0
and consequently
\Phi(\delta_{w}\xi_{e})=0
. On the other hand, as
\Phi
is a left
C(V)
-module map,

\displaystyle\Phi(\delta_{w}\xi_{e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{w}\Phi(\xi_{e})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{w}\sum_{v\in V}\lambda_{v}\delta_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{w}\delta_{w}.

Therefore for any vertex
w\neq s(e)
,
\lambda_{w}=0
. Hence
\Phi(\xi_{e})=\lambda_{s(e)}\delta_{s(e)}
for some
\lambda_{s(e)}\in\mathbb{C}
. For each
e\in E
, we define
\Phi^{e}\in^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
by the following:

\displaystyle\Phi^{e}(\xi_{f}):=\delta_{e,f}\delta_{s(e)}.

Then it can be shown that
\{\Phi^{e}:e\in E\}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear basis for
{}^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
. Now we define
h:\bar{\cal{E}}\rightarrow^{\vee}\!\!\!\mathcal{E}
by

h(\bar{\xi_{e}})=\Phi^{e}
. Clearly this is a vector space isomorphism. To show that this is a right
C(V)
-isomorphism, it suffices to show that
h
is a right
C(V)
-linear map. To that end let
\phi\in C(V)
. Then
h(\bar{\xi_{e}}.\phi)=h(\bar{\phi^{\ast}\xi_{e}})=\phi(s(e))\Phi^{e}
.
But for any
f\in E
,
(\Phi^{e}.\phi)(\xi_{f})=\delta_{e,f}\phi\delta_{s(e)}
. On the other hand,

\phi(s(e))\Phi^{e}(\xi_{f})=\delta_{e,f}\phi(s(e))\delta_{s(e)},

and therefore

h(\bar{\xi_{e}}.\phi)=\Phi^{e}.\phi=h(\bar{\xi_{e}}).\phi.

Let
\xi=\sum_{e\in E}\lambda_{e}\xi_{e},\ \eta=\sum_{e\in E}\mu_{e}\xi_{e}\in C(E)
. Then by definition of the seequiliniear pairing
h_{\cal{E}}(-,-)
,

\displaystyle h_{\cal{E}}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle h(\bar{\eta})(\xi)

\displaystyle\ =

\displaystyle\sum_{e}\bar{\mu_{e}}\lambda_{e}\delta_{s(e)}.

Now it is clear that
h_{\cal{E}}(\xi,\eta)=h_{\cal{E}}(\eta,\xi)^{\ast}
and
h_{\cal{E}}(\xi,\xi)=\sum_{e\in E}|\lambda_{e}|^{2}\delta_{s(e)}\in C(V)^{+}
.

\Box

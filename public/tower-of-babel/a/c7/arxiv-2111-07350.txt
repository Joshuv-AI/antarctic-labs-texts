A modular equality for m m –ovoids of elliptic quadrics
Alexander L. Gavrilyuk and Klaus Metsch and Francesco Pavese

Abstract
An m m -ovoid of a finite polar space 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is a set 𝒪 \mathcal{O} of points such that every maximal subspace of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} contains exactly m m points of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} . In the case when 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is an elliptic quadric 𝒬 − ​ ( 2 ​ r + 1 , q ) \mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q) of rank r r in 𝔽 q 2 ​ r + 2 \mathbb{F}_{q}^{2r+2} , we prove that an m m -ovoid exists only if m m satisfies a certain modular equality, which depends on q q and r r . This condition rules out many of the possible values of m m . Previously, only a lower bound on m m was known, which we slightly improve as a byproduct of our method. We also obtain a characterization of the m m -ovoids of 𝒬 − ​ ( 7 , q ) \mathcal{Q}^{-}(7,q) for q = 2 q=2 and ( m , q ) = ( 4 , 3 ) (m,q)=(4,3) .

Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a finite connected regular graph on
v
vertices and of valency
k
,
Y
a proper subset of the vertex set of
\Gamma
. If
\theta^{+}
and
\theta^{-}
denote the second largest and least eigenvalues of
\Gamma
respectively, then the number
N
of ordered pairs of adjacent vertices of
Y
satisfies (see
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
11
, Proposition 3.8]
)

\theta^{-}|Y|+\frac{k-\theta^{-}}{v}|Y|^{2}\leq N\leq\theta^{+}|Y|+\frac{k-\theta^{+}}{v}|Y|^{2}.

(1.1)

The case of equality in Eq. (
1.1
) often gives rise to interesting combinatorial objects;
in particular, when
\Gamma
is related to
incidence structures in finite geometry.

Let
{\rm PG}(n,q)
denote the projective space of dimension
n
with underlying vector space
V:=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q
elements. For a non-degenerate quadratic (or reflexive sesquilinear) form
f
on
V
, the
classical polar space

\mathcal{P}
associated with
f
is the incidence structure formed by the totally singular (or totally isotropic, respectively) subspaces with respect to
f
; their incidence is defined by symmetrized containment
[
8
,
19
]
. We consider the elements of
\mathcal{P}
as subspaces of
{\rm PG}(n,q)
, so they are projective points, lines, etc. A maximal subspace of
\mathcal{P}
has dimension
r-1
, where
r
is the Witt index of
f
, also called the
rank
of
\mathcal{P}
; such a subspace is called a
generator
. (For further details, the reader is referred to Section
2
.)

A set
\mathcal{O}
of points of
\mathcal{P}
is an
m
-ovoid
if every generator of
\mathcal{P}
meets
\mathcal{O}
in exactly
m
points. Equivalently, if
\Gamma
is the collinearity graph of a finite polar space
\mathcal{P}
, then a set
Y
that attains equality in the left-hand inequality of Eq. (
1.1
) is an
m
-ovoid of
\mathcal{P}
, for some natural
m
(see
[
3
,
11
]
). The notion of
m
-ovoids, which goes back to a classical work of B. Segre
[
24
]
, was introduced by J. Thas for generalized quadrangles in
[
26
]
, and extended to finite polar spaces of higher rank in
[
25
]
. A set of points of
\mathcal{P}
is called
tight

[
12
]
if it attains equality in the right-hand side of Eq. (
1.1
).
In
[
3
]
a uniform algebraic framework for ovoids and tight sets was developed, and their connections with various geometric objects were explored.

The central problem concerning
m
-ovoids in a polar space
\mathcal{P}
is to determine the values of
m
, for which
\mathcal{P}
possesses an
m
-ovoid.
In this paper, we focus on the elliptic polar spaces (quadrics)
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
of rank
r
, which arise from a nondegenerate orthogonal form of Witt index
r
in the
(2r+2)
-dimensional vector space
V
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. An
m
-ovoid of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
has
m(q^{r+1}+1)
points, and it is said to be
trivial
if it is empty (
m=0
) or consists of all points of the space (
m=\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}
). As the complement of an
m
-ovoid is an
\left(\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}-m\right)
-ovoid, one may assume
m\leq\frac{q^{r}-1}{2(q-1)}
.

In 1965 Segre
[
24
, p. 162]
proved that if an elliptic quadric
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(5,q)
of
{\rm PG}(5,q)
,
q
odd, has an
m
-ovoid, then
m=\frac{q+1}{2}
, and he called such a
\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)
-ovoid a
hemisystem
. He also constructed a hemisystem of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(5,3)
, admitting a group isomorphic to
{\rm PSL}(3,4)
. In
[
7
]
, by extending Segre’s result, it was shown that
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(5,q)
,
q
even, possesses no non-trivial
m
-ovoids, see also
[
18
, Section 19.3]
. Several constructions of hemisystems of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(5,q)
have been presented in the literature in the last fifteen years
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
10
,
9
,
23
]
. However, not much is known about
m
-ovoids of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
for
r>2
. It was shown in
[
3
, Theorem 13]
that if an
m
-ovoid of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
exists, then
m\geq(\sqrt{4q^{r+1}+9}-3)/(2q-2)
(see also Remark
1.2
below).

Here we obtain the following (non-existence) result for
m
-ovoids of elliptic quadrics.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
possesses an
m
-ovoid, then

F(m)\equiv 0\pmod{q+1},

(1.2)

where

F(m)=\begin{cases}m^{2}-m&\mbox{~if~}r\mbox{~is odd,}\\
m^{2}&\mbox{~if~}r\mbox{~is even and~}q\mbox{~is even,}\\
m^{2}+\frac{q+1}{2}m&\mbox{~if~}r\mbox{~is even and~}q\mbox{~is odd.}\end{cases}

An analysis of Eq. (
1.2
)
in Section
3
shows that the admissible values of
m
for an
m
-ovoid of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
are asymptotically rare.

Note that certain properties of
m
-ovoids of elliptic quadrics mirror those of tight sets of hyperbolic quadrics
\mathcal{Q}^{+}(2r+1,q)
; in fact, a result of a similar spirit as Theorem
1.1
was shown for tight sets of hyperbolic quadrics
[
16
,
17
,
15
]
. Tight sets, the counterpart of
m
-ovoids, have been studied intensely in recent years (perhaps, with the main focus on those in the hyperbolic quadric
\mathcal{Q}^{+}(5,q)
, also known as the
Cameron-Liebler line classes
in
{\rm PG}(3,q)
,
see
[
14
]
and references therein).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
occupies Sections
2
and
3
,
and it is based on the following approach.
Fix a maximal flag
P_{0}\subset\ell_{0}\subset\pi_{0}\subset\ldots\subset\Pi_{0}
in
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
and define a sequence of quotient polar spaces:
\mathcal{Q}_{r-1}
in
P_{0}^{\perp}/P_{0}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{r-2}
in
\ell_{0}^{\perp}/\ell_{0}
, etc., induced by
\mathcal{Q}_{r}:=\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)
. Suppose that
\mathcal{O}
is an
m
-ovoid of
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
, and

\mu_{0}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{r}\to\mathbb{Z}
is
the characteristic function of
\mathcal{O}
.
In Section
2
, we show that
\mu_{0}
induces a function
\mu_{1}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{r-1}\to\mathbb{Z}
, called a
weighted ovoid
of
\mathcal{Q}_{r-1}
,
which in some sense generalizes the notion of an
m
-ovoid. Furthermore, such a function
\mu_{i}
induces a weighted ovoid
\mu_{i+1}\colon\mathcal{Q}_{r-i-1}\to\mathbb{Z}
, for every
i=1,\ldots,r-2
.
Put
\|\mu_{i}\|^{2}:=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r-i}}\big(\mu_{i}(P)\big)^{2}
, and note that
\|\mu_{0}\|^{2}
is simply equal to
|\mathcal{O}|=m(q^{r+1}+1)
. We prove that, for
i=0,\ldots,r-2
,
\|\mu_{i}\|^{2}
can be expressed via
\|\mu_{i+1}\|^{2}
. Arguing by induction on
i

implies that
\|\mu_{1}\|^{2}\equiv G(q,r,m)\pmod{q+1}
, where
G
is a certain function. On the other hand, as
\|\mu_{0}\|^{2}=m(q^{r+1}+1)
,
applying the induction step with

shows that
\|\mu_{1}\|^{2}
is another function in

, say
\|\mu_{1}\|^{2}=E(q,r,m)
.
Thus,

should have an integer solution in

, which, as shown in Section

, gives the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
.

Finally, in Section
4
, by using the technique developed in Section

, we slightly improve the above-mentioned lower bound for

from
[
3
, Theorem 13]
. We also provide a complete classification of the
m
-ovoids of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(7,2)
and a characterization of the
4
-ovoids of
\mathcal{Q}^{-}(7,3)
.

Let us consider an elliptic polar space (quadric)
\mathcal{Q}_{r}:=\mathcal{Q}^{-}(2r+1,q)

{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)

f({\bf x}):=f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{2r+1})=x_{0}x_{1}+\cdots+x_{2r-2}x_{2r-1}+g(x_{2r},x_{2r+1}),\quad{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2r+2},

\mathcal{Q}_{r}
is

k_{r}:=\frac{(q^{r}-1)(q^{r+1}+1)}{q-1}.

The associated bilinear form
B({\bf x},{\bf y}):=f({\bf x}+{\bf y})-f({\bf x})-f({\bf y})
defines the
polarity

\perp
of
{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)
.
Two points

of
{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)

B({\bf x},{\bf y})=0
. Moreover, two orthogonal points

either coincide,
X=Y

\mathcal{Q}_{r}

For a point

{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)

{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)

\mathcal{Q}_{r}

if and only if

\Pi

. We use the term (
j
-)

to denote a (

is a projective space whose points, lines, etc. are the subspaces of
{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)
of dimension
j+1
,
j+2
, etc., containing
\Pi
.

Furthermore, when a point
P
belongs to
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
,
P^{\perp}
is a tangent hyperplane
\cong{\rm PG}(2r,q)
and

is the quotient projective space

\mathcal{Q}_{r}

Tight sets and

\mathcal{O}

|P^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{O}|=\begin{cases}(m-1)(q^{r}+1)+1&\mbox{ if }P\in\mathcal{O},\\
m(q^{r}+1)&\mbox{ if }P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus\mathcal{O}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Let
\mu\colon{\rm PG}(2r+1,q)\to\mathbb{Z}
be a function defined on the points of

\mu(X)=\mu(X\cap\mathcal{Q}_{r})

\mu
is said to be a

m
-)ovoid
of

\mu(P^{\perp})+q^{r}\mu(P)=m(q^{r}+1)\mbox{ for every point }P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r},

and it immediately follows from Eq. (

) that the
(0,1)
-characteristic function of an

m
-ovoid.

Lemma 2.1

Let

(a)

\mu(\mathcal{Q}_{r})=m(q^{r+1}+1)

(a) Computing in two ways the sum of

\mathcal{E}

(P,R)
such that a point
P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}
, a point
R\notin P_{0}^{\perp}
, and
P\in R^{\perp}
. We will count in two ways the following quantity

\displaystyle S=\sum_{(P,R)\in\mathcal{E}}\mu(P)\mu(R).

For a fixed point
R\in\mathcal{Q}_{r}\setminus P_{0}^{\perp}
, applying Lemma
2.1
(c) to the line
\langle P_{0},R\rangle
gives

\sum_{(P,R)\in\mathcal{E}}\mu(P)=m(q^{r-1}+1)-q^{r-1}\left(\mu(R)+\mu(P_{0})\right).

Hence, since
\sum_{R\notin P_{0}^{\perp}}\mu(R)=q^{r}\left(m(q-1)+\mu(P_{0})\right)
holds by Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{R\notin P_{0}^{\perp}}\left(m(q^{r-1}+1)-q^{r-1}\mu(R)-q^{r-1}\mu(P_{0})\right)\mu(R)

\displaystyle=q^{r-1}\left(\left(m(q^{r-1}+1)-q^{r-1}\mu(P_{0})\right)\left(mq(q-1)+q\mu(P_{0})\right)-\sum_{R\notin P_{0}^{\perp}}\mu(R)^{2}\right).

(2.4)

On the other hand, for a fixed point
P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r}\cap\left(P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}\right)
, the quantity
\sum_{(P,R)\in\mathcal{E}}\mu(R)
equals
\mu(P^{\perp})-\mu\left(\ell_{P}^{\perp}\right)
, where
\ell_{P}
denotes the line of
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
joining
P_{0}
and
P
. Set

\displaystyle S_{1}=\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)\mu(P^{\perp})\;\mbox{ and }\;S_{2}=\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)\mu\left(\ell_{P}^{\perp}\right).\quad\mbox{Then }S=S_{1}-S_{2}.

Since
\mu(P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\})=\left(m-\mu(P_{0})\right)(q^{r}+1)
holds by Eq. (
2.2
), we evaluate
S_{1}
as follows:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\left(m(q^{r}+1)-q^{r}\mu(P)\right)\mu(P)

\displaystyle=m(q^{r}+1)^{2}\left(m-\mu(P_{0})\right)-q^{r}\cdot\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)^{2}.

To evaluate
S_{2}
, observe that

\mu\left(\ell_{P}^{\perp}\right)=m(q^{r-1}+1)-q^{r-1}\mu(\ell_{P})
by Eq. (
2.2
). Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)\left(m(q^{r-1}+1)-q^{r-1}\mu(\ell_{P})\right)

\displaystyle=m(q^{r-1}+1)\left(m-\mu(P_{0})\right)(q^{r}+1)-q^{r-1}\cdot\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)\mu(\ell_{P}).

Further,

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)\mu(\ell_{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathsf{L}(P_{0})}\left(\mu(\ell)-\mu(P_{0})\right)\mu(\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in\mathsf{L}(P_{0})}\left(\mu(P_{0})\left(\mu(\ell)-\mu(P_{0})\right)+\mu_{P_{0}}^{\downarrow}(\ell)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu(P_{0})\left(\mu\left(P_{0}^{\perp}\right)-\mu(P_{0})\right)+\sum_{\ell\in\mathsf{L}(P_{0})}\mu_{P_{0}}^{\downarrow}(\ell)^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu(P_{0})\left(m-\mu(P_{0})\right)(q^{r}+1)+\|\mu_{P_{0}}^{\downarrow}\|^{2}.

Thus, we finally obtain

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=m(q^{r-1}+1)\left(m-\mu(P_{0})\right)(q^{r}+1)-q^{r-1}\left(\mu(P_{0})\left(m-\mu(P_{0})\right)(q^{r}+1)+\|\mu_{P_{0}}^{\downarrow}\|^{2}\right)

so

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=S_{1}-S_{2}

\displaystyle=m(m-\mu(P_{0}))q^{r-1}(q^{r}+1)(q-1)+\mu(P_{0})(m-\mu(P_{0}))q^{r-1}(q^{r}+1)

\displaystyle-q^{r}\cdot\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}\mu(P)^{2}+q^{r-1}\cdot\|\mu_{P_{0}}^{\downarrow}\|^{2}.

(2.5)

Equating Eqs. (
2
) and (
2.5
) and simplifying the result completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Corollary 2.4

For

and every weighted
m
-ovoid
\mu

\mathcal{Q}_{r}

\|\mu\|^{2}\equiv\begin{cases}-2qm^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r}+1)m&\mbox{ if }r\mbox{ is even }\\
(q^{2}+1)m^{2}&\mbox{ if }r\mbox{ is odd }\end{cases}\mod{2(q+1)}.

Proof.

In this proof
\equiv
stands for equivalence modulo
2(q+1)
. We prove the assertion by induction on

\mu(P)=m
for each of the

\mathcal{Q}_{1}

Now suppose that

P_{0}
be any point of
\mathcal{Q}_{r}

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}(q+1)\mu(P)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{P\in P_{0}^{\perp}\setminus\{P_{0}\}}(q+1)\mu(P)\equiv(q+1)(q^{r}+1)\big(m-x\big).

Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r}}\mu(P)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv x^{2}+\big(x+m(q-1)\big)^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r}+1)\big(m-x\big)-\sum_{\ell\in\mathsf{L}(P_{0})}\mu^{\downarrow}_{P_{0}}(\ell)^{2}.

Now we apply the induction hypothesis to the quadric
P_{0}^{\perp}/P_{0}
(with point-set
\mathsf{L}(P_{0})
) induced by
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
and the weighted
(m-x)
-ovoid
\mu^{\downarrow}_{P_{0}}
of
\mathcal{Q}_{r-1}

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r}}\mu(P)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle x^{2}+\big(x+m(q-1)\big)^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r}+1)\big(m-x\big)-(q^{2}+1)(m-x)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(1-q^{2})x^{2}+2xmq(q+1)-x(q+1)(q^{r}+1)-2qm^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r}+1)m

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-2qm^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r}+1)m

where we use

in the last step.
When

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in\mathcal{Q}_{r}}\mu(P)^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle x^{2}+\big(x+m(q-1)\big)^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r}+1)\big(m-x\big)

\displaystyle+2q(m-x)^{2}-(q+1)(q^{r-1}+1)(m-x)

\displaystyle 2(q+1)x^{2}-2xm(q+1)+m^{2}(q^{2}+1)\equiv m^{2}(q^{2}+1)

3
A modular equality for

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an

-ovoid of
\mathcal{Q}_{r}
,

Lemma 3.1

The following holds:

The result follows from Lemma
2.3
applied to
\chi

for any point
P

∎

The following lemma immediately follows from Corollary
2.4
applied to

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\equiv
denote equivalence modulo

.
The following holds:

\|\chi^{\downarrow}_{P_{0}}\|^{2}\equiv\begin{cases}-2q\left(m-\chi(P_{0})\right)^{2}+(q+1)(q^{r-1}+1)\left(m-\chi(P_{0})\right)&\mbox{ if }r\mbox{ is odd,}\\
(q^{2}+1)\left(m-\chi(P_{0})\right)^{2}&\mbox{ if }r\mbox{ is even.}\end{cases}

(3.2)

1.1

Proof.

Fix a point

We now determine the number of solutions of Eq. (

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

(a)

(d)

Let

. From the Chinese Remainder Theorem we have that

If

Abstract




1
Introduction

In the previous paper
[
1
]
, we have studied the time evolution of the lepton number carried by Majorana neutrinos in the Heisenberg picture.
In the present paper, we investigate the same problem in the
the Schrödinger picture.
One can compute the expectation value
of the physical observables at arbitrary time in both Heisenberg picture and
Schrödinger
picture and they are equivalent in this aspect. However,
in order to find how the state evolves with respect to time, it is desirable to formulate the previous framework in the Schrödinger picture.

The operator relation between neutrino/anti-neutrino and Majorana neutrino obtained
in
[
1
]
is expressed as a Bogolyubov transformation

[
2
]
.
Then the two vacua, one which is annihilated by the operators assigned with definite lepton number
and the other annihilated by the operators with definite mass, are related by the transformation.
Using the relation, the initial state with the definite lepton number can be expressed
in terms of the operators with the definite mass applied on the vacuum
annihilated by the operators with definite mass. Then the time evolution of the
initial state can be derived and the expectation value of the lepton number is also
obtained.

The Bogolyubov transformation is applied to the oscillation phenomena
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
.
The Majorana neutrino as a Bogolyubov quasi-particle is also suggested in the literature

[
5
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In section 2, we introduce the Bogolyubov transformation to relate the operator for mass eigenstate to the operator with the definite lepton number. In section 3, the initial
state is given. The time evolution of the state and the expectation value of lepton number are
obtained in section 4.
The numerical calculation is also shown.
Section 5 is devoted to the conclusion and the derivation of
the Bogolyubov transformation and the initial state are given in appendixes.

2
Continuity condition and The Bogolyubov Transformation

In the previous paper
[
1
]
, we consider the situation that the Majorana mass term is turned on at
t=0
by a time step function. The following Lagrangian corresponds to
that situation, but for the single
flavor case,

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\overline{\nu_{L}}i\partial_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}\nu_{L}-\theta(t)\frac{m}{2}\{\overline{(\nu_{L})^{c}}{\nu_{L}}+h.c.\}.

(1)

Then the equation of motion leads to a continuity condition at
t=\pm\epsilon
,

\displaystyle\nu_{L}(t=-\epsilon,{\bf x})=P_{L}\psi_{M}(t=+\epsilon,{\bf x}),

(2)

where
P_{L}
denotes the left-handed projection
\frac{1-\gamma_{5}}{2}
. The left-hand side of Eq.(
2
) is expanded by on-shell massless spinors. Their
coefficients are the annihilation operator denoted by
a(\bf p)
for a neutrino
and the creation operator for an anti-neutrino denoted by
b^{\dagger}(\bf p)
. The right-hand side is expanded by massive on-shell spinors. Their coefficients correspond
to annihilation and creation operators for massive Majorana field
denoted by
a^{(\dagger)}_{M}({\bf p},\lambda)
where
\lambda
denotes the helicities
\lambda=\pm
.
The non-zero momentum
{\bf p}
can be always split into two regions. A hemisphere region is called as
A
region and the other hemisphere region is called as
\bar{A}
. (See for the details in
[
6
]
.) The continuity condition is transfered to the following relations between the operators,

\displaystyle\frac{a(\textbf{p})}{\sqrt{2p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{E_{p}+p}}{2E_{p}}\left(a_{M}(\textbf{p},-)+\frac{im}{E_{p}+p}a^{\dagger}_{M}(-\textbf{p},-)\right),

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{a(-\textbf{p})}{\sqrt{2p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{E_{p}+p}}{2E_{p}}\left(a_{M}(-\textbf{p},-)-\frac{im}{E_{p}+p}a^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{p},-)\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{b(\textbf{p})}{\sqrt{2p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{E_{p}+p}}{2E_{p}}\left(a_{M}(\textbf{p},+)+\frac{im}{E_{p}+p}a^{\dagger}_{M}(-\textbf{p},+)\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{b(-\textbf{p})}{\sqrt{2p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{E_{p}+p}}{2E_{p}}\left(a_{M}(-\textbf{p},+)-\frac{im}{E_{p}+p}a^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{p},+)\right),

(6)

where
p=|\textbf{p}|
,
E_{p}=\sqrt{p^{2}+m^{2}}
and
\textbf{p}\in A
.
To further simplify relations in Eq.(
2
),
we introduce the momentum dependent angle
\phi_{p}

, which is related to the velocity
v_{p}
as
v_{p}=\frac{p}{E_{p}}=\cos 2\phi_{p}
.
Then we rewrite the operator relations in Eqs.(
3
-
6
),

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a(\textbf{p})\\
a^{\dagger}(-\textbf{p})\end{array}\right)=\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\phi_{p}&i\sin\phi_{p}\\
i\sin\phi_{p}&\cos\phi_{p}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{M}(\textbf{p},-)\\
a_{M}^{\dagger}(-\textbf{p},-)\end{array}\right),

(7)

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b(\textbf{p})\\
b^{\dagger}(-\textbf{p})\end{array}\right)=\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\phi_{p}&i\sin\phi_{p}\\
i\sin\phi_{p}&\cos\phi_{p}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{M}(\textbf{p},+)\\
a_{M}^{\dagger}(-\textbf{p},+)\end{array}\right).

(8)

The above relations can be expressed by using the transformation

T(\phi_{p})
defined below,

\displaystyle T(\phi_{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp{[i\phi_{p}(g_{p,+}+g_{p,-})]},

(9)

\displaystyle g_{p,\lambda}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda)\alpha_{M}(-\textbf{p},\lambda)+\alpha^{\dagger}_{M}(-\textbf{p},\lambda)\alpha^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda),

(10)

where
g_{p,\pm}
denote the generators of the transformation. Note that we have
introduced the dimensionless operators,

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{a_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda)}{\sqrt{2E_{p}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(0)}}.

(11)

They satisfy the anti-commutation relations
for
\textbf{p}=\textbf{q}
,

\displaystyle\{\alpha_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda),\alpha^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{q},\lambda^{\prime})\}=\delta_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}.

(12)

Using the transformation
T(\phi_{p})
in Eq.(
9
), one can rewrite the relations in Eqs.(
7
-
8
) between massless operators and massive ones as,

\displaystyle a(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}T(\phi_{p})a_{M}(\textbf{p},-)T^{-1}(\phi_{p}),

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}\left(\cos\phi_{p}a_{M}(\textbf{p},-)+i\sin\phi_{p}a_{M}^{\dagger}(-\textbf{p},-)\right),

\displaystyle a(-\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}T(\phi_{p})a_{M}(-\textbf{p},-)T^{-1}(\phi_{p}),

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}\left(\cos\phi_{p}a_{M}(-\textbf{p},-)-i\sin\phi_{p}a_{M}^{\dagger}(\textbf{p},-)\right),

\displaystyle b(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}T(\phi_{p})a_{M}(\textbf{p},+)T^{-1}(\phi_{p}),

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}\left(\cos\phi_{p}a_{M}(\textbf{p},+)+i\sin\phi_{p}a_{M}^{\dagger}(-\textbf{p},+)\right),

\displaystyle b(-\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}T(\phi_{p})a_{M}(-\textbf{p},+)T^{-1}(\phi_{p}),

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{p}}\left(\cos\phi_{p}a_{M}(-\textbf{p},+)-i\sin\phi_{p}a_{M}^{\dagger}(\textbf{p},+)\right).

The derivation of the relations in Eqs.(
13
-
16
) are shown in the appendix A. The matrix
T
denotes the Bogolyubov transformation

2.1
Relation between two vacua from the Bogolyubov transformation

Since there are two sets of operators,
(a(\textbf{p}),b(\textbf{p}))
and
a_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda=\pm 1)
, one can also define two different vacua. The vacuum denoted by

|0\rangle

is annihilated by
a(\pm\textbf{p})
and
b(\pm\textbf{p})
,

\displaystyle a(\pm\textbf{p})|0\rangle=b(\pm\textbf{p})|0\rangle=0,

(17)

where
\textbf{p}\in A
.
Eq.(
17
) can be translated as,

\displaystyle T(\phi_{p})a_{M}(\pm\textbf{p},\lambda=\pm 1)T^{-1}(\phi_{p})|0\rangle=0.

(18)

The vacuum
|0_{M}\rangle
that is annihilated by the operator
a_{M}(\pm\textbf{p},\lambda=\pm)

satisfies,

\displaystyle a_{M}(\pm\textbf{p},\lambda=\pm)|0_{M}\rangle=0.

(19)

The two vacua are related to each other as,

\displaystyle|0\rangle=T|0_{M}\rangle,\quad T=\prod_{\textbf{p}\in A}T(\phi_{p}).

(20)

Below we explicitly construct the vacuum
|0\rangle
by applying the Bogolyubov transformation
T
on the vacuum
|0_{M}\rangle
.

\displaystyle|0\rangle=T|0_{M}\rangle=\prod_{\textbf{p}\in A}\{\cos^{2}\phi_{p}-\sin^{2}\phi_{p}B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{p})B^{\dagger}_{M-}(\textbf{p})+i\sin\phi_{p}\cos\phi_{p}\sum_{\lambda=\pm}B^{\dagger}_{M\lambda}(\textbf{p})\}|0_{M}\rangle,

(21)

where
B^{\dagger}_{M\lambda}(\textbf{p})
is defined as,

\displaystyle B^{\dagger}_{M\lambda}(\textbf{p})=\alpha^{\dagger}_{M}(-\textbf{p},\lambda)\alpha^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda).

(22)

The derivation can be found in Eq.(
51
) in the appendix.
This bosonic operator creates two Majorana particles with opposite momenta.
We note that there are states of two pairs and one pair of massive Majorana neutrinos in superpositions.
The norm of these states are given by,

\displaystyle\langle 0_{M}|B_{M-}(\textbf{p})B_{M+}(\textbf{p})B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{p})B^{\dagger}_{M-}(\textbf{p})|0_{M}\rangle=1,

(23)

\displaystyle\langle 0_{M}|(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}=\pm}B_{M\lambda^{\prime}}(\textbf{p}))(\sum_{\lambda=\pm}B^{\dagger}_{M\lambda}(\textbf{p}))|0_{M}\rangle=2.

(24)

3
Construction of a one particle state with a definite lepton number

We are ready to build an initial state with a definite lepton number as follows,

\displaystyle|\Psi(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{q}a^{\dagger}(\textbf{q})|0\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{q}\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{q}}(\cos\phi_{q}a^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{q},-)-i\sin\phi_{q}a_{M}(-\textbf{q},-))|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigl[\prod_{\textbf{p}\neq\textbf{q}\in A}\{\cos^{2}\phi_{p}-\sin^{2}\phi_{p}B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{p})B^{\dagger}_{M-}(\textbf{p})+i\sin\phi_{p}\cos\phi_{p}\sum_{\lambda=\pm}B^{\dagger}_{M\lambda}(\textbf{p})\}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\times N_{q}\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{q}}\left(\cos\phi_{q}+i\sin\phi_{q}B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{q})\right)a^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{q},-)|0_{M}\rangle,

where
N_{q}
is a normalization factor given by,

\displaystyle N_{q}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2q(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(0)}}.

(26)

To derive Eq.(
25
), we use Eq.(
21
) and Eq.(
53

4
Time evolution of the initial state and the expectation value for lepton number

The state evolved from the initial state in Eq.(
25
) is obtained;

\displaystyle|\Psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\textbf{k}(\neq\textbf{q})\in A}\{\cos^{2}\phi_{k}-\sin^{2}\phi_{k}e^{-4iE_{k}t}B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{k})B^{\dagger}_{M-}(\textbf{k})+i\sin\phi_{k}\cos\phi_{k}e^{-i2E_{k}t}\sum_{\lambda=\pm 1}B^{\dagger}_{M\lambda}(\textbf{k})\}

(27)

\displaystyle\times N_{q}\sqrt{\cos 2\phi_{q}}e^{-iE_{q}t}\left(\cos\phi_{q}+i\sin\phi_{q}e^{-2iE_{q}t}B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{q})\right)a^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{q},-)|0_{M}\rangle.

We compute the expectation value of lepton number with the state at
t

27

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(t)|L|\Psi(t)\rangle,

(28)

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\prime}\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}2|\textbf{p}|}(a^{\dagger}(\textbf{p})a(\textbf{p})-b^{\dagger}(\textbf{p})b(\textbf{p})).

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\textbf{p}\in A}\frac{Vd^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}l_{\textbf{p}},

where
V=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\textbf{p}=0)
and
l_{\textbf{p}}
is the contribution to the lepton number from the momentum
p
sector
and is rewritten in terms of the operators for massive Majorana particle.

\displaystyle l_{\textbf{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\textbf{p}|}{E_{p}}(n(\textbf{p},-)+n(-\textbf{p},-)-n(\textbf{p},+)-n(-\textbf{p},+))

(30)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{im}{E_{p}}(B_{M-}^{\dagger}(\textbf{p})-B_{M+}^{\dagger}(\textbf{p})+B_{M+}(\textbf{p})-B_{M-}(\textbf{p})),

\displaystyle n(\textbf{p},\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda)\alpha_{M}(\textbf{p},\lambda).

(31)

The expectation value of
l_{\textbf{q}}

|\Psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(t)|l_{\textbf{q}}|\Psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\prod_{\textbf{k}(\neq\textbf{q})\in A}\langle 0_{M}|\{\cos^{2}\phi_{k}-\sin^{2}\phi_{k}e^{4iE_{k}t}B_{M-}(\textbf{k})B_{M+}(\textbf{k})-i\sin\phi_{k}\cos\phi_{k}e^{i2E_{k}t}\sum_{\lambda=\pm 1}B_{M\lambda}(\textbf{k})\}

\displaystyle\langle 0_{M}|a_{M}(\textbf{q},-)(\cos\phi_{q}-i\sin\phi_{q}e^{2iE_{q}t}B_{M+}(\textbf{q}))l_{\textbf{q}}(\cos\phi_{q}+i\sin\phi_{q}e^{-2iE_{q}t}B^{\dagger}_{M+}(\textbf{q}))a^{\dagger}_{M}(\textbf{q},-)|0_{M}\rangle.

5
Conclusions

We study the time evolution of lepton number in the Schrödinger picture for a single
flavor. Similar to the Heisenberg picture, a multi-flavor Schrödinger is also possible.
But the extention to the multi-flavor case is outside of the scope of the paper.
The expectation value is the same as that obtained by Heisenberg picture previously.
For non-relativistic case, it oscillates with the large amplitude (
\langle\Delta L\rangle\simeq\pm 1
).
For relativistic case, it oscillates rapidly with the small amplitude around
\langle L\rangle\simeq 1
.
The vacuum with null lepton number is a superposition of the vacuum for mass eigenstate, a pair of
Majorana particles, and two pairs of Majorana particles.
Similar to the vacuum, the one particle state with the definite lepton number
1
is a
superposition of a mass eigenstate of Majorana neutrino and a state with a Majorana pair and a Majorana neutrino.
These non-trivial superposition of states with different energies give rise to the oscillating behavior for the expectation value of lepton number.

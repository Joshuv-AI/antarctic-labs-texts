Electronic Density of States of a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U\left(1\right) Quantum Spin Liquid with Spinon Fermi Surface. II. Zeeman Magnetic Field Effects
Wen-Yu He, Patrick A. Lee

Abstract
The Zeeman effect lowers the energy of electrons with spin states which are anti-parallel to the applied magnetic field but lifts that of spin parallel states. In quantum spin liquids where the spin and charge degrees of freedom are fractionalized, anomalous Zeeman response may be expected. In the case of spin liquids with spinon Fermi surface, the threshold energy to excite an electronic state is found to exhibit no Zeeman shift. This is specific to the spinon Fermi surface case. In contrast, other gapped spin liquids are expected to exhibit the standard Zeeman shift at the band edge even though they also exhibit spin-charge fractionalization. When gauge field fluctuations are included, we find that the Zeeman shift of the electronic states gets affected by the gauge field induced binding. In the electronic density of states spectra, weak gauge binding induces band edge resonance peaks which exhibit the Zeeman shift in the same direction as that in the standard Zeeman effect, but the shift is reduced as the binding potential increases. With further increase in the binding potential the resonance becomes true in-gap bound states and eventually the Zeeman shift direction reverses so it is opposite to the standard Zeeman effect. We propose that one can perform spin polarized scanning tunneling microscope measurements as a test of the spinon Fermi sea ground state in quantum spin liquid candidate materials.

We will show that such internal composite structure of electrons along with the spinon Fermi sea ground state in the QSL gives rise to the anomalous electronic Zeeman response.

Anomalous Zeeman shift of the bound states

The anomalous Zeeman shift in the strong gauge binding regime is due to the interplay between the Zeeman field and the gauge binding induced energy saving, which is schematically shown in Fig.
3

In the Zeeman field, due to the Pauli paramagnetism, the spinon states with spins parallel to the magnetic field are shifted up in energy, and there are more empty states available to inject a spinon. This increases the binding energy, resulting in a downward shift of the bound state energy. Note that this works only if a large fraction of the unoccupied states up to the upper band edge participate in binding. It requires a strong binding interaction comparable to the bandwidth, which is indeed the case when the bound state is split off below threshold.

V
Conclusions and discussions

In the above sections, the QSL electronic DOS spectra in a Zeeman field is found to exhibit zero shift for the threshold energy, naive shift for the quasi-bound spinon chargon pairs, and anomalous shift for the in-gap bound states. In experiments which inject unpolarized electrons, the Zeeman splitting of the band edge resonance or the bound state will be smeared out by the thermal fluctuations. Therefore to see this effect it will be best to inject electron with one kind of spin via spin polarized STM
Bode
;
Balatsky
. In order to suppress the thermal smearing, it is better to carry out the measurement in the low temperature
k_{\textrm{b}}T\ll\mu_{\textrm{b}}B
, where
k_{\textrm{b}}
is the Boltzman constant. In practice, one would focus on measuring the threshold, Hubbard band widths and the band edge resonance peaks. Observation of the zero Zeeman shift of the threshold, Zeeman field induced change of the Hubbard band widths, and naive Zeeman shift of the band edge resonance peaks if any are evidence supporting the existence of the SFS. If the electronic DOS spectra as a whole exhibits the anomalous Zeeman shift, then our study indicates that the spectra is composed of the bounded spinon chargon pairs. However, it is difficult to realize this limit, because in this case the bound state will exhaust a large fraction of the spectral weight, as seen in Fig.
2
(c). The local electronic DOS spectrum obtained by the STM measurements

are the annihilation (creation) operators for a holon, doublon and spinon respectively. Here
U_{\textrm{b}}
denotes the gauge binding arising from the longitudinal gauge field fluctuations,
\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}
is the spinon band dispersion,
\epsilon_{\bm{k}}
is the band dispersion of the holon and doublon. The relativistic slave rotor has been approximated by the relativistic holon and doublon, which captures the low energy physics near the Hubbard band edges.

In the
U\left(1\right)
quantum spin liquid with spinon Fermi sea, creationg an electron is to create a spinon and simultaneously create a doublon or annihilate a holon. The creation operator for an electronic state is
c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\sigma}=f^{\dagger}_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\left(a_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}}+b^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)
. In the absence of gauge binding, the bare electronic Matsubara Green’s function at
U_{\textrm{b}}=0
takes the form

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle c_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\sigma}\left(\tau\right)c^{\dagger}_{\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime},\sigma}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}\left[\left\langle a^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left(\tau\right)a_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}+\left\langle b_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left(\tau\right)b^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}\right]e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\nu_{n}}G_{f,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n}-i\nu_{n},\bm{k}\right)\left[G_{a}\left(-i\nu_{n},-\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)+G_{b}\left(i\nu_{n},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)\right],

(S2)

where
G_{f,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k}\right)
is the spinon Matsubara Green’s function

\displaystyle G_{f,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k}\right)=-\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau=\frac{1}{i\omega_{n}-\xi_{\bm{k}}},

(S3)

G_{a}\left(-i\nu_{n},-\bm{k}\right)
is the holon Matsubara Green’s function

\displaystyle G_{a}\left(-i\nu_{n},-\bm{k}\right)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle a^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}}\left(\tau\right)a_{-\bm{k}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}e^{-i\nu_{b}\tau}d\tau=\frac{1}{-i\nu_{n}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}}},

(S4)

and
G_{b}\left(i\nu_{n},\bm{k}\right)
is the doublon Matsubara Green’s function

\displaystyle G_{b}\left(i\nu_{n},\bm{k}\right)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle b_{\bm{k}}\left(0\right)b^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle_{0}e^{i\nu_{n}\tau}d\tau=\frac{1}{i\nu_{n}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}}}.

(S5)

Here
\left\langle\dots\right\rangle_{0}
denotes the thermal average calculated from
S
in Eq.
VI
at zero gauge binding. After performing the Matsubara frequency summation, one can obtain the form of the electronic Matasubara Green’s function in Eq.
VI

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle G_{h,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)+G_{d,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)

(S6)

with

\displaystyle G_{h,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{n_{\textrm{F}}\left(\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\right)+n_{\textrm{B}}\left(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)}{i\omega_{n}-\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}},\quad G_{d,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{n_{\textrm{F}}\left(-\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\right)+n_{\textrm{B}}\left(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)}{i\omega_{n}-\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}.

(S7)

The corresponding retarded Green’s function can then be obtained through the analytic continuation
i\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega+i0^{+}

\displaystyle G_{\sigma}^{\textrm{R}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=

\displaystyle G^{\textrm{R}}_{h,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)+G^{\textrm{R}}_{d,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)

(S8)

with

\displaystyle G^{\textrm{R}}_{h,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{n_{\textrm{F}}\left(\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\right)+n_{\textrm{B}}\left(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)}{\omega+i0^{+}-\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}+\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}},\quad G^{\textrm{R}}_{d,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)=\frac{n_{\textrm{F}}\left(-\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}\right)+n_{\textrm{B}}\left(\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\right)}{\omega+i0^{+}-\xi_{\sigma,\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}.

(S9)

Here the first term and second term in
G_{\sigma}^{\textrm{R}}\left(\omega,\bm{k},\bm{k}^{\prime}\right)
describe the states in the lower Hubbard band and upper Hubbard band respectively.

VII
Electronic Density of States in the Quantum Spin Liquid

The electronic density of states at the site
\bm{r}_{i}
takes the form

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{\sigma}\left(\omega\right)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\textrm{Im}\tilde{G}^{\textrm{R}}_{\sigma}\left(\bm{r}_{i},\bm{r}_{i},\omega\right),

(S10)

where
\tilde{G}^{\textrm{R}}_{\sigma}\left(\bm{r}_{i},\bm{r}_{i},\omega\right)
is the electronic retarded Green’s function in real space. The corresponding electronic Matsubara Green’s function in real space takes the form

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left(\bm{r}_{i},\bm{r}_{j},i\omega_{n}\right)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\beta}\left[\left\langle f_{i,\sigma}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\left(0\right)a^{\dagger}_{i}\left(\tau\right)a_{j}\left(0\right)\right\rangle+\left\langle f_{i,\sigma}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\left(0\right)b_{i}\left(\tau\right)b_{j}^{\dagger}\left(0\right)\right\rangle\right]e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau

(S11)

where
\left\langle\dots\right\rangle
denotes the thermal average calculated from
S
in Eq.
VI
that involves the gauge binding
U_{\textrm{b}}
. In the system with translational symmetry, we can perform the Fourier transformation and then get

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\bm{r}}\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\left(\bm{r}_{i},\bm{r}_{j},i\omega_{n}\right)e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\delta\bm{r}}\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{i,\sigma}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\left(0\right)a^{\dagger}_{i}\left(\tau\right)a_{j}\left(0\right)\right\rangle e^{i\omega_{n}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}\right)}d\tau

\displaystyle-\sum_{\delta\bm{r}}\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{i,\sigma}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\left(0\right)b_{i}\left(\tau\right)b_{j}^{\dagger}\left(0\right)\right\rangle e^{i\omega_{n}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}\right)}d\tau

(S12)

The first term can be further simplified as

\displaystyle-\sum_{\delta\bm{r}}\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{i,\sigma}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\left(0\right)a^{\dagger}_{i}\left(\tau\right)a_{j}\left(0\right)\right\rangle e^{i\omega_{n}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}\right)}d\tau=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{\sigma,\bm{k}-\bm{q}}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{\sigma,\bm{k}-\bm{q}}\left(0\right)a^{\dagger}_{-\bm{q}}\left(\tau\right)a_{-\bm{q}}\left(0\right)\right\rangle e^{i\omega_{n}\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}G_{h,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)}{1-\frac{U_{\textrm{b}}}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}G_{h,\sigma}\left(i\omega_{n},\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)},

(S13)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\delta\bm{r}}\int_{0}^{\beta}\left\langle f_{i,\sigma}\left(\tau\right)f^{\dagger}_{j,\sigma}\left(0\right)b_{i}\left(\tau\right)b_{j}^{\dagger}\left(0\right)\right\rangle e^{i\omega_{n}\tau-i\bm{k}\cdot\left(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}\right)}d\tau=

VIII
The Binding Susceptibilities and Binding Equations

In the presence of gauge binding, one can see from Eq.
VII
that the gauge binding
U_{\textrm{b}}
changes the electronic density of states drastically when
1-\frac{U_{\textrm{b}}}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}G^{\textrm{R}}_{h,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)\rightarrow 0
and
1+\frac{U_{\textrm{b}}}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}G^{\textrm{R}}_{d,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)\rightarrow 0
. In the regime of weak gauge binding, the first term in Eq.
VII
gives rise to a resonance peak at the LHB top while the second term in Eq.
VII
brings about a resonance peak at the UHB bottom. When the gauge binding is sufficiently large, the band edge resonane peaks evolve inside the Mott gap and develop into in-gap bound states. The critical conditions
1-\frac{U_{\textrm{b}}}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}G^{\textrm{R}}_{h,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)=0
and
1+\frac{U_{\textrm{b}}}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}G^{\textrm{R}}_{d,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)=0
give the binding equations that determine the bound state band dispersions:

\displaystyle U_{\textrm{b}}\chi_{h,\sigma}\left(\bm{k},\omega\right)=

\displaystyle 1,

(S16)

\displaystyle U_{\textrm{b}}\chi_{d,\sigma}\left(\bm{k},\omega\right)=

\displaystyle 1.

(S17)

Here Eq.
S16
gives the band dispersion for the hole bound state above the LHB while Eq.
S17
gives the band dispersion for the electronic bound state below the UHB. In Eq.
S16
and Eq.
S17
,
\chi_{h,\sigma}\left(\bm{k},\omega\right)
and
\chi_{d,\sigma}\left(\bm{k},\omega\right)
are the susceptibilities to form a hole bound state and an electronic bound state respectively. The two susceptibilities take the form

\displaystyle\chi_{h,\sigma}\left(\bm{k},\omega\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\textrm{Re}G^{\textrm{R}}_{h,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right),\quad\textrm{and}\quad\chi_{d,\sigma}\left(\bm{k},\omega\right)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\bm{q}}\textrm{Re}G^{\textrm{R}}_{d,\sigma}\left(\omega,\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}\right)

(S18)

respectively.

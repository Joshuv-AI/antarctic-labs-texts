Geometric linearisation for optimal transport with strongly p p -convex cost
Lukas Koch

Abstract
We prove a geometric linearisation result for minimisers of optimal transport problems where the cost-function is strongly p p -convex and of p p -growth. Initial and target measures are allowed to be rough, but are assumed to be close to Lebesgue measure.



1  Introduction

The study of the optimal transport problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\lambda,\mu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}c(x-y)\,{\rm d}\pi

(1.1)

is well established. Here
\lambda,\mu
are two (finite) non-negative measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying
\lambda(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We refer the reader to
[
26
]
and
[
23

for an introduction and overview of the literature. When solutions take the form of a transport map
\pi=(\textup{Id}\times T)_{\#}\mu

\displaystyle\mu(T(x))\textup{det}\,(\mathrm{D}T(x))=\lambda(x)

(1.2)

as well as the additional structure condition

\displaystyle T(x)=x+\nabla c^{\ast}(\mathrm{D}\phi),

(1.3)

where
\phi
is a
c
-concave function and
c^{\ast}
denotes the convex conjugate of
c
. Assuming
\mu\sim\lambda\sim 1
and linearising the geometric nonlinearity in (
1.2

\textup{det}(\textup{Id}+A)=1+\textup{tr}\,A+\ldots

\displaystyle\textup{div}\,\nabla c^{\ast}(\mathrm{D}\phi)=\lambda-\mu.

(1.4)

Thus, at least formally, we expect solutions of (
1.1

1.4

1.1
Assumptions on the cost function and its dual

In this section, we explain our assumptions on the cost function
c
. In order to keep the statements of our results short, the conditions (
1.11
)–(
1.14
) below will be assumed to hold throughout the entire paper. We consider cost-functions modeled on the
p
-cost,
c(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{p}
with
p>1
. This is also the primary example of cost functions we have in mind. We emphasize however that the cost functions we consider need not be homogeneous. In fact the assumptions we impose are standard within elliptic regularity theory, see e.g.
[
5
,
20
]
. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. We consider a
C^{1}
-cost function
c\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following properties:
There is
\Lambda\geq 1
such that

(i)

c
is strongly
p
-convex: for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\tau\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\tau(1-\tau)V_{p}(x,y)+c(\tau x+(1-\tau)y)\leq\tau c(x)+(1-\tau)c(y).

(1.11)

where

V_{p}(x,y)=(|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|x-y|^{2}.

(ii)

c
has
p
-growth: for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}|x|^{p}\leq c(x)\leq\Lambda|x|^{p}.

(1.12)

(iii)

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|c(x)-c(y)|\leq\Lambda U_{p}(x,y)

(1.13)

where

U_{p}(x,y)=(|x|+|y|)^{p-1}|x-y|.

(iv)

c
satisfies controlled
p
-growth: For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y)|\leq\Lambda(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert x-y\rvert.

(1.14)

If the choice of
p
is clear from the context, we will write
V=V_{p}
and
U=U_{p}
. We further note the following inequality, valid for any

and with implicit constants depending only on

\displaystyle\lvert V_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})-V_{p}(z_{1},z_{3})\rvert\lesssim(\lvert z_{1}\rvert+\lvert z_{2}\rvert+\lvert z_{3}\rvert)^{p-1}\lvert z_{2}-z_{3}\rvert.

(1.15)

(
1.15
) follows from writing

\displaystyle V_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})-V_{p}(z_{1},z_{3})=\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla_{2}V_{p}(z_{1},sz_{2}+(1-s)z_{3}),z_{2}-z_{3}\rangle\,{\rm d}t.

Here
\nabla_{2}
denotes a derivative with respect to the second variable of
V_{p}
. From elementary calculations

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla_{2}V_{p}(z_{1}+sz_{2}+(1-s)z_{3})\rvert\lesssim(\lvert z_{1}\rvert+\lvert z_{2}\rvert+\lvert z_{3}\rvert)^{p-1},

which gives (
1.15
).

(
1.11
) and the fact that
c
is
C^{1}
imply that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle c(x)\geq c(y)+\langle\nabla c(y),x-y\rangle+\lambda V(x,y),

(1.16)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla c(x)-\nabla c(y),x-y\rangle\geq\lambda V(x,y).

(1.17)

This can be seen by arguing as in the
2
-convex,
2
-growth case, which can be found for example in
[
12
, Chapter IV.4.1]
.

We require some information on the convexity properties of the convex conjugate that follow by adaptions of the
2
-convex,
2
-growth theory in
[
22
]
and
[
13
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we provide proofs of the statements we require. Introduce the convex conjugate
c^{\ast}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
via

c^{\ast}(\xi)=\sup_{x}\langle\xi,x\rangle-c(x).

We remark that since
c
is strongly convex,
C^{1}
and superlinear at infinity,
c^{\ast}
is strongly convex,
C^{1}
and superlinear at infinity. Moreover
\nabla c
and
\nabla c^{\ast}
are homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\nabla c^{\ast}=(\nabla c)^{-1}

[
22
, Theorem 26.5]
.
Note that due to (
1.12
), we have for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle c^{\ast}(\xi)\leq\sup_{x}\langle\xi,x\rangle-\Lambda^{-1}\lvert x\rvert^{p}\lesssim\lvert\xi\rvert^{p^{\prime}}.

A lower bound can be obtained similarly and we deduce:

\displaystyle|\xi|^{p^{\prime}}\lesssim c^{\ast}(\xi)\lesssim|\xi|^{p^{\prime}}.

(1.18)

Due to strict
p
-convexity of
c
,
c^{\ast}
satisfies for any
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})|\lesssim(|\xi_{1}|+|\xi_{2}|)^{p^{\prime}-2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|.

(1.19)

Indeed, for
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using (
1.16
) with the choice
{x=\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})}
,
y=\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})
and Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle V_{p}(\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1}),\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2}))\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{1}-\xi_{2},\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rvert\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert.

Re-arranging, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert(\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rvert^{2})^{\frac{2-p}{2}}.

(1.20)

We claim that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\lvert\xi\rvert^{p^{\prime}-1}\lesssim\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi)\rvert\lesssim\lvert\xi\rvert^{p^{\prime}-1}.

(1.21)

Then (
1.20
) gives (
1.19
). Since
\nabla c^{\ast}=(\nabla c)^{-1}
and both maps are homeomorphisms, to show (
1.21
), it suffices to show that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\lvert x\rvert^{p-1}\lesssim\lvert\nabla c(x)\rvert\lesssim\lvert x\rvert^{p-1}.

(1.22)

Fix

. Since difference quotients of convex functions are non-decreasing, for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, applying also (
1.13
),

\displaystyle\langle\nabla c(x),h\rangle\leq\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{\lvert h\rvert}\lesssim\frac{(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert h\rvert)^{p}}{\lvert h\rvert}.

Applying the above with
h\to th
and choosing

such that
\lvert th\rvert=\lvert x\rvert
, as

was arbitrary, this gives the second inequality in (
1.22
). Note that in particular
\nabla c(0)=0
. Thus, using (
1.11
) with

\displaystyle\lvert x\rvert^{p}\lesssim\langle\nabla c(x),x\rangle\leq\lvert\nabla c(x)\rvert\lvert x\rvert.

After rearranging, this proves the first inequality in (
1.22
). Hence (
1.19
) is established.

Finally, it follows from (
1.19
) and the intermediate value theorem that for
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\lvert c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})\rvert=\Big\lvert\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla c^{\ast}(s\xi_{1}+(1-s)\xi_{2}),\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rangle\,{\rm d}s\Big\rvert\lesssim U_{p^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}).

(1.23)

We also require that
c^{\ast}
is
p^{\prime}
convex, that is for some
C(p,\Lambda)>0
,

\displaystyle c\tau(1-\tau)V_{p^{\prime}}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+c^{\ast}(\tau\xi_{1}+(1-\tau)\xi)2)\leq\tau c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})+(1-\tau)c^{\ast}(\xi_{2}).

(1.24)

Indeed, we can use Taylor’s theorem and (
1.14
) to obtain for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some
C=C(p,n)>0
,

\displaystyle c(y)=

\displaystyle c(x)+\langle\nabla c(x),y-x\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla c(x+t(y-x))-\nabla c(x),y-x\rangle\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(x)+\langle\nabla c(x),y-x\rangle+C(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert x-y\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert y-x\rvert^{2}.

(1.25)

In order to estimate the integral, we used a well-known estimate, see e.g.
[
11
,
4
]
. Recall the Fenchel-Young inequality in the form

\displaystyle c(\xi)+c^{\ast}(x)\geq\langle\xi,x\rangle\quad\forall\xi,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.26)

with equality if and only if
\xi=\nabla c^{\ast}(x)
. Hence, with the choice

, (

) gives

\displaystyle-c^{\ast}(\xi_{2})=

\displaystyle-\sup_{y}\{\langle\xi_{2},y\rangle-c(y)\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-\sup_{y}\left\{\langle\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},y\rangle-C(\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\rvert+\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})-y\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert y-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\right\}.

(1.27)

Note that the supremum is nothing but
\left(CV_{p}(\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1}),\cdot-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\right)^{\ast}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})
. Noting that for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle(\lvert x\rvert+\lvert y\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert y\rvert^{2}\lesssim\begin{cases}\lvert y\rvert^{p}\quad&\text{ if }\{\lvert x\rvert\leq\lvert y\rvert,p\geq 2\}\text{ or }\{\lvert x\rvert\geq\lvert y\rvert,p\leq 2\}\\
\lvert x\rvert^{p-2}\lvert x\rvert^{2}\quad&\text{ if }\{\lvert x\rvert\geq\lvert y\rvert,p\geq 2\}\text{ or }\{\lvert x\rvert\leq\lvert y\rvert,p\leq 2\}\end{cases}

and arguing case by case as for (

), this shows

\displaystyle\left((\lvert x\rvert+\lvert\cdot\rvert)^{p-2}\lvert\cdot\rvert^{2}\right)^{\ast}(\xi)\gtrsim(\lvert x\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\xi\rvert)^{p^{\prime}-2}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}.

In particular, recalling (

), we deduce

\displaystyle V_{p}(\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1}),\cdot)^{\ast}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})\gtrsim(\lvert\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert)^{p^{\prime}-2}\lvert\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\rvert^{2}\gtrsim V_{p^{\prime}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}).

Employing the fact that for any convex

and any

, it holds that
{f(\cdot-x)^{\ast}(\xi)=f^{\ast}(\xi)+\langle x,\xi\rangle}
, we finally conclude,

\displaystyle\left(CV_{p}(\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1}),\cdot-\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1})\right)^{\ast}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})\gtrsim V_{p^{\prime}}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})+\langle\nabla c^{\ast}(\xi_{1}),\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}).

Combining this estimate with (

) gives (

).

1.2
Regularity assumptions on the dual system

Let
R\in(2,3)
. In this section, we state the regularity assumptions we make on distributional solutions
\phi\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(B)

Throughout, we let

centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

B_{r}=B_{r}(0)
and
B=B_{1}(0)
.

\Lambda

p

\Lambda

Given

, we denote by
[\cdot]_{C^{0,\alpha}}
, the
\alpha
-Hölder-seminorm. Given
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
,

and

denote the usual Lebesgue and (fractional) Sobolev spaces. If
\mu
is a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mu\llcorner\Omega
denotes its restriction to
\Omega
. We say a couple of measures
\lambda
,
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is admissible if
\lambda,\mu
are non-negative finite measures satisfying
\lambda(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Given
R>0
, we let
\Pi_{R}(x)=R\frac{x}{|x|}
be the projection onto
\partial B_{R}
and define for every measure
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
the projected measure on
\partial B_{R}
,
\hat{\rho}=\Pi_{R}\#\rho
, i.e.

\displaystyle\int\xi\,{\rm d}\hat{\rho}=\int\xi\left(R\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\,{\rm d}\rho(x).

(2.1)

A set

is said to be

and any points

\sum_{i=1}^{N}c(x_{i}-y_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}c(x_{i}-y_{i+1}),

2.2
Optimal transportation

We recall some definitions and facts about optimal transportation, see
[
26
]
for more details. For this subsection, the full strength of our assumptions (
1.11
)-(
1.14
) is not needed. In fact, assuming that
c
is lower semi-continuous, convex and satisfies
p
-growth (
1.12
) would be sufficient in this subsection.

Given a measure
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote its marginals by

and
\pi_{2}
respectively. The set of measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with marginals

and

and

we define

While our notation is reminiscent of the Wasserstein distance, and in fact gives the (
p
-th power of the) Wasserstein

-distance in the case

, in general it is not a distance on measures.
Under our hypothesis, an optimal coupling always exists and moreover a coupling
\pi
is optimal, if and only if its support is
c
-cyclical monotone.

Moreover, we note the following triangle-type inequality:

Lemma 2.1
.

\varepsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle W_{c}(\mu_{1},\mu_{3})\leq(1+\varepsilon)W_{c}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})+C(\varepsilon)W_{c}(\mu_{2},\mu_{3}).

[
Proof]Due to the gluing lemma, see e.g.
[
23
, Lemma 5.5.]
, there exists
\sigma
, a positive measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

on the first two variables and marginal

and
\pi_{2}

\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}

\mu_{2}

\displaystyle W_{c}(\mu_{1},\mu_{3})\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int c(x-z)\,{\rm d}\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left(\int tc\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)+(1-t)\left(\frac{y-z}{1-t}\right)\,{\rm d}\gamma\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

3  A

\displaystyle\int\zeta(y,z)\overline{\pi}(\,{\rm d}z|y)\mu(\,{\rm d}y)=\int\zeta(y,z)\overline{\pi}(\,{\rm d}y\,{\rm d}z).

As we see from Lemma

[
Proof]Fix

. In this proof

7  The
c^{\ast}
-harmonic approximation result

The goal of this section is to prove the
c^{\ast}
-harmonic approximation result. With the results of the previous sections in hand, we can give a more precise version of Theorem
1.1
. In particular, we can make explicit the problem that
\phi
solves.

To this end, in light of Lemma
5.1
and Lemma

, given
\tau>0
, we fix

such that there exist non-negative
\overline{f}_{R}

such that

\displaystyle W_{c}(f_{R},\overline{f}_{R})+W_{c}(g_{R},\overline{g}_{R})\lesssim\tau E(4)+D(4)

\displaystyle D(R)+\int_{\partial B_{R}}\overline{f}_{R}^{p}+\overline{g}_{R}^{p}\lesssim E(4)+D(4).

Then let
\phi
be a solution with
\int_{B_{R}}\phi\,{\rm d}x=0
of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\,\nabla c^{\ast}(\mathrm{D}\phi)=c_{R}\quad&\text{ in }B_{R}\\

where

) is well-posed.
We emphasize that while we do not make the dependence explicit in our notation,
\phi
depends on the choice of radius

With this notation in place, we state a precise version of our main result.

Theorem 7.1
.

For every
\tau>0
, there exist positive constants

such that if

, then there exists

\displaystyle\int_{\#_{1}}c(x-y-\nabla c^{\ast}(\mathrm{D}\phi))\leq\tau E(4)+C(\tau)D(4),

where
\phi
solves (

).

The proof will be a direct consequence of the lemmata we prove in the following subsections. We begin in Section

by using strong

The first error term arises from the approximation of the boundary data, while the second error term comes from passing to the perspective of trajectories. We construct a competitor to estimate the difference of energies and estimate the two error terms in Section

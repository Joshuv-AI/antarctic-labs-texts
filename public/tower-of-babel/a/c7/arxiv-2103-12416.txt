Chaotic string dynamics in deformed
T

Abstract
Recently, Arutyunov, Bassi and Lacroix have shown that 2D non-linear sigma model with a deformed T 1 , 1 T^{1,1} background is classically integrable [arXiv:2010.05573 [hep-th]]. This background includes a Kalb-Ramond two-form with a critical value. Then the sigma model has been conjectured to be non-integrable when the two-form is off critical. We confirm this conjecure by explicitly presenting classical chaos. With a winding string ansatz, the system is reduced to a dynamical system described by a set of ordinary differential equations. Then we find classical chaos, which indicates non-integrability, by numerically computing Poincaré sections and Lyapunov spectra for some initial conditions.

2
Reducing string sigma model on

R\times\mathcal{T}_{k}^{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda)}

We shall consider a classical string on the background

R\times\mathcal{T}_{k}^{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda)}
.
As discussed in
[
21
]
, the original isometries as
T^{1,1}
are retained in the presence of
B
-field.
In particular, one can easily see in (
1.3
) that constant shifts in
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi)
-directions are still symmetries.

Note that we suppose that the background
R\times\mathcal{T}_{k}^{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda)}
should be obtained from a supergravity embedding of the background (
1.3
) , though it has not been done other than
for special values of the parameters. So this is just analogy of
R\times S^{3}
in the case of AdS
{}_{5}\times
S
5
,
where
R
comes from AdS time.

2.1
String sigma model on
R\times\mathcal{T}_{k}^{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda)}

Let
G_{\mu\nu}
and
B_{\mu\nu}

(\mu,\nu=t,\theta_{1},\phi_{1},\theta_{2},\phi_{2},\psi)
denote the background metric and
B
-field,
h_{ab}

(a,b=\tau,\sigma)
the world-sheet metric, and
X^{\mu}
the target-space coordinates.
The classical action of the string
\sigma
-model is given by

S=-\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\sigma\left(\sqrt{-h}h^{ab}G_{\mu\nu}+\epsilon^{ab}B_{\mu\nu}\right)\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\,,

(2.1)

where
h\equiv\mathrm{det}\,h_{ab}
and the antisymmetric symbol is defined as
\epsilon^{\tau\sigma}=-1
.
The equation of motion for
X^{\mu}
is given by

\partial_{a}\partial^{a}X^{\mu}+\Gamma^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma}h^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{\rho}\partial_{b}X^{\sigma}-\mathcal{T}{}^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma}\varepsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{\rho}\partial_{b}X^{\sigma}=0\,,

(2.2)

where
\varepsilon^{ab}\equiv\epsilon^{ab}/\sqrt{-h}
and

\Gamma^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}G^{\mu\nu}\left(G_{\nu\sigma,\rho}+G_{\rho\nu,\sigma}-G_{\rho\sigma,\nu}\right)\,,\quad\mathcal{T}{}^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}G^{\mu\nu}\left(G_{\rho\sigma,\nu}+G_{\nu\rho,\sigma}+G_{\sigma\nu,\rho}\right)\,.

(2.3)

The equation of motion for
h^{ab}
imposes that the stress-energy tensor
T_{ab}
on the world-sheet should vanish:

0=T_{ab}=G_{\mu\nu}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h_{ab}h^{cd}G_{\mu\nu}\partial_{c}X^{\mu}\partial_{d}X^{\nu}\,.

(2.4)

Note here that the variation of
h^{ab}
is irrelevant to the antisymmetric symbol.
The vanishing stress-energy tensor leads to the Virasoro constraints which restrict the string embedding
specified by
X^{\mu}
.

Thanks to the world-sheet diffeomorphism, one can take the conformal gauge
h_{ab}=e^{2\omega(\tau,\sigma)}\eta_{ab}
, where
\eta_{ab}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)
.
In this gauge, the action takes the form:

S^{(\rm{c})}=\int\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\sigma\left(\eta^{ab}G_{\mu\nu}+\epsilon^{ab}B_{\mu\nu}\right)\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}\,.

(2.5)

The conjugate momentum is given by

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\left(\partial_{\tau}X^{\mu}\right)}=G_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\tau}X^{\nu}+B_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}X^{\nu}\,.

(2.6)

Then the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=p_{\mu}\partial_{\tau}X^{\mu}-\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}G_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\tau}X^{\mu}\partial_{\tau}X^{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}G_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}X^{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}X^{\nu}\,.

(2.7)

While the
B
-field appears in (
2.6
), it cancels with the
B
-field from
\mathcal{L}
in the above expression.
As a result, the canonical stress-energy tensor made of the conjugate momentum gives the same result as (
2.4
) as it can be checked easily.

Once the gauge has been fixed, the Virasoro constraints can be expressed as follows.
The
(\tau,\tau)
-component of the stress-energy tensor is precisely the Hamiltonian,
i.e.
T_{\tau\tau}=\mathcal{H}
and hence
the Virasoro constraints indicate that
\mathcal{H}=0
(the Hamiltonian constraint) .
We also find that
T_{\tau\tau}=T_{\sigma\sigma}
because
T^{a}{}_{a}=0
.
Substituting (
2.6
) into (
2.4
) in the conformal gauge, the other independent component
T_{\tau\sigma}

can be rewritten as

\displaystyle T_{\tau\sigma}=p_{\mu}\partial_{\sigma}X^{\mu}=0\,.

(2.8)

When
k=\lambda^{2}
, this string sigma model has a classical Lax pair and is classically integrable
[
21
]
.
Our purpose here is to see the anticipated non-integrability when
k\neq\lambda^{2}
.

2.2
Reduction of the system with a winding string ansatz

In order to show non-integrability, it is sufficient to find out chaotic motions in a subsector.
Hence it is convenient to consider the simplest subsector which contains chaotic motions.
To reduce the system to a set of ordinary differential equations,
let us suppose the following winding string ansatz:

\begin{split}t&=t(\tau)\,,\quad\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(\tau)\,,\quad\theta_{2}=\theta_{2}(\tau)\,,\\
\phi_{1}&=\phi_{1}(\tau)+\alpha_{1}\sigma\,,\quad\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}(\tau)+\alpha_{2}\sigma\,,\quad\psi=\psi(\tau)+\alpha\sigma\,.\end{split}

(2.9)

This ansatz describes a string wrapped on
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi)
-directions.
Since the periodicity of the compact coordinates are
\phi_{i}\simeq\phi_{i}+2\pi

(i=1,2)
and
\psi\simeq\psi+4\pi
,
and
\sigma\simeq\sigma+2\pi
,
the winding numbers
\alpha_{i}~(i=1,2)
and
\alpha
should satisfy
\alpha_{i},\alpha/2\in\mathbb{Z}
.

By substituting the ansatz (
2.9
) into (
2.6
),
the momentum components associated with the isometries (and hence conserved) are given by

\displaystyle p_{t}

\displaystyle=-\dot{t}\,,

(2.10a)

\displaystyle p_{\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\lambda_{1}^{2}\sin^2 \theta_{1} +\lambda^{2}\cos^{2} \theta_{1})\dot{\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{2}\cos\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}\dot{\phi_{2}}+\lambda^{2}\cos\theta_{1}\dot{\psi}+k\cos\theta_{1}\quantity(\alpha_{2}\cos\theta_{2}+\alpha)\,,

(2.10b)

\displaystyle p_{\phi_{2}}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\lambda_{2}^{2}\sin^2 \theta_{2} +\lambda^{2}\cos^{2} \theta_{2})\dot{\phi_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{2}\cos\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}\dot{\phi_{1}}+\lambda^{2}\cos\theta_{2}\dot{\psi}-k\cos\theta_{2}\quantity(\alpha_{1}\cos\theta_{1}+\alpha)\,,

(2.10c)

\displaystyle p_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\cos\theta_{1}\dot{\phi_{1}}+\lambda^{2}\cos\theta_{2}\dot{\phi_{2}}+\lambda^{2}\dot{\psi}-k\alpha_{1}\cos\theta_{1}+k\alpha_{2}\cos\theta_{2}\,.

(2.10d)

Under the ansatz (
2.9
) , the constraint (
2.8
) can be rewritten as

T_{\tau\sigma}=p_{\phi_{1}}\alpha_{1}+p_{\phi_{2}}\alpha_{2}+p_{\psi}\alpha=0\,.

(2.11)

From this expression, one finds that this constraint can be satisfied by simply taking
p_{\phi_{1}}=p_{\phi_{2}}=p_{\psi}=0
.
It is also helpful to introduce the energy
E
defined as
E\equiv-p_{t}
.
Then, from (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{t}

\displaystyle=E\,,

(2.12a)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{k\cos\theta_{1}\left(\alpha+\alpha_{1}\cos\theta_{1}\right)}{\lambda_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{1}}\,,

(2.12b)

\displaystyle\dot{\phi_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{k\cos\theta_{2}\left(\alpha+\alpha_{2}\cos\theta_{2}\right)}{\lambda_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{2}}\,,

(2.12c)

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}

\displaystyle=k\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}\cos\theta_{1}-\alpha_{2}\cos\theta_{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{1}\left(\alpha+\alpha_{1}\cos\theta_{1}\right)}{\lambda^{2}_{1}\sin^{2}\theta_{1}}-\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{2}\left(\alpha+\alpha_{2}\cos\theta_{2}\right)}{\lambda^{2}_{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{2}}\right)\,.

(2.12d)

The other components are given by

p_{\theta_{i}}=\lambda_{i}^{2}\dot{\theta}_{i}\,.

(2.13)

The string motion is described by coupled ordinary differential equations for
\theta_{i}
.
Substituting (
2.9
) into (
2.2
) and using (
2.12
), we obtain the evolution equations for
\theta_{i}
as

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{\theta_{1}}}{\sin\theta_{1}}=\alpha_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}_{1}}-1+\frac{k^{2}}{\lambda^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}_{1}}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}_{1}\sin^{4}\theta_{1}}\right)\right)\cos\theta_{1}-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{4}}{\lambda^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2}}\cos\theta_{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\alpha_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{4}}\left(\lambda^{2}\lambda^{2}_{1}-k^{2}+\frac{k^{2}\left(\cos^{2}\theta_{1}+1\right)}{\sin^{4}\theta_{1}}\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}k^{2}\cos\theta_{1}}{\lambda^{4}_{1}\sin^{4}\theta_{1}}\,,

(2.14a)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{\theta_{2}}}{\sin\theta_{2}}=\alpha_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}_{2}}-1+\frac{k^{2}}{\lambda^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}_{2}}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\lambda^{2}_{2}\sin^{4}\theta_{2}}\right)\right)\cos\theta_{2}-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{4}}{\lambda^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}}\cos\theta_{1}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\alpha_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{4}}\left(\lambda^{2}\lambda^{2}_{2}-k^{2}+\frac{k^{2}\left(\cos^{2}\theta_{2}+1\right)}{\sin^{4}\theta_{2}}\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}k^{2}\cos\theta_{2}}{\lambda^{4}_{2}\sin^{4}\theta_{2}}\,.

(2.14b)

Note that the two equations are decoupled when
k=\lambda^{2}

and no chaos appears obviously.

The Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=-\frac{1}{2}E^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{1}^{2}}p_{\theta_{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{2}^{2}}p_{\theta_{2}}^{2}+V\quantity(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\,,

(2.15)

where the potential
V(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
is defined as

\displaystyle V\quantity(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\equiv\,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2}}\frac{\left(\lambda^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{1}+\lambda_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{1}\right)\left(k^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{1}+\lambda^{2}\lambda_{1}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{1}\right)}{\sin^{2}\theta_{1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{2\lambda^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}}\frac{\left(\lambda^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{2}\right)\left(k^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{2}+\lambda^{2}\lambda_{2}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{2}\right)}{\sin^{2}\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle-\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\frac{k^{2}-\lambda^{4}}{\lambda^{2}}\cos\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\frac{k^{2}\cot^{2}\theta_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}+\frac{k^{2}\cot^{2}\theta_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\alpha_{1}}{\lambda_{1}^{2}}\quantity(k^2\cot^2\theta_{1}+\lambda^2\lambda^{2}_{1})\cos\theta_{1}+\frac{\alpha\alpha_{2}}{\lambda_{2}^{2}}\quantity(k^2\cot^2\theta_{2}+\lambda^2\lambda^{2}_{2})\cos\theta_{2}\,.

(2.16)

Recall that the Hamiltonian has to be zero, i.e.
\mathcal{H}=0
, due to the Virasoro constraints.
Since the Hamiltonian is conserved,
this constraint is always satisfied along time evolution
once the initial data is prepared so as to satisfy
\mathcal{H}=0
.

The presence of the background
B
-field restricts the domain of the string motion.
When
k\neq 0
, the string cannot reach the poles of
S^{2}
(
\theta_{i}=0
and
\pi
),
because
V\quantity(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
diverges there.
Therefore, the string can move in the range
0<\theta_{i}<\pi
.
When
k=0
, the divergent terms are absent in the potential,
and the string can shrink and pass through the poles.

Note also that the potential is tilted because of the winding in the
\psi
-direction
(i.e.,
\alpha\neq 0
) .
If
\alpha=0
, the potential is symmetric under
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\to(\pi-\theta_{1},\pi-\theta_{2})
, while it is not otherwise.
Hence, there is one global minimum when
\alpha\neq 0
.
In practice, the minimum can be found numerically with specified parameters.

Chaotic string dynamics

2.14
)
by computing Poincaré sections and Lyapunov spectra for some values when
k\neq\lambda^{2}

For numerical computations, we set the parameters as

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=1\,,\quad\alpha=2\,,\quad\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\lambda=1

We have examined various values of

\displaystyle\theta_{2}=\pi~(k=0)\quad\mbox{or}\quad\theta_{2}=\pi/2~(k=0.5,\,10)\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{\theta_{2}}\geq 0\,.

Conclusion and Discussion

In this letter, we have studied a string sigma model on
R\times\mathcal{T}^{(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda)}_{k}
by using a winding string ansatz. We have shown the presence of classical chaos for
k\neq\lambda^{2}
by looking at Poincaré sections. We have also calculated the Lyapunov spectra
and showed that the largest Lyapunov exponent is positive for chaotic string motions.
Thus we conclude that the string sigma model is not classically integrable
when
k\neq\lambda^{2}
as conjectured in
[
21
]
.

In our analysis, we have supposed a supergravity embedding of the background (
1.3
) .
It is a significant future problem to find out this supergravity embedding for general values of the parameters.
Notably, in
[
21
]
, the background (
1.3
) has been derived as a certain limit
of a more general background. It is a challenging problem to try to find out a supergravity embedding
of it. It is also a nice practice to study non-integrable parameter region of it by following the standard manner
as performed in this letter.

On the range of fractal interpolation functions
Bogdan Anghelina, Radu Miculescu, María Antonia Navascués

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, based on the results from [On the localization of Hutchinson–Barnsley fractals, Chaos Solitons Fractals, 173 (2023), 113674], we generate coverings
(consisting of finite families of rhombi) of the graph of fractal interpolation
functions. As a by-product we obtain estimations for the range of such
functions. Some concrete examples and graphical representations are
provided.

Keywords:
iterated function system, fractal interpolation function, range of a function

MSC

Preliminaries

Given a metric space
(X,d)
,
x\in X
and
r>0
we shall use the following notation:

•

B[x,r]=\{y\in X\;|\;d(y,x)\leq r\}

•

\mathcal{P}_{cp}(X)=\{A\subseteq X|A\text{ is non-empty and compact}\}

•

h
is the Hausdorff-Pompeiu metric, described by

\displaystyle h(K_{1},K_{2})=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in K_{1}}d(x,K_{2}),\sup_{x\in K_{2}}d(x,K_{1})\right\},

for all
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{cp}(X)
.

For a Lipschitz function
f:X\to X
we shall denote by
\text{Lip}(f)
the Lipschitz constant of
f
.

Let us recall the following well known result:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A_{1},\;A_{2},\dots,\;A_{n}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be non-empty, bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}
. Then

\sup(A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup\dots\cup A_{n})=\max\{\sup A_{1},\sup A_{2},\dots,\sup A_{n}\}.

Definition 2.1
.

An iterated function system (for short IFS) is a pair
\mathcal{S}=((X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in I})
, where
(X,d)
is a complete metric space and
f_{i}:X\to X
,
i\in I
, are Banach contractions.

The function
F_{\mathcal{S}}:\mathcal{P}_{cp}(X)\to\mathcal{P}_{cp}(X)
, given by

F_{\mathcal{S}}(K)=\bigcup_{i\in I}f_{i}(K),

for every
K\in\mathcal{P}_{cp}(X)
, is called the fractal operator (or the Hutchinson operator) associated with
\mathcal{S}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{S}=((X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in I})
is an iterated function system, then
F_{\mathcal{S}}
is a contraction with respect to
h
. Its unique fixed point is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
and it is called the attractor of
\mathcal{S}
since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\underbrace{(F_{\mathcal{S}}\circ F_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\dots\circ F_{\mathcal{S}})}_{n\text{ times }}(K)=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}},

for each
K\in\mathcal{P}_{cp}(X).

Theorem 2.1

(see Theorem 3.1 from
[
1
]
)
.

For an iterated function system
\mathcal{S}=((X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots n\}})
, we have

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{i\in\{1,\dots,\;n-1\}}B\left[\gamma_{i},Ms_{i}\frac{1+s_{n}}{1-s_{{n-1}}s_{n}}\right]\right)\bigcup B\left[\gamma_{n},Ms_{n}\frac{1+s_{{n-1}}}{1-s_{{n-1}}s_{n}}\right],

where

1.

s_{i}=\text{Lip}(f_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

2.

s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\dots\leq s_{n}

3.

\gamma_{i}
is the unique fixed point of
f_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}

4.

M=\max_{i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}d(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})
.

3
The main results

The framework of our main result

Inspired by
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
(x_{0},y_{0}),(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(x_{n},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

a:=x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{n}=:b

and let us consider the iterated function system
\mathcal{S}=((\mathbb{R}^{2},\rho),(f_{k})_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}})
, where:

•

f_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

f_{k}(x,y)=(a_{k}x+b_{k},c_{k}x+d_{k}y+e_{k}),

for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, where

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{k}-x_{k-1}}{x_{n}-x_{0}}

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{n}x_{k-1}-x_{0}x_{k}}{x_{n}-x_{0}}

\displaystyle d_{k}

\displaystyle\in[0,1)

\displaystyle c_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{k}-y_{k-1}}{x_{n}-x_{0}}-d_{k}\frac{y_{n}-y_{0}}{x_{n}-x_{0}}

\displaystyle e_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{n}y_{k-1}-x_{0}y_{k}}{x_{n}-x_{0}}-d_{k}\frac{x_{n}y_{0}-x_{0}y_{n}}{x_{n}-x_{0}};

•

the complete metric
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is given by

\rho((u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2}))=|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|v_{1}-v_{2}|,

for all
(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where

\theta=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&c_{1}=c_{2}=\dots=c_{n}=0;\\
&\frac{1-\max_{k\in\{1\dots n\}}a_{k}}{2\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}|c_{k}|},&&\text{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, there exists a continuous function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
, which is called an affine fractal interpolation function, such that :

•

f(x_{k})=y_{k},

for all
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}

•

G_{f}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}.

Finding the fixed point of
f_{k}

For a fixed, but arbitrarily chosen
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, from
f_{k}(\gamma_{k})=\gamma_{k}=(u_{k},v_{k})
we obtain

(a_{k}u_{k}+b_{k},c_{k}u_{k}+d_{k}v_{k}+e_{k})=(u_{k},v_{k}),

hence

\begin{cases}a_{k}u_{k}+b_{k}&=u_{k}\\
c_{k}u_{k}+d_{k}v_{k}+e_{k}&=v_{k}.\end{cases}

From the first equation, we deduce that

u_{k}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}.

Substituting in the second equation, we obtain

c_{k}\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}+d_{k}v_{k}+e_{k}=v_{k},

so

v_{k}=\frac{b_{k}c_{k}}{(1-a_{k})(1-d_{k})}+\frac{e_{k}}{1-d_{k}}.

Hence, the (unique) fixed point of
f_{k}
is

\gamma_{k}=\left(\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}},\frac{b_{k}c_{k}}{(1-a_{k})(1-d_{k})}+\frac{e_{k}}{1-d_{k}}\right),

for all
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Finding the Lipschitz constant of
f_{k}

Proposition 3.1
.

In the above mentioned framework, we have

\text{Lip}(f_{k})=\max\{d_{k},a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|\},

for every
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}

Proof.

Let us consider
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
arbitrarily chosen, but fixed.

Note that

\text{Lip}(f_{k})=\sup\mathcal{M},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\rho\bm{(}f_{k}(u_{1},v_{1}),f_{k}(u_{2},v_{2})\bm{)}}{\rho\bm{(}(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\bm{)}}\big|{(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},(u_{1},v_{1})\neq(u_{2},v_{2})}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{a_{k}|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|c_{k}(u_{1}-u_{2})+d_{k}(v_{1}-v_{2})|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|v_{1}-v_{2}|}\big|{(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},(u_{1},v_{1})\neq(u_{2},v_{2})}\right\}.

If
d_{k}=0
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{a_{k}|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|c_{k}(u_{1}-u_{2})|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|v_{1}-v_{2}|}\big|{(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{1}\neq v_{2},u_{1}=u_{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\bigcup\left\{\frac{a_{k}|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|c_{k}(u_{1}-u_{2})|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|v_{1}-v_{2}|}\big|(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{(a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|)|u_{1}-u_{2}|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|}\big|(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|}{1+\theta\left|\frac{v_{1}-v_{2}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right|}\big|(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|}{1+\theta\left|t\right|}\big|t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Since

\sup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|}{1+\theta\left|t\right|}\big|t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}=a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|,

taking into account Lemma
2.1
, we get

\text{Lip}(f_{k})=\max\{0,a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|\}=\max\{d_{k},a_{k}+\theta c_{k}\}.

So, we can suppose that
d_{k}>0
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{a_{k}|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|c_{k}(u_{1}-u_{2})+d_{k}(v_{1}-v_{2})|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|v_{1}-v_{2}|}\big|{(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},v_{1}\neq v_{2},u_{1}=u_{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\bigcup\left\{\frac{a_{k}|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|c_{k}(u_{1}-u_{2})+d_{k}(v_{1}-v_{2})|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|v_{1}-v_{2}|}\big|(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{d_{k}\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_{k}|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta|c_{k}(u_{1}-u_{2})+d_{k}(v_{1}-v_{2})|}{|u_{1}-u_{2}|+\theta\left|v_{1}-v_{2}\right|}\big|(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{d_{k}\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}+d_{k}\frac{v_{1}-v_{2}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}|}{1+\theta\left|\frac{v_{1}-v_{2}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}\right|}\big|(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},u_{1}\neq u_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{d_{k}\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}+d_{k}t|}{1+\theta\left|t\right|}\big|t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

We divide our proof in three cases, namely:

Case 1.
c_{k}=0

Case 2.
c_{k}<0

Case 3.
c_{k}>0

In the first case, we have

\mathcal{M}=\{d_{k}\}\cup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta d_{k}x}{1+\theta x}\big|x\geq 0\right\}.

Since

\sup\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta d_{k}x}{1+\theta x}\big|x\geq 0\right\}=\begin{cases}d_{k},&\qquad d_{k}\geq a_{k}\\
a_{k}=a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|,&\qquad d_{k}<a_{k}\end{cases},

via Lemma
2.1
, we get

\text{Lip}(f_{k})=\sup\mathcal{M}=\max\{d_{k},a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|\}.

In the second case, we have

\mathcal{M}=\{d_{k}\}\cup\mathcal{M}_{1}\cup\mathcal{M}_{2}\cup\mathcal{M}_{3},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{a_{k}+\theta c_{k}+\theta d_{k}t}{1+\theta t}\big|t\geq-\frac{c_{k}}{d_{k}}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{a_{k}-\theta c_{k}-\theta d_{k}t}{1+\theta t}\big|0<t<-\frac{c_{k}}{d_{k}}\right\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\frac{a_{k}-\theta c_{k}-\theta d_{k}t}{1-\theta t}\big|t\leq 0\right\}.

Studying the monotonicity of the expressions that define the sets
\mathcal{M}_{1}
,
\mathcal{M}_{2}
and
\mathcal{M}_{3}

\displaystyle\sup\mathcal{M}_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}d_{k},&\qquad a_{k}+\theta c_{k}\leq d_{k}\\
\frac{a_{k}d_{k}}{d_{k}-\theta c_{k}},&\qquad a_{k}+\theta c_{k}>d_{k}\end{cases},

\displaystyle\sup\mathcal{M}_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{k}-\theta c_{k}=a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|

and

\displaystyle\sup\mathcal{M}_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{k}-\theta c_{k}=a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|,&\qquad a_{k}-\theta c_{k}>d_{k}\\
d_{k},&\qquad a_{k}-\theta c_{k}\leq d_{k}\end{cases}.

Based on Lemma
2.1
, we get

\text{Lip}(f_{k})=\sup\mathcal{M}=\max\{d_{k},\sup\mathcal{M}_{1},\sup\mathcal{M}_{2},\sup\mathcal{M}_{3}\},

hence

\text{Lip}(f_{k})=\max\{d_{k},a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|,\sup\mathcal{M}_{1},\sup\mathcal{M}_{3}\}.

.

Describing the balls with respect to the metric
\rho

For
r\geq 0
and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the closed ball with center
(x,y)
and radius
r
, with respect to
\rho
, is the set

\displaystyle\{

\displaystyle(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}|\rho\bm{(}(x,y),(u,v)\bm{)}\leq r\}

\displaystyle=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}||x-u|+\theta|y-v|\leq r\}=:R[(x,y),r],

whose border is the rhombus having the vertixes
V_{1}\left(x+r,y\right)
,
V_{2}\left(x-r,y\right)
,
V_{3}\left(x,y+\frac{r}{\theta}\right)
and
V_{4}\left(x,y-\frac{r}{\theta}\right).

Remark 3.2
.

Note that

\max\{v\in\mathbb{R}|\text{ there exists }u\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }(u,v)\in R[(x,y),r]\}=y+\frac{r}{\theta}

and

\min\{v\in\mathbb{R}|\text{ there exists }u\in\mathbb{R}\text{ such that }(u,v)\in R[(x,y),r]\}=y-\frac{r}{\theta}.

Obtaining a cover for
G_{f}

Summing up, in the previous framework, via Theorem 2.1, by relabeling the functions
f_{k}
such that
s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\dots\leq s_{n}
, we get the following result:

Theorem 3.1
.

G_{f}\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{k\in\{1,\dots,\;n-1\}}R\left[\gamma_{k},Ms_{k}\frac{1+s_{n}}{1-s_{{n-1}}s_{n}}\right]\right)\bigcup R\left[\gamma_{n},Ms_{n}\frac{1+s_{{n-1}}}{1-s_{{n-1}}s_{n}}\right],

where

\gamma_{k}=\left(\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}},\frac{b_{k}c_{k}}{(1-a_{k})(1-d_{k})}+\frac{e_{k}}{1-d_{k}}\right),

s_{k}=\max\{a_{k}+\theta|c_{k}|,d_{k}\},

for all
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\theta=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,&&c_{1}=c_{2}=\dots=c_{n}=0;\\
&\frac{1-\max_{k\in\{1\dots n\}}a_{k}}{2\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}|c_{k}|},&&\text{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

and

M=\max_{i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\left\{\left|\frac{b_{i}}{1-a_{i}}-\frac{b_{j}}{1-a_{j}}\right|+\theta\left|\frac{c_{i}b_{i}}{(1-a_{i})(1-d_{i})}+\frac{e_{i}}{1-d_{i}}-\frac{c_{j}b_{j}}{(1-a_{j})(1-d_{j})}-\frac{e_{j}}{1-d_{j}}\right|\right\}.

The set

\mathcal{C_{\mathcal{S}}}:=\left(\bigcup_{k\in\{1,\dots,\;n-1\}}R\left[\gamma_{k},Ms_{k}\frac{1+s_{n}}{1-s_{{n-1}}s_{n}}\right]\right)\bigcup R\left[\gamma_{n},Ms_{n}\frac{1+s_{{n-1}}}{1-s_{{n-1}}s_{n}}\right]

will be called the covering of
G_{f}
associated to the system
\mathcal{S}
.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we will denote

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}=\mathcal{C}_{m},

where

\mathcal{S}_{m}=\left((\mathbb{R}^{2},\rho),(f_{k_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{k_{m}})_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\right).

Remark 3.3
.

According to Theorem 3.2 from

1
]
, by applying Theorem 3.1 for the IFSs
\mathcal{S}_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain coverings of
G_{f}
with balls having the radii as small as we want.

Let us note that, in view of Remark 3.2 from
[
1
]
, we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}h(\mathcal{C}_{m},G_{f})=0.

Remark 3.4
.

In the above mentioned framework, for
k_{1},k_{2},\dots k_{m}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
, we have

(f_{k_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f_{k_{m}})(x,y)=(a_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}x+b_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}},c_{k_{1}k_{2},\dots k_{m}}x+d_{k_{1}k_{2},\dots k_{m}}y+e_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}),

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
a_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}
,
b_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}
,
c_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}
,
d_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}
and
e_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}
can be obtained recursively from the following formulas

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}&=a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}\dots k_{m}}\\
b_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}&=a_{k_{1}}b_{k_{2}\dots k_{m}}+b_{k_{1}}\\
c_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}&=c_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}\dots k_{m}}+d_{k_{1}}c_{k_{2}k_{3}\dots k_{m}}\\
d_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}&=d_{k_{1}}d_{k_{2}\dots k_{m}}\\
e_{k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{m}}&=c_{k_{1}}b_{k_{2}\dots k_{m}}+d_{k_{1}}e_{k_{2}\dots k_{m}}+e_{k_{1}},\end{aligned}\right.

which represent a very good tool for improving the efficiency of the computations involved.

Obtaining an estimation for
Imf

Based on Remark
3.2
and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3.2
.

In the previous framework, we have:

Imf\subseteq[A,B],

where

\displaystyle A=\min

\displaystyle\left\{\min_{k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}}\left(\frac{b_{k}c_{k}}{(1-a_{k})(1-d_{k})}+\frac{e_{k}}{1-d_{k}}-\frac{Ms_{k}(1+s_{n})}{\theta(1-s_{n-1}s_{n})}\right),\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{b_{n}c_{n}}{(1-a_{n})(1-d_{n})}+\frac{e_{n}}{1-d_{n}}-\frac{Ms_{n}(1+s_{n-1})}{\theta(1-s_{n-1}s_{n})}\right\}

and

\displaystyle B=\max

\displaystyle\left\{\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}}\left(\frac{b_{k}c_{k}}{(1-a_{k})(1-d_{k})}+\frac{e_{k}}{1-d_{k}}+\frac{Ms_{k}(1+s_{n})}{\theta(1-s_{n-1}s_{n})}\right),\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{b_{n}c_{n}}{(1-a_{n})(1-d_{n})}+\frac{e_{n}}{1-d_{n}}+\frac{Ms_{n}(1+s_{n-1})}{\theta(1-s_{n-1}s_{n})}\right\}.

Corollary 3.1
.

In the previous framework, we have

G_{f}\subseteq[a,b]\times[A,B].

Remark 3.5
.

Note that,
A
and
B
can be algebraically expressed in terms of the data set
(x_{0},y_{0})
,
(x_{1},y_{1}),\dots
,
(x_{n},y_{n})
and the scaling factors
d_{k}
.

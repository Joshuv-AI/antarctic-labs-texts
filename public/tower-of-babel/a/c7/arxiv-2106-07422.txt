Gap asymptotics of the directions in an Ammann–Beenker-like quasicrystal
Gustav Hammarhjelm

Abstract
It is known that the limiting gap distribution of the directions to visible points in planar quasicrystals of cut-and-project type exists as a continuous function F ⁡ ( s ) F(s) . In this article we study the asymptotic behaviour of said limiting gap distribution in the particular case of an Ammann–Beenker-like quasicrystal 𝒫 \mathcal{P} ; more precisely we show that in this case F ( s ) = C 𝒫 s − 2 + 𝒪 ( s − 17 / 8 ) F(s)=C_{\mathcal{P}}s^{-2}+\mathcal{O}(s^{-17/8}) as s → ∞ s\to\infty with an explicit constant C 𝒫 > 0 C_{\mathcal{P}}>0 .

Introduction

To each locally finite point set
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we associate its subset

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}:=\{x\in\mathcal{P}\mid tx\notin\mathcal{P},\forall t\in(0,1)\}

of points
visible
from the origin. Given
T>0
we let
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{T}:=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}\cap B_{T}(0)
, where

B_{T}(0):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left\|x\right\|<T\}.

In dimension
d=2
, we can reduce each
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{T}
to a set of real numbers containing the
directions
in which points of
\mathcal{P}
within
B_{T}(0)
can be seen. More precisely: For
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let
\widehat{N}(T)
denote the number of visible points of
\mathcal{P}
within
B_{T}(0)
. Arrange the normalised arguments of the points of
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{T}
, which are numbers in
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
, in an increasing list as

-\tfrac{1}{2}<\widehat{\xi}_{T,1}<\widehat{\xi}_{T,2}<\cdots<\widehat{\xi}_{T,\widehat{N}(T)}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

In addition, let
\widehat{\xi}_{T,0}:=\widehat{\xi}_{T,\widehat{N}(T)}-1
. For each
1\leq i\leq\widehat{N}_{T}
, we set
\widehat{d}_{T,i}:=\widehat{N}(T)(\widehat{\xi}_{T,i}-\widehat{\xi}_{T,i-1})
, which is the
i
:th
normalised gap
(between the angles of visible points) in
\mathcal{P}
.

We will be interested in the complementary distribution function
F_{T}
of the probability measure

\mu_{T}:=\frac{1}{\widehat{N}_{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{\widehat{N}_{T}}\delta_{\widehat{d}_{T,i}}

comprised of the Dirac measures at the normalised gaps
\widehat{d}_{T,i}
, that is,

F_{T}(s):=\frac{\#\{1\leq i\leq\widehat{N}_{T}\mid\widehat{d}_{T,i}\geq s\}}{\widehat{N}_{T}}=\mu_{T}([s,\infty)).

We say that
the limiting distribution of normalised gaps in

\mathcal{P}
exists if
\mu_{T}
converges weakly to a probability measure
\mu
, that is, if
F_{T}(s)
converges to
F(s):=\mu([s,\infty))
at every continuity point of
F
. In this case, we call
F
the limiting distribution of normalised gaps in
\mathcal{P}
, or simply the limiting gap distribution (of
\mathcal{P}
).

The limiting distribution of normalised gaps is known to exist as a continuous function in several interesting cases. In
[
3
]
, Boca, Cobeli, and Zaharescu determined the limiting distribution of normalised gaps explicitly in the case of
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. In
[
8
]
, Marklof and Strömbergsson investigated the fine-scale distribution of the directions of points in affine lattices and described those distributions in terms of probability measures on associated homogeneous spaces. In
[
1
]
, Baake, Götze, Huck and Jakobi numerically computed the distribution of
\widehat{d}_{T,i}
for large
T
for several prominent examples of quasicrystals, giving indications of what to expect regarding a minimal gap in the distributions as well as asymptotic tail behaviour of the distributions. In
[
11
, Corollary 3]
, Marklof and Strömbergsson proved that the limiting distribution of normalised gaps exists as a continuous function for every planar, regular cut-and-project set. Again, these distributions are described explicitly in terms of a probability measure on an associated homogeneous space of cut-and-project sets. In this article it was also shown that there are quasicrystals with a
minimal gap
, i.e. quasicrystals
\mathcal{P}
such that the limiting distribution function is equal to
0
for all
s<m_{\mathcal{P}}
for some
m_{\mathcal{P}}>0
.

In
[
5
]
the limiting gap distributions of several families of quasicrystals, including the Ammann–Beenker point set and Penrose quasicrystals, were studied. In particular, the minimal gap of the corresponding limiting gap distribution functions were determined.
In the present article, we continue the study of the limiting gap distribution of Ammann–Beenker-like point sets initiated in
[
5
]
by determining the asymptotic behaviour of the limiting gap distribution
F(s)
as
s\to\infty
of a particular cut-and-project set. The main result of the present article is the following; it is proved in
4.25
below.

Theorem
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be the Minkowski embedding of
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Let
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the open, regular octagon of sidelength
\sqrt{2}
centered at the origin, and with sides perpendicularly bisected by the coordinate axes. Let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the cut-and-project set obtained from
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{W}
and let
F(s)
be its associated limiting gap distribution function. Then

F(s)=C_{\mathcal{P}}s^{-2}+\mathcal{O}(s^{-17/8})

as
s\to\infty
, where

C_{\mathcal{P}}:=\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)}{\sqrt{2}\zeta_{K}(2)^{2}}

and
\zeta_{K}
is the Dedekind zeta function of
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
.

The set
\mathcal{P}
is
Ammann–Beenker-like
; it is closely related to the Ammann–Beenker point set. The relation between these two sets will be made precise in
Section

2.3
below. The significant difference is that the Ammann–Beenker point set is a cut-and-project set with a window that differs from
\mathcal{W}
by an application of an invertible linear map. By working with
\mathcal{W}
we get a pleasant window exhibiting a high degree of symmetry, which will simplify some of the calculations in the paper, allowing us to make the asymptotic decay of the limiting gap distribution completely explicit. The main result of this paper should be seen as a proof of concept. The novelty is that we are able to explicitly determine the asymptotic behaviour of the limiting gap distribution of a planar quasicrystal of cut-and-project type.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In
Section

2
, we recall some necessary background, such as the definitions of cut-and-project sets as well as their associated homogeneous spaces. In section
Section

3
we determine the asymptotics of a function
G(s)
that satisfies
-G^{\prime}(s)=F(s)
. Determining the asymptotics of this function will then help us to determine the asymptotics of
F(s)
in
Section

4
.

2.1
Cut-and-project sets and associated homogeneous spaces

We briefly recall cut-and-project sets, following the notation of

If

\displaystyle\pi:

\displaystyle\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathrm{int}}:

\displaystyle\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\longmapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\hskip 28.45274pt

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\longmapsto(x_{d+1},\ldots,x_{n})

denote the projections onto the first

Definition 2.1
.

Given a lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{W}
. If the boundary of

we say that

is

.

With these assumptions on

and
\mathcal{W}
, the resulting cut-and-project set is always Delone, see
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
. Next, we recall the homogeneous space associated to a cut-and-project set, from
[
10
]
.

Let

be given as above and let
G:=\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
,
\Gamma:=\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Fix a lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta>0
,
g\in G
so that
\mathcal{L}=\delta^{1/n}\mathbb{Z}^{n}g
. Let
\varphi_{g}:\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow G
be the embedding given by

A\mapsto g\,\mathrm{diag}(A,I_{m})g^{-1},

where
\mathrm{diag}(A,I_{m})
is the block-diagonal matrix with blocks
A
and
I_{m}
. By Ratner’s theorems, there exists a unique, closed, connected subgroup
H_{g}\subset G
such that
H_{g}\cap\Gamma
is a lattice in
H_{g}
,
\varphi_{g}(\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R}))\subset H_{g}
and such that the closure of
\Gamma\backslash\Gamma\varphi_{g}(\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R}))
in
\Gamma\backslash G
is equal to
\Gamma\backslash\Gamma H_{g}
. Let
X
denote the homogeneous space
(H_{g}\cap\Gamma)\backslash H_{g}
and
\mu
its unique, right
H_{g}
-invariant probability measure. This space can be identified with
\Gamma\backslash\Gamma H_{g}
; we also let
\mu
denote the right
H_{g}
-invariant probability measure on this space. For a fixed

we get, for each

The space of cut-and-project sets obtained as

is the homogeneous space of cut-and-project sets associated to

. We note that for random

in

, the point process

is

-invariant, in view of

.

2.2
The limiting gap distribution of a planar, regular cut-and-project set

We will now recall the characterisation of the limiting gap distribution of a regular, planar cut-and-project set from
[
11
]
. A point set
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to have
asymptotic density
if there is a number
\theta(\mathcal{P})
, the
density
of
\mathcal{P}
, such that

\lim_{T\to\infty}T^{-d}\#(\mathcal{P}\cap TD)=\theta(\mathcal{P})\mathrm{vol}(D)

holds for all Jordan measurable
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is well-known that regular cut-and-project sets have an asymptotic density and in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
it was shown that the density of the subset of visible points of such a set also exists.

We now fix
d=2
and a regular cut-and-project set
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{L})
. Fix
g\in G
and
\delta>0
so that
\mathcal{L}=\delta^{1/n}\mathbb{Z}^{n}g
. Let
X
be the homogeneous space associated to
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{L})
. Given
s>0
, let

\mathfrak{C}(s):=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x_{1}<1,|x_{2}|<s\theta(\widehat{\mathcal{P}})^{-1}\}

(this is well defined, since
0<\theta(\widehat{\mathcal{P}})\leq\theta(\mathcal{P})
for regular cut-and-project sets). Combining Corollary 3, Theorem 4 and Section 11 of
[
11
]
we find that the limiting gap distribution of
\mathcal{P}
exists as a continuous function given by

F(s):=-\frac{d}{ds}\mu(\{x\in X\mid\widehat{\mathcal{P}^{x}}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)=\emptyset\}).

(1)

In the present article we study the asymptotics of
F(s)
as
s\to\infty
in the particular case of an Ammann–Beenker like point set, which we will now proceed to define.

The Ammann–Beenker-like point set
\mathcal{P}

3
Asymptotics of
\mu(\{x\in X\mid\widehat{\mathcal{P}^{x}}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)=\emptyset\})

As a first step, we determine the asymptotics of

G(s):=\mu(\{x\in X\mid\widehat{\mathcal{P}^{x}}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)=\emptyset\})

as
s\to\infty
. In view of (
1

-G(s)

is, as we will see, simpler than calculating the asymptotics of
F(s)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda:=1+\sqrt{2}
be the fundamental unit of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Given
s>0
, let
r\in\mathbb{Z}
be the integer so that
\lambda^{2r}\leq s^{1/4}<\lambda^{2(r+1)}
. Let also
T(s)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the open triangle with vertices
(0,0)
and
(s/\theta(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}))^{1/2}(1,\pm 1)
and set

\mathcal{T}(s):=(T(s)\times\mathcal{W})\mathrm{diag}(\lambda^{-2r},\lambda^{-2r},\lambda^{2r},\lambda^{2r}).

We then have

G(s)=\int_{X}I(\mathcal{L}(x)\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset)\,d\mu(x).

(Here and throughout the article,
I(\cdot)
denotes the indicator function.)

Proof.

As
\mathfrak{C}(s)
is star-shaped with respect to the origin, we have

\{x\in X\mid\widehat{\mathcal{P}^{x}}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)=\emptyset\}=\{x\in X\mid\mathcal{P}^{x}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)=\emptyset\}.

Due to the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariance of
x\mapsto\mathcal{P}^{x}
we have

\mu(\{x\in X\mid\mathcal{P}^{x}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)=\emptyset\})=\mu(\{x\in X\mid\mathcal{P}^{x}\cap\mathfrak{C}(s)A=\emptyset\})

for each
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, we may freely modify
\mathfrak{C}(s)
by multiplying with
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-matrices, and since the areas of
\mathfrak{C}(s)
and
T(s)
coincide we have

G(s)=\mu(\{x\in X\mid\mathcal{P}^{x}\cap T(s)=\emptyset\}).

If
x=\Gamma ghg^{-1}
we have, using the definition of
\mathcal{P}^{x}
, that
\mathcal{P}^{x}\cap T(s)=\emptyset
is equivalent with
\mathcal{L}(x)\cap(T(s)\times\mathcal{W})=\emptyset
. Since
\mathcal{L}(x)=\mathcal{L}h
and
\mathrm{diag}(\lambda^{-2r},\lambda^{-2r},\lambda^{2r},\lambda^{2r})
commutes with
h\in H
and fixes
\mathcal{L}
the proof of the lemma is complete.

0<\left\|v(\alpha_{21},\alpha_{22})\right\|\leq C
. Furthermore,
\alpha_{21},\alpha_{22}
can be chosen to be relatively prime at the expense of enlarging
C
slightly. Indeed, suppose
d
is a greatest common divisor of
\alpha_{21}
and
\alpha_{22}
. We may assume that
1\leq d<\lambda
and hence
|\sigma(d)|\gg 1
. Letting
\alpha_{21}^{\prime}=\alpha_{21}/d
and
\alpha_{22}^{\prime}=\alpha_{22}/d
there is an absolute constant
C^{\prime}
so that
0<\left\|v(\alpha_{21}^{\prime},\alpha_{22}^{\prime})\right\|\leq C^{\prime}
. Hence,
y_{1}^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime}\geq(C^{\prime})^{-1}
and so
y_{1}^{\prime}y_{2}^{\prime}\geq(C^{\prime})^{-2}=:t
.

It now suffices to show that we can modify
h
with
y_{1}y_{1}\geq t
,
y_{1}/y_{2}\in[\lambda^{-2},\lambda^{2}]
so that
0\leq\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<\pi
. To this end, assume first that
y_{1}/y_{2}\in[\lambda^{-2},1]
. Then, if

h^{\prime}=h(x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime},\theta_{1}^{\prime},\theta_{2}^{\prime}):=M_{1}h

we have

y_{1}y_{2}=y_{1}^{\prime}y_{2}^{\prime}
,
y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{2}^{\prime}=\lambda^{2}(y_{1}/y_{2})\in[1,\lambda^{2}]
,
\theta_{1}^{\prime}=\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}^{\prime}=\theta_{2}+\pi
. If
y_{1}/y_{2}\in[1,\lambda^{2}]
, multiply
h
with
M_{-1}
instead. To modify
\theta_{1}
, modify
h
with
\sigma(M_{\pm 1})
in place of
M_{\pm 1}
. ∎

3.2
Reduction of
\mathfrak{D}_{t}
for lattices avoiding large balls

Given
t>0
so that
\Gamma_{K}\mathfrak{D}_{t}=H
,
we now intend to improve the Siegel domain
\mathfrak{D}_{t}
by removing some redundance, that is, we study when
h,h^{\prime}\in H
give rise to the same lattice, i.e. when
\mathcal{L}h=\mathcal{L}h^{\prime}
. We will use the following notation throughout the article: if
f,g
are non-negative functions, we write
f\ll g
to indicate that there is an absolute constant
C>0
so that
f\leq Cg
; if
f\ll g
and
g\ll f
we write
f\asymp g
.

The following lemma is inspired by
[
9
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Take

h=h(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}

and suppose
\mathcal{L}h
avoids a ball of large radius
R
. Then
y_{1},y_{2}\gg R
.

Proof.

We can write
8^{1/4}gh
explicitly as

\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}&y_{1}^{-1}x_{1}&y_{2}&y_{2}^{-1}x_{2}\\
\sqrt{2}y_{1}&\sqrt{2}y_{1}^{-1}x_{1}&-\sqrt{2}y_{2}&-\sqrt{2}y_{2}^{-1}x_{2}\\
0&y_{1}^{-1}&0&y_{2}^{-1}\\
0&\sqrt{2}y_{1}^{-1}&0&-\sqrt{2}y_{2}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{diag}(k(\theta_{1}),k(\theta_{2})).

(4)

Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{4}
denote the row vectors of
8^{1/4}gh
so that
b_{1},\ldots,b_{4}
is a lattice basis of
\mathcal{L}h
. We have

\left\|b_{1}\right\|\leq y_{1}+y_{1}^{-1}|x_{1}|+y_{2}+y_{2}^{-1}|x_{2}|.

Since
h\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}
, we have that
y_{1},y_{2}\gg 1
.
We have
y_{i}^{-1}|x_{i}|\ll 1\ll y_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Also,
y_{2}\leq\lambda^{2}y_{1}
. Hence
\left\|b_{1}\right\|\ll y_{1}
, and similarly,
\left\|b_{2}\right\|
,
\left\|b_{3}\right\|
,
\left\|b_{4}\right\|\ll y_{1}
.

Let
\sum_{i=1}^{4}r_{i}b_{i}
be the centre of a ball of radius
R
that has empty intersection with
\mathcal{L}h
. Find integers
n_{i}
with
|n_{i}-r_{i}|\leq 1/2
. Note that
\sum_{i}n_{i}b_{i}\in\mathcal{L}h
and

R<\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{4}(n_{i}-r_{i})b_{i}\right\|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\left\|b_{i}\right\|\ll y_{1}\ll y_{2},

which is the claim of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Fix
t>0
so that
\Gamma_{K}\mathfrak{D}_{t}=H
.
Take
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}
and suppose that
\mathcal{L}h=\mathcal{L}h^{\prime}
. If
\mathcal{L}h
and
\mathcal{L}h^{\prime}
avoid a sufficiently large ball, then the
\theta
-parameters of
h
and
h^{\prime}
agree. Furthermore, if
y_{1},y_{2}
and
y_{1}^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime}
denote the Iwasawa parameters of
h
and
h^{\prime}
, respectively, we have
y_{1}/y_{2}=\lambda^{4r}(y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{2}^{\prime})
for some
r\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Finally, the set

\mathfrak{D}_{t}^{\prime}:=\{h\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}\mid y_{1}/y_{2}\in(-\lambda^{2},\lambda^{2})\}

gives an irredundant representation of almost all lattices
\mathcal{L}h
that avoid a sufficiently large ball; that is, if
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}h=\mathcal{L}h^{\prime}
, then
h=h^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In view of
3.3
, the
y
-parameters of
h
and
h^{\prime}
are large. As usual,
h
and
h^{\prime}
have Iwasawa parameters
x_{i},y_{i},\theta_{i}
and
x_{i}^{\prime},y_{i}^{\prime},\theta_{i}^{\prime}
, respectively. As in the proof of
3.3
, let
b_{i}
and
b_{i}^{\prime}
denote the row vectors of
8^{1/4}gh
and
8^{1/4}gh^{\prime}
respectively. Since the
y
-parameters of
h
and
h^{\prime}
are large, the lengths of
b_{3},b_{4},b_{3}^{\prime}
and
b_{4}^{\prime}
are large. We first claim that

\mathbb{Z}b_{3}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}=\mathbb{Z}b_{3}^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}^{\prime}.

Indeed, take
v=\sum_{i=1}^{4}n_{i}b_{i}\in\mathcal{L}h=\mathcal{L}h^{\prime}
and consider

v\cdot e_{1}k=y_{1}(n_{1}+n_{2}\sqrt{2})
and
v\cdot e_{3}k=y_{2}(n_{1}-n_{2}\sqrt{2})
,
where
k=\mathrm{diag}(k(\theta_{1}),k(\theta_{2}))
. Since for a unit vector
u
we have
|u\cdot v|\leq\left\|v\right\|
by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have that the length of
v
can only be so small that
v=b_{3}^{\prime}
if
n_{1}=n_{2}=0
(recall that
\left\|b_{3}^{\prime}\right\|
is small when
y_{1}^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime}
are large). Therefore,
b_{3}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}b_{3}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}
. Similarly,
b_{4}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}b_{3}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}
and conversely,
b_{3},b_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}b_{3}^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}^{\prime}
.

Now,
\mathbb{Z}b_{3}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}=\mathbb{Z}b_{3}^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}^{\prime}
implies that
|\theta_{i}-\theta_{i}^{\prime}|\in\{0,\pi\}
for
i\in{1,2}
. To verify this, let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the projections onto the first and last two coordinates, respectively. We have

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}b_{3}+\mathbb{R}b_{4})=\mathbb{R}(\sin\theta_{1},\cos\theta_{1})=\mathbb{R}(\sin\theta_{1}^{\prime},\cos\theta_{1}^{\prime})=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}b_{3}^{\prime}+\mathbb{R}b_{4}^{\prime})

which gives
|\theta_{1}-\theta_{1}^{\prime}|\in\{0,\pi\}
. The other claim is proved similarly using
\pi_{2}
. Hence, we have proved the first statement of the lemma.

Using the first statement of the lemma and
\mathbb{Z}b_{3}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}=\mathbb{Z}b_{3}^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}^{\prime}
we find that

\mathbb{Z}(y_{1}^{-1},y_{2}^{-1})+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2}(y_{1}^{-1},-y_{2}^{-1})=\mathbb{Z}((y_{1}^{\prime})^{-1},(y_{2}^{\prime})^{-1})+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2}((y_{1}^{\prime})^{-1},-(y_{2}^{\prime})^{-1}).

We also have
y_{1}y_{2}=y_{1}^{\prime}y_{2}^{\prime}
by comparing the covolumes of
\mathbb{Z}b_{3}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}=\mathbb{Z}b_{3}^{\prime}+\mathbb{Z}b_{4}^{\prime}
. This implies

\mathbb{Z}(y_{2},y_{1})+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2}(y_{2},-y_{1})=\mathbb{Z}(y_{2}^{\prime},y_{1}^{\prime})+\mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2}(y_{2}^{\prime},-y_{1}^{\prime}).

(5)

Note that
(y_{2},y_{1})
is an element of the left hand side of (
5
), and that a general element of the right hand side is of the form
(\alpha y_{2}^{\prime},\sigma(\alpha)y_{1}^{\prime})
with
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Thus, there is
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
so that
y_{2}=\alpha y_{2}^{\prime}
and
y_{1}=\sigma(\alpha)y_{1}^{\prime}
which implies that
y_{2}/y_{2}^{\prime}=\alpha
and
y_{1}/y_{1}^{\prime}=\sigma(\alpha)
. From
y_{1}y_{2}=y_{1}^{\prime}y_{2}^{\prime}
we see that
y_{2}/y_{2}^{\prime}=y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{1}
which implies that
\alpha^{-1}=\sigma(\alpha)
so that
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{*}
. Since
y_{1},y_{2},y_{1}^{\prime},y_{2}^{\prime}
are positive, it follows that both
\alpha
and
\sigma(\alpha)
are positive, hence
\alpha=\lambda^{2r}
for some integer
r
.
Now note that

y_{1}/y_{2}=(\sigma(x)y_{1}^{\prime})/(xy_{2}^{\prime})=(\sigma(x)/x)(y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{2}^{\prime})=\lambda^{-4r}(y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{2}^{\prime}).

Since
y_{1}/y_{2},y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{2}^{\prime}\in[\lambda^{-2},\lambda^{2}]
it follows that
r\in\{-1,0,1\}
.

Note that if
r=0
, then
y_{1}y_{2}=y_{1}^{\prime}y_{2}^{\prime}
and
y_{1}/y_{2}=y_{1}^{\prime}/y_{2}^{\prime}
implies that
y_{1}=y_{1}^{\prime}
and
y_{2}=y_{2}^{\prime}
and also
x_{1}=x_{1}^{\prime}
and
x_{2}=x_{2}^{\prime}
. If
r\in\{-1,1\}
then
y_{1}/y_{2}\in\{\lambda^{-2},\lambda^{2}\}
. Since
\mathfrak{D}_{t}\setminus\mathfrak{D}_{t}^{\prime}
is of measure
0
with respect to Haar measure of
H
, it follow that
\mathfrak{D}_{t}^{\prime}
gives an irredundant representation of almost all lattices
\mathcal{L}h
that avoid a large ball.
∎

3.3
Asymptotics of
G(s)

Fix
t>0
so that
\Gamma_{K}\mathfrak{D}_{t}=H
.
Fix

that is so large that so that
3.4
holds, i.e. so that
\mathfrak{D}_{t}^{\prime}
gives an irredundant representation of the lattices
\mathcal{L}h
,

, that avoid

we find that

\displaystyle G(s)=\int_{X}I(\mathcal{L}(x)\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset)\,d\mu(x)=\int_{\mathfrak{D}_{t}^{\prime}}I(\mathcal{L}h\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset)\,d\mu_{H}(h),

which induces a probability measure on

where

and
h=h(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
.

For each

, let

We have

\begin{split}G(s)&=\underbrace{c_{H}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{Y_{t}}\int_{\mathfrak{F}}y_{1}^{-3}y_{2}^{-3}I(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}h}\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset)\,dx_{1}dx_{2}dy_{1}dy_{2}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}}_{=:G_{M}(s)}\\

(6)

We show in
3.9
below that there is an explicit constant

such that

and then that

as

in

from which we conclude that
G(s)
decays like

as
s\to\infty
.

For fixed
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in[0,\pi)^{2}
, let
L_{1}=L_{1}(\theta_{1})\subset\mathbb{R}
be the projection of
\lambda^{-2r}T(s)k(-\theta_{1})
onto the
x
-axis and
L_{2}=L_{2}(\theta_{2})\subset\mathbb{R}
be the projection of
\lambda^{2r}\mathcal{W}k(-\theta_{2})
onto the
x
-axis. Given

, let
R_{1}(\theta_{1},x)
be the intersection of the vertical line through
(x,0)
and
\lambda^{-2r}T(s)k(-\theta_{1})
and let
R_{2}(\theta_{2},x)
be the intersection of the same line with

.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
h=h(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in\mathfrak{D}_{t}
and
s>0
we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}h}\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset
if and only if
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\cap(y^{-1}_{1}L_{1}(\theta_{1})\times y^{-1}_{2}L_{2}(\theta_{2}))=\emptyset

Proof.

Note that

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}h}=\{(\alpha y_{1},r_{1},\sigma(\alpha)y_{2},r_{2})k\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K},r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\}.

where
k=\mathrm{diag}(k(\theta_{1}),k(\theta_{2}))
. From the definitions of
\mathcal{T}(s)
,
L_{1}(\theta_{1})
and
L_{2}(\theta_{2})
it follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}h}\cap\mathcal{T}(s)\neq\emptyset

such that

which gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
h=h(x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
with
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathfrak{F}
,
(y_{1},y_{2})\in Y_{t}

. If

\mathcal{L}h\cap\mathcal{T}(s)\neq\emptyset

Proof.

From (
4
) it follows that

(\alpha y_{1},y_{1}^{-1}(\alpha x_{1}+\beta),\sigma(\alpha)y_{2},y_{2}^{-1}(\sigma(\alpha)x_{2}+\sigma(\beta)))k\in\mathcal{L}h

(7)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
(here

as usual). In particular, with

we find that
(0,y_{1}^{-1}\beta,0,y_{2}^{-1}\sigma(\beta))k\in\mathcal{L}h
. Since

∎

We will need the following fact.

Lemma 3.7
.

There is a constant

In view of

Lemma 3.8
.

For sufficiently large

G_{M}(s)=\frac{2\sqrt{2}c_{H}}{s}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{J}\int_{Y}\frac{I\left(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\cap\left(y_{1}^{-1}\ell_{1}(\theta_{1})\times y_{2}^{-1}\ell_{2}(\theta_{2})\right)=\emptyset\right)dy_{1}dy_{2}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}}{(y_{1}y_{2})^{3}},

\displaystyle G_{M}(s)

In the next proposition we evaluate the triple integral in (

Proposition 3.9

Proof.

Let

For any choice of a fundamental domain

be a fundamental domain of
\Gamma_{g}\backslash H_{g}

. We have that

\Gamma_{g}\backslash H_{g}
. Note that

is a subgroup of
\Gamma_{g}

(we use

It is verified that

\Gamma_{k}\backslash(H_{g})_{k}

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{F}_{k}}\chi_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}(hM_{k,z})\,d\mu_{k}(h)=\int_{(H_{g})_{k}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}M_{k,z}^{-1}}(h)\,d\mu_{k}(h)

\nu_{z}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{k\in R}\sum_{\gamma\in S^{(k)}}\int_{(H_{g})_{k}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}}(\gamma^{-1}hM_{k,z})\,d\mu_{k}(h).

\int_{U}\nu_{z}(\mathcal{E})\,dz=\int_{U\cap mH_{g}}\sum_{k\in R}\sum_{\gamma\in S^{(k)}}\int_{(H_{g})_{k}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}}(\gamma^{-1}hM_{k,z})\,d\mu_{k}(h)\,dz.

For fixed

(H_{g})_{k}=M^{-1}_{m,k}(H_{g})_{m}M_{m,k}

\displaystyle\int_{(H_{g})_{k}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}}(\gamma^{-1}hM_{k,z})\,d\mu_{k}(h)=\int_{(H_{g})_{m}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}}((M_{m,k}\gamma)^{-1}hM_{m,k}M_{k,z})\,d\mu_{m}(h).

\displaystyle\int_{U}\nu_{z}(\mathcal{E})\,dz=\int_{U\cap mH_{g}}\sum_{k\in R}\sum_{\gamma\in S^{(k)}}\int_{(H_{g})_{m}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}}(M_{m,k\gamma}^{-1}hM_{m,z})\,d\mu_{m}(h)\,dz.

\displaystyle\int_{U}\nu_{z}(\mathcal{E})\,dz=\int_{U\cap mH_{g}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}_{*}}\int_{(H_{g})_{m}}\chi_{\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{E}_{0}}(M_{m,k}^{-1}hM_{m,z})\,d\mu_{m}(h)\,dz.

Using (

\displaystyle\int_{U}\nu_{z}(\mathcal{E})\,dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}_{*}}\int_{H_{g}}\chi_{B_{k}}(M_{m,k}^{-1}h)\,d\mu(h)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}y_{1}(1+\mathcal{O}(s^{-1/4}z_{2}^{\prime}))+\mathcal{O}(s^{-1/2}z_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}y_{1}(1+\mathcal{O}(s^{-1/4}z_{2}^{\prime})).

(39)

By assuming

(40)

\ll s^{-5/4}\sum_{\gamma\in M_{s}}s^{-7/4}|\gamma|^{2}|\gamma|^{-2}=s^{-3}|M_{s}|.

Finally, note that if

4.3.2
The condition
\mathcal{L}_{v}h\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{(0,1,0,1)}

We continue our study of the expression for

\mathcal{L}_{v}h\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\mathcal{L}_{(0,1,0,1)}

\mathcal{I}_{2}(h)

Given
v\in\mathcal{L}
, let
\Pi_{v}=v+\mathbb{R}v_{1}+\mathbb{R}v_{2}=v+\mathbb{R}b_{1}+\mathbb{R}b_{2}
be the

corresponding to

(recall that
\mathcal{L}_{v}=v+\mathbb{Z}v_{1}+\mathbb{Z}v_{2}
). Given

and

, we call

an
exceptional subgrid
of
\mathcal{L}h
if
\mathcal{L}_{v}h\cap\mathcal{T}(s)=\emptyset
but
\Pi_{v}h\cap\mathcal{T}(s)\neq\emptyset

Note that a general vector in
\mathcal{L}
can be written as

v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}):=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\sigma(\beta_{1}),\sigma(\beta_{2}))

for some
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Lemma 4.16
.

For
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
we have

\mathcal{L}_{v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}=\mathcal{L}_{v(\beta_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{2}^{\prime})}

if and only if
-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}=-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}^{\prime}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}^{\prime}
. Thus, every
v\in\mathcal{L}
corresponds to a unique
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, namely
\delta=\delta(v)=-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}
, where
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{2}
is any pair so that
v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{L}_{v}
.

In particular,
v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{L}_{(0,1,0,1)}
if and only if
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in(0,1)+\mathcal{O}_{K}(\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}_{v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})}=\mathcal{L}_{v(\beta_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{2}^{\prime})}
so that, in particular,
v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=v(\beta_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{2}^{\prime})+k_{1}v_{1}+k_{2}v_{2}
for some
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The pair of the first two coordinates of the right hand side reads
(\beta_{1}^{\prime}+(k_{1}+\sqrt{2}k_{2})\alpha_{3},\beta_{2}^{\prime}+(k_{1}+\sqrt{2}k_{2})\alpha_{4})
and must be equal to
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
. From this it follows that
-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}=-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}^{\prime}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}^{\prime}
.

Suppose that
-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}=-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}^{\prime}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}^{\prime}
. Then,
\alpha_{4}(\beta_{1}^{\prime}-\beta_{1})=\alpha_{3}(\beta_{2}^{\prime}-\beta_{2})
. Recalling that
\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}
are relatively prime, it follows that
\beta_{1}^{\prime}-\beta_{1}=\alpha_{3}(k_{1}+\sqrt{2}k_{2})
and
\beta_{2}^{\prime}-\beta_{2}=\alpha_{4}(k_{1}+\sqrt{2}k_{2})
for some integers
k_{1},k_{2}
. It is then verified that
v(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=v(\beta_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{2}^{\prime})+k_{1}v_{1}+k_{2}v_{2}
, from which the other implication follows.

The final claim follows by noting that
(0,1,0,1)=v(0,1)
. This implies that
-\alpha_{4}\beta_{1}+\alpha_{3}\beta_{2}=\alpha_{3}
or, equivalently,
\beta_{1}\alpha_{4}=(\beta_{2}-1)\alpha_{3}
. Using again that
\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}
are relatively prime, it follows that
\beta_{2}-1=x\alpha_{4}
and
\beta_{1}=x\alpha_{3}
for some
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, which gives the last claim.
∎

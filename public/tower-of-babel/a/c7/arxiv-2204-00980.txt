Global existence and optimal decay rate of the classical solution to 3-D Radiative Hydrodynamics model with or without Heat Conductivity
Guiqiong Gong, Jiawei Zhou, Boran Zhu

Abstract
The classical solution of the 3-D radiative hydrodynamics model is studied in H k H^{k} -norm under two different conditions, with and without heat conductivity. We have proved the following results in both cases. First, when the H k H^{k} norm of the initial perturbation around a constant state is sufficiently small and the integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , a unique classical solution to such Cauchy problem is shown to exist. Second, if we further assume that the L 1 L^{1} norm of the initial perturbation is small too, the i-order( 0 ≤ i ≤ k − 2 0\leq i\leq k-2 ) derivative of the solutions have the decay rate of ( 1 + t ) − 3 4 − i 2 (1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{i}{2}} in H 2 H^{2} norm. Third, from the results above we can see that for radiative hydrodynamics, the radiation can do the same job as the heat conduction, which means if the thermal conductivity coefficient turns to 0 0 , because of the effect of radiation, the solvability of the system and decay rate of the solution stay the same. Keywords: Radiation hydrodynamics; Optimal decay rate; Spectrum analysis

Introduction

The key aim of studying the radiation hydrodynamics is to understand the motion of compressible fluids including radiation effects. The importance of thermal radiation in physical problems increases as the temperature is raised. At moderate temperatures, the role of the radiation is transporting energy by radiative processes. At higher temperatures, the energy and momentum densities of the radiation field may become dominate the corresponding fluid quantities. In this paper, we consider a radiative hydrodynamics model of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations coupled with an elliptic equation for radiative flux, which is used to describe the motion of viscous and heat-conducting fluids with radiative effects.
This model always can be used to simulate, for instance, nonlinear stellar pulsation, supernova explosions and stellar winds astrophysics and so on. The compressible radiative hydrodynamics system takes the form of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho\bm{u})=0,\\
&(\rho\bm{u})_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho\bm{u}\otimes\bm{u})+\nabla P=2\mu{\rm div}\left(\mathcal{D}\left(\bm{u}\right)\right)+\mu^{\prime}\nabla{\rm div}\bm{u},\\
&(\rho E)_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho\bm{u}E+\bm{u}P)+{\rm div}\bm{q}=\kappa\Delta\theta+{\rm div}\left(2\mu\mathcal{D}\left(\bm{u}\right)\cdot\bm{u}+\mu^{\prime}{\rm div}\bm{u}\ \bm{u}\right),\\
&-\nabla{\rm div}\bm{q}+\bm{q}+\nabla\left(\theta^{4}\right)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here
\rho
,
\theta
, vectors
\bm{u},\ \bm{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
are respectively the mass density, absolute temperature, velocity field and radiative heat flux. Constants

and the internal energy

E=e+\tfrac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2},\quad P=R\rho\theta,\quad e=C_{v}\theta,

(1.2)

where

and

are the specific gas constants,
C_{v}
denotes specific heat at constant volume.
The fluid is considered to be Newtonian, so
\mathcal{D}\left(\bm{u}\right)
takes the form as

\mathcal{D}\left(\bm{u}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla\bm{u}+(\nabla\bm{u})^{\mathsf{T}}\right),

(1.3)

In this paper, we consider the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) in space

with initial data that

\displaystyle(\rho,\bm{u},\theta)(0,\bm{x})=(\rho_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\theta_{0})(x)\quad{\rm for}\ \ \bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.4)

and the far field behavior that

\displaystyle(\rho,\bm{u},\theta)(t,\bm{x})\rightarrow(\bar{\rho},0,\bar{\theta})\quad{\rm as}\ \ |\bm{x}|\rightarrow 0,t\geq 0,

(1.5)

without loss of generality, we set

Hence, by using the Gronwall’s inequality, we have that

\displaystyle F_{i+1}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim F_{i+1}(0)e^{-t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{3}{2}-(i+1)}\left(\|\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\|\nabla^{i+1}\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}+G_{i+1}^{2}(\tau)\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-(i+1)}\left(F_{i+1}(0)+\|\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\|\nabla^{i+1}\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}+G_{i+1}^{2}(t)\right),

which indicates that

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}+(i+1)}F_{i+1}(t)\lesssim\|\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{1}}+\|\nabla^{i+1}\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{2}+G_{i+1}^{2}(t).

(4.12)

Now add (
4.3
) to (
4.12
), we have

\displaystyle G_{i+1}(t)\lesssim\|\bm{U}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{1}\cap H^{i+3}}+G_{i+1}^{2}(t),

which completes the induction. With Strauss Lemma
2.4
, we can see that
G_{k}(t)
is bounded, which leads to (
4.1
). Apply
\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\ (0\leq|\alpha|\leq k+1)
to
\eqref{cns2}_{4}
, multiply it by
\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\bm{q}
and integrate the resultant equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to get

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}{\rm div}\bm{q}\|^{2}+\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\bm{q}\|^{2}\leq 4\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\nabla\mathcal{m}\|^{2}+\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\nabla\left(\mathcal{m}^{4}\right)\|^{2}+4\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\nabla\left(\mathcal{m}^{3}\right)\|^{2}+6\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\nabla\left(\mathcal{m}^{2}\right)\|^{2}.

We can see the fact from (
4.1
) that the nonlinear terms decay faster than the linear term
4\|\partial_{\bm{x}}^{\alpha}\nabla\mathcal{m}\|^{2}
, which gives (
4.2
).
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

If there exists a small enough constant
\epsilon_{3}>0
such that the initial data satisfies

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{n}_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})\|_{L^{1}\cap H^{k}}\leq\epsilon_{3}\quad(\ \text{for any integer}\ k\geq 2),

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\|\mathcal{n}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}-c_{0}^{\prime}\|(\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})\|_{L^{1}}^{2}>C\epsilon_{3}^{2},

where constants
C^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime}
are determined in Proposition
3.7
.
Then, there exist two positive constants
a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2}
such that

a^{\prime}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\|(\mathcal{n},\bm{u},\mathcal{m},{\rm div}\bm{q})(t)\|\leq a^{\prime}_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},\qquad t\geq 0.

Proof.

From Duhamel’s principle, it holds that

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{n},\bm{u},\mathcal{m})(t,\bm{x})-e^{t\bm{B}}(\mathcal{n}_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})(\bm{x})\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-\tau)\bm{B}}({\rm R_{1}},{\rm R_{2}},{\rm R_{3}})(\tau)\|{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|({\rm R_{1}},{\rm R_{2}},{\rm R_{3}})(\tau)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}\ d\tau

Now with Lemma (
4.1
), the nonlinear source terms can be also estimated as

\displaystyle\|({\rm R_{1}},{\rm R_{2}},{\rm R_{3}})(t)\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|(\mathcal{n},\bm{u},\mathcal{m})(t)\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|(\mathcal{n}_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{1}\cap H^{2}},

\displaystyle\|({\rm R_{1}},{\rm R_{2}},{\rm R_{3}})(t)\|\lesssim\|(\mathcal{n},\bm{u},\mathcal{m})(t)\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|(\mathcal{n}_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{1}\cap H^{2}}.

So we have

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{n},\bm{u},\mathcal{m})(t,\bm{x})-e^{t\bm{B}}(\mathcal{n}_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})(\bm{x})\|\leq C\epsilon_{3}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\ d\tau

thus, from Proposition
3.7
, we know

\displaystyle a_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}-C\epsilon_{3}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\|(\mathcal{n},\bm{u},\mathcal{m})(t)\|\leq a_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}+C\epsilon_{3}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

(4.13)

As for
{\rm div}\bm{q}
, we can see it from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\|{\rm div}\bm{q}(t)\|^{2}-4\left\|\mathcal{m}(t)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\rm div}\bm{q}(t)\|^{2}-4\left\|\frac{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}+1}\widehat{\mathcal{m}}(t)\right\|^{2}\lesssim\left\|\frac{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}{|\bm{\xi}|^{2}+1}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{m}^{4}}+4\widehat{\mathcal{m}^{3}}+6\widehat{\mathcal{m}^{2}}\right)(t)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(\mathcal{m}^{4}+4\mathcal{m}^{3}+6\mathcal{m}^{2}\right)(t)\right\|^{2}\lesssim(1+t)^{-3}\|(\mathcal{n}_{0},\bm{u}_{0},\mathcal{m}_{0})\|_{L^{1}\cap H^{2}},

which combined with (
4.13
) provides the estimate for
{\rm div}\bm{q}
. Let
a^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{a_{1}-C\epsilon_{3}^{2}}{2}
and
a^{\prime}_{2}=2\left(a_{2}+C\epsilon_{3}^{2}\right)
.
Now the proof is completed.
∎

Finally, it is easy to see that Theorem
1.1
follows from Proposition
2.1
and
2.2
; Theorem
1.2
comes from Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
indicates Theorem
1.3
.

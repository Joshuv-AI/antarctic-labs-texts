1.
Introduction

We consider the initial value problem for the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation with double nonlinearities (DINLS)

(1.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}u,&t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{cases}

where
u(t,x)
is a complex-valued function defined on spacetime
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}_{x}
with
N=3,4,5
. Here,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are nonzero real constants,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-b_{1}}{N-2}
,
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
, and
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
.

Due to its Hamiltonian structure, the solutions to the problem (
1.1
) have the following conserved quantity in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(1.2)

E(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{p_{1}+2}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u(t,x)|^{p_{1}+2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{p_{2}+2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u(t,x)|^{p_{2}+2}\right]\,dx,

which we refer to as the
Energy
. The solutions of (
1.1
) also conserve the
Mass
, defined by

(1.3)

M(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx.

The DINLS extends classical equations such as the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) and the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation (INLS), with applications in plasma physics, nonlinear optics, and Bose-Einstein condensation. For instance, when
b_{1}=b_{2}=\lambda_{2}=0
, we have the classical nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation,

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\lambda|u|^{p}u=0\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The case
p=2
is particularly important in physics as it describes the propagation of laser beams in certain types of plasma media (Kerr medium). This equation has been extensively studied (see
[
24
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
13
]
).

When
\lambda_{2}\equiv 0
and
0<b_{1}=b<2
, the equation becomes the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger (INLS) equation,

(1.4)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+\lambda|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}u=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}.

This model describes the propagation of laser beams in nonlinear optics (see
[
23
]
and
[
20
]
). Initial research on local and global well-posedness of this equation was conducted by Geneoud and Stuart
[
16
]
, using the abstract theory by Cazenave
[
7
]
. They showed that the initial value problem (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
0<p<\frac{2-b}{N-2}
(
0<p<\infty
if
N=1,2
) and
0<b<2
. Further studies explored well-posedness, stability of standing waves, scattering of global solutions, blow-up of the critical norm, and concentration of the critical norm (see
[
15
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
4
]
).

When
b_{1}=b_{2}=0
, the equation reduces to the homogeneous double nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) equation:

(1.5)

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda_{1}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\lambda_{2}|u|^{p_{2}}u,

which has received substantial attention recently due to its applications in nonlinear optics and Bose-Einstein condensation (see
[
14
]
). T. Tao, M. Visan, and X. Zhang in
[
27
]
studied global well-posedness in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
N\geq 3
in the mass-subcritical case,
0<p_{1}<p_{2}<\frac{4}{N}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
0<p_{1}<p_{2}\leq\frac{4}{N-2}
under
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda_{2}>0
(defocusing case). They also obtained scattering results for
\frac{4}{N}\leq p_{1}<p_{2}\leq\frac{4}{N-2}
when
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
or
\lambda_{1}<0
(focusing case) and
\lambda_{2}>0
, by imposing a small mass condition. X. Cheng, C. Miao, and L. Zhao in
[
8
]
established the profile decomposition in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and used the concentration-compactness method to show global well-posedness and scattering versus blow-up in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
below the threshold for radial data when
N=3,4
. Y. Luo in
[
21
]
addressed the mass-energy double critical case. Other researchers also explored models with two combined nonlinearities (see
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
).

Recently, for the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation with double nonlinearities (DINLS):

(1.6)

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}u,

M. Zhang and J. Zhang
[
28
]
, in the case
0=b_{1}<b_{2}<\min\{2,N\}
,
p_{1}=\frac{4}{N}
and
0<p_{2}<\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N}
, analyzed blow-up solutions, obtaining sharp thresholds for global existence and blow-up for the Cauchy problem. They proved mass concentration properties, the limiting behavior of blow-up solutions, and proved the nonexistence of minimal mass blow-up solutions. T. Gou, M. Majdoub, and T. Saanouni
[
17
]
studied existence/nonexistence, symmetry, decay, uniqueness, non-degeneracy, and instability of ground states with
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,N\}
,
0<p_{1}<p_{2}<\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
in low dimension. They proved scattering versus blow-up below the ground state energy threshold using Tao’s scattering criterion and Dodson-Murphy’s Virial/Morawetz inequalities, and provided an upper bound on the blow-up rate.

In this paper, we investigate the case where
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
for
N\geq 3
, which is the case in which one of the nonlinearities is energy-critical. We present several cases of global well-posedness for (
1.1
). Additionally, we prove the finite-time blow-up of solutions to (
1.1
) for certain classes of parameters
\lambda_{i}
,
b_{i}
(
i=1,2
), and the power
p_{1}
.

To achieve this, we utilize the
S^{1}(L^{2},I)
space (which will be defined rigorously in the next section), characterized as the closure of the test functions under the norm given by the supremum of all Schrödinger-admissible norms. Our main results are described below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
2<N<6
,
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{6-N}{2}\}
,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
, and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
. Then, for all
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists a unique global solution
u\in C\left([0,\infty);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)\cap S^{1}(L^{2},I)
for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the following cases:

(1)

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
;

(2)

\lambda_{1}>0
,
\lambda_{2}<0
,
p_{1}<p_{2}
and
\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}b_{2}\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
;

(3)

\lambda_{1}>0
,
\lambda_{2}<0
,
b_{2}<b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
.

Moreover, for all compact intervals
I
,

\|u\|_{S^{1}(L^{2},I)}\leq C(E,M).

The theorem above follows from arguments involving local well-posedness results and kinetic energy control. To obtain local well-posedness, we view the first nonlinearity
|u|^{p_{1}}u
as a perturbation to the energy-critical INLS. Consequently, we also establish a stability theory for the energy-critical INLS given by (
1.4
) with
p=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In this context, the restriction on the dimension is essential for the continuity argument, which fails in higher dimensions. This strategy was originally applied by
[
27
]
for the DNLS setting.

The boundedness of the
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm of the solution to (
1.1
) is a consequence of the conserved energy (
1.2
) and specific embeddings from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
L^{p_{1}+2}_{b_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, spaces which will be defined shortly. Here, the signs of the parameters
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, as well as the bounds on
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, are crucial for the necessary control over the kinetic energy.

Now, consider the virial space
\Sigma
defined as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\Sigma=\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,;\,|x|f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}.

Let
u_{0}\in\Sigma
and
u(t)
be the corresponding solution to (
1.4
). Then, by straightforward computations (see Lemma
6.1
), we have that

(1.8)

\displaystyle y(t):=\frac{d}{dt}\|xu(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}(t)\nabla u(t)\cdot x\,dx.

Here, we also present our results on the finite-time blow-up for (
1.1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in\Sigma
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
2<N<6
,
\lambda_{2}<0
,
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{6-N}{2}\}
,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
, and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
. Assume that
u_{0}\in\Sigma
,
y_{0}=y(0)
is positive, and one of the following conditions holds:

i)

\lambda_{1}>0
,
p_{1}<p_{2}
,
b_{1}<b_{2}
, and
E(u)<0
;

ii)

\lambda_{1}>0
,
b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
, and
E(u)<0
;

iii)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N}<p_{1}<p_{2}
,
b_{1}<b_{2}
, and
E(u)<0
;

iv)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N}<p_{1}
,
b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
, and
E(u)<0
;

v)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
p_{1}<p_{2}
,
\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}
, and
E(u_{0})+CM(u_{0})<0
for some suitably large constant
C
(depending on
N
,
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
\lambda_{1}
, and
\lambda_{2}
).

Then the maximum time
T^{*}>0
of existence of the solution
u(t)
to the problem (
1.1
), with initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
, is finite in each of the following three cases. In particular,
0<T^{*}\leq C\frac{\|xu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}}{y_{0}}
and

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\infty.

Note that in both results above, the restriction on
p_{1}
is similar to results found for DNLS in
[
27
]
(when
b_{1}=b_{2}=0
). However, the presence of two inhomogeneous nonlinearities results in a greater restriction on
b_{2}
and consequently on
b_{1}
, which differs from the results of
[
12
]
and
[
4
]
, for instance. In these cases, the parameter
b
can be close to zero, while here we have a stronger restriction:
\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}b_{2}\leq b_{1}<b_{2}
. In this work, we do not cover the high dimension case for DINLS, which remains an open problem.

This paper is organized as follows. Firstly, in Section 2, we discuss Notations and Preliminaries, where we fix the notation used throughout the paper and establish the nonlinear estimates. Next, in Section 3, we develop the stability theory for the energy-critical INLS, i.e., the case
p_{1}=0
for (
1.1
). In Section 4, we prove the local well-posedness for IVP (
1.1
). In Section 5, we impose conditions on the parameters
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
b_{1}
, and
b_{2}
to prove Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section 6, we establish Theorem
1.2
, which ensures the finite-time blow-up of the solutions under certain restrictions on the parameters of (
1.1
).

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

We frequently employ the notation
X\lesssim Y
to denote the existence of a constant
C
such that
X\leq CY
, where
C
may depend on
n
,
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
\lambda_{1}
, and
\lambda_{2}
. Similarly,
X\ll Y
signifies
X\leq cY
for some small constant
c
. The derivative operator
\nabla
specifically refers to the space variable. Occasionally, we use subscripts to denote spatial derivatives and apply the summation convention over repeated indices.

We use
L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to denote the Banach space of functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with the norm

\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\rho}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\;|f(x)|^{\rho}\;dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}

which is finite (with the usual modification when
\rho=\infty
). When
\rho=2
, we simplify
\|f\|_{\rho}
to
\|f\|
. Similarly, for any space-time slab
I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we use
L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
to denote the Banach space of functions
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with the norm

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}L_{x}^{\rho}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\|u\|_{\gamma,\rho}:=\left(\int_{I}\;\|u\|_{\rho}^{\gamma}\;dt\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}

which is finite (with the usual modification when
r=\infty
). We omit mention of the slab
I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
when it is clear from context. Additionally, we define the weighted
L^{p}
norm for any
b>0
as

\|u\|_{L^{p}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The Fourier transform in the Schwarz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx.

We make use of the fractional differentiation operators
|\nabla|^{s}
defined by

\widehat{|\nabla|^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi).

These define the homogeneous Sobolev norms

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{x}}:=\||\nabla|^{s}f\|.

Let
e^{it\Delta}
denote the free Schrödinger propagator given by

\widehat{e^{it\Delta}f}(\xi)=e^{-4\pi^{2}it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi).

Recall that the solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to the forced Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=F(x,t)

satisfies Duhamel’s formula,

(2.1)

u(t)=e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}u(t_{0})-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(\cdot,\tau)\,d\tau,

for any
t_{0}\in I
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. The pair
(\gamma,\rho)
is
Schrödinger-admissible
(or
L^{2}
-admissible) if it satisfies the condition

(2.2)

\frac{2}{\gamma}+\frac{N}{\rho}=\frac{N}{2},

where

(2.3)

2\leq\gamma\leq\frac{2N}{N-2}.

The set of all pairs
(\gamma,\rho)
that are Schrödinger-admissible is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For a time-interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define the
S(L^{2},I)
Strichartz norm by

\|u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}:=\sup_{(\gamma,\rho)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}}\|u\|_{\gamma,\rho}.

We define the associated spaces
S(L^{2},I)
as the closure of the test functions under these norms. We denote the dual space of
S(L^{2},I)
by
S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)
.

For all
L^{2}
-admissible exponents
(\gamma,\rho)
, it holds

(2.4)

\|u\|_{\gamma,\rho}\lesssim\|u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

and consequently

(2.5)

\|u\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\|u\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}.

For a space
X
, we denote
X^{1}
as the space

X^{1}:=\left\{u:u,\nabla u\in X\right\},

provided with the norm

\|u\|_{X^{1}}:=\|u\|_{X}+\|\nabla u\|_{X}.

Throughout the paper, with
2<N<6
,
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
, we define the following Strichartz spaces on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
as the closure of the test functions under the respective norms:

\|u\|_{W^{0}(I)}:=\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-2}}L_{x}^{\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+4-2b_{2}N}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\|u\|_{V^{0}(I)}:=\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-b_{2}}}L_{x}^{\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+2b_{2}-2b_{2}N}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\|u\|_{Z(I)}:=\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-2}}L_{x}^{\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{(N-2b_{2})(N-2)}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

In turn, the spaces
W^{\pm}=W^{\pm}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
are defined by replacing the norm used in
W
with the following:

\|u\|_{W^{\pm}}:=\|u\|_{\left[\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-2}\right]^{\mp},\left[\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+4-2b_{2}N}\right]^{\pm}},

where

(2.6)

\frac{1}{\gamma^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\pm\frac{\eta}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\rho^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\rho}\pm\frac{\eta}{N}

for
\eta\ll 1
. Finally, we denote

W(I):=W^{0}\cap W^{+}\cap W^{-}.

Similarly, we define the space
V(I)
. It is easy to check that the exponents
(\gamma,\rho)
which define the space norms
W(I)
and
V(I)
are Schrödinger-admissible.

Definition 2.3
.

For
N>2
,
0<b<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
, and
0<p<\frac{4-2b}{N-2}
, we define the pair

(2.7)

(\gamma_{b},\rho_{b}):=\left(\frac{4(p+2)}{p(N-2)+2b}\,,\,\frac{N(p+2)}{N+p-b}\right).

Given an arbitrary slab
I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define the space

X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I):=L_{t}^{\gamma_{b_{1}}}L_{x}^{\rho_{b_{1}}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{t}^{\gamma_{b_{1}}^{+}}L_{x}^{\rho_{b_{1}}^{-}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{t}^{\gamma_{b_{1}}^{-}}L_{x}^{\rho_{b_{1}}^{+}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W(I)\cap V(I).

Remark 2.4
.

Note that the pair
(\gamma_{b_{1}},\rho_{b_{1}})
defined in (
2.7
) satisfies condition (
2.2
). Besides, since
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
and
0<b_{1}<2
, we have

\frac{N-2}{2N}<\frac{1}{\rho_{b_{1}}}<\frac{1}{2}.

Thus,
(\gamma_{b_{1}},\rho_{b_{1}})
is Schrödinger-admissible.

Lemma 2.5

Lemma 2.6

(2.9)

\||x|^{-(b_{i}+1)}|u|^{p_{i}}v\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\lesssim|I|^{1-\frac{p_{i}+2}{\gamma_{b_{i}}}}\|\nabla u\|^{p_{i}}_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}\|\nabla v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)},\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ for }i=1,2,

(2.10)

\||x|^{-b_{i}}|u|^{p_{i}}v\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\lesssim|I|^{1-\frac{p_{i}+2}{\gamma_{b_{i}}}}\|\nabla u\|^{p_{i}}_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}\|v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)},\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ for }i=1,2.

Proof.

Consider
(\gamma,\rho)\in\mathcal{A}_{0}

\gamma^{\prime}
and
\rho^{\prime}

\gamma

(2.11)

\displaystyle R=\left(\frac{\|\nabla v\|_{\rho_{-}}}{\|\nabla v\|_{\rho_{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\eta}},

where
\frac{1}{\rho_{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\rho}\pm\frac{\eta}{N}
. Since
N>2
and
b_{i}<\frac{N}{2}
, it follows that
b_{i}<N
. Consequently, if

denotes the ball of radius
R>0
centered at the origin, we have

\displaystyle\|x|^{-b_{i}}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{b_{i}+\eta}}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle=R^{\eta},

\displaystyle\|x|^{-b_{i}}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{b_{i}-\eta}}(B_{R}^{c})}

\displaystyle=R^{-\eta}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding, we obtain for
i=1,2
:

\begin{split}\||x|^{-b_{i}}|u|^{p_{i}}\nabla v\|_{L_{x}^{\rho^{\prime}}}&\lesssim\||x|^{-b_{i}}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{N}{b_{i}+\eta}}(A)}\||u|^{p_{i}}\nabla v\|_{L_{x}^{\beta^{-}_{1}}}\\
&\quad+\||x|^{-b_{i}}\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{N}{b_{i}-\eta}}(A^{c})}\||u|^{p_{i}}\nabla v\|_{L_{x}^{\beta^{+}_{1}}}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla u\|^{p_{i}}_{L_{x}^{\beta}}(R^{\eta}\|\nabla v\|_{\rho^{+}}+R^{-\eta}\|\nabla v\|_{\rho^{-}})\\
&=\|\nabla u\|^{p_{i}}_{L_{x}^{\beta}}(\|\nabla v\|_{\rho^{+}}\|\nabla v\|_{\rho^{-}})^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

where
\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{b_{i}}{N}+\frac{1}{\beta}
,
\frac{1}{\beta_{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\beta}\pm\frac{\eta}{N}
, and

(2.12)

\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{b_{i}}{N}+\frac{p_{i}}{\beta}-\frac{p_{i}}{N}+\frac{1}{\rho}.

Again, using Hölder’s inequality in the inequality above, we obtain

(2.13)

\||x|^{-b_{i}}|u|^{p_{i}}\nabla v\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}\lesssim|I|^{1-\frac{p_{i}+2}{\gamma_{b_{i}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{p_{i}}\left[\|\nabla v\|_{\gamma^{-},\rho^{+}}\|\nabla v\|_{\gamma^{+},\rho^{-}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\frac{1}{\gamma^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\pm\frac{\eta}{2}
and

(2.14)

\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}=\frac{p_{i}}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\gamma}+1-\frac{p_{i}+2}{\gamma_{b_{i}}}.

Firstly, consider
i=1
in (
2.13
). In this case, we take
(\alpha,\beta)=(\gamma,\rho)
in (
2.12
) and (
2.14
), and thus,
(\alpha,\beta)=(\gamma_{b_{1}},\rho_{b_{1}})
, where
(\gamma_{b_{1}},\rho_{b_{1}})
is defined as in (
2.7
). Therefore,

\begin{split}\||x|^{-b_{i}}|u|^{p_{1}}\nabla v\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}&\lesssim|I|^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{\gamma_{b_{1}},\rho_{b_{1}}}^{p_{1}}\left[\|\nabla v\|_{\gamma_{b_{1}}^{+},\rho_{b_{1}}^{-}}\|\nabla v\|_{\gamma_{b_{1}}^{-},\rho_{b_{1}}^{+}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

The inequality above shows (
2.8
) for
i=1
.

Now, consider
i=2
in (
2.13
). Here, we put
(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-2},\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+4-2b_{2}N}\right)
. Hence, from (
2.14
) we obtain

\frac{1}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p_{2}}{2}\frac{N-2}{2(N+2-2b_{2})}=\frac{N-b_{2}}{2(N+2-2b_{2})}.

Since
(\gamma,\rho)
is Schrödinger-admissible, we have
\rho=\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+2b_{2}-2b_{2}N}
. It is easy to see that for these choices, the equation (
2.12
) is satisfied. So,

\||x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}\nabla v\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\||x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}\nabla v\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{W^{0}(I)}^{p_{2}}\left[\|\nabla v\|_{V^{+}(I)}\|\nabla v\|_{V^{-}(I)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The last inequality concludes the proof of (
2.8
). The proof of (
2.9
) and (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) are analogous.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
I
be a compact time interval,
N>2
,
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{\frac{N}{2},2\}
,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
and
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
be nonzero real numbers. Then,

\|\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}u\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\;|I|^{1-\frac{p_{i}+2}{\gamma_{b_{i}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}^{p_{i}}\|u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)},

\|\nabla\left(\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}u\right)\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\;|I|^{1-\frac{p_{i}+2}{\gamma_{b_{i}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}^{p_{i}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}

and

\begin{split}\|\left(\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\right.&\left.\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}u\right)-\left(\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|v|^{p_{1}}v+\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|v|^{p_{2}}v\right)\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I)}\\
\lesssim&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\;|I|^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}^{p_{i}}+\|\nabla v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}^{p_{i}}\right)\|u-v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I)}\end{split}

Proof.

It is known that if
f(u)=|u|^{p}u
, then by the chain rule, we have

\nabla(|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}u)=|x|^{-(b+1)}f(u)+|x|^{-b}f_{z}(u)\nabla u+|x|^{-b}f_{\overline{z}}\overline{\nabla u}

where
f_{z}
and
f_{\overline{z}}
are the usual complex derivatives:

f_{z}(u)=\frac{p+2}{2}|u|^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{\overline{z}}(u)=\frac{p}{2}|u|^{p-2}u^{2}.

Thus,

|\nabla\left(|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}u\right)|\lesssim|x|^{-(b+1)}|u|^{p+1}+|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}|\nabla u|.

In addition, the mean value theorem ensures that

f(u)-f(v)=\int_{0}^{1}\left[f_{z}(v+\theta(u-v))(u-v)+f_{\overline{z}}(v+\theta(u-v))\overline{(u-v)}\right]\,d\theta

for any
u,v\in\mathbb{C}
, which in turn implies that

|f(u)-f(v)|\lesssim\left(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}\right)|u-v|=|u|^{p}|u-v|+|v|^{p}|u-v|.

Thus,

\left||x|^{-b}|u|^{p}u-|x|^{-b}|v|^{p}v\right|\lesssim|x|^{-b}\left(|u|^{p}+|v|^{p}\right)|u-v|.

In this way, the proof of the corollary follows from the above note and Lemma
2.6
.
∎

.

2.6
and Corollary
2.7

.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
N>2
and

. Then the following inequality hold

(2.15)

\||x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}-1}w\nabla v\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}^{p_{2}-1}\|\nabla w\|_{W(I)}\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}

and

(2.16)

\||x|^{-(b_{2}+1)}|u|^{p_{2}-1}wv\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}^{p_{2}-1}\|\nabla w\|_{W(I)}\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}.

In particular,

(2.17)

2.15
). The other inequalities are proven in similar way. Just like in Lemma
2.6
we obtain

with
(\gamma,\rho)
and

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho^{\prime}}=\frac{b_{i}}{N}+\frac{p_{2}}{\beta}-\frac{p_{2}}{N}+\frac{1}{\beta}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}=\frac{p_{2}+1}{\alpha}.

and

in (
2.18
) and (
2.19
).
∎

.

, the result follows.

3.
Energy-critical INLS

In this section we study the initial value problem (IVP)

(3.1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N-2}}u\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{cases}

where
u(x,t)
is a complex-valued function and
N>2
. Here we will omit the index
b
in the spaces
W(I)
,
Z(I)
and its derivatives.

3.1.
Well-posedness

In this section, we establish key results regarding the well-posedness of the energy-critical Inhomogeneous Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation (INLS) in the energy space
H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Our inspiration comes from the interesting work of Tao and Visan
[
26
]
. We have adapted their ideas and studied the well-posedness of the energy-critical problem for the INLS. Specifically, we establish both local and global well-posedness, followed by results related to the existence of solutions to the approximation problem. We start with the local well-posedness.

Theorem 3.1

(Local Well-Posedness)
.

Let
I
be a compact time interval containing
t_{0}
. Suppose
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{x}
satisfies

(3.2)

\|e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}\nabla u_{0}\|_{W(I)}\leq\eta,

for some
0<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
, where
\eta_{0}>0
is a small constant. There exists a unique solution
w
to (
3.1
) satisfying

(3.3)

\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\eta,

(3.4)

\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\eta^{\frac{N+2-2b}{N-2}},

and

(3.5)

\|u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, the maps associating the data
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{x}
with the solution
w
satisfying (
3.3
)-(
3.5
) are Lipschitz.

Proof.

Existence.
We proceed iteratively. For all
t
, we define the following iterates:

\displaystyle u^{(0)}(t)

\displaystyle:=0,

\displaystyle u^{(1)}(t)

\displaystyle:=e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}u_{0},

and for
m\geq 1
,

(3.6)

u^{(m+1)}(t):=e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}u_{0}-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(u^{(m)}(\tau))\;d\tau,

where
F(u)=|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}u
.

Let us first remark that the smallness of the free evolution implies that all the iterates are small. Indeed, by Strichartz estimates and the triangle inequality, followed by Corollary
2.9
and (
3.2
), we have

(3.7)

\|\nabla u^{(m+1)}\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\|\nabla e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}u_{0}\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla F(u^{(m)})\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\lesssim\eta+\|\nabla u^{(m)}\|_{W(I)}^{p+1}.

Using (
3.2
) as the base case for an induction hypothesis and choosing
\eta_{0}
sufficiently small, by continuous argument we deduce

(3.8)

\|\nabla u^{(m+1)}\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\eta\ ,\ \|\nabla u^{(m)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\eta^{p+1}

for all
m\geq 1
.

Next, we consider differences of the form
u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m)}
. By the recurrence relation (
3.6
), in order to estimate
u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m)}
, we need to control
F(u^{(m)})-F(u^{(m-1)})
. By Lemma
2.5
, Remark
2.8
(with
u=u^{(m)}
and
v=u^{(m-1)}
) and (
3.8
), we estimate

\begin{split}\|u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim&\|F(u^{(m)})-F(u^{(m-1)})\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&\|u^{(m)}-u^{(m-1)}\|_{V(I)}\left(\|\nabla u^{(m)}\|_{W(I)}^{p}+\|\nabla u^{(m-1)}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\right)\\
\lesssim&\,\eta^{p}\,\|u^{(m)}-u^{(m-1)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\end{split}

for all
m\geq 1
, where
(\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
is the dual exponent of
\left(\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-b_{2}},\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+2b_{2}-2b_{2}N}\right)
. Also, since
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by hypothesis, we have

\|u^{(1)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

due to Lemma
2.5
.

Choosing
\eta
sufficiently small, we conclude that the sequence
\{u^{(m)}\}_{m}
is Cauchy in
S(L^{2},I)
. Hence, there exists
u\in S(L^{2},I)
such that
u^{(m)}
converges to
u
in
S(L^{2},I)
(and thus also in
V(I)
) as
m\rightarrow 1
. Furthermore, for
m
,
m_{0}
large enough (with
m_{0}
fixed), we have

\begin{split}\|u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq&\|u-u^{(m+1)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}+\|u^{(m+1)}-u^{(m_{0})}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}+\|u^{(m_{0})}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\\
\lesssim&\sum_{j=0}^{m_{0}}\|u^{(j+1)}-u^{(j)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim C(\eta)\|u^{(1)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}},\end{split}

which ensures (
3.5
).

The above argument also shows that
F(u^{(m)})
converges to
F(u)
in
L^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{t}L^{\rho^{\prime}}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
(\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
is the dual exponent of
\left(\frac{2(N+2-2b_{2})}{N-b_{2}},\frac{2N(N+2-2b_{2})}{N^{2}+2b_{2}-2b_{2}N}\right)
. In turn, from Lemma (
2.5
),

\begin{split}\left\|u-e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}u_{0}+i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}F(u(\cdot,\tau))\;d\tau\right\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq&\|u-u^{(m+1)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\\
&+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}\left[F(u)-F(u^{(m)})\right](\cdot,\tau)\;d\tau\right\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\\
\lesssim&\|u-u^{(m+1)}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}+\|F(u)-F(u^{(m+1)})\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}.\end{split}

This implies that
u
satisfies the equation (
2.1
).

Additionally, as
u^{(m)}
converges strongly to
u
in
S(L^{2},I)
and given that
\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}
and
\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}
are bounded according to (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), we conclude that
\nabla u^{(m)}
converges weakly to
\nabla u
in
{W}(I)
and
{S}(L^{2},I)
, obtaining the bounds (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). Furthermore, since
u
belongs to
S^{1}(L^{2},I)
, it also lies in
C([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and serves as a solution to (
2.1
).

Uniqueness.
Let
I
be a time interval containing
t_{0}
, and let
u_{1},u_{2}
be two strong solutions to (
3.1
), meaning that
\nabla u_{1}
,
\nabla u_{2}\in S(L^{2},I)
and satisfying the Duhamell formulation (
2.1
). By a standard argument, we may shrink
I
so that we can construct the solution given by the preceding iteration argument. Without loss of generality, we may take
u_{2}
to be this solution. Thus,
u_{2}
satisfies (
3.3
)-(
3.5
).

Write
v:=u_{1}-u_{2}\in S(L^{2},I)
. It is sufficient to show that
v
vanishes almost everywhere on
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau)\subset I
for some
\tau>0
. In fact, we must prove that the set
\Omega
of all
t
such that
v(t)=0
is an open set. But since such a set is nonempty, for any
t_{1}\in\Omega
, proving that
[t_{1},t_{1}+\tau)\subset\Omega
follows a similar argument.

In the rest of the proof, we will work entirely on the slab
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau)\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since
v(t_{0})=0
, we can take
\tau
as small as necessary such that

\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\|\nabla v\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq\eta,

where
\eta
is a small absolute constant to be chosen later. Similarly, from the hypothesis
\nabla u_{2}\in S(L^{2},I)
and satisfying (
3.3
), we have

\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\eta.

Furthermore, since
u_{j}
,
j=1,2
, satisfy (
2.1
), we have

v(t)=-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}\left[F(u_{1}(s))-F(u_{2}(s))\right]\,ds.

Hence,

\begin{split}\|v\|_{V(I)}\lesssim&\,\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\\
\lesssim&\left(\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{W(I)}^{p}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\right)\|v\|_{V(I)}\\
\lesssim&\,\left(\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}^{p}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\right)\|v\|_{V(I)}\\
\lesssim&\,\eta^{p}\|v\|_{V(I)}.\end{split}

As
v
was already finite in
V
, we conclude (by taking
\eta
small enough) that
v
vanishes almost everywhere in
[t_{0},t_{0}+\tau)
.

Continuous Dependence in Rough Norms.
We now turn to the Lipschitz bound. Let
u
and
\tilde{u}
be solutions of (
3.1
) with initial data
u_{0}
and
\tilde{u}_{0}
, respectively. From Strichartz estimates, Remark
2.8
, and (
2.4
), we obtain

\begin{split}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim&\,\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|+\|F(u)-F(\tilde{u})\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\\
\lesssim&\,\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|+\left(\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}^{p}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\right)\|v\|_{V(I)}\\
\lesssim&\,\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|+\left(\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}^{p}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\right)\|v\|_{S(L^{2},I)}.\end{split}

Since both
u
and
\tilde{u}
satisfy (
3.3
), we conclude that

\|v\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\,\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|+\eta^{p}\|v\|_{S(L^{2},I)}.

By choosing
\eta
small enough, we obtain
\|v\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim\,\|u_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|
as desired.

∎

An immediate consequence of the above theorem and the Strichartz estimates is the global well-posedness result below.

Theorem 3.2

(Global Well-Posedness for Small
H^{1}_{x}
Data)
.

Let
w_{0}\in H^{1}_{x}
be such that

(3.9)

\|w_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}\leq\eta_{0}

for some small absolute constant
\eta_{0}
depending only on the dimension
N
. Then, there exists a unique global solution
w
such that
\nabla u\in S(L^{2},\mathbb{R})
for (
3.1
), satisfying the following estimates:

(3.10)

\|\nabla w\|_{W(\mathbb{R})},\|\nabla w\|_{S(L^{2},\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|w_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}

(3.11)

\|w\|_{S(L^{2},\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Now, we are going to show that control of the solution in the specific norm
\|\cdot\|_{Z(I)}
yields control of the solution in all the
S^{1}(L^{2},I)
-norms.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I
be a compact time interval, and let
w
be the unique solution to (
3.1
) on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
obeying the bound

\|w\|_{Z(I)}\leq L.

Then, if
t_{0}\in I
and
w(t_{0})\in{H}^{1}
, we have

\|w\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq C(L)\|w(t_{0})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

and

\|\nabla w\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\leq C(L)\|w(t_{0})\|_{\dot{{H}^{1}_{x}}}.

Proof.

Subdivide the interval
I
into
N_{0}=C(L)
subintervals
I_{j}=[I_{j},I_{j+1}]
such that

\|w\|_{Z(I_{j})}\leq\eta,

where
\eta
is a small positive constant to be chosen later. By Strichartz and Corollary

, on each

we obtain

\begin{split}\|\nabla w\|_{S(L^{2},I_{j})}\lesssim&\,\|w(t_{0})\|_{\dot{{H}^{1}_{x}}}+\|\nabla(|x|^{-b}|w|^{p}w)\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\\
\lesssim&\,\|w(t_{0})\|_{\dot{{H}^{1}_{x}}}+\|w\|_{Z(I_{j})}^{p}\|\nabla w\|_{W(I_{j})}\\
\lesssim&\,\|w(t_{0})\|_{\dot{{H}^{1}_{x}}}+\eta^{p}\,\|\nabla w\|_{S(L^{2},I_{j})}.\end{split}

Choosing

sufficiently small, we obtain

\|\nabla w\|_{S(L^{2},I_{j})}\lesssim\|w(t_{0})\|_{\dot{{H}^{1}}}.

Similarly, we obtain

\|w\|_{S(L^{2},I_{j})}\lesssim\|w(t_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\eta^{p}\|w\|_{S(L^{2},I_{j})}.

The conclusion follows by adding these estimates over all subintervals

.
∎

3.2.
Short-time pertubations

Next, we aim to establish a stability result for the

-critical INLS equation. This result pertains to the following property: Given an approximate solution

i\tilde{u}_{t}+\Delta\tilde{u}=|x|^{-b}|\tilde{u}|^{\frac{4-2b}{N-2}}\tilde{u}+e,

with
\tilde{u}(0,x)=\tilde{u}_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\tilde{u}_{0}-u_{0}
is small in

, there exists a genuine solution
u
to (
3.1
) that remains very close to
\tilde{u}
in
H^{1}_{x}
-critical norms.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I
be a compact time interval,
2<N<6
,
p=\frac{4-2b}{N-2}
, and

\tilde{u}
be an approximate solution to (

) on

for some function
e
. Suppose that we also have the energy bound

(3.12)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{1}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq E

for some constant

. Let
t_{0}\in I
, and let
u(t_{0})\in H^{1}_{x}
be close to
\tilde{u}(t_{0})
in the sense that

(3.13)

\|u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}\leq E^{\prime}

for some

. Moreover, assume the smallness conditions

(3.14)

\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

(3.15)

\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}(u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0}))]\|_{W(I)}\leq\varepsilon

(3.16)

\|\nabla e\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\leq\varepsilon

for some
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, where
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(E,E^{\prime})>0
is a small constant. Then there exists a solution
u\in S^{1}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
to (
3.1
) on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the specified initial data
u(t_{0})
at time

that satisfies

(3.17)

\|\nabla(u-\tilde{u})\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\varepsilon

(3.18)

(3.19)

\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim E+E^{\prime}

(3.20)

\left\|\nabla F(u,\tilde{u})\right\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\lesssim\varepsilon,

where
F(u,\tilde{u}):=|x|^{-b}\left[|u|^{\frac{4-2b}{N-2}}u-|\tilde{u}|^{\frac{4-2b}{N-2}}\tilde{u}\right]
.

Proof.

We can consider that there exist a solution
u
for (
3.1
) given by Theorem
4.1
. Assume that
t_{0}=\inf I
. Let
v:=u-\tilde{u}
. Then

satisfies the following IVP

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})-e\ \ \ t\in\mathbb{R},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
v(x,t_{0})=v_{0}(x)=u(x,t_{0})-\tilde{u}(x,t_{0})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).\end{cases}

Now, we will work on the slab
[t_{0},T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}=I_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
T\in I
. By Strichartz estimates,

\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}v(t_{0})]\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla[F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})]\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}+\|\nabla e\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}

for any

-admissible pair

. So, from (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) results

(3.22)

\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}\lesssim\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{0})\Delta}v(t_{0})]\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla[F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})]\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}+\varepsilon\lesssim\|\nabla[F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})]\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}+\varepsilon.

In turn, since
0<b<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2},\frac{6-N}{2}\}
and
2<N<6

p=\frac{4-2b}{N-2}>1
. Hence,

\big|\nabla\left[F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})\right]\big|\lesssim\left[|x|^{-(b+1)}\left(|v+\tilde{u}|^{p}+|\tilde{u}|^{p}\right)\right]|v|+|x|^{-b}|v+\tilde{u}|^{p}|\nabla v|+|x|^{-b}\left(|v+\tilde{u}|^{p-1}+|\tilde{u}|^{p-1}\right)|\nabla\tilde{u}||v|.

So, it follows from (
2.15
) that

\begin{split}S(t):=&\;\|\nabla[F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})]\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\leq\;\||x|^{-(b+1)}|v+\tilde{u}|^{p}v\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}+\||x|^{-(b+1)}|\tilde{u}|^{p}v\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}+\||x|^{-b}|v+\tilde{u}|^{p}\nabla v\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\\
&\;+\||x|^{-b}|v+\tilde{u}|^{p-1}|v|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}+\||x|^{-b}|\tilde{u}|^{p-1}|v|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\\
\lesssim&\;\|\nabla[v+\tilde{u}]\|_{W(I)}^{p}\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla(v+\tilde{u})\|_{W(I)}^{p-1}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}.\end{split}

\begin{split}S(t)\lesssim&\;\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}^{p+1}+\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}^{p}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}^{p}\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}\\
\lesssim\;&\left(S(t)+\varepsilon\right)^{p+1}+\varepsilon_{0}\left(S(t)+\varepsilon\right)^{p}+\varepsilon_{0}^{p}\left(S(t)+\varepsilon\right).\end{split}

Thus, a standard continuity argument shows that if we take
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(E,E^{\prime})
sufficiently small we obtain

(3.23)

\|\nabla[F(v+\tilde{u},\tilde{u})]\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\leq\varepsilon\ \ \mbox{for all }\ T\in I_{1},

which implies (
3.20

3.22

Also, by Lemma

\begin{split}\|\nabla(u-\tilde{u})\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim&\|u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}+\|\nabla[F(u,\tilde{u})]\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}+\|\nabla e\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\lesssim E^{\prime}+\varepsilon,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we use (

3.16

Now, an application of (

)-(

), (

) and (

\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2},I)}\lesssim&\;\|u(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}+\|\nabla[|x|^{-b}|u|^{p}u]\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\lesssim\|u(t_{0})-\tilde{u}(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}+\|\tilde{u}(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}+\|\nabla u\|_{W(I)}^{p+1}\\
\lesssim&\;E+E^{\prime}+\left(\|\nabla v\|_{W(I)}+\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{W(I)}\right)^{p+1}\lesssim E+E^{\prime}+(\varepsilon+\varepsilon_{0})^{p+1},\end{split}

which proves (

is sufficiently small depending on

and

.
∎

3.3.
Long-Time Perturbations

Theorem 3.5

4.
Local well-posedness for DINLS

In this section, we prove the local well-posedness for the IVP (
1.1
) with
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
.

Proposition 4.1

(Local Well-Posedness)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{x}
,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be nonzero real constants,
2<N<6
,
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{\frac{N}{2},2\}
,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
, and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
. Given
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
T=T(u_{0})>0
and a unique solution
u
of (
1.1
) in the time interval
[-T,T]
with

u\in C([-T,T]:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T]).

In addition, for all
T^{\prime}<T
, there exists a neighborhood
B
of
u_{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that the function
u_{0}\in B\mapsto u(t)\in C([-T,T]:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])
is Lipschitz.
Moreover, for every
T>0
, there exists
\eta=\eta(T)
such that if

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}\leq\eta,

then (
1.1
) admits a unique strong
H^{1}_{x}
-solution
u
defined on
[-T,T]
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we consider only the case
t>0
. Let
\Gamma
be the operator given by

\Gamma(u)(t)=e^{it\Delta}u(t_{0})-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}G(\cdot,\tau)\,d\tau,

where
G(u)=\lambda_{1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u+\lambda_{2}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}}u
.

Note that from Strichartz estimates and Corollary
2.7
, we have for
k=0,1
we obtain

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{k}\Gamma(u)(t)\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}\lesssim

\displaystyle\,\||\nabla|^{k}e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}+\||\nabla|^{k}G(u)\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},[-T,T])}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\||\nabla|^{k}e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}+C\|\nabla u\|_{W([-T,T])}^{p_{2}}\||\nabla|^{k}u\|_{V([-T,T])}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\,+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}^{p_{1}}\||\nabla|^{k}u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T_{0}]}\|\Gamma(u)(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sum_{k=0}^{1}\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta}|\nabla|^{k}G(u)\|_{S(L^{2},I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\,\|\nabla u\|_{W([-T,T])}^{p_{2}}\||\nabla|^{k}u\|_{V([-T,T])}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\,+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\,\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}^{p_{1}}\||\nabla|^{k}u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}.

Since the
X_{b_{1},b_{2}}
-norm is
L^{2}
-admissible, for any fixed
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{T}>0
(see
[
19
]
, Proposition 5.1) such that

\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-\tilde{T},\tilde{T}])}+\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-\tilde{T},\tilde{T}])}<\epsilon.

By considering the norm

\|v\|_{\Sigma}:=\sup_{[0,T_{0}]}\|v-e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|v\|_{X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])},

we define the space

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a}^{T}:=\left\{v\ :\ v\in C([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \|u\|_{\Sigma}\leq a\right\}

and the metric

d(u,v):=\|u-v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])}.

By a similar argument as in Step 1 of the proof of [
[
7
]
Theorem 4.4.1] (see also [
[
7
]
, Theorem 1.2.5]), we can prove that

is a complete metric space.

Using (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we have that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists
T>0
such that if
u\in\Sigma_{a}^{T}
, then

(4.3)

\|\Gamma(u)\|_{\Sigma}<C\epsilon+C\left(\epsilon^{p_{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}a^{p_{1}}\right)a.

Therefore, if

(4.4)

C\epsilon+C\left(\epsilon^{p_{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}a^{p_{1}}\right)a<a,

we have that
\Gamma(\Sigma_{a}^{T})\subseteq\Sigma_{a}^{T}
.

Additionally, for the same
T(\epsilon)
from (

\begin{split}\|\Gamma(u)-\Gamma(v)\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}}\lesssim&\,\|u-v\|_{S(L^{2},[-T,T])}\lesssim\|G(u)-G(v)\|_{\gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}\\
\lesssim&\,\displaystyle 2C\left(\|\nabla u\|_{W([-T,T])}^{p_{2}}+T^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}^{p_{1}}\right)\|u-v\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])}\\

Thus, for

we have that
\Gamma
is a contraction. Now, fixing
\epsilon
sufficiently small and choosing

satisfying (
4.5
), where

is given by
\epsilon

) is verified.

Therefore, by the Point Fix Theorem, we establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (

with respect to

which shows that the function

from a neighborhood

of
u_{0}
in

on

In turn, given

, if we choose

and take

replaced by

Proposition 4.2

(Blow-up alternative)
.

Let
(-T_{\min},T_{\max})
be the maximal time interval on which the unique strong

-solution

of (

for every compact time interval

, and the following properties hold:

i)

If
T_{\max}<\infty
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|\nabla u(t)\|=\infty\quad\text{or}\quad\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2},(0,T_{\max}))}=\infty.

ii)

If
T_{\min}<\infty
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\min}}\|\nabla u(t)\|=\infty\quad\text{or}\quad\|\nabla u\|_{S(L^{2},(T_{\min},0))}=\infty.

Proof.

We will prove item (i). The second item is analogous.

Since the solution
u
of (
1.1
) belongs to
C([0,T_{\max}]:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T_{\max}])
and
T_{\max}
satisfies (
4.5
), we have

\|u\|_{S^{1}([0,T_{\max}]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}}+C\|u\|_{X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T_{\max}])}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T_{\max}])}^{p_{2}}+T_{\max}^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T_{\max}])}^{p_{1}}\right)<\infty.

Suppose that there exists a sequence
\{t_{j}\}
and a constant
M
such that
t_{j}\rightarrow T_{\max}
and

\|\nabla u(t_{j})\|+\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}}\leq M

for all

. Note that if

, from Strichartz estimates
\|e^{i(t-t_{j})\Delta}\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}}\lesssim\|\nabla u(t_{j})\|
. So, there exists a
\tilde{T}
sufficently small such that
\|e^{i(t-t_{j})\Delta}\nabla u_{0}\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-\tilde{T},\tilde{T}])}

denote the existence time obtained by Theorem
4.1
for all initial data bounded above by

, and let

be such that

.

There exists a unique strong
H^{1}_{x}
-solution
v_{k}
of (

) on

starting from

. Gluing together

1.1
) defined on

, which is impossible since

∎

5.
Global well-posedness to INLS with two power-type nonlinearities

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
. Our main tools will be the uniform control of the kinetic energy for solutions to (DINLS) equation and a“good” local well-posedness theory for the IVP (
1.1
) under some condictions.

5.1.
Kinetic Energy Control

Now, let’s estimate the
\dot{H}^{1}_{x}
-norm of the solution
u
to (
1.1
). From the energy conservation (
1.2
) for solutions of (
1.1
), we have

(5.1)

\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}=E(u_{0})-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{p_{1}+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u(t,x)|^{p_{1}+2}\;dx-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{p_{2}+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b_{2}}|u(t,x)|^{p_{2}+2}\;dx.

Note that if
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are both positive, it follows immediately that we have uniform control of the kinetic energy of the solution:

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\leq 2E(u_{0}),\quad\text{for all }t\in(-T_{\min},T_{\max}).

However, if
\lambda_{1}<0
and
\lambda_{2}>0
, we need to control the potencial intercritical energy in terms of the energy-critical term . For this we use the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\eta>0
,
p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
,
p_{2}=\frac{4-b_{2}}{N-2}
,
b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If

i)

p_{1}<p_{2}
and
\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}b_{2}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}
or

ii)

b_{2}<b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
,

then
u\in L^{p_{1}+2}_{b_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

(5.2)

\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}+2}_{b_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{1}+2}\leq\,\eta\,\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b+2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{2}+2}+C\left(\eta\ ,\ \|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right).

Proof.

Let us remember the elementary inequality

(5.3)

AB\leq\left(\frac{A^{s}}{s}+\frac{B^{s^{\prime}}}{s^{\prime}}\right)

valid for any
A
,
B>0
, with
1<s<\infty
and
s^{\prime}
the dual exponent to
s
.
We split the
L^{p_{1}+2}_{b_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm in two pieces, we must be estimate

\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}+2}_{b_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{1}+2}=\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq 1}\;|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}+1}|u|\,dx+\displaystyle\int_{|x|>1}\,|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}|u|^{2}\,dx=:I+II.

(i)

p_{1}<p_{2}
and
\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}b_{2}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}
.

In the first integral, since
b_{1}\leq b_{2}
we can replace
-b_{1}
by
-b_{2}+t
for some
t>0
and to apply (
5.3
) for
A=\eta^{\frac{1}{s}}|u|^{p_{1}+1}
,
B=\eta^{-\frac{1}{s}}
and
s=\frac{2p_{2}+1}{2p_{1}+1}>1
, to obtain

\begin{split}I\leq&\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq 1}\;|x|^{-b_{2}}\left[\eta|u|^{p_{2}+1}+\eta^{-\frac{s^{\prime}}{s}}\right]|u|\,dx\leq\eta\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq 1}\;|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}+2}\;dx+C_{\eta}\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq 1}\;|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|\,dx\\
\leq&\;\eta\,\|u\|_{L^{p+2+2}_{b_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{2}+2}+C_{\eta}\,\||x|^{-b_{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\{|x|\leq 1\})}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\eta\,\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{2}+2}+C_{\eta}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we use the fact the
b_{2}<\frac{N}{2}
.

In turn, we rewrite the integral
II
by

II=\displaystyle\int_{|x|>1}\;|x|^{-b_{1}+\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}}\left[|x|^{-\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}}|u|^{p_{1}}\right]|u|^{2}\;dx\leq\displaystyle\int_{|x|>1}\;\left[|x|^{-\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}}|u|^{p_{1}}\right]|u|^{2}\;dx,

where the last inequality is derived from
\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}\leq b_{1}
. Now, taking
A=\eta^{\frac{1}{s}}|x|^{-\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}}|u|^{p_{1}}
,
B=\eta^{-\frac{1}{s}}
and
s=\frac{p_{2}}{p_{1}}>1
in (
5.3
) we obtain

II\leq\eta\,\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{2}+2}+C_{\eta}\,\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}

and the proposition is showed for this case.

(ii)

b_{2}<b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
.

In this case we have

\begin{split}I\leq&\left(\int_{|x|<1}|x|^{-b_{1}(\frac{N}{b_{1}+\varepsilon})}\,dx\right)^{\frac{b_{1}+\varepsilon}{N}}\left(\int_{|x|<1}|u|^{\frac{N(p_{1}+2)}{N-b_{1}-\varepsilon}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{N-b_{1}-\varepsilon}{N}}\leq C\left(\int_{|x|<1}|u|^{\frac{N(p_{1}+2)}{N-b_{1}-\varepsilon}}\right)^{\frac{N-b_{1}-\varepsilon}{N}},\end{split}

where
\varepsilon
chosen sufficiently small satisfying

(5.4)

\varepsilon<N\left[1-\frac{b_{1}}{N}-\frac{p_{1}+2}{p_{2}+2}\right].

By interpolation, since that
b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
and
p_{1}+2<(p_{2}+2)\left(1-\frac{b_{1}}{N}\right)
we obtain from last inequality that

\begin{split}I\leq C\|u\|^{\theta(p_{1}+2)}\left(\int_{|x|<1}|u|^{p_{2}+2}\right)^{\frac{(p_{1}+2)}{p_{2}+2}(1-\theta)}\leq C\|u\|^{\theta(p_{1}+2)}\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}}^{(p_{1}+2)(1-\theta)}.\end{split}

where

(5.5)

\theta=\frac{2}{p_{2}}\left[\frac{p_{2}+2}{p_{1}+2}\left(1-\frac{b_{1}}{N}\right)-1-\varepsilon\frac{p_{2}+2}{N(p_{1}+2)}\right]

Thus, using inequality (
5.3
) with
A=\eta^{\frac{1}{s}}\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}}^{(p_{1}+2)(1-\theta)}
,
B=\eta^{-\frac{1}{s}}C\|u\|^{\theta(p_{1}+2)}
and
s=\frac{p_{2}+2}{(p_{1}+2)(1-\theta)}>1
, we estimate

I\leq\eta\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}}^{(p_{2}+2)]}+C(\eta\,,\,\|u\|)

Now, we will estimate the integral II. For this, note that

(5.6)

\begin{split}\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b_{1})}{N-2\varepsilon}}\leq&\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{-b_{1}+2\varepsilon b_{2}\frac{(N-b_{1})(N-2)}{(N-2\varepsilon)(N-b_{2})}}\left||x|^{-b_{2}\frac{(N-2)}{2(N-b_{2})}}u\right|^{4\varepsilon\frac{(N-b_{1})}{(N-2\varepsilon)}}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b_{1})}{N-2\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon)}\,dx\\
\leq&\,C_{N}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4\varepsilon(N-b_{1})}{N-2\varepsilon}}\left[\int|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}+2}\,dx\right]^{(1-2\varepsilon)\frac{2(N-b_{1})}{(N-2\varepsilon)(p_{2}+2)}},\end{split}

where

\frac{1}{N}\left(b_{1}-(1-2\varepsilon)b_{2}\frac{(N-b_{1})(N-2)}{(N-2\varepsilon)(N-b_{2})}\right)>1-2\varepsilon\frac{N-b_{1}}{N-2\varepsilon}-(1-2\varepsilon)\frac{(N-b_{1})(N-2)}{(N-2\varepsilon)(N-b_{2})}>0,

what is always holds for
b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
,
b_{1}<N
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small satisfying

(5.7)

\varepsilon<\frac{N(2-b_{2})(b_{1}(N_{2})}{2[(N-b_{1})(2-b_{2})+2(N-b_{2})]}

In the similar way, we obtain

(5.8)

\begin{split}\int_{|x|>1}|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b_{1})}{N-2+2\varepsilon}}\leq&\,C_{N}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4\varepsilon(N-b_{1})}{N-2+2\varepsilon}}\left[\int|x|^{-b_{2}}|u|^{p_{2}+2}\,dx\right]^{(1-2\varepsilon)\frac{2(N-b_{1})}{(N-2+2\varepsilon)(p_{2}+2)}},\end{split}

with

\frac{1}{N}\left(b_{1}-(1-2\varepsilon)b_{2}\frac{(N-b_{1})(N-2)}{(N-2-2\varepsilon)(N-b_{2})}\right)>1-\frac{2\varepsilon(N-b_{1})}{N-2+2\varepsilon}-(1-2\varepsilon)\frac{(N-b_{1})(N-2)}{(N-2+2\varepsilon)(N-b_{2})}>0.

We can reduce the condition above to
b_{2}<b_{1}<N
,
b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
and

(5.9)

\begin{split}0<\varepsilon<\frac{(N-2)(b_{1}-b_{2})}{2[(N-b_{1})(2-b_{2})-(N-b_{2})]}.\end{split}

By taking
\varepsilon
satisfying (
5.7
) and (
5.9
), and so interpolation (
5.6
) and (
5.8
) we obtain

(5.10)

\|u\|_{L_{b_{1}}^{p_{\beta}}(\{|x|>1\})}\leq C\|u\|^{2\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L_{b_{2}}^{p_{2}+2}}^{1-2\varepsilon}

where

\frac{1}{p_{\beta}}=\beta\frac{N-2\varepsilon}{2(N-b_{1})}+(1-\beta)\frac{N-2+2\varepsilon}{2(N-b_{1})}.

for any
\beta\in[0,1]
. In particular, we have (
5.10
) holds for any
p_{\beta}
satisfying
p_{\beta}+2<(p_{2}+2)\left(1-\frac{b_{1}}{N}\right)
. More precisely, for any
b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}.
we have

II\leq C\|u\|^{(p_{1}+2)2\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L_{b_{2}}^{p_{2}+2}}^{(p_{1}+2)(1-2\varepsilon)}\leq\eta\|u\|_{L^{p_{2}+2}_{b_{2}}}^{p_{2}+2}+C(\eta\,,\,\|u\|).

∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Consider the initial value problem (IVP) (
1.1
) with
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
. Then, for all times
t
for which the solution is defined, we have

(5.11)

\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}\leq C(E(u_{0}),M(u_{0}))

uniformly in time, under each of the following conditions

i)

\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
;

ii)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
\lambda_{2}>0
,
p_{1}<p_{2}
, and
\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2}\}
;

iii)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
\lambda_{2}>0
, and
b_{2}<b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
.

Proof.

We need to prove cases
ii)
and
iii)
. From (
5.1
), we have

\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}=2E(u_{0})+\frac{2|\lambda_{1}|}{p_{1}+2}\|u(t)\|_{L_{b_{1}}^{p_{1}+2}}^{p_{1}+2}-\frac{2|\lambda_{2}|}{p_{2}+2}\|u(t)\|_{L_{b_{2}}^{p_{2}+2}}^{p_{2}+2}.

Applying Proposition
5.1
with
\eta=\frac{(p_{1}+2)|\lambda_{2}|}{(p_{2}+2)|\lambda_{1}|}
, we estimate

\begin{split}\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}&=2E(u_{0})+\frac{2|\lambda_{2}|}{p_{2}+2}\|u(t)\|_{L_{b_{2}}^{p_{2}+2}}^{p_{2}+2}\\
&\quad-\frac{2|\lambda_{2}|}{p_{2}+2}\|u(t)\|_{L_{b_{2}}^{p_{2}+2}}^{p_{2}+2}+C(M(u_{0}))\\
&\leq C(M(u_{0}),E(u_{0})),\end{split}

where the constant does not depend on time.
∎

5.2.
Good Local Well-Posedness

We know that the solution of the initial value problem (IVP) (
1.1
), as found in Proposition
4.1
, belongs to
C([-T,T]:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}([-T,T])
. Now, we aim to find a time
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}})
such that
u\in S^{1}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\|u\|_{S^{1}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(E,M),

where
E
and
M
denote the mass and energy of the system. In other words, the existence time
T
of the solution for IVP (
1.1
) will depend solely on the norm
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
2<N<6
,
0<b_{1},b_{2}<\min\{2,\frac{N}{2},\frac{6-N}{2}\}
,
0<p_{1}<\frac{4-2b_{1}}{N-2}
, and
p_{2}=\frac{4-2b_{2}}{N-2}
. Suppose

i)

\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}>0
;

ii)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
\lambda_{2}>0
,
p_{1}<p_{2}
and
\frac{p_{1}b_{2}}{p_{2}}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}
;

iii)

\lambda_{1}<0
,
\lambda_{2}>0
and
b_{2}<b_{1}<\frac{N(p_{2}-p_{1})}{p_{2}+2}
.

Given
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{x}
, there exists
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}})
sufficiently small such that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) has a unique strong solution in
C([0,T],H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
satisfying

\|u\|_{S^{1}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(E,M).

Proof.

By practice, consider
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1
.

From Proposition
4.1
, we may assume that there exists a strong solution
u
to (
1.1
) on the slab
[-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and show that
u
has a finite
X^{1}_{b_{1},b_{2}}
bound on this slab as long as
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{x}})
is sufficiently small.

Let
w
be the unique strong global solution to the energy-critical equation (
3.1
) with initial data
w_{0}=u_{0}
at time
t=0
.
From Strichartz estimates we have
\|\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{0}\|_{W([-T,T])}\leq\|\nabla w_{0}\|<\infty
. So, we can consider
T
sufficiently small such that
\|\nabla e^{it\Delta}w_{0}\|_{W([-T,T])}
also is small and by Theorem
3.1
we have that
\|\nabla w\|_{S(L^{2},[-T,T])}
is bounded.

Then, as
X_{b_{1},b_{2}}
-norm is admissible, we can subdivide
[0,T]
into
J=J(E,\eta)
subintervals
I_{j}=[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
such that

(5.12)

\|\nabla w\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{j})}\sim\eta

for some small
\eta
to be specified later.

The nonlinear evolution of
w
being small on the interval
I_{j}
implies that the linear evolution is also small on
I_{j}
. In fact, by using Strichartz estimates and Corollary (
2.9
), we obtain the following inequality

\begin{split}\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{j})\Delta}w(t_{j})]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{j})}\leq&\;\|\nabla w\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{j})}+\||x|^{-b_{2}}|w|^{p_{2}}w\|_{2,\frac{2N}{N+2}}\\
\leq&\;\eta+C\;\|\nabla w\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{j})}^{p_{2}+1}\leq\eta+C\eta^{p_{2}+1}\end{split}

where
C
is an absolute constant that depends on the Strichartz constant. By choosing
\eta
sufficiently small, for any
0\leq j\leq J
, we obtain the following estimate

(5.13)

\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{j})\Delta}w(t_{j})]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{j})}\leq 2\eta.

To estimate
u
on the interval
I_{0}
, recalling that
u(0)=w(0)=u_{0}
, we use Strichartz estimates and Corollary
2.7
:

\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}\leq&\;\|\nabla[e^{it\Delta}w_{0}]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}+C\;|I_{0}|^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}^{p_{1}+1}+\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}^{p_{2}+1}\\
\leq&\;2\eta+C\;T^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}^{p_{1}+1}+\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}^{p_{2}+1}.\end{split}

Assuming that
\eta
and
T
are sufficiently small, a standard continuity argument then yields

(5.14)

\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}\leq 4\eta.

Thus, by Sobolev embedding, we get

\|u\|_{Z(I_{0})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{W^{0}(I_{0})}\leq 4C\eta.

By definition of the
X_{b_{1},b_{2}}
-norm, (
3.25
) holds on
I=I_{0}
for
L=4C\eta
.
Moreover, from Lemma
3.3
and Corollary
5.2
, we have

\|\nabla w\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\leq C(\eta)\|\nabla w_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C(E(u_{0}),M(u_{0})).

Therefore, we have proved that (
3.24
) holds with
E=C(E(u_{0}),M(u_{0}))
. Also, as (
3.26
) holds with
E^{\prime}=0
, we are in the position to apply the stability result from Theorem
3.5
, provided the error, which in this case is the first nonlinearity, is sufficiently small. By Corollary
2.7
,

\|\nabla(|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u)\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I_{0})}\lesssim T^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{0})}^{p_{1}+1}\lesssim T^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}(4\eta)^{p_{1}+1},

where in the last inequality we use (
5.14
). Thus, choosing
T
sufficiently small (depending only on the energy and the mass of the initial data), we get

(5.15)

\|\nabla(|x|^{-b_{1}}|u|^{p_{1}}u)\|_{S^{\prime}(L^{2},I_{0})}\leq\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(E(u_{0}),M(u_{0}))
is a small constant to be chosen later. Taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, the hypotheses of Theorem
3.5
are satisfied, which implies that the conclusion holds. In particular,

(5.16)

\|\nabla(u-w)\|_{S(L^{2},I_{0})}\leq\,C(E,M)\,\varepsilon,

for a small positive constant
C
that depends only on the dimension
N
.
By Strichartz estimates, (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) imply

(5.17)

\|u(t_{1})-w(t_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{x}}\lesssim\,C(E,M)\,\varepsilon,

(5.18)

\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{1})\Delta}(u(t_{1})-w(t_{1}))]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}}\lesssim\,C(E,M)\,\varepsilon.

Using (
5.13
), (
5.17
), (
5.18
), Corollary
2.7
, and Strichartz estimates, we estimate

\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}\leq&\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{1})\Delta}u(t_{1})]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}^{p_{1}+1}+C\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}^{p_{2}+1}\\
\leq&\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{1})\Delta}w(t_{1})]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}+\|\nabla[e^{i(t-t_{1})\Delta}(u(t_{1})-w(t_{1}))]\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}\\
&+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}^{p_{1}+1}+C\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}^{p_{2}+1}\\
\leq&2\eta+C(E,M)\,\varepsilon+CT^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}^{p_{1}+1}+C\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}^{p_{2}+1}.\end{split}

A standard continuity argument then yields

\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}(I_{1})}\leq 4\eta,

provided

By induction, for every

\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])}\leq 4J\eta=C(E).

Now, to prove that

, we proceed as follows.
By Strichartz estimates, Corollary

Similarly,

(5.22)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{S(L^{2},[0,T])}\lesssim&\,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+T^{1-\frac{p_{1}+2}{\gamma_{b_{1}}}}\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])}^{p_{1}}\|u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])}\\
&+\|\nabla u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])}^{p_{2}}\|u\|_{X_{b_{1},b_{2}}([0,T])}.\end{split}

Subdividing

This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given an initial condition
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

u
for the initial value problem (IVP) (

) extends for all time. Suppose, by contradiction, that the maximum existence time

for the solution

Define

based on Theorem

, where
\|u\|_{S^{1}(L^{2},[0,T])}\leq C(E,M)
, and

depends only on

and

. As a reminder, the Corollary ensures uniform control of the kinetic energy of

\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(E,M)
for all

into disjoint intervals

Spin and Valley Hall effects induced by asymmetric interparticle scattering
M. M. Glazov, L. E. Golub

Abstract
We develop the theory of the spin and valley Hall effects in two-dimensional systems caused by asymmetric – skew – scattering of the quasiparticles. The collision integral is derived in the third order in the particle-particle interaction with account for the spin-orbit coupling both for bosons and fermions. It is shown that the scattering asymmetry appears only in the processes where the interaction between the particles in the initial and intermediate state is present. We show that for degenerate electrons or nondegenerate particles the spin and valley currents induced by interparticle collisions are suppressed with their steady-state values being proportional to the squared temperature or density, respectively. Our results imply non-Fermi liquid properties of electrons in the presence of electron-electron skew scattering. Strong deviations from conventional picture of interparticle scattering are also demonstrated for the skew scattering of two-dimensional degenerate bosons, e.g. excitons or exciton polaritons: The spin or valley current of degenerate bosons contains the enhancement factor exponentially growing with increase in the particle density.



I
Introduction

Spin-dependent effects in condensed matter result from a coupling between the electron orbital and spin degrees of freedom caused by the spin-orbit interaction. Spin currents
Engel et al. 2007
take a special place among of the most exciting phenomena in the field both from fundamental and applied physics viewpoints.
An interesting issue here
is the possibility to have a spin current in thermodynamic equilibrium with the most prominent example being the Rashba medium – a system with linear in the momentum spin-dependent terms in the electron or hole Hamiltonian
Rashba and Sheka 1959
;
Rashba 1960
;
Rashba and Sherman 1988
– where the spin can flow even if the system is not perturbed
Rashba 2003
;
Sonin 2007
. However, in a uniform media these currents do not result in any spin accumulation. The spin transport and spin accumulation arises in the presence of an external electric field where the spin Hall effect (SHE) arises
Dyakonov and Perel’ 1971
;
Hirsch 1999
, see Ref.
Engel et al. 2007
for review. It results in a conversion of the particle flux to the transverse spin current and subsequent accumulation of the spin polarization at the sample’s edges
Dyakonov 2017
. Generalization of this phenomenon to the multivalley systems is the valley Hall effect (VHE), i.e., the conversion of the particle flux to the perpendicular opposite flows of particles belonging to two different valleys
Mak et al. 2014
;
Glazov and Golub 2020a
;
Glazov and Golub 2020b
;
Glazov 2021
.

The microscopic mechanisms of SHE and VHE are naturally related to the spin-orbit coupling and can be both intrinsic and extrinsic
Engel et al. 2005
, and the dominant one in many cases is the skew-scattering mechanism associated with the spin- or valley-dependent asymmetry of the particle scattering by a defect
Mott 1929
.
This skew scattering has been studied in both bulk semiconductors and heterostructures as well as in metals for the scattering by both impurities and acoustic phonons
Gurevich and Yassievich 1963
;
Abakumov and Yassievich 1972
;
Glazov and Golub 2020a
. The possibility of an asymmetric – skew – electron-electron scattering has been briefly discussed in the literature in the context of the spin Hall drag in double quantum well structures
Badalyan and Vignale 2009
and recently in Ref.
Glazov 2021
for the spin accumulation in the hydrodynamic electron transport regime, see also Ref.
Fruchart et al. 2022
. However, the theory of the electron-electron skew scattering is far from being complete.

In this work, we consider the SHE and VHE caused by the inter-particle collisions only. This situation is highly relevant, e.g. for two-dimensional (2D) high-mobility semiconductors and graphene where electron-electron scattering dominates over the impurity and phonon scattering and controls the transport effects
de Jong and Molenkamp 1995
;
Bandurin et al. 2016
;
Moll et al. 2016
;
Sulpizio et al. 2019
;
Gusev et al. 2020
;
Ku et al. 2020
;
Mönch et al. 2022
;
Gupta et al. 2021
, see Ref.
Narozhny 2022
for review.
We argue that the skew scattering and, hence, the SHE and VHE are possible at the inter-particle scattering.
We derive the kinetic equation with allowance for the skew scattering processes both for fermions and bosons and demonstrate asymmetric in spin and momenta terms in the collision integral. They appear beyond the Born approximation for the scattering amplitude. We show that for the spin or valley Hall effect to take place, the scattering should occur due to the interaction of the particles in the initial and intermediate states.
It results in
unconventional behavior of SHE and VHE in both degenerate Fermi and Bose as well as in nondegenerate gases.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we present the kinetic equation and derive the collision integral with allowance for the asymmetric scattering in the third-order perturbation theory. We demonstrate derivation by two methods, using the scattering matrix and using the Keldysh diagram technique. Section
III
contains the theory of the SHE and VHE at the interparticle collisions and particular results for the Fermi, Boltzmann and Bose systems.
General implications of the obtained results and conclusion are presented in the end of the paper, Sec.
IV
. Details of calculations are given in Appendices.

\text{St}_{\rm sk}
is zero at the equilibrium distribution function
f_{k}^{0}
. This fact follows from the general arguments: for the spin-orbit coupling in the form of Eq. (
11
) any equilibrium spin currents are forbidden. One can also demonstrate it explicitly making use of the fact that the equilibrium distribution functions depend on the energy only and using the energy and momentum conservation laws in Eq. (
14
) and explicit form of
\mathcal{S}_{z}

B
for details.

III
Spin and Valley Hall effects

Following Ref.
Glazov and Golub 2020a
we define the spin/valley current as

\bm{j}_{\rm SHE/VHE}\equiv\bm{j}^{\rm s}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s,\bm{k}}s\frac{\hbar\bm{k}}{m}f_{\bm{k},s}.

(16)

In the studied situation the electrons are unpolarized, thus it is sufficient to calculate the current for
s=+
branch, while the current in the
s=-
branch has the same magnitude and opposite direction.
We now turn to the calculation of the SHE and VHE from the kinetic Eq. (
1
) with the collision integrals (
14
) and (
7
). We treat the spin-orbit coupling as a small perturbation, accordingly, we solve Eq. (
1
) by iterations.

We calculate the velocity generation rate:

\left<\dot{\bm{V}}\right>=\sum_{\bm{k}}{\hbar\bm{k}\over m}\text{St}_{\rm sk}[f_{\bm{k}}].

(17)

Analysis shows (see Appendix
C
) that
this generation rate is nonzero only if the following conditions are met in Eq. (
14
) for
\text{St}_{\rm sk}

\bm{p}^{\prime}=\bm{k},\quad\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{p},\quad\bm{p}^{\prime\prime}=\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}.

(18)

In other words, the only relevant scattering processes are
\bm{k}+,\bm{p}-\rightarrow\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}+,\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}-\rightarrow\bm{p}+,\bm{k}-
.

Therefore we simplify the velocity generation rate
applying Eq. (
18
) everywhere except for the
\delta
-functions:

\left<\dot{\bm{V}}\right>={64\pi^{2}\over m}V_{0}^{3}\xi\\
\times\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}\bm{p}^{\prime\prime},\bm{k}\bm{p}}\delta(\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}})\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p},\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{p}^{\prime}}\\
\times\delta(\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{p^{\prime\prime}}-\varepsilon_{k^{\prime\prime}}-\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}})\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}^{\prime\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}+\bm{p}^{\prime}}\\
\times\bm{k}{\mathcal{S}}_{z}(1\pm f_{\bm{k}})(1\pm f_{\bm{p}})f_{\bm{k}}f_{\bm{p}}f_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}(1\pm f_{\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}}).

(19)

Since generation of the spin and valley Hall current is a nonequilibrium response to the external force
\bm{F}
, substituting the distributions
f_{\bm{k}}=f_{k}^{0}-\hbar(\bm{v}_{dr}\cdot\bm{k})\differential f_{k}^{0}/\differential\varepsilon_{k}
with the drift velocity
\bm{v}_{dr}=\bm{F}\tau_{p}/m
and linearizing Eq. (
19
) in
\bm{v}_{dr}
we obtain

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=v_{dr,x}{16\pi^{2}\hbar\over mT}V_{0}^{3}\xi\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}\bm{p}^{\prime\prime},\bm{k}\bm{p}}\delta(\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}})\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p},\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{p}^{\prime}}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})(1\pm f_{p}^{0})f_{k}^{0}f_{p}^{0}f_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{0}(1\pm f_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{0})\\
\times\delta(\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{p^{\prime\prime}}-\varepsilon_{k^{\prime\prime}}-\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}})\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}^{\prime\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}+\bm{p}^{\prime}}\bm{\mathcal{S}}\cdot\quantity{\qty[ \bm k \qty(1\pm 2 f_k^0)+\bm p \qty(1\pm 2 f_p^0) + \bm k'' \qty(1 \pm 2 f_{k''}^0)] \times(\bm k-\bm p) }.

(20)

Here we assume
\bm{v}_{dr}\parallel x
and
\tau_{p}
to be energy independent, accordingly we calculate the generation rate of the transverse to
\bm{F}
velocity,

\bm{\mathcal{S}}=(\bm{k}+\bm{p}-2\bm{k}^{\prime\prime})\times(\bm{k}-\bm{p})
, and
T
is the temperature in energy units.

In order to find the spin or valley current in the steady state one has to take into account its relaxation. If the dominant relaxation mechanism is the elastic scattering by impurities or quasielastic scattering by phonons, then

j^{s}_{y}=\tau_{p}\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>.

(21a)

However, we are mainly interested in the situation where the interparticle collisions dominate the relaxation. Conserving the total momentum of the colliding quasiparticles they result in the relaxation of the spin and valley currents. Generally, these collisions are inelastic and the solution of the corresponding kinetic equation is quite involved
Glazov and Ivchenko 2002
;
Glazov and Ivchenko 2004
. To illustrate the effect and provide an estimate of its magnitude it is convenient to introduce a relaxation time approximation and present the current in the form similar to Eq. (
21a
)

j^{s}_{y}=\tau_{\rm sc}\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>

(21b)

with
\tau_{\rm sc}
being the corresponding spin current relaxation time, it is derived in Appendix
D
and defined by Eq. (
39
). Note that by contrast to relatively slow relaxation of odd harmonics of spin-averaged distribution (or lack of relaxation in the case of the first harmonic)
Ledwith et al. 2019
, the interpartcle collisions result in the efficient relaxation of both first and third angular harmonics of the spin and valley distrubtion
Glazov and Ivchenko 2004
.

Still, the expressions for the SHE and VHE currents for arbitrary statistics of quasiparticles are quite involved. Below we consider the most illustrative and experimentally relevant limits of Fermi, Boltzmann and Bose gases.

III.1
Fermi gas

In the case of degenerate electrons, a dramatic suppression of the SHE and VHE at the electron-electron collisions occurs.
We recall that the electron-electron scattering time responsible for the spin current relaxation at
E_{\rm F}\gg T
behaves as

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm sc}}=C\frac{T^{2}}{E_{F}},

(22)

where the constant
C
weakly depends on the temperature
Glazov and Ivchenko 2004
: The Pauli exclusion principle blocks electron-electron collisions at low temperatures. Inspecting general Eq. (
20
) we note that the factors
f^{0}_{k^{\prime\prime}}(1-f^{0}_{k^{\prime\prime}})
should provide additional reduction of the phase space
and one can expect
\left\langle\dot{V}_{y}\right\rangle\propto T^{3}
.

However the analysis shows that it is not the case, and
\left\langle\dot{V}_{y}\right\rangle
is expected to behave as
T^{4}
.
Indeed, to obtain a result in the lowest order in
T/E_{F}
we can set all absolute values of the wavevectors equal to the Fermi wavevector
k=k^{\prime}=k^{\prime\prime}=p=p^{\prime}=p^{\prime\prime}=k_{\rm F}

everywhere except for the equilibrium occupations.
Then we come to the energy integrals (
q=k,p
or
k^{\prime\prime}
)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential\varepsilon_{q}(1-f_{q}^{0})f_{q}^{0}(1-2f_{q}^{0})\propto\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential\varepsilon_{q}\derivative[2]{f_q^0}{\varepsilon_q},

(23)

which are exponentially small at
E_{\rm F}\gg T
.

Hence, the non-zero result for the scattering rate can be obtained in the next order in
T/E_{\rm F}
where one takes into account deviations of the absolute values of the wavevectors from
k_{\rm F}
. Consequently, we expect for degenerate electrons

j_{y}^{s}\sim N_{1}v_{dr,x}\times\xi k_{\text{F}}^{2}\times gV_{0}\times\quantity({T \over E_\text{F}})^{2}.

(24)

As a result, the generation of the spin and valley currents due to the electron-electron scattering is suppressed. By contrast, interparticle scattering results in relatively efficient relaxation of the spin and valley currents.

III.2
Boltzmann gas

For Boltzmann statistics we have for both bosons and fermions
f_{k}^{0}=N_{1}/(gT)\exp(-\varepsilon_k/T){\ll 1}
where
N_{1}
and
g=m/(2\pi\hbar^{2})
are the two-dimensional particle density and the density of states per one spin or valley.

Calculations presented in Appendix
E
(see also Appendix
D.1
) yield
the steady-state spin current value in the form

j_{y}^{s}=-{256\sqrt{3}\over\pi}N_{1}v_{dr,x}\times\xi k_{T}^{2}\times gV_{0}\times{E_{\text{F}}\over T}.

(25)

Here
N_{1}v_{dr,x}
is the particle current in one spin subband,
\xi k_{T}^{2}
is the dimensionless spin-orbit coupling strength (
k_{T}=\sqrt{2mT}/\hbar
is the thermal wavevector),
gV_{0}
is the non-Born parameter, and the factor
E_{\text{F}}/T=4\pi N_{1}/k_{T}^{2}\ll 1
stems from occupations of intermediate states. In derivation we used the spin-current relaxation time for the non-degenerate case, Eq. (
44
):

{1\over\tau_{\text{sc}}}={2m\over\hbar^{3}}V_{0}^{2}N_{1}.

(26)

It is instructive to compare the result (
25
) with the spin current induced by the scattering by the static short-range impurities with the same density
N_{1}
in the absence of electron-electron collisions. In these situations the relaxation rates for the spin currents are about the same. From Eq. (22) of Ref.
Glazov and Golub 2020a
we obtain

j_{y}^{s}\sim N_{1}v_{dr,x}\times\xi k_{T}^{2}\times gV_{0},

(27)

where the numerical coefficient on the order of unity is omitted. Comparing Eqs. (
25
) and (
27
) we observe that the spin and valley currents induced by the electron-electron collisions have additional smallness
\sim E_{\rm F}/T
related, as discussed above, to the fact that the skew scattering requires
the intermediate state
\bm{k}^{\prime\prime}
to be occupied.
In the case of impurity scattering the intermediate state occupancies play no role thus parametrically enhancing the generation rate.
However, while for reasonable electron densities
N_{1}\sim 10^{11}\text{cm}^{-2}
,
m=0.1\penalty\ m_{0}
and at room temperature the small parameter
E_{\rm F}/T
is about
0.1
,
the numerical prefactor in Eq. (
25
)
256\sqrt{3}/\pi\approx 141.1
provides significant enhancement of the effect. Thus, in the state of the art samples the interparticle scattering effect on the spin current can be pronounced.

III.3
Degenerate Bose gas

While degeneracy in Fermi systems results in suppression of the interparticle scattering effects, in degenerate Bose gas stimulated scattering processed dominate. As a result, for degenerate bosons the occupancy of the intermediate states is expected to significantly enhance the skew scattering effect compared to the enhancement of the spin current relaxation rate.

To illustrate the enhancement we observe that for the degenerate Bose gas, the distribution function can be represented as
f_{k}^{0}=T/(\varepsilon_{k}-\mu)\gg 1
with the chemical potential
\mu<0
,
\absolutevalue{\mu}\ll T
. For energies
\varepsilon_{k}-\mu\gtrsim T
the distribution function exponential decays, such energy range is irrelevant for the following. Calculations presented in Appendix
F
demonstrate the exponentially large steady-state value of the spin/valley current:

j_{y}^{s}=-\mathcal{C}\times v_{dr,x}gT\times\xi k_{T}^{2}\times gV_{0}\times\exp({2N_1 \over gT}).

(28)

Here
gT
is the characteristic concentration of particles where the gas becomes degenerate, consequently
2N_{1}/(gT)>1
for degenerate bosons, and the numerical coefficient calculated in Appendix
F
is

\mathcal{C}\approx{1340.4}
. In deriving Eq. (
28
) we used the value of the spin current relaxation rate (
49
):

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm sc}}=\mathcal{C}_{\tau}\times 2^{11}{g^{2}TV_{0}^{2}\over\hbar}\exp({N_1\over gT}),

(29)

where
\mathcal{C}_{\tau}\approx 0.36
is the numerical coefficient, for calculations see Appendix
D.2
.

Equation (
28
) shows a possibility of a dominant role of the skew scattering at low enough temperatures in Bose systems. However, the analysis presented above is valid for temperatures exceeding the temperature of the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless transitions to the superfluid state. The analysis of the collective properties of bosons with the skew scattering processes included goes beyond the scope of the present work.

IV
Conclusion

In this work we have developed a theory of the spin and valley Hall effects in two-dimensional systems in the presence of frequent collisions between the quasiparticles. We have addressed both fermions, electrons in quantum wells or two-dimensional semiconductors, and bosons, e.g., excitons or exciton-polaritons. We have focused on the skew scattering effect where the colliding particles with the opposite spin or valley indices scatter in the opposite directions. The skew scattering results in the generation of a spin or valley current in the transverse direction to the particle current induced, e.g., by a real or synthetic electric field. The relaxation of the spin and valley current also assumed to occur due to the interparticle scattering.

We have derived the asymmetric – skew – contribution to the interparticle collision integral in the third order of perturbation theory in the interaction potential. We have demonstrated that occupancies of intermediate states play a crucial role in the effect: the skew scattering occurs in the processes where the particle in the initial state interacts with the particle in the intermediate state. For fermions, for example, the two-particle intermediate states which are
empty of fully occupied play no role. It results in a specific behavior of the spin and valley current generation rates. In a non-degenerate gas the generation rate is proportional to the squared density of the particles, while in a degenerate Fermi gas the generation rate demonstrates
T^{4}
temperature dependence. By contrast, for degenerate Bose gas this results in exponential enhancement of the spin and valley currents with increasing of particle concentration or decreasing the temperature.
Moreover, in the Boltzmann and Fermi gases, the parametrically small spin or valley current is additionally increased because of numerical factors on the order

10^{3}\ldots 10^{4}
that appear in the resulting expressions.

Hence, an asymmetry of the interparticle scattering results in deviations of a classical Fermi-liquid picture where in the processes of quasiparticle scattering occupancies of intermediate states play no role. By contrast, for bosons, the role of the skew scattering turns out to be significant when the Bose gas becomes degenerate. The analysis of the non-Fermi liquid effects for degenerate electrons and effects of asymmetry in interactions of bosons on collective phenomena is a separate problem for further studies.

\cos{\varphi_{\bm k}}
where
\varphi_{\bm{k}}
is the angle between the
x
-axis and
\bm{k}
and sum over
\bm{k}
with the result

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm sc}}=-\frac{\sum_{\bm{k}}\cos{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}\mathop{\rm St}{[k_{x}f_{k}^{0}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})]}}{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}kf_{k}^{0}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})}.

(39)

Substitution of Eq. (
38
) yields

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm sc}}=\frac{4\pi V_{0}^{2}/\hbar}{\sum_{\bm{k}}kf_{k}^{0}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})}\\
\times\sum\limits_{\bm{k}\bm{p}\bm{k}^{\prime}\bm{p}^{\prime}}\delta_{\bm{k}+\bm{p},\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{p}^{\prime}}\delta(\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{p}-\varepsilon_{k^{\prime}}-\varepsilon_{p^{\prime}})\\
\times\quantity(k-\bm k \cdot\bm k'/k)f_{k}^{0}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})F(\bm{p};\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{p}^{\prime}),

(40)

where we made substitutions
\cos{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}k_{x}\to{k/2}
,
\cos{\varphi}_{\bm{k}}k_{x}^{\prime}\to{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{k}^{\prime}/(2k)}
taking into account the isotropy in the
xy
plane.
Then using the notations
\bm{\varkappa}
,
\bm{\varkappa}^{\prime}
introduced in Eq. (
34
) we obtain

{1\over\tau_{\text{sc}}}={2mV_{0}^{2}\over\hbar^{3}}{\sum\limits_{\bm{k},\bm{\varkappa},\bm{\varkappa}^{\prime}}\left<{\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{\varkappa}-\bm{\varkappa}^{\prime})\over k}f_{k}^{0}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})F(\bm{p};\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{p}^{\prime})\right>\over\sum\limits_{\bm{k}}kf_{k}^{0}(1\pm f_{k}^{0})}.

(41)

Here angular brackets mean averaging over directions of the vector
\bm{\varkappa}^{\prime}
at
\varkappa^{\prime}=\varkappa
, and
\bm{p}=\bm{k}-\bm{\varkappa}
,
\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{k}+(\bm{\varkappa}^{\prime}-\bm{\varkappa})/2
,
\bm{p}^{\prime}=\bm{k}-(\bm{\varkappa}^{\prime}+\bm{\varkappa})/2
.

D.1
Relaxation rate at Boltzmann statistics

For non-degenerate particles (Boltzmann statistics) we take in Eq. (
41
)
f^{0}_{k}=(N_{1}/gT)\exp(-\varepsilon_k/T)\ll 1
.
Then
we obtain

{1\over\tau_{\text{sc}}}={2mV_{0}^{2}\over\hbar^{3}}{\sum\limits_{\bm{k},\bm{\varkappa}}{\bm{k}\cdot\bm{\varkappa}\over k}f_{k}^{0}f_{\bm{k}-\bm{\varkappa}}^{0}\over\sum\limits_{\bm{k}}kf_{k}^{0}}.

(42)

Performing angular averaging with help of
\left<\cos{\theta}\exp(a\cos{\theta})\right>_{\theta}=I_{1}(a)
,
we get

{1\over\tau_{\text{sc}}}={4\pi V_{0}^{2}N_{1}\over\hbar Tk_{T}^{3}/(8\sqrt{\pi})}\sum\limits_{\bm{k},\bm{\varkappa}}\varkappa I_{1}\quantity({2k\varkappa\over k_T^2})\exp(-{2k^2+\varkappa^2 \over k_T^2}).

(43)

Then integrating over absolute values of
k
and
\varkappa
we
obtain

{1\over\tau_{\text{sc}}}={2m\over\hbar^{3}}V_{0}^{2}N_{1}.

(44)

D.2
Relaxation rate at Bose-Einstein statistics

For degenerate Bose particles
we take in Eq. (
41
)

f^{0}_{k}=T/(\varepsilon_{k}+\absolutevalue{\mu})\gg 1,

(45)

where
\mu<0
is related to the particle density with a fixed spin/ in a given valley via
N_{1}=gT\ln(T/\abs{\mu})
.
Then we obtain

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm sc}}=\frac{2^{9}mV_{0}^{2}g^{2}T^{4}}{\hbar^{3}\mu^{2}}\sqrt{2m\absolutevalue{\mu}\over\hbar^{2}}{1\over\sum_{\bm{k}}k\quantity(f_{k}^0)^{2}}\mathcal{C}_{\tau},

(46)

where

\mathcal{C}_{\tau}=\\
\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\differential X\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\differential Y\Biggl<{Y(1-\cos\varphi)+\sqrt{XY}[\cos(\theta-\varphi)-\cos\varphi]\over\sqrt{X+Y+2\sqrt{XY}\cos\theta}}\\
\times{1\over(X+Y+1)^{2}-4XY\cos^{2}(\theta-\varphi)}\\
\times{1\over(X+Y+1)^{2}-4XY\cos^{2}\theta}\Biggr>_{\varphi,\theta}.

(47)

Here we used
X=\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+\bm{p}}/\absolutevalue{\mu}
,
Y=\varepsilon_{\varkappa}/\absolutevalue{\mu}
. Note that while the simplified form of the distribution function (
45
) is valid for
\varepsilon_{k}\ll T
, the integrals in Eq. (
47
) converge at
X,Y\to\infty
, thus we can extend the integration up to
+\infty
.

Since

\sum_{\bm{k}}k\quantity(f_{k}^0)^{2}={\pi gT^{2}\over 2\absolutevalue{\mu}}\sqrt{2m\absolutevalue{\mu}\over\hbar^{2}},

(48)

we obtain

\frac{1}{\tau_{\rm sc}}=2^{11}{(gT)^{2}V_{0}^{2}\over\hbar\absolutevalue{\mu}}\mathcal{C}_{\tau}.

(49)

For numerical evaluation of the 4-fold integral (
47
) it is convenient to change the variables formally introducing
R
and
\alpha
as

X=R\cos{\alpha}
,
Y=R\sin{\alpha}
.
Correspondingly, the integration is carried out over a sector in the plane
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\pi/2
, and
R\in[0,\infty)
. Numerical calculation shows that

\mathcal{C}_{\tau}\approx 0.36.

(50)

Appendix E
Spin and valley current
generation rate
at Boltzmann statistics

At Boltzmann statistics we have

f_{k^{\prime}}^{0}f_{p^{\prime}}^{0}f_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{0}=f_{{\absolutevalue{\bm K - \bm\varkappa}\over 2}}^{0}f_{{\absolutevalue{\bm K + \bm\varkappa}\over 2}}^{0}f_{{\absolutevalue{\bm K - \bm Q}\over 2}}^{0}=\quantity({N_1\over gT})^{3}\exp[-{\hbar^{2}\over 4mT}\quantity({3\over 2}K^2 + \varkappa^2 + {1\over 2}Q^2 - \bm K \cdot\bm Q)],

(51)

and obtain from Eq. (
37
):

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=v_{dr,x}{16m\over T\hbar^{3}}V_{0}^{3}\xi\quantity({N_1\over gT})^{3}\sum_{\bm{K},\bm{\varkappa},\bm{Q}}\exp[-{\hbar^{2}\over 4mT}\quantity({3\over 2}K^2 + \varkappa^2 + {1\over 2}Q^2 - K Q\cos\theta)]{\sin\varphi\over\absolutevalue{\sin\varphi}}\quantity[3 K\sin(\theta-\varphi)- Q\sin\varphi]\varkappa.

(52)

Here
\theta
is an angle between
\bm{K}
and
\bm{Q}
,
and
\varphi
is the angle between
\bm{Q}
and
\bm{\varkappa}
.

Averaging over

\theta

yields:

\left<\exp(a\cos\theta)\right>_{\theta}=I_{0}(a),\qquad\left<\exp(a\cos\theta)\sin(\theta-\varphi)\right>_{\theta}=-\sin\varphi I_{1}(a),

(53)

and we get

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=-v_{dr,x}{{16}m\over T\hbar^{3}}V_{0}^{3}\xi\quantity({N_1\over gT})^{3}\sum_{\bm{K},\bm{\varkappa},\bm{Q}}\exp[-{\hbar^{2}\over 4mT}\quantity({3\over 2}K^2 + \varkappa^2 + {1\over 2}Q^2 )]{\sin^{2}\varphi\over\absolutevalue{\sin\varphi}}\quantity[3 K I_1\qty({\hbar^2K Q\over 4mT})+ QI_0\qty({\hbar^2K Q\over 4mT})]\varkappa.

(54)

Averaging over
\varphi
and summation over
\bm{\varkappa}
yield:

\left<{\sin^{2}\varphi\over\absolutevalue{\sin\varphi}}\right>_{\varphi}={2\over\pi},\qquad\sum_{\bm{\varkappa}}\varkappa\exp(-{\hbar^2\varkappa^2\over 4mT})={1\over\sqrt{\pi}}\quantity({mT\over\pi\hbar^2})^{3/2},

(55)

therefore we get

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=-v_{dr,x}{{32}m\over T\hbar^{3}\sqrt{\pi}}V_{0}^{3}\xi\quantity({N_1\over gT})^{3}\quantity({mT\over\pi\hbar^2})^{3/2}\sum_{\bm{K},\bm{Q}}\exp[-{\hbar^{2}\over 4mT}\quantity({3\over 2}K^2 + {1\over 2}Q^2 )]\quantity[3 K I_1\qty({\hbar^2K Q\over 4mT})+ QI_0\qty({\hbar^2K Q\over 4mT})].

(56)

Calculations show:

\sum_{\bm{K},\bm{Q}}\exp[-{\hbar^{2}\over 4mT}\quantity({3\over 2}K^2 + {1\over 2}Q^2 )]3KI_{1}\quantity({\hbar^2K Q\over 4mT})=\sum_{\bm{K},\bm{Q}}\exp[-{\hbar^{2}\over 4mT}\quantity({3\over 2}K^2 + {1\over 2}Q^2 )]QI_{0}\quantity({\hbar^2K Q\over 4mT})\\
=\quantity({mT\over\hbar^2})^{5/2}{2\sqrt{3}\over\pi^{3/2}}.

(57)

Therefore we finally get for the generation rate:

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=-N_{1}v_{dr,x}{256\sqrt{3}\over\pi}gV_{0}\xi k_{T}^{2}{E_{\rm F}\over T}\times{1\over\tau_{\rm sc}}.

(58)

Appendix F
Calculation of the spin current
at Bose statistics

At
f^{0}_{k}=T/(\varepsilon_{k}+\absolutevalue{\mu})\gg 1
we obtain from Eq. (
37
):

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=-v_{dr,x}2^{20}\pi g^{4}V_{0}^{3}\xi k_{T}^{2}{T^{5}\over\hbar\absolutevalue{\mu}^{3}}\mathcal{C}_{g},

(59)

where

\mathcal{C}_{g}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\differential X_{K}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\differential X_{Q}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\differential X_{\varkappa}\Biggl<{\absolutevalue{\sin\varphi}\sqrt{X_{\varkappa}}\over\quantity[\qty(X_K + X_\varkappa+1)^2-4X_K X_\varkappa\cos^2(\theta-\varphi))^2]^{2}\quantity(X_K + X_Q - 2\sqrt{X_KX_Q}\cos\theta+1)^{2}}\\
\times\quantity[{2\qty(X_K + X_\varkappa+1) \sqrt{X_K}\cos\theta\over\qty(X_K + X_\varkappa+1)^2- 4X_KX_\varkappa\cos^2(\theta-\varphi)} + {\sqrt{X_K}\cos\theta+\sqrt{X_Q} \over X_K + X_Q - 2\sqrt{X_KX_Q}\cos\theta+1} ]\Biggr>_{\varphi,\theta}.

(60)

Here we introduced dimensionless energies
X_{q}=\varepsilon_{q}/\absolutevalue{\mu}
(
q=K,Q,\varkappa
), and, as before, the upper limits for integration were extended to
+\infty
due to the convergence of integrals.

Numerical calculation yields
\mathcal{C}_{g}\approx 0.3
.

Using Eq. (
49
) we can rewrite the velocity generation rate in the form

\left<\dot{V}_{y}\right>=-v_{dr,x}gT\xi k_{T}^{2}gV_{0}{T^{2}\over\absolutevalue{\mu}^{2}}\times{\mathcal{C}\over\tau_{\rm sc}},

(61)

where
\mathcal{C}={2^{9}\pi\mathcal{C}_{g}/\mathcal{C}_{\tau}}\approx{1340.4}
.

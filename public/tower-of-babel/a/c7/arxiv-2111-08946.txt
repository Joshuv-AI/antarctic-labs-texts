Global Strong Solutions to the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann System with Soft Potential in a Bounded Domain
Fucai Li, Yichun Wang Department of Mathematics, Nanjing University Nanjing, 210093, P.R. China

Abstract
. Boundary effects are crucial for dynamics of dilute charged gases governed by the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann (VPB) system. In this paper, we study the existence and regularity of solutions to the VPB system with soft potential in a bounded convex domain with in-flow boundary condition. We establish the existence of strong solutions in the time interval [ 0 , T ] [0,T] for an arbitrary given T > 0 T>0 when the initial distribution function is near an absolute Maxwellian. Our contribution is based on a new weighted energy estimate in some W 1 , p W^{1,p} space and L x 3 ​ L v 1 + L_{x}^{3}L_{v}^{1+} space for soft potential. By using the classical L 2 L^{2} – L ∞ L^{\infty} method and bootstrap argument, we extend the local solutions from small time scale to large time scale.

Introduction

Vlasov-Boltzmann equation is a classical model describing the dynamics and collision processes of dilute charged particles with a given field.
Subjected to a field
E
, the evolution of the distribution function
F(t,x,v)
of the particles is governed by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F+E\cdot\nabla_{v}F=Q(F,F).

For the elastic collisions, the Boltzmann collision operator
Q(\cdot,\cdot)
in (
1.1
) is given as the following non-symmetric form:

\displaystyle Q(F_{1},F_{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}b_{0}(\theta)[F_{1}(u^{\prime})F_{2}(v^{\prime})-F_{1}(u)F_{2}(v)]\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{gain}}(F_{1},F_{2})-Q_{\mathrm{loss}}(F_{1},F_{2}),

where
\gamma
equals to
1-\frac{4}{s}
with inverse power
1<s<4
, and
(u,v)
and
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
denote the velocities of the particles before and after the collision, which satisfy
u^{\prime}=u-[(u-v)\cdot\omega]\omega
,
v^{\prime}=v-[(v-u)\cdot\omega]\omega
and
|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}=|u^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}|^{2}
.
Throughout this paper, we assume

(1.2)

\displaystyle\gamma\in(-3,0),\,\,0<b_{0}(\theta)\leq C\cos\theta,

where
\cos\theta=\omega\cdot\frac{u-v}{|u-v|}
. We remark that if (
1.2
) holds, then the collision kernel
in
Q(\cdot,\cdot)
is called soft potential with Grad’s angular cutoff.

It is well-known that a global Maxwellian
\mu
satisfies
Q(\mu,\mu)=0
, where
\mu(v):=e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}
.

It is important for physical applications to consider the Vlasov-Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) coupled with a self-consistent electric field. In this case, we obtain the so-called Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann (VPB) system, namely,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F-\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}F=Q(F,F),

where
-\nabla_{x}\phi=E
in (
1.1
) and the self-consistent potential
\phi
is determined by the Poisson equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v-\rho_{0}.

Here the background density
\rho_{0}
is a constant.
Without loss of generality, by some rescaling, we assume that
\rho_{0}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu(v)\mathrm{d}v
.

We consider the system (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. We supply the Poisson equation (
1.4
)
with the zero Neumann boundary condition:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=\,0\,\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\quad\partial\Omega

and the equation (
1.3
) with the so-called in-flow boundary condition:

(1.6)

\displaystyle F(t,x,v)=G(t,x,v)\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,(x,v)\in\gamma_{-},

where
G
is a given function, the incoming set is defined as

\displaystyle\gamma_{-}:=\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v<0\},

and
n(x)
is the outward unit normal vector at a boundary point
x
.
We also define
\gamma_{+}
and
\gamma_{0}
as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{outgoing}\,\,\gamma_{+}:=

\displaystyle\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v>0\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{grazing}\,\,\gamma_{0}:=

\displaystyle\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v=0\}.

We make a perturbation around
\mu
, i.e.
F(t,x,v)=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f(t,x,v)
, to the problem (
1.3
)-(
1.6
).
Then the corresponding problem reads

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla_{x}\phi_{f}\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{v}{2}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{f}f+Lf=\Gamma(f,f)-v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{f}\sqrt{\mu},

(1.8)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi_{f}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)\sqrt{\mu(v)}\mathrm{d}v\,\,\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,\Omega,\,\,\,\frac{\partial\phi_{f}}{\partial n}=0\,\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,

(1.9)

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=g(t,x,v)\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,(x,v)\in\gamma_{-},

where
g
satisfies
G=\mu+\sqrt{\mu}g
,
and the initial datum
f|_{t=0}=f(0,x,v):=f_{0}(x,v)=(F(0,x,v)-\mu)/\sqrt{\mu}
. Here we use the notation
\phi_{f}
to emphasize the correspondence between
\phi
and
f
.

The nonlinear operator
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
and linear operator
L
in (
1.7
) are defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}Q(\sqrt{\mu}f_{1},\sqrt{\mu}f_{2}),

and

\displaystyle Lf=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}(Q(\mu,\sqrt{\mu}f)+Q(\sqrt{\mu}f,\mu)),

respectively.
And

L
can be further split into
L=\nu-K
. The collision frequency
\nu(v)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}b_{0}(\theta)\mu(u)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega
for
-3<\gamma<0
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{\gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{\gamma}}(1+|v|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\leq\nu(v)\leq C_{\gamma}(1+|v|^{2})^{\gamma/2}.

Equivalently, it holds that
\nu(v)\sim(1+|v|^{2})^{\gamma/2}\sim\langle v\rangle^{\gamma}
.

K
is an integral kernel defined as

\displaystyle Kf=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}b_{0}(\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)}\{f(u^{\prime})\sqrt{\mu(v^{\prime})}+f(v^{\prime})\sqrt{\mu(u^{\prime})}\}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega

(1.10)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\mu(v)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}b_{0}(\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)}f(u)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega:=K_{2}f-K_{1}f.

The nonlinear term
\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})
is equal to

(1.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}b_{0}(\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)}\{f_{1}(u^{\prime})f_{2}(v^{\prime})-f_{1}(u)f_{2}(v)\}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega:=\Gamma_{\mathrm{gain}}(f_{1},f_{2})-\Gamma_{\mathrm{loss}}(f_{1},f_{2}).

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the VPB system in bounded domains for soft potentials, i.e. the problem (
1.7
)-(
1.9
) under the assumption (
1.2
). To state our result, we need some preliminaries.

First, we give some explanations on the bounded domain
\Omega
. As
[
1
]
said, a
C^{3}
domain means that for any
p\in\partial\Omega
, there exist sufficiently small
\delta_{1},\delta_{2}>0
, and an one-to-one and onto
C^{3}-
map

\displaystyle\eta_{p}:\{x_{\parallel}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x_{\parallel}|<\delta_{1}\}\rightarrow

\displaystyle\,\partial\Omega\cap B(p,\delta_{2}),

\displaystyle x_{\parallel}=(x_{\parallel,1},x_{\parallel,2})\mapsto

\displaystyle\,\eta_{p}(x_{\parallel,1},x_{\parallel,2}).

And a convex domain means that there exists a
C_{\Omega}>0
such that for all
p\in\partial\Omega
and
\eta_{p}
, and for all
x_{\parallel}
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\sum^{2}_{i,j=1}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\eta_{p}(x_{\parallel})\cdot n(x_{\parallel})\leq-C_{\Omega}|\zeta|^{2}\,\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,\,\mathrm{all}\,\,\,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Next, for given
(t,x,v)
, let
[x(s),v(s)]=[x(s;t,x,v),v(s;t,x,v)]

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x(s)}{\mathrm{d}s}=v(s),\,\,\,\frac{\mathrm{d}v(s)}{\mathrm{d}s}=E(s,x(s))

with the initial condition:
[x(t;t,x,v),v(t;t,x,v)]=[x,v]
.

For
(t,x,v)
, we define the
backward exit time
as

\displaystyle t_{b}(t,x,v):=\sup\{s\geq 0:x(\tau)\in\Omega\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,\mathrm{all}\,\tau\in(t-s,t)\}.

We also define
x_{b}(t,x,v):=x(t-t_{b})
and
v_{b}(t,x,v):=v(t-t_{b})
.
Clearly, for any
x\in\Omega
,
t_{b}(t,x,v)
is well-defined for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. If
x\in\partial\Omega
,
t_{b}
is well-defined for all
v\cdot n(x)>0
. Thanks to Lemma 2 in
[
1
]
, if
\Omega
is convex in the sense of (
1.12
), and

satisfies

, and

, then it holds

.

As in
[
1
]
, for a given function

satisfies (
1.7
), we define a kinetic weight as

(1.13)

\displaystyle\alpha_{f,\varepsilon}(t,x,v):=\widetilde{\chi}\left(\frac{t-t_{b}+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)|n(x_{b})\cdot v_{b}|+\left[1-\widetilde{\chi}\left(\frac{t-t_{b}+\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\right)\right].

Here we have used a smooth function
\widetilde{\chi}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
satisfying

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}(\tau)=0,\,\tau\leq 0;\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\widetilde{\chi}(\tau)=1,\,\tau\geq 1;

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\widetilde{\chi}(\tau)\in[0,4]\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,\mathrm{all}\,\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

It is important to notice that

\displaystyle\alpha_{f,\varepsilon}(t,x,v)=|n(x)\cdot v|\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\gamma_{-},

which is the main motivation to bring in this definition.
Another crucial property of this kinetic weight is that, along the trajectory, we have

\displaystyle[\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}-\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}]\alpha_{f,\varepsilon}(t,x,v)\equiv 0.

This invariance is due to the fact that
\frac{\mathrm{d}(t-t_{b})}{\mathrm{d}t}\equiv 0
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{b}}{\mathrm{d}t}\equiv 0
and
\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{b}}{\mathrm{d}t}\equiv 0
. Hence,
\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{f,\varepsilon}(t,x,v)}{\mathrm{d}t}\equiv 0
.

We denote for the constants
\vartheta>0
and
\theta>0

\displaystyle\widetilde{\vartheta}(t):=\vartheta\left(1+\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\theta}}\right),

and we introduce a new time-velocity weight for the soft potential, namely,

\displaystyle w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(v)=e^{\widetilde{\vartheta}|v|^{2}}.

We need to define the boundary integration for

,

, that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{\pm}}g\mathrm{d}\gamma=\int_{\gamma_{\pm}}g(x,v)|n(x)\cdot v|\mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}v,

where

is the standard surface measure on
\partial\Omega
. We also denote for

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{\pm}}|h|^{p}\mathrm{d}\gamma:=|h|^{p}_{p,\pm}.

If
h\in L^{p}(\gamma_{\pm})
, we say

,
1\leq p<\infty
. For
p=\infty
, we regard
L^{\infty}(\gamma)
to be equal to
L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3})

Throughout this paper,

means that there is a constant

such that

.

\displaystyle f_{0}(x,v)=g(0,x,v)\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\,\gamma_{-},

Let us sketch our ideas and novelties in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Contrasted with the work
[
1
]
, where the hard sphere potential
(\gamma=1)
for Boltzmann collision operator
Q(F,F)
was considered, we consider the soft potential
(-3<\gamma<0)
case, which causes the singularity as relative velocity tends to zero.

To treat the singularity of
K=K_{2}-K_{1}
[see (
1
)], we introduce a smooth cut-off function
0\leq\chi\leq 1
such that
\chi(s)=1
if
s\geq 2\varepsilon
;
\chi(s)=0
if
s\leq\varepsilon
. Then we have

(1.15)

\displaystyle K_{1}=K_{1}^{\chi}+K^{1-\chi}_{1},\,\,\,K_{2}=K_{2}^{\chi}+K^{1-\chi}_{2},

where

\displaystyle K_{1}^{\chi}f=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}\chi(|u-v|)b_{0}(\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)}\sqrt{\mu(v)}f(u)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega,

\displaystyle K_{2}^{\chi}f=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}\chi(|u-v|)b_{0}(\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)}\{f(u^{\prime})\sqrt{\mu(v^{\prime})}+f(v^{\prime})\sqrt{\mu(u^{\prime})}\}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega

(1.16)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{k}_{2}^{\chi}(v,u)f(u)\mathrm{d}u.

In this paper, we regard the kernel
\mathbf{k}_{2}^{\chi}(v,u)
corresponding to
\sqrt{\mu(\cdot)}=e^{-\frac{1}{4}|\cdot|^{2}}
as “standard”.

\bullet
We emphasize that the structures of the time-velocity weight will be more subtle in the soft potential case than in the hard sphere case while proving a priori estimates and global existence of solutions to the system (
1.7
)–(
1.9
).

There are two main reasons to introduce the velocity-time weight function
w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(v)=e^{\vartheta(1+1/(1+t)^{\theta})|v|^{2}}
. We point out that the following heuristic calculations are crucial for Sections 3 and 5.

(1). It is clear that
w_{\vartheta}\leq w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\leq w_{2\vartheta}
. When
0<\vartheta\ll 1
, it holds
w_{\vartheta}(v)\leq e^{\frac{1}{4}|v|^{2}}
, i.e.
\sqrt{\mu(v)}\leq w_{\vartheta}^{-1}(v)
.
We have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{gain}}^{\chi}(f,g)+\Gamma_{\mathrm{gain}}^{\chi}(g,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}\chi(|u-v|)b_{0}(\theta)\sqrt{\mu(u)}(f(u^{\prime})g(v^{\prime})+f(v^{\prime})g(u^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}\chi(|u-v|)b_{0}(\theta)w_{\vartheta}^{-1}(u)(w_{\vartheta}^{-1}(u^{\prime})g(v^{\prime})+w_{\vartheta}^{-1}(v^{\prime})g(u^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{k}_{2,\vartheta}^{\chi}(v,u)g(u)\mathrm{d}u.

For the standard
\mathbf{k}_{2}^{\chi}(v,u)
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{k}_{2}^{\chi}(v,u)g(u)\mathrm{d}u=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|u-v|^{\gamma}\chi(|u-v|)b_{0}(\theta)w_{1/4}^{-1}(u)(w_{1/4}^{-1}(u^{\prime})g(v^{\prime})+w_{1/4}^{-1}(v^{\prime})g(u^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathbf{k}_{2,1/4}^{\chi}(v,u)g(u)\mathrm{d}u.

Thus
\mathbf{k}_{2,1/4}^{\chi}(v,u)=\mathbf{k}_{2}^{\chi}(v,u)\lesssim\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{s_{0}}{8}|u-v|^{2}-\frac{s_{0}}{8}\frac{(|v|^{2}-|u|^{2})^{2}}{|v-u|^{2}}\right)}{|v-u|(1+|v|+|u|)^{1-\gamma}}
by Lemma
2.1
. Through the same procedure to estimate
\mathbf{k}_{2,\vartheta}^{\chi}(v,u)
, we have
\mathbf{k}_{2,\vartheta}^{\chi}(v,u)\lesssim\frac{\exp\left(-\rho|u-v|^{2}-\rho\frac{(|v|^{2}-|u|^{2})^{2}}{|v-u|^{2}}\right)}{|v-u|(1+|v|+|u|)^{1-\gamma}}
, where
\rho:\vartheta=\frac{s_{0}}{8}:\frac{1}{4}
. Hence

,
x_{b}(t,x,v)\rightarrow x
and
v_{b}(t,x,v)\rightarrow v
, so the second term on the right-hand side of (

) vanishes. Hence we obtain that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x,v}f(t,x,v)||_{\gamma_{-}}=

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x,v}[g(t-t_{b},x_{b},v_{b})]|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla_{t,x,v}g|\cdot\left|\frac{\partial(t-t_{b},x_{b},v_{b})}{\partial(x,v)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla_{t,x,v}g|\cdot\frac{1}{|n(x_{b})\cdot v_{b}|}\,\,\,(\mathrm{using\,\,Lemma}\,\ref{lemma 3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla_{t,x,v}g|\cdot\frac{1}{|n(x)\cdot v|}

By the definition of the kinetic weight
\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}
, we know that
\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}=|n(x)\cdot v|^{\beta}
on
(x,v)\in\gamma_{-}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f|_{p,-}^{p}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}^{p}|\partial g|^{p}|n(x)\cdot v|^{\beta p-p+1}\mathrm{d}|v_{\parallel}|\mathrm{d}v_{\perp}\mathrm{d}S_{x}\mathrm{d}s,

where
v_{\parallel}=\{v\cdot n(x)\}n(x)
,
v_{\perp}=v-v_{\parallel}
,
|v_{\parallel}|=|n(x)\cdot v|
and
\mathrm{d}v=\mathrm{d}|v_{\parallel}|\mathrm{d}v_{\perp}
. We notice that
\beta p-p+1>\frac{p-2}{p}p-p+1>-1
, so
|n(x)\cdot v|^{\beta p-p+1}=|v_{\parallel}|^{\beta p-p+1}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{1}_{|v_{\parallel}|}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{v_{\perp}})=L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f|_{p,-}^{p}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}\|w_{4\vartheta}\partial g\|^{p}_{\infty}.

In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f(t)\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f(t)\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f(0)\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f(0)\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}g\|^{p}_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\gamma_{-})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|w_{4\vartheta}\partial g\|^{p}_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|w_{\frac{4\vartheta}{s_{0}}}f\|_{\infty}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f\|_{p}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle+O(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla_{x,v}f\|^{p}_{\infty}.

For given
f_{0}
and
g
as the initial datum and the in-flow condition,
by using the condition (
3.3
), we make
O(\varepsilon)
term be absorbed into the first line on right-hand side above when
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. Eventually, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f(t)\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f(t)\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f(0)\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f(0)\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}g\|^{p}_{L^{p}((0,T)\times\gamma_{-})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|w_{4\vartheta}\partial g\|^{p}_{\infty}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|w_{\frac{4\vartheta}{s_{0}}}f\|_{\infty}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}f\|_{p}^{p}+\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\alpha^{\beta}_{f,\varepsilon}\partial f\|_{p}^{p}\right).

Using Gronwall’s inequality, we finally obtain the desired result and hence complete the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(f-g)(t)\|_{1+\delta}^{1+\delta}+\int_{0}^{t}|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(f-g)|_{1+\delta,+}^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(f-g)(0)\|_{1+\delta}^{1+\delta}+\int_{0}^{t}|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(f-g)|_{1+\delta,-}^{1+\delta}+\int_{0}^{t}|w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(f-g)|^{\delta}\cdot|\mathrm{RHS}\,\,\mathrm{of}\,\,\eqref{f-g}|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}s.

(1).
w_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(K[f-g]+\Gamma(f,f)-\Gamma(g,g)-v\cdot\nabla\phi_{f-g}\sqrt{\mu})

For the term

Note that for

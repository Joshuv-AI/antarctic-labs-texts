How did Euler solve the equation x ​ y ​ z ​ ( x + y + z ) = a xyz(x+y+z)=a ?
Seiji Tomita, Oliver Couto

Abstract
In this paper, we derived the parametric solution of Euler and Elkies in an elementary manner. In addition we proved there are infinitely many parametric solutions of Euler’s and Elkies’s family of solutions.



1. Introduction

According to Elkies
[
1
]
, in 1749 Euler takes a look at
xyz(x+y+z)=a
and says that he has found, with quite some effort.

His parametric solution is as follows.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{6ast^{3}(at^{4}-2s^{4})^{2}}{(4at^{4}+s^{4})(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\frac{s^{5}(4at^{4}+s^{4})^{2}}{t(at^{4}-2s^{4})(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\frac{(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}{s^{3}t(4at^{4}+s^{4})}

We don’t know how he got his solution.

In 2014, Elkies looked for a solution, and he got some solutions using algebraic geometry.

Simpler solution is as follows.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{(s^{4}-4a)^{2}}{2s^{3}(s^{4}+12a)}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{2a(3s^{4}+4a)^{2}}{s^{3}(s4-4a)(s^{4}+12a)}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s^{4}+12a)}{2(3s^{4}+4a)})

In 2022,inspired by the problem posted on MathStackExchange
[
2
]
, it is related to our problem,
so we decided to work on elementary derivation of Euler’s solution.

We proved that there are infinitely many parametric solutions of Euler’s family of solutions.

In addition we derived Elkies’s solution in an elementary manner and proved that there are infinitely many parametric solutions of Elkies’s family of solutions.

Moreover we showed the small positive solutions table for
a<100
.

Furthermore, parametric solution of
wxyz(w+x+y+z)\ =\ a
was shown in Appendix.

An infinitely many solutions are generated from infinite order point of elliptic curve using group law.
In this context, we call generated solutions are family of solutions of infinite order point.

2. Derive Euler’s solution

According to Elkies, Euler found a parametric solution of
xyz(x+y+z)=a
below.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{6ast^{3}(at^{4}-2s^{4})^{2}}{(4at^{4}+s^{4})(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\frac{s^{5}(4at^{4}+s^{4})^{2}}{t(at^{4}-2s^{4})(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\frac{(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}{s^{3}t(4at^{4}+s^{4})}

We prove that Euler’s family of solutions has an infinitely many parametric solutions where
a
is arbitrary.

Proof.

xyz(x+y+z)\ =\ a

We define Euler’s solution form as follows.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}as^{s}_{1}t^{t}_{1}A^{2}}{BC}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}s^{s}_{2}t^{t}_{2}B^{2}}{AC}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{3}s^{s}_{3}t^{t}_{3}C}{B}

Taking
(s1,s2,s3)=(1,5,-3),\ (t1,t2,t3)=(3,-1,-1),\ (c1,c2,c3)=(6,\frac{3}{2},\frac{2}{3})
, then

\displaystyle x\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{6Ast^{3}A^{2}}{BC}

\displaystyle y\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{3}{2}\frac{s^{5}B^{2}}{tAC}

\displaystyle z\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{2}{3}\frac{C}{s^{3}tB}

Hence

xyz(x+y+z)\ =\ \frac{a(36As^{4}t^{4}A^{3}+9s^{8}B^{3}+4C^{2}A)}{C^{2}B}

RHS of above equation must be a,then

36As^{4}t^{4}A^{3}+9s^{8}B^{3}+4C^{2}A=C^{2}B

Since
C
must be rational number then discriminant must be square number.

Let
U=\frac{A}{B}
we get quartic curve

V^{2}=-16at^{4}U^{4}+4at^{4}U^{3}-4s^{4}U+s^{4}

Quartic is birationally equivalent to an elliptic curve.

E:Y^{2}-4s^{2}YX+8s^{2}at^{4}Y=X^{3}-4s^{4}X^{2}+64at^{4}s^{4}X-256at^{4}s^{8}

E has a point
P(X,Y)=(4s^{4},\ 16s^{6}-8s^{2}at^{4})
.

Hence we can obtain a point
2Q(U)=\cfrac{at^{4}-2s^{4}}{4at^{4}+s^{4}}
from
2P(X,Y)
using group law.

Then we obtain

(A,B,C)=(at^{4}-2s^{4},4at^{4}+s^{4},-s^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}+2a^{2}t^{8})

Finally, we obtain an Euler’s solution.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{6ast^{3}(at^{4}-2s^{4})^{2}}{(4at^{4}+s^{4})(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\frac{s^{5}(4at^{4}+s^{4})^{2}}{t(at^{4}-2s^{4})(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\frac{(2a^{2}t^{8}+10as^{4}t^{4}-s^{8})}{s^{3}t(4at^{4}+s^{4})}

Similarly, we can obtain other new solutions using group law with
P(X,Y)=(4s^{4},16s^{6}-8s^{2}at^{4})
.

Hence Euler’s family of solutions has an infinitely many parametric solutions.

For instance, we can obtain a new solution using
3P(X,Y)
as follows.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{18as^{5}t^{3}(-2s^{4}+at^{4})^{2}(-s^{8}+10at^{4}s^{4}+2a^{2}t^{8})^{2}(a^{2}t^{8}-5s^{8}+14at^{4}s^{4})}{(at^{4}+s^{4})(a^{3}t^{12}+3s^{4}a^{2}t^{8}+111at^{4}s^{8}+s^{12})(a^{6}t^{24}+6s^{4}a^{5}t^{20}-255s^{8}a^{4}t^{16}-790a^{3}t^{12}s^{12}-2253a^{2}t^{8}s^{16}-264s^{20}at^{4}+s^{24})}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\frac{-(at^{4}+s^{4})^{2}(a^{3}t^{12}+3s^{4}a^{2}t^{8}+111at^{4}s^{8}+s^{12})^{2}(a^{2}t^{8}-5s^{8}+14at^{4}s^{4})}{s^{3}t(-2s^{4}+at^{4})(-s^{8}+10at^{4}s^{4}+2a^{2}t^{8})(a^{6}t^{24}+6s^{4}a^{5}t^{20}-255s^{8}a^{4}t^{16}-790a^{3}t^{12}s^{12}-2253a^{2}t^{8}s^{16}-264s^{20}at^{4}+s^{24})}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{2s(a^{6}t^{24}+6s^{4}a^{5}t^{20}-255s^{8}a^{4}t^{16}-790a^{3}t^{12}s^{12}-2253a^{2}t^{8}s^{16}-264s^{20}at^{4}+s^{24})}{(a^{2}t^{8}-5s^{8}+14at^{4}s^{4})t(at^{4}+s^{4})(a^{3}t^{12}+3s^{4}a^{2}t^{8}+111at^{4}s^{8}+s^{12})}

The proof is completed.

3. Derive Elkies’s solution

According to Elkies, Elkies found a parametric solution of
xyz(x+y+z)=a
below.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(s^{4}-4a)^{2}}{s^{3}(s^{4}+12a)}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{2a(3s^{4}+4a)^{2}}{s^{3}(s^{4}-4a)(s^{4}+12a)}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{s(s^{4}+12a)}{3s^{4}+4a}

We show Elkies’s family of solutions has an infinitely many parametric solutions where
a
is arbitrary.

Proof.

xyz(x+y+z)\ =\ a

Taking

\displaystyle x\

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{A^{2}}{s^{3}C}

\displaystyle y\

\displaystyle=\frac{2aB^{2}}{s^{3}AC}

\displaystyle z\

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{sC}{B}

Hence

xyz(x+y+z)\ =\ \frac{1}{4}\frac{a(A^{3}B+4aB^{3}+s^{4}C^{2}A)}{s^{8}C^{2}}

RHS of above equation must be
a
,then

A^{3}B+4aB^{3}+s^{4}C^{2}A-4s^{8}C^{2}=0

(1)

Since
C
must be rational number then discriminant must be square number.

v^{2}=-4(A-4s^{4})s^{4}B(A^{3}+4aB^{2})

Let
B=-A+4s^{4}
then

\displaystyle V^{2}

\displaystyle=A^{3}+4aB^{2}

\displaystyle=A^{3}+4aA^{2}-32s^{4}aA+64s^{8}a

(2)

In order to find a parametrization for
A,V
, substitute
(A,V)=(s^{4}+p,s^{6}+qs^{4}+rs^{2})
to (2).

We obtain
(p,q,r)=(-4a,0,12a)
then
(A,B,C)=(s^{4}-4a,3s^{4}+4a,s^{4}+12a)
.

Finally, we obtain Elkies’s solution.

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(s^{4}-4a)^{2}}{s^{3}(s^{4}+12a)}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{2a(3s^{4}+4a)^{2}}{s^{3}(s^{4}-4a)(s^{4}+12a)}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{s(s^{4}+12a)}{3s^{4}+4a}

From (2), we define elliptic curve E

E:V^{2}=A^{3}+4aA^{2}-32s^{4}aA+64as^{8}

Since E has a point
P(A,V)=(s^{4}-4a,s^{6}+12as^{2})
,

we can obtain a point
2P(A)=\cfrac{1}{4}\cfrac{s^{16}-464s^{12}a+1632s^{8}a^{2}+768a^{3}s^{4}+256a^{4}}{s^{4}(s^{4}+12a)^{2}}
.

According to Nagell-Lutz theorem, the point
P(A,V)
is not a point of finite order.

Hence Elkies’s family of solutions has an infinitely many parametric solutions.

We can obtain new solutions using group law with
P(A,V)=(s^{4}-4a,s^{6}+12as^{2})
.

For instance, we can obtain a new solution using
2P(A,V)
as follows.

We obtain

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{(-4a+s^{4})(s^{12}-460s^{8}a-208a^{2}s^{4}-64a^{3})}{s^{4}(s^{4}+12a)^{2}}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\frac{(-4a+5s^{4})(4a+3s^{4})(s^{8}+56s^{4}a+16a^{2})}{s^{4}(s^{4}+12a)^{2}}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{8}\frac{(-16a^{2}-32s^{4}a+s^{8})(s^{16}+1136s^{12}a-928s^{8}a^{2}+1792a^{3}s^{4}+256a^{4})}{s^{8}(s^{4}+12a)^{3}}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{4}\frac{(-4a+s^{4})^{2}(s^{12}-460s^{8}a-208a^{2}s^{4}-64a^{3})^{2}}{s^{3}(s^{4}+12a)(-16a^{2}-32s^{4}a+s^{8})(s^{16}+1136s^{12}a-928s^{8}a^{2}+1792a^{3}s^{4}+256a^{4})}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{-4(s^{4}+12a)s(-4a+5s^{4})^{2}(4a+3s^{4})^{2}(s^{8}+56s^{4}a+16a^{2})^{2}a}{(s^{16}+1136s^{12}a-928s^{8}a^{2}+1792a^{3}s^{4}+256a^{4})(-16a^{2}-32s^{4}a+s^{8})(-4a+s^{4})(s^{12}-460s^{8}a-208a^{2}s^{4}-64a^{3})}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{4}\frac{(-16a^{2}-32s^{4}a+s^{8})(s^{16}+1136s^{12}a-928s^{8}a^{2}+1792a^{3}s^{4}+256a^{4})}{s^{3}(-4a+5s^{4})(4a+3s^{4})(s^{8}+56s^{4}a+16a^{2})(s^{4}+12a)}

The proof is completed.

5. Final remarks

We used the Euler’s solution form, combination of
(A,B,C)
to derive Euler’s parametric solution.

So, we think there must be parametric solutions other than Euler’s form.

The same could be said of Elkies case in a similar way.
Furthermore, it might be interesting to construct the parametric solutions using
(A,B,C,D)
.

Appendix

We consider the extension of
xyz(x+y+z)=a
and show
wxyz(w+x+y+z)=a
has infinitely many parametric solutions where
a
is arbitrary.
The problem
wxyz(w+x+y+z)=1
was posted on mathoverflow.net
[
3
]
.

An equation wxyz(w+x+y+z) = a

has an infinitely many parametric solutions where
a
is arbitrary.

Proof.

wxyz(w+x+y+z)=a

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\frac{ac_{1}t^{t_{1}}}{ABC}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}Bt^{t_{2}}}{A}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{3}At^{t_{3}}}{C}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{4}Ct^{t_{4}}}{B}

Hence

wxyz(w+x+y+z)=\frac{c_{1}t^{t_{1}}c_{2}t^{t_{2}}c_{3}t^{t_{3}}c_{4}t^{t_{4}}(ac_{1}t^{t_{1}}+c_{2}B^{2}t^{t_{2}}C+c_{3}A^{2}t^{t_{3}}B+c_{4}C^{2}t^{t_{4}}A)}{A^{2}B^{2}C^{2}}

RHS of above equation must be a,then

\displaystyle(c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}^{2}t^{t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}+2t_{4}}A-A^{2}B^{2})C^{2}

\displaystyle+c_{1}c_{2}^{2}c_{3}c_{4}t^{t_{4}+t_{3}+t_{1}+2t_{2}}B^{2}C

\displaystyle+ac_{1}^{2}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}t^{t_{4}+t_{3}+t_{2}+2t_{1}}+c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}^{2}c_{4}t^{t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{4}+2t_{3}}A^{2}B=0

Since
C
must be rational number then discriminant must be square number.

\displaystyle v^{2}

\displaystyle=4c_{1}c_{2}c_{3}^{2}c_{4}t^{t_{4}+t_{2}+t_{1}+2t_{3}}B^{3}A^{4}

\displaystyle-4c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{2}c_{3}^{3}c_{4}^{3}Bt^{3t_{4}+3t_{3}+2t_{2}+2t_{1}}A^{3}

\displaystyle+a4c_{1}^{2}c_{2}c_{3}c_{4}t^{t_{4}+t_{3}+t_{2}+2t_{1}}B^{2}A^{2}

\displaystyle-a4c_{1}^{3}c_{2}^{2}c_{3}^{2}c_{4}^{3}t^{3t_{4}+2t_{3}+2t_{2}+3t_{1}}A

\displaystyle+c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{4}c_{3}^{2}c_{4}^{2}B^{4}t^{2t_{4}+2t_{3}+4t_{2}+2t_{1}}

Obviously, quartic is birationally equivalent to an elliptic curve.

For instance, take
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})=(1,1,1),(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=(1,1,1),B=1
, then

v^{2}=4t^{5}A^{4}-4t^{10}A^{3}+4t^{5}aA^{2}-4t^{10}aA+t^{10}

Quartic is transformed to an elliptic curve below.

E:Y^{2}-4t^{5}aYX-8t^{15}Y=X^{3}+(4t^{5}a-4t^{10}a^{2})X^{2}-16t^{15}X-64t^{20}a+64t^{25}a^{2}

E
has a point
P(X,Y)=(-4t^{5}a+4t^{10}a^{2},\ -16t^{10}a^{2}+16t^{15}a^{3}+8t^{15})
.

We obtain

2P(X)=\frac{4((-a^{2}-2a^{5}+a^{8})t^{20}+(-2a^{4}-a-4a^{7})t^{15}+(1+6a^{6}+6a^{3})t^{10}+(-2a^{2}-4a^{5})t^{5}+a^{4})}{((2a^{3}+1)t^{5}-2a^{2})^{2}}

According to Nagell-Lutz theorem, the point
P(X,Y)
is not a point of finite order, hence we can obtain infinitely many parametric solutions.

Thus we can obtain a quartic point

2Q(A)=\frac{-t^{5}(-2a^{2}+2t^{5}a^{3}+t^{5})}{t^{10}a^{4}-2t^{5}a^{3}-t^{5}+a^{2}}

Then we obtain
(A,C)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{-t^{5}(-2a^{2}+2t^{5}a^{3}+t^{5})}{t^{10}a^{4}-2t^{5}a^{3}-t^{5}+a^{2}}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{10}+a-2t^{5}a^{2}+t^{10}a^{3}}{t^{10}a^{2}-1}

Finally we obtain
(w,x,y,z)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\frac{-a(t^{10}a^{4}-2t^{5}a^{3}-t^{5}+a^{2})(t^{5}a+1)(t^{5}a-1)}{t^{4}(-2a^{2}+2t^{5}a^{3}+t^{5})(t^{10}+a-2t^{5}a^{2}+t^{10}a^{3})}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{-(t^{10}a^{4}-2t^{5}a^{3}-t^{5}+a^{2})}{t^{4}(-2a^{2}+2t^{5}a^{3}+t^{5})}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{-t^{6}(-2a^{2}+2t^{5}a^{3}+t^{5})(t^{5}a+1)(t^{5}a-1)}{(t^{10}a^{4}-2t^{5}a^{3}-t^{5}+a^{2})(t^{10}+a-2t^{5}a^{2}+t^{10}a^{3})}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{(t^{10}+a-2t^{5}a^{2}+t^{10}a^{3})t}{(t^{5}a+1)(t^{5}a-1)}

In this way we can obtain infinitely many parametric solutions.

The proof is completed.

Detectable Electric Current induced by Dark Matter Axion in a Conductor
Aiichi Iwazaki

Abstract
We propose a way of detecting dark matter axion by using two slabs of conductor. The flat surfaces are put to meet face to face so that they are parallel to each other. External magnetic field B B parallel to the surfaces is impressed. Radiations converted from the axion arise between two slabs. When we tune the spacing l l between two surfaces such as l = π / m a l=\pi/m_{a} with axion mass m a m_{a} , a resonance occurs so that the radiations become strong. Furthermore, electric current flowing on the surface of the slabs is enhanced. We show that the electric current is large enough to be detectable at the resonance. l=\pi(2n+1)/m_{a}
with non negative integer
n
. )
The resonance implies that electric and magnetic field of radiation is enhanced compared with the radiation in eq(
20
).
With the choice of
l=\pi/m_{a}
, we obtain
\delta=\pi/2
so that
A=0,\quad E_{1}=E_{a},\quad b_{1,2}=-\frac{E_{a}}{m_{a}\delta_{e}}
.
Therefore, the radiation fields
E_{z}(\mbox{outside})
and
B_{y}(\mbox{outside})
are enhanced
with the enhancement factor
1/m_{a}\delta_{e}
,

\displaystyle E_{z}(\mbox{outside})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}E_{a}}{m_{a}\delta_{e}}\sin(m_{a}x)\sin(m_{a}t-\frac{\pi}{4})

(24)

\displaystyle B_{y}(\mbox{outside})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{2}E_{a}}{m_{a}\delta_{e}}\cos(m_{a}x)\sin(m_{a}t+\frac{\pi}{4}).

(25)

We also have electric field inside the conductor
x>0

E_{z}(\mbox{inside})=E_{a}\exp(-\frac{x}{\delta_{e}})\cos(m_{a}t-\frac{x}{\delta_{e}}),

(26)

which induces large oscillating electric current
\sigma E_{a}\exp(-\frac{x}{\delta_{e}})\cos(m_{a}t-\frac{x}{\delta_{e}})

in the surface.

We estimate the electric current
I_{a}(m_{a}t)=L\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\sigma E_{a}\exp(-x/\delta_{e})\cos(m_{a}t-x/\delta_{e})=L\sigma\delta_{e}E_{a}\sin(m_{a}t+\pi/4)/\sqrt{2}\equiv I_{a}\sin(m_{a}t+\pi/4)
with the length
L

of the slab in
y
direction,

I_{a}=\frac{\sigma\delta_{e}E_{a}L}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\sigma\delta_{e}g_{a\gamma\gamma}a_{0}BL}{\sqrt{2}}=0.7\times 10^{-9}\mbox{A}\Big(\frac{10^{-5}\rm eV}{m_{a}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{B}{5\rm T}\Big)\Big(\frac{L}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sigma}{3.3\times 10^{7}\rm eV}\Big)

(27)

where we have adopted high quality
6
N copper with electrical conductivity
\sigma\simeq 3.3\times 10^{7}
eV at low temperature of the order of
1
K.
We have used the parameters of the dark matter axion energy density
\rho_{a}=0.3\mbox{GeV}\,\mbox{cm}^{-3}=m_{a}^{2}a_{0}^{2}/2

and the axion model
g_{\gamma}=1
(
g_{a\gamma\gamma}=g_{\gamma}\alpha/\pi f_{a}
). These values are used
throughout the paper.

The power of Joule heating by the electric current
I_{a}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle LR\sigma E_{a}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\exp(-\frac{2x}{\delta_{e}})\,\overline{\cos^{2}(m_{a}t-x/\delta_{e})}=\frac{LR\delta_{e}\sigma E_{a}^{2}}{4}

(28)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 0.3\times 10^{-22}\mbox{W}\Big(\frac{10^{-5}\rm eV}{m_{a}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{B}{5\rm T}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{3.3\times 10^{7}\rm eV}\Big)\Big(\frac{L}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{R}{10\rm cm}\Big)

where
R
denotes a side length of the slab ( the surface area is
R\times L
).

LR\delta_{e}
is the volume of the conductor in which the electric current flows.

\overline{O}
is time average of
O
.
In order to detect the electric current, we amplify the power
P_{a}
using LC circuit
sikivie
;
kishimoto
.

Because thermal noise of the system with temperature
T=1
K
is given by

P_{T}=\frac{T\delta\omega}{2\pi}\simeq 3.3\times 10^{-20}\mbox{W}\Big(\frac{T}{1\rm K}\Big)\Big(\frac{\delta\omega}{10^{-6}m_{a}}\Big)\Big(\frac{m_{a}}{10^{-5}\rm eV}\Big),

(29)

the signal to noise ratio is given by

\displaystyle\frac{S}{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{a}}{P_{T}}Q_{LC}\sqrt{\frac{\delta\omega t_{obs}}{2\pi}}

(30)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 4.5\times 10^{-2}Q_{LC}\sqrt{\frac{t_{obs}}{1\rm sec}}\Big(\frac{10^{-5}\rm eV}{m_{a}}\Big)\Big(\frac{B}{5\rm T}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{3.3\times 10^{7}\rm eV}\Big)\Big(\frac{L}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{R}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{1\rm K}{T}\Big)\Big(\frac{10^{-6}m_{a}}{\delta\omega}\Big)^{1/2}

where
Q_{LC}
denotes
Q
factor in LC circuit which is used to amplify the power
P_{a}
.

t_{obs}
is the observation time.

Q_{LC}
is given such that
Q_{LC}=\frac{1}{R_{LC}}\sqrt{L_{i}/C}
with inductance
L_{i}
of coil, capacitance
C
of condenser
and resistance
R_{LC}
of the circuit. Especially,
R_{LC}=R/(\sigma L\delta_{e})
. Thus, we find

Q_{LC}=\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma}{m_{a}}}\times\sqrt{\frac{L_{i}}{C}}\Big(\frac{L}{R}\Big)\simeq 2.5\times 10^{6}\sqrt{\frac{10^{-5}\mbox{eV}}{m_{a}}}\sqrt{\frac{L_{i}}{C}}\Big(\frac{L}{R}\Big).

(31)

Therefore, we obtain sufficiently large
S/N
ratio to detect the electric current induced by the dark matter axion,
even if the observational time
t_{obs}
is
1
second.

We would like to point out that
Q
value associated with the absorption by the flat conductors is
derived using the definition
Q=m_{a}U/2P_{a}
with the energy
U
of radiations in the space between two slabs,

U\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int(\overline{E_{z}^{2}(\mbox{outside})}+\overline{B_{y}^{2}(\mbox{ouside})})dV=\frac{LRE_{a}^{2}l}{2(m_{a}\delta_{e})^{2}}

(32)

with
l=\pi/m_{a}
.
We find that

Q=\frac{m_{a}l}{\delta_{e}\sigma(m_{a}\delta_{e})^{2}}=\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma}{m_{a}}}\simeq 2\times 10^{6}\sqrt{\frac{10^{-5}\mbox{eV}}{m_{a}}}

(33)

The formula is almost identical to the
Q_{LC}
factor in the LC circuit.

In order to obtain the high value of the electric current
I_{a}
, we need to exactly tune the spacing
l=\pi/m_{a}
between two slabs.
But, such an exact tuning would be not possible because
\delta_{e}=\sqrt{2/m_{a}\sigma}\simeq 1.6\times 10^{-6}\mbox{cm}\sqrt{10^{-5}\mbox{eV}/m_{a}}
.
In actual experiment we tune it approximately. Thus,
we need to see electric current and its power in the approximate tuning.

We note that in general, electric current
I_{a}(l)
flowing the slab is given by

\displaystyle I_{a}(l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L\sigma E_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\exp(-\frac{x}{\delta_{e}})\Big(\cos(m_{a}t-x/\delta_{e})+A\sin(m_{a}t-x/\delta_{e})\Big)

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L\sigma\delta_{e}E_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big(\sin(m_{a}t+\frac{\pi}{4})+A\sin(m_{a}t-\frac{\pi}{4})\Big),

and the power of Joule heating
P_{a}(l)
is

\displaystyle P_{a}(l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle LR\sigma E_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\exp(-\frac{2x}{\delta_{e}})\overline{\Big(\cos(m_{a}t-x/\delta_{e})+A\sin(m_{a}t-x/\delta_{e})\Big)^{2}}

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{LR\delta_{e}\sigma(E_{1}^{2}+(E_{1}A)^{2})}{4},

where the parameters
E_{1}
and
A
depend on the spacing
l
between two slabs.

When we take the spacing

l=\pi/m_{a}+\delta l
with
\delta l\ll\pi/m_{a}
( then,
\tan\delta\simeq-2/(m_{a}\delta l)
), the parameters
E_{1}
and
E_{1}A
are found such as

E_{1}=-\frac{E_{a}(y+1)}{2y^{2}+2y+1},\quad E_{1}A=\frac{E_{a}y}{2y^{2}+2y+1}\quad\mbox{with}\quad y\equiv\delta l/2\delta_{e}.

(36)

The tuning
y\ll 1
would be very difficult because

\delta_{e}\simeq 1.6\times 10^{-6}\mbox{cm}\sqrt{10^{-5}\mbox{eV}/m_{a}}
.
But even if we tune
y
such as
y\sim 10^{2}
, i.e.
\delta l\sim 10^{-4}
cm,
we obtain electric current
I_{a}(l=\pi/m_{a}+\delta l)=-I_{a}(\delta l)\cos(m_{a}t)
,

I_{a}(\delta l)=\frac{\sigma\delta_{e}E_{a}L}{2y}\simeq 1.4\times 10^{-11}\mbox{A}\Big(\frac{10^{-4}\rm cm}{\delta l}\Big)\Big(\frac{10^{-5}\rm eV}{m_{a}}\Big)\Big(\frac{B}{5\rm T}\Big)\Big(\frac{L}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sigma}{3.3\times 10^{7}\rm eV}\Big),

(37)

and the corresponding power
P_{a}(\delta l)\equiv P_{a}(l=\pi/m_{a}+\delta l)
,

P_{a}(\delta l)=\frac{LR\delta_{e}\sigma E_{a}^{2}}{8y^{2}}\simeq 1.5\times 10^{-26}\mbox{W}\Big(\frac{10^{-4}\rm cm}{\delta l}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{10^{-5}\rm eV}{m_{a}}\Big)^{3/2}\Big(\frac{B}{5\rm T}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{3.3\times 10^{7}\rm eV}\Big).\Big(\frac{L}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{R}{10\rm cm}\Big)

(38)

Then, we find the signal to noise ratio
S(\delta l)/N

\displaystyle\frac{S(\delta l)}{N}=\frac{P_{a}(\delta l)}{P_{T}}Q_{LC}\sqrt{\frac{\delta\omega t_{obs}}{2\pi}}

(39)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 2.3\times 10^{-5}Q_{LC}\sqrt{\frac{t_{obs}}{1\rm sec}}\Big(\frac{10^{-4}\rm cm}{\delta l}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{10^{-5}\rm eV}{m_{a}}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{B}{5\rm T}\Big)^{2}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{3.3\times 10^{7}\rm eV}\Big)\Big(\frac{L}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{R}{10\rm cm}\Big)\Big(\frac{1\rm K}{T}\Big)\Big(\frac{10^{-6}m_{a}}{\delta\omega}\Big)^{1/2}

where
Q_{LC}=\frac{1}{R_{LC}}\sqrt{L_{i}/C}\sim 10^{6}\sqrt{L_{i}/C}\sqrt{10^{-5}\mbox{eV}/m_{a}}
with the use of
L=10
cm and
R=10
cm.

In the actual experiment with the use of
Q_{LC}=10^{6}
, we can search the spacing
\Delta l\sim 6
cm within
1
day,
if we tune the spacing with the speed
\partial_{t}\delta l\sim 10^{-4}\rm cm/s
(
t_{obs}=1
sec for
S/N>1
),
It covers the range of the QCD axion mass
m_{a}\sim 10^{-5}\mbox{eV}
because
\pi/m_{a}\sim 6
cm.
On the other hand, in order to cover the range of the axion mass
\sim 10^{-4}
eV (
\pi/m_{a}\sim 6\times 10^{-1}
cm ),
we can search it for
10
days if we tune the spacing with the speed
\partial_{t}\delta l\sim 10^{-4}\rm cm/10^{2}s
(
t_{obs}=10^{2}
sec for
S/N>1
).
But, to cover the range of
m_{a}=10^{-3}
eV, we need
t_{obs}=10^{6}
sec for
S/N>1
so that it takes more than
10
years to
search the spacing
\Delta l\sim 0.06
cm.
However, if we can tune the spacing
\delta l=10^{-5}
cm, by taking
\partial_{t}\delta l\sim 10^{-5}\rm cm/10^{2}s

(
t_{obs}=10^{2}
sec for
S/N>1

\Delta l\sim 0.06
cm within

10
day even for

eV.

On the other hand, when we search the mass
\sim 3\times 10^{-6}
eV (

cm

In this way, it turns out that the new way of the axion detection is very effective

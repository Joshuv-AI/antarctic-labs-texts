An almost complete t t -intersection theorem for permutations


Abstract
. For any ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 and n > ( 1 + ϵ ) ​ t n>(1+\epsilon)t , n > n 0 ​ ( ϵ ) n>n_{0}(\epsilon) we determine the size of the largest t t -intersecting family of permutations, as well as give a sharp stability result. 2.1.
Sketch of the proof.

The rough structure of the proof is described below. We introduce and use a refinement of the spread approximation technique. Given the previous results, say, from
[
32
]
, we may for simplicity assume that
t\geqslant n^{1-\epsilon/2}
for
\epsilon=0.01\eta
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the largest
t
-intersecting family of permutations.

First, we find a family
\mathcal{S}
of partial permutations of size somewhat smaller than
n
(a concrete dependence is crucial here), such that it covers most of the extremal family
\mathcal{F}
. At the same time, the family
\mathcal{S}
is
t^{\prime}
-intersecting for some
t^{\prime}
that is a bit smaller than
t
:
t^{\prime}=t-n^{1-\epsilon/2}
. In this part, we trade
t
for
t^{\prime}
in order to guarantee that the sets in
\mathcal{S}
have a relatively small uniformity.

Second, we use the ‘‘peeling’’ technique to analyze the structure of
\mathcal{S}
. In this part, we use the condition on the uniformity of
\mathcal{S}
in order to guarantee that the main contribution to the size of the family comes from partial permutations from
\mathcal{S}
of size at most
t+k
, where
k=n^{1-\epsilon}
. This, in turn, allows to get a subset
X
of size
t^{\prime}
, such that
\mathcal{F}(X)
is rather dense in
\Sigma_{n}(X)
(much denser than the original family is in
\Sigma_{n}
).

Third, we employ a greedy procedure that at each step uses the set
X
as above, and manage to construct a much better spread approximation
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
of the family. In particular,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
only contains sets of size
t+O(n^{1-\epsilon})
and is
t
-intersecting.

The last step is to apply and analyze the peeling procedure for the refined approximation
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Within the procedure, we detect the most ‘important’ layer and then show that this layer must be the only layer present in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and thus in
\mathcal{F}
, assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is extremal. After some analysis, this allows us to discern the structure isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{k}
for some
k
.

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{F}_{A_{i}}(A_{i})|.

This implies that
|\mathcal{G}_{i}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|\mathcal{F}_{A_{i}}(A_{i})|
.

\mathcal{G}_{i}
is
\frac{r}{2}
-spread for both

. Since

\frac{r}{2}>2^{11}\log_{2}(2n)

What follows is an application of Theorem
4

\beta=\log_{2}(2n)

\delta=(2\log_{2}(2n))^{-1}

\frac{r}{2}\delta>2^{10}

implies that a
\frac{1}{2}
-random subset

2.4.
The peeling procedure and the structure of spread approximations for large
t
-intersecting families

In this section, we analyze the ‘peeling’ procedure that captures the structure of the
t
-intersecting families of partial permutations. One of the key objectives is to control the number of permutations coming from higher uniformity partial permutations.

We say that a
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{T}
is
maximal
if for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
and any proper subset
X\subsetneq T
there exist
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
such that
|T\cap T^{\prime}|<t
and, moreover,
T_{1}\not\subset T_{2}
for
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{T}
is an
antichain
).

Observation 8
.

For any positive integers
n,p
and a
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{S}\subset\Sigma^{\leqslant q}_{n}
, there exists a maximal
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{T}
such that for every
T\in\mathcal{T}
there is
S\in\mathcal{S}
such that
T\subseteq S
. Consequently, for any family
\mathcal{F}\subset\Sigma_{n}
we have
\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{S}]\subset\mathcal{F}[\mathcal{T}]
.

The easiest way to see it is to construct
\mathcal{T}
by repeatedly replacing sets in
\mathcal{S}
by their proper subsets while preserving the
t
-intersecting property.

Consider a
t
-intersecting family
\mathcal{S}\subset\Sigma^{\leqslant q}_{n}
. Let us iteratively define the following series of families.

(1)

Put
\mathcal{T}_{q-t}=\mathcal{S}
.

(2)

For
k=q-t,q-t-1,\ldots,0
we put
\mathcal{W}_{k}=\mathcal{T}_{k}\cap{[n]\choose t+k}
and let
\mathcal{T}_{k-1}
be the family given by Observation
8
when applied to
\mathcal{T}_{k}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{k}
playing the role of
\mathcal{S}
.

Thus, we ‘peel’ the sets of the largest uniformity in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
and replace the resulting family by a maximal
t
-intersecting family using Observation
8
. Remark that
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is
t
-intersecting for each
k=q-t,q-t-1\ldots,0
by definition. We summarize the basic properties of these series of families in the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

The following properties hold for each
k=q-t,q-t-1\ldots,0
.

(i)

All sets in
\mathcal{T}_{k}
have size at most
t+k
.

(ii)

For
k\leqslant q-t-1
we have
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}_{k+1}]\subset\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}_{k}]\cup\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{W}_{k+1}]
.

(iii)

There is no set
X
of size
\leqslant t+k-1
such that
\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)
(or any subfamily of
\mathcal{T}_{k}(X)
) is
\alpha
-spread for
\alpha>k
.

Proof.

(i) This easily follows by reverse induction on
k
. The base case is that all sets in
\mathcal{S}
have size at most
q
, and the induction step is via the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{k-1}
.

(ii) We have
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}_{k+1}]=\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}_{k+1}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{k+1}]\cup\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{W}_{k+1}]
and, by the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, we have
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}_{k}]\supset\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{T}_{k+1}\setminus\mathcal{W}_{k+1}]
.

(iii) Assume that there is such a set
X
. By the maximality of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
, there must be a set
F\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
such that
|X\cap F|=z<t
.
At the same time, the family
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is
t
-intersecting. So, for each set
G\in\mathcal{W}_{k}[X]
we have
|G\cap F|\geqslant t
, and thus
|F\cap(G\setminus X)|\geqslant t-z
for each such
G
. Thus,
\mathcal{W}_{k}[X]\subset\cup_{S\in{F\choose t-z}}\mathcal{W}_{k}[X\cup S]
and so, using the
\alpha
-spreadness of
\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)
, we get

\displaystyle|\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)|\leqslant

\displaystyle\ {|F|-z\choose t-z}\max_{F^{\prime}\in{F\choose t-z}}|\mathcal{W}_{k}(X\cup F^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ {|F|-z\choose t-z}\alpha^{-(t-z)}|\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \prod_{i=1}^{t-z}\frac{|F|-t+i}{i}\alpha^{-(t-z)}|\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ (|F|-t+1)^{t-z}\alpha^{-(t-z)}|\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)|<|\mathcal{W}_{k}(X)|,

where the last inequality is due to
|F|-t+1\leqslant k<\alpha
. This a contradiction.
∎

Our next goal is to bound the size of
\mathcal{W}_{k}
.
Consider two sets
A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{k}
such that
A\cap B=I
,
|I|=t
. (There are such sets by maximality of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.) Recall that the sizes of sets of
A,B
are at most
t+k
. Any set
C\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
intersects both
A
and
B
in at least
t
elements. Denote
C_{0}=C\cap I
,
C_{1}=C\cap(A\setminus I)
and
C_{2}=C\cap(B\setminus I)
. Then, putting
|C_{0}|=t-j
, we must have
|C_{1}|,|C_{2}|\geqslant j
. Select
C_{1}^{\prime}\subset C_{1},C_{2}^{\prime}\subset C_{2}
such that
|C_{1}^{\prime}|=|C_{2}^{\prime}|=j
. The family
\mathcal{W}_{k}(C_{0}\cup C_{1}^{\prime}\cup C_{2}^{\prime}\cup X)
by Lemma
9
(iii) is not
\alpha
-spread subfamily for
\alpha>k
and any
X
, and thus
|\mathcal{W}_{i}(C_{0}\cup C_{1}^{\prime}\cup C_{2}^{\prime})|\leqslant k^{k-j}
by Observation
2
. From here, we see that we can upper bound the number of sets in
\mathcal{W}_{k}
as follows:

|\mathcal{W}_{k}|\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{k}{t\choose t-j}{|A|-t\choose j}{|B|-t\choose j}k^{k-j}\leqslant\sum_{j=0}^{k}f(j),

where

(4)

f(j):={t\choose t-j}{k\choose j}^{2}k^{k-j}.

Note that

\frac{f(j)}{f(j+1)}=\frac{j+1}{t-j}\Big(\frac{j+1}{k-j}\Big)^{2}k=\frac{(j+1)^{3}k}{(t-j)(k-j)^{2}}.

The observation below is based on simple calculations that check whether the last ratio above is bigger than

or not.

Observation 10
.

For sufficiently large
t
,
t\geqslant k
, we have the following. Denote by

the value at which

attains its maximum.

(1)

For

such that

j_{0}=(1+o(1))(tk)^{1/3}
.

(2)

For

.

(3)

For

.

(4)

For

, we have

.

Moreover,
|\mathcal{W}_{i}|\leqslant(k+1)f(j_{0})
in general and
|\mathcal{W}_{i}|\leqslant(1+o(1))f(k)=(1+o(1)){t\choose t-k}
for

.

We will need the following concrete statement concerning the size of
\Sigma_{n}[\mathcal{W}_{k}].

Lemma 11
.

For any
\epsilon>0
,
n\geqslant(1+100\epsilon)t
,
t>t_{0}(\epsilon)
and
t^{0.01}\leqslant k\leqslant 2\epsilon t
we have

(5)

\frac{|\mathcal{W}_{k}|(n-t-k)!}{(n-t)!}\leqslant 2^{-k}.

Proof.

Using (

) and the inequality

, we get

(6)

f(j)\leqslant\Big(\frac{e^{3}tk^{2}}{j^{3}}\Big)^{j}k^{k-j}.

Let us first consider the case when
k\geqslant t^{0.99}
. Then we can apply Observation
10
(1) and get that the fraction in (
6
) for
f(j_{0})
is simplified to
((1+o(1))e^{3}k)^{j_{0}}
. We thus get

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{W}_{k}|(n-t-k)!}{(n-t)!}\leqslant

\displaystyle(k+1)\frac{f(j_{0})(n-t-k)!}{(n-t)!}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{((1+o(1))e^{3}k)^{j_{0}}k^{k-j_{0}+1}}{(n-t-k)^{k}}\leqslant\frac{((1+o(1))e^{3}k)^{k}}{(n-t-k)^{k}}\leqslant\ 2^{-k},

where the last inequality is due to
n-t-k\geqslant 98\epsilon t\geqslant 49k>2.1e^{3}k
.

Next, assume that

. In this regime, according to Observation

, we have
tk^{2}/j_{0}^{3}\leqslant(1+o(1))t^{0.99}
, and thus we can conclude that (
6
) implies the following inequality.

f(j)\leqslant(Ct^{0.99})^{k},

where

is some absolute constant. In this regime, we can conclude the following.

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathcal{W}_{k}|(n-t-k)!}{(n-t)!}\leqslant

\displaystyle(k+1)\frac{f(j_{0})(n-t-k)!}{(n-t)!}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)(Ct^{0.99})^{k}}{(n-t-k)^{k}}\leqslant\ 2^{-k},

where in the last inequality we use that
n-t-k\geqslant\epsilon t
and the fact that
t>t_{0}(\epsilon)
.
∎

2.5.
Finding a dense piece of
\mathcal{F}
and a sharper spread approximation

We return to the notation used in Theorem
6

\mathcal{F}(X)
is much denser in

\mathcal{F}
in

Theorem 12

Assume that

Assume that
\epsilon\in(0,1/2)

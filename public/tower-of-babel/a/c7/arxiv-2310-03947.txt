On growth error bound and Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz condition
Qinian Jin

Abstract
. In this paper, by using the proximal point algorithm, we provide a more accessible and elementary proof of the fact that Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz conditions imply the growth error bounds for convex functions which has been established before via a subgradient flow. We further extend the result for nonconvex functions. Finally we show that every definable function in an o-minimal structure must satisfy a growth error bound.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
90C31, 90C30, 90C25

1.
Introduction

Many algorithms have been developed to solve minimization problems of the form

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}f(x),

(1.1)

where
X
is a Hilbert space and
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper lower
semi-continuous function. Various favorable conditions have been
proposed on
f
to guarantee desired convergence rates for many specific algorithms.
When
f
is a proper convex function, the following error bound conditions have
been introduced at a point
\bar{x}\in S:=\arg\min_{x\in X}f(x)
to derive linear
convergence for a number of first order optimization methods
(
[
4
,
5
,
14
,
23
,
24
]
):

\bullet

Quadratic growth condition
: there exist
r>0
and
\gamma>0

such that

d^{2}(x,S)\leq\gamma(f(x)-f_{*}),\qquad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x}).

\bullet

Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) condition with exponent
1/2
: there exist
r>0

and
\gamma>0
such that

f(x)-f_{*}\leq\gamma d^{2}(0,\partial f(x)),\qquad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x}).

\bullet

Subdifferential error bound condition
: there exist constants
r>0

and
\gamma>0
such that

d(x,S)\leq\gamma d(0,\partial f(x)),\qquad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x}).

\bullet

Proximal error bound condition
: there exist
r>0
and
\gamma>0

such that

d(x,S)\leq\gamma\|x-\mbox{prox}_{f}(x)\|,\qquad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x}).

where,
f_{*}:=f(\bar{x})
,
\partial f
and
\mbox{prox}_{f}
denote the convex subdifferential and
the proximal mapping of
f
respectively, and
d(x,S)
denotes the distance from
x
to

S
, i.e.

d(x,S):=\inf_{z\in S}\|x-z\|.

Note that the subdifferential error bound condition is the metric subregularity of
\partial f

at the point
(\bar{x},0)
in the graph of
\partial f
(
[
12
,
15
]
). When
f
is
continuously differetiable, the KL condition with exponent
1/2
is also called the
Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition (
[
21
,
22
,
25
]
).

These error bound conditions constitute important mathematical concepts with
diverse applications in optimization, analysis, and variational inequalities.
They play a crucial role in establishing convergence properties
of algorithms, characterizing critical points, and understanding the behavior
of functions in various settings. Thus, the exploration of relationships among
these error bound conditions becomes a compelling endeavor, which has witnessed
recent advancements.

Notably, the equivalence between the subdifferential error bound condition and
the proximal error bound condition has been established in
[
14
]
. This
equivalence is demonstrated through the utilization of properties of proximal mapping,
resulting in a relatively straightforward argument. Based on Ekeland’s variational
principle, the equivalence between the quadratic growth condition and the
subdifferential error bound condition has been proved in
[
2
,
3
]
.
Furthermore, the equivalence between the quadratic growth condition and the KL
condition with exponent
1/2
has been established in
[
9
]
as a special
case of a more general result. Actually, it has been proved in
[
9
]
that
if a proper, lower semi-continuous, convex function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]

satisfies at a point

\bar{x}\in S
the growth error bound

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq\varphi(f(x)-f_{*}),\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f<f(\bar{x})+\eta\}

(1.2)

for some
r>0
,
\eta\in(0,\infty]
and a desingularizing function

\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}(0,\eta)
, where

{\mathcal{K}}(0,\eta):=\{\varphi\in C[0,\eta)\cap C^{1}(0,1):\varphi(0)=0,\varphi\mbox{ is concave},\varphi^{\prime}(s)>0\},

and if there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle s\varphi^{\prime}(s)\geq c\varphi(s)\quad\mbox{for all }s\in(0,\eta),

(1.3)

then
f
must satisfy at
\bar{x}
the KL condition

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f_{*})d(0,\partial f(x))\geq 1,\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})<f<f(\bar{x})+\eta\}

(1.4)

and, moreover, the converse holds without requiring (
1.3
). The proof
that (
1.2
) implies (
1.4
) is somewhat straightforward. However, establishing
the reverse implication is highly nontrivial. The proof provided in

[
9
]
is based on the subgradient flow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\dot{x}(t)\in-\partial f(x(t))\quad\mbox{for almost all }t\in(0,\infty),\\
x(0)=x\end{array}\right.

generated by
\partial f
and hence the argument relies heavily on the theory of differential
inclusion which involves a lot of machinery.

In this note, by using the proximal point algorithm we will provide an alternative argument
to show that the KL condition (
1.4
) implies the growth error bound (
1.2
) for
proper lower semi-continuous convex functions, offering a more accessible and elementary proof.
Furthermore, we will extend our argument to demonstrate that, for proper lower semi-continuos
nonconvex functions, (
1.4
) still implies (
1.2
) on a possibly smaller set. As a
direct consequence, we may use the result in
[
8
]
, which states that every definable
function in an o-minimal structure must satisfy the KL condition, to conclude that every
definable function satisfies the growth error bound (
1.2
). Nevertheless, we will provide
a direct derivation of this fact because the proof of KL property of definable functions in

[
8
]
involves a lot of machinery, including the Whitney stratification and the
projection formula related to the stratum.

2.
KL condition implies growth error bound

We start by proving that the KL condition implies the growth error bound for proper lower
semi-continuous convex functions.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, lower semi-continuous, convex function
with
S:=\arg\min_{x\in X}f(x)\neq\emptyset
. Let
\bar{x}\in S
and
f_{*}:=f(\bar{x})
.
If there exist
r>0
,
\eta>0
and a desingularizing function

\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}(0,\eta)
such that

\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f_{*})d(0,\partial f(x))\geq 1

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f_{*}<f<f_{*}+\eta\}
, then

d(x,S)\leq 2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*})

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f_{*}\leq f<f_{*}+\eta\}
.

Proof.

Given any
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f_{*}<f<f_{*}+\eta\}
and consider the sequence

\{x^{k}\}
defined by the proximal point algorithm

x^{k}=\arg\min_{z\in X}\left\{f(z)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|z-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right\},\quad k=1,2,\cdots

with
x^{0}:=x
, where
\tau>0
is a constant. Because
f
is convex and lower
semi-continuous, we have the following results (see
[
6
, Theorem 28.1]

for instance):

\bullet

\|x^{k+1}-\bar{x}\|\leq\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|
for all
k\geq 0
.
This implies
x^{k}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
for all
k\geq 0
.

\bullet

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})
for all
k
. Consequently

x^{k}\in\{f_{*}\leq f<f_{*}+\eta\}
for all
k\geq 0
.

\bullet

f(x^{k})\to f_{*}
and
x^{k}\rightharpoonup x^{*}
as
k\to\infty
for
some
x^{*}\in S
. where
\rightharpoonup
denotes the weak convergence in
X
.

We now show that for all integers
k\geq 1
there holds

\displaystyle 2\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\leq\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\left[\varphi(f(x^{k})-f_{*})-\varphi(f(x^{k+1})-f_{*})\right].

(2.1)

If
f(x^{k})=f_{*}
, then by the definition of
x^{k+1}
we have

\displaystyle f(x^{k+1})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}\leq f(x^{k})

(2.2)

which implies
f(x^{k+1})=f_{*}
and
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
and hence (
2.1
) holds trivially. So we may assume

f(x^{k})>f_{*}
. By the definition of
x^{k}
we have
(x^{k-1}-x^{k})/\tau\in\partial f(x^{k})
. Thus, by the given condition
we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(f(x^{k})-f_{*})\geq\frac{1}{d(0,\partial f(x^{k}))}\geq\frac{\tau}{\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|}.

(2.3)

It follows from the concavity of
\varphi
, (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle\varphi(f(x^{k+1})-f_{*})

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(f(x^{k})-f_{*})+\varphi^{\prime}(f(x^{k})-f_{*})(f(x^{k+1})-f(x^{k}))

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(f(x^{k})-f_{*})-\frac{\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}}{2\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|}.

Therefore

\displaystyle 2\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+\frac{\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{2}}{\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\left[\varphi(f(x^{k})-f_{*})-\varphi(f(x^{k+1})-f_{*})\right]

which shows (
2.1
) again.

For any integer
l>1
, by summing (
2.1
) over
k
from
1
to
l-1
we can obtain

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\left[\varphi(f(x^{1})-f_{*})-\varphi(f(x^{l})-f_{*})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\varphi(f(x^{1})-f_{*})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\varphi(f(x^{0})-f_{*}).

Consequently

\displaystyle\|x^{l}-x^{0}\|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{l-1}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\leq 2\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+2\varphi(f(x^{0})-f_{*}).

(2.4)

Since
x^{l}\rightharpoonup x^{*}
as
l\to\infty
, we have

\|x^{*}-x\|^{2}=\lim_{l\to\infty}\langle x^{l}-x,x^{*}-x\rangle\leq\liminf_{l\to\infty}\|x^{l}-x\|\|x^{*}-x\|

which implies
\|x^{*}-x\|\leq\liminf_{l\to\infty}\|x^{l}-x\|
. Therefore, by using
x^{*}\in S
,

x^{0}=x
and (
2.4
) we can obtain

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq\|x^{*}-x\|\leq\liminf_{l\to\infty}\|x^{l}-x^{0}\|\leq 2\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*}).

By the definition of
x^{1}
we have
\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}\leq 2\tau(f(x^{0})-f(x^{1}))\leq 2\tau(f(x)-f_{*})
. Therefore

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq 2\sqrt{2\tau(f(x)-f_{*})}+2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*})

which is valid for all
\tau>0
. Letting
\tau\to 0
then shows the result. ∎

For a given convex function, we may use Theorem
2.1
to derive the growth error
bound of its Moreau envelop from the growth error bound of the function itself. Recall
that for a proper, lower semi-continuous, convex function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
,
its Moreau envelop
M_{\lambda f}
, for any
\lambda>0
, is defined by

M_{\lambda f}(x):=\inf_{x\in X}\left\{f(z)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad x\in X.

It is well known (
[
6
]
) that
M_{\lambda f}
is a continuous differentiable convex
function with
M_{\lambda f}(x)\leq f(x)
for all
x\in X
,

\min_{x\in X}f(x)=\min_{x\in X}M_{\lambda f}(x),

and
f
and
M_{\lambda f}
have the same set of minimizers; moreover

\displaystyle\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(x-\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\right),\quad x\in X,

(2.5)

where
\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}
denotes the proximal mapping of
f
with parameter
\lambda
, i.e.

\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x):=\arg\min_{x\in X}\left\{f(z)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|z-x\|^{2}\right\},\quad x\in X.

By the firmly nonexpansiveness of the proximal mapping,
\nabla M_{\lambda f}
is Lipschitz continuous
with constant
1/\lambda
. We have the following result.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, lower semi-continuous, convex function with

S:=\arg\min_{x\in X}f(x)\neq\emptyset
. If
f
satisfies a growth error bound at a point

\bar{x}\in S
, i.e. there exist
C>0
,
r>0
,
\eta\in(0,\infty]
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq C\left(f(x)-f(\bar{x})\right)^{\alpha}

(2.6)

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})\leq f<f(\bar{x})+\eta\}
, then there exists a
constant
\tilde{C}>0
such that

d(x,S)\leq\tilde{C}\left(M_{\lambda f}(x)-M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\right)^{\min\{\alpha,1/2\}}

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\leq M_{\lambda f}<M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})+\eta\}
.

Proof.

By using (
2.6
) and
[
9
, Theorem 5 (ii)]
we first have

\displaystyle\left(f(x)-f(\bar{x})\right)^{1-\alpha}\leq\frac{C}{\alpha}d(0,\partial f(x))

(2.7)

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})\leq f<f(\bar{x})+\eta\}
. We next show that

M_{\lambda f}
satisfies the KL condition at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\min\{1-\alpha,1/2\}
.
Let
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\leq M_{\lambda f}<M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})+\eta\}

be any point. By noting that

M_{\lambda f}(x)=f(\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)-x\|^{2}

and (
2.5
), we have
f(\bar{x})\leq f(\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))\leq M_{\lambda f}(x)<f(\bar{x})+\eta
and

\displaystyle M_{\lambda f}(x)-M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})=f(\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))-f(\bar{x})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}.

By the nonexpansiveness of the proximal mapping, we have

\|\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)-\bar{x}\|=\|\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)-\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\|\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|<r.

Thus we may use (
2.7
) to conclude

\displaystyle M_{\lambda f}(x)-M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\leq\left(\frac{C}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}d(0,\partial f(\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)))^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}.

By the definition of
\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)
we have

\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(x-\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x))\in\partial f(\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)).

and thus
d(0,\partial f(\mbox{prox}_{\lambda f}(x)))\leq\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|
. Therefore

\displaystyle M_{\lambda f}(x)-M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\leq\left(\frac{C}{\alpha}\right)^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{2}

Note that

\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|=\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)-\nabla M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\|x-\bar{x}\|<\frac{r}{\lambda}.

Thus, there must exist a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle M_{\lambda f}(x)-M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\leq C_{1}\|\nabla M_{\lambda f}(x)\|^{\min\{\frac{1}{1-\alpha},2\}}

(2.8)

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})\leq M_{\lambda f}\leq M_{\lambda f}(\bar{x})+\eta\}
.
Finally, by using (
2.8
) and Theorem
2.1
we can complete the proof immediately.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, lower semi-continuous, convex function that
is a KL function with exponent
\gamma\in(0,1]
. It has been proved in
[
19
, Theorem 3.4]

that if
\gamma\in(0,2/3)
then its Moreau envelop
M_{\lambda f}
for any
\lambda>0
is a KL function
with exponent
\max\{1/2,\gamma/(2-2\gamma)\}
. In the proof of Corrolary
2.2
, we have
actually obtained the tighter result that
M_{\lambda f}
is a KL function with exponent
\max\{1/2,\gamma\}

for all
\gamma\in(0,1]
. This tight result has been proved in
[
28
]
recently as a special case
of a general result with an involved argument, see Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.1 in
[
28
]
.
Our derivation is simple and straightforward.

Next we will consider extending Theorem
2.1
to nonconvex functions.
We need to replace the convex subdifferential by the Fréchet subdifferential.
Recall that for a proper function
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
its Fréchet
subdifferential at a point
x\in\mbox{dom}(f):=\{x\in X:f(x)<\infty\}
is the set

\hat{\partial}f(x)
consisting of all vectors
\xi\in X
satisfying

\liminf_{y\neq x,y\to x}\frac{f(y)-f(x)-\langle\xi,y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0.

When
x\notin\mbox{dom}(f)
, we set
\hat{\partial}f(x)=\emptyset
. By definition it
is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\hat{\partial}(f+g)(x)=\nabla f(x)+\hat{\partial}g(x),\quad\forall x\in X

(2.9)

if
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuously differentiable and
g:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
is
proper.

In the following result we will extend Theorem
2.1
to nonconvex functions by
showing that if
f
satisfies a KL condition at a point
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}(f)
then

f
satisfies a growth error bound at
\bar{x}
on a possibly smaller set.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f:X\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, weakly lower semi-continuous function and

\bar{x}\in\emph{dom}(f)
. If there exist
r>0
,
\eta>0
and a desingularizing
function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}(0,\eta)
such that

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))d(0,\hat{\partial}f(x))\geq 1

(2.10)

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})<f<f(\bar{x})+\eta\}
, then there exist

0<\tilde{r}\leq r
and
0<\tilde{\eta}\leq\eta
such that

d(x,S)\leq 2\varphi(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))

for all
x\in B_{\tilde{r}}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})\leq f<f(\bar{x})+\tilde{\eta}\}
,
where
S:=\{x\in X:f(x)\leq f(\bar{x})\}
.

Proof.

By considering the function
\tilde{f}(x):=\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\}
, which is still proper
and weakly lower semi-continuous, and noting that

\bar{x}\in S=\arg\min_{x\in X}\tilde{f}(x)

and
\hat{\partial}\tilde{f}(x)=\hat{\partial}f(x)
for
x
satisfying
f(x)>f(\bar{x})
, it suffices to prove the result
by assuming that
\bar{x}
is a minimizer of
f
. In the following we set
f_{*}:=f(\bar{x})
.

Since
\varphi\in C[0,\eta)
and
\varphi(0)=0
, we may take
\tilde{r}\in(0,r]
and

\tilde{\eta}\in(0,\eta]
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{r}+2\varphi(\tilde{\eta})<r.

(2.11)

Let
x\in B_{\tilde{r}}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})<f<f(\bar{x})+\tilde{\eta}\}
be any
fixed point, we define
x^{0}:=x
and

\displaystyle x^{k}\in\arg\min_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left\{f(z)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|z-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right\}

(2.12)

for
k=1,2,\cdots
, where
\tau>0
is a constant. Since
S\neq\emptyset
,
f
is bounded
from below. Thus, by using the weak lower semi-continuity of
f
, it is easy to show that the
sequence
\{x^{k}\}
is well-defined for
\tau>0
; due to the possible non-convexity of

f
, (
2.12
) may have many solutions, we take
x^{k}
to be any one of them.
The sequence
\{x^{k}\}
does not possess the nice convergence property in general as in the
convex situation. However, we may follow
[
4
,
5
]
and use (
2.10
) to show
that
\{x^{k}\}
converges to a point in
S
with which we can establish the desired
result.

Indeed, by the definition of
\{x^{k}\}
we have

\displaystyle f(x^{k})+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\leq f(x^{k-1})

(2.13)

which implies

\displaystyle f_{*}\leq f(x^{k})\leq\tilde{f}(x^{0})=f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\tilde{\eta}\quad\mbox{ for all }k\geq 0.

(2.14)

By the optimality of
x^{k}
and (
2.9
) we also have

\frac{1}{\tau}(x^{k-1}-x^{k})\in\hat{\partial}f(x^{k})

and thus

\displaystyle d(0,\hat{\partial}f(x^{k}))\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|.

(2.15)

We will use an induction argument to show that
x^{k}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
for all

k\geq 0
if

\displaystyle 0<\tau<\frac{1}{8\tilde{\eta}}\left(r-\tilde{r}-2\varphi(\tilde{\eta})\right)^{2}

(2.16)

which will be assumed in the following. Since
x^{0}=x\in B_{\tilde{r}}(\bar{x})
and

\tilde{r}<r
, this is trivial for
k=0
. By using (
2.13
) with
k=0
we also have

\displaystyle\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|\leq\sqrt{2\tau(f(x^{0})-f(x^{1}))}\leq\sqrt{2\tau(f(x)-f_{*})}.

(2.17)

Therefore, by using (
2.16
),

\displaystyle\|x^{1}-\bar{x}\|\leq\|x^{0}-\bar{x}\|+\sqrt{2\tau(f(x)-f_{*})}<\tilde{r}+\sqrt{2\tau\tilde{\eta}}<r

which shows
x^{1}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
. Now we assume that
x^{k}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
for all

0\leq k\leq j
for some
j\geq 1
. We will show that
x^{j+1}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
. Based on
(
2.13
) and (
2.15
) we may use the same argument in the proof of Theorem

2.1
to establish (
2.1
), i.e.

\displaystyle 2\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\leq\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\left[\varphi(f(x^{k})-f_{*})-\varphi(f(x^{k+1})-f_{*})\right]

for all
1\leq k\leq j
. Summing the above equation over
k
from
k=1
to
k=j
gives

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{j}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j}\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|+2\varphi(f(x^{0})-f_{*}).

This implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{j}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|+\|x^{j+1}-x^{j}\|\leq\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+2\varphi(f(x^{0})-f_{*}).

(2.18)

Therefore

\displaystyle\|x^{j+1}-\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{j}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|+\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+\|x^{0}-\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|+2\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*}).

Consequently, by virtue of (
2.17
), (
2.11
) and (
2.16
) we then obtain

\displaystyle\|x^{j+1}-\bar{x}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|+2\sqrt{2\tau(f(x)-f_{*})}+2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*})

\displaystyle<\tilde{r}+\sqrt{8\tau\tilde{\eta}}+2\varphi(\tilde{\eta})<r

which implies that
x^{j+1}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
.

Since
x^{k}\in B_{r}(\bar{x})
for all
k\geq 0
, we can see that (
2.18
)
holds for all
j\geq 0
. In particular

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|\leq\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*})<\infty

which shows that
\{x^{k}\}
has a finite length. Therefore
\{x^{k}\}
is a Cauchy
sequence and thus it is convergent, i.e. there is
x^{*}\in X
such that

x^{k}\to x^{*}
as
k\to\infty
. According to (
2.13
) and (
2.14
),
\{f(x^{k})\}

is monotonically decreasing and thus
\bar{f}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x^{k})
exists
with
f_{*}\leq\bar{f}<f_{*}+\tilde{\eta}
. If
\bar{f}\neq f_{*}
, then

f(x^{k})\geq\bar{f}>f_{*}
for all
k
. Thus, by (
2.10
) and (
2.15
) we have

1\leq\varphi^{\prime}(f(x^{k})-f_{*})d(0,\hat{\partial}f(x^{k}))\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\varphi^{\prime}(f(x^{k})-f_{*})\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|.

Since
\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|\to 0
, we must have
\varphi^{\prime}(f(x^{k})-f_{*})\to+\infty

as
k\to\infty
. By the continuity of
\varphi^{\prime}
on
(0,\tilde{\eta})
we then obtain

+\infty=\lim_{k\to\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(f(x^{k})-f_{*})=\varphi^{\prime}(\bar{f}-f_{*})<\infty

which is a contradiction. Thus
\bar{f}=f_{*}
. By the lower semi-continuity
of
f
we have

f_{*}\leq f(x^{*})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}f(x^{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x^{k})=\bar{f}=f_{*}.

Therefore
f(x^{*})=f_{*}
, i.e.
x^{*}\in S
. Consequently, it follows from (
2.18
)
and the definition of
x^{1}
that

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq\|x^{*}-x\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|x^{k}-x^{0}\|\leq 2\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|+2\varphi(f(x^{0})-f_{*})

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2\tau(f(x)-f_{*})}+2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*}).

Letting
\tau\to 0
shows the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For the special case that
f
is differntiable and satisfies the KL condition with exponent
1/2
,
i.e., (
2.10
) with
\varphi(t)=ct^{1/2}
for some constant
c>0
, the result in Theorem

2.3
has been proved in
[
20
]
.

Let us give a brief application of Theorem
2.3
to the Levenberg-Marquardt method
considered in
[
1
]
for solving the system of nonlinear equations

h(x)=0

whose solution set, denoted by
S
, is assumed to be nonempty, where

h:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a continuously differentiable mapping. This problem is
equivalent to solving the minimization problem

\min_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\left\{\psi(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|h(x)\|^{2}\right\}.

The Levenberg-Marquardt method takes the form

x^{k+1}=x^{k}-(\mu_{k}I+J_{k}^{T}J_{k})^{-1}J_{k}^{T}h(x^{k}),

where
J_{k}
denote the Jacobian of
h
at
x^{k}
. The local convergence property
has been analyzed in
[
1
]
. In particular, under the Hölderian local
error bound at a solution
x^{*}\in S
, i.e. there exist
\beta>0
,
r>0
and

\delta\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\beta d(x,S)\leq\|h(x)\|^{\delta},\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(x^{*})

(2.19)

and the Łojasiewicz gradient inequality at
x^{*}
, i.e. there exist
\kappa>0
,

\epsilon>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\psi(x)^{\theta}\leq\kappa\|\nabla\psi(x)\|,\quad\forall x\in B_{\epsilon}(x^{*}),

(2.20)

with careful choices of the regularization parameter
\mu_{k}>0
it has been shown in

[
1
, Theorem 1]
that if
\theta\in(0,1/2]
then
\psi(x^{k})\to 0
and

d(x^{k},S)\to 0
linearly as
k\to\infty
and if
\theta\in(1/2,1)
then there hold
the sublinear convergence rates

\psi(x^{k})=O(k^{-\frac{1}{2\theta-1}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad d(x^{k},S)=O(k^{-\frac{\delta}{2(2\theta-1)}}).

According to Theorem
2.3
, (
2.20
) implies (
2.19
) with
\delta=2(1-\theta)

and therefore (
2.19
) can be dropped from
[
1
, Theorem 2]
.

3.
Growth error bound for definable functions

In
[
8
]
it has been shown that any proper, lower semi-continuous, definable
function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
in an o-minimal structure on the
real field
({\mathbb{R}},+,\cdot)
satisfies a KL condition at each point in its domain.
To be more precise, for any
\bar{x}\in\mbox{dom}(f)
and any
r>0
,
there exist
\eta\in(0,\infty]
and a definable desingularizing function

\varphi\in{\mathcal{K}}(0,\eta)
such that

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))d(0,\partial f(x))\geq 1

(3.1)

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})<f\leq f(\bar{x})+\eta\}
, Here
\partial f(x)

denotes the limiting subdifferential of
f
at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
which is the set consisting of
all those vectors
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that there exist sequences
\{x^{k}\}
and
\{\xi^{k}\}

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with the properties

x^{k}\to x,\quad f(x^{k})\to f(x)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\xi^{k}\in\hat{\partial}f(x^{k})

such that
\xi^{k}\to\xi
as
k\to\infty
. Clearly
\hat{\partial}f(x)\subset\partial f(x)

and hence from (
3.1
) it follows that

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))d(0,\hat{\partial}f(x))\geq 1

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\{f(\bar{x})<f\leq f(\bar{x})+\eta\}
. Consequently
we may use Theorem
2.3
to obtain

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq 2\varphi(f(x)-f_{*})

(3.2)

for all
x\in B_{\tilde{r}}(\bar{x})\cap\{f_{*}\leq f<f_{*}+\tilde{\eta}\}
for some

0<\tilde{r}\leq r
and
0<\tilde{\eta}\leq\eta
, where
S:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:f(x)\leq f(\bar{x})\}
.

Recall that the proof of (
3.1
) given in
[
8
]
involves a lot of
machinery, including the existence of a Whitney stratification for the graph of
f
and
a projection formula of
\partial f(x)
onto the tangent space of the stratum containing
x
.
Thus, the derivation of (
3.2
) using the above procedure can be rather involved.
In the following we will provide a straightforward derivation of (
3.2
) which
avoids using those involved machinery.

Let us first recall the definition of o-minimal structures and definable functions
(see
[
10
,
13
,
16
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

An o-minimal structure on the real field
({\mathbb{R}},+,\cdot)

is a sequence
\mathcal{O}=(\mathcal{O}_{n})_{n}
of collections
\mathcal{O}_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of subsets of

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

For every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the collection
\mathcal{O}_{n}
is closed under Boolean operations, i.e.
finite intersections, unions, and complements.

(ii)

If
A\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
and
B\in\mathcal{O}_{m}
, then
A\times B\in\mathcal{O}_{n+m}
.

(iii)

For any set
A\in\mathcal{O}_{n+1}
there holds
\pi(A)\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
, where

\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
denotes the canonical projection onto
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

(iv)

\mathcal{O}_{n}
contains the family of algebraic subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, that is,
every set of the form
\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:p(x)=0\}
, where
p:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}

is a polynomial function.

(v)

The elements of
\mathcal{O}_{1}
are exactly the finite unions of open intervals and points.

Every set in
\mathcal{O}_{n}
is called a definable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given an o-minimal structure
\mathcal{O}=(\mathcal{O}_{n})_{n}
over the real field
({\mathbb{R}},+,\cdot)
and
a subset
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. A mapping
F:A\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called definable
in
\mathcal{O}
if its graph

\mbox{Gr}(F):=\{x,y)\in A\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:y=F(x)\}

is a definable set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, i.e.
\mbox{Gr}(F)\in\mathcal{O}_{m+n}
.

According to the definition of definable sets and definable functions, it is easy to derive the
following facts (
[
16
]
):

\bullet

Images and inverse images of definable sets under definable functions are definable.

\bullet

The sum and composition of definable functions are definable.

\bullet

If
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a definable set, then
x\to d(x,A)
is a
definable function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. More generally, if
g:A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a definable function that is bounded from below and
F:A\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is a definable mapping, then the function
\varphi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
defined by

\varphi(y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\inf\{g(x):x\in F^{-1}(y)\}&\mbox{ if }F^{-1}(y)\neq\emptyset,\\
+\infty&\mbox{ if }F^{-1}(y)=\emptyset\end{array}\right.

is also definable.

For single variable definable functions there holds the following monotonicity result, see

[
16
, Lemma 2]
for instance.

Lemma 3.3

(Monotonicity lemma)
.

Let
f:(a,b)\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a definable function and
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then there is a finite number of points
a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}<t_{k+1}=b
such that,
on every interval
(t_{i},t_{i+1})
,
f
is
C^{l}
and either strictly monotone or constant.

Next we prove a result which generalizes the well known Łojasiewicz inequality for continuous
definable functions on compact sets, see
[
16
, Theorem 0]
for instance. Our result
allows one of the functions to be lower semi-continuous which is crucial for proving
(
3.2
). We will make use of ideas from
[
17
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
K
be a compact definable set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and let
g:K\to[0,\infty)

and
h:K\to[0,\infty]
be definable functions. If
g
is continuous,
h
is
lower semi-continuous and

\displaystyle\{x\in K:h(x)=0\}\subset\{x\in K:g(x)=0\},

(3.3)

then there exists a strictly increasing, definable, concave function

\varphi\in C[0,\infty)\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
with
\varphi(0)=0
such that

g(x)\leq\varphi(h(x)),\quad\forall x\in K\cap\emph{dom}(h).

Proof.

For any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
we set
h^{-1}(t):=\{x\in K:h(x)=t\}
and similarly for
g^{-1}(t)
.
If
h^{-1}(0)=K
, then
g^{-1}(0)=K
by (
3.3
) and thus the result holds
trivially with
\psi(t)=t
. If
h^{-1}(0)=\emptyset
, then by the lower semi-continuity
and nonnegativity of
h
we can see that
h
has a positive lower bound on
K
.
Since
g
is continuous on
K
and
K
is compact, we have

0\leq M:=\sup_{x\in K}g(x)<\infty

and thus the result holds again with
\psi(t)=Ct
for some positive constant
C
. We therefore
need only to consider the situation that
h^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
and
h^{-1}(0)\neq K
.

Since
K
is a definable set and
h:K\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a definable function,

h(K)
is a definable set in
{\mathbb{R}}
and hence
h(K)
is a finite union of points and
intervals with
0\in h(K)
.

If
0
is an isolated point in
h(K)
, then there is a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

h(x)\geq\varepsilon
whenever
h(x)\neq 0
. Therefore for
x\in K
with
h(x)<\varepsilon

we have
h(x)=0
and hence
g(x)=0
by the given condition (
3.3
); while
for
x\in K
with
h(x)\geq\varepsilon
we have

g(x)\leq M+1\leq\frac{M+1}{\varepsilon}h(x).

We thus obtain the desired result with
\psi(t):=(M+1)t/\varepsilon
.

In the following we therefore assume
0
is not an isolated point in
h(K)
. By the
structure of
h(K)
as a definable set in
{\mathbb{R}}
, we can find
T>0
such that

[0,T]\subset h(K)
. Thus
h^{-1}(t)\neq\emptyset
for each
t\in[0,T]
. Define

\mu(t):=\sup_{x\in h^{-1}(t)}g(x),\qquad t\in[0,T]

Then
\mu
is a definable function on
[0,T]
and
\mu(0)=0
by the given
condition (
3.3
).

We claim that
\mu
is continuous at
t=0
. If not, then there exist
\delta>0
and
a sequence
\{t_{k}\}\subset(0,T]
with
t_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
such that

\mu(t_{k})\geq\delta
for all
k
. By the definition of supremum, there is

x^{k}\in h^{-1}(t_{k})
such that

g(x^{k})\geq\mu(t_{k})-\delta/2\geq\delta/2,\quad\forall k.

Since
\{x^{k}\}\subset K
and
K
is compact, by taking a subsequence if necessary,
we may assume
x^{k}\to\bar{x}
as
k\to\infty
for some
\bar{x}\in K
. By the
continuity of
g
we then have
g(\bar{x})\geq\delta/2>0
. By the lower semi-continuity
of
h
we also have

0\leq h(\bar{x})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}h(x^{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}t_{k}=0

which means
h(\bar{x})=0
. This together with (
3.3
) shows
g(\bar{x})=0

which is a contradiction.

Now we apply Lemma
3.3
to
\mu
and use the continuity of
\mu
at
t=0

to conclude the existence of
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
such that
\mu\in C[0,\varepsilon]\cap C^{1}(0,\varepsilon]
.

\mu(0)=0
, and
\mu
is either constant or strictly monotone on
[0,\varepsilon]
.

If
\mu
is constant on
[0,\varepsilon]
, then by using
\mu(0)=0
we have
\mu(t)=0

for all
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
. Thus for
x\in K
with
h(x)\leq\varepsilon
we have
g(x)=0
;
while for
x\in K
with
h(x)>\varepsilon
we have
g(x)\leq\frac{M+1}{\varepsilon}h(x)
.
Thus the desired result holds with
\psi(t)=(M+1)t/\varepsilon
.

If
\mu
is strictly monotone on
[0,\varepsilon]
, by applying Lemma
3.3
to
\mu

and
\mu^{\prime}
we may have
\mu(t)>0
,
\mu^{\prime}(t)>0
and
\mu^{\prime\prime}(t)
has a constant
sign on
(0,\varepsilon]
by shrinking
\varepsilon
if necessary. By the definition of
\mu
we have
for all
x\in K
with
t:=h(x)\leq\varepsilon
that

g(x)\leq\mu(t)=\mu(h(x)).

In case
\mu^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
on
(0,\varepsilon]
,
\mu
is a concave function on
[0,\varepsilon]
. Therefore,
by introducing the function

\displaystyle\varphi(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}C_{0}\mu(t),&0\leq t\leq\varepsilon,\\
C_{0}\left(\mu(\varepsilon)+\mu^{\prime}(\varepsilon)(t-\varepsilon)\right),&\varepsilon<t<\infty\end{array}\right.

with
C_{0}:=\max\{1,(M+1)/\mu(\varepsilon)\}
and noting that
g(x)<M+1
for
x\in K
, it
is easy to see that
g(x)\leq\varphi(h(x))
for all
x\in K
and

\varphi\in C[0,\infty)\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
is strictly increasing, definable
and concave with
\varphi(0)=0
. In case
\mu^{\prime\prime}>0
on
(0,\varepsilon]
,
\mu
is a convex
function on
[0,\varepsilon]
. By using
\mu(0)=0
we then have
\mu(t)\leq\frac{\mu(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}t

for
t\in[0,\varepsilon]
. Define

\varphi(t)=\max\left\{\frac{\mu(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon},\frac{M+1}{\varepsilon}\right\}t,\quad t\in[0,\infty).

Again we can easily verify
g(x)\leq\varphi(h(x))
for
x\in K
. The proof is therefore complete.
∎

By virtue of Proposition
3.4
, we are now ready to prove a growth error bound
for definable functions which in particular implies (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, lower semi-continuous,
definable function and let
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
be such that

S:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:f(x)\leq\gamma\}\neq\emptyset.

Then for any
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0
there exists a strictly increasing,
definable, concave function
\varphi\in C[0,\infty)\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
with

\varphi(0)=0
such that

\displaystyle d(x,S)\leq\varphi([f(x)-\gamma]_{+})

(3.4)

for all
x\in B_{r}(\bar{x})\cap\emph{dom}(f)
.

Proof.

Let

g(x):=d(x,S)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad h(x):=[f(x)-\gamma]_{+}.

Then
g
is continuous and
h
is lower semi-continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
By the definability of
f
we can see that both
g
and
h
are definable.
Moreover,
h(x)=0
implies
g(x)=0
. Thus, we may apply Proposition
3.4

to
g
and
h
on the compact definable set
\overline{B_{r}(\bar{x})}

to conclude the proof.
∎

For semi-algebraic functions we can improve the result in Theorem
3.5
by establishing
Hölder growth error bounds. To see this, recall that a set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called
semi-algebraic if it is a union of finite many sets of the form

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:f(x)=0\mbox{ and }f_{i}(x)<0\mbox{ for }i=1,\cdots,p\},

where
f,f_{i}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are real polynomials, and a function

f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is called semi-algebraic if its graph is a
semi-algebraic set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
, see
[
11
]
. It is known that

\mathcal{SA}:=\{\mathcal{SA}_{n}\}
is the smallest o-minimal structure on the real field

({\mathbb{R}},+,\cdot)
, where
\mathcal{SA}_{n}
denotes the collection of all semi-algebraic
sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Thus, for a proper, lower semi-continuous, semi-algebraic function

f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
, we may use Theorem
3.5
to conclude that
(
3.4
) holds with a strictly increasing, semi-algebraic, concave function

\varphi\in C[0,\infty)\cap C^{1}(0,\infty)
satisfying
\varphi(0)=0
. Applying the
Puiseux lemma (
[
27
]
) to
\varphi
we can conclude that

\varphi(t)\leq Ct^{\alpha},\quad\forall t\geq 0

for some constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Consequently we have the following result.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, lower semi-continuous, semi-algebraic function
and let
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
be such that
S:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:f(x)\leq\gamma\}\neq\emptyset
.
Then for any
\bar{x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
r>0
there exist constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]

such that

d(x,S)\leq C[f(x)-\gamma]_{+}^{\alpha},\quad\forall x\in B_{r}(\bar{x}).

Remark 3.1
.

For a given semi-algebraic function, Corollary
3.6
establishes the Hölder growth
error bound for some parameter
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Nevertheless, it does not specify the precise
value of
\alpha
. Determining this value presents a challenging task. Interested readers can
find relevant insights in
[
18
]
, where further results on this aspect are discussed.

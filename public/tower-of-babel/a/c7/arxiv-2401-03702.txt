Improved curvature conditions on L 2 × ⋯ × L 2 → L 2 / m L^{2}\times\cdots\times L^{2}\to L^{2/m} bounds for multilinear maximal averages
Chu-hee Cho, Jin Bong Lee, Kalachand Shuin

Abstract
. In this article, we focus on L 2 ( ℝ d ) × ⋯ × L 2 ( ℝ d ) → L 2 / m ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\cdots\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) estimates for multilinear maximal averages over non-degenerate hypersurfaces. Our findings is new for m m -linear averages with m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 , and represent a reproof of the recent result of T. Borges, B. Foster, and Y. Ou on the curvature conditions of the hypersurfaces required in establishing L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) × L 2 ​ ( ℝ d ) → L 1 ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) estimates of bilinear maximal functions.

1.
Introduction

Let
\sigma
be the normalized surface measure on
\Sigma
supported in a unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{md}(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{md}
.
For
\mathrm{F}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
with Schwartz functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define a multilinear averaging operator
\mathrm{A}_{\sigma}
by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)\coloneqq\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x-ty_{i})~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y),

and a maximal average by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{F})(x)\coloneqq\sup_{t>0}|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)|.

One classical example of multilinear maximal function is the bilinear spherical maximal function, which is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{full}(\mathrm{F})(x):=\sup_{t>0}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}f_{1}(x-ty)f_{2}(x-tz)~d\sigma(y,z)\Big|,

where
\sigma
is the normalized surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}
,
d\geq 1
.
This operator was initially introduced by Barrionuevo, Grafakos, He, Honzík, and Oliveira
[
1
]
, and then Heo, Hong, and Yang
[
12
]
improved upon the previous results.
After, Jeong and Lee
[
13
]
have successfully demonstrated the complete (except few border line cases)
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of this operator using a clever idea of slicing argument in dimensions
d\geq 2
.

Boundedness of the bilinear spherical maximal function in dimension
d=1
was later investigated by Christ, Zhou
[
7
]
and Dosidis, Ramos
[
9
]
independently.
Recently, Bhojak, Choudhary, Shrivastava, and the third author of this article
[
2
]
have established end point estimates of bilinear spherical maximal function in dimensions
d=1,2
.
Lee and the third author of this article
[
15
]
extended the slicing technique to deal with boundedness of bilinear maximal functions defined on degenerate hypersurfaces, like
\Sigma_{a_{1},a_{2}}:=\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}:\Phi(y,z)=|y|^{a_{1}}+|z|^{a_{2}}-1=0\}
, for
a_{1},a_{2}\in[1,\infty)
.
Despite of the vanishing curvature conditions of
\Sigma_{a_{1},a_{2}}
, the authors of
[
15
]
were able to apply the slicing argument to study boundedness of the bilinear maximal function defined on
\Sigma_{a_{1},a_{2}}
and proved sharp
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
estimates except for border line cases.
In the slicing argument of
[
15
]
, the non-vanishing gradient
(|\nabla\Phi|\neq 0)
of the hypersurface representing function
\Phi
plays a crucial role.

Therefore, a natural question arises, when we only have information about the curvature conditions of hypersurfaces but not the equation, what can we infer about the boundedness of bilinear and multilinear maximal functions associated with the hypersurfaces?
This problem was first addressed by Grafakos, He, and Honzík
[
10
]
and later Chen, Grafakos, He, Honzík, Slavíková
[
6
]
considered as bilinear analogues of
[
17
]
.
For
m=2
, Chen
et.

al.

[
6
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 2)
.

Let
\sigma
be the surface measure of a compact and smooth surface
\Sigma
without boundary such that
k
of its
2d-1
principal curvatures are non-zero.
Then
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}
maps
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
when
k>d+2
.

Very recently, Borges, Foster, and Ou
[
4
]
have improved the curvature condition to
k\geq d+2
.
Their results are based on studies of Sobolev smoothing estimates for various bilinear maximal operators given by Fourier multipliers, which contain multi-scale maximal functions, and maximal functions with fractal dilation sets.

To state our main results, we begin with mutlilinear local maximal functions.

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})(x)\coloneqq\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x-ty_{i})~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|.

We first establish multilinear local maximal estimates.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s>\frac{(m-1)d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
,
m\geq 2
, and a measure
\sigma
be supported in
\mathbb{B}^{md}(0,1)
and satisfy

|\widehat{\mathrm{d}\sigma}(\xi)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-s}.

Then we have for
2/m\leq p\leq 2
,

\|\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1))}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For
p=2/m
, we have

\|\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By making use of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain estimates for global maximal functions (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m
,
d
,
\sigma
be given as in Theorem
1.2
.
Then we have

\|\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Remark 1
.

In order to discuss optimal range of the above estimates, we consider two operators. First, let
\mathrm{M}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
denote the spherical maximal function on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose Fourier symbol has decay
1
.
Then,
m
-product of
\mathrm{M}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}
obeys that

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{M}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(f_{i})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

whenever
p>3/(2m)
,
p_{i}>3/2
with
1/p=\sum_{i=1}^{m}1/p_{i}
.
This certainly implies
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times\cdots\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
estimates.
On the other hand, for the circular maximal function
\mathrm{M}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
, its Fourier decay of the circular measure is
1/2
and its
m
-product does not satisfy
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\times\cdots\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
estimates
since
\mathrm{M}_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
p>2
. In particular, in bilinear case of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, the condition
s>\frac{(m-1)d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
becomes
s>\frac{d+1}{2}
.
However, it should be noted that
s>\frac{d+1}{2}
is not sharp for Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, and in terms of
k
nonvanishing principal curvatures,
s>\frac{d+1}{2}
is equivalent to
k>d+1
same as
[
4
]
.
In fact, from the results for two maximal operators, one may conjecture for the case of
m=2
that optimal range of
s
for Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
is
s>1/2
.

By multilinear real interpolation, we obtain
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\cdots\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
estimates of
\mathrm{M}^{loc}_{\sigma}
for some
p<2
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
\Sigma
be a compact and smooth hypersurface with non-vanishing principal curvatures
k>(m-1)d+1
. Then, the multilinear local maximal function
\mathrm{M}^{loc}_{\sigma}
maps
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\cdots\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
L^{p/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p>\frac{k-(m-1)d+1}{k-(m-1)d}
.

By Corollary
1.4
, it is shown for
m=2
that
M_{\sigma}^{loc}
satisfies
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
estimates for
p>\frac{k-d+1}{k-d}
.
However, the range of
p
is not sharp as we mentioned in Remark
1
.
It could be conjectured that
p>(k+1)/k
is sharp, which is obtained from validity of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
for
s>1/2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
\alpha
-dimensional measures and weighted estimates

Contents of this subsection are mostly given by Ko, Lee, and Oh
[
14
]
.
Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then for
\alpha\in(0,d]
,
\mu
is said to be
\alpha
-dimensional if there exists a
C>0
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mu(\mathbb{B}^{d}(x_{0},R))\leq CR^{\alpha},

where
\mathbb{B}^{d}(x_{0},R)
denotes a
d
-dimensional ball centered at
x_{0}
with radius
R
.
For an
\alpha
-dimensional measure
\mu
, one can define the following quantity:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},R>0}R^{-\alpha}\mu(\mathbb{B}^{d}(x_{0},R)).

It is known in the literature that one can rewrite
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}f
by making use of a measurable function
\mathbb{t}(\cdot)
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}f(x)=\int f(x-\mathbb{t}(x)y)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

We define a
d
-dimensional measure
\mu
by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}F(x,t)~\mathrm{d}\mu(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1)}F(x,\mathbb{t}(x))~\mathrm{d}x

for any function
F\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
.
Then,
L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1),\mathrm{d}x)
norm of
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}
is equivalent to

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Big(\int|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma}f(x,t)|^{p}~\mathrm{d}\mu(x,t)\Big)^{1/p}

That is,
\mathrm{d}\mu
is a
d
-dimensional measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
.

We restrict our consideration of
\alpha
-dimensional measures on
\mu
given by
\omega~\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t
.
Let
\Omega^{\alpha}
be a collection of non-negative measurable functions
\omega
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
\omega~\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t
is
\alpha
-dimensional.
We use a notation
[\omega]_{\alpha}=\langle\omega~\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\rangle_{\alpha}
.
It is known in
[
14
]
that (
2.5
) is reduced to weighted norms associated with
\omega\in\Omega^{d}
.
The following lemma was already given in
[
14
, Lemma 2.19]
for dimension
d=4
. One can easily extend it to general dimension
d
by modifying the proof of the case of
d=4
, so we omit it.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p\in(0,\infty]
,
\alpha\in(0,d+1]
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\alpha}
.
For a function
F\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
whose Fourier support is a subset of
\mathbb{B}^{d+1}(0,\lambda)
,
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\omega)}\leq C[\omega]_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda^{\frac{d+1-\alpha}{p}}\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t)}.

2.2.
L^{p}
improving estimates for multilinear operators with the limited decay condition

We recall simple estimates for multilinear operators whose symbols satisfy the following decay conditions:

|\mathfrak{m}(\xi)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-s}

for some given
s>0
.
We define the Littlewood-Paley decomposition for further uses.
Let
\Psi
be a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{md}
such that
\widehat{\Psi}\equiv 1
for
1<|\xi|<2
and vanishes outside of
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{md}:1/2<|\xi|<4\}
.
Define
\Psi_{j}(\cdot)=2^{jmd}\Psi(2^{j}\cdot)
and
\Phi(\cdot)=1-\sum_{j\geq 1}\Psi_{j}
.
If we use the decomposition on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say
P_{j}
to denote the
j
-th Littlewood-Paley projection.

Then, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})
be a multilinear operator given by

T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m})}\mathfrak{m}(\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{f}_{i}(\xi_{i})~\mathrm{d}\xi.

Suppose that there is a constant
C>0
such that
|\mathfrak{m}(\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{-s}
for some
s>0
,
and
\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{m})\subset\mathbb{A}^{md}(2^{j})
.
Then we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle C2^{-j(s-\frac{(m-1)d}{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

For
L^{2}
norm we begin with

(2.8)

\displaystyle T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})(x)=\Psi_{j}\ast T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})(x,\dots,x),

where
\widehat{\Psi}_{j}
denotes the
j
-th Littlewood-Paley decomposition in
\mathbb{R}^{md}
.
Taking
L^{2}
norm to (
2.8
), it yields

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})(y)\Psi_{j}(x-y_{1},\dots,x-y_{m})~\mathrm{d}y\Big|^{2}~\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}\Big|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})(y)\Big|^{2}|\Psi_{j}(x-y_{1},\dots,x-y_{m})|~\mathrm{d}y~\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq 2^{j(m-1)d}\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{md})}^{2}.\end{split}

Here we apply Hölder’s inequality in the first inequality and compute
x
-integral first in the second inequality.
By the Plancherel theorem and the conditions of
\mathfrak{m}
, we obtain

\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2^{-j(s-\frac{(m-1)d}{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

By (
2.9
) one can check that for general
p\geq 1

\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 2^{j\frac{(m-1)d}{p}}\|T_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{md})}.

∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We make use of the Littlewood-Paley decomposition to obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})(x)\leq\sum_{j\geq 0}\mathrm{M}_{\sigma_{j}}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})(x).

Note that
\sigma_{j}=\sigma\ast\Psi_{j}
for
j>0
and
\sigma_{0}=\sigma\ast\Phi
.
It is worth noting that
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma_{0}}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})
is bounded pointwisely by product of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions
\mathrm{M}_{HL}(f_{i})
.
Thus we only have to consider
j\geq 1
cases.

Note that
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma_{j}}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})(x)=\sup_{1<t<2}|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)|
,
where
\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)
is given by

\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m})}\widehat{\sigma}_{j}(t\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{f}_{j}(\xi_{i})~\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since
1<t<2
, one can say that
2^{j-2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+2}
in the support of
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}(t\cdot)
, hence
|\xi_{i}|\lesssim 2^{j}
for each
i=1,\dots,m
.
Moreover, there is at least one
i=1,\dots,m
such that
2^{j-2}\leq|\xi_{i}|\leq 2^{j+2}
.
We first obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s(m,d)=s-\frac{(m-1)d}{2}-\frac{1}{2}
. Then we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C2^{-js(m,d)}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

As a consequence of the lemma
3.1
, we show theorem
1.2
. In fact, by the lemma
3.1
, it follows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}\leq&\sum_{j\geq 0}\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,t)\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}\\
\leq&C\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-js(m,d)2/m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}.\end{split}

Since
s(m,d)>0
, this proves the theorem.
Therefore it remains to prove Lemma
3.1
.

3.1.
Proof of Lemma
3.1

In order to show lemma
3.1
, we use the following lemma in
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
R=1+4\text{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\sigma))
and

K_{j}(\mathbf{x},t)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i\mathbf{x}\cdot\xi}\int\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi it(y\cdot\xi)}~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\widehat{\Psi}(2^{-j}\xi)~\mathrm{d}\xi

for
j\geq 0
and
(\mathbf{x},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{md}\times\mathbb{R}
.
If
|\mathbf{x}|\geq R
and
|t|\leq 2
, then
|K_{j}(\mathbf{x},t)|\lesssim 2^{-jN}(1+|\mathbf{x}|)^{-N}
for any
N>0
.

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem and change of variables, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{j}(\mathbf{x},t)=2^{jmd}\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i2^{j}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\xi-ty\cdot\xi)}\widehat{\Psi}(\xi)~\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).\end{split}

Note that
|\nabla_{\xi}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\xi-ty\cdot\xi)|\geq|\mathbf{x}|/2
for
|\mathbf{x}|\geq R
and
|t|\leq 2
.
Then by iterated use of integration by parts in
\xi
, we have

|K_{j}(\mathbf{x},t)|\chi_{|\mathbf{x}|\geq R}(\mathbf{x})\chi_{|t|\leq 2}(t)\lesssim 2^{jmd}(1+2^{j}|\mathbf{x}|)^{-L}\chi_{|\mathbf{x}|\geq R}(\mathbf{x})

for any
L>0
.
Taking
L\geq md+2N
for a fixed number
N>0
, it follows that

|K_{j}(\mathbf{x},t)|\chi_{|\mathbf{x}|\geq R}(\mathbf{x})\chi_{|t|\leq 2}(t)\leq 2^{-jN}(1+|\mathbf{x}|)^{-N}.

∎

We split the integral range
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into the Ball of radius
R
centered at the origin and the complement, and apply Lemma
3.2
to get

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\|&\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R))}+\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R)^{\mathsf{c}})}\\
\lesssim&\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R))}+2^{-jN}\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}M_{HL}f_{i}\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R)^{\mathsf{c}})},\end{split}

where
A^{\mathsf{c}}
denotes the complement of a set
A
.
Therefore to prove Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show (
3.2
) with left-hand side replaced in to

-norm since the other part can be controlled by the right side norm from the Hardy-Littlewood maximal estimates.

We define
\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{md}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi i(x\cdot(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m})+t\tau)}m(\xi,\tau)\widehat{\Psi}(2^{-j}\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(2^{-j}R^{-1}\tau)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{f}_{i}(\xi_{i})~\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\tau,

where

with a smooth function
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\rho\equiv 1
on
[1,2]
and
R=1+4\text{diam}(\mathrm{supp}(\sigma))
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the space-time Fourier transform.
For
\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{j}}=\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}-\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}
,
\mathcal{F}[\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})]
is supported outside of
\mathbb{B}^{d+1}(0,m2^{j+1})
, and the kernel of
\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{j}}
has nice decay property.
Then, we have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
m(\xi,\tau)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\varphi}_{j})(R^{-1}\tau)
be a symbol of
\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{j}}
with respect to space-time Fourier transform.
Then for any
N\geq d
we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m(\xi,\tau)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\varphi}_{j})(R^{-1}\tau)](\mathbf{x},t)\leq C_{N}2^{-jN}(1+|\mathbf{x}|)^{-N}(1+|t|)^{-N},

where
C_{N}
is independent of

and
\mathbf{x},\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{md}
.

Proof.

Note that

m(\xi,\tau)=\int\widehat{\rho}(\tau+y\cdot\xi)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

For multi-indeces
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=N
, it follows that

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\tau}^{N}\big(m(\xi,\tau)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\varphi}_{j})(R^{-1}\tau)\big)|

is bounded by finite sum of the following term:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int|\widehat{\rho}^{(|\alpha_{1}|+N)}(\tau+y\cdot\xi)|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\times 2^{-j|\alpha_{2}|}\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\varphi}_{j})(R^{-1}\tau),

where
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
.
Since
|\xi|\sim 2^{j}
,
|\tau|>2^{j}R
, and
\rho\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, for any
L\geq 0
there exists a constant
C_{L}
such that

(
3.7
)

\displaystyle\leq C_{L}2^{-j|\alpha_{2}|}\frac{1}{(1+|\tau|)^{L}}\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)(1-\widehat{\varphi}_{j})(R^{-1}\tau)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq C_{L}\frac{1}{(1+|\tau|)^{L/2}}(R2^{j})^{-L/2}\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi).

By (
3.8
) and the integration by parts, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m(\xi,\tau)\widehat{\Psi}_{j}(\xi)

\displaystyle(1-\widehat{\varphi}_{j})(R^{-1}\tau)](\mathbf{x},t)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{R}\,

\displaystyle C_{L}(1+|\mathbf{x}|)^{-N}(1+|t|)^{-N}2^{-j(L/2-md)}.

If we choose
L\geq 2(N+md)
, then we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now we return to the proof of Lemma
3.1
. For
\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{j}}
, apply Lemma
3.3
to have

\sup_{1<t<2}|\mathrm{R}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(x,t)|\leq 2^{-jN}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{M}_{HL}(f_{i})(x).

N
could be sufficiently large, so we obtain the desired result.

To deal with
\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}
, we observe that the Fourier transform of
\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}
.
The Fourier transform in
x
is

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{\mathrm{A}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})}(\cdot,t)(\xi_{1})=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{(m-1)d}}\widehat{\mathrm{d}\sigma}_{j}(t\xi_{1}-t\xi_{2},\dots,t\xi_{m-1}-t\xi_{m},t\xi_{m})\\
&\quad\quad\times\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\cdots\widehat{f}_{m-1}(\xi_{m-1}-\xi_{m})\widehat{f}_{m}(\xi_{m})~\mathrm{d}\xi_{2}\cdots\mathrm{d}\xi_{m}.\end{split}

Since
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f_{i}})\subset\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,2^{j})
and
1<t<2
,
it implies that
\xi_{1}\in\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,m2^{j+1})
, and
\mathcal{F}[\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})](\xi_{1},\tau)
is supported in
\mathbb{B}^{d+1}(0,m2^{j+1})
.
Since
|\widehat{\mathrm{d}\sigma}(\xi)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-s}
and
\widehat{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}}
is supported in
\mathbb{A}^{md}(2^{j})
, we use Young’s inequality in
t
-variable and Lemma
2.2
in
x
-variable to obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}&\leq\|{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}\\
&\leq C\,2^{-j(s-\frac{(m-1)d}{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

To estimate our maximal estimate, we use Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.1
,
which is due to the equivalence (
2.5
).
Then,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,t)\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R))}&\leq R^{\frac{(m-1)d}{2}}\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,t)\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R))}\\
&\lesssim 2^{j/2}\|\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[1,2])}.\end{split}

Combining two estimates (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we obtain

\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\widetilde{\mathrm{A}}_{\sigma_{j}}(\mathrm{F})(\cdot,t)\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,R))}\lesssim 2^{-j(s-\frac{(m-1)d}{2}-\frac{1}{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Hence, the proof is completed.

We end this section with the proof of Corollary
1.4
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

From the Lemma
3.1
we have

\|\mathrm{M}^{loc}_{\sigma_{j}}\|_{L^{2}\times\cdots\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2/m}}\lesssim 2^{-j(k/2-\frac{(m-1)d}{2}-\frac{1}{2})}.

On the other hand, observe that

\mathrm{M}^{loc}_{\sigma_{j}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})(x)\lesssim 2^{j}\prod_{i=1}^{m}M_{HL}f_{i}(x).

Thus we also have
\|\mathrm{M}^{loc}_{\sigma_{j}}\|_{L^{p_{1}}\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim 2^{j}
for
1/m<p\leq\infty
,
1<p_{1},\dots,p_{m}\leq\infty
with
1/p=1/p_{1}+\cdots+1/p_{m}
.
Therefore, applying the real interpolation theory of multi(sub)linear operators and summing over
j
we get
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\cdots\times L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{p/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
estimate of
\mathrm{M}^{loc}_{\sigma}
for
p>\frac{k-(m-1)d+1}{k-(m-1)d}
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We first recall that the global maximal function
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}
is defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}(\mathrm{F})(x)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\,\prod_{i=1}f_{i}(x-2^{-k}ty_{i})~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|.

For any fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the Littlewood-Paley decomposition on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

(4.2)

\displaystyle P_{<k}=I-\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}P_{k+n},

where
P_{k}
denotes the
k
-th Littlewood-Paley projection and the identity operator
I
.
Then we may write

(4.3)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Big(P_{<k}f_{i}+P_{k\leq}f_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}P_{<k}f_{\mu}\Bigr)+\Bigl(\prod_{\nu=1}^{m}P_{k\leq}f_{\nu}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m-1}\frac{1}{\alpha!(m-\alpha)!}\sum_{\tau\in S_{m}}\Bigl(\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}P_{<k}f_{\tau(\mu)}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}P_{k\leq}f_{\tau(\nu)}\Bigr).

For
{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}=(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{m}
, we define for
\alpha=1,\dots,m-1
and
\tau\in S_{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\Bigl(\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}P_{<k}f_{\tau(\mu)}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\tau(\mu)})\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\tau(\nu)}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\tau(\nu)})\Bigr)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{m,\tau}(\mathrm{F})(x)=\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{m}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\Bigl(\prod_{\mu=1}^{m}P_{<k}f_{\mu}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\mu})\Bigr)~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|.

Let us define

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\bf{n}}(\mathrm{F})

\displaystyle:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{m}P_{k+n_{i}}f_{i}(x-2^{-k}ty_{i})~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\bf{n}}^{q}(\mathrm{F})

\displaystyle:=\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{m}P_{k+n_{i}}f_{i}(x-2^{-k}ty_{i})~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|\Big\|_{\ell^{q}({k\in\mathbb{Z}})}.

Then, the global maximal function
\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}
is bounded by a constant multiple of

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\sum_{\tau\in S_{m}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})|+\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F}).

Since the choice of
\tau
for fixed
\alpha
is harmless in estimating
\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})
, we may consider
\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})
instead of
\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha,\tau}(\mathrm{F})
, which is given by

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\Bigl(\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}P_{<k}f_{\mu}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\mu})\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\nu})\Bigr)~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, we use an induction argument. We start with the following lemma, which will play a crucial role to show the theorem when
m=2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
m=2
and
\alpha=1
we have

\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)\leq M_{HL}(f_{1})(x)\times M_{\sigma}^{i}(f_{2})(x),

where
M_{\sigma}^{i}
is defined by

M_{\sigma}^{i}(f)(x)=\sup_{t>0}\int_{\Sigma}|f(x-ty_{i})|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

Proof.

It suffices to show

\sup_{1<t<2}\sup_{y\in\Sigma}|P_{<k}f(x-2^{-k}ty)|\lesssim M_{HL}(f)(x).

Indeed,

\displaystyle P_{<k}f(x-2^{-k}ty)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\,2^{kd}\,\varphi(2^{k}(x-2^{-k}ty-z))~~\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x+2^{-k}z)\,\varphi(ty-z)~~\mathrm{d}z.

Since
y\in\Sigma
and
1<t<2
, it follows that for any
N>0

|P_{<k}f(x-2^{-k}ty)|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x+2^{-k}z)|\frac{C_{N}}{(1+|z|)^{N}}~~\mathrm{d}z\leq M_{HL}(f)(x).

∎

Note that
M_{\sigma}^{i}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whenever
|\widehat{\mathrm{d}\sigma}(\xi)|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-s}
for
s>\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
16
,
17
]
). In fact, from the assumption
s>\frac{(m-1)d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
, it follows
s>\frac{1}{2}
for
m=2
.
Then, by Lemma
4.1
, when
m=2
we prove

\Big\|\sum_{\alpha=1}^{2}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})|\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\times\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

For the summation of
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{n}}
over
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
q=2/m
. Then, we have

\|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{n}}^{q}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)=m^{-1/2}|\mathbf{n}|s(m,d)
.

Note that
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{n}}\leq\mathfrak{S}^{q}_{\mathbf{n}}
due to
\ell^{\infty}\to\ell^{q}
embedding with
q=2/m
.
Hence, it follows that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{M}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)}\prod_{i=1}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Since
2^{-\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)}
is summable over
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
for
s(m,d)>0
, this proves the theorem for the case of
m=2
.

For the induction, we assume that Theorem
1.3
holds for
N
-linear operators with
N=2,\cdots,m-1
.
Note that we already show it holds that when
m=2
.
Under the assumption, we will show the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
\alpha=1,\dots,m
, we have

\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})(x)\lesssim\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}M_{HL}(f_{\mu})(x)\times\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}\int_{\Sigma}\Big|\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\nu})\Big|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y).

Moreover, if we assume Theorem
1.3
holds for
N
-linear operators with
N=2,\cdots,m-1
,
then we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}\int_{\Sigma}\Big|\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\nu})\Big|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big\|_{L^{2/(m-\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)}\lesssim\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Note that the first assertions of the lemma follows directly by the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
For the second assertion, observe that the integrand of the left-hand side of (
4.9
) is an
(m-\alpha)
-sublinear operator, and the symbol has decay
s>\frac{(m-1)d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
.
For
\alpha=m
we have nothing to prove, for
\alpha=m-1
we have a linear maximal average which is surely bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and for
\alpha=m-2
we have a bi(sub)linear operator which is already proved by previous steps.

Therefore it suffices to consider
1\leq\alpha\leq m-3
.
In case of
1\leq\alpha\leq m-3
, observe that the Fourier decay
s>\frac{(m-1)d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
is clearly larger than
\frac{(m-\alpha-1)d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}
which is the condition of Theorem
1.3
for
(m-\alpha)
-sublinear operators.
Since we assume that Theorem
1.3
holds for
N=1,\dots,m-1
, it follows that

\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}\int_{\Sigma}\Big|\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(x-2^{-k}ty_{\nu})\Big|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big\|_{L^{2/(m-\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{d}x)}\leq C\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

Note that we assume Theorem
1.3
holds for
N
-linear operators with
N=2,\cdots,m-1
and prove the
N=2
case.
For general
m
, we make use of Lemma
4.3
to obtain

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\mathfrak{A}_{k}^{\alpha}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\Big\|\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}M_{HL}(f_{\mu})(x)\times\sup_{0<t}\int_{\Sigma}\Big|\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(\cdot-ty_{\nu})\Big|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\leq&\Big\|\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}M_{HL}(f_{\mu})\Big\|_{L^{2/\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\times\Big\|\sup_{0<t}\int_{\Sigma}\Big|\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}f_{\nu}(\cdot-ty_{\nu})\Big|~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big\|_{L^{2/(m-\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\lesssim&\prod_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}\|f_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\times\prod_{\nu=\alpha+1}^{m}\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

By (
4.10
) and Lemma
4.2
, Theorem
1.3
is true under the assumption that
N
cases hold for
N=2,\dots,m-1
.
This closes the induction. Hence, it remains to verify Lemma
4.2
to complete the proof.

4.1.
Proof of Lemma
4.2

We make use of the following scaling:

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{1<t<2}\Big|\int_{\Sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\,

\displaystyle P_{k+n_{i}}f_{i}(x-2^{-k}ty_{i})~~\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|\Big\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=2^{-kmd/2}\,\|\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(P_{n_{1}}f_{1,k},\dots,P_{n_{m}}f_{m,k})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
f_{i,k}(x)=f_{i}(x/2^{k})
.
Then, by Lemma
3.1
for
q=2/m

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{n}}^{q}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-kd}\,\|\mathrm{M}_{\sigma}^{loc}(P_{n_{1}}f_{1,k},\dots,P_{n_{m}}f_{m,k})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-kd}\,2^{-(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|n_{i}|)(2s(m,d))/{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|P_{n_{i}}f_{i,k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-kd}\,2^{-(2\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d))/{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}2^{(dk)/{m}}\|P_{n_{i}+k}f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-(2\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d))/{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|P_{n_{i}+k}f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2/m},

where
\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)=m^{-1/2}|\mathbf{n}|s(m,d)
since
\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|n_{i}|\geq m^{-1/2}|\mathbf{n}|
.
We apply Hölder’s inequality to the last line in the above inequalities to obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{n}}^{q}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\Bigl(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|P_{n_{i}+k}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\Bigr)^{{1}/{2}}.

By the Littlewood-Paley decomposition and Plancherel theorem, one can see that

\Big(\sum_{j}\big\|P_{j}f\big\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}

for
p\geq 2
.
Combining these two estimates, we have

\|\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{n}}^{q}(\mathrm{F})\|_{L^{2/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{-\delta(\mathbf{n},m,d)}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This proves the lemma.

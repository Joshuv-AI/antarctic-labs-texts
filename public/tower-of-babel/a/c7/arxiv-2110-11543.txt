Some properties of certain close-to-convex harmonic mappings Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Xiao-Yuan Wang, Zhi-Gang Wang ∗ , Jin-Hua Fan and Zhen-Yong Hu

Abstract
. In this paper, we determine the sharp estimates for Toeplitz determinants of a subclass of close-to-convex harmonic mappings. Moreover, we obtain an improved version of Bohr’s inequalities for a subclass of close-to-convex harmonic mappings, whose analytic parts are Ma-Minda convex functions.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 30C45, 30C80, 30A10.

1.
Introduction

A complex-valued function
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z:|z|<1\}
is called a harmonic mapping
if
\Delta f=4f_{z\overline{z}}=0
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of sense-preserving harmonic mappings
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
, where

(1.1)

\ h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\ \text{and}\ g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}

are
analytic
functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of univalent mappings.
We observe that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
reduces to the class
\mathcal{S}
of normalized univalent analytic functions,
if the co-analytic part
g\equiv 0
. Denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
the close-to-convex subclass of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
.
If
b_{1}=0
, then the class
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
reduces to
\mathcal{K}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Lewy
[
37
]
proved that
f=h+\overline{g}
is locally univalent
in
\mathbb{D}
if and only if the Jacobian
J_{f}=\left|h^{\prime}\right|^{2}-\left|g^{\prime}\right|^{2}\neq 0
in
\mathbb{D}
. Noting that the harmonic
mapping
f
is sense-preserving, i.e.
J_{f}>0
or

\left|h^{\prime}\right|>\left|g^{\prime}\right|
in
\mathbb{D}
.
At this point, its dilatation
\omega_{f}=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
has the property

\left|\omega_{f}\right|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. The reader can find much information about planar harmonic
mappings from
[
18
,
22
,
46
]
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the class of analytic functions
p
in
\mathbb{D}
of the form

(1.2)

p(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}z^{n}

such that
\operatorname{Re}(p(z))>0
in
\mathbb{D}.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the class of analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)-1=0
, and
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
denotes the class of functions
f\in\mathcal{A}
such that

(1.3)

{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)>\alpha\quad\left(-\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha<1;\ z\in\mathbb{D}\right).

Particularly, the elements in
\mathcal{K}(-1/2)
are close-to-convex but are not necessarily
starlike in
\mathbb{D}
. For
0\leq\alpha<1
, the elements in
\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
are known to be convex functions of order
\alpha
in
\mathbb{D}
.
For more properties of starlike and convex functions, the reader can refer to the
monographs
[
23
,
53
]
.

By making use of the subordination in analytic functions, Ma and Minda
[
42
]
introduced a more general class

\mathcal{C}(\phi)
, consisting of functions in
\mathcal{S}
for which

1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\phi(z).

Here the function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
called Ma-Minda function, is analytic and univalent in
\mathbb{D}
such that
\phi(\mathbb{D})
has
positive real part, symmetric with respect to the real axis, starlike with respect to
\phi(0)=1

and
\phi^{\prime}(0)>0
(for more details, see
[
57
,
50
]
). A Ma-Minda function has the form

\phi(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}z^{n}.

The extremal function
K
for the class
\mathcal{C}(\phi)
is given by

(1.4)

K(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{\zeta}\frac{\phi(t)-1}{t}dt\right)d\zeta\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

which satisfies the condition

1+\frac{zK^{\prime\prime}(z)}{K^{\prime}(z)}=\phi(z).

We recall the following natural class
of close-to-convex harmonic mappings
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
, due to Wang
et al.

[
56
]
(see also
[
47
,
55
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be in the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
if

h\in\mathcal{K}(\alpha)
, for some
\alpha\in\left[-{1}/{2},1\right)
, given by (
1.3
) and
g
satisfies the condition

(1.5)

g^{\prime}(z)=\zeta z^{n}h^{\prime}(z)\quad\left(\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\ {\rm with}\ \left|\zeta\right|\leq\frac{1}{2n-1};\,n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}:=\{1,2,3,\cdots\}\right).

For
n=1
,
\alpha=-{1}/{2}
and
|\zeta|=1
, the class
\mathcal{M}(-{1}/{2},\zeta,1)
was introduced by Bharanedhar and Ponnusamy
[
12
]
. For
n=1
, the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,1)
was studied in
[
9
,
52
]
.

In 2020, Allu and Halder
[
9
]
introduced and investigated the following subclass
\mathcal{HC}(\phi)

of close-to-convex harmonic mappings.

Definition 1.2
.

For
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\zeta|\leq 1
, let
\mathcal{HC}(\phi)
denote the class of harmonic mappings
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathbb{D}
of the form (
1.1
), whose analytic part
h
belongs to
\mathcal{C}(\phi)
and
h^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, along with the condition
g^{\prime}(z)=\zeta zh^{\prime}(z)
.

Motivated essentially by the classes
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
and
\mathcal{HC}(\phi)
, we define a new subclass
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
of close-to-convex harmonic mappings as follows:

Definition 1.3
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{H}
is said to be in the class
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
if

h\in\mathcal{C}(\phi)
and
g
satisfies the condition (
1.5
).

In 2019, Sun
et\ al.

[
51
]
investigated upper bounds of the third Hankel determinants for the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,1,1)
of
close-to-convex harmonic mappings.
In recent years, the Toeplitz determinants and Hankel determinants of functions in the
class
\mathcal{S}
or its subclasses have attracted many
researchers’ attention (see
[
11
,
19
,
33
,
28
,
17
,
20
,
35
,
34
,
36
,
29
]
).
Among them, the symmetric Toeplitz determinant
|T_{q}(n)|
for subclasses of
\mathcal{S}
with small values of
n

and
q
, are investigated by
[
10
,
7
,
49
,
2
,
54
,
58
]
.

The symmetric Toeplitz determinant
T_{q}(n)
for analytic functions
f
is defined
as follows:

T_{q}(n)[f]:=\begin{vmatrix}a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\cdots&a_{n+q-1}\\
a_{n+1}&a_{n}&\cdots&a_{n+q-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\\
a_{n+q-1}&a_{n+q-2}&\cdots&a_{n}\end{vmatrix},

where
n,q\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}
and
a_{1}=1
. In particular, for functions in starlike and convex classes,

T_{2}(2)[f],\,T_{3}(1)[f]
and
T_{3}(2)[f]
were studied by Ali
et\ al.

[
7
]
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the class of analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
|f(z)|<1

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and let
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\{f\in\mathcal{B}:f(0)=0\}
. In
1914
,
Bohr
[
16
]
proved that if
f\in\mathcal{B}
is of the form
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,
then the majorant series
M_{f}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}
of
f
satisfies

(1.6)

M_{f_{0}}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}\leq 1-|a_{0}|=d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
|z|=r\leq 1/3
, where
f_{0}(z)=f(z)-f(0)
. Bohr actually
obtained the inequality (
1.6
) for
|z|\leq 1/6
. Moreover, Wiener, Riesz and Schur,
independently, established the Bohr inequality (
1.6
) for
|z|\leq 1/3

(known as Bohr radius for the class
\mathcal{B}
) and proved that
1/3
is the best possible.

The Bohr phenomenon was reappeared in the
1990
s due to Dixon
[
21
]
. Furthermore, Boas and Khavinson
[
15
]

found
bounds for Bohr’s radius in any complete Reinhard domains.
Other works one can see
[
45
,
14
,
44
,
3
,
4
]
.
In recent years, Bohr inequality and Bohr radius have become an active research field in geometric function theory
(see
[
8
,
6
,
31
,
38
,
43
,
40
,
27
]
).
Furthermore, initiated by the work of
[
32
]
, the Bohr’s phenomenon for the complex-valued harmonic mappings have been widely studied
(see
[
1
,
9
,
25
,
26
,
30
,
41
]
).

In this paper,
we aim at determining the sharp estimates for Toeplitz determinants of the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
.
Moreover, we will derive an improved version of Bohr’s inequalities for the class
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
.

2.
Preliminary results

To prove our main results, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
23
, p. 41]
)
For a function
p\in\mathcal{P}
of the form (
1.2
), the sharp
inequality
\left|p_{n}\right|\leq 2
holds for each
n\geq 1.
Equality holds for the function

p(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z}.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
24
, Theorem 1]
)
Let
p\in\mathcal{P}
be of the form (
1.2
) and
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\left|p_{n}-\mu p_{k}p_{n-k}\right|\leq 2\max\{1,\,|2\mu-1|\}\quad(1\leq k\leq n-1).

If
|2\mu-1|\geq 1
, then the inequality is sharp for the function

p(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z}

or its rotations. If
|2\mu-1|<1
, then the inequality is sharp for

p(z)=\frac{1+z^{n}}{1-z^{n}}

or its rotations.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
56
]
)

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
. Then
the coefficients
a_{k}\ (k\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}\setminus\{1\})
of
h
satisfy

(2.1)

\left|a_{k}\right|\leq\frac{1}{k!}\prod_{j=2}^{k}(j-2\alpha)\ (k\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}\setminus\{1\}).

Moreover, the coefficients
b_{k}\ (k=n+1,n+2,\cdots;n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$})
of
g
satisfy

(2.2)

\left|b_{n+1}\right|\leq\frac{\left|\zeta\right|}{n+1}\ \text{ and }\ \left|b_{k+n}\right|\leq\frac{\left|\zeta\right|}{(k+n)(k-1)!}\prod_{j=2}^{k}(j-2\alpha)\ (k\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}\setminus\{1\};\,n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}).

The bounds are sharp for the extremal function given by

(2.3)

f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dt}{(1-\delta t)^{2-2\alpha}}+\overline{\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\zeta t^{n}}{(1-\delta t)^{2-2\alpha}}dt}\quad(\left|\delta\right|=1;\,z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}).

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
56
]
)

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
with
0\leq\alpha<1
and
0\leq\zeta<\frac{1}{2n-1}\ (n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$})
.
Then

(2.4)

\Phi(r;\alpha,\zeta,n)\leq\left|f(z)\right|\leq\Psi(r;\alpha,\zeta,n)\quad(r=|z|<1),

where

\Phi(r;\alpha,\zeta,n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log(1+r)-\displaystyle\frac{\zeta\,r^{n+1}\,_{2}F_{1}(1,\,n+1;\,n+2;\,-r)}{n+1}&(\alpha=1/2),\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{(1+r)^{2\alpha-1}-1}{2\alpha-1}-\frac{\zeta\,r^{n+1}\,_{2}F_{1}(n+1,\,2-2\alpha;\,n+2;\,-r)}{n+1}&(\alpha\neq 1/2),\end{array}\right.

and

\Psi(r;\alpha,\zeta,n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\log(1-r)+\displaystyle\frac{\zeta\,r^{n+1}\,_{2}F_{1}(1,\,n+1;\,n+2;\,r)}{n+1}&(\alpha=1/2),\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-r)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}+\frac{\zeta\,r^{n+1}\,_{2}F_{1}(n+1,\,2-2\alpha;\,n+2;\,r)}{n+1}&(\alpha\neq 1/2).\end{array}\right.

All these bounds are sharp, the extremal function is
f_{\alpha,\zeta,n}=h_{\alpha}+\overline{g_{\alpha,\zeta,n}}

or its rotations, where

(2.5)

f_{\alpha,\zeta,n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\log(1-z)+\displaystyle\overline{\frac{\zeta\,z^{n+1}\,_{2}F_{1}(1,\,n+1;\,n+2;\,z)}{n+1}}&(\alpha=1/2),\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-z)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1}+\overline{\frac{\zeta\,z^{n+1}\,_{2}F_{1}(n+1,\,2-2\alpha;\,n+2;\,z)}{n+1}}&(\alpha\neq 1/2).\end{array}\right.

The following two results are due to Ma and Minda
[
42
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(\phi)
. Then
zf^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\prec zK^{\prime\prime}(z)/K^{\prime}(z)
and
f^{\prime}(z)\prec K^{\prime}(z)
, where
K
is given by (
1.4
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that
f\in\mathcal{C}(\phi)
and
|z|=r<1
. Then

(2.6)

K^{\prime}(-r)\leq|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq K^{\prime}(r),

where
K
is given by (
1.4
). Equality holds for some
z\neq 0
if and only if
f
is a rotation of
K
.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
[
13
]
)

Let
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
be two analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
and
g\prec f
. Then

(2.7)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}

for
|z|=r\leq 1/3.

Remark 2.1
.

Lemma
2.7
continues to hold for quasi-subordination (cf.
[
5
]
). Moreover, the
bound 1/3 is optimal as shown by
[
48
, Lemma 1]
.

3.
Toeplitz determinants for the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)

In this section, we will give several sharp estimates for Toeplitz determinants
|T_{q}(n)[\cdot]|
of
functions in the class
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
. Then

(3.1)

|T_{2}(n)[h]|\leq\left(\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{j=2}^{n}(j-2\alpha)\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}(j-2\alpha)\right)^{2}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{1\}),

and

(3.2)

|T_{2}(n)[g]|\leq\frac{1}{[(2n-1)(n+1)]^{2}}.

The inequalities in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are sharp for the extremal function given by (
2.3
).

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
. By Lemma
2.3
, we see that

(3.3)

|T_{2}(n)[h]|=|a_{n}^{2}-a_{n+1}^{2}|\leq|a_{n}^{2}|+|a_{n+1}^{2}|

yields (
3.1
). Equality in (
3.3
) holds for the function
h
given by

(3.4)

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dt}{(1-\delta t)^{2-2\alpha}}

\displaystyle=z+\frac{1}{2}(2-2\alpha)\delta^{2}z^{2}+\frac{1}{6}(2-2\alpha)(3-2\alpha)\delta^{3}z^{3}+\frac{1}{24}(2-2\alpha)(3-2\alpha)(4-2\alpha)\delta^{4}z^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{120}(2-2\alpha)(3-2\alpha)(4-2\alpha)(5-2\alpha)\delta^{5}z^{5}+\cdots\quad(\left|\delta\right|=1;\,z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}).

By virtue of (
2.2
), we get the assertion
(
3.2
). The proof of Theorem
3.1
is thus completed.
∎

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,2)

(3.5)

|T_{2}(2)[h]|\leq\frac{2}{9}(1-\alpha)^{2}\left(2\alpha^{2}-6\alpha+9\right),

The inequalities in (

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,2)
. Then

(3.16)

|T_{3}(2)[h]|\leq\begin{cases}{\frac{1}{108}\left(1-\alpha)^{3}(2\alpha^{2}-7\alpha+12)(10\alpha^{2}-27\alpha+36\right)\quad(-\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{7}}),\\
\\[-12.0pt]
{\frac{5}{108}\left(1-\alpha)^{3}(2\alpha^{2}-7\alpha+12)(2\alpha^{2}-4\alpha+7\right)\qquad\ \,(\frac{1}{7}\leq\alpha<1}),\end{cases}

and

(3.17)

|T_{3}(2)[g]|=|2b_{3}^{2}b_{4}|\leq\frac{1}{243}\left(1-\alpha\right).

The inequality in (
3.16
) is sharp for the function
h
given by (
3.4
), and
the inequality in (
3.17
) is sharp for the function
g
defined by

(3.18)

g(z)={\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\zeta t^{2}}{(1-\delta t)^{2-2\alpha}}dt}\quad\left(\left|\delta\right|=1;\,\left|\zeta\right|\leq\frac{1}{3};\,z\in\mbox{$\mathbb{D}$}\right).

.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,2)
. It follows that

T_{3}(2)[h]=\left(a_{2}-a_{4}\right)\left(a_{2}^{2}-2a_{3}^{2}+a_{2}a_{4}\right).

In view of (
3.11
) and Lemma
2.1
, we find that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\left|a_{2}-a_{4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|a_{2}\right|+\left|a_{4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{2}(1-\alpha)p_{1}\right|+\left|\frac{1}{24}(1-\alpha)\left[(1-\alpha)^{2}p_{1}^{3}+3(1-\alpha)p_{1}p_{2}+2p_{3}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\left(1-\alpha)(2\alpha^{2}-7\alpha+12\right).

Next, we shall maximize
\left|a_{2}^{2}-2a_{3}^{2}+a_{2}a_{4}\right|
.
With the help of (
3.11
), Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, we get

(3.20)

\displaystyle|a_{2}^{2}-2a_{3}^{2}+a_{2}a_{4}|

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}}{144}|-5(1-\alpha)^{2}p_{1}^{4}+36p_{1}^{2}-7(1-\alpha)p_{1}^{2}p_{2}-8p_{2}^{2}+6p_{1}p_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)^{2}}{144}\left[5(1-\alpha)^{2}|p_{1}|^{4}+36|p_{1}|^{2}+8|p_{2}|^{2}+6|p_{1}|\left|p_{3}-\frac{7}{6}(1-\alpha)p_{1}p_{2}\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}{\frac{1}{18}\big(1-\alpha)^{2}\big(10\alpha^{2}-27\alpha+36\big)\quad\ \,(-\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{1}{7}}),\\
\\[-12.0pt]
{\frac{5}{18}\big(1-\alpha)^{2}\big(2\alpha^{2}-4\alpha+7\big)\quad\qquad(\frac{1}{7}\leq\alpha<1}).\end{cases}

Therefore, combining (
3.19
) with (
3.20
), we obtain (
3.16
).
By noting that for
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,2)
, we have

(3.21)

\begin{cases}b_{3}=\frac{1}{3}\zeta a_{1},\\
\\[-12.0pt]
b_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\zeta a_{2}.\\
\end{cases}

By means of Lemma
2.1
, we get the assertion (
3.17
).
The sharpness of (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) are similar to that of Theorem
3.2
, we choose to omit the details here.
∎

|T_{2}(2)[h]|\leq 2

|T_{3}(1)[h]|\leq 4

4.
Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)

In this section, we firstly give
the sharp growth estimate for the class
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. Then

(4.1)

L(\zeta,n,r)\leq|f(z)|\leq R(\zeta,n,r),

where

(4.2)

L(\zeta,n,r)=-K(-r)-|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}K^{\prime}(-t)dt,

and

(4.3)

R(\zeta,n,r)=K(r)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}K^{\prime}(t)dt.

The bounds are sharp for the extremal function
f_{\zeta}=h_{\zeta}+\overline{g_{\zeta}}
with
h_{\zeta}=K
, where
K
satisfies (
1.4
)
or its rotations and
g_{\zeta}
satisfies
g^{\prime}_{\zeta}=\zeta z^{n}h^{\prime}_{\zeta}
.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. By Lemma
2.6
, we know that

(4.4)

K^{\prime}(-r)\leq|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq K^{\prime}(r)\quad(|z|=r).

Let
\gamma
be the linear segment joining
0
to
z
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then, we see that

(4.5)

\displaystyle|f(z)|=\left|\int_{\gamma}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\,\,d\theta+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{\theta}}\,\,d\overline{\theta}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma}\left(|h^{\prime}(\theta)|+|g^{\prime}(\theta)|\right)\,|d\theta|=\int_{\gamma}\left(1+|\zeta||\theta|^{n}\right)|h^{\prime}(\theta)|\,|d\theta|.

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we obtain

(4.6)

|f(z)|\leq\int_{0}^{r}\left(1+|\zeta|t^{n}\right)K^{\prime}(t)\,dt=K(r)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}K^{\prime}(t)\,\,dt=R(\zeta,n,r).

Let
\Gamma
be the preimage of the line segment joining
0
to
f(z)
under the function
f
. It follows that

(4.7)

\displaystyle|f(z)|=\left|\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\,d\theta+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{\theta}}\,\,d\overline{\theta}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Gamma}\left(|h^{\prime}(\theta)|-|g^{\prime}(\theta)|\right)\,|d\theta|

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\left(1-|\zeta||\theta|^{n}\right)|h^{\prime}(\theta)|\,|d\theta|.

From (
4.4
) and (
4.7
), we have

(4.8)

|f(z)|\geq\int_{0}^{r}\left(1-|\zeta|t^{n}\right)K^{\prime}(-t)\,dt=-K(-r)-|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}K^{\prime}(-t)\,dt=L(\zeta,n,r).

In view of (
4.6
) and (
4.8
), we deduce that

(4.9)

L(\zeta,n,r)\leq|f(z)|\leq R(\zeta,n,r).

To show the sharpness, we consider the function
f_{\zeta}=h_{\zeta}+\overline{g_{\zeta}}
with
h_{\zeta}=K
or its rotations.
It is easy to see that
h_{\zeta}=K\in\mathcal{C}(\phi)
and
g_{\zeta}
satisfies
g^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z)=\zeta z^{n}h^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z)
, which shows that
f_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. The equality holds on both sides of (
4.4
) for suitable rotations of
K
. For
0\leq\zeta<1/{(2n-1)}
, we see that
f_{\zeta}(r)=R(\zeta,n,r)
and
f_{\zeta}(-r)=-L(\zeta,n,r)
. Hence
|f_{\zeta}(r)|=R(\zeta,n,r)
and
|f_{\zeta}(-r)|=L(\zeta,n,r)
. This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
and
S_{r}
be the area of the image
f(\mathbb{D}_{r})

{\rm(}\mathbb{D}_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\leq 1\}{\rm)}
.
Then

(4.10)

2\pi\int_{0}^{r}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(-t))^{2}\,dt\leq S_{r}\leq 2\pi\int_{0}^{r}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}\,dt.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. Then, the area of image of
\mathbb{D}_{r}
under a harmonic mapping
f
is given by

(4.11)

S_{r}=\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\left(|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\right)\,dxdy=\iint_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}|z|^{2n}\right)|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dxdy.

Since
h\in\mathcal{C}(\phi)
, in view of (
4.4
) and (
4.11
), we have

(4.12)

\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi}t\left(1-|\alpha|^{2}t^{2}\right)(K^{\prime}(-t))^{2}d\theta dt\leq S_{r}\leq\int_{0}^{r}\int_{0}^{2\pi}t\left(1-|\alpha|^{2}t^{2}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}d\theta dt.

Therefore, the assertion (
4.10
) of Proposition
4.2
follows directly from (
4.12
).
∎

In what follows, we derive the Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. Then the majorant series of
f
satisfies the inequality

(4.13)

|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|z|^{n}\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for
|z|=r\leq\min\{1/3,r_{f}\}
, where
r_{f}
is the smallest positive root in
(0,1)
of

L(\zeta,n,1)=M_{K}(r)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}M_{K^{\prime}}(t)\,dt,

and
L(\zeta,n,1)
is given by (
4.2
) with
r=1
.

Proof.

Let
f=h+\overline{g}\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. Since
h\in\mathcal{C}(\phi)
, from Lemma
2.5
, we know that

(4.14)

h^{\prime}\prec K^{\prime}.

Let
K(z)=z+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}k_{n}z^{n}
.
In view of Lemma
2.7
and (
4.14
), we have

(4.15)

1+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|r^{n-1}=M_{h^{\prime}}(r)\leq M_{K^{\prime}}(r)=1+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}n|k_{n}|r^{n-1}

for
|z|=r\leq 1/3
. By integrating (
4.15
) with respect to
r
from
0
to
r
, we get

(4.16)

M_{h}(r)=r+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq r+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}|k_{n}|r^{n}=M_{K}(r)\quad(r\leq 1/3).

From the definition of
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
, we know that

g^{\prime}(z)=\zeta z^{n}h^{\prime}(z).

This relationship along with (
4.15
) yields

(4.17)

\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|r^{n-1}=M_{g^{\prime}}(r)=|\zeta|r^{n}M_{h^{\prime}}(r)\leq|\zeta|r^{n}M_{K^{\prime}}(r)\quad(r\leq 1/3).

By integrating (
4.17
) with respect to
r
from
0
to
r
, it follows that

(4.18)

M_{g}(r)=\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}M_{K^{\prime}}(t)dt\quad(r\leq 1/3).

Therefore, for
|z|=r\leq 1/3
, from (
4.16
) and (
4.18
), we obtain

(4.19)

M_{f}(r)=|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq M_{K}(r)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}M_{K^{\prime}}(t)dt=R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r).

In view of (
4.1
), it is evident that the Euclidean distance between
f(0)
and the boundary of
f(\mathbb{D})
is given by

(4.20)

d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))=\liminf\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)-f(0)|\geq L(\zeta,n,1).

We note that
R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r)\leq L(\zeta,n,1)
whenever
r\leq r_{f}
, where
r_{f}
is the smallest positive root of
R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r)=L(\zeta,n,1)
in
(0,1)
. Let

H_{1}(n,r)=R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r)-L(\zeta,n,1).

Then
H_{1}(n,r)
is a continuous function in
[0,1]
. Since

M_{K}(r)\geq K(r)>-K(-r),

it follows that

(4.21)

\displaystyle H_{1}(n,1)

\displaystyle=R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,1)-L(\zeta,n,1)

\displaystyle=M_{K}(1)+K(-1)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}\left(M_{K^{\prime}}(t)+K^{\prime}(t)\right)dt

\displaystyle\geq K(1)+K(-1)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}\left(M_{K^{\prime}}(t)+K^{\prime}(t)\right)dt>0.

On the other hand,

(4.22)

H_{1}(n,0)=-L(\zeta,n,1)=K(-1)(1-|\zeta|)+n|\zeta|\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}K(-t)\,dt<0.

Therefore,
H_{1}
has a root in
(0,1)
. Let
r_{f}
be the smallest root of
H_{1}
in
(0,1)
. Then
R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r)\leq L(\zeta,n,1)
for
r\leq r_{f}
. Now, in view of the inequalities (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) with the relationship
R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r)\leq L(\zeta,n,1)
for
r\leq r_{f}
, we obtain

|z|+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r^{n}\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for
|z|=r\leq\min\{1/3,r_{f}\}
.
∎

For a particular choice of
\phi
in Theorem
4.1
, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
with
0\leq\alpha<1
and
0\leq\zeta<1/(2n-1)
. Then the inequality (
4.13
) holds for
|z|=r\leq r_{f}
, where
r_{f}
is the smallest root in
(0,1)
of

F_{n}(r):=R(\alpha,\zeta,n,r)-L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)=0.

The radius
r_{f}
is sharp.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.4
,
the Euclidean distance between
f(0)
and the boundary of
f(\mathbb{D})
shows that

(4.23)

d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))=\liminf\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)-f(0)|\geq L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1).

We note that
r_{f}
is the root of the equation
R(\alpha,\zeta,n,r)=L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)
in
(0,1)
. The existence of the root is ensured by the relation
R(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)>L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)
with (
2.4
). For
0<r\leq r_{f}
, it is evident that
R(\alpha,\zeta,n,r)\leq L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)
. In view of Lemma
2.3
and (
4.23
), for
|z|=r\leq r_{f}
, we have

\displaystyle|z|+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|z|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq r_{f}+(|a_{2}|+|b_{2}|)r_{f}^{2}+\sum\limits_{n=3}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r_{f}^{n}

\displaystyle=R(\alpha,\zeta,n,r_{f})\leq L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})).

To show the sharpness of the radius
r_{f}
, we consider the function
f=f_{\alpha,\zeta,n}
, which is defined in Lemma
2.4
. We see that
f_{\alpha,\zeta,n}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}(\alpha,\zeta,n)
. Since the left side of the growth inequality in Lemma
2.4
holds for
f=f_{\alpha,\zeta,n}
or its rotations, we have
d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))=L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)
. Therefore, the function
f=f_{\alpha,\zeta,n}
for
|z|=r_{f}
gives

\displaystyle|z|+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)|z|^{n}

\displaystyle=r_{f}+(|a_{2}|+|b_{2}|)r_{f}^{2}+\sum\limits_{n=3}^{\infty}(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)r_{f}^{n}

\displaystyle=R(\alpha,\zeta,n,r_{f})=L(\alpha,\zeta,n,1)=d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})),

which reveals that the radius
r_{f}
is the best possible. ∎

Now, we give an improved version of Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
. By adding area
quantity
{S_{r}}/{(2\pi)}
with the majorant series of
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
, the sum is still less
than
d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))
for some radius
r\leq\min\{1/3,\widetilde{r}_{f}\}<1
.

Note that the additional term such as
{S_{r}}/{(2\pi)}
to the majorant sum was first mooted by Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
31
]
to refine and improve the Bohr inequality. This variation of Bohr inequality was proved for harmonic mappings in
[
25
]
. Subsequently, several extensions were made by many authors (cf.
[
39
]
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
and
S_{r}
be the area of the image
f(\mathbb{D}_{r})
. Then the inequality

M_{f}(r)+\frac{S_{r}}{2\pi}\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

holds for
|z|=r\leq\min\{1/3,\widetilde{r}_{f}\}
, where
\widetilde{r}_{f}
is the smallest positive root in
(0,1)
of

L(\zeta,n,1)=M_{K}(r)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}M_{K^{\prime}}(t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{r}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}dt,

and
L(\zeta,n,1)
is given by (
4.2
) with
r=1
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{HC}_{n}(\phi)
be of the form (
1.1
). Then, from the right hand inequality in (
4.10
) and (
4.19
), we obtain

(4.24)

\displaystyle M_{f}(r)+\frac{S_{r}}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\leq M_{K}(r)+|\zeta|\int_{0}^{r}t^{n}M_{K^{\prime}}(t)\,dt+\int_{0}^{r}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,r)+\int_{0}^{r}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2n}t^{2}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}\,dt=\widetilde{R}_{f}(n,r)

for
r\leq 1/3
. Suppose that
H_{2}(n,r)=\widetilde{R}_{f}(n,r)-L(\zeta,n,1)
. Then
H_{2}(n,r)
is a continuous function in
[0,1]
. The inequality (
4.22
) yields that
H_{2}(n,0)=-L(\zeta,n,1)<0
. By virtue of (
4.21
), we get

(4.25)

R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,1)-L(\zeta,n,1)>0.

For
|\zeta|\leq 1/(2n-1)
, we observe that

t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}\geq 0,

and hence

(4.26)

\int_{0}^{r}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}dt\geq 0.

From (
4.24
) and (
4.25
), we obtain

H_{2}(n,1)=R_{\mathcal{C}}(n,1)-L(\zeta,n,1)+\int_{0}^{1}t\left(1-|\zeta|^{2}t^{2n}\right)(K^{\prime}(t))^{2}\,dt>0.

Since
H_{2}(n,0)<0
and
H_{2}(n,1)>0
,
H_{2}
has a root in
(0,1)
and choose
\widetilde{r}_{f}
to be the smallest root in
(0,1)
, we know that
\widetilde{R}_{f}(n,r)\leq L(\zeta,n,1)
for
r\leq\widetilde{r}_{f}
. Therefore, by virtue of (
4.20
) and (
4.24
), we conclude that

M_{f}(r)+\frac{S_{r}}{2\pi}\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for
r\leq\min\{1/3,\widetilde{r}_{f}\}
. ∎

in Theorems

.

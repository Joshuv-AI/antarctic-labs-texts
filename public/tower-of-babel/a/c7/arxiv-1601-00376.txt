Variable Modified Newtonian Mechanics I : The Early Universe
C. C. Wong, R.J. Rivers*

Abstract
As it stands, in modelling galaxy structure formation, neither Cold Dark Matter ( Λ \Lambda CDM) nor canonical (Milgrom milgrom ) Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) can easily accommodate the recently discovered appearance of massive galaxies at early times mcgaugh2024 . In this paper we propose a new single-metric universe which, although it fits within the framework of MOND in that there is no new matter in the stress-momentum tensor, favours a stronger MOND-like force law at very early time which may help explain this galactic formation. In particular, the phenomenological acceleration scale a 0 a_{0} of canonical MOND, below which Newtonian gravity is modified milgrom , now depends on the background matter density. As such, the additional strong MOND-like force at high red-shifts diminishes to below Newtonian gravity at solar system scales at late time. This removes the problem with canonical MOND which finds difficulty at solar system Oort-cloud scales at late times tremaine . Our model is a generalised (single-metric) vacuole model without discrete boundaries, and the ability to recover Newtonian gravity at solar system scales provides an acid test. Some years ago Baker baker proposed a related model which interpolated smoothly between the Schwarzschild metric at small scales and the Friedmann-Lema i ^ \hat{i} tre-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric at large scales. The resulting modified Newtonian mechanics (MOND) was incompatible with solar system data. We have found a unique solution within the the same construction scheme as baker which both reproduces Newtonian solar-system data and which generalises MOND as indicated above. It requires no parameters beyond the existing Schwarzschild and FLRW metrics. This is not a universal panacea to mass discrepancy problems but, we suggest, presents a new baseline for calculation.

V
Summary

Given a central point mass in an expanding background, we have shown that it is possible to avoid the extremes of vacuole scenarios and introduce non-Newtonian mechanics in a natural way. The resulting metric has both
the Schwarzschild-Lema
\hat{i}
tre metric and FLRW metric as its asymptotic solutions, with no further parameters and no additional contributions to the stress-energy tensor. This solution smoothes out the implicit vacuoles by modifying the geodesic equation to include a non-Newtonian acceleration appropriate to the interpolating regime which we have termed VMOND. Although the non-Newtonian potential leads to an apparent mass density in the Poisson equation, this is a purely dynamical effect and produces no pressure term. Specifically, the free fall velocity is obtained by simply adding the (negative) Newtonian free fall velocity to the velocity of the Hubble flow, and not their accelerations. In comparison to canonical MOND
milgrom
the acceleration
a_{0}
which marks the boundary between Newtonian and Non-Newtonian behaviour is no longer constant, but depends on the background matter density.

To test the formalism in its simplest form, an immediate consequence for the low-density late-time solar system is that the equilibrium (or turn-around) distance, at which Schwarzschild attraction matches FLRW expansion is bigger than the extended solar system, at
10^{7}\,AU
. This is three orders of magnitude larger than the equilibrium distance for canonical MOND
milgrom
, which falls within the outer Oort cloud and which, contrary to the predictions of simple MOND, arguably behaves in a Newtonian fashion.

At the other extreme of high background density and early time, we explored whether this modified Newtonian equation is useful in mass deficit situations (which requires some astrophysical assumptions beyond the simple metric). In galactic over-density evolution, we find that the non-Newtonian acceleration leads to an early time over-density growth rate
\delta\propto a(t)^{2}
, which could lead to a high turnaround redshift
z_{ta}
and therefore first closest approach (also at high redshift). This could provide enough time for the overdensity to virialise before
z=6.5
, which could contribute to the early appearance of massive galaxies. (JWST observations indicate that a large number of galaxies are already formed by
z=6.5
and with no change of morphology after.)

In fact, we could have gone further. One of us has shown elsewhere
wong
that a Milky Way mass overdensity picks up a growing systematic angular momentum per unit mass before it turnarounds. Assuming the overdensity sphere virialises before it further collapses along the direction of angular momentum and equilibriates to a galactic disk, we find that the disk rotating speed at large radius follows the Tully Fisher relation with an acceleration scale that is close to the MOND acceleration
a_{0}
. In additional, the rotating curves matches the Gaia data very closely.

It is not immediately clear that our new metric avoids anisotropy, which was Baker’s
baker
touchstone for a sensible metric (and which led to his choice
\eta=3
in contrast to our
\eta=3/2
). In the FLRW metric, for an observer in the non-expanding comoving coordinate
\varrho
, a matter dominant universe will appear uniform and isotropic with constant density and zero pressure. Once a significant point mass at the observer is included, the underlying metric is modified. The new metric prescribes a physical distance
r=aL\neq a\varrho
for a free falling particle shell.
As the free-fall radial distance (also for
L
) reduces, the cosmological gravitational mass inside
L
reduces, which could lead to additional pressure. However, for an overdensity evolution at early time and late time, we notice that either in linear or Newtonian perturbation theory and in the late time pragmatic ”Jeans Swindle”, the evolution process is assumed adiabatic (without entropy change). This pressure term then becomes zero. We have followed this common assumption in our model.

As far as we are aware, this is the only model which could produce significant non-Newtonian gravity without modifying Einstein’s Gravity or postulating invisible particles. Given the problems we have with our current cosmological models, we could see our model as providing a new baseline for calculation.

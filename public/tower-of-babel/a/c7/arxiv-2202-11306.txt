1.
Introduction

In his famous paper [6] of 1749, Euler discovered a method of computing values of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
at negative integers, although he never found a convergent analytic expression for
\zeta(s)
at negative integers.
In fact, he introduced the Euler zeta function
\eta(s)
given by

\displaystyle\eta(s)=1-\frac{1}{2^{s}}+\frac{1}{3^{s}}-\frac{1}{4^{s}}+\cdots.

This series converges for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
. The function
\eta(s)
can be analytically continued to the whole complex plane. It is related to the
\zeta(s)
by
\eta(s)=(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s)
, for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. For the purpose of computing
\eta(-n)
, for
n=0,1,2,\dots
, he was led to introduce the Eulerian polynomials
A_{n}(t)
by

\displaystyle\frac{A_{n}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}(j+1)^{n},

the coefficients of which are the Eulerian numbers.
Eulerian numbers and polynomials have long been studied due to their number-theoretic and combinatorial importance, since Euler’s introduction. There are many ways of defining the Eulerian polynomials and numbers, including the ones given in (a)-(h) and (k) in Proposition 3.2. Let
[n]=\left\{1,2,\dots,n\right\}
. A combinatorial interpretation of the Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(t)
is as follows. For each permutation
\sigma
in the symmetric group
S_{n}
, the descent and the excedance of
\sigma
are respectively given by

\displaystyle D(\sigma)=\left\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)\right\},

\displaystyle E(\sigma)=\left\{i\in[n-1]:\sigma(i)>i\right\}.

Then
A_{n}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle A_{n}(t)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}t^{d(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}t^{e(\sigma)},

where
d(\sigma)=|D(\sigma)|,e(\sigma)=|E(\sigma)|
(see [2,7,8,21,25]).

The Stirling number of the second
S_{2}(n,k)
is the number of ways to partition a set of
n
objects into
k
nonempty subsets. Eulerian numbers and the Stirling numbers of the second kind are closely related by the relation due to Frobenius (see Proposition 3.2, (k), (l)).

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
In [19], Koutras generalized the classical Eulerian numbers and polynomials to
p_{n}
-associated Eulerian numbers and polynomials with motivation of providing a unified approach to the study of Eulerian-related numbers and with combinatorial, probabilistic and statistical applications in mind. In addition, he mentioned in the same paper that a lot of special numbers, like the Stirling numbers of the second, the Lah numbers and Gould-Hopper numbers, appear in the expansions of some polynomials in terms of the falling factorials. This observation is the motivation and impetus for the present research on the Stirling numbers of both kinds associated with sequences of polynomials (see [19,20]).

The aim of this paper is to study the Stirling numbers of the second
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
associated with any sequence of polynomials
\mathbb{P}
, which are defined as the coefficients in the expansion of
p_{n}(x)
in terms of the falling factorials
(x)_{k}
. This is done with the help of umbral calculus technique. We illustrate our results with twenty examples. Moreover, we also introduce the Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
associated with any sequence of polynomials
\mathbb{P}
, which are defined as the coefficients in the expansion of
(x)_{n}
in terms of the polynomials
p_{k}(x)
. Again, we study them by means of umbral calculus and illustrate our results with the same twenty examples for the Striling numbers of the second kind.
We note here that the inverse relations for the ordinary Stirling numbers also hold for the Stirling numbers associated with sequences of polynomials
\mathbb{P}
. This observation applied to each individual case yields interesting orthogonality and inverse relations for the twenty examples, which are explicitly stated. For example, in the case of the sequence
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}(x)\right\}
of Bernoulli polynomials, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(m+1)_{k}}{m+1}S_{1}(n,m)S_{2}(j,l)B_{k-j}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}\frac{1}{l!}\binom{n}{j}\frac{(m+1)_{l}}{m+1}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)B_{n-j}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{1}(n,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{k}a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{2}(l,n)\binom{k}{l}B_{k-l}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{l=n}^{k}\frac{S_{1}(k,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{n}a_{k},

where
S_{1}(n,k),S_{2}(n,k),B_{n},(x)_{n}
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind, the Stirling numbers of the second kind, Bernoulli numbers and the falling factorials, respectively.
As another example, in the case of the sequence
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{[n]}\right\}
of central factorials we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)T_{2}(m,k)T_{1}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}T_{1}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)T_{2}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}T_{1}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)T_{2}(l,n)a_{k},

where
T_{1}(n,k)
and
T_{2}(n,k)
are the central factorial numbers of the first kind and the central factorial numbers of the second kind.
In this investigation of Stirling numbers, we are led to discover natural definitions for logarithms and exponentials associated to any delta series which become even clearer when we study the central factorial numbers of both kinds associated with
\mathbb{P}
in our forthcoming paper. In addition, we also go over the Eulerian numbers and polynomials associated with
\mathbb{P}
, of which some are not considered by Koutras [19].
The novelty of this paper is that it is the first paper which studies the Stirling numbers of both kinds associated with any sequence of polynomials in a unified and systematic way with the help of umbral calculus.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, we briefly go over umbral calculus. In Section 3, we introduce the Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with sequences of polynomials. We illustrate our results with twenty examples in Section 4. In Section 5, we introduce the Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials. We illustrate our results in Section 6 with the same examples from Section 4 and thereby get interesting inverse relations for each one. Finally, we conclude our paper in Section 7.

2.
Review of umbral calculus

Here we will briefly go over very basic facts about umbral calculus. For more details on this, we recommend the reader to refer to [1,5,23,24]. We remark that recently umbral calculus has been extended to the case of degenerate umbral calculus in order to treat degenerate special polynomials and numbers, which involves degenerate exponentials (see [12]).
Let
\mathbb{C}
be the field of complex numbers. Then
\mathcal{F}
denotes the algebra of formal power series in
t
over
\mathbb{C}
, given by

\mathcal{F}=\bigg\{f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}~\bigg|~a_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\bigg\},

and
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{C}[x]
indicates the algebra of polynomials in
x
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\mathbb{P}^{*}
be the vector space of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
. If
\langle L|p(x)\rangle
denotes the action of the linear functional
L
on the polynomial
p(x)
, then the vector space operations on
\mathbb{P}^{*}
are defined by

\langle L+M|p(x)\rangle=\langle L|p(x)\rangle+\langle M|p(x)\rangle,\quad\langle cL|p(x)\rangle=c\langle L|p(x)\rangle,

where
c
is a complex number.

For
f(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
\displaystyle f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\displaystyle
, we define the linear functional on
\mathbb{P}
by

(2.1)

\langle f(t)|x^{k}\rangle=a_{k}.

From (
2.1
), we note that

\langle t^{k}|x^{n}\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k},\quad(n,k\geq 0),

where
\delta_{n,k}
is the Kronecker’s symbol.

Some remarkable linear functionals are as follows:

\displaystyle\langle e^{yt}|p(x)\rangle=p(y),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle e^{yt}-1|p(x)\rangle=p(y)-p(0),

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\frac{e^{yt}-1}{t}\bigg|p(x)\bigg\rangle=\int_{0}^{y}p(u)du.

Let

(2.3)

f_{L}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle L|x^{k}\rangle\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

Then, by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we get

\langle f_{L}(t)|x^{n}\rangle=\langle L|x^{n}\rangle.

That is,
f_{L}(t)=L
, as linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
. In fact, the map
L\longmapsto f_{L}(t)
is a vector space isomorphism from
\mathbb{P}^{*}
onto
\mathcal{F}
.

Henceforth,
\mathcal{F}
denotes both the algebra of formal power series in
t
and the vector space of all linear functionals on
\mathbb{P}
.
\mathcal{F}
is called the umbral algebra and the umbral calculus is the study of umbral algebra.
For each nonnegative integer
k
, the differential operator
t^{k}
on
\mathbb{P}
is defined by

(2.4)

t^{k}x^{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(n)_{k}x^{n-k},&\textrm{if $k\leq n$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $k>n$.}\end{array}\right.

Extending (
2.4
) linearly, any power series

f(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}t^{k}\in\mathcal{F}

gives the differential operator on
\mathbb{P}
defined by

(2.5)

f(t)x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}a_{k}x^{n-k},\quad(n\geq 0).

It should be observed that, for any formal power series
f(t)
and any polynomial
p(x)
, we have

(2.6)

\langle f(t)|p(x)\rangle=\langle 1|f(t)p(x)\rangle=f(t)p(x)|_{x=0}.

Here we note that an element
f(t)
of
\mathcal{F}
is a formal power series, a linear functional and a differential operator. Some notable differential operators are as follows:

\displaystyle e^{yt}p(x)=p(x+y),

(2.7)

\displaystyle(e^{yt}-1)p(x)=p(x+y)-p(x),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{yt}-1}{t}p(x)=\int_{x}^{x+y}p(u)du.

The order
o(f(t))
of the power series
f(t)(\neq 0)
is the smallest integer for which
a_{k}
does not vanish. If
o(f(t))=0
, then
f(t)
is called an invertible series. If
o(f(t))=1
, then
f(t)
is called a delta series.

For
f(t),g(t)\in\mathcal{F}
with
o(f(t))=1
and
o(g(t))=0
, there exists a unique sequence
s_{n}(x)
(deg
s_{n}(x)=n
) of polynomials such that

(2.8)

\big\langle g(t)f(t)^{k}|s_{n}(x)\big\rangle=n!\delta_{n,k},\quad(n,k\geq 0).

The sequence
s_{n}(x)
is said to be the Sheffer sequence for
(g(t),f(t))
, which is denoted by
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. We observe from (
2.8
) that

(2.9)

s_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{g(t)}q_{n}(x),

where
q_{n}(x)=g(t)s_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t))
.

In particular, if
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),t)
, then
q_{n}(x)=x^{n}
, and hence

(2.10)

s_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{g(t)}x^{n}.

It is well known that
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
if and only if

(2.11)

\frac{1}{g\big(\overline{f}(t)\big)}e^{x\overline{f}(t)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{s_{k}(x)}{k!}t^{k},

for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\overline{f}(t)
is the compositional inverse of
f(t)
such that
\overline{f}(f(t))=f(\overline{f}(t))=t
.

The following equations (
2.12
), (
2.13
), and (
2.14
) are equivalent to the fact that
s_{n}\left(x\right)
is Sheffer for
\left(g\left(t\right),f\left(t\right)\right)
, for some invertible
g(t)
:

(2.12)

\displaystyle f\left(t\right)s_{n}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=ns_{n-1}\left(x\right),\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),

(2.13)

\displaystyle s_{n}\left(x+y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}s_{j}\left(x\right)q_{n-j}\left(y\right),

with
q_{n}\left(x\right)=g\left(t\right)s_{n}\left(x\right),

(2.14)

s_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\big\langle{g\left(\overline{f}\left(t\right)\right)^{-1}\overline{f}\left(t\right)^{j}}\big|{x^{n}\big\rangle}x^{j}.

If
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
, then the following recurrence relation holds:

(2.15)

s_{n+1}(x)=\bigg(x-\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\bigg)\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(t)}s_{n}(x).

Let
s_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. Then, for any polynomial
p(x)
, we have the polynomial exapnsion given by

(2.16)

\displaystyle p(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{g(t)f(t)^{k}|p(x)\big\rangle}s_{k}(x).

For any formal power series
h(t)
, any polynomial
p(x)
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

(2.17)

\displaystyle\langle{h(at)|p(x)\rangle}=\langle{h(t)|p(ax)\rangle}.

3.
Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with sequences of polynomials

We recall that the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2}(n,k)
are given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},

where the falling factorials
(x)_{n}
are defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad(x)_{0}=1.

More generally, for any real number
\lambda
, the generalized falling factorials are defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad(x)_{0,\lambda}=1.

Then, as a degenerate version of the Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are given by

(3.4)

\displaystyle(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
In (
3.1
) and (
3.4
), we observe that
S_{2}(n,k)
and
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
arise as the coefficients respectively when we expand
x^{n}
and
(x)_{n,\lambda}
in terms of
(x)_{k}
. In addition, the (unsigned) Lah number
L(n,k)
and Gould-Hopper number
G(n,k;r,s)
also appear as the coefficients in the expansion of the rising factorial
\langle{x\rangle}_{n}
and
(rx+s)_{n}
in terms of
(x)_{k}
, where
r\neq 0
. In view of this observation, it seems natural to define the
Stirling numbers of the second associated with
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
as the coefficients when we expand
p_{n}(x)
in terms of
(x)_{k}
:

(3.5)

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
, with
p_{n}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}p_{n,l}x^{l}
. We let

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}.

(a)   Then the Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
are given by

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|p_{n}(x)\rangle}.

More explicitly, it is given by

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)p_{n,l}.

(b)
p_{n,l}=\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1}(k,l)S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
.

(c)  Let
p_{n}(x)
be Sheffer for the pair
(g(t),f(t))
. Then the generating function of
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\bar{f}(t)}-1)^{k}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{\bar{f}(t)^{n}}{n!}.

(d),  Let
\bar{\mathbb{P}}=\left\{\bar{p}_{n}(x)\right\}
, where
\bar{p}_{n}(x)
is the sequence of polynomials defined by
\bar{p}_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\bar{p}_{n}(x)=xp_{n-1}(x),\,\,(n\geq 1)
. Then we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})=S_{2}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})+kS_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P}),\,\,(0\leq k\leq n+1).

Proof.

(a)   By noting
(x)_{n}\sim(1,e^{t}-1)
, this follows from (
2.16
).

From (
2.16
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|p_{n}(x)\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\frac{d}{dx}\big)^{l}p_{n}(x)|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)p_{n,l}.

(b) The identity is immediate from the following observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}p_{n,l}x^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)x^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1}(k,l)S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{l}.

Note here that the explicit expression of
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
also follows from this by inversion.
(c)   From (
2.11
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}(u)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(u)_{k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(u)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\big(1+(e^{\bar{f}(t)}-1)\big)^{u}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\bar{f}(t)}-1)^{k}(u)_{k}.

(d)   This is immediate from the following observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{2}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=xp_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(x-k+k)S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}kS_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{2}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}kS_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k}.

∎

Here we recall some properties of the ‘classical’ Eulerian numbers in Proposition 3.2, which correspond to the sequence
\left\{x^{n}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, and their generalization to any sequence of polynomials by Koutras (see [19]). The Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
are related in a simple way to the Eulerian numbers associated with
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
(see Theorem 3.3, (h)). This generalizes the relation between Stirling numbers of the second kind and Eulerian numbers which is due to Frobenius (see Proposition 3.2, (k)).

The classical Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{n,k}x^{k}
is monic of degree
n-1
, for
n\geq 1
, with
A_{0}(x)=1
. They are recursively defined by the relation in (a) below. As a matter of convenience, we write
A_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}x^{k},\,\,(n\geq 0)
, with the understanding that
A_{n,n}=0
, for
n\geq 1
, and
A_{0,0}=1
. For more details on classical Eulerian numbers and polynomials, we let the reader refer to [7], while for their generalizations to any sequence of polynomials we recommend the reader to refer to the paper [19]. Here the reader ought to note that the definition of Eulerian polynomials associated with sequence of polynomials in [19] are different from ours. Indeed, when
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{n}\right\}
, namely in the case of classical Eulerian polynomials, his definition of the
n
th Eulerian polynomial is
xA_{n}(x)
, for
n\geq 1
. We remark that our definition is the one that Euler defined originally in [6].

The first few terms of
A_{n}(x)
are as follows:

\displaystyle A_{1}(x)=1,A_{2}(x)=1+x,A_{3}(x)=1+4x+x^{2},A_{4}(x)=1+11x+11x^{2}+x^{3},

\displaystyle A_{5}(x)=1+26x+66x^{2}+26x^{3}+x^{4},A_{6}(x)=1+57x+302x^{2}+302x^{3}+57x^{4}+x^{5},

\displaystyle A_{7}(x)=1+120x+1191x^{2}+2416x^{3}+1191x^{4}+120x^{5}+x^{6}.

We list some of the properties of Eulerian polynomials (see [2,7,8,21]).

Proposition 3.2
.

\displaystyle(a)\,\,A_{0}(x)=1,\,\,A_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}A_{k}(x)(x-1)^{n-1-k},\,\,(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle(b)\,\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1-x}{e^{t(x-1)}-x},

\displaystyle(c)\,\,\frac{A_{n}(x)}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}(j+1)^{n},\,\,(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle(d)\,\,A_{0}(x)=1,\,\,A_{n}(x)=\big(1+(n-1)x\big)A_{n-1}(x)+x(1-x)A_{n-1}^{\prime}(x),\,\,(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle(e)\,\,A_{n,k}=(k+1)A_{n-1,k}+(n-k)A_{n-1,k-1},\,\,(n\geq 2,1\leq k\leq n-1),\,\,A_{n,0}=1,

\displaystyle(f)\,\,A_{n,k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}(k+1-i)^{n}\binom{n+1}{i},\,\,(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle(g)(\textrm{Worpitzky identity})\,\,x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}\binom{x+k}{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}\binom{x+n-k-1}{n},\,\,(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle(h)\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{m}i^{n}x^{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+l}\binom{n}{l}\frac{x^{m+1}A_{n-l}(x)}{(x-1)^{n-l+1}}m^{l}+(-1)^{n}\frac{x(x^{m}-1)}{(x-1)^{n+1}}A_{n}(x),\,\,(m\geq 1,n\geq 0),

\displaystyle(i)\,\,A_{n,k}=A_{n,n-1-k},\,\,(n\geq 1,0\leq k\leq n-1),

\displaystyle(j)\,\,A_{n}(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}=n!,\,\,(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle(k)(\textrm{Frobenius})\,\,A_{n,k}=\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}l!S_{2}(n,l)\binom{n-l}{k-l+1}(-1)^{k-l-1},\,\,(n\geq 2,\,1\leq k\leq n-1),

\displaystyle\quad A_{n,0}=1,

\displaystyle(l)\,\,S_{2}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}A_{n,j-1}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
In [19], Koutras defined, motivated by the Worpitzky identity (g), the Eulerian numbers
A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})
associated with
\mathbb{P}
by the coefficients in the expansion of
p_{n}(x)
in terms of
\binom{x+n-k-1}{n}
. Thus

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})\binom{x+n-k-1}{n}.

He also defined the Eulerian polynomial associated with
\mathbb{P}
by

\displaystyle A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}.

Some of the following are already stated in [19]. However, we state and prove them for the sake of completeness.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle(a)\,\,A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{n+1}{l}p_{n}(k-l+1).

\displaystyle(b)\,\,\frac{A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}p_{n}(j+1).

\displaystyle(c)\,\,\textrm{Let $\bar{\mathbb{P}}=\left\{\bar{p}_{n}(x)\right\}$, where $\bar{p}_{n}(x)$ is the sequence of polynomials defined by}

\displaystyle\quad\bar{p}_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\bar{p}_{n}(x)=xp_{n-1}(x),\,\,(n\geq 1).\,\,\textrm{Then we have}

\displaystyle\quad A_{n+1}(x;\bar{\mathbb{P}})=(1+nx)A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})+x(1-x)A_{n}^{\prime}(x;\mathbb{P}),\,\,(n\geq 0).

\displaystyle(d)\,\,A_{n,k}(\bar{\mathbb{P}})=(k+1)A_{n-1,k}(\mathbb{P})+(n-k)A_{n-1,k-1}(\mathbb{P}),\,\,(n\geq 2,\,\,1\leq k\leq n-1),

\displaystyle\quad A_{n,0}(\bar{\mathbb{P}})=\bar{p}_{n}(1)=A_{n-1,0}(\mathbb{P})=p_{n-1}(1),\,\,(n\geq 1).

\displaystyle(e)\,\,\textrm{Assume that $p_{n}(x)$ is Sheffer for the pair $(g(t),f(t))$. Then we have}

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(1-x)e^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}}{g(\bar{f}((1-x)t))}\frac{1}{1-xe^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}}.

\displaystyle(f)\,\,\textrm{Assume that $p_{n}(x)$ is Sheffer for the pair $(1,f(t))$, with $f(-t)=-f(t)$. Then}

\displaystyle\quad A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})_{=}A_{n,n-1-k}(\mathbb{P}),\,\,(n\geq 1,0\leq k\leq n-1).

For the assertions (g), (h), (i) and (j) below, assume that
p_{n}(0)=0
, for
n\geq 1
.

Equivalently,
p_{n,0}=0
, for
n\geq 1.
Then

\displaystyle(g)\,\,S_{2}(n,0;\mathbb{P})=0,\,\,\mathrm{deg}\,A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})\leq n-1,\,\,A_{n,n}(\mathbb{P})=0,\,\mathrm{for}\quad n\geq 1.

More precisely,
A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}p_{n,k}\,x^{n-1}+\cdots.

\displaystyle(h)\,\,A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}j!S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-j}{k-j+1}(-1)^{k-j+1}.

\displaystyle(i)\,\,S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}A_{n,j-1}(\mathbb{P}).

\displaystyle(j)\,\,A_{n}(1;\mathbb{P})=p_{nn}n!.

\displaystyle(k)\,\,p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})\binom{x+n-k-1}{n}.

Proof.

(a) By using Vandermonde convolution formula (see [4,22,23])

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{x}{k}\binom{y}{n-k}=\binom{x+y}{n},

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{n+1}{l}p_{n}(k-l+1)=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{n+1}{l}\sum_{j=0}^{n}A_{n,j}(\mathbb{P})\binom{n+k-j-l}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{n+1}{l}\sum_{j=0}^{k-l}A_{n,j}(\mathbb{P})\binom{n+k-j-l}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}A_{n,j}(\mathbb{P})(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{l=0}^{k-j}(-1)^{k-j-l}\binom{n+1}{l}\binom{n+k-j-l}{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}A_{n,j}(\mathbb{P})(-1)^{k-j}\sum_{l=0}^{k-j}\binom{n+1}{l}\binom{-n-1}{k-j-l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}A_{n,j}(\mathbb{P})(-1)^{k-j}\delta_{k,j}=A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P}).

(b)  By making use of (a), we see that

\displaystyle A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\binom{n+1}{l}p_{n}(k-l+1)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\binom{n+1}{l}x^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(j+1)x^{j}

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(j+1)x^{j}.

(c)   This follows by multiplying the identity in (b) by
x
and then differentiating the resulting identity.

(d)  From (c), it is easy to see that we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}A_{n+1,k}(\bar{\mathbb{P}})x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}kA_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}nA_{n,k-1}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}(k-1)A_{n,k-1}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}kA_{n,k}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}nA_{n,k-1}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k-1)A_{n,k-1}(\mathbb{P})x^{k}.

(e)  By making use of (b), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-x)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(j+1)x^{j}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(j+1)\frac{((1-x)t)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(1-x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}((1-x)t))}e^{(j+1)\bar{f}((1-x)t)},

which gives the desired result.

(f)  Note first that
\bar{f}(-t)=-\bar{f}(t).
Here the assertion is equivalent to showing that
A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})=x^{n-1}A_{n}(\frac{1}{x};\mathbb{P})
, which means it is a Euler-Frobenius polynomial. By using (c), we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(1-x)e^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}}{1-xe^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}}-1=\frac{e^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}-1}{1-xe^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}}.

By invoking
\bar{f}(-t)=-\bar{f}(t)
, we get the desired result, as we see from

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x^{n-1}A_{n}(\frac{1}{x};\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{\bar{f}((x-1)t)}-1}{x-e^{\bar{f}((x-1)t)}}=\frac{e^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}-1}{1-xe^{\bar{f}((1-x)t)}}.

(g)   Let
n\geq 1
. By invoking (b) and Theorem 3.2 (c), we see that

\displaystyle A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{nk}(j+1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{nk}(1-x)^{n-k}(1-x)^{k+1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x^{j}(j+1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{nk}(1-x)^{n-k}A_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{nk}(-1)^{n-k}x^{n-1}+\cdots,

where we used the fact that
A_{k}(x)
is a monic polynomial of degree
k-1
, for
k\geq 1
.

This shows in particular that
A_{n,n}(\mathbb{P})=0
. The fact that
S_{2}(n,0;\mathbb{P})=0
follows from the definition (
3.5
).

(h)  To prove this, we may assume that
n\geq 1
. By using (b) and (
3.5
), we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}p_{n}(k+1)

\displaystyle=(1-x)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)_{j}x^{k},

where we note that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)_{j}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=j-1}^{\infty}(k+1)_{j}x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+j)_{j}x^{k+j-1}=j!x^{j-1}(1-x)^{-(j+1)}.

Thus we see from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}j!x^{j-1}S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})(1-x)^{n-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}j!x^{j-1}S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{n-j}{m}(-1)^{m}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}j!S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-j}{m}(-1)^{m}x^{j+m-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}j!S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-j}{k-j}(-1)^{k-j}x^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j!S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-j}{k-j}(-1)^{k-j}x^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}j!S_{2}(n,j;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-j}{k-j+1}(-1)^{k-j+1}x^{k},

from which the result follows.

(i)  By using (h), the right hand side of (i) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}A_{n,j-1}(\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}\sum_{l=1}^{j}l!S_{2}(n,l;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-l}{j-l}(-1)^{j-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=1}^{k}l!S_{2}(n,l;\mathbb{P})\sum_{j=l}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}\binom{n-l}{j-l}(-1)^{j-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=1}^{k}l!S_{2}(n,l;\mathbb{P})\sum_{j=l}^{k}\binom{k-l}{k-j}\binom{n-l}{n-k}(-1)^{j-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=1}^{k}l!S_{2}(n,l;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-l}{n-k}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\binom{k-l}{k-j}(-1)^{j-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=1}^{k}l!S_{2}(n,l;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-l}{n-k}(-1)^{k-l}\sum_{j=0}^{k-l}\binom{k-l}{j}(-1)^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=1}^{k}l!S_{2}(n,l;\mathbb{P})\binom{n-l}{n-k}(-1)^{k-l}\delta_{k,l}

\displaystyle=S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P}).

(j)   As we saw in the proof of (g) and by using Proposition 3.2 (j), we have

\displaystyle A_{n}(x;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{nk}(1-x)^{n-k}A_{k}(x),

from which the assertion follows.

(k)  This is just the definition in (
3.5
).
∎

4.
Examples on Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with sequences of polynomials

(a) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{n}\right\}
. Then
x^{n}\sim(1,t)
.
By the definition in (
3.5
) and Theorem 3.1, we have (see [4,22,23])

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=S_{2}(n,k),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}.

(b) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(x)_{n,\lambda}\right\}
be the sequence of generalized falling factorials. Then
(x)_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)\big)
. By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have (see [9,10,12,14,16-18])

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k).

Further, by Theorem 3.1, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big((1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{k},

where we use the standard notations for degenerate exponentials:

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{x}{\lambda}},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}.

(c) The rising factorials
\langle x\rangle_{n}
are defined by

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad\langle x\rangle_{0}=1.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\right\}
. Then
\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\sim(1,1-e^{-t}),
and
\langle{x\rangle}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k}
. Here
L(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!}
are the (unsigned) Lah numbers and (see [4,11,22,23])

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=L(n,k).

An expression of the Lah numbers can be derived from Theorem 3.1. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{-\log(1-t)}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,k)(-1)^{l}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log(1-t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle L(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k).

(d) The generalized rising factorials are defined by

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=x(x+\lambda)\cdots(x+(n-1)\lambda),\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad\langle x\rangle_{0,\lambda}=1.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\right\}
. Then
\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\big)
. The degenerate Lah numbers are defined by
\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}
, and hence (see [14])

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=L_{\lambda}(n,k).

An expression of the degenerate Lah numbers can be derived from Theorem 3.1. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,k)(-\frac{1}{\lambda})^{l}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log(1-\lambda t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-\lambda)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(n,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-\lambda)^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k).

(e) The central factorials
x^{[n]}
are defined by (see [3,4,15,23])

\displaystyle x^{[n]}=x(x+\frac{1}{2}n-1)_{n-1},\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad x^{[0]}=1,

where
x^{[n]}\sim(1,f(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})
. Here we note that

\displaystyle\bar{f}(t)=2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)=\log\big(1+\frac{t}{2}(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4})\big).

Carlitz-Riordan and Riordan (see [3,4,15,23]) discussed the numbers
T_{1}(n,k)
and
T_{2}(n,k)
which are respectively called the central factorial numbers of the first kind and of the second kind, and defined by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log\big(1+\frac{t}{2}(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}\big)\big)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k)x^{k},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)x^{[k]}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{[n]}\right\}
. From Theorem 3.1, we get

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)T_{1}(n,l),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{t}{2}(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4})\big)^{k}.

(f) The central Bell polynomials
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)
and the central Bell numbers
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}
are respectively defined by (see [15])

\displaystyle e^{x(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(1).

Then we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\sim\Big(1,f(t)=2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\Big),\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)x^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\right\}
. Then, by Theorem 3.1 and (e), we see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)T_{2}(n,l),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})}-1\big)^{k}.

(g) The numbers
T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are respectively called the degenerate central factorial numbers of the first kind and of the second kind, and defined by (see [16])

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg)\right\}^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

\displaystyle x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{[k]}.

The degenerate central Bell polynomials
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)
and the degenerate central Bell numbers
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}
are respectively defined by (see [16])

\displaystyle e^{x(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t))}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}=\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(1).

Then we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)\sim\Big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg)\Big),\quad\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\right\}
. Then, by Theorem 3.1 and (e), we see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)T_{2,\lambda}(n,l),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{(e_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-e_{\lambda}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(t))}-1\big)^{k}.

(h) Let
x^{[n,\lambda]}
be defined by

\displaystyle x^{[n,\lambda]}=x\big(x+(\frac{1}{2}n-1)\lambda\big)_{n-1,\lambda},\,\,(n\geq 1),\quad x^{[0,\lambda]}=1.

where
x^{[n,\lambda]}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}})\big)
. Here we note that

\displaystyle\bar{f}(t)=\frac{2}{\lambda}\log\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\big(1+\frac{\lambda t}{2}(\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4})\big).

As analogies to the numbers
T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
mentioned in (e) above, we may introduce the numbers
R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
and
R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
which are defined by

\displaystyle x^{[n,\lambda]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{2}{\lambda}\log\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{k},

\displaystyle x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{[k,\lambda]},\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}\big)\Big)^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{[n,\lambda]}\right\}
. From Theorem 3.1, we get

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)R_{1,\lambda}(n,l),\,\,\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{\lambda t+\sqrt{\lambda^{2}t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{\lambda}}-1\bigg)^{k}.

(i) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}^{L}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Lah-Bell polynomials. Then it is given by
e^{x(\frac{t}{1-t})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{L}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
, so that
B_{n}^{L}(x)\sim(1,\frac{t}{1+t})
, and
B_{n}^{L}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)x^{k}
(see [11]). By Theorem 3.1 and (c), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{t}{1-t}}-1)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}L(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we obtain
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
.

(j) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of degenerate Lah-Bell polynomials. Then it is given by
e_{\lambda}^{x}(\frac{t}{1-t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Thus we see that
B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\sim(1,\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda+e^{\lambda t}-1})
, and
B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}
(see [14]). By Theorem 3.1 and recalling from (d) that
\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big((1-\lambda t)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1\big)^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\frac{\lambda t}{1-t})}-1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}L_{-\lambda}(m,k)\frac{1}{m!}\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}L_{-\lambda}(m,k)\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}L(n,m)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{m=k}^{n}L(n,m)L_{-\lambda}(m,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

Thus we obtain
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{m=k}^{n}L(n,m)L_{-\lambda}(m,k).

(k) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bell polynomials given by
e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\sim(1,\log(1+t))
, and
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)x^{k}
(see [4,22,23]). Now, by Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{e^{t}-1}-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(e^{t}-1)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)S_{2}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k).

(l) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of partially degenerate Bell polynomials given by
e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\sim(1,\log_{\lambda}(1+t))
, and
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}
(see [17]). Here
\log_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(t^{\lambda}-1)
is the degenerate logarithm which is the compositional inverse to the degenerate exponential
e_{\lambda}(t)
. From Theorem 3.1 and proceeding just in the case of (k), it is immediate to see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k).

There are several other degenerate versions of Bell polynomials, one of which is the so called fully degenerate Bell polynomials
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
given by
e_{\lambda}^{x}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.
Then
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\sim\big(1,\log_{\lambda}(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1))\big)
, and
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda}
(see [18]). Now, from Theorem 3.1 and recalling from (d) that
\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\big((1-\lambda t)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1\big)^{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1))}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big((1+\lambda(e_{\lambda}(t)-1))^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}L_{-\lambda}(m,k)\frac{1}{m!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{m=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)L_{-\lambda}(m,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

which verifies that we have
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{m=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,m)L_{-\lambda}(m,k).

(m) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{M_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Mittag-Leffler polynomials. That is,
M_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{e^{t}-1}{e^{t}+1})
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\log\big(\frac{1+t}{1-t}\big)
(see [23]).
By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\log(\frac{1+t}{1-t})}-1\big)^{k}=2^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}2^{k}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=2^{k}L(n,k).

(n) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{L_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Laguerre polynomials of order -1 (see [23]).
That is,
L_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{t}{t-1})
, with
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{t}{t-1}
. By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{t}{t-1}}-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}S_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}S_{2}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
.

(o) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bernoulli polynomials. Then
B_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t},t\big)
, and
B_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{n-k}x^{k}
(see [23]). By Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}.

(p) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{E_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Euler polynomials. Then
E_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{e^{t}+1}{2},t\big)
(see [23]).
Analogously to (c), we see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(n-l,k)E_{l},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}.

(q) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(rx+s)_{n}\right\}
, with
r\neq 0
. Then

\displaystyle(rx+s)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G(n,k;r,s)(x)_{k},

where
G(n,k;r,s)
are called the Gould-Hopper numbers (see [19]). Thus

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=G(n,k;r,s).

Noting that
(rx+s)_{n}\sim\big(e^{-\frac{s}{r}t},e^{\frac{t}{r}}-1\big)
, we can derive an expression of Gould-Hopper numbers from Theorem 3.1. Indeed, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}G(n,k;r,s)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=(1+t)^{s}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{r\log(1+t)}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(s)_{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=k}^{l}r^{m}S_{1}(l,m)S_{2}(m,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{l}\binom{n}{l}r^{m}(s)_{n-l}S_{1}(l,m)S_{2}(m,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle G(n,k;r,s)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{l}\binom{n}{l}r^{m}(s)_{n-l}S_{1}(l,m)S_{2}(m,k).

(r) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{b_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind. Then
b_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1},e^{t}-1\big)
, so that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t}{\log(1+t)}(1+t)^{x}
, (see [23]). From Theorem 3.1, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{\log(1+t)}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\log(1+t)}-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}t^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}b_{n-k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\binom{n}{k}b_{n-k},

where
b_{n}=b_{n}(0)
are the Bernoulli numbers of the second.

Alternatively, it is easily shown that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{m}\binom{n}{m}b_{n-m}S_{1}(m,l)S_{2}(l,k).

(s) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{C_{n}(x;a)\right\}
be the sequence of Poisson-Charlier polynomials. Here
C_{n}(x;a)\sim\big(e^{a(e^{t}-1)},a(e^{t}-1)\big)
, with
a\neq 0
, and therefore
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}(x;a)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{-t}\big(1+\frac{t}{a}\big)^{x}
(see [23]). From Theorem 3.1, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}e^{-t}\big(e^{\log(1+\frac{t}{a})}-1\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}a^{-k}t^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}a^{-k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}a^{-k}.

Alternatively, we can also show that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{m}(-1)^{n-m}a^{-m}\binom{n}{m}S_{1}(m,l)S_{2}(l,k).

(t) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
, with
p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)
. This is not a Sheffer sequence. For this, we recall from [13] that

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}B_{m}(x)+(n+1)B_{n}(x).

By Theorem 3.1 and (o), we note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|B_{n}(x)\rangle}=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l}.

Again, by Theorem 3.1, we see that

\displaystyle S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|p_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|B_{m}(x)\rangle}+(n+1)\frac{1}{k!}\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|B_{n}(x)\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{m-k}\binom{m}{l}S_{2}(m-l,k)B_{l}+(n+1)\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l}.

This says that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\{\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}\sum_{l=0}^{m-k}\binom{m}{l}S_{2}(m-l,k)B_{l}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad+(n+1)\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(n-l,k)B_{l}\right\}(x)_{k}.

5.
Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials

As in the ‘classical’ case, we would like to introduce the Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials. Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
.
In view of our definition of the Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
, it is natural to define the Stirling numbers of the first associated with
\mathbb{P}
as the coefficients when we expand
(x)_{n}
in terms of
p_{k}(x)
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
. We let

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x).

(a)   Let
\bar{\mathbb{P}}=\left\{\bar{p}_{n}(x)\right\}
, where
\bar{p}_{n}(x)
is the sequence of polynomials defined by
\bar{p}_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\bar{p}_{n}(x)=xp_{n-1}(x),\,\,(n\geq 1)
. Then we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})=S_{1}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})-nS_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}}),\,\,(0\leq k\leq n+1).

(b)   Assume that
p_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
. Then the Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
are given by

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\frac{1}{k!}\langle{g(t)(f(t))^{k}|(x)_{n}\rangle}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\langle{g(t)f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}.

(c)
(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(-x)=\langle{x\rangle}_{n}
,
(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(-1)=n!.

Proof.

(a)  This is immediate from the following observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{1}(n+1,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=(x)_{n}(x-n)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x)-n\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\bar{p}_{k+1}(x)-n\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{1}(n,k-1;\mathbb{P})\bar{p}_{k}(x)-n\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{1}(n,k;\bar{\mathbb{P}})\bar{p}_{k}(x).

(b)   This follows from (
2.16
).

(c)   This is clear by definition in (
5.1
).
∎

For any delta series
f(t)
, we define the
logarithm associated to
f(t)
by

\displaystyle L_{f(t)}t=f(\log(1+t)),

In case
p_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
,
L_{f(t)}t
may be also denoted by
L_{\mathbb{P}}t
and called the
logarithm associated to
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
. Recalling that
(x)_{n}\sim(1,l(t)=e^{t}-1)
, we note that
L_{f(t)}t=f(\bar{l}(t))
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
p_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)),
and let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
. Then the generating function of the Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with
\mathbb{P}
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(L_{f(t)}t)^{k},

where
L_{f(t)}t=L_{\mathbb{P}}t=f(\log(1+t))
is the logarithm associated to
f(t)
.

Proof.

Assume now that
p_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t))
. Then we observe that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(x)\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=(1+t)^{x}.

On the other hand, we also have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(x)\frac{1}{k!}\big(L_{f(t)}t\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=e^{x\bar{f}\big(f(\log(1+t))\big)}=(1+t)^{x},

where we used the fact that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=e^{x\bar{f}(t)}
. Now, the assertion follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.3
).

We would like to give another proof for this by using Theorem 5.1 (b).

Here we have to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\langle{f(t)^{k}|(x)_{n}\rangle}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(f(\log(1+t)))^{k}.

Equivalently, we need to verify that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\langle{f(t)^{k}|(x)_{n}\rangle}\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{n}}{n!}=(f(t))^{k}.

The left hand side of (
5.4
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,l)\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\langle{f(t)^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}=(f(t))^{k}.

∎

The following orthogonality and inverse relations can be easily shown.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of polynomials such that deg
p_{n}(x)=n,p_{0}(x)=1
. Then we have

\displaystyle(a)\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})S_{2}(k,l;\mathbb{P})=\delta_{n,l},\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})S_{1}(k,l;\mathbb{P})=\delta_{n,l},\,\,(0\leq l\leq n).

\displaystyle(b)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})a_{k}.

\displaystyle(c)\,\,a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2}(k,n;\mathbb{P})c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1}(k,n;\mathbb{P})a_{k}.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
p_{n}(x)
be Sheffer for the pair
(g(t),f(t))
. Then we saw in Theorem 3.2 that the generating function of
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}(e^{\bar{f}(t)}-1)^{k}.

Let
f(t)
be any delta series. After introducing the logarithm associated to
f(t)
, it seems natural to introduce the exponential associated to
f(t)
as

\displaystyle E_{f(t)}t=e^{\bar{f}(t)}-1.

When
p_{n}(x)\sim(g(t),f(t))
,
E_{f(t)}t
may be also denoted by
E_{\mathbb{P}}t
and called the exponential associated to
\mathbb{P}
. Recalling that
(x)_{n}\sim(1,l(t)=e^{t}-1)
, we observe that
E_{f(t)}t=l(\bar{f}(t))
. In summary, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{g(\bar{f}(t))}\frac{1}{k!}(E_{f(t)}t)^{k}.\,

where
E_{f(t)}t=E_{\mathbb{P}}t=e^{\bar{f}(t)}-1
.

As an example, consider the delta series
f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)
. Here
\bar{f}(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)
. Thus we see that

\displaystyle L_{f(t)}t=\frac{1}{\lambda}\big((1+t)^{\lambda}-1\big),\quad E_{f(t)}t=(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1.

Here we note that, in many of the papers on degenerate special polynomials and numbers,
\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(t^{\lambda}-1\big)
is called the degenerate logarithm and denoted by
\log_{\lambda}t
, and
(1+\lambda t)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}
is called the degenerate exponential and denoted by
e_{\lambda}(t)
.

6.
Examples on Stirling numbers of the first kind associated with sequences of polynomials

(a) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{n}\right\}
. Then
x^{n}\sim(1,t)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=S_{2}(n,k)
. As
(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})x^{k}
, we have (see [4,22,23])

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=S_{1}(n,k),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(\log(1+t))^{k}.

(b) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(x)_{n,\lambda}\right\}
be the sequence of generalized falling factorials. Then
(x)_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1)\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
. As
(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(x)_{k,\lambda}
, we have (see [9,10,12,14,16-18])

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=S_{1,\lambda}(n,k),\quad\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(\log_{\lambda}(1+t))^{k},

(see Remark 5.4).

Now, by Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)S_{1,\lambda}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\,\,(0\leq l\leq n),

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{2,\lambda}(k,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}S_{1,\lambda}(k,n)a_{k}.

(c) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\right\}
be the sequence of rising factorials. Then
\langle{x\rangle}_{n}\sim(1,1-e^{-t})
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=L(n,k)
are the (unsigned) Lah numbers. In addition, by Theorem 5.2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{1+t}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)
are the signed Lah numbers (see [4,11,22,23]).
Hence, by Theorem 5.3, we have the identities about Lah numbers given by:

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)L(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\quad\sum_{k=l}^{n}(-1)^{k-l}L(n,k)L(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}L(k,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}(-1)^{k-n}L(k,n)a_{k}.

Note here that the two identities in (
6.1
) are equivalent.

(d) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\right\}
be the sequence of generalized rising factorials. Then
\langle{x\rangle}_{n,\lambda}\sim\big(1,\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=L_{\lambda}(n,k)
are the degenerate Lah numbers given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1-\lambda t)}-1\big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\big((1-\lambda t)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}-1\big)^{k}.

By Theorem 5.2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}(1-(1+t)^{-\lambda})\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-\lambda)^{n-k}L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=(-\lambda)^{n-k}L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(n,k)
(see [14]). Hence, by Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}(-\lambda)^{n-k}L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(n,k)L_{\lambda}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},\,\,\sum_{k=l}^{n}(-\lambda)^{k-l}L_{\lambda}(n,k)L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{\lambda}(n,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-\lambda)^{n-k}L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(n,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}L_{\lambda}(k,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}(-\lambda)^{k-n}L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(k,n)a_{k}.

We remark that
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=(-\lambda)^{n-k}L_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(n,k)=S_{1,-\lambda}(n,k).
Indeed, we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{1}{-\lambda}((1+t)^{-\lambda})-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{-\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,-\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(e) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{[n]}\right\}
be the sequence of central factorials. Then
x^{[n]}\sim(1,f(t)=e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}})
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
,
where
T_{1}(n,k)
are the central factorial numbers of the first kind defined by
x^{[n]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{1}(n,k)x^{k}
. Recall here that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{\frac{t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Now, by Theorem 5.2, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{2}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(\log(1+t))^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)
(see [3,4,15,23]). From Theorem 5.3, we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)T_{2}(m,k)T_{1}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}T_{1}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)T_{2}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{2}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}T_{1}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)T_{2}(l,n)a_{k}.

(f) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of central Bell polynomials. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}^{(c)}(x)\sim\Big(1,f(t)=2\log\big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\big)\Big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k),
where
T_{2}(n,k)
are the central factorial numbers of the second kind defined by
x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2}(n,k)x^{[k]}
.
We recall here that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(2\log\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Now, by Theorem 5.2, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{1}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(\log(1+t))^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{1}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{1}(l,k)
(see [15]). From Theorem 5.3, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)T_{1}(m,k)T_{2}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}T_{2}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)T_{1}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{1}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}T_{2}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)T_{1}(l,n)a_{k}.

(g) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of degenerate central Bell polynomials. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}^{(c)}(x)\sim\Big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\Big(\Big(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\Big)^{2\lambda}-1\Big)\Big),
and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
, where
T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
are the degenerate central factorial numbers of the second kind defined by
(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{[k]}
. Here we recall that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left\{\frac{1}{\lambda}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{t+\sqrt{t^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)^{2\lambda}-1\bigg)\right\}^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}T_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Now, by Theorem 5.2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}T_{1,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(\log(1+t))^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{1,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{1,\lambda}(l,k)
(see [16]).
From Theorem 5.3, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)T_{1,\lambda}(m,k)T_{2,\lambda}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}T_{2,\lambda}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)T_{1,\lambda}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}T_{2,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)T_{1,\lambda}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}T_{2,\lambda}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)T_{1,\lambda}(l,n)a_{k}.

(h) Let
x^{[n,\lambda]}
be the sequence of polynomials defined by
x^{[0,\lambda]}=1,\quad x^{[n,\lambda]}=x\big(x+(\frac{1}{2}n-1)\lambda\big)_{n-1,\lambda},\,\,(n\geq 1)
. Then
x^{[n,\lambda]}\sim\big(1,f(t)=\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}})\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}R_{1,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
,
where
R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
are defined by
x^{[n,\lambda]}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}R_{1,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k}
. Here we recall that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}R_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\big(e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}-e^{-\frac{\lambda t}{2}}\big)\Big)^{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{x^{[n,\lambda]}\right\}
. Now, by Theorem 5.2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}R_{2,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)R_{2,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)R_{2,\lambda}(l,k)
. From Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)R_{2,\lambda}(m,k)R_{1,\lambda}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}R_{1,\lambda}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)R_{2,\lambda}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}R_{1,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)R_{2,\lambda}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}R_{1,\lambda}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)R_{2,\lambda}(l,n)a_{k}.

(i) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}^{L}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Lah-Bell polynomials. Then
B_{n}^{L}(x)\sim(1,\frac{t}{1+t})
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
.

By Theorem 5.1 and (c) above, we see that

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\big\langle{\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{t}{1+t}\Big)^{k}|x^{l}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\sum_{j=k}^{l}(-1)^{j-k}L(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\langle{t^{j}|x^{l}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}S_{1}(n,l)L(l,k).

Therefore we have
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}S_{1}(n,l)L(l,k)
(see [11]). Now, from Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}(-1)^{m-k}S_{1}(n,m)L(m,k)L(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{m-l}L(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)L(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}S_{1}(n,l)L(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}L(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{l}\sum_{l=n}^{k}(-1)^{l-n}S_{1}(k,l)L(l,n)a_{k}.

(j) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of degenerate Lah-Bell polynomials. Then
B_{n,\lambda}^{L}(x)\sim(1,\frac{e^{\lambda t}-1}{\lambda+e^{\lambda t}-1})
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}L(n,l)L_{-\lambda}(l,k).
Now, by Theorem 5.2, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{\log_{\lambda}(1+t)}{1+\log_{\lambda}(1+t)}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{l-k}L(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}(\log_{\lambda}(1+t))^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)L(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus we have
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)L(l,k)
(see [14]).
Now, by Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}(-1)^{m-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,m)L(m,k)L(k,j)L_{-\lambda}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{m-l}L(n,j)L_{-\lambda}(j,k)S_{1,\lambda}(k,m)L(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}L(n,l)L_{-\lambda}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)L(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}L(k,l)L_{-\lambda}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}(-1)^{l-n}S_{1,\lambda}(k,l)L(l,n)a_{k}.

(k) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bell polynomials. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\sim(1,\log(1+t))
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
.
By Theorem 5.1, we get

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\langle{\frac{1}{k!}(\log(1+t))^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\sum_{j=k}^{l}S_{1}(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\langle{t^{j}|x^{l}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)S_{1}(l,k).

Thus we see that
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)S_{1}(l,k)
(see [4,22,23]). Now, by Theorem 5.3, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)S_{1}(m,k)S_{2}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}S_{2}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)S_{1}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)S_{1}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{2}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)S_{1}(l,n)a_{k}.

(l) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of partially degenerate Bell polynomials. Then
\mathrm{Bel}_{n,\lambda}(x)\sim(1,\log_{\lambda}(1+t))
, and

S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
. By Theorem 5.1, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\langle{\frac{1}{k!}(\log_{\lambda}(1+t))^{k}|x^{l}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\sum_{j=k}^{l}S_{1,\lambda}(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\langle{t^{j}|x^{l}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,k).

Hence we have
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)
(see [17]). Now, by Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1}(n,m)S_{1,\lambda}(m,k)S_{2,\lambda}(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)S_{1,\lambda}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,n)a_{k}.

Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of fully degenerate Bell polynomials. Then
\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\sim\big(1,\log_{\lambda}(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}(e^{\lambda t}-1))\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)L_{-\lambda}(l,k).
By Theorem 5.2, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+\log_{\lambda}(1+t))\big)^{k}=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore we get
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)
(see [18]).
Now, by Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,m)S_{1,\lambda}(m,k)S_{2,\lambda}(k,j)L_{-\lambda}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,j)L_{-\lambda}(j,k)S_{1,\lambda}(k,m)S_{1,\lambda}(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)L_{-\lambda}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)L_{-\lambda}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}(k,l)S_{1,\lambda}(l,n)a_{k}.

(m) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{M_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Mittag-Leffler polynomials. Then
M_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{e^{t}-1}{e^{t}+1})
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=2^{k}L(n,k)
. Then, from Theorem 5.2, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{\frac{t}{2}}{1+\frac{t}{2}}\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence we have
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)\frac{1}{2^{n}}
(see [23]). By Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)L(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}(-1)^{k-l}L(n,k)L(k,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{k}L(n,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L(n,k)\frac{1}{2^{n}}a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}2^{n}L(k,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}(-1)^{k-n}L(k,n)\frac{1}{2^{k}}a_{k}.

Note that the same identities were obtained in (c).

(n) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{L_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Laguerre polynomials of order -1.
Then
L_{n}(x)\sim(1,f(t)=\frac{t}{t-1})
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)
. By Theorem 5.1, we see that

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(-1)^{k}\big\langle{\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)^{k}|x^{l}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=k}^{l}L(j,k)\frac{1}{j!}\langle{t^{j}|x^{l}\rangle}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)L(l,k).

Thus we have
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)L(l,k)
(see [23]). Now, from Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}S_{1}(n,m)L(m,k)L(k,j)S_{2}(j,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}(-1)^{j-l}L(n,j)S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)L(m,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l}L(n,l)S_{2}(l,k)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)L(l,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}(-1)^{l}L(k,l)S_{2}(l,n)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}(-1)^{n}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)L(l,n)a_{k}.

(o) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{B_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bernoulli polynomials. Then
B_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{e^{t}-1}{t},t\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}
. By using (
2.7
) and Theorem 5.1, we see that (see [23])

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big\langle{t^{k}\Big|\frac{e^{t}-1}{t}(x)_{n}\Big\rangle}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big\langle{t^{k}\Big|\int_{x}^{x+1}(u)_{n}du\Big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big\langle{t^{k}\Big|\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{S_{1}(n,l)}{l+1}\big((x+1)^{l+1}-x^{l+1}\big)\Big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{1}(n,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{k}\Big\langle{1\Big|(x+1)^{l+1-k}-x^{l+1-k}\Big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{1}(n,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{k}.

Now, by Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(m+1)_{k}}{m+1}S_{1}(n,m)S_{2}(j,l)B_{k-j}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}\frac{1}{l!}\binom{n}{j}\frac{(m+1)_{l}}{m+1}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)B_{n-j}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{2}(l,k)\binom{n}{l}B_{n-l}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{1}(n,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{k}a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{2}(l,n)\binom{k}{l}B_{k-l}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{l=n}^{k}\frac{S_{1}(k,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{n}a_{k}.

(p) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{E_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Euler polynomials. Then
E_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{e^{t}+1}{2},t\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(l,k)E_{n-l}
. By Theorem 5.1, we note that (see [23])

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k!}\big\langle{e^{t}+1\big|t^{k}(x)_{n}\rangle}=\frac{1}{2k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(l)_{k}\big\langle{e^{t}+1\big|x^{l-k}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(l)_{k}(1+\delta_{l,k})=\frac{1}{2k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(l)_{k}+\frac{1}{2}S_{1}(n,k).

Thus, by Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\frac{1}{2}\binom{m}{k}\binom{k}{j}S_{1}(n,m)S_{2}(j,l)E_{k-j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=l}^{k}\frac{1}{2}\binom{k}{j}S_{1}(n,k)S_{2}(j,l)E_{k-j}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}\frac{1}{2}\binom{m}{l}\binom{n}{j}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,m)E_{n-j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\binom{n}{j}S_{2}(j,k)S_{1}(k,l)E_{n-j}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{2}(l,k)E_{n-l}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left\{\frac{1}{2k!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(l)_{k}+\frac{1}{2}S_{1}(n,k)\right\}a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}\binom{k}{l}S_{2}(l,n)E_{k-l}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\left\{\frac{1}{2n!}\sum_{l=n}^{k}S_{1}(k,l)(l)_{n}+\frac{1}{2}S_{1}(k,n)\right\}a_{k}.

(q) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{(rx+s)_{n}\right\}
, with
r\neq 0
. Then
(rx+s)_{n}\sim\big(e^{-\frac{s}{r}t},e^{\frac{t}{r}}-1\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=G(n,k;r,s)
are the Gould-Hopper numbers defined by
(rx+s)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G(n,k;r,s)(x)_{k}
. By (
2.7
), (
2.17
) and Theorem 5.1, we see that (see [19])

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{e^{-\frac{s}{r}t}\big(e^{\frac{t}{r}}-1\big)^{k}\big|(x)_{n}\big\rangle}=\frac{1}{k!}\Big\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}\Big|\Big(\frac{x-s}{r}\Big)_{n}\Big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\frac{1}{r^{l}}\frac{1}{k!}\big\langle{(e^{t}-1)^{k}|(x-s)^{l}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\frac{1}{r^{l}}\sum_{i=k}^{l}S_{2}(i,k)\frac{1}{i!}\big\langle{t^{i}|(x-s)^{l}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{i=k}^{l}\frac{(-s)^{l-i}}{r^{l}}\binom{l}{i}S_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(i,k).

From Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=k}^{n}\sum_{i=k}^{m}\frac{(-s)^{m-i}}{r^{m}}\binom{m}{i}S_{1}(n,m)S_{2}(i,k)G(k,l;r,s)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=l}^{k}\sum_{i=l}^{m}G(n,k;r,s)\frac{(-s)^{m-i}}{r^{m}}\binom{m}{i}S_{1}(k,m)S_{2}(i,l)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}G(n,k;r,s)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\sum_{i=k}^{l}\frac{(-s)^{l-i}}{r^{l}}\binom{l}{i}S_{1}(n,l)S_{2}(i,k)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}G(k,n;r,s)c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\sum_{l=n}^{k}\sum_{i=n}^{l}\frac{(-s)^{l-i}}{r^{l}}\binom{l}{i}S_{1}(k,l)S_{2}(i,n)a_{k}.

(r) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{b_{n}(x)\right\}
be the sequence of Bernoulli polynomials of the second kind. Then
b_{n}(x)\sim\big(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1},e^{t}-1\big)
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\binom{n}{k}b_{n-k}
,
where
b_{n}=b_{n}(0)
are the Bernoulli numbers of the second. By using (
2.12
) and Theorem 5.1 and noting
(x)_{n}\sim(1,e^{t}-1)
, we have (see [23])

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big\langle{\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\Big|(e^{t}-1)^{k}(x)_{n}\Big\rangle}=\binom{n}{k}\Big\langle{\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\Big|(x)_{n-k}\Big\rangle}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k,l)\Big\langle{\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\Big|x^{l}\Big\rangle}=\binom{n}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k,l)B_{l},

where
B_{l}
are the Bernoulli numbers.
Now, by Theorem 5.3, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{l}S_{1}(n-k,m)B_{m}b_{k-l}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{k-l}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{l}S_{1}(k-l,m)B_{m}b_{n-k}=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}b_{n-k}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k,l)B_{l}a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\binom{k}{n}b_{k-n}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\binom{k}{n}\sum_{l=0}^{k-n}S_{1}(k-n,l)B_{l}a_{k}.

(s) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{C_{n}(x;a)\right\}
be the sequence of Poisson-Charlier polynomials. Then
C_{n}(x;a)\sim\big(e^{a(e^{t}-1)},a(e^{t}-1)\big)
, with
a\neq 0
, and
S_{2}(n,k;\mathbb{P})=\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}a^{-k}
. By Theorem 5.1, we get

\displaystyle S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{k}}{k!}\big\langle{e^{a(e^{t}-1)}\big|\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{k}(x)_{n}\rangle}=a^{k}\binom{n}{k}\big\langle{e^{a(e^{t}-1)}\big|(x)_{n-k}\rangle}

\displaystyle=a^{k}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k,l)\mathrm{Bel}_{l}(a),

where
\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)
are the Bell polynomials given by
e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Bel}_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
(see [23]).

Now, by Theorem 5.3, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-k}(-a)^{k-l}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{l}S_{1}(n-k,m)\mathrm{Bel}_{m}(a)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=l}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{k-l}(-1)^{n-k}a^{l-k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{l}S_{1}(k-l,m)\mathrm{Bel}_{m}(a)=\delta_{n,l},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}a^{-k}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a^{k}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}S_{1}(n-k,l)\mathrm{Bel}_{l}(a)a_{k},

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}\binom{k}{n}(-1)^{k-n}a^{-n}c_{k}\Longleftrightarrow c_{n}=\sum_{k=n}^{m}a^{n}\binom{k}{n}\sum_{l=0}^{k-n}S_{1}(k-n,l)\mathrm{Bel}_{l}(a)a_{k}.

(t) Let
\mathbb{P}=\left\{p_{n}(x)\right\}
, with
p_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{k}(x)B_{n-k}(x)
. This is not a Sheffer sequence. Here we would like to describe how to determine
S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})
.
For this, we recall from [13] that

(6.2)

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\frac{2}{n+2}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\binom{n+2}{m}B_{n-m}B_{m}(x)+(n+1)B_{n}(x).

On the one hand, by (o) we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle(x)_{n}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\gamma_{m}B_{m}(x),

where
\gamma_{m}=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{l=m}^{n}\frac{S_{1}(n,l)}{l+1}(l+1)_{m}
.

On the other hand, by definition and from (
6.2
) we also have

\displaystyle(x)_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})p_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})\frac{2}{k+2}\sum_{m=0}^{k-2}\binom{k+2}{m}B_{k-m}B_{m}(x)

(6.4)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})(k+1)B_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}\sum_{k=m+2}^{n}\epsilon_{m,k}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P})B_{m}(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sum_{m=0}^{n}(m+1)S_{1}(n,m;\mathbb{P})B_{m}(x),

where
\epsilon_{m,k}=\frac{2}{k+2}\binom{k+2}{m}B_{k-m},\quad(0\leq m\leq n-2,m+2\leq k\leq n)
.

By comparing (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we have

(6.5)

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}=(m+1)S_{1}(n,m;\mathbb{P})+\sum_{k=m+2}^{n}\epsilon_{m,k}S_{1}(n,k;\mathbb{P}),\quad(0\leq m\leq n),

where we understand that the sum is zero for
m=n-1
and
m=n
. Let
\Gamma
be the column vector consisting of
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
, and let
S
be the column vector consisting of
S_{1}(n,0;\mathbb{P}),S_{1}(n,1;\mathbb{P}),\dots,S_{1}(n,n;\mathbb{P})
. Then, in matrix form, (
6.5
) can be written as
\Gamma=AS
, where
A
is an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
upper triangular matrix with diagonal entries
1,2,\dots,n+1
. Thus
S=A^{-1}\Gamma
.

7.
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced Stirling numbers of both kinds associated with sequences of polynomilas and studied them by using umbral calculus techniques, which was motivated by the observation that many important special numbers appear in the expansions of some polynomials in terms of falling factorials and vice versa. Our results were illustrated by twenty examples, both for the second kind and for the first kind. In all cases of these examples, some interesting inverse relations were obtained as immediate consequences of our results. The novelty of this paper is that it is the first paper which studies the Stirling numbers of both kinds associated with any sequence of polynomials in a unified and systematic way with the help of umbral calculus.

In a forthcoming paper, we study the central factorial numbers of the first kind and those of the second kind associated with sequences of polynomials with the help of umbral calculus techniques.

In general, it is one of our future projects to study special numbers and polynomials by making use of various tools, including combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus,
p
-adic analysis, probability theory, differential equations, analytic number theory, operator theory, special functions and so on.

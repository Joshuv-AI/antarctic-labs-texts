2-step nilpotent L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebras and Hypergraphs
Marco Aldi and Samuel Bevins

Abstract
. We describe a procedure to attach a nilpotent strong homotopy Lie algebra to every simple hypergraph and prove that two hypergraphs are isomorphic if and only if the corresponding strong homotopy Lie algebras are isomorphic. As an application, we characterize hypergraphs admitting a system of distinct representatives in terms of symplectic forms on the corresponding strong homotopy Lie algebra. We conclude with a combinatorial description of the cohomology of these strong homotopy Lie algebras in low degree.



1.
Introduction

Motivated by the study of existence of Anosov diffeomorphisms on nilmanifolds, Dani and Mainkar
[
2
]
introduced a construction that to each simple graph
G
associates a 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}(G)
. An appealing feature of the resulting 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras is that much of their structure (and that of their associated Lie groups and nilmanifolds) can be described in purely graph-theoretical terms. For this reason, the Dani-Mainkar construction has been exploited to exhibit interesting examples of symplectic nilmanifolds
[
14
]
, of Einstein solvmanifolds
[
10
]
, and of geodesic flows on nilmanifolds
[
13
]
.

In a different direction, Mainkar
[
11
]
has shown that two simple graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are isomorphic if and only if
\mathcal{L}(G_{1})
and
\mathcal{L}(G_{2})
are isomorphic as Lie algebras. This result suggests the possibility to translate graph theoretic notions into the language of Lie algebras. Conversely, looking at
\mathcal{L}(G)
through the lenses of Lie theory can shed new light on the underlying graph
G
. For instance, it is natural to look at the Cartan-Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of
\mathcal{L}(G)
as an invariant of
G
. While the general structure of the cohomology of
\mathcal{L}(G)
is not well understood, it is known
[
14
]
that
H^{2}(\mathcal{L}(G))
detects triangles in
G
.

In this paper we extend the Dani-Mainkar construction to simple hypergraphs
G
in such a way that the corresponding Lie-theoretic object
\mathcal{L}(G)
is a 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra. Our first main result is that Mainkar’s theorem still holds: given two simple hypergraphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
,
G_{1}
is isomorphic to
G_{2}
if and only if
\mathcal{L}(G_{1})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}(G_{2})
as
L_{\infty}
-algebras. As a result, simple hypergraphs can be thought of as particular types of
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Our second main result is about the existence of (graded) symplectic forms on
L_{\infty}
-algebras. We show that
\mathcal{L}(G)
admits a symplectic structure if and only if
G
has a system of distinct representatives. This connection is intriguing in light of the role played by systems of distinct representatives in the study of the Quantum Satisfiability Problem
[
9
,
1
]
.

To further illustrate our construction, we conclude our paper with a study of the cohomology of the Maurer-Cartan algebra of
\mathcal{L}(G)
. One novelty is that while in the case of graphs the Cartan-Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra of the corresponding Dani-Mainkar Lie algebra is always finite dimensional, this is not necessarily the case for simple hypergraphs with edges containing an odd number of vertices. Nevertheless, we prove that
H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is finite dimensional for every integer
i
and for every simple finite hypergraph
G
. Hence the dimensions of these spaces can be thought of as invariants of the hypergraph
G
which, at least in simple cases, we are able to explicitly express in terms of combinatorial data on
G
.

2.
The 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra of a hypergraph

We find it convenient to use grading conventions for
L_{\infty}
-algebras adapted from
[
5
]
, the original source for nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathfrak{g}^{i}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded real vector space that is finite dimensional in each degree and bounded below. Let
\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}
be the shifted dual, so that
(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})^{i}
is the dual vector space of
\mathfrak{g}^{1-i}
. For each
k>0
, let
l_{k}
be a sequence of graded antisymmetric operations on
\mathfrak{g}
of degree
2-k
(i.e.
l_{k}:\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is homogeneous linear of degree 2) and let
d_{k}:\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}\to{\rm Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})
be the corresponding (degree 1) transpose maps. We say that the maps
\{l_{k}\}
form an
L_{\infty}
-algebra (or strong homotopy Lie algebra)
on
\mathfrak{g}
if the unique extension of
d=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}d_{k}
to
{\rm Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee})
by graded derivation is a differential i.e.
d^{2}=0
. If this is the case, we denote by
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})=({\rm Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[1]^{\vee}),d)
its
Maurer-Cartan algebra
(an explanation for this terminology may be found in
[
6
]
) and by
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
the corresponding cohomology.

Remark 2
.

In
[
7
]
and references therein, an
L_{\infty}
-structure on
a graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}

is defined as a coalgebra differential on the graded
symmetric coalgebra
S^{\prime}[s\mathfrak{g}]
on the suspension
s\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Under the circumstances of the present paper, this characterization is equivalent to Definition
1
above.

Definition 3
.

Two
L_{\infty}
-algebras with underlying graded vector spaces
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
are
isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism
f:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
of graded vector spaces such that the induced map
f^{\bullet}:C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{2})\to C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
of differential graded algebras is an isomorphism.

Remark 4
.

Equivalently,
f:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\to\mathfrak{g}_{2}
induces an isomorphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras if
l_{k,2}(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{k}))=f(l_{k,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}))
for each
k\geq 1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, where
l_{k,1}
(resp.
l_{k,2}
) are the
L_{\infty}
operations on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
(resp. on
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
).

Definition 5
.

The
lower central filtration

[
5
]
of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\{l_{k}\}
on
\mathfrak{g}
is the canonical decreasing filtration defined inductively by
F^{1}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}
and, for each
i>1
,

(1)

F^{i}\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}=i}l_{k}(F^{i_{1}}\mathfrak{g},\ldots,F^{i_{k}}\mathfrak{g})\,.

An
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
if
F^{N}\mathfrak{g}=0
for all sufficiently large
N
.

Definition 6
.

We say that an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\{l_{k}\}
on
\mathfrak{g}
is
2-step nilpotent
if

(2)

l_{k}(l_{j}(\mathfrak{g},\ldots,\mathfrak{g}),\mathfrak{g},\ldots,\mathfrak{g})=0

for all
k,j\geq 1
.

Remark 7
.

Let
\{l_{k}\}
be a 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra on
\mathfrak{g}
such that
l_{k}=0
for all
k\geq N
. Then
F^{N}\mathfrak{g}=0
, and thus
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\})
, is nilpotent.

Definition 8
.

We define the
commutator
of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\{l_{k}\}
on
\mathfrak{g}
to be the
L_{\infty}
-subalgebra

(3)

l(\mathfrak{g})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}l_{k}(\mathfrak{g},\ldots,\mathfrak{g})\,.

Example 9
.

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{0}
and
l_{k}=0
for all
k\neq 2
, then
(\mathfrak{g},l_{2})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra if and only if it is a Lie algebra. In this case,
l(\mathfrak{g})=l_{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is the ordinary Cartan-Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}
is 2-step nilpotent as an
L_{\infty}
-algebra if and only if it is 2-step nilpotent as a Lie algebra.

Definition 10
.

A
hypergraph

G
is a set (the
vertices
)
V(G)
together with, for each
k>0
, collections (the
k
-
edges
) of (unordered)
k
-element subsets of
V(G)
. We use the notation
E(G)=\bigcup_{k}E_{k}(G)
for the set of all
edges
of
G
. We say that
G
is
finite
if
V(G)
is a finite set. We say that
G
is
{\it simple}
if the symmetric difference of any two distinct edges is nonempty. A hypergraph
G
is
k
-uniform
if
E_{i}(G)=\emptyset
for all
i\neq k
.

Definition 11
.

Let
G
be a finite simple hypergraph with
n
vertices and let
V
be the real vector space generated the elements
x_{i}
labeled by
i\in V(G)
. For each
k>0
let
W_{k}
be the subspace of
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}V
generated by
x_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i_{k}}
such that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\notin E_{k}(G)
.
The 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra of
G
has underlying graded vector space

(4)

\mathcal{L}(G)=V[0]\oplus(V/W_{1})[1]\oplus((\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}V)/W_{2})[0]\oplus\cdots\oplus((\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{n}V)/W_{n})[2-n]\,.

The
L_{\infty}
-algebra operations
l_{k}
are given by the canonical projections
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}V\to(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}V)/W_{k}
i.e.

(5)

l_{k}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})=x_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i_{k}}\mod W_{k}

for all
x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}\in V
. In all other cases (when at least one of the arguments is in
(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{p}V)/W_{i}[2-p]
for some
p\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
),
l_{k}
is declared to vanish.

Remark 12
.

If
G
is a finite simple graph, then
\mathcal{L}(G)
coincides with the 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra constructed by Dani and Mainkar
[
2
]
.

Remark 13
.

In practice, it is convenient to fix an order on
V(G)
, or equivalently, to identify
V(G)
with
\{1,\ldots,|V(G)|\}
with the standard order. Then
\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}V/W_{k}
acquires a canonical basis consisting of elements of the form
x_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i_{k}}
where
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
and
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E_{k}(G)
. Let
x_{i}^{*}
,
x_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}}^{*}
be the elements of the dual basis labeled by all vertices
i\in V(G)
and edges
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E(G)
. Then the components
d_{k}
of the differential of the Maurer-Cartan algebra
C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{L}(G))
can be written explicitly as
d_{k}(x_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}}^{*})=x_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{*}
.

Theorem 14
.

Two finite simple hypergraphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
are isomorphic if and only if
\mathcal{L}(G)
and
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
are isomorphic as
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Proof.

The particular case in which
G
and
G^{\prime}
are graphs is proved in
[
11
]
. The general case is proved along the same lines, we provide a sketch for sake of completeness. If
f:G\to G^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of hypergraphs, it is easy to see that the induced map
f:\mathcal{L}(G)\to\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
is an isomorphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras. For the converse, assume that
f:\mathcal{L}(G)\to\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
is an isomorphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras and that
E_{k}(G)
and
E_{k}(G^{\prime})
are empty for all
k
greater than a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
V
be as in Definition
11
i.e. the vector space generated by vectors
x_{i}
labeled by
i\in V(G)
. Let
T
be the group of all
L_{\infty}
-algebra automorphisms
\tau
of
\mathcal{L}(G)
such that
\tau_{|_{V}}\in{\rm GL}(V)
. Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\rm GL}(V)
be the subgroup of all linear automorphisms of the form form
\tau_{|_{V}}
for some
\tau\in T
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{G}
is the group of automorphisms
g
of
V
such that the induced maps
\wedge^{k}g:\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}V\to\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{k}V
preserve
W_{k}
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
. Since these conditions can be expressed as polynomial equations,
\mathcal{G}
is an algebraic group. Let
D
be the subgroup of
{\rm GL}(V)
consisting of all automorphisms diagonalized by the standard basis of vertices of
G
. Since
D(W_{k})\subseteq W_{k}
for all
k=1,\ldots,n
, then
D
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
.

We construct a new hypergraph
G^{\prime\prime}
in the following way. Let
V^{\prime}
be the vector space generated by
V(G^{\prime})
and let
W_{k}^{\prime}
be such that

(6)

\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})=V^{\prime}[0]\oplus(V^{\prime}/W^{\prime}_{1})[1]\oplus((\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{2}V^{\prime})/W_{2}^{\prime})[0]\oplus\cdots\oplus((\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{n}V^{\prime})/W_{n}^{\prime})[2-n]\,,

where
n=|V(G^{\prime})|
.
Let
\pi:\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})\to V^{\prime}
be the canonical projection. We define
G^{\prime\prime}
to be the hypergraph with vertices

(7)

V(G^{\prime\prime})=\{\pi(f(x_{i}))\,|\,i\in V(G)\}

and edges

(8)

E_{k}(G^{\prime\prime})=\{\{\pi(f(x_{i_{1}})),\ldots,\pi(f(x_{i_{k}}))\}\,|\,\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E_{k}(G)\}\,.

Let
l(\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime}))
be the commutator of
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
. Since
f
is an isomorphism of
L_{\infty}
-algebras, we have
\sum_{i\in V(G)}a_{i}\pi(f(x_{i}))=0
. This implies that
f\left(\sum_{i\in V(G)}a_{i}x_{i}\right)
is an element of
l(\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime}))
and thus
\sum_{i\in V(G)}a_{i}x_{i}
is an element of
l(\mathcal{L}(G))
. On the other hand, by construction
x_{i}\in V
for every
i\in V(G)
. Therefore,
\sum_{i\in V(G)}a_{i}x_{i}=0
. Since
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is a linearly independent set, then
a_{i}=0
for all
i\in V(G)
. Since
\mathcal{L}(G)
and
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
are isomorphic, then

(9)

|V(G)|=\dim(\mathcal{L}(G)/l(\mathcal{L}(G)))=\dim(\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})/l(\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})))=|V(G^{\prime})|\,.

On the other hand,
|V(G^{\prime})|=|V(G^{\prime\prime})|
because
V(G^{\prime})
and
V(G^{\prime\prime})
are both bases of the same vector space. Hence
\pi\circ f:V(G)\to V(G^{\prime\prime})
is a bijection identifying edges of
G
with edges of
G^{\prime\prime}
and thus defining a graph isomorphism.

We claim that the subgroup
D^{\prime\prime}\subseteq{\rm GL}(V^{\prime})
consisting of elements diagonalized by the basis
V(G^{\prime\prime})=\{\pi(f(x_{i}))\,|\,i\in V(G)\}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Let
d^{\prime\prime}\in D^{\prime\prime}
with eigenvalues
d^{\prime\prime}_{i}
such that,
d^{\prime\prime}(\pi(f(x_{i})))=d^{\prime\prime}_{i}\pi(f(x_{i}))
for some nonzero
d^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and for all
i\in V(G)
. Take
i_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{k}^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime})
where
\{i_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{k}^{\prime}\}\notin E_{k}(G^{\prime})
. Since
x_{i_{j}^{\prime}}\in V^{\prime}
and
V(G^{\prime\prime})
is a basis of
V^{\prime}
, we can represent these elements as

(10)

x_{i_{j}^{\prime}}=\displaystyle{\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,j}\pi(f(x_{p}))}

for suitable coefficients
a_{p,j}\in\mathbb{R}
. We observe that the subspace
W_{k}^{\prime}
is invariant under the action of
d^{\prime\prime}
in the following way. Since
l_{k}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}^{\prime}})=0\mod W_{k}^{\prime}
, we see that

(11)

l_{k}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}^{\prime}})=l_{k}\left(\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,1}\pi(f(x_{p})),\ldots,\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,k}\pi(f(x_{p}))\right)=0\mod W_{k}^{\prime}\,.

Therefore, since
l_{k}(v_{1}^{\prime}+w_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{k}^{\prime}+w_{k}^{\prime})=0
if and only if
l_{k}(v_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,v_{k}^{\prime})=0
for all
v_{j}^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
and
w_{j}^{\prime}\in\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathord{\hbox{\raise 10.00012pt\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{{\hbox{{$\bigwedge$}}}}}}}^{i}V^{\prime}\right)/W_{i}^{\prime}
,

(12)

l_{k}\left(\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,1}x_{p},\ldots,\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,k}x_{p}\right)=0\mod W_{k}\,.

Let
\sigma\in{\rm GL}(V)
such that
\sigma(x_{i})=d_{i}^{\prime\prime}x_{i}
for each
i\in V(G)
. Then
\sigma
is in
D
, the collection of automorphisms of
V
diagonalized by the
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in V(G)}
. Since
D
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
, we have

(13)

l_{k}\left(\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,1}\sigma(x_{p}),\ldots,\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,k}\sigma(x_{p})\right)=0\mod W_{k}\,,

which implies

(14)

l_{k}\left(\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,1}d_{i_{1}}^{\prime\prime}x_{p},\ldots,\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,k}d_{i_{k}}^{\prime\prime}x_{p}\right)=0\mod W_{k}\,,

and thus

(15)

l_{k}\left(\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,1}d_{i_{1}}^{\prime\prime}\pi(f(x_{p})),\ldots,\sum_{p\in V(G)}a_{p,k}d_{i_{k}}^{\prime\prime}\pi(f(x_{p}))\right)=0\mod W_{k}^{\prime}\,.

Hence
l_{k}(d^{\prime\prime}(x_{i_{1}}),\ldots,d^{\prime\prime}(x_{i_{k}}))=0
modulo
W_{k}^{\prime}
and
l_{k}(d^{\prime\prime}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}}),\ldots,d^{\prime\prime}(x_{i_{k}^{\prime}}))=0
in
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
. Hence
D^{\prime\prime}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Upon passing to the complexification of all vector spaces involved,
D^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime\prime}
are maximal tori in the connected component of the identity of the algebraic group
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Therefore (see e.g.
[
15
]
)
D^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime\prime}
are conjugated i.e. there exists an element
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
such that

(16)

D^{\prime}=gD^{\prime\prime}g^{-1}\,.

Then for each
i^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime})
, choose nonzero scalars
d_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}
such that (unless
\{i_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{m}^{\prime}\}=\{j_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,j_{m}^{\prime}\}
),

(17)

{\prod_{l=1}^{m}d_{i_{l}}^{\prime}\neq\prod_{l=1}^{m}d_{j_{l}}^{\prime}}\,.

Then, it follows that for each
d^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}
, there exists an element
d^{\prime\prime}\in D^{\prime\prime}
such that
d^{\prime}=gd^{\prime\prime}g^{-1}
. Then
d^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime\prime}
are similar and share the same eigenvalues and so are the same up to a permutation. Therefore, a bijection
f^{\prime}
exists between
V(G^{\prime})
and
V(G^{\prime\prime})
such that

(18)

d^{\prime\prime}(f^{\prime}(x_{i^{\prime}}))=d_{i}^{\prime}f^{\prime}(x_{i^{\prime}}),\quad\forall i^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime}).

Lastly, we establish that
\{i_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{k}^{\prime}\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime})
if and only if
\{f^{\prime}(i_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,f^{\prime}(i_{k}^{\prime})\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime\prime})
.
We know that
\{i_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{k}^{\prime}\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime})
if and only if
l_{k}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}^{\prime}})\neq 0
and
\{f^{\prime}(i_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,f^{\prime}(i_{k}^{\prime})\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime\prime})
if and only if
l_{k}(f^{\prime}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}}),\ldots,f^{\prime}(x_{i_{k}^{\prime}}))\neq 0
. Then
f^{\prime}(x_{i^{\prime}})=\pi(f(x_{i}))
for some
i\in V(G)
, so
\{f^{\prime}(i_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,f^{\prime}(i_{k}^{\prime})\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime\prime})
if and only if
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E_{k}(G)
. Since
f
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra isomorphism and
\pi
is the canonical linear map, it follows that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E_{k}(G)
if and only if
l_{k}(\pi(f(x_{i_{1}})),\ldots,\pi(f(x_{i_{k}})))\neq 0
in
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
. If follows from (
17
) that
\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_{i_{j}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is an eigenvalue of an extended automorphism
d^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime})
if and only if
l_{k}(f^{\prime}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}}),\ldots,f^{\prime}(x_{i_{k}^{\prime}}))\neq 0
in
\mathcal{L}(G^{\prime\prime})
. Since
d^{\prime}=gd^{\prime\prime}g^{-1}
for some
g\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
,

(19)

l_{k}(g^{-1}(d^{\prime}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}})),\ldots,g^{-1}(d^{\prime}(x_{i_{k}^{\prime}})))=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_{i_{j}^{\prime}}^{\prime}\right)l_{k}(g^{-1}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}}),\ldots,g^{-1}(x_{i_{k}^{\prime}})).

Moreover,
g
being an
L_{\infty}
-automorphism,
l_{k}(g^{-1}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}}),\ldots,g^{-1}(x_{i_{k}^{\prime}}))\neq 0
if and only if
l_{k}(x_{i_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}^{\prime}})\neq 0
. Thus,
\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_{i_{j}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is an eigenvalue of
d^{\prime\prime}
if and only if
\prod_{j=1}^{k}d_{i_{j}^{\prime}}^{\prime}
is an eigenvalue of
d^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\{i_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{k}^{\prime}\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime})
if and only if
\{f^{\prime}(i_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,f^{\prime}(i_{k}^{\prime})\}\in E_{k}(G^{\prime\prime})
from which we conclude that
f^{\prime}
is a graph isomorphism between
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
.

∎

3.
System of Distinct Representatives and Symplectic Forms

Definition 15
.

Let
G
be a hypergraph. A
system of distinct representatives
is an injection
f:E(G)\to V(G)
such that
f(e)\in e
for every
e\in E(G)
.

Example 16
.

If
G
is a (finite simple) graph, then
G
has a system of distinct representatives if and only if every connected component of
G
has at most as many edges as vertices. For more general hypergraphs, the condition of having a system of distinct representatives is less trivial. For instance, if
V(G)=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
and
E(G)=\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{3,4\},\{2,4\},\{4,5,6\}\}
then
G
is connected and
|E(G)|=|V(G)|
but, for instance by applying Hall’s Marriage Theorem (see e.g.
[
8
]
),
G
does not have a system of distinct representatives.

Definition 17
.

A
symplectic form
on an
L_{\infty}
-algebra on the graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear map
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}

that is non-degenerate and closed as an element of
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark 18
.

The following Lemma is a generalization of Proposition 4.8 in
[
4
]
to 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebras.

Lemma 19
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\})
be a 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra with symplectic form
\omega
. Then
2\dim l(\mathfrak{g})\leq\dim\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},y_{1},\ldots,y_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is 2-step nilpotent, then

(20)

l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p-1},\widehat{x_{p}},x_{p+1},\ldots,x_{k},l_{j}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{j}))=0

for every
p\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
.
Taking into account the definition of the differential of the Maurer-Cartan algebra, and the fact that
\omega
is closed, we obtain

(21)

\omega(l_{k}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}),l_{j}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{j}))=(d_{k}\omega)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},l_{j}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{j}))=0\,.

Hence
l(\mathfrak{g})
is an isotropic subspace for the bilinear form
\omega
on the real vector space underlying
\mathfrak{g}
. As a result, the dimension of
l(\mathfrak{g})
is at most half of the dimension of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is what we needed to prove.
∎

Remark 20
.

The above calculation shows that a symplectic form
\omega
on a 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
defines an injective linear map
\omega^{\flat}:l(\mathfrak{g})\to(\mathfrak{g}/l(\mathfrak{g}))^{\vee}
. In the particular case of
L_{\infty}
-algebras of the form
\mathcal{L}(G)
for some finite simple hypergraph
G
endowed with a system of distinct representatives
f:E(G)\to V(G)
, we also have an induced map from
l(\mathcal{L}(G))={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}(E(G))
to
(\mathcal{L}(G)/(l(\mathcal{L}(G))))^{\vee}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}(V(G))^{\vee}
defined by
x_{I}\mapsto x_{f(I)}^{*}
for every
I\in E(G)
. The connection between these two constructions is provided by the following theorem.

Theorem 21
.

Let
G
be a finite simple hypergraph. The 2-step nilpotent
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathcal{L}(G)
is symplectic if and only if
|E(G)|+|V(G)|
is even and
G
has a system of distinct representatives.

Proof.

Assume
|E(G)|+|V(G)|
is even and let
f:E(G)\to V(G)
be a system of distinct representatives. Then
S=V(G)\setminus f(E(G))
has an even number of elements. Let
\sigma
be a fixed-point free involution of
S
. Then

(22)

\omega=\sum_{I\in E(G)}x_{I}^{*}x_{f(I)}^{*}+\sum_{i\in S,i<\sigma(I)}x_{i}^{*}x_{\sigma(i)}^{*}

is a symplectic form on
\mathcal{L}(G)
. For the converse, assume
\omega
is a symplectic form on
\mathcal{L}(G)
. By Lemma
19
, this implies that
|E(G)|\leq|V(G)|
. Moreover,
\omega
is of the form

(23)

\omega=\sum_{i,j\in V(G)}\omega_{i,j}x_{i}^{*}x_{j}^{*}+\sum_{I\in E(G)}\varphi_{I}x_{I}^{*}\,,

where
\varphi_{I}=\omega^{\flat}(x_{I})
. Suppose
G
does not have a system of distinct representatives. By Hall’s Marriage Theorem, for some positive integer
r
there exist edges
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\in E(G)
such that
|I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}|<r
. Since, by Remark
20
,
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{I_{1}},\ldots,\varphi_{I_{r}})
has dimension
r
, there exist
p\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and
i\notin I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}
such that
\varphi_{I_{p}}(x_{i})\neq 0
. Let
I_{p}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}
. In order for
(d\omega)(x_{i},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}})
to vanish, there has to be
J\in E_{k}(G)
such that
I_{p}\cup J=\{x_{i},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}\}
and
i\in J
. Then
|I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r}\cup J|<r+1
. Iterating this construction we add an edge at each step, until eventually we obtain a collection
\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{r},\ldots,I_{r^{\prime}}\}
of edges such that
|I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r^{\prime}}|<r^{\prime}
but
\varphi_{I_{p}}(x_{i})=0
for all
i\notin I_{1}\cup\cdots\cup I_{r^{\prime}}
. This contradicts the linear independence of
\{\varphi_{I_{1}},\ldots,\varphi_{I_{r^{\prime}}}\}
and concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 22
.

Taking Example
16
into account, Theorem
21
can be thought of as generalizing to hypergraphs the characterization of symplectic Lie algebras associated with graphs found in
[
14
]
.

Let
G
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph with a transfer filtration of type
|V(G)|-|E(G)|+1
in the sense of
[
1
]
. Then
G
has a system of distinct representatives and thus
\mathcal{L}(G)

Lemma 24
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded vector space, let
d,\Delta:\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet+1}
be commuting differentials, and let
\theta:\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet-1}
be a linear map such that
\theta\Delta+\Delta\theta={\rm id}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}=\theta\Delta\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
, and let
D=\theta\Delta d:\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{D}^{\bullet+1}
. Then

1)

D^{2}=0
and there is a long exact sequence

(24)

\cdots\to H^{i-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)\xrightarrow{\theta d}H^{i-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)\xrightarrow{\Delta}H^{i}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet},d)\xrightarrow{\theta\Delta}H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)\xrightarrow{\theta d}H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)\to\cdots

2)

Assume further that
\Delta=d\varphi-\varphi d
for some linear map
\varphi:\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}
such that
[\varphi,\theta]=0
. Then
\ker((\theta d)^{n})\subseteq{\rm im}(\theta d)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

A lengthy but straightforward verification shows that

(25)

0\to(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet-1},-D)\xrightarrow{\Delta}(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet},d)\xrightarrow{\theta\Delta}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)\to 0

is a short exact sequence of cochain complexes.
Taking the resulting long exact sequence and using the isomorphism
H^{i-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)\cong H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet-1},-D)
, we obtain (
24
). It is straightforward to check from the definition that the connecting homomorphism is indeed
\theta d
. This proves 1). To prove 2), we first observe that
[\theta d,\varphi]=\theta\Delta
which acts as the identity on
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}
. If
x\in\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}
is such that
(\theta d)^{n}x=0
then

(26)

x=\theta d\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\varphi^{k}(-\theta d)^{k-1}}{k!}\right)x.

explicitly showing that
\ker((\theta d)^{n})\subseteq{\rm im}(\theta d)
.
∎

Example 25
.

Let
(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet},d)
be the Cartan-Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be closed, so that
\ker(\alpha)
is a codimension 1 ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\Delta
denotes multiplication by
\alpha
and
\theta
denotes contraction with an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\alpha(x)=1
, then
(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
is isomorphic to the Cartan-Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra of
\ker(\alpha)
and (
24
) specializes to the Dixmier long exact sequence
[
3
]
, generalizing
[
14
]
.

Example 26
.

Our main application is to the case in which
(\mathcal{C}^{\bullet},d)
is the Maurer-Cartan algebra of an
L_{\infty}
-algebra of the form
\mathcal{L}(G)
for some finte simple hypergraph
G
. Generalizing the use of the Dixmier exact sequence in
[
14
]
, we select a vertex, say,
1\in V(G)
and remove it from
G
while keeping track of the edges that contained it. The result is the complex
(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
whose underlying graded vector space
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}
consists of polynomials in
{\rm Sym}((\mathcal{L}(G))^{\vee})
that do not contain the (odd) variable
x_{1}^{*}
. The differential
D
acts as follows.
D(x_{i}^{*})=0
for all
i>1
,
D(x_{I}^{*})=0
if
I
is an edge of
G
that contains
1
, and
D(x_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}}^{*})=x_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{*}
whenever
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E_{k}(G)
and
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}
. If
\Delta
denotes multiplication by
x_{1}^{*}
and
\theta=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}^{*}}
then it is easy to see that the assumptions of part 1) of Lemma
24
are satisfied. Hence (
24
) holds with connecting homomorphism
\delta=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}^{*}}\circ d
. In particular,
H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is generated as a vector space by the coimage of
\delta
acting on
H^{i-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
and the cocycles that map onto the kernel of
\delta
acting on
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
under the projection
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}^{*}}\circ x_{1}^{*}
. In the particular case in which
\{1\}\in E_{1}(G)
,
\Delta=d\varphi-\varphi d
holds with
\varphi
being multiplication by
x_{1}^{*}
. Then the assumptions of part 2) of Lemma
24
are also satisfied. Since
\mathcal{D}^{\bullet}
is generated by monomials of the form
(x_{\{1\}}^{*})^{r}x_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots x_{j_{k}}^{*}x_{I_{j_{1}}}^{*}\cdots x_{I_{j_{l}}}^{*}
,
each of which is
\ker(\delta^{N})
for sufficiently large
N
, then, by Lemma
24
,
\delta
is surjective. Thus,
H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is isomorphic, as a vector space, to the kernel of
\delta
acting on
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
.

Theorem 27
.

Let
G
be a finite simple hypergraph. Then
H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is finite dimensional for every
i\geq 0
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
|V(G)|
, using Lemma
24
as described in Example
26
. If
\{1\}\notin E_{1}(G)
then, by induction hypothesis,
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
and
H^{i-1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
are finite-dimensional and hence so is
H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
by part 1) of Lemma
24
. On the other hand, if
\{1\}\in E_{1}(G)
, then part 2) of Lemma
24
shows that
H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is isomorphic to the kernel of
\delta=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}^{*}}\circ d
acting on
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
. The vector space
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
can be further identified with polynomials in
x_{\{1\}}^{*}
with coefficients in a finite dimensional vector space
U^{i}
, which is closed under the action of
\delta
. Moreover, arguing as in Example
26
,
U^{i}=\ker\delta^{N}
for some positive integer
N
. Whence we obtain a filtration

(27)

\ker\delta\subseteq\ker\delta^{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\ker\delta^{N-1}\subseteq\ker\delta^{N}=U^{i}\,.

Consider a (finite) basis
\{\gamma_{j}\}
of
U^{i}/\ker\delta^{N-1}
. Then the most general element of
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)
modulo polynomials in
x_{\{1\}}^{*}
with coefficients in
\ker\delta^{N-1}
is of the form
\sum_{j}Q_{j}\gamma_{j}
for some
Q_{j}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\{1\}}^{*}]
. Hence
\delta(\sum_{j}Q_{j}\gamma_{j})=\sum_{j}Q^{\prime}_{j}\gamma_{j}
modulo polynomials in
x_{\{1\}}^{*}
with coefficients in
\ker\delta^{N-1}
, where
Q_{j}^{\prime}
denotes the derivative of
Q_{j}
with respect to
x_{\{1\}}^{*}
. Therefore,
\sum_{j}Q_{j}\gamma_{j}
is in the kernel of
\delta
if and only if
Q_{j}
is a constant polynomial for all
j
. Similarly, let
\{\beta_{k}\}
be a basis of
\ker\delta^{N-1}/\ker\delta^{N-2}
so that the most general element in
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)/(\ker\delta^{N-2})[x_{\{1\}}^{*}]
can be written as
\sum_{j}Q_{j}\gamma_{j}+\sum_{k}R_{k}\beta_{k}
,
R_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\{1\}}^{*}]
. Moreover, there exist real numbers
a_{jk}
such that
\delta\gamma_{j}=\sum_{k}a_{jk}\beta_{k}
for all
j
. Hence

(28)

\delta\left(\sum_{j}Q_{j}\gamma_{j}+\sum_{k}R_{k}\beta_{k}\right)=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{j}a_{jk}Q_{k}+R_{k}^{\prime}\right)\beta_{k}

modulo polynomials with coefficients in
\ker\delta^{N-2}
. It follows that
\sum_{j}a_{jk}Q_{k}+R_{k}^{\prime}=0
so that, for each
k
,
R_{k}
is a polynomial of degree at most one in the variable
x_{\{1\}}^{*}
. Continuing the process, we see that in order for a linear combination in
H^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)/(\ker^{N-p})
to lift to an element in the kernel of
\delta
acting on
H^{i}(\mathcal{D},D)
, the degree of the polynomials in
x_{\{1\}}^{*}
appearing as coefficients cannot be greater than
p-1\leq N
.

∎

Definition 28
.

Let
G
be a finite simple hypergraph. For every
i\geq 0
, the
i
-th
L_{\infty}
-Betti number of
G
is the integer
b_{i}(G)=\dim H^{i}(\mathcal{L}(G))
. The
L_{\infty}
-Poincaré series of
G
is the formal power series
P_{t}(G)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}(G)t^{i}
.

Remark 29
.

The terminology of Definition
28
is justified by the fact that if
G
is a graph then, by a theorem of Nomizu
[
12
]
,
P_{t}(G)
coincides with the (topological) Poincaré
polynomial of the compact nilmanifold associated to
\mathcal{L}(G)
.

Remark 30
.

Suppose
E_{1}(G)=\emptyset
. Then Theorem
27
is trivial since
C^{k}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is finite dimensional for every
k
. More precisely, the generating function for
\dim(C^{k}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is

(29)

(1+t)^{|V(G)|}\frac{\prod_{p=1}^{\infty}(1+t^{2p-1})^{|E_{2p}(G)|}}{\prod_{q=1}^{\infty}(1-t^{2q})^{|E_{2q+1}(G)|}}\,,

which provides explicit upper bounds for the
L_{\infty}
-Betti numbers of
G
. In particular, if
G
is a hypergraph such that
E_{2k+1}(G)=\emptyset
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
then
P_{t}(G)
is a polynomial.

Example 31
.

Let
G
be the hypergraph with
k
vertices and a single
k
-edge. If
k
is even, then (
29
) specializes to
(1+t)^{k}(1+t^{k-1})
. Moreover, in this case the differential maps
x_{\{1,2,\ldots,k\}}^{*}
to
x_{1}^{*}\cdots x_{k}^{*}
and acts trivially on other monomials. Hence

(30)

P_{t}(G)=(1+t)^{k}(1+t^{k-1})-t^{k-1}-t^{k}\,.

On the other hand, if
k
is odd then (
29
) specializes to

(31)

\frac{(1+t)^{k}}{1-t^{k-1}}

and the only non-zero differentials are the ones mapping

(32)

(x_{\{1,2,\ldots,k\}}^{*})^{j}\mapsto jx_{1}^{*}\cdots x_{k}^{*}(x_{\{1,2,\ldots,k\}}^{*})^{j-1}

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence

(33)

P_{t}(G)=\frac{(1+t)^{k}-t^{k-1}-t^{k}}{1-t^{k-1}}\,.

Remark 32
.

Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be finite simple hypergraphs and consider their disjoint union
G_{1}+G_{2}
. Then
\mathcal{L}(G_{1}+G_{2})=\mathcal{L}(G_{1})\oplus\mathcal{L}(G_{2})
and, using Künneth’s Theorem,
P_{t}(G_{1}+G_{2})=P_{t}(G_{1})P_{t}(G_{2})
.

Remark 33
.

In all examples that we have calculated, the
L_{\infty}
-Poincaré series
P_{t}(G)
is a rational function of
t
. It would be interesting to know if this is a general feature.

Example 34
.

Let
G_{1}
be the hypergraph with
|V(G)|=1=|E_{1}(G)|
. A straightforward calculation shows that
P_{t}(G_{1})=1
. Hence, for any hypergraph
G_{2}
,
P_{t}(G_{1}+G_{2})=P_{t}(G_{1})P_{t}(G_{2})=P_{t}(G_{2})
. In particular, we see that the
L_{\infty}
-Poincaré series does not distinguish the non-isomorphic hypergraphs
G_{1}+G_{2}
and
G_{2}
and hence is not a complete invariant of hypergraphs.

Proposition 38
.

Let
G
be a finite simple hypergraph. Then
b_{1}(G)-|V(G)|+|E_{1}(G)|
is the number of 2-edges
\{i,j\}
such that
\{k\}\in E_{1}(G)
for at least one
k\in\{i,j\}
.

Proof.

If
E_{1}(G)=\emptyset
then the statement reduces to
b_{1}(G)=|V(G)|
, which is a particular case of Proposition
36
. Hence we can work by induction on the number of
1
-edges. Assume
\{1\}\in E_{1}(G)
and let
G\setminus\{1\}
be the hypergraph obtained by removing
1\in V(G)
and all the edges
e\in E(G)
such that

. By induction, it suffices to show that if
\{1\}\in E_{1}(G)
, then
b_{1}(G)-b_{1}(G\setminus\{1\})
is the number of
e\in E_{2}(G)
such that
1\in e
. This can be shown using Lemma
24
, as follows. Since
\{1\}\in E_{1}(G)
, then
H^{1}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is isomorphic to the kernel of
\delta
acting on
H^{1}(\mathcal{D}^{\bullet},D)

is of the form

(34)

\xi=\sum_{\gamma}P_{\gamma}(x_{\{1\}}^{*})\gamma+\sum_{j}Q_{j}(x_{\{1\}}^{*})x_{\{1,j\}}^{*}\,,

where
\gamma
ranges over a basis of
H^{1}(\mathcal{L}(G\setminus\{1\}))

ranges over all the vertices connected to

\delta(x_{\{1,j\}}^{*})=x_{j}^{*}=d(x_{\{j\}}^{*})

∎

Proposition 39
.

Let
G
be a finite simple hypergraph and let
N
be the smallest integer such that
E_{N}(G)\neq\emptyset
. If
N>1
, then

(35)

b_{N}(G)=\binom{|V(G)|}{N}-\binom{|V(G)|}{N+1}+(N-1)|E_{N}(G)|+\sum_{S\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{N+1}}\min(\nu(S),1)\,,

where
\nu(S)
is the number
N
-edges with vertices all contained in
S
.

Proof.

C^{N}(\mathcal{L}(G))
is generated by two types of monomials: type I)
x_{i_{1}}^{*}\cdots x_{i_{N}}^{*}
, and type II)
x_{i_{0}}^{*}x_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}\}}^{*}
. There are
\binom{|V(G)|}{N}
monomials of type I) and they are all cocycles. Of these
|E_{N}(G)|
are coboundaries for a total contribution of
\binom{|V(G)|}{N}-|E_{N}(G)|
linearly independent cocycles from monomials of type I). Since
N
is the smallest possible edge size, no linear combination of monomials of type II) can be a coboundary. In order to count type II) cocycles, it is helpful to consider two subcases. If
i_{0}\in\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}\}

i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{N}

x_{i_{0}}^{*}x_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{N}\}}^{*}

\xi=\sum\alpha_{j}x_{i_{j}}^{*}x_{\{i_{0},i_{i},\ldots,\widehat{i_{j}},i_{N}\}}^{*}\,,

\nu(S)-1

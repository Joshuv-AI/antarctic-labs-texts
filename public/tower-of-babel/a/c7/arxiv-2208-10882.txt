Broader view of bimetric MOND
Mordehai Milgrom

Abstract
All existing treatments of bimetric MOND (BIMOND) – a class of relativistic versions of MOND – have dealt with a rather restricted subclass: The Lagrangian of the interaction between the gravitational degrees of freedom – the two metrics – is a function of a certain single scalar argument built from the difference in connections of the two metrics. I show that the scope of BIMOND is much richer: The two metrics can couple through several scalars to give theories that all have a “good” nonrelativistic (NR) limit – one that accounts correctly, à-la MOND, for the dynamics of galactic systems, including gravitational lensing . This extended-BIMOND framework exhibits a qualitative departure from the way we think of MOND at present, as encapsulated, in all its aspects, by one “interpolating function” of one acceleration variable. After deriving the general field equations, I pinpoint the subclass of theories that satisfy the pivotal requirement of a good NR limit. These involve three independent, quadratic scalar variables. In the NR limit these scalars all reduce to the same acceleration scalar, and the NR theory then does hinge on one function of a single acceleration variable – representing the NR MOND interpolating function, whose form is largely dictated by the observed NR galactic dynamics. However, these scalars take different values, and behave differently, in different relativistic contexts. So, the full richness of the multivariable Lagrangian, as it enters cosmology, for example, or gravitational-wave dynamics, is hardly informed by what we learn of MOND from observations of galactic dynamics. In this paper, I present the formalism, with some generic examples. I also consider some cosmological solutions where the two metrics are small departures from one Friedman-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker metric. This may offer a framework for describing cosmology within the extended BIMOND.

Introduction

The MOND paradigm
Milgrom 1983
extends Newtonian dynamics and general relativity, so as to account for the dynamics of galactic systems, and of the Universe at large, without “dark matter.” MOND proposes that standard dynamics break down in the limit where accelerations in a system are around or below a certain acceleration
a_{0}

a_{0}
– MOND posits that dynamics become spacetime scale invariant, at least in the nonrelativistic (NR) limit, and for systems whose dynamics is governed by gravity
Milgrom 2009
. In the high-acceleration limit, MOND posits a rapid return to standard dynamics (a “correspondence principle”).

Already from the above basic axioms, a large number of “MOND laws” of galactic dynamics follow

The MOND constant appears in various roles in the MOND predictions, and so its value could be deduced from several independent observations, all giving, consistently, a value of

The dynamics of galactic systems is highly NR, and accounting for them within present accuracy does not require a relativistic theory: The highest mean-field gravitational potentials encountered in such systems (and the squared velocities virially related to them) are
\lesssim 10^{-5}c^{2}

However, it has been clear from the outset that we must construct relativistic theories whose NR limit complies with MOND’s basic tenets.
General relativity (GR) reduces to Newtonian dynamics in the NR limit; so if MOND is to extend the latter in the NR regime, there must be a relativistic theory that extends GR and that describes MOND phenomenology in this limit. To boot, there are central astrophysical phenomena that do require a relativistic description.

One of these concerns gravitational lensing by galactic systems and large-scale structure. We are then still in the weak-field limit of gravity itself – the gravitational field is produced by slow moving bodies, and the potential is

We can tell to what extent this equality holds in nature, by comparing the observed motion of slow particles – e.g., of stars or gas rotating around a galaxy – with gravitational lensing by the same galaxy, and see if they are governed by a single potential.

If we want such a comparison to pertain to the MOND-vs-dark-matter issue, we need to make it in low-acceleration systems – i.e., where so-called “mass anomalies” prevail according to MOND – because MOND is posited to coincide very nearly with GR in the high-acceleration regime.

According to a theorem by Lovelock

GR is the only local, generally covariant theory in four-dimensional spacetime, derivable from an action, with a single metric as the gravitational degree of freedom, whose field equations are of second order, and that has special relativity as its no-gravity limit.
A relativistic extension of MOND, as any other generalization of GR, must then break with one of these restrictions.

In MOND, which introduces a fiducial constant with the dimensions of acceleration, this fact is further brought home to us for the following reason: In constructing a relativistic action, we need to identify a quantity constructed from the degrees of freedom – a scalar, if we want the theory to be covariant – that has the dimensions of acceleration. This theory, beyond reproducing NR MOND phenomenology (including lensing), is said to imply that the propagation speed of tensor gravitational waves equals that of light under all circumstances, and it can also reproduce the observed properties of the microwave background radiation and of the formation of large-scale structure.

This relative-acceleration tensor can be used to construct various “acceleration scalars” by various contractions of powers of

From these, one can construct terms in a BIMOND action that represent interactions between the two metrics, and one can choose these interactions so as to give MOND phenomenology in the NR limit.

We also want to retain all the successes of GR in the high-acceleration regime. To achieve this, BIMOND is constructed by adding the BIMOND interaction term to the standard Einstein-Hilbert actions of the two metrics, and decreeing that this interaction vanishes in the high-acceleration limit.

The general, schematic form of the BIMOND gravitational action is thus

I=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int d^{4}x~[A|g|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}R+\hat{A}|\hat{g}|^{\scriptscriptstyle 1/2}\hat{R}+v(g,\hat{g})\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{\scriptscriptstyle-2}\mathcal{M}(\{\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}C\})],

\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}=a_{0}^{-1}
is the MOND length, where I use units where

(5)

\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle\Upsilon}=\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}\Upsilon
.
The form of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Z})
is then determined from MOND phenomenology in the NR limit (it is tantamount to the so-called MOND interpolating function). Thus in such limited BIMOND versions, what we know from the NR phenomenology fully determines the theory also in the relativistic regime.

The main result of this work is that there is a much larger subclass of BIMOND theories that have a single-potential NR MOND phenomenology. Instead of one, there are several independent quadratic scalars (and all their linear combinations) that yield such a phenomenology. The interaction Lagrangian may thus be taken as a multivariable function of these,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Z}_{1},\mathcal{Z}_{2},...)
. In the NR limit, however, all these scalars tend to the same
NR scalar acceleration variable,
\bar{\mathcal{Z}}
, and the interaction reduces to a function of this single variable,
\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{\mathcal{Z}})
, yielding the NR, MOND interpolating function, which is determined by NR phenomenology. As a consequence, knowledge of
\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{\mathcal{Z}})
constrains the full
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Z}_{1},\mathcal{Z}_{2},...)
only very poorly. For example, there are acceleration scalars,
\mathcal{Z}
, that do not contribute at all in the NR limit, but may be important beyond it, and so, the dependence of the theory on them disappears altogether in the NR limit.

Here, I ignore the important questions of stability properties and the possible existence of ghosts in such theories. These theories are in any event, at best, effective theories that clearly do not encapsule the whole story. So perhaps even theories that on the face of it are unstable may be cured by adding elements. In particular, this may be so in the context of MOND, where departures from (the stable) GR are characterized by a MOND length
\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle M}\sim\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle U}
and a MOND time of the order of the Hubble time.

As shown in Ref.
Milgrom 2019a
, BIMOND, with the “auxiliary” metric constrained to be flat, is equivalent to so-called
f(Q)
formulations of MOND. So the present study also enlarges the class of MOND
f(Q)
theories with a good NR limit.

Even if BIMOND itself turns out to be lacking in some regards (for example, if it is not able to describe the observed cosmology in full), it is useful to study it as a possible guide to better theories.

I note, finally, that all full-fledged, relativistic MOND theories propounded to date (including the one I discuss here), might be described as “modified gravity,” in the sense that they involve a modification of the Einstein-Hilbert action – the “gravitational,” or the “free” action of gravity in GR – without modifying the matter actions. The latter comprise the free (inertial) matter actions, and the interactions between matter degrees of freedom. There might be other routes to a fundamental MOND theory, involving, for example, modifications of the free matter actions (perhaps all parts of the action). These go under the name of “modified inertia.” Only preliminary suggestions in this vein have been considered so far, and only for the NR regime (see the recent Ref.
Milgrom 2022
).

II.1
Symmetrization with respect to metric interchange

There are interesting BIMOND theories that do not treat the two metrics in a symmetric way. For example, the MOND theory described in Ref.
Milgrom 2019a
, as well as
f(Q)
formulations of MOND
Milgrom 2019a
;
D’Ambrosio 2020
are equivalent to a metric-asymmetric BIMOND, with the auxiliary metric constrained to be flat.
But, symmetric theories have their appeal, and restricting ourselves to treating them, as I do henceforth, reduces the treatment to more manageable proportions.

Such a symmetry of the theory can be achieved in different ways. For example, we can employ scalars that are themselves not necessarily symmetric to metric interchange, but symmetrize the theory by taking with each term in the Lagrangian a term that is gotten from it by metric interchange.
Alternatively, we can construct the Lagrangian from scalars that have built-in metric-exchange symmetry, which is how I proceed here, for the sake of concreteness.

Since
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}
is antisymmetric to metric interchange, a product of an even number of them is interchange symmetric. But to form scalars we have to contract the indices using the two metrics and their inverses. A product of two
C
s cannot be contracted with the metrics in a symmetric way because it presents two upper and four lower indices to contract.

Thus, I have in mind linear combinations of the symmetrized

\frac{1}{2}[\textbf{Q}(\textbf{g},\hat{\textbf{g}})+\textbf{Q}(\hat{\textbf{g}},\textbf{g})],

(10)

for example, we can take as symmetrize basic scalars

\textbf{Q}_{i}(\textbf{g},\hat{\textbf{g}})=\frac{1}{2}[\textbf{Q}_{i}(\textbf{g},\textbf{g})+\textbf{Q}_{i}(\hat{\textbf{g}},\hat{\textbf{g}})]~~~~(i=1,...,5)

(11)

with
\textbf{Q}_{i}(\textbf{g},\textbf{g})
corresponding to the scalars in Eq. (
9
).

In what follows I shall speak of
S_{i}
with the understanding that they are symmetrized with respect to metric interchange.

Having said that, in the application of BIMOND I shall consider in this paper, the two metrics will be small departures from a common reference metric
{\bf\tilde{g}}
, and I shall consider the lowest order in these departures.
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}
vanish when the metrics are equal and are of first order in their small difference. Thus, to lowest order only
\textbf{Q}({\bf\tilde{g}},{\bf\tilde{g}})
will be used to construct the scalars, and this gives, automatically, interchange-symmetric scalars. So the whole issue of the symmetry will not really be brought to bear here.

III
The theory

After all the restrictions described above – made essentially for the sake of concreteness and tractability – I consider the following BIMOND action, symmetric in the two metrics, and employing only quadratic scalars:

I=I_{\scriptscriptstyle G}(g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu})+I_{\scriptscriptstyle M}(g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},\Psi)+\hat{I}_{\scriptscriptstyle M}(\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},\hat{\Psi}),

IV
The nonrelativistic limit

The weak-field limit (WFL) of BIMOND, to be discussed in more detail in Sec.
IV.1
, concerns systems where the metrics depart only a little from a common Minkowski metric, in which limit, the departure from Minkowski is treated to lowest order. In the important special case of the nonrelativistic (NR) limit, the gravitational field is also static, or time independent; to wit, the field varies on timescales much longer than the light crossing time over the system under study. This means, in particular, that the sources of gravity in the system are slowly moving – static in the limit. Their energy-momentum tensor has only a time-time component equal to the mass density, and the metric is taken to be time independent.

This situation applies to the dynamics in galactic systems, including the description of gravitational lensing by galactic systems.

Following Ref.
milgrom09b
, write for the weak-field limit

g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\phi\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},~~~~\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\hat{\phi}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}.

(32)

Defining
\phi\equiv(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}-g_{\scriptscriptstyle 00})/2
and
\hat{\phi}\equiv(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}-\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle 00})/2
, we have
h_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}=\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}=0
.
Also, denote
e_{\scriptscriptstyle i}\equiv h_{\scriptscriptstyle 0i}=\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0i}
(Roman letters are used for space indices).

We denote the differences and sums of the potentials

\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=\phi-\hat{\phi},~~~h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}~~~\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=\phi+\hat{\phi},~~~h^{\scriptscriptstyle+}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}.

(33)

We wish to solve the field equations to first order in the potentials

\phi,~\hat{\phi},~h_{\scriptscriptstyle ij},~\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij},~e_{\scriptscriptstyle i},~\hat{e}_{\scriptscriptstyle i}
(I call them
\psi
when I refer to them collectively).

The dominant, lowest-order terms in the field equations are of the order
\partial^{2}\psi
. A close inspection shows that the
\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
terms can be neglected in our approximation. They are either of order
(\partial\psi)^{2}
, which are of order
\psi\ll 1
relative to the dominant term, or they are of order
a_{0}^{2}
, which contribute a density of the order of the dark-energy density [by Eq. (
1
)], which we neglect in the context of local systems.

As to the
\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
terms, we can replace in them the metrics themselves – but not their derivatives – by
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
. We also replace the covariant derivatives by derivatives, because the correction to this is also of order
(\partial\psi)^{2}
.
This leaves us with

-\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\approx\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\approx\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\equiv[\sum_{m}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}}{\partial\mathcal{Z}_{m}}S^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}(m)-\frac{1}{2}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda(\mu}\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu)}+\frac{1}{2}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}(\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma\alpha}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\alpha}-\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}C^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta})]_{\scriptscriptstyle,\gamma},

(34)

where in
\mathcal{Z}_{m}
and
S^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}(m)
all contractions and raising and lowering indices are done with
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
.

It is useful to consider the sum and the difference of the field equations for the two sectors.
In the WFL we then have

G_{\mu\nu}+\hat{G}_{\mu\nu}=-8\pi G(T^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}),

(35)

and

G_{\mu\nu}-\hat{G}_{\mu\nu}+2\bar{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=-8\pi G(T^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\hat{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}),

(36)

The WFL, Einstein tensors are linear in
\partial\psi
; so the sum equation of the WFL of BIMOND is equivalent to the WFL of GR for the potentials
\psi^{\scriptscriptstyle+}\equiv\psi+\hat{\psi}
. This sum sector also enjoys the full gauge freedom; thus its solutions are those of GR.

As to the difference sector,
G_{\mu\nu}-\hat{G}_{\mu\nu}
is linear in
\partial^{2}\psi^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
(where
\psi^{\scriptscriptstyle-}=\psi-\hat{\psi}
); specifically, to lowest order

\delta G_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}-\hat{G}_{\mu\nu}\approx\delta R_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}\delta R_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\beta}=[C_{\scriptscriptstyle(\mu}\delta^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu)}-C^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}(C^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}-\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma})]_{\scriptscriptstyle,\gamma}.

(37)

(
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
are linear in
\partial\psi^{\scriptscriptstyle-}
.)

Substituting this and expression (
34
) in Eq. (
36
) gives the difference equation

[\sum_{m}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}}{\partial\mathcal{Z}_{m}}S^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}(m)]_{\scriptscriptstyle,\gamma}=-4\pi G(T^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\hat{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}).

(38)

All the above is valid in the general WFL.

Specialize now to the NR limit, where all the potentials are time independent, and the NR matter energy-momentum tensors are
T^{\scriptscriptstyle M}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\rho\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu 0}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu 0}
, and similarly for the hatted one.

For the difference field equation, we have to the desired order,

(39)

\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle 0}=-\vec{\nabla}\cdot{\bf e},~~~~\bar{C}^{\scriptscriptstyle i}=h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,j}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i},~~~~C_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}=0,~~~~C_{\scriptscriptstyle i}=\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}

(40)

(

h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
).
All the quadratic scalars, with all possible contractions, reduce in the NR limit to linear combinations of the following five basic NR scalars:

\bar{S}_{1}=-2(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i}(2h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ki,j}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i})-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\times{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{2},

(41)

\bar{S}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}),

(42)

\bar{S}_{3}=(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle il,l}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})-(\nabla\cdot{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{2},

(43)

\bar{S}_{4}=4(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}-2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i},

(44)

\bar{S}_{5}=10(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i}(3h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle kj,i}-2h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ki,j})+2\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}-\frac{3}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i})-2e^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle i,j}e^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle j,i}.

(45)

To get a theory with “correct” lensing,
\phi
must be all that remains in the NR limit, the NR solutions of the difference field equations (
38
) must thus have
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=0
, resulting in
h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=0
.
I now proceed to determine the subclass of BIMOND theories for which this is the case.

The NR field equations can be derived directly from the NR Lagrangian

\bar{L}=-\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int d^{\scriptscriptstyle 3}x[(\vec{\nabla}\phi)^{2}+(\vec{\nabla}\hat{\phi})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+a_{0}^{2}\mathcal{M}(\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{2},...)-2(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}-\phi\rho-\hat{\phi}\hat{\rho}],

(46)

where the variables
\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
are linear combinations of the five NR basic scalars in Eqs. (
41
)-(
45
) (in units of
a_{0}^{2}
).

This Lagrangian is obtained from the relativistic one by adhering to the NR approximation. For example, we take the volume element in the interaction term (not in the Einstein terms) to be
1
because the
\psi
corrections to it would act like a density of order
a_{0}^{2}\sim\rho_{\scriptscriptstyle\Lambda}
.

The schematic form of all the variable
\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{i}
is

\bar{S}_{m}=a_{\scriptscriptstyle m}(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+2b_{\scriptscriptstyle m}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}(\partial h^{\scriptscriptstyle*})_{\scriptscriptstyle i}+c_{\scriptscriptstyle m}(\partial h^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{2}+d_{\scriptscriptstyle m}(\partial e^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{2},

(47)

with
a_{\scriptscriptstyle m},~b_{\scriptscriptstyle m},~c_{\scriptscriptstyle m},~d_{\scriptscriptstyle m}
numerical coefficients.

The
\mu\nu
component of the general field equations is gotten by variation of the action over
g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
. In the NR case, the
ij
components are thus gotten by varying over
h_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
and
\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
, and the
0i
components by variation over
e_{\scriptscriptstyle i}
and
\hat{e}_{\scriptscriptstyle i}
. However,
\phi
and
\hat{\phi}
appear in all the diagonal elements as
\phi\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
; so, varying over
\phi
and
\hat{\phi}
gives the sum of the
\mu\mu
components of the field equations (not the trace).

Starting with
{\bf e}
, we see from Eq. (
47
) that, since
{\bf e}
does not couple to the other degrees of freedom in any of the NR scalars, the three
0i
components of the difference equation are of the form

[\partial(\sum_{m}d_{\scriptscriptstyle m}\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{M}}}{\partial\bar{S}_{m}}\partial e^{\scriptscriptstyle*})]_{\scriptscriptstyle i}=0,

(48)

with
\bar{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{S}_{m})
the reduction of the interaction function to the NR case and is a function of the NR scalar variables
\bar{S}_{m}
.
(There are three indices implicit in Eq. (
48
), two from the derivatives and one from
e^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
; two are contracted and one,
i
, remains free.) Thus, with zero boundary conditions at infinity, this admits the solution
{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=0
.

This is a general result that applies to any choice of scalars constructed from any power of
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
. To see why, note in Eq. (
39
) that
{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
appears only in
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
with one time component, and that the other nonvanishing components have an even number of time components. Since we are contracting with
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
,
\partial{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
cannot appear in just one
C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
factor, lest this scalar involves an odd number of time indices, which cannot be contracted with
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
. But then, the
0i
equations will always be at least linear in
\partial{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
, and
{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=0
obtains. This result is related to the fact that in the NR limit the sources are taken as static, so the matter energy-momentum tensor is invariant to time reversal, under which
{\bf e}^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
changes sign.

Variation over
\phi,~\hat{\phi}
gives a difference equation of the schematic form

\partial_{i}\{\sum_{m}\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{M}}}{\partial\bar{S}_{m}}[a_{\scriptscriptstyle m}\partial_{i}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}+b_{\scriptscriptstyle m}(\partial h^{\scriptscriptstyle*})_{\scriptscriptstyle i}]\}\propto\rho-\hat{\rho},

(49)

with
(\partial h^{\scriptscriptstyle*})_{\scriptscriptstyle i}
standing for terms with derivatives of
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
with a free index
i
(such as
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ji,j}
or
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}
).
Varying over
h_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
and
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
give the
ij
components of the difference equation in the schematic form

\{\partial[\sum_{m}\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal{M}}}{\partial\bar{S}_{m}}(b_{\scriptscriptstyle m}\partial\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}+c_{\scriptscriptstyle m}\partial h^{\scriptscriptstyle*})]\}_{ij}=0,

(50)

where the subscript
ij
can come from derivatives of
\phi
or
h
, or from subscripts of
h
.

Equation (
50
) tells us that the obstacle to a good NR theory are the terms with
\partial\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
, which come from scalars where
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
and
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}
couple: If these terms do not vanish identically, then
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}=0
is not a solution, because it would imply
\partial\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=0
.

A good NR limit is thus gotten if (and only if) the NR interaction Lagrangian can be written as a function of scalars that do not contain such mixed terms.
Equation (
50
) is then satisfied for
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}=0
, when we impose this as a boundary condition at infinity, for which all
\bar{S}_{m}
in Eq. (
49
) becomes
\propto(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
, and it becomes a MOND equation sourced by
\rho-\hat{\rho}

(see details below).

There are two mixed terms that can (and do) appear in the NR scalars (
41
)-(
45
):
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}
and
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}
. Of the five independent NR scalars we can thus form three independent ones that do not have mixed terms.
The most general such combinations are of the form

S_{s,q,p}=sS_{1}-qS_{2}+(q-s)(S_{4}-S_{5})+pS_{3},

(51)

with
s
,
q
, and
p
numbers. The NR limit of such a scalar is

\bar{S}_{s,q,p}=(4s-6q)(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+(\frac{s}{2}-\frac{3q}{4})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}+\frac{q}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,j}-(p+\frac{q}{2})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}+(\frac{p}{4}+\frac{q}{2}-\frac{s}{4})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}+ph^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,j}.

(52)

The single scalar variable used in previous work is gotten with
s=q=1
,
p=0
.

It is convenient to also work with the combinations

u\equiv 4s-6q,~~~~~v\equiv p+\frac{q}{2},

(53)

because they appear often, and in terms of which

\bar{S}_{q,u,v}=u(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}+\frac{u}{8}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}+\frac{q}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,j}-vh^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}+\frac{1}{4}(v-\frac{u}{4})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}+(v-\frac{q}{2})h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ik,k}h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij,j}.

(54)

We can write

S_{q,u,v}=qS_{q}+uS_{u}+vS_{v};~~~~~S_{q}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(3S_{1}-2S_{2}-S_{3}-S_{4}+S_{5}),~~~S_{u}\equiv\frac{1}{4}(S_{1}-S_{4}+S_{5}),~~~S_{v}\equiv S_{3}.

(55)

For scalars with
u=0
,
(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
disappears altogether in the NR limit. So to get a meaningful theory we need that at least one of the scalars has
u\not=0
(and that cancellation does not occur otherwise).

Alternatively to starting from the NR Lagrangian, we can calculate
S^{\scriptscriptstyle\gamma}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and substitute in the difference field equation (
38
). The only relevant components are
S^{\scriptscriptstyle i}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, because of the time independence. One finds that for the
S_{q,u,v}
scalars

S^{\scriptscriptstyle i}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0j}
are linear only in
\partial e
,
S^{\scriptscriptstyle i}_{\scriptscriptstyle jk}
only in
\partial h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}
, causing the
0i
and
jk
equations to yield
e^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle i}=h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle ij}=0
, as desired, while
S^{\scriptscriptstyle i}_{\scriptscriptstyle 00}\propto\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle,i}
.

For solutions of the NR field equations (i.e., those with
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=0
) all the “good” scalars reduce to a single scalar

\bar{S}_{s,q,p}=u(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}.

(56)

I shall restrict myself, hereafter, to the subclass of BIMOND theories where the interaction Lagrangian is a function of only scalars of this type.

To summarize, if the interaction is a function of scalars of the
S_{q,u,v}
type, the two metrics are of the single-potential type

g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\phi\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},~~~~~\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-2\hat{\phi}\delta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},

(57)

with the two potentials determined by the equations

2\Delta\phi+\vec{\nabla}\cdot[\mu(|\vec{\nabla}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}|/a_{0})\vec{\nabla}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}]-\Delta\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=8\pi G\rho,

(58)

2\Delta\hat{\phi}-\vec{\nabla}\cdot[\mu(|\vec{\nabla}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}|/a_{0})\vec{\nabla}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}]+\Delta\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}=8\pi G\hat{\rho},

(59)

\mu=\sum_{m}u_{\scriptscriptstyle m}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}}{\partial\mathcal{Z}_{m}},

(60)

with the variables in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{Z}_{m})
given by
\mathcal{Z}_{m}=S_{m}/a_{0}^{2}
(
S_{m}=S_{q_{\scriptscriptstyle m},u_{\scriptscriptstyle m},v_{\scriptscriptstyle m}}
), reducing here to
\mathcal{Z}_{m}=\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{m}=u_{\scriptscriptstyle m}(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}/a_{0}^{2}
(
u_{\scriptscriptstyle m}=4s_{\scriptscriptstyle m}-6q_{\scriptscriptstyle m}
).

Since all variables become equal up to a numerical factor, we can write
\mathcal{M}(\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{1},\bar{\mathcal{Z}}_{2},...)=\bar{\mathcal{M}}[(\nabla\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*})^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}/a_{0}^{2}]
, a function of a single variable. Then

\mu(x)=\frac{d\bar{\mathcal{M}}(z)}{dz}|_{\scriptscriptstyle z=x^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}}.

(61)

Or, if we express the Lagrangian density as a function of
S_{q},~S_{u},~S_{v}
, then

\mu(x)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}}{\partial\mathcal{Z}_{u}}|_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle u}=x^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},~\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle q}=\mathcal{Z}_{\scriptscriptstyle v}=0}.

(62)

Taking the difference of the two field equations yields

\vec{\nabla}\cdot[\mu(|\vec{\nabla}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}|/a_{0})\vec{\nabla}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle*}]=4\pi G(\rho-\hat{\rho}),

(63)

which epitomizes the MOND phenomenology, with
\mu(x)
playing the role of the MOND interpolating function.

The sum of the equations gives

\Delta\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle+}=4\pi G(\rho+\hat{\rho}),

(64)

where
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle+}\equiv\phi+\hat{\phi}
; so
\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle+}
equals the Newtonian potential sourced by
\rho+\hat{\rho}
.

In the Newtonian limit,
\mu(x\rightarrow\infty)\rightarrow 1
, the two sectors decouple, with each potential satisfying its own Poisson equation.

An important lesson from these results is that studying NR systems informs us in a rather limited way on the dependence of the interaction on the different scalars. We can only learn about the behavior of
\mathcal{M}
on a limited subspace of the variable space through
\bar{\mathcal{M}}(z)
.
For example, if
\mathcal{M}
depends on scalars with
u=0
, we can gain no information on this dependence from the NR behavior.

IV.1
The general weak-field case

For the single-variable case, the WFL of BIMOND, and gravitational waves, were discussed in some detail in Ref.
milgrom14b
. Much of the discussion there carries
mutatis mutandis
to the several-variable case. Here I just briefly describe the form of good scalars in the WFL.

Write in the general WFL

g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},~~~~\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},

(65)

and treat the theory to lowest order in
h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
.
We saw that the theory decouples for the perturbations
h^{\scriptscriptstyle\pm}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\pm\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, where the
+
sector is equivalent to GR with all the gauge freedom.
9
9

9

I call the difference here
h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
to distinguish it from
h^{\scriptscriptstyle*}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
used in the NR limit.

For the difference sector, the five basic scalars with all possible contractions, reduce in the WFL to

S_{1}=-\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\rho,\gamma}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\rho,\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\rho,\gamma}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\gamma,\rho},~~~~~~S_{2}=\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\mu,\mu}-\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha},~~~~~~S_{3}=h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\mu,\mu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu,\nu}+\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu,\nu}

S_{4}=\frac{1}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\alpha},~~~~~~S_{5}=\frac{3}{4}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\rho,\gamma}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\rho,\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\rho,\gamma}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\gamma,\rho},

(66)

where for each pair of repeated indices one is understood to be raised with
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and the pair summed over.
So, the WFL of the good scalars
S_{s,q,p}
is

S_{s,q,p}=(\frac{s}{2}-\frac{3q}{4})h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}+\frac{q}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda,\nu}-(p+\frac{q}{2})h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}+(\frac{p}{4}+\frac{q}{2}-\frac{s}{4})h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}+ph^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda,\lambda}

(67)

or

S_{q,u,v}=\frac{u}{8}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu})+\frac{q}{2}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda,\nu}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda,\lambda})+v(h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu})^{2}.

(68)

It would be interesting to investigate the properties of gravitational waves in the theories that employ these scalars.
For example, there might be some particularly happy choices of
s,~q,~p
in the context of gravitational waves.
Such studies are beyond the scope of this paper.

One interesting result is that scalars that disappear from the NR theory – and so the dependence on which cannot be deduced from observations of galactic systems – are still important in the context of gravitational waves. These are the scalars of the type
q=2s/3
(
u=0
), whose WFL is

S_{q,0,v}=v(h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu})^{2}+\frac{q}{2}(h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\lambda}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda,\nu}-h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\lambda,\lambda})

(69)

If, in addition,
q=0
, we have

S\propto(h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\nu}-\frac{1}{2}h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\mu})^{2},

(70)

which vanishes if
h^{\scriptscriptstyle-}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
satisfies the harmonic gauge. Reference
milgrom14b
discussed the relevance of the harmonic gauge, even here, where we do not have gauge freedom in the difference sector.

V
Some thoughts on BIMOND cosmology

Given the underlying relativistic theory, such as some version of BIMOND, a cosmology does not follow from first principles. As in GR, construction of a cosmology requires much observational input from our real Universe, concerning, e.g., the material content and its properties, and the initial conditions. In GR cosmology, when observations could not be accommodated unless one invokes dominant contribution from dark matter, it was invoked even if its existence is not suggested by any compelling theory. The introduction of a small cosmological constant (or “dark energy”) contribution has also been forced by observations, despite its constituting a great headache for theory. As regards initial conditions, it was only observations (e.g. of the thermal CMB) that pointed to a beginning in a singularity (as opposed, e.g., to a steady-state Universe), and we are still groping for a concrete initial scenario (such as various models of inflation) to account for various observations, such as the initial injection of (quantum) fluctuations that have led to the observed structure, and the CMB anisotropies.

In the case of BIMOND, the preceding discussion shows that we still have much freedom in choosing the specific version of the theory. This choice concerns whether the theory is symmetric in the two metrics, to the choice of scalar arguments, and to the exact form of the interaction Lagrangian function. And, the need for observational input in constructing a cosmological model is even more acute than in GR, mainly because we have to deal with two sectors, one of which is not even observable at present: Is the material content of the twin sector similar to that in ours? Is it distributed in the same way? Such questions arise naturally
10
10

10

It was shown in Ref.
milgrom10b
, that in the MOND regime, matter and twin matter repel each other. So structures of the two are expected to be separated. Twin matter also repels (matter) light in the MOND regime; so twin-matter bodies constitute diverging gravitational lenses. We may be able to exploit this to learn about the distribution of twin matter.

Exploring cosmology within the extended BIMOND framework is far beyond the scope of this paper. This class of theories must admit a large number of cosmological solutions that might be of observational interest.

To get a glimpse of how, for example, the different scalars might enter cosmology in different ways (in the NR limit they all reduce to the same scalar), I consider briefly a class of model solutions. In these, the two metrics are very small departures from some FLRW geometry.

V.1
Small departures from FLRW

We seek solutions of extended BIMOND for which there exists a frame in which

g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},~~~~\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},

(71)

where
\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
is of the FLRW form. To simplify the presentation, I specialize to the case where
\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
has zero spatial curvature:

\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(-1,a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}),

(72)

with
a(t)
the cosmological scale factor.

We will consider
h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
– which are injected as initial conditions of the Hubble-Lemaitre expansion phase from an earlier phase, such as an inflation phase – to be very small,
h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu},~\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\lll 1
, uncorrelated, random fluctuations whose time and space averages vanish.
11
11

11

If indeed our cosmology is of this sort, it would justify our taking the background for the weak-field limit to be, locally, a double Minkowski spacetime.

We also assume that these potentials vary rapidly and have short-wavelengths, namely, that all during the Hubble-Lemaitre expansion phase, the frequencies are much smaller than the expansion rate, and the wavelengths are much shorter than the horizon size:
(\dot{a}/a)|h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}|\lll|h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,0}|,~a^{\scriptscriptstyle-1}|h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,x}|
(
x
are the comoving space coordinates). We will thus neglect everywhere terms of order
h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
themselves, or higher, but not in their derivatives, which are not assumed small.
12
12

12

If we measure
|\partial h|=\upsilon a_{0}
in units of
a_{0}
, then, the wavelength
\lambda\sim|h|/|\partial h|\sim(|h|/\upsilon)\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle H}
, where
\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle H}
is today’s Hubble distance, and where I used the coincidence (
1
). So
\lambda\lll\ell_{\scriptscriptstyle H}
is equivalent to
|h|\lll\upsilon
.
These potentials constitute then a background of stochastic gravitational waves, whose propagation properties need to be deduced separately from the cosmological considerations, by studying the WFL on a double Minkowski background (because the wavelength and periods are very small compared with cosmological scales). Because the derivatives of these fluctuations, which enter the interaction Lagrangian, are not small, they can contribute appreciably to the interaction EMT,
\mathcal{T}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
, and potentially introduce an extra matterlike contribution, which in turn, affects the evolution of
a(t)
.

Even within this limited class of cosmologies there is a wide scope of possibilities.
I thus further restrict myself to a simple ansatz, where the trial stochastic potentials are of the form

h_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=-2\theta~{\rm diag}(1,a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}),~~~~\hat{h}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=-2\hat{\theta}~{\rm diag}(1,a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}),

(73)

similar to the solutions of the NR limit (but the potentials here are time dependent).
For the “relative-acceleration tensor” we then have

C^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\lambda\sigma}(\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\sigma,\nu}+\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\nu\sigma,\mu}-\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu,\sigma}),

(74)

where

\delta g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=g_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\hat{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=-2\varphi~{\rm diag}(1,a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle 2}),~~~~~\delta g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=g^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}-\hat{g}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}=2\varphi~{\rm diag}(1,a^{\scriptscriptstyle-2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle-2},a^{\scriptscriptstyle-2}),

(75)

and
\varphi=\theta-\hat{\theta}
. In expression (
74
), terms of order
\varphi\partial\tilde{g}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
and
\varphi\partial\varphi
were neglected.

In making this ansatz, I am inspired by the success of Skordis and Zlosnik
Skordis and Zlosnik 2021
in having propounded, recently, a relativistic version of MOND that achieves several important desiderata. In particular, this theory is said to reproduce the effects of the putative dark matter in cosmology.
In essence, Skordis and Zlosnik
Skordis and Zlosnik 2021
developed a version of TeVeS using an idea discussed, e.g., in Ref.
Scherrer 2004
, to the effect that a scalar field subject to a certain potential that depends on its gradient can act as cosmological dark matter,” under certain conditions. Reference
Skordis and Zlosnik 2021
did not add a new degree of freedom to TeVeS, but rather employed the scalar field that is anyhow needed in TeVeS.

This success brings to mind the possibility that the same idea can be grafted on BIMOND, to reproduce cosmologically viable solutions without new contributions to the matter actions (and energy-momentum tensor), which would count as dark matter. We would want the dark-matter-like contribution to come from the modified gravitational action, namely, to be encapsulated in
\mathcal{T}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
.

In BIMOND, we do not have a scalar
per se
as a gravitational degree of freedom. But the hope is that there might be cosmologically relevant solutions of BIMOND involving a function that plays the role of the scalar.
13
13

13

Note that our
\theta
,
\hat{\theta}
, and
\varphi
are not scalars, as the metrics are supposed to have the above form in some specific frame.
So here I am only trying one possible ansatz involving a geometry characterized by a small departure from FLRW, encapsuled in a single function. There may be other such ansatzes.

For the above ansatz, I derive here the field equations for the general choice of scalars, laying the ground for a future more detailed study of cosmological solutions of this type.

For the metrics of the form (
73
) we have to lowest order in

V.1.2
The average Bianchi identity

Starting from the general Bianchi identity (
18
), we can replace the metric determinants by that of the reference metric in our approximation, yielding
{\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}}_{\scriptscriptstyle;\nu}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}}_{\scriptscriptstyle~~~:\nu}=0
.
Write this as

(\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu})_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}+(\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha})\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}+(\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu})\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}+\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}+\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}\delta\hat{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}\delta\hat{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}=0,

(97)

where
\delta\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}=\Gamma^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}
,
\delta\hat{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}=\hat{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}

(
\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha}_{\scriptscriptstyle\beta\gamma}
is the connection of the reference FLRW metric).

Averaging this relation as before over scales much smaller than cosmologically relevant, but much larger than the wavelengths and periods, the terms with
\delta\Gamma
average to zero. The terms of the form
(\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha})\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}
average to

\langle\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}+{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}\rangle\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\alpha}\tilde{\Gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle\alpha\nu}
, and similarly for the third term.
The average of the first term can be replaced by
\langle\mathcal{T}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}+\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}
, where the derivative here applies to the secular, cosmological changes of the average EMTs.
16
16

16

For a quantity
A
such as ours, write
\langle A_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}\rangle=\langle A\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}+\langle(A-\langle A\rangle)_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}\rangle=\langle A\rangle_{\scriptscriptstyle,\nu}
, since
A-\langle A\rangle
is fast oscillating, and so are its derivatives.

The spatial derivatives vanish, because, as usual, we assume spatial homogeneity of the averages.
So, in all, we have for the averaged Bianchi identity

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}_{\scriptscriptstyle~~~|\nu}=0,

(98)

where “
|
” is the covariant derivative with respect to the reference, FLRW metric. The Bianchi identity thus constitutes the standard cosmological conservation of the phantom-matter component represented by
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mu\nu}
.

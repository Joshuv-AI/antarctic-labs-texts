Magnetic corrections to the fermionic Casimir effect in Horava-Lifshitz theories
Andrea Erdas

Abstract
In this paper I investigate the effect of a magnetic field on the Casimir effect due to a massless and charged fermion field that violates Lorentz invariance according to the Horava-Lifshitz theory. I focus on the case of a fermion field that obeys MIT bag boundary conditions on a pair of parallel plates. I carry out this investigation using the ζ \zeta -function technique that allows me to obtain Casimir energy and pressure in the presence of a uniform magnetic field orthogonal to the plates. I investigate the cases of the parameter associated with the violation of Lorentz invariance being even or odd and the cases of weak and strong magnetic field, examining all possible combinations of the above quantities. In all cases I obtain simple and very accurate analytic expressions of the magnetic field dependent Casimir energy and pressure.

I
Introduction

The Casimir effect was theoretically predicted 75 years ago
Casimir:1948dh
. In its simplest form, quantum field theory effects produce an attraction between two uncharged conducting plates facing each other in vacuum. The first experimental tests of it
Sparnaay:1958wg
were done ten years after Casimir’s theoretical work and were relatively consistent with the theoretical prediction. The effect has been successfully confirmed by many increasingly accurate experimental test
Bordag:2001qi
;
Bordag:2009zz
in the decades that followed Casimir’s paper, where he studied quantum fluctuations of the electromagnetic field in vacuum and discovered they produce an attraction of the plates.
Quantum fluctuations of other fields have also been found to produce Casimir forces, which are strongly dependent on the boundary conditions at the plates and on the geometry of the plates
Boyer:1974
;
Boyer:1968uf
.
Dirichlet, Neumann or mixed boundary conditions are suitable for the scalar and electromagnetic fields and have been used extensively when investigating the Casimir effect due to fields of that kind, but cannot be used for fermionic fields
Ambjorn:1981xv
. Bag boundary conditions, initially introduced to find a solution to confinement, are used for fermionic fields
Chodos:1974je
;
Johnson:1975zp
within the context of the Casimir effect.

The violation of Lorentz symmetry has become the subject of theoretical investigations in the last two decades, when several models that cause a space-time anisotropy have been proposed
Horava:2009uw
;
Ferrari:2010dj
;
Ulion:2015kjx
, within the broader context of searching for a theory of quantum gravity, or string theory. While these models break Lorentz invariance at the Planck scale, repercussions of the space-time anisotropy can be observed at much lower energy through the Casimir effect, in the case of Lorentz symmetry breaking scalar fields
Cruz:2017kfo
;
Cruz:2018bqt
and fermionic fields
daSilva:2019iwn
, where the authors analyze the Casimir effect caused by a massless fermion field that violates Lorentz symmetry according to the Horava-Lifshitz model.

In recent years, authors have examined magnetic correction to the Casimir effect caused by charged scalar fields
Cougo-Pinto:1998jun
;
Cougo-Pinto:1998fpo
;
Erdas:2013jga
;
Erdas:2013dha
and by Majorana fermion fields
Erdas:2010mz
in Lorentz-symmetric space-time, and to the Casimir effect caused by a charged scalar field that breaks Lorentz invariance
Erdas:2020ilo
;
Erdas:2021xvv
. However, a study of the magnetic effects on the Casimir effect caused by a charged fermion field that breaks Lorentz invariance has not been done. In this work I will investigate how a uniform magnetic field affects the Casimir effect due to a massless and charged fermion field
that violates Lorentz invariance according to the Horava-Lifshitz model. This fermion field will satisfy MIT bag boundary conditions on two identical parallel plates facing each other and the magnetic field will be uniform and perpendicular to the plates.

In Sec.
II
of this paper I introduce the model of a fermion field that breaks Lorentz symmetry according to the theory of Horava-Lifshitz, discuss how to make the field satisfy bag boundary conditions on the plates and then obtain the field vacuum energy without a magnetic field and in the presence of the magnetic field. In Sec.
III
, I use the zeta function regularization to calculate the Casimir energy of this system and the Casimir pressure it causes on the plates, in the presence of the external magnetic field. The Horava-Lifshitz theory associates a parameter with the Lorentz symmetry breaking mechanism, and this parameter is a positive integer called critical exponent. In this section two separate calculations are needed for even and odd values of the critical exponent,
\xi
, and they are presented in two subsections. I present a summary and discussion of my results in Sec.
IV
. In the Appendix I use the generalized zeta function technique to show how to calculate the Casimir energy for the special case where the critical exponent
\xi=1
.

II
The model

In this work I use
\hbar=c=1
and study the Casimir effect due to a massless fermion field
\psi
of charge
e
that violates Lorentz symmetry according to the Horava-Lifshitz model, proposed by Horava in Ref.
Horava:2009uw
. This theoretical model has a Lagrangian for the field
\psi
that violates the Lorentz symmetry
daSilva:2019iwn
;
Farias:2011aa

{\cal L}={\bar{\psi}}\left[i\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}+i^{\xi}l^{\xi-1}(\gamma^{i}\partial_{i})^{\xi}\right]\psi,

(II.1)

where
\xi
, the critical exponent, is a positive integer and describes the violation of Lorentz symmetry, while the length parameter
l
keeps
\cal L
with the correct dimensionality. It is evident that, when
\xi=1
, the Lagrangian shown above is the standard Dirac Lagrangian for massless fermions and does not violate Lorentz symmetry. In Ref.
daSilva:2019iwn
the authors obtain, from
\cal L
, the equation of motion of the fermion field
\psi

\left[i\gamma^{0}\partial_{t}+i^{\xi}l^{\xi-1}(\gamma^{i}\partial_{i})^{\xi}\right]\psi=0,

(II.2)

which can be viewed as a ”Lorentz violating” Dirac equation when
\xi\neq 1
, and obtain all the solutions that obey MIT bag boundary conditions on two identical large parallel plates of area
L^{2}
, one located at
z=0
and the other at

z=a
, with
L\gg a
. They find that any real value of the momentum components
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
is allowed, while only discrete values of
k_{z}
are allowed. If the critical exponent
\xi
is an odd number, then
k_{z}
can only take the following discrete values

k_{z}=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\pi}{a},

(II.3)

where
n=0,1,2,\cdots
. If
\xi
is even then
k_{z}
can only take the following values

k_{z}={n\pi\over a},

(II.4)

where
n=1,2,3,\cdots
.

The vacuum energy
E_{0}=\langle 0|H|0\rangle
of the quantum field
\psi
is obtained using the following Hamiltonian operator

H=i\int_{V}d^{3}x\,\psi^{\dagger}\dot{\psi},

(II.5)

where
V=L^{2}a
is the volume contained between the two plates. It is straightforward to find

E_{0}=-C\left(L\over 2\pi\right)^{2}l^{\xi-1}\sum_{n}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dk_{x}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dk_{y}\left(k^{2}_{x}+k^{2}_{y}+k^{2}_{z}\right)^{\xi/2},

(II.6)

where
C=2
is the spin degeneracy factor and, when expressing
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
in polar coordinates, one immediately obtains

E_{0}=-{L^{2}\over\pi}l^{\xi-1}\sum_{n}\int^{\infty}_{0}dk\,k\left(k^{2}+k^{2}_{z}\right)^{\xi/2},

(II.7)

where
k_{z}
takes the form shown in Eq. (
II.3
) if
\xi
is odd, or takes the form shown in Eq. (
II.4
) if
\xi
is even. The vacuum energy
E_{0}
as shown in Eq. (
II.7
) is divergent and, once it is renormalized
through a regularization procedure, it is rendered finite and yields the Casimir energy
E_{C}
of the fermionic quantum field confined between the two plates.

The novelty of this work is the presence of a uniform magnetic field
\vec{B}
pointing in the
z
-direction, i.e. perpendicular to the plates. In the presence of a magnetic field the spin degeneracy is removed and, instead of the real-valued
k_{x}
and
k_{y}
, discrete Landau levels appear. The vacuum energy is therefore

E_{0}=E_{+}+E_{-},

(II.8)

where
E_{+}
is the contribution of the spin up component of
\psi
to the vacuum energy, and
E_{-}
the contribution of the spin down component. I find

E_{\pm}=-{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}l^{\xi-1}\sum_{n,\ell}\left[(2\ell+1)eB\pm eB+k^{2}_{z}\right]^{\xi/2},

(II.9)

where
(2\ell+1)eB
are the Landau energy levels with
\ell=0,1,2,\cdots
, and
\pm eB
is the spin contribution.

III
Casimir energy and pressure

The Casimir energy
E_{C}
is obtained by renormalizing the vacuum energy
E_{0}
. This renormalization process produces a finite Casimir energy and can be accomplished
using one of several regularization techniques. Ref.
daSilva:2019iwn
used the Abel-Plana summation method, in this paper I use the zeta function technique.
Starting from Eqs. (
II.8
) and (
II.9
), I define

E_{C}=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}l^{-(2s+1)}\sum_{n,\ell}\left\{\left[(2\ell+1)eB+eB+k^{2}_{z}\right]^{-s}+\left[(2\ell+1)eB-eB+k^{2}_{z}\right]^{-s}\right\},

(III.1)

where

s=-{\xi+\epsilon\over 2}.

(III.2)

Using the following identity

z^{-s}={1\over\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{s-1}e^{-zt},

(III.3)

I rewrite Eq. (
III.1
) as

E_{C}=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}{l^{-(2s+1)}\over\Gamma(s)}\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{s-1}e^{-k^{2}_{z}t}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left[e^{-2(\ell+1)eBt}+e^{-2\ell eBt}\right],

(III.4)

and, using this other identity

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left[e^{-2(\ell+1)z}+e^{-2\ell z}\right]=\coth(z),

(III.5)

I find

E_{C}=-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}{l^{-(2s+1)}\over\Gamma(s)}\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{s-1}e^{-k^{2}_{z}t}\coth(eBt).

(III.6)

At this point, I will find analytical forms for the two asymptotic cases of weak magnetic field,
eB\ll a^{-2}
, and strong magnetic field,
eB\gg a^{-2}
.

When the magnetic field is weak, I use

\coth(eBt)\simeq{1\over eBt}+{eBt\over 3}-{(eBt)^{3}\over 45}+{\cal O}(B^{5}),

(III.7)

into (
III.6
) and, after a change of integration variable, obtain

E_{C}\simeq-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}\over 2\pi}{l^{-(2s+1)}\over\Gamma(s)}\left[\Gamma(s-1)\sum_{n}(k^{2}_{z})^{-(s-1)}+{e^{2}B^{2}\over 3}\Gamma(s+1)\sum_{n}(k^{2}_{z})^{-(s+1)}+{\cal O}(B^{4})\right],

(III.8)

where, once the limit is taken, the first term will give the part of
E_{C}
that does not depend on the magnetic field, while the second term, proportional to
B^{2}
, will give the leading magnetic correction to
E_{C}
.

In the case of strong magnetic field I use the following approximation

\coth(eBt)\simeq 1

(III.9)

into (
III.6
) and, once I change the integration variable, I obtain

E_{C}\simeq-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}l^{-(2s+1)}\sum_{n}(k^{2}_{z})^{-s}.

(III.10)

Since the discrete values of
k_{z}
take a different form when the critical exponent is even, Eq. (
II.4
), or odd, Eq. (
II.3
), I will calculate the two asymptotic limits of
E_{C}
separately for each case.

III.1
Casimir energy and pressure for even
\xi

When
k_{z}
takes the form shown in Eq. (
II.4
), I evaluate the following sums in terms of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta_{R}(z)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(k^{2}_{z})^{-(s-1)}=\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s-1)}\zeta_{R}(2s-2),

(III.11)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(k^{2}_{z})^{-s}=\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2s}\zeta_{R}(2s),

(III.12)

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(k^{2}_{z})^{-(s+1)}=\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s+1)}\zeta_{R}(2s+2).

(III.13)

Next, I use the sums evaluated in Eqs. (
III.11
) and (
III.13
), and insert these results into Eq. (
III.8
) to find

E_{C}\simeq-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}\over 2\pi}{l^{-(2s+1)}\over\Gamma(s)}\left[\Gamma(s-1)\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s-1)}\zeta_{R}(2s-2)+{e^{2}B^{2}\over 3}\Gamma(s+1)\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s+1)}\zeta_{R}(2s+2)+{\cal O}(B^{4})\right].

(III.14)

Last, I calculate the weak magnetic field limit of
E_{C}
for
\xi=2m
with
m=1,2,\cdots
by taking the limit for
\epsilon\to 0
to obtain

E_{C}\simeq{L^{2}\over 2\pi}{l^{(2m-1)}}\left[{1\over m+1}\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{2(m+1)}\zeta_{R}(-2m-2)+{e^{2}B^{2}\over 3}m\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{2(m-1)}\zeta_{R}(-2m+2)+{\cal O}(B^{4})\right],

(III.15)

and find that the part of
E_{C}
that does not depend on the magnetic field vanishes for all even values of
\xi
, since
\zeta_{R}(-2n)=0
for
n=1,2,\cdots
, thus confirming the result obtained in Ref.
daSilva:2019iwn
. The magnetic correction, however, does not vanish for
\xi=2
(
m=1
) because
\zeta_{R}(0)=-{1\over 2}
and, in this case, I find

E_{C}=-{L^{2}l\over 12\pi}e^{2}B^{2},

(III.16)

where I use an equality since all higher order magnetic corrections vanish for
\xi=2
. For even values of the critical exponent higher than
\xi=2
the Casimir energy vanishes exactly, including low and high order magnetic corrections.

In the case of strong magnetic field, I insert into Eq. (
III.10
) the value of the sum I obtained in Eq. (
III.12
) and find

E_{C}\simeq-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}l^{-(2s+1)}\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2s}\zeta_{R}(2s).

(III.17)

Once I take the limit
\epsilon\to 0
and substitute
\xi=2m
, I obtain

E_{C}\simeq-{L^{2}eB\over 2\pi}l^{(2m-1)}\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{2m}\zeta_{R}(-2m),

(III.18)

which vanishes for any
m\geq 1
.

The Casimir pressure is defined as

P_{C}=-{1\over L^{2}}{\partial E_{C}\over\partial a},

(III.19)

and therefore, in the weak magnetic field limit, it vanishes for all even values of the critical exponent including
\xi=2
since, in that case, the non-vanishing value of
E_{C}
I obtained is independent of
a
.
P_{C}
also vanishes in the strong magnetic field limit for all even values of
\xi
, since
E_{C}=0
when the critical exponent is even.

III.2
Casimir energy and pressure for odd
\xi

In this case
k_{z}
takes the form shown in Eq. (
II.3
), and I obtain the following results for the sums of Eqs. (
III.8
) and (
III.10
)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k^{2}_{z})^{-(s-1)}=\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s-1)}\left[2^{2(s-1)}-1\right]\zeta_{R}(2s-2),

(III.20)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k^{2}_{z})^{-s}=\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2s}\left(2^{2s}-1\right)\zeta_{R}(2s),

(III.21)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k^{2}_{z})^{-(s+1)}=\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s+1)}\left[2^{2(s+1)}-1\right]\zeta_{R}(2s+2).

(III.22)

I use the values of two of these infinite sums into Eq. (
III.8
) to find, for weak magnetic field, the following

\displaystyle E_{C}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{L^{2}\over 2\pi}{l^{-(2s+1)}\over\Gamma(s)}\left[\Gamma(s-1)\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s-1)}\left[2^{2(s-1)}-1\right]\zeta_{R}(2s-2)\right.

(III.23)

\displaystyle\left.+{e^{2}B^{2}\over 3}\Gamma(s+1)\left({\pi\over a}\right)^{-2(s+1)}\left[2^{2(s+1)}-1\right]\zeta_{R}(2s+2)+{\cal O}(B^{4})\right].

To calculate the weak limit of
E_{C}
for
\xi=2m+1
, with
m=0,1,2,\cdots
, I take the limit for
\epsilon\to 0
and find

E_{C}\simeq-L^{2}\!\left({\pi l\over a}\right)^{2m}\!\!\left\{{\pi^{2}\over(2m+3)a^{3}}\left[1-2^{-(2m+3)}\right]\zeta_{R}(-2m-3)+{2m+1\over 12\pi^{2}}e^{2}B^{2}a\left[1-2^{-(2m-1)}\right]\zeta_{R}(1-2m)+{\cal O}(B^{4})\!\right\}\!.

(III.24)

I now call
E^{0}_{C}
the part of the Casimir energy that does not depend on the magnetic field, and
M_{C}
the magnetic correction.

For
\xi=1
(
m=0
) the Lorentz symmetry is not violated by the fermion field and, since
\zeta_{R}(-3)={1\over 120}
, I find

E_{C}^{0}=-L^{2}{7\over 4}\left({\pi^{2}\over 720a^{3}}\right),

(III.25)

which agrees with Ref.
daSilva:2019iwn
and with the earlier work on the Casimir energy of massless fermions
DePaola:1999im
. The magnetic correction for
\xi=1
appears to be divergent, since it is well known that
\zeta_{R}(1)=\infty
. However, this divergence is an artifact of a logarithmic dependence of
M_{C}
on
a
.
M_{C}
, for
\xi=1
, can be calculated using another method, the generalized zeta function technique discovered by Hawking
Hawking:1976ja
, to find a finite result
Erdas:2010mz
. This calculation is shown in the Appendix, where I find

M_{C}=L^{2}{e^{2}B^{2}a\over 12\pi^{2}}\left[\gamma_{E}+\ln\left({2a\sqrt{eB}\over\pi}\right)\right],

(III.26)

where
\gamma_{E}=0.5772
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
M_{C}
is negative when
a\sqrt{eB}\ll 1
(weak magnetic field).

When
\xi\geq 3
(
m\geq 1
), I find

E_{C}^{0}=-L^{2}{\pi^{(2m+2)}l^{2m}\over(2m+3)a^{(2m+3)}}\left[1-2^{-(2m+3)}\right]\zeta_{R}(-2m-3),

(III.27)

which is negative when
\xi=1,5,9,\cdots
, positive when
\xi=3,7,11,\cdots
, and fully agrees with what the authors of Ref.
daSilva:2019iwn
obtained using a different regularization method.
The magnetic correction is

M_{C}\simeq-L^{2}{(2m+1)\pi^{(2m-2)}l^{2m}\over 12a^{(2m-1)}}e^{2}B^{2}\left[1-2^{-(2m-1)}\right]\zeta_{R}(-2m+1)+{\cal O}(B^{4}),

(III.28)

negative when
\xi=5,9,13\cdots
, positive when
\xi=3,7,11,\cdots
.

I use the definition of Casimir pressure of Eq. (
III.19
) and, in the weak magnetic field limit, I find

P_{C}\simeq-{7\over 4}\left({\pi^{2}\over 240a^{4}}\right)-{e^{2}B^{2}\over 12\pi^{2}}\left[1+\gamma_{E}+\ln\left({2a\sqrt{eB}\over\pi}\right)\right]+{\cal O}(B^{4})

(III.29)

an attractive pressure for
\xi=1
, and

P_{C}\simeq-{\pi^{2}\over a^{4}}\left({\pi l\over a}\right)^{2m}\left[1-2^{-(2m+3)}\right]\zeta_{R}(-2m-3)-{4m^{2}-1\over 12\pi^{2}}\left({\pi l\over a}\right)^{2m}e^{2}B^{2}\left[1-2^{-(2m-1)}\right]\zeta_{R}(-2m+1)+{\cal O}(B^{4}),

(III.30)

for
\xi\geq 3
(
m\geq 1
). Notice that
\zeta_{R}(-2m-3)
and
\zeta_{R}(-2m+1)
are negative when
m
is odd and are positive when

is even, for

,
thus the pressure is repulsive when
\xi=3,7,11,\cdots
, attractive when
\xi=5,9,13,\cdots
. Notice also that the magnetic correction increases the pressure in both cases of attractive and repulsive pressure. Finally, it is important to point out that, for any given value of

, the pressure is stronger for larger values of
\xi
.

In the limit of strong magnetic field I use the value of the sum of Eq. (
III.21
) into Eq. (
III.10
) and, after taking the limit for
\epsilon\to 0
, obtain

E_{C}\simeq{L^{2}eB\over 2}{(\pi l)^{2m}\over a^{(2m+1)}}\left[1-2^{-(2m+1)}\right]\zeta_{R}(-2m-1),

(III.31)

where I used
\xi=2m+1

is negative when
\xi=1,5,9,\cdots
, and it is positive when

. The strong magnetic field Casimir pressure is

and is attractive when

.

IV
Discussion and conclusions

In this paper, I used the zeta function technique to investigate the Casimir effect of a Lorentz violating massless fermion field in the presence of a constant magnetic field perpendicular to the plates under the assumption that the quantum field satisfies bag boundary conditions at the plates. I obtained an integral expression for the Casimir energy (
III.6
) exact to all orders in the magnetic field
B
and used it to find simple analytic expressions of the Casimir energy and pressure in the case of weak magnetic field,
eB\ll a^{-2}
, and strong magnetic field,
eB\gg a^{-2}
, for even values of the critical exponent, in Sec.
III.1
, and for odd values of
\xi
, in Sec.
III.2
.

I find that, when the critical exponent is even, the weak magnetic field correction
M_{C}
to the Casimir energy is zero except for the case of
\xi=2
, where
M_{C}
takes a constant value, independent of the plate distance
a
. My study confirms that, for weak magnetic field and even values of the critical exponent,
E_{C}^{0}
(the piece of the Casimir energy that does not depend on the magnetic field) vanishes as reported in Ref.
daSilva:2019iwn
. I find that the strong magnetic field Casimir energy is zero for all even values of
\xi
. The Casimir pressure is also zero for all even values of
\xi
and for weak and strong magnetic field.

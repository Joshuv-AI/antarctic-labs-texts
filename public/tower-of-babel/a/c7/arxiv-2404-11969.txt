Abstract
. We study the principle φ → ⁡ φ \varphi\to\necessary\varphi , known as Strength or the Completeness Principle , over the constructive version of Löb’s Logic. We consider this principle both for the modal language with the necessity operator \necessary and for the modal language with the Lewis arrow 𝐽 \strictif , where Löb’s Logic is suitably adapted. For example, \necessary is defined as ⊤ 𝐽 ( ⋅ ) \top\strictif(\cdot) . Central insights of provability logic, like the de Jongh-Sambin Theorem and the de Jongh-Sambin-Bernardi Theorem, take a simple form in the presence of Strength. We present these simple versions. We discuss the semantics of two salient systems and prove uniform interpolation for both. In addition, we sketch arithmetical interpretations of our systems. Finally, we describe the various connections of our subject with Computer Science.

1.
Introduction

Strength is strange. What counts as strength in one context may appear as weakness in another.
Classical logic is strong. It can prove more than intuitionistic logic.
However, particularly when the logical vocabulary is extended with new operators, classical logic is not open to a number of possibilities. It walls itself in by excluding too much.
Principles that trivialise over the classical base can axiomatize important logics over the intuitionistic base. The latter is more openminded---a form of strength.

2.7.
Classical Variant of Strong Löb

It is easy to see that the principle
{\sf L}_{\necessary}
for
\bot
is
\neg\neg\,\necessary\bot
, which in the classical setting collapses to
\necessary\bot
. In other words, the resulting logic is that of the
verum
operator: anything prefixed by
\necessary
becomes equivalent to
\top
.

In the opening paragraph of this paper,
we claimed that non-triviality of principles like strong Löb illustrates that there is strength in intuitionistic
openmindedness. Naturally, openmindedness as a form of strength may require certain refinements.
A natural one would be to say that
\Lambda
is stronger than
\Lambda^{\prime}
if there exists a recursive
“translation function”
t
(not necessarily compositional, but satisfying perhaps some additional requirements instead)
which is sound and faithful, i.e.,
\varphi\in\Lambda^{\prime}
iff
t(\varphi)\in\Lambda
. When comparing intuitionistic and classical
modal logics this way, one can often take one of “negative translations”: Glivenko, Kuroda, Kolmogorov or suitable variants of
Gödel-Gentzen (none of them being compositional in the sense introduced above). See Litak et al.
[
LPR17
]
for a
more detailed discussion. Nevertheless, there are examples of propositional systems based on intuitionistic logic whose

3.2.
A Beth Rule over

We interpose a brief subsection on (a strong version of) the Beth rule. This does have
a connection with fixed points since Smoryński used Beth’s Theorem to

[

A logic

\top

\vdash_{\varLambda}(\varphi p\wedge\varphi q)\to(p\leftrightarrow q)

\vdash_{\varLambda}\varphi p\to(p\leftrightarrow\varphi\top)
.
Here
p
is the variable of substitution. We assume that

does not occur in
\varphi
.
(Note that
\varphi p=\varphi
. We exhibit the

We note that the
\top
-Beth Rule is not an admissible rule in the official sense of the word since it involves
explicit mention of variables.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
\varLambda
extends
{\sf HLC}^{\sharp}+{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
.
Then,
\varLambda
is closed under the
\top
-Beth Rule.

Proof.

Suppose
\varLambda
extends
{\sf HLC}^{\sharp}+{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
. Let
p
is the variable of substitution and suppose
q
does not occur in
\varphi
.

Suppose
\vdash_{\varLambda}(\varphi p\wedge\varphi q)\to(p\leftrightarrow q)
.
Taking

\vdash_{\varLambda}(\varphi p\wedge\varphi\top)\to(p\leftrightarrow\top)

and, hence, (a)

\vdash_{\varLambda}\varphi p\to(\varphi\top\to p)

Conversely, by
\mathsf{Su}_{3}
, we may conclude that (b)
\vdash_{\varLambda}\varphi p\to(p\to\varphi\top)

Combining (a) and (b), we find
\vdash_{\varLambda}\varphi p\to(p\leftrightarrow\varphi\top)

3.5.
Primitive Fixed Points

In Subsection
3.4
, we proved a converse, Theorem
3.13

version of the de Jongh-Sambin Theorem, i.e. Theorem
3.12
. Here we used
the specific explicit form of the fixed points. What happens if, instead, the fixed points are primitively added,
so that we cannot make use of information on what the fixed point looks like?
In that case, we also have a converse, but we do need
{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
in order to derive
\mathsf{sL}
.
6
6

6

That
{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
is really needed can be seen from the
fact that (i)
\mathsf{sL}
gives
{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
over
{\sf HLC}^{\sharp}
, and (ii) the possibility to extend finite tree-models of
{\sf HLC}^{\sharp}
with fixed points.

We study the addition of primitive fixed points for modalised formulas to
{\sf HLC}^{\sharp}+{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
. We show that the added fixed points
give us the Strong Löb Principle. The reasoning is a minor adaptation of Löb’s classical proof in
[
Löb55
]
.

We need to define the notion of a fixed point logic for explicit guarded fixed points. There are three (main) options (as far as we can see).
We can take each fixed point just a constant.
If we do that however, we do lose the substitution property, so the resulting system is not really a logic.
Secondly, we can add a fixed point operator that binds variables.
The advantage of the variable-binding approach is that we can make visible that taking fixed points commutes with substitution.
This seems to be an important insight. In the case at hand, where we study Strong Löb, this insight is immediate. It also holds
more generally, for weaker forms of Löb’s Principle, but in those cases to check it is more delicate.
Thirdly we can develop the theory on cyclic syntax. (This third option was explored for the case of classical provability logic without
the principle 4 in
[
Vis21
]
.)

In the present paper, we will follow a middle road related to the second option: we add a fixed point operator that can only bind one designated
variable that we write as
\ast
. To study the addition of a variable binding operator in the full sense,
one needs to develop some machinery that is beyond the scope of the present paper. We hope to return to these matters in
a subsequent paper.

We prove
\mathsf{sL}
using the addition of primitive fixed points as constants.
We define system
\mathsf{iA}_{\mathsf{fp}}
. The language of
\mathsf{iA}_{\mathsf{fp}}
is given by
\varphi
in the following grammar:

\bullet

\pi::=p_{0}\mid p_{1}\mid p_{2}\dots
,

\bullet

\varphi::=\pi\mid\top\mid\bot\mid\digamma\chi\mid(\varphi\wedge\varphi)\mid(\varphi\vee\varphi)\mid(\varphi\to\varphi)\mid(\varphi\strictif\varphi)
,

\bullet

\psi::=\ast\mid\pi\mid\top\mid\bot\mid\digamma\chi\mid(\psi\wedge\psi)\mid(\psi\vee\psi)\mid(\psi\to\psi)\mid(\psi\strictif\psi)
,

\bullet

\chi::=\pi\mid\top\mid\bot\mid\digamma\chi\mid(\chi\wedge\chi)\mid(\chi\vee\chi)\mid(\chi\to\chi)\mid(\psi\strictif\psi)
.

We note that
\ast
is supposed to be bound by the lowest
\digamma
that is above it in the parse tree.
We are allowed to substitute
\varphi
-formulas in
\varphi
-,
\psi
and
\chi
-formulas in the usual way.

The reasoning system of
\mathsf{iA}_{\mathsf{fp}}
is given as follows.

\bullet

We have the axioms of
{\sf HLC}^{\sharp}
extended to the new language.

\bullet

We add as axioms
fixed point equations:
\digamma\chi\leftrightarrow\chi\digamma\chi
, where
\ast
functions as the variable of substitution.

\bullet

We add necessitation for the extended system.

Theorem 3.16
.

\mathsf{iA}_{\mathsf{fp}}+{{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}}\vdash\mathsf{sL}

Proof.

We reason in
\mathsf{iA}_{\mathsf{fp}}+{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}
. Suppose (†)
\necessary\varphi\to\varphi
. Let

Suppose

{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}

\necessary\ell
and, by the fixed point equation, that
\necessary(\ell\to\varphi)

\necessary\varphi
. By
(†), we have
\varphi
. We cancel our assumption

, to obtain (‡)
\ell\to\varphi
. It follows that
\necessary(\ell\to\varphi)
. Hence,
again by the fixed point equation,
\ell
. Combining this with (‡), we find
\varphi
.
❑

It is easy to see that
\ell^{\prime}:=\digamma(\necessary\ast\to\varphi)
gives us an alternative proof.

We note that via Strong Löb we have explicit and unique fixed points, so:

\mathsf{iA}_{\mathsf{fp}}+{\mathsf{S}}_{\necessary}\vdash\digamma\chi\leftrightarrow\chi\top.

5.
Uniform Interpolation

In this section, we prove uniform interpolation for our central systems. We begin with formulating the main result, with the remainder of the section devoted to its proof.

Let
\varLambda
be one of
{\mathsf{iSL}}_{\mathsf{a}}
and
{\sf iSL}^{+}_{\sf a}
. We write
|X|
for the cardinality of
X
. We have:

Theorem 5.1

(Uniform Interpolation)
.

Consider any formula
\varphi
and any finite set of variables
{\widetilde{q}}
. Let

\nu:=|\{\psi\in{\sf Sub}(\varphi)\mid\psi\text{ is a variable, an implication or a Lewis implication}\}|

We have the following.

1.

There is a formula
\exists{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi
, the
post-interpolant
of
\varphi
, such that:

a.

{\sf PV}(\exists{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi)\subseteq{\sf PV}(\varphi)\setminus{\widetilde{q}}

b.

For all
\psi
with
{\sf PV}(\psi)\cap{\widetilde{q}}=\emptyset
, we have:

\varLambda\vdash\varphi\rightarrow\psi\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\varLambda\vdash\exists{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi\rightarrow\psi.

c.

\mathfrak{c}(\exists{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi)\leq 2\nu+2
.

2.

There is a formula
\forall{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi
, the
pre-interpolant
of
\varphi
, such that:

a.

{\sf PV}(\forall{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi)\subseteq{\sf PV}(\varphi)\setminus{\widetilde{q}}

b.

For all
\psi
with
{\sf PV}(\psi)\cap{\widetilde{q}}=\emptyset
, we have:

\varLambda\vdash\psi\rightarrow\varphi\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\varLambda\vdash\psi\rightarrow\forall{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi.

c.

\mathfrak{c}(\forall{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi)\leq 2\nu+1
.

We note that uniform interpolation strengthens interpolation, since the uniform interpolants just depend on one of the
two formulas involved in interpolation. In classical logic the post- and the pre-interpolant are interdefinable.
In constructive logic, the post-interpolant is definable from the pre-interpolant by taking

\exists{\widetilde{q}}\;\varphi:\leftrightarrow\forall r\,(\forall{\widetilde{q}}\,(\varphi\to r)\to r),

where
r
is a fresh variable. However, we cannot,
prima facie
, generally define a pre-interpolant from a post-interpolant.

Example 5.2
.

The following counter-example was suggested to us by Marta Bilkova. The positive fragment of
\mathsf{IPC}
has post-interpolation,
but not pre-interpolation. The fact that the positive fragment has post-interpolation was shown by Dick de Jongh and Zhiguang Zhao in their article

[
dJZ15
]
. The fragment cannot have post-interpolation, since, for example,
\forall p\;p
defines
\bot
.
❍

Characterizing quantum coherence based on the nonclassicality of the Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobability
Agung Budiyono 1,2,3, Joel F. Sumbowo 1,3, Mohammad K. Agusta 2,3, Bagus E. B. Nurhandoko 4

Abstract
In recent years, there is a huge interest in the characterization, quantification and manipulation of quantum coherence, a defining nonclassical feature of quantum mechanics, by regarding it as a resource in quantum information processing. On the other hand, since the early days, there has been a research program using the nonclassical values of some quasiprobability distributions, i.e., those that deviate from the conventional real and nonnegative probability, to indicate the nonclassical aspects of quantum phenomena. Previously, we linked this two nonclassical features of quantum mechanics, showing that coherence in a quantum state can indeed be quantitatively characterized by using the total nonreality of the associated Kirkwood-Dirac (KD) quasiprobability. Here, we develop another faithful quantifier of coherence based on the KD nonclassicality which captures simultaneously the total nonreality and the total negativity of the real part of the KD quasiprobability. This is motivated by the fact that the real part of the KD quasiprobability appears independently of the imaginary part, and its negativity has been used to characterize quantumness in different areas of quantum science and technology. The proposed coherence quantifier, which we call KD-nonclassicality coherence, gives a lower bound to the l 1 l_{1} -norm coherence. It also gives a lower bound to the uncertainty of outcomes of measurement described by a projection-valued measure corresponding to the incoherent orthonormal basis quantified by half of the Tsallis entropy with entropy index 1 / 2 1/2 . For arbitrary pure states, the KD-nonclassicality coherence and the Tsallis-entropy-based measurement uncertainty are shown to be identical. Moreover, KD-nonclassicality coherence is upper bounded by the state purity and maximized by the maximally coherent states. We then sketch a variational scheme for its direct estimation in laboratory and discuss an application for the characterization of static susceptibility.

III
Quantifying quantum coherence using the Kirkwood-Dirac nonclassicality

Since the KD quasiprobability is an informationally equivalent representation of the quantum state, it is instructive to study the way in which the KD quasiprobability encodes the nonclassical aspects of a quantum state such as coherence, entanglement and general quantum correlation. IV
Upper bounds for the KD-nonclassicality coherence:
l_{1}
-norm coherence, measurement uncertainty and purity

Let us proceed to develop several upper bounds for the KD-nonclassicality coherence.

First, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1
. The KD-nonclassicality coherence of a state
\varrho
on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
relative to an incoherent orthonormal basis
\{\ket{a}\}
of
\mathcal{H}
gives a lower bound to the corresponding
l_{1}
-norm coherence defined in Eq. (
10
), i.e.,

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})\leq C_{l_{1}}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\}).

(12)

Proof
. One first has, from the definition of the KD-nonclassicality coherence in Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b}\big|\sum_{a^{\prime}}\braket{b|a}\braket{a|\varrho|a^{\prime}}\braket{a^{\prime}|b}\big|-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,a^{\prime},b}\big|\braket{a|\varrho|a^{\prime}}\braket{b|a}\braket{a^{\prime}|b}\big|-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\big|\braket{a|\varrho|a^{\prime}}|\sum_{b_{*}}|\braket{b_{*}|a}\braket{a^{\prime}|b_{*}}\big|-1,

where
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})
is a second basis which achieves the supremum. On the other hand, using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have
\sum_{b_{*}}|\braket{b_{*}|a}\braket{a^{\prime}|b_{*}}|\leq(\sum_{b_{*}}|\braket{b_{*}|a}|^{2}\sum_{b_{*}^{\prime}}|\braket{a^{\prime}|b^{\prime}_{*}}|^{2})^{1/2}=1
, where we have used the completeness relation
\sum_{b_{*}}\ket{b_{*}}\bra{b_{*}}=\mathbb{I}
. Moreover, the equality (i.e., the maximum) is obtained when the pair of bases
\{\ket{a}\}
and
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
are mutually unbiased so that
|\braket{a|b_{*}}|=1/\sqrt{d}
for all
{(a,b_{*})}
. Inserting this into Eq. (
13
), we finally obtain

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a,a^{\prime}}\big|\braket{a|\varrho|a^{\prime}}|-1

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\neq a^{\prime}}\big|\braket{a|\varrho|a^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{l_{1}}(\varrho;\{\ket{a}\}),

where we have used the normalization
\sum_{a}\braket{a|\varrho|a}=1
. ∎

Next, recall that the KD-nonclassicality coherence captures the noncommutativity between the state
\varrho
and the projection-valued measure
\{\Pi_{a}\}
associated with incoherent orthonormal basis
\{\ket{a}\}
, i.e., there is a subset of
\{a\}
such that
[\Pi_{a},\varrho]\neq 0
. Such noncommutativity must lead to uncertainty of outcomes of the measurement described by the projection-valued measure
\{\Pi_{a}\}
over the state
\varrho
. Since the measurement uncertainty must also take into account the classical uncertainty originating from the statistical mixing in the preparation of the state
\varrho
when it is not pure, the measurement uncertainty must somehow give another upper bound to the KD-nonclassicality coherence. The following proposition corroborate the above intuition.

Proposition 2
. The KD-nonclassicality coherence of a quantum state
\varrho
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with a finite dimension
d
relative to the incoherent orthonormal basis
\{\ket{a}\}
of
\mathcal{H}
is upper bounded by the uncertainty of the outcomes of measurement described by the projection-valued measure
\{\Pi_{a}\}
over the state
\varrho
as

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\sqrt{{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)}-1

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S_{\frac{1}{2}}(\{{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)\})

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}-1,

(17)

where
S_{\frac{1}{2}}(\{{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)\}):=2(\sum_{a}\sqrt{{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)}-1)
is the Tsallis entropy with entropy-index
q=1/2
capturing the randomness of drawing samples from the probability
{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)

Tsallis on Tsallis entropy
. Moreover, for pure states, the inequality in Eq. (
15
) becomes an equality.

Proof
. We first have, from the definition of the KD-nonclassicality coherence in Eq. (
7
),

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})+1

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a}\sum_{b}\Big|\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b}\Pi_{a}\varrho)}{{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b}\varrho)}\Big|{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b}\varrho)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\Big(\sum_{b_{*}}\Big|\frac{{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\Pi_{a}\varrho)}{{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\varrho)}\Big|^{2}{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\varrho)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\Big(\sum_{b_{*}}\frac{|{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\Pi_{a}\varrho)|^{2}}{{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\varrho)}\Big)^{1/2},

(19)

where
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})
in Eq. (
18
) is a second basis which achieves the supremum and we have also used the Jensen inequality. Next, applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the numerator on the right-hand side of Eq. (
19
), i.e.,
|{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\Pi_{a}\varrho)|^{2}=|{\rm Tr}((\Pi_{b_{*}}^{1/2}\Pi_{a}\varrho^{1/2})(\varrho^{1/2}\Pi_{b_{*}}^{1/2}))|^{2}\leq{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b_{*}}\Pi_{a}\varrho\Pi_{a}){\rm Tr}(\varrho\Pi_{b_{*}})
, and using the completeness relation
\sum_{b_{*}}\Pi_{b_{*}}=\mathbb{I}
, we obtain

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\big({\rm Tr}(\Pi_{a}^{2}\varrho)\big)^{1/2}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\sqrt{{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S_{\frac{1}{2}}(\{{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)\}),

where we have used
{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{a}^{2}\varrho)={\rm Tr}(\Pi_{a}\varrho)={\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)
. Since the maximum of the entropy is obtained when
{\rm Pr}(a|\varrho)=1/d
, one has, upon inserting into Eq. (
15
),
C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})\leq\sqrt{d}-1
.

To prove the second half of the proposition, first, for pure states
\varrho=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
, the KD-nonclassicality coherence reads

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi};\{\Pi_{a}\})=\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b}\big|\braket{b|a}\braket{a|\psi}\braket{\psi|b}\big|-1.

(20)

Next, for a Hilbert space with any finite dimension
d
, it is always possible to find a triple of mutually unbiased bases (MUB)

. Let then
\ket{\psi}
be an element of a basis, say
\{\ket{c}\}
. Then, it is always possible to find a basis
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
which is mutually unbiased with both
\{\ket{a}\}
and
\{\ket{c}\}
. In this case, we have
|\braket{\psi|b_{*}}|=|\braket{b_{*}|a}|=1/\sqrt{d}
, so that inserting these into Eq. (
20
) we get

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi};\{\Pi_{a}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\big|\braket{a|\psi}\big|-1

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}\sqrt{{\rm Pr}(a|\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi})}-1.

∎

Hence, the KD-nonclassicality coherence indeed captures measurement uncertainty, in the sense that it gives a lower bound to the latter that is quantified by half of the Tsallis entropy (with entropy index

Note however that the measurement uncertainty also takes into account the contribution from the classical uncertainty originating from the statistical mixing of the state when it is not pure, so that it is not vanishing for any incoherent mixed state. For a single qubit, this can be seen in Fig. 2 for
\gamma\neq 1
when
\theta=0,\pi
. As an extreme example, one can see that for both the maximally coherent state in
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\ket{\psi_{\rm mc}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{a}e^{i\theta_{a}}\ket{a}
,

The above observation suggests that it is desirable to have an upper bound in terms of the state purity, which captures the intuition that the coherence should decrease when the purity decreases. Indeed, for the case of a single qubit, this intuition is corroborated in Fig. 2. Namely, as we decrease the purity by decreasing
r
, the KD-nonclassicality coherence also decreases, but the measurement uncertainty increases. We obtain the following general result.

Proposition 3
. The KD-nonclassicality coherence of a state
\varrho
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with a finite dimension
d
relative to any incoherent orthonormal basis
\{\ket{a}\}
of
\mathcal{H}
is bounded from above as

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{1/2}{\rm Tr}(\varrho^{2})^{1/2}-1

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d}-1.

(23)

The upper bound in Eq. (
22
) is attained when the second orthonormal basis
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
which achieves the supremum in Eq. (
7
) is mutually unbiased with the incoherent orthonormal basis
\{\ket{a}\}
. For a pure state, the upper bound in Eq. (
23
) is attained for maximally coherent states:
\ket{\psi_{\rm mc}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{a}e^{i\theta_{a}}\ket{a}
,
\theta_{a}\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\ket{\psi_{\rm mc}}
is also maximally coherent relative to
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
.

Proof
. From the definition of the KD-nonclassicality coherence, we directly have

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b}|\braket{a|\varrho|b}||\braket{b|a}|-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d^{1/2}\big(\sum_{a,b_{*}}|\braket{a|\varrho|b_{*}}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d^{1/2}{\rm Tr}(\varrho^{2})^{1/2}-1,

(25)

where
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
is a basis which achieves the supremum, and we have made use of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to get Eq. (
24
) and the completeness relation for the orthonormal basis to get Eq. (
25
). The inequality in Eq. (
23
) is then obtained by noting the fact that the purity of a quantum state
\varrho
is not larger than 1, i.e.,
{\rm Tr}(\varrho^{2})\leq 1
.

Further, the condition for equality in Eq. (
24
), which is obtained using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, is
|\braket{a|\varrho|b_{*}}|=\eta_{\varrho}|\braket{a|b_{*}}|
for some scalar
\eta_{\varrho}
which is independent of the index
(a,b_{*})
. This can only be true if
|\braket{a|\varrho|b_{*}}|
is separable into a function of
(a,b_{*})
and that of
\varrho
, or, if
|\braket{a|\varrho|b_{*}}|
is a uniform function independent of
a
and
b_{*}
. Since the former condition does not apply, the latter must be true. But in this case,
|\braket{a|b_{*}}|
must also be a uniform function independent of
(a,b_{*})
. To guarantee normalization, we thus have
|\braket{a|b_{*}}|=1/\sqrt{d}
for all
a,b_{*}
, which means that the pair of bases
\{\ket{a}\}
and
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
are mutually unbiased.

Next, it is clear that the inequality in Eq. (
23
) becomes equality when
{\rm Tr}(\varrho^{2})=1
, i.e., when the state is pure:
\varrho=\ket{\psi}\bra{\psi}
. Hence, combining with the condition for the equality in Eq. (
22
) above, we must have:
|\braket{a|\psi}\braket{\psi|b_{*}}|=\alpha_{\psi}|\braket{a|b_{*}}|=\alpha_{\psi}/\sqrt{d}
, for some scalar
\alpha_{\psi}
which is independent of the index
(a,b_{*})
. Following the same argument as for the case of mixed states, this can only be true if
|\braket{a|\psi}|
and
|\braket{b_{*}|\psi}|
are uniform functions independent of
a
and
b_{*}
. To guarantee normalization, for
d
-dimensional Hilbert space, we must have
|\braket{a|\psi}|=|\braket{b_{*}|\psi}|=1/\sqrt{d}
. This means
\ket{\psi}
is maximally coherent with respect to both
\{\ket{a}\}
and
\{\ket{b_{*}}\}
. ∎

As intuitively expected, the upper bound for the KD-nonclassicality coherence of Eq. (
22
) decreases as the purity decreases. In particular, when the state is maximally mixed, i.e.,
\varrho=\mathbb{I}/d
, the right-hand side of Eq. (
22
) is vanishing. Hence, as the amount of the coherence is concerned, the upper bound in Eq. (
22
) is more informative than that in Eq. (
15
).

V
Experimental estimation and application of the KD-nonclassicality coherence

V.1
Hybrid quantum-classical variational scheme for direct estimation of the KD-nonclassicality coherence

Given an unknown quantum state, one of the important problems in the quantification of the quantum coherence of the state is to develop a coherence quantifier which can be translated directly to a set of operational procedures in laboratory without first estimating the full density matrix via the quantum state tomography. Such an experimental scheme to estimate the coherence of an unknown quantum state is not only important to better understand the operational meaning of quantum coherence, it may also lead to useful insight to conceive new applications of the concept of coherence.

Fortunately, the KD-nonclassicality coherence defined in Eq. (
7
) can be measured or estimated directly, thanks to the fact that the real and imaginary parts of the KD quasiprobability can be estimated directly. Different methods for the estimation of the KD quasiprobability have been proposed in the literature. The earliest method is by using weak measurement with postselection for the measurement of weak value

Cohen estimating of weak value with strong measurements
;

. Let us stress that the real and imaginary parts of the KD quasiprobability can be measured independently of each other, suggesting that they may capture two different notion of quantum statistics.

VI
Summary and Remarks

We have argued that the KD nonclassicality, which captures simultaneously the total nonreality of the KD quasiprobability and the total negativity of its real part, maximized over one of the pair defining orthonormal bases, can be used to quantitatively characterize the coherence in the associated quantum state relative to the other defining orthonormal basis. We showed that the quantity so defined, called KD-nonclassicality coherence, possesses certain plausible properties expected for a faithful quantifier of coherence. We obtained upper bounds in terms of
l_{1}
-norm coherence, the uncertainty in the measurement described by the projection-valued measure corresponding to the incoherent basis quantified by Tsallis entropy with entropy index
1/2
, and the purity of the quantum state. We showed that for arbitrary pure state, the KD-nonclassicality coherence is equal to the Tsallis-entropy-based measurement uncertainty, and it is maximized by the maximally coherent state. We sketched a scheme for a direct estimation of the KD-nonclassicality coherence using a variational quantum circuit implementable in the currently available NISQ hardware, and discussed an application to characterize the static susceptibility.

The KD-nonclassicality coherence proposed in this work complements the KD-nonreality coherence based on solely the total sum of the absolute nonreal part of the KD quasiprobability proposed in Ref.
Agung KD-nonreality coherence
. In particular, Propositions 2 and 3 show that KD-nonclassicality coherence and KD-nonreality coherence of Ref.
Agung KD-nonreality coherence
are upper bounded differently by the measurement uncertainty and state purity, suggesting that they may capture two distinguished notions of quantum uncertainty. To this end, it is interesting to further study in the future the relation between the two quantifiers of coherence. Our results thus strengthen the quantitative link between the quantumness manifests in the nonclassical values of KD quasiprobability and the nonclassicality captured by the concept of quantum coherence. Moreover, it reveals the role of the quantum coherence in diverse physical phenomena in which the nonclassical values of KD quasiprobability have been shown to be crucial, and vice versa. We hope that our approach may further disclose the deep link between the quantumness captured by the anomalous nonclassical values of KD quasiprobability and the associated concept of strange weak values, and the different forms of nonclassicality captured by the concept of quantum state such as coherence
Agung KD-nonreality coherence
, asymmetry
Agung translational asymmetry from nonreal weak value
;
Agung trace-norm asymmetry
, general quantum correlation
Agung KD general quantum correlation
and entanglement.

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{A,b}\big|\sum_{a\in A}{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b}\Pi_{a}\varrho))\big|-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b}|{\rm Tr}(\Pi_{b}\Pi_{a}\varrho)|-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho;\{\Pi_{a}\}).

∎

Property (vii)

Nonincreasing under a controlloed incoherent permutation of the element of the incoherent basis
.

Before proceeding with a proof, let us first justify the plausibility of the conditions for the property (vii) mentioned in the main text. Consider a class of completely positive trace-preserving linear maps or quantum channel which can be expressed in terms of free dilation as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\varrho_{S})=\sum_{j}{\rm Tr}_{E}\big((\mathbb{I}_{S}\otimes P_{j})U_{SE}(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})U_{SE}^{\dagger}\big),

(44)

where
\varrho_{S}
is the state of the (principal) system,
\varrho_{E}
is the state of an ancilla (auxiliary system),
U_{SE}
is a unitary which couples the system and the ancilla,
\{P_{j}\}
is a projection-valued measure describing projective measurement on the ancilla, and the partial trace is taken over the ancilla. Moreover, by free dilation we mean that the following conditions are satisfied. First, the state of the ancilla
\varrho_{E}
and the projection-valued measure
\{P_{j}\}
are incoherent relative to an a priori fixed basis
\{\ket{e}_{E}\}
. And second, the action of the coupling unitary takes the form:

\displaystyle U_{SE}\ket{a}_{S}\ket{e}_{E}=\ket{\mu_{e}(a)}_{S}\ket{e}_{E},

(45)

where
\ket{a}_{S}
is the element of the incoherent basis of the principal system and
\{\mu_{e}(a)\}
is a set of permutations of the elements of the incoherent basis of the principal system depending on the element of the incoherent basis of the ancilla.
U_{SE}
is therefore a control incoherent permutation transformation. For example, if both the system
S
and the ancilla
E
are qubit (two-dimensional system), and their incoherent bases are given by the computational basis
\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\}
, and assuming
\mu_{e}(a)=a+e\mod{2}
, then
U_{SE}
is just the CNOT gate.
U_{SE}
thus generalizes the free permutation unitaries in the resource theory of coherence by making it dependent on the state of the ancilla.

We can then state the following condition mentioned in the Property (vii).

Proposition 4
. The completely positive trace-preserving quantum operation
\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\cdot)
defined in Eq. (
44
) is incoherence preserving, namely it maps an incoherent state onto an incoherent state.

Proof
. Consider an arbitrary incoherent state of the principal system:
\varrho_{S}=\sum_{a}p_{a}\ket{a}\bra{a}
, where
\{p_{a}\}
,
p_{a}\geq 0
,
\sum_{a}p_{a}=1
, are probabilities. Further, since the state of the ancilla
\varrho_{E}
is incoherent with respect to the ancillary basis
\{\ket{e}\}
, it can be decomposed as
\varrho_{E}=\sum_{e}r_{e}\ket{e}\bra{e}
, where
\{r_{e}\}
,
r_{e}\geq 0
,
\sum_{e}r_{e}=1
, are probabilities. Then one has, upon employing Eq. (
45
),

\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\varrho_{S})

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{E}\big(U_{SE}(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})U_{SE}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,e,e^{\prime}}p_{a}r_{e}{\rm Tr}_{E}\big((\mathbb{I}_{S}\otimes\ket{e^{\prime}}\bra{e^{\prime}})U_{SE}(\ket{a}\bra{a}\otimes\ket{e}\bra{e})U_{SE}^{\dagger}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,e,e^{\prime}}p_{a}r_{e}{\rm Tr}_{E}\big(\ket{\mu_{e}(a)}\bra{\mu_{e}(a)}\otimes\ket{e}\bra{e}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e}r_{e}\sum_{a}p_{a}\ket{\mu_{e}(a)}\bra{\mu_{e}(a)}.

\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\varrho_{S})
is thus a statistical mixture of the elements of the incoherent basis, hence it is again an incoherent state. It can also be seen in Eq. (
46
) that the quantum operation
\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\varrho_{S})
maps the incoherent state as:
\sum_{a}p_{a}\ket{a}\bra{a}\mapsto\sum_{a}p_{a}\ket{\mu_{e}(a)}\bra{\mu_{e}(a)}
, by permuting each elements as
a\mapsto\mu_{e}(a)
with a probability
r_{e}=\braket{e|\varrho_{E}|e}
, and then delete the records on
e
. ∎

With the above clarification, the proof of Property (vii) then proceeds as follows.

Proof
. First, using the Stinespring’s dilation we have

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\varrho_{S});\{\Pi_{a}\})+1

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b}\big|\braket{b|\Pi_{a}{\rm Tr}_{E}\big(U_{SE}(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})U_{SE}^{\dagger}\big)|b}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b,e}\big|\braket{b,e|(\Pi_{a}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{E})U_{SE}(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})U_{SE}^{\dagger}|b,e}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b,e}\big|\braket{b,e|U_{SE}U_{SE}^{\dagger}(\Pi_{a}\otimes\Pi_{e})U_{SE}(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})U_{SE}^{\dagger}|b,e}\big|

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{\mu_{e}^{-1}(a),b,e}\big|\braket{b,e|U_{SE}(\Pi_{\mu^{-1}_{e}(a)}\otimes\Pi_{e})(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})U_{SE}^{\dagger}|b,e}\big|.

(49)

Here, the basis of the ancilla inserted in Eq. (
47
) is arbitrary and we have chosen the incoherent basis
\{\ket{e}\}
, in Eq. (
48
) we have inserted the identity
U_{SE}U_{SE}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{I}_{SE}
, and to get Eq. (
49
) we have used
U_{SE}^{\dagger}\ket{a}\otimes\ket{e}=\ket{\mu_{e}^{-1}(a)}\otimes\ket{e}
which follows from the definition of the controlled incoherent permutation unitary in Eq. (
45
), where
\mu_{e}^{-1}(a)
is the inverse permutation of
\mu(a)
. Next, expanding
\ket{b}=\sum_{a}\braket{a|b}\ket{a}
, and again using
U_{SE}^{\dagger}\ket{a}\otimes\ket{e}=\ket{\mu_{e}^{-1}(a)}\otimes\ket{e}
, we get

\displaystyle U_{SE}^{\dagger}\ket{b,e}=\sum_{a}\braket{a|b}\ket{\mu_{e}^{-1}(a)}\ket{e}=\ket{\nu_{e}(b)}\ket{e},

(50)

where
\ket{\nu_{e}(b)}:=\sum_{a}\braket{a|b}\ket{\mu_{e}^{-1}(a)}
. Moreover, one can check that for each
e
, the set
\{\ket{\nu_{e}(b)}\}
comprises an orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space, i.e.,
\braket{\nu_{e}(b)|\nu_{e}(b^{\prime})}=\delta_{bb^{\prime}}
and
\sum_{b}\ket{\nu_{e}(b)}\bra{\nu_{e}(b)}=\mathbb{I}
. Inserting Eq. (
50
) into Eq. (
49
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\Phi_{\rm CIP}(\varrho_{S});\{\Pi_{a}\})+1

(51)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{\nu_{e}(b)}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{\mu_{e}^{-1}(a),\nu_{e}(b),e}\big|\braket{\nu_{e}(b),e|(\Pi_{\mu^{-1}_{e}(a)}\otimes\Pi_{e})(\varrho_{S}\otimes\varrho_{E})|\nu_{e}(b),e}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{\ket{b}\}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rm o}(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{a,b,e}|\braket{b|\Pi_{a}\varrho_{S}|b}|\braket{e|\varrho_{E}|e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e}\big(C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho_{S};\{\Pi_{a}\})+1\big)\braket{e|\varrho_{E}|e}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\rm KD}^{\rm NCl}(\varrho_{S};\{\Pi_{a}\})+1.

(53)

Here, in Eq. (
51
) we have made relabelling of the indexes of the bases of the principal system and noted the fact that
\braket{e|\varrho_{E}|e}\geq 0
, to get Eq. (
52
) we have used the definition of the KD-nonclassicality coherence, and Eq. (
53
) is due to the normalization
\sum_{e}\braket{e|\varrho_{E}|e}=1
. ∎

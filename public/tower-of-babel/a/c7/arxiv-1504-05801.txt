Some identities of symmetry for q q -Euler polynomials under the symmetric group of degree n n arising from fermionic p p -adic q q -integrals on ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p}
Dmitry V. Dolgy, Dae San Kim, Taekyun Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate some new symmetric identities for the q q -Euler polynomials under the symmetric group of degree n n which are derived from fermionic p p -adic q q -integrals on ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B68, 11S80, 05A19, 05A30

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime number such that
p\equiv 1\pmod{2}
. Throughout
this paper,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\mathbb{C}_{p}

will denote the ring of
p
-adic integers, the field of
p
-adic
rational numbers and the completion of the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
Let
q
be an indeterminate in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
such that
\left|1-q\right|_{p}<p^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
.
The
p
-adic norm is normalized as
\left|p\right|_{p}=\frac{1}{p}

and the
q
-analogue of the number
x
is defined as
\left[x\right]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{x}}{1-q}
.
Note that
\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}\left[x\right]_{q}=x
.

As is well known, the Euler numbers are defined by

E_{0}=1,\quad\left(E+1\right)^{n}+E_{n}=2\delta_{0,n},\quad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}\right),

with the usual convention about replacing
E^{n}
by
E_{n}
(see

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
8
,
10
,
5
,
7
,
11
,
6
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
).

The Euler polynomials are given by

E_{n}\left(x\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}x^{n-l}E_{l}=\left(E+x\right)^{n},\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-1,key-2}{}{}]}}\right).

In
[
8
]
, Kim introduced Carlitz-type
q
-Euler numbers as follows:

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{0,q}=1,\quad q\left(q\mathcal{E}_{q}+1\right)^{n}+\mathcal{E}_{n,q}=\left[2\right]_{q}\delta_{0,n},\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-5}{}{}]}}\right),

with the usual convention about replacing
\mathcal{E}_{q}^{n}
by

\mathcal{E}_{n,q}
.

The Carlitz-type
q
-Euler polynomials are also defined as

(1.2)

\mathcal{E}_{n,q}\left(x\right)=\left(q^{x}\mathcal{E}_{q}+\left[x\right]_{q}\right)^{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}q^{lx}\mathcal{E}_{l,q}\left[x\right]_{q}^{n-l},\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-3,key-5}{}{}]}}\right).

Let
C\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
be the space of all
\mathbb{C}_{p}
-valued continuous functions
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then, for
f\in C\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
, the fermionic
p
-adic

q
-integral on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined by Kim as

(1.3)

\displaystyle I_{-q}\left(f\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f\left(x\right)d\mu_{-q}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\left[p^{N}\right]_{-q}}\sum_{x=0}^{p^{N}-1}f\left(x\right)\left(-q\right)^{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1+q}{1+q^{p^{N}}}\sum_{x=0}^{p^{N}-1}f\left(x\right)\left(-q\right)^{x},\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-5,key-6,key-7,key-8,key-9,key-10,key-11}{}{}]}}\right).

From (
1.3
), we note that

(1.4)

q^{n}I_{-q}\left(f_{n}\right)+\left(-1\right)^{n-1}I_{-q}\left(f\right)=\left[2\right]_{q}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\left(-1\right)^{n-1-l}f\left(l\right),\quad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right),\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-5}{}{}]}}\right).

The Carlitz-type
q
-Euler polynomials can be represented by the
fermionic
p
-adic
q
-integral on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as follows:

(1.5)

\mathcal{E}_{n,q}\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[x+y\right]_{q}^{n}d\mu_{-q}\left(y\right),\quad\left(n\geq 0\right),\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-5}{}{}]}}\right).

Thus, by (
1.5
), we get

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n,q}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}q^{lx}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y\right]_{q}^{l}d\mu_{q}\left(y\right)\left[x\right]_{q}^{n-l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}q^{lx}\mathcal{E}_{l,q}\left[x\right]_{q}^{n-l},\quad\left(\text{see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{Number}{key-5}{}{}]}}\right).

From (
1.4
), we can easily derive

(1.7)

q\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[x+1\right]_{q}^{n}d\mu_{-q}\left(x\right)+\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[x\right]_{q}^{n}d\mu_{-q}\left(x\right)=\left[2\right]_{q}\delta_{0,n},\quad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}\right).

The equation (
1.7
) is equivalent to

(1.8)

q\mathcal{E}_{n.q}\left(1\right)+\mathcal{E}_{n,q}=\left[2\right]_{q}\delta_{0,n},\quad\left(n\geq 0\right).

The purpose of this paper is to give some new symmetric identities
for the Carlitz-type
q
-Euler polynomials under the symmetric
group of degree
n
which are derived from fermionic
p
-adic
q
-integrals
on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

2.
Symmetric identities for
\mathcal{E}_{n,q}\left(x\right)
under

S_{n}

Let
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
w_{1}\equiv w_{2}\equiv w_{3}\equiv\cdots\equiv w_{n}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.
Then, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\left[p^{N}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\sum_{y=0}^{p^{N}-1}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}\left(-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}\right)^{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m=0}^{w_{n}-1}\sum_{y=0}^{p^{N}-1}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)\left(m+w_{n}y\right)+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}

\displaystyle\times\left(-1\right)^{m+y}q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}\left(m+w_{n}y\right)}.

Thus, by (
2.1
), we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\frac{1}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{l}-1}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}}q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{l}-1}\sum_{m=0}^{w_{n}-1}\sum_{y=0}^{p^{N}-1}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}+m+y}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)m+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)y}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)\left(m+w_{n}y\right)+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}.

As this expression is invariant under any permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
,
we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

w_{1}\equiv w_{2}\equiv\cdots\equiv w_{n}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
. Then,
the following expressions

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{\sigma\left(l\right)}-1}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}}q^{w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(i\right)}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(i\right)}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}\left(y\right)

are the same for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
,
\left(n\geq 1\right).

Now, we observe that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}.

By (
2.3
), we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}t}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{m}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}\mathcal{E}_{m,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}.

For
m\geq 0
, from (
2.4
), we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)y+\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\right)x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}\mathcal{E}_{m,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right),\quad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Therefore, by Theorem
2.1
and (
2.5
), we obtain the
following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
w_{1},\dots w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
w_{1}\equiv w_{2}\equiv\cdots\equiv w_{n}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.
For
m\geq 0
, the following expressions

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}\right]_{q}^{m}}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{\sigma\left(l\right)}-1}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}}q^{w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(i\right)}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{E}_{m,q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}\left(w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}x+w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}}\right)

are the same for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
.

It is not difficult to show that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}}{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}+q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\left[y+w_{n}x\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}.

Thus, by (
2.6
), we get

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{m}d\mu_{q^{-w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left(\frac{\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}}{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}}\right)^{m-l}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}^{m-l}q^{lw_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{l}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left(\frac{\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}}{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}}\right)^{m-l}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}^{m-l}

\displaystyle\times q^{lw_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\mathcal{E}_{l,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x\right).

From (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\frac{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{m}}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{w_{l}-1}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}k_{l}}q^{w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left[y+w_{n}x+w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{w_{j}}\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}^{n}d\mu_{-q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(y\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\frac{\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{l}}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}^{m-l}\mathcal{E}_{l,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\prod_{s=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{s}=0}^{w_{s}-1}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}k_{j}}q^{\left(l+1\right)w_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q^{w_{n}}}^{m-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n-1}}}}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right]_{q}^{l}\left[w_{n}\right]_{q}^{m-l}\mathcal{E}_{l,q^{w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1}}}\left(w_{n}x\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\hat{T}_{m,q^{w_{n}}}\left(w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n-1}\mid l\right),

where

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\hat{T}_{m,q}\left(w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1}\mid l\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{s=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{s}=0}^{w_{s}-1}q^{\left(l+1\right)\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n-1}w_{i}\right)k_{j}\right]_{q}^{m-l}\left(-1\right)^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}k_{j}}.

As this expression is invariant under any permutation in
S_{n}
, we have the following
theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

w_{1}\equiv w_{2}\equiv\cdots\equiv w_{n}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
. For
m\geq 0
,
the following expressions

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\frac{1}{\left[2\right]_{q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}w_{\sigma\left(2\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\binom{m}{l}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}w_{\sigma\left(j\right)}\right]_{q}^{l}\left[w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}\right]_{q}^{m-l}

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{E}_{l,q^{w_{\sigma\left(1\right)}\cdots w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}}}\left(w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}x\right)\hat{T}_{m,q^{w_{\sigma\left(n\right)}}}\left(w_{\sigma\left(1\right)},w_{\sigma\left(2\right)},\dots,w_{\sigma\left(n-1\right)}\mid l\right)

are the same for any
\sigma\in S_{n}
.

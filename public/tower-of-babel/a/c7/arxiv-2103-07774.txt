Tykhonov Well-posedness of a Heat Transfer Problem with Unilateral Constraints
Mircea Sofonea, Perpignan, Domingo A. Tarzia, Rosario

Abstract




Abstract.
We consider an elliptic boundary value problem with unilateral constraints and subdifferential boundary conditions. The problem describes the heat transfer in a domain
D\subset{{\rm I}\mkern-3.0mu{\rm R}}^{d}
and its weak formulation is in the form of a hemivariational inequality for the temperature field, denoted by
{\cal P}
. We associate to Problem
{\cal P}
an optimal control problem, denoted by
{\cal Q}
. Then, using appropriate Tykhonov triples, governed by a nonlinear operator
G
and a convex
\widetilde{K}
, we provide results concerning the well-posedness of problems
{\cal P}
and
{\cal Q}
. Our main results are Theorems
14
and
18
, together with their corollaries. Their proofs are based on arguments of compactness, lower semicontinuity and pseudomonotonicity.
Moreover, we consider three relevant perturbations of the heat transfer boundary valued problem which lead to penalty versions of Problem
{\cal P}
, constructed with particular choices of
G
and
\widetilde{K}
. We prove that Theorems
14
and
18
as well as their corollaries can be applied in the study of these problems, in order to obtain various convergence results.

Keywords
: heat transfer problem, unilateral constraint, subdifferential boundary condition, hemivariational inequality, optimal control, Tykhonov well-posedness, approximating sequence, convergence results.

The main task in the analysis and control of inequality problems is to provide their well-posedness. The concepts of well-posedness vary from problem to problem and from author to author. A few examples are the concept of well-posedness in the sense of Hadamard for partial differential equations, the concept of well-posedness in the sense of Tykhonov for a minimization problem, the concept of well-posedness in the sense of Levitin-Polyak for a constrainted optimization problem, among others.
The concept of Tykhonov well-posedness (well-posedness, for short)

Moreover, the Tykhonov well-posedness of an antiplane shear problem was studied in our recent paper

In this paper we consider a stationary boundary value problem
which describes the heat transfer in a domain
D\subset{{\rm I}\mkern-3.0mu{\rm R}}^{d}

[

4

As a consequence, the weak formulation of the problem leads to an elliptic hemivariational inequality, denoted by
{\cal P}

{\cal Q}

Our aim in this paper is twofold. The first one is to study the well-posedness of the hemivariational inequality
{\cal P}
. The second one is to study the well-posedness of the associated optimal control problem
{\cal Q}
. To this end, for both problems, we use specific
Tykhonov triples, constructed with approximating sets
govened by a penalty-type operator and a set of constraints. Proving the well-posedness of the corresponding hemivariational inequality and the associated optimal control problem in this functional setting is non standard and represents the main trait of novelty of our paper. Moreover, the well-posedness of these problems implies general convergence results which allows us to deduce the weak-strong dependence of the solution to Problem
{\cal P}
with respect to the data, its approch by a penalty-like method as well as the weak compactness of the set of solution to Problem
{\cal Q}

represent a continuation of our previous results obtained in
[

4

We start with some notation and preliminaries on hemivariational inequalities and send the reader to

X

.

For real valued functions defined on
X
we recall the following definitions.

Definition 1

A function
j\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be

locally Lipschitz
,
if for every

x\in X
, there exists
U_{x}
a neighborhood of
x
and a constant
L_{x}>0

such that
|j(y)-j(z)|\leq L_{x}\|y-z\|_{X}
for all
y
,
z\in U_{x}
. For such functions the

generalized (Clarke) directional derivative
of
j
at the point

x\in X
in the direction
v\in X
is defined
by

j^{0}(x;v)=\limsup_{y\to x,\ \lambda\downarrow 0}\frac{j(y+\lambda v)-j(y)}{\lambda}.

The
generalized gradient (subdifferential)
of
j
at
x

is a subset of the dual space
X^{*}
given by

\partial j(x)=\{\,\zeta\in X^{*}\mid j^{0}(x;v)\geq{\langle\zeta,v\rangle}\quad\forall\,v\in X\,\}.

The function
j
is said to be
regular
(in the sense
of Clarke) at the point
x\in X
if for all
v\in X
the one-sided directional
derivative
j^{\prime}(x;v)
exists and
j^{0}(x;v)=j^{\prime}(x;v)
.

We shall use the following properties of the generalized directional derivative and the generalized gradient.

Proposition 2

Assume that
j\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is a locally Lipschitz function. Then the following hold:

(a)

For every
x\in X
, the function

X\ni v\mapsto j^{0}(x;v)\in\mathbb{R}
is positively homogeneous,
i.e.,
j^{0}(x;\lambda v)=\lambda j^{0}(x;v)
for all

\lambda\geq 0
,
v\in X
.

(b)

For every
v\in X
, we have

j^{0}(x;v)=\max\,\{\,\langle\xi,v\rangle\ :\ \xi\in\partial j(x)\,\}
.

We shall use these definitions and properties both in the case when
X={{\rm I}\mkern-3.0mu{\rm R}}
and
X=V
where
V
is a Sobolev-type space which will defined in Section
3
. Next, we proceed with the definition of some classes of nonlinear operators.

Definition 3

An operator
A\colon X\to X^{*}
is said to be:

a) monotone,
if for all

,
v\in X
, we have
\langle Au-Av,u-v\rangle\geq 0
;

b) strongly monotone, if there exists

such that

\langle Au-Av,u-v\rangle\geq m_{A}\|u-v\|_{X}^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\ u,\,v\in X;

c) bounded, if

maps bounded sets of
X

into bounded sets of
X^{*}
;

d) pseudomonotone,
if it is bounded and
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
X

\displaystyle\limsup\,\langle Au_{n},u_{n}-u\rangle\leq 0

imply
\displaystyle\liminf\,\langle Au_{n},u_{n}-v\rangle\geq\langle Au,u-v\rangle

e) demicontinuous,

We shall use the following result related to the pseudomonotonicity of operators.

Proposition 4

Assume that the operator

is bounded, demicontinuous and monotone. Then

is pseudomonotone.

We end this section by recalling some preliminaries concerning the concept of well-posedness in the sense of Tykhonov. For more details in the matter we send the reader to
[
41
]
.

Assume that
{\cal M}
is an abstract mathematic object called generic “problem”, associated to a metric space
X
. Problem
{\cal M}
could be an equation, a minimization problem, a fixed point problem, an optimal control problem, an inclusion or an inequality problem. Its rigorous statement vary from exemple to example. We associate to
{\cal M}
the concept of “solution” which follows from the context and which
will be clearly defined in each exemple we present below in this paper.
We now introduce the following definitions.

Definition 6

A Tykhonov triple for Problem
{\cal M}
is a mathematical object of the form
\mbox{{${\cal T}$}}=(I,\Omega,\mbox{{${\cal C}$}})
where
I
is a given nonempty set,
\Omega:I\to 2^{X}-\{\emptyset\}
and
\mbox{{${\cal C}$}}\subset\mbox{{${\cal S}$}}(I)
,
\mbox{{${\cal C}$}}\neq\emptyset
.

Note that in this definition and below in this paper
\mbox{{${\cal S}$}}(I)
represents the set of sequences of elements of
I
and
2^{X}
denotes the set of parts of the space
X
. A typical element of
I
will be denoted by
\theta
and a typical element of
\mbox{{${\cal S}$}}(I)
will be denoted by
\{\theta_{n}\}
. We refer to the set
I
as the set of indices. Moreover, for any
\theta\in I
we refer to the set
\Omega(\theta)\subset X
as an approximating set and
{\cal C}
will represent the so-called convergence criterion.

Definition 7

Given a Tykhonov triple
\mbox{{${\cal T}$}}=(I,\Omega,\mbox{{${\cal C}$}})
, a sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset X
is called an approximating sequence if there exists a sequence
\{\theta_{n}\}\subset\mbox{{${\cal C}$}}
such that
u_{n}\in\Omega(\theta_{n})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Note that approximating sequences always exist since, by assumption,
\mbox{{${\cal C}$}}\neq\emptyset
and, moreover, for
any sequence
\{\theta_{n}\}\subset\mbox{{${\cal C}$}}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\Omega(\theta_{n})
is not empty. Therefore, Definition
7
above make sense.

Definition 8

Given a Tykhonov triple
\mbox{{${\cal T}$}}=(I,\Omega,\mbox{{${\cal C}$}})
, Problem
{\cal M}
is said to be :

a) (strongly) well-posedness if it has a unique solution and every approximating sequence converges in
X
to this solution;

b) weakly well-posedness if it has a unique solution and every approximating sequence converges weakly in
X
to this solution;

c) weakly generalized well-posedness if it has at least one solution and every approximating sequence contains a subsequence which converges weakly in
X
to some point of the solution set.

We remark that the concept of approximating sequence above depends on the Tykhonov triple
{\cal T}
and, for this reason, we sometimes refer to approximating sequences with respect
{\cal T}
. As a consequence, the concepts of strongly, weakly and weakly generalized well-posedness depend on the Tykhonov triple
{\cal T}
and, therefore, we refer to them as strongly, weakly and weakly generalized well-posedness with respect
{\cal T}
, respectively.

The heat transfer problem

In this section we introduce the heat transfer problem, list the assumptions on the data, derive its variational formulation and state our optimal control problem. The problem under consideration is the following.

Problem

Find a temperature field

\displaystyle u\geq 0,\qquad-\Delta u-f\geq 0,\qquad u(\Delta u+f)=0\qquad{\rm a.e.\ in\ }D,

(3.1)

\displaystyle u=0\hskip 68.2866pt{\rm a.e.\ on\ }\Gamma_{1},

(3.2)

\displaystyle u=b\hskip 68.2866pt{\rm a.e.\ on\ }\Gamma_{2},

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\in\partial j_{\nu}(u)\hskip 22.76219pt{\rm a.e.\ on\ }\Gamma_{3}.

(3.4)

In (

)–(
3.4

(

on the space
X=V
with
\widetilde{c}_{0}=\sqrt{2\,{\it meas}(\Gamma_{3})}\,{\overline{c}}_{0}c_{3}
,
\widetilde{c}_{1}=\sqrt{2}\,{\overline{c}}_{1}c_{3}^{2}
and
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j_{\nu}}c_{3}^{2}
. In addition,

j^{0}(u;v)=\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{3}}j_{\nu}^{0}(u,v)\,da\qquad\forall\,u,\ v\in V.

(3.26)

Next, we turn to the variational formulation of Problem
{\cal H}
. To this end we assume that
u
is a regular function which satisfies (
3.1
)–(
3.4
) and we consider an arbitrary element
v\in K
. Then, using an integration by parts if follows that

\int_{D}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(v-u)\,dx+\int_{D}\Delta u\cdot(v-u)\,dx=\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,(v-u)\,da.

Therefore, by (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) we find that

\int_{D}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(v-u)\,dx\geq\int_{D}f(v-u)\,dx+\int_{\Gamma_{3}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,(v-u)\,da

(3.27)

and, using the boundary condition (
3.4
) combined with definition of the Clarke subdifferential, we obtain that

\int_{\Gamma_{3}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\,(v-u)\,da\geq-\int_{\Gamma_{3}}j^{0}_{\nu}(u;v-u)\,da.

(3.28)

We now combine inequalities (
3.27
) and (
3.28
), then use notation (
3.24
) and equality (
3.26
) to see that

a(u,v-u)+j^{0}(u;v-u)\geq(f,v-u)_{L^{2}(D)}.

Finally, using this inequality and the regularity
u\in K
, guaranteed by (
3.1
)–(
3.3
), we deduce the following variational formulation of Problem
{\cal H}
.

Problem

{\cal P}
.
Find
u\in V
such that

u\in K,\quad a(u,v-u)+j^{0}(u;v-u)\geq(f,v-u)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall\,v\in K.

(3.29)

We refer to inequality (
3.29
) as a hemivariational inequality with unilateral constraint.
A function
u\in V
which satisfies (
3.29
)
and is called a
weak solution
to the heat transfer problem
{\cal H}
.

We now introduce the set of admissible pairs for inequality Problem
{\cal P}
defined by

{\cal V}_{ad}=\{\,(u,f)\in K\times L^{2}(D)\ \mbox{such that}\ (\ref{hv})\ \mbox{holds}\,\}.

(3.30)

We also assume that
\mbox{{${\cal L}$}}:V\times L^{2}(D)\to{{\rm I}\mkern-3.0mu{\rm R}}
is a given cost functional which satisfies the following conditions.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{For all sequences }\{u_{n}\}\subset V\mbox{ and
}\{f_{n}\}\subset L^{2}(D)\mbox{ such that}\\[5.69054pt]
u_{n}\rightarrow u\mbox{\ \ in\ \ }V,\ \ f_{n}\rightharpoonup f\mbox{\ \ in\ \ }L^{2}(D),\ \mbox{we have}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\,{\cal L}(u_{n},f_{n})\geq{\cal L}(u,f).\par\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{ There exists}\ h:L^{2}(D)\to{{\rm I}\mkern-3.0mu{\rm R}}\ \mbox{such that}\\[5.69054pt]
{\rm(a)}\ \ {\cal L}(u,f)\geq h(f)\quad\forall\,u\in V,\ f\in L^{2}(D).\\[5.69054pt]
{\rm(b)}\ \ \|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\to+\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ h(f_{n})\to\infty.\end{array}\right.

Example 12

A typical example of function
{\cal L}
which satisfies conditions
(\ref{L1})
and
(\ref{L2})
is obtained by taking

{\cal L}(u,f)=g(u)+h(f)\qquad\forall\,u\in V,\ f\in L^{2}(D),

where

is a continuous positive function and

is a weakly lower semicontinuous coercive function, i.e., it satisfies condition

.

We also assume that
\mbox{{${\cal L}$}}:V\times L^{2}(D)\to{{\rm I}\mkern-3.0mu{\rm R}}
is a
given cost function and we associate to Problem
{\cal P}
the following optimal control problem.

Problem

{\cal Q}
.
Find
(u^{*},f^{*})\in{\cal V}_{ad}
such that

\mbox{{${\cal L}$}}(u^{*},f^{*})=\min_{(u,f)\in{\cal V}_{ad}}\mbox{{${\cal L}$}}(u,f).

(3.39)

Under the previous assumptions, the unique solvability of Problem
{\cal P}
as well as the solvability of Problem

37
]
and
[

]
, respectively.
Our aim in what follows is to study the well-posedness of these problems and to derive some consequences. To this end, as already mentionned in Section

4
A well-posedness result

This section is devoted to the well-posedness of the Problem
{\cal P}
, in the sense precised in Section
2
, with
X=V

\widetilde{K}
, a penalty operator

such that the following conditions hold.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{\rm(a)}\quad\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}}\ \mbox{\rm is a closed convex subset of}\ V.\\[5.69054pt]
\mbox{\rm(b)}\quad K\subset\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mbox{\rm(a)}\quad G:V\to V^{*}\ \ \mbox{is a

We start with the following result.

Proposition 13

Assume that
(\ref{b})
–
(\ref{sm})
,
(\ref{wK})
and

hold. Then for each
\lambda>0
and

(4.8)

Proof.
Define the operator

A:V\to V^{*}
by equality

\langle Au,v\rangle=a(u,v)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\langle Gu,v\rangle\qquad\forall\,u,\ v\in V.

Then, using the definition (
3.24
) of the form

, inequality (
3.6
) and the properties (
4
)(a) of the operator
G
,
it is easy to see that the operator

is bounded, demicontinuous and, moreover,

\langle Au-Av,u-v\rangle\geq\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}\,\|u-v\|_{V}^{2}\qquad\forall\,u,\,v\in V.

(4.9)

Therefore, it follows from Proposition
4
that

is pseudomonotone. In addition, inequality (
4.9
) shows that

is strongly monotone with constant
m_{A}=\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}
. On the other hand,
the functional

is linear
and continuous on

and, therefore, it defines an element in
V^{*}
. We note that assumption (
3

K\neq\emptyset
, hence inclusion (
4
)(b) guarantees that
\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover,
recall assumption
(
4

11
and the smallness assumption (
3.22
). All these ingradients allow us to apply Theorem
5
in order to deduce the unique solvabilty of inequality (

), which concludes the proof.

\Box

We now take
\mbox{{${\cal T}$}}=(I,\Omega,\mbox{{${\cal C}$}})

where

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98857ptI=\{\,\theta=(\lambda,{f})\ :\ \lambda>0,\ {f}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\,\},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98857pt\Omega(\theta)=\{\,u\in\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}}\ {\rm such\ that}\ (\ref{hvp})\ {\rm holds}\,\}\qquad\forall\,\theta=(\lambda,f)\in I,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98857pt\mbox{{${\cal C}$}}=\{\,\{\theta_{n}\}\ :\ \theta_{n}=(\lambda_{n},f_{n})\in I\ \ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\ \ \lambda_{n}\to 0,\ \ f_{n}\rightharpoonup f\ \ {\rm in}\ L^{2}(D)\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty\,\}.

Then, using Proposition

Our main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 14

Assume that
(\ref{f})
–
(\ref{sm})
,
(\ref{wK})
and

with respect to the Tykhonov triple

Proof.
Following Definition
8
a), the proof is carried out in two main steps.

i)
Unique solvability of Problem
{\cal P}
.

Note that assumptions (
4

4

and

for all

, respectively. Moreover, with this particular choice

), for any

to Problem

is a direct consequence of Proposition

ii)
Convergence of approximating sequences.

To proceed, we
consider an approximating sequence for the Problem
{\cal P}
, denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
. Then, according to Definition

it follows that
there exists a sequence
\{\theta_{n}\}
of elements of

, with
\theta_{n}=(\lambda_{n},f_{n})
, such that
u_{n}\in\Omega(\theta_{n})

for each

and, moreover,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\to 0,

(4.13)

\displaystyle f_{n}\rightharpoonup f\quad{\rm in}\quad L^{2}(D).

(4.14)

Note that the inclusion
u_{n}\in\Omega(\theta_{n})
combined with definition (
4.11
) imples that
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle u_{n}\in\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}},\quad a(u_{n},v-u_{n})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\,\langle Gu_{n},v-u_{n}\rangle+j^{0}(u_{n},;v-u_{n})

(4.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq({f}_{n},v-u_{n})_{L^{2}(D)}\quad\forall\,v\in\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}}.

Our aim in what follows is to
prove the convergence

u_{n}\to u\quad{\rm in}\quad V,\quad{\rm as}\quad n\to\infty.

(4.16)

To this end we proceed in three intermediate steps that we present below.

ii-a)
A first weak convergence result
. We claim that there is an element
{\widetilde{u}}\in\mbox{{$\widetilde{K}$}}
and

\{u_{n}\}
,

To prove the claim, we establish the boundedness of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
in

. Let

and let
u
be the solution of Problem
{\cal P}
. We use assumption (
4
)(b)
and take

in (

\displaystyle a(u_{n},u_{n}-u)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\,\langle Gu_{n},u-u_{n}\rangle+j^{0}(u_{n},u-u_{n})+(f_{n},u_{n}-u)_{L^{2}(D)}.

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}\,\|u_{n}-u\|_{V}^{2}\leq a(u,u-u_{n})+j^{0}(u_{n};u-u_{n})+(f_{n},u_{n}-u)_{L^{2}(D)}.

On the other hand, by Proposition
2

\displaystyle j^{0}(u_{n};u-u_{n})=j^{0}(u_{n};u-u_{n})+j^{0}(u;u_{n}-u)-j^{0}(u;u_{n}-u)

Finally, note that

a(u,u-u_{n})+(f_{n},u_{n}-u)_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\big(\|u\|_{V}+\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\big)\|u_{n}-u\|_{V}.

We now combine inequalities (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}\,\|u_{n}-u\|_{V}^{2}\leq\big(\|u\|_{V}+\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(D)}\big)\big)\|u_{n}-u\|_{V}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+\alpha_{j_{\nu}}c_{3}^{2}\|u_{n}-u\|_{V}^{2}+(\widetilde{c}_{0}+\widetilde{c}_{1}\|u\|_{V})\|u_{n}-u\|_{V}.

Note that by (

. Therefore, using inequality (

u_{n}\rightharpoonup{\widetilde{u}}\ \ {\rm in}\ \ V,\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ n\to\infty,

with some

. Moreover, assumption (

ii-b)
A property of the weak limit.

Next, we show that

Let

be a given element in

and let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\,\langle Gu_{n},u_{n}-v\rangle\leq a(u_{n},v-u_{n})+j^{0}(u_{n},v-u_{n})+(f_{n},u_{n}-v)_{L^{2}(D)}.

Then, using arguments similar to those used in the proof of (

We now take

Problem

\displaystyle\Delta u+f=\frac{1}{\lambda}\,p_{0}(u)\qquad{\rm a.e.\ in\ }D,

\displaystyle u=0\hskip 85.35826pt{\rm a.e.\ on\ }\Gamma_{1},

(6.2)

\displaystyle u=b\hskip 85.35826pt{\rm a.e.\ on\ }\Gamma_{2},

(6.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\in\partial j_{\nu}(u)\hskip 39.83385pt{\rm a.e.\ on\ }\Gamma_{3}.

(6.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{0}\colon D\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\ \mbox{is such that}\\[2.84526pt]

{\rm(b)\ }(p_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},r)-p_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},s))\,(r-s)\geq 0\\
\qquad\quad\mbox{for all}\ \ r,\,s\in\mathbb{R},\ {\rm a.e.\ }\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}}\in D,\\[2.84526pt]
{\rm(c)}\ \mbox{\boldmath{$x$}}\mapsto p_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},r){\rm\ is\ measurable\ on}\ D\ {\rm for\ all\ }r\in\mathbb{R},\\[2.84526pt]
{\rm(d)}\ p_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath{$x$}},r)=0\ \ \mbox{if and only if}\ \ r\geq 0,\ {\rm a.e.}\ \mbox{\boldmath{$x$}}\in D.\end{array}\right.

Revisiting Co-Occurring Directions: Sharper Analysis and Efficient Algorithm for Sparse Matrices
Luo Luo, Cheng Chen, Guangzeng Xie, Haishan Ye

Abstract
We study the streaming model for approximate matrix multiplication (AMM). We are interested in the scenario that the algorithm can only take one pass over the data with limited memory. The state-of-the-art deterministic sketching algorithm for streaming AMM is the co-occurring directions (COD), which has much smaller approximation errors than randomized algorithms and outperforms other deterministic sketching methods empirically. In this paper, we provide a tighter error bound for COD whose leading term considers the potential approximate low-rank structure and the correlation of input matrices. We prove COD is space optimal with respect to our improved error bound. We also propose a variant of COD for sparse matrices with theoretical guarantees. The experiments on real-world sparse datasets show that the proposed algorithm is more efficient than baseline methods.



1
Introduction

A large scale machine learning system usually receives data sequentially and it is often impossible to exactly store the entire data set. Thus, the approximate matrix multiplication (AMM) in the streaming fashion is an important and fundamental task for scientific computation and big data analysis. For example, the product of matrices from multi-modal datasets captures the correlation between different modalities. In addition, many classical algorithms including canonical correlation analysis
(
Hotelling 1992
)
,
generalized eigenvector decomposition
(
Golub and Loan 1996
)
,
partial least squares
(
Wegelin 2000
)
, spectral co-clustering
(
Dhillon 2001
)
require to perform approximate matrix multiplication when the data set is very large.
On the other hand, data matrices from real-world are usually low-rank and sparse, which motivated us to design efficient and effective sparse algorithms.

This paper considers streaming AMM problem as follows. Give two large matrices
{\bf X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{x}}
and
{\bf Y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{y}}
, we are interested in finding a low-rank estimator
{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}
to approximate
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
, where
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d_{x}}
,
{\bf B}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d_{y}}
and
m
is much smaller than
n
,
d_{x}
and
d_{y}
.
We focus on the row update model, that is, the algorithm receives rows of
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
sequentially and it only takes one pass over input matrices with limited memory.
The key challenge for this problem is to reduce the space/time complexity while maintaining the approximation error.

Inspired by the idea of finding frequent items
(
Misra and Gries 1982
)
,
Liberty 2013
proposed frequent directions algorithm (FD), which considers the symmetric case of AMM such that
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
(a.k.a., the covariance sketching).
FD achieves optimal tradeoffs between space cost and approximation error
(
Woodruff 2014a
;
Ghashami et al. 2016
;
Ghashami and Phillips 2014
)
.
Moreover, we can combine FD with subspace power iteration
(
Woodruff 2014b
;
Musco and Musco 2015
)
to design an algorithm which is efficient for sparse matrix multiplication
(
Ghashami, Liberty, and Phillips 2016
)
, called sparse frequent directions (SFD).
Recently,
Huang 2019
integrated random sampling
(
Drineas, Kannan, and Mahoney 2006
)
into FD to reduce its time complexity.
Luo et al. 2019
introduced a regularization term for FD, which makes the estimator is more friendly to inverse operation. FD technique can also be used to accelerate many popular machine learning models, such as convex online optimization
(
Luo et al. 2016
;
Luo et al. 2019
)
, factorization machine
(
Luo et al. 2018
)
, linear contextual bandits
(
Kuzborskij, Cella, and Cesa-Bianchi 2019
;
Chen et al. 2020
)
and ridge regression
(
Shi and Phillips 2020
;
Dickens 2020
)
.

Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
proposed a variant of FD called co-occurring directions (COD) for streaming AMM. COD shrinks the singular values of input matrices
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
simultaneously at each iteration. It is shown that COD has significantly better performance than other sketching algorithms
(
Ye, Luo, and Zhang 2016
;
Drineas, Kannan, and Mahoney 2006
;
Clarkson and Woodruff 2017
;
Sarlos 2006
)
on AMM problem empirically.
However, the existing spectral error bound of COD can not completely explain its high performance. It depends on the Frobenius norm of
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
, which ignores the potential low-rank structure of the data matrix. Specifically, in the case of
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
, the procedure of COD degrades to FD, but its error bound is worse than that of FD.
Another deterministic sketching method for AMM, which we call FD-AMM
(
Ye, Luo, and Zhang 2016
)
, directly adopts FD to sketch the concatenated matrix
{\bf Z}=[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]
. The output of the algorithm is an approximation of
{\bf Z}^{\top}{\bf Z}
, whose sub-matrix corresponds to an estimator of
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
.

In this paper, we provide a sharper analysis for co-occurring directions (COD). We give a new spectral norm error bound which considers the potential low-rank structure of the target matrix. Our bound could be much tighter than
Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
’s (
Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
) results when the spectrum of the exact matrix product is dominated by its top singular values. In addition, we prove that the space complexity of COD is optimal to attain our improved error bound. Furthermore, in the case of
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
, our result matches the error bound of FD.

We further propose sparse co-occurring directions (SCOD) and provide an error bound matches our improved analysis on standard COD while the running time of the algorithm mainly depends on the non-zero entries of input matrices. We conduct numerical experiments on cross-language datasets to show that SCOD has better performance than state-of-the-art algorithms empirically. Concurrent to our work,
Wan and Zhang 2020
have also proposed a similar COD based algorithm to address streaming AMM with sparse inputs but their error bound does not consider the potential low-rankness.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, define the notation used in this paper and introduce the
background of related algorithms for streaming AMM.
In Section
3
, we provide our new error bound for COD algorithm and show the corresponding space lower bound.
In Section
5
, we propose SCOD and give its theoretical guarantees.
In Section
6
, we conduct the numerical experiments to show the superiority of SCOD. We defer detailed proof of some lemmas and theorems into supplementary materials.
We conclude our work in Section
7
.

Notations and Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce the notation will be used in this paper. Then we give the backgrounds of frequent directions and related algorithms for AMM.

2.1
Notations

We let
{\bf I}_{p}
be the
p\times p
identity matrix and
{\bf 0}_{p\times q}
be the
p\times q
matrix of all zeros. For an
p\times q
matrix
{\bf A}=[A_{ij}]
, we denote
({\bf{a}}^{(i)})^{\top}
be its
i
-th row,
{\rm nnz}({\bf A})
be the number of non-zero entries of
{\bf A}
. The condensed singular value decomposition (SVD) of
{\bf A}
is defined as
{\bf U}{\bf{\Sigma}}{\bf V}^{\top}
where
{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times r}
and
{\bf V}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times r}
are column orthogonal,
{\bf{\Sigma}}=\diag(\sigma_{1}({\bf A}),\sigma_{2}({\bf A}),\dots,\sigma_{r}({\bf A}))
with
\sigma_{1}({\bf A})\geq\sigma_{2}({\bf A})\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{r}({\bf A})>0
places the nonzero singular values on its diagonal entries and
r
is the rank of
{\bf A}
.
We have
\sigma_{i}({\bf A})=0
for any
i>r
.
Additionally, we let
\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}^{2}({\bf A})}

be the Frobenius norm,
\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{2}=\sigma_{1}({\bf A})
be the spectral norm,
\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{*}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}({\bf A})
be the nuclear norm and
\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}({\bf A})
be the Ky Fan
k
-norm. We also denote
{\bf A}_{k}
as the best rank-
k
approximation to
{\bf A}
for any unitary invariant norms, that is,
{\bf A}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}({\bf A}){\bf{u}}_{i}{\bf{v}}_{i}^{\top}
, where
{\bf{u}}_{i}
and
{\bf{v}}_{i}
are the
i
-th column of
{\bf U}
and
{\bf V}
respectively.

2.2
Frequent Directions

Frequent directions
(
Liberty 2013
;
Ghashami et al. 2016
)
is a deterministic algorithm for covariance sketching. Given any matrix
{\bf X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
and sketch size
m
that is much smaller than
n
and
d
, FD processes the rows of
{\bf X}
one by one and produces a sketch matrix
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2m\times d}
to approximate
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf X}
by
{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A}
. We present the details of FD in Algorithm
1
, which requires
{\mathcal{O}}(md)
space and
{\mathcal{O}}(mnd)
time complexity. The algorithm has the following theoretical guarantees.

Lemma 1

(
Ghashami and Phillips 2014
;
Ghashami et al. 2016
)
.

The output
{\bf A}
of Algorithm
1
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf X}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{m-k}\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|{\bf X}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)

(1)

for any
k<m
.

Ghashami et al. 2016
also prove FD is space optimal with respect to the guaranteed accuracy in Lemma
1
.
Note that the shrinking step in line
8
of the algorithm is necessary because the output could be extremely worse without this operation
(
Desai, Ghashami, and Phillips 2016
;
Luo et al. 2019
)
.

2.3
Sketching Algorithms for AMM

It is natural to exploit the idea of FD to solve general AMM problem
(
Ye, Luo, and Zhang 2016
)
. We can concatenates the input matrix
{\bf X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{x}}
and
{\bf Y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{y}}
to construct a larger matrix
{\bf Z}=[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(d_{x}+d_{y})}
, and then apply FD on
{\bf Z}
to approximate
{\bf Z}^{\top}{\bf Z}
by
{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf C}
, where
{\bf C}=[{\bf A},{\bf B}]
,
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{x}}
and
{\bf B}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{y}}
.
The top right sub-matrix of the
{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf C}
, i.e.,the matrix
{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}
is an approximation of
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
. Intuitively, this algorithm wastes a large proportion of cost to approximate
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}^{\top}{\bf Y}
(the other sub-matrices of
{\bf Z}^{\top}{\bf Z}
), which is unnecessary for the AMM task.

Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
proposed the
co-occurring directions (COD) for AMM. We present its detailed procedure in Algorithm
2
. Each iteration of COD constructs the column basis of
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
by QR factorization independently and executes the shrinkage step on the small interaction matrix
{\bf R}_{x}{\bf R}_{y}^{\top}
.
We point out that both COD and FD-AMM requires
{\mathcal{O}}(m(d_{x}+d_{y}))
space and
{\mathcal{O}}(mn(d_{x}+d_{y}))
time complexity.
However, COD looks more reasonable than FD-AMM since all of its operations surrounds approximating
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
. The numerical experiments
(
Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
)
show that COD performs significantly better than FD-AMM
(
Ye, Luo, and Zhang 2016
)
and other AMM algorithms
(
Drineas, Kannan, and Mahoney 2006
;
Clarkson and Woodruff 2017
;
Sarlos 2006
)
when input matrices is dense.
We can prove that COD holds the guaranteed accuracy as follows.

Lemma 2

(
Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
)
.

The output
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
of Algorithm
2
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}}{m}.

(2)

Unfortunately, the result of Lemma
2
does not reveal the advantage of COD entirely. Consider that case of
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
, the procedure of COD will reduce to FD, but the error bound of (
2
) becomes a special case of (
1
) in Lemma
1
with
k=0
. The real-world dataset typically enjoys some approximate low-rank structure, which leads to the right-hand side of bound (
1
) could be much
smaller than the one of (
2
). Hence although COD has better empirical performance, the existing error bounds are not tight enough.

3
Sharper Analysis for COD

In this section, we provide a tighter error bound for COD. We let
\delta^{(t)}
be the value of
\delta
at time step
t
. If
the algorithm does not enter the “then” section in the
t
-th step, then we have
\delta^{(t)}=0
. Similarly, let
{\bf A}^{(t)}
,
{\bf B}^{(t)}
,
{\bf Q}_{x}^{(t)}
,
{\bf Q}_{y}^{(t)}
,
{\bf U}^{(t)}
,
{\bf{\Sigma}}^{(t)}
,
{\bf V}^{(t)}
and
\widehat{{\bf{\Sigma}}}^{(t)}
be the corresponding variables after the main loop has been executed for
t
times. Additionally, we use
{\widehat{\bf A}}^{(t)}
and
{\widehat{\bf B}}^{(t)}
to represent the matrices after insert operations (line
5
-
6
) have been executed at the
t
-th iteration. We need the following two lemmas for proving our main results.

Lemma 3

(
Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
)
.

The output matrices
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
of Algorithm
2
satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\delta^{(t)}

(3)

and

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\big\|_{*}\leq\big\|{\bf X}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\bf Y}\big\|_{F}-m\sum_{t=1}^{n}\delta^{(t)}.

(4)

Lemma 4
.

The output of Algorithm
2
holds that

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{*}-\big\|{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\big\|_{*}\leq\sum_{i=k+1}^{d}\sigma_{i}({\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y})+k\sum_{t=1}^{n}\delta^{(t)}.

(5)

Lemma
4
is the key lemma of our proof. It improves the result in analysis of COD
(
Mroueh, Marcheret, and Goel 2017
)
. The term
\sum_{i=k+1}^{d}\sigma_{i}({\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y})
on the right-hand side of (
5
) considers the potential approximate low-rank structure of
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
, which leads to a tighter error bound of COD as follows.

Theorem 1
.

The output of Algorithm
2
holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{m-k}\Big(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\Big).

for any
k<m
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\delta^{(t)}
.
Connecting inequality (
4
) in Lemma
3
and the result of Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle m\Delta+\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\right\|_{*}-\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\right\|_{*}-\left\|{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{*}\leq\sum_{i=k+1}^{d}\sigma_{i}({\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y})+k\Delta,

that is

\Delta\leq\frac{1}{m-k}\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\right\|_{k}\right)
.
Substituting above bound of
\Delta
into inequality (
4
) of Lemma
3
,
we finish the proof of this theorem.
∎

To achieve the accuracy that
\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\leq{\varepsilon}
, the previous error bound (Lemma
2
) requires the sketch size to be at least
m_{1}=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}
,
while Theorem
1
only requires the sketch size

m_{2}=k+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\right\|_{k}\right)
.
When input matrices
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
have strongly correlation and approximate low-rank structure,
m_{2}
could be much smaller than
m_{1}
.

In addition, the error bound of Theorem
1
matches that of FD (Lemma
1
) when
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
:

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf X}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{m-k}\big(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}({\bf X}^{\top}{\bf X})\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m-k}\big(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}({\bf X})\big)=\frac{1}{m-k}\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|{\bf X}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)\!.

On the other hand, the previous error bound (Lemma
2
) is worse than that of FD (Lemma
1
) in the symmetric case of
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
.

4
Space Lower Bounds Analysis

In this section, we show that COD is space optimal with respect to our new error bound in Theorem
1
. We first introduce the following lemma for low-rank matrices.

Lemma 5

(
Kapralov and Talwar 2013
)
.

For each
\delta>0
there exits a set of matrices
{\mathcal{Q}}=\{{\bf Q}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Q}_{N}\}
and
N=2^{\Omega(\ell(d-\ell)\log(1/\delta))}
, where
{\bf Q}_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell\times d}
with
{\bf Q}_{i}{\bf Q}_{i}^{\top}={\bf I}_{\ell}
, such that
\left\|{\bf Q}_{i}{\bf Q}_{j}^{\top}\right\|_{2}<1-\delta
.

By using Lemma
5
, we can construct a sets contains exponential number of matrices that each pair of them are not “too close”. The formalized result is shown in Lemma
6
.

Lemma 6
.

For each
\delta>0
and
d_{x}\leq d_{y}
there exits a set of matrices
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\ell}=\{({\widehat{\bf X}}^{(1)},{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(1)}),\cdots,({\widehat{\bf X}}^{(N)},{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(N)})\}
, where
N=2^{\Omega(\ell(d_{y}-\ell)\log(1/\delta))}
and
{\widehat{\bf X}}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell\times d_{x}},{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell\times d_{y}}
satisfy
{\widehat{\bf X}}^{(i)}{\widehat{\bf X}}^{{(i)}\top}={\bf I}_{\ell}~\text{and}~{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(i)}\top}={\bf I}_{\ell}

for any
i=1,\dots,n
and

\displaystyle\big\|{\widehat{\bf X}}^{{(i)}\top}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(i)}\top}-{\widehat{\bf X}}^{{(j)}\top}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(j)}\top}\big\|_{2}>\sqrt{2\delta}

for any
j\neq i
.

Proof.

Based on Lemma
5
, there exist a set of matrices

{\mathcal{Y}}=\{{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(1)},\cdots,{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(N)}\}
,
where
N=2^{\Omega(\ell(d-\ell)\log(1/\delta))}
; and
{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\ell\times d}
satisfies
{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(i)}\top}={\bf I}_{\ell}
and
\big\|{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(j)}\top}\big\|<1-\delta
.
We further set
{\widehat{\bf X}}^{(i)}=[{\bf I}_{\ell},{\bf 0}_{\ell\times(d_{x}-\ell)}]
.
We have

\displaystyle\big\|{\widehat{\bf X}}^{{(i)}\top}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}-{\widehat{\bf X}}^{{(j)}\top}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(j)}\big\|_{2}^{2}=\big\|{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}-{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(j)}\big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|({\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}-{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(j)})({\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(i)}\top}-{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(j)}\top})\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2-\big\|{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(j)}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(i)}\top}+{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{{(j)}\top}\big\|_{2}\geq 2\delta,

where we use the definition of
{\widehat{\bf X}}^{(i)}
,
{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)}
and the fact
\left\|{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf A}\right\|_{2}=\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{2}^{2}
.
∎

Then we present a lower bound of space complexity for approximate matrix multiplication, which matches the memory cost of COD. Hence, we can conclude that COD is space optimal with respect to the guaranteed accuracy in Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2
.

We consider any matrix sketching algorithm with inputs as
{\bf X}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{x}}
and
{\bf Y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{y}}
and outputs
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d_{x}}
and
{\bf B}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d_{y}}
with guarantee

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{m-k}\Big(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\Big)

for any
k<m
. Assuming that a constant number of bits is required to describe a word (i.e., a unit of memory), then the algorithm requires at least
\Omega(m(d_{x}+d_{y}))
bits of space.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
d_{y}\geq d_{x}
.
Let
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\ell}=\{({\widehat{\bf X}}^{(1)},{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(1)}),\cdots,({\widehat{\bf X}}^{(N)},{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(N)})\}
be the set of matrices defined in Lemma
6
with
\ell=m/4
,
\delta=1/8
and
N=2^{\Omega(\frac{m}{4}\cdot(d_{y}-m/4)\log(8))}
.
We construct matrices

{\bf X}^{(i)}=[{\widehat{\bf X}}^{(i)};{\bf 0}_{(n-m/4)\times d_{x}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{x}}

and

{\bf Y}^{(i)}=[{\widehat{\bf Y}}^{(i)};{\bf 0}_{(n-m/4)\times d_{y}}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{y}}

for
i=1,\dots,N
.
Then we have
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\ell}=\{({\bf X}^{(i)},{\bf Y}^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^{N}
which satisfies

\left\|{\bf X}^{{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{(i)}-{\bf X}^{{(j)}\top}{\bf Y}^{(j)}\right\|_{2}>1/2

for each
i\neq j
.
Let
({\bf A},{\bf B})
be the output of the matrix sketching algorithm with input
({\bf X}^{(i)},{\bf Y}^{(i)})
.
The guarantee of the algorithm indicates

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf X}^{{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{(i)}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\big\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{m-k}\left(\frac{m}{4}-k\right)\leq\frac{1}{4}.

Hence, each
({\bf A},{\bf B})
only encodes one matrix pencil in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\ell}
(the product of the matrices), which means that the lower bound of space complexity to attach the desired accuracy is

\log_{2}N=\Omega(md_{y})=\Omega(m(d_{x}+d_{y}))
bits.
∎

5
Sparse Co-Occurring Directions

In this section, we proposed a variant of COD for sparse AMM. We also prove its error bound is similar to our improved result of COD.

5.1
The Algorithm

We describe details of our sparse co-occurring directions (SCOD) in Algorithm
4
.
The procedure of SCOD maintains the sparse data in two buffer matrices
{\bf X}^{\prime}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime}
.
The algorithm restricts the non-zero entries in buffers to be less than
m(d_{x}+d_{y})
and the number of row of each buffer is at most
d_{x}+d_{y}
. When the buffers are full, we perform subspace power method (SPM)
(
Woodruff 2014b
;
Musco and Musco 2015
)
to approximate the data in the buffers by low-rank matrices
{\widetilde{\bf X}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d_{x}}
and
{\widetilde{\bf Y}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d_{y}}
such that
{\bf X}^{\prime\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime}\approx{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{\top}{\widetilde{\bf Y}}
. We present the procedure of SPM in Algorithm
3
.

Let
{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(i)}
and
{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}
be the results of
{\widetilde{\bf X}}
and
{\widetilde{\bf Y}}
after Algorithm
4
has executed “then” section for
i
-times. Define

{\bf C}=[{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(1)};\cdots;{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(T)}]
and

{\bf D}=[{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(1)};\cdots;{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(T)}]

where
T
is the number of total times we enter “then” section of the algorithm. Then
{\bf C}
and
{\bf D}
are the estimators of
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
respectively and the procedure of SCOD can be regarded as as running standard COD on input matrices
{\bf C}
and
{\bf D}
in streaming fashion.
Since the row numbers of buffers
{\bf X}^{\prime}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime}
could be much larger than
m
, the operations on dense matrices (line
14
-
20
) will not be executed frequently. Hence, SCOD is much more efficient than COD for sparse inputs.

5.2
Analysis of Error Bound

The analysis of SCOD is more challenging than sparse frequent directions (SFD)
(
Ghashami, Liberty, and Phillips 2016
)
which only addresses the case of
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
. The reason is the “mergeability property” of FD
(
Ghashami et al. 2016
;
Desai, Ghashami, and Phillips 2016
;
Ghashami, Liberty, and Phillips 2016
)
only works for Frobenius norm and it is not applicable to COD.

The approximation error of SCOD comes from two parts: the compressing error from sub-routine SPM and the merge error from estimating
{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}
by
{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}
.
We first consider a single call of SPM, which approximation error can be bounded as follows.

Lemma 7
.

Let
q=\tilde{\Theta}(\log(md_{1}/p)/{\varepsilon})
for Algorithm
3
, then the output
{\bf Z}
satisfies

\left\|{\bf M}-{\bf Z}{\bf Z}^{\top}{\bf M}\right\|_{2}\leq(1+{\varepsilon})\sigma_{m+1}({\bf M})

with probability at least
1-p
.

Based on Lemma
7
, we can bound the total compressing error of SOCD by the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Setting
q_{i}=\tilde{\Theta}(\log(md_{1}/p_{i})/{\varepsilon})
and
p_{i}=\delta/2i^{2}
, then we have then Algorithm
4
holds that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}\right\|_{2}\leq\dfrac{1+{\varepsilon}}{m-k}\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right),

for any
k<m
and
{\varepsilon}>0
with probability
1-\delta
.

Proof.

Let
{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}
be the value of
{\bf X}^{\prime}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime}
when we execute subspace power methods at
i
-th time in line
8
of Algorithm
4
, then we have

\displaystyle{\bf X}=[{\bf X}^{\prime(1)};\dots;{\bf X}^{\prime(T)}]\text{~~and~~}{\bf Y}=[{\bf Y}^{\prime(1)};\dots;{\bf Y}^{\prime(T)}].

Using Lemma
7
with
{\bf M}={\widetilde{\bf X}}^{{(i)}\top}{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}
and
q=q_{i}
, then with probability
1-p_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{{(i)}\top}{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}-{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\right\|_{2}\leq(1+{\varepsilon})\sigma_{m+1}\left({\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+{\varepsilon}}{m-k}\left(\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{k}\right),

(6)

where the last step use
Srebro, Rennie, and Jaakkola 2005
’s (
Srebro, Rennie, and Jaakkola 2005
) Lemma 1 such that

\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\|_{*}\leq\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}\|_{F}\|{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\|_{F}
.

Summing over inequality (
6
) with
i=1,\dots,T
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}\big\|_{2}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}-{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{{(i)}\top}{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+{\varepsilon}}{m-k}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1+{\varepsilon}}{m-k}\left(\big\|{\bf X}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\bf Y}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right)

with probability
1-\delta
. The last inequality is based on the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the triangle inequality of Ky Fan
k
-norm. Note that the failure probability is no more than
p_{1}+\dots+p_{T}=\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{T}1/i^{2}\leq\delta
.
∎

Unlike SFD
(
Ghashami et al. 2016
)
which introduces a verifying step to boost the success probability, our method instead requires
q_{i}
to be increased logarithmically to ensure the error bound of SCOD holds with probability at least
1-\delta
for given
\delta\in(0,1)
.
Another important property of SCOD is that the compression step shrink the magnitude of the product of input matrices. The steps in line
10
-
11
of Algorithm
4
balance the singular values of
{\widetilde{\bf X}}
and
{\widetilde{\bf Y}}
, which leads to the following lemma:

Lemma 9
.

Algorithm
4
holds that

\displaystyle\big\|{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}\leq\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}.

Since the analysis of merging error is similar to standard COD, we can establish the error bound of SCOD by using above lemmas.

Theorem 3
.

Setting
q_{i}=\tilde{\Theta}(\log(md_{1}/p_{i})/{\varepsilon})
with constant
{\varepsilon}>0
and
p_{i}=\delta/2i^{2}
, with probability
1-\delta
, the outputs
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
of Algorithm
4
hold that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2+{\varepsilon}}{m-k}+\frac{(1+{\varepsilon})k}{(m-k)^{2}}\right)\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right)

for all
k<m
.

Proof.

Consider that
{\bf A}
and
{\bf B}
can be viewed as the output of running Algorithm
2
with input matrices

\displaystyle{\bf C}=[{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(1)};\cdots;{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(T)}]\text{~~and~~}{\bf D}=[{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(1)};\cdots;{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(T)}].

Following the proof of Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}-{\bf A}^{\top}\bf{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{m-k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}\big\|_{k}\right)\\
\leq&\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{T}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}+\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}\big\|_{k}}{m-k}\\
\leq&\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{T}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf X}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\widetilde{\bf Y}}^{(i)}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}+k\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}\big\|_{2}}{m-k}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{m-k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}\big\|{\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}\big\|{\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}\big\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{(1+{\varepsilon})k}{(m-k)^{2}}\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right)\\
\leq&\left(\frac{1}{m-k}+\frac{(1+{\varepsilon})k}{(m-k)^{2}}\right)\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right)\end{split}

where we use Lemma
8
,
9
and triangle inequality.

Combing above results and Lemma
8
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}\right\|_{2}+\left\|{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf D}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2+{\varepsilon}}{m-k}+\frac{(1+{\varepsilon})k}{(m-k)^{2}}\right)\left(\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-\big\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}\big\|_{k}\right),

with probability at least
1-\delta
.
∎

5.3
Complexity Analysis

We use the constant-word-size model for our analysis like that of sparse FD
(
Ghashami et al. 2016
)
. We suppose floating point numbers are represented by a constant number of bits, random access into memory requires
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
time and multiplying a sparse matrix
{\bf M}
by a dense vector requires
{\mathcal{O}}({\rm nnz}({\bf M}))
time and storing
{\bf M}
requires
{\mathcal{O}}({\rm nnz}({\bf M}))
space.

The procedure of SCOD (Algorithm
4
) implies the buffer
{\bf X}^{\prime}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime}
is sparse and contains at most
m(d_{x}+d_{y})
non-zero entries and it is not difficult to verify that all dense matrices in the algorithm cost no more than
{\mathcal{O}}(m(d_{x}+d_{y}))
space.
Hence, the space complexity of SCOD is
{\mathcal{O}}(m(d_{x}+d_{y}))
in total which is the same as COD (Algorithm
2
).

Then we analyze the time complexity of SCOD.
The constraints on buffer size means we have

\displaystyle T\leq\frac{{\rm nnz}({\bf X})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y})}{m(d_{x}+d_{y})}+\frac{n}{d_{x}+d_{y}}.

Since each QR factorization or SVD on
m\times d
matrix cost
{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}d)
time, the operation on dense matrices of Algorithm
4
from line
9
-
20
requires at most

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}(m^{2}(d_{x}+d_{y})T)={\mathcal{O}}(m({\rm nnz}({\bf X})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y}))+m^{2}n).

Note that SCOD calls SPM with input
{\bf M}={\bf X}^{\prime\top}{\bf Y}^{\prime}
. Since both
{\bf X}^{\prime}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime}
are sparse, it is unnecessary to construct
{\bf M}
explicitly and we can multiply
{\bf X}^{\prime}
and
{\bf Y}^{\prime}
on
{\bf G}
separately in line
3
of Algorithm
3
. Then the time complexity of executing SPM needs

{\mathcal{O}}(mq_{i}({\rm nnz}({\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}))+m^{2}d_{x})

when the algorithm enters “then” section at the
i
-th time. Following the upper bound of
T
and the setting of
q_{i}
in Theorem
3
, the calls of SPM in Algorithm
3
entirely takes at most

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left(mq_{i}({\rm nnz}({\bf X}^{\prime{(i)}})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y}^{\prime{(i)}}))+m^{2}d_{x}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left(mq_{T}({\rm nnz}({\bf X})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y}))+Tm^{2}d_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(m({\rm nnz}({\bf X})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y}))+m^{2}n\big).

Hence, the total time complexity of proposed SCOD is
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(m({\rm nnz}({\bf X})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y}))+m^{2}n\big)
.

Conclusion

In this paper, we first improved the error bound of a deterministic sketching algorithm COD for streaming AMM problem.
In symmetric case, our result matches the error bound of classical algorithm FD.
We also proved COD matches the space lower bound complexity to achieve our error bound.
In addition, we proposed a sparse variant of COD with a reasonable error bound.
The experimental results show that the proposed algorithm has better performance than baseline methods in practice.

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{n}\delta^{{(t)}},

\displaystyle\big\|({\widehat{\bf A}}^{(t)})^{\top}{\widehat{\bf B}}^{(t)}\big\|_{*}-\big\|({\bf A}^{(t-1)})^{\top}{\bf B}^{(t-1)}\big\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|({\widehat{\bf A}}^{(t)})^{\top}{\widehat{\bf B}}^{(t)}-({\bf A}^{(t-1)})^{\top}{\bf B}^{(t-1)}\big\|_{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|{\bf{x}}^{(t)}({\bf{y}}^{(t)})^{\top}\big\|_{*}=\big\|{\bf{x}}^{(t)}\big\|_{2}\big\|{\bf{y}}^{(t)}\big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{*}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n}\big\|{\bf{x}}^{(t)}\big\|_{2}\big\|{\bf{y}}^{(t)}\big\|_{2}-m\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{m}^{(t)}\leq\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{F}\left\|{\bf Y}\right\|_{F}-m\sum_{t=1}^{n}\sigma_{m}^{(t)}.

Appendix F
Additional Discussion on SFD-AMM

Ghashami et al. 2016
proposed a variant of FD for sketching sparse matrices called sparse frequent directions (SFD).
Given input matrix
{\bf Z}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
, the algorithm output
{\bf C}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf Z}^{\top}{\bf Z}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf C}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha m-k}\left(\left\|{\bf Z}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|{\bf Z}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)

with high probability for any
k<\alpha m
, where
\alpha
is a constant depends on the accuracy of SPM. SFD requires
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\big(m\cdot{\rm nnz}({\bf Z})+m^{2}n\big)
time complexity and
{\mathcal{O}}(md)
space. The procedure of SFD is similar to SCOD in the case of
{\bf X}={\bf Y}
, but includes additional shrinking operation on the output of SPM
(
Ghashami, Liberty, and Phillips 2016
)
to apply the “mergeability porperty” of FD
(
Ghashami et al. 2016
;
Desai, Ghashami, and Phillips 2016
)
in their analysis.

For streaming AMM with sparse input, it is natural to combine the idea of SFD with FD-AMM directly which leads to the algorithm sparse FD-AMM (SFD-AMM).
Similar to FD-AMM, SFD-AMM applies SFD on concatenated matrix
{\bf Z}=[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times(d_{1}+d_{2})}
and its output
{\bf C}
which can be written as
{\bf C}=[{\bf A},{\bf B}]
, where
{\bf A}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{x}}
and
{\bf B}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d_{y}}
.
Then we use
{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}
to approximate
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}-{\bf A}^{\top}{\bf B}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|{\bf Z}^{\top}{\bf Z}-{\bf C}^{\top}{\bf C}\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha m-k}\left(\left\|{\bf Z}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|{\bf Z}_{k}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right).

(12)

The time complexity of SFD-AMM has the same order as SCOD since
{\rm nnz}({\bf Z})={\rm nnz}({\bf X})+{\rm nnz}({\bf Y})
. It is not easy to compare the error bound (
12
) with SCOD (Theorem
3
) in general because there does not exist simple relationship between the singular values of
{\bf Z}=[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]
and
{\bf X}^{\top}{\bf Y}
. However, SCOD always performs better than SFD-AMM empirically as we observed in Section
6
.

In theoretical, we can improve the time complexity of SFD-AMM to achieve the error bound of (
12
) by integrating random sampling
(
Huang 2019
)
.
However, the implementation of this strategy requires the value of
k
is given.
Unfortunately, it is difficult to select a suitable
k
for streaming setting in general. In contrast, the value of
k
in SFD-AMM or SCOD is only for theoretical analysis and it is no related to the implementation of algorithms.

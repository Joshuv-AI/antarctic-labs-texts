Abstract
. We prove that any τ \tau -tilting finite incidence algebra of a finite poset is representation-finite, and that any 𝐠 \mathbf{g} -tame incidence algebra of a finite simply connected poset is tame. As the converse of these assertions are known to hold, we obtain characterizations of τ \tau -tilting finite incidence algebras and 𝐠 \mathbf{g} -tame simply connected incidence algebras. Both results are proved using the theory of concealed algebras. The former will be deduced from the fact that tame concealed algebras are τ \tau -tilting infinite, and to prove the latter, we show that wild concealed algebras are not 𝐠 \mathbf{g} -tame. We conjecture that any incidence algebra of a finite poset is wild if and only if it is not 𝐠 \mathbf{g} -tame, and prove a result showing that there are relatively few possible counterexamples. In the appendix, we determine the representation type of a τ \tau -tilting reduction of a concealed algebra of hyperbolic type.

Introduction

Historical notes and motivation

Representation type.

Representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras is at its core a tool to better understand algebras. Even though it can be traced back further, its current state was formed by Gabriel
[
Gab72
,
Gab73
,
Gab80
]
using his language of quivers. The quiver-theoretic manner of approaching algebras provides the means of translating questions of algebraic structures into questions of linear algebra. However, the main selling point was arguably Gabriel’s classification of finite-dimensional hereditary algebras with finitely many indecomposable modules in terms of Dynkin diagrams.

The question of how many indecomposable modules an algebra admits, has historically been a driving factor in the study of algebras. The well known Brauer–Thrall conjectures (see e.g.
[
Jan57
,
Rin80
]
) are directly tied to this question. With regards to this question, algebras fall into three main categories of increasing complexity: finite, tame and wild type. Drozd famously showed that any algebra is either tame or wild, but never both
[
Dro80
]
. Hence, there is a dichotomy of algebras by representation type.

Around the same time as Gabriel proved his aforementioned result, the
Kyiv-school
studied and classified poset-representations of finite type
[
NRi72
,
Kle72
,
NRi75
]
. This result was highly influential and valuable in classifications of more general algebras, as one could often reduce to the case of posets. The main example of interest for us is Loupias’s classification of representation-finite incidence algebras of posets
[
Lou75a
,
Lou75b
]
.

(
\tau
-)tilting.
Inspired by the methods used in Gabriel’s classification, a new object was introduced, namely tilting modules
[
GP72
,
BGP73
,
APR79
,
BB80
,
HR82
]
. This gave a method of constructing a new algebra from another while carrying over known structure. It paved the way for a staggering amount of progress, and is still an important tool for modern representation theorists. It is indeed fundamental to the arguments in this paper.

In an effort to close gaps in the theory of tilting when the underlying algebra is not hereditary, Adachi, Iyama and Reiten
[
AIR14
]
generalized it to that of
\tau
-tilting theory. Their approach took inspiration from the theory of cluster algebras
[
FZ02a
,
FZ02b
,
BFZ05
,
FZ07
]
, as well as cluster tilting
[
BMR07
]
.
By having a bijection with functorially finite torsion classes and providing a module-theoretic interpretation of
2
-term silting objects,
\tau
-tilting was soon accepted as an interesting and worthwhile direction of study.

\mathbf{g}
-representation type:
\tau
-tilting finiteness and
\mathbf{g}
-tameness.

In a similar fashion as indecomposable modules, the amount of
\tau
-rigid indecomposable modules over an algebra has been closely studied. In fact, for hereditary algebras this amount is directly related to the algebras representation type. We say that an algebra is
\tau
-tilting finite
(or
\mathbf{g}
-finite
) if it only admits a finite number of
\tau
-rigid indecomposable modules
[
DIJ19
]
. One easily observes that an infinite directed component in the AR-quiver provides infinitely many
\tau
-rigid modules, whence hereditary algebras of infinite representation type are
\tau
-tilting infinite, since they admit two such components (one postprojective and one preinjective).

Further, we say that an algebra is
\mathbf{g}
-tame
if the
\mathbf{g}
-vector fan is dense in the real Grothendieck group
[
AY23
]
. It has been shown that tame representation type is sufficient for
\mathbf{g}
-tameness
[
PYK23
]
, and for hereditary algebras it
is
in fact also necessary
[
Hil06
]
. We do however have examples of
\mathbf{g}
-tame algebras of wild representation type (for instance wild local algebras and certain Jacobian algebras
[
Yur20
]
). At the time of writing, the authors are not familiar with a definition of
\mathbf{g}
-wildness of an algebra, apart from simply negating the notion of
\mathbf{g}
-tameness.

Having, for the hereditary case, two equivalent notions to finite and tame representation type, it is natural to ask how big of a defect we might get for less well-behaved algebras. We look at the slightly more rowdy class of incidence algebras in this paper. Both the classification of minimally representation-infinite incidence algebras by Loupias
[
Lou75a
,
Lou75b
]
and the criterion of tameness for incidence algebras by Leszczyński
[
Les03
]
will be prominent in the following.

A

An

A poset is

Remark 2.8

For incidence algebras of posets, the choice of field does not matter when it comes to representation-finiteness. Indeed, the incidence

Loupias devises two reduction procedures of incidence algebras that preserve representation-finiteness
[

, Propositions 1.2 and 1.3]
.
Given a poset
P
, a subposet of
P
is also called a

of
P

A poset

is a

of
P
if there exists a surjective order-preserving map

with connected fibers. An
elementary contraction

is a contraction for which there exists exactly one element
x\in R
such that
|c^{-1}(x)|=2
and
|c^{-1}(r)|=1
for all
r\in R\setminus\{x\}
. Informally, an elementary contraction identifies two adjacent elements in
P
. Thus, there are as many elementary contractions as there are arrows in the Hasse quiver of
P
.
It can be shown that all contractions are composites of elementary contractions.

We say that a poset
P

can be reduced to

if it is the case that
L
can be obtained from

by subtraction and contraction in a finite number of steps. We may assume that each step either removes a single vertex or contracts an arrow in the Hasse quiver.

If

is a subposet of

, one sets up a
\mathbf{k}
-algebra isomorphism

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({L}\right)\simeq e\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({P}\right)e,

where

is the idempotent
\sum_{i\in L}e_{i}
and the terms

in the sum are primitive idempotents corresponding to the elements in

(2.a)

\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({L}\right))\simeq\operatorname{mod}(e\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({P}\right)e){\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{\Hom_{e\Lambda e}({e\Lambda},\,{-})}}\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({P}\right)).

If

is a contraction of

P\twoheadrightarrow R
.
Since the module category
\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({P}\right))
is equivalent to the functor category
\operatorname{mod}(\mathbf{k})^{P}
, and similarly
\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({R}\right))\cong\operatorname{mod}(\mathbf{k})^{R}
, one can induce a functor

(2.b)

\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({R}\right))\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\operatorname{mod}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{k}}\left({P}\right))

Perturbed ( 𝟐 ​ 𝒏 − 𝟏 ) \boldsymbol{(2n-1)} -Dimensional Kepler Problem and the Nilpotent Adjoint Orbits of 𝑼 ⁡ ( 𝒏 , 𝒏 ) \boldsymbol{U(n,n)}
Anatol ODZIJEWICZ

Abstract
We study the regularized ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) (2n-1) -Kepler problem and other Hamiltonian systems which are related to the nilpotent coadjoint orbits of U ⁡ ( n , n ) U(n,n) . The Kustaanheimo–Stiefel and Cayley regularization procedures are discussed and their equivalence is shown. Some integrable generalization (perturbation) of ( 2 ​ n − 1 ) (2n-1) -Kepler problem is proposed.

In Section

2
Grassmannian
\boldsymbol{{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)}
and related vector bundles

In this section we will study some canonically defined bundles over the Grassmannian
{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
of
n
-dimensional complex vector subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
. Let us recall that
{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
is a
n^{2}
-dimensional compact complex analytic manifold homogenous with respect to the natural action of
{\rm GL}(2n,\mathbb{C})
.

We begin with defining the following complex analytic bundles over
{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
. Namely, we consider the bundle
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}\colon\mathcal{N}\to{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
whose fibres consist of nilpotent elements of
\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})
. The total space of this bundle is defined as

\mathcal{N}:=\big\{(\mathcal{Z},z)\in\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})\times{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)\colon\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{Z})\subset z\subset\operatorname{Ker}(\mathcal{Z})\big\}

and
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}
is the projection of
\mathcal{N}
on the second component of the cartesian product. One easily sees that
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}\colon\mathcal{N}\to{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
is a complex vector bundle of rank
n^{2}
. The subset
\operatorname{pr}_{1}(\mathcal{N})\subset\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})
consists of such elements
\mathcal{Z}\in\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})
, which satisfy
\mathcal{Z}^{2}=0
and have rank
k:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{Z})
, where
1\leq k\leq n
.

Next the bundle
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\to{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
is a bundle of idempotents, i.e.,

\mathcal{P}:=\big\{(p,z)\in\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})\times{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)\colon p^{2}=p,\,\operatorname{Im}(p)=z\big\},

where
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}
is the projection of
\mathcal{P}
on the second component of cartesian product. We note that
\operatorname{pr}_{1}(\mathcal{P})
consists of such idempotents in
\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})
that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\operatorname{Im}(p))=n
.

In order to make the structure of
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\to{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
more transparent, we formulate the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

The bundle
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\mathcal{P}\!\to\!{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
is an affine bundle with
\pi_{\mathcal{N}}\colon\mathcal{N}\!\to\!{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)

as the structural vector bundle, i.e., for any
z\in{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)
the vector space
\mathcal{N}_{z}:=\pi^{-1}_{\mathcal{N}}(z)
acts in a transitive and free way on the fibre
\mathcal{P}_{z}:=\pi^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}}(z)
.

Proof.

For
p\in\mathcal{P}_{z}
and
\mathcal{Z}\in\mathcal{N}_{z}
we have

(p+\mathcal{Z})^{2}=p^{2}+\mathcal{Z}^{2}+p\mathcal{Z}+\mathcal{Z}p=p+\mathcal{Z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(p+\mathcal{Z})z=pz=z.

This shows that
p+\mathcal{Z}\in\mathcal{P}_{z}
.

For
p,p^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{z}
we have

(p^{\prime}-p)^{2}=p^{\prime 2}+p^{2}-p^{\prime}p-pp^{\prime}=p^{\prime}+p-p-p^{\prime}=0

and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\operatorname{Im}(p^{\prime}-p)\leq n
. Thus,
p^{\prime}-p=:\mathcal{Z}\in\mathcal{N}_{z}
. Due to the above facts one has free and transitive action of
\mathcal{N}_{z}
on
\mathcal{P}_{z}
.
∎

We note that for
p^{\prime},p\in\mathcal{P}_{z}
the following equalities hold

p^{\prime}p=p\qquad\text{and}\qquad pp^{\prime}=p^{\prime}.

Subsequently, using the Cartan–Killing form

\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})\times\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})\ni(\mathcal{Z}_{1},\mathcal{Z}_{2})\to\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{Z}_{1}\mathcal{Z}_{2})\in\mathbb{C}

(2.1)

we will identify the dual space
\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})^{*}
with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})
.

For any
p\in\operatorname{pr}_{1}(\mathcal{P})
one has the open subset

\Omega_{p}:=\big\{z\in{\rm Gr}\big(n,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\big)\colon z\cap(1-p)\mathbb{C}^{2n}=\{0\}\big\}

of the Grassmannian. We define a chart
\phi_{p}\colon\Omega_{p}\to(1-p)\mathfrak{gl}(2n,\mathbb{C})p\cong\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
in the following way. The decomposition

defines the projection
q_{z}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
on subspace

\operatorname{Im}(1-q_{z})=\operatorname{Im}(1-p)

2.1

(2.2)

The equality (

In order to find the transition maps

From (

\displaystyle q_{z}=q_{z}^{\prime}q_{z}=(Z^{\prime}+p^{\prime})q_{z}=p^{\prime}q_{z}+Z^{\prime}p^{\prime}q_{z},

\displaystyle q_{z}=(p+Z)q_{z}=(p+Z)pq_{z}=p^{\prime}(p+Z)pq_{z}+(1-p^{\prime})(p+Z)pq_{z}

\displaystyle\hphantom{q_{z}}{}=p^{\prime}(p+(1-p)Z)pq_{z}+(1-p^{\prime})(p+(1-p)Z)pq_{z}.

Expressions (

Y\big(\upsilon^{+},\zeta^{+},\upsilon,\zeta\big):=\frac{1}{\zeta^{+}\zeta}\left[\zeta\upsilon^{+}+\upsilon\zeta^{+}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\upsilon^{+}\zeta+\zeta^{+}\upsilon\big)E\right].

(5.6)

\mathcal{R}

Example 5.2

In this example we assume

\Omega:=\big\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}v\\
\zeta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{0}_{+-}\colon v^{+}\zeta\not=0\big\}

The meaning of the first example will be explained at the end of this section. The second example illustrates another possibility to define a local diffeomorphism

Having in mind a physical interpretations of the discussed Hamiltonian systems, we will consider the case

Expanding

In this case we assume that

Using the identity

valid for Pauli matrices

Summing up the above facts we state that the Hamiltonian system

-dimensional Kepler system written in the “fictitious time”

For an exhaustive description of the regularized Kepler problem we address to original papers of Moser
[

and of Kustaanheimo and Stiefel

In order to express

of

6
An integrable generalization of
\boldsymbol{(2n-1)}
-dimensional

Kepler problem

We present here an integrable Hamiltonian system which will be a natural generalization (preturbation) of regularized
(2n-1)
-dimensional Kepler problem discussed in Section
4
.

Therefore, assuming for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
the convention

z^{l}:=\begin{cases}z^{l}&\text{for }l\geq 0,\\
\bar{z}^{-l}&\text{for }l<0\end{cases}

we define the following Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=h_{0}\big(|\eta_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\eta_{n}|^{2},|\xi_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\xi_{n}|^{2}\big)+g_{0}\big(|\eta_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\eta_{n}|^{2},|\xi_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\xi_{n}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{H =}{}\times\big(\eta_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\eta_{n}^{k_{n}}\xi_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\xi_{n}^{l_{n}}+\eta_{1}^{-k_{1}}\cdots\eta_{n}^{-k_{n}}\xi_{1}^{-l_{1}}\cdots\xi_{n}^{-l_{n}}\big),

(6.1)

on the symplectic manifold
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2n},{\rm d}\gamma_{+-}\big)
, where
h_{0}
and
g_{0}
are arbitrary smooth functions of
2n
real variables and
k_{1},\ldots k_{n},l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let us note here that taking in (
6.1
)
h_{0}=I_{++}
and
g_{0}=0
we obtain
(2n-1)
-dimensional regularized Kepler Hamiltonian on
\mathcal{T}_{+-}^{0}/_{\sim}
. We see from (
6.1
) that
H
is a radical generalization of
I_{++}
. Nevertheless, as we will show in the subsequent, the Hamiltonian system
\big(\mathcal{T}_{+-}^{0}/_{\sim},H/_{\sim}\big)
is still integrable in quadratures.

For this reason, according to
[
29
]
, we define, for
r=1,\ldots,2n
, the functions

\displaystyle I_{r}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{r,j}|\eta_{j}|^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{r,n+j}|\xi_{j}|^{2},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{r}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa_{j,r}\phi_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa_{n+j,r}\phi_{n+j},

where
\eta_{j}=|\eta_{j}|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi_{j}}
,
\xi_{j}=|\xi_{j}|{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\phi_{n+j}}
. By definition the real
2n\times 2n
matrix
[\rho_{r,s}]
is invertible and the matrix
[\kappa_{r,s}]
is its inverse. The functions
(I_{1},\ldots,I_{2n},\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{2n})
form a system of coordinates on the open subset

\Omega^{2n}:=\big\{(\eta,\xi)\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|\eta_{1}|\neq 0,\ldots,|\eta_{n}|\neq 0,|\xi_{1}|\neq 0,\ldots,|\xi_{n}|\neq 0\big\}

of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.
They are a canonical coordinates for symplectic form
{\rm d}\gamma_{+-}
, i.e., their Poisson brackets satisfy

\displaystyle\{I_{r},I_{s}\}=0,\qquad\{I_{r},\psi_{s}\}=\delta_{rs},\qquad\{\psi_{r},\psi_{s}\}=0.

What is more, one easily checks that for
r=2,\ldots,2n
one has
\{H,I_{r}\}=0
if and only if

\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\rho_{r,j}l_{j}+\rho_{r,n+j}k_{j})=\delta_{r1}.

(6.3)

So, the Hamiltonian system on
\big(\Omega^{2n},{\rm d}\gamma_{+-}\big)
given by the Hamiltonian (
6.1
) is integrable and
H,I_{2},\ldots,I_{2n-1}
are its functionally independent integrals of motion in involution. Considering
(I_{2},\ldots,I_{2n})
as the components

{\bf J}(\eta^{+},\xi^{+},\eta,\xi)=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{2}(\eta^{+},\xi^{+},\eta,\xi)\\
\vdots\\
I_{2n}(\eta^{+},\xi^{+},\eta,\xi)\end{matrix}\right)

(6.4)

of the momentum map
{\bf J}\colon\Omega^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}
, where one identifies
\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}
with the dual space to the Lie algebra of
(2n-1)
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{2n-1}:=\underbrace{U(1)\times\cdots\times U(1)}_{2n-1}
, we can apply Marsden–Weinstein reduction procedure to
\big(\Omega^{2n},{\rm d}\gamma_{+-},H\big)
. In this way we reduce the above Hamiltonian system to
{\bf J}^{-1}(c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})/\mathbb{T}^{2n-1}\cong{}]a,b[{}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\omega_{\rm red}={\rm d}I_{1}\wedge{\rm d}\psi_{1}
as a symplectic form, where
(I_{1},\psi_{1})\in{}]a,b[{}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
, and the Hamiltonian (
6.1
) after the reduction to
{\bf J}^{-1}(c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})/\mathbb{T}^{2n-1}
assumes the following form

H_{\rm red}=H_{0}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})+2\sqrt{G_{0}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})}\cos\psi_{1},

where
H_{0}(I_{1},I_{2},\ldots,I_{2n})
and
G_{0}(I_{1},I_{2},\ldots,I_{2n})
are defined as the superpositions of the functions

\displaystyle h_{0}\big(|\eta_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\eta_{n}|^{2},|\xi_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\xi_{n}|^{2}\big)

and

\displaystyle\big(g_{0}\big(|\eta_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\eta_{n}|^{2},|\xi_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|\xi_{n}|^{2}\big)\big)^{2}|\eta_{1}|^{2|k_{1}|}\cdots|\eta_{n}|^{2|k_{n}|}|\xi_{1}|^{2|l_{1}|}\cdots|\xi_{n}|^{2|l_{n}|}

with the map inverse to the map defined in (
6.2
). For the explicit expression for
a
and
b
see
[
29
]
.

The Hamilton equations defined by
H_{\rm red}
in the canonical coordinates
(I_{1},\psi_{1})
have form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}I_{1}}{{\rm d}t}=2\sqrt{G_{0}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})}\sin\psi_{1},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\psi_{1}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial I_{1}}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})+\frac{\partial G_{0}}{\partial I_{1}}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})\cos\psi_{1}.

From (
6.5
) and
E:=H_{\rm red}(I_{1}(t),\psi_{1}(t),c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})={\rm const}
, where
E
is the total energy of the system, we obtain

\left(\frac{{\rm d}I_{1}}{{\rm d}t}\right)^{2}=4G_{0}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n})-(E-H_{0}(I_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{2n}))^{2}.

(6.6)

Separating variables in (
6.6
) we integrate it by quadratures. Next, using integrals of motion
I_{2},\ldots,I_{2n}
, we integrate our initial system defined on
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2n},{\rm d}\gamma_{+-}\big)
by the Hamiltonian (
6.1
). A detailed description of this method of integration can be found in
[
29
, Section 2]
.

Now let us assume that the last two of integrals of motion
I_{2},\ldots,I_{2n-1},I_{2n}
are given by

\displaystyle I_{2n-1}:=I_{++}=\eta^{+}\eta+\xi^{+}\xi,\qquad I_{2n}:=I_{+-}=\eta^{+}\eta-\xi^{+}\xi.

Hence, from (
6.3
), we obtain the conditions

k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad l_{1}+\dots+l_{n}=0

on the exponents
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n},l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
, which guarantee integrability of the Hamiltonian system
\big(\mathbb{C}^{2n},{\rm d}\gamma_{+-},H\big)
. Because
I_{+-}
is one of the integrals of motion, we find that the reduced system
\big(\mathcal{T}_{+-}^{0}/_{\sim},H/_{\sim}\big)
is also integrable. So, using the symplectomorphism
\mathcal{C}_{\rm reg}\circ\mathcal{K}_{\rm reg}\colon\dot{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,0}/_{\sim}\to\mathcal{T}_{+-}^{0}/_{\sim}
, see diagram (
4.11
), we obtain an integrable Hamiltonian system on
\dot{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,0}/_{\sim}
with Hamiltonian
(H/_{\sim})\circ\mathcal{C}_{\rm reg}
.

In the particular case, if
k\in\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\}
and
l\in\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\}
then
-k\in\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\}
and
-l\in\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\}
, the Hamiltonian (
6.1
) depends on the matrix elements of
I_{+}
and
I_{-}
only. So, in this case we obtain from (
4.22
) that the Hamiltonian
(H/_{\sim})\circ\mathcal{C}_{\rm reg}
could be defined as the reduction
\tilde{H}/_{\sim}
to
\dot{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}}_{1,0}/_{\sim}
of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\tilde{H}=h_{0}\big(N^{-}_{11},\ldots,N^{-}_{nn},N^{+}_{11},\ldots,N^{+}_{nn}\big)+g_{0}\big(N^{-}_{11},\ldots,N^{-}_{nn},N^{+}_{11},\ldots,N^{+}_{nn}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{H} =}{}\times\big[\big(N^{-}_{i_{1}j_{1}}\big)^{k_{i_{1}}}\cdots\big(N^{-}_{i_{r}j_{r}}\big)^{k_{i_{r}}}\big(N^{+}_{a_{1}b_{1}}\big)^{l_{a_{1}}}\cdots\big(N^{+}_{a_{s}b_{s}}\big)^{l_{a_{s}}}+\text{h.c.}\big]

(6.7)

on
H(n)\times H(n)
, where
N^{\pm}_{kl}:=\frac{1}{2}(R_{kl}\pm M_{kl})
,
R
and
M
depend on
(Y,X)
by (

\{k_{i_{1}},\ldots,k_{i_{r}}\}\subset\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}\}
and
\{l_{a_{1}},\ldots,l_{a_{s}}\}\subset\{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}\}
satisfy
k_{i_{m}}=-k_{j_{m}}>0
for
m=1,2,\ldots,r
and
l_{a_{m}}=-l_{b_{m}}>0
for
m=1,2,\ldots,s
.

Ending this section, we write the Hamiltonian (

) in the more explicit form for the case

. In this case the integrals of motion
M
and
R
can be written in terms of Pauli matrices

M=M_{0}E+\vec{M}\cdot\vec{\sigma}\qquad\text{and}\qquad R=R_{0}E+\vec{R}\cdot\vec{\sigma},

where
\vec{M}
and
\vec{R}
are angular momentum and Runge–Lenz vector, respectively. Using the linear relation

\left(\begin{matrix}|\eta_{1}|^{2}\\
|\eta_{2}|^{2}\\
|\xi_{1}|^{2}\\
|\xi_{2}|^{2}\end{matrix}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}1&1&-1&-1\\

1&1&1&1\\

R_{3}\\
M_{0}\\

and defining

and

we write this Hamiltonian as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{H}=\tilde{h}_{0}(R_{0},R_{3},M_{0},M_{3})+\tilde{g}_{0}(R_{0},R_{3},M_{0},M_{3})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{H} =}{}\times\big((R_{\sigma}-M_{\sigma})^{k}(R_{\sigma^{\prime}}+M_{\sigma^{\prime}})^{l}+(R_{-\sigma}-M_{-\sigma})^{k}(R_{-\sigma^{\prime}}+M_{-\sigma^{\prime}})^{l}\big),

where

\tilde{h}_{0}
,

are arbitrary smooth functions. Let us note that

leads to the reduced system
\mathcal{T}_{+-}^{0}/_{\sim}

In order to represent this Hamiltonian in the canonical coordinates

5.7

\displaystyle\vec{M}=2\vec{x}\times\vec{y},

(6.9)

\displaystyle\vec{R}=\big(1-{\vec{y}}^{2}\big)\vec{x}+2\vec{y}(\vec{x}\cdot\vec{y}).

(6.10)

After substituting (

), (

into (

Detection of twice-punctured tori in hyperbolic knot complements
Yi Wang

Abstract
We extend the methods of [ 22 ] to show that certain families of essential twice-punctured tori are detected by an ideal point on the character variety, and that the limiting character at these ideal points is irreducible. This completes the proof that all essential punctured tori in two-bridge knots are detected by an ideal point in the S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{C}) character variety and initiates the study of punctured tori in pretzel knots.

Introduction

Given a one-cusped hyperbolic 3-manifold
M
, the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety

X(M)
is an associated algebraic set. The character variety is a source of arithmetic invariants of 3-manifolds and provides a bridge between low-dimensional topology and arithmetic geometry. For instance, Chinburg-Reid-Stover
[
3
]
defined a particular hyperbolic knot invariant called the
canonical Azumaya algebra

A_{k(C)}
, associated to the function field
k(C)
of an irreducible component
C
of
X(M)
. A K-theoretic argument was used in
[
3
]
to extend equivalence classes of Azumaya algebras over
ideal points
, i.e. “points at infinity”, of
C
. In
[
22
]
, the author initiated the study of
tautological extension
of
A_{k(C)}
over ideal points of
C
. Tautological extension is intimately connected to
Culler-Shalen theory

[
5
]
, which associates ideal points of
C
to essential surfaces in
M
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
C\subset X(M)
be a one-dimensional irreducible component of the character variety, and let
x\in C
be an ideal point. An essential surface
S\subset M
is said to be
detected by
x
if
S
is the essential surface associated to
x
via the process described in
[
5
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
x\in C
be an ideal point, and let
S\subset M
be the detected surface. Given a connected component
M_{i}\subset M\setminus S
, the
limiting character at
M_{i}
is

\chi_{i}^{\infty}=\lim_{\chi\to x}\chi|_{\pi_{1}(M_{i})}

(1)

It is a consequence of the work in
[
5
]
that
\chi_{i}^{\infty}(g)
takes finite values for all
g\in\pi_{1}(M_{i})
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
x\in C
be an ideal point, and let
S\subset M
be the detected surface. The canonical Azumaya algebra
A_{k(C)}

tautologically extends over
x
if there exists a connected component

such that the limiting character at
M^{\prime}
is an irreducible
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character of
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})
.

This is a refinement of the Chinburg-Reid-Stover invariant because it provides an explicit Azumaya algebra over the valuation ring associated to the ideal point. In addition, tautological extension is an invariant that comes from the hyperbolic structure of JSJ decompositions of toroidal Dehn fillings of
K
, which gives topological meaning to the explicit algebraic extension of
A_{k(C)}
over ideal points in contrast to the purely algebraic methods from
[
3
]
. The first theorem establishing tautological extension to a class of hyperbolic 3-manifolds, proven in
[
22
]
, is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
22
]

Let
M=S^{3}\setminus K
be a hyperbolic knot complement, and let
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset M
be a system of disjoint non-parallel once-punctured JSJ tori with slope 0. Suppose the following:

•

All irreducible components of
X(M)
containing irreducible characters are norm curves.

•

No JSJ complementary region of
M(0)
is Seifert fibered over the annulus.

•

No JSJ torus in
M(0)
bounds two hyperbolic components on both sides.

Then

In other words, genus one Seifert surfaces that cap off to a JSJ decomposition are detected by an ideal point of the character variety; that ideal point is the one that admits tautological extension. The following corollary establishes an infinite example of ideal points over which

extends:

Corollary 1.1
.

Any genus one Seifert surface in a genus one nonfibered two-bridge knot complement

C
in

. In addition,

tautologically extends over that ideal point.

2.2
The twisted
I
-bundle over the Klein bottle

One space that often appears in JSJ decompositions of Dehn fillings capping of twice-punctured tori in knot complements is the
twisted
I
-bundle over the Klein bottle
, denoted
\tilde{K}
. This is an orientable 3-manifold with torus boundary. For an in-depth discussion of
\tilde{K}
, we refer the reader to
[
13
]
.

We will study representations of
\pi_{1}(\tilde{K})
. It is known that

\pi_{1}(\tilde{K})=\langle s,t\mid s^{2}t^{2}=1\rangle

(2)

The incompressible torus boundary has fundamental group generated by the meridian
st

s^{2}
. It will be useful to compute the representation and character varieties of this group. Utilizing the trace relation

\text{tr}(AB)=\text{tr}(A)\text{tr}(B)-\text{tr}(AB^{-1})

(3)

we can determine that the character variety is a complex cubic surface:

X(\tilde{K})=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\mid xyz-x^{2}-y^{2}=0\right\}

(4)

with coordinates are
x=\text{tr}(s),y=\text{tr}(t),z=\text{tr}(st)
. We now determine all the irreducible
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representations of
\pi_{1}(\tilde{K})
. Any such representation is conjugate to

\rho(s)=\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
r&y^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

(5)

Determining
\rho(s^{2}t^{2})
and setting the entries equal to the identity, we obtain that any irreducible representation must satisfy

\rho(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&1\\
0&\mp i\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&0\\
r&\mp i\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho(st)=\begin{pmatrix}r-1&-i\\
-ri&-1\end{pmatrix}

(6)

which correspond to the point
(0,0,r-2)\in X(\tilde{K})
. The reducible representations can be conjugated into

\rho(s)=\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}y&r\\
0&y^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

(7)

Once again solving for
\rho(s^{2}t^{2})=I
, we find that the reducible representations can be given by two families.

1.

The representations that satisfy

\rho(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&1\\
0&\mp i\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&r\\
0&\mp i\end{pmatrix}

(8)

which correspond to the points
(0,0,\pm 2)\in X(\tilde{K})
.

2.

The representations that satisfy

\rho(s)=\begin{pmatrix}x&1\\
0&x^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm x^{-1}&\mp 1\\
0&\pm x\end{pmatrix}

(9)

which correspond to the points
(a,\pm a,\pm 2)\in X(\tilde{K})

We will be interested in representations of
\pi_{1}(\tilde{K})

\text{tr}(st)=z=\pm 2
and
\text{tr}(s^{2})=x^{2}-2=\pm 2

satisfying this condition are
(0,0,\pm 2)

\rho_{A}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&1\\
0&\mp i\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho_{A}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&0\\
4&\mp i\end{pmatrix}

(10)

\rho_{B}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&1\\
0&\mp i\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \ \ \rho_{B}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&r\\
0&\mp i\end{pmatrix}

(11)

\rho_{C}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&1\\

2.3
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-compatibility

Suppose
M=S^{3}\setminus K
is a hyperbolic knot complement, with
\mathbb{T}
a system of twice-punctured JSJ tori with slope
\beta
. We must analyze the behavior of the signs of the traces of the boundary components of
M(\beta)
. In particular, the proof of the main theorem requires a choice of a set of characters on the JSJ complementary regions
M(\beta)
such that the traces on the boundary tori match. This property, defined in
[
22
]
, is known as
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-compatibility
. Proving
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-compatibility makes heavy use of
negative-flexibility
, which we define here:

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(M,\rho)
consisting of a 3-manifold
M
with torus boundary components
T_{1},\dots,T_{m}
and a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(M)\to PSL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is called
negative-flexible
if for any
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})\in\pi_{1}(T_{1})\times\dots\times\pi_{1}(T_{m})
with
\gamma_{i}
simple and nontrivial, there exists a lift
\hat{\rho}:\pi_{1}(M)\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\text{tr}(\hat{\rho}(\gamma_{i}))=-2
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

To begin, we recall the following lemma, proved in
[
22
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
M
is either hyperbolic or Seifert-fibered over
A^{2}(q)
. Let

\rho=\begin{cases}\text{holonomy reprsentation of }M&$M$\text{ hyperbolic}\\
\text{holonomy representation of }A^{2}(q)&$M$\text{ Seifert-fibered over }A^{2}(q)\end{cases}

(16)

Then
(M,\rho)
is negative-flexible.

We now prove similar lemmas for two more types of spaces.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
M
is Seifert-fibered over
D(p,q)
with
p\geq 2,q>2
. Let
\rho
be the holonomy representation of
D(p,q)
. Then
(M,\rho)
is negative-flexible.

Proof.

The holonomy representation of the thrice-punctured sphere, whose fundamental group is given by
\langle a,b,c\mid c=ab\rangle
, is

\rho(a)=\left[\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right]\ \ \ \ \ \rho(b)=\left[\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-2&1\end{pmatrix}\right]\ \ \ \ \ \rho(c)=\left[\pm\begin{pmatrix}-3&2\\
-2&1\end{pmatrix}\right]

(17)

In particular, for any lift
\hat{\rho}
of this representation to
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, if the signs of
\hat{\rho}(a)
and
\hat{\rho}(b)
are the same, then the sign of
\hat{\rho}(c)
is negative, and if the traces of
\hat{\rho}(a)
and
\hat{\rho}(b)
are different, then the sign of
\hat{\rho}(c)
is positive. The holonomy representation of
D^{2}(p,q)
,
p\geq 2,q>2
is thus given by

\rho(a)=\left[\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right]\ \ \ \ \ \rho(b)=\left[\pm\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2p}&0\\
x_{q}&\xi_{2p}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right]\ \ \ \ \ \rho(c)=\left[\pm\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{2p}+2x_{q}&2\xi_{2p}^{-1}\\
x_{q}&\xi_{2p}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\right]

(18)

where

\xi_{2p}+\xi_{2p}^{-1}+2x_{q}=\xi_{2q}+\xi_{2q}^{-1}

(19)

Since the holonomy of
D^{2}(p,q)
is a continuous deformation of the holonomy of the thrice-punctured sphere, it follows that the signs of the traces of a lift of
a
and
b
are equal if and only if
c
lifts to a negative trace, as is true for the thrice-punctured sphere. We will show that in all cases, the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-homologically trivial element of
\pi_{1}(\partial M)
, i.e. the one with trace sign fixed in all lifts, must lift to a negative trace. Let
h
be the regular fiber.

Case 1: Either
p
or
q
are even.
Without loss of generality, let
q
be even. In this case,
c^{q}h^{r}=1
for
r
odd. Since
q

\hat{\rho}:\pi_{1}(M)\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})

. By Lemma 3.1 in
[

]

\hat{\rho}
,
\hat{\rho}(h)=\pm I
. Since

\text{tr}(\hat{\rho}(h))=-2
. Since
\text{tr}(\hat{\rho}(h))=-2

\hat{\rho}

Case 2:

In this case,
b^{p}h^{r_{1}}=c^{q}h^{r_{2}}=1
.

Subcase 2.1

\hat{\rho}

,

\hat{\rho}

Subcase 2.2

\hat{\rho}

\hat{\rho}(h^{r_{1}})=1

Proof.

4&\mp i\end{pmatrix}

\rho_{B}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}\pm i&1\\

Proof.

By assumption,

C

1.

Type 1: the trace functions of non-trivial peripheral elements are nonconstant. These are norm curves.

2.

Type 2: The trace function of every peripheral element is constant.

3.

Type 3: There is one primitive peripheral element, up to inverses, whose trace function is constant.

If a component

C

5
]
Theorem 2.2.1). Now suppose

∎

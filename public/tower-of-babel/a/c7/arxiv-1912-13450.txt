A classical mechanical model of two interacting massless particles in de Sitter space and its quantization Journal: Physics Letters B
Naohiro Kanda, Satoshi Okano

Abstract
A conformally invariant model of two interacting massless particles in Minkowski space was proposed by Casalbuoni and Gomis [ 1 ] . We generalize this model to the case of de Sitter space from the perspective of geodesic distance, in such a way that the resulting, generalized action reduces to the original action in a limit that de Sitter radius goes to infinity. We analyze the Hamiltonian formulation in accordance with Dirac’s prescription for constrained Hamiltonian systems and carry out its subsequent canonical quantization in coordinate representation following DeWitt. As the result, we derive a fourth-order differential wave equation for bilocal fields that, in the infinite radius limit, reproduces one obtained in the original model for Minkowski space case.



1
Introduction

The early universe during cosmological inflation can be approximated as de Sitter space
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
On the other hand, the universe after inflation approaches Minkowski space, which can be regarded as a limit that de Sitter radius
R
goes to infinity.
During inflation and for a while after its end, the energy scale of the universe remains much higher than the electroweak scale.
In the period, except for the Higgs particle, all the elementary particles included in the standard model remain massless.
In particular, after inflation, their dynamics should have conformal invariance in Minkowski space.
However, until the end of inflation, during which the arena of physics is de Sitter space, it is not clear whether conformal invariance is realized.
This observation motivates us to consider a massless system that does not have conformal invariance at the beginning but restores it in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
.
In this letter, we construct such a model at classical and quantum mechanical levels.

A conformally invariant model of interacting massless particles in Minkowski space was given by Casalbuoni and Gomis
[
1
]
.
An action for two free massless particles in Minkowski space is

\displaystyle S_{0}=\int d\tau L_{0},\;\quad L_{0}=-\sum\limits^{2}_{i=1}\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}_{i}\dot{x}^{\nu}_{i}}{2e_{i}}\>,

(1)

where
\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3
,
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(+,-,-,-)
,
x^{\mu}_{i}(\tau),i=1,2
are the spacetime coordinates for the two particles,
e_{i}(\tau)
are einbeins,
\tau
is the worldline parameter common to the two particles and
\dot{x}^{\mu}_{i}=dx_{i}^{\mu}/d\tau
.
The free action
S_{0}
is invariant under both reparameterization of
\tau
and conformal transformations in Minkowski space.
An interaction term
S_{\mathrm{int}}
that has the same invariance as
S_{0}
and does not include the velocities
\dot{x}_{i}
is uniquely determined
[
1
]
as

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{int}}=\int d\tau V(r)\>,\quad V(r)=-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\frac{\sqrt{e_{1}e_{2}}}{r^{2}}\>,

(2)

where
\alpha
is the coupling constant,
r^{\mu}:=x_{1}^{\mu}-x_{2}^{\mu}
are the relative coordinates and
r^{2}=\eta_{\mu\nu}r^{\mu}r^{\nu}
.
Thus, the conformally invariant action for two interacting massless particles is given as

\displaystyle S:=S_{0}+S_{\mathrm{int}}=\int d\tau\left(-\sum^{2}_{i=1}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}_{i}\dot{x}^{\nu}_{i}}{e_{i}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\frac{\sqrt{e_{1}e_{2}}}{r^{2}}\right)\>.

(3)

In the Hamiltonian formulation based on this action
S
, first-class constraints associated with the reparameterization invariance occur.
After canonical quantization, they are replaced with the physical state conditions and read as a fourth-order differential equation:

\displaystyle\left(\square_{1}\square_{2}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{16r^{4}}\right)\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})=0\>,

(4)

where
\square_{i}=\partial^{2}/\partial x_{i\mu}\partial x^{\mu}_{i}
and
\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})
is a wave function of the system, which we call a bilocal field in the sense that the wave function depends on two spacetime points.
Our purpose is to generalize the action
S
and its consequent wave equation to the case of de Sitter space.

In generalizing the action (
3
), we rely on the fact that the squared relative coordinates
r^{2}
are considered as a special case of a squared geodesic distance.
A geodesic distance
\sigma(x_{1},x_{2})
between two points
x_{1}^{\mu}(\tau)
and
x_{2}^{\mu}(\tau)
in a general spacetime endowed with a metric
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{1},x_{2})=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}ds\sqrt{\pm g_{\mu\nu}(x){x}^{\prime\mu}{x}^{\prime\nu}}\>,

(5)

with
x_{1}^{\mu}(\tau)=x^{\mu}(s_{1}),\;x_{2}^{\mu}(\tau)=x^{\mu}(s_{2})
, where
x^{\mu}(s)
is the geodesic curve joining the two points,
s
is a parameter of the geodesic connecting the two points,
x^{\prime\mu}:=dx^{\mu}/ds
, and the positive (negative) sign is chosen if the geodesic is timelike (spacelike).
In particular,
\sigma^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=\pm r^{2}
holds for Minkowski space.
Hence, the system described by the action (
3
) can be interpreted as a system of two particles interacting with each other along the geodesic connecting them.
(For example, such a 2-particle system in a curved spacetime has been considered for the shockwave background in Ref.
[
5
]
.)

From this point of view, we generalize the action (
3
) to a general curved background as

\displaystyle S_{g}

\displaystyle=\int d\tau\left(-\sum^{2}_{i=1}\frac{1}{2}\frac{g_{\mu\nu}(x_{i})\dot{x}^{\mu}_{i}\dot{x}^{\nu}_{i}}{e_{i}}\mp\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\frac{\sqrt{e_{1}e_{2}}}{\sigma^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)\>,

(6)

where the negative (positive) sign is chosen if the two particles are timelike (spacelike) sepalated.
For a spacetime such that
\sigma^{2}\rightarrow\pm r^{2}
as
g_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\eta_{\mu\nu}
, this action smoothly reduces to the conformally invariant action (
3
) in Minkowski space.
However, it is noted that the generalized action (
6
) itself does not necessarily have conformal symmetry before taking such a limit.

S_{g}

]

This description enables us to express the geodesic distance in a simple form without taking a particular coordinate system of de Sitter space.
In Sec. 3, we consider the Hamiltonian formulation in accordance with Dirac’s procedure

for constrained systems.
In Sec. 4, new canonical variables constituting canonical pairs in the sense of Dirac brackets are introduced.
In Sec. 5, we apply the canonical quantization procedure in coordinate representation for curved configuration spaces following DeWitt

.
Sec. 6 is devoted to conclude this letter.

In this section, we specify the form of the action (

\displaystyle=-\sum^{2}_{i=1}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{X}^{2}_{i}}{e_{i}}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\frac{\sqrt{e_{1}e_{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}-\sum^{2}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}(X^{2}_{i}+R^{2})\>.

(15b)

5
Canonical quantization

We set the commutation relations between
\hat{f}
and
\hat{g}
corresponding to functions
f
and
g

\displaystyle[\hat{f}\,,\>\hat{g}]=i\widehat{\{f,\>g\}}_{\mathrm{D}}

(55)

in units
\hbar=1
. The symbol
\widehat{\{f,\>g\}}_{\mathrm{D}}
denotes the operator corresponding to the Dirac brackets
\{f,g\}_{\mathrm{D}}
.
From Eq. (
49

\displaystyle\big[\hat{x}_{i}^{\mu},\;\hat{p}_{j\nu}\big]

\displaystyle=i\delta_{ij}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\>,

\displaystyle\big[\hat{e},\;\hat{\pi}\big]

\displaystyle=i\>.

(56)

We consider the quantum mechanics characterized by the above commutation relations in the coordinate representation, where the configuration space is the de Sitter space endowed with the metric (
9
).
The coordinate representation for curved configuration spaces was considered by DeWitt
[
9
]
.
Following DeWitt, we take the simultaneous eigenstates of
\hat{x}_{i},\;\hat{e}

\displaystyle\hat{x}^{\mu}_{i}|x_{1},x_{2},e\rangle

\displaystyle=x_{i}^{\mu}|x_{1},x_{2},e\rangle\>,

(57)

\displaystyle\hat{e}|x_{1},x_{2},e\rangle

\displaystyle=e|x_{1},x_{2},e\rangle\>

(58)

as a basis of the Hilbert space.
Then we expand an arbitrary state
|\varphi\rangle
in terms of the basis vectors in the form

\displaystyle|\varphi\rangle=\int\Big(\prod_{i=1,2}\sqrt{-g(x_{i})}d^{4}x_{i}\Big)de\,\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},e)|x_{1},x_{2},e\,\rangle\>,

(59)

with
g(x)=\det g_{\mu\nu}(x)
and wave functions
\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},e)\equiv\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\varphi\rangle
.
In addition, we assume that the eigenvectors
|x_{1},x_{2},e\,\rangle
satisfy the normalization condition

\displaystyle\langle x^{\prime\prime}_{1},x^{\prime\prime}_{2},e^{\prime\prime}|x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},e^{\prime}\rangle=\delta(e^{\prime\prime}-e^{\prime})\prod_{i=1,2}\frac{\delta^{4}(x_{i}^{\prime\prime}-x_{i}^{\prime})}{\sqrt{-g(x_{i}^{\prime})}}\>,

(60)

which is consistent with the completeness relation

\displaystyle 1=\int\prod_{i=1,2}\sqrt{-g(x_{i})}d^{4}x_{i}de\,|x_{1},x_{2},e\rangle\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\>\>.

(61)

On the basis vectors satisfying Eq. (
60
), the momentum operators
\hat{p}_{i}{}_{\mu}
and
\hat{\pi}
can be represented
[
9
]
as

\displaystyle\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\hat{p}_{i\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}_{i}}-\frac{i}{2}\Gamma_{\mu}(x_{i})\right)\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\>,

(62)

\displaystyle\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\hat{\pi}

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial e}\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\>,

(63)

where
\Gamma_{\mu}(x):=\Gamma^{\nu}_{\nu\mu}
and the Christoffel symbol
\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}
is defined as

\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\sigma\tau}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\nu}}g_{\tau\mu}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}g_{\tau\nu}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\tau}}g_{\mu\nu}\right)\>.

For confirming the hermiticity of
\hat{p}_{i\mu}
, namely
\langle\psi|\hat{p}_{i\mu}\varphi\rangle=\langle\hat{p}_{i\mu}\psi|\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\hat{p}_{i\mu}=(-g(x_{i}))^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{\mu}}\right)(-g(x_{i}))^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle x_{1},x_{2},e|\>.

(64)

\displaystyle\square^{(dS)}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g(x_{i})}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{\mu}}\sqrt{-g(x_{i})}g^{\mu\nu}(x_{i})\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}^{\nu}}

\displaystyle=-(-g(x_{i}))^{-\frac{1}{4}}\hat{p}_{i\mu}\sqrt{-g(x_{i})}g^{\mu\nu}(x_{i})\hat{p}_{i\nu}(-g(x_{i}))^{-\frac{1}{4}}\>\>

(65)

in the
x

6
Conclusion

In this letter, we have generalized the conformally invariant action
S
, Eq. (
3
), for two interacting massless particles in Minkowski space to the case of de Sitter space from the perspective of geodesic distance.
To begin with, we have defined the action
S_{g}
, Eq. (
6
), as a generalization of
S
to general spacetimes that become close to Minkowski space in a certain limit.
After that, we have specified the action
S_{\mathrm{dS}}
, Eq. (
13
), as
S_{g}
for the case of de Sitter space in terms of the embedding coordinates
X_{i}^{A}
with the explicit expression of the geodesic distance (
11
) and also confirmed that
S_{\mathrm{dS}}
really reproduces
S
in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
with the coordinate chart (
8
).
In the Hamilton analysis, according to Dirac’s prescription
[
8
]
for the constrained Hamiltonian systems, we have carried out the classification of the constraints and have defined the Dirac bracket from the second-class constraints.
Then we have found that the new variables (
47
) constitute the canonically conjugate pairs in the sense of Dirac brackets.
Following DeWitt
[
9
]
, we have performed the subsequent canonical quantization of the system in coordinate representation where the configuration space is
\mathrm{dS}_{4}
endowed with the metric (
9
).
By choosing the operator ordering (
68
) for
\widehat{P}_{i}^{2}
, the physical state conditions (
66
) associated with the first-class constraints (
52
) has been read as the fourth-order differential equation (
72
) for bilocal fields
\varphi(x_{1},x_{2})
, which can be recognized as a de Sitter version of the wave equation (
4
) derived based on the action (
3
) in Ref.
[
1
]
.

It seems worthwhile to quantize the model governed by the action (
6
) in path integral formulation in curved spaces, which was studied for a nonrelativistic particle in Ref.
[
12
]
and for a relativistic particle in a manifestly covariant way in Ref.
[
13
]
.

It is also interesting to consider the dynamics of bilocal fields satisfying the wave equation (
72
), including to confirm the well-posedness of (
72
), which may possibly be relevant to higher derivative theories of scalar fields in curved spacetime
[
14
]
and/or higher spin theories
[
15
]
.

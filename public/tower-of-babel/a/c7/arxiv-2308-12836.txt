Abstract
. This article undertakes an analysis of the one-dimensional heat equation, wherein the Dirichlet condition is applied at the left end and Neumann condition at the right end. The heat equation is restructured as a non-self-adjoint 2 × 2 2\times 2 unbounded block operator matrix pencil. The spectral, pseudospectral, and ( n , ϵ ) (n,\epsilon) -pseudospectral enclosures of the 2 × 2 2\times 2 unbounded block operator matrix pencil are explored to scrutinize the heat operator pencil. The plots of the discretized equation are depicted to illustrate the observations.

1.
Introduction

The heat transfer through an ideal rod of length
d
with thermal diffusivity
c^{2}
subject to the Dirichlet condition at the left and Neumann condition at the right end is formulated as,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}},\ \ 0\leq x\leq d,\ \ t\geq 0,\ \ \phi(0,t)=\phi_{x}(d,t)=0.

The domain of the solution (one-dimensional heat equation) is a semi-infinite strip of width
d
that continues indefinitely in time. The probabilistic approach, variational iteration, finite difference, and finite element are among the few methods used to study the one-dimensional heat equation; see
[
2
,
12
,
16
,
22
]
. The one-dimensional heat equation is reformulated in this article as a linear evolution process on a suitable Hilbert space. The closed linear operator pencil arising from the heat equation is illustrated as follows. Let
u=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\phi\\
\phi_{x}\end{pmatrix}
, then the system (
1.1
) is converted into

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}u_{t}=\mathcal{A}u,\ \ \mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}c^{2}\cfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}&&\\
&&\end{pmatrix},\ \ \mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
\cfrac{\partial}{\partial x}&-I\end{pmatrix},\ \ u_{1}(0,t)=u_{2}(d,t)=0.

Suppose
u
is variably separable and let

u(x,t)=e^{\lambda t}\psi(x)=e^{\lambda t}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}(x)\\
\psi_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Then the system (
1.2
) is converted into the generalized eigenvalue problem of the form

(1.3)

\displaystyle(\lambda B-A)\psi=0,\ \ A=\begin{pmatrix}c^{2}\cfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}&&\\
&&\end{pmatrix},\ \ B=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
\cfrac{d}{dx}&-I\end{pmatrix},\ \ \psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(d)=0.

Thus, the one-dimensional heat equation defined in (
1.1
) is transformed into a linear operator pencil, a
2\times 2
unbounded block operator matrix pencil. If
(\lambda B-A)\psi=0
for some
\psi\neq 0
, then
\lambda
is called the generalized eigenvalue and
\psi
is the corresponding generalized eigenfunction. The domains
D(A)
and
D(B)
are suitable subspaces of the Hilbert space
L^{2}[0,d]\times L^{2}[0,d]
defined by

\displaystyle D(A)

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{2}[0,d]:v^{\prime}\ \textnormal{is absolutely continous},\ v^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}[0,d]\right\}\times L^{2}[0,d],

and

\displaystyle D(B)

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{2}[0,d]:v\ \textnormal{is absolutely continous},\ v^{\prime}\in L^{2}[0,d]\right\}\times L^{2}[0,d].

Note that
B^{2}=I
on
D(B)
and for
\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in L^{2}[0,d]\times L^{2}[0,d]
,

\displaystyle\|\psi\|^{2}=\int_{0}^{d}\left(\left|\psi_{1}(x)\right|^{2}+\left|\psi_{2}(x)\right|^{2}\right)\ dx.

In
[
11
]
, Nagel introduces the matrix theory for unbounded operator matrices. One can find vast information about block operator matrices in
[
21
]
. This article focuses on the pseudospectral study of closed linear operator pencil and
2\times 2
unbounded block operator matrix pencil to analyze the one-dimensional heat equation.

1.1.
Background and Outline

The operators that arise from the physical applications are closed but unbounded, and due to this, closed operators gained attention in the mathematical field. For more about closed operators, refer
[
7
,
8
]
. Throughout this article,
\mathbb{H}
denotes a complex Hilbert space, and
I,{\bf 0}
are the identity operator, zero operators on
\mathbb{H}
. Further
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H}),\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
are the set of all closed, bounded operators on
\mathbb{H}
. The domain of
A\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
is denoted as
D(A)
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then the closed linear operator pencil

(A,B)(\lambda)=\lambda B-A

is defined on
D(A)\cap D(B)
. If both
A
and
B
are self-adjoint, then
(A,B)
is called a self-adjoint pencil. For more information on self-adjoint operator pencils, see
[
10
]
. The generalized resolvent of
(A,B)
is defined by

\rho(A,B)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:(\lambda B-A)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})\}.

The spectrum of
(A,B)
is defined by
\sigma(A,B)=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(A,B)
. The generalized eigenvalues of
(A,B)
is defined by

\sigma_{e}(A,B)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda B-A\ \textnormal{is not one-one}\}.

The spectral analysis of operators fails to provide the correct information while dealing with non-normal operators. During the 1990s, Trefethen advocated the concept of pseudospectrum in the context of non-normal operators resulting from the discretization of certain non-symmetric differential operators; see
[
18
,
19
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
and
\epsilon>0
, the
\epsilon
-pseudospectrum of
A
is defined by

\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A)=\sigma(A)\cup\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left\|(\lambda I-A)^{-1}\right\|\geq\epsilon^{-1}\right\}.

Pseudospectra has been extensively studied due to its applications in numerical analysis and differential equations. It is used to study the norm behavior of
A^{n}
(
n=1,2,\ldots
) and
e^{tA}
(
t>0
), analyze iterative methods for the solutions of
Ax=b
, and in stability analysis of the method of lines discretizations of time-dependent partial differential equations; see
[
13
,
18
,
20
]
. Over the periods, pseudospectrum has been studied for various differential operators like wave, convection-diffusion, and Orr-Sommerfield operators; see
[
3
,
14
,
15
]
. Pseudospectra provides more stable information about non-self-adjoint operators under various limiting procedures than the spectrum; see
[
1
]
. Later on, Hansen introduced the more general
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectra for linear operators on separable Hilbert spaces and pointed out that they have numerous attractive features with pseudospectra but offer a better insight into the approximation of the spectrum; see
[
4
,
5
]
. The
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectrum of bounded linear operator pencils is studied in
[
9
]
.

The article is organized as follows. Section 1 illustrates the formulation of the one-dimensional heat equation into a closed linear operator pencil. The suitable substitution and the separation of variables transform the one-dimensional heat equation to a
2\times 2
unbounded block operator matrix pencil. Section 2 studies the
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectra of closed linear operator pencil. Several important properties of the
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectrum of a closed linear operator pencil are developed. The equivalent definitions for the
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectra of closed linear operator pencil and the characterization of the
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectra of self-adjoint operator pencil are done. Section 3 concerns the
2\times 2
unbounded block operator matrix pencil. Using the generalized Frobenius-Schur factorization, we furnish the spectral, pseudospectral, and
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectral enclosures for
2\times 2
unbounded block operator matrix pencil. Section 4 identifies the heat operator pencil as a non-self-adjoint pencil. The eigenvalue analysis and the pseudospectral enclosure of the heat operator pencil are done. The pseudospectrum of discretized heat equation is plotted to illustrate the results.

2.
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudopsectra of the closed linear operator pencil

This section studies the
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectrum of a closed linear operator pencil for a general purpose. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denote the set of all positive integers. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
,
D(z,r):=\{\lambda:|\lambda-z|\leq r\}
and
D(0,r)
is denoted as
\Delta_{r}
. For
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
,
\overline{\Omega}:=\{\overline{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Omega\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
. The
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectrum of
(A,B)
is denoted by
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)

and is defined by

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)=\sigma(A,B)\cup\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}\right\}.

The generalized
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudoresolvent of
(A,B)
is denoted by
\rho_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
and is defined by

\rho_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)=\rho(A,B)\cap\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}<\epsilon^{-1}\right\}.

The following observations about the pseudospectra of closed linear operator pencil are trivial.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
. Then

(1)

\sigma(A,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
.

(2)

If
B=I
, then
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,I)=\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A)
.

(3)

If
n=0
, then
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)=\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
and
\epsilon>0
. Then
\sigma(A+C,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)
for every
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
with
\|C\|\leq\epsilon
.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda\in\sigma(A+C,B)
and
\lambda\notin\sigma(A,B)
, then

\lambda B-A-C=\left(\lambda B-A\right)\left(I-\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{-1}C\right).

It follows that

1\leq\left\|\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{-1}C\right\|\leq\left\|\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{-1}\right\|\left\|C\right\|.

Hence
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)
for
\|C\|\leq\epsilon
.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
. Then the following holds.

(1)

\Lambda_{n+1,\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
.

(2)

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon_{1}}(A,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{n,\epsilon_{2}}(A,B)
for every
0<\epsilon_{1}\leq\epsilon_{2}
.

(3)

\displaystyle\sigma(A,B)=\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
.

(4)

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)+\Delta_{\delta}\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)+\Delta_{\delta}\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon+\delta\|B\|}(A,B)
for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
and
\delta>0
.

(5)

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(\alpha A,\alpha B)=\Lambda_{n,\frac{\epsilon}{|\alpha|}}(A,B)
for every
\alpha\neq 0
.

(6)

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(\beta A+\alpha B,B)=\alpha+\beta\,\Lambda_{n,\frac{\epsilon}{|\beta|}}(A,B)
for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\beta\neq 0.

Proof.

(1)

Suppose
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n+1,\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
. Then

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-1}\leq\left\|[(\lambda B-A)^{-1}]^{2^{n+1}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|[(\lambda B-A)^{-1}]^{2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}}\left\|[(\lambda B-A)^{-1}]^{2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|[(\lambda B-A)^{-1}]^{2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}.

Hence
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
.

(2)

Let
0<\epsilon_{1}\leq\epsilon_{2}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon_{1}}(A,B)
. If
\lambda\in\sigma(A,B)
, then
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon_{2}}(A,B)
. If
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon_{1}}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
, then

\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon_{1}^{-1}\geq\epsilon_{2}^{-1}.

Hence
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon_{2}}(A,B)
.

(3)

If
\lambda\in\sigma(A,B)
, then
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
for every
\epsilon>0
and
\displaystyle\lambda\in\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
. Next assume that
\displaystyle\lambda\in\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
and
\lambda\notin\sigma(A,B)
, then

\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}\ \ \ \ \text{for every}\ \epsilon>0.

Hence
\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|=0
, a contradiction.

(4)

From (1),
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)
and hence
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)+\Delta_{\delta}\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)+\Delta_{\delta}
. Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)
,
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, and
|\mu|\leq\delta
. If
\lambda\in\sigma(A,B)
, from Lemma
2.3
,

\lambda+\mu\in\sigma(A+\mu B,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{\delta\|B\|}(A,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon+\delta\|B\|}(A,B).

If
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
and
\lambda+\mu\in\sigma(A,B)
, then
\lambda+\mu\in\Lambda_{\epsilon+\delta\|B\|}(A,B)
. Further if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
and
(\lambda+\mu)B-A
is invertible. Choose
x\in D(A)
with
\|x\|=1
such that
\|(\lambda B-A)x\|\leq\epsilon
, then

\displaystyle 1=\|x\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\left(\lambda+\mu\right)B-A\right)^{-1}\left(\left(\lambda+\mu\right)B-A\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\epsilon+\delta\|B\|\right)\left\|\left(\left(\lambda+\mu\right)B-A\right)^{-1}\right\|.

Hence
\lambda+\mu\in\Lambda_{\epsilon+\delta\|B\|}(A,B)
.

(5)

Let
\alpha\neq 0
, then
\sigma(\alpha A,\alpha B)=\sigma(A,B)
. Also

\displaystyle\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(\alpha A,\alpha B)\setminus\sigma(\alpha A,\alpha B)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\|(\lambda\alpha B-\alpha A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\frac{|\alpha|}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\frac{\epsilon}{|\alpha|}}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B).

(6)

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
\beta\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\rho(\beta A+\alpha B,B)

\displaystyle=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left(\lambda B-\beta A-\alpha B\right)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left(\frac{\lambda-\alpha}{\beta}B-A\right)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})\right\}.

i.e.,
\lambda\in\sigma(\beta A+\alpha B,B)\Longleftrightarrow\frac{\lambda-\alpha}{\beta}\in\sigma(A,B)\Longleftrightarrow\lambda\in\alpha+\beta\,\sigma(A,B).
Also

\displaystyle\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(\beta A+\alpha B,B)\setminus\sigma(\beta A+\alpha B,B)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\|(\lambda B-\beta A-\alpha B)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \frac{1}{|\beta|}\left\|\left(\frac{\lambda-\alpha}{\beta}B-A\right)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \frac{\lambda-\alpha}{\beta}\in\Lambda_{n,\frac{\epsilon}{|\beta|}}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ \lambda\in\alpha+\beta\,\Lambda_{n,\frac{\epsilon}{|\beta|}}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B).

∎

The following theorem presents some equivalent definitions for
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectra of closed linear operator pencil.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

z\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
.

(2)

z\in\sigma(A,B)\cup\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\,\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{2^{n}}u\right\|\leq{\epsilon}^{2^{n}},u\in D(A)\cap D(B),\|u\|=1\right\}.

(3)

z\in\sigma(A,B)\cup\Big\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\left[(\lambda B-A)^{2^{n}}-E\right]u=0,\|E\|\leq{\epsilon}^{2^{n}},u\in D(A)\cap D(B),\|u\|=1\Big\}.

Proof.

(1)
\Longrightarrow
(2). If
z\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
, then
\left\|(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}
also there exists
x\in D(A)\cap D(B)
with
\|x\|=1
such that
\left\|(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}x\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}
. Define
v=(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}x
and
\displaystyle u=\frac{v}{\|v\|}
. Then
v\neq 0
and

\left\|(zB-A)^{2^{n}}\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|=\frac{\|x\|}{\left\|(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}x\right\|}\leq\epsilon^{2^{n}}.

(2)
\Longrightarrow
(3). Suppose
\left\|(zB-A)^{2^{n}}u\right\|\leq{\epsilon}^{2^{n}}
for some
u\in D(A)\cap D(B)
with
\|u\|=1
. Then there exists
\phi\in\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
such that
\|\phi\|=1
and
\phi(u)=\|u\|=1
. Define rank one operator
E:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}

E(x)=\phi(x)(zB-A)^{2^{n}}u.

Then
\|E\|\leq{\epsilon}^{2^{n}}
and
\left[(zB-A)^{2^{n}}-E\right]u=0
.

(3)
\Longrightarrow
(1). Suppose
\left[(zB-A)^{2^{n}}-E\right]u=0
for some
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
with
\|E\|\leq{\epsilon}^{2^{n}}
and
u\in D(A)\cap D(B)
with
\|u\|=1
. For
z\notin\sigma(A,B)
, we have
u=(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}Eu
, also

\displaystyle 1=\|u\|=\left\|(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}Eu\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(zB-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|{\epsilon}^{2^{n}}.

Thus
z\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B).

∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
and
B(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}
is bounded for some
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(A,B)
. Then

(\lambda B-A)^{-1}=(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right)^{m}\left[B(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\right]^{m},

for every
\lambda
with
\displaystyle\left|\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right|<\frac{1}{\left\|B(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\right\|}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\rho(A,B)
, then

\displaystyle(\lambda B-A)^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\left[\left(A-\lambda_{0}B\right)-\left(\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right)B\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\left(A-\lambda_{0}B\right)^{-1}\left[I-\left(\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right)B\left(A-\lambda_{0}B\right)^{-1}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\left[I-\left(\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right)B(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\right]^{-1}.

If
\displaystyle\left|\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right|<\frac{1}{\left\|B(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\right\|}
, then

(\lambda B-A)^{-1}=(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right)^{m}\left[B(\lambda_{0}B-A)^{-1}\right]^{m}.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
and
B(\lambda B-A)^{-1}
is bounded for every
\lambda\in\rho(A,B)
. Then the resolvent function of the operator pencil
(A,B)
is analytic on
\rho(A,B)
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\rho(A,B)
,

(\lambda B-A)^{-1}-(\mu B-A)^{-1}=(\mu-\lambda)(\lambda B-A)^{-1}B(\mu B-A)^{-1}.

Then

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\mu}\frac{(\lambda B-A)^{-1}-(\mu B-A)^{-1}}{\lambda-\mu}=-(\mu B-A)^{-1}B(\mu B-A)^{-1}.

∎

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
. If
B(\lambda B-A)^{-1}
is bounded for every
\lambda\in\rho(A,B)
, then

(i)

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

Any bounded component of
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
has non empty intersection with
\sigma(A,B)
.

Proof.

(i)

From Theorem
2.6
,
\sigma(A,B)
is closed. Define
f:\rho(A,B)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
by

f(\lambda)=\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}.

Then
f
is continuous and

\left\{\lambda\in\rho(A,B):\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}\right\}=f^{-1}\left(\left[\epsilon^{-1},\infty\right)\right),

is closed. Hence
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
is closed.

(ii)

Suppose
\Omega
be a bounded component of
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)
such that
\Omega\cap\sigma(A,B)=\emptyset
. Then

\Omega\subseteq\rho(A,B)\cap\left\{\lambda\in\rho(A,B):\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}\right\}.

Let
G=\Omega\cap\left\{\lambda\in\rho(A,B):\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|>\epsilon^{-{2^{n}}}\right\}
. We claim that
G
is open. Let
\mu\in G
, since
\mu\in\left\{\lambda\in\rho(A,B):\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|>\epsilon^{-{2^{n}}}\right\}
, there exists
r_{\mu}>0
, such that
D(\mu,r_{\mu})\subset\left\{\lambda\in\rho(A,B):\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|>\epsilon^{-{2^{n}}}\right\}.
Since
\Omega
is component,
\mu\in\Omega
and
D(\mu,r_{\mu})
is connected,
D(\mu,r_{\mu})\subset\Omega.
Hence
D(\mu,r_{\mu})\subset G
. Now the map

\lambda\mapsto\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|,

defined from
G
to
\mathbb{C}
is subharmonic and continuous on
\overline{G}
. For any boundary point
\lambda\in G
,
\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|=\epsilon^{-{2^{n}}}
. But for
\lambda\in G
,
\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|>\epsilon^{-{2^{n}}}
. This contradicts the maximum principle.

∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
such that
D(A),D(B),D(A+B),D(AB)
are dense in
\mathbb{H}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
.

(i)

If
A
or
B
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
, then
\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A^{*},B^{*})=\overline{\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A^{*},B^{*})}
.

(ii)

If
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
, then
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A^{*},B^{*})=\overline{\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A^{*},B^{*})}
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
A
or
B
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
, then
A^{*}+B^{*}=\left(A+B\right)^{*}
; see
[
17
]
. If
\lambda\in\sigma(A,B)
then
\overline{\lambda}\in\sigma(A^{*},B^{*})
. Further if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
, then

\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-1}\right\|=\left\|\left(\ \overline{\lambda}B^{*}-A^{*}\right)^{-1}\right\|\geq\epsilon^{-1}.

The latest step follows as
(\lambda B-A)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
; see
[
18
]
. Hence
\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\overline{\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A^{*},B^{*})}
. Since
D(A),D(B)
are dense in
\mathbb{H}
, the other inclusion follows.

(ii)

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
, then
(A+B)^{*}=A^{*}+B^{*}
and
B^{*}A^{*}=\left(AB\right)^{*}
; see
[
17
]
. If
\lambda\in\sigma(A,B)
then
\overline{\lambda}\in\sigma(A^{*},B^{*})
. Further if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\setminus\sigma(A,B)
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{H})
,

\left(\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{-2^{n}}\right)^{*}=\left(\left(\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{2^{n}}\right)^{-1}\right)^{*}=\left(\left(\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{2^{n}}\right)^{*}\right)^{-1}=\left(\overline{\lambda}B^{*}-A^{*}\right)^{-2^{n}}.

Thus

\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}=\left\|\left(\left(\lambda B-A\right)^{-2^{n}}\right)^{*}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}=\left\|\left(\ \overline{\lambda}B^{*}-A^{*}\right)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\geq\epsilon^{-1}.

Hence
\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\overline{\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A^{*},B^{*})}
. Since
D(A),D(B)
are dense in
\mathbb{H}
, the other inclusion follows.

∎

The following theorem characterizes the
(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectrum of a self-adjoint operator pencil.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
be self-adjoint operators with
B
invertible and
AB=BA
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
and
\epsilon>0
,

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\sigma(A,B)+\Delta_{\epsilon\left\|B^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}+\Delta_{\epsilon\left\|B^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}.

Proof.

Suppose
B
is invertible and self-adjoint. Then
B^{-1}A
is self-adjoint and
\sigma(A,B)=\sigma(B^{-1}A)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
; see
[
8
]
. Since
AB=BA
, for
\lambda\notin\sigma(A,B)
, (refer
[
7
]
)

\left\|(\lambda B-A)^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\leq\left\|B^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\left\|(\lambda I-B^{-1}A)^{-1}\right\|=\frac{\left\|B^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}{\text{dist}\left(\lambda,\sigma(B^{-1}A)\right)}.

Thus

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\sigma(A,B)+\Delta_{\epsilon\left\|B^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}+\Delta_{\epsilon\left\|B^{-2^{n}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}.

∎

Corollary 2.11
.

If
A\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
is self-adjoint,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
\epsilon>0
. Then

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(A)\subseteq\sigma(A)+\Delta_{\epsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{R}+\Delta_{\epsilon}.

The transformation of the one-dimensional heat equation into
2\times 2

(n,\epsilon)
-pseudospectral enclosures of the
2\times 2
unbounded block operator pencil. The following provides the generalized Frobenius-Schur factorization for
2\times 2
unbounded block operator matrix pencil. For more on Frobenius-Schur factorization of block operator matrices, see
[
6
,
21
]
. Consider the
2\times 2
block operator matrices with the entries as closed operators (unbounded operators) on
\mathbb{H}
. Let
A_{i},B_{i}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{H})
(
i=1,2,3,4
),

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&A_{2}\\
A_{3}&A_{4}\end{pmatrix}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\mathcal{B}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}&B_{2}\\
B_{3}&B_{4}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle=\left(D(A_{1})\cap D(A_{3})\right)\times\left(D(A_{2})\cap D(A_{4})\right),

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle=\left(D(B_{1})\cap D(B_{3})\right)\times\left(D(B_{2})\cap D(B_{4})\right).

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then

(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})(\lambda)=\lambda\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}&\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\\
\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}&\lambda B_{4}-A_{4}\end{pmatrix}

is defined on
D(\mathcal{A})\cap D(\mathcal{B})
. If
\lambda\in\rho(A_{4},B_{4})
, then

(3.1)

\lambda\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}I&F_{1}(\lambda)\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda B_{1}-S_{1}(\lambda)&\\
&\lambda B_{4}-A_{4}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
G_{1}(\lambda)&I\end{pmatrix},

where

, and
G_{1}(\lambda)=\left(\lambda B_{4}-A_{4}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)
. This factorization is called the generalized Frobenius-Schur factorization along the first complement. If
\lambda\in\rho(A_{1},B_{1})

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
F_{2}(\lambda)&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}&\\
&\lambda B_{4}-S_{2}(\lambda)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&G_{2}(\lambda)\\
&I\end{pmatrix},

, and

(L1)

(A_{4},B_{4})
is closed and densely defined,

(H3)

for every

\delta_{2}=\left\|\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right\|
.

Proof.

Suppose
\lambda\in\rho(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
, from Theorem
3.2
,

\left(\lambda\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}\right)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&-G_{2}(\lambda)\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}&\\
&\left(\lambda B_{4}-S_{2}(\lambda)\right)^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
-F_{2}(\lambda)&I\end{pmatrix}.

If
G_{2}(\lambda)F_{2}(\lambda)=F_{2}(\lambda)G_{2}(\lambda)=0
,
G_{2}(\lambda)(\lambda B_{4}-S_{2}(\lambda))^{-1}=(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1})^{-1}G_{2}(\lambda)
, and
F_{2}(\lambda)(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1})^{-1}=(\lambda B_{4}-S_{2}(\lambda))^{-1}F_{2}(\lambda)
, then the decomposition matrices are mutually commutative. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\left(\lambda\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}\right)^{-2^{n}}=\begin{pmatrix}I&G_{2}(\lambda)^{2^{n}}\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-2^{n}}&\\
&\left(\lambda B_{4}-S_{2}(\lambda)\right)^{-2^{n}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
F_{2}(\lambda)^{2^{n}}&I\end{pmatrix}.

Also

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}I&G_{2}(\lambda)^{2^{n}}\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\begin{pmatrix}I&\left(\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\right)\right)^{2^{n}}\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\right\|+\left\|\begin{pmatrix}&&\left(\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\right)\right)^{2^{n}}\\
&&\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left\|\left(\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\right)\right)^{2^{n}}\right\|.

Similarly

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
F_{2}(\lambda)^{2^{n}}&I\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
\left(\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right)^{2^{n}}&I\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\right\|+\left\|\begin{pmatrix}&&\\
\left(\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right)^{2^{n}}&&\end{pmatrix}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\left\|\left(\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right)^{2^{n}}\right\|.

Denote
\delta_{1}=\left\|\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\right)\right\|
and
\delta_{2}=\left\|\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right\|
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\right)\right)^{2^{n}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\left(\lambda B_{2}-A_{2}\right)\right\|^{2^{n}}=\delta_{1}^{2^{n}},

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right)^{2^{n}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\lambda B_{3}-A_{3}\right)\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-1}\right\|^{2^{n}}=\delta_{2}^{2^{n}}.

Combining the above inequalities,

\left\|\left(\lambda\mathcal{B}-\mathcal{A}\right)^{-2^{n}}\right\|\leq\left(1+\delta_{1}^{2^{n}}\right)\left(1+\delta_{2}^{2^{n}}\right)\max\left\{\left\|\left(\lambda B_{1}-A_{1}\right)^{-{2^{n}}}\right\|,\left\|\left(\lambda B_{4}-S_{2}(\lambda)\right)^{-{2^{n}}}\right\|\right\}.

Hence for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\epsilon>0
,

\Lambda_{n,\epsilon}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\subseteq\left(\Lambda_{n,\epsilon\left(1+\delta_{1}^{2^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\left(1+\delta_{2}^{2^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}\left(A_{1},B_{1}\right)\right)\cup\left(\Lambda_{n,\epsilon\left(1+\delta_{1}^{2^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}\left(1+\delta_{2}^{2^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{n}}}}\left(S_{2}(\lambda),B_{4}\right)\right).

∎

4.
Pseudospectra of the one-dimensional heat operator pencil

4.1.
Eigenvalue analysis

Consider the heat transfer through a rod of thermal diffusivity
c^{2}
and length
d
. The reformulation of the one-dimensional heat equation subject to the Dirichlet condition at the left and Neumann condition at the right end, along with the variable separable condition, leads to
2\times 2
block operator matrix pencil
(A,B)
, where

(4.1)

A=\begin{pmatrix}c^{2}\cfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}&&\\
&&\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ B=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
\cfrac{d}{dx}&-I\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\lambda
be a generalized eigenvalue and
\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}
be the corresponding generalized eigenfunction of the heat operator pencil, then the boundary conditions give
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(d)=0
. The adjoint of
A
and
B
are

A^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}c^{2}\cfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}&&\\
&&\end{pmatrix}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ B^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}I&\cfrac{d}{dx}\\
&-I\end{pmatrix}.

The domains
D(A^{*}),D(B^{*})
are dense subspaces of Hilbert space
L^{2}[0,d]\times L^{2}[0,d]
defined by

\displaystyle D(A^{*})

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L^{2}[0,d]:v^{\prime}\ \textnormal{is absolutely continous},\ v^{\prime\prime}\in L^{2}[0,d]\right\}\times L^{2}[0,d],

\displaystyle D(B^{*})

\displaystyle=L^{2}[0,d]\times\left\{v\in L^{2}[0,d]:v\ \textnormal{is absolutely continous},\ v^{\prime}\in L^{2}[0,d]\right\}.

The
2\times 2
block operator matrix pencil
(A,B)
corresponding to the one-dimensional heat equation is non-self-adjoint. The generalized eigenvalues and the generalized eigenfunctions of
(A,B)
are determined as follows. Let
\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}
, then

\displaystyle(\lambda B-A)\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-c^{2}\cfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}&\\
\lambda\cfrac{d}{dx}&-\lambda I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Thus
(\lambda B-A)\psi=0
gives two simultaneous differential equations

\displaystyle c^{2}\cfrac{d^{2}\psi_{1}}{dx^{2}}=\lambda\psi_{1}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \lambda\cfrac{d\psi_{1}}{dx}=\lambda\psi_{2}.

By solving the above system of equations, we get

\psi_{1}(x)=\left(\alpha e^{\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}x}{c}}+\beta e^{-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}x}{c}}\right)\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\psi_{2}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{c}\left(\alpha e^{\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}x}{c}}-\beta e^{-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}x}{c}}\right),

for some arbitary constants
\alpha,\beta
. The boundary conditions are
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(d)=0
and hence
\lambda=0
can not be an eigenvalue. The boundary condition
\psi_{1}(0)=0
implies

\psi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\sinh\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}x}{c}\\
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{c}\,\cosh\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}x}{c}\end{pmatrix},

where
\lambda
is the non-zero generalized eigenvalue of the operator pencil
(A,B)
. The condition
\psi_{2}(d)=0
implies
\cosh\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}d}{c}=0
and

\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}=c\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\pi i}{d}.

Hence the generalized eigenvalues of the operator pencil
(A,B)
are

\lambda_{n}=-c^{2}\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{d}\right)^{2},

and the corresponding generalized eigenfunctions are

\psi^{n}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\sinh\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}x}{c}\\
\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}}{c}\,\cosh\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}x}{c}\end{pmatrix}.

4.2.
Psuedospectral analysis

This section identifies the pseudospectral enclosure of the heat operator pencil
(A,B)
using Theorem
3.4
. Denote
D=\cfrac{d}{dx}
. The generalized Frobenius-Schur factorization along the first complement decomposes the heat operator pencil
(A,B)
into

\lambda B-A=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}&\\
&-\lambda I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
-D&I\end{pmatrix}.

The generalized Frobenius-Schur factorization along the second complement decomposes the heat operator pencil
(A,B)
into

\lambda B-A=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
\lambda D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}&\\
&-\lambda I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
&I\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(A,B)
be the heat operator pencil and
\epsilon>0
. Then

\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq c^{2}\sigma(D^{2})+\Delta_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})},

where
\delta_{1}=|\lambda|\left\|D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}\right\|
and
\Delta_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})\}
.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,

\lambda B-A=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-c^{2}\cfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}&\\
\lambda\cfrac{d}{dx}&-\lambda I\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}&\\
\lambda D&-\lambda I\end{pmatrix}

For
\lambda\in\rho(D^{2})
, the generalized Frobenius-Schur factorization along the second complement decomposes the heat operator pencil
(A,B)
into

\lambda B-A=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
\lambda D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}&\\
&-\lambda I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
&I\end{pmatrix}

From Theorem
3.2
, for
\lambda\in\rho(D^{2})
,

(\lambda B-A)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2})^{-1}&\\
&-\cfrac{1}{\lambda}I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&\\
-\lambda D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}&I\end{pmatrix}.

From Green’s function, for
f\in L^{2}[0,d]
,

\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}(f)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}c}\int_{x}^{d}\left(e^{\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}(x-t)}{c}}-e^{-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}(x-t)}{c}}\right)\,f(t)\ dt.

Then

\displaystyle D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2c^{2}}\int_{x}^{d}\left(e^{\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}(x-t)}{c}}+e^{-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}(x-t)}{c}}\right)\,f(t)\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2c}\left[\frac{1}{c}\int_{x}^{d}e^{\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}(x-t)}{c}}f(t)\,dt+\frac{1}{c}\int_{x}^{d}e^{-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}(x-t)}{c}}f(t)\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2c}\left[\left(\sqrt{\lambda}I-cD\right)^{-1}(f)+\left(\sqrt{\lambda}I+cD\right)^{-1}(f)\right].

Hence

\lambda D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\lambda}{2c}\left[\left(\sqrt{\lambda}I-cD\right)^{-1}+\left(\sqrt{\lambda}I+cD\right)^{-1}\right].

From Theorem
3.4
,

\Lambda_{\epsilon}(A,B)\subseteq\Lambda_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}\left(c^{2}D^{2}\right)\cup\Lambda_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}({\bf 0})=c^{2}\,\Lambda_{\frac{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}{c^{2}}}\left(D^{2}\right)\cup\Delta_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})},

where
\delta_{1}=\left\|\lambda D\left(\lambda I-c^{2}D^{2}\right)^{-1}\right\|
and
\Delta_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})\right\}
. Since
D^{2}
is self-adjoint and
0\in\sigma(D^{2})
, from Corollary
2.11
,

c^{2}\,\Lambda_{\frac{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}{c^{2}}}\left(D^{2}\right)\cup\Delta_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}=c^{2}\sigma(D^{2})+\Delta_{\epsilon(1+\delta_{1})}.

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
v=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in L^{2}[0,d]\times L^{2}[0,d]
. Define

\displaystyle w(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{-v_{1}(-x)+v_{2}(-x)}{\sqrt{2}},&-d\leq x\leq 0,\\
\frac{v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x)}{\sqrt{2}},&0\leq x\leq d.\end{cases}

Then
w\in L^{2}[-d,d]
and
\|w\|=\|v\|
.

Proof.

Suppose
v_{1},v_{2}\in L^{2}[0,d]
and
w
be defined as above, then

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-d}^{d}\left|v(x)\right|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-d}^{0}\left|-v_{1}(-x)+v_{2}(-x)\right|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int^{d}_{0}\left|v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{d}^{0}\left|-v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x)\right|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int^{d}_{0}\left|v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int^{d}_{0}\left|-v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x)\right|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int^{d}_{0}\left|v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x)\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int^{d}_{0}\left|v_{1}(x)\right|^{2}+\left|v_{2}(x)\right|^{2}dx=\|v\|^{2}.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

Consider the heat equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=c^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x^{2}},\ \ 0\leq x\leq d,\ \ t\geq 0,\ \ \phi(0,t)=\phi_{x}(d,t)=0.

Suppose
\phi

\phi(x,t)=\phi_{1}(x)\,\phi_{2}(t)

\xi\in L^{2}[-d,d]

\xi(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{-\phi_{1}(-x)+\phi_{1}^{\prime}(-x)}{\sqrt{2}},&-d\leq x\leq 0,\\

4.2

\phi_{0}^{j}=0

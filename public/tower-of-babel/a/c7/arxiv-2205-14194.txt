On sums of hyper-Kloosterman Sums
Jack Buttcane

Abstract
. A formula of Kuznetsov allows one to interpret a smooth sum of Kloosterman sums as a sum over the spectrum of G ​ L ​ ( 2 ) GL(2) automorphic forms. In this paper, we construct a similar formula for the first hyper-Kloosterman sums using G ​ L ​ ( 3 ) GL(3) automorphic forms, resolving a long-standing problem of Bump, Friedberg and Goldfeld. Along the way, we develop what are apparently new bounds for the order derivatives of the classical J J -Bessel function, and we conclude with a discussion of the original method of Bump, Friedberg and Goldfeld.

In 1988, Bump, Friedberg and Goldfeld (BFG) published a paper
[
7
]
detailing the Fourier and spectral expansions of a spherical Poincaré series for
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
.
At the end of that paper, they theorized that one could isolate the contribution of the hyper-Kloosterman sum terms from that of the remaining Weyl cells.
Their particular method does not appear to be effective, but this idea has led to the long-standing goal of providing a spectral interpretation for a sum of hyper-Kloosterman sums.
In this paper, we provide one possible spectral expansion; the representation is not unique.

The desire to study such Poincaré series comes from the success of Kuznetsov’s formulas for
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
.
Kuznetsov was attempting to address a conjecture of Linnik
[
29
]
on the average of the classical Kloosterman sums

\operatorname{Kl}_{2}(m,n,c)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c)\\
xy\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{mx+ny}{c}\right),\qquad e\left(x\right):=\exp(2\pi ix),

which states that there should be good cancellation among the terms of an average over the moduli
c
.
Kuznetsov solved this problem in
[
25
]
by relating the spectral and Bruhat expansions of (Fourier coefficients of) Poincaré series over
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
to obtain a pair of formulas of the shape

\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Kl}_{2}(m,n,c)}{c}F_{1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{c}\right)+\text{trivial term}=\sum_{\phi}\frac{\lambda_{\phi}(n)\overline{\lambda_{\phi}(m)}}{L(1,{\mathrm{Ad}}^{2}\phi)}F_{2}(\mu_{\phi})+\text{continuous term},

where the sum on the right is over a basis of (minimal-weight) Maass cusp forms for
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
with Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{\phi}(m)
and spectral parameters
\mu_{\phi}
, and the (mostly) arbitrary test functions
F_{1},F_{2}
are related by Bessel or inverse-Bessel transforms (giving two formulas).
Kuznetsov’s formulas are now fundamental to modern number theory as one of very few examples where we can detect cancellation in an average over the moduli of an exponential sum.

The paper
[
7
]
of BFG determined explicitly the Bruhat decomposition of (Fourier coefficients of) an
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
Poincaré series, which now contains not just one, but several different exponential sums corresponding to the elements of the Weyl group (essentially the permutation group on three letters).
The papers
[
11
,
15
,
16
]
used this to construct the generalizations of the spectral (i.e. arbitrary test functions on the spectral side) Kuznetsov formula for
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
.

At the end of
[
7
]
, the authors conjectured that a properly constructed kernel function would isolate the different Weyl-element terms in the Fourier coefficients of the corresponding Poincaré series; we address their proposed method in
section

8
.
The paper
[
8
]
was able to accomplish this for the long Weyl element, using an idea of Cogdell and Piatetski-Shapiro
[
17
]
, the end result being a Kuznetsov-type formula having the (reasonably) arbitrary test function on the sum of long-element Kloosterman sums and no other Weyl element terms.
(The paper
[
14
]
did this first, in a special case and using a different tactic.)

The long-element arithmetic Kuznetsov formula of
[
8
]
is a significant advancement in that it shows we can construct such formulas outside the setting of rank-one groups (i.e.
SL(2)
vs a rank-two group such as
SL(3)
), but the sheer complexity of the long-element Kloosterman sum makes it difficult to believe one could find such sums “in the wild” (i.e. outside the context of
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
Poincaré series).
The more arithmetically interesting sums correspond to the order-three Weyl elements
w_{4}
and
w_{5}
.
The
w_{5}
Kloosterman sum (in the notation of
[
8
]
) is

\displaystyle S_{w_{5}}(m,n,c)=

\displaystyle\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}c_{2}=n_{2}c_{1}^{2}\\
c_{1}|c_{2}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{1}),x_{2}\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{2})\\
(x_{1},c_{1})=(x_{2},c_{2}/c_{1})=1\end{subarray}}e\left(m_{1}\frac{\bar{x_{1}}x_{2}}{c_{1}}+m_{2}\frac{\bar{x_{2}}}{c_{2}/c_{1}}+n_{1}\frac{x_{1}}{c_{1}}\right),

for
m,n,c\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
c_{1}c_{2}\neq 0
.
In particular, at
n_{2}=m_{1}
,

S_{w_{5}}(m,n,c)=c_{1}\,\delta_{c_{2}=c_{1}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{1})\\
xyz\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,c_{1})\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{n_{1}x+m_{1}y+m_{2}z}{c_{1}}\right),

and the three-variable sum at right is
\operatorname{Kl}_{3}(n_{1},m_{1},m_{2},c)
, the first hyper-Kloosterman sum.

The hyper-Kloosterman sums
\operatorname{Kl}_{3},\operatorname{Kl}_{4},\ldots
are relatively easy to locate in the context of other problems, and these have been studied, e.g. in
[
20
,
23
]
.
The
w_{4}
Kloosterman sums are symmetric to the
w_{5}
sums, and the Kloosterman sums for the remaining Weyl elements are degenerate (i.e. either trivial or
\operatorname{Kl}_{2}
sums).

In this paper, we achieve a spectral interpretation for a sum of the
w_{5}
Kloosterman sums (hence also for a sum of
\operatorname{Kl}_{3}
sums) over the moduli:
Writing

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{3}^{d*}}\ldots d_{H}\Xi

as shorthand for the full spectral expansion in
L^{2}(SL(3,\mathbb{Z})\backslash SL(3,\mathbb{R}))
(excluding the constant function and residual spectrum) over bases of minimal-weight forms (see
section

5.2
), we have

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}
is smooth and compactly supported outside the interval
(-T_{1},T_{1})
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{2}
, then

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\neq c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
m_{1}c_{2}=n_{2}c_{1}^{2}\end{subarray}}S_{w_{5}}(m,n,c)f\left(C^{3}\frac{m_{1}^{2}m_{2}n_{1}}{n_{2}c_{1}^{3}}\right)=\left|m_{1}m_{2}n_{1}\right|\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{3}^{d*}}\frac{\lambda_{\Xi}(n)\overline{\lambda_{\Xi}(m)}}{L(1,{\mathrm{Ad}}^{2}\Xi)}\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu_{\Xi})d_{H}\Xi,

where
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)=\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu,f,T_{1})
is made explicit in the next section.

This formula differs from the previous such formulas in several ways.
First and foremost, the spectral interpretation is not unique; the function
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)
depends on the choice of two auxiliary functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
and a parameter
X
that must be taken large relative to
CT_{1}^{-1/3}
.
The spectral sum concentrates near the self-dual cusp forms, i.e. the symmetric squares of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
cusp forms.
Specifically, the main contribution comes from cusp forms whose spectral parameters are (up to permutation) of the form
(a+r,-a+r,-2r)
with
a\asymp X^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
r\ll X^{\epsilon}
; note that the self-dual forms occur at exactly
r=0
.
(The parameter
X
does not appear on the left-hand side of (
1
), but does affect the shape of the sum on the right-hand side; this is part of what we mean by “the spectral interpretation is not unique”.)
This behavior was predicted in
[
8
]
(see problems I, III and IV there).

From the
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
Weyl law, the number of such forms is
X^{2+\epsilon}
and the function
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)
is of size
C^{3}X^{-3/2}
on this region, so the trivial bound on the spectral side has become
C^{3}X^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\gg C^{\frac{7}{2}+\epsilon}
.
Meanwhile, the Deligne bound on the
w_{5}
Kloosterman sum is roughly
c^{2+\epsilon}
, so the trivial bound on the Kloosterman sum side is
C^{3+\epsilon}
.
This is the second difference from previous formulas, as the spectral sums there were typically of length
C^{\epsilon}
.
This increase in the trivial bound will certainly increase the difficulty in applying the formula
1
(or
3
), but we expect applications still exist as it is an exact identity and we can apply the usual tools of analytic number theory (averaging, Poisson summation, etc.) to it; we give more details in the next section.

Finally, a related question is whether an arbitrary “nice” function can be expanded into Bessel functions.
We answered this question for the long-element Bessel function in
[
8
]
, but we can extend this now to the Bessel functions attached to the other Weyl elements.
In particular, we have

Theorem 2
.

For
f:\mathbb{R}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}
smooth and compactly supported,

f(y)=\sum_{d\geq 0}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{d}_{0}}\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)\,K^{d}_{w_{5}}((1,y),\mu)\mathbf{sin}^{d*}(\mu)d\mu

where
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)=\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu,f)
has super-polynomial decay in
\mu
.

Thus the
K^{d}_{w_{5}}(y,\mu)
Bessel functions give a basis for the smooth, compactly supported functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
.
(Though the notation suggests otherwise, the
K^{d}_{w_{5}}(y,\mu)
Bessel function is only properly defined at
y_{1}=1

However, it seems unlikely that the coefficients
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)
are uniquely determined by the inner product

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(y_{2})\overline{K^{d}_{w_{5}}((1,y_{2}),\mu)}\frac{dy_{2}}{\left|y_{2}\right|^{3}},

as they are for the long-element Bessel function.

Expansions using the Bessel functions attached to the remaining Weyl elements are now known either classically or by symmetry with
K^{d}_{w_{5}}(y,\mu)

The proof of

We note that the increase in the trivial bound does not rule out practical applications:

•

The purpose of such a formula is to change the nature of the summation in hopes of finding cancellation, and certainly the nature of the spectral sum is different from that of the sum of hyper-Kloosterman sums.

•

At
X\asymp C
, the trivial bound on the spectral side is precisely halfway between the trivial bound and the term-counting bound
C^{4}
(i.e. using
\left|S_{w_{5}}(m,n,c)\right|\leq c_{1}c_{2}
) on the hyper-Kloosteman side, so applying the above formula is less costly than opening the hyper-Kloosterman sums, provided
m
and
n
are not too large.

•

In case the formula is being used inside sums over the coordinates of
m
and
n
, one might hope to extract asymptotics for the spectral and long-element terms of
3
, whose first terms must then cancel.

•

If one can handle a moderately long sum of the long-element Kloosterman sums, it makes sense to apply
3
with
X
less than
C
; see
section

5.5.2
.

•

As usual, the analysis of this paper relates to the place at infinity and applies nearly verbatim if one changes the particular discrete group, nebentypus, cusp, etc.
In particular, in the case where we do not have square-root cancellation bounds for the hyper-Kloosterman sums, these results should become highly nontrivial, with the caveat that we are using the arithmetically-weighted Weyl law which also depends (somewhat) on the the strength of such bounds.

2.
Further Explorations

We summarize the remaining problems related to the
GL(3)
Kuznetsov formulas:

•

For the remaining
GL(3)
Kloosterman sums, those corresponding to the transpositions
w_{2},w_{3}
are the classical Kloosterman sums, for which the summation formulas are due to Kuznetsov, while the summation formula for the remaining order-three Weyl element
w_{4}
follows immediately from the above by replacing all of the relevant functions with their duals
f^{\iota}(g):=f(g^{\iota})
,
g^{\iota}:=w_{l}\left({g}^{T}\right)^{-1}w_{l}
using
w_{4}=w_{5}^{\iota}
.

•

We have only analyzed the case where
m
and
n
are small compared to
c
; in particular, we have assumed
C\geq 1
.
It might yield interesting results to study the case
C<1
, i.e.
m_{1}m_{2}n_{1}>c_{1}^{3}
.

•

In
section

5.5.2
, we see that taking
X
a little smaller than
C
results in a sum over the moduli of the long-element Kloosterman sums where one moduli is essentially fixed and the other ranges over a region of size
C^{2}
.
As the long-element Kloosterman sum factors into a (short sum of) product(s) of two
\operatorname{Kl}_{2}
Kloosterman sums
[
31
]
(see also
[
26
]
), one might hope that this would yield to
GL(2)
methods, e.g. the classical Kuznetsov formulas.

•

A consequence of the expansion of
2
is that one can construct the formula of
3
by adding the spectral Kuznetsov formulas for all
d
with individually chosen test functions
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)
, though this is not the approach of the current paper.
Again, the coefficients
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)
are not uniquely determined by
f
and it remains an open question whether one can choose these coefficients such that

\sum_{d\geq 0}\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{d}_{0}}\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)\,K^{d}_{w}(y,\mu)\mathbf{sin}^{d*}(\mu)d\mu

is
f(y)
for
w=w_{5}
and zero unless
w=w_{5},w_{l}
(as we have done) and yet have the trivial bound on both the spectral expansion and long-element Kloosterman term be comparable to the trivial bound on the sum of hyper-Kloosterman sums.
The analysis of
section

5.5.2
suggests a negative answer.
In any case, an explicit determination of the class of functions
\widehat{F}^{d}(\mu)
that make (
1
) true for a given
f
is highly desirable.

•

We note that the support of
\widetilde{F}^{d}_{0}(\mu)
being effectively at
\min_{i}\left|\mu_{i}\right|=0
makes it tantalizing to think that a (limit of) properly constructed test function(s) would result in
\widetilde{F}^{d}_{0}(\mu)
supported on the symmetric squares of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
cusp forms; in that case, the number of such forms with
\left\|\mu\right\|\ll X^{1/2}
is
\asymp X
(by the
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
Weyl law) so the trivial bound would become
C^{3}X^{-1/2+\epsilon}\asymp C^{5/2+\epsilon}
at
X\asymp C
.
(This relates to the so-called “spectral gap” and “conductor-dropping” phenomena in the sense that it suggests there are few, if any, points in the spectrum close to the symmetric squares and the apparent difference in the trivial bounds is due to the relative sparsity of symmetric squares in the whole
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
spectrum.)
Furthermore, this leads one to think that the proper trace formula for hyper-Kloosterman sums should involve an expansion over just these symmetric squares.
Unfortunately, the effect of
f
on the spectral expansion is precisely orthogonal to the self-dual forms – i.e. if we write
\mu=(a,-a,0)+(r,r,-2r)
with
a
large, then
\widetilde{F}^{d}_{0}(\mu)
will depend on
f
solely through
\widehat{f}(\cdot,-1-2r)
(i.e. there is no interaction between
f
and
a
, though deeper asymptotics do depend on the moments
\widehat{f}(\cdot,-1-2r+n)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
), but the self-dual forms occur at
r=0
.

•

Lastly, one approach to isolating the symmetric squares in the
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
spectrum (á la Langlands’ Beyond Endoscopy
[
27
]
) would be to take
\widehat{f}
approaching a point mass (e.g. taking
f
of the form
f(y^{\delta})
for
\delta>0
small), but this is beyond the scope of the current paper.

Define

We tend not to distinguish between elements of

On

\sin\theta_{1}&\cos\theta_{1}&0\\

with

,

Define characters of

by

,

and of

,

.
Call

the (Harish-Chandra/Langlands) spectral parameters of an automorphic representation/automorphic form.

The representations of

are given by the Wigner-

matrices.
Up to equivalence, there is one (irreducible, unitary) representation
\mathcal{D}^{d}:K\to GL(2d+1,\mathbb{C})
of each odd dimension

,
d\geq 0
, where

corresponds to the trivial representation.

The Weyl group in
G
is, up to scalar multiples, the set of all matrices with exactly one 1 on each row and column.
We write
W=\left\{I,w_{2},w_{3},w_{4},w_{5},w_{l}\right\}=W_{3}\cup w_{2}W_{3}
with

, where

\displaystyle w_{2}=-\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
1\\
&&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad w_{3}=-\begin{pmatrix}1\\
&&1\\
&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle w_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}&1\\
&&1\\
1\end{pmatrix},\qquad w_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}&&1\\
1\\
&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad w_{l}=-\begin{pmatrix}&&1\\
&1\\

The Weyl group acts on
\mu
and
\delta
by
p_{\mu^{w}}(y)=p_{\mu}(wyw^{-1})
and

.
We also define

The Jacquet-Whittaker function is given by the (Jacquet) integral

3.1.
The Weyl law

For

, define

For

3.2.

Define the (normalized) Mellin transform of the Bessel functions by

(6)

\displaystyle K^{d}_{w_{l}}(y,\mu)=:

\displaystyle\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}\left|4\pi^{2}y_{1}\right|^{1-s_{1}}\left|4\pi^{2}y_{2}\right|^{1-s_{2}}\widehat{K}^{d}_{w_{l}}(s,{\mathop{\,sgn}}(y),\mu)\frac{ds_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{ds_{2}}{2\pi i},

The unbounded portion of the contours in each

must pass to the left of

(and the finite part must pass to the right of the poles of the integrands) to maintain absolute convergence for

Write

and

then for

(7)

\displaystyle\widehat{K}^{d}_{w_{l}}(s,v,\mu)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\mathbf{csc}^{d}(\mu)\sum_{w\in W_{3}}\chi_{d}^{w}(v)\,G^{v_{1},v_{2}}(s,\mu^{w}).

where

\displaystyle G^{++}(s,\mu):=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{1})\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{2})\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{3})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{1})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{2})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{3})}{3\pi^{2}\Gamma(s_{1}+s_{2})}\prod_{i<j}\sin\pi(\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}),

\displaystyle G^{+-}(s,\mu):=

\displaystyle\sin\pi(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})\frac{\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{2})\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{3})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{1})\Gamma(1-s_{1}-s_{2})}{\Gamma(1-s_{1}+\mu_{1})\Gamma(1-s_{2}-\mu_{2})\Gamma(1-s_{2}-\mu_{3})},

\displaystyle G^{-+}(s,\mu):=

\displaystyle\sin\pi(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})\frac{\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{3})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{1})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{2})\Gamma(1-s_{1}-s_{2})}{\Gamma(1-s_{1}+\mu_{1})\Gamma(1-s_{1}+\mu_{2})\Gamma(1-s_{2}-\mu_{3})},

\displaystyle G^{--}(s,\mu):=

\displaystyle\sin\pi(\mu_{1}-\mu_{3})\frac{\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{1})\Gamma(s_{1}-\mu_{3})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{1})\Gamma(s_{2}+\mu_{3})}{\Gamma(1-s_{1}+\mu_{2})\Gamma(1-s_{2}-\mu_{2})\Gamma(s_{1}+s_{2})}.

Note: Compared to

from the

For

(8)

\displaystyle\widehat{K}_{w_{l}}^{d}(s,v,\mu^{d}(r))=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}(-\varepsilon_{1}\varepsilon_{2})^{d}B^{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}}_{w_{l}}\left(s,r\right)Q(d,s_{1}-r)Q(d,s_{2}+r),

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{2}+s\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-s\right)},

B\left(s_{1}+2r,1-s_{1}-s_{2}\right)&\mbox{if }\varepsilon=(+,-),\\

3.3.
Oscillatory integrals

We will engage in some stationary phase analysis of oscillatory integrals.
Suppose
\omega:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is smooth with fixed compact support and satisfies

for some

Also suppose

Lemma 5

(
[

, Lem 8.1]
)
.

Suppose that

on the support of
\omega
for some

, then for any

\mathcal{I}\ll_{A}T\left(\left(\frac{QR}{\sqrt{Y}}\right)^{-A}+(RU)^{-A}\right).

In particular, for any

, unless

.

Proposition 6

(
[
5
, Prop 8.2]
)
.

Suppose that there is a unique

in the support of
\omega
such that
\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})=0
, that

is supported on an interval of length
\ll U_{1}\geq U
, that

on the support of

where

We now discuss how to remove the technical assumption that

8.
An aside on the method of Bump, Friedberg and Goldfeld

In their Proposition 8.1, BFG
[
7
]
consider the behavior as
y_{1},y_{2}
become arbitrarily large in the Fourier coefficients of a Poincaré series with kernel function

f_{\text{BFG}}(xyk)=\psi_{m}(x)\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{1}y_{2}\leq 1\\
y_{1}\geq 1\end{subarray}}y_{1}^{2s_{1}+s_{2}}y_{2}^{2s_{2}+s_{1}}\exp\left(-2\pi(m_{1}y_{1}+m_{2}y_{1})\right).

Provided
{\mathop{Re}}(s_{1})
and
{\mathop{Re}}(s_{2})
(hence also
{\mathop{Re}}(2s_{1}+s_{2})
and
{\mathop{Re}}(2s_{2}+s_{1})
) are sufficiently large, the Poincaré series with this kernel and its Fourier coefficients will converge, but
f_{\text{BFG}}
and hence the Poincaré series are not smooth, and hence there is no reason to believe the spectral expansion should apply.
Of course, this is easily fixed by replacing
\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{1}y_{2}\leq 1\\
y_{1}\geq 1\end{subarray}}
with a smooth approximation.

Since
f_{\text{BFG}}
already has good decay at each
y_{i}=0,\infty
, one might consider replacing it with some function
f(xyk)=\psi_{m}(x)f(y)
which is smooth and compactly supported in
y\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}
to bypass all concerns of convergence.
For such an
f
, we can apply the spherical case of the Whittaker expansion (
12
), which reduces to

\displaystyle f(g)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{0}_{0}}\widehat{f}(\mu)W^{0}(g,\mu)\mathbf{sin}^{0*}(\mu)d\mu,

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\mu):=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f(y)W^{0}(y,-\mu)dy.

This allows us to apply the definition of the Bessel functions and Whittaker inversion in the proof of
section

5.2
, so equality of the two interpretations of the Fourier coefficient looks like

(36)

\displaystyle p_{\rho}(m)\int_{\mathcal{B}^{0*}}\frac{\lambda_{\Xi}(n)\overline{\lambda_{\Xi}(m)}}{L(1,{\mathrm{Ad}}^{2}\Xi)}\widehat{f}(\mu)W^{0}(y,\mu_{\Xi})d_{H}\Xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W}\sum_{c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}^{2}}S_{w}(m,n,c)p_{\rho^{w}}(n)F(y,mcwn^{-1}w^{-1}),

where

F(y,t):=\int_{\mathfrak{a}^{0}_{0}}\widehat{f}(\mu)K^{0}_{w}(t,\mu)W^{0}(y,\mu)\mathbf{sin}^{0*}(\mu)d\mu.

Note that both the spectral side and every Weyl cell of the arithmetic side have a term
W^{0}(y,\mu)
, which decays exponentially as
y_{1},y_{2}\to\infty
on compact subsets in
\mu
.
So every term in (
36
) is decaying to zero as
y_{1},y_{2}\to\infty
, and for
{\mathop{Re}}(s_{1}),{\mathop{Re}}(s_{2})
sufficiently large, this should also apply to the construction of BFG.
In fact, for smooth and compactly supported
f
, the function
\widehat{f}
has super-polynomial decay in
\mu
, and hence every term of (
36
) has super-polynomial decay in
y_{1},y_{2}
; we expect this to continue to hold for any smoothed (but not necessarily compactly supported) version of the BFG construction.

If we return to
f_{\text{BFG}}
with the understanding that both the
w_{5}
and
w_{l}
terms are decaying in
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
, we can find the difficulty by explicitly writing out the two terms.
We take
m=n=c=I
for simplicity and note that the slight difference in the definitions of
w_{l}
is not significant here.
Writing
f_{y}(g)=f_{\text{BFG}}(gy)
and applying some simplifying substitutions (see the first paragraph of
[
10
, Sect 5]
), we have

\displaystyle T_{w_{l}}(f_{y})(I)=

\displaystyle y_{1}^{2-s_{1}-2s_{2}}y_{2}^{2-2s_{1}-s_{2}}\iiint\limits_{R_{w_{l}}(y)}\left(1+x_{1}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{3s_{2}}{2}}\left(1+x_{2}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{3s_{1}}{2}}\left(1+x_{3}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1-3s_{1}-3s_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp-\left(\frac{1}{y_{2}}\frac{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}}{\left(1+x_{2}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}+\frac{1}{y_{1}}\frac{\sqrt{1+x_{2}^{2}}}{\left(1+x_{1}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times e\left(\frac{1}{y_{1}}\frac{x_{1}\sqrt{1+x_{2}^{2}}}{\left(1+x_{1}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}+\frac{1}{y_{2}}\frac{x_{1}x_{3}+x_{2}\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}{\left(1+x_{2}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times e\left(-y_{1}\frac{x_{2}x_{3}+x_{1}\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+x_{2}^{2}}}-y_{2}x_{2}\right)dx_{1}\,dx_{2}\,dx_{3},

\displaystyle T_{w_{5}}(f_{y})(I)=

\displaystyle y_{1}^{2-s_{1}-2s_{2}}y_{2}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\iint\limits_{R_{w_{5}}(y)}\left(1+x_{1}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1-3s_{1}-3s_{2}}{2}}\left(1+x_{3}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{3s_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp-\left(y_{2}\frac{\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}}+\frac{1}{y_{1}y_{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}\left(1+x_{3}^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times e\left(\frac{1}{y_{1}y_{2}}\frac{x_{3}}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}\left(1+x_{3}^{2}\right)}+y_{2}\frac{x_{1}x_{3}}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}}-y_{1}x_{1}\right)dx_{1}\,dx_{3},

where

\displaystyle R_{w_{l}}(y)=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\middle|\,\frac{1}{\left(1+x_{1}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{2}^{2}}\left(1+x_{3}^{2}\right)}\leq y_{1}y_{2},\frac{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}}{\left(1+x_{2}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}\geq y_{2}\right\},

\displaystyle R_{w_{5}}(y)=

\displaystyle\left\{(x_{1},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,\middle|\,\frac{1}{\left(1+x_{1}^{2}\right)\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}\leq y_{1},\frac{\sqrt{1+x_{3}^{2}}}{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{2}}}\geq\frac{1}{y_{2}}\right\}.

The argument in
[
7
, Prop 8.1]
was simply that as
y_{1},y_{2}\to\infty
,
R_{w_{l}}(y,c)\to\phi
, while
R_{w_{5}}(y,c_{1})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and this is clearly true.
On the other hand, looking at the integrals themselves, the integrand of
T_{w_{5}}(f_{y})
clearly has exponential decay on any fixed compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
y_{2}\to\infty
.
We suggest that actually, for any smooth approximation of
f_{\text{BFG}}
, both
T_{w}(f_{y})
have super-polynomial decay and this is coming from the interplay of the exponential and oscillating factors (i.e. the exponential decay factor is very small at any stationary points of the oscillation).

Wavefront sets of convolutions of distributions with (weighted) line integral distributions
Brian Sherson

Abstract




Excluding pathological cases of curves
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right),\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
for some
\varepsilon>0
, the following line integral:

\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}},\phi\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\left\|\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right\|\,dt,\quad\phi\in\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

defines a distribution. Moreover, if we replace
\left\|\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right\|

with any bounded positive weight function
\upsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right)\right)

for some
\varepsilon>0
, the following also defines a distribution:

\left\langle\mathcal{T}_{\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}},\upsilon},\phi\right\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(t\right)\,dt,\quad\phi\in\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right),

provided that either
\left\|\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right\|
is bounded
away from zero, or
\upsilon
decays sufficiently fast so that the
above integral converges for any
\phi\in\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

The convolution of distributions
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, once of which
has compact support, is defined in Hörmander
[
hormander1990AnaLinParDifOpeIDisTheFouAna
]
,
as the unique distrubtion
u=u_{1}\star u_{2}
satisfying:

u_{1}\star\left(u_{2}\star\phi\right)=u\star\phi,\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right).

As such,
w\star\mathcal{T}_{\gamma}
and
w\star\mathcal{T}_{\gamma,\upsilon}

are well-defined as distributions whenever
w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
This will give rise to formally defining the notations

\displaystyle w\star\mathcal{T}_{\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\left\|\gamma^{\prime}\left(t\right)\right\|\,dt,

\displaystyle w\star\mathcal{T}_{\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}},\upsilon}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(t\right)\,dt,

both of which will agree with the usual notion of an integral converging
for almost every
\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whenever
w\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.
We will explore such convolutions, as well as their wavefront sets,
particularly exploring how the convolution scatters the singularities
of
w
. However, we will require a more direct formulation of such
convolutions than the definition of convolution given in Hörmander
provides for.

To avoid pathological cases, we will focus on choices of
w
and

\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}
for which given any
\vec{\mathbf{x}}
,
\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)

lies outside the support of
w
for
t
sufficiently large.

1.
The Distributional Directional Antiderivative

We may begin by extending the idea of directional antiderivatives
to compactly-supported distributions. In particular, given
\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
,
we want to focus on the directional antiderivatives of the form:

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt,\quad\vec{\mathbf{u}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},s\in\mathbb{R}.

This is equivalent to choosing
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)=-t\vec{\mathbf{v}}
.
When defining
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
for a distribution
w
, we will
replace the requirement that
w
be compactly-supported with a weaker
condition.

Definition 1.1

(Distributional directional antiderivative)
.

Let
\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
,

w\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, and suppose that
w

satisfies a support condition

(1.1)

t_{\min}=\min_{\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{supp}\,w}\vec{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{v}}>-\infty.

Now choose a
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{0}\,dx=1
, and
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{0}\subseteq\left(-\infty,t_{\min}\right)
.
For
\phi\in\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we define

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds,\quad\vec{\mathbf{u}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},t\in\mathbb{R},

where
\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}:\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\rightarrow\mathscr{S}\left(\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}\right)

denotes the x-ray transform restricted to the direction
\vec{\mathbf{v}}
, and
the tensor product
\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}
is interpreted
as:

\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\psi_{0}\left(s\right),\quad\vec{\mathbf{u}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},s\in\mathbb{R}.

We then define the
distributional directional antiderivative

by

\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w,\phi\right\rangle=-\left\langle w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle.

Since we defined
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}
in a way that depends on an
arbitrary choice of
\psi_{0}
, we will want to verify that a different
choice of
\psi_{0}
will not alter
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}
.

Proposition 1.2
.

While
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi
depends on choice of
\psi_{0}
,

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
does not, so long as
\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{0}\subseteq\left(-\infty,t_{\min}\right)
.

Let
\psi_{0},\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
both
have support in
\left(-\infty,t_{\min}\right)
, and take
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{0}

and
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{1}
as defined above in terms of
\psi_{0}

and
\psi_{1}
, respectively. Then for
\phi\in\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

we observe that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{0}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{1}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt-\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{1}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\psi_{0}\left(s\right)-\psi_{1}\left(s\right)\right)\,ds.

For
t
below or above both supports of
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
,
this integral is zero. In particular, the support of
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{0}\phi-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{1}\phi

is contained inside
\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}+\left(-\infty,t_{\min}\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}
.
Hence:

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{0}w,\phi\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{1}w,\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{0}\phi\right\rangle+\left\langle w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{1}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{0}\phi-\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}^{1}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

We now wish to verify that
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}
acts on functions
in
\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
satisfying the support
condition
1.1
in the desired manner.

Proposition 1.3

(Distributional Anti-partial derivative of
\mathcal{L}^{1}
functions)
.

If
f\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
satisfies the
support condition
1.1
, then
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}f

is in fact a function given by

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt.

Let
t_{\min}=\min_{\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{supp\,}f}\vec{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{v}}
, and choose

\psi_{0}
as described in
1.1
. Observe:

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}f,\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle f,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)\,d\vec{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left(\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{1}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right)\,ds\,dt\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\int_{t}^{\infty}\left(\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{1}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right)\,ds\,dt\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\left(\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{1}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right)\,dt\,ds\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt\,\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt-\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt\,\psi_{0}\left(s\right)\,ds\,\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}

The latter integral vanishes for all
s
, since:

\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt=0,\quad s<t_{\min},

and:

\psi_{0}\left(s\right)=0,\quad s>t_{\min}.

Hence:

\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}f,\phi\right\rangle=\int_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}f\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt\,\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\,d\vec{\mathbf{u}}.\qed

Proposition 1.4
.

For
w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
:

\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=w,\quad\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=w.

We first observe that for
\phi\in\mathscr{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,

\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi=0
, and so:

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds=\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right).

Then:

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w,\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w,\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle w,\phi\right\rangle.

On the other hand,

\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right),

and since
\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}
is supported away
from the support of
w
, a similar computation also yields:

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=w.\qed

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and assume
U
is invariant
under translation in the direction
-\vec{\mathbf{v}}
. That is,
U-t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\subseteq U

for
t\geq 0
. If
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
and are equal on
U
, then
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w_{1}=\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w_{2}

are equal on
U
.

Let
\phi
be supported in
U
. Then if we inspect:

\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds,

Then for
\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\notin U
, we must have
\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\notin U

for
\tau\geq t
and so:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\psi_{0}\left(s\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\int_{t}^{\infty}\psi_{0}\left(s\right)\,ds.

In particular,
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=0

when
\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\notin U
and

t\geq t_{\min}
.
In particular:

\mathrm{supp}\,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\subseteq U\cup V,\quad V=\left\{\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}:t<t_{\min}\right\}.

We now choose a partiton of unity
\rho_{U}
and
\rho_{V}
for

U
and
V
so that
\rho_{U}+\rho_{V}=1
on
U\cup V
,
\mathrm{supp}\,\rho_{U}\subseteq U
,
and
\mathrm{supp}\,\rho_{V}\subseteq V
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w_{1},\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w_{1},\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w_{1},\rho_{U}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle-\underbrace{\left\langle w_{1},\rho_{V}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle}_{=0}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w_{2},\rho_{U}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle-\underbrace{\left\langle w_{2},\rho_{V}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle}_{=0}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w_{2},\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w_{2},\phi\right\rangle.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
t_{0}>t_{\max}=\max_{\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{supp}\,w}\vec{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{v}}
,
then define
w^{\star}
by:

\left\langle w^{\star},\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle w,\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}+t\vec{v}\right)-\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle.

Then
w^{\star}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}
are distribution
with odd and even symmetries across the hyperplane
\left\{t=t_{0}\right\}
,
and furthermore,
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}
is compactly supported,
and is equal to
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
on
\left\{t<t_{0}\right\}
.

We observe

\displaystyle\left\langle w^{\star},\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle w,\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{v}\right)-\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w,\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{v}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w^{\star},\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle.

This implies that if
\phi
has even symmetry across
\left\{t=t_{0}\right\}
,
then
\left\langle w^{\star},\phi\right\rangle=0
. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star},\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=-\left\langle w^{\star},\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left\{\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{v}\right)\right\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=-\left\langle w^{\star},\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=-\left\langle w^{\star},\int_{2t_{0}-t}^{\infty}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\left\langle w^{\star},\int_{-\infty}^{2t_{0}-t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\left\langle w^{\star},\int_{t}^{\infty}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=-\left\langle w^{\star},\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\left(\psi_{0}\left(s\right)-\psi_{0}\left(s\right)+\psi_{0}\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=-\left\langle w^{\star},\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\right\rangle+\left\langle w^{\star},\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{t}\psi_{0}\left(s\right)-\psi_{0}\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\,ds\right\rangle.

We then observe that
\int_{-\infty}^{t}\psi_{0}\left(s\right)-\psi_{0}\left(2t_{0}-s\right)\,ds

has even symmetry across
\left\{t=t_{0}\right\}
, and so:

\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star},\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}+\left(2t_{0}-t\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star},\phi\right\rangle.

The symmetry easily implies that
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}
must
be compactly-supported. Furthermore, it is clear that
w=w^{\star}

on
\left\{t<t_{0}\right\}
, so we have
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}

on
\left\{t<t_{0}\right\}
.

WF\left(w\right)
and

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)
. But first, we start with the

Theorem 1.7

(Microlocal property
[
hormander1990AnaLinParDifOpeIDisTheFouAna
]
)
.

If
P
is a differential operator of order

m
with
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
coefficients on a manifold
X
,
thenLet
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
and
a\in S^{\infty}
; then

WF\left(u\right)\subseteq\mathrm{Char\,}P\cup WF\left(Pu\right),\quad u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}\left(X\right),

where the characteristic set
\mathrm{Char\,}P
is defined by

\mathrm{Char}\,P=\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in T^{\star}\left(X\right)\,\middle|\,P_{m}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)=0\right\}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},

and
P_{m}
is the principal symbol of
P
.

In the case that
P
is merely a directional derivative, i.e.,
P=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}
,
then
P_{m}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)=i\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{v}}
, and so

\mathrm{Char}\,P=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}.

While
1.7
implies that
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}
will

at most

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left(\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}\backslash\vec{\mathbf{0}}\right)
, the
following result states that
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w

will
not
contain an element
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}

altogether.

Theorem 1.8

(Main Result)
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
and
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
. If:

WF\left(w\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

then:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Let
w^{\star}
be the distribution extending
w
, having odd symmetry
across a plane
\left\{t=t_{0}\right\}
, with
t_{0}
large enough
so that
w=w^{\star}
on
\left\{t<t_{0}\right\}
. (
1.6
).
Then:

WF\left(w^{\star}\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and it will suffice to show that:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Indeed, since
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}
and
w^{\star}
are
compactly-supported distribution, their Fourier transforms exist as
entire functions, and:

i\tau\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\widehat{w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right),\quad\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Because of the odd symmetry of
w^{\star}
across the plane
\left\{t=t_{0}\right\}
,

\widehat{w^{\star}}
vanishes on
\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
, and also:

\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{\widehat{w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)}{i\tau},&\text{if }\tau\neq 0,\\
-i\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\widehat{w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\right),&\text{if }\tau=0,\end{cases}

for
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. The case

\tau=0
comes from an application of l’Hôpital’s
rule. However,
-i\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\widehat{w^{\star}}=-\widehat{tw^{\star}}
,
and:

WF\left(-tw^{\star}\right)\subseteq WF\left(w^{\star}\right),

so we can expect
-i\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\widehat{w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)

to decay rapidly in an open conic neighborhood
\Gamma
of
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}
.
For our following argument, we will require that
\Gamma
be chosen
to be convex in
\tau
, i.e., if
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau_{k}\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\Gamma

for
k=1,2
, and
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}
, then
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\Gamma

for
\tau_{1}\leq\tau\leq\tau_{2}
. In particular, we can set:

\Gamma=\left\{\sigma\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}+\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}:\sigma,\tau>0,\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}\cap\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}^{\perp},\max\left\{\left\|\xi\right\|,\left|\tau\right|\right\}<\varepsilon\sigma\right\},

for some
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. Then for each
N\geq 0
,
we can choose
C_{N}
so that:

\left|\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\widehat{w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left\|\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\right\|^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}},\quad\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},\tau\in\mathbb{R},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\Gamma.

This bound on the derivative then gives us the following bound on

\widehat{w^{\star}}
:

\left|\widehat{w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left\|\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\right\|^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}}\left|\tau\right|,\quad\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},\tau\in\mathbb{R},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\Gamma.

It then follows immediately that:

\left|\widehat{\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right|\leq C_{N}\left(1+\left\|\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\right\|^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{-\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}},\quad\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},\tau\in\mathbb{R},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}+\tau\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\Gamma.

As this is true for arbitrary
N\geq 0
, this proves that:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w^{\star}
on
\left\{t<t_{0}\right\}
,
we can say that:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(\left\{t<t_{0}\right\}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and then let
t_{0}\rightarrow\infty
to obtain:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.\qed

The above result implies that the only way
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w

can include an element
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}

in its wavefront set is if
w
itself already contains some element
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}
. The following
result further refines the previous result by describing a necessary
condition on
\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
in order for
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
to contain

\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)
in its wavefront set. Intuition
tells us that
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0}-t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)
must already
belong to the wavefront set of
w
for some
t\geq 0
.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
assume
U
is invariant under translation in the direction
-\vec{\mathbf{v}}
,
and
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
. If:

(1.2)

WF\left(w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

then:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Let
U^{\star}
be an open subset of
U
whose closure is entirely
contained in
U
, and is also closed under translation in the direction

-\vec{\mathbf{v}}
, and choose a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\psi\geq 0

supported in
U
that is equal to 1 on
U^{\star}
. Then
\psi w=w

on
U^{\star}
, so
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi w\right)=\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
,
and in fact, we also have
\psi\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w

on
U^{\star}
. Furthermore,
1.2
implies:

WF\left(\psi w\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and so:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi w\right)\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

We then have:

\displaystyle WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U^{\star}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi w\right)\right)\cap\left(U^{\star}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi w\right)\right)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\emptyset.

Since
U^{\star}
was arbitrary, and for each
\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in U
, we can
find such a
U^{\star}
containing
\vec{\mathbf{x}}
, we can deduce that:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.\qed

Corollary 1.10

(Propagation of singularities of the distributional directional antiderivative)
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
define:

(1.3)

R_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\left\{\vec{\mathbf{x}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}:t\geq 0\right\},\quad\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1},

and let:

V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}=\bigcup_{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)}R_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right),\quad U_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}=V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}^{C},\quad\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}.

Then:

\displaystyle WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq WF\left(w\right)\cup\bigcup_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\left(V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)

(1.4)

\displaystyle=WF\left(w\right)\cup\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\,\middle|\,\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in WF\left(w\right),\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\perp\vec{\mathbf{v}},t\geq 0\right\}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w=w
, we can already narrow
down
WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)
to:

(1.5)

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\subseteq WF\left(w\right)\cup\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}\right).

We want to be able to replace
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}

with
\bigcup_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}}\left(V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)
.

We next observe that for each
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
, since

w
is compactly-supported,
V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}
must be closed. Then

U_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}
is an open set that is invariant under translation
in the direction of
-\vec{\mathbf{v}}
, and:

WF\left(w\right)\cap\left(U_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and so:

(1.6)

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Therefore, if
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)
,
but
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\notin WF\left(w\right)
,
then
1.5
indicates that
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
,
and then
1.6
would require that
\vec{\mathbf{x}}\notin U_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}
,
and so
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}_{0}\right)\in V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}
.

Note that if we lift the restriction of compact support on
w
, we
must instead use:

V_{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}}=\overline{\bigcup_{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)}R_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)}.\qed

We now strengthen
1.9
by with the next result.
Intuitively, even if
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\in WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)
,
if
WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)
omits some
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+t_{0}\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)
,
for some
t_{0}>0
, then the only way for
WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)

to pick up any more elements of the form
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)

for
t>t_{0}
is for
WF\left(w\right)
to contain some
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+t_{1}\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)
,
for some
t_{1}>t_{0}
.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}
,
and
U_{0}
be a bounded open set,
t_{1}>0
, and

U_{t}=U+t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

U=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq t_{1}}U_{t}.

If:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U_{0}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and:

WF\left(w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

then:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

We may assume without loss of generality that
U_{0}
is convex,
otherwise, we can apply the following argument to every convex open
subset of
U_{0}
. Let
U_{0}^{\star}
be an open set whose closure
is contained in
U_{0}
, then define
U^{\star}
in much the same
way as
U
. Now let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(U\right)

be equal to 1 on
U^{\star}
. Consider the distributional partial
derivative:

\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)=\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w+\psi\cdot w.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\psi
vanishes on
U^{\star}
, we have

\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)=w
on

U^{\star}
, and since:

WF\left(w\right)\cap\left(U^{\star}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)\subseteq WF\left(w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

we have:

WF\left(\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\right)\cap\left(U^{\star}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Furthermore, since:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U_{0}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

it must also follow that:

WF\left(\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\right)\cap\left(U_{0}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and so:

WF\left(\mathcal{D}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\right)\cap\left(\left(U_{0}\cup U^{\star}\right)\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

We can replace
U_{0}\cup U^{\star}
with
\bigcup_{t\leq 0}U_{t}\cup U^{\star}
,
as that introduces no points that are inside the support of
\psi

(hence the requirement that
U_{0}
be convex), and is also invariant
under translation in the direction
-\vec{\mathbf{v}}
, and so we again apply

1.8
to obtain:

WF\left(\psi\cdot\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(\left(U_{0}\cup U^{\star}\right)\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

In particular,

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U^{\star}\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset,

and since
U_{0}^{\star}
was arbitrary, we can replace
U^{\star}

with
U
:

WF\left(\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w\right)\cap\left(U\times\left\{\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.\qed

We now wish to extend the distributional directional antiderivative
further by replacing the support condition
1.1

with an even weaker condition, that there exists a
t_{\min}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp}\right)

such that

(1.7)

\mathrm{supp}\,w\subseteq\left\{\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\,\middle|\,\vec{\mathbf{u}}\in\vec{\mathbf{v}}^{\perp},t>t_{\min}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\right\}.

Notice this includes the previous support condition by considering
the case that
t_{\min}
is a constant. If we let
\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\vec{\mathbf{x}}-t_{\min}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}
,
then the pullback
\chi^{\star}w
has the support condition
\inf_{\vec{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathrm{supp}\,\chi^{\star}w}\vec{\mathbf{x}}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{v}}>0
,
and so we can define
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
by conjugating
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}

with the pullback map
\chi^{\star}
. We will want to be sure that
this does not change
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
, however.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
w
satisfy
1.1
, and define
\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\vec{\mathbf{x}}-t_{\min}\vec{\mathbf{v}}
.
Then
\chi^{-\star}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\chi^{\star}w=\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
.

It is important to note that
\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\chi^{\star}=\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}
.
For some
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{-}\right)
,
we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\chi^{-\star}\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\chi^{\star}w,\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\chi^{\star}w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\chi^{\star}\phi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\chi^{\star}w,\int_{-\infty}^{t}\chi^{\star}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\left(\chi^{\star}\phi\right)\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\chi^{\star}w,\int_{-\infty}^{t}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(s-t_{\min}\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\chi^{\star}w,\int_{-\infty}^{t-t_{\min}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(s+t_{\min}\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\chi^{\star}w,\int_{-\infty}^{t-t_{\min}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)-\mathcal{X}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\otimes\chi^{-\star}\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+s\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,ds\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle\chi^{\star}w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(t-t_{\min}\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w,\chi^{-\star}\left\{\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+\left(t-t_{\min}\right)\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle w,\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}\phi\left(\vec{\mathbf{u}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w,\phi\right\rangle,

where
\chi^{-\star}\psi_{0}\left(t\right)=\psi_{0}\left(t+t_{\min}\right)
.
∎Thus, we may define
\mathcal{I}_{\vec{\mathbf{v}}}w
when
w
satisfies the
weaker support condition as follows:

.

. Define

.

so long as

is satisfied.

1.10

\displaystyle=WF\left(w\right)\cup\chi^{-\star}\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}+t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\eta}}_{0}\right)\,\middle|\,\right.

Definition 1.14
.

We will use the more familiar notation:

\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-t\vec{\mathbf{v}}\right)\,dt

to refer to

.

2.
General line integrals

Now that we have given meaning to the integral
1.8
,
we now wish to give meaning to the following integral:

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(t\right)\,dt,

given a
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right);\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

and positive-valued
\upsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right),\mathbb{R}\right)
,
for some
\varepsilon>0
, with
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(0\right)=\vec{\mathbf{0}}
,
and
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}^{\prime}\left(t\right)\neq 0
for all
t>-\varepsilon
. It
will also be necessary to impose a support condition that
w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\gamma\left(t\right)\right)

has bounded support in the variable
t
to avoid a pathological choice
of
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}
, e.g, a choice of
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}
, that given some

w\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, the above integral
may fail to converge for
\vec{\mathbf{x}}
in some open set.

We observe that in the case that
w\in\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
,
the above integral can be interpreted as

\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y+t\right)\right)\,dt\right|_{y=0}.

The notation
w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)
refers
to pulling back
w
by the map
\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right)=\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)
.
We then compute the distributional antiderivative in the direction

\left(\vec{\mathbf{0}},-1\right)
, the direction corresponding to the negative

y
-axis. This antiderivative is then pulled back by the map

\psi_{0}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},0\right).

For ease of notation, we will specialize to the case
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(0\right)=\vec{\mathbf{0}}
.
A result for the general case can be achieved via translations.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
w
be a distribution in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right);\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

a curve for some
\varepsilon>0
, with
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(0\right)=\vec{\mathbf{0}}

and
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}^{\prime}\left(t\right)\neq 0
, and assume the pullback

\chi^{\star}w=w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)

has support bounded in
y
. Then the integral:

\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\,dt,

is defined as:

\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y+t\right)\right)\,dt\right|_{y=0}.

That is,

\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\,dt=\psi_{0}^{\star}\mathcal{I}_{\left(\vec{\mathbf{0}},-1\right)}\left(\chi^{\star}w\right).

If additionally,
\upsilon\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right)\right)

is a positive-valued weight function, we can then define:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(t\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y+t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(y+t\right)\,dt\right|_{y=0}

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}^{\star}\mathcal{I}_{\left(\vec{\mathbf{0}},-1\right)}\left(\left(\chi^{\star}w\right)\upsilon\right).

We then extend
1.10
to a more general result
for the integral
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
w\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

is a distribution such that the support in
y
of
w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)

is bounded, then:

\displaystyle WF\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(t\right)\,dt\right\}\backslash WF\left(w\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\subseteq\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\,\middle|\,\exists t\geq 0:\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\perp\gamma^{\prime}\left(t\right)\And\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)\right\}.

We may, without loss of generality, set
\upsilon=1
, as doing so
will not alter the wavefront sets involved in this proof. With:

\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right)=\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right).

We observe that:

\mathcal{D}\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right)=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&\gamma^{\prime}\left(y\right)\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathcal{D}\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right)^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
\gamma^{\prime}\left(y\right)^{T}\end{bmatrix},

and so
\ker\mathcal{D}\chi\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right)^{T}
is trivial, indicating
that the pullback
w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)
is
indeed well-defined, and:

WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}\subseteq\chi^{\star}WF\left(w\right),

Where:

(2.3)

\chi^{\star}WF\left(w\right)=\left\{\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\gamma^{\prime}\left(y\right)\cdot\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\right):\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)\right\}.

We now wish to show that:

WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}=\chi^{\star}WF\left(w\right).

Indeed, let
y_{0}>-\varepsilon
, and define:

\psi_{y_{0}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\vec{\mathbf{x}}+\gamma\left(y_{0}\right)\\
y_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

Then:

\mathcal{D}_{\psi_{y_{0}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.\quad\mathcal{D}_{\psi_{y_{0}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix},

and so:

\ker\mathcal{D}_{\psi_{y_{0}}}\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}\right)^{T}=\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{0}},\eta\right):\eta\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Therefore, the set of normals for
\psi_{y_{0}}
satisfies:

N_{\psi_{y_{0}}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\left(-\varepsilon,\infty\right)\right)\times\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{0}},\eta\right):\eta\in\mathbb{R}\right\},

and so
N_{\psi_{y_{0}}}\cap WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}=\emptyset
.
Thus, the pullback
\psi_{y_{0}}^{\star}\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}

is well-defined, and is in fact equal to
w
. Then:

(2.4)

WF\left(w\right)=WF\left(\psi_{y_{0}}^{\star}\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}\right)\subseteq\psi_{y_{0}}^{\star}WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\},

where:

\displaystyle\psi_{y_{0}}^{\star}WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right):\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}+\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y_{0}\right),y_{0}\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\eta\right)\right)\in WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}\right\}.

Thus, if we chose
\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y_{0}\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\gamma^{\prime}\left(y_{0}\right)\cdot\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\right)\in\chi^{\star}WF\left(w\right)
,
this choice was based on choosing
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y_{0}\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)
.
We then have by
2.4
that
\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y_{0}\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in\psi_{y_{0}}^{\star}WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}
.
That is,

\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y_{0}\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\eta\right)\right)=\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y_{0}\right)+\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y_{0}\right),y_{0}\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\eta\right)\right)\in WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\},

for some
\eta
. Of course, one only needs to choose
\eta=\gamma^{\prime}\left(y_{0}\right)\cdot\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}
,
and this will yield the desired set inclusion.

Next, we can use
1.9
to describe
WF\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y+t\right)\right)\,dt\right\}

as follows:

\displaystyle WF\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y+t\right)\right)\,dt\right\}\backslash WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\subseteq\bigcup_{\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\eta\right)\perp\left(\vec{\mathbf{0}},-1\right)}\left\{\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y-t\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\eta\right)\right):\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt\left.\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},\eta\right)\right)\in WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\},t\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\bigcup_{\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y-t\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},0\right)\right):\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},0\right)\right)\in\chi^{\star}WF\left(w\right),t\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\bigcup_{\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y-t\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},0\right)\right):\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\perp\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right),t\geq 0\right\}.

Finally,

\displaystyle WF\left\{\left.\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y+t\right)\right)\,dt\right|_{y=0}\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\subseteq\chi_{0}^{\star}WF\left\{w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)\right)\right\}\cup\chi_{0}^{\star}\left\{\left(\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},y-t\right),\left(\vec{\mathbf{\xi}},0\right)\right):\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\left.\xi\in\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right)^{\perp},\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(y\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right),t\geq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt=WF\left(w\right)\cup

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\,\middle|\,\exists t\geq 0:\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\in\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)^{\perp}\right\}.

It should be noted that the integral
2.1
reduces
to the distributional anti-partial derivative developed in the previous
section when
\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}
parametrizes a ray:

\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)=t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\quad t\geq 0,\vec{\mathbf{v}}\in\mathcal{S}^{n-1}.

Furthermore, the result obtained in
2.2
in
this case is:

\displaystyle WF\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}w\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right)\right)\upsilon\left(t\right)\,dt\right\}\backslash WF\left(w\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt\subseteq\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\,\middle|\,\exists t\geq 0:\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\perp\gamma^{\prime}\left(t\right)\And\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-\vec{\mathbf{\gamma}}\left(t\right),\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.00005pt=\left\{\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\,\middle|\,\exists t\geq 0:\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\perp\vec{\mathbf{v}}\And\left(\vec{\mathbf{x}}-t\vec{\mathbf{v}},\vec{\mathbf{\xi}}\right)\in WF\left(w\right)\right\},

which is an equivalent formulation to
1.4
.

Algebraicity of ratios of special L L -values for GL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{GL}(n)
Ankit Rai, Gunja Sachdeva

Abstract
We prove, under certain assumptions, algebraicity of the ratio L ⁡ ( m , Π × χ ) / L ⁡ ( m , Π × χ ′ ) L(m,\Pi\times\chi)/L(m,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime}) , where Π \Pi is a cuspidal automorphic cohomological unitary representation of GL n ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) , and χ \chi , χ ′ \chi^{\prime} are finite order Hecke characters such that χ ∞ = χ ∞ ′ = sgn r \chi_{\infty}=\chi^{\prime}_{\infty}=\mathrm{sgn}^{r} , and m , r m,r are specific positive integers which depends only on Π ∞ \Pi_{\infty} . The methods in this article are a generalization of those in the work of Mahnkopf [ Cohomology of arithmetic groups, parabolic subgroups and the special values of L L -functions of GL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{GL}(n) , J. Inst. Math. Jussieu, 4 (2005)].

Introduction

The investigation into the algebraic properties of critical values of
L
-functions has a rich history, notably concerning the
L
-functions associated with holomorphic modular forms pioneered by Manin and Shimura in the 1960s.
In 1979, Deligne
[
Del79
]
formulated conjectures regarding the special values of
L
-functions associated with motives. Given the close relationship between automorphic representations and motives, it is natural to anticipate similar conjectures concerning the algebraic nature of special values of automorphic
L
-functions.
This article provides (partial) confirmation of Deligne’s conjecture within the realm of automorphic representations for the specific case under consideration. To present the main theorems of this article, we now introduce the necessary terminology.

Let
G_{n}
denote the algebraic group
\mathrm{GL}(n)
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
T_{n}\subset B_{n}\subset G_{n}
respectively denote the maximal torus given by the diagonal matrices and the Borel subgroup given by the upper triangular matrices.
The results in this article are already known in the case when
n
is even, due to Mahnkopf
[
Mah05
, p. 557, Remark]
.
We thus proceed with the assumption that
n
odd.
Consider the natural identification
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cong X^{*}(T_{n})
given by
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\mapsto\big(\diag(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\mapsto t^{a_{1}}_{1}\cdots t^{a_{n}}_{n}\big)
. Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},0,-\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},\dots,-\mu_{1})
be a character of
T_{n}
and
\lambda=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},-\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},\dots,-\mu_{1})
be a character of
T_{n-1}
.
Assume that
\mu
is a dominant character. Let
E_{\mu}
and
E_{\lambda}
be the finite dimensional irreducible representations with highest weights
\mu
and
\lambda
respectively.
Let

of type
(2,\dots,2,1)

denote the standard parabolic subgroup of type
(2,\dots,2,1,1)

Let

Any (irreducible) automorphic representation admits a factorization as a tensor product

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Pi
and
\Sigma
be as above. Assume that
\pi
satisfies
\mathbf{Assumption}(\mu,\chi_{n-2},\chi_{n-1})
. Then there exists a non zero complex number
p(\Pi_{\infty},\Sigma_{\infty})
such that

L(\tfrac{1}{2},\Pi\times\Sigma)\sim_{~\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}p(\Pi_{f})p(\Sigma_{f})p(\Pi_{\infty},\Sigma_{\infty})

where
\sim_{~\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}
represents equality up to an element of the algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
.

(
As
L(\tfrac{1}{2},\Pi\times\Sigma)\sim_{~\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}L^{S}(\tfrac{1}{2},\Pi\times\Sigma)
, we can consider the partial
L
-function
L^{S}
in which the Euler product is taken over places outside
S
containing the places of ramification of
\Pi
or of
\Sigma
.
)

The quantities
p(\Pi_{f})
and
p(\Sigma_{f})
arise from the comparison of the two rational structure on the representations; one arising from the Whittaker model (that is, via Fourier coefficients) and the other obtained by the restriction of the Betti rational structure under the maps in equation (
2
) below.
These are commonly called as the Betti-Whittaker periods. A priori these periods may be unrelated to those put forth by Deligne, but a relation is conjectured in
[
Bla97
, §L]
(see also
[
Rag12
, §6]
). A few related results can be found in
[
GrH16
,
GrL17
]
.
The period
p(\Sigma_{f})
is further analyzed and is shown that it decomposes into a product involving a specific transcendental quantity and a product of certain
L
-functions for the group
G_{2}
(see Lemma
7.2
).
The complex number
p(\Pi_{\infty},\Sigma_{\infty})
is obtained by evaluating a pairing of two chosen differential forms at infinity, the latter eventually amounting to a sum of local Rankin-Selberg integrals for certain vectors in
\Pi_{\infty}
and
\Sigma_{\infty}
(See §
6.2
).
Finally, Theorem
1.1
yields the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Pi
be a unitary cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{A})
which is cohomological with respect to the finite dimensional representation
E_{\mu}
.
Let
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
be two distinct finite order algebraic Hecke characters such that
\chi_{\infty}=\chi^{\prime}_{\infty}=\epsilon(\Pi_{\infty})\mathrm{sgn}
. Assume that either
n=5
or
\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\geq 1
, then

\frac{L(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1,\Pi\times\chi)}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}.

Before we delve into the history of the problem and the technical aspects of the current article, it is important to discuss the implicit and explicit assumptions. We remark that
\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1
is a critical point of the
L
-functions
L(s,\Pi\times\chi)
and
L(s,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})
(see §
2.4
).
In addition to the assumptions of nonvanishing of certain
L
-values mentioned in the statements of the theorems above, we also require that the
L
-values
L(1,\Pi\times\chi)
and
L(1,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})
do not vanish in order to deduce Theorem
1.2
from Theorem
1.1
.
Such nonvanishing results are known from the work of Jacquet and Shalika
[
Sar02
]
. If
\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\geq 1
, then the nonvanishing hypothesis is known from the general results of Shalika.
Note that the above theorem provides additional evidence supporting a conjecture of Blasius
[
Bla97
]
for regular algebraic cuspidal representations that are cohomological with respect to nonregular weights.

In this manuscript we have chosen
\mathbb{Q}
as the base field to simplify the representation theory at infinity. This simplification enables a clearer presentation of the methods needed to establish the holomorphy, injectivity and Galois equivariance of the map ‘Eis’ (see §
4

As pointed out to us by an anonymous referee, the methods of this article should extend to any number field.

This article grew out of an attempt to generalise Mahnkopf’s approach in
[

]
to treat nonregular coefficient systems in the case when

He then constructs a

can be realized in the boundary cohomology of

with the chosen coefficient system

.
This is elaborated in detail in
[

(n-3,1,1)
. Instead, Mahnkopf suggests a generalization by considering a parabolic subgroup of the type
(2,\dots,2,1,1)
(see
[
Mah05
, p.635]

in this article.
When
\Sigma
is an isobaric sum of unitary cuspidal automorphic representation over a CM field, Grobner
[

, Grobner-Lin
[

In contrast to these results, we have treated the case with the base field

Experts will recognize that Theorem

1.1
Organization of the article

The article is organized as follows:

§1.3 gives an indexing of the notations used in this article.

§2 gives a short introduction to the various notions used in this article.

§3.1 contains necessary results on cohomological representations, in particular, the proof of the fact that
\Sigma_{\infty}
, a reducible representation of
G_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
, is cohomological.
§3.2 discusses results on global intertwining operators. These will be used to prove that a certain Eisenstein series is well-defined at a certain point and gives an intertwining map from the representation
\Sigma_{f}
to the cohomology of the locally symmetric space associated to
G_{n-1}
.

§4 proves the aforementioned result on Eisenstein series and also obtains the Galois equivariance of the map
\mathrm{Eis^{coh}}
.

§5 pins down the choices of Whittaker vectors
w_{\Pi}
and
w_{\Sigma}
for
\Pi
and
\Sigma
respectively, such that the global Rankin-Selberg integral
\Psi(1/2,w_{\Pi},w_{\Sigma})
(see §
5.3
) is the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function up to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
.

§6 completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

§7 relates the Betti-Whittaker period of
\Sigma
to the Betti-Whittaker period of
\pi
and certain
L
-values. Finally, §7.3 finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Preliminaries

2.1
Automorphic representations

Let
\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}
denote the space of automorphic form on
G_{n}
which transforms as a character
\eta:Z_{n}(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. This space comes equipped with the right regular action of
G_{n}(\mathbb{A})
.
To an automorphic form
\phi
on
G_{n}
and any parabolic subgroup
M_{P}U_{P}=P\subset G_{n}
, we may associate a function
\phi^{U_{P}}\in C^{\infty}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})U_{P}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))
defined as follows

\phi^{U_{P}}(g)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{U_{P}(\mathbb{A})}f(ug)du.

It is known that the constant term determine many aspects of the theory of automorphic form. An automorphic form
\phi\in\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}
is said to be a cusp form iff
\{\phi^{U_{P}}\equiv 0\;|\;P\subset G_{n}\text{ is a parabolic subgroup}\}
.
The subspace of all cusp forms is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{cusp}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}
and can be easily seen to be preserved under the action of
G_{n}(\mathbb{A})
. Infact this representation decomposes with finite multiplicity (with multiplicity one for
G_{n}
) and each of the irreducible constituents are called as cuspidal automorphic representation.

The subspace
\mathcal{A}_{cusp}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}\subset\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}
admits a direct summand and this summand is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{Eis}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}
.
The subscript ‘Eis’ is assigned to remember that the this subspace is generated by cuspidal Eisenstein series and its residues; a nontrivial fact due to Waldspurger. This subspace is well studied and in various contexts among whom this work crucially depend on the results of Franke
[
Fra98
,
FrS98
]
and its refinement in
[
Gro13
]
.
We now mention a key construction which forms the building blocks of
\mathcal{A}_{Eis}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\eta}
.
For a parabolic subgroup
U_{P}M_{P}=P\subset G_{n}
, denote by
\mathrm{Ind}^{G_{n}(\mathbb{A})}_{P(\mathbb{A})}
the normalized induction functor. For
\sigma
a cusp automorphic representation of the Levi
M_{P}(\mathbb{A})
and
\chi
a character of the center of the Levi,
the induced representations
\mathrm{Ind}^{G_{n}(\mathbb{A})}_{P(\mathbb{A})}(\sigma\otimes\chi^{s})
for
s\in\mathbb{C}
admits a map to the space of automorphic forms
\mathcal{A}\left(G_{n}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A})\right)
via Eisenstein series. More precisely the realization of induced representations as functions is a two step process as below

\mathrm{Ind}^{G_{n}(\mathbb{A})}_{P(\mathbb{A})}(\sigma\otimes\chi^{s})\hookrightarrow C^{\infty}(P(\mathbb{Q})U_{P}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A})))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Eis}}\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A})).

It is known the Eisenstein series converges when
s
lies in a certain half plane, and can be continued meromorphically to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
, thus the above map makes sense for any
s\in\mathbb{C}
(taking residues if needed which we will not need to consider). We refer the reader to
[
Bor79
]
for more on automorphic forms and automorphic representations.

2.2
Locally symmetric spaces and their cohomology

For any compact open subgroup
K_{f}\subset G_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and a maximal connected compact subgroup
K^{0}_{n,\infty}
of
\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
, define the associated locally symmetric spaces to be

\tilde{S}_{n}(K_{f})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A})/K_{f}K_{n,\infty}^{0},\quad S_{n}(K_{f})\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A})/K_{f}K_{n,\infty}^{0}Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{0},

and call the quotient map
p:\tilde{S}_{n}(K_{f})\rightarrow S_{n}(K_{f})
. There is a
\pi_{0}(G_{n}(\mathbb{R}))\times G_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-module

H^{\bullet}(S_{n},E)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varinjlim{}_{K_{f}}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(S_{n}(K_{f}),E),

and the cohomology of
S_{n}(K_{f})
can be recovered by taking invariants under the action of the group
K_{f}
. Recall the well-known
G_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{f})\times\pi_{0}(G_{n}(\mathbb{R}))
-equivariant isomorphism known as Borel’s conjecture and now a theorem due to Franke
[
Fra98
]

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(S_{n},E)\simeq\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},K^{0}_{n,\infty}Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{0};\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))\otimes E)(\chi_{E}).

Let
\iota:G_{n-1}\rightarrow G_{n}
be the map
g\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}g&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}
. Then
\iota
induces a map on locally symmetric spaces of respective groups, which will also be denoted by
\iota
.

2.2.1
Cuspidal cohomology

The inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{{cusp}}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))
of the space of cusp forms in the space of automorphic functions induces, via results of Borel and Franke, an injection in
(\mathfrak{g}_{n},K_{n,\infty})
-cohomology. Cuspidal cohomology is defined to be the image

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{cusp}(S_{n}(K_{f}),E)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\im\left(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},K^{0}_{n,\infty}Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{0};\mathcal{A}_{cusp}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))^{K_{f}}\otimes E)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(S_{n}(K_{f}),E)\right).

Using the decomposition of the space of cusp forms into a direct sum of cuspidal automorphic representations, we get the following fundamental decomposition of
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{cusp}(S_{n},E)
as
\pi_{0}(G_{n}(\mathbb{R}))\times G_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-modules

\mathrm{H}_{cusp}^{\bullet}(S_{n},E)=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Pi\subset\mathcal{A}_{cusp}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))\end{subarray}_{\chi_{E}}}\Pi_{f}\otimes\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},K^{0}_{n,\infty}Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{0};\Pi_{\infty}\otimes E).

2.2.2
Eisenstein cohomology

The inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{Eis}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\chi_{E}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\chi_{E}}
induces a map in
(\mathfrak{g}_{n},K^{0}_{n,\infty})
-cohomology. Define

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{Eis}(S_{n}(K_{f}),E)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{im}\left(\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},K_{n,\infty}Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})^{0};\mathcal{A}_{Eis}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))^{K_{f}}\otimes E)_{\chi_{E}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(S_{n}(K_{f}),E)\right).

This subspace of cohomology is called the Eisenstein cohomology.
The decomposition of the space
\mathcal{A}_{Eis}(G_{n}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G_{n}(\mathbb{A}))_{\chi_{E}}
according to their cuspidal support allows us to understand the Eisenstein cohomology in terms of simpler objects (See
[
Fra98
,
FrS98
]
).
Unlike cuspidal cohomology, the classes in Eisenstein cohomology often cannot be represented by forms with compact support.

.

7.3
Conclusion of the proof of theorem

Let
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{(n-3)/2}
be distinct cuspidal automorphic representation of
G_{2}
such that
\pi_{i\infty}=D_{2\mu_{i}+n-2i}
, and
\Pi,\chi,\chi^{\prime}
be given as in the statement of the theorem.
Grunwald-Wang theorem asserts that it is possible to choose an auxiliary Hecke character
\eta
of finite order such that
\eta_{\infty}=\epsilon(\Pi_{\infty})\mathrm{sgn}
,
\eta
is unramified outside of
S_{\pi}\cup S_{\chi}\cup S_{\chi^{\prime}}
and
\eta_{v}\notin\{\chi_{v},\chi^{\prime}_{v}\}
for
n\notin S_{\pi}\cup S_{\chi}\cup S_{\chi^{\prime}}
.
We first consider the case
\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\geq 1
. There is the following equality,

\frac{L(s,\Pi\times\Sigma(\pi,\chi^{\prime},\eta))}{L(s,\Pi\times\Sigma(\pi,\chi,\eta))}=\frac{L(s+\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1/2,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(s+\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1/2,\Pi\times\chi)}.

For
s=1/2
, Theorem
1.1
applied to the above equation implies

\frac{L(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1,\Pi\times\chi)}=\frac{p(\Sigma(\pi,\chi^{\prime},\eta))}{p(\Sigma(\pi,\chi,\eta))}.

(24)

Applying Lemma
7.2
to the equation (
24
) we get the equality

\frac{L(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1,\Pi\times\chi)}\sim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\frac{v_{\Sigma(\pi,\chi^{\prime},\eta)}(1)}{v_{\Sigma(\pi,\chi,\eta)}(1)}.

Using the Casselman-Shalika formula the above ratio can be evaluated to be the ratio

\frac{v_{\Sigma(\pi,\chi^{\prime},\eta)}(1)}{v_{\Sigma(\pi,\chi,\eta)}(1)}=\frac{\prod_{i}L(1-(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1/2),\pi_{i}\times\chi^{-1})}{\prod_{i}L(1-(\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+1/2),\pi_{i}\times\chi^{\prime-1})}\cdot\frac{L(2\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+2,\chi\times\eta^{-1})}{L(2\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+2,\chi^{\prime}\times\eta^{-1})}.

Note that
1/2-\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
is a critical points for the
L
-functions
L(s,\pi_{i}\times\eta)
for any finite order Hecke character and
0\leq i\leq(n-3)/2
. Since the rationality of the ratios of the
\mathrm{GL}(2)
and
\mathrm{GL}(1)

L
-functions at all critical points is known from the work of Shimura, the proof of the theorem is complete in this case.

Let us now assume that
n=5
, then
(n-3)/2=1
and we call
\pi_{1}
to be
\pi
. The only case that remains is to consider
\mu_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}=0
.
Let
\eta^{\prime}
be a finite order Hecke character such that
\eta^{\prime}_{\infty}=\mathrm{sgn}
, and
\eta^{\prime}
is unramified outside of
S_{\pi}
and
L(1/2,\pi\times\eta^{\prime})\neq 0
. Let
\eta^{\prime\prime}=\eta^{-1}\chi
and define
\tilde{\pi}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\pi\otimes\eta^{\prime\prime}
.
Since we are allowed to choose
\pi
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\pi
is cohomological and
L(1/2,\pi\times\chi)\neq 0
.
Grunwald-Wang theorem asserts that it is possible to choose a finite order Hecke character
\psi^{\prime\prime}
such that
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{\infty}=\mathrm{sgn}
, whereas
\chi_{v}\neq\psi^{\prime\prime}_{v}
,
\chi^{\prime}_{v}\neq\eta^{\prime\prime}_{v}
for any
v\in S_{\pi}\cup S_{\chi}\cup S_{\chi^{\prime}}
and
\eta^{\prime\prime}_{v}
is unramified for
v\notin S_{\pi}\cup S_{\chi}\cup S_{\chi^{\prime}}
.
A theorem of Rohrlich
[
Roh89
, Theorem]
implies that there exists
\psi^{\prime}
a character of finite order which is unramified outside of
S_{\pi}\cup S_{\chi}\cup S_{\chi^{\prime}}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\psi^{\prime}_{\infty}
is trivial which furthermore satisfies
L(1/2,\pi\times\psi^{\prime\prime}\psi^{\prime})\neq 0
.
Let
\psi=\psi^{\prime}\psi^{\prime\prime}
, then
\psi
satisfies the following :
\psi_{\infty}=\mathrm{sgn}
,
\psi_{v}\neq\chi_{v},\chi^{\prime}_{v}
for any
v\in S_{\pi}\cup S_{\chi}\cup S_{\chi^{\prime}}
. These conditions on the characters ensure that the results from previous sections in particular Lemma
7.1
and Theorem
1.1
can be applied.
Similarly choose
\pi^{\prime}
which is a twist of
\pi
by a Hecke character of finite order which is trivial at infinity and unramified outside
S_{\pi}
, and another finite order Hecke character
\psi^{\prime}
such that
L(1/2,\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi^{\prime})L(1/2,\pi^{\prime}\times\psi^{\prime})\neq 0
. Consider the ratio,

\frac{L(s,\Pi\times\Sigma(\pi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime},\eta))}{L(s,\Pi\times\Sigma(\pi,\chi,\eta))}=\frac{L(s+1/2,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(s+1/2,\Pi\times\chi)}.

For
s=1/2
, Theorem
1.1
applied to the above equation implies

\frac{L(1,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(1,\Pi\times\chi)}=\frac{p(\Sigma(\pi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}))}{p(\Sigma(\pi,\chi,\psi))}.

(25)

Observe that
p^{\mathrm{aut-B}}(\pi,\Sigma_{\infty})
and
p^{\mathrm{W-aut}}(\pi_{f})
is invariant under twist by a finite order Hecke character which is trivial at infinity since these numbers arise when comparison of rational structures over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. Applying Lemma
7.2
coupled with this observation we get

\frac{L(1,\Pi\times\chi^{\prime})}{L(1,\Pi\times\chi)}\sim_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}\frac{L(1/2,\pi^{\prime}\times\chi^{\prime-1})L(2,\chi^{\prime-1}\psi^{\prime})}{L(1/2,\pi\times\chi^{-1})L(2,\chi^{-1}\psi)}.

Since the rationality of the ratios of the
\mathrm{GL}(2)
and
\mathrm{GL}(1)

L
-functions is known, the proof of the theorem for
n=5
is complete.

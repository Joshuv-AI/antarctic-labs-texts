1.
introduction

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring. If
K
is an ideal of
A
then
\overline{K}
denotes the integral closure of
K
.
In this paper we relate local cohomology and integral closure. We prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring and let
I
be an ideal in
A
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{J_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be a multiplicative filtration of ideals in
A
such that
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}J_{n}
is a finitely generated
A
-algebra. Let
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
and assume
I^{n}\subseteq J_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. The following two assertions are equivalent:

(1)

For all
i\geq 0
we have
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
.

(2)

J_{n}\subseteq\overline{I^{n}}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Note the assertion (2)
\implies
(1) is trivial and is left to the reader. The real content of the result is (1)
\implies
(2).

The essential ingredient is the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring and
I\subseteq J\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
with
\ell(J/I)<\infty
. Set
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
and
\mathcal{S}=A[Jt]
. Suppose for all
i\geq 0
we have
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
. Then
J\subseteq\overline{I}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
1.2
by taking an appropriate Veronese of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F})
and an appropriate localization. However we need to ensure that
JA_{P}
does not blowup to
A_{P}
while
IA_{P}
remains
P
-primary. So we need the following result:

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d\geq 0
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Set
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
and
\mathcal{S}=A[t]
. Then there exists
i\geq 0
such that
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n\geq 0
.

Here is an overview of the contents of this paper. In section two we discuss a few preliminary results that we need. In section three we prove Theorem
1.2
. In section four we prove
Lemma
1.3
. Finally in section five we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we discuss a few preliminary results that we need. In this paper all rings considered are Noetherian.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring of dimension
d
and let
I
be an ideal in
A
. Let
\mathcal{R}=A[It]
. Let
J\supseteq I
be another ideal and set
\mathcal{S}=A[Jt]
. Note
\mathcal{S}
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module.

2.1
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M_{n}
be an
\mathcal{R}
-module. We say
M
is a
quasi-finite

\mathcal{R}
-module if

M_{n}
is a finitely generated
A
-module for all
n\geq 0
and
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(M)_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
. If the residue field of
A
is uncountable and
M
is a quasi-finite
\mathcal{R}
-module then there
exists
xt\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
which is
M
-filter regular, i.e.,
(0\colon_{M}xt)_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
, see
[
3
, 2.7]
.

2.2
.

Set
\mathcal{L}^{J}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A/J^{n+1}
. Note
\mathcal{S}
is a
\mathcal{R}
-submodule of
A[t]
and
A[t]/\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{L}^{J}(-1)
. Thus
\mathcal{L}^{J}
is an
\mathcal{R}
-module.
This module was introduced in
[
6
]
.

2.3
.

Let
G_{J}(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}J^{n}/J^{n+1}
be the associated graded ring of
J
. Then
G_{J}(A)
is a quotient ring of
\mathcal{S}
and so an
\mathcal{R}
-module.
The exact sequence

0\rightarrow\frac{J^{n}}{J^{n+1}}\rightarrow\frac{A}{J^{n+1}}\rightarrow\frac{A}{J^{n}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{for $n\geq 0$},

induces an exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow G_{J}(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{J}(-1)\rightarrow 0.

2.4
.

Assume
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary. The multiplicity of
I
is defined as

e_{0}(I)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d!}{n^{d}}\ell(A/I^{n}).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we give:

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We make several reductions to prove the result.

(1) It suffices to assume that the residue field of
A
is uncountable.

If the residue field
k
of
A
is finite or countably infinite then we go to the flat extension
B=A[[X]]_{\mathfrak{m}A[[X]]}
. The residue field of
B
is
k((X))
which is uncountable.
Set
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\mathcal{R}\otimes_{A}B=B[IBt]
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}\otimes_{A}B=B[JBt]
. Then note that

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
is a flat extension of
\mathcal{R}
. We also have
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. It follows from
[
1
, 4.3.2]
that for all
i\geq 0

H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})\otimes_{A}B.

Therefore
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
.
As we are assuming that the result holds for
B
we get
JB\subseteq\overline{IB}
. As
B
is faithfully flat over
A
we have

J=JB\cap A\subseteq\overline{IB}\cap A=\overline{I}.

(for the first equality see
[
5
, 7.5]
and for the second see
[
4
, 1.6.2]
).

(2) We assert that
J^{n}\cap H^{0}_{I}(A)=0
for all
n\gg 0
.

It follows easily from the Artin-Rees lemma that
H^{0}_{I}(A)\cap I^{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
(say from
n\geq r
).
As
\ell(J/I)<\infty
we have
\mathfrak{m}^{s}J\subseteq I
for some
s\geq 1
. Then
J^{s+1}\subseteq I
. It follows that
J^{r(s+1)}\cap H^{0}_{I}(A)=0
.

(3) We may assume that the analytic spread of
I
is positive.

Suppose
I
has analytic spread zero. Then
I
is nilpotent. So
H^{0}_{I}(A)=A
. By (2) it follows that
J
is also nilpotent. So trivially we have
J\subseteq\overline{I}
.

(4) We may assume that
\operatorname{grade}I>0
.

By (3) we may assume that the analytic spread of
I
is positive. Suppose
\operatorname{grade}I=0
. Set
B=A/H^{0}_{I}(A)
,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=B[It]
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=B[Jt]
. Consider the natural exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
. Then
K_{n}=J^{n}\cap H^{0}_{I}(A)=0
for
n\gg 0
. Thus
K
is
\mathcal{R}_{+}
-torsion. So
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(K)=K
and
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(K)=0
for
i>0
.
It follows that we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})=H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\ \text{for}\ i>0.

It follows that
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
.
By graded independence theorem of local cohomology
[
1
, 4.2.1]
we have

H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cong H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\quad\text{for all}\ i\geq 0.

So
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
. As
\operatorname{grade}IB>0
and we are assuming the result holds when
\operatorname{grade}IB>0
; we have

JB\subseteq\overline{IB}
. By
[
4
, 1.2.5]
we have that
IB
is a reduction of
JB
. Say
J^{r+1}B=IJ^{r}B
. By (2), say
J^{n}\cap H^{0}_{I}(A)=0
for
n\geq s.

Let
n\geq\max\{r,s\}
. We have

J^{n+1}+H^{0}_{I}(B)=IJ^{n}+H^{0}_{I}(B).

Let
a\in J^{n+1}
. Then
a=b+c
where
b\in JI^{n}
and
c\in H^{0}_{I}(A)
. Then
c\in J^{n+1}\cap H^{0}_{I}(A)=0
. So
J^{n+1}=IJ^{n}
. Thus
I
is a reduction of
J
. Again by
[
4
, 1.2.5]

we have
J\subseteq\overline{I}
.

After these preliminaries we prove the result by induction on
d
. By (1) we may assume the residue field of
A
is uncountable. By (3) the result hold when
d=0
.

We first prove the result when
d=1
.
By (3) we may assume that
I
has positive analytic spread. By (4) we may assume
\operatorname{grade}I>0
. In particular
A
is Cohen-Macaulay and
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary.
We have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules
0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow A[t]\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{J}(-1)\rightarrow 0
. As
\operatorname{grade}I>0
we have
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(A[t])=0
. We have an inclusion

H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{L}^{J})\subseteq H^{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})
. So
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{L})_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
. Thus
\mathcal{L}^{J}
is a quasi-finite
\mathcal{R}
-module. Also as again
\operatorname{grade}I>0
we have that
\mathcal{L}^{I}
is
a quasi-finite
\mathcal{R}
-module. As the residue field of
A
is uncountable there exists
x\in I
such that
xt
is
\mathcal{L}^{I}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{J}
-filter regular, see
2.1
. Note
x
is
I
-superficial. So
(x)
is a reduction of
I
. Therefore
I^{n}\subseteq(x)
for all
n\gg 0
. As
J^{s}\subseteq I
for some
s
it follows that
J^{n}\subseteq(x)
for all
n\gg 0
. Set
W=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}J^{n+1}/I^{n+1}
. We have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow W\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{J}\rightarrow 0.
Going mod
xt
and as
xt
is
\mathcal{L}^{I}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{J}
-filter regular we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow V\rightarrow W/xtW\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{I}/xt\mathcal{L}^{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{J}/xt\mathcal{L}^{J}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{where $V$ has finite length.}

We note that

(\mathcal{L}^{J}/xt\mathcal{L}^{J})_{n}=A/(J^{n+1},x)=A/(x)\quad\text{for all $n\gg 0$}.

Similarly
(\mathcal{L}^{I}/xt\mathcal{L}^{I})_{n}=A/(x)
for all
n\gg 0
. It follows that
(W/xtW)_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
. Note
xt
is also
W
-filter regular. It follows that
\ell(J^{n}/I^{n})
is constant for all
n\gg 0
. So
e_{0}(I)=e_{0}(J)
. As
A
is Cohen-Macaulay it follows from Rees multiplicity theorem, see
[
8
]
(also see
[
4
, 11.3.1]
). that
I
is a reduction of
J
. So
J\subseteq\overline{I}
.

Now assume
d\geq 2
and the result has been proved for all rings of dimension
\leq d-1
. By (3) we may assume that
I
has positive analytic spread. By (4) we may assume
\operatorname{grade}I>0
.
We have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules
0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow A[t]\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{J}(-1)\rightarrow 0
. As
\operatorname{grade}I>0
we have
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(A[t])=0
. We have an inclusion

H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{L}^{J})\subseteq H^{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})
. So
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{L}^{J})_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
. So
\mathcal{L}^{J}
is a quasi-finite
\mathcal{R}
-module. Also as
\operatorname{grade}I>0
we have that
\mathcal{L}^{I}
is
a quasi-finite
\mathcal{R}
-module. As the residue field of
A
is uncountable there exists
x\in I
such that
xt
is
\mathcal{L}^{I}\oplus\mathcal{L}^{J}
-filter regular, see
2.1
. Note
(J^{n+1}\colon x)=J^{n}
for all
n\gg 0.
Similarly
(I^{n+1}\colon x)=I^{n}
for all
n\gg 0
. In particular
x
is
A
-regular. Set
B=A/(x)
,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=B[It]
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=B[Jt]
. We have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow 0.

It follows that
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\overline{\mathcal{S}})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
. We have an exact sequence
of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{for $n\geq 1$ we have}\ K_{n}=\frac{J^{n}\cap(x)}{xJ^{n-1}}.

Note
K_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
. So
K
is
\mathcal{R}
-torsion. It follows that
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
.
By graded independence theorem of local cohomology,
[
1
, 4,2,1]
, we have

H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cong H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\quad\text{for all}\ i\geq 0.

So
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
.
As
\dim B=d-1
, by our induction hypotheses we have
JB\subseteq\overline{IB}
. So
IB
is a reduction of
JB
. Thus there exists
m\geq 1
such that
J^{m+1}B=IJ^{m}B
.
We also have
(J^{n+1}\colon x)=J^{n}
say for
n\geq r
. Let
n\geq\max\{m,r+1\}
.
Then
J^{n+1}+(x)=IJ^{n}+(x)
. Let
a\in J^{n+1}
. Then
a=b+x\theta
where
b\in IJ^{n}
. Note
x\theta\in J^{n+1}
. So
\theta\in J^{n}
. It follows that
a\in JI^{n}
.
Thus
J^{n+1}=IJ^{n}
. So
I
is a reduction of
J
. It follows that
J\subseteq\overline{I}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1.3

In this section we give
a proof of Lemma
1.3
. The case when
\dim A=0
has to be proved separately.

Proof of Lemma
1.3
when
\dim A=0
.

We note that as
I
is nilpotent we get that
\mathcal{R}_{+}
is nilpotent. So
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{S}
. The result follows.
∎

When
d\geq 1
we need the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.3
)
Assume
d\geq 1
. If
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
then
\operatorname{grade}I>0
.

Proof.

Suppose if possible
\operatorname{grade}I=0
. Then
U=H^{0}_{I}(A)\neq 0
. There exists
n>0
such that
I^{n}U=0
.
It follows that

\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}Ut^{n}\subseteq H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}).

This contradicts the fact that
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
.
∎

We use induction on
d\geq 1
to prove Lemma
1.3
. The following result helps in the base case
d=1
.

Proposition 4.2

(with hypotheses as in Lemma
1.3
)
.

Assume further
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Then
H^{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose if possible
H^{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
. We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{I}(-1)\rightarrow 0.

So
H^{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{L}^{I})_{n}=0
for all
\mathfrak{n}\gg 0
. Let
\mathfrak{M}
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
\mathcal{R}
. Then by
[
7
, 3.1]
we have
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{L})\cong H^{i}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L})
for all
i\geq 0
. So

H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L})_{n}=0
for all say for
n\geq r
.
Set
G=G_{I}(A)
the associated graded ring of
A
with respect to
I
.
We have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow G\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{I}(-1)\rightarrow 0
of
\mathcal{R}
-modules. This yields a long exact sequence in cohomology

H^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L})_{n-1}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G)_{n}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L}^{I})_{n}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L}^{I})_{n-1}\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G)_{n}=0.

So we have surjections
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L}^{I})_{n}\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L}^{I})_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. As
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L})_{n}=0
for all
n\geq r
it follows that infact
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(L)_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As
H^{0}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\mathcal{L})_{n}=0
for
n<0
it follows that
H^{1}_{\mathfrak{M}}(G)_{n}=0
for
n\leq 0
. This is a contradiction, see
[
1
, 17.1.10]
.
Thus
H^{1}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}\neq 0
for infinitely many
n\geq 0
.
∎

We now give

Proof of Lemma
1.3
when
\dim A>0
.

We prove the result by induction on
d=\dim A
. We first consider the case when
d=1
. If
H^{0}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for

then by
4.1
we have
\operatorname{grade}I>0
. Thus

is Cohen-Macaulay. By

we get

for infinitely many
n\geq 0
. The result follows in this case.

Now assume
d\geq 2
and assume the result holds for local rings of dimension

.
Assume if possible
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for all
n\gg 0
and for all
i\geq 0
.
By
4.1
we have
\operatorname{grade}I>0
. Let
x\in I
be
A
-regular. Then

is
\mathcal{S}
-regular.
We have an exact sequence of
\mathcal{R}
-modules

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(-1)\xrightarrow{xt}\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}/xt\mathcal{S}\rightarrow 0.

It follows that
H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}/xt\mathcal{S})_{n}=0
for all

and for all

.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we give

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By an exercise problem in
[
2
, 4.5.12]
, some Veronese of
\mathcal{S}
is standard graded. Say
\mathcal{S}^{<l>}=A[Jt]
. Local cohomology commutes with the Veronese functor
[
1
, 12.4.6]
. So we have

H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}^{<l>}_{+}}(\mathcal{S}^{<l>})_{n}=H^{i}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{S})_{nl}=0\quad\text{for all $n\gg 0$ and for all $i\geq 0$}.

Furthermore
\mathcal{R}^{<l>}=A[I^{l}t]
. If we prove that
A[Jt]
is a finite
A[I^{l}t]
module (equivalently
J\subseteq\overline{I^{l}}
) then as
\mathcal{S}
is a finite
A[Jt]
-module, it follows that
\mathcal{S}
is a finite
\mathcal{R}
-module. Thus it suffices to assume
\mathcal{S}=A[Jt]
for some ideal
J
in
A
.

Suppose if possible
J\nsubseteq\overline{I}
. Then
K=J\cap\overline{I}
is a proper subset of
J
containing
I
. Set
\mathcal{T}=A[Kt]
. As
I
is a reduction of
K
we get
\sqrt{\mathcal{R}_{+}\mathcal{T}}=\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{+}}

Abstract
Let ϕ ⁡ ( n , H ) \phi(n,H) be the largest integer such that, for all graphs G G on n n vertices, the edge set E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) can be partitioned into at most ϕ ⁡ ( n , H ) \phi(n,H) parts, of which every part either is a single edge or forms a graph isomorphic to H H . Pikhurko and Sousa conjectured that ϕ ⁡ ( n , H ) = ex ⁡ ( n , H ) \phi(n,H)=\mathrm{ex}(n,H) for χ ⁡ ( H ) ⩾ 3 \chi(H)\geqslant 3 and all sufficiently large n n , where ex ⁡ ( n , H ) \mathrm{ex}(n,H) denotes the maximum number of edges of graphs on n n vertices that does not contain H H as a subgraph. A ( k , r ) (k,r) -fan is a graph on ( r − 1 ) ​ k + 1 (r-1)k+1 vertices consisting of k k cliques of order r r which intersect in exactly one common vertex. In this paper, we verify Pikhurko and Sousa’s conjecture for ( k , r ) (k,r) -fans. The result also generalizes a result of Liu and Sousa.

t\geqslant 2
, then at least two subsets of
A_{1},\ldots,A_{r-1}
satisfying that
|\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{s}\}\cap A_{i}|=q
by the choice of
q
. By Claim
6
, at least one of
V_{i}
,
i\in[1,r-1]
with
|V_{i}|=\left\lceil{\frac{n}{r-1}}\right\rceil
. Hence we can choose
V_{1}
and
i_{0}\neq 1
such that
|V_{1}|\leq\left\lceil{\frac{n}{r-1}}\right\rceil
and
|V_{i_{0}}|=\left\lceil{\frac{n}{r-1}}\right\rceil
. So,

\deg_{G^{\prime}}(u)\leqslant n-\left\lceil{\frac{n}{r-1}}\right\rceil-(r-1)q\leqslant\left\lfloor{\frac{r-2}{r-1}n}\right\rfloor-s.

Here we need to show that
G^{\prime}-u
is not an extremal graph for
F_{k,r}
. Let
H=G^{\prime}-u
.
Consider
u_{12}^{i_{0}}\in{V(H)}
. Then
\deg_{H}(u_{12}^{i_{0}})\leq n-\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil-(r-1)<\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}(n-1)\right\rfloor
. By Lemma
5
,
H
is not an extremal graph on
n-1
vertices for
F_{k,r}
and hence we are done.

If
t=1
, then
q=\frac{s-1}{r-1}+1
. Choose
i_{0}
such that
|V_{i_{0}}|=\max\limits_{\ell\in[2,r-1]}|V_{\ell}|
. Then
|V_{i_{0}}|\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rfloor
, otherwise, by Claim
6
,
|V_{1}|+...+|V_{r-1}|\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil+(r-2)(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rfloor-1)<n.
Hence, by Claim
6
,

\deg_{G^{\prime}}(u)\leqslant n-\left\lfloor{\frac{n}{r-1}}\right\rfloor-(r-1)q=\left\lfloor{\frac{r-2}{r-1}n}\right\rfloor-s-(r-1)<\left\lfloor{\frac{r-2}{r-1}n}\right\rfloor-s.

So we are done in this case.

(II).
Suppose that
G
contains a copy of
F_{qk,r}
with center
u\in{A_{1}\cup...\cup A_{r-1}}
and
q\geqslant 2
. We choose
u
so that
q
is maximum. If
q\geqslant{s+1}
then we are done since
F_{qk,r}
contains
s+1
edge-disjoint copies of
F_{k,r}
. So
q\leqslant{s}
. Then we must have
\deg_{G}(u)<n-\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil-(r-2)\right)+(q+1)k
, otherwise,
\deg_{G[A_{i}]}(u)\geq(q+1)k
for some
i\in[1,r-1]
with
u\in A_{i}
, then Claim
9
guarantees that there exists a copy of
F_{(q+1)k,r}
centered at
u
in
G
, contradicting the choice of
q
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by deleting the edges of the copy of
F_{qk,r}
and the edges of any further
s-q
copies of
F_{k,r}
. We have

\displaystyle\deg_{G^{\prime}}(u)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle n-\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil-(r-2)\right)+(q+1)k-1-(r-1)qk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor+(r-3)-((r-2)q-1)k

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor+(r-3)-(2r-5)k\ \ \mbox{(since $q\geq 2$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor-\left[\frac{(r-2)(k-1)}{2}+1\right]-\frac{(r-2)(k-1)}{2}-(r-3)k

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor-s\ \ \mbox{(since $s<\frac{(r-2)(k-1)}{2}+1$ by Claim\penalty\ \ref{Fact:upbound for s})}.

Hence
u
is the desired vertex and we are done.

(III).
Now, suppose that
(I)
and
(II)
do not hold. We obtain
G^{\prime}
from
G
by deleting the edges of any
s
copies of
F_{k,r}
. If there is a copy centered at
u\in{B_{1}\cup...\cup B_{r-1}}
, then, by Claim
6
,

\displaystyle\deg_{G^{\prime}}(u)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle n-\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil-(r-2)\right)-(r-1)k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor-(r-1)(k-1)-1

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor-s\ \ \mbox{(since $s<\frac{(r-2)(k-1)}{2}+1$ by Claim\penalty\ \ref{Fact:upbound for s})}.

Hence, all the centers of the
s
copies of
F_{k,r}
lie in
A_{1}\cup...\cup A_{r-1}
. By
(II)
, they must be pairwisely distinct. By
(I)
, at most
s-1
of them have degree at least
k+s-1
in
G[A_{i}]
,
i\in[1,r-1]
. Hence, at least one of the centers, say
u\in{A_{i}}
with
\deg_{G[A_{i}]}(u)\leqslant{k+s-2}
. By Claim
6
and
s<\frac{(r-2)(k-1)}{2}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\deg_{G^{\prime}}(u)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle n-\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{r-1}\right\rceil-(r-2)\right)+k+s-2-(r-1)k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor-(r-2)(k-1)+s-2

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{r-2}{r-1}n\right\rfloor-s.

Hence
u
is a desired vertex in
G^{\prime}
and we are done.

This completes the proof of Claim
8
and also completes the proof of Theorem
3
.

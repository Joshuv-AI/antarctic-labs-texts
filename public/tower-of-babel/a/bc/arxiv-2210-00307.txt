Abstract
This paper is devoted to primal conditions of error bounds for a general function. In terms of Bouligand tangent cones, lower Hadamard directional derivatives and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess of subsets, we provide several necessary and/or sufficient conditions of error bounds with mild assumptions. Then we use these primal results to characterize error bounds for composite-convex functions (i.e. the composition of a convex function with a continuously differentiable mapping). It is proved that the primal characterization of error bounds can be established via Bouligand tangent cones, directional derivatives and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess if the mapping is metrically regular at the given point. The accurate estimate on the error bound modulus is also obtained.

Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to study error bounds for an inequality defined by a composite-convex function; i.e. the composition of a convex function with a continuously differentiable mapping. Error bounds of extended-real-valued functions have been intensively studied for more than half a century. Error bounds have played an important role in various aspects of optimization and variational analysis including for instance, sensitivity analysis of linear programming (cf.

When dealing with error bounds, a large literature is devoted to provide dual characterizations and criteria in terms of subdifferentials or normal cones. To the best of our knowledge,

It is noted that a pretty natural idea is to study error bounds in terms of various primal derivative-like objects such as directional derivatives, contingent cones or slopes. Several criteria for error bounds were worked out in

Inspired by this issue, our goal in this article is to discuss error bounds of the inequality defined by a composite-convex function. Our work is to provide primal characterizations of error bounds and the accurate estimate on the error bound modulus in terms of the notions of Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess of a set beyond another one, of the Bouligand tangent cone and of the lower Hadamard directional derivative.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some definitions and preliminary results. In Section 3, we consider two concepts of metric regularity (of multifunctions) and the Shapiro first order contact property that are used in our analysis. Section 4 is devoted to the study of error bounds for the inequality defined by a composite-convex function. We first consider error bounds for a general inequality defined by a proper lower semicontinuous (not necessarily convex) function with the Shapiro first order contact property, and provide sufficient and/or necessary primal conditions of error bounds in terms of the Bouligand tangent cone, the lower Hadamard directional derivative and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess of subsets (see Theorems 4.1 and 4.2). When these results are applied to error bounds of composite-convex inequalities, the primal results on error bounds and on the error bound modulus can be obtained (see Theorems 4.4 and 4.5).

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space (or Euclidean space). Let
\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}
denote the closed unit ball of
X
. For
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
and
\delta>0
, let

\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
denote the open ball with center
\bar{x}
and
radius
\delta
. For a subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{X}
, we denote by
{\rm cl}(\Omega)
,
{\rm int}(\Omega)
and
{\rm bd}(\Omega)
the closure, the interior and the boundary of
\Omega
, respectively.

Let
A
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{X}
and
\bar{x}\in A
. We denote by

\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\bar{x}):=\mathop{\rm Limsup}\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{A-\bar{x}}{t}

the
Bouligand tangent cone
(also called
contingent cone
) of
A
at
\bar{x}
. Thus,
v\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\bar{x})
if and only if there exist a sequence
\{v_{n}\}
in
X
converging to
v
and a sequence

\{t_{n}\}
in
(0,\;+\infty)
decreasing to 0 such that
\bar{x}+t_{n}v_{n}\in A
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathbb{N}
denotes
the set of all natural numbers.

For any subsets
C
and
D
of
\mathbb{X}
, the
excess
of
C
beyond
D
is defined as:

\mathbf{e}(C,D):=\sup_{x\in C}\mathbf{d}(x,D),

(2.1)

where
\mathbf{d}(x,D):=\inf\{\|x-y\|:y\in D\}
and the convention is used that

\mathbf{e}(\emptyset,D):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0,&{\rm if}\ D\not=\emptyset,\\
\infty,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

The following proposition provides a characterization for the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess. We refer the reader to
(
DR2
, Page 138)
(or
(
WZ2022
, Proposition 3.1)
) for the proof in details.

Proposition 1

Let
C
and
D
be subsets of
\mathbb{X}
. Then

\mathbf{e}(C,D)=\inf\{\tau\geq 0:C\subseteq D+\tau\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}\}.

(2.3)

Given an extended-real-valued lower semicontinuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, we denote by
{\rm dom}(\varphi):=\{u\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi(u)<+\infty\}
its domain and by

{\rm epi}(\varphi):=\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{R}:\varphi(x)\leq\alpha\}

its epigraph.
\varphi
is said to be proper if its domain is nonempty.

The
lower Hadamard directional derivative
of
\varphi
at
x
along direction
h
is defined as

\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x;h):=\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0^{+},h^{\prime}\rightarrow h}\frac{\varphi(x+th^{\prime})-\varphi(x)}{t}.

(2.4)

We close this section with the following lemma cited from
(
SYZ
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space and
\Omega
be a nonempty closed
subset of
\mathbb{X}
. Let
\gamma\in(0,\;1)
. Then for any
x\not\in\Omega

there exists
z\in\Omega
such that

\gamma\|x-z\|<\min\{\mathbf{d}(x,\Omega),\mathbf{d}(x-z,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\Omega,z))\}.

In particular, if
\mathbb{X}
is of finite dimension,
z
can be chosen as the projection of
x
onto
\Omega
.

3
Metric regularity and the Shapiro first order contact property

This section recalls the two important concepts of
metric regularity
and of
Shapiro first order contact property
that are used in our analysis. It is known that metric regularity of multifunctions occurs to be closely related to Lipschtizian properties of inverse mappings. This well-known and significant property is an extension of surjectivity to nonlinear/set-valued mappings and goes back to the Banach-Schauder open mapping theorem and to the Lyusternik-Graves theorem. We first recall the definition of metric regularity.

Definition 3.1.
(i)
Let
F:\mathbb{X}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{Y}
be a multifunction between two Banach spaces and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
. Recall that
F
is said to be metrically regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x})
, if there exists a constant
\kappa\in(0,+\infty)
along with neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{y}
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,F^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa\mathbf{d}(y,F(x))\ \ {\it for\ all}\ (x,y)\in U\times V.

(3.1)

(ii)
A single-value mapping
\varPsi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
is said to be metrically regular at
\bar{x}
if
\varPsi
is metrically regular at
\bar{x}
for
\varPsi(\bar{x})
.

It is known from
Asen-book
;
M2
;
Penot2013
;
ioffe-book
;
thibault
that metric regularity of a multifunction is proved to be equivalent to the covering property. We refer for instance the readers to
(
M2
, Theorem 1.52)
for more details on this equivalence relationships and the modulus estimates for metric regularity and the covering property. Further the readers are invited to consult
ioffe-JAMS-1
;
ioffe-JAMS-2
for a survey and the development on metric regularity and
NTVT
for some regular properties of graphical tangent and normal cones to paraconvex multifunctions.

From
(
M2
, Lemma 1.56)
, we always have the automatic closedness of the derivative image for single-valued metrically regular mappings. The following proposition, as a result of independent interest, weakens the assumption of metric regularity therein.

Proposition 2

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
be a mapping between two Banach spaces and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
. Suppose that
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
and that there exist
\kappa,r>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(\bar{x},\varphi^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa\|\varphi(\bar{x})-y\|\ \ \forall y\in Y:\|y-\varphi(\bar{x})\|<r.

(3.2)

Then
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(\mathbb{X})
is a closed subspace of
\mathbb{Y}
.

Proof.
Let
y_{0}\in{\rm cl}(\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(\mathbb{X}))
. Then we can find a sequence
\{y_{k}\}
in
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(\mathbb{X})
such that

y_{k}\rightarrow y_{0}\ {\rm and}\ \|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|<\frac{1}{2^{k}}\ {\rm for\ all}\ k.

(3.3)

We claim that there exists a sequence
\{x_{k}\}
in
X
such that

\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|<\frac{3\kappa}{2^{k}}\ {\rm and}\ \|y_{k}-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{k})\|<\frac{1}{2^{k}}\ {\rm for\ all}\ k.

(3.4)

Granting this, one has
\{x_{k}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{X}
that converges to some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{X}
and it follows from (
3.4
) that
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{k})\rightarrow y_{0}
, which gives
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{0})=y_{0}
.

We define
x_{k}
iteratively. Let
x_{1}\in X
be such that
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{1})=y_{1}
. Suppose
x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}
have been given to satisfy (
3.4
) and construct
x_{k+1}
as follows.

Choose
u_{k+1}\in\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(y_{k+1})-x_{k}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be sufficiently small such that

3\varepsilon\kappa<\frac{1}{2^{2}},\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2^{k+2}}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \varepsilon\|u_{k+1}\|<\frac{1}{2^{k+2}}.

(3.5)

Since
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then there exists
\delta\in(0,r)
such that

\|\varphi(\bar{x}+w)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(w)\|<\varepsilon\|w\|\ \ \forall w:\|w\|<\delta.

(3.6)

Take
t_{k}>0
sufficiently small such that

\max\Big\{\frac{3t_{k}}{2^{k}},\frac{3\kappa}{2^{k}}t_{k},t_{k}\|u_{k+1}\|\Big\}<\delta.

Then by (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), one has

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1})-\varphi(\bar{x})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1})-\varphi(\bar{x})-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(t_{k}u_{k+1})\|+\|t_{k}\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u_{k+1})\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon t_{k}\|u_{k+1}\|+t_{k}(\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\|+\|y_{k}-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{k})\|)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle t_{k}\big(\frac{1}{2^{k+2}}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\big)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{3t_{k}}{2^{k}}<\delta<r.

This and (
3.2
) imply that

\mathbf{d}(\bar{x},\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1})))\leq\kappa\|\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1})-\varphi(\bar{x})\|<\frac{3\kappa}{2^{k}}t_{k}

and thus there is
w_{k}\in\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1}))
such that

\|\bar{x}-w_{k}\|<\frac{3\kappa}{2^{k}}t_{k}.

Let
v_{k}:=\frac{w_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}
and
x_{k+1}:=x_{k}+v_{k}
. Then

\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|=\|v_{k}\|<\frac{3\kappa}{2^{k}}.

To complete the proof, it remains to show that

\|y_{k+1}-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{k+1})\|<\frac{1}{2^{k}}.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.5
) with (
3.6
), one has

\|\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k})-\varphi(\bar{x})-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(t_{k}v_{k})\|<\varepsilon\|t_{k}v_{k}\|<\varepsilon t_{k}\frac{3\kappa}{2^{k}}<\frac{t_{k}}{2^{k+2}}

(3.8)

and

\|\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1})-\varphi(\bar{x})-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(t_{k}u_{k+1})\|<\varepsilon\|t_{k}u_{k+1}\|<\frac{t_{k}}{2^{k+2}}.

(3.9)

Note that
\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}v_{k})=\varphi(\bar{x}+t_{k}u_{k+1})
and it follows from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) that

\|\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(v_{k})-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u_{k+1})\|<\frac{1}{2^{k+2}}+\frac{1}{2^{k+2}}=\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}.

This means that

\|y_{k+1}-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{k+1})\|=\|\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u_{k+1}+x_{k})-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(x_{k}+v_{k})\|<\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}.

The proof is complete.

As an appropriate substitute of convexity, we consider a tangential concept introduced in
(

, Definition 2.1)
under the name of

-convexity and known in the literature as the Shapiro

-order contact property.

Definition 3.2.

Let
A
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{X}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that
A
is said to have the

-order Shapiro contact property at

, if for any
\varepsilon>0

\mathbf{d}(x-u,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,u))\leq\varepsilon\|x-u\|^{p}\ \ \forall x,u\in A\cap\mathbf{B}(a,\delta).

(3.10)

In the remainder of this paper, we will use the terminology Shapiro first order contact property in place of

-order Shapiro contact
property.

In

.

, Propositon 2.1)

To prove

5
, we need the following two lemmata which are of independent interest.

Lemma 2

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{X}
. Suppose that
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
and image
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(\mathbb{X})
is of the second category. Then there exists
\mu_{0}>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(u,\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(v))\leq\mu_{0}\|\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v\|\ \ \forall(u,v)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}.

(3.13)

Further, assume that
\varphi
is continuously differentiable at
\bar{x}
. Then there exist
\mu,\delta>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(u,\triangledown\varphi(x)^{-1}(v))\leq\mu\|\triangledown\varphi(x)(u)-v\|\ \ \forall(x,u,v)\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}.

(3.14)

Proof.
Since
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(\mathbb{X})
is of the second category, it follows from the open mapping theorem that there exists

such that

2l\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}).

(3.15)

Let
(u,v)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
. By virtue of (
3.15
), one has

\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v\in\|\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v\|\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}}\subseteq\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})\Big(\frac{\|\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v\|}{2l}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}\Big).

(3.16)

Note that
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v)=u-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(v)
by the linearity of
\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})
and then (
3.16
) gives that

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(u,\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(0,u-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(0,\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})^{-1}(\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2l}\|\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})(u)-v\|.

This means that (
3.13
) holds with
\mu_{0}:=\frac{1}{2l}
.

To prove (
3.14
), we next show that there exists
\delta>0
such that

l\mathbf{B}_{Y}\subseteq\triangledown\varphi(x)(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}})\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(3.17)

Noting that
\varphi
is continuously differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then there exists
\delta>0
such that
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable on
\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and

\|\triangledown\varphi(x)-\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})\|<\frac{l}{2}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)

(3.18)

(taking a smaller
\delta
if necessary). Then by (
3.15
) and (
3.18
), one has

2l\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}}\subseteq(\triangledown\varphi(x)+\triangledown\varphi(\bar{x})-\triangledown\varphi(x))(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}})\subseteq\triangledown\varphi(x)(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}})+\frac{l}{2}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}}\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

By the Rädstrom cancellation lemma

, this gives that

\frac{3l}{2}\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{Y}}\subseteq{\rm cl}(\triangledown\varphi(x)(\mathbf{B}_{\mathbb{X}}))\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta).

(3.19)

Applying

have the same interior and thus that (

Lemma 3

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
be a mapping and
A
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{Y}
. Suppose that
\bar{x}\in\varphi^{-1}(A)
is such that
\varphi
is Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
and metrically regular around
\bar{x}
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that

\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\varphi^{-1}(A),x)=\triangledown\varphi(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\varphi(x)))\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap\varphi^{-1}(A).

(3.20)

Proof.
Since
\varphi
is metrically regular around
\bar{x}
, there exist
\kappa>0
along with neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\varphi(\bar{x})
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,\varphi^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa\|\varphi(x)-y\|\ \ \forall(x,y)\in U\times V.

(3.21)

Take
\delta>0
sufficiently small such that

\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\subseteq U\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \varphi(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta))\subseteq V.

(3.22)

Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap\varphi^{-1}(A)
. Choose any
u\in\triangledown\varphi(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\varphi(x)))
. Then
\triangledown\varphi(x)(u)\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\varphi(x))
and thus there exist
t_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
v_{k}\rightarrow\triangledown\varphi(x)(u)
such that

\varphi(x)+t_{k}v_{k}\in A\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ k.

By virtue of (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), for any
k
sufficiently large, one has

\mathbf{d}(x+t_{k}u,\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(x)+t_{k}v_{k}))\leq\kappa\|\varphi(x+t_{k}u)-\varphi(x)-t_{k}v_{k}\|

and consequently there is
x_{k}\in\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(x)+t_{k}v_{k})\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(A)
such that

\|x+t_{k}u-x_{k}\|<2\kappa\|\varphi(x+t_{k}u)-\varphi(x)-t_{k}v_{k}\|.

(3.23)

Denote
u_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-x}{t_{k}}
for all
k

\varphi(x+t_{k}u)=\varphi(x)+\triangledown\varphi(x)(t_{k}u)+o(t_{k})

and then (
3.23

\|u_{k}-u\|<2\kappa\left\|\triangledown\varphi(x)(u)-v_{k}+\frac{o(t_{k})}{t_{k}}\right\|,

which implies that
u_{k}\rightarrow u
as
v_{k}\rightarrow\triangledown\varphi(x)(u)
. Noting that

, it follows that

and thus

\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\varphi^{-1}(A),x)\supseteq\triangledown\varphi(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\varphi(x))).

(3.24)

On the other hand, let

. Then there exist
t_{k}\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
h_{k}\rightarrow h
such that
x+t_{k}h_{k}\in\varphi^{-1}(A)
for all

. Using the Fréchet differentiability of
\varphi
at

\varphi(x+t_{k}h_{k})=\varphi(x)+\triangledown\varphi(x)(t_{k}h_{k})+o(\|t_{k}h_{k}\|).

Denote

Then
v_{k}\rightarrow\triangledown\varphi(x)(h)
and
\varphi(x)+t_{k}v_{k}=\varphi(x+t_{k}h_{k})\in A
, which implies that
\triangledown\varphi(x)(h)\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\varphi(x))
. Hence

\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\varphi^{-1}(A),x)\subseteq\triangledown\varphi(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(A,\varphi(x)))

and (
3.20
) holds by (
3.24
). The proof is complete.

Proof of

4
Main results

In this section, we study error bounds of the inequality defined by a general function and aim to provide primal criteria of error bounds via Bouligand tangent cones, lower Hadamard directional derivatives and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess of subsets. Then we apply these primal results to error bounds of the composite-convex function, and establish primal characterizations of error bounds and an accurate estimate of the error bound modulus in terms of Bouligand tangent cones, directional derivatives of convex functions and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess. We first consider error bounds of the inequality defined by a general lower semicontinuous function.

Given a proper lower semicontinuous extended-real-valued function
\varphi:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, we consider the following inequality:

\varphi(x)\leq 0.

(4.1)

We denote by
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:\varphi(x)\leq 0\}
the solution set. Recall that inequality (
4.1
) is said to have a local error bound at
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
, if there exist
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\leq\tau[\varphi(x)]_{+},\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta),

(4.2)

where
[\varphi(x)]_{+}:=\max\{\varphi(x),0\}
. We denote by

\tau(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},\bar{x}):=\inf\{\tau>0:\ {\rm there\ exists}\ \delta>0\ {\rm such\ that}\ \eqref{3.2}\ {\rm holds}\}

(4.3)

the local error bound modulus of
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
at
\bar{x}
.

The theory of error bounds has been a subject of intense study during many years due to its numerous applications in optimization and variational analysis. They include for instance areas like sensitivity analysis of linear programming, convergence analysis of descent methods, the feasibility problems and the domain of image reconstruction. This notion has been proved to have close connections with several notions in convex analysis and approximation like the basic constraint qualification (BCQ), the Abadie constraint qualification (ACQ), the strong conical hull intersection property (CHIP), the linear regularity and normal property, the metric subregularity as well as the calmness of multifunctions.

The following theorem gives necessary and/or sufficient criteria of local error bounds for the inequality (
4.1
) in terms of the Bouligand tangent cone, the lower Hadamard directional derivative and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess of a set beyond another set.

Theorem 4.1

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper lower semicontinuous function and
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
.

(i)

Suppose that
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
has the Shapiro first order contact property around
\bar{x}
and inequality (
4.1
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
. Then there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\mathbf{e}\big(\varphi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{H}}(x,\cdot)^{-1}(-\infty,1],\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)\big)\leq\tau

(4.4)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)
with
\varphi(x)=0
.

(ii)

Suppose that
{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(0)
,
\varphi
has the epigraphical Shapiro first order contact property at
\bar{x}
and that there exist
\tau,r\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
4.4
) holds for all
x\in{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)
. Then inequality (
4.1
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
.

Proof.
(i) By the local error bound of inequality (
4.1
) at
\bar{x}
, there exist
\tau,\delta\in(0,+\infty)
such that (
4.2
) holds. Note that
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
has the Shapiro first order contact property around
\bar{x}
and thus there exists
r\in(0,\delta)
such that
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
has the Shapiro first order contact property on
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)
. Let
x\in\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},r)
with
\varphi(x)=0
. Take any
h\in X
such that
\varphi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{H}}(x,h)\leq 1
. We need to show that

\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\tau.

(4.5)

Note that
\varphi^{\prime}_{\mathbf{H}}(x,h)\leq 1
and then there exist
t_{n}\rightarrow 0^{+}
and
h_{n}\rightarrow h
such that

\frac{\varphi(x+t_{n}h_{n})-\varphi(x)}{t_{n}}<1+\frac{1}{n}.

(4.6)

If there exists a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\varphi(x+t_{n_{k}}h_{n_{k}})\leq 0
for all
k
, then one has
h\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)
and thus (
4.5
) holds by
\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))=0
.

We next consider the case that
\varphi(x+t_{n}h_{n})>0
for all
n
. Since
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
has the Shapiro first order contact property at
x
, by Proposition 2.1, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
r_{k}\in(0,+\infty)
such that
\mathbf{B}(x,r_{k})\subseteq\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and

\mathbf{d}(u-x,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\mathbf{d}(u,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})+\frac{1}{k}\|u-x\|\ \ \forall u\in\mathbf{B}(x,r_{k}).

(4.7)

Take a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\|t_{n_{k}}h_{n_{k}}\|<\frac{1}{k}
. Then by virtue of (
4.2
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), one has

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(x+t_{n_{k}}h_{n_{k}}-x,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(x+t_{n_{k}}h_{n_{k}},\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})+\frac{1}{k}t_{n_{k}}\|h_{n_{k}}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau\varphi(x+t_{n_{k}}h_{n_{k}})+\frac{1}{k}t_{n_{k}}\|h_{n_{k}}\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\tau\big(1+\frac{1}{n_{k}}\big)t_{n_{k}}+\frac{1}{k}t_{n_{k}}\|h_{n_{k}}\|

thanks to
\varphi(x)=0
. This implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(h_{n_{k}},\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\tau\big(1+\frac{1}{n_{k}}\big)+\frac{1}{k}\|h_{n_{k}}\|

and consequently (
4.5
) holds by letting

.

(ii) Let
\varepsilon>0
such that
(1+\tau)\varepsilon<1
. Since
\varphi
has the Shapiro first order contact property at
\bar{x}
, then there exists
\delta_{1}\in(0,r)
such that

\mathbf{d}((x-u,\alpha-\beta),\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}({\rm epi}(\varphi),(u,\beta)))<\varepsilon(\|x-u\|+\|\alpha-\beta\|)

(4.8)

holds for all
(x,\alpha),(u,\beta)\in{\rm epi}(\varphi)\cap(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta_{1})\times(\varphi(\bar{x})-\delta_{1},\varphi(\bar{x})+\delta_{1}))
.

Take
\delta\in(0,\frac{\delta_{1}}{2})
and let

. Then
\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|<\delta
. Choose any

such that

\gamma>\max\Big\{\frac{\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})}{\delta},(1+\tau)\varepsilon\Big\}.

(4.9)

By using

,

such that

\gamma\|x-a\|\leq\min\{\mathbf{d}(x-a,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},a)),\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\}.

(4.10)

and it follows from (
4.8

\mathbf{d}((x-a,\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)),\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}({\rm epi}(\varphi),(a,\varphi(a))))<\varepsilon(\|x-a\|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|).

Thus, there is
(h,\alpha)\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}({\rm epi}(\varphi),(a,\varphi(a)))={\rm epi}(\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(a,\cdot))
such that

\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(a,h)\leq\alpha\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \|x-a-h\|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)-\alpha|<\varepsilon(\|x-a\|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|).

(4.11)

Case 1:
\alpha=0
. Then for any

, one has
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(a,\lambda h)\leq 0
and then (
4.4

This means

by letting

. By virtue of (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), one has

\displaystyle\gamma\|x-a\|\leq\mathbf{d}(x-a,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},a))\leq\|x-a-h\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\|x-a\|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|x-a\|+\varepsilon\varphi(x)

\varphi(x)>0
and
\varphi(a)=0
. This implies that

(\gamma-\varepsilon)\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\leq(\gamma-\varepsilon)\|x-a\|<\varepsilon\varphi(x).

(4.12)

\alpha\not=0

\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(a,\cdot)

\varphi_{H}^{\prime}\big(a,\frac{h}{|\alpha|}\big)\leq 1.

This and (

\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},a))\leq\tau|\alpha|.

By virtue of (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), one has

\displaystyle\gamma\|x-a\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(x-a,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},a))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x-a-h\|+\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},a))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x-a-h\|+\tau(|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)-\alpha|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\|x-a\|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|)+\tau(\varepsilon\|x-a\|+\varepsilon|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|+|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|)

\displaystyle(\tau+\varepsilon+\tau\varepsilon)|\varphi(x)-\varphi(a)|

\displaystyle(\tau+\varepsilon+\tau\varepsilon)\varphi(x)

\varphi(x)>0
and
\varphi(a)=0
. By letting

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon-\tau\varepsilon)\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})<(\tau+\varepsilon+\tau\varepsilon)\varphi(x)

and consequently

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(x,\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\leq\frac{\tau+\varepsilon+\tau\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon-\tau\varepsilon}\varphi(x).

(with the constant
\max\{\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon},\frac{\tau+\varepsilon+\tau\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon-\tau\varepsilon}\}

4.1

,

\alpha<+\infty

4.4
) holds if and only if

\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\tau\max\{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h),0\}\ \ \forall h\in X.

(4.14)

Proof.
The necessity part. Suppose that (
4.4
) holds. Let
h\in X
be such that
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)\leq 0
. Then for any
\lambda>0
, one has
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\lambda h)\leq 0
and it follows from (
4.4
) that

\mathbf{d}(\lambda h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\tau

This implies that

\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\frac{\tau}{\lambda}\ \ {\rm for\ any}\ \lambda>0.

By letting
\lambda\rightarrow+\infty
, one has
h\in\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)
as
\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)
is closed, which implies that (
4.14
) holds.

Let
h\in X
be such that
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)>0
. Note that
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(a,\cdot)
is positively homogeneous and then

\varphi_{H}^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(x,\frac{h}{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)}\right)=1.

By virtue of (
4.4
), one has

d\left(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\frac{h}{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)}h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)\right)\leq\tau

and consequently

\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\tau\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h),

which implies that (
4.14
) holds.

The sufficiency part. Suppose that (
4.14
) holds. Let
h\in X
be such that
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)\leq 1
. Then (
4.14
) implies that

\mathbf{d}(h,\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x))\leq\tau\max\{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h),0\}\leq\tau.

Hence (
4.4
) holds. The proof is complete.
\Box

The following theorem follows immediately from
Theorem

4.1
and
Proposition

6
.

Theorem 4.3

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
and
\tau>0
. Suppose that
{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(0)
,
\varphi
has the epigraphical Shapiro first order contact property at
\bar{x}
and that
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
has the Shapiro first order contact property around
\bar{x}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

Inequality (
4.1
) has the local error bound at
\bar{x}
with constant
\tau>0
;

(ii)

There exists
\delta>0
such that (
4.4
) holds for all
x\in{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
;

(ii)

There exists
\delta>0
such that (
4.14
) holds for all
x\in{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
.

Given
x\in X
, we can consider the inequality
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\leq 0
defined by the lower Hadamard directional derivative
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)
and study global error bounds
of the inequality
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\leq 0
; that is, there exists
\tau>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(h,S_{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)})\leq\tau\max\{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h),0\}\ \ {\rm for\ all}\ h\in X,

(4.15)

where
S_{\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)}:=\{u\in X:\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,u)\leq 0\}
.

The following corollary, immediate from
Theorem

4.1
, shows that the local error bound of
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
at
\bar{x}
is, to some degree, equivalent to the global error bound of the inequality
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\leq 0
for all
x
close to
\bar{x}
with the same constant. This corollary is inspired from
(
WZ2018
, Theorem 4.4)
which studies the convex inequality defined by the Clarke directional derivative of a locally Lipschitz function and its global error bound.

Corollary 1

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
and
\tau>0
. Assume that
{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(0)
,
\varphi
has the epigraphical Shapiro first order contact property at
\bar{x}
and that
\mathbf{S}_{\varphi}
has the Shapiro first order contact property around
\bar{x}
.

(i)

Suppose that inequality (
4.1
) has the local error bound at
\bar{x}
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x\in{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
, the inequality
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\leq 0
has the global error bound with the same constant.

(ii)

Suppose that there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that
\ker\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\subseteq\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)
for all
x\in U\cap{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})
. Then inequality (
4.1
) has the local error bound at
\bar{x}
if and only if there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x\in{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})\cap\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
, the inequality
\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,\cdot)\leq 0
has the global error bound with the same constant.

Proof.
By the definition of the lower Hadamard directional derivative, for any
x\in{\rm bd}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi})
, one can verify that

\{h\in X:\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)<0\}\subseteq\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{S}_{\varphi},x)\subseteq\{h\in X:\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)\leq 0\}

and thus the conclusions follow from

\Box

Now, we are in a position to study error bounds of a composite-convex function. We apply main results obtained above to establish primal characterizations of error bounds and give an accurate estimate of the error bound modulus in terms of Bouligand tangent cones, directional derivatives of convex functions and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess.

Throughout the rest of this section, we always assume that that
\mathbb{Y}
is a Banach space,
g:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Y}
is a continuously differentiable mapping and that
f:\mathbb{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is a proper lower semicontinuous and convex function.

We consider the following composite-convex inequality:

(f\circ g)(x)\leq 0.

(4.16)

We denote by
\mathcal{S}:=\{x\in X:(f\circ g)(x)\leq 0\}
the solution set of (
4.16
). For any given
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{S}
, we denote by
\tau(\mathcal{S},\bar{x})
, defined as said in (
4.3
), the local error bound modulus of
\mathcal{S}
at
\bar{x}
.

Theorem 4.4

Denote
S_{f}:=\{y\in Y:f(y)\leq 0\}
and assume that
{\rm bd}(S_{f})\subseteq f^{-1}(0)
. Let
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{S}
be such that
g(\bar{x})\in{\rm int}({\rm dom}f)
and
g
is metrically regular around
\bar{x}
. Then composite-convex inequality (
4.16
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
if and only if

\limsup_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm bd}(\mathcal{S})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\;\mathbf{e}\big(\{h\in X:\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h))\leq 1\},\triangledown g(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f},g(x))\big)<+\infty.

Further, one has the following accurate estimate for the local error bound modulus:

\tau(\mathcal{S},\bar{x})=\limsup_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm bd}(\mathcal{S})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\mathbf{e}\big(\{h\in X:\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h))\leq 1\},\triangledown g(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f},g(x))\big).

To prove
Theorem

4.4
, we need the following lemma which is of some independent interest.

Lemma 4

Let
\varphi:=f\circ g
and
\bar{x}\in X
be such that
g(\bar{x})\in{\rm int}({\rm dom}f)
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that

\varphi_{H}^{\prime}(x,h)=\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h))\ \ \forall h\in X

(4.17)

holds for all
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
.

Proof.
Note that
g(\bar{x})\in{\rm int}({\rm dom}f)
and then
(
Ph
, Proposition 1.6)
implies that
f
is locally Lipschtizian around
g(\bar{x})
; that is, there exist
L,r>0
such that

|f(y_{1})-f(y_{2})|\leq L\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|\ \ \forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),r).

(4.18)

Using the continuity of
g
, there is
\delta>0
such that

g(\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta))\subseteq\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),r).

(4.19)

Let
x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and take any
h\in X
. Then for any
t>0
sufficiently small and
h^{\prime}
close to
h
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}[\varphi(x,th^{\prime})-\varphi(x)]=\frac{1}{t}[f(g(x,th^{\prime}))-f(g(x))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}[f(g(x)+\triangledown g(x)(th^{\prime})+o(t))-f(g(x))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}[f(g(x)+\triangledown g(x)(th^{\prime})+o(t))-f(g(x)+\triangledown g(x)(th))]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{t}[f(g(x)+\triangledown g(x)(th))-f(g(x))].

Using (
4.18
) and (
4.19
), for any
t>0
sufficiently small and
h^{\prime}
close to
h
, one has

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}[f(g(x)+\triangledown g(x)(th^{\prime})+o(t))-f(g(x)+\triangledown g(x)(th))]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L}{t}\|t\triangledown g(x)(h^{\prime}-h)+o(t)\|\rightarrow 0\ (t\rightarrow 0^{+},h^{\prime}\rightarrow h).

Then

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0^{+},h^{\prime}\rightarrow h}\frac{1}{t}[\varphi(x,th^{\prime})-\varphi(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}[f(g(x)+t\triangledown g(x)(h))-f(g(x))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h)).

This means that (
4.17
) holds. The proof is complete.
\Box

Proof of
Theorem

4.4
.
Let
\varphi:=f\circ g
. By the metric regularity of
g
around
\bar{x}
, there exist
\kappa,\delta_{0}>0
such that

\mathbf{d}(x,g^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa\|g(x)-y\|\ \ \forall(x,y)\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta_{0})\times\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),\delta_{0}).

(4.20)

Note that
{\rm bd}(S_{f})\subseteq f^{-1}(0)
and then

{\rm bd}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq g^{-1}({\rm bd}(S_{f}))\subseteq g^{-1}(f^{-1}(0))=\varphi^{-1}(0).

By virtue of
Lemma

3

and
Proposition

5
,
there is
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
sufficiently small such that
g^{-1}(S_{f})
has the Shapiro first order contact property on
\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)
and

\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(g^{-1}(S_{f}),x)=\triangledown g(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f}),g(x))\ \ \forall x\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\cap g^{-1}(S_{f}).

(4.21)

This means that
\mathcal{S}
has the Shapiro first order contact property around
\bar{x}
as
\mathcal{S}=g^{-1}(S_{f})
.

Define
\varPsi:X\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Y\times\mathbb{R}
as

\varPsi(x,r):=(g(x),r)\ \ \forall(x,r)\in X\times\mathbb{R}.

Then one can verify that

{\rm epi}(\varphi)=\varPsi^{-1}({\rm epi}(f)).

For any
(x,y)\in\mathbf{B}(\bar{x},\delta)\times\mathbf{B}(g(\bar{x}),\delta)
and any
r,s\in(\varphi(\bar{x})-\delta,\varphi(\bar{x})+\delta)
, one has

\varPsi^{-1}(y,s)=g^{-1}(y)\times\{s\}

and it follows from (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}((x,r),\varPsi^{-1}(y,s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}((x,r),g^{-1}(y)\times\{s\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}(x,g^{-1}(y))+|r-s|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\|g(x)-y\|+|r-s|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\kappa+1)\|\varPsi(x,r)-(y,s)\|,

which implies that
\varPsi
is metrically regular around
(\bar{x},\varphi(\bar{x}))
. Thus,
Proposition

5
gives that
\varPsi^{-1}({\rm epi}(f))
has the Shapiro first order contact property at
(\bar{x},\varphi(x))
and so
\varphi
has the epigraphical Shapiro first order contact property at
\bar{x}
(thanks to
{\rm epi}(\varphi)=\varPsi^{-1}({\rm epi}(f))
).

Finally, by applying
Theorem

4.2
and
Lemma

4
,
one can obtain the proof of
Theorem

4.4
.
The proof is complete.
\Box

The following theorem follows immediately from
Theorem

4.4
.

Theorem 4.5

Suppose that
f
is continuous and
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{S}
is such that
g
is metrically regular around
\bar{x}
. Then composite-convex inequality (
4.16
) has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
if and only if

\limsup_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm bd}(\mathcal{S})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\mathbf{e}\big(\{h\in X:\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h))\leq 1\},\triangledown g(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f},g(x))\big)<+\infty.

Further, one has the following accurate estimate for the local error bound modulus:

\tau(\mathcal{S},\bar{x})=\limsup_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm bd}(\mathcal{S})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\mathbf{e}\big(\{h\in X:\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h))\leq 1\},\triangledown g(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f},g(x))\big).

The following example is to show the application of Theorem 4.5 when one verifies the error bound modulus of the composite-convex inequality.

Example 4.1.
Let
f(y):=y-1
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
g(x):=x^{3}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{x}:=1
. We consider the composite-convex inequality:

(f\circ g)(x)\leq 0.

Then one can verify that
\mathcal{S}=S_{f}=(-\infty,1]
,
\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f},g(\bar{x}))=(-\infty,0]
and

\{h:\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(\bar{x}),\triangledown g(\bar{x})(h))\leq 1\}=(-\infty,\frac{1}{3}].

This implies that

\limsup_{x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm bd}(\mathcal{S})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bar{x}}\mathbf{e}\big(\{h\in X:\mathbf{d}^{+}f(g(x),\triangledown g(x)(h))\leq 1\},\triangledown g(x)^{-1}(\mathbf{T}^{\mathbf{B}}(S_{f},g(x))\big)=\frac{1}{3}.

Hence Theorem 4.5 implies that
\mathcal{S}
has a local error bound at
\bar{x}
and moreover
\tau(\mathcal{S},\bar{x})=\frac{1}{3}
.
\Box

Finally, we give an example to show the local error bound may not be satisfied if the metric regularity assumption is dropped in Theorem 4.5.

Example 4.2.
Let
f(y):=y
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
g(x):=x^{3}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{x}:=0
. We consider the composite-convex inequality:

(f\circ g)(x)\leq 0.

Then
\mathcal{S}=S_{f}=(-\infty,0]
, and one can verify that
g
is not metrically regular at
\bar{x}
since
\triangledown g(\bar{x})=0
is not surjective. However, for any
x>0
sufficiently small, one has

\frac{d(x,\mathcal{S})}{f(g(x))}=\frac{x}{x^{3}}\rightarrow+\infty,\ \ {\rm as}\ x\rightarrow 0^{+}.

This means that
\mathcal{S}
has no local error bound at
\bar{x}
, and thus the conclusions in Theorem 4.5. do not hold.
\Box

5
Conclusions

This paper is to establish primal characterizations of error bounds for a composite-convex inequality (that is defined by a composition of a convex function with a continuously differentiable mapping). To this aim, several primal necessary and/or sufficient conditions for a general inequality, under the assumption of Shapiro contact property, are given in terms of Bouligand tangent cones, directional derivatives and the Hausdorff-Pompeiu excess. Then it is proved that the composite-convex inequality satisfies the Shapiro contact property at the given point where the continuously differentiable mapping is metrically regular and thus primal characterizations of error bounds can be obtained. Our works actually extend the existing primal results on error bounds for the convex inequality to the non-convex case. The future work would be to investigate a broader class of non-convex inequality for which these primal results on error bounds are valid.

Abstract
The paper is concerned with a class of stochastic evolution equations in Hilbert space with random coefficients driven by Teugel’s martingales and an independent multi-dimensional Brownian motion and its optimal control problem. Here Teugels martingales are a family of pairwise strongly orthonormal martingales associated with Lévy processes (see Nualart and Schoutens [ 16 ] ). There are three major ingredients. The first is to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solutions by continuous dependence theorem of solutions combining with the parameter extension method. The second is to establish the stochastic maximum principle and verification theorem for our optimal control problem by the classic convex variation method and dual technique. The third is to represent an example of a Cauchy problem for a controlled stochastic partial differential equation driven by Teugels martingales which our theoretical results can solve.



Keywords
: Stochastic Evolution Equation; Teugels Martingales; Optimal Control; Stochastic Maximum Principle; Verification Theorem

\displaystyle~~~~~~+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(t,{\bar{X}}(t))-{\bar{\sigma}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t))\|_{l^{2}(H)}^{2}dt\bigg]\bigg\}.

Proof.

The estimate (

3.2

3.2

For the sake of simplicity,
in the following discussion, we will
use the following shorthand notation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\hat{X}}(t)\triangleq X(t)-{\bar{X}}(t),\quad\hat{x}\triangleq x-\bar{x},\\
&\Lambda\triangleq\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}_{H}+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|b(t,{\bar{X}}(t))-{\bar{b}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t))\|_{H}^{2}dt\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|g(t,{\bar{X}}(t))-{\bar{g}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t))\|_{H}^{2}dt\bigg]\\
&\quad\quad\quad+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\sigma(t,{\bar{X}}(t))-{\bar{\sigma}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t))\|_{l^{2}(H)}^{2}dt\bigg],\end{split}

(3.9)

and for

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\tilde{\phi}}(t)\triangleq\phi(t,{X}(t))-{\bar{\phi}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t)),{\hat{\phi}}(t)\triangleq\phi(t,{\bar{X}}(t))-{\bar{\phi}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t)),{\Delta\phi}(t)\triangleq\phi(t,{X}(t))-{\phi}(t,{\bar{X}}(t)),~~t\in[0,T],\end{split}

and

and using Assumptions
3.1
-
3.2
and the elementary inequalities

we get that

\displaystyle||\hat{X}(t)||_{H}^{2}=

\displaystyle||\hat{x}||^{2}_{H}+2\int_{0}^{t}\langle A(s)\hat{X}(s),\hat{X}(s)\rangle ds+2\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),\tilde{b}(s))_{H}ds+\int_{0}^{t}||B(s)\hat{X}(s)+\tilde{g}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\tilde{\sigma}^{i}(s),\tilde{\sigma}^{j}(s))_{H}d[H^{i}(s),H^{j}(s)]+2\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),B(s)\hat{X}(s)+\tilde{g}(s))_{H}dW(s)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),\tilde{\sigma}^{i}(s))dH^{i}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\hat{x}||^{2}_{H}+2\int_{0}^{t}\bigg[\langle A(s)\hat{X}(s),\hat{X}(s)\rangle+||B(s)\hat{X}(s)||_{H}^{2}\bigg]ds+\int_{0}^{t}||\Delta g(s)+\hat{g}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds

(3.11)

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}(B(s)\hat{X}(s),\Delta g(s)+\hat{g}(s))_{H}ds+2\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),\Delta b(s)+\hat{b}(s))_{H}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}||\Delta\sigma(s)+\hat{\sigma}(s)||_{l^{2}(H)}ds+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\tilde{\sigma}^{i}(s),\tilde{\sigma}^{j}(s))_{H}d\{[H^{i}(s),H^{j}(s)]-\langle H^{i}(s),H^{j}(s)\rangle\}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),B(s)\hat{X}(s)+\tilde{g}(s))_{H}dW(s)+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),\tilde{\sigma}^{i}(s))dH^{i}(s)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\Lambda-\alpha\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{X}(s)\|_{V}^{2}ds+K\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{X}(s)\|_{H}^{2}dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\tilde{\sigma}^{i}(s),\tilde{\sigma}^{j}(s))_{H}d\{[H^{i}(s),H^{j}(s)]-\langle H^{i}(s),H^{j}(s)\rangle\}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),B(s)\hat{X}(s)+\tilde{g}(s))_{H}dW(s)+2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}(\hat{X}(s),\tilde{\sigma}^{i}(s))dH^{i}(s)

inequality, we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[||\hat{X}(t)||_{H}^{2}]+\alpha\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}||\hat{X}(s)||_{V}^{2}ds\bigg]\leq K\Lambda+K{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{X}(s)\|_{H}^{2}dt\bigg].\end{split}

(3.12)

Then by virtue of Grönwall’s inequality to

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[||\hat{X}(t)||_{H}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}||\hat{X}(s)||_{V}^{2}ds\bigg]\leq&K\Lambda.\end{split}

(3.13)

The proof is complete.

,
\sigma
and a priori estimate (
3.2
),
it follows that

\displaystyle||\Gamma(x_{1}(\cdot))-\Gamma(x_{2}(\cdot))||^{2}_{{M}_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||X_{1}(\cdot)-X_{2}(\cdot)||^{2}_{{M}_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K|\rho-\rho_{0}|^{2}\cdot||x_{1}(\cdot)-x_{2}(\cdot)||^{2}_{{M}_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V)}.

Here
K
is a positive constant independent of
\rho
. If
|\rho-\rho_{0}|<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{K}}
, the mapping
\Gamma
is strictly contractive in

{M}_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V)
. Hence it implies that
the SEE (
3.14
) with the coefficients
(A,B,\rho b+b_{0},\rho g+g_{0},\rho\sigma+\sigma_{0})
admits a unique solution

X(\cdot)\in{M}_{\cal F}^{2}(0,T;V)
.
From Lemma
3.3
, the uniqueness and existence of the solution to the SEE (
3.14
) is true for
\rho=0
. Then starting from
\rho=0
, one can reach
\rho=1
in finite steps and this finishes
the proof of solvability of the SEE (
3.1
). This completes the proof.

4
Formulation of Optimal Control Problem

Let
U
be a real-valued Hilbert space standing for the control space. Let
{\mathscr{U}}
be a nonempty convex
closed subset of
U
. An admissible control process
u(\cdot)\triangleq\{u(t),0\leq t\leq T\}
is defined
as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

A stochastic process
u(\cdot)

defined on
[0,T]\times\Omega
is called an admissible control process if it is a predictable process such that

u(\cdot)\in{M}^{2}_{\mathscr{F}}(0,T;U)
and
u(t)\in{\mathscr{U}}
, a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s..
Write
{\cal A}
for the set of all admissible control processes.

In the Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*}),

for any admissible control
u(\cdot)\in\cal A,

we consider the following controlled
SEE driven by Teugels martingales

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)=&\ [A(t)X(t)+b(t,X(t),u(t))]dt+[B(t)X(t)+g(t,X(t),u(t))]dW(t)\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{i}(t,X(t-),u(t))dH^{i}(t),\\
X(0)=&\ x,\quad t\in[0,T]\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

with the cost functional

\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l(t,x(t),u(t))dt+\Phi(x(T))\bigg].

(4.2)

where the coefficients
satisfy the following
basic assumptions:

Assumption 4.1
.

(i)

A:[0,T]\times\Omega\longrightarrow{\mathscr{L}}(V,V^{*})

and

B:[0,T]\times\Omega\longrightarrow{\mathscr{L}}(V,H)

are operator-valued stochastic processes satisfying (i) in Assumption
3.1
;

(iii)

b,g:[0,T]\times\Omega\times H\times{\mathscr{U}}\rightarrow H
are
\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{B}(H)\times\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{U})/\mathscr{B}(H)
measurable
mappings and
\sigma=(\sigma)_{i=1}^{\infty}:[0,T]\times\Omega\times H\times\mathscr{U}\longrightarrow l^{2}(H)
is a
\mathscr{P}\times\mathscr{B}(H)\times\mathscr{B}(U)/\mathscr{B}(l^{2}(H))
-measurable mapping such that
b(\cdot,0,0),g(\cdot,0,0)\in{M}^{2}_{\mathscr{F}}(0,T;H),\sigma(\cdot,0,0)\in{M}_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;l^{2}(H)).
Moreover, for almost all
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
,
b
,
g
and
\sigma
are Gâteaux differentiable in
(x,u)
with continuous bounded Gâteaux derivatives

b_{x},g_{x},\sigma_{x},b_{u},g_{u}
and
\sigma_{u}
;

(iv)

l:[0,T]\times\Omega\times H\times{\mathscr{U}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a

{\mathscr{P}}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}(H)\otimes{\mathscr{B}}({\mathscr{U}})/{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}})
-measurable mapping and
\Phi:\Omega\times H\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}

is a
{\mathscr{F}}_{T}\otimes{\mathscr{B}}(H)/{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}})
-measurable
mapping.
For almost all
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
,
l
is continuous Gâteaux
differentiable in
(x,u)

with continuous Gâteaux derivatives
l_{x}
and
l_{u}
, and
\Phi

is Gâteaux differentiable in
x

with continuous
Gâteaux derivative
\Phi_{x}
.
Moreover, for almost all
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
(x,u)\in H\times{\mathscr{U}}

\displaystyle|l(t,x,u)|\leq C(1+\|x\|^{2}_{H}++\|u\|_{U}^{2}),

\displaystyle\|l_{x}(t,x,u)\|_{H}++\|l_{u}(t,x,u)\|_{U}\leq C(1+\|x\|_{H}+\|u\|_{U}),

and

\displaystyle|\Phi(x)|\leq C(1+\|x\|^{2}_{H}),

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{x}(x)\|_{H}\leq C(1+\|x\|_{H}).

For any admissible control
u(\cdot),
the solution of the system (
4.1
), denoted by
X^{u}(\cdot)
or
X(\cdot),

if its dependence on

u(\cdot)
is clear from the context, is called the
state process corresponding to the control process
u(\cdot)
, and

(u(\cdot);X(\cdot))
is called an
admissible pair.
The following result gives the well-posedness of the state equation as well as some useful estimates.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let Assumption
4.1
be satisfied. Then for any admissible control
u(\cdot)
,
the state equation
(
4.1
)
has a unique solution
X^{u}(\cdot)\in M_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V).

Moreover, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[||\hat{X}^{u}(t)||_{H}^{2}]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|X^{u}(t)\|_{V}^{2}dt\bigg]\leq K\bigg\{1+||x||_{H}^{2}+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{U}^{2}dt\bigg]\bigg\}\end{split}

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J(u(\cdot))|<\infty.\end{split}

(4.4)

Furthermore, let
X^{v}(\cdot)
be
the state process corresponding to
another admissible control
v(\cdot),

then

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}[||\hat{X}^{u}(t)-X^{v}(t)||_{H}^{2}]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|X^{u}(t)-{X}^{v}(t)\|_{V}^{2}dt\bigg]\leq K{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{U}^{2}dt\bigg].

(4.5)

Proof.

Under Assumption
4.1
, by Theorem
3.1
, we can get directly
the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the state equation (
3.1
). And
the estimates (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) can be obtained by
the estimates (
3.7
) and (
3.2
), respectively.
Furthermor from Assumption

4.1
and the estimate (
4.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J(u(\cdot))|\leq K\bigg\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathbb{E}\bigg[||X(t)||_{H}^{2}\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}||u(t)||^{2}_{U}dt\bigg]+1\bigg\}\leq K\bigg\{1+||x||_{H}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}||u(t)||_{U}^{2}dt\bigg]\bigg\}<\infty.\end{split}

(4.6)

The proof is complete.
∎

Therefor by Lemma
4.1
, we claim that the cost functional (
4.2
)
is well-defined.
Our optimal control problem can be stated as follows.

Problem 4.2
.

Find an admissible control process
{\bar{u}}(\cdot)\in{\cal A}

\displaystyle J({\bar{u}}(\cdot))=\inf_{u(\cdot)\in{\cal A}}J(u(\cdot)).

(4.7)

The admissible control

4.2

5
Regularity Result for the Adjoint Equation

For any admissible pair
(\bar{u}(\cdot);\bar{X}(\cdot))
, the corresponding adjoint
processes is defined as
a triple
(\bar{p}(\cdot),\bar{q}(\cdot),\bar{r}(\cdot))
of
stochastic processes, which is
a solution to
the following
backward stochastic evolution equation
(BSEE for short) driven by Teugels martingales, called the
adjoint equation,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d\bar{p}(t)=&-\bigg[A^{*}(t)\bar{p}(t)+b_{x}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{p}(t)+B^{*}(t)\bar{q}(t)+g_{x}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{q}(t)\\
&\quad\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma{{}^{i}}_{x}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{r}^{i}(t)+l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bigg]dt\\
&\quad\quad+\bar{q}(t)dW(t)+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bar{r}^{i}(t)dH^{i}(t),~~~~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
\bar{p}(T)=&\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T)).\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(5.1)

Here
A^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator of
the operator
A.
Similarly, we can define
the corresponding adjoint operator for other coefficients.

Under Assumptions
4.1
, we have the following
basic result for the adjoint process.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let Assumptions
4.1

be satisfied. Then for any admissible pair

(\bar{u}(\cdot);\bar{X}(\cdot)),

there exists a unique adjoint process

(\bar{p}(\cdot),\bar{q}(\cdot),\bar{r}(\cdot))\in M_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V)\times M_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;H)\times M_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;l^{2}(H)).

Moreover, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\bar{p}(t)\|_{V}^{2}dt\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\bar{q}(t)\|_{H}^{2}dt\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|r(t)\|_{l^{2}(H)}^{2}dt\bigg]\leq K\bigg\{{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\|_{H}^{2}dt\bigg]+\mathbb{E}[||\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T))||_{H}^{2}]\bigg\}.\end{split}

(5.2)

Proof.

From the property of adjoint operator,
the adjoint operator
A^{*}
of
A
and the
adjoint operator
B^{*}
of
B

also satisfies (i) in Assumption
3.1
. Therefore, similarly
to Theorem
3.1
, the
existence and uniqueness of the
solution can be proved by Galerkin approximations and
parameter extension method.
∎

by

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t,x,u,p,q,r):=\left(b(t,x,u),p\right)_{H}+\left(g(t,x,u),q\right)_{H}+\left(\sigma(t,x,u),r(t)\right)_{l^{2}(H)}+l(t,x,u).

(5.3)

Using Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
,
the adjoint equation (

)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d\bar{p}(t)=&-\bigg[A^{*}(t)\bar{p}(t)+B(t)^{*}\bar{q}(t)+\bar{\cal H}_{x}(t)\bigg]dt+\bar{q}(t)dW(t)+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bar{r}^{i}(t)dH^{i}(t),~~~~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
\bar{p}(T)=&\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T)),\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(5.4)

where we denote

\displaystyle\bar{\cal H}(t)\triangleq{\cal H}(t,\bar{x}(t),\bar{u}(t),\bar{p}(t),\bar{q}(t),\bar{r}(t)).

(5.5)

6
Stochastic Maximum Principle

6.1
Variation of the State Process and Cost Functional

Let
({\bar{u}}(\cdot);{\bar{X}}(\cdot))
be an optimal pair of Problem

4.2
. Define a
convex perturbation of
\bar{u}(\cdot)
as follows:

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}(\cdot)\triangleq{\bar{u}}(\cdot)+\epsilon(v(\cdot)-{\bar{u}}(\cdot)),\quad 0\leq\epsilon\leq 1,

where
v(\cdot)
is an arbitrarily
admissible control. Since the control domain
\mathscr{U}
is
convex,
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
is also an element of

\cal A.

We denote by
X^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
the state process
corresponding to the control
u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot).

Now we introduce the following first order
variation equation:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} dY(t)=&\ [A(t)Y(t)+b_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))Y(t)+b_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))(v(t)-\bar{u}(t))]dt\\
&+[B(t)Y(t)+g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))Y(t)+g_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))(v(t)-\bar{u}(t))]dW(t)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigg[\sigma^{i}_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t))Y(t-)+\sigma^{i}_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t))(v(t)-\bar{u}(t))\bigg]dH^{i}(t),\\
Y(0)=&0.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.1)

Under Assumption
4.1
, by
Theorem
3.1
, we see that
the variation equation
(
6.1
) has a unique
solution
Y(\cdot)\in M_{\mathscr{F}}^{2}(0,T;V).

Lemma 6.1
.

Let Assumption
4.1
be satisfied. Then we have the following estimates:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\|X^{\epsilon}(t)-{\bar{X}}(t)\|^{2}_{H}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|X^{\epsilon}(t)-{\bar{X}}(t)\|^{2}_{V}dt\bigg]=O(\epsilon^{2}),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\|X^{\epsilon}(t)-{\bar{X}}(t)-\varepsilon Y(t)\|^{2}_{H}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|X^{\epsilon}(t)-{\bar{X}}(t)-\varepsilon Y(t)\|^{2}_{V}dt\bigg]=o(\epsilon^{2})\ .

(6.3)

Proof.

From the estimate (
4.5
),
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\|X^{\epsilon}(t)-{\bar{X}}(t))\|^{2}_{H}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|X^{\varepsilon}(t)-{\bar{X}}(t)\|_{V}^{2}dt\bigg]&\leq K{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)\|_{U}^{2}dt\bigg]\\
&=K\varepsilon^{2}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|v(t)-\bar{u}(t)\|_{U}^{2}dt\bigg]\\
&=O(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}.

(6.4)

Denote

\displaystyle\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t):=X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)-\varepsilon Y(t).

(6.5)

From Taylor expanding, we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} d\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)=&\ [A(t)\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)+b_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)+\alpha^{\varepsilon}(t)]dt+[B(t)\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)+g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)+\beta^{\varepsilon}(t)]dW(t)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigg[\sigma^{i}_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t))\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)+\gamma^{i\varepsilon}(t)\bigg]dH^{i}(t),\\
X(0)=&\ x,\quad t\in[0,T],\end{aligned}\right.

(6.6)

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \alpha^{\varepsilon}(t)=&\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\big(b_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-b_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t))\\
&+\big(b_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-b_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t))\bigg]d\lambda,\\
\beta^{\varepsilon}(t)=&\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\big(g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-g_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t))\\
&+\big(g_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-g_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t))\bigg]d\lambda,\\
\gamma^{i\varepsilon}(t)=&\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\big(\sigma^{i}_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t-)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t-)-\bar{X}(t-)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t))-\sigma^{i}_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t))\big)(X^{\varepsilon}(t-)-\bar{X}(t-))\\
&+\big(\sigma^{i}_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t-)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t-)-\bar{X}(t-)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-\sigma^{i}_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t))\big)(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t))\bigg]d\lambda.\end{aligned}\right.

(6.7)

From the estimates (
3.7
),
(
6.2
) and Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\|\Xi(t)\|^{2}_{H}\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\|\Xi(t)\|^{2}_{V}dt\bigg]\leq K\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}||\alpha^{\varepsilon}(t)||^{2}_{H}dt\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}||\beta^{\varepsilon}(t)||^{2}_{H}dt\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}||\gamma^{\varepsilon}(t)||^{2}_{l^{2}(H)}dt\bigg]\bigg\}=o(\varepsilon).

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let Assumption
4.1
be satisfied. Let
({\bar{u}}(\cdot);{\bar{X}}(\cdot))
be
an optimal pair of
Problem
4.2
associated with
the first order variation process
Y(\cdot)
(see (
6.1
)). Then,

\displaystyle\begin{split}J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(\bar{u}(\cdot))=&\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\bigg[(\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T)),Y(T))_{H}\bigg]+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),Y(t))_{H}dt\bigg]\\
&+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),v(t)-u(t))_{U}dt\bigg]+o(\varepsilon).\end{split}

(6.8)

Proof.

From the definition of the cost functional (see (
4.2
)), we have

\displaystyle J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(\bar{u}(\cdot))={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\Big(l(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t),u^{\varepsilon}(t))-l(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)\Big)dt\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Phi(X^{\varepsilon}(T))-\Phi(\bar{X}(T))\bigg]=:I_{1}+I_{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}

(6.9)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}=&{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\Big(l(t,X^{\varepsilon}(t),u^{\varepsilon}(t))-l(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)\Big)dt\bigg],\\
I_{2}=&\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Phi(X^{\varepsilon}(T))-\Phi(\bar{X}(T))\bigg].\end{split}

(6.10)

Let us concentrate on
I_{1}.

In terms of Taylor expanding, Lemma
6.1
and
the control convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{1}=&\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\big(l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t))d\lambda dt\bigg]\\
&+E\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\big(l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t)+\lambda(X^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{X}(t)),\bar{u}(t)+\lambda(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t)))-l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(u^{\varepsilon}(t)-\bar{u}(t))d\lambda dt\bigg]\\
&+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)\Xi^{\varepsilon}(t)dt\bigg]+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)Y(t)dt\bigg]\\
&+\varepsilon E\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\big)(u(t)-\bar{u}(t))dt\bigg]\\
=&\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))Y(t)dt\bigg]+\varepsilon E\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))(u(t)-\bar{u}(t))dt\bigg]\\
&+o(\varepsilon).\end{split}

(6.11)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{2}=\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T))Y(T)\bigg]+o(\varepsilon).\end{split}

(6.12)

Then putting (
6.11
) and
(
6.12
) into (
6.9
),
we get (
6.8
). The proof is complete.

∎

6.2
Main Results

Now we are in position to state and prove the maximum principle for Problem
4.2
.

Theorem 6.3

(
Maximum Principle).

Let Assumption
4.1
be satisfied.
Let
({\bar{u}}(\cdot);{\bar{X}}(\cdot))
be an optimal pair of
Problem
4.2
associated with
the adjoint processes
({\bar{p}}(\cdot),{\bar{q}}(\cdot),\bar{r}(\cdot)).

Then the following minimum condition holds:

\displaystyle\big({\cal H}_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t),\bar{p}(t-),\bar{q}(t),\bar{r}(t)),v-{\bar{u}}(t)\big)_{U}\geq 0,\quad\quad\forall v\in{\mathscr{U}},for~a.e.~t\in[0,T],{\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

(6.13)

Proof.

Recalling the adjoint equation
(
5.4
)
and the first order variational
equation (
6.1
), and then applying Itô formula to

(\bar{p}(t),Y(t))_{H},
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[(\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T)),Y(T))_{H}]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),Y(t))_{H}dt\bigg]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(v(t)-\bar{u}(t),b_{u}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{p}(t)+g_{u}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{q}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma{{}^{i}}_{u}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{r}^{i}(t)\bigg)_{U}dt\bigg].\end{split}

(6.14)

Since
\bar{u}(\cdot)
is the optimal
control,
from (
6.8
), the duality relation
(
6.14
) and the definition of the
Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
(see (
5.3
))
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\leq&\lim_{\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-J(\bar{u}(\cdot))}{\varepsilon}\\
=&\mathbb{E}[\big(\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T)),Y(T)\big)_{H}]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(l_{x}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),Y(t))_{H}dt\bigg]+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),v(t)-u(t))_{U}dt\bigg]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(v(t)-\bar{u}(t),b_{u}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{p}(t)+g_{u}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{q}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma{{}^{i}}_{u}^{*}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t))\bar{r}^{i}(t)\bigg)_{U}dt\bigg]\\
&+\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}(l_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t)),v(t)-\bar{u}(t))_{U}dt\bigg]\\
=&E\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(v(t)-\bar{u}(t),{\cal H}_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t),\bar{p}(t),\bar{q}(t),\bar{r}(t)))_{U}dt\bigg].\end{split}

(6.15)

This implies the minimum condition
(
6.13
) holds since
v(\cdot)

is any given admissible control.
∎

7
Verification Theorem

In the following, we give a sufficient condition of optimality for the existence of an optimal control of
Problem
4.2
,
which is the so-called verification theorem.

Theorem 7.1

(
Verification Theorem).

Let Assumption
4.1
be satisfied.
Let
({\bar{u}}(\cdot);{\bar{X}}(\cdot))
be an admissible pair of
Problem
4.2
associated with
the adjoint processes
(\bar{p}(\cdot),\bar{q}(\cdot),\bar{r}(\cdot)).
Suppose that

{\cal H}(t,x,u,{\bar{p}}(t),{\bar{q}}(t),\bar{r}(t))
is convex in
(x,u)
,
and
\Phi(x)
is convex in
x
, moreover assume
that the following optimality condition holds for almost all
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
:

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t,{\bar{X}}(t),\bar{u}(t),{\bar{p}}(t),{\bar{q}}(t),\bar{r}(t))=\min_{u\in{\mathscr{U}}}{\cal H}(t,{\bar{X}}(t),u,{\bar{p}}(t),{\bar{q}}(t),\bar{r}(t)).

(7.1)

Then
({\bar{u}}(\cdot);{\bar{X}}(\cdot))

is an optimal pair of Problem
4.2
.

Proof.

Let
({u}(\cdot);{X}(\cdot))
be an any given admissible pair. To simplify our notations, we define

\displaystyle\begin{split}&b(t)\triangleq b(t,X(t),u(t)),{\bar{b}}(t)\triangleq b(t,{\bar{X}}(t),{\bar{u}}(t)),\\
&g(t)\triangleq g(t,X(t),u(t)),{\bar{g}}(t)\triangleq g(t,{\bar{X}}(t),{\bar{u}}(t)),\\
&\sigma{{}^{i}}(t)\triangleq\sigma{{}^{i}}(t,X(t-),u(t)),{\bar{\sigma}{{}^{i}}}(t)\triangleq\sigma{{}^{i}}(t,{\bar{X}}(t-),{\bar{u}}(t)),\\
&{\cal H}(t)\triangleq{\cal H}(t,X(t),u(t),{\bar{p}}(t),{\bar{q}}(t),\bar{r}(t)),\\
&{\bar{\cal H}}(t)\triangleq{\cal H}(t,\bar{X}(t),\bar{u}(t),{\bar{p}}(t),{\bar{q}}(t),\bar{r}(t)).\end{split}

(7.2)

From the definitions of the cost functional

J(u(\cdot))
and the Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
(see (
4.2
) and (
5.3
)),
we can represent
J(u(\cdot))-J(\bar{u}(\cdot))
as follows:

\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))-J({\bar{u}}(\cdot))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg({\cal H}(t)-{\bar{\cal H}}(t)-({\bar{p}}(t),b(t)-{\bar{b}}(t))_{H}-({\bar{q}}(t),g(t)-{\bar{g}}(t))_{H}

(7.3)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}({\bar{r}}^{i}(t),\sigma{{}^{i}}(t)-{\bar{\sigma}{{}^{i}}}(t))_{H}\bigg)dt\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\Phi(X(T))-\Phi({\bar{X}}(T))\bigg].

Then recalling the
adjoint equation (
5.1
) and applying Itô’s formula to
({\bar{p}}(t),X(t)-{\bar{X}}(t))_{H}
, we get that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg(({\bar{p}}(t),b(t)-{\bar{b}}(t))_{H}+({\bar{q}}(t),g(t)-{\bar{g}}(t))_{H}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}({\bar{r}}^{i}(t),\sigma{{}^{i}}(t)-{\bar{\sigma}{{}^{i}}}(t))_{H}\bigg)dt\bigg]

(7.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}({\bar{\cal H}}_{x}(t),X(t)-{\bar{X}}(t))_{H}dt\bigg]+{\mathbb{E}}\Big[(\Phi_{x}(\bar{X}(T)),X(T)-{\bar{X}}(T))_{H}\Big].

Then substituting (
7.4
) into (
7.3
) leads to

\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))-J({\bar{u}}(\cdot))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}\bigg({\cal H}(t)-{\bar{\cal H}}(t)-({\bar{\cal H}}_{x}(t),X(t)-{\bar{X}}(t))_{H}\bigg)dt\bigg]

(7.5)

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{E}}[\Phi(X(T))-\Phi({\bar{X}}(T))-(\Phi_{x}({\bar{X}}(T)),X(T)-{\bar{x}}(T))_{H}].

On the other hand, the convexity of

{\cal H}(t)
and
\Phi(x)
yields

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t)-{\bar{\cal H}}(t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle({\bar{\cal H}}_{x}(t),X(t)-{\bar{X}}(t))_{H}+({\bar{\cal H}}_{u}(t),u(t)-{\bar{u}}(t))_{U},

(7.6)

and

\displaystyle\Phi(X(T))-\Phi({\bar{X}}(T))\geq(\Phi_{x}({\bar{X}}(T)),x(T)-{\bar{x}}(T))_{H}.

(7.7)

In addition, the optimality condition (
7.1
) and the convex optimization principle
(see Proposition 2.21 of
[
8
]
) yield that for almost all
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
,

\displaystyle({\bar{\cal H}}_{u}(t),u(t)-{\bar{u}}(t))_{U}\geq 0.

(7.8)

Then putting (
7.6
), (
7.7
) and (
7.8
) into (
7.5
), we get that

\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))-J({\bar{u}}(\cdot))\geq 0.

(7.9)

Therefore, since
u(\cdot)
is arbitrary,
{\bar{u}}(\cdot)
is an optimal control process and
({\bar{u}}(\cdot);{\bar{X}}(\cdot))
is an optimal pair. The proof is
complete.
∎

8
Application

In this section, we will apply our theoretical results to solve a specific
exampl i.e., an
optimal control problem for a controlled Cauchy problem
driven by Teugels martingales.

First of all,
let us recall some preliminaries of Sobolev spaces.
For
m=0,1
, we define the space
H^{m}\triangleq\{\phi:\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ \mbox{for any}\ \alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})\ \mbox{with}\ |\alpha|:=|\alpha_{1}|+\cdots+|\alpha_{d}|\leq m\}
with the norm

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{m}\triangleq\left\{\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\phi(z)|^{2}dz\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We denote by
H^{-1}
the dual space of
H^{1}
. We set
V=H^{1}
,
H=H^{0}
,
V^{*}=H^{-1}
. Then
(V,H,V^{*})
is a Gelfand triple.

We choose control domain
{\mathscr{U}}=U=H
. The admissible control
set
\cal A
is defined as
{M}^{2}_{\mathscr{F}}(0,T;U).

For any admissible control
u(\cdot,\cdot)\in{M}^{2}_{\mathscr{F}}(0,T;U),

we consider a controlled Cauchy problem, where the system
is given by a stochastic partial differential equation driven by
Brownian motion
W

and Poisson random
martingale in the following
divergence form:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} dy(t,z)=&\ \big\{\partial_{z^{i}}[a^{ij}(t,z)\partial_{z^{j}}y(t,z)]+b^{i}(t,z)\partial_{z^{i}}y(t,z)+c(t,z)y(t,z)+u(t,z)\big\}dt\\
&+\{\partial_{z^{i}}[\eta^{i}(t,z)y(t,z)]+\rho(t,z)y(t,z)+u(t,z)\}dW(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}[\Gamma^{i}(t,z)y(t,z)+u(t,z)]dH^{i}(t),\\
y(0,z)=&\ \xi(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}\quad(t,z)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

(8.1)

where the coefficients
a^{ij},b^{i},\eta^{i},c,\rho:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\Gamma^{i}:[0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
are given random mappings
and satisfy the suitable measurability.
Here we also use the Einstein summation convention to

\partial_{z^{i}}[a^{ij}(t,z)\partial_{z^{j}}y(t,z)],b^{i}(t,z)\partial_{z^{i}}y(t,z)

and
\partial_{z^{i}}[\eta^{i}(t,z)y(t,z)].

For any admissible control
u(\cdot,\cdot)\in\cal A
, the following
definition gives the generalized weak solution to (
8.1
).

Definition 8.1
.

An
\mathbb{R}
-valued,
{\mathscr{P}}\times{\mathscr{B}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-measurable process
y(\cdot,\cdot)
is called a solution to
(
8.1
), if
y(\cdot,\cdot)\in{\cal M}_{\cal F}^{2}(0,T;H)
such that for every
\phi\in H
and a.e.
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y(t,z)\phi(z)dz=&\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(z)\phi(z)dz-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a^{ij}(s,z)\partial_{z^{j}}y(s,z)\partial_{z^{i}}\phi(z)dzds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[b^{i}(s,z)\partial_{z^{i}}y(s,z)\\
&+c(s,z)y(s,z)+u(s,z)\bigg]\phi(z)dzds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\eta^{i}(s,z)y(s,z)\partial_{i}\phi(z)dzdW(s)\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\rho(s,z)y(s,z)+u(s,z)\bigg]\phi(z)dzdW(s)+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\Gamma^{i}(s,z)y(s,z)+u(s,z)\bigg]\phi(z)dzdH^{i}(t).\end{split}

(8.2)

For any admissible control process
u(\cdot,\cdot)
and the solution
y(\cdot,\cdot)
of the
corresponding state equation (
8.1
), the objective of the control problem is to minimize the following cost functional

\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}y^{2}(T,z)dz+\iint_{[0,T]\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}y^{2}(s,z)dsdz+\iint_{[0,T]\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}u^{2}(s,z)dsdz\bigg].

(8.3)

To make the control problem well-defined,
we make the following assumptions
on the coefficients
a
,
b
,
c
,
\eta
,
\rho
,
\Gamma
, for some fixed constants
K\in(1,\infty)
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
:

Assumption 8.1
.

The functions
a
,
b
,
c
,
\eta
, and
\rho
are

{\mathscr{P}}\times{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-measurable with
values in the set of real symmetric

d\times d
matrices,
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\mathbb{R}}
,
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and
{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively, and are bounded by
K
.
The function
\Gamma
is

{\mathscr{P}}\times{\mathscr{B}}(E)\times{\mathscr{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
-measurable with
value
{l^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and is bounded by
K
.
\xi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Assumption 8.2
.

The super-parabolic condition holds, i.e.,

\displaystyle\kappa I+\eta(t,z)(\eta(t,z))^{*}\leq 2a(t,\omega,z)\leq KI,\quad\forall(t,\omega,z)\in[0,T]\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
I
is the
(d\times d)
-identity matrix.

In order to apply our abstract theoretical results in Section 6 and 7 to our optimal control problem, now we begin to transform
(
8.1
) into a SEE driven by
Teugels martingales in
the form of (
3.1
).
Set

\displaystyle X(t)\triangleq y(t,\cdot),

\displaystyle(A(t)\phi)(z)\triangleq\partial_{z^{i}}[a^{ij}(t,z)\partial_{z^{j}}\phi(z)]+b^{i}(t,z)\partial_{z^{i}}\phi(z)+c(t,z)\phi(z),\quad\forall\phi\in V,

\displaystyle(B(t)\phi)(z)\triangleq\partial_{z^{i}}[\eta^{i}(t,z)\phi(z)]+\rho(t,z)\phi(z),\quad\forall\phi\in V,

\displaystyle b(t,\phi,u)\triangleq u,\quad\forall\phi\in H,u\in{\cal U},

\displaystyle g(t,\phi,u)\triangleq u,\quad\forall\phi\in H,u\in{\cal U},

\displaystyle\sigma{{}^{i}}(t,\phi,u)\triangleq\Gamma^{i}(t)\phi+u,\quad\forall\phi\in H,u\in\cal U,

\displaystyle l(t,\phi,u)\triangleq(\phi,\phi)_{H}+(u,u)_{U},\quad\forall\phi\in H,u\in\cal U,

\displaystyle\Phi(\phi)\triangleq(\phi,\phi)_{H},\quad\forall\phi\in H.

In the Gelfand triple
(V,H,V^{*})
, using the above notations, we can rewrite the state equation (
8.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} dX(t)=&\ [A(t)X(t)+b(t,X(t),u(t))]dt+[B(t)X(t)+g(t,X(t),u(t))]dW(t)\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sigma{{}^{i}}(t,X(t),u(t))dH^{i}(t),\\
X(0)=&\ x,\quad t\in[0,T],\end{aligned}\right.

(8.4)

and the cost functional (
8.3
)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle J(u(\cdot))={\mathbb{E}}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}l(t,x(t),u(t))dt+\Phi(x(T))\bigg]{\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}

(8.5)

where we set

\displaystyle\begin{split}&l(t,x,u)\triangleq(x,x)_{H}+(u,u)_{H},\forall x\in H,u\in U,\\
&\Phi(x)\triangleq(x,x)_{H},\forall x\in H.\end{split}

(8.6)

Thus this optimal control problem is transformed into Problem

4.2
as a special case.
Under Assumptions
8.1
-
8.2
,
it is easy to check that the coefficients of this optimal control problem satisfy
Assumptions
4.1
. So in this
cas Theorem
6.3
and

7.1
hold.
Moreover, from
the a priori estimate (
3.2
),
it is easy to see that the cost functional
J(u(\cdot))
is
the strictly convex, coerciv lower-semi continuous functional defined
on the reflexive Banach space

{M}^{2}_{\mathscr{F}}(0,T;U).

Therefor the uniqueness and existence of the optimal control
can be obtained by the convex optimality
principle (see Proposition 2.12 of
[
8
]
).
Let
(\bar{u}(\cdot),\bar{X}(\cdot))

be the optimal pair. In the following, we will give the
duality characterization of the optimal
control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
by the maximum
principle. More precisely, in this case the corresponding Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
becomes

\displaystyle{\cal H}(t,x,u,p,q,r):=\left(u,p\right)_{H}+\left(u,q\right)_{H}+\left(\Gamma(t)x+u,r(t)\right)_{l^{2}(H)}+(x,x)_{H}+(u,u)_{H}.

(8.7)

Let
(\bar{u}(\cdot);\bar{X}(\cdot))

be an optimal pair. Then
the corresponding adjoint equation
becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d\bar{p}(t)=&-\bigg[A^{*}(t)\bar{p}(t)+B^{*}(t)\bar{q}(t)+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Gamma^{i*}(t)\bar{r}^{i}(t)+2\bar{X}(t)\bigg]dt+\bar{q}(t)dW(t)+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\bar{r}^{i}(t)dH^{i}(t),0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
\bar{p}(T)=&2\bar{X}(T),\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(8.8)

where

\displaystyle A^{*}(t)\phi(z)\triangleq-\partial_{z^{i}}[a^{ij}(t,z)\partial_{z^{j}}\phi(z)]+\partial_{z^{i}}[b^{i}(t,z)\phi(z)]+c(t,z)\phi(z),\quad\forall\phi\in V,

\displaystyle B^{*}(t)\phi(z)\triangleq-\eta^{i}(t,z)\partial_{z^{i}}\phi(z),\quad\forall\phi\in H,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{i*}(t)\phi(z)\triangleq\Gamma^{i}(t,z)\phi(z),\quad\forall\phi\in H.

Since
{\mathscr{U}}=U
, there is no constraint on the control and therefore the minimum condition (
6.13
)
becomes

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{u}(t,\bar{X}(t-),\bar{u}(t),\bar{p}(t-),\bar{q}(t),\bar{r}(t))=0,

(8.9)

which imply that

\displaystyle 2{\bar{u}}(t)+\bar{p}(t-)+\bar{q}(t)+\bar{r}(t)=0,

(8.10)

a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s..
Thus the optimal control
{\bar{u}}(\cdot)
is given by

\displaystyle{\bar{u}}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\bar{p}(t-)+\bar{q}(t)+\bar{r}(t)\bigg].

Remark 8.1
.

The above example
can be regarded as
a special case of the infinite-dimensional
linear-quadratic control problem
driven by Teugels martiangles
which can also be applied to
some more practical problems
such as the partial observation optimal
control driven by Teugels martingales and the optimal harvesting problem
associated with Lévy processes and so on. And we will give
detailed investigations on these applications in our future
publication.

9
Conclusion

In this paper, we have developed an infinite-dimensional optimal control problem of the
stochastic evolution system
driven by Teugels martingales.
We have considered the control variable enters the diffusion of the state equation and the control domain is convex.
We first provided the existence uniqueness and continuous dependence theorems of solutions to SEE driven by
Teugels martingales.
Then we established necessary and sufficient conditions for optimal controls in the form of maximum principles by convex variational technique.
As an application, we considered an optimal control problem of a Cauchy problem for a controlled stochastic partial differential equation and obtained the dual characterization of the optimal control in terms of
the solution to the corresponding stochastic Hamiltonian system.
And further investigates will be carried out on the optimal control problem under convex control domain assumption and
more practical applications in our future
publications

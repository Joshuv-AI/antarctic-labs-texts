Relating entropies of quantum channels
Dariusz Kurzyk, Łukasz Pawela, Zbigniew Puchała

Abstract
In this work, we study two different approaches to defining the entropy of a quantum channel. One of these is based on the von Neumann entropy of the corresponding Choi-Jamiołkowski state. The second one is based on the relative entropy of the output of the extended channel relative to the output of the extended completely depolarizing channel. This entropy then needs to be optimized over all possible input states. Our results first show that the former entropy provides an upper bound on the latter. Next, we show that for unital qubit channels, this bound is saturated. Finally, we conjecture and provide numerical intuitions that the bound can also be saturated for random channels as their dimension tends to infinity.

Introduction

One of the important areas of quantum information theory refers to an
entropic picture of quantum states and operations. It is well known
that the entropic uncertainty principle can be applied in quantum key
distribution
protocols
[
1
,
2
]
in
order to quantify the performance of these protocols. Another
possible area where such an approach prevails is resource
theory
[
3
]
. The entropic approach in the
description of quantum states can also be useful in
studies of quantum phenomena such as correlations or
non-locality
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
Another essential aspect of quantum information theory is studying
the time evolution of quantum systems interacting with the
environment. Entropic characterization of quantum operations can be
helpful in the investigation of decoherence induced by quantum
channel
[
8
,
9
]
. There exists
also numerous approaches to the formulation of entropic uncertainty
principles
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
,
which can be useful in the analysis of quantum key distribution,
quantum
communication or characterization of generalized measurements. In

[
8
,
9
]
entropy of quantum channel is defined
as the entropy of the state corresponding to the channel by the Jamiołkowski
isomorphism.

In quantum information theory, the relative entropy

D(\rho\|\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\log\rho-\rho\log\sigma)
plays an
important
role
[
15
]
and can be useful in quantifying the
difference
between two quantum states. In terms of quantum distinguishability,
relative
entropy can be interpreted as a distance between two quantum
states. Nevertheless, it is crucial to remember that it is not a
metric as it does
not fulfill the triangle inequality. It can be noticed that quantum
transformation
\Phi
cannot increase of a distinguishability between
quantum states
\rho
,
\sigma
what can be written as

D(\rho\|\sigma)\geq D\big(\Phi(\rho)\|\Phi(\sigma)\big)
. This fact, sometimes called the
data processing inequality plays an important role in the context of
hypothesis
testing
[
16
]
. The quantum relative entropy can also
be useful in the quantification of quantum entanglement. In this
context, the amount of the entanglement of a quantum state
\rho

can be defined as an optimal
distinguishability of the state
\rho
from separable states e.g.

\min\limits_{\sigma\in\mathrm{SEP}}D(\rho||\sigma)
, where
minimization
is performed over separable states. It can be noticed that the
relative
entropy can also be used to define of von Neumann entropy of
\rho
as

S(\rho)=\log d-D(\rho\|{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}/d)
. This definition shows that
the entropy of a quantum state is related to its distance from the
maximally
mixed state. This approach to von Neumann entropy is useful to define
the
entropy of quantum channels. Formally it was studied by Gour and
Wilde
[
17
]
, where relative entropy of quantum
channels was
introduced. According to their approach, the von Neumann entropy of
the quantum
channel is given by an optimized relative entropy of the output from
an
extended channel relative to the output of the extended depolarizing
channel. The optimization
is performed over all possible input states.
Moreover, there is also a possibility to define other information
measures, e.g., conditional entropy or manual information, in terms
of relative entropy.
Recently the relative entropy of quantum channel and its
generalizations are used in resource theory
[
18
]
,
studies of quantum channels e.g.
distinguishability
[
19
]
, quantum channel
discrimination
[
20
]
or channel
capacity
[
21
,
22
]
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
denote complex Euclidean spaces, let
\dim(\mathcal{X})

denote the dimension of the space
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
denotes a
set of linear operators from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathcal{Y}
. For simplicity we will
write
\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X})\equiv\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X})
.
If
\rho\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X})
is Hermitian
(
\rho=\rho^{\dagger}
), positive
semi-definite (
\rho\geq 0
) and a trace-one (
\mathrm{Tr}{\rho}=1
) linear
operator
then
\rho
is called as density operator. To keep our expressions
simple, we
will write the density operator corresponding to a pure states as
lowercase
Greek letters
\phi\equiv|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|
. In order to keep track of
subspaces of
composite systems we will write
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{B})
.

The set of all such mapping
\Phi:\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X})\to\mathrm{L}({\mathcal{Y}})
will be
denoted by

\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
and for brevity, we will write

\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{X})\equiv\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
. A
mapping that is completely positive and trace-preserving is called a
quantum
channel. The set of all quantum channels will be denoted
\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
. There
exists a well-known bijection between the sets
\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
and

\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{Y}\otimes\mathcal{X})
, the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism. It is given by the
relation

D_{\Phi}=(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi^{+})

(1)

where
|\phi^{+}\rangle=\sum_{i}^{d}|ii\rangle
and
D_{\Phi}
is called the
dynamical matrix or Choi
matrix. Normalized
D_{\Phi}
known as Choi-Jamiołkowski state and
will be denoted as
J_{\Phi}=D_{\Phi}/d
.

The von Neumann entropy of
\rho\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X})
is defined by the
following formula

H(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\rho,

(2)

similarly to the classical Shannon entropy. This equation can be
rewritten using the notion of relative entropy, which is defined for
states
\rho
and
\sigma
analogously to its classical
counterpart
[
23
]

D(\rho\|\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma).

(3)

Here we use the convention that
D(\rho\|\sigma)
is finite when

\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
. Otherwise, we
put

D(\rho|\sigma)=\infty
. Thus, we can rewrite Eq. (
2
) as

H(\rho)=\log\dim(\mathcal{X})-D(\rho\|\rho_{*}),

(4)

where
\rho_{*}={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}/\dim(\mathcal{X})
.

The definition of quantum relative entropy can be extended to the
case of quantum channels in the following
manner
[
17
]

D(\Phi\|\Psi)=\sup_{\rho_{AR}\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{R})}D\Big((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\rho_{AR})\|(\Psi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\rho_{AR})\Big).

(5)

The state
\rho_{AR}
can be chosen as a pure state and the space

\mathcal{X}_{R}
can be
isomorphic to
\mathcal{X}_{A}
. Utilizing Eq. (
5
) we get the
following
definition of the entropy of a quantum channel

Definition 1
(​​
[
17
]
)

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{A},\mathcal{X}_{B})
. Then its entropy

H(\Phi)
is defined as

H(\Phi)=\log\dim\mathcal{X}_{B}-D(\Phi||\mathcal{R}),

(6)

where
\mathcal{R}\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{A},\mathcal{X}_{B})
is the depolarizing channel

\mathcal{R}:\rho\mapsto(\mathrm{Tr}\rho)\ {\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}/\dim(\mathcal{X}_{B})
.

The quantum entropy was also defined in the same matter in

[
16
]
. However, there exists an earlier definition
of entropy
of a quantum channel. In
[
8
,
9
]
, the quantum
channels was
characterized by the
map entropy
, which was defined as the
entropy of
corresponding Jamiołkowski state. It reads

Definition 2
(​​
[
8
]
)

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{A},\mathcal{X}_{B})
. Its entropy

H(\Phi)
is given by
the entropy of the corresponding Choi-Jamiołkowski state

H^{K}(\Phi)=H(J_{\Phi}).

(7)

The above entropy achieves its minimal value of zero for any unitary
channel
and the maximal value of
2\log\dim\mathcal{X}_{B}
for the completely
depolarizing
channel. Based on these two definition we arrive at the following
observation.

Lemma 1

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X}_{A},\mathcal{X}_{B})
. The two possible definitions of
quantum
channel entropy
H(\Phi)
and
H^{K}(\Phi)
fulfill the following
relation

H(\Phi)\leq H^{K}(\Phi)-\log\dim\mathcal{X}_{B}

(8)

Proof.

The proof follows from a direct inspection

H(\Phi)=\log\dim\mathcal{X}_{B}-\sup_{|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{X}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{R}}D\left((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\psi)\|(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\psi)\right).

(9)

Let us denote
\sigma_{BR}=\left(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\right)(\psi)
. Now we note that
\left(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\right)(\psi)={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}/\dim(\mathcal{X}_{B})\otimes\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\psi

and we use the well
known identity
\log({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\rho)={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\log\rho
and
we have

H(\Phi)=\log\mathcal{X}_{B}-\sup_{|\phi\rangle\in\mathcal{X}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{R}}\Big(-H(\sigma_{BR})-\mathrm{Tr}\sigma_{BR}\big(\frac{{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}}{\dim(\mathcal{X}_{B})}\otimes\log\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\psi\big)\Big).~

(10)

Finally we note that
\mathrm{Tr}\sigma_{BR}\big({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\log(\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\psi)\big)=\mathrm{Tr}\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\sigma_{BR}\log\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\psi
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{B}\sigma_{BR}=\mathrm{Tr}_{A}\psi
. Putting this into Eq. (
10
)
along with
the fact that
J_{\Phi}
is normalized we get the desired result.

The main focus of this work is to find instances that saturate the
inequality in
Lemma
1
. We will mainly focus on the study of unital
qubit channels.

3
Quantum unital qubit channels

In this section we will focus our attention on unital qubit channels,
that is

\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
such that
\Phi({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
. Our goal here is to
show
that the supremum present in Eq. (
5
) is achieved for the
maximally
entangled state
|\phi^{+}\rangle
. This can be formally written as the
following
theorem

Theorem 2

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, such that
\Phi({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
. Then

H(\Phi)=H^{K}(\Phi)-\log 2.

(11)

The remainder of this section contains technical lemmas which
combined give the
proof of Theorem
2
.

A generic two-qubit state can be written as

|\psi_{AR}\rangle=U\otimes V(\sqrt{p}|00\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}|11\rangle),

(12)

for some qubit unitary matrices
U,V
. Let us note that the quantum
relative entropy is
unitarily invariant
D(\rho\|\sigma)=D(U\rho U^{\dagger}\|U\sigma U^{\dagger})
.
Moreover, we use the same the fact that the Jamiołkowski
matrix of channel
\Phi_{A}(\rho)=\Phi(W\rho W^{\dagger})
has the
same spectrum
as the Jamiołkowski matrix of channel
\Phi
, where
A
is a unitary
matrix.
Thus, we can skip the unitary
operations in our further investigations. We may perform the
optimization taking
into account only the parameter

p
which quantifies the amount of entanglement between the input
qubits. In
order to further simplify notation we will write

|\psi_{AR}\rangle=\sqrt{p}|00\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}|11\rangle=|\sqrt{P}\rangle\rangle

(13)

where
|X\rangle\rangle
denotes the vectorization of the matrix
X

and

\sqrt{P}=\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{1-p})
and we define

\phi_{P}=|\sqrt{P}\rangle\rangle\langle\langle\sqrt{P}|

(14)

In the next step we will check the symmetry of
D(\Phi\|\mathcal{R})
with
respect
to the parameter
p
. Hence, we formulate the first lemma.

Lemma 3

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and let
\phi_{P}
be a two-qubit state as
in Eq. (
14
). Then
D(\Phi\|\mathcal{R})
is symmetric in the parameter
p
.

Proof.

Let us denote
Q=\mathrm{diag}(1-p,p)
. It can be checked that

\big((\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\sqrt{P})D_{\Phi}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\sqrt{P})(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})\big)^{*}=({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\sqrt{Q})D_{\Phi}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\sqrt{Q}).

(15)

This observation combined with the fact

(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})=({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\sqrt{P})D_{\Phi}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\sqrt{P}),

(16)

gives the symmetry of the entropy

H\Big((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})\Big)=H\Big((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{Q})\Big).

(17)

As for the term
\mathrm{Tr}\Big((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})\log(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})\Big)

observe that

\log\big((\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})\big)={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\log(P/2).

(18)

Finally

(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})\Big(\log\big(\frac{1}{2}{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes P\big)\Big)(\sigma_{y}\otimes\sigma_{y})=\log\big(\frac{1}{2}{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes Q\big).

(19)

Combining all of these observations yields the lemma.

Subsequently, we prove in next lemma the concavity
D(\Phi\|\mathcal{R})

with respect to the parameter
p
.

Lemma 4

Given a unital channel
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and let
\phi_{P}
be a
two-qubit
state as in Eq. (
14
). Then the function
f(p)=D\Big((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})||(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})\Big)
is concave.

Proof.

For the purpose of this proof let us denote
\rho(p)=(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P})
. Let us also denote

\begin{split}g(p)=&\mathrm{Tr}\rho(p)\log\rho(p),\\
l(p)=&\mathrm{Tr}\rho(p)\log(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(\phi_{P}).\end{split}

(20)

A direct calculation shows that
l(p)=h(p)+\log 2
, where

h(p)
is the
point entropy. From this it follows that

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}l}{\mathrm{d}p^{2}}=-\frac{1}{p(1-p)}<0.

(21)

For
g(p)
we calculate

\begin{split}\frac{dg}{dp}=&\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\rho^{\prime}(p)\log\rho(p)\Big)+\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\rho(p)\frac{d}{dp}\Big(\log\rho(p)\Big)\Big)\\
=&\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\rho^{\prime}(p)\log\rho(p)\Big)+\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\rho(p)\rho^{-1}(p)\rho^{\prime}(p)\Big).\end{split}

(22)

Observing that
\rho^{\prime}(p)=\sqrt{J_{\Phi}}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\rho(p))\sqrt{J_{\Phi}}

we see that the second term in Eq. (
22
) is
equal to zero.
Hence, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}p^{2}}=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho^{\prime}(p)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}(\log\rho(p))\right).

(23)

From Taylor expansion of derivative formulae for matrix logarithms

[
24
]
we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\log\rho(p)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(s(\rho(p)-{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})+{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(p)\Big(s(\rho(t)-{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})+{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-1}\mathrm{d}s.

(24)

Thus,

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}g}{\mathrm{d}p^{2}}=&\mathrm{Tr}\Bigg(\rho^{\prime}(p)\int_{0}^{1}\Big(s\big(\rho(p)-{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\big)+{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-1}\rho^{\prime}(p)\Big(s\big(\rho(p)-{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\big)+{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-1}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg)\\
\leq&\mathrm{Tr}\Bigg(\rho^{\prime}(p)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(s\big(\rho(p)-{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\big)+{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-2}\mathrm{d}s\Bigg).\end{split}

(25)

Now we will focus on the last integral above,

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(s\,\rho(p)+(1-s)\,{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-2}ds&=U\Bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\Big(s\,\lambda(A)+(1-s)\,{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\Big)^{-2}ds\Bigg)U^{\dagger}=\\
&=U\lambda^{-1}(\rho)U^{\dagger}=\rho(p)^{-1},\end{split}

(26)

where
\lambda(\rho)
is a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues of

\rho
on a diagonal and
U
is a unitary matrix.
According to the above considerations

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}g}{dp^{2}}\leq&\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\big(\rho^{\prime}(p)\big)^{2}\rho^{-1}(p)\Big)\\
=&\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{D_{\Phi}}\Big({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\rho(p)\Big)\sqrt{D_{\Phi}}\big(\sqrt{D_{\Phi}}\big)^{-1}\big({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes P\big)^{-1}\big(\sqrt{D_{\Phi}}\big)^{-1}\sqrt{D_{\Phi}}\Big({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\rho(p)\Big)\sqrt{D_{\Phi}}\\
=&\mathrm{Tr}J({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\rho(p)P^{-1}\rho(p))=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Tr}_{A}J)\rho(p)P^{-1}\rho(p)=\mathrm{Tr}P^{-1}=\frac{1}{p(1-p)}.\end{split}

(27)

Combining this with Eq (
21
) we see that
f(p)
is concave.

Based on the above lemmas, it can be concluded that supremum in

D(\Phi\|\mathcal{R})
, where
\Phi({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}
, is obtained for
p=\frac{1}{2}
,
which
indicates it is achieved for the maximally entangled state

|\phi^{+}\rangle
.
Thus, combination of the lemmas proves Theorem
2
.

4
Asymptotic case

In this section, we will show that Eq.
8
is
saturated in
the case of large system size. Firstly, let us denote
\dim(\mathcal{X}_{A})=\dim(\mathcal{X}_{B})=d
.
Numerical investigations lead us to formulate the following
conjecture.

Conjecture 1

Let
\Phi\in\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{X})
and
d=\dim(\mathcal{X})
. Then as
d\to\infty

H(\Phi)\simeq H^{K}(\Phi)-\log d\simeq\log d-\frac{1}{2}+o(1),

(28)

where
\Phi
chosen randomly according to measures introduced
in
[
25
]
.

To provide some intuition behind this conjecture, we first state
a theorem which tells us about the distribution of eigenvalues of
the output of an extended random quantum channel, when the input
is also chosen randomly.

This is summarized as Theorem
5
.

Theorem 5

Let
\Phi
be a random channel with Jamiołkowski matrix

D_{\Phi}
, we assume
that the limiting distribution of eigenvalues of
D_{\Phi}
is
given by
\mu
.
Let
\phi
be a random pure state with the limiting distribution
of Schmidt
values given by
\nu
. We define

\sigma=(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|),

(29)

then the limiting distribution of eigenvalues of
\sigma
is
given by
\mu\boxtimes\nu
.

Proof.

Note that

|\phi\rangle=(W\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i,i\rangle,

(30)

where
W
is a random unitary matrix and
\{|i\rangle\}
is the
computational
basis.

\begin{split}\sigma=(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)&=\sum_{ij}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})((W\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})|i,i\rangle\langle j,j|(W^{\dagger}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}))\\
&=\sum_{ij}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}(\Phi_{W}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|i,i\rangle\langle j,j|),\end{split}

(31)

where
\Phi_{W}(\rho)=\Phi(W\rho W^{\dagger})
, note that
Jamiołkowski matrix
of channel
\Phi_{W}
has the same spectrum that the
Jamiołkowski matrix of
channel
\Phi
. Next we write

\begin{split}\sigma&=\sum_{ij}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}(\Phi_{W}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|i,i\rangle\langle j,j|)\\
&=\sum_{ij}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}\Phi_{W}(|i\rangle\langle j|)\otimes|i\rangle\langle j|\\
&=({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda}))D_{\Phi_{W}}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda})).\end{split}

(32)

Now note, that the eigenvalues of
\sigma
are the same as
eigenvalues of

D_{\Phi_{W}}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\mathrm{diag}({\lambda}))
, which gives the
result.

Now, we have the following intuition behind our conjecture. Combining
the
results
from
[
26
,
27
]

with
[
25
,
28
]
we have for
large
d

and uniform distribution of channels

H^{K}(\Phi)=2\log d-\frac{1}{2}+o(1).

(33)

Next, we have the following result. Let
|\phi\rangle
be a random pure
state with
the Schmidt numbers chosen according to some measure
\nu
and let

|\phi\rangle

be free from
\Phi
. Then the output state has its spectrum given by
the free
multiplicative convolution
\mu\boxtimes\nu
, where
\mu
is the
distribution
of eigenvalues of
D_{\Phi}
.

Let us consider following optimization target

D(\Phi\|\mathcal{R})=\sup_{|\phi\rangle\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{R})}D\Big((\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\|(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)\Big),

(34)

where
|\phi\rangle=(U\otimes V)\sum_{i}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}|i,i\rangle
for
some unitary matrices
U
,
V
. Note that optimization result is
invariant
under local operations
U
,
V
on
|\phi\rangle
, but it depends on

\lambda_{i}
. It can be checked that

\sigma=(\Phi\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)=({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda}))D_{\Phi}({\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\lambda}))

(35)

and

\gamma=(\mathcal{R}\otimes{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl})(|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)={\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}/d\otimes\mathrm{diag}(\lambda).

(36)

Next consider
D(\Phi\|\mathcal{R})=\sup_{|\phi\rangle\in\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{X}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{R})}\mathrm{Tr}\sigma\log\sigma-\mathrm{Tr}\sigma\log\gamma
.
Moreover,

\begin{split}\mathrm{Tr}\sigma\log\gamma&=\mathrm{Tr}D_{\Phi}\cdot{\rm 1\hskip-2.56073ptl}\otimes\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda\log\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)\\
&=\mathrm{Tr}\mathrm{Tr}_{A}D_{\Phi}\cdot\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda\log\frac{\lambda}{d}\right)=-H(\lambda)-\log d\end{split}

(37)

The above expression reaches minimum for uniform distributed

\lambda
and
them is equal to
\mathrm{Tr}\sigma\log\gamma=-2\log d
. Since
\sigma
has
spectrum given by

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we discuss two approaches to entropic
quantification of quantum channels. We begin our studies with a
formulation of a lemma, which describes a relation between the
entropy of quantum channels proposed by Gour and Wilde

[
17
]
and entropy of Jamiołkowski matrix of
quantum channels
[
8
,
9
]
.
We show that both definitions give the same value up to an
additive constant in the case of the quantum unital qubit
channels. This part of our considerations uses the mathematical
language of distinguishability of quantum states and channels.
Therefore we assume that obtained results can be used to study
resource theories and hypothesis testing.
We also provide a conjecture backed by numerical experiments that
both formulas provide the same results up to an additive constant
in the case of large system size.

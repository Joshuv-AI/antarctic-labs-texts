Abstract
. Given positive integers n ≥ s n\geq s , we let ex ⁡ ( n , s ) \mathrm{ex}(n,s) denote the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph G G on n n vertices with α ⁡ ( G ) ≤ s \alpha(G)\leq s . In the early sixties Andrásfai conjectured that for n / 3 < s < n / 2 n/3<s<n/2 the function ex ⁡ ( n , s ) \mathrm{ex}(n,s) is piecewise quadratic with critical values at s / n = k / ( 3 ​ k − 1 ) s/n={k}/({3k-1}) for k ∈ ℕ k\in{\mathds{N}} . 1.
Introduction

The structure of dense triangle-free graphs has been the subject of extensive studies
for a long time. The first result in this direction is Mantel’s celebrated theorem
[
17
]

from 1907, which states that balanced bipartite graphs are the densest
triangle-free graphs.
It is natural to ask for the densest triangle-free graphs when we impose some additional
restrictions on them; for instance, we may bound their chromatic or independence number.

Let us first discuss the case when we require the chromatic number of a dense triangle-free
graph to be large. It is easy to see that in this case the appropriate measure of the density
of a graph is not the number of its edges (as one can always add a small graph of large
chromatic number to a complete bipartite graph), but rather its minimum degree.
This avenue of research was started by Andrásfai, Erdős, and Sós
[
2
]
who
showed that among triangle-free graphs with chromatic number three those with
the largest minimum degree are ‘balanced blow-ups’ of the pentagon. Erdős and
Simonovits
[
11
]
noticed that a construction due to Hajnal shows that for
every
k\geq 2
,
\varepsilon>0
, and sufficiently large
n
, there exists a triangle-free graph
on
n
vertices whose minimum degree is larger than
(1/3-\varepsilon)n
, and whose
chromatic number is
k
. On the other hand, they conjectured that every triangle-free
graph on
n
vertices whose minimum degree is larger than
n/3
is
3
-colourable.
This was refuted by Häggkvist
[
14
]
, who found a
10
-regular triangle-free graph
on
29
vertices whose chromatic number is four. Jin
[
15
]
showed that this example
is insofar optimal that every triangle-free graph whose minimum degree is strictly
larger than
10n/29
has chromatic number three. Moreover, Thomassen
[
20
]
proved
that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon}
such that every
triangle-free
n
-vertex graph with minimum degree at least
(1/3+\varepsilon)n
has
chromatic number at most
c_{\varepsilon}
, and Łuczak
[
25
]
supplemented this
result by proving, roughly speaking, that for some constant
C_{\varepsilon}
there are at
most
C_{\varepsilon}
‘types’ of such graphs.
Finally, Brandt and Thomassé
[
7
]
characterised all triangle-free graphs on
n

vertices whose minimum degree is larger than
n/3
; their theorem, stated in
Section
5
below, plays a decisive rôle in the proof of our main result.

In this article, however, we mainly study triangle-free graphs
G
with bounded
independence number
\alpha(G)
. More specifically, we are interested in the behaviour
of the function
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
, which for
n\geq s\geq 1
gives the largest number of edges
in a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices whose independent sets have at most the
size
s
, i.e.,

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=\max\{e(G)\colon v(G)=n,\ G\not\supseteq K_{3},\ \text{ and }\alpha(G)\leq s\}\,.

Notice that Mantel’s theorem yields

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor\quad\text{ for every }s>\lfloor n/2\rfloor\,.

Next we observe that in a triangle-free graph the neighbourhood of every vertex
forms an independent set, which implies the so-called
trivial bound

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\leq ns/2

for all
n
and
s
. Brandt
[
8
]
provided several explicit constructions showing
that this upper bound is asymptotically optimal for
s\leq n/3
, i.e., that we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=ns/2+o(n^{2})

in this range. Thus, it remains to study the behaviour of
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
for
s/n\in(1/3,1/2)
.

This line of research was started over 50 years ago by Andrásfai
[
3
]
,
who proved

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=n^{2}-4ns+5s^{2}\quad\text{ for }s/n\in[2/5,1/2]\,.

He also speculated that
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
might be a piecewise quadratic function with cusps at
points of the form
s=kn/(3k-1)
. We slightly revised his conjecture in
[
22
]
and
resolved the next case by showing

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}\quad\text{ for }s/n\in[3/8,2/5]\,.

The new version of the conjecture reads as follows.

Conjecture 1.1
.

If
n/3<s\leq n/2
, then

(1.1)

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=\min_{k}g_{k}(n,s)\,,

where

(1.2)

g_{k}(n,s)=k(k-1)n^{2}/2-k(3k-4)ns+(3k-4)(3k-1)s^{2}/2

for every
k\geq 1
.

Let us remark that we also have a conjecture on the extremal graphs for which
equality holds in (
1.1
). Since the definition of these graphs requires some
preparation, we state this stronger conjecture only at the end the article.

As for the function
g(n,s)=\min_{k}g_{k}(n,s)
, which stands
on the right side of (
1.1
), an elementary calculation (see
[
22
]
*Cor. 2.7)
shows that for
k\geq 2
and
\frac{k}{3k-1}n\leq s<\frac{k-1}{3k-4}n
we
have
g(n,s)=g_{k}(n,s)
. Thus, for fixed
n
the function
g(n,s)
is piecewise quadratic
in
s\in(n/3,n/2)
with cusps at the points
s=kn/(3k-1)
for
k\geq 2
.

The main goal of this work is to add further plausibility to Conjecture
1.1

by proving it whenever
s/n
is slightly larger than one of the ‘critical points’
k/(3k-1)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
k\geq 2
there exists
\gamma=\gamma(k)>0
such that

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=g_{k}(n,s)=\min_{\ell}g_{\ell}(n,s)

whenever

\frac{k}{3k-1}n\leq s\leq\left(\frac{k}{3k-1}+\gamma\right)n\,.

For instance, this holds for
\gamma(k)=(600k^{6})^{-1}
.

Along the way, we establish the following minimum degree version of Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq s\geq 1
. If
H
denotes a triangle-free graph on
n

vertices with
\alpha(H)\leq s
and

\delta(H)>\frac{k+1}{3k+2}n\,,

then
e(H)\leq g_{k}(n,s)
.

Let us mention that similar problems could be and, in many cases, have been,
considered for
K_{r}
-free graphs and, more generally, for
H
-free graphs for
any given graph
H
. It hardly seems necessary to recall that Turán’s problem to
determine the maximum number of edges in an
H
-free graph on
n
vertices is
fairly well understood thanks to the work of Turán himself
[
21
]
, Erdős,
Stone, and Simonovits
[
13
,
10
]
. The studies of the
chromatic threshold

(equal to 1/3 for triangle-free graphs by the aforementioned result of Thomassen
[
20
]
)
were begun by Łuczak and Thomassé
[
24
]
and culminated in the work of
Allen
et al.

[
1
]
who determined this parameter for
H
-free graphs
when an arbitrary graph
H
is given (for the precise definition of the ‘chromatic threshold’
we refer to either of those two articles).

The question on the behaviour of
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
considered in this work belongs to an area
called
Ramsey-Turán theory
, which has been initiated by Vera T. Sós and
extensively investigated during the last fifty years. There is a comprehensive survey
on this subject by Sós and Simonovits
[
18
]
. Important milestones
in the Ramsey-Turán theory of general
K_{r}
-free
graphs were obtained by Bollobás,
Erdős, Hajnal, Sós, Szemerédi
[
5
,
9
,
12
,
19
]
, and, more recently,
by Lüders and Reiher
[
16
]
. Due to their work, we asymptotically
know the value of

\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,s)=\max\{e(G)\colon v(G)=n,\ G\not\supseteq K_{r},\ \text{ and }\alpha(G)\leq s\}

for all
r\geq 3
provided that
s/n\ll r^{-1}
is sufficiently small.
It would, of course, be interesting to study this function for larger values of
s/n
as
well, but, as the present article demonstrates, even the case
r=3
of triangles seems
to be fairly difficult.

We believe that with considerably more work the methods of this article would allow
to prove Theorem
1.3
under the weaker assumption
\delta(H)>n/3
as well, which
would lead to some numerical improvement on the value of
\gamma(k)
in Theorem
1.2
.

The structure of the article is the following. In Section
2
we start with
the definition and some basic properties of the blow-up operation. The two subsequent
sections define and study Andrásfai and Vega graphs, which – by a result of
Brandt and Thomassé –
are the
main protagonists in the story of dense triangle-free graphs (see
Theorem
5.1
below).
In particular, in this part of the article we prove some special cases of
Theorem
1.2
addressing blow-ups of these two types of graphs (see
Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
4.1
). In Section
5
these results
will be employed in the proofs of the Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.
Moreover, we shall state there a precise version of our conjecture on extremal cases
in Conjecture
1.1
.
These are the same as the extremal graphs for the two aforementioned lemmata, which
we characterise in Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
4.4
, respectively.

2.
Blow-ups of graphs

Given a graph
F
with vertex set
V(F)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
, a
blow-up of
F

is a graph
H
obtained from
F
upon replacing its vertices by
independent sets
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}
, and each of its edges
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(F)
,

1\leq i<j\leq r
, by the complete bipartite graph
K(V_{i},V_{j})
between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
.
The sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
are called the
vertex classes
of
H
. As above, we shall
always denote the vertices of the original graph
F
by lower case letters and the vertex
classes of
H
by capitalised versions of the same letters. A blow-up is
proper
if
all vertex classes are non-empty and
balanced
if all of them are of the same size.
As the isomorphism type of
H
depends only on the sizes of its vertex classes it will be
convenient to write
H=F(h)
, where the function
h\colon V(F)\longrightarrow{\mathds{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is defined
by
h(v_{i})=|V_{i}|
for every
v_{i}\in V(F)
. In the special case where
h
is the constant
function attaining always the value
p
it will be convenient to write
H=F(p)
.
For later use we remark that a blow-up of a blow-up of
F
is again a blow-up of
F
.

Resuming the discussion of the blow-up
H
of
F
with vertex classes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}

we set

N(V_{i})=\bigcup_{v_{j}\in N(v_{i})}V_{j}

for each of these vertex classes, where
N(v_{i})
denotes the neighbourhood of
v_{i}

in
F
. Clearly, all vertices in
V_{i}
have the neighbourhood
N(V_{i})
in
H
and,
consequently, every non-empty vertex class
V_{i}
satisfies

(2.1)

\delta(H)\leq|N(V_{i})|\leq\Delta(H)\,.

Now simple averaging leads to the following observation.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
H
be an
n
-vertex blow-up of a
k
-regular graph
F
on the
r
-element
vertex set
V(F)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
.

(

)

We have

(2.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}|N(V_{i})|=kn\,.

(

)

If the blow-up
H
is proper, then

\delta(H)\leq\frac{kn}{r}\leq\Delta(H)\,.

Proof.

The double counting argument

\sum_{i=1}^{r}|N(V_{i})|=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{v_{j}\in N(v_{i})}|V_{j}|=\sum_{j=1}^{r}|N(v_{j})|\,|V_{j}|=k\sum_{j=1}^{r}|V_{j}|=kn

establishes part
( )
‣
2.1
. If
H
is a proper blow-up, then (
2.1
)
yields

r\delta(H)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|N(V_{i})|\leq r\Delta(H)

and part
( )
‣
2.1
follows.
∎

The second part of the foregoing fact has the following useful consequence.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d,k\geq 2
be two integers and let
J
be a graph. Suppose that
J
has a
k
-regular
proper blow-up
F
on
3k-1
vertices and that
H
denotes a further proper blow-up of
J

having
n
vertices. If

(2.3)

\frac{d+1}{3d+2}n<\delta(H)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Delta(H)<\frac{d-1}{3d-4}n\,,

then
k=d
.

Proof.

Notice that every balanced blow-up
H(p)
of
H
is a proper blow-up of
J
as well.
By applying this observation to a sufficiently large integer
p
we learn that we may
assume, without loss of generality, that
H
is a proper blow-up of
F
.
Now Fact
2.1
( )
‣
2.1
reveals

\delta(H)\leq\frac{kn}{3k-1}\leq\Delta(H)\,,

which together with (
2.3
) implies

\frac{d+1}{3d+2}<\frac{k}{3k-1}<\frac{d-1}{3d-4}\,.

Taking reciprocals we obtain

3-\frac{1}{d-1}<3-\frac{1}{k}<3-\frac{1}{d+1}\,,

i.e.,
d-1<k<d+1
.
∎

The main result of this section is the following upper bound on the number of edges
of a blow-up.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
F
is a
k
-regular graph on
r
vertices
and that
H
is an
n
-vertex blow-up of
F
all of whose vertex classes have at
least the size
x
. If we have
|N(Z)|\leq s
for every vertex class
Z
of
H
, then

(2.4)

e(H)\leq\frac{ns}{2}-\frac{x(rs-kn)}{2}\,.

Moreover, if
rs\neq kn
and (
2.4
) holds with equality, then

\bullet

at least one vertex class of
H
has size
x
;

\bullet

every vertex class
V_{i}
of
H
with
|V_{i}|>x

satisfies
|N(V_{i})|=s
.

Proof.

As usual, we write the vertex set of
F
in the form
V(F)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
.
Setting
x_{i}=|V_{i}|
for
i\in[r]
we have

\displaystyle 2e(H)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(H)}\deg_{H}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|V_{i}|\cdot|N(V_{i})|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}x\cdot|N(V_{i})|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(x_{i}-x)\cdot|N(V_{i})|

\displaystyle\leq x\sum_{i=1}^{r}|N(V_{i})|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}(x_{i}-x)s\overset{\eqref{eq:1734}}{=}xkn+s(n-xr)=ns-x(rs-kn)\,,

which proves the desired upper bound on
e(H)
.

Suppose from now on that this estimate holds with equality and that
rs\neq kn
.
This means that
(x_{i}-x)|N(V_{i})|=(x_{i}-x)s
holds for every
i\in[r]
,
or in other words that
x_{i}>x
implies
|N(V_{i})|=s
. This proves the second bullet.
Now, if the first bullet fails, we have
|N(V_{i})|=s
for every
i\in[r]

and (
2.2
) yields the contradiction
rs=kn
.
∎

Let us notice the following consequence of the above result.

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose that
k\geq 2
is a natural number,
F
is a
k
-regular graph
on
3k-1
vertices and
H

is an

-vertex blow-up of
F
. If
|N(Z)|\leq s
and

hold for every vertex class

of

Moreover, if

, then

(

)

kn/(3k-1)\leq s\leq(k-1)n/(3k-4)
;

(

)

H
contains a vertex class of size
(k-1)n-(3k-4)s
;

(

)

the neighbourhood of each vertex class of
H
containing
more than
(k-1)n-(3k-4)s

.

Proof.

In order to prove the desired upper bound on

we remark that Lemma

applied to

Let us now study the case that equality holds in this estimate. If clause
( )
‣

The remaining two clauses follow from the moreover-part in Lemma

In other words,

3.
Andrásfai graphs and their blow-ups

The characterisation of triangle-free graphs on
n
vertices whose minimum degree is larger
than
n/3
due to Brandt and Thomassé
[
7
]
involves two explicit families
of such graphs, called
Andrásfai graphs
and
Vega graphs
(see
Theorem
5.1
below). In this section we study the first of these graph sequences,
which has been introduced by Andrásfai in
[
3
]
and has been rediscovered several
times throughout the years.

One way to construct a
k
-regular triangle-free graph is to take an Abelian
group
{\mathds{G}}
, a symmetric sum-free subset
S
of size
k
, and to form the
Cayley graph
\textrm{Cayley}({\mathds{G}};S)
. A natural (and, as we shall soon argue,
generic) example occurs when we take the cyclic group
{\mathds{Z}}_{3k-1}
and its
sum-free subset
S_{k}=\{k,k+1,\ldots,2k-1\}
.
The
Andrásfai graph
\Gamma_{k}
is defined to be the corresponding Cayley graph

\textrm{Cayley}({\mathds{Z}}_{3k-1};S_{k})
. Describing the same graph in more concrete terms,
we set

V(\Gamma_{k})=\{v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{3k-2}\}

and declare the adjacencies in
\Gamma_{k}
by

(3.1)

v_{i}v_{j}\in E(\Gamma_{k})\,\,\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\,\,k\leq|i-j|\leq 2k-1

for all vertices
v_{i},v_{j}\in V(\Gamma_{k})
.
For instance,
\Gamma_{1}=K_{2}
,
\Gamma_{2}=C_{5}
, and Figure
3.1
shows some further
Andrásfai graphs.

Figure 3.1
.
Andrásfai graphs

\Gamma_{3}
,
\Gamma_{4}
,
\Gamma_{5}
, and
\Gamma_{6}
.

Let us remark that given an Abelian group
{\mathds{G}}
containing a sum-free set
S

with
|S|>|{\mathds{G}}|/3
one can show by means of Kneser’s theorem (see
[
23
]
) that
for some positive integer
k
there
exists a homomorphism
\varphi\colon{\mathds{G}}\longrightarrow{\mathds{Z}}_{3k-1}

satisfying
\varphi[S]\subseteq S_{k}
.
Such a group homomorphism
\varphi
induces a graph homomorphism

\varphi_{\star}\colon\textrm{Cayley}({\mathds{G}};S)\longrightarrow\Gamma_{k}
,
or, in other words, it indicates that
\textrm{Cayley}({\mathds{G}};S)
is contained in a sufficiently
large blow-up of
\Gamma_{k}
. These considerations reveal that balanced blow-ups of
Andrásfai graphs are universal in the class of triangle-free Cayley graphs whose
density is larger than
1/3
. Somewhat relatedly, a finite graph is a subgraph of
a blow-up of an Andrásfai graph if and only if it is isomorphic to a subgraph of the
infinite triangle-free Cayley graph
\textrm{Cayley}\bigl({\mathds{R}}/{\mathds{Z}};(1/3,2/3)+{\mathds{Z}}\bigr)
,
see
[
4
]
*Lemma 2.1.
We proceed with three well known, useful properties of Andrásfai graphs.

Fact 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer.

(

)

We have
\Gamma_{k-1}\subseteq\Gamma_{k}
.
Conversely, if we remove a vertex from
\Gamma_{k}
, then the resulting
graph is a subgraph of a proper blow-up of
\Gamma_{k-1}
. Consequently,
every subgraph of
\Gamma_{k}
is a subgraph of a proper blow-up of
\Gamma_{\ell}

for some
\ell\leq k
.

(

)

The chromatic number of
\Gamma_{k}
is
3
.

(

)

Every independent set in
\Gamma_{k}
is contained
in the neighbourhood of some vertex of
\Gamma_{k}
. In particular,
\alpha(\Gamma_{k})=k
.

Proof.

The first part of
( )
‣
3.1
follows from
\Gamma_{k-1}\cong\Gamma_{k}-\{v_{0},v_{k},v_{2k}\}
.
Furthermore, the graph
\Gamma_{k}^{-}=\Gamma_{k}-\{v_{k}\}

satisfies
N(v_{0},\Gamma_{k}^{-})\subseteq N(v_{1},\Gamma_{k}^{-})

and
N(v_{2k},\Gamma_{k}^{-})\subseteq N(v_{2k-1},\Gamma_{k}^{-})
,
which proves
\Gamma_{k}^{-}
to be a subgraph of the blow-up
of
\Gamma_{k}-\{v_{0},v_{k},v_{2k}\}
obtained by doubling the vertices
v_{1}

and
v_{2k-1}
. This establishes the second part of
( )
‣
3.1
and the
last part follows inductively.

Proceeding with
( )
‣
3.1

( )
‣
3.1
,

\Gamma_{k}
contains a pentagon

as a subgraph, whence
\chi(\Gamma_{k})\geq\chi(\Gamma_{2})=3
.
To verify the reverse inequality, we partition

into the three independent sets

\{v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}\}
,
\{v_{k},v_{k+1},\ldots,v_{2k-1}\}
, and

\{v_{2k},v_{2k+1},\ldots,v_{3k-2}\}
.

Finally, let
S\subseteq V(\Gamma_{k})
be a nonempty independent set we want to cover by
the neighbourhood of an appropriate vertex. By symmetry we may suppose that

,
which due to (
3.1
) entails
S\subseteq\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{2k-1}\}

, where

(5.1)

\frac{k}{3k-1}n\leq s\leq\left(\frac{k}{3k-1}+\gamma\right)n\,.

Since
\gamma
is sufficiently small, we have
s<(k-1)n/(3k-4)
and
Fact
5.2
implies
e(G)\leq g_{\ell}(n,s)
for every
\ell\neq k
.
On the other hand, Lemma
3.5
yields

(5.2)

e(G)=\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\geq g_{k}(n,s)

and it remains to prove that this holds with equality. It will be convenient to
rewrite (
5.2
) as

e(G)\geq g_{k}(n,s)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[ns-\bigl((k-1)n-(3k-4)s\bigr)\bigl((3k-1)s-kn\bigr)\bigr]\,.

Since

0\leq(3k-1)s-kn<3k\gamma n

and

(k-1)n-(3k-4)s\leq\frac{(3k-1)(k-1)}{k}s-(3k-4)s=\frac{s}{k}\,,

we have

\bigl((k-1)n-(3k-4)s\bigr)\bigl((3k-1)s-kn\bigr)<3\gamma ns\,,

and thus

ns-2e(G)<3\gamma ns\,.

Therefore, the set

(5.3)

A=\left\{v\in V(G)\colon d(v)<\left(1-\zeta\right)s\right\}

satisfies

\zeta s|A|\leq\sum_{v\in V}\left(s-d(v)\right)=ns-2e(G)<3\gamma ns

and, consequently,

(5.4)

|A|<\frac{3\gamma n}{\zeta}=\frac{n}{25k^{4}}\,.

Now our argument will proceed as follows. We shall verify that the graph
G^{\prime}=G-A
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.3

and, hence,
e(G^{\prime})
is bounded from above by
g_{k}(n-|A|,s)
. Then, using the
fact that all vertices in
A
are of small degree, we derive an upper bound
of
g_{k}(n,s)
on the number of edges in
G
.

For the minimum degree of
G^{\prime}
we obtain

\displaystyle\delta(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\zeta\right)s-|A|>\frac{k}{3k-1}n-\frac{n/2}{8k^{2}}-\frac{n}{25k^{4}}

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{k+1}{3k+2}+\frac{1}{12k^{2}}-\frac{1}{16k^{2}}-\frac{1}{100k^{2}}\right)n

\displaystyle>\frac{k+1}{3k+2}n\geq\frac{k+1}{3k+2}|V(G^{\prime})|\,.

As the graph
G^{\prime}
is triangle-free and satisfies
\alpha(G^{\prime})\leq s
, this shows that
Theorem
1.3
applies to
G^{\prime}
and we are led to

(5.5)

e(G^{\prime})\leq g_{k}(n-|A|,s)=g_{k}(n,s)-|A|\left(k(k-1)n-\tfrac{1}{2}k(k-1)|A|-k(3k-4)s\right)\,.

Now our choice of
\zeta
and
\gamma
yields

\displaystyle\bigl((k-1)(3k-1)-\zeta\bigr)s

\displaystyle\leq\left((k-1)(3k-1)-\frac{1}{8k^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{k}{3k-1}+\frac{1}{600k^{6}}\right)n

\displaystyle<\left(k(k-1)+\frac{1}{200k^{4}}-\frac{1}{8k(3k-1)}\right)n

\displaystyle<\left(k(k-1)+\frac{1}{50k^{2}}-\frac{1}{25k^{2}}\right)n<k(k-1)\left(1-\frac{1}{50k^{4}}\right)n

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:0027}}{<}k(k-1)(n-\tfrac{1}{2}|A|)\,,

and for this reason (
5.5
) can be continued to

\displaystyle e(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq g_{k}(n,s)-\bigl((k-1)(3k-1)-\zeta-k(3k-4)\bigr)|A|s

\displaystyle=g_{k}(n,s)-(1-\zeta)|A|s\,.

Every vertex in
A
has degree at most
(1-\zeta)s
in
G
, so we arrive at

(5.6)

e(G)\leq e(G^{\prime})+(1-\zeta)|A|s\leq g_{k}(n,s)\,,

which together with (
5.2
) concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
. ∎

Finally, let us remark that our results allow to determine the extremal graphs for
Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Suppose that
k\geq 2
and that
G
denotes a triangle-free graph on
n

vertices with
\alpha(G)\leq s
for some integer
s
satisfying

\frac{k}{3k-1}n\leq s\leq\left(\frac{k}{3k-1}+\frac{1}{600k^{6}}\right)n\,.

If
e(G)=\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
, then
G
is isomorphic to a graph in
\mathscr{G}^{n}_{k}(s)\cup\mathscr{H}^{n}_{k}(s)
.

Proof.

Following the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we see that (
5.6
)
holds with equality, for which reason the set
A
defined in (
5.3
)
has to be empty. In other words,
G^{\prime}=G
and the proof of Theorem
1.3

discloses that
G^{\prime}
is a blow-up of either the Andrásfai graph
\Gamma_{k}
, or of
a Vega graph
\Upsilon^{\mu\nu}_{i}
with
k=9i-(6+\mu+\nu)
. In the former case,
Lemma
3.5
shows that
G
is isomorphic to a graph in
\mathscr{G}^{n}_{k}(s)

and in the latter case we apply Lemma
4.4
.
∎

In fact, we strongly suspect that these are the only extremal graphs for the whole
range of
s
, i.e., that the following stronger version of our initial conjecture holds.

Conjecture 5.4
.

If
n/3<s\leq n/2
, then

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=\min_{k}g_{k}(n,s)\,,

where
g_{k}(n,s)
is defined by (
1.2
).
Moreover, each extremal graph with
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
edges is isomorphic to one of the graphs
from the families
\mathscr{G}^{n}_{k}(s)
and
\mathscr{H}^{n}_{k}(s)
described in the Definitions
3.4

and
4.3
, respectively.

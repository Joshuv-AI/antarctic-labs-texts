The Newform K K -Type and p p -adic Spherical Harmonics
Peter Humphries

Abstract
. Let K ≔ GL n ⁡ ( 𝒪 ) K\coloneqq\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}) denote the maximal compact subgroup of GL n ⁡ ( F ) \GL_{n}(F) , where F F is a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers 𝒪 \mathcal{O} . We study the decomposition of the space of locally constant functions on the unit sphere in F n F^{n} into irreducible K K -modules; for F = ℚ p F=\mathbb{Q}_{p} , these are the p p -adic analogues of spherical harmonics. As an application, we characterise the newform and conductor exponent of a generic irreducible admissible smooth representation of GL n ⁡ ( F ) \GL_{n}(F) in terms of distinguished K K -types. Finally, we compare our results to analogous results in the archimedean setting.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Space
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
as a
K
-Module

Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, maximal prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
, and uniformiser
\varpi
, so that
\varpi\mathcal{O}=\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
for some prime power
q
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the finite field of order
q
. Thus either
F
is a finite extension of the
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
for some prime
p
or
F
is the field of formal Laurent series
\mathbb{F}_{q}((t))
. We let
K_{n}\coloneqq\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O})
denote the maximal compact subgroup of
\GL_{n}(F)
, which is unique up to conjugacy; when it is clear from context, we write
K
in place of
K_{n}
. Throughout we assume that
n\geq 2
.

The group
K_{n}
acts transitively on unit sphere
S^{n-1}
in
F^{n}
via the group action
k\cdot x\coloneqq xk
for
k\in K_{n}
and
x\in S^{n-1}
, where

S^{n-1}\coloneqq\left\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in F^{n}:\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n}|\}=1\right\};

here
|\cdot|
denotes the absolute value on
F
normalised such that
|\varpi|=q^{-1}
. The stabiliser subgroup of
K_{n}
with respect to the point
e_{n}\coloneqq(0,\ldots,0,1)\in S^{n-1}
is

(1.1)

K_{n-1,1}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in K_{n}:a\in K_{n-1},\ b\in\Mat_{(n-1)\times 1}(\mathcal{O})\right\},

which has the structure of the outer semidirect product
K_{n-1}\ltimes\mathcal{O}^{n-1}
. It follows that
S^{n-1}\cong K_{n-1,1}\backslash K_{n}
. Note that
K_{n-1,1}
is the maximal compact subgroup of the mirabolic subgroup

(1.2)

\mathrm{P}_{n}(F)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{n}(F):a\in\GL_{n-1}(F),\ b\in\Mat_{(n-1)\times 1}(F)\right\}

of
\GL_{n}(F)
.

Let
R
denote the right regular representation of
K_{n}
on the space of locally constant functions
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
; thus
(R(k)\cdot f)(x)\coloneqq f(xk)
for
f\in C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
,
k\in K_{n}
, and
x\in S^{n-1}
. A natural question to ponder is the following.

Question
.

What is the decomposition of the right regular representation
R
of
K_{n}
on
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
into irreducible smooth representations of
K_{n}
? Equivalently, which irreducible smooth representations of
K_{n}
have a nontrivial
K_{n-1,1}
-fixed vector?

We study this problem in
Section
2
. While the general classification of irreducible smooth representations of
K_{n}
is unknown for
n\geq 3
, we show that the representations having a nontrivial
K_{n-1,1}
-fixed vector can be explicitly described; in particular, a precise resolution of this question is given in
Theorem
2.16
. The irreducible smooth representations of interest are indexed by pairs
(\chi,m)
of characters
\chi
of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
and nonnegative integers
m\geq c(\chi)
, where
c(\chi)
denotes the conductor exponent of
\chi
. The character
\chi
is the central character of this representation, while
m
is its level, namely the minimal nonnegative integer for which this representation factors through
\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})
. We denote by
(\tau_{\chi,m},\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}))
the irreducible smooth representation associated to such a pair, where
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
is a finite-dimensional vector subspace of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
; this may be thought of as the nonarchimedean analogue of the vector space of spherical harmonics of degree
m
on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, when
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we may think of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
as being the space of
p
-adic spherical harmonics of character
\chi
and level
m
.

Remark 1.3
.

The decomposition attained in
Theorem
2.16
is not, in essence, fundamentally new. With
K_{n}=\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O})
replaced by
\SL_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, this decomposition was previously achieved (with scant proofs) by Petrov
[
Pet82
]
in a seemingly neglected paper; our method follows that sketched by Petrov and achieves the same decomposition when
n\geq 3
. A closely related result, via slightly different methods, is due to Hill
[
Hil94
, Proposition 3.3]
, who studies instead the decomposition of
C^{\infty}(\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})K_{n-1,1}\backslash K_{n})
, where
\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})
denotes the centre of
K
; note that we may identify
\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})K_{n-1,1}\backslash K_{n}
with
(n-1)
-dimensional
F
-projective space
F\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

1.2.
Zonal Spherical Functions

We next study the subspace of locally constant functions in
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
that are
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant in
Section
3
. Each irreducible subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
has a one-dimensional subspace of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant functions, so that there exists a unique function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
satisfying
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(xk^{\prime})=P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(x)
for all
x\in S^{n-1}
and
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}
and
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
. We call
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
the zonal spherical function on
S^{n-1}
of character
\chi
and level
m
. These are the nonarchimedean analogues of zonal spherical harmonics (or ultraspherical polynomials).

We give an explicit formula for
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
in
Proposition
3.4
. Using this, we prove a nonarchimedean analogue of the addition formula for spherical harmonics in
Lemma
3.14
. A consequence of this is
Corollary
3.20
, which states that
(\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}))P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
; this implies that each element of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
is
equal
to a matrix coefficient of
\tau_{\chi,m}
.

1.3.
The Newform and the Conductor Exponent

We apply this theory of
p
-adic spherical harmonics in
Section
4
to previous work of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
JP-SS81
]
. They prove the existence of a distinguished vector, the newform, associated to a given generic irreducible admissible representation
(\pi,V_{\pi})
of
\GL_{n}(F)
(or more generally to an induced representation of Langlands type). This vector is invariant under a certain congruence subgroup
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
of
K_{n}
and is the minimal such nontrivial vector in the sense that there are no nontrivial vectors invariant under
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
with
\ell<m
. This minimal value of
m
is called the conductor exponent of
\pi
and is denoted by
c(\pi)
.

We give alternative characterisations of the newform and conductor exponent in
Theorem
4.11
. Using the work of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
JP-SS81
]
, we show that the conductor exponent
c(\pi)
of
\pi
is the minimal nonnegative integer
m
for which there exists a nontrivial
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant
\tau_{\chi,m}
-isotypic vector in
V_{\pi}
for some character
\chi
of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
; necessarily,
\chi
must then be equal to
\chi_{\pi}
, the restriction from
F^{\times}
to
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
of the central character
\omega_{\pi}
of
\pi
. We also show that the newform is precisely the nonzero vector, unique up to scalar multiplication, that is
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant and
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
-isotypic; for this reason, we name
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
the newform
K
-type. Our methods also allow us to describe precisely in
Proposition
4.26
the multiplicity with which a representation
\tau_{\chi,m}
occurs in the
K
-type decomposition of
\pi
.

These different characterisations open up new avenues of approach to studying properties of the newform. We give a simple example of one such property in
Corollary
4.35
, where we show that the matrix coefficient associated to the newform may be explicitly described in terms of the zonal spherical function
P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}(S^{n-1})
.

1.4.
The Archimedean Theory

Finally, in
Section
5
, we compare our results to analogous results in the archimedean setting. The archimedean analogue of the maximal compact subgroup
K_{n}=\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O})
of
\GL_{n}(F)
is the orthogonal group
\Ogp(n)
if
F=\mathbb{R}
and the unitary group
\Ugp(n)
if
F=\mathbb{C}
. The decomposition of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
into irreducible
K_{n}
-modules in these settings is well-known: it is the classical theory of spherical harmonics. These are the restriction to the unit sphere of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of a given degree if
F=\mathbb{R}
or of a given bidegree if
F=\mathbb{C}
. In both cases, there exist analogues of the the zonal spherical functions
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
.

The archimedean analogue of the theory of newforms and conductor exponents of a generic irreducible admissible smooth representation of
\GL_{n}(F)
was recently developed by the author
[
Hum20
]
. Over archimedean local fields, the approach of Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
JP-SS81
]
via congruence subgroups is no longer applicable, and instead the development of the theory of newforms is via distinguished
K_{n}
-types. Thus our alternative characterisation in the nonarchimedean setting proven in
Theorem
4.11
serves to unify these two different settings.

2.
p
-adic Spherical Harmonics

Our goal is to decompose the right regular representation of
K
on
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
into irreducible smooth representations. Here
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
is the space of locally constant functions
f:S^{n-1}\to\mathbb{C}
, namely functions that are right-invariant by a compact open subgroup of
K
. We define a
K
-invariant inner product on
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})\ni f_{1},f_{2}
via

(2.1)

\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle\coloneqq\int_{K}f_{1}(e_{n}k)\overline{f_{2}}(e_{n}k)\,dk,

where
dk
denotes the Haar probability measure on the compact group
K
. We also recall that given a closed subgroup
H
of a compact profinite group
G
and a one-dimensional representation
\psi
of
H
, the induced representation
\Ind_{H}^{G}\psi
is the vector space of locally constant functions
\phi:G\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfy
\phi(hg)=\psi(h)\phi(g)
for all
g\in G
and
h\in H
, upon which
G
acts via right translations.

Our first observation is that the space
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
may be identified with the induced representation of the trivial representation of
K_{n-1,1}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

As
K
-modules, the space
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
is isomorphic to
\Ind_{K_{n-1,1}}^{K}1
.

Proof.

Given
f\in C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
, define
\phi(k)\coloneqq f(e_{n}k)
; then clearly
\phi\in\Ind_{K_{n-1,1}}^{K}1
. Conversely, given
\phi\in\Ind_{K_{n-1,1}}^{K}1
,
\phi(k)
is dependent only on
e_{n}k
. Since for each
x\in S^{n-1}
, there exists some
k\in K
for which
x=e_{n}k
, the function
f(x)\coloneqq\phi(k)
is well-defined, which gives us an element of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
.
∎

2.1.
Reduction to
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})

We next study subspaces of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
consisting of locally constant functions invariant under certain congruence subgroups. For each nonnegative integer
m
, let
K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
denote the principal congruence subgroup of level
m
of
K
, namely

K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\coloneqq\left\{k\in K:k-1_{n}\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right\},

where
1_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix. This is a normal subgroup of
K
. These subgroups allow us to construct a filtration of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
via the subspaces

C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}\coloneqq\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(S^{n-1}):f(xk)=f(x)\text{ for all $x\in S^{n-1}$ and $k\in K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})$}\right\}

of
K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
-invariant locally constant functions on
S^{n-1}
. We observe that
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is contained in
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})}
for all nonnegative integers
m\leq\ell
. Furthermore, the union
\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is equal to
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
.

We also define the congruence subgroup
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
of
K
by

K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K:c\in\Mat_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\ d-1\in\mathfrak{p}^{m}\right\}

for each nonnegative integer
m
, so that
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{0})=K
, while if
m\geq 1
,
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
implies that
a\in K_{n-1}
and
d\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
; we make note of the fact that
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
contains
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
and
K(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
as subgroups whenever
\ell\geq m
.

Lemma 2.3
.

As
K
-modules,
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is isomorphic to
\Ind_{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}1
.

Proof.

From
Lemma
2.2
,
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is isomorphic as a
K
-module to the space of locally constant functions
\phi:K\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfy
\phi(k^{\prime}kk^{\prime\prime})=\phi(k)
for all
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}
,
k\in K
, and
k^{\prime\prime}\in K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
. Since
K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
is a normal subgroup of
K
, this is equal to the space of locally constant functions
\phi:K\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfy
\phi(k^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime}k)=\phi(k)
for all
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}
,
k^{\prime\prime}\in K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, and
k\in K
. The result then follows from the fact that
K_{n-1,1}K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, which is clearly true if
m=0
; for
m\geq 1
, it is immediate that
K_{n-1,1}K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\subseteq K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, while the fact that
K_{n-1,1}K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\supseteq K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
can be seen directly, since for
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, we have that

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&bd^{-1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1_{n-1}-a^{-1}bd^{-1}c&0\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.\qed

Corollary 2.4
.

We have that

(2.5)

\dim C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=\begin{cases}1&if $m=0$,\\
q^{(m-1)n}(q^{n}-1)&if $m\geq 1$.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have that
\dim C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=\dim\Ind_{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}1
from
Lemma
2.3
. Since the trivial representation is one-dimensional, the dimension of the monomial representation
\Ind_{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}1
is simply the index
[K:K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})]
of the subgroup
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
in
K
. We claim that this is precisely the right-hand side of (
2.5
). Indeed, we have that

\left[K:K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right]=\left[K/K(\mathfrak{p}^{m}):K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})/K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right]=\left[\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m}):\mathrm{P}_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right],

where
\mathrm{P}_{n}
denotes the mirabolic subgroup (
1.2
). We may identify
\mathrm{P}_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})\backslash\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})
with

(2.6)

\overline{S}^{n-1}\coloneqq\left\{\overline{x}=(\overline{x_{1}},\ldots,\overline{x_{n}})\in(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})^{n}:\max\{|\overline{x_{1}}|,\ldots,|\overline{x_{n}}|\}=1\right\},

where
\overline{x_{j}}
denotes a coset of
\mathfrak{p}^{m}
in
\mathcal{O}
of the form
x_{j}+\mathfrak{p}^{m}
with a representative
x_{j}\in\mathcal{O}
, and
|\overline{x_{j}}|
is equal to
|x_{j}|
if the representative
x_{j}
is not in
\mathfrak{p}^{m}
and is equal to
q^{-m}
otherwise; it is not hard to see that
|\overline{x_{j}}|
is independent of the choice of representative
x_{j}
and hence is well-defined. Now a straightforward induction on
m
argument shows that the set
\overline{S}^{n-1}
has cardinality
q^{(m-1)n}(q^{n}-1)
.
∎

2.2.
Reduction to
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})

Next, we decompose
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
further into subspaces of locally constant functions with a prescribed central character, noting that the centre of
K
is
\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})\coloneqq\{z(a)\coloneqq\diag(a,\ldots,a)\in K:a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}\}
. Given a character
\chi
of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, we define the subspace of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
consisting of
K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
-invariant (and hence locally constant) functions on
S^{n-1}
with central character
\chi
by

C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}\coloneqq\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}:f(ax)=\chi(a)f(x)\text{ for all $a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}$ and $x\in S^{n-1}$}\right\}.

Clearly
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is trivial if
m<c(\chi)
, where
c(\chi)
denotes the conductor exponent of
\chi
, namely the least nonnegative integer
m
for which
\chi
is trivial on
1+\mathfrak{p}^{m}
. Moreover,
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is contained in
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})}
for all
\ell\geq m\geq c(\chi)
and any two subspaces
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi_{1}}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m_{1}})}
and
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi_{2}}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m_{2}})}
are mutually orthogonal with respect to the inner product (
2.1
) whenever
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
.

For each nonnegative integer
m
, the congruence subgroup
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
of
K
is defined by

K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K:c\in\Mat_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right\},

so that
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{0})=K
, while if
m\geq 1
,
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
implies that
a\in K_{n-1}
and
d\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. We observe that
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
contains
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
,
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
, and
K(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
as subgroups whenever
\ell\geq m
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
denote the set of (continuous) characters
\chi:\mathcal{O}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
; necessarily the image of such a character is in the unit circle
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}:|z|=1\}
. For
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
, let
\psi_{\chi}
be the character of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\chi)})\ni k_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
given by
\psi_{\chi}(k_{0})\coloneqq\chi(d)
, which is a one-dimensional representation of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\chi)})
; by restriction, this is also a one-dimensional representation of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
whenever
m\geq c(\chi)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
and
m\geq c(\chi)
,
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is isomorphic as a
K
-module to
\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}
.

Proof.

From
Lemma
2.3
,
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is isomorphic as a
K
-module to the space of locally constant functions
\phi:K\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfy
\phi(z(a)k_{1}k)=\chi(a)\phi(k)
for all
z(a)\in\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})
,
k_{1}\in K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, and
k\in K
. It remains to note that
\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

We have that

(2.9)

\dim C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=\begin{cases}1&if $m=c(\chi)=0$,\\
q^{(m-1)(n-1)}\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}&if $m\geq\max\{c(\chi),1\}$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have that
\dim C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=\dim\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}
from
Lemma
2.7
. Since
\psi_{\chi}
is one-dimensional, the dimension of the monomial representation
\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}
is simply the index of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
in
K
, so that

\dim\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}=\begin{cases}\left[K:K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right]&if $m\geq c(\chi)$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

This precisely the right-hand side of (
2.9
), since
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
is a normal subgroup of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
with quotient isomorphic to the finite abelian group
\mathcal{O}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, which has cardinality
q^{m-1}(q-1)
, together with the fact that
[K:K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})]=[K:K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})][K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}):K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})]
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

We have the orthogonal decomposition

(2.11)

C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\\
0\leq c(\chi)\leq m\end{subarray}}C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}.

Proof.

Since
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
is a normal subgroup of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
with quotient isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, we have that

(2.12)

\Ind_{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}1=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\\
0\leq c(\chi)\leq m\end{subarray}}\psi_{\chi}

and so by inducing in stages,

\Ind_{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}1=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\\
0\leq c(\chi)\leq m\end{subarray}}\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}.

Together with
Lemma
2.7
, this gives the orthogonal decomposition (
2.11
).
∎

2.3.
Irreducible Representations

The spaces
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
are not irreducible if
m>c(\chi)
since
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
contains
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})}
for all
\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}
. Thus we are led to study the orthogonal complement
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}\ominus C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m-1})}
of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m-1})}
in
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
with respect to the inner product (
2.1
). For
m\geq c(\chi)
, define

(2.13)

\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})\coloneqq\begin{cases}C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\chi)})}&if $m=c(\chi)$,\\
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}\ominus C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m-1})}&if $m>c(\chi)$,\end{cases}

As
K
-modules,

(2.14)

\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})\cong\begin{cases}\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\chi)})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}&if $m=c(\chi)$,\\
\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}\ominus\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m-1})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}&if $m>c(\chi)$.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.15
.

We have that

\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})=\begin{cases}1&if $c(\chi)=m=0$,\\
q\frac{q^{n-1}-1}{q-1}&if $c(\chi)=0$ and $m=1$,\\
q^{(c(\chi)-1)(n-1)}\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}&if $c(\chi)=m\geq 1$,\\
q^{(m-2)(n-1)}\frac{(q^{n}-1)(q^{n-1}-1)}{q-1}&if $m>\max\{c(\chi),1\}$.\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows immediately from (
2.9
) and (
2.13
).
∎

The spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
are
K
-invariant; furthermore, any two subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{\chi_{1},m_{1}}(S^{n-1})
and
\mathcal{H}_{\chi_{1},m_{1}}(S^{n-1})
are mutually orthogonal whenever either
\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2}
or
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
. We claim that these subspaces are irreducible, which thereby completes the decomposition of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
into irreducible
K
-modules.

Theorem 2.16
.

For each character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
and for each integer
m\geq c(\chi)
, the
K
-module
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
is irreducible, and we have the orthogonal decompositions

(2.17)

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\ell=c(\chi)}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\chi,\ell}(S^{n-1}),

(2.18)

\displaystyle C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}\\
0\leq c(\chi)\leq m\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}).

For
n=2
, Casselman
[
Cas73b
, Proposition 1]
studies the decomposition into irreducible representations of
\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}\cong C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
; see also
[
BP17
, Section 3.3]
for the case
\chi=1
.

Let
\widehat{K}
denote the set of equivalence classes of irreducible smooth representations of
K
, and write
\tau_{\chi,m}
for the representation in
\widehat{K}
given by right translations on the finite-dimensional vector space
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. We have now classified precisely which representations in
\widehat{K}
have a
K_{n-1,1}
-fixed vector.

Corollary 2.19
.

For every irreducible smooth representation
\tau\in\widehat{K}
,

\dim\Hom_{K_{n-1,1}}\left(1,\tau|_{K_{n-1,1}}\right)=\begin{cases}1&if $\tau=\tau_{\chi,m}$ for some $\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}$ and $m\geq c(\chi)$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

In particular, the subspace

\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}\coloneqq\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}):f(xk^{\prime})=f(x)\text{ for all $x\in S^{n-1}$ and $k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}$}\right\}

of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant functions in
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
is one-dimensional.

Proof.

We observe that
\tau_{\chi_{1},m_{1}}
is isomorphic to
\tau_{\chi_{2},m_{2}}
if and only if
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}
and
m_{1}=m_{2}
by examining the dimensions and central characters of these representations. It follows that
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
is multiplicity-free, so that
(K_{n},K_{n-1,1})
is a Gelfand pair. The result then follows via (
2.18
) and Frobenius reciprocity.
∎

The proof of
Theorem
2.16
requires the following lemma.

Lemma 2.20
.

For each nonnegative integer
m
, we have the double coset decomposition

K=\bigsqcup_{\ell=0}^{m}K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\begin{pmatrix}1_{n-1}&0\\
\varpi^{\ell}e_{n-1}&1\end{pmatrix}K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}).

For
n=2
, this follows from
[
Cas73b
, Lemma 1]
and
[
Sch02
, Lemma 2.1.1]
, while the same result with
K_{n}=\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O})
replaced by
\SL_{n}(\mathcal{O})
is implicit in the work of Chang
[
Cha98
]
.

Proof.

Let
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K
with
a\in\Mat_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}(\mathcal{O})
,
b\in\Mat_{(n-1)\times 1}(\mathcal{O})
,
c\in\Mat_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathcal{O})
, and
d\in\mathcal{O}
. There are three cases to consider.

(1)

If
\max\{|c_{1}|,\ldots,|c_{n-1}|\}\leq q^{-m}
, then
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\begin{pmatrix}1_{n-1}&0\\
\varpi^{m}e_{n-1}&1\end{pmatrix}K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
.

(2)

If
\max\{|c_{1}|,\ldots,|c_{n-1}|\}=q^{-\ell}
for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}
, then
a\in K_{n-1}
; as
\varpi^{-\ell}ca^{-1}\in S^{n-2}
, there exists some
\alpha\in K_{n-1}
such that
e_{n-1}\alpha^{-1}=\varpi^{-\ell}ca^{-1}
, and we have that

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1_{n-1}&0\\
\varpi^{\ell}e_{n-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{-1}a&\alpha^{-1}b\\
0&-ca^{-1}b+d\end{pmatrix}.

(3)

Finally, if
\max\{|c_{1}|,\ldots,|c_{n-1}|\}=1
, then
c\in S^{n-2}
. By
[
Cha98
, Lemma 3]
, there exists
\beta\in\Mat_{(n-1)\times 1}(\mathcal{O})
such that
\det(a-\beta c)\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, so that
a-\beta c\in K_{n-1}
. As
c(a-\beta c)^{-1}\in S^{n-2}
, there exists some
\alpha\in K_{n-1}
such that
e_{n-1}\alpha^{-1}=c(a-\beta c)^{-1}
, and we have that

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1_{n-1}&0\\
e_{n-1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha^{-1}(a-\beta c)&\alpha^{-1}(b-\beta d)\\
0&-c(a-\beta c)^{-1}(b-\beta d)+d\end{pmatrix}.\qed

Proof of
Theorem
2.16
.

For
m\geq c(\chi)
, we identify
\End_{K}(\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi})
with the space of locally constant functions
\phi:K\to\mathbb{C}
that satisfy
\phi(k_{0}kk_{0}^{\prime})=\psi_{\chi}(k_{0})\psi_{\chi}(k_{0}^{\prime})\phi(k)
for all
k\in K
and
k_{0},k_{0}^{\prime}\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
. From
Lemma
2.20
, we deduce that for each integer
m\geq c(\chi)
,

\dim\End_{K}\left(\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}\right)=m-c(\chi)+1.

Since
\Ind_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}^{K}\psi_{\chi}\cong\bigoplus_{\ell=c(\chi)}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\chi,\ell}(S^{n-1})
from (
2.14
), we conclude that
\End_{K}(\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}))
is one-dimensional by induction, and hence that
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
is irreducible. Finally, the orthogonal decompositions (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) are clear via (
2.11
) and the fact that the union of the spaces
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is equal in
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
.
∎

Remark 2.21
.

The key input to the proof of
Theorem
2.16
is the fact that the double coset space
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\backslash K/K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
has cardinality
m+1
, which is a consequence of
Lemma
2.20
. We sketch below a more geometric proof of this fact. First, we note that this double coset space is also equal to
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\backslash K/K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
. Since these groups all contain
K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
as a normal subgroup, the cardinality of this double coset space is the same as that of

(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})/K(\mathfrak{p}^{m}))\backslash(K/K(\mathfrak{p}^{m}))/(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})/K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})),

which in turn is the same as that of

\mathrm{P}_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})\backslash\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})/\mathrm{P}_{(n-1,1)}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m}).

Here
\mathrm{P}_{n}
denotes the mirabolic subgroup (
1.2
) while
\mathrm{P}_{(n-1,1)}
denotes the standard maximal parabolic subgroup of type
(n-1,1)
. As in (
2.6
), we identify
\mathrm{P}_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})\backslash\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})
with
\overline{S}^{n-1}
. Since
K_{n-1}
acts transitively on
S^{n-2}
, we deduce that the action of
\mathrm{P}_{(n-1,1)}(\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{m})
on
\overline{S}^{n-1}
has
m+1
orbits, which are given by

\left\{\overline{x}=(\overline{x_{1}},\ldots,\overline{x_{n}})\in\overline{S}^{n-1}:\max\{|\overline{x_{1}}|,\ldots,|\overline{x_{n-1}}|\}=q^{-j}\right\}

for
j\in\{0,\ldots,m\}
.

3.
Zonal Spherical Functions

Let

C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}\coloneqq\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(S^{n-1}):f(xk^{\prime})=f(x)\text{ for all $x\in S^{n-1}$ and $k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}$}\right\}

denote the subspace of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant locally constant functions on the unit sphere. We identify precisely which elements of this lie in
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
for each character
\chi
of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
and nonnegative integer
m\geq c(\chi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The subspace
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}\cap C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
has dimension
m-c(\chi)+1
and is spanned by the functions

(3.2)

\phi_{\chi,\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\chi(x_{n})&if $\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}\leq q^{-\ell}$,\\
0&if $q^{-\ell}<\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}\leq 1$,\end{cases}

for
\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m\}
. Furthermore, for
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\geq c(\chi)
,

(3.3)

\left\langle\phi_{\chi,\ell_{1}},\phi_{\chi,\ell_{2}}\right\rangle=\begin{cases}1&if $\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=0$,\\
\frac{q-1}{q^{(\max\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}-1)(n-1)}(q^{n}-1)}&if $\max\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}\geq 1$.\end{cases}

Proof.

That the dimension of this subspace is
m-c(\chi)+1
is a direct consequence of (
2.17
) and
Corollary
2.19
. It is then straightforward to see that
\phi_{\chi,\ell}
is an element of this subspace for each
\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m\}
and that these are linearly independent. Finally, the identity (
3.3
) is immediate from the definition (
3.2
) of
\phi_{\chi,\ell}
, for this implies that

\left\langle\phi_{\chi,\ell_{1}},\phi_{\chi,\ell_{2}}\right\rangle=\vol(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{\max\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}}))=\frac{1}{\left[K:K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{\max\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\}})\right]},

which is precisely the right-hand side of (
3.3
).
∎

It is clear that
\phi_{\chi,c(\chi)}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,c(\chi)}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}
and that
\phi_{\chi,c(\chi)}(e_{n})=1
. We show that there exist similar elements
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}
for each
m\geq c(\chi)
, which must be a linear combination of the functions
\phi_{\chi,c(\chi)},\ldots,\phi_{\chi,m}
. We deduce the precise linear combination by making use of the fact that
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
is orthogonal to
P_{\chi,j}^{\circ}
whenever
j\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

For each
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
and
m\geq c(\chi)
, there exists a unique locally constant function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
satisfying
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(xk^{\prime})=P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(x)
for all
x\in S^{n-1}
and
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}
and
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
. This function is given by

(3.5)

P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\begin{cases}\chi(x_{n})&if $\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}\leq q^{-m}$,\\
\alpha_{\chi,m;m-1}\chi(x_{n})&if $m>c(\chi)$ and $\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}=q^{-m+1}$,\\
0&otherwise,\end{cases}

where

(3.6)

\alpha_{\chi,m;m-1}=\begin{cases}-\frac{q-1}{q(q^{n-1}-1)}&if $m=1$,\\
-\frac{1}{q^{n-1}-1}&if $m\geq 2$.\end{cases}

In particular, for all
k\in K
, we have that

(3.7)

P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(e_{n}k)=\overline{P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}}(e_{n}k^{-1}).

Definition 3.8
.

We call
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
the zonal spherical function on
S^{n-1}
of character
\chi
and level
m
.

Proof of
Proposition
3.4
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}
is one-dimensional from
Corollary
2.19
, there is a unique function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}
satisfying
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
. From (
3.2
), there exist constants
\alpha_{\chi,m;\ell}\in\mathbb{C}
for
\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m\}
such that

\displaystyle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=c(\chi)}^{m}\alpha_{\chi,m;\ell}\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\alpha_{\chi,m;m}\chi(x_{n})&if $\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}\leq q^{-m}$,\\
\alpha_{\chi,m;\ell}\chi(x_{n})&if $q^{-m}<\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}=q^{-\ell}\leq q^{-c(\chi)}$,\\
0&if $q^{-c(\chi)}<\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}\leq 1$,\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\phi_{\chi,\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-\phi_{\chi,\ell+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&for $\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}$,\\
\phi_{\chi,m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})&for $\ell=m$,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\chi(x_{n})&if $\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}$ and $\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}=q^{-\ell}$,\\
\chi(x_{n})&if $\ell=m$ and $\max\{|x_{1}|,\ldots,|x_{n-1}|\}\leq q^{-m}$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

Since
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
, we have that
\alpha_{\chi,m;m}=1
. To deduce properties of the remaining coefficients, we make use the fact that

(3.9)

\beta_{\chi,m;\ell}\coloneqq\left\langle\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell},\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell}\right\rangle=\begin{cases}\left\langle\phi_{\chi,\ell},\phi_{\chi,\ell}\right\rangle-\left\langle\phi_{\chi,\ell+1},\phi_{\chi,\ell+1}\right\rangle&if $\ell\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}$,\\
\left\langle\phi_{\chi,m},\phi_{\chi,m}\right\rangle&if $\ell=m$\\
\end{cases}

from (
3.3
), while

(3.10)

\left\langle\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell_{1}},\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell_{2}}\right\rangle=0\ \text{ if $\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}$}

since these have disjoint support. The zonal spherical function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
is orthogonal to
P_{\chi,j}^{\circ}
for all
j\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}
as
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
and
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,j}(S^{n-1})
are mutually orthogonal. Writing

P_{\chi,j}^{\circ}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{\ell=c(\chi)}^{j-1}\alpha_{\chi,j;\ell}\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+\sum_{\ell=j}^{m}\varphi_{\chi,m;\ell}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

we deduce from (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) that for each
j\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}
,

(3.11)

\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,j}^{\circ}\right\rangle=\sum_{\ell=c(\chi)}^{j-1}\alpha_{\chi,m;\ell}\overline{\alpha_{\chi,j;\ell}}\beta_{\chi,m;\ell}+\sum_{\ell=j}^{m}\alpha_{\chi,m;\ell}\beta_{\chi,m;\ell}=0.

Using (
3.11
), we shall prove by induction that

(3.12)

\alpha_{\chi,m;\ell}=\begin{cases}1&if $\ell=m$,\\
-\frac{\beta_{\chi,m;m}}{\beta_{\chi,m;m-1}}&if $m>c(\chi)$ and $\ell=m-1$,\\
0&otherwise,\end{cases}

which yields (
3.5
); the identity (
3.6
) then follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.9
). The base case of (
3.12
) is
m=c(\chi)
, so that
\ell=c(\chi)
, in which case we have that
\alpha_{\chi,c(\chi);c(\chi)}=1
since
P_{\chi,c(\chi)}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
. Now we suppose that (
3.12
) holds with
m
replaced by
j
for each
j\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-1\}
. From (
3.11
), we have by the induction hypothesis that for
m\geq c(\chi)+2
,

\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,c(\chi)}^{\circ}\right\rangle-\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,c(\chi)+1}^{\circ}\right\rangle=\alpha_{\chi,m;c(\chi)}\beta_{\chi,m;c(\chi)}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\chi,c(\chi)+1;c(\chi)+1}}{\beta_{\chi,c(\chi)+1;c(\chi)}}\right)=0.

Thus
\alpha_{\chi,m;c(\chi)}=0
. Proceeding inductively, we conclude that for all
j\in\{c(\chi),\ldots,m-2\}
,

\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,j}^{\circ}\right\rangle-\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,j+1}^{\circ}\right\rangle=\alpha_{\chi,m;j}\beta_{\chi,m;j}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{\chi,j+1;j+1}}{\beta_{\chi,j+1;j}}\right)=0,

so that
\alpha_{\chi,m;j}=0
. Finally, for
m\geq c(\chi)+1
, we have that

\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,m-1}^{\circ}\right\rangle=\alpha_{\chi,m;m-1}\beta_{\chi,m;m-1}+\beta_{\chi,m;m}=0,

which yields the remaining case
\ell=m-1
of (
3.12
).

It remains to prove (
3.7
). It suffices to show that for all
k\in K
,

(3.13)

\phi_{\chi,\ell}(e_{n}k)=\overline{\phi_{\chi,\ell}}(e_{n}k^{-1})

for each nonnegative integer
\ell\geq c(\chi)
, since
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
may be written as a linear combination of such functions. The identity (
3.13
) is clear if
k\notin K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
, for then both sides are zero. If
k\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
, so that
k^{-1}\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
as well, then
e_{n}k\prescript{t}{}{e}_{n}e_{n}k^{-1}\prescript{t}{}{e}_{n}-1\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
as
kk^{-1}=1_{n}
, and consequently
\chi(e_{n}k\prescript{t}{}{e}_{n})=\overline{\chi}(e_{n}k^{-1}\prescript{t}{}{e}_{n})
since
\ell\geq c(\chi)
, which yields the result.
∎

The zonal spherical function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
is useful for understanding various properties of the space
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. Our first application is the following lemma, which may be thought of as the addition theorem for
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\{Q_{j}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. Then for any
x\in S^{n-1}
and
k\in K
, we have that

(3.15)

\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}Q_{j}(x)\overline{Q_{j}}(e_{n}k)=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(xk^{-1}).

Proof.

Since

(3.16)

Q(xk)=(\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot Q)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot Q,Q_{j}\right\rangle Q_{j}(x)

for any
Q\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
and
x\in S^{n-1}
, we have that for any
k\in K
,

(3.17)

\begin{split}\delta_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}&=\left\langle Q_{\ell_{1}},Q_{\ell_{2}}\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot Q_{\ell_{1}},\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot Q_{\ell_{2}}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot Q_{\ell_{1}},Q_{j_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle Q_{j_{2}},\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot Q_{\ell_{2}}\right\rangle\left\langle Q_{j_{1}},Q_{j_{2}}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot Q_{\ell_{1}},Q_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle Q_{j},\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot Q_{\ell_{2}}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle Q_{\ell_{1}},\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot Q_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot Q_{j},Q_{\ell_{2}}\right\rangle.\end{split}

For fixed
k\in K
, we now define
\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
by

\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}Q_{j}(x)\overline{Q_{j}}(e_{n}k).

Then from (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we have that for all
k_{1}\in K
,

(3.18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}kk_{1}}(xk_{1})&=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}Q_{j}(xk_{1})\overline{Q_{j}}(e_{n}kk_{1})\\
&=\sum_{j,\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k_{1})\cdot Q_{j},Q_{\ell_{1}}\right\rangle\left\langle Q_{\ell_{2}},\tau_{\chi,m}(k_{1})\cdot Q_{j}\right\rangle Q_{\ell_{1}}(x)\overline{Q_{\ell_{2}}}(e_{n}k)\\
&=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}Q_{\ell}(x)\overline{Q_{\ell}}(e_{n}k)\\
&=\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(x).\end{split}

We use (
3.18
) with
x
replaced by
xk
and
k_{1}
replaced by
k^{-1}k^{\prime}k
for
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}
. Since
e_{n}k^{\prime}=e_{n}
, we deduce that

\left(\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{\prime})\cdot\left(\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}\right)\right)(x)=\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(xk^{\prime}k)=\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(xk)=\left(\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}\right)(x)

for all
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}
. As
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})^{K_{n-1,1}}
is one-dimensional, it follows that
\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}
must be a constant multiple of
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
, and this constant is readily seen to be
\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(e_{n}k)
upon taking
x=e_{n}
. So for all
x\in S^{n-1}
and
k\in K
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}Q_{j}(x)\overline{Q_{j}}(e_{n}k)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}\right)(xk^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{e_{n}k}(e_{n}k)P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(xk^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left|Q_{j}(e_{n}k)\right|^{2}P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(xk^{-1}).

It remains to show that for all
k\in K
,

(3.19)

\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left|Q_{j}(e_{n}k)\right|^{2}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}).

To prove (
3.19
), we take
x=e_{n}k
and
k_{1}=k^{-1}
in (
3.18
) in order to see that

\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left|Q_{j}(e_{n}k)\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left|Q_{j}(e_{n})\right|^{2}

for all
k\in K
and hence the left-hand side is a constant. Integrating over
K\ni k
, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}|Q_{j}(e_{n}k)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}|Q_{j}(e_{n}k)|^{2}\,dk

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle Q_{j},Q_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}).\qed

A simple consequence of
Lemma
3.14
is the following result showing that certain matrix coefficients of
\tau_{\chi,m}
are themselves elements of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
.

Corollary 3.20
.

The reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
is
(\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1}))\tau_{\chi,m}(k^{-1})\cdot P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
, so that for all
P\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
and

,

(3.21)

P(e_{n}k)=\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})\left\langle\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot P,P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}\right\rangle.

In particular,

(3.22)

\left\langle P_{\chi,m}^{\circ},P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}.

One can also show (
3.22
) in a more direct fashion by combining (
3.3
), (
3.5
), and (
3.6
).

Proof.

From (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we have that

\displaystyle P(e_{n}k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}\left\langle P,Q_{j}\right\rangle Q_{j}(e_{n}k)

\displaystyle=\left\langle P,\sum_{j=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})}Q_{j}\overline{Q_{j}}(e_{n}k)\right\rangle

The identity (

4.1.
The Newform and the Conductor Exponent

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\GL_{n}(F)
. Thus there exist positive integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
for which
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}=n
and essentially square-integrable representations
(\pi_{j},V_{\pi_{j}})
of
\GL_{n_{j}}(F)
of the form
\sigma_{j}\otimes\left|\det\right|^{t_{j}}
, where
\sigma_{j}
is square-integrable and
t_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
\Re(t_{1})\geq\cdots\geq\Re(t_{r})
, such that

\pi=\Ind_{\mathrm{P}(F)}^{\GL_{n}(F)}\mathop{\vphantom{\bigotimes}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxtimes$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxtimes$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxtimes$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxtimes$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}\pi_{j},

the representation obtained by normalised parabolic induction from the standard upper parabolic subgroup
\mathrm{P}(F)=\mathrm{P}_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(F)
of
\GL_{n}(F)
. One can take as a model for
\pi
the space of smooth functions
f:\GL_{n}(F)\to V_{\pi_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{\pi_{r}}
, upon which
\pi
acts via right translations, that satisfy

f(umg)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left|\det m_{j}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}(n-2(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j-1})-n_{j})}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}\pi_{j}(m_{j})\cdot f(g)

for all
u\in\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)
, the unipotent radical of
\mathrm{P}(F)
,
m=\blockdiag(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)
, the Levi subgroup of
\mathrm{P}(F)
, and
g\in\GL_{n}(F)
. We note that every generic irreducible admissible smooth representation of
\GL_{n}(F)
is isomorphic to some induced representation of Langlands type; see, for example,
[
JS83
]
.

For each nonnegative integer
m
and each character
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
for which
0\leq c(\chi)\leq m
, we define the subspaces

\displaystyle V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{v\in V_{\pi}:\pi(k)\cdot v=v\text{ for all }k\in K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right\},

\displaystyle V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{v\in V_{\pi}:\pi(k)\cdot v=\psi_{\chi}(k)v\text{ for all }k\in K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})\right\},

where
\psi_{\chi}
denotes the character of
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
corresponding to
\chi
. The former is the subspace of
V_{\pi}
of
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
-invariant vectors; the latter is the subspace of
(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\psi_{\chi})
-equivariant vectors.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\GL_{n}(F)
. For each nonnegative integer
m
, the subspace
V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is the image of the projection map
\Pi^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}:V_{\pi}\to V_{\pi}
given by

(4.2)

\Pi^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}(v)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\vol(K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}))}\int_{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}\pi(k)\cdot v\,dk,

while
V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}
is the image of the projection map
\Pi^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}:V_{\pi}\to V_{\pi}
given by

(4.3)

\Pi^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}(v)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\int_{K}\pi(k)\cdot v\,dk&if $m=c(\chi)=0$,\\
\frac{1}{\vol(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}))}\int_{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}\overline{\chi}(e_{n}k\prescript{t}{}{e}_{n})\pi(k)\cdot v\,dk&if $m>0$.\end{cases}

Finally, we have that

(4.4)

V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}=\begin{cases}V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}&if $\chi=\chi_{\pi}$,\\
\{0\}&otherwise,\end{cases}

where
\chi_{\pi}\coloneqq\omega_{\pi}|_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
with
\omega_{\pi}:F^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
the central character of
\pi
.

Proof.

The two identities
\Pi^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}(V_{\pi})=V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
and
\Pi^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}(V_{\pi})=V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}
are clear. Since
\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})=K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
and
\pi|_{\mathrm{Z}(\mathcal{O})}=\omega_{\pi}|_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}=\chi_{\pi}
, the identities
V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi_{\pi}}=V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
and
V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi}=\{0\}
for
\chi\neq\chi_{\pi}
then follow.
∎

A fundamental result concerning induced representations of Langlands type is the existence of a newform. Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
JP-SS81
]
have shown that each induced representation of Langlands type
(\pi,V_{\pi})
of
\GL_{n}(F)
contains a distinguished vector
v^{\circ}\in V_{\pi}
, the newform, whose complexity is measured in a natural way by a nonnegative integer
c(\pi)
, the conductor exponent. This generalises a result of Casselman
[
Cas73a
]
, who proved this for
n=2
, and observed that when
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\pi
is the local component of an automorphic representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, this is the adèlic reformulation of the Atkin–Lehner theory of newforms for classical modular forms
[
AL70
]
.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
JP-SS81
, Théorème (5)]
)
.

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\GL_{n}(F)
. There exists a minimal nonnegative integer
m=c(\pi)
for which the space
V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi_{\pi}}
is nontrivial, in which case it is one-dimensional.

Definition 4.6
.

The nonzero vector
v^{\circ}\in V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})}=V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)}),\chi_{\pi}}
, unique up to scalar multiplication, is called the newform of
\pi
. The nonnegative integer
c(\pi)
is called the conductor exponent of
\pi
. Elements of
V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi_{\pi}}
for
m>c(\pi)
are called oldforms.

Remark 4.7
.

The proof of
[
JP-SS81
, Théorème (5)]
contains a gap; correct proofs were later independently given by Jacquet
[
Jac12
, Theorem 1]
and Matringe
[
Mat13
, Corollary 3.3]
.

Remark 4.8
.

As proven in
[
JP-SS81
]
, there are other ways to characterise the newform and the conductor exponent of
\pi
instead of in terms of the subspace
V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})}
of
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})
-invariant vectors in
V_{\pi}
. The conductor exponent
c(\pi)
is precisely the nonnegative integer for which the epsilon factor
\varepsilon(s,\pi,\psi)
associated to
\pi
is of the form

\varepsilon(s,\pi,\psi)=\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{2},\pi,\psi\right)q^{-c(\pi)\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)},

where
\psi:F\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is an unramified additive character of
F
. The newform is such that when viewed in the Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
of
\pi
, it is the unique Whittaker function
W^{\circ}
satisfying
W^{\circ}\left(g\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=W^{\circ}(g)
for all
g\in\GL_{n}(F)
and
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1}
that is a test vector for the local
\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n-1}
Rankin–Selberg integral whenever the second representation is unramified, so that

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\GL_{n-1}(F)}W^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W^{\prime\circ}(g)\left|\det g\right|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,dg=L(s,\pi\times\pi^{\prime})

for all spherical induced representations of Langlands type
\pi^{\prime}
of
\GL_{n-1}(F)
with spherical Whittaker function
W^{\prime\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi^{\prime},\overline{\psi})
normalised such that
W^{\prime\circ}(1_{n-1})=1

[
JP-SS81
, Théorème (4)]
(see additionally
[
Jac12
]
and
[
Mat13
, Corollary 3.3]
).

4.2.
The Newform
K
-Type

We shall show that the newform lies in a distinguished
K
-type of
\pi
. To begin, for each irreducible smooth representation
\tau\in\widehat{K}
, we define the projection map
\Pi^{\tau}:V_{\pi}\to V_{\pi}
by

\Pi^{\tau}(v)\coloneqq\int_{K}\xi^{\tau}(k)\pi(k)\cdot v\,dk,

where

\xi^{\tau}(k)\coloneqq(\dim\tau)\Tr\tau(k^{-1})

is the elementary idempotent associated to
\tau
. The image of
V_{\pi}
under
\Pi^{\tau}
is the
\tau
-isotypic subspace
V_{\pi}^{\tau}
of
V_{\pi}
, which is finite-dimensional since
\pi
is admissible. We say that
\tau
is a
K
-type of
\pi
if
\Hom_{K}(\tau,\pi|_{K})
is nontrivial, in which case
\dim V_{\pi}^{\tau}=\dim\tau\dim\Hom_{K}(\tau,\pi|_{K})>0
, and we call
\dim\Hom_{K}(\tau,\pi|_{K})
the multiplicity of
\tau
in
\pi
.

In general, the
K
-type decomposition of an induced representation of Langlands type
\pi
is not known except in special cases. For
n=2
, this follows from work of Casselman
[
Cas73b
]
, Silberger
[
Sil70
]
, and Hansen
[
Han87
]
; for
n=3
and
\pi
a principal series representation, this problem has been studied by Campbell and Nevins
[
CN09
,
CN10
]
and Onn and Singla
[
OS14
]
.

We do not attempt to determine the full
K
-type decomposition of
\pi
, which is undoubtedly challenging, since no explicit description of
\widehat{K}
currently exists for
n>2
. Rather, we study the multiplicity with which particular representations
\tau\in\widehat{K}
appear in the
K
-type decomposition of
\pi
, namely those representations containing a
K_{n-1,1}
-fixed vector.

To do so, we define the projection map
\Pi^{K_{n-1,1}}:V_{\pi}\to V_{\pi}
given by

\Pi^{K_{n-1,1}}(v)\coloneqq\int_{K_{n-1,1}}\pi(k^{\prime})\cdot v\,dk^{\prime},

where
dk^{\prime}
denotes the Haar probability measure on the compact group
K_{n-1,1}
, so that the image of
\Pi^{K_{n-1,1}}
is the subspace of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant vectors. The composition of the two projections
\Pi^{\tau}
and
\Pi^{K_{n-1,1}}
is the projection

(4.9)

\left(\Pi^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}\right)(v)\coloneqq\left(\Pi^{K_{n-1,1}}\circ\Pi^{\tau}\right)(v)=\left(\Pi^{\tau}\circ\Pi^{K_{n-1,1}}\right)(v)=\int_{K}\xi^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}(k)\pi(k)\cdot v\,dk

onto the subspace of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant
\tau
-isotypic vectors

V_{\pi}^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}\coloneqq\left(\Pi^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}\right)(V_{\pi})=\left\{v\in V_{\pi}^{\tau}:\pi(k^{\prime})\cdot v=v\text{ for all $k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1,1}$}\right\}.

Here

(4.10)

\xi^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}(k)\coloneqq\int_{K_{n-1,1}}\xi^{\tau}(k^{\prime}k)\,dk^{\prime}=\int_{K_{n-1,1}}\xi^{\tau}(kk^{\prime})\,dk^{\prime}.

Finally, for any nonnegative integer
m
, we set

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(m)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\widehat{K}\\
c(\tau)=m\end{subarray}}V_{\pi}^{\tau},

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\widehat{K}\\
c(\tau)=m\end{subarray}}V_{\pi}^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}.

Here the level
c(\tau)
of
\tau
is defined to be the minimal nonnegative integer
m
for which the kernel of
\tau
contains
K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
, which is necessarily finite since
\tau
is smooth. Thus
V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}
is the subspace of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant vectors that are linear combinations of
\tau
-isotypic vectors for some
\tau\in\widehat{K}
of level
c(\tau)=m
. We now show that the newform and the conductor exponent may be characterised in terms of
V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}
.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\GL_{n}(F)
. For any nonnegative integer
m
, we have that

(4.12)

\dim V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}=\begin{cases}\binom{m-c(\pi)+n-2}{n-2}&if $m\geq c(\pi)$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

In particular, the minimal nonnegative integer
m
for which
V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}
is nontrivial is
m=c(\pi)
. Furthermore, we have that

(4.13)

V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}=\begin{cases}V_{\pi}^{\tau_{\chi_{\pi},m},K_{n-1,1}}&if $m\geq c(\pi)$,\\
\{0\}&otherwise,\end{cases}

and that

(4.14)

V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}=V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m}),\chi_{\pi}}=\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{m}V_{\pi}(\ell)^{K_{n-1,1}}=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{\ell=c(\pi)}^{m}V_{\pi}^{\tau_{\chi_{\pi},\ell},K_{n-1,1}}&if $m\geq c(\pi)$,\\
\{0\}&otherwise.\end{cases}

In particular,

(4.15)

V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})}=V_{\pi}^{K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)}),\chi_{\pi}}=V_{\pi}(c(\pi))^{K_{n-1,1}}=V_{\pi}^{\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)},K_{n-1,1}}.

Definition 4.16
.

We call
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
the newform
K
-type of
\pi
.

Remark 4.17
.

This gives alternative characterisations of the newform and conductor exponent of
\pi
: we may
define
the conductor exponent
c(\pi)
of
\pi
to be the minimal nonnegative integer
m
for which the space
V_{\pi}(m)^{K_{n-1,1}}=V_{\pi}^{\tau_{\chi_{\pi},m},K_{n-1,1}}
is nontrivial, while we may
define
the newform to be the nonzero vector, unique up to scalar multiplication, lying in
V_{\pi}(c(\pi))^{K_{n-1,1}}=V_{\pi}^{\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)},K_{n-1,1}}
. Of course, this definition is somewhat circular in practice, since, as we shall shortly see, we use
Theorem
4.5
(or rather a consequence thereof) in order to prove
Theorem
4.11
.

Theorem
4.11
gives additional information about the newform not immediately apparent from
Theorem
4.5
: not only is the newform the unique vector, up to scalar multiplication, that is
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})
-invariant (or, equivalently,
(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)}),\psi_{\chi_{\pi}})
-equivariant), it is also the unique vector, up to scalar multiplication, that is
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant and
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
-isotypic; moreover, there are no nontrivial
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant
\tau_{\chi,m}
-isotypic vectors with
m<c(\pi)
or
\chi\neq\chi_{\pi}
. We have also shown that the space
V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
of oldforms of level
m\geq c(\pi)
decomposes into the direct sum of the subspaces of
K_{n-1,1}
-invariant
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},\ell}
-isotypic vectors in
V_{\pi}
with
\ell\in\{c(\pi),\ldots,m\}
, each of which has dimension
\binom{\ell-c(\pi)+n-2}{n-2}
respectively.

Before we prove
Theorem
4.11
, we return to the projection
\Pi^{\tau,K_{n-1,1}}
defined in (
4.9
) in terms of integration against the function

Proposition 4.29

Let

For nonzero

P(x)=\left(\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\right)^{2}\langle P,P\rangle\int_{K}\frac{\left\langle\pi(k^{-1})\cdot v,v^{\circ}\right\rangle}{\langle v,v\rangle}\left(\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}(k)\cdot P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}\right)(x)\,dk.

Proof.

5.
Archimedean Analogues

5.1.
Archimedean Spherical Harmonics

The archimedean analogues of the results in
Sections
2
and
3
are well-known; we briefly survey them for the sake of comparison. In place of a nonarchimedean field
F
, we instead work with an archimedean field, which is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
.

5.1.1.
F=\mathbb{R}

The maximal compact subgroup
K_{n}
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is the orthogonal group
\Ogp(n)
. This acts transitively on unit sphere

S^{n-1}\coloneqq\left\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}=1\right\}

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via the group action
k\cdot x\coloneqq xk
for
k\in K_{n}
and
x\in S^{n-1}
. The stabiliser subgroup of
K_{n}
with respect to the point
e_{n}\coloneqq(0,\ldots,0,1)\in S^{n-1}
is

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in K_{n}:k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1}\right\},

which we freely identify with
K_{n-1}
. It follows that
S^{n-1}\cong K_{n-1}\backslash K_{n}
.

Note that the subgroup (
5.1
) does not, at first glance, appear to be the direct archimedean analogue of the subgroup
K_{n-1,1}
as in (
1.1
), since
K_{n-1,1}\cong K_{n-1}\ltimes\mathcal{O}^{n-1}
. In the archimedean setting, on the other hand, there is no analogue of
\mathcal{O}^{n-1}
; nonetheless, the subgroup (
5.1
), like the nonarchimedean subgroup (
1.1
), is the maximal compact subgroup of the mirabolic subgroup (
1.2
).

The decomposition of the right regular representation of
K_{n}
on
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
is precisely the theory of spherical harmonics. This is best understood in terms of homogeneous harmonic polynomials, for which we follow
[
AH12
, Chapter 2]
.

Given a nonnegative integer
m
, let
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the vector space consisting of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
m
, namely the set of polynomials
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that are annihilated by the Laplacian

\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}

and satisfy
P(\lambda x_{1},\ldots,\lambda x_{n})=\lambda^{m}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. This space has dimension
1
for
n=1
and
m\in\{0,1\}
and has dimension

\binom{m+n-2}{n-2}+\binom{m+n-3}{n-3}=\frac{(2m+n-2)(m+n-3)!}{m!(n-2)!}=\frac{2m+n-2}{m+n-2}\binom{m+n-2}{n-2}

for
n\geq 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
denote the vector space of the restriction of elements of
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the unit sphere; via the homogeneity of elements of
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, these spaces are isomorphic. Elements of
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
are called spherical harmonics of degree
m
. The group
K_{n}
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
via right translation, which descends to an action on
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
. As a
K_{n}
-module,
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
is irreducible, and for
n\geq 2
, we have the orthogonal decomposition

C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1}),

analogous to the decomposition (
2.18
) of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
.

We may view
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
as the real analogue of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. In turn, the real analogue of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is
\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(S^{n-1})
, the space of spherical harmonics of degree at most
m
. As
K_{1}=\Ogp(1)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, which is the real analogue of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, characters of
K_{1}
are of the form
\sgn^{\kappa}
for
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
, where
\sgn(x)\coloneqq x/|x|
. In particular, the central character
\chi
of the
K_{n}
-module
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
is simply
\sgn^{m\pmod{2}}
, which is determined by the parity of the nonnegative integer
m
. So the real analogue of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
, in terms of its orthogonal decomposition (
2.17
), is precisely

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv\kappa\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(S^{n-1})

for
\chi=\sgn^{\kappa}
with
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
and
m\geq\kappa
, while the analogue the orthogonal decomposition (
2.11
) of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is simply

\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(S^{n-1})=\bigoplus_{\kappa\in\{0,1\}}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv\kappa\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{m}\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(S^{n-1}).

There exists a unique spherical harmonic
P_{m}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
satisfying
P_{m}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
and
P_{m}^{\circ}(xk)=P_{m}^{\circ}(x)
for all
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in S^{n-1}
and
k=\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in K_{n}
with
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1}
, namely

P_{m}^{\circ}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu=0\\
\nu\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{i^{\nu}m!\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{2^{\nu}\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)!(m-\nu)!\Gamma\left(\frac{\nu+n-1}{2}\right)}\left(x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n-1}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\nu}{2}}x_{n}^{m-\nu}.

This is the zonal spherical harmonic on
S^{n-1}
of degree
m
, which is the real analogue of the zonal spherical function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. From this, the addition theorem for
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
takes precisely the same form as the nonarchimedean result given in
Lemma
3.14
; similarly,
(\dim\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1}))P_{m}^{\circ}
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
, akin to
Corollary
3.20
.

5.1.2.
F=\mathbb{C}

The maximal compact subgroup
K_{n}
of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the unitary group
\Ugp(n)
. This acts transitively on unit sphere

S^{n-1}\coloneqq\left\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:z_{1}\overline{z_{1}}+\cdots+z_{n}\overline{z_{n}}=1\right\}

in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
via the group action
k\cdot x\coloneqq xk
for
k\in K_{n}
and
x\in S^{n-1}
. (It behoves us to point out that as a real topological manifold, this should be viewed as the
(2n-1)
-dimensional unit sphere, but we use the notation
S^{n-1}
for the sake of consistency.) Just as for the real case, the stabiliser subgroup of
K_{n}
with respect to the point
e_{n}\coloneqq(0,\ldots,0,1)\in S^{n-1}
is the subgroup (
5.1
), which we freely identify with
K_{n-1}
, so that
S^{n-1}\cong K_{n-1}\backslash K_{n}
.

The decomposition of the right regular representation of
K_{n}
on
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
is again the theory of spherical harmonics, with the additional complexification that one must consider the
bidegree
of such a spherical harmonic. This is best understood in terms of homogeneous harmonic polynomials, for which we follow
[
Rud08
, Chapter 12]
.

Given a pair of nonnegative integers
m_{1},m_{2}
, let
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote the vector space consisting of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of bidegree
(m_{1},m_{2})
, namely the set of polynomials
P(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},\overline{z_{1}},\ldots,\overline{z_{n}})
in
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
that are annihilated by the Laplacian

\Delta=4\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{j}}}

and satisfy
P(\lambda z_{1},\ldots,\lambda z_{n},\overline{\lambda z_{1}},\ldots,\overline{\lambda z_{n}})=\lambda^{m_{1}}\overline{\lambda}^{m_{2}}P(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},\overline{z_{1}},\ldots,\overline{z_{n}})
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. This space has dimension
1
for
n=1
and dimension

\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2}+n-1)(m_{1}+n-2)!(m_{2}+n-2)!}{m_{1}!m_{2}!(n-2)!(n-1)!}=\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}+n-1}{n-1}\binom{m_{1}+n-2}{n-2}\binom{m_{2}+n-2}{n-2}

for
n\geq 2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
denote the vector space of the restriction of elements of
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
to the unit sphere; via the homogeneity of elements of
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, these spaces are isomorphic. Elements of
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
are called spherical harmonics of bidegree
(m_{1},m_{2})
. The group
K_{n}
acts on
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
via right translation, which descends to an action on
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
. As a
K_{n}
-module,
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
is irreducible, and for
n\geq 2
, we have the orthogonal decomposition

C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})=\bigoplus_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1}),

analogous to the decomposition (
2.18
) of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})
.

We may view
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
as the complex analogue of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. In turn, the complex analogue of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
is
\bigoplus_{m_{1}+m_{2}\leq m}\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
, the space of spherical harmonics of total degree at most
m
. As
K_{1}=\Ugp(1)\cong\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, which is the complex analogue of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
, characters of
K_{1}
are of the form
e^{i\kappa\arg}
for
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
e^{i\arg(z)}\coloneqq z^{1/2}\overline{z}^{-1/2}
. In particular, the central character
\chi
of the
K_{n}
-module
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
is
e^{i(m_{1}-m_{2})\arg}
, which is determined by the difference of
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
. So the complex analogue of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})_{\chi}^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
, in terms of its orthogonal decomposition (
2.17
), is precisely

\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+m_{2}\leq m\\
m_{1}-m_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})

for
\chi=e^{i\ell\arg}
with
m\geq|\ell|
, while the orthogonal decomposition (
2.11
) of
C^{\infty}(S^{n-1})^{K(\mathfrak{p}^{m})}
has the complex analogue

\bigoplus_{m_{1}+m_{2}\leq m}\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})=\bigoplus_{|\ell|\leq m}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+m_{2}\leq m\\
m_{1}-m_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1}).

There exists a unique spherical harmonic
P_{m}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
satisfying
P_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\circ}(e_{n},e_{n})=1
and
P_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\circ}(zk,\overline{zk})=P_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\circ}(z,\overline{z})
for all
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in S^{n-1}
and
k=\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in K_{n}
with
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1}
, namely

P_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{\circ}(z,\overline{z})\coloneqq\sum_{\nu=0}^{\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}\binom{m_{1}}{\nu}\binom{m_{2}}{\nu}}{\binom{\nu+n-2}{n-2}}\left(z_{1}\overline{z_{1}}+\cdots+z_{n-1}\overline{z_{n-1}}\right)^{\nu}z_{n}^{m_{1}-\nu}\overline{z_{n}}^{m_{2}-\nu}.

This is the zonal spherical harmonic on
S^{n-1}
of bidegree
(m_{1},m_{2})
, which is the complex analogue of the zonal spherical function
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(S^{n-1})
. Once more, the addition theorem for
\mathcal{H}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(S^{n-1})
takes the same form as
Lemma
3.14
and
(\dim\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1}))P_{m}^{\circ}
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}_{m}(S^{n-1})
.

5.2.
Archimedean Newform Theory for
\GL_{n}

Finally, we consider the archimedean analogues of the results in
Section
4
; these are due to Popa
[
Pop08
]
for
\GL_{2}
and to the author
[
Hum20
]
for
\GL_{n}
with
n
arbitrary.

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\GL_{n}(F)
, where
F
is an archimedean local field, so that
F
is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. There is no obvious analogue in the archimedean setting of the nonarchimedean congruence subgroups
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
and
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{m})
of
K_{n}
(though cf.
[
JN19
]
). This prevents one from defining the newform and conductor exponent of
\pi
via the subspace
V_{\pi}^{K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})}
of
K_{1}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)})
-invariant vectors as in
Definition
4.6
.

As highlighted in
Remark
4.8
, one can instead characterise the nonarchimedean newform, when viewed in the Whittaker model, as the unique Whittaker function
W^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
that is both right
K_{n-1}
-invariant, with
K_{n-1}
embedded in
K_{n}
as the subgroup (
5.1
), and is a test vector for the local
\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n-1}
Rankin–Selberg integral whenever the second representation is unramified. It is shown in
[
Hum20
, Theorem 4.17]
that this characterisation does indeed also hold in the archimedean setting, in that there is a unique such Whittaker function satisfying these two conditions. One would also like to show that the conductor exponent is characterised via the epsilon factor
\varepsilon(s,\pi,\psi)
; while this is the case when
F
is nonarchimedean, this is unfortunately insufficient when
F
is archimedean, for then
\varepsilon(s,\pi,\psi)
is simply an integral power of
i
, and this integer is only determined modulo
4
.

Nonetheless, the archimedean analogue of
Theorem
4.11
holds; more precisely, the archimedean analogues of (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) are true via
[
Hum20
, Theorems 4.7 and 4.12]
. Here the nonarchimedean subgroup
K_{n-1,1}
of
K_{n}
is replaced by the archimedean subgroup (
5.1
), which both share the property that they are the stabiliser subgroup of
K_{n}
with respect to the point
e_{n}\in F^{n}
; moreover, the notion of ordering
K_{n}
-types by their
level
in the nonarchimedean setting is replaced by ordering
K_{n}
-types by their
Howe degree
in the archimedean setting (cf.
[
Hum20
, Section 4.2]
).

In
[
Hum20
, Definition 4.8]
, the author has
defined
the conductor exponent
c(\pi)
of an induced representation of Langlands type
(\pi,V_{\pi})
of
\GL_{n}
over an archimedean field
F
to be the minimal nonnegative integer
m
for which
\pi
contains a
K_{n}
-type
\tau
of Howe degree
m
having a nontrivial
K_{n-1}
-fixed vector, with
K_{n-1}
embedded in
K_{n}
as the subgroup (
5.1
). This distinguished
K_{n}
-type
\tau=\tau^{\circ}
is the newform
K_{n}
-type and appears with multiplicity one in
\pi
. The author has also
defined
the newform in the archimedean setting to be the nonzero vector in
V_{\pi}
, unique up to scalar multiplication, that is invariant under the subgroup (
5.1
) and is
\tau^{\circ}
-isotypic.

We observed in
Remark
4.17
that
Theorem
4.11
gives alternative characterisations of the newform and conductor exponent in the nonarchimedean setting in terms of
K_{n}
-types. In conjunction with
[
Hum20
, Theorem 4.7]
, this characterisation thereby unifies the nonarchimedean and archimedean treatments of the newform and the conductor exponent.

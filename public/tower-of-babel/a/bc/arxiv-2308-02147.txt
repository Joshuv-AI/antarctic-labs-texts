Bi- g g -frame and characterizations of bi- g g -frame and Riesz basis
Sayyed Mehrab Ramezani

Abstract
In this paper, we define the concept of the bi- g g -frame and then show some properties of the bi- g g -frame. Similarly to bi- g g -frame, we can define Bi- g g -Bessel sequences, tight bi- g g -frame, and the Parseval bi- g g -frame. Moreover, we define the bi- g g -frame operator. Finally we present characterizations of bi- g g -frame and Riesz basis.



1
Introduction and preliminaries

Frames for Hilbert space were formally defined by Duffin and Schaeffer
[
5
]
in 1952 while studying some problems in non-harmonic Fourier series.
Recall that for a Hilbert space
{H}
and a countable index set
J
,
a collection
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subset{H}
is called a frame for the Hilbert space

{H}
if there exist two positive constants
a
,
b
such that for all
f\in{H}

a\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in J}|\langle f,f_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq b\|f\|^{2};

(1)

a
and
b
are called the lower and upper frame bounds, respectively.
If only the right-hand inequality in (
1
) is satisfied,
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is called a Bessel sequence for
{H}
with Bessel bound
b
.
The bounded linear operator
T
is defined by

T:\ell^{2}(J)\longrightarrow{H},\hskip 28.45274pt{T}\{c_{j}\}_{j\in J}=\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}f_{j},

which is called the synthesis operator of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
Moreover,
{T}^{*}f=\{\langle f,f_{j}\rangle\}_{j\in J}
for all
f\in{H}
. The map
T^{*}
is called the analysis operator of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
The bounded linear operator
S

is also defined by

S=TT^{*}:H\longrightarrow H,\hskip 28.45274ptS(f)=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle f_{j},

which is called the frame operator of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
For more information about
the frames see
[
3
]
.

Two Bessel sequences
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in J}
are said to be duals for
{H}
if the following equalities hold

f=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle g_{j}=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,g_{j}\rangle f_{j},\ \text{for all f}\in{H.}

Note that because
S:H\longrightarrow H
by
S(f)=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle f_{j}
is bijective, self-adjoint and

f=S(S^{-1}f)=\sum_{j\in J}\langle S^{-1}f,f_{j}\rangle f_{j}=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,S^{-1}f_{j}\rangle f_{j},

hence the sequence
\{S^{-1}f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is also a frame by Corollary
1.1.3

[
3
]
and it is called the
canonical dual of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
Dual frames are important in reconstructing vectors (or signals) in terms of the
frame elements.
Wenchang Sun
[
7
]
has provided characterizations of
g
-frames and has proved that
g
-frames share many useful
properties with frames. Ramezani and Nazari
[
6
]
have gave equivalent conditions for a
g
-orthonormal basis and characterize all
g
-Riesz basis for a separable Hilbert, starting with a given
g
-orthonormal basis. Assuming that

\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
are two Hilbert spaces and
\{\mathcal{K}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a sequence of closed Hilbert subspaces of
\mathcal{K}
. For each
j\in J,

\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K}_{j}\right)
is the collection of all bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}_{j}
.
A sequence
\{\Lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H},{\mathcal{K}}_{j}),\ {j\in J}\}
is called a generalized frame, or simply a

g
-frame, for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\{{\mathcal{K}}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
if there are two positive constants
A
and
B
such that

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in J}\|\Lambda_{j}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2},\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in\mathcal{H}).

(2)

A
and
B
are called the lower and upper
g
-frame bounds, respectively.
If only the right-hand inequality in (
2
) is satisfied,
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is called a
g
-Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}
with respect to
\{{\mathcal{K}}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
with
g
-Bessel bound
B
.
The bounded linear operator
T
is defined by

T:\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{K}_{j}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H},\hskip 28.45274pt{T}\{g_{j}\}_{j\in J}=\sum_{j\in J}\Lambda_{j}^{*}g_{j},

which is called the synthesis operator of
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
Moreover,
{T}^{*}f=\{\langle\Lambda_{j}f\rangle\}_{j\in J}
. The map
T^{*}
is called the analysis operator of
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
The bounded linear operator
S_{\Lambda}

is also defined by

S_{\Lambda}=TT^{*}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H},\hskip 28.45274ptS_{\Lambda}(f)=\sum_{j\in J}\Lambda_{j}^{*}\Lambda_{j}f,

which is called the
g
-frame operator of
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.

Weighted and controlled frames have been introduced recently to improve
the numerical efficiency of iterative algorithms for inverting the frame operator
on abstract Hilbert spaces
[
1
]
. By decreasing the ratio of the frame bounds, weighting improves the numerical efficiency of iterative algorithms, such as the ”frame algorithm”
[
3
]
for the inversion of the frame operator.
However, they have been employed earlier in
[
2
]
for spherical
wavelets.
Let
GL(H)
be the set of all the bounded operators with a bounded inverse. A frame controlled by the operator
C
or
C
-controlled frame is a family of
vectors
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subseteq{H}
, such that there exist two constants
A_{c}>0
and
B_{c}<\infty
,
satisfying

A_{c}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle\langle Cf_{j},f\rangle\leq B_{c}\|f\|^{2};

(3)

for every
f\in{H}
, where
C\in GL({H})
. Every frame is an
I
-controlled frame. Hence
the controlled frames are generalizations of frames.
The controlled frame operator

S_{c}
is defined by

S_{c}f=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle Cf_{j}=CS,\hskip 28.45274pt(f\in{H}),

(4)

where
S
is the frame operator of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
.
The synthesis operator for a
C
-controlled frame
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is defined as follows

T_{c}(\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j\in J})=\sum_{j\in J}\alpha_{j}Cf_{j}=CT,

where
T
is the synthesis operator of
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and

S_{c}=T_{c}T^{*}
.

C
-Controlled frame
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and Bessel sequences
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in J}
are said to be the
C
-controlled duals for
{H}
if the following equality holds.

f=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,g_{j}\rangle Cf_{j},\ \text{for all f}\in{H.}

M. Firouzi Parizi, A. Alijani M. A. Dehghan
[
4
]
have defined
the concept of biframe that is proposed as a generalization of controlled frames and a special case of pair
frames. A biframe is a pair

\left(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J},\{g_{j}\}_{j\in J}\right)

of sequences in the Hilbert space
U
, if there
exist positive constants
c
and
d
such that

c\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{j}\rangle\langle g_{j},f\rangle\leq d\|f\|^{2},\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in U).

(5)

Through the exciting developments
in the biframes and controlled frames, we introduce the notion of bi-
g
-frames
in Hilbert spaces and show some properties of the bi-
g
-frame then establish a relationship between bi-
g
-frame and the Riesz basis.

g
-frames and their operator

In the following of this paper,
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{V}
are two Hilbert spaces and
\{\mathcal{V}_{j}:\ j\in J\}
is a sequence of
subspaces of
\mathcal{V}
, where
J
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
.
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}_{j})
is the collection of all bounded linear operators
from
\mathcal{U}
into
\mathcal{V}_{j}
.
In this section, we define the concept of bi-
g
-frame then show some properties of the bi-
g
-frame.

Definition 2.1

Let
\{\Lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}_{j})\}
and
\{\Gamma_{j}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}_{j})\}
are two sequence for
\mathcal{U}
with respect to
\mathcal{V}_{j}
.
A pair
(\Lambda,\Gamma)=(\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j\in J},\{\Gamma_{j}\}_{j\in J})
is called a bi-
g
-frame for

\mathcal{U}
with respect to
\mathcal{V}_{j}
if there exist positive constants
C
and
D
such that

C\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in J}\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle_{\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{V}_{j}}\leq D\|f\|^{2},\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in\mathcal{U}).

(6)

we call
C
and
D
the lower and upper bi-
g
-frame bounds, respectively. If only the right-hand inequality of (
6
) is satisfied, we call

(\Lambda,\Gamma)

the bi-
g
-Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{U}
with respect to

\{\mathcal{V}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
with bi-
g
-Bessel bound
D
. If
C=D=\lambda
, we call
(\Lambda,\Gamma)
the tight bi-
g
-frame. Moreover, if
C=D=1
, we call
(\Lambda,\Gamma)
the Parseval bi-
g
-frame.

Example 2.2

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a separable Hilbert space and
\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be two frames for
\mathcal{U}
. Let
\Lambda_{f_{j}}
and
\Gamma_{g_{j}}
be
the functional induced by
f_{j}
and
g_{j}
respectively, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Lambda_{f_{j}}(f)=\langle f,f_{j}\rangle\\
\\
\Gamma_{g_{j}}(f)=\langle f,g_{j}\rangle\end{array}\right.

A pair
(\Lambda_{f},\Gamma_{g})=(\{\Lambda_{f_{j}}\}_{j\in J},\{\Gamma_{g_{j}}\}_{j\in J})
is a bi-
g
-frame for

\mathcal{U}
with respect to
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
(\Lambda,\Gamma)
be a bi-
g
-frame for
\mathcal{U}
with respect to

\{\mathcal{V}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
. We define the bi-
g
-frame operator
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}

as follows:

S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}:\mathcal{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U},\hskip 28.45274ptS_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f)=\sum_{j\in J}\Gamma^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}f,

(7)

Theorem 2.3

Let
(\Lambda,\Gamma)
be a bi-
g
-frame for
\mathcal{U}
with respect to

\{\mathcal{V}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
with bounds
C
and
D
. Then
the following statements are true:

1.

The operator
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
is well defined, bounded, positive, invertible with

\|S^{-1}_{\Lambda,\Gamma}\|\leq\frac{1}{C}
and
S^{*}_{\Lambda,\Gamma}=S_{\Gamma,\Lambda}
.

2.

(\Lambda,\Gamma)
is a bi-
g

(\Gamma,\Lambda)
is a bi-
g

Proof 2.4

1.

For each
f\in\mathcal{U}
we have

\displaystyle|\langle\sum_{j=n_{1}}^{n_{2}}\Gamma^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}f,f\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\sum_{j=n_{1}}^{n_{2}}\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n_{1}}^{n_{2}}|\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle|.

Now we see from (
6
) that the series in (
7
) are convergent. Therefore,
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
is well defined. On the other hands

\displaystyle\langle S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f),f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{j\in J}\Gamma^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle,

so

C\|f\|^{2}\leq\langle S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f),f\rangle\leq D\|f\|^{2},\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in\mathcal{U}),

and this shows that
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
is positive and and bounded operator.
To prove that
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
is an invertible operator, we need to show that
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
and
S^{*}_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
are injective and have closed ranges. For this

\displaystyle\langle S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{j\in J}\Gamma^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in J}\langle f,\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Gamma_{j}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,\sum_{j\in J}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Gamma_{j}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,S_{\Gamma,\Lambda}(g)\rangle.

Hence
S^{*}_{\Lambda,\Gamma}=S_{\Gamma,\Lambda}
. By the definition of bi-
g
-frame,
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
and
S_{\Gamma,\Lambda}
are injective and have a closed range as in the proof of Theorem
4.8

[
4
]
. Let
g\in\mathcal{U}
be such that
\langle S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f),g\rangle=0

for every
f\in\mathcal{U}
. Then we have
\langle f,S_{\Gamma,\Lambda}(g)\rangle=0
. This implies that
S_{\Gamma,\Lambda}(g)=0
and therefore

g=0
. Hence
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(\mathcal{U})=\mathcal{U}
. Consequently,
S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}
is invertible and

C\|f\|^{2}\leq\langle S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f),f\rangle\leq\|S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f)\|\|f\|,\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in\mathcal{U}),

then

C\|f\|\leq\|S_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f)\|,\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in\mathcal{U}),

so

C\|S^{-1}_{\Lambda,\Gamma}(f)\|\leq\|f\|,\hskip 85.35826pt(f\in\mathcal{U}),

consequently

\displaystyle\|S^{-1}_{\Lambda,\Gamma}\|\leq\frac{1}{C}.

2.

Let
(\Lambda,\Gamma)
be a bi-
g
-frame for
\mathcal{U}

\{\mathcal{V}_{j}\}_{j\in J}

and

and this means that

belongs to

\overline{\sum_{j\in J}\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle_{\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{V}_{j}}}=\sum_{j\in J}\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle_{\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{V}_{j}},

and therefore

{\sum_{j\in J}\langle\Gamma_{j}f,\Lambda_{j}f\rangle_{\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{V}_{j}}}=\sum_{j\in J}\langle\Lambda_{j}f,\Gamma_{j}f\rangle_{\bigoplus_{j\in J}\mathcal{V}_{j}},

so,
(\Gamma,\Lambda)
is a bi-
g
-frame for
\mathcal{U}
with respect to

\{\mathcal{V}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
with bounds
C
and
D
.
The converse statement is obtained in a similar way.

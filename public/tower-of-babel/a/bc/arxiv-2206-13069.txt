Honest Confidence Bands for Isotonic Quantile Curves
Lutz Dümbgen, Lukas Lüthi

Abstract




Abstract.

We provide confidence bands for isotonic quantile curves in nonparametric univariate regression with guaranteed given coverage probability. The method is an adaptation of the confidence bands of Dümbgen and Johns (2004) for isotonic median curves.

AMS subject classification:

63G08, 62G15, 62G20

Key words:

Binomial distribution, union-intersection test.

1
Introduction

Let
(X_{1},Y_{1}),(X_{2},Y_{2}),\ldots,(X_{n},Y_{n})
be independent random pairs consisting of covariate values
X_{i}
in some real interval
\mathcal{X}
and response values
Y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. We assume that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{i}\leq y\,|\,X_{i})=F(y\,|\,X_{i})
with unknown conditional distribution functions
F(\cdot\,|\,x)
,
x\in\mathcal{X}
. For any fixed
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
, let
Q_{\gamma}(x)
be a
\gamma
-quantile of
F(\cdot\,|\,x)
, that is,

F(Q_{\gamma}(x)-|\,x)\ \leq\ \gamma\ \leq\ F(Q_{\gamma}(x)\,|\,x).

Our goal is to compute a
(1-\alpha)
-confidence band
(L,U)
for
Q_{\gamma}
under the sole assumption that
Q_{\gamma}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is isotonic, i.e. non-decreasing. Precisely, we want to determine functions
L=L(\cdot,\mathrm{data})
and
U=U(\cdot,\mathrm{data})
on
\mathcal{X}
with values in
[-\infty,\infty]
such that

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(L\leq Q_{\gamma}\leq U\ \text{on}\ \mathcal{X})\ \geq\ 1-\alpha

whenever
Q_{\gamma}
is isotonic. In Section
2
we describe the construction of the bands. It is a simplified and generalized version of the confidence bands of
Dümbgen and Johns 2004
who treated the case
\gamma=0.5
only. The bands are also similar in spirit to the confidence bands of
Dimitriadis et al. 2022
for univariate binary regression or the confidence bands of
Yang and Barber 2019
for isotonic regression with subgaussian errors. An important property of the bands is that their computation requires only
O(n^{2})
steps. An alternative method to construct confidence bands for isotonic quantile curves has been proposed recently by
Chatterjee and Sen 2021
, but it requires certain a priori knowlegde about the conditional distribution functions
F(\cdot\,|\,x)
,
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Section
3
presents some asymptotic properties of the bands as the sample size
n
tends to infinity. The results are similar to the results of
Dümbgen and Johns 2004
and
Mösching and Dümbgen 2020
. The latter paper treats point estimation of the conditional distribution functions
F(\cdot\,|\,x)
under the assumption that they are isotonic in
x\in\mathcal{X}
with respect to the usual stochastic order. Section
4
illustrates the bands with some numerical examples. There it is also illustrated what happens if the quantile functions are even assumed to be S-shaped, that is, isotonic and convex-concave, and if we replace our confidence band with the hull of all S-shaped functions fitting within the band. This stronger shape-constraint is plausible in many applications, see
Feng et al. 2021
and the references cited therein. The latter paper treats least squares estimation of S-shaped mean functions.

2
Construction of the bands

We condition on the observed values
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, so they become fixed real numbers, and the observations
Y_{i}
are independent with distribution functions
F_{i}:=F(\cdot\,|\,X_{i})
. For an interval
B\subset\mathcal{X}
, let

N(B)\ :=\ \#\{i\colon X_{i}\in B\}.

Suppose that
g:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is a candidate for
Q_{\gamma}
. If
N(B)>0
and
g=Q_{\gamma}
, then the distribution of the random sum

S_{\ell}(B,g)\ :=\ \#\{i\colon X_{i}\in B,Y_{i}\leq g(X_{i})\}

is stochastically greater than or equal to a binomial distribution with parameters
N(B)
and
\gamma
. That is, for any integer
k\geq 0
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(S_{\ell}(B,g)\leq k\bigr)\ \leq\ F_{N(B),\gamma}(k),

where
F_{m,p}
denotes the distribution function of the binomial distribution with parameters
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in[0,1]
. Analogously, the distribution of the random sum

S_{u}(B,g)\ :=\ \#\{i\colon X_{i}\in B,Y_{i}\geq g(X_{i})\}

is stochastically greater than or equal to a binomial distribution with parameters
N(B)
and
1-\gamma
. For our confidence bands, we need an analogous statement simultaneously for all intervals
B\subset\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 1
.

There exists a coupling between the observations
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
and stochastically independent Bernoulli variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in\{0,1\}
with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(\xi_{i})=\gamma
, such that for any version
g
of
Q_{\gamma}
and all intervals
B\subset\mathcal{X}
,

S_{\ell}(B,g)\ \geq\ T_{\ell}(B):=\sum_{i\colon X_{i}\in B}\xi_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{u}(B,g)\ \geq\ T_{u}(B):=\sum_{i\colon X_{i}\in B}(1-\xi_{i}).

Proof of Lemma
1
.

Let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
be independent random variables with uniform distribution on
(0,1)
. Then the quantile transformations

Y_{i}\ :=\ F_{i}^{-1}(U_{i})=\min\{y\in\mathbb{R}\colon F_{i}(y)\geq U_{i}\}

yield independent random variables
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
with distribution functions
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
, respectively. For any version
g
of
Q_{\gamma}
and
1\leq i\leq n
, it follows from
F_{i}(g(X_{i}))\geq\gamma
that

1_{[Y_{i}\leq g(X_{i})]}\ =\ 1_{[F_{i}(g(X_{i}))\geq U_{i}]}\ \geq\ 1_{[U_{i}\leq\gamma]}.

Similarly, the inequality
F_{i}(g(X_{i})-)\leq\gamma
implies that

1_{[Y_{i}\geq g(X_{i})]}\ =\ 1_{[F_{i}(y)<U_{i}\ \text{for all}\ y<g(X_{i})]}\ \geq\ 1_{[F_{i}(g(X_{i})-)<U_{i}]}\ \geq\ 1_{[U_{i}>\gamma]}=1-1_{[U_{i}\leq\gamma]}.

Consequently,
\xi_{i}:=1_{[U_{i}\leq\gamma]}
defines Bernoulli variables
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
with the desired properties.
∎

The confidence band can be constructed as follows. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a given family of compact intervals
B\subset\mathcal{X}
with endpoints in
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. Suppose that for any integer
m\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have specified integers
c_{\ell}(m),c_{u}(m)\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
such that

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(T_{\ell}(B)\geq c_{\ell}(N(B))\ \text{and}\ T_{u}(B)\geq c_{u}(N(B))\ \ \text{for all}\ B\in\mathcal{B}\bigr)\ \geq\ 1-\alpha.

(1)

Then we may claim with confidence
1-\alpha
that for any version
g
of
Q_{\gamma}
and all intervals
B\in\mathcal{B}
,

S_{\ell}(B,g)\ \geq\ c_{\ell}(N(B))\quad\text{and}\quad S_{u}(B,g)\ \geq\ c_{u}(N(B)).

(2)

Let
Y_{B:1}\leq Y_{B:2}\leq\cdots\leq Y_{B:N(B)}
be the order statistics of the observations
Y_{i}
with
X_{i}\in B
, and let
Y_{B:0}:=-\infty
,
Y_{B:N(B)+1}:=\infty
. Then it follows from isotonicity of
g
that

S_{\ell}(B,g)\ \geq\ c_{\ell}(N(B))\quad\text{implies that}\quad g(\max(B))\ \geq\ Y_{B:c_{\ell}(N(B))},

because
S_{\ell}(B,g)\leq\#\bigl\{i\colon X_{i}\in B,Y_{i}\leq g(\max(B))\bigr\}
. Likewise,

S_{u}(B,g)\ \geq\ c_{u}(N(B))\quad\text{implies that}\quad g(\min(B))\ \leq\ Y_{B:N(B)+1-c_{u}(N(B))}.

Assuming that
Q_{\gamma}
is isotonic,
Y_{B:c_{\ell}(N(B))}
is a lower bound for all values
Q_{\gamma}(x)
,
x\geq\max(B)
, and
Y_{B:N(B)+1-c_{i}(N(B))}
is an upper bound for all values
Q_{\gamma}(x)
,
x\leq\min(B)
. These considerations lead to the following
(1-\alpha)
-confidence band
(L,U)
for
Q_{\gamma}
:

\displaystyle L(x)\ :=\

\displaystyle\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}\colon\max(B)\leq x}\,Y_{B:c_{\ell}(N(B))},

(3)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}\colon\max(B)\leq x}\,\min\bigl\{r\in[-\infty,\infty)\colon S_{\ell}(B,r)\geq c_{\ell}(N(B))\bigr\}

\displaystyle U(x)\ :=\

\displaystyle\inf_{B\in\mathcal{B}\colon\min(B)\geq x}\,Y_{B:N(B)+1-c_{u}(N(B))}

(4)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\inf_{B\in\mathcal{B}\colon\min(B)\geq x}\,\max\bigl\{r\in(-\infty,\infty]:S_{u}(B,r)\geq c_{u}(N(B))\bigr\}

with the conventions that
\sup(\emptyset):=-\infty
and
\inf(\emptyset):=\infty
, where
S_{\ell}(B,r):=\#\{i\colon X_{i}\in B,Y_{i}\leq r\}
and
S_{u}(B,r):=\#\{i\colon X_{i}\in B,Y_{i}\geq r\}
for
r\in[-\infty,\infty]
.

Finding the critical values
c_{\ell}(m)
and
c_{u}(m)
.

Let
\tilde{n}\leq n
be the number of elements of the set
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. Then
\mathcal{B}
consists of at most
\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}+1)/2
different intervals. By Bonferroni’s inequality, for any choice of the bounds
c_{\ell}(m)
and
c_{u}(m)
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(

\displaystyle T_{\ell}(B)<c_{\ell}(N(B))\ \text{or}\ T_{u}(B)<c_{u}(N(B))\ \text{for some}\ B\in\mathcal{B}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum_{m=1}^{n}h_{m}\bigl[F_{m,\gamma}(c_{\ell}(m)-1)+F_{m,1-\gamma}(c_{u}(m)-1)\bigr],

(5)

where
h_{m}
is the number of intervals
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
N(B)=m
. Now let

c_{\ell}(m)\ :=\ F_{m,\gamma}^{-1}(\kappa)\quad\text{and}\quad c_{u}(m)\ :=\ F_{m,1-\gamma}^{-1}(\kappa)

(6)

for some
\kappa\in(0,1]
. With a simple bisection search, one can find a maximal value of
\kappa
such that the bound (
5
) is not larger than
\alpha
. Since
\sum_{m=1}^{n}H(m)\leq\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}+1)/2
and
F_{m,p}(F_{m,p}^{-1}(\kappa)-1)\leq\kappa
, we know that

\kappa\ \geq\ \frac{\alpha}{2\#\mathcal{B}}\ \geq\ \frac{\alpha}{\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}+1)}.

The simple choice
\kappa=\alpha/(\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}+1))
would be similar to the approach of
Yang and Barber 2019
or
Dimitriadis et al. 2022
.

Alternatively, we propose to use the critical values in (
6
) with
\kappa
equal to a Monte-Carlo estimate of the
\alpha
-quantile of the random variable

\min_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\ \min\bigl\{F_{N(B),\gamma}(T_{\ell}(B)),F_{N(B),1-\gamma}(T_{u}(B))\bigr\}.

(7)

Both proposals correspond to a union-intersection test: For each interval
B\in\mathcal{B}
and any candidate
g
for
Q_{\gamma}
, one may interpret

F_{N(B),\gamma}(S_{\ell}(B,g))

as a p-value of the null hypothesis that
Q_{\gamma}\leq g
on
B
, and

F_{N(B),1-\gamma}(S_{u}(B,g))

is a p-value of the null hypothesis that
Q_{\gamma}\geq g
on
B
.

Choosing
\mathcal{B}
.

In principle, one could take
\mathcal{B}
to be the set of all
\tilde{n}(\tilde{n}+1)/2
compact intervals
B\subset\mathcal{X}
with endpoints in
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. But for very large values
\tilde{n}
, the Bonferroni version of our critical values
c_{\ell}(m)
and
c_{u}(m)
may become rather conservative, while Monte-Carlo simulation of (
7
) may be too time-consuming. Alternatively, we propose to specify a subset
\mathcal{D}
of
\{1,\ldots,\lceil\tilde{n}/2\rceil\}
and to restrict ourselves to intervals
B\subset\mathcal{X}
such that the cardinality of
B\cap\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is in
\mathcal{D}
. Specific choices are the intersection of
\{1,\ldots,\lceil\tilde{n}/2\rceil\}
with the set of numbers
a_{\ell}:=1+\ell(\ell-1)/2
,
\ell\geq 1
. That is,
a_{1}=1
and
a_{\ell+1}-a_{\ell}=\ell
. Here,
\#\mathcal{D}=O(\tilde{n}^{1/2})
and thus,
\#\mathcal{B}=O(\tilde{n}^{3/2})
. Alternatively, one could intersect
\{1,\ldots,\lceil\tilde{n}/2\rceil\}
with the set of Fibonacci numbers
1,2,3,5,8,13,\ldots
or the powers
2^{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 0
. In both cases,
\#\mathcal{D}=O(\log\tilde{n})
and
\#\mathcal{B}=O(\tilde{n}\log\tilde{n})
.

Explicit computation of
L
and
U
.

Let
z_{1}<\cdots<z_{\tilde{n}}
be the elements of
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
. Note first that
L\equiv-\infty
on
(-\infty,z_{1})
, and
L
is constant on all intervals
[z_{j},z_{j+1})
,
1\leq j\leq\tilde{n}
, where
z_{\tilde{n}+1}:=\infty
. Similarly,
U\equiv\infty
on
(z_{\tilde{n}},\infty)
, and
U
is constant on all intervals
(z_{j-1},z_{j}]
,
1\leq j\leq\tilde{n}
, where
z_{0}:=-\infty
. Thus it suffices to compute
\boldsymbol{L}=(L(z_{j}))_{j=1}^{\tilde{n}}
and
\boldsymbol{U}=(U(z_{j})_{j=1}^{\tilde{n}}
.

Now we describe an algorithm to compute
\boldsymbol{L}
in
O(n^{2})
steps. The same algorithm can be applied to compute
\boldsymbol{U}=(U(z_{j}))_{j=1}^{\tilde{n}}
by replacing temporarily the observations
(X_{i},Y_{i})
with
(-X_{i},-Y_{i})
and
\gamma
with
1-\gamma
. This is possible because our methods of setting
\kappa
for the critical values in (
6
) are invariant under these replacements.

In what follows, let
N_{j}:=\#\{i\colon X_{i}\leq z_{j}\}
for
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,\tilde{n}\}
. Suppose that for some
k\in\{1,\ldots,\tilde{n}\}
, the value
L(z_{k-1})
is known already, where
L(z_{0}):=-\infty
. Then
L(z_{k})
is the smallest number
r\in[L(z_{k-1}),\infty)
such that

S([z_{j},z_{k}],r)\ \geq\ c_{\ell}(N_{k}-N_{j-1})\quad\text{for all}\ [z_{j},z_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}.

To determine this number
r
, we start with
r=L(z_{k-1})
and check for
j=k,k-1,k-2,\ldots
whether
[z_{j},z_{k}]
belongs to
\mathcal{B}
and, if yes, satisfies
S([z_{j},z_{k}],r)\geq c_{\ell}(N_{k}-N_{j-1})
. While doing this, we determine the next candidate

r_{\rm new}\ :=\ \min\bigl(\{\infty\}\cup\{Y_{i}\colon X_{i}\in[z_{j},z_{k}],Y_{i}>r\bigr)

for
r
. As soon as
[z_{j},z_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}
but
S([z_{j},z_{k}],r)<c_{\ell}(N_{k}-N_{j-1})
, we replace
r
with
r_{\rm new}
and reset
j
to
k
. This leads to the algorithm described in Table
1
.

\begin{array}[]{|l|}\hline\cr r\leftarrow-\infty\\
\text{for}\ k=1,2,\ldots,n\ \text{do}\\
\quad j\leftarrow k+1\\
\quad S\leftarrow 0\\
\quad r_{\rm new}\leftarrow\infty\\
\quad\text{while}\ j>1\ \text{do}\\
\quad\quad j\leftarrow j-1\\
\quad\quad S\leftarrow S+\sum_{i\colon X_{i}=z_{j}}1_{[Y_{i}\leq r]}\\
\quad\quad r_{\rm new}\leftarrow\min\bigl(\{r_{\rm new}\}\cup\{Y_{i}\colon X_{i}=z_{j},Y_{i}>r\}\bigr)\\
\quad\quad\text{if}\ S<c_{\ell}(N_{k}-N_{j-1})\ \text{then}\\
\quad\quad\quad r\leftarrow r_{\rm new}\\
\quad\quad\quad j\leftarrow k+1\\
\quad\quad\quad S\leftarrow 0\\
\quad\quad\quad r_{\rm new}\leftarrow\infty\\
\quad\quad\text{end if}\\
\quad\text{end while}\\
\quad L(z_{k})\leftarrow r\\
\text{end for}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Table 1:
Algorithm to compute the lower confidence bounds
L(\cdot)
.

That this algorithm has running time
O(n^{2})
can be verified as follows: For a given pair
(k,r)
in
\{1,\ldots,\tilde{n}\}\times(\{-\infty\}\cup\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\})
, we initialise and update
S
and
r_{\rm new}
for
j=k,k-1,k-2,\ldots
until
j=1
or
[z_{j},z_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}
but
S([z_{j},z_{k}])<c_{\ell}(N_{k}-N_{j-1})
. This requires
O(n)
steps. The next pair
(k^{\prime},r^{\prime})
satisfies either
k^{\prime}>k
or
r^{\prime}>r
. Consequently, from the starting point
(k,r)=(1,-\infty)
, we arrive at the point
(k,r)=(\tilde{n},L(z_{\tilde{n}}))
in at most
\tilde{n}-1+n=O(n)
steps. Hence, the total running time is of order
O(n^{2})
.

3
Asymptotics

Similarly as in
Dümbgen and Johns 2004
,
Mösching and Dümbgen 2020
and
Dimitriadis et al. 2022
, we consider a triangular scheme of observations. For each sample size
n\geq 1
, we observe
(X_{n1},Y_{n1}),\ldots,(X_{nn},Y_{nn})
with fixed numbers
X_{n1}\leq\ldots\leq X_{nn}
in
\mathcal{X}
and independent random variables
Y_{n1},\ldots,Y_{nn}
, where
Y_{ni}
has distribution function
F(\cdot\,|\,X_{ni})
. Let
(L_{n},U_{n})
be the confidence band described before for sample size
n
, based on a family
\mathcal{B}_{n}\subset\mathcal{B}_{n}^{*}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{*}
is the family of all compact intervals with endpoints in
\{X_{n1},\ldots,X_{nn}\}
. We analyze asymptotic properties of
(L_{n},U_{n})
on a bounded open interval
(a_{0},b_{o})\subset\mathcal{X}
under the following three assumptions.

(A)
For any set
B_{*}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{*}
, there exists a set
B\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
such that
B\subset B^{*}
and
N_{n}(B)\geq N_{n}(B^{*})/2
.

(B)
Let
\mathrm{Leb}(\cdot)
denote Lebesgue measure, and let
N_{n}(B)=\#\{i\colon X_{ni}\in B\}
for
B\subset\mathcal{X}
. There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for sufficiently large
n
,

N_{n}(B)\ \geq\ C_{1}n\,\mathrm{Leb}(B)

for arbitrary intervals
B\subset(a_{o},b_{o})
such that
\mathrm{Leb}(B)\geq C_{2}\log(n)/n
.

(C)
There exist constants
D_{1},D_{2}>0
such that for all
x\in(a_{o},b_{o})
, the
\gamma
-quantile
Q_{\gamma}(x)
is unique, and

\frac{F(Q_{\gamma}(x)+h\,|\,x)-\gamma}{h}\ \geq\ D_{1}

for all nonzero
h\in[-D_{2},D_{2}]
.

One can easily show that assumption (A) is satisfied for all proposals of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in the previous section. Assumption (B) is satisfied, for instance, in a deterministic setting with fixed design points
X_{ni}=G^{-1}(i/n)
for some distribution function
G
on
\mathcal{X}
such that
G^{\prime}
exists and is bounded away from
0
on
(a_{o},b_{o})
. If the
X_{ni}
are the order statistics of
n
independent random variables with such a distribution function
G
, the arguments in Section 4.3 of
Mösching and Dümbgen 2020
can be modified to show that Condition (A) is satisfied almost surely, provided that
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are chosen appropriately.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that assumptions (A,B,C) are satisfied. Let
\rho_{n}=\log(n)/n
. There exist constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
C_{1},C_{2},D_{1},D_{2}
with the following properties:

(i) Suppose that
Q_{\gamma}
is constant on
(a_{o},b_{o})
. Then with asymptotic probability one,

\displaystyle U_{n}(x)\

\displaystyle\leq\ p(x)+C\sqrt{\rho_{n}/(b_{o}-x)}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in(a_{o},b_{o}-C^{\prime}\rho_{n}],

\displaystyle L_{n}(x)\

\displaystyle\geq\ p(x)-C\sqrt{\rho_{n}/(b_{o}-x)}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in[a_{o}+C^{\prime}\rho_{n},b_{o}).

(ii) Suppose that
p
is Hölder-continuous with exponent
\beta\in(0,1]
and constant
\lambda>0
on
(a_{o},b_{o})
, that is,
\bigl|Q_{\gamma}(x)-Q_{\gamma}(x^{\prime})\bigr|\leq\lambda|x-x^{\prime}|
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in(a_{o},b_{o})
. Then for
\delta_{n}:=(\lambda^{-2}\rho_{n})^{1/(2\beta+1)}
,

\sup_{x\in(a_{o},b_{o}-\delta_{n}]}\,\bigl(U_{n}(x)-Q_{\gamma}(x)\bigr)^{+},\,\sup_{x\in(a_{o}+\delta_{n},b_{o})}\,\bigl(Q_{\gamma}(x)-L_{n}(x)\bigr)^{+}\ \leq\ C\lambda^{1/(2\beta+1)}\rho_{n}^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}.

(iii) Suppose that
Q_{\gamma}
is discontinuous at some point
x_{o}\in(a_{o},b_{o})
. Then with asymptotic probability one,

\displaystyle U_{n}(x)\

\displaystyle\leq\ Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}-)+C\sqrt{\rho_{n}/(x_{o}-x)}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in(a_{o},x_{o}-C^{\prime}\rho_{n}],

\displaystyle L_{n}(x)\

\displaystyle\geq\ Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}+)-C\sqrt{\rho_{n}/(x-x_{o})}\quad\text{for all}\ x\in[a_{o}+C^{\prime}\rho_{n},b_{o}).

Part (i) shows that for arbitrary fixed numbers
a_{o}<a<b<b_{o}
,

\sup_{x\in(a_{o},b]}\,(U_{n}-p)^{+}(x)+\sup_{x\in[a,b_{o})}\,(p-L_{n})^{+}(x)\ =\ O_{p}(\rho_{n}^{1/2})

Part (iii) shows that for with asymptotic probability one,

U_{n}(x_{\ell})\ <\ \frac{Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}-)+Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}+)}{2}\ <\ L_{n}(x_{r})

for
a_{o}<x_{\ell}<x_{o}-D\rho_{n}
and
x_{o}+D\rho_{n}<x_{r}<b_{o}
, where
D
is the maximum of
C^{\prime}
and
4C^{2}\bigl(Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}+)-Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}-)\bigr)^{-2}
. Consequently, whenever
Q_{\gamma}
has a discontinuity at some point in
(a_{o},b_{o})
, the confidence band
(L_{n},U_{n})
crosses a horizontal line on an interval of length
O(\rho_{n})
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For symmetry reasons, it suffices to prove the claims about the lower bound function
L_{n}(\cdot)
. For notational convenience, the additional subscript
n
for the triangular scheme is often dropped. In what follows,
C_{o}
denotes a generic universal constant, and
C^{\prime}
denotes a generic constant depending possibly on
C_{1},D_{1}
.

Applying Hoeffding’s inequality
(
Hoeffding 1963
)
to binomial distributions leads to the inequalities

c_{\ell}(m)\ \geq\ m\gamma-\sqrt{m\log(1/\kappa)/2},

(8)

where
c_{\ell}(m)=F_{m,\gamma}^{-1}(\kappa)
. Since
\kappa\geq\alpha/[n(n+1)]
, we may conclude from (
9
) that for
1\leq m\leq n
and sufficiently large
n
,

c_{\ell}(m)\ \geq\ m\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{m\log(n)},

(9)

provided that
C_{o}>1
. For
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\Sigma_{\ell}(B,r)\ :=\ \operatorname{\mathbb{E}}S_{\ell}(B,r).

Then it follows from
Mösching and Dümbgen 2020
that with asymptotic probability one,

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{B},r\in\mathbb{R}}\,N(B)^{-1/2}\bigl|(S_{\ell}-\Sigma_{\ell})(B,r)\bigr|\ \leq\ C_{o}\sqrt{\log(n)},

(10)

provided that
C_{o}>1
.

From now on, we always assume that
n
is large enough such that all inequalities for
N(\cdot)
in assumption (A) are satisfied, and we tacitly assume that inequalities (
9
) and (
10
) hold true as well. For
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,N(B)\}
, it follows from (
10
) that

j\ \leq\ S_{\ell}(B,Y_{B:j})\ \leq\ \Sigma_{\ell}(B,Y_{B:j})+C_{o}\sqrt{N(B)\log(n)}.

Plugging in
j=c_{\ell}(N(B))
and using (
9
), we may conclude that for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\Sigma_{\ell}(B,Y_{B:c_{\ell}(N(B))})\ \geq\ N(B)\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B)\log(n)},

provided that
C_{o}>2
. Combining this inequality with the definition (
3
) of
L
shows that
L
is bounded from below by the following deterministic function
\tilde{L}
:

\tilde{L}(x)\ :=\ \sup_{B\in\mathcal{B}\colon\max(B)\leq x}\,\inf\bigl\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon\Sigma_{\ell}(B,r)\geq N(B)\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B)\log(n)}\bigr\}.

Consequently, it suffices to verify the assertions for
\tilde{L}
instead of
L
.

As to part (i), suppose that
Q_{\gamma}\equiv q
on
(a_{o},b_{o})
. For
x\in[a_{o}+C_{2}\rho_{n},b_{o})
, let
B(x)
be the largest interval in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
which is contained in
(a_{o},x]
. By assumptions (A-B),
N(B(x))\geq 2^{-1}C_{1}n(x-a_{o})
, and assumption (C) implies that for
h\in(0,D_{2}]
,

\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),q-h)\ =\ \sum_{i\colon X_{i}\in B(x)}F(Q_{\gamma}(X_{i})-h\,|\,X_{i})\ \leq\ N(B(x))\gamma-D_{1}N(B(x))h.

Consequently,
\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x))\geq N(B(x))\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}
implies that

h\ \leq\ C_{o}D_{1}^{-1}\sqrt{\log(n)/N(B(x))}\ \leq\ h_{n}(x):=2^{1/2}C_{o}D_{1}^{-1}C_{1}^{-1/2}\sqrt{\rho_{n}/(x-a_{o})}.

If we restrict
x
to
[a_{0}+C^{\prime}\rho_{n},b_{o})
with sufficiently large
C^{\prime}\geq C_{2}
, the number
h_{n}(x)
is no larger than
D_{2}
, and we may conclude that

\tilde{L}(x)\ \geq\ \inf\bigl\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),r)\geq N(B(x))\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}\bigr\}\ \geq\ q-h_{n}(x),

which proves part (i).

Concerning part (ii), suppose that
Q_{\gamma}
is Hölder-continuous on
(a_{o},b_{o})
with exponent
\beta\in(0,1]
and constant
\lambda>0
. Since
\delta_{n}/\rho_{n}\to\infty
, we may assume that
\delta_{n}\geq C_{2}\rho_{n}
. For
x\in[a_{o}+\delta_{n},b_{o})
, let
B(x)
be the largest interval in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
which is contained in
(x-\delta_{n},x]
. Then assumptions (A-B) imply that

N(B(x))\geq 2^{-1}C_{1}n\delta_{n}
. For
h\in(0,D_{2}]
, it follows from assumption (C) that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),Q_{\gamma}(x)-h)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{i\colon X_{i}\in B(x)}F(Q_{\gamma}(X_{i})-h\,|\,X_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum_{i\colon X_{i}\in B(x)}F(Q_{\gamma}(x)+L\delta_{n}^{\beta}-h\,|\,X_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\ N(B(x))-D_{1}N(B(x))(h-L\delta_{n}^{\beta})^{+}.

Consequently,
\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),Q_{\gamma}(x)-h)\geq N(B(x))\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}
implies that

h\ \leq\ L\delta_{n}^{\beta}+C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}\ \leq h_{n},

where

h_{n}\ :=\ L\delta_{n}^{\beta}+2^{1/2}C_{o}C_{1}^{-1/2}\sqrt{\rho_{n}/\delta_{n}}\ =\ (1+2^{1/2}C_{o}C_{1}^{-1/2})\lambda^{1/(2\beta+1)}\rho_{n}^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}.

Since
h_{n}\to 0
, the requirement
h_{n}\leq D_{2}
is satisfied for sufficiently large
n
, and then for all
x\in[a_{o}+\delta_{n},b_{o})
,

\tilde{L}(x)\ \geq\ \inf\bigl\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),r)\geq N(B(x))\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}\bigr\}\ \geq\ Q_{\gamma}(x)-h_{n},

which proves part (ii).

Finally, suppose that
Q_{\gamma}
is discontinuous at
x_{o}\in(a_{o},b_{o})
. For
x\in(a_{o},x_{o}-C_{2}\rho_{n}]
, let
B(x)
be the largest interval in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
which is contained in
[x,x_{o})
. By assumptions (A-B),
N(B(x))\geq 2^{-1}C_{1}n(x_{o}-x)
. For
h\in(0,D_{2}]
, it follows from assumption (C) that

\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),Q_{\gamma}(x_{o}-)-h)\ \leq\ \sum_{i\colon X_{i}\in B(x)}F(Q_{\gamma}(X_{i})-h\,|\,X_{i})\ \leq\ N(B(x))\gamma-D_{1}N(B(x))h.

Consequently,
\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x))\geq N(B(x))\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}
implies that

h\ \leq\ C_{o}D_{1}^{-1}\sqrt{\log(n)/N(B(x))}\ \leq\ h_{n}(x):=2^{1/2}C_{o}D_{1}^{-1}C_{1}^{-1/2}\sqrt{\rho_{n}/(x_{o}-x)}.

If we restrict
x
to
(a_{o},x_{o}-C^{\prime}\rho_{n}]
for sufficiently large
C^{\prime}\geq C_{2}
, then
h_{n}(x)\leq D_{2}
, and we may conclude that

\tilde{L}(x)\ \geq\ \inf\bigl\{r\in\mathbb{R}\colon\Sigma_{\ell}(B(x),r)\geq N(B(x))\gamma-C_{o}\sqrt{N(B(x))\log(n)}\bigr\}\ \geq\ q-h_{n}(x),

which proves part (iii).
∎

An stronger shape constraint.

In some applications, it is plausible that the quantile functions should be isotonic and convex or isotonic and concave. Both constraints are special cases of the more general assumption that the quantile functions are S-shaped. That means, they are isotonic on
\mathcal{X}
, and for some inflection point
\mu\in[-\infty,\infty]
, they are convex on
\mathcal{X}\cap(-\infty,\mu]
and concave on
[\mu,\infty)\cap\mathcal{X}
. Since S-shapedness implies isotonicity, assuming the quantile function
Q_{\gamma}
is S-shaped allows for the following refinement of our
(1-\alpha)
-confidence band
(L,U)
for
Q_{\gamma}
. We compute
(\tilde{L},\tilde{U})
with

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(x)\

\displaystyle:=\ \inf\bigl\{S(x)\colon S\ \text{S-shaped},L\leq S\leq U\bigr\},

\displaystyle\tilde{U}(x)\

\displaystyle:=\ \sup\bigl\{S(x)\colon S\ \text{S-shaped},L\leq S\leq U\bigr\}.

Precisely, we choose a fine grid
M
of potential values for the inflection point
\mu
of
Q_{\gamma}
and determine

\displaystyle\tilde{L}(x,\mu)\

\displaystyle:=\ \inf\bigl\{S(x)\colon S\ \text{S-shaped with inflection point}\ \mu,\,L\leq S\leq U\bigr\},

\displaystyle\tilde{U}(x,\mu)\

\displaystyle:=\ \sup\bigl\{S(x)\colon S\ \text{S-shaped with inflection point}\ \mu,\,L\leq S\leq U\bigr\}.

These functions
\tilde{L}(\cdot,\mu)
and
\tilde{U}(\cdot,\mu)
are easily determined, and then we approximate
\tilde{L}(x)
and
\tilde{U}(x)
with
\min_{\mu\in M}\tilde{L}(x,\mu)
and
\max_{\mu\in M}\tilde{U}(x,\mu)
, respectively.

For our numerical example with sample size
n=500
, the bands
(\tilde{L},\tilde{U})
differ only little from
(L,U)
, but for sample size
n=2500
, the improvement is substantial; see Figures
9
,
10
and
11
. The original band
(L,U)
is depicted with thin black lines, and the refined band
(\tilde{L},\tilde{U})
is depicted with blue lines.

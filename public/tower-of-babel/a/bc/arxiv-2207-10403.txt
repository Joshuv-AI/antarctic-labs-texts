Quantum graphs: Coulomb-type potentials and exactly solvable models
Yuriy Golovaty

Abstract
. We study the Schrödinger operators on a non-compact star graph with the Coulomb-type potentials having singularities at the vertex. The convergence of regularized Hamiltonians H ε H_{\varepsilon} with cut-off Coulomb potentials coupled with ( α ​ δ + β ​ δ ′ ) (\alpha\delta+\beta\delta^{\prime}) -like ones is investigated. The 1 1 D Coulomb potential and the δ ′ \delta^{\prime} -potential are very sensitive to their regularization method. The conditions of the norm resolvent convergence of H ε H_{\varepsilon} depending on the regularization are established. The limit Hamiltonians give the Schrödinger operators with the Coulomb-type potentials a mathematically precise meaning, ensuring the correct choice of vertex conditions. We also describe all self-adjoint realizations of the formal Coulomb Hamiltonians on the star graph.

1.
Introduction

In recent decades, the theory of differential operators on metric graphs has been the subject of systematic and extensive study, in particular due to numerous possible applications in solid-state physics and engineering. However, from the point of view of physics, the most exciting application of this theory is quantum graphs.
Quantum dynamics usually exhibits high complexity, especially propagation in ramiﬁed structures. Quantum graphs provide us with quite effective mathematical models with which we can study the quantum systems in a framework that makes explicit solutions possible. There is extensive literature on quantum graphs; we refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
and the bibliography therein.

A quantum graph is a metric graph equipped with Schrödinger differential expressions acting on edges and some conditions at vertices (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). For quantum graphs, there is a wide variety of conditions coupling the wave functions at vertices, in contrast to quantum systems with point interactions on the line.
This makes the theory of Schrödinger operators on graphs much richer.
However, the rich set of possible vertex coupling conditions complicates the construction of solvable models for specific quantum processes because the problem of choosing physically motivated conditions arises.
The mathematical approach to the construction of such models, in addition to the experimental one, consists in different approximations of Hamiltonians on graphs. The approximation of a graph by thin tubular domains
is the most natural. The problem then is to analyse the convergence of
3
D Hamiltonians as the tubular networks shrink to the graph and obtain the limit point interactions at the vertices
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
Another way of finding physically motivated point interactions is the approximation of quasi-one-dimensional meso- or nano-scale systems by Schrödinger operators on a graph with potentials singularly perturbed in a neighbourhood of the vertices
[
12
,
13
,
16
,
14
,
15
]
. Then the limit Hamiltonian, obtained in a proper operator topology, is the desired solvable model of the system.
Also interesting is the inverse problem, which consists in constructing approximations of given point interactions at a vertex by some regular Hamiltonians
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. It has been proven in
[
19
]
that any singular vertex coupling can be approximated by a family of quantum graphs with simultaneous perturbation of the geometry of the graph and the potentials.

In this paper, we construct solvable models for some non-relativistic quantum processes in ramiﬁed structures.
We study the Schrödinger operators on a star graph with the Coulomb-type potentials having singularities at the vertex. We investigate the convergence of Hamiltonians
H_{\varepsilon}
with regularised (cut-off) potentials to give the operators with the Coulomb potentials a mathematically precise meaning and find physically motivated conditions at the vertex. The results obtained in this paper remain valid for general quantum graphs. In this model, there is no interaction between vertices, and only the behavior of the Coulomb-type potential in the vicinity of vertex is significant. Since we are interested in vertex couplings caused by the Coulomb singularity, we study the case of a star-shaped quantum graph.
Our analysis of the Coulomb Hamiltonian can be viewed as a continuation of
[
20
]
, where we have found conditions of the norm resolvent convergence of
H_{\varepsilon}
on the line and have constructed the solvable models for the one-dimension hydrogen atom.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce notation and basic deﬁnitions, and then we state our main results (Theorems
1
-
3
) on the norm resolvent convergence of
H_{\varepsilon}
. We also discuss the solvable models constructed in Theorem
1
and compare them with previous studies dealing with partial cases of the problem. Theorem 4 shows the connection between the resonant decomposition of the graph and the structure of the boundary operator.
In Section
3
, we describe all self-adjoint realisations of the formal Schrödinger operators on a graph with the Coulomb-type potentials. The proof of the main results is given in Sections
4
and
5
.

2.
Main results and discussion

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a noncompact star graph consisting of
n
semi-inﬁnite edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
meeting at a single vertex
a
. The graph is considered as a planar metric graph with the metrics
coming from the natural embedding of
\mathcal{G}
into
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
A function
\phi
on the graph
\mathcal{G}
is the collection
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, where
\phi_{k}\colon e_{k}\to\mathbb{C}
is a function on the edge
e_{k}
. Let
\phi(a)
be the vector
(\phi_{1}(a),\dots,\phi_{n}(a))^{T}
of the function values at the vertex understood as one-sided limits when the vertex is approached from a
particular edge. We will also use
\phi^{\prime}(a)
to denote the vector
(\phi_{1}^{\prime}(a),\dots,\phi_{n}^{\prime}(a))^{T}
, where the one-sided derivative
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(a)
is taken in the direction from the vertex into the edge
e_{k}
. Here and subsequently,
T
denotes the transpose. We adhere to the convention that a function
\phi
belongs to some space
X(\mathcal{G})

if
\phi_{k}
belongs to
X(e_{k})
for all
k=1,\dots,n
, i.e.,
X(\mathcal{G})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}X(e_{k})
and
\|\phi\|_{X(\mathcal{G})}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\phi_{k}\|_{X(e_{k})}
.

We define the function
Q
on
\mathcal{G}
as

Q_{k}(x)=\frac{q_{k}}{|x-a|}\quad\text{for }x\in e_{k}.

(2.1)

Here
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
are real numbers. We call
Q
a Coulomb-type potential on the graph (see Figs.
1
and
2
).
Since
Q
has a non-integrable singularity at the vertex
a
, we consider the regularized potentials
Q_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

Q_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}\phantom{\frac{\ln\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}}Q(x),&\text{if \ }|x-a|>\varepsilon,\\
\frac{\ln\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\,\varkappa\left(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a)\right),&\text{if \ }|x-a|<\varepsilon,\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
\varkappa
is a real
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})
-function such that
\varkappa=0
for
|x-a|>1
, and
\varepsilon
is a small positive parameter. We introduce the family of potentials

W_{\varepsilon}(x)=Q_{\varepsilon}(x)+\varepsilon^{-2}V\left(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a)\right)+\varepsilon^{-1}U\left(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a)\right),

(2.3)

where
U,V\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
are bounded measurable functions of compact support. Similarly to the case
n=2
, when
\mathcal{G}
can be regarded as the line, we hereafter interpret the families
\varepsilon^{-1}U(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))
and
\varepsilon^{-2}V(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))
as
\delta
- and
\delta^{\prime}
-like potentials respectively (see Fig.
3
). Here
\delta
is Dirac’s function. Let

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G})=\left\{\phi\in W_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{G})\colon\phi\text{ is continuous at }a,\;\textstyle\sum^{n}_{k=1}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(a)=0\right\}

be the space of
W^{2}_{2}(\mathcal{G})
-functions subject to the Kirchhoff conditions at the vertex
a
.
We study the convergence as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the Schrödinger operators

H_{\varepsilon}\phi=-\phi^{\prime\prime}+W_{\varepsilon}\phi,\qquad\mathop{\rm dom}H_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}),

where
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is the second order derivative of
\phi
along edges.

q_{k}=-1
for all

.

Before stating our main result, we introduce some notation. Let us consider the Schrödinger operator

S\psi=-\psi^{\prime\prime}+V\psi\quad\text{on }\mathcal{G},\qquad\mathop{\rm dom}S=\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}).

Definition 1
.

We say that
S
possesses a
zero-energy resonance
(alternatively the potential
V
is
resonant
) if there exists a non-trivial solution
\psi
of the equation
-\psi^{\prime\prime}+V\psi=0
on
\mathcal{G}

subject to the Kirchhoff conditions at
a
that is bounded on the whole graph. We will call
\psi
the
half-bound state
of
S
.

This definition generalizes the notion of zero-energy resonance for the Schrödinger operators on the line
[
21
]
.
Denote by
\Psi_{V}
the linear space of all half-bound states of
S
. On each edge the half-bound state is constant outside the support of
V
as a bounded solution of the equation
\psi^{\prime\prime}=0
. Let
\psi^{\infty}_{k}
be the limit of
\psi
along the edge
e_{k}
as
|x|\to\infty
. We introduce the map
\ell\colon\Psi_{V}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by
\ell(\psi)=(\psi^{\infty}_{1},\dots,\psi^{\infty}_{n})^{T}
.

Definition 2
.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{V}
the image of
\Psi_{V}
under the map
\ell
and call it the
resonant space
of the potential
V
. We say that
V
is
non-resonant
if
\mathcal{R}_{V}
is trivial.

If
\psi^{\infty}_{k}=0
, then
\psi_{k}
is identically zero outside
\mathop{\rm supp}V
and hence
\psi_{k}=0
on the whole edge
e_{k}
as a solution of the linear differential equation. Therefore
\ell
is the injection of
\Psi_{V}
into
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. So
\dim\mathcal{R}_{V}=\dim\Psi_{V}
. As we will see below, the dimension of
\Psi_{V}
cannot exceed
n-1
.
Assume that
\dim\Psi_{V}=r
and fix a basis
\psi^{(1)},\dots,\psi^{(r)}
in
\Psi_{V}
.
Let
M
and
N
be the Hermitian matrices of dimension
r
with the entries

m_{ij}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}U\psi^{(j)}\overline{\psi^{(i)}}\,d\mathcal{G},\qquad n_{ij}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\varkappa\psi^{(j)}\overline{\psi^{(i)}}\,d\mathcal{G}

respectively. Here
d\mathcal{G}
is the induced Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{G}
.
Also, let
K
be the diagonal
n\times n
matrix having
q_{k}
from (
2.1
) as its diagonal entries.

We denote by
L
the
n\times r
matrix that is formed by the columns

l_{1}=\ell(\psi^{(1)}),\;l_{2}=\ell(\psi^{(2)}),\;\dots\;,\;l_{r}=\ell(\psi^{(r)}).

(2.4)

The entry
l_{ij}
of
L
is the limit of half-bound state
\psi^{(j)}
as
x
goes to infinity along the edge
e_{i}
.
Let
L^{+}
denote the pseudoinverse of
L
. Since
\ell
is an injection,
L
is full column rank and its pseudoinverse can be computed as

L^{+}=(L^{*}L)^{-1}L^{*},

(2.5)

where
L^{*}
is the Hermitian conjugate of
L
. This particular pseudoinverse constitutes a left inverse. In this case, we have
L^{+}L=E
, where
E
is the
r\times r
identity matrix.

Given a Coulomb-type potential
Q
, we introduce the space

\mathcal{W}_{Q}=\left\{\phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\colon\phi,\,\phi^{\prime}\in AC_{loc}(\mathcal{G}),\>-\phi^{\prime\prime}+Q\phi\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})\right\},

where
AC_{loc}(\mathcal{G})
is the set of absolutely continuous functions on any compact subset of
\mathcal{G}
which does not contain the vertex
a
. In the next section we show that the vector
\phi(a)=(\phi_{1}(a),\dots,\phi_{n}(a))^{T}
is well defined for every
\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}
. However, the vector
\phi^{\prime}(a)=(\phi_{1}^{\prime}(a),\dots,\phi_{n}^{\prime}(a))^{T}

may be undefined, because the first derivative of
\phi_{k}
may have a logarithmic singularity at
a
. Instead of
\phi^{\prime}(a)
we will use the vector
\phi^{[1]}(a)
consisting of the quasi-derivatives

\phi^{[1]}_{k}(a)=\lim_{e_{k}\ni x\to a}\big(\phi^{\prime}_{k}(x)-q_{k}\phi_{k}(a)\ln|x-a|\big).

We say the self-adjoint operators
H_{\varepsilon}
converge as
\varepsilon\to 0
to the operator
\mathcal{H}
in the norm resolvent sense if the resolvents
(H_{\varepsilon}-\zeta)^{-1}
converge to
(\mathcal{H}-\zeta)^{-1}
in the norm operator topology for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{V}
be the resonant space of the potential
V
present in (
2.3
). Suppose that the following inclusion holds

\mathcal{R}_{V}\subset\ker(NL^{+}-L^{*}K).

(2.6)

Then the operator family
H_{\varepsilon}
converges, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, in the norm resolvent sense to the operator
\mathcal{H}
defined by
\mathcal{H}\phi=-\phi^{\prime\prime}+Q\phi
on functions
\phi
from the domain

\mathop{\rm dom}\mathcal{H}=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{W}_{Q}\colon\phi(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V},\;\;ML^{+}\phi(a)-L^{*}{\phi}^{[1]}(a)=0\right\}.

(2.7)

Also, we can estimate the rate of the resolvent convergence.

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, the inequality

\|(H_{\varepsilon}-\zeta)^{-1}-(\mathcal{H}-\zeta)^{-1}\|\leq C(1+|\mathop{\rm Im}\zeta|^{-1})\,\varepsilon^{1/4}

holds for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
with
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\zeta
.

Let us state several corollaries of the main theorem. Some of them recover known results on the approximations of quantum-graph vertex couplings.

2.1.
\delta
couplings on graphs

The following result itself is so well known that it is even difficult to say by whom it was first published (see, for instance,
[
12
]
).

Corollary 1
.

Assume that
W_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}U(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))
, i.e.,
Q
,
V
and
\varkappa
are equal to zero. Then the operators
H_{\varepsilon}
converge in the norm resolvent topology
to the operator
\mathcal{H}
acting as the negative second derivative on functions
\phi
in
W_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{G})
obeying the vertex conditions

\phi_{1}(a)=\phi_{2}(a)=\cdots=\phi_{n}(a),\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(a)-\phi_{1}(a)\int_{\mathcal{G}}U\,d\mathcal{G}=0.

(2.8)

Proof.

Our only task is to show how the vertex conditions

\phi(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V},\quad ML^{+}\phi(a)-L^{*}{\phi}^{[1]}(a)=0

(2.9)

transform into (
2.8
).
Since
Q=0
and
\varkappa=0
, we have
\mathcal{W}_{Q}=W_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{G})
,
{\phi}^{[1]}(a)=\phi^{\prime}(a)
,
K=0
and
N=0
.
Therefore
NL^{+}-L^{*}K=0
, and condition (
2.6
) is trivially fulfilled.

\delta
- and
\delta^{\prime}
-like localized potentials

The potential
V=0
is resonant. The corresponding half-bound states are constant functions on
\mathcal{G}
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{V}
is a one-dimension space in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
generated by the vector
\mathbf{1}=(1,1,\dots,1)^{T}
. Also,
M
is the
1\times 1
matrix with the single entry
m_{11}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}U\,d\mathcal{G}
.
The inclusion
\phi(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V}
says that
\phi(a)=c\cdot\mathbf{1}
for some constant
c
, and therefore
\phi_{1}(a)=\cdots=\phi_{n}(a)
.
The matrix
L
consists of the single column
\mathbf{1}
. Applying (
2.5
) we compute
L^{+}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\;\,\frac{1}{n}\;\dots\;\frac{1}{n}\right)
. Then we have

ML^{+}\phi(a)-L^{*}\phi^{\prime}(a)=\begin{pmatrix}m_{11}\end{pmatrix}{\textstyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\;\,\frac{1}{n}\;\dots\;\frac{1}{n}\right)}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}(a)\\
\phi_{2}(a)\\
\ldots\\
\phi_{n}(a)\end{pmatrix}-(1\;\,1\;\dots\;1)\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\prime}(a)\\
\phi_{2}^{\prime}(a)\\
\ldots\\
\phi_{n}^{\prime}(a)\end{pmatrix}\\
=\frac{m_{11}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}(a)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(a)=m_{11}\phi_{1}(a)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}^{\prime}(a)=0,

since
\phi
is continuous at
a
.
∎

2.2.
\delta^{\prime}
couplings on graphs

The Schrödinger operators
S_{\varepsilon}=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\varepsilon^{-2}V(\varepsilon^{-1}x)
on the line and their norm resolvent convergence are related to the problem of
\delta^{\prime}
-potential and the scattering on localized dipole-type potentials.
In 1986, Šeba
[
22
]
argued that for any potential
V
of compact support the operators
S_{\varepsilon}
converge to the direct sum
S_{-}\oplus S_{+}
of the unperturbed half-line Schrödinger operators subject
to the Dirichlet boundary conditions at the origin. One therefore could suggest that no non-trivial interpretation of
\delta^{\prime}
-potentials is possible. However, such a conclusion contradicted the numerical analysis of
exactly solvable models with the resonant piecewise constant potentials, performed by Zolotaryuk et al.
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. We have revised Šeba’s result in
[
26
]
. Although the result remains correct for non-resonant potentials
V
, it has been shown that the resonant potentials produce the limit operator
S_{0}
defined by
S_{0}\phi=-\phi^{\prime\prime}
on functions
\phi
in
W_{2}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
obeying the interface conditions
\psi^{-}\phi(+0)-\psi^{+}\phi(-0)=0
,
\psi^{+}\phi^{\prime}(+0)-\psi^{-}\phi^{\prime}(-0)=0
.
Here
\psi
is a half-bound state of the operator
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V
on
\mathbb{R}
and
\psi^{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\psi(x)
.
In
[
27
]
, the result has been extended to potentials from the Faddeev-Marchenko class; see also

[
8
,
9
]
for the convergence of
S_{\varepsilon}
in the case
\int_{\mathbb{R}}V\,dx\neq 0
and some applications to quantum waveguides,
[
28
]
for the problem on a half-line, and

[
29
,
30
]
for the asymptotics of eigenvalues for Schrödinger operators perturbed by the
\delta^{\prime}
-like potentials.

The following statement generalizes the above results to star graphs.

Corollary 2
.

Assume that
W_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-2}V(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))
and let
\mathcal{R}_{V}
be the resonant space of
V
.
Then
H_{\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{H}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, in the norm resolvent sense, where
\mathcal{H}

acts as the negative second derivative on functions
\phi
from the domain

\mathop{\rm dom}\mathcal{H}=\left\{\phi\in W_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{G})\colon\phi(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V},\;\;\phi^{\prime}(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V}^{\perp}\right\}.

In particular, if the potential
V
is non-resonant, then

\mathcal{H}=D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{n},

where
D_{k}
is the free Schrödinger operator on
e_{k}
subject to the Dirichlet condition
\phi_{k}(a)=0
at the vertex.

Proof.

As in Corollary
1
, condition (
2.6
) is trivially fulfilled, and hence
the operators
H_{\varepsilon}
converge. The equality
ML^{+}\phi(a)-L^{*}{\phi}^{[1]}(a)=0
becomes
L^{*}\phi^{\prime}(a)=0
, since
M=0
and
{\phi}^{[1]}(a)=\phi^{\prime}(a)
. Therefore
\langle l_{1},\phi^{\prime}(a)\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}=0,\;\dots\;,\>\langle l_{r},\phi^{\prime}(a)\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}=0
, where the vectors
l_{k}
are given by (
2.4
) and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is the standard Hermitian inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. This shows that actually
\phi^{\prime}(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V}^{\perp}
, because
l_{1},\dots,l_{r}
form a basis in
\mathcal{R}_{V}
. In the case
\mathcal{R}_{V}=\{0\}
, the inclusion
\phi(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V}
says that
\phi_{k}(a)=0
for all
k=1,\dots,n
. In addition, the condition
\phi^{\prime}(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V}^{\perp}
trivially holds.
∎

The vertex conditions described in the corollary are called
scale-invariant
, because they do not couple the function values
\phi(a)
and the values of derivatives
\phi^{\prime}(a)
. Then the operator
\mathcal{H}
has no eigenvalues, and its continuous spectrum coincides with
[0,+\infty)
. There are also other properties of the operators with the scale-invariant vertex couplings, such as the factorization
of quantum graph Hamiltonians and the energy independence of vertex scattering matrices
[
4
]
.

For a
3
-edge star graph these conditions were first obtained by Man’ko
[
13
]
in the context of scattering on the
\delta^{\prime}
-like potentials. The conditions were written as

\frac{\phi_{1}(a)}{\theta_{1}}=\frac{\phi_{2}(a)}{\theta_{2}}=\frac{\phi_{3}(a)}{\theta_{3}},\qquad\theta_{1}\phi_{1}^{\prime}(a)+\theta_{2}\phi_{2}^{\prime}(a)+\theta_{3}\phi_{3}^{\prime}(a)=0

for the case of simple resonance when
\mathcal{R}_{V}
is generated by the vector
\theta=(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
and

\eta_{1}\phi_{1}(a)+\eta_{2}\phi_{2}(a)+\eta_{3}\phi_{3}(a)=0,\qquad\frac{\phi_{1}^{\prime}(a)}{\eta_{1}}=\frac{\phi_{2}^{\prime}(a)}{\eta_{2}}=\frac{\phi_{3}^{\prime}(a)}{\eta_{3}}

for the case of double resonance when the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{R}_{V}
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
is generated by the vector
\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3})
. Here we adopt the convention that
\phi_{k}(a)=0
if
\theta_{k}=0
and
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(a)=0
if
\eta_{k}=0
.

2.3.
\alpha\delta^{\prime}+\beta\delta
couplings on graphs

The proof of Corollary
2
remains valid for a more general setting. Namely, the negative second derivative operator subject to the scale-invariant vertex couplings is the limit operator also for the family
H_{\varepsilon}
with the potentials

W_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-2}V(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))+\varepsilon^{-1}U(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))

if
M=0
. In the other case, we have the following statement.

Corollary 3
.

If the potential
W_{\varepsilon}
has the form
\varepsilon^{-2}V(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))+\varepsilon^{-1}U(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))
, then the operators
H_{\varepsilon}
converge to
\mathcal{H}
in the norm resolvent sense, where
\mathcal{H}

acts as the negative second derivative on functions
\phi
from the domain

\mathop{\rm dom}\mathcal{H}=\left\{\phi\in W_{2}^{2}(\mathcal{G})\colon\phi(a)\in\mathcal{R}_{V},\;\;ML^{+}\phi(a)-L^{*}\phi^{\prime}(a)=0\right\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{H}=D_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus D_{n}
if
V
is non-resonant.

Exner and Man’ko
[
14
]
have studied the convergence of the Schrödinger operators with the potentials
\frac{\lambda(\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,V(\varepsilon^{-1}(x-a))
, where
\lambda(\varepsilon)=1+\lambda\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Their results correspond to the case when
U=\lambda V
. In our notation, the vertex couplings obtained in
[
14
]
have the form

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(a)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}l_{ij}\phi_{i}(a)=0,\quad j=r+1,\dots,n,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}(a)+\sum_{j=r+1}^{n}l_{ij}\phi_{j}^{\prime}(a)-\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{ij}\phi_{j}(a)=0,\quad i=1,\dots,r.

(2.11)

Let us show that this result is consistent with the above statement. To see this, note that conditions (
2.10
), (
2.11
) are written using the real matrix
L
of the form

L=\begin{pmatrix}\;E\;\\
L_{0}\end{pmatrix},

(2.12)

where
E
is the identity matrix of size
r
and
L_{0}
is the
(n-r)\times r
matrix with the real entries
l_{ji}
,
j=r+1,\dots,n
,
i=1,\dots,r
(see (
2.4
)). The fact is that we can choose a real-valued basis in
\Psi_{V}
(possibly after rearranging the edges of
\mathcal{G}
) for which
L
has the form (
2.12
).
To make sure that (
2.10
), (
2.11
) can be written in the form (
2.9
)
we first notice that the vectors

\displaystyle\leq c_{6}\left(\;\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{1/4})}\kern-2.0pt|x|^{-2}|\rho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,d\mathcal{G}+\kern-2.0pt\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon^{1/4},2)}\kern-2.0pt|x|^{-2}|\rho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,d\mathcal{G}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{6}\sup_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{1/4})}|\rho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\kern-2.0pt\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{1/4})}\kern-2.0pt|x|^{-2}\,d\mathcal{G}+c_{6}\sup_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon^{1/4},2)}|x|^{-2}|\rho^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\kern-2.0pt\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon^{1/4},2)}\kern-4.0ptd\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle\leq c_{7}\left(\varepsilon^{3/2}\kern-2.0pt\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{1/4})}\kern-2.0pt|x|^{-2}\,d\mathcal{G}+\varepsilon^{1/2}\right)\|f\|^{2}\leq c_{8}\varepsilon^{1/2}\|f\|^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have

(i)

y_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\to y(0)
;

(ii)

\max\limits_{x\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}|y_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq c
with constant being independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

(i)
Fix an edge
e_{k}
. Then
y_{\varepsilon,k}
is a family of solutions of the equation

-\frac{d^{2}Y}{d\tau^{2}}+\frac{q_{k}}{\tau}\,Y=\zeta Y+f(\tau)

(5.1)

for
\tau\in[\varepsilon,+\infty)
. This family admits the representation

y_{\varepsilon,k}(\tau)=b_{\varepsilon}^{k}Y_{\zeta,0}(\tau)+Y_{\zeta,f}(\tau),\quad\tau\geq\varepsilon,

(5.2)

where
b_{\varepsilon}^{k}
is a constant,
Y_{\zeta,f}
is a
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-solution of (
5.1
) and
Y_{\zeta,0}
is a
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-solution of the corresponding homogeneous equation. The solution
Y_{\zeta,0}
can be expressed in terms of the Whittaker functions (see
[
45
]
for details). In view of Proposition
1
, both
Y_{\zeta,0}
and
Y_{\zeta,f}
are bounded in a neighbourhood of the origin and have finite values
Y_{\zeta,0}(+0)
and
Y_{\zeta,f}(+0)
. Moreover
b_{\varepsilon}^{k}\to b_{0}^{k}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, because of
y_{\varepsilon,k}\to y_{k}
in
L^{2}(e_{k})
. Hence,

y_{\varepsilon,k}(a^{\varepsilon}_{k})\to b_{0}^{k}Y_{\zeta,0}(+0)+Y_{\zeta,f}(+0),\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

for each
k=1,\dots,n
. It remains to notice that
y_{k}=b_{0}^{k}Y_{\zeta,0}+Y_{\zeta,f}
on
e_{k}
.

(ii)
The function
u_{\varepsilon}(t)=y_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon t)
is a solution of the boundary value problem

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}=\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon\;\varkappa(t)u_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon U(t)u_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{2}\zeta u_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{2}f(\varepsilon t),\quad t\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}(\Gamma),\quad u_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Gamma}=y_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}.

Then the standard elliptic estimate gives us

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c_{1}\big(\|y_{\varepsilon}|_{\partial\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\|_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}+\varepsilon|\ln\varepsilon|\;\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\varepsilon^{2}\|f(\varepsilon\,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\big)\leq c_{2}.

Indeed, the norm

is bounded as

, by part
(i)
. Moreover

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c_{3}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|y_{\varepsilon}\|,\qquad\|f(\varepsilon\,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\leq c_{4}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\|f\|.

By the Sobolev imbedding theorems, we have

∎

Proposition 7
.

The function
y
is continuous at the vertex
a=0
, i.e.,

y_{1}(0)=y_{2}(0)=\cdots=y_{n}(0).

Proof.

Given
i
and
j
, we introduce the function

h_{ij}(x)=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}|x|&\text{for }x\in e_{i},\\
-|x|&\text{for }x\in e_{j},\\
\phantom{-1}0&\text{on other edges}\end{cases}

Multiplying the equation

-y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\varepsilon^{-1}\ln\varepsilon\;\varkappa\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)y_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{-1}U\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)y_{\varepsilon}=\zeta y_{\varepsilon}+f(x),\quad x\in\Gamma_{\varepsilon}

by

y_{\varepsilon}(a^{\varepsilon}_{i})-y_{\varepsilon}(a^{\varepsilon}_{j})=\varepsilon\big(y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a^{\varepsilon}_{i})-y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a^{\varepsilon}_{j})\big)\\
-\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\ln\varepsilon\,\varkappa\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+\varepsilon^{-1}U\left(\tfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)-\zeta-f(x)\right)h_{ij}(x)y_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mathcal{G}.

(5.4)

By (
3.2
) and (
5.2
), we have
|y_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a^{\varepsilon}_{k})|\leq c_{1}|\ln\varepsilon|
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for all

.
Set

. Then the estimate

\left|\int_{\Gamma_{\varepsilon}}h_{ij}(x)w_{\varepsilon}(x)\,d\mathcal{G}\right|\leq\left|\int_{e_{i}^{\varepsilon}\cup e_{j}^{\varepsilon}}|x||w_{\varepsilon}(x)|\,d\mathcal{G}\right|\leq c\varepsilon^{3/2}

.

6
.
∎

Integrating equation (

) over

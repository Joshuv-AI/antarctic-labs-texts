Nevanlinna counting functions and pull-back measures on maximal ideal space of H ∞ H^{\infty} Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author. This work is supported in part by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant Nos. 12001293, 11701422, 12171353).
Yong-Xin Gao, Yuxia Liang, and Ze-Hua Zhou*

Abstract
. In this paper we give precise characterizations of the relation between the Nevanlinna counting function and pull-back measure of an analytic self-map of the unit disk near the boundary. We show that it is quite worth considering these two concepts on the maximal ideal space of the bounded analytic functions.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 47B33; Secondary 30H05, 46J15.

1.
introduction

For a given analytic self-map
\varphi
of the unit disk
D
, the Nevanlinna counting function
N_{\varphi}
is defined as

N_{\varphi}(a)=\sum_{\varphi(z)=a}\log\frac{1}{|z|}

for
a\in\varphi(D)
. If
a\in D\backslash\varphi(D)
just define
N_{\varphi}(a)=0
. This function has its origin in value distribution theory, and plays an important role in the study of the composition operators, see
[
6
]
for example.

Another concept this paper concerns with is the pull-back measure
\mu_{\varphi}
of an analytic self-map
\varphi
. It can be defined as a Borel measure on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. Here
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
denotes the maximal ideal space of the Banach algebra of bounded analytic functions on
D
. The definition of
\mu_{\varphi}
is given in Section 3.

There is a strong connection between the Nevanlinna counting function and pull-back measure of
\varphi
. As a typical example, let’s recall some results on the properties of the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
acting on Hardy space
H^{2}
. All of these properties can be characterized in terms of either
N_{\varphi}
or
\mu_{\varphi}
.

(a). It is well known that
C_{\varphi}
is always bounded on
H^{2}
. So we can find a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(r\text{e}^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta\leqslant c\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(r\text{e}^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta

(1.1)

for any
f\in H^{2}
. The famous Carleson embedding theorem, see
[
4
]
, states that (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following: there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{W}(\zeta,h))\leqslant c^{\prime}\cdot h

for any
|\zeta|=1
and
h>0
, where
\hat{W}(\zeta,h)
denotes the closure of the Carleson window

W(\zeta,h)=\{r\text{e}^{i\theta}\in D:1-h<r<1\text{\quad}\text{and}\quad|\text{e}^{i\theta}-\zeta|<h\}

in
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. From another viewpoint, according to the Littelwood-Paley identity:

\|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi\|_{2}^{2}=|f(\varphi(0))|^{2}+2\int_{D}|f^{\prime}(w)|^{2}N_{\varphi}(w)dv(w),

the boundedness of
C_{\varphi}
can also be derived from the fact that the function
\frac{N_{\varphi}(a)}{-\log|a|}
is bounded as
|a|\to 1
.

(b). It is proved in
[
18
]
that the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
on
H^{2}
has closed range if and only if we can find constants
\delta>0
and
C>0
such that

v(T_{\delta}\cap W(\zeta,h))\geqslant C\cdot h^{2}

for all
\zeta\in\partial D
and
h>0
, where
T_{\delta}=\{z\in D:\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}>\delta\}
and
v
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
D
.
Whereas a much earlier result in
[
5
]
shows that
C_{\varphi}
has closed range if and only if there is a constant
C^{\prime}>0
such that

\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{F})\geqslant C^{\prime}\cdot\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}(F)

for any Borel subset
F
of
\partial D
. Here the meaning of
\hat{F}
is illustrated in our Section 2.

(c). The compactness of a composition operator can also be characterized by using either the Nevanlinna counting function or pull-back measure. In
[
17
]
Shapiro proves that
C_{\varphi}
is compact on
H^{2}
if and only if

\lim_{|a|\to 1}\frac{N_{\varphi}(a)}{-\log|a|}=0.

While in
[
13
]
MacCluer shows that the compactness of
C_{\varphi}
is also equivalent to the vanishing Carleson measure condition of
\mu_{\varphi}
, that is,

\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{W}(\zeta,h))=\text{o}(h)

as
h\to 0
uniformly in
\zeta\in\partial D
.

Based on the above, it would be quite interesting to investigate the connection between the Nevanlinna counting function
N_{\varphi}
and pull-back measure
\mu_{\varphi}
directly without the composition operator
C_{\varphi}
as a bridge.

In 2011, Lefèvre etc
[
12
]
revealed some relations between these two concepts near the boundary of
D
. They proved that the maximal Nevanlinna counting function and pull-back measure can be dominated by each other up to constants near the boundary. Precisely speaking, one has the following Theorem A, which is the combination of Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 in
[
12
]
.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Then for
\zeta\in\partial D
and
0<h<(1-|\varphi(0)|)/4
one has

\frac{1}{196}\sup_{W(\zeta,h/24)}N_{\varphi}\leqslant\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{W}(\zeta,h))\leqslant 64\sup_{W(\zeta,64h)}N_{\varphi}.

Later in 2016,
[
7
]
gave another proof of this theorem.

With a little more efforts,
[
12
]
also got an improvement of Theorem A: the pull-back measure of a Carleson window can actually be controlled by the average of
N_{\varphi}
on a larger Carleson window. Let
S(\zeta,h)=\{z\in D:|z-\zeta|<h\},
and
\hat{S}(\zeta,h)
be the closure of
S(\zeta,h)
in
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. Recall that
S(\zeta,h)
are equivalent choices of the Carleson windows
W(\zeta,h)
. The following Theoren B is Theorem 4.2 in
[
12
]
:

Theorem B
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Then for
\zeta\in\partial D
and
0<h<(1-|\varphi(0)|)/8
one has

\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,h))\leqslant\frac{128\times 17^{2}}{v(S(\zeta,17h))}\int_{S(\zeta,17h)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z).

In this paper, we will give a precise equality relating this two concepts on Carleson windows. As corollaries, we can get Theorem A and Theorem B easily, with much better constants.

However, we are not content with these results. From the composition operator’s properties (a) - (c) above, we can see that it should be much more important to relate the pull-back measure of
\varphi
to the function
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
other than
N_{\varphi}(z)
. This is another main aim of this paper. We will prove that the average of
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
on a Carleson window equals to the average of the pull-back measures of some other Carleson windows. As a consequence, we can show that the pull-back measure and the function
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
can be dominated by each other up to constants.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 3, we will introduce the definition of the pull-back measure
\mu_{\varphi}
on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, and prove some properties of it. Then in Section 4 we study the relationship between
N_{\varphi}
and
\mu_{\varphi}
near the boundary. We will give a precise version of Theorem B in this section, which is Theorem
4.3
. Finally in Section 5 we will investigate the connection between
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
and
\mu_{\varphi}
. Then we will present some corollaries and applications. The main result of this section is Theorem
5.3
.

2.
preliminaries

First of all, let’s introduce some notations used throughout this paper.

Let
H^{\infty}
be the space of bounded analytic functions on the unit disk
D
, and
L^{\infty}(\partial D)
be the space of
L^{\infty}
functions on the unit circle
\partial D
. Both of the spaces are equipped with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, making them to be Banach algebras.

The
maximal ideal space
of a Banach algebra is the collection of all non-zero multiplicative linear functionals on it, equipping with the weak * topology. We use
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
to denote the maximal ideal space of
H^{\infty}
. By Corona Theorem,
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
is a compact Hausdorff space that contains
D
as a dense subset.

The maximal ideal space of
L^{\infty}(\partial D)
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
. It is well known that
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
is exactly the Shilov boundary of
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, see Theorem 1.7 in Chapter V of
[
9
]
for example.

The
Gelfand transform
of a function
f\in H^{\infty}
, denoted by
\hat{f}
, is defined as

\hat{f}(m)=m(f)\quad,\quad m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}.

It is a continuous function on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
and coincides with
f
on
D
. Therefore, by identifying a function
f
with its Gelfand transform
\hat{f}
, we can regard
H^{\infty}
as a subalgebra of
C(\mathfrak{M}_{\infty})
. The Gelfand transform on
L^{\infty}(\partial D)
is defined in a similar way.

For each
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
there exists a unique positive Borel measure
\sigma_{m}
on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
such that
\sigma_{m}(\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}})=1
and

\hat{f}(m)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}}\hat{f}d\sigma_{m}

for all
f\in H^{\infty}
.
\sigma_{m}
is called the
representing measure for
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. In the rest part of this paper, we will denote the representing measure for the point
0
briefly by
\sigma
. By the Riesz representation theorem,
\sigma
is just the measure determined by the linear functional

g\mapsto\int_{0}^{2\pi}g(\text{e}^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

for
g\in L^{\infty}(\partial D)=C(\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}})
.

For any Borel subset
F\subset\partial D
, let
\chi_{F}\in L^{\infty}(\partial D)
be the characteristic function of
F
. Then
\hat{\chi}_{F}
is a continuous fuction on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
that assumes only the value
0
and
1
. Define

\hat{F}=\{m\in\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}:\hat{\chi}_{F}(m)=1\},

then it is easy to see that
\hat{F}
is a clopen subset in
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
, and
\sigma(\hat{F})=\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}(F)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Although
\hat{\chi}_{F}
is defined on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
, in what follows we will always identify
\hat{\chi}_{F}
with its Poisson integral, which is a function on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. That is,

\hat{\chi}_{F}(m)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}}\hat{\chi}_{F}d\sigma_{m}

for each
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. It is easy to see that
\hat{\chi}_{F}
is continuous on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
and harmonic on
D
. In fact, by Theorem 4.5 in Chapter II of
[
9
]
, there exists a function
h
invertible in
H^{\infty}
such that
\chi_{F}=\log|h|
almost everywhere on
\partial D
. Then on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
we have
\hat{\chi}_{F}
equals to
\log|\hat{h}|
.

For each
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we set
G_{F}(\alpha)=\{m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}:\hat{\chi}_{F}(m)\geqslant\alpha\}
, and
L_{F}(\alpha)=\{z\in D:\hat{\chi}_{F}(z)=\alpha\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose F is an arc on
\partial D
, then
z\in G_{F}(\alpha)\cap D
if and only if
z
lies in the disc (or half plane) intersect with
\partial D
in
F
, and at an angle of
\pi(1-\alpha)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
F=\{\text{e}^{i\theta}:\theta_{0}<\theta<\pi-\theta_{0}\},
where
\theta_{0}\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
. Then for
z\in D
,

\displaystyle\chi_{F}(z)=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\pi-\theta_{0}}\frac{\text{e}^{i\theta}+z}{\text{e}^{i\theta}-z}\cdot\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\arg\frac{-\text{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}-z}{\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}-z}+\frac{\theta_{0}}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}.

So
\chi_{F}(z)\geqslant\alpha
if and only if

\arg\frac{-\text{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}-z}{\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}-z}\geqslant\pi\alpha+\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta_{0}.

This means that
z
lies in the open disc intersect with
\partial D
in
F
, and at an angle
\pi(1-\alpha)

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Lemma
2.2
shows that for any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the sets
G_{F}(\alpha)
are equivalent choices of the classical Carleson windows
W(\zeta,h)
or
S(\zeta,h)
. In fact, there exist positive constants
C
and
C^{\prime}
, depending only on
\alpha
, such that for each arc
F
with centre
\zeta
and length
2h
on
\partial D
we have

\hat{W}(\zeta,Ch)\subset G_{F}(\alpha)\subset\hat{W}(\zeta,C^{\prime}h).

Note that sets
G_{F}(\alpha)
are Möbius invariant, i.e., for any automorphism
\phi
of
D
, since
\hat{\chi}_{\phi(F)}=\hat{\chi}_{F}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\phi^{-1}
, therefore

\phi(G_{F}(\alpha))=G_{\phi(F)}(\alpha).

The advantage of using
G_{F}(\alpha)
instead of
W(\zeta,h)
or
S(\zeta,h)
can be shown in the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

3.
carleson measure on maximal ideal space of
H^{\infty}

Each analytic self-map
\varphi
of
D
has a unique continuous extension as a self-map of
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, that is,

\varphi(m)(f)=m(f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi)

for
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
and
f\in H^{\infty}
. In fact, we can prove that
\varphi
is analytic on each Gleason part in the sense of the following Proposition
3.1
. Recall that the
pseudo-hyperbolic distance on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
is defined as

\rho(m_{1},m_{2})=\{|\hat{f}(m_{2})|:f\in H^{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1,\hat{f}(m_{1})=0\}

for
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. The set

P(m)=\{m^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}:\rho(m^{\prime},m)<1\}

is called
the Gleason part containing
m
. For each
a\in D
, let
L_{a}(z)=\frac{a+z}{1+\overline{a}z}
.
It was proved by Hoffman in
[
10
]
that if
\{z_{\alpha}\}
is a net in
D
converging to a point
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, then
L_{z_{\alpha}}
converges to a continuous map, denoted by
L_{m}
, in the product topology in
\left(\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}\right)^{D}
. It was also shown in
[
10
]
that
L_{m}
maps the unit disk
D
onto the Gleason part
P(m)
, and
L_{m}
is either a constant or an injection. As a consequence, each Gleason part is either a single point or an analytic disk.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Then for each
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, there exists an analytic self-map
\phi
of
D
with
\phi(0)=0
such that

\varphi\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}L_{m}(z)=L_{\varphi(m)}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\phi(z)

for
z\in D
.

Proof.

Fix
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. Suppose
\{z_{\alpha}\}
is a net in
D
that converges to
m
. Let

\phi_{\alpha}=L_{\varphi(z_{\alpha})}^{-1}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}L_{z_{\alpha}}.

Then
\phi_{\alpha}
is analytic on
D
and
\phi_{\alpha}(0)=0
. Since
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}\subset\overline{D}^{D}
which is compact, by passing to a subnet we may assume that
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}
converges to a
\phi\in\overline{D}^{D}
. By a normal family argument we can see this convergence is uniform on compact subsets in
D.
Note
\phi(0)=0
, so
\phi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Since

\varphi\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}L_{z_{\alpha}}=L_{\varphi(z_{\alpha})}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\phi_{\alpha}.

Thus the final result follows by lemma 2.2 in
[
3
]
,
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Then

\rho(\varphi(m_{1}),\varphi(m_{2}))\leqslant\rho(m_{1},m_{2})

for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. Particularly, for any
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, the set
\varphi(P(m))
is contained in
P(\varphi(m))
.

Proof.

If
\rho(\varphi(m_{1}),\varphi(m_{2}))
is
0
or
1
, the result is obvious.

Now assume that
0<\rho(m_{1},m_{2})<1
, then
m_{2}
and
m_{1}
belong to a same nontrivial Gleason part. So there exists
w\in D
such that
m_{2}=L_{m_{1}}(w)
. Thus by Proposition
3.1
, there exists an analytic self-map
\phi
of
D
such that
\phi(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\varphi(m_{2})=L_{\varphi(m_{1})}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\phi(w).

Also note that
\varphi(m_{1})=L_{\varphi(m_{1})}(0)
. So by (6.12) in
[
10
]
,

\displaystyle\rho(\varphi(m_{1}),\varphi(m_{2}))\leqslant\rho(0,\phi(w))\leqslant

\displaystyle\rho(0,w)=\rho(m_{1},m_{2}).

∎

A result of M. Behrens states that
\varphi
is an inner function if and only if
\varphi
maps
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
into itself. We now prove a stronger result here.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Let
F=\{\zeta\in\partial D:|\varphi^{*}(\zeta)|=1\}
, where
\varphi^{*}(\zeta)
represents the radial limit of
\varphi
at
\zeta
. Then
\varphi
maps
\hat{F}
into the Shilov boundary
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary inner function
u
. Then by Proposition 2.25 in
[
6
]
,
(u\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi)^{*}=u^{*}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi^{*}
almost everywhere on
\partial D
, so
|(u\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi)^{*}(\zeta)|=1
for almost every
\zeta\in F
. Therefore we have

|u\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi|\cdot\chi_{F}
equals to
\chi_{F}
as functions in
L^{\infty}(\partial D)
, hence the Gelfand transforms of
\chi_{F}\cdot|u\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi|
and
\chi_{F}
are the same. So for
m\in\hat{F}
, one has

\hat{\chi}_{F}(m)\cdot|\hat{u}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(m)|=\hat{\chi}_{F}(m)=1,

i.e.,
|\hat{u}(\varphi(m))|=1
. By a theorem of Newman, see Theorem 2.2 in Chapter V of
[
9
]
, we must have
\varphi(m)
belongs to
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
.
∎

The
pull-back measure

\mu_{\varphi}
of an analytic self-map
\varphi
is defined as

\mu_{\varphi}(E)=\sigma\left(\varphi^{-1}(E)\cap\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}\right)

for any Borel subset
E
of
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. It is easy to check that
\mu_{\varphi}
is always a Carleson measure on
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
. In fact, according to the proof of the boundness of
C_{\varphi}
on
H^{2}
, see the proof of Theorem 5.1.5 in
[
14
]
for example, we can know that for any Borel set
F\subset\partial D,

\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}}\hat{\chi}_{F}d\mu_{\varphi}\leqslant C\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}}\hat{\chi}_{F}d\sigma=C\sigma(\hat{F}),

where
C=\frac{1+|\varphi(0)|}{1-|\varphi(0)|}
. So

\displaystyle\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F}(\alpha)\right)\leqslant\frac{C}{\alpha}\int_{G_{F}(\alpha)}\hat{\chi}_{F}d\mu_{\varphi}\leqslant\frac{C}{\alpha}\cdot\sigma(\hat{F}).

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Then

\mu_{\varphi}(E)=0

whenever
E
is contained in
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}\backslash(D\cup\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}})
.

Proof.

Let
F_{n}=\{\zeta\in\partial D:|\varphi^{*}(\zeta)|<1-1/n\}
, and let
g_{n}=\varphi^{*}\cdot\hat{\chi}_{F_{n}}\in L^{\infty}(\partial D)
. Then
\|g_{n}\|_{\infty}<1
. So for any
m\in\hat{F}_{n}
, we have
|\hat{\varphi}(m)|=|\hat{g}_{n}(m)|<1
. This means that
\varphi(m)\in D
. Therefore we have
\varphi(\hat{F}_{n})
is contained in
D
.

Now let
F_{0}=\{\zeta\in\partial D:|\varphi^{*}(\zeta)|=1\}
. By Theorem
3.3
,
\varphi(\hat{F}_{0})\subset\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
. Note that
\partial D\backslash\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}
is of measure zero, so

\displaystyle\sigma(\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{F}_{n})=

\displaystyle\sigma(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{F}_{n})+\sigma(\hat{F}_{0})=1.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\varphi}\left(\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}\backslash(D\cup\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}})\right)\leqslant\sigma(\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}\backslash\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{F}_{n})=0.

∎

4.
nevanlinna counting functions near the boundary

The aim of this section is to give a precise relation between the Nevanlinna counting function and pull-back measure on Carleson windows. The main result in this section is Theorem
4.3
.

Our main tool is the following Stanton’s formula (see
[
8
]
, Theorem 2): Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
and
G
is a subharmonic function from
D
to
\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 1}\int_{\partial D}G(\varphi(r\zeta))\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}(\zeta)=G(\varphi(0))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\Delta G(z)N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z).

(4.1)

The following formula, which is Theorem 8.16 in
[
16
]
, will also be used repeatedly in this paper: Suppose
f
:
X\to[0,+\infty)
is Borel measurable on
(X,d\mu)
. Function
H
is monotonic on
[0,+\infty)
, absolutely continuous on
[0,T]
for all
T>0
, and
H(0)=0
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{X}H\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}fd\mu=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu\{f>t\}H^{\prime}(t)dt.

(4.2)

The derivatives in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are taken as distributions.

It is well known that the function
N_{\varphi}
satisfies the sub-mean value property, see Lemma 3.18 in
[
6
]
. Futhermore, it is shown in
[
1
]
that
N_{\varphi}
equals to the function

\displaystyle\overline{N}_{\varphi}(a)=-\log|a-\varphi(0)|+\int_{\partial D}\log|a-\varphi^{\ast}(\zeta)|d\sigma(\zeta)

outside a set of logarithmic capacity zero in
D
. Note that
\int_{\partial D}\log|a-\varphi^{\ast}(\zeta)|d\sigma(\zeta)
is the potential of
\mu_{\varphi}
, so by Theorem 3.1.2 in
[
15
]
,
\overline{N}_{\varphi}
is subharmonic on
D\backslash\varphi(0)
. (Also see Theorem 2.3 in
[
1
]
.)

First we begin with the following proposition. In what follows, we use
ds
to denote the Lebesgue line element on the plane.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. For a given bounded linear fractional map
f(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}
on
D
with
ad-bc=1
, let
G_{f}(t)=\{m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}:|\hat{f}(m)|\geqslant t\}
and
L_{f}(t)=\{z\in D:|f(z)|=t\}
. Define

\displaystyle J_{t}=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\int_{L_{f}(t)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{|cz+d|^{2}}ds(z).

It follows that

(i) if
\varphi(0)\in G_{f}(t)
, then

\displaystyle J_{t}=

\displaystyle-\log\frac{|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|}{t}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du,

(ii) if
\varphi(0)\notin G_{f}(t)
, then

\displaystyle J_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du.

Proof.

Take
\Phi(t)=\log t
. Fix
t_{2}>t_{1}>0
and define

H(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(t_{1}),&0\leqslant t<t_{1},\\
\Phi(t),&t_{1}\leqslant t\leqslant t_{2},\\
\Phi(t_{2}),&t>t_{2}.\end{array}\right.

By (
4.1
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}}H(|\hat{f}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi|)d\sigma=H(|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\Delta(H\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}|f|)\cdot N_{\varphi}dv.

(4.3)

The left side of (
4.3
) equals to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}}H(|\hat{f}|)d\mu_{\varphi}=

\displaystyle\Phi(t_{1})+\int_{0}^{\infty}H^{\prime}(u)\cdot\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(t_{1})+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du,

(4.4)

where the first equality follows from (
4.2
).

Now let’s deal with the right hand side of (
4.3
). First note that

\displaystyle H(|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(t_{1}),&\varphi(0)\notin G_{f}(t_{1}),\\
\Phi(|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|),&\varphi(0)\in G_{f}(t_{1})\backslash G_{f}(t_{2}),\\
\Phi(t_{2}),&\varphi(0)\in G_{f}(t_{2}).\end{array}\right.

Let
\tau=1/f
, then
\tau
maps
G_{f}(t)\cap D
onto
\overline{D(0,1/t)}\cap\tau(D)
. Since

\displaystyle\Delta(H\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}|f|)=

\displaystyle\left(H^{\prime\prime}(|f|)+\frac{H^{\prime}(|f|)}{|f|}\right)\cdot|f^{\prime}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(H^{\prime\prime}(|f|)\cdot|f|^{4}+H^{\prime}(|f|)\cdot|f|^{3}\Big)\cdot|\tau^{\prime}|^{2},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\Delta(H\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}|f|)\cdot N_{\varphi}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\Big(H^{\prime\prime}(|f|)\cdot|f|^{4}+H^{\prime}(|f|)\cdot|f|^{3}\Big)N_{\varphi}\cdot|\tau^{\prime}|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\int_{\tau(L_{\frac{1}{r}})}\left(H^{\prime\prime}(\frac{1}{r})\cdot\frac{1}{r^{4}}+H^{\prime}(\frac{1}{r})\cdot\frac{1}{r^{3}}\right)N_{\varphi}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\tau^{-1}(w)ds(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(u\cdot H^{\prime}(u)\right)^{\prime}\cdot J_{u}du,

where the last equality follows by a change of variable
u=1/r.
The sub-mean value property of
N_{\varphi}
guarantees that
J_{u}
is continuous with respect to
u
. And
\left(u\cdot H^{\prime}(u)\right)^{\prime}=\delta_{t_{1}}-\delta_{t_{2}},

where
\delta_{t_{j}}
represents the Dirca measure on
\mathbb{R}
at
t_{j}
, which is a distribution of order zero. So

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\Delta(H\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}|f|)\cdot N_{\varphi}dv=J_{t_{1}}-J_{t_{2}}.

(4.8)

Now combining (
4.3
) - (
4.8
), if
\varphi(0)\notin G_{f}(t_{1})
then

J_{t_{1}}-J_{t_{2}}=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du.

Note that
\lim_{t_{2}\to\infty}J_{t_{2}}=0
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle J_{t_{1}}=\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du.

If
\varphi(0)\in G_{f}(t_{1})\backslash G_{f}(t_{2})
, then

J_{t_{1}}-J_{t_{2}}=\Phi(t_{1})-\Phi(|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|)+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du.

So we have

\displaystyle J_{t_{1}}=-\log\frac{|f\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|}{t_{1}}+\int_{t_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du.

∎

As a simple application, we can calculate the integration of
N_{\varphi}(z)
on any circle or disk contained in
D
in terms of the pull-back measure, which is the main result in
[
11
]
. Note that the proof in
[
11
]
is quite different form ours here.

Corollary 4.2

(Theorem 3.1 in
[
11
]
)
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Then

(i)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}N_{\varphi}(r\text{e}^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}=-\log^{+}\frac{|\varphi(0)|}{r}+\int_{r}^{1}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}\backslash D(0,u))}{u}du,

where
\log^{+}x=\max\{0,\log x\}
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{D(0,r)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)=r^{2}\overline{N}_{\varphi}(0)+r^{2}\int_{D(0,r)}\left(\frac{|z|^{2}}{2r^{2}}-\log\frac{|z|}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)d\mu_{\varphi}(z)

for
0<r<|\varphi(0)|.

Proof.

Take
f(z)=z
in Proposition
4.1
, then
G_{f}(t)=\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}\backslash D(0,t)
and

\displaystyle J_{t}=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\int_{|z|=t}N_{\varphi}(z)ds(z).

Then (
i
) follows directly from Proposition
4.1
.

For (
ii
), since
\varphi(0)\in G_{f}(r)
, so for any
r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
we have
\varphi(0)\in G_{f}(r^{\prime})
, whence by Proposition
4.1
we have

\displaystyle J_{r}-J_{r^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\log\frac{r}{r^{\prime}}-\int_{r^{\prime}}^{r}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r^{\prime}}^{r}\frac{D(0,u)}{u}du.

Note that
\lim_{r^{\prime}\to 0}J_{r^{\prime}}=\overline{N}_{\varphi}(0)
, so

\displaystyle J_{r}=\overline{N}_{\varphi}(0)+\int_{0}^{r}\frac{D(0,u)}{u}du.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{D(a,r)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{r}tJ_{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{r}\left[t\overline{N}_{\varphi}(0)+t\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(D(0,u))}{u}du\right]dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\overline{N}_{\varphi}(0)+\int_{0}^{r}\frac{r^{2}-u^{2}}{u}\mu_{\varphi}(D(0,u)))du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{2}\overline{N}_{\varphi}(0)+r^{2}\int_{D(0,r)}\left(\frac{|z|^{2}}{2r^{2}}-\log\frac{|z|}{r}-\frac{1}{2}\right)d\mu_{\varphi},

where the last equality follows from (
4.2
).

∎

We are more interested in the integrations of
N_{\varphi}
on Carleson windows, which can also be calculated with the help of Proposition
4.1
. Recall that
S(\zeta,h)=\{z\in D:|z-\zeta|<h\}
. Now we can give our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. If
\varphi(0)\notin S(\zeta,h)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{S(\zeta,h)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)=\int_{0}^{h}\frac{h^{2}-u^{2}}{u}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,u))du.

Proof.

Take
f(z)=z-\zeta
in Proposition
4.1
, then
G_{f}(h)=\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}\backslash\hat{S}(\zeta,h)
. So

J_{h}=\frac{1}{2\pi h}\int_{\{z\in D:|z-\zeta|=h\}}N_{\varphi}(z)ds(z).

Now if we take
h^{\prime}\in(0,h)
, then by Proposition
4.1
we have

\displaystyle J_{h^{\prime}}-J_{h}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}+\int_{h^{\prime}}^{h}\frac{\mu_{\phi}(G_{f}(u))}{u}du

\displaystyle=-\int_{h^{\prime}}^{h}\frac{\mu_{\phi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,u))}{u}du.

Since
\lim_{|z|\to 1}N_{\varphi}(z)=0
, so
J_{h^{\prime}}\to 0
as
h^{\prime}\to 0
. Therefore we have

J_{h}=\int_{0}^{h}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}\left(\hat{S}(\zeta,u)\right)}{u}du.

And then

\displaystyle\int_{S(\zeta,h)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{h}tJ_{t}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{h}dt\int_{0}^{t}\frac{t\cdot\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,u))}{u}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}\frac{h^{2}-u^{2}}{u}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,u))du.

∎

As a corollary, Theorem B (Theorem 4.2 in
[
12
]
) can be got easily from our Theorem
4.3
, with much better constants.

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. If
\varphi(0)\notin S(\zeta,2h)
, then

\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,h))<\frac{2}{v(S(\zeta,2h))}\int_{S(\zeta,2h)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)

Proof.

It is easy to see that
v(S(\zeta,2h))
is less than
2h^{2}
, so according to Theorem
4.3
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(S(\zeta,2h))}\int_{S(\zeta,2h)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\int_{h}^{2h}\frac{4h^{2}-u^{2}}{u}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,u))du

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,h))}{2h^{2}}\int_{h}^{2h}\frac{4h^{2}-u^{2}}{u}du

\displaystyle>\frac{\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,h))}{2}.

∎

5.
average of
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
near the boundary

As we have mentioned in Section 1, the behavior of
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
near the boundary should be more closely related to the pull-back measure
\mu_{\varphi}
. Since
\frac{-\log|z|}{1-|z|}\to 1
as
|z|\to 1
, we may use the function
\frac{2N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}
instead of
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
in some cases, for the sake of simplifying the calculations. The main result of this section is Theorem
5.3
.

To investigate the relationship between the function
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}
and measure
\mu_{\varphi}
, it is more convenience to use the Carleson windows of the form
G_{F}(\alpha)
, other than
W(\zeta,h)
or
S(\zeta,h)
. Recall that for a Borel set
F\subset\partial D
, we set
L_{F}(\alpha)=\{z\in D:\hat{\chi}_{F}(z)=\alpha\}
and
G_{F}(\alpha)=\{m\in\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}:\hat{\chi}_{F}(z)\geqslant\alpha\}
.

Before we can prove Theorem
5.3
, we need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
F
be an arc on
\partial D
. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, on
L_{F}(\alpha)
one has

\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\chi}_{F}}{\partial z}\right|=\frac{\sin\pi\alpha}{\pi(1-|z|^{2})}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
F=\{\text{e}^{i\theta}:\theta_{0}<\theta<\pi-\theta_{0}\}
where
\theta\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
. Then

\hat{\chi}_{F}(z)=\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\pi-\theta_{0}}P_{z}(\text{e}^{i\theta})\frac{d\theta}{2\pi},

where the Possion kernel
P_{z}(\text{e}^{i\theta})=\text{Re}\frac{\text{e}^{i\theta}+z}{\text{e}^{i\theta}-z}.
Notice that
\frac{\partial P_{z}(\text{e}^{i\theta})}{\partial z}=\frac{\text{e}^{i\theta}}{(\text{e}^{i\theta}-z)^{2}}
, thus

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{\chi}_{F}}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\pi-\theta_{0}}\frac{\text{e}^{i\theta}}{(\text{e}^{i\theta}-z)^{2}}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos\theta_{0}}{\pi i}\frac{1}{(\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}-z)(\text{e}^{-i\theta}+z)}.

If
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\theta_{0}}{\pi}
, such that
L_{F}(\alpha)
is the segment joint
-\text{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}
and
\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}
, we have

(\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}-z)(\text{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}+z)=1-|z|^{2}

for
z\in L_{F}(\alpha)
. So

\frac{\partial\hat{\chi}_{F}}{\partial z}=\frac{\cos\theta_{0}}{\pi i}\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}

on
L_{F}(\alpha)
.

For the general
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we may take
\phi
to be the automorphism with fixed points
\pm i
such that
\phi
maps
-\text{e}^{-i\theta_{0}}
and
\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}
to
-\text{e}^{-i\tilde{\theta}_{0}}
and
\text{e}^{i\tilde{\theta}_{0}}
respectively, where
\tilde{\theta}_{0}=\pi\alpha-\pi/2
. Let
\tilde{F}=\{\text{e}^{i\theta}:\tilde{\theta}_{0}<\theta<\pi-\tilde{\theta}_{0}\}
. Then
\phi(L_{F}(\alpha))=L_{\tilde{F}}(\alpha)
, which is a segment. So if we write
w=\phi(z)
, then for
z\in L_{F}(\alpha)
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\chi}_{F}}{\partial z}(z)\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\chi}_{\tilde{F}}}{\partial w}(w)\right|\cdot|\varphi^{\prime}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\tilde{\theta}_{0}}{\pi(1-|w|^{2})}\cdot\frac{1-|w|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\pi\alpha}{\pi(1-|z|^{2})},

where the first equality follows from the fact that
\hat{\chi}_{F}=\hat{\chi}_{\tilde{F}}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\phi
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
F
be an arc on
\partial D
. Then for any non-negative function
g\in L^{1}(D)
, we have

\int_{D}gdv=\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\frac{g(z)(1-|z|^{2})}{2\sin\pi\alpha}ds(z).

Proof.

Assume that
F=\{\text{e}^{i\theta}:\theta_{0}<\theta<\pi-\theta_{0}\}.
Let
\tau(z)=\frac{1+\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}z}{\text{e}^{i\theta_{0}}-z}
, then
\tau
maps
D
onto the half-plane
\Pi^{+}=\{z:\text{Re}z>0\}
, and
\tau(L_{F}(\alpha))=\{r\text{e}^{i\theta}:\theta=\pi\alpha-\pi/2\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{D}gdv=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}d\theta\int_{0}^{\infty}g\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\tau^{-1}(r\text{e}^{i\theta})\cdot\left|(\tau^{-1})^{\prime}(r\text{e}^{i\theta})\right|^{2}\cdot rdr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}g\cdot\omega,

where

\displaystyle\omega=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left|\tau^{\prime}(z)\right|^{-2}d|\tau(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|(1+\text{e}^{i\theta}z)(\text{e}^{i\theta}-z)|}{2\cos\theta_{0}}ds(z)

on
L_{F}(\alpha)
. According to the proof of Lemma
5.1
, for
z\in L_{F}(\alpha)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{|(1+\text{e}^{i\theta}z)(\text{e}^{i\theta}-z)|}{2\cos\theta_{0}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\left|\frac{\partial\hat{\chi}_{F}}{\partial z}(z)\right|^{-1}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{2\sin\pi\alpha}.

So

\displaystyle\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}g\cdot\omega=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sin\pi\alpha}\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}g(z)(1-|z|^{2})ds(z),

hence

\int_{D}gdv=\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\frac{g(z)(1-|z|^{2})}{2\sin\pi\alpha}ds(z).

∎

The following theorem states that the average of the function
\frac{2N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}
on Carleson window
G_{F}(\alpha)
equals to the average of the pull-back measures of
G_{F}(u)
for
u\in(\alpha,1)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose
\varphi
an analytic self-map of
D
and
F
is an arc on
\partial D
. Then for
\alpha>\hat{\chi}_{F}(\varphi(0))
we have

\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}ds(z)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\sin\pi\alpha}\int_{\alpha}^{1}\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F}(u)\right)du.

Proof.

Assume that
F=\{\text{e}^{i\theta}:\theta_{0}<\theta<\pi-\theta_{0}\}
. Fix
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
such that
\hat{\chi}_{F}(\varphi(0))<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<1
, and let

I(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{1},&0\leqslant\alpha<\alpha_{1},\\
\alpha,&\alpha_{1}\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\alpha_{2},\\
\alpha_{2},&\alpha>\alpha_{2}.\end{array}\right.

Then by (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}}I\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\hat{\chi}_{F}d\mu_{\varphi}=\alpha_{1}+\int_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F}(u)\right)du.

(5.1)

Since
\varphi(0)\notin G_{F}(\alpha_{1}),
then
I\left(|\chi_{F}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|\right)=\alpha_{1}
. So we have

\displaystyle\left(|\chi_{F}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)|\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{D}\Delta(I\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\chi_{F})\cdot N_{\varphi}dv=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+2\int_{D}I^{\prime\prime}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\chi_{F}\cdot\left|\frac{\partial\chi_{F}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}N_{\varphi}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+\int_{0}^{1}I^{\prime\prime}(\alpha)\cdot J_{\alpha}d\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}+J_{\alpha_{1}}-J_{\alpha_{2}}.

(5.2)

where

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle 2\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\left|\frac{\partial\chi_{F}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}N_{\varphi}\cdot\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{2\sin\pi\alpha}ds(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\pi\alpha}{\pi^{2}}\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}ds(z).

Notice that the last equality of (
5
) relays on that
J_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to
\alpha
. This will be illustrated in the last part of this proof.

By (
4.1
), the equations (
5.1
) and (
5
) should be equal to each other, i.e.,

J_{\alpha_{1}}-J_{\alpha_{2}}=\int_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F}(u)\right)du.

Note that
\lim_{\alpha_{2}\to 1}J_{\alpha_{2}}=0
since
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}
is bounded near the boundary. So we have

J_{\alpha}=\int_{\alpha}^{1}\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F}(u)\right)du

for
\alpha>\hat{\chi}_{F}(\varphi(0))
.

Now let’s show that
J_{\alpha}
is continuous for
\alpha>\hat{\chi}_{F}(\varphi(0))
. Let
\tau
be the same map as in the proof of Lemma
5.2
. Then

J_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{N}_{\varphi}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\tau^{-1}(r\text{e}^{i\theta})}{r}dr,

where
\theta=\pi\alpha-\pi/2
. Let
\eta(z)=\log z
, which is bi-holomorphic from the half-plane
\Pi^{+}=\{z:\text{Re}z>0\}
onto the strip
T=\{z:-\frac{\pi}{2}<\text{Im}z<\frac{\pi}{2}\}
. Then

J_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{N}_{\varphi}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\tau^{-1}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\eta^{-1}(t+i\theta)dt.

Then it is clear that
J_{\alpha}
is continuous with respect to
\theta
(as well as
\alpha
), since
\overline{N}_{\varphi}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\tau^{-1}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\eta^{-1}
is subharmonic on
T\backslash\{\eta\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\tau\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\varphi(0)\}.

∎

As a corollary, we now prove that the average of
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
on
S(\zeta,h)
can also be dominated by the pull-back measure. Remark
5.6
shows that this corollary is an improvement of Theorem A.

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an analytic self-map of
D
. Assume
\zeta\in\partial D
and
h\in(0,1).
If
\varphi(0)\notin S(\zeta,h)
, then there exists constant
c_{h}>1
depending only on
h
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(S(\zeta,h))}\int_{S(\zeta,h)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}dv(z)\leqslant c_{h}\cdot\frac{\int_{0}^{h}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt}{\int_{0}^{h}\sigma(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt}.

(5.3)

Moreover, we can further more require that
\lim_{h\to 0}c_{h}=1
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the result to the function
\frac{2N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}
instead of
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
.

For
t\in(0,h)
we have
\hat{S}(\zeta,t)=G_{F_{t}}(\alpha_{t})
where
F_{t}
is the arc
\partial D\cap\overline{S(\zeta,t)}
, and it is easy to see that
\alpha_{t}\to\frac{1}{2}
as
t\to 0
. So by Theorem
5.3
we have

\displaystyle\int_{S(\zeta,h)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}dv(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{h}dt\int_{L_{F_{t}}(\alpha_{t})}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}ds(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\int_{0}^{h}dt\int_{\alpha_{t}}^{1}\frac{\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F_{t}}(u)\right)}{\sin\pi\alpha_{t}}du

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\pi\int_{0}^{h}\frac{1-\alpha_{t}}{\sin\pi\alpha_{t}}\cdot\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{c_{h}\pi}{2}\int_{0}^{h}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt,

where
\lim_{h\to 0}c_{h}=1
.
Also note that

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{V(S(\zeta,h))}{h^{2}/2}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\sigma(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))}{t/\pi}=1,

so by taking the constant
c_{h}
slight larger, we have

\frac{1}{v(S(\zeta,h))}\int_{S(\zeta,h)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}dv(z)\leqslant c_{h}\cdot\frac{\int_{0}^{h}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt}{\int_{0}^{h}\sigma(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt}.

∎

Example 5.5
.

By taking
\varphi
to be an inner function with
\varphi(0)=0
, we can see estimation (
5.3
) is sharp. In fact, by Lemma 3.27 in
[
6
]
,
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
equals
1
almost everywhere in
D
in this situation. So the left side of (
5.3
) is
1
for any
\zeta\in\partial D
and
h\in(0,1)
such that
\varphi(0)\notin S(\zeta,h)
. On the other hand, by Theorem 3.8
[
6
]
we can know that
C_{\varphi}
is an isometry on
H^{2}
. Therefore we have
\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))=\sigma(\hat{S}(\zeta,t)).
So the right side of (
5.3
) equals to
c_{h}
, which converges to
1
.

Remark 5.6
.

Note that Corollary
5.4
is stronger than the results in
[
12
]
since Theorem A can be got from this corollary with better constants. In fact, let
a=(1-h/2)\zeta
, then
D(a,h/2)\subset S(\zeta,h)
.
According to the sub-mean value property of
N_{\varphi}
and the proof of Corollary
5.4
we can know that

\displaystyle N_{\varphi}(a)\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(D(a,h/2))}\int_{D(a,h/2)}N_{\varphi}(z)dv(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{8}{h}\int_{S(\zeta,h)}\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}dv(z)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi c_{h}}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,t))dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 4\pi c_{h}\cdot\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,h)).

So

\displaystyle\sup_{S(\zeta,h)}N_{\varphi}\leqslant 4\pi c_{h}\cdot\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{S}(\zeta,2h)),

where
\lim_{h\to 0}c_{h}=1
.

Finally, we’d like to point out that Theorem
5.3
sheds a light on the behavior of the function
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
on the Shilov boundary
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
: we strongly suspect that
\frac{N_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
is well defined and continuous on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
. More precisely, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture
.

Let
\tau_{\varphi}(z)=\frac{\overline{N}_{\varphi}(z)}{-\log|z|}
. Then the followings hold.

(i) Suppose
m\in\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
and
\{z_{\alpha}\}
is a net in
D
such that
z_{\alpha}\to m
in
\mathfrak{M}_{\infty}
, then
\tau(z_{\alpha})
converges. So if we define this limitation the value of
\tau_{\varphi}
at
m
, then
\tau_{\varphi}
is well defined on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
.

(ii) Moreover,
\tau_{\varphi}
is continuous on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
, and the restriction of
\tau_{\varphi}
on
\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}
is exactly the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
\mu_{\varphi}|_{\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}}
with respect to
\sigma
.

In fact, by Theorem
5.3
, for any Borel set
F\subset\partial D
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\tau_{\varphi}(z)ds(z)=

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\frac{2N_{\varphi}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}ds(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\sin\pi\alpha}\int_{\alpha}^{1}\mu_{\varphi}\left(G_{F}(u)\right)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\mu_{\varphi}\left(\bigcap_{u=\alpha}^{1}G_{F}(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{F}),

where the last equality follows from Proposition
3.4
. If the statement (
i
) of this conjecture is true, since
\tau_{\varphi}
is upper semi-continuous then by a similar way with the proof of Theorem 7 in
[
2
]
,
\tau_{\varphi}
has radial limit almost everywhere on
\partial D
. So

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 1}\int_{L_{F}(\alpha)}\tau_{\varphi}(z)ds(z)=\int_{F}\tau_{\varphi}(\text{e}^{i\theta})d\theta.

As a consequence we have

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{F}}\tau_{\varphi}d\sigma=\mu_{\varphi}(\hat{F}).

(5.4)

By Theorem 4.3 of Chapter V in
[
9
]
,
L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}},d\sigma)=C(\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}}).
So (
5.4
) implies that
\tau_{\varphi}
equals to
d\mu_{\varphi}/d\sigma
as functions in
C(\mathfrak{M}_{L^{\infty}})
. Therefore, if one can prove the first part of this conjecture, then the second part follows directly as a corollary of Theorem
5.3
. Note that the second part of this conjecture gives a fabulous explanation why the Nevanlinna counting function and pull-back measure are so closely related with each other.

Reconstruction of cracks in Calderón’s inverse conductivity problem using energy comparisons
Henrik Garde, Michael S. Vogelius

Abstract
. We derive exact reconstruction methods for cracks consisting of unions of Lipschitz hypersurfaces in the context of Calderón’s inverse conductivity problem. Our first method obtains upper bounds for the unknown cracks, bounds that can be shrunk to obtain the exact crack locations upon verifying certain operator inequalities for differences of the local Neumann-to-Dirichlet maps. This method can simultaneously handle perfectly insulating and perfectly conducting cracks, and it appears to be the first rigorous reconstruction method capable of this. Our second method assumes that only perfectly insulating cracks or only perfectly conducting cracks are present. Once more using operator inequalities, this method generates approximate cracks that are guaranteed to be subsets of the unknown cracks that are being reconstructed.



Keywords: Calderón’s problem,
electrical impedance tomography,
cracks,
inclusion detection.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R30, 35R05, 47H05.

1.1.
Some related results

For large classes of cracks there are optimal (non-constructive) unique identifiability results using merely two boundary measurements
[
2
,
4
,
9
]
. However, the
reconstruction of general cracks
from finitely many measurements appears to be an open problem. For spatial dimension
d=2
there is a factorisation method
[
3
]
to reconstruct
either
perfectly insulating cracks
or
perfectly conducting cracks from an ND map. The results of
[
3
]
resemble the two-dimensional version of Theorem
1.3
, in the sense that one determines if an artificial crack is a subset of the unknown crack. For perfectly insulating cracks, probe and enclosure methods are discussed in
[
22
,
23
]
. These methods determine the location of “boundary singularities” associated with the cracks or the convex hull of the cracks, respectively. It should also be mentioned that these reconstruction methods assume that
\gamma_{0}\equiv 1
.

Our results Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
are applicable in spatial dimension
d\geq 2
for very general cracks and also for non-homogeneous background conductivities. It appears that Theorem
1.2
is the only proven method capable of reconstructing cracks with both perfectly insulating and perfectly conducting parts. Moreover, the test operators
\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}
and
\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}
are precisely the same test operators that are used in
[
6
,
12
]
for reconstructing general inclusions of positive volume (the support of conductivity perturbations on open sets). So numerical implementations of this method directly applies to cracks without requiring any modifications. Arguably Theorem
1.3
becomes demanding to implement numerically for
d>2
and is likely only suitable for computations in
d=2
, while the numerical implementation of Theorem
1.2
very naturally generalises to higher dimensions using a peeling approach as in
[
14
]
.

For inclusions of positive volume, the monotonicity of ND mappings with respect to the conductivity coefficient was used in
[
29
]
to give bounds on the inclusions. In
[
19
,
20
]
this approach was proven to give an exact reconstruction method for finite perturbations to the background conductivity, also in cases with both positive and negative perturbations if lower and upper bounds are known for the perturbed conductivity. This was generalised in
[
6
]
, where the perturbed conductivity could now simultaneously have parts with finite positive and negative perturbations as well as extreme parts that are perfectly insulating and perfectly conducting. Moreover, the need for lower and upper bounds was removed. Finally, in
[
12
]
the method was shown to also handle degenerate and singular perturbations based on
A_{2}
-Muckenhoupt weights, allowing for continuous decay to zero and growth to infinity. This is currently the most general method for reconstructing inclusions of positive volume in Calderón’s problem based on a local ND map. Rigorous connections have also been made to practically relevant electrode models
[
13
,
14
,
17
,
21
]
.

The same monotonicity-based methodology has also been used to design a reconstruction method for the partial data Calderón problem, for the case of layered piecewise constant conductivities
[
10
,
11
]
; note that this is not a finite-dimensional setting, as the piecewise constant partitioning is also reconstructed. In finite-dimensional settings this methodology has led to Lipschitz stability results in Calderón’s problem with finitely many measurements
[
17
]
and to reformulating the finite-dimensional Calderón problem as a convex semidefinite optimisation problem
[
18
]
. The methods are based on the unique continuation principle and its connection to the localised potentials from
[
15
]
. For applications to other inverse problems, we refer to the list of references in
[
17
]
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
also rely on localised potentials. However, the approach here is quite different since the known localisation results apply to open sets, and there is no open set inside the cracks to localise in. Moreover, the monotonicity inequalities in
[
6
, Appendix A]
become trivial in the limit of approximating cracks by open sets. Additionally, it should be noted that using approximations of cracks with open sets and applying the results from
[
6
]
will not result in proofs of our main results. The key ingredient for our proofs turns out to be the localisation on open sets containing parts of the cracks, to give simultaneous blow-up of potentials for conductivity profiles both with and without the cracks, and crucially also to have blow-up for their difference. The latter is the most technically difficult to obtain, and here the constructive version of localised potentials
[
15
, Lemma 2.8]
turns out to be invaluable, since the existence results for localised potentials would not necessarily give blow-up for the difference of two localised sequences of potentials.

Forward problems

Let
\Sigma

(\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{\infty})

\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}=\{v\in H_{\diamond}^{1}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{0}})\mid\text{$v$ is locally constant on $\Sigma_{\infty}$}\},

where the

, see for instance

on the entire domain

\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
, equivalent to the

-norm, is

\lVert v\rVert_{*}=\Bigl(\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr)^{1/2},

with associated inner product
1
1

1

Throughout this paper we assume, without loss of generality, that all our solutions and function spaces are real-valued.

\langle w,v\rangle_{*}=\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the Lax–Milgram lemma, the interior electric potential
u
in (
1
), with
D_{0}=\Sigma_{0}
and
D_{\infty}=\Sigma_{\infty}
, is the unique solution in
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
of the weak problem

\langle u,v\rangle_{*}=\langle f,v|_{\Gamma}\rangle,\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}.

(2.1)

We will sometimes use the notation
u=u_{\Sigma_{0},f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
to clarify which cracks (possibly empty) are present, and which current density,

, is used. Define the functional
J:\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}\to\mathbb{R}
by

J(v)=\lVert v\rVert_{*}^{2}-2\langle f,v|_{\Gamma}\rangle,\qquad v\in\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}.

With this definition,

is also the unique minimiser of

(cf.
[
16
, Remark 12.23]
and
[
7
, Theorem 1.1.2]
). The corresponding local ND map
\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}:f\mapsto u|_{\Gamma}
satisfies

\langle\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}f,f\rangle=\langle u|_{\Gamma},f\rangle=\langle f,u|_{\Gamma}\rangle=\lVert u\rVert_{*}^{2},

and is a compact self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
.

For
C\in\mathcal{A}
and
f\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
, let
u_{C,f}^{\emptyset}
be the potential coming from a conductivity with perfectly insulating inclusions in
C
and
\gamma_{0}
elsewhere. Then
u_{0}=u_{C,f}^{\emptyset}
is the unique solution in

\mathcal{H}_{C}^{\emptyset}=H_{\diamond}^{1}(\Omega\setminus C)

of the variational problem

\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x=\langle f,v|_{\Gamma}\rangle,\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}_{C}^{\emptyset}.

Likewise, let
u_{\emptyset,f}^{C}
be the potential coming from a conductivity with perfectly conducting inclusions in
C
and
\gamma_{0}
elsewhere. Then
u_{\infty}=u_{\emptyset,f}^{C}
is the unique solution in

\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{C}=\{w\in H_{\diamond}^{1}(\Omega)\mid w\text{ is locally constant in }C\}

of the variational problem

\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\nabla u_{\infty}\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x=\langle f,v|_{\Gamma}\rangle,\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{C}.

In terms of the functional

\widetilde{J}(v)=\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,v|_{\Gamma}\rangle,

u_{0}
is the unique minimizer of
\widetilde{J}|_{\mathcal{H}_{C}^{\emptyset}}
and
u_{\infty}
is the unique minimizer of
\widetilde{J}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{C}}
. The corresponding ND maps are compact self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
and satisfy

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0}|_{\Gamma},f\rangle=\langle f,u_{0}|_{\Gamma}\rangle=\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u_{0}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{\infty}|_{\Gamma},f\rangle=\langle f,u_{\infty}|_{\Gamma}\rangle=\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u_{\infty}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma

(\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{\infty})
collection of cracks. Given any

, then

\Sigma\subset C\qquad\text{implies}\qquad\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}\geq\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}.

Proof.

For

denote
u_{0}=u_{C,f}^{\emptyset}
,
u_{\infty}=u_{\emptyset,f}^{C}
, and
u=u_{\Sigma_{0},f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
. Since
\Sigma\subset C
we have
u|_{\Omega\setminus C}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}^{\emptyset}
and
u_{\infty}|_{\Omega\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{0}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
. We now compare the ND maps, while taking the related minimization properties from section
2
into account

\displaystyle-\langle\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,u|_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,u|_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u_{0}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,u_{0}|_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}f,f\rangle,

and

\displaystyle-\langle\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus C}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u_{\infty}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,u_{\infty}|_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u_{\infty}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,u_{\infty}|_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\langle f,u|_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}f,f\rangle.

Proposition 3.2
.

\Sigma

(i)

(ii)

For

. Since

Let

and
P^{\perp}
be the orthogonal projections of
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}})^{\perp}
, respectively, in the
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{*}
inner product. Likewise we let
Q
and
Q^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal projections of
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\emptyset}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}})^{\perp}
, respectively, in the
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{*}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
(\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{\infty})
collection of cracks and let the projections
P
,
P^{\perp}
,
Q
, and
Q^{\perp}
be given as above. For

we denote
u_{0}=u_{\Sigma_{0},f}^{\emptyset}
,
u_{\infty}=u_{\emptyset,f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
, and

.

(i)

We have
u_{\infty}=Pu

\langle(\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}})f,f\rangle=\lVert P^{\perp}u\rVert_{*}^{2}.

(ii)

We have

\langle(\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}})f,f\rangle=\lVert Q^{\perp}u_{0}\rVert_{*}^{2}.

Proof of (i): From the weak formulation for

However, by the weak formulation for

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\langle f,w|_{\Gamma}\rangle=\langle u,w\rangle_{*}=\langle\nabla u|_{V},F\rangle_{L^{2}(V)^{d}}.

(5.2)

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

and let

(ii)

Proof.

We give a proof of (i), and note that the proof of (ii) is almost the same with obvious modifications.

Proof of “
\subseteq
”: Let

, then there exists
F_{1}\in L^{2}(V)^{d}
such that

with

. We have

(5.3)

Let
W_{1},W_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\Sigma_{0}\Subset W_{1}\Subset W_{2}\subseteq V

-Urysohn lemma
[
8
, Lemma 8.18]
, we may construct
\varphi\in C_{\textup{c}}^{\infty}(W_{2})
with

\varphi\equiv 1
in
W_{1}
. In the following we will let

be arbitrary but fixed, and let

has vanishing mean on
\partial V
. Moreover, we also define

We note that

(extended by zero to
{\overline{\Sigma_{0}}}

5.3

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{V}\gamma_{0}\nabla{w}\cdot\nabla v_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega\setminus V}\gamma_{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{V}\gamma_{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\gamma_{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{V}\gamma_{0}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x

Define

and

where

Since

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
(\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{\infty})
collection of cracks. Assume that
\gamma_{0}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
and is positive.

(i)

If
\Sigma_{0}\neq\emptyset

such that
u_{\emptyset,f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}\neq u_{\Sigma_{0},f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
.

(ii)

then there exists

such that
u_{\Sigma_{0},f}^{\emptyset}\neq u_{\Sigma_{0},f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}

Proof.

Proof of (i): It suffices to prove that there exists an
f\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial n}u_{\emptyset,f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}\text{ does not vanish identically on }\Sigma_{0}.

Let
\Phi(x,y)
denote the (fundamental) solution to the problem

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\gamma_{0}\nabla\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y))

\displaystyle=\delta_{y}\quad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{\infty}},

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|\partial\Omega|}\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\hskip-62.59596pt\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y)\text{ is locally constant on }\Sigma_{\infty},

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{i}}\Bigl[\gamma_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,y)\Bigr]\mathrm{d}S

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{for each component $\Sigma_{i}$ of $\Sigma_{\infty}$}.

In terms of this
\Phi
we have the representation formula

u_{\emptyset,f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(y)=\int_{\Gamma}\Phi(x,y)f(x)\,\mathrm{d}S_{x},

and so

\frac{\partial}{\partial n}u_{\emptyset,f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(y)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Phi(x,y)f(x)\,\mathrm{d}S_{x}\quad\text{on }\Sigma_{0}.

Now suppose that
\frac{\partial}{\partial n}u_{\emptyset,f}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
vanishes identically on
\Sigma_{0}
for all
f\in L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
. Let
\psi
be a non-zero continuous function on
\Sigma_{0}
, supported away from
\partial\Sigma_{0}
, then it would follow that

0=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\psi(y)\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Phi(x,y)f(x)\,\mathrm{d}S_{x}\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}=\int_{\Gamma}f(x)\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Phi(x,y)\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}\,\mathrm{d}S_{x}

for all

. In other words, there is a constant
C
such that

\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Phi(x,y)\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}\equiv C\quad\text{on }\Gamma.

We also have that

\gamma_{0}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Phi(x,y)\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}=\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Bigl(\gamma_{0}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{x}}\Phi(x,y)\Bigr)\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}\equiv 0\quad\text{on }\Gamma.

By unique continuation of the operator
\nabla\cdot(\gamma_{0}\nabla(\,\cdot\,))
it follows that

\int_{\Sigma_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial n_{y}}\Phi(x,y)\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}S_{y}\equiv C\quad\text{in }\Omega\setminus\overline{\Sigma_{0}}.

The jump relation for double layer potentials (see
[

This is contrary to the initial assumption that
\psi
is non-zero.

5.9
) and (
5.10
) this
g
satisfies
g\in R(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(V))
and
g\not\in R(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y))
. Moreover,
(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y))^{*}
is injective by (
5.2
) and unique continuation. We then apply the constructive result on localised potentials in Lemma
4.4
, and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

\xi_{n}=\bigl(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y)(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y))^{*}+\tfrac{1}{n}I\bigr)^{-1}g

and

f_{n}=\frac{\xi_{n}}{\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y))^{*}\xi_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(Y)^{d}}^{3/2}}.

Lemma
4.4
implies that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(Y)^{d}}

\displaystyle=0,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(V))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\infty,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\text{and }~\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert A^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\infty,

(5.14)

simultaneously. Since
\Sigma_{\infty}\Subset Y
, Proposition
5.1
(ii) gives that
R(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(Y))=R(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(Y))
. This range equality, together with (
5.12
) and Lemma
4.3
, implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(Y))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(Y)^{d}}=0.

(5.15)

From Proposition
5.1
(ii) we have
R(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(V\cup Y))=R(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(V\cup Y))
. So from (
5.2
) and Lemma
4.3
there exists a
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(V))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V)^{d}}^{2}+\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(Y))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(Y)^{d}}^{2}\hskip-85.35826pt

\displaystyle=\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(V\cup Y))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V\cup Y)^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(V\cup Y))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V\cup Y)^{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq c\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}(V))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V)^{d}}^{2}.

(5.16)

A combination of (
5.13
), (
5.15
), and (
5.16
) gives

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lVert(L_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}(V))^{*}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}(V)^{d}}=\infty.

(5.17)

We are now ready to associate the different operators to differences of ND mappings. Let
\widehat{u}_{n}=u_{\emptyset,f_{n}}^{\emptyset}
. Then (
5.2
), (
5.15
), and (
5.17
) translate into

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{V}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{n}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{n}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(5.18)

Using
[
6
, Lemma A.1(ii)]
, we obtain the existence of a constant
K>0
, independent of
f_{n}
, such that

0\leq\langle(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{W})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle\leq K\int_{W}\lvert\nabla\widehat{u}_{n}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.19)

Let
\breve{u}_{n}
equal
u_{W,f_{n}}^{\emptyset}
in
\Omega\setminus W
and satisfy the following Dirichlet problem in
W
:

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\gamma_{0}\nabla\breve{u}_{n})

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in the interior of }W,

\displaystyle\breve{u}_{n}

\displaystyle=u_{W,f_{n}}^{\emptyset}\quad\text{on }\partial W.

The result in
[
6
, Lemma 5.3]
ensures that if an extreme inclusion (either perfectly insulating or perfectly conducting) is introduced,
compactly contained
in
Y
where the energy of
\widehat{u}_{n}
tends to zero, then the corresponding electric potentials with this new conductivity profile will have the same localisation as in (
5.18
). For a perfectly insulating inclusion,
W\Subset Y
, the localisation applies to
\breve{u}_{n}
, using precisely the extension in
W
defined above. We thus have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{Y}\lvert\nabla\breve{u}_{n}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(5.20)

Use of
[
6
, Lemma A.1(iii)]
yields

0\leq\langle(\Lambda_{W}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle\leq\mathop{\textup{ess\,sup}}(\gamma_{0})\int_{W}\lvert\nabla\breve{u}_{n}\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.21)

From (
5.18
)–(
5.21
) we now conclude

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(\Lambda_{W}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{W})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle=0.

It remains to prove that
\langle(\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle\to\infty
for
n\to\infty
. Let
u_{n}=u_{\Sigma_{0},f_{n}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
and
\widetilde{u}_{n}=u_{\emptyset,f_{n}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}
, then from Lemma
4.1
(i) we have

\langle(\Lambda_{\Sigma_{0}}^{\Sigma_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\Sigma_{\infty}})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle=\lVert u_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{n}\rVert_{*}^{2}\geq\mathop{\textup{ess\,inf}}(\gamma_{0})\int_{V}\lvert\nabla(u_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{n})\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

At the same time from (
5.11
)

A^{*}f_{n}=\nabla(u_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{n})|_{V},

so use of (
5.14
) concludes the proof.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
D
be a
(D_{0},D_{\infty})
collection of cracks. Given any
C\in\mathcal{A}
, then

\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}\geq\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}\qquad\text{implies}\qquad D\subset C.

Proof.

We will prove the contrapositive statement, i.e. assume
D\not\subset C
. As
C
is closed there is a relatively open part of
D
that belongs to
\Omega\setminus C
. Now we will show that either
\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}\not\geq\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}
or
\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}\not\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}
.

Since
C
has connected complement we have either of two cases, which will dictate which operator inequality that will fail to hold:

(a)

There are
V,W\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mathop{\textup{dist}}(V,W)>0
and non-empty
\chi\in\mathcal{X}
, such that

\chi\subseteq D_{0},\qquad\chi\Subset V,\qquad C\subseteq W,\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{\infty}\Subset W.

(b)

There are
V,W\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mathop{\textup{dist}}(V,W)>0
and non-empty
\chi\in\mathcal{X}
, such that

\chi\subseteq D_{\infty},\qquad\chi\Subset W,\qquad C\subseteq V,\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{0}\Subset V.

Case (a): Since
C\cup D_{\infty}\subseteq W
then
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
and (the second inequality of) Proposition
3.1
give

\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}\leq\Lambda_{W}^{\emptyset}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D_{\infty}}\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{W}.

As
\chi\subseteq D_{0}
then Proposition
3.2
(i) gives

\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}\geq\Lambda_{\chi}^{D_{\infty}}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=(\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D_{\infty}})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}})

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda_{W}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{W})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{\chi}^{D_{\infty}}).

Now, since
\emptyset\neq\chi\Subset V
and
D_{\infty}\Subset W
, Proposition
5.4
(i) gives the existence of a sequence
(f_{n})
in
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle=-\infty,

and in particular we conclude that
\Lambda_{C}^{\emptyset}\not\geq\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}
.

Case (b): Since
C\cup D_{0}\subseteq V
then
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
and (the first inequality of) Proposition
3.1
gives

\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{\emptyset}\leq\Lambda_{V}^{\emptyset}.

As
\chi\subseteq D_{\infty}
then Proposition
3.2
(ii) gives

\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}\leq\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{\chi}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}

\displaystyle=(\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C})

\displaystyle\leq(\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{\chi}-\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{V}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{V}).

Now, since
\emptyset\neq\chi\Subset W
and
D_{0}\Subset V
, Proposition
5.4
(ii) gives the existence of a sequence
(g_{n})
in
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C})g_{n},g_{n}\rangle=-\infty,

and in particular we conclude that
\Lambda_{D_{0}}^{D_{\infty}}\not\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{C}
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
D\in\mathcal{X}
.

(i)

Given any
\chi\in\mathcal{X}
, then

\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}\geq\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset}\qquad\text{implies}\qquad\chi\subseteq D.

(ii)

Given any
\chi\in\mathcal{X}
, then

\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D}\qquad\text{implies}\qquad\chi\subseteq D.

Proof.

We will prove the contrapositive statements, i.e., assume
\chi\not\subseteq D
.

Proof of (i): We need to show that
\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}\not\geq\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset}
. There exist
V,W\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mathop{\textup{dist}}(V,W)>0
and a non-empty
\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, such that

\chi^{\prime}\subseteq\chi,\qquad\chi^{\prime}\Subset V,\qquad\text{and}\qquad D\subset W.

Using Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
(i) we have

\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}\leq\Lambda_{W}^{\emptyset}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset}\geq\Lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\emptyset}.

Hence

\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset}=(\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset})\leq(\Lambda_{W}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\chi^{\prime}}^{\emptyset}).

Now, since
\emptyset\neq\chi^{\prime}\Subset V
, Proposition
5.4
(i) gives the existence of a sequence
(f_{n})
in
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset})f_{n},f_{n}\rangle=-\infty,

and in particular we conclude that
\Lambda_{D}^{\emptyset}\not\geq\Lambda_{\chi}^{\emptyset}
.

Proof of (ii): We need to show that
\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}\not\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D}
. There exist
V,W\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mathop{\textup{dist}}(V,W)>0
and a non-empty
\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}
, such that

\chi^{\prime}\subseteq\chi,\qquad\chi^{\prime}\Subset W,\qquad\text{and}\qquad D\subset V.

Using Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
(ii) we have

\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D}\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{V}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}\leq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi^{\prime}}.

Hence

\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D}=(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D})\leq(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi^{\prime}}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset})+(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\emptyset}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{V}).

Now, since
\emptyset\neq\chi^{\prime}\Subset W
, Proposition
5.4
(ii) gives the existence of a sequence
(g_{n})
in
L^{2}_{\diamond}(\Gamma)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle(\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}-\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D})g_{n},g_{n}\rangle=-\infty,

and in particular we conclude that
\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{\chi}\not\geq\Lambda_{\emptyset}^{D}
.
∎

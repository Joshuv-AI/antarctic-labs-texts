Generalized stepwise transmission irregular graphs
Yaser Alizadeh, Sandi Klavžar, Zohre Molaee

Abstract
The transmission Tr G ​ ( u ) {\rm Tr}_{G}(u) of a vertex u u of a connected graph G G is the sum of distances from u u to all other vertices. G G is a stepwise transmission irregular (STI) graph if | Tr G ​ ( u ) − Tr G ​ ( v ) | = 1 |{\rm Tr}_{G}(u)-{\rm Tr}_{G}(v)|=1 holds for any edge u ​ v ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) uv\in E(G) . In this paper, generalized STI graphs are introduced as the graphs G G such that for some k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 we have | Tr G ​ ( u ) − Tr G ​ ( v ) | = k |{\rm Tr}_{G}(u)-{\rm Tr}_{G}(v)|=k for any edge u ​ v uv of G G . It is proved that generalized STI graphs are bipartite and that as soon as the minimum degree is at least 2 2 , they are 2-edge connected. Among the trees, the only generalized STI graphs are stars. The diameter of STI graphs is bounded and extremal cases discussed. The Cartesian product operation is used to obtain highly connected generalized STI graphs. Several families of generalized STI graphs are constructed.

Consider the complete bipartite graph

-STI graphs.

Our first result collects the structural properties of
k
-STI graphs. To this end, recall that vertices
u
and

are

if for every

we have
uw\in E(G)
if and only if

. Note that in our definition of twin vertices we put no condition whether

and

are adjacent. We say that

is

, if it contains no twins.

Theorem 2.1

If
k\geq 1
and
G
is a
k
-STI graph, then the following hold.

(i)

G
is a bipartite, twin-free graph.

(ii)

n(G)\equiv k\pmod{2}
.

(iii)

If
\delta(G)\geq 2
, then
G
is
2
-edge connected.

(iv)

If
k\in[2]
, then
G
is either
2
-connected or
G\in\{P_{3},K_{1,3}\}
.

Proof.
(i) Suppose on the contrary that
G
contains an odd cycle
v_{0}v_{1}\ldots v_{2p}
. As
G
is a
k
-STI graph, we have
{\rm Tr}_{G}(v_{0})\equiv{\rm Tr}_{G}(v_{2})\pmod{2k}
,
\ldots
,
{\rm Tr}_{G}(v_{2p-2})\equiv{\rm Tr}_{G}(v_{2p})\pmod{2k}
, so that also
{\rm Tr}_{G}(v_{0})\equiv{\rm Tr}_{G}(v_{2p})\pmod{2k}
. Since
v_{0}v_{2p}\in E(G)
, this is not possible. Hence
G
is bipartite. It is straightforward to see that if
u
and
v
are twins, then
{\rm Tr}_{G}(u)={\rm Tr}_{G}(v)
, thus
G
must also be twin-free.

(ii) Let
e=uv\in E(G)
, and assume without loss of generality that
{\rm Tr}_{G}(u)={\rm Tr}_{G}(v)+k
. As just proved in (i),
G
is bipartite, hence Theorem
1.1
yields that
{\rm Tr}_{G}(u)-{\rm Tr}_{G}(v)=k=n_{u}(e)-n_{v}(e)
. Using once more that
G
is bipartite we also have
n_{u}(e)+n_{v}(e)=n(G)
. It follows that
n(G)+k=2n_{u}(e)
which means that
n(G)\equiv k\pmod{2}
.

(iii) Suppose on the contrary that
e=uv
is a bridge of
G
. Let
G-e=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
, where
u\in V(G_{1})
and
v\in V(G_{2})
. We may assume without loss of generality that
n_{1}=n(G_{1})\geq n_{2}=n(G_{2})
. By (i) we know that

is bipartite, hence using Theorem
1.1
we have

k=I(uv)=n_{u}(e)-n_{v}(e)=n_{1}-n_{2}\,.

Since
\delta(G)\geq 2
and so also
\deg_{G}(v)\geq 2
, there exists a vertex

such that

. Then

I(vw)=n_{v}(e^{\prime})-n_{w}(e^{\prime})\geq(n_{1}+1)-(n_{2}-1)=k+2\,,

a contradiction.

(iv) In
[
13
, Proposition 5]
is was proved that all 1-STI graphs but the path

are
2
-connected. Hence it remains to consider the case

. It is straightforward to check that

is the only

-STI graph of order at most
4
. Assume in the rest that

is a
2
-STI graph with

. If
G
has a pendant edge

then
2=|{\rm Tr}_{G}(u)-{\rm Tr}_{G}(v)|=n-2
which is not possible since

. Suppose on the contrary that

contains a cut vertex

. Let
G_{1}
be a component of

with the minimum order

. Notice that

. As
\delta(G)\geq 2
, we have
n_{1}\geq 2
, and let

such that
vw,zw\in E(G)
. By (i)

is bipartite and hence

2={\rm Tr}_{G}(z)-{\rm Tr}_{G}(w)\geq(n-n_{1}+1)-(n_{1}-1)=n-2n_{1}+2\geq 3\,.

This contradiction completes the argument.

\square

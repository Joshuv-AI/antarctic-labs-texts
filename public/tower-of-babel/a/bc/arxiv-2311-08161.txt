Intersection formulas on moduli spaces of unitary shtukas Thanks: B.H. was supported in part by NSF grant DMS-2101636.
Yongyi Chen and Benjamin Howard

Abstract
. Feng-Yun-Zhang have proved a function field analogue of the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula, relating special cycles on moduli spaces of unitary shtukas to higher derivatives of Eisenstein series. We prove an extension of this formula in a low-dimensional case, and deduce from it a Gross-Zagier style formula expressing intersection multiplicities of cycles in terms of higher derivatives of base-change L L -functions.



1.
Introduction

Feng-Yun-Zhang have defined special cycle classes on moduli spaces of unitary shtukas, and proved an arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula (in the sense of Kudla and Kudla-Rapoport) relating them to nonsingular Fourier coefficients of higher derivatives of Siegel Eisenstein series.

One would like to extend these results to singular coefficients, and then exploit this connection between arithmetic geometry and automorphic forms to prove a Gross-Zagier style formula relating intersection multiplicities of special cycles to higher derivatives of Langlands
L
-functions.
Both problems are difficult because the moduli spaces on which the special cycles live are almost never proper.

The goal of this paper is to prove an arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula for (some) singular Fourier coefficients in a low-dimensional case, and then use it to formulate and prove a Gross-Zagier style formula in a way that circumvents the failure of properness of the moduli space of shtukas on which the special cycles live. This Gross-Zagier style formula is conditional on the modularity conjecture of Feng-Yun-Zhang.

1.1.
The Feng-Yun-Zhang classes

Let
k
be a finite field of odd cardinality
q
, and fix a finite étale double cover

X^{\prime}\to X

of smooth, projective, geometrically connected curves over
k
.
Denote by
\sigma\in\Aut(X^{\prime}/X)
the nontrivial Galois automorphism.

Fix integers
n\geq 1
and
r\geq 0
. Our étale double cover determines a moduli stack
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}
of
\mathrm{U}(n)
-shtukas with
r
legs.
It is a Deligne-Mumford stack over
k
, locally of finite type, equipped with a smooth morphism

\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}\to(X^{\prime})^{r}

of relative dimension
r(n-1)
. In particular,
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}
is smooth over
k
of dimension
rn
.
It is nonempty if and only if
r
is even (
[
FYZa
, Lemma 6.7]
), which we assume from now on.

To a pair
(\mathcal{E},a)
consisting of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X^{\prime}
of rank
m\leq n
and a hermitian morphism
a\colon\mathcal{E}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
(in the sense of §
1.4
), Feng-Yun-Zhang
[
FYZa
, §7]
associate a
naive special cycle

\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)
.
This is a Deligne-Mumford stack equipped with a finite morphism

\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)\to\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}.

The naive special cycle has expected dimension
r(n-m)
, but this expectation is rarely fulfilled.
To correct for this, one finds in
[
FYZb
, Definition 4.8]
the construction of a cycle class

(1.1.1)

[\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)]\in\mathrm{Ch}_{r(n-m)}(\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a))

on
\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)
of the desired dimension.
When no confusion can arise, we denote in the same way the image of this class under the pushforward

(1.1.2)

\mathrm{Ch}_{r(n-m)}(\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a))\to\mathrm{Ch}^{rm}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}).

These special cycles are the function field analogues of the Kudla-Rapoport cycles
[
KR14
]
on Shimura varieties for unitary groups of signature
(n-1,1)
.

It sometimes happens that the naive special cycle
\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)
is a proper
k
-stack.
When this is the case the above pushforward can be refined to a homomorphism

(1.1.3)

\mathrm{Ch}_{r(n-m)}(\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a))\to\mathrm{Ch}_{c}^{rm}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)})

valued in the Chow group with proper support, and so (
1.1.1
) determines a class there as well.
Using the intersection pairing

(1.1.4)

\Ch^{r(n-m)}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)})\times\Ch_{c}^{rm}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)})\to\Ch_{c}^{rn}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)})

and the degree map

(1.1.5)

\deg\colon\mathrm{Ch}_{c}^{rn}(\Sht_{\mathrm{U}(n)}^{r})\to\mathbb{Q},

we obtain a
\mathbb{Q}
-valued intersection between
[\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)]
and any codimension
r(n-m)
cycle class on
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}
.

1.2.
The modularity conjecture

Let us restrict to the case
n=2m
, so that the special cycles (
1.1.1
) determine classes in the middle codimension Chow group of
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}
.

Denote by
F=k(X)
the field of rational functions on
X
, and similarly for
F^{\prime}=k(X^{\prime})
. Let

\chi\colon\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

be an unramified Hecke character whose restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
is trivial.

Let
\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
be the space of automorphic forms on the rank
2m
quasi-split unitary group
H_{m}
over
F
.
Any form
f\in\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
fixed by the standard maximal compact open subgroup

K_{m}\subset H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
is determined by its
geometric Fourier coefficients

f_{(\mathcal{E},a)}
. These are defined in §
2.2
, but at the moment all the reader needs to know is that this is a collection of complex numbers indexed by pairs
(\mathcal{E},a)
consisting of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
m
on
X^{\prime}
and a hermitian morphism
a\colon\mathcal{E}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
.

More generally, suppose
C
is any
\mathbb{C}
-vector space. A function

f:H_{m}(F)\backslash H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})/K_{m}\to C

will be referred to as an
unramified automorphic form valued in
C
. Any linear functional
\lambda:C\to\mathbb{C}
determines a
\mathbb{C}
-valued automorphic form
\lambda\circ f
, and the
geometric Fourier coefficients
of
f
are the unique
f_{(\mathcal{E},a)}\in C
satisfying

\lambda(f_{(\mathcal{E},a)})=(\lambda\circ f)_{(\mathcal{E},a)}.

The following is the modularity conjecture of
[
FYZb
, Conjecture 4.15]
, restricted to the case of cycles in middle codimension.
Abbreviate

(1.2.1)

d(\mathcal{E})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\deg(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})-\deg(\mathcal{E})}{2}=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})\deg(\omega_{X})-\deg(\mathcal{E})

for any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X^{\prime}
.

Conjecture 1.2.1

(Feng-Yun-Zhang)
.

There is an unramified automorphic form

Z^{r,\chi}\colon H_{m}(F)\backslash H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})/K_{m}\to\mathrm{Ch}^{rm}(\mathrm{Sht}^{r}_{U(n)})_{\mathbb{C}}

whose geometric Fourier coefficients are the rescaled special cycles

Z^{r,\chi}_{(\mathcal{E},a)}=\frac{\chi(\det(\mathcal{E}))}{q^{md(\mathcal{E})}}\,[\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}}(a)]\in\mathrm{Ch}^{rm}(\mathrm{Sht}^{r}_{U(n)})_{\mathbb{C}}.

See §
2.3
for the meaning of
\chi(\det(\mathcal{E}))
.

Our first result provides some evidence toward this conjecture in the low-dimensional case of
m=1
and
n=2
, in the same style as
[
FYZb
, Part 3]
.

Theorem 1.2.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is a line bundle on
X^{\prime}
, and
a_{2}\colon\mathcal{E}_{2}\cong\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}
is a hermitian isomorphism. The naive special cycle

\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})\to\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(2)}

is proper over
k
, and
there exists a
K_{1}
-fixed automorphic form
\mathscr{D}\in\mathcal{A}(H_{1})
, depending on
(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})
, whose geometric Fourier coefficients are given by

(1.2.2)

\mathscr{D}_{(\mathcal{E}_{1},a_{1})}=\frac{\chi(\mathcal{E}_{1})}{q^{d(\mathcal{E}_{1})}}\,\deg\big([\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{1}}(a_{1})]\cdot[\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})]\big)

for every line bundle
\mathcal{E}_{1}
on
X^{\prime}
and every hermitian map
a_{1}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\vee}
.

In other words, if we hold
(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})
fixed and allow
(\mathcal{E}_{1},a_{1})
to vary, the intersection multiplicities on the right hand side of (
1.2.2
) are the geometric Fourier coefficients of an automorphic form.

This result appears in the text as part (3) of Theorem
3.3.1
.
The automorphic form
\mathscr{D}
is essentially the kernel function appearing in the
new way
integrals of
[
PSR88
,
Qin07
]
; see Remark
2.4.4
.

The core of the proof of Theorem
1.2.2
is Theorem
3.2.6
.
This latter theorem generalizes the Feng-Yun-Zhang arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula on
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(2)}
, which relates the degrees of certain
0
-cycles to nonsingular Fourier coefficients of Eisenstein series, to include at least some singular Fourier coefficients.

1.3.
Intersections with arithmetic theta lifts

We continue to assume that
n=2m
is even, and that

\chi:\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

is an unramified Hecke character whose restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
is trivial.

Assuming Conjecture
1.2.1
, for a
K_{m}
-fixed form
f
in an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation
\pi\subset\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
we define the
arithmetic theta lift

(1.3.1)

\vartheta^{r,\chi}(f)=\int_{H_{m}(F)\backslash H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}f(h)Z^{r,\chi}(h)\,dh\in\Ch^{rm}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)})_{\mathbb{C}}.

Following ideas of Kudla
[
Kud04
, §8]
in the context of Shimura varieties, developed further by Liu
[
Liu11
]
and Li-Liu
[
LL21
]
,
one could hope for an
arithmetic Rallis inner product formula

(1.3.2)

\deg\big(\vartheta^{r,\chi}(f)\cdot\vartheta^{r,\chi}(f)\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{=}}C\cdot\frac{d^{r}}{ds^{r}}\Big|_{s=0}L(s+1/2,\mathrm{BC}(\pi)\otimes\chi)

(for some explicit constant
C
)
relating the self-intersection multiplicity of
\vartheta^{r,\chi}(f)
to the
r^{\mathrm{th}}
central derivative of the twisted base-change
L
-function at the center of its functional equation.

Unfortunately, in the present context the left hand side of (
1.3.2
) is not defined, as the moduli space
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}
is not proper as soon as
n\geq 2
.
While one can form the self-intersection of
\vartheta^{r,\chi}(f)
as an element in the usual Chow group (i.e. not the Chow group with proper support) of
0
-cycles on
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}
, there is no analogue of the degree map (
1.1.5
) on this Chow group.

To circumvent this, fix a pair
(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})
consisting of a rank
m
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{2}
on
X^{\prime}
and a hermitian morphism

a_{2}:\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}
, and
assume
that the associated naive special cycle

\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})\to\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)}

is proper over
k
. Using (
1.1.4
) and (
1.1.5
) to intersect

[\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})]\in\Ch^{rm}_{c}(\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(n)})

against the arithmetic theta lift (
1.3.1
), we propose the following Gross-Zagier style intersection formula.

Conjecture 1.3.1
.

Assuming the properness of
\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})
, for any
K_{m}
-invariant
f\in\pi\subset\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
as above we have

(1.3.3)

\displaystyle\deg\big(\vartheta^{r,\chi}(f)\cdot[\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})]\big)

\displaystyle=f_{(\mathcal{E}_{2},-a_{2})}\frac{q^{md(\mathcal{E}_{2})}}{(\log q)^{r}}\frac{d^{r}}{ds^{r}}\Big|_{s=0}\left(q^{ns\deg(\omega_{X})}L(s+1/2,\mathrm{BC}(\pi)\otimes\chi)\right),

where
f_{(\mathcal{E}_{2},-a_{2})}
is the geometric Fourier coefficient in the sense of §
2.2
.

The properness assumption imposed on
\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})
is very strong.
Although no proof is provided, it is claimed in
[
FYZb
, Example 4.20]
that it holds
whenever
m=1
and
a_{2}:\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}
is injective.
There is circumstantial evidence that properness may also hold for some middle codimension special cycles on
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(4)}
. For
m>2
there is no expectation that any middle codimension special cycles on
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(2m)}
are proper.
The point we wish to convey is that the above conjecture predicts that
every
instance of a proper special cycle in middle dimension has its own higher derivative intersection formula, and so even these low-dimensional cases are of interest.

Remark 1.3.2
.

Conjecture
1.3.1
is perhaps less like the Gross-Zagier theorem and more like a hybrid of the arithmetic Rallis inner product formula and the Gross-Kohnen-Zagier theorem.
An analogue of it on quaternionic Shimura curves over
\mathbb{Q}
can be deduced easily from
[
KRY06
, Corollary 1.0.7]
.

Our second main result is the proof of the above conjecture in the simplest case, stated as part (4) of Theorem
3.3.1
in the body of the text.
This result is conditional on Conjecture
1.2.1
, which is needed to even define the arithmetic theta lift (
1.3.1
).

Theorem 1.3.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is a line bundle on
X^{\prime}
(so
m=1
and
n=2
) and
a_{2}\colon\mathcal{E}_{2}\cong\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}
is a hermitian isomorphism. The naive special cycle

\mathcal{Z}^{r}_{\mathcal{E}_{2}}(a_{2})\to\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(2)}

is proper over
k
, and the equality (
1.3.3
) holds.

Proving Theorems
1.2.2
and
1.3.3
under the weaker hypothesis that the hermitian morphism
a_{2}\colon\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}
is injective might be within reach.
Even more interesting would be to find examples of proper special cycles of middle codimension on
\Sht^{r}_{\mathrm{U}(4)}
, and extend our results to that setting.

2.
Eisenstein series on unitary groups

This section begins by establishing some basic notation for quasi-split unitary groups, and the Siegel Eisenstein series on them.
Once that is done we prove two results.

The first, Theorem
2.4.2
is a modest generalization of the famous doubling formula of Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis
[
PSR87
]
, providing the link between Siegel Eisenstein series and base-change
L
-functions. This will be needed in the proof of Theorem
3.3.1
.

The second, Proposition
2.5.1
, is an explicit formula relating singular Fourier coefficients of an Eisenstein series on a unitary group of rank
4
to non-singular Fourier coefficients of an Eisenstein series on a unitary group of rank
2
.
This is one of the main ingredients in the proof of Theorem
3.2.6
.

2.1.
The quasi-split unitary group

For an integer
n\geq 1
, abbreviate

w_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2n}(F).

We endow the space of column vectors
W_{n}=(F^{\prime})^{2n}
with the
standard skew-hermitian form
h_{n}(x,y)={}^{t}x\cdot w_{n}\cdot\sigma(y)
.
The associated rank
2n
quasi-split unitary group over
F
is denoted

H_{n}=\mathrm{U}(W_{n})\subset\mathrm{Res}_{F^{\prime}/F}\GL_{2n}.

The
standard Siegel parabolic

P_{n}\subset H_{n}

is the subgroup of matrices whose lower left
n\times n
block vanishes.
Its unipotent radical is denoted

N_{n}=\left\{n(b)=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n}&b\\
&I_{n}\end{pmatrix}:b\in\Herm_{n}\right\}

while its Levi factor is denoted

M_{n}=\left\{m(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\\
&\sigma({}^{t}\alpha^{-1})\end{pmatrix}:\alpha\in\mathrm{Res}_{F^{\prime}/F}\GL_{n}\right\}.

The
standard compact open subgroup
of
H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
is

K_{n}=\prod_{v}K_{n,v}
, where for any place
v
of
F
we set

K_{n,v}=H_{n}(F_{v})\cap\GL_{2n}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}_{v}}).

Remark 2.1.1
.

The Haar measure on
H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
is always normalized so that the standard compact open
K_{n}
has volume
1
, and

[H_{n}]
is given the induced quotient measure.
We give
\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\cong N(\mathbb{A}_{F})
the self-dual Haar measure; explicitly, this is the one for which the compact open subgroup

\prod_{v}\Herm_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\subset\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})

of hermitian matrices with integral entries has volume

q^{-n^{2}\deg(\omega_{X})/2}
. The induced quotient measure on
[\Herm_{n}]\cong[N]
has volume
1
.

2.2.
Fourier coefficients

Fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi_{0}\colon k\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. As in
[
FYZa
, §2.2]
,
we use the
k
-linear
residue map

\mathrm{Res}\colon\omega_{F}\to k

to define a canonical pairing

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F},\omega_{F})\times\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\to k

by
\langle T,b\rangle=\mathrm{Res}(-\mathrm{Tr}(Tb))
. Its composition with
\psi_{0}
is denoted

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\psi_{0}}\colon\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F},\omega_{F})\times\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Any automorphic form
f\in\mathcal{A}(H_{n})
has a Fourier expansion

(2.2.1)

f(g)=\sum_{T\in\Herm_{n}(F,\omega_{F})}f_{T}(g),

in which the
T
-coefficient is

f_{T}(g)=\int_{[\Herm_{n}]}f(n(b)g)\,\langle T,b\rangle_{\psi_{0}}\,db,

with Haar measure as in Remark
2.1.1
.

Suppose
f\in\mathcal{A}(H_{n})
is right invariant under
K_{n}
.
By the Iwasawa decomposition,
f
is determined by its restriction to
P_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\subset H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, and hence is determined by the collection of functions

\{f_{T}|_{M_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\}_{T\in\Herm_{n}(F,\omega_{F})}.

As explained in
[
FYZa
, §2.6]
, this gives rise to a collection of
geometric Fourier coefficients of
f
:
complex numbers
f_{(\mathcal{E},a)}
indexed by pairs
(\mathcal{E},a)
consisting of a rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X^{\prime}
and a hermitian morphism
a\colon\mathcal{E}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}.

The precise definition of
f_{(\mathcal{E},a)}
is as follows.
The generic fiber of
\mathcal{E}
is a free
F^{\prime}
-module of rank
n
, and upon choosing an isomorphism

\mathcal{E}_{F^{\prime}}\cong(F^{\prime})^{n}

the hermitian morphism
a
is given by a hermitian matrix
T\in\Herm_{n}(F,\omega_{F})
.
For every place
v
of
F^{\prime}
there is an
\alpha_{v}\in\GL_{n}(F^{\prime}_{v})
such that the completed stalk of
\mathcal{E}
at
v
is identified with the lattice
\alpha_{v}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}_{v}}^{n}\subset(F_{v}^{\prime})^{n}
.
Setting
\alpha=\prod_{v}\alpha_{v}\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}})
, we define

f_{(\mathcal{E},a)}=f_{T}(m(\alpha)).

Remark 2.2.1
.

We identify the set of rank
n
vector bundles on
X^{\prime}
with the double quotient

\GL_{n}(F^{\prime})\backslash\GL_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}})/\prod_{v}\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}_{n}})

by sending
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\alpha
with
\alpha
as above. In particular,

q^{\deg(\det(\mathcal{E}))}=|\det(\alpha)|_{F^{\prime}}.

2.3.
Siegel Eisenstein series

Fix an unramified Hecke character
\chi\colon\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and denote by
\chi_{0}
its restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
.
We always assume

(2.3.1)

\chi_{0}=1\quad\mbox{or}\quad\chi_{0}=\eta,

where
\eta
is the quadratic character (
1.4.1
).
We often use the isomorphism

\mathrm{Pic}(X^{\prime})\cong(F^{\prime})^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}/\prod_{v}\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}_{v}}^{\times}

of Remark
2.2.1
to view
\chi
as a character

\chi\colon\mathrm{Pic}(X^{\prime})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Remark 2.3.1
.

We will follow the discussion of
[
FYZa
]
for Eisenstein series on
H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, but warn the reader that
throughout
loc. cit.
it is assumed that the Hecke character
\chi
satisfies
\chi_{0}=\eta^{n}
.
Thus some of the formulas of
loc. cit.
require small modifications to account for our weaker assumption (
2.3.1
).
This weaker assumption is the only one imposed in
[
Tan99
]
, and everything we need to know about Eisenstein series can be deduced from the results found there.

For a complex variable
s
, denote by

I_{n}(s,\chi)=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}^{H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}(\chi|\cdot|_{F^{\prime}}^{s+\frac{n}{2}})

the unnormalized smooth induction.
Here we are viewing both
\chi
and
|\cdot|_{F^{\prime}}
as characters of
P_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
by composing them with the homomorphism

(2.3.2)

P_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\to\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}

sending an element of
P_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\subset\GL_{2n}(\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}})
to the determinant of its upper left
n\times n
block.

Given any
\Phi_{s}\in I_{n}(s,\chi)
with
\mathrm{Re}(s)>n/2
, the summation

E(g,\Phi_{s})=\sum_{\gamma\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H_{n}(F)}\Phi_{s}(\gamma g)

is absolutely convergent and defines a Siegel Eisenstein series on
H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.
Suppose
T\in\Herm_{n}(F,\omega_{F})
is nonsingular, in the sense that
\det(T)\neq 0
after fixing some trivialization
\omega_{F}\cong F
.
As in
[
FYZa
, §2.2]
, modified to account for the choice of Haar measure in Remark
2.1.1
, the corresponding Fourier coefficient is

(2.3.3)

E_{T}(g,\Phi_{s})=\int_{\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi_{s}\big(w_{n}^{-1}n(b)g\big)\,\langle T,b\rangle_{\psi_{0}}\,db.

There is a unique standard section
s\mapsto\Phi_{s}^{\circ}\in I_{n}(s,\chi)
satisfying
\Phi_{s}^{\circ}(k)=1
for all
k\in K_{n}
. We call this the
normalized spherical section
, and denote the associated
spherical Eisenstein series
by

E(g,s,\chi)=E(g,\Phi_{s}^{\circ}).

The standard
L
-function
L(s,\eta^{i}\chi_{0})
satisfies the functional equation

(2.3.4)

q^{\frac{s}{2}\deg(\omega_{X})}L(s,\eta^{i}\chi_{0})=q^{\frac{1-s}{2}\deg(\omega_{X})}L(1-s,\eta^{i}\chi_{0}).

The product of
L
-functions

\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}L(2s+i,\eta^{i-n}\chi_{0})

agrees with the
\mathscr{L}_{n}(s)
defined in
[
FYZa
, §2.6]
when
\chi_{0}=\eta^{n}
.

Because of the assumption (
2.3.1
), there is an intertwining operator

M_{n}(s)\colon I_{n}(s,\chi)\to I_{n}(-s,\chi)

defined for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>n/2
by

(2.3.5)

(M_{n}(s)\Phi_{s})(g)=\int_{\Herm_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi_{s}\left(w_{n}^{-1}n(b)g\right)\,db.

By combining the local calculation of
[
Tan99
, Proposition 2.1]
, as simplified on page 170 of
loc. cit.
, with the global function equation (
2.3.4
), one finds that the unramified spherical section satisfies

(2.3.6)

M_{n}(s)\Phi_{s}^{\circ}=q^{-2ns\deg(\omega_{X})}\frac{\mathscr{L}_{n}(-s,\chi_{0})}{\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})}\cdot\Phi^{\circ}_{-s}.

It follows that
E(g,s,\chi)
has meromorphic continuation, and that the renormalized Eisenstein series

(2.3.7)

\widetilde{E}(g,s,\chi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}q^{ns\deg(\omega_{X})}\cdot\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})\cdot E(g,s,\chi)

of
[
FYZb
, §9.5]
satisfies the functional equation

(2.3.8)

\widetilde{E}(g,s,\chi)=\widetilde{E}(g,-s,\chi).

For nonsingular
T
, the integral (
2.3.3
) determined by the normalized spherical section
\Phi_{s}^{\circ}
can be expressed as products of local representation densities. Hence the same is true of the geometric Fourier coefficient
E_{(\mathcal{E},a)}(s,\chi)
indexed by a rank
n
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X^{\prime}
and an
injective
hermitian morphism
a\colon\mathcal{E}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
. We only need the simplest case of these formulas, in which
a
is an isomorphism.

Proposition 2.3.2
.

If
\mathcal{E}
is a rank
n
vector bundle on
X^{\prime}
, and
a\colon\mathcal{E}\cong\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
is a hermitian isomorphism, then

q^{ns\deg(\omega_{X})}\cdot\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})\cdot E_{(\mathcal{E},a)}(s,\chi)=\chi(\det(\mathcal{E})).

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
FYZa
, Theorem 2.8]
, slightly extended
(see Remark
2.3.1
) using the calculation of unramified local Whittaker functions found in
[
Tan99
, Proposition 3.2]
.
∎

2.4.
The duplication formula

In this subsection we assume that
n=2m
is even.
We continue to work with an unramified Hecke character
\chi\colon\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, but now assume
\chi_{0}=1
instead of the weaker (
2.3.1
).

Consider the Eisenstein series

E(g,s,\chi)=\sum_{\gamma\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H_{n}(F)}\Phi^{\circ}_{s}(\gamma g)

on
H_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
associated to the normalized spherical section
\Phi_{s}^{\circ}\in I_{n}(s,\chi)
.
We are interested in its pullback via the
standard embedding

i_{0}\colon H_{m}\times H_{m}\to H_{n}

defined by

(2.4.1)

i_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&&b_{1}&\\
&a_{2}&&b_{2}\\
c_{1}&&d_{1}&\\
&c_{2}&&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Definition 2.4.1
.

The
doubling kernel
is the two-variable automorphic form

(2.4.2)

D(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)=E(i_{0}(g_{1},g_{2}),s,\chi)\in\mathcal{A}(H_{m}\times H_{m}).

For any
T_{2}\in\Herm_{m}(F,\omega_{F})
define the
new way kernel

(2.4.3)

D_{\square,T_{2}}(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)=\int_{[\Herm_{m}]}D(g_{1},n(b)g_{2},s,\chi)\,\langle T_{2},b\rangle_{\psi_{0}}\,db

as the
T_{2}
-coefficient of the doubling kernel with respect to the variable
g_{2}
.

As in (
2.2.1
), any automorphic form
f\in\mathcal{A}(H_{m}\times H_{m})
in two variables has a double Fourier expansion

f(g_{1},g_{2})=\sum_{T_{1},T_{2}\in\Herm_{m}(F,\omega_{F})}f_{T_{1},T_{2}}(g_{1},g_{2}).

The double Fourier coefficients of the doubling kernel (
2.4.2
)
are related to the Fourier coefficients of
E(g,s,\chi)
by

D_{T_{1},T_{2}}(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)=\sum_{T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}T_{1}&*\\
*&T_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Herm_{n}(F,\omega_{F})}E_{T}(i_{0}(g_{1},g_{2}),s,\chi),

and so the new way kernel (
2.4.3
) has Fourier expansion

\displaystyle D_{\square,T_{2}}(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T_{1}\in\Herm_{m}(F)}D_{T_{1},T_{2}}(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
*&T_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Herm_{n}(F,\omega_{F})}E_{T}(i_{0}(g_{1},g_{2}),s,\chi).

As in §
2.2
, we can take geometric Fourier coefficients of the doubling kernel (
2.4.2
) in the second variable.
The result is, for every pair
(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})
consisting of a rank
m
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}_{2}
on
X^{\prime}
and a hermitian map
a_{2}\colon\mathcal{E}_{2}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}
, an automorphic form

(2.4.4)

D_{\square,(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})}(g_{1},s,\chi)\in\mathcal{A}(H_{m})

in the variable
g_{1}
whose geometric Fourier coefficients are related to those of the Eisenstein series
E(g,s,\chi)
by

(2.4.5)

D_{(\mathcal{E}_{1},a_{1}),(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})}(s,\chi)=\sum_{a=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{1}&*\\
*&a_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)}E_{(\mathcal{E},a)}(s,\chi).

On the right hand side
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
, and
the sum is over all hermitian maps
a\colon\mathcal{E}\to\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
for which the composition

\mathcal{E}_{i}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{inc.}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}\xrightarrow{a}\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\vee}\oplus\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{2}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{proj.}}\sigma^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\vee}

agrees with
a_{i}
for both
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Theorem 2.4.2

(The duplication formula)
.

If
f\in\pi
is a
K_{m}
-fixed vector in an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation

\pi\subset\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
, then for all
g_{2}\in H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{[H_{m}]}D(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}=\frac{L(s+1/2,\mathrm{BC}(\pi)\otimes\chi)}{\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})}\cdot\chi(\det(g_{2}))\cdot f(g_{2}^{\dagger}).

Here
g\mapsto g^{\dagger}
is the automorphism of
H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
defined on block matrices by

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}^{\dagger}=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\
-c&d\end{pmatrix},

where each block
a,b,c,d
has size
m\times m
.
The
L
-function on the right hand side is the twisted base-change
L
-function as defined in
[
Clo91
]
. See also
[
Min11
]
.

Remark 2.4.3
.

In the equality of Theorem
2.4.2
, if one takes the Petersson inner product of both sides against any cusp form
\breve{f}\in\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
the resulting formula is the usual
doubling formula
of Piatetski-Shapiro and Rallis
[
PSR87
]
, extended to the unitary case by Li
[
Li92
]
; see also
[
Liu11
]
.
By varying the auxiliary form
\breve{f}
, the duplication formula above would follow immediately from the doubling formula if one knew a priori that the integral on the left hand side of Theorem
2.4.2
defined a
cuspidal
automorphic form in the variable
g_{2}
. Thus the only real new information in Theorem
2.4.2
is the cuspidality of the left hand side.

Before giving the proof, we restate Theorem
2.4.2
in the precise form in which we will use it.
If the automorphic form
f(g)
in Theorem
2.4.2
has
T^{\mathrm{th}}
Fourier coefficient
f_{T}
, then the automorphic form
f(g^{\dagger})
has
T^{\mathrm{th}}
Fourier coefficient
f_{-T}(g^{\dagger})
. Thus taking the
T_{2}
-coefficient in both sides of Theorem
2.4.2
yields the formula

(2.4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{[H_{m}]}D_{\square,T_{2}}(g_{1},g_{2},s,\chi)f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{L(s+1/2,\mathrm{BC}(\pi)\otimes\chi)}{\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})}\cdot\chi(\det(g_{2}))\cdot f_{-T_{2}}(g_{2}^{\dagger}).

Equivalently, expressed in the language of geometric Fourier coefficients,

(2.4.7)

\displaystyle\int_{[H_{m}]}D_{\square,(\mathcal{E}_{2},a_{2})}(g_{1},s,\chi)f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{L(s+1/2,\mathrm{BC}(\pi)\otimes\chi)}{\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})}\cdot\chi(\det(\mathcal{E}_{2}))\cdot f_{(\mathcal{E}_{2},-a_{2})}.

Remark 2.4.4
.

Extending ideas of Böcherer
[
Bo83
,
Bo85
]
from symplectic groups to unitary groups,
Ikeda
[
Ike94
]
showed that the new way kernel (
2.4.3
) is a linear combination of Eisenstein series and theta series for the hermitian space determined by
T_{2}
.
As explained in
[
GS20
]
, if one substitutes this linear combination into the integral on the left hand side of (
2.4.6
) and sets
g_{2}=1
, the resulting formula is essentially the
new way integral
of
[
PSR88
,
Qin07
]
.
This explains our choice of terminology for (
2.4.3
).

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem
2.4.2
.
In addition to the standard embedding (
2.4.1
), we will make use of the twisted embedding

(2.4.8)

i\colon H_{m}\times H_{m}\to H_{n}

defined by

i(g_{1},g_{2})=i_{0}(g_{1},g_{2}^{\dagger})
. Set

\delta=\begin{pmatrix}&&I_{m}&\\
&I_{m}&&\\
-I_{m}&I_{m}&&\\
&&I_{m}&I_{m}\end{pmatrix}\in H_{n}(F)\cap K_{n}\subset\GL_{2n}(F^{\prime}).

Lemma 2.4.5
.

Consider the doubled space
W_{n}^{\Delta}=W_{m}\oplus W_{m}
endowed with the skew-hermitian form
h_{n}^{\Delta}=h_{m}\oplus-h_{m}
.
There is an isomorphism

H_{n}^{\Delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}U(W_{n}^{\Delta})\cong H_{n}

identifying the canonical inclusion

i^{\Delta}\colon H_{m}\times H_{m}\to H_{n}^{\Delta}

with the twisted embedding (
2.4.8
),
and identifying the stabilizer
P_{n}^{\Delta}\subset H_{n}^{\Delta}
of the diagonal Lagrangian

\{(x,x):x\in W_{m}\}\subset W_{n}^{\Delta}

with the conjugate
\delta^{-1}P_{n}\delta\subset H_{n}
of the standard Siegel parabolic.

Proof.

Using the standard basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in(F^{\prime})^{2m}=W_{m}
, we define a basis

e^{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,e^{\Delta}_{n},f^{\Delta}_{1},\ldots,f^{\Delta}_{n}\in W_{n}^{\Delta}
by

\displaystyle e^{\Delta}_{i}=\begin{cases}(e_{i},0)&\mbox{if }1\leq i\leq m\\
(0,e_{i-m})&\mbox{if }m<i\leq n\end{cases}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{\Delta}_{i}=\begin{cases}(f_{i},0)&\mbox{if }1\leq i\leq m\\
(0,-f_{i-m})&\mbox{if }m<i\leq n.\end{cases}

The
F^{\prime}
-linear isomorphism
W_{n}^{\Delta}\cong(F^{\prime})^{2n}=W_{n}
determined by this basis is an isometry, and the induced isomorphism
of unitary groups has the desired properties.
∎

Proposition 2.4.6
.

For any cuspidal automorphic form
f\in\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
and any standard section
\Phi_{s}\in I_{n}(s,\chi)
we have the equality

\int_{[H_{m}]}E(i(g_{1},g_{2}),\Phi_{s})f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}=\int_{H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi_{s}(\delta i(g_{1},g_{2}))f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

for all
g_{2}\in H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

Proof.

The proof follows a similar line of reasoning as in
[
PSR87
]
, where it is shown that the two sides of the desired equality agree after taking the Petersson inner product against any cusp form in
\mathcal{A}(H_{m})
.
See Remark
2.4.3
.

By mild abuse of notation, we denote by

(2.4.9)

H_{m}\times H_{m}^{\prime}\subset H_{n}

the image of the twisted embedding (
2.4.8
).
In other words, abbreviate
H_{m}=i(H_{m},1)
and
H_{m}^{\prime}=i(1,H_{m})
.
Using Lemma
2.4.5
, one sees that (
2.4.9
) is the stabilizer of an orthogonal splitting

W_{n}=V\oplus V^{\prime}

with
V
isometric to
(W_{m},h_{m})
and
V^{\prime}
isometric to
(W_{m},-h_{m})
.

Inserting the definition of the Eisenstein series and unfolding shows that

(2.4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{[H_{m}]}E(i(g_{1},g_{2}),\Phi_{s})\,f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H_{n}(F)/H_{m}(F)}\int_{I_{\gamma}(F)\backslash H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi_{s}(\gamma i(g_{1},g_{2}))\,f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

where
I_{\gamma}=H_{m}\cap\gamma^{-1}P_{n}\gamma
.
It follows from Lemma
2.4.5
that
I_{\delta}=1
, and we are reduced to proving the vanishing of the integrals indexed by all
\gamma\neq\delta
.

In practice, this amounts to upgrading the analysis of right
(H_{m}\times H_{m}^{\prime})
-orbits in
P_{n}\backslash H_{n}
found in
[
PSR87
, §2]
to an analysis of the right
H_{m}
-orbits.
Say that a subgroup
I\subset H_{m}
is
negligible
if there exists a proper parabolic subgroup
Q\subset H_{m}
with unipotent radical
N
such that
N\subset I\subset Q.

Lemma 2.4.7
.

Consider the action of the subgroup
H_{m}\subset H_{n}
on the set of all Lagrangian subspaces
L\subset W_{n}
.

(1)

The set of Lagrangians
L\subset W_{n}
satisfying
L\cap V=0
is a single
H_{m}
-orbit, and the stabilizer in
H_{m}
of any such
L
is trivial.

(2)

If
L\subset W_{n}
is a Lagrangian for which
L\cap V\neq 0
, its stabilizer in
H_{m}
is negligible.

Proof.

The proof of
[
PSR87
, Lemma 2.1]
establishes a bijection between the set of Lagrangians
L\subset W_{n}
and the set of triples
(Y,Y^{\prime},\alpha)
in which

•

Y\subset V
and
Y^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
are totally isotropic subspaces,

•

\alpha\colon Y^{\perp}/Y\cong(Y^{\prime})^{\perp}/Y^{\prime}
is an isometry.

The bijection sends a Lagrangian
L
to the triple defined by
Y=L\cap V
and
Y^{\prime}=L\cap V^{\prime}
together with the isometry characterized by

\alpha(y)=y^{\prime}\iff y+y^{\prime}\in L

for all
y\in Y^{\perp}
and
y^{\prime}\in(Y^{\prime})^{\perp}
.
Under this bijection, the action of the subgroup
H_{m}\times H_{m}^{\prime}\subset H_{n}
on the set of Lagrangians translates to an action on triples:
an element
(h,h^{\prime})
acts by

(Y,Y^{\prime},\alpha)\mapsto(hY,h^{\prime}Y^{\prime},h^{\prime}\alpha h^{-1}),

in which
h^{\prime}\alpha h^{-1}
is the composition

(hY)^{\perp}/hY\xrightarrow{h^{-1}}Y^{\perp}/Y\xrightarrow{\alpha}(Y^{\prime})^{\perp}/Y^{\prime}\xrightarrow{h^{\prime}}(h^{\prime}Y^{\prime})^{\perp}/h^{\prime}Y^{\prime},

and we are identifying
H_{m}\cong\mathrm{U}(V)
and
H_{m}^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{U}(V^{\prime})
.

Consider the set of all Lagrangians
L\subset W_{n}
for which
L\cap V=0
. These correspond to triples of the form

(0,0,\alpha)
, where
\alpha\colon V\cong V^{\prime}
is an isometry. The action of
H_{m}\cong\mathrm{U}(V)
on this set is simply transitive, proving (1).

To prove (2), suppose
L
corresponds to a triple
(Y,Y^{\prime},\alpha)
with
Y\neq 0
. The stabilizer of
L
in
H_{m}
is

\displaystyle\{h\in H_{m}:(hY,Y^{\prime},\alpha h^{-1})=(Y,Y^{\prime},\alpha)\}

\displaystyle=\{h\in H_{m}:hY=Y\mbox{ and }h=\mathrm{id}\mbox{ on }Y^{\perp}/Y\},

which is contained in the proper parabolic subgroup stabilizing
Y
, and contains its unipotent radical.
Hence this stabilizer is negligible.
∎

To complete the proof of Proposition
2.4.6
, we make use of the bijection

P_{n}(F)\backslash H_{n}(F)\cong\{\mbox{Lagrangian subspaces }L\subset W_{n}\}.

If
\gamma\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H_{n}(F)
corresponds to
L\subset W_{n}
, then
I_{\gamma}\subset H_{m}
is equal to the stabilizer of
L
in
H_{m}
. By the lemma such an
I_{\gamma}
is either trivial or negligible, and the
\gamma
for which
I_{\gamma}=1
form a single orbit under
H_{m}(F)
.

Having noted above that
I_{\delta}=1
, we have now proved that
I_{\gamma}\subset H_{m}
is negligible for any double coset

\gamma\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H_{n}(F)/H_{m}(F)

with
\gamma\neq\delta
.
In other words, there exists a proper parabolic subgroup
Q_{\gamma}
with unipotent radical
N_{\gamma}
satisfying

N_{\gamma}\subset I_{\gamma}\subset Q_{\gamma}.

The integral

\int_{[N_{\gamma}]}\Phi_{s}(\gamma i(g_{1},g_{2}))\,f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}=\Phi_{s}(\gamma i(1,g_{2}))\int_{[N_{\gamma}]}f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

vanishes by the cuspidality of
f
, and therefore so does the integral indexed by
\gamma
on the right hand side of (
2.4.10
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.4.2
.

Proposition
2.4.6
implies the first equality in

(2.4.11)

\displaystyle\chi(\det(g_{2}))^{-1}\int_{[H_{m}]}D(g_{1},g_{2}^{\dagger},s,\chi)f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\chi(\det(g_{2}))^{-1}\int_{H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi^{\circ}_{s}(\delta i(g_{1},g_{2}))f(g_{1})\,dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\chi(\det(g_{2}))^{-1}\int_{H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi^{\circ}_{s}(\delta i(g_{2}g_{1},g_{2}))f(g_{2}g_{1})\,dg_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi^{\circ}_{s}(\delta i(g_{1},1))f(g_{2}g_{1})\,dg_{1}.

For the final equality we have used the observation that the composition

H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\xrightarrow{g\mapsto\delta i(g,g)\delta^{-1}}P_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\xrightarrow{\eqref{parabolic character}}\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}

agrees with the determinant on
H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, a consequence of Lemma
2.4.5
.

Viewing (
2.4.11
) as an automorphic form in the variable
g_{2}\in H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, it is a
K_{m}
-fixed vector in the space of
\pi
.
Indeed, the second integral in (
2.4.11
) is right
K_{m}
-invariant because
\Phi_{s}^{\circ}
is right
K_{n}
-invariant, and
i(K_{m},K_{m})\subset K_{n}
.
The final integral in (
2.4.11
) is a linear combination of right translates of
f
, and hence lies in
\pi
.
Up to scaling,
f\in\pi
is the unique
K_{m}
-fixed vector, and we deduce that

\int_{H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi^{\circ}_{s}(\delta i(g_{1},1))\,f(g_{2}g_{1})\,dg_{1}=c(s)\cdot f(g_{2})

for some
c(s)
independent of
g_{2}
.

To compute the scalar
c(s)
, let
\breve{f}\in\breve{\pi}
be the
K_{m}
-fixed vector in the contragredient representation of
\pi
, normalized by
\langle f,\breve{f}\rangle=1
.
Pairing both sides of the above equality against
\breve{f}
results in

\int_{H_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\Phi^{\circ}_{s}(\delta i(h,1))\,\langle\pi(h)f,\breve{f}\rangle\,dh=c(s).

The left hand side is the well-known doubling zeta integral studied in
[
Li92
,
PSR87
]
, see also
[
HKS96
,
Liu11
]
, and we deduce

c(s)=\frac{L(s+1/2,\mathrm{BC}(\pi)\otimes\chi)}{\mathscr{L}_{n}(s,\chi_{0})}.

This completes the proof of the duplication formula.
∎

2.5.
Degenerate coefficients in low rank

We return to the assumption that the unramified Hecke character
\chi\colon\mathbb{A}_{F^{\prime}}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
satisfies (
2.3.1
).

We are interested in degenerate coefficients of the Eisenstein series

3.2.
A low rank case of Conjecture

We will prove Conjecture

Some of the preparatory results needed for this result work in greater generality, so for the moment we work with any positive integers

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a connected quasi-smooth derived Artin stack over
k
with classical truncation
i\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathscr{X}
. If
X
is a local complete intersection over
k
of dimension
\dim(X)=\operatorname{vd}(\mathscr{X})
, where
\operatorname{vd}(\mathscr{X})
denotes the virtual dimension of
\mathscr{X}
, then
\mathscr{X}
is classical and
i\colon X\hookrightarrow\mathscr{X}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The derived pullback of
i
along any derived affine smooth chart
\Spec(R)\to\mathscr{X}
is the canonical inclusion

\Spec(\pi_{0}(R))\hookrightarrow\Spec(R),

where
\pi_{0}(R)
is a local complete intersection and
\dim(\pi_{0}(R))=\operatorname{vd}(R)
.
As the hypotheses and conclusion of the lemma are both smooth-local in
\mathscr{X}
, we may assume
\mathscr{X}=\Spec(R)
and
X=\Spec(\pi_{0}(R))
.

In this setting, the canonical map
R\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{0}(R)
corresponding to
i
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{0}
, so the fiber of this map is 1-connective. By
[
Lur
, Corollary 25.3.6.4]
, the relative cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{\pi_{0}(R)/R}=\mathbb{L}_{i}
is 2-connective, i.e. of Tor-amplitude in degrees
\leq-2
. On the other hand, both
R
and
\pi_{0}(R)
are quasi-smooth, so
\mathbb{L}_{R/k}
and
\mathbb{L}_{\pi_{0}(R)/k}
are perfect of Tor-amplitude in degrees
\geq-1
, which implies that
\mathbb{L}_{i}
is perfect of Tor-amplitude in degrees
\geq-2
. Combining these two observations, it follows that
\mathbb{L}_{i}\simeq M[2]
for some locally free
\pi_{0}(R)
-module
M
. Finally,

\rank(M)=\chi(\mathbb{L}_{i})=\chi(\mathbb{L}_{\pi_{0}(R)/k})-\chi(\mathbb{L}_{R/k})=\dim(\pi_{0}(R))-\operatorname{vd}(R)=0,

so
M
is the zero module, and
\mathbb{L}_{i}\simeq 0
. As
i
also clearly induces an isomorphism on classical truncations, it follows by
[
Lur
, Corollary 25.3.6.6]
that
i
is an isomorphism.
∎

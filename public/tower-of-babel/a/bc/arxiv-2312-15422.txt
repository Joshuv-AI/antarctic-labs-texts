Closest targets in Russell graph measure of strongly monotonic efficiency for an extended facet production possibility set
Kazuyuki Sekitani a and Yu Zhao b

Abstract
This study modifies the Russell graph measure of efficiency to find the closest target from the assessed decision-making unit to the efficient frontier of an empirical production possibility set using an extended facet approach. The closest target requires a single improvement in either an output term or an input term, and needs some positive levels of all inputs to achieve the output target. The practical advantage of the proposed efficiency measure is demonstrated numerically in comparison to other existing non-radial efficiency measures.

Introduction

Data envelopment analysis (DEA) is a mathematical programming method to measure the relative efficiency of the production activities called decision-making units (DMUs).
Some objective functions of DEA models are nonlinear with respect to the levels of input and output improvement.
For example, the DEA model of the Russell graph measure
(
Färe et al. (1985)
, RM;)
has a sum of linear ratios as the objective function and
the slacks-based measure
(
Tone (2001)
, SBM;)
is formulated as a DEA model with a linear fractional function.
SBM is also known as the enhanced Russell graph measure
(
Pastor et al. (1999)
, ERGM;)
.
Recently,
Salahi et al. (2019)
developed robust RM and ERGM DEA models under certain types of uncertainties, while
Tone et al. (2020)
proposed a modified SBM DEA model that can fully rank all DMUs.

For an evaluated DMU, DEA provides the efficiency (inefficiency) score and a projection point as the improvement target. Both SBM (ERGM) and RM include an efficiency measure, i.e., monotonicity, such that a superior DMU has a better efficiency score than a less effective one, which is a desirable property of the models.
However, their projection points are not always the closest ones,
implying a lack of similarity to the evaluated DMU. The concept of similarity of the projection point has been incorporated into prior studies on the least-distance DEA
(
Frei & Harker (1999)
;
Briec (1999)
;
Ando et al. (2012)
;
Aparicio (2016)
, e.g.,)
and its extensions
(
Portela et al. (2003)
;
Tone (2010)
;
Fukuyama et al. (2014)
;
Kao (2022)
, e.g.,)
. The least-distance projection point is known as the closest target of the evaluated DMU.
Previous studies have attempted to reverse the optimisation of the conventional DEA model to find the closest targets.
For example,
Aparicio et al. (2007)
and
Tone (2010)
converted the original SBM model into a maximisation model to bring the projection point of the SBM model closer to the evaluated DMU
(
Kao (2022)
;
Kao (2022)
, see also)
. However, their modified SBM models do not satisfy monotonicity
(
Fukuyama et al. (2014)
, see a counterexample of)
.
As argued by
Ando et al. (2012)
, not only does the least-distance-based inefficiency measure suffer from the failure of weak monotonicity over the efficient frontier, but so does the additive-form least-distance efficiency measure.
The latter has an equivalent objective function to that of the range adjusted measure (RAM) as developed by
Cooper et al. (1999)
.

From a practical perspective, it is necessary to develop a DEA model that can resolve a conflict between the closest targets and strong monotonicity of efficiency measures.
Strong monotonicity is commonly regarded as a more desirable property than weak monotonicity for a well-defined efficiency measure, particularly in the context of least-distance DEA
(
Baek & Lee (2009)
;
Pastor & Aparicio (2010)
, For a detailed discussion, see, e.g.,)
.
In fact,
Aparicio & Pastor (2014)
and
Zhu et al. (2022)
incorporated
an extended facet approach
(
Bessent et al. (1988)
;
Green et al. (1996)
;
Olesen & Petersen (1996)
, e.g.,)

into the output-oriented least-distance efficiency measure for determining the closest target from the assessed DMU to the strongly efficient frontier of the extended production possibility set.
They also demonstrated that strong monotonicity can be ensured under the assumption of the existence of full-dimensional efficient facets
(
Olesen & Petersen (1996)
, FDEFs; e.g.,)
.

Olesen & Petersen (1996)
and
Räty (2002)
have indicated that the extension of FDEFs carries significant implications from an economic perspective. For instance, by extending FDEFs,
the marginal rates of substitution along the frontier are clearly defined and determined.
This approach helps to avoid issues associated with a lack of data variation. Consequently, this approach allows for the consideration of a well-defined efficient frontier,
meaning that we operate not within the standard DEA technology but within an extended production possibility set.
We investigates several technical issues on the extended production possibility set and propose a maximum Russell graph measure (max RM) that provides the closest target for the assessed DMU while satisfying strong monotonicity.

2
Russell graph measure and least-distance DEA

Denote
P
as the production possibility set. We assume that
P
is a polyhedron that includes all convex combinations of the observed input–output vector
(\bm{x}_{j},\bm{y}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
for all
j=1,\dots,n
, while satisfying free disposability
(
Shephard (1970)
;
Färe et al. (1985)
, e.g.,)
.

The efficiency of each DMU is typically measured by comparing it to a dominating projection point on the efficient frontier of the production possibility set.
Traditional radial efficiency measures developed by
Charnes et al. (1978)
and
Banker et al. (1984)
identify the dominating projection points relative to the weakly efficient frontier of
P
, which is defined as

\partial^{w}\left(P\right):=\left\{\,(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\mid(\bm{x}^{\prime},-\bm{y}^{\prime})<(\bm{x},-\bm{y})\Longrightarrow(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y}^{\prime})\notin P\,\right\}.

Consider a subset of
\partial^{w}\left(P\right)
as follows:

\partial^{s}\left(P\right):=\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\left|(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y}^{\prime})\not=(\bm{x},\bm{y}),(\bm{x}^{\prime},-\bm{y}^{\prime})\leq(\bm{x},-\bm{y})\Longrightarrow(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y}^{\prime})\notin P\right.\right\}.

The definitions of
\partial^{s}\left(P\right)
and
\partial^{w}\left(P\right)
imply that
\partial^{s}\left(P\right)\subseteq\partial^{w}\left(P\right)
. A DMU that satisfies
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\partial^{w}\left(P\right)\setminus\partial^{s}\left(P\right)
is said to be weakly efficient, and one that satisfies
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\partial^{s}\left(P\right)
is strongly efficient.

The RM
(
Färe et al. (1985)
)

is a non-radial efficiency measure for non-oriented DEA models and the RM DEA model is formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\mbox{$F(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=$}\min.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi_{r}}\right)

(1)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in P,

(2)

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1\,(i=1,\ldots,m),\ 1\leq\phi_{r}\,(r=1,\ldots,s),

(3)

where
\bm{e}_{l}
is the
l^{\mbox{th}}
unit vector of a proper dimensional space.
The RM DEA model in equations (
1
)–(
3
) is a nonlinear programming problem and can be solved using a second-order cone programming reformulation
(
Sueyoshi & Sekitani (2007)
)
or a semidefinite programming reformulation
(
Halická & Trnovská (2018)
)
.
For an optimal solution
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
of (
1
)–(
3
),
the projection point of
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is then defined as
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}^{*}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)
.
The following theorem ensures that this projection point provided by the RM DEA model lies on the strongly efficient frontier.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
and let
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
be an optimal solution of (
1
)–(
3
). Then,

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}^{*}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P).

See Theorem 3.1 of
Sekitani & Zhao (2023)
for the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,
F(\bm{x},\bm{y})
has a positive value not more than 1. Moreover, the function
F:P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}\to(0,1]
has the following property:

Theorem 2.2
.

For each
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,

F(\bm{x},\bm{y})>F(\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{y}})\mbox{ if }(\bm{x},-\bm{y})\leq(\bar{\bm{x}},-\bar{\bm{y}})\mbox{ and }(\bm{x},-\bm{y})\not=(\bar{\bm{x}},-\bar{\bm{y}}).

(4)

See Proposition 8 of
Sueyoshi & Sekitani (2009)
for the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Following
Färe et al. (1985)
, condition (
4
) of
F
is a desirable property of an efficiency measure and
an efficiency measure satisfying (
4
) is called strongly monotonic. The efficiency measure
F
satisfies

F(\bm{x},\bm{y})\geq F(\bar{\bm{x}},\bar{\bm{y}})\mbox{ if }(\bm{x},-\bm{y})\leq(\bar{\bm{x}},-\bar{\bm{y}}),

(5)

which is weaker than property (
4
). An efficiency measure satisfying (
4
) is called weakly monotonic.
Therefore, the RM
F
is strongly and weakly monotonic over
P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
.

The projection point achieving the closest target plays an important role in benchmark selection and environment efficiency measures, as shown by
Ruiz & Sirvent (2022)
and
An et al. (2015)
.
However, the RM DEA model in (
1
)–(
3
) cannot guarantee the closest target.
The closest target setting has been widely studied in the least-distance DEA literature, which assumes that the closer projection point is easier to improve for the assessed DMU.
A commensurable inefficiency measure of the least-distance DEA model, which was developed by
Aparicio et al. (2007)
, is formulated as

\displaystyle\mbox{$H(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=$}\min.

\displaystyle\left\|\left(1-\theta_{1},\cdots,1-\theta_{m},\phi_{1}-1,\cdots,\phi_{s}-1\right)\right\|_{1}

(6)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P)

(7)

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1\,(i=1,\ldots,m),\ 1\leq\phi_{r}\,(r=1,\ldots,s),

(8)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}
is the
L_{1}
norm, i.e.,
\left\|\bm{z}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|z_{i}|
for
\bm{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k})
.
Let
(\bm{\theta}^{\prime},\bm{\phi}^{\prime})
be an optimal solution of the model in (
6
)–(
8
). The closest target of
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is then given by
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{\prime}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}^{\prime}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)
.
The projection point
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}^{*}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)
of the RM DEA model in (
1
)–(
3
) is not the closest target to
\partial^{s}(P)
if

\displaystyle\left\|\left(1-\theta_{1}^{*},\cdots,1-\theta_{m}^{*},\phi^{*}_{1}-1,\cdots,\phi^{*}_{s}-1\right)\right\|_{1}>

\displaystyle\left\|\left(1-\theta_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,1-\theta_{m}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}_{1}-1,\cdots,\phi^{\prime}_{s}-1\right)\right\|_{1},

or, equivalently,
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}^{*}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}>\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{\prime}
.

The least-distance inefficiency measure
H
over
P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
is represented by the function
H:P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}\to[0,\infty)
.

Ando et al. (2012)
demonstrated that the inefficiency measure
H
satisfies neither weak nor strong monotonicity.

To ensure strong monotonicity of inefficiency measures of the least-distance DEA models,

Aparicio & Pastor (2014)
adopted an extended facet approach
(
Bessent et al. (1988)
;
Green et al. (1996)
;
Olesen & Petersen (1996)
, e.g.,)
and formulated an output–oriented version
of the model in (
6
)–(
8
) as

\displaystyle\mbox{$H^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=$}\min.

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\phi_{1}-1,\cdots,\phi_{s}-1\right)\right\|_{1}

(9)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left(\bm{x},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})

(10)

\displaystyle 1\leq\phi_{r}\ (r=1,\ldots,s),

(11)

where
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
represents an extended facet production possibility set (which will be discussed in detail later).
Then, we have the function
H^{+}:P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}\to[0,\infty)
as an inefficiency measure of the output-oriented least-distance DEA model. While
H^{+}
satisfies strong monotonicity, one practical disadvantage of the inefficiency measure is that the worst score is preliminarily unknown, whereas any efficiency score of an efficiency measure is defined from
0
to
1
(see
Russell & Schworm (2009)
for the range of an efficiency measure). As a result, inefficiency measures are not as popular as efficiency measures in DEA applications.

3
Maximum Russell graph measure and free-lunch issue in the extended facet production possibility set

To resolve the conflict between a strongly monotonic efficiency measure and the closest target, we consider a non-oriented DEA model,

\displaystyle\max.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi_{r}}\right)

(12)

s.t.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\ \sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})

(13)

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1\,(i=1,\ldots,m),\ 1\leq\phi_{r}\ (r=1,\ldots,s),

(14)

which is a hybrid model of the RM DEA model in (
1
)–(
3
) and
the least-distance DEA model in (
6
)–(
8
) with a slight modification by replacing
\partial^{s}(P)
with
\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})
. This modification is critical, because without it,
there exists a counterexample in Appendix
A
showing that the unmodified efficiency measure fails to satisfy the strong monotonicity.

As preparation for clarifying the properties of our proposed model, we will now show how to expand
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
from
P
and further discuss some technical details on
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
.
Assuming variable returns to scale (VRS), we can empirically construct the production possibility set
P
as follows:
P:=

\left\{\left(\bm{x},\bm{y}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{+}\left|\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\bm{x}_{j}\leq\bm{x},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\bm{y}_{j}\geq\bm{y},\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}=1,\bm{\lambda}\geq\bm{0}\right.\right\}.

(15)

A subset
\cal F
of
P
is called a face if there exists a coefficient vector
(\bm{v},\bm{u},\psi)\in\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

{\cal F}=P\cap\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})|-\bm{v}^{\top}\bm{x}+\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{y}-\psi=0\right\}
and

-\bm{v}^{\top}\bm{x}++\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{y}-\psi\leq 0\mbox{ for any }(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P.

If a face
\cal F
satisfies
{\cal F}\subseteq\partial^{s}(P)
, then
\cal F
is called an efficient face. The efficient face
\cal F
satisfies

{\cal F}=P\cap\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})|-\bm{v}^{\top}\bm{x}+\bm{u}^{\top}\bm{y}-\psi=0\right\}

for some
(\bm{v},\bm{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
and some
\psi\in\mathbb{R}
. See
Fukuyama & Sekitani (2012)
’s Lemma 2.
Furthermore, a face
\cal F
is termed as a FDEF if
{\cal F}\subseteq\partial^{s}(P)
and
\dim{\cal F}=m+s-1

(
Olesen & Petersen (1996)
, e.g.,)
.

Suppose now that
P
has at least one FDEF. Let
{\cal F}_{1},\cdots,{\cal F}_{K}
be all the FDEFs of
P
. Then, there exist
(\bm{v}^{k},\bm{u}^{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
and
\psi^{k}
such that

{\cal F}_{k}=P\cap\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})|-\bm{v}^{k\top}\bm{x}+\bm{u}^{k\top}\bm{y}-\psi^{k}=0\right\}
for all
k=1,\ldots,K
.

In
Olesen & Petersen (1996)
and
Aparicio & Pastor (2013)
;
Aparicio & Pastor (2014)
,
the extended facet approach defines
an expansion of
P
based on the FDEFs as

\displaystyle P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}:=\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{+}\left|-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}v^{k}_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}u^{k}_{r}y_{r}\leq\psi^{k},k=1,\ldots,K\right.\right\},

(16)

which is called the extended facet production possibility set.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that all the FDEFs of
P
defined by (
15
) are
{\cal F}_{1},\cdots,{\cal F}_{K}
and let
J^{k}=\left\{\,j\,\left|(\bm{x}_{j},\bm{y}_{j})\in{\cal F}_{k}\,\right\}\right.
.
The envelopment form of
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
defined by (
16
) can be represented as

\displaystyle\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{+}\left|\sum_{j\in J^{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}\bm{x}_{j}\leq\bm{x},\sum_{j\in J^{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}\bm{y}_{j}\geq\bm{y},\sum_{j\in J^{k}}\lambda_{j}^{k}=1,k=1,\ldots,K\right\}.\right.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The extended facet approach is one of expanding the original production possibility set
P
.
Podinovski (2004)
shows that the production trade-off assumption expands
P
and
the expanding set may violate the no free lunch axiom, which is discussed in
Shephard (1970)
,
Färe et al. (1985)
, and
Russell & Schworm (2018)
.

\cal P
is characterised as an existence of a certain input–output vector of

.

If there exists
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{s}\setminus\{0\}
such that
(\bm{0},\bm{y})\in\cal P
, then the production possibility set
\cal P
allows free lunch and
(\bm{0},\bm{y})
is called a free-lunch vector.

4
Properties of maximum Russell graph measure

Let
(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})
be a feasible solution of the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
).
Then, it follows from (
14
) that
the objective function value of any feasible solution of the DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
) ranges from 0 to 1.
Let
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
be the maximum value of the DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
). We have

0<G(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq 1\mbox{ for all }(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}.

We therefore call
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
a max RM on
P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
.

The max RM satisfies strong monotonicity. To show this, let

\displaystyle g(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}):=\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi_{r}}\right).

(17)

Define now

\displaystyle G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=\max\left\{\,g(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\left|\,\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{w}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}),\\
\eqref{p3}\end{array}\right\}.\right.

Since we have

\displaystyle G(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\max\left\{\,g(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\,\left|\,\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}),\\
~\eqref{p3}\end{array}\right\},\right.

and
\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})\subseteq\partial^{w}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})
, we then obtain the following property:

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,

G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\geq G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\delta_{i}:=1-\theta_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then, we can obtain

g(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})=\frac{m}{m+s}+\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-\delta_{i})+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi_{r}}\right).

Define
g^{\prime}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\phi}):=\frac{1}{m+s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-\delta_{i})+\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{1}{\phi_{r}}\right)
. We, therefore, have

g(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})=\frac{m}{m+s}+g^{\prime}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\phi})
and
g^{\prime}
is a quasi-convex function over
\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}_{++}
satsifying

g^{\prime}(\bm{\delta},\bm{\phi})>g^{\prime}\left(\bar{\bm{\delta}},\bar{\bm{\phi}}\right)\mbox{ if }(\bm{\delta},-\bm{\phi})\leq(\bar{\bm{\delta}},-\bar{\bm{\phi}})\mbox{ and }(\bm{\delta},-\bm{\phi})\not=(\bar{\bm{\delta}},-\bar{\bm{\phi}}).

(24)

Let
\bm{e}
be a vector in a properly dimensional space, where all components are 1.
The efficiency measure
G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
satisfies weak monotonicity
(
Sekitani & Zhao (2023)
)
and the following statements hold:

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,

G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\max\left\{\,\begin{array}[]{l}\max\left\{\left.g(\bm{e}-{(1-\theta^{\prime}_{i})\bm{e}_{i}},\bm{e})\right|\,i=1,\ldots.m\right\}\\
\max\left\{\left.g(\bm{e},{\bm{e}+(\phi^{\prime}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}})\right|\,r=1,\ldots.s\right\}\end{array}\,\right\},

(25)

where

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}_{i}=

\displaystyle\min\left\{\theta\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\bm{x}-(1-\theta)x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\bm{y}\right)\in P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}\\
0\leq\theta\leq 1\end{array}\right.\right\}\mbox{ for all }i=1,\ldots,m,

and

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{r}=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\phi\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\bm{x},\bm{y}+(\phi-1)y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}\\
1\leq\phi\end{array}\right.\right\}\mbox{ for all }r=1,\ldots,s.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,
\bm{\theta}^{\prime}
of (
4.2
) and
\bm{\phi}^{\prime}
of (
4.2
) satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\bm{x}-(1-\theta^{\prime}_{i})x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\bm{y}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})\mbox{ and }\theta^{\prime}_{i}>0

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=g(\bm{e}-(1-{\theta^{\prime}_{i})\bm{e}_{i}},\bm{e})\,

\displaystyle\left(\bm{x},\bm{y}+(\phi^{\prime}_{r}-1)y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=g(\bm{e},\bm{e}+({\phi^{\prime}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}}),

and

G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\max\left\{\,\begin{array}[]{l}\max\left\{\,g(\bm{e}-(1-{\theta^{\prime}_{i})\bm{e}_{i}},\bm{e})\,\left|\,\theta^{\prime}_{i}>0\,\right.\right\}\\
\max\left\{\,g(\bm{e},\bm{e}+({\phi^{\prime}_{r}-1)\bm{e}_{r}})\,\left|\,r=1,\ldots,s\,\right.\right\}\end{array}\right\}.

(30)

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma
4.3
implies that
G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
provides the projection point onto
\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})\setminus\partial^{w}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}})
.
Therefore, we have
G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
for any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
. Moreover,
Theorem
4.4
shows that the efficiency measure
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
satisfies strong monotonicity on
P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,

G^{w}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
. Moreover, the efficiency measure
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is strongly monotonic on
P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Theorem
4.4
.

We next show the properties of the projection point provided by the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
).
The commensurable Hölder input distance function is defined as

D^{-}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1-\theta_{i})\left|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\bm{y}\right)\in\partial^{w}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}),\ \bm{0}\leq\bm{\theta}\leq\bm{e}\right\}\right.

and the commensurable Hölder output distance function is

D^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=\min\left\{\sum_{r=1}^{s}(\phi_{r}-1)\left|\left(\bm{x},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{w}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}),\bm{e}\leq\bm{\phi}\right\}.\right.

We obtain the following results from Corollary 3 of
Briec (1999)
:

\displaystyle D^{-}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=1-\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}\min\left\{\,\theta\,\left|\,(\bm{x}-(1-\theta)x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\bm{y})\in P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}\right\}\right.,

(31)

\displaystyle D^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\min_{r=1,\ldots,s}\max\left\{\,\phi\,\left|\,(\bm{x},\bm{y}+(\phi-1)y_{r}\bm{e}_{r})\in P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}\right\}-1\right..

(32)

Using
D^{-}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
and
D^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, the max RM can be computed as follows:

Theorem 4.5
.

For any
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{m+s}_{++}
,

G(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\frac{1}{m+s}\times\max\left\{m+s-D^{-}(\bm{x},\bm{y}),\ m+s-\frac{D^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y})}{1+D^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y})}\right\}.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Theorem
4.5
.

A projection point provided by the commensurable Hölder input distance function
D^{-}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is called the input-oriented closest target and
a projection point provided by the commensurable Hölder output distance function
D^{+}(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is called the output-oriented closest target.
Theorem
4.5
indicates that a projection point provided by the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
) is either the input-oriented closest target or the output-oriented closest target.
Furthermore, expression (
30
) in Lemma
4.3
implies that there exist a positive input element in the projection point. These properties of the projection point provided by the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
)
are summarised as follows:

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose that
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
and let
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
be an optimal solution of the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
). Then,
the projection point
(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi^{*}_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r})
is either the
input-oriented closest target or the output-oriented one. Moreover,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{++}
and

(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi^{*}_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r})

is not a free-lunch vector.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Corollary
4.6
.

If
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
is represented by a Minkowski sum of a convex hull of a
finite number of vertices and a conical hull of a finite number of rays, then two optimisation problems,

\min\left\{\theta\left|(\bm{x}-(1-\theta)x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\bm{y})\in P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}\right\}\right.\mbox{ and }\max\left\{\phi\left|(\bm{x},\bm{y}+(\phi-1)y_{r}\bm{e}_{r})\in P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}\right\},\right.

are formulated as linear programming (LP) problems
(see
Sekitani & Zhao (2023)
, for details)
, and hence,
G(\bm{x},\bm{y})
can be solved by
(m+s)
LPs.

5
Comparisons with SBM and its variations

5.1
SBM and maximum SBM

Pastor et al. (1999)
modified the objective function
g(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})
of the RM DEA model into
\rho(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi}):=

\frac{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}}{\frac{1}{s}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}}

and developed an ERGM DEA model

\min\left\{\left.\rho(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\right|\,\eqref{Q2},\ \eqref{Q3}\right\},

which is equivalent to the SBM DEA model.
The following hybrid non-oriented DEA models between the SBM and the least-distance inefficiency measure are from
Aparicio et al. (2007)
;
Tone (2010)
and
Aparicio & Pastor (2014)
:

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\left\{\,\rho(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\left|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P),\eqref{Q3}\right\}\right.

(33)

and

\displaystyle\max

\displaystyle\left\{\,\rho(\bm{\theta},\bm{\phi})\left|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)\in\partial^{s}(P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}),\eqref{Q3}\right\}\right.,

(34)

respectively.

Fukuyama et al. (2014)
showed that the efficiency measure provided by the hybrid non-oriented DEA model in (
33
) violates weak monotonicity.

Sekitani & Zhao (2023)
later showed that the efficiency measure provided by the hybrid non-oriented DEA model in (
34
) violates strong monotonicity, while
Aparicio & Pastor (2014)
showed that an output-oriented version of (
34
) satisfies strong monotonicity.
An efficiency measure provided by the hybrid non-oriented DEA model in (
34
) is called a max SBM, which is denoted by
\Gamma(\bm{x},\bm{y})
for each
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
.

For each
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
, an optimal solution
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
of the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
) is
a feasible solution of the max SBM DEA model in (
34
). Since Corollary
4.6
implies
\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{++}
, we have

0<\rho(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})\leq\Gamma(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\rho(\bm{\theta}^{\#},\bm{\phi}^{\#}),

where
(\bm{\theta}^{\#},\bm{\phi}^{\#})
is an optimal solution of the max SBM DEA model in (
34
).
It follows from
\rho(\bm{\theta}^{\#},\bm{\phi}^{\#})>0
that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta^{\#}_{i}>0
and
\bm{\theta}^{\#}\not=\bm{0}
.
This means that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta^{\#}_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i}\not=\bm{0}
and
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta^{\#}_{i}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi^{\#}_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)
is not a free-lunch vector.
The following corollary implies that the projection point provided by the max SBM DEA model in (
34
) is not a free-lunch vector.

Corollary 5.1
.

Suppose that
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
and let
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
be an optimal solution of the max SBM DEA model in (
34
). Then,

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i}\not=\bm{0}
and
the projection point

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta_{i}^{*}x_{i}\bm{e}_{i},\sum_{r=1}^{s}\phi^{*}_{r}y_{r}\bm{e}_{r}\right)

is not a free-lunch vector.

The projection point provided by the max SBM DEA model in (
34
) may have an improvement input value of
0
, while the projection point provided by the max RM DEA model in (
12
)–(
14
)
avoids a completely vanishing improvement input.
The existence of a completely vanishing improvement input leads to the violation of strong monotonicity of the efficiency measure
\Gamma
as follows:

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in P\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+s}_{++}
and let
(\bm{\theta}^{*},\bm{\phi}^{*})
be an optimal solution of the max SBM DEA model in (
34
).
If
\theta_{i}^{*}=0
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, then,

\Gamma(\bm{x},\bm{y})\leq\Gamma(\bm{x}+\tau\bm{e}_{i},\bm{y})\mbox{ for any }\tau>0.

See Appendix
B
for the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Corollaries
5.1
and
4.6
guarantee that both the max SBM and max RM provide projection points that are not a free-lunch vector; however,
the max SBM may have a completely vanishing improvement input of the projection point.
Theorem
5.2
shows that the max SBM
\Gamma
violates strong monotonicity if a completely vanishing improvement input exists.
Theorem
4.4
means that the max RM
G
has a theoretical advantage of strong monotonicity over the max SBM
\Gamma
.

also allows free lunch.

has three inputs and two outputs, and its extended facet production possibility set is constructed by the supporting hyperplanes of three FDEFs:

generated from the dataset in

has 18 FDEFs and at least one of the 18 FDEFs has a nonpositive coefficient for the intercept term.

The DEA models under the extended facet production possibility set
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
require either enumerating all FDEFs of the original production possibility set
P
or a
mixed integer programming (MIP) solver
(
Olesen & Petersen (1996)
)
. Enumerating all the FDEFs has high computational complexity and the MIP solver needs precision parameter tuning to satisfy the feasibility and optimality conditions.
Therefore, computational complexity and calculation accuracy are serious problems for solving the DEA models under
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
.

We identified two issues associated with the extended facet production possibility set
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
: the existence of a free lunch vector and the vanishing of an input.
It has been confirmed that both issues occur in four real-world datasets and cannot be ignored practically. The issue of the vanishing of an input occurs independently of the existence of free lunch vectors and is more likely to occur.
The vanishing of an input is a serious concern because it not only impairs the practicality of projection points but also undermines the reliability of efficiency measures.
The proposed max RM DEA model can avoid the issue of the vanishing of an input with certainty. It is a future task to verify the existence of DEA models other than the proposed max RM DEA model that can avoid the issue of the vanishing of an input.

An extended facet production possibility set
P_{\scalebox{0.5}{\it EXFA}}
is a kind of an empirical production possibility set that expands from the original one
P
.
Imposing the production trade-off assumption
(
Podinovski (2004)
)
on the production possibility set
P
expands the original one
P
, which is also an empirical expanded production possibility set from
P
.
The production possibility under a production trade-off assumption is often formulated as a dual form of weight restrictions such as assurance region methods.
The existing DEA models under the production trade-off assumption associated with assurance region methods never need the enumeration of all FDEFs and MIP solvers
(
Podinovski (2007)
, see,)
.
This may overcome the problems of computational complexity and calculation accuracy on the empirical expanded production possibility set. In fact, Theorems 4.1, 4.2, and 4.3 of
Sekitani & Zhao (2023)
show
that an output-oriented DEA model associated with a certain assurance region method does not need to enumerate all FDEFs and solving
s
LPs is sufficient to compute the model.
Therefore, Theorem
4.5
and Corollary
4.6
imply that
the similar merit of computation could hold for the max RM DEA model under the empirical expanded production possibility set.

LP solvability and no free-lunch closest target setting will be beneficial for various DEA applications under more complex structures and circumstances, such as network DEA and imprecise DEA. As part of further research, we aim to modify the max RM DEA model to estimate the best-case efficiency under uncertainty, as discussed in
Toloo & Mensah (2019)
and
Toloo et al. (2022)
.

Nested Bethe Ansatz for RTT–Algebra of U q ​ ( sp ​ ( 2 ​ n ) ) U_{q}\bigl(\mathrm{sp}(2n)\bigr) Type


Abstract
We study the highest weight representations of the RTT–algebras for the R–matrix of sp q ​ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{sp}_{q}(2n) type by the nested algebraic Bethe ansatz. It is a generalization of our study for R–matrix of sp ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{sp}(2n) and so ⁡ ( 2 ​ n ) \mathrm{so}(2n) type.

Introduction

The formulation of the quantum inverse scattering method, or
algebraic Bethe ansatz, by the Leningrad school
[
1
]

provides eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the transfer matrix. The
latter is the generating function of the conserved quantities of a
large family of quantum integrable models. The transfer matrix
eigenvectors are constructed from the representation theory of the
RTT–algebras. In order to construct these eigenvectors, one should
first prepare Bethe vectors, depending on a set of complex
variables. The first formulation of the Bethe vectors for the

\mathrm{gl}(n)
–invariant models was given by P.P. Kulish and N.Yu.
Reshetikhin in
[
2
]
where the nested algebraic Bethe ansatz
was introduced. These vectors are given by recursion on the rank of
the algebra. Our calculation is some
q
–generalization of the
construction which we published in recent works
[
3
]
,

[
4
]
and
[
6
]
for the non-deformed case of

\mathrm{sp}(2n)
,
\mathrm{so}(2n)
and
\mathrm{sp}(4)
. Our construction of
Bethe vectors used the new RTT–algebra
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
which is
defined in section 3 and is not the RTT–subalgebra of

\mathrm{sp}_{q}(2n)
type. This algebra has two RTT–subalgebras of

\mathrm{gl}_{q}(n)
type and the study of the nested Bethe ansatz for
this RTT–algebra is in progress. The simplest case for
n=2
was
really solved and we will publish it in the next paper. Our
construction of Bethe vectors is in any sense a generalization of
Reshetikhin’s results
[
7
]
. Another approach to the
nested Bethe ansatz for very special representations of
RTT–algebras of
\mathrm{sp}(2n)
type was given by Martin and Ramas

[
8
]
.

The proofs of many claims are explained from the reason of transparency
of the main text in the Appendix.

Basic definitions and notation

Let indices go trough the set
\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,\pm n\}
. We will
denote by
\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}
the matrices that have all elements equal to
zero with the exception of the element on the
i
-th row and
k
-th
column that is equal to one. Then relation

\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}\mathbf{E}^{s}_{r}=\delta^{k}_{r}\mathbf{E}^{s}_{i}
is valid and

\mathbf{I}={\textstyle\sum\limits_{k=-n}^{n}}\mathbf{E}^{k}_{k}
is the unit matrix.

We will consider the R–matrix of
U_{q}\bigl(\mathrm{sp}(2n)\bigr)
which
has the shape

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{R}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha(x)}\Bigl({\textstyle\sum\limits_{i,k;\,i\neq\pm k}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{k}_{k}+f(x){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}+f(x^{-1}q^{-n-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{-i}_{-i}+\\[9.0pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt+g(x){\textstyle\sum\limits_{k<i}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}-g(x^{-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i<k}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}-\\[9.0pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt-g(xq^{n+1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{k<i}}q^{k-i}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{k}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{-i}_{-k}+g(x^{-1}q^{-n-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i<k}}q^{k-i}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{k}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{-i}_{-k}\Bigr)\end{array}

where
\epsilon_{i}=\mathrm{sign}(i)
and

f(x)=\frac{xq-x^{-1}q^{-1}}{x-x^{-1}}\,,\qquad g(x)=\frac{x(q-q^{-1})}{x-x^{-1}}\,,\qquad\alpha(x)=1+\frac{q-q^{-1}}{x-x^{-1}}\,.

This R–matrix satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation

\mathbf{R}_{1,2}(x)\mathbf{R}_{1,3}(xy)\mathbf{R}_{2,3}(y)=\mathbf{R}_{2,3}(y)\mathbf{R}_{1,3}(xy)\mathbf{R}_{1,2}(x)

and is invertible. Concretely, the inverse matrix has the form

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{R}^{-1}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha(x^{-1})}\Bigl({\textstyle\sum\limits_{i,k;\,i\neq\pm k}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{k}_{k}+f(x^{-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}+f(xq^{-n-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{i}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{-i}_{-i}-\\[9.0pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt-g(x){\textstyle\sum\limits_{k<i}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}+g(x^{-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i<k}}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}+\\[9.0pt]
\hskip 85.35826pt+g(xq^{-n-1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{k<i}}q^{i-k}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{k}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{-i}_{-k}-g(x^{-1}q^{n+1}){\textstyle\sum\limits_{i<k}}q^{i-k}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{k}\mathbf{E}^{i}_{k}\otimes\mathbf{E}^{-i}_{-k}\Bigr)\end{array}

The RTT–algebra of
U_{q}\bigl(\mathrm{sp}(2n)\bigr)
type is an
associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
with unit that is generated by

T^{i}_{k}(x)
for which the monodromy operator

\mathbf{T}(x)={\textstyle\sum\limits_{i,k=-n}^{n}}\mathbf{E}^{k}_{i}\otimes T^{i}_{k}(x)

fulfills the RTT–equation

\mathbf{R}_{1,2}(xy^{-1})\mathbf{T}_{1}(x)\mathbf{T}_{2}(y)=\mathbf{T}_{2}(y)\mathbf{T}_{1}(x)\mathbf{R}_{1,2}(xy^{-1})\,.

From the invertibility of the R–matrix we have that the operator

H(x)=\operatorname{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits\bigl(\mathbf{T}(x)\bigr)={\textstyle\sum\limits_{i=-n}^{n}}T^{i}_{i}(x)

fulfills the equation
H(x)H(y)=H(y)H(x)
for any
x
and
y
.

We suppose that in the representation space
\mathcal{W}
of the RTT–algebra
\mathcal{A}

there exists a vacuum vector
\omega\in\mathcal{W}
, for which

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{A}\omega
and

T^{i}_{k}(x)\omega=0\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i<k\,,\qquad T^{i}_{i}(x)\omega=\lambda_{i}(x)\omega\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i=\pm 1,\,\pm 2,\,\ldots,\,\pm n\,.

In the vector space
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{A}\omega
, we will look for
eigenvectors of
H(x)
.

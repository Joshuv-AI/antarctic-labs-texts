Weakly maxitive set functions and their possibility distributions Thanks: We thank two anonymous referees for their helpful suggestions and remarks.
Michael Kupper, José M. Zapata

Abstract
. The Shilkret integral with respect to a completely maxitive capacity is fully determined by a possibility distribution. In this paper, we introduce a weaker topological form of maxitivity and show that under this assumption the Shilkret integral is still determined by its possibility distribution for functions that are sufficiently regular. Motivated by large deviations theory, we provide a Laplace principle for maxitive integrals and characterize the possibility distribution under certain separation and convexity assumptions. Moreover, we show a maxitive integral representation result for weakly maxitive non-linear expectations. The theoretical results are illustrated by providing large deviations bounds for sequences of capacities, and by deriving a monotone analogue of Cramér’s theorem. Key words: Shilkret integral, capacity, possibility distribution, weak maxitivity, large deviation principle, Laplace principle AMS 2020 Subject Classification: Primary 28E10, 60A86; Secondary 28A25, 28C10, 60F10



1.
Introduction

Non-additive set functions, fuzzy measures and capacities play an important role in the theory of decision making under risk and uncertainty.
They appear in possibility theory, idempotent/tropical mathematics and related fields, where maxitive measures and their maxitive integrals are studied; see, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
,
19
,
30
,
33
]
.
In this article we focus on the
Shilkret integral
introduced in
[
30
]
.
An extension of this integral to functions taking negative values, and its complete characterization was given in
[
6
,
7
]
. On the one hand, if
\Pi
is a possibility measure
[
33
]
, then the Shilkret integral is given by

\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\Pi=\sup_{x}f(x)\pi(x),

where
\pi
denotes the corresponding possibility distribution. In this case, both the set function
\Pi
and the Shilkret integral
\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\Pi
are completely maxitive.
On the other hand, for the particular set function
\bar{\Pi}_{A}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in A)^{1/n}
, where
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of random variables,
the Laplace principle from the theory of large deviations ensures under reasonable assumptions that

\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\bar{\Pi}=\sup_{x}f(x)e^{-I(x)},

where
I
denotes the
rate function; cf. Puhalskii
[
28
]
. Although the two representations for the Shilkret integral are very similar, in general, the second one is only valid for certain continuous functions. Thus, the set function
\bar{\Pi}
satisfies only a weaker form of maxitivity, and consequently is not a possibility measure. Nevertheless,
\bar{\pi}(x)=e^{-I(x)}
can still be viewed as a corresponding possibility distribution. In this article, we characterize the class of set functions which admit a possibility distribution in a weaker topological sense by means of different notions of maxitivity.

In Section
2
, we introduce some basics on possibility distributions and motivate our results.
Section
3
includes the key bounds on maxitive integrals and their connection to weakly maxitive set functions.
In Section
4
, we focus on a maxitive integral representation result for weakly maxitive non-linear expectations.
The results are then applied in Section
5
, where we show that the basic results of large deviations theory are valid for general maxitive integrals; in particular, the equivalence
between the monotone large deviation principle and the monotone Laplace principle. In Section
6
, we provide conditions ensuring that the rate function is convex and study the corresponding representation.
Finally, in Section
7
, the theoretical results are illustrated with two examples.
On the one hand, we study the asymptotic behaviour of a sequence of capacities by providing some large deviations bounds, on the other hand we establish a monotone analogue of Cramér’s theorem for the sample mean of i.i.d. sequences. The paper concludes with an appendix, where we provide some separation results in preordered topological groups.

Background and motivation

Let
E
be a non-empty set and
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of subsets of
E
such that
\emptyset,E\in\mathcal{A}
. A set function
\Pi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
capacity
, if
\Pi_{\emptyset}=0
,
\Pi_{E}=1
, and
\Pi_{A}\leq\Pi_{B}
whenever
A\subset B
.
1

1

Typically
\mathcal{A}
is endowed with some algebraic structure. Here, for the sake of generality we do not assume any structrure on
\mathcal{A}
.

Among the most important examples of capacities, we find probability measures in standard probability theory
[
18
]
, possibility measures in possibility

[
11
]
, and upper/lower probabilities in the theory of imprecise probabilities
[
32
]
. A capacity
\Pi
is called a

[
33
]
, if there exists a
possibility distribution

\pi\colon E\to[0,1]
such that

(2.1)

\Pi_{A}=\sup_{x\in A}\pi(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }A\in\mathcal{A}.

A capacity
\Pi
which admits a possibility distribution is automatically completely maxitive, i.e.,

(2.2)

\Pi_{A}\leq\vee_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\Pi_{A_{i}}\quad\mbox{ whenever }A\subset\cup_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}A_{i},

for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
and every family
(A_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\subset\mathcal{A}
. Conversely, if
\mathcal{A}
is closed under arbitrary unions and complements, then any supremum preserving capacity
\Pi
admits a
possibility distribution which is uniquely determined by
\pi(x)=\Pi_{\cap\{A\in\mathcal{A}\colon x\in A\}}
for all
x\in E
; cf.
[
10
]
.
Under additional continuity on the capacity and the assumption that
\mathcal{A}
is rich enough,
2

2

E.g. the collection
\mathcal{A}
forms a
\sigma
-algebra on a separable metric space which contains all Borel sets.
the existence of a possibility distribution is guaranteed if
\Pi
is only finitely maxitive, i.e.,
\Pi_{A}\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}\Pi_{B_{i}}
for all
A,B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}
; cf.
[
1
,
24
]
.

The
Shilkret integral
of a function
f\colon E\to[0,\infty]
with respect to a capacity
\Pi
is defined by

(2.3)

\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\Pi:=\underset{c\in(0,\infty)}{\sup}c\Pi_{\{f>c\}}.

Originally, the Shilkret integral was introduced for supremum preserving capacities, also called maxitive probabilities or idempotent probabilities
[
30
]
.
However, this definition is valid for general capacities (not necessarily maxitive) provided that
f
is
\mathcal{A}
-measurable, i.e.,
\{f>c\}\in\mathcal{A}
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
In case that
\Pi
admits a possibility distribution
\pi
, then the Shilkret integral takes the form

(2.4)

\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\Pi=\sup_{x\in E}f(x)\pi(x)

for all
\mathcal{A}
-measurable functions
f\colon E\to[0,\infty]
.

In this article, we analyze to what extent a capacity is still determined by
a possibility distribution if the representation (
2.4
) only holds for a certain class of
\mathcal{A}
-measurable functions, e.g., all continuous, continuous bounded, or continuous increasing functions.
3
3

3

Here, we assume that
E
is a topological preordered space.

We will see that this exactly holds when the capacity satisfies a weaker form of maxitivity.
4
4

4

I.e.
\Pi_{A}\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}\Pi_{B_{i}}

for (upwards closed) closed sets
A
and (upwards closed) open sets
B_{1},\dots,B_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}
.
We emphasize that no additional continuity on
\Pi
is required.
To do so, we will provide bounds for the Shilkret integral with respect to general capacities.
For a capacity
\Pi
and a function
\pi\colon E\to[0,1]
, a key result of this work states that

(2.5)

\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\Pi\geq\sup_{x\in E}f(x)\pi(x)\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\Pi_{O}\geq\sup_{x\in O}\pi(x),

where the first inequality holds for all (increasing) lower semicontinuous functions
f
, and the second inequality holds for all (upwards closed) open sets
O
. Likewise,

(2.6)

\int^{S}f\,{\rm d}\Pi\leq\sup_{x\in E}f(x)\pi(x)\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad\Pi_{C}\leq\sup_{x\in C}\pi(x),

where the first inequality holds for all (increasing) upper semicontinuous functions
f
, and the second inequality holds for all (upwards closed) closed sets
C
.
Instead of assuming the rather restrictive assumption (
2.1
), the relation between the capacity and its possibility distribution is relaxed to
inequalities which are required only for certain nice topological sets. As a consequence, if the capacity
\Pi
satisfies both the upper and lower bound, then the Shilkret integral has the representation (
2.4
) for all (increasing) continuous functions.

The Shilkret integral is only defined for non-negative functions.
In order to deal with real-valued functions, we present and prove our results in terms of the maxitive integral introduced by Cattaneo
[
7
]
, which is obtained as a transformation of the Shilkret integral. We say that a set function
J\colon\mathcal{A}\to[-\infty,0]
is a
concentration
if
J_{E}=0
,
J_{\emptyset}=-\infty
, and
J_{A}\leq J_{B}
whenever
A\subset B
. In other words,
J
is a concentration if and only if
e^{J}
is a capacity.
The maxitive integral of an
\mathcal{A}
-measurable function
f\colon E\to[-\infty,\infty)
with respect to the concentration
J
is defined as

(2.7)

\phi_{J}(f):=\log\int^{S}e^{f}\,{\rm d}e^{J}=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\}.

As discussed in
[
7
]
, the functional
\phi_{J}
shares the properties of a monetary risk measure
[
13
]
,
and satisfies in particular the
translation property

\phi_{J}(f+c)=\phi_{J}(f)+c
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
In contrast, the Shilkret integral (
2.3
) fails the translation property unless the capacity
\Pi
only assumes the values
0
and
1
; cf.
[
9
]
.
In particular, aside from this degenerate case, the Shilkret integral is neither a coherent prevision, nor a monetary risk measure.
By defining the rate function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
by
I(x):=-\log\pi(x)
, the bound (
2.5
) takes the form

(2.8)

\phi_{J}(f)\geq\sup_{x\in E}\{f(x)-I(x)\}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad J_{O}\geq-\inf_{x\in O}I(x),

for all (increasing) lower semicontinuous functions
f
, and all (upwards closed) open sets
O
, and
the bound (
2.6
) translates to

(2.9)

\phi_{J}(f)\leq\sup_{x\in E}\{f(x)-I(x)\}\quad\mbox{if and only if}\quad J_{C}\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x),

for all (increasing) upper semicontinuous functions
f
, and all (upwards closed) closed sets
C
.

Similar type of bounds appear in the theory of large deviations, where the capacity has the special form
{\Pi}_{A}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in A)^{1/n}

for a sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of random variables with values in a completely regular topological space
E
.
In that case, the corresponding concentration is given by
J_{A}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in A)
with respective maxitive integral

\phi_{J}(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\exp(nf(X_{n}))]
for all bounded continuous functions
f
on
E
. Then the equivalences (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) amount to the well-known equivalence between the large deviation principle (LDP) and the Laplace principle (LP); cf.
[
5
,
28
]
. In this article, we will show that the key concepts of large deviations theory can be understood and extended to the framework of
weakly maxitive concentrations and their maxitive integrals; e.g., the equivalences (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) establish the equivalence between the LDP and the LP for general concentrations. This covers situations that are not captured by the standard setting of large deviations theory.
For instance, in the theory of imprecise probability
[
32
]
, we may be interested in large deviations bounds for upper probabilities of the form
\overline{\mathbb{P}}(A)=\sup_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{P}(A)
for a set
\mathcal{P}
of probability measures.
Then we consider the concentration
J_{A}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\overline{\mathbb{P}}(X_{n}\in A)
, for which we will show
in Subsection
7.1
that its maxitive integral is given by

\phi_{J}(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exp(nf(X_{n}))>x\right){\rm d}x.

Standard large deviations theory provides conditions such that the usual upper bound
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in C)\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x)
is valid for all closed sets
C\subset E
, and the usual lower bound
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in O)\geq-\inf_{x\in O}I(x)
holds for all open sets
O\subset E
.
However, one may be interested in finding bounds on certain smaller classes of sets.
One of the features of the presented framework is that, by considering a preorder relation
\leq
on
E
, we may restrict ourselves to the class of upwards closed sets
for which we characterize large deviation bounds. As a result, we obtain a monotone version of Cramér’s theorem which provides new large deviations bounds for the sample mean of i.i.d. sequences.

3.
Bounds for maxitive integral and weak maxitivity

In this section, we introduce the basic concepts and provide the key bounds for concentrations and the respective maxitive integrals.
These bounds ensure a weak form of maxitivity which allows to connect concentrations and their maxitive integrals with rate functions.

3.1.
Setting and notation

Let
(E,\leq)
be a topological preordered space.
5
5

5

Recall that a preorder is a reflexive and transitive binary relation. We do not assume any relations between
the topology and the preorder.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a base of the topology, and define
\mathcal{U}_{x}:=\{U\in\mathcal{U}\colon x\in U\}
for all
x\in E
.
Moreover, for
A\subset E
, we define the

and the

as

{\uparrow}A:=\{y\in E\colon x\leq y\mbox{ for some }x\in A\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad{\downarrow}A:=\{y\in E\colon y\leq x\mbox{ for some }x\in A\}.

We say that

is

if

, and

if

.
Let

denote the collection of all subsets

which are open and upwards closed, and
\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
be the collection of all subsets
A\subset E

be the set of all

which are compactly generated, i.e.,
C={\uparrow}K
for some compact

.
Similarly, we define the corresponding collections
\mathcal{O}^{\downarrow}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}
of downwards closed sets, which are open and closed, respectively.

Throughout this section, we work under the following assumption.

Assumption 3.1
.

For every
x,y\in E
with
x\leq y
, we assume that

(A)

for every
U_{x}\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
there exists
U_{y}\in\mathcal{U}_{y}
such that
U_{y}\subset{\uparrow}U_{x}
.

(B)

for every

there exists
U_{x}\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
such that

.

In case that the preorder is trivial, i.e.,

if and only if

, then
\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
coincide with the collections of all open and closed subsets of

Remark 3.2
.

Let

is a subset of

and

Then the binary relation defined by

Direct verification shows that

satisfies Assumption

.

and
\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}

We denote by

lower semicontinuous functions

The following assertions hold.

(i)

A\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}

(ii)

A\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}

Proof.

(i) Suppose that

We show that

By contradiction, assume that

3.2.
Bounds for maxitive integrals

In accordance with Section
2
, we next introduce the key concepts of this article. Let
J
be a
concentration
on
\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}:=\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}\cup\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
, i.e., a set function
J\colon\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}\to[-\infty,0]
which satisfies
J_{\emptyset}=-\infty
,
J_{E}=0
, and
J_{A}\leq J_{B}
whenever
A\subset B
.
The respective
maxitive integral

\phi_{J}
on
{LU}^{\uparrow}:={L}^{\uparrow}\cup{U}^{\uparrow}
is defined by

(3.1)

\phi_{J}(f):=\begin{cases}\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\},&\mbox{ if }f\in{L}^{\uparrow},\\
\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f\geq c\}}\},&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

As discussed in the previous section,
e^{J}
is a capacity and
\phi_{J}
is a transformed version of the Shilkret integral; cf. Cattaneo
[
7
]
.
Notice that
\phi_{J}
is well-defined due to Lemma
3.3
. By considering the extended concentration
\bar{J}_{A}:=\inf\{J_{B}\colon B\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow},\,A\subset B\}
for all
A\subset E
, it follows by direct verification that for all
f\in{LU}^{\uparrow}
,

\phi_{J}(f)=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+\bar{J}_{\{f\geq c\}}\}=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+\bar{J}_{\{f>c\}}\}.

This shows that definition (
3.1
) is consistent with that in (
2.7
).
8
8

8

In particular, for
f\in{L}^{\uparrow}\cap{U}^{\uparrow}
, it follows that the two definitions in (
3.1
) coincide.
The functional (
3.1
) shares the properties of a non-linear expectation, i.e., it is constant preserving
\phi_{J}(c)=c
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
, and monotone
\phi_{J}(f)\leq\phi_{J}(g)
whenever
f\leq g
. Also, the maxitive integral has the translation property
\phi_{J}(f+c)=\phi_{J}(f)+c
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Next, we show that the concentration can be recovered from the maxitive integral by the evaluation at indicator functions.
We always make the convention that
-\infty\cdot 0=0
, so that the indicator function
-\infty 1_{A^{c}}
assumes the value
-\infty
on
A^{c}
and zero on
A
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
J
be a concentration. Then, for every
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
,

J_{A}=\phi_{J}(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})=\underset{r<0}{\inf}\phi_{J}(r1_{A^{c}}).

Proof.

Suppose, for instance, that
A\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
.
Then,
A^{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}
due to Lemma
3.3
, and therefore
-\infty 1_{A^{c}}\in{L}^{\uparrow}
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{J}(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{-\infty 1_{A^{c}}>c\}}\}=\underset{0\leq c}{\sup}\{c+J_{\emptyset}\}\vee\underset{c<0}{\sup}\{c+J_{A}\}=J_{A}.

Now, let
r<0
, so that
r1_{A^{c}}\in{L}^{\uparrow}
. Then,

\displaystyle\phi(r1_{A^{c}})

\displaystyle=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{r1_{A^{c}}>c\}}\}

\displaystyle=\underset{c<r}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{r1_{A^{c}}>c\}}\}\vee\underset{r\leq c<0}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{r1_{A^{c}}>c\}}\}\vee\underset{0\leq c}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{r1_{A^{c}}>c\}}\}

\displaystyle=r\vee J_{A}\vee(-\infty)=r\vee J_{A}.

Hence, by letting
r\to-\infty
, we conclude
\underset{r<0}{\inf}\phi_{J}(r1_{A^{c}})=J_{A}
.
∎

In accordance with Section
2
, we say that a concentration
J
admits a rate function if there exists a function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
such that
J_{A}=-\inf_{x\in A}I(x)
for all
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
.
In that case, the concentration is completely maxitive in the sense that
J_{A}\leq\vee_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}J_{A_{i}}
for every family
(A_{i})_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\subset\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}

and
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
with
A\subset\cup_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}A_{i}
, and the maxitive integral
\phi_{J}(f)
admits the representation
\sup_{x\in E}\{f(x)-I(x)\}
for all
f\in{LU}^{\uparrow}
.
In the following, we relax the relation between concentrations and rate functions.
As a first main result, we obtain that the maxitive integral satisfies the following upper and lower bounds.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
J
be a concentration and
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
be a function.
Then, the following equivalences hold. First,

(3.2)

-\inf_{x\in O}I(x)\leq J_{O}\quad\mbox{for all }O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}

if and only if

(3.3)

\phi_{J}(f)\geq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}\quad\mbox{ for all }f\in L^{\uparrow}.

Second,

(3.4)

J_{C}\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x)\quad\mbox{for all }C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}

if and only if

(3.5)

\phi_{J}(f)\leq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}\quad\mbox{ for all }f\in U^{\uparrow}.

Third,

(3.6)

J_{C}\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x)\quad\mbox{for all }C\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\uparrow}

if and only if

(3.7)

\phi_{J}(f)\leq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}\quad\mbox{ for all }f\in U_{c}^{\uparrow}.

Proof.

Suppose that inequality (
3.2
) holds. Let
f\in L^{\uparrow}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Using the definition of the maxitive integral
\phi_{J}
and the inequality
J_{\{f>r\}}\geq-\inf_{x\in\{f>r\}}I(x)
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{J}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\underset{r\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\big\{r-\inf_{x\in\{f>r\}}I(x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\underset{r\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\;\underset{x\in\{f>r\}}{\sup}\{r-I(x)\}

\displaystyle\geq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\;\underset{y\in\{f>f(x)-\varepsilon\}}{\sup}\{f(x)-\varepsilon-I(y)\}

\displaystyle\geq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-\varepsilon-I(x)\}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain
\phi_{J}(f)\geq\sup_{x\in E}\{f(x)-I(x)\}
.

Conversely, suppose that inequality (
3.3
) holds. Let
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
, so that
-\infty 1_{O^{c}}\in L^{\uparrow}
.
Then, by Proposition
3.4
,

J_{O}=\phi_{J}(-\infty 1_{O^{c}})\geq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{-\infty 1_{O^{c}}(x)-I(x)\}=-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I(x).

Now, suppose that inequality (
3.4
) holds. Let
f\in U^{\uparrow}
. Using the definition of the maxitive integral
\phi_{J}
and the inequality

J_{\{f\geq r\}}\leq-\inf_{x\in\{f\geq r\}}I(x)
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{J}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{r\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\big\{r-\inf_{x\in\{f\geq r\}}I(x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\underset{r\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\;\underset{x\in\{f\geq r\}}{\sup}\{r-I(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{r\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\;\underset{x\in\{f\geq r\}}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}.

Conversely, suppose that inequality (
3.5
) holds. Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
, so that
-\infty 1_{C^{c}}\in U^{\uparrow}
.
It follows from Proposition
3.4
that

J_{C}=\phi_{J}(-\infty 1_{C^{c}})\leq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{-\infty 1_{C^{c}}(x)-I(x)\}=-\underset{x\in C}{\inf}I(x).

Finally, the equivalence between the inequalities (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) follows along the same line of argumentation by replacing
\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
by
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\uparrow}
and
U^{\uparrow}
by
U_{c}^{\uparrow}
, respectively.
∎

Notice that the upper/lower bound in the previous result are specified by upper/lower semicontinuous functions. In the same spirit, in the context of the Hausdorff moment problem
[
22
]
, the natural extension of the moment sequence is also determined by semicontinuous functions.

Given a concentration
J
, our goal now is to find a rate function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
which satisfies the lower bound (
3.2
) and upper bounds (
3.4
) or (
3.6
). We first focus on the lower bound. From the previous theorem, we immediately see that

I_{\min}(x):=\sup_{f\in L^{\uparrow}}(f(x)-\phi_{J}(f))\quad\mbox{for all }x\in E

satisfies
I_{\min}(x)\geq f(x)-\phi_{J}(f)
for all
x\in E
and
f\in L^{\uparrow}
, and therefore the inequalities (
3.3
) and (
3.2
).
We refer to
I_{\min}
as the
minimal rate function
. Notice that
I_{\min}
is minimal in the class of functions
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
which satisfy the inequalities (
3.2
) and (
3.3
).
9
9

9

In fact, if
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is another function for which inequality (
3.3
) holds, then
I(x)\geq f(x)-\phi_{J}(f)
for all
x\in E
and
f\in L^{\uparrow}
, and therefore
I(x)\geq\sup_{f\in L^{\uparrow}}(f(x)-\phi_{J}(f))=I_{\min}(x)
for all
x\in E
.
By definition, the function
I_{\rm min}
is lower semicontinuous and increasing. Moreover, it is determined through the concentration function as follows.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
I_{\rm min}
be the minimal rate function associated to a concentration
J
. Then, for every
x\in E
,

-I_{\rm min}(x)=\underset{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}{\inf}J_{{\uparrow}U}.

Proof.

Let
x\in E
and
U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
.
By Lemma
3.3
, we have
({\uparrow}U)^{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}
, and therefore
-\infty 1_{({\uparrow}U)^{c}}\in{L}^{\uparrow}
.
Using the definition of the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
and Proposition
3.4
, we obtain
I_{\rm min}(x)\geq-\phi_{J}(-\infty 1_{({\uparrow}U)^{c}})=-J_{{\uparrow}U}
.
Hence, by taking the supremum over all
U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
,

I_{\rm min}(x)\geq-\underset{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}{\inf}J_{{\uparrow}U}.

As for the other inequality, let
f\in L^{\uparrow}
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
f
is lower semicontinuous, there exists
U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
such that
f(z)\geq f(x)-\varepsilon
for all
z\in U
.
In particular,
f(z)\geq f(x)-\varepsilon
for all
z\in{\uparrow}U
because
f
is increasing. Then, by monotonicity and the translation property of
\phi_{J}
and Proposition
3.4
,

\displaystyle\phi_{J}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\phi_{J}((f(x)-\varepsilon)1_{{\uparrow}U}-\infty 1_{({\uparrow}U)^{c}})

\displaystyle=f(x)-\varepsilon+\phi_{J}(-\infty 1_{({\uparrow}U)^{c}})

\displaystyle=f(x)-\varepsilon+J_{{\uparrow}U}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we get
\phi_{J}(f)\geq f(x)+J_{{\uparrow}U}\geq f(x)+\inf_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}J_{{\uparrow}U}
.
This shows that for all
f\in L^{\uparrow}
,

\underset{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}{\inf}J_{{\uparrow}U}\leq-f(x)+\phi_{J}(f).

By taking the infimum over all
f\in L^{\uparrow}
, we conclude
\underset{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}{\inf}J_{{\uparrow}U}\leq-I_{\rm min}(x)
.
∎

For the minimal rate function to satisfy the upper bound (
3.4
) or (
3.6
), additional assumptions on the concentration are required.
We will see that the concentration must necessarily fulfil a certain form of maxitivity if the lower and upper bounds simultaneously hold.
This is the context of the next subsection.

3.3.
Weak maxitivity

As discussed in Section
2
, a capacity which admits a possibility distribution in the sense of (
2.4
) (respectively a concentration which admits rate function) is completely maxitive. In case that inequality (
2.2
) is only assumed for sequences or a finite sets, then the capacity is called countably maxitive or finitely maxitive, respectively. For a detailed discussion on different maxitivity concepts, we refer to
[
7
]
.
In case that a concentration satisfies only upper and lower bounds, we obtain a weaker form of maxitivity.

Definition 3.7
.

A concentration function
J
is called
weakly maxitive
if

J_{A}\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}J_{B_{i}}\quad\mbox{ for all }A\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}\mbox{ and }B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow},\,n\in\mathbb{N},\mbox{ such that }A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}.

Likewise, a function
\phi\colon{LU}^{\uparrow}\to[-\infty,\infty]
is called
weakly maxitive
if

\phi(f)\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}\phi(g_{i})\mbox{ for all }f\in{U}^{\uparrow}\mbox{ and }g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in{L}^{\uparrow},\,n\in\mathbb{N},\mbox{ such that }f\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
3.5
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that a concentration
J
satisfies the inequalities (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) for a
function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
. Then,
J
and
\phi_{J}
are weakly maxitive.

Proof.

Let
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and
B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}
.
Then, by monotonicity of
J
and the inequalities (
3.4
) and (
3.2
),

J_{A}\leq-\inf_{x\in A}I(x)\leq-\inf_{x\in\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{i}}I(x)=-\wedge_{i=1}^{n}\inf_{x\in B_{i}}I(x)\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}J_{B_{i}}.

Furthermore, let
f\in U^{\uparrow}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in L^{\uparrow}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
f\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}
. Then, it follows from Theorem
3.5
that

\displaystyle\phi_{J}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{\vee_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(x)-I(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{g_{i}(x)-I(x)\}

\displaystyle\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{J}(g_{i}).\qed

We now turn to the converse question and provide bounds for weakly maxitive concentrations.
Thereby, we focus on the upper bound.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that
J
is weakly maxitive. Then,
J
satisfies the upper bound (
3.6
) for the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
.

Proof.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\uparrow}
. By definition,
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and
C={\uparrow}K
for some compact
K\subset E
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, by Lemma
3.6
and compactness, there exist
x_{i}\in K
and
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{x_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
such that
K\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}
and

-J_{{\uparrow}U_{i}}\geq(I_{\rm min}(x_{i})-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}\quad\mbox{ for all }i=1,\ldots,n.

Consequently, since
{\uparrow}U_{i}\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
due to Lemma
3.3
,
C\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\uparrow}U_{i}
,
and
J
is weakly maxitive,

-J_{C}\geq\wedge_{i=1}^{n}-J_{{\uparrow}U_{i}}\geq\wedge_{i=1}^{n}(I_{\rm min}(x_{i})-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}\geq(\underset{x\in K}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain that

-J_{C}\geq\inf_{x\in K}I_{\rm min}(x)=\inf_{x\in C}I_{\rm min}(x),

where the last equality holds because
I_{\rm min}
is increasing. ∎

For the minimal rate function to satisfy the stronger upper bound (
3.4
), we need an additional tightness assumption.

Definition 3.10
.

A concentration
J
is called
tight
, if for all
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and

\varepsilon>0
, there exists
K\subset E
compact with
{\uparrow}(C\cap K)\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}_{c}
such that

(3.8)

J_{C}\leq\left(J_{{\uparrow}(C\cap K)}+\varepsilon\right)\vee(-\varepsilon^{-1}).

Notice that
C\cap K
is compact. In typical situations,
{\uparrow}(C\cap K)
is closed, see Lemma
A.2
in Appendix A.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
J
be an weakly maxitive concentration which is tight. Then,
J
satisfies the upper bound (
3.4
) with minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
.

Proof.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
J
is tight, there exists
K\subset E
compact with
{\uparrow}(C\cap K)\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}_{c}
such that inequality (
3.8
) holds. Hence, we can apply Proposition
3.9
to obtain

\displaystyle J_{C}

\displaystyle\leq\left(J_{{\uparrow}(C\cap K)}+\varepsilon\right)\vee(-\varepsilon^{-1})\leq\left(-\underset{x\in{\uparrow}(C\cap K)}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)+\varepsilon\right)\vee(-\varepsilon^{-1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(-\underset{x\in C}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)+\varepsilon\right)\vee(-\varepsilon^{-1}).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that
J_{C}\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I_{\rm min}(x)
.
∎

The results achieved so far can be summarized as follows. For a weakly maxitive concentration
J
there exists a function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
such that

(3.9)

-\inf_{x\in O}I(x)\leq J_{O}\quad\mbox{and}\quad J_{C}\leq-\inf_{x\in C}I(x)

for all
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}_{c}
, respectively
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
if the concentration is in addition tight. In this case,
the respective maxitive integral is given by
\phi_{J}(f)=\sup_{x\in E}(f(x)-I(x))
for all
f\in L^{\uparrow}\cap U^{\uparrow}_{c}
and
f\in L^{\uparrow}\cap U^{\uparrow}
, respectively. Moreover, the function
I
can be replaced by the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
. In other words, the class of weakly maxitive concentrations (capacities) can be connected with a rate function (possibility distribution), which fully determines the associated maxitive integral for sufficiently regular functions. To what extent the rate function is unique will be discussed in Section
5
,
and how it can be determined by means of convex duality arguments in Section
6
.

4.
Representation of weakly maxitive non-linear expectations

As remarked in the previous section, the maxitive integral (
3.1
) has the same properties as a non-linear expectation which satisfies the translation property.
In the following, we focus on the converse direction and investigate when a non-linear expectation with the translation property can be represented as a maxitive integral.
To do so, the following continuity condition is necessary.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
f\in{LU}^{\uparrow}
,

\underset{N\to\infty}{\lim}\phi_{J}(f\wedge N)=\phi_{J}(f)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underset{N\to\infty}{\lim}\phi_{J}(f\vee(-N))=\phi_{J}(f).

Proof.

Let
f\in{L}^{\uparrow}
, the other case follows with similar arguments. By definition of the maxitive integral, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\phi_{J}(f\wedge N)

\displaystyle=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{(f\wedge N)>c\}}\}=\underset{c<N}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\}\vee\underset{N\leq c}{\sup}\{c+J_{\emptyset}\}=\underset{c<N}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\},

and therefore,

\underset{N\to\infty}{\lim}\phi_{J}(f\wedge N)=\underset{N\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\phi_{J}(f\wedge N)=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{c<N}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\}=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\}=\phi_{J}(f).

As for the second statement, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\phi(f\vee(-N))

\displaystyle=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f\vee(-N)>c\}}\}

\displaystyle=\underset{c<-N}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f\vee(-N)>c\}}\}\vee\underset{-N\leq c}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f\vee(-N)>c\}}\}

\displaystyle=\underset{c<-N}{\sup}\{c+J_{E}\}\vee\underset{-N\leq c}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\}

\displaystyle\leq(-N)\vee\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+J_{\{f>c\}}\}

\displaystyle=(-N)\vee\phi_{J}(f).

Hence, by monotonicity of
\phi_{J}
,

\phi_{J}(f)\leq\underset{N\to\infty}{\lim}\phi_{J}(f\vee(-N))\leq\underset{N\to\infty}{\lim}(-N)\vee\phi_{J}(f)=\phi_{J}(f).\qed

As shown in
[
7
, Corollary 6]
, every finitely maxitive non-linear expectation with the translation property admits a representation in terms of a maxitive integral; see also
[
7
, Corollary 7]
and
[
20
, Proposition 2.2]
.
We next provide a related representation result for weakly maxitive non-linear expectations.
Let
\overline{L}^{\uparrow}
,
\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
, and
\overline{LU}^{\uparrow}
denote the sets of all functions in
{L}^{\uparrow}
,
{U}^{\uparrow}
, and
{LU}^{\uparrow}
, respectively, which are bounded from above. Moreover, let
C_{b}^{\uparrow}
be the space of all increasing bounded continuous functions
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
\psi\colon\overline{LU}^{\uparrow}\to[-\infty,\infty)
satisfies

(i)

\psi(0)=0
,

(ii)

\psi(f)\leq\psi(g)
whenever
f\leq g
,

(iii)

\psi(f+c)=\psi(f)+c
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
,

with concentration
J^{\psi}_{A}:=\psi(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})
for all
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
.
If
\psi
is weakly maxitive,
10
10

10

I.e.,
\phi(f)\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}\phi(g_{i})
for all
f\in\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
f\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}
.

then

\psi(f)=\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f)\quad\mbox{for all }f\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}\cap\overline{U}^{\uparrow}.

Proof.

First, suppose that
f\in C^{\uparrow}_{b}
and let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a<f(x)<b
for all
x\in E
.
Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and define for
0\leq j\leq N-1
,

a_{N,j}:=a+j\frac{b-a}{N},\quad O_{N,j}=\{f>a_{N,j}\},\quad\mbox{and}\quad C_{N,j}=\{f\geq a_{N,j}\}.

We consider the simple functions

l_{N}:=\vee^{N-1}_{j=0}\big(-\infty 1_{O_{N,j}^{c}}+a_{N,j}1_{O_{N,j}}\big)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad u_{N}:=\vee^{N-1}_{j=0}\big(-\infty 1_{C_{N,j}^{c}}+a_{N,j}1_{C_{N,j}}\big).

By construction, it holds
l_{N}\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}
,
u_{N}\in\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
, and
f-\frac{b-a}{N}\leq l_{N}\leq u_{N}\leq f
.
Using the definition of the maxitive integral and the translation property of
\psi
,

\displaystyle\phi_{J^{\psi}}(l_{N})

\displaystyle=\vee_{j=0}^{N-1}\{a_{N,j}+J_{O_{N,j}}^{\psi}\}

\displaystyle=\vee_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi\big(-\infty 1_{O_{N,j}^{c}}+a_{N,j}1_{O_{N,j}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\psi(l_{N}).

Moreover, it follows from

u_{N}\leq l_{N}+\frac{b-a}{N}=\vee^{N-1}_{j=0}\big(-\infty 1_{O_{N,j}^{c}}+\left(a_{N,j}+\tfrac{b-a}{N}\right)1_{O_{N,j}}\big),

and the weak maxitivity of
\psi
that

\displaystyle\psi(u_{N})

\displaystyle\leq\vee_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi\Big(-\infty 1_{O_{N,j}^{c}}+\left(a_{N,j}+\tfrac{b-a}{N}\right)1_{O_{N,j}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{b-a}{N}+\vee_{j=0}^{N-1}\psi\big(-\infty 1_{O_{N,j}^{c}}+a_{N,j}1_{O_{N,j}}\big)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{b-a}{N}+\phi_{J^{\psi}}(l_{N}).

In combination with
\phi_{J^{\psi}}(l_{N})\leq\psi(l_{N})\leq\psi(u_{N})
, we obtain

|\psi(u_{N})-\phi_{J^{\psi}}(l_{N})|\leq\tfrac{b-a}{N}.

Hence, as a consequence of the monotonicity and translation property of
\psi
and
\phi_{J^{\psi}}
,

\displaystyle|\psi(f)-\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq|\psi(f)-\psi(u_{N})|+|\psi(u_{N})-\phi_{J^{\psi}}(l_{N})|+|\phi_{J^{\psi}}(l_{N})-\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{b-a}{N}+\tfrac{b-a}{N}+\tfrac{b-a}{N}.

Letting
N\to\infty
results in
\psi(f)=\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f)
as desired.

Second, suppose that
f\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}\cap\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, so that
f\vee(-N)\in C^{\uparrow}_{b}
, and therefore
\psi(f\vee(-N))=\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f\vee(-N))
due to the previous step.
Moreover, since
\psi
is weakly maxitive,

\psi(f)\leq\psi(f\vee(-N))\leq\psi(f)\vee(-N).

Hence, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\phi_{J^{\psi}}(f\vee(-N))=\lim_{N\to\infty}\psi(f\vee(-N))=\psi(f).\qed

Remark 4.3
.

A weakly maxitive non-linear expectation with the translation property is fully determined on increasing continuous bounded functions by its restriction to the indicators
-\infty 1_{A^{c}}
for
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
. Indeed, let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\colon\overline{LU}^{\uparrow}\to[-\infty,\infty)
be weakly maxitive non-linear expectations with the translation property. If
\psi_{1}(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})=\psi_{2}(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})
for all
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
, then Theorem
4.2
implies

\psi_{1}(f)=\psi_{2}(f)
for all
f\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}\cap\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
.

5.
A Laplace principle for maxitive integrals

Theorem
4.2
allows to represent weakly maxitive non-linear expectations with the translation property in terms of a maxitive integral
\phi_{J}
.
In order to find a computable representation of the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
, we aim to find conditions which guarantee that
I_{\rm min}
is attained on certain spaces of bounded functions. More specifically, under an additional separation property, we focus on representations of the form

I_{\rm min}(x)=\sup_{f\in C_{b}^{\uparrow}}\{f(x)-\phi_{J}(f)\}.

We also investigate the relation between the bounds (
3.9
) and a Laplace principle for general concentrations. Throughout this section, let
(E,\leq)
be a topological preordered space.
We fix a base
\mathcal{U}
for the topology of
E
, and set
\mathcal{U}_{x}:=\{U\in\mathcal{U}\colon x\in U\}
for all
x\in E
.
Additionally to Assumption
3.1
, we require the following separation property.

Assumption 5.1
.

For every
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and
x\notin A
, there exists an increasing continuous function
f\colon E\to[0,1]
which satisfies

f(x)=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad A\subset f^{-1}(1).

Similarly, for every
A\in\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}
and
x\notin A
, there exists an increasing continuous function
f\colon E\to[0,1]
such that

A\subset f^{-1}(0)
and
f(x)=1
.

Remark 5.2
.

If the preorder
\leq
is trivial, then the previous assumption corresponds to that of complete regularity.
Both metric spaces, and Hausdorff topological abelian groups are completely regular topological spaces and thus satisfy Assumption
5.1
for the trivial preorder.
Moreover, we show in Theorem

Definition 5.5

Let

6.
Convex rate functions

The explicit determination of the rate function is generally a difficult task.
However, in the particular case where the rate function is convex, one can rely on convex duality arguments.
Throughout this section, let
E
be a locally convex Hausdorff topological real vector space. Moreover, let
E_{+}\subset E
be a convex cone, i.e.,

E_{+}+E_{+}\subset E_{+}
,
\lambda E_{+}\subset E_{+}
for all
\lambda>0
and
0\in E_{+}
.
We endow
E
with the preorder induced by
E_{+}
, i.e.,
x\leq y
if and only if
y-x\in E_{+}
.
Then, the assumptions of Section
5
are satisfied as outlined in Remark
5.2
.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a base for the topology of
E
, and define
\mathcal{U}_{x}:=\{U\in\mathcal{U}\colon x\in U\}
for all
x\in E
.
We first provide a condition which ensures that the minimal rate function is convex.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
J
be a concentration such that

J_{\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}J_{{\uparrow}U}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{{\uparrow}V}\quad\mbox{ for all }U,V\in\mathcal{U}.

Then, the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is convex.

Proof.

Fix
x,y\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Set
z:=\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{2}y
.
Due to Lemma
5.4
, there exists
W\in\mathcal{U}_{z}
such that

-J_{{\uparrow}W}\geq(I_{\rm min}(z)-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}.

Since
O:=\{(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in E\times E\colon\frac{1}{2}\tilde{x}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{y}\in W\}
is open, there exist
U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}

and
V\in\mathcal{U}_{y}
such that
\frac{1}{2}U+\frac{1}{2}V\subset W
.
Then,

-[(I_{\rm min}(z)-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}]\geq J_{{\uparrow}W}\geq J_{\frac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\frac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V}\geq\frac{1}{2}J_{{\uparrow}U}+\frac{1}{2}J_{{\uparrow}V}.

Taking the infimum over all
U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
and then over all
V\in\mathcal{U}_{y}
,

(I_{\rm min}(z)-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}I_{\rm min}(x)+\frac{1}{2}I_{\rm min}(y).

Therefore, since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain

I_{\rm min}(z)\leq\frac{1}{2}I_{\rm min}(x)+\frac{1}{2}I_{\rm min}(y).

Now, consider the set the set
\mathcal{D}:=\{k2^{-n}\colon n,k\in\mathbb{N},\>k\leq 2^{n}\}
of all dyadic rational numbers in the interval
[0,1]
.
By recursion, it can be shown that

I_{\rm min}(z)\leq\lambda I_{\rm min}(x)+(1-\lambda)I_{\rm min}(y),

for every
\lambda\in\mathcal{D}
. As a consequence, since the map
[0,1]\to[0,\infty]
,
\lambda\mapsto I_{\rm min}(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)

is lower semicontinuous and the set
\mathcal{D}
is dense in
[0,1]
, the previous inequality is valid for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
∎

Let
E^{\ast}
be the topological dual space of
E
. We denote by
E^{\ast}_{+}
the set of all
\mu\in E^{\ast}
which are positive, i.e.,
\mu(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in E_{+}
.
The convex conjugate of the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
is defined by
I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu):=\sup_{x\in E}\{\mu(x)-I_{\rm min}(x)\}
for all
\mu\in E^{\ast}
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
J
be a concentration. Suppose that the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
is convex and
I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)\geq\phi_{J}(\mu)
for all
\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}
. Then,

(6.1)

I_{\rm min}(x)=\underset{\mu\in E_{+}^{\ast}}{\sup}\{\mu(x)-\phi_{J}(\mu)\}\quad\mbox{for all }x\in E.

Proof.

The minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
is lower semicontinuous and increasing.
By the Fenchel-Moreau theorem
[
13
, Theorem A.62]
,

\displaystyle I_{\rm min}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mu\in E^{\ast}}\{\mu(x)-I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}}\{\mu(x)-I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)\}\quad\mbox{for all }x\in E.

The second equality holds because
I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)=\infty
whenever
\mu\in E^{\ast}\setminus E^{\ast}_{+}
.
In fact, for
\mu\in E^{\ast}\setminus E^{\ast}_{+}
there exists
y\leq 0
with
\mu(y)>0
, and therefore

\displaystyle I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)

\displaystyle\geq\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\{\mu(x+\lambda y)-I_{\rm min}(x+\lambda y)\}

\displaystyle\geq\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\{\mu(x)+\lambda\mu(y)-I_{\rm min}(x)\}=\infty.

Finally, using the definition of
I_{\min}
and the inequality
I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)\geq\phi_{J}(\mu)
for all
\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}
,

I_{\min}(x)=\sup_{f\in{L}^{\uparrow}}\{f(x)-\phi_{J}(f)\}\geq\sup_{\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}}\{\mu(x)-\phi_{J}(\mu)\}\geq I_{\rm min}(x).\qed

The hypothesis of Proposition
6.2
can be verified in some important situations; e.g., for the asymptotic concentration of sample means of i.i.d. sequences, see Subsection
7.2
. In particular, it is satisfied under the mLDP, which leads to the following result.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
J
be a concentration which satisfies the mLDP with a rate function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
which is convex, increasing and lower semicontinuous.
Then,

I(x)=\underset{\mu\in E_{+}^{\ast}}{\sup}\{\mu(x)-\phi_{J}(\mu)\}\quad\mbox{for all }x\in E.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.6
, it holds
I=I_{\rm min}
. Moreover, it follows from Theorem
3.5
that
\phi_{J}(\mu)=I_{\rm min}^{\ast}(\mu)
for all
\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}
.
Consequently, since
I=I_{\rm min}
is convex, the claim follows from Proposition
6.2
.
∎

We illustrate the theoretical results with two examples.

7.1.
Asymptotic concentration of capacities

Let

(i)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)\leq\mathcal{E}_{n}(g)

(ii)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(f+g)\leq\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)+\mathcal{E}_{n}(g)

(iii)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(f+c)=\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)+c

(iv)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(\lambda f)=\lambda\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)

; see, e.g.,
[

,

]
.

[0,\infty]
-valued sequences, then
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{n}^{i}=\vee_{i=1}^{N}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log a_{n}^{i}.

Lemma 7.2
.

The concentration
J
is weakly maxitive.

Proof.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
and
O_{1},O_{2},\ldots,O_{N}\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
such that

C\subset\cup_{i=1}^{N}O_{1}
, and therefore
\mu_{n}(C)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{n}(O_{i})
. Applying the principle of the largest term,

J_{C}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{n}(O_{i})\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(O_{i})=\vee_{i=1}^{N}J_{O_{i}}.\qed

In particular, the results of Section
3
are applicable. Due to Lemma
3.6
, the minimal rate function is given by

I_{\rm min}(x)=-\sup_{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}\left({\uparrow}U\right)\quad\mbox{for all }x\in E.

As an application of Theorem
3.5
, the discussion thereafter, and Proposition
3.9
, we obtain

(7.1)

-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)\leq J_{O}\quad\mbox{and}\quad J_{C}\leq-\underset{x\in C}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)

for all
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\uparrow}
, and consequently the respective bounds in Theorem
3.5
for the maxitive integral
\phi_{J}
.
Building on Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.3
, the maxitive integral
\phi_{J}
has the following representation in terms of a sequence of Choquet integrals.

Proposition 7.3
.

For every
f\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}\cap\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
,

(7.2)

\phi_{J}(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(\exp(nf))\\
=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\exp(nf)>x\right){\rm d}x.

Proof.

Define
\psi\colon\overline{LU}^{\uparrow}\to[-\infty,\infty)
as the right hand side of (
7.2
).
Inspection shows that
\psi
satisfies properties (i)-(iii) in Theorem
4.2
.
Next, we show that
\psi
is weakly maxitive. To that end, let
f\in\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
,
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{N}\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}
, and
f\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}g_{1}
.
Then, by the principle of the largest term,

\displaystyle\psi(f)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\exp\left(n(\vee_{i=1}^{N}g_{i})\right)>x\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\cup_{i=1}^{N}\{\exp(ng_{i})>x\}\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\exp(ng_{i})>x\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\exp(ng_{i})>x\right){\rm d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\vee_{i=1}^{N}\psi(g_{i}),

which shows that
\psi
is weakly maxitive. Moreover, for
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
,

\displaystyle\psi(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(\exp(n(-\infty)1_{A^{c}})>x\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}\left(1_{A}>x\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\int_{0}^{1}\mu_{n}\left(A\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}\left(A\right)

\displaystyle=J_{A}.

Hence,
J^{\psi}=J
and we can apply Theorem
4.2
to conclude
\psi=\phi_{J}
.

In a second step, we define

\tilde{\psi}\colon\overline{LU}^{\uparrow}\to[-\infty,\infty),\quad\tilde{\psi}(f):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}\big(\exp(nf)\big).

Direct verification shows that
\tilde{\psi}
satisfies properties (i)-(iii) in Theorem
4.2
.
Moreover, it is weakly maxitive. Indeed, let
f\in\overline{U}^{\uparrow}
and
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{N}\in\overline{L}^{\uparrow}
such that
f\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}g_{1}
.
Again, by the principle of the largest term,

\displaystyle{\tilde{\psi}}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(e^{n(\vee_{i=1}^{N}g_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(e^{ng_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(e^{ng_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\vee_{i=1}^{N}{\tilde{\psi}}(g_{i}).

Since
J_{A}=\tilde{\psi}(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})
for all
A\in\mathcal{OC}^{\uparrow}
, it follows from Theorem
4.2
that
\tilde{\psi}=\phi_{J}
.
Together with the first part, we obtain equality (
7.3
).
∎

In contrast to linear expectations in standard probability theory, a sublinear expectation
\mathcal{E}
is in general not determined by its associated capacity
\mu(A)=\mathcal{E}(1_{A})
; see, e.g.,
[
26
]
. In other words, the sublinear expectation
\mathcal{E}
may contain more information than the capacity
\mu
.
However, Proposition
7.3
implies that the asymptotic entropic version
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(\exp(nf))

is fully specified through the sequence of capacities
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by means of the right hand side of equation (
7.2
).

Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.7
, it follows that
\phi_{J}
satisfies the mLP with rate function
I
if and only if
J
satisfies the mLDP with rate function
I
. Under a slightly stronger version of the mLDP, the limit superior in (
7.2
) can even be replaced by a limit. More precisely, we obtain the equivalence between the following versions of the classical large deviation principle and the Laplace principle.

Corollary 7.4
.

Suppose that
(E,\leq)
satisfies Assumption
3.1
and Assumption
5.1
.
Let
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
be an increasing lower semicontinuous function. If
(\mu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

(7.3)

-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I(x)\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(O)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(C)\leq-\underset{x\in C}{\inf}I(x)

for all
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}
, then
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

(7.4)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(\exp(nf))=\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}

for all
f\in C^{\uparrow}_{b}
. The converse assertion holds true if
E
is monotonically normal or
I
has compactly generated sublevel sets.

Proof.

Let
f\in C_{b}^{\uparrow}
. It follows from Theorem
3.5
that

\phi_{J}(f)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(\exp(nf))=\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}.

To show that the previous limit superior is a limit, we define

\underline{\phi}(f):=\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(\exp(nf)).

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in E
.
Since
f
is upper semicontinuous and increasing, there exists
U\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
with
x\in U
such that
\inf_{y\in U}f(y)\geq f(x)-\varepsilon
.
Then,

\displaystyle\underline{\phi}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\underline{\phi}(f1_{U}-\infty 1_{U^{c}})

\displaystyle=f(x)-\varepsilon+\underline{\phi}(-\infty 1_{U^{c}})

\displaystyle=f(x)-\varepsilon+\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(U)

\displaystyle\geq f(x)-\varepsilon-\underset{y\in U}{\inf}I(y)

\displaystyle\geq f(x)-\varepsilon-I(x).

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and taking the supremum over all
x\in E
yields

\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}\leq\underline{\phi}(f)\leq\phi_{J}(f)=\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}.

As for the converse assertion, suppose that
E
is monotonically normal or
I
has compactly generated sublevel sets.
By Theorem
5.7
,

\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(C)=J_{C}\leq-\underset{x\in C}{\inf}I(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}^{\uparrow}.

To show the lower bound, fix
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
. Let
x\in O
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
3.3
,
it holds
O^{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{\downarrow}
. Hence, due to Assumption
5.1
, there exists
f_{m}\in C_{b}^{\uparrow}
such that
f_{m}(x)=0
,
O^{c}\subset f^{-1}_{m}(-m)
, and
-m\leq f(y)\leq 0
for all
y\in E
. We obtain

\displaystyle\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(O)\vee(-m)

\displaystyle=\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}\big(\exp(n(-\infty 1_{O^{c}}))\big)\vee(-m)

\displaystyle\geq\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}\big(\exp(n((-\infty 1_{O^{c}})\vee(-m))\big)

\displaystyle\geq\underline{\phi}(f_{m})=\phi_{J}(f_{m})\geq f_{m}(x)-I(x)=-I(x).

By letting
m\to\infty
and taking the supremum over all
x\in O
,

\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(O)\geq-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I(x).\qed

. Then, the assumptions of Section

.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X_{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}

(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of

-valued random variables defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
For

, we define the concentration

with minimal rate function
{I_{\rm min}}
.

.

For every convex set

,

{J}_{O}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in O)=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in O).

Proof.

The equalities (

) follow from

and

, respectively.
Although

]

∎

Lemma 7.6
.

I_{\rm min}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
is convex.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
27
, Proposition 12.9]
.
Let
U,V
be open and convex sets.
For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
X_{n+1,2n}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=n+1}^{2n}\xi_{i}
.
Since
X_{2n}=\tfrac{1}{2}(X_{n}+X_{n+1,2n})
, as well as
X_{n}
and
X_{n+1,2n}
are independent,

\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}U)\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1,2n}\in{\uparrow}V)=\mathbb{P}(\{X_{n}\in{\uparrow}U\}\cap\{X_{n+1,2n}\in{\uparrow}V\})\leq\mathbb{P}(X_{2n}\in\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V).

Hence,

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}U)+\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}V)\leq\tfrac{1}{2n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{2n}\in\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V).

It follows from Lemma
7.5
that

\displaystyle{J}_{\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V}=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{2n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{2n}\in\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}U+\tfrac{1}{2}{\uparrow}V)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}U)+\tfrac{1}{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}V)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}{J}_{{\uparrow}U}+\tfrac{1}{2}{J}_{{\uparrow}V}.

Since the topology of
E
is generated by the collection of all convex open subsets of
E
,
we can apply Lemma
6.1
to conclude that
I_{\rm min}
is convex.
∎

Definition 7.7
.

An
E
-valued random variable
\xi
is called
convex tight
if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a convex compact
K\subset E
such that
\mathbb{P}(\xi\in K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Moreover,
\xi
is said to be
convex inner regular
if for every
\varepsilon>0
and each convex open
O\subset E
, there exists a convex compact
K\subset O
such that
\mathbb{P}(\xi\in K)\geq\mathbb{P}(\xi\in O)-\varepsilon
.

By adapting
[
27
, Proposition 12.7]
to the present setting,
we remark that every
E
-valued random variable that is convex tight is also convex inner regular. Moreover, if
E
is a separable Banach space, then every random variable is automatically convex tight and convex inner regular; see
[
27
, Proposition 12.4]
and
[
27
, Proposition 12.7]
.

From now on, let
\xi
be an
E
-valued random variable distributed as
\xi_{1}
.
We need the following result from
[
27
]
. For the sake of completeness, we provide a proof.

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
f\colon E\to(0,\infty)
be a Borel measurable function, and suppose that
\xi
is convex tight.
Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a convex compact
K\subset E
such that

\varepsilon^{-1}\wedge\Big(\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)]-\varepsilon\Big)\leq\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K}(\xi)].

Proof.

Suppose first that
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
is bounded.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
\xi
is convex tight, there exists a compact set
K\subset E
such that

\mathbb{P}(\xi\in K^{c})\leq(1-e^{-\varepsilon})\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)]}{M},

where
|f(x)|\leq M
for all
x\in E
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K}(\xi)]+\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K^{c}}(\xi)]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K}(\xi)]+M\mathbb{P}(\xi\in K^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K}(\xi)]+(1-e^{-\varepsilon}){\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)]},

which shows that
\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)]-\varepsilon\leq\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K}(\xi)]
.

In case that
f
is not bounded, due to the monotone convergence theorem, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\varepsilon^{-1}\wedge\Big(\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)]-\varepsilon\Big)\leq\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)\wedge N]-\varepsilon/2.

Since
x\mapsto f(x)\wedge N
is bounded, it follows from the first part that there exists a convex compact
K\subset E
such that

\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)\wedge N]-\varepsilon/2\leq\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[f(\xi)1_{K}(\xi)].\qed

The
logarithmic moment generating function
of
\xi
is defined by

\Lambda\colon E^{\ast}_{+}\to[0,\infty],\quad\Lambda(\mu):=\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\exp(\mu(\xi))].

In addition, we define its positive convex conjugate
\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}\colon E\to[0,\infty]
by

\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}(x)=\underset{\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}}{\sup}\{\mu(x)-\Lambda(\mu)\}.

Then the following monotone version of Cramér’s theorem holds.

Theorem 7.9
.

Suppose that
\xi
is convex tight.
Then,

(7.8)

I_{\rm min}(x)=\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}(x)\quad\mbox{for all }x\in E.

Moreover,

(7.9)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in C)\leq-\inf_{x\in C}\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }C\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\uparrow},

(7.10)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in O)\geq-\inf_{x\in O}\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}.

If additionally
X_{n}
is convex tight for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in O)=-\inf_{x\in O}\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}(x)\quad\mbox{ for all convex }O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}.

Proof.

Fix
\mu\in E^{\ast}_{+}
. Since
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d., we have for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mu)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\exp(n\mu(X_{n}))]

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\exp(n(N\wedge\mu)(X_{n}))]

\displaystyle=\phi_{J}(N\wedge\mu),

where the last equality follows from (
7.5
).
Letting
N\to\infty
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\Lambda(\mu)\geq\phi_{{J}}(\mu).

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma
7.8
, there exists a convex compact
K\subset E
such that

\varepsilon^{-1}\wedge\Big(\Lambda(\mu)-\varepsilon\Big)\leq\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\mu(\xi)}1_{K}(\xi)].

Since
(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d. and
K
is convex,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{n\mu(X_{n})}1_{K}(X_{n})]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\mu(\xi_{1})}\cdots e^{\mu(\xi_{n})}1_{K}(\xi_{1})\cdots 1_{K}(\xi_{n})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\mu(\xi)}1_{K}(\xi)]^{n}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-1}\wedge\Big(\Lambda(\mu)-\varepsilon\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{\mu(\xi)}1_{K}(\xi)]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{n\mu(X_{n})}1_{K}(X_{n})]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in E}\big\{\mu(x)-I_{\rm min}(x)\big\}

\displaystyle=I^{\ast}_{\rm min}(\mu),

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
B.1
in the Appendix B.
Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we obtain
\phi_{J}(\mu)\leq\Lambda(\mu)\leq I^{\ast}_{\rm min}(\mu)
.
Hence, since the minimal rater function
I_{\rm min}
is convex, lower semicontinuous and increasing, in line with Proposition
6.2
, we obtain for all
x\in E
,

\displaystyle I_{\rm min}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\mu\in E_{+}^{\ast}}\{\mu(x)-\phi_{J}(\mu)\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\mu\in E_{+}^{\ast}}\{\mu(x)-\Lambda(\mu)\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{\mu\in E_{+}^{\ast}}\{\mu(x)-I^{\ast}_{\rm min}(\mu)\}

\displaystyle=I_{\rm min}(x).

This shows equation (
7.8
). Moreover, since the concentration
J
is weakly maxitive,
the upper bound (
7.9
) follows directly from Proposition
3.9
.
To show the lower bound (
7.10
), we consider the concentration
\underline{J}_{A}:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in A)
with minimal rate function
\underline{I}_{\min}
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a topological base consisting of open convex sets.
It follows from Lemma
3.6
that for every
x\in E
,

\underline{I}_{\min}(x)=-\underset{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}{\inf}\underline{J}_{U}=-\underset{U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}}{\inf}J_{U}=I_{\rm min}(x)=\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}(x),

where
J_{U}=\underline{J}_{U}
is valid due to (
7.7
) in Lemma
7.5
.
Then the lower bound (
7.10
) follows from the lower bound (
3.2
), which is satisfied for the minimal rate function
\underline{I}_{\min}=\Lambda^{\ast}_{+}
.

Finally, suppose that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the random variable
X_{n}
is convex tight and therefore convex inner regular.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
, a convex
O\in\mathcal{O}^{\uparrow}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
X_{N}
is convex inner regular, there exists a convex compact
K\subset O
such that

\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in O)\leq\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in K)+\varepsilon\leq\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in{\uparrow}K)+\varepsilon.

It follows from Lemma
A.2
that
{\uparrow}K\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\uparrow}
and
{\uparrow}K
is a Borel set.
Since
{\uparrow}K
is convex, we can apply Lemma
7.5
to conclude

{J}_{{\uparrow}K}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}K)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}K),

and therefore

\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in O)\leq\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in{\uparrow}K)+\varepsilon\leq\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in{\uparrow}K)+\varepsilon={J}_{{\uparrow}K}+\varepsilon.

By Proposition
3.9
, it holds
J_{{\uparrow}K}\leq-\underset{x\in{\uparrow}K}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)\leq-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x).

Hence,

\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in O)\leq-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x)+\varepsilon.

Letting
N\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

{J}_{O}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{N}\in O)\leq-\underset{x\in O}{\inf}I_{\rm min}(x),

where the limit above exists due to Proposition
7.5
.
The other inequality follows from the lower bound (

8.
Conclusions

It is well-known that the maxitive integral with respect to a completely maxitive concentration (capacity) is uniquely determined by a rate function (possibility distribution).
In this paper, we have introduced the notion of weak maxitivity, and we have shown that, if a concentration
J
is weakly maxitive and tight, then the corresponding maxitive integral
\phi_{J}

is determined by the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
on the space of all increasing continuous functions.

Furthermore, every maxitive integral
\phi_{J}
is a non-linear expectation with the translation property,
and we have argued that
\phi_{J}
is weakly maxitive if
J
is weakly maxitive.
Conversely, we have shown that every weakly maxitive non-linear expection
\psi
with the translation property has a maxitive integral representation
\psi=\phi_{J}
on the space of all increasing continuous functions.

Motivated by the theory of large deviations, we have provided different representation results for the minimal rate function
I_{\rm min}
.
First, under Assumption
5.1
(which is satisfied if the state space
E
is a preordered topological group), we have seen that the rate function is determined by the space of all bounded increasing continuous functions, i.e.,

I_{\rm min}(x)=\underset{f\in C_{b}^{\uparrow}}{\sup}\{f(x)-\phi_{J}(f)\}.

In addition, we have formulated monotone analogues of the large deviation principle and the Laplace principle, which have been shown to be equivalent under suitable conditions and which uniquely determine the rate function within the class of increasing lower semicontinuous functions.
Second, we have focused on the case where the rate function is a convex function on a locally convex topological real vector space
E
.
In that case, under an additional assumption which is implied by the monotone Laplace principle, the minimal rate function is specified by the dual space, i.e.,

I_{\rm min}(x)=\underset{\mu\in E^{\ast}}{\sup}\{\mu(x)-\phi_{J}(\mu)\}.

Finally, we have shown that standard large deviations theory can be understood within the framework of weakly maxitive concentrations and their maxitive integrals.
In turn, the present framework enlarges the scope of large deviations theory to non-standard situations, which we have illustrated with two examples.
On the one hand, we have covered the asymptotic concentration of capacities on preordered topological spaces.
On the other hand, we have established new large deviation bounds for the sample mean of i.i.d. sequences on upwards closed sets by showing a monotone analogue of Cramér’s theorem on locally convex topological vector spaces.

Long term analysis of splitting methods for charged-particle dynamics
Xicui Li, Bin Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we rigorously analyze the energy, momentum and magnetic moment behaviours of two splitting methods for solving charged-particle dynamics. The near-conservations of these invariants are given for the system under constant magnetic field or quadratic electric potential. By the approach named as backward error analysis, we derive the modified equations and modified invariants of the splitting methods and based on which, the near-conservations over long times are proved. Some numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate these long time behaviours.

Introduction

In this paper, we formulate and analyze two splitting methods for solving the charged-particle dynamics (CPD)
which is described by

\begin{split}&\ddot{x}=v\times B(x)+E(x),\quad\ x(0)=x_{0},\quad v(0)=v_{0},\quad t\geq 0,\end{split}

A(x)

is a given electric field generated by some scalar potential

2
Numerical Methods

In this section, we present two splitting methods and show their elementary features.
To formulate the methods, we split the equation (
1
) into two subflows:

\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
v\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v\times B(x)\\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
v\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
E(x)\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

Then for the first subflow, which is integrable, one can obtain its exact solution:

\Phi^{L}_{t}:\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi(t)\\
\eta(t)\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\xi(0)\\
e^{t\tilde{B}(\xi(0))}\eta(0)\\
\end{array}\right),\quad t\geq 0,

(5)

where
\tilde{B}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}0&B_{3}(x)&-B_{2}(x)\\
-B_{3}(x)&0&B_{1}(x)\\
B_{2}(x)&-B_{1}(x)&0\\
\end{pmatrix}

is defined by
v\times B(x)=\tilde{B}(x)v
.
For the second subflow (a canonical Hamiltonian system), we consider applying the average vector field (AVF) formula
[
26
]
to get its numerical propagator:

\Phi^{iL}_{t}:\ \ \begin{pmatrix}{\xi(t)}\\
{\eta(t)}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\xi(0)+t\eta(0)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}E\left(\rho\xi(0)+(1-\rho)\xi(t)\right)d\rho\\
\eta(0)+t\int_{0}^{1}E\left(\rho\xi(0)+(1-\rho)\xi(t)\right)d\rho\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

Based on this splitting, two methods are formulated as follows.

Algorithm 2.1
(Implicit splitting method)

We denote the numerical solution as
x^{n}\approx x(t_{n}),\,v^{n}\approx v(t_{n})
and choose
x^{0}=x_{0},\,v^{0}=v_{0}
. Taking a symmetric version
[
18
]
:
\Phi_{h}^{i}=\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}\circ\Phi_{h}^{iL}\circ\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L},
then we get its total formula for solving (
1
): for
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle x^{n+1}=

\displaystyle x^{n}+he^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}v^{n}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}E\left(\rho x^{n}+(1-\rho)x^{n+1}\right)d\rho,

(7)

\displaystyle v^{n+1}=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{h}{2}\left({\tilde{B}(x^{n+1})+\tilde{B}(x^{n})}\right)}v^{n}+he^{\frac{h}{2}{\tilde{B}(x^{n+1})}}\int_{0}^{1}E\left(\rho x^{n}+(1-\rho)x^{n+1}\right)d\rho.

Obviously, it is an implicit and typical Strang splitting scheme, and we shall use IMS-O2 to denote the method.

If we make some adjustments to (
6
), we can get the following explicit algorithm.

Algorithm 2.2
(Explicit splitting method)

For the second subflow in (
4
), we linearize the nonlinear integrals in (
6
), and then it is transformed to

\Phi^{eL}_{t}:\\
\begin{pmatrix}{\xi(t)}\\
{\eta(t)}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\xi(0)+t\eta(0)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}E(\xi(0))\\
\eta(0)+\frac{t}{2}\left[E(\xi(0))+E(\xi(t))\right]\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(8)

In that way, the Strang splitting scheme
\Phi_{h}^{e}:=\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}\circ\Phi_{h}^{eL}\circ\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}
for solving (
1
) reads:

\displaystyle x^{n+1}=

\displaystyle x^{n}+he^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}v^{n}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}E(x^{n}),

(9)

\displaystyle v^{n+1}=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{h}{2}\left({\tilde{B}(x^{n+1})+\tilde{B}(x^{n})}\right)}v^{n}+\frac{h}{2}e^{\frac{h}{2}{\tilde{B}(x^{n+1})}}\left[E(x^{n})+E(x^{n+1})\right],

and we shall call it as EXS-O2.

In what follows, we will study the basic properties of these two methods. We begin with their symmetry.
A numerical method denoted by
y^{n+1}=\Phi_{h}(y^{n})
is called symmetric if exchanging
y^{n}\leftrightarrow y^{n+1}
and
h\leftrightarrow-h
leaves the method unaltered. It has been pointed out in
[
18
]
that symmetric methods have excellent long time behavior and play a central role in the geometric numerical integration of differential equations. The following theorem states the symmetry of Algorithms
2.1
and
2.2
.

Theorem 2.3
(Symmetry)

From the symmetric versions:
\Phi_{h}^{i}=\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}\circ\Phi_{h}^{iL}\circ\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}
and
\Phi_{h}^{e}=\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}\circ\Phi_{h}^{eL}\circ\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}
, it follows that Algorithms
2.1
and
2.2
are symmetric.

For these two algorithms, we next investigate and briefly show their local errors.

Theorem 2.4
(Local errors)

For the two methods presented above, under the local assumptions
x(t_{n})=x^{n},v(t_{n})=v^{n},
the local errors are given as

x^{n+1}-x(t_{n+1})=\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),\quad v^{n+1}-v(t_{n+1})=\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),

where the constants symbolized by
\mathcal{O}
are independent of
n
and
h
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notations, we denote

y:=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
v\end{pmatrix},\quad f(y):=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
v\times B(x)+E(x)\end{pmatrix},\quad f_{1}(y):=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
v\times B(x)\end{pmatrix},\quad f_{2}(y)=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
E(x)\end{pmatrix},

and let
\Phi_{h}
stand for the exact flow. Then the original system can be rewritten as
\dot{y}=f(y)=f_{1}(y)+f_{2}(y)
.

By Taylor expansions, we reach

\displaystyle\Phi_{h}(y)=\left[Id+hf+\frac{h^{2}}{2}f^{\prime}f\right](y)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),\quad\Phi_{h}^{L}(y)=\left[Id+hf_{1}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}f^{\prime}_{1}f_{1}\right](y)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),

\displaystyle\Phi_{h}^{iL}(y)=\left[Id+hf_{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}f^{\prime}_{2}f_{2}\right](y)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}),\quad\Phi_{h}^{eL}(y)=\left[Id+hf_{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}f^{\prime}_{2}f_{2}\right](y)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).

Note that
\Phi_{h}^{iL}(y)\neq\Phi_{h}^{eL}(y)
due to their different coefficients of the term
\mathcal{O}(h^{3})
. After some calculations, we get

\displaystyle\Phi_{h}^{i}(y)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}\circ\Phi_{h}^{iL}\circ\Phi_{\frac{h}{2}}^{L}(y)=\left[Id+h(f_{1}+f_{2})+\frac{h^{2}}{2}(f^{\prime}_{1}f_{1}+f^{\prime}_{1}f_{2}+f^{\prime}_{2}f_{1}+f^{\prime}_{2}f_{2})\right](y)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{h}(y)+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).

Even if EXS-O2 is the linearization of IMS-O2, the expansions of
\Phi_{h}^{iL}
and
\Phi_{h}^{eL}
are similar. Hence, it can be verified that this makes no difference to the error result, i.e.,
\Phi_{h}^{e}(y)-\Phi_{h}(y)=\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).

\blacksquare

4
Proofs of the main results

In this section, we rigorously prove the main results given in Section
3.1
. The technical tool so called the backward error analysis
[
18
]
which is indispensible for the study of equations or numerical solutions over long times will be used in the following proofs.

4.1
Backward error analysis

Following the analysis of backward error
analysis, the main idea is to find a modified differential equation whose solution
z(t)
at
t=nh
is equivalent to
the numerical result
x^{n}
produced by the considered method.

EXS-O2.

On the basis of the first equation in (
9
) and the symmetry of EXS-O2, we get

x^{n-1}=x^{n}-he^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}v^{n}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}E(x^{n}).

This result and the first one of (
9
) give

\displaystyle x^{n+1}-2x^{n}+x^{n-1}=h\left(e^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}-e^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}\right)v^{n}+h^{2}E(x^{n}),

\displaystyle x^{n+1}-x^{n-1}=h\left(e^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}+e^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}\right)v^{n}.

Elimination of
v^{n}
leads to

\frac{x^{n+1}-2x^{n}+x^{n-1}}{h^{2}}=\frac{2}{h}\frac{e^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}-e^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}}{e^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}+e^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}}\frac{x^{n+1}-x^{n-1}}{2h}+E(x^{n}).

According to the special scheme of
\tilde{B}(x)
, it is obtained that

\frac{2}{h}\frac{e^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}-e^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}}{e^{\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}+e^{-\frac{h}{2}\tilde{B}(x^{n})}}=\frac{\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B(x^{n})\right|\right)}{\frac{h}{2}\left|B(x^{n})\right|}\tilde{B}(x^{n}),

and such that

\frac{x^{n+1}-2x^{n}+x^{n-1}}{h^{2}}=\frac{\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B(x^{n})\right|\right)}{\frac{h}{2}\left|B(x^{n})\right|}\frac{x^{n+1}-x^{n-1}}{2h}\times B(x^{n})+E(x^{n}).

For a fixed
t
, the function
z(t)
has to satisfy

\frac{z(t+h)-2z(t)+z(t-h)}{h^{2}}=\frac{\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B(z(t))\right|\right)}{\frac{h}{2}\left|B(z(t))\right|}\frac{z(t+h)-z(t-h)}{2h}\times B(z(t))+E(z(t)).

We let
z:=z(t)
and expand the above functions in powers of
h
, so that

\displaystyle\frac{\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|\right)}{\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|}z^{\intercal}S\left[-\frac{h^{2}}{4}E^{\prime}(z)\left(\dot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{6}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)+\cdots\right]\times B+\frac{h^{2}}{4}z^{\intercal}SE^{\prime}(z)\left(\ddot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{12}\ddddot{z}+\cdots\right)+\cdots+\mathcal{O}(h^{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|\right)}{\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|}z^{\intercal}S\left[\frac{h^{2}}{4}Q\left(\dot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{6}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)\right]\times B-\frac{h^{2}}{4}z^{\intercal}SQ\left(\ddot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{12}\ddddot{z}+\cdots\right)+{\mathcal{O}(h^{N}).}

According to the properties of
S
and
Q
, it is obtained that
SQ
is skew-symmetric and
S^{2}Q
is symmetric. Then, we get

z^{\intercal}S\left(\left(Qz^{(2k+1)}\right)\times B\right)=z^{\intercal}S^{2}Qz^{(2k+1)}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(z^{\intercal}S^{2}Qz^{(2k)}-\dot{z}^{\intercal}S^{2}Qz^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2}\left({z^{(k)}}\right)^{\intercal}S^{2}Qz^{(k)}\right)

and

z^{\intercal}SQz^{(2k)}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(z^{\intercal}SQz^{(2k-1)}-\dot{z}^{\intercal}SQz^{(2k-2)}+\cdots+(-1)^{k-1}\left({z^{(k-1)}}\right)^{\intercal}SQz^{(k)}\right).

Therefore, the proof is complete.

\blacksquare

Proof of (
13
).
With the results stated above, the method EXS-O2 conserves the momentum with the accuracy

\displaystyle M(x^{n},v^{n})

\displaystyle=\tilde{M}_{h}^{e}(x^{n},v^{n})+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})=\tilde{M}_{h}^{e}(x^{0},v^{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\tilde{M}_{h}^{e}(x^{k},v^{k})-\tilde{M}_{h}^{e}(x^{k-1},v^{k-1})\right)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})

(20)

\displaystyle=M(x^{0},v^{0})+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})+n\mathcal{O}(h^{N+1})+\mathcal{O}(h^{2})=M(x^{0},v^{0})+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

as long as
nh\leq h^{2-N}
. For IMS-O2, the same discussion applies to
M_{h}^{i}(x,v)
and then (
13
) can be proved.

\blacksquare

In this section, we just considered magnetic moment in constant magnetic field

with

and a skew-symmetric matrix

Specifically, the modified equations (

\displaystyle\left(\dot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{6}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)\times b=

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{h^{2}}{4}E^{\prime}(z)\left(\dot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{6}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)+\cdots\right)\times b.

Based on these results, we can derive two almost invariants close to magnetic moment, which will complete the proof of Theorem
3.3
. To this end, we first prove the following result.

Lemma 4.10

If

, one gets that the functions

along solutions of the modified differential equation (

) for IMS-O2. Here the functions
I_{2j}^{e}(x,v)
and

are
h
-independent and

Proof.

Multiply (
21
) and (
22
) with
\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\left(z^{(2k+1)}\times b\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}I(z,\dot{z}),&\text{$k=0$}\\
\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\left((\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\left(z^{(2k)}\times b\right)-\left(\dddot{z}\times b\right)^{\intercal}\left(z^{(2k-1)}\times b\right)+\cdots+\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{2}\left(z^{(k+1)}\times b\right)^{\intercal}\left(z^{(k+1)}\times b\right)\right),&\text{$k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}$}\end{cases}

Corollary 4.11

(The almost invariants close to magnetic moment)

If
B(x)\equiv B
and
U(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\intercal}Qx+q^{\intercal}x
with
Q\widehat{B}=\widehat{B}Q
, one obtains that the functions

\widehat{I}_{h}^{e}(x,v)=I(x,v)+h^{2}\widehat{I}_{2}^{e}(x,v)+h^{4}\widehat{I}_{4}^{e}(x,v)+\cdots,

\widehat{I}_{h}^{i}(x,v)=I(x,v)+h^{2}\widehat{I}_{2}^{i}(x,v)+h^{4}\widehat{I}_{4}^{i}(x,v)+\cdots,

satisfy

\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{I}_{h}^{e}{(z,\dot{z})}=\mathcal{O}(h^{N})

along solutions of the modified differential equation (
16
) for EXS-O2,
and

\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{I}_{h}^{i}{(z,\dot{z})}=\mathcal{O}(h^{N})

along solutions of the modified differential equation (
17
) for IMS-O2. Here the functions
\widehat{I}_{2j}^{e}(x,v)
and
\widehat{I}_{2j}^{i}(x,v)
are
h
-independent and
\mathcal{O}(h^{N})
refers to the truncation term.

Proof.

On the basis of the conditions above,
-(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}E(z)=(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}(Qz+q)=\frac{d}{dt}\left((\dot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}(Qz+q)\right).

The hand-side of (
23
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{h}{2\left|B\right|\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|\right)}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\left[(Qz+q)+\frac{h^{2}}{4}Q\left(\ddot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{12}\ddddot{z}+\cdots\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\left[\frac{h^{2}}{4}Q\left(\dot{z}+\frac{h^{2}}{6}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)\right]\times b+\mathcal{O}(h^{N}).

Since
\widehat{B}Q
is skew-symmetric and
\widehat{B}^{2}Q
is symmetric, we have

\displaystyle(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}Qz^{(2k)}

\displaystyle=-{\ddot{z}}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}Qz^{(2k)}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{$k=1$}\\
-\frac{d}{dt}\left({\ddot{z}}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}Qz^{(2k-1)}-{\dddot{z}}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}Qz^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+(-1)^{k}\left({z^{(k)}}\right)^{\intercal}\widehat{B}Qz^{(k+1)}\right),&\text{$k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}-\{1\}$}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\left(Qz^{(2k+1)}\right)\times b

\displaystyle=-\ddot{z}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}^{2}Qz^{(2k+1)}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}\dot{z}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}^{2}Q\dot{z},&\text{$k=0$}\\
-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\ddot{z}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}^{2}Qz^{(2k)}-\dddot{z}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}^{2}Qz^{(2k-1)}+\cdots+\frac{(-1)^{(k+1)}}{2}\left({z^{(k+1)}}\right)^{\intercal}\widehat{B}^{2}Qz^{(k+1)}\right),&\text{$k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.$}\end{cases}

For the sake of formal unity, we formulate
-{z^{(l)}}^{\intercal}\widehat{B}^{2}Qz^{(m)}
as
\left(z^{(l)}\times b\right)^{\intercal}\left(Qz^{(m)}\right)\times b
for any integers
l
and
m
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[I(z,\dot{z})+\frac{h^{2}}{12\left|B\right|}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}(\ddot{z}\times b)-\frac{h}{2\left|B\right|\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|\right)}\left(\left(\frac{h^{2}}{12}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)+(\dot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}(Qz+q)\right)\right]=\mathcal{O}(h^{N})

along solutions of the modified differential equation (
16
) for EXS-O2,
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[I(z,\dot{z})+\frac{h^{2}}{12\left|B\right|}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}(\ddot{z}\times b)-\frac{h}{2\left|B\right|\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|\right)}\left(\frac{h^{2}}{12}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{h}{2\left|B\right|\tan\left(\frac{h}{2}\left|B\right|\right)}\left((\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}(Qz+q)+\frac{h^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{h^{2}}{12}(\ddot{z}\times b)^{\intercal}Q\dddot{z}+\cdots\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{h^{2}}{4\left|B\right|}\left(\frac{1}{2}{(\dot{z}\times b)}^{\intercal}(Q\dot{z})\times b+\cdots\right)\right]=\mathcal{O}(h^{N}).

\blacksquare

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented two splitting algorithms and studied their long-term behaviour for solving charged-particle dynamics.
Using backward error analysis, it was shown that these two algorithms have good conservations of energy, momentum and magnetic moment in some special cases. Furthermore, one algorithm was proved to conserve a modified energy exactly. All the results were illustrated by some numerical tests.

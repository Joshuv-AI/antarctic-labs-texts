Three phases of multiple SLE driven by non-colliding Dyson’s Brownian motions
Makoto Katori, Shinji Koshida

Abstract
. The present paper is concerned with properties of multiple Schramm–Loewner evolutions (SLEs) labelled by a parameter κ ∈ ( 0 , 8 ] \kappa\in(0,8] . Specifically, we consider the solution of the multiple Loewner equation driven by a time change of Dyson’s Brownian motions in the non-colliding regime. Although it is often considered that several properties of the solution can be studied by means of commutation relations of SLEs and the absolute continuity, this method is available only in the case that the curves generated by commuting SLEs are separated. Beyond this restriction, it is not even obvious that the solution of the multiple Loewner equation generates multiple curves. To overcome this difficulty, we employ the coupling of Gaussian free fields and multiple SLEs. Consequently, we prove the longstanding conjecture that the solution indeed generates multiple continuous curves. Furthermore, these multiple curves are (i) simple disjoint curves when κ ∈ ( 0 , 4 ] \kappa\in(0,4] , (ii) intersecting curves when κ ∈ ( 4 , 8 ) \kappa\in(4,8) , and (iii) space-filling curves when κ = 8 \kappa=8 .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

Since its introduction in
[
Sch00
]
, Schramm–Loewner evolution (SLE) has been playing one of the central roles in studying critical systems in two dimensions. It is a theory of a random continuous curve as a stochastic analogue of the classical Loewner theory, which we briefly recall. Let
\mathbb{H}
be the complex upper half plane. We consider the chordal Loewner equation driven by a continuous function
\xi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
:

(1.1)

\frac{d}{dt}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-\xi(t)},\quad t\geq 0,\quad g_{0}(z)=z\in\mathbb{H}.

For each
z\in\mathbb{H}
, we define

\tau_{z}:=\sup\set{t\geq 0:\mbox{The solution of (\ref{eq:Loewner}) is well defined up to $t$},\ g_{t}(z)\in\mathbb{H}},

and, for each
t\geq 0
, we set
K_{t}:=\set{z\in\mathbb{H}}{\tau_{z}\leq t}
. Then,
g_{t}
is the conformal map from
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
to
\mathbb{H}
under the hydrodynamical normalization:
\lim_{z\to\infty}|g_{t}(z)-z|=0
.

A particularly significant example is the case when the driving function is given by
\xi(t)=B_{\kappa t}
,
t\geq 0
, where
(B_{t}:t\geq 0)
is a standard Brownian motion and
\kappa>0
. In this case, almost surely, the limit
\eta(t):=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}g_{t}^{-1}(\xi(t)+\sqrt{-1}\varepsilon)\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
exists for all
t\geq 0
,
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
is a continuous curve called an SLE-curve such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\eta(t)=\infty
, and at each
t\geq 0
,
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
is the unbounded component of
\mathbb{H}\backslash\eta(0,t]
. In particular,
\eta
defines a probability law of a curve in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
connecting
0
and
\infty
. We call the probability law or the family of conformal maps
(g_{t}:t\geq 0)
specified by the parameter
\kappa
the SLE
(\kappa)
. It was argued in
[
Sch00
]
that SLE
(\kappa)
with varying
\kappa>0
covers the probability laws on a curve in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
connecting
0
and
\infty
that exhibit the
conformal invariance
and the
domain Markov property
.

The SLE-curves fall into three phases depending on
\kappa

[
LSW04
,
RS05
]
. If
\kappa\in(0,4]
,
\eta
is almost surely a simple curve. If
\kappa\in(4,8)
,
\eta
is almost surely self-intersecting and hits
\mathbb{R}
. If
\kappa>8
,
\eta
is almost surely space-filling.

In relation to critical systems, an SLE-curve is typically a candidate for a scaling limit of a cluster interface in a critical lattice system and, in some models including the critical percolation
[
Smi01
]
, the uniform spanning tree and the related loop-erased random walk
[
LSW04
]
, the critical Ising model
[
CDCH+14
]
, this property has been proved. In this context of critical systems, it is natural to extend the notion of SLE so to describe random multiple curves, which is where the notion of multiple SLE takes place. There are several formulations of multiple SLE, especially two
local
formulations; one way is to define a multiple SLE as a collection of commuting Loewner chains
[
Dub07
]
, and the other way is to consider the multiple Loewner equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}g_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-X_{t}^{(i)}},\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle g_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=z\in\mathbb{H}

driven by
N
continuous stochastic processes
\left(X^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0\right)
,
i=1,\dots,N
as was first introduced in
[
BBK05
]
.
(Yet another formulation of multiple SLE
[
KL07
,
Law09
,
BPW21
]
is referred to as
global
.)
These days, the definition as commuting Loewner chains has been more often adopted in literatures (e.g. in
[
PW19
,
Kar19
,
Kar20
,
Izy20
]
). In fact, the definition is sufficient as far as the probability law of multiple curves is concerned, and more convenient to analyze since each curve can be studied separately. On the other hand, we can only count few papers
[
Gra07
,
RS17
,
HK18
]
where the multiple Loewner equation is discussed, and little is known about basic properties of its solution.
The study of the multiple Loewner equation was initiated by Cardy in
[
Car03
]
in the
radial
case
under the motivation of understanding multiple cluster interfaces.
An advantage of the multiple Loewner equation is that it is more natural in the context of complex analysis and makes clearer sense as a dynamical system.
Furthermore, the multiple Loewner equation manifests the potential interrelation between cluster interfaces and many particle systems
as was already pointed out in
[
Car03
]
.
These aspects of the multiple Loewner equation motivate us to study it in the present paper.

Before formulating our problem, let us see a general property of the solution to the multiple Loewner equation (
1.2
).
For each
z\in\mathbb{H}
, we define a stopping time

\tau_{z}:=\sup\set{t\geq 0:\mbox{The solution of (\ref{eq:multiple_Loewner}) is well defined up to $t$},\ g_{t}(z)\in\mathbb{H}},

and for each
t\geq 0
, we set
K_{t}:=\set{z\in\mathbb{H}:\tau_{z}\leq t}
. Then at each
t\geq 0
,
g_{t}
is the hydrodynamically normalized conformal map from
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
to
\mathbb{H}
.

1.2.
Problem setting

Let us fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta\geq 0
. The
N
-particle Dyson’s Brownian motion model of parameter
\beta
is the system of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) on
\left(Y^{\beta;(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0\right)
,
i=1,\dots,N
that reads
[
Dys62
]

(1.3)

dY^{\beta;(i)}_{t}=dB^{(i)}_{t}+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{dt}{Y^{\beta;(i)}_{t}-Y^{\beta;(j)}_{t}},\quad t\geq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,N,

where
(B^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,
i=1,\dots,N
are independent standard Brownian motions.
It is known
[
CL97
]
that the Dyson’s Brownian motion model (
1.3
) have a unique strong continuous solution for arbitrary initial conditions
Y^{\beta;(i)}_{0}=Y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, and that the solution is almost surely non-colliding if
\beta\geq 1
.
We call a solution of the Dyson’s Brownian motion model (
1.3
) a system of
N
Dyson’s Brownian motions of parameter
\beta
, or simply, Dyson’s Brownian motions.

In this paper, we study the solution of the multiple Loewner equation (
1.2
) when the driving processes are given by
X^{(i)}_{t}=Y^{8/\kappa;(i)}_{\kappa t}
,
t\geq 0
,
i=1,\dots,N
with
\kappa\in(0,8]
such that the initial points
X_{i}:=X^{(i)}_{0}
,
i=1,\dots,N
are distinct. Specifically, these driving processes are almost surely non-colliding, hence, they make sense at any
t\geq 0
. Note that, in general, Dyson’s Brownian motions can be started from an identical point and multiple SLE driven by such Dyson’s Brownian motions was of importance in
[
HK18
]
. In the current paper, however, we concentrate on the case when the initial conditions are given by distinct points. We call the solution
(g_{t}:t\geq 0)
the
N
-SLE
(\kappa)
driven by Dyson’s Brownian motions, or the
N
-SLE
(\kappa)
for short, starting at
\bm{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})
. Let us notice, to be sure, that the driving processes satisfy the system of SDEs

(1.4)

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{(i)}=\sqrt{\kappa}dB_{t}^{(i)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{4dt}{X^{(i)}_{t}-X^{(j)}_{t}},\quad t\geq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,N.

Remark 1.1
.

It is natural to perform a time change of Dyson’s Brownian motions to obtain driving processes as an analogy to the case of the ordinary SLE. On the other hand, it is more non-trivial that the strength of the interaction is fixed at
\beta=8/\kappa
. This phenomenon has been repeatedly observed in literatures
[
Car03
,
BBK05
,
Dub07
]
and is understood as a consequence of the fact that the multiple SLE is consistent with a critical system. In fact, we will rely on coupling of multiple SLE and Gaussian free field (GFF), a representing example of critical system.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
BBK05
,
Dub07
]
, the authors studied conditions on a multiple SLE so that it describes multiple cluster interfaces of a critical system.
In consequence, they found that multiple driving processes must be determined by a single
partition function
.
From this perspective, the system of SDEs (
1.4
) should be expressed as
[
BBK05
]

\displaystyle dX_{t}^{(i)}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\kappa}dB_{t}^{(i)}+\kappa\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\log Z\right)\left(X^{(1)}_{t},\dots,X^{(N)}_{t}\right)dt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{2dt}{X^{(i)}_{t}-X^{(j)}_{t}},

\displaystyle t\geq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,N,

where
Z=Z(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})
is the partition function given by

Z(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2/\kappa},\quad x_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\;i=1,\dots,N,\quad x_{i}\neq x_{j},\;i\neq j.

The goal of the present paper is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3
.

For each
i=1,\dots,N
, almost surely, the limit
\eta^{(i)}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}g^{-1}_{t}(X^{(i)}_{t}+\sqrt{-1}\varepsilon)
exists for all
t\geq 0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}|\eta^{(i)}(t)|=\infty
. Furthermore,

(1)

if
\kappa\in(0,4]
,
\eta^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
are almost surely simple disjoint curves such that
\eta^{(i)}(0,\infty)\subset\mathbb{H}
,
i=1,\dots,N
,

(2)

if
\kappa\in(4,8)
,
\eta^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
are almost surely continuous curves that intersect themselves and
\mathbb{R}
,

(3)

if
\kappa=8
,
\eta^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
are almost surely space filling continuous curves.

Remark 1.4
.

(1)

Theorem
1.3
implies that, at each
t\geq 0
, the domain
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
is the unbounded component of
\mathbb{H}\backslash\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\eta^{(i)}(0,t]
.

(2)

Even if
\kappa>4
, two curves do not cross each other. Indeed, crossing of curves contradicts the continuity of the driving processes.

(3)

If
\kappa>4
, each curve
\eta^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
hits the next curves, but does not hit the second next curves. In fact, if it hits a second next curve, then it contradicts the fact that the next curve in between goes to infinity.

1.3.
Strategy

Before illustrating our strategy to prove Theorem
1.3
, let us consider an alternative one that can be employed when
\kappa\in(0,4]
.
As we have already mentioned, there are two distinguished (local) formulations of multiple SLE; the commuting family of Loewner chains
[
Dub07
]
and the multiple Loewner evolution
[
BBK05
]
.
A possible strategy is to prove the equivalence between these two formulations.
In fact, they can be shown to be equivalent at the infinitesimal level by means of the generators of the relevant Markov processes though an explicit proof has not appeared to the authors’ knowledge.
It is well-known that each member of a commuting Loewner chain of parameter
\kappa
is absolutely continuous with respect to the SLE
(\kappa)
.
Therefore, what happens almost surely to SLE
(\kappa)
happens almost surely to the members of the commuting Loewner chains.
In consequence, we can study the random geometry generated by the multiple SLE regardless of how it is formulated.

The significant assumption in the above argument is that the commuting family of Loewner chains defines multiple curves, or in other words,
the infinitesimal commutation relations among Loewner chains are enhanced to the finite ones.
Note that, however, infinitesimal commutation relations can be integrated to finite ones under the assumption that the curves generated by the Loewner chains are separated, which is valid only when
\kappa\in(0,4]
.
Only in such a case, the infinitesimal equivalence of the two formulations of multiple SLE is also integrated.

We hope to go further beyond this restriction.
Indeed, under the assumption that curves are separated, it is in principle impossible to study if two curves touch each other or not, as is stated in Theorem
1.3
.
For that purpose, we have to employ another method to ensure the existence of multiple curves.
Below, we describe our strategy to prove Theorem
1.3
.

We rely on the notion of coupling between multiple SLE and GFF, which has been studied in
[
Dub09b
,
MS16
]
.
The relevant GFF is given by

(1.5)

h=H-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\arg(\cdot-X_{i}),

where
H
is the zero-boundary GFF on
\mathbb{H}
, the function
\arg
takes values in
(0,\pi)
, and
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\dots,N
, which are supposed to give initial conditions of Dyson’s Brownian motions, are distinct.
Note that the GFF (
1.5
) is the only possibility that can be coupled with multiple SLE when
\kappa\neq 4

[
Kos21
]
.
In
[
KM13
]
, it is understood that the GFF (
1.5
) is a combination of a central charge deformation of the zero-boundary GFF and an insertion of primary fields in the language of conformal field theory.

It is worth noting that the coupling between SLE and GFF has been studied in many other different settings; different domains and different boundary conditions in e.g.
[
IK13
,
QW18
,
BKT18
]
.

For each
i=1,\dots,N
, a random continuous curve
\gamma^{(i)}:[0,\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
such that
\gamma^{(i)}(0)=X_{i}
is characterized by the coupling property with the GFF
h
. Furthermore, under this coupling, the curve,
\gamma^{(i)}
, is determined by the GFF
h
and is called the
flow line
of
h
starting at
X_{i}
.
Therefore, it is ensured that there is a probability law of multiple curves
\left(\gamma^{(i)}:i=1,\dots,N\right)
that is determined by the GFF.
Our strategy consists of two steps:

Step 1:

Show that the random multiple curves
\left(\gamma^{(i)}:i=1,\dots,N\right)
exhibit the analogous properties as those in Theorem
1.3
.

Step 2:

Show that the random geometry generated by
\left(\gamma^{(i)}:i=1,\dots,N\right)
agrees in probability law with that generated by the
N
-SLE
(\kappa)
driven by Dyson’s Brownian motions
(g_{t}:t\geq 0)
.

As we will see in the rest of the paper, both of these steps are completed as consequences of the characterization of the random multiple curves
\left(\gamma^{(i)}:i=1,\dots,N\right)
in terms of coupling with the GFF.
We stress that, though the
Step 1
is thought of a slight extension of the preceding work
[
MS16
]
, the
Step 2
gives a new insight on study of multiple SLE.
Let us describe these steps in more precise language.

Step 1

It should be obvious that the goal of this step is to prove the following statements.

Proposition 1.5
.

We have
\lim_{t\to\infty}|\gamma^{(i)}(t)|=\infty
a.s. for every
i=1,\dots,N
. Furthermore,

(1)

if
\kappa\in(0,4]
,
\gamma^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
are almost surely disjoint simple curves such that
\gamma^{(i)}(0,\infty)\subset\mathbb{H}
,
i=1,\dots,N
,

(2)

if
\kappa\in(4,8)
,
\gamma^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
almost surely intersect themselves and
\mathbb{R}
,

(3)

if
\kappa=8
,
\gamma^{(i)}
,
i=1,\dots,N
are almost surely space-filling.

We will prove Proposition
1.5
in Section

. The idea closely follows
[

]
, where we will consider conditional laws of a single curve given the others taking advantage of the coordinate-free formulation of the coupling (see Proposition

Step 2

Note that, for each
i=1,\dots,N
, the curve

naturally carries a parametrization

. Suppose that the curves

,

are adapted to filtrations
(\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{s_{i}})_{s_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
,

, respectively. When we set, for each

\underline{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}

\left(\mathcal{F}_{\underline{s}}\right)_{\underline{s}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}}

Lemma 1.6
.

There exist unique functions
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};

such that the following property holds.
At each
t\geq 0
, define the subset
\widetilde{K}_{t}\subset\mathbb{H}
so that
\mathbb{H}\backslash\widetilde{K}_{t}

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{g}_{t}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2}{\widetilde{g}_{t}(z)-\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{t}},\quad t\geq 0,\quad\widetilde{g}_{0}(z)=z,

.
Furthermore,

as

,

.

Remarkably, the numerators of the summands are normalized as

.

The remaining task is to show that the driving processes
\left(\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0\right)
,

Lemma 1.7
.

At each

,
\underline{s}(t)=(s_{1}(t),\dots,s_{N}(t))
is an
\left(\mathcal{F}_{\underline{s}}\right)_{\underline{s}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}}

).

Lemmas
1.6
and

, which will be proved in Sect.

Proof of Theorem

.

Since the probability laws of weak solutions to a system of SDEs coincide,
the probability law of
(g_{t}:t\geq 0)

We close Introduction with a few comments.
The coupling of the GFF (
1.5
) and the
N
-SLE
(\kappa)
was found and discussed in our previous works
[
KK20a
,
KK20b
]
, that is, we found that the multiple SLE driven by the time change of
N
Dyson’s Brownian motions of parameter
8/\kappa
satisfying (
1.4
) is coupled with the GFF (
1.5
). Lemmas
1.6
and
1.7
above give the converse assertion. In fact, they say that, if a Loewner chain
(\widetilde{g}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is coupled with the GFF (
1.5
), then the driving processes must satisfy the system of SDEs (
1.4
). Dyson’s Brownian motion model
[
Dys62
]
is one of the most studied models in dynamical extension of random matrix theory (RMT). We emphasize that Lemmas
1.6
and
1.7
are not only essential in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, but also highlight potential interrelation between GFF and RMT.

In this paper, we focus on the case of
\kappa\in(0,8]
when the driving processes are almost surely non-colliding. When
\kappa>8
, the driving processes satisfying (
1.4
) are almost surely colliding. In this case, it is natural to expect that a solution can be continued after collision in a suitable manner so that the multiple SLE is coupled with the same GFF. It turned out, however, technically difficult to deal with this colliding regime in the same method relying on GFF. In fact, when
\kappa>8
, the single parametrization in Lemma
1.6
will break down. It is not obvious that this event of breaking down of the single parametrization exactly corresponds to the collision of driving processes. Hence, it seems still challenging to the authors to study property of the

-SLE
(\kappa)
with
\kappa>8
.

We also concentrate on the case when
X_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
in (
1.5
) are distinct. As we already noted, initial conditions of Dyson’s Brownian motions are not necessarily distinct. If two of initial conditions are identical, however, the method we employ in this paper relying on the coupling with GFF does not seem to be applied. We leave analysis of such cases in future work.

We have introduced our driving processes by setting the parameter of Dyson’s Brownian motion model at
\beta=8/\kappa
.
In the SLE side, SLE
(\kappa)
and SLE
(\kappa^{\prime})
with
\kappa^{\prime}=16/\kappa
are known to be
dual
to each other
[
Dub05
,
Zha08
,
Dub09a
,
Zha10
]
.
In terms of the
\beta
-parameter, the two parameters
\beta=8/\kappa
and
\beta^{\prime}=8/\kappa^{\prime}
are related by
\beta=4/\beta^{\prime}
.
It is remarkable that, for general
\beta
-ensembles, the static version of Dyson models,
these two parameters are also regarded as dual
[
Des09
,
For10
]
.
It would be an interesting direction to consider if these two kinds of duality can be incorporated in the framework of the coupling
between multiple SLEs and GFFs.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Complex analysis

A bounded subset
K\subset\mathbb{H}
is called a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull if
K=\overline{K}\cap\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}\backslash K
is simply connected.
For a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull
K
, there exists a unique conformal map
g_{K}:\mathbb{H}\backslash K\to\mathbb{H}
under the hydrodynamical normalization:
\lim_{z\to\infty}|g_{K}(z)-z|=0
.
The half-plane capacity of
K
is defined by
\mathrm{hcap}(K):=\lim_{z\to\infty}z(g_{K}(z)-z)
. Note that the half-plane capacity is positive and increasing in the sense that, if
K\subset K^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{hcap}(K)\leq\mathrm{hcap}(K^{\prime})

[
Law05
]
.

Let us recall two significant properties of the half-plane capacity: additivity and scaling properties
[
Law05
]
.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{H}
be a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull and
B\subset\mathbb{H}\backslash A
be a subset such that
A\cup B
be a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull.
Then,
g_{A}(B)\subset\mathbb{H}
is a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull, and we have the additivity of the half-plane capacity:

(2.1)

\mathrm{hcap}(A\cup B)=\mathrm{hcap}(A)+\mathrm{hcap}(g_{A}(B)).

For a positive number
\alpha>0
, the dilatation
\psi_{\alpha}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
is defined by
\psi_{\alpha}(z)=\alpha z
,
z\in\mathbb{H}
.
For a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull
A\subset\mathbb{H}
, its image
\psi_{\alpha}(A)
under the dilation is again a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull.
The scaling property of the half-plane capacity is the property that

(2.2)

\mathrm{hcap}(\psi_{\alpha}(A))=\alpha^{2}\mathrm{hcap}(A).

Recall that the solution
(g_{t}:t\geq 0)
of a multiple Loewner equation (
1.2
) is a family of conformal maps
g_{t}:\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}\to\mathbb{H}
,
t\geq 0
. It can be verified from continuity of the driving processes
\left(X^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0\right)
,
i=1,\dots,N
that, at each
t\geq 0
,
K_{t}
is almost surely a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull.
Furthermore, expanding both sides of (
1.2
) around
z=\infty
and comparing the coefficients of
z^{-1}
,
we find that the half-plane capacities
\mathrm{hcap}(K_{t})
,
t\geq 0
satisfy the ordinary differential equation

\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{hcap}(K_{t})=2N,\quad t\geq 0,\quad\mathrm{hcap}(K_{0})=0.

In other words, they are given by
\mathrm{hcap}(K_{t})=2Nt
,
t\geq 0
.

2.2.
GFF terminology

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an open subset with harmonically non-trivial boundary, which implies that a Brownian motion starting at a point in
D
almost surely hits the boundary
\partial D
. We write
C^{\infty}_{0}(D)
for the space of
C^{\infty}
functions compactly supported in
D
, which we equip with the Dirichlet inner product

(f,g)_{\nabla}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{D}(\nabla f)\cdot(\nabla g),\ \ f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(D).

The Hilbert space completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(D)
with respect to the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\nabla}
is denoted by
W(D)
.
Let us fix a complete orthonormal system
\set{\phi_{n}}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
W(D)
and take i.i.d. normal Gaussian random variables
\alpha_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
defined on a probability space
(\Omega^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{GFF}})
. We write the expectation value under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{GFF}}
as
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{GFF}}
.
The zero-boundary GFF on
D
is defined by (see e.g.
[
MS16
]
)

H=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{n}\phi_{n},

which almost surely converges in a space of distributions with test functions in
C_{0}^{\infty}(D)
. Hence, for
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(D)
, the pairing
(H,f)
is well-defined, where
(\cdot,\cdot)
may be regarded as the standard
L^{2}
inner product. Then, motivated by the integration by parts, we may define

(H,f)_{\nabla}:=-\frac{1}{2\pi}(H,\Delta f),\ \ f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(D).

This extends to the whole
W(D)
so that
(H,f)_{\nabla}
is well-defined for
f\in W(D)
. We also have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{GFF}}[(H,f)_{\nabla}(H,g)_{\nabla}]=(f,g)_{\nabla},\ \ f,g\in W(D).

Hence, this defines an isometry
(H,\cdot)_{\nabla}:W(D)\to L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{GFF}})
. We can assume that the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}
is generated by
(H,f)_{\nabla}

f\in W(D)
, and have the Wiener chaos decomposition
[
Jan97
,
She07
]
:

(2.3)

L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{GFF}})\simeq\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}W(D)^{\odot n},

where
W(D)^{\odot n}
is the
n
-fold symmetric tensor product of
W(D)
.

Let
U\subsetneq D
be an open subset. We may regard
W(U)
as a closed subspace of
W(D)
and can restrict the GFF
H
on
D
to
U
. The orthogonal complement
W^{\perp}(U)\subset W(D)
consists of functions that are harmonic on
U
.
We define a
\sigma
-subalgebra
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{U}
generated by
H|_{U}
, equivalently, generated by
(H,f)
,
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U)
. The conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{GFF}}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{U}]:L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{GFF}})\to L^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{GFF}},\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{U},\mathbb{P}^{\mathrm{GFF}})
is realized in the Wiener chaos decomposition (
2.3
) by the projection induced by
W(D)\to W(U)
.
For a closed subset
K\subset D
, we write
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K^{+}}
for the
\sigma
-subalgebra generated by the projection of
H
onto
W^{\perp}(D\backslash K)
.
Then the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{GFF}}[\cdot|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K^{+}}]
is associated with the projection
W(D)\to W^{\perp}(D\backslash K)
in the Wiener chaos decomposition (
2.3
).

According to the orthogonal decomposition
W(D)=W(U)\oplus W^{\perp}(U)
, we write
H\overset{\mathrm{(law)}}{=}H_{U}+H_{U^{\mathrm{c}}}
, where
H_{U}
and
H_{U^{\mathrm{c}}}
are independent random distributions such that (1)
H_{U}
is the zero-boundary GFF on
U
and vanishes outside
U
, and (2)
H_{U^{\mathrm{c}}}
is harmonic on
U
.
Hence, we have the Markov property stated as follows:

Proposition 2.1

(Markov property)
.

For a subdomain
U\subset D
, the conditional law of
H|_{U}
given
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{(D\backslash U)^{+}}
is that of the zero-boundary GFF
H_{U}
plus the harmonic extension of
H|_{\partial U}
onto
U
.

3.
Flow lines of Gaussian free field

3.1.
Configurations and boundary conditions

Let
D\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
and
y
be distinct boundary points aligned counterclockwise in this order. We call a set of these data
\mathcal{C}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
a configuration following
[
Dub09b
]
. An equivalence from a configuration
\mathcal{C}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
to another
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=(D^{\prime};x^{\prime}_{1},\dots,x^{\prime}_{N};y^{\prime})
is an conformal map
\psi:D\to D^{\prime}
such that
\psi(x_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
and
\psi(y)=y^{\prime}
. In this case, we informally write
\psi:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
or
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\psi(\mathcal{C})
.

Given real numbers
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}
, and
\chi
, we call
(\underline{a},\chi)
a set of boundary conditions and define, for each configuration
\mathcal{C}=(D;\underline{x},y)
, a harmonic function
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)}
on
D
in the following procedure. For
\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{H};X_{1},\dots,X_{N};\infty)
, we set

\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)}(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}\arg(z-X_{i}),\quad z\in\mathbb{H}.

For another configuration
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
equivalent to
\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{H};X_{1},\dots,X_{N};\infty)
, we take an equivalence
\psi:\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{C}
and set
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime},(\underline{a},\chi)}:=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)}\circ\psi-\chi\arg\psi^{\prime}
, where
\psi^{\prime}
is the derivative of
\psi
in
z\in D
. When
N\geq 2
, such an equivalence
\psi
is unique if exists. Even if it is not unique when
N=1
, the harmonic function
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime},(\underline{a},\chi)}
is well-defined.
A pair
([\mathcal{C}],(\underline{a},\chi))
of an equivalence class of configurations and a set of boundary conditions is essentially the same object as what is called an imaginary surface in
[
MS16
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A random distribution
h
on
D
with test functions in
C^{\infty}_{0}(D)
is called a GFF if there exists a configuration
\mathcal{C}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
and a set of boundary conditions
(\underline{a},\chi)
such that
h\overset{\mathrm{(law)}}{=}H+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C};(\underline{a},\chi)}
, where
H
is the zero-boundary GFF on
D
. In this case, we also say that
h
is the GFF on
\mathcal{C}
under the boundary conditions
(\underline{a},\chi)
.

Since the restriction of a harmonic function to a subdomain is harmonic, the Markov property of a GFF is obvious; for a GFF
h
on
D
and any subdomain
U\subset D
, the conditional law of
h|_{U}
given
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{(D\backslash U)^{+}}
is the same as that of the zero-boundary GFF on
U
plus the harmonic extension of
h|_{\partial U}
onto
U
. It is not, however, ensured that
h|_{U}
given
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{(D\backslash U)^{+}}
is a GFF on
U
in the sense of Definition
3.1
, i.e., there exists a configuration
\mathcal{C}=(U;\underline{x};y)
and a set of boundary conditions
(\underline{a},\chi)
so that the conditional law of
h|_{U}
given
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{(D\backslash U)^{+}}
agrees with that of
H_{U}+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)}
.

3.2.
Coupling: generality

The usual notion of a Loewner chain in a domain refers to an increasing closed subsets evolving from the boundary.
Let us introduce its generalization, a
Loewner chain in a configuration
, which avoids the other boundary points than the one it starts from.

Definition 3.2
.

Fix an arbitrary
i\in\set{1,\dots,N}
.
A Loewner chain in a configuration
\mathcal{C}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
starting at
x_{i}
is a family of increasing subsets
(K^{(i)}_{t}\subset D:t\geq 0)
such that:

(1)

it is generated by a continuous non-crossing curve
\gamma^{(i)}:[0,\infty)\to\overline{D}
starting at
\gamma^{(i)}(0)=x_{i}
so that, at each
t\geq 0
,
D\backslash K^{(i)}_{t}
is the connected component of
D\backslash\gamma^{(i)}(0,t]
whose closure contains
y
.

(2)

it does not hit
x_{j}
,
j\neq i
and
y
, i.e.,
x_{j}\not\in\overline{K^{(i)}_{t}}
,
j\neq i
and
y\not\in\overline{K^{(i)}_{t}}
for any
t\geq 0
.

Let
\mathcal{C}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
be a configuration and fix arbitrarily
i\in\set{1,\dots,N}
.
We write
\mathcal{U}^{(i)}
for the collection of open neighborhoods of
x_{i}
in
D
equipped with the natural partial order with respect to the inclusion relation.
Let
(\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{U})_{U\in\mathcal{U}^{(i)}}
be a filtration of
\sigma
-algebras labelled by
\mathcal{U}^{(i)}
.
An
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
-GFF is a GFF
h
on
D
such that, for any
U\in\mathcal{U}^{(i)}
,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{U}\subset\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{U}
, and
h|_{D\backslash\overline{U}}
is conditionally independent of
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{U}
given
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{\partial U}
(Note that the conditional independence from
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{U}
follows from the Markov property). We say that a Loewner chain
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
in
\mathcal{C}
starting at
x_{i}
is
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
-adapted if, for any
U\in\mathcal{U}^{(i)}
, its first exit time from
U
is
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{U}
-measurable. Suppose that the Loewner chain
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is generated by a curve
\gamma^{(i)}
. At any
t\geq 0
, we set
D^{(i)}_{t}:=D\backslash K^{(i)}_{t}
and
\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t}:=(D^{(i)}_{t};x_{1},\dots,\gamma^{(i)}(t),\dots,x_{N};y)
.
Given a set of boundary conditions
(\underline{a},\chi)
, we consider the following problem referred to as the coupling problem: find a filtered probability space
\left(\Omega,(\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{U})_{U\in\mathcal{U}^{(i)}},\mathbb{P}\right)
on which a GFF
h
and a Loewner chain
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
in
\mathcal{C}
starting at
x_{i}
are defined, such that

•

the GFF
h
is an
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
-free field,

•

the Loewner chain
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}
-adapted,

•

we have

\mathbb{E}\left[h|_{D^{(i)}_{t}}\Big|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K_{t}^{(i)+}}\right]=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{(i)},(\underline{a},\chi)},\quad t\geq 0,

where
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation value under the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
.

Recall that the conditional expectation value with respect to
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K_{t}^{(i)+}}
takes the harmonic function part of the restriction
h|_{D_{t}^{(i)}}
.
Hence, the last condition is equivalent to that the conditional law of
h|_{D^{(i)}_{t}}
given
K^{(i)}_{t}
agrees with that of the zero boundary GFF on
D^{(i)}_{t}
plus the harmonic function
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}_{t}^{(i)},(\underline{a},\chi)}
.
Note that, at the initial time
t=0
, we must have
h\overset{\mathrm{(law)}}{=}H+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C};(\underline{a},\chi)}
, where
H
is the zero-boundary GFF on
D
, i.e.,
h
is the GFF on
\mathcal{C}
under the boundary conditions
(\underline{a},\chi)
.

Significantly, as was noticed in
[
Dub09b
]
, the above coupling problem is coordinate free in the following sense.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=(D;x_{1},\dots,x_{N};y)
be a configuration equivalent to
\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{H};X_{1},\dots,X_{N};\infty)
under an equivalence
\psi:\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{C}
,
and let
i\in\set{1,\dots,N}
be fixed.
Suppose that we have a Loewner chain
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
in
\mathcal{C}
starting at
X_{i}
and that the pair
\left(h=H+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)},(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)\right)
, where
H
is the zero-boundary GFF on
\mathbb{H}
, gives a solution to the coupling problem for
\mathcal{C}
.
Let
H^{\prime}
be the zero-boundary GFF on
D
and set
h^{\prime}=H^{\prime}+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime},(\underline{a},\chi)}
.
Then, the pair
\left(h^{\prime},\left(\psi^{-1}(K^{(i)}_{t}):t\geq 0\right)\right)
gives a coupling for
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
under the same set of boundary conditions
(\underline{a},\chi)
.

Proof.

Using the family of conformal maps
(g^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
associated with
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[h|_{\mathbb{H}^{(i)}_{t}}\Big|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K_{t}^{(i)+}}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\arg\left(g^{(i)}_{t}(\cdot)-X^{(j)}_{t}\right)-\chi\arg(g^{(i)}_{t})^{\prime}(\cdot),\quad t\geq 0,

where
X^{(j)}_{t}=g^{(i)}_{t}(X_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
.
Note that the composition
g^{(i)}_{t}\circ\psi
is an equivalence from
\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t})
to
\mathcal{C}
, implying that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t}),(\underline{a},\chi)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j}\arg\left(g^{(i)}_{t}(\cdot)-X^{(j)}_{t}\right)-\chi\arg(g^{(i)}_{t})^{\prime}(\cdot)\right)\circ\psi-\chi\arg\psi^{\prime}(\cdot)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t},(\underline{a},\chi)}\circ\psi-\chi\arg\psi^{\prime}(\cdot),\quad t\geq 0.

In particular, we have
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime},(\underline{a},\chi)}=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)}\circ\psi-\chi\arg\psi^{\prime}
.
Noticing the conformal invariance of the zero-boundary GFF, we have

\displaystyle h^{\prime}|_{\psi^{-1}(\mathbb{H}^{(i)}_{t})}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(law)}}{=}\left(H+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C},(\underline{a},\chi)}\right)\Big|_{\mathbb{H}^{(i)}_{t}}\circ\psi-\chi\arg\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(law)}}{=}\left(H\circ g^{(i)}_{t}+\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t},(\underline{a},\chi)}\right)\circ\psi-\chi\arg\psi^{\prime}

conditioned over
K^{(i)}_{t}
. Since
(H\circ g^{(i)}_{t})\circ\psi
is the zero-boundary GFF on
\psi^{-1}(\mathbb{H}^{(i)}_{t})
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[h^{\prime}|_{\psi^{-1}(\mathbb{H}_{t})}\Big|\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{\psi^{-1}(K^{(i)}_{t})^{+}}\right]=\mathfrak{h}_{\psi^{-1}(\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t}),(\underline{a},\chi)},\quad t\geq 0.

Therefore, the desired property holds.
∎

Due to Proposition
3.3
, we can specialize our attention to the case of
\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{H};X_{1},\dots,X_{N};\infty)
without any loss of generality as long as a single Loewner chain is concerned.

3.3.
Flow lines

In the rest of the paper, we consider a specific set of boundary conditions
a_{i}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}
,
i=1,\dots,N
and
\chi=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}-\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}
. Observe that the GFF
h
in (
1.5
) is the one on
\mathbb{H}
under these boundary conditions.
We see that there is a Loewner chain in
\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{H};X_{1},\dots,X_{N};\infty)
starting at each
X_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,N
coupled with this GFF
h
.

Let us arbitrarily fix
i\in\set{1,\dots,N}
, and consider a curve
\gamma^{(i)}:[0,\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
such that
\gamma^{(i)}(0)=X_{i}
that is generated by the SLE
(\kappa,\underline{\rho})
with
\underline{\rho}=(2,\dots,2)
as follows
[
Dub07
]

\frac{d}{dt}g^{(i)}_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g^{(i)}_{t}(z)-\xi^{(i)}_{t}},\quad t\geq 0,\quad g^{(i)}_{0}(z)=z\in\mathbb{H},

where

\displaystyle\xi^{(i)}_{t}

\displaystyle=X_{i}+\sqrt{\kappa}B^{(i)}_{t}+\sum_{j;j\neq i}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2ds}{\xi^{(i)}_{s}-\zeta^{(i,j)}_{s}},

\displaystyle\zeta^{(i,j)}_{t}

\displaystyle=X_{j}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2ds}{\zeta^{(i,j)}_{s}-\xi^{(i)}_{s}},\quad j\neq i,\quad t\geq 0.

Here,
(B^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is a standard Brownian motion.
We write
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
for the increasing family of compact
\mathbb{H}
-hulls generated by
(g^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)

\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}^{(i)}
is the unbounded component of
\mathbb{H}\backslash\gamma^{(i)}(0,t]
at each

.
We also suppose that these processes are adapted to a filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Then, the family
(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is a Loewner chain in
\mathcal{C}=(\mathbb{H};X_{1},\dots,X_{N};\infty)
starting at

. Indeed, the following property follows from a special case of the arguments in
[
KP16
, Appendix A]
and the fact that the Bessel process of dimension
d
almost surely does not hit
0
if

(see e.g.
[
Kat15
, Theorem 1.1]

Proposition 3.4

(
[
KP16
, Proposition A.5]
)
.

Let
i\in\set{1,\dots,N}
be fixed. Then, for each
j\neq i
, the process
(\xi^{(i)}_{t}-\zeta^{(i,j)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is absolutely continuous with respect to a time change of the Bessel process of dimension
8/\kappa+1\geq 2
. In particular, it almost surely does not hit
0
.

Therefore, the conditions
X_{j}\not\in\overline{K^{(i)}_{t}}
,
j\neq i
,

are satisfied.
To describe the coupling, we write
\mathfrak{h}^{(i)}_{t}=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}^{(i)}_{t},(\underline{a},\chi)}
, or explicitly,

\mathfrak{h}^{(i)}_{t}(\cdot):=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\arg\left(g^{(i)}_{t}(\cdot)-\xi^{(i)}_{t}\right)-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{j;j\neq i}\arg\left(g^{(i)}_{t}(\cdot)-\zeta^{(i,j)}_{t}\right)-\chi\arg(g^{(i)}_{t})^{\prime}(\cdot),\quad t\geq 0,

where

. Then
\mathfrak{h}^{(i)}_{t}
is a harmonic function on
\mathbb{H}\backslash K^{(i)}_{t}
at each

. We also set

,

, where

, and write

.
The coupling phenomenon is stated as follows
[
Dub09b
,
MS16
]
:

Proposition 3.5

Let
h
be the GFF in (

) and

For any

, the conditional law of

is the same as that of

. Moreover, the pair
\left(h,(K^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)\right)

Furthermore, we have the following
[
Dub09b
,
MS16
]
:

Theorem 3.6
.

For each fixed
i\in\set{1,\dots,N}
, the random curve
\gamma^{(i)}
is a deterministic functional of the GFF

in (

) so that
\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{t}=\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K^{(i)+}_{t}}
at each

.

When
\kappa\in(0,4)
, for each

, the curve
\gamma^{(i)}
is referred to as the flow line of

starting at
X_{i}
in
[

]
. It was shown therein that the curve corresponding to the parameter

can be realized in the same domain and called the counter flow line. When

, each curve

,

]

4.2.
Proof of Lemma

We define the function

as a solution of the system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs)

(4.1)

\frac{\partial\mathrm{hcap}(\widetilde{K}_{\underline{s}})}{\partial s_{i}}\frac{ds_{i}(t)}{dt}=2,\quad t\geq 0,\quad i=1,\dots,N,

under the initial conditions
s_{i}(0)=0
,

.
Then the desired property is immediately observed from a standard argument in the Loewner theory. In fact, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{i}}\widetilde{g}_{\underline{s}}(z)=\frac{1}{\widetilde{g}_{\underline{s}}(z)-\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{\underline{s}}}\frac{\partial\mathrm{hcap}(\widetilde{K}_{\underline{s}})}{\partial s_{i}},

and
\widetilde{g}_{t}=\widetilde{g}_{\underline{s}(t)}
,
\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{t}=\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{\underline{s}(t)}
,

, and
\widetilde{K}_{t}=\widetilde{K}_{\underline{s}(t)}
,

.
The uniqueness of such a function is obvious from the definition.
Let us see the existence of a solution to (
4.1
).
To this aim, we employ an idea from
[
LSW03
]
to estimate the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial\mathrm{hcap}(\widetilde{K}_{\underline{s}})}{\partial s_{i}}
,
i=1,\dots,N
.
Let us take an arbitrary instance of the GFF (
1.5
). Then, Theorem
3.6
ensures that instances of
\gamma^{(i)}
,

are determined.
We fix

and

, and set

. Then, due to Proposition
1.5
, the curve

lies in

. At each

, we take the compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull

Here,

is the family of compact
\mathbb{H}
-hulls starting at

that is generated by the instance of

, the SLE

with

determined by the instance of the GFF. Notice that the image

-hull

Then, we have

g^{(i)}_{A,s_{i}}\circ g_{A}=f^{(i)}_{s_{i}}\circ g^{(i)}_{s_{i}},\quad s_{i}\geq 0.

\mathrm{hcap}\left(\widetilde{K}_{\underline{s}}\right)=\mathrm{hcap}\left(K^{(i)}_{A,s_{i}}\right)+\mathrm{hcap}(A),\quad\underline{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{i-1},s_{i},s_{i+1},\dots,s_{N}),\quad s_{i}\geq 0.

4.3.

,

is an

\sigma

forms a filtration, to which
(\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)

Our goal is to show that the processes
(\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,

give a weak solution to the system of SDEs (

), which is divided into two parts. Firstly, set
\mathcal{C}_{t}:=\left(\widetilde{\mathbb{H}}_{t};\gamma^{(1)}_{s_{1}(t)},\dots,\gamma^{(N)}_{s_{N}(t)};\infty\right)
,

and consider the following stochastic process

\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot):=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathcal{C}_{t};(\underline{a},\chi)}(\cdot)=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\arg(\widetilde{g}_{t}(\cdot)-\widetilde{X}^{(i)}_{t})-\chi\arg(\widetilde{g}_{t})^{\prime}(\cdot),\quad t\geq 0.

The coupling condition implies the following property:

Proposition 4.2
.

The stochastic process
(\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot):t\geq 0)
is a family of continuous local martingales with cross variation

d\braket{\mathfrak{h}(z),\mathfrak{h}(w)}_{t}=-dG(\widetilde{g}_{t}(z),\widetilde{g}_{t}(w)),\ \ z,w\in\mathbb{H},\ \ 0\leq t\leq\tau_{z}\wedge\tau_{w},

where

is the Green’s function on
\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

From the coupling condition, the conditional law of
h|_{\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}}
given
\mathcal{F}_{t}
agrees with that of
H\circ g_{t}+\mathfrak{h}_{t}
.
Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(D)
be a test function and
\varepsilon>0
be a constant.
Setting
\tau_{f,\varepsilon}=\inf\set{t>0:\mathrm{dist}(K_{t},\mathrm{supp}(f))\leq\varepsilon}
, we can see that
M_{t}:=\mathbb{E}\left[(h,f)|\mathcal{F}_{t\wedge\tau_{f,\varepsilon}}\right]
,
t\geq 0
is a martingale.
Recall that, from Theorem
3.6
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}^{(i)}_{s_{i}(t)}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{GFF}}_{K_{s_{i}(t)}^{(i)+}}
,
t\geq 0
, which implies that
M_{t}=(\mathfrak{h}_{t\wedge\tau_{f,\varepsilon}},f)
,
t\geq 0
. Hence,
(\mathfrak{h}_{t}:t\geq 0)
is a local martingale.
For two test functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
in
\mathbb{H}\backslash K_{t}
we set

E_{t}(f_{1},f_{2}):=\int_{\mathrm{supp}(f_{1})\times\mathrm{supp}(f_{2})}f_{1}(z)G(\widetilde{g}_{t}(z),\widetilde{g}_{t}(w))f_{2}(w)dzdw,\quad 0\leq t\leq\tau_{f_{1},\varepsilon}\wedge\tau_{f_{2},\varepsilon}.

Note that
(h,f_{i})
,
i=1,2
are Gaussian variables with mean
(\mathfrak{h}_{0},f_{i})
,
i=1,2
, respectively and their covariance is
E_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})
. On the other hand,
(H\circ\widetilde{g}_{t},f_{i})
,
i=1,2
are Gaussian variables with mean
0
and their covariance is
E_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})
. Hence,
(\mathfrak{h}_{t},f_{i})
,
i=1,2
must be Gaussian variables with mean
(\mathfrak{h}_{0},f_{i})
,
i=1,2
, respectively, and covariance
E_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})-E_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})
. Therefore, the cross variation of
(\mathfrak{h}_{t}:t\geq 0)
must be as desired.
∎

Therefore, the proof of Lemma
1.7
is reduced to proving the following result:

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose that a Loewner chain
(g_{t}:t\geq 0)
solves the multiple Loewner equation (
1.2
).
Consider the stochastic process

\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot):=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\arg(g_{t}(\cdot)-X^{(i)}_{t})-\chi\arg g^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot),\ \ t\geq 0.

Then
(\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot):t\geq 0)
is a family continuous local martingales with cross variation

d\braket{\mathfrak{h}(z),\mathfrak{h}(w)}_{t}=-dG(g_{t}(z),g_{t}(w)),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{H},\quad 0\leq t\leq\tau_{z}\wedge\tau_{w},

if and only if the driving processes
(X^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,

solve the system of SDEs (

Proof.

It can be argued that
(\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot):t\geq 0)
is a continuous local martingale if and only if so is

\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}(\cdot):=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log(g_{t}(\cdot)-X_{t}^{(i)})-\chi\log g^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot),\ \ t\geq 0.

Note that
\mathfrak{h}_{t}(\cdot)=\mathrm{Im}\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}(\cdot)
,
t\geq 0
.
From the multiple Loewner equation (
1.2
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\log g^{\prime}_{t}(z)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2}{(g_{t}(z)-X^{(i)}_{t})^{2}},\quad z\in\mathbb{H}\quad 0\leq t\leq\tau_{z}.

Let us take generic points
z_{1},\dots,z_{N}\in\mathbb{H}
and set
\tau_{\underline{z}}=\tau_{z_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\tau_{z_{N}}
.
Then, we know that
\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}(z_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
are continuous local martingales and
g_{t}(z_{j})
,
\log g^{\prime}_{t}(z_{j})
,
j=1,\dots,N
are finite variational processes up to
\tau_{\underline{z}}
.
Owing to the implicit function theorem, we can see that each
X^{(i)}_{t}
,
i=1,\dots,N
is a

\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}(z_{j})
,
g_{t}(z_{j})
,
\log g^{\prime}_{t}(z_{j})
,

up to
\tau_{\underline{z}}
. Therefore, it is a semi-martingale due to Itô’s theorem.
We write
X^{(i)}_{t}=M^{(i)}_{t}+F^{(i)}_{t}
,
t\geq 0
,
i=1,\dots,N
, where
(M^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,
i=1,\dots,N
are local martingales and
(F^{(i)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,
i=1,\dots,N
are finite variation processes. Then, we compute the stochastic derivative of
(\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}(\cdot):t\geq 0)
to see

\displaystyle d\overline{\mathfrak{h}}_{t}(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(g_{t}(z)-X^{(i)}_{t})^{2}}\left(\left(-\frac{4}{\sqrt{\kappa}}+2\chi\right)dt+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa}}d\braket{M^{(i)},M^{(i)}}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{g_{t}(z)-X^{(i)}_{t}}\Biggl(dF^{(i)}_{t}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{4}{X^{(i)}_{t}-X^{(j)}_{t}}dt\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{g_{t}(z)-X^{(i)}_{t}}dM^{(i)}_{t},\quad z\in\mathbb{H},\quad 0\leq t\leq\tau_{z}.

Here we used the identity

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z-x_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{z-x_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z-x_{i}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}\frac{1}{x_{i}-x_{j}}

between rational functions, which can be shown either by a direct manipulation or comparing the residues at the poles at

,
i=1,\dots,N

The contribution from the pole of second order at

,

,

,

,

Furthermore, the cross variation of

\displaystyle d\braket{\mathfrak{h}(z),\mathfrak{h}(w)}_{t}=

\displaystyle-dG(g_{t}(z),g_{t}(w))

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{\kappa}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N}\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{1}{g_{t}(z)-X^{(i)}_{t}}\right)\mathrm{Im}\left(\frac{1}{g_{t}(w)-X^{(j)}_{t}}\right)d\braket{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}_{t},

\displaystyle z,w\in\mathbb{H},\quad 0\leq t\leq\tau_{z}\wedge\tau_{w}.

Hence, we have
d\braket{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}_{t}=0
,
t\geq 0
,

.
∎

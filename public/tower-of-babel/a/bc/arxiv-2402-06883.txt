Nakano positivity of singular Hermitian metrics: Approximations and applications
Takahiro Inayama 1, Shin-ichi MATSUMURA 2

Abstract
. This paper studies the approximation of singular Hermitian metrics on vector bundles using smooth Hermitian metrics with Nakano semi-positive curvature on Zariski open sets. We show that singular Hermitian metrics capable of this approximation satisfy Nakano semi-positivity as defined through the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} -equation with optimal L 2 L^{2} -estimates. Furthermore, for a projective fibration f : X → Y f\colon X\to Y with a line bundle L L on X X , we provide a specific condition under which the Narasimhan-Simha metric on the direct image sheaf f ∗ ​ 𝒪 X ​ ( K X / Y + L ) f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L) admits this approximation. As an application, we establish several vanishing theorems.

1.
Introduction

Generalizing the theory from smooth to singular Hermitian metrics is a significant topic in complex geometry.
This aspect is particularly vital when addressing singular Hermitian metrics on (holomorphic) vector bundles.
A pivotal challenge lies in establishing an
appropriate
definition of Nakano positivity
and expanding the theoretical framework beyond smooth Hermitian metrics.
Primarily, there are two approaches to defining Nakano positivity:
The first approach employs an approximation with smooth Hermitian metrics,
initiated by de Cataldo
[
deC98
]
and has been continually explored (see
[
GMY22
,
GMY23
]
for examples).
The second approach employs the
\overline{\partial}
-equation with optimal
L^{2}
-estimates of Hörmander type,
which has recently attracted attention and is developing rapidly (see
[
Ber98
,
HI21
,
Ina22
,
DNWZ23
]
for examples).
Despite these advancements, defining Nakano positivity for singular Hermitian metrics remains an unresolved issue,
with challenges still to be addressed:

(1)

How are the first and second approaches of Nakano positivity related?

(2)

What are typical examples of Nakano positive singular Hermitian metrics?

(3)

Is it possible to derive vanishing theorems for singular Hermitian metrics?

The primary purpose of this paper is to propose a new framework regarding the Nakano positivity of a singular Hermitian metric
h
on a vector bundle
E
that encompasses the existing theories developed by de Cataldo and Guan–Mi–Yuan.
Our definition of Nakano semi-positivity, denoted by
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{s}_{\mathrm{Nak.}}0
,
is based on the existence of a sequence
\{h_{s}\}_{s=1}^{\infty}
of singular Hermitian metrics
converging to
h
, where each metric is smooth and has arbitrarily small Nakano negativity on a Zariski open set
(see Definition
2.4
for the precise condition).

Our definition builds upon the first approach, drawing inspiration from
[
GMY22
,
GMY23
]
.
Compared to the approach in
[
GMY22
,
GMY23
]
, our definition is more flexible
in that
h_{s}
is not required to be smooth on the entire space.
This flexibility enables us to establish Theorem
1.1
,
which demonstrates that the direct image sheaf
f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L))

satisfies our Nakano semi-positivity under a reasonable assumption.
This result partially solves Problem (2) and is also important in terms of applications to algebraic geometry.

2.
Definitions of Nakano positivity and preliminary results

This section is devoted to explaining the definitions of positivity for singular Hermitian metrics
and preliminary results needed for subsequent discussions.
The primary purpose is to introduce a new definition of Nakano positivity mentioned in Section
1
(see Definition
2.4
).

Throughout this paper,
let
X
be a complex manifold of dimension
n
and
E
be a vector bundle of rank
r
on
X
.
Moreover, we adopt the following convention:

Definition 2.1

(Associated Hermitian forms and Nakano/Griffiths positivity)
.

A smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
defines
the Chern curvature and associated Hermitian form:

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\in C^{\infty}(X,\Lambda^{1,1}\otimes\operatorname{End}{(E)})\text{ and }\sqrt{-1}\widetilde{\Theta}_{h}\in C^{\infty}(X,\operatorname{Herm}(T_{X}\otimes E)).

The metric
h
is said to be
Nakano semi-positive

if
\sqrt{-1}\widetilde{\Theta}_{h}
is semi-positive definite for all elements
\tau\in T_{X}\otimes E

and
Griffiths semi-positive
if
\sqrt{-1}\widetilde{\Theta}_{h}
is semi-positive definite
for all decomposable elements
\xi\otimes s\in T_{X}\otimes E
.

In general, for a real
(1,1)
-form
\Xi
valued in
\operatorname{End}{(E)}
,
we can define the associated Hermitian form on
T_{X}\otimes E
, denoted by
\Xi_{h}
.
For instance, for a real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
,
the notation
\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}}
refers to the Hermitian form associated with
\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}
.
These forms are often identified when there is no risk of confusion.

We review the notion of Griffiths positivity for singular Hermitian metrics,
which coincides with the definition involving curvature presented above for smooth Hermitian metrics.

Definition 2.2
.

(Singular Hermitian metrics,
[
BP08
, Section 3]
,
[
HPS18
, Definition 17.1]
,
[
PT18
, Definition 2.2.1]
).
A
singular Hermitian metric

h
on
E
is defined as a measurable map from the base manifold
X

to the space of non-negative Hermitian forms on the fibers of
E
such that
0<\det h<\infty
holds almost everywhere.

Definition 2.3

(Griffiths positivity)
.

A singular Hermitian metric
h
on
E
is said to be
Griffiths semi-positive

if
\log|u|_{h^{*}}
is psh (plurisubharmonic)
for any local (holomorphic) section
u
of
E^{*}
,
where
h^{*}
denotes the induced metric on the dual vector bundle
E^{*}
.

The following definition, inspired by
[
deC98
, Definition 2.4.1]
and
[
GMY23
, Definition 1.1]
,
introduces a new approach to defining singular Nakano positivity via an approximation.

Definition 2.4

(Nakano positivity in the sense of approximations)
.

Let
\omega
be a Hermitian form and
\theta
be a continuous real
(1,1)
-form on
X
.
Consider a singular Hermitian metric
h
on
E
with
h^{*}
being upper semi-continuous
(i.e., the function
|u|^{2}_{h^{*}}
is upper semi-continuous for any local section
u
of
E^{*}
).
We define
the
\theta
-Nakano positivity in the sense of approximations
, denoted by

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{s}_{\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}},

by the existence of
the following data:
(\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{\Sigma_{j,s}\}_{j,s=1}^{\infty},\{h_{j,s}\}_{j,s=1}^{\infty})
consisting of

•

\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is an open cover of
X
such that

X_{j}\Subset X_{j+1}\Subset X
for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
;

•

\Sigma_{j,s}\subset X_{j}
is a (proper closed) analytic subset of
X_{j}
;

•

h_{j,s}
is a
C^{2}
-Hermitian metric on
E|_{X_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j,s}}
;

and satisfying Conditions (a) and (b):

(a)

Set
\Sigma_{j}:=\cup_{s=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{j,s}
.
Then, for any
x\in X_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j}
and
e\in E_{x}
, we have

|e|_{h_{j,s}}\nearrow|e|_{h}\text{ as }s\nearrow\infty.

(b)

There exist continuous functions
\lambda_{j,s}
and
\lambda_{j}
on
\overline{X}_{j}

such that

•

0\leq\lambda_{j,s}\leq\lambda_{j}
on
X_{j}
;

•

\lambda_{j,s}\to 0
almost everywhere on
X_{j}
;

•

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{j,s}}\geq_{\mathrm{Nak.}}(\theta-\lambda_{j,s}\omega)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h_{j,s}}}

holds at any point
x\in X_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j}
.

Note that this definition does not depend on the choice of
\omega
by
X_{j}\Subset X
.

Remark 2.5
.

(1) In contrast to
[
GMY23
, Definition 1.1]
,
our definition does not require

to be

on

allowing more flexibility in addressing Nakano positivity.

(2)
The inequality
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{j,s}}\geq_{\mathrm{Nak.}}(\theta-\lambda_{j,s}\omega)\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h_{j,s}}}
in Condition (b) is valid on

on

satisfying
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq_{\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}}
at any point

is a subset. It can be easily verified that the condition

.

As mentioned in Section

(

.

Consider the same situation as in Definition

(1) We define
the global

-estimates
, denoted by

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{glo.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}}\text{ on }X,

For any data consisting of

We define

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{loc.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}}\text{ on }X,

Therefore, for simplicity, we will write

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}}\text{ instead of }\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{L^{2}}_{\mathrm{glo.}\mathrm{Nak.}}\theta\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E_{h}}.

For smooth Hermitian metrics, it is a direct consequence of the definitions that Nakano positivity implies Griffiths positivity.
This relation can be similarly extended to singular Hermitian metrics after a slight modification.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
h
be a singular Hermitian metric on
E
such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h}\geq^{s}_{\mathrm{Nak.}}0\text{ holds in the sense of Definition \ref{def-singNak:sequenceNew}}.

Assume that
\Sigma_{j}=\cup_{s}\Sigma_{j,s}
is a closed pluripolar set for

.
Then
h
is a.e. Griffiths semi-positive

(
i.e., the function
\log|u|_{h^{*}}
is a.e. psh
for any local holomorphic section
u
of
E^{*}
)
.

Proof.

Consider a holomorphic section
u\in H^{0}(U,E^{*})
defined on an open subset
U\Subset X
and
take a member

from the open cover
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
in Definition
2.4
such that
U\subset X_{j}
.
Then,
[
GMY22
, Proposition 2.3]
indicates that
\log|u|_{h^{*}}
is psh on
U\setminus\Sigma_{j}
.
Since
h^{*}
is upper semi-continuous, the function
\log|u|^{2}_{h^{*}}
is locally bounded above.
Hence, there exists a psh function

on

on

.
(see
[

for example).
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem

This section is devoted to proving Theorem

.
Using the same notation as in Definition
2.4

-spaces.
For a section

\displaystyle L^{2}_{n,q}(X_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j,s},E;\omega,h_{j,s})\cong L^{2}_{n,q}(X_{j},E;\omega,\widetilde{h}_{j,s}).

Note that

Additionally, the analytic subset

We fix a sufficiently large

Define

(c)

Condition (a) is derived from the straightforward computation:

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{H_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{g}+(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{\delta(\varepsilon)}}

\displaystyle\geq-\varepsilon f^{*}\omega_{Y}+(\varepsilon-(1-\varepsilon)\delta(\varepsilon))\omega_{X}+(1-\varepsilon)f^{*}\theta

For

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\varepsilon_{1}}}{H_{\varepsilon_{2}}}

\displaystyle=g^{\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}}\cdot\frac{h_{\delta(\varepsilon_{1})}^{1-\varepsilon_{1}}}{h_{\delta(\varepsilon_{2})}^{1-\varepsilon_{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq g^{\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}}\cdot h_{\delta(\varepsilon_{1})}^{\varepsilon_{2}-\varepsilon_{1}}

Hence, the desired monotonicity follows from
g\leq 1
and
h_{\delta}\geq 1
.

Consider the natural injective sheaf morphism:

f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L)\otimes\mathcal{I}(H_{\varepsilon}))\to f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L))=:\mathcal{E}.

Note that this morphism is an isomorphism on
Y\setminus f(Z_{\delta(\varepsilon)})
by Condition (b).
Let
G_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
G
) be the Narasimhan-Simha metric on
\mathcal{E}

induced by
H_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
h
) and the above morphism (see
[
HPS18
,
PT18
]
).
We aim to show that
G_{\varepsilon}
and
G
satisfy the conditions of Definition
5.1
.
Take an analytic subset
V\subset Y
such that
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth fibration over
Y\setminus V

and

f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}\cong H^{0}(X_{y},\mathcal{O}_{X_{y}}(K_{X_{y}}\otimes L|_{X_{y}}))

for a point
y\in Y\setminus V
.
Here
\mathfrak{m}_{y}\subset\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}
is the maximal ideal of the stalk
\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}

and
X_{y}
denotes the fiber of
f\colon X\to Y
at
y
.
Set
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}:=f(Z_{\delta(\varepsilon)})\cup V
and
\Sigma:=\cup_{0<\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}}\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
The metric
G_{\varepsilon}
is a smooth Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{E}|_{Y\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}
.
Moreover, by
[
Ber09
]
and
[
DNWZ23
, Theorem 1.6]
,
Condition (a) indicates that
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{G_{\varepsilon}}\geq_{\mathrm{Nak.}}(\theta-\lambda_{\varepsilon}\omega_{Y})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{G_{\varepsilon}}
on
Y\setminus\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
Meanwhile, by the construction of Narasimhan-Simha metrics, for a (local) section
s
of
\mathcal{E}
,
the metrics
G_{\varepsilon}
and
G
at
y\in Y\setminus\Sigma
can be expressed as the fiber integral:

|s|^{2}_{G_{\varepsilon}}=\int_{X_{y}}|s|_{X_{y}}|^{2}_{H_{\varepsilon}}\quad\text{ and }\quad|s|^{2}_{G}=\int_{X_{y}}|s|_{X_{y}}|^{2}_{h}.

Hence, Condition (c) indicates that
G_{\varepsilon}\nearrow G
holds at any point in
Y\setminus\Sigma
.
This completes the proof in the case where
Y
is compact.

In the case where
Y
is non-compact, we encounter a new problem:
the inequalities
g\leq 1
and
h\geq 2
are not satisfied even after scaling
g
and
h
with a constant.
We can find such a constant on a relatively compact subset
X_{j}\Subset X
,
but the scaling with this constant alters the target
h
that
requires approximation, and this alteration depends on each
j
.
Therefore, some technical modifications are required to address this issue.
The strategy of the proof is to apply the arguments in the first half for the modified metrics
\widetilde{g}
and
\widetilde{h}
,
which are defined below.

Since
f\colon X\to Y
is a proper fibration,
we can find smooth functions
\varphi_{g}
and
\varphi_{h}
on
Y

such that

\widetilde{g}:=g\cdot e^{-f^{*}\varphi_{g}}\leq 1\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{h}:=h\cdot e^{-f^{*}\varphi_{h}}\geq 2.

Then, we can easily check that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{\widetilde{h}}\geq f^{*}(\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{h})\quad\text{ and }\quad\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{\widetilde{g}}+f^{*}(\omega_{Y}-\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{g})\geq\omega_{X}.

Take an open cover
\{Y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
Y

such that
Y_{j}\Subset Y_{j+1}\Subset Y
and define
X_{j}:=f^{-1}(Y_{j})
.
Note that the open cover
\{X_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
X
clearly satisfies
X_{j}\Subset X_{j+1}\Subset X
.
For each
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
by applying
[
Dem92
, Main Theorem 1.1]
to
h|_{X_{j}}
,
we can find singular Hermitian metrics
\{h_{j,\delta}\}_{\delta>0}
on
L|_{X_{j}}
satisfying (
5.1
) on
X_{j}
.
Precisely,
[
Dem92
, Main Theorem 1.1]
assumes that
X
is compact,
but this theorem remains valid for the relatively compact subset
X_{j}\Subset X

(see
[
Mat22
, Theorem 2.9]
for the detailed argument).
We fix a sufficiently large
c_{j}
such that

c_{j}\omega_{Y}\geq\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(\varphi_{h}-\varphi_{g})\text{ on }Y_{j}.

We define
\lambda_{j,\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon(1+c_{j})\leq(1+c_{j})=:\lambda_{j}
so that

\lambda_{j,\varepsilon}\omega_{Y}\geq\varepsilon(\omega_{Y}+\theta+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}(\varphi_{h}-\varphi_{g}))\text{ on }Y_{j}.

Consider the new metric
{H}_{j,\varepsilon}
defined by

{H}_{j,\varepsilon}=\widetilde{g}^{\varepsilon}\cdot(h_{j,\delta(\varepsilon)})^{1-\varepsilon}e^{f^{*}\varphi_{h}}

Then, Conditions (a), (b), (c) on
X_{j}
can be easily confirmed by the same argument as in the first half.

Let
G_{j,\varepsilon}
(resp.
G_{j}
) be the singular Hermitian metric on
f_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X/Y}+L))|_{Y_{j}}

induced by
H_{j,\varepsilon}
(resp.
h|_{X_{j}}
).
Define
\Sigma_{j}
(resp.
\Sigma_{j,\varepsilon}
) in the same way as
\Sigma
(resp.
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
).
Then, by the same argument as in the first half,
we deduce that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{{G}_{j,\varepsilon}}\geq_{\mathrm{Nak.}}(\theta-\lambda_{j,\varepsilon}\omega_{Y})\otimes\mathrm{Id}
on
Y_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j,\varepsilon}
and

G_{j,\varepsilon}\nearrow G_{j}
on
Y_{j}\setminus\Sigma_{j}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

At the end of this paper, as an application of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
,
we establish Theorem
1.4
.
This theorem generalizes
[
Iwa21
, Theorem 1.5]
,
relaxing its assumptions and extending its applicability to weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifolds.
We emphasize that a generalization to weakly pseudoconvex manifolds is natural from the viewpoint of several complex variables.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Set
f\colon Z:=\mathbb{P}(E)\to X
and
L:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(1)
.
Consider the smooth Hermitian metric
g
on
L
induced by a smooth Hermitian metric on
E
and
f^{*}E\to L
.
It is easy to see that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{g}+f^{*}\omega_{X}\geq\omega_{Z}
holds
for some Hermitian forms.
Since
\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{q}}
is a Kähler current and
f\colon Z\to X
is a proper fibration,
there exists a Hermitian form
\omega^{\prime}
on
X
such that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{h_{q}}\geq f^{*}\omega^{\prime}
.
Let
G
be the Narasimhan-Simha metric
G
on
S^{m}E\otimes\det E

induced by the formula

S^{m}E\otimes\det E\cong f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}(mL+f^{*}\det E)=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}(K_{Z/X}+(r+m+1)L),

where
r:=\operatorname{rank}E
.
Theorem
1.1
shows that

\sqrt{-1}\Theta_{G}\geq^{s}_{\mathrm{Nak.}}\omega^{\prime}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{G}
on
Y

holds in the sense of Definition
2.4
.
Meanwhile, we have
G=S^{m}h\otimes\det h
(see
[
HPS18
, 25]
for example).
Thus, the desired vanishing theorem directly follows from Theorem
1.3
.
∎

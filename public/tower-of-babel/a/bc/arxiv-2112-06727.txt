Affine invariant convergence rates of the conditional gradient method
Javier F. Peña

Abstract
We show that the conditional gradient method for the convex composite problem min x ⁡ { f ⁡ ( x ) + Ψ ⁡ ( x ) } \min_{x}\{f(x)+\Psi(x)\} generates primal and dual iterates with a duality gap converging to zero provided a suitable growth property holds and the algorithm makes a judicious choice of stepsizes. The rate of convergence of the duality gap to zero ranges from sublinear to linear depending on the degree of the growth property. The growth property and convergence results depend on the pair ( f , Ψ ) (f,\Psi) in an affine invariant and norm-independent fashion.

Introduction

We consider the conditional gradient method for the
composite minimization problem

\min_{x}\{f(x)+\Psi(x)\}

where
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\Psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
are closed convex functions,
f
is differentiable on an open set containing
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
, and there is an available oracle for the mapping

g\mapsto\argmin\{\left\langle g,y\right\rangle+\Psi(y)\}.

The traditional format of the conditional gradient method (also known as the Frank-Wolfe algorithm) as discussed in
[
7
,
12
]
corresponds to the case when
\Psi
is the indicator function of a compact convex set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Some of the most attractive features of the conditional gradient method are its affine invariance, norm-independence, and lack of reliance on any projection mapping. These features stand in stark contrast to those of most other first-order methods. It is therefore natural to seek convergence results for the conditional gradient method that are affine invariant and norm-independent as any otherwise convergence result would typically be loose due to dependence on spurious factors.

We show that the conditional gradient method generates primal and dual iterates with a duality gap converging to zero provided a suitable
growth property
holds (see (
10
)) and the algorithm makes a judicious choice of stepsizes (see (
9
) and (
16
)). The rate of convergence of the duality gap to zero ranges from sublinear to linear (see Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
) depending on the degree of the growth property. The growth property and convergence results depend on the pair
(f,\Psi)
in an affine invariant and norm-independent fashion. In fact, our main developments do not rely on any norms at all.

Our work is inspired by recent developments of Nesterov
[
25
]
, Ghadimi
[
10
]
, and Kerdreux et al
[
14
]
.
Under the assumption that the domain of
\Psi
is bounded, Nesterov
[
25
]
established a
{\mathcal{O}}(1/k^{\nu})
convergence rate for the conditional gradient method when
\nabla f
is
\nu
-Hölder continuous. Nesterov
[
25
]
also showed the stronger convergence rate
{\mathcal{O}}(1/k^{2\nu})
under the additional assumption that
\Psi
is strongly convex. Ghadimi
[
10
]

showed an even stronger linear rate of convergence when
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous and
\Psi
is strongly convex for a judicious choice of stepsizes. On the other hand, Kerdreux et al.
[
14
]
considered the case when
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous and
\Psi
is the indicator function of a uniformly convex set. Kerdreux et al.
[
14
]
showed that the convergence rate ranges from sublinear to linear depending on the degree of uniform convexity of the set.
The results in
[
10
,
14
,
25
]
are all norm-dependent since they rely on Hölder continuity and uniform convexity. The norm-dependence of the results in
[
10
,
14
,
25
]
in turn implies that they are not affine invariant when the norms are not affine invariant, which is typically the case.

The affine invariance and norm-independence properties of the conditional gradient method suggest that there should be an affine invariant and norm-independent approach to obtain the sublinear to linear spectrum of convergence rates. The central contribution of this paper is to show that this is indeed the case. To that end, we establish general convergence results (Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
) provided a
growth property
(
10
) holds and the stepsizes are judiciously chosen. The growth property (

proposed by Jaggi
[

]
. It also has a flavor similar to that of the

properties in the context of Bregman proximal methods as discussed in
[
2
,
22
,
23
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.
Our main convergence results (Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
), obtained via the growth property (
10
), are stated in terms of the duality gap between the primal and dual iterates that the algorithm generates.
We also develop parallel results (Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
) on the suboptimality gap via a relaxation (
40
) of the growth property (

).

Aside from its natural compatibility with the properties of the conditional gradient method, the affine invariance and norm independence of our approach yields convergence results that are sharper and more general than previous approaches that do not have these two fundamental properties. More precisely, as we detail in Section
3
, Section
4
, and Section

, our convergence results not only subsume some of the norm-dependent convergence results in
[

,

,

but are also at least as sharp as these previous norm-dependent results for

choice of norms. Put differently, the affine invariance and norm-independence of our main convergence results automatically circumvent any possible spurious overhead due to a particular choice of norms or affine scaling. It is also noteworthy that our main results (Theorem

2
Growth property and affine invariant convergence

Consider the composite minimization problem

\min_{x}\{f(x)+\Psi(x)\}

(1)

where both
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\Psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
are closed convex functions.
Algorithm
1
describes the conditional gradient algorithm for (
1
). The algorithm relies on the following assumptions about (
1
):

A1.

The function
f
is differentiable on an open set containing
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
.

A2.

A point in the following set of minimizers exists and is computable for all
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(f)

\argmin_{s}\{\left\langle\nabla f(x),s\right\rangle+\Psi(s)\}.

(2)

The format of problem (
1
) is the same as that considered in
[
25
,
10
]
. The traditional format of the conditional gradient method, as discussed in
[
7
,
12
]
, corresponds to the special case when
\Psi
is the indicator function
\Psi=\delta_{C}
of some closed convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
A fundamental difference between the conditional gradient method and other popular first-order methods for (
1
), like proximal gradient or more general Bregman proximal gradient methods, is the reliance on the linear oracle (
2
) as opposed to the reliance on a proximal mapping for the function
\Psi
.
This fundamental difference has two profound implications. First, the conditional gradient method is preferable for a problem of the form (
1
) where the proximal mapping for
\Psi
is unavailable or prohibitively expensive but the linear oracle (
2
) is available. This is the case in a large class of problems in data science such as those discussed in
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
32
]
. In addition to the popular setting where
\Psi
is the indicator function of a closed convex set, the articles
[
1
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
17
,
20
,
24
,
32
]
also discuss problems of the form (
1
) where
\Psi
is not an indicator function but a more involved regularization term. This more general version of the conditional gradient method is sometimes called
generalized conditional gradient method.

Second, the reliance on the linear oracle (
2
) as opposed to the reliance on proximal mappings endows the conditional gradient method with the affine invariance and norm-independence properties that play a central role in this article.

Our main developments (Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
) concern the behavior of the gap between the
primal
iterates
x_{k},\;k=0,1,\dots
generated by
Algorithm
1
and the
dual
iterates
g_{k}:=\nabla f(x_{k}),\;k=0,1,\dots
that Algorithm
1
also generates for the Fenchel dual of (
1
), namely

\max_{u}\{-f^{*}(u)-\Psi^{*}(-u)\}.

(3)

Here
f^{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
denote the conjugates of
f
and
\Psi
respectively.
We will rely on the observation that for
g\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the set
\argmin_{y}\{\left\langle g,y\right\rangle+\Psi(y)\}
is precisely the subdifferential
\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)
.
We will also rely on the observation that the closedness of
f
and
\Psi
together with Assumptions A1 and A2 imply that
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)\subseteq{\mathrm{dom}}(f)
and
{\mathrm{dom}}(f^{*})\subseteq-{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi^{*})
.

Algorithm 1

Conditional gradient algorithm

1:

input:

(f,\Psi)
,
x_{0}\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)

2:

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

3:

pick
s_{k}\in\argmin_{y}\{\left\langle\nabla f(x_{k}),y\right\rangle+\Psi(y)\}
and
\theta_{k}\in[0,1]

4:

let
x_{k+1}:=(1-\theta_{k})x_{k}+\theta_{k}s_{k}

5:

end

for

Starting from a current iterate
x
, Algorithm
1
updates
x
to
x_{+}:=x+\theta(s-x)
where
s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)
for
g=\nabla f(x)
, and
\theta\in[0,1]
. The choice of
g=\nabla f(x)
and
s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)
yields the identity

f(x)+f^{*}(g)+\Psi(s)+\Psi^{*}(-g)=\left\langle g,x-s\right\rangle,

which can be equivalently stated as the
Wolfe gap:

f(x)+\Psi(x)+f^{*}(g)+\Psi^{*}(-g)=\left\langle g,x-s\right\rangle+\Psi(x)-\Psi(s)

(4)

or as

f(s)+\Psi(s)+f^{*}(g)+\Psi^{*}(-g)=f(s)-f(x)-\left\langle g,s-x\right\rangle=D_{f}(s,x),

(5)

where
D_{f}
denotes the
Bregman distance
of
f
, that is, the function

D_{f}(y,x)=f(y)-f(x)-\left\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\right\rangle.

In the special case when
\Psi
is the indicator function of a closed convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Wolfe gap (
4
) has the simpler and popular expression

f(x)+\Psi(x)+f^{*}(g)+\Psi^{*}(-g)=\left\langle g,x-s\right\rangle.

Our main developments are stated in terms of the functions
{\mathsf{gap}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
described next.
Define the duality gap function
{\mathsf{gap}}:{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)\times{\mathrm{dom}}(f^{*})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

{\mathsf{gap}}(x,u):=f(x)+\Psi(x)+f^{*}(u)+\Psi^{*}(-u).

(6)

Define
{\mathcal{D}}:{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)\times{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)\times[0,1]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta):=D_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)+\Psi(x+\theta(s-x))-(1-\theta)\Psi(x)-\theta\Psi(s).

(7)

The following proposition establishes a fundamental gap reduction identity connecting
{\mathsf{gap}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi),g=\nabla f(x),
and
s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)
Then for
\theta\in[0,1]
we have

{\mathsf{gap}}(x+\theta(s-x),g)=(1-\theta){\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)+{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta).

(8)

Proof.

Adding (
4
) times
(1-\theta)
and (
5
) times
\theta
we get

(1-\theta)(f+\Psi)(x)+\theta(f+\Psi)(s)+f^{*}(g)+\Psi^{*}(-g)=(1-\theta){\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)+\theta D_{f}(s,x)

Thus

\displaystyle{\mathsf{gap}}(x+\theta(s-x),g)

\displaystyle=(f+\Psi)(x+\theta(s-x))+f^{*}(g)+\Psi^{*}(-g)

\displaystyle=(1-\theta){\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)+\theta D_{f}(s,x)+(f+\Psi)(x+\theta(s-x))-(1-\theta)(f+\Psi)(x)-\theta(f+\Psi)(s).

To finish, observe that

\displaystyle\theta D_{f}(s,x)+(f+\Psi)(x+\theta(s-x))-(1-\theta)(f+\Psi)(x)-\theta(f+\Psi)(s)

\displaystyle=D_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)+\Psi(x+\theta(s-x))-(1-\theta)\Psi(x)-\theta\Psi(s)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta).

∎

Lemma 1
.

Suppose

\beta_{k},\delta_{k}\geq 0
are such that

\beta_{k+1}\leq\beta_{k}(1-\delta_{k}\beta_{k}^{p})\text{ for }k=0,1,\dots.

\beta_{k}\leq\left(\beta_{0}^{-p}+p\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\delta_{i}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

Our two central results, namely Theorem

and Theorem

, concern the sequence
{\mathsf{gap}}_{k},\;k=0,1,\dots

condition for the function

\varphi(\theta):=(1-\theta){\mathsf{gap}}(x_{k},g_{k})+{\mathcal{D}}(x_{k},s_{k},\theta)

\varphi(0)={\mathsf{gap}}(x_{k},g_{k})

Therefore the inequality in (

:

\varphi(\theta)\leq\varphi(0)+\tilde{c}\cdot\theta\cdot\varphi^{\prime}_{+}(0).

for some

.
The Armijo rule condition (

) which corresponds to choosing

.

A straightforward modification of the proof of Theorem

yields the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose

When

Proof.

As noted above, this is a modification of the proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}-\rho\cdot\hat{\theta}_{k}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x_{k},g_{k})+{\mathcal{D}}(x_{k},s_{k},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}-\rho\cdot\hat{\theta}_{k}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x_{k},g_{k})+(1-c)\cdot\theta_{k}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x_{k},g_{k})

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}-(c+\rho-1)\cdot\hat{\theta}_{k}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x_{k},g_{k})

To conclude this section, we next discuss the norm-independence and affine invariance of our developments. The growth property (
10
) as well as Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
are automatically norm-independent since they do not rely on any norms. They are also affine invariant as we next detail.

Consider an affine
reparametrization
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of the form
x:=A\tilde{x}+b
where
A:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a linear bijection. Let
\tilde{f},\tilde{\Psi}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
be defined as the corresponding reparametrizations of
f
and
\Psi
, namely

\tilde{f}(\tilde{x}):=f(A\tilde{x}+b)\;\text{ and }\;\tilde{\Psi}(\tilde{x}):=\Psi(A\tilde{x}+b).

Let
A^{*}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
denote the adjoint of
A
. Some straightforward calculations show that if
x=A\tilde{x}+b
then

\nabla\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})=A^{*}\nabla f(x)\text{ and }\tilde{f}^{*}(A^{*}u)=f^{*}(u)-\left\langle b,u\right\rangle\text{ for all }u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

and likewise for
\Psi
in lieu of
f
. Consequently, if
x=A\tilde{x}+b
then

\partial\Psi^{*}(-A^{*}\nabla f(x))=A\partial\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(-\nabla\tilde{f}(\tilde{x}))+b.

Hence if
x=A\tilde{x}+b
then for
g:=\nabla f(x)
and
\tilde{g}:=\nabla\tilde{f}(\tilde{x})
we have
\tilde{g}=A^{*}g
and
\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)=A\partial\Psi^{*}(-\tilde{g})+b
. Thus we can assume that the oracle in Assumption A2
yield respectively
s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)
and
\tilde{s}\in\partial\tilde{\Psi}^{*}(-\tilde{g})
that satisfy
s=A\tilde{s}+b
.
The identities
x=A\tilde{x}+b,\;\tilde{g}=A^{*}g,\;s=A\tilde{s}+b
in turn imply that

{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)=\widetilde{{\mathsf{gap}}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{g})

and

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)=\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{s},\theta)\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

It thus follows that the growth property (
10
), stepsize selection procedures (
9
) and (
16
), as well as Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
are all affine invariant.

3
Growth property in some special cases

The subsections below detail some straightforward consequences of Theorem
1
when the growth property holds for some special values of
(q,r)
. These special cases recover and sharpen some norm-dependent convergence results previously developed in the state-of-the-art literature on the conditional gradient method
[
14
,
25
,
10
]
. To make the latter connection more precise and explicit, Proposition
2
, Proposition
3
, and Proposition
4
below show that the uniform smoothness and uniform convexity assumptions previously made in each of
[
14
,
25
,
10
]
imply the
(q,r)
growth property (
10
) for various values of
(q,r)
.

We next recall the concepts of uniform smoothness and uniform convexity.
A detailed discussion on these concepts is presented in
[
13
]
. Both uniform smoothness and uniform convexity are stated and formalized in terms of some norm. Thus throughout this section we assume that
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
and let
\|\cdot\|^{*}
denote its dual norm.

A convex function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
is
uniformly smooth
on
C\subseteq{\mathrm{dom}}(f)
with respect to
\|\cdot\|
if there exist constants
q\in(1,2]
and
L>0
such that for
x,y\in C

f(x+\theta(y-x))\geq(1-\theta)f(x)+\theta f(y)-\frac{L}{q}\theta(1-\theta)\|y-x\|^{q}\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

(23)

It is customary to say that
f
is
L
-smooth on
C
when (
23
) holds with
q=2
. In particular,
uniform smoothness
is a relaxation of
smoothness.

It is easy to see that (
23
) holds for
q=\nu+1
when
\nabla f
is
\nu
-Hölder continuous on
C
with Hölder constant
L
. In particular,
f
is
L
-smooth on
C
when
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous on
C
with Lipschitz constant
L
.

As detailed in
[
13
]
, when
f
is differentiable, property (
23
) implies that for all
x,y\in C

D_{f}(y,x)\leq\frac{L}{q}\|y-x\|^{q}.

(24)

A function
\Psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{\infty\}
is
uniformly convex
on
C\subseteq{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
with respect to
\|\cdot\|
if there exist constants
p\geq 2
and
\mu>0
such that for all
x,y\in C
and
\theta\in[0,1]

\Psi(x+\theta(y-x))\leq(1-\theta)\Psi(x)+\theta\Psi(y)-\frac{\mu}{p}\theta(1-\theta)\|y-x\|^{p}\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

(25)

It is customary to say that
\Psi
is strongly convex on
C
when (
25
) holds for
p=2
. In particular,
uniform convexity
is a relaxation of
strong convexity.

Again, as detailed in
[
13
]
, property (
25
) implies that for all
x,y\in C
and
g\in\partial\Psi(y)

\Psi(x)\geq\Psi(y)+\left\langle g,s-y\right\rangle+\frac{\mu}{p}\|x-y\|^{p}.

In particular, property (
25
) implies that if
C={\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
and
s=\argmin_{y}\Psi(y)
then for all
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)

\Psi(x)-\Psi(s)\geq\frac{\mu}{p}\|x-s\|^{p}.

(26)

A closed convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is
uniformly convex
if there exist constants
p\geq 2
and
\mu>0
such that for all
x,y\in C,

\theta\in[0,1]
, and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with
\|z\|\leq 1

x+\theta(y-x)+\frac{\mu}{p}\theta(1-\theta)\|y-x\|^{p}z\in C.

(27)

It is customary to say that
C
is strongly convex when (
27
) holds for
p=2.

Similar to the above implications (
23
)
\Rightarrow
(
24
) and (
25
)
\Rightarrow
(
26
), property (
27
) implies that if
g\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
s=\argmin_{y\in C}\left\langle g,y\right\rangle
then for all
x\in C

\left\langle g,x-s\right\rangle\geq\frac{\mu}{p}\|g\|^{*}\cdot\|x-s\|^{p}.

(28)

3.1
The case
q>1,\;r=0
: sublinear convergence

Suppose the growth property (
10
) holds for
q>1
and
r=0,
that is, for
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi),s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-\nabla f(x))

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)\leq\frac{M\theta^{q}}{q}\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

(29)

In this case
{\mathsf{gap}}_{1}\leq M/q\leq M
and thus Theorem
1
implies that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\mathsf{gap}}_{1}^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}+\frac{1}{q}\cdot\frac{k-1}{M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\right)^{-(q-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{q^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}{M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}+\frac{1}{q}\cdot\frac{k-1}{M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\right)^{-(q-1)}

\displaystyle=M\left(\frac{q}{k-1+q^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}\right)^{q-1}.

(30)

The case
(q,r)=(2,0)
is particularly interesting. In this case we get

{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}\leq\frac{2M}{k+3}.

(31)

This bound applies in the especially important and popular case when
\Psi
is the indicator function
\delta_{C}
of some compact convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as discussed in
[
12
,
7
]
. In this case for
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi),s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-\nabla f(x))

we have

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)=D_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1]

and thus (
29
) holds with
q=2
and
M
equal to the following
curvature constant
defined by Jaggi
[
12
]
, provided that it is finite:

\mathcal{C}_{f}:=\sup_{x,s\in C\atop\theta\in(0,1]}\frac{2D_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)}{\theta^{2}}.

(32)

Therefore (
31
) recovers the iconic convergence rate of the conditional gradient method established in
[
12
]
. However, Theorem
1
reveals that the convergence could be faster as (
29
) may hold for
M\leq\mathcal{C}_{f}
.

The following proposition describes an important class of problems that satisfies (
29
). We rely on the following norm-dependent concept. Suppose
\|\cdot\|
is a norm in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is nonempty and bounded. The
diameter
of
C
is

\mathsf{diam}(C):=\sup_{x,y\in C}\|x-y\|.

Proposition 2
.

The
(q,0)
-growth property (
29
) holds if
f
is
q
-uniformly smooth and
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
is bounded. More precisely, the
(q,0)
-growth property (
29
) holds for
M=L\cdot\mathsf{diam}({\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi))^{q}
if there is a norm
\|\cdot\|
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
(
23
) holds for
x,y\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
.

Proof.

Suppose (
23
) holds for some constants
q>1
and
L>0
. Then (
7
) and (
24
) imply that for all
x,s\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
and
\theta\in[0,1]

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)\leq D_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)\leq\frac{L}{q}\|\theta(s-x)\|^{q}=\frac{L\|s-x\|^{q}}{q}\cdot\theta^{q}.

Thus (
29
) holds for
M=L\cdot\mathsf{diam}({\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi))^{q}
.
∎

Proposition
2
and (
3.1
) readily yield the following norm-dependent convergence result for a class of problems previously considered in
[
10
,
25
]
. Suppose
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
is bounded and
\nabla f
is
\nu
-Hölder continuous on
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
for some
\nu>0
. In this case

is
(\nu+1)
-uniformly smooth on
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
. Thus Proposition

implies that the
(q,0)
-growth property (

) holds for

and

where

is the Hölder continuity constant of

. Therefore (

) implies that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq L\cdot\mathsf{diam}({\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi))^{1+\nu}\left(\frac{\nu+1}{k-1+(\nu+1)^{\frac{\nu+1}{\nu}}}\right)^{\nu}

\displaystyle\leq L\cdot\mathsf{diam}({\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi))^{1+\nu}\left(\frac{\nu+1}{k}\right)^{\nu}.

(33)

Therefore we recover the
{\mathcal{O}}(1/k^{\nu})
convergence results shown in
[

, Corollary 1]
and in
[
10
, Corollary 1]
with an improvement on the latter as (

) does not include the additional log factor in
[

, Corollary 1 and Equation (2.30)]
. The constant in front of

in (

) is simpler and sharper than the constants in
[

, Corollary 1]
and in
[
10
, Equation (2.30)]
. Furthermore, Theorem
1
reveals that the convergence is generally faster
as Proposition

implies that (

) and (
3.1
) will typically hold for
M\leq L\cdot\mathsf{diam}({\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi))^{1+\nu}
.

3.2
The case
(q,r)=(2,1)
: linear convergence

Suppose the growth property (
10
) holds for

that is, for

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)\leq\frac{M\theta^{2}}{2}\cdot{{\mathsf{gap}}}(x,g)\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

In this case Theorem

readily implies that

{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}\leq{\mathsf{gap}}_{0}\cdot\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{M}\right\}\right)^{k}.

(35)

Proposition
3
below describes some important classes of problems that satisfies (

). We omit the proof of Proposition

as it is a special case of the more general Proposition

in Section

below.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose

is
2
-uniformly smooth on
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
.
Then the
(2,1)
-growth property (
34
) holds if
\Psi
is
2
-uniformly convex on
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
, or if

for a

. More precisely:

(a)

in

) and (

) and (

3.3
Other values
(q,r)
: sublinear to linear convergence

Suppose the growth property (
10
) holds for some
q>1,\;r\in(0,1),
and
M>0
. We next discuss the spectrum of convergence rates from sublinear to linear that Theorem
1
yields depending on the values of
(q,r)
. To simplify notation and exposition, we shall assume that
{\mathsf{gap}}_{0}\leq M
throughout this subsection so that we skip the initial linear convergence regime (
14
) in Theorem
1
. This assumption can be relaxed either at the expense of some notational overhead to deal with the initial linear convergence regime (
14
), or at the expense of artificially increasing the constant
M
.

Suppose
{\mathsf{gap}}_{0}\leq M
. Then for
r\in[0,1)
Theorem
1
implies that

{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}\leq\left(\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1-r}{q-1}}}+\frac{1-r}{q}\cdot\frac{k}{M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{r-1}}=M\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q\cdot M^{\frac{r}{q-1}}}\cdot k\cdot\frac{1-r}{q-1}\right)^{-\frac{q-1}{1-r}}.

(36)

For
q>1
fixed, the right-most expression in (

) decreases monotonically with
r
from the sublinear rate

M\left(\frac{q}{k+q}\right)^{q-1}

for
r=0

to the following asymptotic linear rate for
r\uparrow 1

Me^{-\rho k}=M\left(e^{-\rho}\right)^{k},

(37)

where

\rho:=\frac{q-1}{q\cdot M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\in(0,1).

Since
e^{-\rho}\approx 1-\rho
for small
\rho>0
, it follows that the asymptotic linear rate (
37
) is approximately the same linear rate given by Theorem

when

, namely

{\mathsf{gap}}_{k}\leq{\mathsf{gap}}_{0}\left(1-\frac{q-1}{q\cdot M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\right)^{k}.

The following general version of Proposition
3
describes a broad class of problems that satisfies the
(q,r)
-growth property for various values of
(q,r)
depending on the degree of uniform smoothness and uniform convexity of

and
\Psi
.

Proposition 4
.

Suppose
f
is
q
-uniformly smooth on
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
.
Then the
(q,r)
-growth property (
10
) holds for

if
\Psi
is
p
-uniformly convex on
{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)
, or if
\Psi=\delta_{C}
for a
p
-uniformly convex and closed set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\nabla f

, and

\ell:=\inf_{x\in C}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{*}>0.

Proof.

(a)

(38)

Thus proceeding as in part (a), (

∎

.

4
Weak growth property and suboptimality gap

Consider the setting of Section
2
.
Define the
suboptimality gap
function
{\mathsf{subopt}}:{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
as follows

{\mathsf{subopt}}(x):=f(x)+\Psi(x)-\min_{y}\left\{f(y)+\Psi(y)\right\}.

The weak duality property between (
1
) and its dual (
3
) implies that for all
(x,u)\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi)\times{\mathrm{dom}}(f^{*})

{\mathsf{gap}}(x,u)\geq{\mathsf{subopt}}(x).

Therefore any upper bound on the duality gap automatically holds for the suboptimality gap as well. This section takes this observation further: Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
provide analogous statements to
Theorem
1
and Theorem
2

for the suboptimality gap by relying on the following relaxed version of (
10
). Suppose
q>1
and
r\in[0,1]
. We shall say that the triple
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathsf{gap}},{\mathsf{subopt}})
satisfies the
(q,r)
weak growth property
if there exists a finite constant
M>0
such that for all
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi),\,g:=\nabla f(x),
and
s\in\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{1-r}\leq\frac{M\theta^{q}}{q}\cdot{{\mathsf{gap}}}(x,g)\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

(40)

Since
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)\geq{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)\geq 0
, it is immediate that the weak growth property (
40
) holds whenever the growth property (
10
) holds.

The next two results concern the sequence of suboptimality gaps

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}:={\mathsf{subopt}}(x_{k}),\;k=0,1,\dots

for the iterates generated by Algorithm
1
. We omit the proofs of Theorem
3
and Theorem
4
below as they are straightforward extensions of the proofs of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose Algorithm
1
chooses
\theta_{k}\in[0,1]
via (
9
) and
q>1
and
r\in[0,1]
are such that

({\mathcal{D}},{\mathsf{gap}},{\mathsf{subopt}})
satisfy the
(q,r)
weak growth property (
40
) for some finite
M>0
.
Then for
k=0,1,\dots

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k+1}\leq{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\left(1-\frac{q-1}{q}\cdot\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}^{1-r}}{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\right\}\right).

When
r=1
we have linear convergence

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\leq{\mathsf{subopt}}_{0}\left(1-\frac{q-1}{q}\cdot\min\left\{1,\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\right\}\right)^{k}.

When
r\in[0,1)
we have an initial linear convergence regime

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\leq{\mathsf{subopt}}_{0}\left(1-\frac{q-1}{q}\right)^{k},\;k=0,1,2,\dots,k_{0}

where
k_{0}
is the smallest
k
such that

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}^{1-r}\leq M
. Then for
k\geq k_{0}
we have a sublinear convergence regime

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\leq\left({\mathsf{subopt}}_{k_{0}}^{\frac{r-1}{q-1}}+\frac{1-r}{q}\cdot\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}\cdot(k-k_{0})\right)^{\frac{q-1}{r-1}}.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
c,\rho\in(0,1)
are such that
c+\rho>1
and Algorithm
1
chooses
\theta_{k}\in[0,1]
so that
\rho\cdot\hat{\theta}_{k}\leq\theta_{k}\leq\hat{\theta}_{k}
for
\hat{\theta}_{k}
as in (
16
).
Suppose
q>1
and
r\in[0,1]
are such that

({\mathcal{D}},{\mathsf{gap}},{\mathsf{subopt}})
satisfy the
(q,r)
weak growth property (
40
) for some finite
M>0
.
Then for
k=0,1,\dots

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k+1}\leq{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\left(1-(c+\rho-1)\cdot\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{q(1-c)}{M}\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}^{1-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\right\}\right).

When
r=1
we have linear convergence

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\leq{\mathsf{subopt}}_{0}\left(1-(c+\rho-1)\cdot\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{q(1-c)}{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\right\}\right)^{k}.

When
r\in[0,1)
we have an initial linear convergence regime

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\leq{\mathsf{subopt}}_{0}\left(1-(c+\rho-1)\right)^{k},\;k=0,1,2,\dots,k_{0}

where
k_{0}
is the smallest
k
such that
{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}^{1-r}\leq\frac{M}{q(1-c)}
. Subsequently for
k\geq k_{0}
we have a sublinear convergence regime

{\mathsf{subopt}}_{k}\leq\left({\mathsf{subopt}}_{k_{0}}^{\frac{r-1}{q-1}}+\frac{(1-r)(c+\rho-1)}{q-1}\cdot\left(\frac{q(1-c)}{M}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\cdot(k-k_{0})\right)^{\frac{q-1}{r-1}}.

Proposition
5
below provides a nice complement to Proposition
4
(b). Proposition
5
describes a class of problems that
satisfies the weak growth property similar to that in Proposition
4
(b). However, instead of assuming that
\nabla f
is bounded away from zero in
C
, Proposition
5
relies on the following type of Hölderian error bound as in
[
14
,
31
]
. Suppose
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a closed convex set,
f^{\star}:=\min_{x\in C}f(x)
is finite,
X^{\star}:=\{x\in C:f(x)=f^{\star}\}
is nonempty. Suppose
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
and
\gamma\in[0,1]
.
We shall say that
f
satisfies the
\gamma
-Hölderian error bound
on
C
if there exists a finite constant
K>0
such that for all
x\in C

\min_{y\in X^{\star}}\|x-y\|\leq K\cdot(f(x)-f^{\star})^{\gamma}.

(41)

The Hölderian error bound (
41
) is known to hold generically when
C
is a compact set and
f
is subanalytic, as established by Łojasiewicz
[
21
]
, and further developed by Kurdyka
[
18
]
and Bolte et al.
[
3
]
.

Proposition 5
.

The
(q,r)
weak growth property (
40
) holds for
r=\gamma q/p
if

\Psi=\delta_{C}
for some closed and
p
-uniformly convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
f
is
q
-uniformly smooth on
C
, and the
\gamma
-Hölderian error bound (
41
) holds for
\gamma\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Suppose (
23
) holds for some constants
q\in(1,2]
and
L>0
, (
27
) holds for some constants
p\geq 2
and
\mu>0
, and (
41
) holds for some constants
\gamma\in[0,1]
and
K>0
. Then (
28
) implies that for all
x\in C
and
g:=\nabla f(x)
we have

{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)=\left\langle g,x-s\right\rangle\geq\frac{\mu}{p}\|g\|^{*}\cdot\|x-s\|^{p}.

(42)

Next, the convexity of
f
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and (
41
) imply that for
x^{\star}:=\argmin_{y\in X^{\star}}\|x-y\|

{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)=f(x)-f(x^{\star})\leq\left\langle g,x-x^{\star}\right\rangle\leq\|g\|^{*}\cdot\|x-x^{\star}\|\leq K\cdot\|g\|^{*}\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{\gamma}.

(43)

From (
42
) and (
43
) it follows that

\|x-s\|^{p}\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{1-\gamma}\leq\frac{pK}{\mu}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g).

The latter inequality, (
24
), and
{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)\leq{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)
imply that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{1-\frac{\gamma q}{p}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{D}}_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{1-\frac{\gamma q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L}{q}\cdot\theta^{q}\cdot\|s-x\|^{q}\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{\frac{(1-\gamma)q}{p}}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{q}\cdot\theta^{q}\cdot\left(\|s-x\|^{p}\cdot{\mathsf{subopt}}(x)^{1-\gamma}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq L\cdot\left(\frac{pK}{\mu}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\cdot\frac{\theta^{q}}{q}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g).

Therefore (
40
) holds for
r=\gamma q/p
and

M=L\cdot\left(pK/\mu\right)^{q/p}.

∎

5
Local growth property

We next describe an extension of the main developments in Section
2

to a
local
setting. The same extension applies to the developments in Section
4
.

Consider the setting of Section
2
. Suppose
q>1
and
r\in[0,1]
. We shall say that the pair
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathsf{gap}})
satisfies the
(q,r)

local growth property
if there exist constants
\epsilon>0
and
M>0
such that for all
x\in{\mathrm{dom}}(\Psi),\,g:=\nabla f(x),
and
s:=\partial\Psi^{*}(-g)
that satisfy
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)<\epsilon
we have

{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)\leq\frac{M\theta^{q}}{q}\cdot{{\mathsf{gap}}}(x,g)^{r}\text{ for all }\theta\in[0,1].

(44)

It is straightforward to see that local versions of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
also hold if we replace the growth property (
10
) with the local growth property (
44
) provided
{\mathsf{gap}}_{0}={\mathsf{gap}}(x_{0},g_{0})<\epsilon
. It is also evident that the local growth property (
44
) is less stringent than the growth property (
10
). In particular, the natural local version of Proposition
4
holds if the uniform smoothness and uniform convexity assumptions hold on a neighborhood of an optimal solution to (
1
). The following proposition, which is a straightforward extension of some arguments in
[
14
]
,
describes another class of problems that satisfy the local growth condition (
44
). Proposition
6
provides a counterpart to Proposition
4
(b) that relaxes the assumption of
p
-uniform convexity of
C
and boundedness away from zero of
\nabla f
on
C
to the far less stringent local scaling inequality (
45
) that only involves
\nabla f(x^{\star})
where
x^{\star}\in C
is a minimizer of (
1
). This relaxed assumption comes at the expense of downgrading the growth condition for
r=q/p
to the local growth condition for
r=q(q-1)/(p(p-1))
. As noted in
[
14
, Remark 2.6]
, in the special case when
q=p=2
there is no downgrade in the value of
r
. For all other values of
q\in(1,2]
and
p\geq 2
the downgrade factor
(q-1)/(p-1)
on
r
is the price we pay for relaxing the uniform convexity and boundedness assumptions to the less stringent local scaling assumption.

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
\Psi=\delta_{C}
for a closed convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
x^{\star}\in C
is a minimizer of (
1
),
\nu\in(0,1]
, and
p\geq 2
. The local growth property (
44
) holds for
\epsilon=1,\;q=1+\nu,
and
r=q(q-1)/(p(p-1))
if
\nabla f
is
\nu
-Hölder continuous
on
C
and there exists
\sigma>0
such that the following local scaling inequality holds for all
x\in C

\left\langle\nabla f(x^{\star}),x-x^{\star}\right\rangle\geq\sigma\cdot\|x-x^{\star}\|^{p}.

(45)

Proof.

From (
45
) and the convexity of
f
it follows that for
x\in C
and
g=\nabla f(x)

{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)\geq f(x)-f(x^{\star})\geq\left\langle\nabla f(x^{\star}),x^{\star}-x\right\rangle\geq\sigma\cdot\|x-x^{\star}\|^{p}.

(46)

In addition, for
s=\argmin_{y\in C}\left\langle g,y\right\rangle=\argmin_{y\in C}\left\langle\nabla f(x),y\right\rangle
the local scaling inequality (
45
) implies that

\left\langle\nabla f(x^{\star})-\nabla f(x),s-x^{\star}\right\rangle\geq\left\langle\nabla f(x^{\star}),s-x^{\star}\right\rangle\geq\sigma\cdot\|s-x^{\star}\|^{p}.

(47)

Thus the
\nu
-Hölder continuity of
\nabla f
and Hölder’s inequality imply that for some finite Hölder continuity constant
L>0

L\|x^{\star}-x\|^{\nu}\cdot\|s-x^{\star}\|\geq\sigma\cdot\|s-x^{\star}\|^{p}.

Hence
\sigma\cdot\|s-x^{\star}\|^{p-1}\leq L\|x^{\star}-x\|^{\nu}
and thus (
46
) yields

{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)\geq\sigma\cdot\|x-x^{\star}\|^{p}\geq\sigma\cdot\left(\frac{\sigma}{L}\right)^{\frac{p}{\nu}}\|s-x^{\star}\|^{\frac{p(p-1)}{\nu}}

(48)

Let
\epsilon:=1
. From (
46
) and (
48
) it follows that for
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)<\epsilon

\displaystyle\|s-x\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{\star}-x\|+\|s-x^{\star}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}}\cdot\left(\frac{L}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}}\cdot\left(\frac{L}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}.

The last step above holds because
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)<\epsilon=1
and
\nu/(p-1)\in(0,1]
imply that
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}
.
Therefore (
24
) implies that for
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)<\epsilon

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}(x,s,\theta)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{D}}_{f}(x+\theta(s-x),x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L\theta^{1+\nu}}{1+\nu}\cdot\|s-x\|^{1+\nu}

\displaystyle\leq L\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}}\cdot\left(\frac{L}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{1+\nu}\cdot\frac{\theta^{1+\nu}}{1+\nu}\cdot{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)^{\frac{\nu(1+\nu)}{p(p-1)}}.

Therefore whenever
{\mathsf{gap}}(x,g)<\epsilon
the local growth property (
44
) holds for
q=1+\nu
and
r=q(q-1)/(p(p-1))
with

M=L\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{\frac{\nu}{p(p-1)}}}\cdot\left(\frac{L}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{1+\nu}.

∎

ABSTRACT

Zero Distribution of Generated Taylor Polynomials

Our goal in this paper is to study the zero distribution of a sequence of polynomials whose coefficients satisfy a three-term recurrence. Equivalently, these polynomials are Taylor polynomials of a rational function with a polynomial denominator of degree 2. We will use complex analysis to find the bi-variate generating function for that sequence of Taylor polynomials. With this generating function, we prove that the zeros of these Taylor polynomials lie on one side of the closed disk centered at the origin. The radius of the disk is exactly the reciprocal of the modulus of the smaller zero of the degree two denominator.
Finally, we show that the zeros of these Taylor polynomials approach the boundary of the disk above.

10

Lemma 19

Lemma 20

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}G_{m}(z)t^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+t+zt^{2}}

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k}.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right\}
be arbitrary. Using partial fractions, we solve for
A
,
B
, and
C
from the equation below

\frac{1}{a(1-zt)(t_{1}-t)(t_{2}-t)}=\frac{A}{t_{1}-t}+\frac{B}{t_{2}-t}+\frac{C}{1-zt},

which yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(t_{1}-t)(t_{2}-t)(1-zt)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A}{t_{1}-t}+\frac{B}{t_{2}-t}+\frac{C}{1-zt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A(t_{2}-t)(1-zt)+B(t_{1}-t)(1-zt)+C(t_{1}-t)(t_{2}-t)}{(t_{1}-t)(t_{2}-t)(1-zt)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A[zt^{2}-(zt_{2}+1)t+t_{2}]+B[zt^{2}-(zt_{1}+1)t+t_{1}]+C[t^{2}-(t_{1}+t_{2})t+t_{1}t_{2}]}{(t_{1}-t)(t_{2}-t)(1-zt)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(Az+Bz+C)t^{2}+[-A(zt_{2}+1)-B(zt_{1}+1)-(t_{1}+t_{2})C]t+At_{2}+Bt_{1}+Ct_{1}t_{2}}{(t_{1}-t)(t_{2}-t)(1-zt)}.

This yields the following 3 equations:

(0.27)

\displaystyle Az+Bz+C

\displaystyle=0,

(0.28)

\displaystyle A(zt_{2}+1)+B(zt_{1}+1)+(t_{1}+t_{2})C

\displaystyle=0,

(0.29)

\displaystyle At_{2}+Bt_{1}+Ct_{1}t_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}.

If we multiply Equation (
0.27
) by
t_{1}+t_{2}
and subtract Equation (
0.28
) from it, we get

(0.30)

A(t_{1}z-1)+B(t_{2}z-1)=0.

If we multiply Equation (
0.27
) by
t_{1}t_{2}
and subtract Equation (
0.29
) from it, we get

(0.31)

A(t_{1}t_{2}z-t_{2})+B(t_{1}t_{2}z-t_{1})=-\frac{1}{a}.

Multiplying Equation (
0.30
) by
t_{2}
and subtracting Equation (
0.31
) from it yields

(0.32)

B(t_{2}^{2}z-t_{2}-t_{1}t_{2}z+t_{1})=\frac{1}{a}.

Observe that

\displaystyle t_{2}^{2}z-t_{2}-t_{1}t_{2}z+t_{1}

\displaystyle=t_{2}(t_{2}z-1)-t_{1}(t_{2}z-1)

\displaystyle=(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1).

Then we can rewrite Equation (
0.32
) as

B(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1)=\frac{1}{a}.

It follows that

B=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1)}.

Also, Equation (
0.30
) induces

A=-B\frac{t_{2}z-1}{t_{1}z-1}=-\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1)}\frac{t_{2}z-1}{t_{1}z-1}=-\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)}.

On the other hand, Equation (
0.27
) yields that

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=-Az-Bz

\displaystyle=-\left(-\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)}\right)z-\left(\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1)}\right)z

\displaystyle=\frac{(t_{2}z-1)-(t_{1}z-1)}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)}z

\displaystyle=\frac{z^{2}}{a(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)}.

Hence we have shown that

(0.33)

\displaystyle A=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)}

(0.34)

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1)}

(0.35)

\displaystyle C=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{2}}{a(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)}.

Meanwhile, recall that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)t^{m}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})(1-tz)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A}{t_{1}-t}+\frac{B}{t_{2}-t}+\frac{C}{1-zt}.

Observe that, for
t<\min(|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|,|z|)
, we can apply geometric series as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{A}{t_{1}-t}+\frac{B}{t_{2}-t}+\frac{C}{1-zt}=

\displaystyle A\frac{1}{t_{1}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{t_{1}}}+B\frac{1}{t_{2}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{t_{2}}}+C\frac{1}{1-zt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+B\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+C\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}z^{m}.

Moewover, Theorem
4
implies that

A\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+B\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+C\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}z^{m}

is absolutely convergent for
t\in B(\min(|t_{1}|,|t_{2}|,|z|);0)
.
Then Proposition
8
induces that

\displaystyle A\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+B\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+C\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}z^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}\left(\frac{A}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+\frac{B}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+Cz^{m}\right).

Consequently, if we denote
f(t)={\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})(1-zt)}}
, by Proposition
9
, we may deduce that

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)t^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}f^{(m)}(0)t^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}\left(\frac{A}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+\frac{B}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+Cz^{m}\right).

This implies that

P_{m}(z)=\frac{A}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+\frac{B}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+Cz^{m}.

In view of (
0.33
), (
0.34
), (
0.35
), we can observe that

\displaystyle P_{m}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{A}{t_{1}^{m+1}}+\frac{B}{t_{2}^{m+1}}+Cz^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)}\frac{1}{t_{1}^{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{2}z-1)}\frac{1}{t_{2}^{m+1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{z^{2}}{a(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)}z^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-(t_{2}z-1)t_{2}^{m+1}+(t_{1}z-1)t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2})z+(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}.

Then, by Proposition
27
,

\displaystyle P_{m}(z)=

\displaystyle\frac{t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2})z+(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k}.\qed

Here we found how
P_{m}(z)
is defined except at the points
\left\{0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right\}
.
We can find
P_{m}(z)
for
z=0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}
with the following theorem.

Theorem 29

(Corollary 3.8,
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be an open connected set.
If
f
and
g
are analytic on
G
then
f\equiv g
iff
\{z\in G;f(z)=g(z)\}
has a limit point in
G
.

Now we will use Theorem
29
to prove that

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k},

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Lemma 30
.

Let
a,t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
where
at_{1}t_{2}\neq 0
and
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
.
Let
\{P_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
be a sequence of functions of
z
generated as follows:

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)t^{m}=\frac{1}{a(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})(1-zt)}.

Then, for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k}.

Proof.

From Lemma
28
, we obtain that

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k},

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right\}
.
Meanwhile, given that
at_{1}t_{2}\neq 0
, we may rewrite

\frac{1}{a(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})(1-zt)}=\frac{1}{(at^{2}-(t_{1}+t_{2})t+t_{1}t_{2})(1-zt)}

where
t_{1}t_{2}\neq 0
.
Then, by Lemma
26
,
P_{m}(z)
is a polynomial and thus entire.
Notice that

\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k}

is a polynomial and thus entire. We have that

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k},

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right\}.

Thus
\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right\}
is contained in

\left\{z\in\mathbb{C};P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k}\right\}.

Notice that
\mathbb{C}\backslash\left\{0,\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right\}
has a limit point, and thus, by Theorem
29
,

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k},

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

Now we have explicit form of
P_{m}(z)
and we may deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 31
.

Suppose
ac\neq 0
and
b^{2}-4ac\neq 0
, and
r\neq 0
. Let
\left\{P_{m}(z)\right\}_{m\geq 0}

and
\left\{H_{m}(z)\right\}_{m\geq 0}
be two sequences
of polynomials defined by their generating functions:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)t^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(at^{2}+bt+c)(1-tz)},

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}H_{m}(z)t^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left[a\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)^{2}+b\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)+c\right]\left[1-tz\right]}.

Then it holds that

H_{m}(z)=\frac{P_{m}(rz)}{r^{m}}.

Proof.

Let us denote the zeros of
at^{2}+bt+c
by
t_{1},t_{2}
.
We may deduce that
rt_{1},rt_{2}
are the zeros of
\frac{a}{r^{2}}t^{2}+\frac{b}{r}t+c
.
Then Lemma
30
induces that

P_{m}(z)=\frac{1}{a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}(t_{2}^{m+1-k}-t_{1}^{m+1-k})t_{1}^{k}t_{2}^{k}z^{k}.

On the other hand, observe that

\frac{1}{\left[a\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)^{2}+b\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)+c\right]\left[1-\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)\left(rz\right)\right]}=\frac{1}{\left[\frac{a}{r^{2}}t^{2}+\frac{b}{r}t+c\right]\left[1-zt\right]}

With Lemma
30
, we can infer that
H_{m}(z)
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{a}{r^{2}}(rt_{2}-rt_{1})r^{m+1}t_{1}^{m+1}r^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}({t_{2}}^{m+1-k}-{t_{1}}^{m+1-k})r^{m+1-k}{(rt_{1})}^{k}{(rt_{2})}^{k}z^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{m}a(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}({t_{2}}^{m+1-k}-{t_{1}}^{m+1-k}){t_{1}}^{k}{t_{2}}^{k}(rz)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{m}(rz)}{r^{m}}

This concludes that

H_{m}(z)=\frac{P_{m}(rz)}{r^{m}}.\qed

With this corollary, we may trace the zeros with the simpler generating function as described in the following corollary.

Corollary 32
.

Suppose
ac\neq 0
. Let
\left\{P_{m}(z)\right\}_{m\geq 0}

and
\left\{H_{m}(z)\right\}_{m\geq 0}
be two sequences
of polynomials defined by their generating functions:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)t^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(at^{2}+bt+c)(1-tz)},

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}H_{m}(z)t^{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(\frac{ac}{|ac|}t^{2}+\frac{b}{\sqrt{|ac|}}t+1\right)(1-tz)}.

Then each zero of
P_{m}(z)
is a zero of
H_{m}(z)
multiplied by
\left|\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}\right|
.

Proof.

If we consider
t_{1},t_{2}
, the zeros of
at^{2}+bt+c
, then
at^{2}+bt+c=a(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})
.
Given that
ac\neq 0
, we obtain
c\neq 0
.
Thus we may divide both sides by
c
, and it yields the following:

\frac{a}{c}(t-t_{1})(t-t_{2})=\frac{1}{c}(at^{2}+bt+c)=\frac{a}{c}t^{2}+\frac{b}{c}t+1.

This implies that
\frac{a}{c}t^{2}+\frac{b}{c}t+1
has the same zeros as
at^{2}+bt+c
.
Now let
\{G_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
be a sequence of functions of
z
generated as follows:

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}G_{m}(z)t^{m}=\frac{1}{\left(\frac{a}{c}t^{2}+\frac{b}{c}t+1\right)(1-tz)}.

By Lemma
30
, we may deduce that

(0.36)

P_{m}(z)=\frac{G_{m}(z)}{c}.

Recall that
\{H_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
is a sequence of functions of
z
generated as follows:

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}H_{m}(z)t^{m}=\frac{1}{\left(\frac{a}{c}\left(\frac{t}{\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}}\right)^{2}+\frac{b}{c}\frac{t}{\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}}+1\right)(1-tz)}.

Then Corollary
31
induces that

(0.37)

H_{m}(z)=\frac{G_{m}\left(\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}z\right)}{\left(\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}\right)^{m}},

and Equations (
0.36
) and (
0.37
) yield that

P_{m}(z)=\frac{G_{m}(z)}{c}=\frac{1}{c}\left(\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}\right)^{m}H_{m}\left(\frac{z}{\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}}\right)

So, each zero of
P_{m}(z)
is a zero of
H_{m}(z)
multiplied by
\frac{\sqrt{|ac|}}{c}
.
∎

ZERO DISTRIBUTION OF
\{P_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
ABOUT A CIRCLE

In this section, we will prove two of our main theorems.
Let us have a look on some examples.

Zeros inside of the corresponding circles

0.2
shows the zeros of polynomials generated by

\frac{1}{(t^{2}+1t-2)(1-tz)}\text{ and }\frac{1}{(t^{2}+5t+6)(1-tz)}\text{ and }\frac{1}{(t^{2}+t+2)(1-tz)}

respectively.

.
Notice that the roots of

and
-2
.

.

-2
and
-3
.

.

.

Let us first prove a theorem about the zeros inside the circle.

Theorem 33
.

Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
ac<0
and
b\neq 0
.
Let
\{P_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
be a sequence of functions of
z
generated as follows:

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P_{m}(z)t^{m}=\frac{1}{(at^{2}+bt+c)(1-tz)}.

Then all the zeros of
P_{m}(z)
lie on the closed disk centered at 0 with radius
1/|\alpha|
on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
where
\alpha
is the smallest (in modulus) zero of
at^{2}+bt+c
.

Proof.

We will consider
\{H_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
, the sequence of functions of
z
generated as follows;

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}H_{m}(z)t^{m}=\frac{1}{\left(-t^{2}+\frac{b}{\sqrt{-ac}}t+1\right)(1-tz)}.

Using the Quadratic Formula, we may deduce that the zeros of
-t^{2}+\frac{b}{\sqrt{-ac}}t+1
are (note that
b^{2}-4ac>0
since
ac<0
)

\frac{-\frac{b}{\sqrt{-ac}}\pm\sqrt{-\frac{b^{2}}{ac}+4}}{-2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-ac}}\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{-2}.

Let us denote those zeros by
t_{1},t_{2}
where
|t_{1}|\leq|t_{2}|
.
Let
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be arbitrary.

Now we will prove that all the roots of
H_{m}(z)
lie on the closed disk
G
centered at 0 with radius
1/|t_{1}|
.
Here the proof of Lemma
28
may be applied to induce that

H_{m}(z)=\frac{t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2})z+(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}}{-(t_{2}-t_{1})(t_{1}z-1)(t_{2}z-1)t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}}.

Our goal is to use Rouché’s Theorem to show that all the zeros of the numerator
t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2})z+(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}
are on the closed disk
|z|\leq 1/|t_{1}|
.

For the rest of the proof, we shall use the following notation:

\displaystyle p(z)

\displaystyle=-(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}

\displaystyle q(z)

\displaystyle=t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2})z+(t_{2}-t_{1})t_{1}^{m+1}t_{2}^{m+1}z^{m+2}.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary.
Let
z
be arbitrary such that
|z|=1/|t_{1}|+\epsilon
.
Notice that there are 2 cases: either
m
is even or odd.
We will prove that

|p(z)+q(z)|\leq|t_{2}|^{m+1}+\frac{|t_{2}|^{m+2}}{|t_{1}|}+(|t_{2}|^{m+2}+|t_{1}||t_{2}|^{m+1})\cdot\epsilon

in both cases.

1.

m
is even

By Vieta’s Formula,
t_{1}t_{2}=-1
.
Then one of
t_{1},t_{2}
is negative.
If
t_{1}<0
, given
m
is odd, then
t_{1}^{m+1}>0
.
Hence it implies that

|t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}|=t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}=|t_{2}^{m+1}|-|t_{1}^{m+1}|.

Likewise, if
t_{2}<0
, given
m
is even,
t_{2}^{m+1}>0
, and consequently, we may deduce that

|t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}|=t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}=|t_{2}^{m+1}|+|t_{1}^{m+1}|.

In either cases
t_{1}>0
, or
t_{1}<0
, we obtain

(0.38)

|t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}|=|t_{2}^{m+1}|+|t_{1}^{m+1}|.

Similarly, if
t_{1}<0
, then, given
m
is even,
t_{1}^{m+2}>0
, and consequently, we may deduce that

|t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2}|=t_{2}^{m+2}-t_{1}^{m+2}=|t_{2}^{m+2}|-|t_{1}^{m+2}|.

Likewise, if
t_{2}<0
, given
m
is even,
t_{2}^{m+2}>0
, and consequently, we may deduce that

|t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2}|=t_{2}^{m+2}-t_{1}^{m+2}=|t_{2}^{m+2}|-|t_{1}^{m+2}|.

Considering these conditions, we obtain, in either cases
t_{1}>0
, or
t_{1}<0
, that

(0.39)

|t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2}|=|t_{2}^{m+2}|-|t_{1}^{m+2}|.

By sub-additivity,

\displaystyle|p(z)+q(z)|=

\displaystyle|t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}+(t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2})z|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t_{2}^{m+1}-t_{1}^{m+1}|+|t_{1}^{m+2}-t_{2}^{m+2}|\cdot|z|

\displaystyle=

CONCLUSIONS

For a long time, many mathematicians studied the pattern of zeros of functions.
This area of research is still active nowadays.
In this paper, we reviewed what has been studied in the area and made our own contribution to this subject.
In the development of our theorems, we use the classical Rouché’s Theorem, Eneström-Kakeya’s Theorem, and then study the limiting behaviors of the zeros of our sequence of polynomials.

Our main theorems were about the generating function

\frac{1}{(at^{2}+bt+c)(1-zt)}

and a circle centered at the origin with radius
\frac{1}{|\alpha|}
where
\alpha
is a zero of
at^{2}+bt+c
with smallest modulus.
When we were working with Rouché’s Theorem, we found that, when
ac<0
, our zeros lied inside a closed disk, and when
ac>0
, the zeros lied outside an open ball.
Using Eneström-Kakeya Theorem, we prove that, when
ac>0
, the zeros are outside of the closed disk.
However, there is a problem in applying Eneström-Kakeya Theorem for the case
ac<0
.
For any
m\geq 0
, the ratio of the coefficient of
z^{m-2}
to the coefficient of
z^{m-1}
is always greater than
1/|\alpha|
.

In this paper we cover the case
at^{2}+bt+c
having real zeros with different modulus.
In begining of Section ‘Zero Distribution of
\{P_{m}(z)\}_{m\geq 0}
about a Circle’, we saw that zeros are both inside and outside of the circle when
at^{2}+bt+c
has complex zeros.
With Rouché’s Theorem, we might be able to obtain the ratio of the number of zeros inside to the number of zeros outside.
Or we might be able to count exact number of zeros inside and outside of the circle.

In Section ‘Application of Limit Distribution of Zeros’, we only cover the case
at^{2}+bt+c
has real zeros.
An intuition is saying we maybe able to handle the case of imaginary zeros.

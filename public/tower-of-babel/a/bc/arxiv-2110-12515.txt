Abstract
Functional evolution equations are used in the modeling of numerous physical processes. In this work, our main tool is perturbation theory of strongly continuous semigroups. The advantage of this technique is that one can provide functional evolution equations with the explicit representation formulas of the solution. First, we introduce a closed form of the fundamental solution of the evolution equation with a discrete delay using the delayed Dyson-Phillips series. Then we set up the analytical representation formulas of the classical solutions of linear homogeneous/non-homogeneous evolution equations with a constant delay in a Banach space. In the special case, when a strongly continuous group { 𝒯 ⁡ ( t ) } t ∈ ℝ \left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}} commutes with a bounded linear operator A 1 A_{1} , we obtain an elegant formula for the fundamental solution using the powers of the resolvent operator of A 0 A_{0} . Furthermore, we consider delay evolution equations with permutable/non-permutable linear bounded operators and derive crucial results in terms of non-commutative analysis. Finally, we present an example, in the context of a one-dimensional heat equation with a discrete delay to demonstrate the applicability of our theoretical results and give some comparisons with existing results.



Keywords:

Delay evolution equation; perturbation theory; a discrete delay; a delayed Dyson-Phillips series; heat equation with delay

Furthermore, in

In the general case,

can be written in an

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u(t)=A_{0}u(t)+A_{1}u_{t},\quad t\geq 0,\\

In this work, our main tool is the

The paper includes significant updates in the

and is structured as follows. Section

In Section

Finally, Section

Some notations

Let us fix some notations. Let

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
,
\mathbb{R}
and

be the set of real and complex numbers, respectively,
[-\tau,T]=[-\tau,0]\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{n}(i\tau,(i+1)\tau]\subset\mathbb{R}
and
[0,T]=\left\{0\right\}\cup\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{n}(i\tau,(i+1)\tau]\subset\mathbb{R}
where

In the representation of

operator-valued functions, we will use the following

\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\left\{0,1\right\}

\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}=\begin{cases}1,\quad t\geq n\tau,\\
0,\quad t<n\tau,\end{cases}

where

and
\tau>0
.

Alternatively, for the

operator-valued functions, we will also use the

\left(t\right)_{+}=\max\left(t,0\right)=\begin{cases}t,\quad t\geq 0,\\

Semigroup theory.

C_{0}
-semigroups
serve to describe the time evolution of autonomous linear systems. We provide some fundamental facts concerning
C_{0}
-semigroups, their generators and their applications to the
abstract differential equations
in a Banach space
X
which are used throughout this paper.

We shall be concerned with a family of bounded linear operators
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
on a half-line
[0,\infty)
to
\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
is called a
C_{0}
-semigroup
or a
strongly continuous semigroup
satisfying the following hypotheses:

•

\mathcal{T}(0)=I
;

•

\mathcal{T}(t+s)=\mathcal{T}(t)\mathcal{T}(s)
for all
t,s\geq 0
;

•

\lim\limits_{t\to 0_{+}}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(t)x-x\right\rVert=0
for every
x\in X
.

Note that the condition
(iii)
is a condition of
strong continuity
of the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{T}(t)
at point
t=0
. Alternatively, the last condition can be given as follows:

•

\mathcal{T}(t)x
is continuous in
t
on
[0,\infty)
for each
x\in X
.

If
\mathcal{T}(t)
is defined for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the second condition holds for all
t,s\in\mathbb{R}
and the function
t\to\mathcal{T}(t)x
is continuous with respect to
t
on
\mathbb{R}
for every
x\in X
, then
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is called a
C_{0}
-group
.

A linear operator
A_{0}:X\to X
defined by

A_{0}x=\lim_{t\to 0_{+}}\frac{\mathcal{T}(t)x-x}{t},\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}),

where

\mathcal{D}(A_{0})=\left\{x\in X:\quad\lim_{t\to 0_{+}}\frac{\mathcal{T}(t)x-x}{t}\quad\textit{exists}\right\},

is the
infinitesimal generator
of the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
, defined on its domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
.
It is known that the
infinitesimal generator

A_{0}:\mathcal{D}(A_{0})\subseteq X\to X
of a strongly continuous semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
closed
and
densely-defined
linear operator on
X
, i.e.,
\overline{\mathcal{D}(A_{0})}=X
.

For a strongly continuous semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

•

If
x\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
, then
\mathcal{T}(t)x\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
and

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{T}(t)x=A_{0}\mathcal{T}(t)x=\mathcal{T}(t)A_{0}x,\quad t\geq 0;

•

For every
x\in X
, one has

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(s)x\mathrm{d}s\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}),\quad t\geq 0;

•

For every
t\in[0,\infty)
, the following identities hold true:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(t)x-x

\displaystyle=A_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(s)x\mathrm{d}s,\quad x\in X,

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(s)A_{0}x\mathrm{d}s,\quad x\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0}).

It is known that for every strongly continuous semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
, there exists constants
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
M\geq 1
such that

\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(t)\right\rVert\leq M\exp\left(\omega t\right),\quad t\geq 0.

(5)

The
resolvent

\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})=\left(\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}\right)^{-1}
of
A_{0}
is defined on the
resolvent set

\rho(A_{0})
for
A_{0}
:

\rho(A_{0})=\left\{\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{C}:\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}:\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)\to X\quad\textit{is bijective}\right\}

and belongs to
\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
.
The
resolvent

\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})
of
A_{0}
satisfies the following identities:

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}\right)\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})=I,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\left(\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}\right)x=x,\quad x\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right).

Moreover, if
Re(\lambda_{0})>\omega
, then
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(A_{0})
, and

\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\lambda_{0}t\right)\mathcal{T}(t)x\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in X.

(6)

By the formulae (
5

6

\left\lVert\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\right\rVert\leq\frac{M}{Re(\lambda_{0})-\omega}.

In the particular case, if the

A_{0}:X\to X
is
bounded

M>0
such that
\left\lVert A_{0}x\right\rVert\leq M\left\lVert x\right\rVert
for all

•

The domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
is all of
X
, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})=X

•

The

\lim\limits_{t\to 0_{+}}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(t)-I\right\rVert=0.

•

The

-semigroup is defined by the formula

\mathcal{T}(t)=\exp\left(A_{0}t\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}A_{0}^{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\quad t\geq 0.

Perturbation theory
. In many concrete situations, the evolution equation is given as a sum of several terms having different physical meaning and different mathematical properties. In such situations
perturbation theory
plays a very useful tool in the hands of both analyst and physicist. In this part, we provide some fundamental properties on
bounded time-independent perturbations
of
C_{0}
-semigroups (i.e., generator
A_{0}

perturbed
by the
bounded
linear operator
A_{1}
).

If
A_{0}:\mathcal{D}(A_{0})\subseteq X\to X
is an infinitesimal generator of
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
, then the semigroup of bounded linear operators
\left\{\mathcal{S}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by
A_{0}+A_{1}
defined on
\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
can be represented by an
absolutely
and
uniformly
(in every
compact
subset of
[0,\infty)
)
convergent
series as follows:

\mathcal{S}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

(8)

where

\displaystyle S_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle S_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S_{n-1}(t-s)A_{1}\mathcal{T}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad t\geq 0.

Moreover, the strongly continuous semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{S}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
M\geq 1
is satisfying

\left\lVert\mathcal{S}(t)\right\rVert\leq M\exp(\omega_{1}t),\quad\omega_{1}\coloneqq\omega+M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert,\quad t\geq 0.

For
Re(\lambda_{0})>\omega_{1}
, we have

\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+A_{1})x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\lambda_{0}t\right)\mathcal{S}(t)x\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in X.

If
A_{0}
is an infinitesimal generator of
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
A_{1}
is a bounded linear operator, then the
resolvent

\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+A_{1})
of
A_{0}+A_{1}
satisfies the following identities:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+A_{1})x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big]^{n}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big]^{n}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})x,\quad x\in X.

Consider the following abstract Cauchy problem for a linear non-homogeneous evolution equation in a Banach space
X
:

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u(t)=A_{0}u(t)+A_{1}u(t)+g(t),\quad t\geq 0,\\
u(0)=x.\end{cases}

(9)

Let
A_{0}:\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)\subseteq X\to X
be the infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
,
A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
and
g(\cdot)\in\mathbb{C}^{1}\left([0,\infty),X\right)
. Then, for each
x\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
, there exists a unique continuously differentiable solution
u(\cdot):[0,\infty)\to X
of (
9
) which is satisfying
u(t)\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
for
t\in[0,\infty)
with
u(0)=x
. This solution has a closed form:

u(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)x+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

where
\mathcal{S}(t)
is given by (
8
), and

\displaystyle w_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)g(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle w_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}w_{n-1}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}w_{n-1}(t-s)A_{1}\mathcal{T}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad t\geq 0.

A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
and the initial function
\varphi(\cdot)\in\mathbb{C}\left([-\tau,0],X\right)
.

Our main aim is to determine a
closed-form
of the
classical (strong)
solution of an abstract Cauchy problem (
10
).

A classical (strong) solution of the abstract Cauchy problem (
10
) is understood as an operator-valued function
u(\cdot):[-\tau,\infty)\to X
-continuously defined for all
t\geq-\tau
, continuously differentiable for all
t\geq 0
,
u(t)\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
for all
t\geq 0
with
\varphi(0)\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
and satisfying (
10
) for all
t\geq 0
.

Meanwhile, this definition can be generalized to
mild
sense as follows:

A mild solution of the abstract Cauchy problem (
10
) is understood as an operator-valued function
u(\cdot):[-\tau,\infty)\to X
-continuously defined for all
t\geq-\tau
, continuously differentiable for all
t\geq 0
,
u(t)\in X
for all
t\geq 0
and satisfying (
10
) for all
t\geq 0
.

The
fundamental solution
of the abstract initial value problem (
10
) which is the main part of the solution of delay evolution equation can be defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

If
\mathcal{S}(\cdot;\tau):[-\tau,\infty)\to\mathcal{L}(X)
satisfies the following linear homogeneous abstract differential equation with linear operator coefficients:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=A_{0}\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)+A_{1}\mathcal{S}(t-\tau;\tau),\quad t\geq 0,

(11)

under initial conditions

\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\begin{cases}\Theta,\quad-\tau\leq t<0,\\
I,\quad t=0,\end{cases}

(12)

then
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
,
t\geq-\tau
is called the corresponding fundamental solution of an abstract differential equation (
10
) with a constant delay.

Remark 2
.

The fundamental solution
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
,
t\geq-\tau
also satisfies the following differential equation with operator coefficients and initial conditions

\begin{cases}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)A_{0}+\mathcal{S}(t-\tau;\tau)A_{1},\quad t\geq 0,\\
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\begin{cases}\Theta,\quad-\tau\leq t<0,\\
I,\quad t=0.\end{cases}\end{cases}

(13)

This does not mean that
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
commutes individually with the coefficient operators
A_{i}
,
i=0,1
for any
t\in[0,\infty)
.

Proof.

To verify this remark, it is sufficient to compare the Laplace image of the
fundamental solution
as a solution of differential equation with operator coefficients (
11
) with the abstract delay differential equation (
13
).
∎

A
fundamental solution
is an operator-valued function with values in
\mathcal{L}(X)
which can be found with the help of
Laplace transform
technique.
Let
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(A_{0})
. Then,
applying the Laplace integral transform each side of (
11
) with initial conditions (
12
) and using integration by substitution, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t-x

\displaystyle=A_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+A_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t-\tau;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=A_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+A_{1}\int_{-\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}(t+\tau))\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=A_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t,\quad x\in X.

Therefore, for sufficiently large
Re(\lambda_{0})
, the Laplace transform of a
fundamental solution
is defined by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t=\Big(\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}-A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)^{-1}x,\quad x\in X.

(14)

Moreover, the right-hand side of (
14
) is the perturbation of the infinitesimal generator
A_{0}
with a bounded linear operator
\hat{A}_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)
:

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}-A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)^{-1}x=\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}\right)x,\quad x\in X.

(15)

Therefore, for any
x\in X
, the Laplace transform of a
fundamental solution
of (
10
) can be determined by the resolvent of
A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}
as follows:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t=\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}\right)x,\quad\hat{A}_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X).

(16)

The following lemma is given in more general case for closed linear operators and plays a significant role in the proof of Theorem
5
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
A_{0}:\mathcal{D}(A_{0})\subseteq X\to X
be closed linear operator on
X
and assume
A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is such that
\left\lVert A_{1}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\right\rVert<1
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(A_{0})
. Then,
A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}
where
\hat{A}_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is a closed linear operator with domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
and
\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}\right)
exists, and the following identity holds true:

\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big]^{n}\exp(-n\lambda_{0}\tau).

(17)

Proof.

It is obvious that
A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}
with
\hat{A}_{1}=A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is a closed linear operator with domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
.
Since the Neumann series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big]^{n}
converges under the hypotheses
\left\lVert A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\right\rVert<1
, we note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big]^{n}\exp(-n\lambda_{0}\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big]^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\Big(I-A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)^{-1},

and hence that

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda_{0}I-A_{0}-A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\Big(I-A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})^{-1}\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\Big(I-A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)\Big(I-A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=I.

(18)

Furthermore, the range of
\mathcal{R}
is precisely
\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
since the range of
\Big(I-A_{1}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)^{-1}
is
X
. Thus, for a given
x\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
there exists a
y\in X
such that
x=\mathcal{R}y
. Therefore, by (
3
), we attain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\Big(\lambda_{0}I-\Big(A_{0}+A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)\Big)x

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}\Big(\lambda_{0}I-\Big(A_{0}+A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\Big)\Big)\mathcal{R}y

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}y

\displaystyle=x,

so that
\mathcal{R}
is both a left and a right inverse. The proof is complete. ∎

Remark 4
.

Note that under the condition
\left\lVert\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)A_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\right\rVert<1
for some
\lambda_{0}\in\rho(A_{0})
, the following identity also holds true:

\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})A_{1}\Big]^{n}\mathcal{R}(\lambda_{0};A_{0})\exp(-n\lambda_{0}\tau).

The closed-form of a
fundamental solution
to delay evolution equation (
10
) can be expressed with the help of
a delayed Dyson-Phillips series
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A_{0}:\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)\subseteq X\to X
be the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
. Then there is a unique one-parameter family of bounded linear operators
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
strongly continuous on
[-\tau,\infty)
such that
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\Theta
,
-\tau\leq t<0
and
\mathcal{S}(0;\tau)=I
; strongly continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
to
\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
and satisfying

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=A_{0}\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)+A_{1}\mathcal{S}(t-\tau;\tau),\quad t\geq 0.

(19)

This solution has an explicit representation formula:

\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau},\quad t\geq 0,

(20)

where

\displaystyle S_{0}(t,0)=\mathcal{T}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle S_{n}(t,n\tau)=\int_{n\tau}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(21)

Proof.

We divide by the proof some parts:

1.
Since a
fundamental solution
is satisfying initial conditions (
12
), i.e.,
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\Theta
,
-\tau\leq t<0
and
\mathcal{S}(0;\tau)=I
, it is strongly continuous on the initial interval
[-\tau,0]
.

It is obvious that
S_{0}(t,0)=\mathcal{T}(t)
is strongly continuous on
[0,\infty)
that
\left\lVert S_{0}(t,0)\right\rVert\leq M\exp(\omega t)
by (
5
). Suppose
S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}
is like-wise strongly continuous on
[0,\infty)
and that

\left\lVert S_{n}(t,n\tau)\right\rVert\leq M\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n}\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n}}{n!}\exp(\omega t),\quad t\geq n\tau.

(22)

Then
\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n}(s-\tau,n\tau)
will be strongly continuous on
[(n+1)\tau,t]
so that the integral defining
S_{n+1}(t,(n+1)\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq(n+1)\tau}
exists in the strong topology for
t\in[0,\infty)
, by induction principle. Furthermore, by (
5
) and (
22
), we have:

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{n+1}(t,(n+1)\tau)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\int_{(n+1)\tau}^{t}\exp(\omega(t-s))\left\lVert S_{n}(s-\tau,n\tau)\right\rVert\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq M\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n+1}\exp(\omega t)\int_{(n+1)\tau}^{t}\frac{(s-(n+1)\tau)^{n}}{n!}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=M\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n+1}\frac{(t-(n+1)\tau)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\exp(\omega t),\quad t\geq(n+1)\tau.

Finally, for
t_{1}<t_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{n+1}(t_{2},(n+1)\tau)x-S_{n+1}(t_{1},(n+1)\tau)x\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{(n+1)\tau}^{t_{1}}\left\lVert\Big(\mathcal{T}(t_{2}-s)-\mathcal{T}(t_{1}-s)\Big)A_{1}S_{n}(s-\tau,n\tau)x\right\rVert\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(t_{2}-s)\right\rVert\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\left\lVert S_{n}(s-\tau,n\tau)x\right\rVert\mathrm{d}s,\quad x\in X.

(23)

As
t_{1}\to t_{2}
, the integrand in the first term on the right of (
3
) converges to zero boundedly, the integrand of the second term is bounded and the second integral converges to zero boundedly, too. It follows that
S_{n+1}(t,(n+1)\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq(n+1)\tau}
is strongly continuous on
[0,\infty)
. Therefore, with the help of mathematical induction principle, we have showed that
S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}
is well-defined, strongly continuous, and satisfies (
22
) for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence, the series (
20
) is a strongly continuous function on
[0,\infty)
with values in
\mathcal{L}(X)
.

2.
Meanwhile, by making use of the estimation (
22
) and closed-form of a
pure delayed
exponential function, in accordance with the
comparison test
for functional series, the series representing in (
20
) converges in the
uniform operator topology uniformly
for
t
in every compact subset of
[0,\infty)
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\lVert S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq M\exp(\omega t)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n}\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n}}{n!}\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}

\displaystyle=M\exp(\omega t)\exp_{\tau}(\hat{\omega}t),\quad t\geq 0,\quad\hat{\omega}\coloneqq M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau),

where
\exp_{\tau}(\cdot):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
pure delayed
real-valued exponential function defined by

\exp_{\tau}(t)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n}}{n!}\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

On the other hand, the series (
20
) is majorized by the series expansion of
M\exp(\omega_{1}t)
where
\omega_{1}\coloneqq\omega+\hat{\omega}
with
\hat{\omega}\coloneqq M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq M\exp(\omega t)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{\omega}^{n}\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n}}{n!}\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}

\displaystyle\leq M\exp(\omega t)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{\omega}^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=M\exp\Big(\Big(\omega+\hat{\omega}\Big)t\Big)

\displaystyle\coloneqq M\exp(\omega_{1}t),\quad t\geq 0.

Then, for
Re(\lambda_{0})>\omega_{1}
, we can attain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}x\Big]\mathrm{d}t=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)S_{n}(t,n\tau)x\mathrm{d}t,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad x\in X,

where the interchanging of the summation and integration is justified by the
uniform convergence
of the series in the
uniform operator topology
.

Now, if
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, it is a consequence of the uniform convergence of the integral and of the Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle x^{*}\Big[\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)S_{n}(t,n\tau)x\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)x^{*}\Big[S_{n}(t,n\tau)x\Big]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\int_{n\tau}^{t}x^{*}\Big[\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)x\Big]\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}s)\int_{s}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}(t-s))x^{*}\Big[\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)x\Big]\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}s)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)x^{*}\Big[\mathcal{T}(t)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)x\Big]\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}s)x^{*}\Big[\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)x\Big]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=x^{*}\Big[\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)A_{1}\left\{\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}s)S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)x\mathrm{d}s\right\}\Big],\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad x\in X.

Hence, by induction, we derive that

\int_{n\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)S_{n}(t,n\tau)x\mathrm{d}t=\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\Big]^{n}\exp(-n\lambda_{0}\tau)x,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad x\in X.

Therefore, the Laplace transform of the series (
20
) for any
t\in[0,\infty)
is defined by

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\Big[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}x\Big]\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\Big]^{n}\exp(-n\lambda_{0}\tau)x,\quad x\in X.

On the other hand, for
Re(\lambda_{0})>\omega_{1}=\omega+M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp\left(-\omega\tau\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\|A_{1}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\exp(-\lambda_{0}\tau)\|

\displaystyle=\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}(t+\tau))\mathcal{T}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\left\lVert\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{T}(t-\tau)\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-Re(\lambda_{0})t\Big)\left\lVert\mathcal{T}(t-\tau)\right\rVert\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\Big(-\left(Re(\lambda_{0})-\omega\right)t\Big)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)}{Re(\lambda_{0})-\omega}<1.

(24)

Therefore, for the infinitesimal generator
A_{0}
of a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and the bounded linear operator
A_{1}
, by Lemma
3
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}+\hat{A}_{1}\right)x

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-\lambda_{0}t)\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\Big[A_{1}\mathcal{R}\left(\lambda_{0};A_{0}\right)\Big]^{n}\exp(-n\lambda_{0}\tau)x,\quad x\in X.

Therefore, for
Re(\lambda_{0})>\omega_{1}
, the Laplace integral transforms of both
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}
are equal on
[0,\infty)
and hence by the uniqueness theorem of Laplace transform (
[
19
]
, pp. 530), these two functions are equal for any
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Therefore,
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}
converges to a strongly continuous function
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
uniformly with respect to
t
in the uniform operator topology on every compact subsets of
[0,\infty)
and
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
is a fundamental solution of (
10
) for
t\geq 0
, under the initial conditions (
12
).

3.
Alternatively, we can prove that
t\mapsto\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)
satisfies (
10
) for all
t\geq 0
with initial conditions
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\Theta
,
-\tau\leq t<0
and
\mathcal{S}(0;\tau)=I
. Since
\mathcal{T}(0)=I
,
S_{n}(0,n\tau)=\Theta
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\Theta
,
-\tau\leq t<0
and
\mathcal{S}(0;\tau)=I
, i.e., the initial conditions (
12
) are satisfied. Applying (
20
), (
5
) and interchanging of the summation and integration which is justified by the uniform convergence of the series in the uniform operator topology, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}=S_{0}(t,0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathds{1}_{s\geq n\tau}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathds{1}_{s\geq n\tau}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(s-\tau,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{s\geq(n+1)\tau}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}\mathcal{S}(s-\tau;\tau)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0.

First, we need to show that the function
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\mathcal{S}(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
is strongly continuously differentiable. Since
t\mapsto\mathcal{T}(t)
is strongly continuously differentiable for all
t\geq 0
, this implies that
t\mapsto S_{0}(t,0)
is strongly continuously differentiable for all
t\geq 0
. Assuming that this is true for
t\mapsto S_{n}(t,n\tau)
for all
t\geq 0
. Then, by induction, it is easily shown to be true for
t\mapsto S_{n+1}(t,(n+1)\tau)
for all
t\geq 0
.
Furthermore, by Leibniz integral rule, we derive that

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\prime}(t,0)=A_{0}\mathcal{T}(t)=\mathcal{T}(t)A_{0},\quad t\geq 0

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\prime}(t,n\tau)=\int_{n\tau}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}S_{n-1}^{\prime}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

By using (
5
), for
x\in\mathcal{D}(A_{0})
, we obtain by induction from these equations the following bound:

\left\lVert S_{n}^{\prime}(t,n\tau)x\right\rVert\leq M\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n}\exp(\omega t)\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n}}{n!}\left\lVert A_{0}x\right\rVert,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

will exists in the strong topology and be equal to
\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(s)f(t-s)\mathrm{d}s
.

Since

is continuously differentiable for any

, by Leibniz integral rule, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}g(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(s)f(t-s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t)f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(s)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(t-s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(t)f(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)f^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0.

(47)

Next, we prove the equivalence of the equations (
45
) and (
46
). Since
\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)f(s)\mathrm{d}s\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
, by the integration by parts formula, it follows that

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)f^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s=f(t)-\mathcal{T}(t)f(0)+A_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)f(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0.

(48)

As a consequence, from (
14
) and (
48
), we attain the desired result:

\frac{\mathrm{d}g(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}=f(t)+A_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)f(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0.

Further, the continuity of
\mathrm{d}g(t)/\mathrm{d}t
follows from the first part of the lemma using the first representation of
\mathrm{d}g(t)/\mathrm{d}t
given by (
14
). The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 15
.

Let
A_{0}
be the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators
\left\{\mathcal{T}(t)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
. Let
A_{1}\in\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)
and
g(t)
be continuously differentiable function on
[0,\infty)
to
X
. Then there exists a unique continuously differentiable solution

of the abstract Cauchy problem (
44
) for a linear non-homogeneous delay evolution equation which is satisfying
u(t)\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
for all
t\geq 0
with
\varphi(0)\in\mathcal{D}\left(A_{0}\right)
. This solution has a closed form:

u(t)=u_{0}(t)+u_{1}(t),\quad t\geq 0,

(49)

where
u_{0}(t)
is given by (
42
), and

\displaystyle u_{1}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau},\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle w_{0}(t,0)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)g(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle w_{n}(t,n\tau)=\int_{n\tau}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}w_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
14
that
w_{0}(t,0)
is continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
and hence by induction that
w_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}
is like-wise for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
on
[0,\infty)
. In fact, by Lemma
14
, we have

\displaystyle w_{0}^{\prime}(t,0)=\mathcal{T}(t)g(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)g^{\prime}(s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq 0,

\displaystyle w_{n}^{\prime}(t,n\tau)=\int_{n\tau}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{1}w_{n-1}^{\prime}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\geq n\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

By using (
5
) it is now easy to acquire the following estimations for
t\geq n\tau
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert w_{n}(t,n\tau)\right\rVert\leq M\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n}N\exp(\omega t)\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!},

\displaystyle\left\lVert w_{n}^{\prime}(t,n\tau)\right\rVert\leq M\Big(M\left\lVert A_{1}\right\rVert\exp(-\omega\tau)\Big)^{n}N\exp(\omega t)\Big[\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n}}{n!}+\frac{(t-n\tau)^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\Big],

where

.

From these bounds, as in Theorem
5
, this implies that the functional series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}
and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}^{\prime}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}

and

, for

,

\displaystyle u_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}=w_{0}(t,0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w_{n}(t,n\tau)\mathds{1}_{t\geq n\tau}

\displaystyle=w_{0}(t,0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}w_{n-1}(s-\tau,(n-1)\tau)\mathds{1}_{s\geq n\tau}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}u_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(w_{0}(t,0)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{T}(t-s)A_{1}u_{1}(s-\tau)\mathrm{d}s\Big)

Theorem 21
.

A linear operator family
\left\{\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}(l\tau):k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}\subset\mathcal{L}\left(X\right)

(i)
For any (permutable and non-permutable) linear operators

\displaystyle=A_{0}\mathcal{Q}_{k}(l\tau)+A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{k}((l-1)\tau),

(61)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{0}(l\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{k}(-\tau)=\Theta,\quad k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(ii)
If

Proof.

(i)
By making use of the mathematical induction principle, we can prove (

for a fixed

59
) for
l

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}((l+1)\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=l+1}^{k}A_{0}^{k-m}A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{m}(l\tau)

\displaystyle=A_{0}\sum_{m=l+1}^{k-1}A_{0}^{k-1-m}A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{m}(l\tau)+A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{k}(l\tau)

\displaystyle=A_{0}\mathcal{Q}_{k}((l+1)\tau)+A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{k}(l\tau).

This implies that the formula (

To show
(ii)

l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for a fixed

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{k+1}((n+1)\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n+1}^{k}\binom{m-1}{n}A_{0}^{k-1-n}A_{1}^{n+1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)

Lemma 25

\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)=I

k=1

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{1}\mathcal{Q}_{2}(l\tau)\exp\left(-l\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{1}A_{0}\mathcal{Q}_{1}(l\tau)\exp\left(-l\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{1}A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{1}((l-1)\tau)\exp\left(-l\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)

\displaystyle=A_{0}\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)+A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{1}(\tau)\exp\left(-\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)+A_{1}\mathcal{Q}_{1}(0)\exp\left(-\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)

) is true for the case of

\sum_{l=0}^{n}\mathcal{Q}_{n+1}(l\tau)\exp\left(-l\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)=\Big(A_{0}+A_{1}\exp\left(-\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)\Big)^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(68)

and we prove it for

\displaystyle\Big(A_{0}+A_{1}\exp\left(-\tau\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)\Big)^{n+1}

Theorem 31

with zero commutator

\displaystyle A_{2}\coloneqq A_{1}\exp\left(-A_{0}\tau\right)\in\mathcal{L}\left(X\right),\hskip 2.84544ptn\tau<t\leq(n+1)\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

n=0

\displaystyle S_{1}(t,\tau)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(t;\tau)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{l}(t,l\tau)

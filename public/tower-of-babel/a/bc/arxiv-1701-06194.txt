1.
Introduction

Throughout this article,
R
will be a commutative ring with identity. Prime ideals play an essential role in ring theory. A prime ideal
P
of

P
with the property that for
a,b\in R

implies
a\in P
or
b\in P

lie or restrict or enlarge where

lies. In this article we will interested in a generalization obtained by restricting where
ab
lies. A proper ideal
P
of

is weakly prime if for
a,b\in R
with
ab\in P-0
, either
a\in P
or
b\in P
. Weakly prime ideals were studied in some detail by Anderson and Smith (2003) in
[
1
]
. Thus any prime ideal is weakly prime. Bhatwadekar and Sharma (2005) in
[
2
]
recently deﬁned a proper ideal

of an integral domain

to be almost prime if for
a,b\in R
with
ab\in I-I^{2}
, then either
a\in I
or
b\in I
. This deﬁnition can obviously be made for any commutative ring
R
. Thus a weakly prime ideal is almost prime and any proper idempotent ideal is almost prime. Moreover, an ideal
I
of
R
is almost prime if and only if
I/I^{2}
is a weakly prime ideal of
R/I^{2}
. Also almost prime ideals were generalized to

-almost prime as follows; a proper ideal

is called

-almost prime ideal if for any

with

, then either
a\in I
or
b\in I
. With weakly prime ideals and almost prime ideals in mind, we make the following deﬁnition. Let

be a commutative ring and

of

-prime where

taken to be zero or

I
-prime, so we can assume

to be proper ideal of

.
For more details see
[

Example 1.1

Consider the ring

-prime which is not prime nor weakly prime.

Generalized integration operators on analytic tent spaces Thanks: † \dagger Corresponding author.
Rong Yang, Lian Hu and Songxiao Li †

Abstract
. In this paper, the boundedness and compactness of generalized integration operators T g n , k T_{g}^{n,k} between different analytic tent spaces in the unit disc are completely characterized. Keywords : Integration operator, tent space, Hardy space, Bergman space.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit disc on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{T}
its boundary, and
H(\mathbb{D})
be the set of
all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
.
We denote by
{\mathbb{N}}
the set of positive integers.
The Hardy space
H^{p}(0<p<\infty)
consists of all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}<\infty.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha>-1
, the weighted Bergman space
A_{\alpha}^{p}
consists of
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{A_{\alpha}^{p}}^{p}=(\alpha+1)\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)<\infty,

where
dA(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}dxdy
is the normalized Lebesgue area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
\eta\in\mathbb{T}
and
\zeta>\frac{1}{2}
. The non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is defined by

\Gamma(\eta)=\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\eta|<\zeta(1-|z|^{2})\}.
Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. The tent space
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

In particular, for
\alpha=0
, we write
T_{p}^{q}
instead of
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
. For
p=\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, the tent space
T_{\infty}^{q}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T_{\infty}^{q}(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\operatorname{esssup}_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|\right)^{q}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

Notice that the definition of
T^{q}_{\infty}(\alpha)=T^{q}_{\infty}
is independent of
\alpha
.
For
q=\infty
and
0<p<\infty
, the tent space
T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=\operatorname{esssup}_{\eta\in\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{u\in\Gamma(\eta)}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where

S(re^{i\theta})=\left\{\lambda e^{it}:|t-\theta|\leq\frac{1-r}{2},1-\lambda\leq 1-r\right\}
for
re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
and
S(0)=\mathbb{D}
.
Denote
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\cap H(\mathbb{D})
by
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
.

Tent spaces were initially introduced by Coifman, Meyer and Stein in
[
8
]
to study problems in harmonic analysis. They provided a general framework for questions regarding some important spaces such as Hardy spaces and Bergman spaces. The aperture
\zeta
of the non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is suppressed in the above definition since any two apertures generate the same function space with equivalent quasinorms.
By the non-tangential maximal function characterization of the Hardy space,
AT^{q}_{\infty}=H^{q}
, which can be see as the limit of
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
when
p\to\infty
(see

[
28
]
). In particular,
AT_{p}^{p}(\alpha-1)=A^{p}_{\alpha}
, the weighted Bergman space.

In
[
5
,
6
]
, Carleson characterized the positive Borel measures
\mu
on the unit disc
{\mathbb{D}}
such that the identity operator
I_{d}:H^{p}\to L^{p}({d\mu})
is bounded
if and only if
\mu
is a Carleson measure,
that is,

\sup_{I\subset\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|}<\infty
.
Here,
S(I)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:1-|I|\leq|z|<1,\frac{z}{|z|}\in I\}
.
In
[
10
]
, Duren generalized Carleson’s result and descibed the boundedness of the identity operator
I_{d}:H^{p}\to L^{q}({d\mu})
when
0<p\leq q<\infty
.
In
[
16
]
, Luecking investigated the positive Borel measure
\mu
such that differential operator of order
m
mapping
H^{p}
to
L^{q}(d\mu)
boundedly.
In
[
17
]
, Luecking characterized the positive Borel measure
\mu
such that differential operator of order
m
mapping the Bergman space
A^{p}
and the tent space
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
to
L^{q}(d\mu)
boundedly.
Subsequently, the Carleson measure was extended to higher dimensional cases and more general cases, and became a very important tool for the study of function space and operator theory.
See
[
1
,
23
]
for more results of the differential operator of order
m
mapping the tent space
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
into
L^{q}(d\mu)
.

Let
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
0\leq k<n
. The generalized integration operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
is defined by

T_{g}^{n,k}f(z)=I^{n}\left(f^{(k)}(z)g^{(n-k)}(z)\right),\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}).

Here,
I^{n}
is the
n
-th iteration of the integration operator
If(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(t)dt
.
The operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
was first introduced by Chalmoukis
[
7
]
. In particular, when
n=1
and
k=0
, we have

T_{g}^{1,0}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\eta)g^{\prime}(\eta)d\eta=T_{g}f(z).

In 1977, the Volterra integration operator
T_{g}
was first introduced and stuided by Pommerenke in
[
20
]
. It was proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{2}
if and only if
g\in BMOA
.
In 1995, Aleman and Siskakis proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{p}
(
p\geq 1
) if and only if
g\in BMOA
in
[
2
]
.
In 1997, they proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
A^{p}
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{B}
in
[
3
]
.
Recall that the Bloch space
\mathcal{B}
is the class of all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
with

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}=|f(0)|+\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|<\infty.

See
[
18
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
26
,
25
]
and the
references therein for more study of the operator
T_{g}
.
In
[
7
]
, Chalmoukis studied the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
between different Hardy spaces. For example, he showed that
T_{g}^{n,0}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if
g\in BMOA
,
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if
g\in\mathcal{B}
when
k\geq 1
, while
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}+n-k}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|<\infty
when
0<p<q<\infty
.
In
[
9
]
, the authors characterized the boundedness, compactness and Schatten class membership of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
on Bergman spaces induced by doubling weights.
From the result just mentioned, we see that
T_{g}^{n,k}
has a very different behavior from that of
T_{g}
.

Motivated by
[
7
,
9
,
21
]
, in this paper, we study the boundedness and compactness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
. The necessary and sufficient conditions of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
to be boundedly and compactly are given.

This paper is organzied as follows. In Section 2, we mainly provide some related properties of tent spaces. In Section 3 and Section 4, we describe the boundedness and compactness of
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
, respectively.

In this paper, the symbol
E\lesssim F
means that there exists a positive finite constant
C
such that
E\leq CF
. The value of
C
may change from one occurrence to another. The expression
E\asymp F
means that both
E\lesssim F
and
F\lesssim E
hold.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
4
, Lemma 4]

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
\lambda>\max\{1,\frac{p}{q}\}
. Then there are constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(p,q,\lambda,C)
and

C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu(\Gamma(\eta))^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-z\bar{\eta}|}\right)^{\lambda}d\mu\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|\leq C_{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu(\Gamma(\eta))^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|

for every positive measure

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
and

. Then

if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma{(\eta)}}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{np+\alpha}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|<\infty.

Proof.

Form
[

\left\|f^{\prime}(\cdot)(1-|\cdot|)\right\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}\asymp\|f\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)},\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}),

which get the desired result.
∎

[

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
,
f\in AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. Then

|f^{(n)}(z)|\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+n}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

According to
[
27
, Proposition 4.13]
, we have

|f^{(n)}(z)|^{p}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f^{(n)}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(w).

For
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
, using Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f^{(n)}(z)|^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+2+np}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f^{(n)}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{np+\alpha}dA(w)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}+nq+1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\zeta_{+}}(\eta)}|f^{(n)}(w)|^{p}(1-|w|^{2})^{np+\alpha}dA(w)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{q(\alpha+2)}{p}+nq+1}},

which implies the desired result.
∎

3.
The boundedness of
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)

The purpose of this section is to study the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
.
Now, we are in a position to state and prove the main result in this section.

Theorem 3.1

Let

Then

is bounded if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(a) If

0<q=s<\infty

0<t<p<\infty

g^{(n-k)}\in AT_{\frac{pt}{p-t}}^{\infty}(\delta),

\delta=\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}

(d) If

0<p,t<\infty

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

d\mu_{g}(z)=|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{1}{b}}(1-|z|^{2})^{m+\beta+t+1+\frac{n-k-1}{b}}dA(z)
.
From Lemma
2.17
, we know that
D^{(m)}:AT_{\frac{pbt}{pbt-p+t}}^{\frac{qbs}{qbs-q+s}}(\gamma)\to L^{1}(\mu_{g})
is bounded if and only if
g^{(n-k)}\in AT_{\frac{pt}{p-t}}^{\infty}(\delta)
, where
\delta=\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}
.

(d) For
0<q<s<\infty
,
0<p,t<\infty
or
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
, using Proposition
2.16
and
(\ref{4.5})
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{j}|w_{j}|^{\frac{1}{b}}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{\frac{q-s}{qbs}}=\left(\sup_{j}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}<\infty.

Thus, we have
|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}\in l^{\infty}
.
By Lemma
2.18
, we obtain for
b>\max\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{s}\}
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, the operator

D^{(m)}:AT_{\frac{pbt}{pbt-p+t}}^{\frac{qbs}{qbs-q+s}}(\gamma)\to L^{1}(\mu_{g})

is bounded.
Here,
\gamma=\left[(\beta+t)\left(1-\frac{1}{tb}\right)+\frac{\alpha}{pb}\right]\cdot\frac{pbt}{pbt-p+t}
and
d\mu_{g}(z)=|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{1}{b}}(1-|z|^{2})^{m+\beta+t+1+\frac{n-k-1}{b}}dA(z)
.
By Lemma
2.17
,
the operator
D^{(m)}:AT_{\frac{pbt}{pbt-p+t}}^{\frac{qbs}{qbs-q+s}}(\gamma)\to L^{1}(\mu_{g})
is bounded if and only if

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

∎

Next, we consider the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
when
t=\infty
, i.e.,
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to H^{s}
, since
AT_{\infty}^{s}=H^{s}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<p,q,s<\infty
,
\alpha>-2
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
and
(n-k)p-(\alpha+2)>0
. Then
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to H^{s}
is bounded if and only if one of the following conditions holds:

(a) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
2<p<\infty
,

g^{(n-k)}\in AT_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}(\delta),
where
\delta=\left(n-k-1-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{2p}{p-2}
.

(b) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
0<p\leq 2
,

g^{(n-k)}(\cdot)(1-|\cdot|^{2})^{n-k-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\in AT_{\infty}^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}.

(c) If
0<q=s<\infty
,
2<p<\infty
,
g^{(n-k)}\in AT_{\frac{2p}{p-2}}^{\infty}(\delta),
where
\delta=\left(n-k-1-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{2p}{p-2}
.

(d) If
0<q<s<\infty
,
0<p<\infty
or
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<p\leq 2
,

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

Proof.

From
[
19
, Theorem G]
, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}f\|^{s}_{H^{s}}\asymp\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}|d\eta|.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, so we omit the details here.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Using Theorem
3.2
, we can obtain the characterization of the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{s}
, which has already been investigated by Chalmoukis in
[
7
]
.
In particular, we show that

T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{s}
is bounded if and only if
\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{p}+n-k}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|<\infty
when
0<p<s<\infty
.
In addition, when
n=1
and
k=0
, by Theorem
3.2
and the fact that

A^{p}_{\alpha}=AT_{p}^{p}(\alpha-1)
, we can get the characterization of the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}:A^{p}_{\alpha}\to H^{s}
, which has already been studied by Wu in
[
25
]
and subsequently by Miihkinen, Pau, Perälä, and Wang in
[
18
]
in the case of the unit ball.

4.
The compactness of
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)

In this section, we will study the compactness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<p,q,s,t<\infty
,
\alpha,\beta>-2
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
and
(n-k)pt+p(\beta+2)-t(\alpha+2)>0
.
Then
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
is compact if and only if
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
is bounded and one of the following conditions holds:

(a) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
0<t<p<\infty
,

g^{(n-k)}\in AT_{\frac{pt}{p-t}}^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}(\delta),
where
\delta=\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}
.

(b) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
,

\lim_{R\to 1^{-}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\sup_{{z}\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\right)^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}|d\eta|=0.

(c) If
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<t<p<\infty
,

\lim_{|u|\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right){\frac{pt}{p-t}}+1}dA(z)=0.

(d) If
0<q<s<\infty
,
0<p,t<\infty
or
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
,

\lim_{z\to 1^{-}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}=0.

Proof.

Sufficiency.

Consider the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
with the property that
\sup_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}<\infty
. This implies that the set
\{f_{j}\}
is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, and by Montel’s theorem, it constitutes a normal family. Consequently, a uniformly convergent subsequence
\{f_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
can be selected on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to an analytic function
f
. According to Fatou’s Lemma,
f\in AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
. Let
h_{j}=f_{n_{j}}-f
, which also belongs to
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
. The goal is to prove that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}=0
, which would establish that the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
is compact.

(a) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
0<t<p<\infty
,

g^{(n-k)}\in AT_{\frac{pt}{p-t}}^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}(\delta)
, where
\delta=\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}
. Then, by the dominated convergence theorem, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{R\geq R_{0}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\delta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{(p-t)qs}{pt(q-s)}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{q-s}{qs}}<\epsilon.

Noting that
|h_{j}(z)|\to 0
uniformly on any compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
, we can choose
j_{0}
large enough such that
\sup_{j\geq j_{0},|z|\leq R_{0}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|<\epsilon
. Using
(\ref{aa})
, when
j\geq j_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}^{s}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\cap\{|z|\leq R_{0}\}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}+\|h_{j}\|_{AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}^{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\delta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{(p-t)qs}{pt(q-s)}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{q-s}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}.

(b) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
, the assumption
implies that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{R\geq R_{0}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\right)^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{q-s}{qs}}<\epsilon.

Choose
j_{0}
such that
\sup_{j\geq j_{0},|z|\leq R_{0}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|<\epsilon
. Using
(\ref{bb})
, when
j\geq j_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}^{s}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\cap\{|z|\leq R_{0}\}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}+\|h_{j}\|_{AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}^{s}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\right)^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{q-s}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}.

(c) If
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<t<p<\infty
,
the assumption implies that

Hence, for any
\epsilon>0

R_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{D},R\geq R_{0}}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}+1}dA(z)<\epsilon.

Choose
j_{0}
such that
\sup_{j\geq j_{0},|z|\leq R_{0}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|<\epsilon
. Using
(\ref{cc})
, when
j\geq j_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}^{s}\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\cap\{|z|\leq R_{0}\}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}+\|h_{j}\|_{AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}^{s}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\sup_{u\in\mathbb{D},R\geq R_{0}}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s(p-t)}{pt}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}.

(d) If
0<q<s<\infty
,
0<p,t<\infty
or
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
, the assumption implies that
for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}<\epsilon
for any
|z|\geq R_{0}
.
Choose
j_{0}
such that
\sup_{j\geq j_{0},|z|\leq R_{0}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|<\epsilon
.
Assume first that
0<q<s<\infty
,
0<p,t<\infty

q<r<s

(\ref{dd})

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}^{s}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j})^{(n)}\|^{s}_{A^{r}_{\frac{r(\beta+2)}{t}+nr+\frac{r}{s}-2}}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{r}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{r}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{r(\beta+2)}{t}+nr+\frac{r}{s}-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{|z|\leq R_{0}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{r}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{r}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{r(\beta+2)}{t}+nr+\frac{r}{s}-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{r}}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{R_{0}<|z|<1}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{r}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{r}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{r(\beta+2)}{t}+nr+\frac{r}{s}-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}+\epsilon^{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{r}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{r(\alpha+2)}{p}+kr+\frac{r}{q}-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}.

Next, suppose that
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
,
by
(\ref{ee})
and Lemma
2.3
, when
j\geq j_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}^{s}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\cap\{|z|\leq R_{0}\}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\{|z|\leq R_{0}\}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}+\epsilon^{s}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{kt+\frac{t(\alpha+2)}{p}-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}.

Thus,
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
is compact.

Necessity.
Assume that
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
is compact. It is evident that
T_{g}^{n,k}:AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\to AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)
is bounded and
(a)
is true according to Theorem
3.1
. Thus, we only need to prove that
(b)
,
(c)
, and
(d)
hold.
Let
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice and
E=\left\{\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p}^{q}(Z):\|\{\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{q}(Z)}=1\right\}
. For
L>\max\{1,\frac{p}{q},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p}\}

F(\{x_{j}\})(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{L}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z)^{L+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Using Proposition
2.11
, we get
\|F(\{x_{j}\})\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}\lesssim\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{q}(Z)}
.
Since
T_{g}^{n,k}
is compact and
F(\{x_{j}\})
is a bounded set, the set
\{T_{g}^{n,k}\circ F(\{x_{j}\}):x_{j}\in E\}
is relatively compact
and hence a totally bounded set in
T_{t}^{s}(\beta)
. Thus, for any
\epsilon>0
,
there exist a finite number of functions
h_{1},\cdots,h_{N}
, such that
T_{g}^{n,k}\circ F\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}D\left(h_{i},\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)
, where

D\left(h,\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)=\left\{f\in T_{g}^{n,k}\circ F:\|f-h\|_{T_{t}^{s}(\beta)}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right\}.

Noting that
\sup_{i=1,\cdots,N}\|h_{i}^{(n)}\|_{T_{t}^{s}(\beta)}<\infty
, for the above
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{i=1,\cdots,N}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|h_{i}^{(n)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}

for all
R>R_{0}
. Thus, for any
\{x_{j}\}\in E
, there exists some
i_{0}\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that
T_{g}^{n,k}\circ F\in D\left(h_{i_{0}},\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|(T_{g}^{n,k}F)^{(n)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|F^{(k)}(z)g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|F^{(k)}(z)g^{(n-k)}(z)-h_{i_{0}}^{(n)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|h_{i_{0}}^{(n)}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\circ F-h_{i_{0}}\|_{T_{t}^{s}(\beta)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}<\epsilon

for all
R>R_{0}
, that is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}\left(\sum_{j}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k+\frac{2(\alpha+2)}{p}}}\right)^{\frac{t}{2}}|g^{n-k}(z)|^{t}(1-|z|^{2})^{nt+\beta}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{s}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{q}(Z)}^{s}.

for any
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{p}^{q}(Z)
and
R>R_{0}
.
Let
r_{j}(v)
be the Rademacher functions. Replacing
x_{j}
by
x_{j}r_{j}(v)
, and by applying the same technique as in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{t}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|^{t}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{nt-kt+\beta+2-\frac{t(\alpha+2)}{p}}\cdot\chi_{\{|z|\geq R\}}\right)^{\frac{s}{t}}|d\eta|\lesssim\epsilon^{s}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{q}(Z)}^{s}

for
R>R_{0}^{\prime}=\inf\left\{|a_{j}|:D(a_{j},\rho)\subset\{|z|\geq R_{0}\}\right\}
,
where
\chi_{\{|z|\geq R\}}
is the characteristic function.

(b) If
0<s<q<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
, using the principles of duality and factorization for sequence tent spaces, as outlined in Theorem
3.1
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\sup_{{a_{j}}\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{(n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p})\frac{qs}{q-s}}|d\eta|\lesssim\epsilon^{s}

for all

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\sup_{{z}\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}(1-|z|^{2})^{(n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p})\frac{qs}{q-s}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\sup_{{a_{j}}\in\widetilde{\Gamma}(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|^{\frac{qs}{q-s}}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{(n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p})\frac{qs}{q-s}}|d\eta|\lesssim\epsilon^{s}.

(c) If
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<t<p<\infty
,
similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\sum_{a_{j}\in S(u),|a_{j}|\geq R}\left(|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}}\right)^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\lesssim\epsilon^{s}

for all
R>R_{0}^{\prime}
.
Then

\sup_{u\in\mathbb{D},R>R_{0}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right)\frac{pt}{p-t}+1}dA(z)\lesssim\epsilon^{s},

which implies that

\lim_{|u|\to 1^{-}}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{\frac{pt}{p-t}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\left(n-k+\frac{\beta}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{p}\right){\frac{pt}{p-t}}+1}dA(z)=0.

(d) If
0<q<s<\infty
,
0<p,t<\infty
or
0<q=s<\infty
,
0<p\leq t<\infty
, for
l>0
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
, define

F_{a}(z)=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{l}}{(1-\overline{a}z)^{l+\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Observing that
F_{a}
converges to zero uniformly on any compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
as
|a|\to 1^{-}
. The compactness of
T_{g}^{n,k}
implies that

\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|T_{g}^{n,k}F_{a}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}=0.

By Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

|F_{a}^{(k)}(z)g^{(n-k)}(z)|\lesssim\frac{\|(T_{g}^{n,k}F_{a})^{(n)}\|_{AT_{t}^{s}(\beta)}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\beta+2}{t}+\frac{1}{s}+n}}\quad\text{for all}~z\in\mathbb{D}.

Replacing
z
by
a
in the inequality above, we have

\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}|g^{(n-k)}(a)|(1-|a|^{2})^{n-k+\frac{\beta+2}{t}-\frac{\alpha+2}{p}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Kai Wang, Chengxiu Ling ∗

Abstract
. We consider a family of multivariate distributions with heavy-tailed margins and the type I elliptical dependence structure. This class of risks is common in finance, insurance, environmental and biostatistic applications. We obtain the asymptotic tail risk probabilities and characterize the multivariate regular variation property. The results demonstrate how the rate of decay of probabilities on tail sets varies in tail sets and the covariance matrix of the elliptical copula. The theoretical results are well illustrated by typical examples and numerical simulations. A real data application shows its advantages in a more flexible dependence structure to characterize joint insurance losses.

1.
Introduction

Heavy-tail risks characterize the phenomena where huge losses occur with a relatively high probability, such as record-breaking insurance losses, financial asset returns, transmission rates and durations in data networks
(
Resnick, 2007
;
Embrechts et al., 2013
)
. For the joint heavy-tail risk events, the copula allows the modelling of the dependence structure with any choice of marginal risks. While the classical Gaussian copula with log-normal and regularly varying risks have been extensively studied in both theoretical and applied contexts
(
Asmussen and Rojas-Nandayapa, 2008
;
Hua et al., 2014
;
Liu and Yang, 2021
;
Das and Fasen-Hartmann, 2024
)
, the elliptical copula, as a generalization of the Gaussian case, facilitates more dependence structures and are widely used in the fields of statistics, finance, insurance, environment and biology
(
Frahm et al., 2003
)
. For other applications, we refer to risk analysis
(
Usseglio-Carleve, 2018
;
Zuo and Yin, 2021
)
, portfolio theory
(
Lei et al., 2023
)
, biostatistics
(
Lindsey, 1999
)
, bioinformatics
(
Dang et al., 2015
;
He et al., 2019
)
, and climate models
(
Louie, 2014
)
. However, the probability law of heavy tail risks with general elliptical dependence structure remain unclear. Its exploration will help the simultaneous risk inference for many application-oriented problems.

Recall that for an elliptical random vector
\boldsymbol{Z}=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{d})\sim G
with zero means, it follows by
Fang et al. (1990)
that (notation:
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}
stands for equality in distribution)

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}RA{\bf U},

where
R>0
stands for radius, matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with
AA^{\top}=:\Sigma
which is a positive definite covariance matrix, and
{\bf U}
is a
d
-dimensional random vector uniformly distributed on the unit sphere
S_{d}=\{{\bf s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:s_{1}^{2}+\cdots+s_{d}^{2}=1\}
, and
{\bf U}
is independent of
R
. Further, suppose all margins
G_{1},\ldots,G_{d}
are continuous, then the elliptical copula of
\boldsymbol{Z}
is a function
C:[0,1]^{d}\to[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle C_{\Sigma,R}\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\right)=G\left(G_{1}^{\leftarrow}\left(u_{1}\right),\ldots,G_{d}^{\leftarrow}\left(u_{d}\right)\right),\quad 0\leq u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\leq 1,

where
\Sigma
and
R
are defined in Eq.(
1.1
), and

\displaystyle G_{i}^{\leftarrow}\left(u\right):=\inf\left\{x:G_{i}(x)>u\right\},\quad i=1,\ldots,d,

are the marginal left-inverse quantile functions, as well-known concepts like Value-at-Risk (VaR) in financial and risk management
(
Embrechts et al., 2009
)
.
With heavy-tailed marginal risks, a natural question lies in the effect of an elliptical copula and its parameters on the tail behaviour of joint extreme sets. While the case of elliptical copula (
C_{\Sigma,R}
) with the radius
R
(see Eq. (
1.1
)) belonging to the Fréchet max domain of attraction (MDA) is discussed in
Hashorva (2006)
;
Peng (2008)
;
Li and Peng (2009)
;
Klüppelberg et al. (2007)
, it remains a challenge for the consideration of the radius
R
in the Gumbel MDA
(
Leadbetter et al., 2012
, Theorem 1.6.2)
, where

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}\left\{R>u+x/w(u)\right\}}{\mathbb{P}\left\{R>u\right\}}\to\mathrm{e}^{-x},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}

for positive scaling function
w(\cdot)
being a regular varying function with index
-\tau,\,\tau\geq-1
, denoted as
w\in\mathcal{RV}_{-\tau}
, that is
(
Resnick, 2008
)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}w(tx)/w(t)=x^{\tau},\quad x>0.

We denote by
R\in\mbox{\mbox{GMDA}}(w,\tau)
in this context, and call such an elliptical copula with the radius
R\in\mbox{GMDA}(w,\tau)
as type I elliptical copula
(
Hashorva, 2007
)
. One sub-class of type I elliptical family, the elliptical copulas with the Weibullian-type radius
(
Arendarczyk and Dȩbicki, 2011
;
Dȩbicki et al., 2018
)
(defined below in Eq.(
1.3
)) includes well-known copulas like Gaussian (
\beta=-1,\gamma=2
), Laplace (
\gamma=1
), multivariate power exponential, and Kotz-type, which are studied with wide applications
(
Song and Singh, 2010
;
Oh et al., 2021
;
Masarotto and Varin, 2017
)
. The Weibullian-type
R
exhibits power-exponential tail behaviour, i.e., for large
u
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{R>u\right\}\sim Cu^{\beta}\exp(-Lu^{\gamma}),

where
C,L,\gamma>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Clearly,
R\in\mbox{GMDA}(w,\gamma-1)
with
w(u)=\gamma Lu^{\gamma-1}
.

In this paper, we focus on the heavy-tailed joint risk with regularly varying margins, and with the dependence structure of elliptical copulas (
C_{\Sigma,R}
) whose radius
R
is of Weibullian-type. Our contribution lies in the asymptotic probability on the tail set, the rate of decay in the probability of joint tail events, and the multivariate regular variation/hidden regular variation
(
Resnick, 2002
)
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents some preliminaries about the elliptical distribution, the tail set, and certain definitions of multivariate regular variation. The main results of tail asymptotics and multivariate regular variation are presented in Section
3
. Then we provide examples of multivariate power exponential copula, Laplace copula and Gaussian copula in Section
4
and conduct simulations in Section
5
. Finally, we discuss the applications in Section
6
and conclude this paper in Section
7
. All the proofs are deferred to Section
8
.

2.3.
Tail set, threshold and tail probability

For the tail exceedance probabilities, we consider the tail set
A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}
with thresholds
\boldsymbol{x}_{S}
(cf.
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024, Lemma 1)

(2.1)

\displaystyle A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:y_{j}>x_{j},\forall j\in S\right\},

with

\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>tx_{j},j\in S,X_{j^{\prime}}>0,\,j^{\prime}\notin S\right\}

Note that for a heavy-tailed multivariate risk

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>tx_{j},j\in S,X_{j^{\prime}}>0,\,j^{\prime}\notin S\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{F_{j}(X_{j})>F_{j}(tx_{j}),j\in S\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{Z_{j}>G_{1}^{\leftarrow}(F_{j}(tx_{j})),j\in S\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{Z_{j}>\overline{G}_{1}^{\leftarrow}(\overline{F}_{j}(tx_{j})),j\in S\right\},

where the last step is obtained by
the identically distributed marginals of elliptical
\boldsymbol{Z}
. To show Proposition
2.1

) in , we introduce first Lemmas

2.4.
Regular variation

Besides the asymptotic probability on the tail set, we will also characterize the multivariate regular variation property (see Theorem
3.2
). As all type I elliptical copulas admit asymptotic (tail) independence
(
Frahm et al., 2003
)
, we consider the hidden regular variation with extensions to the sub-cones
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}
’s below (cf.
Resnick (2002)
;
Resnick (2008)
;
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024)
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}:=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:y_{(i)}>0\},\ 1\leq i\leq d,

where
y_{(1)}\geq y_{(2)}\geq\cdots\geq y_{(i)}\geq\cdots\geq y_{(d)}
are the associated decreasing ordered statistic of
y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}
. The sub-cone
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{d}=[0,\infty]^{d}\backslash\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
,
\mathbb{E}^{(2)}_{d}=\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{d}\backslash\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{L}_{i}
, where
\mathbb{L}_{i}:=\{t\boldsymbol{e}_{i},\ t>0\},1\leq i\leq d
, are the axes originating at
\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(d)}\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(2)}\subset\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(1)}.

Definition 2.1

(Multivariate regular variation)
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{Y}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d})
is a random vector on
[0,\infty)^{d}
. The distribution of
\boldsymbol{Y}
is multivariate regularly varying with tail measure
\nu(\cdot)
, if there exists a function
b(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
and a non-negative Radon measure
\nu\neq 0
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{Y}}{b(t)}\in\cdot\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\to}}\nu(\cdot)

on the cone
\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{d}=[0,\infty]^{d}\backslash\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
. Here
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\to}}
stands for vague convergence of measures.

The above condition implies there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that
b(\cdot)\in\mathcal{RV}_{1/\alpha}
and the measure
\nu(\cdot)
is
(-\alpha)
-homogeneous, i.e., for
B\subset\mathbb{E}^{(1)}_{d}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nu(cB)=c^{-\alpha}\nu(B),\quad c>0.

We abbreviate the MRV property as
\boldsymbol{Y}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha,b,\nu,\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(1)})
.

The asymptotic independence means the probability of two or more components being simultaneously large with suitable scales
b(\cdot)
is negligible compared with the probability of one component being large. This also indicates the Radon measure
\nu(\cdot)
concentrates on the axes.

Resnick (2002)
considered the fine structures presented in the interior of the cone with relatively crude normalization
b^{*}(t)
, which is called the hidden regular variation.

Definition 2.2

(Hidden regular variation)
.

The random vector
\boldsymbol{Y}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{d})
has a distribution possessing hidden regular variation if, in addition to Eq.(
2.3
), there exists a non-decreasing function
b^{*}(t)\to\infty
such that
b(t)/b^{*}(t)\to\infty
and there exists a non-negative Radon measure
\nu^{*}\neq 0
on
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(2)}
such that

\displaystyle t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{Y}}{b^{*}(t)}\in\cdot\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\to}}\nu^{*}(\cdot)

on the cone
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(2)}
. Then there exists
\alpha^{*}\geq\alpha
such that
b^{*}\in\mathcal{RV}_{1/\alpha^{*}}
, and
\nu^{*},\alpha^{*}
satisfying the analog of Eq.(
2.4
).

For the intrinsic dependence structure in the sub-cones
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)},i=1,\ldots,d
, we borrow the definition of the multivariate regular variation in
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024, Defninition 3)
, followed by a lemma (cf.
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024, Lemma 1)
) to characterize such properties with tail set
A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}
.

Definition 2.3

(
\mathcal{MRV}
on
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)},1\leq i\leq d
)
.

A random vector
\boldsymbol{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is multivariate regularly varying on
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}
if there exists a regularly varying function
b_{i}\in\mathcal{RV}_{1/\alpha_{i}},\alpha_{i}>0
, and a non-null (Borel) measure
\nu_{i}
which is finite on Borel sets bounded away from
\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:y_{(i)}=0\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{Y}}{b_{i}(t)}\in A\right\}=\nu_{i}(A)

for all Borel sets
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}\right)
which are bounded away from
\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:y_{(i)}=0\right\}
with
\nu_{i}(\partial A)=0
. We write
\boldsymbol{Y}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha_{i},b_{i},\mu_{i},\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)})
. The limit measure
\nu_{i}
obtained is homogeneous of order
-\alpha_{i}
, i.e.,
\nu_{i}(cA)=c^{-\alpha_{i}}\nu_{i}(A)
for any
c>0
.

Lemma 2.3

(
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024, Lemma 1)
)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{Y}
be a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
b_{i}\in\mathcal{RV}_{1/\alpha_{i}}
for
\alpha_{i}>0
is a measurable function and
\nu_{i}
is a non-null (Borel) measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)})
which is finite on Borel sets bounded away from
\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}:y_{(i)}=0\}
. Then
\boldsymbol{Y}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha_{i},b_{i},\nu_{i},\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)})
if and only if

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{Y}}{b_{i}(t)}\in A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}=\nu_{i}(A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}})

for all sets
S\subset\mathbb{I}
with
|S|\geq i
, for all
x_{j}>0,\forall j\in S
such that
\boldsymbol{x}_{S}=(x_{j})_{j\in S}
and

\displaystyle A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}=\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}:y_{j}>x_{j},\forall j\in S\}

with
\nu_{i}\left(\partial A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right)=0
.

3.
Main results

In this section, we begin with Theorem
3.1
below for the asymptotic expansion of the tail probability on the
A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}
and discuss the rate of decay
in the probability of joint tail events. Based on the tail behaviour, we then characterize the multivariate regular variation on the sub-cones
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}
’s.

Recall that
{\boldsymbol{X}}\sim F
has heavy-tailed margins and the same elliptical copula as an elliptical random vector
\boldsymbol{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}RA{\bf U}
. All the parameters
(C,L,\beta,\gamma,C^{\prime},L^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
for Weibullian-type radius
R
defined in Eq.(
1.3
) and for margins
Z_{j}
in Eq.(
8.1
) are used throughout the following paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathrm{RVEC}(\alpha,b,\Sigma,R)
with
R
being Weibullian-type and let
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a positive-definite correlation matrix. For a non-empty set
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}
with
|S|\geq 2
, and let
(\lambda_{S},I_{S},J_{S},\boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*},\Upsilon_{S},h_{j}^{S},\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}})
be defined w.r.t.
\Sigma_{S}
as in Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.1
. Let
A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}
be defined in Eq.(
2.1
) and suppose further that

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{j}(t)\sim c_{j}\overline{F}_{1}(t)=c_{j}\left(t^{\alpha}\ell(t)\right)^{-1},\,c_{j}>0,\quad\log(\ell(t))=o({\log(t)}^{1-1/\gamma}).

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}+1-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}}

\displaystyle\times(\log t)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}+1-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\times(C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}.

Note that all type I elliptical copulas admit the asymptotic (tail) independence property
(
Schmidt, 2002
;
Frahm et al., 2003
)
, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>tx_{j},\forall j\in S\right\}=o(\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{1}>tx_{1}\right\})
as
t\to\infty
, for the tail equivalent risk
\boldsymbol{X}
. The dominant term
(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}
is regularly varying with index
-\alpha\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}\leq-\alpha,\lambda_{S}\geq 1,\gamma>0
, and
\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}
indicates the rate of decay in the joint tail probability compared with the marginal risk. Moreover, the rate only depends on the interested set
S
and the elliptical copula determined by
\Sigma
and
R
, with
\lambda_{S}=\lambda_{S}(\Sigma_{S})
and
\mathbb{P}\left\{R>u\right\}\sim Cu^{\beta}\exp(-Lu^{\gamma})
.

Remark 3.1
.

a) Many distributions, e.g., Pareto, Burr, Lévy, Student’s
t
and Fréchet distributions, have regular varying tails satisfying
\overline{F}(t)=x^{-\alpha}\ell(t)
such that

\displaystyle\log\ell(t)=o({\log(t)}^{1-1/\gamma}),\gamma>1.

b) Theorem
3.1
holds also for
\gamma=1
if
\ell(t)\sim c
for some constant
c>0
. In this case,
\log\mathcal{L}(t)=(\log\ell(t))/L+(\beta^{\prime}\log\log t)/L
and the two terms are absorbed into
\boldsymbol{z}_{S}
and
u(t)
in Eq.(
8.4
), respectively. Therefore,
u(t)\sim\log t
as
\log\log t=o(\log t)
. See the Laplace copula for instance in Example
4.2
.

Remark 3.2
.

Our results extend the Gaussian dependence structure studied by
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024)
to be a general elliptical copula with wide applications (cf. Section
6
). Indeed, a straightforward application of our result into Gaussian copula with
L=1/2,\ \gamma=2,\ \beta^{\prime}=-1,\ C^{\prime}=1/\sqrt{2\pi}
, and identically distributed marginal risks (e.g., all
c_{j}
’s equal 1), indicates
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}(2\alpha)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}}(\log t)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{S}}{2}}(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}}(2\sqrt{\pi\alpha})^{\lambda_{S}}\prod_{j\in S}x_{j}^{-\alpha h_{j}^{S}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}(2\pi)^{\frac{\lambda_{S}}{2}}(2\alpha\log t)^{\frac{\lambda_{S}-|I_{S}|}{2}}(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}}\prod_{j\in S}x_{j}^{-\alpha h_{j}^{S}}.

We see that Eq.(
3.1
) is exactly Theorem 3.1 in
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024)
.

Below, we will consider the multivariate regular variation on the sub-cones
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}
and present our second main theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathrm{RVEC}(\alpha,b,\Sigma,R)
where
R
is Weibullian-type and
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is positive-definite correlation matrix. Under the conditions of Theorem
3.1
,

(a)
For subcone
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(1)}
,
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha,b,\nu_{1},\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(1)})
with

\displaystyle\nu_{1}\left([\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-\alpha},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}.

(b)
For subcones
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}
,
2\leq i\leq d
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{S\subset\mathbb{I}:|S|\geq i,\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{S}}^{\top}\Sigma_{I_{S}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{S}}=\min\limits_{\widetilde{S}\subset\mathbb{I},|\widetilde{S}|\geq i}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{\widetilde{S}}}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}_{I_{\widetilde{S}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{\widetilde{S}}}\right\},

\displaystyle I_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\arg\min\limits_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}|I_{S}|.

Then
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha_{i},b_{i},\nu_{i},\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)})
with

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\Sigma_{I_{i}})=\min\limits_{S\subset\mathbb{I},|S|\geq i}\min\limits_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}\geq\boldsymbol{1}_{S}}\boldsymbol{x}_{S}^{\top}\Sigma_{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{S},

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2},

\displaystyle b_{i}^{\leftarrow}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{{}^{\prime}\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha\log t}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{|I_{i}|-1}{2}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}(1-\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2})}(b^{\leftarrow}(t))^{\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}},

and for the set
A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}=\left\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:y_{j}>x_{j},\forall j\in S\right\}
with
\boldsymbol{x}_{S}>\mathbf{0}
for
S\subseteq\mathbb{I}
and
|S|\geq i
, we have

\displaystyle\nu_{i}(A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}},&\ \text{if}\ S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\ \text{and}\ |I_{S}|=|I_{i}|,\\
0,&\ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Remark 3.3
.

The rates of decay in the sub-cones
\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(i)}
are shown with
\alpha_{i}=\alpha\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2},i=1,\ldots,d
. The result with
\gamma=2
is reduced to the Gaussian-like case in
Das and Fasen-Hartmann (2024, Theorem 2)
. Moreover, for the case with
\gamma=1
, Theorem
3.2
holds with the condition for
\ell(t)\sim c
discussed in Remark
3.1
.

4.
Example

A typical class of elliptical copulas is the so-called multivariate power exponential copula. The original distribution, also named the multivariate generalized Gaussian distribution and studied by
Kano (1994)
;
Gómez et al. (1998)
, are widely applied in biostatistics, bioinformatics, and engineering
(
Dang et al., 2015
)
.
A random vector
\boldsymbol{Z}
follows a
d
-dimensional power exponential distribution, denoted by
\boldsymbol{Z}\sim PE_{d}(\boldsymbol{\mu},{\Sigma},\kappa)
, if its density is

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{z},\boldsymbol{\mu},{\Sigma},\kappa)=c_{d}|{\Sigma}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left[(\mathbf{z}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\prime}{\Sigma}^{-1}(\mathbf{z}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\right]^{\kappa}\right\},

where
c_{d}={d\Gamma(d/2)}/\left(\pi^{d/2}\Gamma(1+{d}/({2\kappa}))2^{1+{d}/({2\kappa})}\right)
. Moreover, the
\boldsymbol{Z}
is an elliptical risk with
{\boldsymbol{Z}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\boldsymbol{\mu}+RA{\bf U}
. Here the radius
R>0
, being independent of uniform distributed
\bf U
in
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, has the following density function

(4.1)

\displaystyle h_{R}(r)=\frac{d}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{d}{2\kappa}\right)2^{\frac{d}{2\kappa}}}r^{d-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}r^{2\kappa}\right\},\quad r>0.

Power exponential distributions consist of many well-known risks, including the Laplace distribution (
\kappa=1/2
) and Gaussian distribution (
\kappa=1
). In the following context, we first verify the radius
R
is Weibullian-type by Mill’s ratio, then discuss the solution to the quadratic programming problem with a correlation matrix
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
and equi-correlation matrix
\Sigma_{\rho}\ (\rho_{jk}=\rho\mbox{ for }j\neq k\in\{1,2,3\}
). Finally, we illustrate the examples of the power exponential copula, Laplace copula and Gaussian copula.

Lemma 4.1

(Mills’ ratio inequality)
.

Let
h(r)
be the density of radius
R
in Eq.(
4.1
). For
r>0
, the inequalities

\displaystyle\frac{r}{\kappa(r^{2\kappa}+2)}h(r)\leq\int_{r}^{\infty}h(u)du\leq\frac{1}{\kappa r^{2\kappa-1}}h(r)

hold and for
r\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}h(u)du\approx\frac{1}{\kappa r^{2\kappa-1}}h(r).

Remark 4.1
.

For a multivariate power exponential random vector
\boldsymbol{Z}=RA\boldsymbol{U}
has a density in the form of Eq.(
4.1
). For large
u>0
, by Theorem
4.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{R>u\right\}\sim\frac{1}{\Gamma\left({d}/{\gamma}\right)2^{\frac{d}{\gamma}-1}}u^{d-\gamma}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}u^{\gamma}\right\},\quad\gamma=2\kappa>0,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{Z_{j}>u\right\}\sim\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma(1/2)\Gamma(d/\gamma)2^{d/\gamma}}\biggr(\frac{\gamma L}{2}\biggl)^{-(d-1)/2}u^{d-\gamma(d+1)/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}u^{\gamma}\right\},

indicating that the radius in the power exponential distribution is Weibullian-type.

For a positive definite correlation matrix

\displaystyle\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho_{12}&\rho_{13}\\
\rho_{12}&1&\rho_{23}\\
\rho_{13}&\rho_{23}&1\end{pmatrix},

where
\rho_{jk}\in(-1,1),\forall j\neq k
, we have
|\Sigma|=1-\rho_{12}^{2}-\rho_{13}^{2}-\rho_{23}^{2}+2\rho_{12}\rho_{13}\rho_{23}
and

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-1}=\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\begin{pmatrix}1-\rho_{23}^{2}&\rho_{13}\rho_{23}-\rho_{12}&\rho_{12}\rho_{23}-\rho_{13}\\
\rho_{13}\rho_{23}-\rho_{12}&1-\rho_{13}^{2}&\rho_{12}\rho_{13}-\rho_{23}\\
\rho_{12}\rho_{23}-\rho_{13}&\rho_{12}\rho_{13}-\rho_{23}&1-\rho_{12}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Suppose
|\Sigma|>0
and
\boldsymbol{h}:=\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}>\boldsymbol{0}
. For symmetric
\Sigma^{-1}
,
h_{i}:=\boldsymbol{e}_{i}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}_{i},\ i=1,2,3
, which is defined in Lemma
8.3
, then applying Theorem
3.2
,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{I_{2}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\rho_{*}^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\rho_{*}\\
-\rho_{*}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho_{*}=\max\limits_{1\leq j\neq k\leq 3}\rho_{jk},

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\Sigma_{I_{2}})=\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{2}}^{\top}\Sigma_{I_{2}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{2}}=\frac{2}{1+\rho_{*}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\Sigma)=\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}.

For an equi-correlation matrix
\Sigma_{\rho}
and
|\Sigma_{\rho}|=(1-\rho)^{2}(1+2\rho)
, let
\rho\in(-1/2,1)
be a constant keeping the matrix positive definite, then

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rho}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\rho)^{2}(1+2\rho)}\begin{pmatrix}1-\rho^{2}&\rho^{2}-\rho&\rho^{2}-\rho\\
\rho^{2}-\rho&1-\rho^{2}&\rho^{2}-\rho\\
\rho^{2}-\rho&\rho^{2}-\rho&1-\rho^{2}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{h}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\rho}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1+2\rho}\\
\frac{1}{1+2\rho}\\
\frac{1}{1+2\rho}\end{pmatrix}>\boldsymbol{0}.

By Theorem
3.2
, one choice is
I_{2}=\{1,2\}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}
with

\displaystyle\Sigma_{I_{2}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\rho^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\rho\\
-\rho&1\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\Sigma_{I_{2}})=\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{2}}^{\top}\Sigma_{I_{2}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{2}}=\frac{2}{1+\rho},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\Sigma)=\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}=\frac{3}{1+2\rho}.

Example 4.1

(Power exponential copula)
.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{X}=(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})\in\mathrm{RVEC}(\alpha,b,\Sigma_{\rho},R)
has the regularly varying marginal tails and the power exponential copula, where
\Sigma_{\rho}
is positive definite with
-1/2<\rho<1
. Also assume that
\overline{F}_{1}(t)=\left(t^{\alpha}\ell(t)\right)^{-1}
satisfying
\log(\ell(t))=o({\log(t)}^{1-1/\gamma}),\gamma>1
.
Thus, it follows by Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
that, for
S\subset\{1,2,3\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}(2\alpha\log t)^{\frac{3}{\gamma}-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}-\left(2-\frac{3}{\gamma}\right)\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\times(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(2^{\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma}}\Gamma(3/{\gamma})\gamma)^{\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}

and
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha_{i},b_{i},\nu_{i},\mathbb{E}_{3}^{(i)}),i=1,2,3
, with

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=1,\ \lambda_{2}=\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{2}}^{\top}\Sigma_{I_{2}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{2}}=\frac{2}{1+\rho},\ \lambda_{3}=\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}=\frac{3}{1+2\rho},

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\alpha,\ \alpha_{2}=\alpha\lambda_{2}^{\gamma/2}=\alpha\biggr(\frac{2}{1+\rho}\biggl)^{\gamma/2},\ \alpha_{3}=\alpha\lambda_{3}^{\gamma/2}=\alpha\biggr(\frac{3}{1+2\rho}\biggl)^{\gamma/2},

\displaystyle b_{1}^{\leftarrow}(t)=b^{\leftarrow}(t),

\displaystyle b_{2}^{\leftarrow}(t)=(2^{\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma}}\Gamma(3/{\gamma})\gamma)^{-\lambda_{2}^{\gamma/2}}(2\alpha\log t)^{\left(\frac{3}{\gamma}-2\right)\lambda_{2}^{\gamma/2}+\frac{5}{2}-\frac{3}{\gamma}}(b^{\leftarrow}(t))^{\lambda_{2}^{\gamma/2}},

\displaystyle b_{3}^{\leftarrow}(t)=(2^{\frac{3-\gamma}{\gamma}}\Gamma(3/{\gamma})\gamma)^{-\lambda_{3}^{\gamma/2}}(2\alpha\log t)^{\left(\frac{3}{\gamma}-2\right)\lambda_{3}^{\gamma/2}+3-\frac{3}{\gamma}}(b^{\leftarrow}(t))^{\lambda_{3}^{\gamma/2}}

and

\displaystyle\nu_{1}\left([\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{3}x_{j}^{-\alpha},

\displaystyle\nu_{2}(A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}})=\begin{cases}\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}},&\ \text{if}\ S\in\mathcal{S}_{2}\ \text{and}\ |I_{S}|=2,\\
0,&\ \text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\nu_{3}(A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}})=\begin{cases}\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}},&\ \mbox{if}\ S\in\mathcal{S}_{3}\ \mbox{and}\ |I_{S}|=3,\\
0,&\ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

7.
Conclusion

In conclusion, our study focuses on the probability of tail sets of regularly varying risks under the elliptical copula with a Weibullian-type radius. We provide the asymptotic expansion of the tail probability, demonstrating the connection between the rate of decay in the probability for joint events and the parameters in the elliptical copulas. Additionally, we characterize the multivariate regular variation on the sub-cones, provide illustrative examples and verify the theoretical result through simulations. These findings are valuable for risk measures, risk management and computing tail probabilities for various extreme tail events, with wide applications in finance, insurance, environment and biology.
Furthermore, as a generalization of the Gaussian case, we highlight the potential improvement in the fit of insurance data with elliptical copulas (e.g., Laplace copula). Further work is promising in developing statistical tools for the tail and dependence parameter involved in the heavy tailed risk with elliptical copula, as well as the tail study with asymmetric elliptical dependence.

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta^{*}}(u(t))^{2\beta^{*}}\left(1+\frac{2\beta^{*}}{\lambda_{S}u^{\gamma}(t)}(\boldsymbol{z}_{S}+\log\mathcal{L}(t)\boldsymbol{1}_{S})^{\top}\Sigma_{S}^{-1}\boldsymbol{e}_{S}^{*})\right)

\displaystyle\times(u(t))^{-|I_{S}|}\mathcal{L}(t)^{-L\gamma\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\ t^{-\alpha\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{j\in I_{S}}\exp\left(-h_{j}^{S}L\gamma\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}\log[(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha}C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{1/\gamma L}]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta^{*}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha\log t}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{2\beta^{*}}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\frac{2\beta^{*}\sum_{j\in I_{S}}h_{j}^{S}\log(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha}C^{\prime}\alpha^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}}L^{-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}})}{\alpha\gamma\lambda_{S}\log t}+\frac{2\beta^{*}\log\left(\ell(t)(\log t)^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}}\right)}{\alpha\gamma\log t}\right)

\displaystyle\times\biggr(\frac{\alpha}{L}\biggl)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{\gamma}}(\log t)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{\gamma}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\times(C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}\left(\frac{x_{j}^{\alpha}}{c_{j}}\right)^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}.

Dropping the second and third terms in

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\beta^{*}\sum_{j\in I_{S}}h_{j}^{S}\log(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha}C^{\prime}\alpha^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}}L^{-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}})}{\alpha\gamma\lambda_{S}\log t}+\frac{2\beta^{*}\log\left(\ell(t)(\log t)^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}}\right)}{\alpha\gamma\log t},

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha}{L}\biggl)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|-2\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}{\gamma}}(\log t)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{\gamma}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}+\frac{2\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\times(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}+1-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}+1-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\times(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}.

Consequently, Theorem
3.1
is obtained.
∎

Next, we will present the proof of Theorem
3.2
by utilizing Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

For any
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
,
\nu_{1}\left(\partial[\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right)=0
always hold. By the inclusion-exclusion principle and tail equivalence (with index
\alpha_{1}
) for all margins of
\boldsymbol{X}
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{b_{1}(t)}\in[\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>b_{1}(t)x_{j}\right\}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-\alpha_{1}},

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{b_{1}(t)}\in[\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>b_{1}(t)x_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{j,k=1;j\neq k}^{d}\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>b_{1}(t)x_{j},X_{k}>b_{1}(t)x_{k}\right\}.

a) Since
\mathbb{P}\left\{X_{j}>b_{1}(t)x_{j},X_{k}>b_{1}(t)x_{k}\right\}=o(t^{-1})
for covariance
|\rho_{jk}|<1
in matrix
\Sigma=AA^{\top}
, according to tail independence property for the type I elliptical copula
(
Schmidt, 2002
;
Frahm et al., 2003
)
, the risk
\boldsymbol{X}\in\mathcal{MRV}(\alpha_{1},b_{1},\nu_{1},\mathbb{E}_{d}^{(1)})
and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{b_{1}(t)}\in[\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right\}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{-\alpha_{1}}=\nu_{1}\left([\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{x}]^{c}\right).

b) For
2\leq i\leq d
, by Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}t\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\boldsymbol{X}}{b_{i}(t)}\in A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}b_{i}^{\leftarrow}(t)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}=\nu_{i}(A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}).

Using Theorem
3.1
, we have the asymptotic tail probability

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}+1-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}}(\log t)^{\frac{\beta}{\gamma}+1-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}-\frac{|S|}{2}-\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}

\displaystyle\times(b^{\leftarrow}(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Z_j}}}{{\sim}}

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}}(\log t)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}(1-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2})}

\displaystyle\times(b^{\leftarrow}(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}(C^{\prime}\alpha^{\beta^{\prime}/\gamma}L^{-\beta^{\prime}/\gamma})^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}C^{{}^{\prime}-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha\log t}{L}\biggl)^{-\frac{|I_{S}|}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}(1-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2})}

\displaystyle\times(b^{\leftarrow}(t))^{-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left\{S\subset\mathbb{I}:|S|\geq i,\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{S}}^{\top}\Sigma_{I_{S}}^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{S}}=\min\limits_{\widetilde{S}\subset\mathbb{I},|\widetilde{S}|\geq i}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{\widetilde{S}}}^{\top}\Sigma^{-1}_{I_{\widetilde{S}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{I_{\widetilde{S}}}\right\},

\displaystyle I_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\arg\min\limits_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}|I_{S}|,

implying that, if
S\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}
, then
\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{S}
, while if
S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
, then
|I_{i}|\leq|I_{S}|
.
Hence if
S\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}
, then
\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}<\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}
(
\gamma>0
), and

\displaystyle b_{i}^{\leftarrow}(t)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle C^{{}^{\prime}\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}

\displaystyle\times(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha\log t}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{|I_{i}|-|I_{S}|}{2}+\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\gamma}(\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2})},

where
(t^{\alpha}\ell(t))^{\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}-\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}}
dominates the tail behaviour and
g_{i}(t)=b_{i}^{\leftarrow}(t)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}\to 0
, i.e.,
g_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{RV}_{-a}
with
a=\alpha(\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}-\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2})>0
.

If
S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
and
|I_{i}|<|I_{S}|
, then
\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}=\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}
, and

\displaystyle g_{i}(t)=b_{i}^{\leftarrow}(t)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}\biggr(\frac{\alpha\log t}{L}\biggl)^{\frac{|I_{i}|-|I_{S}|}{2}}\to 0.

If
S\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
and
|I_{i}|=|I_{S}|
, then
\lambda_{i}^{\gamma/2}=\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2}
, and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}b_{i}^{\leftarrow}(t)\mathbb{P}\left\{\boldsymbol{X}\in tA_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{S}\lambda_{S}^{\beta_{\gamma,I_{S},J_{S}}}\prod_{j\in I_{S}}(c_{j}^{-1}x_{j}^{\alpha})^{-h_{j}^{S}\lambda_{S}^{\gamma/2-1}}=\nu_{i}(A_{\boldsymbol{x}_{S}}).

Consequently, the claim in Theorem
3.2
follows.
∎

The Mill’s ratio for Weibullian type radius is derived analogously from the traditional method for Gaussian distribution.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Recalling the density
h(r)=Cr^{d-1}\exp\left(-{{r}^{2\kappa}}/{2}\right),\,r>0,\kappa>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}h(u)du\leq\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{u^{2\kappa-d}}{r^{2\kappa-d}}Cu^{d-1}\exp\left(-\frac{{u}^{2\kappa}}{2}\right)du=-\frac{C}{\kappa r^{2\kappa-d}}\exp\left(-\frac{{u}^{2\kappa}}{2}\right)\bigg|_{r}^{\infty}=\frac{h(r)}{\kappa r^{2\kappa-1}}.

Noting further that
h^{\prime}(r)=((d-1)r^{-1}-\kappa r^{2\kappa-1})h(r)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\kappa+\frac{2\kappa}{r^{2\kappa}}\right)\int_{r}^{\infty}h(u)du

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(\kappa+\frac{2\kappa}{u^{2\kappa}}\right)h(u)du

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\left(\kappa+\frac{2\kappa-d}{u^{2\kappa}}\right)h(u)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{(\kappa u^{2\kappa-1}-(d-1)u^{-1})h(u)u^{2\kappa-1}}{u^{4\kappa-2}}+\frac{(2\kappa-1)u^{2\kappa-2}}{u^{4\kappa-2}}h(u)du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{h(u)}{u^{2\kappa-1}}\bigg|_{r}^{\infty}=\frac{h(r)}{r^{2\kappa-1}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{\infty}h(u)du\geq\frac{r}{\kappa(r^{2\kappa}+2)}h(r),

which completes the proof.
∎

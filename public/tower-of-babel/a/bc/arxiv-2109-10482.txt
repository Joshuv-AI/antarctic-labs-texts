On the comparison between jump processes and subordinated diffusions
Guanhua Liu, Mathav Murugan

Abstract
Given a symmetric diffusion process and a jump process on the same underlying space, is there a subordinator such that the jump process and the subordinated diffusion processes are comparable? We address this question when the diffusion satisfies a sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimate and the jump process satisfies a polynomial-type jump kernel bounds. Under these assumptions , we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions on the jump kernel estimate for such a subordinator to exist. As an application of our results and the recent stability results of Chen, Kumagai and Wang, we obtain parabolic Harnack inequality for a large family of jump processes. In particular, we show that any jump process with polynomial-type jump kernel bounds on such a space satisfy the parabolic Harnack inequality. Keywords: subordination, jump processes, diffusions, parabolic Harnack inequality

Introduction

Let

The jump kernel of the

The processes

where

A well-known important application of (

In this work, we investigate the extent to which subordination can be used to jump processes. In particular, our work addresses the following questions:

(a)

Let

\mu
-symmetric diffusion that satisfies the parabolic Harnack inequality. Given a
\mu
-symmetric jump process

S

Y
?

(b)

In the setting above, does the jump process
Y

Under fairly mild conditions on the jump kernel, we obtain a positive answer to both these questions.
Our answer to question (a) seems to be new even on

2.2
Parabolic Harnack inequality

We recall the definition of parabolic Harnack inequality and it’s relationship to heat kernel bounds. Let

({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be a Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
and let
I
be an open interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
. We say that a function
u:I\rightarrow L^{2}(M,\mu)
is weakly differentiable at
t_{0}\in I
if the function
t\mapsto\langle u(t),f\rangle
is differentiable at
t_{0}
for all
f\in L^{2}(M,\mu)
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(M,\mu)
. By the uniform boundedness principle, there exists a (unique) function
w\in L^{2}(M,\mu)
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow t_{0}}\left\langle\frac{u(t)-u(t_{0})}{t-t_{0}},f\right\rangle=\langle w,f\rangle,\quad\mbox{ for all $f\in L^{2}(M,\mu)$.}

In this case, we say that the function
w
above is the
weak derivative
of
u
at
t_{0}
and write
w=u^{\prime}(t_{0})
.
Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
M
.
A function
u:I\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}
is said to be
caloric
in
I\times\Omega
if
u
is weakly differentiable in the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
at any
t\in I
, and for any
f\in{\mathcal{F}}\cap C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
, and for any
t\in I
,

\langle u^{\prime},f\rangle+{\mathcal{E}}(u,f)=0.

(2.8)

Definition 2.1

(Parabolic Harnack inequality)
.

Let
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be a Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(M,\mu)
and let
\psi
be a scale function.
We say that an MMD space
(X,d,m,{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
satisfies the
parabolic Harnack inequality
with walk dimension
\beta
(abbreviated as
\operatorname{PHI(\psi)}
),
if there exist
0<C_{1}<C_{2}<C_{3}<C_{4}<\infty
,
C_{5}>1
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that for all
x\in X
,

and for any non-negative bounded caloric function

\esssup_{Q_{-}}u\leq C_{5}\essinf_{Q_{+}}u,

\operatorname{PHI(\psi)}

where
Q_{-}=(a+\psi(C_{1}r),a+\psi(C_{2}r))\times B(x,\delta r)
and
Q_{+}=(a+\psi(C_{3}r),a+\psi(C_{4}r)s)\times B(x,\delta r)
.

We recall the following sub-Gaussian heat kernel estimate that is equivalent to the above parabolic Harnack inequality.

Let
({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
be a strongly local, regular, Dirichlet form on

and let
\psi
be a scale function. We say that the Dirichlet form

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{m\bigl(B(x,\psi^{-1}(t))\bigr)}\exp\bigl(-c_{1}\Phi(c_{2}d(x,y),t)\bigr)\qquad\mbox{for $\mu$-a.e.\ $x,y\in M$,}

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{3}}{m\bigl(B(x,\psi^{-1}(t))\bigr)}\qquad\mbox{for $\mu$-a.e.\ $x,y\in M$ with $d(x,y)\leq\delta\psi^{-1}(t)$,}

where

\Phi(R,t):=\Phi_{\psi}(R,t):=\sup_{r>0}\biggl(\frac{R}{r}-\frac{t}{\psi(r)}\biggr),\qquad\mbox{for all $R\geq 0,t>0$.}

(2.11)

We recall volume doubling and reverse volume doubling properties of a metric measure space.
We say that a metric measure space

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq\mu(B(x,r)),\qquad\mbox{for all $x\in M,r>0$.}

2.3
Subordinator and Lévy measure

A subordinator
(S_{t})
is a non-decreasing Lévy process with
S_{0}=0
; that is,
(S_{t})
has independent, stationary increments such that
t\mapsto S_{t}
is continuous in probability. A subordinator is characterized by its
Laplace exponent

\phi:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that

{\mathbb{E}}e^{-\lambda S_{t}}=e^{-t\phi(\lambda)},

(2.12)

where
\phi
is a
Bernstein function
determined by its
drift

a\in[0,\infty)
and
Lévy measure
of the subordinator
\nu
, where
\nu
is a Borel measure on
(0,\infty)
such that

\int_{(0,\infty)}(1\wedge s)\,\nu(ds)<\infty,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\phi(\lambda)=a\lambda+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-s\lambda})\,\nu(ds).

(2.13)

Conversely, any drift
a\in[0,\infty)
and Borel measure
\nu
on
(0,\infty)
that satisfy (
2.13
) uniquely determine the subordinator
(S_{t})
.
We refer the reader to
[
SSV
, Chapter 5]
or
[
Sat
, Chapter 6]
for background on subordinators.

Notation
. In the following, we will use the notation

for quantities

and

to indicate the existence of an
implicit constant
C\geq 1
depending on some inessential parameters such that

. We write

and

Statement of the main results

We now state the main results of this work.
The following theorem characterizes all polyomial type jump kernels on a metric measure space that admits a diffusion satsfying parabolic Harnack inequality.

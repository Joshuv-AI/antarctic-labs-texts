A semigroup approach to stochastic systems with input delay at the boundary
S. Hadd, F.Z. Lahbiri

Abstract
. This work focuses on the well-posedness of abstract stochastic linear systems with boundary input delay and unbounded observation operators. We use product spaces and a semigroup approach to reformulate such delay systems into free-delay distributed stochastic systems with unbounded control and observation operators. This gives us the opportunity to use the concept of admissible control and observation operators as well as the concept of Yosida extensions to prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution process and provide an estimation of the observation process in relation to initial conditions and control process. As an example, we consider a stochastic Schrödinger system with input delay.

Some classical background on semigroup theory is also required. Let
A:D(A)\subset H\to H
be the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
. We denote by
\rho(A)

A
, that is, the set of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that the inverse
(\lambda I-A)^{-1}:=R(\lambda,A)
exists (which is linear bounded due to the closed graph theorem). The spectrum of
A
is the set
\sigma(A)=\mathbb{C}\backslash\rho(A)
. The type of the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is defined by
\omega_{0}(A):=\inf_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\log\|T(t)\|
. According to
[
11
, chap.II, Section 5]
, if
\beta>\omega_{0}(A)
, then
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re}\lambda>\beta\}\subset\rho(A)
and there exists
M\geq 1
such that
\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{\beta t}
for any
t\geq 0
. On one hand, the domain
D(A)
endowed with the following graph norm
\|x\|_{1}:=\|x\|+\|Ax\|,\;x\in D(A),
is a Banach space. On the other hand, for
\alpha\in\rho(A),
we define a new norm
\|x\|_{-1}=\|R(\lambda,A)x\|
for
x\in X
. This norm is independent of
\alpha
. We now define
X_{-1}
as the completion of
X
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{-1}
, which means that
X_{-1}=\overline{X}^{\|\cdot\|_{-1}}
. This space is called the extrapolation space associated with
A
and
X
. As
H
is a Hilbert space, then it is known that
X_{-1}
is isomorphic to the topological dual
D(A^{\ast})^{\prime},
where
A^{\ast}
is the adjoint operator of
A
. Furthermore, the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is extended to another strongly continuous semigroup
(T_{-1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X_{-1},
whose generator
A_{-1}:X\to X_{-1}
is the extension of
A
to
X
, see
[
11
, Chap.II, Theorem 5.5]
.

Throughout this section we use the following notations:
(\mathfrak{X},|\cdot|)
and
(\partial\mathfrak{X},\|\cdot\|_{\partial\mathfrak{X}})
are Hilbert space,
\mathfrak{A}_{m}:D(\mathfrak{A}_{m})\subset\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{X}

\Theta:D(\mathfrak{A}_{m})\to\partial\mathfrak{X}

2.1.
Admissible observation operators

Consider the observed system

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}_{m}z(t),\quad z(0)=x,&t>0,\cr\Theta z(t)=0,&t\geq 0,\cr y(t)=\Upsilon z(t),&t\geq 0,\end{cases}

where
\Upsilon:D(\mathfrak{A}_{m})\to\partial\mathfrak{X}
is a linear operator (not necessarily closed or closeable). In what follow, we assume that the linear operator

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}:=\left(\mathfrak{A}_{m}\right)_{|D(\mathfrak{A})}\quad\text{with domain}\quad D(\mathfrak{A}):=\ker\Theta

generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(\mathfrak{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathfrak{X}
. We consider the linear operator

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}:=\Upsilon\quad\text{with}\quad D(\mathfrak{C}):=D(\mathfrak{A}).

The boundary system (
2.1
) can be reformulated as the following distributed system

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}z(t),\quad z(0)=x,&t>0,\cr y(t)=\mathfrak{C}z(t),&t\geq 0,\end{cases}

Observe that for the initial condition
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, the function
z(t)=\mathfrak{T}(t)x,\;t\geq 0,
is the mild solution of the differential equation in (
2.4
). However, the expression
y(t;x)=\mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{T}(t)x
is well defined only if
x\in D(\mathfrak{A})
. The observed linear system (
2.4
) (or (
2.1
)) is called well-posed if for any
x\in\mathfrak{X},
the output function
t\mapsto y(t;x)
can be extended to a function (denoted by the same symbol)
y(\cdot;x)\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty),\partial\mathfrak{X})
such that
\|y(\cdot;x)\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})}\leq\gamma|x|,
for any
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and
\alpha>0,
where
\gamma:=\gamma(\alpha)>0
is a constant.

In the following we give conditions for which the system (
2.4
) is well-posed. To this end, we need the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

The operator
\mathfrak{C}\in{\mathcal{L}}(D(\mathfrak{A}),\partial\mathfrak{X})
is called an admissible observation operator for
\mathfrak{A},
if for some (hence all)
\alpha>0,
there exists a constant
\gamma:=\gamma(\alpha)>0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int^{\alpha}_{0}\|\mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{T}(t)x\|^{2}_{\partial\mathfrak{X}}dt\leq\gamma^{2}|x|^{2}

for any
x\in D(\mathfrak{A})
. In this case, we also say that
(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{A})
is admissible.

Now if
(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{A})
is admissible then the system (
2.4
) is well-posed. In fact, the estimate (
2.5
) together with the density of
D(\mathfrak{A})
in
\mathfrak{X}
imply that the following map

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}}:D(\mathfrak{A})\to L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X}),\quad x\mapsto\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}}x:=\mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{T}(\cdot)x=y(\cdot;x)

has a unique linear bounded extension to
\mathfrak{X}
(denoted by the same symbol)
\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathfrak{X},L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X}))
. In order to give a representation of
\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}},
let us consider the following operator (called the
Yosida extension
of
\mathfrak{C}
for
\mathfrak{A}
),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}D(\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda})&=\left\{z\in\mathfrak{X}:\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\mathfrak{C}\lambda R(\lambda,\mathfrak{A})z\;\text{exists in}\;\partial\mathfrak{X}\right\}\cr\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda}z&:=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}\mathfrak{C}\lambda R(\lambda,\mathfrak{A})z.\end{split}

It is shown in
[
32
]
that if
\mathfrak{C}
is an admissible observation operator for
\mathfrak{A}
, then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}(t)x\in D(\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda}),\quad\text{and}\quad(\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}}x)(t)=\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{T}(t)x,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{X},\;a.e.\;t>0.

Thus if
(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{A})
is well-posed, then the extended output function of the system (
2.4
) is

\displaystyle y(t;x)=\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{T}(t)x

for any
x\in X,
and a.e.
t>0
.

2.2.
Admissible control operators

Consider the following boundary control system

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}_{m}z(t),\quad z(0)=x,&t>0,\cr\Theta z(t)=u(t),&t\geq 0,\end{cases}

where
u:[0,+\infty)\to\partial\mathfrak{X}
is the control function assumed to be a
2
-integrable function. According to
[
14
]
, the fact that
\Theta
is surjective implies that the domain
D(\mathfrak{A}_{m})
can be viewed as the direct sum of
D(\mathfrak{A})
and
\ker(\lambda-\mathfrak{A}_{m})
for any
\lambda\in\rho(\mathfrak{A})
. In particular, the following inverse exists

\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}:=(\Theta_{|\ker(\lambda-\mathfrak{A}_{m})})^{-1}:\partial\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\ker(\lambda-\mathfrak{A}_{m}),\qquad\lambda\in\rho(\mathfrak{A}).

The operator
\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}
is called the
Dirichlet operator
associated with
\mathfrak{A}_{m}
and
\Theta
, and satisfies
\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\partial\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{X})
for any
\lambda\in\rho(\mathfrak{A})
. We select the following operator

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=(\lambda-\mathfrak{A}_{-1})\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\partial\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{X}_{-1}),

where
\mathfrak{A}_{-1}:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{X}
is the generator of the extrapolation semigroup extension of
(\mathfrak{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
(see notations in the introductory section). As for any
v\in\partial\mathfrak{X}
and
\lambda\in\rho(\mathfrak{A}),
we have
\mathfrak{A}_{m}\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}v=\lambda\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}v,
then
(\mathfrak{A}_{m}-\mathfrak{A}_{-1})\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}v=(\lambda-\mathfrak{A}_{-1})\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}v=\mathfrak{B}v
. As
\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda}
is the inverse of
\Theta,
we obtain

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}_{m}=(\mathfrak{A}_{-1}+\mathfrak{B}\Theta)_{|D(\mathfrak{A}_{m})}.

Using the equality (
2.11
), the boundary system (
2.13
) can be reformulated as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}z(t)+\mathfrak{B}u(t),\quad z(0)=x,\;t>0.

The mild (or integral) solution of the nonhomogeneous equation (
2.12
) is given by

\displaystyle z(t)=\mathfrak{T}(t)x+\int^{t}_{0}\mathfrak{T}_{-1}(t-s)\mathfrak{B}u(s)ds

for any
t\geq 0,\;x\in\mathfrak{X}
and
u\in L^{2}([0,+\infty),\partial\mathfrak{X})
. This solution satisfies
z(t)\in\mathfrak{X}_{-1}
for
t>0
. Usually, we are interested in having
\mathfrak{X}
-valued solution of the equation (
2.12
). To that purpose, we need the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

The operator
\mathfrak{B}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\partial\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{X}_{-1})
is called an admissible control operator for
\mathfrak{A}
if there exists
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{\tau}u:=\int^{\tau}_{0}\mathfrak{T}_{-1}(\tau-s)\mathfrak{B}u(s)ds\in\mathfrak{X}

for any
u\in L^{2}([0,+\infty,\partial\mathfrak{X})
. In this case, we also say that
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B})
is admissible.

Using the closed graph theorem, it is shown in
[
33
]
that if
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B})
is admissible, then

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{2}([0,+\infty),\partial\mathfrak{X}),\mathfrak{X}),\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

Thus if
\mathfrak{B}
is an admissible control operator for
\mathfrak{A},
the solution of (
2.12
) is continuous function from
[0,+\infty)
to
\mathfrak{X}
.

2.3.
Well-posed linear systems

The object of this part it to define the concept of well-posedess for boundary control systems with a point observation. In fact, we consider the augmented system

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}_{m}z(t),\quad z(0)=x,&t>0,\cr\Theta z(t)=u(t),&t\geq 0,\cr y(t)=\Upsilon z(t),&t\geq 0,\end{cases}

where the operators
\mathfrak{A}_{m},\Theta
and
\Upsilon
are defined before as above. According to the previous two subsection, the system (
2.13
) can be reformulated as the following input-output distributed linear system

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}z(t)+\mathfrak{B}u(t),\quad z(0)=x,&t>0,\cr y(t)=\mathfrak{C}z(t),&t\geq 0,\end{cases}

where the operators
\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}
and
\mathfrak{B}
are given by (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.10
), respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that the system (
2.14
) (or (
2.13
)) is well-posed if it admits a continuous mild solution
z:[0,+\infty)\to\mathfrak{X}
and an output function
t\mapsto y(t;x,u)
that can be extended to a locally
2
-integrable function on
[0,+\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\|y(\cdot;x,u)\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})}\leq\kappa\left(|x|+\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})}\right)

for any
x\in X,\alpha>0
and
u\in L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})
, where
\kappa:=\kappa(\alpha)>0
is a constant.

Remark 1
.

(i) As discussed in Subsection
2.2
, a sufficient condition for the existence of a continuous solution
z:[0,+\infty)\to\mathfrak{X}
of the differential equation in (
2.14
) is that
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B})
is well-posed.

(ii) Even if
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B})
is well-posed, the expression
"\mathfrak{C}z(t)"
is not well defined for a general initial condition
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and all control
u\in L^{2}([0,+\infty),\partial\mathfrak{C})
. To overcome this obstacle, for any
t>0,
we define the space

\displaystyle u\in W^{2,2}_{0,t}(\partial\mathfrak{X}):=\{u\in W^{2,2}([0,t],\partial\mathfrak{X}):u(0)=\dot{u}(0)=0\}.

This space is dense in
L^{2}([0,t],\partial\mathfrak{X})
. Now assume (without loss of generality)
0\in\rho(\mathfrak{A})
and take
u\in W^{2,2}_{0,t}(\partial\mathfrak{X})
. Then an integration by parts implies

\displaystyle\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{t}u

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}\mathfrak{T}_{-1}(t-s)(-\mathfrak{A}_{-1})\mathfrak{D}_{0}u(s)ds

\displaystyle=\left[\mathfrak{T}(t-s)\mathfrak{D}_{0}u(s)\right]^{s=t}_{s=0}-\int^{t}_{0}\mathfrak{T}(t-s)\mathfrak{D}_{0}\dot{u}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}_{0}u(t)-\int^{t}_{0}\mathfrak{T}(t-s)\mathfrak{D}_{0}\dot{u}(s)ds.

Thus
\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{t}u\in D(\mathfrak{A}_{m})
for
t\geq 0
. Moreover, for any
(x,u)\in D(\mathfrak{A})\times W^{2,2}_{0,\alpha}(\partial\mathfrak{X}),
for any
t\in[0,\alpha],
we have

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=\Upsilon(\mathfrak{T}(t)x+\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{t}u)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}\mathfrak{T}(t)x+\Upsilon\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{t}u

\displaystyle=\left(\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}}x+{\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}u\right)(t),

where
\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{A}}
is defined in (
2.6
) and

(2.15)

\displaystyle\left({\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}u\right)(t)=\Upsilon\Phi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}_{t}u.

By combining Definition
2.3
and Remark
1
one can easily prove the following result.

Proposition 1
.

The input-output system (
2.14
) (or (
2.13
)) is well-posed if and only if the following assertions hold

(i)

(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B})
is admissible.

(ii)

(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{A})
is admissible.

(iii)

There exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}u\right\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})}\leq\kappa\|u\|_{L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})}

for any
u\in W^{2,2}_{0,\alpha}(\partial\mathfrak{X})
, where
{\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B}}
is the linear operator define in (
2.15
). In this case we also say that
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C})
is well-posed triple.

If the system (
2.14
) is well-posed, then the density of
W^{2,2}_{0,\alpha}(\partial\mathfrak{X})
in
L^{2}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X})
and the inequality (
2.16
) imply that the operator
{\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}
is extended to a linear bounded operator (denoted by the same symbol)
{\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}([0,\alpha],\partial\mathfrak{X}))
for any
\alpha>0
. Furthermore, the extended output function of the system (
2.14
) is given by

(2.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}y(t;x,u)&=\left(\Psi^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{C}}x+\textbf{F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}u\right)(t)\cr&=\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{T}(t)x+\left(\textbf{F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}u\right)(t)\end{split}

for a.e.
t>0,
and all
(x,u)\in\mathfrak{X}\times L^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty),\partial\mathfrak{X})
.

Next, in order to give a complete representation of the output function
y(\cdot)
of the system (
2.14
), we need the following subclass of well-posed systems.

Definition 2.4
.

A well-posed triple
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C})
is called regular if

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\int^{t}_{0}\left({\bf F}^{\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C}}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,+\infty)}v_{0}\right)(s)ds=0

for any
v_{0}\in\partial\mathfrak{X}
.

This definition is also equivalent to
{\rm Range}(\mathfrak{D}_{\lambda})\subset D(\mathfrak{C}_{\Lambda})
for some (hence all)
\lambda\in\rho(\mathfrak{A})
.

According to
[
20
, Lem.3.6]
, if
(\mathfrak{A},\mathfrak{B},\mathfrak{C})
is regular, then

(2.18)

Lemma 3.1
.

The operator
\mathscr{G}:D(\mathscr{A}_{m})\to\mathscr{U}
is surjective.

Proof.

Let

and set

g\end{smallmatrix})\in D(\mathscr{A}_{m})

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The operator
\mathscr{A}_{m}
is closed.

Proof.

Let
(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{n}\\
g_{n}\end{smallmatrix})\in D(\mathscr{A}_{m})
and
(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix}),(\begin{smallmatrix}\overline{x}\\
\overline{g}\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathscr{H}
such that
\|x_{n}-x\|+\|g_{n}-g\|_{2}\to 0
and
\|A_{m}x_{n}-\overline{x}\|+\|g^{\prime}_{n}-\overline{g}\|_{2}\to 0
as

. As
(A_{m},\mathscr{Z})
and
(\frac{d}{d\theta},W^{1,2}([-r,0],\mathscr{U}))
are closed then
x\in\mathscr{Z},

g\in W^{1,2}([-r,0],\mathscr{U}),
and
A_{m}x=\overline{x},

g^{\prime}=\overline{g}
. Moreover, for any
n

Gx_{n}=Lg_{n}
and we can write
x_{n}=x_{n}^{0}+x_{n}^{1}
with
x_{n}^{0}\in D(A)
and
x_{n}^{1}\in\ker(\lambda-A_{m})
. Thus
Gx_{n}^{1}=Lg_{n},
so that
x_{n}^{1}=\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}Lg_{n}
for any
n
. Let show that
Lg_{n}\to Lg
as

. In fact, we know that there exists a set

with Lebsegue measure

. Now choose

and write

(3.6)

\displaystyle g_{n_{k}}=e_{0}g_{n_{k}}(\theta_{0})+h_{n_{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad g=e_{0}g(\theta_{0})+h

where

\displaystyle h_{n}(\theta)=\int^{\theta_{0}}_{\theta}(-g_{n}^{\prime})(\sigma)d\sigma\quad\text{and}\quad h(\theta)=\int^{\theta_{0}}_{\theta}(-g^{\prime})(\sigma)d\sigma.

According to (

),

\displaystyle\|Lg_{n_{k}}-Lg\|\leq|\mu|([-r,0])\left(\|g_{n_{k}}(\theta_{0})-g(\theta_{0})\|+\|h_{n_{k}}-h\|_{\infty}\right).

Using Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|h_{n}-h\|_{\infty}\leq r^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{n}^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}([-r,0],\mathscr{U})}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty).

This implies that
Lg_{n_{k}}\to Lg
as
k\to\infty
, so that
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}Lg_{n}\to\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}Lg
. Thus
x_{n_{k}}^{1}\to\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}Lg
, so that
x_{n_{k}}^{0}\to x-\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}Lg
as

. On the other hand,
Ax_{n_{k}}^{0}=A_{m}x_{n_{k}}-A_{m}x_{n_{k}}^{1}=A_{m}x_{n_{k}}-\lambda x_{n_{k}}^{1}
for any

. This implies that
Ax_{n_{k}}^{0}\to A_{m}x-\lambda\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}Lg
. As
A
is closed then in particular

with means that
Gx=Lg
. Then
(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})\in D(\mathscr{A}_{m})
and
\mathscr{A}_{m}(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
g\end{smallmatrix})=(\begin{smallmatrix}\overline{x}\\
\overline{g}\end{smallmatrix})
. This shows that
\mathscr{A}_{m}
is closed.
∎

Let
\mathbb{L}
as in Example

Proposition 2
.

Assume that
B
is an admissible control operator for
A
. The following operator

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}:=\left(\mathscr{A}_{m}\right)_{|D(\mathscr{A})}\quad\text{with}\quad D(\mathscr{A}):=\ker\mathscr{G}

generates a strongly continuous semigroup
\mathscr{T}:=(\mathscr{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on

given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}T(t)&R(t)\\
0&S(t)\end{pmatrix},\quad t\geq 0,

where

is defined by

\displaystyle R(t)g=\int^{t}_{0}T_{-1}(t-s)B\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}S(s)gds.

Proof.

Let us prove that the family of operators

define a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathscr{H}
. Clearly
\mathscr{T}(0)=I_{\mathscr{H}}
. On one hand, the strong continuity of this family follows from the fact that

as

, for any

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}(t)\mathscr{T}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}T(t+s)&T(t)R(s)+R(t)S(s)\\

Moreover, a simple change of variables shows that

. Thus
\mathscr{T}(t)\mathscr{T}(s)=\mathscr{T}(t+s)
. Let

. Let

\displaystyle R(\lambda,E)=\begin{pmatrix}R(\lambda,A)&\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\mathbb{L}R(\lambda,Q)\\

Let now
(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
f\end{smallmatrix})\in D(\mathscr{A})

f_{0}\end{smallmatrix})\in\mathscr{H}

\displaystyle(\lambda-\mathscr{A})(\begin{smallmatrix}x\\
f\end{smallmatrix})=(\begin{smallmatrix}x_{0}\\
f_{0}\end{smallmatrix}).

In particular, we have

and

. As

then by (

∎

\Phi^{Q,\beta}u\in D(\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda})
for a.e.
t\geq 0
and
u\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathscr{U})
. Now according to (
3.10
) and (
3.16
), we have
\Phi^{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}_{t}u\in D(\mathscr{P}_{\Lambda})
for a.e.
t\geq 0
and
u\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathscr{U})
and the operator

\displaystyle\left({\bf F}^{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B},\mathscr{P}}u\right)(t)=\mathscr{P}_{\Lambda}\Phi^{\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B}}_{t}u=\left({\bf F}^{A,B,C}{\bf F}^{L}u\right)(t),

is linear bounded from
L^{2}([0,\alpha],\mathscr{U})
to
L^{2}([0,\alpha],\mathscr{U})
for any
\alpha>0
. This shows that the triple
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B},\mathscr{P})
is well-posed. Again by regularity of triples
(A,B,C)
and
(Q,\beta,\mathbb{L})
, we have
{\rm Range}(\mathbb{D}_{\lambda})\subset D(C_{\Lambda})
and
{\rm Range}(e_{\lambda})\subset D(\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda})
for some (hence all)
\lambda\in\rho(A)
. This shows that
{\rm Range}(\mathscr{D}_{\lambda})\subset D(\mathscr{P}_{\Lambda})
for
\lambda\in\rho(\mathscr{A})
, due to (
3.10
). Thus
(\mathscr{A},\mathscr{B},\mathscr{P})
is regular. Now, by
[
22
, Theorem 2.15]
, we have
Z(t):=Z^{\xi,\varphi,U}(t)\in D(\mathscr{P}_{\Lambda})
for a.e.
t>0
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., and

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{P}_{\Lambda}Z^{\xi,\varphi,U}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,\alpha;\mathscr{U})}\leq c(\alpha)\left(\|(\begin{smallmatrix}\xi\\
\varphi\end{smallmatrix})\|_{L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{0}}(\Omega,\mathscr{H})}+\|U\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,\alpha;\mathscr{U})}\right)

for any
\alpha>0,

\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{0}}(\Omega,H),

\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{0}}(\Omega,L^{2}([-r,0],\mathscr{U})),

U\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,\alpha;\mathscr{U})
and some constant
c(\alpha)>0
. We denote by

\displaystyle Z^{\xi,\varphi,U}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}X^{\xi,\varphi,U}(t)\\
V(t,\cdot)\end{pmatrix},\qquad t\geq 0.

Then, by using Proposition
2
, the equations (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and the relation (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle X^{\xi,\varphi,U}(t)=T(t)\xi+\int^{t}_{0}T_{-1}(t-s)B\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda}U_{s}ds+\int^{t}_{0}T(t-s)M(X^{\xi,\varphi,U}(s))dW(s),

\displaystyle V(t,\cdot)=U_{t}

for a.e.
t\geq 0
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. Again by
[
22
]
, we have
X^{\xi,\varphi,U}(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda})
and
V(t,\cdot)\in D(\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda})
for a.e.
t>0
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. This implies that
Z^{\xi,\varphi,U}(t)\in D(C_{\Lambda})\times D(\mathbb{L}_{\Lambda})
for a.e.
t>0
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. Thus the results follow by (
3.10
) and (
3.17
).
∎

Example 2
.

Consider the following input-output partial differential stochastic system

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}X(t,x)=\Delta X(t,x)+\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{O}}k(x,y)X(t,y)dy\partial_{t}W(t),&x\in\mathscr{O},\quad t>0,\cr X(0,x)=\xi(x),&x\in\mathscr{O},\cr\nabla X(t,x)|\nu(x)=U(t-r,x),&x\in\partial\mathscr{O},\quad t>0,\cr U(t,x)=\varphi(t,x),&t\in[-r,0],\;x\in\mathscr{O},\cr Y(t,x)=c(x)X(t,x),&x\in\partial\mathscr{O},\quad t\geq 0.\end{cases}

Here,
\mathscr{O}
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a
C^{2}
-boundary
\partial\mathscr{O}
and outer unit normal
\nu(x)
, the initial condition
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathscr{O}),

r>0
is a real number,
k\in L^{2}(\mathscr{O}\times\mathscr{O}),
and
c(\cdot)\in C_{b}(\partial\mathscr{O})
. We set

\displaystyle H=L^{2}(\mathscr{O}),\quad\mathscr{Z}=H^{2}(\mathscr{O}),\quad\mathscr{U}=\mathscr{Y}=L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O}).

For any
f\in H,
we define

\displaystyle\left[M(f)\right](x)=\int_{\mathscr{O}}k(x,y)f(y)dy,\qquad x\in\mathscr{O}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we get
\|M(f)\|_{H}\leq\|k\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O}\times\mathscr{O})}\|f\|_{H}
. We also define the delay operator

\displaystyle Lg=g(-r),\qquad g\in W^{1,2}([-r,0],\mathscr{U}).

Let us now select the following operators

\displaystyle A_{m}:=\Delta,\quad D(A_{m}):=\mathscr{Z},

\displaystyle G:\mathscr{Z}\to\mathscr{U},\quad(G\psi)(x)=\nabla\psi(x)|\nu(x),

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}:\mathscr{Z}\to\mathscr{Y},\quad(\mathscr{C}\psi)(x)=c(x)\psi(x).

We know that the operator

\displaystyle A:=A_{m},\qquad D(A):=\{f\in\mathscr{Z}:(G\psi)(x)=\nabla\psi(x)|\nu(x)=0,\;x\in\partial\mathscr{O}\}

generates an analytic semigroup
T:=(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
. In what follows, we collect from
[
24
, Chap.3]
some facts about fractional operators and their relation to Neumann boundary conditions. We denote by
\phi=\mathbb{D}_{1}\psi\in W^{3/2,2}(\mathscr{O})
the solution of the elliptic boundary-value problem
\Delta\phi=0
on
\mathscr{O}
and
\nabla\phi|\nu=\psi
on
\partial\mathscr{O}
, where
\psi\in L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O})
. Then
\mathbb{D}_{1}
maps
L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O})
continuously into
D((I-A)^{\beta})
for
\beta\in(0,\frac{3}{4})
. On the other hand, by using the analyticity of the semigroup
T,
we obtain

(3.19)

\displaystyle\|(I-A)^{\alpha}T(t-s)(I-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{1}v\|_{H}\leq c_{\beta}(t-s)^{\beta-\alpha-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O})}

for any
v\in L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O}),\;\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in(0,\frac{3}{4}),
where
c_{\beta}>0
is a constant. In particular for
\alpha=0
and
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4})
the control maps
\Phi^{A,B}_{t}
associated to the control operator
B:=(I-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{1}
satisfy
\Phi^{A,B}_{t}:L^{2}([0,+\infty),\mathscr{U})\to H
and
\|\Phi^{A,B}_{t}\|\leq\delta_{\beta}t^{2\beta-1}
for any
t\geq 0
and a constant
\delta_{\beta}>0
. This shows that
(A,B)
is well-posed. On the other hand, the observation operator
\mathscr{C}\varphi=c(\cdot){\rm tr}\varphi
, where
{\rm tr}
is the trace operator, is uniformly bounded from
D((I-A)^{\alpha})
to
L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O})
for
\alpha>\frac{1}{4}
. Now if we set
C:=\mathscr{C}
with domain
D(C)=D(A)
, then for any
\varphi\in D(A)
,
\|CT(t)\varphi\|\leq\gamma t^{-\alpha}
for a constant
\gamma>0
. Thus by choosing
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, we deduce that
(C,A)
is well-posed. To apply theorem
3.3
, we need to prove that the triple
(A,B,C)
is regular. In fact, for
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}),

\beta\in\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4})
and
v\in W^{2,2}([0,t],\mathscr{U})
with
v(0)=0
, we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|{\bf F}^{A,B,C}v=\mathscr{C}\Phi^{A,B}_{\cdot}v\|_{L^{2}([0,t],\mathscr{U})}\leq\kappa_{\beta}t^{\beta-\alpha}\|v\|_{L^{2}([0,t],\mathscr{U})}

for a constant
\kappa_{\beta}>0,
due to (
3.19
). Now by Proposition
1
, the triple
(A,B,C)
is well-posed. On the other hand, let
v_{0}\in\mathscr{U}
. By Hölder’s inequality and the estimate (
3.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int^{t}_{0}\left({\bf F}^{A,B,C}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,+\infty)}v_{0}\right)(s)ds\right\|_{\mathscr{Y}}\leq\kappa_{\beta}t^{\beta-\alpha}\|v_{0}\|_{\mathscr{U}}\underset{t\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}0.

Thus
(A,B,C)
is regular. By a simple computation one can verify that the operator
L
is an admissible observation operator for the left shift semigroup on
L^{2}([-r,0],H)
. Thus the assumption of Theorem
3.3
are satisfied for the stochastic input delay system (
3.18
), so it is a well-posed system.

4.
Application: a stochastic Schrödinger
system

In this section, we are concerned with the well-posedness of a stochastic Schrödinger
equation with a partial Dirichlet input delay control and a collocated observation. We first need some notations and definitions in order to describe the system that will be studied.

Let
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) be an open bounded region with
C^{3}
-boundary

\partial\mathscr{O}=\overline{\Gamma}_{0}\cup\overline{\Gamma}_{1}
,
where
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
are disjoint
parts of the boundary relatively open in
\partial\mathscr{O}
and
{\rm int}(\Gamma_{0})\neq\emptyset
. Let
H=H^{-1}(\mathscr{O})
(the dual space
of the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O})
with respect to the pivot
space
L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
). We define a bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
on

H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O})
by

\displaystyle a(f,g):=\int_{\mathscr{O}}\nabla f(x)\overline{\nabla g(x)}dx,\qquad f,g\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O}).

To this form, we associate the following linear operator
{\mathbb{A}}:D({\mathbb{A}}):=H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O})\to H,

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbb{A}}f,g\rangle=a(f,g),\qquad f,g\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O}),

see e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
28
]
. Clearly
{\mathbb{A}}
defines a canonical isomorphism from
D({\mathbb{A}})
to
H
. Now if we denote by
-\Delta:H^{2}(\mathscr{O})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O})\to L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
the Laplacian operator, then
{\mathbb{A}}
coincides with
-\Delta
on
H^{2}(\mathscr{O})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathscr{O})
. This implies that
{\mathbb{A}}^{-1}f=(-\Delta)^{-1}f
for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
. We also recall (see e.g.
[
21
]
) that
D({\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}})=L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
and
{\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a canonical isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathscr{O})
to
H
. We also have the following continuous embedding:

\displaystyle D({\mathbb{A}})\subset D({\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}})=L^{2}(\mathscr{O})\hookrightarrow H=H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow(D({\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}))^{\prime}\subset D({\mathbb{A}})^{\prime}.

Now consider the system

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX(t,x)=-i\Delta X(t,x)dt+q(x)X(t,x)dW(t),&t>0,\;x\in\mathscr{O},\cr X(0,x)=\xi(x),&x\in\mathscr{O},\cr X(t,x)=0,&t\geq 0,\;x\in\Gamma_{1},\cr X(t,x)=\displaystyle\int^{0}_{-r}d\mu(\theta,x)U(t+\theta,x),&t\geq 0,\;x\in\Gamma_{0},\cr U(t,x)=\varphi(t,x),&-r\leq t\leq 0,\;x\in\Gamma_{0},\cr Y(t,x)=i\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\Delta^{-1}X)}{\partial\nu},&t\geq 0,\;x\in\Gamma_{0},\end{cases}

where

\nu
is the unit normal of
\partial\mathscr{O}
pointing towards the exterior of
\mathscr{O}
,
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathscr{O})
, and for
\{\mu(\theta,x):\theta\in[-r,0],\;x\in\Gamma_{0}\}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mu(\theta,\cdot)\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{2}(\Gamma_{0}))
is a function of bounded variation on
\theta
.
Denote
\mathscr{U}:=L^{2}(\Gamma_{0})
. We consider the following closed operator

\displaystyle A_{m}:=i(-\Delta),\quad D(A_{m})=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathscr{O}):f_{|\Gamma_{1}}=0\}.

We define the following operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}g=qg,\qquad g\in H^{-1}(\mathscr{O}),

\displaystyle Gf:=f_{|\Gamma_{0}},\qquad f\in D(A_{m}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}f=i\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\Delta^{-1}f)}{\partial\nu},\qquad f\in D(A_{m}).

With these notations, the system (
4.1
) takes the form of our model system (
1.1
). Thus to show the well-posednees of the system (
4.1
) it suffices to prove that the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
hold. In fact, we define
A:=(A_{m})_{|D(A)}
with
D(A)=\ker G
. Thus we have

\displaystyle A=i{\mathbb{A}},\qquad D(A)=\{f\in H^{2}(\mathscr{O}):f_{|\partial\mathscr{O}}=0\}.

Let
\mathbb{D}_{0}
(we take
\lambda=0
) be the Dirichlet operator associated with
A_{m}
and
G
. It is determined as follow:
\mathbb{D}_{0}u=v
if and only if
\Delta v=0
in
\mathscr{O},

v=0
on
\Gamma_{1}
and
v=u
on
\Gamma_{0}
. We have
\mathbb{D}_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathscr{U},L^{2}(\mathscr{O}))
. We select
B=(-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{0}=-i{\mathbb{A}}_{-1}\mathbb{D}_{0}
, where
{\mathbb{A}}_{-1}\in{\mathcal{L}}\left(D({\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}}),(D({\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\prime})\right)
is the extension of
{\mathbb{A}}
. Let us show that
B\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathscr{U},(D({\mathbb{A}}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\prime})
is an admissible control operator for
A
. To this end, it suffice to prove that its adjoint
B^{\ast}
is an admissible observation operator for
A
. As shown in
[
15
]
, we have

\displaystyle B^{\ast}f=\left(i\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\Delta^{-1}f)}{\partial\nu}\right)_{|\Gamma_{0}}.

Otherwise,
B^{\ast}=\mathscr{C}_{|D(A)}
. According to
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
, the triple
(A,B,B^{\ast})
is regular. Thus, by Theorem
3.3
, the stochastic
system (
4.1
) is well-posed.

Remark 2
.

We can choose the potential
q
in (
4.1
) to be a stochastic process. In this case, the result of this section remains the same if we assume that
q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathscr{O})
. In fact, we define a multiplication operator on
L^{2}(\Omega,H)
by
(\tilde{\mathscr{M}}f)(\omega,x)=q(\omega,x)f(\omega)
for
x\in\mathscr{O}
and
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. As our main results is based on
[
22

and the proofs of these theorems are not affected by this choice, the result of this section will also not be affected.

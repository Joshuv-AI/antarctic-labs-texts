Short-distance constraints on the hadronic light-by-light
\firstname Johan \lastname Bijnens \fnsep, \firstname Nils \lastname Hermansson-Truedsson \fnsep \fnsep, \firstname Antonio \lastname Rodríguez-Sánchez \fnsep

Abstract
The muon anomalous magnetic moment continues to attract interest due to the potential tension between experimental measurement Muong-2:2006rrc ; Muong-2:2021ojo and the Standard Model prediction Aoyama:2020ynm . The hadronic light-by-light contribution to the magnetic moment is one of the two diagrammatic topologies currently saturating the theoretical uncertainty. With the aim of improving precision on the hadronic light-by-light in a data-driven approach founded on dispersion theory Colangelo:2015ama ; Colangelo:2017fiz , we derive various short-distance constraints of the underlying correlation function of four electromagnetic currents. Here, we present our previous progress in the purely short-distance regime and current efforts in the so-called Melnikov-Vainshtein limit.



1
Introduction

The muon anomalous magnetic moment, or,
a_{\mu}=(g-2)_{\mu}/2
, is a potential probe for new physics beyond the Standard Model (SM)
Aoyama:2020ynm
. Comparing the most recent SM prediction from the White Paper
Aoyama:2020ynm
with the experimental average from Brookhaven National Laboratory
Muong-2:2006rrc
and Fermilab National Laboratory
Muong-2:2021ojo
yields a discrepancy of
4.2\sigma
. The corresponding numbers are

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{\textrm{SM}}=116\,591\,810(43)\times 10^{-11}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{\textrm{exp}}=116\,592\,061(41)\times 10^{-11}\,.

(2)

The SM uncertainty is dominated by hadronic contributions, namely the hadronic vacuum polarisation (HVP) and the hadronic light-by-light (HLbL), which can be calculated either in lattice field theory or using a data-driven approach
Aoyama:2020ynm
. Although the lattice and data-driven predictions for the HLbL agree within uncertainties, there has since the publication of the White Paper arisen a tension for the HVP
Borsanyi:2020mff
. The updated lattice prediction for the HVP predicts a significantly reduced discrepancy for the muon magnetic moment
Borsanyi:2020mff
. There is currently much effort in trying to resolve the tension between the two approaches to calculate the HVP.

In the following, we will be concerned with improving the precision on the HLbL contribution, whose White Paper average is
Aoyama:2020ynm

\displaystyle a_{\mu}^{\textrm{HLbL}}=92(18)\times 10^{-11}\,.

(3)

Diagrammatically, this contribution is given by the topology in Fig.

where the photons have momenta
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
,
q_{3}
and
q_{4}
. The external momentum
q_{4}
is for the
(g-2)_{\mu}
soft, i.e. in the kinematical limit
q_{4}\rightarrow 0
. The remaining three photons have virtual momenta integrated over, which means that in the evaluation of the HLbL there is an intricate mixing between different energy scales. In the dispersive data-driven approach
Colangelo:2015ama
;
Colangelo:2017fiz
, one uses analyticity and unitarity to systematically decompose the HLbL into a sum over hadronic contributions. The appearing form-factors in this decomposition require input from e.g. experiments or the lattice
Aoyama:2020ynm
. The unknown hadronic contributions can, however, be controlled with the help of so-called short-distance constraints (SDCs) on the underlying correlation function of four electromagnetic currents, see Refs.
Melnikov:2003xd
;
Bijnens:2019ghy
;
Bijnens:2020xnl
;
Bijnens:2021jqo
. These kinds of SDCs can be obtained from operator product expansion (OPE) techniques and have been used for model calculations
Bijnens:1995xf
;
Colangelo:2019lpu
;
Colangelo:2019uex
;
Cappiello:2019hwh
;
Leutgeb:2019gbz
;
Colangelo:2021nkr
and a complementary approach building on interpolation techniques
Ludtke:2020moa

2
Some generalities

The HLbL is defined in terms of a correlation function of four electromagnetic currents. The corresponding so-called HLbL tensor is given by

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})=-i\int\frac{d^{4}q_{4}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{4}\int d^{4}x_{i}\,e^{-iq_{i}x_{i}}\right)\,\langle 0|T\left(\prod_{j=1}^{4}J^{\mu_{j}}(x_{j})\right)|0\rangle\,.

(4)

Here, the currents are given by
J^{\mu}(x)=\bar{q}\,Q_{q}\gamma^{\mu}q
where
q=(u,d,s)
and
Q_{q}=\textrm{diag}(e_{q})=\textrm{diag}(2/3,-1/3,-1/3)
is the associated charge matrix for light quarks. Here we use the convention
q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}+q_{4}=0
. The above tensor satisfies the Ward identities

q_{i,\,\mu_{i}}\,\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})=0\,.

(5)

This implies the relation
Aldins:1970id

\hskip-15.0pt\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})=-q_{4,\,\nu_{4}}\frac{\partial\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{4}}}{\partial q_{4,\,\mu_{4}}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,,

(6)

which means that one can access
a_{\mu}^{\textrm{HLbL}}
from the derivative tensor on the right-hand side. The derivative can be Lorentz decomposed into a set of 54 scalar functions
\Pi_{i}

Colangelo:2015ama
;
Colangelo:2017fiz
. The contribution to the magnetic moment can be written in terms of six linear combinations
\hat{\Pi}_{1,4,7,17,39,54}
of the
\Pi_{i}
, namely

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pta_{\mu}^{\mathrm{HLbL}}=\frac{2\alpha^{3}}{3\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dQ_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}dQ_{2}\int_{-1}^{1}d\tau\,\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\,Q_{1}^{3}Q_{2}^{3}\sum_{i=1}^{12}T_{i}(Q_{1},Q_{2},\tau)\,\overline{\Pi}_{i}(Q_{1},Q_{2},\tau)\,,

(7)

where
\overline{\Pi}_{i}
are functions of the six
\hat{\Pi}_{j}
, and the kernels
T_{i}
are known. Note that the integration in (
7
) is for the full HLbL contribution, whereas the kinematic regions for the two types of SDCs considered here correspond to restricted integration domains. Common for the two limits
Q_{i}^{2}\gg\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}^{2}
and
Q_{i}^{2},Q_{j}^{2}\gg Q_{k}^{2},\Lambda^{2}_{\textrm{QCD}}
is the need to define an onset
Q_{\textrm{min}}
of the asymptotic limits:
Q_{i}^{2}>Q_{\textrm{min}}^{2}
and
Q_{i}^{2},Q_{j}^{2}>Q_{\mathrm{min}}^{2}
, respectively. Since there currently is no preferred choice of
Q_{\textrm{min}}
we keep it variable, and note that this ambiguity induces a source of uncertainty in
a_{\mu}^{\mathrm{HLbL}}
.

3
An OPE for three currents

In the purely short-distance limit
Q_{i}^{2}\gg\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}^{2}
, three of the currents in the four-point function defined in (
4
) are close. Naively performing an OPE of these currents works only to leading order, since the next-to-leading order contribution in the systematic expansion is ill-defined in the static limit
q_{4}\rightarrow 0
, which is the limit relevant for the magnetic moment
Bijnens:2019ghy
. The problem arises from propagators of the momentum
q_{4}
which clearly diverge. However, it was shown in Ref.
Bijnens:2019ghy
that the problem can be circumvented by treating the static photon as an external electromagnetic field and instead of the four-point function
\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}
consider a three-point correlator in the presence of that field. For this reason we study

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}}(q_{1},q_{2})=-\frac{1}{e}\int\frac{d^{4}q_{3}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{3}\int d^{4}x_{i}\,e^{-iq_{i}x_{i}}\right)\,\langle 0|T\left(\prod_{j=1}^{3}J^{\mu_{j}}(x_{j})\right)|\gamma(q_{4})\rangle\,,

(8)

where the static background field is captured in the external state. This is related to the full HLbL tensor through
Bijnens:2019ghy

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}}(q_{1},q_{2})=\epsilon_{\mu_{4}}(q_{4})\,\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})=-\epsilon_{\mu_{4}}(q_{4})\,q_{4,\,\nu_{4}}\frac{\partial\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{4}}}{\partial q_{4,\,\mu_{4}}}\,(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,,

(9)

where
\epsilon_{\mu_{4}}(q_{4})
is the polarisation vector of the external photon and the Ward identity (
6
) was used. By performing a systematic background field OPE between the three currents in (
8
) then yields the HLbL derivative through (
9
). The background field induces non-perturbative condensates in the expansion which are different than those appearing in vacuum OPEs
Shifman:1978bx
. The condensates appear from diagrammatic contributions to the correlator in (
8
) where some of the fields are left uncontracted in the Wick expansion. Background field OPEs were first used for nucleon magnetic moments in Refs.
Balitsky:1983xk
;
Ioffe:1983ju
, but were in fact later also employed for the electroweak contribution to
a_{\mu}
in Ref.
Czarnecki:2002nt
.

3.1
Leading order perturbative and non-perturbative corrections

The systematic OPE is done in powers of
1/Q_{i}
and the strong coupling
\alpha_{s}
. Through order
1/Q_{i}^{6}
and
\alpha_{s}
the leading order term and non-perturbative corrections arise from the operators
Bijnens:2019ghy
;
Bijnens:2020xnl

\displaystyle S_{1,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=e\,e_{q}F_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad S_{2,\,\mu\nu}=\bar{q}\,\sigma_{\mu\nu}q\,,\hskip 17.0pt\quad S_{3,\,\mu\nu}=i\,\,\bar{q}\,G_{\mu\nu}q\,,

(10)

\displaystyle S_{4,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=i\,\bar{q}\,\bar{G}_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}q\,,\quad S_{5,\,\mu\nu}=\bar{q}q\;e\,e_{q}F_{\mu\nu}\,,\hskip-10.0pt\qquad S_{6,\,\mu\nu}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\,G_{a}^{\alpha\beta}G^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\;e\,e_{q}F_{\mu\nu}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle S_{7,\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}\,\left(G_{\mu\lambda}D_{\nu}+D_{\nu}G_{\mu\lambda}\right)\gamma^{\lambda}q-(\mu\leftrightarrow\nu)\,,

(12)

\displaystyle S_{\{8\},\,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}\,\left(\bar{q}\,\Gamma q\;\bar{q}\,\Gamma q\right)_{\mu\nu}\,.

(13)

Here, the gluon field is given by
G_{\mu\nu}=ig_{S}\lambda^{a}G^{a}_{\mu\nu}
as well as its dual
\bar{G}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\lambda\rho}G_{\lambda\rho}
. The matrix
\Gamma
is in flavour, spin and colour space . For more detail, we refer the reader to Ref.
Bijnens:2020xnl
. The class of four-quark operators
S_{\{8\},\,\mu\nu}
yields flavour mixing, and in the chiral limit there are only 12 independent operators. In Fig.
2
we show the various diagrammatic contributions appearing in the OPE. The leading order term is given by the perturbative quark-loop in Fig.
2
(a), which in fact coincides with the leading order term in a naive OPE of the four-point function in (
4
). This finding in Ref.
Bijnens:2019ghy
confirmed that indeed the perturbative quark loop describes the leading behaviour in the short-distance limit
Aoyama:2020ynm
.

The obtained systematic expansion allows us to quantify for the first time from where the short-distance representation of the HLbL is valid. We study first the impact of the non-perturbative corrections. We note in passing that one also has to renormalise the condensates, which is described in detail in Ref.
Bijnens:2020xnl

\hskip-20.0pt\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}}(q_{1},q_{2})=\frac{1}{e}\,\vec{C}^{\,T,\,\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}\nu_{4}}_{\overline{MS}}(q_{1},q_{2})\,\langle\vec{Q}_{\overline{MS},\,\mu_{4}\nu_{4}}(\mu)\rangle\,,

(14)

where the perturbative short-distance Wilson coefficients are contained in the vector
\vec{C}^{\,T,\,\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}\nu_{4}}_{\overline{MS}}
and the condensates at renormalisation scale
\mu
in
\langle\vec{Q}_{\overline{MS},\mu_{4}\nu_{4}}(\mu)\rangle
. For convenience, we write

\langle\vec{Q}_{\overline{MS},\,\mu\nu}(\mu)\rangle=e\,\vec{X}\,\langle e_{q}F_{\mu\nu}\rangle\,.

(15)

Numerical estimates for the condensates are obtained from Refs.
Bijnens:2020xnl
;
Shifman:1978bx

3.2
Gluonic corrections

From the above numerical results it appears that the perturbative quark loop is a good description of the short-distance behaviour of the HLbL. However, perturbative order
\alpha_{s}
corrections to the quark loop can also be important, which is what we studied in Ref.
Bijnens:2021jqo
. Including two vertices from the Dyson series with gluon interactions yields two-loop topologies like those in Fig.
7
. Details of the calculation are given in Ref.
Bijnens:2021jqo
together with the
\hat{\Pi}_{i}
, and we report here only the numerical comparison to the quark loop contribution to
a_{\mu}^{\mathrm{HLbL}}
in Fig.
7
. As can be seen, the shift in going from the leading order quark loop to including the two-loop corrections is small. Numerically, it corresponds to a shift around
-10\%
compared to the quark loop. This indeed shows that the quark loop describes the short-distance dynamics of the HLbL to within 10% in the region
Q_{i}^{2}\gg\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}^{2}
, and can be used by dispersive and model studies as in Ref.
Colangelo:2021nkr
.

4
An OPE for two currents

Having concluded the study for the purely short-distance region
Q_{i}^{2}\gg\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}^{2}
we now turn to the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit
Q_{i}^{2},Q_{j}^{2}\gg Q_{k}^{2},\Lambda^{2}_{\textrm{QCD}}
. This was studied first in Ref.
Melnikov:2003xd
. We emphasise that results here are still preliminary, and we exemplify by considering the case
Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2}\gg Q_{3}^{2},\Lambda^{2}_{\textrm{QCD}}
. Recall that for three currents we studied the three-point function in (
8
). In the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit we can instead use the two-point function

\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}=\frac{i}{e^{2}}\int\frac{d^{4}q_{4}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}x_{1}\int d^{4}x_{2}e^{-i(q_{1}x_{1}+q_{2}x_{2})}\langle 0|T\left\{J^{\mu_{1}}(x_{1})J^{\mu_{2}}(x_{2})\right\}|\gamma^{*}(q_{3})\gamma(q_{4})\rangle\,.

(16)

The relation to the four-point HLbL tensor is then (cf. (
9
))

\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}=\epsilon_{\mu_{3}}(q_{3})\,\epsilon_{\mu_{4}}(q_{4})\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}=-\epsilon_{\mu_{3}}(q_{3})\,\epsilon_{\mu_{4}}(q_{4})\,q_{4,\,\nu_{4}}\frac{\partial\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\nu_{4}}}{\partial q_{4,\,\mu_{4}}}\,(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\,.

(17)

In this OPE we will expand in powers of the large momentum
\hat{q}=(q_{1}-q_{2})/2
. We further define a Dirac matrix in terms of the massless quark propagator according to

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu\nu}(k)=\gamma^{\mu}S(k)\gamma^{\nu}\,,\qquad S(k)=\frac{\not{k}}{k^{2}}\,.

(18)

Denoting the perturbative quark loop contribution to the HLbL tensor in (
4
) by
\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}_{\mathrm{quark-loop}}
, one finds in the chiral limit through next-to-leading order the result

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}

\displaystyle\approx{-\frac{e_{q}^{2}}{e^{2}}\,\left\langle\bar{q}(0)\left[\Gamma^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}(-\hat{q})-\Gamma^{\mu_{2}\mu_{1}}(-\hat{q})\right]q(0)\,\big|\gamma^{*}(q_{3})\gamma(q_{4})\right\rangle}

\displaystyle-\frac{ie_{q}^{2}}{e^{2}\hat{q}^{2}}\left(g^{\mu_{1}\delta}g^{\mu_{2}}_{\beta}+g^{\mu_{2}\delta}g^{\mu_{1}}_{\beta}-g^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}g^{\delta}_{\beta}\right)\left(g_{\alpha\delta}-2\frac{\hat{q}_{\delta}\hat{q}_{\alpha}}{\hat{q}^{2}}\right)\left\langle\bar{q}(0)\left[\overrightarrow{\partial}^{\alpha}-\overleftarrow{\partial}^{\alpha}\right]\gamma^{\beta}q(0)\big|\gamma^{*}(q_{3})\gamma(q_{4})\,\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}{F_{\nu_{3}\mu_{3}}F_{\nu_{4}\mu_{4}}}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{3\,\nu_{3}}}\right|_{q_{3}\rightarrow 0}\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{4\,\nu_{4}}}\right|_{q_{4}\rightarrow 0}\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\mu_{3}\mu_{4}}_{\mathrm{quark-loop}}\,.

(19)

We thus see that the two external photons in (
16
) induce new contributions as compared to (
8
). There are several technical issues in obtaining the
\hat{\Pi}_{i}
in the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit which will be explained in our upcoming paper. Note that we have made no ordering on the size of
Q_{3}^{2}
and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{2}
here, except for an omitted gluon operator contribution to the flavour singlet piece when
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}<Q_{3}
. However, the perturbative
Q_{3}^{2}
regime
Q_{1}^{2},Q_{2}^{2}\gg Q_{3}^{2}\gg\Lambda^{2}_{\textrm{QCD}}
corresponds to a special case of the purely short-distance limit
Q_{i}^{2}\gg\Lambda_{\textrm{QCD}}^{2}
discussed above. This means that for
Q_{3}^{2}\gg\Lambda^{2}_{\textrm{QCD}}
in the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit we should reproduce our previous results for the perturbative quark loop as well as the leading order result of Ref.
Melnikov:2003xd
. Evaluating the appearing matrix elements in (
4
) perturbatively, this is indeed the case.

In the non-perturbative region
Q_{3}^{2}\not{\gg}\Lambda^{2}_{\textrm{QCD}}
, the matrix elements in (
4
) must be form-factor decomposed. The leading order term (first row in (
4
)) depends on two form-factors, i.e. the longitudinal and transversal functions discussed in Ref.
Melnikov:2003xd
. At next-to-leading order there are six form-factors, and the exact decomposition will be given in our future paper.

We are currently working on gluonic corrections to the OPE in (
4
). As argued in Ref.
Ludtke:2020moa
, the leading perturbative
\alpha_{s}
correction is expected to be given by the Melnikov-Vainshtein result
Melnikov:2003xd
with an overall factor
-\alpha_{s}/\pi
. In our OPE, through leading order in
\alpha_{s}
the first line in (
4
) instead yields

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}_{\mathrm{LO},\,\alpha_{s}}

\displaystyle\approx{-\frac{e_{q}^{2}}{e^{2}}\,\left(1-\frac{7}{3}\,\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)\,\left\langle\bar{q}(0)\left[\Gamma^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}(-\hat{q})-\Gamma^{\mu_{2}\mu_{1}}(-\hat{q})\right]q(0)\,\big|\gamma^{*}(q_{3})\gamma(q_{4})\right\rangle}\,.

(20)

Although this might seem to be in tension, the Melnikov-Vainshtein result refers to the axial current that preserves chiral symmetry within perturbation theory, related to ours by a finite counterterm
Trueman:1979en
. We are currently working to complete the calculation, taking these subtleties into account.

SDCs in the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit are highly relevant for data-driven approaches to evaluate the HLbL, see e.g. Refs.
Melnikov:2003xd
;
Colangelo:2019lpu
;
Colangelo:2019uex
;
Ludtke:2020moa
;
Colangelo:2021nkr
. Our anticipated results can be used to further improve the data-driven prediction.

5
Conclusions

In these proceedings we have reported on recent progress on the derivation of short-distance constraints for the data-driven evaluation of the HLbL. This hadronic contribution to the muon magnetic moment is particularly complicated since it mixes long- and short-distance dynamics. Constraints in the purely short-distance as well as the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit are important to control the systematic uncertainty in the data-driven approach to the HLbL, both of which we have considered herein. Due to the soft external magnetic field, the constraints are derived using background field operator product expansion techniques. Our main result is a precise description for the HLbL in the purely short-distance regime. In the Melnikov-Vainshtein limit, which is still work in progress, we have reproduced important results in certain limits, and are currently evaluating also novel higher-order non-perturbative as well as gluonic corrections.

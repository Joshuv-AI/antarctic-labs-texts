1.
Introduction

In 1982, S.T. Yau proposed the following conjecture on the concentration of curvatures.

Conjecture 1.1

(S.T. Yau
[
15
]
)
.

For any
n
-dimensional manifolds
(M,g)
with Ricci curvature
Ric_{M}\geq(n-1)\kappa
, we have a prior
L^{1}
-bound for the scalar curvature

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}scal\,\operatorname{d}{vol}_{g}\leq c(n,\kappa)
.

It posits that if the Ricci curvature is bounded from below, then the regions where the scalar curvature is positively concentrated do not have a large volume. One can also compare it to the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem. Despite some progress (see the following theorem) in proving the conjecture under stronger conditions, it remains an open problem in mathematics.

Theorem 1.2

(Petrunin
[
14
]
)
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds if the sectional curvature
\sec_{M}\geq\kappa
.

One can ask similar questions on length metric spaces with synthetic lower curvature bounds. Let
\text{Alex\,}^{n}(\kappa)
be the collection of
n
-dimensional Alexandrov spaces with curvature
\geq\kappa
and
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{k}(X)
be the
k
-stratum singular set of points in
X
whose tangent cones do not split off
\mathds{R}^{k+1}
isometrically. Due to Burago-Gromov-Perelman, and Cheeger-Colding’s work, we have that
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{S}^{k}(X))\leq k
for both Alexandrov spaces
[
2
]
and Ricci limit spaces
[
3
]
. Though the dimension estimate gives a nice control for the singular set,
\mathcal{S}^{k}(X)
may still have infinite
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure (see
[
6
]
). Since a quantitative estimate of
\mathcal{S}^{k}(X)
is needed in geometric analysis, we have the following result in the further study. Let
\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon}(X)
denote the set of points
x\in X
whose tangent cone
T_{x}(X)
is
\epsilon
-away from splitting off
\mathds{R}^{k+1}
. That is, for any
x\in\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon}(X)
, either
T_{x}(X)
doesn’t split off
\mathds{R}^{k}
or
T_{x}(X)=\mathds{R}^{k}\times C(\Sigma^{n-k-1}_{x})
and the Gromov-Hausdorff distance
d_{GH}(\Sigma^{n-k-1}_{x},\mathds{S}_{1}^{n-k-1})\geq\epsilon
. The following Hausdorff measure estimate was given in
[
9
]
by A. Naber and the author:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k}\big(\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon}(X)\cap B_{1}\big)\leq C(n,\epsilon).

(1.1)

It was conjectured
[
9
]
that the upper bound
C(n,\epsilon)
grows in an order of
\epsilon^{1-(n-k)}
, as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Conjecture 1.3

(Li-Naber
[
9
]
)
.

For any
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon}(X)\cap B_{1})<C(n)\epsilon^{1-(n-k)}.

(1.2)

We can re-write inequality (
1.2
) as

\displaystyle\epsilon^{n-k-1}\cdot\mathcal{H}^{k}(\mathcal{S}^{k}_{\epsilon}\cap B_{1})<C(n)

(1.3)

and formulate a much stronger conjecture in a summable form
[
9
]
or the following integral form. This is based on the observation of Gauss-Bonnet Theorem and some higher dimensional examples.
Let
\theta_{k}(x)=2\pi
for
x\in\mathcal{S}^{k-1}(X)
, and
\theta_{k}(x)=2\pi-{\rm diam}(\Sigma^{n-k-1}_{x})
for
x\in\mathcal{S}^{k}(X)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{k-1}(X)
whose tangent cone
T_{x}(X)=\mathds{R}^{k}\times C(\Sigma^{n-k-1}_{x})
. Here
\theta_{k}(x)
measure the
k
-singularity of
x
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

For any
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\theta_{k}^{n-k-1}(x)\cdot\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{k}<C(n)
.
In particular, for
k=n-2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\theta_{n-2}(x)\cdot\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}<C(n)
.

Note that
\mathcal{S}(X)=\cup_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{S}^{k}(X)
contains all geometric singular points, as all tangent cones at
x\notin\mathcal{S}(X)
are isometric to
\mathds{R}^{n}
by definition. However, the metric away from
\mathcal{S}(X)
may still be non-smooth. For instance, if we double an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold
M
with boundary, and glue them along their boundaries, then the glued metric may not be smooth at any of the glued point. The glued points have co-dimension 1 and their tangent cones are all isometric to
\mathds{R}^{n}
if the boundary
\partial M
is smooth. In particular, these points can’t be differentiated from the smooth points by their tangent cones.
In this paper, we define a singularity function
\mathcal{K}(x)\geq 0
which measures the extent to which the metric is from being locally
C^{2}
at a point
x
. See Section
2
for details and examples. In particular, we have that every point in
\mathcal{KS}(X)=\{x\in X\colon\mathcal{K}(x)>0\}
is not locally
C^{2}
. The larger
\mathcal{K}(x)
is, the more curving the space would look like near
x
. Let
\mathcal{KS}_{\epsilon}(X)=\{x\in X\colon\mathcal{K}(x)\geq\epsilon\}
. We have the following quantitative estimates.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,p)\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
with
vol(B_{1}(p))\geq\nu>0
. Then for any
B_{r}\subseteq B_{1}(p)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\mathcal{K}(x)\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq c(n,\nu)r^{n-2}.

(1.4)

In particular, we have the Hausdorff measure estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{KS}_{\epsilon}(X)\cap B_{r}\right)\leq\epsilon^{-1}\cdot c(n,\nu)r^{n-2}

(1.5)

for
\epsilon>0
, and the Hausdorff dimension estimate

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{KS}(X))\leq n-1.

(1.6)

It turns out that
\mathcal{K}(x)\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
can also be viewed as a synthetic curvature measure on Alexandrov spaces, as a counterpart of
scal\,\operatorname{d}{vol}_{g}
on smooth manifolds. Suppose
(M_{i},g_{i})
Gromov-Hausdorff converges to a limit space
(X,d)
, and
scal_{i}
satisfy the integral bounds in Conjecture
1.1
. Passing to a sub-sequence, as a signed measure,
scal_{i}\,\operatorname{d}{vol}_{g_{i}}
converges to a measure on
X
. A. Naber proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5

(Conjecture 2.20 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i})
be a sequence of
n
-dimensional manifolds with lower Ricci or sectional curvature bounds. If
M_{i}
Gromov-Hausdorff converge to
X
without collapsing, then as measure,

scal_{i}\,\operatorname{d}{vol}_{g_{i}}\to{\operatorname{R}}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}+\Phi\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\theta\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2},

where
{\operatorname{R}}
,
\Phi
and
\theta
are locally
L^{1}
-functions with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n}
,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-2}
respectively. The function
\Phi(x)
is supported on an
(n-1)
-rectifiable subset. The function
\theta(x)=\theta_{n-2}(x)
is defined as in Conjecture
1.4
, which is supported on the top stratum of the
(n-2)
-rectifiable singular set
\mathcal{S}(X)=\mathcal{S}^{n-2}(X)
.

The above conjecture is true in the following special cases.

Theorem 1.6

(Petrunin
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(M_{i},g_{i})
be a sequence of
n
-dimensional manifolds with
\sec_{M_{i}}\geq\kappa
and
(M_{i},p_{i},g_{i})\overset{d_{GH}}{\longrightarrow}(M,p,g)
, as
i\to\infty
. If
(M,g)
is an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}(p_{i})}scal_{i}\,\operatorname{d}{vol}_{g_{i}}=\int_{B_{1}(p)}scal\,\operatorname{d}{vol}_{g}.

(1.7)

Theorem 1.7

(Lebedeva-Petrunin
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\{(M_{i},g_{i})\}
be a sequence of
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds with
\sec_{M_{i}}\geq\kappa
and
M_{i}\overset{d_{GH}}{\longrightarrow}X
without collapsing, then the curvature tensors of
M_{i}
weakly converge to a measure-valued tensor
{\operatorname{R}}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}+\Phi\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\theta\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}
on
X
, where
\theta(x)=\theta_{n-2}(x)
is defined as in Conjecture
1.4
.

Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.7
imply that Conjecture
1.4
is true for
k=n-2
and
X
being a non-collapsed limit of manifolds with lower sectional curvature bound. When comparing Theorem
1.7
with Theorem
1.6
, we see that as a limit,
\Phi
should be supported on the set of non-smooth points. However, this is unknown based on
[
7
]
. It remains unclear the relation between the limit measure
\Phi\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
and the underlying geometry of the limit space
X
. For instance, one can ask whether
\Phi
can be defined on the singular space without or independent on the smoothing procedure
M_{i}\to X
. In our case, the
C^{2}
-singularity function
\mathcal{K}(x)
solely depends on the metric
X
, which also measures the bending of the space near
x
. This indicates that
\mathcal{K}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is equivalent to
\Phi\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, and this is true in the simple doubling examples. In light of this,
\mathcal{K}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, as well as
\theta_{n-2}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}
, can be viewed as a synthetic curvature measure on the non-smooth sets in Alexandrov spaces. The definition of
{\operatorname{R}}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}
is not clear to the author at this point, although in the smooth case,
{\operatorname{R}}
can be chosen as the scalar curvature.

By introducing the notion of
\operatorname{R}
,
\mathcal{K}
and
\theta
, we can define a synthetic curvature measure on Alexandrov space
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
as follows:

\displaystyle\mu_{X}={\operatorname{R}}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}+\mathcal{K}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\theta\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}.

(1.8)

The first question one may ask is if
\mu_{X}
is locally finite.

Conjecture 1.8
.

If
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
, then
\int_{B_{1}}\mu_{X}\leq C(n)
.

On can also ask convergence questions similar to Conjecture
1.5
.

Conjecture 1.9
.

If
X_{i}\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
Gromov-Hausdorff converge to
X
without collapsing, then
\mu_{X_{i}}\to\mu_{X}
.

Regarding this conjecture, we have the following partial result.

Theorem B
.

Let
X_{i}\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
and
n\geq 2
. If
(X_{i},p_{i})
Gromov-Hausdorff converges to a smooth manifold
(M,p)
without collapsing. If
\partial M=\varnothing
or the doubling of
M
is smooth, then
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}(p_{i})}\mathcal{K}_{i}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}(p_{i})}\theta_{i}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}=0
.

We would like to point out that it is unknown if
X\setminus\mathcal{KS}(X)
is a smooth manifold away from a zero
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measure subset. According to Theorem
1.6
, this is the gap to improve the conclusion of Theorem B to the following convergence result

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}(p_{i})}{\operatorname{R}}_{i}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}+\mathcal{K}_{i}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\theta_{i}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-2}=\int_{B_{1}(p)}{\operatorname{R}}\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n}.

(1.9)

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we define
\mathcal{K}(x)
and illustrate it with an example. In Section
3
, we establish a new covering theorem for Alexandrov spaces. The theorem states that any compact set can be covered by a controlled number of convex annuli with centers on the given compact set (see Theorem
3.8
). We will apply this covering theorem recursively to form an annulus stratification of
X
. Furthermore, by establishing Lemma
3.10
, we can adjust the intersection angles of the strata to be less than but close to

. In Section

on each stratum (Lemma

5
, we prove Theorem A by integrating the monotonic formulas along with the stratification structures established in Section
3
. We then prove Theorem B using Theorem A and the covering theorems in Section
3
. In the Appendix, we give a simplified proof for Petrunin’s Theorem
1.2
, using the covering techniques established in Section
3
.

2.
Definitions and Examples

We define
\mathcal{K}(x)
in this section. Since we will integrate it with respect to the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure, it suffices to define it away from a set of co-dimension 2.
Let
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(\kappa)
without boundary. If
X
has non-empty boundary, we will consider the doubling of
X
. A subset
W\subseteq X
is said to be locally convex at a point
x
if there exists
r>0
so that
B_{r}(x)\cap W
is convex. According to Theorem
3.8
, all but a finite number of points
x\in B_{1}
admits a subset
W
so that
x\in\partial W
and
W
is locally convex at
x
. The tangent cones
T_{x}(W)
at these points are metric cones with boundaries. If
T_{x}(W)=\mathds{R}^{n-2}\times C([0,\beta])
,
0<\beta\leq\pi
, we define the singularity angle
\Theta(x,\partial W)=\pi-\beta
, otherwise we let
\Theta(x,\partial W)=\pi
. Although
\Theta(x,\partial W)
is determined by
W
but not by
\partial W
itself, we still use the latter in our notion. This is to highlight
x\in\partial W
and that the singularity direction is normal to
\partial W
. We would like to point out that
\Theta(x,\partial W)
only reflects the singularity of
W
with respect to its intrinsic metric, but not the singularity of
X
. In particular, having
\Theta(x,W)>0
does not imply
x\in\mathcal{S}^{n-2}(X)
. For example, a triangle plane
W\subset\mathds{R}^{2}
gives
\Theta(x,\partial W)>0
at the vertex
x\in W
, but there are no singular points in
\mathds{R}^{2}
.

Let
W_{t}=\{x\in W\colon d(x,\partial W)\geq t\}
be the sup-level set and
\partial W_{t}=\{x\in W_{t}\colon d(x,\partial W)=t\}
. Let
\phi_{t}(x)
be the re-parameterized gradient flow (level set flow) of
d_{\partial W}|W
so that
\phi_{t}(x)\in\partial W_{t}
. Since
W
is locally convex at
x
, we have
\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}T_{\phi_{t}(x)}(W_{t})\geq T_{x}(W)
. That is, there is a distance non-increasing map from any Gromov-Hausdorff limit of
T_{\phi_{t}(x)}(W_{t})
as
t\to 0^{+}
to
T_{x}(W)
. This implies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\Theta(\phi_{t}(x),\,\partial W_{t})\leq\Theta(x,\partial W).

In particular, if
\Theta(x,\partial W)=0
, then
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Theta(\phi_{t}(x),\,\partial W_{t})=0
.
For
x\in\partial W
, we define the following directional singularity measure in the sense of distribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Theta(\phi_{t}(x),\,\partial W_{t})}{t},&\text{ if }\Theta(x,\partial W)=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

If
\Theta(x,\partial W)=0
and the metric in a neighborhood of
x
is
C^{2}
, then
W
can be chosen so that
\partial W_{t}
are smooth for
t>0
small. Then we have
\Theta(\phi_{t}(x),\,\partial W_{t})\equiv 0
and
\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W)=0
. This shows that for points
x
satisfying
\Theta(x,\,\partial W)>0
, the metric is not locally
C^{2}
. See Example
2.1
for which
\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W)
is supported on a co-dimension 1 subset.

To capture the singularities along all directions, we shall compute
\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i})
for
n
almost orthogonal subsets
W^{i}
, where
x\in\partial W^{i}
for every
i
. By Remark
3.13
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a co-dimension 2 subset
\mathcal{A}
, so that for any
x\notin\mathcal{A}
, there is a sequence of subsets
W^{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
so that

(i)

x\in\partial W^{i}
,

(ii)

W^{i}
is locally convex at
x
.

(iii)

\{W^{i}\}
are
\epsilon
-orthogonal.

Such a sequence
\{W^{i}\}
is called an
\epsilon
-frame (see Definition
3.9
for details). Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x)=\inf_{\{W^{1},W^{2},\dots,W^{n}\}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i}),

(2.3)

where the infinitum is taking over all family of frames.

Note that the value of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i})
may depend on the choice of frames. However, in Example
2.1
, as a measure,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i})\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is equivalent over different choices of
\epsilon
-frames, provided
0<\epsilon<c(n)
small. We conjecture that this is the case in general. That is, for
\epsilon
-frames
\{W_{1}^{1},W_{1}^{2},\dots,W_{1}^{n}\}
and
\{W_{2}^{1},W_{2}^{2},\dots,W_{2}^{n}\}
at
x
, it holds that

c_{1}(n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{1}}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i}_{1})\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{1}}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i}_{2})\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq c_{2}(n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{B_{1}}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{i}_{1})\,\operatorname{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

If this is true, then the infinitum in (
2.3
) can be dropped. Example
2.1
also indicates that
\mathcal{K}(x)
is equivalent to the Gaussian curvature at
x
in dimension 2.

As mentioned earlier, the following example illustrates the co-dimension 1 behavior of
\mathcal{K}(x)
. It also shows that
\mathcal{K}(x)
correctly captures the
C^{2}
-singularities.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathds{D}
be a flat disk in
\mathds{R}^{2}
with radius
r
. Let
X
be the doubling of
\mathds{D}
. Let
Z\subset X
be the glued two copies of
\partial\mathds{D}
. Construct
M_{i}
by smoothing
X
in a small neighborhood of
Z
, so that
M_{i}\overset{d_{GH}}{\longrightarrow}X
as
i\to\infty
. Note that
X
is flat away from the 1-dimensional subset
Z
. By the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{M_{i}}K_{g_{i}}\operatorname{d}vol_{g_{i}}=4\pi=\int_{Z}K_{x}\operatorname{d}s.

(2.4)

It’s easy to see that
K_{x}=\frac{2}{r}
for every
x\in Z
. This shows that the curvature on
X
is concentrated on the co-dimension 1 subset
Z
. The curvature measure
K_{g_{i}}\operatorname{d}vol_{g_{i}}
converges to measure
K_{x}ds
, supported on
Z
.

We will show that
K_{x}=c\cdot\mathcal{K}(x)
and
Z=\mathcal{KS}(X)
. For any
x\in Z
, there is a convex set
W
so that
x\in\partial W
and
B_{2\epsilon}(x)\cap\partial W
is a local geodesic. Let
W_{t}
be the sup-level set of the level set flow
\phi_{t}(x)
, which is defined in terms of
d_{\partial W}|_{W}
. Let
\delta(t)
be the intersection angle of
\partial W_{t}
and
Z
at
\phi_{t}(x)
. Note that
\Theta(t)=\Theta(\phi_{t}(x),\partial W_{t})
is equal to the exterior angle of
W_{t}
at
\phi_{t}(x)
. Direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}&r\cos\delta(t)=r\cos\delta(0)+t,\\
&\delta(t)=\delta(0)-\frac{\Theta(t)}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W_{0})=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Theta(t)}{t}=\frac{2}{r\sin\delta(0)}.

(2.7)

It’s clear that
\mathcal{K}(y,\partial W_{0})=0
for any
y\in B_{\epsilon}(x)\cap\partial W\setminus\{x\}
.
Note that
\delta(0)
can be chosen arbitrarily close to
0
. Thus
\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W)
may approach to
\infty
. However,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W_{t})dt=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{2}{r\sin\delta(t)}dt=\delta(0)-\delta(T)

(2.8)

is always bounded. One can also see (
5.13
) for a generalized version.

We now choose
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
so that their intersection angle is
\frac{\pi}{2}
. Let
\delta_{i}
be the intersection angle of
\partial W^{i}
and
Z
at
x
,
i=1,2
. We get

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{1})+\mathcal{K}(x,\partial W^{2})=\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{1}{\sin\delta_{1}}+\frac{1}{\sin\delta_{2}}\right).

(2.9)

Note that
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Choosing
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=\frac{\pi}{4}
to minimize the above sum, we get

\mathcal{K}(x)\leq\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{r}
.

3.
Covering by Convex Annuli

We develop a covering technique in this section. Each element in our covering will be an annulus-like region that satisfies a convex property. This technique will be applied recursively to create a stratified covering.

3.1.
Covering by good scale annulus

Let
\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
be an Alexandrov space. Let
p\in X
and
A_{a}^{b}(p)=\{x\in X\colon a\leq d(p,x)\leq b\}
denote the close annuli centered at
p
. In particular
A_{0}^{r}(p)=B_{r}(p)
is the closed geodesic ball and
A_{r}^{r}(p)=\{x\in X\colon d(p,x)=r\}
is the level set.

Definition 3.1

(Good scale annulus)
.

An annulus region
A_{a}^{b}(p)
is said to be an
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annulus if the following hold.

(i)

a\leq\sigma b
.

(ii)

There exists a metric cone
C_{p^{*}}(\Sigma)
so that

\displaystyle d_{GH}\Big(B_{s}(p),\,B_{s}(p^{*})\Big)\leq\epsilon s

(3.1)

for every
s\in[\sigma a,\sigma^{-1}b]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Covering by good scale annuli, Theorem 15.3.14
[
8
]
, Li-Naber
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\epsilon,\sigma>0
and
n\in\mathds{N}
.
For any
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
and closed subset
\Omega\subseteq\bar{B}_{1}
, there exists a collection of
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annuli
\mathcal{C}=\Big\{A_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}(p_{i})\Big\}
so that

(i)

\Omega\subseteq\cup_{i}A_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}(p_{i})
,

(ii)

p_{i}\in\Omega
for every
i
,

(iii)

|\mathcal{C}|<C(n,\epsilon,\sigma)
.

3.2.
Covering by convex annuli

We will show that a good scale annulus can be foliated by convex level sets. Consequently, the covering of good scale annuli can be adjusted to a covering of convex annuli. We first construct a
\left(-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}\right)
-concave function on
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annulus with
\sigma>0
small.

Lemma 3.3

(Concave functions on good scale annulus)
.

There exists a constant
c(n)>0
so that the following hold. Let
A_{a}^{b}(p)
be an
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annulus with
\mathcal{H}^{n}(B_{b}(p))\geq\nu b^{n}>0
. If
0<\sigma\leq\epsilon^{2n}\nu^{2}
, then there exists a
\left(-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}\right)
-concave function
f
on
B_{2b}(p)
so that
f^{-1}(-s)\subseteq A_{(1-4\epsilon)s}^{(1+4\epsilon)s}(p)
for any
0.5a\leq s\leq 2b
.

Proof.

Our constructions are similar to those in
[
5
]
, 3.6 in
[
11
]
, and 7.1.1 in
[
13
]
. However, we require more precise control of the constants.

Let
r=\sigma^{-1}b
. Define quadratic function

\varphi(s)=(s-r)-\frac{\epsilon}{b}(s-r)^{2}.

For any unit speed geodesic
\gamma\subseteq B_{2b}(p)
, we have

\displaystyle(\varphi(d(q,\gamma(s))))^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}-\frac{2\epsilon}{b}\left(\cos^{2}(\angle(\gamma^{+}(t),\uparrow_{\gamma(s)}^{q}))+\frac{d_{q}(\gamma(s))-r}{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}-\frac{2\epsilon}{b}\left(\cos^{2}(\angle(\gamma^{+}(t),\uparrow_{\gamma(s)}^{q}))-2\sigma\right).

(3.2)

Let
Q=\{q_{\alpha}\}
be a collection of maximum number of points in
A_{r}^{r}(p)
so that
d(q_{\alpha_{1}},q_{\alpha_{2}})\geq\delta r
for any
\alpha_{1}\neq\alpha_{2}
.
For each
\alpha
, let
\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}=\{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\}
be a collection of maximum number of points in
B_{\delta r}(q_{\alpha})\cap A_{r}^{r}(p)
so that
d(q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{1}},q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{2}})\geq\delta_{1}\delta r
for any
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
. Because
A_{a}^{b}(p)
is an
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annulus, we have that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle\geq c(n)\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}(p)\cap B_{\delta r}(q_{\alpha}))}{(\delta_{1}\delta r)^{n-1}}\geq c(n)\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{r}(p))}{(\delta_{1}\delta r)^{n-1}}\cdot\left(\frac{\delta r}{r}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq c(n)\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial B_{b}(p))}{\delta_{1}^{n-1}b^{n-1}}\geq c(n)\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n}(B_{b}(p))-(\sigma b)^{n}}{\delta_{1}^{n-1}b^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq c(n)\frac{\nu b^{n}-(\sigma b)^{n}}{\delta_{1}^{n-1}b^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(n)\nu}{\delta_{1}^{n-1}},

(3.3)

for
0<\sigma<\frac{1}{2}\nu
.

Define

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}|}\sum_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}}\varphi\circ d_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}.

Now we estimate the concavity of
f_{\alpha}
. For any
q\in B_{r}(p)
, by (
3.2
) and
b=\sigma r
, we have

\displaystyle(\varphi(d(q,\gamma(s))))^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}+\frac{4\epsilon\sigma}{b}=\frac{1}{r}+\frac{4\epsilon}{r}\leq\frac{2}{r}.

(3.4)

Given a direction
\xi\in\Sigma_{x}
, let
\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}_{x}(\xi)=\left\{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}\colon\left|\angle\left(\xi,\uparrow_{x}^{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{10}\eta\right\}
.
If
q\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}\setminus\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}_{\gamma(s)}(\gamma^{+}(s))
, we have
|\cos(\angle(\gamma^{+}(t),\uparrow_{\gamma(s)}^{q}))|\geq\eta
. Then (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle(\varphi(d(q,\gamma(s))))^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r}-\frac{2\epsilon}{b}(\eta^{2}-2\sigma)\leq\frac{2}{r}-\frac{4\epsilon\eta^{2}}{\sigma r}\leq-\frac{\epsilon\eta^{2}}{\sigma r},

(3.5)

provided
\sigma\leq\epsilon\eta^{2}
.

Note that
\mathcal{\mathcal{}}Q^{\alpha}_{x}(\xi)
is the minority in
\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}
. In fact,
d(p,x)\leq 2b=2\sigma r
and we have

\measuredangle\left({x}\,_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{j}}}^{q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{i}}}\right)\geq\tilde{\measuredangle}\left({x}\,_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{j}}}^{q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{i}}}\right)\geq\tilde{\measuredangle}\left({p}\,_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{j}}}^{q^{\alpha}_{\beta_{i}}}\right)-10\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta\delta_{1}-10\sigma\geq\frac{1}{4}\delta\delta_{1},

provided
\sigma\leq\frac{1}{50}\delta\delta_{1}
.
Thus
\displaystyle|\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}_{x}(\xi)|\leq\frac{c(n)\eta}{(\delta\delta_{1})^{n-1}}
.
By (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(\gamma(s))^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}|}\sum_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}}(\varphi(d(q^{\alpha}_{\beta},\gamma(s))))^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}|}\left(\sum_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}_{\gamma(s)}(\gamma^{+}(s))}(\varphi(d(q^{\alpha}_{\beta},\gamma(s))))^{\prime\prime}+\sum_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\notin\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}_{\gamma(s)}(\gamma^{+}(s))}(\varphi(d(q^{\alpha}_{\beta},\gamma(s))))^{\prime\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(n)\delta_{1}^{n-1}}{\nu}\left(\frac{\eta}{(\delta\delta_{1})^{n-1}}\cdot\frac{2}{r}-\left(\frac{\nu}{\delta_{1}^{n-1}}-\frac{\eta}{(\delta\delta_{1})^{n-1}}\right)\cdot\frac{\epsilon\eta^{2}}{\sigma r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(n)\eta}{\nu r}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{\delta^{n-1}}-\frac{\epsilon\eta\nu}{\sigma}+\frac{\epsilon\eta^{2}}{\delta^{n-1}\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(n)\eta}{\nu r}\cdot\left(\frac{2\epsilon\eta^{2}}{\delta^{n-1}\sigma}-\frac{\epsilon\eta\nu}{\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c(n)\epsilon\eta^{2}}{\nu r\sigma}\cdot\left(\frac{2\eta}{\delta^{n-1}}-\nu\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon},

(3.6)

provided
\sigma\leq\epsilon^{2}\eta^{2}
and
\eta\leq\frac{1}{4}\delta^{n-1}\nu
. For example, we can choose
\delta=\delta_{1}=4\epsilon
,
\eta=\epsilon^{n-1}\nu
and
\sigma=\epsilon^{2}\eta^{2}=\epsilon^{2n}\nu^{2}
. In particular, for any
\lambda<0
, function
f_{\alpha}
is
\lambda
-concave, provided
0<\epsilon\leq\frac{c(n)}{|\lambda|}
.

Now define
\displaystyle f(x)=\min_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(x)
. It’s clear that
f
is also
\left(-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}\right)
-concave in
B_{2b}(p)
. We claim that
|\nabla_{x}f|
is close to 1 for any
x\in A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)
.
For any
q\in\partial B_{r}(p)
and
x\in B_{2b}(p)
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi\circ d_{q}(x)-(d_{q}(x)-r)|

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{b}(d_{q}(x)-r)^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{b}d^{2}_{p}(x)\leq 2\epsilon\cdot d_{p}(x).

(3.7)

On one hand, for any
q_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{Q}
and
q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi\circ d_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}(x)\geq d_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}(x)-r-2\epsilon\cdot d_{p}(x)\geq-d_{p}(x)-2\epsilon\cdot d_{p}(x)\geq-(1+2\epsilon)\cdot d_{p}(x)

(3.8)

Thus
f_{\alpha}(x)\geq-(1+2\epsilon)\cdot d_{p}(x)
for every
\alpha
and

\displaystyle f(x)\geq-(1+2\epsilon)\cdot d_{p}(x).

(3.9)

On the other hand, for any
x\in A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)
, by the good scale annulus structure, there exists
q_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{Q}
so that
\measuredangle\left({p}\,_{x}^{q_{\alpha}}\right)\leq 10(\delta+\epsilon)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\measuredangle\left({p}\,_{x}^{q_{\alpha}}\right)\leq 10(\delta+\epsilon+\delta\delta_{1})\leq 60\epsilon,

(3.10)

for every
q^{\alpha}_{\beta}\in\mathcal{Q}^{\alpha}
, provided
0<\delta\leq 4\epsilon
. By triangle comparison, this implies

\displaystyle d_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}(x)\leq r-d_{p}(x)\cos(60\epsilon).

(3.11)

Thus by (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\varphi\circ d_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq d_{q^{\alpha}_{\beta}}(x)-r+2\epsilon\cdot d_{p}(x)\leq-(\cos(60\epsilon)-2\epsilon)d_{p}(x)

\displaystyle\leq-(1-4\epsilon)d_{p}(x).

(3.12)

Therefore,

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f_{\alpha}(x)\leq-(1-4\epsilon)d_{p}(x).

(3.13)

Combine (
3.9
) and (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(x)+d_{p}(x)}{d_{p}(x)}\right|\leq 4\epsilon.

(3.14)

In particular,
f^{-1}(-s)\subseteq A_{(1-4\epsilon)s}^{(1+4\epsilon)s}(p)
for any
s\leq 2b
.
∎

Now we show that the above concave function induces a convex annulus.

Definition 3.4

(Convex annulus)
.

A triple
(W,p,T)
is said to be an
(\epsilon,\lambda)
-convex annulus if there exist
b>a\geq 0
so that the following hold.

(i)

(Good scale)
A_{a}^{b}(p)
is an
(\epsilon,\epsilon)
-good scale annulus and
A_{a}^{b}(p)\subseteq W\subseteq A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)
.

(ii)

(Convexity) There exists a hyper surface
H\subset X
so that
W=\cup_{0\leq t\leq T}H_{t}
, where
H_{t}=\{x\in W\colon d(x,H)=t\}
and the distance function
d_{H_{t}}
is
\lambda
-concave on the sup-level set
W_{t}=\cup_{t\leq s<T}H_{s}
.

As an abuse of notation, for a convex annulus
(W,p,T)
, we denote the associated
H
and
H_{t}
by
\partial W
and
\partial W_{t}
respectively. We may also omit
p
or
T
in
(W,p,T)
if it there is no ambiguity.

Remark 3.5
.

In our definition, the boundary
\partial W
of a convex annulus
W
is a convex level set. However,
W
may not be a convex set if
a\neq 0
in the associated annular region
A_{a}^{b}(p)
.

Lemma 3.6

(Convex annulus on good scale annulus)
.

There exists a constant
c(n)>0
so that the following hold. Let
A_{a}^{b}(p)
be an
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annulus with
\mathcal{H}^{n}(B_{b}(p))\geq\nu b^{n}>0
. If
0<\sigma\leq\epsilon^{2n}\nu^{2}
, then there exists an
\left(\epsilon,-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}\right)
-convex annulus
W
so that
A_{a}^{b}(p)\subseteq W\subseteq A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)
.

Proof.

Let
f
be defined as in Lemma
3.3
and
H=f^{-1}(-1.8b)
. Then
H\subseteq A_{(1-4\epsilon)1.8b}^{(1+4\epsilon)1.8b}(p)\subseteq A_{1.5b}^{2b}(p)
and

\displaystyle d_{GH}(H,A_{1.8b}^{1.8b}(p))<10\epsilon b.

(3.15)

To construct the convex annulus
W
, we let

H_{t}=\big\{x\in B_{2b}(p)\colon f(x)\geq-1.8b\text{ and }d(x,H)=t\big\}

for
0<t<T=1.8b-0.8a
. Define
W_{t}=\cup_{t\leq s<T}H_{t}
and
W=W_{0}
. By the good scale condition on
A_{a}^{b}(p)
, we have
A_{a}^{b}(p)\subseteq W=\cup_{0\leq t<T}H_{t}\subseteq A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)
. Thus
W
satisfies Definition
3.4
(i).

It follows from the good scale and (
3.15
) that
|\nabla_{x}d_{H}-1|<20\epsilon
for any
x\in A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)\setminus\{p\}
. Therefore, we have
H_{t}\subseteq A_{(1-30\epsilon)(1.8b-t)}^{(1+30\epsilon)(1.8b-t)}(p)
,
d_{GH}(H,A_{1.8b-t}^{1.8b-t}(p))<60\epsilon b
and
||\nabla_{x}d_{H_{t}}|-1|<60\epsilon
for any
x\in A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)\setminus\{p\}
.

Let
\lambda=-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}<-100
. Direct computation shows that level sets
H_{t}
,
0\leq t<T
are all “very” convex and
d_{H_{t}}
is
\frac{1}{2}\lambda
-concave on
W_{t}
for
\epsilon>0
small. For details of the computation one can refer to
[
1
]
. Let us outline the proof below. By (
3.14
), we have
||\nabla_{x}f|-1|\leq 4\epsilon
for any
x\in A_{0.5a}^{2b}(p)\setminus\{p\}
. By Theorem 1.6
[
1
]
, we have that the extrinsic curvature of
H=f^{-1}(-1.8b)
is at least
|\lambda|
, by choosing
\epsilon>0
small.

By Corollary 1.9
[
1
]
and
X\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
, we have that
d_{H}|_{B_{1.5b}(p)}
is concave. Moreover, because the extrinsic curvature of level sets are not less than the model case, due to (4.4) in
[
1
]
, we have that
d_{H}
is
\frac{1}{2}\lambda
-concave on
W
. By Theorem 1.6
[
1
]
again, we have that the extrinsic curvature of
H_{t}
is at least
|\lambda|
for every
0\leq t<T
. Apply Corollary 1.9
[
1
]
once more. We get that
d_{H_{t}}
is
\frac{1}{4}\lambda
-concave on
W_{t}
for every
0\leq t<T
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Because
\partial W=H
is very convex, the gradient flow of
d_{\partial W}|_{B_{1.5b}(p)}
will contract
W\supseteq A_{a}^{b}(p)
to a point. However, we don’t know whether it contracts to a point in finite time if
a>0
. This is because
\nabla_{x}d_{H}
may not be close to 1 in
B_{s}(p)
for
0<s\leq a
small. By the same reason, the concavity of
d_{H_{t}}
may not be preserved for
t>T
.

Theorem 3.8

(Covering by convex annuli)
.

Let
(X,p)\in\text{Alex\,}^{n}(-1)
and
\text{Vol}\left(B_{1}(p)\right)\geq\nu>0
.
For any closed subset
\Omega\subseteq\bar{B}_{1}(p)
, there exists a collection of
\left(\epsilon,-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}\right)
-convex annuli
\mathcal{W}=\{(W_{i},p_{i},T_{i})\}
so that

(i)

\Omega\subseteq\cup_{i}W_{i}
,

(ii)

p_{i}\in\Omega
for every
i
,

(iii)

|\mathcal{W}|<C(n,\nu,\epsilon)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, we can cover
\Omega
by a collection of
(\epsilon,\sigma)
-good scale annuli
\mathcal{C}=\Big\{A_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}(p_{i})\Big\}
, with
p_{i}\in\Omega
and
|\mathcal{C}|<c(n,\epsilon,\sigma)
. For each
i
, by Bishop-Gromov volume comparison, we have

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n}(B_{b_{i}}(p))}{b_{i}^{n}}\geq c(n)\cdot\mathcal{H}^{n}(B_{1}(p))\geq c(n)\nu>0.

By choosing
\sigma=\epsilon^{2n}\nu^{2}
as in the assumption of Lemma
3.6
, every good scale annulus
A_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}(p_{i})
can be covered by an
\left(\epsilon,-\frac{c(n)}{\epsilon}\right)
-convex annulus
W_{i}
with center at
p_{i}
.
∎

3.3.
Adjust the intersection angles

In order to cover
B_{1}
and capture the singularities in all directions, we will recursively apply Theorem
3.8
to establish a stratified covering. Comparison geometry will be applied on each of the strata in the proof of the monotonic formula in Section
4
. This requires that the intersection angles between convex annuli are not greater than
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Definition 3.9
.

A sequence of
(\epsilon,\lambda)
-convex annuli
(W^{i},p_{i},T_{i})
,
i=1,2,\dots,m
is called an
(\epsilon,\lambda,\delta)
-frame if

\displaystyle-2\delta\leq\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}_{t}},\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{j}_{s}}\rangle\leq-\delta

(3.16)

for any
x\in\partial W^{i}_{t}\cap\partial W^{j}_{s}
,
0\leq t<T_{i}
and
0\leq s<T_{j}
. The intersection
\displaystyle M=\cap_{i=1}^{m}\partial W_{t_{i}}^{i}
is called an
(\epsilon,\lambda,\delta)
-corner space of
\{W^{1}_{t_{1}},W^{2}_{t_{m}},\dots,W^{m}_{t_{m}}\}
. It’s clear that
\dim(M)=n-m
. For convenience, we define
M=X
if
m=0
.

Lemma 3.10

(Adjust the intersection angles)
.

Let
(W^{i},p_{i},T_{i})
,
i=1,2,\dots,m
be an
(\epsilon,\lambda,\delta)
-frame. Let
\displaystyle M=\cap_{i=1}^{m}\partial W^{i}
be an
(\epsilon,\lambda,\delta)
-corner space. The following holds for
0<10\epsilon<\delta<\frac{1}{200n}
small and
\lambda<0
.

For any
(\epsilon,\lambda)
-convex annulus
(W,p,T)
with
p\in\cap\partial W^{i}
, given by concave function
f(x)
in Lemma
3.6
, there exists a
(2\epsilon,\frac{1}{2}\lambda)
-convex annulus
(\widetilde{W},p,\frac{1}{2}T)
so that
W_{\frac{1}{2}T}\subseteq\widetilde{W}\subseteq W_{2T}
and

\displaystyle-24\delta\leq\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}},\nabla_{x}d_{\partial\widetilde{W}_{t}}\rangle\leq-12\delta

(3.17)

for any
1\leq i\leq m
,
x\in M\cap\partial\widetilde{W}_{t}
and
0\leq t<\frac{1}{2}T
. In particular

\{(W^{1},p_{1},T_{1}),(W^{2},p_{2},T_{2}),\dots,(W^{m},p_{m},T_{m}),(\widetilde{W},p,\frac{1}{2}T)\}

is an
(\epsilon,\frac{1}{2}\lambda,12\delta)
-frame.

Proof.

For
x\in M\cap W
, define

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(x)=f(x)+20\delta\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(d(x,p_{i})-d(p,p_{i})\right).

(3.18)

It’s clear that
\tilde{f}
is
\frac{1}{2}\lambda
-concave on the good-scale annulus for
\epsilon>0
small. The corresponding convex annulus
\widetilde{W}
is constructed as in Lemma
3.6
. We have
W_{\frac{1}{2}T}\subseteq\widetilde{W}\subseteq W_{2T}
because

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\tilde{f}(x)-f(x)}{d(x,p)}\right|\leq 20\delta.

(3.19)

Because
(W,p)
is an
(\epsilon,\lambda)
-convex annulus and
p,x\in M
, we have

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}},\nabla_{x}f\rangle|\leq 10\epsilon.

(3.20)

Because
(W^{i},p_{i})
are
(\epsilon,\lambda)
-convex annuli, we have

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}},\nabla_{x}d_{p_{i}}\rangle+1|\leq 10\epsilon.

(3.21)

Because
\{(W^{i},p_{i})\}
is an
(\epsilon,\lambda,\delta)
-frame, we have

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}},\nabla_{x}d_{p_{j}}\rangle|\leq 2\delta+10\epsilon.

(3.22)

Combing (
3.20
) – (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}},\nabla_{x}\tilde{f}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 10\epsilon+20\delta(-1+10\epsilon+m(2\delta+10\epsilon))

\displaystyle\leq-18\delta+30\epsilon\leq-15\delta,

(3.23)

provided
10\epsilon<\delta<\frac{1}{200n}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial W^{i}},\nabla_{x}\tilde{f}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-10\epsilon-20\delta(1-10\epsilon+m(2\delta+10\epsilon))

\displaystyle\geq-21\delta-11\epsilon\geq-23\delta.

(3.24)

Inequality (
3.17
) follows by the
\epsilon
-good scale on
\widetilde{W}
and

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla_{x}d_{\partial\widetilde{W}},\nabla_{x}\tilde{f}\rangle-1|\leq 10\epsilon<\delta

(3.25)

for every
x\in\partial\widetilde{W}
.
∎

We now recursively apply Lemma
3.8
to cover corner spaces and adjust the intersections angles by Lemma
3.10
. Then we have the following stratified covering theorem.

Lemma 3.11

(Stratified covering by convex annuli)
.

There exists
c(n)>0
so that the following holds for
\lambda<0<\epsilon<\frac{c(n)}{|\lambda|}
and

K_{M}^{-}(x)=\max\{-\min\{\sec_{M}(x)\},1\}
and
k=\dim(M)=n-m
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap B_{1}(q)}scal_{M}\operatorname{d}\text{vol}_{M}\leq c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left({\rm diam}(M)^{k-2}+\int_{M\cap B_{2}(q)}K_{M}^{-}\right).

(6.2)

Proof.

We prove (
6.2
) by induction on
\dim(M)
. The case of dimension 2 follows from Gauss-Bonnet formula. Let
\{W_{a_{\ell}}^{b_{\ell}}(p_{\ell}),f_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{c(n,\epsilon)}
be a flexible
\epsilon
-good scale annuli covering described as in Lemma
6.4
. Fix
\ell
and denote
p=p_{\ell}
,
a=a_{\ell}
and
b=b_{\ell}
. Let
L_{t}=M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}(t)
be the next stratum of corner space. Because
X
is smooth, we can perturb the defining functions
h_{i}
and
f_{\ell}
so that
M
and
L_{t}
are all smooth. For simplicity of the notation, and for the integration domain only, we will denote
M\cap B_{1}(q)
by
M
, and denote
M\cap B_{2}(q)
by
M^{(2)}
. Similarly, we denote
L_{t}\cap B_{1}(q)
by
L_{t}
, and denote
L_{t}\cap B_{2}(q)
by
L_{t}^{(2)}

Applying the inductive hypothesis on

\{W^{1},W^{2},\dots,W^{m},W_{a}^{b}(p)\},

we have

\displaystyle\int_{L_{t}}scal_{L_{t}}\leq c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left({\rm diam}(L_{t})^{k-3}+\int_{L_{t}^{(2)}}K_{L_{t}}^{-}\right),

(6.3)

where
K_{L_{t}}^{-}(x)=\max\{-\min\{\sec_{L_{t}}(x)\},1\}
.
Most of the following estimates are similar to those in Petrunin’s proof. To relate
scal_{L_{t}}(x)
and
scal_{M}(x)
, we need the following Gauss–Codazzi formula:

\displaystyle scal_{L_{t}}=scal_{M}-2{\rm Ric}_{M}(u,u)+G,

(6.4)

where
G(x)=\sum_{i\neq j}k_{i}(x)k_{j}(x)
and
k_{i}(x)
are the principle curvature of
L_{t}
at
x
in
M
. Let
b^{\prime}\in[b,2b]
be determined latter. Integrating (
6.4
) over
M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]
and by co-area formula, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}scal_{M}=\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}}\frac{scal_{L_{t}}}{|\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|}+\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}(2{\rm Ric}_{M}(u,u)-G),

(6.5)

We use the same Bochner formula as in Petrunin’s proof
[
14
]

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}{\rm Ric}_{M}(u,u)=\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}G+\int_{L_{a}}H-\int_{L_{b^{\prime}}}H,

(6.6)

where
H(x)=\sum k_{i}(x)
is the mean curvature of
L_{t}
in
M
. Plugging (
6.6
) into (
6.5
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}scal_{M}=\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}}\frac{scal_{L_{t}}}{|\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|}+\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}G+2\int_{L_{a}}H-2\int_{L_{b^{\prime}}}H,

(6.7)

It turns out that the right hand side can be controlled by
K_{M}^{-}
and the good scale on
W_{a_{\ell}}^{b_{\ell}}(p_{\ell})
.

First of all, by (
6.4
) again,

\displaystyle G=scal_{L_{t}}-scal_{M}+2{\rm Ric}_{M}(u,u)\leq scal_{L_{t}}+m^{2}K_{M}^{-}

(6.8)

By the good scale on
f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b]
, we have
||\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|-1|<10\epsilon
and
\displaystyle k_{i}(x)\leq\frac{2}{d_{p}(x)}
for every
x\in L_{t}
. Th latter one implies that
\displaystyle H(x)\leq\frac{2m}{d_{p}(x)}
. Plugging these estimates and (
6.8
) into (
6.7
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}scal_{M}

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}}scal_{L_{t}}+m^{2}\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}+c(n)\cdot a^{k-2}-2\int_{L_{b^{\prime}}}H,

(6.9)

We need to find a lower bound of
\displaystyle\int_{L_{b^{\prime}}}H
. Because the intersection angles of the level sets are all strictly less than
\frac{\pi}{2}
, we can choose the directions of
k_{i}
so that

\displaystyle\frac{d(\text{vol}(L_{t}))}{dt}=\int_{L_{t}}\frac{H}{|\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|}.

(6.10)

By the good scale structure, we have that

\frac{d(\text{vol}(L_{t}))}{dt}\geq-c(n)\cdot t^{k-2}

for some
t\in[b,2b]
.
Therefore, we can choose
b^{\prime}\in[b,2b]
so that

\displaystyle\int_{L_{b^{\prime}}}H\geq-c(n)\cdot b^{k-2}.

(6.11)

Plugging this into (
6.9
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}scal_{M}

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}}scal_{L_{t}}+c(n)\cdot\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}+c(n)\cdot b^{k-2},

(6.12)

for some
b^{\prime}\in[b,2b]
. Integrating the inductive hypothesis (
6.3
) over
t\in[a,b^{\prime}]\subseteq[a,2b]
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}}scal_{L_{t}}dt\leq c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left(b^{k-2}+\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}^{(2)}}K_{L_{t}}^{-}\right).

(6.13)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}scal_{M}

\displaystyle\leq c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left(b^{k-2}+\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}^{(2)}}K_{L_{t}}^{-}+\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}\right),

(6.14)

for some
b^{\prime}\in[b,2b]
.

It remains to find an upper bound of
\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}^{(2)}}K_{L_{t}}^{-}
in terms of
\displaystyle\int_{M^{(2)}\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}
. By Gauss–Codazzi formula again,

\displaystyle K_{L_{t}}^{-}\leq K_{M}^{-}+2(H^{-}+m\cdot\max\{k_{i},0\})\cdot(\max\{k_{i},0\}),

(6.15)

where
H^{\pm}(x)=\max\{\pm H(x),0\}
. Because
\displaystyle k_{i}(x)\leq\frac{2}{d_{p}(x)}
and
||\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|-1|<10\epsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{L_{t}}H^{-}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{L_{t}}\frac{H^{-}}{|\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|}

\displaystyle=2\int_{L_{t}}\frac{H^{+}}{|\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|}-2\int_{L_{t}}\frac{H}{|\nabla_{x}f_{\ell}|}

\displaystyle\leq c(n)\cdot t^{k-2}-2\cdot\frac{d(\text{vol}(L_{t}))}{dt}.

(6.16)

Integrating over
t
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M^{(2)}\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}(H^{-}\cdot\max\{k_{i},0\})\leq c(n)\cdot b^{k-2}.

(6.17)

Combining this with (
6.15
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b^{\prime}}\int_{L_{t}^{(2)}}K_{L_{t}}^{-}\leq\int_{M^{(2)}\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}+c(n)\cdot b^{k-2}

(6.18)

Plugging this into (
6.14
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}scal_{M}\leq c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left(b^{k-2}+\int_{M^{(2)}\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}\right).

(6.19)

Note that
scal_{M}(x)\geq-m^{2}\cdot K_{M}^{-}(x)
and
b^{\prime}\in[b,2b]
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{M\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b]}scal_{M}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left(b^{k-2}+\int_{M^{(2)}\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,b^{\prime}]}K_{M}^{-}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot c(n,\epsilon)\cdot\left(b^{k-2}+\int_{M^{(2)}\cap f_{\ell}^{-1}[a,2b]}K_{M}^{-}\right).

(6.20)

The result follows by summing up the above inequality for all
\ell=1,2,\dots,c(n,\epsilon)
.

∎

Non-abelian cohomology of Lie H H -pseudoalgebras and inducibility of automorphisms
Apurba Das

Abstract
. The notion of Lie H H -pseudoalgebra is a higher-dimensional analogue of Lie conformal algebras. In this paper, we classify the equivalence classes of non-abelian extensions of a Lie H H -pseudoalgebra L L by another Lie H H -pseudoalgebra M M in terms of the non-abelian cohomology group H n ​ a ​ b 2 ​ ( L , M ) H^{2}_{nab}(L,M) . We also show that the group H n ​ a ​ b 2 ​ ( L , M ) H^{2}_{nab}(L,M) can be realized as the Deligne groupoid of a suitable differential graded Lie algebra. Finally, we consider the inducibility of a pair of Lie H H -pseudoalgebra automorphisms in a given non-abelian extension. We show that the corresponding obstruction can be realized as the image of a suitable Wells map in the context.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras

The notion of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra over a cocommutative Hopf algebra
H
was introduced by Bakalov, D’Andrea and Kac
[
1
]
as a multivariable generalization of the concept of Lie conformal algebra
[
7
,
15
]
. A Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra is a left
H
-module
L
equipped with a
H^{\otimes 2}
-linear map
[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes L\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}L
, called the pseudobracket, satisfying skew-symmetry and the Jacobi identity (see Definition
2.1
for the detail explanation). Given a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L
, one can associate a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}=H^{*}\otimes_{H}L
called the annihilation algebra of
L
. When
L
is finitely generated as an
H
-module, and
H
is Noetherian then the annihilation algebra
\mathcal{L}
is a linearly compact Lie algebra. Moreover, there is a one-to-one correspondence between Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra representations of
L
and discrete continuous representations of
\mathcal{L}
satisfying a technical condition. Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras are also related to differential Lie algebras of Ritt and Hamiltonian formalism of the theory of nonlinear evolution equations
[
19
]
. In
[
1
]
the authors have introduced the cohomology of a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra with coefficients in a representation and observed that the second cohomology group classifies the set of all equivalence classes of abelian extensions. See also
[
21
]
for the description of the cohomology in terms of Maurer-Cartan elements in a suitable graded Lie algebra. Later, various representation theoretic studies of simple Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras were made in
[
2
,
3
,
8
]
. See also
[
6
,
18
,
22
]
for some other types of pseudoalgebras and associated theories.

1.2.
Non-abelian extensions and cohomology

The non-abelian cohomology was first considered by Eilenberg and Maclane to study group extensions with non-abelian kernel
[
9
]
(see also
[
13
]
). Non-abelian cohomology generalizes the classical group cohomology (with coefficients in a module) that describes abelian extensions. Subsequently, non-abelian extensions and non-abelian cohomology for Lie algebras were studied by Hochschild
[
12
]
. The coefficient in the non-abelian cohomology is not a representation space but another Lie algebra with a map that is a representation up to an inner automorphism. Please see
[
16
,
10
,
11
]
for recent developments on non-abelian extensions of Lie groups, Lie algebras and some other types of algebraic structures. In particular, Frégier
[
11
]
showed that the non-abelian cohomology group of a Lie algebra with values in another Lie algebra can be seen as the Deligne groupoid of a suitable differential graded Lie algebra. Further, the classification of abelian extensions of a Lie algebra by the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology can be realized as the tangent cohomology complex of the Deligne groupoid.

1.3.
The inducibility problem and the Wells exact sequence

Given an extension of some algebraic structure, the problem of inducibility of a pair of automorphisms is another interesting problem of research. This question was first considered by Wells
[
20
]
for abelian extensions of abstract groups and further studied in
[
17
,
14
]
. In particular, Wells constructed a map (now popularly known as the Wells map) and showed that the obstruction for the inducibility of a pair of group automorphisms lies in the image of that map. He also finds a short exact sequence connecting the Wells map and various automorphism groups. Given an abelian extension of Lie algebras, the inducibility problem and the Wells map were studied in
[
5
]
. However, the problem becomes more intricate when we start with non-abelian extensions of Lie algebras. This is simply because the domain and codomain of the Wells map will change. This problem can be stated as follows. Let
0\rightarrow M\xrightarrow{i}E\xrightarrow{p}L\rightarrow 0
be a given non-abelian extension of Lie algebras. For any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(E)
with
\gamma(M)\subset M
, there is a pair of Lie algebra automorphisms
(\gamma|_{M},p\gamma s)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
, where
s
is any section of the map
p
. A pair of Lie algebra automorphisms
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
is said to be inducible if there exists an automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(E)
with
\gamma(M)\subset M
such that
(\gamma|_{M},p\gamma s)=(\beta,\alpha)
. The inducibility problem then asks to find a necessary and sufficient condition for the inducibility of a pair of Lie algebra automorphisms.

1.4.
Layout of the present paper

Our aim in this paper is twofold. In the first part, we consider non-abelian extensions of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras and also introduce the non-abelian cohomology
H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
of a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L
with values in another Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
M
. We show that the set of all equivalence classes of non-abelian extensions of
L
by
M
can be classified by the cohomology group
H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
. This result generalizes the classification result of abelian extensions
[
1
]
. Next, given two Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras

and

, we first construct a suitable differential graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
using a generalization of the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket (see
[
21
]
for the bracket). Finally, we show that the non-abelian cohomology
H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
can be realized as the Deligne groupoid of the differential graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
constructed above. This result generalizes the work of Frégier in the context of pseudoalgebras. However, our proof is new and much simpler than
[
11
]
. We also discuss abelian extensions in terms of the tangent cohomology complex.

In the next part of the paper, we consider the inducibility of a pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms in a given extension. Unlike the Lie algebra case developed in
[
5
]
, we do not restrict ourselves to only abelian extensions. More precisely, we start with a non-abelian extension

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M,[\cdot*\cdot]_{M})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{(E,[\cdot*\cdot]_{E})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras and construct the Wells map

in the context. We show that a pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
is inducible if and only if
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
. It shows that the image
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))
of the Wells maps is an obstruction for the inducibility of the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms. Finally, we derive a short exact sequence (a generalization of the Wells short exact sequence) connecting various automorphism groups and the Wells map.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some necessary background about Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras, their representations and cohomology. In Section

, we study non-abelian extensions and the non-abelian cohomology of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras. We interpret the non-abelian cohomology as the Deligne groupoid in Section
4

Some background on Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras

In this section, we recall some necessary background on Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras. More precisely, we recall representations and cohomology of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras following the references
[
1

Let
H
be a cocommutative Hopf algebra with the comultiplication map
\Delta:H\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}
and the counit map
\varepsilon:H\rightarrow{\bf k}
. We follow Sweedler’s notation to represent the images of the comultiplication map.

Let
L
be a left
H
-module. A
{\bf k}
-linear map
[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes L\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}L
is said to be

(i)
H^{\otimes 2}
-linear
if
[fx*gy]_{L}=((f\otimes g)\otimes_{H}1)[x*y]_{L}
, for all
x,y\in L
and
f,g\in H
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\text{ if }[x*y]_{L}=\sum_{i}(f_{i}\otimes g_{i})\otimes_{H}e_{i}~\text{ then }~[fx*gy]_{L}=\sum_{i}(ff_{i}\otimes gg_{i})\otimes_{H}e_{i}.

(ii)
skew-symmetric
if
[y*x]_{L}=-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}1)[x*y]_{L}
, for all
x,y\in L
. Here
\sigma_{12}:H^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}
is the flip map. The skew-symmetric condition simply means that

\displaystyle\text{ if }[x*y]_{L}=\sum_{i}(f_{i}\otimes g_{i})\otimes_{H}e_{i}~\text{ then }~[y*x]_{L}=-\sum_{i}(g_{i}\otimes f_{i})\otimes_{H}e_{i}.

Let
[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes L\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}L
be a
H^{\otimes 2}
-linear map. Then one can define two
H^{\otimes 3}
-linear maps

\displaystyle[[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}*\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes L\otimes L\rightarrow H^{\otimes 3}\otimes_{H}L~~~\text{ and }~~~[\cdot*[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}]_{L}:L\otimes L\otimes L\rightarrow H^{\otimes 3}\otimes_{H}L

as follows. Let
x,y,z\in L
. If

[x*y]_{L}=\sum_{i}(f_{i}\otimes g_{i})\otimes_{H}e_{i}
and say
[e_{i}*z]_{L}=\sum_{j}(f_{ij}\otimes g_{ij})\otimes_{H}e_{ij},

then
[[x*y]_{L}*z]_{L}
is the element of
H^{\otimes 3}\otimes_{H}L
given by

\displaystyle[[x*y]_{L}*z]_{L}=\sum_{i,j}\big(f_{i}f_{ij(1)}\otimes g_{i}f_{ij(2)}\otimes g_{ij}\big)\otimes_{H}e_{ij}.

(1)

Similarly, if
[y*z]_{L}=\sum_{i}(h_{i}\otimes l_{i})\otimes_{H}d_{i}
and say
[x*d_{i}]_{L}=\sum_{j}(h_{ij}\otimes l_{ij})\otimes_{H}d_{ij}
, then

\displaystyle[x*[y*z]_{L}]_{L}=\sum_{i,j}\big(h_{ij}\otimes h_{i}l_{ij(1)}\otimes l_{i}l_{ij(2)}\big)\otimes_{H}d_{ij}~\text{ as an element of }H^{\otimes 3}\otimes_{H}L.

(2)

2.1 Definition
.

A
Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
(or simply a
Lie pseudoalgebra
if
H
is clear from the context) is a left
H
-module
L
equipped with a
H^{\otimes 2}
-linear map
[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}:L\otimes L\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}L
, called the pseudobracket, satisfying the following properties:

- (skew-symmetry)
[y*x]_{L}=-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}1)[x*y]_{L}
, for all
x,y\in L
,

- (Jacobi identity) for any
x,y,z\in L
,

\displaystyle[[x*y]_{L}*z]_{L}=[x*[y*z]_{L}]_{L}-[y*[x*z]_{L}]_{L}.

(3)

We denote a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra as above by the pair
(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
or simply by
L
.

2.2 Remark
.

(i) When
H={\bf k}
is the base field, a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra is simply a Lie algebra over
{\bf k}
.

(ii) When
H={\bf k}[\partial]
is the algebra of polynomials in one variable
\partial
, a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra is nothing but a Lie conformal algebra
[
4
]
.

Let
(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
and
(L^{\prime},[\cdot*\cdot]_{L^{\prime}})
be two Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras. A
homomorphism
of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras from
L
to
L^{\prime}
is a
H
-linear map
\Theta:L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
that preserve the pseudobrackets, i.e.
[\Theta(x)*\Theta(y)]_{L^{\prime}}=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\Theta)[x*y]_{L}
, for all
x,y\in L.
Further, it is said to be an isomorphism if
\Theta
is an isomorphism of left
H
-modules.

Let
(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
be a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra. A
representation
of
(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
is a left
H
-module
M
equipped with a
H^{\otimes 2}
-linear map
\cdot*\cdot:L\otimes M\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}M
, called the action map, that satisfies

\displaystyle[x*y]_{L}*u=x*(y*u)-y*(x*u),\text{ for all }x,y\in L,u\in M.

It follows that the Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L
is a representation of itself with the action map given by the pseudobracket
[\cdot*\cdot]_{L}
. This is called the adjoint representation.

Let
(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
be a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra and
M
be a representation of it with the action map being given by
\cdot*\cdot:L\otimes M\rightarrow H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}M
. Then there is a cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}(L,M),\delta\}
, where the cochain groups are given by

\displaystyle C^{n}(L,M)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{k}\otimes_{H}M&\text{ if }n=0,\\
\{\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{\otimes n}}(L^{\otimes n},H^{\otimes n}\otimes_{H}M)~|~\theta\text{ is skew-symmetric}\}&\text{ if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

The coboundary map
\delta:C^{n}(L,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(L,M)
is given by

\displaystyle(\delta(1\otimes_{H}u))(x)=\sum_{i}\varepsilon(g_{i})f_{i}\otimes_{H}u_{i},~~\text{ if }x*u=\sum_{i}(f_{i}\otimes g_{i})\otimes_{H}u_{i}

and

\displaystyle(\delta\theta)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}(\sigma_{1\rightarrow i}\otimes_{H}1)~x_{i}*\theta(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j+1}(\sigma_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\rightarrow i\\
2\rightarrow j\end{subarray}}\otimes_{H}1)~\theta([x_{i}*x_{j}]_{L},x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{j}},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

for
1\otimes_{H}u\in C^{0}(L,M)
,
\theta\in C^{n\geq 1}(L,M)
and
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in L
. Here
\sigma_{1\rightarrow i}
and
\sigma_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\rightarrow i\\
2\rightarrow j\end{subarray}}
are respectively the permutations on
H^{\otimes n+1}
given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{1\rightarrow i}(h_{i}\otimes h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{i-1}\otimes h_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n+1})=h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n+1},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\rightarrow i\\
2\rightarrow j\end{subarray}}(h_{i}\otimes h_{j}\otimes h_{1}\otimes\widehat{h_{i}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\widehat{h_{j}}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n+1})=h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{n+1}.

The cohomology groups of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}(L,M),\delta\}
are called the
cohomology of
L
with coefficients in the representation
M
. We denote the corresponding cohomology groups by
H^{\bullet}(L,M).

2.3 Remark
.

(i) When
H={\bf k}
, the above-defined cohomology reduces to the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the Lie algebra
L
with coefficients in the Lie algebra representation

. On the other hand, when
H={\bf k}[\partial]
, it reduces to the cohomology of a Lie conformal algebra
[
4
]
.

(ii) It has been observed in
[

that the second cohomology group

classifies the set of all equivalence classes of abelian extensions of the Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L
by the representation

.

Let
L
be a left
H
-module. Then it has been observed in
[

]
that the (shifted) graded space
C^{\bullet+1}(L,L)=\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}C^{n+1}(L,L)

\displaystyle C^{n}(L,L)=\{\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{\otimes n}}(L^{\otimes n},H^{\otimes n}\otimes_{H}L)|~\theta\text{ is skew-symmetric}\}

inherits a graded Lie bracket. This bracket generalizes the classical Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket and the bracket is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\qquad\llbracket P,Q\rrbracket(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n+1})=(i_{P}Q-(-1)^{mn}i_{Q}P)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n+1}),\text{ where }

\displaystyle(i_{P}Q)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Sh}(m+1,n)}(-1)^{\sigma}(\sigma_{i\rightarrow\sigma(i)}\otimes_{H}1)~Q\big(P(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+1)}),x_{\sigma(m+2)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(m+n+1)}\big),

for
P\in C^{m+1}(L,L)
and
Q\in C^{n+1}(L,L)
. Here
\sigma_{i\rightarrow\sigma(i)}
is the permutation on
H^{\otimes m+n+1}
given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{i\rightarrow\sigma(i)}(h_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{\sigma(m+n+1)})=h_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes h_{m+n+1}.

Further, it has been shown that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra structures on the left

-module

and Maurer-Cartan elements of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{\bullet+1}(L,L),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket).
With the above notation, the coboundary map

of a Lie

-pseudoalgebra

\displaystyle\delta\theta=\llbracket\rho_{L},\theta\rrbracket,\text{ for }\theta\in C^{n}(L,L),

where

4.
Non-abelian cohomology as Deligne groupoid

In this section, we show that the non-abelian cohomology group
H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
of a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L
with values in another Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
M
can be seen as the Deligne groupoid of a suitable differential graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. More precisely, we show that the set of all non-abelian cocycles has a bijection with the set of all Maurer-Cartan elements of
\mathfrak{g}
. Further, the notion of equivalence between non-abelian cocycles coincides with the equivalence between the corresponding Maurer-Cartan elements. In the end, we also discuss abelian extensions in terms of the tangent cohomology complex.

First recall that a
differential graded Lie algebra
(in short dgLa) is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
in which
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{i}
is a graded vector space equipped with a degree
0
bilinear bracket
\llbracket~,~\rrbracket:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

- (graded skew-symmetry)
\llbracket x,y\rrbracket=-(-1)^{|x||y|}\llbracket y,x\rrbracket,

- (graded Jacobi identity)
\llbracket x,\llbracket y,z\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket x,y\rrbracket,z\rrbracket+(-1)^{|x||y|}\llbracket y,\llbracket x,z\rrbracket\rrbracket,

and a degree
1
linear map
d:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
d^{2}=0
and the following Leibniz rule

\displaystyle d\llbracket x,y\rrbracket=\llbracket dx,y\rrbracket+(-1)^{|x|}\llbracket x,dy\rrbracket,\text{for all homogeneous }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
be a differential graded Lie algebra. An element
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}
is said to be a
Maurer-Cartan element
of
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
if the element
\alpha
satisfies

\displaystyle d\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\llbracket\alpha,\alpha\rrbracket=0.

We denote the set of all Maurer-Cartan elements of
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
simply by the notation
MC(\mathfrak{g})
.

There is an equivalence relation on the set
MC(\mathfrak{g})
. First, recall that an element
\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{0}
is said to be
ad-nilpotent
if for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, there exists a natural number
n
such that
(ad_{\beta})^{n}(x)=0
, where
ad_{\beta}=\llbracket\beta,-\rrbracket
. Note that, if
\beta
is ad-nilpotent then one can make sense the operator
e^{ad_{\beta}}
. For an ad-nilpotent element
\beta
, we consider the element
g_{\beta}=-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\frac{1}{(n+1)!}(ad_{\beta})^{n}d\beta.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
be a differential graded Lie algebra with abelian
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
. Let
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in MC(\mathfrak{g})
be two Maurer-Cartan elements. They are said to be
equivalent
(and we write
\alpha\sim\alpha^{\prime}
) if there exists an ad-nilpotent element
\beta
such that

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}=e^{ad_{\beta}}\alpha+g_{\beta}.

It has been observed in
[
11
, Lemma 1.3]
that
\sim
defines an equivalence relation on
MC(\mathfrak{g})
.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
be a differential graded Lie algebra with abelian
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
. The
Deligne groupoid
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the groupoid whose set of objects is
MC(\mathfrak{g})
and whose set of Homs is empty for non-equivalent objects and reduced to one element for equivalent objects.

We denote the set of all connected components of the Deligne groupoid by
\mathcal{MC}(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{MC}(\mathfrak{g}):=MC(\mathfrak{g})/\sim.

Let
(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
and
(M,[\cdot*\cdot]_{M})
be two Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras. Consider the direct sum left
H
-module
L\oplus M
and the associated graded Lie algebra

\displaystyle C^{\bullet+1}(L\oplus M,L\oplus M):=\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{\infty}C^{n+1}(L\oplus M,L\oplus M),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket\big)

described in Section
2
. Since
L
and
M
are both Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras, the direct sum
L\oplus M
inherits a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra structure. This Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra structure gives rise to a Maurer-Cartan element (namely
\rho_{L}+\rho_{M}
) on the above graded Lie algebra. Hence we obtain a differential

\displaystyle d:C^{n}(L\oplus M,L\oplus M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(L\oplus M,L\oplus M),~~d:=\llbracket\rho_{L}+\rho_{M},-\rrbracket

which makes the triple
(C^{\bullet+1}(L\oplus M,L\oplus M),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
into a differential graded Lie algebra.

On the other hand, observe that the left
H
-module
M
can be equipped with a representation of the Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L\oplus M
with the action map

\displaystyle(x,u)*v:=[u*v]_{M},\text{ for }(x,u)\in L\oplus M\text{ and }v\in M.

Hence we may consider the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}(L\oplus M,M),\delta\}
defining the cohomology of the Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L\oplus M
with coefficients in the representation
M
. We define a subcomplex
C^{\bullet}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)
of the complex
C^{\bullet}(L\oplus M,M)
by

\displaystyle C^{\bullet}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)\cong\oplus_{(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}\times(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})}C^{m,n},

where
C^{m,n}
is given by restricting elements of
C^{m+n}(L\oplus M,M)
to
L^{\otimes m}\otimes M^{\otimes n}
.

For any

, it is easy to see from the definition of the bracket
\llbracket~,~\rrbracket
that
\llbracket\rho_{L},f\rrbracket\in C^{m+1,n}
and
\llbracket\rho_{M},f\rrbracket\in C^{m,n+1}
. Hence
d(f)=\llbracket\rho_{L}+\rho_{M},f\rrbracket=\llbracket\rho_{L},f\rrbracket+\llbracket\rho_{M},f\rrbracket
which shows that the map

satisfies

. On the other hand, for

and

, we have

. Hence

\displaystyle\llbracket f,g\rrbracket=i_{f}g-(-)^{(m+n-1)(p+q-1)}i_{g}f~\in C^{m+p,n+q-1}

which implies that
C^{\bullet}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)
is closed for the bracket
\llbracket~,~\rrbracket
. This shows that
C^{\bullet+1}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)
is a differential graded Lie subalgebra of
(C^{\bullet+1}(L\oplus M,L\oplus M),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d)
. In the rest of this section, we will denote the differential graded Lie algebra
\big(C^{\bullet+1}_{>}(L\oplus M,M),\llbracket~,~\rrbracket,d\big)
simply by
\mathfrak{g}
. Note that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{0}=C^{1}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)=C^{1,0}=\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(L,M)

and it can be easily checked that

is abelian and all elements of
\mathfrak{g}^{0}
are ad-nilpotent. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{1}=C^{2}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)=C^{2,0}\oplus C^{1,1}~~~\text{ and }~~~\mathfrak{g}^{2}=C^{3}_{>}(L\oplus M,M)=C^{3,0}\oplus C^{2,1}\oplus C^{1,2}.

4.2 Proposition
.

Let

(i) An element

(ii) Note that two elements

5.
Inducibility of a pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms and the Wells map

Given a non-abelian extension of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras, here we study the inducibility problem for a pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms. To find out the corresponding obstruction, we define the Wells map in the present context. Finally, we construct a short exact sequence connecting various automorphism groups and the Wells map.

Let
0\rightarrow(M,[\cdot*\cdot]_{M})\xrightarrow{i}(E,[\cdot*\cdot]_{E})\xrightarrow{p}(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})\rightarrow 0
be a given non-abelian extension of the Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
L
by the Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
M
. We denote
\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)
by the group of all Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(E)
for which
\gamma(M)\subset M
. For any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)
, we obviously have
\gamma|_{M}\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)
. Further, if
s
is any section of the map
p
, then we can define a
H
-linear map
\overline{\gamma}:L\rightarrow L
by
\overline{\gamma}(x)=p\gamma s(x)
, for
x\in L
. If
s^{\prime}
is any other section of the map
p
, then we have
(s-s^{\prime})(x)\in\mathrm{ker}(p)=\mathrm{im}(i)\cong M
. Therefore,
\gamma(s-s^{\prime})(x)\in M
and hence
p\gamma(s-s^{\prime})(x)=0
. This shows that the map
\overline{\gamma}
is independent of the choice of
s
. Next, we claim that the map
\overline{\gamma}:L\rightarrow L
is a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphism of
L
. To see this, we first observe that the left
H
-module
L
can be regarded as a left
H
-submodule of
E
via any section
s
. The space
E
is isomorphic to
M\oplus s(L)
. With this identification, the map
p
is simply the projection onto
L
. Since
\gamma
is bijective on
E
preserving the
H
-module
M
, it must be bijective on the
H
-module
L
. In other words, the map
\overline{\gamma}=p\gamma s
is bijective on
L
. Further, for any
x,y\in L
, we have

\displaystyle[\overline{\gamma}(x)*\overline{\gamma}(y)]_{L}=~

\displaystyle[p\gamma s(x)*p\gamma s(y)]_{L}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}p\gamma)[s(x)*s(y)]_{E}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}p\gamma)\big([s(x)*s(y)]_{E}-\chi(x,y)\big)~~~~(\text{as }\gamma(M)\subset M\text{ and }p|_{M}=0)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}p\gamma)\big((\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}s)[x*y]_{L}\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}p\gamma s)[x*y]_{L}=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\overline{\gamma})[x*y]_{L}.

Hence
\overline{\gamma}\in\mathrm{Aut}(L)
which proves our claim. Therefore, we obtain a group homomorphism

\displaystyle\tau:\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L),~\tau(\gamma)=(\gamma|_{M},\overline{\gamma}).

5.1 Definition
.

A pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms is said to be
inducible
if there exists an automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)
such that
\tau(\gamma)=(\beta,\alpha)
, i.e., the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
lies in the image of
\tau
.

Let
s:L\rightarrow E
be any section of the map
p
and let
(\chi,\psi)
be the non-abelian
2
-cocycle corresponding to the given non-abelian extension induced by
s
. Let
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
be any pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms. We define a skew-symmetric map
\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{\otimes 2}}(L\otimes L,H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}M)
and a map
\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H^{\otimes 2}}(L\otimes M,H^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{H}M)
by

\displaystyle\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y):=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\chi(\alpha^{-1}(x),\alpha^{-1}(y))~\text{ and }~\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,u):=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(y)),

.

The pair

(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})
with values in
(M,[\cdot*\cdot]_{M}).

Proof.

Since

(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})
. For example, if we replace

by
\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(u),\beta^{-1}(v)

\displaystyle[\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(u))*\beta^{-1}(v)]_{M}=\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),[\beta^{-1}(u)*\beta^{-1}(v)]_{M})-[\beta^{-1}(u)*\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(v))]_{M}

\displaystyle\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta^{-1})[u*v]_{M})

\displaystyle-[\beta^{-1}(u)*(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta^{-1})\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,v)].

If we apply

Note that the non-abelian

is so. We now define a map
\mathcal{W}:\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)\rightarrow H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\big((\beta,\alpha)\big):=[(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,\psi)],

the equivalence class of

.
The map

is called the

. The first important property of the Wells map is given by the following.

5.3 Lemma
.

The Wells map does not depend on the chosen section.

Proof.

Let
s^{\prime}:L\rightarrow E
be any other section of the map
p
and let
(\chi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
be the non-abelian
2
-cocycle corresponding to the given non-abelian extension and induced by the section
s^{\prime}
. We have already seen in Section
3
that the non-abelian
2
-cocycles
(\chi,\psi)
and
(\chi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
are equivalent, and an equivalence is given by the map
\varphi:=s-s^{\prime}
.

Next, we observe that

\displaystyle\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,u)-\psi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,u)=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\big\{\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(u))-\psi^{\prime}(\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(u))\big\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)[\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x)*\beta^{-1}(u)]_{M}=[\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x)*u]_{M},

for
x\in L
,
u\in M
. Similarly, for
x,y\in L
,

\displaystyle\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)-\chi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)=~

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(y))-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}1)~\psi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)}(y,\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x))

\displaystyle-(\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1})[x*y]_{L}+[\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(x)*\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}(y)]_{M}.

This proves that the non-abelian
2
-cocycles
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(\chi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)})
are equivalent. Hence the

-cocycles

\displaystyle(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,\psi)\quad\text{ and }\quad(\chi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi^{\prime}_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})

are equivalent, and equivalence is given by the map
\beta\varphi\alpha^{-1}-\varphi
. This proves the desired result.
∎

5.4 Remark
.

Note that the group
\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
acts on the space
H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
by

\displaystyle(\beta,\alpha)\cdot[(\chi,\psi)]=[(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})],

for any
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
and
[(\chi,\psi)]\in H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
. With this notation, the Wells map
\mathcal{W}
is simply given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=(\beta,\alpha)\cdot[(\chi,\psi)]-[(\chi,\psi)],

where
[(\chi,\psi)]\in H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
is the non-abelian cohomology class corresponding to the given non-abelian extension. This shows that the Wells map can be seen as a principal crossed homomorphism in the group cohomology complex of
\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
with values in
H^{2}_{nab}(L,M)
.

We are now in a position to find a necessary and sufficient condition for a pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms to be inducible.

4 Theorem
.

(Necessary and sufficient condition) Let
0\rightarrow(M,[\cdot*\cdot]_{M})\xrightarrow{i}(E,[\cdot*\cdot]_{E})\xrightarrow{p}(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})\rightarrow 0
be a non-abelian extension of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras. A pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms is inducible if and only if
\mathcal{W}\big((\beta,\alpha)\big)=0
.

Proof.

Let the pair
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
be inducible. In other words, there exists a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphism
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)
such that
\gamma|_{M}=\beta
and
\overline{\gamma}:=p\gamma s=\alpha
(for any section
s
of the map
p
). We define a map
\varphi:L\rightarrow E
by
\varphi(x):=(\gamma s-s\alpha)\alpha^{-1}(x)=(\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-s)(x)
, for
x\in L
. Observe that
p\varphi(x)=(p\gamma s\alpha^{-1}-ps)(x)=0
, for
x\in L
. This shows that
\mathrm{im}(\varphi)\subset\mathrm{ker}(p)=\mathrm{im}(i)\cong M
. Thus,
\varphi
can be realized as a map
\varphi:L\rightarrow M
. Next, if
(\chi,\psi)
is the non-abelian
2
-cocycle corresponding to the given non-abelian extension and induced by the section
s
, then we have

\displaystyle\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,u)-\psi(x,u)=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(\alpha^{-1}(x),\beta^{-1}(u))-\psi(x,u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)[s\alpha^{-1}(x)*\beta^{-1}(u)]_{E}-[s(x)*u]_{E}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[\gamma s\alpha^{-1}(x)*u]_{E}-[s(x)*u]_{E}\quad(\text{as }\beta=\gamma|_{M})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[\varphi(x)*u]_{E}.

Similarly, by a direct calculation, we get that

\displaystyle\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)}(x,y)-\chi(x,y)=\psi(x,\varphi(y))-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}1)~\psi(y,\varphi(x))-(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\varphi)[x*y]_{L}+[\varphi(x)*\varphi(y)]_{M},

for
x,y\in L.
These two observations shows that the non-abelian
2
-cocycles
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(\chi,\psi)
are equivalent by the map
\varphi
. Hence
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=[(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})-(\chi,\psi)]=0
.

Conversely, let
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
be such that
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
. Consider any section
s
of the map
p
and let
(\chi,\psi)
be the non-abelian
2
-cocycle corresponding to the given non-abelian extension and induced by
s
. Since
\mathcal{W}((\beta,\alpha))=0
, it follows that the non-abelian
2
-cocycles
(\chi_{(\beta,\alpha)},\psi_{(\beta,\alpha)})
and
(\chi,\psi)
are equivalent, and say by the map
\varphi:L\rightarrow M
. We now define a
H
-linear map
\gamma:E\rightarrow E
by

\displaystyle\gamma(e)=\gamma(u+s(x))=(\beta(u)+\varphi\alpha(x))+s(\alpha(x)),\text{ for }e=u+s(x)\in E.

Here we have used the fact that any element
e\in E
can be written as
e=u+s(x)
, for some
u\in M
and
x\in L
.

We claim that
\gamma
is bijective. Suppose
\gamma(e)=\gamma(u+s(x))=0
. Then it follows that
s(\alpha(x))=0
. Since
s
and
\alpha
are both injective maps, we have
x=0
. Using this in the definition of
\gamma
, we get that
\beta(u)=0
which implies that
u=0
. That is,
e=u+s(x)=0
. Hence
\gamma
is injective. To show that
\gamma
is surjective, we consider an arbitrary element
e=u+s(x)\in E
. Then the element

and we have
\gamma(e^{\prime})=e
. This proves that
\gamma
is surjective and hence our claim follows.

In the following, we show that the map
\gamma:E\rightarrow E
is a Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra homomorphism. Take any two elements
e=u+s(x)
and
e^{\prime}=v+s(y)
from
E
. Then we have

\displaystyle[\gamma(e)*\gamma(e^{\prime})]_{E}

\displaystyle=[(\beta(u)+\varphi\alpha(x)+s\alpha(x))*(\beta(v)+\varphi\alpha(y)+s\alpha(y))]_{E}

\displaystyle=[\beta(u)*\beta(v)]_{E}+[\beta(u)*\varphi\alpha(y)]_{E}+[\beta(u)*s\alpha(y)]_{E}+[\varphi\alpha(x)*\beta(v)]_{E}+[\varphi\alpha(x)*\varphi\alpha(y)]_{E}

\displaystyle\quad+[\varphi\alpha(x)*s\alpha(y)]_{E}+[s\alpha(x)*\beta(v)]_{E}+[s\alpha(x)*\varphi\alpha(y)]_{E}+[s\alpha(x)*s\alpha(y)]_{E}

\displaystyle=[\beta(u)*\beta(v)]_{E}-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(y,u)+(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(\alpha(y),\beta(u))

\displaystyle\quad-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(\alpha(y),\beta(u))+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(x,v)-\psi(\alpha(x),\beta(v))

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\big(\chi(x,y)-\chi(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))-\psi(\alpha(x),\varphi\alpha(y))+(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}1)\psi(\alpha(y),\varphi\alpha(x))+[x*y]_{L}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}\beta)\psi(\alpha(y),\varphi\alpha(x))+\psi(\alpha(x),\beta(v))+\psi(\alpha(x),\varphi\alpha(y))

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\chi(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}s)[\alpha(x)*\alpha(y)]_{L}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\big([u*v]_{M}-(\sigma_{12}\otimes_{H}1)\psi(y,u)+\psi(x,v)+\chi(x,y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\lambda)[x*y]_{L}+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}s)[\alpha(x)*\alpha(y)]_{L}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\beta)\big([u*v]_{M}+[u*s(y)]_{E}+[s(x)*v]_{E}+\chi(x,y)\big)+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\lambda)[x*y]_{L}

\displaystyle\quad+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}s\alpha)[x*y]_{L}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\gamma)\big([u*v]_{E}+[u*s(y)]_{E}+[s(x)*v]_{E}+\chi(x,y)+(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}s)[x*y]_{L}\big)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\gamma)\big([u*v]_{E}+[u*s(y)]_{E}+[s(x)*v]_{E}+[s(x)*s(y)]_{E}\big)

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\gamma)[(u+s(x))*(v+s(y))]_{E}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}\otimes_{H}\gamma)[e*e^{\prime}]_{E}.

Combining this with the fact that
\gamma
is bijective, we have that
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(E)
. Finally, for
u\in M
and
x\in L
,

\displaystyle\gamma(u)=\gamma(u+s(0))=\beta(u),

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}(x)=p\gamma s(x)=p\gamma(0+s(x))=p(\varphi\alpha(x)+s(\alpha(x)))=ps(\alpha(x))=\alpha(x).

Hence
\tau=(\gamma|_{M},\overline{\gamma})=(\beta,\alpha)
which proves that the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
is inducible.
∎

5.5 Remark
.

It follows from Theorem
4
that
\mathcal{W}\big((\beta,\alpha)\big)
is an obstruction for the inducibility of the pair
(\beta,\alpha)
of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms. Thus, if the Wells map vanishes identically, then any pair of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra automorphisms in
\mathrm{Aut}(M)\times\mathrm{Aut}(L)
is inducible.

In the context of classical Lie algebras, while considering the abelian extensions, it is well-known that the Wells map fits into a short exact sequence
[
5

. Our next aim is to generalize this sequence in the context of Lie
H
-pseudoalgebras (and for non-abelian extensions).

Let
0\rightarrow(M,[\cdot*\cdot]_{M})\xrightarrow{i}(E,[\cdot*\cdot]_{E})\xrightarrow{p}(L,[\cdot*\cdot]_{L})\rightarrow 0
be a given non-abelian extension of Lie
H

\mathrm{Aut}_{M}^{M,L}(E)\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Aut}_{M}^{M,L}(E)=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{M}(E)|~\tau(\gamma)=(\mathrm{id}_{M},\mathrm{id}_{L})\}.

That is,
\mathrm{Aut}_{M}^{M,L}(E)
is the group of all Lie
H

whose restriction on

\mathrm{id}_{M}
and projection on
L

\mathrm{id}_{L}
. Then we have the following result.

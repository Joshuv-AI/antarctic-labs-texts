Planar degeneracy of the three-gluon vertex
A. C. Aguilar, M. N. Ferreira, J. Papavassiliou, L. R. Santos

Abstract
We present a detailed exploration of certain outstanding features of the transversely-projected three-gluon vertex, using the corresponding Schwinger-Dyson equation in conjunction with key results obtained from quenched lattice simulations. The main goal of this study is the scrutiny of the approximate property denominated “planar degeneracy”, unveiled when the Bose symmetry of the vertex is properly exploited. The planar degeneracy leads to a particularly simple parametrization of the vertex, reducing its kinematic dependence to essentially a single variable. Our analysis, carried out in the absence of dynamical quarks, reveals that the planar degeneracy is particularly accurate for the description of the form factor associated with the classical tensor, for a wide array of arbitrary kinematic configurations. Instead, the remaining three form factors display considerable violations of this property. In addition, and in close connection with the previous point, we demonstrate the numerical dominance of the classical form factor over all others, except in the vicinity of the soft-gluon kinematics. The final upshot of these considerations is the emergence of a very compact description for the three-gluon vertex in general kinematics, which may simplify significantly nonperturbative applications involving this vertex.

Planar degeneracy and form factor hierarchies

Diagrammatic representation of the three-gluon vertex.

The full three-gluon vertex is represented diagrammatically in Fig.

The tensor

)
is a minimal modification of the basis
employed in
Pinto-Gómez et al. 2023a
;
Pinto-Gómez et al. 2023b
,
where
\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

was decomposed as

\displaystyle\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{i}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})\,\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{i}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,;

(17)

the tensors
\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{i}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
are related to the
\tau^{\alpha\mu\nu}_{j}(q,r,p)
through the simple relations

\displaystyle\tau^{\alpha\mu\nu}_{j}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{j}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,,\qquad j=1,2,4\,,

\displaystyle\tau^{\alpha\mu\nu}_{3}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{3}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)-\tfrac{3}{2}\,\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{1}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,.

(18)

Hence, the form factors
G_{i}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{i}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
of both bases are connected through the transformations

\displaystyle G_{1}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})+\tfrac{3}{2}\widetilde{\Gamma}_{3}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})\,,

\displaystyle G_{j}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{j}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})\,,\qquad j=2,3,4\,.

(19)

The motivation for using Eq. (
15
), instead of Eq. (
II
), is that, in the soft-gluon limit, the two tensors
\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{1,3}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
survive and become linearly dependent,
i.e.
,

(2/3)\lim_{q\to 0}\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{3}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\lim_{q\to 0}\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{1}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\lim_{q\to 0}\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,,

(20)

such that

\displaystyle\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{1}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)

\displaystyle=(2/3)\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{3}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)=2r^{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)\lim_{q\to 0}P^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(q)\,,

\displaystyle\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{j}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad j=2,4\,.

(21)

Hence, in the basis
\{\mkern 2.0mu\widetilde{\mkern-4.0mu\lambda\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 1.2mu_{i}\}
,

L_{sg}(r^{2})
is not obtained as the limit
of
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
as
q\to 0
,
but rather as a linear combination of
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}(0,r^{2},r^{2})
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{3}(0,r^{2},r^{2})
.
However, as we will show in Sec.
VI
,
G_{3}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
, and hence
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{3}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
, is sizable at
q=0
; such that the equivalent of Eq. (
14
) with
G_{1}
substituted by
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{1}
is a poor approximation in the soft-gluon limit.

In contrast, in the basis of Eq. (
15
), only
\tau_{1}^{\alpha\mu\nu}
is nonvanishing at
q=0
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\tau_{1}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)

\displaystyle=2r^{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)\lim_{q\to 0}P^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(q)\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{j}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad j=2,3,4\,.

(22)

Consequently, comparing Eqs. (
12
) and (
II
), we obtain simply

G_{1}(0,r^{2},r^{2})=L_{sg}(r^{2})\,,

(23)

i.e.
, Eq. (
14
) is promoted to an exact relation in the soft-gluon limit.
As a result, we find that the property of the
planar degeneracy, as captured by Eq. (
14
), is more accurately realized with the basis of Eq. (
15
) than with that of
Eq. (
17
).

Armed with these observations, in the rest of this article we will
focus on three key
issues, namely
the derivation of
L_{sg}(r^{2})
from the vertex SDE,
the dominance of
G_{1}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})

over the other three form factors,
and the quantification
of the planar degeneracy
(or the deviations from it)
in the case of
G_{1}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
,
for general kinematics.

To determine the transversely-projected three-gluon vertex,
{\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}}^{\alpha\mu\nu}
, defined in Eq. (
4
), the first step is to contract each external leg of the SDE of Fig.
3
with a corresponding transverse projector. Then, since we specialize to the
Landau gauge
, the gluon propagator
\Delta^{ab}_{\mu\nu}(q)=-i\delta^{ab}\Delta_{\mu\nu}(q)
assumes the completely transverse form,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu\nu}(q)=\Delta(q^{2})P_{\mu\nu}(q)\,.

(25)

Therefore, the legs of the three-gluon vertices attached to internal lines are automatically projected transversely as well.
Consequently, in the Landau gauge,
the SDE of Fig.
3
furnishes a self-consistent equation for
{\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}}^{\alpha\mu\nu}
, without reference to longitudinal tensor structures
Eichmann et al. 2014
;
Huber 2020
.

In terms of the diagrams shown in Fig.
3
,
the transversely-projected
three-gluon vertex is given by

\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{5}\bar{d}_{i}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,,

(26)

where

\bar{d}_{i}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p):=P_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(q)P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p)\,{d}_{i}^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)\,.

(27)

After carrying out the color algebra, the individual
contributions of the
graphs
{d}_{i}
in Minkowski space are given by

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{1}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=\lambda\!\!\int_{k}\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\alpha\sigma\beta}(q,-k_{1},k)\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\mu\beta\tau}(r,-k,k_{2})\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\nu\tau\sigma}(p,-k_{2},k_{1})\Delta(k_{1}^{2})\Delta(k^{2})\Delta(k_{2}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{2}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\!\!\int_{k}\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\alpha}(q,-k_{1},k)\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\mu}(r,-k,k_{2})\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\nu}(p,-k_{2},k_{1})D(k_{1}^{2})D(k^{2})D(k_{2}^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{3}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\lambda[P^{\alpha}_{\sigma}(q)P^{\mu}_{\beta}(r)-P^{\alpha}_{\beta}(q)P^{\mu}_{\sigma}(r)]\!\!\int_{k}\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\nu\sigma\beta}(p,-k_{3},k)\Delta(k_{3}^{2})\Delta(k^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{4}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=-\bar{d_{3}}^{\,\alpha\nu\mu}(q,p,r)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{5}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=-\bar{d_{3}}^{\,\nu\mu\alpha}(p,r,q)\,,

(28)

where we define
k_{1}=k+q
,
k_{2}=k-r
, and
k_{3}=k+p
, together with
\lambda:=ig^{2}C_{\rm A}/2
, with
C_{\mathrm{A}}
denoting the eigenvalue of the Casimir operator in the adjoint representation
[
C_{\mathrm{A}}=N
for SU(
N
)]. In addition, we denote by

D^{ab}(q)=i\delta^{ab}D(q^{2})
the ghost propagator, whose dressing function,
F(q^{2})
, is given by

D(q^{2})=\frac{F(q^{2})}{q^{2}}\,.

(29)

Furthermore,
{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{abc}_{\mu}(q,r,p)=-gf^{abc}{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\mu}(q,r,p)
stands for the ghost-gluon vertex, whose Lorentz decomposition reads

{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\mu}(q,r,p)=r_{\mu}B_{1}(r,p,q)+q_{\mu}B_{2}(r,p,q)\,;

(30)

at tree-level,
B_{1}^{0}=1
and
B_{2}^{0}=0
. Note that only
B_{1}
contributes to the transversely-projected form,
\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\mu}(q,r,p)
, defined as

\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\mu}(q,r,p):=P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(q){\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{\mu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)=r^{\mu^{\prime}}P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(q)B_{1}(r,p,q)\,.

(31)

Finally, we denote by

\int_{k}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\!\!{\rm d}^{4}k

(32)

the integration over virtual momenta,
where the use of a symmetry-preserving regularization scheme is implicitly assumed.

All quantities appearing in Eq. (
III
) are bare (unrenormalized).
The transition to renormalized quantities is implemented
by means of the standard relations

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=Z^{-1}_{A}\Delta(q^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{\mu}_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q,p,r)

\displaystyle=Z_{1}{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{\mu}(q,p,r)\,,

\displaystyle F_{\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=Z^{-1}_{c}F(q^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{\alpha\mu\nu}_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=Z_{3}{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,,

\displaystyle g_{{\scriptscriptstyle R}}

\displaystyle=Z_{g}^{-1}g\,,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{abcd}_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}\,\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(q,r,p,t)

\displaystyle=Z_{4}{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma^{abcd}_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}(q,r,p,t)\,,

(33)

where the subscript “
R
” denotes renormalized quantities, and
Z_{A}
,
Z_{c}
,
Z_{1}
,
Z_{3}
,
Z_{4}
, and
Z_{g}
are the corresponding (cutoff-dependent) renormalization constants.
In addition, we employ the exact relations

Z_{g}^{-1}=Z_{1}^{-1}Z_{A}^{1/2}Z_{c}\,=Z_{3}^{-1}Z_{A}^{3/2}\,=Z_{4}^{-1/2}Z_{A}\,,

(34)

which are imposed by the various Slavnov-Taylor identities
Taylor 1971
;
Slavnov 1972
.
Substituting the relations of Eq. (
33
) into Eq. (
III
)
and using Eq. (
34
), it is straightforward to
derive
the renormalized version of Eq. (
26
), given by

\displaystyle\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=Z_{3}\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\bar{d}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q,r,p)+Z_{4}\sum_{i=3}^{5}\bar{d}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q,r,p)\,,

(35)

where the
subscript “R” in
\bar{d}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}

denotes that the expressions given in
Eq. (
III
) have been substituted by their renormalized counterparts.
Of course, when the momentum integration over
k

is carried out,
the
\bar{d}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}
diverge; their combined
divergence will be removed subtractively, by adjusting appropriately the vertex renormalization constant
Z_{3}
. Instead, the multiplicative

Z_{4}
will be approximated simply by setting
Z_{4}=1
; this
is consistent with the fact that, at this level of approximation, the four-gluon vertex receives no quantum corrections.

In order to fix the renormalization constants appearing in Eq. (
35
), we adopt the asymmetric MOM renormalization scheme
Aguilar et al. 2020b
;
Aguilar et al. 2021c
;
Aguilar et al. 2021a
;
Aguilar et al. 2021c
. This scheme imposes that

\Delta^{-1}(\mu^{2})=\mu^{2}\,,\qquad F(\mu^{2})=1\,,\qquad L_{sg}(\mu^{2})=1\,,

(36)

which means that the gluon and ghost propagators assume their tree-level values at the subtraction point
\mu
, while an analogous condition is imposed on the three-gluon vertex in the soft-gluon limit.

In applying the asymmetric MOM condition at the level of the SDE, we should keep in mind that the transversely-projected vertex becomes ill-defined in the soft-gluon limit. Nevertheless, the form factor
L_{sg}(r^{2})
is well defined, as mentioned below Eq. (
13
), such that Eq. (
36
) fixes
Z_{3}
uniquely.

To see this, we simply factor out the term
P^{\alpha}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(q)
in Eq. (
35
), which becomes ill-defined in the
q=0
limit. Then, Eq. (
35
) becomes (with
Z_{4}=1
)

\displaystyle P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p){\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)=P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p)\left[Z_{3}\Gamma_{\!0}^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)\,+\sum_{i=1}^{5}d^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q,r,p)\right]\,,

(37)

which is well-defined as
q\to 0
.
Moreover, all terms in the above expression have the same tensor structure, of the form
r^{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)
, which is the only possible Lorentz structure of a generic tensor
T^{\alpha^{\prime}\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)
that is transverse to
r^{\mu}
and
r^{\nu}
. In particular

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p){\rm{I}}\!\Gamma_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r^{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)L_{sg}(r^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p)\Gamma_{\!0}^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r^{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(p)d^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r^{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)d_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}(r^{2})\,,

(38)

where
d_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}(r^{2})
denotes the scalar form factors of
P_{\mu^{\prime}}^{\mu}(r)P_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\nu}(r)d^{\,\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}(0,r,-r)
.

Then, the substitution of the above limits into Eq. (
37
) yields

\displaystyle L_{sg}(r^{2})

\displaystyle=Z_{3}+\sum_{i=1}^{4}d_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}(r^{2})\,,

(39)

where we have used the fact that in the limit
q\to 0
,
d_{5}
vanishes
in its entirety, and, in particular,

d_{5,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}(r^{2})=0
. In addition, notice that in this same limit, the diagrams
d_{3}
and
d_{4}
become equal.

By imposing the renormalization condition of Eq. (
36
), it is straightforward to see that Eq. (
39
) immediately determines that

\displaystyle Z_{3}=1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}d_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}(\mu^{2})\,.

(40)

Thus, substituting Eq. (
40
) into Eq. (
35
), one obtains the renormalized SDE for
\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}
, expressed as

\displaystyle\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}_{\!\!{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\left[1-\sum_{i=1}^{4}d_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}(\mu^{2})\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{5}\bar{d}_{i,{\scriptscriptstyle R}}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,.

(41)

It may seem at this point
that the determination of
Z_{3}
through the asymmetric scheme does not completely eliminate the divergences of the SDE, because, while all “swordfish” diagrams
diverge in general kinematics,
the
Z_{3}
of Eq. (
40
) is independent of
\bar{d}_{5}
. However, a simple
one-loop calculation illustrates
how Eq. (
40
) cancels correctly all divergences.
In particular,
using dimensional regularization, with spacetime dimension
d=4-2\epsilon
, we find that the divergent parts of
\bar{d}_{3,4,5}
are given by

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{3,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,(p_{\mu^{\prime}}g_{\alpha^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}-p_{\alpha^{\prime}}g_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}})P^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(q)P^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r)P^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(p)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{4,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,(r_{\alpha^{\prime}}g_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}-r_{\nu^{\prime}}g_{\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}})P^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(q)P^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r)P^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(p)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{5,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\,(q_{\nu^{\prime}}g_{\alpha^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}-q_{\mu^{\prime}}g_{\alpha^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}})P^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(q)P^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(r)P^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}(p)\,,

(42)

where

A:=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{15\alpha_{s}C_{\rm A}}{32\pi}\right)\,.

(43)

Then, we note that the sum of these divergences results in the tree-level tensor structure, for any kinematic configuration,
i.e.
,

\bar{d}_{3,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}+\bar{d}_{4,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}+\bar{d}_{5,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}=A\,\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,.

(44)

Next, specializing in the soft-gluon limit (
q=0
,
p=-r
), we find

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{3,\rm div}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)=

\displaystyle\,\bar{d}_{4,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)=A\,r_{\alpha^{\prime}}P^{\mu\nu}(r)\lim_{q\to 0}P^{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(q)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{d}_{5,\rm div}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(0,r,-r)=

\displaystyle\,0\,.

(45)

Using Eq. (
38
), it follows that the contributions of the
\bar{d}_{3,4,5}
to the form factor
L_{sg}(r^{2})
are given by

d_{3,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}=d_{4,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}=\frac{A}{2}\,,\quad d_{5,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}=0\,,\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,d_{3,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}+d_{4,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}+d_{5,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}=A\,.

(46)

Finally, using the above expressions in Eq. (
40
), we see that the contribution of
d_{3,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}
and
d_{4,\rm div}^{\,\scriptscriptstyle{sg}}
to the combination
Z_{3}\overline{\Gamma}_{\!0}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)
is precisely the negative of
\bar{d}_{3,\rm div}+\bar{d}_{4,\rm div}+\bar{d}_{5,\rm div}
in general kinematics, given in Eq. (
44
). Hence, the
Z_{3}
defined through the asymmetric MOM scheme captures the correct divergence of all swordfish diagrams,
\bar{d}_{3,4,5}(q,r,p)
, despite the vanishing of
\bar{d}_{5}(0,r,-r)
, thus ensuring the finiteness of the renormalized vertex in general kinematics.

In the following sections, we perform different projections of Eq. (
41
)
in order to extract different kinematic limits. When no ambiguity can arise we drop the index “R” to avoid notation clutter.

IV
Soft-gluon configuration

In this section, we consider the SDE determination of the soft-gluon form factor,
L_{sg}(r^{2})
, from Eqs. (

) and (
40
), by explicitly employing
the approximation given by Eq. (
1
).
Therefore, the level of agreement between the SDE outcome and available lattice data will constitute the first test of the accuracy of Eq. (
1
).

In order to appreciate how the
approximation of Eq. (
1
) is used in this context,
note that
of all vertices
\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)

appearing in the SDE of Fig.
3
, only one supplies a
form factor
L_{sg}(k^{2})
naturally,
i.e.

Infrared features revisited

In this section we take advantage of the simplicity offered by Eq. (

To this end, we begin by disentangling the contributions of the individual terms

As we can see on the left panel of Fig.

Next, we consider the behavior of

VII
Conclusions

We have presented an extensive study of the transversely-projected three-gluon vertex
by means of the SDE that determines its momentum evolution,
making ample use of dynamical ingredients obtained from
large-volume lattice simulations.
The focal point of this investigation is the notable
property of planar degeneracy:
after a judicious choice of the tensor basis,
the classical form factor of the vertex
depends predominantly on a single kinematic variable,
which represents a plane
in the space spanned by
q^{2}
,
r^{2}
, and
p^{2}
.
Our SDE-based approach
affords a valuable vantage point on the
technical details surrounding this
special property, establishing
its range of validity and degree of accuracy,
for a particularly wide range of kinematic configurations.
In fact, our analysis reveals that
the planar degeneracy persists to a high degree of accuracy
for general kinematics, and in particular
for configurations that deviate
completely from the bisectoral limit
(
p^{2}=r^{2}
), considered in
Pinto-Gómez et al. 2023a
.

Of central importance in the present study is the simple equation
given by Eq. (
1
), which leads to a serious reduction of the
technical effort required
when dealing with the three-gluon vertex in nonperturbative computations.
The relation given in Eq. (
1
) emerges by combining the planar
degeneracy with the observation that the classical form factor is considerably larger than all others.
The component
L_{sg}
appearing in
Eq. (
1
) corresponds to the form factor of the soft-gluon limit, a well-known quantity from a variety of lattice studies, and serves as a “benchmark” for the veracity of the SDE results.

The SDE analysis carried out probes the validity of Eq. (
1
),
and uses it in order to obtain a plethora of related results, which, in
turn, demarcate its applicability.
Our findings confirm
that the classical form factor clearly dominates over the
other three for a wide range of kinematics,
with the exception of the region approaching the
soft-gluon limit. However, due to the vanishing of the
corresponding basis elements in this limit,
Eq. (
1
) represents
an excellent approximation for all momenta.

One may wonder whether there exists a basis in which the
planar degeneracy becomes exact. To be sure, it is always possible to construct such a basis, by absorbing the residual dependence of the
{G}_{i}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})
on the other two variables into the basis elements themselves, through

\displaystyle\overline{{\rm{I}}\!\Gamma}^{\,\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}{\widetilde{G}}_{i}(s^{2})\,{\widetilde{\tau}}_{i}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,,

(75)

with

{\widetilde{\tau}}_{i}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p):={G}_{i}(q^{2},r^{2},p^{2})\,{\widetilde{G}}^{-1}(s^{2})\,\tau_{1}^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q,r,p)\,.

(76)

Nonetheless, such a basis would be of no practical advantage,
since it can only be constructed “
a-posteriori
”, namely
after the form factors have been exactly determined.
The truly remarkable observation is that there exists a “simple” basis containing the classical tensor as an element, which exhibits, in a natural way,
a rather accurate manifestation of planar degeneracy.

As mentioned in Sec.
III
,
the SDE for the three-gluon vertex
that we use is obtained from the 3PI three-loop effective action. The appropriate variation
of this action yields also
the SDEs of the gluon and ghost propagator, as well
and the SDE of the ghost-gluon vertex.
All these equations are dynamically coupled to each other,
and, from a strictly
SDE-based point of view, they must be solved simultaneously,
as a coupled system comprised by numerous integral
equations.
Instead, in our approach we treat the
SDE of the three-gluon vertex in isolation,
using lattice ingredients for the gluon and ghost propagators.
Evidently, what one is tacitly assuming when adopting this
approach
is that the lattice results “solve” the corresponding SDEs
to a very good approximation; that this is indeed so
has been shown in detail in
Williams et al. 2016
,
by solving the SDEs and comparing the solutions
with the lattice results.
We have therefore used the coincidence between 3PI SDEs and lattice,
found in
Williams et al. 2016
,
as our basic working hypothesis.

Finally, it would be interesting to investigate whether
some generalized form of planar degeneracy holds for the
transversely-projected four-gluon vertex
Pascual and Tarrach 1980
;
Papavassiliou 1993
;
Hashimoto et al. 1994
;
Driesen and Stingl 1999
;
Kellermann and Fischer 2008
;
Ahmadiniaz and Schubert 2013
;
Gracey 2014b
;
Cyrol et al. 2015
;
Binosi et al. 2014
;
Eichmann et al. 2015
;
Gracey 2017
, which, just as the three-gluon vertex,
is fully Bose-symmetric.
Such a study may be particularly timely,
given that lattice simulations are commencing to probe the basic structures of this vertex
Catumba 2001
.

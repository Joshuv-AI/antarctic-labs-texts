Introduction

Let
B

A derivation

We write
\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)

D:B\to B

The ring

\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)=\{0\}

Given a ring

We say that

We say that

One has:

\text{$B$ is rigid}\overset{\text{\rm def}}{\iff}\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)=\{0\}\ \begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-9.95845pt\Longleftarrow\hskip-14.22636pt\raisebox{1.42262pt}{\tiny$/$}\\[-6.54413pt]
\implies\end{array}\ \operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)=\{0\}\overset{\text{\rm def}}{\iff}\text{$B$ is graded-rigid.}

Also, it is well known that if

is a field of characteristic

{\rm\bf k}
-domain graded by a torsion-free abelian group,

The purpose of the above discussion is twofold: to help connecting the results of this article to existing literature, and to suggest the idea that the set

Geometry

Much of the literature in this area is framed in the language of polarized varieties,
whereas the present article is written in a purely algebraic language.
To facilitate connections between our results and the existing literature, we conclude this introduction
by recalling a few facts about polarized varieties and polar cylinders.
These remarks are not needed for understanding the article, and are not used in the body of the article.

1.3
.

Let
Y
be a normal variety with function field
K
.
We write
\operatorname{\text{\rm Div}}(Y)
(resp.
\operatorname{\text{\rm Div}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)
) for the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module (resp. free
\mathbb{Q}
-module) on the set of prime divisors of
Y
.
The elements of
\operatorname{\text{\rm Div}}(Y)
(resp.
\operatorname{\text{\rm Div}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)

(resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
) of

.
Two
\mathbb{Q}

D-D^{\prime}=\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{Y}(f)
for some

;
we say that
D,D^{\prime}
are
\mathbb{Q}

for some integer

If

\Gamma(U,\mathscr{O}_{Y}(D))=\{0\}\cup\big\{\,f\in K^{*}\,\mid\,\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{U}(f)+D|_{U}\geq 0\,\big\}\qquad\text{($\varnothing\neq U\subseteq Y$ open)}.

A divisor

is

\mathscr{O}_{Y}(D)
is a very ample invertible sheaf.
An

-divisor
of

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta

Suppose that
\Delta
is an ample

-divisor of

an open subset

2.
Preliminaries

This section gathers the definitions and facts that we need in this article.
All results are known, but we provide proofs when we are unable to give a reference.

2.1
.

We use “
\setminus
” for set difference, “
\subset
” for strict inclusion and “
\subseteq
” for general inclusion.
We follow the convention that
0\in\mathbb{N}
.

If
S
is a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{Z}
then
\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(S)
is defined to be the nonnegative generator of
the ideal
\bigcap_{a\in S}a\mathbb{Z}
of
\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, if
S
is an infinite subset of
\mathbb{Z}
then
\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(S)=0
.
Also,
\gcd(S)
is the nonnegative generator of the ideal of
\mathbb{Z}
generated by
S
.

All rings and algebras are assumed to be associative, commutative and unital.
If
A
is a ring then
A^{*}
denotes the set of units of
A
.
If
B
is an algebra over a ring
A
, the notation
B=A^{[n]}
(where
n\in\mathbb{N}
) means that
B
is isomorphic as an
A
-algebra to a polynomial
ring in
n
variables over
A
. If
L/K
is a field extension then
L=K^{(n)}
means that
L
is a purely transcendental extension of
K

of transcendence degree
n
.

The word “domain” means “integral domain”.
We write
\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}A
for the field of fractions of a domain
A
.
If
{\rm\bf k}
is a field, then a
{\rm\bf k}
-domain
is a domain that is also a
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra;
an
affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain
(or
{\rm\bf k}
-affine domain
) is a domain that is a finitely generated
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra.
If
A\subseteq B
are domains then the transcendence degree of
B
over
A
is denoted
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{A}(B)
or
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}(B:A)
.

If
A
is a ring, we write
\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(A)=\big\{\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}A\,\mid\,\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}{\mathfrak{p}}=1\,\big\}
.

If
S
is a subset of a group
G
, then
\langle S\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
G
generated by
S
.

2.2
.

Let
G
be an abelian group (with additive notation).
A
G
-grading
of a ring
B
is a family
\big(B_{i}\big)_{i\in G}
of subgroups of
(B,+)
satisfying
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}

and
B_{i}B_{j}\subseteq B_{i+j}
for all
i,j\in G
.
The phrase “let
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}
be a
G
-graded ring” means that we are considering the ring
B
together with
the
G
-grading
\big(B_{i}\big)_{i\in G}
.

Let
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}
be a
G
-graded ring.

(1)

An element of
B
is
homogeneous
if it belongs to
\bigcup_{i\in G}B_{i}
.
If
x
is a nonzero homogeneous element, the
degree
of
x
,
\deg(x)
, is the unique
i\in G
such that
x\in B_{i}
.
The degree of a non-homogeneous element is not defined.

(2)

B_{0}
is a subring of
B
and is called the
degree-
0
subring
of
B
.
If
B=B_{0}
, we say that the grading is
trivial.

(3)

Given a homogeneous element
f
of
B
,
B_{(f)}
denotes the degree-
0
subring of the
G
-graded ring
B_{f}=S^{-1}B
where
S=\{1,f,f^{2},\dots\}
.
Given a homogeneous prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
B
,
B_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
denotes the degree-
0
subring of the
G
-graded ring

S^{-1}B
where
S
is the set of homogeneous elements of
B\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(4)

If
H
is a subgroup of
G
, define
B^{(H)}=\bigoplus_{i\in H}B_{i}
and note that
the inclusion
B^{(H)}\hookrightarrow B
is a degree-preserving homomorphism of graded rings.

(5)

If
G=\mathbb{Z}
and
B_{i}=0
for all
i<0
, we say that
B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded. In this case we write
B=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B_{i}

and define
B_{+}=\bigoplus_{i>0}B_{i}
, which is an ideal of
B
.

(6)

The subgroup of
G
generated by
\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,B_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}
is denoted
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
.

(7)

We write
\mathbb{T}(B)
for the set of subgroups
H
of
G
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/(H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))
is torsion.

2.3 Theorem

(Thm 1.1 of
[
GY83
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated abelian group and
A
a
G
-graded ring.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

A
is a noetherian ring;

(b)

the ring
A_{0}
is noetherian and the
A_{0}
-algebra
A
is finitely generated;

(c)

every homogeneous ideal of
A
is finitely generated.

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
A
be a ring graded by an abelian group
G
, and let
H
be a subgroup of
G
.

(a)

If
I
is an ideal of
A^{(H)}
then
A^{(H)}\cap IA=I
.

(b)

If
A
is noetherian then so is
A^{(H)}
.

Moreover, if
A
is a domain then the following hold.

(c)

A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})=A^{(H)}

(d)

If
A
is normal then so is
A^{(H)}
.

Proof.

Write
A=\bigoplus_{i\in G}A_{i}
.
We first prove the case
H=0
of (a). Note that
A^{(H)}=A_{0}
.
Let
I
be an ideal of
A_{0}
and let
x\in A_{0}\cap IA
.
Then
x=u_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+u_{n}a_{n}
for some
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in I
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A
.
Moreover, we can arrange that
a_{i}
is homogeneous and
u_{i}a_{i}\in A_{0}\setminus\{0\}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
We have
a_{i}\in A_{d_{i}}
for some
d_{i}\in G
, and
A_{0}\setminus\{0\}\ni u_{i}a_{i}\in A_{0+d_{i}}
, so
d_{i}=0
and hence
a_{i}\in A_{0}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Thus,
x\in I
, showing that (a) is true when
H=0
.
For the general case of (a), let
\pi:G\to\bar{G}=G/H
be the canonical epimorphism and let
B=\bigoplus_{j\in\bar{G}}B_{j}
be the
ring
A
equipped with the
\bar{G}
-grading defined by
B_{j}=\bigoplus_{\pi(i)=j}A_{i}
for each
j\in\bar{G}
.
Since
B_{0}=A^{(H)}
and
B=A
, the claim follows by applying the case “
H=0
” to
B
.
This proves (a).

(b) If
I_{0}\subseteq I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}\subseteq\cdots
is an infinite increasing sequence of ideals in
A^{(H)}

then the sequence
I_{0}A\subseteq I_{1}A\subseteq I_{2}A\subseteq\cdots
stabilizes since
A
is noetherian.
By (a), we have
I_{n}=A^{(H)}\cap I_{n}A
for all
n
, so
(I_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
stabilizes, showing that
A^{(H)}
is noetherian.

(c) Given
x=\sum_{i\in G}x_{i}\in A
(where
x_{i}\in A_{i}
for all
i\in G
), define
S(x)=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,x_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}
.
We claim that

(1)

if
x\in A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})
and
x\neq 0
then
S(x)\cap H\neq\varnothing
.

Indeed, write
x=u/v
where
u,v\in A^{(H)}\setminus\{0\}
.
Then
u\neq 0
implies
S(u)\neq\varnothing
and
xv=u
implies
S(u)\subseteq\big\{\,i+j\,\mid\,i\in S(x),\ j\in S(v)\,\big\}
,
so there exist
i\in S(x)
and
j\in S(v)
such that
i+j\in S(u)\subseteq H
;
since
j\in H
, it follows that
i\in H
, so
S(x)\cap H\neq\varnothing
.
This proves (
1
).

Now consider
x\in A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})
. We can write
x=x_{H}+x^{\prime}
with
x_{H},x^{\prime}\in A
,
S(x_{H})\subseteq H
and
S(x^{\prime})\cap H=\varnothing
.
Since
x
and
x_{H}
belong to
A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})
, we have
x^{\prime}=x-x_{H}\in A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})
.
Since
S(x^{\prime})\cap H=\varnothing
, (
1
) implies that
x^{\prime}=0
, so
x=x_{H}\in A^{(H)}
.
Thus,
A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})=A^{(H)}
.

(d) If
A
is normal then
A^{(H)}=A\cap\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(A^{(H)})
is an intersection of two normal domains and hence is normal.
∎

2.5 Lemma
.

Let
B
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
, let
H
be a subgroup of
G
,
and let
S
be a multiplicative subset of
B^{(H)}\setminus\{0\}
such that each element of
S
is homogeneous. Then

S^{-1}\big(B^{(H)}\big)=\big(S^{-1}B\big)^{(H)}\,.

Proof.

Note that
S^{-1}B
is a
G
-graded domain.
Consider
\frac{b}{s}\in S^{-1}B
where
b
is a homogeneous element of
B\setminus\{0\}
and
s\in S
.
Since
\deg\big(\frac{b}{s}\big)=\deg(b)-\deg(s)
and
\deg(s)\in H
,
we have
\deg(b)\in H

\Leftrightarrow

\deg\big(\frac{b}{s}\big)\in H
, i.e.,

\frac{b}{s}\in S^{-1}\big(B^{(H)}\big)

\Leftrightarrow

\frac{b}{s}\in\big(S^{-1}B\big)^{(H)}
.
∎

The case
G=\mathbb{Z}
of the following result is well known.
We don’t know a reference for the general case, so we include a proof.

2.6 Lemma
.

Let
B
be a ring graded by a torsion-free abelian group
G
.

(a)

If
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}B
and
I
is the ideal of
B
generated by all homogeneous elements of
{\mathfrak{p}}
, then
I\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}B
.

(b)

If
B
is a domain,
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
contains a nonzero homogeneous element of
B
, then
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a homogeneous ideal.

Proof.

(a) Since
G
is torsion-free, there exists an order relation
\leq
on
G
such that
(G,\leq)
is a totally ordered abelian group
(cf.
[
AF88
, Prop. 1.1.7]
); we choose such an order relation.
Proceeding by contradiction, suppose that
x,y\in B\setminus I
are such that
xy\in I
.
Write
x=\sum_{i\in G}x_{i}
and
y=\sum_{i\in G}y_{i}
with
x_{i},y_{i}\in B_{i}
for all
i
.
The sets
S_{x}=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,x_{i}\notin I\,\big\}
and
S_{y}=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,y_{i}\notin I\,\big\}
are nonempty and finite,
so we may define
m_{x}=\max S_{x}
and
m_{y}=\max S_{y}
. Let
x^{\prime}=\sum_{i\leq m_{x}}x_{i}
and
y^{\prime}=\sum_{i\leq m_{y}}y_{i}
;
then
x\equiv x^{\prime}
and
y\equiv y^{\prime}\pmod{I}
, so
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in I
. Moreover, if we write
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in G}z_{i}
with
z_{i}\in B_{i}
for all
i
,
then
x_{m_{x}}y_{m_{y}}=z_{m_{x}+m_{y}}\in I
,
so
x_{m_{x}}y_{m_{y}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
so
x_{m_{x}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
or
y_{m_{y}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
so
x_{m_{x}}\in I
or
y_{m_{y}}\in I
, a contradiction.
This proves (a).

To prove (b), consider
I
as in part (a) and observe that
{\mathfrak{p}}=I
.
∎

2.7 Remark
.

Of course, the above Lemma remains valid if we replace the assumption that
G
is torsion-free
by the assumption that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
is torsion-free.
See
2.2
for the definition of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
.

2.8 Lemma
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{i\in G}R_{i}
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
,
let
S
be the set of all nonzero homogeneous elements of
R

and consider the
G
-graded domain
\mathcal{R}=S^{-1}R=\bigoplus_{i\in G}\mathcal{R}_{i}
.
If
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
where
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
then
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{0}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{r}^{\pm 1}]
where
\mathcal{R}_{0}
is a field that contains
R_{0}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
are
nonzero homogeneous elements of
\mathcal{R}
that are algebraically independent over
\mathcal{R}_{0}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
r\neq 0
.
Observe that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{R})=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Since every nonzero homogeneous element of
\mathcal{R}
is a unit, we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{R})=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,\mathcal{R}_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}

so we can choose nonzero homogeneous elements

is a basis of the free
\mathbb{Z}

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{R})
.
The reader can check that
\mathcal{R}_{0}
and

∎

2.9 Definition
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field and
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}
a
{\rm\bf k}
-algebra graded by an abelian group
G
.
If
{\rm\bf k}\subseteq B_{0}

over

{\rm\bf k}
, or that

is graded
over
{\rm\bf k}
by

.

2.10 Remark
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field,
B
a
{\rm\bf k}
-domain and
G
an abelian group.
If
{\rm\bf k}=\mathbb{Q}
or

is torsion-free then every
G
-grading of

is over
{\rm\bf k}
.
However, this is not true for arbitrary
{\rm\bf k}
and
G
.
For example, the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

admits a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-grading that is not over
\mathbb{C}

(namely,
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}i
).

2.11 Remark
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field and
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}
an affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain graded by an abelian group
G
.
If the grading is over
{\rm\bf k}
then
B
is finitely generated as a
B_{0}
-algebra and
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
is a finitely generated group.
Here is an example showing that these conclusions are not necessarily valid when the grading is not over
{\rm\bf k}
.

Let
R=\mathbb{C}[(\mathbb{Q},+)]
be the group ring of the group
(\mathbb{Q},+)
.
The elements of
R
are
formal sums
\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}a_{q}t^{q}
where
a_{q}\in\mathbb{C}
for all
q
and
a_{q}\neq 0
for at most finitely many
q
,
and where the family
(t^{q})_{q\in\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies
t^{q}t^{r}=t^{q+r}
for all
q,r\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Consider the subfield
{\rm\bf k}=\mathbb{C}((t^{q})_{q\in\mathbb{Q}})
of the field of fractions of
R
;
note that
t
is transcendental over
\mathbb{C}
and that
{\rm\bf k}/\mathbb{C}(t)
is an algebraic extension of infinite degree.
For each
q\in I=[0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
, consider the subspace

of the vector space
{\rm\bf k}
over
\mathbb{C}(t)
.
Then
{\rm\bf k}=\bigoplus_{q\in I}V_{q}
.
Let
G=\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
and let
\pi:\mathbb{Q}\to G
be the canonical homomorphism of the quotient.
Then
\pi|_{I}:I\to G
is bijective; let
\varphi:G\to I
be the inverse of
\pi|_{I}
.
So we have
{\rm\bf k}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}V_{\varphi(g)}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}{\rm\bf k}_{g}
where we define
{\rm\bf k}_{g}=V_{\varphi(g)}
for all

.
This is a
G
-grading of
{\rm\bf k}
such that
{\rm\bf k}_{0}=\mathbb{C}(t)
.
So
{\rm\bf k}
is a
G
-graded affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain,
{\rm\bf k}
is not finitely generated as a
{\rm\bf k}_{0}
-algebra, and
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}({\rm\bf k})=\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
is not finitely generated.

(Lemma 2.2 of

]
)

The set
\mathbb{T}(B)

2.14 Remark
.

Let
B
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
.

(1)

Let
\mathscr{T}(G)
be the set of subgroups
H
of

2.15 Lemma
.

Let

be a noetherian normal domain graded by an abelian group

, and let

(a)

\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(J)=\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(J\cap B^{(H)})
for every ideal

(b)

The map

,
is surjective and satisfies
f^{-1}\big(\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}B^{(H)}\big)=\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}B
.

(c)

If

,
where

(resp.

) denotes the set of height

(resp.

).
Moreover,
f|_{Z}:Z\to Z^{\prime}
is bijective.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,
B^{(H)}
is a noetherian normal domain;
moreover,

is integral over
B^{(H)}
by Rem.
2.14
(4).
By

, the Going-Down Theorem holds for

so assertion (a) follows from
[
Mat80
, (13.C)]
, and (b) follows from (a).
Now assume that

is torsion-free. If
{\mathfrak{P}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}B
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}B^{(H)}
are such that

,
then Lemma

{\mathfrak{P}}
is homogeneous if and only if
{\mathfrak{p}}
is homogeneous.
So
f^{-1}(Z^{\prime})=Z
. It is clear that
f|_{Z}:Z\to Z^{\prime}
is surjective.
Consider
{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z^{\prime}

2.16 Lemma
.

Let
B
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
.

If
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
and
M
is a submonoid of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
then
\langle H\cap M\rangle=H\cap\langle M\rangle
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\langle H\cap M\rangle\subseteq H\cap\langle M\rangle
.
For the reverse inclusion, consider
i\in H\cap\langle M\rangle
.
Since
i\in\langle M\rangle
and
M
is a monoid, there exist
j_{1},j_{2}\in M
such that
i=j_{1}-j_{2}
.
Since
j_{1}\in\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
and
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
, there exists
n>0
such that
(n+1)j_{1}\in H
.
Define
i_{1}=(n+1)j_{1}
and
i_{2}=j_{2}+nj_{1}
. Then
i_{1}\in H\cap M
,
i_{2}\in M
, and

H\ni i=(j_{1}+nj_{1})-(j_{2}+nj_{1})=i_{1}-i_{2},

so
i_{2}\in H
, i.e.,
i_{2}\in H\cap M
. So
i=i_{1}-i_{2}\in\langle H\cap M\rangle
.
∎

2.17 Lemma
.

Let
B
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
.

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})=H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\quad\text{for all $H\in\mathbb{T}(B)$.}

Proof.

Consider the submonoid
M=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,B_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}
of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
. Then
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)=\langle M\rangle
and

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})=\langle H\cap M\rangle=H\cap\langle M\rangle=H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
by Lemma
2.16
.
∎

2.18 Corollary
.

Let
B
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
.
For every subgroup
H
of
G
,

H\in\mathbb{T}(B)

\iff

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})
is torsion.

Proof.

It is clear that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})\subseteq H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
, so there is a surjective group homomorphism

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})\to\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/(H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))
and consequently
(\Leftarrow)
is true.
Conversely, if
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
then
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/(H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))
by Lemma
2.17
,
and since
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/(H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))
is torsion it follows that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})
is torsion, so
(\Rightarrow)
is true.
∎

2.19 Notation
.

Let
B
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
. For each
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
, we define

\mathbb{T}_{H}(B)=\big\{\,H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B)\,\mid\,H^{\prime}\subseteq H\,\big\}.

2.20 Lemma
.

Given a domain
B
graded by an abelian group
G
,

\mathbb{T}(B^{(H)})=\mathbb{T}_{H}(B)\quad\text{for all $H\in\mathbb{T}(B)$.}

Proof.

Since
B^{(H)}
is an
H
-graded ring,
\mathbb{T}(B^{(H)})
is a collection of subgroups of
H
;
clearly,
\mathbb{T}_{H}(B)
too is a collection of subgroups of
H
.
So it suffices to show that, for each subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
H
, we have
H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B^{(H)})\Leftrightarrow H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B)
.
Let
H^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
H
.
Let
U=H^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
,
V=H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
and
W=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
, and note that
U\subseteq V\subseteq W
and (since
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
)
W/V
is torsion;
so
V/U
is torsion if and only if
W/U
is torsion.
Since
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})=H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
by Lemma
2.17
, we have

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)})/(H^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B^{(H)}))=(H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))/(H^{\prime}\cap H\cap\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))=V/U,

so
H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B^{(H)})

\Leftrightarrow

V/U
is torsion.
Clearly,
H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B)

\Leftrightarrow

W/U
is torsion.
We already noted that
V/U
is torsion if and only if
W/U
is torsion, so

H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B^{(H)})\Leftrightarrow H^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(B)
, as desired.
∎

2.21 Lemma

From derivations of
B

The main result of the section is Thm

3.1 Lemma
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a domain of characteristic
0
graded by an abelian group
G
and suppose that
\mathcal{B}=R[t]=R^{[1]}

where
R
is a graded subring of
\mathcal{B}
and
t
is homogeneous.
For each
H\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{B})
, we have
R^{(H)}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}\big(\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\big)
.

Proof.

Note that
I=\big\{\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\,\mid\,m\deg(t)\in H+\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)\,\big\}
is a nonzero ideal of
\mathbb{Z}
, because
H\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{B})
.
Let
n>0
be such that
I=n\mathbb{Z}
. We claim that

(2)

there exists
i\in G
such that
R_{i}\neq 0
and
i+n\deg(t)\in H
.

Indeed, we have
n\deg(t)\in\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)+H
, so we may choose
g\in\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)
and
h\in H
such that
n\deg(t)=g+h
.
Since
M=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,R_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}
is closed under addition and the subgroup of
G
generated by
M
is
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)
,
we have
g=i_{1}-i_{2}
for some
i_{1},i_{2}\in M
. Since
H\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{B})
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
mi_{1}\in H
.
Define
i=(m-1)i_{1}+i_{2}
, then
i\in M
(so
R_{i}\neq 0
) and
i+n\deg(t)=(m-1)i_{1}+i_{2}+g+h=mi_{1}+h\in H
,
which proves (
2
).

Choose
i
as in (
2
), choose
\rho\in R_{i}\setminus\{0\}
,
and choose
d\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
di\in H
(
d
exists because
H\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{B})
).
Define
\xi=\rho^{d}
and
\tau=\rho t^{n}
; these are nonzero homogeneous elements of
R^{(H)}
and
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
respectively,
and
\tau
is transcendental over
R^{(H)}
.
We claim that

(3)

R^{(H)}[\tau]\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\subseteq\big(R^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}[\tau].

The first part of (
3
) is clear.
To prove the second part, note that
each element of
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
is a finite sum of elements
rt^{m}
where
r
is a homogeneous element of
R\setminus\{0\}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
\deg(rt^{m})\in H
. For such an element
rt^{m}
, we have
\deg(r)+m\deg(t)\in H
and
\deg(r)\in\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)
,
so
m\in I=n\mathbb{Z}
and hence
m=nk
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
rt^{m}=r(t^{n})^{k}=r\rho^{-k}\tau^{k}
.
Choose
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell d-k\geq 0
; then

(4)

\textstyle rt^{m}=\frac{r\rho^{\ell d-k}}{\xi^{\ell}}\tau^{k}\,.

Since
\deg(rt^{m}),\deg(\xi),\deg(\tau)\in H
, (
4
) gives
\deg(r\rho^{\ell d-k})\in H
,
so
r\rho^{\ell d-k}\in R^{(H)}
and hence
rt^{m}\in\big(R^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}[\tau]
by (
4
) again.
This proves
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\subseteq\big(R^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}[\tau]
and completes the proof of (
3
).

Now (
3
) implies that
\big(\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}=S[\tau]=S^{[1]}
where
S=\big(R^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}
,
so we may consider the derivative
\frac{d}{d\tau}:S[\tau]\to S[\tau]
.
Let
\Delta=\xi\frac{d}{d\tau}:(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})_{\xi}\to(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})_{\xi}
;
then
\Delta\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}\big((\mathcal{B}^{(H)})_{\xi}\big)
and
\ker\Delta=S
.
We claim that
\Delta(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
.
To see this, consider the same element
rt^{m}
as before and let us check that
\Delta(rt^{m})\in\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
.
If
m=0
then
\deg(r)=\deg(rt^{m})\in H
, so
rt^{m}=r\in R^{(H)}
and hence
\Delta(rt^{m})=0\in\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
.
If
m>0
then
k>0
, so (
4
) gives

\textstyle(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})_{\xi}\ni\Delta(rt^{m})=\xi\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(\frac{r\rho^{\ell d-k}}{\xi^{\ell}}\tau^{k}\big)=\xi\cdot\frac{r\rho^{\ell d-k}}{\xi^{\ell}}\cdot k\tau^{k-1}=kr\rho^{d-1}t^{n(k-1)}\in\mathcal{B}.

Since
\mathcal{B}\cap(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})_{\xi}=\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
by Lemma
2.4
, we get
\Delta(rt^{m})\in\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
.
Since every element of
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
is a finite sum of elements
rt^{m}
of this type, we obtain
\Delta(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
.
Let
D:\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\to\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
be the restriction of
\Delta
.
Then
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}\big(\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\big)
and
\ker D=\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\cap\ker\Delta=\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\cap\big(R^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}
.
Since
R
is factorially closed
in
\mathcal{B}=R^{[1]}
, it follows that
R^{(H)}
is factorially closed in
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}
,
so
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\cap\big(R^{(H)}\big)_{\xi}=R^{(H)}
and hence
\ker D=R^{(H)}
.
We have
D(\tau)=\xi\frac{d}{d\tau}(\tau)=\xi\neq 0
, so
D\neq 0
and hence
R^{(H)}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}\big(\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\big)
.
∎

3.2 Theorem
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0
, let
B
be an affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain graded over
{\rm\bf k}
by an abelian group
G
, and let
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
.

(a)

For each
A\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}(B)
, we have
A^{(H)}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}(B^{(H)})
.

(b)

The map
\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}(B)\to\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}(B^{(H)})
,
A\mapsto A^{(H)}
, is injective.

(c)

If
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\neq\{0\}
then
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B^{(H)})\neq\{0\}
.

(d)

If
G
is torsion-free and
B
is non-rigid then
B^{(H)}
is non-rigid.

Proof.

(a) Let
A\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}(B)
.
Choose
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\setminus\{0\}
such that
\ker D=A
, and choose a homogeneous element
t\in B
such that
D(t)\neq 0
and
D^{2}(t)=0
.
Consider the homogeneous element
a=D(t)
of
A\setminus\{0\}
and
let
\mathcal{B}=B_{a}
and
R=A_{a}
; then
\mathcal{B}=R[t]=R^{[1]}
by Lemma
2.22
(b).
Since
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B})=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
, we have
\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{T}(B)
and hence
H\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{B})
;
so
R^{(H)}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}\big(\mathcal{B}^{(H)}\big)
by Lemma
3.1
.
Choose
\Delta\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(\mathcal{B}^{(H)})
such that
\ker(\Delta)=R^{(H)}
.

Since
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\deg(a^{n})\in H
; let
\alpha=a^{n}
and observe that
\mathcal{B}=B_{\alpha}
.
By Lemma
2.5
, we have
\mathcal{B}^{(H)}=\big(B_{\alpha}\big)^{(H)}=\big(B^{(H)}\big)_{\alpha}
, so

\Delta\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}\big((B^{(H)})_{\alpha}\big)
.
Since (by Cor.
2.13
)
B^{(H)}
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine,
Lemma
2.21
implies that there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}

such that
\alpha^{\ell}\Delta:(B^{(H)})_{\alpha}\to(B^{(H)})_{\alpha}
maps
B^{(H)}
into itself.
Let
\delta:B^{(H)}\to B^{(H)}
be the restriction of
\alpha^{\ell}\Delta
; then
\delta\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B^{(H)})

and
\ker(\delta)=B^{(H)}\cap\ker(\Delta)=B^{(H)}\cap R^{(H)}
. To finish the proof of (a), it suffices to
verify that

(5)

B^{(H)}\cap R^{(H)}=A^{(H)}\quad\text{and}\quad A^{(H)}\neq B^{(H)}.

The fact that
A
is factorially closed in
B
implies that
B\cap R=B\cap A_{a}=A
, so

A^{(H)}\subseteq B^{(H)}\cap R^{(H)}\subseteq B^{(H)}\cap B\cap R=B^{(H)}\cap A=A^{(H)},

which proves the first part of (
5
).
To prove the second part, recall that
D(t)\neq 0
.
Since
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
, we can pick
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
t^{m}\in B^{(H)}
.
Since
D(t^{m})=mt^{m-1}D(t)\neq 0
, we have
t^{m}\notin A
, so
t^{m}\notin A^{(H)}
and hence
A^{(H)}\neq B^{(H)}
.
So (
5
) is true and assertion (a) is proved.

(b) Suppose that
A_{1},A_{2}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm KHLND}}(B)
are such that
A_{1}^{(H)}=A_{2}^{(H)}
.
Consider a nonzero homogeneous element
a\in A_{1}
. Since
H\in\mathbb{T}(B)
, we can pick
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
a^{m}\in A_{1}^{(H)}
.
Then
a^{m}\in A_{2}^{(H)}\subseteq A_{2}
, so
a\in A_{2}
since
A_{2}
is factorially closed in
B
.
This shows that
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}
, and
A_{1}=A_{2}
follows by symmetry. This proves (b).

Part (c) is an obvious consequence of part (a).

(d) Since
G
is torsion-free and
B
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine and non-rigid,
Lemma
2.25
implies that
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\neq\{0\}
, so
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B^{(H)})\neq\{0\}
by (c), so
B^{(H)}
is non-rigid.
∎

4.
The group
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)

This section is devoted to defining the subgroup
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)
of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
and proving some of its properties.
We will see that
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)
is related to locally nilpotent derivations in several ways;
this idea is first encountered in Thm
4.3
, and is further developed in subsequent sections.

Recall that we defined
\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)=\big\{\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}B\,\mid\,\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}{\mathfrak{p}}=1\,\big\}
for any ring
B
.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
.
Given
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}B
,
the set
\mathbb{M}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,B_{i}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{p}}\,\big\}
is a submonoid of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
.
The subgroup of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
generated by
\mathbb{M}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
is denoted
\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
.
We define the subgroup
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)
of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
by

\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\begin{cases}\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)}\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})&\text{if $\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)\neq\varnothing$,}\\
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)&\text{if $\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)=\varnothing$.}\end{cases}

We say that
B
is
saturated in codimension
1
if
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
.

4.2 Lemma
.

Let
B=\bigoplus_{i\in G}B_{i}
be a domain graded by an abelian group
G
.
If
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a homogeneous prime ideal of
B
then
\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})
is the subgroup of
G
generated by

\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,(B/{\mathfrak{p}})_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}=\big\{\,i\in G\,\mid\,B_{i}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{p}}\,\big\}=\mathbb{M}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
,
so
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

4.3 Theorem
.

Let
B
be a domain of characteristic
0
graded by an abelian group
G
.

(a)

For each
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\setminus\{0\}
,
there exists a height
1
homogeneous prime ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
B
satisfying:

(i)

some homogeneous element

satisfies
D(t)\neq 0
and
D^{2}(t)=0
;

(ii)

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\ker D)
.

(b)

\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\ker D)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
for all
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)
.

(c)

If
B
is saturated in codimension

then
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\ker D)=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
for all
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)

Proof.

(a) Let
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\setminus\{0\}
and let
A=\ker D
.
Choose a homogeneous
t\in B
such that
Dt\in A\setminus\{0\}
.
Let
S
be the set of homogeneous elements of

.
Note that
S^{-1}B
and
S^{-1}A
are
G
-graded

-domains,

Since

is a unit of

S^{-1}B=(S^{-1}A)[t]=(S^{-1}A)^{[1]}
by Lemma

The condition

By basic properties of localization, it follows that

is a height

It is clear that

is homogeneous, so

is homogeneous.
We have

(b) Let

. It is clear that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\ker D)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
, so it’s enough to prove
that
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\ker D)

If

of

We have

Since

(c) The assumption means that

4.6 Proposition

Let
G
be an abelian group and let

be a

-algebra.

(a)

Let

-algebra

Then
{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
is the submonoid of

and
\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
is the subgroup of

.

(b)

The set
\big\{\,\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}(B)\,\big\}

(c)

The set
\big\{\,\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,\text{${\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}(B)$ and ${\mathfrak{p}}$ is homogeneous}\,\big\}
is finite.

(d)

Let

and let

be the set of all

satisfying

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)&\text{if $P=\varnothing$.}\end{cases}

It is straightforward to see that if
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}(B)
then
{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
is the submonoid of

generated by

(we leave this to the reader).
This implies that
\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})

4.9 Lemma

If

Consider the map

then

Proof.

7.6 Remark
.

Let

7.7 Lemma
.

Let

\mathbb{Z}
-graded domain.

(a)

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)=e(B)\mathbb{Z}

\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\bar{e}(B)\mathbb{Z}

(b)

Proof.

\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)=e(B)\mathbb{Z}

Let
Z
denote the set of all height

homogeneous prime ideals of

If

If

\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z}\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z}\bigg(e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\mathbb{Z}\bigg)=\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}\big\{\,e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z\,\big\}\mathbb{Z}=\bar{e}(B)\mathbb{Z}\,.

Indeed, the first equality is Prop.

the second one follows from the fact that

the third one is the definition of “lcm” and the last one is the definition of

This proves (a). Assertion (b) follows from (a) and Def.

.

7.11 Notation

7.12 Lemma
.

The operation
\odiv
has the following properties.

(a)

If

and

.

(b)

If

and

•

m\odiv n=1\Leftrightarrow m\mid n
,

•

(m\odiv n)\mid m\mid\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(m\odiv n,n)
.

(c)

If
B
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded domain then
B^{(m\odiv e(B))}=B^{(m)}
for all

Proof.

Assertions (a) and (b) are easy. We prove (c).
We have
(m\odiv e(B))\mid m

.
To prove the reverse inclusion, consider

.
Then

,
so

is a multiple of both

and

, i.e.,
\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(m\odiv e(B),e(B))\mid d
.
Since
m\mid\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(m\odiv e(B),e(B))
by (b), we get

, so

.

\bar{e}(B)=0
.
Since
\bar{e}(B)\neq e(B)
, we have
Z\neq\varnothing
;
since
B
is noetherian,
\big\{\,e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z\,\big\}
is a finite set by part (3) of Rem.
7.6
;
since
\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}\big\{\,e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z\,\big\}=\bar{e}(B)=0
,
there exists
{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z
such that
e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=0
.
Thus,

(14)

B_{\ell}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.

Define
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}={\mathfrak{p}}\cap B_{0}
.
To prove that
B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded, assume the contrary.
Since
B_{j}\neq 0
for some
j>0
, this means that there exist
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
i<0<j
,
B_{i}\neq 0
and
B_{j}\neq 0
.
Choose
x_{i}\in B_{i}\setminus\{0\}
and
x_{j}\in B_{j}\setminus\{0\}
;
since
x_{i},x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have
x_{i}^{j}x_{j}^{|i|}\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
, so
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}\neq 0
.
Cor.
2.13
with
H=0
implies that
B_{0}
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine.
Since
x_{j}
is transcendental over
B_{0}
, we have
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{B_{0}}(B)>0
;
since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}\neq 0
and
B_{0}
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine, we have
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B_{0}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{0})<\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B_{0})\leq\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)-1
.
On the other hand, we have
B_{0}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}\cong B/{\mathfrak{p}}
by (
14
),
and (since
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}{\mathfrak{p}}=1
and
B
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine)
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)-1
,
so
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B_{0}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{0})=\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\rm\bf k}(B)-1
, a contradiction.

So
B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded and consequently
B_{+}=\bigoplus_{i>0}B_{i}
is a prime ideal of
B
.
Since
B_{j}\neq 0
for some
j>0
, we have
B_{+}\neq 0
, so
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(B_{+})\geq 1
.
We have
B_{+}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}
by (
14
), so
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(B_{+})=1
, which proves (a).

(b) By (a),
B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded and
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(B_{+})=1
.
By the proof of (a),
{\rm\bf k}\subseteq B_{0}
and both
B
and
B_{0}
are
{\rm\bf k}
-affine.
Since
B/B_{+}\cong B_{0}
and
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(B_{+})=1
, we have
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{B_{0}}(B)=1
.

We first prove the case where
B_{0}
is a field.
Let
S=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(B_{i}\setminus\{0\})
and
\mathcal{B}=S^{-1}B
.
Then
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{B}_{i}
where
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is a field that contains
B_{0}
.
By Lemma
2.8
,
there exists a nonzero homogeneous element
t\in\mathcal{B}
of degree
e(B)
such that

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}[t^{\pm 1}]
is the ring of Laurent polynomials over
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.
In particular,
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}(\mathcal{B})=1
.
We also have
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{B_{0}}(\mathcal{B})=\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}_{B_{0}}(B)=1
, so
\mathcal{B}_{0}/B_{0}
is an algebraic extension of fields.
Since
B
is normal, it follows that
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subseteq B
and hence that
\mathcal{B}_{0}=B_{0}
.
So
B\subseteq\mathcal{B}=B_{0}[t^{\pm 1}]
. Since
B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded, it follows that
B\subseteq B_{0}[t]
.
Since
B_{0}
is a field, it is clear that some monic polynomial
f(t)\in B_{0}[t]
belongs to
B
; so
t
is integral over
B
; since
B
is normal and
t\in S^{-1}B
,
we get
t\in B
and hence
B=B_{0}[t]=B_{0}^{[1]}
, as desired.

Now consider the general case.
This time, let
S=B_{0}\setminus\{0\}
and
\mathcal{B}=S^{-1}B=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{B}_{i}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{0}=\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}B_{0}
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{B}_{+}\cap B=B_{+}
; by basic properties of localization, it follows that
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(\mathcal{B}_{+})=\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(B_{+})=1
.
Since
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is a field and
\mathcal{B}
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded normal affine
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-domain such that
\operatorname{\text{\rm ht}}(\mathcal{B}_{+})=1
,
the preceding paragraph implies that
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{0}[t]=\mathcal{B}_{0}^{[1]}
where
t
is homogeneous of degree
e(\mathcal{B})=e(B)>0
.
Multiplying
t
by an element of
S
, we may arrange that
t\in B
.
Since
B
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine, we have
B={\rm\bf k}[f_{1}(t),\dots,f_{n}(t)]
where
f_{i}(t)\in\mathcal{B}_{0}[t]
for all
i
.
Clearly, there exists
s\in S
such that all coefficients of
f_{1}(t),\dots,f_{n}(t)
belong to
(B_{0})_{s}
.
Thus,
B_{s}=(B_{0})_{s}[t]=\big((B_{0})_{s}\big)^{[1]}
, which proves (b).
∎

7.20 Corollary
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0
and
B=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded normal affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain.
If
e(B)\neq 0=\bar{e}(B)
then there exists
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\setminus\{0\}
such that
\ker(D)=B_{0}
. In particular,
B
is non-rigid.

Proof.

First consider the case where
B_{j}\neq 0
for some
j>0
.
By Lemma
7.19
(b), there exist
s\in B_{0}\setminus\{0\}
and a homogeneous element
t\in B\setminus\{0\}
of
positive degree such that
B_{s}=(B_{0})_{s}[t]=\big((B_{0})_{s}\big)^{[1]}
.
For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
s^{m}\frac{d}{dt}:(B_{0})_{s}[t]\to(B_{0})_{s}[t]
belongs to
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B_{s})\setminus\{0\}
.
Since
B
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine, we can choose
m
such that
s^{m}\frac{d}{dt}
maps
B
into itself;
then
D=(s^{m}\frac{d}{dt})|_{B}:B\to B
belongs to
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\setminus\{0\}
and
\ker(D)=B_{0}
.
This proves the result in this case.

If no
j>0
is such that
B_{j}\neq 0
, then
let
B^{-}
be the ring
B
with the opposite grading, i.e.,
B^{-}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}^{-}
where
B_{i}^{-}=B_{-i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We have
e(B^{-})\neq 0=\bar{e}(B^{-})
, and there exists
j>0
such that
B_{j}^{-}\neq 0
.
By the preceding paragraph, there exists
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B^{-})\setminus\{0\}
such that
\ker(D)=B_{0}^{-}
.
Clearly, the same
D
satisfies
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\setminus\{0\}
and
\ker(D)=B_{0}
.
∎

The set
M(B)

By Cor.
7.16
(a), the problem of describing
\operatorname{\text{\rm NR}}(B)
reduces to that of describing
M(B)
.
So it is interesting to ask what can be said about
M(B)
.
Let us agree that a
primitive set
is a subset
Y
of
\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}

such that the conditions
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
and
y\mid y^{\prime}
imply
y=y^{\prime}
.
It is obvious that
M(B)
is primitive, and Prop.
7.21
shows that it is finite.

7.21 Proposition
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0

and
B
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded normal affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain.

(a)

M(B)
is a finite primitive subset of the set of divisors of
\bar{e}(B)
.

(b)

If
e(B)=0
then
M(B)
is either
\varnothing
or
\{1\}
.

(c)

If
e(B)\neq 0
and
\bar{e}(B)=0
then
M(B)=\{1\}
.

Proof.

(b) If
e(B)=0
then
B^{(d)}=B
for all positive integers
d
, so
\operatorname{\text{\rm NR}}(B)
is either
\varnothing
or
\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}

and consequently (b) is true.

(c) If
e(B)\neq 0
and
\bar{e}(B)=0
then
Cor.
7.20
implies that
B
is non-rigid, so
1\in\operatorname{\text{\rm NR}}(B)
and hence
M(B)=\{1\}
.

(a) It is obvious that
M(B)
is a primitive set.
If
\bar{e}(B)\neq 0
then Cor.
7.16
(b) implies that
M(B)
is included in the set of divisors of
\bar{e}(B)
,
so (a) is true in this case.
If
\bar{e}(B)=0
then (b) and (c) imply that
M(B)
is either
\varnothing
or
\{1\}
, so (a) is true in this case as well.
∎

7.22 Remark
.

Let us say that a subset
Y
of
\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
is
realizable

if there exists a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded normal affine
\mathbb{C}
-domain
B
satisfying
M(B)=Y
.
Prop.
7.21
implies that every realizable set is finite and primitive,
but
we don’t know if every finite primitive set is realizable.

For instance, we don’t know whether
\{6,10\}
is realizable.
We will learn more about realizable sets in Section
8
, by studying Pham-Brieskorn rings.

8.
Pham-Brieskorn rings

Recall from Cor.
7.16
(a) that the problem of describing
\operatorname{\text{\rm NR}}(B)
reduces to that of describing
M(B)
.
The main result of this section (Thm
8.7
) describes the set
M(B)
for Pham-Brieskorn rings
B
that satisfy a certain condition.

8.1 Notation
.

Given
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
(where
n\geq 3
), we define:

(1)

L=\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})

(2)

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,

J_{i}=\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}
and
L_{i}=\displaystyle\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}\big\{\,a_{j}\,\mid\,j\in J_{i}\,\big\}
.

(3)

S({\mathbf{a}})=S(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\big\{\,j\,\mid\,1\leq j\leq n\text{ and }L_{j}\neq L\,\big\}

(4)

The
cotype
of
{\mathbf{a}}
is the cardinality of
S({\mathbf{a}})
. Note that

\operatorname{\text{\rm cotype}}({\mathbf{a}})=\operatorname{\text{\rm cotype}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}.

(5)

Let
D
be the set of positive divisors of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L}{L_{i}}
and define a map
f:D\to(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{n}

by declaring that if
d\in D
then
f(d)=(a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n}^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
where:

(15)

a_{i}^{\prime}=\gcd(a_{i},L_{i})\,{\textstyle\left[\frac{L/L_{i}}{\gcd(d,L/L_{i})}\right]},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

It follows from (
15
) that
a_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}\gcd(d,L/L_{i})
(and hence
a_{i}^{\prime}\mid a_{i}
) for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
Consequently,
f(1)=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
.

8.2 Notation
.

If
{\rm\bf k}
is a field of characteristic
0
,
n\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, define

B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}={\rm\bf k}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]/(X_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+X_{n}^{a_{n}})
.
This is called a
Pham-Brieskorn ring
, and it is well known that

B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is a normal domain.

Consider the
\mathbb{N}
-grading of
{\rm\bf k}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that (for each
i=1,\dots,n
)

X_{i}
is homogeneous of degree
d_{i}=L/a_{i}
, where
L=\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
;
then
X_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+X_{n}^{a_{n}}
is homogeneous and consequently
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded.
We have
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}={\rm\bf k}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, where (for each
i
)
x_{i}\in B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
denotes the canonical image of
X_{i}
.
Clearly,
x_{i}\neq 0
is homogeneous of degree
d_{i}
, and if
n\geq 4
then
x_{i}
is a prime element of
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
.

When considering a Pham-Brieskorn ring
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
,
we shall use the notations
L
,
J_{i}
,
L_{i}
,
x_{i}
,
d_{i}
and
f:D\to(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{n}

(defined in
8.1
and
8.2
) without necessarily recalling their definitions.
Note that all those notations are uniquely determined by
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
.

8.3
.

For each
n\geq 3
, let
\Gamma_{n}
be the set of

(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
such that

\min(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})>1
and at most one
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
satisfies
a_{i}=2
.

It is well known and easy to see that

(16)

for every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{n}\setminus\Gamma_{n}
, the ring
B_{\mathbb{C};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is non-rigid.

It is conjectured that the following statement
\mathscr{P}(n)
is true for all
n\geq 3
:

\mathscr{P}(n)
:

For every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\Gamma_{n}
, the ring
B_{\mathbb{C};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is rigid.

It is easy to see that if
B_{\mathbb{C};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is rigid then so is
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
for any field
{\rm\bf k}
of characteristic
0
.
So
\mathscr{P}(n)
can also be written as follows:

\mathscr{P}(n)
:

For every field
{\rm\bf k}
of characteristic
0
and every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\Gamma_{n}
, the ring
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is rigid.

The current status of this conjecture is as follows.

•

\mathscr{P}(3)
is true, by Lemma 4 of
[
KZ00
]
.

•

\mathscr{P}(4)
is true, by
[
Chi23
]
and
[
CD23
]
.
(The PhD thesis
[
Chi23
]
shows, among other things, that if

B_{\mathbb{C};2,3,4,12}
and

are rigid then
\mathscr{P}(4)
is true.
These two rings are shown to be rigid in the preprint
[
CD23
]
.)

•

For
n>4
, only special cases of

are known to be true. (See for instance Lemma
8.4
.)

8.4 Lemma

(Cor. 4.16 of
[
CD20
]
)
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0
,
n\geq 3
and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\Gamma_{n}
.
If
\operatorname{\text{\rm cotype}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\geq n-2
, then
B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is rigid.

8.5 Notation
.

Let
B=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}
and
B^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}B_{i}^{\prime}
be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded rings.

(1)

We write
B\cong B^{\prime}
to indicate that there exists an isomorphism of rings
\varphi:B\to B^{\prime}

satisfying
\varphi(B_{i})=B_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

We write
B\overset{\text{\tiny$*$}}{\cong}B^{\prime}
to indicate that there exists an isomorphism of rings
\varphi:B\to B^{\prime}

such that, for every
x\in B
,
x
is homogeneous in
B
if and only if
\varphi(x)
is homogeneous in
B^{\prime}

(but the degree of
x
in
B
is not necessarily equal to that of
\varphi(x)
in
B^{\prime}
).
Note that if
B\overset{\text{\tiny$*$}}{\cong}B^{\prime}
then
e(B)
is not necessarily equal to
e(B^{\prime})
.
Also note that

if
B\overset{\text{\tiny$*$}}{\cong}B^{\prime}
then
B
and
B^{\prime}
have the same rigidity status,

because whether or not
B
(or
B^{\prime}
) is rigid is independent of the grading.

The following result computes
e(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})
and
\bar{e}(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})
,
and shows that if
d
is a positive divisor of
\bar{e}(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})

then
(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})^{(d)}
is again a Pham-Brieskorn ring.
More precisely, we have
(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})^{(d)}\overset{\text{\tiny$*$}}{\cong}B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n}^{\prime}}

where
a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n}^{\prime}
are defined in the statement of part (d) of the Lemma.

8.6 Lemma
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0
,
n\geq 3
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

(a)

The integers
\frac{L}{L_{1}},\dots,\frac{L}{L_{n}}
are pairwise relatively prime.

(b)

e(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})=1
and
\bar{e}(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L}{L_{i}}
.

(c)

B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
is saturated in codimension
1
if and only if
\operatorname{\text{\rm cotype}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0
.

(d)

Let
d
be a positive divisor of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L}{L_{i}}
and consider
f(d)=(a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n}^{\prime})
as in part
(5)
of Notation
8.1
.
Then
(B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}})^{(d)}\overset{\text{\tiny$*$}}{\cong}B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n}^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Let
B=B_{{\rm\bf k};a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}
.
For each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
x_{i}\neq 0
is homogeneous of degree
d_{i}
;
so
e(B)=\gcd(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=\gcd(\frac{L}{a_{1}},\dots,\frac{L}{a_{n}})=\frac{L}{\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}=1
.
Define

\hat{d}_{i}=\gcd\big\{\,d_{j}\,\mid\,j\in J_{i}\,\big\}\quad\text{for each $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$.}

Then
\hat{d}_{1}=\gcd(d_{2},\dots,d_{n})\linebreak=\gcd(\frac{L}{a_{2}},\dots,\frac{L}{a_{n}})=\frac{L}{\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}(a_{2},\dots,a_{n})}=\frac{L}{L_{1}}
,
and by the same argument we get

\textstyle\hat{d}_{i}=\frac{L}{L_{i}}\quad\text{for all $i\in\{1,\dots n\}$.}

If
i\neq j
then
\gcd(L/L_{i},L/L_{j})=\gcd(\hat{d}_{i},\hat{d}_{j})=\gcd(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=1
, which proves (a).

(b) We already proved that
e(B)=1
.
If
Z
denotes the set of height
1
homogeneous prime ideals of
B
then:

•

for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, some
{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z
satisfies
x_{i}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
;

•

for each
{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z
, at most one
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
satisfies
x_{i}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
,
and if
x_{i}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
then
e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=\gcd\big\{\,d_{j}\,\mid\,j\in J_{i}\,\big\}=\hat{d}_{i}=L/L_{i}
.

It follows that
\big\{\,e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in Z\,\big\}=\{1,\frac{L}{L_{1}},\dots,\frac{L}{L_{n}}\}
,
so
\bar{e}(B)=\operatorname{\text{\rm lcm}}\big({\textstyle\frac{L}{L_{1}},\dots,\frac{L}{L_{n}}}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L}{L_{i}}
, the last equality by (a).
This proves (b), and (c) follows from (b).

(d) Let
d
be a divisor of
\bar{e}(B)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L}{L_{i}}
.
Define
\alpha_{i}=\gcd(d,\hat{d}_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Let us prove that

(17)

\text{for every $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$,}\qquad\gcd(d_{i},\alpha_{i})=1,\quad d\mid d_{i}\alpha_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{i}=a_{i}^{\prime}\alpha_{i}.

Indeed, let
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
Since
\alpha_{i}\mid\hat{d}_{i}
and
\gcd(d_{i},\hat{d}_{i})=\gcd(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})=1
, the first part of (
17
) is clear.
To prove the second part, we first show that

(18)

for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\frac{L}{L_{j}}
divides
d_{i}\hat{d}_{i}
.

To see this, we note that

Before proving (b), we show that

(26)

If
x,y\in B\setminus\{0\}
are homogeneous and such that
x/y\in B_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
then there exist homogeneous
elements
\alpha,\beta\in B\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
x/y=\alpha/\beta
.

To see this, we first choose
u\in B
and
v\in B\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
x/y=u/v
.
Write
u=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}u_{i}
and
v=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}v_{i}
where
u_{i},v_{i}\in B_{i}
for all
i
,
and choose
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v_{j}\in B_{j}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Then the equality
x\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}v_{i}=y\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}u_{i}
implies that
xv_{j}=yu_{i}
for some
i
.
So
x/y=u_{i}/v_{j}
. If
u_{i}\in{\mathfrak{p}}
then
u_{i}/v_{j}
belongs to
{\mathfrak{p}}B_{\mathfrak{p}}
, contradicting
x/y\in B_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
.
So
u_{i}\in B_{i}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
, which proves (
26
).

(b) Let
g
be a homogeneous element of
{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
{\mathfrak{p}}B_{\mathfrak{p}}=gB_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Let
\delta=\gcd(\deg(g),e(B/{\mathfrak{p}}))
.
Pick any
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
B_{i}\neq 0
and pick
h\in B_{i}\setminus\{0\}
.
For some
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
h/g^{\nu}\in B_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
;
so (
26
) implies that
h/g^{\nu}=\alpha/\beta
for some homogeneous
\alpha,\beta\in B\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Then
\delta\mid e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\deg(\gamma)
for each
\gamma\in\{\alpha,\beta\}
.
We have
\deg(h)-\nu\deg(g)=\deg(\alpha)-\deg(\beta)
, so
\delta
divides
\deg(h)=i
.
This being true for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
B_{i}\neq 0
, we conclude that
\delta\mid e(B)
.
Clearly,
e(B)\mid\delta
; so (b) is true.

(c) Let
g
be as in (a) and (b).
Let
\varepsilon=e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})
and
d=e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})/e(B)=\varepsilon/e(B)
.
Since
e(B)\mid\deg(g)
,

\deg(g^{d})
belongs to
\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
, which is the group generated by
\big\{\,i\in\mathbb{N}\,\mid\,(B/{\mathfrak{p}})_{i}\neq 0\,\big\}
;
so there exist
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(B/{\mathfrak{p}})_{i}\neq 0
,
(B/{\mathfrak{p}})_{j}\neq 0
, and
\deg(g^{d})=i-j
;
consequently, there exist homogeneous elements
u,v\in B\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
\deg(g^{d})=\deg(v)-\deg(u)
.
Then
\xi=g^{d}u/v
belongs to the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
of
B_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
.
Let us prove that
{\mathfrak{m}}=\xi B_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
.

Consider an arbitrary nonzero element
\frac{x}{s}
of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
x,s\in B\setminus\{0\}
are homogeneous of the same degree and
s\notin{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Define
k=v^{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\big(\frac{x}{s}\big)
.
Then
k>0
, because
\frac{x}{s}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}B_{\mathfrak{p}}
; it follows that
\frac{x}{sg^{k}}\in B_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
,
so (
26
) implies that
\frac{x}{sg^{k}}=\frac{b_{m}}{c_{\ell}}
for some

b_{m}\in B_{m}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
and
c_{\ell}\in B_{\ell}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since
c_{\ell},b_{m}\in B\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have
\ell,m\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
\frac{x}{s}=g^{k}\frac{b_{m}}{c_{\ell}}
, we have
k\deg(g)+m-\ell=\deg(x)-\deg(s)=0
;
it follows that
\varepsilon\mid k\deg(g)
, or equivalently,
d\mid k\frac{\deg(g)}{e(B)}
.
Part (b) shows that
d=\frac{\varepsilon}{e(B)}
is relatively prime to
\frac{\deg(g)}{e(B)}
, so
d\mid k
.
Write
k=k_{0}d
(where
k_{0}>0
), then

\frac{x}{s}=g^{k_{0}d}\,\frac{b_{m}}{c_{\ell}}=\left(g^{d}\frac{u}{v}\right)^{k_{0}}\frac{v^{k_{0}}b_{m}}{u^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}}=\xi^{k_{0}}\frac{v^{k_{0}}b_{m}}{u^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}}

where
\frac{v^{k_{0}}b_{m}}{u^{k_{0}}c_{\ell}}\in B_{({\mathfrak{p}})}^{*}
.
Since
k_{0}>0
, we have
\frac{x}{s}\in\xi B_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
, which shows that
{\mathfrak{m}}=\xi B_{({\mathfrak{p}})}
.
Consequently,
e_{\mathfrak{p}}=v^{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\xi)=v^{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}(g^{d}u/v)=d
, which proves (c).
∎

9.4 Corollary
.

Let the assumptions and notations be as in paragraph
9.2
.
Then
B
is saturated in codimension
1
if and only if
e_{\mathfrak{p}}=1
for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in Y^{(1)}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.7

if and only if
e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=e(B)
for all

.
So the claim follows from part (c) of Lemma

.

Proof of Thm

{\mathfrak{p}}=xB\in Y^{(1)}

9.3

(28)

\textstyle\gcd(d,e(B/xB))=e(B)\quad\text{and}\quad e_{\mathfrak{p}}=\frac{e(B/xB)}{e(B)}.

Consider
C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}\subset Y
and
C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{X}\subset X
as in

C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}=\mathbb{V}_{+}(x)\subset Y

Let

and note that

is a subring of
K(Y)

We claim:

(29)

if

then
\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{U}(f)\geq 0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}(f)\leq 0

Indeed, for each
{\mathfrak{q}}\in Y^{(1)}\setminus\{{\mathfrak{p}}\}

R=B_{(x)}\subseteq B_{({\mathfrak{q}})}
and hence
v_{\mathfrak{q}}^{Y}(f)\geq 0

\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{U}(f)\geq 0
.

for some

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}(f)>0

where

and

are homogeneous and
\deg(xb)=\deg(s)
;
then

, so

; since
x
is prime and
x\nmid s
in

, we obtain

.
So (

) is true.
Consequently, the map

\deg_{R}:R\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{-\infty\},\quad\text{$\deg_{R}(f)=-v_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}(f)$ for all $f\in R$}

is a well-defined degree function.
Consider the associated graded ring
\operatorname{\text{\rm Gr}}(R)
determined by
(R,\deg_{R})

recall that
\operatorname{\text{\rm Gr}}(R)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}R_{\leq n}/R_{<n}

Since
v_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}:K(Y)\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
is surjective and
\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}R=K(Y)

We claim (see Notation

(30)

\operatorname{\text{\rm Gr}}(R)\cong\big(B/xB\big)^{\langle e_{\mathfrak{p}}d\rangle}.

Let us prove (

Given

and note that
S(f)
is a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{N}
.
Clearly, if

then

.
We claim that

(31)

\text{for each $f\in R\setminus\{0\}$,}\quad S(f)=\big\{\,n\in\mathbb{N}\,\mid\,n\geq e_{\mathfrak{p}}\deg_{R}(f)\,\big\}.

Indeed, let
f\in R\setminus\{0\}

n\in S(f)\iff fx^{n}\in B\iff\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{X}(fx^{n})\geq 0
.
Consider the
\mathbb{Q}

D\mapsto D^{*}
from
\operatorname{\text{\rm Div}}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Y)

\textstyle\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{X}(fx^{n})=\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{X}(f)+n\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{X}(x)=\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{X}(f)+nC_{\mathfrak{p}}^{X}=(\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{Y}(f)+\frac{n}{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y})^{*},

and since

we have

\textstyle n\in S(f)\iff\operatorname{{\rm div}}_{Y}(f)+\frac{n}{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}C_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}\geq 0\iff v_{\mathfrak{p}}^{Y}(f)\geq-\frac{n}{e_{\mathfrak{p}}}\iff\deg_{R}(f)\leq\frac{n}{e_{\mathfrak{p}}},

where the middle equivalence follows from

(see (

This proves (

Now (

\displaystyle f\in R_{\leq n}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\deg_{R}(f)\leq n\Leftrightarrow e_{\mathfrak{p}}n\in S(f)\Leftrightarrow fx^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}n}\in B;

\displaystyle f\in R_{<n}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\deg_{R}(f)<n\Leftrightarrow e_{\mathfrak{p}}n>\min S(f)\Leftrightarrow e_{\mathfrak{p}}n-1\in S(f)\Leftrightarrow fx^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}n-1}\in B

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow fx^{e_{\mathfrak{p}}n}\in xB.

11.
A result about
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)

The aim of this section is to prove Thm

.

Given a subgroup
H
of an abelian group
G
, we define the
torsion-closure of
H
in
G
to be

\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}(H,G)=\big\{\,x\in G\,\mid\,\text{there exists $m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}$ such that $mx\in H$}\,\big\}
.

11.1 Lemma
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group
G
such that
G/H
is not torsion.
Let
\mathcal{H}(H,G)
be the set of all subgroups
H^{\prime}
of
G
such that
H\subseteq H^{\prime}\subset G
and
G/H^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
\mathcal{H}(H,G)\neq\varnothing
and the intersection of all elements of
\mathcal{H}(H,G)
is
\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}(H,G)
.

Proof.

Viewing
G
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module, let
S=\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and consider the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
G_{\mathbb{Q}}=S^{-1}G

and the canonical homomorphism
\lambda:G\to G_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
x\mapsto x/1
.
For each subgroup
K
of
G
, let
K_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the subspace
S^{-1}K
of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
and note that
\lambda^{-1}(K_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}(K,G)
.

Since
G/H
is not torsion, there exists
y\in G\setminus\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}(H,G)
.
Consider any such
y
. Consider the vector
\lambda(y)\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
and note that
\lambda(y)\notin H_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Choose a vector subspace
V_{y}
of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
H_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq V_{y}\subset G_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}/V_{y})=1
, and
\lambda(y)\notin V_{y}
.
Let
H_{y}=\lambda^{-1}(V_{y})
. Then
y\notin H_{y}
and
H\subseteq H_{y}\subset G
; since
G/H_{y}
is torsion-free (and hence free, since
G
is finitely generated),
and since
S^{-1}(G/H_{y})\cong G_{\mathbb{Q}}/V_{y}\cong\mathbb{Q}
, we have

; so

.
This shows that
\mathcal{H}(H,G)\neq\varnothing
and

, where

denotes the intersection of all elements of
\mathcal{H}(H,G)
.
This being true for each

, we obtain

, and the reverse inclusion is clear.
∎

and let
\pi:\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\to\Omega
be the canonical homomorphism of the quotient group.
Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the ring
B
endowed with the
\Omega
-grading defined by

\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{\omega\in\Omega}\mathcal{B}_{\omega}
, where
\mathcal{B}_{\omega}=\bigoplus_{\pi(i)=\omega}B_{i}
for each
\omega\in\Omega
.
We refer to this as “the natural
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/H
-grading”.
It satisfies
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B})=\Omega
. Observe that
\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(\mathcal{B})=\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
and let us show that

(40)

\pi\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\big)=\overline{\mathbb{M}}(\mathcal{B},{\mathfrak{p}})\quad\text{for every ${\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)$.}

Indeed, let
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
. If
i\in{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
then
B_{i}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{p}}
,
so
\mathcal{B}_{\pi(i)}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{p}}
(because
B_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\pi(i)}
) and hence
\pi(i)\in\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{B},{\mathfrak{p}})
.
Thus,
\pi\big({\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\big)\subseteq\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{B},{\mathfrak{p}})
.
Conversely, consider
\omega\in\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{B},{\mathfrak{p}})
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{\omega}=\bigoplus_{\pi(i)=\omega}B_{i}
is not included in
{\mathfrak{p}}
,
so there exists
i\in\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
such that
\pi(i)=\omega
and
B_{i}\nsubseteq{\mathfrak{p}}
; then
i\in{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
and hence

\omega\in\pi\big({\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\big)
.
This shows that
\pi\big({\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\big)=\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{B},{\mathfrak{p}})
, and it follows that

\pi\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\big)=\overline{\mathbb{M}}(\mathcal{B},{\mathfrak{p}})
.
So (
40
) is proved.
(
Caution:
Assertion (
40
) implies that
\pi\big(\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)\subseteq\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B})
,
but equality does not necessarily hold.)

The
rank
of an abelian group
G
is the dimension of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G
.
See Rem.
3.4
for the definition of
\operatorname{\text{\rm ML}}(B)
.

11.4 Theorem
.

Let
{\rm\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0
and
B
a normal affine
{\rm\bf k}
-domain graded over
{\rm\bf k}
by an abelian group
G
.
Assume that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)
is not torsion and define
r=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}\big(\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)
and
W=\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}(\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))
.

(a)

There exists
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
is not torsion.
Consider such a
{\mathfrak{p}}
and define

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{H}\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)
and
T_{\mathfrak{p}}=\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B))
.
Then the following hold.

(i)

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\varnothing

(ii)

For each
H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, there exists
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)
such that
\ker(D)=B^{(H)}
.

(iii)

There exists a subset
\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)
such that
\bigcap_{D\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}}}\ker(D)=B^{(T_{\mathfrak{p}})}
.

(b)

There exists a subset
\Delta
of
\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)
such that
\bigcap_{D\in\Delta}\ker(D)=B^{(W)}
.

(c)

There exists a field
K
such that

\operatorname{\text{\rm ML}}(B)\subseteq B^{(W)}\subseteq K\subseteq\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(B)
and
\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(B)=K^{(r)}
.

In particular,
\operatorname{\text{\rm trdeg}}(B:\operatorname{\text{\rm ML}}(B))\geq r
.

(d)

B
is non-rigid, and if
G
is torsion-free then
\mathscr{X}(B)=\mathbb{T}(B)
.

Proof.

Since
B
is
{\rm\bf k}
-affine and the grading is over
{\rm\bf k}
,
B
is finitely generated as a
B_{0}
-algebra and
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
is finitely generated.
Consequently,
r
is finite (so
r\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
).

(a) If
\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)=\varnothing
then
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
by definition, contradicting the assumption that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)
is not torsion.
So
\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)\neq\varnothing
.

By Prop.
4.6
, the nonempty set
\big\{\,\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\,\mid\,{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)\,\big\}
is finite.
So we can choose
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
such that
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
.
There must exist
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
is not torsion.
This proves the first claim in assertion (a).

Consider
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})
is not torsion and define

\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
as in the statement of (a).
Lemma
11.1
implies that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\varnothing
(which proves (a-i)) and

(41)

\textstyle\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}}H=T_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let
\pi:\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\to\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/H
be the canonical homomorphism.
The definition of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}
implies that
\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\subseteq H=\ker(\pi)
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/H\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\mathcal{B}(H)
denote the ring
B
endowed with the natural
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/H
-grading (see paragraph
11.3
)
and note that
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B}(H))=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/H\cong\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)=\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(H))
, we have
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(H))
; thus,

\textstyle\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B}(H))\ =\ \bigcap_{{\mathfrak{q}}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(\mathcal{B}(H))}\overline{\mathbb{M}}(\mathcal{B}(H),{\mathfrak{q}})\ \subseteq\ \overline{\mathbb{M}}(\mathcal{B}(H),{\mathfrak{p}})=\pi\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}})\big)=0

where the penultimate equality follows from (
40
).
Since
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B}(H))\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{B}(H))=0
,
Cor.
7.20
implies that there exists
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(\mathcal{B}(H))
such that
\ker(D)=\mathcal{B}(H)_{0}=B^{(H)}
.
Since
D\in\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)
, this proves (a-ii).
Since
H
is a proper subgroup of
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
, we have
B^{(H)}\neq B
and hence
D\neq 0
. So
B
is non-rigid (this is claimed in (d)).

For each
H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, choose
D_{H}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)
such that
\ker(D_{H})=B^{(H)}
(possible by (a-ii)).
Define
\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}}=\big\{\,D_{H}\,\mid\,H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,\big\}
.
Then
\bigcap_{D\in\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}}}\ker(D)=\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{p}}}B^{(H)}=B^{(T_{\mathfrak{p}})}
,
the last equality by (
41
).
This proves (a-iii) and completes the proof of (a).

(b) Recall that we chose
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Spec}}^{1}(B)
such that
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
,
and that we noted that
I\neq\varnothing
, where

I=\big\{\,i\,\mid\,\text{$1\leq i\leq n$ and $\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})$ is not torsion}\,\big\}.

Since
\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
,
Lemma
11.2
gives
W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)
.
We have

\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)=T_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
for each
i\in I

and
\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus I
, so

(42)

\textstyle W=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{\text{\rm torc}}\big(\overline{\mathbb{M}}(B,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}),\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)\big)=\bigcap_{i\in I}T_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}.

For each
i\in I
, consider a subset
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
of
\operatorname{\text{\rm LND}}(B)
such that
\bigcap_{D\in\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}}\ker(D)=B^{(T_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}})}

(
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
exists by (a-iii)).
Let
\Delta=\bigcup_{i\in I}\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}
. Then

\textstyle\bigcap_{D\in\Delta}\ker(D)=\bigcap_{i\in I}\bigcap_{D\in\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}}\ker(D)=\bigcap_{i\in I}B^{(T_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}})}=B^{(W)}

where the last equality follows from (
42
). This proves (b).

(c) Let
R
denote
B
endowed with the natural
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/W
-grading (see paragraph
11.3
).
Since
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)=\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/W
is finitely generated and torsion-free,
and
\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}(\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/W)=\operatorname{\text{\rm rank}}(\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(B)/\operatorname{\bar{}\mathbb{G}}(B))=r\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we have
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(R)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.
Lemma
2.8

implies that there exists a field
K
such that
R_{0}\subseteq K\subseteq\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(R)
and
\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(R)=K^{(r)}
.
Since
R_{0}=B^{(W)}\supseteq\operatorname{\text{\rm ML}}(B)
by (b), we have
\operatorname{\text{\rm ML}}(B)\subseteq K
. Since
\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(B)=\operatorname{\text{\rm Frac}}(R)
, (c) follows.

(d) We already proved that
B
is non-rigid.
If moreover
G
is torsion-free then Lemma
2.25
implies that
\operatorname{\text{\rm HLND}}(B)\neq\{0\}
,
so Thm
3.2
(c) gives
\mathscr{X}(B)=\mathbb{T}(B)
.
∎

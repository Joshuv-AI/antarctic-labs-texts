Backward behavior and determining functionals for chevron pattern equations
V. K. Kalantarov 1 , H. V. Kalantarova 2 , and O. Vantzos 3

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to the study of the backward behavior of solutions of the initial boundary value problem for the chevron pattern equations under homogeneous Dirichlet’s boundary conditions. We prove that, as t → ∞ t\rightarrow\infty , the asymptotic behavior of solutions of the considered problem is completely determined by the dynamics of a finite set of functionals. Furthermore, we provide numerical evidence for the blow-up of certain solutions of the backward problem in finite time in 1D.



1.
Introduction

We consider the system of equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\tau\partial_{t}A=A+\Delta A-\phi^{2}A-|A|^{2}A-2ic_{1}\phi\partial_{y}A+i\beta A\partial_{y}\phi,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi=-\mathbb{L}\phi-h\phi+\phi\lvert A\rvert^{2}-c_{2}\mbox{Im}\left[A^{\ast}\partial_{y}A\right],\quad(x,y)\in\Omega,\quad t>0,

where the linear operator
\mathbb{L}
is given by

\mathbb{L}:=-D_{1}\partial^{2}_{x}-D_{2}\partial^{2}_{y},

and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded domain with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega

(1.3)

A\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\phi\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad t>0,

and the initial conditions

(1.4)

A\Big|_{t=0}=A_{0},\quad\phi\Big|_{t=0}=\phi_{0},\quad(x,y)\in\Omega.

In the context of this study,

(1.5)

D_{1}>0,\ D_{2}>0,\ c_{1}\geq 0,\ c_{2}\geq 0,\ h\geq 0,\ \beta\in\mathbb{R}

are given parameters, the complex valued function
A

denotes its complex conjugate, and the real valued function

are unknown functions. This coupled system of equations was proposed by Rossberg et al.
[

]

to model the formation and evolution of patterns in nematic liquid crystals subject to convection driven by an electric field, sometimes accompanied with magnetic field. These works were motivated by a number of experimental studies

A typical experimental setup used in these investigations consists of a nematic liquid crystal confined between two planar transparent glass plates, placed parallel at a distance of approximately

The results of the described paradigmatic experiment, revealed that through use of either microscopes (with or without polarizers) or via laser diffraction various pattern formations are observable: Williams domain, Grid-Pattern, chevron patterns, etc.. The formation of these patterns is governed by experimentally easily controllable parameters, the amplitude and the frequency of the applied voltage and the magnitude of magnetic field. The advantage of liquid crystals over other fluids in these experiments besides its transparency and its phase transition temperatures being close to the room temperature, which makes detection of patterns easier,

3.
Backward behavior

In this section, we will prove the backward uniqueness of a solution to the problem (

1.4

The backward uniqueness of solution to the problem follows from the corresponding result for an abstract differential equation in a Hilbert space

(3.2)

\partial_{t}\Phi=-\mathbb{L}\Phi-h\Phi+\Phi|A|^{2}+\tilde{\phi}(Aa^{*}+\tilde{A}^{\ast}a)-c_{2}\mbox{Im}\left[a^{\ast}\partial_{y}A+\tilde{A}^{\ast}\partial_{y}a\right].

\displaystyle\phi_{t}+D_{1}\partial^{2}_{x}\phi-h\phi+|A|^{2}\phi=0,\quad x\in(0,L),\quad t>0,

(3.12)

\displaystyle A\big|_{x=0}=A\big|_{x=L}=\phi\big|_{x=0}=\phi\big|_{x=L}=0,

In fact suppose that
[A,\phi]
is a local solution of the problem corresponding to the initial data
[A_{0},\phi_{0}]

Multiplying the equation (

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|A(t)\|^{2}=-\|A(t)\|^{2}+\|\partial_{x}A(t)\|^{2}+(\phi^{2}(t),|A(t)|^{2})+\int_{0}^{L}|A(x,t)|^{4}dx.

Thanks to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have

\int_{0}^{L}|A(x,t)|^{2}dx\leq\sqrt{L}\left(\int_{0}^{L}|A(x,t)|^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus employing the last inequality and the Poincare inequality we obtain that the function
\Psi(t)=\|A(t)\|^{2}
satisfies the differential inequality

\Psi^{\prime}(t)\geq-2\Psi(t)+2\lambda_{1}\Psi(t)+2L^{-1}\Psi^{2}(t).

It is clear that if
\Psi_{0}:=\Psi(0)=\|A_{0}\|^{2}>(1-\lambda_{1})L

T_{0}\leq\int_{\Psi_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{2(L^{-1}s^{2}+(\lambda_{1}-1)s)},

i.e., the solution of the problem (
3.10

4.
Numerical Blow-up of the Backward System

To illustrate numerically the blow-up of
\lVert A\rVert
in the 1D backward system (
3.10
), we consider the case where
\phi=0

and note immediately that the resulting equation
\partial_{t}A=-\partial^{2}_{x}A+(\lvert A\rvert^{2}-1)A
can not be integrated numerically
in a straightforward manner because of the ’wrong’ sign of the Laplacian
\partial^{2}_{x}A
.
It is a basic result in numerical analysis that a direct numerical integration of this equation would be dominated by spurious high frequency modes.

To progress, we notice that the equation, in its weak form, can be thought of as the
maximizing
L^{2}
-gradient flow

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}A,u)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(A)(u),\quad u\in H^{1}_{0}([0,L]),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{x}A\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\lVert\lvert A\rvert^{2}-1\rVert^{2}\,.

Given that the main challenge with integrating this gradient flow numerically is the uncontrollable divergence of the gradient
\partial_{x}A
,
we consider instead the maximizing gradient flow of the functional
\mathcal{F}
in the
H^{1}_{\epsilon}
-norm

\lVert A\rVert_{H^{1}_{\epsilon}}^{2}:=\lVert A\rVert^{2}+\epsilon\lVert\partial_{x}A\rVert^{2},\quad\epsilon>0,

which can be written as:

(\partial_{t}A,u)+\epsilon(\partial_{x}\partial_{t}A,\partial_{x}u)=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(A)(u)\,.

This regularization controls the ‘rate of ascent’ of
A
in
H^{1}
instead of
L^{2}
,
and so penalizes the rapid divergence of the gradient that accompanies the introduction of spurious oscillatory modes.
In strong form, it yields the regularized PDE

(4.1)

\partial_{t}A-\epsilon\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{t}A=-\partial^{2}_{x}A+(\lvert A\rvert^{2}-1)A\,.

Proceeding with the numerical integration of (
4.1
), we assume that
A(t^{k})=A^{k}
and apply
operator splitting
to do the integration in two steps:
first integrate the non-linear part

(4.2)

\partial_{t}\hat{A}-(\lvert\hat{A}\rvert^{2}-1)\hat{A}=0,\quad\hat{A}(t^{k})=A^{k},

to calculate
\hat{A}^{k+1}=\hat{A}(t^{k+1})
,
t^{k+1}=t^{k}+\tau
, and then the linear part

(4.3)

(id-\epsilon\partial_{x}^{2})\partial_{t}A=-\partial_{x}^{2}A,\quad A(t^{k})=\hat{A}^{k+1},

to update
A
to the next time step.

The first step (
4.2
) can be integrated exactly:

\hat{A}(x,t)=\frac{A^{k}(x)}{\sqrt{\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert^{2}+(1-\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert^{2})e^{2(t-t^{k})}}}\,.

This blows up for
\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert>1
at the time

t=t^{k}+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert^{2}}{\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert^{2}-1}\right)\,.

It follows that

(4.4)

\hat{A}^{k+1}=\frac{A^{k}}{\sqrt{\lvert A^{k}\rvert^{2}+(1-\lvert A^{k}\rvert^{2})e^{2\tau}}}\,,

as long as the (global) time step
\tau
is chosen so that

\tau<\tau_{blow}=\inf_{x}\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\max(1,\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert^{2})}{\max(1,\lvert A^{k}(x)\rvert^{2})-1}\right)\,.

Having selected a suitable
\tau
, in particular ensuring that
\tau<\epsilon
, the second step (
4.3
) can be discretized implicitly

(id-\epsilon\partial_{x}^{2})\frac{A-\hat{A}^{k+1}}{\tau}=-\partial_{x}^{2}A\\
\Rightarrow A^{k+1}=\left(id-(\epsilon-\tau)\partial_{xx}^{2}\right)^{-1}(id-\epsilon\partial_{xx}^{2})\hat{A}^{k+1}\,.

The operator on the right-hand side is positive definite (for
\tau<\epsilon
) and is straightforward to calculate numerically with spectral methods (FFT).
Moreover, all the eigenvalues of the operator have modulus smaller than 1, which reaffirms the control of high frequency modes by the regularization.

In figure
2
, we show the results of applying the scheme to the initial condition
A_{0}(x)=5\sin^{3}(20\pi x/L)+2\sin^{3}(12\pi x/L)-\sin^{3}(4\pi x/L)

with
L=10
. We discretize the interval
[0,L]
with 1000 uniformly distributed points, and we track the evolution of
A
up to the time
t_{blow}
,
when the adaptive time step
\tau\approx 0
up to machine precision (
\sim 10^{-16}
).
We repeat the numerical experiment with different values for the regularizing parameter
\epsilon\in\{0.5,0.1,0.05,0.01,0.005,0.001,0.0005\}
, with figure 2 corresponding to
\epsilon=0.1
.
The results are presented in figure
3
, where we see that the numerical solution blows up at earlier times
t_{blow}
for smaller values of
\epsilon
,
with a measured rate of
t_{blow}\sim\epsilon^{0.21}
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.

5.
Determining functionals

In this section, we will show that finitely many linear functionals defined on the phase space uniquely determine the asymptotic behavior of solutions.

To this end, first we derive a uniform a priory estimates of solutions to the problem in the phase space
V^{1}:=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Multiplying the equation (
1.1
) with
-\Delta A^{\ast}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

(5.1)

\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla A\|^{2}+\|\Delta A\|^{2}+(\phi^{2},\lvert\nabla A\rvert^{2})+(|A|^{2},|\nabla A|^{2})\\
\leq\|\nabla A\|^{2}+2\lvert(A\partial_{x}A^{\ast},\phi\partial_{x}\phi)\rvert+2\lvert(A\partial_{y}A^{\ast},\phi\partial_{y}\phi)\rvert\\
+2c_{1}\|\phi\partial_{y}A\|\|\Delta A\|+\lvert\beta\rvert\lVert A\partial_{y}\phi\rVert\lVert\Delta A\rVert

Utilizing the inequality

(5.2)

\|\nabla v\|^{2}\leq\|v\|\|\Delta v\|,\ \ \forall v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

the Ladyzhenskaya inequality

(5.3)

\|v\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\|v\|\|\nabla v\|,\ \ \forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

the interpolation inequality,

(5.4)

\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq d_{0}\lVert\Delta v\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla v\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall v\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

the Young’s inequality

(5.5)

ab\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{p}a^{p}+\frac{1}{q\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}b^{q},\mbox{for all}\ a,b,\varepsilon>0,\ \mbox{with}\ q=p/(p-1),1<p<\infty,

we derive the following inequality from (
5.1
)

(5.6)

\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla A\|^{2}+\|\Delta A\|^{2}+(\phi^{2},\lvert\nabla A\rvert^{2})+(|A|^{2},|\nabla A|^{2})\\
\leq 3\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert^{2}+2|(A\partial_{x}A^{\ast},\phi\partial_{x}\phi)\rvert\\
+2\lvert(A\partial_{y}A^{\ast},\phi\partial_{y}\phi)\rvert+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\lVert\phi\partial_{y}A\rVert^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\partial_{y}\phi\rVert^{2}.

We obtain the following estimates for the terms on the right hand side of (
5.6
) by using the following inequality

(5.7)

\nu_{0}\|\Delta\phi\|^{2}\leq\|\mathbb{L}\phi\|^{2}\leq\nu_{1}\|\Delta\phi\|^{2},\quad\nu_{0}>0,\quad\nu_{1}>0,

in addition to (
5.2
), (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
):

2\lvert(A\partial_{x}A^{\ast},\phi\partial_{x}\phi)\rvert+2\lvert(A\partial_{y}A^{\ast},\phi\partial_{y}\phi)\rvert\\
\leq 4\lVert A\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla A\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\\
\leq 8\lVert A\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\phi\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}+\frac{\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}}{4},\quad\quad\quad\

\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\lVert\phi\partial_{y}A\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\\
\leq\frac{c_{1}^{2}d_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\Delta A\rVert\lVert\nabla A\rVert\leq\frac{c_{1}^{2}d_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert A\rVert^{1/2}\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{3/2}\\
\leq\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2}+\frac{c_{1}^{8}d_{0}^{8}}{4\varepsilon^{7}}\lVert\phi\rVert^{8}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert A\rVert^{2}\\
\leq\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon,c_{1},d_{0})\lVert\phi\rVert^{4}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}\lVert A\rVert^{2}.

By following the same sequence of steps, we can similarly get

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\partial_{y}\phi\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\lVert\Delta\phi\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon,\beta,d_{0})\lVert A\rVert^{4}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}.

Thus we infer from (
5.6
) that

(5.8)

\frac{\tau}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla A\|^{2}+(1-4\varepsilon)\|\Delta A\|^{2}+(\phi^{2},\lvert\nabla A\rvert^{2})+(|A|^{2},|\nabla A|^{2})\\
\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4\nu_{0}}\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}+\frac{\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}}{4}\\
+C(\varepsilon,c_{1},d_{0})\lVert\phi\rVert^{4}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}\lVert A\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon,\beta,d_{0})\lVert A\rVert^{4}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}.

Next, we multiply the equation (
1.2
) in
L^{2}(\Omega)
with
-\mathbb{L}\phi
:

(5.9)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbb{L}\phi,\phi)+\|\mathbb{L}\phi\|^{2}+h(\mathbb{L}\phi,\phi)\\
\leq(|\phi||A|^{2},|\mathbb{L}\phi|)+|c_{2}|(|A|\lvert\partial_{y}A\rvert,|\mathbb{L}\phi|)

Arguing similarly to the calculation of the estimate (
5.8
) and employing the interpolation inequality

\|v\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\leq d_{1}\|\nabla v\|^{\frac{2}{3}}\|v\|^{\frac{1}{3}},

we obtain:

\displaystyle(\lvert\phi\rvert\lvert A\rvert^{2},\lvert\mathbb{L}\phi\rvert)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\lVert\phi\rVert_{L^{6}}^{2}\lVert A\rVert_{L^{6}}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{d_{1}^{6}}{4\varepsilon}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4/3}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2/3}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{8/3}\lVert A\rVert^{4/3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon,d_{1})(\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}+\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}),

\displaystyle\lvert c_{2}\rvert(\lvert A\rvert\lvert\partial_{y}A\rvert,\lvert\mathbb{L}\phi\rvert)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{4\varepsilon}(\lvert A\rvert^{2},\lvert\partial_{y}A\rvert^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\lVert\partial_{y}A\rVert^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2}c_{2}^{2}d_{0}^{2}}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{2}\lVert\Delta A\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2})+C(\varepsilon,c_{2},d_{0})\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{2}.

By using the above estimates, we infer from (
5.9
)

(5.10)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbb{L}\phi,\phi)+(1-2\varepsilon)\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}-\varepsilon\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2}+h(\mathbb{L}\phi,\phi)\\
\leq C(\varepsilon,d_{1})(\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}+\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4})+C(\varepsilon,c_{2},d_{0})\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}.

Finally adding (
5.8
) and (
5.10
) we get

(5.11)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla A\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{L}\phi,\phi)\right]+(1-5\varepsilon)\|\Delta A\|^{2}\\
+(1-2\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{4\nu_{0}})\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}+(\phi^{2},\lvert\nabla A\rvert^{2})+(\lvert A\rvert^{2},\lvert\nabla A\rvert^{2})\\
\leq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon)\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}+\frac{\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}}{4}+C(\varepsilon,c_{1},d_{0})\lVert\phi\rVert^{4}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}\lVert A\rVert^{2}\\
+C(\varepsilon,\beta,d_{0})\lVert A\rVert^{4}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon,d_{1})(\lVert\phi\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}+\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4})\\
+C(\varepsilon,c_{2},d_{0})\lVert A\rVert^{2}\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}

Since the semigroup generated by the problem is dissipative in
V^{0}
there exist
T_{0}>0
and
M_{0}>0
such that

\lVert A(t)\rVert^{2}+\lVert\phi(t)\rVert^{2}\leq M_{0},\quad\forall t\geq T_{0}.

Thus, by choosing
\varepsilon=\min\{\frac{1}{10},\frac{2\nu_{0}}{8\nu_{0}+1}\}
we obtain from (
5.11
) that

\frac{d}{dt}[\tau\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{2}+(\mathbb{L}\phi,\phi)]+\lVert\Delta A\rVert^{2}\lVert\mathbb{L}\phi\rVert^{2}\\
\leq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}\lVert A\rVert^{2}+C(\varepsilon,M_{0},\beta,d_{0},d_{1})(\lVert\nabla A\rVert^{4}+\lVert\nabla\phi\rVert^{4}).

Finally, by using the uniform Gronwall Lemma
2.3
, we infer from the last inequality that there exists

R_{0}>0
, depending only on the parameters of the system, such that

(5.12)

\|\nabla A\|^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}\leq R_{0},\ \ \forall t\geq T_{1},

i.e., the semigroup generated by the problem is bounded dissipative in
V^{1}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

It remains to show that the condition (
2.4
) is satisfied, i.e.,

(5.13)

\|f_{j}(a,\Phi)\|_{H^{-\gamma}}\leq M(R_{0})(\|\nabla a\|+\|\nabla\Phi\|),\ \ j=1,2,

with some
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

In fact, employing the estimate (
5.12
), the following inequality (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
, Chp. 1)

\|v\|_{H^{-\delta}(\Omega)}\leq d_{0}\|v\|_{L^{2/(1+\delta)}(\Omega)}

which is valid for
\delta\in(0,1)
, and the fact that
H^{1}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
L^{\frac{2}{\delta}}(\Omega)
we can bound the last term on the right hand side of (
3.4
)

\|i\beta[\tilde{A}\partial_{y}\Phi]\|_{H^{-\delta}(\Omega)}\leq d_{0}|\beta|\|\tilde{A}\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2/(1+\delta)}}\\
\leq d_{0}|\beta|\|\tilde{A}\|_{L^{2/\delta}}\lVert\partial_{y}\Phi\rVert\leq Cd_{0}\|\nabla\tilde{A}\|\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|\leq C(R_{0})\|\nabla\Phi\|.

Similarly we can show that

\|i\beta[a\partial_{y}\phi]\|_{H^{-\delta}(\Omega)}\leq C(R_{0})\|\nabla a\|.

Utilizing the same arguments we can get necessary bounds for all terms on the right-hand side of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) and obtain the desired estimates (
5.13
).
Thus thanks to the Theorem
2.6
we have

Abstract
We derive a system of stochastic partial differential equations satisfied by the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix whose entries are the Brownian sheets. We prove that the sequence { L d ​ ( s , t ) , ( s , t ) ∈ [ 0 , S ] × [ 0 , T ] } d ∈ ℕ \left\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in[0,S]\times[0,T]\right\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}} of empirical spectral measures of the rescaled matrices is tight on C ⁡ ( [ 0 , S ] × [ 0 , T ] , 𝒫 ⁡ ( ℝ ) ) C([0,S]\times[0,T],\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})) and hence is convergent as d d goes to infinity by Wigner’s semicircle law. We also obtain PDEs which are satisfied by the high-dimensional limiting measure.

Introduction

This paper concerns the eigenvalues of the Brownian sheet matrix
{\bm{X}}=\{\bm{X}(s,t),0\leq s,t<\infty\}
, which is
a symmetric-matrix-valued process with entries
X_{ij}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
given by

X_{ij}(s,t)=\begin{cases}b_{ij}(s,t),&i<j,\\
\sqrt{2}b_{ii}(s,t),&i=j,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
b=\left\{b_{ij}(s,t),0\leq s,t<\infty\right\}_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d}
is a family of independent Brownian sheets.

After the fundamental work
[
25
]
which established the celebrated Wigner’s semicircle law, Brownian motion as a
one-parameter stochastic process was introduced into random matrix theory by Dyson
[
8
]
. Since then, there
has been fruitful literature on the Dyson Brownian motion which is the system of eigenvalues of symmetric Brownian matrix (see, e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
18
,
1
,
9
]
and the references therein), in which Itô’s calculus
has played a key role. By studying the high-dimensional limit of the empirical measures of the Dyson Brownian motion, one can
provide a dynamical proof for Wigner’s semicircle law (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
). The Dyson Brownian motion is also closely
related to interacting particle systems, and the equation (known as the McKean-Vlasov equation) satisfied by its limiting empirical measure
appears naturally in the study of propagation of chaos for large systems of interacting particles (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
11
,
19
]
).

Multiparameter stochastic processes (or random fields) are a natural extension of one-parameter processes, they arise naturally in statistical
mechanics (e.g. Brownian sheet appears in the Ising model
[
14
]
and interacting particle systems
[
15
]
), and systematic theories
have been developed (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
13
]
and the references therein). Motivated by the close connection between random matrix
theory and interacting particle systems, it is natural to develop theories for random matrix with entries being random fields. Recently,
the problem on the collision of eigenvalues of symmetric (Hermitian) matrix whose entries are independent Gaussian fields was investigated
in
[
12
,
20
]
, which to our best knowledge are the only literature on random matrix whose entries are random fields.

Another motivation for studying the Brownian sheet matrix
\bm{X}
is from free probability theory. As shown in
[
24
,
23
]
, many theorems and
concepts in free probability have classical probability analogs, and furthermore free probability is closely connected with random matrix theory. In
particular, free Brownian motion can be viewed as the high-dimensional limit of rescaled Brownian motion matrix which is define by (
1.1
) with

b
being a family of independent Brownian motions. Stochastic calculus for free Brownian motion was developed in
[
3
]
. Free fractional
Brownian motion arose naturally in
[
16
]
when studying the central limit theorem for long-range dependence time series in free probability,
and the stochastic calculus was developed in
[
7
]
. It was shown in
[
17
]
that free fractional Brownian motion is the
high-dimensional limit of empirical measures of the eigenvalues of rescaled fractional Brownian motion matrices.
We remark that the free Brownian motion and the free fractional Brownian motion in
[
3
,
16
,
17
,
7
]
are one-parameter
stochastic processes, and we believe that our study of the Brownian sheet matrix in this paper will provide a useful building block for constructing
free random fields.

In the present paper we shall derive a system of stochastic partial differential equations (
3
) for the eigenvalue processes
of the Brownian sheet matrix
\bm{X}
given by (
1.1
), obtain the tightness of the spectral empirical measures (Theorem
4.1
),
and show that the limit measure satisfies a McKean-Vlasov equation (
4.15
) and a Burgers’ equation (
4.22
).
We briefly explain the structure of the paper below.

Though the Brownian sheet is a simple multivariable extension
of standard Brownian motion,
the stochastic calculus for the Brownian sheet that one needs for deriving the stochastic partial differential equations for
the eigenvalues of the Brownian sheet matrix turns out to be highly non-trivial and cannot be adapted directly from the
classical Itô calculus. In Section
2
, we follow the approach of Cairoli and Walsh in
[
4
]

and develop stochastic calculus tools for the multi-dimensional Brownian sheet on the plane for our purpose. The main results in
this section are Theorems
2.5
and
2.6
which are multi-dimensional Green’s formulas.

In Section
3
, by applying classical Itô’s formula together with Green’s formulas (Theorems
2.5

and
2.6
),
we derive the system of stochastic partial differential equations (
3
) for the eigenvalues of the Brownian sheet matrix

\bm{X}
. Compared with the following system of SDEs for the classical Dyson Brownian motion: for
1\leq i\leq d
,

d\lambda_{i}(t)=\sqrt{2}dW_{i}(t)+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(t)-\lambda_{j}(t)}dt,

(1.2)

where
W=(W_{1},\dots,W_{d})
is a standard
d
-dimensional Brownian motion, we remark that
eq. (
3
) bears some resemblance to (
1.2
) but has several extra high-order terms.

In Section
4
, we study the high-dimensional limit of empirical distributions for the eigenvalue processes of
\bm{X}
.
In Section
4.1
, we establish the tightness of the set of empirical spectral
measures which are viewed as
C([0,S]\times[0,T],\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
-valued random elements (see Theorem

4.1
). This guarantees that every sequence of the empirical spectral measures has a subsequence
which converges weakly. The tightness together with the classical Wigner’s semicircle law implies the existence and
uniqueness of the high-dimensional limit of the empirical spectral measures (see Theorem
4.2
).
In Section
4.2
, we derive partial differential equations (
4.15
) and (
4.22
) that are satisfied
by the limiting measure, by using the property of the semicircle distribution.

Finally, in Appendix
A
we provide some results in matrix analysis which are needed in our analysis.

Define the partial order “

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{G},\mathbb{P})

\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{z},z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\}

\sigma

for each

4.

for each

and

are conditionally independent given

Here, for

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{z}^{1}=\mathcal{F}_{s\infty}:=\underset{v}{\vee}\mathcal{F}_{sv};~~~\mathcal{F}_{z}^{2}=\mathcal{F}_{\infty t}:=\underset{u}{\vee}\mathcal{F}_{ut}.

In particular, the augmented filtration generated by a finite family of independent Brownian sheets satisfies the
above conditions.

Let

is integrable.
We recall the definitions of martingale, strong martingale, weak martingale, and increasing process relative to

]

Definition 1

2.

Suppose

and

such that

(s,s^{\prime}]\times(t,t^{\prime}]

Y((z,z^{\prime}])=Y_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}-Y_{st^{\prime}}-Y_{s^{\prime}t}+Y_{st}.

Definition 2

(a)

1.

Y

2.

\mathbb{E}[Y((z,z^{\prime}])|\mathcal{F}_{z}]=0

(b)

Definition 3

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n}\sup_{z\prec z_{0}}(M_{z}^{n}-M_{z})^{4}\right]\leq 8\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n}\sup_{z\prec z_{0}}|M_{z}^{n}|^{4}+\sup_{z\prec z_{0}}|M_{z}|^{4}\right]\leq 16\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{z\prec z_{0}}|M_{z}|^{4}\right]<\infty.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the dominated convergence theorem and the continuity of
M
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg(\int_{R_{z_{0}}}(M^{n}-M)dN\bigg)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{R_{z_{0}}}(M^{n}-M)^{2}d\langle N\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n,z}(M_{z}^{n}-M_{z})^{2}\langle N\rangle_{z_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{n,z}(M_{z}^{n}-M_{z})^{4}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\langle N\rangle_{z_{0}}^{2}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,~n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.31)

Similarly, we can show the following
L^{2}
-convergence,

\displaystyle\int_{H_{z_{0}}}\widetilde{M}_{s,t_{0}}^{n}N(ds,t)\rightarrow\int_{H_{z_{0}}}M(s,t)N(ds,t),~n\rightarrow\infty.

(2.32)

Recalling that
\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{MN}^{n}(z)=(\psi\cdot MN)_{z}
with
\psi(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{1}_{[z_{1}{\curlywedge\atop\curlywedge}z_{2}]}
,
by (
2.29
), (
2.30
), (
2.31
) and (
2.32
), we have

\displaystyle(\psi\cdot MN)_{z_{0}}=\int_{H_{z_{0}}}M(s,t)N(ds,t)-\int_{R_{z_{0}}}MdN,

and hence by the definition (
2.2
) of
J_{MN}
, we have

J_{MN}(z_{0})=(\psi\cdot MN)_{z_{0}}.

(2.33)

Therefore, we can calculate
\langle J_{MN}\rangle
by (
2.22
),

\displaystyle\langle J_{MN}\rangle_{z}=\iint_{R_{z}\times R_{z}}\mathbf{1}_{[z_{1}{\curlywedge\atop\curlywedge}z_{2}]}d\langle M\rangle_{z_{1}}d\langle N\rangle_{z_{2}}=\int_{R_{z}}d_{t}\langle M\rangle_{st}d_{s}\langle N\rangle_{st},

and hence

d\langle J_{MN}\rangle_{st}=d_{t}\langle M\rangle_{st}d_{s}\langle N\rangle_{st}.

(2.34)

Furthermore, the following equality holds,

\displaystyle J_{MN}(s_{0},t_{0})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{0}}M(s,t_{0})N(ds,t_{0})-\int_{R_{s_{0}t_{0}}}M(s,t)dN(s,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{0}}N(s_{0},t)M(s_{0},dt)-\int_{R_{s_{0}t_{0}}}N(s,t)dM(s,t).

(2.35)

This can be deduced by rewriting (
2.2
) as follows

\displaystyle\int_{R_{z_{0}}}M^{n}dN

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2^{n}-1}M_{z_{i,j}}N({\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\sqsubset\mkern 2.0mu$\cr$\mkern 2.0mu\sqsupset$\cr}}}_{i,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2^{n}-1}M_{z_{i,j}}\left(N(\delta_{i+1,j})-N(\delta_{i,j})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\left(M_{z_{i+1,j}}N(\delta_{i+1,j})-M_{z_{i,j}}N(\delta_{i,j})\right)+\sum_{i,j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\left(M_{z_{i,j}}-M_{z_{i+1,j}}\right)N(\delta_{i+1,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}M_{z_{2^{n},j}}N(\delta_{2^{n},j})-\sum_{i,j=0}^{2^{n}-1}M(\epsilon_{i,j})\left(N(\delta_{i,j})+N({\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\sqsubset\mkern 2.0mu$\cr$\mkern 2.0mu\sqsupset$\cr}}}_{i,j})\right).

By letting
n
go to infinity, we get for
\psi(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{1}_{[z_{1}{\curlywedge\atop\curlywedge}z_{2}]}
,

(\psi\cdot NM)_{z_{0}}=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}M(s_{0},t)N(s_{0},dt)-\int_{R_{z_{0}}}MdN.

This together with (
2.33
) implies (
2.2
).

2.3
Multi-dimensional Green’s formula for martingales on the plane

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
2.5
, the multi-dimensional Green’s formula on the plane.
Let
\{M^{(i)}(s,t),(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
be a family of independent continuous strong martingales on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}

with finite fourth moment. We assume that the increasing process
\langle M^{(i)}\rangle
is deterministic for every
1\leq i\leq d
.
Let
F_{j}=F_{j}(s,t),1\leq j\leq d
be a sequence of predictable processes of the form,

\displaystyle F_{j}(s,t)=F_{j}(s,0)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,i}(s,r)M^{(i)}(s,dr)+\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,0}(s,r)dr,

(2.36)

where
f_{j,i},1\leq j\leq d,0\leq i\leq d
are
\mathcal{F}
-predictable processes.

Theorem 2.5
.

Fix
s_{0},t_{0}>0
. Suppose that
\{F_{j}(s,t)\}_{1\leq j\leq d}
are predictable processes given by (
2.36
).
Assume

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}f_{j,i}(s,t)^{2}d_{t}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{s_{0}t}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}\right]<\infty,\qquad\forall 1\leq i,j\leq d,

(2.37)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}f_{j,0}(s,t)^{2}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}dt\right]<\infty,\qquad\forall 1\leq j\leq d.

(2.38)

Then for any rectangle
A\subseteq R_{s_{0}t_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\partial A}F_{j}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}F_{j}(s,t)dM^{(j)}(s,t)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,i}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,0}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)dt.

(2.39)

Proof.

We will follow the argument in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 6.1]
. Let
A=[s_{1},s_{2}]\times[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset[0,s_{0}]\times[0,t_{0}]
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
F_{j}=0
on the line segment with endpoints
(s_{1},t_{1})
and
(s_{2},t_{1})
.
Indeed, noting
F_{j}(s,t)=F_{j}(s,t_{1})+(F_{j}(s,t)-F_{j}(s,t_{1})),
it follows from

\displaystyle\int_{A}F_{j}(s,t_{1})dM^{(j)}(s,t)=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}F_{j}(s,t_{1})\left(M^{(j)}(ds,t_{2})-M^{(j)}(ds,t_{1})\right)=\int_{\partial A}F_{j}(s,t_{1})M^{(j)}(ds,t),

that (
2.5
) holds for
F_{j}(s,t)
if and only if
it holds for
F_{j}(s,t)-F_{j}(s,t_{1})
.

Next, we consider the case that each stochastic partial derivative
f_{j,i}(s,t)\equiv f_{j,i}\in\mathcal{F}_{s_{1},t_{1}}
is a constant function
for
1\leq j\leq d,0\leq i\leq d
. Then by (
2.36
), we have

\displaystyle F_{j}(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}f_{j,i}\left(M^{(i)}(s,t)-M^{(i)}(s,t_{1})\right)+f_{j,0}(t-t_{1}),(s,t)\in A,1\leq j\leq d.

(2.40)

On one hand, noting that
J_{MN}(A)=J_{MN}(s_{2},t_{2})-J_{MN}(s_{1},t_{2})-J_{MN}(s_{2},t_{1})+J_{MN}(s_{1},t_{1})
, it follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\int_{A}f_{j,i}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial A}f_{j,i}M^{(i)}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)-\int_{A}f_{j,i}M^{(i)}(s,t)dM^{(j)}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial A}f_{j,i}\left(M^{(i)}(s,t)-M^{(i)}(s,t_{1})\right)M^{(j)}(ds,t)-\int_{A}f_{j,i}\left(M^{(i)}(s,t)-M^{(i)}(s,t_{1})\right)dM^{(j)}(s,t).

(2.41)

Here
\int_{\partial A}
is a line integral on
\partial A
with clockwise as its positive direction.

On the other hand, Itô’s formula yields

\displaystyle\int_{A}f_{j,0}(t-t_{1})dM^{(j)}(s,t)

\displaystyle=f_{j,0}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}(t-t_{1})\left(M^{(j)}(s_{2},dt)-M^{(j)}(s_{1},dt)\right)

\displaystyle=f_{j,0}(t_{2}-t_{1})\left(M^{(j)}(s_{2},t_{2})-M^{(j)}(s_{1},t_{2})\right)-f_{j,0}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(M^{(j)}(s_{2},t)-M^{(j)}(s_{1},t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial A}f_{j,0}(t-t_{1})M^{(j)}(ds,t)-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}f_{j,0}M^{(j)}(ds,t)\right)dt.

(2.42)

By (
2.40
), (
2.3
) and (
2.3
), we get (
2.5
). Thus, we have proved the theorem
for the case that all stochastic partial derivatives are constant functions. Note that for
A=\cup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}
where

A_{i}
are disjoint rectangles, one has
\int_{\partial A}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\partial A_{i}}
and
\int_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{A_{i}}
.
Therefore, (
2.5
) also holds for the case that all stochastic partial derivatives are simple functions.

For the general case, recall that the martingales
\{M^{(i)}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are independent and the
increasing processes
\{\left\langle M^{(i)}\right\rangle\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are deterministic. By (
2.37
) and (
2.38
), for
0\leq i\leq d
,
1\leq j\leq d
,
we can find sequences
\{f_{j,i}^{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of bounded simple functions such that as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,t)-f_{j,i}(s,t)\right)^{2}\right]d_{t}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{s_{0}t}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}\rightarrow 0,~1\leq i,j\leq d,

(2.43)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,t)-f_{j,0}(s,t)\right)^{2}\right]dtd_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}\rightarrow 0,\qquad 1\leq j\leq d.

(2.44)

Define

\displaystyle F_{j}^{(n)}(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)M^{(i)}(s,dr)+\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)dr,~1\leq j\leq d.

Then (
2.5
) holds for the family
\big\{F_{j}^{(n)}\big\}_{1\leq j\leq d}
, and it remains to take the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We deal with the left-hand side of (
2.5
) first. It follows from (
2.43
) that,
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, for
1\leq i,j\leq d

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\partial A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,i}(s,r)M^{(i)}(s,dr)M^{(j)}(ds,t)-\int_{\partial A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)M^{(i)}(s,dr)M^{(j)}(ds,t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=1,2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)M^{(i)}(s,dr)M^{(j)}(ds,t_{k})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=1,2}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]d_{r}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{sr}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=1,2}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]d_{r}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{s_{0}r}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0.

(2.45)

Similarly, by (
2.44
), we have as
n\to\infty
, for
1\leq j\leq d
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\partial A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,0}(s,r)drM^{(j)}(ds,t)-\int_{\partial A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)drM^{(j)}(ds,t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=1,2}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t_{k}}\left(f_{j,0}(s,r)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)dr\right)^{2}\right]d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}t_{0}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,0}(s,r)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]drd_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0.

(2.46)

Hence, combing (
2.3
) with (
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\partial A}F_{j}^{(n)}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\partial A}F_{j}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Next, we deal with the first term on the right-hand side of (
2.5
). By (
2.43
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,i}(s,r)M^{(i)}(s,dr)dM^{(j)}(s,t)-\int_{A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)M^{(i)}(s,dr)dM^{(j)}(s,t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)M^{(i)}(s,dr)\right)^{2}\right]d\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st}

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]d_{r}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{sr}d\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]d_{r}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{sr}d\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,i}(s,r)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]d_{r}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{s_{0}r}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty,\ \ \ \forall 1\leq i,j\leq d.

(2.47)

Similarly, by (
2.44
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,0}(s,r)drdM^{(j)}(s,t)-\int_{A}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)drdM^{(j)}(s,t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A}t_{0}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,0}(s,r)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]drd\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st}

\displaystyle\leq t_{0}\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,0}(s,r)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,r)\right)^{2}\right]drd_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty,\ \forall 1\leq j\leq d.

(2.48)

Hence, (
2.3
) and (
2.3
) imply

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}F_{j}^{(n)}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)\rightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}F_{j}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Next we deal with the limit of the second term on the right hand side of (
2.5
). By (
2.34
) and
(
2.43
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{A}f_{j,i}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}(s,t)-\int_{A}f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}(s,t)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{A}\left|f_{j,i}(s,t)-f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,t)\right|^{2}d_{t}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{s_{0}t}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}\right]

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty,\ ~\forall 1\leq i,j\leq d.

(2.49)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,i}^{(n)}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}(s,t)\rightarrow\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,i}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(i)},M^{(j)}}(s,t)

in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Lastly, we deal with the limit of the third term on the right hand side of (
2.5
). By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
and (
2.44
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{A}f_{j,0}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)dt-\int_{A}f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)dt\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\left(f_{j,0}(s,t)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,t)\right)M^{(j)}(ds,t)dt\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\int_{T}^{t_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{S}^{s_{2}}\left(f_{j,0}(s,t)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,t)\right)M^{(j)}(ds,t)\right)^{2}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\left(t_{2}-t_{1}\right)\int_{T}^{t_{2}}\int_{S}^{s_{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(f_{j,0}(s,t)-f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,t)\right)^{2}\right]\big\langle M^{(j)}(ds,t)\big\rangle dt

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty,\ \forall 1\leq j\leq d.

(2.50)

Thus, we have the following convergence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,0}^{(n)}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)dt\rightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,0}(s,t)M^{(j)}(ds,t)dt.

The proof is concluded.
∎

Similarly, for predictable processes of the form

\displaystyle F_{j}(s,t)=F_{j}(0,t)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{s}f_{j,i}(r,t)M^{(i)}(dr,t)+\int_{0}^{s}f_{j,0}(r,t)dr,\ \ 1\leq j\leq d,

(2.51)

where
f_{j,i},\,1\leq j\leq d,\,0\leq i\leq d
are
\mathcal{F}
-predictable processes,
we have the following Green’s formula.

Theorem 2.6
.

Fix
s_{0},t_{0}>0
. Suppose that
\{F_{j}(s,t)\}_{1\leq j\leq d}
are predictable processes
given by (
2.51
). Assume

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}f_{j,i}(s,t)^{2}d_{s}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}d_{t}\big\langle M^{(j)}\big\rangle_{st_{0}}\right]<\infty,\quad\forall 1\leq i,j\leq d,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}f_{j,0}(s,t)^{2}d_{t}\big\langle M^{(i)}\big\rangle_{s_{0}t}ds\right]<\infty,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq d.

Then for any rectangle
A\subseteq R_{s_{0}t_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\partial A}F_{j}(s,t)M^{(j)}(s,dt)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}F_{j}(s,t)dM^{(j)}(s,t)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,i}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(j)},M^{(i)}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{A}f_{j,0}(s,t)M^{(j)}(s,dt)ds.

Proof.

Noting that by the second equality of (
2.2
), we have that for the left-hand side of (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\int_{A}f_{j,i}(s,t)dJ_{M^{(j)},M^{(i)}}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial A}f_{j,i}M^{(i)}(s,t)M^{(j)}(s,dt)-\int_{A}f_{j,i}M^{(i)}(s,t)dM^{(j)}(s,t).

The rest of the proof is the same as that of Theorem
2.5
and thus is omitted.
∎

2.4
Quadratic covariations of
J_{MN}
and
J_{M^{\prime}N^{\prime}}

Let
M,N,M^{\prime},N^{\prime}
be continuous martingales belonging to
\mathfrak{M}_{s}^{4}(z_{0}).
In this subsection, for the completion of the theory, we shall derive the quadratic covariation for
J_{MN}=\psi\cdot MN
and
J_{M^{\prime}N^{\prime}}=\psi\cdot M^{\prime}N^{\prime}

with
\psi(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{1}_{[z_{1}{\curlywedge\atop\curlywedge}z_{2}]}
which are defined in Section
2.2
.
More specifically, we aim to show

d\langle J_{MN},J_{M^{\prime},N^{\prime}}\rangle_{st}=d_{t}\langle M,M^{\prime}\rangle_{st}d_{s}\langle N,N^{\prime}\rangle_{st}.

(2.52)

Recall that
J_{MN}
can be approximated by
J^{n}_{MN}
as in (
2.24
), and that one can approximate the
function
\psi(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{1}_{[z_{1}{\curlywedge\atop\curlywedge}z_{2}]}
by

\displaystyle\psi=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i,j,k,l\in\mathbf{I}_{n}}\psi_{ijkl},

where
\psi_{ijkl}(z_{1},z_{2})=\mathbf{1}_{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\sqsubset\mkern 2.0mu$\cr$\mkern 2.0mu\sqsupset$\cr}}}_{i,j}}(z_{1})\mathbf{1}_{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\sqsubset\mkern 2.0mu$\cr$\mkern 2.0mu\sqsupset$\cr}}}_{k,l}}(z_{2})
and
\mathbf{I}_{n}
is a subset of

\{(i,j,k,l),i,j,k,l\in 1,\dots,2^{n}\}
which consists of
(i,j,k,l)
satisfying
0\leq i<k\leq 2^{n}-1
and
0\leq l<j\leq 2^{n}-1
. Denote by
\mathbf{J}_{n}
the subset of
\mathbf{I}_{n}\times\mathbf{I}_{n}
such that for
((i,j,k,l),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime},k^{\prime},l^{\prime}))\in\mathbf{J}_{n}
, the four rectangles
A={\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\sqsubset\mkern 2.0mu$\cr$\mkern 2.0mu\sqsupset$\cr}}}_{i,j}
,
B={\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\sqsubset\mkern 2.0mu$\cr$\mkern 2.0mu\sqsupset$\cr}}}_{k,l}
,

3
SPDEs for the eigenvalue processes

In this section, we will derive a system of SPDEs satisfied by the eigenvalue processes of the Brownian sheet
matrix
\bm{X}
defined in (
1.1
). We assume that the family
b(s,t)
of independent Brownian sheets have deterministic initial values such that the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix
\bm{X}(0,0)
are distinct.

Recall that the standard
1
-dimensional Brownian sheet
\{B(s,t),(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\}
is a centered Gaussian random
field with covariance function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[B(s_{1},t_{1})B(s_{2},t_{2})\right]=(s_{1}\wedge s_{2})(t_{1}\wedge t_{2}).

It follows directly from Lévy’s characterization of Brownian motion that for any fixed
t_{1},\ t_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{t_{1}}}B(t_{1},\cdot),\ \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{t_{2}}}B(\cdot,t_{2})

are standard
1
-dimensional Brownian motions.

Consider the Brownian sheet matrix defined in (
1.1
). As in Appendix
A
, for
1\leq i\leq d
,
let
\lambda_{i}(s,t):=\Phi_{i}(b(s,t))=\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(\bm{X}(s,t))
be the
i
-th biggest eigenvalue of
\bm{X}(s,t)
, where the
function
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}:\mathbf{S}_{d}\to\mathbb{R}
maps a
d\times d
symmetric matrix
A\in\mathbf{S}_{d}
to its

i
-th biggest eigenvalue
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(A)
.

Let
S,T>0
be constants. By applying Itô’s formula to
\lambda_{i}(S,\cdot)
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(0,0)=\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(S,0)=\Phi_{i}(b(S,T))-\Phi_{i}(b(S,0))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(S,t))b_{kh}(S,dt)+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}(b(S,t))\langle b_{kh}(S,dt)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(S,t))b_{kh}(S,dt)+\dfrac{S}{2}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}(b(S,t))dt.

(3.1)

By (
3
) and (
A.21
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(0,0)=\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(S,t))b_{kh}(S,dt)+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{S}{\lambda_{i}(S,t)-\lambda_{j}(S,t)}dt.

(3.2)

We shall express the right-hand side of (
3.2
) as a sum of double integrals on
[0,S]\times[0,T]
.
We first deal with the second term.

For
i\neq j
, as in Appendix
A
we denote for any

,

\Psi_{ij}(x)=\frac{1}{\Phi_{i}(x)-\Phi_{j}(x)}.

(3.3)

By Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{S}{\lambda_{i}(S,t)-\lambda_{j}(S,t)}=S\Psi_{ij}(b(S,t))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}ds+\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{S}s\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))b_{kh}(ds,t)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{S}s\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}(b(s,t))\langle b_{kh}(ds,t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}ds+\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{S}s\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))b_{kh}(ds,t)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{S}st\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}(b(s,t))ds.

(3.4)

Substituting (
A.23
) into (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{S}{\lambda_{i}(S,t)-\lambda_{j}(S,t)}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}ds+\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{S}s\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))b_{kh}(ds,t)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{2st}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{3}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))}\sum_{l:l\neq i,j}\dfrac{st}{\left(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{l}(s,t)\right)\left(\lambda_{j}(s,t)-\lambda_{l}(s,t)\right)}ds.

(3.5)

Lastly, we substitute (
3

3.2

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(0,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(S,t))b_{kh}(S,dt)+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}dsdt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}s\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))b_{kh}(ds,t)dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{2st}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{3}}dsdt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j\neq l:j\neq i,l\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))}\dfrac{st}{\left(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{l}(s,t)\right)\left(\lambda_{j}(s,t)-\lambda_{l}(s,t)\right)}dsdt.

The last term on the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(0,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(S,t))b_{kh}(S,dt)+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}dsdt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}s\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))b_{kh}(ds,t)dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{2st}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{3}}dsdt.

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}\chi_{\epsilon}(\Phi)\right)(b(0,t))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{s}\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}\chi_{\epsilon}(\Phi)+\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial\chi_{\epsilon}}{\partial x_{l}}(\Phi)\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{l}}{\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}\right)(b(r,t))b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}(dr,t)

Therefore, substitute (

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(0,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}\iint_{R_{ST}}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))db_{kh}(s,t)+\sum_{k\leq h}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\iint_{R_{ST}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}(b(s,t))dJ_{b_{kh}b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\leq h}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\iint_{R_{ST}}\dfrac{t}{2}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}(b(s,t))b_{kh}(s,dt)ds+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}dsdt

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\sum_{k\leq h}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))sb_{kh}(ds,t)dt+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\dfrac{2st}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{3}}dsdt.

(3.12)

Noting that by (
3.3
),

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))

\displaystyle=\dfrac{-1}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}-\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{j}}{\partial b_{kh}}\right),

we have, by (
A.3
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{\partial\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Therefore, (
3
) can be written in a symmetric form: for
(S,T)\prec\prec(\sigma,\tau)
and
1\leq i\leq d
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(S,T)-\lambda_{i}(0,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}\iint_{R_{ST}}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))db_{kh}(s,t)+\sum_{k\leq h}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\iint_{R_{ST}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}(b(s,t))dJ_{b_{kh}b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\leq h}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\iint_{R_{ST}}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}(b(s,t))\Big(tb_{kh}(s,dt)ds+sb_{kh}(ds,t)dt\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{S}\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}+\dfrac{2st}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{3}}\right)dsdt.

(3.13)

Recalling that we have assumed the initial eigenvalues are distinct, by the continuity of eigenvalue functions, we have
(0,0)\prec\prec(\sigma,\tau)
a.s.
Thus, for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
, we have the following formal partial differential equations
near the initial point
(0,0)
:
for
1\leq i\leq d
,

\displaystyle d\lambda_{i}(s,t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}(b(s,t))db_{kh}(s,t)+\sum_{k\leq h}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}(b(s,t))dJ_{b_{kh}b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}(s,t)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\leq h}\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}(b(s,t))\Big(tb_{kh}(s,dt)ds+sb_{kh}(ds,t)dt\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j:j\neq i}\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t)}+\dfrac{2st}{(\lambda_{i}(s,t)-\lambda_{j}(s,t))^{3}}\right)dsdt.

4
High-dimensional limit of the empirical spectral distributions

In this section, we study the high-dimensional limit of empirical spectral measure of the rescaled Brownian sheet matrices. In Section
4.1
, we first obtain the tightness of the empirical spectral measures (Theorem
4.1
), and then show the convergence by Wigner’s theorem (Theorem
4.2
). In Section
4.2
, we derive a PDE for the Stieltjes transform of the limiting measure and also a McKean-Vlasov equation for the limiting measure.

4.1
Tightness and high-dimensional limit

For every integer
d\geq 1
, let
\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)
be a
d\times d
matrix given by (
1.1
), and
\{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s,t):1\leq i\leq d\}

be the set of eigenvalues of
\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)
. Define the empirical spectral measure of
\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)/\sqrt{d}

\displaystyle L_{d}(s,t)(dx)=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\delta_{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s,t)/\sqrt{d}}(dx).

(4.1)

For a measurable function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we write

\displaystyle\langle g,L_{d}(s,t)\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)L_{d}(s,t)(dx)=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}g\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s,t)}{\sqrt{d}}\right).

(4.2)

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of probability measures
on
\mathbb{R}
equipped with its weak topology and
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be the set of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
that vanish at infinity.
Throughout this subsection, let
S
and
T
be two fixed positive numbers, and recall the notation

R_{ST}=[0,S]\times[0,T].

The following tightness criterion for probability-measure-valued stochastic processes is a straightforward generalization of

[
21
, Proposition B.3]
(see also
[
18
, Section 3]
where this criterion was applied implicitly).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\mu_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C(R_{ST},\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
be a sequence of probability-measure-valued
random fields. Assume the following conditions are satisfied:

(A)

there exists a non-negative function
\varphi(x)
satisfying
\lim\limits_{|x|\to+\infty}\varphi(x)=+\infty
and

\displaystyle\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle\varphi,\mu_{d}(s,t)\right\rangle\right|^{\gamma}\right]<\infty,~\forall(s,t)\in R_{ST},

for some
\gamma>0
;

(B)

there exists a countable dense subset
\{f_{i}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, such that for some positive constants
a_{1}>1
and
a_{2}>1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle f_{i},\mu_{d}(s_{2},t_{2})\right\rangle-\left\langle f_{i},\mu_{d}(s_{1},t_{1})\right\rangle\right|^{a_{1}}\right]\leq C_{f_{i},S,T}|(s_{2},t_{2})-(s_{1},t_{1})|^{a_{2}}

for all
(s_{1},t_{1}),(s_{2},t_{2})\in R_{ST},d\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\in N
, where
C_{f_{i},S,T}
is a constant depending only on
S
,
T
and
f_{i}
.

Then the set
\{\mu_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
of
C(R_{ST},\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
-valued random elements is tight, i.e., it induces a tight family of probability measures on
C(R_{ST},\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
.

The Kolmogorov continuity theorem for random fields (see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 2.5.1 in Chapter 5]
) implies that, on every compact interval, the
Brownian sheet is
\beta
-Hölder continuous for
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. The following lemma is a direct consequence of Fernique’s theorem (
[
10
]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there exists a positive constant
\delta=\delta(\beta,S,T)
depending only on
(\beta,S,T)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\delta\left\|B\right\|_{\beta;R_{ST}}^{2}\right)\right]<\infty,

where

\displaystyle\left\|B\right\|_{\beta;R_{ST}}=\sup_{(s_{1},t_{1}),(s_{2},t_{2})\in R_{ST}}\dfrac{\left|B(s_{2},t_{2})-B(s_{1},t_{1})\right|}{|(s_{2},t_{2})-(s_{1},t_{1})|^{\beta}}

(4.3)

is the
\beta
-Hölder norm of
B
on the rectangle
R_{ST}
.

Now we are ready to derive the following result on the tightness of the sequence
\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that there exists a nonnegative function
\varphi(x)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with bounded derivative, such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|x|\to+\infty}\varphi(x)=+\infty~\mbox{ and }~\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\left\langle\varphi,L_{d}(0,0)\right\rangle<\infty.

(4.4)

Then the sequence
\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
C(R_{ST},\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

Let
f
be an arbitrary continuously differentiable function with bounded derivative. By the mean value theorem and
the Hoffman-Wielandt inequality (see e.g.
[
1
, Lemma 2.1.19]
), we have for
(s_{2},t_{2}),(s_{1},t_{1})\in R_{ST}
,

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle f,L_{d}(s_{2},t_{2})\right\rangle-\left\langle f,L_{d}(s_{1},t_{1})\right\rangle\right|^{2}=\left|\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(f\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s_{2},t_{2})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)-f\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s_{1},t_{1})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|f\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s_{2},t_{2})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)-f\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}^{d}(s_{1},t_{1})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right|^{2}\leq\dfrac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{d^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left|\lambda_{i}^{d}(s_{2},t_{2})-\lambda_{i}^{d}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\left|X_{ij}^{d}(s_{2},t_{2})-X_{ij}^{d}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{2}=\dfrac{2\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}{d^{2}}\sum_{i\leq j}\left|b_{ij}(s_{2},t_{2})-b_{ij}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{2}.

(4.5)

Noting that
\{b_{ij}(s,t)\}_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d}
are standard Brownian sheets, by (
4.1
) and the Minkowski inequality,
we have for some
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\left\langle f,L_{d}(s_{2},t_{2})\right\rangle-\left\langle f,L_{d}(s_{1},t_{1})\right\rangle\right|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{4\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}}{d^{4}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i\leq j}\left|b_{ij}(s_{2},t_{2})-b_{ij}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{4\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}}{d^{4}}\left(\sum_{i\leq j}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|b_{ij}(s_{2},t_{2})-b_{ij}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{4}\right]\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{4\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}}{d^{4}}\left(\dfrac{d(d+1)}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left|b_{11}(s_{2},t_{2})-b_{11}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{4}\right]\right)^{1/2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{(d+1)^{2}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}}{d^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|b_{11}(s_{2},t_{2})-b_{11}(s_{1},t_{1})\right|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|b_{11}\right\|_{\beta;R_{ST}}^{4}|(s_{2},t_{2})-(s_{1},t_{1})|^{4\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(\beta,f^{\prime},S,T)|(s_{2},t_{2})-(s_{1},t_{1})|^{4\beta},

(4.6)

where
C(\beta,f^{\prime},S,T)
is a finite positive constant by Lemma
4.2
.

As a consequence, Condition (A) in Lemma
4.1
is satisfied with
\gamma=4
. Moreover, if we choose
\beta\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, then assumption (
4.4
) and (
4.1
) together yield Condition (B) in Lemma
4.1
with
a_{1}=4
,
a_{2}=4\beta
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
being a sequence of functions in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with bounded derivative that is dense in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. Then the proof is concluded by Lemma
4.1
. ∎

Remark 4.1
.

In the above proof, the independence of the Brownian sheets
b_{ij}
(
i\leq j
) actually is not used.

Denote by
\mu_{sc}(dx)
the semicircle distribution, i.e.
\mu_{sc}(dx)=p_{sc}(x)dx
, where the density function is given by

\displaystyle p_{sc}(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}1_{[-2,2]}(x).

Let
\{\tilde{\mu}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}
be an element in
C(R_{ST},\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
such that
\tilde{\mu}(s,t)
is a probability measure with density function
\tilde{p}_{s,t}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{st}}p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})
. That is,
\tilde{\mu}(s,t)
is a rescaled semicircle distribution. Here, we use the convention that
\tilde{\mu}(s,t)(dx)=\delta_{0}(dx)
if
st=0
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume the same condition as in Theorem
4.1
. Also assume that
\{\bm{X}^{d}(0,0),d\in\mathbb{N}\}
are symmetric deterministic matrices such that

\displaystyle D:=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)\right\|<\infty,

where
\|\cdot\|
is the operator norm (the operator norm of a symmetric matrix is its largest eigenvalue), and that
L_{d}(0,0)
converges weakly to some probability measure
\mu(0,0)
as
d
goes to infinity.

Then, as
d\to\infty
,
\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}
converges in probability to
\{\mu(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}
in
C(R_{ST},\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}))
which is given by

\displaystyle\mu(s,t)=\tilde{\mu}(s,t)\boxplus\mu(0,0),

(4.7)

where
\boxplus
is the free additive convolution of two probability measures (
[
1
, Definition 5.3.20]
).

Proof.

For any fixed
(s,t)\in R_{ST}
with
st>0
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)-\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)\right)+\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(0,0).

By the self-similarity property of the Brownian sheet, one can see that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{std}}\left(\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)-\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)\right)
is a
d\times d

Wigner matrix (see e.g.
[
1
, Section 2.1]
for the definition). By Wigner’s semicircle law (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 2.1.1]
),
the empirical spectral measure of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{std}}\left(\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)-\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)\right)
converges in probability to the semicircle distribution

\mu_{sc}
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
as
d\to\infty
. Thus, the empirical spectral measure of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)-\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)\right)

converges in probability to the measure
\tilde{\mu}(s,t)
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
as
d\to\infty
. Note that the empirical spectral measure of the
matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)
is
L_{d}(0,0)
, which converges to
\mu(0,0)
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, by
[
1
, Theorem 5.4.5]
,
for every
(s,t)\in R_{ST}
with
st>0
, the empirical spectral measure of the matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)
converges in probability
to the measure
\mu(s,t)
given by (
4.7
) in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
as
d
goes to infinity. Moreover, when
st=0
,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(0,0)
, and the empirical spectral measures converge to
\mu(0,0)
in
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
.

By Theorem
4.1
, the sequence
\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight. Let
\{\nu(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}

be the weak limit of an arbitrary convergent subsequence of
\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
. Noting that for every fixed

(s,t)\in R_{ST}
,
L_{d}(s,t)
is the empirical spectral measure of the matrix
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\bm{X}^{d}(s,t)
and it converges
in probability to the deterministic measure
\mu(s,t)
, we can conclude that
\nu(s,t)=\mu(s,t)
for
(s,t)\in R_{ST}
.

Therefore, the limit of any convergent subsequence of
\{L_{d}(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}
is the deterministic measure

\{\mu(s,t),(s,t)\in R_{ST}\}
given by (
4.7
). The proof is concluded.
∎

4.2
PDEs for the limit measure

It is known (see e.g.
[
1
]
) that the high-dimensional limit
\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)
of the empirical measures of Dyson Brownian
motion (
1.2
) satisfies the following McKean-Vlasov equation,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)=\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)\hat{\mu}_{t}(dy),~~\mbox{for }f\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

(4.8)

The Stieltjes transform

\hat{G}_{t}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-x}\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx),\mbox{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}

of
\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)
solves the following complex version of inviscid Burgers’ equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{G}_{t}(z)+\hat{G}_{t}(z)\partial_{z}\hat{G}_{t}(z)=0.

In this subsection, we will derive parallel PDEs for the limit
\mu(s,t)
(see Theorem
4.2
) of the empirical spectral
measures of the rescaled Brownian sheet matrices. We remark that the equations are obtained by the properties the semicircle
distribution and may have other equivalent forms.

Assume
\mu(0,0)(dx)=\delta_{0}(dx)
, then the limiting measure
\mu(s,t)(dx)=\tilde{\mu}(s,t)(dx)
, recalling that

\tilde{\mu}(s,t)(dx)=\tilde{p}_{s,t}(x)dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{st}}p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})dx

is a rescaled semicircle distribution. Thus,
for a test function
f
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}\left\langle f,\mu(s,t)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{st}}f(x)p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\sqrt{st}x)p_{sc}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\sqrt{s}x}{2\sqrt{t}}f^{\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)p_{sc}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x^{2}}{4}f^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)p_{sc}(x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x}{4\sqrt{st}}f^{\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)p_{sc}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x^{2}}{4(st)^{3/2}}f^{\prime\prime}(x)p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x}{4(st)^{3/2}}f^{\prime}(x)p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4st}\left\langle x^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(x),\mu(s,t)\right\rangle+\dfrac{1}{4st}\left\langle xf^{\prime}(x),\mu(s,t)\right\rangle.

(4.9)

Noting that the density of the measure
\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)
is
\hat{p}_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{t})
, the left-hand side of
(
4.8
) can be written as

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}f(x)p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{t})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\sqrt{t}x)p_{sc}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{t}}f^{\prime}(\sqrt{t}x)p_{sc}(x)dx.

(4.10)

Similarly, the right-hand side of (
4.8
) can be written as

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}\hat{\mu}_{t}(dx)\hat{\mu}_{t}(dy)=\dfrac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(\sqrt{t}x)-f^{\prime}(\sqrt{t}y)}{\sqrt{t}(x-y)}p_{sc}(x)p_{sc}(y)dxdy.

(4.11)

Substituting (
4.2
) and (
4.11
) into (
4.8
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}xf^{\prime}(\sqrt{t}x)p_{sc}(x)dx=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(\sqrt{t}x)-f^{\prime}(\sqrt{t}y)}{x-y}p_{sc}(x)p_{sc}(y)dxdy,~\forall t>0.

(4.12)

Taking derivative with respect to
t
for both sides, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{t}x)p_{sc}(x)dx=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{xf^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{t}x)-yf^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{t}y)}{x-y}p_{sc}(x)p_{sc}(y)dxdy,~\forall t>0.

(4.13)

Now, combining (
4.2
), (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}\left\langle f,\mu(s,t)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x^{2}}{4}f^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)p_{sc}(x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{x}{4\sqrt{st}}f^{\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)p_{sc}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{xf^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)-yf^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{st}y)}{x-y}p_{sc}(x)p_{sc}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{4\sqrt{st}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(\sqrt{st}x)-f^{\prime}(\sqrt{st}y)}{x-y}p_{sc}(x)p_{sc}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{xf^{\prime\prime}(x)-yf^{\prime\prime}(y)}{x-y}\cdot\dfrac{1}{st}p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})p_{sc}(y/\sqrt{st})dxdy

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}\cdot\dfrac{1}{st}p_{sc}(x/\sqrt{st})p_{sc}(y/\sqrt{st})dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{xf^{\prime\prime}(x)-yf^{\prime\prime}(y)}{x-y}\mu(s,t)(dx)\mu(s,t)(dy)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{4}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x)-f^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}\mu(s,t)(dx)\mu(s,t)(dy).

(4.14)

Appendix A
Some lemmas in matrix calculus

In this Appendix, we provide some results in matrix analysis which are used in Sections 3 and 4.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d}),b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{d})
be two
d
-dimensional vectors such that
\|a\|=\|b\|=1
and
a\cdot b=0
. Then

\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}(a_{i}b_{j}+a_{j}b_{i})^{2}=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}(a_{i}a_{j}+b_{i}b_{j})^{2}=2.

Proof.

This is elementary to verify:

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}(a_{i}b_{j}+a_{j}b_{i})^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\left(a_{i}^{2}b_{j}^{2}+a_{j}^{2}b_{i}^{2}+2a_{i}b_{i}a_{j}b_{j}\right)=2\|a\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}+2(a\cdot b)^{2}=2.

Similarly, one can show
\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d}(a_{i}a_{j}+b_{i}b_{j})^{2}=2
.
∎

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k<h}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{\left|U_{ki}U_{hj}+U_{hi}U_{kj}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}+4\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{\left|U_{ki}U_{kj}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{\sum_{k,h}\left|U_{ki}U_{hj}+U_{hi}U_{kj}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}}=2\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}},

where the last equality follows from the orthogonality of
U
and Lemma
A.1
. This proves (
A.21
).

Next, we show (
A.3
). By the chain rule, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}=(\mathbf{1}_{[k<h]}+\sqrt{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{[k=h]})\bigg(\sum_{k^{\prime}<h^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kh}\partial X_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}+2\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kh}\partial X_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}^{2}}\bigg).

(A.24)

We choose the parameter
\theta=X_{kh}
and
\xi=X_{h^{\prime}k^{\prime}}
in (
A.2
). The terms with second order or third order derivative
vanish and we only need to consider the terms with only the first order derivative. Note that for indices
1\leq p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq d

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}<h^{\prime}}\left(U^{\textsf{T}}\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial X_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}U\right)_{p_{1}p_{2}}\left(U^{\textsf{T}}\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial X_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}}U\right)_{q_{1}q_{2}}+2\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{d}\left(U^{\textsf{T}}\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial X_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}}U\right)_{p_{1}p_{2}}\left(U^{\textsf{T}}\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial X_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}}U\right)_{q_{1}q_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}<h^{\prime}}(U_{k^{\prime}p_{1}}U_{h^{\prime}p_{2}}+U_{h^{\prime}p_{1}}U_{k^{\prime}p_{2}})(U_{k^{\prime}q_{1}}U_{h^{\prime}q_{2}}+U_{h^{\prime}q_{1}}U_{k^{\prime}q_{2}})+2\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{d}U_{k^{\prime}p_{1}}U_{k^{\prime}p_{2}}U_{k^{\prime}q_{1}}U_{k^{\prime}q_{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{d}U_{k^{\prime}p_{1}}U_{k^{\prime}q_{1}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{h^{\prime}=1}^{d}U_{h^{\prime}p_{2}}U_{h^{\prime}q_{2}}\bigg)+\bigg(\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{d}U_{k^{\prime}p_{1}}U_{k^{\prime}q_{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{h^{\prime}=1}^{d}U_{h^{\prime}p_{2}}U_{h^{\prime}q_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{[p_{1}=q_{1}]}\mathbf{1}_{[p_{2}=q_{2}]}+\mathbf{1}_{[p_{1}=q_{2}]}\mathbf{1}_{[p_{2}=q_{1}]}.

(A.25)

Now taking sum over
(k^{\prime},h^{\prime})
for (
A.2
) (i.e. taking sum over the non-zero terms including
U^{\textsf{T}}\partial_{\xi}XU
), and
applying (
A
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}\leq h^{\prime}}\dfrac{\partial^{3}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}\partial b_{k^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{2}}=2\left(\mathbf{1}_{[k<h]}+\sqrt{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{[k=h]}\right)\sum_{j:j\neq i}\bigg(\frac{(U^{\textsf{T}}\partial_{\theta}X)_{jj}}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}-\frac{(U^{\textsf{T}}\partial_{\theta}X)_{ii}}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\bigg).

This together with (
A
) yields the first equality of (
A.3
). The second equality of (
A.3
) now follows (
A.18
):

\displaystyle 2\left(2\,\mathbf{1}_{[k<h]}+\sqrt{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{[k=h]}\right)\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{U_{kj}U_{hj}-U_{ki}U_{hi}}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\left(\mathbf{1}_{[k<h]}+\sqrt{2}\,\mathbf{1}_{[k=h]}\right)\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\bigg(\dfrac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{j}}{\partial X_{kh}}-\dfrac{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kh}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j:j\neq i}\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{j}}{\partial b_{kh}}-\dfrac{\partial\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}}\right).

This proves (
A.3
).

Now we show (
A.23
). Note that for
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial b_{kh}}\left(-\Psi_{ij}^{2}\dfrac{\partial(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\leq h}2\Psi_{ij}^{3}\left(\dfrac{\partial(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}}\right)^{2}-\sum_{k\leq h}\Psi_{ij}^{2}\dfrac{\partial^{2}(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}.

(A.26)

For the first term of (
A
), by (
A.18
) and the orthogonality of the columns of
U
, for
i\neq j
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}2\Psi_{ij}^{3}\bigg(\dfrac{\partial(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{3}}\left(\sum_{k<h}\left(\dfrac{\partial(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(\dfrac{\partial(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kk}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{3}}\left(\sum_{k<h}\bigg(\dfrac{\partial(\tilde{\Phi}_{i}-\tilde{\Phi}_{j})}{\partial X_{kh}}\bigg)^{2}+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\bigg(\dfrac{\partial(\tilde{\Phi}_{i}-\tilde{\Phi}_{j})}{\partial X_{kk}}\bigg)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{3}}\left(4\sum_{k<h}\left(U_{ki}U_{hi}-U_{kj}U_{hj}\right)^{2}+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left(U_{ki}^{2}-U_{kj}^{2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{4}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{3}}\sum_{k,h=1}^{d}\left(U_{ki}U_{hi}-U_{kj}U_{hj}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{8}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{3}}=\dfrac{8}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{3}},

(A.27)

where the last step follows from Lemma
A.1
.

For the second term of (
A
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}\Psi_{ij}^{2}\dfrac{\partial^{2}(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{2}}\left(\sum_{k<h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{i}}{\partial b_{kk}^{2}}-\sum_{k<h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{j}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{j}}{\partial b_{kk}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})^{2}}\left(\sum_{k<h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kh}^{2}}+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kk}^{2}}-\sum_{k<h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{j}}{\partial X_{kh}^{2}}-2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{j}}{\partial X_{kk}^{2}}\right).

(A.28)

By (
A.19
), the orthogonality of the columns of
U
, and Lemma
A.1
, for
i\neq j
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k<h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kh}^{2}}+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}{\partial X_{kk}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k<h}2\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{\left|U_{ki}U_{hl}+U_{hi}U_{kl}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}2\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{\left|U_{ki}U_{kl}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{2\sum_{k<h}\left|U_{ki}U_{hl}+U_{hi}U_{kl}\right|^{2}+4\sum_{k}\left|U_{ki}U_{kl}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{\sum_{k,h}\left|U_{ki}U_{hl}+U_{hi}U_{kl}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}=\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}.

(A.29)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k<h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{j}}{\partial X_{kh}^{2}}+2\sum_{k=1}^{d}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\tilde{\Phi}_{j}}{\partial X_{kk}^{2}}=\sum_{l:l\neq j}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}.

(A.30)

Putting (
A
) and (
A.30
) to (
A
) yields that the second term of (
A
) now is

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}\Psi_{ij}^{2}\dfrac{\partial^{2}(\Phi_{i}-\Phi_{j})}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}-\sum_{l:l\neq j}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}\Bigg).

(A.31)

By substituting (
A
) and (
A.31
) into (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq h}\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{ij}}{\partial b_{kh}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{8}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{3}}-\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{l:l\neq i}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}-\sum_{l:l\neq j}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{4}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{3}}-\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{l:l\neq i,j}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}}-\sum_{l:l\neq i,j}\dfrac{2}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{4}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{3}}-\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\sum_{l:l\neq i,j}\dfrac{2\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}\right)}{\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}\right)\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}\right)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{4}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{3}}+\dfrac{1}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})}\sum_{l:l\neq i,j}\dfrac{2}{\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{l}\right)\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}\right)}.

This proves (
A.23

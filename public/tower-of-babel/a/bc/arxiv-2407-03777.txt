Semi- and Fully-Discrete Analysis of Lowest-Order Nonstandard Finite Element Methods for the Biharmonic Wave Problem
Neela Nataraj, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier, Aamir Yousuf

Abstract




Abstract:
This paper discusses lowest-order nonstandard finite element methods for space discretization and explicit and implicit schemes for time discretization of the biharmonic wave equation with clamped boundary conditions. A modified Ritz projection operator defined on
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
ensures error estimates under appropriate regularity assumptions on the solution. Stability results and error estimates of optimal order are established in suitable norms for the semidiscrete and explicit/implicit fully-discrete versions of the proposed schemes. Finally, we report on numerical experiments using explicit and implicit schemes for time discretization and Morley, discontinuous Galerkin, and C
0
interior penalty schemes for space discretization, that validate the theoretical error estimates.

Keywords.
Biharmonic wave equation, Modified Ritz projection, Stability, Error estimates.

65M60, 65M06, 65M15

Introduction

1.1
Scope and Problem Formulation

The biharmonic wave model finds application in representing various physical phenomena, such as the bending of plates, and elasticity in thin plates under dynamic loading conditions. This is also a foundational part for more advanced linear and nonlinear models such as Kirchhoff-type equations using Monge–Ampère forms
[
19
,
23
,
42
]
, which in turn serve as model for the transmission of waves, damping phenomena, standing waves, properties of ferromagnetic materials, and vibration modes within plates.

This paper analyses lowest-order nonstandard finite element methods (FEMs) for space discretization and explicit leapfrog/implicit Newmark time discretization schemes for the biharmonic wave equation

(1.1)

u_{tt}+\Delta^{2}u=f(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times\left(0,T\right],

with initial and clamped boundary conditions

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\;u(x,0)=u^{0}(x),&\\
\hskip 0.28436ptu_{t}(x,0)=v^{0}(x)&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
\;u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times\left(0,T\right].\end{cases}

Here
\Omega
is a bounded polygonal Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
and outward-pointing unit normal
n
,

\Delta^{2}u:=\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{4}}+2\>\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial y^{4}}

denotes the biharmonic operator,
\partial u/\partial n=\nabla u\cdot n
is the outer normal derivative of
u
on
\partial\Omega
,
u_{t}
,
u_{tt}
denote the first- and second-order derivatives with respect to time, respectively, and
f\in L^{2}(L^{2}(\Omega))
is a given source function.

Despite being a classical problem, research on the biharmonic problem remains an area of active interest, as evidenced by recent contributions
[

, for fourth-order problems is notoriously challenging. Consequently, nonstandard FEMs such as the Morley FEM
[
13
,
45
,
50
]
, discontinuous Galerkin (dG) FEM
[
29
,
28
]
, and the
C^{0}
interior penalty (

[
39
,
49
]
present attractive alternatives.
Recently, an abstract framework for analyzing lowest-order FEMs for the clamped plate biharmonic problem has been established in
[
14
]
. One aim of this paper is to investigate and adapt such an analysis for the case of biharmonic wave problems.

Fourth-order parabolic problems have been addressed using the Morley FEM
[
21
]
, dG FEM
[
30
]
, and the
C^{0}
interior penalty method
[
34
]
, typically employing the backward Euler method for time discretization. Similar numerical methods have been extensively studied for second-order hyperbolic equations, as seen in

,

,

,

,

,

]
. However, despite their importance in applications, the literature on the numerical approximation of the fourth-order biharmonic wave equation is relatively sparse. Notable contributions include
[
5

, where the biharmonic wave problem is discretized using classical

for the fourth-order Kirchhoff–Love dynamic plate equations using Lagrange FEs in space with both explicit and implicit central difference schemes in time. Additionally, error estimates for the fourth-order wave equation have been derived using a combination of discrete Galerkin and second-order accurate methods for space and time discretizations

1.3
Specific Contributions

We now describe the main contributions of this paper.

•

To the best of our knowledge, this is the
first
attempt to analyze the biharmonic wave equation for nonstandard FEM for spatial and the explicit/implicit schemes for time discretization.

•

The Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition for wave equation is extensively discussed in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
33
]
), but its analysis specifically targeted for the biharmonic wave equation was still
unavailable
.
The CFL condition for explicit time discretization scheme applied to
u_{tt}+\Delta u=f
(resp.
u_{tt}+\Delta^{2}u=f
) reads
k\leq C_{\text{CFL}}h
(resp.
k\leq C_{\text{CFL}}h^{2}
), where
h
represents the maximal mesh-size of a quasi-uniform triangulation of the spatial domain. Here

{C_{\text{CFL}}:=\beta^{-1/2}C^{-1}_{\text{inv}}},

where
C_{\text{inv}}
is the constant from discrete inverse inequality applied to piecewise
H^{1}
(resp.
H^{2}
) functions and
\beta
is the continuity constant corresponding for the bilinear form in the weak formulation for wave (resp. biharmonic wave) equations.
One key observation is that the form of constant that appears in the CFL condition is invariant in the discretization
using explicit scheme for temporal discetization.

•

The CFL condition also shows that explicit schemes applied to higher-order equations (in space) face stringent stability and convergence restrictions due to the conditions on the time step and motivates the
implicit scheme
for temporal discretization discussed in this article.
In comparison to the explicit scheme, the
implicit scheme
discussed in this article (a) removes the necessity for the quasi-uniformity assumption on the mesh and (b) relaxes the constraints imposed by the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition.

•

The regularity results advanced in this work (see Lemma
1.1
) are established under (a) certain smoothness assumptions on
f
and its derivatives and (b) for non-homogeneous initial conditions, which are different from those in
[
46
, Theorem 7.1]
. This is attained by using the approach of explicit solution representation for proving the regularity results rather than using the energy arguments as discussed in
[
46
]
.

•

We employ a modified Ritz projection (see the definition of
\mathcal{R}_{h}
in (
2.8
)) on
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
defined with the help of the companion operator (cf.
[
14
]
).
It is also important to note that this modified Ritz projection readily yields
L^{2}-
estimates (see Corollary
3.9
, Remark
4.4
for the fully-discrete schemes) based on the energy norm error estimate of the solution and
L^{2}-
error estimate of time derivative of the solution without the need for any additional analysis.

•

The test function in the continuous weak form is selected following an approach that consists in lifting discrete functions to
H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
using a suitably defined
smoother

[
14
]
. This
novel
technique in the context of evolution problems helps to bound the semidiscrete and fully-discrete errors by manipulating the continuous and discrete formulations. This is a significant improvement in comparison to more standard approaches that assume higher regularity of the continuous solution and in which the PDE (
1.1
) is tested against a function from the discrete space. Moreover, the new approach facilitates a more elegant error analysis avoiding extra boundary terms that arise in the standard error analysis typically used in the literature, see for example,
[
32
]
.

•

Given the regularity of the exact solution
u
, as discussed in Lemma
1.1
, quasi-optimal convergence is achieved in both the maximum error in energy and the
L^{2}-
norm over a finite time interval. For the semidiscrete scheme, we show convergence rates of
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\gamma_{0}})
and
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{2\gamma_{0}})
, respectively, where
\gamma_{0}\in(1/2,1]
is the index of elliptic regularity of the biharmonic operator and
h
represents the spatial mesh-size. On the other hand, for the explicit/implicit fully-discrete schemes, the convergence rates are
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\gamma_{0}}+k^{2})
and
{\mathcal{O}}(h^{2\gamma_{0}}+k^{2})
, in energy and
L^{2}
norms, where
k
represents the time step.

Regularity.

It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
3
, p. 761]
) that the eigenvalue problem

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}\psi=\lambda\psi\qquad\qquad\;&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\hskip 1.42271pt\quad\psi=0,\ \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n}=0\;\;\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

admits an increasing sequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty
and the corresponding family of eigenfunctions

form an orthonormal basis of

. Let us define an unbounded operator
(A,D(A))
in

by

D(A)=\{\psi\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega):\Delta^{2}\psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}\text{ and }A\psi=\Delta^{2}\psi
for all

. Further, for any

, we define

A^{r}w=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{r}(w,\psi_{n})\psi_{n}\quad\text{and}\quad D(A^{r})=\bigg\{w=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w_{n}\psi_{n}:w_{n}\in\mathbb{R},\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}w_{n}\right|^{2r}<\infty\bigg\}.

Moreover, the space

is equipped with the norm

(1.5)

\lVert w\rVert_{D(A^{r})}=\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2r}\left|(w,\psi_{n})\right|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

In particular,
\|\cdot\|_{D(A^{0})}=\|\cdot\|
and

and

is the index of elliptic regularity of the biharmonic operator (see further details in, e.g.,

7
]
).

Lemma 1.1

(Regularity)
.

Let

be elliptic regularity index of the biharmonic operator. The assumptions

\displaystyle u^{0}\in D(A^{2}),\ v^{0}\in D(A^{3/2}),\text{ and }f(0)\in D(A),\;f_{t}(0)\in D(A^{1/2}),\;f_{tt}(0)\in L^{2}(\Omega),

\lVert u_{t}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{2+\gamma_{0}}(\Omega))},

\lVert u_{tt}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))},

\lVert u_{tt}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(H^{2+\gamma_{0}}(\Omega))}
,
\lVert u_{ttt}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}
, and

2.2

The error control in the semidiscrete and fully-discrete approximation is estimated with the help of the Ritz projection operator

defined from

to the conforming finite dimensional space

does not hold for

for the nonstandard schemes discussed in this paper since the bilinear form

may not be defined for functions in

The alternative ideas that define Ritz projections for nonconforming methods (see for example,
[
21
]
for the fourth-order nonlinear parabolic extended Fisher–Kolmogorov equation), typically assume higher regularity of the solution in space. In this article we resolve this issue by means of a modified Ritz projection (see (

) below) that employs a smoother
Q:V_{h}\rightarrow H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Such a smoother

is defined as
JI_{\rm M}
, where

(resp.

2.2
(resp. Definition

Definition 2.1

(Morley Interpolation
[
14
]
)
.

For all

, the extended Morley interpolation operator
I_{\rm M}:H^{2}({\mathcal{T}})\rightarrow{\rm M}({\mathcal{T}})
is defined by

(I_{\rm{M}}v_{\rm{pw}})(z):=|\mathcal{T}(z)|^{-1}\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}(z)}(v_{\rm{pw}}|_{K})(z)\quad\text{and}\quad\displaystyle\fint_{e}\frac{\partial(I_{\rm M}v_{\rm{pw}})}{\partial n}\,\mathrm{d}s:=\fint_{e}\Big\{\!\!\!\Big\{\frac{\partial v_{\rm{pw}}}{\partial n}\Big\}\!\!\!\Big\}\,\mathrm{d}s.

In case of an interior vertex

represents the collection of attached triangles, and

indicates the number of such triangles connected to vertex

(Companion Operator and Properties

2.3
Error Estimates

The stability and error estimates for the semidiscrete scheme are presented in this subsection. The proofs herein employ the following useful result.

Lemma 2.4

(Gronwall’s Lemma
[
16
]
)
.

Let
g
,
h
, and
r
be non-negative integrable functions on
[0,T]
and let
g
satisfy

\displaystyle g(t)\leq h(t)+\int_{0}^{t}r(s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\text{ for all }t\in(0,T).
Then,

\displaystyle g(t)\leq h(t)+\int_{0}^{t}h(s)r(s)e^{\int_{s}^{t}{r(\tau)\;\text{d}\tau}}\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{for all $t\in(0,T)$}.

Remark 2.5
.

The constants in the stability/error bounds established in this section do not depend exponentially on the final time
T
. This is achieved by carefully manipulating the coefficients of the last terms in the above displayed inequality in such a way that
r(\tau)=1/T
. Then it suffices to
apply Lemma
2.4

to obtain

e^{\int_{s}^{t}{1/T\;\text{d}\tau}}\leq e
.

3
Explicit Leapfrog Scheme

This section describes an explicit, fully-discrete scheme for (

). The stability analysis is carried out in Theorem

and the corresponding error estimates are presented in Subsection

. Two approaches work for the error analysis: Theorem

Even if the use of either approach is common in the literature, to the best of our knowledge, this article is the first to use both approaches and combine lowest-order FE discretization and explicit/implicit time discretization for biharmonic wave equations. We will present details for the explicit leapfrog scheme in this section, whereas the implicit Newmark scheme will be addressed in Section

For a positive integer

This section and the rest of the paper uses the discrete Gronwall Lemma as stated below.

Lemma 3.4

(Discrete Gronwall Lemma
[
37
]
)
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}
,
\{b_{n}\}
, and
\{c_{n}\}
be three non-negative sequences, with
\{c_{n}\}
monotone, that satisfy

\displaystyle a_{n}+b_{n}\leq c_{n}+\mu\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}a_{m},\quad\mu>0,\ a_{0}+b_{0}\leq c_{0}.

Then for
n\geq 0,
it holds that

a_{n}+b_{n}\leq c_{n}e^{n\mu}.

Remark 3.5
.

The constant in the stability/error bounds established in this and the next section does not depend exponentially on the final time
T
. This is achieved by manipulating the coefficients of the terms on the right-hand side such that, when the discrete Gronwall inequality is applied,
c_{n}
depends linearly on
T
, and
\mu=\frac{k}{T}
. Hence after application of discrete Gronwall,
e^{\mu}\leq e
does not depend on
T
, which ensures that the constant in the inequality depends linearly on
T
.

3.1
Stability Analysis

Theorem 3.6

(Stability)
.

Under the CFL condition
k\leq{\beta^{-1/2}C^{-1}_{\rm{inv}}}h^{2}
and the assumption that the triangulation
{\mathcal{T}}
is quasi-uniform, the scheme (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) is stable. Moreover, for
1\leq m\leq N-1
, the following bound holds:

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert+\lVert U^{m+1/2}\rVert_{h}\lesssim\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert+\lVert U^{1/2}\rVert_{h}+\lVert f\rVert_{L^{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}.

The constant absorbed in ”
\lesssim
” above depends on
\alpha,\,\beta\;
from (
2.2
), and
T
.

Proof
.

Choose
v_{h}=2k\delta_{t}U^{n}
as a test function in (
3.4
) to obtain

(3.5)

(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}U^{n},2k\delta_{t}U^{n})+a_{h}(U^{n},2k\delta_{t}U^{n})=(f^{n},2k\delta_{t}U^{n}).

The identities
\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}U^{n}=k^{-1}(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}-\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2})
and
2k\delta_{t}U^{n}=k(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2})
lead to

(3.6)

(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{2}U^{n},2k\delta_{t}U^{n})=(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}-\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2})=\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}-\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2}.

Elementary manipulations show

\displaystyle U^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(U^{n+1/2}+U^{n-1/2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}k(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}-\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}),

\displaystyle 2k\delta_{t}U^{n}

\displaystyle=2(U^{n+1/2}-U^{n-1/2})=k(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}).

This allows to derive the following relations

\displaystyle a_{h}(U^{n},2k\delta_{t}U^{n})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(U^{n+1/2}+U^{n-1/2},U^{n+1/2}-U^{n-1/2})

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}k^{2}a_{h}(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}-\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2})

\displaystyle=a_{h}(U^{n+1/2},U^{n+1/2})-a_{h}(U^{n-1/2},U^{n-1/2})

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}k^{2}a_{h}(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2})+\frac{1}{4}k^{2}a_{h}(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}),

(3.7b)

\displaystyle(f^{n},2k\delta_{t}U^{n})

\displaystyle=k(f^{n},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}).

A straightforward combination of (
3.6
), (
3.7a
)-(
3.7b
)
in (
3.5
) and summation from
n=1
to
n=m
with
1\leq m\leq N-1
result in

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle+a_{h}({U^{m+1/2}},{U^{m+1/2}})-\frac{1}{4}k^{2}a_{h}({\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2})+\frac{1}{4}k^{2}a_{h}(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2})

\displaystyle\quad=\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+a_{h}(U^{1/2},U^{1/2})+k\sum_{n=1}^{m}(f^{n},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}).

Observe that
\frac{1}{4}k^{2}a_{h}\left(\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\right)
on the left-hand side is non-negative and so (
2.2
) leads to

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\alpha\lVert{U^{m+1/2}}\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{4}k^{2}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+\beta\lVert U^{1/2}\rVert_{h}^{2}+k\sum_{n=1}^{m}(f^{n},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}).

Lemma
3.3
shows that
\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert_{h}\leq{C_{\text{inv}}h^{-2}}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert
. This estimate and the CFL condition
k\leq{C^{-1}_{\rm inv}}\beta^{-1/2}h^{2}
control the left-hand side of (
3.8
) as

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\alpha\lVert{U^{m+1/2}}\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{4}k^{2}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}\geq\frac{3}{4}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\alpha\lVert U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}.

A consequence of Cauchy–Schwarz and Young inequalities (with
a=\lVert f^{n}\rVert
,
b=\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert
, and
\epsilon=2T
) reads

\displaystyle(f^{n},\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2})

\displaystyle\leq\;\lVert f^{n}\rVert\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert\leq\;{T}\lVert f^{n}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4T}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}+\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;{T}\lVert f^{n}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2T}\left(\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2}\right).

On the other hand, we note that

\displaystyle k\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert f^{n}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq mk\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq T\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{m}\left(\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n-1/2}\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+2\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}.

A combination of the above relations with the bound

(used twice) in (

) provides

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}\lesssim\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert U^{1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}+\lVert f\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(L^{2}(\Omega))}+k/T\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}U^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}.

A discrete Gronwall inequality from Lemma
3.4
concludes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\zeta^{m+1/2}\rVert_{h}^{2}\lesssim h^{4\gamma_{0}}N_{(f,u)}^{2}+{k^{4}}M_{(u)}^{2}+\frac{k}{T}\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\lVert\zeta^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2}_{h}+\frac{k}{T}\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\lVert\bar{\partial}_{t}\zeta^{n+1/2}\rVert^{2},

Conclusions

This article provides a comprehensive analysis of the biharmonic wave equation, combining nonstandard finite element methods for spatial discretization with explicit and implicit schemes for temporal discretization. A CFL condition specifically tailored to the biharmonic wave equation is derived. Though the explicit scheme for time discretization is attractive, we have found that it requires a quasi-uniform mesh, and in turn a stringent CFL condition. On the other hand, the implicit schemes discussed in this paper relax both requirements. A modified Ritz projection and a novel lifting technique provide a different perspective to the error analysis and facilitate error estimates under sharper regularity assumptions on the data in comparison to the results available in the literature.
The study demonstrates quasi-optimal convergence for semidiscrete and fully-discrete schemes, and this provides a robust foundation for problems for future study; for example, nonlinear plate problems
[
18
]
, coupled multiphysics (poroelastic
[
40
]
, thermoelastic
[
47
]
, thermoelastic-diffusion
[
2
]
) thin plate models. We also mention that a posteriori error analysis and adaptivity for this family of schemes is a direction we will explore in future work.

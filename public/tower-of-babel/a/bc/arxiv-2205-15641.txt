On the braided Connes-Moscovici construction
Ivan Bartulović

Abstract
. In 1998, Connes and Moscovici defined the cyclic cohomology of Hopf algebras. In 2010, Khalkhali and Pourkia proposed a braided generalization: to any Hopf algebra H H in a braided category ℬ \mathcal{B} , they associate a paracocyclic object in ℬ \mathcal{B} . In this paper we explicitly compute the powers of the paracocyclic operator of this paracocyclic object. Also, we introduce twisted modular pairs in involution for H H and derive (co)cyclic modules from them. Finally, we relate the paracocyclic object associated with H H to that associated with an H H -module coalgebra via a categorical version of the Connes-Moscovici trace.

1.
Introduction

Cyclic (co)homology of algebras was introduced in the 1980s by Connes
[
3
,
4
]
and Tsygan
[
11
]
independently.
To any algebra is associated a cocyclic vector space (that is, a cocyclic object in the category of vector spaces) whose cohomology is called the cyclic cohomology of the algebra.
The notion of a (co)cyclic object in a category, introduced by Connes
[
3
]
, is a generalization of the notion of a (co)simplicial object in that category.

Cyclic cohomology has been considered in various versions and generalizations.
In particular, in
[
6
]
, Connes and Moscovici defined the Hopf cyclic cohomology by associating a cocyclic vector space to a Hopf algebra
H
over
\mathbb{C}
endowed with a modular pair in involution (that is, a pair
(\delta,\sigma)
where
\delta\colon H\to\mathbb{C}
is a character and
\sigma\in H
is a grouplike element verifying the modular pair condition
\delta(\sigma)=1
and a certain involutivity condition).
Also, in
[
5
]
, they relate the Hopf cyclic cohomology of
H
to the cyclic cohomology of an
H
-module algebra by means of a trace map.

Braided monoidal categories were defined by Street and Joyal in the 1980s and appeared in many areas of mathematics such as low-dimensional topology and representation theory.
Several generalizations of cyclic (co)homology were introduced in the braided setting.
In this paper, we focus on the braided generalization of the Connes-Moscovici construction due to Khalkhali and Pourkia
[
8
]
.
Let
H
be a Hopf algebra in a braided monoidal category
\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{B},\otimes,\mathbb{1})
. A modular pair for
H
is a pair
(\delta,\sigma)
, where
\delta\colon H\to\mathbb{1}
is an algebra morphism and
\sigma\colon\mathbb{1}\to H
is a coalgebra morphism such that
\delta\sigma=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{1}}
. In
[
8
]
, Khalkhali and Pourkia associate to any modular pair
(\delta,\sigma)
for
H
a paracocyclic object
\textbf{CM}_{\bullet}(H,\delta,\sigma)=\{\textbf{CM}_{n}(H,\delta,\sigma)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{B}
.
This object is cocyclic if it satisfies the cocyclicity condition: for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\tau_{n}(\delta,\sigma))^{n+1}=\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes n}},

where
\tau_{\bullet}(\delta,\sigma)=\{\tau_{n}(\delta,\sigma)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the paracocyclic operator of
\textbf{CM}_{\bullet}(H,\delta,\sigma)
.
As already noticed in
[
6
]
(corresponding to the case
\mathcal{B}=\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}
), verifying the cocyclicity condition (if true) is a rather technical task.
Khalkhali and Pourkia proved
[
8
, Theorem 7.3]
that if
(\delta,\sigma)
is a so called braided modular pair in involution, then
(\tau_{2}(\delta,\sigma))^{3}
is equal to the square of the braiding of
H
with itself.
In particular, if
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric, then
(\tau_{2}(\delta,\sigma))^{3}=\mathrm{id}_{H^{\otimes 2}}
.
They also state a similar claim about
(\tau_{n}(\delta,\sigma))^{n+1}
, which implies the cocyclicity condition when
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric, see
[
8
, Remark 7.4]
.

Our first main result is a complete computation (by means of the Penrose graphical calculus) of the powers (up to
n+1
) of the paracocyclic operator
\tau_{n}(\delta,\sigma)
associated with a modular pair
(\delta,\sigma)
for
H
, see Theorem
2
.
Next, assume that
\mathcal{B}
has a twist
\theta
. We introduce the notion of a
\theta
-twisted modular pair in involution for
H
(see Section
4.1
) and prove (see Corollary
4
) that if
(\delta,\sigma)
is such a pair, then the associated paracocyclic operator satisfies the following twisted cocyclicity condition: for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\tau_{n}(\delta,\sigma))^{n+1}=\theta_{H^{\otimes n}}.

When
\mathcal{B}
is further
\Bbbk
-linear, we derive (co)cyclic
\Bbbk
-modules from a
\theta
-twisted modular pair
(\delta,\sigma)
by composing
\textbf{CM}_{\bullet}(H,\delta,\sigma)
with the functors
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},-)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(-,\mathbb{1})
, see Section
4.3
.
Note that if
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric, then a braided modular pair in involution in the sense of
[
8
]
is a
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{B}}
-twisted modular pair in involution, where
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{B}}
is the trivial twist of
\mathcal{B}
, and so the associated paracocyclic operator satisfies the cocyclicity condition.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra in braided category
\mathcal{B}
with a twist
\theta
. Our second main result is the construction of traces à la Connes-Moscovici.
More precisely, let
C
be a
H
-module coalgebra, that is, a coalgebra in the category of right
H
-modules in
\mathcal{B}
.
Inspired by a construction of Akrami and Majid
[
1
]
, we associate to
C
a paracocyclic object
\textbf{C}_{\bullet}(C)
in
\mathcal{B}
.
We introduce the notion of a
\delta
-
invariant

\sigma
-
trace
for
C
and derive from each such trace a natural transformation from
\textbf{CM}_{\bullet}(H,\delta,\sigma)
to
\textbf{C}_{\bullet}(C)
, see Theorem
7
. This generalizes the standard Connes-Moscovici trace.
We provide examples of traces in the case where
\mathcal{B}
is a ribbon category and
H
is its coend (see Section
5.3
).

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we review braided monoidal categories, Hopf algebras, and graphical calculus. Section
3
is devoted to preliminaries on simplicial, paracyclic, and cyclic objects in a category.
In Sections
4
and
5
, we state our main results and their corollaries. Sections
6
and
7
are devoted to the proofs of Theorems
2
and
7
.
In
8.
Appendix
, we provide an alternative proof (by using the Penrose graphical calculus) of the fact that the object
\textbf{CM}_{\bullet}(H,\delta,\sigma)
defined in
[
8
]
is paracocyclic.

Throughout the paper,
\Bbbk
denotes any commutative ring.
The class of objects of a category
\mathcal{B}
is denoted by
\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})
.

2.
Preliminaries on monoidal categories and braided Hopf algebras

In this section, we recall some algebraic preliminaries used in the paper. We first recall some facts about braided monoidal categories and the Penrose graphical calculus. Next, we recall definitions of categorical Hopf algebras and related concepts. We finish with a recall on pivotal categories and coends. For a more comprehensive introduction, see
[
12
]
.

2.1.
Conventions

In what follows, we suppress in our formulas the associativity and unitality constraints of the monoidal category.
This does not lead to any ambiguity since Mac Lane’s coherence theorem (see
[
9
]
) implies that all possible ways of inserting these constraints give the same results. We will denote by
\otimes
and
\mathbb{1}
the monoidal product and unit object of a monoidal category. For any objects
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of a monoidal category with
n\geq 2
, we set

X_{1}\otimes X_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n}=(\cdots((X_{1}\otimes X_{2})\otimes X_{3})\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{n-1})\otimes X_{n}

and similarly for morphisms.
A monoidal category is
\Bbbk
-linear if its Hom sets have a structure of a
\Bbbk
-module such that the composition and monoidal product of morphisms are
\Bbbk
-bilinear.

2.2.
Braided categories

A
braiding
of a monoidal category
(\mathcal{B},\otimes,\mathbb{1})
is a family
\tau=\{\tau_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X\}_{X,Y\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})}
of natural isomorphisms such that

(1)

\displaystyle\tau_{X,Y\otimes Z}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{Y}\otimes\tau_{X,Z})(\tau_{X,Y}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z})\text{ and }

(2)

\displaystyle\tau_{X\otimes Y,Z}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{X,Z}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Y})(\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\tau_{Y,Z})

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})
.
A
braided category
is a monoidal category endowed with a braiding.

A braiding
\tau
of
\mathcal{B}
is
symmetric
if for all
X,Y\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})
,

\tau_{Y,X}\tau_{X,Y}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y}.

A
symmetric category
is a category endowed with a symmetric braiding.

2.3.
Twists for braided categories

A
twist
for a braided monoidal category
\mathcal{B}
is a natural isomorphism
\theta=\{\theta_{X}\colon X\to X\}_{X\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})}
such that

(3)

\theta_{X\otimes Y}=(\theta_{X}\otimes\theta_{Y})\tau_{Y,X}\tau_{X,Y}

holds for all
X,Y\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})
.
Note that this implies that
\theta_{\mathbb{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{1}}
. For example, any ribbon category (see Section
2.16
) has a canonical twist.

Note that when
\mathcal{B}
is symmetric, a twist for
\mathcal{B}
is nothing but a monoidal natural isomorphism of the identity functor
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
. In particular,
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{B}}=\{\mathrm{id}_{X}\colon X\to X\}_{X\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{B})}
is a twist for
\mathcal{B}
.

By a
braided category with a twist
, we mean a braided category endowed with a twist.

2.5.
Categorical algebras

An
algebra
in a monoidal category
\mathcal{B}
is a triple
(A,m,u)
, where
A
is an object of
\mathcal{B}
,
m\colon A\otimes A\to A
and

and

2.6.
Categorical coalgebras

A
coalgebra
in a monoidal category
\mathcal{B}
is given by a triple
(C,\Delta,\varepsilon)
, where
C
is an object of
\mathcal{B}
,
\Delta\colon C\to C\otimes C
and
\varepsilon\colon C\to\mathbb{1}
are morphisms in
\mathcal{B}
, called
comultiplication
and
counit
respectively, which satisfy the coassociativity and counitality axioms:

(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\Delta=(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\Delta)\Delta\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathrm{id}_{C}\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta=\mathrm{id}_{C}=(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{C})\Delta.

A

between coalgebras

and

2.10.
Categorical modules

Let

2.15.
Pivotal categories

A
pivotal
category is a monoidal category
\mathcal{B}
such that each object
X
of

\mathcal{B}
has a
dual
object
X^{*}
and four morphisms

\displaystyle\text{ev}_{X}\colon X^{*}\otimes X\to\mathbb{1},\quad\text{coev}_{X}\colon\mathbb{1}\to X\otimes X^{*},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\text{ev}}_{X}\colon X\otimes X^{*}\to\mathbb{1},\quad\widetilde{\text{coev}}_{X}\colon\mathbb{1}\to X^{*}\otimes X,

satisfying some conditions. Briefly, these say that the associated left/right
dual functors coincide as monoidal functors (see
[
12
, Chapter 1]
for more details). The latter implies that the dual morphism
f^{*}\colon Y^{*}\to X^{*}
of a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathcal{B}
is computed by

\displaystyle f^{*}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{id}_{X^{*}}\otimes\widetilde{\text{ev}}_{Y})(\mathrm{id}_{X^{*}}\otimes f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Y^{*}})(\widetilde{\text{coev}}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Y^{*}})=

\displaystyle=(\text{ev}_{Y}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{{X^{*}}})(\mathrm{id}_{{Y^{*}}}\otimes f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{{X^{*}}})(\mathrm{id}_{{Y^{*}}}\otimes\text{coev}_{X}).

We extend the graphical calculus for monoidal categories (see Section
2.4
) to pivotal categories by orienting arcs. If an arc colored by
X
is oriented upwards, the represented object in source/target of corresponding morphism is
X^{*}
.
For example,
\mathrm{id}_{X},\mathrm{id}_{X^{*}}
, and a morphism

are depicted by

\mathrm{id}_{X}=\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{idX.eps}}}\;,\quad\mathrm{id}_{X^{*}}=\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{idXD.eps}}}\;=\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{idX.eps}}}\;,\quad\text{and}\quad f=\,\psfrag\psfrag\psfrag\psfrag\psfrag\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{exmorcgCM.eps}}}\;.

The morphisms
\text{ev}_{X},\widetilde{\text{ev}}_{X},\text{coev}_{X}
, and
\widetilde{\text{coev}}_{X}
are respectively depicted by

\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{evlcg.eps}}}\;,\quad\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{evrcg.eps}}}\;,\quad\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{coevlcg.eps}}}\;,\quad\text{and}\quad\,\psfrag\raisebox{-0.45pt}{\scalebox{0.75}{\includegraphics{coevrcg.eps}}}\;.

2.17.
Coends

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be any categories and
F\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
a functor.
A
dinatural transformation
between
F
and an object
D
in
\mathcal{D}
is a function
d
that assigns to any object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
a morphism
d_{X}\colon F(X,X)\to D
such that for all morphisms

in
\mathcal{C}
the following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{F(Y,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F(\mathrm{id}_{Y},f)}
\scriptstyle{F(f,\mathrm{id}_{X})}
\textstyle{F(X,X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d_{X}}
\textstyle{F(Y,Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d_{Y}}
\textstyle{D.}

A
coend
of a functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is a pair
(C,i)
where
C
is an object of
\mathcal{D}
and
i
is a dinatural transformation from
F
to
C
, which is universal among all dinatural transformations.
More precisely, for any dinatural transformation
d
from
F
to
D
, there exists a unique morphism
\varphi\colon C\to D
in
\mathcal{D}
such that

for all
X\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
.
A coend
(C,i)
of a functor
F
, if it exists, is unique up to a unique isomorphism commuting with the dinatural transformation.

3.
Simplicial, paracyclic, and cyclic objects

In this section we recall the notions of (co)simplicial, para(co)cyclic, and (co)cyclic objects in a category.

3.1.
The simplicial category

The
simplicial category

\Delta
is defined as follows.
The objects of
\Delta
are the nonnegative integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
A morphism
n\to m
in
\Delta
is an increasing map between sets
[n]=\{0,\dots,n\}
and
[m]=\{0,\dots,m\}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq i\leq n
, the
i
-th
coface

\delta_{i}^{n}\colon n-1\to n
is the unique increasing injection from
[n-1]
into
[n]
which misses
i\in[n]
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j\leq n
, the
j
-th
codegeneracy

\sigma_{j}^{n}\colon n+1\to n
is the unique increasing surjection from
[n+1]
onto
[n]
which sends both
j
and
j+1
to
j
.

It is well known (see
[
10
, Lemma 5.1]
) that morphisms in
\Delta
are generated by cofaces
\{\delta_{i}^{n}\}_{0\leq i\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
and codegeneracies
\{\sigma_{j}^{n}\}_{0\leq j\leq n,n\in\mathbb{N}}
subject to the simplicial relations (
SR
):

(SR)

\ \begin{aligned} \delta_{j}\delta_{i}&=\delta_{i}\delta_{j-1}\text{\quad for }i<j,\\
\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}&=\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j+1}\text{\quad for }i\leq j,\\
\sigma_{j}\delta_{i}&=\begin{cases}\delta_{i}\sigma_{j-1}&\text{for }i<j,\\
\mathrm{id}_{n}&\text{for }i=j,~~i=j+1,\\
\delta_{i-1}\sigma_{j}&\text{for }i>j+1.\end{cases}\end{aligned}

3.2.
The paracyclic category

The
paracyclic category

\Delta C_{\infty}
is defined as follows.
The objects of
\Delta C_{\infty}
are the nonnegative integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The morphisms are generated by morphisms
\{\delta_{i}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},0\leq i\leq n}
, called
cofaces
, morphisms
\{\sigma_{j}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},0\leq j\leq n}
, called
codegeneracies
, and isomorphisms
\{\tau_{n}\colon n\to n\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, called
paracocyclic operators
, satisfying the simplicial relations (
SR
) and the following
paracyclic compatibility relations
(
PCR
):

(PCR)

\ \begin{aligned} \tau_{n}\delta_{i}&=\delta_{i-1}\tau_{n-1}\text{\quad for }1\leq i\leq n,\\
\tau_{n}\delta_{0}&=\delta_{n},\\
\tau_{n}\sigma_{i}&=\sigma_{i-1}\tau_{n+1}\text{\quad for }1\leq i\leq n,\text{ and }\\
\tau_{n}\sigma_{0}&=\sigma_{n}\tau_{n+1}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Note that
\Delta
is a subcategory of
\Delta C_{\infty}
.

3.3.
The cyclic category

The
cyclic category

\Delta C
is defined as follows.
The objects of
\Delta C
are the nonnegative integers
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The morphisms are generated by morphisms
\{\delta_{i}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},0\leq i\leq n}
, called
cofaces
, morphisms
\{\sigma_{j}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},0\leq j\leq n}
, called
codegeneracies
, and isomorphisms
\{\tau_{n}\colon n\to n\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, called
cocyclic operators
, which satisfy the relations (
SR
), (
PCR
), and the
cyclicity condition
(
CC
):

(CC)

\tau_{n}^{n+1}=\mathrm{id}_{n}.

Note that
\Delta C
is a quotient of
\Delta C_{\infty}
.

3.4.
(Co)simplicial, para(co)cyclic, and (co)cyclic objects in a category

Let
\mathcal{C}
be any category.
A
simplicial object
in
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
X\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
, a
paracyclic
object in
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
\Delta C_{\infty}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
, and a
cyclic object
in
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
\Delta C^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}.

Dually, a
cosimplicial object
in
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
\Delta\to\mathcal{C}
, a
paracocyclic
object in
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
\Delta C_{\infty}\to\mathcal{C}
, and a
cocyclic object
in
\mathcal{C}
is a functor
\Delta C\to\mathcal{C}.
A (co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic object in the category of sets (respectively, of
\Bbbk
-modules) are called
(co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic sets
(respectively,
\Bbbk
-modules
).

A
morphism
between two (co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic objects is a natural transformation between them.
One often denotes the image of a morphism
f
under a (co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic by the same letter
f
.

Since the categories
\Delta,\Delta C_{\infty},\Delta C
are defined by generators and relations, a (co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic object in a category is entirely determined by the images of the generators satisfying the corresponding relations.
For example, a paracocyclic object
Y
in
\mathcal{C}
may be seen as a family
Y_{\bullet}=\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of objects of
\mathcal{C}
equipped with morphisms
\{\delta_{i}^{n}\colon Y_{n-1}\to Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},0\leq i\leq n}
, called
cofaces
, morphisms
\{\sigma_{j}^{n}\colon Y_{n+1}\to Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N},0\leq j\leq n}
, called
codegeneracies
, and isomorphisms
\{\tau_{n}\colon Y_{n}\to Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, called
paracocyclic operators
, subject to the relations (
SR
) and (
PCR
).
Similarly, a morphism
\beta\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
between two paracocyclic objects
Y_{\bullet}
and
Y^{\prime}_{\bullet}
in
\mathcal{C}
is explicited as a family
\{\beta_{n}\colon Y_{n}\to Y^{\prime}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

\displaystyle\beta_{n}\delta_{i}^{n}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i}^{n}\beta_{n-1}\quad\text{for any }n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\text{ and }0\leq i\leq n,

\displaystyle\beta_{n}\sigma_{j}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}^{n}\beta_{n+1}\quad\text{for any }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }0\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle\beta_{n}\tau_{n}

\displaystyle=\tau_{n}\beta_{n}\quad\text{for any }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Clearly, the composition of a (co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
with a functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is a (co)simplicial/para(co)cyclic/(co)cyclic object
FX
in
\mathcal{D}
. In particular, useful examples are provided by the covariant and the contravariant
\mathrm{Hom}
-functors
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(I,-)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,I)
, where
I
is an object of
\mathcal{C}
. In this case, we denote:

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(I,X)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(I,-)\circ X\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,I)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(-,I)\circ X.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a
\Bbbk
-linear braided category with a twist
\theta
. If
Y_{\bullet}=\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a paracocyclic object in
\mathcal{B}
such that its paracocyclic operator satisfies
\tau_{n}^{n+1}=\theta_{Y_{n}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(a)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{\bullet})=\{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a cocyclic
\Bbbk
-module,

(b)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(Y_{\bullet},\mathbb{1})=\{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(Y_{n},\mathbb{1})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a cyclic
\Bbbk
-module.

Proof.

Let us prove
(a)
. By composition with
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},-)\colon\mathcal{B}\to\textbf{Mod}_{\Bbbk}
, we obtain that
\{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a paracocyclic
\Bbbk
-module. Let us now verify that (
CC
) holds.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The morphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},-)(\theta_{Y_{n}})\colon\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{n})\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{n})
is the
\Bbbk
-linear morphism given by
f\mapsto\theta_{Y_{n}}f
.
The naturality of
\theta
and the fact that
\theta_{\mathbb{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{1}}
imply that
\theta_{Y_{n}}f=f\theta_{\mathbb{1}}=f
for all
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{n})
.
Then, using the functoriality of
Y_{\bullet}
and the hypothesis that
Y_{\bullet}(\tau_{n}^{n+1})=\theta_{Y_{n}}
, we have

\left(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{\bullet})(\tau_{n})\right)^{n+1}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},-)\left(Y_{\bullet}(\tau_{n}^{n+1})\right)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},-)(\theta_{Y_{n}})=\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{1},Y_{n})}.

Part
(b)
is proved similarly.
∎

3.5.
Cyclic (co)homology

To any cyclic
\Bbbk
-module
X\colon\Delta C^{\mathrm{op}}\to\textbf{Mod}_{\Bbbk}
, one can associate a bicomplex
\textbf{CC}(X)
(see
[
13
]
).
The
n
-th
cyclic homology

HC_{n}(X)
of
X
is defined as the
n
-th homology of the total chain complex associated to the chain bicomplex
\textbf{CC}(X)
.
A morphism between cyclic
\Bbbk
-modules induces a levelwise morphism in cyclic homology.

Similarly, to any cocyclic
\Bbbk
-module
Y\colon\Delta C\to\textbf{Mod}_{\Bbbk}
, one can associate a cochain bicomplex
\textbf{CC}(Y)
, obtained by a construction dual to the one of a chain bicomplex.
The
n
-th
cyclic cohomology

HC^{n}(Y)
of
Y
is defined as the
n
-th cohomology of the total cochain complex associated to the cochain bicomplex
\textbf{CC}(Y)
.
A morphism between cocyclic
\Bbbk
-modules induces a levelwise morphism in cyclic cohomology.

Abstract
Analyses of B B meson decays to charmless hadronic final states are an important part of the Belle II program. They are sensitive to effects from non-standard model physics and provide experimentally precise constraints on the weak interactions of quarks. We present recent Belle II results on branching fractions and direct C ​ P CP -violating asymmetries of the decays B 0 → K + ​ π − B^{0}\rightarrow K^{+}\pi^{-} , B + → K + ​ π 0 B^{+}\rightarrow K^{+}\pi^{0} , B + → K 0 ​ π + B^{+}\rightarrow K^{0}\pi^{+} , and B 0 → K 0 ​ π 0 B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}\pi^{0} , and use these to test the standard model through an isospin-based sum rule. In addition, we measure the branching fraction and direct C ​ P CP asymmetry of the decay B + → π + ​ π 0 B^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{0} and the branching fraction of the decay B 0 → π + ​ π − B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-} , which contribute towards the determination of the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa angle ϕ 2 \phi_{2} . The data are collected with the Belle II detector from the SuperKEKB asymmetric-energy e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider, consisting of 387 × 10 6 387\times 10^{6} Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) → B ​ B ¯ \Upsilon(4S)\rightarrow B\bar{B} events. We obtain − 0.03 ± 0.13 ± 0.04 -0.03\pm 0.13\pm 0.04 for the sum rule, in agreement with the standard model expectation of zero and with a precision comparable to the best existing determinations.

Motivation

1.1
B\rightarrow K\pi
Decays: Isospin Sum Rule

For
B\rightarrow K\pi
decays, one may construct a sum rule exploiting isospin symmetry using linear combinations of the branching fractions and
CP
asymmetries
[
1
]
. This reduces the impact of theoretical uncertainties.
The sum rule parameter

I_{K\pi}=\mathcal{A}_{K^{+}\pi^{-}}+\mathcal{A}_{K^{0}\pi^{+}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{B}_{K^{0}\pi^{+}}}{\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}\pi^{-}}}\frac{\tau_{B^{0}}}{\tau_{B^{+}}}-2\mathcal{A}_{K^{+}\pi^{0}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}\pi^{0}}}{\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}\pi^{-}}}\frac{\tau_{B^{0}}}{\tau_{B^{+}}}-2\mathcal{A}_{K^{0}\pi^{0}}\cdot\frac{\mathcal{B}_{K^{0}\pi^{0}}}{\mathcal{B}_{K^{+}\pi^{-}}}

(1)

is predicted to be zero in the Standard Model (SM) within
1\%

[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, therefore providing a null test for the SM.

\mathcal{A}_{K\pi}
and
\mathcal{B}_{K\pi}
(neutral or charged
K
and
\pi
) are the direct
CP
asymmetry and the
CP
-averaged branching fraction of a
B\rightarrow K\pi
decay, and
\tau_{B^{0}}
and
\tau_{B^{+}}
are the neutral and charged
B
meson lifetimes, respectively. Charge conjugation is implied unless otherwise indicated.
The time-integrated
CP
asymmetry parameter is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{X}=\frac{\Gamma(\bar{B}\rightarrow\bar{X})-\Gamma(B\rightarrow X)}{\Gamma(\bar{B}\rightarrow\bar{X})+\Gamma(B\rightarrow X)},

(2)

where
\Gamma
is the partial decay width to the final state
X
.

\mathcal{A}_{X}
corresponds to the direct
CP
asymmetry of charged
B
mesons and, in the absence of
CP
violation in flavor oscillations, also neutral
B
mesons. This is a good approximation in the case of
B^{0}
-
\bar{B}^{0}
mixing.

The current world average of the sum rule parameter is
I_{K\pi}=-0.13\pm 0.11

[
5
]
, and its precision is limited by the statistical uncertainty of
\mathcal{A}_{K^{0}\pi^{0}}

[
6

2
Analysis Strategy

The analyses of all the decays follow a similar strategy. A common selection is applied to all relevant final state particles. Multivariate algorithms are developed for each mode to suppress the continuum background, which originates from
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\bar{q}
processes, where
q
indicates a
u
,
d
,
s
, or
c
quark.
Event shape variables, results of the
B
meson decay vertex fit, flavor-tagger outputs (except for
B^{0}\rightarrow K_{S}^{0}\pi^{0}
decay), and the beam-constrained mass
M_{bc}=\sqrt{(E_{beam}^{*}/c^{2})^{2}-(p_{B}^{*}/c)^{2}}
are used to train a boosted decision tree (BDT).
E_{beam}^{*}
and
p_{B}^{*}
are the beam energy and
B
meson momentum, respectively, in the center-of-mass frame. A loose requirement is applied to the BDT output, removing
90\%-99\%
of the continuum background.
In order to improve the
M_{bc}
resolution for decays containing a neutral
\pi^{0}
, the measured
\pi^{0}
momentum in the calculation of
M_{bc}
is replaced by

\boldsymbol{p}_{\pi^{0}}^{*^{\prime}}=\sqrt{(E_{beam}^{*}-E_{h}^{*})^{2}/c^{2}-m_{\pi^{0}}^{2}c^{2}}\times\frac{\boldsymbol{p}_{\pi^{0}}^{*}}{|\boldsymbol{p}_{\pi^{0}}^{*}|},

(3)

where
E_{h}^{*}
is energy of the other hadron in the
B
candidate decay,
\boldsymbol{p}_{\pi^{0}}^{*}
is the measured
\pi^{0}
momentum, and
m_{\pi^{0}}
is the known
\pi^{0}
mass.
This substitution improves the resolution of
M_{bc}
and reduces its correlation to
\Delta E
.

The signal yields are measured with unbinned maximum likelihood fits to the energy difference
\Delta E=E_{B}^{*}-E_{beam}^{*}
and the transformed BDT output
C^{\prime}
.

E_{B}^{*}
is the energy of the
B
meson in the center-of-mass frame, and the BDT output is transformed using probability integral transformation
[
12
]
.
The two-dimensional probability density functions (PDF) of
\Delta E
and
C^{\prime}
is assumed to be a product of two independent one-dimensional PDFs, as the correlation between the two variables is small.
The
CP
asymmetries are determined using the charge of the final state particles for flavor-specific channels.
For the
B^{0}\rightarrow K_{S}^{0}\pi^{0}
channel the
B
mesons decay to the same
CP
eigenstate, and a category-based flavor tagger is used to determine the flavor of the
B^{0}
, calibrated using
B\rightarrow D^{(*)}h^{+}
decays
[
13
]
.

The analysis procedures are first studied with simulated events before being applied to the data sample.
Several abundant control channels with similar features to the signal channels are used to correct for discrepancies between data and simulation, and to estimate systematic uncertainties.

An element-wise RSAV algorithm for unconstrained optimization problems Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Shiheng Zhang, Jiahao Zhang, Jie Shen, Guang Lin

Abstract
We present a novel optimization algorithm, element-wise relaxed scalar auxiliary variable (E-RSAV), that satisfies an unconditional energy dissipation law and exhibits improved alignment between the modified and the original energy. Our algorithm features rigorous proofs of linear convergence in the convex setting. Furthermore, we present a simple accelerated algorithm that improves the linear convergence rate to super-linear in the univariate case. We also propose an adaptive version of E-RSAV with Steffensen step size. We validate the robustness and fast convergence of our algorithm through ample numerical experiments.

Introduction

Optimization of neural network parameters is an area of active research with significant progress in recent years. However, it continues to pose formidable challenges, mainly due to vanishing gradients
[
9
]
, overfitting
[
13
]
, and the necessity for adaptive learning rate methods to avoid convergence to local minima
[
12
,
28
]
. Several approaches, such as batch normalization
[
10
]
and adaptive gradient descent with energy (AEGD)
[
14
]
, a relaxed scalar auxiliary variable (RSAV)
[
15
,
29
]
, have demonstrated promise in addressing some of these obstacles. The most commonly used approach for obtaining the update rule involves reducing a non-convex loss function, for instance, the mean square error,
f(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(y_{i}-\hat{y_{i}}(\bm{x}))^{2}

[
16
]
.

In the realm of mathematical optimization, it is customary to investigate the feasibility of unconstrained minimization problems that take the form:

\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(\bm{x})

(1)

In this setting, we assume the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is differentiable. Notably, this formulation represents a fundamental optimization problem that encompasses linear programming and least-square problems as particular cases. Furthermore, it has a complete analytical theory, as established in
[
4
]
.

In the 1980s, a connection between the unconstrained minimization problem (
1
), in whichthe target function
f(\bm{x})
is to be minimized over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and an ordinary differential equation (ODE) problem was established
[
5
,
18
,
33
]
. Specifically, a gradient descent method for the problem (
1
),
\bm{x}^{n+1}=\bm{x}^{n}-\Delta t\nabla f(\bm{x}(t))
, can be considered as a numerical scheme of a gradient flow

\frac{d\bm{x}}{dt}=-\nabla f(\bm{x}(t)),

(2)

where the initial point is
\bm{x}(0)=\bm{x}_{0}
. A minimizer
\bm{x}^{*}
of
f(\bm{x})
is then obtained as
\bm{x}^{*}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bm{x}(t)
, where
\bm{x}(t)
satisfies equation (
2
). Recently, there has been significant research examining the connection between minimization problems and ODE problems, including investigations into Nesterov’s accelerated gradient, NAG
[
26
]
. In the domain of machine learning, NAG has risen to prominence as a robust optimization tool, underscoring the need for effective numerical methods for solving such problems.

Additionally, the equation (
2
) belongs to a notable class of ODEs known as gradient flows, which are ubiquitous in various fields such as fluid dynamics and material science problems
[
1
,
2
,
6
]
. It is desirable, sometimes necessary, for the numerical scheme to adhere to fundamental physical laws, including the energy dissipation law
\frac{df\left(\bm{x}(t)\right)}{dt}\leq 0
. Certain contemporary literature has proposed several energy-dissipative numerical schemes, including the convex splitting schemes
[
7
,
8
,
19
]
, stabilization methods
[
23
,
32
]
,
[
27
,
30
]
, scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) methods
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, and invariant energy quadratization (IEQ) approaches.

The treatment of the minimization problem as a gradient flow problem has gained popularity in optimization algorithms due to its robustness and generality.
Recently, Liu & Tian
[
14
]
developed AEGD which applied the IEQ to the optimization process, and Liu, Shen & Zhang
[
15
]
applied the relaxed SAV technique to optimization. These methods ensure unconditional energy dissipation by introducing a kind of modified energy.
However, the introduced modified energy may exhibit inconsistencies with the original energy, as the original energy may not necessarily monotonically decrease during iterations. Despite its potential, several challenges remain in the application of gradient flow methods to minimization problems. One of the challenges faced in designing optimization algorithms based on gradient flow is to maintain the physical law while designing the numerical scheme. Another challenge is to improve the convergence rate by selecting an appropriate step size. Avoiding oscillations in gradient descent methods is also a challenge that needs to be addressed. Additionally, incorporating an adaptive algorithm can help save computation costs. To improve the performance of optimization algorithms based on gradient flow, further research is needed to address these challenges.

Within the context of the SAV approach, a new variable
r=\sqrt{f(\bm{x}(t))}
is defined as the scalar auxiliary variable, and subsequently, an extended system needs to be solved.

However, the numerical solution
r^{n+1}
may deviate significantly from
\sqrt{f(\bm{x}(t_{n+1}))}
.
Drawing inspiration from the enhancement in IEQ and SAV presented in
[
11
]
, the consistency between the modified and the original energy can be achieved by incorporating a relaxation step at the conclusion of each iteration. Based on this, we propose a new approach, element-wise RSAV (E-RSAV), in which the auxiliary variable is element-wise, allowing more flexibility in adjusting the learning rate element-wise. Importantly, the introduced modified energy remains dissipative unconditionally, where the modified and original energy are inherently connected.

More precisely, the unconditionally modified energy dissipation can be obtained for each element of the vector
\bm{x}
, which facilitates the use of adaptive step size during iteration. To achieve this, we define an indicator,
\alpha=\text{mean}(\frac{\bm{r}^{n}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}})
such that we can adjust the step size
\Delta t
according to the indicator’s deviation from 1 and with the Steffensen step size
[
31
]
, leading to an adaptive version of the E-RSAV, which, hereafter referred as AE-RSAV, may avoid oscillation and accelerate the convergence. We will show that by selecting the appropriate step size, the real energy will also be dissipative, which allows us to prove that it converges linearly. We also show that the convergence rate can be accelerated to superlinear in the univariate case.

In conclusion, our primary advancements include:

1.

We propose a novel optimizer, E-RSAV, designed to significantly improve the performance of RSAV in large-dimensional problems, particularly in the context of machine learning.

2.

We provide a rigorous proof of the convergence rate for the E-RSAV algorithm in the convex setting.

3.

We demonstrate that in the univariate case, the linear convergence rate can be elevated to a super-linear rate.

4.

We introduce an indicator to monitor the performance of the optimization process and propose the AE-RSAV algorithm, which incorporates the indicator and provides guidelines on how to modify the step size when the indicator exceeds a certain threshold.

5.

Through numerical experiments, we demonstrate that our algorithm achieves high accuracy and fast convergence.

The structure of this article is outlined as below: Section 2 introduces the proposed E-RSAV algorithm. Section 3 presents the convergence analysis of RSAV and E-RSAV in the convex setting. We propose in Section 4 an enhanced E-RSAV algorithm and show that it has a superlinear convergence rate. In Section 5, several numerical experiments are presented to validate the effectiveness of the new algorithm, followed by some conclusions in Section 6.

2
The RSAV and element-wise RSAV algorithms

We start by recalling the SAV and RSAV schemes introduced in
[
15
]
for optimization problems, followed by the construction of element-wise SAV and RSAV schemes. We also show that these schemes are unconditionally stable with the modified energy.

2.1
SAV and relaxed SAV

Assuming, without loss of generality,
f(\bm{x})\geq\delta>0
for all
\bm{x}
, we can define an auxiliary scalar variable as
r(t)=\sqrt{f(\bm{x}(t))}
, subsequently extend the gradient flow (
2
) to:

\displaystyle\frac{d\bm{x}}{dt}+\mathcal{L}(t)\bm{x}+\frac{r}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}(t))}}(\nabla f(\bm{x}(t))-\mathcal{L}(t)\bm{x})=0

(3)

\displaystyle r_{t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}(t))}}\nabla f(\bm{x}(t))^{T}\frac{d\bm{x}}{dt}.

(4)

If we consider
r(0)=\sqrt{f(\bm{x}|_{t=0})}
, then the solution
\bm{x}
of (
2
) along with
r(t)=\sqrt{f(\bm{x})}
also represents a solution pair for the above-expanded system.

In general, we can split the cost function as follows:

f(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}(t)\bm{x},\bm{x})+[f(\bm{x})-\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}(t)\bm{x},\bm{x})],

(5)

where
\mathcal{L}(t)
is a self-adjoint positive semi-definite linear operator. In this paper, we mostly consider the trivial splitting
\mathcal{L}(t)\equiv 0
or
(\mathcal{L}(t)\bm{x})_{i}=\lambda_{i}(t)x_{i},\,i=1,2,\cdots,m
where
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,x_{m})
.
Then, we
consider the following time discretization scheme for the expanded system:

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n})+\frac{{r}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})^{T}\frac{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}{\Delta t},

(7)

where we assume
\mathcal{L}^{n}\approx\mathcal{L}(t_{n})
to be self-adjoint and positive semi-definite.
In the following context, we will refer to
f(\bm{x}^{n+1})
as the “original energy” and
(r^{n+1})^{2}
as the “modified energy”.

The above SAV scheme is very efficient, since the coupled system (
6
)-(
7
) can be decoupled into two linear systems of the subsequent structure
[
21
]
:

\alpha\bm{x}+\mathcal{L}^{n}\bm{x}=\bm{h}.

(8)

The scheme (
6
)-(
7
) is unconditional energy dissipative for the modified energy
({r}^{n+1})^{2}
. However, the equation used to compute
{r}^{n+1}
has little correlation with
\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}
, leading to inconsistencies between
\bm{r}^{n+1}
and
\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}
in numerical experiments.
To address this issue, we adopt a relaxation step
[
11
]
that strengthens the relationship between
{r}^{n+1}
and
\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}
. More precisely, the RSAV scheme is as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n})+\frac{\tilde{r}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})^{T}\frac{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}{\Delta t},

(10)

\displaystyle r^{n+1}=\eta_{0}\tilde{r}^{n+1}+(1-\eta_{0})\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})},

(11)

where, for a given
\psi\in(0,1)
,
\eta_{0}
is the smallest number in
[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle(r^{n+1})^{2}-(\tilde{r}^{n+1})^{2}\leq\frac{\psi}{\Delta t}\|x^{n+1}-x^{n}\|^{2},

(12)

where
\psi
is a parameter of our choice, and is usually set as
\psi=0.95
in practice. We refer to
[
11
]
(see (
20
) below) for an explicit formula to determine
\eta_{0}
.

2.2
Element-wise SAV and element-wise RSAV schemes

The scheme (
6
)-(
7
), in the case of
\mathcal{L}^{n}=0
, can be interpreted as a GD scheme with a single learning rate
\Delta t\frac{{r}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}
. However, it may converge slowly if the components of
\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})
have large variations. In this case, it is preferable to have element-wise learning rates. To this end, we modify the SAV scheme (
6
)-(
7
) into the following element-wise SAV scheme:

\displaystyle\frac{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t}+(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}))_{i}+\frac{{{r}}_{i}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x}^{n})}{\partial x_{i}}=0,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,m,

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{{{r}}_{i}^{n+1}-r_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x}^{n})}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,m.

(14)

Note that with
\mathcal{L}^{n}=0
, the above scheme is essentially the same as the AEGD algorithm in
[
14
]
.

Let
\bm{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},\cdots,r_{i},\cdots,r_{m})
be denoted, with
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\|\cdot\|
representing the inner product and norm, respectively, in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Theorem 1
.

The E-SAV algorithm (
13
)-(
14
) is unconditionally energy dissipative, characterized by the fact that

\displaystyle\|\bm{r}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\bm{r}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\|\bm{r}^{n+1}-\bm{r}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}),\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n})-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\|\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}\|^{2}\leq 0.

In particular, if
(\mathcal{L}^{n}\bm{x})_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{n}x_{i}
with
\lambda_{i}^{n}\geq 0
for all
i,n
, we have the element-wise inequality

(r_{i}^{n+1})^{2}-(r_{i}^{n})^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{\Delta t}(x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n})^{2}\leq 0,\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m.

Proof.

Multiplying (
13
) (resp. (
14
)) with
{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}
(resp.
2r_{i}^{n+1}{\Delta t}
) and taking the sum of the results, we derive

\displaystyle({r}_{i}^{n+1})^{2}-({r}_{i}^{n})^{2}

\displaystyle+({r}_{i}^{n+1}-{r}_{i}^{n})^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle=-(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}))_{i}\cdot(x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n})-\frac{1}{\Delta t}(x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n})^{2}.

Summing up (
15
) for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
, we derive

\displaystyle\|\bm{r}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\bm{r}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\bm{r}^{n+1}-\bm{r}^{n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}),\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n})-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\|\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}\|^{2}.

Remark 2
.

For general
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, the components
(x_{1}^{n+1},\cdots,x_{m}^{n+1})
in (
13
)-(
14
) are coupled. However, if
(\mathcal{L}^{n}\bm{x})_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{n}x_{i}
, then
\{x_{i}^{n+1}\}
in (
13
)-(
14
) are decoupled from each other, and can be efficiently solved.

Accordingly, we can construct the element-wise RSAV scheme as follows: For
i=1,2,\cdots,m
,

\displaystyle\frac{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t}+(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}))_{i}+\frac{{\tilde{r}}_{i}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x}^{n})}{\partial x_{i}}=0,

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{{\tilde{r}}_{i}^{n+1}-r_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\frac{\partial f(\bm{x}^{n})}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}{\Delta t},

(17)

\displaystyle r_{i}^{n+1}=\eta_{i0}\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1}+(1-\eta_{i0})\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})},

(18)

where, for a given
\psi\in(0,1)
,
\eta_{i0}
is the smallest number in
[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle(r_{i}^{n+1})^{2}-(\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1})^{2}\leq\frac{\psi}{\Delta t}\left({x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}\right)^{2}.

(19)

Following
[
11
]
,
\eta_{i0}
can be determined as follows:

\begin{split}&\eta_{i0}=\min_{\eta_{i}\in[0,1]}\eta_{i},\text{ such that }\\
&(\eta_{i}\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1}+(1-\eta_{i})\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})})^{2}-(\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1})^{2}\leq\frac{\psi}{\Delta t}\left({x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}}\right)^{2},\end{split}

which can be reduced to

\eta_{i0}=\min_{\eta_{i}\in[0,1]}\eta_{i},\text{ such that }a\eta_{i}^{2}+b\eta_{i}+c\leq 0,

(20)

where

\displaystyle a=(\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}-\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1})^{2},

(21)

\displaystyle b=2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}(\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1}-\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}),

\displaystyle c=f(\bm{x}^{n+1})-(\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1})^{2}-\frac{\psi}{\Delta t}({x_{i}^{n+1}-x_{i}^{n}})^{2}.

If
a=0
, i.e.,
\tilde{r}_{i}^{n+1}=\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}
, we set
\eta_{i0}=0
. Otherwise, the solution to the problem (
20
) can be written as

\eta_{i0}=\max\left\{\frac{-b-\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a},0\right\}.

(22)

It is easy to check that
b^{2}-4ac\geq 0
for any
\Delta t
.

Assuming that
(\mathcal{L}^{n}\bm{x})_{i}=\lambda_{i}^{n}x_{i}
, the E-RSAV algorithm is given in

3
Convergence analysis of the RSAV and E-RSAV schemes

In this section, we assume
f(\bm{x})
to be
L
-smooth (see definition below) and carry out a convergence analysis for the RSAV and E-RSAV schemes.
We note that for the special case of
\mathcal{L}\equiv 0
, the rate at which both SAV and E-SAV schemes converge was established in
[
14
]
. We also note that in
[
15
]
, the SAV scheme was formulated as a line search method and some convergence criteria were derived.

Definition 4
.

A function
f
is
L
-smooth if there is a non-negative constant
L
with
\norm{\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)}\leq L\norm{x-y}
holding for all
x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
\nabla f
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous.

3.1
Positive lower bound of
r^{n}
for the RSAV scheme

The convergence theory of gradient descent emphasizes the importance of maintaining the learning rate above a certain positive constant. As evidenced in
[
14
]
, for the SAV scheme (
6
)-(
7
) when
\mathcal{L}\equiv 0
and
f
is
L
-smooth, the term
r^{n}
remains bounded above a positive constant. We show below that this is also true for the RSAV scheme (
9
)-(
11
).

Let us denote
g(\bm{x})=\sqrt{f(\bm{x})}
, and it can be readily demonstrated that
g(\bm{x})
is also bounded below with a positive constant
\sqrt{\delta}
. We can rewrite (
9
)-(
10
) as

\displaystyle\frac{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n})+2{\tilde{r}^{n+1}}\nabla g(\bm{x}^{n})=0,

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}}{\Delta t}=\nabla g(\bm{x}^{n})^{T}\frac{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}{\Delta t},

(27)

\displaystyle r^{n+1}=\eta_{0}\tilde{r}^{n+1}+(1-\eta_{0}){g(\bm{x}^{n+1})}.

(28)

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that
f
is L-smooth, and let
r^{n}
be generated by the RSAV scheme (
9
)-(
11
) with
\mathcal{L}
being positive semi-definite. Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bm{x}^{n}=\bm{x}^{*}
, and there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
such that for
\Delta t\leq C_{1}
, we then obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r^{n}=r^{*}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{2}>0,\;\text{ and }\nabla f(\bm{x}^{*})=0.

Proof.

First, it is easy to show that for a
L
-smooth function
f
, if
f
has a positive lower bound
\delta
, then
g
is
L_{g}
-smooth with
L_{g}=\frac{L}{2\delta}
.

Taking the inner product with
(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n})
of the equation (
26
) and combining it with equation (
27
), we obtain

\left(\tilde{r}^{n+1}\right)^{2}-\left(r^{n}\right)^{2}+\left(\tilde{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}\right)^{2}=-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\norm{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}^{2}-\left(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}),\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}\right).

Summing up the above with (
12
), since
\mathcal{L}^{n}
is positive semi-definite, we can obtain

\displaystyle\norm{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}^{2}\leq\frac{\Delta t}{1-\psi}\left(\left(r^{n}\right)^{2}-\left({r}^{n+1}\right)^{2}\right).

(29)

Hence,

for some

Taking the sum of the aforementioned for

Hence,

On the other hand, we derive from (

Hence we have

if

.
Furthermore, we observe from (

is actually a convex combination of
\tilde{r}^{n+1}
and

and it is also a decreasing sequence.

Without loss of generality, let’s consider a positive integer

such that the inequality

(32)

For any

\displaystyle g(\bm{x}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq g(\bm{x}^{n})+\nabla g(\bm{x}^{n})\left(\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}\right)+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\norm{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}^{2}

(33)

\displaystyle\leq g(\bm{x}^{n})+\tilde{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\norm{\bm{x}^{n+1}-\bm{x}^{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq g(\bm{x}^{n})+{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}+\frac{\Delta tL_{g}}{2\left(1-\psi\right)}\left(\left(r^{n}\right)^{2}-\left({r}^{n+1}\right)^{2}\right).

Summing up the above from
N
to

g(\bm{x}^{K+1})\leq g(\bm{x}^{N})+{r}^{K+1}-r^{N}+\frac{\Delta tL_{g}}{2\left(1-\psi\right)}\left(\left(r^{N}\right)^{2}-\left({r}^{K+1}\right)^{2}\right).

(34)

Let
K
go to
+\infty

g(\bm{x}^{*})\leq g(\bm{x}^{N})+{r}^{*}-r^{N}+\frac{\Delta tL_{g}}{2\left(1-\psi\right)}\left(\left(r^{N}\right)^{2}-\left({r}^{*}\right)^{2}\right),

(35)

from which we can derive

(36)

Next we bound the difference between

and

\displaystyle r^{N}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N}\right)=\eta_{0}\tilde{r}^{N}-\eta_{0}g\left(\bm{x}^{N}\right)=\eta_{0}\left(\tilde{r}^{N}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N}\right)\right).

(37)

By equation (

\displaystyle\tilde{r}^{N}-r^{N-1}

\displaystyle=\nabla g\left(\bm{x}^{N-1}\right)\cdot\left(\bm{x}^{N}-\bm{x}^{N-1}\right)

\displaystyle=g\left(\bm{x}^{N}\right)-g\left(\bm{x}^{N-1}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\bm{x}^{N}-\bm{x}^{N-1}\right)^{T}\nabla^{2}g(\xi_{N})\left(\bm{x}^{N}-\bm{x}^{N-1}\right).

\norm{a}^{2}_{H}=a^{T}Ha
, we find from the above that

\displaystyle\tilde{r}^{N}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N}\right)

\displaystyle=r^{N-1}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N-1}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\norm{\bm{x}^{N}-\bm{x}^{N-1}}^{2}_{\nabla^{2}g(\xi_{N})}

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}\left(\tilde{r}^{N-1}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N-1}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\norm{\bm{x}^{N}-\bm{x}^{N-1}}^{2}_{\nabla^{2}g(\xi_{N})}

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}\left(r^{N-2}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N-2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\norm{\bm{x}^{N-1}-\bm{x}^{N-2}}^{2}_{\nabla^{2}g(\xi_{N-1})}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\norm{\bm{x}^{N}-\bm{x}^{N-1}}^{2}_{\nabla^{2}g(\xi_{N})}

Noting that

for all

\displaystyle|\tilde{r}^{N}-g\left(\bm{x}^{N}\right)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{g}\Delta t}{2(1-\psi)}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\left(r^{k-1}\right)^{2}-\left({r}^{k}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{g}\Delta t}{2(1-\psi)}\left(\left(r^{0}\right)^{2}-\left({r}^{N}\right)^{2}\right).

Hence, we derive from the above and (

Let

(40)

Finally, let

in (
9

3.3
Dissipation of the original energy

We showed in Section 2 the modified energy of the E-RSAV approach remains dissipative. Next, we show that when
\mathcal{L}\equiv 0
, the original energy of E-RSAV is also dissipative when the step size
\Delta t
is sufficiently small.

Theorem 7
.

Assuming that
f
is L-smooth and has a positive lower bound
\delta
, then the solution
\bm{x}^{n+1}
of the E-RSAV scheme with
\mathcal{L}=0
satisfies the discrete dissipation law
f(\bm{x}^{n+1})\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})
with
\Delta t\leq\min(C_{2},\frac{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})})
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(\bm{x}^{n})=f^{*}
.

Proof.

Denoting
\xi_{i}^{n}=\frac{r_{i}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}}{\left(1+\Delta t\frac{({\partial_{i}f(\bm{x}^{n})})^{2}}{2{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}\right)^{-1}r_{i}^{n}}
and

\bm{\xi}^{n}=(\xi_{1}^{n},\xi_{2}^{n},\cdots,\xi_{m}^{n})^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle f(\bm{x}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=f(\bm{x}^{n}-\Delta t\bm{\xi}^{n}\odot\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n}))

(56)

\displaystyle=f(\bm{x}^{n})-\Delta t\left(\bm{\xi}^{n}\odot\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})\right)^{T}\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta t\bm{\xi}^{n}\odot\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})\right)^{T}\nabla^{2}f(z)\left(\Delta t\bm{\xi}^{n}\odot\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})\right).

Noticing that
\xi^{n}_{min}\norm{\nabla f(x^{n})}^{2}\leq\left(\bm{\xi}^{n}\odot\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})\right)^{T}\nabla f(x^{n})\xi^{n}_{max}\norm{\nabla f(x^{n})}^{2}
where

\xi^{n}_{min}=\min(\bm{\xi}^{n})
and
\xi^{n}_{max}=\max(\bm{\xi}^{n})
, we have

f(\bm{x}^{n+1})\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})-\Delta t\xi^{n}_{min}\norm{\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})}^{2}+\frac{L}{2}(\Delta t\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}\norm{\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})}^{2}.

(57)

If
\Delta t\leq\frac{2\xi^{n}_{min}}{L(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}}
, then
f(\bm{x}^{n+1})\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})
. Furthermore, utilizing the positive lower bound
r^{*}
in Theorem
6
and
r_{i}^{0}=\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{0})}
for
\forall i
, we can have

\frac{2\xi^{n}_{min}}{L(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}}=\frac{2(r_{i}^{n+1})_{min}}{L(r_{i}^{n+1})_{max}^{2}}\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}\geq\frac{2\delta}{L}\frac{(r_{i}^{n+1})_{min}}{(r_{i}^{n+1})_{max}^{2}}\geq\frac{2\delta}{L}\frac{(r_{i}^{n+1})_{min}}{(r_{i}^{0})_{max}^{2}}\geq\frac{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})}.

Therefore, if
\Delta t\leq\min(C_{2},\frac{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})})
, we can ensure
f(\bm{x}^{n+1})\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})
.

3.4
Convergence analysis of the E-RSAV

We first recall the following lemma:

Lemma 8
.

(cf.
[
3
]
)

f
satisfies the Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality if
there exists a
\mu>0
such that

\mu\left(f(x)-f^{\star}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\norm{\nabla f(x)}^{2}.

(58)

Theorem 9
.

Let a sequence
\{\bm{x}^{n}\}
be generated by the E-RSAV with
\mathcal{L}\equiv 0
. Suppose
f
satisfying the Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality and being bounded from below by positive constant
\delta
, then for any
\gamma<\frac{\delta^{2}}{{8L(f(\bm{x}^{0}))^{2}}}
, there exists
\Delta t_{1},\Delta t_{2}>0
such that if
\Delta t_{1}\leq\Delta t\leq\min(C_{2},\frac{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})},\Delta t_{2})
, we have

f(\bm{x}^{n+1})-f^{\star}\leq\left(1-2\mu\epsilon_{n}\right)\left(f(\bm{x}^{n})-f^{\star}\right),

(59)

where
\nu>0
is the constant in (
58
) and
\gamma\leq\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2L}
.

Proof.

Subtracting
f^{\star}
from both sides of inequality (
57
) in Theorem
7
and using Lemma
8
, we have

\displaystyle f(\bm{x}^{n+1})-f^{\star}

\displaystyle\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})-f^{\star}-(\Delta t\xi^{n}_{min}-\frac{L}{2}(\Delta t\xi^{n}_{max})^{2})\norm{\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})-f^{\star}-2\mu(\Delta t\xi^{n}_{min}-\frac{L}{2}(\Delta t\xi^{n}_{max})^{2})\left(f(\bm{x}^{n})-f^{\star}\right)

\displaystyle=:\left(1-2\mu\epsilon_{n}\right)\left(f(\bm{x}^{n})-f^{\star}\right).

where
\epsilon_{n}=\Delta t\xi^{n}_{min}-\frac{L}{2}(\Delta t\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}
. To achieve the first-order convergence rate, it is necessary that
\epsilon_{n}\in(0,1)
.

An upper bound on
\epsilon_{n}
can be obtained directly, as it is a quadratic function of
\Delta t
that achieves its maximum value at
\Delta t=\frac{\xi^{n}_{min}}{L(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}}
.

\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{\xi^{n}_{min}}{L(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}}\xi^{n}_{min}-\frac{L}{2}\left(\frac{\xi^{n}_{min}}{L(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}}\right)^{2}(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}=\frac{(\xi^{n}_{min})^{2}}{2L(\xi^{n}_{max})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2L}.

To obtain a lower bound, we can rewrite
\epsilon_{n}
as follows:

\epsilon_{n}=\Delta t\frac{(r_{i}^{n+1})_{min}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}-\frac{L}{2}(\Delta t\frac{(r_{i}^{n+1})_{max}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}})^{2}.

From Theorem
7
, we can obtain
\epsilon_{n}\geq 0
if
\Delta t\leq\min(C_{2},\frac{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})})
. Clearly, if we need
\epsilon_{n}\geq\gamma>0
, we need a tighter bound on
\Delta t
. Since
f(\bm{x}^{n+1})\leq f(\bm{x}^{n})
and
r_{i}^{0}=\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{0})}
for
\forall i
, we obtain

\epsilon_{n}\geq\Delta t\frac{r^{*}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{0})}}-\frac{L}{2}(\Delta t\frac{(r_{i}^{0})_{max}}{\sqrt{\delta}})^{2}\geq\Delta t\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{2\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{0})}}-\frac{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})}{2{\delta}}\Delta t^{2}:=h(\Delta t).

Denoting
\omega=\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{0})}}
, we can rewrite
h
as

h(\Delta t)=-\frac{L}{2\omega^{2}}\Delta t^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\Delta t.

To obtain
h(\Delta t)\geq\gamma>0
, we need

\frac{\omega^{3}-\sqrt{\omega^{6}-8\gamma{L}{\omega^{2}}}}{2L}\leq\Delta t\leq\frac{\omega^{3}+\sqrt{\omega^{6}-8\gamma{L}{\omega^{2}}}}{2L},

and
\omega^{6}-8\gamma{L}{\omega^{2}}\geq 0

which requires
\gamma\leq\frac{\omega^{4}}{8L}=\frac{\delta^{2}}{8L(f(\bm{x}^{0}))^{2}}
. Hence,
setting
\Delta t_{1}=\frac{\omega^{3}-\sqrt{\omega^{6}-8\gamma{L}{\omega^{2}}}}{2L}
and
\Delta t_{2}=\frac{\omega^{3}+\sqrt{\omega^{6}-8\gamma{L}{\omega^{2}}}}{2L}
, we can easily show that if
\Delta t_{1}\leq\Delta t\leq\min(C_{2},\frac{\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}}{Lf(\bm{x}^{0})},\Delta t_{2})
, then
\gamma\leq\epsilon_{n}\leq\frac{1}{2L}
.

Remark 10
.

A analogous result can be attained for the RSAV scheme(see also
[
15
]
).

4
Enhanced convergence rates

We showed in the last section that the E-RSAV algorithm exhibits a linear convergence rate. It is known that the selection of step size is a crucial factor in gradient descent algorithms, as demonstrated by the superlinear convergence rate of the secant method with step size
\Delta t=\frac{x_{n}-x_{n-1}}{f^{\prime}\left(x_{n}\right)-f^{\prime}\left(x_{n-1}\right)}
, and the quadratic convergence rate of the Newton method with step size
\frac{1}{f^{\prime\prime}\left(x_{n}\right)}
for univariate optimization problems. In this section, we demonstrate that by selecting an appropriate step size, the linear convergence rate of E-SAV and E-RSAV can be enhanced to achieve a superlinear convergence rate in the univariate case and present an adaptive version of E-RSAV which accelerates the convergence rate of E-RSAV in the multivariate case.

4.1
Super Linear Convergence rate of univariate E-SAV and E-RSAV

For the sake of simplifying the presentation, we consider the E-SAV scheme with
\mathcal{L}\equiv 0
in the univariate case (Noted similar results can be achieved for the E-RSAV scheme by substituting
r^{n+1}
with
\tilde{r}^{n+1}
):

\displaystyle\frac{x^{n+1}-x^{n}}{\Delta t}=-\frac{{r}^{n+1}}{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}}f^{\prime}(x^{n}),

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{{r}^{n+1}-r^{n}}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{f(x^{n})}}f^{\prime}(x^{n})\frac{x^{n+1}-x^{n}}{\Delta t}.

(61)

We can derive from the above that
r^{n+1}=\frac{1}{1+\Delta t\frac{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}{2{f(x^{n})}}}r^{n}=\frac{2{f(x^{n})}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}r^{n}
. Then, E-SAV can be rewritten as the following iterative method:

x^{n+1}=x^{n}-\Delta t\frac{2{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}f^{\prime}(x^{n})

(62)

with the learning rate
\eta_{n}=\Delta t\frac{2{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}
.

Assuming that
x^{\star}
is the optimal point and denoting
\varepsilon_{n}=x^{n}-x^{\star}
, we subtract
x^{\star}
from both sides of the equation (
62
) to derive

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{n}-\Delta t\frac{2{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}f^{\prime}(x^{n})

(63)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}\left(2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}\right)-2\Delta t{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}f^{\prime}(x^{n})}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}.

Applying the Taylor expansion to
f^{\prime}
around
x^{n}
, we derive

0=f^{\prime}(x^{\star})=f^{\prime}(x^{n})-f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})\varepsilon_{n}+\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2},

(64)

where
\xi^{\star}_{n}
lies between
x^{n}
and
x^{\star}
. Substituting this expression into the numerator of the second equation in (
63
) yields:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}\left(2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}\right)-2\Delta t{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}f^{\prime}(x^{n})}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}({x^{n}})^{2}}}

(65)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}\left(2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{{\left(f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})\varepsilon_{n}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\right)}^{2}}\right)}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta t{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})\varepsilon_{n}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\right)}}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}\left(2{f(x^{n})}-2\Delta t\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})\right)}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon^{2}_{n}\left({{\left(f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})-\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\varepsilon_{n}\right)}^{2}}+2\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\right)}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}.

A straightforward approach to obtaining a quadratically convergent algorithm is to set
\Delta t=\frac{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}}{r^{n}}\frac{1}{f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})}
. However, since computing second-order derivatives can be costly or may not be possible, we can instead set

\Delta t=\frac{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}}{r^{n}}\frac{x^{n}-x^{n-1}}{f^{\prime}(x^{n})-f^{\prime}(x^{n-1})}.

(66)

Then by taking Taylor expansion of
f^{\prime}(x^{n-1})
about
x^{n}
,

f^{\prime}\left(x^{n-1}\right)=f^{\prime}\left(x^{n}\right)+f^{\prime\prime}\left(x^{n}\right)\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)+\frac{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}{2}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)^{2},

(67)

where
\xi_{n}^{\ddagger}\text{ lies between }x^{n-1}\text{ and }x^{n}
, we can rewrite
\Delta t
as follows:

\Delta t=\frac{\sqrt{f(x^{n})}}{r^{n}}\frac{1}{f^{\prime\prime}\left(x^{n}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)}.

(68)

By substituting the expression for
\Delta t
from equation (
68
) into the first term of the numerator in the last equation of (
65
), we obtain

\displaystyle 2{f(x^{n})}-2\Delta t\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})

\displaystyle=2{f(x^{n})}\left(1-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})}{f^{\prime\prime}\left(x^{n}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle={f(x^{n})}\left(\frac{{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)}{f^{\prime\prime}\left(x^{n}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)}\right).

Then the last equation of (
65
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon_{n}{f(x^{n})}\left(\frac{{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)}{f^{\prime\prime}\left(x^{n}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{f^{(3)}(\xi_{k}^{\ddagger})}\left(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\right)}\right)}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta t\varepsilon^{2}_{n}\left({{\left(f^{\prime\prime}(x^{n})-\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\varepsilon_{n}\right)}^{2}}+2\sqrt{f(x^{n})}r^{n}\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(\xi^{\star}_{n})}{2}\right)}{2{f(x^{n})}+\Delta t{f^{\prime}(x^{n})^{2}}}.

We can then derive that

\begin{gathered}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left|\varepsilon_{n}\right|}{\left|\varepsilon_{n-1}\right|}=0,\\
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left|\varepsilon_{n+1}\right|}{\left|\varepsilon_{n}\varepsilon_{n-1}\right|}=\left(\frac{f^{(3)}\left(x^{*}\right)}{2f\left(x^{*}\right)f^{(2)}\left(x^{*}\right)}\right)^{2},\end{gathered}

(69)

which implies that the convergence rate of the modified algorithm with
\Delta t
given by (
66
) is
\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

4.2
AE-RSAV with Steffensen step size

To fully take advantage of the unconditional energy dissipation of E-RSAV, we propose below an adaptive version of the algorithm, AE-RSAV. By carefully selecting the step size, we can accelerate the algorithm’s performance. We introduce an indicator
\alpha=\text{mean}(\frac{\bm{r}^{n}}{\sqrt{E^{n}}})
, which demonstrates the ratio of the modified and the original energy. When the ratio is close to 1, we continue to use the current step size; when it deviates significantly from 1, it suggests that the step size needs to be reduced.

For further enhancement in efficacy, we should also consider using a suitable accelerated method. However,
the accelerated method discussed above is only applicable in the univariate case, as extending
(
66
) to the multivariate case is not straightforward. Nevertheless, this method provides us with insight that E-RSAV can be accelerated by choosing an
appropriate step size.
On the other hand, Steffensen’s acceleration method
[
24
,
25
]
is applicable to multivariate cases, so we will adopt the Steffensen step size
[
31
]
and introduce our adaptive step size:

\Delta t_{n}=\frac{\phi_{n}\left\|\nabla f\left(\bm{x}_{n}\right)\right\|^{2}}{\left[\nabla f\left(\bm{x}_{n}+\nabla f\left(\bm{x}_{n}\right)\right)-\nabla f\left(\bm{x}_{n}\right)\right]^{\top}\nabla f\left(\bm{x}_{n}\right)},

(70)

where
\phi_{n}=\frac{\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n})}}{r^{n}}\frac{\|\bm{x}^{n}-\bm{x}^{n-1}\|^{2}}{(\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n})-\nabla f(\bm{x}^{n-1}))^{T}(\bm{x}^{n}-\bm{x}^{n-1})}
. We observe that (
70
) captures a significant amount of gradient information and is similar to (
66
) but applicable to multivariate cases.

Building on the aforementioned premise, we put forth the AE-RSAV algorithm
algorithm

3
:

then

for

for
i=1,\cdots,m

Update
{r}_{i}^{n+1}={\eta}_{i0}\tilde{{r}}_{i}^{n+1}+(1-{\eta}_{i0})\sqrt{f(\bm{x}^{n+1})}
for

Update

\bm{x}^{n+1}

By incorporating Steffensen’s step size in the AE-RSAV algorithm, we can improve its performance. However, we should also consider the computational cost of Steffensen method and balance it against the potential performance gains. In practice, the decision of whether to use Steffensen method may depend on the specific problem and available computational resources.

5.1
Convex functions

Consider the following minimization problem:

\min f(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{N/2}x_{2i-1}^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2}x_{2i}^{2},

N

\mathcal{L}=0

\mathcal{L}=0

\Delta t=0.1
and
\Delta t=1

\Delta t=10
and
\Delta t=20

6
Conclusions

We proposed a new optimization algorithm, element-wise SAV with relaxation (E-RSAV), that satisfies an unconditionally energy dissipation law and possesses excellent convergence properties. We provided rigorous proofs for its linear convergence rate in the convex setting and proposed an improved algorithm which is shown to have a super-linear convergence rate in the univariate case. We also proposed an adaptive version of the E-RSAV (AE-RSAV) which combines the advantages of E-RSAV with adaptive step size based on Steffensen’s method. The unconditional energy dissipation property of our algorithm is particularly useful in ensuring the stability of the optimization process. Our numerical results for convex/non-convex optimizations and for using PINN to solve Burger’s equation demonstrate that the AE-RSAV algorithm outperforms some existing optimization methods, providing a new and powerful tool for solving optimization problems.
It is hoped that our contributions will serve as a catalyst for continued exploration in this field and make a significant impact on the development of optimization algorithms for solving complex problems in machine learning, material science, and fluid dynamics.

Limit laws for random walks in a dynamic path-cone mixing random environment
Stein Andreas Bethuelsen Florian Völlering

Abstract
We study the asymptotic behaviour of a random walk whose evolution is dependent on the state of an itself dynamically evolving environment. Assuming that the environment decorrelates with time by satisfying the ”path-cone”-mixing property introduced in [ 8 , Bethuelsen and Völlering] , we prove a strong law of large numbers and large deviation estimates. Moreover, under a mild assumption on the decay rate of this mixing property, we obtain a functional central limit theorem under the annealed law.



1
Introduction, the model and main results

1.1
Introduction

Random walks evolving in a random environment have been studied extensively since its origin in
[
28
]
. This model on
\mathbb{Z}
is by now well understood and known to exhibit phenomena that fundamentally distinguishes its behaviour from that of a standard simple random walk.
The same model on higher dimensional lattices has also attracted much interest, but here less is known and several fundamental questions remain open. See for instance
[
18
]
for an overview.

In recent years, driven by applications in physics and biology, random walks moving in a dynamically evolving environment have gained increasing interest.
For such models, qualitatively much is by now known under various types of
fast mixing
assumptions on the environment. In these cases, the environment de-correlates sufficiently fast for the increments of the random walk to be approximately independent on large time scales. Hence, its behaviour resembles that of a simple random walk. On the contrary, heuristics and simulation studies indicate that it may have anomalous behaviour when the environment is
slowly mixing

[
6
]
. In this paper, we focus on the fast-mixing regime and add to the literature new results about the limiting behaviour for a general class of models.

The
cone-mixing
condition introduced in
[
14
,
29
]
for static environments and adapted in
[
5
]
to the dynamic setting is one characterisation of fast-mixing.
In
[
8
]
we introduced what we in this paper name the
path-cone
mixing condition, which is a weakening of the cone-mixing condition. Therein we proved (among others) the strong law of large numbers for the position of the random walk. The purpose of the current paper is to present improvements to this work, particularly by deriving large deviation bounds (see Theorem
1.2
) and the scaling to a Brownian motion (see Theorem
1.3
).

In the following subsection we provide a precise definition of the ‘random walk in dynamic random environment’ model. Our main results on the asymptotic behaviour of the random walk are then presented in Subsection
1.3
. The remaining sections summarize the proofs of the main results.

1.2
The model

We now introduce in general terms the model of a random walk in a dynamic random environment (RWDRE), partly following the same notation and conventions as in
[
8
]
. As our
environment
we consider a (random) configuration
\omega\in\Omega
where
\Omega:=E^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}}
for some finite set
E
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and we denote by
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
its law. Here,
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
denotes the set of probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
where
\mathcal{F}
is the standard product
\sigma
-algebra generated by the cylinder events.
We assume throughout that
\mathbb{P}
is measure preserving with respect to translations, that is, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(\theta_{x,t}\cdot),

(1.1)

where
\theta_{x,t}
denotes the shift operator
\theta_{x,t}\omega(y,s)=\omega(y+x,s+t)
.
What we have in mind is that
\mathbb{P}
is the path measure of a stochastic process whose state space is
E^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, but note that our setup is more general than this.

The
random walk

(X_{t})
is a discrete-time stochastic process on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We assume that its transition probabilities depend on the state of the environment within a finite region
\Delta\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
around its current location. To be more precise, enumerate the set
\Omega_{0}:=E^{\Delta}=\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,K}
with
K=|E|^{|\Delta|}
. For each
i\in\{1,\dots,K\}
, we assume given a certain prescribed probability distribution on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, denoted by
\alpha(i,\cdot)
.
Then, given
\omega\in\Omega
and denoting by
o\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the origin, the evolution of
(X_{t})
is such that
P_{\omega}(X_{0}=o)=1
and, for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle P_{\omega}(X_{t+1}=y+z\mid X_{t}=y)=\alpha(i,z),\quad\text{ if }\theta_{y,t}\omega=\omega_{i}\text{ on }\Delta.

(1.2)

We denote by
\mathcal{R}:=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\alpha(i,z)>0\text{ for some }i\}
its range and assume throughout that this is a finite set.

The law of the random walk when we have conditioned on the entire environment,
P_{\omega}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}((\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}})
, is called the
quenched
law. We denote its corresponding
\sigma
-algebra by
\mathcal{G}
.
For
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
, we denote by
P_{\mathbb{P}}\in\mathcal{M}_{1}\left(\Omega\times(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\right)
the joint law of
(\omega,X)
, that is,

P_{\mathbb{P}}(B\times A)=\int_{B}P_{\omega}(A)d\mathbb{P}(\omega),\quad B\in\mathcal{F},A\in\mathcal{G}.

(1.3)

The marginal law of
P_{\mathbb{P}}
on
(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is the
annealed
law of
(X_{t})
.

1.3
Main results

Before presenting our main results about the asymptotic behaviour of
(X_{t})
, we first introduce the necessary notation and recall the ”path-cone”-mixing property introduced in
[
8
]
. For this let, for
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{t}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon x=\sum_{i=1}^{t}y_{i},y_{i}\in\mathcal{R}\}

(1.4)

be all points in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that
X_{t}
in principle can attain. Further, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(l):=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}\colon x\in\mathcal{R}_{t}+\Delta\text{ and }t\geq l\}

(1.5)

and denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{C}(l)}
the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}
generated by the cylinders in
\mathcal{C}(l)
.
Then, the cone-mixing property
of
[
5
, Definition 1.1]

alluded to in the introduction translates into requiring that

\sup_{B\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{C}(t)}}\sup_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{\leq 0}}|\mathbb{P}(B\mid A)-\mathbb{P}(B)|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty,

(1.6)

where
\mathcal{F}_{\leq 0}
is the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}
generated by the cylinders in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\{\dots,-2,-1,0\}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that, since the model of
[
5
]
evolves in continuous-time, they considered cylinders on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
with inclination
\theta
, requiring the equivalent of (
1.6
) to hold for all
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
. Since our process
(X_{t})
evolves in discrete-time, it cannot exit
\mathcal{C}(0)
and therefore it suffices in our case to consider the cones defined as in (
1.5
).

The path-cone mixing property is the weakening of (
1.6
) by restricting the conditioning to events along possible random walk paths only. To make this more precise, denote by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-k}:=\left\{(\gamma_{-k},\gamma_{-k+1},...,\gamma_{0})\colon\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1}\in\mathcal{R},-k\leq i<0,\gamma_{0}=o\right\};

(1.7)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-\infty}:=\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\Gamma_{-k},

(1.8)

i.e. the set of all possible backwards random walk trajectories from
(o,0)
of finite length. Given
\gamma\in\Gamma_{-\infty}
and

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{|\gamma|})\in\Omega_{0}^{|\gamma|}
, let

A(\gamma,\sigma):=\bigcap_{i=-{|\gamma|}}^{-1}\left\{\theta_{\gamma_{i},i}\omega=\sigma_{-i}\text{ on }\Delta\right\}

(1.9)

denote a particular observation of a random walk along the path
\gamma
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\gamma):=\left\{\bigcap_{i=-{|\gamma|}}^{-1}\left\{\theta_{\gamma_{i},i}\omega=\sigma_{-i}\text{ on }\Delta\right\}\colon\sigma\in\Omega_{0}^{|\gamma|}\text{ and }\mathbb{P}(A(\gamma,\sigma))>0\right\}

(1.10)

the set of all possible observations. Moreover, let

\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in{\Gamma_{-\infty}}}\mathcal{A}(\gamma)
. We say that
\mathbb{P}
is
path-cone mixing
if
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{\phi}(t)=0
, where

\widetilde{\phi}(t):=\sup_{B\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{C}(t)}}\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}}|\mathbb{P}(B\mid A)-\mathbb{P}(B)|.

(1.11)

See
[
8
, Section 2]
for a thorough discussion of distributions
\mathbb{P}
satisfying (
1.11
). These include the path measure of uniquely ergodic interacting particle systems converging to its stationary distribution sufficiently fast. The condition is, however, not restricted to this class and, particularly in high dimensions, one may expect it to hold for a larger class of interacting particles systems having multiple stationary distributions.

In addition to mixing of the dynamic environment, one form of lack of memory is often pivotal in studies of RWDRE models.
We say that the RWDRE model is (uniformly)
elliptic
if there is an
\epsilon>0
such that either of the two following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a)\>\inf_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{-\infty}}\inf_{\sigma\in\Omega_{0}}\mathbb{P}(\omega=\sigma\text{ on }\Delta\mid A)>\epsilon.\\
&b)\>\sup_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\inf_{i\in\{1,\dots,K\}}\{\alpha(i,z)\}>\epsilon.\end{split}

(1.12)

Note that Condition a) concerns the environment only and implies that, irrespectively of its past behaviour,
(X_{t})
may observe any element of
\Omega_{0}
with a probability of at least
\epsilon
.
Condition b) on the other hand is a condition on the jump kernel of
(X_{t})
and does not concern the environment.

The path-cone mixing property was utilised in
[
8
]
to prove, among others, that
(X_{t})
satisfies the Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN), assuming that
(X_{t})
satisfies the ellipticity condition b).
We next present an extension of this result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider an elliptic and path-cone mixing RWDRE model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

i)

There exists a
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{X_{t}}{t}=v\quad P_{\mathbb{P}}\text{-almost surely}\text{ and in }L^{1}.

(1.13)

ii)

For every
\epsilon>0
there exists constants
C,c>0
such that

P_{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\frac{X_{t}}{t}-v\right|>\epsilon\right)\leq Ce^{-ct},\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{N},

(1.14)

with
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as in (
1.13
).

Theorem
1.2
i) extends
[
8
, Corollary 1.4]
by relaxing the ellipticity assumption for the SLLN
and to hold in
L^{1}
.
Moreover, the Large Deviation Bounds (LDBs) of Theorem
1.2
ii) are new and extend results obtained in
[
4
,
7
]

to environments satisfying far less stringent space-time mixing assumptions. Note also that in
[
12
]
a large deviation principle was established under the quenched law under rather general assumptions on the dynamic environment. Particularly, since the rate function in the quenched setting is upper bounded by annealed large deviation bounds, by Theorem
1.2
ii), the corresponding rate function has a unique zero at
x=v
.

We do not expect the assumptions for the SLLN to be sharp. In particular, restricted to models on
\mathbb{Z}
with nearest neighbour jumps, a SLLN was recently obtained in
[
9
]
under a seemingly less restrictive mixing assumption. Therein they also obtained LDBs, but with a polynomial decay in (
1.14
).
See also
[
2
]
for an extension of the SLLN to models with finite range jumps.

To conclude the annealed functional central limit theorem (aFCLT) we are in need of a slightly stronger ellipticity property. We say that the RWDRE model is (uniformly)
strongly elliptic
if either condition a) above holds or it satisfies condition
b^{\prime})
given by:

\displaystyle b^{\prime})\>\exists\;\epsilon>0\>\colon\inf_{z\in\mathcal{R}}\inf_{i\in\{1,\dots,K\}}\{\alpha(i,z)\}>\epsilon.

(1.15)

Theorem 1.3
.

Consider a path-cone mixing and strongly elliptic RWDRE model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

i)

If there is
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the variance of
\alpha\cdot X_{n}

diverges, then there is a slowly varying function
h\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\left(\frac{\alpha\cdot X_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-\alpha\cdot v\lfloor nt\rfloor}{\sqrt{nh(n)}}\right)_{t\in[0,1]}\overset{P_{\mathbb{P}}}{\implies}(B_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}

(1.16)

where
\overset{P_{\mathbb{P}}}{\implies}
denotes convergence in distribution under the annealed law
P_{\mathbb{P}}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
with respect to the Skorohod topology and
(B_{t})
is a standard Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}
.

ii)

If
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{\phi}(t)t^{2+\delta}=0
for some
\delta>0
, then furthermore there is a
\sigma\in(0,\infty)
such that statement of i) holds with
h(n)=\sigma
.

iii)

If
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{\phi}(t)t^{2+\delta}=0
for some
\delta>0
and for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{o\}
the variance of
\alpha\cdot X_{n}

diverges, then

\left(\frac{X_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-v\lfloor nt\rfloor}{\sqrt{n}}\right)_{t\in[0,1]}\overset{P_{\mathbb{P}}}{\implies}(B_{t}^{\Sigma})_{t\in[0,1]}

(1.17)

where
(B_{t}^{\Sigma})
is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance matrix
\Sigma>0
.

Previously the aFCLT has been proven in
[
15
]
for random walks in static random environments (note also the recent improvements
[
21
,
22
]
), which was adapted to the dynamic setting in
[
3
, Chapter 3]
. The mixing assumptions needed therein are more restrictive than cone-mixing and hence, Theorem
1.3
provides an extension of these works. The aFCLT was also obtain in
[
26
]
under a mixing assumption similar to cone-mixing. Lastly, we note that the aFCLT follows from the methodology developed in
[
17
]
for Markovian environments when combined with the results of
[
8
, Corollary 1.6]
, under the assumption that the environment is path-cone mixing with
\widetilde{\phi}(t)
decaying at an exponential rate.

We believe that the assumption in Theorem
1.3
that the variance of
(X_{n})
diverges is redundant in most cases, but refer to Section
3.3
for a more thorough discussion of this matter. We also believe that the assumption on
\widetilde{\phi}(t)
in statement ii) and iii) can be considerably relaxed. In particularly, one might expect that the local environment process considered in Section
2
should mix at the same rate as
\widetilde{\phi}(t)
in which
case
a decay at order
\log(t)^{-(2+\delta)}
for some
\delta>0
would be sufficient.
The main theorems might also hold beyond the modelling setting of this paper. However, the assumptions that
E
and
\mathcal{R}
are finite come natural in the setting of path-cone mixing environments and are used at several places in the proofs.

Having obtained the aFCLT in Theorem
1.3
, it is also natural to ask if the same statement holds with respect to the quenched law for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e. realization of the dynamic environment. General quenched central limit theorems have been obtained e.g. in
[
17
,
16
]
under more stringent mixing assumptions than path-cone mixing. We leave the extension of these to our setting for future research.

2
The local environment process

Inspired by
[
16
]
, we study the so-called
local environment process
.
To introduce this process precisely, we first recall that
the so-called
environment process
is the process
(\omega_{t}^{EP})
on
\Omega

given by

(\omega_{t}^{EP})=(\theta_{(X_{t},t)}\omega),\quad t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(2.1)

This process was the main object of study in our previous work
[
8
]
and contains information about the entire environment as seen from the walker. The
local environment process
restricts to local information about the environment and is the process
(\xi_{t})=(\xi_{t}^{(1)},\xi_{t}^{(2)})
on
\Xi=(\Omega_{0},\mathcal{R})

given by

\displaystyle\xi_{t}^{(1)}(x)=\omega_{t}^{EP}(x,0),\quad x\in\Delta;\quad

\displaystyle\xi_{t}^{(2)}:=X_{t+1}-X_{t}.

(2.2)

Thus,
(\xi_{t}^{(1)})
is the projection of the environment process onto
\Delta
, that is, the environment the random walk needs to observe in order to determine its future behaviour, whereas
(\xi_{t}^{(2)})
is simply the jump taken by the random walk at time
t
. Particularly, note that
X_{n}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}\xi_{t}^{(2)}
.

In the following, we write
P_{-k}
to denote the law of
(\xi_{t})
shifted by time
t=-k
, that is, the law of
(\hat{\xi}_{t})_{t\geq-k}
where

\hat{\xi}_{t}=\xi_{t+k}
,
t\geq-k
. We also denote by
\mathcal{H}
the induced
\sigma
-algebra on
\Xi^{\mathbb{Z}}
. For
i\leq j
, we write
\mathcal{H}_{[i,j]}
for the induced
\sigma
-algebra containing events on
\Xi^{[i,j]}
and write
\mathcal{H}_{\geq t}
for
\mathcal{H}_{[t,\infty)}
.

Now, consider a cylinder event
A\in\mathcal{H}_{[-k,-1]}
. That is, similar to (
1.9
),
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma_{-k}
and
\sigma\in\Omega_{0}^{|\gamma|}
, we have that
A=\bigcap_{i=-k}^{-1}\left\{\hat{\xi}_{i}^{(1)}=\gamma_{-i},\hat{\xi}_{i}^{(2)}=\sigma_{-i}\right\}
.
Then it follow by
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
(see particularly the
last line of the proof) that
P_{-k}(\cdot|A)
equals in law to the process
(\xi_{t})
with initial distribution given by
P_{\mathbb{P}(\cdot|A(\gamma,\sigma))}
.
With this in mind, for
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, consider the uniform mixing quantity

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(t)=\sup_{A_{0},A_{1}\in\mathcal{A}}\sup_{B\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq t}}|P_{\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid A_{0})}(B)-P_{\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid A_{1})}(B)|.

(2.3)

In Section
3
we provide fairly general arguments on how mixing properties of the local environment process imply asymptotic properties of the random walk
(X_{t})
. We summarize our findings in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Consider an elliptic RWDRE model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfying
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\phi}(t)=0
.

i)

The law
\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{-(i)}(\cdot)
converges weakly to some law
\nu
on
(\Xi^{\mathbb{Z}},\mathcal{H})
which is trivial on the tail
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\coloneqq\cap_{s\geq 0}\mathcal{H}_{\geq s}
. Moreover,
\nu
and
P_{\mathbb{P}}
agree on
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
.

ii)

(X_{t})
satisfies the SLLN of Theorem
1.2
i)
with
v=\nu(\xi_{1}^{(2)})
.

iii)

(X_{t})
satisfies the LDBs of Theorem
1.2
ii).

iv)

If the model is strongly elliptic and
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Var_{\nu}[\theta\cdot X_{n}]=\infty
for some
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
(\theta\cdot X_{t})
satisfies the aFCLT of Theorem
1.3
i). Furthermore, if
\sum_{t\geq 1}\sqrt{\widehat{\phi}(2^{t})}<\infty
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}n^{-1}\Var_{\nu}(\alpha\cdot X_{n})
exists and is strictly positive.
Lastly,
(X_{t})
satisfies the aFCLT of Theorem
1.3
iii) whenever
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Var_{\nu}[\theta\cdot X_{n}]=\infty
for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{o\}
for some
\Sigma_{\nu}>0
.

Thus, in order to prove asymptotic properties of
(X_{t})
it is sufficient to control the mixing properties of the local environment process in terms of (
2.3
). The next theorem shows that the latter can be controlled by requiring mixing properties of the underlying environment in terms of the path-cone mixing property of (
1.11
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider an elliptic RWDRE model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

and write
\epsilon=e^{-c_{0}}>0
for the constant in (
1.12
). Then, for any
c_{1}>c_{0}
,

\widehat{\phi}(t)\leq\widetilde{\phi}(c_{1}\log(t))+2\exp(-t^{1-c_{0}/c_{1}}/c_{1}\log(t)).

(2.4)

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is presented in the following section and is based on the
\epsilon
-coin trick introduced in
[
14
,
29
]
. However, unlike the approach therein via the construction of an approximate regeneration time, we control the mixing properties of the local environment process directly. This leads, in our opinion, to a simplification of the proof. Moreover, it yields an estimate on the decay of mixing as seen in (
2.4
) which is interesting in its own.

Proof 2.3

(Proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
)
.

By Theorem
2.2
, if
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{\phi}(t)=0
, then we also have that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\phi}(t)=0
. Hence, we can invoke Proposition
2.1
i)-ii) to conclude Theorem
1.2
. To conclude Theorem
1.3
we note that if
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{\phi}(t)t^{2+\delta}=0
, then Theorem
2.2
yields that
\sum_{t\geq 1}\sqrt{\widehat{\phi}(2^{t})}<\infty
and we can invoke Proposition
2.1
iv).

3
Proofs

Here we provide the proofs of the auxiliary results presented in the previous subsection, Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
respectively. In the final subsection we discuss sufficient conditions for divergence of the variance.

3.1
Proof of Theorem
2.2

Consider an elliptic RWDRE model on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
. We now provide the details of the
\epsilon
-coin trick, where
\epsilon>0
refers to the constant provided by the ellipticity assumption. Informally, this bit of
\epsilon
-probability will allow us to, at least for a finite time window, decouple the random walk from the environment.

Assume first that Condition b) holds and fix a
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
satisfying
\inf_{i}\alpha(i,z)>\epsilon
. Further, let
U=(U_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be an i.i.d. sequence of
Unif(0,1)
-random variables. For a fixed realisation of the environment
\omega
and
U
, similarly to (
1.2
), we consider the random walk
(\tilde{X}_{t})
such that
P_{\omega,u}(\tilde{X}_{0}=o)=1
and, for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle P_{\omega,u}(\tilde{X}_{t+1}=x+y\mid X_{t}=x)=1_{u\leq\epsilon}1_{y=z}+1_{u>\epsilon}\frac{(\alpha(i,y)-\epsilon 1_{y=z})}{1-\epsilon}

(3.1)

\text{if }\theta_{x,t}\omega=\omega_{i}\text{ on }\Delta
.
Note that, when averaging over
U
, the law of
\tilde{X}
agrees with that of
X
.
Further, from
\omega
and
(\tilde{X}_{t})
, we can construct the corresponding local environment
(\tilde{\xi}_{t})
as in (
2.2
), which now depends on the sequence
U
, but again whose law equals that of
(\xi_{t})
when averaging over
U
. In particular, for proving Theorem
2.2
it is sufficient to control the process
(\tilde{X}_{t})
.
For this, the following is a key lemma, where we denote by

\tau_{n}\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq n\colon U_{s}\leq\epsilon\>\forall\>s=t-n+1,\dots,t\},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(3.2)

that is, the first time that the sequence
(U_{t})
successively has taken values below
\epsilon
over a time period of length
n
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that the RWDRE model satisfies Condition b). Then, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sup_{A_{0},A_{1}\in\mathcal{A}}\sup_{B\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}}|P_{\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid A_{0})}(B\mid\tau_{n}=n)-P_{\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid A_{1})}(B\mid\tau_{n}=n)|\leq\widetilde{\phi}(n).

(3.3)

Proof 3.2

(Proof of Lemma
3.1
)
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
A_{0},A_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{H}_{\geq n}
. Denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
a coupling of
\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid A_{0})
and
\mathbb{P}(\cdot\mid A_{1})

3.3
Divergence of the variance

As shown in
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
, for stationary real-valued
\phi
-mixing processes either
\sup_{n}\Var(X_{n})<\infty
or
\Var(X_{n})=nh(n)
for some slowly varying function
h
. In the literature on central limit theorems for weakly dependent random variables it is therefore common practice to assume that the variance diverges to infinity
[
10
]
. General arguments for proving this divergence seem rather scarce with
[
1
]
and
[
13
]
being notable exceptions. The method of the former was applied successfully to prove the aFCLT in
[
26
, Theorem 3.5]
which, when properly adapted to our model setting yields that the variance grows linearly in
n
when
\widetilde{\phi}
decays exponentially fast.

We believe that the assumption on the variance of
X_{n}
in Proposition
2.1
iii)-iv) and Theorem
1.3
for most (if not all) path-cone mixing and strongly elliptic RWDRE models is redundant. For instance, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{o\}
, a simple application of Chebychevs inequality implies that

\Var(\alpha\cdot X_{n})\geq\epsilon^{2}P(|\alpha\cdot X_{n}-\alpha\cdot nv|>\epsilon)

(3.36)

from which it follows that
\limsup_{n}P(|\alpha\cdot X_{n}-\alpha\cdot nv|>\epsilon(n))>0
for some
\epsilon(n)\rightarrow\infty
suffices. Under certain restrictions on the dimension, by the arguments of
[
25
]
, the latter holds even without any mixing requirements. More precisely, assuming that
\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\alpha(i,y)>0\}
span
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for each
i=1,\dots,K
, and reasoning as in the proof of
[
25
, Proposition 1.4]
, we have that there is some universal constant
C>0
such that

P(\|X_{n}-nv\|>\epsilon)\geq 1-C\epsilon^{d}/n^{d/2-K+1}.

(3.37)

Hence, we have
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Var_{\nu}(\alpha\cdot X_{n})=\infty
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\setminus}\{o\}
whenever
d>2(K-1)
.

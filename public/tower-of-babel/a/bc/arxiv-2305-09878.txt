Deterministic generation of arbitrary n-photon states in an integrated photonic system Preprint: APS/123-QED
Fan Xing, Zeyang Liao, Xue-hua Wang

Abstract
Quantum light sources play a vital role in various aspects of quantum information science, but on-demand high-efficient generation of arbitrary multiphoton states which can be easily integrated is still challenging. Here, we propose a chip-integrable scheme to deterministically generate a group of n n photons with very high fidelity based on the long-range collective interaction between the emitters mediated by the waveguide modes. The n n photons are shown to be emitted in a bundle while two successive n n -photon bundles tend to be antibunched which can behave as an n n -photon gun. Our results here can find important applications in the areas such as photonic-chip-based quantum information processing and quantum metrology.

IV
SUMMARY

To conclude, we propose a scheme to deterministically generate arbitrary photon number state on demand in an integrated quantum electrodynamics system. Our results show that by selectively pump certain number of emitters into the excited state they can collectively decay to the ground state and emit certain number of photons into the waveguide. The numerical results show that these photons are emitted by bundles and the photons within each bundle is highly correlated. Our study here can find important applications in the on-chip integrated quantum information technology and quantum metrology.

Appendix B
Quantum Monte Carlo method

To calculate the photon statistics of the output field, it is convenient to use the quantum Monte Carlo method
Zhang and Baranger 2018
;
Plenio and Knight 1998
;
Mølmer et al. 1993
. The effective Hamiltonian of the emitter system can be written as

\displaystyle H_{eff}(t)=H_{coh}(t)-\frac{i\hbar}{2}\sum_{\beta=R,L,\gamma,\gamma_{f}}J_{\beta}^{+}J_{\beta}^{-},

(6)

where
H_{coh}(t)
is given by Eq. (3) and the effective jumping operators
J_{R,L}^{-}=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma_{1D}}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\mp ik_{a}z_{j}}\sigma_{fe}^{j}(t)
,
J_{\gamma}^{-}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{ge}^{j}(t)
, and
J_{\gamma_{f}}^{-}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{f}}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sigma_{gf}^{j}(t)
. Having the effective Hamiltonian, we can use the quantum Monte Carlo method to numerically calculate the dynamics of the system and the photon statistics with the steps as follows:

(1) Suppose the wavefunction at time
t
is
|\varphi(t)\rangle
.

(2) Calculate the probabilities:
P_{R,L}=dt\langle\varphi(t)|J_{R,L}^{+}J_{R,L}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle
is the probability that the emitters jump from the
|e\rangle
state to the
|f\rangle
state and emit a photon to the right (left) direction;
P_{\gamma}=dt\langle\varphi(t)|J_{\gamma}^{+}J_{\gamma}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle
is the probability that the emitters jump from the
|e\rangle
state to the
|g\rangle
state and emit a photon to the free space;
P_{\gamma_{f}}=dt\langle\varphi(t)|J_{\gamma_{f}}^{+}J_{\gamma_{f}}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle
is the probability that the emitters jump from the
|f\rangle
state to the
|g\rangle
state and emit a photon to the free space.

(3) Choose a random number
r
between 0 and 1, and if
(a)
r<P_{\gamma}
:
|\varphi(t+dt)\rangle=J_{\gamma}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle/\sqrt{P_{\gamma}/dt}
, a photon emits to the free space;
(b)
P_{\gamma}<r<P_{\gamma}+P_{R}
,
|\varphi(t+dt)\rangle=J_{R}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle/\sqrt{P_{R}/dt}
, a photon emits to the right within waveguide;
(c)
P_{\gamma}+P_{R}<r<P_{\gamma}+P_{R}+P_{L}
,
|\varphi(t+dt)\rangle=J_{L}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle/\sqrt{P_{L}/dt}
, a photon emits to the left within waveguide;
(d)
P_{\gamma}+P_{R}+P_{L}<r<P_{\gamma}+P_{R}+P_{L}+P_{\gamma_{f}}
,
|\varphi(t+dt)\rangle=J_{\gamma_{f}}^{-}|\varphi(t)\rangle/\sqrt{P_{\gamma_{f}}/dt}
, a photon decay to ground states from
|f\rangle
.
(e)
r>P_{\gamma}+P_{R}+P_{L}+P_{\gamma_{f}}
,
|\varphi(t+dt)\rangle=(I-iH_{eff}dt)|\varphi(t)\rangle/\sqrt{1-(P_{R}+P_{L}+P_{\gamma}+P_{\gamma_{f}})}
, no photon is emitted and the system evolves coherently.

(4) Repeat the above procedures for sufficiently large amount of times, we can then obtain the density of the state at arbitrary time by averaging over the results of each trajectory, i.e.,
|\rho(t)\rangle=\sum_{m}|\varphi_{m}(t)\rangle\langle\varphi_{m}(t)|
where
m
denotes each trajectory and can also calculate the photon statistics including photon counts, photon correlations and so on.

The Riesz Transform and Fractional Integral Operators in the Bessel Setting
Jorge J. Betancor, Xuan Thinh Duong, Ming-Yi Lee, Ji Li, Brett D. Wick

Abstract




Abstract:
Fix
\lambda>0
. Consider
the Bessel operator

\triangle_{\lambda}:=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\frac{d}{dx}
on
\mathbb{R_{+}}
, where
\mathbb{R_{+}}:=(0,\infty)
and
dm_{\lambda}:=x^{2\lambda}dx
with
dx
the Lebesgue measure.
We provide a deeper study of the Bessel Riesz transform and fractional integral operator via the related Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated with
\triangle_{\lambda}
.
Moreover, we investigate some possible characterization of the commutator of fractional integral operator, which was missing in the literature of the Bessel setting.

Keywords
: commutator; Bessel operator; Bessel Riesz transform.

Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 42B30, 42B20, 42B35

1
Introduction and Statement of Main Results

1.1
Background

In 1965, Muckenhoupt and Stein in
[
26
]
introduced the harmonic function theory associated with the Bessel operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
, defined by,

{\triangle_{\lambda}}:=-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\frac{d}{dx},\quad\lambda>0.

They developed a theory in the setting of

{\triangle_{\lambda}}
which parallels the classical one associated to the usual Laplacian
\triangle
. Results on the
{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
-boundedness of conjugate
functions and fractional integrals associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
were
obtained, where
p\in[1,\infty)
,
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
and
{dm_{\lambda}}(x):=x^{2\lambda}\,dx
.

The related elliptic partial differential equation is the following “singular Laplace equation”

\triangle_{t,\,x}(u):=-\partial_{t}^{2}u-\partial_{x}^{2}u-\frac{2\lambda}{x}\partial_{x}u=0

(1.1)

studied by Weinstein
[
36
]
, and Huber in higher dimension in
[
23
]
, where they considered the generalised axially symmetric potentials, and obtained the properties of the solutions of this equation, such as the extension, the uniqueness theorem, and the boundary value problem for certain domains.

Since then, the Bessel context has been extensively studied;
see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
25
,
35
,
38
]
and the references therein.
Comparing to the standard Laplacian on Euclidean space, the key difference is that there is no Fourier transform in the Bessel setting. Thus, some fundamental results on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
were only recently established in the Bessel setting via different approaches that avoided arguments involving the Fourier transform.

For example, in the Euclidean setting, the characterization of the boundedness of the commutator of the Riesz transforms via the BMO space was first established by Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss
[
11
]
. In his setting, the first proof of the lower bound of the commutator utilized a Fourier expansion. A parallel version of
[
11
]
was established in the Bessel setting in
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
via a real analysis method, and later on the characterization of compactness of commutator in the Bessel setting was found in
[
17
]
.

However, there are several other aspects of the commutator
[b,R_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}]
of the Bessel Riesz transform that remain open.

Question 1
: The connection between the commutator of the Bessel Riesz transform and the Besov space or Triebel–Lizorkin space in the Bessel setting ?

Note that in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the Euclidean setting a connection was established by M. Paluszyński
[
29
]
, where the approach depends heavily on the Fourier transform.

Question 2
: The characterization of the endpoint boundedness of the commutator of the Bessel Riesz transform? That is, to investigate the necessary and sufficient conditions on the symbol
b
such that
[b,R_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}]
is bounded from the Bessel Hardy space to
L^{1}
, and from
L^{\infty}
to the Bessel BMO space.

It turns out that a simple duplication of the classical result on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
[
31
]
), especially from
L^{\infty}
to the Bessel BMO space, is not the suitable one, and hence this remains open till now.

Question 3
: The characterization of the boundedness of the commutator
[b,{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}]
of the fractional operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}
?

It turns out to be a more difficult question in comparison to the Euclidean setting. The argument is different from the classical result on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, due to the intrinsic structure of the Bessel Laplacian.

The aim of this paper is to address these questions in the Bessel setting.

1.2
Main results

We will regard
\mathbb{R}_{+}
as a space of homogeneous type, in the sense of Coifman and Weiss,
[
12
]
, with Euclidean metric (i.e., the absolute value
|\cdot|
) and measure
m_{\lambda}
. Specifically, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
r>0
, a set
I(x,r):=B(x,r)\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}
is considered as a ball in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
B(x,r)
is a Euclidean ball with centre
x
and radius
r
. It can be deduced from
[
17
]
that
m_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies a doubling condition stated as follow: for every
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\min\{2^{2},2^{2\lambda+1}\}m_{\lambda}(I)\leq m_{\lambda}(2I)\leq 2^{2\lambda+1}m_{\lambda}(I).

(1.2)

Set
\mathbf{Q}=2\lambda+1
, which is the upper dimension of the measure
m_{\lambda}
.

Next, consider
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,m_{\lambda})
as a space of homogeneous type. We let
\{S_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a family of operators on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
which is an approximation to the identity and set
D_{k}=S_{k}-S_{k+1}
. Full details are provided in Section 2.1. Moreover, for
0<\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}<1
, we let
({\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}))^{\prime}
denote the space of distributions on
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,m_{\lambda})
, with full details in Section 2.2.

We first consider the Besov space
\dot{B}_{\lambda,\infty}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
and then study the commutator with the Riesz transform and fractional integral operators in the Bessel setting.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
0<\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}<1
. For
\beta\in(0,1)
, the Besov space
\dot{B}_{\lambda,\infty}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is the set of distributions
f
in
({\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}))^{\prime}
such that

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{\lambda,\infty}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}:=\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z},\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\end{subarray}}2^{\beta k}|D_{k}f(x)|<\infty.

Next, we provide the definition of the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
via the
operator
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
from the
approximation to identity
\{S_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
0<\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}<1
. For
|\alpha|<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces associated with
{\triangle_{\lambda}}
are defined as follows. Set

\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{\lambda,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):=\big\{f\in({\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}))^{\prime}:\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{\lambda,p}}:<\infty\big\},

where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{\lambda,p}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\Big\|\Big\{\ \sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(2^{k\alpha}|D_{k}(f)|\big)^{q}\Big\}^{1/q}\Big\|_{{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}}\quad&\text{if}\ \ 1\leq q<\infty,\\[9.0pt]
\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{k\alpha}|D_{k}(f)|\Big\|_{{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}}&\text{if}\ \ q=\infty.\end{cases}

It is important to note that the definition of
\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{\alpha,q}_{\lambda,p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is independent of the choice of
\{D_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. When
q=\infty
, this is justified by our Theorem
3.4
. When
1\leq q<\infty
, this can be seen from the Plancherel–Polya type inequality for Triebel–Lizorkin spaces on space of homogeneous type from Han, Müller and Yang
[
19
,
20
]
.

This leads to our first main result: characterisation for the Besov space
\dot{B}_{\lambda,\infty}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
via the commutator
[b,R_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}]
, which is defined as

[b,R_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}](f)=bR_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}(f)-R_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}(bf)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty,0<\beta<1
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

b\in\dot{B}_{\lambda,\infty}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
;

(b)

[b,R_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}]
is bounded from
{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
to
\dot{F}_{\lambda,p}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

This resolves
Question 1
and provides a different method (bypassing the use of the Fourier transform) from the result of M. Paluszyński
[
29
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We now turn to the second main result: characterization of endpoint boundedness for

[b,{R_{\Delta_{\lambda}}}]
. Recall that the BMO space is defined as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

A function
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}({{\mathbb{{\mathbb{R}}}}_{+}},dm_{\lambda})
belongs to
the space
{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
if

\sup_{x,\,r\in(0,\,\infty)}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,\,r))}\displaystyle\int_{I(x,\,r)}\left|f(y)-\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,r))}\displaystyle\int_{I(x,\,r)}f(z)\,dm_{\lambda}(z)\right|\,dm_{\lambda}(y)<\infty.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that
b\in{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then, the following assertions are equivalent:

(a)

[b,{R_{\Delta_{\lambda}}}]
is bounded from
L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})
to
{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
;

(b)

\sup\limits_{I=(x_{0}-r_{0},x_{0}+r_{0})\atop 0<4r_{0}<x_{0}}\log\big(\frac{x_{0}}{r_{0}}\big)\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)}\int_{I}|b(z)-b_{I}|dm_{\lambda}(z)<\infty.

Definition 1.6

(
[
3
]
)
.

A function
a
is called a
(1,2)_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}
-atom
if there exists an open bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

such that
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,supp\,}}(a)\subset I
,
\|a\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\leq[m_{\lambda}(I)]^{-1/2}

and
\int_{0}^{\infty}a(x)\,{dm_{\lambda}}(x)=0.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Assume that
b\in{\rm BMO}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then,
[b,{R_{\Delta_{\lambda}}}]
can be extended from
span
\{(1,2)_{{\triangle_{\lambda}}}
-atoms
\}
to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})
as a bounded operator from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},m_{\lambda})
into

L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},m_{\lambda})
if, and only if,
b
is constant.

Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
give an affirmative answer to
Question 2
. Moreover, it is worth pointing out that comparing the result to the classical result on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
31
]
(the commutator of the Riesz transforms are bounded from
L^{\infty}
to BMO if and only if
b
is a constant), the condition (b) in Theorem
1.5
is new and reveals the intrinsic structure of the Bessel setting.

We now turn to our third main result.
Recall the fractional operator
{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}
with
0<\alpha<{\bf Q}
, defined by

{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}f(x)={1\over\Gamma(\alpha/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{e^{t{\triangle_{\lambda}}}}(f)(x){dt\over t^{1-\alpha/2}}.

We investigate the commutator
[b,{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}](f)(x):=b(x){\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}(f)(x)-{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}(bf)(x)
as follows.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
1<p<q<\infty,{0<\alpha<1}
, and
0<\beta<1
. If
b\in\dot{B}_{\lambda,\infty}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
then

[b,{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}]
is bounded from
{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
to
\dot{F}_{\lambda,q}^{\beta,\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\alpha}{\bf Q}
.

We point out that the reverse of this result is still open.
Moreover, the characterization the
L^{p}-L^{q}
boundedness of
[b,{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}]
is still open, which may or may not correspond to the existing Bessel BMO space.

This paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we provide the necessary preliminaries in the Bessel setting. In Section 3 we provide a characterization of the Besov space in the Bessel setting and provide the proof of Theorem
1.3
. In Section 4 we give the proof of Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
. In the last section we prove Theorem
1.8
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Approximation to the Identity

We first recall the definition of approximation to the identity.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a family of operators
\{S_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an approximation to the identity if
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}S_{k}=Id
,
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to-\infty}S_{k}=0

and moreover, the kernel
S_{k}(x,y)
of
S_{k}
satisfies the following condition: for
\beta,\gamma\in(0,1]
,

{\rm(A_{i})}

for any
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

|S_{k}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,2^{-k}))+m_{\lambda}(I(y,2^{-k}))+m_{\lambda}(I(x,|x-y|))}\bigg(\frac{2^{-k}}{|x-y|+2^{-k}}\bigg)^{\gamma};

{\rm(A_{ii})}

for any
x,y,\widetilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|y-\widetilde{y}|\leq(2^{-k}+|x-y|)/2
,

\displaystyle|S_{k}(x,y)-S_{k}(x,\widetilde{y})|+|S_{k}(y,x)-S_{k}(\widetilde{y},x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,2^{-k}))+m_{\lambda}(I(y,2^{-k}))+m_{\lambda}(I(x,|x-y|))}\bigg(\frac{|y-\widetilde{y}|}{|x-y|+2^{-k}}\bigg)^{\beta}\bigg(\frac{2^{-k}}{|x-y|+2^{-k}}\bigg)^{\gamma};

{\rm(A_{iii})}

for any
x,\,y,\,\widetilde{x},\,\widetilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|x-\widetilde{x}|
,
|y-\widetilde{y}|\leq(2^{-k}+|x-y|)/3
,

\displaystyle|S_{k}(x,y)-S_{k}(x,\widetilde{y})|+|S_{k}(\widetilde{x},y)-S_{k}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,2^{-k}))+m_{\lambda}(I(y,2^{-k}))+m_{\lambda}(I(x,|x-y|))}

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigg(\frac{|x-\widetilde{x}|}{|x-y|+2^{-k}}\bigg)^{\beta}\bigg(\frac{|y-\widetilde{y}|}{|x-y|+2^{-k}}\bigg)^{\beta}\bigg(\frac{2^{-k}}{|x-y|+2^{-k}}\bigg)^{\gamma};

{\rm(A_{iv})}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

{\int_{0}^{\infty}}S_{k}(x,y){\,dm_{\lambda}(y)}={\int_{0}^{\infty}}S_{k}(y,x){\,dm_{\lambda}(y)}=1.

One of the constructions of an approximation to the identity is due to
Coifman, see
[
13
]
. We set
D_{k}:=S_{k}-S_{k+1}
. And it is obvious that the kernel
D_{k}(x,y)

of
D_{k}
satisfies
{\rm(A_{i})}
,
{\rm(A_{ii})}
and
{\rm(A_{iii})}
with
S_{k}(x,y)
replaced by
D_{k}(x,y)
, and

{\int_{0}^{\infty}}D_{k}(x,y){\,dm_{\lambda}(y)}={\int_{0}^{\infty}}D_{k}(y,x){\,dm_{\lambda}(y)}=0

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We now

Definition 2.2

(

]
)
.

Consider the space
(\mathbb{R}_{+},|\cdot|,{dm_{\lambda}})
. Let
0<\gamma,\beta\leq 1
and

A function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

(x_{0},r,\beta,\gamma)
centered at

(i)

x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

|x-y|\leq{\frac{1}{2}}(r+|x-x_{0}|).

f

and that

Let

be the

{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(1,1)
in
the norm of
{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)
when

. If
f\in{\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta,\gamma)
, we then define

\|f\|_{{\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta,\gamma)}:=\|f\|_{{\mathcal{G}}(\beta,\gamma)}
.
({\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta,\gamma))^{\prime}
, the distribution space, is defined to be the set of all
linear functionals
L
from
{\mathop{{\mathcal{G}}}\limits^{\circ}}_{1}(\beta,\gamma)
to
\mathbb{C}
with
the property that there exists

such that for all

2.3
Poisson and Conjugate Poisson Kernels, and the Riesz Transform in the Bessel Setting

Let
P^{[\lambda]}_{t}:=e^{-t\sqrt{\Delta_{\lambda}}}
be the Poisson semigroup associated with
\Delta_{\lambda}
.
We recall the following properties from
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Then
\{P^{[\lambda]}_{t}\}_{t>0}
is
a symmetric diffusion semigroup satisfying that
P^{[\lambda]}_{t}P^{[\lambda]}_{s}=P^{[\lambda]}_{s}P^{[\lambda]}_{t}
for any
t,\,s\in(0,\infty)
,
the
C_{0}
property,
\lim_{t\to 0}P^{[\lambda]}_{t}f=f
in
{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
and

{\rm(S_{i})}

\|P^{[\lambda]}_{t}f\|_{{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}}\leq\|f\|_{{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
t\in(0,\,\infty)
;

{\rm(S_{ii})}

P^{[\lambda]}_{t}f\geq 0
for all
f\geq 0
and
t\in(0,\infty)
;

{\rm(S_{iii})}

P^{[\lambda]}_{t}(1)=1
for all
t\in(0,\,\infty)
.

Next we recall the definitions of the Poisson kernel and conjugate Poisson kernel, see
[
3
]
.
For any
t,\,x,\,y\in(0,\infty)
,

P^{[\lambda]}_{t}f(x):={\int_{0}^{\infty}}P^{[\lambda]}_{t}(x,y)f(y)\,dm_{\lambda}(y),

where

P^{[\lambda]}_{t}(x,y)=\frac{2\lambda t}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(\sin\theta)^{2\lambda-1}}{(x^{2}+y^{2}+t^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}\,d\theta.

If
f\in{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\,dm_{\lambda})}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, the
\Delta_{\lambda}
-conjugate of
f
is defined by setting,
for any
t,\,x,\,y\in(0,\infty)
,

{Q^{[\lambda]}_{t}}(f)(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}{Q^{[\lambda]}_{t}}(x,y)f(y)\,dm_{\lambda}(y),

(2.1)

where

\begin{array}[b]{cl}{Q^{[\lambda]}_{t}}(x,y)&:=-\dfrac{2\lambda}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\dfrac{(x-y\cos\theta)(\sin\theta)^{2\lambda-1}}{(x^{2}+y^{2}+t^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}\,d\theta;\end{array}

see
[
26

),

, i.e.,

\displaystyle{R_{\Delta_{\lambda}}}(f)(x):=\lim_{t\to 0}{Q^{[\lambda]}_{t}}(f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}-\dfrac{2\lambda}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\dfrac{(x-y\cos\theta)(\sin\theta)^{2\lambda-1}}{(x^{2}+y^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}\,d\theta\ f(y){dm_{\lambda}}(y).

(2.2)

38

Proposition 2.5

Let

(b)

For every

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}2^{-k\beta}}\int_{I}|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{1})(y)|dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}2^{-k\beta}}\|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{1})\|_{L^{\bar{s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\ m_{\lambda}(I)^{1-\frac{1}{\bar{s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{1}{s}}2^{-k\beta}}\|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{1})\|_{L^{\bar{s}}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

Then we use
[
28
, Theorem 2.1]
to obtain

\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}2^{-k\beta}}\int_{B}|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{1})(y)|dm_{\lambda}(y)\lesssim\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{1}{s}}2^{-k\beta}}\|(b-b_{I})f_{1}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

It is easy to check

\displaystyle{1\over m_{\lambda}(I)}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|b(x^{\prime})-b_{I}|^{s}|f_{1}(x^{\prime})|^{s}dm_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim{1\over m_{\lambda}(2I)}\int_{2I}|b(x^{\prime})-b_{I}|^{s}|f(x^{\prime})|^{s}dm_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sup_{y\in 2I}|b(y)-b_{I}|\bigg)^{s}{1\over{m_{\lambda}(2I)}}\int_{2I}|f(x^{\prime})|^{s}dm_{\lambda}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sup_{y\in 2I}{1\over m_{\lambda}(I)}\int_{I}|b(y)-b(z)|dm_{\lambda}(z)\bigg)^{s}M^{s}_{s}(f)(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(2^{-k\beta}\sup_{y\in 2I}{1\over m_{\lambda}(I)}\int_{I}\sup_{|y-z|\neq 0}{|b(y)-b(z)|\over|y-z|^{\beta}}dm_{\lambda}(z)\bigg)^{s}M^{s}_{s}(f)(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}^{s}2^{-ks\beta}M^{s}_{s}(f)(x).

We see that

\displaystyle\|(b-b_{I})f_{1}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}2^{-k\beta}m_{\lambda}(I)^{1\over s}M_{s}(f)(x).

Thus, we have that

\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I)^{1+\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}2^{-k\beta}}\int_{I}|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{1})(y)|dm_{\lambda}(y)\\
\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M_{s}(f)(x)

and hence

I\!I\!I_{2}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|M_{s}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

Finally, we estimate
I\!I\!I_{3}
. By (
5.2
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}}\bigg|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{2})(y)-{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{2})(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}[K^{\alpha}(y,z)-K^{\alpha}(x,z)](b(z)-b_{I})f_{2}(z)dm_{\lambda}(z)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{|x-y|}{|x-z|}\frac{|x-z|^{\alpha}}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,|x-z|))}|b(z)-b_{I}||f_{2}(z)|dm_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}}\int_{(2I)^{c}}\frac{|x-y|}{|x-z|}\frac{|x-z|^{\alpha}}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,|x-z|))}|b(z)-b_{I}||f(z)|dm_{\lambda}(z).

Since

m_{\lambda}(I(x,r))\sim x^{2\lambda}r+r^{\mathbf{Q}}\geq r^{\mathbf{Q}},

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}m_{\lambda}(I)^{\frac{\alpha}{\mathbf{Q}}}}\bigg|{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{2})(y)-{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}((b-b_{I})f_{2})(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{j-k}<|x-z|\leq 2^{j-k+1}}2^{-j(1-\alpha)}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,|x-z|))}|b(z)-b_{I}||f(z)|dm_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,2^{j-k}))}\int_{|x-z|\leq 2^{j+1-k}}2^{-j(1-\alpha)}\big(|b(z)-b_{2^{j}I}|+|b_{2^{j}I}-b_{I}|\big)|f(z)|dm_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{-k\beta}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-j(1-\alpha)}}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,2^{j-k}))}\int_{|x-z|\leq 2^{j-k+1}}|b(z)-b_{2^{j}I}||f(z)|dm_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j(1-\alpha)}\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}2^{(j-k)\beta}M(f)(x)j

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M_{ts}(f)(x)+\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M(f)(x)\Big)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j(1-\alpha-\beta)}j

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M_{ts}(f)(x)+\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}M(f)(x)\Big)

for
\alpha+\beta<1
.

Hence, we have

I\!I\!I_{3}\lesssim\|b\|_{\Lambda^{\beta}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}

for
\alpha+\beta<1
.
Since
{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\alpha/2}{\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-\beta/2}={\triangle_{\lambda}}^{-{(\alpha+\beta)}/2}
, the assumption
\alpha+\beta<1
can be removed by the method in
[
29
, Facts 1.9 and 1.10 ]
. The proof is complete.
∎

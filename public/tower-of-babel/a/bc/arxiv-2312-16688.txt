Wigner 6 ​ j 6j symbols with gluon lines: completing the set of 6j symbols required for color decomposition Preprint: MCnet-23-24
Stefan Keppeler, Simon Plätzer, Malin Sjodahl

Abstract
We construct a set of Wigner 6 ​ j 6j symbols with gluon lines (adjoint representations) in closed form, expressed in terms of similar 6 ​ j 6j symbols with quark lines (fundamental representations). Together with Wigner 6 ​ j 6j symbols with quark lines, this gives a set of 6 ​ j 6j symbols sufficient for treating QCD color structure for any number of external particles, in or beyond perturbation theory. This facilitates a complete treatment of QCD color structure in terms of orthogonal multiplet bases, without the need of ever explicitly constructing the corresponding bases. We thereby open up for a completely representation theory based treatment of SU(N) color structure, with the potential of significantly speeding up the color structure treatment.

VI
Conclusions and outlook

In the present paper we have shown how to calculate a
set of Wigner
6j
coefficients with adjoint representations.
Together with a set of previously derived
6j
s
Alcock-Zeilinger et al. 2023
,
this set constitutes a complete set of
6j
s required to decompose any
color structure, to any order into orthogonal multiplet
bases, cf. Eq. (
10
).

This opens up for the usage of orthogonal representation theory based
color bases also for processes with high multiplicities, including the
analysis of evolution equations in color space

Plätzer 2023
.

We note, however, that the present work does not close
the research area of representation theory based treatment
of color structure. In particular, more general
6j
symbols
are required for fully general multiplet bases (with vertices
between general representations). We believe that this
can be addressed with similar methods.

Appendix D
Vertex normalizations leading to non-trivial
3j
symbols

All explicit formulae for Wigner
6j
symbols in this article, in
particular the results in Sec.
V
, are valid for
vertices normalized such that all non-vanishing
3j
symbols are equal
to
1
.
While this normalization is convenient, it differs from normalizations
typically applied in the context of QCD.
We therefore here give a simple rule for how
to transform any of our
6j
symbols when changing the normalization
of any
3j
symbol.

Assume we have calculated the
6j
symbol

\hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{83.44908pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{6j-with-alpha-beta-gamma--Vbullet}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt\,,

(100)

whereby we chose the normalization

\hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{49.30551pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{3j-alpha-beta-gamma}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt=1\,.

(101)

If we prefer this
3j
symbol to be equal to
C\neq 1
we define a vertex

\hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{67.30777pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{Vertex-betaOut-gammaIn-alphaIn--Vsquare}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt=\sqrt{C}\ \hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{67.30777pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{Vertex-betaOut-gammaIn-alphaIn}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt\,,

(102)

which then satisfies

\hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{51.58202pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{3j-alpha-beta-gamma--Vsquare}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt=C\ \hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{49.30551pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{3j-alpha-beta-gamma}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt=C\,.

(103)

Consequently,

\hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{84.58733pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{6j-with-alpha-beta-gamma--Vsquare}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt=\sqrt{C}\ \hskip-0.76823pt\parbox{83.44908pt}{\hskip 2.15277pt\includegraphics[]{{6j-with-alpha-beta-gamma--Vbullet}}\hskip 2.15277pt}\hskip-0.76823pt\,.

(104)

In short:
For each vertex whose
3j
symbol you normalize to a number

\neq 1
multiply our
6j
symbol by the square root of the value of
your
3j
symbol in order to obtain the value of the
6j
symbol with
your normalization convention.

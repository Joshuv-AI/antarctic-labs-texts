Precise large deviations of sums of widely dependent random variables and its applications
Zhaolei Cui and Yuebao Wang 1 School of mathematics and statistics, Changshu Institute of Technology, Suzhou 215000, China2 School of Mathematical Sciences, Soochow University, Suzhou 215006, China

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we obtain some results on precise large deviations for non-random and random sums
of widely dependent random variables with common dominatedly varying tail distribution or consistently varying tail distribution
on
(-\infty,\infty)
.
Then we apply the results to reinsurance and insurance and give some asymptotic estimates on proportional reinsurance, random-time ruin probability
and the finite-time ruin probability.

Keywords:
precise large deviation; widely dependent random variables;
dominatedly varying tail distribution; consistently varying tail distribution;
proportional reinsurance; random-time ruin probability; finite-time ruin probability

2000 Mathematics Subject Classification:
Primary 60F10; 60F05; 60G50

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
X_{i},\ i\geq 1
be random variables
with common distribution
F
on
(-\infty,\infty)
, and
S_{\tau}=\sum_{k=1}^{\tau}X_{k}
be the random sum generated by
X_{i},\ i\geq 1
and a nonnegative integer valued random variable
\tau
.
In particular, if
\tau=n
, then
S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}
is called the partial sum or non-random sum for all
n\geq 0
, where
S_{0}=0
.
Here, we call the random sum and the non-random sum together as the sum of random variables.

It is well known that, the precise large deviation of the sums of random variables is a important part of the large deviation theory.
One of the basic research objects is the uniform asymptotic of
P(S_{n}>x)
for
x\geq\gamma n
as
n\to\infty
where
\gamma
is any positive constant.
Compared with the corresponding research of
\ln P(S_{n}>x)
, the result of
P(S_{n}>x)
is more precise,
meanwhile, the latter frequently needs stronger conditions than the former for the common distribution and mutual relationship of random variables.
The purpose of this paper is to study the precise large deviation of the sums of some dependent random variables.
Thus, before giving the main results,
we first introduce the concepts of related distribution class and dependent structure of random variables in this section.

1.1
Some distribution classes

In this paper, all limit relations refer to
x\to\infty
without a special statement.
Let two functions
g_{1}(\cdot)
and
g_{2}(\cdot)
be positive eventually.
We set
g_{1,2}=\limsup\frac{g_{1}(x)}{g_{2}(x)}
.
Then
g_{1}(x)=O\big(g_{2}(x)\big)
means
g_{1,2}<\infty
,
g_{1}(x)\lesssim\ (\text{or}\ \gtrsim)\ g_{2}(x)
means
g_{1,2}\ \big(\text{or}\ g_{2,1}\big)\leq 1
,
g_{1}(x)\sim g_{2}(x)
means
g_{1,2}=g_{2,1}=1
, and
g_{1}(x)=o\big(g_{2}(x)\big)
means
g_{1,2}=0
.
In addition, we denote the positive part of random variable
X
by
X^{+}=X\textbf{1}_{\{X\geq 0\}}
,
the negative part by
X^{-}=-X\textbf{1}_{\{X<0\}}
, the tail of distribution
F
by
\overline{F}=1-F
, and the
n
-fold convolution of
F
with itself by
F^{*n},\ n\geq 1
, where
F^{*1}=F
.

We say that the distribution class

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\big\{F:\overline{F}(x-t)\sim\overline{F}(x)\ \text{for each}\ t\in(-\infty,\infty)\big\}

is long-tailed, and the distribution class

\displaystyle{\cal S}=\big\{F\in\mathcal{L}:\overline{F^{*2}}(x)\sim 2\overline{F}(x)\big\}.

is subexponential.

In addition, we set

{\overline{F}}_{*}(y)=\liminf\frac{{\overline{F}}(xy)}{{\overline{F}}(x)},~~{\overline{F}}^{*}(y)=\limsup\frac{{\overline{F}}(xy)}{{\overline{F}}(x)}
for each
y>0

and
L_{F}=\lim\limits_{y\downarrow 1}\overline{F}_{*}(y)
.
Then we say that, the distribution classes

\displaystyle{\cal D}=\big\{F:{\overline{F}}^{*}(y)<\infty\ \text{for each}\ y>1\big\},\ \ \ {\cal C}=\{F:L_{F}=1\}\ \ \ \text{and}

\mathcal{ERV}=\bigcup_{0<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty}\mathcal{ERV}(\alpha,\beta)=\bigcup_{0<\alpha\leq\beta<\infty}\{F:y^{-\beta}\leq\overline{F}_{*}(y)\leq\overline{F}^{*}(y)\leq y^{-\alpha}\ \text{for each}\ y>1\}

are dominated varying tailed, consistently varying tailed and extended regularly varying tailed, respectively.
Particularly, if
\alpha=\beta
, then
\mathcal{ERV}(\alpha,\beta)
reduces to the regularly varying tailed distribution class, denoted
by
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
.
Some properties of these distributions are introduced by the following two propositions.

Proposition 1.1
.

1) The following relations hold:

F\in\mathcal{D}\Longleftrightarrow\overline{F}_{*}(y)>0\ for\ each\ y>1\Longleftrightarrow\overline{F}_{*}(y)>0\ for\ some\ y>1

2) The following inclusion relations are proper:

\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{ERV}(\alpha,\beta)\subset\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{L}.

Further, we denote the moment index of
F
by
I_{F}=\sup\{s:\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{s}F(dy)<\infty\}
, and
the upper Matuszewska index and lower Matuszewska index of distribution
F
by

\displaystyle J_{F}^{+}=-\lim\limits_{y\to\infty}\frac{\ln\overline{F}_{*}(y)}{\ln y}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ J_{F}^{-}=-\lim\limits_{y\to\infty}\frac{\ln\overline{F}^{*}(y)}{\ln y}.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
F
be a distribution in class
\mathcal{D}
.

1) If a constant
p<J_{F}^{-}
, then
\overline{F}(x)=o(x^{-p})
.

2) If a constant
p>J_{F}^{+}
, then
x^{-p}=o\big(\overline{F}(x)\big)
, and there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{F}(x)}{\overline{F}(y)}\leq C_{1}\Big(\frac{x}{y}\Big)^{p},\ \ y\geq x\geq C_{2}.

3)
0\leq J_{F}^{-}\leq I_{F}\leq J_{F}^{+}<\infty
.

The above concepts and corresponding properties can be found in some references,
such as Feller
[
9
]
, Bingham et al.
[
2
]
, Cline and Samorodnitsky
[
6
]
, Embrechts et al.
[
8
]
, and Tang and Tsitsiashvili
[
36
]
.

1.2
Some dependent structures

Based on the notion of negatively orthant dependence structure of random variables,
Wang et al.
[
43
]
introduced the notion of widely orthant dependence structure.

By definition,
X_{i},i\geq 1
are said to be widely upper orthant dependent (WUOD),
if for each
n\geq 1
, there exists some positive number
g_{U}(n)
such that,

\displaystyle P\Big(\bigcap^{n}_{i=1}\{X_{i}>x_{i}\}\Big)\leq g_{U}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{n}P(X_{i}>x_{i})\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ x_{i}\in(-\infty,\infty)\ \text{and}\ 1\leq i\leq n;

(1.1)

they are said to be widely lower orthant dependent (WLOD),
if for each
n\geq 1
, there exists some positive number
g_{L}(n)
such that,

\displaystyle P\Big(\bigcap^{n}_{i=1}\{X_{i}\leq x_{i}\}\Big)\leq g_{L}(n)\prod_{i=1}^{n}P(X_{i}\leq x_{i})\ \ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ x_{i}\in(-\infty,\infty)\ \text{and}\ 1\leq i\leq n;

(1.2)

and they are said to be widely orthant dependent (WOD) if they are both WUOD and WLOD.
WUOD, WLOD and WOD structures can be called widely dependent (WD) as a joint name.
And
g_{U}(n),~g_{L}(n)
,
n\geq 1
, are called dominating coefficients.

Clearly,
g_{U}(n)\geq 1,~g_{L}(n)\geq 1,n\geq 2
and
g_{U}(1)=g_{L}(1)=1
.
And some basic properties of WOD random variables are as follows, see Proposition 1.1 of Wang et al.
[
43
]
.

Proposition 1.3
.

1) Let
X_{i},i\geq 1
be WLOD (WUOD). If
f_{i}(\cdot),i\geq 1
are nondecreasing,
then
f_{i}(X_{i}),i\geq 1
are still WLOD (WUOD);
if
f_{i}(\cdot),i\geq 1
are nonincreasing, then
f_{i}(X_{i}),i\geq 1
are WUOD (WLOD).

2) If
X_{i},i\geq 1
are nonnegative and WUOD, then for each
n\geq 1
,

E\prod^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}\leq g_{U}(n)\prod^{n}_{i=1}EX_{i}.

In particular, for each
n\geq 1
and any
s>0
,

Ee^{s\sum^{n}_{i=1}X_{i}}\leq g_{U}(n)\prod^{n}_{i=1}Ee^{s{X_{i}}}.

Further, Wang et al.
[
43
]
provided some examples of WD random variables,
which showed that the WD structure may contain common negatively dependent random variables,
some positively dependent random variables, and some others.
For example, when
g_{U}(n)=g_{L}(n)=M
for all
n\geq 1
and some positive constant
M
,
inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) describe extended negatively upper and lower orthant dependent (ENUOD and ENLOD) random variables, respectively.

X_{i},i\geq 1
are said to be extended negatively orthant dependent (ENOD) if they are both ENUOD and ENLOD.
ENOD, ENUOD, ENLOD random variables are called collectively END r.v.s, see Liu
[
20
]
.
More specially, if
M=1
, then we have the corresponding notions of NUOD, NLOD, NOD and ND random variables,
see, for example, Ebrahimi and Ghosh
[
7
]
and Block et al.
[
3
]
.

For the research on WD random variables, in addition to
[
43
]
,
please see Wang and Cheng
[
37
]
and Wang and Cheng
[
38
]
for renewal theory,
Liu et al.
[
21
]
and Wang et al.
[
39
]
for risk theory,
Wang et al.
[
45
]
for precise large deviation theory,
Qiu and Chen
[
32
]
, Wang et al.
[
44
]
, Naderi et al.
[
25
]
, Wang and Hu
[
40
]
, Chen et al.
[
4
]
,
Istv
\acute{a}
n et al.
[

]
and Wu et al.
[
46
]
for limit theory and statistical theory, among others.

Besides, there are also many results on ND and END random variables, which will not be described in detail here.

1.3
Main results

Some earlier work on the precise large deviation for non-random sums of independent and identically distributed random variables
can be found in Heyde
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{H1967a}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{H1967b}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{H1968}{}{}]}
and Nagaev
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{N1969a}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{N1969b}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{N1969c}{}{}]}
.
With the development of the research of precise deviation, the classic case of the common distribution
F\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
on
[0,\infty)
is attributed to Nagaev
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{N1973}{}{}]},\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{N1979}{}{}]}
; the case of
F\in\mathcal{ERV}(\alpha,\beta)
can be referred to Cline and Hsing
[
5
]
,
Klüppelberg and Mikosch
[
16
]
, Mikosch and Nagaev
[
24
]
, Tang et al.
[
35
]
, among others;
and the case of
F\in\mathcal{C}
was discussed by Ng et al.
[
31
]
and Wang and Wang
[
41
]
.

For dependent random variables, there are also many corresponding results, see for example, Konstantinides and Mikosch
[
17
]
geted some important results on the precise large deviation of sums for a stochastic recurrence equation;
later, Mikosch and Wintenberger
[
23
]
studied the same problem for a stationary regularly varying sequence of random variables.
In this paper, we focus on another kind of dependent structure for random variables.
Theorem 3.1 of Tang
[
34
]
studies the precise large deviation of NOD random variables
with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{C}
on
(-\infty,\infty)
.
Thereafter, Theorem 2.1 of Liu
[
20
]
replaces the condition
xF(-x)=o\big(\overline{F}(x)\big)
in
[
34
]
with the condition

\displaystyle F(-x)=o\big(\overline{F}(x)\big)

(1.3)

and gets same result for ENOD random variables.
In addition, Theorem 2.1 of Wang et al.
[
42
]
starts to study the precise large deviation for
F\in\mathcal{D}
on
[0,\infty)
.
Generally, Theorem 1 of Wang et al.
[
45
]
discusses the case of WOD random variables with different distributions.
However, just like
[
34
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
45
]
still require the restriction of moment that

E(X_{1}^{-})^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
.
And in
[
45
]
, the range of the dominating coefficients of WOD random variables is too small
to obtain the corresponding result as the following (
1.4
).
In our view, Corollary 1 of Wang et al.
[
45
]
gives such a result for identically distributed WOD random variables that,
for each integer
m\geq 1
and
v\in\big(0,m(m+1)^{-1}\big)
,
if
\max\{g_{U}(n),\ g_{L}(n)\}=O(n^{m^{-1}})
for all
n\geq 1
, then for each
\gamma>0
,

\overline{F}_{*}(v^{-1})\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\inf\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\leq\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\leq\overline{F}_{*}^{-1}(v^{-1}).

Clearly, when
v\uparrow 1
,
\overline{F}_{*}(v^{-1})\uparrow L_{F}
and
m\uparrow\infty
, the latter implies that

X_{i},\ i\geq 1
be ENOD.

In the above all results, the condition that
\mu=EX_{1}=0
is required.

Extending on the known results in
[
34
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
42
]
and
[
45
]
, we study the precise large deviations of dependent random variables
with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}
or
F\in\mathcal{C}
on
(-\infty,\infty)

for the two cases that
E|X_{1}|<\infty
or
EX_{1}^{-}<\infty
and
E(X_{1}^{+})^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
.
For the former, we study it for ENOD random variables without the requirements
E(X_{1}^{-})^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
and
\mu=0
.
Of course, this result includes Theorem 2.1 of Liu
[
20
]
for the case that
\mu=0
.
For the latter, we significantly expand the range of the dominating coefficients for WOD random variables with mean
\mu=0
and give the standard and meaningful result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{i},i\geq 1
be ENOD random variables with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}
on
(-\infty,\infty)
.
If
E|X_{1}|<\infty
and the condition (
1.3
) is satisfied, then for each
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle L_{F}\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\inf\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\leq\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\leq L_{F}^{-1}.\ \

(1.4)

In particular, if
F\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then for each
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\Big|\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}-1\Big|=0.\ \

(1.5)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X_{i},i\geq 1
be WOD random variables with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}
on
(-\infty,\infty)
.
Assume that
E(X_{1}^{+})^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
,
\mu=0
and (
1.3
) is satisfied.

1) If there is some
a>0
such that
g_{U}(n)=O(n^{a})
, then the asymptotic upper bound of (
1.4
) holds for each
\gamma>0
.

2) If there is some
b>0
such that
g_{L}(n)=O(n^{b})
and
g_{U}(n)=O(n^{r-1})
, then the asymptotic lower bound of (
1.4
) holds for each
\gamma>0
.

3) If all conditions in 1) and 2) are satisfied, then (
1.4
) holds for each
\gamma>0
.
Further, if
F\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then (
1.5
) holds for each
\gamma>0
.

We prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
in Section 2.
Further, we investigate the precise large deviations for random sums of WOD random variables in Section 3.
Finally, in Section 4 we apply the above results to insurance and reinsurance
and give some asymptotic estimates on proportional reinsurance, random-time ruin probability
and the finite-time ruin probability.
In addition, we try to explain the meaning of WOD structure of random variables in a nonstandard renewal risk model.

2
Proofs of the main results

2.1
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Because
L_{F}=1
for
F\in{\mathcal{C}}
, we just need to prove (
1.4
) for each
\gamma>0
.
To this end, we need the following two lemmas for nonnegative random variables.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X_{i},i\geq 1
be nonnegative WUOD random variables with common distribution
F
.
If
F\in\mathcal{D}
and

\displaystyle g_{U}(n)n\overline{F}(n)\to 0,\ \ \ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty,

(2.1)

then for each
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\geq 1.

(2.2)

Proof.

According to the above conditions, it holds uniformly for all
x\geq\gamma n
that

\displaystyle P(S_{n}>x)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}P(X_{i}>x)-\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}P(X_{i}>x,X_{j}>x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\overline{F}(x)\big(1-g_{U}(n)n\overline{F}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\overline{F}(x)\big(1-g_{U}(n)n\overline{F}(\gamma n)\big)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle n\overline{F}(x),\ \ \ \ \text{as}\ \ n\to\infty.

Therefore, (
2.2
) holds.

\square

Clearly, the condition (
2.1
) implies
x\overline{F}(x)\to 0
,
which is slightly weaker than the condition that
\mu=EX_{1}<\infty
.

The following lemma is different from Theorem 3.1 of Ng et al.
[
31
]
, where the main research object is
P(S_{n}-n\mu>x)
.
However, the former plays an important role in the application, see Proposition
4.1
-Proposition

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X_{i},i\geq 1
be nonnegative ENUOD random variables with common distribution
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
and finite mean
\mu
.
Then for each
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle 1\leq\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\inf\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\leq\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{n\overline{F}(x)}\leq L_{F}^{-1}.\ \ \

(2.3)

In particulary, if
F\in{\mathcal{C}}
, then for each
\gamma>0
, (
1.5
) holds.

Proof.

We also only prove (
2.3
) for each
\gamma>0
.

First, according to Lemma
2.1
, by
\mu<\infty
and
g_{U}(n)=M
for some

, we immediately give the asymptotic lower bound of (
2.3
) for each
\gamma>0
.

Nexst, we prove the asymptotic upper bound of (
2.3
).
There are only a few small differences between the following proof and the proof of Theorem 3.1 in Ng et al.
[
31
]
.
For the sake of completeness, we still give the details of the proof.
For any constant
v\in(0,1)
, we denote
\overline{X_{i}}=\min\{X_{i},vx\},\ i\geq 1
with common distribution
F_{1}
,
and
\overline{S_{n}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\overline{X_{i}},\ n\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle P(S_{n}>x)\leq n\overline{F}(vx)+P(\overline{S_{n}}>x).

(2.4)

In order to estimate
P(\overline{S_{n}}>x)
in (
2.4
), we define an function

\displaystyle a(x)=\max\{-\ln\big(n\overline{F}(vx)\big),1\},\ \ \ x\in[0,\infty).

By

x\overline{F}(x)\to 0
, thus

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\inf\limits_{x\geq\gamma n}a(x)\geq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}-\ln(v\gamma)^{-1}\big(v\gamma n\overline{F}(v\gamma n)\big)=\infty.

For any two constants
\tau>1
and

such that

\displaystyle s(x)=\frac{a(x)-\rho\tau\ln a(x)}{vx}>0,\ \ \ x\in[x_{1},\infty)\ \ \text{for some}\ x_{1}>0.

Because

are also ENUOD, by Proposition
1.2
2) and inequality

,
we know that, for all integers

,

\displaystyle\frac{P(\overline{S_{n}}>x)}{n\overline{F}(vx)}\leq Me^{-s(x)x+a(x)}\big(Ee^{s(x)\overline{X_{1}}}\big)^{n}

(2.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Me^{-s(x)x+a(x)}\Big(\int_{0-}^{vx}(e^{s(x)y}-1)F_{1}(dy)+\int_{vx}^{\infty}(e^{s(x)vx}-1)F_{1}(dy)+1\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Me^{-s(x)x+a(x)+n\int_{0-}^{vx}(e^{s(x)y}-1)F_{1}(dy)}=Me^{I(n,x)}.\ \ \

Further, we estimate
n\int_{0-}^{vx}(e^{s(x)y}-1)F_{1}(dy)
.
According to Proposition
1.2

e^{u}-1\leq ue^{u},\ u\in[0,\infty)
,
F\in{\cal D}
and

,

\displaystyle n\int_{0-}^{vx}(e^{s(x)y}-1)F_{1}(dy)=n\Big(\int_{0-}^{vxa^{-\tau}(x)}+\int_{vxa^{-\tau}(x)}^{vx}\Big)(e^{s(x)y}-1)F_{1}(dy)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n\int_{0-}^{vxa^{-\tau}(x)}s(x)ye^{s(x)y}F(dy)+ne^{s(x)vx}\overline{F}\big(vxa^{-\tau}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu ns(x)e^{s(x)vxa^{-\tau}(x)}+C_{2}ne^{s(x)vx}a^{\rho\tau}(x)\overline{F}(vx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu ns(x)e^{a^{1-\tau}(x)}+C_{2}

Then by (

),
\tau>1

, for each

\displaystyle I(n,x)\leq\mu ns(x)e^{a^{1-\tau}(x)}+C_{2}-s(x)x+a(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu\gamma^{-1}v^{-1}vxs(x)e^{a^{1-\tau}(x)}+C_{2}-v^{-1}vxs(x)+a(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\gamma^{-1}v^{-1}\mu e^{a^{1-\tau}(x)}-\big(v^{-1}-\frac{\rho\tau\ln a(x)}{a(x)}-1\big)\Big)a(x)+C_{2}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}v^{-1}\mu-(v^{-1}-1)a(x)+C_{2},\ \ \ \ x\geq\gamma n\ \text{and}\ n\to\infty.

Thus, by (
2.5
), (
2.7
),
F\in{\cal D}
and (
2.1
), it holds uniformly for all

4
Some applications

In this section, we specially introduce a quasi renewal counting process of a nonstandard renewal risk model for some insurance companies.

In this model, let the claim sizes
Y_{i},i\geq 1
be WOD random variables with the common distribution
G
on
[0,\infty)
,
the finite mean
\mu_{G}
and the dominating coefficients
g_{U}^{G}(n)
and
g_{L}^{G}(n),\ n\geq 1
;
the inter-arrival times
Z_{i},i\geq 1
also be WOD random variables with common distribution
H
on
[0,\infty)
,
the finite mean
\mu_{H}
and the dominating coefficients
g_{U}^{H}(n)
and
g_{L}^{H}(n),\ n\geq 1
.
And
Y_{i},i\geq 1
are independent of
Z_{i},i\geq 1
.
The times of successive claims,
\tau_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i},n\geq 1
, constitute a quasi renewal counting process

N(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\textbf{1}_{\{\tau_{n}\leq\ t\}}
and the corresponding renewal function
\lambda(t)=EN(t),\ t\geq 0
.
For convenience, we might as well assume that
\inf\{t:\lambda(t)>0\}=0
.
In addition, let
c>0
be the constant interest rate and
x>0
be the initial capital of the insurance company.
In order to ensure the normal operation of the insurance company,
a safe load condition that
\mu_{G}<c\mu_{H}
is usually assumed.

Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.4 of Wang and Cheng
[
37
]
or Theorem 4 of Chen et al.
[
4
]

established the following results for the counting process
N(t)
:

Lemma 4.1
.

In the above nonstandard renewal risk model, it is specially assumed that
Z_{i},\ i\geq 1
are ENOD random variables,
then

\displaystyle\lambda(t)\sim\mu_{H}^{-1}t\ \ \text{and}\ \ N(t)\sim\mu_{H}^{-1}t,\ a.s.,\ \ \text{thus}\ \ N(t)\sim\lambda(t),\ a.s.\ \ \text{as}\ \ t\to\infty.

(4.1)

In the following result, 1) is from Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.4 of Wang and Cheng
[
38
]
,
which improves the corresponding results of Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.4 in Wang and Cheng
[
37
]
;
2) is from Lemma 2.2 of Wang and Cheng
[
37
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the above nonstandard renewal risk model, if
EZ_{1}^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
,

g_{U}^{H}(n)=o(n^{a})
for some
a>0
and
g_{L}^{H}(n)=o(n^{b})
for some
b>0,\ n\to\infty
,
then

1) (
4.1
) holds;

2) (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) hold for any
a>0
and
p>J_{G}^{+}
.

Here, we note that, when
N(t)=n
for given
t>0
, because
\tau_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z_{i}\leq t
,

Z_{i},1\leq i\leq n
are in a certain kind of negative dependence.
Therefore, the hypothesis that
Z_{i},i\geq 1
are ENOD or WOD is reasonable.

In the following, we only study the case that
Y_{i},i\geq 1
are ENOD random variables with common distribution
G\in\mathcal{C}
and omit the corresponding results for WOD random variables.
It should be pointed out that the former has the non-centralization shape,
which can better describe practical problems.

4.1
Proportional reinsurance

In the above nonstandard renewal risk model, at time
t\geq 0
, a reinsurance company covers percent
q_{1}\cdot 100
of the total claim amount
S_{N(t)}^{G}=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}Y_{i}
for some
q_{1}\in(0,1)
;
the reinsurance company benefits percent
q_{2}\cdot 100
of the total income
cS_{N(t)}^{H}=c\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}Z_{i}
for some
q_{2}\in(0,1)
.
Then we respectively call the proportional reinsurance and the proportional net loss at time
t\geq 0
for the reinsurance company by

R_{11}(t)=q_{1}S_{N(t)}^{G}
and

R_{12}(t)=q_{1}S_{N(t)}^{G}-q_{2}cS_{N(t)}^{H}=q_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}\big(Y_{i}-q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}cZ_{i}\big)=:q_{1}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{i}.

Let
F
be the distribution of
X_{1}
and
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\ n\geq 1
.
Clearly,
F
is supported on
(-\infty,\infty)
.

Now, we give some results on precise deviation of the above two objects.

Proposition 4.1
.

In the above nonstandard renewal risk model, let
Y_{i},i\geq 1
be ENOD random variables
and
Z_{i},i\geq 1
be ENOD random variables.
If
EZ_{1}^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
, then for each
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma\lambda(t)}\Big|\frac{P\big(R_{11}(t)>x\big)}{\lambda(t)\overline{G}(q_{1}^{-1}x)}-1\Big|=0

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma\lambda(t)}\Big|\frac{P\big(R_{12}(t)>x\big)}{\lambda(t)\overline{G}(q_{1}^{-1}x)}-1\Big|=0.

(4.3)

Proof.

We only prove (
4.3
).
Along the same line of Theorem
3.1
, we take any fixed constant
\delta\in(0,1)
and split
P\big(R_{12}(t)>x\big)
as follows:

\displaystyle P\big(R_{12}(t)>x\big)=P\big(S_{N(t)}>q_{1}^{-1}x\big)

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{0\leq n<(1-\delta)\lambda(t)}+\sum\limits_{(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\leq n\leq(1+\delta)\lambda(t)}+\sum\limits_{n>(1+\delta)\lambda(t)}\Big)P\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(Y_{i}-q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}cZ_{i}\big)>q_{1}^{-1}x,N(t)=n\Big)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{11}(x,t)+\Sigma_{12}(x,t)+\Sigma_{13}(x,t).

For
\Sigma_{11}(x,t)
, similar to the proof of (
3.6
), according to Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
4.1
,
it holds uniformly for all
x\geq\gamma\lambda(t)\geq\gamma q_{1}\lfloor(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\rfloor
that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{11}(x,t)\leq P\Big(\sum_{0\leq i<(1-\delta)\lambda(t)}Y_{i}>q_{1}^{-1}x\Big)P\big(N(t)<(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\big)=o\big(\lambda(t)\overline{G}(x)\big),\ \ \text{as}\ t\to\infty.

(4.5)

For
\Sigma_{12}(x,t)
, we denote the set

A_{n+1}=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\leq t,\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}z_{i}>t,z_{i}\geq 0,1\leq i\leq n+1\Big\},\ \ n\geq 1.

According to (
1.5
) of Theorem
1.1
and dominated convergence theorem, respectively,
when
t\to\infty
, it holds uniformly for all
x\geq\gamma\lambda(t)\geq\frac{q_{1}\gamma}{1+\delta}n

and
(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\leq n\leq(1+\delta)\lambda(t)
that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{12}(x,t)=\sum\limits_{(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\leq n\leq(1+\delta)\lambda(t)}\int_{A_{n+1}}P\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}>\frac{x}{q_{1}}+\frac{q_{2}c}{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\Big)P(Z_{i}\in dz_{i},1\leq i\leq n+1)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\leq n\leq(1+\delta)\lambda(t)}\int_{A_{n+1}}n\overline{G}\Big(\frac{x}{q_{1}}+\frac{q_{2}c}{q_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\Big)P(Z_{i}\in dz_{i},1\leq i\leq n+1)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(1-\delta)\lambda(t)\leq n\leq(1+\delta)\lambda(t)}nP\big(N(t)=n\big)\overline{G}(q_{1}^{-1}x)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\lambda(t)\overline{G}(q_{1}^{-1}x)\ \ \text{as},\ t\to\infty.

For
\Sigma_{13}(x,t)
, by Lemma
4.2
and
EZ_{1}^{r}<\infty
for some
r>1
,
(
3.1
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) hold for any
a>0
and
p>J_{G}^{+}
.
Thus, by the proof of (
3.13
), we know that, it holds uniformly for all
x\geq\gamma\lambda(t)
that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{13}(x,t)=o\big(\lambda(t)\overline{G}(x)\big),\ \ \ \ \text{as}\ \ t\to\infty.

(4.7)

From (
4.4
)-(
4.7
), we know that (
4.3
) holds.

4.2
The random-time ruin probabilities

In standard renewal risk model, the finite-time ruin probability at some time
t\in[0,\infty)
is defined by

\displaystyle\psi(x;t)=P\Big(\sup\limits_{0\leq n\leq N(t)}S_{n}>x\Big).

(4.8)

Further, let
\tau
be a nonnegative integer valued random variable, which is independent of
Y_{i},Z_{i},i\geq 1
.
Then the random-time ruin probability is defined by

\displaystyle\psi(x;\tau)=P\Big(\sup\limits_{0\leq n\leq N(\tau)}S_{n}>x\Big),

(4.9)

where
q_{1}=q_{2}=1
such that
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}-cZ_{i}),\ n\geq 1
.

For these concepts and related research with heavy tailed claim sizes, please refer
to Asmussen
[
1
]
, Tang
[
33
]
, Leipus and Šiaulys
[
19
]
, Wang et al.
[
39
]
, and so on.

In this subsection, we use Theorem
1.1
to give the asymptotic estimation of random-time ruin probabilities for the above nonstandard renewal risk model.

Proposition 4.2
.

In the above nonstandard renewal risk model, let
Y_{i},i\geq 1
be ENOD random variables
and
Z_{i},i\geq 1
also be ENOD random variables.
If

\displaystyle P\big(N(\tau)>x\big)=o\big(\overline{G}(x)\big),

(4.10)

then

\displaystyle P\big(\psi(x;\tau)>x\big)\sim E\lambda(\tau)\overline{G}(x).

(4.11)

Proof.

We split
P\big(\psi(x;\tau)>x\big)
as follows:

\displaystyle P\big(\psi(x;\tau)>x\big)=\Big(\int_{[0,\infty)\cap\{x<t\}}+\int_{[0,\infty)\cap\{x\geq t\}}\Big)P\big(\sup_{0\leq n\leq N(t)}S_{n}>x\big)P(\tau\in dt)

(4.12)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}(x)+I_{2}(x),\ \ \ x\geq 0.

For
I_{1}(x)
, we first give the following fact.
Because
\tau
is independent of
N(x)
for each
x>0
, according to Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle P\big(N(\tau)>2^{-1}x\big)\geq P\big(N(\tau)>2^{-1}x,\ \tau>x\big)\geq P\big(N(x)>2^{-1}x\big)P(\tau>x)\sim P(\tau>x).

Then by (
4.10
) and
G\in\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{D}
, we know that

\displaystyle I_{1}(x)\leq P(\tau>x)\lesssim P\big(N(\tau)>2^{-1}x\big)=o\big(\overline{G}(2^{-1}x)\big)=o\big(\overline{G}(x)\big).

(4.13)

For
I_{2}(x)
, according to Theorem
1.1
, by
\overline{F}(x)\sim\overline{G}(x)
, there exists an integer
n_{0}\geq 1
such that, when
n\geq n_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq n}\max\Big\{\frac{P\big(S_{n}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)},\frac{P\big(S_{n}^{G}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)}\Big\}\leq 2;

(4.14)

when
1\leq n\leq n_{0}
, by (
4.14
),

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq n}\frac{P\big(S_{n}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)}\leq\sup_{x\geq n_{0}}\frac{P\big(S_{n}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)}+\sup_{n_{0}>x\geq n}\frac{P\big(S_{n}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq n_{0}}n_{0}\frac{P\big(S_{n_{0}}^{G}>x\big)}{n_{0}\overline{G}(x)}+\frac{1}{\overline{G}(n_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2n_{0}\overline{G}^{-1}(n_{0}).

Combining (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\geq n}\max\Big\{\frac{P\big(S_{n}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)},\frac{P\big(S_{n}^{G}>x\big)}{n\overline{G}(x)}\Big\}\leq 2n_{0}+\frac{1}{\overline{G}(n_{0})}\ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ n\geq 1.

(4.16)

Now we further split
I_{2}(x)
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{2}(x)=\Big(\int_{\{t:\ x\geq\max\{t,n)\}}+\int_{\{t:\ 0\leq t\leq x<n\}}\Big)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\big(N(t)=n\big)P\big(S_{n}>x\big)P(\tau\in dt)

(4.17)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{21}(x)+I_{22}(x).

We first deal with
I_{21}(x)
. By (
4.10
), we have
E\lambda(\tau)<\infty
.
According to (
4.16
),
E\lambda(\tau)<\infty
, dominated convergence theorem and
F\in\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{S}
, we know that

\displaystyle\lim\frac{I_{21}(x)}{\overline{G}(x)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\big(N(t)=n\big)\lim\frac{P(S_{n}>x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\textbf{1}_{\{t:\ x\geq\max\{t,n)\}}(t)P(\tau\in dt)

(4.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nP\big(N(t)=n\big)P(\tau\in dt)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E\lambda(\tau).

Next, we deal with
I_{22}(x)
.
By (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle I_{22}(x)\leq P\big(N(\tau)>x\big)=o\big(\overline{G}(x)\big).

(4.19)

Combining (
4.12
), (
4.13
), (
4.17
), (
4.18
) and (
4.19
), we know that (
4.11
) holds.

\hskip 0.0pt\Box

In particular, we take
\tau=t
for some
t>0
in Proposition
4.2
,
then we obtain an asymptotic formula of the finite-time ruin probability in the above nonstandard renewal risk model.

Corollary 4.1
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
4.2
, for each
t>0
,

\displaystyle P\big(\psi(x;t)>x\big)\sim\lambda(t)\overline{G}(x).

(4.20)

This example also shows that,
P\big(N(\tau)>x\big)=O\big(P(\tau>ax)\big)
is not true for any
a\in(0,1)
,
and many
\tau
satisfies the condition (
4.10
).

4.3
The finite-time ruin probabilities

Because the initial capital and operating time of an insurance company are very large,
it is necessary to study the precise large deviation of the finite-time ruin probability in the above nonstandard renewal risk model.

Proposition 4.3
.

Under the conditions of Proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\sup\limits_{x\geq\gamma\lambda(t)}\Big|\frac{\psi(x;t)}{\lambda(t)\overline{G}(x)}-1\Big|=0.

(4.21)

Proof.

On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle\psi(x;t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\Big(\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big(Y_{i}-cZ_{i}\big)>q_{1}^{-1}x,N(t)=n\Big)

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}>x\Big)P\big(N(t)=n\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P\big(S_{N(t)}^{G}>x\big).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\psi(x;t)\geq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(Y_{i}-cZ_{i}\big)>x,N(t)=n\Big)=P\big(S_{N(t)}>x\big).

(4.23)

Then, (
4.21
) holds after we use proof methods of Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
4.1
to deal with (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), respectively.

Now, we introduce the sequence space

\displaystyle h_{p}=\left\{x:\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(k|\Delta x_{k}|\right)^{p}<\infty~\textrm{ and }~\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}x_{k}=0\right\}\quad\quad(1<p<\infty)

Define the sequence

, which will be frequently used, by the

(2.1)

\displaystyle y_{k}=(Mx)_{k}=k(x_{k}-x_{k+1}).

\displaystyle m_{nk}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}n&,&\quad(n=k)\\
-n&,&\quad(n+1=k)\\

Theorem 2.2
.

h_{p}=\ell_{p}\cap\int bv^{p}=\ell_{p}\cap\int bv_{0}^{p}

Proof.

We consider

\displaystyle k\Delta x_{k}\leq x_{k}+\Delta(kx_{k}).

Then, for
x\in\ell_{p}\cap\int bv^{p}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k|\Delta x_{k}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|+\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\Delta(kx_{k})|

and from
|a+b|^{p}\leq 2^{p}\left(|a|^{p}+|b|^{p}\right),(1\leq p<\infty)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{p}|\Delta x_{k}|^{p}\leq 2^{p}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n}|x_{k}|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\Delta(kx_{k})|^{p}\right].

For each positive integer
r
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}k^{p}|\Delta x_{k}|^{p}\leq 2^{p}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{r}|x_{k}|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}|\Delta(kx_{k})|^{p}\right].

and as
r\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{p}|\Delta x_{k}|^{p}\leq 2^{p}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|x_{k}|^{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\Delta(kx_{k})|^{p}\right].

and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}x_{k}=0
. Then
x\in h_{p}
and

(2.6)

\displaystyle\ell_{p}\cap\int bv^{p}\subset h_{p}.

Let
x\in h_{p}
and we consider

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|x_{k+1}|^{p}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\Delta(kx_{k})|^{p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{p}|\Delta x_{k}|^{p}.

The the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|x_{k+1}|^{p}
is convergent from the definition of
\ell_{p}
. Also

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\Delta(kx_{k})|^{p}<\infty
and therefore
x\in\ell_{p}\cap\int bv^{p}
. Then

(2.7)

\displaystyle h_{p}\subset\ell_{p}\cap\int bv^{p}.

Form (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we obtain
h_{p}=\ell_{p}\cap\int bv^{p}
.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

The sequence space
h_{p}
is a
BK
-space with

Proof.

If

is any sequence, we write
\sigma_{n}(x)=M_{n}x
. Let

be given. Then there exists

\displaystyle|\sigma_{n}(x)|<\varepsilon/2

for all

. Now let

\displaystyle\left|\sigma_{n}\left(x-x^{[m]}\right)\right|\leq\left[\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\bigg|k(\Delta x_{k})\bigg|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq|\sigma_{n}(x)|+|\sigma_{m}(x)|<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon

Theorem 2.5
.

Define a sequence

of elements of the space

for every fixed

\displaystyle b_{n}^{(k)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\frac{1}{k}&,&\quad(n\leq k)\\

\displaystyle x=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}b^{(k)}

Proof.

It is clear that
\{b^{(k)}\}\subset h_{p}

(2.11)

\displaystyle Mb^{(k)}=e^{k}\in\ell_{1},\quad(k=0,1,2,...).

1\leq p<\infty

be given. For every non-negative integer

(2.12)

\displaystyle x^{[m]}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\lambda_{k}b^{(k)}.

Then, we obtain by applying

\displaystyle Mx^{[m]}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\lambda_{k}Mb^{(k)}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(Mx)_{k}e^{k}

\displaystyle\left\{M(x-x^{[m])}\right\}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}0&,&\quad(0\leq i\leq m)\\
(Mx)_{i}&,&\quad(i>m)\end{array};\quad\quad(i,m\in\mathbb{N}).\right.

Given
\varepsilon>0

such that

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}|i.(\Delta x)_{i}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

\displaystyle\|x-x^{[m]}\|_{h_{p}}=\left[\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}|i.(\Delta x)_{i}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leq\left[\sum_{i=m_{0}}^{\infty}|i.(\Delta x)_{i}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon

which proves that

is represented as in (

To show the uniqueness of this representation, we assume that

with the notation of (
2.1

to

by

. The linearity of

is clear. Since the linear transformation

is continuous we have at this stage that

\displaystyle(Mx)_{n}=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}\{Mb^{(k)}\}_{n}=\sum_{k}\mu_{k}e_{n}^{k}=\mu_{n};\quad(n\in\mathbb{N})

which contradicts the fact that
(Mx)_{n}=\lambda_{n}
for all

. Hence, the representation (

is unique.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Except the case
p=2
, the space
h_{p}
is not an inner product space, therefore not a Hilbert space for
1<p<\infty
.

Proof.

For
p=2
, we will show that the space
h_{2}
is a Hilbert space. Since the space
h_{p}
is a
BK
-space from Theorem
2.3
, the space

is a
BK
-space, for
p=2
. Also its norm can be obtained from an inner product, i.e.,
\|x\|_{h_{2}}=\langle k\Delta x,k\Delta x\rangle^{1/2}
holds. Then the space
h_{2}
is a Hilbert space.

Now consider the sequences
e_{1}=(1,0,0,0,\cdots)
and
e_{2}=(0,1,0,0,\cdots)
. Then we see that
\|e_{1}+e_{2}\|_{h_{p}}^{2}+\|e_{1}-e_{2}\|_{h_{p}}^{2}\neq 2.\big(\|e_{1}\|_{h_{p}}^{2}+\|e_{2}\|_{h_{p}}^{2}\big)
, i.e., the norm of the space
h_{p}
does not satisfy the parallelogram equality, which menas that the norm cannot be obtained from inner product. Hence, the space
h_{p}
with

is a Banach space that is not a Hilbert space.
∎

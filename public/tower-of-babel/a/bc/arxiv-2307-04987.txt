Inflationary magnetogenesis with a self-consistent coupling function
Yu Li, Le-Yao Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we discuss the inflationary magnetogenesis scenario, in which the coupling function is introduced to break the conformal invariance of electromagnetic action. Unlike in conventional models, we deduce the Maxwell’s equations under the perturbed FRW metric. We found that, the self-consistency of the action depends on the form of the coupling function when the scalar mode perturbations have been considered. Therefore, this self-consistency can be seen as a restriction on the coupling function. In this paper, we give the restrictive equation for coupling function then obtain the specific form of the coupling function in a simple model. We found that the coupling function depends on the potential of the inflaton and thus is model dependent. We obtain the power spectrum of electric field and magnetic field in large-field inflation model. We also found that the coupling function is a incresing function of time during slow-roll era as most of inflationary magnetogenesis models, it will lead to strong coupling problem. This issue is discussed qualitatively by introducing a correction function during the preheating.

Primordial magnetic field; inflationary magnetogenesis; cosmology perturbations.

2
Maxwell’s equations under perturbated FRW background

To get the restrict equation for
f(\phi)
, let us consider the FRW metric with scalar mode of inhomogeneous perturbations in longitudinal gauge:

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1+2\Phi)dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{2}(\eta)\left[-(1+2\Phi)d\eta^{2}+(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\right]

(2)

where
\Phi
is Bardeen potential,
t
is cosmic time and
\eta
is conformal time.
The action of matter during inflation can be written as
[
26
]
:

S=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu}f^{2}(\varphi)F_{\mu\alpha}F_{\nu\beta}\right]-\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\mu}\varphi+V(\varphi)\right]

where
\varphi(t,{\bf x})=\phi(t)+\delta\phi(t,{\bf x})
is the inflaton and its perturbation.

F_{\alpha\beta}=A_{\beta;\alpha}-A_{\alpha;\beta}=A_{\beta,\alpha}-A_{\alpha,\beta}

is the electromagnetic field tensor, with
A_{\alpha}
being the standard electromagnetic 4-potential.
f(\varphi)
is the coupling function which is introduced to break the conformally invariant of
the standard electromagnetic action
[
26
]
. For the convenience of discussion, we expand the coupling function as:

f^{2}(\varphi)=f^{2}(\phi+\delta\phi)\approx\left[f(\phi)+\frac{df}{d\varphi}\Big|_{\phi}\delta\phi\right]^{2}\approx f^{2}(\phi)\left[1+\mathcal{G}(\phi)\delta\phi\right]

(4)

where

\mathcal{G}(\phi)\equiv\frac{2}{f(\phi)}\frac{df}{d\varphi}\Big|_{\phi}

(5)

It is worth to notice that
\mathcal{G}
depend only on time or, in other words, it is scale-independent.

In the model we discuss here, we treat the electromagnetic field as a “test” field which means
that
F_{\alpha\beta}
do not affect the evolution of the background (
a
and
\phi
) and perturbations (
\Phi
and
\delta\phi
),
but the background and perturbations can affect the evolution of electromagnetic field.

The Maxwell’s equations can be obtain by using the action (
3

\partial_{\rho}\left[\sqrt{-g}f^{2}(\varphi)g^{\sigma\mu}g^{\rho\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\right]=0

For conformal time, the time component of Eq.(
6
) (
\sigma=0

\partial_{i}\left[f^{2}(\phi)(1-2\Phi+\mathcal{G}\delta\phi)\delta^{ij}F_{0j}\right]=0

In Minkowski spacetime, Eq.(
7
) is noting but
\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}=0
.
This is the secondary constraint equation for source-free electromagnetic field (one can refer to Appendix E in
[
46
]
).We will see later, this equation is trivial equation in FRW background and is non-trivial in perturbed background.

The space component of Eq.(
6
) (
\sigma=i
) can also be obtain similarly:

\displaystyle(1-2\Phi+\mathcal{G}\delta\phi)A^{\prime\prime}_{j}-(1+2\Phi+\mathcal{G}\delta\phi)\nabla^{2}A_{j}

\displaystyle+\left[\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}(1-2\Phi+\mathcal{G}\delta\phi)-2\Phi^{\prime}+\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\delta\phi+g\delta\phi^{\prime}\right]A^{\prime}_{j}

\displaystyle+(2\Phi_{,k}+\mathcal{G}\delta\phi_{,k})\delta^{k\ell}(A_{\ell,j}-A_{j,\ell})=0

(8)

where
\nabla^{2}\equiv\delta^{k\ell}\partial_{k}\partial_{\ell}
is Laplace operator and
′
denote the derivative with respect to conformal time.
For the convenience of discussion, we assume that
A_{i}
can be expressed as perturbation expansion:

A_{i}=A_{i}^{(0)}+A_{i}^{(1)}+A^{(2)}_{i}+\cdots

(9)

where
O[A_{i}^{(0)}]\sim O[\Phi]
.

In this paper we adopt the Coulomb gauge:
A_{0}(\eta,{\bf x})=0,~\partial_{j}A^{j}(\eta,{\bf x})=0
.
Under this gauge, Maxwell’s equations can be rewritten as:

\partial_{i}\left\{\mathcal{G}\delta\phi[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-4\Phi[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-2A^{(0)}_{j}\Phi^{\prime}\right\}\delta^{ij}=0

and

\left\{[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}A^{(0)}_{j}\right\}+\left\{[A^{(1)}_{j}]^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}[A^{(1)}_{j}]^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}A^{(1)}_{j}\right\}-Q_{j}^{(1)}=0

where:

\displaystyle Q_{j}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(2\Phi-\mathcal{G}\delta\phi)\left\{[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}A^{(0)}_{j}\right\}

(12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(2\Phi-\mathcal{G}\delta\phi)^{\prime}[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}+4\Phi\nabla^{2}A^{(0)}_{j}+\delta^{k\ell}(\mathcal{G}\delta\phi+2\Phi)_{,k}(A^{(0)}_{j,\ell}-A^{(0)}_{\ell,j})

Notice that, all the order in the left of Eq.(
11
) should be zero, therefore the space component of Maxwell equations lead to

\displaystyle[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}A^{(0)}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(13)

\displaystyle~[A^{(1)}_{j}]^{\prime\prime}+\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}[A^{(1)}_{j}]^{\prime}-\nabla^{2}A^{(1)}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{j}^{(1)}

(14)

If scalar perturbations are not taken into account (

From Eq.(

In fact, Eq.(

besides the coupling function and

should satisfy the Eq.(

One can choose a coupling function which satisfy Eq.(

From Eq.(
10
), one can obtain that

\mathcal{G}\delta\phi[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-4\Phi[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}-2A^{(0)}_{j}\Phi^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}(\eta)

where

In order to get the specific form of the coupling function, we consider the slow-roll era of inflation first.
During the slow-roll inflation, the super-Hubble scale Fourier mode of Bardeen potential

satisfy

\Phi^{\prime}_{k}=0,~~~~\Phi_{k}\approx\epsilon\mathcal{H}\frac{\delta\phi_{k}}{\phi^{\prime}}

where

is slow-roll parameter, and dot denote the derivative with respect to cosmic time.

is conformal Hubble parameter and

is Fourier mode of
\delta\phi
.
If one consider the large scales only, then we have:

\Phi^{\prime}\approx 0,~~~~\Phi\approx\epsilon\mathcal{H}\frac{\delta\phi}{\phi^{\prime}}

Insert Eq.(

\mathcal{G}=4\frac{\epsilon\mathcal{H}}{\phi^{\prime}}+\frac{\mathbb{C}(\eta)}{\delta\phi[A^{(0)}_{j}]^{\prime}}

From the Eq.(

\mathcal{G}=4\frac{\epsilon\mathcal{H}}{\phi^{\prime}}

Although the above discussion only considers the behavior at large scales, the form of the

During the slow-roll inflation, the Klein-Gorden equation of

f(\phi)\propto\exp\left(-\int^{\phi}\frac{V_{,\phi}}{V}d\phi\right)\propto V^{-1}

Eq.(

3
Power spectrum of electromagnetic field in large-field model

To obtain the power spectrum of electromagnetic field, we consider the
large-field inflation with polynomial potentials:

V(\phi)=\Lambda^{4}\left(\frac{\phi}{\mu}\right)^{p}~~~~(p>0)

(22)

where
\Lambda
is the “height” of potential, corresponding to the vacuum energy density during inflation, and
\mu
is the “width” of the potential, corresponding to the change in the field value
\Delta\phi
during inflation
[
49
]
.

According to Eq.(
21
), the coupling function in this large-field model is

f(\phi)\propto\phi^{-p}

(23)

By using
\dot{\phi}\approx-V,_{\phi}/3H,~~3H^{2}\approx V
during slow-roll inflation one can have
[
50
]
:

\ln\frac{a}{a_{i}}=-\frac{1}{2p}(\phi^{2}-\phi_{i}^{2})

(24)

where
a_{i}
and
\phi_{i}
is the scale factor and value of inflaton at the beginning of the inflation. And then the coupling function can be written as the function of scale factor:

f(a)\propto\left(-2p\ln\frac{a}{a_{i}}+\phi_{i}^{2}\right)^{-p/2}

(25)

This form of
f(\phi)
is different from conventional models (see
[
15
]
for review). In these models, it is often assumed that the coupling function has a power law form of scale factor.
However, Eq.(
25
) shows that the form of the self-consistent coupling function is not a power law form as in conventional models.
This means that the power-law-like coupling function does not
satisfy the self-consistent condition Eq.(
15
). This is the main difference between the model we discuss here and the conventional models.

During slow-roll inflation era, the scale factor as the function of confomal time
a(\eta)
can be assumed
as:

a(\eta)=a_{i}\left|\frac{\eta}{\eta_{i}}\right|^{1+\beta}

(26)

where
\eta_{i}
is the conformal time when the inflation begin. The case
\beta=-2
corresponds to de Sitter space-time. During the inflation,
\eta\rightarrow 0_{-}
. Insert Eq.(
26
) into Eq.(
25
) we have:

f\propto\left[-2p(1+\beta)\ln\left|\frac{\eta}{\eta_{i}}\right|+\phi^{2}_{i}\right]^{-p/2}

(27)

It is worth noting that the coupling function will diverges at the conformal time

|\eta_{\infty}|=|\eta_{i}|\exp\left[\frac{\phi^{2}_{i}}{2p(1+\beta)}\right]

(28)

When
\eta=\eta_{\infty}
,
\phi=0
, which means that the slow roll phase has been ended before
\eta_{\infty}
.

Insert Eq.(
28
) into Eq.(
27
) one can get:

f\propto\left[2p(1+\beta)\ln\left|\frac{\eta_{\infty}}{\eta}\right|\right]^{-p/2}\propto\left[\ln\left|\frac{\eta_{\infty}}{\eta}\right|\right]^{-p/2}

(29)

The next step is to solve Eq.(
13
) by using coupling function Eq.(
27
) or Eq.(
29
). Before that, it is convenient to
set
\mathcal{A}\equiv a(\eta)f(\phi(\eta))A^{(0)}_{j}(\eta,k)
, where
A^{(0)}_{j}(\eta,k)

is Fourier mode of
A^{(0)}_{j}
. Eq.(
13
) can be written as
[
15
]
:

\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}(\eta,k)+\left(k^{2}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\right)\mathcal{A}(\eta,k)=0

(30)

At the beginning of inflation,
\eta\approx\eta_{i}
.
This means
f^{\prime\prime}/f\approx 0
and the Eq.(
30
) change to

\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}+k^{2}\mathcal{A}=0~~\Rightarrow~~\mathcal{A}\propto\exp(\pm ik\eta)

(31)

At small scale limit, the solution should be “negative frequency”
[
27
]
. In order to satisfy the Wronskian condition
[
27
]
, the solution of
\mathcal{A}
should be

\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}\exp(-ik\eta)

(32)

At the late time of inflation when
\eta\approx\eta_{\infty}
we have

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}\approx\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)T^{-2}

(33)

where
T\equiv|\eta_{\infty}|-|\eta|=\eta-\eta_{\infty}
, therefore
T<0
during the slow-roll inflation.
Notice that
dT=d\eta
, then the Eq.(
30
) change to

\frac{d^{2}}{dT^{2}}\mathcal{A}+\left[k^{2}-\frac{p}{2}\left(\frac{p}{2}+1\right)T^{-2}\right]\mathcal{A}=0

(34)

The general solution of Eq.(
34
) is

\mathcal{A}=(-kT)^{1/2}\left[C_{1}(k)J_{\nu}(-kT)+C_{2}(k)J_{-\nu}(-kT)\right]

(35)

where
\nu\equiv(1+p)/2
.

When
|\eta|\gg|\eta_{\infty}|
, Eq.(
35
) should be approximated to Eq.(
32
). While, when

|\eta|\gg|\eta_{\infty}|
,
T\approx\eta
, therefore Eq.(
35
) just need to be approximated to

\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}\exp(-ikT)

(36)

Eq.(
36
) can be used to determine the coefficient
C_{1},C_{2}
.
We focus on the behavior on large scale, so we take the large scale limit:
-k\eta\rightarrow 0\Rightarrow-kT\rightarrow 0
. After ignoring the decay term, we have:

\mathcal{A}\approx k^{-1/2}c(\gamma)(-kT)^{1-\gamma}

(37)

where

c(\gamma)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2^{3-2\gamma}}}\frac{\exp[i\pi(1+\gamma)/2]}{\Gamma(3/2-\gamma)\cos(\pi\gamma)}

and
\gamma\equiv 1+p/2>1
. Therefore the power spectrum of magnetic field is

\frac{d\rho_{B}}{dk}=\frac{1}{k}\frac{d\rho_{B}}{d\ln k}=\frac{c^{2}(\gamma)}{2\pi^{2}k}H^{4}(k\eta)^{6-2\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\infty}}{\eta}\right)^{2-2\gamma}

The power spectrum of electric field can also be obtain:

\frac{d\rho_{E}}{dk}=\frac{1}{k}\frac{d\rho_{E}}{d\ln k}\approx\frac{d^{2}(\gamma)}{2\pi^{2}k}H^{4}(k\eta)^{4-2\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\eta_{\infty}}{\eta}\right)^{-2\gamma}

where

d(\gamma)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\exp[i\pi(1+\gamma)/2]}{2^{-\gamma+1/2}\Gamma(-\gamma+1/2)\cos(\pi\gamma)}

From Eq.(
39
,
40
) we can see that the spectral index of magnetic power spectrum is
n_{B}=6-2\gamma
and the spectral index of electric power spectrum is
n_{E}=4-2\gamma
.
Therefore, scale invariant spectrum of magnetic field can be got when
\gamma=3
i.e.

.
When
\gamma=2
i.e.

one can get the scale invariant spectrum of electric field. If the magnetic field spectrum is scale invariant (

Because of
\gamma>1
, it is can be seen from Eq.(

. We assume that the moment when the slow-roll ends is

, and the value of inflaton at this moment is

\left|\frac{\eta_{\infty}}{\eta_{end}}\right|-1\approx\ln\left|\frac{\eta_{\infty}}{\eta_{end}}\right|=-\frac{\phi_{end}^{2}}{2p(1+\beta)}\equiv\mathcal{Y}

Insert Eq.(
42
) into Eq.(
39
,
40
) one can estimate the power spectrum of magnetic and electric field at the end of slow-roll inflation:

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{B}}{d\ln k}\big|_{end}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}(\gamma)}{2\pi^{2}}H^{4}_{end}\left(\frac{k}{a_{end}H_{end}}\right)^{6-2\gamma}\mathcal{Y}^{2-2\gamma}

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{E}}{d\ln k}\big|_{end}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}(\gamma)}{2\pi^{2}}H^{4}_{end}\left(\frac{k}{a_{end}H_{end}}\right)^{4-2\gamma}\mathcal{Y}^{-2\gamma}

To avoid the backreaction problem, the energy density of electromagnetic field can not exceed the energy density of the inflaton, this require that

\frac{d\rho_{B}}{d\ln k}\big|_{end}+\frac{d\rho_{E}}{d\ln k}\big|_{end}<\rho_{end}

We focus on scale invariant spectrum of magnetic field, i.e.
\gamma=3,p=4
, then Eq.(
45
) means

\frac{H_{end}^{4}}{2\pi^{2}}\mathcal{Y}^{-6}d^{2}(\gamma)\left(\frac{k}{a_{0}H_{0}}\right)^{-2}\left(\frac{a_{0}H_{0}}{a_{end}H_{end}}\right)^{-2}<\frac{3}{8\pi}H^{2}_{end}M_{pl}^{2}

The ratio
(a_{0}H_{0})/(a_{end}H_{end})
can be estimated as
[
27
]
:

\frac{a_{0}H_{0}}{a_{end}H_{end}}\approx 1.51\times 10^{-29}\frac{h}{R},~~~~~~(h\approx 0.72)

(47)

where
R
depend on the reheating phase, and for simple estimate one can chose

as in
[
27
]
. Insert these into Eq.(

\rho_{end}<\left(\phi_{end}M_{pl}^{1/3}\right)^{8}\times 10^{-42}

If one require the
\rho_{end}
should be satify the requirements of nuleosynthesis (
\rho_{nuc}\approx 10^{-85}M_{pl}^{4}
), then

\phi_{end}>10^{-43}M_{pl}^{4/3}

This means that as long as the slow-roll era ends before the inflaton decays too small, the backreaction problem can be avoided.

We also can estimate the present day value of magnetic field strength simply. From Eq.(
43
) we know that
the power spectrum of the model in this paper is amplified by the factor
\mathcal{Y}^{2-2\gamma}
compare with the conventional model
[

,

]
.
This factor is independ on the scale factor. If one assume the instant reheating, then the present day power spectrum of magnetic field is also amplified by this factor.

While, this factor is depend on the detail of the inflation model, specifically on
\phi_{end}
.
For example,
for scale invariant magnetic pectrum, i.e.
V(\phi)\propto\phi^{4}
, this factor is
(8/\phi_{end}^{2})^{4}
.
In
\phi^{4}
inflation model, the slow-roll era ends at

[

compare with the conventional model
[

]
. This means that the magnetic field on coherence scale 1Mpc today is

This result satisfy the lower bound of

B\sim 10^{-15}G

Notice that the lower limit of

Therefore, we can use

4
Strong coupling

As the last part of this paper, we will discuss the problem of strong coupling qualitatively.
From Eq.(
27
) we know that the coupling function is monotonically increasing function during the
slow-roll of inflation. This will lead to the strong coupling problem which was first pointed out in
[
52
]
. To avoid this problem, one can assume
a decreasing coupling function during the preheating era like in
[
33
]
.

However, in this paper, we found that the coupling fucntion should satisfy the Eq.(
15
).
An attractive possibility is that this equation can lead to an decrease coupling function after the slow-roll of inflation.
Therefore it is interesting to discuss the
behavior of coupling function during the preheating.

It should be noted that, Eq.(
27
) is satisfied only in slow-roll era. During the preheating, the coupling function should be obtain by solving the Eq.(
13
,
15
) together.
One can eliminate the
A_{j}
by combining Eq.(
13
,
15
) and get

\mathcal{D}_{1}h^{\prime}+\mathcal{D}_{2}h+\mathcal{D}_{3}h^{2}+\mathcal{D}_{4}=0

(51)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}=\phi^{\prime 2}-2\mathcal{H}q

(52)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}=4q\phi^{\prime 2}-8\mathcal{H}q^{2}-2\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime\prime}+2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}q+2\mathcal{H}q^{\prime}

(53)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{3}=-\phi^{\prime 2}+2\mathcal{H}q

(54)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{4}=-4\phi^{\prime 2}q^{\prime}-\phi^{\prime 4}+4\mathcal{H}q\phi^{\prime 2}-4\mathcal{H}^{2}q^{2}

\displaystyle~~~~~~+8\phi^{\prime}\phi^{\prime\prime}q-8\mathcal{H}^{\prime}q^{2}

(55)

\displaystyle q\equiv\frac{\Phi}{\delta\phi}\phi^{\prime},~~h\equiv\mathcal{G}\phi^{\prime}=2\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}

(56)

Eq.(
51
) is the equation that the coupling function needs to satisfy during the preheating. On large scale, the Fourier mode of Bardeen potential and perturbation of inflaton can be written as
[
53
]

\Phi_{k}=\mathcal{C}\left(1-\frac{H}{a}\int adt\right),~~~\delta\phi_{k}\approx\mathcal{C}\dot{\phi}\left(a^{-1}\int adt\right)

(57)

Consider the case where the magnetic field is scale invarant
p=4
, which means that we should consider the

\phi^{4}
preheating. In this model of preheating,
a\propto\sqrt{t}
and the evolution of
\phi
can be approximated as
[
51
]

\phi\approx\frac{\tilde{\phi}}{a}\cos(0.8472\sqrt{\lambda}\tilde{\phi}\eta)

(58)

For qualitative discussion, we assume

\phi=\frac{1}{\eta}\cos\eta

(59)

Figure 1:
Coupling function during
\phi^{4}
preheating with no correction.

The evolution curve of the coupling function given in the Fig.
1
. It can be seen that the coupling function will increase during the preheating era. This will lead to strong coupling problem.

In conventional models, one strategy to solve strong coupling problem is to ansatz a decreasing coupling function during the preheating era directly as in
[
33
]
. However, in the models we consider here, the coupling function should satisfy Eq.(
51
), which means that the coupling function is model dependent. In other words, to get the decreasing coupling function, we should modify the preheating model.

We consider a toy model in which there are some modifications at the beginning of
\phi^{4}
preheating. As the preheating process goes on, the modificatons will vanish and the preheating model will approximate to the
\phi^{4}
model.

These modifications affect both the dynamics of background
\phi,~\mathcal{H}...
and perturbation
\Phi,~\delta\phi...
, and affect the Eq.(
51
). We assume that the effect of these modifications of preheating model change the Eq.(
51
) to

\mathcal{D}_{1}\hat{h}^{\prime}+\mathcal{D}_{2}\hat{h}+\mathcal{D}_{3}\hat{h}^{2}+\mathcal{D}_{4}=0

(60)

where

\hat{h}(\eta)\equiv h(\eta)+r(\eta)

(61)

and
r(\eta)
is a decreasing function of
\eta
. In Eq.(
60
), the dynamics of
\phi,\mathcal{H},\delta\phi,\Phi
are the same as in
\phi^{4}
model, which means that we assume the modifications of preheating model equivalent to introduce a correction function
r(\eta)
in Eq.(
51
).

As the preheating proceeds, the correction function will tend to zero
and
\hat{h}\rightarrow h
, therefore
h
will satisfy the Eq.(
51
) at late time of preheating and the preheating model approximate to the
\phi^{4}
model as we assumed.

One simple choice of
r(\eta)
is

r(\eta)=\frac{1}{\eta^{n}}

(62)

Notice that the form of
r(\eta)
depend on the modifications of preheating model, then the parameter
n
can be seen as a parameter of preheating model. This parameter can be choosen to avoid the strong problem.

are given in Fig.

.
It can be seen that a decreasing coupling function can be obtained by selecting the appropriate parameter n (i.g.
n=1
in Fig.

), and then the strong coupling can be avoided.

5
Summary and discussion

In this paper, we discuss the inflationary magnetogenesis with a coupling function which can keep the action to be self-consistent. This self-consistence coming from the time component of Maxwell’s equation which is the secondary constraint for electromagnetic field. Under the FRW metric, this is a trival equation. However, once we consider the perturbed metric, this equation become non-trival and can be seen as a
restrict equation for coupling function
f(\phi)
, see Eq.(
10
). Taking this as a starting point, we calculated the power spectrum of the electric and magnetic fields in the large-scale inflation model.
We estimate the present day value of magnetic field, and the result satisfy the lower bound of
\gamma
-ray observation.

We found that, the power spectrum obtained in this paper is multiplied by a factor related to
\phi_{end}
compared to the conventional model
[
15
,
41
]
. This means that one can generate the required magnetic field by choosing a suitable inflation model, or conversely use the magnetic field observed today to limit
\phi_{end}
. This provides a possibility to use today’s large-scale magnetic field strength to estimate the value of the inflaton field at the end of the slow-roll.

On the other hand, to avoid the backreaction problem at the end of inflation, the value of inflaton at the end of slow-roll era
\phi_{end}
should have a lower limit (
\sim 10^{-43}
)(see Eq.(
49
)). This lower limit is so small that the upper limit of the factor
\mathcal{Y}
can be large. This means that the model discussed in this paper can generate a sufficiently strong magnetic field without causing backreaction problem.

The strong coupling problem can also appear in the situation which is discussed here. One way to solve the problem of strong coupling is to introduce an decreasing coupling function in the preheating era. In this paper, the coupling function is determined by Eq.(
7
) or Eq.(
51
) in preheating era. Unfortunately these equations give an increasing coupling function. To make the coupling function change to a decreasing function, it is necessary to introduce a correction function in the early stage of preheating.
So it is a very interesting open topic to find a suitable preheating model in which the coupling function can naturally be determined as an decreasing function.

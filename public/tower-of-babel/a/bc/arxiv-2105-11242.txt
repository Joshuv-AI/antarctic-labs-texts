Homogenization problems for the compressible Navier-Stokes system in 2D perforated domains
Šárka Nečasová, Jiaojiao Pan

Abstract
In this paper, we study the homogenization problems for the stationary compressible Navier-Stokes system in a bounded 2D domain, where the domain is perforated with very tiny holes (or obstacles) whose diameters are much smaller than their mutual distances. We obtain that the process of homogenization doesn’t change the motion of the fluids. From another point of view, we obtain the same system of equations in the asymptotic limit. It is the first result of homogenization problem in the compressible case in 2 dimension.



Keywords.
Homogenization; Navier-Stokes system; Perforated domains; Bogovskii operator.

Mathematics Subject Classification.
35B27, 76M50, 76N06.

Introduction

Homogenization of colorblue the Newtonian fluid in physical domains perforated by a large number of tiny holes plays an important role in fluid mechanics and has gain lots of interest. Viscous fluid flows passing a great many fixed solid obstacles is a situation frequently occurring in real applications referring to
[
27
]
. Based on these applications, the models like stationary, viscous fluid flows represented by the the standard Stokes or Navier-Stokes system of equations in porous medium could be of vital importance.

The typical diameter and mutual distance of these holes become the main factors in the asymptotic behavior of fluid flows
in the regime where the number of holes tends to infinity and their size tends to zero. With the increasing number of holes, the limit motion of fluid flow approaches a state governed by certain
homogenized
equations which are homogeneous in form of different cases (without obstacles).

Allaire
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
(or earlier results by Tartar
[
28
]
) provided a systematical study of Stokes and/or Navier-Stokes system for three different circumstances where the holes are periodically distributed with different size. Roughly speaking, the asymptotic limit behavior is governed by Darcy’s law when the holes are of supercritical size;
when the holes are of the critical size, the asymptotic limit behavior gives rise to Brinkman’s law; the subcritical size of holes makes no differences in the motion of the asymptotic limit - the limit problem coincides with the original system of equations.

Moreover, relevant results has been extended to the evolutionary (time-dependent) incompressible Navire-Stokes system by Mikelić
[
23
]
and Allaire
[
2
]
, and more recently in
[
14
]
. For the evolutionary barotropic (compressible) Navier-Stokes system, Masmoudi
[
22
]
obtained that the homogenization
limit was governed by porous medium equation (Darcy’s law) where the diameter of the holes is comparable to their mutual distance (critical size), and similar results for the full Navier-Stokes-Fourier system were obtained in
[
15
]
. In
[
10
,
11
,
20
]
, the authors considered the case of small holes for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations and in
[
21
]
, steady compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier system is considered, homogenized equations mentioned above remain the same asymptotic limit as the original ones. Let us also mention the case when the hole is the moving rigid body. In the case of the compressible fluid with the moving rigid body the homogenization was study by Bravin and Nečasová
[
5
]
.

1.1
Problem formulation

Similar to the case in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]

\varepsilon

a_{\varepsilon}=e^{-\sigma\varepsilon^{-\alpha}}

Considering a bounded domain

of class

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{k\in K_{\varepsilon}}T_{\varepsilon,k},\ \ K_{\varepsilon}:=\{k|\ \varepsilon\bar{C}_{k}\subset\Omega\}

T_{\varepsilon,k}:=x_{\varepsilon,k}+a_{\varepsilon}T_{k}\subset\subset B(x_{\varepsilon,k},b_{0}a_{\varepsilon})\subset\subset\varepsilon C_{k}\subset\Omega,

(1.2)

with

C_{k}:=(0,1)^{2}+k,\ \ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\ a_{\varepsilon}=e^{-\sigma\varepsilon^{-\alpha}}\ \mbox{for}\ \ \alpha>2.

(1.3)

Here
x_{\varepsilon,k}\in T_{\varepsilon,k},\ b_{0}>0
and
\sigma
is positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
, for each

is a simply connected bounded domain of class

,
B(x,r)
denotes the open ball centered at

with radius

in
R^{2}
.
The diameter of each
T_{\varepsilon,k}
is of size

O(a_{\varepsilon})
and their mutual distance is
O(\varepsilon)
, where their total number

can be estimated as

|K_{\varepsilon}|\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1+o(1)).

For convenience, we use the symbol

to denote the space of functions in
L^{q}(\Omega)
with zero integral mean

L^{q}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{q}(\Omega):\,\int_{\Omega}f\,{\rm d}{x}=0\right\}.

(1.4)

Then, we consider the following stationary (compressible)
Navier-Stokes system in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}

{\rm div\,}(\varrho{\bf u})=0,

(1.5)

{\rm div\,}(\varrho{\bf u}\otimes{\bf u})+\nabla p(\varrho)={\rm div\,}{\mathbb{S}}(\nabla{\bf u})+\varrho{\bf f}+{\bf g},

(1.6)

{\mathbb{S}}(\nabla{\bf u})=\mu\left(\nabla{\bf u}+\nabla^{t}{\bf u}-\frac{2}{3}({\rm div\,}{\bf u}){\mathbb{I}}\right)+\eta({\rm div\,}{\bf u}){\mathbb{I}},\ \mu>0,\ \eta\geq 0.

(1.7)

Here
\varrho

{\bf u}
is the velocity field,

{\mathbb{S}}(\nabla{\bf u})

stands for the Newtonian viscous stress tensor with constant viscosity coefficients

In the spatial domain

{\bf u}=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

(1.8)

For the sake of simplicity, we concentrate on the

pressure-density state relationship

p(\varrho)=a\varrho^{\gamma},\ a>0,

(1.9)

with the adiabatic exponent
\gamma
, which will be specified as follows.

The motion of the fluid is driven by the volume force
{\bf f}
and nonvolume force
{\bf g}
, defined on the whole domain
\Omega
and independent of
\varepsilon
,
those are supposed to be uniformly bounded,

\|{\bf f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{2})}+\|{\bf g}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;R^{2})}\leq C<\infty.

(1.10)

Specifically, we use the symbol
C
to denote a generic bounded constant that may vary from line to line in the following contents but it is independent of the parameters of the problem, in particular of
\varepsilon
. Furthermore, we use the symbol
\tilde{h}
to denote the
zero-extension
of

in

, which means

\tilde{h}=h\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\ \ \ \tilde{h}=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ R^{2}\setminus\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

(1.11)

To be consistent with its physical interpretation,
the density
\varrho
is non-negative and we fix the total mass of the fluid to be

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{0<\varepsilon<1}M_{\varepsilon}\leq M_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varrho\ {\rm d}{x}\leq\sup_{0<\varepsilon<1}M_{\varepsilon}<\infty.

(1.12)

In particular, the

introduced by Allaire
[
3
]
can be used in a compatible way in

to construct the inverse of the

Finally, we obtain the main results listed in Section

2.4

Here, we introduce

Lemma 2.4

Up to a substraction of subsequence, there holds for any
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\psi\left(p(\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})-(\frac{4\mu}{3}+\eta){\rm div\,}\tilde{\bf u}_{\varepsilon}\right)\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\ {\rm d}{x}=\int_{\Omega}\psi\left(\overline{p(\varrho)}-(\frac{4\mu}{3}+\eta){\rm div\,}{\bf u}\right)\varrho\ {\rm d}{x}.

(2.39)

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

The main idea is to give proper test functions via taking advantage of Fourier multiplier and Riesz type operators. Referring to Section 1.3.7.2 in
[
24
]
or Section 10.16 in
[
12
]
for the definitions and properties used here of Fourier multiplier and Riesz operators, we choose proper test functions defined by

\psi\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}),\quad\psi\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\varrho),

(2.40)

where
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)

Observing that

By virtue of

\|\nabla\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1+}(R^{2};R^{2\times 2})}\leq C\,\|1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1+}(R^{2};R)}\leq C.

That means
\nabla\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\in L^{2\gamma}(R^{2};R^{2\times 2})\bigcap L^{1+}(R^{2};R^{2\times 2})

2\gamma>2+>d=2

[
24

[

\|\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(R^{2};R^{2})}\leq\|\nabla\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2\gamma}(R^{2};R^{2\times 2})}\leq C\,\|1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2\gamma}(R^{2};R)}\leq C.

(2.42)

Again by the embedding theorem in homogeneous Sobolev spaces, we have for any
f\in L^{q}(R^{2}),\ {\rm supp}\,f\subset\Omega
:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Delta^{-1}(f)\|_{L^{q^{*}}(R^{2};R^{2})}\leq C\,\|f\|_{L^{q}(R^{2})},\quad\frac{1}{q^{*}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2},\ \mbox{if}\ 1<q<2,

(2.43)

since
1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1+}(R^{2};R)\bigcap L^{2^{\gamma}}(R^{2};R)

1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(R^{2};R),\ 1+<p<2\gamma
and

\|\nabla\Delta^{-1}(1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{q^{*}}(R^{2};R^{2})}\leq C\,\|1_{\Omega}\tilde{\varrho}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(R^{2};R)}\leq C,

where
\frac{1}{q^{*}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}
,
1+<q<2
,

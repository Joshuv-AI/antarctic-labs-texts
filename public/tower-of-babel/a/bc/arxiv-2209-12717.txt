Quasi-invariant states


Abstract
We develop the theory of quasi–invariant (resp. strongly quasi–invariant) states under the action of a group G G of normal ∗ * –automorphisms of a ∗ * –algebra (or von Neumann alegbra) 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . We prove that these states are naturally associated to left– G G – 1 1 –cocycles. If G G is compact, the structure of strongly G G –quasi–invariant states is determined. For any G G –strongly quasi–invariant state φ \varphi , we construct a unitary representation associated to the triple ( 𝒜 , G , φ ) (\mathcal{A},G,\varphi) . We prove, under some conditions, that any quantum Markov chain with commuting, invertible and hermitean conditional density amplitudes on a countable tensor product of type I factors is strongly quasi–invariant with respect to the natural action of the group 𝒮 ∞ \mathcal{S}_{\infty} of local permutations and we give the explicit form of the associated cocycle. This provides a family of non–trivial examples of strongly quasi–invariant states for locally compact groups obtained as inductive limit of an increasing sequence of compact groups.

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
on

\mathbb{N}^{*}
and on
\mathcal{A}

In the literature,

3.1

In this section, we use the symbolic expressions
\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
and

It is known that there is no general way to give a meaning to

However the combined expression

In subection

widely used in quantum field theory (Feynman integral) and they also appear in the theory of

Furthermore, once correctly defined by (

Proposition 3

Any product state on
\mathcal{A}

\varphi=\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\varphi_{n}=\bigotimes_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\rm{Tr}(W_{n}\,\cdot\,)=:\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}j_{n}(W_{n})\,\cdot\,\right)

invertible for each

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\ ,\ \exists c_{n}>0\ \colon\ W_{n}\geq c_{n}

(3.7)

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
–quasi–invariant with cocycle

–strongly quasi–invariant iff the

mutually commute.

Proof
.
Let
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
with support
\Lambda_{g}
. Note that
g(\Lambda_{g})=\Lambda_{g}
. Therefore, for any
n\in\Lambda_{g}
and

one has

\displaystyle\varphi(g(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}j_{n}(W_{n})g(a)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(g\left(g^{-1}\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}j_{n}(W_{n})\right)a\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(g^{-1}\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}j_{n}(W_{n})\right)a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}j_{g^{-1}n}(W_{n})a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(\left(\prod_{n\in\Lambda_{g}^{c}}j_{n}(W_{n})\right)\left(\prod_{n\in\Lambda_{g}}j_{g^{-1}n}(W_{n})\right)a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rm{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(\left(\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}j_{n}(W_{n})\right)\left(\prod_{n\in\Lambda_{g}}j_{n}(W^{-1}_{n})\right)\left(\prod_{n\in\Lambda_{g}}j_{g^{-1}n}(W_{n})\right)a\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\left(\prod_{n\in\Lambda_{g}}j_{n}(W^{-1}_{n})\right)\left(\prod_{n\in\Lambda_{g}}j_{g^{-1}n}(W_{n})\right)a\right)=\varphi\left(x_{g}a\right)

Condition (
3.7
) guarantees that

is bounded and the definition of

\Lambda_{g}
that
x_{g}
is Hermitian. Therefore
\varphi
is
\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
–quasi–invariant with cocycle given by (
3.8
).

\qquad\square

Remark
.
1) Notice that condition (

if

2) Notice that the cocycle

Theorem 2

Proof
.
(i) follows from the fact that
\psi

=\psi\left(x_{[1,N]}x_{[1,N]}^{-1}g^{-1}\left(x_{[1,N]}\right)a\right)\\
=\varphi\left(x_{[1,N]}^{-1}g^{-1}\left(x_{[1,N]}\right)a\right)

Since

and

is

\mathcal{S}_{\infty}
–quasi invariant with cocycle

To prove (iii) notice that, if

=\langle a_{[1,M]}\cdot\Psi\,,\,\pi_{\psi}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{M}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)\pi_{\psi}\left(\prod_{k=M+1}^{N}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)b_{[1,M]}\cdot\Psi\rangle

where, in the last two equalities, we have used the fact that, for local algebras localized on
disjoint sets, both their commutativity and the factorizability of the state

\langle\Psi\,,\,\pi_{\psi}\left(\prod_{k=M+1}^{N}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)\cdot\Psi\rangle\ \langle\Psi\,,\,a_{[1,M]}^{*}\pi_{\psi}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{M}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)b_{[1,M]}\cdot\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\hbox{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left(\prod_{k=M+1}^{N}j_{k}(W_{\infty})\prod_{k=M+1}^{N}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\langle\Psi\,,\,a_{[1,M]}^{*}\pi_{\psi}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{M}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)b_{[1,M]}\cdot\Psi\rangle

=\langle\Psi\,,\,a_{[1,M]}^{*}\pi_{\psi}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{M}j_{k}(W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k})\right)b_{[1,M]}\cdot\Psi\rangle

because the

\hbox{Tr}_{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is factorizable in the given localization. This proves that the limit (

\pi_{\psi}(\mathcal{A}_{[1,M]})^{\prime\prime}\cdot\Psi

(i) follows.

(vi) follows from the fact that weak convergence of a norm bounded sequence of operators on a dense subspace of a Hilbert space implies weak convergence on the whole space.

To prove (v), we consider

\|x_{[1,N]}-x_{[1,M]}\|\overset{\eqref{df-xn-fi}}{=}\left\|\prod_{k=1}^{N}W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k}-\prod_{k=1}^{M}W_{\infty}^{-1}W_{k}\right\|

with

and any sequence

4

Let

be a group of
*
–automorphisms of

\mathcal{A}
. In the following the cyclic representation of

shall be denoted

Theorem 4

If
\varphi
is
(G,x)
–strongly quasi–invariant,
there exists a unique unitary representation
U
of
G

on
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
characterized by the property

U_{g}\pi(a)\Phi=\pi(g(a)x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}}))\Phi\ ;\quad\forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}

(4.1)

. Moreover

u_{g}(\pi(a)):=U^{*}_{g}\pi(a)U_{g}=\pi(g^{-1}(a))\ ;\quad\forall\,g\in G\ ;\quad\forall\,a\in\mathcal{A}

(4.2)

Proof
. In the above notations, for any
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
g\in G
,
one has

\displaystyle\langle U_{g}\pi(a)\Phi,\ U_{g}\pi(b)\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{df-Ug}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle\pi(g(a)x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi,\pi(g(b)x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,\pi(x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}}g(a^{*}b)x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}}g(a^{*}b)x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(g(a^{*}b)x_{g^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(g(a^{*}b\cdot g^{-1}(x_{g^{-1}})))

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{df-G-q-inv}}{=}

\displaystyle\varphi(x_{g}a^{*}bg^{-1}(x_{g^{-1}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi(a^{*}bg^{-1}(x_{g^{-1}})x_{g})

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{xg-1}}{=}

\displaystyle\varphi(a^{*}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\pi(a)\Phi,\pi(b)\Phi\rangle

Thus
U_{g}
is isometric. Now, if
g,g^{\prime}\in G
, then

\displaystyle U_{g}U_{g^{\prime}}\pi(a)\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{g}\pi(g^{\prime}(a)x^{1/2}_{g^{\prime-1}})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(g[g^{\prime}(a)x^{1/2}_{g^{\prime-1}}]x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)g(x^{1/2}_{g^{\prime-1}})x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)g(x^{1/2}_{g^{\prime-1}})x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{xs=g(ys)}}{=}

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)g(x_{g^{\prime-1}})^{1/2}x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{xg-comm-xg'}\,,\,\eqref{xg-comm-g(xg')}}{=}

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)(g(x_{g^{\prime-1}})x_{g^{-1}})^{1/2})\Phi

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{xg-comm-g(xg')}}{=}

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)(x_{g^{-1}}g(x_{g^{\prime-1}}))^{1/2})\Phi

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{xg-1G-left-cocy}}{=}

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)x_{g^{\prime-1}g^{-1}}^{1/2})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(gg^{\prime}(a)x_{(gg^{\prime})^{-1}}^{1/2})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{gg^{\prime}}\pi(a)\Phi

In particular, putting first

and then

, so that

Finally, for

and

\displaystyle U^{*}_{g}\pi(a)U_{g}\pi(b)\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{g^{-1}}\pi(a\cdot g(b)\cdot x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(g^{-1}[ag(b)x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}}]\cdot x^{1/2}_{g})\Phi

\displaystyle\pi(g^{-1}(a)b\cdot[g^{-1}(x^{1/2}_{g^{-1}})x^{1/2}_{g}])\Phi

and, by (

\square

Proposition 4

The family

Lemma 8

Suppose that a state

–quasi–invariant with cocycle

Matching Powers: Macaulay2 Package
Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. We introduce the Macaulay2 package MatchingPowers . It allows to compute and manipulate the matching powers of a monomial ideal. The basic theory of matching powers is explained and the main features of the package are presented.

1.
Basic Properties of Matching Powers

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring with coefficients over a field
K
. Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
S
. The
k
th
matching power
of
I
, denoted by
I^{[k]}
is the monomial ideal generated by the products
u_{1}\cdots u_{k}
such that
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}
is a monomial regular sequence of length
k
contained in
I
. The biggest
k
such that
I^{[k]}\neq 0
is called the
monomial grade
of
I
and it is denoted by
\nu(I)
. The concept of matching powers was first introduced in the squarefree case in
[
6
]
and later extended to all monomial ideals in
[
3
]
.

One of the main motivations for considering matching powers comes from graph theory. Let
G
be a finite simple graph on vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. The
edge ideal
of
G
is the squarefree monomial ideal
I(G)
generated by the monomials
x_{e}=x_{i}x_{j}
such that
e=\{i,j\}
is an edge of
G
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}
be
k
edges of
G
, then
x_{e_{1}}\cdots x_{e_{k}}
is a generator of
I(G)^{[k]}
if and only if
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
is a
k
-matching of
G
. This observation justifies the choice to name the powers
I^{[k]}
the matching powers of
I
.

Similarly, for a non-squarefree example, we can consider edge ideals of
weighted oriented graphs
. Recall that a (
vertex
)-
weighted oriented graph

\mathcal{D}=(V(\mathcal{D}),E(\mathcal{D}),w)
consists of an underlying graph
G
, with
V(\mathcal{D})=V(G)=[n]
, on which each edge is given an orientation and it is equipped with a
weight function

w:V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. The
weight
of a vertex
i\in V(G)
, denoted by
w_{i}
, is the value
w(i)
of the weight function at
i
. The directed edges of
\mathcal{D}
are denoted by pairs
(i,j)\in E(\mathcal{D})
to reflect the orientation, hence
(i,j)
represents an edge directed from
i
to
j
. The
edge ideal
of
\mathcal{D}
is defined as the ideal

I(\mathcal{D})\ =\ (x_{i}x_{j}^{w_{j}}\ :\ (i,j)\in E(\mathcal{D})).

As before, given
k
generators
u_{\ell}=x_{i_{\ell}}x_{j_{\ell}}^{w_{j_{\ell}}}
of
I(\mathcal{D})
, with
e_{\ell}=(i_{\ell},j_{\ell})\in E(\mathcal{D})
, for
\ell=1,\dots,k
, the product
u_{1}\cdots u_{k}
is a generator of
I(\mathcal{D})^{[k]}
if and only if
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
is a
k
-matching of the underlying simple graph
G
of
\mathcal{D}
.

In the remaining part of this section, we summarize some of the main results proved in
[
3
, Section 1]
.

Let
G(I)
be the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. The
initial degree
of
I
is defined as
\textup{indeg}(I)=\min\{\deg(u):u\in G(I)\}
. For a monomial
u\in S
, the
x_{i}
-degree
of
u
is defined as
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:x_{i}^{j}\ \textup{divides}\ u\}
. Whereas, the
support
of
u
is the set
\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{i:\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>0\}
. Following
[
7
]
, we define the
bounding multidegree
of
I
as

{\bf deg}(I)\ =\ (\deg_{x_{1}}(I),\dots,\deg_{x_{n}}(I)),

with

\deg_{x_{i}}(I)\ =\ \max_{u\in G(I)}\deg_{x_{i}}(u),\ \ \textup{for all}\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

Furthermore, if
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we set
|{\bf a}|=a_{1}+\dots+a_{n}
.

For a monomial
u=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}\in S
, we set
u^{\wp}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\prod_{j=1}^{a_{i}}x_{i,j})
in the polynomial ring
K[x_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq a_{i}]
. The
polarization
of
I
is defined to be the squarefree ideal
I^{\wp}
of
S^{\wp}=K[x_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq\deg_{x_{i}}(I)]
whose minimal generating set is
G(I^{\wp})=\{u^{\wp}:u\in G(I)\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. The following facts hold.

(i)

I^{[k]}=(u_{1}\cdots u_{k}\ :\ u_{i}\in G(I),\ \operatorname{supp}(u_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}(u_{j})=\emptyset,\ 1\leq i<j\leq k)
.

(ii)

I^{[k]}\neq 0
if and only if
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
.

(iii)

(I^{[k]})^{\wp}=(I^{\wp})^{[k]}
for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
.

(iv)

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{[k]})=\operatorname{depth}(S^{\wp}/(I^{\wp})^{[k]})-|{\bf deg}(I)|+n
, for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
.

(v)

\operatorname{depth}(S/I^{[k]})\geq\textup{indeg}(I^{[k]})-1+(n-|{\bf deg}(I)|)
.

Based on inequality (v), we define the
normalized depth function
of
I
as

g_{I}(k)\ =\ \operatorname{depth}(S/I^{[k]})+|{\bf deg}(I)|-n-(\textup{indeg}(I^{[k]})-1),

for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
. In the squarefree case, this is the function introduced in
[
6
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
it was proved that
[
3
]

Corollary 1.2
.

We have
\nu(I)=\nu(I^{\wp})
and
g_{I}(k)=g_{I^{\wp}}(k)
for all
k
.

It was conjectured in
[
6
]
that
g_{I}
would be non-increasing for all squarefree monomial ideals
I
. Because of Corollary
1.2
, it the above conjecture was true, then
g_{I}
would be non-increasing for all monomial ideals
I

[
3
, Proposition 1.8]
. However, very recently, Fakhari
[
18
]
constructed a family of monomial ideals
I
such that the difference
g_{I}(2)-g_{I}(1)
may be arbitrary large, thus disproving the above conjecture.

It is an open question whether for any sequence of non-negative integers
a_{1},\dots,a_{g}
, there exists a monomial ideal
I
such that
\nu(I)=g
and
g_{I}(k)=a_{k}
for all
k=1,\dots,g
.

[

]

2.
The Package

The purpose of the package
MatchingPowers
is to provide the tools to manipulate and calculate the matching powers of a monomial ideal
I
.

The
multidegree
of a monomial
u\in S
is defined to be the vector

{\bf deg}(u)\ =\ (\deg_{x_{1}}(u),\dots,\deg_{x_{n}}(u)).

Whereas, for
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{n}
, we set
{\bf x^{a}}=\prod_{i}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
. In particular,
u={\bf x}^{{\bf\deg}(u)}
.

Given two monomial ideals
I,J\subset S
, we define the
matching product
of
I
and
J
to be the monomial ideal

I*J\ =\ (uv\ :\ u\in I,v\in J,\ u,v\ \textup{is a regular sequence}).

It is not difficult to see that

I*J\ =\ (uv\ :\ u\in G(I),\ v\in G(J),\ \operatorname{supp}(u)\cap\operatorname{supp}(v)=\emptyset).

Moreover,
I^{[2]}=I*I
and by induction
I^{[k]}=I^{[k-1]}*I
for all
k\geq 2
.

Finally, we recall that a graded ideal
I\subset S
is
linearly related
if
I
is generated in single degree, say
d
, and the first syzygy module of
I
is generated
d+1
.

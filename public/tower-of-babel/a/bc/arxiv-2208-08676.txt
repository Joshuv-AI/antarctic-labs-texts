Einstein-Klein-Gordon spacetimes in the harmonic near-Minkowski regime
Philippe G. LeFloch, Yue Ma

Abstract
We study the initial value problem for the Einstein-Klein-Gordon system and establish the global nonlinear stability of massive matter in the near-Minkowski regime when the initial geometry is a perturbation of an asymptotically flat, spacelike hypersurface in Minkowski spacetime and the metric enjoys the harmonic decay 1 / r 1/r (in term of a suitable distance function r r at spatial infinity). Our analysis encompasses matter fields that have small energy norm and solely enjoys a slow decay at spacelike infinity. Our proof is based on the Euclidean-hyperboloidal foliation method recently introduced by the authors, and distinguishes between the decay along asymptotically hyperbolic slices and the decay along asymptotically Euclidean slices. We carefully analyze the decay of metric component at the harmonic level 1 / r 1/r , especially the metric component in the direction of the light cone. In presence of such a slow-decaying matter field, we establish a global existence theory for the Einstein equations expressed as a coupled system of nonlinear wave and Klein-Gordon equations.

Introduction

1.1
Global evolution problem for the Einstein-matter equations

Main purpose.

We consider solutions to the Einstein equations in the near-Minkowski regime and study the global nonlinear evolution problem when a suitable initial data set is prescribed, consisting of a Riemannian metric, a symmetric two-tensor, and matter data. These data are assumed to be close to a sufficiently flat and asymptotically Schwarzschild-like perturbation of a hypersurface in Minkowski spacetime. For the
vacuum
Einstein equations, this problem was solved first by Christodoulou and Klainerman
[

Our aim in the present paper is to solve the global evolution problem for the Einstein-matter system when the matter field under consideration is
massive.
We are going to describe the problem and first state a simplified version of our main result (Theorem

Einstein equations and massive fields.

We consider four-dimensional manifolds
(\mathscr{M},g)
in which
\mathscr{M}\simeq[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
g
is a Lorentzian metric with signature
(-,+,+,+)
and Levi-Civita connection
\nabla_{g}=\nabla
. Greek indices
\alpha,\beta,\ldots
describe
0,1,2,3
, and we denote by
R_{\alpha\beta}
the Ricci curvature of
g
and by
R=g^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}
its scalar curvature. Throughout, we use implicit summation over repeated indices, as well as raising and lowering indices with respect to the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}
and its inverse denoted by
g^{\alpha\beta}
.

We impose that Einstein-matter equations hold, namely

G_{\alpha\beta}=8\pi\,T_{\alpha\beta},

(1.1)

in which the left-hand side is Einstein’s curvature tensor defined (in abstract indices) as

G_{\alpha\beta}=R_{\alpha\beta}-{R\over 2}\,g_{\alpha\beta}.

(1.2)

The right-hand side of (
1.1
) is the energy-momentum tensor, which depends upon the matter content and, for instance, is taken to vanish identically for vacuum Einstein spacetimes, so that (
1.1
) then reduces to the Ricci-flat condition
R_{\alpha\beta}=0
.

While our method of analysis should apply to many massive matter models, we are interested here in Klein-Gordon scalar fields
\phi:\mathscr{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with energy-momentum tensor

T_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{\alpha}\phi\nabla_{\beta}\phi-\Big({1\over 2}\nabla_{\gamma}\phi\nabla^{\gamma}\phi+U(\phi)\Big)g_{\alpha\beta}.

(1.3)

Here, the potential function
U=U(\phi)
depends on the nature of the matter under consideration and, throughout, we assume that

U(\phi)={1\over 2}c^{2}\phi^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\phi^{3})

(1.4)

for some (mass-like) constant
c>0
.
Recalling the Bianchi identity
\nabla^{\alpha}R_{\alpha\beta}={1\over 2}\nabla_{\beta}R
, we have
\nabla^{\alpha}G_{\alpha\beta}=0
and, consequently,

\nabla^{\alpha}T_{\alpha\beta}=0.

(1.5)

In turn, we deduce that
\phi
satisfies the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation

\Box_{g}\phi-U^{\prime}(\phi)=0.

(1.6)

For instance, when
U(\phi)={c^{2}\over 2}\phi^{2}
, this is
\Box_{g}\phi-c^{2}\phi=0
, which is linear in
\phi
but also involves the unknown metric.

Initial value problem.

For suitable initial data, the equation (
1.6
) is expected to uniquely determine the evolution of the matter.
Our challenge is precisely to study the nonlinear coupling problem when the metric
g
itself is one of the unknowns and solves Einstein equations with suitably prescribed initial data.
When a foliation by spacelike hypersurfaces is chosen and a suitable gauge choice is made, we use Latin indices varying between
1
and
3
and the Einstein equations decompose into
and evolution equations

G_{ab}=8\pi\,T_{ab},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pta,b=1,2,3.

(1.7)

and constraint equations

G_{00}=8\pi\,T_{00},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002ptG_{0a}=8\pi\,T_{0a},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pta,b=1,2,3.

(1.8)

The initial value problem is then formulated by specifying a Riemannian metric
g_{0}
and a symmetric two-tensor field
k_{0}
, defined on a manifold with topology
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and satisfying the constraints (
1.8
), namely

\displaystyle R_{g_{0}}+(\text{Tr}_{g_{0}}k_{0})^{2}-|k_{0}|_{g_{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle=16\pi T_{00},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{Div}_{g_{0}}\big(k_{0}-(\text{Tr}_{g_{0}}k_{0})g_{0}\big)=-8\pi T_{0{\scriptscriptstyle\mspace{.5mu}\bullet\mspace{.5mu}}}.

(1.9)

where
R_{g_{0}}
denotes the scalar curvature,
\text{Tr}_{g_{0}}
the trace, and
\text{Div}_{g_{0}}
the divergence of the metric
g_{0}
.
We then seek a globally hyperbolic Cauchy development
[
7
,
8
]
consisting of a spacetime metric
g
together with a matter field
\phi
satisfying Einstein’s evolution equations and defining a manifold whose induced geometry (first- and second-fundamental forms) on an initial hypersurface is given by the pair
(g_{0},k_{0})
.

1.2
Self-gravitating fields in the asymptotically Schwarzschild regime

Wave-Klein-Gordon system of interest.

We work on the manifold
\mathscr{M}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{1+3}_{+}:=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+3},t\geq 1\}
covered by a single coordinate chart
(t,x)=(t,x^{a})
with
t\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We also write
r^{2}:=|x|^{2}=\sum_{a=1}^{3}|x^{a}|^{2}
. We introduce the Minkowski metric
g_{\textbf{Mink}}:=-dt^{2}+\sum_{a}(dx^{a})^{2}
, and observe that
\mathscr{M}
is the future of the initial hypersurface
\{t=1\}
. We also introduce the
outgoing light cone

\hskip 20.00003pt\mathscr{L}:=\big\{r=t-1\big\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3+1}

(1.10)

and its constant-
t
slices are denoted by
\mathscr{L}_{t}
.

More precisely, we rely on global coordinate functions
x^{\alpha}:\mathscr{M}\to\mathbb{R}
(with
\alpha=0,1,2,3
) satisfying
the wave gauge conditions

\Box_{g}x^{\alpha}=0,

(1.11)

and we express the Einstein equations as a nonlinear system of second-order partial differential equations, supplemented with second-order constraints. The unknowns are the metric coefficients
g_{\alpha\beta}
and the matter field
\phi
. Specifically, we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Box}_{g}g_{\alpha\beta}=\mathbb{F}_{\alpha\beta}(g,g;\partial g,\partial g)-16\pi\,\big(\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+U(\phi)g_{\alpha\beta}\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{\Box}_{g}\phi-U^{\prime}(\phi)=\,0,

(1.12)

where

\widetilde{\Box}_{g}:=g^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\partial_{\alpha^{\prime}}\partial_{\beta^{\prime}}
denotes a modified wave operator, and the wave gauge constraints take the form

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=g^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}=0,\hskip 20.00003pt\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}={1\over 2}\,g^{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\big(\partial_{\alpha}g_{\beta\lambda^{\prime}}+\partial_{\beta}g_{\alpha\lambda^{\prime}}-\partial_{\lambda^{\prime}}g_{\alpha\beta}\big).

(1.13)

The nonlinearities
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{P}+\mathbb{Q}
have an important structure especially in connection with the Euclidian-hyperboloidal foliation, which is described and play a central role in
[
30
]
.

Merging the Minkowski and the Schwarzschild solutions.

In wave coordinates the Schwarzschild metric
g_{\textbf{Sch}}
takes the form (with
\omega_{a}:=x_{a}/r
)

\displaystyle g_{\textbf{Sch},00}

\displaystyle=-\frac{r-m}{r+m},\hskip 20.00003ptg_{\textbf{Sch},0a}=0,\hskip 20.00003ptg_{\textbf{Sch},ab}=\frac{r+m}{r-m}\omega_{a}\omega_{b}+\frac{(r+m)^{2}}{r^{2}}(\delta_{ab}-\omega_{a}\omega_{b}).

(1.14)

Consider a (regular) cut-off function
\chi^{\star}(r)
that vanishes for
r\leq 1/2
and is identically
1
for all
r\geq 3/4
. Given a (small) mass coefficient
m>0
we introduce the reference metric

g^{\star}=g_{\textbf{Mink}}+\chi^{\star}(r)\,\chi^{\star}(r/(t-1))(g_{\textbf{Sch}}-g_{\textbf{Mink}}),\hskip 20.00003ptt\geq 2.

(1.15)

This metric coincides with
g_{\textbf{Mink}}
in the cone
\big\{r/(t-1)<1/2\big\}
and coincides with
g_{\textbf{Sch}}
in the exterior
\big\{r/(t-1)\geq 3/4\big\}
which contains the light cone
\mathscr{L}
. This metric satisfies the so-called light-bending property, saying by definition that the light cone coefficient

r\,g^{\star}(\mathbf{l},\mathbf{l})=4m+\mathcal{O}(1/r)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ for the metric }g^{\star},

(1.16)

is positive, where the
light cone direction
is defined by

\mathbf{l}:=\partial_{t}-(x^{a}/r)\partial_{a}.

(1.17)

This is essentially the same construction as in
[
35
]
.

Class of initial data sets.

We are interested in solutions to the Einstein-matter system in the harmonic regime (corresponding to
\lambda=1
in
[
30
]
) and we establish a sharp decay property in the case where the reference metric is constructed from the Schwarzschild metric. The initial metric
g_{0}
is assumed to be sufficiently close to the Euclidean metric while the initial second fundamental form
k_{0}
is sufficiently small. Let us introduce the following decomposition (and
a,b=1,2,3
)

g_{0ab}=g^{\star}_{0ab}+u_{0ab}=\delta_{ab}+h^{\star}_{0ab}+u_{0ab},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002ptk_{0ab}=k^{\star}_{0ab}+l_{0ab}.

(1.18)

We aim at covering a variety of asymptotic behaviors and, at this juncture, it is convenient to introduce the following terminology.

•

The part
h^{\star}_{0}
is referred to as the
initial reference
and will be assumed to be small in a (weighted, high-order) pointwise norm.

•

The part
u_{0}
is referred to as the
initial perturbation
and will be assumed to be small in (weighted, high-order) energy norm.

An example of a such decomposition is provided by the construction in Lindblad and Rodnianski
[
35
]
, where the initial data is decomposed as the sum of a finite-energy perturbation plus an (asymptotically) Schwarzschild metric outside of a compact set (with sufficiently small and positive mass). In our theory, the two parts are treated differently. Indeed,
h_{0}^{\star}
is the initial trace of
h^{\star}
while
u_{0}
is propagated.

For the metric perturbation, we introduce the energy norms

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\text{metric}}_{\kappa,N}(g_{0},k_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq N}\big\|\langle r\rangle^{\kappa+|I|}\big(|\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{x}u_{0}|+|\partial_{x}^{I}u_{1}|\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

(1.19)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\text{matter}}_{\mu,N}(\phi_{0},\phi_{1}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq N}\big\|\langle r\rangle^{\mu+|I|}(|\partial_{x}^{I}\partial_{x}\phi_{0}|+|\partial_{x}^{I}\phi_{0}|+|\partial_{x}^{I}\phi_{1}|)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

While our proof below will provide a more general result as stated below, we find it convenient to state first our result for the perturbation of the Schwarzschild solution.

Given an initial data set we decompose it according to (
1.18
) and we introduce the
linear development
denoted by
u_{\textbf{init}}
of the initial data set
(u_{0\alpha\beta},u_{1\alpha\beta})
, that is, we introduce the solution to the (free, linear) wave equation with this initial data. It was established in
[
30
, Proposition
]
that, under the assumption that the norm above is finite,

|u_{\textbf{init}}|\lesssim C_{0}\epsilon(t+r+1)^{-1}.

(1.20)

Our main assumption beyond the smallness on the norms (
1.19
) is the following sign condition which we referred to as the
light-bending condition

\inf_{\mathscr{M}^{\textbf{near}}_{\ell}}\big(4m+r\,u_{\textbf{init}}(\mathbf{l},\mathbf{l})\big)\geq\epsilon_{\star}.

(1.21)

Here, a parameter
\ell\in(0,1/2]
being fixed once for all, we have defined the
near-light cone
domain to be

\mathscr{M}^{\textbf{near}}_{\ell}:=\Big\{t\geq 2,\hskip 10.00002ptt-1\leq r\leq\frac{t}{1-\ell}\Big\},

(1.22)

where in agreement with
[
30
]
it is convenient to restrict attention to
t\geq 2
. Interestingly, this condition can be easily satisfied when

•

either
\epsilon
is small with respect to
\epsilon_{\star}
, so that the contribution
m
from the Schwarzschild metric dominates,

•

both initial data
u_{0}\geq 0
and
u_{1}\geq 0
are non-negative (as is clear from the fact that fundamental solution to the wave equation is a non-negative measure),

•

or yet a combination of the above two extreme examples, namely, the negative contribution of the perturbation is small with respect to the Schwarzschild mass.

Main statement.

We are in a position to state our main result. In fact, a more general statement concerning perturbations of reference metrics with harmonic decay is actually established in this paper; see Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 1.1

(Nonlinear stability of self-gravitating Klein-Gordon fields. Perturbations of the Schwarzschild solution)
.

A constant
C_{\star}>0
being fixed, the following result holds for all sufficiently small
\epsilon,\epsilon_{\star}
satisfying
\epsilon\leq C_{\star}\epsilon_{\star}
. Consider the reference metric
g^{\star}
defined in (
1.15
) by merging together the Minkowski metric
g_{\textbf{Mink}}
and the Schwarzschild metric with mass
\epsilon_{\star}
along a light cone. Consider a set of contraint-satisfying initial data
(g_{0},k_{0},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
, a large integer
N
, and some exponents
(\kappa,\mu,\epsilon)

satisfying

\kappa\in(1/2,1),\hskip 20.00003pt\mu\in(3/4,1),\hskip 20.00003pt\kappa\leq\mu.

(1.23)

Then provided the initial data satisfies the light bending condition (
1.21
) together with the smallness condition

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\text{metric}}_{\kappa,N}(g_{0},k_{0})+\mathbf{E}^{\text{matter}}_{\mu,N}(\phi_{0},\phi_{1})\leq\epsilon,

(1.24)

the maximal globally hyperbolic Cauchy development of
(g_{0},k_{0},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
associated with the Einstein-massive field system (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) is future causally geodesically complete, and asymptotically approaches Minkowski spacetime in all (timelike, null, spacetime) directions. Moreover, the component
g(\mathbf{l},\mathbf{l})
has a harmonic decay and enjoys the light bending condition, namely

|g(\mathbf{l},\mathbf{l})|\lesssim{\epsilon_{\star}+\epsilon\over t+r+1},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\inf_{\mathscr{M}^{\textbf{near}}_{\ell}}r\,g(\mathbf{l},\mathbf{l})\geq\epsilon_{\star}/2.

(1.25)

Harmonic decay.

Our proof of Theorems
1.1
and
3.1
follows from the general method in
[
30
]
in which we now take the parameter
\lambda
therein to be the critical value
\lambda=1
.
We would like to point out here several significant differences and several new ingredients that are required for the proof in the present paper.
First of all, the use of a hierarchy property for commutators was not necessary for the range
\lambda<1
, but turns out to be essential now. On the other hand, we revisit
[
30
, Section
]
concerning the pointwise decay of metric components (and their derivatives) and we use a decomposition of the wave operators
in which the component
H^{\mathcal{N}}{}^{00}
is directly controlled.
In turn, in the analysis in
[
30
, Section
]
we now decouple the contributions of the Hessian and of the commutators. Interestingly, this leads to significant simplifications in comparison with the arguments in
[
30
]
, while simultaneously new estimates are required.
For instance, in the analysis of the (now harmonic) decay of the null metric component in
[
30
, Section
]
, we no longer introduce on the “loss” exponent
\theta
. In order to recover the desired
1/r
behavior for the metric, the application of the Kirchhoff formula must now be done at the
1/r
level of decay, and this requires a sharp control of the source-terms in the wave equation. Such terms are due to the Ricci curvature of the reference metric (assumed to have sufficient decay, and even to vanish for the Schwarzschild metric), the commutators (discussed earlier) and the quasilinear terms (also discussed earlier). Observe that it is also important to distinguish between zero-order estimates (i.e. without differentiation) and high-order estimates.
Finally, we point out that, interestingly, our results also apply in the
vacuum spacetimes
by taking
\phi
to vanish identically: our proof then is somewhat simpler than the one in
[
36
]
, thanks to the fact that it takes advantage of the light-bending condition —a consequence of the assumed positivity of the mass of the Schwarzschild metric.

2
Euclidean-hyperboloidal foliation and functional inequalities

2.1
Spacetime foliation and vector fields

Preliminary.

We provide here an overview of the technical tools introduced by the authors in
[
30
]
when developing the Euclidean-Hyperboloidal Foliation Method. This method generally applies to establish the global existence of solutions with small amplitude for coupled systems of wave and Klein-Gordon equations. The method relies on the following key ingredients.

•

Construction of a spacetime foliation consisting of asymptotically hyperboloidal slices and asymptotically flat slices.

•

Functional analysis tools on such a foliation: admissible vector fields, Sobolev inequalities, and energy estimates.

•

Sup-norm estimates for wave equations and Klein-Gordon equations.

For the full set of notions and results, we refer to
[
30
]
.

Foliation of interest.

In order to foliate
\mathscr{M}
by spacelike hypersurfaces, we introduce a cut-off function
\chi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
satisfying

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\leq 0,\\
1,&x>1.\end{cases}

(2.1a)

We also introduce the
hyperboloidal and Euclidean radii
at a time
s

{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{H}}(s):={1\over 2}(s^{2}-1),\hskip 20.00003pt{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{E}}(s):={1\over 2}(s^{2}+1),

(2.1b)

Then, a function
\xi
referred to as the
foliation coefficient
is defined by

\xi(s,r):=1-\chi(r-{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{H}}(s))=\begin{cases}1,\quad&r<{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{H}}(s),\\
0,\quad&r>{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{E}}(s).\end{cases}

(2.1c)

We next define the
Euclidean–hyperboloidal time function

T=\mathbf{T}(s,r)
by solving the ordinary differential equation

\partial_{r}\mathbf{T}(s,r)=\frac{r\,\xi(s,r)}{(s^{2}+r^{2})^{1/2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathbf{T}(s,0)=s.

(2.1d)

In turn, our foliation
\mathscr{M}_{s}:=\{(t,x^{a})\in\mathscr{M}\,/\,t=\mathbf{T}(s,r)\}
is a one-parameter family of asymptotically Euclidean, spacelike hypersurfaces. The future of the initial surface
\{t=1\}
is decomposed as

\{t\geq 1\}=\mathscr{M}^{\textbf{init}}\cup\bigcup_{s\geq 2}\mathscr{M}_{s},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathscr{M}_{s}=\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\cup\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{M}}_{s}\cup\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{s},

(2.2a)

with
\mathscr{M}^{\textbf{init}}:=\{(t,x)\,/\,1\leq t\leq T(2,r)\}
and

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{H}}_{s}:

\displaystyle=\big\{t=\mathbf{T}(s,|x|),\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt|x|\leq{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{H}}(s)\big\}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{asymptotically hyperboloidal,}

(2.2b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{M}}_{s}:

\displaystyle=\big\{t=\mathbf{T}(s,|x|),\hskip 10.00002pt\,{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{H}}(s)\leq|x|\leq{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{E}}(s)\big\}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{merging (or transition),}

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{s}:

\displaystyle=\big\{t=\mathbf{T}(s),\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{E}}(s)\leq|x|\big\}

\displaystyle\text{asymptotically Euclidean}.

We also write
\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{EM}}_{s}:=\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{E}}\cup\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{M}}
.

Frames of interest.

The following terminology will be used.

•

The semi-hyperboloidal frame
(SHF)

\partial^{\mathcal{H}}_{0}:=\partial_{t},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\partial^{\mathcal{H}}_{a}=\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{H}}_{a}:=\frac{x^{a}}{t}\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}

(2.3a)

is defined in
\mathscr{M}_{s}
and is the appropriate frame in the hyperboloidal domain in order to establish the relevant
decay properties in timelike and null directions.
Some of our arguments will involve radial integration based on

\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{H}}_{r}:=(x^{a}/r)\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
.

•

The semi-null frame
(SNF)

\partial^{\mathcal{N}}_{0}:=\partial_{t},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\partial^{\mathcal{N}}_{a}=\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{N}}_{a}:={x^{a}\over r}\partial_{t}+\partial_{a}

(2.3b)

is defined in
\mathscr{M}_{s}

except
on the center line
r=0
, and is the appropriate frame within the Euclidean-merging domain in order to exhibit the structure of the nonlinearities of the field equations and, in turn, to establish the relevant
decay properties in spatial and null directions
.

•

The Euclidean–hyperboloidal frame
(EHF) is defined as

\displaystyle\partial^{\mathcal{E\hskip-1.70709ptH}}_{0}:=\partial_{t},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\partial^{\mathcal{E\hskip-1.70709ptH}}_{a}

\displaystyle=\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{EH}}_{a}:=\partial_{a}+(x^{a}/r)\partial_{r}\mathbf{T}\,\partial_{t}=\partial_{a}+x^{a}\xi(s,r)(s^{2}+r^{2})^{-1/2}\partial_{t}

(2.3c)

consists of tangent vectors
\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{EH}}_{a}
to the slices
\mathscr{M}_{s}
. Observe that
\partial^{\mathcal{E\hskip-1.70709ptH}}_{a}=\partial^{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
in
\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{H}}_{s}
, while
\partial^{\mathcal{E\hskip-1.70709ptH}}_{a}=\partial_{a}
in
\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{E}}_{s}
. The expressions of the vectors
\partial^{\mathcal{E\hskip-1.70709ptH}}_{a}
are more involved in the merging
\mathscr{M}^{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
, where the vectors
\partial^{\mathcal{E\hskip-1.70709ptH}}_{a}
interpolate between
\partial^{\mathcal{H}}_{a}
and
\partial_{a}
. Some of our arguments will involve radial integration based on

\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{EH}}_{r}:=(x^{a}/r)\not{\partial}^{\mathcal{EH}}_{a}
.

Admissible vector fields.

Minkowski spacetime admits three sets of
Killing fields
.

•

The spacetime translations generated by the vector fields
\partial_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=0,1,2,3
).

•

The Lorentz boosts generated by the vector fields

L_{a}:=x_{a}\partial_{t}+t\,\partial_{a}
(
a=1,2,3
).

•

The spatial rotations generated by the vector fields

\Omega_{ab}:=x_{a}\partial_{b}-x_{b}\partial_{a}
(
a,b=1,2,3
).

We refer to
\partial_{\alpha},L_{a},\Omega_{ab}
as the
admissible fields
which commute with the wave and Klein-Gordon operators in Minkowski spacetime. In defining high-order norms, we combine admissible vector fields together. An operator
Z=\partial^{I}L^{J}\Omega^{K}
is called an
ordered admissible operator.
To such an operator
Z=\partial^{I}L^{J}\Omega^{K}
, we associate its

Weighted energy inequality.

For the foliation under consideration, the fundamental energy functional associated with the wave equation and, more generally, the Klein-Gordon equations involve the weight
\zeta=\zeta(t,x)
defined by

\zeta(s,r)^{2}:=1-\frac{r^{2}\xi^{2}(s,r)}{s^{2}+r^{2}}=\begin{cases}s^{2}/t^{2},\quad&r<{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{H}}(s),\\
1,\quad&r>{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{E}}(s).\end{cases}

(2.5)

In addition, we introduce a weight which reduces to a constant in the interior of the light cone and is essentially the distance to the light cone in the exterior domain. Throughout, we are given a smooth and non-decreasing function

for

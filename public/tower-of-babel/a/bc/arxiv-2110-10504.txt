Analogs of Bol operators on superstrings
Sofiane Bouarroudj a,∗ , Dimitry Leites a,b , Irina Shchepochkina c

Abstract
. The Bol operators are unary differential operators between spaces of weighted densities on the 1-dimensional manifold invariant under projective transformations of the manifold. On the 1 | n 1|n -dimensional supermanifold (superstring) ℳ \mathcal{M} , we classify analogs of Bol operators invariant under the simple maximal subalgebra 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} of the same rank as its simple ambient superalgebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} of vector fields on ℳ \mathcal{M} and containing all elements of negative degree of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} in a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -grading. We also consider the Lie superalgebras of vector fields 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} preserving a contact structure on the superstring ℳ \mathcal{M} . We have discovered many new operators.

1.1.
Veblen’s problem

On a given

{\mathcal{M}}

of diffeomorphisms of

by invertible changes of coordinates as follows:

A(t)(X):=\rho(J_{A})(t(A^{-1}(X))\text{~~for any $A\in\mathop{\mathrm{Diff}}\nolimits{\mathcal{M}}$, $X\in{\mathcal{M}}$, $t\in T(V)$}.

In 1928, at the IMC, O. Veblen

(see

]

Being purely algebraic and local (hence,

, see
[

invariant operator from the space of densities with compact support to the space of functions; for just one more known example of

invariant operator, first discovered by A.A. Kirillov only by its symbol, see

A laconic way to describe

\bullet

Odd form

. For any

\bullet

Even form
\alpha_{0}
. For any

M_{f}=(2-E)(f){\frac{\partial}{\partial{\tau}}}-Le_{f}-(-1)^{p(f)}{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\tau}}}E,

(here the
y_{i}
are
all the coordinates except
\tau

(27)

Le_{f}=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i\leq n}\left({\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{q_{i}}}}\ {\frac{\partial}{\partial{\xi_{i}}}}+(-1)^{p(f)}{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\xi_{i}}}}\ {\frac{\partial}{\partial{q_{i}}}}\right).

(28)

\begin{array}[]{l}L_{K_{f}}(\alpha_{1})=2{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}}\alpha_{1}=K_{1}(f)\alpha_{1},\\
L_{M_{f}}(\alpha_{0})=-(-1)^{p(f)}2{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\tau}}}\alpha_{0}=-(-1)^{p(f)}M_{1}(f)\alpha_{0},\end{array}

it follows that
K_{f}\in{\mathfrak{k}}(2n+1|m)
and
M_{f}\in{\mathfrak{m}}(n)
. (These vector fields actually span the respective Lie superalgebras.)
Observe that

p(Le_{f})=p(M_{f})=p(f)+{\bar{1}}.

\bullet
To the (super)commutators
[K_{f},K_{g}]
or
[M_{f},M_{g}]

there correspond

of the generating functions:

\begin{array}[]{l}{}[K_{f},K_{g}]=K_{\{f,\;g\}_{k.b.}};\qquad{}[M_{f},M_{g}]=M_{\{f,\;g\}_{m.b.}}.\end{array}

The explicit formulas for the contact brackets are as follows. Let

(resp.
\tau
).

The

\{-,-\}_{P.b.}

in the realization with the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}\{f,g\}_{P.b.}&=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i\leq n}\ \bigg(\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{p_{i}}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{q_{i}}}}-\ {\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{q_{i}}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{p_{i}}}}\bigg)\\
&-(-1)^{p(f)}\bigg(\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\theta}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{\theta}}}+\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j\leq m}\left(\displaystyle{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\xi_{j}}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{\eta_{j}}}}+{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\eta_{j}}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{\xi_{j}}}}\right)\bigg)\\
&\text{ for any }f,g\in{\mathbb{C}}[p,q,\xi,\eta,\theta].\end{array}

The
Buttin bracket

\{-,-\}_{B.b.}
is given by the formula

(30)

\{f,g\}_{B.b.}=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i\leq n}\ \bigg({\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{q_{i}}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{\xi_{i}}}}+(-1)^{p(f)}\ {\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{\xi_{i}}}}\ {\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{q_{i}}}}\bigg)\text{ for any }f,g\in{\mathbb{C}}[q,\xi].

In terms of the Poisson and Buttin brackets,

(31)

\{f,g\}_{k.b.}=(2-E)(f){\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{t}}}-{\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial{t}}}(2-E)(g)-\{f,g\}_{P.b.}

The case
{\mathfrak{k}}(1|2)

This case is exceptional. The algebra
{\mathfrak{k}}(1|2)_{0}
of degree 0 elements is commutative and
\dim{\mathfrak{k}}(1|2)_{0}=2
, so there are 2 traces on
{\mathfrak{k}}(1|2)_{0}

{\mathfrak{k}}(1|2)

Let
\alpha_{1}:=dt+\xi d\eta+\xi d\eta

\tilde{K}_{\xi}(f)=(-1)^{p(f)}(\partial_{\eta}-\xi\partial_{t})(f),\ \tilde{K}_{\eta}(f)=(-1)^{p(f)}(\partial_{\xi}-\eta\partial_{t})(f)\text{~~and~~}\tilde{K}_{1}=\partial_{t}

(33)

\mathop{\text{\rm div}}\nolimits_{2}:=\tilde{K}_{\eta}\tilde{K}_{\xi}-\tilde{K}_{1}.

Let
\beta
be the symbol of the class of a pseudo-differential form that can be “visualized” as
\beta:=\sqrt{[d\xi(d\eta)^{-1}]}
. Its actual expression is irrelevant, we need to know only how the Lie derivative acts on it. Let

be the weight with respect to

and

(34)

{\text{wht}}(\xi)=(1,1)
and

.

Since it is natural to have a unit of weight be equal to 1, not 2, we take the square root.

The Lie derivative acts as follows

(35)

\begin{array}[]{l}L_{K_{f}}(\alpha_{1}^{a}\beta^{b})=(2a\partial_{t}(f)+(-1)^{p(f)}b\mathop{\text{\rm div}}\nolimits_{2}(K_{f}))(\alpha_{1}^{a}\beta^{b})\\
=((2a-(-1)^{p(f)}b)\partial_{t}(f)+(-1)^{p(f)}b\tilde{K}_{\eta}\tilde{K}_{\xi}(f))(\alpha_{1}^{a}\beta^{b}).\end{array}

The space

of weighted densities over

. (The above description is OK unless the characteristic of the ground field is equal to
2
, cf.
[
BGLLS
]

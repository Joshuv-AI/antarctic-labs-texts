Flat comodules and contramodules as directed colimits, and cotorsion periodicity
Leonid Positselski

Abstract
. This paper is a follow-up to [ 47 ] . We consider two algebraic settings of comodules over a coring and contramodules over a topological ring with a countable base of two-sided ideals. These correspond to two (noncommutative) algebraic geometry settings of certain kind of stacks and ind-affine ind-schemes. In the context of a coring 𝒞 \mathcal{C} over a noncommutative ring A A , we show that all A A -flat 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -comodules are ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -directed colimits of A A -countably presentable A A -flat 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -comodules. In the context of a complete, separated topological ring ℜ \mathfrak{R} with a countable base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of two-sided ideals, we prove that all flat ℜ \mathfrak{R} -contramodules are ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -directed colimits of countably presentable flat ℜ \mathfrak{R} -contramodules. We also describe arbitrary complexes, short exact sequences, and pure acyclic complexes of A A -flat 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -comodules and flat ℜ \mathfrak{R} -contramodules as ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} -directed colimits of similar complexes of countably presentable objects. The arguments are based on a very general category-theoretic technique going back to an unpublished 1977 preprint of Ulmer and rediscovered in [ 38 ] . Applications to cotorsion periodicity and coderived categories of flat objects in the respective settings are discussed. In particular, in any acyclic complex of cotorsion ℜ \mathfrak{R} -contramodules, all the contramodules of cocycles are cotorsion.



Introduction

The classical
Govorov–Lazard theorem

[
18
,
23
]
says
that all flat modules (over an arbitrary associative ring
R
) are
directed colimits of projective modules, and in fact, even of finitely
generated free
R
-modules.
In the context of algebraic geometry, over a nonaffine scheme
X
,
there are usually
not
enough projective objects in the abelian
category of quasi-coherent sheaves
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
, which makes the role of
flat quasi-coherent sheaves even more important than the role of
flat modules in module theory.
What should a suitable version of the Govorov–Lazard theorem say
about flat quasi-coherent sheaves?

One could try to use locally free or locally projective quasi-coherent
sheaves in the role of projective modules.
But it is still an open problem whether there are enough locally
projective quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
under any reasonable
assumptions on a scheme
X

[
52
]
.
On the other hand, it is known that there are enough flat
quasi-coherent sheaves on any quasi-compact semi-separated
scheme
[
25
, Section 2.4]
,
[
12
, Lemma A.1]
.
So describing flat quasi-coherent sheaves is a worthwhile undertaking.

The approach in this paper follows the idea that, in many homological
algebra contexts, one can use objects of finite projective dimension
in lieu of projective ones.
It is known that any countably presentable flat
R
-module has
projective dimension
\leq 1

[
17
, Corollary 2.23]
.
Thus our suggested answer to the question in the first paragraph is
this: the Govorov–Lazard theorem in algebraic geometry should tell us
that any flat sheaf is a directed colimit of locally countably
presentable flat ones.
Notice, for comparison, that any finitely presentable flat module is
projective.

A partial result was obtained in the paper
[
16
]
, where it was
shown that, under certain additional assumptions on a quasi-compact
semi-separated scheme
X
, any flat quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
is
a directed colimit of locally countably generated flat quasi-coherent
sheaves locally of projective dimension
\leq 1

[
16
, Theorem B or Theorem 4.9]
.
In full generality, the desired assertion for quasi-compact
quasi-separated schemes (or even more generally, for countably
quasi-compact, countably quasi-separated schemes)

was proved in
the preprint
[
47
]
: any flat quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
is
an
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimit of locally countably presentable
flat quasi-coherent sheaves
[
47

.
Here an
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimit
means the directed colimit
of a diagram indexed by an
\aleph_{1}
-directed poset, i. e., a poset
in which every countable subset has an upper bound.

The present paper aims to extend the results of
[

]
to two
algebraic geometry settings more general than schemes: the stacks

In fact, we don’t assume our rings to be commutative in this paper,

1.
Preliminaries on Accessible Categories

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal.
We refer to
[
1

Definition 1.13(1), and Remark 1.21]
for the discussion of

\kappa
-directed posets

\kappa

Let

\kappa
-directed (equivalently,

\kappa

An object

is said to be
\kappa

[
1
, Definition 1.13(2)]

We will denote the class of all

-presentable objects of

by
\mathsf{C}_{<\kappa}\subset\mathsf{C}
.
The
\aleph_{0}
-presentable objects are called finitely presentable
(see
[
1
, Definition 1.1]
); and the

-presentable objects
can be similarly called

.

This category-theoretic terminology is consistent with

Given an associative ring

, an

is

-presentable as an object of the category of left

Given an additive category

, we denote by

the category of (unbounded) cochain complexes in

.
Clearly, if

-directed colimits exist in

The following lemma is standard.

Lemma 1.1
.

(a)

(b)
Let
\kappa
be an

be an additive category with

Then any complex of
\kappa
-presentable objects of

-presentable object in

Proof.

Part (a) holds, because directed colimits commute with finite limits

Part (b) is true due to the fact that

-directed colimits
commute with countable limits of abelian groups.
∎

A category

with

.
A

.

The Govorov–Lazard description of flat

]

[

]
,

The following lemma illustrates the significance of countably
presentable (i. e.,
\aleph_{1}
-presentable) flat modules.

Lemma 1.3
.

Any countably presentable flat module over an associative ring

The second assertion of the lemma follows from the first one

2.
Preliminaries on Corings and Comodules

The following definitions seem to go back to Sweedler’s
paper
[
51
]
.
A detailed exposition can be found in the book
[
6
]
.
The book
[
28
]
and the survey paper
[
30
, Sections 2.5
and 3.4]
can be used as further references.

A
coring
over an associative ring
A
is a comonoid object in
the monoidal category of
A
-
A
-bimodules with respect to
the tensor product over
A
.
In other words, a coring
\mathcal{C}
over
A
is an
A
-
A
-bimodule
endowed with
A
-
A
-bimodule maps of
comultiplication

\mu\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}
and
counit

\epsilon\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow A

satisfying the usual coassociativity and counitality axioms.
Specifically, the two compositions
(\mu\otimes{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{C}})\circ\mu
and

({\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\mu)\circ\mu
must be equal to each other,

\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C},

and both the compositions
(\epsilon\otimes{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{C}})\circ\mu
and

({\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\epsilon)\circ\mu
must be equal to
the identity map
{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{C}}
,

\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{C}.

The category of left
A
-modules
A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is a left module category
(with respect to the tensor product operation) over the monoidal
category of
A
-
A
-bimodules
A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Bimod--}}}A
.
A
left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule
is a comodule object in the module
category
A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
over the comonoid object
\mathcal{C}
in the monoidal
category
A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Bimod--}}}A
.
In other words, a left comodule
\mathcal{M}
over
\mathcal{C}
is a left
A
-module
endowed with a left
A
-module map of
left coaction

\nu\colon\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}
satisfying the usual coassociativity and
counitality axioms together with the comultiplication map
\mu
and
the counit map
\epsilon
of the coring
\mathcal{C}
.
Specifically, the two compositions
(\mu\otimes{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{M}})\circ\nu
and

({\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\nu)\circ\nu
must be equal to each other,

\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M},

and the composition
(\epsilon\otimes{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{M}})\circ\nu
must be equal to
the identity map
{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathcal{M}}
,

\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}.

We denote the additive category of left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules by

\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
, and use the notation
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}({-},{-})
for the groups
of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.
A
\mathcal{C}
-comodule is said to be
A
-flat
if it is flat as
an
A
-module.
The full subcategory of
A
-flat left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules is denoted
by
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}_{A{\operatorname{\mathsf{-flat}}}}\subset\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a coring over a ring
A
.

(a)
All colimits exist in the additive category of
left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules.
The forgetful functor
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
preserves colimits
(in particular, coproducts).

(b)
If
\mathcal{C}
is a flat right
A
-module, then the category

\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
is a Grothendieck abelian category, and the forgetful
functor
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is exact.

(c)
Conversely, if the category
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
is abelian

and
the forgetful functor
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\longrightarrow A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is exact,
then
\mathcal{C}
is a flat right
A
-module.

(d)
There exists a coring
\mathcal{C}
such that the category

\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
is
not
abelian.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from the fact that the tensor product functor

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}{-}
preserves colimits in
A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Parts (b–c) are covered by
[
6
, Sections 18.6, 18.14
and 18.16]
; see also
[
30
, Proposition 2.12(a)]
.
A counterexample for part (d) can be found
in
[
29
, Example C.1.1]
.
∎

When
\mathcal{C}
is not a flat right
A
-module, exact sequences of
arbitrary left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules are not well-behaved.
But there is still a well-behaved notion of a
short exact
sequence of
A
-flat left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules
, defined as
a short sequence of
A
-flat left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules that is exact
in

We refer to the survey
[
7
]

Lemma 2.2
.

The additive category of
A
-flat
\mathcal{C}
-comodules

\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}_{A{\operatorname{\mathsf{-flat}}}}
endowed with the class of short exact sequences
defined above is an exact category.

Proof.

Essentially, it suffices to check that the kernel of any surjective
morphism of
A
-flat
\mathcal{C}
-comodules exists in
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}_{A{\operatorname{\mathsf{-flat}}}}

and agrees with the kernel of the same morphism computed in
the category of flat
A
-modules.
For this purpose, one needs to use the fact that the functors

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}{-}
and
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}{-}
take short exact sequences
of flat
A
-modules to short exact sequences of
A
-modules.
∎

More generally, an exact sequence of left
A
-modules is called

pure
if it stays exact after the functor

is
applied to it, for every right
A
-module

.
An
A

\mathcal{C}
-comodules

\mathcal{C}
-comodules that is pure exact as

A
-modules.
The category of left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
with
A
-pure short

A left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule is said to be

-module.
Similarly, a
\mathcal{C}
-comodule is said to be

-finitely

-module.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a coring over a ring
A
. Then

(a)

-finitely presentable
\mathcal{C}
-comodules are

Example 2.5
.

This example is due to Kontsevich and
Rosenberg
[
21
, Section 2]
,
[
22
]
.
Let
X
be a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme, and let

X=\bigcup_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
be a finite covering of
X
by affine
open subschemes.
Let
U
denote the disjoint union
U=\coprod_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
; so

U
is an affine scheme and there is a natural surjective flat affine
morphism of schemes
U\longrightarrow X
.
Let
A=\mathcal{O}(U)
and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{O}(U\times_{X}U)
denote the rings of functions
on the affine schemes
U
and
U\times_{X}U
.
Then the two projections
p_{1}
,
p_{2}\colon U\times_{X}U\rightrightarrows U

induce two ring homomorphisms
A\rightrightarrows\mathcal{C}
, endowing
\mathcal{C}

with two
A
-module structures (which we view as the “left” and
the “right” one).

The diagonal morphism
\delta\colon U\longrightarrow U\times_{X}U
induces a ring
homomorphism
\delta^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow A
.
Furthermore, we have
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{O}((U\times_{X}U)\times_{U}(U\times_{X}U))=\mathcal{O}(U\times_{X}U\times_{X}U)
.
The projection
q\colon U\times_{X}U\times_{X}U\longrightarrow U\times_{X}U
dropping
the second (inner) coordinate induces a ring homomorphism

q^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}
.
The maps
\epsilon=\delta^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow A
and
\mu=q^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}
that we have constructed endow
the
A
-
A
-bimodule
\mathcal{C}
with a coring structure
(over the commutative ring
A
).

For any quasi-coherent sheaf
N
on
X
, the inverse image of
N

with respect to the morphism
U\longrightarrow X
produces a quasi-coherent
sheaf
M
on
U
, that is an
A
-module
\mathcal{M}
.
The inverse images of
M
with respect to the two projections

U\times_{X}U\rightrightarrows U
are naturally isomorphic as
quasi-coherent sheaves on
U\times_{X}U
, that is
p_{1}^{*}M\simeq p_{2}^{*}M
.
The corresponding bimodules of sections are
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}
and

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}
; so we have a natural isomorphism of
A
-
A
-bimodules

\mathcal{M}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}\simeq\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}
.
Precomposing this isomorphism with the map
\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{C}

induced by the “left” map
A\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}
(induced by
p_{1}
), we obtain
a map
\nu\colon\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\otimes_{A}\mathcal{M}
, endowing
\mathcal{M}
with a left

\mathcal{C}
-comodule structure.
The assignment
N\longmapsto\mathcal{M}
is an equivalence of abelian categories

X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\simeq\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.
Here
X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
denotes the abelian category of quasi-coherent sheaves
on
X
.

Remark 2.6
.

A quasi-coherent sheaf
F
on a scheme
X
is said to be
flat
if
the tensor product functor
F\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}{-}\,\colon X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}\longrightarrow X{\operatorname{\mathsf{--qcoh}}}
is
exact, or equivalently, the
\mathcal{O}(U)
-module
F(U)
is flat for every
affine open subscheme
U\subset X
.
It suffices to check the latter condition for the affine open
subschemes
U=U_{\alpha}
from a given affine open covering

X=\bigcup_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
of the scheme
X
.

Now let
X
be a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme.
Then it follows that, in the context of
Example
2.5
, a quasi-coherent sheaf on
X
is
flat if and only if the corresponding
\mathcal{C}
-comodule is
A
-flat.

By
[
25
, Section 2.4]
or
[

, Lemma A.1]
, any quasi-coherent
sheaf on a quasi-compact semi-separated scheme
X
is a quotient sheaf
of a flat quasi-coherent sheaf.
Therefore, the corings
\mathcal{C}
appearing in
Example
2.5
also have the property that any

\mathcal{C}
-comodule is a quotient comodule of an
A
-flat
\mathcal{C}
-comodule.

The following theorem describing

-flat

Lemma 4.1

We obtain a commutative diagram of a morphism of short exact sequences

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P^{\prime\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

5.

In this section we deduce certain conditional results, based on
the techniques of
[
47
, Section 8]
and generalizing some results
from
[
47
, Section 9]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a coring over a ring
A
such that
\mathcal{C}
is a flat right

A
-module (so the category of left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules is abelian
by Lemma
2.1
(b)).
Consider the following condition on the coring
\mathcal{C}
:

(
*
)

Every left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule is a quotient comodule of
an
A
-flat left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule.

Two classes of corings
\mathcal{C}
known to satisfy (
*
) are described in
Remark
2.6
and
Lemma
2.4
(geometrically, these correspond
to quasi-compact semi-separated schemes and a certain kind of
smooth stacks).
We are not aware of any example of a coring
\mathcal{C}
over
A
that is flat
as a (left and) right
A
-module but does not satisfy (
*
).

We will also need a second condition:

(
{*}{*}
)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule such that
the underlying
A
-module of
\mathcal{M}
has finite projective dimension
as an object of the abelian category
A{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Then
\mathcal{M}
has a finite projective dimension as an object of
the abelian category
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.

Remark 5.1
.

The corings
\mathcal{C}
corresponding to quasi-compact semi-separated schemes

X
as per Example
2.5
satisfy (
{*}{*}
).
This is the result of
[
47
, Theorem 5.3]
.

On the other hand, when
A=k
is a field and
\mathcal{C}
is a coalgebra
over
k
, the condition (
{*}{*}
) asks the comodule category

\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
to have finite homological dimension.
This, of course, does
not
hold in general.
For example, if
\mathcal{C}
is a finite-dimensional coalgebra over
k
,
then the category of
\mathcal{C}
-comodules is equivalent to the category
of modules over the
k
-vector space dual finite-dimensional algebra,

\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}\simeq\mathcal{C}^{*}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
Taking
\mathcal{C}^{*}
to be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra of infinite
global dimension, one obtains an example when (
{*}{*}
) does not hold.

Informally speaking, (
{*}{*}
) means that “the coring
\mathcal{C}
has
finite homological dimension relative to the ring
A
”.
In the context of algebraic geometry, one can expect this to be
satisfied for many stacks in characteristic zero, such as
the quotient stacks
X/G
of algebraic varieties
X
by actions of
algebraic groups
G
(because the homological dimension of
the category of algebraic group representations does not exceed
the dimension of the group, in characteristic zero).
On the other hand, in prime characteristic
p
, the quotient of
a point by the action of a finite group of the order divisible by
p

does
not
satisfy (
{*}{*}
), as the previous paragraph explains.

See Remarks
5.7

and
6.6

below for a further discussion.

We denote by
\Ext_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}({-},{-})
the Ext groups in the abelian
category
\mathcal{C}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Comod}}}
.
A left
\mathcal{C}
-comodule
\mathcal{B}
is said to be

cotorsion

[

, Section C.2]
if
\Ext_{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B})=0

for all
A
-flat left
\mathcal{C}
-comodules

In this section we also deduce some results conditional upon

We start with an abstract category-theoretic discussion of absolutely
acyclic complexes in exact categories of finite homological dimension.
Given an additive category
\mathsf{E}
, we denote by
\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{E})

the triangulated homotopy category of (unbounded cochain
complexes in)
\mathsf{E}
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category.
Any (termwise admissible) short exact sequence of complexes in
\mathsf{E}

can be viewed as a bicomplex with three rows, so one can construct its
total complex.
A complex in
\mathsf{E}
is said to be
absolutely
acyclic

[
28
, Sections 2 and 4]
,
[
29
, Section A.1]
,

[
36
, Section 5.1]
if it belongs to the minimal thick subcategory
of the homotopy category
\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{E})
containing the totalizations of
short exact sequences in
\mathsf{E}
.
In other words, a complex in
\mathsf{E}
is absolutely acyclic if and only if
it can be constructed as a homotopy direct summand of a complex obtained
from the totalizations of short exact sequences of complexes in
\mathsf{E}

by passing to cones of closed morphisms of complexes a finite number of times.

Equivalently, the full subcategory of absolutely acyclic complexes
in
\mathsf{Com}(\mathsf{E})
can be constructed as the closure of the full subcategory
of contractible complexes under extensions (in the termwise exact
structure on
\mathsf{Com}(\mathsf{E})
) and direct summands.
This is essentially shown in
[
46
, Proposition 8.12]
.

Denote by
\Ext_{\mathsf{E}}^{*}({-},{-})
the Yoneda Ext functor in an exact
category
\mathsf{E}
.
The exact category
\mathsf{E}
is said to have
homological
dimension
\leq d
(where
d\geq-1
is an integer) if

\Ext_{\mathsf{E}}^{n}(X,Y)=0
for all
X
,
Y\in\mathsf{E}
and
n>d
.

Lemma 6.1
.

In an exact category of finite homological dimension, all acyclic
complexes are absolutely acyclic.

Proof.

This is
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathsf{E}
be an exact category of homological dimension
\leq d
,
where
d
is a finite integer.
Then any absolutely acyclic complex in
\mathsf{E}
is a direct summand of
the totalization of a
(d+\penalty 2)
-term exact complex

0\longrightarrow E^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}_{d}\longrightarrow E^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}_{d-1}\longrightarrow\dotsb\longrightarrow E^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}_{0}\longrightarrow E^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}_{-1}\longrightarrow 0
of complexes in
\mathsf{E}
.

Proof.

This result is a particular case of a similar assertion for

exact DG-categories
, which is essentially proved
in
[
36
, proof of Proposition 8.8]
.
More precisely, as a particular case of
[
36
, proof of
Proposition 8.8]
(corresponding to
[
36
, Examples 4.39
and 6.1(4)]
) one obtains the claim that any absolutely
acyclic complex
A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
in
\mathsf{E}
is a
homotopy direct summand
of
the totalization
T^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
of a
(d+\penalty 2)
-term exact complex of
complexes in
\mathsf{E}
.
Here a “homotopy direct summand” means a direct summand in
the homotopy category
\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{E})
.
It remains to show that a homotopy direct summand can be turned into
a strict direct summand.

Now we have a pair of closed morphisms of complexes
i\colon A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow T^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
and
p\colon T^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
such that the composition

pi\colon A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is homotopic to the identity morphism

{\mathrm{id}}\colon A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
.
It follows that the difference
pi-{\mathrm{id}}\colon A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
factorizes
as
A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
, where
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is the cone of
identity morphism
{\mathrm{id}}\colon A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
and
A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

is the natural closed morphism, while
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is some
arbitrary closed morphism.
Hence the identity morphism
{\mathrm{id}}\colon A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
factorizes
through the direct sum
T^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\oplus C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
as a morphism
in the category of complexes in
\mathsf{E}
.
Finally, we point out that
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is the totalization of
a
2
-term exact complex
0\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow A^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow 0

of complexes in
\mathsf{E}
.
This takes care of all cases with
d\geq 0
, while in the trivial
case of
d=-1
we have
\mathsf{E}=0
and there is nothing to prove.
∎

Let
\mathsf{F}
be an exact category.
A class of objects
\mathsf{G}\subset\mathsf{F}
is said to be

interits an exact category structure from
\mathsf{F}

See
[

, Section 12]
or
[

7.
Preliminaries on Topological Rings and Contramodules

The material of this section goes back to
[
28
, Remark A.3]
,

[
29
, Appendix E]
,
[
30
, Section 2.1]
,
and
[
43
, Section 5]
.
For more recent references, see
[
33
, Section 2]

or
[
32
, Section 2]
.
The exposition in
[
44
, Sections 6–7]
may be the most
accessible one for a beginner.
In this paper, we are mostly interested in contramodules over
complete, separated topological associative rings with a countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open two-sided ideals.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a topological abelian group where open subgroups form
a base of neighborhoods of zero.
Consider the natural map to the directed limit
\lambda_{\mathfrak{A}}\colon\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}}\mathfrak{A}/\mathfrak{U}
, where
\mathfrak{U}
ranges over
the open subgroups of
\mathfrak{A}
.
The topological group
\mathfrak{A}
is said to be
separated
if
the map
\lambda_{\mathfrak{A}}
is injective, and
complete
if

\lambda_{\mathfrak{A}}
is surjective.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with a base of
neighborhoods of zero consisting of open right ideals.
A right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
is said to be
discrete
if the action
map
\mathcal{N}\times\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}
is continuous in the given topology of
\mathfrak{R}

and the discrete topology on
\mathcal{N}
.
Equivalently, a right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
is discrete if, for every
element
x\in\mathcal{N}
, the annihilator of
x
in
\mathfrak{R}
is an open right ideal.
The full subcategory of discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\subset{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{R}
is a Grothendieck abelian category.
The inclusion functor
{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod--}}}\mathfrak{R}
is exact and preserves
all colimits.

Given an abelian group
A
and a set
X
, we use
A[X]=A^{(X)}
as
a notation for the direct sum of
X
copies of
A
.
The elements of
A[X]
are interpreted as finitely supported formal
linear combinations
\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}x
of elements of
X
with
the coefficients
a_{x}\in A
(where
a_{x}=0
for all but a finite subset
of indices
x\in X
).

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a complete, separated topological abelian group with a base
of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open subgroups.
Given a set
X
, we denote by
\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
the directed limit of abelian
groups
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}}(\mathfrak{A}/\mathfrak{U})[X]
(where
\mathfrak{U}
ranges
over the open subgroups of
\mathfrak{A}
).
The elements of
\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
are interpreted as infinite formal linear
combinations
\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}x
with the families of coefficients

a_{x}\in\mathfrak{A}

converging to zero in the topology of
\mathfrak{A}
.
The latter condition means that, for every open subgroup

\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{A}
, one has
a_{x}\in\mathfrak{U}
for all but a finite subset of
indices
x\in X
.

Given a map of sets
X\longrightarrow Y
, the induced (pushforward) map

\mathfrak{A}[[f]]\colon\mathfrak{A}[[X]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}[[Y]]
is defined by the rule

\sum_{x\in X}a_{x}x\longmapsto\sum_{y\in Y}b_{y}y
, where

b_{y}=\sum_{x\in X}^{f(x)=y}a_{x}
for every
y\in Y
.
Here the latter infinite sum (defining the element
b_{y}\in\mathfrak{A}
) is

not
formal; rather, it is understood as the limit of finite
partial sums in the topology of
\mathfrak{A}
.
Due to this construction, the assigment
X\longmapsto\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
is
a covariant functor
\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
from the category of sets to
the category of abelian groups.
We will mostly view it as an endofunctor on the category of sets,

\mathfrak{A}[[{-}]]\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
, ignoring the abelian group structures
on the sets
\mathfrak{A}[[X]]
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring where open right
ideals form a base of neighborhoods of zero.
Then the functor
\mathfrak{R}[[{-}]]\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
acquires a natural
structure of a
monad
on the category of sets.
We refer to
[
24
, Chapter VI]

(see also
[
44
, Section 6]
) for the background discussion of
monads and algebras over monads.
Specifically, for any set
X
, the monad unit
\epsilon_{X}\colon X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
is the obvious natural map taking every element
x\in X

to the formal linear combination
\sum_{y\in X}r_{y}y
, where
r_{x}=1

and
r_{y}=0
for all
y\neq x
.

The monad multiplication
\phi_{X}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{R}[[X]]]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
is
the natural “opening of parentheses” map producing a formal linear
combination from a formal linear combination of formal linear
combinations.
This construction involves taking products of pairs of elements in
\mathfrak{R}

and infinite sums of zero-convergent families of elements.
The conditions that
\mathfrak{R}
is complete, separated, and open right ideals
form a base of neighborhoods of zero guarantee the convergence.

Left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
are defined as algebras over
the monad
(\mathfrak{R}[[{-}]],\epsilon,\phi)
on the category of sets.
We usually prefer to speak of
modules
(rather than algebras)

over monads
in this context, as contramodules are more similar
to modules over rings than to algebras.
So, specifically, a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
is a set endowed
with a
left contraaction
map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}

satisfying the following
contraassociativity
and

contraunitality
axioms.
The two compositions
\pi\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
\pi\circ\mathfrak{R}[[\pi]]
must
be equal to each other,

\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]]]\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P},

and the composition
\pi\circ\epsilon_{\mathfrak{P}}
must be ideal to
the identity map
{\mathrm{id}}_{\mathfrak{P}}
,

\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}.

We denote the category of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules by
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Restricting the contraaction map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
to
the subset of finitely supported formal linear combinations

\mathfrak{R}[\mathfrak{P}]\subset\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
, one constructs the underlying left

\mathfrak{R}
-module structure on a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}

[
44
, Sections 6.1–6.2]
.
Hence the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
.
The category of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is abelian.
All set-indexed products and coproducts (hence also limits and
colimits) exist in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
The forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is exact, faithful,
and preserves infinite products.
We use the notation
\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}({-},{-})
for the groups of morphisms
in the abelian category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

For any set
X
, the set/abelian group
\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
has a natural
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure with the contraaction map

\pi=\phi_{X}\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{R}[[X]]]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
.
The
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{R}[[X]]
are called the
free

left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
For any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
morphisms
\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
correspond bijectively to maps of sets

X\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
; so there is a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

\Hom^{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{R}[[X]],\mathfrak{P})\simeq\Hom_{\mathsf{Sets}}(X,\mathfrak{P}).

There are enough projective objects in the abelian category

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
; the projective
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are precisely
the direct summands of the free ones.

Given a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
and a closed subgroup

\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}\subset\mathfrak{P}
the image of
the composition
\mathfrak{A}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
of the natural
inclusion
\mathfrak{A}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
with the contraaction
map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
.
So
\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}
is a subgroup in
\mathfrak{P}
(since
\pi
is a left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule morphism, hence in particular an abelian group
homomorphism, and
\mathfrak{A}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
is subgroup in
\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
).
If
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
is a closed left ideal, then
\mathfrak{J}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}
is
an
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule in
\mathfrak{P}
(since
\mathfrak{J}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
is
an
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule in the free contramodule
\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
).

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module and
\mathfrak{P}
be a left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.
Then the
contratensor product

\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}
is the abelian
group constructed as the cokernel of (the difference of)
the natural pair of maps

\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\,\rightrightarrows\,\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{P}.

Here the first map
\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{P}
is
induced by the contraaction map
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
, while
the second map
\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{P}
is
defined by the rule

y\otimes\sum\nolimits_{p\in\mathfrak{P}}r_{p}p\longmapsto\sum\nolimits_{p\in\mathfrak{P}}yr_{p}\otimes p

for all
y\in\mathcal{N}
and
\sum_{p\in\mathfrak{P}}r_{p}p\in\mathfrak{R}[[\mathfrak{P}]]
.
Here the sum in the right-hand side is well-defined, because one has

yr_{p}=0
in
\mathcal{N}
for all but a finite subset of indices
p\in\mathfrak{P}
.

The contratensor product functor
\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\colon{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\times\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
preserves all colimits in both the arguments.
For any discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
and any set
X
, one has
a natural isomorphism of abelian groups

\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\simeq\mathcal{N}[X]=\mathcal{N}^{(X)}.

For any closed right ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, one has

\mathfrak{J}\rightthreetimes(\mathfrak{R}[[X]])=\mathfrak{J}[[X]]\subset\mathfrak{R}[[X]].

For any open right ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
and any left

\mathfrak{R}
-contamodule
\mathfrak{P}
, one has

(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\simeq\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}).

In particular,
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{R}[[X]]\simeq\mathfrak{R}[[X]]/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{R}[[X]])=\mathfrak{R}[[X]]/\mathfrak{I}[[X]]\simeq(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})[X]
.

For any open two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
and any left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, the quotient group
\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})

has a natural module structure over the quotient ring
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
.
The subgroup
\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}
is an
\mathfrak{R}
-subcontramodule in
\mathfrak{P}
,
as mentioned above; and the quotient
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule structure
on
\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})
comes from the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module structure.

8.
Separated and Flat Contramodules

In this section we discuss contramodules over a topological ring
with a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero.
There are no new results in this section, which is based
on
[
29
, Section E.1]
and
[
43
, Section 6]
.
A brief survey is available in
[
30
, Section 3.3]
.
A more recent exposition can be found in
[
32
, Sections 4–5]
.
A connection with algebraic geometry is discussed
in
[
35
, Chapter 3]
.

Given a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, consider the natural map to
the directed limit
\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})
, where
\mathfrak{I}
ranges
over the open right ideals of
\mathfrak{R}
.
A left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
is said to be
separated
if
the map
\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}
is injective, and
complete
if

\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}
is surjective.
We denote full subcategory of separated
\mathfrak{R}
-contamodules by

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}\subset\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals.
Then all left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are complete (but they

need not
be separated).

Proof.

This goes back, at least, to
[
28
, Lemma A.2.3 and Remark A.3]
.
Counterexamples of nonseparated contramodules appeared
in
[
28
, Section A.1]
(see also
[
49
, Example 2.5]
,

[
54
, Example 3.20]
,
[
30
, Section 1.5]
,

[
31
, Example 2.7(1)]
, and a further discussion in
Remark
8.5
below).
The completeness assertion for contramodules over topological rings
with a countable topology base consisting of two-sided ideals can be
found in
[
29
, Lemma E.1.1]
, and the general case of a countable
topology base of right ideals is covered by
[
43
, Lemma 6.3(b)]
.
See also
[
30
, Lemmas 3.6 and 3.9]
.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals.
Then, for any nonzero left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
, there exists
an open right ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
such that
\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P})\neq 0
.

Proof.

This assertion belongs to a class of results known as “contramodule
Nakayama lemmas” and going back, at least,
to
[
28
, Lemma A.2.1]
.
The assertion for contramodules over topological rings with
a countable topology base of two-sided ideals can be found
in
[
29
, Lemma E.1.2]
, and the general case of a countable base
of right ideals is covered by
[
43
, Lemma 6.14]
.
See also
[
30
, Lemmas 2.1(b) and 3.22]
.
∎

A left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}
is called
flat
if
the contratensor product functor
{-}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\penalty\mathfrak{F}\colon\allowbreak{\operatorname{\mathsf{Discr--}}}\mathfrak{R}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
is exact on the abelian category of
discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules.
All projective
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are separated and flat.
All directed colimits and coproducts of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
(computed in the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
) are flat.
We denote the full subcategory of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules by

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}\subset\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

When
\mathfrak{R}
has a base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of
open two-sided ideals
\mathfrak{I}
, the discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-modules are
the directed unions of modules over the quotient rings
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
.
For any right
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
N
and any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule

\mathfrak{P}
, one has

N\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\simeq N\otimes_{\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}}\mathfrak{P}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}).

Hence, in this case, a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}
is flat
if and only if the left
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
\mathfrak{F}/(\mathfrak{I}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{F})
is flat
for every open two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals.
Then all
flat
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are separated.

Proof.

The case of topological rings with a countable topology base of
two-sided ideals can be found in
[
29
, Lemma E.1.7]

(cf. the definition of a “flat contramodule”
in
[
29
, the paragraph before Lemma E.1.4]
, which is stated
differently than our definition above).
The general case of a countable base of right ideals is covered
by
[
43
, Corollary 6.15]
.
See also
[
30
, Propositions 3.7(a) and 3.10(a)]
.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals.
Then

(a)
the class of all flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed
under extensions and kernels of epimorphisms in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
;

(b)
for any discrete right
\mathfrak{R}
-module
\mathcal{N}
,
the contratensor product functor
\mathcal{N}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\penalty{-}\,\colon\allowbreak\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
takes short exact sequences of flat left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules to short exact sequences of abelian groups.

Proof.

The case of topological rings with a countable topology base of
two-sided ideals can be found in
[
29
, Lemmas E.1.4 and E.1.5]
.
The general case of a countable base of right ideals is covered
by
[
43
, Lemma 6.7 or 6.10, Corollaries 6.8, 6.13 and 6.15]
, with
a summary in
[
43
, Corollary 7.1(a–b)]
.
See also
[
30
, Propositions 3.7(b) and 3.10(b)]
.
∎

In particular, according to
Lemma
8.4
(a), the full subcategory of
flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
is closed under extensions
in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
when the topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
has a countable base of
neighborhoods of zero.
So the full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
inherits an exact category
structure from the abelian exact structure of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
in this case.

Remark 8.5
.

Lemma
8.4
tells us that the class
of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is well-behaved (under the assumption of
a countable topology base in
\mathfrak{R}
).
But the class of separated
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is
not

well-behaved.

In suffices to consider the case of the ring of
p
-adic integers

\mathfrak{R}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(in the
p
-adic topology), or the ring of formal Taylor
power series
\mathfrak{R}=k[[t]]
in one variable
t
over a field
k
(in
the
t
-adic topology).
In both cases, the forgetful functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
is
fully faithful; in fact, even the forgetful functors
\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
and
k[[t]]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow k[t]{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}
are fully faithful.
The essential images of these forgetful functors are described
in
[
30
, Section 2.2]
and the references therein.

With these descriptions in mind, there is a now-classical
counterexample showing that the the full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}

is not closed under extensions in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

[
49
, Example 2.5]

and the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
is
not

abelian
[
31
, Example 2.7(1)]
.
Furthermore, the full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
is
not

closed under directed colimits, and in fact,
not
even closed
under coproducts in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

[
30
, Section 1.5]
.
(See also
[
35
, Example 3.1]
and
Example
8.9
below.)

However, separated contramodules are sometimes easier to work with
than arbitrary ones, as the following lemma illustrates.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring, and let

\mathfrak{R}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{1}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{2}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{3}\supset\dotsb
be a countable
base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{R}
consisting of open

two-sided
ideals
\mathfrak{I}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{R}
,
n\geq 1
.

(a)
The full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
of separated

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

Proof.

In part (a), the equivalence of categories assigns to a separated

\mathfrak{R}

Here the

The equivalence of categories in part (b) is obtained by restricting

Lemma

plays an important role here.

Proposition 9.1

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

\mathfrak{R}[[Y]]\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}[[X]]

and 6.4]

[

of the proof of Lemma 6.3]
).
Alternatively, one can refer to Theorem
1.9

or a suitable additive version of

∎

Now let
\mathfrak{R}

-contramodule

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals.
Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule such that the separated
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
is flat.
Then the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
is flat and separated, and
the natural morphism
\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}\colon\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

This is an intermediate step in the proofs
of
[
29

and
[
43
, Corollary 6.15]

(which are restated above as Lemma
8.3
).
The argument is based on the contramodule Nakayama
lemma (Lemma
8.2
above).
Alternatively, the same proof can be phrased as follows.
One observes that, for any left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
,
the adjunction morphism
\lambda_{\mathfrak{R},\mathfrak{P}}
induces an isomorphism
of the contratensor product functors
{-}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\simeq{-}\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
(essentially, the nonseparated part of

\mathfrak{P}
is killed by contratensor products).
This shows that flatness of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
implies flatness
of
\mathfrak{P}

Then the separatedness and completeness of
\mathfrak{P}
follow from
Lemmas
8.1

and
8.3
.
∎

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals, and let
\kappa
be an uncountable regular cardinal.
Then the category of separated left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
is locally
\kappa
-presentable.
A separated left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{M}
is

\kappa
-presentable as an object of the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}

if and only if it has the form
\mathfrak{M}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
, where
\mathfrak{P}

is a
\kappa
-presentable object of the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Proof.

One needs to observe that the full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}\subset\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is closed under
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimits.
Indeed, for any closed subgroup
\mathfrak{A}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, the functor

\mathfrak{P}\longmapsto\mathfrak{A}\rightthreetimes\mathfrak{P}
commutes with
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimits
(since the functor
X\longmapsto\mathfrak{A}[[X]]\colon\mathsf{Sets}\longrightarrow\mathsf{Sets}
does),
and countable directed limits also commute with
\aleph_{1}
-directed
colimits.
Consequently, the inclusion functor
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

preserves
\kappa
-directed colimits, and it follows that the left
adjoint functor
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}\colon\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
takes

\kappa
-presentable objects to
\kappa
-presentable objects.
This proves the “if” assertion.

Furthermore, the functor
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}
, being a left adjoint, preserves
all colimits.
Hence all colimits exist in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
and can be constructed
by applying
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}
to the colimits in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Since all objects of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
are
\kappa
-directed colimits
of
\kappa
-presentable objects by
Proposition
9.1
(a), so are
all the objects of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
.
Thus the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
is locally
\kappa
-presentable.

To deduce the assertion “only if”, we notice that all the objects of

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
are
\kappa
-directed colimits of separated
contramodules of the desired form
\mathfrak{M}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
with

\mathfrak{P}\in\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{<\kappa}
, as we have just shown.
It remains to check that the class of all separated contramodules of
the desired form
\mathfrak{M}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
is closed under direct summands.
Let us prove this class is closed under cokernels in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
.

Suppose given a morphism
g\colon\mathfrak{M}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{N}
, where

\mathfrak{M}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{P})
,
\mathfrak{N}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}(\mathfrak{Q})
, and
\mathfrak{P}
,

\mathfrak{Q}\in\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{<\kappa}
.
Then the natural (adjunction) morphisms
\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{M}
and

\mathfrak{Q}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{N}
are surjective.
Let
\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}
be a surjective morphism onto
\mathfrak{P}
from
a free left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule with less than
\kappa
generators.
Then the composition
\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{M}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{N}
can be lifted
to a morphism
f\colon\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{Q}
.
The cokernel of the morphism
g
in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
can be obtained
by applying
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{R}}
to the cokernel of the morphism
f
in

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
The latter cokernel is
\kappa
-presentable in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
∎

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{F}

∎

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring, and let

\mathfrak{R}
consisting of open

two-sided
ideals
\mathfrak{I}_{n}\subset\mathfrak{R}

Then the projective dimension of a flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}

(as an object of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}

(a) iteratively
and keeping in mind that projective contramodules are flat, one shows

\mathfrak{R}

\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}

10.
Flat Contramodules as Directed Colimits

The following theorem describing flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is the second
main result of this paper.
It should be compared with the discussion of the conventional finite,
rather than countable Govorov–Lazard theorem for flat contramodules
in the paper
[
42
, Section 10 and Example 13.4]
.
The counterexamples in
[
42
]
show that the finite Govorov–Lazard
theorem fails for flat contramodules over topological rings

without
a countable base of neighborhoods of zero
(it is still an open question whether it holds under the assumption of
a countable base).
Theorem
10.1
is also
a contramodule analogue of
[
47
, Theorems 2.4 and 3.5]

and Theorem
3.1
above.

We refer to Lemmas
9.4

and
9.5
for
an explication of what is meant by a countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule in
Theorem
10.1

(i. e., countably presentable in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
or
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{sep}}
, as
described in Propositions
9.1
(a)
and
9.3
).

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open

two-sided
ideals.
Then the category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
of flat left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is
\aleph_{1}
-accessible.
The
\aleph_{1}
-presentable objects of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
are
precisely all the countably presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
Consequently, every flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is
an
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimit of countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.

Proof.

The full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
is closed under directed
colimits in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Therefore, all countably presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are

\aleph_{1}
-presentable in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
.

The full assertion of the theorem is obtained by applying
Theorem
1.6
for
\kappa=\aleph_{1}
and

\lambda=\aleph_{0}
.
The argument is based on the description of the category of
flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules provided by
Lemma
8.6
(b)
and the description of countably presentable separated/flat

\mathfrak{R}
-countramodules provided by
Lemma
9.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{1}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{2}\supset\mathfrak{I}_{3}\supset\dotsb
be
a countable base of neighborhoods of zero in
\mathfrak{R}
consisting of open
two-sided ideals.
Let
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{L}
be the Cartesian product of the categories of flat left
modules over the rings
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}
, taken over the integers
n\geq 1
;
so
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{L}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})
.
Consider the following pair of parallel functors
F_{1}
,
F_{2}\colon\mathsf{K}\rightrightarrows\mathsf{L}
.
The functor
F_{1}
takes a collection of flat modules

(K_{n}\in\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})_{n\geq 1}
to the collection of
flat modules
L_{n}=\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}\otimes_{\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n+1}}K_{n+1}
.
The functor
F_{2}
is the identity functor.

Then Lemma
8.6
(b)
tells us that the isomorpher category
\mathsf{C}
is equivalent to
the category of flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
.
The categories
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
are
\aleph_{1}
-accessible
by Lemma
1.2
, and their Cartesian product

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}_{n}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Mod}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})
is
\aleph_{1}
-accessible
by Proposition
1.4
.
Theorem
1.6
is applicable, and it tells us that
the category
\mathsf{C}
is
\aleph_{1}
-accessible.
Comparing the description of the
\aleph_{1}
-presentable objects of

\mathsf{C}
provided by Theorem
1.6
with the description
of the
\aleph_{1}
-presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules provided by
Lemma
9.5
one
can see that these two classes of objects coincide, as desired.
∎

Let us also provide a description of arbitrary complexes of flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules as directed colimits.

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open

two-sided
ideals.
Then the category
\mathsf{Com}(\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})
of complexes of flat
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is
\aleph_{1}
-accessible.
The
\aleph_{1}
-presentable objects of
\mathsf{Com}(\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})
are
precisely all the complexes of countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
Consequently, every complex of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is
an
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimit of complexes of countably
presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.

Proof.

All complexes of countably presentable
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are

\aleph_{1}
-presentable as objects of
\mathsf{Com}(\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})
by
Proposition
9.1
(b).
Since the full subcategory
\mathsf{Com}(\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})
is closed under
directed colimits in
\mathsf{Com}(\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}})
, it follows that all complexes
of countably presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are

\aleph_{1}
-presentable in
\mathsf{Com}(\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}})
.

The full assertion of the proposition is obtained by combining
the results of
Theorems
10.1

and
1.9
.
The argument is similar to the proof of
Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Remark 10.3
.

One can restrict the results of this section to commutative topological
rings
\mathfrak{R}
, interpret commutative topological rings as ind-affine
ind-schemes (as per Example
8.9
), and
then generalize to non-ind-affine ind-schemes.
To avoid a detailed discussion of the basics of the ind-scheme theory
(see
[
35
, Section 1.2]
and the references therein), let us
say that a
strict ind-quasi-compact ind-quasi-separated

\aleph_{0}
-ind-scheme

\mathfrak{X}
can be defined as an ind-object in
the category of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes representable by
a countable directed diagram of closed immersions of quasi-compact
quasi-separated schemes
X_{1}\longrightarrow X_{2}\longrightarrow X_{3}\longrightarrow\dotsb
.

An “
\mathcal{O}^{p}
-module on
\mathfrak{X}
” (in the terminology
of
[
4
, Section 7.11.3]
) or a
pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaf
on
\mathfrak{X}
in our preferred terminology of
[
35
, Section 3.1]

can be then defined as a sequence of quasi-coherent sheaves
M_{n}
of
the schemes
X_{n}
together with isomorphisms of quasi-coherent sheaves

M_{n}\simeq i_{n}^{*}M_{n+1}
on
X_{n}
, where
i_{n}\colon X_{n}\longrightarrow X_{n+1}

denote the closed immersion morphisms (
n\geq 1
).
A pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaf
(F_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is said to be

flat
if the quasi-coherent sheaf
F_{n}
on
X_{n}
is flat
for every
n\geq 1
.
The flat pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves form a well-behaved (exact)
full subcategory in a badly behaved additive category of arbitrary
pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves on
\mathfrak{X}
(see
Example
8.9
and
Remark
8.5
).

The argument similar to the proof of
Theorem
10.1

and based on the result of
[
47
, Theorem 2.4]
shows that
the category of flat pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaves on
\mathfrak{X}
is

\aleph_{1}
-accessible, and the flat pro-quasi-coherent
pro-sheaves
(F_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with locally countably presentable
flat quasi-coherent sheaves
F_{n}
on
X_{n}
are
the
\aleph_{1}
-presentable objects of this category.
The flat pro-quasi-coherent pro-sheaf version of
Proposition
10.2
also holds,
with the same proof.
The same applies to the results of
Proposition
11.3
and
Corollary
11.4
below.

11.
Exact Sequences of Flat Contramodules as Directed Colimits

Let us start with a contramodule analogue/generalization of
Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open

two-sided
ideals.
Then the kernel of any surjective morphism from a countably
presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule to a countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is a countably presentable (flat)

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule.

Proof.

The class of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under kernels of
surjective morphisms in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
by
Lemma
8.4
(a).
Now if
0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{H}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow 0
is a short exact
sequence of flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules, then
Lemma
8.4
(b) tells us that

0\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{H}\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence of
left
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules for any open two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.
If the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{G}
are countably presentable,
then so are the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}
and

(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{G}
, by
Lemma
9.5
.
So Lemma
4.1
tells us that
the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{H}
is countably presentable,
and it remains to apply
Lemma
9.5
again
in order to conclude that the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{H}
is
countably presentable.

Alternatively, one can deduce the lemma from
Corollary
9.7
essentially in
the same way as the proof of Lemma
4.1

deduces it from Lemma
1.3
.
Then one needs to use the facts that the kernel of any surjective
morphism of countably presentable
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is a countably
generated
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule, and any countably generated projective

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is countably presentable.
∎

12.
Cotorsion Periodicity for Contramodules

For the sake of completeness of the exposition, we start with
presenting a weak version of flat/projective periodicity
for contramodules.
The definition of a
periodic object
(with respect to a class
of objects in an abelian category) was given in
Section
5
.
The following proposition is a contramodule generalization
of
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
and
[
26
, Remark 2.15]

(see also
[
9
, Proposition 7.6]
).
For a strong version of flat/projective periodicity theorem for
contramodules (with a proof based on the results of this paper)
see the recent preprint
[
41
]
.

Proposition 12.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open

two-sided
ideals.
Then

(a)
any projective-periodic flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is
projective;

(b)
in any acyclic complex of projective
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
with flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules of cocycles, the contramodules of
cocycles are actually projective (so the complex is contractible).

Proof.

This is an easy corollary of the the flat/projective periodicity for
modules over a ring (
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
26
, Remark 2.15]
)
together with the basic properties of flat and projective contramodules
(Lemmas
8.4
(b)
and
8.7
).

Part (a): let
0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow 0
be a short
exact sequence of flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules with a projective

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{P}
.
By Lemma
8.4
(b), for any open
two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, the short sequence of left

\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules
0\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}\longrightarrow 0
is exact.
Clearly, the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}
is flat and
the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}
is projective
(see the discussion in
Sections
7
–
8
, cf. Lemmas
8.6
(b)
and
8.7
).
By
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
, it follows that
the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-module
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{F}
is projective for every
open two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
.
Using Lemma
8.7
,
we conclude that the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{F}
is projective.

Part (b): let
\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
be a pure acyclic complex of projective
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.
By Lemma
8.4
(b), for any open
two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}\subset\mathfrak{R}
, the complex of left

\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is (pure) acyclic.
Moreover, if
\mathfrak{Z}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, are the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules of
cocycles of the complex
\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
, then
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{Z}^{n}
are
the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules of cocycles of the complex

(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
.
The complex
(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is also a complex of
projective left
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules.
By
[
26
, Theorem 8.6 and Remark 2.15]

or
[
9
, Proposition 7.6]
, it follows that the complex

(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is contractible and the
\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I}
-modules

(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{I})\odot_{\mathfrak{R}}\mathfrak{Z}^{n}
are projective.
Once again, we use
Lemma
8.7
in order to
conclude that the
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{Z}^{n}
are projective and
the complex
\mathfrak{P}^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is contractible.
∎

Now we pass to the cotorsion periodicity for
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules,
which is the main topic of this section.
Let us denote by
\Ext^{\mathfrak{R},*}({-},{-})
the Ext groups in
the abelian category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
A left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule
\mathfrak{B}
is said to be
cotorsion

[
29
, Section E.5]
,
[
43
, Definition 7.3]
if

\Ext^{\mathfrak{R},1}(\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{B})=0
for all flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{F}
.

Introduce the notation
\mathsf{B}=\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
for the full subcategory
of cotorsion
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules in the abelian category

\mathsf{C}=\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Obviously, the full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
is closed under
extensions in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
, so it inherits an exact category structure
from the abelian exact structure of
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

The following corollary summarizes some of our previous results and
observations.
It is a contramodule version of
[
47
, Corollary 5.6 and Lemma 9.1]

and Corollary
5.2
above.

Corollary 12.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring.

(a)
Assume that
\mathfrak{R}
has a countable base of neighborhoods
of zero consisting of open right ideals.
Then the class all flat left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is resolving
and closed under directed colimits in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
Consequently, one has
\Ext^{\mathfrak{R},n}(\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{B})=0
for all flat
left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{F}
, all cotorsion left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
\mathfrak{B}
, and all integers
n\geq 1
.
The directed colimit functors in the exact category of flat
contramodules
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
are exact (i. e., the directed
colimits of admissible short exact sequences are admissible
short exact sequences).

(b)
Assume that
\mathfrak{R}
has a countable base of neighborhoods
of zero consisting of open two-sided ideals.
Then any flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule is a directed (in fact,

\aleph_{1}
-directed) colimit of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules having
projective dimensions not exceeding
1
in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Proof.

Part (a): the full subcategory
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}\subset\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
is
resolving by Lemma
8.4
(a) and
closed under directed colimits according to the discussion in
Sections
7
–
8
.
The Ext vanishing assertion follows by
[
47
, Lemma 6.1]
.
The directed colimits in the exact category
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}_{\mathsf{flat}}
(though
not in the abelian category

The definition of a
coresolving subcategory
is dual to that
of a resolving one.
A class of objects
\mathsf{B}
in an abelian category
\mathsf{C}
is said to be

cogenerating
if every object of
\mathsf{C}
is a subobject of
an object from
\mathsf{B}
.
We will say that a class of objects
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{C}
is

coresolving
if it is cogenerating, closed under extensions,
and closed under cokernels of monomorphisms in
\mathsf{C}
.

Proposition 12.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open
right ideals.
Then the class
\mathsf{B}=\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
of all cotorsion left

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is coresolving in
\mathsf{C}=\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Proof.

It is obvious from the definition that the class of all cotorsion

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under extensions, and it follows from
the higher Ext vanishing assertion of
Corollary
12.2
(a)
that
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}^{\mathsf{cot}}
is also closed under cokernels of monomorphisms
in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
The most nontrivial assertion in the proposition is that the class
of cotorsion
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is cogenerating.
This is a part of
[
43
, Corollary 7.8]
.
(Notice that there are usually
not
enough injective objects
in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.)
∎

Recall the notation
\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{E})
for the homotopy category of
an additive category
\mathsf{E}
.
For an abelian (or exact) category
\mathsf{E}
, we denote by
\mathsf{Ac}(\mathsf{E})\subset\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{E})
the full subcategory of acyclic complexes.
So the derived category
\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{E})
is the Verdier quotient category

\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{E})=\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{E})/\mathsf{Ac}(\mathsf{E})
.

Proposition 12.6
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be an abelian category with exact functors of countable
product, and let
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{C}
be a cogenerating subcategory closed
under countable products.
Then for any complex
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
in
\mathsf{C}
there exists a complex
B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

in
\mathsf{B}
together with a quasi-isomorphism
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
of
complexes in
\mathsf{C}
.
Consequently, the inclusion of additive categories
\mathsf{B}\longrightarrow\mathsf{C}

induces a triangulated equivalence of Verdier quotient categories

\frac{\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{B})}{\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{B})\cap\mathsf{Ac}(\mathsf{C})}\overset{\simeq}{\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu}\frac{\mathsf{K}(\mathsf{C})}{\mathsf{Ac}(\mathsf{C})}\,=\,\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{C}).

Proof.

The proof of the first assertion is similar to the construction
of
K
-projective and
K
-injective resolutions of complexes of
modules in
[
50
, Section 3]
.
Let us spell out the construction as follows.
For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, consider the canonical truncation

\tau_{\geq n}C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
of the complex
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
.
Since the complex
\tau_{\geq n}C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is bounded below and
the subcategory
\mathsf{B}\subset\mathsf{C}
is cogenerating, one can easily find
a (bounded below) complex
{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
in
\mathsf{B}
together with
a quasi-isomorphism
\tau_{\geq n}C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
in
\mathsf{C}
.
Moreover, one can choose the complex
{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
so that
the morphism of complexes
\tau_{\geq n}C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

is a termwise monomorphism.
The composition
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow\tau_{\geq n}C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

provides a morphism of complexes
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
.

Now the induced morphism into the product
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow B^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{}^{(n)}D^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is a termwise monomorphism of complexes
in
\mathsf{C}
inducing monomorphisms on all the cohomology objects.
Furthermore,
B^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}
is a complex in
\mathsf{B}
.
Next we apply the same construction to the cokernel of the morphism
of complexes
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow B^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}
, embedding the complex

B^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}/C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
into a complex
B^{1,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}
in
\mathsf{B}
in such a way
that the morphism of complexes
B^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}/C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow B^{1,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}
is
(not only termwise monic, but also) induces monomorphisms on all
the cohomology objects.
Iterating the procedure, we obtain an exact complex of complexes

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muB^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muB^{1,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muB^{2,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

such that passing to the cohomology objects produces an exact complex

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muH^{*}(C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muH^{*}(B^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muH^{*}(B^{1,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muH^{*}(B^{2,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}})\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

of graded objects in
\mathsf{C}
.

Finally, we totalize the bicomplex
B^{{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}
by taking infinite
products along the diagonals.
Put
B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}=\Tot^{\sqcap}(B^{{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}})
to be the product totalization.
Then
B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}
is a complex in
\mathsf{B}
.
Furthermore, the resulting morphism of complexes
C^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}\longrightarrow B^{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}

is a quasi-isomorphism by the next Lemma
12.7
.
This proves the first assertion of the proposition, and the second
one then follows by
[
47
, Lemma 9.5]
.
An opposite version of this argument can be found in
[
29
, proof of
Proposition A.4.3]
.
∎

Lemma 12.7
.

Let
\mathsf{C}
be an abelian category with exact functors of countable
product, and let

0\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{0,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{1,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{2,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5muC^{3,{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}\mskip 1.5mu\relbar\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mskip 1.5mu\dotsb

be a bounded below complex of complexes in

Assume that, passing to the cohomology objects, we obtain an acyclic

of graded objects in
\mathsf{C}
.
Then the product totalization
\Tot^{\sqcap}(C^{{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}},{\text{\smaller\smaller$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}})
of
the bicomplex

is an acyclic complex in
\mathsf{C}
.

Proof.

This is a particular case of
[

, Theorem 7.4]
or (essentially)

[

, Lemma A.4.4]
.

The following lemma is a contramodule analogue of
Corollary
6.4
.

Lemma 13.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open

two-sided
ideals.
Then

(a)
the full subcategory
\mathsf{G}
of all countably presentable
flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is self-resolving in the abelian category

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
;

(b)
the homological dimension of the exact category
\mathsf{G}

(with the exact structure inherited from
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
) does not
exceed
1
.

Proof.

Part (a): the class of all flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under
extensions in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
by
Lemma
8.4
(a).
The assertion that the class of all countably presentable

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under extensions is provable in
the same way as the similar assertion for modules over a ring.
Hence the class
\mathsf{G}
of all countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under extensions in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
The class
\mathsf{G}
is also closed under kernels of epimorphisms in

\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
by Lemma
11.1
.
Finally, the class
\mathsf{G}
is self-generating in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
, since
every object of
\mathsf{G}
is a quotient object in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
of an object
from
\mathsf{G}
(namely, of the free
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodule with a countable
set of generators) that is projective in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.

Part (b): the assertion follows directly from
Corollary
9.7
.
One can also invoke Lemma
6.3
here
to the effect that it follows from part (a) that the Ext groups computed
in
\mathsf{G}
agree with the ones computed in
\mathfrak{R}{\operatorname{\mathsf{--Contra}}}
.
∎

The following theorem is a contramodule/ind-affine ind-scheme version
of
[
14
, Theorem 2.4 (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)]
or

[
26
, Theorem 8.6 (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)]
,
and an analogue of
Theorem
6.5
above.
It should be also compared to
[
46
, Corollary 0.5 or
Proposition 8.13]
.
In the latter context,
Theorem
13.2
can
be interpreted as saying that (over a topological ring
\mathfrak{R}
with
a countable topology base of two-sided ideals)
for the exact
category of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules, the derived category coincides
with the
(
suitably defined
)
coderived category
.

Theorem 13.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a complete, separated topological ring with
a
countable
base of neighborhoods of zero consisting of open

two-sided
ideals.
Then the following classes of complexes of left
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules
coincide:

(1)

all pure acyclic complexes of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules;

(2)

the closure of the class of all contractible complexes of
countably presentable flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules under extensions and
directed colimits;

(3)

all
\aleph_{1}
-directed colimits of totalizations of short
exact sequences of complexes of countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules.

Proof.

The totalization of a short exact sequence of complexes is an extension
of the two cones of identity endomorphisms of the rightmost and
leftmost complexes in the short exact sequence.
Furthermore, the class of all pure acyclic complexes of flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules is closed under extensions and directed colimits
(by Lemmas
8.4
(a)
and
8.8
), and
the contractible complexes of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are pure acyclic.
This proves the inclusions (3)
\subset
(2)
\subset
(1).

Conversely, to prove that (1)
\subset
(3), recall that all pure
acyclic complexes of flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are
\aleph_{1}
-directed
colimits of pure acyclic complexes of countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules by
Corollary
11.4
.
The exact category
\mathsf{G}
of countably presentable flat

\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules has homological dimension
\leq 1

by Lemma
13.1
(b).
Hence the pure acyclic complexes of countably presentable
flat
\mathfrak{R}
-contramodules are absolutely acyclic by
Lemma
6.1
, and it remains to refer
to Proposition
6.2

(for
d=1
).
∎

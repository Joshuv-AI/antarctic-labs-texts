1.
Introduction

The Bernoulli numbers
B_{n}
and the Euler numbers
E_{n}
are respectively defined by

(1)

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-4]).

The first few terms of
B_{n}
are given by:

(2)

\displaystyle B_{0}=1,\,B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\,B_{2}=\frac{1}{6},\,B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30},\,B_{6}=\frac{1}{42},\,B_{8}=-\frac{1}{30},\,B_{10}=\frac{5}{66},\,

\displaystyle B_{12}=-\frac{691}{2730},\,B_{14}=\frac{7}{6},\,B_{16}=-\frac{3617}{510},\,B_{18}=\frac{43867}{798},\,B_{20}=-\frac{174611}{330},\dots;

\displaystyle B_{2k+1}=0,\,\,(k\geq 1).

The first few terms of
E_{n}
are given by:

(3)

\displaystyle E_{0}=1,\,E_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\,E_{3}=\frac{1}{4},\,E_{5}=-\frac{1}{2},\,E_{7}=\frac{17}{8},\,E_{9}=-\frac{31}{2},\,E_{11}=\frac{691}{4},\,

\displaystyle E_{13}=-\frac{5461}{2},\,E_{15}=\frac{929569}{16},\,E_{17}=-\frac{3202291}{2},\,E_{19}=\frac{221930581}{4},\dots;

\displaystyle E_{2k}=0,\,\,(k\geq 1).

A random variable
X
is the Laplacian random variable with parameters
a
and
b(>0)
, which is denoted by
X\sim L(a,b)
, if its probability density function is given by

(4)

f(x)=\frac{1}{2b}e^{-\frac{|x-a|}{b}},\ x\in(-\infty,\infty),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,7]),

where
a
is the local parameter and
b(>0)
is the scale parameter.

The Euler’s product expansion for the sine function is the identity

(5)

\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{x^{2}}{j^{2}}\bigg),\quad\big(x\in(-\infty,\infty)\big),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2]).

This identity was used by Euler in 1735 to give a solution of the Basel problem.

Let
X\sim L(0,1)
, and let
(X_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of
X
. In this note, we determine the moments of the random variable
Y=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}
. Indeed, we show that
E[Y^{2n}]=(-1)^{n}\big(\frac{2n}{2^{2n}}E_{2n-1}+B_{2n}\big)
,
(n\in\mathbb{N})
, and that all odd moments of
Y
vanish (see Theorem 2.1).

2.
Moments of a random variable arising from Laplacian random variable

For
X\sim L(0,1)
, let us assume that
(X_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
X
. From (
4
), we note that

(6)

\displaystyle E\Big[e^{\frac{X_{k}}{2\pi k}t}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\frac{x}{2\pi k}t}e^{-|x|}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{x\big(1+\frac{t}{2\pi k}\big)}dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\big(1-\frac{t}{2\pi k}\big)x}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2\pi k}t}+\frac{1}{1-\frac{t}{2\pi k}}\bigg]=\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{t}{2\pi k}\big)^{2}},

where
k
is a positive integer and
-2\pi<t<2\pi
.

Thus, by (
6
), we get

(7)

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{t}{2\pi k}\big)^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}E\Big[e^{\frac{X_{k}}{2\pi k}t}\Big]=E\bigg[\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\frac{X_{k}}{2\pi k}t}\bigg]

\displaystyle=E\bigg[e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2\pi k}t}\bigg].

On the other hand, by (
5
), we get

(8)

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{t}{2\pi k}\big)^{2}}\bigg)=\cfrac{\frac{t}{2}}{\sin\frac{t}{2}}=\cfrac{\frac{t}{2}}{\frac{e^{\frac{it}{2}}-e^{-\frac{it}{2}}}{2i}}=i\frac{t}{e^{\frac{it}{2}}-e^{-\frac{it}{2}}}

\displaystyle=it\bigg(\frac{e^{\frac{it}{2}}-1+1}{e^{it}-1}\bigg)=\frac{it}{2}\bigg(\frac{2}{e^{\frac{it}{2}}+1}\bigg)+\frac{it}{e^{it}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{it}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}\cfrac{\big(\frac{it}{2}\big)^{n}}{n!}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{(it)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{it}{2}+\frac{it}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{2n-1}\cfrac{\big(\frac{it}{2}\big)^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}+1-\frac{it}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{2n}}{(2n)!}(it)^{2n}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n-1)!}E_{2n-1}\bigg(\frac{t}{2}\bigg)^{2n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n)!}B_{2n}t^{2n}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\bigg(\frac{2n}{2^{2n}}E_{2n-1}+B_{2n}\bigg)\frac{t^{2n}}{(2n)!}.

By (
7
) and (
8
), we get

(9)

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\bigg(\frac{2n}{4^{n}}E_{2n-1}+B_{2n}\bigg)\frac{t^{2n}}{(2n)!}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\cfrac{1}{1-\big(\frac{t}{2k\pi}\big)^{2}}\bigg)=E\Big[e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}t}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{n}\bigg]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
9
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
X\sim L(0,1)
, let
(X_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
X
. Then we have

E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{2n}\bigg]=(-1)^{n}\bigg(\frac{2n}{2^{2n}}E_{2n-1}+B_{2n}\bigg),

and

E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{2n-1}\bigg]=0,\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Remark 2.2
.

As is known, the Bernoulli and Euler numbers are related by:

(10)

E_{n}=-\frac{2(2^{n+1}-1)}{n+1}B_{n+1},\quad(n\geq 0).

For example, this follows from the equation (14) of [6].
Thus, from Theorem 2.1 and (
10
), we have the following alternative expression:

(11)

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{2n}\bigg]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2^{2n-1}}\Big)B_{2n}

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{1}{2^{2n-1}}\Big)|B_{2n}|,\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Thus we have

E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{2n}\bigg]\sim|B_{2n}|,\,\,\textnormal{as}\,\,\,n\rightarrow\infty,\quad E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{2n+1}\bigg]=|B_{2n+1}|,\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Finally, we illustrate Theorem 2.1 by using (
11
).

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg]=\frac{1}{12},\quad E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{4}\bigg]=\frac{7}{240},

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{6}\bigg]=\frac{31}{1344},\quad E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{8}\bigg]=\frac{127}{3840},

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{10}\bigg]=\frac{2555}{33792},\quad E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{12}\bigg]=\frac{1414477}{5591040},

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{14}\bigg]=\frac{57337}{49152},\quad E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{16}\bigg]=\frac{118518239}{16711680},

\displaystyle E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{18}\bigg]=\frac{5749691557}{104595456},\quad E\bigg[\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{X_{k}}{2k\pi}\bigg)^{20}\bigg]=\frac{91546277357}{173015040}.

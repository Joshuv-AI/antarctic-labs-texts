Determination of lower order perturbations of a polyharmonic operator in two dimensions
Rajat Bansal, Venkateswaran P. Krishnan, Rahul Raju Pattar

Abstract
. We study an inverse boundary value problem for a polyharmonic operator in two dimensions. We show that the Cauchy data uniquely determine all the anisotropic perturbations of orders at most m − 1 m-1 and several perturbations of orders m m to 2 ​ m − 2 2m-2 under some restriction. The uniqueness proof relies on the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} -techniques and the method of stationary phase.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected bounded domain with smooth boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This paper aims to study the inverse boundary value problem in two dimensions for a polyharmonic operator of the form:

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}=\partial^{m}\bar{\partial}^{m}+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{A_{j,k}\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}},\quad m\geq 2.

Note that
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is identified with
z=x_{1}+ix_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\partial=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x_{1}}-i\partial_{x_{2}}\right),

\bar{\partial}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x_{1}}+i\partial_{x_{2}}\right)
.

The polyharmonic operators have applications in physics and geometry, for example the Kirchhoff-Love plate equation in the theory of elasticity and the study of the Paneitz-Branson operator in conformal geometry; see
[
GGS10
]
.

In this paper we uniquely determine the coefficients
A_{j,k}
from the set of Cauchy data

(1.2)

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L})=\left\{\left(u|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}^{2}u|_{\partial\Omega}\ldots,\partial_{\nu}^{(2m-1)}u|_{\partial\Omega}\right):u\in H^{2m}(\Omega),\mathcal{L}u=0\right\},

where
\nu
is an outer unit normal to
\bd
.

To the best of our knowledge, there are few prior works investigating inverse problems for lower order perturbation of polyharmonic operator (especially for
m\geq 3
) in two dimensions compared to higher dimensions. For
m=2
, in
[
Ike91
]
Ikehata proved a local uniqueness of a potential
V\in L^{2}(\Omega)
associated to the Calderón problem for
(\Delta^{2}+V)u=0
with
\|V\|_{L^{2}}
small. In
[
Ike93
]
, the same author studied a local uniqueness theorem for the Calderón problem for a perturbed biharmonic operator related to Love-Kirchhoff plate theory. In
[
Ike96
]
, the author studied the relationship between two D-N maps in the linear theory of elasticity in two dimensions and the linearized Calderon problem for anisotropic fourth order equation. In
[
IY15
,
LUW15
]
, the authors have studied Navier-Stokes equation in two dimensions using the biharmonic operator.

In dimensions
n\geq 3,
Krupchyk, Lassas, and Uhlmann
[
KLU14
]
established that the Cauchy data for a polyharmonic operator uniquely determines first order perturbations. This work was extended by many authors; see, for instance,
[
GK16
,
BG19
,
BKS21
,
BG22
]
. Till now, the perturbations considered for the polyharmonic operator are of order at most
m
in
n\geq 3
. Moreover, in
[
SS23
]
, the authors considered the linearized Calderón inverse problem for polyharmonic operator and recovered several lower order coefficients up to order
2m-1
in
n\geq 3
.

In this paper, we establish that the Cauchy data for a polyharmonic operator in two dimensions uniquely determines all anisotropic perturbations of order at most
m-1
and several perturbations of orders
m
to
2m-2
with some restrictions. This restriction is captured in the following representation of the operator
\mathcal{L}
as

(\partial\bar{\partial})^{m}+A_{m-1,m-1}(\partial\bar{\partial})^{m-1}+\sum_{l=1}^{m-2}{\left(\sum_{j+k=m-l-1}{A_{j+l,k+l}\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}}\right)(\partial\bar{\partial})^{l}}+\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\sum_{j+k=l}{A_{j,k}\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}}.

The constraint on the coefficients of orders
m
to
2m-2
is required for the techniques employed in this paper to work, mainly, to make the equation for the amplitude of complex geometric optics (CGO) solutions to be independent of the coefficients.

An early study of inverse boundary value problems for second-order operators was carried out by Calderón
[
Cal80
]
. Sylvester and Uhlmann
[
SU87
]
obtained the first uniqueness result for the Calderón problem. In two dimensions, the Calderón problem was studied by Nachman
[
Nac96
]
, Brown-Uhlmann
[
BU97
]
, and finally Astala–Päivärinta
[
AP06
]
. Nachman required two derivatives to convert the conductivity equation into the Schrödringer equation. The paper of Astala and Päivärinta solved Calderón’s problem most generally for
L^{\infty}
conductivity.

Our approach relies on two main techniques - the
\bar{\partial}
-techniques and the method of stationary phase. These techniques were first used by Bukhgeim in his seminal work
[
Buk08
]
to recover the zeroth order perturbation of the Laplacian in two dimensions that has led to many developments in the study of two-dimensional inverse boundary value problems. However, his proof only gives uniqueness for potentials in the class
W^{1,p}
,
p>2
as pointed out in Blåsten’s licentiate thesis
[
Blå11
]
. The corresponding partial data problem was considered by Imanuvilov, Uhlmann and Yamamoto in
[
IUY10
]
and Guillarmou and Tzou in
[
GT11a
]
. The inverse problem for magnetic Schrödinger equation in
n=2
using Bukhgeim approach was studied by Lai in
[
Lai11
]
and for partial data by Imanuvilov, Uhlmann and Yamamoto in
[
IUY12
]
and by Tzou in
[
Tzo17
]
. We refer the interested reader to the survey article
[
GT13
]
for a detailed overview of results and techniques for inverse problems in two dimensions.

Now we state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a simply connected bounded domain with smooth boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
be two operators of the form (
1.1
) with coefficients
A_{j,k},\tilde{A}_{j,k}\in W^{j+k+1,p}(\Omega),\ p>2
, respectively. Assume that

(1.3)

\partial_{\nu}^{l}{A}_{j,k}=\partial_{\nu}^{l}{\tilde{A}}_{j,k}\text{ and }{A}_{0,k}=\tilde{A}_{0,k}\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\quad\text{ for }0\leq l\leq j-1,\ 0\leq j,k\leq m-1.

Then
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{C}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
implies that
A_{j,k}=\tilde{A}_{j,k}
on
\Omega
for
0\leq j,k\leq m-1.

We require the condition (
1.3
) on the coefficients to make the boundary terms
zero
when we apply integration by parts on the integral identity and also to apply the method of stationary phase as explained in Section
3
.

Alternatively, one can consider a Cauchy data set of the form

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})=\left\{\left(u|_{\partial\Omega},\ldots,(-\Delta)^{m-1}u|_{\partial\Omega},(\partial_{\nu}u)|_{\partial\Omega},\cdots\partial_{\nu}(-\Delta)^{m-1}u|_{\partial\Omega}\right):u\in H^{2m}(\Omega),\mathcal{L}u=0\right\},

as
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L})
can be obtained from
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})
by an explicit description for the Laplacian in the boundary normal coordinates, see
[
LU89
]
. We have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

With the hypothesis as in Theorem
1.1
,
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
implies that
A_{j,k}=\tilde{A}_{j,k}
on
\Omega
for
0\leq j,k\leq m-1.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need to construct so-called complex geometric optics (CGO) solutions. The next theorem gives the existence of such solutions in our setting. Let us fix some notation before stating the theorem. Let
\Phi=i(z-z_{0})^{2}
where
z_{0}\in\Omega
and
dS
be the surface measure on
\bd
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
a
be smooth function such that
\bar{\partial}^{m}a=0
in
\Omega
. If
A_{j,k}\in W^{j+k+1,p}(\Omega)
, for some
p>2
, then for all small
h>0
there exist solutions
u\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
to

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}u=\partial^{m}\bar{\partial}^{m}u+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{A_{j,k}\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}u}=0,\quad\text{ in }\Omega,

of the form

(1.5)

u=e^{\Phi/h}(a+r_{h}),

where the correction term
r_{h}
satisfies
\|r_{h}\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}=O(h^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon>0
.

2.
CGO Solutions

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
as stated in Introduction.
For this we need Cauchy operators -
\partial^{-1},\bar{\partial}^{-1}
which are defined by

\partial^{-1}f(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(\xi)}{\bar{z}-\bar{\xi}}d\xi\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{\partial}^{-1}f(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\Omega}\frac{f(\xi)}{z-\xi}d\xi,

for all
z
where the integrals converge. The mapping properties of these operators are listed in the following lemma. For the proofs of these properties, we refer to
[
Vek62
, Chapter I]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The operators
\partial^{-1}
and
\bar{\partial}^{-1}
have the following properties:

(1)

If
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
then
\partial\partial^{-1}f=\bar{\partial}\bar{\partial}^{-1}f=f
.

(2)

The operators
\partial^{-1},\bar{\partial}^{-1}
are bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega),1<p<\infty
.

(3)

If
1<p<\infty
, then
\partial^{-1},\bar{\partial}^{-1}:L^{p}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,p}(\Omega)
are bounded.

Now we begin the construction of CGO solutions by writing

\mathcal{L}u=\partial^{m}\bar{\partial}^{m}u+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{A_{j,k}\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}u}=0

in the following form

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}u=\partial^{m}\bar{\partial}^{m}u+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j}(A_{j,k}^{\prime}\bar{\partial}^{k}u)}=0,

where we can define
A^{\prime}_{j,k}\in W^{j+k+1,p}(\Omega)
uniquely satisfying

(2.2)

A_{j,k}=\sum_{l=j}^{m-1}{\binom{l}{j}\partial^{l-j}A^{\prime}_{l,k}}.

Substituting
u=e^{\Phi/h}f
in (
2.1
), we have

\partial^{m}\left(e^{\Phi/h}\bar{\partial}^{m}f\right)+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j}\left(e^{\Phi/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k}f\right)}=0.

Now, we write
G=e^{\Phi/h}\bar{\partial}^{m}f
and the above expression takes the form

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\partial}^{m}f&=e^{-\Phi/h}G\\
\partial^{m}G&=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j}\left(e^{\Phi/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k}f\right)}\end{split}

The problem here is that
|e^{\pm\Phi/h}|
grows too fast when
h\to 0
. This can be solved by choosing
G=e^{\bar{\Phi}/h}g
to get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{m}f

\displaystyle=e^{(\bar{\Phi}-\Phi)/h}g

(2.5)

\displaystyle\partial^{m}g

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j}\left(e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k}f\right)}

For (
2.4
), we take the solution

(2.6)

f=a+\bar{\partial}^{-m}\left(e^{(\bar{\Phi}-\Phi)/h}g\right),\quad\text{ where }\bar{\partial}^{m}a=0

and for (
2.5
) we choose the solution

(2.7)

g=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j-m}\left(e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k}f\right)}.

By combining these two, we get an integral equation for
g
of the form

(2.8)

g+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j-m}\left(e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k-m}\left(e^{(\bar{\Phi}-\Phi)/h}g\right)\right)}=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j-m}\left(e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k}a\right)}.

The above expression for
g
can be written in the form

(2.9)

(I-\mathcal{S}_{h})g=w,

where

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{h}(v)&=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j-m}\left(e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k-m}\left(e^{(\bar{\Phi}-\Phi)/h}v\right)\right)}\\
w&=-\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{\partial^{j-m}\left(e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}A^{\prime}_{j,k}\bar{\partial}^{k}a\right)}.\end{split}

The existence of a CGO solution of the form (
1.5
) to the equation (
1.4

). To this end, we estimate the norm of
\mathcal{S}_{h}
for which we need the following crucial operator bound from

.

Let

3.
Proof of uniqueness of coefficients

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We start by deriving an integral identity.
Let
u_{1},v\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
be such that

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}u_{1}=0,\quad\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{*}v=0.

By assuming
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{C}(\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
there exists
u_{2}\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
satisfying

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \tilde{\mathcal{L}}u_{2}&=0\\
u_{2}|_{\bd}&=u_{1}|_{\bd},\\
(\partial_{\nu}u_{2})|_{\bd}&=(\partial_{\nu}u_{1})|_{\bd},\\
\vdots\quad\quad&\quad\quad\vdots\\
(\partial_{\nu}^{(2m-1)}u_{2})|_{\bd}&=(\partial_{\nu}^{(2m-1)}u_{1})|_{\bd}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Note that

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}(u_{1}-u_{2})=\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}(\tilde{A}_{j,k}-A_{j,k})\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}u_{1}.

Now we use integration by parts and (

) to obtain the following integral identity

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}{(u_{1}-u_{2})\overline{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}^{*}v}\,dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}{\left[\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}(\tilde{A}_{j,k}-A_{j,k})\partial^{j}\bar{\partial}^{k}u_{1}\right]\bar{v}\,dx}.

By our assumption (
1.3
), we get the following integral identity

(3.3)

\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}\left((-1)^{j}\int_{\Omega}{({\tilde{A}^{\prime}}_{j,k}-{A^{\prime}}_{j,k})\bar{\partial}^{k}u_{1}\partial^{j}\bar{v}\,dx}\right)=0.

where
\mathcal{L}(u_{1})=0
and

.

By using Theorem
1.3

and

By using

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{j}\int_{\Omega}{\left[e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}(\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}-A^{\prime}_{j,k})(\bar{\partial}^{k}a\partial^{j}\bar{s}_{h}+\bar{\partial}^{k}r_{h}\partial^{j}\bar{b}+\bar{\partial}^{k}r_{h}\partial^{j}\bar{s}_{h})\right]}

, we have the following identity

(3.5)

\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}\left((-1)^{j}\int_{\Omega}{{e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}(\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}-A^{\prime}_{j,k})(\bar{\partial}^{k}a)(\partial^{j}\bar{b})}}\right)\\
=\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{C_{j,k}(z_{0})he^{(\Phi(z_{0})-\bar{\Phi}(z_{0}))/h}(\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}(z_{0})-{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}(z_{0}))\bar{\partial}^{k}a(z_{0})\partial^{j}\bar{b}(z_{0})}+o(h)

where

for all

.
To obtain (

.
Let
B_{j,k}=\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}-A^{\prime}_{j,k}\in W^{j+k+1,p}(\Omega)\subset C^{j+k}(\bar{\Omega})

B_{j,k}

\chi B_{j,k}+(1-\chi)B_{j,k}

Using the method of stationary phase we obtain the following identity

(3.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}

\displaystyle\left((-1)^{j}\int_{\Omega}{{e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}\chi B_{j,k}(\bar{\partial}^{k}a)(\partial^{j}\bar{b})}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{C_{j,k}(z_{0})he^{(\Phi(z_{0})-\bar{\Phi}(z_{0}))/h}\chi(z_{0})B_{j,k}(z_{0})\bar{\partial}^{k}a(z_{0})\partial^{j}\bar{b}(z_{0})}+o(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}{C_{j,k}(z_{0})he^{(\Phi(z_{0})-\bar{\Phi}(z_{0}))/h}(\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}(z_{0})-{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}(z_{0}))\bar{\partial}^{k}a(z_{0})\partial^{j}\bar{b}(z_{0})}+o(h).

Next we use integration by parts to show that the contribution from the terms

o(h).

\Omega

(3.7)

\displaystyle=h\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}\left((-1)^{j}\int_{U_{l}}{{e^{(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})/h}\partial\left(\frac{\chi_{l}(1-\chi)B_{j,k}(\bar{\partial}^{k}a)(\partial^{j}\bar{b})}{\partial(\Phi-\bar{\Phi})}\right)}}\right).

Next, we use the fact that

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Using the estimates (
3.5
) - (
3.10
) and matching the asymptotics as
h\to 0
, we obtain

(3.11)

0=\sum_{j,k=0}^{m-1}C_{j,k}(z_{0})(\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}(z_{0})-{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}(z_{0}))\bar{\partial}^{k}a(z_{0})\partial^{j}\bar{b}(z_{0}).

We now show that
A^{\prime}_{0,0}=\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{0,0}
. To this end, let us choose
a=b=1
. With this choice we obtain

\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{0,0}(z_{0})={A}^{\prime}_{0,0}(z_{0}).

Since for any
z_{0}\in\Omega
we can choose
\Phi
with a unique critical point at
z_{0}
, we have

\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{0,0}={A}^{\prime}_{0,0}\quad\text{in}\ \Omega.

Next to show that
A^{\prime}_{0,1}=\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{0,1},
we rewrite (
3.11
) by setting the term
\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{0,0}-{A}^{\prime}_{0,0}=0.
Then, we choose
a=\bar{z},b=1
to obtain

A^{\prime}_{0,1}=\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{0,1},\quad\text{in}\ \Omega.

Similarly, one can show
A^{\prime}_{j,k}=\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}
in an increasing order for
j+k
by choosing

a=\frac{\bar{z}^{k}}{k!}\ \text{ and }b=\frac{\bar{z}^{j}}{j!}

and applying the above procedure to obtain

\tilde{A}^{\prime}_{j,k}={A}^{\prime}_{j,k}\ \text{in}\ \Omega\quad\text{for all}\ \ 0\leq j,k\leq m-1.

From (
2.2
), we readily obtain that

\tilde{A}_{j,k}={A}_{j,k}\ \text{in}\ \Omega\quad\text{for all}\ \ 0\leq j,k\leq m-1.

This proves Theorem
1.1
.

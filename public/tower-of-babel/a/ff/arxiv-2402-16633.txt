Junction in a thin multi-domain for nonsimple grade two materials in BH
Rita Ferreira, José Matias, Elvira Zappale

Abstract
. We consider a thin multi-domain in ℝ N {\mathbb{R}}^{N} , with N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , consisting of a vertical rod on top of a horizontal disk made of non-simple grade-two materials or multiphase ones. In this thin multi-domain, we consider a classical hyperelastic energy and complement it by adding an interfacial energy with a bulk density of the kind W ⁡ ( D 2 ​ U ) W(D^{2}U) , where W W is a continuous function with linear growth at ∞ \infty and D 2 ​ U D^{2}U denotes the Hessian tensor of a vector-valued function U U that represents a deformation of the multi-domain. Considering suitable boundary conditions on the admissible deformations and assuming that the two volumes tend to zero at the same rate, we prove that the limit model is well posed in the union of the limit domains, with dimensions 1 1 and N − 1 N-1 , respectively, and its limiting energy keeps memory of the original full dimensional trace constraints in a more accurate way than previous related models in the literature. Moreover, we show that the limit problem is uncoupled if N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 , and “partially” coupled if N = 2 N=2 . MSC (2010): 49J45, 74B20, 74C99, 74K10, 74K20, 74K30, 74K35, 78M30, 78M35. Keywords: junction in thin multi-domains, beam, wire, thin film, bounded hessian, dimension reduction, Γ \Gamma -convergence, plasticity, martensite-austenite, trace constraints, non-simple grade-two materials, interfacial energy. Introduction

In the field of material science and engineering, the study
of thin structures is crucial for
understanding and predicting the behavior of complex materials, which have
a significant importance due to their applications in various industries,
including aerospace, civil engineering, and technology development.
Thin structures are geometrically slender
and exhibit behaviors that classical three-dimensional models fail to accurately
predict due to the significant difference in scale along at least one dimension.

The variational analysis within the context of nonlinear elasticity provides
a robust framework for understanding and predicting the behavior of these
complex materials and structures,

Of particular importance

can lead to new and complex behaviors not observed in simpler structures.

In this manuscript, we adopt a variational approach to study thin multi-structures,

In

In this work, as we focus on thin multi-domains, a penalization between interfaces as the one in

2.1.
Functional Spaces

Let

and let

We represent by

the Borel
\sigma

\mathcal{O}
,
and by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
the Banach space of all
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued

The duality pairing between
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
C_{0}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})

is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, where, as usual,

is the closure with respect to the supremum norm,
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
,
of the space of all continuous functions with compact support,
C_{c}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
The space of smooth functions with compact support is denoted either by
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
or by
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
and its dual by
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m}

We use the subscript “per” within functional spaces when considering functions
that are
1

that are
Q

Q
representing the unit cube in the ambient
space. For instance,
C^{\infty}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell};\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the space of all
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued

C^{\infty}
functions in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
that are
Q:=(0,1)^{\ell}
periodic.
Similarly,

L^{p}_{per}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{m})
with
Q=(0,1)^{\ell}
is the space of all locally integrable
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
that are
Q
periodic.

We denote by
BV(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
the space of function of bounded variation,
which is the space of all functions
u\in L^{1}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
whose distributional
derivative,
Du
, can be identified with a Radon measure in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell})
. We use the standard notation for
BV
functions,
cf.
[
2
]
.
Moreover, the space of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued functions with bounded
hessian on
\mathcal{O}

, defined in

[
24
,
22
]
, is the space of functions

for which the distributional derivative of the gradient,

, can
be identified with a Radon measure in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O};(\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times\ell})^{m})
; precisely,

\displaystyle BH(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m}):=

\displaystyle\,\{u\in W^{1,1}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m}):|D(\nabla u)|(\mathcal{O})<+\infty\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\{u\in W^{1,1}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m}):|D^{2}u|\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O})\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\{u\in L^{1}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m}):Du\in BV(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell})\}.

We observe that in the above and in the sequel, we adopt the notation for second-order derivatives of vector-valued functions used in the papers
[
39

42

dealing with junction problems that are related to ours, for a more straightforward reference. As such, if

2.3.
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexity

Here, we recall the notion of
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexity and some of its
main properties (cf.
[
33
]
,
[
18
,
17
]
), including its relationship with convexity and quasiconvexity.
These properties will be useful in the proof of our representation result.

We consider a finite family of linear operators

\left(A^{(i)}\right)_{i=1}^{\ell}\subset\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathbb{R}^{\kappa})
,
with
\mathrm{Lin}(X,Y)
the vector space of linear mappings from
a vector space
X

into a vector space
Y
, which associated matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{\kappa\times m}
we still represent by
A^{(i)}
. Then, we
define a first-order linear operator
\mathcal{A}
by setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}v:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}A^{(i)}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}}\quad\text{for }v:\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}.

We assume that
\mathcal{A}
satisfies the constant-rank
property, which states that there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\rank\left(\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}A^{(i)}w_{i}\right)=r\quad\text{for all }w\in S^{\ell-1},

where
S^{\ell-1}
is the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.

Definition
2.11 (
\mathcal{A}
-free fields).

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
be
an open set. For
p\in[1,+\infty)
, we say that a function
u\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
is
\mathcal{A}
-free, written
u\in\ker\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{A}u=0
, if

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left(A^{(i)}u\right)\cdot\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{i}}\,dx=0\quad\text{for all }\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}).

Similarly, we say
that a function
v\in L^{p}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell};\mathbb{R}^{m})
is
\mathcal{A}
-free, written
v\in\ker\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{A}v=0
, if

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\int_{Q}\left(A^{(i)}v\right)\cdot\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{i}}\,dx=0\quad\text{for all }\psi\in C^{1}_{per}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell};\mathbb{R}^{\kappa}),

where, we recall,
Q
is the
unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
Analogously, we say that a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{m})
is
\mathcal{A}
-free, written
\mu\in\ker\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{A}\mu=0
, if

\mathcal{A}\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}A^{(i)}\partial_{i}\mu=0\hbox{ in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{k}).

Definition
2.12 (
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvex functions).

We say that a Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}

is
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvex if

f(\xi)\leq\int_{Q}f(\xi+w(x))\,dx

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

and
w\in C^{\infty}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell};\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap\ker\mathcal{A}
, with

\int_{Q}w(y)dy=0.

Definition
2.13 (
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvex envelope).

Given a Borel
function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to R
, the
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvex envelope
(or
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexification)
of
f
at
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is given by

Q_{\mathcal{A}}f(\xi):=\inf\left\{\int_{Q}f(\xi+w(x))dx:w\in C^{\infty}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell};\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap\ker{\mathcal{A}},\,\int_{Q}w(y)dy=0\right\}.

Note that
Q_{\mathcal{A}}f
is the greatest
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvex
function majorized by
f
. Moreover, as observed in the following
remark, the notion of
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexification extends
to
the
\mathcal{A}
-free setting
the notion of convexity and quasiconvexity.

Remark
2.14 (Well-known properties of
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexity,
cf.
[
10
,
33
]
).

(i)

In the
\mathcal{A}\equiv 0
case, which corresponds to
no constraints on the admissible fields,
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexity
reduces to convexity by Jesen’s inequality. In particular, given
a function

f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
, we have that
Q_{\mathcal{A}}f\equiv f^{**}
for
\mathcal{A}\equiv 0
, where

f^{**}(\xi)=\sup\{g(\xi)\colon g\leq f,\,g\hbox{ is convex}\,\},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(ii)

In the
\mathcal{A}\equiv\curl
case, meaning

\mathcal{A}v=0\quad\text{if, and only if,}\quad\frac{\partial v_{jk}}{\partial x_{i}}-\frac{\partial v_{ji}}{\partial x_{k}}=0\kern 5.0pt\text{for all }1\leq j\leq m,1\leq i,k\leq\ell,

where
v:\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell}
is a matrix-valued
function, then

\displaystyle\ker\left(\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}A^{(i)}w_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell}\colon\left(\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}A^{(i)}w_{i}\right)V=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell}:V=a\otimes w\hbox{ for some }a\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\right\}\quad\text{for $w\in S^{\ell-1}.$}

In this case,
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexity reduces to quasiconvexity,
and the condition in Definition
2.12
becomes

f(\xi)\leq\int_{Q}f(\xi+\nabla w(x))\,dx\quad\hbox{ for
every $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell}$ and
}w\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

while
Q_{\mathcal{A}}f\equiv Qf
,
where

Qf(\xi):=\inf\left\{\int_{Q}f(\xi+\nabla w(x))\,dx\colon w\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{m})\right\}.

(iii)

In the case of second-order gradients, in which

v\in L^{p}(\mathcal{O};(\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times\ell}_{s})^{m})
and
\mathcal{A}v=0

if, and only if,
v=\nabla^{2}\phi
for some
\phi\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
then
\mathcal{A}
-quasiconvexity reduces to 2-quasiconvexity,
where the latter is the notion in (ii) with “
\nabla w
” replaced
by “
\nabla^{2}w
”.

In our context, with the set-up introduced in the Introduction, we deal with
the first-order linear differential
operator of constant rank given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}:(v,\zeta)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(v,\zeta)=(\mathcal{A}^{1}v,\mathcal{A}^{2}\zeta)

where, for
v:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\big(\mathbb{R}_{s}^{(n-1)\times(N-1)}\big)^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{1}v:=\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}v_{kj}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}v_{ki}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N-1,1\leq k\leq d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

and, for
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}\zeta:=\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\zeta_{j}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\zeta_{i}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Following
[
33
,
39
]
, the Kernel of the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
can be characterized as follows.
If
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\ker\mathcal{A},
then
there exist

and

such that

\displaystyle x_{1}=b_{1}\otimes w_{1}\otimes w_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{2}=b_{2}\otimes w_{2}.

We further refer to
[
39
, Remark 2.4]
for more details
on the description of the operator and its kernel in the case
in which

and

. In particular,
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is a constant-rank operator. Moreover (cf.
[
39
, (2.8)–(2.12)]
),
the
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
-quasiconvexification of the function

Fix

To prove the claim, let

\displaystyle|D^{2}u|(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{0})<\varepsilon.

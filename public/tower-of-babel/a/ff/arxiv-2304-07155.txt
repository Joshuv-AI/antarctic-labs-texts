Abstract
. We prove cocontinuity of the max \max -tensor product of C*-categories and develop a framework to perform factorization homology in a C*-setting. In such context, we specialize some results of D. Ben-Zvi, A. Brochier and D. Jordan. As a consequence of our constructions, we realize quantum Hamiltonian reduction in terms of bimodules over a factor N N . We also provide a GNS-type reconstruction theorem for C*-algebra objects in categories of bimodules over a II 1 \II_{1} -factor, enhancing a realization theorem due to C. Jones and D. Penneys.

1.
Introduction

This paper is an attempt of bringing ideas and techniques from geometric representation theory and topological quantum field theory (TQFT) to operator algebras. The guiding principle is that many interesting algebraic structures arise as linear representations of topological categories. An unital algebra, for instance, determines and is completely determined by a functor from the tensor category of oriented intervals to the category of vector spaces. Frobenius algebras are in bijection with tensor functors from the category of 2-dimensional cobordisms to the category of vector spaces. Homology theories can also be placed into this picture by reading off the Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms. It says that a homology theory is a tensor functor from the category of CW-complexes to the category of chain complexes, satisfying the excision formula. Every homology theory is determined by its value on a point, the simplest of all CW-complexes. In particular, every chain complex defines a homology theory for which its value on the point is the given chain complex.

Our goal in this work is to, following
[
17
,
4
,
6
,
7
]

\Mfld_{2}
whose objects are compact oriented surfaces, morphisms are smooth orientation preserving embeddings, and the tensor structure is given by disjoint union. A homology theory for surfaces will then be a tensor functor
F:\Mfld_{2}\to\mathcal{S}
taking values in a symmetric tensor category
\mathcal{S}
, satisfying an excision formula. In
\Mfld_{2}
, the simplest object is the disk
D

[

is sifted cocomplete and the tensor structure is sifted cocontinuous, a tensor functor

. The difference, comparing with classical homology theories, is that

The procedure that takes an

In

2.
Preliminaries on Factorization Homology

Factorization homology, as developed in
[
4
,
3
,
17
]
, is a topological incarnation of the notion of factorization algebras introduced in
[
5
]
, and elaborated in the context of perturbative QFT in
[
10
,
11
]
.

Generally, factorization homology is formulated in terms of functors between higher categories. In this paper, the target of those functors will be (2,1)-categories, allowing us to simplify the exposition.

Let
M,N
be smooth oriented surfaces. Define
\Emb(M,N)
to be the space of oriented smooth embeddings of
M
into
N
. Consider it as a topological space, endowed with the compact open topology. The fundamental groupoid
\Pi(\Emb(M,N))
is then the groupoid of isotopies between smooth oriented embeddings
M\hookrightarrow N
.

Definition 2.1
.

Define
\Mfld^{2}_{\Or}
as the (2,1)-category such that

•

objects are smooth oriented surfaces;

•

1-morphisms are the spaces of smooth oriented embeddings between smooth oriented surfaces;

•

2-morphisms are isotopies between smooth oriented embeddings.

In other words,
\Mfld^{2}_{\Or}
is the topological enriched category which associates to each pair
(M,N)
of smooth oriented surfaces the topological groupoid
\Pi(\Emb(M,N))
. Together with the operation
\sqcup
of disjoint union,
\Mfld_{\Or}^{2}
becomes a symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category having the empty set
\emptyset
as the monoidal unit.

Generally, functors out of
\Mfld^{2}_{\Or}
can be thought of as invariants of oriented surfaces, but one usually wishes for a compatibility with respect to disjoint unions, i.e., compatibility with respect to the monoidal structure, and also some formula allowing the computation of the invariant of a manifold, when it is obtained by gluing other two, in terms of the invariants associated to each manifold in the gluing, i.e., an excision formula. Factorization homology will give a recipe to construct such functors.

Suppose
(\mathcal{S},\boxtimes)
is a symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category whose objects are certain linear categories, and the 2-morphisms are natural isomorphisms, and suppose that
F:\Mfld^{2}_{\Or}\to(\mathcal{S},\boxtimes)
is a monoidal functor. Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the disk. Observe that the value
\mathcal{C}:=F(D)\in\mathcal{S}
comes equipped with certain operations
\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes n}\to\mathcal{C}
which are images of the oriented smooth embeddings
D^{\sqcup n}\hookrightarrow D
under
F
. Isotypic embeddings are sent to natural isomorphic functors. Such a structure is known as an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra in
\mathcal{S}
. In the following,
\Disk^{2}_{\Or}
is the full monoidal subcategory of
\Mfld^{2}_{\Or}
generated by the disk
D
. Being singly generated as a monoidal category, any strict monoidal functor out of
\Disk^{2}_{\Or}
is determined by its value at
D
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category. An
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra in
\mathcal{S}
is a monoidal functor
F:\Disk^{2}_{\Or}\to\mathcal{S}
. Generally, an
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebra in a symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{S}
is a monoidal functor
\Disk_{n}^{\Or}\to\mathcal{S}
.

With some abuse of language,
F(D)
itself will be referred to as an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra.

Let
F:\Mfld^{2}_{\Or}\to\mathcal{S}
be a monoidal functor. Then
\mathcal{C}=F(D)
is an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-algebra. Given an oriented surface
M
,
F(M)
has the following property: in the figure below, any commutative diagram as in the left side induces through
F
a commutative diagram as in the right side:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(D)^{\sqcup n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(D)^{\sqcup m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(M)}

If
\mathcal{S}
is cocomplete, then
F(M)
is the universal object having this property. In this case, one usually writes

F(M)=\underset{(D)^{\sqcup n}\hookrightarrow M}{\text{colim}}\mathcal{C}^{\boxtimes n}=:\int_{M}\mathcal{C}\ ,

the integral notation at the right-hand-side indicating that
F
can be computed via a “passage from local-to-global” procedure. Thus, when
\mathcal{C}
is cocomplete,
F
is completely determined by its value at
D
.

Definition 2.3
.

In the above,
\int_{(-)}\mathcal{C}=F
is called the factorization homology with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}
and with values in
\mathcal{S}
.

In what follows we shall write
I
for the oriented unit interval. Let
M
be a surface obtained by a collar gluing of the surfaces
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
along a 1-dim. manifold
M_{0}
:

M\simeq M_{1}\underset{M_{0}\times I}{\sqcup}M_{2}

There is an embedding
M_{0}\times I\sqcup M_{0}\times I\hookrightarrow M_{0}\times I
by stacking cylinders. By functoriality, there is an induced functor

\int_{M_{0}\times I}\mathcal{C}\ \boxtimes\int_{M_{0}\times I}\mathcal{C}\to\int_{M_{0}\times I}\mathcal{C}\ .

This gives a monoidal structure to the factorization homology over
M_{0}\times I
.

Also, there are embeddings

M_{1}\sqcup M_{0}\times I\hookrightarrow M_{1}\ \text{and}\ M_{0}\times I\sqcup M_{2}\hookrightarrow M_{2}\ ,

providing factorization homology over
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
with a right module structure and a left module structure over
\int_{M_{0}\times I}\mathcal{C}
, respectively. A monoidal functor
F=\int_{(-)}\mathcal{C}
as above is said to have the excision property with respect to collar gluing if

\int_{M}\mathcal{C}\simeq\int_{M_{1}}\mathcal{C}\underset{\int_{\small M_{0}\times I}\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\int_{M_{2}}\mathcal{C}\ .

(

If
(\mathcal{S},\boxtimes)
is a cocomplete symmetric monoidal (2,1)-category and

3.
C*-categorical Framework

In this Section we establish the categorical framework for C
∗
-algebraic factorization homology. We shall write
\Vect
for the category of vector spaces and linear maps,
\Vect_{\fd}
for its full subcategory of finite dimensional vector spaces,
\mathrm{Hilb}
for the category of Hilbert spaces and bounded linear maps and
\mathrm{Hilb}_{\fd}
for its full subcategory of finite dimensional Hilbert spaces.

3.1.
C*-categories

A linear category is a small category
\mathcal{C}
enriched over
\Vect
: for any pair
(X,Y)
of objects in
\mathcal{C}
,
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
is a vector space, and the composition of morphisms is bilinear. A
*
-structure on a linear category
\mathcal{C}
is a contravariant endofunctor
*:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
which at the level of objects is the identity map
\Obj(\mathcal{C})\to\Obj(\mathcal{C})
and which is involutive at the level of morphisms:
*\circ*:\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,X)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
is the identity for all
X,Y\in\Obj(\mathcal{C})
.
*
-structures are also known as
dagger-structures
. Write
X\in\mathcal{C}
if
X\in\Obj(\mathcal{C})
. The definition below follows
[
2
]
and
[
14
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
*
-category
\mathcal{C}
is a C*-category if

(1)

for every
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
, every
f\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
, there exists
g\in\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
such that
f^{*}\circ f=g^{*}\circ g
;

(2)

for every
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
, the function
\|\cdot\|:\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)\to[0,+\infty)
given by

\|f\|^{2}:=\sup\{|\lambda|\geq 0\ |\nexists(\ f^{*}\circ f-\lambda\mathrm{id}_{X})^{-1}\}

makes
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Y)
into a Banach space, with the additional properties
\|g\circ f\|\leq\|g\|\|f\|
for every pair
(f,g)
of composable morphisms, and
\|f^{*}\circ f\|=\|f\|^{2}
for all morphisms
f
.

In particular, a C*-category is a category enriched over the category of Banach spaces, where the morphisms in the latter category are the contractive linear maps, such that every endomorphism algebra
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
is a C*-algebra. We shall consider only categories with direct sums. In this case, condition (1) in the above definition is redundant, since it can be derived from the C*-identity together with an amplification trick.

In a C*-category
\mathcal{C}
, a morphism
f
is said to be unitary if
f^{*}=f^{-1}
. If
f
is an endomorphism, it is said to be self-adjoint if
f^{*}=f
and a projection if
f=f^{*}=f^{2}
. A morphism
f
such that
ff^{*}
is a projection is called a partial isometry, and in this case it also follows that
f^{*}f
is a projection. Given two C*-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, a
*
-functor from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a linear functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
which commutes with the respective
*
-structures:
F(f)^{*}=F(f^{*})
for every morphism
f
in
\mathcal{C}
. If
F
and
F^{\prime}
are
*
-functor between C*-categories, a natural transformation
\eta:F\to F^{\prime}
is called a unitary natural isomorphism if every component
\eta_{X}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(F(X),F^{\prime}(X))
is unitary.

Definition 3.2
.

Define
\Clin
to be the (2,1) category consisting of

•

objects: C*-categories with direct sum;

•

1-morphisms:
*
-functors;

•

2-morphisms: unitary natural isomorphisms between functors.

Remark 3.3
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors make a distinction between categories with or without units, but wee shall deal only with unital categories.

3.2.
The monoidal structure of
\Clin

In
[
2
]
it is shown that
\Clin
is cocomplete, i.e., it has small colimits. It has at least two monoidal products
\underset{\min}{\boxtimes}
and
\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}
, induced by the min and max tensor products of C*-algebras, respectively. The main result of this section is that
\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}
is cocontinuous. This monoidal structure is an operator algebraic version of the Deligne-Kelly monoidal product.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be C*-categories with direct sums. We first introduce an intermediate category
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
and then define
\mathcal{C}\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{D}
to be its Karoubi completion. Objects in
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}
are pairs
(X,Y)\in\Obj(\mathcal{C})\times\Obj(\mathcal{D})
; they will be denoted by
X\boxtimes Y
. Define

\End_{\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}}(X\boxtimes Y):=\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)\underset{\max}{\otimes}\End_{\mathcal{D}}(Y)\ ,

the
\max
tensor product of the C*-algebras
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
and
\End_{\mathcal{D}}(Y)
. Given objects
X\boxtimes Y
and
X^{\prime}\boxtimes Y^{\prime}
, there is an inclusion

\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,X^{\prime})\underset{\mathbb{C}}{\otimes}\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(Y,Y^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\End_{\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}}((X\boxtimes Y)\oplus(X^{\prime}\boxtimes Y^{\prime}))\ .

Then
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}\boxtimes\mathcal{D}}(X\boxtimes Y,X^{\prime}\boxtimes Y^{\prime})
is defined as the closure of the image of this inclusion. Direct sums of objects are defined coordinatewisely and extended to morphisms in the obvious way.

Definition 3.4
.

Define

Proposition 3.9

In
\Clin

-algebras are C*-tensor categories,

3.3.
Module categories

If
\mathcal{M}
is a linear category, the endofunctor category
\End(\mathcal{M})
, objects being endofunctors and morphims natural transformations, has a canonical structure of monoidal category: the tensor product is given by composition, the unit is the identity functor
\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
, and all the structure morphisms are strict, i.e., they are all identity morphisms. When
\mathcal{M}
is a C*-category, we denote by
\End(\mathcal{M})
the C*-category of
*
-endofunctors of
\mathcal{M}
, with completely bounded natural transformations as morphisms. More precisely, a natural transformation
\eta:F\to F^{\prime}
between two
*
-functors is determined by a natural collection
(\eta_{X}\in\Hom_{\mathcal{M}}(F(X),F^{\prime}(X)))_{X\in\mathcal{M}}
, and we ask for

\sup_{X}\|\eta_{X}\|<\infty\ .

Definition 3.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a C*-tensor category. A right
\mathcal{C}
-module category is a category
\mathcal{M}
with a tensor functor
\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\End(\mathcal{M})
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a C*-category and the functor
\mathcal{C}\to\End(\mathcal{M})
is a
*
-tensor functor, we say that
\mathcal{M}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-module C*-category.

Let
(\mathcal{M},F:\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\End(\mathcal{M}))
, be a
\mathcal{C}
-module C*-category as in the above definition. We shall write

F(X)(m)=:m\triangleleft X\ ,\ \forall m\in\mathcal{M},\ \forall\ X\in\mathcal{C}\ ,

and will often omit
F
. Then the unitary tensor structure of
F
says that there are natural unitary isomorphisms

\displaystyle(m\triangleleft X)\triangleleft Y

\displaystyle\simeq m\triangleleft(X\otimes Y)\ ,

\displaystyle m\triangleleft{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\simeq m\ ,

with further compatibility conditions.

Here is a way of constructing module categories. Given an algebra object

, let
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{A}
be the category of free left
A

There is a converse construction: an object

Theorem 3.23
.

Suppose that
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is a
*
-functor between C*-categories admitting a right adjoint
F^{R}
. Then
F^{R}
is automatically a
*
-functor.

Proof.

In any
*
-category
\mathcal{A}
, the involution provides anti-linear isomorphisms
\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X^{\prime})\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(X^{\prime},X)
, for all
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
. This is quite a strong feature about
*
-categories, which implies for instance that if a
*
-functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
admits a right adjoint
F^{R}
, then
F^{R}
is also a left adjoint for
F
. Indeed, if the the adjunction
F\leftrightarrow F^{R}
is witnessed by a natural transformation
\Psi=\{\Psi_{X,Y}:\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),Y)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,F^{R}(Y))\}
, then
\Phi:=\{\Phi_{Y,X}:\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(F^{R}(Y),X)\to\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(Y,F(X))\}
defined via the commutativity of the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(F^{R}(Y),X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(Y,F(X))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,F^{R}(Y))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),Y)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{Y,X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{X,Y}^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*}

witnesses an adjunction
F^{R}\leftrightarrow F
. From this fact we shall deduce the Theorem.

The strategy to prove that
F^{R}
is a
*
-functor goes as follows. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a C*-category, the space
\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,F^{R}(Y))
has a canonical structure of a right Hilbert C*-module over
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
, where the action is given by
f\triangleleft a:=f\circ a
, and the
\mathcal{C}(X)
-valued inner product is given by
\left\langle f,g\right\rangle:=f^{*}\circ g
. We define a Hilbert C*-module, over
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
, on
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),Y)
sucht that
\Psi_{X,Y}
is a isomorphism of Hilbert C*-modules. Then a morphism
T\in\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(Y,Y^{\prime})
defines an adjointable linear map between the Hilbert C*-modules
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),Y)
and
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),Y^{\prime})
, which corresponds to the adjointable linear map
F^{R}(T)\circ(-):\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,F^{R}(Y))\to\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(X,F^{R}(Y^{\prime}))
. The Theorem is then obtained by observing that both
F^{R}(T)^{*}\circ(-)
and
F^{R}(T^{*})\circ(-)
are adjoints of this map, and therefore must coincide. Let us check this.

The
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
-action of
\Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(F(X),Y)
is given by

f\triangleleft a:=\Psi_{X,Y}^{-1}(\Psi_{X,Y}(f)\circ a)\ ,

and the
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(X)
-valued inner product is given by

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle:=\Psi_{X,Y}(f)^{*}\circ\Psi_{X,Y}(g)\ .

By construction,
\Psi_{X,Y}
is an isomorphism of Hilbert C*-modules. In particular there is an identification

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}(X)}(\mathcal{D}(F(X),Y),\mathcal{D}(F(X),Y^{\prime}))=\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}(X)}(\mathcal{C}(X,F^{R}(Y)),\mathcal{C}(X,F^{R}(Y^{\prime}))\Psi_{X,Y}\ .

Given
T\in\mathcal{D}(Y,Y^{\prime})
the map
F^{R}(T)\circ(-):\mathcal{C}(X,F^{R}(Y))\to\mathcal{C}(X,F^{R}(Y^{\prime}))
is adjointable, the adjoint given by
F^{R}(T)^{*}\circ(-)
. From the above equality,
T\circ(-):\mathcal{D}(F(X),Y)\to\mathcal{D}(F(X),Y^{\prime})
is adjointable. One has

\displaystyle\left\langle T\circ f,g\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}(T\circ f)^{*}\circ\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}(g)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}((T\circ f)^{**})^{*}\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}(g)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{Y^{\prime},X}(f^{*}\circ T^{*})\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}(g)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{Y,X}(f^{*})F^{R}(T^{*})\Psi_{X,Y^{\prime}}(g)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{X,Y}(f)^{*}\Psi_{X,Y}(T^{*}\circ g)\ ,

by naturality of
\Phi

\displaystyle M_{B}\otimes X

\displaystyle=act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})\otimes X

\displaystyle\simeq act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}\triangleleft X)

\displaystyle=act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}\otimes F(X)

\displaystyle\simeq act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}(F(X)\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle=act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}(X\triangleright{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\simeq X\otimes act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle=X\otimes M_{B}\ \

where all the isomorphisms are canonical, unitary and natural in

The multiplication

is provided by the adjunction data between

and

\displaystyle M_{B}\otimes M_{B}

\displaystyle=act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})\otimes act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\simeq act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}\triangleleft act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}))

\displaystyle=act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}(act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}))\overset{act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}(\epsilon_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}})}{\longrightarrow}act_{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}}}^{R}({\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{B}})=M_{B}\ ,

where

To show that

was strict, the equality would be tautological since we would have

Since every monoidal category is equivalent to a strict one, the claim about the commutativity of

Remark 4.1

In the purely algebraic setting of linear categories, one has to distinguish between factorization homology for framed surfaces and factorization homology for oriented surfaces. This is so because for the latter the

Every braided unitary tensor category has a canonical ribbon/balancing structure (

4.1.
Punctured surfaces

Punctured surfaces are obtained by collar gluing of handles into a disk along marked intervals on the boundary of the the disk. The gluing is described by a combinatorial data called gluing pattern, a bijection

(i,i^{\prime})
of the
i

.

Examples:

(1)

\left\langle\mu^{H}((a^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{i,(1)}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(1)})\otimes\mu^{\mathcal{F}}(a^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{i,(2)}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(2)})),\eta^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(1)}\otimes\eta^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(2)}\right\rangle\to\left\langle\mu^{H}((a^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(1)}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(1)})\otimes\mu^{\mathcal{F}}(a^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(2)}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(2)})),\eta^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(1)}\otimes\eta^{{\mathds{1}}_{\mathcal{C}}}_{(2)}\right\rangle\ .

Step 3:
One has

\displaystyle\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{(1)}\otimes\xi^{\mathds{1}}_{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y}\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Y}\otimes R_{Y}

\displaystyle\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{(1)}\otimes\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z}\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Z}\otimes R_{Z}\ .

Thus the convergence in Step 2 becomes

\sum_{X,Y}\sum_{Z\leq X\otimes Y}\left\langle\mu^{H}\left((a_{i})^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Y}\right),\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Z}\right\rangle\to\sum_{X,Y}\sum_{Z\leq X\otimes Y}\left\langle\mu^{H}\left(a^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Y}\right),\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Z}\right\rangle\ ,

i.e.,

\sum_{X,Y}\sum_{Z\leq X\otimes Y}\left\langle(a_{i})^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\triangleright\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Y},\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Z}\right\rangle\to\sum_{X,Y}\sum_{Z\leq X\otimes Y}\left\langle a^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\triangleright\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Y},\eta^{{\mathds{1}}}_{Z}\right\rangle\ ,

Step 4:
There are inclusions
\mathcal{L}^{2}(N)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}^{2}(N)\otimes\mathcal{F}^{(}{\mathds{1}})\hookrightarrow H(\mathcal{F})
, where a vector
\xi\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(N)
corresponds to the vector

\sum_{X}\xi^{X}_{(1)}\otimes\xi^{X}_{(2)}=\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{(1)}\otimes\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{(2)}=\sum_{X}\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\otimes R_{X}\ ,

and where in the last expression the only non-zero factor is when
X={\mathds{1}}
, in which case
\xi^{{\mathds{1}}}_{{\mathds{1}}}=\xi
. Then Step 3 reduces to

\sum_{X}\left\langle(a_{i})^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\triangleright\xi,\eta\right\rangle\to\sum_{X}\left\langle a^{{\mathds{1}}}_{X}\triangleright\xi,\eta\right\rangle\ .

∎

Given a closed surface
\Sigma
, we were able to describe factorization homology over
\Sigma
in terms of
A_{\Sigma^{\circ}}
, the C*-algebra in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
representing factorization homology of
\Sigma^{\circ}:\Sigma\setminus D
, and the reflection equation algebra
\mathcal{F}
. Observe that, since here
\mathcal{C}
is a fusion category, both
A_{\Sigma^{\circ}}
and
\mathcal{F}
are compact, i.e., they are C*-algebras in
\mathcal{C}
. Using
H
we can also realize the quantum Hamiltonian reduction of Proposition
4.10
using Propostion
6.2
:

Theorem 6.4
.

There is an isomorphism

\End_{\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{S})\simeq\Hom_{\Bim(N)}(\mathcal{L}^{2}(N),H(A_{\Sigma^{0}})\underset{M_{\mathcal{F}}}{\otimes}\mathcal{L}^{2}(N))\

between the endomorphisms of the quantum structure sheaf on
\Sigma
and the central vectors in

.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.10
, and since

is a fully faithful
*
-functor, there is an isomorphism

\End_{\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma})\simeq\Hom_{\Bim(N)}(\mathcal{L}^{2}(N),H(A_{\Sigma^{0}}\underset{M_{\mathcal{F}}}{\otimes}\mathcal{L}^{2}(N)))\ .

Appendix A
Yetter-Drinfeld C
∗
-algebras and centrally cyclic bimodule C
*
-categories

This Appendix gives a characterization of certain bimodule C
∗
-categories over a given UTC
\mathcal{C}
in terms of internal C
∗
-algebra objects. Among the examples are C
∗
-tensor categories
\mathcal{B}
for which the bimodule structure is induced by a unitary tensor functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{B}
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an UTC. A centrally pointed
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C
∗
-category
(\mathcal{M},m,\alpha)
is a
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C
∗
-category
\mathcal{M}
together with a distinguished object
m\in\mathcal{M}
and an unitary natural isomorphism

\alpha=\{\alpha_{U}:m\triangleleft U\simeq U\triangleright m\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}

which is coherent wrt. the tensor structure of
\mathcal{C}
. If
(\mathcal{M},m,\alpha)
is a centrally pointed
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C
∗
-category such that
m
is cyclic, we say that
(\mathcal{M},m,\alpha)
is a centrally cyclic
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C
∗
-category.

When
\mathcal{C}=\Rep\mathbb{G}
is the representation category of a compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
, it is shown in
[
13
]
that the category of centrally pointed bimodule C
∗
-categories is equivalent to Yetter-Drinfeld
\mathbb{G}
-C
∗
-algebras, i.e., the category of continuous actions of the Drinfeld double
\mathcal{D}\mathbb{G}
of
\mathbb{G}
on unital C
∗
-algebras. This will justify Definition
A.4
below.

Up to unitary equivalence of
\mathcal{C}
-module C
∗
-categories, we can assume that
(\mathcal{M},m)
is, as a cyclic right
\mathcal{C}
-module C
∗
-category, of the form
(\mathcal{M}_{A},A)
, where
A=(A,\mu)
is a C
∗
-algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathcal{M}_{A}
is the Karoubi completion of the full subcategory of free left
A
-modules of the form
A\otimes U
, with
U\in\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{A},A)
be a cyclic right
\mathcal{C}
-module C
∗
-category as above and let
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{M}_{A}\to\mathcal{M}_{A}
be a left
\mathcal{C}
-module C
∗
-structure on
\mathcal{M}_{A}
. Suppose that there is an unitary natural isomorphism

\alpha:\mathcal{C}\triangleright A\simeq A\triangleleft\mathcal{C}

which is coherent wrt. the tensor structure of
\mathcal{C}
. Then there are unitary isomorphisms

U\triangleright A\simeq U\otimes A\ ,

natural in
U
.

Proof.

Since the right
\mathcal{C}
-module structure is given by
A\triangleleft U=A\otimes U
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{A}(U\triangleright A,A)\overset{\alpha_{U}\circ(-)}{\simeq}\mathcal{M}_{A}(A\otimes U,A)\simeq\Vect(\mathcal{C})(U,A)\ .

Hence, the internal endomorphism of
A
wrt. the left
\mathcal{C}
action is
A
itself.
∎

Since every object of
\mathcal{M}_{A}
is a subobject of one of the form
A\otimes U
, the Lemma above says that the left
\mathcal{C}
-action on
\mathcal{M}_{A}
is unitarily equivalent to
V\triangleright(A\otimes U)=V\otimes A\otimes U
. Under this identification, the unitary isomorphism
\alpha_{U}:A\otimes U\simeq U\triangleright A
becomes an isomorphism
\sigma_{U}:A\otimes U\simeq U\otimes A
, coherent wrt. the the tensor structure of
\mathcal{C}
. This means exactly that
\sigma=\{\sigma_{U}\}_{U\in\mathcal{C}}
is a half-braiding on the object
A\in\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. Moreover, each component
\sigma_{U}
is a morphism of left
A
-modules (i.e., a morphism in
\mathcal{M}_{A}
), where the object
U\otimes A
has the left
A
-module structure given by

A\otimes U\otimes A\overset{\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{U}^{-1}}{\simeq}A\otimes A\otimes U\overset{\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{U}}{\longrightarrow}A\otimes U\overset{\sigma_{U}}{\simeq}U\otimes A\ .

Equivalently,

\sigma_{U}(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{U})=(\mathrm{id}_{U}\otimes\mu)(\sigma_{U}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\sigma_{U})\ .

This equation characterizes the multiplication
\mu:A\otimes A\to A
as a morphism
(A,\sigma)\otimes(A,\sigma)\to(A,\sigma)
of half-braidings in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.

Remark A.3
.

In the above, objects of the form
U\otimes A
, endowed with the left
A
-module structure induced by the half-braiding
\sigma
, are strictly speaking not objects in the category
\mathcal{M}_{A}
, but isomorphic as left
A
-modules to objects of that category. We shall however abuse notation and write for example
\mathcal{M}_{A}(U\otimes A,A\otimes V)
. This will not cause any trouble since we are always considering full subcategories of the category of left
A
-modules.

Observe that, starting with a centrally cyclic
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C
∗
-category of the form
(\mathcal{M}_{A},A,\alpha)
and considering the induced half-braiding
(A,\sigma)
, the unitarity of
\alpha_{U}\in\mathcal{M}_{A}(A\otimes U,U\triangleright A)
corresponds to the unitarity of
\sigma_{U}
as an element of
\mathcal{M}_{A}(A\otimes U,U\otimes A)
.

Definition A.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an UTC. A C
∗
-algebra object in
\mathcal{Z}(\Vect(\mathcal{C}))
is an algebra
(A,\mu,\sigma)\in\mathcal{Z}(\Vect(\mathcal{C}))
together with a
*
-algebra structure
j
such that, wrt. the corresponding dagger structure on
(\mathcal{M}_{A},A)
, the components
\sigma_{U}\in\mathcal{M}_{A}(A\otimes U,U\otimes A)
of
\sigma
are unitary and such that the
*
-algebra
(A,\mu,j)
in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
is a C
∗
-algebra. A Yetter-Drinfeld C
∗
-algebra in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
is a C
∗
-algebra object in
\mathcal{Z}(\Vect(\mathcal{C}))
.

The discussion preceding Definition
A.4
proves

Theorem A.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an UTC. Then the category of centrally cyclic
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C
∗
categories is equivalent to the category of Yetter-Drinfeld C
∗
-algebras in
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition A.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an UTC and
A=(A,\mu,j)
be a C
∗
-algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. A unitary half-braiding
\sigma
on
A
is a half-braiding on
A
such that
(A,\mu,\sigma,j)
is a Yetter-Drinfeld C
∗
-algebra in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
.

Appendix B
Maximal braided tensor product

A prominent construction in factorization homology is the braided tensor product of module categories. In this section we provide a concrete description of maximal braided tensor products between cyclic
\mathcal{C}
-module C*-categories. The discussion however makes sense in a more general context; we don’t need to assume that
\mathcal{C}
has a braiding, and the braided tensor product will still make sense as long as the algebras representing the module categories possess an additional structure, that of Yetter-Drinfled algebras. We shall discuss such structures shortly.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an unitary tensor category,
\mathcal{M}_{A}
a cyclic
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule C*-category and
\mathcal{M}_{B}
be cyclic left
\mathcal{C}
-module
C^{*}
-category, corresponding to the C*-algebras
A
and
B
, respectively. Write
\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}
for their maximal, relative tensor product (see
[
2
]
, Section 5). To define a left action of
\mathcal{C}
on
\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}
is the same as defining a tensor functor
\mathcal{C}\to\End(\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B})
. This is the same as defining a
*
-functor
\mathcal{C}\to\Bal_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B},\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B})
.

Denote by
\gamma:\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}\to\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}
the canonical
\mathcal{C}
-balanced
*
-functor. For
U\in\mathcal{C}
, let

U\blacktriangleright(-):=\gamma\circ(U\triangleright(-)\boxtimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}}):\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}\to\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}\ .

If
\beta=\{\beta_{X_{A},Y_{B};V}:\gamma(X_{A}\triangleleft V\boxtimes Y_{B})\simeq\gamma(X_{A}\boxtimes V\triangleright Y_{B})\}_{X_{A},Y_{B},V}
is the balancing structure of
\gamma
, then

\beta^{U}:=\left\{\beta_{U\triangleright X_{A},Y_{B};V}:U\blacktriangleright(X_{A}\triangleleft V\boxtimes Y_{B})\simeq U\blacktriangleright(X_{A}\boxtimes V\triangleright Y_{B})\right\}_{X_{A},Y_{B},V}

is a balancing structure on
U\blacktriangleright(-)
. If
T\in\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(U,U^{\prime})
, a natural transformation from
U\blacktriangleright(-)
to
U^{\prime}\blacktriangleright(-)
is given by
\gamma(T\triangleright(-)\boxtimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}_{B}})
. We obtain thus an action
\mathcal{C}\curvearrowright\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}
, and the same reasoning, exchanging left and right actions, gives a left action
\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}\curvearrowleft\mathcal{C}
. We will denoted these action by
\triangleright
and
\triangleleft
, respectively.

We claim that, if
A
and
B
are Yetter-Drinfeld C*-algebras in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
, the C*-algebra object representing
\mathcal{M}_{A}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{M}_{B}
is also a Yetter-Drinfeld C*-algebra.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are cyclic bimodule C
∗
-categories, with central generators
m
and
n
, respectively. That is to say,
\underline{\End}(m)
and
\underline{\End}(n)
are Yetter-Drinfeld C*-algebras in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
. As a direct consequence of the definitions and the above constructions, we see that there is a canonical unitary natural isomorphism

U\triangleright\gamma(m\boxtimes n)\simeq\gamma(m\boxtimes n)\triangleleft U\ ,

with respect to the variable
U\in\mathcal{C}
. That is, the object
\gamma(m\boxtimes m)\in\mathcal{M}\boxtimes_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{N}
is central with respect to the bimodule structure. Any functor
\mathcal{M}\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}
being another
C^{*}
-category, is completely determined by its restriction to objects of the form
U\triangleright m\boxtimes n\triangleleft V
, for
U,V\in\mathcal{C}
, and the morphisms between them. This holds in particular for the canonical balanced functor
\gamma:\mathcal{M}\underset{\max}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{M}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{N}
, which corresponds to the identity endofunctor of
\mathcal{M}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{N}
. Together with the centrality of
\gamma(m\boxtimes n)
, this implies that this element is a generating object for the relative tensor product. The following Theorem summarizes the discussion.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},m)
and
(\mathcal{N},n)
be as above, corresponding to Yetter-Drinfeld
C^{*}
-algebras
A:=\underline{\End}(m)
and
B:=\underline{\End}(n)
respectively. For the universal relative tensor product
\mathcal{M}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{N}
, one has canonical left and right
\mathcal{C}
-actions and a canonical object
m_{A\boxtimes B}
such that,
(\mathcal{M}\underset{\mathcal{C}}{\boxtimes}\mathcal{N},m_{A\boxtimes B})
is a cyclic
\mathcal{C}
-bimodule
C^{*}
-category with central generator. It is defined to be the maximal braided tensor product of
(\mathcal{M},m)
and
(\mathcal{N},n)
.

The
C^{*}
-algebra object in
\Vect(\mathcal{C})
representing the braided tensor products of the module categories is denoted by
A\boxtimes B
, and it is called the braided tensor product of the Yetter-Drinfeld
C^{*}
-algebra objects
A
and
B
.

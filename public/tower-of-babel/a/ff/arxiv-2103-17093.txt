Exceptional collections, t-structures, and categorical action of shifted q=0 affine algebra, 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} case
You-Hung Hsu

Abstract
. In this article, we show that the categorical action of the shifted q = 0 q=0 affine algebra 𝒰 ˙ 0 , N ​ ( L ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ) \dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2}) can be used to construct (or induce) t-structures on the weight categories. The main idea is to interpret the exceptional collection constructed by Kapranov [ Kap85 ] , [ Kap88 ] as convolution of Fourier-Mukai kernels in the categorical action via using the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem. In particular, when the categories are the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians. The t-structure we obtain is precisely the exotic t-structure defined by Bezrukavnikov [ Be1 ] , [ Be2 ] of the exceptional collections given by Kapranov. As an application, we calculate the matrix coefficients for generators of the shifted q = 0 q=0 affine algebra on the basis given by Kapranov exceptional collections.

1.
Introduction

The derived categories of coherent sheaves on algebraic varieties plays a central role in modern algebraic geometry and related areas. In particular, when the varieties are spaces of importance to geometric representation theory, e.g., Springer fibres, Nakajima quiver varieties, then their (equivariant) derived category of coherent sheaves also provide a good source for categorification of representations of certain Hecke algebras or quantum groups.

There are some important devices from homological algebras like exceptional objects and t-structures that would be frequently used to study them. Also, these devices have applications to geometric representation theory for the varieties that we mention above, for example, see
[
Be3
]
.

1.1.
Motivation

In the paper
[
Hsu
]
, the author introduce the shifted
q=0
affine algebra
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
, and he give a definition of its categorical action. Then he showed that there is a categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{n})
on the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on partial flag varieties
\bigoplus_{\underline{k}}\mathcal{D}^{b}(Fl_{\underline{k}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}))
. In this paper we focus on the
n=2
case; i.e., we have the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

We try to relate the notion of categorical action defined in
loc. cit.
to the exceptional objects and t-structures on those weight categories. More precisely, since we constructed a categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
, when it pass to the Grothendieck group
\bigoplus_{k}K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
we obtain a representation of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. Usually in representation theory, it would be good to find a basis for a representation such that generators of the algebra have a particularly simple action on it, e.g., crystal bases or canonical bases.

So the motivation of this paper comes from finding a good or natural basis for the Grothendieck group
K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
and to study how the generators of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
acting on them. Finally, throughout this article, all functors between derived categories are assumed to be derived functors, e.g., we will write
f^{*}
instead of
Rf^{*}
and so on.

1.2.
Basis from exceptional collections

The Grassmannians and partial flag varieties are homogeneous spaces; i.e., they are certain quotient
G/P
of a complex semisimple algebraic group
G
by a parabolic subgroup
P
. The structure of their bounded derived category of coherent sheaves, denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{b}(G/P)
, has been studied over many years. In particular, by Beilinson, Kapranov
[
B
]
,
[
Kap85
]
,
[
Kap88
]
for type A, and by Kuznetsov, Polishchuk
[
KP
]
for other general types. Their studies lead to the construction of exceptional collections on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(G/P)
. We also refer to
[
Ku
]
and
[
PS
]
for some cases studies, and
[
BLVdB
]
and
[
E
]
for a characteristic free point of view.

Since the geometry we use to construct the categorical action is the partial flag varieties of type A, the exceptional collections were constructed by Beilinson and Kapranov, see Corollary
2.12
,
2.16
. Moreover, those exceptional collections are full; i.e., the smallest triangulated subcategory containing the exceptional collection is the whole derived category. Thus when it pass to the Grothendieck group, we get a basis for the representation.

Let
P(a,b)
the set of Young diagrams
\lambda
such that
\lambda_{1}\leq a
and
\lambda_{b+1}=0
. From Corollary
2.16
, the exceptional collection for
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
is given by
\{\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}\ |\ \lambda\in P(N-k,k)\}
, where
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
is the Schur functor associate to the Young diagram
\lambda
. Here
\mathcal{V}
is the tautological rank
k
bundle on
\mathbb{G}(k,N)
. Passing to the Grothendieck group, we obtain the basis
\{[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]\ |\ \lambda\in P(N-k,k)\}
for
K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

So we try to understand how the main generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
and
f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
acting on
\{[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]\}
for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that the main generators of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
acting on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
is defined by using the following correspondence diagram

\textstyle{Fl(k-1,k)=\{0\overset{k-1}{\subset}V^{\prime}\overset{1}{\subset}V\overset{N-k}{\subset}\mathbb{C}^{N}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}
\textstyle{\mathbb{G}(k-1,N)}

(1.1)

where
Fl(k-1,k)
is the
3
-step partial flag variety and the numbers above the inclusion indicate the increasing of dimensions. Here
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are natural projections. Let
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
be the tautological bundles on
Fl(k-1,k)
of rank
k
,
k-1
, respectively. Then there is a natural line bundle
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
on
Fl(k-1,k)
. The generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
acting on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
by lifting to the following functor

{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}:=p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\otimes(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime})^{r}):\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k-1,N))

and similarly for the lift of
f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
to
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
.

After decategorifying, it is not easy to calculate the action of generators on this basis directly by definition. For example, when
r=0
, we have to calculate
{e}1_{(k,N-k)}([\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}])=p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}([\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]))
for all
\lambda
, which may take an amount of efforts.

To solve this, we lift it to the categorical level, which means we study the categorical action
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on the exceptional collection
\{\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}\}
. The key observation is to interpret
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}
as convolution of Fourier-Mukai kernels by using the relative version of Borel-Weil-Bott theorem, see Theorem
5.5
and Corollary
5.7
in Section
5.1
for details.

Since the functor
{\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
is a Fourier-Mukai transformation with kernel given by
\mathcal{E}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}=\iota_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Fl(k-1,k)}
, where
\iota:Fl(k-1,k)\rightarrow\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k-1,N)
. The calculation of
{\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})
is given by convolution of Fourier-Mukai kernels, which can be calculated by using the relations in the definition of categorical action. As an application, when we pass to the Grothendieck group, we obtain the result for
{e}1_{(k,N-k)}([\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}])
. Similarly for the functor
{\sf{F}}1_{(k,N-k)}
and
{f}1_{(k,N-k)}([\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}])
, see Proposition
6.1
and Corollary
6.2
in Section
6.1
for details.

However, for
{e}_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
and
{f}_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
with
r,s\neq 0
, the above method fails. Since lifting to the categorical levels, the calculation of
{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})
and
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})
involves relations that are not defined in the definition of categorical action. We solve this by the help of dual exceptional collections, see Definition
4.9
for the definition. By definition, after passing to the Grothendieck group, the dual exceptional collection can be thought as a dual basis for the exceptional collection with respect to the bilinear Euler form
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(-,-[i])
.

The dual exceptional collection for
\{\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}\}
is given by
\{\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|]\ |\ \mu\in P(k,N-k)\}
, and
|\mu|
is the sum of all boxes in
\mu
. So every element
[\mathcal{X}]\in K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
can be written as

[\mathcal{X}]=\sum_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}(-1)^{|\lambda|}\mathrm{gdim}R\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{*}}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}),\mathcal{X})[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]

(1.2)

where
\lambda^{*}
is the conjugate partition of
\lambda
and
\mathrm{gdim}
is the notation for graded dimension, i.e.,

\mathrm{gdim}R\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{dim}\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

for all
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

Now the key observation is
{e}_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
can be written as
e1_{(k,N-k)}
conjugated by powers of determinant line bundles. More precisely, we let
g:K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\rightarrow K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
be the linear map given by multiplication with
[\det(\mathcal{V})]
. Then
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}=g^{-r}eg^{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. So to calculate
{e}_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}([\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}])
with
r\neq 0
, we first apply
g^{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
to
[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]
and then using (
1.2
) to express
[\det(\mathcal{V})]^{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]
in terms of linear combination of the basis
\{[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}]\ |\ \lambda\in P(N-k,k)\}
. Next, we apply
e1_{(k,N-k)}
to the linear combination and using the result we already know for
{e}1_{(k,N-k)}([\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}])
to simplify it. Then applying
g^{-r}1_{(k-1,N-k+1)}
to the rest terms and using (
1.2
) again to express those terms as linear combination of the basis
\{[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}]\ |\ \lambda^{\prime}\in P(N-k+1,k-1)\}
. Finally, we have determine the coefficients for each
[\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}]
to be non-zero by using the Borel-Weil-Bott Theorem. Similarly for
{f}_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
with
s\neq 0
. We refer the readers to Theorem
6.5
and
6.10
for details. It would be interesting to see the geometric and representation-theoretic meanings behind these matrix coefficients.

1.3.
Relation to t-structures

When we study the categorical action on the exceptional collections given by Beilinson and Kapranov, we also found it relates to theory of t-structures. Roughly speaking, there are two descriptions of a t-structure on the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians. One is defined by using the exceptional collection given by Kapranov, and the other is defined by using the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

Let us explain a bit about the motivation. Denote
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of a complex simple algebraic group
G
, and
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be the subvariety of nilpotent elements. In
[
Be1
]
, R. Bezrukavnikov constructed a new t-structure, which he called it the exotic t-structure, on the derived category of
G
-equivariant coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{N}
. This t-structure has two descriptions, one is defined by the perverse t-structure on the equivariant coherent sheaves, the other is defined by a quasi-exceptional set, where the conditions are a bit weaker than the definition of a exceptional collection. Then he proved that the two descriptions coincide, and thus give a elementary way to prove the Lusztig-Vogan bijection.

Since we have the exceptional collection and its dual exceptional collection given by Kapranov on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. By R. Bezrukavnikov’s work
[
Be1
]
(see Theorem
4.11
), we obtain an exotic t-structure on these categories
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
, denoted by
(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k),\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k))
, see Corollary
4.15
.

On the other hand, peoples also use categorical actions of affine braid groups and quantum affine algebras to defined t-structures. For example, Bezrukavnikov and Mirkovic
[
BM
]
defined exotic t-structures on derived category of coherent sheaves on varieties related to the Springer resolution
\pi:\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
by using a certain affine braid group actions that developed in
[
BR
]
on these categories.

Similarly, Cautis-Koppensteiner
[
CKo
]
defined exotic t-structures on derived category of coherent sheaves on convolution varieties related to Beilinson-Drinfeld and affine Grassmannians by using categorical action of quantum affine algebras. Their work also recover the t-structures in
[
BM
]
and
[
BMR
]
.

Motivated by this, it is natural to ask whether the exotic t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k),\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k))
can be described by the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
that defined by the author.

We achieve this by first showing that given an abstract categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
and a fixed t-structure on the highest weight category
\mathcal{K}(0,N)
. Then with some extra assumptions we can use the action to construct (or induce) t-structure on the other weight categories
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
, see Theorem
5.8
for details.

The main tool we use is a result by Polishchuk
[
Po
]
, see Theorem
4.4
. Basically, we have to construct functors
\varphi_{k}:\mathcal{K}(0,N)\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
such that
(\varphi_{k})^{R}\circ\varphi_{k}
is t-exact for all
k
, then we translate the t-structure from
\mathcal{K}(0,N)
to
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
via
\varphi_{k}
. The definition of the functors
\varphi_{k}
can be thought as an abstracting of the tilting bundle
\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}
of the Kapranov exceptional collection. Moreover, we can show that the functors
({\sf{F}}_{N-k-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{R}
and
({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2]
are t-exact on these t-structures.

As a corollary, by modifying the functor
\varphi_{k}
we can obtain another t-structure on
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
such that
({\sf{F}}_{N-k-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}
and
({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2]
are t-exact on these t-structures, see Corollary
5.9
.

Then we apply to the case where the weight categories are
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
, the highest weight category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N))
is the category of graded vector spaces. We equip the highest weight category with the standard t-structure, then we induce a t-structure on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
by Corollary
5.9
, denoted by
(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{act}(k,N-k),\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{act}(k,N-k))

Now we have two t-structures on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
, we prove that the two t-structures agree, i.e.
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k)=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{act}(k,N-k)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k)=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{act}(k,N-k)
, see Theorem
5.10
.

1.4.
Some further remarks

We mention some remarks about topics that we do not address in this article but would like to study them in the future.

First, since all the results in this paper are for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
case, we would like to generalize them to the
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
case in the future.

Second, in
[
Be1
]
,
[
Be2
]
, Bezrukavnikov also study the heart of the t-structure of a quasi-exceptional set. In particular, he give a description of the irreducible objects in this heart. However, in this paper, we do not study the heart of the t-structure of the Kapranov exceptional collection. So we would like to understand the irreducible objects in this t-structure, especially its relation to the categorical action of shifted
q=0
affine algebra.

Also, since the Kapranov exceptional collection is strong and full, the direct sum of the exceptional objects in this exceptional collection, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}
, is a tilting bundle on
\mathbb{G}(k,N)
. We have the following equivalence

R\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{T},):\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{D}^{b}(A^{op}-mod)

(1.3)

where
A:=\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{T})
.

We expect that under this derived equivalence, the heart of this t-structure is equivalent to the abelian category of (right) modules over the endomorphism algebra of the tilting bundle. More precisely, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Under the above equivalence (
1.3
), the restriction of the functor to the heart of the exotic t-structure gives an equivalence between abelian categories.

R\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{T},):\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k)\cap\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}_{ex}(k,N-k)\xrightarrow{\cong}A^{op}-mod

Next, recall that in
[
BM
]
, they defined the exotic t-structures by using the affine braid group action and define the notion of braid positive for those t-structures. According to
[
CK
]
, categorical
\mathfrak{g}
actions can be used to construct braid group actions, and from Theorem 4.1 in
[
CKo
]
, they show that the t-structure constructed from the categorical action of quantum affine algebra is braid positive.

For categorical action of shifted
q=0
affine algebra, the author do not know whether such an action can be used to construct braid group actions or not. However, we still expect that such an action can be used to construct an action that is similar to braid group, e.g., braid monoid, and to understand more about the t-structure of the Kapranov exceptional collection.

Finally, in
[
KP
]
, Kuznetsov-Polishchuk constructed exceptional collections for general type Grassmannians and flag varieties
G/P
, they conjecture that such exceptional collections are full and strong. Since in this paper we interpret objects in the Kapranov exceptional collection as convolution of Fourier-Mukai kernels, we also expect that the shifted
q=0
affine algebras for general type (not defined yet) can be used to partially address the conjectures by Kuznetsov-Polishchuk.

2.
Generators for the derived categories of coherent sheaves on Grassmannians

In this section, we recall the works of A. Beilinson
[
B
]
and M. Kapranov
[
Kap85
]
,
[
Kap88
]
about the structures of bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on projective space, Grassmannians. More precisely, we give the exceptional collections for their bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves.

2.1.
Schur functors and tensor products

First, we recall briefly a basic tool from representation theory which would be used in later sections.

The materials for Schur functors follows from Chapter 4 in
[
FH
]
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
be a non-increasing sequence of positive integers. We can represent
\lambda
as a Young diagram with
n
rows, aligned on the left, such that the
i
th row
has exactly
\lambda_{i}
cells. The size of
\lambda
, denoted by
|\lambda|
, is the number
|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}
. The transpose diagram
\lambda^{*}
is obtained by exchanging rows and columns of
\lambda
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
be a sequence of non-increasing positive integers. The Schur functor
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}
associated to
\lambda
is defined as a functor

\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}:\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}

such that for any vector space
V
,
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}V
coincides with the image of the Young symmetrizer
c_{\lambda}
in the space of tensors of
V
of rank
n
: i.e.,
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}V=\text{Im}(c_{\lambda}|_{V^{\otimes n}})
.

An useful application of the Schur functors comes out in the description of the spaces of
exterior power of a tensor product, which is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Kap85
]
Lemma 0.5)
.

Let
V
and
W
be two vector spaces and let
p
be a non-negative integer. Then we have a natural isomorphism of
\mathrm{GL}(V)\times\mathrm{GL}(W)
-modules

\bigwedge^{p}(V\otimes W)\cong\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=p}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}V\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{*}}W

where
\lambda
runs all Young diagram of size
p
and each summand occurs with multiplicity one.

2.2.
Exceptional collections and Fourier-Mukai transformations

In this section, we briefly recall the definition of exceptional objects/collections and Fourier-Mukai transformations. We follow the book
[
Huy
]
for the definitions and details.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear triangulated category.

Definition 2.3
.

An object
E\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
is called exceptional if

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E[l])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\ \text{if}\ l=0\\
0&\ \text{if}\ l\neq 0.\end{cases}

Then we define the notion of exceptional collections.

Definition 2.4
.

An ordered collection (sequence)
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
, where
E_{i}\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, is called an exceptional collection (sequence) if each
E_{i}
is exceptional and

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[l])=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&\ \text{if}\ l=0,\ i=j\\
0&\ \text{if}\ i>j\ \text{or if}\ l\neq 0,\ i=j.\end{cases}

The collection (sequence) is called strong exceptional if in addition

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[l])=0\ \text{for all}\ i,\ j\ \text{and}\ l\neq 0.

Remark 2.5
.

An exceptional collection (sequence)
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
is called full if the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
containing all the objects
E_{1},...,E_{n}
is
\mathcal{D}
itself.

Next, we introduce the language of Fourier-Mukai transformations. For
X
a smooth complex projective variety, we will work with the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
, which we always denote it by
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
. Throughout this article, all pullback, pushforward, Hom, and tensor product functors of sheaves will be derived functors.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
,
Y
be two smooth projective varieties. A Fourier-Mukai kernel is any object
\mathcal{P}
of the
derived category of coherent sheaves on
X\times Y
. Given
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Y)
, we may define
the associated Fourier-Mukai transform, which is the functor

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathcal{F}\mapsto\pi_{2*}(\pi_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{P})

where
\pi_{1}
,
\pi_{2}
are natural projections.

We call
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
the Fourier-Mukai transform with (Fourier-Mukai) kernel
\mathcal{P}
. For convenience, we would just write FM for Fourier-Mukai.

The first property of FM transforms is that they have left and right adjoints that are themselves FM transforms.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Huy
]
Proposition 5.9)
.

For
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
is the FM transform with kernel
\mathcal{P}
, define

\mathcal{P}_{L}=\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\pi^{*}_{2}\omega_{Y}[\mathrm{dim}Y],\ \mathcal{P}_{R}=\mathcal{P}^{\vee}\otimes\pi^{*}_{1}\omega_{X}[\mathrm{dim}X].

Then

\Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{L}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(X),\ \Phi_{\mathcal{P}_{R}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)

are the left and right adjoints of
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
, respectively.

The second property is the composition of FM transforms is also a FM transform.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Huy
]
Proposition 5.10)
.

Let
X,Y,Z
be smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. Consider objects
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Y)
and
\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y\times Z)
. They define FM transforms
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{Q}}:\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(Z)
. We would use
\ast
to denote the operation for convolution, i.e.

\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P}:=\pi_{13*}(\pi_{12}^{*}(\mathcal{P})\otimes\pi_{23}^{*}(\mathcal{Q}))

Then for
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Z)
, we have
\Phi_{\mathcal{Q}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}\cong\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Moreover by
[
Huy
]
remark 5.11, we have
(\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P})_{L}\cong(\mathcal{P})_{L}\ast(\mathcal{Q})_{L}
and
(\mathcal{Q}\ast\mathcal{P})_{R}\cong(\mathcal{P})_{R}\ast(\mathcal{Q})_{R}
.

3.1.
Shifted
q=0

Definition 3.1
.

Denote by
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
the associative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by

\begin{split}\{&1_{(k,N-k)},\ e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)},\ f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)},\ (\psi^{+})^{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)},\ (\psi^{-})^{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)},\ h_{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)}\ |\\
&\ 0\leq k\leq N,\ -k-1\leq r\leq 0,\ 0\leq s\leq N-k+1\},\end{split}

with the following relations

\begin{split}&1_{(k,N-k)}1_{(l,N-l)}=\delta_{k,l}1_{(k,N-k)},\ e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}=1_{(k-1,N-k+1)}e_{r},\ f_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}=1_{(k+1,N-k-1)}f_{r},\\
&(\psi^{+})^{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)}=1_{(k,N-k)}(\psi^{+})^{\pm 1},\ h_{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)}=1_{(k,N-k)}h_{\pm 1},\end{split}

(U01)

\{(\psi^{+})^{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)},(\psi^{-})^{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)},h_{\pm 1}1_{(k,N-k)}\ |\ 0\leq k\leq N\}\ \mathrm{pairwise\ commute},

(U02)

(\psi^{+})^{\pm 1}\cdot(\psi^{+})^{\mp 1}1_{(k,N-k)}=1_{(k,N-k)}=(\psi^{-})^{\pm 1}\cdot(\psi^{-})^{\mp 1}1_{(k,N-k)},

(U03)

e_{r}e_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}=-e_{s+1}e_{r-1}1_{(k,N-k)},

(U04)

f_{r}f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}=-f_{s-1}f_{r+1}1_{(k,N-k)},

(U05)

\psi^{\pm}e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}=-e_{r+1}\psi^{\pm}1_{(k,N-k)},

(U06)

\psi^{\pm}f_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}=-f_{r-1}\psi^{\pm}1_{(k,N-k)},

(U07)

\displaystyle[h_{\pm 1},e_{r}]1_{(k,N-k)}=0

\displaystyle[h_{\pm 1},f_{r}]1_{(k,N-k)}=0,

(U08)

[e_{r},f_{s}]1_{(k,N-k)}=\begin{cases}\psi^{+}h_{1}1_{(k,N-k)}&\text{if}\ r+s=N-k+1\\
\psi^{+}1_{(k,N-k)}&\text{if}\ r+s=N-k\\
0&\text{if}\ -k+1\leq r+s\leq N-k-1\\
-\psi^{-}1_{(k,N-k)}&\text{if}\ r+s=-k\\
-\psi^{-}h_{-1}1_{(k,N-k)}&\text{if}\ r+s=-k-1\end{cases},

(U09)

for any
r,s
such that the above relations make sense.

3.2.
The categorical action

In this section, we recall the definition of the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

Definition 3.2
.

A categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
action consists of a target 2-category
\mathcal{K}
, which is triangulated,
\mathbb{C}
-linear and idempotent complete. The objects in
\mathcal{K}
are

\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{K})=\{\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)\ |\ 0\leq k\leq N\}

where each
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
is also a triangulated category, and each Hom space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(k,N-k),\mathcal{K}(l,N-l))
is also triangulated.

The morphisms are given by

(1)

1-morphisms:

\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
,
{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}=\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}{\sf{E}}_{r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}=\boldsymbol{1}_{(k+1,N-k-1)}{\sf{F}}_{s}
,
({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm})^{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}=\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm})^{\pm 1}
,
{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}=\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}
, where
-k-1\leq r\leq 0
,
0\leq s\leq N-k+1
. Here
\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
is the identity functor of
\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)
.

Subject to the following relations.

(1)

The space of maps between any two 1-morphisms is finite dimensional.

(2)

{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
are adjoint to each other, i.e.
({\sf{H}}_{1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}{\sf{H}}_{-1}\cong({\sf{H}}_{1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{R}
.

(3)

The left and right adjoints of
\sf{E}_{r}
and
\sf{F}_{s}
are given by conjugation of
\sf{\Psi}^{\pm}
up to homological shifts. More precisely,

(a)

({\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{R}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}{({\sf{\Psi}}^{+})^{r+1}}{\sf{F}}_{i,N-k+2}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+})^{-r-2}[-r-1]

(b)

({\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{r+k-1}{\sf{F}}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-r-k}[r+k]

(c)

({\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{R}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-s+1}{\sf{E}}_{-k-2}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{s-2}[s-1]

(d)

({\sf{F}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+})^{-s+N-k-1}{\sf{E}}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+})^{s-N+k}[-s+N-k]
.

(4)

({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm})^{\pm 1}({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm})^{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}\cong({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm})^{\pm 1}({\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm})^{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
.

(5)

{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}\cong{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
.

(6)

{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}\cong{\sf{\Psi}}^{\pm}{\sf{H}}_{\pm 1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}
.

(7)

The relations between
{\sf{E}}_{r}
,
{\sf{E}}_{s}
are given by the following

{\sf{E}}_{r+1}{\sf{E}}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}\cong\begin{cases}{\sf{E}}_{s+1}{\sf{E}}_{r}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}[-1]&\text{if}\ r-s\geq 1\\
0&\text{if}\ r=s\\

(8)

The relations between
{\sf{F}}_{r}
,
{\sf{F}}_{s}
are given by the following
We have

0&\text{if}\ r=s\\

(9)

(10)

In this section, we recall the main theorem (Theorem 5.2) in
[

]

We denote
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
to be the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{G}(k,N)

\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)

To define those

We define correspondences via 3-steps partial flag variety

Next, we introduce new varieties

\begin{split}X(k):=\{&(V^{\prime\prime\prime},V,V^{\prime},V^{\prime})\in\mathbb{G}(k-1,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k+1,N)\ |\ \\
&V^{\prime\prime\prime}\subset V\subset V^{\prime},\ V^{\prime\prime\prime}\subset V^{\prime\prime}\subset V^{\prime}\}.\end{split}

On

, we have the divisor
D(k)\subset X(k)
that defined by

\begin{split}D(k):=\{&(V^{\prime\prime\prime},V,V^{\prime\prime},V^{\prime})\in\mathbb{G}(k-1,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k+1,N)\ |\ \\
&V^{\prime\prime\prime}\subset V=V^{\prime\prime}\subset V^{\prime}\}\end{split}

which is cut out by the natural section of the line bundles
\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}/\mathcal{V})

\mathcal{H}om(\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime\prime},\mathcal{V}^{\prime}/\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime})

4.
t-structures via exceptional collections

In this section, we recall the definition and some basic properties of t-structures.
We also give the tools for dealing with t-structures that would be used in the next section. Then we mention a result of R. Bezrukavnikov for how to construct a t-structure from an exceptional collections. Finally, we apply this to the Kapranov exceptional collection constructed in Section
2
.

4.1.
t-structures and related tools

Let us recall the definition of t-structures. Fix
\mathcal{D}
be a (
\mathbb{C}
-linear) triangulated category.

Definition 4.1
.

A t-structure on
\mathcal{D}
is a pair (
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
) of two full subcategories of
\mathcal{D}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\leq-1}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
, where we define
\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}[-n]
and
\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}:=\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}[-n]
.

(2)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(X,Y)=0
for all
X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0})
and
Y\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1})
.

(3)

For all
X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D})
, there is an exact triangle

Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow Z\rightarrow Y[1]
where
Y\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0})
and
Z\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 1})
.

A t-structure on
\mathcal{D}
gives rise to an abelian category.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a t-structure (
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
) on
\mathcal{D}
, the heart (core) is the intersection
\mathcal{D}^{\heartsuit}:=\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}
.

Let
\iota^{\leq n}:\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
and
\iota^{\geq n}:\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be the inclusion functors. Then
\iota^{\leq n}
has a right adjoint
\tau^{\leq n}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\leq n}
. Similarly,
\iota^{\geq n}
has a left adjoint
\tau^{\geq n}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\geq n}
. Then we have the cohomological functor

H^{n}=\tau^{\leq n}\circ\tau^{\geq n}=\tau^{\geq n}\circ\tau^{\leq n}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\heartsuit}.

We give the definitions for the notions of weakly generate and conservative.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be triangulated categories. We say that a collection of objects
\{X_{s}:s\in S\}
weakly generate
\mathcal{C}
if
\mathrm{Hom}(X_{s}[n],Y)=0
for all
s\in S
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
implies
Y=0
. A functor
\Phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is called conservative if
\Phi(X)=0
implies
X=0
.

The following result will be the main tool for us to construct t-structures in the following section.

Theorem 4.4

(
[
Po
]
, Theorem 2.1.2,)
.

Let
\Phi:\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
be a functor of cocomplete triangulated categories which commute with all small coproducts and that admits a left adjoint functor
\Phi^{L}:\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
and
\mathcal{D}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
be full subcategories such that
\Phi(\mathcal{C})\subset\mathcal{D}
,
\Phi^{L}(\mathcal{D})\subset\mathcal{C}
and
\Phi(X)\in\mathcal{D}\Rightarrow X\in\mathcal{C}
for any
X\in\text{Ob}(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}})
.

Assume that
\mathcal{D}
has a t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})
such that
\Phi\circ\Phi^{L}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is right t-exact. Then there exists a unique t-structure on
\mathcal{C}
with

\mathcal{C}^{\geq 0}=\{X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C})\ |\ \Phi(X)\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0})\}.

Moreover, the functor
\Phi
is t-exact with respect to this t-structure.

We will always assume that whenever we want to apply Theorem
4.4
to a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
between triangulated categories, we can extend it to a functor
\tilde{F}:\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{D}}
between cocomplete triangulated categories which satisfies all the conditions in the statement of Theorem
4.4
.

The reason for this is that in all the examples we consider the categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
will be bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
and
\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
for smooth varieties
X
and
Y
. We take
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
to be the corresponding cocomplete unbounded derived categories of quasi-coherent sheaves
\mathcal{D}_{qcoh}(X)
and
\mathcal{D}_{qcoh}(Y)
.

Any functor
\Phi:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
will be given by a FM kernel, and thus naturally extends to quasi-coherent sheaves. Furthermore, all such functors will have right adjoints and commute with small coproducts by the Adjoint functor theorem. Finally,
\Phi
will always be conservative and by Lemma
4.5
below we have
\Phi(X)\in\mathcal{D}
implies
X\in\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 4.5

(
[
CKo
]
, Lemma 2.3)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth varieties and
\Phi:\mathcal{D}_{qcoh}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{qcoh}(Y)
be a functor that given by a FM kernel in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X\times Y)
. If the restriction of
\Phi
to
\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
is conservative, then for any
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}_{qcoh}(X)
,
\Phi(\mathcal{F})\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(Y)
if and only if
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(X)
.

We collect some technical results that we will need about t-structures.

Lemma 4.6

(
[
Po
]
, Lemma 1.1.1)
.

Let
\Phi:\mathcal{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{2}
be a conservative t-exact functor between triangulated categories. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\leq 0}=\{X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})\ |\ \Phi(X)\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\leq 0})\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\geq 0}=\{X\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{1})\ |\ \Phi(X)\in\text{Ob}(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\geq 0})\}

Lemma 4.7

(
[
CKo
]
, Lemma 2.6)
.

Let

be a t-structure on
\mathcal{C}
and suppose

. Similarly, if

Lemma 4.8

(
[
CKo
]

Suppose we have two t-structures

on
\mathcal{C}

One of the applications for exceptional collections is to construct t-structures on triangulated categories. In order to construct t-structures on
\mathcal{D}

Definition 4.9
.

Let

.

The set

is exceptional provided that
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
is, where the order on
\{F_{1},...,F_{n}\}
is opposite to that on
\{E_{1},...,E_{n}\}
.

Theorem 4.11

For a set

5.
t-structure via categorical action

In this section, we prove the main theorem of this article. Roughly speaking, given a categorical action of the shifted
q=0
affine algebra and a fixed t-structure on the highest height category, we use this action to translate this t-structure to other weight categories.

From Theorem
3.4
, we have categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. So we can apply this main theorem to get t-structures on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. When the highest weight category is equipped with the standard t-structure, we show that the t-structure we obtain on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
is precisely the t-structure from Corollary
4.15
.

Before we move to the main theorem, we need a tool from representations theory which would relate the Kapranov exceptional collection to the categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0.N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. Moreover, this also serve as a motivation for us to construct the t-structure from categorical action.

5.1.
Borel-Weil-Bott theorem and its relative version

In this section, we recall the classical Borel-Weil-Bott theorem (for type A) and extend it to a relative version. We refer to
[
D
]
and
[
Lur
]
for more details.

Let
r\geq 2
be a positive integer. Consider the full flag variety

Fl(r)=\{0=V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset...\subset\mathbb{C}^{r}=V_{r}\ |\ \dim V_{i}=i\}.

Recall some basic stuffs for the theorem. Let
G=\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
(or
\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
) and choose a Borel subgroup
B
(invertible upper triangular matrices) with a maximal torus
T
(invertible diagonal matrices) such that
T\subset B\subset G
and
G/B=Fl(r)
. Also, let
U\subset B
be the unipotent subgroup such that
B=TU
. A character of
B
is a morphism
\lambda:B\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
, it is a one-dimensional representation of
B
. Let
B
act on
G\times\mathbb{C}
by
b(g,t)=(gb^{-1},\lambda(b)t)
for all
b\in B
and
(g,t)\in G\times\mathbb{C}
.

This action is free and we define the quotient
L_{\lambda}=G\times_{B}\mathbb{C}:=(G\times\mathbb{C})/B
. The map
L_{\lambda}\rightarrow G/B
defined by
(g,t)B\mapsto gB
is a equivariant line bundle on the full flag variety
G/B
.

Note that since
B=TU
, any such character
\lambda
is uniquely determined by its restriction to
T
. Furthermore, for
T
is the group of invertible diagonal matrices, we can see that each
\lambda:T\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
is of the form

diag(a_{1},...,a_{r})\mapsto a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}...a_{r}^{\lambda_{r}}

for some
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We denote
\Lambda(T)
the multiplicative group of characters of
T
(also called weights). The above identifies
\Lambda(T)
to the additive group
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
.

Thus, a weight
\lambda:T\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
corresponds to a
r
-tuple of integers
(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{r})
, and we have the following definition.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a weight
\lambda
is dominant (resp. regular dominant) if
\lambda_{1}\geq...\geq\lambda_{r}
(resp.
\lambda_{1}>...>\lambda_{r}
).

Definition 5.2
.

The fundamental weights are
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},...,\chi_{r-1}
such that

\chi_{i}=(1,...,1\ (i\ \text{times}),0,..,0\ (r-i\ \text{times})).

Let
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\chi_{i}=(r-1,r-2,..,1,0)
this is the half-sum of positive roots.

Denote
W
to be the Weyl group corresponds to the pair
(G,T)
, which is juts the symmetric group
S_{r}
. Then
W
acts on
\Lambda(T)
in the following way. For any element
w\in W
, we define
w\star\lambda:=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
. Also, for any
w\in W
, the length of
w
is defined to be

l(w):=\#\{(i,j)|1\leq i<j\leq r,w(i)>w(j)\}.

We let
w_{0}
to be the reverse permutation, i.e.
w_{0}(i)=r-i+1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
. It has the longest length
r(r-1)/2
, which is equal to the dimension of the full flag variety
Fl(r)
.

Then we can state the Borel-Weil-Bott theorem (for type A).

Theorem 5.3
.

(Borel-Weil-Bott)
Let
\lambda:T\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
be a weight, and
L_{\lambda}
be the associated equivariant line bundle on
G/B
. Then we have the following cases

(1) If
-\lambda
is dominant, then we have
H^{0}(G/B,L_{\lambda})
is an irreducible representation of
G
with lowest weight
\lambda
(and therefore dual to the irreducible representation of
G
with highest weight
-\lambda
), i.e.
H^{0}(G/B,L_{\lambda})\simeq(\mathbb{S}_{-\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}
. Also,
H^{i}(G/B,L_{\lambda})=0
for all
i\neq 0
.

(2)If
-\lambda
is not dominant, and there is no
w\in W
such that
w\star(-\lambda)
is dominant, then
H^{i}(G/B,L_{\lambda})=0
for all
i
.

(3)If
-\lambda
is not dominant, and there is an unique
w\in W
such that
w\star(-\lambda)
is dominant, then
H^{l(w)}(G/B,L_{\lambda})
is dual to the irreducible representation of
G
with highest weight
w\star(-\lambda)
, i.e.
H^{l(w)}(G/B,L_{\lambda})\simeq(\mathbb{S}_{w\star(-\lambda)}\mathbb{C}^{r})^{\vee}
. All other cohomologies vanish.

(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}({\sf{F}}_{\lambda}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k}[-k(N-k)+N]:\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(0,N)

(5.6)

which is conservative from the above argument.

Moreover, we have

which is also t-exact on the t-structure

from the assumption.

Thus we can apply Theorem
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
to the functor
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}
, the induced t-structure on

is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)^{{}^{\prime}\leq 0}=\{X\in\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)\ |\ (\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(X)\in\mathcal{K}(0,N)^{\leq 0}\},\\
&\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)^{{}^{\prime}\geq 0}=\{X\in\mathcal{K}(k,N-k)\ |\ (\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(X)\in\mathcal{K}(0,N)^{\geq 0}\}.\end{split}

(5.7)

Next, we check that the functors
({\sf{F}}_{N-k-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}
and
({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2]
are t-exact on those induced t-structures. Similarly as the proof of Theorem
5.8
, we have to calculate
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k-1})^{R}\circ({\sf{F}}_{N-k}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)})^{L}
and
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}\circ({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2]
.

We calculate each term of
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k-1})^{R}\circ({\sf{F}}_{N-k}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)})^{L}
, which is the following

({\sf{F}}_{\lambda}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}\circ({\sf{F}}_{N-k}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)})^{L}[-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N].

(5.8)

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},..,\lambda_{k-1})\in P(N-k+1,k-1)
,

Using the definition of categorical action, we have

\displaystyle({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}\circ({\sf{F}}_{N-k}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)})^{L}[-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N]

\displaystyle\cong({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}{\sf{E}}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+})^{-1}[1-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N]\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}

\displaystyle(\varphi^{\prime}_{k-1})^{R}({\sf{F}}_{N-k}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)})^{L}

) becomes

\displaystyle({\sf{F}}_{\lambda}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{F}}_{N-k}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(k-1,N-k+1)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k}({\sf{\Psi}}^{+})^{-k}[-k(N-k)+N]\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}

(5.9)

where

.

\displaystyle({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k}\circ({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}[-k(N-k)+N]

\displaystyle\cong({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{k-1}{\sf{F}}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-k}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}[k-k(N-k)+N]

This implies that

\displaystyle(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}\circ({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2]

\displaystyle\cong(\varphi_{k})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{-N+k}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k}({\sf{E}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)})^{L}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2][-k(N-k)+N]

5.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&({\sf{F}}_{\lambda}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{E}}{\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2][-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N]{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}\\
&\cong({\sf{E}}{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k}}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2][-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N]{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}\end{split}

(5.10)

By the proof of Theorem
5.8
, in the case where
{\sf{E}}{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k}}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)}\ncong 0
, (
5.10
) becomes

\displaystyle({\sf{E}}{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k}}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})[k-2][-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N]{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}

\displaystyle\cong(({\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}+1}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k-1}+1}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)})^{R}({\sf{\Psi}}^{-})^{-N+k-1}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{-k+1}[-(k-1)(N-k+1)+N]{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}

and

, which means that it is a direct summand of

∎

5.3.
Application to
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))

From Theorem
3.4
, we have a categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. We would like to apply Corollary
5.9
to get t-structures on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

For the highest weight
(0,N)
, we have
\mathbb{G}(0,N)=pt
. So the highest weight category is
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N))=\mathcal{D}^{b}(\text{Vect})
, which is the category of finite dimensional graded vector spaces. We equip
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N))
with the standard t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(0,N),\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N))
; i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(0,N)=\langle\{\mathbb{C}[d]\ |\ d\geq 0\}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)=\langle\{\mathbb{C}[d]\ |\ d\leq 0\}\rangle.

We would like to apply Corollary
5.9
to obtain t-structures
(\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k),\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k))
on each weight category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. So we need to know what are the functors
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
in Corollary
5.9
when the categories are
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

Since we are in the geometric setting, all the functors are given by FM kernels and composition of functors are given by convolution of kernels. Recall that the definition of
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
.

\varphi^{\prime}_{k}:=({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{N-k}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{k}\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}{\sf{F}}_{\lambda}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)}[k(N-k)-N]:\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N))\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))

By Definition
3.3
, we know what are the kernels for
{\sf{\Psi}^{\pm}}
and
{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{i}}
. An easy calculation tells us that the kernel for
({\sf{\Psi}^{-}})^{N-k}({\sf{\Psi}^{+}})^{k}
is given by

({\Psi^{-}})^{N-k}\ast({\Psi^{+}})^{k}\cong\Delta(k)_{*}\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{V})^{-N+k}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{k}[N-2k(N-k)]\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N))

Note that
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{V})^{-N+k}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{k}
is the anti-canonical bundle, denoted by
\omega^{-1}_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}
on
\mathbb{G}(k,N)
. On the other hand, by Corollary
5.7
, we know that the kernel for
{\sf{F}}_{\lambda}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)}={\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{F}}_{\lambda_{k}}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(0,N)}
is given by

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{1}}\ast...\ast\mathcal{F}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\cong\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)).

Thus the kernel for
\varphi_{k}
is given by

\omega^{-1}_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[-k(N-k)]\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)).

(5.11)

To apply Corollary
5.9
, first, we have to check that the image of
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
weakly generate
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. This is easy to see since that
\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}
is a tilting bundle on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
, and tensoring by an line bundle or homological shifts do not change the tilting property. We have
\mathbb{G}(0,N)=pt
and
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N))
is generated by the one-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{C}
. So the image of
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
is a tilting bundle, which weakly generate
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

Next, we have to check
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
is t-exact on the standard t-structure
(\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(0,N),\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N))
. We already know the kernel for
\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
is (
5.11
), by Proposition
2.7
, we have the kernel for right adjoint functor
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}
is given by

\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)]\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(0,N)).

(5.12)

Thus
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
is given by convolution of kernels

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\{\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)]\}\ast\{\omega^{-1}_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[-k(N-k)]\}\\
&\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in P(N-k,k)}\pi_{13*}((\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})\end{split}

(5.13)

where
\pi_{13}:\mathbb{G}(0,N)\times\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(0,N)\rightarrow\mathbb{G}(0,N)\times\mathbb{G}(0,N)
is the natural projection.

Since
\pi_{13}
is just the projection from
\mathbb{G}(k,N)
to a point,
\pi_{13*}((\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})
is precisely the sheaf cohomology of
(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}
; i.e.,
\pi_{13*}((\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})=H^{*}(\mathbb{G}(k,N),(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})
.

We know that

H^{*}(\mathbb{G}(k,N),(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})\cong R\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)},(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{*}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})

and from Corollary
2.16
,
\mathcal{R}_{(k,N-k)}
is a strong full exceptional collection. This implies that
\mathrm{Ext}^{*}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})
only have degree 0 part; i.e.,
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})
.

So (
5.13
) becomes
\bigoplus_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in P(N-k,k)}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V},\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})
, which is a vector space concentrate in homological degree 0. Thus
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}\varphi^{\prime}_{k}
is t-exact on the standard t-structure.

Now we can apply Corollary
5.9
to get the induced t-structure, denoted by
(\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k),\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k))
on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. It has the following description

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)

\displaystyle:=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\ |\ (\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(X)\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)

\displaystyle:=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\ |\ (\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(X)\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(0,N)\}.

The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 5.10
.

The induced t-structure on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
from the standard t-structure on
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(0,N))
under the categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
action is precisely the t-structure from Corollary
4.15
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k):=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\ |\ (\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(X)\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)=\langle\{\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d]\ |\ \lambda\in P(N-k,k),\ d\leq 0\}\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k):=\{X\in\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))\ |\ (\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(X)\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(0,N)\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)=\langle\{\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|+d]\ |\ \mu\in P(k,N-k),\ d\geq 0\}\rangle.

Proof.

To prove this theorem, it suffices to prove
\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)\subset\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)
and
\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)\subset\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)
. Then by Lemma
4.8
to get the result.

From Corollary
4.15
, we know that the t-structure
(\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k),\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k))
can be described by exceptional collections. So to prove
\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)\subset\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)
and
\mathcal{D}_{ex}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)\subset\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)
, it suffice to check on each generators; i.e.,
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d]\in\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)
for all
d\leq 0
and
\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|+d^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)
for all
d^{\prime}\geq 0
.

First, we show that
\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|+d^{\prime}]\in\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\leq 0}(k,N-k)
for all
d^{\prime}\geq 0
. To show this, it suffices to show that
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])\in\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}(0,N)
. Since
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}
is a FM transform with FM kernel given by (
5.12
), by definition we have to calculate the following

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])=\pi_{2*}(\pi^{*}_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})[-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)])\\
&\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\in P(N-k,k)}\pi_{2*}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)-|\mu|+d^{\prime}]).\end{split}

(5.14)

where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are the natural projection from
\mathbb{G}(k,N)\times\mathbb{G}(0,N)
to
\mathbb{G}(k,N)
,
\mathbb{G}(0,N)
respectively.

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{N-k})
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},..,\lambda_{k})
with
0\leq\mu_{N-k}\leq...\leq\mu_{1}\leq k
and
0\leq\lambda_{k}\leq...\leq\lambda_{1}\leq N-k
. Since
\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}=\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{V})^{-N+k}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\cong\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{V})^{N-k}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{-k}\otimes(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{S}_{(\mu_{1},...,\mu_{N-k})}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{V})^{N-k}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{-k}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{(-\lambda_{k},..,-\lambda_{1})}\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{S}_{(\mu_{1}-k,...,\mu_{N-k}-k)}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\mathbb{S}_{(-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)}\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{S}_{(-\mu_{N-k}+k,...,-\mu_{1}+k)}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{(-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)}\mathcal{V}.

Since
\pi_{2}
is projection from
\mathbb{G}(k,N)
to a point,
\pi_{2*}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])
is calculating the sheaf cohomology. More precisely,

\displaystyle\pi_{2*}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{G}(k,N),\mathbb{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])

\displaystyle\cong H^{*}(\mathbb{G}(k,N),\mathbb{S}_{(-\mu_{N-k}+k,...,-\mu_{1}+k)}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{(-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)}\mathcal{V}[k(N-k)-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])

Note that we have
0\leq-\mu_{1}+k\leq...\leq-\mu_{N-k}+k\leq k
and
0\leq-\lambda_{1}+N-k\leq...\leq-\lambda_{k}+N-k\leq N-k
, so by Lemma
4.14
, we conclude that

\displaystyle H^{i}(\mathbb{G}(k,N),\mathbb{S}_{(-\mu_{N-k}+k,...,-\mu_{1}+k)}(\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{S}_{(-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)}\mathcal{V}[k(N-k)-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0&\text{for all}\ i,\ \text{if}\ (-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)\neq(-\mu_{N-k}+k,...,-\mu_{1}+k)^{*}\\
0&\text{for all}\ i\neq-d^{\prime},\text{if}\ (-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)=(-\mu_{N-k}+k,...,-\mu_{1}+k)^{*}\\
\mathbb{C}&\text{for all}\ i=-d^{\prime},\text{if}\ (-\lambda_{k}+N-k,..,-\lambda_{1}+N-k)=(-\mu_{N-k}+k,...,-\mu_{1}+k)^{*}.\\
\end{cases}

This implies that (
5.14
) is isomorphic to

. Thus
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(\mathbb{S}_{\mu}\mathbb{C}^{N}/\mathcal{V}[-|\mu|+d^{\prime}])\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)
.

Next, we show that
\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d]\in\mathcal{D}_{act}^{\geq 0}(k,N-k)
for all

. Similarly, we have to show that
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d])\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)
. Again, by definition of the FM transform and using the same argument to simplify, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d])=\pi_{2*}(\pi^{*}_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d]\otimes\omega_{\mathbb{G}(k,N)}\otimes\bigoplus_{\lambda^{\prime}\in P(N-k,k)}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\mathcal{V})^{\vee}[k(N-k)])\\

(5.15)

where
0\leq\lambda_{k}\leq...\leq\lambda_{1}\leq N-k
and
0\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\leq...\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leq N-k

0\leq N-k-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leq...\leq N-k-\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\leq N-k

If we denote
(N-k-\lambda^{\prime}_{k},...,N-k-\lambda^{\prime}_{1})

\lambda^{\prime\prime}

\lambda^{\prime\prime}
to
\lambda

is the multiplicity called the Littlewood-Richardson coefficients.

So we have to calculate

. Note that since
0\leq\lambda_{k}\leq...\leq\lambda_{1}\leq N-k
and
0\leq N-k-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}\leq...\leq N-k-\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\leq N-k
, the possible range for

are

0\leq\nu_{k}\leq....\leq\nu_{1}\leq 2(N-k)

By Lemma
4.14

for some finite index set

\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)
. Hence we show that
(\varphi^{\prime}_{k})^{R}(\mathbb{S}_{\lambda}\mathcal{V}[d])\in\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}(0,N)
.
∎

6.
Passage to the Grothendieck groups

One of the property for full exceptional collections in a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is that, when pass to the K-theory (Grothendieck group), by definition, they give semiorthogonal basis with respect to the bilinear Euler form
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(-,-[i])
.

From Corollary
2.16
, we have a full exceptional collection
R_{(k,N-k)}
for
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N)))
. So when pass to the K-theory, we obtain a basis for
K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. On the other hand, from Theorem
3.4
, we also have a categorical action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
. Thus, it induces an action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
on
\bigoplus_{k}K(\mathbb{G}(k,N))
.

In this section, as an application, we calculate the matrix
coefficients for the generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
,
f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}_{0,N}(L\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
. In fact, the result we obtain holds for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
since the generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)}
,
f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
can be defined for all
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

This would not be done by direct calculation using the definition of the generators
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)},\ f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
, i.e. K-theoretical Fourier-Mukai transformation. Note that
e_{r}1_{(k,N-k)},\ f_{s}1_{(k,N-k)}
can be defined as

e1_{(k,N-k)}
,
f1_{(k,N-k)}
up to some conjugation of determinant line bundles (see
6.2
for details). So we need to calculate
e1_{(k,N-k)}
,
f1_{(k,N-k)}
first. This is done by calculated at the categorical level via using Theorem
5.5
and convolution of kernels, then pass to the K-theory to obtain the result.

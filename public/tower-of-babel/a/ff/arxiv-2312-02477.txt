Josephus Nim


Abstract
Here, we present a variant of Nim with two piles. In the first pile, we have stones with a weight of 1 1 , and in the second pile, we have stones with a weight of − 2 -2 . Two Players take turns to take stones from one of the piles, and the total weight of stones to be removed should be equal to or less than half of the total weight of the stones in the pile. The player who removed the last stone or stones is the winner of the game. The authors discovered that when ( n , m ) (n,m) is a previous player’s winning position, 2 ​ m + 1 2m+1 is the last remaining number of the Josephus problem, where there are n n numbers, and every second number is to be removed. There are similar relations between the position of which the Grundy number is s s and the n − s n-s -th removed number.

The classic game of Nim is played with stone piles. A player can remove any number of stones from any one pile during their turn; the player who takes the last stone is considered the winner.

There are many variants of the classical game of Nim. In Maximum Nim, we place an upper bound

There are simple relations between Grundy numbers in this game and the Josephus problem. This fact is remarkable since the games of Nim and Josephus’ problems are entirely different.
This game was proposed by S. Takahashi, who is one of the authors of the present article.

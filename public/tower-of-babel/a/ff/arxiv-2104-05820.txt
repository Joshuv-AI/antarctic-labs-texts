Abstract
. The rational Chow ring of the moduli space ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} of curves of genus g g is known for g ≤ 6 g\leq 6 . Here, we determine the rational Chow rings of ℳ 7 , ℳ 8 , \mathcal{M}_{7},\mathcal{M}_{8}, and ℳ 9 \mathcal{M}_{9} by showing they are tautological. The key ingredient is intersection theory on Hurwitz spaces of degree 4 4 and 5 5 covers of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} via their associated vector bundles. The main focus of this paper is a detailed geometric analysis of special tetragonal and pentagonal covers whose associated vector bundles on ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} are highly unbalanced, expanding upon previous work of the authors in the more balanced case. In genus 9 9 , we use work of Mukai to present the locus of hexagonal curves as a global quotient stack, and, using equivariant intersection theory, we show its Chow ring is generated by restrictions of tautological classes.

Remark 1.5

Despite their complicated looking presentations, the tautological rings above have many nice properties. Faber proved that they are Gorenstein rings with socle in degree

Several different methods of producing relations among tautological classes in arbitrary genus have found only the Faber–Zagier relations, which may suggest that the Gorenstein property only occurs in low genus cases (see

1.1.
Overview of the proof

Our basic approach is to use the stratification of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
by
gonality
, the minimal degree of a map
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Precisely, let us define

\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}:=\{[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}:C\text{ has a }g^{1}_{k}\},

which is the locus of curves of gonality less than or equal to
k
. For
g=7,8
, a general curve of genus
g
has gonality
5
, so our stratification takes the form

\mathcal{M}^{2}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}^{3}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}^{4}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}^{5}_{g}=\mathcal{M}_{g}.

In genus
9
, a general curve has gonality
6
, so we have one more stratum

\mathcal{M}^{2}_{9}\subset\mathcal{M}^{3}_{9}\subset\mathcal{M}^{4}_{9}\subset\mathcal{M}^{5}_{9}\subset\mathcal{M}_{9}^{6}=\mathcal{M}_{9}.

It suffices to show for each
k
that all classes supported on
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}
are tautological up to classes supported on
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1}
. In other words, we must show that every class in
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1})
pushes forward to a class in
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1})
that is the restriction of a tautological class on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

We shall call a class on
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1})
tautological if it is the restriction of a tautological class from
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
As
k
increases, each stratum
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1}
becomes more complicated.
Our main contribution is a better understanding of the strata for
k=4,5
, which was the main stumbling block in extending previous work on low genus curves.
We now explain our process in more detail, starting with the curves of lowest gonality and working upwards.

(1) An easy start

Faber
[
13
]
showed that the fundamental class of any Brill–Noether locus of the expected dimension is tautological. In particular the fundamental class of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}
is tautological.
It is well known that
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{2})\cong\mathbb{Q}
for all
g
. By a result of Patel-Vakil
[
26
]
,
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{2})
is generated by the restriction of
\kappa_{1}
for all
g\neq 3
.
Using Faber’s result and the push-pull formula this establishes for
g\neq 3
that

(1.1)

all classes supported on
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}
are tautological.

(See Remark
2.5
for an alternative argument when
g=3
.)
Note that (
1.1
) already establishes that
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g})=R^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
for
g\leq 4
.
More generally, using the push-pull formula and Faber’s result, if the Chow ring of each locally closed stratum
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1})
were generated by the restrictions of tautological classes for all
k
, we would be done. However, this is not the case for
k>3
.

(2) Why it must get harder

For
k=4,5
,
the Chow ring of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1}

is
not
in general generated by restrictions of tautological classes. By considering curves of bidegree
(4,4)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, one can show that when
g\geq 8
, the map
\beta:\mathcal{H}_{4,g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4}
is an isomorphism away from loci of codimension
2
in both spaces. Therefore, the Picard rank conjecture, proved by Deopurkar–Patel
[
9
]
for
k=4,5
, shows that

\dim A^{1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3})=\dim A^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{4,g})=2.

Hence, the first Chow group of the locally closed stratum
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}
cannot be generated by the restriction of
\kappa_{1}
. The analogous result holds for
k=5
when
g\geq 10
.
Furthermore, it is known that there exist classes supported on
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4}
that are not tautological in some genera:
van Zelm
[
35
]
has shown that the fundamental class of the bielliptic locus
\mathcal{B}_{12}\subset\mathcal{M}_{12}
is
not
a tautological class. This makes the tetragonal locus (Section
4
) one of the most interesting parts, and it will, of course, require some special observations about genus
7
,
8
, and
9
curves. (In Section
4.3
, we also explain how to prove
A^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g})=R^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g})
for
g=5
and
6
using our techniques.) In Section
4.7
, we discuss why our techniques cannot access the bielliptic locus when
g\geq 11
. In the case
g=10
, we prove Theorem
1.7
: the class of the bielliptic locus
\mathcal{B}_{10}\subset\mathcal{M}_{10}
is tautological.

(3) Using the Hurwitz space

Our approach is to study the Chow rings of the Hurwitz stacks
\mathcal{H}_{4,g}
and
\mathcal{H}_{5,g}
parametrizing degree
4
and
5
covers, respectively, of the projective line. Let
\beta:\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
denote the forgetful map. The induced map
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1})\to\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1}
is proper and surjective, and thus induces a surjection on rational Chow groups.
In
[
3
]
, we showed that for
k=4,5
, classes in the
tautological ring of

\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g})
(see Definition
2.2
) push forward to tautological classes on
\mathcal{M}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g}
(see Theorem
2.4
).
This is a useful (and non-trivial) tool because there are tautological classes on the Hurwitz space which are
not
pullbacks of tautological classes on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
Thus, we wish to show that the Chow rings of
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g})
are tautological for
k=4,5
and
g=7,8,9
. We succeed in proving this in each of these cases except
k=4,g=9
, where some additional special arguments are used. These arguments are carried out in Section
4
for
k=4
and Section
5
for
k=5
.

To accomplish this, we further stratify
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
.
Given a cover
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we define
E_{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})^{\vee}
and
F_{\alpha}:=\ker(\Sym^{2}E_{\alpha}\to\alpha_{*}\omega_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2})
, which are vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
See Section
2.1
for an elaboration of the properties of
E_{\alpha}
and
F_{\alpha}
.
We then stratify
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
by the pair of splitting types of
E_{\alpha}
and
F_{\alpha}
.
Each of these “pair splitting loci” has a nice description as a quotient stack (Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
).
As a starting point, our previous work
[
2
,
3
]
shows that the Chow ring of a union
\Psi
of the several largest strata is generated by tautological classes (Proposition
2.9
). This result allows us to narrow down the possible sources of non-tautological classes: they all occur on the complement of
\Psi
. Some “bad” pair splitting loci
\Sigma_{i}
remain outside
of
\Psi
and not inside
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1})
. These bad
\Sigma_{i}
are the main focus of this paper. Part of the difficulty of these strata is that they all occur in the “unexpected (pair) codimension” in the sense of Deopurkar–Patel
[
9
, Remark 4.2]
.

(4) The key coincidence and work to be done.

Using universal degeneracy formulas from
[
18
]
, we show that the fundamental class of a single splitting locus (i.e. where
one
of the two vector bundles has a given splitting type) is tautological if it occurs in the “expected codimension.”
Perhaps the most surprising part of the proof is the following coincidence (when
(k,g)\neq(4,9)
): after excising strata contained in
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g})
, every bad
\Sigma_{i}
can be realized as a single splitting locus, and that single splitting locus occurs in the expected codimension (proofs of Lemmas
4.8
,
4.9
,
5.7
,
5.9
). Hence, the fundamental class of the closure of each bad
\Sigma_{i}
is tautological in
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g}))
. That these fundamental classes are tautological is a “coincidence of small numbers.” It in fact fails for
k=4
,
g=12
by the result of van Zelm
[
35
]
.

We then study the Chow rings of the locally closed strata
\Sigma_{i}
.
Using the description of
\Sigma_{i}
as a quotient, we show that the Chow ring of each stratum
A^{*}(\Sigma_{i}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{k-1}))
is generated by restrictions of tautological classes on
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
(Sections
4.2
and
5.2
). This last step requires a geometric understanding of the equations that define
C
inside the associated scroll
\mathbb{P}E_{\alpha}^{\vee}
, and when a collection of equations of this type fail to define a smooth curve or produce a curve of gonality less than
k
. These ideas do extend to arbitrary genus, unlike the results in the previous paragraph concerning fundamental classes. We state them as broadly as possible for arbitrary genera as they may be of future use.

(5) The further work in genus
9

As the genus increases, the luck with fundamental classes starts to run out and more subtle arguments are required.
In the case
g=9,k=4
, we encounter two bad pair strata
\Sigma_{i}
that occur in unexpected codimension and cannot be realized as a single splitting locus. Although we do not compute their classes on
\mathcal{H}_{4,9}
, we still manage to show that their push forwards to
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
are tautological. For example, one of these problem strata corresponds to the locus of plane sextics with one double point (Lemma
4.14
). The class of this locus is tautological on
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
because it is a Brill–Noether locus of the expected codimension. We then show that the Chow ring of this stratum is generated by the pullback of
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, which is a stronger statement than being generated by restrictions of tautological classes on
\mathcal{H}_{4,9}
. By the push-pull formula, the push forward of every class supported on this stratum is tautological on
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
(though we remain unsure if they are tautological upstairs on
\mathcal{H}_{4,9}
). We deal with the other problem stratum by showing that its union with the bielliptic locus
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{B}_{9})
has tautological fundamental class on
\mathcal{H}_{4,9}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3})
and a trick explained in Figure
4
.

In genus
9
, we must also deal with curves of gonality
6
. The approach we take to these curves in Section
6
is quite different from the approach taken to curves of gonality
5
and below because there is no uniform description for degree
6
covers in terms of associated vector bundles.
Instead, using results of Mukai
[
22
]
, we realize
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{5}
(up to a
\mu_{2}
gerbe) as a global quotient of an open subvariety of a Grassmannian by
\SP_{6}
. The tautological subbundle on the resulting Grassmann bundle over
\BSp_{6}
is the Hodge bundle (up to possibly twisting by a line bundle, see Lemma
6.9
). It then remains to see that Chern classes of the rank
6
vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
associated to this quotient are tautological.
We see this by proving that the rank
21
vector bundle
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{V}
is the bundle of
21
quadrics that cut out a canonical genus
9
curve (Lemma
6.10
). From this, it follows that the Chern classes of
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{V}
are tautological, and, using the splitting principle, the Chern classes of
\mathcal{V}
are seen to be tautological as well.

1.2.
Notations and conventions.

All schemes in this paper are taken over a fixed algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
or
p>5
. All Chow rings are taken with rational coefficients. We use the subspace convention for projective bundles and Grassmann bundles.

2.
Hurwitz Schemes and the Tautological Ring

In order to study the loci
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{5}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4}
, we will study the Hurwitz stacks
\mathcal{H}_{4,g}
and
\mathcal{H}_{5,g}
parametrizing degree
4
and
5
covers, respectively, of the projective line.

Definition 2.1
.

The unparametrized Hurwitz stack
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
is the stack whose objects over a scheme
S
are of the form
(C\rightarrow P\rightarrow S)
where
P\rightarrow S
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration,
C\rightarrow P
is a finite flat finitely presented morphism of constant degree
k
, and the composition
C\rightarrow S
is a family of smooth genus
g
curves.

The Hurwitz stack
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
admits a universal diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}_{k,g}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

The universal diagram furnishes several natural classes in the Chow ring of
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The tautological ring
R^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})
is the subring of
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})
generated by classes of the form

f_{*}(c_{1}(\omega_{f})^{i}\cdot\alpha^{*}c_{1}(\omega_{\pi})^{j}).

If
U\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
is an open substack of
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
, we define the tautological ring of the open
R^{*}(U)
to be the image of the tautological ring under the restriction map

A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})\rightarrow A^{*}(U).

Remark 2.3

(A note on the
\SL_{2}
quotient)
.

The Hurwitz stack
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
is the
\PGL_{2}
quotient of the parametrized Hurwitz scheme
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}
.
One can also take the quotient of
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}
by
\SL_{2}
. The map
[\mathcal{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}/\SL_{2}]\to[\mathcal{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}/\PGL_{2}]=\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
is
a
\mu_{2}
-banded gerbe. It is a general fact that,
with rational coefficients
, the pullback map along any gerbe banded by a finite group induces an isomorphism on Chow rings
[
27
, Section 2.3]
.
In particular,
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})\cong A^{*}([\mathcal{H}_{k,g}^{\dagger}/\SL_{2}])
.
The benefit of the
\SL_{2}
quotient is that the pullback of the universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration to the
\SL_{2}
quotient is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle, i.e. it is equipped with a line bundle of relative degree
1
.
Since we work with rational coefficients throughout, we do not distinguish the
\PGL_{2}
and
\SL_{2}
quotients and freely assume that
\mathcal{P}
is equipped with a line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1)
of relative degree
1
. The push forward
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1)
is the pullback of the universal rank
2
vector bundle on
\BSL_{2}
.

By forgetting the map
C\rightarrow P
, we obtain a morphism

\beta:\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}.

Let

\beta^{\prime}:\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g}

be the restriction of
\beta
to
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{k-1}_{g})
.
In
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
, we showed the following result relating the tautological rings of the relevant Hurwitz stacks and
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
k=4,5
. The map
\beta^{\prime}
is proper, so the induced push forward map

2.1.
The Casnati–Ekedahl structure theorem

Here, we recall the Casnati–Ekedahl structure theorems for finite Gorenstein covers. The structure theorems furnish distinguished tautological classes, which we call the Casnati–Ekedahl classes, abbreviated CE classes.

We begin with the most general statement, which holds for covers of every degree. Given a degree
k
cover
\alpha:X\rightarrow Y
where
Y
is integral, one obtains an exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow E_{\alpha}^{\vee}\rightarrow 0,

where
E_{\alpha}
is a vector bundle of rank
k-1
.
When
\alpha
is Gorenstein,
\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong(\alpha_{*}\omega_{\alpha})^{\vee}
. Pulling back and using adjunction, we therefore obtain a map

\omega_{\alpha}^{\vee}\to(\alpha^{*}\alpha_{*}\omega_{\alpha})^{\vee}\rightarrow\alpha^{*}E_{\alpha}^{\vee},

which induces a map
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}E_{\alpha}^{\vee}
that factors
\alpha:X\rightarrow Y
.
The Casnati–Ekedahl structure theorem gives a resolution of the ideal sheaf of
X
inside of
\mathbb{P}E_{\alpha}^{\vee}

[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(Casnati–Ekedahl
[
4
]
)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be schemes,
Y
integral and let
\alpha:X\rightarrow Y
be a Gorenstein cover of degree
k\geq 3
. There exists a unique
\mathbb{P}^{k-2}
-bundle
\gamma:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow Y
and an embedding
i:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}
such that
\alpha=\gamma\circ i
and
X_{y}:=\alpha^{-1}(y)\subset\gamma^{-1}(y)\cong\mathbb{P}^{k-2}
is a nondegenerate arithmetically Gorenstein subscheme for each
y\in Y
. Moreover, the following properties hold.

(1)

\mathbb{P}\cong\mathbb{P}E_{\alpha}^{\vee}
where
E_{\alpha}^{\vee}:=\coker(\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
, and
i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\cong\omega_{\alpha}
.

(2)

There is a unique up to unique isomorphism exact sequence of locally free
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}
sheaves

(2.2)

0\rightarrow\gamma^{*}F_{k-2}(-k)\rightarrow\gamma^{*}F_{k-3}(-k+2)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\gamma^{*}F_{1}(-2)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow 0.

where
F_{i}
is locally free on
Y
.
The restriction of the exact sequence above to a fiber gives a minimal free resolution of
X_{y}:=\alpha^{-1}(y)
. Moreover the resolution is self-dual, so there is a canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}}(F_{i},F_{k-2})\cong F_{k-2-i}
.

(3)

The ranks of the
F_{i}
are

\rank F_{i}=\frac{i(k-2-i)}{k-1}{{k}\choose{i+1}}.

(4)

There is a canonical isomorphism
F_{k-2}\cong\det E_{\alpha}
.

In the cases
k=4,5
, self-duality of the resolution determines all of the bundles
F_{i}
in terms of
E_{\alpha}
and
F_{\alpha}:=F_{1}
and tensor products and determinants thereof.
Twisting (
2.2
) by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(2)
and pushing forward, we see that
F_{\alpha}=\ker(\Sym^{2}E_{\alpha}\to\alpha_{*}\omega_{\alpha}^{\otimes 2})
. We shall use this notation throughout.

Applying this to the universal cover

\alpha:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{P}
over
\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
, we obtain vector bundles
\mathcal{E}:=E_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{F}:=F_{\alpha}
on
\mathcal{P}
.
The bundle
\mathcal{E}
is sometimes called the “universal Tschirnhausen bundle” and has degree
g+k-1
on the fibers of
\pi:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{H}_{k,g}
(see e.g.
[
2
, Example 3.1]
).
Next, let
z:=-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(\omega_{\pi})=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1))
.
For
i=1,\ldots,k-1
, we define classes
a_{i}\in A^{i}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})
and
a_{i}^{\prime}\in A^{i-1}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})
by the formula

(2.3)

a_{i}:=\pi_{*}(z\cdot c_{i}(\mathcal{E})),\quad a_{i}^{\prime}:=\pi_{*}(c_{i}(\mathcal{E}))\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad c_{i}(\mathcal{E})=\pi^{*}a_{i}+\pi^{*}a_{i}^{\prime}z.

Similarly, we define

(2.4)

b_{i}:=\pi_{*}(z\cdot c_{i}(\mathcal{F})),\quad b_{i}^{\prime}:=\pi_{*}(c_{i}(\mathcal{F}))\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad c_{i}(\mathcal{F})=\pi^{*}b_{i}+\pi^{*}b_{i}^{\prime}z.

Finally, we set
c_{2}:=c_{2}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1))\in A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{k,g})
, so
z^{2}=-\pi^{*}c_{2}\in A^{2}(\mathcal{P})
.

Definition 2.7
.

We define
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime},c_{2}
to be the
Casnati–Ekedahl (CE) classes
.

Note that the CE classes generate all
\pi_{*}

and the Chern classes of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
.
In
[

, we proved that the CE classes (together with some suitable generalizations when

Lemma 2.8
.

The Casnati–Ekedahl classes lie in the tautological ring

. Conversely, when

Furthermore, we proved in

when

Let

3.
Splitting Loci

Every vector bundle
E
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
splits as a direct sum of line bundles,
E\cong\mathcal{O}(e_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{r})
. We call the tuple of integers
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
with
e_{1}\leq\cdots\leq e_{r}
the
splitting type
of
E
and abbreviate the corresponding sum of line bundles by
\mathcal{O}(\vec{e}):=\mathcal{O}(e_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{r})
.

If
E
is a vector bundle on a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle
\pi:P\to B
, then the base
B
is stratified by locally closed subvarieties called
splitting loci

\Sigma_{\vec{e}}(E):=\{b\in B:E|_{\pi^{-1}(b)}\cong\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})\}.

The above equation describes splitting loci set-theoretically. Below, we give a moduli-theoretic interpretation.
Though not necessary here, equations giving a subscheme structure to
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}(E)\subset B
in terms of Fitting supports can be found in
[
18
, Section 2]
.

Suppose
W
is a rank
2
vector bundle with trivial determinant.
We say that a vector bundle
E
on
\pi:\mathbb{P}W\to B
is a
family of vector bundles of splitting type
\vec{e}
if
B
admits a cover
U_{i}
so that:

•

there exist isomorphisms
\psi_{i}:W|_{U_{i}}\cong\mathbb{A}^{2}\times U_{i}
, linear in
\mathbb{A}^{2}
(and therefore
\pi^{-1}(U_{i})\cong U_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
).

•

there exist isomorphisms
\phi_{i}:E|_{\pi^{-1}(U_{i})}\cong q_{i}^{*}\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})
, where
q_{i}:\pi^{-1}(U_{i})\cong U_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the composition of the isomorphism above with the second projection.

This gives rise to gluing data on the overlaps which satisfy a cocycle condition on the triple overlaps.
The data of the vector bundle
W
is equivalent to the data of a principal
\SL_{2}
bundle. A family of vector bundles of splitting type
\vec{e}
, is equivalent to the data of:

•

transition functions for
W
over
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, i.e. maps
\psi_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to\SL_{2}
satisfying the cocycle condition
\psi_{ik}=\psi_{ij}\circ\psi_{jk}
on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\cap U_{k}

•

transition functions for
E
over
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, i.e. maps
\phi_{ij}:U_{i}\cap U_{j}\to\Aut(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e}))
such that when restricted to the triple overlap
U_{i}\cap U_{j}\cap U_{k}
we have

\phi_{ik}=\phi_{ij}\circ(\psi_{ij}\cdot\phi_{jk})
where
\psi_{ij}
acts on
\phi_{jk}
by change of coordinates (made precise below).

The action of
\SL_{2}
on
\Aut(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e}))
that arises above can be described concretely as follows.
We have

\Aut(\vec{e}):=\Aut(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e}))\subset H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e}))=\bigoplus_{i,j}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{j}-e_{i})).

We let
\SL_{2}
act on a factor
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(e_{j}-e_{i}))
via the
(e_{j}-e_{i})
th symmetric power of the standard representation (if
e_{j}-e_{i}<0
then this cohomology group is
0
).
The cocycle conditions above are described by multiplication in the semidirect product
\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e})
.

By this discussion, a family of vector bundles of splitting type
\vec{e}
over
B
determines a principal
\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e})
bundle on
B
and vice versa. In other words, the universal
\vec{e}
splitting locus is the classifying stack
B(\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e}))
.
Let us write
\pi:\mathcal{P}\to B(\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e}))
for the universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle (which is pulled back from
\BSL_{2}
),
and let
\mathcal{V}(\vec{e})
denote the universal vector bundle of splitting type
\vec{e}
on
\mathcal{P}
.

Suppose that
\vec{e}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})
consists of distinct degrees
d_{1}<\cdots<d_{s}
and that
d_{i}
occurs with multiplicity
n_{i}
.
Then, we have

\Aut(\vec{e})=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\GL_{n_{i}}\ltimes\prod_{i<j}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(d_{j}-d_{i}))^{\oplus(n_{i}n_{j})}.

Elements of
\Aut(\vec{e})
can be represented by block upper triangular matrices where the off diagonal entries are polynomials of the specified degrees on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The
\SL_{2}
action is trivial on the block diagonal matrices (the product of
\GL_{n_{i}}
subgroup). It follows that

(3.1)

\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e})\cong\left(\SL_{2}\times\prod_{i=1}^{s}\GL_{n_{i}}\right)\ltimes\prod_{i<j}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(d_{j}-d_{i}))^{\oplus n_{i}n_{j}}.

Hence, we have a map
\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e})\to\prod_{i=1}^{s}\GL_{n_{i}}
.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{i}
on
B(\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e}))
be the pullback of the tautological rank
n_{i}
vector bundle from the
\BGL_{n_{i}}

The Harder-Narasimhan filtration on the restriction of
\mathcal{V}(\vec{e})
to each fiber of
\mathcal{P}\to B
induces a filtration of
\mathcal{V}(\vec{e})
where the successive quotients are
(\pi^{*}\mathcal{N}_{i})(d_{i})
. We call this the

of
\mathcal{V}(\vec{e})
and we call the bundles
\mathcal{N}_{i}
on
B(\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e}))
the

\mathcal{V}(\vec{e})
.

Meanwhile, we also have an inclusion
\SL_{2}\times\prod_{i=1}^{s}\GL_{n_{i}}\to\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e})
. This induces a map
\varphi:\BSL_{2}\times\prod_{i=1}^{s}\BGL_{n_{i}}\to B(\SL_{2}\ltimes\Aut(\vec{e}))
, which by (

) is an affine bundle.
The pullback

is again the tautological rank
n_{i}
bundle coming from the

We have the fiber diagram

Each splitting locus of the form

\Sigma^{\dagger}\cong[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f})]

3.2.

\mathcal{H}_{5,g}

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
be the universal CE bundles on

Let
\vec{e}

\vec{f}
be a splitting type of rank

and degree

Similar to the previous subsection, we describe each splitting locus of the form

as a quotient stack. Again this description is well-known, though in varying language (see for example
[
9
, p. 24]
,
[
5
, Theorem 3.8]

In degree

\displaystyle\Phi:H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})\otimes\mathcal{O}(-g-4)\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathcal{O}(\vec{f}))

\displaystyle\qquad\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})^{\vee}}(1)\otimes\gamma^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-g-4)\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathcal{O}(\vec{f})))

Sections of the right-hand side are represented by

\mathbb{P}\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})^{\vee}

Given

can be viewed as a linear map

is the intersection of

with

. The Grassmann bundle

has degree
5
and codimension
3
in each fiber over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, so one expects this intersection to be a degree
5

\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
U\subset H
be the open subvariety of sections
\eta
such that

is a smooth, irreducible degree
5
cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Considering its Hilbert polynomial, one can show that such a cover will have genus
g
. It turns out — essentially from the Casnati–Ekedahl structure theorem and further work of Casnati
[
5
]
— that all degree
5
, genus
g
smooth covers
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with

and

Precisely, there is a natural action of
\SL_{2}\ltimes(\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f}))
on

. Since

\rho:\mathcal{H}_{5,g}^{\dagger}\to\mathcal{H}_{5,g}
be the parametrized Hurwitz space and set

so

.

We have

\Sigma^{\dagger}\cong[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f})]
. Therefore,

\Sigma=[\Sigma^{\dagger}/\SL_{2}]\cong[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/\SL_{2}\ltimes(\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f}))].

The proof is very similar to Lemma
3.10
. There is a map

[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f})]\to\Sigma^{\dagger}

that comes from sending a section

to the associated cover

.
The categories

and

are readily seen to be subcategories of

and

respectively, defined in

.
∎

It follows from Lemma

that the
\vec{e},\vec{f}
splitting locus is irreducible of codimension

(3.4)

h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})))+h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{O}(\vec{f})))-h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})\otimes\mathcal{O}(-g-4)\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathcal{O}(\vec{f}))

inside
\mathcal{H}_{5,g}
. In light of Proposition
2.9
, our primary interest will be in strata where the last term

.

4.
The Tetragonal Locus

In this section, we study the stratification of
\mathcal{H}_{4,g}
by the pair splitting loci of the CE bundles
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
.
Given a degree
4
, genus
g
cover
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we let
E=E_{\alpha}
and
F=F_{\alpha}
be the associated vector bundles as in Section
2.1
.
Since they are vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the bundles
E
and
F
split.

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(e_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{3})

\displaystyle e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq e_{3}

and

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(f_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{2})

\displaystyle f_{1}\leq f_{2}.

In this section, we use the roman font,
E
and
F
, to denote vector bundles of a fixed splitting type. By slight abuse of notation, we sometimes write
E=\vec{e}
to mean
E\cong\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})
.

When
C
is not hyperelliptic, the splitting type of
E
can be interpreted geometrically as follows: under the canonical embedding, the fibers of
\alpha
span a
2
-plane. The union of these two planes is called the
associated
3
-fold scroll.

The embedding
C\subset\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
given by the Casnati–Ekedahl theorem is constructed so that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}}(1)|_{C}=\omega_{\alpha}=\omega_{C}\otimes\alpha^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\vee}
.
Let
\gamma:\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the structure map.
Then, the associated scroll is the image of
\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}\to\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
via the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}}(1)\otimes\gamma^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
on
\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
.

Meanwhile, the bundle
F
parametrizes the pencil of relative quadrics that define
C\subset\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
. If
X,Y,Z
are relative coordinates on
\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
corresponding to a splitting, then the pencil is generated by

(4.1)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=p_{1,1}X^{2}+p_{1,2}XY+p_{2,2}Y^{2}+p_{1,3}XZ+p_{2,3}YZ+p_{3,3}Z^{2}

(4.2)

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=q_{1,1}X^{2}+q_{1,2}XY+q_{2,2}Y^{2}+q_{1,3}XZ+q_{2,3}YZ+q_{3,3},Z^{2},

where
p_{i,j}
and
q_{i,j}
are polynomials on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degrees

\deg(p_{i,j})=e_{i}+e_{j}-f_{1}\qquad\text{and}\deg(q_{i,j})=e_{i}+e_{j}-f_{2}.

For a stratum to be non-empty,
\vec{e}
and
\vec{f}
must satisfy certain constraints, which we collect below.
Considering the defining sequence (
2.1
) of
E_{\alpha}
, we see that
\deg(E)=-\deg(\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C})=-\chi(\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C})+4=g+3
. By
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
, one must have
\det E\cong\det F
, so

(4.3)

e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}=f_{1}+f_{2}=g+3.

For a cover to be irreducible, we must have
1=h^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},E^{\vee})+1
. This implies
e_{1}\geq 1
. An upper bound on the largest part was given in
[
9
, Proposition 2.6]
:

(4.4)

e_{1}\geq 1\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad e_{3}\leq\frac{g+3}{2}.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
31
, p. 127]
) that

(4.5)

e_{1}=1
if and only if
C
is hyperelliptic,

in which case
\alpha
factors as
C\xrightarrow{h}\mathbb{P}^{1}\xrightarrow{i}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
h:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the hyperelliptic map and
i:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a degree
2
cover.

We now turn to the geometry of the quadrics that cut out
C
.
If
p_{1,1}=0
and
q_{1,1}=0
, then
V(p,q)
contains the section
Y=Z=0
. Thus,

(4.6)

\text{$p_{1,1}$ and $q_{1,1}$ cannot both be $0$}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad 2e_{1}\geq f_{1}.

If
q_{1,1}=q_{1,2}=q_{2,2}=0
, then the quadric
q
is divisible by
Z
. That is,
V(q)
is reducible, so
C
, being irreducible, must lie in one component. Then fibers of
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
would then each span a line under the canonical embedding, giving
C
a
g^{2}_{4}
, which is impossible when
g>3
.

(4.7)

\text{$q_{1,1},q_{1,2}$ and $q_{2,2}$ cannot all be $0$}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad 2e_{2}\geq f_{2}.

On the other hand, if
q_{1,1}=q_{1,2}=q_{1,3}=0
, then
V(q)
is singular all along the section
Y=Z=0
. Therefore, in order for
C
to be smooth, no other quadric in the pencil can vanish at any point along the section
Y=Z=0
:

(4.8)

if
q_{1,1}=q_{1,2}=q_{1,3}=0
, then
p_{1,1}
must be non-vanishing on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

In terms of splitting types this implies,

(4.9)

if
f_{2}>e_{1}+e_{3}
, then
f_{1}=2e_{1}
.

Let us write
\Psi:=\mathcal{H}_{4,g}\smallsetminus\Supp R^{1}\pi_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\otimes\Sym^{2}\mathcal{E})
.
By Proposition
2.9
, we know that
A^{*}(\Psi)
is generated by tautological classes.
The complement of
\Psi
is the union of splitting loci which satisfy
2e_{1}-f_{2}\leq-2
. We will therefore need some results concerning the Chow rings of locally closed strata
\Sigma_{\vec{e}}(\mathcal{E})\cap\Sigma_{\vec{f}}(\mathcal{F})
for such
\vec{e},\vec{f}
, which we prove in Section
4.2
.
In Sections
4.4
,
4.5
,
4.6
, we specialize to the cases
g=7,8,9
respectively.

4.1.
Strategy

Our basic strategy will be as follows:

(1)

Use
conditions (
4.3
) – (
4.9
)
to determine the allowed pairs of splitting types
\vec{e},\vec{f}
. The partial order on splitting types of Section
3
induces a partial order on pairs of splitting types by
({\vec{e}}\penalty\hskip 1.66672pt^{\prime},\vec{f}^{\prime})\leq(\vec{e},\vec{f})
if
{\vec{e}}\penalty\hskip 1.66672pt^{\prime}\leq\vec{e}
and
\vec{f}^{\prime}\leq\vec{f}
.

(2)

Starting with strata at the bottom of our
\leq
order and working upwards, show that for each stratum outside of
\Psi
, at least one of the following is satisfied:

(a)

the stratum is contained in
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3})

(b)

its fundamental class in
\mathcal{H}_{4,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3})
is tautological (modulo classes supported on strata below it in the partial order)
and
the Chow ring of the locally closed stratum is generated by the restrictions of CE classes.

(c)

the push forward of its fundamental class to
\mathcal{M}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}
is tautological
and
the Chow ring of the locally closed stratum is generated by the restrictions of
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
.

Case (c) will only be needed in genus
9
; thus, in genus
7
and
8
, we will actually establish that
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{4,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}))
is generated by CE classes.

Remark 4.1
.

We note the “trade-off” between choices (b) and (c) above. If a class is tautological on
\mathcal{H}_{4,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3})
, then its push forward to
\mathcal{M}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g}^{3}
is tautological by Theorem
2.4
. On the other hand,
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
are CE classes, but need not generate all CE classes. Therefore, in (b) if we prove the stronger statement about the fundamental class, we only need the weaker statement about the Chow ring; in (c) if we only prove the weaker condition about the fundamental class, we need the stronger statement about the Chow ring.

4.2.
Chow rings of very unbalanced splitting strata

In Lemma
3.10
, each
\vec{e},\vec{f}
splitting locus
\Sigma=\Sigma_{\vec{e}}(\mathcal{E})\cap\Sigma_{\vec{f}}(\mathcal{F})
was described as a quotient of the form
[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/G]
, where
G:=\SL_{2}\ltimes(\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f}))
.
The quotient
[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/G]
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}

[U/G]
, and

is an open subvariety of affine space. Hence, there is a series of surjections

(4.10)

A^{*}(BG)\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}([U/G])\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}(\Sigma),

We gave generators for
A^{*}(BG)
in Lemma
3.3
. To show that
A^{*}(\Sigma)
is generated by CE classes, it will suffice to show that the images of these generators under (
4.10
) can be written in terms of CE classes. Similarly, to show the stronger statement that
A^{*}(\Sigma)
is generated by
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
, we must show that the images of the generators of
A^{*}(BG)
under (
4.10
) are all expressible in terms of
\kappa_{1}
and

\dim\mathrm{PS}\leq h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(6))-1-\dim\GL_{3}=28-1-9=18.

Lemma 4.15
.

The push forward
\beta_{*}[\overline{\Sigma}_{6}]
is tautological on
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
. Hence, the push forward of any class supported on
\Sigma_{8}\cup\Sigma_{7}\cup\Sigma_{6}
is tautological on
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
.

Proof.

If
\Sigma_{6}\to\mathcal{M}_{9}
has positive dimensional fibers, then
\beta_{*}[\overline{\Sigma}_{6}]=0
, which is tautological. So let us assume
\Sigma_{6}\to\mathcal{M}_{9}
is generically finite onto its image, in which case
\beta_{*}[\overline{\Sigma}_{6}]
is a multiple of
[\beta(\overline{\Sigma}_{6})]
.
By
4.14
, we have
\beta(\overline{\Sigma}_{6})\subset\overline{\mathrm{PS}}
(where the closure of
\mathrm{PS}
is taken in
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
).
It follows that
\dim\mathrm{PS}\geq\dim\Sigma_{6}=18
. By (

), we conclude that
\dim\mathrm{PS}=18
and such curves possess finitely many
g^{2}_{6}
’s.
Now, both
\beta(\overline{\Sigma}_{6})
and
\overline{\mathrm{PS}}
are irreducible of dimension
18
, so they must be equal. Therefore, we wish to show that
[\overline{\mathrm{PS}}]
is tautological on
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}
.
We know by Lemma
4.12
that all classes supported on
\mathcal{B}_{9}
are tautological, so it suffices to work on the further open
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus(\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}\cup\mathcal{B}_{9})
.

Let

be the Brill–Noether number. In particular,
\rho(9,2,6)=-6
, so the plane sextics are “expected” to occur in codimension

on
\mathcal{M}_{9}
.
On the open
\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus(\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}\cup\mathcal{B}_{9})

is
\mathrm{PS}
, which has dimension

, so it is a Brill–Noether locus of the expected dimension. (Notice we need to work on the complement of
\mathcal{M}_{9}^{3}\cup\mathcal{B}_{9}

Moreover, each curve in
\mathrm{PS}
possesses finitely many

\mathcal{B}_{9}

We can therefore apply Faber’s argument as in
[

on the open

.

is tautological in

By Lemma
4.3
(

)(ii), we know that

is generated by the pullback of

and
\kappa_{2}

∎

We now complete the goal of this subsection.

All classes supported on
\mathcal{M}_{9}^{4}

Proof.

By Proposition

Combining this with Theorem

4.7.
The end of our luck: bielliptics in genus

We point out here one last coincidence in genus

By

\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{B}_{g})=\begin{cases}\Sigma_{(2,h,h+1)}(\mathcal{E})\cap\Sigma_{(4,g-1)}(\mathcal{F})&\text{if $g$ even}\\

Using similar techniques as in genus
7,8,9
we establish that the fundamental class of the bielliptic locus

is tautological.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Using (
4.3
) – (

) in genus 10, one sees that

occurs only with

(

5.
The Pentagonal Locus

In this section, we show that
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{4}_{g}))
is generated by tautological classes for
g=7,8,9
. Given a degree
5
, genus
g
cover
\alpha:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, let
E=E_{\alpha}
and
F=F_{\alpha}
be the associated vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as in Section
2.1
.
Let
\gamma:\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
The line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}}(1)\otimes\gamma^{*}\omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
defines a map
\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}\to\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
such that the composition
C\subset\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}\to\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
is the canonical embedding.
The bundles
E
and
F
split

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(e_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}(e_{4})

\displaystyle e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq e_{3}\leq e_{4}

and

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(f_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{4})\oplus\mathcal{O}(f_{5})

\displaystyle f_{1}\leq f_{2}\leq f_{3}\leq f_{4}\leq f_{5}.

As in the degree
4
case, the splitting types of
E
and
F
give a stratification of
\mathcal{H}_{5,g}
.
This stratification was studied by Schreyer
[
31
]
when
g=7,8
, and Sagraloff
[
30
]
when
g=9
. (The translation between our notation and Schreyer’s is that
a_{i}=f_{i}-4
; the splitting type of
E
determines the type of Schreyer’s determinantal surface
Y
.)

The condition to be inside
\Psi=\mathcal{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\Supp(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}\otimes\det\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathcal{F})
is that

(5.1)

e_{1}+f_{1}+f_{2}-(g+4)\geq-1\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad e_{1}+f_{1}+f_{2}\geq\begin{cases}10&\text{if $g=7$}\\
11&\text{if $g=8$.}\\
12&\text{if $g=9$.}\end{cases}

Just as in the degree
4
case, there are several constraints on the splitting types. We collect some of these constraints below. Using these constraints, we recover the stratifications found by Schreyer
[
31
]
in genus
7
and
8
and Sagraloff
[
30
]
in genus
9
.

To begin, we know that
\deg(E)=g+4
and
\det E^{\otimes 2}\cong\det F
, so we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}=g+4,

(5.3)

\displaystyle f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}+f_{4}+f_{5}=2g+8.

By
[
9
, Proposition 2.6]
, we have

(5.4)

\frac{g+4}{10}\leq e_{1}\leq\frac{g+4}{4}\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{4}\leq\frac{2g+8}{5}.

Because
F
is a subbundle of
\Sym^{2}E
,

(5.5)

f_{5}\leq 2e_{4}.

Note that equations (
5.2
)–(
5.5
) always reduce us to a finite list of allowed splitting types.

Next, we introduce some notation. Every section in
H:=H^{0}(E\otimes\det E^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}F)
can be represented by a skew symmetric matrix

(5.6)

M=\left(\begin{matrix}0&L_{1,2}&L_{1,3}&L_{1,4}&L_{1,5}\\
-L_{1,2}&0&L_{2,3}&L_{2,4}&L_{2,5}\\
-L_{1,3}&-L_{2,3}&0&L_{3,4}&L_{3,5}\\
-L_{1,4}&-L_{2,4}&-L_{3,4}&0&L_{4,5}\\
-L_{1,5}&-L_{2,5}&-L_{3,5}&-L_{4,5}&0\end{matrix}\right),

where
L_{i,j}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(f_{i}+f_{j})\otimes\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})\otimes\mathcal{O}(-g-4))
.
The equations defining
D(\Phi(\eta))\subset\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
are the
5
Pfaffian quadrics listed below:

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=L_{2,5}L_{3,4}-L_{2,4}L_{3,5}+L_{2,3}L_{4,5}

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=L_{1,5}L_{3,4}-L_{1,4}L_{3,5}+L_{1,3}L_{4,5}

\displaystyle Q_{3}

\displaystyle=L_{1,5}L_{2,4}-L_{1,4}L_{2,5}+L_{1,2}L_{4,5}

\displaystyle Q_{4}

\displaystyle=L_{1,5}L_{2,3}-L_{1,3}L_{2,5}+L_{1,2}L_{3,5}

\displaystyle Q_{5}

\displaystyle=L_{1,4}L_{2,3}-L_{1,3}L_{2,4}+L_{1,2}L_{3,4}.

Corresponding to the splitting of
E=\mathcal{O}(\vec{e})
, we can take coordinates
X_{1},\dots,X_{4}
on
\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
. The
L_{i,j}
are linear homogeneous polynomials in the
X_{k}
whose coefficients are elements of
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(f_{i}+f_{j}+e_{k}-g-4))
. We write these as:

\displaystyle L_{i,j}

\displaystyle=a_{i,j}X_{1}+b_{i,j}X_{2}+c_{i,j}X_{3}+d_{i,j}X_{4}.

If
L_{1,2}
and
L_{1,3}
were identically zero, then
Q_{5}
would be reducible, which is impossible because
C
is irreducible. Therefore, we must have

(5.7)

f_{1}+f_{3}+e_{4}\geq g+4

If
X_{4}
divides
L_{1,2},L_{1,3},
and
L_{1,4}
, then
Q_{5}
is reducible. This will occur if
a_{1,4},b_{1,4},c_{1,4}
all identically vanish. In order for
a_{1,4},b_{1,4},
and
c_{1,4}
to not all identically vanish, we must have

(5.8)

f_{1}+f_{4}+e_{3}\geq g+4.

Similarly, if
X_{4}
divides
L_{1,2}
,
L_{1,3}
and
L_{2,3}
, then
X_{4}
divides
Q_{5}
and
Q_{5}
is reducible. To prevent this, we must have

(5.9)

f_{2}+f_{3}+e_{3}\geq g+4.

Note that the curve
C
cannot contain the section defined by
X_{2}=X_{3}=X_{4}=0
. Otherwise, it would be reducible. Therefore, at least one of the
Q_{i}
must have a nonzero coefficient of
X_{1}^{2}
. If the coefficient of
X_{1}
is zero in
L_{1,2},\ldots,L_{1,5}
and
L_{2,3},L_{2,4}
, and
L_{2,5}
, then the coefficient of
X_{1}^{2}
vanishes for all
Q_{i}
. Therefore, we must have that

(5.10)

f_{2}+f_{5}+e_{1}\geq g+4.

Similarly, we note that we must have

(5.11)

f_{3}+f_{4}+e_{1}\geq g+4.

Indeed, if not, then the coefficient of
X_{1}
vanishes for all
L_{i,j}
except possibly
j=5
. It follows that none of the quadrics have an
X_{1}^{2}
term in them, and thus they contain the section
X_{2}=X_{3}=X_{4}=0
, so
C
would be reducible.

If all
a_{1,j}=b_{1,j}=0
, then the quadrics
Q_{2},\ldots,Q_{5}
all vanish on
V(X_{3},X_{4})
. The remaining equation

Q_{1}
then cuts out a divisor on the surface
V(X_{3},X_{4})
, so either
C
is reducible or is entirely contained in
V(X_{3},X_{4})
. But this is impossible because then in the canonical embedding would send five points on
C
to a common line, which means
C
has a
g^{3}_{5}
. Projection from a point gives a
g^{2}_{5}
, so
C
would have genus at most
6
.
To prevent this,

(5.12)

f_{1}+f_{5}+e_{2}\geq g+4.

Another bad thing is if
L_{1,2},L_{1,3},L_{2,3},L_{1,4},L_{2,4}
are all zero on
V(X_{3},X_{4})
. In this case, the restriction of the quadrics to
V(X_{3},X_{4})
is
Q_{1}=L_{2,5}L_{3,4},Q_{2}=L_{1,5}L_{3,4},Q_{3}=Q_{4}=Q_{5}=0
, so
V(Q_{1})
and
V(Q_{2})
share
1
-dimensional component inside
V(X_{3},X_{4})
. To prevent this, we need

(5.13)

f_{2}+f_{4}+e_{2}\geq g+4.

Next, we note some conditions that imply
C
has a special linear series.
If
a_{1,5}=0
and
a_{2,3}=0
, then the curve meets the line
V(X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})
along
V(a_{2,5}a_{3,4}-a_{3,5}a_{2,4})
.
The degree of
V(a_{2,5}a_{3,4}-a_{3,5}a_{2,4})
is
\deg(a_{2,5})+\deg(a_{3,4})=2e_{1}-f_{1}
.
If
e_{1}=2
, the line
V(X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})
is contracted in the map
\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}\to\mathbb{P}^{g-1}
. If
2e_{1}-f_{1}=1
, then
C
meets
V(X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})
in a point
p
. Under the canonical, this point
p
lies in the span of the five points in a fiber so
p
plus the
g^{1}_{5}
is a

g^{2}_{6}
on
C
.
This yields the condition

(5.14)

if
e_{1}=2
and
e_{1}+f_{1}+f_{5},e_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}<g+4
and
2e_{1}-f_{1}=1
, then
C
has a
g^{2}_{6}
.

As another source of special linear series,
Schreyer shows
[
31
, p. 136]
that if

, then

lies on a certain determinantal surface, which is birational to a Hirzebruch surface

for

. Schreyer determines determines the class of the image of

on this Hirzebruch surface in

. In the case

and projection onto the other factor determines another pencil on

\mathbb{F}_{1}
admits a map to

with the

(which Schreyer calls

, p. 65]
(to translate our splitting types,

(5.15)

if
f_{2}-f_{1}=k
and
L_{1,2}=0
, then
C
possesses a
g^{1+k}_{f_{1}}
.

The final condition we note concerns the situation when

. In this case,
a_{1,2}=a_{1,3}=a_{2,3}=a_{1,4}=a_{2,4}=0
.
Restricting the five quadrics

to the line

we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{1}|_{Z}=a_{2,5}a_{3,4}X_{1}^{2},\qquad\qquad Q_{2}|_{Z}=a_{1,5}a_{3,4}X_{1}^{2},\qquad\qquad Q_{3}|_{Z}=Q_{4}|_{Z}=Q_{5}|_{Z}=0.

In particular, if

, then

would contain the line

, but such a curve would be reducible. Therefore,

(5.16)

if

, then

.

5.1.
Strategy

The strategy for the pentagonal locus is the same as, or even simpler than, the strategy for the tetragonal locus.

(1)

Use conditions (
5.2
)–(
5.13
) to determine the allowed pairs of splitting types
\vec{e},\vec{f}
. The partial order on splitting types of Section
3
induces a partial order on pairs of splitting types by
({\vec{e}}\penalty\hskip 1.66672pt^{\prime},\vec{f}^{\prime})\leq(\vec{e},\vec{f})
if
{\vec{e}}\penalty\hskip 1.66672pt^{\prime}\leq\vec{e}
and
\vec{f}^{\prime}\leq\vec{f}
.

(2)

Starting with strata at the bottom of the partial ordering and working upwards, show that for each stratum outside of
\Psi
at least one of the following is satisfied:

(a)

the stratum is contained in
\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{4}_{g})
.

(b)

its fundamental class in
\mathcal{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{4}_{g})
is tautological (modulo classes supported on strata below it in the partial order)
and
the Chow ring of the locally closed stratum
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4})
is generated by the restrictions of CE classes.

This will establish that
A^{*}(\mathcal{H}_{5,g}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}^{4}_{g}))
is generated by CE classes when

. In Section

, we show that the Chow rings of certain

are generated by restrictions of CE classes. Then, in Sections

,
5.4
,
5.5
, we treat the cases

respectively.

5.2.
Chow rings of very unbalanced splitting strata

In Lemma
3.11
, we described each pair splitting locus as a quotient
\Sigma=[(U\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/G]
where
G=\SL_{2}\ltimes(\Aut(\vec{e})\times\Aut(\vec{f}))
, and
U\subset H:=H^{0}(E\otimes\det E^{\vee}\otimes\wedge^{2}F)
was the open subvariety of sections
\eta
so that the Pfaffian locus
D(\Phi(\eta))\subseteq\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}
is a smooth, irreducible curve. Let
U^{\prime}\subset U
be the further open where
C=D(\Phi(\eta))
does not possess a
g^{1}_{d}
for

, so
\Sigma^{\prime}:=\Sigma\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{g}^{4})=[(U^{\prime}\times\mathbb{G}_{m})/G]
.
We have a series of surjections

(5.17)

A^{*}(BG)\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}([U^{\prime}/G])\twoheadrightarrow A^{*}(\Sigma^{\prime}),

The first map is induced by pullback from the structure map
v:[U^{\prime}/G]\rightarrow BG
.
It will suffice to show that the images of generators on the left are expressible in terms of CE classes. To see this, we will need to know about the relations that come from the complement of
U^{\prime}\subset H
.

We consider several different “shapes” of splitting types that occur for pentagonal strata.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\vec{e}=(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})
and
\vec{f}=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4},f_{5})
satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

e_{1}<e_{2}=e_{3}<e_{4}
,

(2)

f_{1}=f_{2}<f_{3}=f_{4}<f_{5}
,

(3)

e_{4}+f_{1}+f_{2}=g+4
,

(4)

e_{1}+f_{3}+f_{4}=g+4
,

(5)

-e_{1}f_{1}+e_{2}f_{1}-e_{2}f_{3}+e_{4}f_{3}+e_{1}f_{5}-e_{4}f_{5}\neq 0
.

Let
\Sigma
denote the corresponding stratum with splitting types
\vec{e}
and
\vec{f}
. Then
A^{*}(\Sigma^{\prime})
is generated by the restrictions of CE classes.

6.
The General Genus 9 Curve

Mukai
[
22
]
completely characterized canonical curves of genus
9
without a
g^{1}_{5}
as linear sections of a symplectic Grassmannian. We briefly recall his construction here.
Let
V
be a six-dimensional vector space equipped with a symplectic form
\sigma
. The
symplectic Grassmannian

Sp(3,V)\subset G(3,V)
parametrizes three-dimensional
symplectic subspaces

U\subset V
, i.e. subspaces such that
\sigma|_{U}=0
.
The Grassmannian
G(3,V)
embeds in
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{3}V)\cong\mathbb{P}^{19}
via the Plücker embedding. Contracting with the symplectic form gives a map

\sigma^{\sharp}:\wedge^{3}V\to V,

and the symplectic Grassmannian is the intersection of
G(3,V)
with
\mathbb{P}(\ker\sigma^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{3}V)
. Note that the subspace
\mathbb{P}(\ker\sigma^{\sharp})\subset\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{3}V)
corresponds to subspace of symmetric matrices in Mukai’s description of the Plücker embedding
[
22
, p. 1544]
.

Recall that we use the subspace convention for Grassmannians and projective spaces. For example, given a globally generated rank
3
vector bundle
E
on
C
, the evaluation map
H^{0}(E)\to E
determines a map
C\to G(3,H^{0}(E)^{\vee})
by considering the dual
E^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{0}(E)^{\vee}
. Similarly, the canonical embedding sends a curve
C\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(\omega_{C})^{\vee})
.
The following is an amalgamation of Mukai’s Theorems A, B, and C of
[
22
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
C
is a smooth curve of genus
9
with no
g^{1}_{5}
. Then there is a unique rank
3
vector bundle
E
on
C
with the following properties:

(1)

\det E\cong\omega_{C}
.

(2)

h^{0}(C,E)=6
.

(3)

E
is globally generated and for every
3
-dimensional subspace
U\subset H^{0}(E)
, the evaluation homomorphism
U\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\rightarrow E
is injective or everywhere of rank
2
.

The bundle
E
induces a morphism
C\rightarrow G(3,H^{0}(E)^{\vee})
whose image is contained in the symplectic Grassmannian
Sp(3,H^{0}(E)^{\vee})\subset G(3,H^{0}(E)^{\vee})
. The curve
C
in its canonical embedding is obtained by intersecting
Sp(3,H^{0}(E)^{\vee})\subset\mathbb{P}(\ker\sigma^{\sharp})\cong\mathbb{P}^{13}
with an eight dimensional linear subspace
\mathbb{P}^{8}\subset\mathbb{P}^{13}
. Such a linear space is unique up to the action of
\PGSp_{6}
, the subgroup of
\PGL_{6}
fixing the one dimensional space spanned by a symplectic form.

Moreover, a canonical curve
C
of genus
9
is the intersection
\mathbb{P}^{8}\cap Sp(3,6)
if and only if
C
has no
g^{1}_{5}
.

Let
\Delta\subset G(9,14)=\mathbb{G}(8,13)
be the locus of linear subspaces whose intersection with
Sp(3,6)\subset\mathbb{P}(\ker\sigma^{\sharp})=\mathbb{P}^{13}
is not a smooth genus
9
curve.
The above theorem provides a morphism

(6.1)

\phi:[G(9,14)\smallsetminus\Delta/\PGSp_{6}]\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{9}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}^{5}_{9}.

We wish to show that
\phi
is an isomorphism. The basic idea of our proof is modeled after
[
27
, Theorem 5.7]
.
In particular, we make use of the following standard lemma, whose proof we include for completeness.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a separated morphism of connected smooth Deligne–Mumford stacks that are of finite type over a field. Suppose that

(1)

the characteristic of the ground field is zero,

(2)

f
induces an isomorphism on stabilizer groups of geometric points, and

(3)

f
induces a bijection on geometric points.

Then
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
a:Y\to\mathcal{Y}
be a connected, smooth cover by a smooth scheme
Y
.
Let
X:=\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathcal{Y}}Y
be the fiber product, so we have a diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Suppose
x:\Spec k\to X
is a geometric point. The stabilizer group
G_{x}
of
x
is equal to the fiber product of stabilizer groups
G_{b(x)}\times_{G_{f(b(x))}}G_{g(x)}
. But
Y
is a scheme, so
G_{g(x)}
is trivial. Hence,
G_{x}=\ker(G_{b(x)}\to G_{f(b(x)})
, which is trivial by hypothesis.
By
[
7
, Theorem 2.2.5]
, it follows that
X
is an algebraic space.
Further, the map
f^{\prime}:X\to Y
is quasi-finite and separated so by
[
32
,
Tag 03XX
]
, we know
X
is a scheme.
Because
f
induces an isomorphism on stabilizer groups of geometric points, the map
f^{\prime}:X\to Y
is a bijection on geometric points.
Because
a
is smooth and
\mathcal{X}
is smooth and connected, we know
X
is also smooth and connected.
Working in characteristic zero, the map
f^{\prime}
is generically smooth, hence birational. Now, Zariski’s Main Theorem shows that
f^{\prime}:X\to Y
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose the characteristic of the ground field is zero. The map
\phi
induces an isomorphism on stabilizer groups of geometric points.

Proof.

In characteristic zero, finite group schemes are smooth, so it suffices to show the map induces a bijection on the finite stabilizer groups.
Suppose

is a geometric point. Such a point is the data of
(V,\sigma,W)
where

is a six-dimensional vector space,

is a symplectic form remembered up to scaling and

is a
9

The stabilizer group of

is the subgroup of elements
\gamma\in\PGSp_{6}\subset\PGL_{6}
that send

into itself.
The image

is the genus
9

The

Remark 6.6

In positive characteristic,

Our task is now to compute generators for the Chow ring of

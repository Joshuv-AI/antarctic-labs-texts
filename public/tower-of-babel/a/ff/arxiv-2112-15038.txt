Estimating the Cosmological Constant from Shadows of Kerr–de Sitter Black Holes
Misba Afrin * \orcidicon, Sushant G. Ghosh \orcidicon

Abstract
The Event Horizon Telescope collaboration has revealed the first direct image of a black hole, as per the shadow of a Kerr black hole of general relativity. However, other Kerr-like rotating black holes of modified gravity theories cannot be ignored, and they are essential as they offer an arena in which these theories can be tested through astrophysical observation. This motivates us to investigate asymptotically de Sitter rotating black holes wherein interpreting the cosmological constant Λ \Lambda as the vacuum energy leads to a deformation in the vicinity of a black hole—new Kerr–de Sitter solution, which has a richer geometric structure than the original one. We derive an analytical formula necessary for the shadow of the new Kerr–de Sitter black holes and then visualize the shadow of black holes for various parameters for an observer at given coordinates ( r 0 , θ 0 ) (r_{0},\theta_{0}) in the domain ( r 0 , r c ) (r_{0},\;r_{c}) and estimate the cosmological constant Λ \Lambda from its shadow observables. The shadow observables of the new Kerr–de Sitter black holes significantly deviate from the corresponding observables of the Kerr–de Sitter black hole over an appreciable range of the parameter space. Interestingly, we find a finite parameter space for ( Λ \Lambda , a a ) where the observables of the two black holes are indistinguishable.

Introduction

Because of the black hole’s defining property at the event horizon and the surrounding photon region, it casts a dark region over the observer’s celestial sky, which is known as a shadow
(
Bardeen 1973
;
Falcke et al. 2000
)
, which is an optical appearance cast by a black hole when it is in front of a distant luminous source. It is a natural result of Einstein’s theory of general relativity (GR), so it can provide us with information on the properties of the black hole. The seminal works of Synge
(
Synge 1966
)
and Luminet
(
Luminet 1979
)
led the foundation for the shadows of black holes. Synge
(
Synge 1966
)
was the first to study the angular radius of the photon capture region around the Schwarzschild black hole. Interestingly, the photon sphere, in the case of a Kerr black hole, becomes a ”photon region” which is filled by spherical light-like geodesics at a sphere
r=
constant. Bardeen
Bardeen 1973
first correctly analysed the shadow of the Kerr black hole and showed that the spin would distort the shape of the shadow, and that the deviation of the shadow, from a circle, is proportional to the spin of the black hole. Furthermore, Teo
(
Teo 2021
)
analysed pictures of individual spherical light-like geodesics in the Kerr spacetime.
The application of shadow in unravelling gravity’s useful near-horizon features is a valuable tool for testing GR. Thereby, the discussion was extended for other black holes, e.g., for the Kerr–Newman spacetime

(
de Vries 2000
)
, for
\delta=2
Tomimatsu–Sato spacetimes

(
Bambi and Yoshida 2010
)
, for black holes in extended Chern–Simons
modified gravity
(
Amarilla et al. 2010
)
, in a Randall–Sundrum
braneworld scenario
(
Amarilla and Eiroa 2012
)
, and a Kaluza–Klein
rotating dilaton black hole
(
Amarilla and Eiroa 2013
)
,
for the Kerr–NUT
spacetime
(
Abdujabbarov et al. 2013
)
,
for multi-black holes
(
Yumoto et al. 2012
)
, for regular black holes
(
Li and Bambi 2014
;
Kumar and Ghosh 2020
;
Kumar et al. 2020
;
Kumar et al. 2019
;
Ghosh et al. 2020
;
Amir et al. 2020
;
Abdujabbarov et al. 2016
;
Amir and Ghosh 2016
)
and for higher-dimensional black holes
(
Papnoi et al. 2014
;
Ahmed et al. 2020a
;
Ahmed et al. 2020b
;
Amir et al. 2018
;
Eiroa and Sendra 2018
;
Vagnozzi and Visinelli 2019
;
Banerjee et al. 2020
)
.
The shadows of modified theories of gravity black holes cast smaller and more distorted shadows when compared with that of the Kerr black hole
(
Amarilla et al. 2010
;
Amarilla and Eiroa 2012
;
Amarilla and Eiroa 2013
;
Amir et al. 2018
;
Singh and Ghosh 2018
;
Mizuno et al. 2018
;
Ghosh et al. 2020
;
Kumar and Ghosh 2020
;
Afrin et al. 2021
)
. Moreover, the spin, mass parameter and possibly other parameters of black holes can be calculated
(
Hioki and Maeda 2009
;
Tsupko 2017
;
Cunha et al. 2019a
;
Cunha et al. 2019b
;
Kumar and Ghosh 2020
;
Khodadi et al. 2020
)
. In addition, shadow also finds applications in testing theories of gravity
(
Kramer et al. 2004
)
, and Johannsen and Psaltis

(
Johannsen and Psaltis 2010
)
explored how to test
the no-hair theorem using the shadow of a black hole. Most investigations have been largely based
on ray tracing in the respective spacetimes, rather than on analytical
studies of the geodesic equation under the assumption that the observer is
at infinity. This prescription does not work when the black holes are no longer asymptotically flat but instead when it is asymptotically dS
/
AdS, and a cosmological horizon encompassing the event horizon is present
(
Perlick et al. 2018
;
Kumar et al. 2021
;
Neves 2020
;
Maluf and Neves 2021
;
Grenzebach et al. 2014
;
Stuchlík et al. 2018
;
Charbulák and Stuchlík 2017
)
. Therefore, the observer is located at the so-called domain of external communication, which is the region defined between the event horizon and the cosmological horizon in asymptotically de Sitter spacetimes
(
Perlick et al. 2018
;
Kumar et al. 2021
)
.
Indeed, Rindler and Ishak
(
Rindler and Ishak 2007
)
realized that the cosmological constant
\Lambda
does influence the gravitational lensing features because it changes the angle measurements as well as the angular radius of the shadow
Perlick et al. 2018
.

This paper investigates shadows of recently obtained Kerr black holes with a cosmological constant—new Kerr–de Sitter spacetimes
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
—and shows our prescription’s applicability for the determination of the black hole parameters, emphasizing the comparison with the original Kerr–de Sitter black hole shadows. We found that the horizons’ structure contrast from the original Kerr–de Sitter horizons and expression for the photon sphere radii are also different from those in the original Kerr–de Sitter. Furthermore, we found changes in the photon regions of new Kerr–de Sitter black holes, which affect the black hole shadows.

2.1
A New Kerr–de Sitter Black Hole

The Kerr–de Sitter solution of Carter
(
Carter 1973
)
leaves the cosmological constant
\Lambda
immaculate. However, the
\Lambda
in the strong field regime, such as in the vicinity of a black hole, is no longer constant in quantum field theory
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
. Recently, Ovalle et al.
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
interpreted the cosmological constant
\Lambda
as
vacuum energy
and implemented the so-called gravitational decoupling (GD) approach
(
Ovalle 2017
;
Ovalle 2019
;
Contreras et al. 2021
)
to obtain a new Kerr–de Sitter black hole solution which is geometrically richer and has an impact of rotation in the form of a warped curvature
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
.
The new Kerr–de Sitter black holes in the Boyer–Lindquist coordinates read
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\right]dt^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}dr^{2}+\Sigma d\theta^{2}-2a\sin^{2}{\theta}\left[1-\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}}{\Sigma}\right]dtd\phi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sin^{2}{\theta}}{\Sigma}\left[(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-\Delta a^{2}\sin^{2}{\theta}\right]d\phi^{2},

(3)

where
\Delta=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr-{(\Lambda r^{4})}/{3}
and
\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta
. The metric (
2.1
) describes the rotating black holes in asymptotically de Sitter or anti-de Sitter, respectively, when
\Lambda>0
or
\Lambda<0
and encompass the Kerr black hole in the absence of the cosmological constant (
\Lambda=0
) and Schwarzschild–de Sitter black hole (
a=0
)
(
Ovalle 2017
)
.
When written in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, metric (
2.1
) is same as the Kerr black hole with
M
replaced by
m(r)=M+\frac{\Lambda}{6}r^{3}
.
Thus, the new Kerr–de Sitter black hole is different from the Kerr–de Sitter black hole (
2
), which is a
\Lambda
-vacuum solution.
In contrast, the new Kerr–de Sitter metric (
2.1
) is not a
\Lambda
-vacuum solution and has a curvature warped in space given by
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
:

R(r,\theta)=-4\Lambda\left[\frac{r^{2}}{r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}{\theta}}\right],

(4)

which is obeyed by the Carter’s Kerr–de Sitter metric in the equatorial plane (
\theta=\pi/2
). Further, the deformation in
R
becomes most prominent as
r\sim a
and vanishes for
r>>a
(cf. Figure
1
).

Energy Conditions

If
T_{\mu\nu}
is the energy momentum associated with matter for metric (
2.1
), we can define the components of the energy momentum tensor which in the orthonormal frame reads
(
Bambi and Modesto 2013
;
Neves and Saa 2014
)
:

T^{(a)(b)}=e_{\mu}^{(a)}e_{\nu}^{(b)}G^{\mu\nu},

(5)

where
e_{\mu}^{(a)}
is the basis for the usual orthonormal tetrad
(
Bambi and Modesto 2013
;
Ghosh 2015
;
Ghosh 2016
;
Neves and Saa 2014
)
.
We can write the components of the energy momentum tensor as
(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)

\displaystyle{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{p}_{r}=\frac{2\,r^{2}}{\Sigma^{2}}\,{m}^{\prime}=\Lambda\,\frac{r^{4}}{\Sigma^{2}}=\frac{\tilde{\Lambda}^{2}}{\Lambda}\ ,

(6)

\displaystyle{p}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{p}_{\phi}=-\frac{r}{\Sigma}\,{m}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2\left(r^{2}-\Sigma\right)}{\Sigma^{2}}\,{m}^{\prime}=\rho-2\,\Lambda\,\frac{r^{2}}{\Sigma}\ .

(7)

The weak energy condition
(
Bambi and Modesto 2013
;
Ghosh 2015
;
Ghosh 2016
;
Ghosh and walia 2021
;
Neves and Saa 2014
)
requires
\rho\geq 0
and
\rho+P_{i}\geq 0
(
i=r,\theta,\phi
). Obviously:

\rho+P_{\theta}=\rho+P_{\phi}=-\frac{\Lambda r^{2}(2\Sigma-r^{2})}{\Sigma^{2}}.

(8)

Clearly, the weak energy conditions are satisfied for
\Lambda<0
. However, in general, as seen from Figure
2
, it may not hold. On the other hand, the dominant energy condition demands that:

\rho\geq 0,\,\rho\geq|{P}_{i}|\quad\left(i=r,\theta,\phi\right).

(9)

The dominant energy condition holds for
\Lambda>0
, but is found to be violated for
\Lambda<0

(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
. Furthermore, the strong energy condition given by

\rho+{P}_{r}+2\,{p}_{\theta}\geq 0,\,{\rho}+{p}_{r}\geq 0,\,{\rho}+{p}_{\theta}\geq 0

(10)

is satisfied for
\Lambda<0
but is violated for
\Lambda>0

(
Ovalle et al. 2021
)
. Our results show that the energy conditions may not be prevented, e.g., cf. Figure
2
. The weak energy condition is not satisfied for Kerr–de Sitter black holes, but the violation can be minimal, depending on the value of parameters, as shown in Figure
2
. Despite violations, such solutions are essential from a phenomenological point of view, and they are also essential as astrophysical black holes are rotating
(
Bambi and Modesto 2013
;
Ghosh 2015
;
Ghosh 2016
;
Ghosh and walia 2021
;
Neves and Saa 2014
)
.

Similar to the Kerr–de Sitter metric (

and

For
\Lambda>0
, the region between the event horizon (
r_{+}^{NK}
) and cosmological horizon (

) is called the

,
r_{+}^{NK}
is the future inner horizon while
r_{c}^{NK}
is the future outer horizon, and crossing any of these two horizons will break the causal connection with region
r_{+}^{NK}<r<r_{c}^{NK}

(
Kumar et al. 2021
)
.

3
Photon Region and Shadow in Asymptotic de Sitter Spacetime

In order to discuss the photon region
(
Teo 2021
;
Johnson et al. 2020
)
and black hole shadow
(
Kumar et al. 2020a
;
Kumar et al. 2019
;
Kumar et al. 2020b
;
Ghosh et al. 2020
)
in the new Kerr–de Sitter spacetime, it is pertinent to study the motion of a test particle. We consider the black hole in a luminous background and the photons from a source, due to gravitational lensing
(
Kumar et al. 2020
;
Islam et al. 2020
;
Kumar and Ghosh 2020
)
, get deflected before reaching an observer. The photons, depending on the energy, may get scattered, captured or move in unstable orbits
(
Chandrasekhar 1985
;
Kumar et al. 2020
)
. Tracing back these photon trajectories we get the black hole shadow
(
Grenzebach et al. 2014
;
Kumar and Ghosh 2020
;
Kumar et al. 2020a
;
Kumar et al. 2019
;
Kumar et al. 2020b
;
Kumar et al. 2021
;
Afrin et al. 2021
)
. Hence, we need to discuss the null geodesics for the new Kerr–de Sitter metric (
2
).
Owing to the axial and time translational symmetries alongside a hidden symmetry
(
Carter 1968
)
, the spacetime (
2.1
) gives four constants of motion along each null geodesic: the mass
g_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}=0
, total energy
E=-p.\partial_{t}
, angular momentum
\mathcal{L}_{z}=p.\partial_{\phi}
and Carter constant
\mathcal{Q}
. We follow the Carter’s separable method to solve the Hamilton-Jacobi equation to obtain the following equations of motion around the new Kerr–de Sitter metric in the first order differential form
(
Carter 1968
;
Chandrasekhar 1985
)
:

\displaystyle\Sigma\dot{t}=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Delta}(\mathcal{E}(r^{2}+a^{2})-a\mathcal{L}_{z})-a(a\mathcal{E}\sin^{2}{\theta}-\mathcal{L}_{z}),

(13)

\displaystyle\Sigma\dot{\phi}=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{\Delta}(\mathcal{E}(r^{2}+a^{2})-a\mathcal{L}_{z})-\left(a\mathcal{E}-\frac{\mathcal{L}_{z}}{\sin^{2}{\theta}}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}\dot{r}^{2}=

\displaystyle\Big((r^{2}+a^{2})E-a\mathcal{L}_{z}\Big)^{2}-\Delta\Big({\mathcal{K}}+(aE-\mathcal{L})^{2}\Big)\equiv\mathcal{R}(r)\ ,

(15)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}\dot{\theta}^{2}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}-\left(\frac{{L_{z}}^{2}}{\sin^{2}\theta}-a^{2}E^{2}\right)\cos^{2}\theta\equiv\Theta(\theta)\ ,

(16)

where the separability constant
\mathcal{K}
is related to the Carter constant
\mathcal{Q}
through
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{K}+(a\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{L}_{z})^{2}

(
Chandrasekhar 1985
)
. Further, dimensionless impact parameters
\xi=\mathcal{L}_{z}/\mathcal{E}\text{,}\;\eta=\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{E}^{2}

(
Chandrasekhar 1985
)
reduce the degrees of freedom of the photon geodesics from three to two.

We note that the Equation (

) can be recast as
(
Kumar et al. 2020
)
:

\dot{r}^{2}+V_{eff}(r)=0,

(17)

where
V_{eff}
is the effective radial potential for the photons and is given by

4
Parameter Estimation and Relative Difference of Shadow Observables

Estimation of the black hole parameters has been under constant endeavour
(
Roelofs et al. 2021
;
Broderick et al. 2021
;
Broderick et al. 2020
;
Feng and Wu 2017
;
Narayan et al. 2008
;
Narayan 2005
)
and the shadows have been utilized to estimate the parameters in both GR
(
Hioki and Maeda 2009
;
Tsukamoto et al. 2014
;
Tsupko 2017
)
as well as in modified theories of gravity (MoGs)
(
Kumar and Ghosh 2020
;
Afrin and Ghosh 2021
;
Ghosh et al. 2021
)
. Interestingly, though the black hole spin and various other hairs have been estimated, the shadow has not been utilized for the estimation of the cosmological constant
\Lambda
thus far. In the present study, we intend to estimate the
\Lambda
in addition to the spin using shadow observables.
Einstein introduced the cosmological constant
\Lambda

to balance the evolutionary models with repulsion to set a
steady-state but later abandoned as a blunder. One can introduce
\Lambda
as the vacuum energy required to drive
inflation
(
Akcay and Matzner 2011
)
. The accelerating expansion may even be interpreted as the continuation of inflation, possibly at a slower rate than in the early universe. The cosmological constant
\Lambda
has a dimension
(length)^{2}
, and as per the classical general relativity, one has no preferred choice of what the length scale defined by
\Lambda
could be.

Hioki and Maeda approximated the shadow silhouette to a reference circle coinciding with the top (
X_{t}
,
Y_{t}
), bottom (
X_{b}
,
Y_{b}
) and right (
X_{r},0
) extremes of the shadow; the shadow size and deviation from a perfect circle was thus characterized by two observables, the shadow radius (
R_{s}
) and distortion (

)
(

)
which are defined as

\displaystyle R_{s}=\frac{(X_{t}-X_{r})^{2}+Y_{t}^{2}}{2|X_{r}-X_{t}|},\;\;\delta_{s}=\frac{|X_{l}-X^{{}^{\prime}}_{l}|}{R_{s}},

(27)

where the shadow symmetry concerning the
x
axis implies
(X_{b},Y_{b})=(X_{t},-Y_{t})
. Furthermore, the reference circle and leftmost edge of the shadow silhouette intersect with the
x
axis at (
X^{{}^{\prime}}_{l}
, 0) and (
X_{l}
, 0), respectively,
(
Hioki and Maeda 2009
)
. However, the requirement of the shadow to be approximated by a circle is found to not always be feasible viz., for haphazard shadows in MoGs
(
Schee and Stuchlik 2009
;
Johannsen 2013
;
Tsukamoto et al. 2014
;

;

and also for noisy data

\displaystyle A=2\int_{r_{p}^{-}}^{r_{p}^{+}}\left(Y(r_{p})\frac{dX(r_{p})}{dr_{p}}\right)dr_{p}\;,\;D=\frac{X_{r}-X_{l}}{Y_{t}-Y_{b}}.

5
Conclusions

We explored the various spacetime properties in the strong-field regime of the new Kerr–de Sitter black holes and found the curvature’s warping effect. The horizons significantly deviate from the original Kerr–de Sitter horizons. We obtained an expression for the photon sphere radii and found that they are different from those in the original Kerr–de Sitter. Consequently, they impact the photon region’s structure in the new Kerr–de Sitter black holes and the black hole shadows. The new Kerr–de Sitter black hole, not asymptotically flat, has a cosmological horizon which prevents us from considering a static observer at an arbitrarily large distance and is compelling to restrict it within the region (
r_{+},r_{c}
). Thus, our formalism allows for observers at any Boyer–Lindquist coordinates in the domain of outer communication. We derived an analytical formula for the new Kerr–de Sitter black hole shadows using a detailed analysis of the photon regions.
We present observables
A
along with
D
characterizing the new Kerr–de Sitter shadow shape to estimate the cosmological constant
\Lambda
. Interestingly, we find a finite parameter space for (
\Lambda
,
a
) where the two observables
A
and
D
of the new Kerr–de Sitter black holes and the Kerr–de Sitter black hole are indistinguishable.

Finally, our results, in the limit
\Lambda\to 0
, go over to those of the Kerr black holes.

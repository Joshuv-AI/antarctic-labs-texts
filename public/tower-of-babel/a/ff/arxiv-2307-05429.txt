A study of spirallike domains: polynomial convexity, Loewner chains and dense holomorphic curves Thanks: Sanjoy Chatterjee is supported by CSIR fellowship (File No-09/921(0283)/2019-EMR-I). Sushil Gorai is partially supported by a Core Research Grant (CRG/2022/003560) of SERB, Govt. of India
Sanjoy Chatterjee and Sushil Gorai

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove that the closure of a bounded pseudoconvex domain, which is spirallike with respect to a globally asymptotic stable holomorphic vector field, is polynomially convex. We also provide a necessary and sufficient condition, in terms of polynomial convexity, on a univalent function defined on a strongly convex domain for embedding it into a filtering Loewner chain. Next, we provide an application of our first result. We show that for any bounded pseudoconvex strictly spirallike domain Ω \Omega in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} and given any connected complex manifold Y Y , there exists a holomorphic map from the unit disc to the space of all holomorphic maps from Ω \Omega to Y Y . This also yields us the existence of 𝒪 ⁡ ( Ω , Y ) \mathcal{O}(\Omega,Y) -universal map for any generalized translation on Ω \Omega , which, in turn, is connected to the hypercyclicity of certain composition operators on the space of manifold valued holomorphic maps.

1.
Introduction and statements of the results

The domains we study in this paper are pseudoconvex domains that are spirallike with respect to certain holomorphic vector fields.
Recall that
a holomorphic vector field

\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

\Omega

V(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+b_{i}(z)\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}},

Any holomorphic map

. We will use matrices with complex entries while talking about linear vector fields on
\mathbb{C}^{n}

We will need the notion of spirallike domain with respect to a holomorphic vector field from

Next, we briefly mention some notions of stability of the equilibrium point of a system of differential equations (see

Let

(1.1)

\displaystyle\frac{dX(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=f(X(t)),~~X(0)=x_{0}.

Assume that the solution of the system (

•

•

A vector field

In this paper we study the polynomial convexity of the closure of pseudoconvex strictly spirallike domains. We also study the embedding a univalent function into a filtering Loewner chain through polynomial convexity property along the lines of Hamada
[

1.1.
Polynomial convexity

For a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
the Polynomially convex hull of
K
, denoted by
\widehat{K}
, is defined by

\widehat{K}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|p(z)|\leq\sup_{w\in K}|p(w)|,\forall p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}]\}.

We say that
K
is polynomially convex if
\widehat{K}=K
. Polynomial convexity is the main ingredient in the study of uniform approximation by polynomials. In
\mathbb{C}
, a compact subset
K
is polynomially convex if and only if
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
is connected. In general, for
n>1
, it is difficult to determine whether a compact subset in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is polynomially convex or not. It is known that any compact convex subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is polynomially convex. In particular, the closure of any bounded convex domain is polynomially convex. However, in
[
26
, Example 2.7]
, it was shown that the closure of a bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain with a smooth boundary may not be polynomially convex. In
[
27
]
, Joiţa gave an example of a strongly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with real analytic boundary whose closure is not polynomially convex. The doamin in the example of Joiţa
[
27
]
is a is also a Runge domain.
This raises a natural question:
For which classes of pseudoconvex domains the closure is polynomially convex?
Hamada
[
21
]
proved that bounded pseudoconvex domains, which are strictly spirallike with respect to certain linear vector field, have polynomially convex closure.

Result 1.2

(Hamada)
.

Let
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\inf_{||z||=1}{\sf Re}\langle Az,z\rangle>0
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

be a bounded pseudoconvex domain containing the origin such that

e^{-tA}w\in\Omega
for all
t>0
and for all
w\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then,
\overline{\Omega}
is polynomially convex.

In this article, we are able to provide a generalization of
Result

1.2
, which enlarges the class of bounded pseudoconvex domains that have polynomially convex closure. We need the following definition for the demonstration of our results.
The first result of this paper states that the conclusion of
Result

1.2
is also true if the domain
D
is spirallike with respect to any asymptotic stable holomorphic vector field. More precisely, we present:

Theorem 1.3

Let

such that

is strictly spirallike domain with respect to

It is proved in

(in

Hamada also provided an example in

The following corollary gives a condition of polynomial convex closure in terms of the defining function of the domain. For a holomorphic vector field

.

.

Let

The embedding of a univalent map defined on a bounded strongly convex domain into some Loewner chain is the subject of our second result. 1.3.
Dense holomorphic curves

Next, we will demonstrate an application of
Theorem

1.3
in the context of finding a dense holomorphic map and constructing universal mapping. For any two complex manifolds
X,Y
, the set of all holomorphic maps from
X
to
Y
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}(X,Y)
. If
Y
is
\mathbb{C}
then the set of all holomorphic functions on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{O}(X)
. Let
Y
be a complex manifold. The main question here is:
For a given complex manifold
Z
, does there exists a holomorphic map
f\colon Z\to Y
such that
f(Z)
is dense in
Y
?
In this case, we say that
f
is a dense holomorphic map from
Z
to
Y
. In
[
41
]
, Winkelmann proved that if
X
and
Y
are irreducible complex spaces and
X
admits a non-constant bounded holomorphic function then there exists a holomorphic map from
X
to
Y
with dense image (for the notions of complex manifold and complex space see
[
16
]
). In
[
14
]
, Forstnerič and Winkelmann showed that if
X
is a connected complex manifold then the set of all holomorphic maps
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
with
\overline{f(\mathbb{D})}=X
is dense in
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},X)
with respect to the compact open topology. In this paper we consider the holomorphic maps with value in
\mathcal{O}(X,Y)
, where
X
and
Y
are complex manifolds.
Let
X,Y,Z
be connected complex manifolds and
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{O}(X,Y)
. We say that a map
f\colon Z\to\mathcal{S}
is holomorphic if the map
\hat{f}\colon Z\times X\to Y
defined by
\hat{f}(z,x)=f(z)(x)
is holomorphic. In this case,
\hat{f}
is said to be associated holomorphic map for

. Following the terminology introduced by Kusakabe
[
30
]
, we say a subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{O}(X,Y)
is

-dominated if there exists a dense holomorphic map

.
In
[
30
]
, Kusakabe proved the following result.

Result 1.13
.

[
30

Let
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

is
\mathbb{D}
-dominated.

By demonstrating an example

1.4.
Universal mappings and composition operators

We also apply
Theorem

1.14
to obtain a result providing the existence of a universal mapping. In
[
5
]
, Birkhoff constructed for every sequence of real number
\{b_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbf{N}}
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}b_{k}=\infty
, a holomorphic function
F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
, with the property that if
F_{k}\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
F_{k}(z)=F(z+b_{k})
then
\{F_{k}|k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
with respect to the compact open topology. Such a function is called
universalfunction
. Later, Seidel and Walsh
[
39
]
proved the result for the unit disc replacing the Euclidian translation with a non-Euclidian translation. In
[
31

, Fernando proved that any compactly divergent sequence of automorphisms of the open unit ball and the polydisc admits a universal function.
Existence of universal function has a very close connection with the hypercyclicity of a composition operator (See
[
19
]
for a nice survey in this topic).

Let

be a self-map on a topological vector space

is said to be
hypercyclic with respect to

(n_{k})

such that
\{T^{n_{k}}(x)|k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
X
, where
T^{m}:=\underbrace{T\circ T\circ\cdots\circ T}_{\text{m times}}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

[
42
]
, Zaja̧c proved the following result:

r>0
and

\Omega=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\;|\;|z_{1}|<r,~|z_{2}|<e^{-|z_{1}|})\}.

Clearly,
\Omega
is a Hartogs domain (see
[
40
, Page 406]
) over an open ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{C}
. Since
-\log{e^{-|z_{1}|}}=|z_{1}|
is plurisubharmonic function hence from
[
40
, page 406]
, we get that
\Omega
is pseudoconvex domain.

Now consider the vector field
F(z_{1},z_{2})=(-2z_{1},-3z_{2}+z_{1}z_{2})
. The flow of the vector field is defined by
X(t,z)=\left(z_{1}e^{-2t},z_{2}e^{-3t}e^{\frac{z_{1}}{2}(1-e^{-2t})}\right)
, where
(t,z)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We show that
\Omega
is strictly spirallike with respect to
F
.
Let
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega
and
t>0
. Suppose that
w_{1}=z_{1}e^{-2t}
and
w_{2}=z_{2}e^{-3t}e^{\frac{z_{1}}{2}(1-e^{-2t})}
. Clearly,
|w_{1}|<r
for all
t>0
. Since, for all
t>0
, we have
(1-e^{-2t})(-|z_{1}|+\frac{{\sf Re}(z_{1})}{2})-3t<0
, hence for all
t>0
, we have the following

\displaystyle|w_{2}|

\displaystyle=|z_{2}|e^{-3t+\frac{{\sf Re}(z_{1})}{2}(1-e^{-2t})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-|z_{1}|-3t+\frac{{\sf Re}(z_{1})}{2}(1-e^{-2t})}

\displaystyle<e^{-|w_{1}|}.

Therefore,
\Omega
is a bounded pseudoconvex domain that is a strictly spirallike domain with respect to the complete globally asymptotic stable vector field
F
. Clearly,
\Omega
is not convex.

Let for
j=\{1,2\}

V_{j}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}|z_{j}=0\}
. Clearly,
\Omega
is a pseudoconvex Reinhardt domain such that
\Omega\cap V_{j}\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, from
[
43
, Theorem 2]
it follows that
\Omega
is
c_{\Omega}
-finitely compact.

Therefore, the conclusion of
Theorem

1.14
,
Theorem

1.20
is true for this domain.

Next, we give an example of a non-convex, strongly pseudoconvex domain with polynomially convex closure that is biholomorphic to a bounded strongly convex domain. Therefore, the conclusion of
Theorem

1.10
as well as
Theorem

1.9
holds.

Example 6.2
.

Let
\Omega_{1}=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{1}|^{2}|z_{2}|^{2}-1<0\}
. Here,
\Omega_{1}
is a strongly convex domain. Since
\Omega_{1}
is a circular domain hence from Cartan’s Theorem
[
8
]
, it follows that any biholomorphism from the open unit ball onto
\Omega_{1}
is a linear map. Therefore, the defining function of
\Omega_{1}
can not contain the term
|z_{1}|^{2}|z_{2}|^{2}
. Hence,
\Omega_{1}
is not biholomorphic to the open unit ball.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}
be any non-convex simply connected domain. Suppose that
p,q\in U
can not be connected by a straight line contained in
U
. By Riemann mapping theorem, we get there exists a biholomorphism
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to U
such that
f(0)=p
and
f(x)=q
. Clearly
x\neq 0
. Choose
\varepsilon>0
such that
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{|x|}-1
. Let
\mathbb{D}(0,1+\epsilon)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}||z|<1+\varepsilon\}
. Note that
\overline{\Omega}_{1}\Subset\mathbb{D}(0,1+\epsilon)\times\mathbb{D}(0,1+\epsilon)
.

Let
G\colon\mathbb{D}(0,1+\epsilon)\times\mathbb{D}(0,1+\epsilon)\to U\times\mathbb{D}(0,1+\epsilon)
defined by
G(z,w)=(f(\frac{z}{1+\varepsilon}),w)
. Here
G(0,0)
and
G((1+\varepsilon)x,0)
can not be connected by a straight line by construction. Hence,
G(\Omega_{1})
is not convex. Since
G
has holomorphic extension on a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}_{1}
, hence
G(\Omega_{1})
is strongly pseudoconvex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
boundary. Note that
G
is a biholomorphism from a star-shaped domain onto a Runge domain. Hence, in view of
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
, we conclude that
G
can be approximated by
Aut(\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Consequently,
G(\overline{\Omega}_{1})
is polynomially convex.
Therefore, from
Theorem

1.10
, we conclude that there exists
\Psi\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that
\Psi(G(\Omega_{1}))
is convex. Equivalently,
G
can be embedded into a filtering Loewner chain.

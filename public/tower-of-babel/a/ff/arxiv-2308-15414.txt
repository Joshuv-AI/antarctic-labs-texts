Duality and Norm Completeness in the Classes of Limitedly-Lwc and Dunford–Pettis-Lwc Operators


Abstract
We investigate the duality and norm completeness in the classes of limitedly L-weakly compact and Dunford–Pettis L-weakly compact operators from Banach spaces to Banach lattices.

Introduction

The theory of L-weakly compact (briefly,
Lwc
) sets and operators was developed
by P. Meyer-Nieberg in the beginning of seventies in order to diversify the concept
of weakly compact operators via imposing the Banach
lattice structure on the range of operators
[
17
]
. Dunford–Pettis sets appeared
a decade later in the work
[
3
]
of K. T. Andrews.
Shortly thereafter, J. Bourgain and J. Diestel introduced limited sets and operators
[
6
]
.
Since then L-weakly compact operators and limited operators have attracted
permanent attention and inspiring researchers. Recently further related classes of operators were introduced
and studied by many authors (see, for example,
[
2
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
19
,
20
]
, and references therein).
Using the Meyer-Nieberg approach for the Dunford–Pettis and for limited
(instead of bounded) sets in the domain, we introduce
Dunford–Pettis
Lwc
and the limitedly
Lwc
operators.
We study the duality and norm completeness in classes of these operators.

Throughout the text: vector spaces are real; operators are linear and bounded;
X
and
Y

stand for Banach spaces,
E
and
F
for Banach lattices;
B_{X}
for the closed unit ball of
X
;

\text{\rm L}(X,Y)
for the space of all bounded operators from
X
to
Y
;

E_{+}
for the positive cone of
E
;

\text{\rm sol}(A):=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}[-|a|,|a|]

for the solid hull of
A\subseteq E
;

E^{a}:=\{x\in E:|x|\geq x_{n}\downarrow 0\Rightarrow\|x_{n}\|\to 0\}

for the order continuous part of
E
; and
X^{\prime}
for the norm-dual of
X
.
We identify
X
with its image
\widehat{X}
in
X^{\prime\prime}

under the canonical embedding
\hat{x}(f)=f(x)
.
For further terminology on Banach lattices, see
[
1
,
15
,
18
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we introduce Dunford–Pettis

and

Among other things, we characterize Dunford–Pettis sets via limited sets in

.
Section 3 is devoted to complete norms on spaces of such operators

2
Main Definitions and Basic Properties

In this section we collect main definitions, introduce the Dunford–Pettis
L
-weakly compact and limitedly

L
-weakly compact operators from a Banach space to a Banach lattice,
and study their basic properties.
We begin with the following crucial definition belonging to P. Meyer-Nieberg
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A subset
A
of
F
is called an
Lwc
set whenever
each disjoint sequence in
\text{\rm sol}(A)
is norm-null.
A bounded operator
T:X\to F
is an
Lwc
operator
(briefly,
T\in\text{\rm Lwc}(X,F)
) if
T(B_{X})
is an
Lwc
subset of
F
.

It can be easily seen that
B_{E}
is not
Lwc
unless

\dim(E)<\infty
.
For every
Lwc
subset
A
of
E
, we have
A\subseteq E^{a}
.
Indeed, otherwise there is
a\in A

with
|a|\in E\setminus E^{a}
, and hence there exists a disjoint sequence

(x_{n})
in
[0,|a|]\subseteq\text{\rm sol}(A)

with
\|x_{n}\|\not\to 0
.
The next important fact goes back to Meyer-Nieberg (cf.
[
1
, Thm.5.63]

and
[
7
, Prop.2.2]
for more general setting).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A\subseteq E
and
B\subseteq E^{\prime}
be nonempty bounded sets. Then
every disjoint sequence in
\text{\rm sol}(A)
is uniformly null
on
B
iff every disjoint sequence in
\text{\rm sol}(B)
is uniformly null on
A
.

We include a proof of the following certainly well known fact.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
L
be a nonempty bounded subset of
F^{\prime}
. TFAE.

i)

L
is an
Lwc
subset of
F^{\prime}
.

ii)

Each disjoint sequence in
B_{F}
is uniformly null on
L
.

iii)

Each disjoint sequence in
B_{F^{\prime\prime}}
is uniformly null on
L
.

Proof.

Since
\|f\|=\sup\{|f(x)|:x\in B_{X}\}=\sup\{|y(f)|:y\in B_{X^{\prime\prime}}\}
then

(f_{n})\ \text{\rm in}\ X^{\prime}\ \text{\rm converges uniformly on}\ B_{X}\ \text{\rm iff it converges uniformly on}\ B_{X^{\prime\prime}}

under the identification of
f\in X^{\prime}
with
\hat{f}\in X^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
Now, applying Proposition
2.2
first to
A=B_{F}
and
B=L
,
and then to
A=L
and
B=B_{F^{\prime\prime}}
we obtain that both ii) and iii) are equivalent to the condition that
every disjoint sequence in
\text{\rm sol}(L)
is norm null,
which means that
L
is an
Lwc
subset of
F^{\prime}
.
∎

The following notions are due to K. Andrews,
J. Bourgain, and J. Diestel.

Definition 2.4
.

A bounded subset
A
of
X
is called:

a)

a Dunford–Pettis set (briefly,
A
is
DP
)
if
(f_{n})
is uniformly null on
A
for each

w
-null
(f_{n})
in
X^{\prime}
(see
[
3
, Thm.1]
).

b)

a limited set if
(f_{n})
is uniformly null on
A

for each
w
∗
-null
(f_{n})
in
X^{\prime}
(see
[
6
]
).

In reflexive spaces,
DP
sets and limited sets
agree with relatively compact sets
[
6
]
.
In general,

A\ \text{\rm is relatively compact}\ \Longrightarrow A\ \text{\rm is limited}\ \Longrightarrow A\ \text{\rm is DP}
.
The unit ball
B_{X}
is not limited in
X

unless
\dim(X)<\infty
, and the limited sets
are relatively compact in separable and in reflexive Banach spaces
[
6
]
.

B_{c_{0}}
is not limited in
c_{0}
, yet
\widehat{B_{c_{0}}}
is
limited in
c^{\prime\prime}_{0}=\ell^{\infty}
by Phillip’s lemma.
B_{c_{0}}
is
DP

because
c^{\prime}_{0}=\ell^{1}
has the Schur property.
The
DP
sets turn to limited
while embedded in the bi-dual.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
A\subseteq X
. TFAE:

i)

A
is a
DP
subset of
X
.

ii)

\widehat{A}
is limited in
X^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

i)\Longrightarrow ii)
Assume
A
is a
DP
subset of
X
.
Let
f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
in
X^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
Then
f_{n}|_{X}:=f_{n}|_{\widehat{X}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}}}{{\to}}0
in
X^{\prime}
,
since
g(f_{n}|_{X})=\hat{g}(\widehat{f_{n}|_{X}})=\hat{g}(f_{n})=f_{n}(g)\to 0

for each
g\in X^{\prime\prime}
. By the assumption,
(f_{n}|_{X})
is uniformly null on
A
,
and hence
(f_{n})
is uniformly null on
\widehat{A}
as desired.
Therefore,
\widehat{A}
is limited in
X^{\prime\prime}
.

ii)\Longrightarrow i)
Suppose
\widehat{A}
is limited in
X^{\prime\prime}
.
Let
f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}}}{{\to}}0
in
X^{\prime}
.
Then
\widehat{f_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
in
X^{\prime\prime\prime}

and, as
\widehat{A}
is limited,
(\widehat{f_{n}})
is uniformly null on
\widehat{A}
.
Hence

\sup_{a\in A}|f_{n}(a)|=\sup_{a\in A}|\hat{a}(f_{n})|=\sup_{a\in A}|\widehat{f_{n}}(\hat{a})|=\sup_{b\in\widehat{A}}|\widehat{f_{n}}(b)|\to 0
.
Therefore
(f_{n})
is uniformly null on
A
, which means that
A
is
DP
in
X
.
∎

Proposition 2.6

For a nonempty bounded subset

, TFAE.

i)

ii)

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
T\in\text{\rm L}(X,F)
. TFAE.

i)

T
takes limited subsets of
X
onto
Lwc
subsets of
F
.

ii)

T
takes compact subsets of

onto
Lwc
subsets of
F
.

iii)

\{Tx\}
is an
Lwc
subset of
F
for each

.

iv)

.

v)

T^{\prime}f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
in
X^{\prime}
for each disjoint bounded
sequence
(f_{n})
in
F^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The implications
i)\ \Longrightarrow\ ii)\ \Longrightarrow\ iii)
are trivial,
while
iii)\ \Longrightarrow\ iv)

yields because each
Lwc
subset of
F
lies in
F^{a}
.

iv)\ \Longrightarrow\ v)
:
Let
(f_{n})
be a disjoint bounded sequence in

and
x\in X
.
Since
T(X)\subseteq F^{a}
,
\{Tx\}
is an
Lwc
set, and hence

T^{\prime}f_{n}(x)=f_{n}(Tx)\to 0
by Proposition
2.2
. As

is arbitrary,
(T^{\prime}f_{n})
is
\text{\rm w}^{\ast}
-null.

v)\ \Longrightarrow\ i)
:
Assume in contrary
T(L)
is not an
Lwc
set in

for some non-empty

of

. By Proposition

, there exists
a disjoint sequence

(g_{n})
of

that is not uniformly null on

.
Therefore
(T^{\prime}g_{n})
is not uniformly null on

T^{\prime}g_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm w}^{\ast}}}{{\to}}0
and the limitedness of

.
The obtained contradiction completes the proof.
∎

3
The Banach Lattice Case

Here, in the Banach lattice setting, we investigate the completeness
of linear spans of positive operators introduced in Section 2 in the regular norm.
We begin with some technical notions.
Let
\emptyset\neq{\cal P}\subseteq\text{\rm L}(E,F)
.
The set
{\cal P}
is said to satisfy the
domination property
if

0\leq S\leq T\in{\cal P}\ \Longrightarrow\ S\in{\cal P}
.
We say that
T\in\text{\rm L}(E,F)
is

{\cal P}
-
dominated
, if
\pm T\leq U\in{\cal P}
.
An operator
T:E\to F
is called an
r
-
{\cal P}
-
operator

if
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
, where
T_{1},T_{2}
are positive operators of
{\cal P}
.
We denote the collection of all
r
-
{\cal P}
-operators from

E
to
F
by
\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\cal P}\subseteq\text{\rm L}(E,F)
,
{\cal P}\pm{\cal P}\subseteq{\cal P}\neq\emptyset
,
and
T\in\text{\rm L}(E,F)
. Then the following holds.

i)

T\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)\Longleftrightarrow T\ \text{is a}\ {\cal P}\text{-dominated\ operator\ from}\ {\cal P}
.

ii)
Assume the modulus
|T|
of
T
exists in
\text{\rm L}(E,F)
and

{\cal P}
possesses the domination property. Then

T\in\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}(E,F)\Longleftrightarrow|T|\in{\cal P}
.

Proof.

i)
Let
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
, where
T_{1},T_{2}\in{\cal P}
are positive.

{\cal P}\pm{\cal P}\subseteq{\cal P}
implies
T\in{\cal P}
and

U=T_{1}+T_{2}\in{\cal P}
. From
\pm T\leq U
, we obtain that
T
is
{\cal P}
-dominated.

Now, let
T\in{\cal P}
be
{\cal P}
-dominated.
Take
U\in{\cal P}
such that
\pm T\leq U
.
Since
T=U-(U-T)
, and both
U
and
U-T
are
positive operators in
{\cal P}
,
T
is an
r
-
{\cal P}
-operator.

ii)
First assume
|T|\in{\cal P}
.
Since
T=T_{+}-T_{-}
,
0\leq T_{\pm}\leq|T|\in{\cal P}
, the domination property
implies that
T_{+}
and
T_{-}
are positive operators in
{\cal P}
, and hence
T=T_{+}-T_{-}

is an
r
-
{\cal P}
-operator.

Now, assume
T
is an
r
-
{\cal P}
-operator.
Then there are positive

and

|T|=T_{+}+T_{-}\in{\cal P}

Let

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\cal P}
be a closed in the operator norm subspace
of
\text{\rm L}(E)

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}=\inf\{\|S\|:\pm T\leq S\in{\cal P}\}

{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}(E)
of all

{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}
-operators in

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\geq\|T\|_{r}\geq\|T\|

({\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}},\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}})

Proof.

It should be clear that
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}

\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\geq\|\cdot\|_{r}\geq\|\cdot\|

Take a sequence

WLOG,
\|T_{n+1}-T_{n}\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}<2^{-n}

\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-}{\cal P}}\geq\|\cdot\|

\|T-T_{n}\|\to 0

Since

Pick

\pm(T_{n+1}-T_{n})\leq S_{n}

T_{n+1}x^{+}-T_{n}x^{+}\leq Sx^{+}\ \ \text{and}\ \ -T_{n+1}x^{-}+T_{n}x^{-}\leq Sx^{-}

[formula]

for each

As

for all

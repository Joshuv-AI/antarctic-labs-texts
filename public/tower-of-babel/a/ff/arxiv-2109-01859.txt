On spectral Petrov-Galerkin method for solving fractional initial value problems in weighted Sobolev space
Shengyue Li, Wanrong Cao, Zhaopeng Hao

Abstract




Abstract
:
In this paper, we investigate a spectral Petrov-Galerkin method for fractional initial value problems. Singularities of the solution at the origin inherited from the weakly singular kernel of the fractional derivative are considered, and the regularity is constructed for the solution in weighted Sobolev space. We present an optimal error estimate of the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method, and prove that the convergence order of the method in the weighted
L^{2}
-norm is
3\alpha+1
for smooth source term, where
\alpha
is the order of the fractional derivative.
An iteration algorithm with a quasi-linear complexity is considered to solve the produced linear system. Numerical experiments verify the theoretical findings and show the efficiency of the proposed algorithm, and exhibit that the presented numerical method works well for some time-fractional diffusion equations after suitable temporal semi-discrete.

Keywords
: fractional derivative singularity, weighted Sobolev space, spectral method, optimal error estimates

Introduction

Time-fractional differential equations (TFDEs) have attracted significant attention due to the capability of modeling some long-time memory complex systems and anomalous diffusion problems
[
10
,
33
,
21
,
34
]
. With the increasing application, numerical methods have also been well studied for TFDEs, such as finite difference schemes
[
44
,
11
,
15
,
30
]
, finite element methods
[
28
,
32
,
31
]
, spectral methods
[
27
,
45
,
14
]
, etc. The model TFDE has the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u+Au=g(x,t),&x\in\Omega,\ t\in I,\quad\quad\ \\
&u(x,0)=0,&x\in\Omega,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \\
&u=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t\in I,\ \ \ \ \ \end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval, the linear operator
A
on
\Omega
is positive definite,
g
is a given source term, and
{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative with
0<\alpha<1
, defined by

{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}y(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{y^{\prime}(s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}ds.

By some suitable space discretization, the TFDE (
1.1
) can be converted a fractional initial value problem (FIVP). In Example
6.4
of Section
6
, we give the details of solving (
1.1
) by applying the proposed algorithm in this framework, where
-A
is the Laplacian operator.

As a simple but widely occurring model, the FIVP has been well studied in standard Sobolev space
[
13
,
16
,
27
,
28
,
45
]
. The exact solution of fractional differential equations (FDEs) inherits singularities caused by the weakly singular kernel in fractional derivative operators, so the regularity will be very low in standard Sobolev space even for smooth data. The fact has been revealed in
[
13
,
25
,
26
,
38
]
, which motivated the use of weighted Sobolev space to better incorporate singularities at the endpoints.

Consider the following fractional initial value problem (FIVP)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u+\lambda u&=f(t),\ t\in I=(0,T],\\
u(0)&=0,\end{aligned}

(1.2)

where
\lambda
is a nonnegative constant,
f(t)
is a given function. In spite of the available explicit form of the solution to FIVP (
1.2
), which has been given in
[
16
]
, the exact solution expressed by Mittag-Leffler functions is hard to use directly, either not trivial to observe its regularity.
In this paper, we analyze the regularity of the solution in weighted Sobolev space, and investigate the convergence order of a spectral Petrov-Galerkin method based on the regularity results.

For fractional boundary value problems, the regularity of solutions has been well investigated in weighted Sobolev space, see
[
2
,
3
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
46
]
. For FIVP (
1.2
) with
\lambda=0
, Zhang
[
45
]
has considered its regularity in weighted Sobolev space, and presented an optimal error estimates of spectral Petrov-Galerkin and collocation methods. However, the semi-discrete in temperal of the TFDE (
1.1
) leads to (
1.2
) with
\lambda\neq 0
. Actually, this more general case can provide a framework for dealing with some diverse fractional problems, which appear in plenty of models, e.g., anomalous diffusion model on fractals
[
33
]
, options pricing model in financial market
[
10
]
, viscoelastic models in blood flow
[
34
]
, etc. Hence, it is meaningful to reveal the regularity of solution to the FIVP (
1.2
) in weighted Sobolev space and to design its efficient numerical approximation.

In this work, we present a full regularity analysis in weighted Sobolev space for the FIVP (
1.2
) by using a bootstrapping technique. Specifically, we prove that the regularity index for
t^{-\alpha}u
is
\alpha+\min\{r,2\alpha+1-\varepsilon\}
when
\lambda>0
, where
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary small number and
r\geq 0
is regularity index of
f
in a weighted Sobolev space, see Theorem
3.3
. Moreover, when the source term
f\in C^{1}[0,T]
and vanishes at the origin, we analyze the regularity of the solution by converting the original problem to a weakly singular Volterra integral equation and presenting the expression of the solution. It indicates that the regularity indexes of
t^{-\alpha}u
respectively are
3\alpha+1-\varepsilon
and
3\alpha+3-\varepsilon
for smooth enough source term
f
with
f(0)\not=0
and
f(0)=0
.

In order to avoid the loss of accuracy due to the singularity near the initial or boundary, some advances have been made among the numerical community. Nonuniform grids have been used to keep errors small near the singularity
[
44
,
43
,
32
]
; Correction terms were adopted to present algorithms with globally high-order convergence
[
29
,
11
,
42
]
; Non-polynomial basis functions have been employed to compensate for the weakly singular behavior of solutions at the endpoints and enhance the accuracy of numerical methods
[
12
,
41
,
14
]
.

Over the past two decades, spectral methods based on the nonpolynomial basis functions were proposed for solving fractional model. In
[
39
]
and
[
40
]
, Zayernouri and Karniadakis developed an exponentially accurate fractional spectral collocation method with poly-fractionomials for solving steady-state and time-dependent FDEs. In
[
13
]
, Chen, Shen, and Wang studied approximation properties of generalized Jacobi polynomials in weighted Sobolev space and developed a spectral Petrov-Galerkin method for FIVPs without reaction terms. Recently, in
[
14
]
, the authors developed a spectral Galerkin method in time with the log orthogonal functions for solving subdiffusion equations which shows the spectral accuracy, provided some assumptions on the smoothness of data.

In the current work,
we employ the weighted basis
t^{\alpha}P_{N}(I)
to approximate the solution of FIVP (
1.2
) in the framework of Petrov-Galerkin method, where
P_{N}(I)
is the set of algebraic polynomials of order up to
N
. Though the idea has been widely used to construct spectral methods
[
45
,
13
,
41
]
for FDEs to recover accuracy from the endpoint singularity, the occurrence of the reaction term makes the error estimate in weighted Sobolev space more challenging and complicated. Thanks to the idea of introducing an ultra-weak formulation for fractional elliptic equations
[
23
]
, we consider also a weak Petrov-Galerkin formulation of (
1.2
) and are able to prove the regularity in weighted Sobolev space when the regularity index of
f
is
r\geq-\alpha
. Based on this formulation, we present an optimal error estimate for the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method.
For smooth enough source term
f
, the convergence order, in weighted
L^{2}
-norm, is
3\alpha+3-\varepsilon
if
f(0)=0
, and
3\alpha+1-\varepsilon
if
f(0)\neq 0
, while for relatively rough
f
, the order of
r+\alpha
can be obtained, see Theorem
5.1
.

In summary, the main contributions of this work lie in

•

giving a full regularity analysis of FIVP (
1.2
) in weighted Sobolev space for both smooth and rough data,

•

proving optimal error estimates for the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method without any regularity assumption on the analytical solution,

•

providing a framework to solve TFDEs after suitable semi-discrete in space.

To the best of our knowledge, we are not aware of any other work presenting optimal error estimates of spectral Petrov-Galerkin methods for the FIVP (
1.2
) with
\lambda>0
. Here error estimates are consistent with the regularity results and numerical experiments confirm these theoretical predictions.

The remainder of this paper is outlined as follows. In Section 2, we introduce Jacobi polynomials and fractional Sobolev spaces. The preliminary definitions and necessary lemmas are also given in this section. For the regularity index
r\geq 0
, the regularity of exact solution of the FIVP (
1.2
) is studied in weighted Sobolev space in Section
3
. In Section
4
, the well-posedness and regularity of solution of the weak formulation are presented in weighted Sobolev space for
r\geq-\alpha
. Based on the regularity analysis, the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method is presented and its optimal error estimate is given in section
5
. In section
6
, we present both direct and iteration solvers and give several numerical experiments to verify the theoretical findings, where it is observed that the convergence order and accuracy of the numerical solutions in standard
L^{2}
-norm are higher than its in weighted
L^{2}
-norm, and the presented iteration solver is efficient; see Example
6.1
.

Preliminary

In this section, we recall some necessary notations and definitions of fractional derivatives, Jacobi polynomials, and Sobolev spaces to be used later.

Definition 2.1

(
[
35
]
)
.

For
0<\alpha<1
, the left and right fractional integrals are defined, respectively, as

\displaystyle{}_{0}I_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{u(s)}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}ds,\ t>0,

\displaystyle{}_{t}I_{T}^{\alpha}u(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{T}\frac{u(s)}{(s-t)^{1-\alpha}}ds,\ t<T.

For
0<\alpha<1
, the left and right Caputo fractional derivatives are defined by

{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)={}_{0}I_{t}^{1-\alpha}(Du(t)),\ t>0,\ {}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}u(t)=-{}_{t}I_{T}^{1-\alpha}(Du(t)),\ t<T,

and the left and right Riemann-Liouville fractional derivatives are defined by

{}^{RL}_{\ \ \!0}\!D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=D({}_{0}I_{t}^{1-\alpha}u(t)),\ t>0,\ {}^{RL}_{\ \ t}\!D_{T}^{\alpha}u(t)=-D({}_{t}I_{T}^{1-\alpha}u(t)),\ t<T.

The two definitions are linked by the following relationships

{}^{RL}_{\ \ \!0}\!D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=\frac{u(0)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}}+{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t),\ \ {}^{RL}_{\ \ t}\!D_{T}^{\alpha}u(t)=\frac{u(T)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)(T-t)^{\alpha}}+{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}u(t).

(2.1)

Note that, by virtue of (
2.1
), as the homogeneous condition considered in (
1.2
), the Caputo definition coincides with the Riemann-Liouville version. Hence, many useful tools established by using Riemann-Liouville derivatives can be applied here.

According to the inverse property in
[
16
]
, for any absolutely integrable function
v
and real
\alpha\geq 0
,

\displaystyle{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}{}_{0}I_{t}^{\alpha}v(t)=v(t),\mbox{ a.e. in I}.

(2.2)

Lemma 2.2

(
[
45
]
)
.

Supposing that
u\in H^{\alpha}(I)
,

, and

\displaystyle({}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u,v)=({}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}u,{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha_{2}}v),

(2.3)

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
, and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
35
]
)
.

If
\phi(x)\in L^{p}(I)

\psi(x)\in L^{q}(I)

\displaystyle({}_{0}I_{t}^{\alpha}\phi,\psi)=(\phi,{}_{t}I_{T}^{\alpha}\psi),

Jacobi polynomials

For
\gamma,\beta>-1
,

,

is the classical Jacobi polynomial
[

•

Orthogonality.

The Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}
are mutually orthogonal: for
\gamma,\beta>-1
,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-x)^{\gamma}(1+x)^{\beta}P_{m}^{\gamma,\beta}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}dx=\delta_{mn}|\|P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}\||^{2},

where

\displaystyle|\|P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}\||^{2}=\frac{2^{\gamma+\beta+1}}{2n+\gamma+\beta+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\gamma+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(n+\gamma+\beta+1)}.

•

Fractional integral.

For
\gamma\in R
,
\beta>-1
, the fractional integral of the weighted Jacobi polynomial
[
4
]
is

{}_{-1}I_{x}^{\alpha}((1+x)^{\beta}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(x))=\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta+\alpha+1)}(1+x)^{\beta+\alpha}P_{n}^{\gamma-\alpha,\beta+\alpha}(x),

Hence,

\displaystyle{}_{0}I_{t}^{\alpha}(t^{\beta}Q_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s^{\beta}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(\frac{2s}{T}-1)}{(t-s)^{1-\alpha}}ds

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{\text{Let $s=\frac{(r+1)T}{2}$}}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{-1}^{\frac{2t}{T}-1}\frac{\frac{(r+1)^{\beta}T^{\beta}}{2^{\beta}}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(r)}{[t-\frac{(r+1)T}{2}]^{1-\alpha}}\ \frac{T}{2}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{-1}^{\frac{2t}{T}-1}\frac{(r+1)^{\beta}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(r)}{(\frac{2t}{T}-r-1)^{1-\alpha}}\ \frac{T^{\beta+\alpha}}{2^{\beta+\alpha}}dr

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{\text{Let $\frac{2t}{T}-1=x$}}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{-1}^{x}\frac{(r+1)^{\beta}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(r)}{(x-r)^{1-\alpha}}\ \frac{T^{\beta+\alpha}}{2^{\beta+\alpha}}dr

\displaystyle={}_{-1}I_{x}^{\alpha}((1+x)^{\beta}P_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(x))\ \frac{T^{\beta+\alpha}}{2^{\beta+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta+\alpha+1)}(1+x)^{\beta+\alpha}P_{n}^{\gamma-\alpha,\beta+\alpha}(x)\ \frac{T^{\beta+\alpha}}{2^{\beta+\alpha}}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{\text{Let $x=\frac{2t}{T}-1$}}}\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta+\alpha+1)}(1+\frac{2t}{T}-1)^{\beta+\alpha}P_{n}^{\gamma-\alpha,\beta+\alpha}(\frac{2t}{T}-1)\ \frac{T^{\beta+\alpha}}{2^{\beta+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\beta+\alpha+1)}t^{\beta+\alpha}Q_{n}^{\gamma-\alpha,\beta+\alpha}(t).

(2.6)

Fractional derivative.

Fractional Sobolev space

When

Defined by interpolation.
Following

Equivalent norm in

For any

, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{k}v\|^{2}_{{\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx\geq\int_{\frac{1}{4}T}^{T}\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{\frac{1}{4}T}^{T}(T-x)^{\gamma+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx=C\|D^{k}\widehat{v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{(\gamma+k)}(J)},

and similarly

\|D^{k}v\|^{2}_{{\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}}}\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}T}\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx\geq C\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}T}x^{\beta+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx=C\|D^{k}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{(\beta+k)}(J)}.

Thus
v\in H^{s}_{(\beta)}(J)
and
\widehat{v}\in H^{s}_{(\gamma)}(J)
by the definition of
H^{s}_{(\eta)}(J)
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|D^{k}v\|^{2}_{{\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}T}\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx+\int_{\frac{1}{4}T}^{T}\omega^{\gamma+k,\beta+k}(D^{k}v)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|D^{k}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{(\beta+k)}(J)}+\|D^{k}\widehat{v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{(\gamma+k)}(J)}).

The conclusion is proved when
s
are nonnegative integers.
For
s\in R^{+}
, it can be obtained by the space interpolation theory
[
1
]
.
∎

Regularity

In this section, we investigate the regularity of the solution to (
1.2
) in weighted Sobolev space. Throughout the paper,
C
and

denote generic constants independent of the truncation number

.

The weak formulation of the problem (

) is: give

, to find

\displaystyle a(u,v):=\left({}_{0}D_{t}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u,{}_{t}D_{T}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v\right)+\lambda(u,v)=\langle f,v\rangle,\ \forall\,v\in{}_{0}H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I).

(3.1)

The well-posedness of the problem (
3.1
) can be established refer to
[
17
]
. As no new techniques are used here, we omit the proof and give the result as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

For all
0<\alpha<1
and

, the problem (
3.1
) exists a unique solution
u\in{}_{0}H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
such that

\|u\|_{H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

The regularity in weighted Sobolev space has been proved for the solution of initial value problem
{}_{-1}D_{x}^{\alpha}u=f(x),\ x\in(-1,1)
in
[
45
]
. By using variable substitution, we can obtain the regularity results for the problem (
1.2
) with
\lambda=0
directly.

Theorem 3.2
.

For the problem (
1.2
) with
\lambda=0
, assume that
\gamma>\alpha-1
. If
\omega^{0,\alpha-\beta}f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta-\alpha}}(I)

(r\geq 0)

or

(r\geq 0)

.

Lemma 3.1

(
[

]
)
.

For any
r
such that
1<r<+\infty
and for any real number

the following inequalities hold:
\forall\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{1}|\phi(x)|^{r}x^{\beta}dx\leq\left(\frac{r}{|\beta+1|}\right)^{r}\int_{0}^{1}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{r}x^{\beta+r}dx.\end{aligned}

(3.2)

In
[

]
, the authors give the restrictions of

and

to ensure
x^{p}\phi(x)\in H^{q}_{(\sigma)}(J)
for

with

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

,

,

,

, and

(3.3)

Remark 3.3
.

Except for the different regions considered, the only difference between Lemma

and the Theorem 6.4 in

Actually, for

to bound the integral by using Hardy’s inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{2/3}^{3/2}|1-z|^{-1-2(q-n)}|z^{p-j}-1|^{2}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{2/3}^{1}(1-z)^{-1-2(q-n)}(z^{p-j}-1)^{2}dz+\int_{1}^{3/2}(z-1)^{-1-2(q-n)}(z^{p-j}-1)^{2}dz

However, from Lemma

Lemma 3.4

(
[

)
.

Let

,

, and

(3.4)

where

s
.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\phi(x)\in H^{s}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
with

\omega^{0,\alpha}\phi(x)\in H^{\min(s,2\alpha+1-\varepsilon)}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
with arbitrarily small

.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have that

\phi(x)\in H^{s}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)\Longleftrightarrow\phi(x)\in H^{s}_{(\alpha)}(J)\;\text{and}\;\widehat{\phi}(x)\in H^{s}_{(0)}(J),

(3.5)

where
\widehat{\phi}(x):=\phi(T-x)
.

Let
\Psi(x):=\omega^{0,\alpha}(x)\phi(x)=x^{\alpha}\phi(x)
and denote
\widehat{\Psi}(x):=\Psi(T-x)=\left(T-x\right)^{\alpha}\widehat{\phi}(x)
.

When
\phi(x)\in H^{s}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
, by using (
3.5
) and Lemma
3.2
with
\mu=\alpha
,

, choosing

\Psi(x)\in H^{\min(s,2\alpha+1-\varepsilon)}_{(0)}(J)
.
Moreover, by the definition of space

, we have

\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{H^{s}_{(\alpha)}(J)}\leq C\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{H^{s}_{(0)}(J)},

thus
\widehat{\phi}(x)\in H^{s}_{(\alpha)}(J)
.
Using Lemma
3.4
, it follows that

\|\widehat{\Psi}\|_{H^{s}_{(\alpha)}(J)}\leq\|\omega^{\alpha,0}\|_{C^{\lceil s\rceil}(J)}\|\widehat{\phi}\|_{H^{s}_{(\alpha)}(J)},

that is,
\widehat{\Psi}(x)\in H^{s}_{(\alpha)}(J)
.

Based on above analysis, using Lemma

again, we obtain that
\Psi(x)\in H^{\min(s,2\alpha+1-\varepsilon)}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For the problem (
1.2
) with
\lambda>0
, if
f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\cap H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
with
r\geq 0
, then we have
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in H^{\alpha+\min(2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,r)}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
.

Proof.

For
f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\cap H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
with
r\geq 0
, by Theorem
3.1
, there exists a unique solution
u\in H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
. Note that
H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)\subset H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
. In fact,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|u\|_{H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}&=\|u\|^{2}_{{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}+\iint_{\Omega_{I,3/2}}(T-x)^{\frac{3\alpha}{2}}x^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+\alpha}}dydx\\
&\leq C\Big[\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{I}\int_{I}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{1+\alpha}}dydx\Big]=C\|u\|_{H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.\end{aligned}

Hence, by
u\in H^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
, we have

{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u=f-\lambda u:=\hat{f}\in H^{\min(\frac{\alpha}{2},r)}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
. By using Theorem
3.2
with
\gamma=\beta=\alpha
, we have
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in H^{\alpha+\min(\frac{\alpha}{2},r)}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
.

If
r\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}
, then
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in H^{\frac{3\alpha}{2}}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
,
by using Lemma
3.5
we have
u\in H^{\min(\frac{3\alpha}{2},2\alpha+1-\varepsilon)}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
. Then
\hat{f}\in H^{\min(\frac{3\alpha}{2},2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,r)}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
, and
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in H^{\alpha+\min(\frac{3\alpha}{2},2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,r)}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
.

Similarly, if
r\geq\frac{3\alpha}{2}
, we can follow the argument to lift the regularity. Suppose that after repeating the lifting procedure
k
times, where
k
is the least integer such that
(k+1/2)\alpha\geq 2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,
we have

\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in H^{\alpha+\min((k+1/2)\alpha,2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,r)}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)=H^{\alpha+\min(2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,r)}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I).

∎

Lemma 3.6

(

)
.

Let
v(x)=x^{\mu}
. Then
v\in H^{s}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}(I)
for

.

Remark 3.7
.

It is well known that the FIVP (

) is equivalent to the weakly singular Volterra integral equation

u(t)=g(t)-{}_{0}I_{t}^{\alpha}(\lambda u(t)),\ t\in[0,T].

(3.6)

where

.

(Theorems 6.1.2 and 6.1.6) and

, the solution of (

) is

u(t)=\frac{f(0)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}t^{\alpha}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\cdot t^{k\alpha+1},

where coefficients

are some constants. Thus when

, by Lemma

, the regularity index of solution

is at least

in

4
Petrov-Galerkin formulation

In this section, we introduce a Petrov-Galerkin formulation and study its well-posedness and regularity. The idea has been well used in
[
23
]
. We consider the Petrov-Galerkin ultra-weak formulation of the FIVPs (
1.2
): Given
f\in H^{-\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\ \cap\ H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
, to find
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
such that

b(u,v):=(u,{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}v))+\lambda(u,v)_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}=\langle f,v\rangle_{\omega^{\alpha,0}},\ \forall v\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I),

(4.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}
denotes the
L^{2}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}
duality pair of
H^{-\mu}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}(I)
and
H^{\mu}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}(I)
,
\gamma,\beta>-1
and
\mu\geq 0
.

To establish the well-posedness of this problem, we need to consider the adjoint problem of original problem (
1.2
):

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}z+\lambda z&=g(t),\ t\in I=(0,T),\\
z(T)&=0.\end{aligned}

(4.2)

Its Petrov-Galerkin weak formulation is: Given
g\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)\ \cap\ H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
, to find
\hat{z}\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
such that

\widetilde{a}(\hat{z},w):=({}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}\hat{z}),w)_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}+\lambda(\omega^{\alpha,0}\hat{z},w)_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}=\langle g,w\rangle_{\omega^{0,\alpha}},\ \forall w\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I).

(4.3)

The well-posedness of problem (
4.3
) can be established similarly as that in Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
g\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)\ \cap\ H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
, there exists a unique solution
\hat{z}\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
to (
4.3
) satisfying

\|\hat{z}\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}\leq C\|g\|_{{\omega^{0,\alpha}}}.

(4.4)

Theorem 4.1
.

For
f\in H^{-\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\ \cap\ H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
, there exists a unique solution
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
to (
4.1
) such that

\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{-\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}.

(4.5)

Proof.

The well-posedness of problem (
4.1
) is guaranteed by the well-known Babuška-Aziz theorem
[
5
]
.

To establish the continuity of bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)
on
L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)\times H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
,
we write

\displaystyle v=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}v_{n}^{\alpha,0}Q_{n}^{\alpha,0}(x),\ \forall v\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I).

(4.6)

Let
\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}
. Note that by Stirling’s formula,
\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}\approx n^{\alpha}
, i.e. there exists
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}n^{\alpha}\leq\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}\leq c_{2}n^{\alpha}
.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
4.6
), (
2.8
) and the fact that
h_{n}^{0,\alpha}=h_{n}^{\alpha,0}
, we have

\displaystyle(u,{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}v))

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\|{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}v)\|_{{\omega^{0,\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\Big(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(v_{n}^{\alpha,0})^{2}(\lambda_{n}^{\alpha})^{2}h_{n}^{0,\alpha}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\|v\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}

and

(u,v)_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}\leq\|u\|_{{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}\|v\|_{{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}\leq C\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\|v\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}.

Thus for
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
and
v\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
,

|b(u,v)|\leq C\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\|v\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}.

(4.7)

Moreover, for any
w\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
,
\hat{w}:=\omega^{0,\alpha}w\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
, the weak formulation (
4.3
) can be rewritten as: Given
g\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)\ \cap\ H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
, to find
\hat{z}\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
such that

\widetilde{a}(\hat{z},\omega^{0,-\alpha}\hat{w})=({}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}\hat{z}),\hat{w})+\lambda(\omega^{\alpha,0}\hat{z},\hat{w})=\langle g,\hat{w}\rangle,\ \forall\hat{w}\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I).

Then for any
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
,
\hat{u}:=\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
and thus
\hat{u}\in H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
, by Lemma
4.1
, there exists a unique
\hat{v}\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
such that

\widetilde{a}(\hat{v},\omega^{0,-\alpha}\hat{w})=(\hat{u},\hat{w})=(u,\hat{w})_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}},\ \forall\hat{w}\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I),

(4.8)

with

\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}\leq C\|\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\|_{{\omega^{0,\alpha}}}=C\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}.

(4.9)

Substituting
\hat{w}
by
u
in (
4.8
), we have

b(u,\hat{v})=(u,{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}\hat{v}))+\lambda(u,\hat{v})_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}=\widetilde{a}(\hat{v},\omega^{0,-\alpha}u)=\|u\|^{2}_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}.

According to above derivation, for any
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
, there exists a unique
\hat{v}\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
such that
b(u,\hat{v})=\|u\|^{2}_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}
.
Combining it with (
4.9
) we obtain

\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}\frac{b(u,v)}{\|v\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}}\geq\frac{\|u\|^{2}_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}}{\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}}\geq\frac{1}{C}\|u\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}},\ \forall\ 0\not=u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I).

(4.10)

For any
0\not=v\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)
, taking
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u={}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}v)+\lambda\omega^{\alpha,0}v
in (
4.1
), we have

\sup\limits_{0\not=u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}b(u,v)\geq\|{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}v)+\lambda\omega^{\alpha,0}v\|^{2}_{{\omega^{0,\alpha}}}>0,\ \forall\ 0\not=v\in H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I).

(4.11)

Combining (
4.7
), (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we obtain that there exists a unique solution
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
such that (
4.5
) holds. ∎

The above theorem leads to the following regularity result with more general data.

Theorem 4.2
.

For the problem (
4.1
) with
\lambda=0
, if
f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\cap H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
and
r\geq-\alpha
, then
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in H^{r+\alpha}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
.

Proof.

For
f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\cap H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
with
r\geq-\alpha
, by Theorem
4.1
, we have
u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}(I)
, then
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}(I)
. It is legitimate to write

u=\omega^{0,\alpha}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}Q_{n}^{0,\alpha}(x),\quad f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}Q_{n}^{\alpha,0}(x).

By (
2.7
) and the equation
{}_{0}D^{\alpha}_{t}u=f
, we have

{}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha}u=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}Q_{n}^{\alpha,0}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}Q_{n}^{\alpha,0}(x),

thus
u_{n}=f_{n}/\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}
. By the norm (
2.9
) and using
\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}\approx n^{\alpha}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\right\|_{H_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}^{\alpha+r}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(u_{n}\right)^{2}h_{n}^{0,\alpha}\left(1+n^{2}\right)^{\alpha+r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(f_{n}/\lambda_{n}^{\alpha}\right)^{2}h_{n}^{\alpha,0}\left(1+n^{2}\right)^{\alpha+r}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(f_{n}\right)^{2}h_{n}^{\alpha,0}\left(1+n^{2}\right)^{r}=C\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}^{2}.

(4.12)

This completes the proof.

∎

For
\lambda>0,
the following regularity result can be obtained by the same argument based on the bootstrapping technique used in Theorem
3.3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For the problem (
4.1
) with
\lambda>0
, if
f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\cap H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
with
r\geq-\alpha
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\|\omega^{0,-\alpha}u\|_{H^{\alpha+\min(2\alpha+1-\varepsilon,r)}_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}.

(4.13)

5
Spectral Petrov-Galerkin method

Now, we are in the position to consider a spectral Petrov-Galerkin method for the FIVPs (
1.2
) and present its stability and error estimate.

We define the finite-dimensional spaces,

\displaystyle U_{N}=\{u|u=t^{\alpha}v,v\in V_{N}(I)\},\ V_{N}=P_{N}(I),

(5.1)

where
P_{N}(I)
is the set of all algebraic polynomials of degree at most
N
in
I
.
The spectral Petrov-Galerkin method is: Given
f\in H^{r}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}(I)\ \cap\ H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(I)
with
r\geq-\alpha
, to find
u_{N}\in U_{N}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} b(u_{N},v_{N})=(u_{N},{}_{t}D_{T}^{\alpha}(\omega^{\alpha,0}v_{N}))+\lambda(u_{N},v_{N})_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}=\langle f,v_{N}\rangle_{\omega^{\alpha,0}},\ \forall v_{N}\in V_{N}.\end{aligned}

(5.2)

Let
\pi_{N}^{\gamma,\beta}:L^{2}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}(I)\rightarrow P_{N}(I)
,
\gamma,\ \beta>-1
, is the
L^{2}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}(I)
-orthogonal projection

\displaystyle(\pi_{N}^{\gamma,\beta}u-u,v)_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}=0,\ \forall v\in P_{N}(I),

(5.3)

which can also be expressed by

\displaystyle\pi_{N}^{\gamma,\beta}u(x)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}\hat{u}_{n}Q_{n}^{\gamma,\beta},\ \hat{u}_{n}=\frac{(u,Q_{n}^{\gamma,\beta})_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}}{\|Q_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}\|^{2}_{{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}}}.

(5.4)

To implement the scheme (
5.2
), we need to take
u_{N}
,
v_{N}
of the form

\displaystyle u_{N}=t^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}u_{n}Q_{n}^{0,\alpha},\ \ \ v_{N}=Q_{j}^{\alpha,0},\ \ j=0,1,\cdots,N.

(5.5)

Before presenting our theoretical analysis, we need the following projection error estimate.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
20
]
)
.

For any
v\in H_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}^{r}(I)
and for all
0\leq r_{1}\leq r
,

\displaystyle\|\pi_{N}^{\gamma,\beta}v-v\|_{H^{r_{1}}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}}\leq C(N(N+\gamma+\beta))^{\frac{r_{1}-r}{2}}|v|_{H^{r}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}},

(5.6)

where
C
is a generic positive constant independent of any function
v
,
N
,
\gamma
,
\beta
.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
22
]
)
.

For any
\omega^{-\gamma,-\beta}v\in H_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}^{r}(I)
with
0\leq r\leq N
,

\displaystyle\|\omega^{\gamma,\beta}\pi_{N}^{\gamma,\beta}(\omega^{-\gamma,-\beta}v)-v\|_{{\omega^{-\gamma,-\beta}}}\leq CN^{-r}|\omega^{-\gamma,-\beta}v|_{H^{r}_{\omega^{\gamma,\beta}}},

(5.7)

where
C
is a generic positive constant independent of any function
v
,
N
,
\gamma
,
\beta
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
f\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{r}(I)\cap H^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
with
r\geq-\alpha,
there exists a unique solution
u_{N}
to (
5.2
) such that for sufficiently large
N

\left\|u_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq C\|f\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{-\alpha}}.

Moreover, suppose u solves
\eqref{uweak},
then we have the error estimate

\left\|u-u_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq CN^{-m}\|f\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{r}},

where
m
is the regularity index of
\omega^{0,-\alpha}u
in
H_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}^{m}(I)
.

Proof.

We first prove the well-posedness of the discrete problem (
5.2
). Note that by (
2.8
) and definition (
5.4
) of projection
\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}
,

b(u_{N},v-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)=\lambda(u_{N},v-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}.

(5.8)

For
0\not=u_{N}\in U_{N}
, by (
4.10
), we have

\frac{1}{C}\|u_{N}\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{b(u_{N},v)}{\|v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}=\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{b(u_{N},v-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)}{\|v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}+\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{b(u_{N},\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)}{\|v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}

(5.9)

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Lemma
5.1
, taking
r_{1}=0
,
r=\alpha
, we obtain

\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{\lambda(u_{N},v-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}{\|v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{\lambda\|u_{N}\|_{{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}\|v-\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v\|_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}{\|v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}\leq c\lambda N^{-\alpha}\|u_{N}\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}.

(5.10)

Using the equivalent norm (
2.9
) we have

\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{b(u_{N},\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)}{\|v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}\leq\sup\limits_{0\not=v\in H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}\frac{b(u_{N},\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v)}{\|\pi_{N}^{\alpha,0}v\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}=\sup\limits_{0\not=v_{N}\in V_{N}}\frac{b(u_{N},v_{N})}{\|v_{N}\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}

(5.11)

By (
5.8
), substituting (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) into (
5.9
), it follows that

\sup\limits_{0\not=v_{N}\in V_{N}}\frac{b(u_{N},v_{N})}{\|v_{N}\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{\alpha}}}\geq(\frac{1}{C}-c\lambda N^{-\alpha})\left\|u_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}},\quad\forall 0\neq u_{N}\in U_{N}.

(5.12)

For sufficiently large
N
, the inf-sup condition holds, which leads to the desired
conclusion.

Next we present the error estimate of the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method.
For any
\phi_{N}\in U_{N}
, we have

\left\|u-u_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq\left\|u-\phi_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}+\left\|u_{N}-\phi_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}},\ \forall\ \phi_{N}\in U_{N}

By (
5.12
) and the fact that for any
v_{N}\in V_{N}
,
b\left(u_{N}-u,v_{N}\right)=0
,

\left\|u_{N}-\phi_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq C\sup_{0\neq v_{N}\in V_{N}}\frac{b\left(u_{N}-\phi_{N},v_{N}\right)}{\left\|v_{N}\right\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}}=C\sup_{0\neq v_{N}\in V_{N}}\frac{b\left(u-\phi_{N},v_{N}\right)}{\left\|v_{N}\right\|_{H^{\alpha}_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}}}\leq C\left\|u-\phi_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}

Taking
\phi_{N}=\omega^{0,\alpha}\pi_{N}^{0,\alpha}(\omega^{0,-\alpha}u)
, by (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and Lemma
5.2
, we obtain

\left\|u-u_{N}\right\|_{{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}\leq CN^{-m}|\omega^{0,-\alpha}u|_{H_{\omega^{0,\alpha}}^{m}}\leq CN^{-m}\|f\|_{H_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}^{r}}.

∎

6
Numerical example

In this section, we firstly present a fast iteration algorithm for the linear system produced by the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method, which is based on the fast polynomial transform and allows quasilinear computational cost
O(N\log^{2}N)
and linear storage
O(N)
. Secondly, we provide three numerical examples to verify the theoretical findings. In Example
6.1
, the smooth source term
f
is adopted, and
f
with a weak singularity at an interior in Example
6.2
,
f
with weak singularity at the origin in Example
6.3
. It is shown from the data that the numerical results are consistent with the conclusions in Theorem
5.1
, Theorem
3.3
and Remark
3.7
, correspondingly. Moreover, a time fractional diffusion problem with the operator
A=-\Delta
in (
1.1
) is considered in Example
6.4
to verify that our theoretical analysis and numerical method are valid for some time-fractional differential problems.

In the computation, we take
\lambda=1
. Since exact solutions are unavailable, we measure the errors in the following sense:

E_{N}=\frac{\|u_{N}-u_{ref}\|_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}}{\|u_{ref}\|_{\omega^{0,-\alpha}}},

where
u_{ref}
is the reference solution computed by the same solver but with a very fine resolution,
u_{ref}:=u_{1024}
.

6.1
Numerical implementation

In this part, we describe the numerical implementation of the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method and present a fast iterative solver inspired by the related discussion in
[
37
]
and
[
23
]
.

Substituting (
5.5
) into the spectral Petrov-Galerkin scheme (
5.2
) and using properties of Jacobi polynomial, we observe

AU=F,

(6.1)

where
U=(u_{0},u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})^{T}
,
F=(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{N})^{T}
with
f_{k}=(f,Q^{\alpha,0}_{k})_{\omega^{\alpha,0}}
,
k=0,1,\cdots,N
. Here
A=S+\lambda M
, where
S
is a diagonal matrix

S=\text{diag}(\lambda_{0}^{\alpha}h_{0}^{\alpha,0},\lambda_{1}^{\alpha}h_{1}^{\alpha,0},\cdots,\lambda_{N}^{\alpha}h_{N}^{\alpha,0})^{T}

and
M
is a dense matrix with the following entries

M_{k,n}=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\alpha,\alpha}Q_{n}^{0,\alpha}(x)Q_{k}^{\alpha,0}(x)dx,\ k,n=0,1,\cdots,N.

To solve (
6.1
) directly, the Gauss-Jacobi quadrature rules can be employ to obtain
f_{k}
and
M_{k,n}
. As the system is dense, a direct solver requires
O(N^{2})
storage and
O(N^{3})
computational complexity.

Based on the analysis in the previous sections, the convergence order and accuracy of the numerical solution are relatively low with rough right-hand function and small
\alpha
. In this case, we need to take large truncation number
N
to improve the accuracy of the numerical solution, which will increase the computational cost of the direct solver significantly.

To overcome this drawback, following the idea in
[
23
]
we use the fixed-point iteration

U^{m+1}=U^{m}+P^{-1}(F-AU^{m}),

(6.2)

where the preconditioner
P=S+\lambda Q
is a diagonal matrix with
Q=\text{diag}(h_{0}^{\alpha,\alpha},h_{1}^{\alpha,\alpha},\cdots,h_{N}^{\alpha,\alpha})
and the initial guess can be chosen as the numerical solution obtained by a direct method with
N=8
. The iterations end when the maximum iteration number
100
is reached or the condition
\|U^{m+1}-U^{m}\|_{2}/\|U^{m+1}\|_{2}<\varepsilon
is met, where we take
\varepsilon=10^{-7}
and
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{2}
denotes the classical Euclidean norm. To contain the information of the reaction term,
Q
is introduced in the current preconditioner to replace the identity matrix used in the existing literature. The results in Table
2
indicate that the new preconditioner can gurantee that iteration numbers are independent of
\alpha
and
N
.

In each iteration, we compute the matrix-vector product without forming a matrix by applying the fast polynomial transform and the fast matrix-vector product for Toeplitz-dot-Hankel matrix
[
37
]
.
Let
Q^{\gamma,\beta}=(Q_{0}^{\gamma,\beta},Q_{1}^{\gamma,\beta},\cdots,Q_{N}^{\gamma,\beta})^{T}
,
C^{\gamma\rightarrow\sigma,\beta}
and
C^{\sigma,\delta\rightarrow\beta}
are lower triangular matrices with constant entries

(C^{\gamma\rightarrow\sigma,\beta})_{n,k}=c_{n,k}^{\gamma\rightarrow\sigma,\beta},\ (C^{\sigma,\delta\rightarrow\beta})_{n,k}=c_{n,k}^{\sigma,\delta\rightarrow\beta},\ n,k=0,1\cdots,N,

where
c_{n,k}^{\gamma\rightarrow\sigma,\beta}
and
c_{n,k}^{\sigma,\delta\rightarrow\beta}
satisfy
[
4
]

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\gamma,\beta}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}^{\gamma\rightarrow\sigma,\beta}Q_{k}^{\sigma,\beta}(x)\ \mbox{and}\ Q_{n}^{\sigma,\delta}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}^{\sigma,\delta\rightarrow\beta}Q_{k}^{\sigma,\beta}(x).

(6.3)

That is,
Q^{\gamma,\beta}=C^{\gamma\rightarrow\sigma,\beta}Q^{\sigma,\beta},\ Q^{\sigma,\delta}=C^{\sigma,\delta\rightarrow\beta}Q^{\sigma,\beta}
. According to the above representation, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} M&=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\alpha,\alpha}Q^{\alpha,0}(Q^{0,\alpha})^{T}dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\alpha,\alpha}C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}Q^{\alpha,\alpha}(C^{0\rightarrow\alpha,\alpha}Q^{\alpha,\alpha})^{T}dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\omega^{\alpha,\alpha}C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}Q^{\alpha,\alpha}(Q^{\alpha,\alpha})^{T}(C^{0\rightarrow\alpha,\alpha})^{T}dx\\
&=C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}H^{\alpha}(C^{0\rightarrow\alpha,\alpha})^{T},\end{aligned}

where
H^{\alpha}=\text{diag}(h_{0}^{\alpha,\alpha},h_{1}^{\alpha,\alpha},\cdots,h_{N}^{\alpha,\alpha})
. Denote
u_{N}=t^{\alpha}\hat{u}_{N}
, by using (
6.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{N}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}u_{n}Q_{n}^{0,\alpha}(x)=(Q^{0,\alpha})^{T}U=(Q^{\alpha,\alpha})^{T}(C^{0\rightarrow\alpha,\alpha})^{T}U:=(Q^{\alpha,\alpha})^{T}U^{\alpha,\alpha}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{N}u_{n}^{\alpha,\alpha}Q_{n}^{\alpha,\alpha}(x)

(6.4)

where
U^{\alpha,\alpha}=(u_{0}^{\alpha,\alpha},u_{1}^{\alpha,\alpha},\cdots,u_{N}^{\alpha,\alpha})^{T}
can be observed by the fast polynomial transform
[
37
]
.
Note that from
[
4
]

c_{n,k}^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}=\frac{(-1)^{n-k}(2k+2\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+2\alpha+1)\Gamma(n-k-\alpha)\Gamma(n+k+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(-\alpha)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(n+k+2\alpha+2)}.

Thus, for the connection coefficients matrix
C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}
we can write

\displaystyle C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}=(T\circ H)D,

(6.5)

where
D
is diagonal matrix,
T
is a Toeplitz matrix,
H
is a Hankel matrix and ‘
\circ
’ is the Hadamard matrix product, i.e., entrywise multiplication between two matrices. Specifically, for
0\leq k\leq n\leq N
,

\displaystyle(D)_{k,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2k+2\alpha+1)\Gamma(k+2\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(-\alpha)\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)},

\displaystyle(T)_{n,k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-k}\frac{\Gamma(n-k-\alpha)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)},\ (H)_{n,k}=\frac{\Gamma(n+k+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+k+2\alpha+2)}.

Using (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), it follows that

MU=C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}H^{\alpha}(C^{0\rightarrow\alpha,\alpha})^{T}U=C^{\alpha,0\rightarrow\alpha}H^{\alpha}U^{\alpha,\alpha}=(T\circ H)DH^{\alpha}U^{\alpha,\alpha}:=(T\circ H)v.

Now we are in the position to use the pivoted Cholesky algorithm approximating the Hankel matrix
H
by a low rank matrix and the fast Toeplitz matrix-vector product. As shown in
[
37
]
, the present fast iteration solver allows the quasilinear computational cost
O(N\log^{2}N)
and the linear storage
O(N)
.

Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied a spectral Petrov-Galerkin method for fractional initial value problems with Caputo fractional derivative, which can provide an efficient framework for dealing with some time-fractional differential problems.
To capture the singularity of the solution at the origin, we have analyzed the regularity of the solution in weighted Sobolev space by using a bootstrapping technique.
When
\lambda>0
, if the regularity index of
f
is
r\geq-\alpha
in weighted Sobolev space, the regularity index of
t^{-\alpha}u
is
\alpha+\min(r,2\alpha+1-\varepsilon)
, where
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary small number and
\alpha
is the fractional order. Moreover, the stability and an optimal error estimate of the spectral Petrov-Galerkin method have been established in a weighted
L^{2}
-norm.

Numerical examples verify the theoretical findings for the FIVPs (
1.2
) with source terms which are smooth enough (see Example
6.1
), with weak singularity at an interior (see Example
6.2
) and with weak singularity at the origin (see Example
6.3
). That is, the tested convergence orders in the weighted
L^{2}
-norm are correspondingly consistent with the expected ones in Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
5.1
and Remark
3.7
. In additional, it is exhibited that our theoretical analysis and numerical method are also applicable for a large class of problems including the time-fractional diffusion equations (see Example
6.4
).

To implement the presented spectral Petrov-Galerkin method efficiently, we have also presented a fast iteration algorithm for solving the resulting linear system, which reduces the computational cost from
O(N^{3})
to
O(N\log^{2}N)
and the storage from
O(N^{2})
to
O(N)
, compared with the direct solver.

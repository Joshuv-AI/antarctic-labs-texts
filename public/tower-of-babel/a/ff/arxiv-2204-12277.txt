On regularity and existence of weak solutions to nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck type equations with rough coefficients
Prashanta Garain and Kaj Nyström

Abstract
. We consider nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck type equations of the form (0.1) ( ∂ t + X ⋅ ∇ Y ) u = ∇ X ⋅ ( A ( ∇ X u , X , Y , t ) ) . (\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u,X,Y,t)). The function A = A ⁡ ( ξ , X , Y , t ) : ℝ m × ℝ m × ℝ m × ℝ → ℝ m A=A(\xi,X,Y,t):\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m} is assumed to be continuous with respect to ξ \xi , and measurable with respect to X , Y X,Y and t t . A = A ⁡ ( ξ , X , Y , t ) A=A(\xi,X,Y,t) is allowed to be nonlinear but with linear growth. We establish higher integrability and local boundedness of weak sub-solutions, weak Harnack and Harnack inequalities, and Hölder continuity with quantitative estimates. In addition we establish existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to a Dirichlet problem in certain bounded X X , Y Y and t t dependent domains. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35K65, 35K70, 35H20, 35R03. Keywords and phrases: Kolmogorov equation, parabolic, ultraparabolic, hypoelliptic, nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equations, existence, uniqueness, regularity.



1.
Introduction and statement of main results

Several important evolution equations arising in kinetic theory, mathematical physics and probability can be written in the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f=\mathcal{Q}(f,\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t),

where
(X,Y,t):=(x_{1},...,x_{m},y_{1},...,y_{m},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
N=2m
,
m\geq 1
, and the coordinates
X=(x_{1},...,x_{m})
and
Y=(y_{1},...,y_{m})
are,
respectively, the velocity and the position of the system. In its simplest form,

\mathcal{Q}(f,\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t)=\nabla_{X}\cdot\nabla_{X}f=\Delta_{X}f,

the equation in (
1.1
) was introduced and studied by Kolmogorov in a famous note published in 1934 in Annals of Mathematics, see
[
25
]
. In this case Kolmogorov noted that the equation in (
1.1
) is an example of a degenerate parabolic
operator having strong regularity properties and he proved that the equation has a fundamental solution which is smooth off its diagonal. In fact, in this case the equation
in (
1.1
) is hypoelliptic, see
[
24
]
.

In kinetic theory,
f
represents the evolution of a particle
distribution

f(X,Y,t):U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R},\quad U_{X},\ U_{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},

subject to geometric restrictions and models for the interactions and collisions between particles. In this case the left-hand side in (
1.1
) describes the evolution of
f
under the action of transport, with the free
streaming operator. The right-hand side describes elastic collisions through the nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator. The Boltzmann equation is an integro-
(partial)-differential equation with nonlocal operator in the kinetic variable
X
. The
Boltzmann equation is a fundamental equation in kinetic theory in the sense that it has been derived rigorously, at least in some settings, from microscopic first
principles. In the case of so called Coulomb interactions the Boltzmann collision operator is
ill-defined and Landau proposed an alternative operator for these interactions, this operator is now called the Landau or the Landau-Coulomb operator. This operator can be stated as in (
1.1
) with

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t)=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(f)\nabla_{X}f+B(f)f),

where again
A(f)=A(f)(X,Y,t)
and
B(f)=B(f)(X,Y,t)
are nonlocal operators in the variable
X
. In this case the equation in (
1.1
) is a nonlinear, or rather quasilinear, drift-diffusion equation with coefficients given by convolution like averages of the unknown. As mentioned above the Landau equation is considered fundamental because of its close link to the Boltzmann equation for Coulomb interactions.

In the case of long-range interactions, the Boltzmann and Landau-Coulomb operators show local ellipticity provided
the solution enjoys some pointwise bounds on the associated hydrodynamic fields and the local entropy. Indeed, assuming certain uniform in
(Y,t)\in U_{Y}\times I
bounds on local mass, energy, and entropy, see
[
30
,
33
]
, one can prove that

\displaystyle 0<\Lambda^{-1}I\leq A(f)(X,Y,t)\leq\Lambda I,\quad|B(f)(X,Y,t)|\leq\Lambda,

for some constant
\Lambda\geq 1
and for
(X,Y,t)\in U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I
, i.e., under these assumptions the operator
\mathcal{Q}
in (
1.2
) and in the Landau equation becomes locally uniformly elliptic. As a consequence, and as global well posedness for the Boltzmann
equation and the construction of solutions in the large is an outstanding open problem, the study of conditional regularity for the Boltzmann and Landau equations has become a way to make progress on the regularity issues for these equations. We refer to
[
11
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
28
,
33
,
36
,
37
]
for more on the connections between Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equations, the Boltzmann and Landau equation, statistical physics and conditional regularity.

Based on the idea of conditional regularity one is lead to study the local regularity of weak solutions to the equation in
(
1.1
) with

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t)=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(X,Y,t)\nabla_{X}f)+B(X,Y,t)\nabla_{X}f,

assuming that
A
is measurable, bounded and uniformly elliptic, and that
B
is bounded. In
[
20
]
, see also
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
for subsequent developments, the authors extended, for equations as in (
1.1
) assuming (
1.3
), the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser (DGNM) theory, which in its original form only considers elliptic
or parabolic equations in divergence form, to hypoelliptic equations with rough coefficients including the one in (
1.1
) assuming (
1.3
).
[
20
]
has spurred considerable activity in the field, see below for a literature review, as the results proved give the correct scale- and translation-invariant estimates for local Hölder continuity and the Harnack inequality for weak solutions.

In this paper we consider equations as in (
1.1
) with

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(f,\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t)=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t)),

subject to conditions on
A
which allow
A
to be a nonlinear function of
\nabla_{X}f
. In this case we refer to the equations in (
1.1
) as nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck type equations with rough coefficients. Our contributions is twofold. First, we establish higher integrability (Theorem
1.1
) and local boundedness (Theorem
1.2
) of weak sub-solutions, weak Harnack and Harnack
inequalities (Theorem
1.3
), and Hölder continuity with quantitative estimates (Theorem
1.4
), for the equation

(1.5)

(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u,X,Y,t)).

Second, we establish existence and uniqueness, in certain bounded
X
,
Y
and
t
dependent domains, for a Dirichlet problem
involving the equation in (
1.5
)
also allowing for boundary data and a right hand side (Theorem
1.5
). In the linear case, if
A(X,Y,t)
is a uniformly elliptic positive definite matrix with bounded measurable coefficients, then
A(\xi,X,Y,t)=A(X,Y,t)\xi
satisfies the hypothesis we impose on the symbol
A
, and in this case the equation in (
1.5
) reduces to the equation

(1.6)

(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(X,Y,t)\nabla_{X}u).

Concerning regularity, our results therefore generalize
[
20
]
,
[
22
,
23
]
, to nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck type equations with rough coefficients.

To the best of our knowledge, nonlinear equations of the form in (
1.5
) have so far not been investigated in the literature, and the purpose of this paper is to contribute to the regularity and existence theory for these equations. We believe that generalizations of the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser (DGNM) theory to nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck type equations with rough coefficients are relevant and interesting. We also believe that our treatment of the Dirichlet problem is new and enlightening.

1.1.
The symbol

We consider equations as in (
1.5
) subject to conditions on

. Concerning the symbol

, where

\mathbb{R}^{m}

Definition 1
.

Let
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then

is said to belong to the class
M(\Lambda)
if

A=A(\xi,X,Y,t):\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is continuous with respect to
\xi
, measurable with respect to
X,Y
and
t
, and

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad|A(\xi,X,Y,t)|\leq\Lambda|\xi|,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad A(\xi,X,Y,t)\cdot\xi\geq\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{2},

(1.7)

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad A(\lambda\xi,X,Y,t)=\lambda A(\xi,X,Y,t)\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},

for almost every
(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Lambda\in[1,\infty)
. Then
A
is said to belong to the class
R(\Lambda)
if

A=A(\xi,X,Y,t):\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is continuous with respect to
\xi
, measurable with respect to
X,Y
and
t
, and

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad|A(\xi_{1},X,Y,t)-A(\xi_{2},X,Y,t)|\leq\Lambda|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|,

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad(A(\xi_{1},X,Y,t)-A(\xi_{2},X,Y,t))\cdot(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})\geq\Lambda^{-1}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2},

(1.8)

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad A(\lambda\xi,X,Y,t)=\lambda A(\xi,X,Y,t)\quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},

for almost every
(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and for all
\xi_{1},\,\xi_{2},\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that (
2
)-
(iii)
implies that
A(0,X,Y,t)=0
for a.e.
(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Hence we deduce from (
2

(i),\,(ii)
and
(iii)
that

R(\Lambda)\subset M(\Lambda)
.

1.2.
Dilations and group law

We will often use the notation
(Z,t)=(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
to denote points. The natural family of dilations for our operators and equations,
(\delta_{r})_{r>0}
, on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
is defined by

(1.9)

\delta_{r}(X,Y,t)=(rX,r^{3}Y,r^{2}t),

for
(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
r>0
. Our classes of operators are closed under the group law

(1.10)

(\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})\circ(Z,t)=(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{t})\circ(X,Y,t)=(\tilde{X}+X,\tilde{Y}+Y+t\tilde{X},\tilde{t}+t),

where
(Z,t),\ (\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Note that

(1.11)

(Z,t)^{-1}=(X,Y,t)^{-1}=(-X,-Y+tX,-t),

and hence

(1.12)

(\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})^{-1}\circ(Z,t)=(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{t})^{-1}\circ(X,Y,t)=(X-\tilde{X},Y-\tilde{Y}-(t-\tilde{t})\tilde{X},t-\tilde{t}),

whenever
(Z,t),\ (\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Given
(Z,t)=(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
we let

(1.13)

\|(Z,t)\|=\|(X,Y,t)\|:=|(X,Y)|\!+|t|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad|(X,Y)|=\big|X\big|+\big|Y\big|^{1/3}.

Given
{r}>0
and
(\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})=(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, we let

(1.14)

\displaystyle Q_{r}:=\{(X,Y,t):|X|<{r},|Y|<{r}^{3},-{r}^{2}<t<0\},\quad Q_{r}(\tilde{Z},\tilde{t}):=(\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})\circ Q_{r}.

We refer to

as a cylinder centered at
(\tilde{Z},\tilde{t})
and of radius

.

1.5.
Known regularity results

As mentioned, the equation in (
1.6
), possibly also allowing for lower order terms, has attracted considerable attention in recent years. Anceschi-Cinti-Pascucci-Polidoro-Ragusa
[
2
,
12
,
34
]
proved local boundedness of weak sub-solutions of (
1.6
) and some versions thereof. Their approach is based on the Moser’s iteration technique, the use of fundamental solutions and a Sobolev type inequality is crucial. It is worth noting that while the results in these papers are stated assuming only bounded and measurable coefficients, an implicit regularity assumption on the coefficients is imposed as the authors use a stronger notion of weak solutions assuming also

(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. It is unclear for what assumptions on the coefficients such weak solutions can be constructed. Bramanti-Cerutti-Manfredini-Polidoro-Ragusa
[
8
,
32
,
35
]
proved
L^{p}
estimates, interior Sobolev regularity and local Hölder continuity of weak solutions of (
1.6
) imposing additional assumptions on the coefficients beyond bounded, measurable and elliptic. In fact it was only recently that Golse-Imbert-Mouhot-Vasseur
[
20
]
proved local boundedness, Harnack inequality and local Hölder continuity of (true) weak solutions of (
1.6
) based on De-Giorgi and Moser’s iteration technique. Still, it seems unclear to us how the authors actually resolve questions concerning the existence of weak solutions unless smooth coefficients are assumed qualitatively. However, subsequent developments have appeared in
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
. A weak Harnack inequality for weak super-solutions of (
1.6
) has been obtained by Guerand-Imbert
[
22
]
and this has been generalized by Anceschi-Rebucci
[
3
]
. In
[
23
]
, Guerand-Mouhot revisited the theory for the linear equation in (
1.6
), also allowing for lower order terms, and gave lucid, novel and short proofs of the De Giorgi
intermediate-value Lemma, weak Harnack and Harnack
inequalities, and the Hölder continuity with quantitative
estimates.
[
23
]
is an essentially self-contained account of the linear theory. Local Hölder continuity results are also proved in Wang-Zhang
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
for various linear analogues of (
1.6
). We emphasize that all results mentioned concern linear equations. Zhu
[
41
]
proved local boundedness and local Hölder continuity of weak solutions of (
1.6
) when the drift term
\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y}
is replaced by
\partial_{t}+b(X)\cdot\nabla_{Y}
for some nonlinear function

1.6.
Known existence results

Boundary value problems for equations as in (
1.6
) but in non-divergence form were studied by Manfredini
[
31
]
who proved existence of strong solutions for the Dirichlet problem assuming Hölder continuous coefficients. Lanconelli-Lascialfari-Morbidelli
[

,
27
]
considered a quasilinear case, still in non-divergence form, allowing the coefficients to depend not only on
(X,Y,t)
but also the solution
u
, and as a function of
(X,Y,t)
the coefficients are assumed to be with Hölder continuous. In fact, functional analytic approaches to weak solutions to Kramers equation and Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equations have only recently been developed.
Albritton-Armstrong-Mourrat-Novack
[
1
]
have developed a functional analytic approach to study well-posedness of Kramers equation, and its parabolic analogue

(1.24)

\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{X}u+X\cdot\nabla_{X}u+X\cdot\nabla_{Y}u+b\cdot\nabla_{X}u=g^{*},

for suitable
g^{*}
. Equation (
1.24
) is often referred to as the kinetic Fokker-Planck equation. Litsgård-Nyström
[
29
]
studied existence and uniqueness results for the (linear) Dirichlet problem associated with (
1.6
), with rough coefficients
A
. In particular, in
[
29
]
Theorem
1.5
is proved in the case when

A(\xi,X,Y,t)=A(X,Y,t)\xi
. However, existence and uniqueness for (
1.5
) do not seem to have been studied in the literature so far. It is important to note
that Theorem
1.5
states, similar to
[
29
]
, the existence of a unique weak solution

to the problem in
(
1.21
) in the sense of Definition
4
below. The latter is, as it assumes no knowledge of underlying traces, trace spaces and extension operators in the functional setting considered, a weaker formulation of the Dirichlet problem compared to what one usually aims for. Indeed, this is one way to formulate a weak form of the
Dirichlet problem which circumvents a largely open problem in the context of kinetic Fokker-Planck equations, linear as well as non-linear, and that is the problem of a well defined trace operator and trace inequality. We refer to Section
6
for more.

1.7.
Proofs

2.1.
Function spaces

We denote by
{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
the Sobolev
space of functions
g\in L^{2}(U_{X})
whose distributional gradient in
U_{X}
lies in
(L^{2}(U_{X}))^{m}
, i.e.

\displaystyle{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X}):=\{g\in L_{X}^{2}(U_{X})\mid\nabla_{X}g\in(L^{2}(U_{X}))^{m}\},

and we set

||g||_{{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})}:=\bigl(||g||_{L^{2}(U_{X})}^{2}+||\,|\nabla_{X}g|\,||_{L^{2}(U_{X})}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2},\ g\in{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X}).

We let
{H}_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})
denote the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(U_{X})
in the norm of
{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
and we recall, as
U_{X}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, that

C^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}})
is dense in
{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
. In particular, equivalently we could define
{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
as the closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}})

in the norm
||\cdot||_{{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})}
. Note that as
{H}_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})
is a Hilbert space it is reflexive, hence
({H}_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X}))^{\ast}=H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})
and
(H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))^{\ast}={H}_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})
, where
()^{\ast}
denotes the dual.
Based on this we let
{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})
denote the dual to
{H}_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})
acting on functions in
{H}_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})
through the
duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}),H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})}
. We let
L^{2}_{Y,t}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X}))
be the space of
measurable function
u:V_{Y,t}\to H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})
equipped with the norm

||u||^{2}_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))}:=\iint_{V_{Y,t}}||u(\cdot,Y,t)||_{{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

L^{2}_{Y,t}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))
is defined analogously. In analogy with the definition of
{H}_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
, we let
W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
be the closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
in the norm

(2.1)

\displaystyle||u||_{W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}

\displaystyle:=\bigl(||u||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))}^{2}+||(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}.

In particular,
W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
is a Banach space and
u\in W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
if and only if

(2.2)

\displaystyle u\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})).

Note that the dual of
L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X}))
, denoted by
(L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})))^{\ast}
, satisfies

(L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})))^{\ast}=L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})),

and, as mentioned above,

(L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})))^{\ast}=L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})).

Finally, the spaces
L_{Y,t,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(U_{X}))
,
L_{Y,t,\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X,\mathrm{loc}}^{-1}(U_{X}))
, and
W_{\mathrm{loc}}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
are defined in the natural way. The topological boundary of
U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
is denoted by
\partial(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
. Let
N_{Y,t}
denote the outer unit normal to
V_{Y,t}
. We define a subset

\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})\subset\partial(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
, the Kolmogorov boundary of
U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
, as in (
1.22
). We let
C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
and
C^{\infty}_{X,0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
be the set of functions in

C^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
which vanish on
\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
and
\{(X,Y,t)\in\partial{U_{X}}\times\overline{V_{Y,t}}\}
, respectively. We let
W_{0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
and
W_{X,0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
denote the closure in the norm of

of

and

2.2.
Weak solutions

We here introduce the notion of weak solutions.

Definition 3
.

Let
g^{*}\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))
. A function

is said to be a weak sub-solution (or super-solution) to the equation

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u-\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u,X,Y,t))+g^{*}=0\text{ in }\ U_{X}\times V_{Y,t},

if for every
V_{X}\times V_{Y}\times J\Subset U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
, and for all non-negative
\phi\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y}\times J,H_{X,0}^{1}(V_{X}))
, we have

\displaystyle\iiint_{V_{X}\times V_{Y}\times J}A(\nabla_{X}u,X,Y,t)\cdot\nabla_{X}\phi\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\iint_{V_{Y}\times J}\ \langle g^{\ast}(\cdot,Y,t)+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u(\cdot,Y,t),\phi(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 0\quad(\text{ or }\geq).

We say that
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
is a weak solution to the equation (
2.3
) if equality holds in (
3
) without a sign restriction on
\phi
.

Note that if
u
is a weak sub-solution (or super-solution) of (
2.3
) in the sense of Definition
3
above, with
g^{\ast}\equiv 0
, then

\displaystyle\iint_{V_{X}\times V_{Y}}u(X,Y,t_{2})\phi(X,Y,t_{2})\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y-\iint_{V_{X}\times V_{Y}}u(X,Y,t_{1})\phi(X,Y,t_{1})\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint u(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})\phi\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\iint A(\nabla_{X}u,X,Y,t)\cdot\nabla_{X}\phi\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq 0\quad(\text{ or }\geq),

whenever
\phi\in C^{\infty}((t_{1},t_{2}),C^{\infty}_{0}(V_{X}\times V_{Y}))
, is non-negative function.
Furthermore, equality holds in (
2.2
) for every weak solution
u
of (
2.3
) without a sign restriction on
\phi
.

.

Assume

.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

The purpose of the section is to prove Theorem
1.5
. As
g\in W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
we can in the following assume, without loss of generality, that
g\equiv 0
.

In domains of the form
U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I
instead of
U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
, one may attempt different approaches to prove Theorem
1.5
, and perhaps the most natural first approach is to add the term
\epsilon\Delta_{Y}
to the operator and to instead consider the problem

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u_{\epsilon},X,Y,t))+\epsilon\Delta_{Y}u_{\epsilon}-(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u_{\epsilon}=g^{*}&\text{in}\ U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I,\\
u_{\epsilon}=0&\text{on}\ \partial_{p}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I).\end{cases}

Here
\partial_{p}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)
is now the (standard) parabolic boundary of
U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I
, i.e.,

\partial_{p}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I):=(\partial(U_{X}\times U_{Y})\times\overline{I})\cup((U_{X}\times U_{Y})\times\{0\}).

The existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to (
5.1
) is classical and one easily deduces that

\displaystyle\||\nabla_{X}u_{\epsilon}|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}+\epsilon\||\nabla_{Y}u_{\epsilon}|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq c\|g^{*}\|_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(U_{Y}\times I,{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}\times\||u_{\epsilon}|+|\nabla_{X}u_{\epsilon}|\|_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)},

for some positive constant
c
, independent of
\epsilon
. By the standard Poincaré inequality, applied on
U_{X}
to
u_{\epsilon}(\cdot,Y,t)
with
(Y,t)
fixed, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}\leq c\||\nabla_{X}u_{\epsilon}|\|_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}.

Hence, using Cauchy-Schwarz we can conclude that

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}+\||\nabla_{X}u_{\epsilon}|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}+\epsilon\||\nabla_{Y}u_{\epsilon}|\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq c\|g^{*}\|^{2}_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(U_{Y}\times I,{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))},

for a constant
c
which is independent of
\epsilon
. The idea is then to let

\epsilon\to 0
and in this way construct a solution to the problem in (
2.7
). To make this operational, already in the linear case,
A(\xi,X,Y,t)=A(X,Y,t)\xi
, one seems to need some uniform estimates up to the Kolmogorov boundary
\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(U_{X}\times U_{Y}\times I)
to get a solution in the limit. In addition, in the nonlinear case considered in this paper we also need to ensure that
\nabla_{X}u_{\epsilon}\to\nabla_{X}u
pointwise a.e as
\epsilon\to 0
and how to achieve this is even less clear. One approach is to try to adapt the techniques of Boccardo and Murat
[
6
]
but it seems unclear how to make this approach operational in our case due to the presence of the term
\epsilon\Delta_{Y}u_{\epsilon}
in the approximating equation.

In this paper we will instead prove Theorem
1.5
by using a variational approach recently explored in Albritton-Armstrong-Mourrat-Novack
[
1
]
and Litsgård-Nyström
[
29
]
. We will prove that the solution to (
1.21
) can be obtained as the minimizer of a uniformly convex functional. The fact that a parabolic equation can be cast as the first variation of a uniformly convex integral functional was first discovered by Brezis-Ekeland
[
9
,
10
]
and for a modern treatment of this approach, covering uniformly elliptic parabolic equations of second order in the more general context of uniformly monotone operators, we refer to
[
4
]
which in turn is closely related to
[
19
]
, see also
[
18
]
.

5.1.
Variational representation of the symbol

To make the approach operational we will use a variational representation of the mapping
\xi\mapsto A(\xi,X,Y,t)
, for each
(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, that we learned from
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
and we refer to these papers for more background. Indeed, by
[
5
, Theorem 2.9]
, there exists
\tilde{A}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
satisfying the following properties, for
\Gamma:=2\Lambda+1
and for each
(X,Y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. First, the mapping

(5.5)

(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\tilde{A}(\xi,\eta,X,Y,t)-\frac{1}{2\Gamma}(|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})\quad\text{is convex}.

Second, the mapping

(5.6)

(\xi,\eta)\mapsto\tilde{A}(\xi,\eta,X,Y,t)-\frac{\Gamma}{2}(|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})\quad\text{is concave}.

Third, for every
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have

(5.7)

\tilde{A}(\xi,\eta,X,Y,t)\geq\xi\cdot\eta,

and

(5.8)

\tilde{A}(\xi,\eta,X,Y,t)=\xi\cdot\eta\iff\eta=A(\xi,X,Y,t).

Note that the choice of
\tilde{A}
is in general not unique. Note also that (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) imply, in particular that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\Gamma}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{A}(\xi_{1},\eta,X,Y,t)+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{A}(\xi_{2},\eta,X,Y,t)

(5.9)

\displaystyle-\tilde{A}(\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{2},\eta,X,Y,t)\leq\frac{\Gamma}{2}|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}.

5.2.
Setting up the argument

To ease the notation we will in the following at instances use the notation

W:=W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}),\quad W_{0}:=W_{0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}),

and we let

\mathcal{L}u:=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u,X,Y,t))-(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u.

Given an arbitrary pair
(f,\mathbf{j})
such that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\quad f\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathbf{j}\in L^{2}(V_{Y,t},L^{2}(U_{X})))^{m},

we introduce

(5.11)

\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{j}]:=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}(\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)-\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j})\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using this notation, and given an arbitrary pair
(f,f^{\ast})
such that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad f\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f^{\ast},\ f^{\ast}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})),

we set

(5.13)

J[f,f^{*}]:=\inf\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{g}]\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t,

where the infimum is taken with respect to the set

(5.14)

\bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in(L^{2}(V_{Y,t},L^{2}(U_{X})))^{m}\mid{\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{g}}=f^{*}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f\bigr\}.

The condition

{\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{g}}=f^{*}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f,

appearing in (
5.14
), should be interpreted as stating that

(5.15)

-\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\mathbf{g}\cdot\nabla_{X}\phi\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t=\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle f^{*}(\cdot,Y,t)+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),\phi\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t,

for all
\phi\in L^{2}(V_{Y,t},H^{1}_{X,0}(U_{X}))
. Finally, for
g^{*}\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))
fixed we introduce

(5.16)

\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast}):=\{(f,\mathbf{j})\in W_{0}\times(L^{2}(V_{Y,t},L^{2}(U_{X})))^{m}\mid\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{j}=g^{\ast}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f\}.

5.3.
\mathcal{J}
is uniformly convex on
\mathcal{A}(g^{*})

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
g^{*}\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))
be fixed and let
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
be the set introduced in (
5.16
). Then
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
is non-empty.

Proof.

Take
f\in W_{0}
and consider the equation

(5.17)

\displaystyle\Delta_{X}v(X,Y,t)=(g^{\ast}(X,Y,t)+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(X,Y,t))\in H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}),

for
\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t
-a.e
(Y,t)\in V_{Y,t}
. By the Lax-Milgram theorem this equation has a (unique) solution
v(\cdot)=v(\cdot,Y,t)\in H^{1}_{X,0}(U_{X})
and

(5.18)

\displaystyle||\nabla_{X}v||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},L^{2}(U_{X}))}\leq c||g^{\ast}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}<\infty,

as
f\in W_{0}
. In particular,

(5.19)

(f,\nabla_{X}v)\in\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast}),

and hence
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
is non-empty.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The functional
\mathcal{J}
introduced in
(
5.11
) is uniformly convex on
\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
.

Proof.

Note that if
(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{\mathbf{j}})\in\mathcal{A}(0)
, then
(f+\tilde{f},\mathbf{j}+\tilde{\mathbf{j}})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
and

(f-\tilde{f},\mathbf{j}-\tilde{\mathbf{j}})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
. Consider
(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
. We first consider the term

-\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

We have

\displaystyle-\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}f\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle g^{\ast}(\cdot,Y,t)+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t)),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle g^{\ast}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.20)

\displaystyle+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t)),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

Recall that
W_{0}=W_{0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
is the closure in the norm of

W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
of
C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
. In particular, there exists
\{f_{j}\}
,
f_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
such that

||f-f_{j}||_{W}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,

and consequently

||(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})(f-f_{j})||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty.

Using this we see that

(5.21)

\begin{split}&\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\geq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{j}(\cdot,Y,t),f_{j}(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

However, using that
f_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
we see that

(5.22)

\begin{split}&\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{j}(\cdot,Y,t),f_{j}(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{j}f_{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{j}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{X}}\iint_{\partial V_{Y,t}}f_{j}^{2}(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}X\geq 0,\end{split}

by the divergence theorem and the definition of the Kolmogorov boundary. Hence,

\displaystyle-\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}f\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{j}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.23)

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle g^{\ast}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using this, and observing that,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\bigl(\nabla_{X}(f+\tilde{f})\cdot(\mathbf{j}+\tilde{\mathbf{j}})+\nabla_{X}(f-\tilde{f})\cdot(\mathbf{j}-\tilde{\mathbf{j}})-2\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j}\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.24)

\displaystyle=-\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\nabla_{X}\tilde{f}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{j}}\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t,

we can conclude that

(5.25)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\bigl(\nabla_{X}(f+\tilde{f})\cdot(\mathbf{j}+\tilde{\mathbf{j}})+\nabla_{X}(f-\tilde{f})\cdot(\mathbf{j}-\tilde{\mathbf{j}})-2\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j}\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0,

over the set
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
. Hence it suffices to prove that

\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

is uniformly convex over the set
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
. With
(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
and
(\tilde{f},\tilde{\mathbf{j}})\in\mathcal{A}(0)
as above, (
5.5
) implies that

\frac{1}{2}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}(f+\tilde{f}),\mathbf{j}+\tilde{\mathbf{j}},\cdot)+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}(f-\tilde{f}),\mathbf{j}-\tilde{\mathbf{j}},\cdot)-\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},\cdot)\geq\frac{1}{2\Gamma}\left(|\nabla_{X}\tilde{f}|^{2}+|\tilde{\mathbf{j}}|^{2}\right).

We also have

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})\tilde{f}\|_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H^{-1}(U_{X}))}\leq\|\tilde{\mathbf{j}}\|_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}.

Thus

\displaystyle\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}(f+\tilde{f}),\mathbf{j}+\tilde{\mathbf{j}},\cdot)+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}(f-\tilde{f}),\mathbf{j}-\tilde{\mathbf{j}},\cdot)-\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},\cdot)\biggr)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\Gamma}\left(||\nabla_{X}\tilde{f}||^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}+\|(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})\tilde{f}\|^{2}_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H^{-1}(U_{X}))}+||\tilde{\mathbf{j}}||^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4c\Gamma}\left(||\tilde{f}||^{2}_{W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}+\||\tilde{\mathbf{j}}||^{2}_{L^{2}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}\right),

by using the (standard) Poincaré inequality. Hence
\mathcal{J}
is uniformly convex on
\mathcal{A}(g^{*})
.
∎

5.4.
Correspondence between weak solutions and minimizers

As the functional
\mathcal{J}
is uniformly convex over
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
there exists a unique minimizing pair
(f_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
such that

\displaystyle(f_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1}):=

\displaystyle\argmin_{(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})}\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{j}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\argmin_{(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})}\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}(\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)-\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j})\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note that

\displaystyle\min_{(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})}\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{j}]=\min_{f\in W_{0}}J[f,g^{*}].

Moreover, by construction of
\tilde{A}
, see (
5.7
), we have

(5.26)

J[f_{1},g^{\ast}]\geq 0.

Lemma 5.3
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between weak solutions in the sense of equation (
2.7
) to
\mathcal{L}u=g^{\ast}
in
U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
, such that
u\in W_{0}
, and null minimizers of
J[\cdot,g^{\ast}]
.

Proof.

To prove the lemma we need to prove that for every
f\in W_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle f\mbox{ solves $\mathcal{L}u=g^{\ast}$ in the weak sense in $U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}$}\iff J[f,g^{\ast}]=0.

Indeed, the implication ”
\implies
” is clear since if
f
solves
\mathcal{L}u=g^{\ast}
in the weak sense, then

(f,A(\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t))\in\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{J}[f,A(\nabla_{X}f,X,Y,t)]=0=J[f,g^{\ast}].

Conversely, if
J[f,g^{\ast}]=0
, then
f=f_{1}
and

(5.27)

\mathcal{J}[f_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1}]=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}(\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f_{1},\mathbf{j}_{1},X,Y,t)-\nabla_{X}f_{1}\cdot\mathbf{j}_{1})\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Using (
5.8
), we see that the identity (
5.27
) implies that

\mathbf{j}_{1}=A(\nabla f_{1},\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)\quad\text{a.e. in }U_{X}\times V_{Y,t},

and by the definition of the set
\mathcal{A}(g^{\ast})
,

\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{j}_{1}=g^{\ast}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{1}.

Hence
f_{1}
indeed solves

\nabla_{X}\cdot A(\nabla f_{1},\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)-(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{1}=g^{\ast}

in the weak sense. I.e., we recover that
f=f_{1}
is indeed a weak solution of
\mathcal{L}u=g^{\ast}
. In particular, the fact that there is at most one solution to
\mathcal{L}u=g^{*}
is clear.
∎

5.5.
An associated perturbed convex minimization problem

Using (
5.26
) and Lemma
5.3
we see that to complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
it remains to prove that

(5.28)

J[f_{1},g^{*}]\leq 0.

In order to do so, we introduce the perturbed convex minimization problem defined, for every
f^{*}\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))
, by

G(f^{*}):=\inf_{f\in W_{0}}\bigl(J[f,f^{*}+g^{*}]{-\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle f^{*}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\bigr).}

As

G(0)=\inf_{f\in W_{0}}J[f,g^{*}],

we see that to prove (
5.28
) is suffices to prove that
G(0)\leq 0
.

Lemma 5.4
.

G
is a convex, locally bounded from above and lower semi-continuous functional on
L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))
.

Proof.

For every pair
(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(f^{*}+g^{*})
, we have

\nabla_{X}\cdot\mathbf{j}=f^{*}+g^{*}+(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f,

and thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{j}]

\displaystyle=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}(\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)-\nabla_{X}f\cdot\mathbf{j})\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t{+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(f^{*}+g^{*})(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{j}]{-\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle f^{*}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle g^{*}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

Taking the infimum over all
(f,\mathbf{j})
satisfying the affine constraint
(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(f^{*}+g^{*})
we obtain the quantity
G(f^{*})
, i.e.,
G(f^{*})
can be expressed as

G(f^{*})=\inf_{(f,\mathbf{j}):\ (f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(f^{*}+g^{*})}\bigl(\mathcal{J}[f,\mathbf{j}]{-\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle f^{*}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigr).

In particular,
G(f^{*})
can be expressed as the infimum of

\displaystyle\iiint_{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}}\tilde{A}(\nabla_{X}f,\mathbf{j},X,Y,t)\,\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle g^{*}(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.29)

\displaystyle+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

with respect to
(f,\mathbf{j})
such that
(f,\mathbf{j})\in\mathcal{A}(f^{*}+g^{*})
. We now recall the argument in (
5.21
) and (
5.22
). In particular, given
f\in W_{0}
there exists
\{f_{j}\}
,
f_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
such that

(5.30)

||f-f_{j}||_{W}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,

and consequently

||(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})(f-f_{j})||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty.

Using (
5.21
) and (
5.22
) we have

\begin{split}&\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{X}}\iint_{\partial V_{Y,t}}f_{j}^{2}|(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}|\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}X.\end{split}

Obviously we get the same limit in (
5.5
) independent of what sequence
\{f_{j}\}
chosen as long as (
5.30
) holds. Now consider

f,g\in W_{0}
and let
\{f_{j}\}
,
f_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
,
\{g_{j}\}
,
g_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathcal{K},0}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
, be such that

(5.31)

||f-f_{j}||_{W}+||g-g_{j}||_{W}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,

Then

(5.32)

||(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)-(\tau f_{j}+(1-\tau)g_{j})||_{W}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,

for all
\tau\in[0,1]
. Hence

\displaystyle\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)(\cdot,Y,t),(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{X}}\iint_{\partial V_{Y,t}}(\tau f_{j}+(1-\tau)g_{j})^{2}|(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}|\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}X

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{X}}\iint_{\partial V_{Y,t}}\tau f_{j}^{2}|(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}|\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}X

(5.33)

\displaystyle+\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{U_{X}}\iint_{\partial V_{Y,t}}(1-\tau)g_{j}^{2}|(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}|\,\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}X,

and we deduce that

\displaystyle\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)(\cdot,Y,t),(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\tau\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.34)

\displaystyle+(1-\tau)\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})g(\cdot,Y,t),g(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t.

In particular, we can conclude that the mapping

f\to\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)(\cdot,Y,t),(\tau f+(1-\tau)g)(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

is convex on
W_{0}
. Using this, and (
5.5
), we see that the expression in (
5.5
) is convex as a function of
(f,f^{*},\mathbf{j})
and this proves that
G
is convex. Furthermore, using (
5.17
), (
5.18
), and (
5.19
) we can conclude that the infimum of the expression in (
5.5
) is finite, hence
G(f^{*})<\infty
. In particular, the function
G
is locally bounded from above. These two properties imply that
G
is lower semi-continuous, see
[
17
, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2]
.
∎

5.6.
The convex dual of
G

We denote by
G^{*}
the convex dual of
G
, defined for every

h\in(L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X})))^{\ast}=L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X})),

as

G^{*}(h):=\sup_{f^{*}\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}\bigl(-G(f^{*})+\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle f^{*}(\cdot,Y,t),h(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t\bigr).

Let
G^{**}
be the bidual of
G
. Since
G
is lower semi-continuous, we have that
G^{**}=G
(see
[
17
, Proposition 4.1]
), and in particular,

G(0)=G^{**}(0)=\sup_{h\in L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X,0}^{1}(U_{X}))}\bigl(-G^{*}(h)\bigr).

In order to prove that
G(0)\leq 0
, it therefore suffices to show that

(5.35)

To continue we note that we can rewrite

Lemma 5.5

Consider

5.10.

Assume that

6.
Future research and open problems

In this paper we have initiated the study of weak solutions, and their regularity, for what we call nonlinear Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck type equations. We believe that there are many directions to pursue in this field and in the following we formulate a number of problems.

Let

and for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Given

\mathcal{L}_{A,p}
through

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{A,p}u:=\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u(X,Y,t),X,Y,t))-(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u(X,Y,t).

This defines a class of strongly degenerate nonlinear parabolic PDEs modelled on the classical PDE of Kolmogorov and the
p
-Laplace operator, and to our knowledge there is currently no literature devoted to these operators. The results established in this paper concern
\mathcal{L}_{A,2}
assuming that
A\in M(\Lambda)
or
A\in R(\Lambda)
. We see a number of interesting research problems.

Problem 1:
Establish existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to the Dirichlet problem

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{A,p}u=g^{*},\quad&\textrm{ in }U_{X}\times V_{Y,t},\\
u=g,\quad&\textrm{ on }\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}).\end{cases}

Problem 2:
Prove higher integrability, local boundedness, Harnack inequalities and local Hölder continuity of weak solutions for the equation
\mathcal{L}_{A,p}u=0

in the case
p\neq 2
. This is a challenging problem and the first step is probably to figure out how to replace the result of Bouchut
[
7
]
, or the use of the fundamental solution constructed by Kolmogorov, in this case. The problem is already very interesting for the prototype

(6.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\cdot(|\nabla_{X}u(X,Y,t)|^{p-2}\nabla_{X}u(X,Y,t))-(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u(X,Y,t)=0.

Problem 3:
Consider the equation in (
6.4
). Prove bounds for
\nabla_{X}u
and local Hölder continuity of
\nabla_{X}u
. Note that this must be a difficult problem in the nonlinear setting due to the lack of ellipticity in the variable
Y
. Again, the right place to start is probably to (simply) consider
the equation

\nabla_{X}\cdot(A(\nabla_{X}u))-(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u=0,

where
A(\xi)
has linear growth, i.e. a nonlinear
p=2
case.

Finally, we discuss the very formulation of the Dirichlet problem. Consider the geometry of
U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
and let
\Gamma:=\partial U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}
and

\displaystyle\Sigma^{+}

\displaystyle:=\{(X,Y,t)\in\overline{U_{X}}\times\partial V_{Y,t}\mid(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}>0\},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{(X,Y,t)\in\overline{U_{X}}\times\partial V_{Y,t}\mid(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}=0\},

(6.5)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{-}

\displaystyle:=\{(X,Y,t)\in\overline{U_{X}}\times\partial V_{Y,t}\mid(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}<0\}.

Using this notation
\partial_{\mathcal{K}}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})=\Gamma\cup\Sigma^{-}
. Recall that
W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
is defined as the closure of
C^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
in the norm

(6.6)

\displaystyle||u||_{W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}

\displaystyle:=\bigl(||u||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))}^{2}+||(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})u||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}.

Assuming
u\in W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
, it is relevant to define and study
the trace of
u
to
\Gamma\cup\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma^{-}
. We let
=B^{2,2}_{1/2}(\partial U_{X})
denote the Besov space defined as the trace space of
H_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
to
\partial U_{X}
(this space is often denoted
H^{1/2}(\partial U_{X})
in the literature). It is well known, that if
U_{X}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, then there exists
a bounded continuous non-injective operator
T:H_{X}^{1}(U_{X})\to B^{2,2}_{1/2}(\partial U_{X})
, called the trace operator, and a bounded continuous operator
E:B^{2,2}_{1/2}(\partial U_{X})\to H_{X}^{1}(U_{X})
called the extension operator. The trace space of
L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},H_{X}^{1}(U_{X}))
on
\Gamma
is therefore
L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},B^{2,2}_{1/2}(\partial U_{X}))
and

||u||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},B^{2,2}_{1/2}(\partial U_{X}))}\leq c||u||_{W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}.

The trace to
\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma^{-}
is less clear. Indeed, recall that the space
W_{X,0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
is defined as the closure in the norm of

W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
of
C_{X,0}^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
. In particular, given
f\in W_{X,0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
there exists
\{f_{j}\}
,
f_{j}\in C_{X,0}^{\infty}(\overline{U_{X}\times V_{Y,t}})
such that

||f-f_{j}||_{W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,

and consequently,

||(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})(f-f_{j})||_{L_{Y,t}^{2}(V_{Y,t},{H}_{X}^{-1}(U_{X}))}\to 0\mbox{ as }j\to\infty.

Using this we see that

\displaystyle\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f_{j}(\cdot,Y,t),f_{j}(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(6.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{j\to\infty}\iint_{\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma^{-}}f_{j}^{2}(X,Y,t)\,(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}.

The first obstruction to a trace inequality is that
(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}
is a signed measure on
\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma_{0}\cup\Sigma^{-}
. Assuming that

f\in W_{0}(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
we deduce that

(6.8)

\displaystyle\iint_{V_{Y,t}}\langle(\partial_{t}+X\cdot\nabla_{Y})f(\cdot,Y,t),f(\cdot,Y,t)\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}Y\,\mathrm{d}t

(6.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{j\to\infty}\iint_{\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma_{0}}f_{j}^{2}(X,Y,t)\,(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}.

Hence, in this case

(6.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\iint_{\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma_{0}}f_{j}^{2}(X,Y,t)\,(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t}\leq c||f||_{W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})},

and we see that we can extract a subsequence of
\{f_{j}\}
converging in
L^{2}(K,(X,1)\cdot N_{Y,t}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{Y,t})
whenever
K
is a compact subset of
\Sigma^{+}
. At the expense of additional notation the roles of
\Sigma^{+}
and
\Sigma^{-}
can be interchanged in this argument. This observation highlights the difficulty concerning the possibility of a trace inequality and concerning the identification of the trace space for
W(U_{X}\times V_{Y,t})
. This explains why we in this paper, as in
[
29
]
, have used the weaker formulation of the Dirichlet problem introduced.

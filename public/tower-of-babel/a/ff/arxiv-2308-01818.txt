Abstract
. In this paper we deal with the problem of describing the dual space ( ℬ κ 1 ) ∗ ({\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{\kappa})^{*} of the Bernstein space ℬ κ 1 {\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{\kappa} , that is the space of entire functions of exponential type (at most) κ > 0 \kappa>0 whose restriction to the real line is Lebesgue integrable. We provide several characterizations, showing that such dual space can be described as a quotient of the space of entire functions of exponential type κ \kappa whose restriction to the real line are in a suitable BMO \operatorname{BMO} -type space, or as the space of symbols b b for which the Hankel opertor H b H_{b} is bounded on the Paley–Wiener space ℬ κ / 2 2 {\mathcal{B}}^{2}_{\kappa/2} . We also provide a characterization of ( ℬ κ 1 ) ∗ ({\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{\kappa})^{*} as the BMO \operatorname{BMO} space w.r.t. the Clark measures of the inner function e i ​ κ ​ z e^{i\kappa z} on the upper half-plane, in analogy with the known description of the dual of backward-shift invariant 1 1 -spaces on the torus. Furthermore, we show that the orthogonal projection P κ : L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) → ℬ κ 2 P_{\kappa}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathcal{B}}^{2}_{\kappa} induces a bounded operator form L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}) onto ( ℬ κ 1 ) ∗ ({\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{\kappa})^{*} . Finally, we show that ℬ κ 1 {\mathcal{B}}^{1}_{\kappa} is the dual space of the suitable VMO \operatorname{VMO} -type space or as the space of symbols b b for which the Hankel opertor H b H_{b} on the Paley–Wiener space ℬ κ / 2 2 {\mathcal{B}}^{2}_{\kappa/2} is compact.

Final remarks

The Bernstein spaces are particular cases of de Branges spaces, see,
for example,
[
dB68
]
and
[
Rom14
]
. Indeed the entire function

is a Hermite Biehler function and, if

{\mathcal{B}}^{p}_{\pi}=\left\{f\text{ entire }:f(z)/e^{-i\pi z},\ f^{\#}(z)/e^{-i\pi z}\in H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}^{+})\right\}={\mathcal{H}}^{p}(e^{-i\pi\cdot}).

If

is any Hermite Biehler and

, we observe that, when

,

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{e^{i\pi\alpha}E(z)+e^{-i\pi\alpha}E^{\#}(z)}{e^{i\pi\alpha}E(z)-e^{-i\pi\alpha}E^{\#}(z)}\right)>0.

Therefore, thanks to the Herglotz theorem
[
dB68
, Theorem 4]
, there exists a positive, Poisson integrable, Borel measure

such that

\frac{e^{i\pi\alpha}E(z)+e^{-i\pi\alpha}E^{\#}(z)}{e^{i\pi\alpha}E(z)-e^{-i\pi\alpha}E^{\#}(z)}=ip_{\alpha}z+c_{\alpha}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{1}{t-z}-\frac{t}{1+t^{2}}\right)d\sigma_{\alpha}(t).

The measures
\sigma_{\alpha}
are the Clark measures associated to

E
. For a complete description of these measures, we refer
to
[
CMR06
, Chapter 9]
and to

[
Sak07
]
.

If
E(z)=e^{-i\pi z}
, we are able to compute explicitely the associated Clark measures. Indeed

\operatorname{supp}{\sigma_{\alpha}}=\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:e^{-i\pi\alpha}e^{i\pi t}=e^{i\pi\alpha}e^{-i\pi t}\right\}=\left\{t\in\mathbb{R}:e^{2i\pi t}=e^{2i\pi\alpha}\right\}=\left\{t=n+\alpha:n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}=:\mathbb{Z}_{\alpha}

and

\sigma_{\alpha}(n+\alpha)=\frac{1}{|(e^{-i\pi(\cdot)})^{\prime}|}=\frac{1}{\pi}.

It follows that the space
{\mathcal{X}}_{\alpha}
can be equivalently described through the Clark measures.

Abstract
This letter investigates a fluid antenna (FA)-assisted integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) system, with joint antenna position optimization and waveform design. We consider enhancing the sum-rate maximization (SRM) and sensing performance with the aid of FAs. Although the introduction of FAs brings more degrees of freedom for performance optimization, its position optimization poses a non-convex programming problem and brings great computational challenges. This letter contributes to building an efficient design algorithm by the block successive upper bound minimization and majorization-minimization principles, with each step admitting closed-form update for the ISAC waveform design. In addition, the extrapolation technique is exploited further to speed up the empirical convergence of FA position design. Simulation results show that the proposed design can achieve state-of-the-art sum-rate performance with at least 60% computation cutoff compared to existing works with successive convex approximation (SCA) and particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithms.

However, the concept of ISAC increases the resource demands required to effectively fulfill both.

Recently, fluid antennas (FAs) have been proposed as a novel technique to further improve the spectral efficiency of the MIMO systems

7
]
.
The increasing body of studies has shown that the optimized FA positions can benefit the communication performance.
In
[

and

In addition, the joint design with FA positions requires dedicated optimization techniques to improve the spectral efficiency
[
7
]
or ISAC performance
[
10
,
11
]
.
In
[
10
]
, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) was adopted to address FA-assisted ISAC problems, while DRL consumes a large amount of training data and time.
In
[
11
]
, the FAs with predetermined discrete positions were considered to balance the ISAC performance, and effective FA ports were activated by sparsity constraints.
Moreover, movable antennas (MAs) with the same function as the FAs were proposed to enhance the ISAC system
[
12
,
13
]
.
In
[
12
]
, the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm was employed to design the MA positions for achieving better ISAC, while the PSO algorithm may have high computational overhead and the performance can be sensitive to the number of particles and searches.
In
[
13
]
, a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) was used to assist the MA system in sensing non-line-of-sight targets, and the optimized MA positions were achieved by the successive convex approximation (SCA) algorithm.
While the existing works have demonstrated the potential improvement of considering FAs in ISAC, the challenge of the high computational complexity of joint design remains a key issue, as also pointed out in
[
14
]
.

This letter aims to alleviate the design computational complexity in FA-aided ISAC systems under a proposed formulation for optimized sum-rate performance under sensing requirements. The development is based on the majorization minimization (MM) principle, where a novel proximal distance algorithm (PDA) is proposed to obtain the closed-form beamformer at each iteration, and an extrapolated projected gradient (EPG) method is proposed to speed up the optimization of antenna positions. Compared to the existing widely adopted PSO, our approach makes more use of the problem structure and shows improved performance and reduced complexity. Simulations demonstrate that our algorithm achieves top-tier sum-rate performance with at least 60% less computation compared to existing methods like SCA and PSO. For larger FA scales, it provides over 40% sum-rate improvement compared to the SCA method, with significant computational savings and over 150 times faster performance than the PSO method. Additionally, incorporating FA results in a more than 30% performance boost compared to traditional ISAC without FA.

III
Beamformer and APV Optimization

We aim to optimize the beamformer and APV to maximize the multiuser sum rate while the probing power in the target direction is above a prefixed level.
The problem formulation of the joint design is

\displaystyle\max_{{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}}\,\,\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\rm log}_{2}\,(1+\gamma_{k})

(5a)

s.t.

\displaystyle~\mathcal{C}_{\sf BS}:\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bm{w}}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{max},

(5b)

\displaystyle~\mathcal{C}_{t}\,\,\,:P({\bm{w}},{\bm{t}},\theta)\geq P_{t},

(5c)

\displaystyle~\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR}:t_{1}\geq 0,~t_{M}\leq D,

(5d)

\displaystyle~\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC}:t_{m}-t_{m-1}\geq D_{0},~m=2,3,\dots,M.

(5e)

In problem (
5
), the constraint (
5b
) portrays the power budget at the BS;
constraints (
5d
) and (
5e
) restrict the FA positions in the interval
[0,D]
, and ensure that the inter-antenna spacing is no less than
D_{0}
, respectively.

Note that problem (
5
) is highly non-convex with respect to the design parameters
({\bm{w}},{\bm{t}})
since the objective and the constraint (
5c
) are non-convex. Plus, the design of FA positions amounts to the optimization of the unit circle manifold. These properties make problem (
5
) difficult to solve.

To achieve an efficient design algorithm for problem (
5
), we take advantage of the block variable structure and extend the block successive upper bound minimization (BSUM) method
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
to handle it.
The unique challenge is to incorporate FA positions in the development.
We outline the key steps of the tailored algorithm as follows.

\begin{split}\min_{{\bm{w}},{\bm{t}},\bm{{\bm{u}}},\bm{\rho}}~{\cal F}({\bm{w}},{\bm{t}},\bm{{\bm{u}}},\bm{\rho})~~\mbox{s.t. }\mathcal{C}_{\sf BS},~\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR},~\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC},~\mathcal{C}_{t},\end{split}

(6)

where

{\cal F}({\bm{w}},{\bm{t}},\bm{{\bm{u}}},\bm{\rho})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}[{\bm{w}}_{k}^{\rm H}{\mathbb{A}}({\bm{t}}){\bm{w}}_{k}-2\mathscr{R}\{{\bm{b}}_{k}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}}){\bm{w}}_{k}\}-{\rm log}(\rho_{k})]

with
{\bm{u}}\in\mathbb{C}^{K}
and
\bm{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{K}
being introduced auxiliary variables;
{\mathbb{A}}({\bm{t}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}|u_{k}|^{2}{\bm{h}}_{k}({\bm{t}}){\bm{h}}_{k}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}})+\sigma_{k}^{2}
;
{\bm{b}}_{k}({\bm{t}})=\rho_{k}u_{k}{\bm{h}}_{k}({\bm{t}})
; and
\mathscr{R}\{x\}
denoting the real part of
x
.
By leveraging the above transformation, we apply BSUM and obtain the following

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}^{\ell+1}=

\displaystyle~\arg\min_{{\bm{u}}\in\mathbb{C}^{K}}{\cal F}({\bm{w}}^{\ell},{\bm{t}}^{\ell},\bm{{\bm{u}}},\bm{\rho}^{\ell});

(7a)

\displaystyle\bm{\rho}^{\ell+1}=

\displaystyle~\arg\min_{\bm{\rho}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{K}}{\cal F}({\bm{w}}^{\ell},{\bm{t}}^{\ell},\bm{{\bm{u}}}^{\ell+1},\bm{\rho});

(7b)

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}^{\ell+1}=

\displaystyle~\arg\min_{{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\sf BS}\cap\mathcal{C}_{t}}{\cal F}({\bm{w}},{\bm{t}}^{\ell},\bm{{\bm{u}}}^{\ell+1},\bm{\rho}^{\ell+1});

(7c)

\displaystyle{\bm{t}}^{\ell+1}=

\displaystyle~\arg\min_{{\bm{t}}\in\mathcal{C}_{t}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC}}{\cal F}({\bm{w}}^{\ell+1},{\bm{t}},\bm{{\bm{u}}}^{\ell+1},\bm{\rho}^{\ell+1}).

(7d)

Notably, the optimizations of
{\bm{u}}
in (
7a
) and
\bm{\rho}
in (
7b
) have closed-form solutions, which are given by

\begin{split}u_{k}^{\ell+1}=\frac{{\bm{h}}_{k}^{\rm H}{\bm{w}}_{k}}{\sum_{i=1}^{K}|{\bm{h}}_{k}^{\rm H}{\bm{w}}_{i}|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}},~\rho_{k}^{\ell+1}=\frac{1}{1-(u_{k}^{\ell+1})^{*}{\bm{h}}_{k}^{\rm H}{\bm{w}}_{k}}\end{split}

for any
k\in{\cal K}
; and
(\cdot)^{*}
denotes the complex conjugate.

However, the optimizations of the beamformer
{\bm{w}}
and the APV
{\bm{t}}
do not have closed forms.
We custom-build efficient algorithms for these two subproblems.

III-A

Beamformer Optimization

The subproblems (
7c
) involve coupled constraints, and the projections onto the coupled constraints can be difficult to calculate.
To tackle this challenge, we propose a PDA that can effectively decouple the constraints and yield efficient closed-form updates.
By PDA, we approximate (
7c
) by

\begin{split}\min_{{\bm{w}}}~{\cal F}({\bm{w}})+\bar{\rho}\,{\rm dist}^{2}({\bm{w}},\mathcal{C}_{\sf BS})+\bar{\rho}\,{\rm dist}^{2}({\bm{w}},\mathcal{C}_{t}),\end{split}

(8)

where
\mbox{dist}({\bm{w}},\mathcal{X})
denotes the distance from point
{\bm{w}}
to set
\mathcal{X}
;

\bar{\rho}>0
is a given penalty parameter. Obviously, the optimal solution of problem (
8
) is equivalent to the solution of problem (
7c
) when
\bar{\rho}
is large.

Still, the distance function is variational and does not have an explicit expression.
We address this issue by considering majorizating them by

\mbox{dist}({\bm{w}},\mathcal{C}_{\sf BS})\leq\|{\bm{w}}-{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{\sf BS}\|_{2},~\mbox{dist}({\bm{w}},\mathcal{C}_{t})\leq\|{\bm{w}}-{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{t}\|_{2},

(9)

where
{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{\sf BS}=\Pi_{{\cal C}_{\sf BS}}({\bm{w}})
;
{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{t}=\Pi_{{\cal C}_{t}}({\bm{w}})
; and the notation

\Pi_{{\cal C}_{{\cal X}}}({\bm{w}})=\arg\min_{{\bm{y}}\in{\cal X}}\|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{w}}\|_{2}^{2}

denotes projecting
{\bm{w}}
onto
\mathcal{X}
. By meticulous calculation, we show that

\begin{split}[{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{\sf BS}]_{k}=&\begin{cases}{\bm{w}}_{k},&\mbox{if }\|{\bm{w}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{max},\\
\sqrt{P_{max}}\frac{{\bm{w}}_{k}}{\|{\bm{w}}\|_{2}},&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
\end{split}

\begin{split}[{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{t}]_{k}=&\begin{cases}{\bm{w}}_{k},~~~{\rm if}~\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bm{w}}_{k}^{\rm H}{\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta){\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta){\bm{w}}_{k}\geq P_{t},\\
\left({\mathbb{I}}-\mu{\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta){\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta)\right)^{-1}{\bm{w}}_{k},~~~{\rm otherwise},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\mu=\frac{1}{\|{\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta)\|_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bm{w}}_{k}^{\rm H}{\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta){\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta){\bm{w}}_{k}}{|{\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta){\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta)|^{2}P_{t}}}.

The derivation of the projection
{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{t}
is shown in Appendix A.

By the distance majorization, at iteration
{\bm{w}}
, one only needs to solve an unconstrained quadratic programming problem, given by

\begin{split}\min_{{\bm{w}}}~{\cal F}({\bm{w}})+\bar{\rho}\left(\|{\bm{w}}-{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{\sf BS}\|_{2}^{2}+\|{\bm{w}}-{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\right),\end{split}

(10)

which has an optimal closed-form solution, i.e.,

\begin{split}{\bm{w}}_{k}=\left({\mathbb{A}}+2\bar{\rho}{\mathbb{I}}\right)^{-1}\left[\bar{\rho}\left([{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{\sf BS}]_{k}+[{\widetilde{}{\bm{w}}}_{t}]_{k}\right)+{\bm{b}}_{k}\right],\,k\in{\cal K}.\end{split}

(11)

The PDA was summarized in Algorithm 1. Note that we also apply the extrapolation in step 3, which was shown to be able to numerically accelerate the algorithmic convergence
[
19
]
.

III-B

APV Optimization

An EPG algorithm is proposed for handling problem (
7d
), which takes the following updates

\begin{split}&{\bm{t}}^{i+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{t}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC}}\left({\bm{z}}^{i}-\eta\nabla_{{\bm{t}}}{\cal F}({\bm{z}}^{i}|{\bm{t}}^{i})\right),\\
&{\bm{z}}^{i+1}={\bm{t}}^{i+1}+\zeta_{i+1}({\bm{t}}^{i+1}-{\bm{t}}^{i}),\end{split}

(12)

where
\eta>0
is the descent step length, which is obtained by the backtracking line search;

\nabla_{{\bm{t}}}{\cal F}({\bm{z}}^{i}|{\bm{t}}^{i})
denotes the gradient of
{\cal F}
at
{\bm{z}}^{i}
, and
the parameter
\zeta_{i+1}=\frac{\alpha_{i+1}-1}{\alpha_{i+1}}
,
\alpha_{i+1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4\alpha_{i}^{2}}}{2}
with
\alpha_{1}=0
.

Defining
{\bm{w}}_{k}={\bm{a}}_{k}+j{\bm{b}}_{k}
,
{\mathbb{C}}_{k}={\bm{a}}_{k}{\bm{a}}_{k}^{\rm T}+{\bm{b}}_{k}{\bm{b}}_{k}^{\rm T}
,
{\mathbb{D}}_{k}={\bm{a}}_{k}{\bm{b}}_{k}^{\rm T}-{\bm{b}}_{k}{\bm{a}}_{k}^{\rm T}
,
{\bm{g}}_{k}=[g_{1,k},g_{2,k},\dots,g_{M,k}]^{\rm T}
,
{\bm{q}}_{k}=[q_{1,k},q_{2,k},\dots,q_{M,k}]^{\rm T}
,
g_{m,k}={\rm cos}(v_{k}t_{m})
, and
q_{m,k}={\rm sin}(v_{k}t_{m})
for any
k\in{\cal K}
, we have

\begin{split}f_{k,i}&=|{\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta_{k}){\bm{w}}_{i}|^{2}={\bm{g}}_{k}^{\rm T}{\mathbb{C}}_{i}{\bm{g}}_{k}+{\bm{q}}_{k}^{\rm T}{\mathbb{C}}_{i}{\bm{q}}_{k}+2{\bm{g}}_{k}^{\rm T}{\mathbb{D}}_{i}{\bm{q}}_{k},\\
h_{k,k}&=\mathscr{R}\{{\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta_{k}){\bm{w}}_{k}\}={\bm{g}}_{k}^{\rm T}{\bm{a}}_{k}+{\bm{q}}_{k}^{\rm T}{\bm{b}}_{k}.\end{split}

Thus, the derivatives of
f_{k,i}
and
h_{k,k}
with respect to
{\bm{t}}
gives

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial f_{k,i}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}=\frac{\partial f_{k,i}}{\partial{\bm{g}}_{k}}\frac{\partial{\bm{g}}_{k}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}+\frac{\partial f_{k,i}}{\partial{\bm{q}}_{k}}\frac{\partial{\bm{q}}_{k}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}\\
&=2v_{k}\left[\mathrm{diag}({\bm{g}}_{k})({\mathbb{C}}_{i}{\bm{q}}_{k}-{\mathbb{D}}_{i}{\bm{g}}_{k})-\mathrm{diag}({\bm{q}}_{k})({\mathbb{C}}_{i}{\bm{g}}_{k}+{\mathbb{D}}_{i}{\bm{q}}_{k})\right],\\
&\frac{\partial h_{k,k}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}=\frac{\partial h_{k,k}}{\partial{\bm{g}}_{k}}\frac{\partial{\bm{g}}_{k}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}+\frac{\partial h_{k,k}}{\partial{\bm{q}}_{k}}\frac{\partial{\bm{q}}_{k}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}\\
&=v_{k}\left[\mathrm{diag}({\bm{g}}_{k}){\bm{b}}_{k}-\mathrm{diag}({\bm{q}}_{k}){\bm{a}}_{k}\right].\end{split}

Next,
\nabla_{{\bm{t}}}{\cal F}({\bm{t}})
can be derived and is given by

\begin{split}\nabla_{{\bm{t}}}{\cal F}({\bm{t}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\{\rho_{k}|u_{k}|^{2}\delta_{k}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\frac{\partial f_{k,i}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}-2\mathscr{R}\{\rho_{k}u_{k}^{*}\delta_{k}\}\frac{\partial h_{k,k}}{\partial{\bm{t}}}\right\}.\end{split}

Moreover,
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_{t}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC}}(\bm{\kappa})
can be obtained by solving the following problem

\begin{split}\min_{{\bm{t}}}~\|{\bm{t}}-\bm{\kappa}\|_{2}^{2}~~~\mbox{s.t. }~{\bm{t}}\in\mathcal{C}_{t}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC},\end{split}

(13)

where
\bm{\kappa}
is a point to project.
However, the constraint
\mathcal{C}_{t}
is non-convex.
Therefore, we construct a concave quadratic surrogate function to minorize
{\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta){\mathbb{R}}_{w}{\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta)
as

\begin{split}{\mathbb{a}}^{\rm H}({\bm{t}},\theta){\mathbb{R}}_{w}{\mathbb{a}}({\bm{t}},\theta)\geq g({\bm{t}}|\tilde{}{\bm{t}})\triangleq{\bm{t}}^{\rm T}{\mathbb{D}}{\bm{t}}-2{\bm{d}}^{\rm T}{\bm{t}}+c,\end{split}

(14)

where
{\mathbb{D}}=-v^{2}\left(\mathrm{diag}({\bm{r}})-{\mathbb{R}}\right)
,

{\bm{d}}[n]=v\sum_{m=1}^{M}|R_{mn}|{\rm sin}(f(\tilde{t}_{n},\tilde{t}_{m}))-v^{2}\sum_{m=1}^{M}|R_{mn}|(\tilde{t}_{n}-\tilde{t}_{m}),

\begin{split}c=&\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{M}|R_{mn}|[{\rm cos}(f(\tilde{t}_{n},\tilde{t}_{m}))+v{\rm sin}(f(\tilde{t}_{n},\tilde{t}_{m}))(\tilde{t}_{n}-\tilde{t}_{m})\\
&-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}(\tilde{t}_{n}-\tilde{t}_{m})^{2}],\end{split}

with
{\bm{r}}=\left[\sum_{m=1}^{M}|R_{m1}|,\sum_{m=1}^{M}|R_{m2}|,\dots,\sum_{m=1}^{M}|R_{mM}|\right]
;
v=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}{\rm cos}(\theta)
;
[{\mathbb{R}}]_{mn}=|R_{mn}|
;
f(\tilde{t}_{n},\tilde{t}_{m})=v(\tilde{t}_{n}-\tilde{t}_{m})+\angle R_{mn}
;
\tilde{}{\bm{t}}=[\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2},\dots,\tilde{t}_{M}]^{\rm T}
;
R_{mn}
is an element of the
m
th row and
n
th column of
{\mathbb{R}}_{w}
for
n,m=1,2,\dots,M
; and
\tilde{}{\bm{t}}
denotes any determined value for
{\bm{t}}
.

Therefore, the problem (
13
) is reformulated as follows

\begin{split}\min_{{\bm{t}}}~\|{\bm{t}}-\bm{\kappa}\|_{2}^{2}~~\mbox{s.t. }~{\bm{t}}\in\mathcal{C}_{q}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf FR}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\sf AC},\end{split}

(15)

where the feasible space
\mathcal{C}_{q}
of
{\bm{t}}
is restricted by
g({\bm{t}}|{\bm{t}}^{i})\geq P_{t}
.

Problem (
15
) is a convex quadratically constrained quadratic program (QCQP) problem, which can be solved efficiently with off-the-shelf toolboxes, such as CVX.

III-C

Computational Complexity Analysis

In this subsection, we analyze the complexity of the proposed BSUM algorithm in (
7
).
The computational complexity of the BSUM algorithm is
{\cal O}(M^{4.5}+M^{2}(MK+M+K))
.
This is because the overall complexity of the closed-form
{\bm{u}}
, as well as
\bm{\rho}
, is
{\cal O}(M^{2}K)
, the complexity for beamformer optimization (
7c
) is
{\cal O}(M^{3}+M^{2}K)
, and the complexity for APV optimization (
7d
) is
{\cal O}(M^{4.5}+M^{3}K)
.

V
Conclusion

This letter proposed an efficient algorithm for an FA-assisted ISAC system, aiming at maximizing the multiuser sum rates with the required sensing requirement.
Relying on the MM algorithm, the PDA was proposed to iteratively update the closed-form beamformer, and the EPG was presented to efficiently configure the FA positions.
Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed algorithm can realize FA position configuration more than 60% faster than the SCA and PSO algorithms.

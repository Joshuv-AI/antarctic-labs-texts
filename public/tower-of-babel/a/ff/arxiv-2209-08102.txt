Dark matter solution to the H 0 H_{0} and S 8 S_{8} tensions, and the integrated Sachs-Wolfe void anomaly Preprint: APS/123-QED
Krishna Naidoo, Mariana Jaber, Wojciech A. Hellwing, Maciej Bilicki

Abstract
We consider a phenomenological model of dark matter with an equation-of-state w w that is negative and changing at late times. We show this couples the H 0 H_{0} and S 8 S_{8} tensions, alleviating them both simultaneously, reducing the H 0 H_{0} tension from ∼ 5 ​ σ \sim 5\sigma to ∼ 3 ​ σ \sim 3\sigma and the S 8 S_{8} tension from ∼ 3 ​ σ \sim 3\sigma to ∼ 1 ​ σ \sim 1\sigma . Furthermore, the model provides an explanation for the anomalously large integrated Sachs-Wolfe (ISW) effect from cosmic voids, a unique consequence of the changing and negative equation-of-state. Observations of high ISW from cosmic voids may therefore be evidence that dark matter plays a significant role in both the H 0 H_{0} and S 8 S_{8} tensions. We predict the ISW from cosmic voids to be a factor of up to ∼ 2 \sim 2 greater in this model than what is expected from the standard model Λ ​ CDM \rm\Lambda CDM . These results extend to other degenerate models of dark matter, such as unified or interacting dark matter and dark energy models.



I
Introduction

The emergence of strong tensions in the constraints of the Hubble constant
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
parameters, between early
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020a
)
and late universe-based observations
(
Riess et al. 2016
;
Asgari et al. 2021
;
Abbott et al. 2022
;
Dark Energy Survey and Kilo-Degree Survey
Collaboration et al. 2023
;
Dalal et al. 2023
)
, have placed intense scrutiny on observations, methods and the assumptions of the standard model of cosmology
\rm\Lambda CDM
(see
Verde et al. 2019
;
Knox and Millea 2020
;
Di Valentino et al. 2021a
;
Jedamzik et al. 2021
;
Di Valentino et al. 2021b
;
Perivolaropoulos and Skara 2022
;
Shah et al. 2021
;
Abdalla et al. 2022
for reviews on the Hubble tension). One of these assumptions is the presence of cold dark matter; a yet-to-be-detected massive particle that is the dominant source of gravitation in the universe. Dark matter’s interactions with standard model particles and forces is known to be very weak and the specific properties of dark matter are often assumed to manifest only on small cosmological scales.

However, with no direct observations of dark matter, there is little we can presume about its properties, other than its gravitational effects and weak interactions with known particles. Rather than consider an endless list of dark matter model extensions we can instead consider phenomenological scenarios that allow us to determine the general observational implications of a whole family of models, including their role in tensions and anomalies. With this in mind, we explore the implication of a subset of the generalised dark matter model
(
Hu 1998
)
, in a spatially flat universe with a cosmological constant (
\Lambda
). The equation-of-state (EoS) for dark matter (
w_{\rm dm}
) is allowed to be non-zero and evolving at late times but with null speed of sound and viscosity. For consistency with constraints from the early universe
(
Xu and Chang 2013
;
Kopp et al. 2018
)
the EoS is assumed to be effectively zero at early times. We will refer to this model as
evolving
dark matter (eDM), to distinguish it from other models often abbreviated to WDM (such as warm dark matter), and with the full model with
\Lambda
referred to as
\rm\Lambda eDM
.

While the
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
tensions are widely discussed in the community, a lesser known anomaly is the observation of larger than expected integrated Sachs-Wolfe
(
Sachs and Wolfe 1967
, ISW;)
signal from cosmic voids
(
Granett et al. 2008
;
Cai et al. 2014
;
Kovács 2018
)
(the void-ISW anomaly). This anomaly is strongest for ‘photometric’ voids,
i.e.
voids measured from photometric observations of galaxies which are preferentially elongated along the line-of-sight (LOS)
(
Kovács et al. 2019
)
and ranging in significance from 2-4
\sigma

Granett et al. 2008
;
Cai et al. 2014
;
Kovács et al. 2019
, while for ‘spectroscopic’ voids (smaller and not aligned with the LOS) the ISW is larger but to a lesser extent and remains consistent with
\rm\Lambda CDM

(
Nadathur and Crittenden 2016
)
. In contrast, observations of void lensing are either consistent with
\rm\Lambda CDM
or lower than expected
(
Kovács et al. 2022a
)
.

The aim of this paper is to establish the role dark matter can play in alleviating tensions in cosmology and in explaining the void-ISW anomaly. The latter has rarely been considered part of the tension discussion, but in this paper we show the ISW is very sensitive to late-time changes in dark matter’s EoS and can provide a unique test for a dark matter (or an interacting dark sector) solution to tensions. This paper is organised as follows: (1) we discuss the assumptions and theoretical background of
\rm\Lambda eDM
and the implications for the
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
tensions, and the ISW and lensing from cosmic voids; (2) we describe
\rm\Lambda eDM
’s parameter degeneracies and methods used for parameter inference, (3) we discuss the constraints on
\rm\Lambda eDM
parameters using cosmological observations; and (4) we discuss the implications for tensions and anomalies in cosmology.

II
Theory

II.1
\rm\Lambda eDM
and Tensions

The evolution of the dark matter density
\rho_{\rm dm}
can be described as a function of the scale factor
a
and a time-varying EoS
w_{\rm dm}(a)
defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm dm}(a)=\frac{\rho_{\rm dm,0}}{a^{3}}\,W(a),

(1)

\displaystyle W(a)=\exp\left(3\int_{a}^{1}\frac{w_{\rm dm}(a^{\prime})}{a^{\prime}}{\rm d}a^{\prime}\right),

(2)

where
\rho_{\rm dm,0}
is the dark matter density today and
a^{\prime}
a dummy variable for the integration by the scale factor. This general solution is derived from the assumption of energy conservation and the continuity equation. We consider the following function for the EoS,

w_{\rm dm}(a)=\begin{cases}w_{\rm dm,0}\left(\frac{a-a_{\rm nz}}{1-a_{\rm nz}}\right),\,\,&\text{for }a\geq a_{\rm nz},\\
0\vphantom{\frac{0}{0}},&\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}

(3)

i.e.
a linearly increasing/decreasing EoS where
w_{\rm dm,0}
is the current value of the dark matter EoS and
a_{\rm nz}
the scale factor at which dark matter’s EoS becomes non-zero. At early times (i.e. for
a<a_{\rm nz}
) constraints from CMB measurements have shown the EoS must be very close to zero (
|w_{\rm dm}|\lesssim 0.001
)
Xu and Chang 2013
;
Kopp et al. 2018
, however, at late times the constraints are more relaxed
Kopp et al. 2018
. For this reason we fix the EoS at early times to zero and allow the EoS to be non-zero at late times. However, this means the EoS is non-differentiable at
a_{\rm nz}
, a detail that should be revisited in future studies of the model. Generalised dark matter models with a cosmological constant, like the model considered here, are equivalent to interacting dark energy models
de Cesare and Wilson-Ewing 2022
, models where dark matter interacts with dark energy. We can therefore think of this model describing a dark matter-dark energy interaction
Farrar and Peebles 2004
, which could occur only at low energies or densities, or through the decay of unstable dark matter particles with very long half-lives (see
Peebles 2012
for the evanescent matter model with similar properties). In both cases the interaction is only relevant at very late-times and would grow over time, following the behaviour of
\rm\Lambda eDM
. In section
III.1
we will show the sensitivity to
w_{\rm dm,0}
is much stronger than
a_{\rm nz}
and that the parameters are strongly degenerate. We break this degeneracy by setting
a_{\rm nz}=0.5
, meaning dark matter’s EoS only becomes non-zero at
z\leq 1
.

The total matter density is defined by

\rho_{\rm m}(a)=\frac{\rho_{\rm m,0}}{a^{3}}\,\Gamma(a),

(4)

with the additional term

\Gamma(a)=f_{\rm b}+f_{\rm\nu}+f_{\rm dm}\,W(a).

(5)

Here
f_{\rm i}
is the fraction of the matter constituent
\rm i
to the total matter content today (
i.e.

f_{\rm i}=\rho_{\rm i,0}/\rho_{\rm m,0}
); the subscripts
\rm b
indicate baryons,
\nu
non-relativistic neutrinos and
\rm dm
dark matter. Another useful relation is the derivative of
\Gamma(a)
with respect to
\ln a
, which is given by

\Upsilon(a)=\frac{{\rm d}\,\Gamma(a)}{{\rm d}\ln a}=3\,f_{\rm dm}\,\frac{w_{\rm dm}(a)\,W(a)}{\Gamma(a)}.

(6)

In
\rm\Lambda CDM
these functions simplify to
\Gamma(a)=1
and
\Upsilon(a)=0
.

We modify the background evolution and the linear perturbation equations of dark matter in the cosmological Boltzmann solver
CLASS

(
Blas et al. 2011
)
, following the above relations and equation 2 of
(
Kopp et al. 2018
)
. From
CLASS
we output the power spectrum and growth functions.

To understand the role that
\rm\Lambda eDM
may play in the
H_{0}
tension we fix the conditions of the early universe to those inferred by Planck CMB measurements
(
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020a
)
. This is carried out by fixing the primordial power spectrum, optical depth and the physical densities of baryons and dark matter to
A_{\rm s}=2.101\times 10^{-9}
,
n_{\rm s}=0.9649
,
\tau=0.0544
and
\Omega_{\rm b}h^{2}=0.02236
, respectively. For dark matter this means fixing its value at early times to

\Omega_{\rm dm}W_{0}h^{2}=\Omega_{\rm dm}^{\rm init}h^{2}=0.1202,

(7)

where
\Omega_{\rm dm}^{\rm init}
is the fractional density of dark matter if the EoS is zero and
W_{0}=W(a_{\rm nz})
.

In Fig.
1
we fix the parameters of
\rm\Lambda eDM
as described above while varying a single parameter – the Hubble constant
H_{0}
. In this plot we show that changing
H_{0}
to values above CMB constraints (of
H_{0}=67.27\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
indicated with a solid blue line) requires dark matter’s EoS to be negative. A consequence of a high
H_{0}
is a decrease in the power spectrum amplitude and therefore low
\sigma_{8}
. Since
\sigma_{8}\propto S_{8}
this naturally couples the two tensions. If
H_{0}
is set to the values observed by SH0ES
(
Riess et al. 2021a
)
(of
H_{0}=73.3\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
) we obtain
\sigma_{8}
\sim 0.5
.

II.2
Implications for the ISW and lensing

The ISW
T_{\rm ISW}
is defined as

\frac{T_{\rm ISW}(\hat{\eta})}{T}=\frac{2}{c^{3}}\int_{0}^{\chi_{\rm LS}}\dot{\Phi}\left[\chi\hat{\eta},t(\chi)\right]\,a(\chi)\,{\rm d}\chi,

(8)

where
\dot{\Phi}
is the time-derivative of the gravitational potential
\Phi
,
c
is the speed of light,
\chi
is the transverse comoving distance in a direction
\hat{\eta}
and
\chi_{\rm LS}
is the comoving distance to the last scattering surface. The gravitational potential is related to the density contrast
\delta
by the Poisson equation which in the linear regime simplifies to

\Phi(x,t)=\frac{3}{2}H_{0}^{2}\,\Omega_{\rm m}\,\Gamma(t)\,\frac{D(t)}{a(t)}\,\nabla^{-2}\delta(x,0),

(9)

where
\Omega_{\rm m}
is the total fractional matter density today,
D(t)
the linear growth function and
\delta(x)
the density contrast today (
i.e.
at
z=0
). Consequently, the time-derivative is given by

\dot{\Phi}(x,t)=H(t)\,\Big[f(t)-1+\Upsilon(t)\Big]\Phi(x,t).

(10)

where
f(t)
is the linear growth rate.

The lensing convergence is given by

\kappa(\hat{\eta})=\frac{3H_{0}^{2}}{2c^{2}}\,\Omega_{\rm m}\,\int_{0}^{\chi_{\rm s}}\frac{\chi}{\chi_{\rm s}}\,(\chi_{\rm s}-\chi)\frac{\Gamma(\chi)}{a(\chi)}\,\delta(\chi)\,{\rm d}\chi,

(11)

where
\chi_{\rm s}
is the comoving distance to the lensing source.

Both the ISW and lensing are dependent on
\Gamma(t)
(see Eqs.
8
,
9
,
10
and
11
), while the ISW is also dependent on
\Upsilon(t)
(see Eq.
8
and Eq.
10
). This means the ISW and lensing signals will be different in
\rm\Lambda eDM
than in
\rm\Lambda CDM
and can be constrained from studies cross-correlating galaxy surveys with weak lensing and CMB temperature maps. However, cross-correlation studies of the ISW typically have very large errors since the signal is highest on large angular scales and subject to cosmic variance (see
Hang et al. 2021
). This makes distinguishing the effect of
\rm\Lambda eDM
this way rather challenging. Voids on the other hand allow us to improve the signal-to-noise by stacking void-ISW signals. They are extremely sensitive to
\rm\Lambda eDM
because they accumulate the departures in
\Gamma(t)
and
\Upsilon(t)
from
\rm\Lambda CDM
; an effect which is more relevant for larger voids elongated along the LOS, such as those measured by photometric surveys.

The ISW and
\kappa
profiles for three types of voids are explored, assuming the elliptical void profile of
(
Kovács and García-Bellido 2016
)

z
voids refers to typical voids found from spectroscopic surveys
(
Nadathur and Hotchkiss 2015

and for which the role of the ISW from voids has generated considerable discussion

\rm\Lambda CDM

\rm\Lambda eDM

\rm\Lambda CDM

\sqrt{\sigma_{8}^{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}/\sigma_{8}^{\Lambda{\rm eDM}}}

is the
\sigma_{8}
obtained from
\rm\Lambda CDM
and
\sigma_{8}^{\Lambda{\rm eDM}}
from
\rm\Lambda eDM
.

III.2
Parameter Inference

Constraints on cosmological parameters were obtained using the cosmological Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) software
Cobaya

Torrado and Lewis 2019
;
Torrado and Lewis 2021
. This was done using a combination of Planck CMB measurements, Pantheon Type Ia supernovae, constraints on the absolute magnitude of Type Ia supernovae, baryonic acoustic oscillations and redshift space distortion measurements. Specifically:

(i)

Planck CMB measurements of temperature, polarisation auto/cross correlation for high-
\ell
(i.e.
\ell>30
), temperature and polarisation auto correlation for low-
\ell
(i.e.
\ell\leq 30
) and lensing
Planck Collaboration et al. 2020b
. The joint likelihood for Planck CMB measurements are referred to in the paper as P18.

(ii)

Pantheon Type Ia supernovae
Scolnic et al. 2018
referred to as SN.

(iii)

SH0ES Cepheid constraints on the Type Ia absolute magnitude
Riess et al. 2021b
, referred to as SN+
M_{B}
.

(iv)

Baryonic acoustic oscillations and redshift space distortion measurements from 6dF, SDSS DR7 and BOSS
(
Beutler et al. 2012
;
Ross et al. 2015
;
Alam et al. 2017
)
, which are referred to as BAO.

\rm\Lambda CDM
and
\rm\Lambda eDM
are obtained by sampling the posterior by running an MCMC. In both cases, this means varying the standard
\rm\Lambda CDM
parameters, i.e. primordial power spectrum amplitude
A_{\rm s}
, the spectral tilt
n_{\rm s}
, the sound horizon
\theta_{*}
, the optical depth
\tau
, baryon density
\Omega_{\rm b}h^{2}
and dark matter density
\Omega_{\rm dm}h^{2}
. For
\rm\Lambda eDM
we also vary the dark matter EoS today
w_{\rm dm,0}
. In
\rm\Lambda eDM
there is a strong degeneracy between
\Omega_{\rm dm}
and
w_{\rm dm,0}
, which makes sampling the posterior extremely difficult. To remove this degeneracy and improve the efficiency of the MCMC sampling, we instead sample the initial dark matter density, i.e.
\Omega_{\rm dm}^{\rm init}
which is related to
\Omega_{\rm dm}
by the relation
\Omega_{\rm dm}=\Omega_{\rm dm}^{\rm init}/W_{0}
, for
\rm\Lambda CDM
the two are equivalent since
W_{0}=1
. Note, the addition of the Type Ia absolute magnitude from SH0ES means we have to additionally sample
M_{B}
as a sampled parameter. In Fig.
5
we show the posterior of the sampled
\rm\Lambda eDM
parameters, showing that with the exception of
w_{\rm dm,0}
the posteriors are generally quite Gaussian. A comparison to
\rm\Lambda CDM
shows the constraints on these parameters are close to identical for P18, since the high redshift CMB measurements are unable to constrain the late evolution of dark matter’s EoS. To ensure the MCMC chains have converged we make use of
Cobaya
’s inbuilt measure of the
R-1
Gelman-Rubin statistics
Lewis 2013
. The chains are assumed to be converged once
R-1<0.01
.

Defining
S_{8}
for
\rm\Lambda eDM
is rather challenging since the general definition
S_{8}=\sigma_{8}\sqrt{0.3/\Omega_{\rm m}}
in the literature generally assumes the contribution of cold dark matter to
\Omega_{\rm m}
. Assuming that this functional form is really trying to capture an amplitude to the power spectra and define this in relation to the fiducial
\rm\Lambda CDM
constraints where
\Omega_{\rm m}\simeq 0.3
the most sensible definition of
S_{8}
will use
\Omega_{\rm dm}^{\rm init}
since this sets the shape and amplitude of the initial power spectra and using
\sigma_{8}
which captures
\rm\Lambda eDM
’s dampening effect. Therefore, we define

S_{8}=\sigma_{8}\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm m}^{\rm init}/0.3}

(12)

where
\Omega_{\rm m}^{\rm init}=\Omega_{\rm dm}^{\rm init}+\Omega_{\rm b}+\Omega_{\nu}^{\rm NR}
and
\Omega_{\nu}^{\rm NR}
is the contribution from non-relativistic neutrinos.

V
Discussions

In this paper we introduce
\rm\Lambda eDM
, a phenomenological model of dark matter with a non-zero EoS at late times. We show that
\rm\Lambda eDM
is able to simultaneously ease the
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
tensions while also providing an explanation for the void-ISW anomaly. This is the first model, to our knowledge, that can consistently explain all three with a single modification.

Previous solutions to the
H_{0}
tension (such as early dark energy
Poulin et al. 2019
) provide a high
H_{0}
at the cost of greater clustering and therefore larger
\sigma_{8}
– putting them in conflict with the
S_{8}
tension
(
Vagnozzi 2021
;
García Escudero et al. 2022
)
.
\rm\Lambda eDM
, on the other hand, does not require changing the physical densities of dark matter at last scattering but instead imposes a late change to the evolution of dark matter. Since dark matter has never been directly observed, it is reasonable to suspect the simple assumptions of cold dark matter may be incomplete. Best fit constraints on
\rm\Lambda eDM
imply dark matter’s EoS is negative at late times, strongly implying interactions between dark matter and dark energy
(
de Cesare and Wilson-Ewing 2022
)
, which have been shown to alleviate both the
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
tensions
(
Pourtsidou and Tram 2016
;
Kumar and Nunes 2016
;
Kumar et al. 2019
;
Di Valentino et al. 2020
;
Lucca and Hooper 2020
;
Poulin et al. 2022
)
; if this is proven to be true this will have a profound impact on fundamental physics.

However
\rm\Lambda eDM
does not only alleviate the
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
tensions, it also provides an explanation to a long-standing anomaly -- the void-ISW anomaly, while remaining consistent with void lensing measurements. This allows the model to be tested in the near future, including testing other shapes and parameterisations for the EoS, from galaxy surveys such as Euclid
1
1

1

https://www.euclid-ec.org/
and LSST
2
2

2

https://www.lsst.org/
. So far, observations appear to be consistent with constraints to
\rm\Lambda eDM
, with the ISW of voids predicted to be roughly a factor of
\sim 2
and lensing to be a factor of
\sim 0.9
with respect to
\rm\Lambda CDM
. Future measurements of void ISW and lensing, and other probes (such as galaxy cluster abundances, galaxy lensing and CMB temperature auto and cross-correlations) will allow us to provide tighter constraints on
\rm\Lambda eDM
and help determine whether new physics in dark matter (or an interacting dark sector) is the cause of the
H_{0}
and
S_{8}
tension.

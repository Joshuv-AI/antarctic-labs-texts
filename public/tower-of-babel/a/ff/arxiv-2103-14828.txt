Specialization morphisms
Ildar Gaisin, John Welliaveetil

Abstract
. We define the notion of a specialization morphism from a locally noetherian analytic adic space to a scheme. This captures the (classical) specialization morphism associated to a formal scheme. There is a well behaved theory of compactifications and it turns out that the classical specialization morphism is proper in this setup. As an application, we show that the nearby cycles functor commutes with lower shriek in great generality.

Unless otherwise stated, for what follows
X

By

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}

Definition 2.1

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}(U)

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U)\text{ }\lvert f\text{ vanishes at all points }x\in X\right\}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U)\text{ }\lvert\text{ }f_{x}\in\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}\right\},

where

is the image of

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}

We denote by

At this point

adic

(V)

Definition 2.2

The objects of the category
(V)

(X,\mathcal{O}_{X},(\lvert\cdot(x)\lvert)_{x\in X})

(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}

We will also compare
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
with the presheaf of topologically nilpotent elements (cf. Proposition

Definition 2.3
.

Let

\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}

denote the subpresheaf of ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}
, given by for an open

\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}(U):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U)\text{ }\lvert f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}(U)^{\circ\circ}\right\}

To get things off the ground, we begin by proving some expected inclusions.

Lemma 2.4
.

The presheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}
is a subpresheaf of
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
. Furthermore, for each
x\in X
,

\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subsetneq\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\circ\circ}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq X
be open. If
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}(U)
then
\lvert f(x)\lvert^{n}\to 0
for all
x\in U
(by continuity of the valuation
x
). In particular this implies the image of
f
in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
, must lie in
\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}
. Therefore
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}
is a subpresheaf of
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
.

All inclusions are now obvious except the first. The inequality is obvious due to the existence of pseudouniformizers in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\circ\circ}
. Thus it remains to show
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\circ\circ}
. We can assume

, where

is a Tate ring. Let
t
be any pseudouniformizer in

. Then observe that
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is uniquely
t

is a unit). Thus
\mathfrak{m}_{x}=t\cdot\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\circ\circ}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
x\in X
, the stalks
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\text{red}},x}
are naturally isomorphic to
k(x)^{+}/k(x)^{\circ\circ}
. In particular
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\text{red}},x}
are valuation rings.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
denote the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
. Then
k(x)^{+}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
and
k(x)^{\circ\circ}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
. The first point is by definition. To see the second point, first note that
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is radical and hence a prime ideal in
k(x)^{+}
(every radical ideal in a valuation ring is prime). Now
k(x)^{\circ\circ}
is the unique prime ideal of
k(x)^{+}
of height 1 so it suffices to show

0\neq\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset k(x)^{\circ\circ}.

The inequality follows from Lemma
2.4
, so we prove the inclusion. Let
y\in X
be the unique rank 1 generalization of
x
. Let
\tilde{z}\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
be any element. Then
\tilde{z}
is the image of some element
z\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}(U)
for some
U\subset X
open containing both
x
and
y
. By definition the image of
z
in
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},y}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}
is topologically nilpotent in
k(y)
. Finally by
[
8
, Lemma 1.1.10(iii)]
the natural map
k(x)\to k(y)
is a homeomorphism onto it’s image and so in particular
k(x)^{\circ\circ}=k(y)^{\circ\circ}\cap k(x)
. Therefore
\tilde{z}\in k(x)^{\circ\circ}
.
We compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X_{\text{red}},x}

\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{\simeq}\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}

\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{\simeq}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})/(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x})

\displaystyle\overset{(iii)}{\simeq}k(x)^{+}/k(x)^{\circ\circ}

where (i) is by definition, (ii) follows from the identity
R/M=(R/I)/(M/I)
and (iii) follows from above.
∎

At the level of stalks we have the obvious inclusion
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}
. At rank 1 points this inclusion becomes an equality.

Corollary 2.7
.

Suppose
x\in X
corresponds to a rank 1 valuation (i.e. a maximal point). Then
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}=\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{+}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\text{red}},x}=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}=k(x)^{\circ}/k(x)^{\circ\circ}
is a field. Therefore
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}
must be the maximal ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}
.
∎

In some sense the sheaf
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
is a
universal
object which contains
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
and a pseudouniformizer at all stalks. The following lemma makes this precise.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be any subpresheaf of ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}
which is contained in
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
such that
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subsetneq\mathscr{F}_{x}
at all points
x\in X
and
\mathscr{F}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is a prime ideal.
Then the associated sheaf
\mathscr{F}^{\text{a}}=\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
.

Proof.

We have
0\neq\mathscr{F}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{+}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}=k(x)^{+}
and by the proof of Lemma
2.6
, the quotient
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
coincides with the unique prime ideal of
k(x)^{+}
of height 1. Therefore the first inclusion
\mathscr{F}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+},x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
is actually an equality. Therefore the inclusion
\mathscr{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
induces an isomorphism on stalks and one obtains the conclusion as sheafification does not change stalks.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following

\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}

Proposition 2.9
.

The sheafification of the presheaf of topologically nilpotent elements
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}
identifies with
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
.

Proof.

Let

.
By Lemma
2.4
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\circ\circ}

is a subpresheaf of
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}^{+}}
and

\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subsetneq\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\circ\circ}

Observe that for every rational subset

which is a neighbourhood of

is a radical ideal.

We deduce from this that

\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\circ\circ}
and

are radical as well.
Since

is a valuation ring, an ideal of

is radical if and only if it is prime.
It follows that

We can now conclude the proof by using Lemma
2.8

Lemma 3.3

6.

Our goal in this section is to prove a base change theorem for

Owing to the fact that we only construct fibre products for specialization
morphisms when the base change map is étale and the fact that arbitrary projective
limits of adic spaces do not exist, we formulate a

that takes into account these challenges.
In the case when the target of a proper specialization map is strictly local then we
have the familiar statement as in Corollary

We then employ Theorem

to

\Phi\colon j_{2!}j^{*}_{2}R^{+}\beta_{*}j_{1!}\to R^{+}\beta_{*}j_{1!}.

Observe that it suffices to show that
if

\sF\in\mathcal{D}^{+}(X_{\text{\'{e}t}},A)
,
t\in T\smallsetminus S

and
n\in\mathbb{Z}

then

[R^{n}\beta_{*}j_{1!}(\sF)]_{\overline{t}}=0
.
By Corollary
6.18
, for every

n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have an isomorphism

(27)

[R^{n}\beta_{*}j_{1!}(\sF)]_{\overline{t}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\varinjlim_{(W,\overline{w})}R^{n}\Gamma(\beta_{W}^{-1}(w),j_{1!}(\sF))

where the colimit on the right runs over all étale neighbourhoods
(W,\overline{w})

of
(T,\overline{t})
.
Let
(W,\overline{w})
be an étale neighbourhood of
(T,\overline{t})
and let

X_{W}
be the preimage of
X
for the morphism

Y_{W}\to Y
. If
j_{1W}
denotes the open embedding

X_{W}\hookrightarrow Y_{W}
then
by
[
8
, Theorem 5.2.2(iv)]
,
we have an isomorphism

(j_{1!}(\sF))_{|Y_{W}}\xrightarrow{\sim}j_{1W!}(\sF_{|X_{W}}).

Since
\beta_{W}^{-1}(w)\cap X_{W}=\emptyset
, we see that

R^{n}\Gamma(\beta_{W}^{-1}(w),j_{1!}(\sF))=0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

We work within the context of
Situation
7.2
.

(1)

Let
I
be an injective
A
-module on

. Then, for every

,

R^{n}\beta_{*}j_{1!}(I)=0.

(2)

There is a natural equivalence

R^{+}\beta_{*}\circ j_{1!}\xrightarrow{\sim}j_{2!}\circ R^{+}\alpha_{*}.

Proof.

We verify part (1) as follows. Let
Z
be the fibre product
Y\times_{T}S
, which exists by Proposition

. Consider the commutative diagram coming from the universal property of fibre products

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{4}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{2}}

Note that since

and

are étale, so is

by

By Remark

, since

).

Then

\displaystyle j_{2}^{*}\circ R^{+}\beta_{*}\circ j_{1!}

\displaystyle\overset{(i)}{=}R^{+}\gamma_{*}\circ j_{4}^{*}\circ j_{1!}

\displaystyle\overset{(ii)}{=}R^{+}\gamma_{*}\circ j_{3!}

\displaystyle\overset{(iii)}{=}R^{+}\alpha_{*}

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
\alpha\colon X\to S
be a separated and finite type specialization morphism. Let
g\colon S\to T
be a separated and
finite type morphism of schemes. Then
g\circ\alpha
is a separated and finite type specialization morphism. Moreover, if

T
is quasi-compact and quasi-separated,
there is a natural equivalence

R^{+}(g\circ\alpha)_{!}\simeq R^{+}g_{!}\circ R^{+}\alpha_{!}

of functors
\mathcal{D}^{+}(X_{\text{\'{e}t}},A)\to\mathcal{D}^{+}(T_{\text{\'{e}t}},A)
.

Proof.

The strategy is very similar to the proof of Lemma
5.3
. The fact that the composition
g\circ\alpha
is a separated and finite type specialization morphism follows from Proposition
3.22
. For the second part consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{X}^{/S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

where the top left triangle is the universal compactification of
\alpha
via Theorem

, the bottom right triangle is a compactification of

via
[

, Theorem 4.1]
. Let us
explain the top right square. The

is separated and of finite type by Proposition

. Thus again by Theorem

, it admits a universal compactification which we denote by the triple

. In particular all the horizontal arrows (in the diagram) are quasi-compact open embeddings and the vertical arrows are proper. Therefore the composition

Then

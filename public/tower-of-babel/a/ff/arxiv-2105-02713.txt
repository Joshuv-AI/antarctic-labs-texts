Two-loop corrections to the Higgs trilinear coupling in BSM models with classical scale invariance Talk presented at the International Workshop on Future Linear Colliders (LCWS2021), 15-18 March 2021. C21-03-15.1. Preprint: DESY-21-059 Preprint: OU-HET-1098
Johannes Braathen, Shinya Kanemura, Makoto Shimoda

Abstract
Classical scale invariance (CSI) is an attractive concept for BSM model building, explaining the apparent alignment of the Higgs sector and potentially relating to the hierarchy problem. Furthermore, a particularly interesting feature is that the Higgs trilinear coupling λ h ​ h ​ h \lambda_{hhh} is universally predicted at one loop in CSI models , and deviates by 67% from its (tree-level) SM prediction. This result is however modified at two loops, and we review in these proceedings our calculation Braathen et al. 2021 of leading two-loop corrections to λ h ​ h ​ h \lambda_{hhh} in models with classical scale invariance, taking as an example a CSI scenario of a Two-Higgs-Doublet Model. We find that the inclusion of two-loop effects allows distinguishing different scenarios with CSI, although the requirement of reproducing the known 125-GeV Higgs-boson mass severely restricts the allowed values of λ h ​ h ​ h \lambda_{hhh} .



I
Introduction

The Higgs trilinear coupling
\lambda_{hhh}
is a unique probe to investigate the structure of the Higgs sector and the nature of the electroweak phase transition (EWPT), as well as to search for indirect signs of New Physics. While the discovery of Higgs boson has established the mechanism of electroweak symmetry breaking (EWSB), little is known about the structure of the Higgs potential. Currently, only the location of the electroweak (EW) minimum – obtained from the Higgs vacuum expectation value (VEV) – and the curvature of the potential around this minimum – related to the Higgs mass – are known. The determination of
\lambda_{hhh}
is therefore of great importance, as this coupling decides behaviour of the Higgs potential away from its EW minimum. It is for example known that a large deviation (of order 20%) of
\lambda_{hhh}
from its SM prediction is necessary to have an EWPT of strong first-order nature
Grojean et al. 2005
;
*Kanemura:2004ch
– which is itself a requirement for successful EW baryogenesis scenarios
Sakharov 1967
;
*Kuzmin:1985mm
;
*Cohen:1993nk
.

The Higgs trilinear coupling can also play an important role in scrutinising possible Beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) theories. One such example comes from theories with classical scale invariance (CSI), in which mass-dimensionful terms are forbidden in the Lagrangian at the tree level. While this symmetry is often imposed at the Planck scale to address the hierarchy problem, requiring CSI at (or around) the EW scale is also of great interest for model building, for a number of phenomenological reasons. First, such models constitute an ideal example of
alignment

Gunion and Haber 2003

without decoupling
: the couplings of the Higgs boson – which corresponds to the direction in field space along which the EW symmetry is broken radiatively, à la Coleman-Weinberg
Coleman and Weinberg 1973
;
*Gildener:1976ih
– are automatically SM-like at the tree level, and at the same time, BSM states cannot be decoupled because of the absence of mass terms in the Lagrangian. Another discriminative feature of these models is that at one loop they
universally
predict the value of
\lambda_{hhh}
to deviate by 67% from the corresponding tree-level SM prediction, as found in Ref.
Hashino et al. 2016a
.

Although this CSI prediction deviates significantly from the SM, it is still well within what is presently allowed by experimental results. Indeed, the current best limits on
\lambda_{hhh}
have been obtained by the ATLAS collaboration using LHC Run 2 single-Higgs production searches, and give
-3.2<{\lambda_{hhh}}/\lambda_{hhh}^{\text{SM}}<11.9
at 95% confidence level (CL)
ATL 2019
. However, the determination of
\lambda_{hhh}
will be substantially improved at future colliders: the HL-LHC could reach an accuracy of 50%
Cepeda et al. 2019
, while a lepton collider, such as the ILC, might achieve a precision of some tens of percent
Fujii et al. 2017
;
*Roloff:2019crr
(with high centre-of-mass energies) – for a complete review, see
e.g.
Ref.
de Blas et al. 2019
. The large one-loop deviation in
\lambda_{hhh}
predicted in CSI models would then be detectable in a foreseeable future.

It is then also important to investigate how higher-order effects affect this prediction, especially as two-loop corrections to
\lambda_{hhh}
have been found to be significant in several non-CSI models
Braathen and Kanemura 2019
;
*Braathen:2019zoh
(see also Ref.
Senaha 2019
). For this reason, we computed in Ref.
Braathen et al. 2021
the dominant two-loop corrections to
\lambda_{hhh}
in models with CSI. Crucially, we found that the inclusion of two-loop contributions spoils the universality of the prediction for
\lambda_{hhh}
– a result we illustrated both in an
N
-scalar model and in a CSI variant of Two-Higgs-Doublet Model (2HDM). Even though the allowed ranges of BSM parameters are strongly constrained in CSI models, in particular by perturbative unitarity
Lee et al. 1977
and the requirement of reproducing the known 125-GeV mass of the SM-like Higgs boson, the two-loop corrections to
\lambda_{hhh}
give rise to a further deviation of 10-30% from the SM, and can allow distinguishing different scenarios of a given CSI model. Summarising our findings from Ref.
Braathen et al. 2021
, we discuss in these proceedings how the calculation of the Higgs trilinear coupling in CSI theories is modified at two loops, before showing examples of numerical results for a CSI 2HDM.

II
The Higgs trilinear coupling in CSI theories

We begin by describing the effective-potential calculation of the Higgs trilinear coupling in CSI theories, first reproducing the known universal one-loop result of
e.g.
Ref.
Hashino et al. 2016a
, and next illustrating how this is altered at two loops. At both one- and two-loop orders, the computation of contributions to the effective potential
V_{\text{eff}}
is greatly simplified in CSI theories. This is because all mass-dimensionful parameters in the Lagrangian vanish due to the requirement of classical scale invariance, and thus the only possible source of mass is through a particle’s coupling to the Higgs boson and its VEV
v
. In turn, this means that the field-dependent mass
m_{i}(h)
of any particle
i
can always be expressed in terms of its mass-independent mass
m_{i}
as

\displaystyle m_{i}^{2}(h)=m_{i}^{2}\left(1+\frac{h}{v}\right)^{2}\,,

(1)

no matter the nature of field
i
.

II.1
\lambda_{hhh}
at one loop

At one loop, the effective potential is calculated using the well-known supertrace formula
Jackiw 1974
. Together with eq. (
1
), one finds that along the Higgs-field direction
V_{\text{eff}}
can be written as

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}(h)=A^{(1)}\ (v+h)^{4}+B^{(1)}\ (v+h)^{4}\log\frac{(v+h)^{2}}{Q^{2}}\,,

(2)

where
Q
is the renormalisation scale and the dimensionless coefficients
A^{(1)}
and
B^{(1)}
read

\displaystyle A^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}v^{4}}\bigg\{\tr\left[M_{S}^{4}\left(\log\frac{M_{S}^{2}}{v^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]-4\tr\left[M_{f}^{4}\left(\log\frac{M_{f}^{2}}{v^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]+3\tr\left[M_{V}^{4}\left(\log\frac{M_{V}^{2}}{v^{2}}-\frac{5}{6}\right)\right]\bigg\}\,,

\displaystyle B^{(1)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}v^{4}}\big\{\tr\left[M_{S}^{4}\right]-4\tr\left[M_{f}^{4}\right]+3\tr\left[M_{V}^{4}\right]\big\}\,,

(3)

with
M_{S},\,M_{f},\,M_{V}
the scalar, fermion, and gauge-boson mass matrices respectively.

If we want to derive
\lambda_{hhh}
by an effective-potential calculation, we must also take into account the conditions imposed by the lower-order derivatives of
V_{\text{eff}}
,
i.e.
the tadpole equation and the Higgs effective-potential mass. Specifically, these yield the following relations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V_{\text{eff}}}{\partial h}\bigg|_{h=0}=0=

\displaystyle\ 2v^{3}\left(2A^{(1)}+B^{(1)}+2B^{(1)}\log\frac{v^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}V_{\text{eff}}}{\partial h^{2}}\bigg|_{h=0}\equiv[M_{h}^{2}]_{V_{\text{eff}}}=

\displaystyle\ 2v^{2}\left(6A^{(1)}+7B^{(1)}+6B^{(1)}\log\frac{v^{2}}{Q^{2}}\right)=8v^{2}B^{(1)}\,,

(4)

which allow determining
A^{(1)}
and
B^{(1)}
entirely in terms of the Higgs VEV
v
and its effective-potential (or curvature) mass
[M_{h}^{2}]_{V_{\text{eff}}}
. It then follows that the third derivative of
V^{(1)}
is also entirely fixed in terms of
v
and
[M_{h}^{2}]_{V_{\text{eff}}}
and one obtains
Hashino et al. 2016a

\displaystyle\lambda_{hhh}\equiv\frac{\partial^{3}V_{\text{eff}}}{\partial h^{3}}\bigg|_{h=0}=\frac{5[M_{h}^{2}]_{V_{\text{eff}}}}{v}=\frac{5}{3}(\lambda_{hhh}^{\text{SM}})^{(0)}\,,

(5)

where
(\lambda_{hhh}^{\text{SM}})^{(0)}
denotes the tree-level prediction for the trilinear coupling in the SM. It is important to stress here that this result is
totally independent
of the particle content or scenario of CSI model.

II.2
\lambda_{hhh}
at two loops

This discussion is however modified if one considers the inclusion of two-loop corrections to the effective potential, and to the Higgs trilinear coupling derived from it. Indeed, two-loop contributions to
V_{\text{eff}}
consist of one-particle-irreducible vacuum bubble diagrams, and their analytical expressions are known to contain not only non-logarithmic and logarithmic terms, but also squared-logarithmic terms – see
e.g.
Ref.
Ford et al. 1992
. This means that the potential is now of the form

\displaystyle V_{\text{eff}}=A\ (v+h)^{4}+B\ (v+h)^{4}\log\frac{(v+h)^{4}}{Q^{2}}+C\ \log^{2}\frac{(v+h)^{2}}{Q^{2}}\,,

(6)

where
A
and
B
receive both one- and two-loop contributions (respectively
A^{(1)},\ B^{(1)}
and
A^{(2)},\ B^{(2)}
), while
C
is a new type of coefficient that only enters from two loops.

If one then repeats the steps described above for the calculation of
\lambda_{hhh}
,
A
and
B
can still be eliminated using the tadpole equation and Higgs curvature mass relation, however,
C
remains. One obtains finally at two loops

\displaystyle{\lambda_{hhh}}=\frac{5[M_{h}^{2}]_{V_{\text{eff}}}}{v}+32Cv\,,

(7)

i.e.
there is a new contribution to the Higgs trilinear coupling that depends on the squared-logarithmic piece of the two-loop effective potential. Moreover, because the effective-potential contributions are typically model-dependent, this result means that the universality of the result for
\lambda_{hhh}
is
lost
at two loops.

III
\lambda_{hhh}
at two loops in a CSI 2HDM

We consider now a specific model, namely the classically scale-invariant version of the Two-Higgs-Doublet Model, devised in Ref
Lee and Pilaftsis 2012
(and studied also in Refs.
Lane and Shepherd 2019
;
Lane and Pilon 2020
;
Brooijmans et al. 2020
). Although it shares many similarities to the usual 2HDM (see
e.g.
Ref.
Branco et al. 2012
for a review), the CSI 2HDM differs by some key aspects which we briefly recall here. First of all, the scalar potential contains no mass terms, only quartic interactions, and is written, assuming CP conservation, in terms of two
SU(2)_{L}
doublets
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
of hypercharge
\frac{1}{2}
as

\displaystyle V^{(0)}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}|\Phi_{1}|^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{4}+\lambda_{3}|\Phi_{1}|^{2}|\Phi_{2}|^{2}+\lambda_{4}|\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{5}\big[(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2})^{2}+\text{h.c.}\big]\,.

(8)

The physical spectrum is the same as in the usual 2HDM, with two CP-even Higgs bosons
h
and
H
(we assume the lighter one
h
to be SM-like), a CP-odd Higgs boson
A
, and a charged Higgs boson
H^{\pm}
. However, a particular feature of the CSI 2HDM is that its scalar sector is automatically aligned at tree level – this can be understood as a consequence of the relations between quartic couplings arising from the tadpole equations (in the absence of mass terms in
V^{(0)}
).

The steps of our computation of the leading two-loop contributions to
\lambda_{hhh}
can be summarised as follows (for more details we refer the reader to Ref.
Braathen et al. 2021
):

1.

We start by computing the dominant contributions to the two-loop effective potential
V^{(2)}
, employing expressions from Refs.
Martin 2002
;
*Martin:2003qz
;
*Degrassi:2009yq
;
*Braathen:2016cqe
. In the CSI 2HDM, these are diagrams involving the heavy BSM scalars and the top quark.

2.

We then extract from these the coefficient of the terms proportional to
\log^{2}(v+h)^{2}/Q^{2}
– what we had defined as
C
in eq. (
6
).

3.

This allows us to derive directly an
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme expression for
\lambda_{hhh}
, using eq. (
7
).

4.

Finally, to obtain an on-shell scheme result for the Higgs trilinear coupling, we include the necessary finite counterterms to express our result in terms of OS renormalised quantities, and we include finite wave-function renormalisation effects.

Detailed expressions for the two-loop corrections to
\lambda_{hhh}
in the CSI 2HDM can be found in section IV and appendix C of Ref.
Braathen et al. 2021
.
At this point, we should emphasise that we are performing here an effective-potential calculation of
\lambda_{hhh}
, and thereby we are not including effects from non-vanishing external momenta – however, we expect these to be subleading. We will also neglect any loop induced deviation from the alignment occurring at tree level – these effects are known to be small,
c.f.
Refs.
Lane and Pilon 2020
;
Braathen et al. 2017
.

\delta R
in the Higgs trilinear coupling, defined as
\delta R\equiv\lambda_{hhh}^{\text{2HDM}}/\lambda_{hhh}^{\text{SM}}-1
.

IV
Conclusion

We have summarised here our recent paper
Braathen et al. 2021
, in which we performed the first explicit two-loop calculation of the Higgs trilinear coupling
\lambda_{hhh}
in theories with CSI. Our results are significant in that they match the level of accuracy reached for non-CSI extensions of the SM in Refs.
Braathen and Kanemura 2019
;
*Braathen:2019zoh
. Furthermore, we have shown that the inclusion of two-loop contributions breaks the universality of the prediction of
\lambda_{hhh}
in CSI models. This implies that accessing the value
\lambda_{hhh}
could allow distinguishing different scenarios and different parameter points of a given CSI model. However, we have also pointed out the important fact that perturbative unitarity, as well as the requirement of generating the correct SM-like Higgs mass at loop level, strongly constrain the allowed range of parameters in CSI theories, and in turn the possible values of
\lambda_{hhh}
. For the CSI 2HDM considered here, we predict a BSM deviation of 90-110% of the Higgs trilinear coupling from its (two-loop) SM prediction. Finally, while it may be difficult to use a measurement of the Higgs trilinear coupling on its own to distinguish variants of a BSM theory with or without CSI – as we saw in figs.
1
and
3
– we note that this could be achieved using the
synergy
of a measurement of
\lambda_{hhh}
with either signals from collider searches, or gravitational-wave experiments – as studied for instance in Ref.
Hashino et al. 2016b
.

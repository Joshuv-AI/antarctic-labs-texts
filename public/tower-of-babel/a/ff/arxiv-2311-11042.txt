A New Invariant of Lattice polytopes
Winfried Bruns, Takayuki Hibi

Abstract
. The maximal degree of monomials belonging to the unique minimal system of monomial generators of the canonical module ω ⁡ ( K ⁡ [ 𝒫 ] ) \omega(K[{\mathcal{P}}]) of the toric ring K ⁡ [ 𝒫 ] K[{\mathcal{P}}] defined by a lattice polytope 𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} will be studied. It is shown that if 𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} possesses an interior lattice point, then the maximal degree is at most dim 𝒫 − 1 \dim{\mathcal{P}}-1 , and that this bound is the best possible in general.

Primary 52B20; Secondary 05E40

Introduction

The original motivation of the present paper is to investigate the maximal degree of monomials belonging to the unique minimal system of monomial generators of the canonical module of the toric ring defined by a lattice polytope.

Let
T=K[x_{1},x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{m},x_{m}^{-1},t,t^{-1}]
denote the Laurent polynomial ring in
m+1
variables over a field
K
. One associates each
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
with the Laurent monomial
{\bf x}^{\mathbf{a}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{a_{m}}\in T
. Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d
(we do not exclude
d<m
). One naturally identifies
{\mathcal{P}}
with
{\mathcal{P}}\times\{1\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
. Let
C({\mathcal{P}})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
denote the rational polyhedral cone spanned by
{\mathcal{P}}\times\{1\}
. The
toric ring
of
{\mathcal{P}}
is the subring

K[{\mathcal{P}}]=K[{\bf x}^{\mathbf{a}}t^{n}:({\mathbf{a}},n)\in C({\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}]

of
T
. The algebraic and combinatorial study of toric rings is contained in several books, for example in
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
. We set
\deg({\bf x}^{\mathbf{a}}t^{n})=n
for all
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
. Then
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
is a positively graded domain. It is not necessarily generated by degree
1
elements; if it is so, one says that
{\mathcal{P}}
is integrally closed in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
or, in other terminology, satisfies the integer decomposition property (IDP). (For the normality of
{\mathcal{P}}
one must replace
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
as the lattice of reference by the sublattice generated by the lattice points in
{\mathcal{P}}
.)

The toric ring
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
is a normal semigroup ring, and therefore Cohen–Macaulay by Hochster’s theorem. By the theorem of Danilov–Stanley its canonical module
\omega(K[{\mathcal{P}}])
is generated by those
{\bf x}^{\mathbf{a}}t^{n}
for which
({\mathbf{a}},n)
is in the (relative) interior of
C({\mathcal{P}})
. Let
G(\omega(K[{\mathcal{P}}]))
denote the unique minimal system of monomial generators of
\omega(K[{\mathcal{P}}])
. We study the maximal degree of monomials belonging to
G(\omega(K[{\mathcal{P}}]))
. Among several results, especially Corollary
3.4
says that, if a lattice polytope of dimension
d\geq 2
possesses an interior lattice point, then the maximal degree of monomials in
G(\omega(K[{\mathcal{P}}]))
is at most
d-1
. Furthermore, the upper bound is best possible (Corollary
4.4
).

Normaliz

[
3
]
was helpful to the preparation of the present paper. The methods used below are inspired by those in
[
1
, Section 1.2]
.

Instead of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
one can start with a free abelian group
M
as is common in toric geometry. However, since our target is convex polytopes themselves, in the present paper, we work in the frame of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
.

1.
Reduced
{\mathcal{P}}
-degree

In the following we will often switch from a polytope
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
to
{\mathcal{P}}\times\{1\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
without changing the name, and we may also identify monomials and lattice points.

Recall that a convex polytope
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of dimension
d
is a
lattice polytope
if each vertex of
{\mathcal{P}}
belongs to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
. Let

M({\mathcal{P}})=\sum_{x\,\in\,({\mathcal{P}}\times\{1\})\,\cap\,{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\,x\subset C({\mathcal{P}})

be the semigroup generated by
({\mathcal{P}}\times\{1\})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
. Furthermore, let
\operatorname{Int}C({\mathcal{P}})
denote the interior of
C({\mathcal{P}})
and
M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})=\operatorname{Int}C({\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
. As already said, the monomials associated with the lattice points in
M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
span the canonical module of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
.

In accordance with the degree of monomials introduced above, the degree of
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d},y_{m+1})
belonging to
C({\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
is
\deg y=y_{m+1}
. Each
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
can be expressed as
y=z+w
with
z\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
and
w\in M({\mathcal{P}})
in such a way that
z
has minimal degree for all possible choices of
w
. The
reduced
{\mathcal{P}}
-degree
of
y
is

\operatorname{rdeg}y=\operatorname{rdeg}_{\mathcal{P}}y=\deg z.

One says that
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
is
M({\mathcal{P}})
-irreducible
if
\operatorname{rdeg}y=\deg y
. Clearly the set
G({\mathcal{P}})
of all
M({\mathcal{P}})
-irreducible elements is the unique minimal system of generators of
M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
with respect to the action of
M({\mathcal{P}})
by addition.

In the present paper, the invariant, called the
int
∗
degree
,

(1)

\max\{\operatorname{rdeg}y:y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})\}

of
{\mathcal{P}}
is studied. As indicated above, a bound for this invariant implies the same bound for the minimal set of generators of the canonical module of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
.

2.
Lattice simplices

First, the int
∗
degree (
1
) of a lattice simplex is studied.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a lattice simplex of dimension
d
. Then

\max\{\operatorname{rdeg}y:y\in M^{*}(\sigma)\}\leq d+1.

Proof.

Let
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be the vertices of
{\mathcal{P}}
. Each
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
is expressed uniquely as
y=\sum_{i=0}^{d}q_{i}x_{i}
with each
q_{i}>0
. Let
q_{i}^{\prime}=q_{i}-\lceil q_{i}-1\rceil
. Then
0<q_{i}\leq 1
. One has
y=z+w
, where

z=\sum_{i=0}^{d}q^{\prime}_{i}x_{i},\qquad w=\sum\lceil q_{i}-1\rceil x_{i}.

Since
\deg z\leq d+1
, one has
\operatorname{rdeg}y\leq d+1
, as desired.
∎

A lattice simplex
\sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is said to be
empty
if
\sigma\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
coincides with the set of vertices of
\sigma
. Nontrivial empty
d
-simplices exist for all
d\geq 3
; see
[
2
, Remark 2.55]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a lattice simplex of dimension
d
. If
\sigma
is nonempty, then

\max\{\operatorname{rdeg}y:y\in M^{*}(\sigma)\}\leq d.

Proof.

Let
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be the vertices of
\sigma
. The proof of Lemma
2.1
says that
y\in M^{*}(\sigma)
is
M(\sigma)
-irreducible if and only if

(2)

y=\sum_{i=0}^{d}q_{i}x_{i},\qquad 0<q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{d}\leq 1.

Our work is then to show that
y
cannot be
M(\sigma)
-irreducible if
\deg y=d+1
. Obviously, one has
y=x_{0}+\cdots+x_{d}
if
\deg y=d+1
. Since
\sigma
is nonempty, there exists
x\in\sigma\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
with
x=\sum_{i=0}^{d}r_{i}x_{i}
with each
0\leq r_{i}<1
and
\sum_{i=0}^{d}r_{i}=1
. Since
0\leq r_{i}<1
for all
i
, one has
y-x\in M^{*}(\sigma)
. Thus
y
cannot be
M(\sigma)
-irreducible.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a lattice simplex of dimension
d
and
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
the vertices of
\sigma
. If
\sigma
is empty, then

\operatorname{rdeg}(x_{0}+\cdots+x_{d})=d+1

and, for all
y\in M^{*}(\sigma)
, one has

\operatorname{rdeg}y\neq d.

Proof.

Since
\sigma
is empty,

M(\sigma)={\mathbb{Z}}_{+}x_{0}+\cdots+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}x_{d}

and
x_{0},\ldots,x_{d}
are linearly independent. Thus
(x_{0}+\cdots+x_{d})-w\not\in M^{*}(\sigma)
for all nonzero
w\in M(\sigma)
, and
x_{0}+\cdots+x_{d}
is
M(\sigma)
-irreducible.

Now, suppose that there exists
y\in M^{*}(\sigma)
with
\operatorname{rdeg}y=d
which is
M(\sigma)
-irreducible. Then
y
is of the form (
2
),
(x_{0}+\cdots+x_{d})-y
is of degree
1
and belongs to
C(\sigma)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
. Hence
(x_{0}+\cdots+x_{d})-y
must be a vertex of
\sigma
, say,
x_{0}
. Thus
y=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}
, which cannot belong to
M^{*}(\sigma)
.
∎

3.
Lattice polytopes

Now we study the int
∗
degree (
1
) of a general lattice polytope.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d
.

(a)

One has
\operatorname{rdeg}y\leq d+1
for all
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
.

(b)

The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{P}}
is not an empty simplex;

(ii)

\operatorname{rdeg}y\leq d
for all
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma
be a lattice triangulation of
{\mathcal{P}}
. In other words,
\Sigma
is a triangulation of
{\mathcal{P}}
for which each
\sigma\in\Sigma
is an empty simplex, and consequently, each
x\in{\mathcal{P}}\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
is a vertex of some
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
denote the subset of
\Sigma
consisting of those
\sigma\in\Sigma
for which
(\sigma\setminus\partial\sigma)\cap\partial{\mathcal{P}}=\emptyset
. (
\partial
denotes the relative boundary.)

Since
{\mathcal{P}}
is the disjoint union of
\sigma\setminus\partial\sigma
with
\sigma\in\Sigma
, it follows that
\operatorname{Int}C({\mathcal{P}})
is the disjoint union of
\operatorname{Int}C(\sigma)
with
\sigma\in\Sigma^{\prime}
. Thus (a) follows from Lemma
2.1
. On the other hand, (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) in (b) follows from Lemma
2.3
.

Now, in order to prove (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) in (b), suppose that
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
belongs to
\operatorname{Int}C(\sigma)
with
\sigma\in\Sigma^{\prime}
. One may assume that
\dim\sigma=d
. Since
\operatorname{rdeg}_{\mathcal{P}}y\leq\operatorname{rdeg}_{\sigma}y\leq d+1
, it is enough to exclude
\operatorname{rdeg}_{\sigma}y=d+1
. Let
\operatorname{rdeg}_{\sigma}y=d+1
. Then
y=x_{0}+x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}
, where
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
are the vertices of
\sigma
. Since
{\mathcal{P}}
is not an empty simplex, one has
{\mathcal{P}}\neq\sigma
, which guarantees the existence of a facet
\tau
of
\sigma
with
\tau\setminus\partial\tau\subset{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial{\mathcal{P}}
. Let, say,
x_{0}\not\in\tau
. Since
(\tau\setminus\partial\tau)\cap\partial{\mathcal{P}}=\emptyset
, it follows that
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}\in{\rm Int}C({\mathcal{P}})
. Thus
y=x_{0}+(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d})
and
\operatorname{rdeg}y\leq d
, a contradiction.
∎

When
({\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial{\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\neq\emptyset
, the invariant (
1
) can be improved.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d\geq 2
and suppose that
({\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial{\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\neq\emptyset
. Then
\operatorname{rdeg}y\leq d-1
for all
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.2
, a special lattice triangulation using interior lattice points effectively is required.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a lattice polytope with an interior lattice point. Then
{\mathcal{P}}
has a lattice triangulation
\Sigma
for which
\sigma\subset\partial{\mathcal{P}}
if all vertices of
\sigma
are in
\partial{\mathcal{P}}
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be a lattice triangulation of
\partial{\mathcal{P}}
and
x
an interior point of
{\mathcal{P}}
. Then one defines the triangulation
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{P}}
by
\Delta\cup\{\operatorname{conv}(\sigma,x):\sigma\in\Delta\}
. Finally
\Sigma
is obtained from
\Sigma^{\prime}
by stellar subdivision with respect to the remaining interior points of
{\mathcal{P}}
in an arbitrary order.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a lattice triangulation constructed in Lemma
3.3
. In particular, every
\sigma\in\Sigma
with
(\sigma\setminus\partial\sigma)\cap\partial{\mathcal{P}}=\emptyset
possesses a vertex belonging to
({\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial{\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
. Let
y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
belong to
\sigma\setminus\partial\sigma
with
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Let
y=\sum_{i=0}^{q}r_{i}x_{i}
, where
x_{0},\ldots,x_{q}
are the vertices of
\sigma
, where
x_{0}\in({\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial{\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
and where
q\leq d
. Let
s_{i}=r_{i}-\lceil r_{i}-1\rceil
. Then
0<s_{i}\leq 1
for
i=0,1,\ldots,q
and

y=\sum_{i=0}^{q}s_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{q}\lceil r_{i}-1\rceil x_{i}.

Assume first that
s_{0}<1
and
y_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{q}s_{i}x_{i}\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
. Then
\deg y_{0}=\sum_{i=0}^{q}s_{i}\leq q\leq d
. Thus, by using Lemma
2.3
, one has
\operatorname{rdeg}y_{0}<d
. Finally, if
s_{0}=1
, then
r_{0}
is a positive integer and

y=x_{0}+\left[(r_{0}-1)x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}r_{i}x_{i}\right].

Thus
\operatorname{rdeg}y=1
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d
and
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
its toric ring. Then the maximal degree of monomials belonging to the unique minimal system of monomial generators of the canonical module of
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
is bounded by
d
.

If
{\mathcal{P}}
possesses an interior lattice point, it is bounded by
d-1
.

Remark 3.5
.

The bound in Corollary
3.4
is the degree bound for the minimal system of generators of the canonical module with respect to the subalgebra generated by the degree
1
elements of the toric ring. In general the minimal system of generators with respect to the full toric ring is smaller, as Proposition
3.6
below shows, but in general it is difficult to get information on it. In the next section we will consider examples in which the toric ring is generated in degree
1
and the bound in Corollary
3.4
is optimal.

Recall that a lattice
d
-simplex with vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is
unimodular
if its
d
-dimensional volume is
1/d!
. An equivalent condition is that the lattice vectors
(v_{0},1),\dots,(v_{d},1)
generate a direct summand of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
, or in algebraic terms, that the toric ring is isomorphic to a polynomial ring in
d+1
variables, generated by the monomials
{\bf x}^{(v_{0},1)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(v_{d},1)}
as a
K
-algebra.
In particular a unimodular simplex is empty.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\sigma
be an empty lattice
d
-simplex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

(a)

If
\sigma
is unimodular, then the canonical module of
K[\sigma]
is generated by a single element of degree
d+1
as a
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
-module.

(b)

If
\sigma
is non-unimodular, then the canonical module of
K[\sigma]
is generated by elements of degree
\leq d-1
as a
K[{\mathcal{P}}]
-module.

Proof.

For (a) it is enough to observe that every lattice point in
\operatorname{Int}C({\mathcal{P}})
has a unique representation as a linear combination of
(v_{0},1),\dots,(v_{d},1)
with nonnegative coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For (b) we use that there must exist a nonzero
w=q_{0}(v_{0},1)+\dots+q_{d}(v_{d},1)\in C({\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
with
0\leq q_{i}<1
for
i=0,\dots,d
. After our previous results we must only exclude that
y=(v_{0},1)+\dots+(v_{d},1)
belongs to the minimal system of generators. But this is clear, since
y-w\in\operatorname{Int}C({\mathcal{P}})
.
∎

4.
Examples

It is easy to find a
d
-simplex
\sigma
with
\operatorname{rdeg}x=d
for all
x\in M^{*}(\sigma)
.

Example 4.1
.

Define
\sigma
by its vertices

0,2e_{1},e_{2}\dots,e_{d}
, where
e_{i}
is the
i
th unit vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Then
\operatorname{Int}C(\sigma)
is generated by
(1,\dots,1,d)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
with respect to the action of
M(\sigma)
by addition. Moreover the toric ring
K[\sigma]
is generated by the monomials
{\bf x}^{\mathbf{a}}t
where
{\mathbf{a}}\in M(\sigma)
.

Now we give an example that shows the optimality of Theorem
3.2
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, denote the polytope defined by the system of inequalities

\displaystyle 0\leq x_{i}\leq 2,\qquad 1\leq i\leq d-1,

\displaystyle 0\leq x_{d}\leq d,

\displaystyle x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}\leq d+1.

It is known
[
4
, Example 2.6 (c)]
that
{\mathcal{P}}
is a lattice polytope of dimension
d\geq 2
whose toric ring is generated in degree
1
. One has
(1,\ldots,1)\in{\mathcal{P}}\setminus\partial{\mathcal{P}}
. Let

y_{i}=(1,\ldots,1,(i-1)d+i,i)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1},\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,d-1.

Then
y_{i}\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
with
\deg y_{i}=i
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Each
y_{i}
is
M({\mathcal{P}})
-irreducible.

Proof.

Let
y_{i}=z+w
with
z\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})
and
w\in M({\mathcal{P}})
. Let

z=(1,\ldots,1,a,j),\qquad w=(0,\ldots,0,b,k).

Then

(d-1)+a<j(d+1),\qquad b\leq kd,\qquad i=j+k.

Thus

(i-1)d+i=a+b<j(d+1)-(d-1)+kd=(i-1)d+(j+1).

Hence
i<j+1
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

One has
\{\operatorname{rdeg}y:y\in M^{*}({\mathcal{P}})\}=\{1,2,\ldots,d-1\}
.

The above observation shows that Theorem
3.2
cannot be improved even if one replaces the action of
M({\mathcal{P}})
by that of the semigroup
C({\mathcal{P}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{m+1}
: the two semigroups coincide for
{\mathcal{P}}
.

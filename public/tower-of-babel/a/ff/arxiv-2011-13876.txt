On quotients of congruence subgroups of braid groups Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Classification: Primary 20F36. Secondary 20H05. Key words and phrases: Braid group, Congruence subgroup.
Jessica Appel, Wade Bloomquist, Katie Gravel, Annie Holden

Abstract
. The integral Burau representation provides a map from the braid group into a group of integral matrices. This allows for a definition of congruence subgroups of the braid group as the preimage of the usual principal congruence subgroups of integral matrices. We explore the structure these congruence subgroups by examining some of the quotients that may arise in the series induced by divisibility of levels. We build on the work of Stylianakis on symmetric quotients of congruence subgroups, which itself generalizes the quotient of the braid group by the pure braid group. We accomplish this by utilizing results of Newman on integral matrices and explicitly finding elements in the preimage of any transposition. Our generalization is made possible by avoiding the use of a generating set for congruence subgroups. We find further generalizations based on results of Brendle and Margalit as well as Kordek and Margalit on the level four congruence subgroup. This gives families of quotients which are not isomorphic to symmetric groups.



1.
Introduction

In this brief note, we use elementary techniques to explore how known results on factor groups in the series of congruence subgroups of braid groups can be generalized. For each non-negative integer
\ell
, the level
\ell
congruence subgroup of the braid group,
B_{n}[\ell]
, is the kernel of the composition of the integral Burau representation with the
\bmod

\ell
reduction map. The integral Burau representation has found uses in many branches of mathematics. For the sake of brevity, we refer the reader to the introduction of
[
3
]
where a list of citations covering applications in algebraic geometry, dynamics, number theory, and topology can be found. Additional details on the integral Burau representation can be found in Section
3
of Margalit’s survey of open problems and questions concerning mapping class groups
[
6
]
. Our perspective will be based on viewing the integral Burau representation of the braid group as an analog of the symplectic representation of the mapping class group of a surface. This viewpoint was noted in Section
2.1
of
[
3
]
for the unreduced Burau representation and is stated here in Lemma
2.2
for the reduced Burau representation, following the work of
[
4
]
.

Historically, the study of quotients of congruence subgroups of braid groups began with Arnol‘d
[
2
]
. He showed that the level
2
congruence subgroup of the braid group may be identified with the pure braid group. Alternatively stated as:

Theorem 1.1

(Arnol‘d, 1968)
.

For each
n
,

B_{n}/B_{n}[2]\cong S_{n}.

As a corollary to constructing generators of congruence subgroups of prime level Stylianakis,
[
8
]
, proved the following extension:

Theorem 1.2

(Stylianakis, 2016)
.

For each
n
and each odd prime
p
,

B_{n}[p]/B_{n}[2p]\cong S_{n}.

Without constructing a generating set, we provide an additional extension of this result using results on integral matrices due to Newman
[
7
]
and providing an explicit surjection from
B_{n}[\ell]
onto
S_{n}
, namely:

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.1
)
.

For each
n
and each odd
\ell
,

B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[2\ell]\cong S_{n}.

The first generalization of the above results to even levels, can be found in Brendle and Margalit’s study of the level
4
braid group
[
3
]
. They found the following:

Theorem 1.4

(Brendle and Margalit, 2018)
.

For each
n
,

B_{n}[2]/B_{n}[4]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{n}{2}}.

Again using results on integral matrices and providing the surjection explicitly, we extend as follows:

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

For each
n
and each odd
\ell
,

B_{n}[2\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{n}{2}}.

In exploring the cohomology of the level
4
subgroups, Kordek and Margalit,
[
5
]
, proved the following

Theorem 1.6

(Kordek and Margalit, 2019)
.

For each
n
,

B_{n}/B_{n}[4]\cong\mathcal{Z}_{n},

where
\mathcal{Z}_{n}
is a non-split extension of
S_{n}
by
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{n}{2}}
.

We see that taken with this result our Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
imply the following generalization:

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

For each
n
and each odd
\ell
,

B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell]\cong\mathcal{Z}_{n}.

A natural direction for future work would be to look into levels which are multiples of higher powers of
2
. The first step in this direction would be understanding either
B_{n}/B_{n}[8]
or
B_{n}[4]/B_{n}[8]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling the definition of congruence subgroups of braid groups. Our description of the integral Burau representation will be given explicitly on generators due to the nature of the arguments we will use below. Although this choice may seem to hide some of the rich topological content of this representation, we will describe how that can be recovered in Lemma
2.2
.

Definition 1
.

The reduced integral Burau representation is a representation of the
n-
strand braid group,
\rho_{-1}:B_{n}\rightarrow GL(n-1,\mathbb{Z})
defined on generators as

\displaystyle\rho_{-1}(\sigma_{1})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
1&1\end{array}\right)\oplus\text{Id}_{n-3}

\displaystyle\rho_{-1}(\sigma_{n-1})=\text{Id}_{n-3}\oplus\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)

and for
1<i<n-1

\rho_{-1}(\sigma_{i})=\text{Id}_{i-2}\oplus\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&-1&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&1&1\\
\end{array}\right)\oplus\text{Id}_{n-i-2}.

Now recall for integral matrices it is possible to define a natural reduction
\bmod

\ell
map component wise, namely

r_{\ell}:GL(n-1,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow GL(n-1,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}).

Then noting that each generator was sent to a integral matrix the level
\ell
congruence subgroup of the braid group is the kernel of the composition of these maps, meaning

B_{n}[\ell]:=ker(r_{\ell}\circ\rho_{-1}).

Remark 2.1
.

This definition is equivalent to the definition found in
[
3
]
, which uses the unreduced Burau reprsentation. The equivalence can be seen as the unreduced Burau representation factors into a direct sum of the reduced Burau representation and a
1-
dimensional representation.

We now provide a collection of lemmas.

Lemma 2.2

(Proposition
2.1

[
4
]
)
.

The reduced integral Burau representation is conjugate to the symplectic representation

\rho:B_{2g+b}\rightarrow\begin{cases}\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})&b=1\\
(\mathrm{Sp}(2g+2,\mathbb{Z}))_{y_{g+1}}&b=2\end{cases}

coming from the standard action on homology of the surface which double covers the punctured disk, where
(\mathrm{Sp}(2g+2,\mathbb{Z}))_{y_{g+1}}
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{Sp}(2g+2,\mathbb{Z})
which fixes the homology class associated one of the two boundary components.

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
m
and for each
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
\sigma_{i}^{m}\in B_{n}[m]
.

Proof.

This follows either from a direct computation of matrix multiplication using the above definition, or as a direct application of Lemma
2.2
. For a discussion of the latter approach see relation “
R3
: Powers of Dehn Twists” in
[
8
]
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For each
m
and for each
1\leq i<j\leq n
we have
A_{i,j}^{m}\in B_{n}[2m]
, where

A_{i,j}=\sigma_{j-1}\sigma_{j-2}...\sigma_{i+2}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}^{2}\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_{i+2}^{-1}...\sigma_{j-2}^{-1}\sigma_{j-1}^{-1}

are generators of
PB_{n}
.

Proof.

For any
i
and
j
, we have that
A_{i,j}
is a conjugate of
\sigma_{i}^{2}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\sigma_{i}^{2m}\in B_{n}[2m]
. Then as
r_{2m}\circ\rho_{-1}
is a group homomorphism, the result follows.
∎

Made relevant in this context by Lemma
2.2
, we have the following result on integral symplectic matrices due to Newman.

Lemma 2.5

(Theorem
VII.22

[
7
]
)
.

Let
D=\frac{ab}{GCD(a,b)}
, then

\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{Z})[D]=\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{Z})[a]\cap\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{Z})[b]

We now apply this to congruence subgroups of the braid group.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m=\prod_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
. Then

\bigcap_{i}B_{n}[p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}]=B_{n}[m],

and in particular, for
\ell
odd we have

B_{n}[2^{\alpha}\ell]=B_{n}[2^{\alpha}]\cap B_{n}[\ell].

Proof.

The following is a generalization of Lemma
6.2
in
[
8
]
. As any multiple of
m
is also a multiple of each
p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
we have immediately from the definition that
B_{n}[m]\leq\bigcap_{i}B_{n}[p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}]
. The reverse inclusion,

\bigcap_{i}B_{n}[p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}]\leq B_{n}[m]
,
follows from Lemma
2.2
and repeated applications of Lemma
2.5
.

∎

3.
Quotients of Congruence Subgroups

We may now prove our generalizations as stated in the introduction.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\ell
be odd, then

B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[2\ell]\cong S_{n}.

Proof.

The following is a variation of the proof of Theorem
6.1
given in
[
8
]
. We utilize our more general Lemma
2.6
, and the use of a generating set for
B_{n}[\ell]
is avoided.

Let
\tau:B_{n}\rightarrow S_{n}
be the natural surjective homomorphism which sends each braid generator
\sigma_{i}
to the transposition
(i,i+1)
. We have that
\ker(\tau)=PB_{n}=B_{n}[2]
, as proved by Arnol‘d
[
2
]
. Let
\tau_{\ell}
be the restriction of
\tau
to the subgroup
B_{n}[\ell],

\displaystyle\tau_{\ell}:=\tau|_{B_{n}[\ell]}:B_{n}[\ell]\rightarrow S_{n}.

As
\ell
is odd, we have that

\displaystyle\tau(\sigma_{i}^{\ell})=(i,i+1)^{\ell}=(i,i+1),

meaning
\tau_{\ell}
is surjective utilizing Lemma
2.3
.

Finally we have

\displaystyle\ker(\tau_{\ell})=\ker(\tau)\cap B_{n}[\ell]\cong B_{n}[2]\cap B_{n}[\ell]\cong B_{n}[2\ell],

where the last isomorphism is a consequence of Lemma
2.6
, and our result follows from the first isomorphism theorem.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\ell
be odd, then

B_{n}[2\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell]\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{n}{2}}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\phi:PB_{n}\rightarrow H_{1}(PB_{n};\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{n}{2}}

be the
\bmod

2
abelianization map of the pure braid group. An explicit description of
\phi
is given by viewing
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\binom{n}{2}}
as the free abelian group on the image of the
\binom{n}{2}
generators of
PB_{n}
which were denoted
A_{i,j}
in Corollary
2.4
. The kernel of
\phi
is known to be
B_{n}[4]
(the main theorem of
[
3
]
). Now let
\phi_{\ell}
be the restriction of
\phi
to the subgroup
B_{n}[2\ell]\leq B_{n}[2]=PB_{n}
. From Corollary
2.4
, we have that
A_{i,j}^{\ell}\in B_{n}[2\ell]
. Now as
\ell
is odd we have

\displaystyle\phi(A_{i,j}^{\ell})=\phi(A_{i,j})^{\ell}=\phi(A_{i,j}),

and so
\phi_{\ell}
is a surjection. Additionally utilizing Lemma
2.6
we have

\displaystyle ker(\phi_{\ell})=\ker(\phi)\cap B_{n}[2\ell]=B_{n}[4]\cap B_{n}[2]\cap B_{n}[\ell]=B_{n}[4]\cap B_{n}[\ell]=B_{n}[4\ell],

where we have also used that
B_{n}[4]\leq B_{n}[2]
and so
B_{n}[4]\cap B_{n}[2]=B_{n}[4].
Then our desired result follows from the first isomorphism theorem.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\ell
be odd, then we have

B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell]\cong B_{n}/B_{n}[4].

Proof.

The third isomorphism theorem gives us that

(B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell])/(B_{n}[2\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell])\cong B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[2\ell],

meaning the two rows of the following commutative diagram are exact:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}[2]/B_{n}[4]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}/B_{n}[4]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}/B_{n}[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}[2\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[4\ell]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{n}[\ell]/B_{n}[2\ell]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

where the vertical arrows are
\ell^{\text{th}}
power maps used to construct isomorphisms in Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
. Then our result follows from an application of the five lemma.

∎

Torus orbit closures and 1-strip-less tableaux
Carl Lian

Abstract
. We compare two formulas for the class of a generic torus orbit closure in a Grassmannian, due to Klyachko and Berget-Fink. The naturally emerging combinatorial objects are semi-standard fillings we call 1-strip-less tableaux .



1.
Introduction

Let
\Gr(r,n)
be the Grassmannian of
r
-planes in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, on which an
n
-dimensional torus
T
acts in the standard way. Let
x\in\Gr(r,n)
be a general point, let
X:=\overline{T\cdot x}
be the closure of the orbit of
x
, and let
[X]\in H^{2(r-1)(n-r-1)}(\Gr(r,n))
be the corresponding cohomology class. Such classes have received considerable attention in the literature
[
Kly85
,
Spe09
,
BF17
,
LPST20
,
NT23
]
; the author’s own interest in them stems from their relationship to fixed-domain curve counts in the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}

[
L23
]
.

We have the following two formulas for
[X]
originally due to Klyachko and Berget-Fink, respectively, obtained via different methods.

Theorem 1
.

[
Kly85
, Theorem 6]

Write

[X]=\sum_{\mu}\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}\sigma_{\mu}

in terms of the basis of Schubert cycles
\sigma_{\mu}
. Then, we have

\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{m(\mu)}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}|\ssyt_{r-j}(\mu^{j})|,

where:

•

m(\mu)
denotes the number of parts of
\mu
equal to
n-r
(the largest possible),

•

\mu^{j}
denotes the partition of length
r-j
obtained from
\mu
by removing
j\leq m(\mu)
parts of size
n-r
, and

•

|\ssyt_{r^{\prime}}(\mu^{\prime})|
denotes the number of semi-standard Young tableaux (SSYTs) of shape
\mu^{\prime}
filled with entries
1,2,\ldots,r^{\prime}
.

See §
2
for a detailed discussion of notational conventions for Schubert calculus and related combinatorics.

Theorem 2
.

[
BF17
, Theorem 5.1]

We have

[X]=\sum_{\lambda\subset(n-r-1)^{r-1}}\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}},

where, for any partition
\lambda\subset(n-r-1)^{r-1}
,
\widetilde{\lambda}
denotes its complement inside the rectangle
(n-r-1)^{r-1}
.

Therefore, for abstract reasons, the following holds.

Theorem 3
.

We have

\sum_{\lambda\subset(n-r-1)^{r-1}}\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}=\sum_{\mu}\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}\sigma_{\mu},

where the
\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}
are as in Theorem
1
.

The purpose of this paper is to give a direct combinatorial proof of Theorem
3
. Our main calculation applies Coskun’s geometric Littlewood-Richardson rule
[
Cos09
]
to the left-hand side in such a way that the coefficients
\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}
emerge naturally. In fact, we arrive at the following combinatorial interpretation for the coefficients
\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}
:

Theorem 4
.

\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}
is equal to the number of 1-strip-less SSYTs (see §
2.1
) of shape
\mu
, filled with entries
1,2,\ldots,r
.

In particular, in the alternating sum of Theorem
1
, the first term dominates, and we get a
non-negative
interpretation of the integers
\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}
. In fact, the following version of Theorem
4
was known to Klyachko
[
Kly95
, Theorem 3.3]
: the number
\Gamma^{\mu}_{r,n}
was shown to count the number of
standard
Young tableaux of shape
\overline{\lambda}
, the complement of
\lambda
inside the rectangle
(n-r)^{r}
, with exactly
(r-1)
descents. Such objects are naturally in bijection with 1-strip-less SSYTs; we describe this bijection (explained to us by Philippe Nadeau) in the appendix.

In our calculation, the 1-strip-less SSYTs are the naturally occurring objects. As we will see in §
3
, the geometric Littlewood-Richardson rule allows us to express the products
\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
in terms of simple Schubert classes, see Corollary
9
. From here, the Pieri rule allows us on the one hand to interpret the coefficients in the Schubert basis as counts of 1-strip-less tableaux in §
4
, and on the other hand to recover Klyachko’s formula in §
5
.

2.1.
Young tableaux

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
be a partition. Our convention throughout is that the parts of
\lambda
are non-increasing
(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0)
, and furthermore that
\lambda
is defined implicitly with respect to an integer
n
for which
\lambda_{1}\leq n-r
(equivalently,
\lambda\subset(n-r)^{r}
). The Young diagram of
\lambda
is taken to be left- and upward-justified, with larger parts depicted on top.

The
complement
of
\lambda
, denoted
\overline{\lambda}
, is the partition
(n-r-\lambda_{r},\ldots,n-r-\lambda_{1})
. Pictorially, the complement
\overline{\lambda}
is obtained as the (rotated) complement of
\lambda
inside the rectangle
(n-r)^{r}
. Note that we distinguish the notation
\overline{\lambda}
and the notation
\widetilde{\lambda}
appearing in Theorem
2
.

Below, the Young diagram of the partition
\lambda=(10,9,4,2)
, defined with respect to
n=14
is shown. Its complement (before rotation)
\overline{\lambda}=(8,6,1,0)
is shaded in gray.

A
strip

S
of
\lambda
is a subset of
n-r
boxes in the Young diagram of
\lambda
satisfying the following two properties:

•

No two boxes of
S
lie in the same column.

•

Given any distinct boxes
b_{1},b_{2}
of
S
, if
b_{1}
lies in a column to the left of
b_{2}
, then
b_{1}
does not also lie in a row above
b_{2}
.

An example of a strip in the partition
\lambda=(10,9,4,2)
, where we take
n=14
, is shaded below. However, the same set of boxes is
not
a strip if
\lambda
is defined with respect to
n>14
.

A
semi-standard Young tableau (SSYT)
of shape
\lambda
is a filling of the boxes of
\lambda
with the entries
1,2,\ldots,r
so that entries increase weakly across rows and strictly down columns. We will often abuse notation, using the letter
\lambda
to denote either a partition or a SSYT of that shape. The number of SSYTs of shape
\lambda
is denoted
|\ssyt_{r}(\lambda)|
, and is given by the formula

|\ssyt_{r}(\lambda)|=s_{\lambda}(1^{r})=\prod_{u\in\lambda}\frac{r+c(u)}{h(u)},

see
[
Sta99
, Corollary 7.21.4]
. Here,
s_{\lambda}(1^{r})
denotes the Schur function associated to
\lambda
, specialized so that the first
r
variables are equal to 1 and all others are equal to zero. In the last formula, the product is over all boxes
u
, and if
u
is in the
i
-th row and
j
-th column of
\lambda
, then by definition,
c(u)=j-i
and
h(u)
is the total number of boxes either to the right (and in the same row) of, below (and in the same column), or equal to
u
.

The
k
-weight

w_{k}(\lambda)
of a SSYT
\lambda
is the number of appearances of the entry
k
. The
type
of
\lambda
is the tuple
(w_{1}(\lambda),w_{2}(\lambda),\ldots,w_{r}(\lambda))
.

For
i=1,2,\ldots,r
, an
(i)
-strip
of a SSYT is a strip, all of whose boxes are filled with the entry
i
. A
0-strip
(written without parentheses) is, by definition, an
(i)
-strip for some
i
. A SSYT is
0-strip-less
if it contains no 0-strips. Below, the SSYT of shape
\lambda=(10,9,4,2)
(with
n=14
) has a
(3)
-strip and no other 0-strips. If we take instead
n>14
, then the same SYT is 0-strip-less.

2.2.
Schubert calculus

Let
W
be a vector space of dimension
n
, assumed over
\mathbb{C}
for concreteness. Then,
\Gr(r,W)\cong\Gr(r,n)
is the Grassmannian of
r
-dimensional subspaces of
W
.

Fix a complete flag
F
of subspaces

0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{n}=W.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
be a partition, with
\lambda_{1}\leq n-r
. Then, the Schubert variety
\Sigma^{F}_{\lambda}\subset\Gr(r,W)
is by definition the closed subvariety of (complex) codimension
|\lambda|
consisting of subspaces
V\subset W
of dimension
r
for which

\dim(V\cap F_{n-r+i-\lambda_{i}})\geq i

for
i=1,\ldots,r
. The class of
\Sigma^{F}_{\lambda}
in
H^{2|\lambda|}(\Gr(r,W))
is denoted
\sigma_{\lambda}
.

3.
Application of the Littlewood-Richardson rule

In this section, we express the products
\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
appearing on the left hand side of Theorem
3
in terms of simple Schubert classes. The main result is Corollary
9
. We apply Coskun’s Littlewood-Richardson rule
[
Cos09
]
; we will only need some aspects of the algorithm, which we review as we need them. Other versions of the Littlewood-Richardson rule can presumably prove the same formula; our presentation is largely idiosyncratic.

We first carry out
[
Cos09
, Algorithm 3.19]
to express the class
\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
in terms of a
Mondrian tableau
. Geometrically, this will interpret
\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
as the class of a subvariety of
\Gr(r,n)
defined generically by the condition that
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
contain a basis of vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}
, where each
v_{j}
is constrained to lie in a particular subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r-1},0)
, so that
\widetilde{\lambda}=(n-r-1-\lambda_{r-1},\ldots,n-r-1-\lambda_{1},0)
. We also write
\lambda_{r}=0
and
\lambda_{0}=n-r-1
. Fix a basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then,
\sigma_{\lambda}
is the class of the Schubert variety of
V\in\Gr(r,n)
satisfying

\dim(V\cap\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-r-\lambda_{j}+j}\rangle)\geq j

for
j=1,\ldots,r
, and
\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
is the class of the Schubert variety of
V\in\Gr(r,n)
satisfying

\dim(V\cap\langle e_{n-r-\lambda_{j-1}+j-1},\ldots,e_{n}\rangle)\geq r+1-j

for
j=1,\ldots,r
.

Because these two Schubert varieties are defined with respect to the transverse flags

\displaystyle 0\subset\langle e_{1}\rangle\subset\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},

\displaystyle 0\subset\langle e_{n}\rangle\subset\langle e_{n-1},e_{n}\rangle\subset\cdots\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},

the product
\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
is represented by the generically transverse intersection of these two Schubert varieties. Combining the two conditions above for each
j
, we obtain the
r
conditions

\dim(V\cap\langle e_{n-r-\lambda_{j-1}+j-1},\ldots,e_{n-r-\lambda_{j}+j}\rangle)\geq 1.

for
j=1,\ldots,r
, as subspaces of
V
of dimensions
j,r+1-j
must intersect non-trivially. By
[
Cos09
, Theorem 3.21]
and its proof, these conditions suffice to understand the class
\sigma_{\lambda}\sigma_{\widetilde{\lambda}}
. More precisely:

Remark 10
.

We explain the geometric content of the formula (

In the limit, the analysis in the proof of

Proposition 15
.

The coefficient of
\sigma_{\mu}
in

. We also adopt here the notation of Theorem

for the integers

Proof.

For a given

, fix

. It suffices to identify the number

as the coefficient of

in the sum

\sum_{0=s_{0}<s_{1}<\cdots<s_{\ell}=r}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\sigma_{(n-1)^{s_{j}-s_{j-1}-1},n-1-(a_{s_{j}}-a_{s_{j-1}})}

, where the value of

is fixed (and equal to the given

Consider the set of fillings counted by

with entries

. For a given such filling, let

. On the other hand, the coefficient of

in the product

Therefore, given a 0-strip-less SSYT of shape

a_{s_{j}}=jn-(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j})-(j-1).

for

Hawking radiation of a multi-fractional Schwarzschild black hole
Peng Huang, Fang-Fang Yuan

Abstract
Following the initial work of Calcagni et al. on the black holes in multi-fractional theories, we focus on the Schwarzschild black hole in multi-fractional theory with q q -derivatives. After presenting its Hawking and Hayward temperatures in detail, we verify these results by appealing to the well-known Hamilton-Jacobi and null geodesic methods of the tunnelling approach to Hawking radiation. A special emphasis is placed on the difference between the geometric and fractional frames.

Schwarzschild black hole in theory
T_{q}

As an approximation of the multi-fractional theory
T_{\gamma}
with fractional derivatives,
the theory
T_{q}
with
q

It also has the structure of a fractal spacetime

Here we collect the basics of the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole constructed in
Calcagni:2017ymp

and fix the notations to facilitate our discussions in later sections.

In the multi-fractional theory
T_{q}
, the gravitational actions can be found by making the following substitutions:

x^{\mu}\ \rightarrow\ q^{\mu}(x^{\mu});\ \partial_{\mu}\ \rightarrow\ v^{-1}_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
with
v_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}q^{\mu}
.
Correspondingly, a measure factor/profile
v(x)=\prod_{\mu}v_{\mu}(x^{\mu})
need to be added.
As argued to be a very effective first-order coarse-graining approximation of the most general multi-fractional
measure
Calcagni:2016xtk
, the following binomial measure without log oscillations has been used in
Calcagni:2017ymp

to obtain the Schwarzschild solution:

q^{\mu}(x^{\mu})=x^{\mu}+\frac{l_{\ast}}{{\alpha}}{\rm sgn}(x^{\mu})|\frac{x^{\mu}}{l_{\ast}}|^{\alpha}
with
0\leq{\alpha}\leq 1
.
For the spherical coordinates, it is a useful approximation to only deform the radius as

\displaystyle q^{({\alpha})}=r\Big|1\pm\frac{1}{{\alpha}}l^{1-{\alpha}}_{\ast}r^{{\alpha}-1}\Big|\ .

(1)

Furthermore, the geometric radius in the theory
T_{\gamma}
is
q(r)
exactly.
For both theoretical and experimental reasons,
the case with
{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}
is rather unique since it constitue a critical point for phase transition.
This value of multi-fractional index
{\alpha}
is systematically assumed in
Calcagni:2017ymp
.

In the geometric frame (integer picture) with geometric coordinate
q
,
the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole has the same form as the usual metric in general relativity:

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(q)dt^{2}+f^{-1}(q)dq^{2}+q^{2}d\Omega^{2}\ ,

(3)

\displaystyle f(q)=1-\frac{r_{0}}{q}\ ,\quad r_{0}\equiv 2GM\ .

For
{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}
, the geometric radius here is
q=|r\pm 2\sqrt{l_{\ast}r}|
.
Assuming
r>4l_{\ast}
, we will leave out the absolute value symbol.
The locations of the horizons can be found as:

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{h}}\equiv\sqrt{r^{\pm}_{h}}=\sqrt{r_{0}+l_{\ast}}\pm\sqrt{l_{\ast}}\ .

(4)

To make the transition to fractional frame (fractional picture), we use the simple relation as:
dq=\partial_{r}q\cdot dr\equiv v_{r}\cdot dr
.
Since only the spherical radius has been deformed, the measure factor reduces to
v=\prod_{\mu}v_{\mu}\equiv v_{r}
. To avoid the cluster of notations, we will write
v_{r}
as
v
keeping in mind that
conceptly speaking they are not the same thing.
Therefore, the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole in fractional frame with fractional coordinate
r
is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f^{-1}(r)v^{2}dr^{2}+q^{2}(r)d\Omega^{2}\ ,

(5)

\displaystyle f(r)\equiv f(q(r))=1-\frac{r_{0}}{r\pm 2\sqrt{l_{\ast}r}}\ ,\quad v\equiv v_{r}=1\pm\sqrt{\frac{l_{\ast}}{r}}\ .

(6)

As will be clear below, almost all the discussions in this paper actually apply to the case with general multi-fractional index
{\alpha}
.
Then we would have

\displaystyle v^{({\alpha})}\equiv v^{({\alpha})}_{r}=1\pm l^{1-{\alpha}}_{\ast}r^{{\alpha}-1}\ .

(7)

Here
r>{\alpha}^{-\frac{1}{1-{\alpha}}}l_{\ast}
has been assumed to leave out the absolute value symbol around the geometric coordinate
q
in (
1

This assumption is obviously stricter than the condition for
v^{({\alpha})}_{r}
to be positive which is
r>l_{\ast}

III
Black hole temperature

In the geometric frame with geometric coordinate
q
, the temperature of the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole (
3
) is easily found to be:

\displaystyle T_{g}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{q}f(q)|_{r_{h}}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{r_{0}}{q^{2}}|_{r_{h}}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{0}}\ .

Here
q|_{r_{h}}=r_{0}
has been used.

As stressed in
Calcagni:2017ymp
, all physical observables should be computed in the fractional frame.
Correspondingly, a temperature for this black hole has been proposed there as
T_{h}=\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{4\pi}|_{r_{h}}

which has an unclear physical meaning to us.
In this work, we study instead the following two more solidly founded versions of the black hole temperature.

In the fractional frame with fractional coordinate
r
,
the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole (
5
) is still static
and has a timelike Killing vector
\zeta=\partial_{t}
.
Thus the general formula can be employed to obtain its (Killing-)Hawking temperature:

\displaystyle T_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{f^{\prime}\Big(\frac{f}{v^{2}}\Big)^{\prime}}|_{r_{h}}

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{f^{\prime}\frac{f^{\prime}}{v^{2}}}|_{r_{h}}=\frac{f^{\prime}}{4\pi v}|_{r_{h}}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{0}}\ .

Along with the relation
f|_{r_{h}}=0
, we also have

\displaystyle f^{\prime}|_{r_{h}}\equiv\partial_{r}f|_{r_{h}}=\partial_{q}f\cdot v|_{r_{h}}=\frac{v}{r_{0}}\ ,

(10)

which has been used to simplify the above calculation.
Notice that this Hawking temperature is the same as the result (
8
) in the geometric frame.

For the metric in (
5
),
one can also use the Kodama vector
\widetilde{\zeta}=\frac{1}{v}\partial_{t}
to define another surface gravity
Kodama:1979vn
;
Hayward:1997jp
. This leads to its (Kodama-)Hayward temperature

2
2

2

The relation
\widetilde{T}_{h}=\frac{1}{v}T_{h}
here bears a resemblance to an equation found in
Calcagni:2017ymp

for the
T_{v}
-Schwarzschild black hole in Jordan and Einstein frames.
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\Big(\frac{f}{v^{2}}\Big)^{\prime}|_{r_{h}}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime}}{4\pi v^{2}}|_{r_{h}}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{0}v}|_{r_{h}}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{0}\big(1\pm\sqrt{\frac{l_{\ast}}{r_{h}}}\big)}\ .

Inserting the expression (
4
) for the horizons, the final form is

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{h}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{0}}\frac{1}{1\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}}{l_{\ast}}+1}\pm 1}}\ .

(12)

Noting the following relation

\displaystyle v=\frac{r_{0}}{2r(1-f)}+\frac{1}{2}\ ,\quad v|_{r_{h}}=\frac{r_{0}}{2r_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}\ ,

(13)

we obtain an equivalent form for the Hayward temperature as

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{h}=\frac{1}{2\pi r_{0}\big(1+\frac{r_{0}}{r_{h}}\big)}\ .

(14)

For the case with general multi-fractional index
{\alpha}

\displaystyle v^{({\alpha})}=\frac{{\alpha}r_{0}}{r(1-f)}+1-{\alpha}\ ,\quad\widetilde{T}^{({\alpha})}_{h}=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{0}\big(1-{\alpha}+\frac{{\alpha}r_{0}}{r_{h}}\big)}\ .

(15)

It is obvious that in the geometric frame, the Killing vector and the Kodama vector coincide with each other,
and there is no need to distinguish between the two temperatures there

On the other hand,

IV
Hamilton-Jacobi method in fractional frame

The Hamilton-Jacobi tunneling method
Srinivasan:1998ty
;
Shankaranarayanan:2000qv
;
Angheben:2005rm

can be straightforwardly utilized to derive the temperature (
8
) in geometric frame.
Since this is basically the same as the case of unfractional static black holes with
g_{tt}g_{rr}=-1
,
we demote the corresponding discussion to Appendix
A
.
In this section,
we shall focus on the case of fractional frame.

Recalling the metric in (
5
), we have the Klein-Gordon equation as

\displaystyle f^{-1}\partial^{2}_{t}\phi+\frac{f}{v^{2}}\Big(\partial^{2}_{r}+\frac{2}{q}\partial_{r}+\partial_{r}\ln v\cdot\partial_{r}\Big)\phi=\frac{m^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\phi\ ,

(16)

where the angular parts have been ignored in the s-wave approximation.
In the above expression, the part
g^{rr}(\partial^{2}_{r}+\partial_{r}\ln v\cdot\partial_{r})
clearly reminds us of
the multi-fractional Laplace-Beltrami operator on Minkowski spacetime in
Calcagni:2016azd
.

Using the ansatz
\phi(t,r)=\exp[-\frac{i}{\hbar}I(t,r)]
, and retaining the leading order in
\hbar
,
we obtain the following relativistic Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}I)^{2}-\frac{f^{2}}{v^{2}}(\partial_{r}I)^{2}-m^{2}f=0\ .

(17)

Corresponding to the Killing energy
\omega=-\partial_{t}I
, the solution for the action
I
is given as

\displaystyle I_{\pm}(t,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega t\pm W(r)\ ,

\displaystyle W(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dr\ \frac{v}{f}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-m^{2}f}\ .

(18)

In the near horizon approximation,
an application of the method of residues (or Feynman’s
i\epsilon
prescription) leads to

4
4

4

One may also try to use the polar coordinates as in
Akhmedov:2006pg
to derive this result.

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\ W(r)=\frac{\pi\omega v}{f^{\prime}}|_{r_{h}}\ .

(19)

A technical point here is that
v
and
f
do not have the same zeros.
Besides, we have
(\frac{f}{v})^{\prime}|_{r_{h}}=\frac{1}{v}f^{\prime}|_{r_{h}}
.
Recalling the master equation (
38
), we now obtain the temperature as follows

\displaystyle T_{h}=\frac{f^{\prime}}{4\pi v}|_{r_{h}}\ .

(20)

This clearly reproduces the Hawking temperature in (
9
).

The above discussion (together with the result in Appendix
A
) confirms our previous finding
that for the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole,
the Hawking temperature has the same expression in the geometric and fractional frames.
In the framework of the Hamilton-Jacobi tunnelling method,
it has also been proposed in
Akhmedov:2006pg
that
any coordinate transformation involving
only spatial coordinates should yield the same result for the temperature.

A different perspective has been advocated in
Angheben:2005rm

which argues for the importance of proper distance to arrive at the rigorous result.
Due to the singular nature of the metric (
5
),
the proper radial distance here is not
r
, but

\displaystyle\sigma=\int dr\ \frac{v}{\sqrt{f}}\ .

(21)

In this way, assuming the near horizon expansion, we would have

\displaystyle W(\sigma)=\frac{2v}{f^{\prime}}|_{r_{h}}\int\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4}\frac{f^{\prime 2}}{v^{2}}|_{r_{h}}m^{2}}\ .

(22)

This leads to
{\rm Im}\ W(\sigma)=2\ {\rm Im}\ W(r)
.
However, now we should use the master equation in regular coordinates which is

\displaystyle-\ {\rm Im}\ I_{-}=\frac{\omega}{2T_{H}}\ .

(23)

Considering that the temporal contribution is zero in this case,
we again get the same result as in (
20
).

Notice that although here
r_{h}
is two-valued,
we do not need to resort to the technique in
Angheben:2005rm
designed for the black holes with multiple horizons.

If using Kodama energy
\widetilde{\omega}=\frac{1}{v}\omega
instead, then we would have
\partial_{t}I=-v\widetilde{\omega}
.
This leads to the following solution

\displaystyle I_{\pm}=v(-\widetilde{\omega}t+\widetilde{W})\ .

(24)

Along a similar procedure, we can reobtain the Hayward temperature as (cf. (
11
))

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{h}=\frac{f^{\prime}}{4\pi v^{2}}|_{r_{h}}\ .

(25)

V
Null geodesic method

In spite of some conceptual issues
Kerner:2006vu
;
Vanzo:2011wq
,
the null geodesic tunnelling method has captured some essential features of the Hawking radiation,
and can still be used to derive the black hole temperature.
For recent applications, see
Johnson:2018gjr
;
Amirfakhrian:2018ehm
;
Johnson:2019kda
;
Eslamzadeh:2020rbd
.

This method requires a transition to the regular Painlevé-Gullstrand coordinates
which can be implemented by the following transformation

\displaystyle t_{p}=t\pm\int dr\ \frac{1}{f}\sqrt{v^{2}-f}\ .

(26)

Now the metric for
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole in fractional frame becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}_{p}\pm 2\sqrt{v^{2}-f}dt_{p}dr+dr^{2}+q^{2}(r)d\Omega^{2}\ .

(27)

For an s-wave, the null geodesic is given by

\displaystyle\dot{r}\equiv\frac{dr}{dt_{p}}=\pm v-\sqrt{v^{2}-f}\ .

(28)

Corresponding to an outgoing positive energy particle which crosses the horizon,
the action can be written as

\displaystyle I_{-}=\int^{r_{+}}_{r_{-}}dr\ p^{(+)}_{r}=-\omega\int^{r_{+}}_{r_{-}}\frac{dr}{\dot{r}_{(+)}}\ .

(29)

Here the Hamilton’s equation
\dot{r}=\frac{dH}{dp_{r}}
and the relation
dH=-d\omega
have been used.

Combining the above two equations, we obviously have

\displaystyle I_{-}=\omega\int^{r_{+}}_{r_{-}}dr\ \frac{v}{f}\bigg(1+\sqrt{1-\frac{f}{v^{2}}}\bigg)\ .

(30)

A standard calculation then leads to

\displaystyle I_{-}=\omega\cdot\pi i\cdot\frac{v}{f^{\prime}}\cdot 2\ .

(31)

Recalling the master equation (
23
) in regular coordinates,
one easily obtains the Hawking temperature in fractional frame as
T_{h}=\frac{f^{\prime}}{4\pi v}|_{r_{h}}
(cf. (
9
)).

When setting
v\rightarrow 1,\ r\rightarrow q
,
the above discussion reduces to the case of geometric frame.
However, since both
v
and
q
depend on the fractional coordinate
r
explicitly,
this only happens when
r\rightarrow\infty
.
In general situations,
the multi-fractional effects may still exist in the fractional frame.

VI
Conclusion

To sum up the main results in this work,
we firstly gave the Hawking and Hayward temperatures (
9
,
11
) in some detail
for the Schwarzschild black hole in multi-fractional theory
T_{q}
with
q
-derivatives
Calcagni:2017ymp
.
Then the Hamilton-Jacobi and null geodesic tunnelling methods were applied to rederive these results.
Although our initial motivation was to study the multi-fractional effects on the tunnelling approach itself,
at least in the case of theory
T_{q}
, we haven’t find any notable modification.
One may attribute this to the special nature of the theory with
q
-derivatives,
and expect the situation to be different for the theory
T_{\gamma}
with fractional derivatives.

Other possible directions for further research are as follows.
In the first place,
one may reconsider some issues which have been studied in the framework of tunnelling approach
such as fermion tunneling, charged and rotating black holes, dynamical horizons, modified gravities, etc.
Secondly,
one may trace back to the origins to revisit the Hawking radiation and Unruh effect in multi-fractional theories.
As for the black hole thermodynamics,
one may wonder if there is a possible connection to Barrow’s fractal entropy
Barrow:2020tzx
.
This may still necessitate the need to go beyond the multi-fractional theory
T_{q}

5
5

5

For the Hawking temperature of
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole,
the area law continues to hold as one can easily seen by use of the relation
S=\int\frac{dM}{T(M)}
.
.
Finally,
besides the requisite constructions of more general multi-fractional black holes, their spacetime structures including the near horizon and asymptotic symmetries
may be rather interesting to investigate in depth.

Appendix A
Hamilton-Jacobi method in geometric frame

In contrast with the original null geodesic method
Parikh:1999mf
of the tunneling approach to Hawking radiation,
the Hamilton-Jacobi method
Srinivasan:1998ty
;
Shankaranarayanan:2000qv
;
Angheben:2005rm

is proved to be more transparent and rigorous and thus more widely used

6
6

6

However, an argument can be given to verify the equivalence of a generalised null geodesic method
with the latter
Vanzo:2011wq
.
.
Here we demonstrate this procedure to obtain the temperature (
8
) in geometric frame

7
7

7

Although this Schwarzschild-like metric is in a singular gauge,
the method is still applicable.
.
The main intention is to benefit the comparison with the case of fractional frame in Section
IV
.

The starting point is the following Klein-Gordon equation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi)=\frac{m^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\phi\ .

(32)

In the s-wave approximation, the angular parts can be ignored.
For the
T_{q}
-Schwarzschild black hole (
3
) in geometric frame,
the above equation becomes

\displaystyle f^{-1}(q)\partial^{2}_{t}\phi+f(q)\Big(\partial^{2}_{q}+\frac{2}{q}\partial_{q}\Big)\phi=\frac{m^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\phi\ .

(33)

Using the ansatz
\phi(t,q)=\exp[-\frac{i}{\hbar}I(t,q)]
and invoking the WKB approximation,
to leading orger in
\hbar
, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}I)^{2}-f^{2}(q)(\partial_{q}I)^{2}-m^{2}f(q)=0\ .

(34)

This is essentially the relativistic Hamilton-Jacobi equation
g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{m}I\partial_{\nu}I+m^{2}=0
.

For the Killing energy
\omega=-\partial_{t}I
, the solution can be found to be

\displaystyle I_{\pm}(t,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\omega t\pm W(q)\ ,

\displaystyle W(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dq\ \frac{1}{f(q)}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-m^{2}f(q)}\ .

(35)

Assuming the near horizon expansion, one has

\displaystyle W(q)=\int dq\ \frac{1}{\partial_{q}f(q)(q-q|_{r_{h}})}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-m^{2}\partial_{q}f(q)(q-q|_{r_{h}})}\ .

(36)

Notice that here
q|_{r_{h}}
is actually single-valued.
At this point, we may use the method of residues (basically Feynman’s
i\epsilon
prescription) to arrive at the following result

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\ W(q)=\frac{\pi\omega}{\partial_{q}f(q)}\ .

(37)

To obtain the black hole temperature, we need a master equation which is

8
8

8

This may be interpreted e.g. as a consequence
of the principle of detailed balance
P_{-}=\exp(-\frac{\omega}{T})P_{+}

Banerjee:2008cf
.

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\ (I_{+}-I_{-})=\frac{\omega}{2T_{H}}\ .

(38)

Notice that the covariant formalism of
Vanzo:2011wq
is based on an integral form of this equation.
Additionally, for black holes in regular (such as Painlevé-Gullstrand) coordinates,
the first term on the left hand side is actually zero
since the plus sign corresponds to the ingoing particles.
Finally, we have

\displaystyle T_{g}=\frac{\omega}{4\ {\rm Im}\ W(q)}=\frac{\partial_{q}f(q)}{4\pi}|_{r_{h}}\ .

(39)

Thus the temperature (
8
) in geometric frame has been reproduced.

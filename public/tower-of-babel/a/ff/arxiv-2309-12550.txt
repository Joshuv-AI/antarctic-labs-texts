Spectral inclusions of perturbed normal operators and applications
Javier Moreno, Monika Winklmeier

Abstract
We consider a normal operator T T on a Hilbert space H H . Under various assumptions on the spectrum of T T , we give bounds for the spectrum of T + A T+A where A A is T T -bounded with relative bound less than 1 but we do not assume that A A is symmetric or normal. If the imaginary part of the spectrum of T T is bounded, then the spectrum of T + A T+A is contained in the region between two hyperbolas whose asymptotic slope depends on the T T -bound of A A . If the spectrum of T T is contained in a bisector, then the spectrum of T + A T+A is contained in the area between certain rotated hyperbolas. The case of infinite gaps in the spectrum of T T is studied. Moreover, we prove a stability result for the essential spectrum of T + A T+A . If A A is even p p -subordinate to T T , then we obtain stronger results for the localisation of the spectrum of T + A T+A . Mathematics Subject Classification (2020). Primary 47A55; Secondary 47A10, 47B12, 47B28. Keywords. Normal operator; sectorial operator; perturbation theory; p p -subordinate perturbation.

Introduction

Perturbation theory of linear operator has a long history.
Vast parts of the literature are dedicated to the perturbation theory of selfadjoint linear operators
where the perturbation is symmetric and small in some sense with respect to the unperturbed operator, see for instance the classic monograph
[
Kat95
]
.

Recently however, there is growing interest in the spectral theory of non-selfadjoint operators.
Such operators may be used to model open physical systems where certain quantities are not conserved.
For instance, in
[
RR20
,
HKS15
]
the Laplace operator on a quantum graph with non-selfadjoint boundary conditions is studied.
Here the unperturbed operator is not selfadjoint and not even similar to a selfadjoint operator.
Perturbations of non-selfadjoint operators have been studied for example in the influential books

[
GK69
]
by Gohberg and Krein,
[
DS88b
,
DS88c
]
by Dunford and Schwartz
and
[
Mar88
]
by Markus.
The case of a normal discrete operator with a
p
-subordinate perturbation was studied by
Markus and Matsaev (
[
MM81
]
,
[
Mar88
, Theorem 6.12]
).
Wyss
[
Wys10
]
could extend their results by allowing for weaker hypotheses on the asymptotic behaviour of eigenvalues of the normal discrete operator.
Shkalikov also studied
p
-subordinate perturbations of normal operators in
[
Shk16
]
where he considered other kinds of perturbations, for example relatively bounded perturbations with relative bound equal to zero.

Analytic bounds for the spectrum of non-selfadjoint operators are valuable because it is in general difficult to obtain good numerical bounds.
In this paper we will study the spectra of perturbations of normal operators where the perturbation is either relatively bounded or
p
-subordinate.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
T
and

\displaystyle\|Ax\|^{2}\leq a^{2}\|x\|^{2}+b^{2}\|Tx\|^{2},\qquad x\in\mathcal{D}(T).

(1)

The infimum

of all

such that (
1
) is satisfied for some

is called the
T
-
bound
of
A
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
T
and
A
be linear operators on a Banach space.
We say that
A
is

-subordinate
to
T
for some

if
\mathcal{D}(T)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(A)
and if there exists

\displaystyle\|Ax\|\leq c\|x\|^{1-p}\|Tx\|^{p},\qquad x\in\mathcal{D}(T).

The infimum over all constants

If

The authors give sufficient conditions for gaps in the spectrum of

In the present work however we assume that the unperturbed operator is only normal instead of selfadjoint which makes the situation more complicated and appropriate conditions on its spectrum have to be identified that allow us to establish perturbational results about its spectrum.
In Proposition
3.1
we assume that
T
is normal and that the imaginary part of the spectrum of
T
is bounded.
Then the spectrum of
T+A
is contained in the region between two hyperbolas which are shifted away from the real axis.
If in addition we assume that the resolvent set of
T
contains a strip parallel to the imaginary axis, we can provide a condition under which the resolvent set of
T+A
still contains a (possibly smaller) strip and we give estimates for
\|(T+A-z)^{-1}\|
for

in that strip, see Theorem
3.3

If the bound on the imaginary part of the spectrum of
T
is 0, we recover the spectral inclusion from Theorem

and the estimate for the resolvent operator, see (

).
If the real part of the spectrum of
T
is lower bounded, then we show in Corollary

that the real part of the spectrum of
T+A
remains lower bounded, which is a generalisation of Corollary 2.4 in
[
CT16
]
.

We can even allow for normal operators with unbounded imaginary part of the spectrum.
We show in Theorem
4.6
that if the spectrum of the normal operator
T
is contained in two sectors symmetric around the real axis, then the spectrum of
T+A
is contained in two possibly bigger sectors and some neighbourhood of
0
if the perturbation
A
is small enough.
More precisely, the exterior of certain shifted and rotated hyperbolas is contained in the resolvent set of
T+A
.
If we set the opening angles of the sectors equal to

, we again recover the results from Theorem
A
.
We can also show that a spectral free strip

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\alpha\leq\re(\lambda)\leq\beta\}\subseteq\rho(T)

(5)

of the unperturbed operator leads to a possibly smaller spectral free strip of
T+A
if the perturbation is small enough.
That the strips under consideration are parallel to the real or the imaginary axis is a choice of convenience.
Clearly, if
T
is normal, then so is
e^{\mathrm{i}\phi}T
for any
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
and we could as well consider other infinite strips which are not necessarily parallel to the axes.

The paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2
we use a Neumann series argument and the spectral theorem to establish a basic criterion for
z
to belong to
\rho(T+A)
and we give estimates for
\|(T+A-z)^{-1}\|
for such
z
.
An important ingredient here is that

\|(T-z)^{-1}\|=\dist(z,\sigma(T))^{-1}
for all
z\in\rho(T)
since
T
is a normal operator.
Moreover we prove a perturbation theorem for the essential spectrum of normal operators.
In Section
3
we consider normal operators whose imaginary part is bounded.
Under a relatively bounded perturbation, the imaginary part of the spectrum may become unbounded but it is contained in the region between two shifted hyperbolas.
Theorem
3.3
shows that a spectral free strip of
T
may give rise to spectral free strip of
T+A
if the perturbation
A
is small enough.
In Section
4
we investigate normal (bi)sectorial-like operators.
Our main theorem, Theorem
4.6
, shows that the spectrum of
T+A
away from 0 is contained in two sectors if the relative bound of
A
is small enough.
The amount by which the opening angle of the new sector increases depends on the
T
-bound of
A
.
Moreover, we give sufficient conditions for a spectral free strip around the imaginary axis to remain open.

The special case when the spectrum of
T
is contained in only one sector has implications for semigroup generator properties of
T_{A}
which will be dealt with in more detail in a forthcoming paper.
In Section
5
we apply our method to several other situations.
Most notably we study the case when infinitely many strips parallel to the imaginary axis belong to the resolvent set of
T
, see Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
.
In Section
6
we consider
p
-subordinate perturbations.
In this case, we can prove spectral enclosures even if the spectrum of
T
is bounded only by parabolas as opposed to linear functions in the sectorial case.
Section
7
contains applications to a non-selfadjoint quantum graph and to a first order system of differential equations.
Several estimates that are used throughout this work are colleted in Appendix
A
.

Perturbation of normal operators

In this section we will establish our notation and provide some basic facts on normal operators.
The main result in this section is Proposition
2.1
on which most of our results are based.
Recall that a closed densely defined operator
T
on a Hilbert space
H
is called

TT^{*}=T^{*}T
.
Note that for a normal operator
\mathcal{D}(T)=\mathcal{D}(T^{*})
and
\|Tx\|=\|T^{*}x\|
for all

.
As for selfadjoint operators, there is a spectral theorem for normal operators:
There exists a unique projection valued measure

\sigma(T)
of
T
such that

T=\int_{\sigma(T)}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}E(\lambda),

see for instance,
[
Sch12
]
or
[
vN22
]
.
An important consequence which we will use frequently is that

\|(T-\lambda)^{-1}\|=\frac{1}{\dist(\lambda,\sigma(T))}.

In this work, the operators
T
and
A
and the constants
a,b,c
will always satisfy one of the following assumptions.

Assumption 1
.

T
is a closed normal operator on a Hilbert space
H
and

is
T
-bounded with

-bound

and the constants
a\geq 0
and

Assumption 2
.

T
is a closed normal operator on a Hilbert space
H
and

is
p
-subordinate to

and the constant

Note that under either of the assumptions the perturbed operator

Recall that the resolvent set

of a closed linear operator consists of all

such that

In this case, the resolvent operator

Much of perturbation theory is based on the well-known fact that

Proposition 2.1

Let

Then for every

(i)

If
\displaystyle\|A(T-z)^{-1}\|<1

\|(T+A-z)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{\|(T-z)^{-1}\|}{1-\|A(T-z)^{-1}\|}.

(ii)

\displaystyle\|A(T-z)^{-1}\|\leq\sup_{t\in\sigma(T)}\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}|t|^{2}}}{|t-z|}.

(iii)

If
\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\sigma(T)}\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}|t|^{2}}}{|t-z|}<1

\|(T+A-z)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{\|(T-z)^{-1}\|}{1-\|A(T-z)^{-1}\|}\leq\frac{1}{\dist(z,\sigma(T))}\frac{1}{1-\sup_{t\in\sigma(T)}\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}|t|^{2}}}{|t-z|}}.

If

(ii)

Since

\displaystyle\|A(T-z)^{-1}x\|\leq\|\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}|T|^{2}}(T-z)^{-1}x\|\leq\sup_{t\in\sigma(T)}\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}|t|^{2}}}{|t-z|}

(6)

where the last inequality follows from the spectral theorem for normal operators.
Since

(iii)

3

Except for Proposition

we will always assume in this section that the imaginary part of the spectrum of the normal operator

is bounded.

For
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
we define the sets

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hyp}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}\gamma^{2}}{1-b^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}(\re z)^{2}}{1-b^{2}}<(\im z)^{2},\ \im z>0\right\}.

(11)

\mathrm{Hyp}_{\gamma}
is the set of all points above the hyperbola in the complex which passes through
\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}\gamma^{2}}{1-b^{2}}}
and has the asymptotes
\im z=\pm\frac{b}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}\re z
for
\re z\to\pm\infty
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T
and
A
be as in Assumption
1
.
Moreover, we assume that
\im\sigma(T)\leq\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\mathrm{i}\gamma+\mathrm{Hyp}_{\gamma}\subseteq\rho(T+A).

(12)

For
z\in\mathrm{i}\gamma+\mathrm{Hyp}_{\gamma}
we have the resolvent estimate

\|(T+A-z)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{\im z-\gamma-\sqrt{a^{2}+2b^{2}\gamma^{2}-2b^{2}\gamma\im z+b^{2}|z|^{2}}}.

(13)

Proof.

Let
z=\mu+\mathrm{i}\nu\in\mathbb{C}
with
\nu>\gamma
.
Then
z\in\rho(T)
and it belongs to
\rho(T+A)
if

\sup_{\im t\leq\gamma}H_{z}(t)<1
.
By Lemma
A.2
,

has no local extrema, so we obtain

\sup_{\im t\leq\gamma}H_{z}(t)=\sup_{\im t=\gamma}H_{z}(t)=\sup_{\im t=\gamma}\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}|t|^{2}}{|t-z|^{2}}\leq b^{2}+\frac{b^{2}\mu^{2}}{(\nu-\gamma)^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}\gamma^{2}}{(\nu-\gamma)^{2}}

(14)

where the last inequality follows from (
68
) in Lemma
A.3
.
Clearly, the right hand side is smaller than 1 if and only if

\frac{b^{2}\mu^{2}}{1-b^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}\gamma^{2}}{1-b^{2}}<(\im z-\gamma)^{2},

which is true if and only if

.
The resolvent estimate (

)
follows from Proposition
2.1
(iii)

together with (
14
) and

\|(T-z)^{-1}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\dist(z,\sigma(T))^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{(\im z-\gamma)^{2}}
.
∎

Bisectorial and sectorial operators

We will denote the closed sector of angle
\theta
and centred at
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
by

S_{\theta}(\lambda):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg(z-\lambda)|\leq\theta\}\cup\{\lambda\}.

For
\alpha,\beta
, with
\alpha\leq\beta
we define

\Omega(\alpha,\beta,\theta):=S_{\theta}(\beta)\cup-S_{\theta}(-\alpha).

(31)

Figure 2:
The set
\Omega(\alpha,\beta,\theta)=S_{\theta}(\beta)\cup-S_{\theta}(\alpha)
.

Definition 4.1
.

An operator
T
on a Hilbert space
H
is called
sectorial
if
\sigma(T)\subseteq-S_{\theta}(0)
for some
\theta\in[0,\pi)
and if for every
\theta^{\prime}\in(\theta,\pi)
there exists a constant
M\geq 0
such that

\|(T-z)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{M}{|z|}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-S_{\theta^{\prime}}(0))
.
An operator
T
is called
quasi-sectorial
if there exists some
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that
T+cI
is sectorial.

We call the operator
T

bisectorial
if

\sigma(T)\subseteq\Omega(0,0,\theta)
for some
\theta\in[0,\pi)
and if for every
\theta^{\prime}\in(\theta,\pi)
there exists a constant
M\geq 0
such that

\|(T-z)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{M}{|z|}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega(0,0,\theta^{\prime})
.

Since
\|(T-z)^{-1}\|=\dist(z,\sigma(T))
for all
z\in\rho(T)
if
T
is a normal operator, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T
be a closed normal operator on a Hilbert space
H
.

(i)

If
\sigma(T)\subseteq\Omega(\alpha_{T},\beta_{T},\theta)
, then, for any
c\in(\alpha_{T},\beta_{T})
, the operator
T-cI
is bisectorial with a possibly larger angle
\theta^{\prime}>\theta
.

(ii)

If there exists
\mu,M\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(\mu+\mathrm{i}\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}\in\rho(T)
and
\|(T-\mu-\mathrm{i}\nu)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{M}{|\nu|}
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, then
T
is bisectorial.

In our first theorem we will show that the spectrum of
T+A
is contained in a set of the form (
31
) if
T
is a normal bisectorial operator and
A
is a sufficiently small perturbation.
We saw in Section
3
that the guaranteed bounds for the spectrum of
T+A
bend away from the boundary of
\sigma(T)
by the angle
\arctan\frac{b}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}
.
Therefore, if
\sigma(T)
is contained in some
\Omega(\alpha,\beta,\theta)
and if we want a half-plane or at least a strip to be contained in
\rho(T+A)
, then
it is reasonable to assume that the constant
b
should satisfy
b<\cos\theta
in order to guarantee that
\theta+\arctan\frac{b}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}}}<\pi/2
.

In the following we will need sets of the form

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi}(\delta+\mathrm{Hyp}_{\gamma})=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\begin{aligned} &\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}\gamma^{2}+b^{2}(\re(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\phi}(z-\delta)))^{2}]}{1-b^{2}}<(\im(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\phi}(z-\delta)))^{2},\\
&\im(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\phi}(z-\delta))>0\end{aligned}\right\}

which is the region above a hyperbola shifted away from the real axis by
\delta
and then rotated by
\phi
,
see Figure

.

Let

Then the asymptotes of the hyperbola

\displaystyle\im z

\displaystyle=(\re z-\delta)\tan(\phi+\phi_{b})\text{ for }\re z\to\infty,

\displaystyle=(\re z-\delta)\tan(\phi-\phi_{b})\text{ for }\re z\to-\infty.

\displaystyle\alpha_{T+A}:=\alpha_{T}+\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}\alpha_{T}^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}\tan^{2}\theta}},\qquad\beta_{T+A}:=\beta_{T}-\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}\beta_{T}^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-b^{2}\tan^{2}\theta}}.

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+\frac{|z|}{\min\{\mu-\alpha_{T},\beta_{T}-\mu\}}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{\left(\min\{\mu-\alpha_{T},\beta_{T}-\mu\}\right)^{1-p}}

whereas if

We conclude that, in any case,

Remark A.1
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\im w\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tau|}{|\tau-w|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|w|}{|\im w|}

(66)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\gamma}\frac{|t|^{2}}{|t-w|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\frac{(\re w)^{2}}{(\gamma-\im w)^{2}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(\gamma-\im w)^{2}}.

(67)

Proof.

In fact, if
\re w\neq 0
, then the supremum in (
66
) is a maximum and it is attained at
\tau=\frac{|z|^{2}}{\mu}
.
If
\re w=0
, then the function on the left hand side has no maximum and

\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tau|}{|\tau-w|}=\im_{|\tau|\to\infty}\frac{|\tau|}{|\tau-w|}=1=\frac{|w|}{|\im w|}
.

For the proof of (
67
) observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\gamma}\frac{|t|^{2}}{|t-w|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\tau+\mathrm{i}\gamma|^{2}}{|\tau+\mathrm{i}\gamma-w|^{2}}=\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\tau^{2}}{|\tau+\mathrm{i}\gamma-w|^{2}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{|\tau+\mathrm{i}\gamma-w|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathrm{i}\gamma-w|^{2}}{(\im(\mathrm{i}\gamma-w))^{2}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(\im(\mathrm{i}\gamma-w))^{2}}

where we used (
66
) to estimate the first term.
The second term clearly attains its maximum when
\tau=\re w
.
The claim follows if we observe that

\frac{|\mathrm{i}\gamma-w|^{2}}{(\im(\mathrm{i}\gamma-w))^{2}}=1+\frac{(\re w)^{2}}{(\im(\mathrm{i}\gamma-w))^{2}}
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

For
a,b\geq 0
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
fixed, the function

H_{z}:\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z\}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad H_{z}(t)=\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}|t|^{2}}{|t-z|^{2}}

does not have any local maximum and

\lim_{|t|\to\infty}H_{z}(t)=b^{2}.

Consequently, for every set
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
with unbounded boundary
\partial U
, we have that

\sup_{t\in U}H_{z}(t)=\sup_{t\in\partial U}H_{z}(t).

If
U
is unbounded but
\partial U
is bounded, then

\sup_{t\in U}H_{z}(t)=\max\{b^{2},\sup_{t\in\partial U}H_{z}(t)\}.

Proof.

The cases when
z=0
or
b=0
are clear. So let us now assume that
b>0
and
z\neq 0
.
Let us restrict
H_{z}
to
t\in\mathbb{C}
with
t=rz
for
r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1\}
.
Then

f(r):=H_{z}(rz)=\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}r^{2}|z|^{2}}{|z|^{2}(r-1)^{2}}=b^{2}+\frac{a^{2}+|z|^{2}b^{2}}{|z|^{2}(r-1)^{2}}+\frac{2b^{2}}{r-1}.

It is easy to see that
f
has only one local extremum; it is a global minimum
and it is attained at
r_{0}=-\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}|z|^{2}}
and
f(r_{0})=\frac{b^{2}a^{2}}{b^{2}|z|^{2}+a^{2}}<b^{2}
.
Therefore
H_{z}
cannot have a local maximum in any
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{z\}
with
\arg t=\arg(\pm z)
.
If
\arg t\neq\arg\pm z
, then we write
t=z+R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi}
with
R=|t-z|
and appropriate
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

H_{z}(z+R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\phi+h)})=\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}|z+R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\phi+h)}|^{2}}{R^{2}}
is strictly monotonic in
h
for
h\in\mathbb{R}
in a small enough neighbourhood of zero, see Figure
14
.
Therefore
H_{z}
cannot have a local extremum in
t
.

Figure 14:
Illustration for the proof of Lemma
A.2
.
If
h\in\mathbb{R}
, then
t^{\prime}=z+R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\phi+h)}
varies on the red circle leaving
|z-t^{\prime}|
constant while
|t^{\prime}|
is strictly monotonic for
h
in a neighbourhood of
0
.

∎

\displaystyle=H_{\mu}(t-\mathrm{i}\nu)+b^{2}\frac{\sigma^{2}-(\sigma-\nu)^{2}}{|t-z|^{2}}.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in S_{\theta}(\beta)}H_{z}(t)=\sup_{t\in\partial S_{\theta}(\beta)}H_{z}(t)\leq\sup_{t\in\partial S_{\theta}(\beta)}H_{\mu}(t-\mathrm{i}\nu)+\sup_{t\in\partial S_{\theta}(\beta)}b^{2}\frac{\sigma^{2}-(\sigma-\nu)^{2}}{|t-z|^{2}}.

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\partial S_{\theta}(\beta)}H_{\mu}(t-\mathrm{i}\nu)

Proof.

It suffices to show (

Let

.
For

\delta

\inf_{t\in\Pi(\beta)}\delta_{z}(t)=\inf_{t\in\partial(\Pi(\beta))}\delta_{z}(t).

Let
t=\tau+\mathrm{i}\sigma\in\Pi(\beta)
.
Hence
\sigma=\pm(\tau-\beta)^{2}
.
Let us first assume that
\sigma=(\tau-\beta)^{2}

\displaystyle\delta_{z}(t)=(\tau-\mu)^{2}+(\nu-(\tau-\beta)^{2})^{2}=(\tau-\mu)^{2}-2\nu(\tau-\beta)^{2}+(\tau-\beta)^{4}+\nu^{2}.

(82)

Sum of elements in finite Sidon sets II
Yuchen Ding

Abstract
. A set S ⊂ { 1 , 2 , … , n } S\subset\{1,2,...,n\} is called a Sidon set if all the sums a + b ⁡ ( a , b ∈ S ) a+b~~(a,b\in S) are different. Let S n S_{n} be the largest cardinality of the Sidon sets in { 1 , 2 , … , n } \{1,2,...,n\} . In this note, we give an extension of the above formula. 1.
Introduction

The notion of the Sidon set dates back to a paper of Sidon
[
23
]
where he investigated the coefficients of the Fourier series. Since then, the researches of Sidon sets became rich after the past ninety years. Let
n
be a positive integer and
S
be a subset of the first
n
numbers
\{1,2,...,n\}
. We call
S
a Sidon set if all the sums
a+b~~(a,b\in S)
are different. Denoting by
S_{n}
the largest cardinality of the Sidon sets in
\{1,2,...,n\}
, i.e.,

S_{n}=\max_{S\subset\{1,2,...,n\},~S~\text{Sidon}}|S|.

It is a natural question to ask the magnitude of the number
S_{n}
. And there are a few literatures involving this magnitude. In 1941, Erdős and Turán
[
12
]
proved that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leqslant\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leqslant\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\leqslant 1.

Actually, they gave more explicit upper bound
S_{n}\leqslant n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/4})
. This upper bound was sharpen to
S_{n}\leqslant n^{1/2}+n^{1/4}+1
by Lindström
[
17
]
. Later, Cilleruelo
[
6
]
improved slightly this bound by showing that
S_{n}\leqslant n^{1/2}+n^{1/4}+1/2
. In about 2017, Professor Yong–Gao Chen in Nanjing Normal University showed me that the original method of Erdős and Turán with some more elaborate calculations can already lead to the bound
S_{n}\leqslant n^{1/2}+n^{1/4}+5/8
(private communications). Recently, Balogh, Füredi and Roy
[
3
]
proved that
S_{n}\leqslant n^{1/2}+0.998n^{1/4}
for sufficiently large
n
by combining the methods of Erdős–Turán and Lindström.

In the other direction, Chowla
[
5
]
and Erdős
[
9
]
independently found that the constructions of Singer
[
24
]
and Bose
[
4
]
provided the evidences of
S_{n}\geqslant(1+o(1))n^{1/2}
. Based on the results of gaps between primes (up to the year of 1999), it has been remarked by Ruzsa
[
22
]
that
S_{n}\geqslant n^{1/2}+O(n^{11/40+\varepsilon})
, where
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary small constant. This lower bound together with the upper bound give us that

S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O(n^{11/40+\varepsilon}).

(1.1)

It was conjectured by Erdős
[
11
]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O(n^{\varepsilon})
. He offered one thousand dollars for the first one to solve this conjecture.

In the famous book ‘
Unsolved Problems in Number Theory
’ of Guy
[
13
, Problem E28]
, it asked what is the superemum of the sum of reciprocal terms taken from a Sidon set. It has been proved that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq\{1,2,..,n\}\\
\text{Sidon}\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\in S}\frac{1}{a}

is near
2
for sufficiently large
n
. For results of this topic, one can refer to
[
1
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
.

For the collections of researches about Sidon sets, one can refer to the excellent survey article
[
19
]
and P.hD. thesis
[
20
]
of O’Bryant.

In the Lindström argument for bounding the
maximal size of a Sidon set, it arises naturally the estimate of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}\\
S\subset[1,n]~\text{Sidon}\end{subarray}}(n+1-i)a_{i}.

In 2021, the author
[
8
]
considered the magnitude of the sum of elements in Sidon sets. To be precise, let
S
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
with
|S|=S_{n}
, using the asymptotic formula of
S_{n}
in equation (
1.1
) the author proved for any
\varepsilon>0

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O(n^{111/80+\varepsilon}).

(1.2)

In this subsequent note, we provide the asymptotic formulae of the following two type summations:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}\\
S\subset[1,n]~\text{Sidon}\end{subarray}}a_{i}^{\ell}~~~\qquad~~~\text{and}~~~\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}\\
S\subset[1,n]~\text{Sidon}\end{subarray}}ia_{i}^{\ell}.

Using these estimates with
\ell=1
, we finally attain the asymptotic formula of

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}\\
S\subset[1,n]~\text{Sidon}\end{subarray}}(n+1-i)a_{i}

which arose in the text of Lindström. Although this formula can not effect the upper bound of the maximal Sidon sets in Lindström’s argument, this is of interest itself.

Our first theorem deals with the general case of equation (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
. Then for any positive integer
\ell
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{i}^{\ell}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\ell+1}tn^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(n^{\ell+3/8}\right),~&~\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
\\
\frac{1}{\ell+1}tn^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(n^{\ell+1/4}\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t}\right),~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}.\end{cases}

Several corollaries can be deduced from Theorem
1.1
. The asymptotic formula mentioned in the abstract is proved by taking
t=S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O(n^{21/80})
(see Lemma
2.10
).

Taking
\ell=1
, we have the following corollary, which is another extension of equation (
1.2
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}nt+O(n^{11/8}),~&\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
\\
\frac{1}{2}nt+O\left(n^{5/4}\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t}\right),~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}.\end{cases}

Taking
t=S_{n}
and then Corollary
1.2
reduces to the following Corollary
1.3
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
S
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
with
|S|=S_{n}
, then we have

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O(n^{221/160}).

It is certainly that
11/8<221/160<111/80
, so this is a small improvement of the error term in equation (
1.2
). In the former article, the author pointed out that one can improve the error term in equation (
1.2
) to
O(n^{11/8})
under the assumption
S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/4})
. This assumption is still open at present due to the barrier of the gaps between primes. By some applications of the mean value estimate rather than the direct use of the size of Sidon sets, we can prove the following stronger result comparing with Corollary
1.3
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
S
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
with
|S|=S_{n}
, then for all
n\leqslant N
but at most
O(N/(\log N)^{7/19})
exceptions, we have

\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O(n^{11/8}\log n).

From Theorem
1.4
, we immediately have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
S
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
with
|S|=S_{n}
, then the asymptotic formula
\sum_{a\in S}a=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O(n^{11/8}\log n)
is true for almost all integers
n
.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we establish the last theorem in this note.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
. Then for any positive integer
\ell
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{t}ia_{i}^{\ell}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\ell+2}t^{2}n^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(n^{\ell+3/8}t\right),~&~\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
\\
\frac{1}{\ell+2}t^{2}n^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(n^{\ell+1/4}t\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t}\right),~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}.\end{cases}

By Theorem
1.6
, we finally offer the asymptotic formula of the summation occurred in the Lindström article.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
S=\{a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<a_{t}\}
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{t}(n+1-i)a_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}n^{2}t+O\left(n^{19/8}\right),~&~\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
\\
\frac{1}{2}n^{2}t+O\left(n^{9/4}\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t}\right),~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}.\end{cases}

2.
Lemmas

In this section, we provide some lemmas which shall be used in the proofs of our theorems.

Lemma 2.8

(Baker–Harman–Pintz)
.

[
2
]
Let
p_{k}
be the
k
–th prime, then we have

p_{k+1}-p_{k}\ll p_{k}^{0.525}.

Lemma 2.9

(Bose)
.

[
4
]
Let
p
be a prime, then there are at least
p
elements between
[1,p^{2}-1]
such that all the sums of two of these elements are different modulo
p^{2}-1
. In other words, we have
S_{p^{2}-1}\geqslant p
for primes
p
.

Lemma 2.10
.

For
n\geqslant 2
, we have
S_{n}=n^{1/2}+O(n^{21/80})
.

This is mentioned in
[
19
]
without proof and we offer one here for complement.

Proof.

Let
p_{i}
be the
i
–th prime. Suppose that
p_{t}
is the largest prime such that
p_{t}^{2}-1<n
, then we have

p_{t}^{2}-1<n\leqslant p_{t+1}^{2}-1.

(2.1)

By Lemmas
2.8
and
2.9
, it follows that

S_{n}\geqslant S_{p_{t}^{2}-1}\geqslant p_{t}=p_{t+1}-(p_{t+1}-p_{t})\geqslant\sqrt{n+1}+O(p_{t}^{0.525}).

From the equation (
2.1
), it is clear that
p_{t}^{0.525}\ll n^{0.525/2}=n^{21/80}
. Now the lemma follows from the fact that
S_{n}\leqslant n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/4})
.
∎

Lemma 2.11

(Cilleruelo)
.

[
7
]
Given a Sidon set
A\subset\{1,2,...,n\}
with
|A|=n^{1/2}-L
. Then every subinterval
I\subset[1,n]
with length
cn
contains
c|A|+E_{I}
elements of
A
, where

|E_{I}|\leqslant 52n^{1/4}(1+c^{1/2}n^{1/8})(1+L_{+}^{1/2}n^{-1/8}),~~L_{+}=\max\{0,L\}.

Let
B_{n}
denote the
n
–th Bernoulli number, i.e., it is given by

\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}}{n!}x^{n}.

It is well known that (see for example
[
14
]
)

B_{0}=1,~B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},~B_{2}=\frac{1}{6},~B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30},~B_{6}=\frac{1}{42},~B_{8}=-\frac{1}{30},~B_{10}=\frac{5}{66},...

and
B_{n}=0
for odd integers
n\geqslant 3
. The Bernoulli polynomial
B_{n}(x)~(n=0,1,2,3,...)
is defined by

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}B_{i}x^{n-i},

(2.2)

where
\binom{n}{i}=\frac{n!}{i!(n-i)!}
. We have the following identity.

Lemma 2.12
.

[
14
, Page 93]

For natural numbers
r
and
x
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{x-1}n^{r-1}=\frac{1}{r}(B_{r}(x)-B_{r}).

3.
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We start with calculations of the summation
\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}S(k)
, where
S(k)=|S\cap[1,k]|
for any
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
. On one hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}S(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in S\\
1\leqslant a\leqslant k\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{a\in S}\sum_{a\leqslant k\leqslant n}k^{\ell-1}.

(3.1)

By Lemma
2.12
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{a\leqslant k\leqslant n}k^{\ell-1}=\frac{1}{\ell}B_{\ell}(n+1)-\frac{1}{\ell}B_{\ell}(a).

(3.2)

Taking equation (
3.2
) into equation (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}S(k)=\frac{1}{\ell}B_{\ell}(n+1)t-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{a\in S}B_{\ell}(a).

(3.3)

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.11
with
A=S
and
I=S\cap[1,k]
and Lemma
2.12
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}S(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}|S\cap[1,k]|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{k}{n}t+E_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{n(\ell+1)}\left(B_{\ell+1}(n+1)-B_{\ell+1}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}E_{k},

(3.4)

where
|E_{k}|\leqslant 52n^{1/4}(1+(k/n)^{1/2}n^{1/8})(1+L_{+}^{1/2}n^{-1/8}),~~L_{+}=\max\{0,n^{1/2}-t\}.
Hence, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in S}B_{\ell}(a)

\displaystyle=\left(B_{\ell}(n+1)-\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}\frac{B_{\ell+1}(n+1)}{n}\right)t+\frac{\ell t}{(\ell+1)n}B_{\ell+1}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}E_{k}.

(3.5)

From the definition of the Bernoulli polynomial (equation (
2.2
)), we have

\displaystyle B_{\ell}(n+1)-\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}\frac{B_{\ell+1}(n+1)}{n}=\frac{1}{\ell+1}n^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(n^{\ell-1}\right)

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle B_{\ell}(a)=a^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(a^{\ell-1}\right).

(3.7)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}a_{i}^{\ell}=\sum_{a\in S}a^{\ell}=\frac{1}{\ell+1}tn^{\ell}+O_{\ell}\left(tn^{\ell-1}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}E_{k}.

(3.8)

It remains to bound the sum involving
E_{k}
.
Since

\displaystyle|E_{k}|

\displaystyle\ll\begin{cases}n^{1/4}\left(1+(k/n)^{1/2}n^{1/8}\right),~&\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
n^{1/8}\left(1+(k/n)^{1/2}n^{1/8}\right)\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t},~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\ll\begin{cases}n^{1/4}+k^{1/2}n^{-1/8},~&\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
\left(n^{1/8}+k^{1/2}n^{-1/4}\right)\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t},~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4},\end{cases}

(3.9)

it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\ell-1}E_{k}\ll\begin{cases}n^{\ell+3/8},~&~\text{if~}t\geqslant n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}\\
n^{\ell+1/4}\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t},~&\text{if~}t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}.\end{cases}

(3.10)

Now the theorem follows from the trivial estimates

tn^{\ell-1}\ll n^{\ell+1/8}

for
t<n^{1/2}+O(n^{1/4})
and

tn^{\ell-1}\ll n^{\ell+1/4}\sqrt{n^{1/2}-t}

provided that
t<n^{1/2}-n^{1/4}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The proof is an application of the mean value idea. For any
1\leqslant n\leqslant N
, let
\mathscr{S}_{n}
be a Sidon set in
\{1,2,...,n\}
with
|\mathscr{S}_{n}|=S_{n}
. By Lemma
2.10
and equation (
3.5
) with
\ell=1
and
S=\mathscr{S}_{n}
, we know that

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathscr{S}_{n}}a

\displaystyle=\left(B_{1}(n+1)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{B_{2}(n+1)}{n}\right)S_{n}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}+O(n^{101/80})-\sum_{k=1}^{n}E_{n,k},

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle|E_{n,k}|\leqslant 52n^{1/4}(1+(k/n)^{1/2}n^{1/8})(1+L_{n}^{1/2}n^{-1/8}),~~L_{n}=\max\{0,n^{1/2}-S_{n}\}.

(3.12)

In another word, we shall have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{a\in\mathscr{S}_{n}}a-\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}\right|\ll n^{101/80}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}|E_{n,k}|.

(3.13)

By equation (
3.13
), we immediately obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\sum_{a\in\mathscr{S}_{n}}a-\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}\right|\ll N^{181/80}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|E_{n,k}|.

(3.14)

We now treat the summation involving
E_{n,k}
. From equation (
3.12
), we know that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|E_{n,k}|\ll(n^{1/4}+L_{n}^{1/2}n^{1/8})\sum_{k=1}^{n}(1+k^{1/2}n^{-3/8})\ll n^{11/8}+L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}.

(3.15)

From which it can be deduced that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|E_{n,k}|\ll\sum_{n=1}^{N}(n^{11/8}+L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4})\ll N^{19/8}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}.

(3.16)

Suppose that
p_{\ell}
is the least prime such that
N\leqslant p_{\ell}^{2}-1
, where
p_{i}
is the
i
–th prime and we define
p_{0}
to be
1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{m}^{2}-1<n\leqslant p_{m+1}^{2}-1\end{subarray}}L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}.

(3.17)

Recall that
S_{p^{2}-1}\geqslant p
from Lemma
2.9
, thus by the definition of
L_{n}
in equation (
3.12
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{m}^{2}-1<n\leqslant p_{m+1}^{2}-1\end{subarray}}L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{m}^{2}-1<n\leqslant p_{m+1}^{2}-1\end{subarray}}n^{5/4}(n^{1/2}-p_{m})^{1/2}

\displaystyle<\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{m}^{2}<n\leqslant p_{m+1}^{2}\end{subarray}}n^{5/4}(n^{1/2}-p_{m})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ll p_{m+1}^{5/4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{m}^{2}<n\leqslant p_{m+1}^{2}\end{subarray}}(n^{1/2}-p_{m})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ll p_{m+1}^{5/4}\int_{p_{m}^{2}}^{p_{m+1}^{2}}(x^{1/2}-p_{m})^{1/2}dx

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{t=x^{1/2}-p_{m}}_{x=(t+p_{m})^{2}}}p_{m+1}^{5/4}\int_{0}^{p_{m+1}-p_{m}}2t^{1/2}(t+p_{m})dt

\displaystyle\ll p_{m+1}^{5/4}(p_{m+1}-p_{m})^{5/2}+p_{m+1}^{5/4}p_{m}(p_{m+1}-p_{m})^{3/2}

\displaystyle\ll p_{m+1}^{9/4}(p_{m+1}-p_{m})^{3/2}.

(3.18)

Taking this into equation (
3.17
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}p_{m+1}^{9/4}(p_{m+1}-p_{m})^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}p_{m+1}^{11/4}(p_{m+1}-p_{m})

\displaystyle\leqslant p_{\ell}^{11/4}\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}(p_{m+1}-p_{m})

\displaystyle\leqslant p_{\ell}^{15/4}.

(3.19)

Recall that
p_{\ell-1}^{2}-1<N\leqslant p_{\ell}^{2}-1
from the definition of
\ell
, so we have

\displaystyle p_{\ell}=p_{\ell-1}+(p_{\ell}-p_{\ell-1})\leqslant\sqrt{N+1}+O(p_{\ell-1}^{0.525})\ll N^{1/2}.

(3.20)

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}L_{n}^{1/2}n^{5/4}\ll N^{15/8}.

(3.21)

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\sum_{a\in\mathscr{S}_{n}}a-\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}\right|\ll N^{19/8}

(3.22)

from equations (
3.14
), (
3.16
) and (
3.21
). Now let
\mathscr{A}
be the set of integers
n\leqslant N
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathscr{S}_{n}\end{subarray}}a-\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}\right|>n^{11/8}\log n.

(3.23)

If there are more than
N/(\log N)^{7/19}
integers in
\mathscr{A}
, we shall have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathscr{A}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathscr{S}_{n}\end{subarray}}a-\frac{1}{2}n^{3/2}\right|>\sum_{n\leqslant N/(\log N)^{7/19}}n^{11/8}\log n\gg N^{19/8}(\log N)^{1/8}.

(3.24)

This a contradiction with equation (
3.22
) for sufficiently large
N
, which means that we should have

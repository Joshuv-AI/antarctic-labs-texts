1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be a field and
V
a finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector-space.
Let
G\subseteq\GL(V)
be a finite group.
Then the action of
G
on
V
induces an action on
V^{*}
which extends to
an action by graded
\Bbbk
-algebra automorphisms of

S:=\Sym V^{*}
.
By
S^{G}
we denote the ring of invariants.

Suppose that the characteristic of
\Bbbk
is
p>0
and that

G
is a finite
p
-group.
Various authors have studied necessary and/or sufficient conditions
for the invariant ring
S^{G}
to be a polynomial ring; see, e.g.

[
SW99
]
,

[
Bro05
]
,

[
Bro06
]
,

[
Bro10
]
,

[
KM22
]
,

[
Bra22
]
, and references therein.

If
S^{G}
is a polynomial ring then
S
is a free
S^{G}
-module, so
S^{G}

is a direct summand of
S
. (See Section
4
for (well-known)
remarks about direct summands.) The converse is not in general true; in the
non-modular case, every invariant ring is a direct summand of the
respective symmetric algebra. However, for
actions of finite
p
-groups in characteristic
p>0
, the
Shank-Wehlau-Broer conjecture
(R. J. Shank and
D. L. Wehlau
[
SW99
, Conjecture 1.1]
,
reformulated by
A. Broer
[
Bro05
, Corollary 4, p. 14]
)
is the following:

1.1 Conjecture
.

S^{G}
is a polynomial ring if it is a direct summand of
S
as an

S^{G}
-module.

In this paper we prove a few results in the direction of settling
Conjecture
1.1
.
Let
W\subseteq V^{G}
be a subspace.
We recall the following definition
from
[
Bro10
, p. 406]
.
The
Hilbert ideal of
G
in
S
relative to
W
, denoted

\mathfrak{h}_{G,S,W}
,
is the
S
-ideal
((W^{\perp}S)\cap S^{G})S
,
where
W^{\perp}=\ker(V^{*}\longrightarrow W^{*})
,
i.e., the subspace of linear forms on
V
that vanish on
W
.
(We will often call it a
relative Hilbert ideal
.)
The
Hilbert ideal

\mathfrak{h}_{G,S}
is the
S
-ideal
generated by the invariant polynomials of positive degree, i.e., by

(S^{G})_{+}
.
Note that
\mathfrak{h}_{G,S}
is
\mathfrak{h}_{G,S,0}
.
If
S^{G}
is a direct summand, then every generating set
of
\mathfrak{h}_{G,S}
(as an
S
-ideal) consisting of homogeneous
invariant elements generates
S^{G}
as a
\Bbbk
-algebra;
see
[
Ben93
, Theorem 1.6.3]

or
[
DK15
, Theorem 2.2.10]
for
a proof. Hence if
S^{G}
is a direct summand and

\mathfrak{h}_{G,S}
is a complete intersection, then
S^{G}
is a
polynomial ring. Conversely, if
S^{G}
is a polynomial ring then it is a
direct summand of
S
and

\mathfrak{h}_{G,S}
is a complete intersection.

We now state our results about Conjecture
1.1

when
\dim_{\Bbbk}V
is small.
If
\dim_{\Bbbk}V=2
, then

\Bbbk
-linearly independent linear

-orbit product of

In higher dimensions, we prove the following:

.

1.3 Theorem

When

2.3 Discussion
.

Let

be noetherian

-graded algebras with

.
Suppose that

is a polynomial ring and that

is a free

-module.
Then

is a polynomial ring. To see this, let

be a minimal
graded free resolution of
A/A_{+}
as an

-module. Then

is a graded free resolution of

as an

-module (since

is a free

-module). It is minimal since

is a polynomial ring,

is bounded, so

is bounded. Hence

is a polynomial ring.
See, e.g.,

for details.

2.4 Discussion
.

In order to work in arbitrary characteristic,
we modify the definition of terminal variables
from
[

]

Fix a basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
of

.
Say that

(g-1)x_{j}\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle

.

Suppose that
x_{j}
is a terminal variable.
Define
\Bbbk

locally finite iterative higher derivations

\Delta_{j}^{(l)}:S\longrightarrow S
as follows.
Let

be a new variable and extend the

-action on

to

with

acting trivially on

.
Let
\phi_{j}
be the ring map

given by

x_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if}\;i\neq j,\\
x_{j}+t&\text{if}\;i=j.\end{cases}

We now show

is

-equivariant since

is a terminal variable.
Let

.
If

,

.
On the other hand, since

\alpha\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
, it follows that

\phi_{j}(gx_{j})=(x_{j}+\alpha)+t=g(\phi_{j}(x_{j}))
.
Hence
\phi_{j}
is
G
-equivariant.
Define

by
\phi_{j}(f)=\sum\Delta^{(l)}_{j}(f)t^{l}
.
Since

is

.

2.5 Proposition

Assume that

is a terminal variable. Let

is a free
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module.
Then

\dim_{\kappa({\mathfrak{p}})}(S\otimes_{R}\kappa({\mathfrak{p}}))=\dim_{K}(S\otimes_{R}K).

Therefore

\dim_{K}(S\otimes_{R}K)=\dim_{\Bbbk}(S\otimes_{R}\Bbbk)
.
Using the Nakayama lemma, we see that
S
is a free
R
-module.
Therefore, by Discussion
2.3
,
R
is a polynomial ring.
∎

4.
Direct summands

In this section, we collect some (well-known) remarks about
direct summands of rings.
Recall that a subring
R
of a ring
S
is a
direct summand
if
there exists an
R
-linear map
\psi:S\longrightarrow R
such that
\psi\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{R}
, where
i:R\longrightarrow S
is the inclusion map.

4.1 Proposition
.

Let
R\subseteq S
be integral domains. Suppose that
there exists
s_{0}\in S
such that
the
R
-submodule
Rs_{0}
of
S
is a free
R
-module
and is a direct summand of
S
as an
R
-module.
Then
R
is a direct summand of
S
.
In particular, if
R
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded polynomial
ring and
S
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Cohen-Macaulay domain that is a
finitely generated
R
-module, then
R
is a direct summand of
S
.

Proof.

Let
h:S\longrightarrow S
be the
R
-linear map
s\mapsto ss_{0}
,

\pi:S\longrightarrow Rs_{0}
be the
R
-linear projection map and

j:Rs_{0}\longrightarrow R
the
R
-linear map
rs_{0}\mapsto r
.
Define
\psi=j\circ\pi\circ h
.
Then
\psi\circ i=\mathrm{id}_{R}
; this proves the first assertion.
To prove the second assertion, note, by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula,
that
S
is a free
R
-module. Now choose
s_{0}\in S
that can be extended
to an
R
-basis of
S
.
∎

4.2 Proposition
.

Let
R\subseteq A\subseteq S
be rings. If
R
is a direct summand of

S
, then it is a direct summand of
A
.

Proof.

There exists an
R
-linear map
\psi:S\longrightarrow R
such that
\psi(r)=r

for each
r\in R
. Take
\psi|_{A}:A\longrightarrow R
.
∎

5.
A composition series of
G

As mentioned in the introduction, we are interested in the following
situation:
\charact\Bbbk=p>0
and
G
is a
p
-group
generated by elements that act as transvections (i.e. non-diagonalizable
pseudoreflections) on
V
.

5.1 Notation
.

The set
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
will denote a basis of
V
as in
Lemma
2.7
.
We write
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
for the dual basis of
V^{*}
dual to

\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.
For
g\in G
, define

\beta_{g}:=\max\{j\mid x_{j}\;\text{appears in}\;gx_{i}-x_{i}\;\text{for some}\;i\}.

Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of pseudoreflections of
G
.
Define
\beta_{G}:=\max\{\beta_{g}\mid g\in\mathcal{P}\}
.
∎

5.2 Discussion
.

With respect to the ordered basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, elements of
G

act on

V^{*}
as upper triangular matrices, with
1
s on the diagonal.
Let
g\in\mathcal{P}
. Then
g
is given by a matrix of the form

\begin{bmatrix}I_{\beta_{g}}&A\\
0&I_{n-\beta_{g}}\end{bmatrix}

where
\rank A=1
. Fix a non-zero column vector

\begin{bmatrix}c_{1}\\
\vdots\\
c_{\beta_{g}}\end{bmatrix}

of
A
. Let
l=\sum_{i=1}^{\beta_{g}}c_{i}x_{i}
, which is an element of

\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{\beta_{g}}\rangle
.
Hence we have the following:

(a)

For all
j\leq\beta_{g}
,
gx_{j}=x_{j}
; in particular, if
f\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\beta_{g}}]\subseteq S
, then
gf=f
.

(b)

For all
j>\beta_{g}
,
gx_{j}-x_{j}\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{\beta_{g}}\rangle
, and, therefore,
is divisible by
l
in
S
.

On
V
, with respect to the basis

\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
,
g
is given by

\begin{bmatrix}I_{\beta_{g}}&0\\
-A^{\text{tr}}&I_{n-\beta_{g}}\end{bmatrix}

Hence
\beta_{g}=\max\{i\mid gv_{i}\neq v_{i}\}
.
∎

Note that
\beta_{g}
depends on the choice of the ordered basis

\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.

5.3 Lemma
.

Let
g,h\in\mathcal{P}
.

(a)

ghg^{-1}\in\mathcal{P}
and
\beta_{ghg^{-1}}=\beta_{h}
.

(b)

If
\beta_{g}=\beta_{h}
then
gh=hg
.

Proof.

(
a
):
If
j>\beta_{h}
, then
\Bbbk\langle v_{j},\dots,v_{n}\rangle
is stable
under the action of
G
and is fixed by
h
,
so
ghg^{-1}
fixes
v_{j}
, from which it follows that

\beta_{ghg^{-1}}\leq\beta_{h}
.
Equality now holds by symmetry.

(
b
):
Let
m=\beta_{g}=\beta_{h}
. Let
1\leq i\leq n
.
Then, we saw in Discussion
5.2
,

gx_{i}-x_{i}\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
.
Similarly
hx_{i}-x_{i}\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
.
Hence, using Discussion
5.2
again,
we see that
g(hx_{i}-x_{i})=hx_{i}-x_{i}
and
h(gx_{i}-x_{i})=gx_{i}-x_{i}
.
Therefore

g(h(x_{i}))=g(x_{i}+(hx_{i}-x_{i}))=x_{i}+(gx_{i}-x_{i})+(hx_{i}-x_{i})=h(g(x_{i})).\qed

Using the above lemma, we see that there is a composition series of
G

consisting of subgroups generated by transvections.

5.4 Proposition
.

G
has a composition series

0=G_{0}\subsetneq G_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq G_{k}=G

such that for each
1\leq l\leq k
,
G_{l}
is a transvection group and

G_{l}/G_{l-1}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and is generated by the
residue class of a transvection.

Proof.

For
1\leq l\leq\beta_{G}
, define
G_{l}
to be the group generated by

\{g\in\mathcal{P}\mid\beta_{g}\leq l\}
. By Lemma
5.3

G_{l}
is normal in
G_{l+1}
and
G_{l+1}/G_{l}
is an abelian group
generated by the residue classes of
\{g\in\mathcal{P}\mid\beta_{g}=l+1\}
.
Refine this filtration, if necessary.
∎

We give the explicit description of such a
filtration for Nakajima groups in Example
9.2
.
Various authors (see,
e.g.,
[
Bra22
, Section 1]
)
have used inertia subgroups of prime
ideals generated by linear subspaces of
V^{*}
(the space of homogeneous
linear forms in
S
) to study homological properties of
S^{G}
. In
Example
9.3
, we show that our filtration is not
necessarily given by such inertia subgroups.
Moreover, the reason for looking at the above composition series is the
hope that we could prove Conjecture
1.1

by induction on the order of the group.
When
G
is a
p
-group and
\charact\Bbbk=p
, if the invariant ring is a
direct summand, then
G
is generated
by transvections
[
Bro05
, Theorem 2(ii)]
.
Therefore we would like the subgroups in the composition series to be
generated by transvections.
Example
9.3
shows that inertia subgroups need not be
generated by transvections, in general.

We end this section with some observations. A partial converse to the next
proposition will be proved in Section
6
(see
Proposition
6.7
).

5.5 Proposition
.

0=G_{0}\subsetneq G_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq G_{k}=G
be the
filtration of Proposition
5.4
.
Assume that
S^{G_{l}}
is a direct summand of
S
for each
l
.
Then
S^{G_{l}}
is a polynomial ring for each
l
.

Proof.

Since
S=S^{G_{0}}
is a polynomial ring, we may assume, by induction, that

S^{G_{l-1}}
is a polynomial ring.
Note that
S^{G_{l}}
is a direct summand of
S^{G_{l-1}}
.
Write
A=S^{G_{l-1}}
and
R=S^{G_{l}}
.
Then there exists a homogeneous
a\in A
such that
A=R[a]
;
see
[
Bro06
, Theorem 4, p. 588]
.
Moreover, there exist
r_{1},r_{2}\in R
such that

R[a]\simeq R[T]/(T^{p}-r_{1}T-r_{2})
.
Hence
A
is a free
R
-module.
Hence
R
is a polynomial ring, by Discussion
2.3
.
∎

6.
Relative Hilbert ideals at consecutive stages in the composition
series

6.1 Notation
.

Let

0=G_{0}\subsetneq G_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq G_{k}=G

be the filtration from
Proposition
5.4
.
Write
G^{\prime}=G_{k-1}
.
We denote by
\sigma
a transvection in
G\smallsetminus G^{\prime}
.
Let
R=S^{G}
and
A=S^{G^{\prime}}
.
Let
<
denote the degree-lexicographic monomial order on
S
with

x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{n}
.

6.2 Lemma
.

Suppose that
R
is a direct summand of
A
.
Let
d_{0}=\min\{d\mid A_{d}\neq R_{d}\}
.

(a)

\dim_{\Bbbk}A_{d_{0}}/R_{d_{0}}=1
.

(b)

A=R[a]
for all
a\in A_{d_{0}}\smallsetminus R_{d_{0}}
.

Proof.

(
a
): By
[
Bro06
, Theorem 4,
p. 588]
, there exists a homogeneous
a_{0}\in A\smallsetminus R
such that
A=R[a_{0}]
. It is immediate that
\deg a_{0}=d_{0}
.
Suppose there exists
a\in A_{d_{0}}\smallsetminus R_{d_{0}}
that is

\Bbbk
-linearly independent of
a_{0}
in
A_{d_{0}}/R_{d_{0}}
. Then
a\not\in R[a_{0}]
since the degrees of
a
and
a_{0}
are the same. This proves
the assertion.

(
b
):
Suppose that
a\in A_{d_{0}}\smallsetminus R_{d_{0}}
.
Let
a_{0}
be as in (
a
).
Then, by (
a
),

a+\lambda a_{0}\in R_{d_{0}}
for some
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
Hence
a_{0}\in R[a]
, so
A=R[a]
.
∎

6.3 Lemma
.

We may assume
\sigma(x_{i})=x_{i}
for all
i\neq n
.

Proof.

Let
i_{\sigma}=\text{max}\{i\mid\sigma x_{i}\neq x_{i}\}
.
Note that
i_{\sigma}>\beta_{\sigma}
.
For
i>\beta_{\sigma}
, define
\lambda_{i}
by
\sigma x_{i}-x_{i}=\lambda_{i}(\sigma x_{i_{\sigma}}-x_{i_{\sigma}})
.
We define a change of coordinates as follows:

x^{\prime}_{i}:=\begin{cases}x_{i},&\text{if}\;i\leq\beta_{\sigma},\\
x_{i}-\lambda_{i}x_{i_{\sigma}},&\text{if}\;i>\beta_{\sigma}\;\text{and}\;i\not\in\{i_{\sigma},n\},\\
x_{n},&\text{if}\;i=i_{\sigma},\\
x_{i_{\sigma}},&\text{if}\;i=n.\\
\end{cases}

Note that the action of
G
on
\Bbbk\langle x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}\rangle
is
‘upper-triangular’.
Let
\tau\in G
be a transvection. Then

\max\{j\mid x_{j}\;\text{appears in}\;\tau x_{i}-x_{i}\;\text{for some}\;i\}=\max\{j\mid x^{\prime}_{j}\;\text{appears in}\;\tau x^{\prime}_{i}-x^{\prime}_{i}\;\text{for some}\;i\}.

We see this as follows. Both sides give the maximum
j
that appears in a
linear form defining
the reflecting hyperplane of
\tau
.
However the equation of the reflecting
hyperplane of
\tau
involves only
x_{i}
with

i\leq\beta_{\sigma}
.
Let
\alpha_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{\beta_{\sigma}}x_{\beta_{\sigma}}
be
a linear form that defines the reflecting hyperplane of
\tau
.
Then in the new coordinates

\alpha_{1}x^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{\beta_{\sigma}}x^{\prime}_{\beta_{\sigma}}

defines the reflecting hyperplane of
\tau
.
Hence
\beta_{\tau}
does not change after this change of coordinates.
∎

6.4 Lemma
.

Suppose that
R
is a direct summand of
A
.
Then there exists a minimal algebra generating set
\{x_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\}

of
A
with
m\geq n
such that

(a)

there exists a unique
2\leq i_{0}\leq m
such that

a_{i_{0}}\not\in R
.

(b)

x_{1}<\In_{>}(a_{2})<\cdots<\In_{>}(a_{m})
;

(c)

there exists a unique
2\leq i_{1}\leq m
such that

\In_{>}(a_{i_{1}})=x_{n}^{\deg a_{i_{1}}}
.

Moreover,
\deg a_{i_{1}}
is a power of
p
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.2
,

A=\Bbbk[x_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}]
for some
a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\in A
with
m\geq n+1
, such that all but one of them is in
R
.
From this we choose a minimal generating set
which we continue to call

x_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}
,
m\geq n
, after relabelling the elements, if
necessary.
Note that all but one of the elements of the minimal generating set is in

R
.
This proves (
a
).

Now suppose that
a,a^{\prime}\in A
are part of a
\Bbbk
-algebra
generating set
X
of
A
with
\In_{>}(a)=\In_{>}(a^{\prime})
that
satisfies (
a
).
If
a\not\in R
or
a^{\prime}\not\in R
, assume, after relabelling if
necessary, that
a\in R
and
a^{\prime}\not\in R
.
Note that
(X\cup\{a^{\prime}+\lambda a\})\smallsetminus\{a^{\prime}\}
generates
A
for all
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
. For a suitable
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
,
\In_{>}(a^{\prime}+\lambda a)<\In_{>}(a)
. Therefore, without loss of generality we can assume

\In_{>}x_{1}<\In_{>}a_{2}<\cdots<\In_{>}a_{m}
.
This proves (
b
).

To prove (
c
),
let

i_{1}:=\min\{i\mid\In_{>}(a_{i})=x_{n}^{\deg a_{i}}\}.

(This set is non-empty since
S
is integral over
A
.)
Now, for all
r\in A
, if
\deg_{x_{n}}(r)=p^{e}m

with
p\nmid m
, then
\deg_{x_{n}}(\Delta_{x_{n}}^{(m-1)p^{e}}r)=p^{e}
.
Hence, if
\deg_{x_{n}}(a_{i_{1}})
is not a power of
p
, we can find an element

r\in A
such that
\deg r<\deg a_{i_{1}}
and
\In_{>}(r)=x_{n}^{\deg r}
.
Hence there must exist
j<i_{1}
such that

a_{j}\in A
and
\In_{>}(a_{j})=x_{n}^{\deg a_{j}}
, contradicting the
minimality of
i_{1}
.
Therefore write
\deg a_{i_{1}}=p^{e_{1}}
.

Now suppose that
j>i_{1}
and
\In_{>}(a_{j})=x_{n}^{\deg a_{j}}
.
If
p^{e_{1}}\nmid\deg a_{j}
, then applying

\Delta_{x_{n}}^{(t)}
to it for a suitable
t
, we obtain
a\in A
with

\In_{>}(a)=x_{n}^{\deg a}
and
\deg a<\deg(a_{i_{1}})
, which implies
that there
exists
i<i_{1}
with
\In_{>}(a_{i})=x_{n}^{\deg a_{i}}
, contradicting the
choice of
i_{1}
. Hence
p^{e_{1}}\mid\deg a_{j}
. We now consider the
different cases.

Case 1:
i_{0}=i_{1}
. Then
a_{j}\in R
.
Assume, for now, that
p^{e_{1}+1}\nmid\deg a_{j}
.
Applying
\Delta_{x_{n}}^{(t)}
to it for a suitable
t
, we obtain
a\in R

with
\In_{>}(a)=x_{n}^{\deg a}
and
\deg a=\deg(a_{i_{1}})
.
Hence
a
belongs to the subalgebra of
A
generated by

\{a_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq m,\deg a_{i}\leq\deg(a_{i_{1}})\}
.
However, since
i_{0}=i_{1}
and
\In_{>}(a)=x_{n}^{\deg a_{i_{1}}}

it follows that
a=\lambda a_{i_{1}}+a^{\prime}
for some
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
and
a^{\prime}\in R
, contradicting the fact that
a\in R
.
Hence
p^{e_{1}+1}\mid\deg a_{j}
.
Then we may replace
a_{j}
by

a_{j}-\lambda\left(\prod_{\sigma}a_{i_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\deg a_{j}}{p\deg a_{i_{1}}}}

for a suitable
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
This may violate (
b
), but the steps in its proof
may be repeated to ensure that (
b
) is satisfied.

Case 2:
i_{0}\neq i_{1}
. Then
a_{i_{1}}\in R
.
We may replace
a_{j}
by

a_{j}-\lambda a_{i_{1}}^{\frac{\deg a_{j}}{\deg a_{i_{1}}}}

for a suitable
\lambda\in\Bbbk^{\times}
.
Again, steps in the proof of (
b
)
may be repeated to ensure that (
b
) is satisfied.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

6.5 Lemma
.

Adopt the notation from Lemma
6.4
.
Assume that
\beta_{\sigma}>1
.
Then
a_{2}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]^{G}
.

Proof.

Since
A\cap\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]\subseteq R
,
it suffices to show that

a_{2}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]
.
By way of contradiction assume that
{x_{n}}
divides some terms of
a_{2}
.
Then in every such term, the exponent of
{x_{n}}
must be a power of
p
; for
otherwise, by taking
\Delta_{x_{n}}^{(t)}a_{2}
for a suitable
t
, we get a
(non-zero)
element of
R\smallsetminus\Bbbk[x_{1}]
, contradicting the minimality of

\In_{>}(a_{2})
. Using the same argument, we see that
a_{2}
must be of the
form

a_{2,0}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}x_{1}^{\deg a_{2}-p^{i}}{x_{n}}^{p^{i}}

with
a_{2,0}\in\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]
and
\lambda_{i}\in\Bbbk
.
Hence

(\sigma-1)a_{2}=(\sigma-1)a_{2,0}+\sum_{i\geq 0}\lambda_{i}x_{1}^{\deg a_{2}-p^{i}}(\sigma-1){x_{n}}^{p^{i}}.

By Lemma
6.3
,
(\sigma-1)a_{2,0}=0
.
Note that
(\sigma-1)x_{n}\not\in\Bbbk\langle x_{1}\rangle
.
Therefore, unless
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 0
,

(\sigma-1)a_{2}
would be a (non-zero) element of

with

\In_{>}((\sigma-1)a_{2})<\In_{>}(a_{2})
, contradicting the choice of
a_{2}
.
Hence
\lambda_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 0
.
∎

6.6 Lemma
.

Adopt the notation of Lemma
6.4
.
Suppose that
R
is a direct summand of
A
.
Let
W=\Bbbk\langle v_{n}\rangle
.
If
i_{0}=i_{1}
, then

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime},S,W}=\mathfrak{h}_{G,S,W}=(x_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{i_{0}-1},a_{i_{0}+1},\ldots,a_{m})S.

Proof.

Write
\Lambda=\{x_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{i_{0}-1},a_{i_{0}+1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
.
Let
a\in W^{\perp}S\cap A
be a homogeneous element. Write

a=\Phi(x_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})

where
\Phi
is a polynomial in
m
variables

\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}
.
Since

\Lambda\subseteq W^{\perp}S
, it follows that no term of

\Phi(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})

is a power of
\xi_{i_{0}}
.
Hence

, and, therefore,

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime},S,W}\subseteq\Lambda S
.
Moreover,
\Lambda\subseteq W^{\perp}S\cap R
, so we get

\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime},S,W}\subseteq\Lambda S\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{G,S,W}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime},S,W},

proving the lemma.
∎

As an application, we prove a partial converse to
Proposition
5.5
.

6.7 Proposition
.

Adopt the notation of Lemma
6.4
.
Suppose that
i_{0}=i_{1}
and that
R
is a polynomial ring.
Then
A
is a polynomial ring.

Proof.

Note that
R
is a polynomial ring if and only if
S
is a free
R
-module.
The ‘only if’ direction follows from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula;
see Discussion
2.3
for the ‘if’ direction.

R
is a polynomial ring if and only if

\dim_{\Bbbk}S/\mathfrak{h}_{G,S}=|G|
.
Similarly
A
is a polynomial ring if and only if

\dim_{\Bbbk}S/\mathfrak{h}_{G^{\prime},S}=|G^{\prime}|
.
Let
W=\Bbbk\langle v_{n}\rangle
.

Step 1.
\min\{\deg r\mid r\in R\smallsetminus W^{\perp}S\}=p\deg a_{i_{0}}
.
Proof: Let
r\in R\smallsetminus W^{\perp}S
be of the smallest degree.
Then
\In_{>}(r)=x_{n}^{\deg r}
.
By considering
\Delta_{n}^{(t)}r,t\geq 1
,
we see that
\deg r
is a power of
p
.
By the definition of
i_{1}
,
\deg r\geq\deg a_{i_{1}}
.
Since
i_{0}=i_{1}
,
r\neq a_{i_{1}}
.
Hence
\deg r>\deg a_{i_{1}}
.
Since
\deg a_{i_{1}}
is a power of
p
, it follows that

\deg r\geq p\deg a_{i_{1}}=p\deg a_{i_{0}}
.
On the other hand
\prod_{\sigma}a_{i_{0}}\in R\smallsetminus W^{\perp}S
, so

\deg r\leq p\deg a_{i_{0}}
.

Step 2. Let
r_{0}\in R\smallsetminus W^{\perp}S
be such that
\deg r_{0}=p\deg a_{i_{0}}
.
Since

\Ass_{S}(S/\mathfrak{h}_{G,S,W})=W^{\perp}S

(Corollary
3.2
)

a_{i_{0}}
and
r_{0}
are non-zero-divisors on

S/\mathfrak{h}_{G,S,W}
.

Step 3. Note that
R/(W^{\perp}S\cap R)
is a
1
-dimensional polynomial ring
(Proposition
2.2
).
Hence
R/(W^{\perp}S\cap R)\simeq\Bbbk[r_{0}]

Similarly

8.1 Lemma
.

Let
G
be a transvection group of order
p^{2}
.
Then
S^{G}
is a polynomial ring.

Proof.

Every group of order
p^{2}
is abelian. Let
\sigma,\tau\in G
be
transvections that generate
G
. Fix equations
l_{\sigma}
and
l_{\tau}

defining the reflecting hyperplanes of
\sigma
and
\tau
respectively.
Note that
\sigma\tau\sigma^{-1}
is a transvection, with reflecting
hyperplane defined by
\sigma l_{\tau}
; similarly,

\tau\sigma\tau^{-1}
is a transvection, with reflecting
hyperplane defined by
\tau l_{\sigma}
.
Since
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
, we see that
\sigma l_{\tau}=l_{\tau}
; in
any case,
\tau l_{\tau}=l_{\tau}
. Therefore
l_{\tau}\in S^{G}
. Similarly

l_{\sigma}\in S^{G}
.

If
l_{\tau}
and
l_{\sigma}
are linearly dependent over
\Bbbk
, then
G

has exactly one reflecting hyperplane. Therefore
using
[
LS87
, Theorem 5, Appendix]

we see that

is a polynomial ring.

Otherwise, we could assume that
l_{\sigma}=x_{1}
and
l_{\tau}=x_{2}
.
After permuting the variables
x_{3},\ldots,x_{n}
and replacing
x_{1}

by a suitable scalar multiple of it,
we may assume that
\sigma x_{3}=x_{3}+x_{1}
. For all
i\geq 4
, after
replacing
x_{i}
by
x_{i}+\lambda x_{3}
for a suitable
\lambda\in\Bbbk
,
we may assume that
\sigma x_{i}=x_{i}
.
Similarly, if there exists
i\geq 4
such that
\tau x_{i}\neq x_{i}
, we may
assume that
\tau x_{4}\neq x_{4}
and that that
\tau x_{i}=x_{i}
for all

.
Hence
S^{G}=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{4}]^{G}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Bbbk[x_{5},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Therefore we may assume that

.
Further replacing
x_{3}
with
x_{3}+\lambda x_{4}
for a suitable
\lambda
,
we may assume that
\tau x_{3}=x_{3}

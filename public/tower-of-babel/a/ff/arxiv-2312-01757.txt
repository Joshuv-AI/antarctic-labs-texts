There are no unconditional Schauder frames of translates in L p ​ ( ℝ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}) , 1 ⩽ p ⩽ 2 1\leqslant p\leqslant 2 Thanks: Research supported by ISF Grant No. 1044/21.
Nir Lev, Anton Tselishchev

Abstract
. It is known that a system formed by translates of a single function cannot be an unconditional Schauder basis in the space L p ​ ( ℝ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}) for any 1 ⩽ p < ∞ 1\leqslant p<\infty . To the contrary, there do exist unconditional Schauder frames of translates in L p ​ ( ℝ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}) for every p > 2 p>2 . The existence of such a system for 1 < p ⩽ 2 1<p\leqslant 2 , however, has remained an open problem. In this paper the problem is solved in the negative: we prove that none of the spaces L p ​ ( ℝ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}) , 1 ⩽ p ⩽ 2 1\leqslant p\leqslant 2 , admits an unconditional Schauder frame of translates.

46B15, 46E30

1.
Introduction

1.1.

A system of vectors
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in a Banach space
X

is called a
Schauder basis
if every
x\in X
admits a unique series expansion

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}x_{n}
where
\{c_{n}\}
are scalars.
In this case there exist
biorthogonal continuous linear functionals
\{x_{n}^{*}\}

such that the coefficients of the series expansion are given by

c_{n}=x_{n}^{*}(x)
(see e.g.
[
Woj91
, Chapter II.B]
).
If the series converges unconditionally
(i.e. if it converges for any rearrangement of its terms)
for every
x\in X
, then
\{x_{n}\}

is said to be an
unconditional
Schauder basis.

Given a function
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote its translates by

(T_{\lambda}g)(x)=g(x-\lambda),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

A long-standing problem which is still open, asks
whether the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1<p<\infty
, admits
a Schauder basis consisting of translates of a single function.
This problem was first posed for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
in
[
OZ92
]
,
and later for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
in
[
OSSZ11
, Problem 4.4]
.
We note that in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
there are no Schauder bases of translates,
see e.g.
[
OSSZ11
, Theorem 1.7, Proposition 1.8]
.

It is known that a system formed by translates of a single function
cannot form an
unconditional
Schauder basis in
any of the spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1<p<\infty
.
This was proved in
[
OZ92
]
for
p=2
, in
[
OSSZ11
]
for
1<p\leqslant 4
,
and in
[
FOSZ14
]
for
p>4
.

1.2.

If
X
is a Banach space with dual space
X^{*}
, then a system

\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
X\times X^{*}
is called a

Schauder frame
(or a quasi-basis) if every
x\in X
has a series expansion

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x)x_{n}.

(1.2)

If the series (
1.2
)
converges unconditionally for every
x\in X
, then

\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}
is called an
unconditional
Schauder frame.
We note that if
\{x_{n}\}
is a Schauder basis
with biorthogonal coefficient functionals
\{x_{n}^{*}\}
,
then
\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}
is a Schauder frame.
To the contrary, for a Schauder frame
\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}
the series expansion
(
1.2
) need not be unique and the coefficient functionals
\{x_{n}^{*}\}

need not be biorthogonal to
\{x_{n}\}
. Hence
Schauder frames form a wider class of representation systems than Schauder bases.

It was proved in
[
FOSZ14
]
that in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p>2
,
there exist unconditional Schauder frames consisting
of translates of a single function, i.e. of the form

\{(T_{\lambda_{n}}g,g_{n}^{*})\}
where
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,

\{\lambda_{n}\}
are real numbers, and

\{g_{n}^{*}\}
are continuous linear functionals on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover,
\{\lambda_{n}\}
may be chosen to be

an arbitrary unbounded sequence
,
and in particular, it may increase arbitrarily fast.

On the other hand, it has been known that in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
there are no
unconditional Schauder frames of translates, see
[
BC20
, Section 4.3]
.

The existence of an unconditional Schauder frame of translates in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,

1<p\leqslant 2
, has remained an open problem.
The main goal of the present paper is to solve this problem in the negative.
We will prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
there does not exist
in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
any unconditional Schauder frame
consisting of translates, that is, there is no unconditional Schauder frame of the form

\{(T_{\lambda_{n}}g,g_{n}^{*})\}
where
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,

\{\lambda_{n}\}
are real numbers, and

\{g_{n}^{*}\}
are continuous linear functionals on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

A special case of this result, where
the coefficient functionals
\{g_{n}^{*}\}
are assumed to be

seminormalized
, was proved in
[
BC20
]
.
(Recall that a system of vectors
\{x_{n}\}

in a Banach space
X
is said to be seminormalized
if there exist positive constants
A,B
such that
A\leqslant\|x_{n}\|\leqslant B

for every
n
.)

We note that contrary to Theorem
1.1
, there do exist (not unconditional) Schauder frames
of translates in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for any
1\leqslant p<\infty
, see
[
FPT21
, Section 4]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

we give some preliminary background. In Section
3
we prove
Theorem
1.1
. Finally, Section
4
contains some additional remarks.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we state some known facts that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.

We start with a basic property of unconditional Schauder frames, that can be proved
using the uniform boundedness principle in the same way
as a similar statement for unconditional Schauder bases (see e.g.
[
AK16
, Proposition 3.1.3]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{(x_{j},x_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an unconditional Schauder frame in
a Banach space
X
. Then there exists a constant
K
such that for any

x\in X
and for any
choice of scalars
\theta_{j}
satisfying
|\theta_{j}|\leqslant 1
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\theta_{j}x_{j}^{*}(x)x_{j}\Big\|\leqslant K\|x\|

(2.1)

and the series in (
2.1
) converges unconditionally.

We remark that the condition (
2.1
) is often
indicated by saying that the Schauder frame

\{(x_{j},x_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is
K
-unconditional
.

2.2.

We will use
\{r_{n}(t)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
to denote the sequence of Rademacher functions
defined on the segment
[0,1]
by
r_{n}(t)=\operatorname{sign}\sin(2^{n}\pi t)
.
The next lemma formulates the fact that for
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
the space
L^{p}
has cotype
2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
. There exists a constant
C_{p}
such that for any finite
number of functions
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots f_{N}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant C_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)f_{j}\Big\|_{p}\,dt.

(2.2)

This is a key point where the assumption
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
is used
in the proof. Note that inequality (
2.2
) does not hold for
p>2
.

A proof of Lemma
2.2
, as well as other information about the
notions of type and cotype of a Banach space,
can be found e.g. in
[
Woj91
, Chapter III.A]
.
In fact,
C_{p}
may be chosen to be an absolute constant not depending on
p
, but
we will not use this fact.

2.3.

A set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is said to be
uniformly discrete
if there exists
\delta>0
such that

|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|\geqslant\delta
for any two distinct points
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
.
We will use the following elementary fact (for a proof, see
[
OSSZ11
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, and suppose that

\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is a
uniformly discrete set. Then for any bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
we have

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\int_{I}|T_{\lambda}f|^{p}<+\infty.

(2.3)

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
, and assume that there exists
in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
an unconditional Schauder frame of the form

\{(T_{\lambda_{j}}g,g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
where
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,

\{\lambda_{j}\}
are real numbers, and

\{g_{j}^{*}\}
are continuous linear functionals on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
Our goal is to show that this assumption leads to a contradiction.
With no loss of generality we may assume that
\|g\|_{p}=1
.

A standard approach involves an application of Lemma
2.1
which implies that

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}r_{j}(t)g_{j}^{*}(f)T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K\|f\|_{p},\quad t\in[0,1],

(3.1)

where
\{r_{j}(t)\}
is the sequence of Rademacher functions. If we now take

\int_{0}^{1}dt
and use Lemma
2.2
, and finally also take the limit as
N\to\infty
,
we obtain the
\ell^{2}
-estimate

\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant C_{p}K\|f\|_{p}

(3.2)

(note that we have used the assumption
\|g\|_{p}=1
).
However this estimate for the coefficients
\{g_{j}^{*}(f)\}
is not good
enough to yield the full statement of Theorem
1.1
,
and so our approach will involve a certain improvement of the inequality (
3.2
).

We will present the proof in several steps.

3.1.
Step 1: An
\ell^{1}
-estimate for blocks of coefficients

Let us fix
\delta>0
small enough
such that
\|T_{a}g-T_{b}g\|_{p}\leqslant 1/2

whenever
a,b
are two real numbers satisfying
|a-b|\leqslant\delta
.
We consider a partition of
\mathbb{R}
into
intervals
[k\delta,(k+1)\delta)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. It induces a partition of the
unconditional Schauder frame into “blocks”

\{(T_{\lambda_{j}}g,g_{j}^{*})\}_{j\in A_{k}}
, where

A_{k}:=\{j:\lambda_{j}\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta)\},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.3)

It is important to keep in mind that some of the sets
A_{k}
may be infinite, since
the sequence
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
does not necessarily tend to
\pm\infty

and thus a bounded interval may in principle contain infinitely many elements
\lambda_{j}
of the sequence.

For any
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
we thus have the unconditionally convergent series expansion

f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}g_{j}^{*}(f)T_{\lambda_{j}}g.

(3.4)

We now make the following observation: given arbitrary nonnegative scalars
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in A_{k}}
such that
only finitely many of them are nonzero, we have

\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}.

(3.5)

Indeed, using the fact that
\|T_{k\delta}g-T_{\lambda_{j}}g\|_{p}\leqslant 1/2

for each
j\in A_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geqslant\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}T_{k\delta}g\Big\|_{p}-\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}(T_{k\delta}g-T_{\lambda_{j}}g)\Big\|_{p}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}\Big)-\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}(T_{k\delta}g-T_{\lambda_{j}}g)\Big\|_{p}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}a_{j}

(3.7)

(again using
\|g\|_{p}=1
) which shows that (
3.5
) is valid.

Assume that for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the set
A_{k}
is finite. Then, if
we put
a_{j}=|g_{j}^{*}(f)|
in the inequality (
3.5
), we arrive at the estimate

\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\leqslant 2\,\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\;T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}.

(3.8)

Next we observe that (
3.8
)
holds also if
A_{k}
is an infinite set. Indeed, the series
on the right hand side is guaranteed to
converge unconditionally due to Lemma
2.1
,
so the inequality can be established
by applying the estimate (
3.8
) to
the coefficients
\{g_{j}^{*}(f)\}
in increasing finite subsets
of
A_{k}
, and passing to the limit.

Moreover, again by Lemma
2.1
we have

\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\;T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K\|f\|_{p},

(3.9)

hence (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) imply the
\ell^{1}
-estimate

\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\leqslant 2K\|f\|_{p}

(3.10)

for the coefficients in the
A_{k}
block of the series expansion (
3.4
).

3.2.
Step 2: Compactness of partial sum operators

For each
N
we let

S_{N}f=\sum_{|k|\leqslant N}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}g_{j}^{*}(f)T_{\lambda_{j}}g,\quad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),

(3.11)

be the
N
’th partial sum of the series expansion (
3.4
).
We note that some of the blocks
A_{k}
may be infinite, hence the
series (
3.11
) may contain infinitely many terms. However, the series
converges unconditionally due to Lemma
2.1
.

We consider
S_{N}
as a linear operator on the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
Our goal is to show that
S_{N}

is a compact operator. This is obvious if all the blocks
A_{k}
happen to
be finite, since in this case
S_{N}
is an operator of finite rank.
In the general case, where some of the blocks
A_{k}
may be infinite,
we argue as follows.

It would be enough to prove that for each fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the operator
B_{k}
defined by

B_{k}f=\sum_{j\in A_{k}}g_{j}^{*}(f)T_{\lambda_{j}}g

(3.12)

is compact. We will
show that
B_{k}
can be approximated by operators of finite rank.
Let
\varepsilon>0
, and choose
\eta>0
small enough
so that we have
\|T_{a}g-T_{b}g\|_{p}\leqslant\varepsilon

whenever
a,b
are two real numbers satisfying
|a-b|\leqslant\eta
.
Let us take a finite
\eta
-net for the segment
[k\delta,(k+1)\delta)
,
that is, a finite set
F
such that each point of
the segment
[k\delta,(k+1)\delta)
lies within distance at most
\eta

from some point of
F
. For each
j\in A_{k}
we choose
a point
\sigma_{j}
in this
\eta
-net
F
satisfying

|\sigma_{j}-\lambda_{j}|\leqslant\eta
. Then

\|T_{\sigma_{j}}g-T_{\lambda_{j}}g\|_{p}\leqslant\varepsilon
, for each
j\in A_{k}
.

Define an operator
E_{k}
by

E_{k}f=\sum_{j\in A_{k}}g_{j}^{*}(f)(T_{\sigma_{j}}g-T_{\lambda_{j}}g),\quad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}).

(3.13)

We show that the series (
3.13
) converges unconditionally and defines a continuous linear operator

E_{k}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, using the estimate (
3.10
) we obtain

\|E_{k}f\|_{p}\leqslant\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\cdot\|T_{\sigma_{j}}g-T_{\lambda_{j}}g\|_{p}\leqslant\varepsilon\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\leqslant 2K\varepsilon\|f\|_{p},

(3.14)

which establishes the unconditional convergence of
the series (
3.13
) as well as the continuity of the operator
E_{k}
.
Moreover, we obtain that
\|E_{k}\|\leqslant 2K\varepsilon
.

Note that we have

(B_{k}+E_{k})f=\sum_{j\in A_{k}}g_{j}^{*}(f)T_{\sigma_{j}}g,\quad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),

(3.15)

the series (
3.15
) again being unconditionally convergent.
But recall that each
\sigma_{j}
belongs to
the finite
\eta
-net
F
of the segment
[k\delta,(k+1)\delta)
.
Hence the image of the operator
B_{k}+E_{k}
lies in the
finite-dimensional linear subspace spanned by the translates

\{T_{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in F}
, which implies that

B_{k}+E_{k}
is an operator of finite rank.

We have thus shown that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists an operator
B_{k}+E_{k}

of finite rank, that approximates
B_{k}

in the operator norm with error at most
2K\varepsilon
.
Hence
B_{k}
is a compact operator, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
As a consequence,
S_{N}
must be compact.

3.3.
Step 3: An application of cotype
2
inequality

Our next goal is to use the estimate (
3.8
)
in order to obtain an improvement of the inequality (
3.2
).
The key observation is that we will get a better estimate if instead of multiplying each
summand by a random sign separately as in (
3.1
),
we will do it for each block.

Indeed, by an application of Lemma
2.1
we have

\Big\|\sum_{|k|\leqslant N}r_{n_{k}}(t)\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\;T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}\leqslant K\|f\|_{p},\quad t\in[0,1],

(3.16)

where
\{r_{n_{k}}(t)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is some enumeration of the
sequence of Rademacher functions. If we take

\int_{0}^{1}dt
and use Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

C_{p}^{-1}\Big(\sum_{|k|\leqslant N}\Big\|\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\;T_{\lambda_{j}}g\Big\|_{p}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leqslant K\|f\|_{p}.

(3.17)

Finally, combining this inequality together with (
3.8
) and
taking the limit as
N\to\infty
, we arrive at the key estimate

\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\Big)^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\leqslant 2KC_{p}\|f\|_{p},

(3.18)

which is a “mixed”
(\ell^{1},\ell^{2})
-estimate for the coefficients
\{g_{j}^{*}(f)\}
that
improves on the standard
\ell^{2}
-estimate given in (
3.2
).

3.4.
Step 4: Compactness of restriction operator

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an arbitrary bounded interval, and let

R_{I}
be the operator of
restriction to the interval
I
, that is,
R_{I}f:=f|_{I}
. Then

R_{I}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{p}(I)
is a continuous linear operator.

Consider again the sequence of partial sum operators
\{S_{N}\}
defined by
(
3.11
). Then for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
we
have
(S_{N}f)|_{I}\to f|_{I}
in
L^{p}(I)
, that is,

R_{I}\circ S_{N}\to R_{I}
in the strong operator topology.
If we show that
\{R_{I}\circ S_{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence of operators
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{p}(I)

(with respect to the operator norm), then it would follow that
R_{I}\circ S_{N}\to R_{I}

in the operator norm. But as we have shown above,

S_{N}
, and hence also
R_{I}\circ S_{N}
, are compact operators.
We will therefore obtain that
R_{I}
must be a compact operator as well,
which leads to a contradiction.

(We note that this approach,
based on compactness of the restriction operator
R_{I}
,
goes back to
[
OSSZ11
]
and has been used also in
[
FOSZ14
]
,
[
BC20
]
.)

It remains to prove that

\{R_{I}\circ S_{N}\}
is a Cauchy sequence. For any
M>N
we have

\displaystyle\|R_{I}\circ(S_{M}-S_{N})f\|_{p}=\Big\|\sum_{N<|k|\leqslant M}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}g_{j}^{*}(f)(T_{\lambda_{j}}g)|_{I}\Big\|_{p}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\sum_{N<|k|\leqslant M}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\cdot\|(T_{\lambda_{j}}g)|_{I}\|_{p}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\sum_{N<|k|\leqslant M}\Big(\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\Big)\cdot\max_{\lambda\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]}\|(T_{\lambda}g)|_{I}\|_{p}.

(3.21)

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we can choose a point
\lambda^{(k)}
in the segment

[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]
such that

\max_{\lambda\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]}\|(T_{\lambda}g)|_{I}\|_{p}=\|(T_{\lambda^{(k)}}g)|_{I}\|_{p}.

(3.22)

Assume first that
1<p\leqslant 2
, and let
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
be the exponent conjugate to
p
.
Using (
3.22
) and Hölder’s inequality we obtain that the quantity (
3.21
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{N<|k|\leqslant M}\Big(\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\Big)\|(T_{\lambda^{(k)}}g)|_{I}\|_{p}

(3.23)

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\Big)^{p^{\prime}}\Big]^{1/p^{\prime}}\Big[\sum_{N<|k|\leqslant M}\int_{I}|T_{\lambda^{(k)}}g|^{p}\Big]^{1/p}.

(3.24)

We observe that the sequence
\{\lambda^{(k)}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
can be partitioned into
two uniformly discrete subsequences (since each one of
\{\lambda^{(2k)}\}
and
\{\lambda^{(2k+1)}\}
is uniformly discrete).
Hence Lemma
2.3
implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N(\varepsilon)

such that the second factor in (
3.24
)
will be smaller than
\varepsilon
for
M>N>N(\varepsilon)
.
The first factor can be estimated using the fact that
p^{\prime}\geqslant 2
and inequality (
3.18
).
It implies that for
M>N>N(\varepsilon)
the quantity (
3.24
) does not exceed

\Big[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\Big)^{2}\Big]^{1/2}\varepsilon\leqslant 2KC_{p}\,\varepsilon\|f\|_{p}.

(3.25)

Therefore, for
M>N>N(\varepsilon)
we have

\|R_{I}\circ(S_{M}-S_{N})\|\leqslant 2KC_{p}\,\varepsilon
,
which shows that
\{R_{I}\circ S_{N}\}
is indeed a Cauchy sequence
and leads to the desired contradiction.

Lastly, in the remaining case where
p=1
, we simply replace
the first factor in (
3.24
) with

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\{\sum_{j\in A_{k}}|g_{j}^{*}(f)|\big\}

and the proof continues in the same way. However we note
that in this case a more general result is actually true: it is known that
in the space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
there do not exist any unconditional Schauder frames,
whether formed by translates or not (see
[
BC20
, Section 4.3]
).

4.
Remarks

4.1.

Theorem
1.1
can be easily generalized to the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It is also easy to extend the result to unconditional Schauder frames formed
by translates of a finite number of functions. That is, for
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
there does not exist
in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
any unconditional Schauder frame of the form

\{(g_{j},g_{j}^{*})\}
such that each
g_{j}
is a translate of a function
from some finite set
G\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

4.2.

In fact our proof establishes the following more general
version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the system
\{(T_{\lambda_{j}}g,g_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

forms an unconditional Schauder frame for a (closed, linear)
subspace
X\subset L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
. Then for every bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
the restriction operator

R_{I}:X\to L^{p}(I)
is compact.

Moreover, in this case the subspace
X
embeds into
\ell^{p}
, see
[
FOSZ14
, Proposition 5.3]
.

4.3.

Let
q\geqslant 2
, and let
p=q/(q-1)
be the exponent conjugate to
q
. By identifying the dual space of
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
with
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
in the usual way,
one can define the translates
T_{\lambda}g^{*}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, of
a continuous linear functional
g^{*}
on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
.
This allows to consider Schauder frames in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
of the form

\{(g_{n},T_{\lambda_{n}}g^{*})\}
, namely, where the coefficient functionals
are translates of a single functional
g^{*}
.
As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
we can then obtain the following result
(compare with
[
FOSZ14
, Corollary 3.3]
):

Theorem 4.2
.

For
q\geqslant 2
there does not exist
in the space
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
any unconditional Schauder frame of the form

\{(g_{n},T_{\lambda_{n}}g^{*})\}
where
g_{n}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
,

g^{*}
is a continuous linear functional on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
, and

\{\lambda_{n}\}
are real numbers.

This can be deduced from Theorem
1.1
using the following
fact (see, for example, the discussion before the proof of
[
FOSZ14
, Corollary 3.3]
): if

\{(x_{j},x_{j}^{*})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is an unconditional Schauder frame in
a
reflexive
Banach space
X
, then the system

\{(x_{j}^{*},x_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forms an
unconditional Schauder frame for the dual space
X^{*}
.

We outline a short proof of the latter fact. It is straightforward to verify that every

x^{*}\in X^{*}
has a weakly unconditionally convergent expansion

x^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x^{*}(x_{j})x_{j}^{*}

(due to reflexivity, the weak convergence in
X^{*}
is the same as the weak-
*
convergence). On the other hand,
X^{*}
being reflexive does not contain any subspace isomorphic to
c_{0}

(see
[
Woj91
, Theorem II.A.14]
), hence every weakly unconditionally
convergent series in
X^{*}
is unconditionally convergent
(see
[
Woj91
, Proposition II.D.5]
).

4.4.

One can consider also translates of a function
g
on the circle
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.
By an argument similar to
[
OZ92
, Theorem 1]
one can show that
the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, does not admit
a Schauder basis formed by translates of a single function.
On the other hand, it can be deduced from

[
FPT21
, Section 4]
that there do exist Schauder frames
of translates in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
for any
1\leqslant p<\infty
.
Our technique allows us to establish that Schauder
frames of this form cannot be unconditional:

Theorem 4.3
.

There does not exist in the space

L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
any unconditional Schauder frame
of translates, i.e. of the form

\{(T_{\lambda_{n}}g,g_{n}^{*})\}
where
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,

\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{T}
,
and
\{g_{n}^{*}\}
are continuous linear functionals on
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
.

This can be proved by adapting Steps 1 and 2 from
the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Based on these steps
one can show that the existence of an unconditional Schauder frame
of translates
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
implies compactness of the identity operator, a contradiction.

4.5.

A system of vectors
\{x_{n}\}
in a Hilbert space
H
is called
a
frame
if there are positive constants
A,B
such that
the inequalities

A\|x\|^{2}\leqslant\sum_{n}|\langle x,x_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leqslant B\|x\|^{2}

hold for every
x\in H
.
In this case there exists another frame
\{y_{n}\}
(called the “dual” frame)
such that the system

\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}
forms an unconditional Schauder frame in
H
,
where the dual space
H^{*}
is identified with
H
in the usual way
(see
[
You01
, Section 4.7]
).

It was proved in
[
CDH99
]
that
in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
there does not exist any frame formed
by translates of a single function. This result can now be viewed
as a special case of Theorem
1.1
.

The following open problem was communicated to us by D. Freeman:
If
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}
is an unconditional Schauder frame in a Hilbert space
H
,
does there exist a sequence of nonzero scalars
\{\alpha_{n}\}
such that

\{\alpha_{n}x_{n}\}
and
\{\alpha_{n}^{-1}y_{n}\}
are both frames in
H
?
For more information related to this problem we refer the reader to
[
BFPS24
]
.

In this direction, the following result was obtained in
[
HLLL14
, Theorem 3.13]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}
be an unconditional Schauder frame in a Hilbert space
H
.
If both
\inf_{n}\|x_{n}\|
and
\inf_{n}\|y_{n}\|
are nonzero,
then
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
are both frames.

We use this opportunity to present a short, self-contained proof of this fact.

Proof.

Due to Lemma
2.1
we have

\|\sum_{n}r_{n}(t)\langle x,y_{n}\rangle x_{n}\|^{2}\leqslant K^{2}\|x\|^{2}
,

x\in H
,
where
\{r_{n}(t)\}
is the sequence of Rademacher functions. If we take

\int_{0}^{1}dt
then orthogonality of Rademacher functions gives

\sum_{n}|\langle x,y_{n}\rangle|^{2}\|x_{n}\|^{2}\leqslant K^{2}\|x\|^{2}
.
Using the assumption that
\inf_{n}\|x_{n}\|>0
we conclude
that
\{y_{n}\}
is a Bessel sequence (see
[
You01
, Section 4.2]
).

The system
\{(y_{n},x_{n})\}
is also
an unconditional Schauder frame in
H
(see Section
4.3
),
hence by symmetry also
\{x_{n}\}
is a Bessel sequence.
We can therefore define a continuous linear operator
A:H\to\ell^{2}

by
Ax=\{\langle x,x_{n}\rangle\}
. The
adjoint operator
A^{*}:\ell^{2}\to H
is given by

A^{*}(\{c_{n}\})=\sum_{n}c_{n}x_{n}
.
The fact that
\{(x_{n},y_{n})\}
is a Schauder frame
and
\{y_{n}\}
is a Bessel sequence implies
that
A^{*}
is surjective,
which in turn is equivalent to the existence of a constant
c>0

such that
\|Ax\|^{2}\geqslant c\|x\|^{2}
,
x\in H
(see
[
Woj91
, I.A.13]
).
This inequality together with the boundedness of
A

means that
\{x_{n}\}
is a frame. Finally, by symmetry also

\{y_{n}\}
is a frame, and so the claim is proved.
∎

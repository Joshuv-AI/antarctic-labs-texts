Dirichlet problem for harmonic maps from strongly rectifiable spaces into regular balls in CAT ⁡ ( 1 ) \operatorname{CAT}(1) spaces
Yohei Sakurai

Abstract
. In this note, we study the Dirichlet problem for harmonic maps from strongly rectifiable spaces into regular balls in CAT ⁡ ( 1 ) \operatorname{CAT}(1) space. Under the setting, we prove that the Korevaar-Schoen energy admits a unique minimizer.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53E23; Secondly 58E20

1.
Introduction

1.1.
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
target

The study of harmonic maps between singular spaces is one of the central topics in geometric analysis since the pioneering work by Gromov-Schoen
[
24
]
.
In an earlier stage,
the theory has been developed by Korevaar-Schoen
[
30
]
, Jost
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
and Lin
[
32
]
,
independently.
Korevaar-Schoen
[
30
]
have introduced an energy for
L^{2}
-maps from a Riemannian domain into a complete metric space,
and derived its basic properties;
for instance,
the existence of energy density,
the consistency of Sobolev functions,
and its lower semicontinuity.
Furthermore,
they have proven that
if the target space is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space (i.e., non-positively curved space in the sense of A. D. Alexandrov),
then the energy admits a unique minimizer for the Dirichlet problem.

One of the research directions is to generalize the theory for non-smooth source spaces.
Such attempts have been done by Gregori
[
16
]
, Eells-Fuglede
[
11
]
, Kuwae-Shioya
[
31
]
, and so on.
Gregori
[
16
]
and Eells-Fuglede
[
11
]
have dealt with a domain of a Lipschitz manifold and that of a Riemannian polyhedron,
respectively.
Kuwae-Shioya
[
31
]
have examined a metric measure space satisfying the so-called strongly measure contraction property of Bishop-Gromov type, called
\operatorname{SMCPBG}
space.
A typical example of
\operatorname{SMCPBG}
space is an Alexandrov space,
which is a metric space equipped with the notion of a lower sectional curvature bound.

In recent years,
the theory of metric measure spaces with a lower Ricci curvature bound has been vastly developed.
In the literature,
the main research object is the so-called
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
space,
which has been introduced by Ambrosio-Gigli-Savaré
[
1
]
and Gigli
[
14
]
.
It is natural to ask whether the Korevaar-Schoen theory can be extended to
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.
Here we notice that
an
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
space is not necessarily a
\operatorname{SMCPBG}
space (especially, in the collapsed case) since the Bishop type inequality is required in the
\operatorname{SMCPBG}
condition,
and hence the framework of Kuwae-Shioya
[
31
]
does not cover
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.
Gigli-Tyulenev
[
22
]
were able to work in a more general context,
covering the
\operatorname{RCD}
setting.
In their framework,
source spaces are assumed to be locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable and to satisfy a Poincaré inequality (more precisely, see Subsection
2.2
).
Under this setting,
they have defined a Korevaar-Schoen type energy and deduced some fundamental results such as the existence of energy density and its lower semicontinuity.
They have concluded the solvability of the Dirichlet problem when the target space is a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space (see
[
22
, Theorem 6.4]
).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable space satisfying a Poincaré inequality,
and let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
{\rm X}
with
{\mathfrak{m}}({\rm X}\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Let
{\rm Y}_{\rm o}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
Let
\bar{u}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{\rm o})
be a Korevaar-Schoen type Sobolev map which determines a boundary value.
Then the Korevaar-Schoen type energy
{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}:L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{\rm o})\to[0,+\infty]
admits a unique minimizer.

Thanks to Theorem
1.1
,
one can introduce the notion of harmonic map from an
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
space to a
\operatorname{CAT}(0)
space.
Very recently,
Gigli
[
16
]
has shown a quantitative Lipschitz estimate for such harmonic maps,
and produced a Cheng type Liouville theorem (
[
7
]
) based on
[
10
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
(see also
[
40
]
,
[
41
]
).
Mondino-Semola
[
35
]
have also obtained a similar result, independently.

1.2.
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
target

The purpose of this note is to yield an analogue of Theorem
1.1
for the case where the target space is a regular ball in a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space (i.e., geodesic ball whose radius is strictly less than
\pi/2
).
In the case where the source space is a Riemannian domain,
the solvability of the Dirichlet problem has been established by Serbinowski
[
37
]
.
Similarly to the non-positively curved case,
the result in
[
37
]
has been extended to non-smooth source spaces (see Eells-Fuglede
[
11
]
, Fuglede
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
for Riemannian polyhedra, and Huang-Zhang
[
25
]
for Alexandrov spaces).
We now aim to generalize it for
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
spaces.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be an infinitesimally Hilbertian, locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable space satisfying a Poincaré inequality,
and let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
{\rm X}
with
{\mathfrak{m}}({\rm X}\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Let
{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space,
and let
\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})
be a regular ball
(
i.e., a closed ball of radius
\rho\in(0,\pi/2)
centered at
{\rm o}
)
.
Let
\bar{u}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}(o))
be a Korevaar-Schoen type Sobolev map which determines a boundary value.
Then the Korevaar-Schoen type energy
{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}:L^{2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))\to[0,+\infty]
admits a unique minimizer.

Theorem
1.2
enables us to introduce the notion of harmonic map from an
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
space into a regular ball in a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space.
When the source space is a Riemannian domain,
Zhang-Zhong-Zhu
[
41
]
have obtained a quantitative Lipschitz estimate and a Choi type Liouville theorem (
[
8
]
).

2.
Preliminaries

We say that

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is a metric measure space
if
({\rm X},{\sf d})
is a complete separable metric space,
and
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a non-negative Borel measure,
which is finite on bounded sets.
This section is devoted to preliminaries for metric measure spaces.

2.1.
Sobolev functions

We briefly recall the non-smooth differential calculus on metric measure spaces.
The readers can refer to
[
15
]
,
[
18
]
for the details.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a metric measure space and let
C([0,1],{\rm X})
be the set of all curves in
{\rm X}
defined on
[0,1]
with the uniform topology.
For
t\in[0,1]
the
evaluation map

{\rm e}_{t}:C([0,1],{\rm X})\to{\rm X}
is defined as
{\rm e}_{t}(\gamma):=\gamma_{t}
.
A curve
\gamma\in C([0,1],{\rm X})
is said to be
absolutely continuous
if there is
f\in L^{1}(0,1)
such that

(2.1)

{\sf d}(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{s})\leq\int_{s}^{t}f(r)\,{\rm d}r

for all
t,s\in[0,1]
with
s<t
.
For an absolutely continuous
\gamma
,
the minimal
f
satisfying (
2.1
) in the a.e. sense is called the
metric speed
and it is denoted by
|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|
.
A Borel probability measure
\pi
on
C([0,1],{\rm X})
is said to be a
test plan
if

\int_{0}^{1}\int|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|^{2}\,d\pi(\gamma)\,{\rm d}t<+\infty,\quad({\rm e}_{t})_{\#}\pi\leq C\,{\mathfrak{m}}

for some
C>0
.
The
Sobolev class
S^{2}({\rm X})
is the set of all Borel functions
f:{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
there exists a non-negative
G\in L^{2}({\mathfrak{m}})
such that

(2.2)

\int|f(\gamma_{1})-f(\gamma_{0})|\,{\rm d}\pi(\gamma)\leq\int\int_{0}^{1}G(\gamma_{t})|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,{\rm d}t\,{\rm d}\pi(\gamma)

for all test plans
\pi
.
For
f\in S^{2}({\rm X})
,
a non-negative function
G\in L^{2}({\mathfrak{m}})
satisfying (
2.2
) is called a
weak upper gradient
,
and the minimal one in the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. sense is called the
minimal weak upper gradient
and it is denoted by
|Df|
.
The space

W^{1,2}({\rm X}):=L^{2}({\mathfrak{m}})\cap S^{2}({\rm X})

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{1,2}({\rm X})}^{2}:=\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathfrak{m}})}+\||Df|\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathfrak{m}})}

is called
Sobolev space
and it can be proved that it is a Banach space.

It is well-known that
there exists a unique couple
(L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X}),{\rm d})
,
where
L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X})
is an
L^{2}({\mathfrak{m}})
-normed
L^{\infty}({\mathfrak{m}})
-module and
{\rm d}:S^{2}({\rm X})\to L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X})
is a linear operator such that the following hold (see e.g.,
[
15
]
,
[
18
, Theorem 4.1.1]
):

(1)

L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X})
is generated by
\{{\rm d}f\mid f\in S^{2}({\rm X})\}
;

(2)

for every
f\in S^{2}({\rm X})
, it holds that

|{\rm d}f|=|Df|\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e.}.

The space
L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X})
and the operator
{\rm d}
are called the
cotangent module
and the
differential
,
respectively.
The
tangent module

L^{2}(T{\rm X})
is defined as the dual module of
L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X})
.
For
f\in S^{2}({\rm X})
,
let
\operatorname{Grad}(f)
be the set of all
v\in L^{2}(T{\rm X})
such that

{\rm d}f(v)=|{\rm d}f|^{2}=|v|^{2}\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e.}.

Note that
\operatorname{Grad}(f)
is non-empty (see
[
18
, Remark 4.2.10]
).
The following chain rule holds (see e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 4.2.15]
):

Theorem 2.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
f\in S^{2}({\rm X})
and
v\in\operatorname{Grad}(f)
.
If
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz,
then
\varphi^{\prime}\circ fv\in\operatorname{Grad}(\varphi\circ f)
,
where
\varphi^{\prime}\circ f
is arbitrarily defined on the inverse image of the non-differentiability points of
\varphi
.

A metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is said to be
infinitesimally strictly convex
when for every
f\in S^{2}({\rm X})
the set
\operatorname{Grad}(f)
is a singleton;
in this case,
the unique element is denoted by
\nabla f
.
Also,

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is said to be
infinitesimally Hilbertian
if
W^{1,2}({\rm X})
is a Hilbert space.

We have the following characterization (see e.g.,
[
18
, Theorem 4.3.3]
):

Theorem 2.2

(
[
18
]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian;

(2)

L^{2}(T^{*}{\rm X})
and
L^{2}(T{\rm X})
are Hilbert modules;

(3)

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally strictly convex,
and for all
f,g\in W^{1,2}({\rm X})
,

\nabla(f+g)=\nabla f+\nabla g\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e.};

(4)

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally strictly convex,
and for all
f,g\in W^{1,2}({\rm X})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathfrak{m}})
,

\nabla(fg)=f\nabla g+g\nabla f\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e.}.

In the case where
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian,
we denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the pointwise scalar product on
L^{2}(T{\rm X})
.

2.2.
Doubling, Poincaré and strong rectifiability

Here we review a volume doubling property, a Poincaré inequality and the strongly rectifiability on metric measure spaces.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a metric measure space.
For
r>0
and
x\in{\rm X}
,
we denote by
B_{r}(x)
the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
.
A metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is said to be
locally uniformly doubling
if for every
R>0
there exists
C_{R}>0
such that

{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{2r}(x))\leq C_{R}\,{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))

for all
x\in{\rm X}
and
r\in(0,R)
.
We also say that

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
satisfies a
Poincaré inequality
if for every
R>0
there are
C_{R},\lambda=\lambda_{R}>0
such that

\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-f_{B_{r}(x)}|\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}\leq C_{R}\,r\fint_{B_{\lambda r}(x)}\operatorname{lip}(f)\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}

holds for every Lipschitz function
f:{\rm X}\to\mathbb{R}
and for all
x\in{\rm X}
and
r\in(0,R)
,
where
f_{B_{r}(x)}:=\fint_{B_{r}(x)}f\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}
and
\operatorname{lip}(f)
is the pointwise Lipschitz constant.

We say that
a metric measure space
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is a
d
-dimensional strongly rectifiable space
if
for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an
\varepsilon
-atlas

\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon}:=\{(U^{\varepsilon}_{i},\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{i})\}_{i}
such that the following hold:

(1)

Borel subsets
\{U^{\varepsilon}_{i}\}_{i}
forms a partition of
{\rm X}
up to an
{\mathfrak{m}}
-negligible set;

(2)

\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{i}
is a
(1+\varepsilon)
-bi-Lipschitz map from
U^{\varepsilon}_{i}
to
\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{i}(U^{\varepsilon}_{i})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(3)

for some
c_{i}>0
, we have

c_{i}\,\mathcal{L}^{d}|_{\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{i}(U^{\varepsilon}_{i})}\leq(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{i})_{\#}{\mathfrak{m}}|_{U^{\varepsilon}_{i}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)c_{i}\,\mathcal{L}^{d}|_{\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{i}(U^{\varepsilon}_{i})}.

Remark 2.3
.

For
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,\infty)
,
every
\operatorname{RCD}(K,N)
space is a locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable space satisfying a Poincaré inequality.
Actually,
the doubling property is due to
[
33
]
,
[
38
]
,
[
39
]
,
the Poincaré inequality is due to
[
36
]
.
Moreover,
as stated in
[
22
, Theorem 2.19]
,
the strong rectifiability is a consequence of the results by Brué-Semola
[
6
]
, Gigli-Pasqualetto
[
19
]
, Kell-Mondino
[
28
]
, Mondino-Naber
[
34
]
, De Philippis-Marchese-Rindler
[
9
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a strongly rectifiable space,
and let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence with
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
.
A family
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
of atlases is said to be
aligned
if
for all
n,m
and
(U^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i},\varphi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i})\in\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
,
(U^{\varepsilon_{m}}_{j},\varphi^{\varepsilon_{m}}_{j})\in\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{m}}
,
the map
\varphi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i}-\varphi^{\varepsilon_{m}}_{j}
is
(\varepsilon_{n}+\varepsilon_{m})
-Lipschitz on
U^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i}\cap U^{\varepsilon_{m}}_{j}
.
It has been observed in
[
20
, Theorem 3.9]
,
[
22
, Subsection 2.4]
that
for any sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
,
an aligned family of atlases
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
exists.

2.3.
Approximate metric differentiability

We further review the notion of approximate metric differentiability of maps introduced by Kirchheim
[
29
]
and Gigli-Tyulenev
[
22
]
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a metric measure space,
and let
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be a metric space.
Let
u:{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}
be a Borel map.
The
pointwise Lipschitz constant of
u
at
x\in{\rm X}
is defined as

\operatorname{lip}(u)(x):=\varlimsup_{y\to x}\frac{{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}(x,y)}

if
x
is not an isolated point,
and
\operatorname{lip}(u)(x):=0
otherwise.
For a Borel subset
U\subset{\rm X}
,
a point
x\in U
is said to be a
density point of
U
if

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap U)}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}=1.

The
approximate Lipschitz constant
is defined by

\aplip(u)(x):=\aplims_{y\to x}\frac{{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}(x,y)},

where the right hand side means the approximate upper limit (see e.g.,
[
22
, Subsection 2.1]
for the precise definition).
We have the following (see
[
22
, Proposition 2.5]
):

Proposition 2.5

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a uniformly locally doubling space,
and let
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be a complete metric space.
Let
U
be a Borel subset of
{\rm X}
,
and let
u:U\to{\rm X}
be Lipschitz.
Then for every density point
x\in{\rm X}
of
U
we have

\operatorname{lip}(u)(x)=\aplip(u)(x).

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
d
-dimensional strongly rectifiable space,
and let
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
be an aligned family of atlases for a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
with
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
.
Let
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be a metric space.
We denote by
{\sf sn}^{d}
the set of all semi-norms on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the complete separable metric

{\sf D}({\mathfrak{n}}_{1},{\mathfrak{n}}_{2}):=\sup_{z}|{\mathfrak{n}}_{1}(z)-{\mathfrak{n}}_{2}(z)|,

where the supremum is taken over all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
|z|\leq 1
.
We write
\|{\mathfrak{n}}\|:={\sf D}({\mathfrak{n}},0)
.
We say that
a Borel map
u:{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}
is
approximately metrically differentiable at
x\in{\rm X}
relatively to
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
if the following hold:

(1)

For every
n
, there exists
i=i_{x,n}
such that

x
belongs to
U_{i}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
, it is a density point of
U_{i}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
and
\varphi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i}(x)
is a density point of
\varphi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i}(U^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i})
;

(2)

there exists
{\sf md}_{x}(u)\in{\sf sn}^{d}
, called the
metric differential of
u
at
x
, such that

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\aplims_{y\to x,\,y\in U_{i}^{\varepsilon_{n}}}\frac{\big|{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(y),u(x))-{\sf md}_{x}(u)(\varphi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i}(y)-\varphi^{\varepsilon_{n}}_{i}(x))\big|}{{\sf d}(y,x)}=0.

Moreover,
Gigli-Tyulenev
[
22
]
have shown the following (see
[
22
, Lemma 3.4]
):

Lemma 2.6

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a strongly rectifiable space,
and let
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
be an aligned family of atlases for a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n}
with
\varepsilon_{n}\downarrow 0
.
Let
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be a complete metric space.
If
u:{\rm X}\to{\rm Y}
is approximately metrically differentiable at
x\in{\rm X}
relatively to
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
,
then

\aplip(u)(x)=\|{\sf md}_{x}(u)\|.

Gigli-Tyulenev
[
22
]
have further concluded the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. approximately metrical differentiability of Borel maps satisfying the Lusin-Lipschitz property (see
[
22
, Proposition 3.6]
).

2.4.
Korevaar-Schoen space

In this section
we recall the formulation and basic results concerning the Korevaar-Schoen type energy introduced in Gigli-Tyulenev
[
22
]
.
Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a metric measure space,
and let
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\rm X}
.
Let
{\rm Y}_{\rm o}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed complete metric space.

We denote by
L^{0}(\Omega,{\rm Y})
the set of all Borel maps (up to
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. equality) from
\Omega
to
{\rm Y}
with separable range.
Let
L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{\rm o})
be the set of all
u\in L^{0}(\Omega,{\rm Y})
such that

\int_{\Omega}{\sf d}_{{\rm Y}}^{2}(u(x),{\rm o})\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)<+\infty,

which is endowed with the metric

{\sf d}_{L^{2}}(u,v):=\left|\int_{\Omega}{\sf d}_{\rm Y}^{2}\big(u(x),v(x)\big)\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(x)\right|^{1/2}.

Since
{\rm Y}
is complete,
it can be proved that so is
L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}})
.

Let
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}})
.
For
r>0
and
x\in\Omega
,
the
approximate energy
is defined by

{\sf ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left|\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\right|^{1/2}}&\textrm{if}\ B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
Korevaar-Schoen space
{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{\rm o})
is defined by the set of all
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{\rm o})
such that

(2.3)

{\sf E}_{2}^{\Omega}(u):=\varliminf_{r\downarrow 0}\,\int_{\Omega}\,{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega]\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}<+\infty.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
denote the Sobolev space provided by means of
[
22
, Definition 5.2]
.
One can also introduce the associated Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}})
as the set of all
u\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}})
such that
there is
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
such that for all
1
-Lipschitz functions
f:{\rm Y}\to\mathbb{R}
we have
f\circ u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
and
|D(f\circ u)|\leq G

{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. on
\Omega
(see
[
22
, Definition 5.3]
).

3.1.
Technical lemma

This subsection is devoted to the proof of a technical lemma.
Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a metric measure space,
and let
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\rm X}
.
Let
{\rm Y}_{\rm o}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed complete metric space.
Let
u,v,w\in L^{2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}})
.
For
\alpha>0
and
x\in\Omega
,
we define the following (Borel) sets

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Omega_{v,\alpha,x}

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\Omega\mid{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))\geq\alpha\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{w,\alpha,x}

\displaystyle:=\{y\in\Omega\mid{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(w(x),w(y))\geq\alpha\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{v,w,\alpha,x}

\displaystyle:=\Omega_{v,\alpha,x}\cup\Omega_{w,\alpha,x}.

We also define a
modified approximate energy
by

(3.2)

{\sf ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega;v,w,\alpha](x):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left|\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega_{v,w,\alpha,x}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\right|^{1/2}}&\textrm{if}\ B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega,\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We now state and prove our key lemma (cf.
[
37
, Lemma 1.12]
,
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
):

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable space satisfying a Poincaré inequality,
and let
\Omega
be an open subset.
Let
{\rm Y}_{\rm o}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed complete metric space.
Let
u,v,w\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,{\rm Y}_{\rm o})
and
\alpha>0
fixed.
Then we have

(3.3)

{\sf ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega;v,w,\alpha]\to{\rm e}_{2}[u]\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$ and in $L^{2}(\Omega)$ as $r\downarrow 0$}.

Proof.

We first prove the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. convergence.
Note that
for
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
the following hold:

(1)

{\sf ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[u](x),{\sf ks}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[v](x),{\sf ks}_{2,r}[w,\Omega](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[w](x)
as
r\downarrow 0
;

(2)

\operatorname{lip}u(x)<+\infty
.

Indeed,
the first property is a consequence of Theorem
2.8
.
Concerning the second one,
Theorem
2.8
implies that

u
is approximately metrically differentiable
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
relatively to an aligned family
\{\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n}
of atlases.
For such an
x\in\Omega
,
and for each
n
, there is
i=i_{x,n}
such that

x
belongs to a chart
U_{i}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
of
\mathcal{A}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
,
and it is a density point of
U_{i}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
.
Thanks to
[
22
, (2.15), Corollary 3.10]
,
we may assume that
u|_{U_{i}^{\varepsilon_{n}}}
is Lipschitz.
By Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
2.6
,

\operatorname{lip}u(x)
coincides with
\|{\sf md}_{x}(u)\|
,
which is finite.

Let
x\in\Omega
be such that two properties above hold:
we shall prove (
3.3
) for such an
x
.
Since

{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega;v,w,\alpha](x)={\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)-\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,w,\alpha,x}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y),

it suffices to show that

(3.4)

\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,w,\alpha,x}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\to 0

as
r\downarrow 0
.
We have

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,w,\alpha,x}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,w,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{{\rm Y}}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}+\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{w,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\right)\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{{\rm Y}}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\left({\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x)+{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[w,\Omega](x)\right)\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{{\rm Y}}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}^{2}(x,y)},

where the last inequality follows from

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,\alpha,x}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\leq{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x),

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{w,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{w,\alpha,x}}\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(w(x),w(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\leq{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[w,\Omega](x).

Therefore,
by letting
r\downarrow 0
we obtain (
3.4
) and the sought
{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. convergence.
The
L^{2}
-convergence follows from
{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega;v,w,\alpha]\leq{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega]
and
[
22
, Lemma 3.14]
.

\Box

3.2.
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
spaces

In this subsection we shall review some basic properties of
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
spaces.
We refer to
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
.
For
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
,
let
M_{\kappa}
be the
2
-dimensional space form of constant curvature
\kappa
.
Let
D_{\kappa}
be the diameter of
M_{\kappa}
;
more precisely,

D_{\kappa}:=\begin{cases}\pi/\sqrt{\kappa}&\textrm{if}\ \kappa>0,\\
+\infty&\textrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be a metric space.
A curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\rm Y}
is said to be a
geodesic
if

{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{s})=|s-t|{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})

for all
t,s\in[0,1]
.
Further,

({\rm Y},{\sf d})
is called a
geodesic space
if for any pair of points
x,y\in{\rm Y}
,
there is a geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to{\rm Y}
with
\gamma_{0}=x
and
\gamma_{1}=y
.
For
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
,
a complete geodesic space
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
is said to be a
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
-space
if it satisfies the following
\kappa
-triangle comparison principle
:
for every geodesic triangle
\triangle_{pqr}
in
{\rm Y}
whose perimeter is less than
2D_{\kappa}
,
and for every point
x
on the segment between
q
and
r
,
it holds that

{\sf d}_{{\rm Y}}(p,x)\leq{\sf d}_{\kappa}(\bar{p},\bar{x}),

where
{\sf d}_{\kappa}
is the Riemannian distance on
M_{\kappa}
,
and
\bar{p}
and
\bar{x}
are comparison points in
M_{\kappa}
.

Let us recall the following basic properties (see e.g.,
[
5
, Proposition 1.4]
):

Proposition 3.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

For
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
,
let
({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y})
be a
\operatorname{CAT}(\kappa)
space.
Then we have:

(1)

For each pair of points
x,y\in{\rm Y}
with
{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(x,y)<D_{\kappa}
,
there exists a unique geodesic from
x
to
y
;
moreover,
it varies continuously with its end points;

(2)

any ball in
{\rm Y}
whose radius is less than
D_{\kappa}/2
is convex;
namely,
for each pair of points in such a ball,
the unique geodesic joining them is contained the ball.

Remark 3.3
.

In
[
5
]
,
Proposition
3.2
has been deduced from the following:
let
\gamma,\eta:[0,1]\to{\rm Y}
be geodesics such that

\gamma_{0}=\eta_{0},\quad{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{1},\eta_{1})+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\eta_{1},\eta_{0})<2D_{\kappa}.

For
l\in(0,D_{\kappa})
,
we assume
{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\leq l
and
{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})\leq l
.
Then there is a constant
c_{\kappa,l}>0
depending only on
\kappa,l
such that
for all
t\in[0,1]
,

(3.5)

{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{t},\eta_{t})\leq c_{\kappa,l}\,{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{1},\eta_{1}).

We now collect some useful estimates on
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
spaces,
which have been stated in
[

]
without proof.
The detailed proofs can be found in
[

, Appendix A]
.
The first one is the following (see

Lemma 3.4

(
[

]
)
.

Let

be a
\operatorname{CAT}(1)

Then we have

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})}{2}

\displaystyle\,{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{1/2},\eta_{1/2})+\frac{1}{4}({\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})-{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\eta_{0})+{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{1},\eta_{1})\right)

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Cub}({\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\eta_{0}),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{1},\eta_{1}),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})-{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{1/2},\eta_{1/2})),

Lemma 3.5

(

]
)
.

\displaystyle-2(1-t)(s-t){\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})({\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})-{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}))

3.3.

In this subsection
we are going to obtain some energy estimates which will be exploited in the proof of our main theorem.
Let
({\rm X},{\rm d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be a locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable space satisfying a Poincaré inequality,
and let
\Omega
be an open subset of
X
.
Let
{\rm Y}_{\rm o}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space.
For
\rho\in(0,\pi/2)
,
we denote by
\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})
the closed ball in
{\rm Y}
of radius
\rho
centered at
{\rm o}
.
In view of Proposition
3.2
,

\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})=(\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}),{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
is a pointed
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space with itself.
We call
\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})
a
regular ball
.

For
u,v\in L^{0}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
and

,
we define a map
{\sf G}^{u,v}_{t}:\Omega\to\bar{{\rm B}}_{r}({\rm o})
by
x\mapsto{\sf G}^{u(x),v(x)}_{t}
,
where
{\sf G}^{u(x),v(x)}
denotes the unique geodesic from
u(x)
to
v(x)
.
By virtue of Proposition
3.2
,

{\sf G}^{u,v}_{t}
belongs to
L^{0}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
.
By the same argument as in
[
22
, Section 6]
,
we see that if
u,v\in L^{2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
, then
{\sf G}^{u,v}_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
,
and it is the unique geodesic from
u
to

.

We begin with the following energy density estimate (cf.
[
37
, Lemma 1.13]
):

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
u,v\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})),m:={\sf G}^{u,v}_{1/2}
and
{\sf d}:={\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u,v)
.
Then we have
m\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})),{\sf d}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})

and

(3.6)

\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m]+\frac{1}{4}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u]+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[v]\right)\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$}.

Proof.

We check
m\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})),{\sf d}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.
The claim for
{\sf d}
immediately follows from the triangle inequality.
For what concerns

let us set
\alpha:=2(\pi-2\rho)>0
.
For
x\in\Omega
,
we define
\Omega_{u,\alpha,x},\Omega_{v,\alpha,x},\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x}
as in (
3.1
).
For all
y\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x}
,
we have

\displaystyle{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),v(x))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(x),u(y))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(y),u(x))<4\rho+\alpha=2\pi,

\displaystyle{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(y),u(y))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(y),v(x))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))<4\rho+\alpha=2\pi.

Therefore,
by using (
3.5
) for
l=2\rho
twice via the midpoint of
u(y)
and
v(x)
,
for all
y\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x}
,

{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))\leq c_{\rho}({\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))

for some constant
c_{\rho}>0
depending only on
\rho
.
This implies

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[m,\Omega](x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c^{2}_{\rho}}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x}}\,\left(\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{r^{2}}+\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))}{r^{2}}\right)\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x}}\,\frac{{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{2}_{\rho}\,\left({\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)+{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x)\right)+\frac{4\rho^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2c^{2}_{\rho}+\frac{4\rho^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\,\left({\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)+{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x)\right),

where we used

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{u,v,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{u,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}+\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x)\cap\Omega_{v,\alpha,x})}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)+{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x).

Integrating (
3.7
) over
\Omega
,
and letting
r\downarrow 0
,
we deduce
m\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
.

We prove (
3.6
).
Now let us set
\beta:=\pi-2\rho>0
and for
x\in\Omega
define
\Omega_{u,\beta,x},\Omega_{v,\beta,x},\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}
as in (
3.1
) again.
For all
y\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}
,
there holds

{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),v(x))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(y),u(y))+{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(y),u(x))<2\beta+4\rho=2\pi.

Lemma
3.4
tells us that for all
y\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}
,

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}(x)}{2}

\displaystyle\,{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))+\frac{1}{4}({\sf d}(x)-{\sf d}(y))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))+{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))\right)

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Cub}({\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y)),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y)),{\sf d}(x)-{\sf d}(y),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))).

We shall write the last term as
\operatorname{Cub}(x,y)
for short.
It follows that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}(x)}{2}

\displaystyle\,{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[m,\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)+\frac{1}{4}{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[{\sf d},\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)+{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x)\right)+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}}\,\frac{\operatorname{Cub}(x,y)}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y),

where
{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[m,\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)
and
{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[{\sf d},\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)
are defined as (
3.2
).

By Theorem
2.8
and Lemma
3.1
,
and by the same argument in the proof of Lemma
3.1
,

{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

{\sf ks}_{2,r}[m,\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[m](x),{\sf ks}_{2,r}[{\sf d},\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[{\sf d}](x)
as
r\downarrow 0
;

(2)

{\sf ks}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[u](x),{\sf ks}_{2,r}[v,\Omega](x)\to{\rm e}_{2}[v](x)
as
r\downarrow 0
;

(3)

\operatorname{lip}u(x),\operatorname{lip}v(x),\operatorname{lip}{\sf d}(x),\operatorname{lip}m(x)<+\infty
.

We now fix such a point
x\in\Omega
.
Let us verify

(3.9)

\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}}\,\frac{\operatorname{Cub}(x,y)}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\to 0

as
r\downarrow 0
.
Each term in (
3.9
) can be written as

(3.10)

\frac{f(x)}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}}\,\frac{{\sf d}^{\theta_{1}}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y)){\sf d}^{\theta_{2}}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))({\sf d}(x)-{\sf d}(y))^{\theta_{3}}{\sf d}^{\theta_{4}}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)

for
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{4}\geq 0
such that
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}+\theta_{4}\geq 3
and at least one of
i=1,\dots,4
has
\theta_{i}\geq 2
(see Remark
3.6
).
Let us assume
\theta_{3}\geq 2
since the other cases can be handled in a similar way.
For the absolute value of (
3.10
) we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{|f(x)|}{{\mathfrak{m}}(B_{r}(x))}\int_{B_{r}(x)\setminus\Omega_{u,v,\beta,x}}\,\frac{{\sf d}^{\theta_{1}}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y)){\sf d}^{\theta_{2}}_{\rm Y}(v(x),v(y))|{\sf d}(x)-{\sf d}(y)|^{\theta_{3}}{\sf d}^{\theta_{4}}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)

\displaystyle\leq|f(x)|\,r^{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}+\theta_{4}-2}\,{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[{\sf d},\Omega;u,v,\beta](x)\,I,

where

I:=\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}}\left(\frac{{\sf d}^{\theta_{1}}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}^{\theta_{1}}(x,y)}\frac{{\sf d}^{\theta_{2}}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))}{{\sf d}^{\theta_{2}}(x,y)}\frac{|{\sf d}(x)-{\sf d}(y)|^{\theta_{3}-2}}{{\sf d}^{\theta_{3}-2}(x,y)}\frac{{\sf d}^{\theta_{4}}_{\rm Y}(m(x),m(y))}{{\sf d}^{\theta_{4}}(x,y)}\right).

By
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}+\theta_{4}-2\geq 1
and by the choice of
x
,
the right hand side of (
3.11
) goes to
0
as
r\downarrow 0
.
Thus,
we confirm the validity of (
3.9
).
Letting
r\downarrow 0
in (
3.8
),
we can derive the desired assertion.

\Box

We next prove the following (cf.
[
37
, Lemma 1.14]
):

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume that

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian.
For
u\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})),\eta\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,1])
,
we define
u_{\eta}:\Omega\to\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})
by
u_{\eta}:={\sf G}^{u,{\rm o}}_{\eta}
and
{\sf d}:={\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u,{\rm o})
.
Then we have
u_{\eta}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})),{\sf d}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
and

{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u_{\eta}]\leq\frac{\sin^{2}(1-\eta){\sf d}}{\sin^{2}{\sf d}}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u]-{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]\right)+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[(1-\eta){\sf d}]\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$}.

Proof.

Let us verify
u_{\eta}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})),{\sf d}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.
Similarly to the proof of Lemma
3.7
,
the thesis for
{\sf d}
follows from the triangle inequality.
By virtue of (
3.5
) for
l=\rho
,
for all
x,y\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u_{\eta}(x),u_{\eta}(y))

\displaystyle\leq c_{\rho}\,{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))+|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(y),{\rm o})

\displaystyle\leq c_{\rho}\,{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y))+\rho|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|

for some constant
c_{\rho}>0
depending only on
\rho
.
Therefore,

{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u_{\eta},\Omega](x)\leq 2c^{2}_{\rho}\,{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)+2\rho^{2}{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[\eta,\Omega](x).

Integrating this estimate over
\Omega
,
and letting
r\downarrow 0
,
we conclude
u_{\eta}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
.

Using Lemma
3.5
,
for all
x,y\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u_{\eta}(x),u_{\eta}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sin^{2}(1-\eta(x)){\sf d}(x)}{\sin^{2}{\sf d}(x)}\left({\sf d}^{2}_{{\rm Y}}(u(x),u(y))-({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+(1-\eta(x))^{2}({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))^{2}+{\sf d}^{2}(x)(\eta(y)-\eta(x))^{2}

\displaystyle-2(1-\eta(x)){\sf d}(x)(\eta(y)-\eta(x))({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Cub}(\eta(y)-\eta(x),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y)),{\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u_{\eta}(x),u_{\eta}(y))).

Setting
\xi:=1-\eta
,
we also have

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\sf d}^{2}_{\rm Y}(u_{\eta}(x),u_{\eta}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sin^{2}\xi(x){\sf d}(x)}{\sin^{2}{\sf d}(x)}\left({\sf d}^{2}_{{\rm Y}}(u(x),u(y))-({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\xi^{2}(x)({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))^{2}+{\sf d}^{2}(x)(\xi(y)-\xi(x))^{2}

\displaystyle+2\xi(x){\sf d}(x)(\xi(y)-\xi(x))({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Cub}(\xi(x)-\xi(y),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y)),{\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u_{\eta}(x),u_{\eta}(y)))

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin^{2}\xi(x){\sf d}(x)}{\sin^{2}{\sf d}(x)}\left({\sf d}^{2}_{Y}(u(x),u(y))-({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\xi^{2}(x)({\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x))^{2}+{\sf d}^{2}(x)(\xi(y)-\xi(x))^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(x){\sf d}(x)}{2}\left[\{(\xi+{\sf d})(x)-(\xi+{\sf d})(y)\}^{2}-\{(\xi-{\sf d})(x)-(\xi-{\sf d})(y)\}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\operatorname{Cub}(\xi(x)-\xi(y),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u(x),u(y)),{\sf d}(y)-{\sf d}(x),{\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u_{\eta}(x),u_{\eta}(y))).

Dividing the later expression by
r^{2}

and integrating in
y
over
B_{r}(x)
lead to

\displaystyle{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u_{\eta},\Omega](x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sin^{2}\xi(x){\sf d}(x)}{\sin^{2}{\sf d}(x)}\left({\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[u,\Omega](x)-{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[{\sf d},\Omega](x)\right)

\displaystyle+\xi^{2}(x){\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[{\sf d},\Omega](x)+{\sf d}^{2}(x){\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[\xi,\Omega](x)

\displaystyle+\frac{\xi(x){\sf d}(x)}{2}\left({\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[\xi+{\sf d},\Omega](x)-{\sf ks}^{2}_{2,r}[\xi-{\sf d},\Omega](x)\right)+\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\,\frac{\operatorname{Cub}(x,y)}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y),

where
\operatorname{Cub}(x,y)
denotes the cubic terms in (
3.12
).
In the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
3.7
,
we see

\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\,\frac{\operatorname{Cub}(x,y)}{r^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}(y)\to 0\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. $x\in\Omega$}

as
r\downarrow 0
.
By virtue of Theorem
2.8
,
we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u_{\eta}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sin^{2}\xi{\sf d}}{\sin^{2}{\sf d}}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u]-{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]\right)+\xi^{2}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]+{\sf d}^{2}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi]+\frac{\xi{\sf d}}{2}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi+{\sf d}]-{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi-{\sf d}]\right)\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$}.

Now,
it suffices to prove that

\xi^{2}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]+{\sf d}^{2}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi]+\frac{\xi{\sf d}}{2}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi+{\sf d}]-{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi-{\sf d}]\right)={\rm e}^{2}_{2}[\xi{\sf d}]\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$}.

Thanks to Proposition
2.9
,
for any
u\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
,
we have
|Du|=c_{d}\,{\rm e}_{2}[u]

{\mathfrak{m}}
-a.e. on
\Omega
,
and hence it is enough to show that

(3.13)

\xi^{2}|D{\sf d}|^{2}+{\sf d}^{2}|D\xi|^{2}+\frac{\xi{\sf d}}{2}\left(|D(\xi+{\sf d})|^{2}-|D(\xi-{\sf d})|^{2}\right)=|D(\xi{\sf d})|^{2}\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$}.

Since
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian,
Theorem
2.2
tells us that

(3.14)

|D(\xi{\sf d})|^{2}=\langle\nabla(\xi{\sf d}),\nabla(\xi{\sf d})\rangle=\xi^{2}|D{\sf d}|^{2}+{\sf d}^{2}|\nabla\xi|^{2}+2\xi{\sf d}\langle\nabla\xi,\nabla{\sf d}\rangle\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$},

where we used the Leibniz rule.
Furthermore,
by the linearity of the gradient,

(3.15)

|D(\xi+{\sf d})|^{2}-|D(\xi-{\sf d})|^{2}=\langle\nabla(\xi+{\sf d}),\nabla(\xi+{\sf d})\rangle-\langle\nabla(\xi-{\sf d}),\nabla(\xi-{\sf d})\rangle=4\langle\nabla\xi,\nabla{\sf d}\rangle\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$}.

Combining (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) yields

|D(\xi{\sf d})|^{2}=\xi^{2}|D{\sf d}|^{2}+{\sf d}^{2}|D\xi|^{2}+\frac{\xi{\sf d}}{2}\left(|D(\xi+{\sf d})|^{2}-|D(\xi-{\sf d})|^{2}\right)\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$},

and this is nothing but (
3.13
),
thus concluding the proof.

\Box

We now prove that the energy
{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2}
satisfies a certain kind of convexity (cf.
[
22
, Proposition 1.15]
):

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume that

({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian.
For
u,v\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
,
we set
m:={\sf G}^{u,v}_{1/2}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
and
{\sf d}:={\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u,v),{\mathfrak{d}}:={\sf d}_{{\rm Y}}(m,{\rm o})\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\eta\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,1])
be a function determined by solving

(3.16)

\frac{\sin(1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}}}{\sin{\mathfrak{d}}}=\cos\frac{{\sf d}}{2},

and define
m_{\eta}:={\sf G}^{m,{\rm o}}_{\eta}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
.
Then we have

{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2}(m_{\eta})+\cos^{8}\rho\,{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2}\left(\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2}(u)+\frac{1}{2}{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2}(v).

Proof.

Combining Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
,
we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m_{\eta}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sin^{2}(1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}}}{\sin^{2}{\mathfrak{d}}}({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m]-{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\mathfrak{d}}])+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[(1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}}]

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m]-{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\mathfrak{d}}])+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[(1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u]+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[v]\right)-\frac{1}{4}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]-\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\mathfrak{d}}]+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[(1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}}]\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$.}

We now observe that the following holds:

(3.18)

{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[(1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}}]+\frac{\cos^{4}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\cos^{4}{\mathfrak{d}}}{1-\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\sin^{2}{\mathfrak{d}}}\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}\left[\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}}\right]=\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\mathfrak{d}}]+\frac{1}{4}{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[{\sf d}]\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$.}

In view of Proposition
2.9
,
this is equivalent to the following:

|D((1-\eta){\mathfrak{d}})|^{2}+\frac{\cos^{4}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\cos^{4}{\mathfrak{d}}}{1-\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\sin^{2}{\mathfrak{d}}}\,\left|D\left(\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}}\right)\right|^{2}=\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,|D{\mathfrak{d}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}|D{\sf d}|^{2}\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$.}

Since
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian,
it can be derived from Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
(chain rule, Leibniz rule), the definition of
\eta
, and a straightforward calculation.
Combining (
3.17
) and (
3.18
),
we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m_{\eta}]+\cos^{8}\rho\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}\left[\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m_{\eta}]+\cos^{4}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\cos^{4}{\mathfrak{d}}\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}\left[\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[m_{\eta}]+\frac{\cos^{4}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\cos^{4}{\mathfrak{d}}}{1-\cos^{2}\frac{{\sf d}}{2}\,\sin^{2}{\mathfrak{d}}}\,{\rm e}^{2}_{2}\left[\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm e}^{2}_{2}[u]+{\rm e}^{2}_{2}[v]\right)\quad\text{${\mathfrak{m}}$-a.e. on $\Omega$.}

By integrating this inequality over
\Omega
,
we obtain the desired one.

\Box

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
({\rm X},{\sf d},{\mathfrak{m}})
be an infinitesimally Hilbertian, locally uniformly doubling, strongly rectifiable space satisfying a Poincaré inequality,
and let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
{\rm X}
with
{\mathfrak{m}}({\rm X}\setminus\Omega)>0
.
Let
{\rm Y}_{{\rm o}}=({\rm Y},{\sf d}_{\rm Y},{\rm o})
be a pointed
\operatorname{CAT}(1)
space,
and let
\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o})
be a regular ball.
Let
(u_{n})_{n}\subset{\sf KS}^{1,2}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
stand for a minimizing sequence of
{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}
.
By Proposition
2.10
,
the functional
{\sf E}_{2,\bar{u}}^{\Omega}
is lower semicontinuous,
and hence
it is sufficient to show that
(u_{n})_{n}
is an
L^{2}(\Omega,\bar{{\rm B}}_{\rho}({\rm o}))
-Cauchy sequence.

Set
I:=\lim_{n}{\sf E}_{2,\bar{u}}^{\Omega}(u_{n})=\inf{\sf E}_{2,\bar{u}}^{\Omega}
.
We also set

m_{n,m}:={\sf G}^{u_{n},u_{m}}_{1/2},\quad{\sf d}_{n,m}:={\sf d}_{\rm Y}(u_{n},u_{m}),\quad{\mathfrak{d}}_{n,m}:={\sf d}_{\rm Y}(m_{n,m},{\rm o}),\quad m_{n,m,\eta}:={\sf G}^{m_{n,m},{\rm o}}_{\eta_{n,m}},

where
\eta_{n,m}\in{\sf KS}^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,1])
is defined as (
3.16
).
Proposition
3.9
yields

\displaystyle\cos^{8}\rho\,{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}\left(\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}_{n,m}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}_{n,m}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}(u_{n})+\frac{1}{2}{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}(u_{m})-{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}(m_{n,m,\eta})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}(u_{n})+\frac{1}{2}{\sf E}^{\Omega}_{2,\bar{u}}(u_{m})-I;

in particular,

\varlimsup_{n,m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|D\left(\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}_{n,m}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}_{n,m}}\right)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}=0.

Furthermore,
a Poincaré inequality under Dirichlet boundary conditions (see e.g.,
[
22
, Lemma 6.3]
,
[
2
, Subsection 5.5]
) together with Proposition
2.10
leads us to

\lim_{n,m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{\tan\frac{{\sf d}_{n,m}}{2}}{\cos{\mathfrak{d}}_{n,m}}\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}=0.

Thus,

\lim_{n,m\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|{\sf d}_{n,m}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{\mathfrak{m}}=0
since
|{\sf d}_{n,m}|^{2}\leq 4|\tan({\sf d}_{n,m}/2)|^{2}
and
|\cos{\mathfrak{d}}_{n,m}|^{2}\leq 1
over
\Omega
.
This concludes the proof.

\Box

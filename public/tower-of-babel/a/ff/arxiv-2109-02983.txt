A two-component nonlinear variational wave system
Peder Aursand, Anders Nordli

Abstract
. We derive a novel two-component generalization of the nonlinear variational wave equation as a model for the director field of a nematic liquid crystal with a variable order parameter. The two-component nonlinear variational wave equation admits solutions locally in time. We show that a particular long time asymptotic expansion around a constant state in a moving frame satisfy the two-component Hunter–Saxton system.



1.
Introduction

The nonlinear variational wave equation
\psi_{tt}-c(\psi)(c(\psi)\psi_{x})_{x}=0
was derived by Saxton
[
24
]
as a model of the director field of a nematic liquid crystal. The nonlinear variational wave equation has recieved wide attention
[
2
,
5
,
11
,
13
]
due to mathematical challenges in the form of wavebreaking in finite time, and distinct ways to extend the solution to a global weak solution. In this context wavebreaking means that either the time or the space derivative becomes unbounded at certain points, while the solution remains Hölder continuous.

Furthermore, Hunter and Saxton derived the equation
u_{tx}+(uu_{x})_{x}=\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}
from the nonlinear variational wave equation
[
14
]
as an asymptotic equation for small perturbations in the long time regime in a moving frame. The Hunter–Saxton equation share many of the features of the nonlinear variational wave equation such as wavebreaking, conservative and dissipative weak solutions, and Hölder continuity
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
16
,
17
,
26
,
27
]
. In addition it exhibited novel features such as complete integrability and interpretation as a geodesic flow
[
15
,
20
]
. It also proved easier to work with due to there being only one family of characteristics and existence of explicit solutions.

A two-component generalization for the Hunter–Saxton equation was derived from two-component Camassa–Holm
[
8
]
, and independently form the Gurevich–Zybin system
[
23
]
. The two-component generalization is similar to the Hunter–Saxton equation since wavebreaking is also possible, there are both conservative and dissipative weak solutions, and
u
is Hölder continuous
[
12
,
22
,
25
]
. However when the second variable is nonzero almost everywhere initially, there will be no wave breaking
[
22
]
, and in that sense the introduction of a second variable regularizes the equation. The two-component Hunter–Saxton system has, however, not been shown to be related to the theory of nematic liquid crystals, and one of the aims of this paper is to establish that the two-component Hunter–Saxton system can indeed be derived from the theory of nematic liquid crystals, and that the new variable is related to the order parameter.

In the Ericksen–Leslie theory of nematic liquid crystals the configuration is described by a director field
\mathbf{n}
which gives the local orientation of the rods, and an order parameter field
s
which gives the local degree of orientation
[
10
,
21
]
. When the nonlinear variational wave equation was first derived the local degree of orientation was assumed constant, so that a scalar equation was obtained
[
24
]
. Here we will follow a similar route, but account for variable degree of orientation. Thus, in Section
2
, we will arrive at a novel two-component system of nonlinear wave equations. Moreover, we show that a similar asymptotic expansion to the one by Hunter and Saxton
[
14
]
yields the two-component Hunter–Saxton equation. In Section
3
existence of global solutions is shown in the case of constant wave speed, and local solutions is shown to exist in general. The proofs rely on fixed point iterations and standard semigroup theory for evolution equations
[
18
]
.

2.
Derivation of the equations

In Ericksen–Leslie theory a nematic liquid crystal in a domain
\Omega
is desribed by a director field
\mathbf{n}:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2}
and an order parameter
s:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow(-\frac{1}{2},1)

[
10
,
21
]
. The Lagrangian of the system is then
[
10
]
given by

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}=-(s\mathbf{n})_{t}^{2}+W_{2}(s,\nabla s,\mathbf{n},\nabla\mathbf{n})+W_{0}(s),

with the potential energy term
W_{2}

\displaystyle W_{2}(s,\nabla s,\mathbf{n},\nabla\mathbf{n})

\displaystyle=\left(K_{1}+L_{1}s\right)s^{2}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}\right)^{2}+\left(K_{2}+L_{2}s\right)s^{2}\left(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(K_{3}+L_{3}s\right)s^{2}\left|\mathbf{n}\times\nabla\times\mathbf{n}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\left((K_{2}+K_{4})+(L_{2}+L_{4})s\right)s^{2}\left[\mathrm{tr}\>\nabla\mathbf{n}^{2}-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\kappa_{1}+\lambda_{1}s\right)\left|\nabla s\right|^{2}+\left(\kappa_{2}+\lambda_{2}s\right)\left(\nabla s\cdot\mathbf{n}\right)^{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\kappa_{3}+\lambda_{3}s\right)\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}\right)\left(\nabla s\cdot\mathbf{n}\right)+\left(\kappa_{4}+\lambda_{4}s\right)\nabla s\cdot\left((\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{n}\right),

and some function
W_{0}
satisfying

(2.3)

\lim_{s\downarrow-\frac{1}{2}}W_{0}(s)=+\infty,\quad\lim_{s\uparrow 1}W_{0}(s)=+\infty,\text{ and }\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{W_{0}(s)}{s^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}.

We are interested in the case where
\mathbf{n}=(\cos\psi,\sin\psi,0)
, and
s
and
\psi
depends on
t
and
x
only. Then the expression for
W_{2}
can be written

\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle=s^{2}\psi_{x}^{2}\left((K_{1}+L_{1}s)\sin^{2}\psi+(K_{3}+L_{3}s)\cos^{2}\psi\right)

\displaystyle\quad+s_{x}^{2}\left((\kappa_{1}+\lambda_{1}s)\sin^{2}\psi+(\tilde{\kappa}_{2}+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}s)\cos^{2}\psi\right)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\quad-s_{x}\psi_{x}\sin 2\psi\left(\frac{\kappa_{3}+\kappa_{4}}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}}{2}s\right),

where
\tilde{\kappa}_{2}=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{2}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
. We simplify the problem by considering the case where

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{4}=\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{4}=0,

(2.5b)

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=L_{3}=\lambda_{1}=\tilde{\lambda}_{2}=0,

(2.5c)

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1},

(2.5d)

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\kappa}_{2}.

Then with the definition
c(\psi)^{2}=K_{1}\sin^{2}\psi+K_{3}\cos^{2}\psi
, we get the Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{2NVW}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}s_{t}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}s^{2}\psi_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}s^{2}c(\psi)^{2}\psi_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}c(\psi)^{2}s_{x}^{2}+W_{0}(s)

(2.6)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(s\mathbf{n})_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}c(\mathbf{n})^{2}(s\mathbf{n})_{x}^{2}+W_{0}(s).

Definition 2.1
.

We define the two-component nonlinear variational wave system to be

(2.7a)

\displaystyle s^{2}\left(\psi_{tt}-c(\psi)\left(c(\psi)\psi_{x}\right)_{x}\right)+2s\left(\psi_{t}s_{t}-c(\psi)^{2}\psi_{x}s_{x}\right)+c(\psi)c^{\prime}(\psi)s_{x}^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.7b)

\displaystyle s_{tt}-c(\psi)\left(c(\psi)s_{x}\right)_{x}-c(\psi)c^{\prime}(\psi)\psi_{x}s_{x}-s\left(\psi_{t}^{2}-c(\psi)^{2}\psi_{x}^{2}\right)+W_{0}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=0,

(2.7c)

\displaystyle c(\psi)^{2}-K_{1}\sin^{2}\psi-K_{3}\cos^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=0,

(2.7d)

\displaystyle W_{0}

\displaystyle\in C^{4}\left(\left(-\frac{1}{2},1\right)\right).

Remark 2.2
.

The system (
2.7
) is the Euler–Lagrange equations for (
2
).

One can define a conserved energy for classical solutions.

Proposition 2.3
.

Define the energy density

(2.8)

\mathcal{E}=\frac{1}{2}\left(s^{2}(\psi_{t}^{2}+c(\psi)^{2}\psi_{x}^{2})+(s_{t}^{2}+c(\psi)^{2}s_{x}^{2})\right)+W_{0}(s).

and the energy density flux

(2.9)

\mathcal{F}=\left(s^{2}\psi_{t}\psi_{x}+s_{t}s_{x}\right).

Then for classical solutions of (
2.7
) the energy satisfy the equations

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{t}-\left(c(\psi)^{2}\mathcal{F}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.10b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}-\left(\mathcal{E}-2W_{0}(s)\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=0.

We will now derive the two-component Hunter–Saxton system from (
2
). To follow the work of Hunter and Saxton
[
14
]
we introduce
\psi(t,x)=\psi_{0}+\varepsilon u(\varepsilon t,x-c_{0}t)
and
s=s_{0}+\varepsilon r(\varepsilon t,x-c_{0}t)
with
c_{0}^{2}=K_{1}\sin^{2}\psi_{0}+K_{3}\cos^{2}\psi_{0}
. Then expansion of (
2
) in powers of
\varepsilon
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=W_{0}(s_{0})+\varepsilon W_{0}^{\prime}(s_{0})r+\varepsilon^{2}\frac{1}{2}W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})r^{2}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{3}\bigg(c_{0}r_{t}r_{x}+s_{0}^{2}c_{0}u_{t}u_{x}+s_{0}^{2}\left(cc^{\prime}\right)_{0}uu_{x}^{2}+\left(cc^{\prime}\right)_{0}ur_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}W_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})r^{3}\bigg)+O(\varepsilon^{4}).

If we select
s_{0}
such that
W_{0}^{\prime}(s_{0})=W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})=W^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})=0
, the third order terms in the above Lagrangian gives (possibly by rescaling)

(2.12)

\mathcal{L}^{2HS}=u_{t}u_{x}+uu_{x}^{2}+r_{t}r_{x}+ur_{x}^{2}.

Theorem 2.4
.

The two-component Hunter–Saxton system

(2.13a)

\displaystyle(u_{t}+uu_{x})_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho^{2},

(2.13b)

\displaystyle\rho_{t}+(u\rho)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

is the Euler–Lagrange equations for the Lagrangian density (
2.12
) with
\rho=r_{x}
.

3.
The two-component nonlinear variational wave system

We will first consider the case
K_{1}=K_{3}
, that is the wave speed
c
is independent of
\psi
. We will assume
s\geq 0
such that we can introduce the complex variable
\zeta=se^{i\psi}
, and (
2.7
) reduces to

(3.1)

\zeta_{tt}-c^{2}\zeta_{xx}+\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}\left(|\zeta|\right)}{|\zeta|}\zeta=0,

which is a defocusing nonlinear wave equation. Recall that the physical interpretation of
\psi
is an angle, and thus any solutions
\psi
and
\psi+n\cdot 2\pi
should be considered equal from an application point of view. We have conservation laws for energy
\mathcal{E}=\frac{1}{2}|\zeta_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}c^{2}|\zeta_{x}|^{2}+W_{0}(|\zeta|)
and energy flux
\mathcal{F}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\bar{\zeta}_{t}\zeta_{x}+\zeta_{t}\bar{\zeta}_{x}\right)
as follows

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{E}-c^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.2b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{F}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\mathcal{E}-2W_{0}(|\zeta|)\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence both
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{E}\>\mathrm{d}x
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}\>\mathrm{d}x
are conserved for any classical solution with bounded energy. To analyze solutions we will need some conditions on the function
W_{0}
. We will assume that
W_{0}(s)\rightarrow\infty
rapidly enough as
s\rightarrow 1
to be able to bound
\|W_{0}(|\zeta|)\|_{\infty}
in terms of the total energy
E
. In addition we will assume that
W_{0}
is non-negative and that
W_{0}
is well behaved close to
s=0
, and also close to zeros of
W_{0}
. We will also relax (
2.3
) slightly to allow for
s=0
to be a local maximum or minimum instead of a global minimum, in accordance with
[
10
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We will assume that
W_{0}\in C^{4}([0,1))
and non-negative, and that there exists a finite number of
s^{*}
such that
W_{0}(s^{*})=0
, and for all zeros
s^{*}
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{W_{0}(s)-W_{0}(0)}{s^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(0)\in\mathbb{R},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow s^{*}}\frac{W_{0}(s)}{(s-s^{*})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s^{*})\in[0,\infty).

Moreover, we let
W_{0}
satisfy

(3.5)

\int_{s}^{1}W_{0}(u)(1-u)\>du=\infty,

and that there exists
\tilde{s}\in[0,1)
such that
W_{0}(s)>0
and
W_{0}^{\prime}(s)>0
for all
s\in(\tilde{s},1)
.

We define the function spaces for the solutions in the next definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\zeta^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
,
|\zeta^{*}|<1
, and

(3.6)

X_{\zeta^{*}}=\{(\zeta,\sigma)\mid\zeta\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\zeta-\zeta^{*}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\},

and define

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\zeta,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\sigma|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\zeta_{x}|^{2}+W_{0}(|\zeta|),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\zeta,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sigma\bar{\zeta}_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\sigma}\zeta_{x},

and note that both
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are real valued. Let now

(3.9)

X_{E}=\{(\zeta,\sigma)\in X_{\zeta^{*}}\mid|\mathcal{E}\|_{L^{1}}\leq E\},

and denote

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{T,E}

\displaystyle=\left\{\zeta\in C([0,T],W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}))\cap C^{1}([0,T],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})\mid(\zeta(t),\zeta_{t}(t))\in X_{E}\right\}.

In the case
T<\infty
we equip
\mathcal{D}_{T,E}
with the metric
d_{\mathcal{D}_{E,T}}
induced from

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(\|\zeta(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\zeta(t)-\zeta^{*}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\zeta_{t}(t))\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|\zeta_{t}(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|W_{0}(|\zeta(t)|)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\big).

For bounded energy we can now a priori put constraints on possible solutions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
W_{0}
satisfy the conditions of Definition
3.1
. Then there exist positive constants
c_{E},C_{E}
, depending on
W_{0}
and
E
only, such that for any
(\zeta,\sigma)\in X_{E}
we have

\displaystyle\|W_{0}(|\zeta|)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{E},

\displaystyle\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq c_{E}<1.

Moreover for
s\in[0,c_{E}]
there is a positive constant
k_{E}
such that

(3.12)

W_{0}^{\prime}(s)^{2}\leq k_{E}W_{0}(s).

Furthermore, we can define

(3.13)

\displaystyle L_{E}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,c_{E}]}|W_{0}^{\prime}(s)|,

(3.14)

\displaystyle L_{E}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,c_{E}]}|\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(s)}{s}|,

(3.15)

\displaystyle L_{E}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,c_{E}]}|W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s)|.

Proof.

Since
\frac{1}{2}\|\sigma\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}c^{2}\|\zeta_{x}\|_{2}^{2}+\|W_{0}(|\zeta|)\|_{1}\leq E
we have that
|\zeta(x_{1})-\zeta(x_{2})|\leq\frac{\sqrt{2E_{1}}}{c}\sqrt{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}
where
E_{1}=E-\frac{1}{2}\|\sigma\|_{2}^{2}-\|W_{0}(|\sigma|)\|_{1}\geq 0
. Since
\zeta
is continuous we have that there exists
\hat{x}
such that
|\zeta(\hat{x})|=\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
. Then we have that

(3.16)

|\zeta(x)|\geq\begin{cases}\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-\frac{\sqrt{2E_{1}}}{c}\sqrt{|x-\hat{x}|},&\hat{x}-\frac{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}^{2}c^{2}}{2E_{1}}<x<\hat{x}+\frac{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}^{2}c^{2}}{2E_{1}},\\
0,&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

In particular, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}W_{0}(|\zeta(x)|)\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\hat{x}-\frac{c^{2}}{2E_{1}}(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-\tilde{s})^{2}}^{\hat{x}+\frac{c^{2}}{2E_{1}}(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-\tilde{s})^{2}}W_{0}(|\zeta(x)|)\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\hat{x}-\frac{c^{2}}{2E_{1}}(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-\tilde{s})^{2}}^{\hat{x}+\frac{c^{2}}{2E_{1}}(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-\tilde{s})^{2}}W_{0}\left(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-\frac{\sqrt{2E_{1}}}{c}\sqrt{|x-\hat{x}|})\right)\>\mathrm{d}x

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{s}}^{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}}W_{0}(u)\left(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-u\right)\>\mathrm{d}u

By the monotone convergence theorem we have that

(3.18)

\lim_{S\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{s}^{S}W_{0}(u)\left(S-u\right)\>\mathrm{d}u=\int_{s}^{1}W_{0}(u)\left(1-u\right)\>\mathrm{d}u=\infty.

Hence we have the inequality

(3.19)

\int_{\tilde{s}}^{\|\zeta\|_{\infty}}W_{0}(u)\left(\|\zeta\|_{\infty}-u\right)\>\mathrm{d}u\leq E-E_{1},

which proves the existence of
c_{E},C_{E}
such that
\|\zeta\|_{\infty}=c_{E}<1
and
\|W_{0}(|\zeta|)\|_{\infty}=W_{0}(\|\zeta\|_{\infty})=C_{E}<\infty
. The constant can be chosen to depend on
W_{0}
and
E
only.

To prove that
W_{0}^{\prime 2}\leq k_{E}W_{0}
, note that whenever
W_{0}
tends to zero that

(3.20)

\lim_{s\rightarrow s^{*}}\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{W_{0}(s)}=2W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(s^{*})\in[0,\infty).

In between zeros of
W_{0}
the fraction
\frac{W_{0}^{\prime 2}}{W_{0}}
is continuous. Since there is only a finite number of intervals where
W_{0}
is zero and
[0,c_{E}]
is bounded,
\frac{W_{0}^{\prime 2}}{W_{0}}
has to be bounded as well by a constant dependent on
c_{E}
. Since
W_{0}
is non-negative we get the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
in
X_{E}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}W_{0}(|\zeta_{1}(x)|)\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{2}L_{E}^{\prime\prime}|\zeta_{1}-\zeta^{*}|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}L_{E}^{\prime\prime}\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta^{*}\|_{2}^{2},

and similarly, since
W_{0}(|\zeta|)=\int_{|\zeta^{*}|}^{|\zeta|}W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(u)(|\zeta|-u)\>\mathrm{d}u
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|W_{0}(|\zeta_{1}|)-W_{0}(|\zeta_{2}|)|\>\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg|\int_{|\zeta^{*}|}^{|\zeta_{1}|}W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(u)\left(|\zeta_{1}|-|\zeta_{2}|\right)\>\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{|\zeta_{2}|}^{|\zeta_{1}|}W_{0}^{\prime\prime}(u)\left(|\zeta_{2}|-u\right)\>\mathrm{d}u\Bigg|\>\mathrm{d}x

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq L_{E}^{\prime\prime}\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}+L_{E}^{\prime\prime}\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta^{*}\|_{2}\|\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}\|_{2}.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{t0})\in X_{E}
, and assume that
W_{0}
satisfies the conditions in Definition
3.1
. Then there exists a unique global strong energy conserving solution
(\zeta,\zeta_{t})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\infty,E}
of (
3.1
) depending continuously on the initial data
(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{t0})\in X_{E}
.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to show that the solution in strong form

\displaystyle\zeta(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\zeta_{0}(x-ct)+\zeta_{0}(x+ct)\right)+\frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}\zeta_{t0}(y)\>\mathrm{d}y

(3.23)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2c}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x-c(t-s)}^{x+c(t-s)}\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(|\zeta(s,y)|)}{|\zeta(s,y)|}\zeta(s,y)\>\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,

is the unique fixed point of a contraction on
\mathcal{D}_{T,E}
, as is done for Picard iteration for a to be specified
T
. To be able to prove that energy is conserved by strong solutions we will at first assume that the initial data in
X_{E}
is smooth,
(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{t0})\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. We will then prove that the strong solution with smooth initial data preserve energy, and that the solution operator taking initial data in
X_{E}
to solutions in
\mathcal{D}_{T,E}
is continuous and can thus be extended to all of
X_{E}
. We prove that the energy is still conserved for these solutions. Finally we note that the existence time
T
depends on
W_{0}
and
E
only, and thus that the existence time can be extended indefinitely.

We will now assume that
0<E<E^{\prime}
and that
(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{t0})\in X_{E}
and
\zeta_{0}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\zeta_{t0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
(\hat{\zeta},\hat{\zeta}_{t})\in\mathcal{D}_{T,E^{\prime}}\cap C^{2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
with
(\hat{\zeta}(0),\hat{\zeta}_{t}(0))=(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{t0})
. Then let
(\zeta,\zeta_{t})\in C([0,T],X_{E^{\prime}})
,
\zeta\in C^{2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
, be the classical solution of

(3.24)

\zeta_{tt}-c^{2}\zeta_{xx}=-\frac{W^{\prime}_{0}(|\hat{\zeta}|)}{|\hat{\zeta}|}\hat{\zeta},\quad(\zeta,\zeta_{t})|_{t=0}=(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{t0}).

Then by Duhamel’s principle we have

\displaystyle\zeta(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\zeta_{0}(x-ct)+\zeta_{0}(x+ct)\right)+\frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}\zeta_{t0}(y)\>\mathrm{d}y

(3.25a)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2c}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{x-c(t-s)}^{x+c(t-s)}\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(|\hat{\zeta}(s,y)|)}{|\hat{\zeta}(s,y)|}\hat{\zeta}(s,y)\>\mathrm{d}y\>\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\zeta_{t}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}\left(\zeta_{x0}(x+ct)-\zeta_{x0}(x-ct)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\zeta_{t0}(x+ct)+\zeta_{t0}(x-ct)\right)\>\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(|\hat{\zeta}(s,x-c(t-s))|)}{|\hat{\zeta}(s,x-c(t-s))|}\hat{\zeta}(s,x-c(t-s))

(3.25b)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(|\hat{\zeta}(s,x+c(t-s))|)}{|\hat{\zeta}(s,x+c(t-s))|}\hat{\zeta}(s,x+c(t-s))\>\mathrm{d}s.

To prove that the strong solution (
3.25
) is a member of
\mathcal{D}_{T,E^{\prime}}
we will need norm estimates on the solutions. For
(\zeta,\zeta_{t})\in\mathcal{D}_{T,E}
we have that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2c}\int_{x-c(t-s)}^{x+c(t-s)}\frac{W_{0}^{\prime}(|\zeta(s,y)|)}{|\zeta(s,y)|}\zeta(s,y)\>\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq L_{E}(t-s),

(3.27)

is a constant symmetric hyperbolic matrix with eigenvalues
-1,0,1
. We want to show existence of short time solutions from a fix point argument. To be able to make a contraction we will further restrict our space for approximate solutions to

(3.69)

\mathcal{D}_{T,E,L}^{\mathrm{lin}}=\left\{U\in\mathcal{D}_{T,E,L}\mid U(t)\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{6}),\|U_{xx}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{6})}\leq L\right\}.

Let
0<E<E^{\prime},0<L<L^{\prime}
and
U_{0}\in X_{E,L}
with
\|U_{0xx}\|_{\infty}\leq L
be the initial data, then for any
\hat{U}\in\mathcal{D}_{T,E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{lin}}
with
\hat{U}(0)=U_{0}
, the linear system of transport equations

(3.70)

U_{t}-c(\hat{\psi})AU_{x}=F(\hat{U})

can be solved by characteristics. Define the backward characteristics
x_{\pm}
at time
\tau
from point
(t,x)
by

(3.71)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x)=\mp c\bigg(\hat{\psi}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))\bigg),

and note that
x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x)=x_{\pm}(\tau;s,x_{\pm}(s;t,x))
and
x_{\pm}(t;t,x))=x
. Then, by taking the derivative with respect to
s
, we get the following

(3.72)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x)\mp c(\hat{\psi}(\tau,x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x)))\frac{\partial}{\partial x}x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x)=0.

Thus

(3.73)

\displaystyle|x_{\pm}(\tau_{1};t,x)-x_{\pm}(\tau_{2};t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|c\|_{\infty}|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|,

(3.74)

\displaystyle|x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x_{1})-x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{exp}\left\{\left\|\frac{1}{c}\right\|_{\infty}L^{\prime}|t-\tau|\right\}|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

(3.75)

\displaystyle|x_{+}(\tau;t,x)-x_{-}(\tau;t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|c\|_{\infty}|t-\tau|,

(3.76)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}x_{\pm}(\tau;t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|c\|_{\infty}\>\mathrm{exp}\left\{\left\|\frac{1}{c}\right\|_{\infty}L^{\prime}|t-\tau|\right\}.

We need to show that the Duhamel operator
DF(\hat{U})(t)=\int_{0}^{t}T_{\tau}(t-\tau)F(\hat{U}(\tau))\>\mathrm{d}\tau
, where
T_{\tau}
is the solution operator of
U_{t}(t)-c(\psi(t+\tau))AU_{x}(t)=0
, satisfies
DF(\hat{U})\in C([0,\infty),W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{6}))
, and
\|DF(\hat{U}_{1})(t)-DF(\hat{U}_{2})(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(t,E^{\prime},L^{\prime})\|\hat{U}_{1}-\hat{U}_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
for
\hat{U}_{1},\hat{U}_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{T,E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{lin}}
. Since

(3.77)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{c^{\prime}(\psi)}{c(\psi)}=\frac{c^{\prime\prime}(\psi)c(\psi)-c^{\prime}(\psi)^{2}}{c(\psi)^{2}}\psi_{x},

and
F
is a smooth function, we have that for
\hat{U}\in\mathcal{D}_{T,E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{lin}}
there is

(3.78)

\|F(\hat{U}(t))\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}.

Hence the Duhamel operator satisfies
\|DF(\hat{U})(t)\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}t
. Note that

(3.79)

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x_{1})-x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x_{2})\right)=\mp\left(c(\hat{\psi}_{1}(\tau,x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x_{1})))-c(\hat{\psi}_{2}(\tau,x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x_{2})))\right),

and thus

\displaystyle|x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x_{1})-x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|e^{\|c^{\prime}\|_{\infty}L^{\prime}(t-\tau)}

(3.80)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{L^{\prime}}\left(e^{\|c^{\prime}\|_{\infty}L^{\prime}(t-\tau)}-1\right)\|\hat{\psi}_{1}-\hat{\psi}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))},

(3.81)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x}x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x)-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{L^{\prime}}\mathrm{exp}\left\{k_{L^{\prime}}(t-\tau)\right\}\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\hat{\psi}_{1}-\hat{\psi}_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})},

where
C_{L^{\prime}}
and
k_{L^{\prime}}
depends on
c
and
L^{\prime}
only. Thus, since
F
is a smooth function and
\|\hat{U}_{1}\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\|\hat{U}_{2}\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq L^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{1})(\tau,x^{1}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))-F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})(\tau,x^{2}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{1})(\tau,x^{1}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))-F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})(\tau,x^{1}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{t}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})(\tau,x^{1}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))-F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})(\tau,x^{2}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{1})(\tau,x^{1}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))-F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})(\tau,x^{1}_{\pm}(\tau;t,x))\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\quad+t\bar{C}(L^{\prime},E^{\prime})\|\hat{\psi}_{1}-\hat{\psi}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

(3.82)

\displaystyle\leq t\tilde{C}(L^{\prime},E^{\prime})\|\hat{U}_{1}-\hat{U}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{6})}

To compute
\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(DF(\hat{U}_{1})(t)-DF(\hat{U}_{2})(t)\right)
we estimate

\displaystyle\quad\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\int_{0}^{t}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{1})(\tau,x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x))-F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})(\tau,x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x))\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\nabla_{U}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{1})\frac{\partial\hat{U}_{1}}{\partial x}-\nabla_{U}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})\frac{\partial\hat{U}_{2}}{\partial x}\right)(\tau,x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x))\frac{\partial}{\partial x}x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x)\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{U}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})\frac{\partial\hat{U}_{2}}{\partial x}(\tau,x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x))\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x)-x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x)\right)\>\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\left(\nabla_{U}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})\frac{\partial\hat{U}_{2}}{\partial x}(\tau,x_{\pm}^{1}(\tau;t,x))-\nabla_{U}F_{j}(\hat{U}_{2})\frac{\partial\hat{U}_{2}}{\partial x}(\tau,x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x))\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\partial}{\partial x}x_{\pm}^{2}(\tau;t,x)\>\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C_{E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}\left(\mathrm{exp}\left\{\left\|\frac{1}{c}\right\|_{\infty}L^{\prime}t\right\}-1\right)\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\hat{U}_{1}(\tau)-\hat{U}_{2}(\tau)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}\>(\mathrm{exp}\left\{K_{L^{\prime}}t\right\}-1)\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\hat{\psi}_{1}(\tau)-\hat{\psi}_{2}(\tau)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

(3.83)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}\>(\mathrm{exp}\left\{K_{L^{\prime}}t\right\}-1)\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\hat{\psi}_{1}(\tau)-\hat{\psi}_{2}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

That is, we have that

(3.84)

\|U_{1}(t)-U_{2}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{L^{\prime},E^{\prime}}(\mathrm{exp}\{k_{L^{\prime}}t\}-1)\|\hat{U}_{1}-\hat{U}_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})},

and hence for
T
small enough we have that

(3.85)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U_{1}(t)-U_{2}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\hat{U}_{1}(t)-\hat{U}_{2}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Since
U
is continuously differentiable, we can do similar estimates for
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|U_{1,t}(t)-U_{2,t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
directly from the linear system. We need that the energy of the solution is bounded by
E^{\prime}
. Recall that
E(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{E}(t)\>\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}s(t)^{2}(\phi(t)^{2}+\omega(t)^{2})+v(t)^{2}+r(t)^{2}+W_{0}(s(t))\>\mathrm{d}x
, and thus

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathcal{E}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}c(\hat{\psi})\left(s^{2}\phi\omega+vr\right)

\displaystyle=s\phi^{2}\hat{v}+v\hat{s}\hat{\phi}^{2}-\frac{2}{\hat{s}}s^{2}\phi\hat{\phi}\hat{v}

\displaystyle\quad+s\omega^{2}\hat{v}-v\hat{s}\hat{\omega}^{2}+W_{0}^{\prime}(s)\hat{v}-W_{0}^{\prime}(\hat{s})v

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c^{\prime}(\hat{\psi})}{c(\hat{\psi})}\left(s^{2}\omega\hat{\omega}(\hat{\phi}-\phi)+\hat{r}(\hat{\phi}r-\phi\hat{r})+v(\hat{\omega}\hat{r}-\omega r)\right)

(3.86)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c^{\prime}(\hat{\psi})}{c(\hat{\psi})}2s\phi\left(\frac{s}{\hat{s}}\hat{\omega}\hat{r}-\omega c(\hat{\psi})s_{x}\right).

Integration yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2{L^{\prime}}^{2}E(t)+2{L^{\prime}}^{2}E^{\prime}+2{L^{\prime}}^{2}\sqrt{E^{\prime}}\sqrt{E(t)}+\|W_{0}^{\prime}(s)\hat{v}\|_{1}+\|W_{0}^{\prime}(\hat{s})v\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+K\left({L^{\prime}}^{3}\sqrt{E(t)}\sqrt{E^{\prime}}+4{L^{\prime}}\sqrt{E(t)}\sqrt{E^{\prime}}\right)

(3.87)

\displaystyle\quad+K\left(2{L^{\prime}}^{3}\sqrt{E(t)}\sqrt{E^{\prime}}+2E(t)\|c\|_{\infty}L^{\prime}\right)

By Definition
3.1
we have that

\displaystyle\|W_{0}^{\prime}(s)\hat{v}\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\|W_{0}^{\prime}(s)\right\|_{2}\left\|\hat{v}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k_{E(t)}\|W_{0}(s)\|_{1}}\sqrt{E^{\prime}}

(3.88)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k_{E^{\prime}}E(t)}\sqrt{E^{\prime}},

and hence for
t
small enough, depending on
E^{\prime}
,
L^{\prime}
,
W_{0}
, and
c
only,
E(t)\leq E^{\prime}
.

Hence the sequence
U_{n}
, where
U_{n+1}
is the solution to the linear equation with
U_{n}
inserted, is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{D}_{T,E^{\prime},L^{\prime}}
, and hence there is a solution of the equations. Moreover, the limit will satisfy
\mathcal{E}_{t}-(c(\psi)^{2}\mathcal{F})_{x}=0
, and thus conserves energy.
∎

New Bounds on Diffsequences
Alexander Clifton

Abstract
For a set of positive integers D D , a k k -term D D -diffsequence is a sequence of positive integers a 1 < a 2 < ⋯ < a k a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k} such that a i − a i − 1 ∈ D a_{i}-a_{i-1}\in D for i = 2 , 3 , … , k i=2,3,\dots,k . For k ∈ ℤ + k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+} and D ⊂ ℤ + D\subset\mathbb{Z}^{+} , we define Δ ⁡ ( D , k ) \Delta(D,k) , if it exists, to be the smallest integer n n such that every 2 2 -coloring of { 1 , 2 , … , n } \{1,2,\dots,n\} contains a monochromatic D D -diffsequence of length k k . We improve the lower bound on Δ ⁡ ( D , k ) \Delta(D,k) where D = { 2 i ∣ i ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 } D=\{2^{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq{0}}\} , proving a conjecture of Chokshi, Clifton, Landman, and Sawin. We also determine all sets of the form D = { d 1 , d 2 , … } D=\{d_{1},d_{2},\dots\} with d i | d i + 1 d_{i}\mid d_{i+1} for which Δ ⁡ ( D , k ) \Delta(D,k) exists.

Introduction

Ramsey theory questions typically ask about whether a graph contains a monochromatic copy of a particular subgraph when the edges are colored with
r
colors. However, more generally, the question at the heart of Ramsey Theory is whether or not a set retains a certain property when the elements are divided into disjoint subsets. For example, the positive integers contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions, but if we split them into
r
disjoint sets, will there still be a set with arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions? In 1927, van der Waerden
[
Wae27
]
answered this question in the affirmative by showing that every
r
-coloring of the natural numbers will contain arbitrarily long monochromatic arithmetic progressions.

It is natural to ask what other types of structures besides arithmetic progressions exhibit van der Waerden-type theorems. Van der Waerden’s work is predated by Schur
[
Sch17
]
who showed that every
r
-coloring of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
contains some monochromatic triple
(x,y,z)
satisfying
x+y=z
. More recent sequences of study include arithmetic progressions modulo
m

[
LW97
,
LL94
]
, arithmetic progressions with a fixed common difference
[
LW97
,
LL94
,
BGL99
]
, and descending waves
[
BEF90
]
.

Landman and Robertson considered the question of whether an
r
-coloring must produce a monochromatic sequence whose gaps lie in some fixed set. The answer to this question will depend on the choice of
r
as well as the choice of the set of gaps. In
[
LR03
]
, Landman and Robertson introduced the notion of a
D
- diffsequence:

Definition 1
.

For a set
D
of positive integers, a
D
-diffsequence
of length
k
is a sequence of positive integers
a_{1}<a_{2}\dots<a_{k}
such that each gap lies in
D
. That is,

a_{i}-a_{i-1}\in D,

for
i=2,3,\dots,k
.

Definition 2
.

A set
D
is called
r
-accessible
if every
r
-coloring of the positive integers contains arbitrarily long monochromatic
D
-diffsequences.

Note that any
r
-accessible set

is automatically

-accessible for
t<r
. The notion of

The set of Fibonacci numbers is known to be

-accessible
[
LR03
]
but not
4
-accessible
[

]
. The set of primes is not
3
-accessible
[
LR03
]
but the question of whether or not it is
2
-accessible remains unresolved. Results on accessibility for fixed translates of the set of primes are given in
[
LR03
]
and
[

]
. The set of powers of
n
is only
2
-accessible when

since otherwise there is a periodic coloring modulo

that avoids arbitrarily long
D
-diffsequences.

Just as the van der Waerden number

is the smallest

If

Landman and Robertson showed that the set

Theorem 1.1

When

\Delta(D,k)\geq{2^{\sqrt{2k}}\left(\frac{(\sqrt{2}-1)k}{8}-\frac{\sqrt{k}}{8}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{2}}.

We next consider some sets that are not

-accessible.

Proposition 1.2

The set

is not
2
-accessible.

Generalizing both powers of

Let

Let

2

are color

Continuing in this manner, we define

Note that the repeating block of

A monochromatic

Lemma 2.1
.

For

, the following holds for any monochromatic

Proof.

Consider a monochromatic

-diffsequence with respect to
P_{t}
. For each

, we can split each repeating block of size
2^{t}
into
2^{t-m-1}

2^{m+1}
. Note that a sub-block of size
2^{m+1}
looks like a repeating block of
P_{m+1}
(possibly with colors swapped). Thus, by construction, if

, then
a_{i}
cannot be in the first half of any sub-block of size

2^{m}

Keeping with the assumption that

. This means that in between every two gaps of the same size, there is at least one gap of a smaller size. Since there is no way to have a gap of size smaller than

1

for

We will now finish the proof via induction. Suppose that there exists some

{a_{i_{j+1}}-a_{i_{j+1}-1}}

. Consider

Beginning with

gives a constant upper bound,
1+2+\dots+(t-1)=\frac{t(t-1)}{2}

If

and

are in different sub-blocks and different positions within their respective sub-blocks.

In the second case,

is a multiple of

. Therefore, it is at most

, so

If we consider what sub-blocks

lie in and ignore those that are not the first

in their respective sub-block, this corresponds to a monochromatic

-diffsequence with respect to the coloring

.

It is clear that the sum is positive. It can also be bounded above by the following convergent geometric series:

\displaystyle=1-\displaystyle\sum_{i=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+2)(2m+3)\dots(2i)}\pmod{2}.

To reach the desired conclusion, it suffices to demonstrate that

\frac{2}{3}\geq{\displaystyle\sum_{i=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+2)(2m+3)\dots(2i)}}>0.

It is clear that the sum is positive. It can also be bounded above by the following convergent geometric series:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2m+2)^{2i-2m-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{2m+2}}{1-\frac{1}{(2m+2)^{2}}}=\frac{2m+2}{(2m+2)^{2}-1}

Concluding Remarks

In this paper, we improved the lower bound for
\Delta(D,k)
when
D=\{2^{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq{0}}\}
, establishing that
\Delta(D,k)
grows faster than any polynomial. The exponent of the lower bound is
\Theta(\sqrt{k})
while the exponent of the upper bound is
\Theta(k)
. Values computed up to
k=12
align closely with the lower bound so we conjecture that it is asymptotically tight:

Conjecture 5.1
.

When
D=\{2^{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq{0}}\}

\Delta(D,k)=2^{\Theta(\sqrt{k})}.

It does not make sense to consider

2
-accessible. However, it may be interesting to consider other sets that exhibit exponential growth such as

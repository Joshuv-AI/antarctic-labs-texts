Birational Induction of Nilpotent Orbit Covers in Exceptional Types
Matthew Westaway

Abstract
. Let G G be a semisimple simply connected algebraic group over ℂ {\mathbb{C}} of exceptional type. For each G G -equivariant nilpotent cover of a nilpotent coadjoint G G -orbit 𝕆 {\mathbb{O}} , we determine the unique birationally rigid induction datum from which it is birationally induced.

1.
Introduction

Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction was introduced in 1979 by Lusztig and Spaltenstein
[
LS
]
. While it was originally defined in the context of unipotent classes, it can be easily translated into the language of nilpotent orbits in reductive Lie algebras
[
CM
]
, and in this context has had significant applications in Lie-theoretic representation theory. It works in this setting as follows. Let
G
be a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
; this acts on its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
via the adjoint action
1
1

1

In the substance of this paper we prefer to work with the coadjoint action of
G
on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, but these two actions can be identified by means of a non-degenerate
G
-invariant symmetric bilinear form.
and thus partitions
G
into orbits. Of these, we pay particular attention to the
nilpotent
orbits, i.e. those orbits consisting of nilpotent elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Given a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
with Levi decomposition
P=LU
, where
L
is itself a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
, we may also consider the nilpotent
L
-orbits in
{\mathfrak{l}}=\operatorname{Lie}(L)
. Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction is then a process which takes as input a nilpotent
L
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
and produces from it a nilpotent
G
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}={{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})
(we call
(L,{\mathbb{O}}_{L})
an induction datum for
{\mathbb{O}})
. This procedure satisfies a number of nice properties, including that it is transitive, independent of the parabolic subgroup
P
, and behaves predictably on the dimensions of orbits.

One reason why this procedure has turned out to be so useful is that it often allows us to reduce questions about nilpotent orbits to the case of
rigid
nilpotent orbits, i.e. those orbits that cannot be obtained (non-trivially) via Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction. For example, this was a significant tool in resolving the longstanding problem of showing that all finite
W
-algebras have a one-dimensional representation
[
L1
,
P2
,
P3
]
and the related problem in modular representation theory of finding the minimal dimension for representations of reduced enveloping algebras of Lie algebras over fields of positive characteristic
[
P1
,
PT2
]
. Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction also plays an essential role in the classification of sheets
[
Bo
]
, i.e. the irreducible components of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{(m)}:=\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\mid\dim G\cdot x=m\}
for
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and is closely related to parabolic induction of primitive ideals
[
BJ
]
.

One aspect of Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction which can be both a feature and a bug is that a given nilpotent orbit
{\mathbb{O}}
can be induced from multiple different rigid orbits. In order to avoid some of the problems this causes, one option is to look instead at
birational induction
. As initially introduced (see, for example,
[
L2
]
), being birational was a property that a given induction from one nilpotent orbit to another could have or not have, but this was refined in
[
LMM
]
to a procedure similar to Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction except applying to different objects: nilpotent orbit covers. A (
G
-equivariant) nilpotent orbit cover (for a reductive algebraic group
G
) is a homogeneous space
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}
equipped with a
G
-equivariant finite morphism to a nilpotent
G
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}
; in particular, nilpotent orbits are (trivial) covers of themselves. Birational induction then sends a nilpotent orbit cover
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\to{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
for a Levi subgroup
L
in
G
to a nilpotent orbit cover
{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})\to{{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})
, and we call
(L,\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
a birational induction datum for
{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
. This procedure has similar properties to Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction: it is transitive, it is independent of the parabolic subgroup
P
of which
L
is a Levi factor, and it behaves predictably on degrees of nilpotent covers. It also has an additional property which we don’t see for Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction, namely that each nilpotent orbit cover has a
unique
birational induction datum (up to conjugacy) which is birationally rigid, i.e. which cannot be non-trivially obtained through birational induction.

This refinement of Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction is used significantly in
[
LMM
,
MM
]
. Losev, Mason-Brown and Matvieievskyi introduce in
[
LMM
]
a definition of unipotent ideals in
U({\mathfrak{g}})
, in order to define the notion of unipotent representations of a complex reductive Lie algebra. Birational induction is one of the key tools which allows for the computation of the central characters of these unipotent ideals; this also has connections with the central characters for one-dimensional representations of finite
W
-algebras. Parabolic induction of unipotent ideals and of representations of finite
W
-algebras as in
[
L1
]
also appear to be related to birational induction. It has also proved key in constructing and understanding Losev’s orbit method map
[
L2
]
.

When
G
is a semisimple algebraic group of exceptional type, the rigid induction data for each nilpotent
G
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}
are known and can be found in tables in
[
El
,
DE
]
. In this paper, for
G
a semisimple simply connected algebraic group of exceptional type, we determine the birationally rigid birational induction datum for each nilpotent orbit cover of an induced nilpotent orbit, which we compile in Tables
1
through
5
. In other words, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a semisimple simply connected algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
of exceptional type and let
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}\to{\mathbb{O}}
be a
G
-equivariant nilpotent orbit cover, where
{\mathbb{O}}
is not a rigid orbit. Then the (unique) birationally rigid birational induction datum for
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}
is as given in Tables
1
through
5
.

We note that Tables
1
through
5
only include the nilpotent orbit covers for induced nilpotent orbits, since nilpotent orbit covers of rigid nilpotent orbits are always birationally rigid. In order to describe the nilpotent covers, we appeal to the fact that such covers of an orbit
{\mathbb{O}}
(up to isomorphism) are in bijection with subgroups of the component group of the centralizer of an element
e\in{\mathbb{O}}
(up to conjugacy).

Before ending the introduction, let us say a word about what happens when
G
is of classical type. When
G={{\mbox{\rm SL}}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, each orbit is birationally induced from the zero orbit for a suitable Levi subgroup; to determine the birationally rigid birational induction datum for a non-trivial cover, one would want to determine the possible birationally rigid induction data which are not orbits and then use
[
MMY
, Proposition 2.6]
. It is likely that this could be done explicitly using
[
LMM
, Proposition 7.6.4]
. When
G={{\mbox{\rm SO}}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
or
{{\mbox{\rm Sp}}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
, the component group of the centralizer of a nilpotent element is always a product of copies of
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since we only describe this group up to isomorphism, we cannot distinguish between subgroups of the same order. Perhaps the most we can hope for is to determine, for each birationally rigid induction datum
(L,\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
, the degree of the cover
{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
. Each Levi subgroup of
{{\mbox{\rm Sp}}}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
is of the form

{{\mbox{\rm GL}}}_{i_{1}}({\mathbb{C}})\times\cdots\times{{\mbox{\rm GL}}}_{i_{r}}({\mathbb{C}})\times{{\mbox{\rm Sp}}}_{2m}({\mathbb{C}})

for some
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
and
m
such that
n=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{r}+m
, and each birationally rigid cover for such Levi subgroup is of the form

\{0\}\times\cdots\times\{0\}\times\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}

where
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}
is a birationally rigid cover of a nilpotent
{{\mbox{\rm Sp}}}_{2m}({\mathbb{C}})
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\subseteq{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{2m}({\mathbb{C}})
. The orbits
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
can be obtained from
{\mathbb{O}}={{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})
by means of (steps of) the Kempken-Spaltenstein algorithm introduced in
[
PT1
]
. This algorithm consists of making two types of reductions to a partition, called Type 1 and Type 2 reductions in
[
GTW
]
. Let
k
be the number of Type 2 reductions needed to get from
{\mathbb{O}}
to
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
through the Kempken-Spaltenstein algorithm; it should follow from
[
N2
, Claim 2.3.1, 2.3.2]
and
[
MMY
, Proposition 7.6.4]
that

\deg({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\{0\}\times\cdots\times\{0\}\times\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}))=\deg(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})+2k.

For differences when
G={{\mbox{\rm SO}}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
, see
[
N2
, §3]
.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we discuss various preliminary matters, including the precise definition of birational induction and certain (mostly known) results which can be used to understand the effect of birational induction on a nilpotent orbit cover. In Section
3
, we proceed case-by-case through the nilpotent orbits in exceptional Lie algebras, determining the birationally rigid covers which induce to each of their covers. Some classes of orbits we are able to deal with all at once, but we need to consider some orbits individually. Finally, in Section
4
we compile the results of Section
3
into tables.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Algebraic groups and Levi subgroups

In this subsection, we establish some notation and conventions for this paper and recall some well-known facts regarding algebraic groups and their Levi subgroups.

Throughout this paper,
G
denotes a reductive algebraic group over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes its Lie algebra. We fix a maximal torus
T
of
G
and a positive Borel subgroup
B
of
G
containing
T
, and let
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}
be the Lie algebras thereof. We denote by
\Phi
the root system of
G
corresponding to
T
and by
\Phi^{+}
the subset of positive roots corresponding to
B
. Furthermore, we denote by
\Pi
the subset of simple roots of
\Phi^{+}
. Set
W
to be the Weyl group of
G
, and denote by
{\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{g}})
the centre of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

The
parabolic subgroups
of
G
are the closed subgroups of
G
which contain a conjugate of the Borel subgroup
B
; we call those parabolic subgroups containing
B
itself the
standard parabolic subgroups
of
G
. Such subgroups are in bijection with subsets of
\Pi
and each parabolic subgroup is conjugate to a standard parabolic subgroup. Each parabolic subgroup
P
has a Levi decomposition
P=LU
, where
U
is the unipotent radical of
P
and
L
is reductive. We call
L
the
Levi factor
of
P
; by a
Levi subgroup
of
G
we will mean a Levi factor of some parabolic subgroup of
G
. We call
L
a
standard Levi subgroup
of
G
when it is the Levi factor of a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
; clearly each Levi subgroup of
G
is conjugate to a standard Levi subgroup of
G
. To each subset
\Delta\subseteq\Pi
we may define a standard Levi subgroup
L_{\Delta}
as the Levi factor of the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
corresponding to this subset of simple roots; each Levi subgroup of
G
is conjugate to some
L_{\Delta}
. The Weyl group
W
acts on the set of roots
\Phi
; given two subsets
\Delta
,
\Gamma
of
\Pi
, the standard Levi subgroups
L_{\Delta}
and
L_{\Gamma}
are
G
-conjugate if and only if there exists
w\in W
such that
w(\Gamma)=\Delta
.

We may therefore associate to a Levi subgroup
L
of
G
the (unique) Dynkin type of the root system
{\mathbb{Z}}\Delta\cap\Phi
, where
\Delta\subseteq\Pi
is such that
L
is conjugate to
L_{\Delta}
. When
G
is of exceptional type, this determines
L
up to conjugacy in almost all cases; however, in type
G_{2}
there are two non-conjugate Levi subgroups of type
A_{1}
, in type
F_{4}
there are two non-conjugate Levi subgroups for each type
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
and
A_{2}+A_{1}
, and in type
E_{7}
there are two non-conjugate Levi subgroups for each type
3A_{1}
,
A_{3}+A_{1}
and
A_{5}
. In types
G_{2}
and
F_{4}
these Levi subgroups can be distinguished based on root lengths, and we use a tilde to denote the short roots. In type
E_{7}
, we label the two conjugacy classes of Levi subgroups by
(3A_{1})^{\prime}
,
(A_{3}+A_{1})^{\prime}
and
(A_{5})^{\prime}
or by
(3A_{1})^{\prime\prime}
,
(A_{3}+A_{1})^{\prime\prime}
and
(A_{5})^{\prime\prime}
, where the latter notation is used when the subset of simple roots is
W
-conjugate to a subset of the black vertices in the below Dynkin diagram.

We use this notation throughout the paper, including in the tables in Section
4
.

The
rank
of a reductive algebraic group
G
is the dimension of a maximal torus
T\subseteq G
; the semisimple rank of
G
is the rank of the derived subgroup
[G,G]
. Given a Levi subgroup
L
of
G
, the
semisimple corank
of
L
is defined to be the difference between the semisimple rank of
G
and the semisimple rank of
L
. This is equal to the difference between
\left|\Pi\right|
and the number of simple roots in any subset of
\Pi
corresponding to
L
as above.

2.2.
Nilpotent orbits and covers

In this subsection we recall the basics of the theory of nilpotent orbits and nilpotent covers for complex simple Lie algebras. The material on nilpotent orbits is standard and can be found, for example, in
[
CM
]
. For the discussion of orbit covers, we follow
[
LMM
]
.

The algebraic group
G
acts on
{\mathfrak{g}}
via the adjoint action and on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
via the coadjoint action. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
are
G
-equivariantly isomorphic (via a
G
-invariant non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form) there is a correspondence between adjoint and coadjoint
G
-orbits (in
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
respectively); in this paper, we prefer to work with coadjoint
G
-orbits. A coadjoint
G
-orbit, usually denoted
{\mathbb{O}}
in this paper, is called
nilpotent
if
0\in\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}
(see
[
CM
, §1.3]
for various equivalent characterisations). There are only finitely many nilpotent
G
-orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
; we denote by
\operatorname{Nilp}(G)
the set of nilpotent
G
-orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
.

The following proposition is standard and describes what
\operatorname{Nilp}(G)
looks like for each simple algebraic group
G
– note that
\operatorname{Nilp}(G)
depends only on the root system
\Phi
, since all nilpotent
G
-orbits lie inside
[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
and the
G
-action factors through
\operatorname{Ad}(G)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The nilpotent
G
-orbits, when
\Phi
is of classical type, can be indexed as follows:

(1)

When
\Phi=A_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, there is a bijection between nilpotent
G
-orbits and partitions of
n+1
.

(2)

When
\Phi=B_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, there is a bijection between nilpotent
G
-orbits and partitions

of
2n+1
such that each even part of
\underline{p}
occurs an even number of times.

(3)

When
\Phi=C_{n}
,
n\geq 3
, there is a bijection between nilpotent
G
-orbits and partitions
\underline{p}
of
2n
such that each odd part of
\underline{p}
occurs an even number of times.

(4)

When
\Phi=D_{n}
,
n\geq 4
, there is a bijection between nilpotent
G
-orbits and partitions
\underline{p}
of
2n
such that each even part of
\underline{p}
occurs an even number of times, except that we count twice all partitions in which all parts of
\underline{p}
are even (we call such partitions

). We use the labels I and II to differentiate such partitions.

When
\Phi
is of exceptional type, the nilpotent
G
-orbits are listed explicitly (in Bala-Carter notation) in

. There are 20 non-zero nilpotent

-orbits in type

, 44 in type

, 69 in type

We follow
[
CM
]
in labelling the nilpotent

-orbits in exceptional cases via the Bala-Carter labelling – details of this can be found in
[
CM
, §8]
. Nilpotent orbits in all cases can also be labelled by weighted Dynkin diagrams and we shall often use this notation as well (see
[

, §3.5]
for an explanation of how this description works). This is a labelling of the nodes of the Dynkin diagram of

by numbers from the set

2.3.
Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction

In this subsection, we recall the basics of Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction, following
[
CM
, §7]
.

Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi decomposition
P=LU
, where
L
is the Levi factor of
P
and
U
is the unipotent radical of
P
. Denote the corresponding decomposition of Lie algebras by
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{u}}
. Furthermore, set
P^{-}
to be the opposite parabolic of
P
, with unipotent radical
U^{-}
and Levi decompositions
P^{-}=U^{-}L
and
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{u}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{l}}
. Given a subspace
V
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, denote by
V^{\perp}
the subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
consisting of those
\chi
such that
\chi(V)=0
; note that we may identify
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
with
({\mathfrak{u}}^{-})^{*}
and
{\mathfrak{u}}^{\perp}
with
({\mathfrak{p}}^{-})^{*}
. Using a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form, these subspaces of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
may be identified with the subspaces
{\mathfrak{u}}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\subseteq{\mathfrak{l}}^{*}
be a nilpotent
L
-orbit. We may then form the variety
G\times^{P}(\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp})
, where
P
acts on
G
via right multiplication and on
\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
via the coadjoint action. There exists a
G
-equivariant morphism

(2.4)

\mu:G\times^{P}(\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp})\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\qquad(g,\chi)\mapsto g\cdot\chi.

The image of this map coincides with
\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}
for some nilpotent
G
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, by Joseph’s irreducibility theorem
[
Jo
]
. We then say that
{\mathbb{O}}
is
induced
from
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
and that
(L,{\mathbb{O}}_{L})
is an
induction datum
for
{\mathbb{O}}
. This defines a map (called
Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction
, or
induction
)

{{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}:\operatorname{Nilp}(L)\to\operatorname{Nilp}(G),\quad{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\mapsto{{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})={\mathbb{O}}.

This map has three properties of particular note. First, as implied by the notation, Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction is independent of the parabolic subgroup

of

in which

is a Levi factor. Second, it is transitive, in the sense that

is a tower of Levi subgroups in

. Finally, we have the equality

{{\mbox{\rm Codim}}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}^{*}}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})={{\mbox{\rm Codim}}}_{{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}}({{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})),

which can be rephrased as
\dim({{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L}))=\dim{\mathbb{O}}_{L}+2\dim{\mathfrak{u}}
.

-orbit

G
on
G
(resp.

) induces an action of
G
on

. We denote

and we will abuse notation slightly to also refer to elements of

as rigid induction data for
{\mathbb{O}}
. By the semisimple corank of an element of

2.4.
Birational induction

In this subsection we recall the notion of birational induction from
[
L2
]
and
[
LMM
]
, which extends the idea of Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction to the setting of nilpotent covers.

Maintaining the notation from Subsection
2.3
, let
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}
be an
L
-equivariant cover of
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
. The covering map
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\to{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
induces a map
\zeta:\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}])\to\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}
. We hence get an action of
P
on
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}])\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
by letting
L
act diagonally and letting
U
act via

u\cdot(x,\chi)=(x,u\cdot\zeta(x)-\zeta(x)+u\cdot\chi)

for
u\in U
,
x\in\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}])
and
\chi\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp}
. This induces a map

\widetilde{\mu}:G\times^{P}(\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}])\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp})\to G\times^{P}(\overline{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\times{\mathfrak{p}}^{\perp})\xrightarrow{\mu}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}.

Noting that
{\mathbb{O}}={{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})
lies in the image of this map, we define

\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}=\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}({\mathbb{O}}).

This is a
G
-equivariant nilpotent cover of
{\mathbb{O}}
. We have hence defined a map

{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}:\operatorname{Cov}(L,{\mathbb{O}}_{L})\to\operatorname{Cov}(G,{{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})),\quad\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\mapsto\widetilde{\mu}^{-1}({{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L}))=\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}},

which we call
birational induction
. As with Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction, birational induction is independent of the parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
in which
L
is a Levi factor and is transitive by
[
LMM
, Proposition 2.4.1]
. We also have the following result from
[
LMM
, Proposition 2.4.1]
and
[
MMY
, Proposition 2.6]
, which will be very useful in Section
3
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Maintain the notation from above and suppose that
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\to{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
is a degree
m
nilpotent cover of
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
and that
\breve{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\to{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
is a degree
mn
nilpotent cover of
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
which factors through
\breve{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}\to\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L}
, for
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the degree of the covering map
{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})\to{{\mbox{\rm Ind}}}_{L}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{L})
is divisible by
m
, and the degree of the covering map
{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}({\breve{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})\to{{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
is precisely

.

When
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}
is a
G
-equivariant nilpotent cover of a nilpotent
G
-orbit
{\mathbb{O}}
, we call
(L,\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
a
birational induction datum
for

if
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}={{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L}^{G}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}_{L})
. We say that

is

if the only birational induction datum for

is

, i.e. if it cannot be birationally induced from a proper Levi subgroup. If

is birationally rigid and

is a birational induction datum for

, we call

a

for
\widetilde{{\mathbb{O}}}
.

2

We should technically call this a birationally rigid birational induction datum, but for ease of reference we miss out the second “birational”. In

Remark 1
.

Although each nilpotent
G
-orbit can also be viewed as a
G
-equivariant nilpotent cover of itself, it is worth highlighting here a distinction between the theory of nilpotent orbits and the theory of nilpotent orbit covers. When studying nilpotent orbits of an algebraic group
G
, we generally only care about the underlying root system
\Phi
of
G
– in particular, for most questions we care about it doesn’t usually matter whether
G
is reductive or semisimple, or what the isogeny type of
G
is. For example, this is true for most questions about Lusztig-Spaltenstein induction and so we don’t normally need to concern ourselves with the precise form of Levi subgroups.

On the other hand, in the theory of nilpotent orbit covers the precise form of the algebraic group does matter. This is because
G
-equivariant nilpotent orbit covers of
{\mathbb{O}}
(up to isomorphism) are indexed by conjugacy classes of subgroups of
\pi_{1}^{G}({\mathbb{O}})=G_{e}/G_{e}^{\circ}
, and this latter group cannot be described purely in terms of the underlying root system of
G
. For example, when
G={{\mbox{\rm PGL}}}_{n}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we get that
\pi_{1}^{G}({\mathbb{O}})=1
for all nilpotent
G
-orbits
{\mathbb{O}}
, while for
G={{\mbox{\rm SL}}}_{n}
we get that
\pi_{1}^{G}({\mathbb{O}})={\mathbb{Z}}/h{\mathbb{Z}}
where
h
is the greatest common divisor of all the parts of the partition of
n
corresponding to
{\mathbb{O}}
.

When
G
is semisimple and the root system of
G
is indecomposable of exceptional type, we note that
G
is uniquely defined for types
E_{8}
,
F_{4}
and
G_{2}
. On the other hand, for types
E_{6}
and
E_{7}
there are two isogeny classes: simply connected and adjoint. Given a nilpotent
G
-orbit in such cases, the
G
-equivariant fundamental group may include an extra
{\mathbb{Z}}/3{\mathbb{Z}}
factor (in the
E_{6}
case) or an extra
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-factor (in the
E_{7}
case) when
G
is simply connected versus when
G
is adjoint. In this paper, we focus exclusively on the simply connected exceptional groups; in such cases, the
G
-equivariant fundamental groups can be found in
[
CM
, §8.4]
.
3
3

3

For the
G
-equivariant fundamental groups for the classical cases when
G
is simply connected or adjoint, the reader can consult
[
CM
, Corollary 6.1.6]
.

Let
L
be a Levi subgroup of
G
and
{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
a nilpotent
L
-orbit. In Section

, we often need to compute

Let

2.6.
Birationally rigid nilpotent covers

In this subsection, we give some results on birational induction and birationally rigid orbit covers. The vast majority of this material can be found in

In describing the birationally rigid nilpotent

-orbits (that is to say, nilpotent

-orbits which are birationally rigid when trivially viewed as nilpotent covers), the following result allows us to reduce to semisimple algebraic groups of a given isogeny type.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be reductive algebraic groups with the same root system
\Phi
(associated to fixed choices of maximal tori). Let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}
and

be the Lie algebras of

and

respectively. Let
{\mathbb{O}}_{1}
and

be corresponding nilpotent orbits in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}^{*}
and

{\mathbb{O}}_{1}
is birationally rigid if and only if
{\mathbb{O}}_{2}
is birationally rigid.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, let
(L_{i},{\mathbb{O}}_{L_{i}})
be the birationally rigid induction datum for
{\mathbb{O}}_{i}
and write
{\mathfrak{l}}_{i}=\operatorname{Lie}(L_{i})
. By Proposition
2.6
, the Namikawa space
{\mathfrak{P}}({\mathbb{O}}_{i})
for
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{O}}_{i}])
is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{z}}({\mathfrak{l}}_{i})^{*}
. Thus,
{\mathbb{O}}_{i}
is birationally rigid if and only if
{\mathfrak{P}}({\mathbb{O}}_{i})=0
. On the other hand,

{\mathfrak{P}}({\mathbb{O}}_{i})=H^{2}(\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{O}}_{i}])^{\tiny{{\mbox{\rm reg}}}},{\mathbb{C}})\oplus({\mathfrak{h}}_{1}^{i,*})^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{i})}\oplus\cdots\oplus({\mathfrak{h}}_{k_{i}}^{i,*})^{\pi_{1}({\mathcal{L}}_{k_{i}}^{i})}

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{i},\ldots,{\mathcal{L}}_{k_{i}}^{i}
are the symplectic leaves of codimension 2 in
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{O}}_{i}])
and the
{\mathfrak{h}}_{j}^{i,*}
corresponding to these leaves are as defined at the beginning of Subsection
2.5
. Hence
{\mathfrak{P}}({\mathbb{O}}_{i})=0
if and only if
H^{2}(\operatorname{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[{\mathbb{O}}_{i}])^{\tiny{{\mbox{\rm reg}}}},{\mathbb{C}})=0
and
k_{i}=0
. These two properties are geometric in nature; in particular, since
{\mathbb{O}}_{1}
and
{\mathbb{O}}_{2}
are isomorphic as varieties, they hold for
{\mathbb{O}}_{1}
if and only if they hold for
{\mathbb{O}}_{2}
. Hence,
{\mathbb{O}}_{1}
is birationally rigid if and only if
{\mathbb{O}}_{2}
is.

∎

{\mathbb{O}}_{5}
,
\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{5}}^{E_{8}}(\breve{{\mathbb{O}}}_{5})
must be a subgroup of
{\mathsf{S}}_{2}\times{\mathsf{S}}_{3}
of index 3. This forces

\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{5}}^{E_{8}}(\breve{{\mathbb{O}}}_{5}))={\mathsf{S}}_{2}\times{\mathsf{S}}_{2}.

Recall that
\breve{{\mathbb{O}}}_{5}
is not birationally rigid, but is instead birationally induced from the rigid induction datum whose Levi subgroup has type
D_{6}
.

Recall that nilpotent covers of birationally rigid nilpotent covers are birationally rigid in this case. This implies that, since
{\mathsf{S}}_{2}
corresponds to a birationally induced nilpotent cover of
{\mathbb{O}}
, all overgroups of
{\mathsf{S}}_{2}
correspond to birationally induced nilpotent covers of
{\mathbb{O}}
. The only remaining overgroups of
{\mathsf{S}}_{2}
whose corresponding nilpotent cover we have not yet determined are
{\mathsf{S}}_{3}
,
{\mathsf{D}}_{8}
, and
{\mathsf{S}}_{4}
. Furthermore, there are three more birationally rigid induction data for covers of
{\mathbb{O}}
for which we have yet to determine the equivariant fundamental groups of their images under birational induction:
(L_{2},{\mathbb{O}}_{2})
,
(L_{3},\widehat{{\mathbb{O}}}_{3})
, and
(L_{7},{\mathbb{O}}_{7})
. We therefore have

\{\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{2}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{2})),\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{3}}^{E_{8}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{O}}}_{3})),\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{7}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{7}))\}=\{{\mathsf{S}}_{3},{\mathsf{D}}_{8},{\mathsf{S}}_{4}\},

and what remains is to determine which of these birationally induced nilpotent covers corresponds to which of these subgroups.

Since

\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{3}}^{E_{8}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{O}}}_{3}))\leq\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{3}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{3}))=\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{5}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{5}))={\mathsf{S}}_{2}\times{\mathsf{S}}_{3},

the remaining options for
\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{3}}^{E_{8}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{O}}}_{3}))
whose birationally rigid induction data aren’t known are
{\mathsf{S}}_{3}
,
{{\mbox{\rm tw}}}({\mathsf{S}}_{3})
,
{\mathsf{A}}_{3}
or
{{\mbox{\rm tw}}}({\mathsf{S}}_{2})
. Since we precisely know which subgroups can arise from the such data, the only possibility is

\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{3}}^{E_{8}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{O}}}_{3}))={\mathsf{S}}_{3}.

We therefore have

\{\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{2}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{2})),\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{7}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{7}))\}=\{{\mathsf{D}}_{8},{\mathsf{S}}_{4}\}.

Since
{\mathsf{S}}_{2}\times{\mathsf{S}}_{2}=\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{5}}^{E_{8}}(\breve{{\mathbb{O}}}_{5}))=\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{7}}^{E_{8}}(\widehat{{\mathbb{O}}}_{7}))
is an index 2 subgroup of
\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{7}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{7}))
by Proposition
2.3
, we must have

\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{7}}^{E_{8}}({\mathbb{O}}_{7}))={\mathsf{D}}_{8}.

Finally, by process of elimination we get

\pi_{1}^{G}({{\mbox{\rm Bind}}}_{L_{2}}^{G}({\mathbb{O}}_{2}))={\mathsf{S}}_{4}.

The remaining covers are all birationally rigid.

On a Weighted Series of the Hurwitz Zeta Function
Matthew Fox, Chaitanya Karamchedu

Abstract
. In this note we prove that for all a ∈ ℕ a\in\mathbb{N} , x ∈ ℝ + ∪ { 0 } x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\} , and s ∈ ℂ s\in\mathbb{C} with ℜ ⁡ ( s ) > a + 2 \Re(s)>a+2 , the (alternating) weighted series of the Hurwitz zeta function, ∑ k ≥ 1 ( ± 1 ) k ​ ( k + x ) a ​ ζ ​ ( s , k + x ) , \sum_{k\geq 1}(\pm 1)^{k}(k+x)^{a}\zeta(s,k+x), resolves into a finite combination of Hurwitz (Lerch) zeta functions. This applies in Marichal and Zenaïdi’s theory on analogues of the Bohr-Mollerup theorem for higher-order convex functions.



1.
Introduction

The Bohr-Mollerup theorem proves that the only logarithmically convex functions
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
that satisfy
f(x+1)=xf(x)
are the positive constant multiples of the gamma function,
\Gamma(x)
. The equivalent, but additive formulation of this theorem is the following, wherein we write
\Delta
for the unit-step forward difference operator
\Delta:f(x)\mapsto f(x+1)-f(x)
.

Theorem 1

(Bohr-Mollerup
[
3
,
4
]
)
.

All convex solutions
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f(x)=\ln x
are of the form
f(x)=c+\ln\Gamma(x)
for some constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

It is natural to consider analogues of this theorem for different difference equations as well as alternative function properties
\mathfrak{p}
such as monotonicity or higher-order convexity. Indeed, given function properties
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
as well as a function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mathfrak{p}
, it is evidently in the spirit of the Bohr-Mollerup theorem to ask: Does there exist a function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mathfrak{q}
and the difference equation
\Delta f(x)=g(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
? If so, is
f
unique modulo an additive constant? And, if so, does
g
completely determine
f
?

Krull was first to address such questions for eventually convex (concave) functions, which are functions that are convex (concave) on a neighborhood of infinity. Indeed, Krull proves that for any eventually convex (concave) function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying, for all integers
p>0
,

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\Delta^{p}g(x)=0,

(1)

there exists a unique, eventually concave (convex) solution
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f(x)=g(x)
, modulo an additive constant
[
8
,
9
]
. In addition, Krull also establishes that
g
completely determines
f
, and even provides the exact functional relationship. Later, but independently, Webster established the multiplicative version of Krull’s results
[
13
,
14
]
.

While Krull and Webster’s work constitute a considerable generalization of the Bohr-Mollerup theorem to a broader class of functions, the asymptotic condition (
1
) is extremely restrictive and is not satisfied by even simple functions like
g(x)=x\ln x
or apposite functions like
g(x)=\ln\Gamma(x)
(see Theorem
1
). Thus, one naturally seeks to weaken it.

In their monograph
[
11
]
, Marichal and Zenaïdi do exactly this, and in consequence extend the Bohr-Mollerup theorem to an even broader class of functions. Specifically, their results envelop the real-valued, eventually
p
-convex (
p
-concave) functions for every
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{-1\}
, where
\mathbb{N}
includes
0
. Such “higher-order” convex and concave functions naturally appear in Newton interpolation theory
[
5
]
; they are defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{-1\}
and let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an open and convex set. A function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
p
-convex
(
p
-concave
) on
I
if and only if for all systems
x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{p+1}
of
p+2
points in
I
, the divided difference of
f
at
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{p+1}
is non-negative (non-positive)
[
10
,
11
]
. Thus, a
1
-convex
(
1
-concave
) function is an ordinary convex (concave) function, a
0
-convex
(
0
-concave
) function is a weakly monotone increasing (decreasing) function, and a
-1
-convex
(
-1
-concave
) function is a non-negative (non-positive) function. Additionally, we say
f
is
eventually
p
-convex
(
eventually
p
-concave
) if and only if there exists a convex neighborhood of infinity
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
on which
f
is
p
-convex (
p
-concave).

We now state Marichal and Zenaïdi’s main theorem on analogues of the Bohr-Mollerup theorem for higher-order convex and concave functions.

Theorem 2

(Marichal-Zenaïdi
[
11
]
)
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose that the function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is eventually
p
-convex (
p
-concave) and has the asymptotic property that the sequence
n\mapsto\Delta^{p}g(n)
converges to zero. Then there exists a unique, eventually
p
-concave (
p
-convex) solution
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f(x)=g(x)
, modulo an additive constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
g
uniquely determines
f
through the equation

f(x)=c-g(x)+\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\big(g(k)-g(k+x)\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\binom{x}{j}\Delta^{j-1}g(n)\right].

(2)

In expounding their theory, Marichal and Zenaïdi compose a compendium of analogues of Theorem
1
for a variety of important functions, including offshoots of the gamma function like the polygamma,
q
-gamma, and Barnes
G
functions, but also, interestingly, more disparate functions like the Catalan numbers function, the generalized Stieltjes constants, and the Hurwitz zeta function. All of these analogues follow from Theorem
2
.

In particular, for the Hurwitz zeta function

\zeta(s,x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+x)^{s}}

with real
s>1
, they establish the following uniqueness result.

Proposition 1

(Marichal-Zenaïdi
[
11
]
)
.

For all real
s>1
, all eventually monotone (that is,
0
-convex or
0
-concave) solutions
f_{s}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s}(x)=-x^{-s}
are of the form
f_{s}(x)=c_{s}+\zeta(s,x)
for some constant
c_{s}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Together with the function property of eventual monotonicity, they also establish a reciprocal result wherein instead of characterizing
\zeta(s,x)
as the unique, eventually monotone
solution
to a particular difference equation, they characterize the unique, eventually monotone solutions
f_{s}
to the difference equation that
evaluates
to
\zeta(s,x)
, namely
\Delta f_{s}(x)=\zeta(s,x)
.

Proposition 2

(Marichal-Zenaïdi
[
11
]
)
.

For all real
s>2
, all eventually monotone solutions
f_{s}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s}(x)=\zeta(s,x)
are of the form

f_{s}(x)=c_{s}+\zeta(s-1)-\sum_{k\geq 0}\zeta(s,k+x)

(3)

for some constant
c_{s}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Incidentally, since for every real
s>1
the Hurwitz zeta function
\zeta(s,x)
is convex in
x
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, it is not hard to prove the following generalization of Proposition
2
.

Proposition 3
.

For all real
s>2
and
p\in\{0,1\}
, all eventually
p
-concave solutions
f_{s}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s}(x)=\zeta(s,x)
are of the form (
3
).

As a corollary to Propositions
1
and
2
, Marichal and Zenaïdi derive a myriad of intriguing identities involving the Hurwitz zeta function, including

\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta(s,k)=\zeta(s-1).

(4)

We note that while Marichal and Zenaïdi obtain Eq. (
4
) assuming real
s>2
, their identity readily extends to complex
s
in the connected open set
\Omega_{\epsilon}\coloneqq\{s\in\mathbb{C}:\Re(s)>2+\epsilon\}
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
. Indeed, since
\zeta(s-1)
and the partial sums
\big\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta(s,k):n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}
are holomorphic on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
, and since

\sup_{s\in\Omega_{\epsilon}}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta(s,k)\right|<\sum_{k=1}^{n}\zeta(2+\epsilon,k)

(5)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, together Eq. (
4
) and the Weierstrass M-test imply
\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta(s,k)
converges uniformly on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
. Consequently,
\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta(s,k)
is holomorphic on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
, and so, by the identity theorem, Eq. (
4
) holds for all
s\in\Omega_{\epsilon}
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
.

Of course, it is natural to wonder if this argument can accommodate
\epsilon=0
. However, without a stronger bound in Eq. (
5
), the holomorphy of
\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta(s,k)
does not readily extend. In
[
12
]
, however, Paris circumvents this issue by explicitly evaluating the LHS of Eq. (
4
) via the integral representation of
\zeta(s,x)
. Ultimately, he proves that Eq. (
4
) holds on
\Omega_{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon\geq 0
, as desired.

In the same paper
[
12
]
, Paris also investigates the more general series

\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pm 1)^{k}k^{a}\zeta(s,k+x)

(6)

for
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
in the non-alternating (
+
) case and
\Re(s)>a+1
in the alternating (
-
) case.

With
a=0
in the non-alternating case, Paris’ sum (
6
) appears in Eq. (
3
), and in particular controls the functional dependence on
x
of the solutions
f_{s}(x)
in Propositions
2
and
3
. By his same method of explicitly evaluating the sum via the integral representation of
\zeta(s,x)
, Paris solves Eq. (
6
) exactly for
x=0
and
a\in\{0,1,2,3\}
in the non-alternating case, and for
x=0
and
a\in\{0,1,2\}
in the alternating case. In each evaluation, he finds that the series equals some finite combination of Hurwitz zeta functions. This begs the question if that pattern holds in general, for all
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
.

To address this question, we consider the following generalization of Paris’ sum,

\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)\coloneqq\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pm 1)^{k}(k+x)^{a}\zeta(s,k+x),

(7)

where
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
. Note that our sum subsumes Paris’ sum (
6
) whenever ours absolutely converges, because in this case the expression
(k+x)^{a}
can be expanded into a finite combination of Paris’ sum, which can, in turn, be recursively simplified. Thus, a closed-form for
\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)
implies a closed-form for Eq. (
6
).

It is the purpose of this note to solve
\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)
exactly for all
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
in both the non-alternating and alternating cases. In particular, in line with the pattern that Paris observed for (
6
), we show that
\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)
can always be resolved into a finite combination of Hurwitz or Lerch zeta functions, depending on the alternation. Denoting by
B_{a}
the
a
th Bernoulli number (for which we adopt the convention
B_{1}=+\frac{1}{2}
),
B_{a}(x)
the
a
th Bernoulli polynomial,
E_{a}
the
a
th Euler number,
E_{a}(x)
the
a
th Euler polynomial, and
L(\lambda,s,x)
the Lerch zeta function,

L(\lambda,s,x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\exp(2\pi i\lambda n)}{(n+x)^{s}},

(8)

our main results are as follows.

Theorem 3
.

If
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
, then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
,

\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,x)=-\frac{1}{a+1}B_{a+1}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+\frac{1}{a+1}\sum_{j=0}^{a+1}\binom{a+1}{j}B_{j}\zeta(s-a+j-1,x+1).

Theorem 4
.

If
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
, then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\},

\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)=-\frac{1}{2}E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)-\sum_{j=0}^{a}\sum_{\ell=0}^{a-j}\frac{E_{j}}{2^{a-\ell+1}}\binom{a}{j}\binom{a-j}{\ell}L(-1,s-\ell,x+1).

Note that while
\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)
converges when
\Re(s)>a+1
, our work leaves open how to simplify
\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)
for
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)\in(a+1,a+2]
.

Interestingly, if
x=0
, then the form of
\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,x)
simplifies considerably; in particular, it resolves into a finite combination of Riemann zeta functions:

\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,0)=\sum_{k\geq 1}k^{a}\zeta(s,k)=\frac{1}{a+1}\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom{a+1}{j}B_{j}\zeta(s-a+j-1).

Importantly, this identity and the more general non-alternating sum
\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,x)
are relevant in Marichal and Zenaïdi’s theory on analogues of the Bohr-Mollerup theorem, as the next proposition shows.

Proposition 4
.

Let
Q(x)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}x+\cdots+\omega_{\ell}x^{\ell}
be a real, degree-
\ell
polynomial with
\omega_{\ell}\neq 0
. If
\omega_{\ell}>0
(
\omega_{\ell}<0
), then for all real
s>\ell+2
and
p\in\{0,1\}
, all eventually
p
-concave (
p
-convex) solutions
f_{s,Q}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s,Q}(x)=Q(x)\zeta(s,x)
are of the form

f_{s,Q}(x)=c_{s,Q}-\sum_{a=0}^{\ell}\omega_{a}x^{a}\zeta(s,x)+\sum_{a=0}^{\ell}\omega_{a}\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,0)-\sum_{a=0}^{\ell}\omega_{a}\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,x)

for some constant
c_{s,Q}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Of course, there exist many more generalizations of Paris’ sum, including in particular

\sum_{k\geq 1}f(k,x)\zeta(s,g(k,x))

(9)

for functions
f,g:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. As noted by Paris
[
12
]
, sums of this form bear a discretized resemblance to integrals like
\int_{\Omega}f(\alpha)\zeta(s,g(\alpha))\,\mathrm{d}\alpha,
which were studied extensively in
[
6
,
7
]
for
\Omega=(0,1)
and various
f
and
g
. While our results resolve (
9
) whenever
f
is a polynomial of
k+x
and
g(k,x)=k+x
, they do not for more general
f
and
g
, at least not obviously.

2.
Proofs of Results

In this section we prove Propositions
3
and
4
as well as Theorems
3
and
4
. These results rely on a handful of intermediate lemmas. Note that Lemmas
1
,
4
, and
5
are trivial, so we deliberately forgo their proofs.

Lemma 1
.

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>1
, the Hurwitz zeta function satisfies the shift property

\zeta(s,k+x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+k+x)^{s}}=\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{1}{(n+x)^{s}}.

The following is a generalization of both Propositions
2
and
3
. In particular, Proposition
3
follows with
a=0
and
\omega=1
.

Lemma 2
.

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with
\omega\neq 0
. If
\omega>0
(
\omega<0
), then for all real
s>\ell+2
and
p\in\{0,1\}
, all eventually
p
-concave (
p
-convex) solutions
f_{s,a}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s,a}(x)=\omega x^{a}\zeta(s,x)
are of the form

f_{s,a}(x)=c_{s,a}-\omega x^{a}\zeta(s,x)+\omega\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,0)-\omega\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a,x)

(10)

for some constant
c_{s,a}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
s>a+2
, and
p\in\{0,1\}
, and suppose
\omega>0
(
\omega<0
). Then the function
\omega x^{a}\zeta(s,x)
is
p
-convex (
p
-concave) in
x
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover, by Lemma
1
and the bound
s>a+2
, the sequence
n\mapsto\omega\Delta^{p}\big(n^{a}\zeta(s,n)\big)
converges to zero as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Thus, if
g_{s,a}(x)\coloneqq\omega x^{a}\zeta(s,x)
, then the premises of Theorem
2
are satisfied. Therefore, Eq. (
2
) implies that Eq. (
10
) constitutes the unique, eventually
p
-concave (
p
-convex) solutions
f_{s,a}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s,a}(x)=g_{s,a}(x)
, as desired.
∎

Note, for a real, degree-
\ell
polynomial
Q(x)=\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}x+\cdots+\omega_{\ell}x^{\ell}
with
\omega_{\ell}\neq 0
, if
s>1
and
\omega_{\ell}>0
(
\omega_{\ell}<0
), then, for all
p\in\{0,1\}
, the function
Q(x)\zeta(s,x)
is eventually
p
-convex (
p
-concave) in
x
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence, for fixed
s>\ell+2
and all
p\in\{0,1\}
, Theorem
2
and Lemma
2
imply that the unique, eventually
p
-concave (
p
-convex) solutions
f_{s,Q}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to the difference equation
\Delta f_{s,Q}(x)=Q(x)\zeta(s,x)
are of the form
f_{s,Q}(x)=\sum_{a=0}^{\ell}f_{s,a}(x)
. This is Proposition
4
.

Lemma 3
.

If
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
, then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
,

\mathrm{abs}(\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x))\coloneqq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left|\frac{(k+x)^{a}}{(n+k+x)^{s}}\right|<\infty.

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
. As
a\geq 0
,
\mathrm{abs}(\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x))
satisfies

\mathrm{abs}(\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x))\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+k+x)^{\Re(s)-a}}.

Moreover, since
\Re(s)>a+2
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\Re(s)-a\geq 2+\epsilon
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{abs}(\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+k+x)^{2+\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta(2+\epsilon,k+x).

As
x\geq 0
,
\zeta(2+\epsilon,k+x)\leq\zeta(2+\epsilon,k)
for every
k\geq 1
. Hence,

\mathrm{abs}(\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x))\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta(2+\epsilon,k)=\zeta(1+\epsilon),

where the equality follows from Eq. (
4
). Since
\epsilon>0
, the RHS is finite.
∎

Therefore, under the conditions of Lemma
3
, we can permute the terms in
\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)
with impunity, including the terms that arise from different Hurwitz zeta functions. In particular, Lemmas
1
and
3
imply that
\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)
satisfies

\mathcal{Z}_{\pm}(s,a,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pm 1)^{k}\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{(k+x)^{a}}{(n+x)^{s}}

(11)

whenever
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
.

Lemma 4
.

The Bernoulli polynomials satisfy
B_{a}(x+1)=ax^{a-1}+B_{a}(x)
for all
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

[
1
,
2
]
. Therefore, if
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

B_{a}(k+x+1)=B_{a}(x)+a\sum_{j=0}^{k}(j+x)^{a-1}.

Lemma 5
.

The Euler polynomials satisfy
E_{a}(x+1)=2x^{a}-E_{a}(x)
for all
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

[
1
]
. Therefore, if
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then

(-1)^{k}E_{a}(k+x+1)=E_{a}(x+1)+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}(j+x)^{a}.

Lemma 6
.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, the Dirichlet-Hurwitz series of the Bernoulli polynomials satisfies

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(k+x)}{(k+x)^{s}}=-a\zeta(s-a+1,x+1)+\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom{a}{j}B_{j}\zeta(s-a+j,x+1).

Proof.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
. With
B_{1}=+\frac{1}{2}
, the Bernoulli polynomials satisfy
[
1
,
2
]

B_{a}(x)=-ax^{a-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom{a}{j}B_{j}x^{a-j}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(k+x)}{(k+x)^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{(k+x)^{s}}\left(-a(k+x)^{a-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom{a}{j}B_{j}(k+x)^{a-j}\right)

\displaystyle=-a\zeta(s-a+1,x+1)+\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom{a}{j}B_{j}\zeta(s-a+j,x+1),

where the rearrangement is justified because
\Re(s)>a+2
, so the series over the Bernoulli polynomials absolutely converges.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Fix
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)>a+2
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
, the alternating Dirichlet-Hurwitz series of the Euler polynomials satisfies

\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}E_{a}(k+x+1)}{(k+x)^{s}}=\sum_{j=0}^{a}\sum_{\ell=0}^{a-j}\frac{E_{j}}{2^{a-\ell}}\binom{a}{j}\binom{a-j}{\ell}L(-1,s-\ell,x+1).

Proof.

The Euler polynomials satisfy

Proof of Theorem
3

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
and consider the quantity
B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+a\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)
, where
a-1\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Re(s)>a+1
. The premises of Lemma
3
hold, so we are allowed to permute terms with impunity. In particular, by Eq. (
11
),

B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+a\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(x+1)}{{(k+x)^{s}}}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{a(k+x)^{a-1}}{(n+x)^{s}}.

Combining terms with like-denominators gives

B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+a\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(x+1)+a\sum_{j=1}^{k}(j+x)^{a-1}}{(k+x)^{s}}.

Then, by the Bernoulli polynomial recurrence
B_{a}(x+1)=B_{a}(x)+ax^{a-1}
,

B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+a\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(x)+a\sum_{j=0}^{k}(j+x)^{a-1}}{(k+x)^{s}}.

By Lemma
4
, we identify the numerator as
B_{a}(k+x+1)
. Therefore,

B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+a\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(k+x+1)}{(k+x)^{s}},

which, after a further application of the recurrence
B_{a}(x+1)=B_{a}(x)+ax^{a-1}
, yields

B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+a\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)=a\zeta(s-a+1,x+1)+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{B_{a}(k+x)}{(k+x)^{s}}.

Consequently,
\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)
is related to the Dirichlet-Hurwitz series of the Bernoulli polynomials. It thus follows from Lemma
6
that

\mathcal{Z}_{+}(s,a-1,x)=-\frac{1}{a}B_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)+\frac{1}{a}\sum_{j=0}^{a}\binom{a}{j}B_{j}\zeta(s-a+j,x+1).

Taking
a\rightarrow a+1
gives Theorem
3
.∎

Proof of Theorem
4

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
and consider the quantity
E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)-2\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)
, where
a\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Re(s)>a+2
. The premises of Lemma
3
hold, so we are allowed to permute terms with impunity. In particular, by Eq. (
11
),

E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)-2\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{E_{a}(x+1)}{(k+x)^{s}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pm 1)^{k}\sum_{n\geq k}\frac{2(k+x)^{a}}{(n+x)^{s}}.

Combining terms with like-denominators gives

E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)-2\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{E_{a}(x+1)+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j+1}(j+x)^{a}}{(k+x)^{s}}.

By Lemma
5
, the terms in the numerator are related to the Euler polynomials. In particular,

E_{a}(x+1)+2\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j+1}(j+x)^{a}=(-1)^{k+1}E_{a}(k+x+1)+2E_{a}(x+1).

Therefore,

E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)-2\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}E_{a}(k+x+1)}{(k+x)^{s}}+2E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1).

Consequently,
\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)
is related to the alternating Dirichlet-Hurwitz series of the Euler polynomials. It thus follows from Lemma
7
that

\mathcal{Z}_{-}(s,a,x)=-\frac{1}{2}E_{a}(x+1)\zeta(s,x+1)-\sum_{j=0}^{a}\sum_{\ell=0}^{a-j}\frac{E_{j}}{2^{a-\ell+1}}\binom{a}{j}\binom{a-j}{\ell}L(-1,s-\ell,x+1),

as desired. ∎

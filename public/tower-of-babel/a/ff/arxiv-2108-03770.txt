Abstract
In this paper, we construct the wavelet eigenvalue regression methodology (Abry and Didier [ abry:didier:2018:dim2 , abry:didier:2018:n-variate ] ) in high dimensions. We assume that possibly non-Gaussian, finite-variance p p -variate measurements are made of a low-dimensional r r -variate ( r ≪ p r\ll p ) fractional stochastic process with non-canonical scaling coordinates and in the presence of additive high-dimensional noise. The measurements are correlated both time-wise and between rows. Building upon the asymptotic and large scale properties of wavelet random matrices in high dimensions, the wavelet eigenvalue regression is shown to be consistent and, under additional assumptions, asymptotically Gaussian in the estimation of the fractal structure of the system. We further construct a consistent estimator of the effective dimension r r of the system that significantly increases the robustness of the methodology. The estimation performance over finite samples is studied by means of simulations.

2.1
Wavelet analysis

Throughout the paper, we make use of a wavelet multiresolution analysis (MRA; see Mallat
[
mallat:1999
]
, chapter 7), which decomposes
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
into a sequence of
approximation
(low-frequency) and
detail
(high-frequency) subspaces
V_{j}
and
W_{j}
, respectively, associated with different scales of analysis
2^{j}
. In almost all mathematical statements, we make assumptions
(W1-W4)
on the underlying wavelet MRA. Such assumptions are accurately described in Section
A
. In particular, we make use of a compactly supported wavelet basis.

So, let
\phi
and
\psi
be the scaling and wavelet functions, respectively, associated with the wavelet MRA. We further suppose the wavelet coefficients stem from Mallat’s pyramidal algorithm (Mallat
[
mallat:1999
]
, chapter 7). For expositional simplicity, in our description of the algorithm we use the
{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
-valued process
Y
, though analogous developments also hold for both
X
and
Z
. Initially, suppose an infinite time series

\{Y(k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}},

(2.4)

associated with the starting scale
2^{j}=1
(or octave
j=0
), is available. Then, we can apply Mallat’s algorithm to extract the so-named
approximation

(A(2^{j+1},\cdot))
and
detail

(D(2^{j+1},\cdot))
coefficients at coarser scales
2^{j+1}
by means of an iterative procedure. In fact, as commonly done in the wavelet literature, we initialize the algorithm with the process

{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\ni\widetilde{Y}(t):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Y(k)\phi(t-k),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.5)

By the orthogonality of the shifted scaling functions
\{\phi(\cdot-k)\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,

{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\ni A(2^{0},k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{Y}(t)\phi(t-k)dt=Y(k),\quad k\in{\mathbb{Z}}

(2.6)

(see Stoev et al.
[
stoev:pipiras:taqqu:2002
]
, proof of Lemma 6.1, or Moulines et al.
[
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2007:JTSA
]
, p. 160; cf. Abry and Flandrin
[
abry:flandrin:1994
]
, p. 33). In other words, the initial sequence, at octave
j=0
, of approximation coefficients is given by the original time series. To obtain approximation and detail coefficients at coarser scales, we use Mallat’s iterative procedure

A(2^{j+1},k)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{k^{\prime}-2k}A(2^{j},k^{\prime}),\quad D(2^{j+1},k)=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{k^{\prime}-2k}A(2^{j},k^{\prime}),\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.7)

where the (scalar) filter sequences
\{u_{k}:=2^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t/2)\phi(t-k)dt\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
\{v_{k}:=2^{-1/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(t/2)\phi(t-k)dt\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
are called low- and high-pass MRA filters, respectively. Due to the assumed compactness of the supports of
\psi
and of the associated scaling function
\phi
(see condition (
A.2
)), only a finite number of filter terms is nonzero, which is convenient for computational purposes (Daubechies
[
daubechies:1992
]
). Hereinafter, we assume without loss of generality that
\text{supp}(\phi)=\text{supp}(\psi)=[0,T]
(cf. Moulines et al
[
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2007:JTSA
]
, p. 160). Moreover, the wavelet (detail) coefficients
D(2^{j},k)
of
Y
can be expressed as

{\mathbb{R}}^{p}\ni D(2^{j},k)=\sum_{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Y(\ell)h_{j,2^{j}k-\ell},

(2.8)

where the filter terms are defined by

{\mathbb{R}}\ni h_{j,\ell}=2^{-j/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(t+\ell)\psi(2^{-j}t)dt.

(2.9)

If we replace (
2.4
) with the realistic assumption that only a finite length time series

\{Y(k)\}_{k=1,\ldots,n}

(2.10)

is available, writing
\widetilde{Y}^{(n)}(t):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}Y(k)\phi(t-k)
, we have
\widetilde{Y}^{(n)}(t)=\widetilde{Y}(t)
for all
t\in(T,n+1)
(cf. Moulines et al.
[
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2007:JTSA
]
). Noting
D(2^{j},k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{Y}(t)2^{-j/2}\psi(2^{-j}t-k)dt
and
D^{(n)}(2^{j},k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{Y}^{(n)}(t)2^{-j/2}\psi(2^{-j}t-k)dt
, it follows that the finite-sample wavelet coefficients
D^{(n)}(2^{j},k)
of
\widetilde{Y}^{(n)}(t)
are equal to
D(2^{j},k)
whenever
\textnormal{supp }\psi(2^{-j}\cdot-k)=(2^{j}k,2^{j}(k+T))\subseteq(T,n+1)
. In other words,

D^{(n)}(2^{j},k)=D(2^{j},k),\qquad\forall(j,k)\in\{(j,k):2^{-j}T\leq k\leq 2^{-j}(n+1)-T\}.

(2.11)

Equivalently, such subset of finite-sample wavelet coefficients is not affected by the so-named
border effect
(cf. Craigmile et al.
[
craigmile:guttorp:Percival:2005
]
, Percival and Walden
[
percival:walden:2006
]
, Didier and Pipiras
[
didier:pipiras:2010
]
). Moreover, by (
2.11
) the number of such coefficients at octave

is given by
n_{j}=\lfloor 2^{-j}(n+1-T)-T\rfloor
. Hence,
n_{j}\sim 2^{-j}n
for large

. Thus, for notational simplicity we suppose

n_{j}=\frac{n}{2^{j}}

(2.12)

holds exactly and only work with wavelet coefficients unaffected by the border effect.

M(p,r,{\mathbb{R}})\ni P(n)=\Big(\mathbf{p}_{1}(n),\ldots,\mathbf{p}_{r}(n)\Big),\quad\|\mathbf{p}_{q}(n)\|=1,\hskip 8.53581ptq=1,\ldots,r.

(2.13)

For the sake of clarity and mathematical generality, we state the appropriate conditions for the convergence in probability as well as for the asymptotic normality of wavelet log-eigenvalues directly in the wavelet domain. For

,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and a dyadic sequence

, the random vectors

(2.14)

The remaining wavelet random matrix terms

are naturally defined as

\mathbf{W}_{X}(a(n)2^{j}))=\frac{1}{n_{a,j}}\sum^{n_{a,j}}_{k=1}D_{X}(a(n)2^{j},k)D_{X}(a(n)2^{j},k)^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\geq 0}(r,{\mathbb{R}}),

Further define the auxiliary random matrix

\widehat{{\mathbf{B}}}_{a}(2^{j})=P_{H}^{-1}a(n)^{-H-(1/2)I}{\mathbf{W}}_{X}(a(n)2^{j})a(n)^{-H^{*}-(1/2)I}(P_{H}^{*})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\geq 0}(r,{\mathbb{R}}),

as well as its mean
{\mathbf{B}}_{a}(2^{j}):={\mathbb{E}}\widehat{{\mathbf{B}}}_{a}(2^{j})
. The matrix

Assumption

: The wavelet random matrix

+P(n){\mathbf{W}}_{X,Z}(a(n)2^{j})+{\mathbf{W}}^{*}_{X,Z}(a(n)2^{j})P^{*}(n)

(2.22)

(A5)

(A1-A3)
are stated in the wavelet domain. Assumption

holds under very general conditions. In fact, since

and

3
Main results

As mentioned in the Introduction, under the assumptions laid out in Section
2.2
the
r
largest eigenvalues of the random matrix
\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})
display
asymptotic
scaling relationships in high dimensions dictated by the eigenvalues of
H
(see (
1.6
)). Furthermore, under additional assumptions, the fluctuations of the rescaled wavelet log-eigenvalues around their limits are asymptotically Gaussian. For the reader’s convenience, the precise statements are provided in Theorems
B.1
and
B.2
. These facts indicate that the scaling eigenvalues (
1.4
) can be efficiently estimated in high dimensions through a linear regression based on the log-eigenvalues of
\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})
over a range of scales
2^{j_{1}},\ldots,2^{j_{2}}
, provided the scaling factor
a(n)
is sufficiently large. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.1

Let
\{{\mathbf{W}}(a(n)2^{j})\}_{j=j_{1},\ldots,j_{2}}
be the wavelet random matrices corresponding to scales
\{a(n)2^{j_{1}},\ldots,a(n)2^{j_{2}}\}
. Fix a range of octaves

j=j_{1},j_{1}+1,\ldots,j_{2},\quad m:=j_{2}-j_{1}+1.

(3.1)

The
wavelet eigenvalue regression
is given by

\{\widehat{\ell}_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,p(n)}:=\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{j=j_{1}}^{j_{2}}w_{j}\log_{2}\lambda_{i}\big({\mathbf{W}}(a(n)2^{j})\big)-1\Big)\Big\}_{i=1,\ldots,p(n)}.

(3.2)

In (
3.2
),
w_{j}
,
j=j_{1},\ldots,j_{2}
, are weights satisfying the relations

\sum^{j_{2}}_{j=j_{1}}w_{j}=0,\quad\sum^{j_{2}}_{j=j_{1}}jw_{j}=1,

(3.3)

where
w_{j}=1
if
m=1
(see (
3.1
)).

If
{\mathbf{W}}(a(n)2^{j})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{>0}(r,{\mathbb{R}})
, then the estimator (
3.2
) is well defined. Assuming for the moment that the dimension
r
of the hidden fractional process
X
is known, expression (
3.2
) based on the top
r
wavelet eigenvalues can be interpreted as wavelet eigenstructure estimators of the scaling eigenvalues, i.e., we can write

\{\widehat{h}_{q}\}_{q=1,\ldots,r}=\{\widehat{\ell}_{p(n)-r+q}\}_{q=1,\ldots,r}.

(3.4)

Hence, the estimator (
3.4
) can be viewed as a high-dimensional extension of the wavelet eigenvalue regression estimator first developed in Abry and Didier
[
abry:didier:2018:dim2
,
abry:didier:2018:n-variate
]
in a noiseless, low-dimensional context for ofBm.

In the following theorems, we characterize the asymptotic and large scale behavior of the wavelet eigenvalue regression estimator (
3.4
) in high dimensions, namely, its consistency and, under assumptions, asymptotic normality. Consistency is, indeed, a direct consequence of the convergence in probability of the
r
largest rescaled wavelet eigenvalues (cf. Theorem
B.1
). By contrast, establishing the asymptotic normality of the wavelet eigenvalue regression estimator goes beyond the asymptotic distribution of wavelet eigenvalues over large scales in high dimensions (cf. Theorem
B.2
). In fact, it also requires establishing the rate of convergence of the rescaled
r
largest eigenvalues of the deterministic wavelet matrices
{\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})
. In turn, this involves studying the rate of angular convergence of the associated (deterministic) wavelet eigenvectors in terms of the high-dimensional coordinates
P=P(n)
.

Theorem 3.1

Assume
(W1-W4)
and
(A1-A5)
hold and fix
j_{1},j_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j_{1}<j_{2}
. Then,

\Big(\widehat{h}_{q}-h_{q}\Big)_{q=1,\ldots,r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\rightarrow}}0,\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

(3.5)

Theorem 3.2

Suppose
(W1-W4)
and
(A1-A5)
hold, and fix
j_{1},j_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
j_{1}<j_{2}
. Further suppose one of the following conditions holds, namely,

(i)

either

-1/2<h_{1}<\ldots<h_{r}<\infty;

(3.6)

(ii)

or
h_{1}=\ldots=h_{r}
and the functions
\xi_{q}(2^{j})
in (
B.1
) satisfy

q_{1}\neq q_{2}\Rightarrow\xi_{q_{1}}(1)\neq\xi_{q_{2}}(1).

(3.7)

Then,

\sqrt{\frac{n}{a(n)}}\Big(\widehat{h}_{q}-h_{q}\Big)_{q=1,\ldots,r}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathcal{N}}(0,M\Sigma_{\lambda}M^{*}),

(3.8)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, for some weight matrix
M
(see (
C.37
)) and
\Sigma_{\lambda}
as in Theorem
B.2
.

Remark 3.1

Under non-simple scaling matrix eigenvalues and without condition (
3.7
), the asymptotic distribution of
\{\widehat{h}_{q}\}_{q=1,\ldots,r}
is generally expected to be non-Gaussian because, in this case, wavelet log-eigenvalues themselves are generally expected to be non-Gaussian (see the discussion in Abry et al. (2022), Remark 3.1). The broad characterization of the distribution of wavelet log-eigenvalues outside the framework of the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
is currently a topic of research.

In practice, the dimension
r
of the latent process
X
may not be known. In the following definition, we introduce an estimator of
r
based on the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})
.

Definition 3.2

Let
j_{1}<j_{2}
and consider any set of weights
v_{j_{1}},\ldots v_{j_{2}}\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying
\sum_{j=j_{1}}^{j_{2}}v_{j}=1
. Let

\Delta_{i}(j_{1},j_{2}):=\sum_{j=j_{1}}^{j_{2}}v_{j}\frac{\log\lambda_{i}\big(\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})\big)}{\log(a(n)2^{j})}

(3.9)

Given
\kappa>0
, we define

{\widehat{r}}(2^{j_{1}},2^{j_{2}},\kappa):=\#\{i:\Delta_{i}(j_{1},j_{2})>\kappa\}.

(3.10)

Now note that, under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
, the lowest

eigenvalues stay bounded. Hence, for
i\leq p-r
, the quantity
\Delta_{i}(j_{1},j_{2})
tends to zero in probability. On the other hand, for

, still under the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
,
\Delta_{i}(j_{1},j_{2})
converges to
2h_{i-(p-r)}+1>0
in probability, thereby separating the non-scaling and scaling eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{W}}(a(n)2^{j})
. This phenomenon lies behind the following theorem, which establishes the consistency of the estimator

Theorem 3.3

Let
\widehat{r}(2^{j_{1}},2^{j_{2}},\kappa)

) with

and suppose the assumptions of Theorem

hold. Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}\big(\widehat{r}(2^{j_{1}},2^{j_{2}},\kappa)=r\big)=1

(3.11)

Remark 3.2

In the context of the (univariate) wavelet regression, it is common practice to select the weights (
3.3
) to include information about the wavelet variance over the regression scales
2^{j_{1}},\ldots 2^{j_{2}}
(cf. Veitch and Abry
[
veitch:abry:1999
]
). This can lead to improved estimation performance. For convenience, in all simulations we set

entries. At each run, the columns of the coordinates matrix

5
Conclusion and open problems

In this paper, we construct the wavelet eigenvalue regression methodology (Abry and Didier
[
abry:didier:2018:dim2
,
abry:didier:2018:n-variate
]
) in high dimensions. We assume that possibly non-Gaussian, finite-variance
p
-variate measurements are made of a low-dimensional
r
-variate (
r\ll p
) fractional stochastic process with non-canonical scaling coordinates and in the presence of additive high-dimensional noise. The measurements are correlated both time-wise and between rows. Due to the asymptotic and large scale properties of large wavelet random matrices, the wavelet eigenvalue regression is shown to be consistent and (under additional assumptions) asymptotically Gaussian in the estimation of the fractal structure of the underlying measurements. We further construct a consistent estimator of the effective dimension

This research leads to many new research directions, some of which can be briefly described as follows.
(i)

Appendix A
Assumptions on the wavelet multiresolution analysis

In the main results of the paper, we make use of the following conditions on the underlying wavelet MRA.

Assumption
(W1)
:
\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is a wavelet function, namely, it satisfies the relations

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi^{2}(t)dt=1,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}t^{p}\psi(t)dt=0,\quad p=0,1,\ldots,N_{\psi}-1,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}t^{N_{\psi}}\psi(t)dt\neq 0,

(A.1)

for some integer (number of vanishing moments)
N_{\psi}\geq 1
.

Assumption (
W2
)
: the scaling and wavelet functions

\phi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\psi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
are compactly supported

(A.2)

and
\widehat{\phi}(0)=1
.

Assumption
(W3)
: there is
\alpha>1
such that

\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|\widehat{\psi}(x)|(1+|x|)^{\alpha}<\infty.

(A.3)

Assumption (
W4
)
: the function

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}k^{m}\phi(\cdot-k)

(A.4)

is a polynomial of degree
m
for all
m=0,\ldots,N_{\psi}-1
.

Conditions (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) imply that
\widehat{\psi}(x)
exists, is everywhere differentiable and its first
N_{\psi}-1
derivatives are zero at
x=0
. Condition (
A.3
), in turn, implies that
\psi
is continuous (see Mallat
[
mallat:1999
]
, Theorem 6.1) and, hence, bounded.

Note that assumptions (
W1-W4
) are closely related to the broad wavelet framework for the analysis of
\kappa
-th order (
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
) stationary increment stochastic processes laid out in Moulines et al.
[
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2007:Fractals
,
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2007:JTSA
,
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2008
]
and Roueff and Taqqu
[
roueff:taqqu:2009
]
. The Daubechies scaling and wavelet functions generally satisfy (
W1-W4
) (see Moulines et al.
[
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2008
]
, p. 1927, or Mallat
[
mallat:1999
]
, p. 253). Usually, the parameter
\alpha
increases to infinity as
N_{\psi}
goes to infinity (see Moulines et al.
[
moulines:roueff:taqqu:2008
]
, p. 1927, or Cohen
[
cohen:2003
]
, Theorem 2.10.1).

Appendix B
The asymptotic and large scale behavior of the eigenvalues of wavelet random matrices

In this section, we recap the main results in Abry et al.
[
abry:boniece:didier:wendt:2022:wavelet_eigenanalysis
]
. It was established that, after proper rescaling, the
r
largest eigenvalues of a wavelet random matrix
\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})
in high dimensions converge in probability to deterministic functions
\xi_{q}(2^{j})
,
q=1,\ldots,r
. Thus, such functions can be interpreted as
asymptotic rescaled eigenvalues
. Notably, they display a scaling property. Moreover, the remaining
p(n)-r
eigenvalues of a wavelet random matrix are bounded in probability.

Theorem B.1

(a consequence of Theorem 3.1 in Abry et al.
[
abry:boniece:didier:wendt:2022:wavelet_eigenanalysis
]
) Assume
(W1-W4)
and
(A1-A5)
hold, and fix
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, for
p=p(n)
, the limits

\plim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda_{p-r+q}\big(\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})\big)}{a(n)^{2h_{q}+1}}=:\xi_{q}(2^{j})>0,\quad q=1,\ldots,r,

(B.1)

exist, and the deterministic functions
\xi_{q}
satisfy the scaling relation

\xi_{q}(2^{j})=2^{j\hskip 1.42262pt(2h_{q}+1)}\xi_{q}(1).

(B.2)

In addition,

\lambda_{1}\big(\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})\big)\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{p-r}\big(\mathbf{W}(a(n)2^{j})\big)=O_{{\mathbb{P}}}(1).

(B.3)

Moreover, the asymptotic normality of the
r
largest wavelet log-eigenvalues in high dimensions was also established. The statement covers the central cases of simple (
h_{1}<\ldots<h_{r}
) and of identical (
h_{1}=\ldots=h_{r}
) scaling eigenvalues. Note that, by comparison to Theorem
B.1
, the asymptotic normality of wavelet log-eigenvalues requires some additional condition so as to ensure the finite-sample differentiability of the eigenvalues with respect to scaling parameters (see also Remark
3.1
).

Theorem B.2

(a consequence of Theorem 3.2 in Abry et al.
[
abry:boniece:didier:wendt:2022:wavelet_eigenanalysis
]
) Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
, for
p=p(n)
,

{\mathbb{R}}^{m\cdot r}\ni\Big(\sqrt{n_{a,j}}\Big(\log\lambda_{p-r+q}\big({\mathbf{W}}(a(n)2^{j})\big)-\log\lambda_{p-r+q}\big({\mathbb{E}}{\mathbf{W}}(a(n)2^{j})\big)\Big)_{q=1,\ldots,r}\Big)_{j=j_{1},\ldots,j_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathcal{N}}(0,\Sigma_{\lambda})

(B.4)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
\Sigma_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\geq 0}(m\cdot r,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Remark B.1

Note that Assumption
(A4)
(see (
2.23
) and (
2.24
)) implies the analogous Assumption (
A4)
of Abry et al.
[
abry:boniece:didier:wendt:2022:wavelet_eigenanalysis
]
, which in turn is assumed in Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 in Abry et al.
[
abry:boniece:didier:wendt:2022:wavelet_eigenanalysis
]
. The slightly stronger conditions (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), as well as (
2.25
), are needed in Theorem
3.2
in this paper, i.e., in the context of the wavelet eigenvalue regression.

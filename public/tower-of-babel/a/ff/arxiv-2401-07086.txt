Abstract
. On ℝ n , \mathbb{R}^{n}, a classical result due to Bourgain establishes the restricted weak ( n n − 1 , 1 ) (\frac{n}{n-1},1) inequality for the full maximal function M F d ​ σ M_{F}^{d\sigma} associated to the spherical averages. In this work we present an extension to Bourgain’s result on graded Lie groups for a family of full maximal operators. We formulate this extension using the group Fourier transform of the measures under consideration and the symbols of (positive Rockland operators which are) positive left-invariant hypoelliptic partial differential operators on graded Lie groups.

42B25, 22E27.

1.
Introduction

The aim of this work is to formulate certain boundedness properties for the full maximal function on graded Lie groups. Our main goal is to extend the restricted weak
(\frac{n}{n-1},1)
inequality due to Bourgain
[
4
]
for the full maximal function
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
associated to the spherical averages, to a family of full maximal functions on graded Lie groups. As a consequence of interpolating our (restricted weak estimate in the form of a certain)
L^{p,1}\rightarrow L^{p,\infty}
boundedness theorem with the corresponding
L^{\infty}
-boundedness result we immediately obtain the
L^{p,q}
-boundendess of these maximal operators on some Lorentz spaces. Consequently, when
p=q
we provide an extension to the
L^{p}
-boundedness result of
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
due to Stein
[
72
]
for the spherical averages when
p>\frac{n}{n-1},
to the setting of graded Lie groups.

Our main result is Theorem
1.2
below. Our approach will be to identify the information on the problem coming from both, harmonic analysis and representation theory of nilpotent Lie groups. Since we consider graded Lie groups, several techniques from operator theory will be used to deal with the spectral calculus of Rockland operators (which are positive left-invariant hypoelliptic partial differential operators).

1.1.
Short review of the Euclidean setting

To present our main result we briefly discuss the Euclidean setting.
Consider the full maximal function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

{M}^{d\sigma}_{F}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\left|\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(x-ry)d\sigma(y)\right|,\,n\geq 2,

(1.1)

where
d\sigma
is the surface measure on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.
For
n\geq 3,
Stein in
[
72
]
proved the boundedness of

{M}^{d\sigma}_{F}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(1.2)

for all
p>\frac{n}{n-1},
and Bourgain in
[
4
]
also proved that

{M}^{d\sigma}_{F}:L^{\frac{n}{n-1},1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(1.3)

is bounded. Here we recall that the boundedness property in (
1.3
) is equivalent to the restricted weak
(\frac{n}{n-1},1)
boundeness of
M_{F}^{d\sigma}.
We also observe that Stein in
[
72
]
proved that
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
is unbounded
when
p\leq\frac{n}{n-1},
and
n\geq 2
. Then, Bourgain
[

]
proved the boundedness of
M_{F}^{d\sigma},
when

More precisely, Bourgain showed that

{M}^{d\sigma}_{F}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

In view of the boundedness theorem in (

Several other

1.2.
The full maximal function
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
and admissible measures

We consider the full maximal function

M_{F}^{d\sigma}f(x)=\sup_{t>0}\left|\smallint\limits_{G}f\left(x\cdot(t\cdot y)^{-1}\right)d\sigma(y)\right|,

(1.4)

associated to an arbitrary finite Borel measure
d\sigma
with compact support on
G.

In Definition
1.1
below we describe the measures
d\sigma
considered in this work.

Our main motivation is to reformulate the
curvature conditions
available in the Euclidean setting in terms of group Fourier transform type conditions. One reason for this is that these two approaches are equivalent in the Euclidean setting, see e.g
[
69
, Page 622]
. Here,
\widehat{G}
denotes the unitary dual of a graded Lie group
G,
and
\pi\mapsto\widehat{d\sigma}(\pi)
denotes the operator-valued group Fourier transform of
d\sigma
at
\pi\in\widehat{G}.
We refer the reader to Section
2
for details about the Fourier analysis on graded Lie groups. A concise presentation about the properties of Rockland operators can be found in
[
38
, Chapter 4]
. In Section
3
we motivate the
curvature conditions
(ii) and (iii) of Definition
1.1
taking intuition from Fefferman’s discussion in
[
36
, Page 527]
.

Definition 1.1

(Admissible measures)
.

Let
d\sigma
be a finite Borel measure of compact support on a graded Lie group
G
of homogeneous dimension
Q.
Let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator on
G
of homogeneous degree
\nu>0.

We say that
d\sigma
is
(Q,Q_{0},\varepsilon_{0},a)
-
admissible
(or
admissible
for short) if it satisfies the topological property (i) and the “curvature properties” (ii) and (iii) below.

(i)

Let
K
be the support of
d\sigma.
For any
\beta>0,
and each
x\in G,
the measure
\sigma(\mathbb{S}_{\beta})
of the set

\mathbb{S}_{\beta}(x)=\{y\in K:|xy^{-1}|\sim\beta\},

(1.5)

satisfies the growth estimate
1
1

1

Note that in the case where
\mathbb{S}_{\beta}(x)=\emptyset,

\sigma(\mathbb{S}_{\beta}(x))=0.

\sigma(\mathbb{S}_{\beta}(x))\leq\beta^{Q_{0}},

(1.6)

for some
Q_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
0\leq Q_{0}<Q.

(ii)

The group Fourier transform of
d\sigma
satisfies the growth estimate

\sup_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}\|\pi(\mathcal{R})^{\frac{a}{\nu}}\widehat{d\sigma}(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}<\infty,

(1.7)

for some order

(iii)

The following Tauberian inequality

\sup_{s>0;\pi\in\widehat{G}}\|s\frac{d}{ds}\{\widehat{d\sigma}(s\cdot\pi)\}[(s\cdot\pi)(\mathcal{R})]^{\frac{a-\varepsilon_{0}}{\nu}}\|_{\textnormal{op}}<\infty,

(1.8)

holds for some
\varepsilon_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying that

We discuss the differentiability of the mapping
s\mapsto\frac{d}{ds}\{\widehat{d\sigma}(s\cdot\pi)\},
in Subsection
3.0.2
. Indeed, as expected one can define it as a densely defined operator on each representation space.

1.3.
Estimates for
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
on graded Lie groups

Next, we present the main result of this work, that is the extension to the (restricted weak
(\frac{n}{n-1},1)
inequality)
L^{\frac{n}{n-1},1}\rightarrow L^{\frac{n}{n-1},\infty}
estimate by Bourgain (
1.3
) and the
L^{p}
-estimate of Stein (
1.2
) to the setting of graded Lie groups.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\sigma
be a finite Borel measure of compact support on a graded Lie group
G
of homogeneous dimension
Q.
Assume that
d\sigma
is
(Q,Q_{0},\varepsilon_{0},a)
-admissible (as in Definition
1.1
). Let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator on
G
of homogeneous degree
\nu>0.
Let
c:=Q-Q_{0}>0,
and let

D:=2a-\varepsilon_{0}+2c.

(1.9)

Then
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
has the following continuity properties:

(1)

M_{F}^{d\sigma}:L^{\frac{D}{D-c},1}(G)\rightarrow L^{\frac{D}{D-c},\infty}(G)
admits a bounded extension (equivalently,
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
is of restricted weak
(\frac{D}{D-c},1)
type).

(2)

M_{F}^{d\sigma}:L^{p}(G)\rightarrow L^{p}(G)
is bounded for all
\frac{D}{D-c}<p\leq\infty.

Now we briefly discuss the previous result. Its proof is presented in Section
4
.

Remark 1.3
.

In Theorem
1.2
, the boundedness of
M_{F}^{d\sigma}:L^{\frac{D}{D-c},1}(G)\rightarrow L^{\frac{D}{D-c},\infty}(G),
at the end-point
p=\frac{D}{D-c},
is the main estimate. Consequently, the
L^{p}
-estimate in (2) follows by interpolation of (1) with the boundedness of
M_{F}^{d\sigma}:L^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(G).
Moreover, by the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem (for sub-linear operators on Lorentz spaces) due to Hunt
[
48
, Page 804]
2
2

2

A linear version of this theorem was proved by Calderón in
[
8
]
.
, we have that

\boxed{M_{F}^{d\sigma}:L^{p,q}(G)\rightarrow L^{p,s}(G)}

is bounded for all
\frac{D}{D-c}<p\leq\infty,
and for all
1\leq q\leq s\leq\infty.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that we have the inequality

\|M_{F}^{d\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{p,d\sigma}\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)},

(1.10)

for all
\frac{D}{D-c}<p\leq\infty,

and the set

\{f\in L^{p}(G):\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\smallint\limits_{G}f\left(x\cdot(t\cdot y)^{-1}\right)d\sigma(y)=f(x),\,a.e.\}

is closed in
L^{p}(G),
see e.g. Theorem 2.2 of Duoandikoetxea
[
35
, Page 27]
. We can apply this very well-known remark to prove the differentiation criterion

\forall f\in L^{p}(G),\,\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\smallint\limits_{G}f\left(x\cdot(t\cdot y)^{-1}\right)d\sigma(y)=f(x),\,\textnormal{a. e.}

(1.11)

In (
1.11
) we have assumed that the measure
d\sigma
is normalised
\sigma(G)=1.
In some sense the previous result is a noncommutative version of the Stein differentiation theorem, see Section
3
for details.

Remark 1.5
.

We observe that in the Euclidean setting the hypotheses in Theorem
1.2
are satisfied e.g. in the case of the surface measure
d\sigma
on the sphere. Indeed, when
G=\mathbb{R}^{n},

Q=n,

Q_{0}=n-1,

c=1,

a=\frac{n-1}{2},
and
\varepsilon_{0}=1.
In consequence
D
in (
1.9
) agrees with the topological dimension
n.
We then recover the classical range
\frac{n}{n-1}<p\leq\infty,
in the
L^{p}
-boundendess of the maximal function associated to the spherical averages.

Remark 1.6
.

Consider the dyadic maximal function

{M}^{d\sigma}_{D}f(x)=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\smallint\limits_{G}f\left(x\cdot(2^{j}\cdot y)^{-1}\right)d\sigma(y)\right|.

(1.12)

In
[
13

to guarantee the
L^{p}
-boundendess of
{M}^{d\sigma}_{D}.
More precisely, if for some
a>0
the Fourier transform of

satisfies the growth estimate

\max_{\pm}\sup_{\pi\in\widehat{G}}\|\pi(\mathcal{R})^{\pm\frac{a}{\nu}}\widehat{d\sigma}(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}<\infty,

(1.13)

then, the dyadic maximal function

{M}^{d\sigma}_{D}:L^{p}(G)\rightarrow L^{p}(G)
can be extended to a bounded operator for all

This extension was motivated by the seminal work due to C. P. Calderón
[
9
]
. The proof in
[
13

is inspired by the approach developed by Duoandikoetxea and Rubio De Francia in
[

1.5.
Relation with the quantisation on graded Lie groups

From the point of view of the microlocal analysis, in the setting of nilpotent Lie groups, when investigating the boundedness of the full maximal function, the problem of estimating the behaviour of the Fourier transform of the measure involves e.g. the stationary phase method or the Hadamard parametrix method. However, in view of the recent
quantisation theory on graded Lie groups

[
38
]

[

According to this approach, one is expected to involve criteria in terms of the symbols of Rockland operators when formulating the boundedness of operators of relevant interest in harmonic analysis as (singular integrals like) Fourier multipliers, (strongly singular integral operators in the form of) pseudo-differential operators and Fourier integral operators, as well as maximal averages and their corresponding maximal functions.

2.1.
Notation

The Haar measure of a locally compact Hausdorff topological group
G
is denoted by
dx.
We recall that the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the open subsets of
G
is called the Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
As usual, the Haar measure of a Borel set
A\subset G
is denoted by
|A|,
and the measure of
A
with respect to an arbitrary Radon measure
d\sigma
is denoted by
\sigma(A)=\smallint_{A}d\sigma.

Here we always consider measures
d\sigma
taking non-negative values
\sigma(A)\geq 0
(instead of signed or complex measures). We recall that the support
K
of a measure
d\sigma
is the smallest closed set
K
such that
\sigma(G\setminus K)=0.

Here the notations
A\asymp B
and
A\sim B,
both indicate that we have the inequality
C_{1}B\leq A\leq C_{2}B,
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
independent of fundamental parameters, while the notation
A\lesssim B
indicates that we have the inequality
A\leq C_{2}B
with
C_{2}
independent of fundamental parameters.

Finally, for a bounded linear operator
T:X\rightarrow Y
between two Banach spaces
X,Y,
the quantity
\|T\|_{\textnormal{op}}=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\|
denotes its standard operator norm.

2.2.
Nilpotent Lie groups

In this subsection we introduce some basic facts about nilpotent Lie groups. For a consistent treatment on the subject, we refer to Corwin and Greenleaf
[
26
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{R}
with Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot].
A subset
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g},
if
[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{b},
where

[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{g}]:=\{[B,X]:B\in\mathfrak{b},\,X\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

(2.1)

The descending central series of
\mathfrak{g}
is inductively defined by

\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathfrak{g}^{(n+1)}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}]=\mathbb{R}\textnormal{-span}\{[X,Y]:X\in\mathfrak{g},Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{(n)}\,\}.

If
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, then
\phi(\mathfrak{g}^{(k)})\subset\mathfrak{h}^{(k)}.
Note that, in view of the identity of Jacobi, we have that
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{N},

[\mathfrak{g}^{(p)},\mathfrak{g}^{(q)}]\subset\mathfrak{g}^{(p+q)}.
In particular, each
\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g},
that is
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}]\subset\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
if there is an integer
s
in such a way that
\mathfrak{g}^{(s+1)}=\{0\}
but
\mathfrak{g}^{(s)}\neq\emptyset.
If this
s
is minimal, we say that
\mathfrak{g}
is an
s
-step nilpotent Lie algebra
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}
is
s
-step nilpotent Lie algebra if and only if all brackets of at least
s+1
-elements of
\mathfrak{g}
are zero but not all brackets of order
s
are. Consequently, if
\mathfrak{g}
is
nilpotent
(that is,
\mathfrak{g}
is an
s
-nilpotent Lie algebra for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
), its center is not trivial. Indeed, if
\mathfrak{g}
is
s
-step nilpotent, then
\mathfrak{g}^{(s)}
is an abelian Lie algebra (also called central).

Now, let
\mathfrak{g}
be any Lie algebra; let
\mathfrak{g}_{(1)}:=\textnormal{Center}(\mathfrak{g})
be its center, and define

\mathfrak{g}_{(j)}:=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:X\textnormal{ is central mod}\mathfrak{g}_{j-1}\}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}:[X,\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{(j-1)}\}.

Each
\mathfrak{g}_{(j)}
is an ideal. This sequence of ideals is called the
ascending central series of

\mathfrak{g}.

We have that a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
s
-nilpotent if and only if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{(s)}\neq\mathfrak{g}_{(s-1)}.

A
nilpotent Lie group

G
is a Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\cong T_{e}G
is nilpotent (here
e=e_{G}
denotes the neutral element of
G
). If
G
is a connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie group then we have that the exponential mapping

\textnormal{exp}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow G

is an analytic diffeomorphism. This diffeomorphism can be applied, among other things, to reconstruct the group operation in view of the
Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula,
see
[
26
, Section 1.2]
.
In the next subsection we are going to introduce the family of nilpotent Lie groups that will be considered here, namely, nilpotent homogeneous Lie groups and, in particular, graded Lie groups.

2.3.
Graded Lie groups
\&
Rockland operators

Below we introduce some basic facts about the Fourier analysis and the spectral calculus of Rockland operators used in our further analysis. For the aspects of the theory of Rockland operators on graded Lie groups, we follow
[
38
]
.

G
denotes a homogeneous Lie group, that is a connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is endowed with a family of dilations
D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}.
We define it as follows. A family of dilations
\textnormal{Dil}(\mathfrak{g}):=\{D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}:\,r>0\}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a family of automorphisms on
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the following two compatibility conditions:

A.

For every
r>0,

D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}
is a map of the form

D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}=\textnormal{Exp}(\ln(r)A),

for some diagonalisable linear operator
A\equiv\textnormal{diag}[\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{n}]:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}.

B.

\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},
and
r>0,

[D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}X,D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}Y]=D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}[X,Y].

We call the eigenvalues of the matrix
A,

\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\cdots,\nu_{n},
the dilations weights or weights of
G
.

The homogeneous dimension of a homogeneous nilpotent Lie group
G
whose dilations are defined via
D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}=\textnormal{Exp}(\ln(r)A),
is given by

Q=\textnormal{Tr}(A)=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n},

where
\nu_{i},

i=1,2,\cdots,n,
are the eigenvalues of
A.
The family of dilations
\textnormal{Dil}(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
induces a family of mappings on
G
defined via,

\textnormal{Dil}(G):=\{D_{r}:=\exp\circ D_{r,\mathfrak{g}}\circ\exp^{-1}:\,\,r>0\}.

We refer to the elements of this family as the dilations on the group.

To simplify the notation we also denote
r\cdot x=D_{r}(x),

x\in G,

r>0.
The dilations of the group and the Haar measure
dx
on
G
are related by the identity

\smallint\limits_{G}f(x)dx=r^{Q}\smallint\limits_{G}(f\circ D_{r})(x)dx,\,\,\forall f\in L^{1}(G).

A connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
is graded if its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
may be decomposed as the direct sum of subspaces
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s}
such that the following bracket conditions are satisfied:

[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i+j},
where
\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}=\{0\}
if
i+j\geq s+1,
for some
s.

Examples of graded Lie groups are the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{n}
and more generally any stratified group where the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is generated by the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
.

We say that
\pi
is a continuous, unitary, and irreducible representation of the group
G,
if the following properties are satisfied,

C.

\pi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(H_{\pi})),
for some separable Hilbert space
H_{\pi},
i.e.
\pi(xy)=\pi(x)\pi(y)
and for the adjoint of
\pi(x),

\pi(x)^{*}=\pi(x^{-1}),
for every
x,y\in G.
This property says that the representation is compatible with the group operation.

D.

The map
(x,v)\mapsto\pi(x)v,
from
G\times H_{\pi}
into
H_{\pi}
is continuous. This says that the representation is a strongly continuous mapping.

E.

For every
x\in G,
and
W_{\pi}\subset H_{\pi},
if
\pi(x)W_{\pi}\subset W_{\pi},
then
W_{\pi}=H_{\pi}
or
W_{\pi}=\{0\}.
This means that the representation
\pi
is irreducible if its only invariant subspaces are
W=\{0\}
and
W=H_{\pi},
the trivial ones.

Two unitary representations

\pi\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(H_{\pi}))\textnormal{ and }\eta\in\textnormal{Hom}(G,\textnormal{U}(H_{\eta}))

are equivalent if there exists a bounded invertible linear mapping
T:H_{\pi}\rightarrow H_{\eta}
such that for any
x\in G,

T\pi(x)=\eta(x)T.

The relation
\sim
on the set of unitary and irreducible representations
\textnormal{Rep}(G)
defined by:
\pi\sim\eta
if and only if
\pi
and
\eta
are equivalent representations,
is an equivalence relation. The quotient

\widehat{G}:={\textnormal{Rep}(G)}/{\sim}

is called the unitary dual of
G.
The Fourier transform takes values at elements of
\widehat{G},
namely, the Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(G),
at
\pi\in\widehat{G},
is defined by

\widehat{f}(\pi)\equiv[\mathscr{F}f](\pi):=\smallint\limits_{G}f(x)\pi(x)^{*}dx:H_{\pi}\rightarrow H_{\pi},

where the notation
\pi(x)^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator of
\pi(x),

x\in G.

In this work a central notion is the Fourier transform of finite measures. Let
d\sigma
be a finite Borel measure on
G.
We record that its Fourier transform at
\pi\in\widehat{G}
is given by

\boxed{\widehat{d\sigma}(\pi)=\smallint_{G}\pi(x)^{*}d\sigma(x)}

(2.2)

The Fourier transform of a (positive) finite Borel measure is a bounded operator in any representation space, indeed

\displaystyle\forall\pi\in\widehat{G},\,\|\widehat{d\sigma}(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}\leq\smallint_{G}\|\pi(x)^{*}\|_{\textnormal{op}}d\sigma(x)=\sigma(G)<\infty.

The Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(G)
is defined by the smooth functions
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
such that via the exponential mapping
f\circ\exp:\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
can be identified with functions on the Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Then, the Schwartz space on the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
is defined by the image under the group Fourier transform of the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(G),
that is
\mathscr{F}:\mathscr{S}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G}):=\mathscr{F}(\mathscr{S}(G)).

The Fourier inversion formula is given by

\forall f\in L^{1}(G)\cap L^{2}(G),\,\,f(x)=\smallint\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\pi(x)\widehat{f}(\pi)]d\pi,\,\,x\in G.

Here
d\pi
denotes the Plancherel measure on
\widehat{G}.

The
L^{2}
-space on the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
is defined by the completion of the set
\mathscr{S}(G)
with respect to the norm

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}:=\left(\smallint\limits_{\widehat{G}}\|\sigma(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}d\pi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\,\,\sigma(\pi)=\widehat{f}(\pi)\in\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G}),

(2.3)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\textnormal{HS}}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of operators on every representation space. The corresponding inner product on
L^{2}(\widehat{G})
is given by

(\sigma,\tau)_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}:=\smallint\limits_{G}\textnormal{Tr}[\sigma(\pi)\tau(\pi)^{*}]d\pi,\,\,\sigma,\tau\in L^{2}(\widehat{G}).

(2.4)

Then,
the Plancherel theorem says that
\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{G})}
for all
f\in L^{2}(G).

A continuous linear operator
T:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G)
is homogeneous of degree
\nu_{T}\in\mathbb{C}
if for every
r>0
the equality

T(f\circ D_{r})=r^{\nu_{T}}(Tf)\circ D_{r}

holds for every
f\in C^{\infty}(G).

Let us consider
\pi\in\widehat{G},
then
\pi:G\rightarrow U({H}_{\pi})
is strongly continuous. We denote by
{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}
the set of smooth vectors, also called Gårding vectors, formed by those
v\in{H}_{\pi}
such that the function
x\mapsto\pi(x)v,

x\in\widehat{G},
is smooth. Then, a Rockland operator is a left-invariant partial differential operator

\mathcal{R}=\sum_{[\alpha]=\nu}a_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}:C^{\infty}(G)\rightarrow C^{\infty}(G),\quad[\alpha]:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\nu_{i},

which is homogeneous of positive degree
\nu=\nu_{\mathcal{R}}
and such that, for every unitary irreducible non-trivial representation
\pi\in\widehat{G},
its symbol
\pi(\mathcal{R})
defined via the Fourier inversion formula by

\mathcal{R}f(x)=\smallint\limits_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\pi(x)\pi(\mathcal{R})\widehat{f}(\pi)]d\pi,\,\,x\in G,

(2.5)

is injective on
{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}.
Then,
\sigma_{\mathcal{R}}(\pi)=\pi(\mathcal{R})
coincides with the infinitesimal representation of
\mathcal{R}
as an element of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
.

3.
Motivating the curvature conditions

Before presenting the proof of our main theorem in Section
4
, we are going to motivate the curvature conditions (ii) and (iii) in Definition
1.1
. To do so we follow the arguments presented in Fefferman
[
36
, Page 527]
describing the applications of the Tauberian type theorems, as observed by Stein in the proof of his differentiation theorem, see
[
36
, Theorem I.2.2]
. We present our motivation in the next subsection.

3.0.1.
The Tauberian type condition

Here, we are going to expose the arguments in
[
36
, Page 527]
in the setting of homogeneous nilpotent Lie groups. First, we recall the Tauberian theorem:

•

Suppose that
\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{R}\smallint_{0}^{R}F(r)dr
exists and that
\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r|\frac{dF}{dr}|^{2}dr<\infty.
Then,
\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(r)
exists, and equals
\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{R}\smallint_{0}^{R}F(r)dr.

Now, let us consider a compactly supported measure
d\sigma
on
G,
and let us identify the required conditions in order that

\forall f\in L^{2}(G),\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\smallint\limits_{G}f\left(x\cdot(t\cdot y)^{-1}\right)d\sigma(y)=f(x),\,\textnormal{a. e.}

(3.1)

In (
3.1
) we have assumed that the measure
d\sigma
is normalised
\sigma(G)=1.
By the Riesz-Representation theorem we can write
d\sigma(y)=K(y)dy,
where
dy
denotes the Haar measure on
G.
Then, using the notation in (
2.9
), the limit (
3.1
) can be rewritten in terms of the convolution of the group as

\forall f\in L^{2}(G),\,\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f\ast K_{t}(x)=f(x),\,\textnormal{a. e.}

(3.2)

To have control of the operator-valued group Fourier transform
\widehat{f}(\pi)
, we are going to use the following operator inequality,

|\textnormal{Tr}[f(\pi)^{*}\widehat{f}(\pi)B(\pi)]|\leq\|f(\pi)^{*}\widehat{f}(\pi)\|_{S_{1}(H_{\pi})}\|B(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{op}},

(3.3)

where
B(\pi)
is any bounded operator on the representation space
H_{\pi},

\|B(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{op}}
is its operator norm,
S_{1}(H_{\pi})
denotes the ideal of trace class operators on
H_{\pi},
and
\|\cdot\|_{S_{1}(H_{\pi})}
is its norm which is given by the sum of the singular values of the operator. Note that

\|f(\pi)^{*}\widehat{f}(\pi)\|_{S_{1}(H_{\pi})}=\|\widehat{f}(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}=\textnormal{Tr}[\widehat{f}(\pi)^{*}\widehat{f}(\pi)],

(3.4)

is the square of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
\widehat{f}(\pi).

Let
f\in L^{2}(G).
For each fixed
t>0,
define
F(x,t)
by
\widehat{F}(\pi,t)=\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)\widehat{f}(\pi).

The differentiability of the mapping
t\mapsto\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)
will be discussed in Subsection
3.0.2
below.

Using Plancherel theorem we have that

\displaystyle\smallint_{G}\left(\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(x,r)\right|^{2}dr\right)dx

\displaystyle=\smallint_{0}^{\infty}\smallint_{G}r\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(x,r)\right|^{2}dxdr=\smallint_{0}^{\infty}\smallint_{\widehat{G}}r\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\widehat{F}(\pi,r)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}d\pi dr

\displaystyle=\smallint_{0}^{\infty}\smallint_{\widehat{G}}r\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi)\right\}\widehat{f}(\pi)\|_{\textnormal{HS}}^{2}d\pi dr

\displaystyle=\smallint_{0}^{\infty}\smallint_{\widehat{G}}r\textnormal{Tr}[\widehat{f}(\pi)^{*}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi)\right\}\right|^{2}\widehat{f}(\pi)]d\pi dr,

where we have used the notation
|T|^{2}=T^{*}T,
for the operator
T=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi)\right\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\smallint_{0}^{\infty}\smallint_{\widehat{G}}r\textnormal{Tr}[\widehat{f}(\pi)^{*}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi)\right\}\right|^{2}\widehat{f}(\pi)]d\pi dr

\displaystyle=\smallint_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\widehat{f}(\pi)\widehat{f}(\pi)^{*}\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi)\right\}\right|^{2}dr]d\pi

\displaystyle\leq\smallint_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\widehat{f}(\pi)\widehat{f}(\pi)^{*}]\left\|\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi)\right\}\right|^{2}dr\right\|_{\textnormal{op}}d\pi

\displaystyle\leq\smallint_{\widehat{G}}\textnormal{Tr}[\widehat{f}(\pi)\widehat{f}(\pi)^{*}]d\pi\sup_{\pi^{\prime}\in\widehat{G}}\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi^{\prime})\right\}\right\|^{2}_{\textnormal{op}}dr.

Now, if
d\sigma
satisfies the integrability condition

\Sigma_{0}^{2}:=\sup_{\pi^{\prime}\in\widehat{G}}\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi^{\prime})\right\}\right\|^{2}_{\textnormal{op}}dr<\infty,

(3.5)

we have proved that

\smallint_{G}\left(\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(x,r)\right|^{2}dr\right)dx\leq\Sigma_{0}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.6)

This analysis shows that

\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}F(x,r)\right|^{2}dr<\infty,

(3.7)

for almost every
x\in G.
On the other hand,
F(x,r)=f\ast K_{r}(x)
can be understood as the convolution of
f
with a standard approximate identity, (see e.g. Folland and Stein
[
40
, Page 67]
) and
\lim_{R\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{R}\smallint_{0}^{R}F(x,r)dr=f(x)
for almost every
x\in G.

In consequence, for almost every
x\in G,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(x,r)=\lim_{R\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{R}\smallint_{0}^{R}F(x,r)dr=f(x),

proving the following version of the Stein differentiation theorem.

•

The equality:

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\smallint\limits_{G}f\left(x\cdot(t\cdot y)^{-1}\right)d\sigma(y)=f(x),
holds almost everywhere for all
f\in L^{2}(G),
provided that the normalised measure
d\sigma,
being compactly supported, satisfies the following Tauberian-type condition

\sup_{\pi^{\prime}\in\widehat{G}}\smallint_{0}^{\infty}r\left\|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi^{\prime})\right\}\right\|^{2}_{\textnormal{op}}dr<\infty.

(3.8)

In the case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the curvature of the sphere causes a decay of the Fourier transform
\widehat{d\sigma}
at infinity allowing the convergence of the integral (
3.8
) when
n\geq 3
.

We note that if one wants to extend this criterion to other
L^{p}(G)
spaces one could impose conditions on the decay of the derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\{\widehat{d\sigma}(r\cdot\pi^{\prime})\},
indeed taking inspiration from the Hörmander-Mihlin theorem on graded Lie groups, see Fischer and Ruzhansky
[
39
]
. With this kind of criteria in mind, the analysis above suggests that the condition (iii) in Definition
1.1
could be a natural replacement for the curvature conditions available in the Euclidean setting. Since we use Rockland operators in this formulation the group
G
is graded, see
[
38
, Page 172]
. The hypothesis (ii) in Definition
1.1
is an analogy of the standard hypothesis
|\widehat{d\sigma}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{-a},

a>0,
studied e.g. in the Euclidean setting in
[
33
]
by Duoandikoetxea and Rubio de Francia. Note that (i) in Definition
1.1
is a reasonable topological condition.

3.0.2.
About the differentiability of
t\mapsto\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)

We finish this section by discussing the differentiability of the mapping
t\mapsto\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)v,
on the subspace
H_{\pi}^{\infty}
of Gårding vectors
v.
By following Kirillov
[
49
]
, each representation space
H_{\pi}
can be identified with
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k(\pi)}),
and also
H_{\pi}^{\infty}\cong\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{k(\pi)})
can be identified with the Schwartz class on
\mathbb{R}^{k(\pi)}.
Consequently, we deduce that
H_{\pi}^{\infty},
is a dense subspace of
H_{\pi}
with respect to the topology induced by the norm in
H_{\pi}.
We refer the reader to
[
39
, Page 42]
for a famous proof of this fact due to Gårding.

Note that

\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)=\smallint_{G}\pi(t\cdot x)^{*}d\sigma(x)=\smallint_{G}\pi(t\cdot x)^{*}K(x)dx=\smallint_{G}\pi(t\cdot x)K(x^{-1})dx,

(3.9)

where we have written
d\sigma(x)=Kdx
in view of the Riesz-Representation theorem. Let us write (
3.9
) in exponential coordinates
x=\exp(X),

\displaystyle\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\smallint_{\mathfrak{g}}\pi(t\cdot\exp(X))K((\exp{X})^{-1})|\det[D\exp(X)]|dX

\displaystyle=\smallint_{\mathfrak{g}}\pi(\exp(t\cdot X))K((\exp{X})^{-1})|\det[D\exp(X)]|dX.

Since the group
G
is graded, relative to the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{s},
we have a unique representation
X=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\alpha_{i}(X)X_{i},
where
X_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}.
and the
\alpha_{i}(X)
’s are scalars for
1\leq i\leq s.
Note that

t\mapsto(t\cdot X)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}t^{\nu_{i}}\alpha_{i}(X)X_{i}=F_{X}(t)

is a
C^{\infty}
mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{+}.
We also recall that on the set of smooth vectors
H_{\pi}^{\infty},
the mapping
x\mapsto\pi(x)v,
is smooth for any
v\in H_{\pi}^{\infty}.
With the notation above we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)v=\smallint_{\mathfrak{g}}\pi(\exp[F_{X}(t)])vK((\exp{X})^{-1})|\det[D\exp(X)]|dX.

Since
\pi(\exp[F_{X}(\cdot)])v
is obtained by the composition
t\mapsto F_{X}(t)\mapsto\pi(\exp(F_{X}(t)))v,
it is a smooth mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Note also that
K
has compact support, allowing the mapping
\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)v
to be smooth in
t>0.
Then, by defining
\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi):H_{\pi}^{\infty}\rightarrow H_{\pi},
by
\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)v:=\frac{d}{dt}\{\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)v\},
the operator
\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{d\sigma}(t\cdot\pi)
can be realised as a densely defined operator on
H_{\pi}.

4.
Proof of the main Theorem
1.2

4.1.
Reduction of the problem

We are going to split the measure

\widehat{d\sigma}_{1}
localising the low frequencies of the Fourier transform
\widehat{d\sigma}
and with
\widehat{d\sigma}_{2}

\phi\geq 0

\{t\in\mathbb{R}:0\leq t\leq 2\}

\phi(t)=1
for

For our further analysis, for a fixed
\beta>0,
let us define the operator
\phi_{\beta}(\mathcal{R}^{1/\nu})

\Phi_{\beta}=\phi_{\beta}(\mathcal{R}^{1/\nu})\delta.
We denote
\Phi=\Phi_{1},
when
\beta=1
for simplicity and note that in view of (
2.11
),
\Phi_{\beta}=\beta^{-Q}\Phi(\beta^{-1}\cdot).

Let us split the measure
d\sigma
as follows

d\sigma=\Phi_{\tau}*d\sigma+[(\delta-\Phi_{\tau})\ast d\sigma],\,d\sigma_{1}:=\Phi_{\tau}*d\sigma,

(4.1)

where the parameter
\tau,
satisfying for now that

will be chosen later. Let

Let us consider the associated maximal functions for each measure in this partition, respectively,

M_{1}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}|f\ast(\Phi_{\tau}*d\sigma)_{t}(x)|,

(4.2)

and

M_{2}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}|f\ast[(\delta-\Phi_{\tau})\ast d\sigma]_{t}(x)|.

(4.3)

In what follows, we shall investigate the
L^{2}
-theory of the operator
M_{2}
and the

-theory of

The

will allow us to estimate the

-operator norm of

On the other hand, since the

-theory for

is related to the growth properties of the measure

, in our further analysis we prove the weak (1,1) boundedness of

using the property (i) in Definition

. Once estimated the operator norms of

and of

we establish the restricted weak estimate for

in Subsection

which proves (1) in Theorem

.

4.2.
The
L^{2}
-theory for
M_{2}

Now we are going to estimate the

-operator norm of

For this we require the
curvature conditions
(
1.7
) and (
1.8
). We are going to use the inequality

\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{2}|F(x,t)|^{2}\leq\sup_{t>0}\left|\smallint_{0}^{t}F(x,s)F^{\prime}(x,s)ds\right|\leq\smallint\limits_{0}^{\infty}|F(x,s)||F^{\prime}(x,s)|ds

\leq\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{\infty}|F(x,s)|^{2}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{\infty}|sF^{\prime}(x,s)|^{2}\frac{ds}{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

applied to

Observe that

4.3.
The
L^{1}
-theory for
M_{1}

Let us prove the weak (1,1) type of
M_{1}.
This part of the proof will be important in identifying the topological condition in (

) that the measure

must satisfy. To do so, we take inspiration from the original proof of the
L^{p}
result by Stein, when making the corresponding analysis in Littlewood-Paley components. So, the arguments about the weak (1,1) type are classical and we closely are going to follow the accurate approach in Grafakos
[

, Pages 479-480]
, adapted to the nilpotent setting.

First, let us prove the pointwise estimate

M_{1}f(x)\lesssim\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}\mathscr{M}f(x),

(4.18)

where
\mathscr{M}
denotes the standard Hardy-Littlewood maximal function (see Folland and Stein
[
40
, Chapter 2]
),

\mathscr{M}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}|B(x,r)|^{-1}\smallint_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|dy.

(4.19)

Using (
4.18
) we shall prove that
M_{1}
satisfies the weak (1,1) inequality

\boxed{|\{x\in G:M_{1}f(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}\}|\lesssim\frac{\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}}{\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}}

(4.20)

For the proof of (
4.18
) it suffices to prove the inequality

|\Phi_{\tau}*d\sigma(x)|\lesssim C_{M}\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}(1+|x|)^{-M},

(4.21)

for any
M>Q.

Note that
\phi\in\mathscr{S}(G)
is a function in the Schwartz class, and we have the estimate
|\Phi(z)|\leq C_{M}(1+|z|)^{-M}.
Consequently,

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\tau}*d\sigma(x)|

\displaystyle=\smallint_{G}|\Phi_{\tau}(xy^{-1})|d\sigma(y)=\tau^{-Q}\smallint_{G}|\Phi(\tau^{-1}\cdot(xy^{-1}))|d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{M}\tau^{-Q}\smallint_{G}(1+|\tau^{-1}(xy^{-1})|)^{-M}d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=\tau^{-Q}\smallint_{G}(1+\tau^{-1}|(xy^{-1})|)^{-M}d\sigma(y).

In particular for any
N>M>Q,
we also have the estimate

|\Phi_{\tau}*d\sigma(x)|\leq C_{N}\tau^{-Q}\smallint_{G}(1+\tau^{-1}|(xy^{-1})|)^{-N}d\sigma(y).

(4.22)

Let
K
be the support of
d\sigma
and let us consider the regions

S_{-1}(x)=\{y\in K:\tau^{-1}|xy^{-1}|\leq 1\},

(4.23)

and for any

let

(4.24)

By hypothesis we have that

\sigma(S_{r}(x))\leq(2^{r}\tau)^{Q_{0}}.

(4.25)

Note also that when
y\in S_{r}(x)\subset K\subset B(e,R),
where

|xy^{-1}|\leq 2^{r}\tau,
and then from the inequality

we deduce that
|x|\leq 2^{r}\tau+R.

Let

be such that

From the observations above we have that

\displaystyle\smallint_{G}(1+\tau^{-1}|(xy^{-1})|)^{-N}d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=-1}^{\infty}\smallint_{S_{r}(x)}(1+\tau^{-1}|(xy^{-1})|)^{-N}d\sigma(y)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\sum_{r=j_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-rN}\chi_{B(e,2^{r}\tau+R)}(x)\sigma(S_{r}(x))

Note that, allowing

we can estimate

\displaystyle=\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}\sum_{r=-1}^{j_{0}}2^{-(N-Q_{0})r}\lesssim\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}.

On the other hand, for

Consequently,

\displaystyle\tau^{-Q}\sum_{r=j_{0}+1}^{\infty}2^{-rN}\chi_{B(e,2^{r}\tau+R)}(x)\sigma(K)

4.4.
The restricted weak estimate for
M_{F}^{d\sigma}

Let
A\subset G
be a Borel measurable set and let
f=\chi_{A}
be its characteristic function. We are going to prove the estimate

\displaystyle\boxed{|\{x\in G:M_{F}^{d\sigma}f(x)>\lambda\}|\lesssim{\lambda^{-\frac{D}{D-c}}}|A|}

(4.27)

where
D:=2a-\varepsilon_{0}+2c.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

In view of (

) and of (
4.20
),
the weak (1,1) boundedness of
M_{1}
together with the

-boundedness of

provides the following estimates when

(4.28)

and

\left|\left\{x\in G:M_{2}f(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}\right|\leq\smallint\limits_{\{x\in G:M_{2}f(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}\}}\frac{M_{2}f(x)^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}dx\lesssim\frac{\tau^{2a-\varepsilon_{0}}}{\lambda^{2}}|A|.

(4.29)

So, we have proved that

\displaystyle|\{x\in G:M_{F}^{d\sigma}f(x)>\lambda\}|\lesssim\left(\frac{\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}}{\lambda}+\frac{\tau^{2a-\varepsilon_{0}}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)|A|.

Now, let us make an optimization procedure on the constant on the right-hand side. Let us consider the function

\displaystyle\varkappa(\tau):=\frac{\tau^{Q_{0}-Q}}{\lambda}+\frac{\tau^{2a-\varepsilon_{0}}}{\lambda^{2}}.

Let
\lambda<1.
Since
Q-Q_{0}>0,
we have that

\tau_{0}=\left(\frac{Q-Q_{0}}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}}\lambda\right)^{\frac{1}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}+Q-Q_{0}}},

(4.30)

is a critical point of
\varkappa(\tau),
namely, it satisfies that
\varkappa^{\prime}(\tau_{0})=0.
We also observe that

\varkappa(\tau_{0})=C(a,\varepsilon_{0},Q,Q_{0})\lambda^{-\frac{2a-\varepsilon_{0}+2c}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}+c}},\,c=Q-Q_{0},

(4.31)

where the constant
C(a,\varepsilon_{0},Q,Q_{0})
depends on
a,\varepsilon_{0},Q,Q_{0},
but not on
\lambda.

A direct computation shows that

C(a,\varepsilon_{0},Q,Q_{0})=F^{\frac{2a-\varepsilon_{0}}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}+Q-Q_{0}}}+F^{\frac{Q_{0}-Q}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}+Q-Q_{0}}},\,F:=\frac{Q-Q_{0}}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}}.

Moreover,

\varkappa^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{0})=F^{\frac{2a-\varepsilon_{0}-2}{2a-\varepsilon_{0}+Q-Q_{0}}}(2a-\varepsilon_{0})(2a-\varepsilon_{0}+Q-Q_{0})>0,

(4.32)

showing that
\tau=\tau_{0}
is a local minimum of
\varkappa(\tau).
Because of the assumption
0<\lambda<1,
and since
2a-\varepsilon_{0}+Q-Q_{0}>0,
we can choose
\tau=\tau_{0}
in our analysis above, that automatically will satisfy the condition
\tau^{-1}\gg 1
imposed at the beginning. So, this choice of
\tau
implies the estimate

\displaystyle|\{x\in G:M_{F}^{d\sigma}f(x)>\lambda\}|\lesssim{\lambda^{-\frac{D}{D-c}}}|A|,

(4.33)

where
D=2a-\varepsilon_{0}+2c.

We observe that only the inequality in (
4.33
) when
\lambda<1
is relevant for the proof. Indeed, by the Riesz-representation theorem we can write
d\sigma=Kdx,
where
dx
denotes the Haar measure on
G.
In consequence, we have that

\displaystyle M_{F}^{d\sigma}(\chi_{A})(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}|\chi_{A}\ast K_{t}(x)|\leq\sup_{t>0}\|\chi_{A}\ast K_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\chi_{A}\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{t>0}\|K_{t}\|_{L^{1}}=\|K\|_{L^{1}(G)}=\sigma(G).

Without loss of generality if we consider the measure
d\sigma
to be normalised, then we have the inequality
M_{F}^{d\sigma}(\chi_{A})(x)\leq 1.
Consequently if
\lambda>1,

|\{x\in G:M_{F}^{d\sigma}f(x)>\lambda\}|=0.

The estimate (
4.33
) proves that
M_{F}^{d\sigma}
is of restricted weak
(\frac{D}{D-c},1)
type. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.

5.
Final remarks

5.0.1.
About our main result

This paper aims to extend the approach of Bourgain to analyse the boundedness of the full maximal function in a class of homogeneous nilpotent Lie groups, admitting the existence of left-invariant hypoelliptic partial differential operators, and then we assume our group to be a graded Lie group in view of the Helffer and Nourrigat solution in
[
45
]
of the Rockland conjecture. Our main result is the restricted weak
(p,1)
estimate presented in Theorem
1.2
.

5.0.2.
Applications: differentiability of functions on graded Lie groups

We also note, as expected, that the boundedness properties of maximal functions, and in particular our main Theorem
1.2
, have an immediate consequence in the differentiations of functions in the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(G)
, extending the classical Stein differentiation theorem, see Remark
1.4
. An excellent introduction to this topic is the one given by Stein in
[
73
]
.

5.0.3.
Relation of our approach with the global quantisation on graded Lie groups

As expected, there arise several difficulties in proving our main Theorem
1.2
. One of the main obstacles is to deal with the non-commutative setting of nilpotent Lie groups
G
. In this setting, the Fourier transform is operator-valued taking values at the unitary and irreducible representations of the group, that are elements of the unitary dual
\widehat{G}
.
Nevertheless, here we follow one of the recent developments of the theory of pseudo-differential operators on nilpotent Lie groups, for instance, the
global quantisation on graded Lie groups
.

According to this research perspective the symbol classes and the boundedness properties of operators can be formulated in terms of criteria involving orders measured by the symbols of Rockland operators. Indeed, this approach has been developed recently in the theory of pseudo-differential operators of Fischer and Ruzhansky
[
38
]
, as a global extension to the local approach developed by Hörmander
[
47
]
. For the aspects of the global theory of pseudo-differential operators on compact Lie groups we refer to Ruzhansky, Turunen and Wirth
[
63
,
64
]
and to
[
18
,
21
]
.

So, motivated by this new research perspective that by itself is a source of open problems, particularly in harmonic analysis and PDE, in our analysis of the full maximal function, we have formulated our hypotheses for a class of measures whose Fourier transform is closely related to the Hörmander symbol classes developed in
[
38
]
, see Definition
1.1
. Then, a criterion on the corresponding measure
d\sigma
has been formulated in Theorem
1.2
in order to guarantee the boundedness of
M_{F}^{d\sigma}

Abstract

1.
Introduction

Through J. Bourgain’s work about dispersive equations on rational and irrational torus, the role of the analytic number theory and in particular, the analysis of the
Waring
problem
via the recent complete solution in
[
6
,
7
]
of
Vinogradov’s mean value conjecture
, has shown to be relevant for several problems in harmonic analysis and dispersive PDE. In this work we follow the approach of Bourgain in order to investigate the extension of Strichartz estimates on an arbitrary simple compact Lie group
G
(endowed with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric
g

r_{s,2}(R)

R,

1.1.
Outline

Let
(M,g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Let
u(t,x)=e^{it\Delta_{g}}
be the solution of the Schr̈odinger equation for the positive Laplacian
\Delta_{g}
on a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g).
We record that an estimate of the type

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{p}(I,L^{q}(M))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)},\,\,

(1.1)

is called a
classical Strichartz estimate
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
is a closed interval. It is well known that the Sobolev exponent
s
in (
1.10
) is expected to satisfy the inequality

s\geq s_{0}(p,q)=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}.

(1.2)

The validity of this kind of Strichartz estimates is very well known from the work of Burq, Gérard and Tzvetkov
[
8
]
. Their estimate is as follows.

Let
d\geq 1.
The solution to the linear Schrodinger equation

iu_{t}+\Delta_{g}u=0,\,\,u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\,\,(t,x)\in[0,1]\times M,

(1.3)

satisfies the estimate

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{p}([0,1],L^{q}(M))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{p}}(M)},\,\,

(1.4)

where
(p,q)
are Lebesgue indices satisfying the admissibility condition

2/p+d/q=d/2,\,\,(p,q,d)\neq(2,\infty,2).

The optimality of (
1.4
) in the end-point case
p=2
was proved in
[
8
]
for spheres
\mathbb{S}^{d}.
Moreover, they have proved the following general statement when adapting Bourgain’s argument to this generality level.

Let
d\geq 2.
Assume that
(M,g)
is a compact Riemannian manifold whose geodesics are closed and with a common period. Then the estimate (
1.4
) can be improved as

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{4}([0,1]\times M)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)},\,\,s>s_{0}(d),\,\,

(1.5)

where

s_{0}(2)=1/8,\,s_{0}(d)=\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2},\,\forall d\geq 3.

(1.6)

Moreover, if
M=\mathbb{S}^{d}
is endowed with the canonical metric, the Sobolev regularity order
s>s_{0}(d)
in (
1.5
) is sharp.

We note that the the regularity order in (
1.6
) agrees with the one obtained by Bourgain in the case of the torus only when
d\geq 3.
Indeed, on the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d},
and by computing explicitly

\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}=\|u^{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

Bourgain showed that:

if
d=2,
for every
\varepsilon>0,

\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{d})},
and when
d\geq 3,
the following estimate

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{4}([0,1]\times M)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)},\,\,s>s_{0}(d),\,\,

(1.7)

holds for
s_{0}(d)={d}/{4}-{1}/{2}.

Due to the general perspective of the results in (
1.4
) or in (
1.5
) the sharpness of these results is an interesting problem. The best-studied situation is when
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Gamma
is a torus for some rank
d
lattice. Improvements of (
1.7
) for general indices of
p
is a significant problem due to its connections with
weak turbulence
for non-linear Schrödinger equations, see e.g.
[
10
, Page 703]
.

Recently, in a series of works, Zhang in
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
has obtained a number of improvements in the setting of simple compact Lie groups for Strichartz estimates of the Schrödinger equation by obtaining analogues of (
1.5
) and extensions of it for all
1<p<\infty.
Zhang’s result can be stated as follows, see
[
36
, Theorem 1]
in the compact Lie group setting.

Let
(G,g)
be a compact Lie group of rank
\ell\geq 2
equipped with the canonical
Killing metric
g
and let
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
be a closed interval. Then the solution to (
1.3
) satisfies the estimate

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{p}(I\times G)}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}(G)},

(1.8)

provided that
p\geq 2+\frac{8}{\ell},
for all
s
such that

s\geq\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}.

(1.9)

The aim of this work is to analyse the case of reverse Strichartz estimates on a compact Lie group
G
. By
reverse Strichartz estimates
on a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
we mean Sobolev estimates of the form

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{q}(M,\,L^{p}(I))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(M)}.\,\,

(1.10)

The main goal of this work is to estimate the Sobolev index
s_{0}^{R}(p,q)
that allows the validity of (
1.10
) when
s\geq s_{0}^{R}(p,q).
The Sobolev index
s_{0}^{R}(p,q)
is expected to hold the inequality

s_{0}^{R}(p,q)\geq s_{0}(p,q)=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{q}.

(1.11)

In particular, when
p=q
we expect to provide a new value for the index
s_{0}^{R}(p,p),
just when
p\in[2,2+\varepsilon]
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Indeed, with
\varepsilon=2+\frac{4(d+\ell)}{d\ell},
is known from the works of Zhang
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
that one can take
s_{0}^{R}(p,p)=s_{0}(p,p),
if
p\in[2+\frac{4(d+\ell)}{d\ell},\infty).

Here, we consider only the case where
G
is a simple and compact Lie group, because we make use of explicit representations of the spectrum of the Laplace operator, which modulo a factor, can be written in terms of a sum of squares. Indeed, it is a very well-known fact that for a fixed set of simple positive roots
\Sigma
with corresponding fundamental weights
\overline{\omega}_{1},...,\overline{\omega}_{n},
and each highest weight

\small{{\tilde{\nu}}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{l}{\tilde{\nu}}_{j}\overline{\omega}_{j}},\,{\tilde{\nu}}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

on a compact Lie group
G,
one can associate an eigenvalue
\lambda_{{\tilde{\nu}}}
for the positive Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{G}
given by

\lambda_{{\tilde{\nu}}}=(\eta,\eta)-({\tilde{\nu}}+\eta,{\tilde{\nu}}+\eta),

(1.12)

Conversely, every eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
can be written in the form of (
1.12
). Due to the importance that it has for us the explicit construction of the spectrum of the Laplace operator in terms of the root systems, we compute it on every simple and simply connected
G.
Since these groups are classified by the root system associated with their Cartan subalgebras, in terms of their Dynkin diagrams, we split our analysis into the following cases:

•

the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is
A_{\ell},
in which case
G\cong\textnormal{SU}(\ell+1).

•

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is
B_{\ell},
in which case
G\cong\textnormal{Spin}(2\ell+1).

•

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is
C_{\ell},
in which case
G\cong\textnormal{Sp}(\ell).

•

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is
D_{\ell},
in which case
G\cong\textnormal{Spin}(2\ell).

•

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is one of the exceptional Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{f}_{4}
or
\mathfrak{e}_{8},
in which case
G\cong G_{2},

G\cong F_{4},
or
G\cong E_{8},
respectively.

For our further analysis, we exclude the case
\ell=1
since
\textnormal{Spin(3)}\cong\textnormal{SU}(2)\cong\mathbb{S}^{3}
and the Strichartz estimates in the case of the spheres have been analysed in the work of Burq, Gerard, and Tzvetkov
[
8
]
. Having explained this classification, we are ready to present our result.

1.2.
Main result

In the case where
2\leq p<\infty
and
2\leq q\leq\infty,
the results of this paper can be summarised in Theorem
1.1
below. In view of Remark
2.4
, we only consider the validity of the Strichartz estimates in the case where
G
is a connected simple and simply connected compact Lie group. However, our analysis also covers the case where
G
is not simply connected. Indeed, if
G
is a connected (not necessarily simply-connected) compact simple Lie group, we have that

G\cong\tilde{G}/\Gamma,

where
\tilde{G}
is a connected simply-connected compact simple Lie group (diffeomorphic to the universal cover of
G
) and
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
\tilde{G}.
This property allows us to conclude the inclusion

\textnormal{Spec}(G)\subseteq\textnormal{Spec}(\tilde{G}).

(1.13)

As in our approach we apply an explicit construction of the spectrum of the Laplacian we have proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty.
Let
(G,g)
be a compact connected simple Lie group of rank
\ell\geq 2
endowed with a Riemmanian bi-invariant metric
g
and let
\tilde{G}
be its universal cover. Let

m=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\ell+1,&&\textnormal{if}\ \tilde{G}\cong\textnormal{SU($\ell+1$)}\ \textnormal{or}\ \tilde{G}\cong G_{2},\\
\ell,&&\textnormal{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(1.14)

Let
T=2\pi b\gamma,
where
b
is a non-negative integer depending on
G
and
\gamma
is a geometric constant defined by the metric
g.
Then

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,2\pi b\gamma])}\lesssim_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(G)},

(1.15)

where

s\geq s_{0}^{R}(p,q):=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)

if
m\geq 5,

s>s_{0}^{R}(p,q)
if
m=2,3,4
and

\sigma(q):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{d-1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right),&&\textnormal{if}\ 2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\\
\\
\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{q},&&\textnormal{if}\ \frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}<q\leq{\infty.}\end{array}\right.

Now, we briefly discuss this result and its relation to the existent results on compact manifolds.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that when
\tilde{G}\cong\textnormal{SU}(\ell+1)
or if
\tilde{G}\cong\textnormal{G}_{2},
the root system of the Lie algebra is constructed in an Euclidean space of dimension
\ell+1.
Also, if
\tilde{G}
is not diffeomorphic to
\textnormal{SU}(\ell+1)
or to
\textnormal{G}_{2},
its root system is constructed in an Euclidean
\ell
-dimensional space. These facts justify the two values
m=\ell,
or
m=\ell+1,
of
m
defined in (
1.14
).

Remark 1.3
.

On
\mathbb{R}^{n},
the sharp Strichartz estimate

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.16)

is valid for all the
admissible pairs

(p,q),
i.e.
p
and
q
satisfying
2/p+d/q=d/2,
see Ginibre and Velo
[
11
]
and Keel and Tao
[
22
]
. Note that by the Sobolev embedding theorem one has that the estimate

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{p}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.17)

is valid with

s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{r}\geq 0,

(1.18)

where
p,q\geq 2,
and
(p,r,d)\neq(2,\infty,2).
The triples
(p,q,r)
satisfying (
1.17
) and (
1.18
) are usually called
scale-invariant
. However, on compact manifolds the Strichartz estimates as in (
1.16
) are expected to fail, while in the setting of compact manifolds the validity of an estimate of the type (
1.17
)
is expected to hold only for positive values of
s>0.
Moreover, is also expected to have Strichartz estimates for
(p,q,r)
being a non-admissible triple and with
s>\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p}-\frac{d}{r},
see e.g. the discussion in Zhang
[
34
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Recently Zhang in
[
37
]
has improved the validity of Strichartz estimates (
1.8
) where
s\geq\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}
and
p\geq 2+\frac{8(s_{0}-1)}{s_{0}\ell},
where
s_{0}=2d/(d-\ell).
Note that for
s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d+2}{p}
one has that
p\geq 2+\frac{4(d+\ell)}{d\ell}.
So, the contributions of our result for
p
in the interval

[2,2+\frac{4(d+\ell)}{d\ell}]

are new.
If we denote by

s_{0}^{R}(p,q):=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)

(1.19)

the Sobolev exponent obtained in Theorem
3.3
,
one has the inequality
s_{0}^{R}(p,q)\geq s_{0}(p,q).
Indeed, for
q\in[2,\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}],
one can prove that

s_{0}^{R}(p,q)-s_{0}(p,q)=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{d+1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\geq 0,

while for
2\in\left(\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\infty\right)
one has that

s_{0}^{R}(p,q)-s_{0}(p,q)=\frac{m-1}{2}\geq 0.

We do not expect that the index
s_{0}^{R}(p,q)
will be sharp when
p\rightarrow 2^{+},
but still when
q=2,
the index
s_{0}^{R}(p,2)
is sharp with respect to the approach used in this work.

2.
Tools from Lie theory and from analytic number theory

2.1.
Spectrum of the Laplacian on compact simple Lie groups

We will find the spectrum of the Laplacian operator on a Lie group via representations of its Lie algebra and provide some explicit examples of the eigenvalues in compact and simply-connected Lie groups. For this, we shall follow
[
2
,
3
,
30
,
38
,
39
]
.

Let
G
be a
n
-dimensional connected and simply connected compact simple Lie group whose corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the real compact form of a complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}.
Let us endow
\mathfrak{g}
with the
Ad
-invariant inner product
g
given by a multiple
\gamma>0
of the negative of the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
, that is,
g=-\gamma\kappa,\ \textnormal{where}\ \kappa(X,Y):=\textnormal{Tr(ad(}X\textnormal{)ad(}Y\textnormal{))}
. It is known that
g
induces a bi-invariant Riemannian metric on
G
which will be also denoted by
g
. The Laplacian on
G
is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{G}:=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}^{2},

where
\mathbb{X}=\{X_{1},...,X_{n}\}
is a
g
-orthonormal basis of left-invariant vector fields. Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{k}
(i.e.
\mathfrak{t}
is nilpotent and self-normalising) and denote by
\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})
its corresponding real form so that we can consider the root system

\Pi:=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})^{*}\setminus\{0\}|\exists X\in\mathfrak{g}\setminus\{0\}\ \textnormal{such that}\ [H,X]=\alpha(H)X,\ \forall H\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})\}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})^{*}.

Fix subsets
\Pi^{+}\subseteq\Pi
and
\Sigma=\{\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{\ell}\}\subseteq\Pi^{+}
of positive roots and positive simple roots respectively. The restriction
(\cdot,\cdot):=\gamma\kappa\left|{}_{\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})}\right.
on
\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})
is non-degenerate and it induces a quadratic form on
\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})^{*}
(also denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
) as follows: since
(\cdot,\cdot)
is non-degenerate, then the map
H\mapsto\alpha_{H}:=(H,\cdot)
is an isomorphism between
\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})^{*}
; this means that for each
\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})
there exists a unique
H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\alpha(H)=(H_{\alpha},H),\ \forall H\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})
, so we define
(\alpha,\beta):=(H_{\alpha},H_{\beta}).

The fundamental weights
\overline{\omega}_{1},...,\overline{\omega}_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\Sigma
are defined by the relations

(\overline{\omega}_{i},\alpha_{j})=\delta_{ij}\frac{(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j})}{2},\ i,j=1,...,\ell,

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol.
For a given a complex finite dimensional representation
\rho:\mathfrak{k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, there exists a decomposition

V=\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha\in\Phi_{\rho}}V_{\alpha},

where
V_{\alpha}:=\{v\in V:\rho(H)v=\alpha^{\mathbb{C}}(H)v,\forall H\in\mathfrak{t}\}
and
\Phi_{\rho}:=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{t}(\mathbb{R})^{*}:V_{\alpha}\neq\{0\}\}.
The elements of
\Phi_{\rho}
and the subspaces
V_{\alpha},\ \alpha\in\Phi_{\rho},
are called the
weights
and the
weight subspaces
of the representation
\rho
respectively. If
\alpha,\beta\in\Phi_{\rho}
, we say that
\alpha
is
higher
than
\beta
whenever
\alpha-\beta
is a linear combination of positive roots with non-negative coefficients. A
highest weight
of
\rho
is a weight
\tilde{\nu}
which is higher than every other weight. The following is classical result by Cartan
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem of the highest weight)
.

With the notations above we have that the following assertions hold:

(1)

Every finite-dimensional irreducible complex representation
\rho:\mathfrak{k}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
has a unique highest weight
\tilde{\nu}
which can be written as a linear combination of the fundamental weights
\overline{\omega}_{1},...,\overline{\omega}_{\ell}
with integral non-negative coefficients, that is,

\tilde{\nu}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}\tilde{\nu}_{j}\overline{\omega}_{j},\ \tilde{\nu}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(2.1)

Moreover, two irreducible finite-dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{k}
with the same highest weight are equivalent.

(2)

Every functional of the form (
2.1
) is the highest weight of an irreducible complex representation of
\mathfrak{k}.

It is a known fact that if
\theta:G\rightarrow GL(V)
is an irreducible complex finite-dimensional representation of the group
G
, then the function

\varphi=\frac{1}{d}\chi_{\theta},

(2.2)

where
d=\textnormal{dim}(V)
and
\chi_{\theta}
is the character of the representation
\theta,
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
and conversely, for each
\lambda\in\textnormal{Spec}(\mathcal{L}_{G})\setminus\{0\}
there exists a eigenfunction of the form (
2.2
) associated to the eigenvalue
\lambda.
Furthermore, for each
\lambda\in\textnormal{Spec}(\mathcal{L}_{G})\setminus\{0\}
, there exists a real irreducible representation
\theta_{0}:G\rightarrow GL(V)
such that
\varphi=\frac{1}{d}\chi_{\theta_{0}}
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
corresponding to
\lambda
(see
[
2
, Corollary 3.2]
). The next proposition provides a formula to compute
\textnormal{Spec}(\mathcal{L}_{G})
in terms of real irreducible representations of
G.
We refer to
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
for a proof.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\theta_{0}:G\rightarrow GL(V)
be an irreducible real
d
-dimensional representation of
G
and
\rho_{0}:=(d\theta_{0})_{e}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(V)
its corresponding infinitesimal representation. Then

\lambda_{\theta_{0}}:=-\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\textnormal{Tr}[\rho_{0}(X_{j})^{2}]\in\textnormal{Spec}(\mathcal{L}_{G}).

(2.3)

Since we are assuming that
G
is simply connected, then the restriction
\rho\left|{}_{\mathfrak{g}}\right.
of an irreducible complex representation
\rho
of
\mathfrak{k}
induces an irreducible real representation
\rho_{0}
of
\mathfrak{g}
whose character is uniquely determined by the highest weight
\tilde{\nu}
of
\rho.
On the other hand, irreducible real finite-dimensional representations of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
are in bijective correspondence with irreducible real finite-dimensional representations of the group
G.
So we can parametrise the nonzero eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
with the set of all highest weights
\tilde{\nu}
of irreducible complex finite-dimensional representations of
\mathfrak{k}
which, due to Theorem
2.1
, is nothing but the set of all linear combinations of the fundamental weights with non-negative integral coefficients. Moreover, we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
2
, Theorem 5.20]
)
For each highest weight
\tilde{\nu}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{l}\tilde{\nu}_{j}\overline{\omega}_{j},\ \tilde{\nu}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
the associated eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{G}
is given by

\lambda_{\tilde{\nu}}=(\eta,\eta)-(\tilde{\nu}+\eta,\tilde{\nu}+\eta),

(2.4)

where

\eta:=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\Pi^{+}}\alpha.

Remark 2.4
.

It is well-known that if
p:M\rightarrow N
is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers and
\varphi
is an eigenfunction of the Laplacian operator in
N
, then
\varphi\circ\pi
is an eigenfunction of the Laplacian operator in
M
corresponding to the same eigenvalue
[
4
]
. As a consequence of this fact we have that
\textnormal{Spec}(N)\subseteq\textnormal{Spec}(M)
. In particular, if
G
is a connected (not necessarily simply-connected) compact simple Lie group, then
G\cong\tilde{G}/\Gamma
, where
\tilde{G}
is a connected simply-connected compact simple Lie group (diffeomorphic to the universal cover of
G
) and
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
\tilde{G}
, every bi-invariant metric
g
on
G
is induced by a bi-invariant metric
\tilde{g}
on
\tilde{G}
in such a way that the projection
\pi:\tilde{G}\rightarrow\tilde{G}/\Gamma\cong G
is a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers. This allows us to conclude that, in this situation we have

\textnormal{Spec}(G)\subseteq\textnormal{Spec}(\tilde{G}),

(2.5)

With the notations in
[
20
, Chapter 12]
, we can construct all the irreducible root systems starting from an Euclidean space endowed with an inner product

Reverse Strichartz estimates

In this section, we analyse the Strichartz estimates on compact connected simple Lie groups. It was mentioned in the introduction, we exclude the case

.

Lemma 3.1

(Sogge-Hörmander)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group.
Let
p\in[2,\infty]
and
P_{\lambda_{\nu}}:L^{2}(G)\rightarrow H_{\lambda_{\nu}}
be the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace
H_{\lambda_{\nu}}
corresponding to
\lambda_{\nu}
. Then

\left|\left|P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}\right|\right|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C\sqrt{\lambda_{\nu}}^{\sigma(p)}\left|\left|P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(G)},

(3.1)

where

\sigma(p):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{d-1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right),&&\textnormal{if}\ 2\leq p\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\\
\\
\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{p},&&\textnormal{if}\ \frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}<p\leq\infty.\end{array}\right.

and with the constant
C
in (
3.1
) depending on geometric data of
G.

The following lemma will be crucial in our further analysis. We provide some crude Strichartz estimates that for a range of values of
(p,q)
that could be non-sharp with respect to the values provided by Burq, Gerard and Tzvetkov
[
8
]
, specially if the pair
(p,q)
is admissible. However, in the next sections we will clarify, for which values of
(p,q)
the following lemma provides improvements of the regularity orders in
[
8
]
, in our setting. We observe that these improvements are with respect to the established regularity orders for the Lie groups that we analyse below, but not in the setting of the manifolds considered in
[
8
]
, where their result is sharp on any manifold having closed geodesics with a common period. As it was mentioned in the introduction, we conclude that the geometry of the manifold has a strong incidence in the improvement of Strichartz estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty),

q\in[2,\infty],
and let
b
be as in Table 1. Let
T=2\pi b\gamma.
Then

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,T])}\lesssim\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}N_{R}R^{2(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2})}}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where, for any
R\in\mathbb{N},

N_{R}=\#\left\{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right\}
is the number of solutions of the equation
\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma},
and
\alpha=1/2-1/p
is a loss of regularity order of the
L^{p}
-estimate with respect to the
L^{2}
-norm in the time variable. Moreover, for
p=2,
we have the identity

\forall x\in G,\,||u(\cdot,x)||_{L^{2}([0,T])}=\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2TN_{R}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.2)

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Since
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(G)
, the solution of the linear Schrödinger equation is given by

\forall t\in[0,T],\,\forall x\in G,\,\,u(t,x)=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}t}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x).

In order to estimate the
L^{p}([0,T])
-norm of
u
, we shall use the Wainger-Sobolev embedding theorem, which states that for
1<r<p<\infty,
we have that

\left|\left|\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}e^{itn}\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi])}\lesssim\left|\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n|^{\alpha}a_{n}e^{itn}\right|\right|_{L^{r}([0,2\pi])},\ \alpha=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}.

In particular, when
r=2,
and for any fixed
x\in G,
let us consider the function

f(t)=\sum_{R=0}^{\infty}a_{R}e^{itR},

defined by the Fourier coefficients

a_{R}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:b\gamma\lambda_{\nu}=R}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x),

or in other words, defined by

f(t)=\sum_{R=0}^{\infty}a_{R}e^{itR}=\sum_{\nu}e^{itb\gamma\lambda_{\nu}}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x).

Then the Wainger Sobolev embedding theorem implies that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma\tau}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}a_{R}e^{iR\tau}\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left|\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}R^{\alpha}a_{R}e^{iR\tau}\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma\tau}(\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma)^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi])}.

To estimate the
L^{p}
-norm
||u(t,x)||_{L^{p}([0,2\pi b\gamma])},
let us apply the change of variables
t\mapsto\tau,

t:=b\gamma\tau,
and the estimate
L^{p}
-
L^{2}
-estimate above as follows,

\displaystyle||u(t,x)||_{L^{p}([0,2\pi b\gamma])}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}t}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi b\gamma])}

\displaystyle=(b\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma\tau}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle\lesssim(b\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma\tau}(\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma)^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi])}.

The change of variables
\tau\mapsto s,

s=b\gamma\tau,
gives

\displaystyle(b\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma\tau}(\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma)^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi])}

\displaystyle=(b\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}(\lambda_{\nu}b\gamma)^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi b\gamma])}

\displaystyle=(b\gamma)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}(b\gamma)^{\alpha}\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}\lambda_{\nu}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi b\gamma])}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}\lambda_{\nu}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|_{L^{2}([0,2\pi b\gamma])},

where
\alpha=1/2-1/p.
The last expression above can be computed as
follows:

\displaystyle\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}\lambda_{\nu}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T])}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}\lambda_{\nu}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}\lambda_{\nu}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right)\left(\sum\limits_{\nu^{\prime}}e^{-i\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}s}\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\overline{P_{\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}}u_{0}(x)}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum\limits_{\nu}\sum\limits_{\nu^{\prime}}e^{i(\lambda_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})s}(\lambda_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\overline{P_{\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}}u_{0}(x)}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu,\nu^{\prime}\\
\lambda_{\nu}=\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{i(\lambda_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})s}ds\right](\lambda_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\overline{P_{\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}}u_{0}(x)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu,\nu^{\prime}\\
\lambda_{\nu}\neq\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}e^{i(\lambda_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})s}ds\right](\lambda_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\overline{P_{\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}}u_{0}(x)}.

In the last equality, we can interchange the integration and summation symbols since
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(G).
Recalling that
T=2\pi b\gamma,
we have that

\int_{0}^{T}e^{i(\lambda_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})s}ds=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}T,&&\textnormal{if}\ \lambda_{\nu}=\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}},\\
0,&&\textnormal{if}\ \lambda_{\nu}\neq\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}.\end{array}\right.

Hence, these orthogonality relations allow us to write the equalities

\displaystyle\left|\left|\sum\limits_{\nu}e^{i\lambda_{\nu}s}\lambda_{\nu}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T])}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu,\nu^{\prime}\\
\lambda_{\nu}=\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}T(\lambda_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}})^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{\nu}}u_{0}(x)\overline{P_{\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}}u_{0}(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ \sum\limits_{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}}\ \sum\limits_{\nu^{\prime}:\lambda_{\nu^{\prime}}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}}T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}\sum\limits_{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}}1

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}N_{R},

where

N_{R}:=\sum\limits_{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}}1=\#\left\{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right\}.

So far, we have shown that

||u(t,x)||^{2}_{L^{p}([0,T])}\lesssim\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}N_{R},

or, equivalently,

||u(t,x)||_{L^{p}([0,T])}\lesssim\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}N_{R}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

and also the following equality when

||u(t,x)||_{L^{2}([0,T])}=\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2T\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}N_{R}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By computing the
L^{q}(G)
-norm of

we have that

\displaystyle||u(t,x)||_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,T])}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{G}||u(t,x)||^{q}_{L^{p}([0,T])}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{G}\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\ 2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}N_{R}\right]^{\frac{q}{2}}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{G}\left(2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}N_{R}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dx\right]^{\frac{2}{q}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the last inequality is nothing but the Minkowski integral inequality. Note that

\displaystyle\left\{\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{G}\left(2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}N_{R}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dx\right]^{\frac{2}{q}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{R=0}^{\infty}2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}N_{R}\left[\int_{G}\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}(x)\right|^{q}dx\right]^{\frac{2}{q}}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{R=0}^{\infty}2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}N_{R}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{q}(G)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

and, by Lemma

, we know that
\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{q}(G)}\lesssim\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\sigma(q)}}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}
so that

\displaystyle\left\{\sum_{R=0}^{\infty}2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}N_{R}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{q}(G)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\sum_{R=0}^{\infty}2T\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{2\alpha}N_{R}\left(\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right)^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\sigma(q)}}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2T}}{(b\gamma)^{(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}})}}\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}N_{R}R^{2(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}})}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence

||u(t,x)||_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,T])}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{2T}}{(b\gamma)^{(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}})}}\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}N_{R}R^{2(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}})}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Reverse Strichartz estimates for compact simple Lie groups)
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty].
Let
G
be a compact connected simple Lie group of rank
\ell\geq 2
, let
b
as in Theorem
2.12
, and let
m
be defined by

m=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\ell+1,&&\textnormal{if}\ \tilde{G}\cong\textnormal{SU($\ell+1$)}\ \textnormal{or}\ \tilde{G}\cong G_{2},\\
\ell,&&\textnormal{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{G}
is the universal cover of
G
.
Let
T=2\pi b\gamma.
Then

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,2\pi b\gamma])}\lesssim_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(G)},

(3.3)

where
s\geq\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)
if
m\geq 5,

s>\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)
if
m=2,3,4
and

\sigma(q):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{d-1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right),&&\textnormal{if}\ 2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\\
\\
\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{q},&&\textnormal{if}\ \frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}<q\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\infty.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
G
be a compact connected simple Lie group of rank
\ell\geq 2
. By Theorem
2.12
, there exist integers
b,R_{0}
and an integer matrix of rank
\ell
such that every eigenvalue of the Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{G}
can be written as

\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{1}{b\gamma}\left(n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m}^{2}-R_{0}\right),

(3.4)

where
[n_{1}\ \cdots\ n_{m}]^{T}=A[\nu_{1}\ \cdots\nu_{\ell}]^{T}
for some
(\nu_{1},...,\nu_{\ell})\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}.
On the other hand, in view of Lemma
3.2
we have the following estimate

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,2\pi b\gamma])}\lesssim\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}N_{R}R^{2(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}})}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.5)

with

N_{R}=\#\left\{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right\}

and
\alpha=1/2-1/p
. To estimate
N_{R}
observe that since
A
has maximal rank equal to
\ell
, then the linear map
T_{A}:\nu\mapsto A\nu,
from
\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is injective.
Define the exceptional set

\mathscr{N}_{R}=\{Y=(n_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}\in\textnormal{Im}(T_{A}):n_{1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{m}^{2}=R+R_{0},\,\,\textnormal{ and }A\nu=Y\Longrightarrow\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}\}.

(3.6)

Since there is a bijective correspondence between elements
Y=(n_{1},n_{2},\cdots,n_{m})\in\mathscr{N}_{R}
with solutions of the equation
\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma},
that indeed is provided by the identity
A\nu=Y
, we have that

N_{R}=\#\left\{\nu:\lambda_{\nu}=\frac{R}{b\gamma}\right\}=\#\mathscr{N}_{R}\leq r_{m,2}(R+R_{0}).

(3.7)

Recalling Theorem
2.13
we obtain the following inequalities for any
\varepsilon>0
:

\displaystyle\bullet\ r_{2,2}(R+R_{0})\lesssim_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle(R+R_{0})^{\varepsilon}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\epsilon},

\displaystyle\bullet\ r_{3,2}(R+R_{0})\lesssim\

\displaystyle(R+R_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}[\log(4(R+R_{0}))(\log(\log(4(R+R_{0})))]

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle(R+R_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle R^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\bullet\ r_{4,2}(R+R_{0})\lesssim\

\displaystyle(R+R_{0})\log(\log(R+R_{0}))\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(R+R_{0})^{1+\varepsilon}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{1+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\bullet\ r_{m,2}(R+R_{0})\lesssim\

\displaystyle(R+R_{0})^{\frac{m-2}{2}}\lesssim R^{\frac{m-2}{2}}

or, equivalently,
r_{m,2}(R+R_{0})\lesssim R^{\frac{m-2}{2}+\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
if
m=2,3,4
and for all
\varepsilon\geq 0
if
m\geq 5.

Now, we can estimate the right hand side of (
3.5
) as follows

\displaystyle\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}N_{R}R^{2(\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}})}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum\limits_{R=0}^{\infty}R^{2\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{m-2}{4}+\alpha+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\frac{\sigma(q)}{2}}\right)}\left|\left|P_{\frac{R}{b\gamma}}u_{0}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\asymp\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\varepsilon+\frac{m-2}{2}+2\alpha+\sigma(q)}}.

Observing that
\alpha=1/2-1/p,
and that

\frac{m-2}{2}+2\alpha+\sigma(q)=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q),

(3.8)

we have proved (
3.3
). The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(Strichartz estimates on compact Lie groups)
.

Let
2\leq q\leq p<\infty.
Let
G
be a compact connected simple Lie group of rank
\ell\geq 2
, let
b
as in Theorem
2.12
, and let
m
be defined by

m=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\ell+1,&&\textnormal{if}\ \tilde{G}\cong\textnormal{SU($\ell+1$)}\ \textnormal{or}\ \tilde{G}\cong G_{2},\\
\ell,&&\textnormal{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{G}
is the universal cover of
G
.
Let
T=2\pi b\gamma.
Then

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi\gamma b],L^{q}(G))}\lesssim_{s}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(G)},

(3.14)

where
s\geq\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)
if
m\geq 5,

s>\frac{m}{2}-\frac{2}{p}+\sigma(q)
if
m=2,3,4
and

\sigma(q):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{d-1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right),&&\textnormal{if}\ 2\leq q\leq\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\\
\\
\frac{d-1}{2}-\frac{d}{q},&&\textnormal{if}\ \frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}<q\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\infty.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let us use the Minkowski integral inequality with
p/q\geq 1.
Indeed, observe that

\displaystyle\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{p}([0,2\pi\gamma b],L^{q}(G))}

\displaystyle=\left(\smallint_{0}^{2\pi\gamma\beta}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L^{q}(G)}dt\right)^{1/p}=\left(\left(\smallint_{0}^{2\pi\gamma\beta}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L^{q}(G)}dt\right)^{q/p}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\smallint_{0}^{2\pi\gamma\beta}\left(\smallint_{G}|u(t,x)|^{q}dx\right)^{p/q}dt\right)^{q/p}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\smallint_{G}\left(\smallint_{0}^{2\pi\gamma\beta}|u(t,x)|^{q\times p/q}dt\right)^{q/p}dx\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\left(\smallint_{G}\|u(\cdot,x)\|^{q}_{L^{p}([0,2\pi\gamma b])}dx\right)^{1/q}=\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{q}(G,L^{p}[0,2\pi b\gamma])}.

In view of the previous inequality, it follows from (
3.3
) the estimate in (
3.14
). The proof is complete.
∎

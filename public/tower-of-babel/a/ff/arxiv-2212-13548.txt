Hard Lefschetz properties, complete intersections and numerical dimensions
Jiajun Hu and Jian Xiao

Abstract
. We study the positivity of complete intersections of nef classes. We first give a sufficient and necessary characterization on the complete intersection classes which have hard Lefschetz property on a compact complex torus, equivalently, in the linear case. In turn, this provides us new kinds of cohomology classes which have Hodge-Riemann property or hard Lefschetz property on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold. We also give a complete characterization on when the complete intersection classes are non-vanishing on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold. Both characterizations are given by the numerical dimensions of various partial summations of the given nef classes. As an interesting byproduct, we show that the numerical dimension endows any finite set of nef classes with a loopless polymatroid structure.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we work over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
, while corresponding results will be mentioned when they also hold over an arbitrary algebraically closed field
k
.

As summarized in
[
Huh18
]
, given a mathematical object
X
of “dimension”
d
, it is important to study whether a Kähler package holds on
X
. More precisely, sometimes one can construct from
X
in a natural way a real or complex graded vector space (or even a graded algebra)

A(X)=\bigoplus_{q=0}^{d}A^{q}(X),

together with a bilinear map
P:A(X)\times A(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and a graded linear map
L:A^{\bullet}(X)\rightarrow A^{\bullet+1}(X)
that is symmetric with respect to
P
. The linear operator
L
usually comes from a convex cone
K(X)
in the space of linear operators on
A(X)
.
For a nonnegative integer
q\leq d/2
, a
Kähler package
on
X
usually means the following properties:

(PD):

The bilinear paring

A^{q}(X)\times A^{d-q}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\ (\xi,\eta)\mapsto P(\xi,\eta)

in non-degenerate (the Poincaré duality for
X
)

(HL):

For any
L_{1},...,L_{d-2q}\in K(X)
, the linear map

A^{q}(X)\rightarrow A^{d-q}(X),\ \xi\mapsto\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d-2q}L_{k}\right)\cdot\xi

is bijective (the hard Lefschetz theorem for
X
).

(HR):

For any
L_{1},...,L_{d-2q+1}\in K(X)
, the quadratic form

A^{q}(X)\times A^{q}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\ (\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mapsto(-1)^{q}P\left(\xi_{1},\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d-2q}L_{k}\right)\overline{\xi_{2}}\right)

is positive definite on the kernel of the linear map

A^{q}(X)\rightarrow A^{d-q+1}(X),\ \xi\mapsto\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d-2q+1}L_{k}\right)\cdot\xi

(the Hodge-Riemann relation for
X
).

Sometimes, the set
K(X)
is a convex cone of
A^{1}(X)
, which is called the Kähler cone of
X
. In last decades, novel and exciting Kähler packages were discovered and have been playing key roles in algebra, combinatorics and geometry, see e.g.
[
Huh18
,
Huh22
,
Wil18
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we are interested in the HL and HR parts, and study for which kinds of
L_{1},...,L_{d-2q}
the HL or HR property will hold. We focus on the model and original case – the Kähler package on a compact Kähler manifold.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
\omega
a Kähler class on
X
. As a fundamental piece of Hodge theory, for any integers
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
, the complete intersection class

\Omega=\omega^{n-p-q}\in H^{n-p-q,n-p-q}(X,\mathbb{R}),

has the following properties:

(HL):

The linear map

\Omega:H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{n-q,n-p}(X,\mathbb{C}),\ \phi\mapsto\Omega\cdot\phi

is an isomorphism.

(HR):

The quadratic form
Q
on
H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
, defined by

Q(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=c_{p,q}\Omega\cdot\varphi_{1}\cdot\overline{\varphi_{2}},\ \text{where}\ c_{p,q}=\mathrm{i}^{q-p}(-1)^{(p+q)(p+q+1)/2},

is positive definite on the primitive space
P^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
with respect to
(\Omega,\omega)
, i.e.,
\phi\in P^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
if and only if
\Omega\cdot\omega\cdot\phi=0
.

Note that we arrive at a special case of the above abstract setting for
A(X)
when
p=q
.

We call that
the class
\Omega
has HL property
and
the pair
(\Omega,\omega)
has HR property
.

By replacing the above
\Omega=\omega^{n-p-q}
by an arbitrary cohomology class
\Omega\in H^{n-p-q,n-p-q}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
\omega
by an arbitrary
(1,1)
class
\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
,
it is interesting to study:

When does the class
\Omega
have HL property and when does the pair
(\Omega,\eta)
have HR property? (In order to define the primitive space we need the class
\Omega
coupling with an
(1,1)
class
\eta
.)

A complete answer seems unreachable at this moment, nevertheless, we first study a subclass of
\Omega
, coming from complete intersections:

Question 1.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, and let
\alpha_{1},....,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, then under which assumptions does the complete intersection class

\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot....\cdot\alpha_{n-p-q}

have HL property and does the pair
(\Omega,\eta)
have HR property?

Known results

In
[
DN06
]
(see also
[
Tim98
,
Gro90
]
for related results) and
[
Cat08
]
, it was proved that given any Kähler classes
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{n-p-q+1}
,
the complete intersection class

\Omega=\omega_{1}\cdot...\cdot\omega_{n-p-q}

has HL property,
and the pair
(\Omega,\omega_{n-p-q+1})
has HR property.
This greatly generalizes the classical Kähler package to the mixed situation, in the sense that
\Omega
can be a complete intersection of different Kähler classes. This result has important applications in high dimensional complex dynamics.

In the previous works of the second author
[
Xia21a
,
Xia21b
]
(see also
[
Zha22
]
for further refinement), inspired by Hodge index theorems deduced from complex Hessian equations and hyperbolic polynomials, it was realized that the classical positivity assumption – being Kähler or ample – can be weakened to still ensure that
\Omega
has HL and HR properties.

More precisely, let
\omega
be a Kähler class and
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m-1}
be
m
positive
(1,1)
classes – whose definition will be recalled in Section
2
, then by
[
Xia21a
]
, for
(p,q)=(1,1)
,
the complete intersection class

\Omega=\omega^{n-m}\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-2}

has HL property, and the pair
(\Omega,\alpha_{m-1})
has HR property.
Note that an
m
positive
(1,1)
class (
m<n
) can be degenerate and even negative in certain directions, which is a positivity notion much weaker than being Kähler or ample. For general
(p,q)
, let
p,q,m
be integers such that
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq m\leq n
. Let
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{n-m}
be Kähler classes, and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m-p-q+1}
be semi-positive and
m
positive, then by
[
Xia21b
, Theorem A and Remark 3.9]
we have
that the complete intersection class

\Omega=\omega_{1}\cdot...\cdot\omega_{n-m}\cdot\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-p-q}

has HL property, and that the pair
(\Omega,\alpha_{m-p-q+1})
has HR property.
These results pushforward
[
DN06
]
and
[
Cat08
]
to the mixed setting allowing degenerate positivity.

Still with degenerate positivity, in a more geometric setting
[
dCM02
]
proved the following hard Lefschetz property: if
L
is a line bundle on a complex projective manifold
X
such that a positive power of
L
is generated by its global sections, then the class
\Omega=c_{1}(L)^{n-p-q}

has HL property if and only if
L
is lef, in the sense that

mL=f^{*}A

for some projective semismall morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, where
A
is an ample line bundle on
Y
. In particular, the pair
(\Omega,c_{1}(L))
has HR property. The map
f:X\rightarrow Y
is called semismall if
\dim Y^{k}+2k\leq\dim X
, where
Y^{k}=\{y\in Y|\dim f^{-1}(y)=k\}
. This has been playing important roles in the study of Hodge theory of algebraic maps.

More recently, it was proved in a series of works
[
RT19
,
RT21
,
RT22
]
that characteristic forms/classes of ample vector bundles also produce Kähler packages. Let
\omega
be a Kähler class and
E
an ample vector bundle on
X
, then for
(p,q)=(1,1)
,
the Schur class

\Omega=s_{\lambda}(E),\ \text{with}\ |\lambda|=n-2,

has HL property and the pair
(\Omega,\omega)
has HR property.
Here we only mention a prototype of their results, for more general statements, we refer the reader to the aforementioned references.

Our results

When all the
(1,1)
classes
\alpha_{k}
are nef, we give a complete characterization on those
\Omega
which have HL property, by using the positivity properties of various partial summations of
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q}
, on a compact complex torus, equivalently, in the linear case.

To present our result, we first introduce some notations.

Given a nef
(1,1)
class
\alpha
on a compact Kähler manifold
X
, its numerical dimension, denoted by
\nd(\alpha)
, is defined by

\nd(\alpha)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{N}|\alpha^{k}\neq 0\}.

Equivalently, fix a Kähler class
\omega
,
\nd(\alpha)
is the largest integer
k
such that
\alpha^{k}\cdot\omega^{n-k}>0
.

We denote the set of indices
\{1,2,...,m\}
by
[m]
. Given finite vectors
v_{1},...,v_{m}
in a vector space
V
, for
I\subset[m]
write

v_{I}=\sum_{i\in I}v_{i}.

For a subset
I\subset[m]
, we denote the cardinality of
I
by
|I|
.

Theorem A
.

Let
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma
be a compact complex torus of dimension
n
and
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n-p-q}
. Then for HL property, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the intersection class
\Omega
has HL property;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|+p+q
.

For HR property, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the pair
(\Omega,\eta)
has HR property for
\eta
with
\nd(\eta)\geq p+q
;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|+p+q
.

Theorem
A
is closely related to Alexandrov’s seminal work
[
Ale38
]
and Panov’s further generalization
[
Pan85
]
, see Remark
3.8
. Indeed, Alexandrov’s and Panov’s related results are special cases of Theorem
A
when
p=q=1
,
p=q=0
.

As a consequence of Theorem
A
, we obtain new kinds of cohomology classes on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold, which have HL and HR properties.
The following result generalizes
[
Xia21b
]
.

Corollary A
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n-p-q}
.
Assume that there exists a smooth semi-positive representative
\widehat{\alpha}_{i}
in each class
\alpha_{i}
, such that for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\widehat{\alpha}_{I}
is
|I|+p+q
positive in the sense of forms, and that there exists a smooth semi-positive representative
\widehat{\eta}
in
\eta
, such that
\widehat{\eta}
is
p+q
positive, then

•

the complete intersection class
\Omega
has HL property;

•

the pair
(\Omega,\eta)
has HR property.

Remark 1.2
.

Similar to Theorem
A
, we expect a complete characterization of HL property for complete intersections of nef classes on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold. The requirement for numerical dimensions of various partial summations should be necessary. However, it is clear that this is not sufficient. The sufficiency is more subtle, we hope to address this in a future work.

We consider HL and HR properties as positivity properties of
\Omega
.

Besides HL or HR property, we also study a weaker positivity notion, that is, the non-vanishing property of the complete intersection class

\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}

when each
\alpha_{k}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is a nef class.

Applying Theorem
A
to the case
p=q=0
, for nef classes
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
on a compact complex torus the following are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n}>0
;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[n]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|
.

It is natural to expect that this also holds on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold.
Indeed, we obtain the following complete characterization.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
1\leq m\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the intersection class
\Omega\neq 0
in
H^{m,m}(X,\mathbb{R})
;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[m]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|
.

Similar result also holds for compact Kähler varieties (see Corollary
4.7
).
In the Kähler setting, the above
\Omega\neq 0
is equivalent to that
\Omega
has a representative which is a non-zero positive current of bidegree
(m,m)
.

Remark 1.3
.

The characterization in Theorem
B
structurally looks similar to the characterization of hard Lefschetz property. Indeed, our proof applies a Hodge index type theorem,
and the same criterion holds on a smooth projective variety over an arbitrary algebraically closed field.

Theorem
B
is analogous to the convexity result

[
Sch14
, Theorem 5.1.8]
. Given convex bodies
K_{1},...,K_{n}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the following statements are equivalent:

•

The mixed volume
V(K_{1},...,K_{n})>0
;

•

There are segments
S_{i}\subset K_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, with linearly independent directions;

•

\dim K_{I}\geq|I|
for any
I\subset[n]
.

Among the applications of Theorem
B
, we use it to study when the multidegrees are positive in the Kähler setting (see Theorem
5.2
), extending related results in
[
CCRL+20
]
, where multidegrees for algebraic varieties and their connection with algebra and combinatorics were systematically explored.

In the algebraic setting, the characterization of multidegrees is used to show that the support of multidegrees is a discrete polymatroid, described as the integer points in a generalized permutohedron. These results are closely related to submodular functions.

We relate the characterization of positivity of multidegrees of a Kähler submanifold to numerical dimensions. Combining with the submodularity property in the algebraic setting, we are motivated to ask whether the numerical dimension of nef classes is a submodular function.

We prove the following general result.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
, then for any three nef classes
A,B,C\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
on
X
, we always have

\nd(A+B+C)+\nd(C)\leq\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C).

As a consequence, for any finite set of nef classes
E=\{B_{1},...,B_{m}\}
on
X
, for
I\subset[m]
set

r(I)=\nd(B_{I})

with the convention that
r(\emptyset)=0
, then the function
r(\cdot)
endows with
E
a loopless polymatroid structure.

Remark 1.4
.

The analogous result also holds on a smooth projective variety over an arbitrary algebraically closed field. We expect that Theorem
C
holds on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold, and establish a special case for semi-positive classes (see Proposition
5.10
).

We apply this kind of results to show that discrete polymatroids appear naturally in the study of positivity of multidegrees in the Kähler setting and in the study of HL property in the linear setting (see Corollary
5.13
, Proposition
5.14
).

Organization

This paper is organized as follows. We present some basic definitions and notations in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we give a sufficient and necessary characterization on complete intersection classes of nef classes to have HL property on a compact complex torus, and discuss its some applications. In Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some basic definitions, notations and results that will be used in the paper.

We denote by

•

\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
the space of
(p,q)
forms on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with constant coefficients;

•

\Lambda^{p,p}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\subset\Lambda^{p,p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
the subspace of real
(p,p)
forms.

Fix a coordinate system
(z_{1},...,z_{n})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, a real
(1,1)
form

\alpha=\mathrm{i}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j}

is called

•

semi-positive
if the
n\times n
Hermitian matrix
[a_{ij}]
is semi-positive;

•

Kähler
if
[a_{ij}]
is positive definite.

We sometimes identify the
(1,1)
form
\alpha
with the matrix
[a_{ij}]
, for example,
\ker\alpha
means the kernel of the linear transform associated to
[a_{ij}]
.

Fix a Kähler metric
\omega
with constant coefficients on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. A real
(1,1)
form
\alpha\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is called

•

m
positive
with respect to
\omega
, if

\alpha^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}>0

for every
k
with
1\leq k\leq m
.

This kind of positivity notion arises naturally in the study of Hessian equations.
Assume that

\omega=\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{i},\ \alpha=\mathrm{i}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{i},

then
\alpha
is
m
positive with respect to
\omega
if and only if

s_{k}(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})>0,\ 1\leq k\leq m,

where
s_{k}
is the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial with
n
variables.

The form
\alpha
is called

•

exactly
m
positive
if it is
m
positive but not
m+l
positive for any
l\geq 1
.

It is easy to see:

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
\alpha\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is semi-positive, then

\alpha
is
m
positive if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(1)

\alpha
has no less than
m
positive eigenvalues;

(2)

\alpha^{m}\wedge\omega^{n-m}>0
;

(3)

\codim\ker\alpha\geq m
, where

\displaystyle\ker\alpha

\displaystyle=\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\alpha(v,w)=0,\forall w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

\displaystyle=\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\alpha(v,v)=0\}.

On a complex manifold
X
, similar positivity notions (e.g.,
m
positivity, exact
m
positivity) can be defined if the forms satisfy the positivity requirement at every point of
X
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with a Kähler metric
\widehat{\omega}
and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
, then
\alpha
is called nef if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a smooth representative
\widehat{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\in\alpha
such that

\widehat{\omega}_{\varepsilon}>-\varepsilon\widehat{\omega}.

Equivalently,
\alpha
is nef if and only if it is in the closure of the Kähler cone of
X
. Furthermore, if
X
is projective and
\alpha
is a real divisor class, then
\alpha
is nef if and only if

\alpha\cdot[C]\geq 0

for any effective
1
-cycle
C
. The analytic nefness and algebraic nefness are equivalent for divisor classes on a complex projective manifold.

Let
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma
be a compact complex torus. It is well known that, by harmonic forms with respect to the standard Euclidean metric
\omega=\mathrm{i}\sum dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{i}
on
X
, we have

H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})\cong\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

and the cup product of cohomology classes is identified with the wedge product of differential forms. A class
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is nef if and only if it has a representative
\widehat{\alpha}\in\alpha
such that
\widehat{\alpha}
is a semi-positive form with constant coefficients.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold with a Kähler class
\omega
and
\alpha\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
a nef class, then the numerical dimension of
\alpha
is given by

\displaystyle\nd(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\max\{k|\alpha^{k}\cdot\omega^{n-k}>0\}

\displaystyle=\max\{k|\alpha^{k}\neq 0\}.

The following lemma is clear.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma
be a compact complex torus, and
\alpha
a nef class on
X
. Let
\widehat{\alpha}\in\alpha
be the semi-positive representative with constant coefficients. Then
\nd(\alpha)
is the number of positive eigenvalues of
\widehat{\alpha}
and is also equal to
\codim\ker\widehat{\alpha}
.

A
polymatroid
on a finite set
E
is given by a rank function
r:2^{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying the following axioms:

•

(Submodularity) For any
A_{1},A_{2}\subset E
, we have
r(A_{1}\cup A_{2})+r(A_{1}\cap A_{2})\leq r(A_{1})+r(A_{2})
;

•

(Monotonicity) For any
A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset E
, we have
r(A_{1})\leq r(A_{2})
;

•

(Normalization) For the empty set
\emptyset
,
r(\emptyset)=0
.

A polymatroid is called loopless, if the rank of any nonempty subset is nonzero.

A polymatroid is a
matroid
if it also satisfies the “boundedness” axiom: for any
A\subset E
, we have

r(A)\leq|A|.

A discrete polymatroid
\mathcal{P}
on
[m]
with the rank function
r
is a collection of points in
\mathbb{N}^{m}
of the following form

\mathcal{P}=\{\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},...,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}|n_{[m]}=r([m])\ \text{and}\ n_{I}\leq r(I),\ \forall I\subsetneq[m]\}.

Example 2.3
.

(see
[
CCRL+20
, Definition 2.16]
)
Let
V_{1},...,V_{m}
be linear subspaces of a vector space
V
over some field

r(I)=\dim(\sum_{i\in I}V_{i}),\ I\subset[m],

defines a polymatroid on
[m]
.
A matroid/polymatroid of this kind is called linear or representable over the field

.

Let

be a field extension and let

, be intermediate field extensions, then the rank function

defines a polymatroid, where
\wedge_{i\in I}L_{i}
is the smallest subfield in

.
A matroid/polymatroid of this type is called algebraic over the field

.

Over a field of characteristic zero, the algebraic and representable polymatroids coincide

]
.

The Vamós matroid

In the study of combinatorial properties of numerical dimensions, the “reverse Khovanskii-Teissier inequalities” noted in

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

A_{1},...,A_{k},B,C_{1},...,C_{n-k}
be nef
(1,1)
classes. Then the following inequality holds:

\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}(A_{1}\cdot...\cdot A_{k}\cdot B^{n-k})(B^{k}\cdot C_{1}\cdot...\cdot C_{n-k})\geq(B^{n})(A_{1}\cdot...\cdot A_{k}\cdot C_{1}\cdot...\cdot C_{n-k}).

The terminology “reverse Khovanskii-Teissier inequalities” was first introduced in the work
[
LX16
]
, due to the fact that the classical Khovanskii-Teissier inequalities give a lower bound of the intersection number
A_{1}\cdot...\cdot A_{k}\cdot C_{1}\cdot...\cdot C_{n-k}
and our result gives an upper bound by interpolating an auxiliary nef class
B
.

Remark 2.5
.

For a projective variety over an arbitrary algebraically closed field, analogous estimates were established in
[
JL21
]
by using multipoint Okounkov bodies.

3.
Hard Lefschetz property

In this section, we give a sufficient and necessary characterization on complete intersection classes of nef classes to have HL property on a compact complex torus.

Recall that we are going to prove:

Theorem 3.1

(=Theorem
A
)
.

Let
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma
be a compact complex torus of dimension
n
and
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n-p-q}
. Then for HL property, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the intersection class
\Omega
has HL property;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|+p+q
.

For HR property, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the pair
(\Omega,\eta)
has HR property for
\eta
with
\nd(\eta)\geq p+q
;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|+p+q
.

The result is inspired by
[
Pan85
]
and
[
Xia21a
,
Xia21b
]
.

As explained in Section
2
, on a compact complex torus this is equivalent to the Kähler package in the linear setting: we identify
H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
with
\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and identify nef classes with semi-positive forms with constant coefficients on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In the following, we use these identifications.

We first show that the positivity assumptions imply HL or HR property.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
and
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be semi-positive
(1,1)
forms.
Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q}
.
Assume that
\eta
is
p+q
positive and
\alpha_{I}
is
|I|+p+q
positive for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
. Then the followings holds:

(1)

The form
\Omega
has HL property, i.e., the linear map given by wedge product with
\Omega
:

\Omega:\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\rightarrow\Lambda^{n-q,n-p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is an isomorphism.

(2)

The pair
(\Omega,\eta)
has HR property, i.e., the quadratic form

Q(\Phi,\Psi)=c_{p,q}\Omega\wedge\Phi\wedge\overline{\Psi}

is positive definite on the primitive space

P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{\Phi\in\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\Omega\wedge\eta\wedge\Phi=0\}.

It is clear that the HR property implies the HL property.

Denote the HL and HR statements of Proposition
3.2
by
\HL_{n}
and
\HR_{n}
respectively.
We prove the result by induction on dimension, which is inspired by
[
Tim98
]
(see also
[
Xia21b
]
).

We first show that
\HR_{n-1}\Rightarrow\HL_{n}
, whose proof is carried out in Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q}
be real
(1,1)
forms as in Proposition
3.2
.
Assume
\HR_{n-1}
, then for any
\Phi\in\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
satisfying

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q}\wedge\Phi=0

and
for any hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have that

Q_{H}(\Phi_{|H},\Phi_{|H}):=c_{p,q}{\alpha_{1}}_{|H}\wedge...\wedge{\alpha_{n-p-q-1}}_{|H}\wedge\Phi_{|H}\wedge\overline{\Phi_{|H}}\geq 0.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=

\displaystyle\{I\subset[n-p-q-1]|\dim\ker\alpha_{I}=n-p-q-|I|\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{I\subset[n-p-q-1]|\alpha_{I}\ \text{is exactly}\ p+q+|I|\ \text{positive}\}.

We first consider the case when
\mathcal{I}\neq\emptyset
.

In this case, note that for any
I\in\mathcal{I}
, we have

|I|\leq n-p-q-1.

It follows that

\dim\ker\alpha_{I}=n-p-q-|I|\geq 1.

We take
0\neq v_{I}\in\ker\alpha_{I}
for each
I\in\mathcal{I}
.

Let

\mathcal{H}=\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|v_{I}\notin H,\forall I\in\mathcal{I}\}.

We claim that for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
, the forms

({\alpha_{1}}_{|H},...,{\alpha_{n-p-q-1}}_{|H},{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H})

satisfies the positivity conditions of
\HR_{n-1}
, i.e.,

•

{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H}
is
p+q
positive;

•

{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}
is
|I|+p+q
positive for any
I\subset[n-p-q-1]
.

The former is obvious, since

\alpha_{n-p-q}
is
p+q+1
positive by our assumption and
H
is a hyperplane. To show the latter, we consider two cases.
If
I\in\mathcal{I}
, then

\ker{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}=H\cap\ker\alpha_{I}\subsetneq\ker\alpha_{I},

since
H
does not contain a non-zero vector
v_{I}
of
\ker\alpha_{I}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\dim\ker{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}

\displaystyle=\dim\ker\alpha_{I}-1

\displaystyle=(n-1)-p-q-|I|,

which implies that
{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}
is
p+q+|I|
positive.
If
I\notin\mathcal{I}
, then
\alpha_{I}
is
p+q+|I|+1
positive by the definition of
\mathcal{I}
. Then
{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}
is
automatically
p+q+|I|
positive.

This finishes the proof of the claim.

Still suppose
H\in\mathcal{H}
. By assumption,

{\alpha_{1}}_{|_{H}}\wedge...\wedge{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|_{H}}\wedge\Phi_{|_{H}}=0.

Using the above claim and the assumption
\HR_{n-1}
, we see that

Q_{H}(\Phi_{|H},\Phi_{|H})\geq 0.

On the other hand,
\mathcal{H}
is nothing but a projective space removing finitely many proper linear subspaces. Then
\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
By continuity, we get that

Q_{H}(\Phi_{|H},\Phi_{|H})\geq 0,

for any hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Now we consider the remaining case when
\mathcal{I}=\emptyset
. By the definition of
\mathcal{I}
, in this case, for any
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\alpha_{I}
is at least
p+q+|I|+1
positive. For any
H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, the forms

({\alpha_{1}}_{|H},...,{\alpha_{n-p-q-1}}_{|H},{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H})

satisfy the positivity condition of
\HR_{n-1}
,
thus the above discussion still holds.

This finishes the proof.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q}
be real
(1,1)
forms as in Proposition
3.2
.
Assume
\HR_{n-1}
, then any
\Phi\in\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
satisfying

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q}\wedge\Phi=0

vanishes.

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{J}=\{I\subset[n-p-q-1]|\ker\alpha_{I}\not\subset\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\}.

As in Lemma
3.3
, we first consider the case when
\mathcal{J}\neq\emptyset
.

In this case, take a

v_{I}\in\ker\alpha_{I}\backslash\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}

for each
I\in\mathcal{J}
.

Let

\mathcal{S}=\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\subset H\ \text{and}\ v_{I}\notin H,\forall I\in\mathcal{J}\}.

We first show that
\Phi_{|_{H}}=0
for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
.

In fact, for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, since
\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\subset H
we can diagonalize
\alpha_{n-p-q}
in the following way:

\alpha_{n-p-q}=\mathrm{i}\gamma_{1}dH\wedge d\overline{H}+\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\gamma_{j}dH_{j}\wedge d\overline{H_{j}},

with
\gamma_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{j}\geq 0
for
2\leq j\leq n
. Here,
dH
denotes the direction orthogonal to the plane
H
.

Then
\Omega\wedge\Phi=0
implies that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=c_{p,q}\Omega\wedge\Phi\wedge\overline{\Phi}

\displaystyle=c_{p,q}\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q-1}\wedge\Phi\wedge\overline{\Phi}\wedge(\mathrm{i}\gamma_{1}dH\wedge\overline{dH}+\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\gamma_{j}dH_{j}\wedge\overline{dH_{j}})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1}Q_{H}(\Phi_{|H},\Phi_{|H})+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\gamma_{j}Q_{H_{j}}(\Phi_{|{H_{j}}},\Phi_{|{H_{j}}})

Lemma
3.3
implies that

Q_{H}(\Phi_{|H},\Phi_{|H})\geq 0

and

Q_{H_{j}}(\Phi_{|{H_{j}}},\Phi_{|{H_{j}}})\geq 0

for any
2\leq j\leq n
.
Then all the summands of the above equation must be zero. Together with
\gamma_{1}>0
, we get

Q_{H}(\Phi_{|H},\Phi_{|H})=0

Moreover, it is not hard to check that the forms
{\alpha_{1}}_{|H},...,{\alpha_{n-p-q-1}}_{|H},{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H}
have the desired positivity for
\HR_{n-1}
:

•

it is clear that
{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H}
is
p+q
positive on
H
;

•

for
I\subset[n-p-q-1]
, if
I\in\mathcal{J}
, since
v_{I}\in\ker\alpha_{I}
and
v_{I}\notin H
,
{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}
is
|I|+p+q
positive;

•

for
I\subset[n-p-q-1]
, if
I\notin\mathcal{J}
, we have
\ker\alpha_{I}\subset\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}
, which implies that

\ker\alpha_{I\cup\{n-p-q\}}=\ker\alpha_{I}.

Since
\alpha_{I\cup\{n-p-q\}}
is
|I|+p+q+1
positive by assumption,
\alpha_{I}
is also
|I|+p+q+1
positive, yielding that
{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}
is
|I|+p+q
positive.

It follows from
\HR_{n-1}
that
\Phi_{|H}=0
for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, as desired.

By the definition of
\mathcal{S}
, we have

\mathcal{S}=\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\subset H\}\backslash\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{J}}\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|v_{I}\in H\}.

Note that
v_{I}\notin\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}
for any
I\in\mathcal{J}
. Hence
\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|v_{I}\in H\}
intersects
properly with

\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\subset H\}.

Therefore,

\overline{\mathcal{S}}=\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\subset H\}.

By continuity, we get that
\Phi_{|H}=0
for any
H\supset\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}
.

By assumption,
\alpha_{n-p-q}
is
p+q+1
positive, that is,

\dim\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\leq n-1-p-q.

Hence for any
w_{1},...,w_{p+q}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,

\dim\Span\{w_{1},...,w_{p+q},\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\}\leq n-1.

We can extend this linear subspace to a hyperplane
H\in\overline{\mathcal{S}}
. Then

\Phi(w_{1},...,w_{p+q})=\Phi_{|H}(w_{1},...,w_{p+q})=0.

Since
w_{1},...,w_{p+q}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
are arbitrary, we get
\Phi=0
.

In the case when
\mathcal{J}=\emptyset
, we let

\mathcal{S}=\{H\in\Gr^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}\subset H\}.

It is not hard to check that for any
H\in\mathcal{S}
, the forms
{\alpha_{1}}_{|H},...,{\alpha_{n-p-q-1}}_{|H},{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H}
have the desired positivity for
\HR_{n-1}
:

•

{\alpha_{n-p-q}}_{|H}
is
p+q
positive on
H
;

•

for any
I\subset[n-p-q-1]
, we have
\ker\alpha_{I}\subset\ker\alpha_{n-p-q}
, which implies that
{\alpha_{I}}_{|H}
is
|I|+p+q
positive.

Thus the above argument for
\mathcal{J}\neq\emptyset
works in the same way.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Next we show that
\HL_{n}\Rightarrow\HR_{n}
, whose proof is carried out in Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Notations and assumptions as in Proposition
3.2
,
assume
\HL_{n}
, then we have that

•

the Lefschetz decomposition holds, i.e., the

Q
-orthogonal decomposition:

\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\eta\wedge\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\oplus P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

where we use the convention that
\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{0\}
when
p=0
or
q=0
.

•

\dim P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\dim\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})-\dim\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta,\eta
have the desired positivity for
\HL_{n}
on
\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, therefore by the assumption
\HL_{n}
,

\Omega\wedge\eta^{2}:\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\rightarrow\Lambda^{n-q+1,n-p+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is a linear isomorphism.
In particular,

\eta:\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\rightarrow\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is an injection and

\Omega\wedge\eta:\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\rightarrow\Lambda^{n-q+1,n-p+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is a surjection with
\ker\Omega\wedge\eta=P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Again, since
\Omega\wedge\eta^{2}
is an isomorphism, we have

\eta\wedge\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cap P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{0\}.

Take the dimensions into account, one finds that

\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\eta\wedge\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\oplus P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

The orthogonality is straightforward by definition.

Finally the identity for
\dim P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
follows from the discussions above.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Notations and assumptions as in Proposition
3.2
,
assume
\HL_{n}
, then the quadratic form

Q(\Phi,\Psi)=c_{p,q}\Omega\wedge\Phi\wedge\overline{\Psi}

is positive definite on the primitive space

P^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\{\Phi\in\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\Omega\wedge\eta\wedge\Phi=0\}.

Proof.

Fix a Kähler form
\omega
and for
t\in[0,1]
set

\alpha_{j}^{t}=(1-t)\alpha_{j}+t\omega,\eta^{t}=(1-t)\eta+t\omega,\Omega^{t}=\alpha_{1}^{t}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q}^{t}

and

Q_{t}(\Phi,\Psi)=c_{p,q}\Omega^{t}\wedge\Phi\wedge\overline{\Psi}.

By Lemma
3.5
, we have the
Q_{t}
-orthogonal decomposition:

\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})=\eta^{t}\wedge\Lambda^{p-1,q-1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\oplus P^{p,q}_{t},

where

P^{p,q}_{t}=\{\Phi\in\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})|\Omega^{t}\wedge\eta^{t}\wedge\Phi=0\}.

By Lemma
3.5
again, the dimension of
P^{p,q}_{t}
is stable under deformation.

Since
Q_{1}
is positive definite on
P^{p,q}_{1}
by the classical Hodge theory and the quadratic form
Q_{t}
is non-degenerate in the course of deformation by the assumption
\HL_{n}
, it follows that
Q_{0}
is positive definite on
P^{p,q}_{0}
.

This finishes the proof.
∎

We have finished the proof that the positivity assumption implies HL or HR property.

Next we show that the the positivity assumption is also necessary.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
and
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q}\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be semi-positive
(1,1)
forms.
If there exists some
I\subset[n-p-q]
such that
\alpha_{I}
is not
|I|+p+q
positive, then the form

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q}

does not have HL property.

Proof.

We may suppose
I=[k]
for some
1\leq k\leq n-p-q
and assume that

\alpha_{[k]}=\alpha_{1}+...+\alpha_{k}

is not
k+p+q
positive.

Without loss of generalities, by taking a coordinates transform we can diagonalize
\alpha_{[k]}
as following:

\alpha_{[k]}=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{k+p+q-1}\gamma_{j}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z_{j}},\ \gamma_{j}\geq 0.

Since
\ker\alpha_{[k]}\subset\ker\alpha_{j}
for any
j\in I
, the product of
\alpha_{j}
takes the form

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset[k+p+q-1]\\
|I|=|J|=k\end{subarray}}u_{I,J}dz_{I}\wedge d\overline{z_{J}}.

Consider the linear subspaces:

\displaystyle H_{1}=\Span\{dz_{I}\wedge d\overline{z_{J}}|I,J\subset[k+p+q-1],|I|=p,|J|=q\}\subset\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

and

\displaystyle H_{2}=\Span\{dz_{I}\wedge d\overline{z_{J}}|I,J\subset[k+p+q-1],|I|=p+k,|J|=q+k\}\subset\Lambda^{p+k,q+k}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Note that
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are stablized by
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}
in the sense that

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}\wedge H_{1}\subset H_{2}.

When
p=0
or
q=0
, it is easy to see that
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}\wedge H_{1}=\{0\}
, therefore
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}
is not injective on
\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.

Next we consider the case when
p\geq 1,q\geq 1
.
Note that

\displaystyle\dim H_{1}

\displaystyle=\binom{k+p+q-1}{p}\binom{k+p+q-1}{q},

\displaystyle\dim H_{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{k+p+q-1}{p+k}\binom{k+p+q-1}{q+k}.

It follows that

\dim H_{1}>\dim H_{2}

and thus

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}:\Lambda^{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\rightarrow\Lambda^{p+k,q+k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})

is not injective. A fortiori,
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n-p-q}
is not injective.
∎

Together with Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.7
, we finish the proof of Theorem
A
.

Remark 3.8
.

Let
\Herm_{n}
be the space of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices, and let
A_{1},...,A_{n}\in\Herm_{n}
. The mixed discriminant of
A_{1},...,A_{n}
is given by

D(A_{1},...,A_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t_{1}...\partial t_{n}}\det(t_{1}A_{1}+...+t_{n}A_{n}).

The matrices
A_{1},...,A_{n-2}
induce a quadratic form
Q
on
\Herm_{n}
:

\displaystyle Q:

\displaystyle\Herm_{n}\times\Herm_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(A,B)\mapsto D(A,B,A_{1},...,A_{n-2})

If all
A_{1},...,A_{n-2}
are positive definite, then
Q
is a Lorentzian form (i.e., a quadratic form with signature
(+,-,\cdots,-)
), which was shown in the seminal work of Alexandrov
[
Ale38
]
(see also
[
Ale96
]
).

Panov
[
Pan85
]
generalized this important result to semi-positive real symmetric matrices
A_{1},...,A_{n-2}
under certain non-degenerate assumption and proved that the assumption is also necessary. While Panov’s results are only stated for real symmetric matrices, it also works for Hermitian matrices without any difficulty.

We give a brief summary of Panov’s work.
Denote the number of positive eigenvalues of a semi-positive matrix
A
by
r(A)
.
Assume that
A_{1},...,A_{n}\in\Herm_{n}
are semi-positive, then the following equivalence holds:

•

D(A_{1},A_{2},...,A_{n})>0
;

•

r(A_{I})\geq|I|,\ \text{for any}\ I\subset[n]
.

and the following statements are equivalent:

•

Q=D(-,-,A_{1},...,A_{n-2})
is a Lorentzian form on
\Herm_{n}
;

•

r(A_{I})\geq|I|+2,\ \text{for any}\ I\subset[n-2]
.

Furthermore, the work
[
Pan85
]
also give a thorough description of the degeneration of
Q
when
A_{1},...,A_{n-2}
do not satisfy the above non-degenerate condition.

Note that we have the isomorphism

\displaystyle\phi:

\displaystyle\Herm_{n}\rightarrow\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n}),

\displaystyle A=[a_{jk}]\mapsto\mathrm{i}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}a_{jk}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z_{k}}.

Denote
\alpha_{k}=\phi(A_{k})
, then we have (up to a constant volume form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
)

D(A_{1},...,A_{n})=\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n}.

By the above identification, one can use the
p=q=1
and
p=q=0
cases of Theorem
A
to deduce Panov’s results.

In the following, we discuss some consequences of Theorem
A
.

The first is an application to the case
p=q=0
that we mentioned in Remark
3.8
. We restate it using positivity. Note that the semi-positive
(1,1)
forms
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
has HL property if and only if

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n}>0.

Therefore, applying Theorem
A
, we get a description of positivity of complete intersection of semi-positive real
(1,1)
forms.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n}\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be semi-positive
(1,1)
forms. Then the following statements are equivalent:

•

\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{n}>0

•

\alpha_{I}
is
|I|
positive, for any
I\subset[n]
.

The second is an application to the products of forms satisfying HL property.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k},\beta_{1},...,\beta_{l}\in\Lambda^{1,1}_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
be semi-positive
(1,1)
forms with
k+l\leq n
. Denote
\Phi=\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k}
and
\Psi=\beta_{1}\wedge...\wedge\beta_{l}
. Assume that
\Phi,\Psi
have HL property, then
\Phi\wedge\Psi
also has HL property.

Proof.

By Theorem
A
,
\Phi
has HL property on
\Lambda^{n-k}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
if and only if for any subset
I\subset[k]
,

\dim\ker\alpha_{I}\leq k-|I|.

Similarly,
\Psi
has HL property on
\Lambda^{n-l}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
if and only if for any subset
J\subset[l]
,

\dim\ker\beta_{J}\leq l-|J|.

Therefore, for any subset
I\subset[k]
and any subset
J\subset[l]
,

\dim\ker(\alpha_{I}+\beta_{J})\leq\min(k-|I|,l-|J|)\leq k+l-(|I|+|J|).

Together with

\dim\ker\alpha_{I}\leq k+l-|I|

for any subset
I\subset[k]
and

\dim\ker\beta_{J}\leq k+l-|J|

any subset
J\subset[l]
, we obtain that
\Phi\wedge\Psi
also has HL property on
\Lambda^{n-k-l}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by
Theorem
A
again.

∎

Using the linear version, we obtain new kinds of cohomology classes on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold, which have HL and HR properties. Recall that we need to prove:

Corollary 3.11

(=Corollary
A
)
.

Let X be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
0\leq p,q\leq p+q\leq n
. Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n-p-q}
.
Assume that there exists a smooth representative
\widehat{\alpha}_{i}
in each class
\alpha_{i}
, such that for any subset
I\subset[n-p-q]
,
\widehat{\alpha}_{I}
is
|I|+p+q
positive in the sense of forms, and that there exists a smooth representative
\widehat{\eta}
in
\eta
, such that
\widehat{\eta}
is
p+q
positive, then

•

the complete intersection class
\Omega
has HL property;

•

the pair
(\Omega,\eta)
has HR property.

The proof from the local setting to the global setting is standard, following essentially from
[
DN06
]
(see also
[
Xia21b
]
). For reader’s convenience, we give a sketch of the key argument.

We denote the space of smooth complex
(p,q)
forms on
X
by
\Lambda^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and the space of primitive
(p,q)
classes with respect to
(\Omega,\eta)
by
P^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
.

The global setting can be reduced to the local setting by solving a
dd^{c}
-equation.

Lemma 3.12
.

Notations as in Corollary
3.11
, denote
\widehat{\Omega}=\widehat{\alpha}_{1}\wedge...\wedge\widehat{\alpha}_{n-p-q}
, then for any smooth
d
-closed form
\widehat{\Phi}\in\Lambda^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
with its class
\{\widehat{\Phi}\}\in P^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
, there is a smooth
(p-1,q-1)
form
\widehat{F}
such that

(1)

\widehat{\Omega}\wedge\widehat{\eta}\wedge dd^{c}\widehat{F}=\widehat{\Omega}\wedge\widehat{\eta}\wedge\widehat{\Phi}.

Proof.

By applying Theorem
A
, the proof is similar to
[
DN06
, Proposition 2.3]
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.11
.

Assume that
\Phi\in P^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
and let
\widehat{\Phi}
be a smooth representative of the class
\Phi
. Then by Lemma
3.12
, there is a smooth form
\widehat{F}
such that

(2)

\widehat{\Omega}\wedge\widehat{\eta}\wedge(\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F})=0.

Thus
\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F}
is a primitive
(p,q)
form with respect to
\widehat{\Omega}\wedge\widehat{\eta}
.

Applying Theorem
A
, we have that

(3)

c_{p,q}\widehat{\Omega}\wedge(\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F})\wedge\overline{(\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F})}\geq 0

at every point.
By Stokes formula,

(4)

Q(\Phi,\Phi)=c_{p,q}\int\widehat{\Omega}\wedge(\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F})\wedge\overline{(\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F})}\geq 0.

Moreover, if
Q(\Phi,\Phi)=0
, then we have equalities everywhere. In particular, (
3
) is an equality at every point. By Theorem
A
, this yields

\widehat{\Phi}-dd^{c}\widehat{F}=0

on
X
. Thus
\Phi=0
in
H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
. This finishes the proof of the global HR.

As an immediate consequence, we get the global HL. We only need to check that

\Omega:H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{n-q,n-p}(X,\mathbb{C})

is injective. Assume that
\Phi\in H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
satisfies
\Omega\cdot\Phi=0
, then
\Phi
is primitive and
Q(\Phi,\Phi)=0
. By HR,
\Phi=0
, which finishes the proof of the HL.

This finishes the proof of Corollary
3.11
.

∎

4.
Non-vanishing property

Applying Theorem
A
to the case
p=q=0
, for nef classes
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-p-q},\eta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
on a compact complex torus the following are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n}>0
;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[n]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|
.

It is natural to expect that this also holds on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold.

In this section, we give a complete characterization on the non-vanishing of complete intersections of nef classes. We are going to prove:

Theorem 4.1

(=Theorem
B
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
,
1\leq m\leq n
and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the intersection class
\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\neq 0
in
H^{m,m}(X,\mathbb{R})
;

(2)

for any subset
I\subset[m]
,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|
.

The proof applies a generalized version of Hodge index theorem on compact Kähler manifolds, whose algebraic setting had been proved in
[
Luo90
]
. In the following, we prove a Kähler version by similar argument.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-2}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n-2}
and

Q(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\Omega\cdot\beta_{1}\cdot\beta_{2}

the quadratic form on
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Assume that
\alpha
is a real
(1,1)
class satisfying
Q(\alpha,\alpha)>0
, then for any
\beta\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfying

Q(\alpha,\beta)=0

one has
Q(\beta,\beta)\leq 0
, and the equality holds if and only if
\Omega\cdot\beta=0
in
H^{n-1,n-1}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Fix a Kähler class
\omega
on
X
. For
t>0
, consider

\Omega_{t}=(\alpha_{1}+t\omega)\cdot...\cdot(\alpha_{n-2}+t\omega)

and the corresponding quadratic form
Q_{t}
. By
[
DN06
,
Cat08
]
or Corollary
A
,
Q_{t}
has signature
(+,-,...,-)
, which yields that for any
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
,

Q_{t}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{2}\geq Q_{t}(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})Q_{t}(\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}).

Let
t
tend to zero, we thus obtain the Hodge index inequality

Q(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})^{2}\geq Q(\alpha^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})Q(\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}).

Together with the assumption on
\alpha,\beta
and letting
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha,\beta^{\prime}=\beta
, we have

Q(\beta,\beta)\leq 0.

This inequality implies that
Q
is non-positive on the hyperplane

P=\ker\Omega\cdot\alpha\curvearrowright H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R}).

By the assumption,
Q
is positive definite on the subspace
\mathbb{R}\alpha
. Therefore,
Q
has signature of the form
(+,-,...,-,0,...,0)
.

According to the signature, we fix a basis
e_{k}
(with
e_{1}=\alpha
),
1\leq k\leq h^{1,1}
, satisfying that
Q(e_{k},e_{l})=0
for
k\neq l
,
Q(e_{1},e_{1})>0
,
Q(e_{i},e_{i})<0
for
2\leq i\leq s
and
Q(e_{i},-)=0
for
s+1\leq i\leq n
. Thus
\beta\in P
yields that

\beta=\sum_{i\geq 2}c_{i}e_{i}.

If we further have
Q(\beta,\beta)=0
, then

\beta=\sum_{i:\ Q(e_{i},-)=0}c_{i}e_{i},

implying that
\Omega\cdot\beta=0
.

This finishes the proof.
∎

As a corollary, one can get the characterization of equality of a generalized version of Teissier’s proportionality problem by using
(n-1,n-1)
classes.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n-2}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes on
X
. Denote
\Omega=\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{n-2}
. Assume that
\alpha,\gamma\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
are nef class satisfying that

Q(\alpha,\gamma)^{2}=Q(\alpha,\alpha)Q(\gamma,\gamma),

then
\Omega\cdot\gamma
and
\Omega\cdot\alpha
are proportional.

Proof.

Without loss of generalities, we can assume that both
\Omega\cdot\gamma
and
\Omega\cdot\alpha
are non-zero.

If
Q(\alpha,\alpha)>0
, we just need to note that

\beta:=\gamma-\frac{Q(\alpha,\gamma)}{Q(\alpha,\alpha)}\alpha\in P=\ker\Omega\cdot\alpha

and
Q(\beta,\beta)=0
, then Proposition
4.2
implies the result.

If
Q(\alpha,\alpha)=0
, then by the assumption we also have
Q(\alpha,\gamma)=0
.
If
Q(\gamma,\gamma)>0
, then we may replace
\alpha
by
\gamma
and the previous discussion applies.
Thus we may suppose
Q(\gamma,\gamma)=0
. Consider the subspace
\Span\{\alpha,\gamma\}
. If it is of dimension one, then there is nothing to prove. Therefore we may assume that it is of dimension two. Fix a Kähler class
\omega
, then
\Span\{\alpha,\gamma\}
has a nontrivial intersection with the hyperplane
\ker\Omega\cdot\omega
, i.e., there exist two numbers
c,d
, not all zero, such that
Q(\omega,c\alpha+d\gamma)=0
. The aforementioned vanishing properties of
\alpha,\gamma
imply that

Q(c\alpha+d\gamma,c\alpha+d\gamma)=0.

Then applying Proposition
4.2
again shows that
\Omega\cdot(c\alpha+d\gamma)=0
, implying the result.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We remark that Proposition
4.2
and Corollary
4.3
were also recently proved in
[
Zha21
]
with a different method, and similar results for
b
-divisors in the algebraic setting were studied in
[
DF22
]
.

Now we can prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

It is clear if the intersection class
\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\neq 0
, then
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|
for any subset
I\subset[m]
.

For the converse direction, we prove the result by induction on
m
.

Assume that the result holds for
m-1
nef classes, in particular, we have

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m+1}>0.

We first show that under the assumption on
I=[m]
, i.e.,

\alpha_{[m]}^{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}>0,

we must have

(5)

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\alpha_{[m]}\cdot\omega^{n-m}>0.

Otherwise, assume that

(6)

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\alpha_{[m]}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0,

then by Corollary
4.3
,

\alpha_{[m]}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=c\alpha_{1}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m}

for some
c>0
. By (
6
), this implies that

\alpha_{[m]}^{2}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0.

Applying Corollary
4.3
again, we get

\alpha_{[m]}^{2}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=c^{\prime}\alpha_{[m]}\cdot\alpha_{2}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m}

for some
c^{\prime}>0
, yielding that

\alpha_{[m]}^{3}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0.

By iteration, we obtain

\alpha_{[m]}^{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0,

which is a contradiction with the assumption on
I=[m]
.
Therefore, we must have

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\alpha_{[m]}\cdot\omega^{n-m}>0.

We show that this implies

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}>0,

finishing the induction process.
Otherwise,

(7)

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0.

Then by Corollary
4.3
, we get that the classes

\alpha_{1}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m},\ \alpha_{2}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}

are proportional. By induction on
m-1
nef classes,

\alpha_{1}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m+1}>0

and

\alpha_{2}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m+1}>0,

therefore, there is some
c^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

\alpha_{1}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=c^{\prime\prime}\alpha_{2}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m},

which by (
7
) yields that

\alpha_{1}^{2}\cdot\alpha_{3}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0.

The same argument shows that for every
1\leq k\leq m-1
,

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\alpha_{k}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0.

Together with (
7
), we get that

\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m-1}\cdot\alpha_{[m]}\cdot\omega^{n-m}=0,

which contradicts with (
5
).

This finishes the proof.
∎

.

Let

be a smooth projective variety over

Let

and

Remark 4.6
.

The analogy of Theorem
4.1
holds on any smooth projective variety
X

.

More precisely, fix an ample divisor class
H
, for any nef divisor classes
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\in N^{1}(X)

the intersection product

also extends to compact Kähler varieties.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler variety of dimension
n
,
1\leq m\leq n
and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
be nef classes on
X
. Fix a Kähler class
\omega
on
X
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

the intersection number
\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}\cdot\omega^{n-m}>0
;

(2)

for any subset

,
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|
.

Proof.

We only need to show that the assumption on numerical dimensions implies the non-vanishing of intersection number.

Let
\mu:\widehat{X}\rightarrow X
be a modification such that

is a compact Kähler manifold. It is clear that
\nd(\alpha_{I})\geq|I|

5.
Polymatroid structures

In this section, we discuss various combinatorial structures closely related to Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
.

Our starting point is the application to multidegrees in the Kähler setting.

In
[
CCRL+20
]
, the authors systematically explored when the multidegrees of an algebraic subvariety
X
(over an arbitrary field
k
) of the product of projective spaces

\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{m}

is positive and studied its algebraic and combinatorial aspects.
To introduce our result, we give a brief introduction to their nice result.
For simplicity, assume that the field
k
is algebraically closed and
X
is irreducible, for each

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},...,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}

with

n_{[m]}=n_{1}+...+n_{m}=\dim X,

the multidegrees of
X
of type
\mathbf{n}
with respect to
\mathbb{P}
, denoted by
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
, is the number of intersection points (counting multiplicities) of
X
with the product

L_{1}\times...\times L_{m}\subset\mathbb{P},

where
L_{k}\subset\mathbb{P}_{k}
is a general linear subspace of codimension
n_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq m
. Equivalently,

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)=H_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot...\cdot H_{m}^{n_{m}}\cdot[X],

where
H_{k}
is the hyperplane divisor class on
\mathbb{P}_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq m
.

Given a subset
I\subset[m]
, denote by
\pi_{I}
the projection

\pi_{I}:\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{i_{1}}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{i_{|I|}}.

The the positivity of
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
is determined by the dimensions of
\pi_{I}(X)
, more precisely, the following statements are equivalent:

•

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0
;

•

\dim\pi_{I}(X)\geq n_{I},\forall I\subset[m]
.

When
m=2
, the positivity of
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
was considered in
[
Tru01
,
Huh12
]
.

It is easy to generalize multidegrees to the setting when the total space
\mathbb{P}
is replaced by other product spaces.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Kähler submanifold of
Y=(Y_{1},\omega_{1})\times...\times(Y_{m},\omega_{m})
, where each
Y_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq m
, is a compact Kähler manifold with a fixed Kähler class
\omega_{k}
. Then for each

\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},...,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}

with

n_{[m]}=n_{1}+...+n_{m}=\dim X,

the multidegrees of
X
of type
\mathbf{n}
with respect to
Y
, denoted by
\deg_{Y}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
, is defined by

\deg_{Y}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)=\omega_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot...\cdot\omega_{m}^{n_{m}}\cdot[X].

For
I\subset[m]
, denote by
\pi_{I}
the projection

\pi_{I}:Y=Y_{1}\times...\times Y_{m}\rightarrow Y_{i_{1}}\times...\times Y_{i_{|I|}}.

As an application of Theorem
4.1
, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for the fundamental question when
\deg_{Y}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0
in the Kähler setting.

Theorem 5.2
.

Setting as in the above definition, then the following statements are equivalent:

•

\deg_{Y}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0
;

•

\dim\pi_{I}(X)\geq n_{I}
for any
I\subset[m]
.

Proof.

Denote by
\alpha_{i}=(\pi_{i}^{*}\omega_{i})_{|X}
, then

\deg_{Y}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)=\int_{X}\alpha_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}^{n_{m}}.

Write

\widetilde{\alpha}_{I}=n_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{1}}+...+n_{i_{|I|}}\alpha_{i_{|I|}}

for
I\subset[m]
, applying Theorem
4.1
to the nef classes

\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{1},....,\alpha_{m},...,\alpha_{m}

with each
\alpha_{k}
repeating
n_{k}
times,
it is clear that the intersection number is positive if and only if

\displaystyle\nd(\widetilde{\alpha}_{I})

\displaystyle=\nd(n_{i_{1}}\alpha_{i_{1}}+...+n_{i_{|I|}}\alpha_{i_{|I|}})

\displaystyle\geq n_{I}

for any
I\subset[m]
.

We claim that
\nd(\widetilde{\alpha}_{I})=\nd(\alpha_{I})=\dim\pi_{I}(X)
, where the first equality is obvious.

To this end, without loss of generality, we may assume that
X\subset Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
.
We show that

\nd(\alpha_{1})=\dim\pi_{1}(X).

Suppose
\dim\pi_{1}(X)=d_{1}
. Let
A
be the analytic subset of
X
where

\text{rank}(d{\pi_{1}}_{|X})<d_{1}.

Then
\pi_{1}(A)
is an analytic subset of
Y_{1}
by Remmert’s proper mapping theorem. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\dim\pi_{1}(A)

\displaystyle=\max\{\text{rank}(d\pi_{1})_{x}|x\in A_{\text{reg}}\}

\displaystyle<d_{1}.

Hence
\pi_{1}(A)
is a proper subset of
\pi_{1}(X)
. Let
B=\pi_{1}(A)\cup\pi_{1}(X)_{\text{sing}}
. Then

\pi_{1}:X\backslash\pi_{1}^{-1}(B)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)\backslash B

is a submersion. It is clear that for any
y\in X\backslash\pi_{1}^{-1}(B)
,

\alpha_{1}^{d_{1}}(y)>0,\ \text{and}\ \alpha_{1}^{l}(y)=0

for any
l>d_{1}
.
Since
X\backslash\pi_{1}^{-1}(B)
is a dense open set of
X
, together with the semi-positivity of
\alpha_{1}
, we find that

\nd(\alpha_{1})=d_{1}=\dim\pi_{1}(X).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

By Corollary
4.7
, Theorem
5.2
extends to compact Kähler varieties.

It was shown in
[
CCRL+20
]
that in the algebraic case,
r(I):=\dim\pi_{I}(X)
is a submodular function by interpreting
\dim\pi_{I}(X)
as the transcendental degree of an extension field.

Combining with our geometric observation that
\dim\pi_{I}(X)=\nd(\widetilde{\alpha}_{I})
, it motivate us to consider if the numerical dimension is a submodular function. We prove the following result:

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
X
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
, then for any three nef classes
A,B,C\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
on
X
, we always have

\nd(A+B+C)+\nd(C)\leq\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C).

Proof.

In order to prove the inequality, it is sufficient to verify the following statement:

Let
k,l,m
be non-negative integers such that

\displaystyle(A+C)^{k+1}=0,

\displaystyle(B+C)^{l+1}=0,

\displaystyle C^{m}\neq 0,C^{m+1}=0,

then
(A+B+C)^{k+l-m+1}=0
.

To this end, it is equivalent to prove that for any triple of nonnegative integers
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
satisfying

s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}=k+l-m+1,

we have that

A^{s_{1}}\cdot B^{s_{2}}\cdot C^{s_{3}}=0.

Fix a very ample class
\omega
, and let
V
be an irreducible subvariety in the cycle class
\omega^{n-(k+l-m+1)}
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, set
D=\varepsilon\omega+C
.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have that

\displaystyle A^{s_{1}}\cdot B^{s_{2}}\cdot C^{s_{3}}\cdot[V]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(k+l-m+1)!}{s_{2}!(k+l-m+1-s_{2})!}\frac{(A^{s_{1}}\cdot C^{s_{3}}\cdot D^{s_{2}}\cdot[V])(B^{s_{2}}\cdot D^{k+l-m+1-s_{2}}\cdot[V])}{D^{k+l-m+1}\cdot[V]}.

For notational simplicity, in the following we use the notations
\simeq,\preceq
: for two real numbers
a,b
,
a\simeq b
if there are two constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}a\leq b\leq c_{2}a
;
a\preceq b
if there is a constant
c>0
such that
a\leq cb
.

By the assumption
C^{m}\neq 0,C^{m+1}=0
,

D^{k+l-m+1}\cdot[V]\simeq\varepsilon^{k+l-2m+1}+...

where
...
is a term with higher power than
\varepsilon^{k+l-2m+1}
. Similarly, by the assumptions

(A+C)^{k+1}=0,(B+C)^{l+1}=0,

we get that

\displaystyle A^{s_{1}}\cdot C^{s_{3}}\cdot D^{s_{2}}\cdot[V]\preceq\varepsilon^{l-m+1}+...,

\displaystyle B^{s_{2}}\cdot D^{k+l-m+1-s_{2}}\cdot[V]\preceq\varepsilon^{k-m+1}+....

Putting the above estimates together implies that

A^{s_{1}}\cdot B^{s_{2}}\cdot C^{s_{3}}\cdot[V]\preceq\frac{\varepsilon^{k+l-2m+2}+...}{\varepsilon^{k+l-2m+1}+...},

which tends to zero as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Therefore,

A^{s_{1}}\cdot B^{s_{2}}\cdot C^{s_{3}}=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

We expect that Theorem
5.4
also holds for nef classes on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold, this depends on a relative version of the reverse Khovanskii-Teissier inequalities by replacing the cycle class
[V]
by a general complete intersection of transcendental Kähler classes.

Remark 5.6
.

By the definition of numerical dimension, for any two nef
(1,1)
classes
A,B
, it is clear that

\nd(A+B)\leq\nd(A)+\nd(B).

This is a special case of Theorem
5.4
when
C=0
.

Remark 5.7
.

Notations as Theorem
5.4
, by the definition of numerical dimension we have that

\nd(A+B+C)\geq\max\{\nd(A+C),\nd(B+C)\},

which yields that

2(\nd(A+B+C)+\nd(C))\geq\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C).

Therefore combining with Theorem
5.4
,

\frac{1}{2}(\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C))\leq\nd(A+B+C)+\nd(C)\leq\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C).

Corollary 5.8
.

Given any finite nonzero nef classes
B_{1},...,B_{m}
on a complex projective manifold, which we identify the collection with the indices set
E=[m]
. For any
I\subset[m]
, set
r(I)=\nd(B_{I})
with the convention that
r(\emptyset)=0
. Then the function
r(\cdot)
endows with
E
a loopless polymatroid structure.

Proof.

For any nonempty
I,J\subset E
, it is clear that

\nd(B_{I}+B_{J}+B_{I\cap J})=\nd(B_{I\cup J}).

Applying Theorem
5.4
to
B_{I},B_{J},B_{I\cap J}
yields the submodularity:

r(I\cup J)+r(I\cap J)\leq r(I)+r(J).

It is obvious that for any
I\subset J\subset E
,
r(I)\leq r(J)
– this verifies the monotonicity.

For any nonempty
I\subset E
,
r(I)\neq 1
, which verifies the loopless.

This finishes the proof.

∎

Combining Theorem
5.4
with Corollary
5.8
,
we finish the proof of Theorem
C
.

Remark 5.9
.

By Remark
2.5
, it is clear that the same argument works on any smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field, therefore the analogous result also holds in this setting.

Next we give an alternative approach to the submodularity of numerical dimension for semi-positive classes on an arbitrary compact Kähler manifold. This follows from the characterization of positivity of complete intersection of nef classes given by
Theorem
B
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
A,B,C\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nef classes with semi-positive representatives. Then we have that

\nd(A+B+C)+\nd(C)\leq\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C).

Proof.

We may fix a semipositive representative in each class, denoted by
\widehat{A},\widehat{B},\widehat{C}
.
Fix a Kähler class
\omega
on
X
, and denote a Kähler metric in
\omega
by
\widehat{\omega}
.

By Theorem
B
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\widehat{C}^{\nd(C)}\wedge(\widehat{A}+\widehat{B})^{\nd(A+B+C)-\nd(C)}\wedge\widehat{\omega}^{n-\nd(\widehat{A}+\widehat{B}+\widehat{C})}

\displaystyle=C^{\nd(C)}\cdot(A+B)^{\nd(A+B+C)-\nd(C)}\cdot\omega^{n-\nd(A+B+C)}

\displaystyle>0.

By semi-positivity of the volume form

\widehat{C}^{\nd(C)}\wedge(\widehat{A}+\widehat{B})^{\nd(A+B+C)-\nd(C)}\wedge\widehat{\omega}^{n-\nd(A+B+C)}

at any point and positivity of the integration, there is some point
x
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{C}(x)^{\nd(C)}\wedge(\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{B}(x))^{\nd(A+B+C)-\nd(C)}\wedge\widehat{\omega}(x)^{n-\nd(A+B+C)}

\displaystyle>0.

Then by Corollary
3.9
or Theorem
B
,

•

\widehat{C}(x)
is
\nd(C)
positive;

•

\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x)
is
\nd(A+B+C)
positive.

On the other hand, we claim that
\widehat{C}(x)
is exactly
\nd(C)
positive and
\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x)
is exactly
\nd(A+B+C)
positive.
If
\widehat{C}(x)
is not exactly
\nd(C)
positive, then we will have that

\widehat{C}(x)^{\nd(C)+1}\wedge\widehat{\omega}(x)^{n-\nd(C)-1}>0.

Then it follows that

C^{\nd(C)+1}\cdot\omega^{n-\nd(C)-1}=\int_{X}\widehat{C}^{\nd(C)+1}\wedge\widehat{\omega}^{n-\nd(C)-1}>0

by the semi-positivity of
\widehat{C}
. This contradicts to the definition of
\nd(C)
. By the same way, we get that
\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x)
is exactly
\nd(A+B+C)
positive.
Thus we have that

\displaystyle\codim\ker(\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x))=\nd(A+B+C),

\displaystyle\codim\ker(\widehat{C}(x))=\nd(C).

Linear algebraic argument (or applying Theorem
5.4
to some abelian variety) implies that

\displaystyle\codim\ker(\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x))+\codim\ker(\widehat{C}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\codim\ker(\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{C}(x))+\codim\ker(\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x)).

Again by semi-positivity, we have that

\displaystyle\codim\ker(\widehat{A}(x)+\widehat{C}(x))\leq\nd(A+C),

\displaystyle\codim\ker(\widehat{B}(x)+\widehat{C}(x))\leq\nd(B+C).

Take all estimates above into account, we get that

\nd(A+B+C)+\nd(C)\leq\nd(A+C)+\nd(B+C).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Just as Theorem
C
, the numerical dimension endows a finite set of semi-positive classes with a polymatroid structure.

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
X
be a a compact Kähler manifold and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}\in H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{R})
be nonzero semi-positive classes on
X
. Then

r(I)=\nd(\alpha_{I}),\forall I\subset[m]

endows
[m]
with a loopless polymatroid structure.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
5.10
and the same argument as Corollary
5.8
.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

It is interesting to see whether the above polymatroids given by nef or semi-positive classes are algebraic (or even representable), see Example
2.3
.

In the case when
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
are given by semi-ample line bundles on a complex projective manifold
X
, the polymatroid is representable. We can assume that every
\alpha_{k}
is given by a free line bundle
A_{k}
. Let

\phi_{k}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}_{k}=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,A_{k}))

be the Kodaira map defined by
A_{k}
, then we have a morphism

\phi=\phi_{1}\times...\times\phi_{m}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{1}\times...\times\mathbb{P}_{m}.

Denote
Y=\phi(X)\subset\mathbb{P}
, then we have that
\dim\pi_{I}(Y)=\kappa(A_{I})=\nd(A_{I})
. By
[
CCRL+20
]
,
r(I)=\dim\pi_{I}(Y)
endows the set
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
with an algebraic polymatroid structure. Since over a field of characteristic zero any algebraic polymatroid is representable, the desired result holds. One such construction for matroids also appeared in
[
HW17
]
where the projective manifold is a wonderful compactification and can be further generalized to polymatroids
[
CHL+22
]
.

However, in general one cannot reduce the nef case to the semi-ample case, since there exist nef
(1,1)
classes which cannot be made to be semi-ample by any modifications.

Let
X\subset Y=Y_{1}\times...\times Y_{m}
be in the setting of Definition
5.1
, define
the support of
X
with with respect to
Y
by

\MSupp_{Y}(X)=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}|\deg^{\mathbf{n}}_{Y}(X)>0\}.

Corollary 5.13
.

The support of
X
with with respect to
Y
,
\MSupp_{Y}(X)
, is a discrete polymatroid.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.2
, we have that

\MSupp_{Y}(X)=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}|n_{[m]}=\dim X=n,\dim\pi_{I}(X)\geq n_{I},\forall I\subsetneq[m]\}.

As we see in the proof of Theorem
5.2
,

\nd(\alpha_{I})=\dim\pi_{I}(X).

Here the
\alpha_{I}
are semi-positive classes on
X
. Then by Proposition
5.10
,

r(I)=\nd(\alpha_{I})=\dim\pi_{I}(X)

is a rank function. It follows that
\MSupp_{Y}(X)
is a discrete polymatroid with rank function
r(I)
by the submodular theorem (see
[
CL22
]
).
∎

This extends related result of
[
CCRL+20
]
to the Kähler setting.
For a projective variety over an algebraically closed field,
[
BH20
]
also proved the result using the theory of Lorentzian polynomials.

Finally, in the linear setting we show that discrete polymatroids appear naturally in the HL property.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
X=\mathbb{C}^{n}/\Gamma
be a compact complex torus of dimension
n
, and let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{m}
be nef
(1,1)
classes on
X
. Denote

M=\{\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},...,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}|\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}=m,\ \alpha_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}^{n_{m}}\ \text{has HL property.}\},

then
M
is a discrete polymatroid with the rank function
r(I)=\nd(\alpha_{I})-(n-m)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
A
, if
\alpha_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{m}^{n_{m}}
has HL property, then we must have

\nd(\alpha_{k})\geq n-m+1.

Therefore, for any
I\subset[m]
,

r(I)=\nd(\alpha_{I})-(n-m)\in\mathbb{N}.

By Theorem
A
again, we have that

M=\{\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},...,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}|\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}=m,\ r(I)\geq n_{I},\forall I\subsetneq[m]\}.

On the other hand, by Corollary
5.11
,
r(\cdot)
is a rank function on
[m]
.

Thus,
M
is a discrete polymatroid with the rank function
r(I)=\nd(\alpha_{I})-(n-m)
.

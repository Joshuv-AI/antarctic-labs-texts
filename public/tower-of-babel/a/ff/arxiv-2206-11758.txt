Blow-up for semilinear parabolic equations in cones of the hyperbolic space


Abstract
. We investigate existence and nonexistence of global in time nonnegative solutions to the semilinear heat equation, with a reaction term of the type e μ ​ t ​ u p ​ ( μ ∈ ℝ , p > 1 ) , e^{\mu t}u^{p}\,(\mu\in\mathbb{R},p>1), posed on cones of the hyperbolic space. Under a certain assumption on μ \mu and p p , related to the bottom of the spectrum of − Δ -\Delta in ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , we prove that any solution blows up in finite time, for any nontrivial nonnegative initial datum. Instead, if the parameters μ \mu and p p satisfy the opposite condition we have: (a) blow-up when the initial datum is large enough, (b) existence of global solutions when the initial datum is small enough. Hence our conditions on the parameters μ \mu and p p are optimal. We see that blow-up and global existence do not depend on the amplitude of the cone. This is very different from what happens in the Euclidean setting (see [ 2 ] ), and it is essentially due to a specific geometric feature of ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} .

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the
n-
dimensional hyperbolic space, and let
r=r(x)
denote the distance of a point
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
from a fixed reference point
o
. Consider the unit sphere
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
centered at
o
and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
be a relatively open domain. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the cone with vertex at
o\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
determined by the domain
\Omega
.
We introduce global polar coordinates
(r,\theta)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
centered at
o
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
denotes the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. With a slight abuse of notation we will identify
\mathcal{S}^{n-1}\equiv\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Then the cone
\mathcal{C}
can be described as follows

\mathcal{C}=\{x=(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\,|\,r>0,\,\theta\in\Omega\}=(0,\infty)\times\Omega.

In this paper, we investigate finite-time blow-up of nonnegative solutions to problem

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=e^{\mu t}u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathcal{C}\times(0,T),\\
u=0&\text{ in }\partial\mathcal{C}\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{0}&\text{ on }\mathcal{C}\times\{0\},\end{cases}

with

p>1,\,\,\,\,\,\,T>0,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mu\in\mathbb{R},\,\,\,\,\,\,u_{0}\geq 0\text{ continuous and bounded}.

Here and in the following
T\in(0,\infty]
denotes the maximal existence time of the solution
u
, while
\Delta
stands for the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. It is clear that, when we consider the case
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we have
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{H}^{n}
and no boundary conditions are given. Hence problem (
1
) in this case reads

(2)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=e^{\mu t}u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{0}&\text{ on }\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{0\}.\end{cases}

In
[
2
]
, the authors addressed problem

(3)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathfrak{D}\times(0,T),\\
u=0&\text{ in }\partial\mathfrak{D}\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{0}&\text{ on }\mathfrak{D}\times\{0\},\end{cases}

where

\mathfrak{D}:=\{x=(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r>0,\theta\in\Omega\}

is a cone of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
(r,\theta)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
are polar coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Omega
is a relatively open domain in the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is shown that existence and nonexistence of global solutions is closely related to the domain
\Omega
. To be more specific,
we introduce some preliminary material. Let
\Delta_{\theta}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and consider the eigenvalue problem on
\Omega

(4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\theta}\psi=\omega\psi&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\psi=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

In particular we denote by
\omega_{1}\geq 0
the first eigenvalue of problem (
4
) and by
\psi_{1}
the corresponding positive eigenfunction, normalized so that

\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}d\sigma_{\theta}=1.

Obviously
\omega_{1}=0
if and only if
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, that is
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{D}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case
\psi_{1}
is a positive constant.

Let
\lambda:=-\gamma_{-}
, where
\gamma_{-}
is the negative root of

\gamma(\gamma+n-2)=\omega_{1}\,.

In
[
2
]
it is shown that if

1<p<1+\frac{2}{2+\lambda},

then any solution to problem (
3
) blows up in finite time. On the other hand, problem (
3
) possesses global in time solutions, for suitable initial data
u_{0}
, provided that

1+\frac{2}{\lambda}<p<\frac{n+1}{n-3}\quad\text{ for }n>3,

p>1+\frac{2}{\lambda}\quad\text{ for }n=2,3\,.

Note that conditions on
p
ensuring existence or nonexistence of global in time solutions of (
3
) depend on
\Omega
through the quantity
\lambda
.

Let us mention that when
\mathfrak{D}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, problem (
3
) was studied in
[
4
]
(see also
[
5
]
). It is shown that any solution blows up in finite time, whenever

1<p\leq 1+\frac{2}{n},

while a global in time solution exists, provided that

p>1+\frac{2}{n}\,,

and the initial datum is small enough.

In
[
1
]
the authors studied problem (
2
). We recall that
\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}
is the bottom of the spectrum of the operator
-\Delta
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.
It is then established that if

(5)

\mu>(p-1)\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),

then any solution blows up in finite time. On the other hand (see also
[
11
]
), if

(6)

\mu\leq(p-1)\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),

and
\mu\geq 0
then a global in time solution exists, provided that
u_{0}
is sufficiently small.

In this paper we show that existence and nonexistence of global solutions to problem (
1
) on
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
do not depend on the domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and that the critical exponent phenomenon is always the same as for the whole space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. This is in striking contrast with respect to the Euclidean case. In fact, consider any
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We prove that:

i.

if condition (
5
) holds, then any solution to problem (
1
) blows up in finite time;

ii.

if condition (
6
) holds and
u_{0}
is large enough, then the corresponding solution to problem (
1
) blows up in finite time;

iii.

if condition (
6
) holds and
u_{0}
is small enough, then there exists a global in time solution to problem (
1
).

Observe that the conditions which guarantee existence or nonexistence of global in time solutions are completely independent of
\Omega
. Moreover, since condition (
6
) is the opposite of (
5
), we see that our assumptions on the parameters
\mu
and
p
are optimal. We also explicitly remark that, for every cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the threshold case
\mu=(p-1)\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
belongs to the regime where global in time solutions can exist; on the contrary, in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the threshold case
p=1+\frac{2}{n}
belongs to the regime where all solutions exhibit blow up in finite time.

Note that when
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and thus problem (
1
) becomes (
2
), our results recover those established in
[
1
,
11
]
for problem (
2
).

Let us also explicitly note that when
\mu=0
, condition (
6
) is always fulfilled. Hence from our results it follows that, for every
p>1
, any solution to problem

(7)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathcal{C}\times(0,T),\\
u=0&\text{ in }\partial\mathcal{C}\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{0}&\text{ on }\mathcal{C}\times\{0\}\end{cases}

blows up in finite time, provided that
u_{0}
is large enough. On the other hand, for every
p>1
, problem (
7
) admits a global in time solution, whenever
u_{0}
is sufficiently small.

In order to briefly explain such enormous difference between
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, consider the Laplace operator in polar coordinates.
In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, obviously we have:

\Delta u=u_{rr}+\frac{n-1}{r}\,u_{r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\theta}u,

where the subscript
r
is used to denote a partial derivative with respect to that variable. On the other hand, on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
we have

\Delta u=u_{rr}+(n-1)\coth r\,u_{r}+\frac{1}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Delta_{\theta}u.

Observe that in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the coefficient
\frac{1}{r^{2}}
multiplies
\Delta_{\theta}
, whereas in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
we have
\frac{1}{(\sinh r)^{2}}
. Clearly,
\frac{1}{(\sinh r)^{2}}
tends to
0
as
r
tends to
+\infty
much faster than

\frac{1}{r^{2}}
. Consequently, in
\mathbb{R}^{n},
the term
\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\theta}u
plays a certain role and existence and nonexistence results depend on the domain
\Omega
where
\Delta_{\theta}
is considered. On the other hand, in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
the term
\frac{1}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Delta_{\theta}u
is somehow negligible. So,
\Omega
does not have any role in determining existence or nonexistence of global solutions.

The proof of finite-time blow-up is based on a generalization of the Kaplan method (see
[
6
]
; see also
[
2
]
) on unbounded cones of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, with differential equations involving time dependent coefficients. In particular we show that, if
u
is a solution of problem (
1
), for functions
\Phi
satisfying suitable conditions, the function

G(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha t}u(r,\theta,t)\psi_{1}(\theta)\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}dr

is a supersolution of an appropriate ordinary differential equation. In such equation a time dependent coefficient appears. To obtain this we develop some ideas used in
[
2
]
. Then we specialize our choice of the function
\Phi
, which is a subsolution of an appropriate auxiliary problem. This point is very different from the Euclidean case addressed in
[
2
]
, since it is strictly related to the geometry of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Moreover, in
[
2
]
the authors did not need to consider time dependent coefficients, as instead we do here.

In view of our choice of
\Phi
, we can infer that
G(t)
is a supersolution of another suitable ordinary differential equation. From this we can deduce that
G(t)

must blow up in finite time, and this yields blow-up for solutions to problem (
1
).

Finally, global existence of solutions for small nonnegative initial data
u_{0}
is obtained by means of suitable supersolutions and local barrier functions at points of the boundary of the domain.

We conclude the introduction with a final remark. In
[
1
]
the authors showed also that if
q>-1
,
p>1
and
u_{0}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is sufficiently small, then there exist global solutions of problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=t^{q}u^{p}&\mbox{in }\mathbb{H}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{0}&\mbox{on }\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\{0\}.\end{cases}

Adapting the arguments used to study problem (
1
) one can show that if
q>-1
,
p>1
,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is a relatively open domain and
\mathcal{C}=(0,\infty)\times\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then

(8)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=t^{q}u^{p}&\text{ in }\mathcal{C}\times(0,T),\\
u=0&\text{ in }\partial\mathcal{C}\times(0,T),\\
u=u_{0}&\text{ on }\mathcal{C}\times\{0\},\end{cases}

admits global in time solutions, provided
u_{0}\geq 0
on
\mathcal{C}
is sufficiently small; on the other hand, if
u_{0}\geq 0
on
\mathcal{C}
is large enough, then the corresponding solution of (
8
) blows up in finite time.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we state our main results concerning both finite-time blow-up of solutions and global existence. In Section
3
we deduce the key property of the function
G
. Then in Section
4
we introduce the function
\Phi
and we show our blow-up results, while in Section
5
we prove existence of global in time solutions for suitably small nonnegative initial data.

3.
A preliminary estimate

The following proposition can be regarded as a generalization of the Kaplan method in unbounded cones of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Moreover, a time dependent coefficient appears in the estimate.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u
be a solution of problem (
1
),
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Phi\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
be a nonnegative function satisfying the following assumptions

i)

\Phi(r)\sim l\,r^{m}
as
r
tends to
0
, with
l\in(0,\infty)
and
m\geq 2
;

ii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr=1
;

iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|\Phi_{r}(r)|+|\Phi_{rr}(r)|\right)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr<\infty
.

Then the function

(11)

G(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha t}u(r,\theta,t)\psi_{1}(\theta)\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}dr

satisfies

(12)

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(t)\geq e^{(\mu-(p-1)\alpha)t}(G(t))^{p}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha t}u(r,\theta,t)\Psi_{1}(\theta)\left(\Delta\Phi(r)+\alpha\Phi(r)-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Phi(r)\right)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}dr

for all
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

We consider the first eigenvalue
\omega_{1}\geq 0
and the first positive eigenfunction
\psi_{1}
of problem (
4
), normalized with
\|\psi_{1}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
. We define

\tilde{u}(r,t)=e^{\alpha t}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}(\theta)u(r,\theta,t)\,d\sigma_{\theta}

for
r>0
,
t\in(0,T)
. Note that
\tilde{u}
inherits the same regularity of
u
in
(0,\infty)\times(0,T)
and that

G(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}(r,t)\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr.

Then we have

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t}

\displaystyle=\alpha e^{\alpha t}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}u\,d\sigma_{\theta}+e^{\alpha t}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}u_{t}\,d\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\alpha\tilde{u}+e^{\alpha t}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}\Delta u\,d\sigma_{\theta}+e^{\alpha t}\int_{\Omega}e^{\mu t}\psi_{1}u^{p}\,d\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\alpha\tilde{u}+e^{\alpha t}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}\left(u_{rr}+(n-1)\coth r\,u_{r}+\frac{1}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Delta_{\theta}u\right)\,d\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,+e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}\int_{\Omega}\psi_{1}u^{p}\,d\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\alpha\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}_{rr}+(n-1)\coth r\,\tilde{u}_{r}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\tilde{u}+e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\psi_{1}\,d\sigma_{\theta},

where we have integrated by parts twice on
\Omega
in order to obtain the last equality. Then using Jensen’s inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{t}

\displaystyle=\alpha\tilde{u}+\Delta\tilde{u}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\tilde{u}+e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\psi_{1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha\tilde{u}+\Delta\tilde{u}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\tilde{u}+e^{(\alpha+\mu)t}\left(\int_{\Omega}u\psi_{1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\alpha\tilde{u}+\Delta\tilde{u}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\tilde{u}+e^{\left(\mu-(p-1)\alpha\right)t}\tilde{u}^{p}.

Now for arbitrary
0<\varepsilon<R
we consider a smooth cutoff function
\zeta=\zeta(r)
such that
\zeta\equiv 0
in
(0,\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2})\cup(2R,\infty)
,
\zeta\equiv 1
in
(\varepsilon,R)
and
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
in
(0,\infty)
, with

0\leq\zeta^{\prime}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in }\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2},\epsilon\right),\qquad-\frac{C}{R}\leq\zeta^{\prime}\leq 0\quad\text{in }(R,2R),

and

|\zeta^{\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\quad\text{in }\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2},\epsilon\right),\qquad|\zeta^{\prime\prime}|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\quad\text{in }(R,2R),

for some
C>0
. Note that

|\Delta\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\text{ on }\,\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2},\epsilon\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad|\Delta\zeta|\leq\frac{C}{R}\,\text{ on }\,(R,2R).

Define for every
t\in(0,T)

\displaystyle G^{\varepsilon,R}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha t}\zeta(r)u(r,\theta,t)\psi_{1}(\theta)\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}}^{2R}\tilde{u}(r,t)\zeta(r)\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr.

Note that
t\mapsto G^{\varepsilon,R}(t)
and
t\mapsto G(t)
are functions of class
C^{1}
in
(0,T)
. Using the above inequalities we obtain

\displaystyle(G^{\varepsilon,R})^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\alpha\tilde{u}+\Delta\tilde{u}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\tilde{u}\right)\zeta\Phi(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+e^{\left(\mu-(p-1)\alpha\right)t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}^{p}\zeta\Phi(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr.

Note that integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta\tilde{u}\,\zeta\Phi(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}\Delta(\zeta\Phi)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}\left(\zeta\Delta\Phi+2\Phi_{r}\zeta_{r}+\Phi\Delta\zeta\right)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

and hence

\displaystyle(G^{\varepsilon,R})^{\prime}(t)\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\alpha\Phi+\Delta\Phi-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Phi\right)\tilde{u}\zeta(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+e^{\left(\mu-(p-1)\alpha\right)t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}^{p}\zeta\Phi(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr+\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}\left(2\Phi_{r}\zeta_{r}+\Phi\Delta\zeta\right)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr.

Now we need to pass to the limit as
R
tends to
\infty
and
\varepsilon
tends to
0
. Since
u(\cdot,t)
, and hence
\tilde{u}(\cdot,t)
, is bounded in
(0,\infty)
for every fixed
t\in(0,T)
, by the integrability assumptions on
\Phi

(ii)
then we have

for every

\displaystyle(G^{\varepsilon,R})^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(e^{\alpha t}u(r,\theta,t))_{t}\zeta(r)\psi_{1}(\theta)\Phi(r)(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,d\sigma_{\theta}dr

for every

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\alpha\Phi+\Delta\Phi-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Phi\right)\tilde{u}(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,+e^{\left(\mu-(p-1)\alpha\right)t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{u}^{p}\Phi(\sinh r)^{n-1}\,dr

. This, combined with (

∎

4.
Blow-up: proofs

Let
\Phi_{0}
be defined on the hyperbolic space by

\Phi_{0}(x)\equiv\Phi_{0}(r(x)):=r^{m}e^{-kr^{2}}

for
m\geq 2
,
k>0
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and let
\omega_{1}\geq 0
be the smallest Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\theta}
on
\Omega
. Let

\alpha>\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

and

m\geq 2,\qquad\quad m(m-1)>\omega_{1}.

Then there exists
k_{0}=k_{0}(n,m,\alpha,\omega_{1})>0
such that for every
k\in(0,k_{0}]
one has

(15)

\Delta\Phi_{0}+\alpha\Phi_{0}\geq\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Phi_{0}\qquad\text{ in }\mathbb{H}^{n}.

Proof.

Since
m\geq 2
we have
\Phi_{0}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and

\displaystyle(\Phi_{0})_{r}

\displaystyle=(mr^{m-1}-2kr^{m+1})e^{-kr^{2}}

\displaystyle(\Phi_{0})_{rr}

\displaystyle=\left(m(m-1)r^{m-2}-2k(2m+1)r^{m}+4k^{2}r^{m+2}\right)e^{-kr^{2}}

Using polar coordinates
(r,\theta)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, a simple computation yields

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi_{0}+\alpha\Phi_{0}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Phi_{0}

\displaystyle=(\Phi_{0})_{rr}+(n-1)\coth r(\Phi_{0})_{r}+\alpha\Phi_{0}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}\Phi_{0}

\displaystyle=r^{m-2}e^{-kr^{2}}\Big[m(m-1)-2k(2m+1)r^{2}+4k^{2}r^{4}+m(n-1)r\coth r

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt-2(n-1)kr^{3}\coth r+\alpha r^{2}-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}r^{2}\Big]

and the above quantity is nonnegative if and only if

(16)

\displaystyle m(m-1)+m(n-1)r\coth r-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}r^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+r^{2}\left[4k^{2}r^{2}-2(n-1)kr\coth r-(2k(2m+1)-\alpha)\right]\geq 0.

We claim that there exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(\alpha)>1
and
k_{0}=k_{0}(n,m,\alpha,\omega_{1})>0
such that

(17)

4k^{2}r^{2}-2(n-1)kr\coth r-(2k(2m+1)-\alpha)\geq 0

for every
r\geq R_{0}
and
0<k\leq k_{0}
. Indeed, since
\alpha>\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}
, we can find
R_{0}>1
such that

(18)

\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}(\coth r)^{2}-\alpha<\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}-\alpha\right)<0

for every
r\geq R_{0}
. Now let
k_{0}=k_{0}(n,m,\alpha,\omega_{1})>0
be such that for every
0<k\leq k_{0}
one has

(19)

\displaystyle 2k(2m+1)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}-\alpha\right)<0,\quad\text{and}

(20)

\displaystyle m(m-1)-\omega_{1}-2k(2m+1)R_{0}^{2}-2k(n-1)R_{0}^{3}\coth R_{0}>0;

here we have used our assumptions,
m(m-1)>\omega_{1}
and
\alpha>\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}
. Then, for every
k\in(0,k_{0}]
and every
r\geq R_{0}
, we can think of the left-hand side of (
17
) as a quadratic polynomial in
r
, having discriminant

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{1}}{4}

\displaystyle:=4k^{2}\left[\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}(\coth r)^{2}+2k(2m+1)-\alpha\right]

\displaystyle<4k^{2}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}-\alpha\right)+2k(2m+1)\right]<0,

by (
18
) and (
19
). Thus (
17
) holds for every
k\in(0,k_{0}]
and every
r\geq R_{0}
.

Now we claim that (
16
) holds for every
k\in(0,k_{0}]
and every
r\geq 0
, and thus (
15
) holds for every
k\in(0,k_{0}]
. Indeed, for every
r\geq R_{0}
by (
17
) we have

\displaystyle m(m-1)+m(n-1)r\coth r-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}r^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r^{2}[4k^{2}r^{2}-2(n-1)kr\coth r-(2k(2m+1)-\alpha)]

\displaystyle\geq m(n-1)r\coth r+m(m-1)-\omega_{1}>0.

On the other hand for every
r\in[0,R_{0}]
by (
20
) we have

\displaystyle m(m-1)+m(n-1)r\coth r-\frac{\omega_{1}}{(\sinh r)^{2}}r^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r^{2}[4k^{2}r^{2}-2(n-1)kr\coth r-(2k(2m+1)-\alpha)]

\displaystyle\geq m(m-1)-\omega_{1}-2k(2m+1)R_{0}^{2}-2k(n-1)R^{3}_{0}\coth R_{0}>0,

and thus we conclude.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We consider the function defined for every

by

\Phi(x)\equiv\Phi(r(x))=C\Phi_{0}(r)=Cr^{m}e^{-kr^{2}},

and with

. Let

Setting for every

Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequalities using Fractional Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces
Nguyen Anh Dao

Abstract
. Our main purpose is to establish Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequalities using fractional homogeneous Sobolev spaces, and homogeneous Besov spaces. In particular, we extend some of the results obtained by the authors in [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 7 , 16 , 21 ] .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46E35; Secondary 46B70.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in
the following Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality:

For every
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
, and for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2},q\leq\infty
, there holds

(1.1)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{L^{q}}\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}}\,,

where

\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1}{q}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\,,

and
\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes by the homogeneous Sobolev space (see its definition in Section 2).

It is known that such an inequality of this type plays an important role in the analysis of PDEs. When
\alpha_{i}
,
i=1,2
are nonnegative integer numbers, (
1.1
) was obtained independently by Gagliardo
[
9
]
and Nirenberg
[
17
]
.
After that, the inequalities of this type have been studied by many authors in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
13
,
16
,
15
,
21
]
, and the references cited therein.

The case
q=\infty
can be considered as a limiting case of (
1.1
), i.e:

(1.2)

\|D^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{L^{\infty}}\|D^{\alpha_{2}}f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{L^{p_{2}}}\,,\quad\forall f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,,

with
p_{1}=\frac{p_{2}\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}
.
Obviously, this inequality fails if
\alpha_{1}=0
.

An partial improvement of (
1.2
) in terms of BMO space was obtained by Meyer and Rivière
[
15
]
:

(1.3)

\|Df\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\rm{BMO}}\|D^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\,,

for all
f\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thanks to (
1.3
), the authors proved a regularity result for a class of stationary Yang–Mills fields in high dimension.

After that (
1.4
) was extended to higher derivatives by the authors in
[
20
,
16
]
. Precisely, there holds true

(1.4)

\|D^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{{\rm BMO}}\|D^{\alpha_{2}}f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{L^{p_{2}}}\,,

for all
f\in{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap W^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
p_{2}>1
.

Recently, the author et al.
[
7
]
improved (
1.4
) by means of the homogeneous Besov spaces. For convenience, we recall the result here.

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem 1.2,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
m,k
be integers with
1\leq k<m
. For every
s\geq 0
, let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that

D^{m}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1\leq p<\infty
; and
f\in\dot{B}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, we have
D^{k}f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
r=p\left(\frac{m+s}{k+s}\right)
, and

(1.5)

\|D^{k}f\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{m-k}{m+s}}_{\dot{B}^{-s}}\left\|D^{m}f\right\|^{\frac{k+s}{m+s}}_{L^{p}}\,,

where we denote
\dot{B}^{\sigma}=\dot{B}^{\sigma,\infty}_{\infty}
,
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
(see the definition of Besov spaces in Section 2).

Remark 1.1
.

Obviously, (
1.5
) is stronger than (
1.4
) when
s=0
since
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We emphasize that (
1.5
) is still true for
k=0
when
s>0
.

Remark 1.2
.

In studying the space
{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, A. Cohen et al.,
[
5
]

proved (
1.5
) for the case
k=0,m=p=1,s=n-1,r=\frac{n}{n-1}
by using wavelet decompositions (see
[
11
]
for the case
k=0,m=1,p\geq 1,r=p\big(\frac{1+s}{s}\big)
, with
s>0
).

Inequality (
1.1
) in terms of fractional Sobolev spaces has been investigated by the authors in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
21
]
and the references therein. Surprisingly, there is a border line for the limiting case of Gagliardo–Nirenberg type inequality. In
[
1
]
, Brezis–Mironescu proved that the following inequality

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\theta}_{W^{\alpha,p}}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{W^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}}\,,

with
\alpha_{1}=\theta\alpha+(1-\theta)\alpha_{2}
,
\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}
, and
\theta\in(0,1)

holds if and only if

(1.7)

\alpha-\frac{1}{p}<\alpha_{2}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}\,.

As a consequence of this result,
the following inequality

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Df\|_{L^{1}}

fails whenever
0<\alpha_{1}<1
.

We note that the limiting case of (
1.6
) reads as:

(1.8)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}}\,,

where
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
, and
\alpha_{1}p_{1}=\alpha_{2}p_{2}
.

When
\alpha_{2}<1
, Brezis–Mironescu improved (
1.8
) by means of
{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
using the Littlewood–Paley decomposition. Very recently, Van Schaftingen
[
21
]
studied (
1.8
) for the case
\alpha_{2}=1
on a convex open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying certain condition. Particularly, he proved that

(1.9)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{1-\alpha_{1}}_{{\rm BMO}}\|Df\|^{\alpha_{1}}_{L^{p_{2}}}

where
0<\alpha_{1}<1
,
p_{1}\alpha_{1}=p_{2}
,
p_{2}>1
.

Inspired by the above results, we would like to study (
1.1
) by means of fractional Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces. Moreover, we also improve the limiting cases (
1.8
), (
1.9
) in terms of
\dot{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Main results

Our first result is to improve (
1.1
) by using fractional Sobolev spaces, and homogeneous Besov spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma>0
, and
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\infty
. Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
be such that
p_{1}=p_{2}\big(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}{\alpha_{1}+\sigma}\big)
, and
p_{2}(\alpha_{2}+\sigma)>1
. If
f\in\dot{B}^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f\in\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, there is a positive constant
C=C(n,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},p_{2},\sigma)
such that

(1.10)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\leq C\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\sigma}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}}\,.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that
(
1.10
) is not true for the limiting case
\sigma=\alpha_{1}=0,p_{1}=\infty
, even (
1.7
) holds, i.e:
\alpha_{2}-\frac{1}{p_{2}}>0
. Indeed, if it is the case, then (
1.10
) becomes

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}}\,.

Obviously,
the inequality cannot happen since

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

However, if
\alpha_{1}
is positive, then
(
1.10
) holds true with
\sigma=0
. This assertion is in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

\alpha_{2}>\alpha_{1}>0
, and let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
be such that
p_{1}=\frac{\alpha_{2}p_{2}}{\alpha_{1}}
, and
\alpha_{2}p_{2}>1
. If
f\in\dot{B}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f\in\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, we have

(1.11)

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{\dot{B}^{0}}\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}}\,.

Our paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we provide the definitions of fractional Sobolev spaces and homogeneous Besov spaces. Section 3 is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
. Moreover, we also obtain the homogeneous version of (
1.6
) with an elementary proof, see Lemma
3.3
.
Finally, we prove
\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{s,p}}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s,p}}
for
0<s<1
,
1\leq p<\infty
in the Appendix section.

2.
Definitions and preliminary results

2.1.
Fractional Sobolev spaces

Definition 2.1
.

For any
0<\alpha<1
, and for
1\leq p<\infty
,
we denote
\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(resp.
W^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) by the homogeneous fractional
Sobolev space (resp. the inhomogeneous fractional Sobolev space), endowed by the semi-norm:

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}}=\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)|^{p}}{|h|^{n+\alpha p}}dhdx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,

and the norm

\|f\|_{W^{\alpha,p}}=\left(\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}+\|f\|^{p}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

When
\alpha\geq 1
, we can define the higher order fractional Sobolev space as follows:

Denote
\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
by the integer part of
\alpha
. Then, we define

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&\|D^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}f\|_{L^{p}},\quad\text{if }\,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.\\
&\|D^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}f\|^{p}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}},\quad\text{otherwise}\,.\end{array}\right.

In addition, we also define

\|f\|_{W^{\alpha,p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&\|f\|_{W^{\alpha,p}},\quad\text{if }\,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.\\
&\left(\|f\|^{p}_{W^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}}+\|D^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}f\|^{p}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\text{otherwise}\,.\end{array}\right.

Notation

Through the paper, we accept the notation

\dot{W}^{\alpha,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\dot{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
; and
\dot{W}^{0,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty.

In addition, we always denote constant by C, which may change
from line to line. Moreover, the notation
C(\alpha,p,n)
means that
C
merely depends
on
\alpha,p,n
.
Next, we write
A\lesssim B
if there exists a constant
c>0
such
that
A<cB
. And we write
A\approx B
iff
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.

2.2.
Besov spaces

To define the homogeneous Besov spaces, we recall the Littlewood–Paley decomposition (see
[
22
]
). Let
\phi_{j}(x)
be the inverse Fourier transform of the
j
-th component of the dyadic decomposition i.e.,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\phi}(2^{-j}\xi)=1

except
\xi=0
, where

{\rm supp}(\hat{\phi})\subset\left\{\frac{1}{2}<|\xi|<2\right\}
.

Next, let us put

\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),D^{\alpha}\hat{f}(0)=0,\,\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},\text{ multi-index}\right\}\,,

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Schwartz space as usual.

Definition 2.2
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and for every
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the homogeneous Besov space is denoted by

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}=\left\{f\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}<\infty\right\}\,,

with

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jsq}\|\phi_{j}*f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,\text{ if }\,1\leq q<\infty,\\
&\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{2^{js}\|\phi_{j}*f\|_{L^{p}}\right\}\,,\text{ if }\,q=\infty\,.\end{array}\right.

When
p=q=\infty
, we denote
\dot{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}=\dot{B}^{s}
for short.

The following characterization of
\dot{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
is useful for our proof below.

Theorem 2.1

(see Theorem 4, p. 164,
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\big\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\big\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence of functions such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}&{\rm supp}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\subset B(0,\varepsilon),\quad\big\{\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\big\}\subset\big\{\widehat{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)\not=0\big\},\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\gamma}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=0,\,\text{for all multi-indexes }\,|\gamma|<k,\text{ where $k$ is a given integer},\\
&\big|D^{\gamma}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)\big|\leq C\varepsilon^{-(n+|\gamma|)}\,\text{ for every multi-index }\gamma\,.\end{array}\right.

Assume
s<k
. Then, we have

f\in\dot{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Leftrightarrow\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left\{\varepsilon^{-s}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}\right\}<\infty\,.

We end this section by recall the following result (see
[
7
]
).

Proposition 2.1

(Lifting operator)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\gamma
be a multi-index. Then,

\partial^{\gamma}
maps
\dot{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\dot{B}^{s-|\gamma|}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

3.
Proof of the Theorems

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We first prove Theorem
1.2
for the case
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq 1
. After that, we consider
\alpha_{i}\geq 1
,
i=1,2
.

i) Step 1:
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq 1
.
We divide our argument into the following cases.

a) The case
p_{1}=p_{2}=\infty
,
0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<1
.
Then, (
1.10
) becomes

(3.1)

\|f\|_{\dot{C}^{\alpha_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\sigma}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}_{\dot{C}^{\alpha_{2}}}\,.

To prove (
3.1
), we use a characterization of homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}
in Theorem
2.1
, and the fact that
\dot{B}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincides with
\dot{C}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in(0,1)
(see
[
10
]
).

Then, let us recall sequence
\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
in Theorem
2.1
.

For
\delta>0
, we write

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\alpha_{1}}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}\varepsilon^{-\alpha_{2}}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\varepsilon<\delta\big\}}+\varepsilon^{-(\alpha_{1}+\sigma)}\varepsilon^{\sigma}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\varepsilon\geq\delta\big\}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha_{2}}}+\delta^{-(\alpha_{1}+\sigma)}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}\,.

Minimizing the right hand side with respect to
\delta
in the indicated inequality yields

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{-\alpha_{1}}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha_{2}}}^{\frac{\alpha_{1}+\sigma}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}\,.

Since the last inequality holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, then we obtain (
3.1
).

Remark 3.1
.

It is not difficult to observe that the above proof also adapts to the two following cases:

\bullet

\alpha_{1}=0
,
\alpha_{2}<1
,
\sigma>0
. Then, we have

(3.3)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha_{2}}}^{\frac{\sigma}{\alpha_{2}+\sigma}}\,.

\bullet

\alpha_{1}=0
,
\alpha_{2}<1
,
\sigma>0
. Then, we have

(3.4)

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}_{\dot{B}^{0}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha_{2}}}^{\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}}\,.

This is Theorem
1.3
when
p_{i}=\infty
,
i=1,2
.

To end part
a)
, it remains to prove (
1.10
) for the case
\alpha_{2}=1
. That is

(3.5)

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{\sigma}{1+\sigma}}\,.

The proof is similar to the one of (
3.1
). Hence, it suffices to prove that

(3.6)

\varepsilon^{-\alpha_{1}}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathbf{1}_{\big\{\varepsilon<\delta\big\}}\leq\delta^{1-\alpha_{1}}\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}}\,.

Indeed, using vanishing moment of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
and the mean value theorem yields

\displaystyle\big|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f(x)\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}(f(x)-f(x-y))\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}}|y||\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)|\,dy\leq\varepsilon\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\varepsilon\|Df\|_{L^{\infty}}\,.

Thus, (
3.6
) follows easily.

By repeating the proof of (
3.2
), we obtain (
3.5
).

b) The case
p_{i}<\infty,\,i=1,2
.
Then, the proof follows by way of the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. For every
s>0
, if
f\in\dot{B}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then there exists a positive constant
C=C(s,\alpha,p)
such that

(3.7)

|f(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}}^{\frac{\alpha}{s+\alpha}}\big[\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)(x)\big]^{\frac{s}{s+\alpha}}\,,\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,

with

\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)(x)=\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left(\fint_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{|f(x)-f(x-y)|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha p}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

Remark 3.2
.

When
\alpha=1
, then (
3.7
) becomes

(3.8)

|f(x)|\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}}^{\frac{1}{s+1}}\big[\mathbf{M}(|Df|)(x)\big]^{\frac{s}{s+1}}\,,\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

This inequality was obtained by the authors in
[
7
]
. As a result, we get

(3.9)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}}^{\frac{1}{s+1}}\|Df\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\,,

with
p_{1}=p_{2}\big(\frac{s+1}{s}\big)
,
p_{2}\geq 1
.

This is also Theorem
1.2
when
\alpha_{1}=0
,
\alpha_{2}=1
,
s=\sigma>0
.

Remark 3.3
.

Obviously, for
1\leq p<\infty
we have
\|\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,p}}
, and
\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,1}(f)(x)\leq\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Next, applying Lemma
3.1
to
s=\sigma,\alpha=\alpha_{2}
,
p=p_{2}
, and taking the
L^{p_{1}}
-norm of (
3.7
) yield

\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}^{\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\sigma+\alpha_{2}}}\left(\int\big|\mathbf{G}_{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}(f)(x)\big|^{\frac{\sigma p_{1}}{\sigma+\alpha_{2}}}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}}\leq\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}}^{\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\sigma+\alpha_{2}}}\|f\|^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma+\alpha_{2}}}_{\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}}\,,

with
p_{1}=p_{2}\big(\frac{\sigma+\alpha_{2}}{\sigma}\big)
.

Hence, we obtain Theorem
1.2
for the case
\alpha_{1}=0
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let us recall sequence
\{\varphi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
above. Then, we have from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f(x)|+|f(x)-\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f(x)|:=\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2}\,.

We first estimate
\mathbf{I}_{1}
in terms of
\dot{B}^{-s}
. Thanks to Theorem
2.1
, we get

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}=\varepsilon^{-s}\varepsilon^{s}|\varphi_{\varepsilon}*f(x)|\leq C\varepsilon^{-s}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}}\,.

For
\mathbf{I}_{2}
, applying Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}|f(x)-f(x-y)|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=\varepsilon^{\frac{n}{p}+\alpha}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{|f(x)-f(x-y)|}{\varepsilon^{\frac{n}{p}+\alpha}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{n}{p}+\alpha}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\frac{|f(x)-f(x-y)|^{p}}{\varepsilon^{n+\alpha p}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{n}{p}+\alpha}\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big|B(0,\varepsilon)\big|^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)(x)\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha}\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)(x)\,.

Note that the last inequality follows by using the fact
\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-n}
.

By combining (
3.10
) and (
3.1
), we obtain

|f(x)|\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{-s}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\mathbf{G}_{\alpha,p}(f)(x)\right)\,.

Since the indicated inequality holds true for
\varepsilon>0
, then minimizing the right hand side of this one yields the desired result.

Hence, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Next, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<1
. Let
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
, and
r>1
be such that

(3.12)

\frac{1}{p_{1}}=\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{2}}\,.

If
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\dot{W}^{\alpha_{2},p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
f\in\dot{W}^{\alpha_{1},p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\hat{\psi}(\xi)=1
on
\{1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
, and
\widehat{\psi}=\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{k=1}\widehat{h}^{k}
, with
h^{k}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(2^{j-1},2^{j})}\big\|\frac{\widehat{h}^{k}_{j}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\|_{L^{1}}\leq C,\quad k=1,\dots,n\,,

where
h^{k}_{j}(x)=2^{-jn}h^{k}_{j}(2^{-j}x)
, and constant
C>0
is independent of
k,j
. Actually, we only need
(
4.9
)
holds for
\big\|\frac{\widehat{h}^{k}_{j}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\|_{\mathcal{M}}
instead of

\big\|\frac{\widehat{h}^{k}_{j}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\|_{L^{1}}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the space of bounded measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\|\mu\|_{\mathcal{M}}
is the total variation of
\mu
.

Next, from the construction of functions
h^{k}
,
k=1,\dots,n
, there exists a universal constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big\{\frac{\widehat{h^{k}_{j}}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\}\big\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{1}\big\|\frac{\widehat{h}^{k}_{j}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\|_{L^{1}}\,.

With the last inequality noted, we deduce from (
4.9
) that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(2^{j-1},2^{j})}\big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big\{\frac{\widehat{h^{k}_{j}}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\}\big\|_{L^{1}}\leq CC_{1},\quad k=1,\dots,n\,.

Now, observe that

h^{k}_{j}*f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big\{\frac{\widehat{h^{k}_{j}}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\}*\Delta_{te_{k}}f\,.

Thus, it follows from the triangle inequality, (
4.10
), and Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\big\|\psi_{j}*f\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\big\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}h^{k}_{j}*f\big\|_{L^{p}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big\|h^{k}_{j}*f\big\|_{L^{p}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big\{\frac{\widehat{h^{k}_{j}}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\}*\Delta_{te_{k}}f\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big\{\frac{\widehat{h^{k}_{j}}(\xi)}{e^{it\xi_{k}}-1}\big\}\big\|_{L^{1}}\big\|\Delta_{te_{k}}f\big\|_{L^{p}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big\|\Delta_{te_{k}}f\big\|_{L^{p}}\,,\quad\text{for all }t\in(2^{j-1},2^{j})\,.

On the other hand, it is clear that
\hat{\psi}(\xi)\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=\hat{\varphi}(\xi)
since
{\rm supp}(\hat{\varphi})\subset\{1/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
.

Hence, we obtain from (
4
) that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}*f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}=\|\varphi_{j}*\psi_{j}*f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\leq\|\varphi_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{1}}\|\psi_{j}*f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big\|\Delta_{te_{k}}f\big\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

for all
t\in(2^{j-1},2^{j})
.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-j\alpha p}\|\varphi_{j}*f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-j\alpha p}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\fint^{2^{j}}_{2^{j-1}}\big\|\Delta_{te_{k}}f\big\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,dt\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\Delta_{te_{k}}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha p}}

which yields

\|f\|^{p}_{\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{p,p}}\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int^{\infty}_{0}\|\Delta_{te_{k}}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha p}}\,.

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1

A Note on the Compactness of the Resolvent of the Age-Diffusion Operator
Christoph Walker

Abstract
. The generator of the semigroup associated with linear age-structured population models including spatial diffusion is shown to have compact resolvent.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
47D06, 35B35, 35M10, 92D25

1.
Introduction and Main Results

The dynamics of a population consisting of individuals structured by age and spatial position is governed by equations of the form

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+\partial_{a}u\,

\displaystyle=A(a)u\,,\qquad t>0\,,\quad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u(t,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,u(t,a)\,\mathrm{d}a\,,\qquad t>0\,,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle u(0,a)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(a)\,,\qquad a\in(0,a_{m})\,,

where
u=u(t,a)
denotes the population density

u:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times J\rightarrow E_{0}

with values in a Banach space
E_{0}
representing the spatial heterogeneity.
The age of individuals is denoted by
a\in J:=[0,a_{m}]
with
maximal age
a_{m}\in(0,\infty)
. Spatial movement of individuals is described by the age-dependent (usually: differential) operator
A
, for which we assume that
there is
\rho>0
with

(1.2a)

A\in C^{\rho}\big(J,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\,.

\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
stands for the space of generators of analytic semigroups on
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
equipped with the operator norm in
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
.
Death processes of individuals are incorporated into the operator
A
and spatial boundary conditions into the domain of definition
E_{1}
. We impose that

(1.2b)

E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\mathrel{\lhook\joinrel\relbar\kern-3.44444pt\joinrel\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow}}}E_{0}\,,

that is,
E_{1}
is a densely and compactly embedded subspace of the Banach space
E_{0}
.
Equation (
1.1b
) is the birth law with birth rate
b
, for which we shall assume that

(1.2c)

b\in L_{\infty}\big(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})\big)\,,\quad\theta\in\{0,\vartheta\}\,,

for some fixed
\vartheta\in(0,1)
. Here, the space
E_{\vartheta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\vartheta}
is an interpolation space with admissible interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\vartheta}
(see
[
1
]
). In fact, given any admissible interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
with
\theta\in(0,1)
we use the notion
E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}
and set

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}:=L_{1}(J,E_{\theta})\,,\quad\theta\in[0,1]\,.

In (
1.1c
),
u_{0}
represents the initial population. The main features of (
1.1
) are that the evolution equation (
1.1a
) has a hyperbolic character if spatial movement is neglected (i.e.
A=0
) or a parabolic character if aging is neglected and that condition (
1.1b
) is nonlocal with respect to age.

Age-structured population models (with and without spatial diffusion) have been the subject of intensive research in the past; we refer e.g. to
[
13
]
and the references therein for more details and concrete examples.

Realistic models for the evolution of age- and spatially structured populations are nonlinear and involve density-dependent vital rates
b
or nonlinear operators
A
incorporating nonlinear death processes or nonlinear diffusion. The study of linear equations like (
1.1
) is important for the understanding of such more complex models. For instance, they arise as (part of the) linearization when investigating stability properties of equilibria
[
12
]
. It is well-known that the solutions to the linear problem (
1.1
) may be represented by a semigroup on the phase space
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
. We refer to
[
13
,
7
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
9
]
and the references therein for the investigation of this semigoup though this list is far from being complete.

It follows from
[
10
, Theorem 2.3 (c)]
that the generator
\mathbb{A}:\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})\subset\mathbb{E}_{0}\to\mathbb{E}_{0}
of this semigroup is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A}):=\bigg\{\psi\in C(J,E_{0})\,;\,

\displaystyle\exists\,\zeta_{\psi}\in\mathbb{E}_{0}\,\text{such that $\psi$ is the mild solution to}

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\psi=A(a)\psi-\zeta_{\psi}(a)\,,\ a\in J\,,\ \ \psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\bigg\}

and

\mathbb{A}\psi:=\zeta_{\psi}\,.

Mild solution here means that

\psi(a)=\Pi(a,0)\psi(0)-\int_{0}^{a}\Pi(a,\sigma)\,\zeta(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,

where

\big\{\Pi(a,\sigma)\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0})\,;\,a\in J\,,\,0\leq\sigma\leq a\big\}

denotes the parabolic evolution operator on
E_{0}
with regularity subspace
E_{1}
in the sense of
[
1
, Section II.2.1]
(existence and uniqueness of
\Pi
is ensured by (
1.2a
) and
[
1
, II. Corollary 4.4.2]
).
With this notation, the linear problem (
1.1
) can be reformulated as the Cauchy problem

(1.3)

u^{\prime}(t)=\mathbb{A}u(t)\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\qquad u(0)=u_{0}

in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Since
\mathbb{A}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(e^{t\mathbb{A}})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, there is for each initial value
u_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
a unique solution

u\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{E}_{0})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{+},D(\mathbb{A}))

to (
1.3
), where
D(\mathbb{A})
means the domain
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
equipped with the graph norm. Precise information on
D(\mathbb{A})
– which is defined implicitly initially – is thus pertinent, also with regard to evolution problems involving time-dependent operators
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}(t)
, see
[
11
]
. For instance, one can show
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
that

\left\{\psi\in\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap W_{1}^{1}(J,E_{0})\,;\,\psi(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\,\psi(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\right\}

is a core for
\mathbb{A}
; that is, a dense subspace of
D(\mathbb{A})
. Owing to (
1.2b
) and
[
6
, Corollary 4]
, this space is also compactly embedded into
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Moreover, from
[
10
, Theorem 2.3 (c)]
it follows that
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
for each
\theta\in[0,1)
, where the spaces
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
play an important role in the study of nonlinear problems as domains of definitions for the nonlinearities.
Herein we prove that these embeddings are compact:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose (
1.2
). Then
\mathbb{A}
has compact resolvent. In fact, for every
\theta\in[0,1)
, the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
is compact.

Compact resolvent means that
(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}
is a compact operator on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
for every
\lambda>0
large enough. In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, the definition of
D(\mathbb{A})
entails to derive compactness of the mapping

\zeta\mapsto\int_{0}^{a}\Pi(a,\sigma)\,\zeta(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

in
\mathbb{E}_{0}=L_{1}(J,E_{0})
. To this end we shall follow the lines of
[
2
]
.

Besides compactness of the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{0}
, Theorem
1.1
also yields information on the spectrum of
\mathbb{A}
, e.g. that it consists of eigenvalues only. This information was derived previously in
[
10
]
by showing that the semigroup
(e^{t\mathbb{A}})_{t\geq 0}
is eventually compact (under slightly stronger assumptions). However, for perturbations of the form
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
(arising, for instance, when linearizing equations incorporating nonlinear vital rates), the eventual compactness of the corresponding semigroup has recently been obtained in
[
12
]
, but only for particular perturbations
\mathbb{B}
. Nonetheless, Theorem
1.1
ensures for general perturbations
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
with
\alpha\in[0,1)
the compactness of the resolvent of
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
and thus provides spectral properties for perturbed operators
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
:

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose (
1.2
). Let
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
for some
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Then
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
with domain
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
generates a strongly continuous semigroup
(e^{t(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and has compact resolvent. In particular, the spectrum
\sigma(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})
is a pure point spectrum without finite accumulation point.

If
E_{0}
is an ordered Banach space and

(1.4a)

A(a)
is resolvent positive for each
a\in J

and

(1.4b)

b\in L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}_{+}(E_{0}))\,,

then the semigroup
(e^{t\mathbb{A}})_{t\geq 0}
generated by
\mathbb{A}
is positive on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, see
[
10
]
; that is,
\mathbb{A}
is resolvent positive.

In case that
E_{0}
is a Banach lattice, more information on the spectral bound

s(\mathbb{A}):=\sup\{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\,;\,\lambda\in\sigma(\mathbb{A})\}

is available, see
[
3
]
:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
E_{0}
be a Banach lattice and suppose (
1.2
) and (
1.4
). If
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{L}_{+}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
for some
\alpha\in[0,1)
, then
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
generates a positive semigroup
(e^{t(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
with
e^{t\mathbb{A}}\leq e^{t(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})}
for
t\geq 0
. Moreover,
s(\mathbb{A})\leq s(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})
and

(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\leq(\lambda-\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B})^{-1}\,,\quad\lambda>s(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})\,.

If
s(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})>-\infty
, then
s(\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B})
is an eigenvalue of
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
possessing an eigenvector
\psi\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
with
\psi\geq 0
. E.g. this is the case if

b(a)\Pi(a,0)\in\mathcal{L}_{+}(E_{0})
is strongly positive for
a
in a subset of
J
of positive measure.

We prove the above statements in the next sections. To this end, we assume throughout the following (
1.2
).

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

I. Compact Resolvent

First note that, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the family

\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma):=e^{-\lambda(a-\sigma)}\Pi(a,\sigma)\,,\qquad a\in J\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\,,

is the evolution operator associated with
-\lambda+A
. Moreover, according to
[
1
, II. Lemma 5.1.3]
there are
\varpi\in\mathbb{R}
and
M_{\vartheta}\geq 1
with

(2.1)

\|\Pi(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta})}+(a-\sigma)^{\vartheta}\,\|\Pi(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\vartheta})}\leq M_{\vartheta}e^{\varpi(a-\sigma)}\,,\quad 0\leq\sigma\leq a\leq a_{m}\,.

We may choose
\lambda>0
in the resolvent set of
\mathbb{A}
so large such that for the operator

Q_{\lambda}:=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\,\mathrm{d}a\in\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta})

we have
\|Q_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta})}<1
(see (
1.2c
) and (
2.1
)).
Consider a bounded sequence
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Then, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\psi_{j}:=(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\phi_{j}\in\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})\,,

and the definition of
\mathbb{A}
implies

(2.2)

\psi_{j}(a)=\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\psi_{j}(0)+\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\phi_{j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,

and

(2.3)

(1-Q_{\lambda})\psi_{j}(0)=\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi_{j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\,.

In particular, since by (
1.2c
) and (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle\Bigg\|

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi_{j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\Bigg\|_{E_{\vartheta}}

\displaystyle\leq M_{\vartheta}\,\|b\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta}))}\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\int_{0}^{a}e^{(-\lambda+\varpi)(a-\sigma)}(a-\sigma)^{-\vartheta}\,\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle=M_{\vartheta}\,\|b\|_{L_{\infty}(J,\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta}))}\,\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\int_{\sigma}^{a_{m}}e^{(-\lambda+\varpi)(a-\sigma)}(a-\sigma)^{-\vartheta}\,\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq c\|\phi_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,,

the sequence

\left(\int_{0}^{a_{m}}b(a)\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\,\phi_{j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}a\right)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}

is bounded in
E_{\vartheta}
. Thus, (
2.3
) and
\|Q_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta})}<1
imply that

(2.4)

(\psi_{j}(0))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\ \text{ is bounded in}\ E_{\vartheta}\,.

We adopt the proof of
[
2
]
in order to prove that
the set
\{u_{j}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
, given by

u_{j}(a):=\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\phi_{j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\,,

is relatively compact in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. We split this into two steps:

(i)
Let
\mu>0
be fixed and define

v_{j}^{\mu}(a):=\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,a)u_{j}(a)=\int_{0}^{a}\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)\phi_{j}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,\quad a\in J\,,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}\,,

where we used the evolution property
1
1

1

Set
A(a):=A(a_{m})
for
a>a_{m}
to obtain
A\in C^{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0}))
. The uniqueness assertion of
[
1
, II. Corollary 4.4.2]
ensures that the corresponding evolution operator then also extends
\Pi
.

\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,a)\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)=\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)\,,\quad\sigma\leq a\leq\mu+a\,.

Analogously to the derivation of (
2.4
), the sequence

(2.5)

\displaystyle\text{$(v_{j}^{\mu})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}$ is bounded in $\mathbb{E}_{\vartheta}$}\,.

Note that the space
E_{\vartheta}
embeds compactly into
E_{0}
due to (
1.2b
).

For the equi-integrability recall from
[
1
, Section II.2.1]
that the evolution operator has the continuity property
\Pi\in C(\Delta_{J}^{*},\mathcal{L}(E_{0}))
, where
\Delta_{J}^{*}:=\{(a,\sigma)\in J\times J\,;\,\sigma<a\}
. Uniform continuity on compact subsets of
\Delta_{J}^{*}
implies that, given
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\eta:=\eta(\varepsilon,\mu)>0
such that

\|\Pi_{\lambda}(a_{1},\sigma_{1})-\Pi_{\lambda}(a_{2},\sigma_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\leq\varepsilon

whenever
(a_{i},\sigma_{i})\in\Delta_{J}^{*}
,
\mu\leq a_{i}-\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
|(a_{1},\sigma_{1})-(a_{2},\sigma_{2})|\leq\eta
.
We use this and (
2.1
) to derive, for
h\in(0,\eta)
with
0<a\leq a+h\leq a_{m}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}(a+h)-v_{j}^{\mu}(a)\|_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{a+h}\|\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a+h,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{a}\|\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a+h,\sigma)-\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq c(a_{m})\int_{a}^{a+h}\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{a}\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

and therefore

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a+h)-\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\leq hc(a_{m})\|\phi_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}+\varepsilon a_{m}\|\phi_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}\,,

with tilde referring to trivial extension. Since
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we deduce

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\,\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{a_{m}}\|\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a+h)-\tilde{v}_{j}^{\mu}(a)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}a=0\,.

That is,
\{v_{j}^{\mu}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equi-integrable. Therefore, taking into account (
2.5
) we are in a position to apply
[
6
, Theorem 3]
and derive that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\{v_{j}^{\mu}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}\ \text{ is relatively compact in $\mathbb{E}_{0}$ for $\mu>0$}\,.

(ii)
We consider the limit
\mu\to 0
. Given arbitrary
\varepsilon\in(0,a_{m})
, we argue as in part
(i)
to find
\eta(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\|\Pi_{\lambda}(a_{1},\sigma_{1})-\Pi_{\lambda}(a_{2},\sigma_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\leq\varepsilon

whenever
(a_{i},\sigma_{i})\in\Delta_{J}^{*}
,
\varepsilon\leq a_{i}-\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
|(a_{1},\sigma_{1})-(a_{2},\sigma_{2})|\leq\eta(\varepsilon)
. Thus, for
0<\mu<\eta(\varepsilon)
and
a\in J
with
a\geq\varepsilon
, we obtain from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}(a)-u_{j}(a)\|_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{a-\varepsilon}\|\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)-\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{a-\varepsilon}^{a}\big(\|\Pi_{\lambda}(\mu+a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}+\|\Pi_{\lambda}(a,\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\big)\,\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{a-\varepsilon}\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+c(a_{m})\int_{a-\varepsilon}^{a}\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,,

while, for
0\leq a\leq\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}(a)-u_{j}(a)\|_{E_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq c(a_{m})\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|\phi_{j}(\sigma)\|_{E_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|v_{j}^{\mu}-u_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq c(a_{m})\,\varepsilon\,\|\phi_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}+\varepsilon\,a_{m}\,\|\phi_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}

so that, since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary,

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to 0}\,\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\,\|v_{j}^{\mu}-u_{j}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}=0\,.

Together with (
2.6
) we conclude that
\{u_{j}\,;\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
.

(iii)
Finally, since

\|\Pi_{\lambda}(a+h,0)-\Pi_{\lambda}(a,0)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta},E_{0})}\leq ch^{\vartheta}\,,\quad 0\leq a\leq a+h\leq a_{m}\,,

according to
[
1
, II.Equation(5.3.8)]
, we infer from (
2.4
) and the Arzelà - Ascoli Theorem that
(\Pi_{\lambda}(\cdot,0)\psi_{j}(0))_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
C(J,E_{0})
. Consequently, the previous step
(ii)
and (
2.2
) entail that
(\psi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is relatively compact in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. Therefore,
\mathbb{A}
has compact resolvent.

II. Compact Embedding

Since
\mathbb{A}
has compact resolvent, the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{0}
is compact. The compact embedding into
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
for
\theta\in(0,1)
fixed follows by an interpolation argument. Indeed, choosing
\varepsilon>0
with
\theta<1-3\varepsilon
we set
\theta_{k}:=\theta(1-k\varepsilon)^{-1}
for
k=1,2,3
so that
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\theta_{3}<1
. Then
[
1
, Equation I.(2.5.2)]
yields for the continuous interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta_{3},\infty}^{0}
that

\big(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}\big)_{\theta_{3},\infty}^{0}\hookrightarrow\big(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}\big)_{\theta_{2},1}=\big(L_{1}(J,E_{0}),L_{1}(J,E_{1-\varepsilon})\big)_{\theta_{2},1}

while
[
8
, Theorem 1.18.4]
ensures for the real interpolation functor
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta_{2},1}
that

\big(L_{1}(J,E_{0}),L_{1}(J,E_{1-\varepsilon})\big)_{\theta_{2},1}\doteq L_{1}\big(J,(E_{0},E_{1-\varepsilon})_{\theta_{2},1}\big)\,.

Finally,
[
1
, I. Remark 2.11.2 (a)]
implies

L_{1}\big(J,(E_{0},E_{1-\varepsilon})_{\theta_{2},1}\big)\hookrightarrow L_{1}\big(J,E_{(1-\varepsilon)\theta_{1}}\big)=\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\,.

Gathering these findings we obtain the continuous embedding

\big(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}\big)_{\theta_{3},\infty}^{0}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\theta}

so that there is
c>0
with

(2.7)

\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{\theta}}\leq c\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0}}^{1-\theta_{3}}\,\|\phi\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}}^{\theta_{3}}\,,\quad\phi\in\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}\,.

Now, if
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a bounded sequence in
D(\mathbb{A})
, then it is also bounded in
\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}
due to the continuous embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{1-\varepsilon}
. Moreover, since the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{0}
is compact, we may assume without loss of generality that it is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. According to (
2.7
) it is also a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
and thus converges. Consequently, the embedding
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
is compact. This proves Theorem
1.1
.∎

3.
Proof of Corollary
1.2

It was shown in
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
that
\mathbb{A}+\mathbb{B}
with domain
\mathrm{dom}(\mathbb{A})
generates a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{E}_{0}
when
\mathbb{B}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{\alpha},\mathbb{E}_{0})
for some
\alpha\in[0,1)
.
Thus, for
\lambda>0
sufficiently large, both
\lambda-\mathbb{A}
and
\lambda-\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B}
are invertible with

(\lambda-\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B})^{-1}=(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\big(1-\mathbb{B}(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\big)^{-1}\,.

Since
(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})
is compact according to Theorem
1.1
while

\big(1-\mathbb{B}(\lambda-\mathbb{A})^{-1}\big)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{E}_{0})

since
D(\mathbb{A})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}
(see
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
), it follows that
(\lambda-\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{B})^{-1}
is compact.
This proves Corollary
1.2
.∎

4.
Proof of Corollary
1.3

Let
E_{0}
be a Banach lattice and assume, in addition to (
1.2
), also (
1.4
). Corollary

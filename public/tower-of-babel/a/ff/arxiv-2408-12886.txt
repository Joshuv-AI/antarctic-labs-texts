On Uniform Functions on Configuration Spaces of Large Scale Interacting Systems Thanks: This work is supported by JST CREST Grant Number JPMJCR1913 and KAKENHI 24K21515.
Kenichi Bannai, Makiko Sasada

Abstract
. Stochastic large scale interacting systems can be studied via the observables, i.e. functions on the underlying configuration space. In our previous article, we introduced the concept of uniform functions , which are suitable class of functions on configuration spaces underlying stochastic systems on infinite graphs. An important consequence is the successful characterization of conserved quantities without introducing the notion of stationary distributions. In this article, we further develop the theory of uniform functions and construct the theory independent of any choice of a base state. Furthermore, we generalize the notion of interactions given in our previous article to accommodate the case where there are multiple possible state transitions on adjacent vertices. We then prove that if the interaction is exchangeable , then any uniform function which gives a global conserved quantity can be expressed as a sum of local conserved quantities of the interaction. Contrary to our previous article, we do not need to assume that the interaction is irreducibly quantified. This shows that our theory of uniform functions on configuration spaces over infinite graphs with transition structure given by an exchangeable interaction is a natural framework to study general stochastic large scale interacting systems. While some of the ideas in this article are based on our previous article, the article is logically independent and self-contained.

Introduction

Deriving the macroscopic evolution from the dynamics of microscopic systems is a very fundamental and challenging task. As a mathematically rigorous theory, hydrodynamic limits for stochastic large scale interacting systems have been widely studied. In order to provide a new perspective for the analysis of such large scale interacting systems, in our previous article
[
BKS20
]
, we introduced the concept of configuration space with transition
structure and the associated space of
uniform functions
.
The uniform functions form a suitable class of functions including the conserved quantities on
configuration spaces on infinite graphs
underlying stochastic large scale interacting systems.
The theory was extended in
[
BS21L2
]
under certain assumptions to give a proof of
Varadhan’s decomposition for hydrodynamic limits.
In
[
BKS20
]
and
[
BS21L2
]
, the theory was constructed using a choice of a
fixed base state of the local state space.
In this article, we construct the theory independent of any
such choice of base state.
Furthermore, we expand the notion
of interactions to accommodate the case when the
transitions between states on adjacent vertices is given by a general
symmetric
digraph
(i.e.
directed graph
),
which we again call an
interaction
.
We then prove that if the interaction is
exchangeable
,
then any uniform function on the configuration space
invariant under transitions can be
expressed as the sum of the local conserved quantities of the interaction.
This is a generalization of
[
BKS20
]
*Theorem 3.7.
Although we build on ideas initiated in
[
BKS20
]
,
our article is logically independent of such results.

In constructing our large scale interacting system, we express the possible transition

Our construction allows not only nearest neighbor models but more general models by changing the graph

. For the exclusion
\phi_{\operatorname{ex}}
,
if we take the graph

to be the Euclidean lattice

, then this model underlies the nearest neighbor exclusion process.
The exclusion process is one of the most fundamental models of the interacting particle systems
and has been studied extensively
[
Spo91
,
KL99
,

,

,

,

.

Moreover, if we take the graph

be

.
This versatility of the model is one reason that

Given an interaction
\phi\subset(S\times S)\times(S\times S)
, we say that a function

\xi\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a

for

, if the function

\tilde{\xi}\colon S\times S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(2)

\tilde{\xi}(s_{1},s_{2})\coloneqq\xi(s_{1})+\xi(s_{2}),\qquad\forall(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S

is constant on the connected components of the graph
(S\times S,\phi)
.
In other words,
\xi
is a function whose sum is preserved by transitions of an interaction.

For a conserved quantity
\xi
, let
\xi_{x}(\eta)\coloneqq\xi(\eta_{x})
for any
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
.
Then by definition,
\xi_{x}
for any
x\in X
is local at
x
with radius
0
, hence by
Proposition

1.1
,

\xi_{X}\coloneqq\sum_{x\in X}\xi_{x}

defines a uniform function.
For the case
(\{0,1\},\phi_{\operatorname{ex}})
, the function
\xi\colon\{0,1\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

given by

is a conserved quantity of
(\{0,1\},\phi_{\operatorname{ex}})
.
If
X
is finite,
then
\xi_{X}\colon\{0,1\}^{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a function such that

\xi_{X}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in X}\xi(\eta_{x})

gives the total number of particles of

.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that an interaction
(S,\phi)

is

if and only if
for any
(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S
,
the configurations

(s_{1},s_{2})
and
(s_{2},s_{1})
are in the same connected component of
the graph
(S\times S,\phi)
.

The interaction
(\{0,1\},\phi_{\operatorname{ex}})
is exchangeable.
The article
[
BKSWY24
]
gives classifications and examples of various
exchangeable interactions, hence there are an abundance of examples of exchangeable interactions.
Let
(X,E)
be connected and locally finite
as in
Proposition

1.1
.
Since a uniform function is given by an infinite sum, it is in general not an actual function on the configuration space.
However, one may define the difference of values of a uniform function
at
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
with respect to any transition
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
.
Hence a uniform function behaves as a potential on the configuration space.

We say that a uniform function on
S^{X}
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
,
if the difference of values with respect to any transition is
zero
. The main theorem of our article is as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(=
Theorem

3.11
)
.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction which is exchangeable,
and assume that
(X,E)
is connected, locally finite and infinite.
Suppose
f
is a uniform function on
S^{X}

which is invariant via transitions of

(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Then there exists a conserved quantity
\xi\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
f=\xi_{X}
.

A function which is invariant via transitions of
S^{X}
may be
viewed as a global conserved quantity of
S^{X}
.

Theorem

1.3
implies that any global conserved quantity expressed by a uniform function
is given at the sum of a local conserved quantity of the interaction.
Hence our theory of uniform functions gives a class of well-behaved
global conserved quantities of the configuration space.
Contrary to
[
BKS20
]
, we do not need to assume that
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified.

Theorem

1.3
may be interpreted in terms of uniform cohomology.
Let
C_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})
be the space of local functions, and let
C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})
be the space of local functions modulo the subspace of constant functions. In other words,
C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})\coloneqq C_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})/\sim
, where
f\sim g
for
f,g\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})
if and only if
f-g
is a constant function.
We may define the differential

{\partial}\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X})

by

{\partial}f(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\coloneqq f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)

for any

(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E},

where
C^{1}(S^{X})
is the subspace of
{\mathrm{Map}}(\Phi_{E},\mathbb{R})
consisting of functions
\omega\colon\Phi_{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that
\omega(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=-\omega(\eta^{\prime},\eta)
for any
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
.
Denote by
C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
the space of uniform functions modulo the subspace of constant functions.
We may linearly extend the above differential to obtain a differential

{\partial}\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X}).

We define the
0
-th uniform cohomology by

H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\coloneqq{\mathrm{Ker}\,}\bigl({\partial}\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X})\bigr).

As an analogy of the fact that
0
-th cohomology
H^{0}(X)
coincides with the space of functions
constant on the connected components of the graph
(X,E)
(see
[
BKS20
]
*Proposition A.8),
we may prove that the
0
-th uniform cohomology
H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})

coincides with the space of uniform functions invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
,
i.e. constant on the connected components of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Combining
Theorem

1.3
with this fact, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.4

(=
Corollary

4.5
)
.

Suppose
(S,\phi)
and
(X,E)
are as in
Theorem

1.3
.
Then we have a canonical isomorphism

\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)\cong H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X}),\qquad\xi\mapsto\xi_{X},

where
\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
denotes the space of conserved quantities of
(S,\phi)
modulo the subspace of constant functions.

If
X
is infinite, then
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
in general has an infinite number of connected components.
Hence the graph cohomology
H^{0}(S^{X})
, which coincides with the space of functions constant
on the connected components of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
is in general infinite dimensional.
However, if
\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
is finite dimensional, as in the case when
S
is a finite set,
then
Corollary

1.4
implies that the space
H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
, which may be interpreted as the
space of uniform functions constant on the connected components of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
,
is finite dimensional.
The theory of uniform functions allows for the construction of such well-behaved cohomology theory.
We emphasize that Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
hold in general only when
X
is infinite.

2.
Uniform Functions on Configuration Spaces

In this section, we give the definition of uniform functions on the space of configurations on
a symmetric digraph. We then prove in
Proposition

2.5
that the space of uniform
functions for distinct choice of the base state
*\in S
are canonically isomorphic.
Let
S
be a non-empty set, which we call the
local state space
.
An element of
S
expresses a state of a system on a single site.
Furthermore, let
(X,E)
be a symmetric digraph. In other words,
X
is a set called the
set of vertices
,

E\subset X\times X
is a set called the
set of edges
, and we assume that
e=(oe,te)\in E
if and only if

\bar{e}=(te,oe)\in E
.
The set
X
gives the underlying space for the system. The edges
E
express the vertices in
X
whose local states interact with each other.
We say that the graph
(X,E)
is
locally finite
,
if
E_{x}\coloneqq\{e\in E\mid oe=x\}
is a finite set for any
x\in X
.
Here
oe\in X
is the origin of the edge
e
.
In what follows, we will assume that the graph
(X,E)
is connected and locally finite.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
configuration space
of states
S
on
(X,E)
by
S^{X}\coloneqq\prod_{x\in X}S
.

We call any element
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
a
configuration
.
The configuration space expresses all of the possible configuration of states that our model may take.
The configurations will be quantified via the observables, i.e. functions
f\colon S^{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on the
configuration space.
We say that
f\colon S^{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
local function
, if there exists a finite
\Lambda\subset X

such that the value
f(\eta)
depends only on the states on
\Lambda
, i.e.
(\eta_{x})_{x\in\Lambda}\in S^{\Lambda}
. Such function corresponds to a function in
C(S^{\Lambda})
,
where
C(S^{\Lambda})\coloneqq{\mathrm{Map}}(S^{\Lambda},\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of real valued functions on
S^{\Lambda}
.
If we let
\mathscr{I}
be the set of finite subsets
\Lambda\subset X
, then
the space of local functions is given as
C_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})=\bigcup_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}}C(S^{\Lambda})
.

Fix
*\in S
, which we call the base state, and let
S^{X}_{*}
be the subset of
S^{X}
consisting of configurations
whose components are
*
for all but finite
x\in X
.
We denote by
\star
the configuration in
S^{X}
whose components are
all at base state. Then
\star\in S^{X}_{*}
.
For
\Lambda\subset X
, we have
C(S^{\Lambda})\subset C(S^{X}_{*})
.
For any function
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
and
\Lambda\subset X
,
let
\iota^{\Lambda}_{*}f\in C(S^{\Lambda})
be the function

\iota^{\Lambda}_{*}f(\eta)\coloneqq f(\eta|_{\Lambda}),\quad\forall\eta\in S^{X}_{*},

where
\eta|_{\Lambda}\in S^{X}_{*}
is defined as the configuration whose component for
x\in\Lambda
coincides
with that of
\eta
, and is at base state
*
for components outside
\Lambda
.
This defines a linear operator
\iota^{\Lambda}_{*}\colon C(S^{X}_{*})\rightarrow C(S^{\Lambda})\subset C(S^{X}_{*})
.
In particular, if
\Lambda
is finite, then
\iota^{\Lambda}_{*}f
is a local function.
We define the space of
local functions with exact support
\Lambda
by

C_{\Lambda}(S^{X}_{*})\coloneqq\bigl\{f\in C(S^{\Lambda})\mid\text{$\forall$ finite $\Lambda^{\prime}\subset X$ such that
$\Lambda\not\subset\Lambda^{\prime}$, we have $\iota^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{*}f=0$}\bigr\}.

A local function
f\in C(S^{\Lambda})
is in
C_{\Lambda}(S^{X}_{*})
if and only if
f(\eta)=0
for any
configuration
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{\Lambda}
satisfying
\eta_{x}=*
for some
x\in\Lambda
.
For any
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
, we define the support of
\eta
by
{\mathrm{Supp}}(\eta)\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\eta_{x}\neq*\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
(f^{*}_{\Lambda})
is a system of functions such that
f^{*}_{\Lambda}\in C_{\Lambda}(S^{X}_{*})

for any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
. Then
f=\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}}f^{*}_{\Lambda}
gives a well-defined function on
S^{X}_{*}
.

Proof.

For any
\eta\in S^{X}_{*}
, we have
f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)\neq 0
if and only if
\Lambda\subset{\mathrm{Supp}}(\eta)
.
Since the support
{\mathrm{Supp}}(\eta)
is finite,
if we are given functions
f^{*}_{\Lambda}\in C_{\Lambda}(S^{X}_{*})
for any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
, then
we see that

f(\eta)=\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}}f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)=\sum_{\Lambda\subset{\mathrm{Supp}}(\eta)}f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)

is a well-defined finite sum. Hence the right hand side of
Eq.

3

defines a function on
S^{X}_{*}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

For any function
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
, there exists a unique system of functions
(f^{*}_{\Lambda})
in
C_{\Lambda}(S^{X}_{*})

for any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
such that

(3)

f=\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}}f^{*}_{\Lambda}

as a function on
S^{X}_{*}
.

Proof.

If such
(f^{*}_{\Lambda})
satisfying (
3
) exists,
then by definition of

C_{\Lambda}(S^{X}_{*})
, we see that

(4)

\iota^{\Lambda}_{*}f=\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}

for any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
.
We will construct
f^{*}_{\Lambda}
satisfying (
4
)
by induction on the number of elements in
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
.
If
\Lambda=\emptyset
, then we let
f^{*}_{\emptyset}\coloneqq\iota^{\emptyset}_{*}f=f(\star)
.
For any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
, assume that there exists
f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}

for any
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda

such that
\iota^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{*}f=\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}
is satisfied for any proper subset
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset\Lambda
.
We let

f^{*}_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\iota_{*}^{\Lambda}f-\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}.

Then for any
\Lambda^{\prime}\in\mathscr{I}
such that
\Lambda\not\subset\Lambda^{\prime}
, we have
\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda
.
Hence

\iota^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{*}f^{*}_{\Lambda}=\iota^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{*}(\iota_{*}^{\Lambda}f)-\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda}\iota_{*}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=\iota_{*}^{\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}}f-\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}}\iota_{*}^{\Lambda^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=0,

where the
second equality follows from the definition of
\iota^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{*}
and the fact that
f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}\in C_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}(S^{X})
and
the last equality follows from the induction hypothesis for
\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda
.
This shows that
f^{*}_{\Lambda}\in C_{\Lambda}(S^{X})

3.
Configuration Space with Transition Structure

In this section, we first define the notion of interactions and conserved quantities.
Then we will consider the configuration space on an underlying graph given by the interaction,
and prove our main theorem.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a non-empty set.
We say that
\phi\subset(S\times S)\times(S\times S)
is an
interaction
,
if
(S\times S,\phi)
is a symmetric digraph.
In other words, we have
(s_{1},s_{2})\in\phi

(s_{2},s_{1})\in\phi

(S,\phi)
as an interaction,
and the graph

The set

represents all of the possible configurations on two adjacent vertices of an underlying graph,
and the associated graph expresses all of the possible transitions
of
S\times S
.

Remark 3.2
.

In

to
\eta^{\prime}
.
Thus, intuitively, a microscopic stochastic process associated to the interaction
(S,\phi)
on a connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
would be irreducible.
Since macroscopically, the microscopic stochastic movements of the local states should be negligible,
the observables of our microscopic configuration space relevant for the
associated macroscopic dynamics obtained by taking the hydrodynamic limit
should be invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
This is the reason we are interested in functions on
S^{X}
which are invariant via transitions.

The uniform functions of
Definition

2.4
is not in itself a function on
S^{X}
.
Hence we first define what is meant for a uniform function to be invariant
via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
For any
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
, since
f
is not generally a function on
S^{X}
,
the values
f(\eta)
and
f(\eta^{\prime})
may not be defined for
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
. However,
given a finite sequence of transitions from
\eta
to
\eta^{\prime}
,
we may define a well-defined difference
f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)
as follows.
Due to this property, uniform functions may be understood as a kind of potential
on the configuration space.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any uniform function
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
and
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}
such that

\Delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\eta_{x}\neq\eta^{\prime}_{x}\}
is a finite set,
then the difference

f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)

extending the difference of local functions gives a well-defined value.
In particular, given any
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
,
the difference
f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)
gives a well-defined value.

Proof.

Consider a base state
*\in S
and an expansion

f=\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{R}}f^{*}_{\Lambda}
of
Proposition

2.3
for some
R>0
.
Then for any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{R}
such that
\Lambda\cap\Delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}=\emptyset
,
we have
f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)=f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta^{\prime})
. Hence the sum of the difference

(8)

f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)\coloneqq\sum_{\Lambda\cap\Delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset}(f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta^{\prime})-f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta))

gives a well-defined finite sum and is independent of the choice of the base state
*\in S
.
By definition, this difference extends the difference for local functions.
The statement for a finite sequence of transitions
from
\eta
to
\eta^{\prime}
follows from the fact that
\Lambda_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}
is finite
in this case.
∎

Definition 3.9
.

We say that a uniform function
f
is
invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
,
if for any transition
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
,
the difference
f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)=0
.

Let
f
be a uniform function which is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Then by definition, the realization
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X}_{*})

for any base point
*\in S^{X}
is a function which is constant on the connected components of

(S^{X}_{*},\Phi_{E}^{*})
where
\Phi_{E}^{*}:=\Phi_{E}\cap(S^{X}_{*}\times S^{X}_{*})
.
The uniform functions
\xi_{X}
obtained from a conserved quantity
\xi\in\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)

gives an example of a uniform function which is invariant via transitions.

Lemma 3.10
.

For any conserved quantity
\xi\in\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
, the uniform function
\xi_{X}
of
Definition

3.6
is invariant via transitions
of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.

Proof.

We select a base state
*\in S
.
For any
\xi\in\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
,
we take the normalization
\xi(*)=0
.
Then
\xi_{x}\in C_{\{x\}}(S^{X}_{*})
.
Hence the sum
\xi_{X}=\sum_{x\in X}\xi_{x}
is in fact the expansion
Eq.

3
.
Suppose
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
.
By the definition of
\Phi_{E}
,
there exists
e=(x,y)\in E
such that
\eta^{\prime}_{z}=\eta_{z}
for
z\neq x,y

and
((\eta_{x},\eta_{y}),(\eta^{\prime}_{x},\eta^{\prime}_{y}))\in\phi
.
Hence by
the definition of a conserved quantity (
7
), we have

\xi(\eta_{x})+\xi(\eta_{y})=\xi(\eta^{\prime}_{x})+\xi(\eta^{\prime}_{y})
.
This shows that

\xi_{X}(\eta^{\prime})-\xi_{X}(\eta)=0,

hence that
\xi_{X}
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
∎

As in
Definition

1.2
of the introduction, we say that an interaction
(S,\phi)
is
exchangeable

if and only if
for any
(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S
,
the configurations

(s_{1},s_{2})
and
(s_{2},s_{1})
are in the same connected component of
(S\times S,\phi)
.
We may now state our main theorem.

Theorem 3.11
.

Suppose
(S,\phi)
is an interaction which is exchangeable,
and let
(X,E)
be a connected and locally finite symmetric digraph such that
X
is infinite.
Let
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
be a uniform function which is invariant via transitions of

(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Then there exists a conserved quantity
\xi\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
f=\xi_{X}
.

Note that contrary to
[
BKS20
]
, we do not need to assume that the
interaction
(S,\phi)
is irreducibly quantified.
The global observables of the
entire large scale interacting system expressed by a uniform function
which is invariant via transitions may be regarded as a global
conserved quantity.
The above theorem implies that the global conserved quantity of the
entire system
is always given as the sum of a local conserved quantity
\xi
associated to a local interaction.

We will prove
Theorem

3.11
at the end of this section.
In what follows, we assume that
(S,\phi)
is an interaction which is exchangeable
and that
(X,E)
is connected and locally finite.
We first prove that the reshuffling of the components of a configuration
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}

in a configuration space for a connected graph
preserves the connected components of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Consider
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
.
For any
x,y\in X
, if we let
\eta^{x,y}
be the configuration obtained from
\eta
by exchanging the

x
and
y
components so that
\eta^{x,y}_{x}\coloneqq\eta_{y}
and
\eta^{x,y}_{y}\coloneqq\eta_{x}
.
For any
e\in E
, since we have assumed that
(S,\phi)
is exchangeable,

(\eta_{oe},\eta_{te})
and
(\eta_{te},\eta_{oe})
are in the same connected component of
(S\times S,\phi)
.
Hence the configuration
\eta^{oe,te}

and
\eta
are in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
More generally, for
x,y\in X
, since we have assumed that

(X,E)
is connected, there exists a path
\vec{\gamma}=(e^{1},\ldots,e^{N})

from
x
to
y
. By successively exchanging the components of
\eta
with respect to
oe^{i},te^{i}

for the edges
e^{i}
in
\vec{\gamma}
from
i=1
to
N
and then in the opposite order from
i=N-1
to
i=1
, we see that
\eta^{x,y}
and
\eta
are in the connected component
of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
. This gives the following.

Lemma 3.12
.

For a finite
\Lambda\subset X
, let
\sigma\colon\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda

be a bijection of sets. Then for any configuration
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
, the configuration
\eta^{\sigma}

given by
\eta^{\sigma}_{x}\coloneqq\eta_{\sigma x}

for
x\in\Lambda
and
\eta^{\sigma}_{x}=\eta_{x}
for
x\not\in\Lambda

is in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
as that of
\eta
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that any bijection
\sigma\colon\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda
is obtained by
successively exchanging two elements of
\Lambda
, and that
\eta^{x,y}
and
\eta

are in the same connected component of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})

for any
x,y\in X
.
∎

We next prove the following.

Lemma 3.13
.

Consider
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
for a base state
*\in S
.
For any
x\in X
, let
f^{*}_{\{x\}}
be the function
with exact support
\{x\}
in the expansion
Eq.

3
. If
f
is invariant via
transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
, then the functions

f^{*}_{\{x\}}\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

are equal as functions on
S
for any
x\in X
.

Proof.

We let
s\in S
. For
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, consider configurations
\eta=(\eta_{x}),\eta^{\prime}=(\eta^{\prime}_{x})\in S^{X}_{*}

such that the component of
\eta
at
x
and
\eta^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}
coincides with
s
,
and all of the other components coincide with the base state.
By
Lemma

3.12
, the configurations
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the same connected component of
S^{X}_{*}\subset S^{X}
, there exists a sequence of transitions from
\eta
to
\eta^{\prime}
.
Since
f
is invariant via transitions, we have

f^{*}_{\{x\}}(s)=f^{*}_{\{x\}}(\eta)=f(\eta)-f(\star)=f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\star)=f^{*}_{\{x^{\prime}\}}(\eta^{\prime})=f^{*}_{\{x^{\prime}\}}(s^{\prime})

as desired. For the first and last equality, we have used the identification of
C(S)

with
C(S^{\{x\}})
and
C(S^{\{x^{\prime}\}})\subset C(S^{X}_{*})
.
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

Assume that
X
is infinite,
and let
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
. If
f
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
,
then for a base state
*\in S
and
f
normalized so that
f(\star)=0
, we have

f=\sum_{x\in X}f^{*}_{\{x\}}

for the expansion of
Proposition

2.3
.

Proof.

Since
f
is uniform, there exists
R>0
such that
f^{*}_{\Lambda}=0
if
{\mathrm{diam}}(\Lambda)>R
.
It is sufficient to prove that for any finite
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
such that
|\Lambda|\geq 2
,
we have
f^{*}_{\Lambda}=0
. We consider
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}
, and assume that
f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=0

for any
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
such that
2\leq|\Lambda^{\prime\prime}|<|\Lambda|
. This condition is trivially
true if
|\Lambda|=2
. Take
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset X
such that
|\Lambda^{\prime}|=|\Lambda|

and
{\mathrm{diam}}(\Lambda^{\prime})>R
. Such
\Lambda^{\prime}
exists since
(X,E)
is locally finite and infinite.
By construction,
f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=0
. Consider any
\eta\in S^{\Lambda}
, and denote again by
\eta

the configuration in
S^{X}_{*}
whose components coincides with that of
\eta

for
x\in\Lambda
and is at base state for
x\not\in\Lambda
.
We fix a bijection
\Lambda\cong\Lambda^{\prime}
,
x\mapsto x^{\prime}
and let
\eta^{\prime}
be
the configuration in
S^{X}_{*}
such that
\eta^{\prime}_{x^{\prime}}\coloneqq\eta_{x}
for
x^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}

and is at base state for
x^{\prime}\not\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. Since
\eta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\eta

by rearranging the components, we see from
Lemma

3.12
that
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are in the
same connected component of
S^{X}_{*}
. Hence there exists a finite sequence of transitions from
\eta
to
\eta^{\prime}
.
Since
f
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
, we have
f(\eta)=f(\eta^{\prime})
. Then

\displaystyle f(\eta)

\displaystyle=\iota^{\Lambda}_{*}f(\eta)=f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)+\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}(\eta)=f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)+\sum_{x\in\Lambda}f^{*}_{\{x\}}(\eta),

\displaystyle f(\eta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\iota^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{*}f(\eta^{\prime})=f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(\eta^{\prime})+\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}(\eta^{\prime})=\sum_{x\in\Lambda^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\{x\}}(\eta^{\prime})

since
f^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=0
.
By
Lemma

3.13
, we have
\sum_{x\in\Lambda}f^{*}_{\{x\}}(\eta)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda^{\prime}}f^{*}_{\{x\}}(\eta^{\prime})
.
This shows that for any
\eta\in S^{\Lambda}
, we have
f^{*}_{\Lambda}(\eta)=0
, hence
f^{*}_{\Lambda}=0
. Induction on the number of elements of
\Lambda
completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.11
.

For
x\in X
, let
\xi\coloneqq f^{*}_{\{x\}}\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
viewed as a function on
S
.
By
Lemma

3.13
, the function
\xi
on
S
is independent of the choice of
x
,
and we have
\xi_{x}=f^{*}_{\{x\}}
as a function on
S^{X}
, and by

Proposition

2.3
and
Lemma

3.14
,
we have
f=\sum_{x\in X}\xi_{x}
. In order to show that
\xi
is a conserved quantity,
consider an edge
e=(x_{1},x_{2})\in E
. Consider any
(s_{1},s_{2}),(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})\in S\times S

such that
((s_{1},s_{2}),(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2}))\in\phi
.
If we let
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}_{*}
to be the configuration such that
(\eta_{x_{1}},\eta_{x_{2}})=(s_{1},s_{2})
,

(\eta^{\prime}_{x_{1}},\eta^{\prime}_{x_{2}})=(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})
, and
\eta_{x},\eta^{\prime}_{x}
is at base state for
x\neq x_{1},x_{2}
.
Then by the construction, we have
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
. This shows that

\xi(s_{1})+\xi(s_{2})=\xi_{x_{1}}(\eta)+\xi_{x_{2}}(\eta)=f(\eta)=f(\eta^{\prime})=\xi_{x_{1}}(\eta^{\prime})+\xi_{x_{2}}(\eta^{\prime})=\xi(s^{\prime}_{1})+\xi(s^{\prime}_{2}).

This proves that
\xi
is a conserved quantity, and

as desired.
∎

4.
The
0
-th Uniform Cohomology of the Configuration Space

In this section, we will interpret our main result
Theorem

3.11
in terms of
the
0
-th cohomology of the configuration space with transition structure
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
We first review the cohomology of general graphs
(see for example
[
BKS20
]
*Appendix A).

Definition 4.1
.

For any symmetric digraph
(X,E)
, we let

\displaystyle C(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathrm{Map}}(X,\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle C^{1}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathrm{Map}}^{\mathrm{alt}}(E,\mathbb{R}),

where
{\mathrm{Map}}^{{\mathrm{alt}}}(E,\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\{\omega\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mid\forall(x,y)\in E\,\,\omega(y,x)=-\omega(x,y)\}
.
Furthermore, we define the differential

(9)

\partial\colon C(X)\rightarrow C^{1}(X),\qquad f\mapsto\partial f

by
\partial f(e)\coloneqq f(te)-f(oe)
for any
e\in E
. We define the
cohomology
of
(X,E)
by

\displaystyle H^{0}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathrm{Ker}\,}\partial,

\displaystyle H^{1}(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq C^{1}(X)/\partial C(X),

and
H^{m}(X)\coloneqq\{0\}

for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\neq 0,1
.

The
0
-th cohomology
H^{0}(X)
is known to be the space of
functions which are constant on the connected components of
(X,E)
(see
[
BKS20
]
*Proposition A.8).
We will apply the above construction to the configuration space with transition structure.
Let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction, and let
(X,E)
be a locally finite connected symmetric digraph.
We let
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
be the configuration space with transition structure of
Definition

3.7

associated to
(S,\phi)
and
(X,E)
. Following
Definition

4.1
, the cohomology of the
configuration space with transition structure is given by

\displaystyle C(S^{X})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathrm{Map}}(S^{X},\mathbb{R}),

\displaystyle C^{1}(S^{X})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathrm{Map}}^{\mathrm{alt}}(\Phi_{E},\mathbb{R}),

where
{\mathrm{Map}}^{{\mathrm{alt}}}(\Phi_{E},\mathbb{R})\coloneqq\{\omega\colon\Phi_{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\mid\forall(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}\,\,\omega(\eta^{\prime},\eta)=-\omega(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\}
.
Furthermore, we define the differential

(10)

\partial\colon C(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X}),\qquad f\mapsto\partial f

by
\partial f(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\coloneqq f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)
for any
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
. We define the
cohomology
of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
by

(11)

\displaystyle H^{0}(S^{X})

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathrm{Ker}\,}\partial,

\displaystyle H^{1}(S^{X})

\displaystyle\coloneqq C^{1}(S^{X})/\partial C(S^{X}),

and
H^{m}(S^{X})\coloneqq\{0\}

for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\neq 0,1
.

When
X
is infinite, the graph
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
generally has an infinite number of connected
components. Since the cohomology
H^{0}(S^{X})

is equivalent to functions on
X
which are constant on the connected components
of
(X,E)
, this implies that
H^{0}(S^{X})
is infinite dimensional.
We will replace the functions
C(S^{X})={\mathrm{Map}}(S^{X},\mathbb{R})
with the space of uniform functions
to construct a suitable definition of uniform cohomology.

First, since the constant function maps to zero, the differential
Eq.

10

induces a differential

\partial\colon C^{0}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X}).

The restriction to local functions gives

(12)

\partial\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X}).

We may linearly extend this differential to uniform functions as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

The differential
\partial\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X})
extends to a differential

\partial\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X})

on the space of uniform functions.

Proof.

For any
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
, by definition, there exists
(x,y)\in E
such that
\eta_{z}=\eta^{\prime}_{z}
for
z\neq x,y
.
Hence
\Delta_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}}=\{x\in X\mid\eta_{x}\neq\eta^{\prime}_{x}\}
is a finite set.
By
Lemma

3.8
, we have a well-defined difference
{\partial}f(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)
.
Hence defines a function
\partial f\colon\Phi_{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
The map
f\mapsto\partial f
gives a differential
linearly extending the differential
Eq.

12
.
∎

Using this differential, we may define the
0
-th uniform cohomology as follows.

Definition 4.3
.

Following
Eq.

11
, we define the
0
-th uniform cohomology of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
by

H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\coloneqq{\mathrm{Ker}\,}\bigl(\partial\colon C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X})\bigr).

As an analogy of the fact that
0
-th cohomology
H^{0}(X)
coincides with the space of functions
constant on the connected components of
(X,E)
(see
[
BKS20
]
*Proposition A.8),
we may prove that the
0
-th uniform cohomology
H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})

coincides with the space of functions constant on the connected
components of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
, i.e.
functions invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Consider a uniform function
f\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
.
Then
f\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
if and only if
f
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
.
Then for any
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
,
since
{\partial}f=0
, we have

{\partial}f(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)=0.

This proves that
f
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Conversely, suppose
f
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Then for any
(\eta,\eta^{\prime})\in\Phi_{E}
, we have
{\partial}f(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=f(\eta^{\prime})-f(\eta)=0
.
This proves that
f\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
as desired.
∎

Our main theorem gives the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction which is exchangeable, and let
(X,E)
be a connected
and locally finite symmetric digraph
such that
X
is infinite.
Then we have a canonical isomorphism
\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)\cong H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
, given by
\xi\mapsto\xi_{X}

for any conserved quantity
\xi\in\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\xi\in\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
. Then by
Lemma

3.10
, the uniform function

\xi_{X}
is invariant via transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
.
Hence by
Lemma

4.4
, we have
\xi_{X}\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
.
This gives a homomorphism
\iota\colon\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
.
Fix a base
*\in S
and take a representative of
\xi
so that
\xi(*)=0
,
and let
x\in X
. Then for any
s\in S
, let
\eta^{s}\in S^{X}
be the configuration
such that
\eta^{s}_{x}\coloneqq s
and
\eta^{s}_{z}\coloneqq*
for
z\neq x
. Then
\xi(s)=\xi_{X}(\eta^{s})
.
Hence if
\xi_{X}=0
in
H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
, then this implies that
\xi(s)=0

for any
s\in S
, hence
\iota
is injective.
Next, suppose
f\in H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
. Then by

Lemma

4.4
, the uniform function
f
is invariant via
transitions of
(S^{X},\Phi_{E})
. Hence by
Theorem

3.11
,
there exists
\xi\in\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)
such that
f=\xi_{X}
.
This proves that
\iota
is surjective, hence that we have an
isomorphism
\operatorname{Consv}^{\phi}(S)\cong H^{0}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
as desired.
∎

Entropy, Derivation Operators and Huffman Trees
Simon Burton

Abstract
We build a theory of binary trees on finite multisets that categorifies, or operationalizes, the entropy of a finite probability distribution. Multisets operationalize probabilities as the event outcomes of an experiment. Huffman trees operationalize the entropy of the distribution of these events. We show how the derivation property of the entropy of a joint distribution lifts to Huffman trees.



1
Entropy is…

We define an
un-normalized
probability distribution
x

of length
n
to be a sequence of non-negative real values

x=(x_{1},...,x_{n}).

The
norm
of an un-normalized probability distribution
x
is
the sum of the components:

|x|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}.

We look for a continuous real valued function
w
on a variable number of arguments
such that

\displaystyle w(x_{11},\ldots,x_{1k_{1}},\ldots,x_{n1},\ldots,x_{nk_{n}})=w\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}}x_{1j},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{k_{n}}x_{nj}\biggr)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w(x_{i1},\ldots,x_{ik_{i}})

(1)

for real values
c,x_{i}\geq 0,
and

\displaystyle w(cx_{1},\ldots,cx_{n})=cw(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

(2)

for real values
c,x_{i}\geq 0.

Up to scale this has unique solution

w(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\log_{q}x_{i}-\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\biggr)\log_{q}\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\biggr),

with the convention that
a\log a
is zero when
a
is zero.
The scale corresponds to choice of logarithm base
q.

We consider this sequence of non-negative reals
(x_{1},...,x_{n})
to
be an
un-normalized probability distribution,

and the function
w(\cdot)
is the
un-normalized entropy.

When we have
\sum_{i}x_{i}=1
then

-w(x_{1},...,x_{n})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\log x_{i}=h(x_{1},...,x_{n})

where
h
is the usual entropy function of the probability
distribution
(x_{1},...,x_{n}).

Given two un-normalized probability distributions

x=(x_{1},...,x_{m})
and
y=(y_{1},...,y_{n})

we define the product as the length
mn
un-normalized probability
distribution:

xy:=(x_{1}y_{1},...,x_{1}y_{n},x_{2}y_{1},...,x_{m}y_{n}).

Then we have that
w(\cdot)
is a
derivation:

\displaystyle w(xy)=|x|w(y)+w(x)|y|.

(3)

This follows from the fact that the function

f(a)=a\log a,
satisfies
f(ab)=f(a)b+af(b).

Our aim below will be to categorify, or operationalize,
the equations (1), (2) and (3).

2
Multisets categorify probabilities

The first step is to replace real numbers by the natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
,
following the work of
[
2
]
.
Given a finite set
A,

a
multiset
over
A
will be the set of
\mathbb{N}
-linear
formal combinations of elements of
A
.
We denote the set of multisets over
A
as
\mathbb{N}[A]
.
We think of
A
as a space of events and a
multiset
X\in\mathbb{N}[A]
as
a record of how many times we have observed each event.

We either write multisets using set notation
X=\{a,b,2c\}

or as a formal sum
X=a+b+2c.

The
zero multiset
,
0\in\mathbb{N}[A]
has all zero coefficients,

0=\sum_{a\in A}0\cdot a.

The
norm
of
a multiset
X\in\mathbb{N}[A]
is denoted
|X|\in\mathbb{N}.

For
X=\sum_{a\in A}n_{a}\cdot a
we have
|X|:=\sum_{a\in A}n_{a}.

This is the total number of observations.

Elements of
\mathbb{N}
are called
scalars.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we write
kX
for the left multiplication of
k
on
X.

This is given by
kX:=\sum_{a\in A}(kn_{a})\cdot a.

We also call
kX
a
scalar multiple
of
X.

If we are also given
Y\in\mathbb{N}[B]
as
Y=\sum_{b\in B}m_{b}\cdot b,

the sum
X+Y\in\mathbb{N}[A\cup B]
is

X+Y:=\sum_{a\in A\cup B}(n_{a}+m_{b})\cdot a.

The product
X\times Y\in\mathbb{N}[A\times B]
is

X\times Y:=\sum_{(a,b)\in A\times B}(n_{a}m_{b})\cdot ab.

where we understand
ab
to be shorthand notation for the pair
(a,b).

These are the usual rules of polynomial addition and multiplication.
A
monomial
is a multiset with just one non-zero component,
such as
x\cdot a\in\mathbb{N}[A]
with
x\in\mathbb{N},a\in A.

The intersection
X\cap Y\in\mathbb{N}[A\cap B]
is

X\cap Y:=\sum_{a\in A\cap B}(n_{a}m_{a})\cdot a.

Two multisets
X
and
Y
are
disjoint
when
X\cap Y=0.

We extend the definition of the un-normalized entropy function
w(\cdot)
to multisets.
Given
X=\sum_{a\in A}n_{a}\cdot a
, we define

w(X):=w(\{n_{a}\}).

This is well defined because the function
w(\cdot)
is commutative in
its arguments.

3
Trees over multisets

The next definition formalizes a notion of binary trees with
nodes decorated by multisets.
Here we stick to binary trees, even though this theory
generalizes to a theory of
q
-ary trees with
q=2,3,4,...

Definition 1.

A
tree over a multiset
is defined recursively as

(1) For any multiset
X
,
\mathrm{Tree}(X)
is a tree over
X
.

(2) If
X
and
Y
are disjoint multisets,
and
\Delta_{X}
is a tree over
X
and
and
\Delta_{Y}
is a tree over
Y
, then

\mathrm{Tree}(X+Y,\Delta_{X},\Delta_{Y})
is a tree over
X+Y.

Notation such as
\Delta_{X}
will denote a
generic tree over the multiset
X
.
We consider
\mathrm{Tree}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
to be commutative in the last two arguments, so that

\mathrm{Tree}(X+Y,\Delta_{X},\Delta_{Y})
and

\mathrm{Tree}(X+Y,\Delta_{Y},\Delta_{X})
denote the same tree.

Informally,
a tree corresponds to a way of parenthesizing the expression for
X
.
For example, given
X=a+b+2c\in\mathbb{N}[\{a,b,c\}]
the tree

\mathrm{Tree}(a+b+2c,\mathrm{Tree}(a+b,\mathrm{Tree}(a),\mathrm{Tree}(b)),\mathrm{Tree}(2c))

corresponds to the parenthesized expression
(((a)+(b))+(2c)).

Definition 2.

Given a scalar

and multisets
X,Y

we have that
k
acts on trees recursively as

(1)
k\cdot\mathrm{Tree}(X):=\mathrm{Tree}(k\cdot X)
.

(2)
k\cdot\mathrm{Tree}(X+Y,\Delta_{X},\Delta_{Y}):=\mathrm{Tree}(k\cdot(X+Y),k\cdot\Delta_{X},k\cdot\Delta_{Y}).

Definition 3.

A multiset
X
acts on trees recursively as

(1)
X\cdot\mathrm{Tree}(Y):=\mathrm{Tree}(X\times Y)
.

(2)
X\cdot\mathrm{Tree}(Y+Z,\Delta_{Y},\Delta_{Z}):=\mathrm{Tree}(X\times(Y+Z),X\cdot\Delta_{Y},X\cdot\Delta_{Z}).

We think of this action as
X

For

(1)

(2) If

\mathrm{Tree}(Y+Z,\Delta_{Y},\Delta_{Z}).

When

\Delta_{Y}

\mathrm{Leaf}(X,\Delta_{Y}).

From Definition 1, we see that if a multiset

\Delta_{X}.

In this case, we define the

\Delta_{X}+\Delta_{Y}

by attaching
\Delta_{Y}

Instead of formalizing this with another recursive definition,

The
product
of two trees
\Delta_{X}
and
\Delta_{Y}
is
a sum of trees, which we write as

\Delta_{X}\times\Delta_{Y}=X\Delta_{Y}+\Delta_{X}Y.

This is not quite a formal definition of the product, but

We could also try to formalize

\Delta_{X}Y

these trees, but this only works up to some judicious applications of associativity.
We write this formula for the product because it suggests

Definition 5.

For
X
a leaf of a tree the
\mathbb{N}

X

defined recursively as

(1)
X

\mathrm{Tree}(X).

(2) If
X

then

X
has depth

\mathrm{Tree}(Y+Z,\Delta_{Y},\Delta_{Z}).

We also write this function as
\mathrm{Depth}(X,\Delta_{Y}),

for
X
a leaf of
\Delta_{Y}.

Definition 6.

The
weight
of a tree
\Delta_{X}

is written
W(\Delta_{X})\in\mathbb{N}
.
This is the depth weighted sum of the norm of the leaves of
\Delta_{X}:

W(\Delta_{X}):=\sum_{\mathrm{Leaf}(Y,\Delta_{X})}\mathrm{Depth}(Y,\Delta_{X})|Y|.

First we show the weight function respects thickenings.

Theorem 7.

For
k\in\mathbb{N},
tree
\Delta_{X}
and multiset
Y
,

\displaystyle W(k\cdot\Delta_{X})

\displaystyle=kW(\Delta_{X})

\displaystyle W(Y\cdot\Delta_{X})

\displaystyle=|Y|W(\Delta_{X})

Proof:
The left multiplication in Definition 2 & 3 distributes over the leaves.

The next theorem says that
W(\cdot)
is a derivation on trees.

Theorem 8.

For trees
\Delta_{X}
and
\Delta_{Y}
we have

W(\Delta_{X}\times\Delta_{Y})=|X|W(\Delta_{Y})+W(\Delta_{X})|Y|.

Proof:
By inspection of the construction of
\Delta_{X}\times\Delta_{Y}.

Using this theorem we can write the cute formula:

W(\Delta_{X}^{n})=n|X|^{n-1}W(\Delta_{X}).

An important class of trees has only monomial multisets on leaves.
These trees correspond to fully parenthesized expressions for a multiset.

Definition 9.

A
monomial tree over a multiset
is defined recursively as

(1) For any monomial
X
,
\mathrm{Tree}(X)
is a monomial tree over
X
.

(2) If
X
and
Y
are disjoint multisets,
and
\Delta_{X}
is a monomial tree over
X
and
and
\Delta_{Y}
is a monomial tree over
Y
, then

\mathrm{Tree}(X+Y,\Delta_{X},\Delta_{Y})
is a monomial tree over
X+Y.

Lemma 10.

The product of two monomial trees is monomial.

Proof:
By inspection of the product construction.

Theorem 11.

For any monomial tree
\Delta_{X}
we have

w(X)\leq W(\Delta_{X}).

Proof:
This is well known, see
[
3
,
5
]

The next question is, when does equality obtain in this theorem?

4
Huffman trees

Given a multiset
X
,
The idea with Huffman coding is to
sequentially build a tree over
X
from the bottom-up in an agglomerative way.
We start with a collection of trees, one for each monomial in
X
,
and then sequentially join pairs of trees, minimizing the norm
of the joined multisets as the sequence proceeds
[
3
]
.

It turns out this algorithm is optimal, as opposed to
the top-down algorithm,
which is divisive: start with a single tree
\mathrm{Tree}(X)
and sequentially
perform optimal splitting operations, such as
\mathrm{Tree}(X)\mapsto\mathrm{Tree}(X,\mathrm{Tree}(Y),\mathrm{Tree}(Z)).

Definition 12.

Given a multiset
X
a
Huffman tree over
X

is any tree over
X
that can be constructed as follows.
Writing
X=\sum n_{a}\cdot a\in\mathbb{N}[A]

we proceed inductively:

(Step 1) Define a finite set of monomial trees

\Omega_{1}:=\{\mathrm{Tree}(n_{a}\cdot a)\}_{a\in A}.

(Step
n+1
) Given the finite set of trees

\Omega_{n}=\{\Delta_{X_{1}},...,\Delta_{X_{m}}\}

we define
\Omega_{n+1}
as follows.
Choose any
1\leq i<j\leq m
such that

|X_{i}+X_{j}|\leq|X_{i^{\prime}}+X_{j^{\prime}}|\ \ \forall 1\leq i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq m.

Then

\Omega_{n+1}:=\{\Delta_{X_{1}},...\widehat{\Delta_{X_{i}}},...,\widehat{\Delta_{X_{j}}},...\Delta_{X_{m}},\mathrm{Tree}(X_{i}+X_{j},\Delta_{X_{i}},\Delta_{X_{j}})\}.

(Final step.)
After a finite number of steps,
\Omega_{n}
will contain
a single tree over
X
. This tree is the constructed Huffman tree.

It follows that any Huffman tree is a monomial tree.

We write
\Gamma_{X}
for a generic Huffman tree over
X
.
The next theorem says that Huffman trees are
characterized as those trees that minimize
W(\cdot).

Theorem 13.

For any monomial tree
\Delta_{X}
we have

W(\Gamma_{X})\leq W(\Delta_{X}).

Conversely, if
\Delta_{X}^{\prime}
is a monomial tree such that

W(\Delta_{X}^{\prime})\leq W(\Delta_{X})

for all monomial
\Delta_{X}
then
\Delta_{X}^{\prime}
is a Huffman tree.

Proof:
This is well known, see
[
3
,
5
]

Corollary 14.

If
\Gamma_{X}
and
\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}
are two Huffman trees for the same
multiset
X
, then

W(\Gamma_{X})=W(\Gamma_{X}^{\prime}).

Because of this corollary, we may as well define
W(X):=W(\Gamma_{X})
where

\Gamma_{X}
is any Huffman tree for
X
.

Definition 15.

A multiset
X\in\mathbb{N}[A]
is
dyadic
when

X=\sum_{a\in A}2^{k_{a}}\cdot a\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \sum_{a\in A}2^{k_{a}}=2^{k},

with
k_{a}\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}.

The next theorem says that decategorifying
Huffman trees gives the un-normalized entropy for
scalar multiples of dyadic multisets.

Theorem 16.

X
is a scalar multiple of a dyadic multiset iff

w(X)=W(X).

Proof:
See
[
5
]
section 5.3.

For

X

\Gamma_{kX}:=k\Gamma_{X}

For disjoint dyadic multisets

W(\Gamma_{X+Y})=W(\mathrm{Tree}(X+Y,\Gamma_{X},\mathrm{Tree}(Y)))+W(\Gamma_{Y}).

The next theorem categorifies Eq. (3).
It states that Huffman trees are a derivation over multisets.

Theorem 19.

If the multisets
X
and
Y

the product of Huffman trees

\Gamma_{X\times Y}:=\Gamma_{X}\times\Gamma_{Y}=X\Gamma_{Y}+\Gamma_{X}Y.

Final thoughts

The importance of Huffman coding is highlighted by the
following limit, which holds for any multiset
X:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{W(X^{n})}{n|X|^{n-1}}=w(X).

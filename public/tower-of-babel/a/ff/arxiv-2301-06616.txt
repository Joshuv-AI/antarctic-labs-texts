Stability of the de-Sitter spacetime. The anisotropic case.
Przemysław Bieniek, Jan Chojnacki, Jan H. Kwapisz, Krzysztof A. Meissner

Abstract
We elaborate on the cosmological implications of the recently established non-perturbative O ⁡ ( D , D ) O(D,D) -symmetric approach. Following the α ′ \alpha^{\prime} -complete Friedmann equations, we provide a qualitative description of the dynamics of anisotropy perturbations. We find that the spacetime stability depends on the sign of the generalized dilaton time derivative. We consider isotropic limits and find possible mechanism for pre-big bang cosmology, goverend by the value of cosmological constant.

Introduction

The discovery of the positive cosmological constant has put the existence of de-Sitter solutions and their stability as one of the central research questions within the string theory community. The so-called de-Sitter conjecture states that the de-Sitter vacua are unstable within string theory
Obied et al. 2018
;
Garg and Krishnan 2019
;
Ooguri et al. 2019
.

On the other hand a suitable
O(D,D)
formalism is at hand to study the de-Sitter solutions. As found by K.A. Meissner and G. Veneziano
Meissner and Veneziano 1991a
;
Meissner and Veneziano 1991b
, in
D+1
dimensions the low-energy stringy effective action once reduced to time-dependent backgrounds possess continuous
O(D,D)
symmetry, see also
Sen 1991
;
Maharana and Schwarz 1993
. The symmetry emerges in the massless sector of (super) string theories: scalar dilaton
\phi(x)
, the spin-2 graviton
G_{\mu\nu}
and anticommuting Kalb-Ramond 2-form field
B_{\mu\nu}
. The low-energy action takes the form

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{1+D}x\,\sqrt{-G}e^{-2\phi}(R+4G^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{12}H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho}),

(1)

where
D
is the number of spatial dimensions,
R
is the Ricci scalar curvature of the metric
G_{\mu\nu}
and
H_{\mu\nu\rho}
is a field strength of
B_{\mu\nu}
, defined as
H_{\mu\nu\rho}:=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu\rho}+\partial_{\nu}B_{\rho\mu}+\partial_{\rho}B_{\mu\nu}
.
For the time-dependent backgrounds
G_{\mu\nu}=:g_{\mu\nu}(t)
,
B_{\mu\nu}=:b_{\mu\nu}(t)
,
\phi=:\phi(t)
the effective action (
I
) may be expressed in the manifestly
O(D,D)
invariant form:

\displaystyle I\varpropto\int dt\,e^{-\Phi}\left[-\dot{\Phi}^{2}-\frac{1}{8}\textrm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{\dot{S}}^{2}\right)\right],

(2)

We call
\Phi=2\phi-\ln\sqrt{\det g}
the generalized (shifted) dilaton field and
g
is the spatial metric tensor. Following the formalism of
Meissner and Veneziano 1991a
,
2d
-dimensional matrix
\mathcal{S}
is defined:

\mathcal{S}=\begin{pmatrix}bg^{-1}&g-bg^{-1}b\\
g^{-1}&-g^{-1}b\end{pmatrix}.\\

(3)

The global
O(D,D)
group acts as

\displaystyle\Phi\xrightarrow{}\Phi,\quad\mathcal{S}\xrightarrow{}\Omega^{T}\mathcal{S}\Omega.

(4)

As shown by
Meissner 1997
also for the
\alpha^{\prime}
corrected actions the fields can be transformed in such a way that
O(D,D)
symmetry is manifest. Using this reasoning, in
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019a
, the full, non-perturbative form of the
\alpha^{\prime}
corrected effective action on cosmological backgrounds has been derived

\displaystyle I=\int\,dt\,e^{-\Phi}\left[-\dot{\Phi}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{\prime k-1}\left(c_{k}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k}\right)\right.\right.

(5)

\displaystyle+\textrm{multitrace}\Bigl)\Biggr].

(6)

A generic multitrace term at order
\alpha^{\prime k-1}
is of the following form:

\prod_{i}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2n_{i}}\right)

(7)

with
\sum_{i}n_{i}=k
and
n_{i}>1
. Note that also a cosmological constant term may be added in the
O(D,D)
manner,
Bernardo et al. 2020
. The higher order potential terms in
\Phi
will be
O(D,D)
symmetric as well, but will cause the instability in the equations
Bieniek et al. 2022
.

This general form of the string theory effective action and the associated equations revealed a new possibility of a non-perturbative de-Sitter solution once summing all the possible
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections in the
O(D,D)
formalism
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019a
, see also other developments here
Bernardo et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019b
;
Bernardo and Franzmann 2020
;
Núñez and Rost 2021
;
Basile and Platania 2021a
;
Basile and Platania 2021b
;
Basile and Platania 2021c
. Finally, in our previous article, we studied the isotropic case for constant shifted dilaton
\dot{\Phi}=0

Bieniek et al. 2022
as well as in the presence of cosmological constant.

In this work, we extend our study towards the anisotropic case and the stability of the de-Sitter solution across different spatial dimensions. As it will turn out, the
O(D,D)
framework will not be suitable to study the anisotropies, thus here we have adopted the perturbative approach including the multitrace effects. We also comment on possible Pre-Bing Bang scenarios.

II
Anisotropic perturbations

The anisotropic solutions for the full
\alpha^{\prime}

O(d,d)
dynamics have been discussed in

Bernardo et al. 2021
;
Núñez and Rost 2021
. Yet in both of the articles only a special class of the anisotropies have been covered, namely for the
d-n
static dimensions,
i.e.

H_{i}=0
for
i\in(n,d)
. As we will shortly discuss, the full unperturbative, dynamical investigation of anisotropic perturbations within Hohm-Zwiebach setup is beyond the feasible scope of the theory. We are however able to recognise stability features present in the isotropic non-perturbative case.

To investigate the stability of the
d
dimensional de-Sitter solution, let us now consider the anisotropic case:

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{diag}\left(a_{1}^{2}(t),a_{2}^{2}(t),\ldots,a_{d}^{2}(t)\right)=\mathbf{a}^{2},

\displaystyle b_{ij}

\displaystyle=0.

(8)

In that case, the matrix
\mathcal{S}
equals:

\mathcal{S}=\begin{pmatrix}0&g\\
g^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf{a}^{2}\\
\mathbf{a}^{-2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

And its derivative is:

\dot{\mathcal{S}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\mathbf{a}\dot{\mathbf{a}}\\
-2\mathbf{a}^{-3}\dot{\mathbf{a}}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(10)

Then it can be seen that the square of the derivative takes a diagonal form:

\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2}=-4\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{H}^{2}&0\\
0&\mathbf{H}^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(11)

where we define
\mathbf{H}=\dot{\mathbf{a}}\mathbf{a}^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{d})
.

In
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019a
, a simpler case of
a_{1}=a_{2}=\ldots=a_{d}
is considered. It is then shown that in the low-energy effective action the multitrace terms can be ignored, because they contribute exclusively to the value of the constants
c_{k}
. It is because then (as one can see by setting all
H_{i}
to be equal in (
11
)):

\mathrm{Tr}(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2l})=(-1)^{l}2^{2l+1}dH^{2l}.

(12)

Since the multitrace terms at order
\alpha^{\prime k-1}
consist of the traces of powers of
\dot{\mathcal{S}}
that sum to
2k
, they will all have the form
CH^{2k}
, where
C
is constant. Therefore, they will all sum to a term proportional to
H^{2k}
and can be included by simply changing the constants
c_{k}
.

However, the anisotropic case is different. A generic trace appearing in the action is:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2l})=(-4)^{l}\mathrm{Tr}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{H}^{2l}&0\\
0&\mathbf{H}^{2l}\end{pmatrix}=(-4)^{l}2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{i}^{2l}.

(13)

Plugging this into (
7
), it is easily seen that the multitraces will introduce terms proportional to

\displaystyle\prod_{i}H^{2n_{i}}_{j_{i}}

(14)

where some of the
j_{i}
are not equal, into the action. The consequence of this is that the equations become hard to derive, and they would be a lot more complicated than in the isotropic case.

However, in
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019a
, it has been shown that the first multitrace terms will appear at the third order in
\alpha^{\prime}
. Therefore we will disregard the multitrace terms if we truncate the expressions to a sufficiently small order. Alternatively, the issue of existence of de-Sitter vacua can be investigated by assuming, that the matrices
g
,
\dot{g}
, and
\dot{b}
commute (the so called commuting matrices ansatz
Núñez and Rost 2021
). That approach allows for some non-perturbative analysis, but results in very complicated equations which are not suitable for stability analysis. In our study, we take one another perspective: the perturbative approach quantifying multitrace effects affecting the stability of a general anisotropic solution.

The equations derived in
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019a
by a direct variation of the action (without the multitrace terms) with the added contribution from the cosmological constant are:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{\prime k-1}kc_{k}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k-1}-\dot{\Phi}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k-1}+\mathcal{S}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\ddot{\Phi}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{\prime k-1}kc_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k})

\displaystyle=0,

(16)

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{\prime k-1}(2k-1)c_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k})-\Lambda

\displaystyle=0.

(17)

They follow from variation with respect to
\mathcal{S}
,
\Phi
, and the lapse function
n(t)
, which has been set to 1.
In the considered case, the equation (
17
) transforms into:

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left((-\alpha^{\prime})^{k-1}2^{2k+1}(2k-1)c_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d}H_{i}^{2k}\right)-\Lambda

\displaystyle=0,

(18)

the following relation between
\dot{\Phi}^{2}
and
\ddot{\Phi}
holds:

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left((-\alpha^{\prime})^{k-1}2^{k+1}(k-1)c_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d}H_{i}^{2k}\right)-\Lambda

\displaystyle=\ddot{\Phi},

(19)

and (
15
) gives
d
equations of the form:

\left[\frac{d}{dt}-\dot{\Phi}\right]\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-\alpha^{\prime})^{k-1}2^{2k-2}kc_{k}H_{i}^{2k-1}=0.

(20)

II.1
The perturbative approach

Now, we proceed to the calculation of the terms introduced to the equations by the first multitrace term. The part of the action that includes it is:

I^{(m)}=\int\,dte^{-\Phi}\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\right)^{2}.

(21)

In order to vary it with respect to
\mathcal{S}
, we use an approach laid out in
Hohm and Zwiebach 2019a
. First, we perform a variation as if
\mathcal{S}
was an unconstrained variable:

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{S}}I_{m}

\displaystyle=\int\,dte^{-\Phi}8\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{d\delta\mathcal{S}}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=8\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\int\,dte^{-\Phi}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\left(\delta\mathcal{S}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\right)-\delta\mathcal{S}\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\right)

\displaystyle=8\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\int\,dte^{-\Phi}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\delta\mathcal{S}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\dot{\Phi}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}-\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\right)\right).

(22)

The expression multiplying
\delta\mathcal{S}
defines
F_{\mathcal{S}}^{(m)}
. The contribution to equation (
15
) is given by

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{S}}^{(m)}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{\mathcal{S}m}-\mathcal{S}F_{\mathcal{S}m}\mathcal{S}).

(23)

Using
\mathcal{S}^{2}=1
,
\mathcal{S}\dot{\mathcal{S}}=-\dot{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k-1}\mathcal{S}=-\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k-1}-2\mathcal{S}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{2k}
, it can be brought to the following form:

\displaystyle E_{\mathcal{S}}^{(m)}=-8\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\frac{d}{dt}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}+\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\mathcal{S}\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\dot{\Phi}-\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\right)\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{3}\right].

(24)

Using (
13
), this can be shown to give the following corrections to equations (
20
):

\displaystyle E_{H_{i}}^{(m)}=2^{11}\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\left[3\left(\sum_{j}H_{j}^{4}\right)\dot{H_{i}}H_{i}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\left(\sum_{j}H_{j}^{4}\right)\dot{\Phi}H_{i}^{3}+4\left(\sum_{j}\dot{H_{j}}H_{j}^{3}\right)H_{i}^{3}\right].

(25)

Additionally, the equations (
16
) and (
17
) will receive contributions from the multitrace term. For our purposes, only the change to the latter needs to be calculated. After restoring
g_{00}=-n(t)
, the multitrace term will be multiplied by
\frac{1}{n^{7}}
. Therefore, its contribution to the equation will be:

\displaystyle E_{n}^{(m)}=-7\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\dot{\mathcal{S}}^{4}\right)\right)^{2}=-7\cdot 2^{10}\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,1}\left(\sum_{j}H_{j}^{4}\right)^{2}.

(26)

Finally, the equations to the third order take the form:

\displaystyle-\left[\frac{d}{dt}-\dot{\Phi}\right](4c_{1}H_{i}-32\alpha^{\prime}c_{2}H_{i}^{3}

\displaystyle+192\alpha^{\prime 2}c_{3}H_{i}^{5}-2^{10}\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,0}H_{i}^{7})+E_{H_{i}}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=0,

(27)

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}^{2}+8c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}^{2}-96\alpha^{\prime}c_{2}\sum_{i=j}^{d}H_{j}^{4}

\displaystyle+5\cdot 2^{7}\alpha^{\prime 2}c_{3}\sum_{i=j}^{d}H_{j}^{6}-7\cdot 2^{9}\alpha^{\prime 3}c_{4,0}\sum_{i=j}^{d}H_{j}^{8}+\Lambda+E_{n}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=0.

(28)

Using equation (
28
),
\dot{\Phi}
can be expressed in terms of
H_{i}
:

\dot{\Phi}=\mathrm{sgn}(\dot{\Phi})\sqrt{-8c_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}^{2}+96c_{2}\alpha^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}^{4}-5\cdot 2^{7}c_{3}\alpha^{\prime 2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{j}^{6}+7\cdot 2^{9}c_{4,0}\alpha^{\prime 3}\sum_{i=j}^{d}H_{j}^{8}-E_{n}^{(m)}+\Lambda}.

(29)

By plugging (
29
) into (
27
), we get
d
differential equations for the
H_{i}
’s, where the only appearance of
\Phi
is the sign of its derivative. They are linear equations for
\dot{H_{i}}^{\prime}s
and can be used to express them in terms of
H_{i}
and
\mathrm{sgn}(\dot{\Phi})
.
Time evolution of
\dot{\Phi}
is entirely determined by the evolution of anisotropic Hubble parameter. We note that the sign of the generalised dilaton time derivative, is governed by the equation of motion containing the second time derivative, following from (
16
). Moreover, we should consider such constants
c_{n}
, so that the expression under the squere root is positively defined or find a dynamical mechanism restricting the flow into this area. We discuss such mechanism in section
IV
.

Exact solution to zero-order anisotropic equations

If we disregard the
\alpha^{\prime}
terms in the system of equations (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
) for the case considered in section
II
with
\Lambda=0
, they can be solved analytically. We consider the cases
\Lambda=0

\Lambda\neq 0

III.1
Zero potential

In this case, (
16
) and (
17
) imply the following relation:

\dot{\Phi}^{2}=\ddot{\Phi}.

(30)

The general solution for
\Phi
is given by:

\displaystyle\Phi(t)

\displaystyle=-C_{1}\mathrm{log}(t-t_{0})+\Phi_{0},

(31)

with
C_{1}\in\{0,1\}
. For
C_{1}=0
the Eq.(
15
) implies that
H_{i}
are also constant. For
C_{1}=1
, after plugging (
31
) into (
15
), one gets:

\dot{H_{i}}+\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}H_{i}=0.

(32)

They can be solved to give:

H_{i}(t)=\frac{\beta_{i}}{t-t_{0}},

(33)

where
\beta_{i}
are constants. By plugging (
33
) and (
31
) into (
17
), a constraint on
\beta_{i}
’s is:

\sum_{j}\beta_{j}^{2}=1.

(34)

Now, since
H_{i}=\frac{\dot{a}_{i}}{a_{i}}
, we solve for
a_{i}
and obtain:

a_{i}(t)=D_{i}(t-t_{0})^{\beta_{i}}.

(35)

And since
\phi=\frac{1}{2}(\Phi+\mathrm{log}(\sqrt{\mathrm{det}g}))
and
\sqrt{\mathrm{det}g}=\prod_{j}a_{j}
, we have:

\phi(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{j}\beta_{j}-1\right)\mathrm{log}(t-t_{0})+\phi_{0}.

(36)

Interestingly, for
\sum_{j}\beta_{j}=1
the dilaton remains constant for time dependent
g
. The dilaton and Ricci scalar curvature are singular for
t\xrightarrow{}t_{0}
. This means that the action is infinite at this limit and in the spirit of Finite Action Principle
J.D.Barrow and Tipler 1988
;
Barrow 2020
;
Lehners and Stelle 2019
;
Borissova and Eichhorn 2020
;
Jonas et al. 2021
;
Chojnacki and Kwapisz 2021
such solution will be dynamically excluded, as the exponential weight in the path integral will vanish.
There is however, an exception to this. Notice, when
\sum\beta_{i}=1
the dilaton and curvature remain finite. Assuming all
|\beta_{i}|
are equal, the only possible choice of
\beta_{i}
is
\{-\frac{1}{3},-\frac{1}{3},-\frac{1}{3},+\frac{1}{3},\dots,\frac{1}{3},\}
corresponding to six contracting dimensions and three expanding. Originally, this result was discovered by Meissner and Veneziano in
Meissner and Veneziano 1991b
. At higher orders of
\alpha^{\prime}
, the exact solution is much harder to obtain, since terms without
\Phi
in (
16
) and (
17
) differ and (
31
) doesn’t hold.

III.2
Cosmological constant potential

Now, (
16
) together with (
17
) give us:

\dot{\Phi}^{2}=\ddot{\Phi}+\Lambda.

(37)

This equation has two solutions:

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{log}\left[\mathrm{cosh}\left(\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(t_{0}-t\right)\right)\right]+\Phi_{0};

(38)

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{log}\left[\mathrm{sinh}\left(\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(t_{0}-t\right)\right)\right]+\Phi_{0};

(39)

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\pm\sqrt{\Lambda}t+\Phi_{0}.

(40)

Then, similarly as before, we can obtain solutions for
a_{i}
:

\displaystyle a_{1i}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left[2\gamma_{i}\mathrm{arctan}\left(\mathrm{tanh}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(t_{0}-t\right)}{2}\right)\right)\right];

(41)

\displaystyle a_{2i}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tanh}^{\alpha_{i}}\left[\frac{\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(t_{0}-t\right)}{2}\right];

(42)

\displaystyle a_{3i}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{exp}\left[\pm\delta_{i}\mathrm{exp}\left[\pm\sqrt{\Lambda}\left(t-t_{0}\right)\right]\right],

(43)

where
a_{ni}
are solutions of equations with
\Phi_{n}
. Incidentally, (
42
) was obtained by a different method in
Meissner and Veneziano 1991c
. Then, (
17
) gives us the following conditions for the coefficients:

\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}^{2}=-1;\\
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}^{2}=1;\\
\sum_{i}\delta_{i}^{2}=0.

(44)

For solutions (
41
) and (
43
), these conditions lead to a complex metric, and thus are not physical.

III.3
Pre-Big Bang comments

We briefly note a possibility of Pre-Big Bang solutions
Gasperini and Veneziano 1992

Gasperini and Veneziano 1993

The answer to this question possibly lays already at the zeroth-order. Equations (

) at
H=0
,
h=0

\dot{\Phi}
so

. Consider a case, where the evolution starts at a point on

Similar thing may happen on the red line for the perturbative approach, avoiding the forbidden region effectively. We have verified it is possible using the corrected equivalent of (

V
Conclusions

Our investigation reveals that the de-Sitter solution (an isotropic one with constant
H
greater than 0), that to the third order in
\alpha^{\prime}
, no stable solution of this kind is possible, because either the only stable point is
H=0
(for
\dot{\Phi}<0
), or there are no stable points. This confirms the previous studies done at
\alpha^{\prime}{}^{0}
.

However, no conclusions can be drawn about the stability of non-perturbative de-Sitter vacua, since higher-order terms can change the dynamics of the system. However, our analysis entails that
for small values of
H
the phase diagrams at
\alpha^{\prime}{}^{0}
and
\alpha^{\prime}{}^{3}
do not differ significantly supporting the claim for the lack of de-Sitter solutions at the observed value of cosmological constant
Obied et al. 2018
;
Garg and Krishnan 2019
;
Ooguri et al. 2019
.
Yet, for the non-perturbative, isotropic study see our previous work
Bieniek et al. 2022
.

Furthermore, we have discussed a possible Pre-Big Bang solution in a universe with negative cosmological constant.

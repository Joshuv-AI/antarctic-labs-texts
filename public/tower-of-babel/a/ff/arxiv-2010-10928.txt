Abstract
High-performance p-type oxide thin film transistors (TFTs) have great potential for many semiconductor applications. However, these devices typically suffer from low hole mobility and high off-state currents. We fabricated p-type TFTs with a phase-pure polycrystalline Cu 2 ​ O \text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} semiconductor channel grown by atomic layer deposition (ALD). The TFT switching characteristics were improved by applying a thin ALD Al 2 ​ O 3 \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} passivation layer on the Cu 2 ​ O \text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} channel, followed by vacuum annealing at 300 ∘ C. Detailed characterisation by TEM-EDX and XPS shows that the surface of Cu 2 ​ O \text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O} is reduced following Al 2 ​ O 3 \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} deposition and indicates the formation of 1–2 nm thick CuAlO 2 \text{CuAlO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}} interfacial layer. This, together with field-effect passivation caused by the high negative fixed charge of the ALD Al 2 ​ O 3 \text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}} , leads to an improvement in the TFT performance by reducing the density of deep trap states as well as by reducing the accumulation of electrons in the semiconducting layer in the device off-state.

Introduction

Metal-oxide thin film transistors (TFTs) have attracted increasing interest especially in display technologies owing to their optical transparency and high mobility, low processing temperatures and material costs, and mechanical flexibility
Hosono 2012
. This has led to the development of high-performance n-type semiconducting oxide materials, such as amorphous indium-gallium-zinc-oxide (IGZO) with electron mobility of several tens of cm
2
V
-1
s
-1

Sheng et al. 2019
. However, the full utilisation of oxides in p-n junction based electronics and complementary metal oxide semiconductor (CMOS) integrated circuits, is still hindered by the lack of high performance p-type oxides. The reason for the challenges in achieving feasible hole conductivity are the differences in the electronic structures of the n- and p-type oxides
Wang et al. 2016
. The transport path of holes in p-type oxides, valence band maximum (VBM) consists typically of localised anisotropic oxygen 2p orbitals, which results in large hole effective mass and low mobility. In addition, the concentration of holes in oxides is often limited by the high formation energy of the cation vacancies, as well as the annihilation of holes due to the low formation energy of the oxygen vacancies
Wang et al. 2016
. In case of cuprous oxide
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
, however, the valence band is formed by the hybridisation of the O 2p and Cu 3d orbitals, resulting in a less localized VBM and pathway for hole transportation for holes formed via copper vacancies (V
Cu
) as acceptor states
Nolan and Elliott 2006
. Such special configuration and high hole mobility have made
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
an extensively studied p-type oxide for TFTs
Al-Jawhari 2015
, and due to to its other advantageous properties, such as material abundance and solar absorbance, it has also been investigated as a potential candidate for multiple device applications ranging from photovoltaics to sensors
Zhang and Gong 2019
.

\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
layers for TFTs are traditionally fabricated by physical vapour deposition (PVD) methods, such as pulsed lased deposition (PLD)
Matsuzaki et al. 2008
;
Zou et al. 2010
;
Ran et al. 2015
and sputtering
Fortunato et al. 2010
;
Jeong et al. 2013
;
Han et al. 2016
. Solution-based processing methods have also been used, such as spin coating
Kim et al. 2013
;
Jang et al. 2016
, electrodeposition
Musselman et al. 2012
and inkjet printing
Baby et al. 2015
. For scalable device applications it is crucial to be able to deposit films with uniform and controllable thickness and composition over large areas, preferably at low or moderate temperatures. Atomic layer deposition (ALD) has been proven invaluable for the fabrication of modern microelectronics, where it is used to produce ultra-thin high-quality dielectric films for devices including metal-oxide semiconductor field effect transistors (MOSFET) and dynamic random access memories (DRAM). ALD has the potential to be extended in production of active device layers. It has already shown to be capable of depositing n-type semiconducting films, such as IGZO
Cho et al. 2019
, with properties compatible with what has been achieved by PVD techniques
Sheng et al. 2019
;
Cho et al. 2019
. The successful application of ALD grown n-type semiconducting oxides in TFTs has been demonstrated both on rigid and flexible substrates
Sheng et al. 2018
;
Sheng et al. 2018
. Development of ALD processes for p-type materials (NiO, CuO
x
, SnO) has been mostly of interest for photovoltaics, especially in perovskite and tandem solar cells, where they can be used as electron-blocking and hole transport layers
Zardetto et al. 2017
. However, some examples of other electronics applications, such as p-type TFTs with ALD grown semiconductor channels have been published
Sheng et al. 2018
;
Maeng et al. 2016
;
Kim et al. 2017
. For example, high performing TFTs with ALD grown CuO
x
films (consisting of both
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
and
CuO
phases) have been reported by Maeng et al.
Maeng et al. 2016
. Their devices showed an unusually high field effect mobility of µ
FE
= 5.6 cm
2
V
-1
s
-1
, which is higher than the µ
FE
of any reported CuO
x
device in the literature. Unfortunately, to our best knowledge, these results have not yet been consistently reproduced, nor are there other reports of the use of ALD
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
in TFTs. However, ALD was used to demonstrate high-performance p-type TFTs with SnO channel
Kim et al. 2017
. There it was observed that applying an
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
channel passivation significantly improves the TFT performance via the reduction of trap states at the interface.

Here, we investigate the influence of an ALD
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
passivation layer on the performance of p-type TFTs with an ALD grown
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
channel. We show that passivation and subsequent vacuum annealing improve the transistor performance metrics. In addition to device measurements, the
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
/
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
interface was characterised in detail by using x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) to obtain more information of the surface reactions taking place during the deposition of the
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
on the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
and how the passivation layer and the oxide interface affect device performance.

layer is required. Therefore the key to the improved characteristics are likely in the properties of the ALD
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
film itself.

A plausible reason for the observed behaviour is the high negative fixed charge density (
Q
f
=
10^{-13}
cm
-2
) of the ALD
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
, which has traditionally been utilised in c-Si solar cells where it reduces the recombination losses on the Si surface via surface defect density reduction and by field-effect passivation. The field-effect passivation is based on the reduction of electron or hole concentrations on the surface/interface by the means of an intrinsic internal electric field
Hoex et al. 2008
;
Dingemans and Kessels 2012
. In our measured TFT data the impact of the field-effect passivation is indicated by the negative shift in the V
TH
and the reduction in the I
off
. It has previously been reported by Han et al. that the high off-state current in the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
TFTs is due to accumulation of minority charge carriers (electrons) at positive gate voltage regime
Han and Flewitt 2017
. The application of the field-effect passivation by ALD
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
on the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
channel and subsequent annealing can be an effective way in reducing the accumulation via electrostatic shielding, which leads to the orders of magnitude lower off-state current and, hence, improves the performance of TFTs with
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
p-channels.

3
Conclusions

p-Type thin film transistors with ALD grown phase pure polycrystalline
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
channel layer were fabricated. The TFTs with as-deposited films showed only limited switching performance, due to the unoptimised film properties and processing parameters, but the characteristics were improved by depositing an 10 nm ALD
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
passivation layer on the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
channel and by subsequent annealing at 300
∘
C in low vacuum. The analysis of the transfer characteristics indicates that the improvement is due to the reduced number of trap states at the channel. The detailed investigation of the
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
/
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
interface by XPS and TEM showed a partial reduction of the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
and possible formation of a 1-2 nm thick
\text{CuAlO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
layer. The p-type
\text{CuAlO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
layer with a low Cu vacancy formation energy can be beneficial to the device operation, but cannot solely explain the better performance of the
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
passivated TFTs. Hence, we conclude that the main benefit of the
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}
passivation comes from its high negative fixed charge density that reduces the accumulation of electrons in the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
channel when positive gate voltage is applied and, thus, reduces the I
off
of the devices. While the field-effect passivation may not be applicable to tradition nanoscale Si-based CMOS devices, as it influences the V
th
and the transport of charge carriers in ways that can be detrimental to the circuit operation, it can be an useful tool in the development of alternative approaches that utilise p-type oxide semiconductors with moderate charge carrier density.

Experimental

The
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
films were grown on 5 cm
\times
5 cm substrates of thermally grown
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
on p-Si (resistivity 0.001 Ωcm, Si-Mat) by atomic layer deposition at 200
∘
C in an ASM F-120 reactor. Copper(II) acetate
\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{OAc}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
with source temperature of 185
∘
C, and water vapor were used as precursors. Each
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
ALD cycle consisted of 2 s
\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{OAc}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
pulse / 2 s purge / 1.5 s
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}

A fluorine-free precursor was chosen because residual fluorine impurities can affect the electrical properties of the films, F being a known n-type dopant, and, additionally lead to poor adhesion of the films due to the accumulation of the fluorine into the interfaces
Gandikota et al. 2000
. Details of the growth chemistry and materials characterisation are published by Iivonen et al. in Ref.
Iivonen et al. 2019
. No further optimisation of the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
film processing or thickness was done regarding the device operation. Bottom gate TFT structures, with the Si substrate acting as a common gate and thermally grown 100 nm thick
\text{SiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
as a gate dielectric, were fabricated with a standard photolithography and nanofabrication methods to test the performance of the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}

\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
films were patterned by wet etching using diluted (0.025 M aq.) HCl, and 100 nm thick Au source and drain electrodes were deposited by thermal evaporation (Edwards Coating System E306A), with a base pressure of
10^{-6}
mbar. After electrode patterning, the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
channel was passivated by a 10 nm
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{3}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{3}}\text{}}

∘
C with trimethylaluminium (TMA, Sigma Aldrich) and water vapour. Finally, the devices were annealed in 1.5 mbar
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
at 200–400
∘
C for 10 minutes.

Film thickness was determined with x-ray reflectivity (XRR) using a PANalytical X’Pert Pro MPD diffractometer, which was also used for x-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements. The measurements were performed in the grazing incidence (GIXRD) geometry at an incidence angle of 1
∘
. The same geometry was used with the high-temperature GIXRD measurements, where Anton-Paar HTK1200N furnace was used for sample heating in 20 mbar
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
(
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}
flow 40 sccm) and data were collected at 150–600
{}^{\circ}C
with 15
{}^{\circ}C
intervals. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) images were taken with Bruker Multimode 8. The electrical properties of the
\text{Cu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\vphantom{\text{2}}\smash[t]{\text{2}}}^{\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\hphantom{\text{2}}\text{}}\text{O}
films were characterised by Hall-effect measurements using van der Pauw configuration with a magnetic field of 0.2 T at room temperature (MMR Technologies Hall System). Thin film transistors were measured using a Cascade probe station and Agilent B1500A semiconductor device parameter analyser. The electrical characterisations were performed in dark to suppress the film photoconductivity. In the interface examinations Escalab (Thermo Fisher Scientific) x-ray microprobe was used for x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and the results were analysed using CasaXPS processing software. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) imaging was done using FEI TALOS T200X operated at 200 kV with EDS for elemental mapping.

A Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem for normal varieties and semistable bundles on degenerating families Thanks: R.W.’s research is supported in part by NSF grants DMS-1906403 and DMS-2204346.
Xuemiao Chen, Richard A. Wentworth

Abstract
. In this paper, we first prove a Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem over projective normal varieties smooth in codimension two. As a consequence we deduce the polystability of (dual) tensor products of stable reflexive sheaves, and we give a new proof of the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality, along with a precise characterization of the case of equality. This also improves several previously known algebro-geometric results on normalized tautological classes. We study the limiting behavior of semistable bundles over a degenerating family of projective normal varieties. In the case of a family of stable vector bundles, we study the degeneration of the corresponding HYM connections and these can be characterized from the algebro-geometric perspective. In particular, this proves another version of the singular Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem for the normal projective varieties in the central fiber. As an application, we apply the results to the degeneration of stable bundles through the deformation to projective cones, and we explain how our results are related to the Mehta-Ramanathan restriction theorem.

1.
Introduction

Two fundamental results in the study of stable bundles over compact
Kähler and projective algebraic manifolds are
the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau (DUY) theorem and the Mehta-Ramanathan (MR)
restriction theorem. The DUY theorem
proves the existence of a Hermitian-Einstein (HE) metric
on any
slope stable
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a compact Kähler manifold
(Y,\omega)

[
8
,
32
,
9
]
.
The associated Chern connections are called Hermitian-Yang-Mills (HYM)
connections.
The result was generalized by Bando and
Siu to stable reflexive sheaves via the
introduction of
admissible
metrics
[
1
]
.
Important consequences include the polystability of tensor products of
stable reflexive sheaves, as well as the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality and
a characterization of the case of equality in terms of projectively flat bundles.
It moreover gives equivalent characterizations of reflexive sheaves with
nef normalized tautological class.
We refer to
[
24
]
for a more detailed discussion of this.

Suppose now that
(Y,\omega)
is a Hodge manifold, i.e.
[\omega]=c_{1}(L)
for some
ample line bundle
L\to Y
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a semistable (resp. stable) bundle over
Y
.
The MR restriction theorem states for
k
sufficiently large,

\mathcal{F}|_{V}
is semistable (resp. stable) for generic
V\in\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(Y,L^{k}))

[
22
,
10
]
.
This result was instrumental in Donaldson’s proof of the DUY theorem in
higher dimensions
[
9
]
.
We refer to
[
17
]
for a discussion
of some other important consequences of the restriction theorem.

The first result of this paper is a version of the
DUY and Bando-Siu
theorems for stable reflexive sheaves over projective normal varieties
Y

which are smooth in codimension
2
. A motivation for a generalization to the singular case comes from
the corresponding consequences mentioned above, just as the smooth case.

Next we study the behavior of semistable bundles on degenerating families,
which gives us another version of the DUY theorem. The motivation here is to give a strategy for an analytic proof of the MR theorem.
The idea goes as follows.
Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a stable bundle over a smooth projective manifold
(Y,\omega,L)
,
with
V\subset Y
as above.
Deform
Y
to the projective normal cone
(see Section
5
for details).
The naive guess is that the HYM connection on
Y
deforms to a HYM connection on the
cone, which by the symmetry of the cone
should have implications for the (semi)stability of the restriction.
There is price to pay here due to the fact that we have introduced
singularities on the base.
Nevertheless,
assuming the restriction theorem holds on the cone, then by a continuity argument
one can conclude that the restriction
\mathcal{F}\bigr|_{V}

is semistable (resp. stable).
Working backwards,
in the case where the restriction theorem actually does hold,
one can show that the sheaf defined by the limiting HYM connection is
indeed of cone type, in the obvious sense.
However, there are plenty of examples that tell us that
this is
not
always true, i.e. the limiting HYM connection does not have
to be a cone in general. Also, as in the singular DUY theorem, a technical
difficulty is that we introduced a singularity to the base, and the known analytic
results about admissible HYM connections on smooth manifolds
are not known to hold across the singular set in general.

Given the discussion above, a closely related question naturally arises:
what is the structure of the limiting HYM connection in general?
This leads to the study of the behavior of semistable bundles over a
degenerating family
of smooth varieties with a normal central fiber.
It turns out that an algebraic geometric result can be constructed
in this setting using known techniques,
and this can in turn be used to characterize the analytic
degeneration. In the special case of a deformation to the projective cone,
this picture is closely related to the MR restriction theorem.
Indeed, we show that the limiting HYM connection on the cone
restricts to the HYM connection on
\mathcal{F}|_{V}
, if the latter is assumed to be stable.
Thus, the HYM connection on
\mathcal{F}|_{V}
is obtained from the HYM connection
on
\mathcal{F}
by deforming the Kähler metric on the base.

1.1.
Main results

We now state more precisely the results sketched above.

1.1.1.
Singular DUY theorem

In this section, we assume that
(Y,\omega)
is a normal projective
variety smooth in codimension
2
, with

Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
and
\omega=\omega_{FS}|_{Y^{reg}}
.
Slope stability of reflexive sheaves on
Y
with respect to

\omega
can be defined as in the nonsingular case,
and this agrees with the algebro-geometric notion of slope stability with
respect to the hyperplane section
D
on
Y
(see Section

2.2
).
With this understood, the first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
Singular DUY)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a stable reflexive sheaf over
(Y,\omega)
. Then there exists an admissible HE metric on
\mathcal{F}

that is unique up to a constant scaling.
Moreover, for any local holomorphic section
s
of
\mathcal{F}
,

\log^{+}|s|^{2}\in W^{1,2}_{loc}\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}
.

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we have (see Corollary

2.17
),

Corollary 1.2
.

Given
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
stable reflexive sheaves over

(Y,\omega)
, then
(\Sym^{k}\mathcal{F}_{1})^{**},(\wedge^{k}\mathcal{F}_{1})^{**},(\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{2})^{**}
are all polystable.

As in the case of smooth manifolds, Theorem
1.1

gives rise to an analytic proof of the Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality of
Miyaoka
[
23
, Cor. 4.7]
, as
well as a characterization of the case of equality.
For the definitions of Chern classes and the pairing below, see Section

2.2
.

Corollary 1.3

(
Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality)
.

Let
[H]
be the class of a hyperplane in
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
Suppose
\mathcal{F}\to Y
is a stable reflexive sheaf. Then

(2rc_{2}(\mathcal{F})-(r-1)c_{1}(\mathcal{F})^{2})\cdot[H]^{n-2}\geq 0\ .

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}
is projectively flat outside
\Sing(\mathcal{F})
.

Remark 1.4
.

We emphasize that the inequality in Corollary

1.3

is well known. For projective varieties it can be reduced to the
surface case by using the MR restriction theorem

[
23
]
. More recently, it was extended to
Kähler spaces
[
33
]
. The novelty in the statement
above is the characterization of the case of equality.

In Section
3
, we use Theorem

1.1
to improve results of Höring-Peternell
on normalized tautological classes.

1.1.2.
Degeneration

Let
p:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
be a flat family of
projective varieties so that
X_{t}:=p^{-1}(t)
is smooth for
t\neq 0
and

X_{0}
is normal. A family
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t\neq 0}
of semistable torsion-free sheaves

\mathcal{E}_{t}\to X_{t}
is called “algebraic” if we can
find a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{X}
, such that

\mathcal{E}\bigr|_{X_{t}}\simeq\mathcal{E}_{t}
.
We say
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{X}
is a semistable degeneration of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t\neq 0}

if furthermore
\mathcal{E}
is flat over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(cf.
[
17
, p. 34]
), and
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{E}|_{X_{0}}
is torsion-free and semistable. Recall that
for a semistable sheaf,
{\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})
and
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0})
denote the
associated graded sheaf of the Jordan-Hölder filtration of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, and its
associated codimension
2
cycle, respectively
(see Definition
4.3
).
On the algebraic side, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

A semistable degeneration always exists for any algebraic family

\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}
where
\mathcal{E}_{t}
are semistable bundles for all
t\neq 0
. Furthermore, for any two
semistable degenerations
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
,

{\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})^{**}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0})^{**}
.
If
X_{0}
is smooth in codimension
2
, then we also have
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0})=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0})
.

Next, assume that
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}
are stable vector bundles for
t\neq 0
, and let
A_{t}
be the corresponding admissible HYM connections. Known analytic results tell us that
we can take a gauge theoretical limit for sequences
t_{i}\to 0
. The limiting data consists of two
parts: a smooth HYM connection
A_{\infty}
defined outside some analytic subvariety

\Sigma
of
X_{0}
, and
the so-called blow-up locus
\Sigma_{b}=\sum_{k}m_{k}^{an}\Sigma_{k}
, which is an
integral linear combination of pure codimension
2
subvarieties (see Section
4.2
for more details). Let
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be the reflexive sheaf defined by
A_{\infty}
over
X_{0}^{reg}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Fix
\mathcal{E}
to be any degeneration of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t}
where
\mathcal{E}_{t}
are stable vector bundles for
t\neq 0
and
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is torsion-free semistable. Then:

(I)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
can be extended to be a semistable reflexive sheaf

\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
over
X_{0}
. Furthermore, for any local section
s
of
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
,
\log^{+}|s|^{2}\in W_{loc}^{1,2}\cap L_{loc}^{\infty}
;

(II)

{\rm Gr}(\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}})^{**}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})^{**}
. In
particular, if
X_{0}
is smooth in codimension
2
, then
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})^{**}
;

(III)

If
X_{0}
is smooth in codimension
2
, then
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0})=\Sigma_{b}
.

In particular, this gives the following version of DUY theorem

Corollary 1.7

(
Singular DUY for degenerations)
.

Given
\mathcal{E}
a semistable degeneration of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t}
where
\mathcal{E}_{t}
are stable vector bundles and
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is torsion-free semistable. Then there exists an admissible
HE metric on
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{**}
.

1.1.3.
Relation to the Mehta-Ramanathan restriction theorem

As another application of the results above,
in Section
5
, we
study the degeneration of a semistable bundle through
the deformation to projective cones of smooth divisors, and we
show how our results are related to
the MR restriction theorem. In a sense,
the result obtained here (see Theorem
5.7
and Corollaries
5.8
)
describes the general picture when the restriction theorem might fail,
i.e. the nongeneric setting.
Even when the restriction theorem holds for a given stable bundle, it does
not, of course, apply to the restriction of HYM connections in general.
Corollary
5.10
, however, can be viewed as an analytic version of
the restriction theorem: the deformation to the projective cone
gives a way to interpolate to the HYM connection of the restriction.

Remark 1.8
.

Another interesting question is whether we can use the HYM connections on the
projective cones to recover the original HYM connection
on
\mathcal{F}

via a limit
as the degree increases.
This is motivated by the observation that high degree hypersurfaces
can be chosen to cover the ambient variety as the degree
\to\infty
.
If indeed this can be made rigorous it could lead to an analytic proof of the
Mehta-Ramananthan restriction theorem. We leave this for future study.

1.2.
Sketch of the proofs

We first need definitions of Chern classes.
Since we are working over singular varieties,
Chern classes are naturally defined in corresponding
homology groups following
[
2
]
. The first Chern class
can be always defined, and the second Chern class is defined when the
base variety is smooth in codimension
2
.
Corresponding Chern numbers, e.g.
slopes
,
can be computed algebraically.
The technical difficulty here is to show that the curvature of the
Chern connection for the
admissible Hermitian metric can indeed be used to compute
Chern numbers as in the Chern-Weil theory of the smooth case.
This boils down to the fact that the corresponding curvature terms
indeed define closed currents across the
singular set, as well as an application of Poincaré duality in the ambient smooth manifold (see Proposition
2.6
).
It is here where
we need to work with normal complex analytic subvarieties of smooth ones with induced metrics.

Given the above, the idea for the proof of the singular
DUY theorem is to use gauge theoretical methods going back to
Donaldson
[
8
]
, Uhlenbeck-Yau
[
32
]
and Bando-Siu
[
1
]
. We use
Hironaka’s theorem on resolution of singularities
to simultaneously resolve
\mathcal{F}
and
Y
and obtain a holomorphic
vector bundle
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
on a smooth resolution
\widehat{Y}\to Y
.
Since
Y
is projective, it can be shown quite
easily that
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
is stable with respect to a small perturbation

\omega_{\epsilon}
of the pullback of
\omega
.
In particular, by the DUY theorem
we have a family of HYM connections

A_{\epsilon}
on
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
. After passing to a subsequence we may find an Uhlenbeck limit
A_{\infty}

of
A_{\epsilon}
over the regular part
Y^{reg}
of
Y
. Then one can show that
A_{\infty}
defines a reflexive sheaf isomorphic to the original
\mathcal{F}
, so the conclusion follows. This is due to a theorem of Siu and a use of MR restriction theorem.

The proof of the existence of a semistable degeneration of semistable bundles is a slight generalization of Langton’s results (
[
19
]
) by following the argument in
[
17
]
. Now we explain about the uniqueness. For two different semistable degenerations, by
restricting to a flat family of high degree curves, one can get the
uniqueness of
{\rm Gr}^{**}
. For the blow-up locus, we need to assume
X_{0}

is smooth in codimension
2
so that we can restrict our family to
a family of high degree smooth surfaces,
and thus get uniqueness of the
algebraic blow-up locus
\mathcal{C}
. This relies crucially on the known
results for the compactification of semistable torsion-free sheaves over smooth
projective surfaces.

Assume the family above consists of stable torsion-free sheaves

\mathcal{E}_{t}
.
We can take a gauge theoretical limit of the corresponding HYM
connections
A_{t}
and get an admissible HYM connection
A_{\infty}
and the blow-up
locus
\Sigma_{b}
which is an integer linear combination of pure codimension

2
subvarieties of
X_{0}
. Since in this case, we deal with normal projective varieties in general, the fact that
A_{\infty}
defines a semistable reflexive sheaf over
X_{0}
is very technical. Assuming this, since we are working in the projective case, we can realize
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
as the double dual of some sheaf represented by an element in a certain Quot scheme which naturally carries the information of the blow-up locus. This transforms our problem into a similar problem which can be dealt with as the uniqueness properties of the semistable degenerations.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be the reflexive sheaf
A_{\infty}
defines over
X_{0}^{reg}
. Now we first explain why
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
can be extended to be a reflexive sheaf over
X_{0}
. To solve these technical issues, the projectivity plays a key role
in that we can construct a
lot of sections of the twisted bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(k)
for
k
fixed but large.
Indeed, twisting by the pullback of the positive line bundle on
Y
,
we know that the bundles
\mathcal{E}_{t}(k)
have many sections.
We wish to take limits to produce nontrivial
sections of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(k)
. Using the HYM condition,
the construction proceeds once one proves that the Sobolev constant of

(X_{t},\omega_{t})
has a uniform bound which follows from a result of Leon Simon.

Given the above, we want to show that
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
can be extended as a
coherent reflexive sheaf over
X_{0}
. This follows from an induction argument
(cf.
[
25
]
).
We start with one limiting section

s
: it will generate a rank
1
saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{L}
of

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
. Furthermore, by the Remmert-Stein theorem
it can be extended to a rank
1
reflexive sheaf over
Y
.
Now by increasing
k
, we can find another section that generates a higher
rank coherent subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
, since by the Riemann-Roch theorem

\mathcal{L}(k)
cannot contain all the limiting sections for
k
large.
This now gives us a rank
2
saturated coherent subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}

which can also be extended as a coherent sheaf over
X_{0}
.
Continuing in this way, the result is obtained. Furthermore, one can show
the limiting sections generate
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
outside some codimension
2
subvariety. Thus the extension follows, the fact that
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
is semistable again follows from certain regularity results, which enable us to do integration by parts.

Admissible HYM connections

Let

A Hermitian metric

•

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}

•

\int_{Y}|F_{h}|^{2}<\infty
,
1
1

1

The integration
is understood to be taken over the smooth locus
Y^{reg}
.

where

of the pair

.
If furthermore,

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}=\mu\Id

2.2.
Chern classes

We need definitions of Chern classes of coherent sheaves on singular
varieties. It seems there is not a standard definition,
but we are only
interested in
\ch_{1}
and
\ch_{2}
, and for these different methods likely
give the same answer with our assumptions.
Most importantly, we wish to compare these Chern classes with the currents

It will be useful to use the approach of Baum-Fulton-MacPherson

[

Recall the notation of the previous section, although here we choose an
arbitrary embedding

, where

As will be clear in the following, we will define the first Chern class in general

Let

Choose a locally free resolution on

Let

Since
\jmath_{\ast}\mathcal{F}
is supported on
Y

\ch^{\ast}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet})

defines a class in

(cf.
[
30

We set:

\ch^{M}_{Y}(\mathcal{F}):=A(\ch^{\ast}(\mathcal{F}_{\bullet}))\in H_{\ast}(Y)\ .

Here,

Define:

(2.2)

Then

In the case where

For

3.
Bogomolov-Gieseker equality and nef tautological

We begin this section with the

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Let

[

.

\mathcal{L}

These arguments can be further used to improve on several previous

corresponding to the case of the normalized tautological class of

\mathcal{F}
being nef, and
\mathcal{F}
pseudo-effective, which we will give definitions below. We start with

Definition 3.1
.

Given
\mathcal{F}

•

\mathcal{F}

•

Following the argument as
[
24
, Thm. 4.1]
, we will prove the following generalization of Corollary
1.3
.

Theorem 3.2

(
Normalized tautological class is nef)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a reflexive sheaf over
Y
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is locally free over
Y^{reg}
and
\zeta_{\mathcal{F}}
is nef.

(2)

\mathcal{F}
is semistable for some ample divisor
A
(not necessarily the one from the embedding

) and

(2rc_{2}(\mathcal{F})-(r-1)c_{1}(\mathcal{F})^{2})\cdot[A]^{n-2}=0\ ;

(3)

\mathcal{F}
admits a filtration:

0\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{m}=\mathcal{F}
,
where
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
are projectively flat over
Y^{reg}
and
\mu(\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\mu(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Given any sheaf
\mathcal{H}
, we denote

\Delta(\mathcal{H}):=2rc_{2}(\mathcal{H})-(r-1)c_{1}^{2}(\mathcal{H})\ .

We assume (1) holds and prove (2).
By definition, we know for any smooth curve
C
lying in
Y^{reg}
as
the intersections of hypersurfaces in
|\mathcal{O}(A)|

This implies
\mathcal{F}
is semistable.
Choose

to be a smooth projective surface in

as an intersection of smooth hypersurfaces in

so that
\mathcal{F}|_{S}
is locally free and semistable.
Since

Suppose (2) holds. We prove (3) by induction on rank,

, and

(b)

to get

over

.
We first prove

. Choose a smooth projective surface

, and so that
\mathcal{E}_{1}|_{S}

By

Together with the Bogomolov inequality for

.
Thus

But then the argument in

Now suppose (3) holds and we prove (1). Obviously,

(

In this section, we fix
\mathcal{X}
to be a subvariety of

The algebraic side

We use the hyperplane section

sheaves on the fibers of
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Definition 4.1
.

\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{E}
is flat over

\mathcal{E}|_{X_{t}}\cong\mathcal{E}_{t}

is called a semistable degeneration of

\mathcal{E}_{0}
is torsion-free and semistable.

We need the following simple observation following from the flatness
assumption.

Lemma 4.2
.

For different semistable degenerations
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{E}
of the same family
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t\neq 0}
,

\det\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}\cong\det\mathcal{E}_{0}
.

To state the main results of this section, we make the following
definition. Given a semistable sheaf
\mathcal{F}
over a projective normal variety

with a fixed polarization. Let
{\rm Gr}(\mathcal{F})
be the
graded sheaf associated to a Jordan-Hölder filtration of
\mathcal{F}
.
Assume that
Y
is smooth in codimension
2
. Let

\mathcal{T}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{F})^{**}/{\rm Gr}(\mathcal{F})
. Then
\dim\supp(\mathcal{T})\leq n-2
.

Definition 4.3

(Lemma)
.

The algebraic blow-up locus of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

(4.1)

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{F})=\sum m_{k}^{alg}\Sigma_{k}\ ,

where
\Sigma_{k}
are irreducible codimension
2
subvarieties of

and

h^{0}(D,\mathcal{T}|_{D})
where

We refer the readers for detailed proof in Section

in
[

]
,
which is done for smooth projective varieties but can be seen to work here.

4.1.1.
Hecke transform and existence of semistable degenerations

In this section, we will prove the first part of Theorem
1.5
, i.e. a
semistable degeneration always exists which is a slight generalization of

[
19
]
using the same argument outlined in
[
17
, p. 59]
, where the
completeness of the moduli space of semistable sheaves over a fixed
projective manifold is studied. For the interest of the reader, the extension of a sheaf
across a negative divisor is also studied in
[
4
]
.

We fix
\mathcal{E}
to be any reflexive sheaf over
\mathcal{X}
so that

\mathcal{E}|_{X_{t}}=\mathcal{E}_{t}
for
t\neq 0
. This always exists by assumption. Since
\mathcal{E}
is torsion-free, we know
\mathcal{E}
always defines a flat family on
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We start with the following observation which generalizes
[
5
, Lemma 3.23]
by the same argument.

Lemma 4.4
.

\mathcal{E}_{0}
is torsion-free.

Proof.

Fix any
x\in X_{0}
. By the assumptions, we can represent
\mathcal{E}
near
x
as

(4.2)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}|_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus n_{1}}\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus n_{2}}\ .

where
U
is an open neighborhood of
x
in
\mathcal{X}
. Restricting this exact sequence to
D=U\cap X_{0}
, one gets an exact sequence

\text{Tor}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D},\text{Im}(\phi))\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}|_{D}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n_{1}}_{D}\ .

Since
\text{Im}(\phi)
is torsion-free, by
using the natural resolution for
\mathcal{O}_{D}
we have

\text{Tor}_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{D},\text{Im}(\phi))=0
.
This implies

\mathcal{E}|_{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n_{1}}_{D}
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}|_{D}
is torsion-free.
∎

Given any saturated subsheaf
\underline{\mathcal{S}}\subset\mathcal{E}_{0}
, i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{0}/\underline{\mathcal{S}}
is torsion-free. Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be the sheaf given by the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow(\iota_{0})_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{0}/\underline{\mathcal{S}})\longrightarrow 0\ .

Definition 4.5
.

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is called a Hecke transform of
\mathcal{E}
along
\underline{\mathcal{S}}
.

By repeating exactly the same argument as
[
4
, Lemma 2.4]
, we have

Lemma 4.6
.

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is reflexive and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}
is torsion-free. Furthermore, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{0}/\underline{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{S}}\longrightarrow 0\ .

Now we start with
\mathcal{E}
as above. We define a sequence of
\mathcal{E}^{k}
inductively by defining
\mathcal{E}^{1}=\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{k+1}
to be the Hecke transform of
\mathcal{E}^{k}
along the maximal destabilizing subsheaf
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
of
\mathcal{E}^{k}_{0}
. For each
\mathcal{E}^{k}
, we can associate it with a nonnegative number

\beta_{k}:=\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{k}_{0})=\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k})-\mu(\mathcal{E}_{0})

which measures how far
\mathcal{E}^{k}
is from being semistable. Note that
\beta_{k}=0
if and only if

\mathcal{E}^{k}
is a semistable degeneration of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t\neq 0}
. In the following, we denote

\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}=\mathcal{E}^{k}_{0}/\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}.

By definition, we have the following exact sequence

(4.3)

0\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{0}^{k-1}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}\longrightarrow 0\ ,

and by Lemma
4.6
, we have

(4.4)

0\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{k}_{0}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}\longrightarrow 0\ .

Lemma 4.7
.

\beta_{k}
decreases and for
k
large, the following hold:

•

\beta_{k}=\beta_{k-1}
;

•

there are natural isomorphisms
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\cong\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
and
\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\cong\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{k}=\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\oplus\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
.

Proof.

It follows from (
4.4
) that
\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k})\leq\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})

since through the last map in the exact sequence, one has a map

\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
. Indeed, this
follows from
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
being semistable if the map is
nontrivial. Otherwise, we have a nontrivial map
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}
and the maximal destabilizing sheaf of
\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}
has slope strictly smaller than
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
. In particular, we know
\beta_{k}
decreases as
k
increases, thus must stabilize for
k
large since it is nonnegative. Now we assume the equality holds
\beta_{k}=\beta_{k-1}
i.e.
\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k})=\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})
. Then we must have an injective map
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
. Otherwise, we have an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}_{k}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}

where
\underline{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}_{k}\subset\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
. This implies
\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k})<\mu(\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1})
, which is a contradiction. Given this, if the equality holds, we have the following exact sequence by the sequence (
4.4
)

0\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}=\mathcal{E}^{k}_{0}/\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}/\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\longrightarrow 0\ ,

thus an injective map

\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}

for
k
large. Since

\mu(\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1})=\mu(\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k})
, the
inclusion map must induce an isomorphism in codimension
1
, thus
\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}^{**}
are the same for
k
large. This then implies
\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k-1}\cong\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
for
k
large. Thus
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\cong\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k-1}
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main results by following the argument
in
[
17
, p. 59]
.

Proposition 4.8
.

For
k
larger,
\mathcal{E}^{k}

is a semistable
degeneration of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}\}_{t}
for
k
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Otherwise, by replacing
\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}^{k_{0}}
for some
k_{0}
large, we assume all the conditions in Lemma

hold below. Denote
\underline{\mathcal{F}}=\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k}
and
\underline{\mathcal{G}}=\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
. Let
R
be the complete DVR associated to the point

, and let
\mathcal{X}_{R}
be the fiber over
\text{Spec}(R)
. Denote
z
to be the generator of the maximal ideal of

and the fibration still as

. Abusing notation, we continue to denote by

\mathcal{E}^{k}

\mathcal{E}^{k}
to
\mathcal{X}_{R}
. Then
\mathcal{E}^{k}

\mathcal{Q}^{k}=\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{k}

\mathcal{Q}^{k}|_{X_{0}}\cong\mathcal{G}

Also we need the following well-known lemma.

. Here
\pi_{1}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}
denotes the natural projection map.

Example 5.3
.

In the construction, let
X
be a Riemann surface of
g\geq 1
and
V
be a point
p
. In this case, one readily checks that
\overline{C(V)}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
while
X_{0}
has to be singular since its arithmetic genus is
g\geq 1
.

Example 5.4
.

In the construction, if
X
is Fano or a simply connected Calabi-Yau manifold,
X_{0}
always coincides with
\overline{C(V)}
.

Example 5.5
.

In the construction, we take
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
L=\mathcal{O}(k)
and
V
to be a degree
k

smooth hypersurface. In this case, it is known that
V
must be
projectively normal (see

[
15
, Ex. II.8.4]
). Furthermore, all the conditions in Lemma
5.2
are satisfied. The central fiber
X_{0}
is normal and
X_{0}=\overline{C(V)}
. When
k=1
, we know
\overline{C(V)}=\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

We will use the following notation for the cone
\overline{C(V)}

•

denote the vertex of
\overline{C(V)}
by
o\in\overline{C(V)}
;

•

let
\pi:\overline{C(V)}\setminus\{o\}\rightarrow V
be the natural surjective map;

•

denote
\iota:\overline{C(V)}\setminus\{o\}\hookrightarrow\overline{C(V)}
the natural embedding.

For use later, we observe the following

Corollary 5.6

(HE metrics on cone-type sheaves)
.

Let

\mathcal{E}=\iota_{*}\pi^{*}(\mathcal{E}|_{V})
be a polystable stable reflexive sheaf over

\overline{C(V)}
, then an admissible HE metric on
\mathcal{E}
can be given
by

h:=\frac{|\widehat{Z}|^{2\mu}}{|Z|^{2\mu}}\pi^{*}\underline{h}

where
\underline{h}
is a HE metric on
\mathcal{E}|_{V}
and
\mu=\mu(\mathcal{E})
. In
particular, the admissible HE metric on
\mathcal{E}
restricts to the
admissible HE metric on
\mathcal{E}|_{V}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

h

is also an admissible HE metric on
\mathcal{E}
. Indeed, by definition

F_{h}=\mu\partial\bar{\partial}\ln|Z|^{2}\Id-\mu\partial\bar{\partial}\ln|\widehat{Z}|^{2}\Id+F_{\pi^{*}\underline{h}}\ .

Since

\omega_{0}|_{C(V)}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\ln(1+|z|^{2})=\frac{|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\ln|z|^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{-1}\partial|z|^{2}\wedge\bar{\partial}|z|^{2}}{(|z|^{2}+1)^{2}|z|^{2}}\ ,

where
z=(\frac{Z_{1}}{Z_{N+1}},\cdots,\frac{Z_{N}}{Z_{N+1}})
, by a direct computation
we have

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}F_{\pi^{*}\underline{h}}=\frac{|Z|^{2}}{|\widehat{Z}|^{2}}\mu\Id\ .

Similarly,

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}\partial\bar{\partial}\ln|\widehat{Z}|^{2}=\frac{|Z|^{2}}{|\widehat{Z}|^{2}}\Id\ .

In particular, we have

\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{0}}F_{h}=\mu\Id
. Also it is obvious that the

L^{2}
norm of
F_{h}
is bounded over
\overline{C(V)}
, since it has
quadratic blow-up when
n\geq 3
, and
\sqrt{-1}F_{\underline{h}}=\mu\id\omega_{FS}|_{V}
when
n=2
. The conclusion follows.
∎

In
the following, we will work under the assumption that

Notice that a semistable bundle
\mathcal{F}\to X

.
As a special case of Theorem
1.5
, we have

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume
X_{0}
is normal.
A semistable degeneration of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}=\mathcal{F}\}_{t}
always exists. For different semistable degenerations
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
,

{\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})^{**}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0})^{**}\ ,

and when
n\geq 3
,

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0})=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\prime})
.

Assume now that
\mathcal{F}
is stable, and let
A_{t}
be the unique HYM connection on

\mathcal{E}_{t}=\mathcal{F}
with respect to the corresponding induced metric. As a special case of the discussion in Section
4.2
,
by passing to a subsequence, we can assume
A_{t}
converges to
A_{\infty}

with blow-up locus
\Sigma_{b}
subvarieties of
X_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
be the
reflexive sheaf over
X_{0}
defined by
A_{\infty}
. As a consequence of Theorem
1.6
, we have

Corollary 5.8
.

Assume
X_{0}
is normal. For each limiting pair
(A_{\infty},\Sigma_{b})
,

•

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
can be extended to a reflexive sheaf
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}
over

\overline{C(V)}
. Furthermore, for any
q>1
; for any global section
s
,
\log^{+}|s|^{2}\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}
;

•

{\rm Gr}(\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}})^{**}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})^{**}
;

•

Assume
n\geq 3
. Then

\Sigma_{b}=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{E}_{0})
for any semistable degeneration
\mathcal{E}
of
\{\mathcal{E}_{t}=\mathcal{F}\}
through the deformation to projective cones and
\overline{\mathcal{E}_{\infty}}={\rm Gr}(\mathcal{E}_{0})^{**}

Remark 5.9
.

•

In a sense, under the given assumptions, our results produce a
canonical object when the restrictions of semistable bundles to
smooth hypersurfaces fail to be semistable.

•

Using the example
\mathcal{E}=T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
and
V=\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
,
A_{\infty}
does not
have a cone property. Indeed, in this example
A_{\infty}
will be the
original HYM connection because the isomorphism class of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}

is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
invariant. This is very different from
[
6
]
.

By replacing
L
with
L^{k}
in the construction and choosing
V
generic,
we know from the MR restriction theorem that
\mathcal{F}|_{V}
is stable. In this
case, a natural semistable degeneration
\mathcal{E}
can be constructed so that

\mathcal{E}_{0}|_{\overline{C(V)}\setminus\{o\}}=\pi^{*}(\mathcal{E}|_{V})
. By Corollary
5.6
and Corollary
5.8
, we have the following analytic version of MR restriction theorem for stable bundles

Corollary 5.10
.

Assume
X_{0}=\overline{C(V)}
is normal and that
\mathcal{F}|_{V}
is stable.
Then
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}=\iota_{*}\pi^{*}(\mathcal{F}|_{V})
and
A_{\infty}|_{V}
is the HYM connection on
\mathcal{F}|_{V}
.

Proof.

The sheaf part is clear. For the connection part, it suffices to notice
that for the HE metric constructed on
\iota_{*}\pi^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{V})
in Corollary

5.6
, the sheaf is generated by
L^{2}
sections, as is the sheaf
defined by the limiting HYM connection. In particular, the isomorphism between
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}

and
\iota_{*}\pi^{*}(\mathcal{F}_{V})
is uniformly bounded, thus parallel as in
Corollary
2.17
.

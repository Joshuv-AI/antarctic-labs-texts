Flavor Phenomenology of Light Dark Vectors
Jordi Folch Eguren, Sophie Klingel, Emmanuel Stamou, Mustafa Tabet, Robert Ziegler

Abstract
Light dark matter with flavor-violating couplings to fermions may be copiously produced in the laboratory as missing energy from decays of SM particles. Here we study the effective Lagrangian of a light dark vector with generic dipole or vector couplings. We calculate the resulting two-body decay rates of mesons, baryons and leptons as a function of the dark vector mass and show that existing experimental limits probe UV scales as large as 10 12 ​ G ​ e ​ V 10^{12}\,{\mathrm{G}eV} . We also derive the general RGEs in order to constrain the flavor-universal UV scenario, where all flavor violation arises radiatively proportional to the CKM matrix.

Introduction

In recent years light new particles interacting very weakly with the Standard
Model (SM) have gained increased interest. The so far negative results on
searches for heavy particles above the electroweak scale at the LHC and
high-intensity experiments have increased the interest in less explored scenarios, with
additional degrees of freedom beyond the SM with masses at sub-GeV scales. Such
particles can be motivated by dynamics addressing the Strong CP Problem (in case
of the QCD axion) or the origin of neutrino masses (in case of sterile
neutrinos), but probably the main motivation is the possibility that such light
particles could be connected to the origin of particle dark matter
(DM)
[
1
]
.

In this context a popular scenario is the dark photon
[
2
,
3
]
, which is either itself DM or is the only mediator (“Vector
Portal”) between the SM and a hidden “dark sector”, which contains one or
several DM particles
[
4
,
5
]
, see
Ref.
[
6
]
for a review. The term “dark photon” usually
refers to a light vector particle coupled to the SM only via kinetic mixing or
dipole operators and that is often taken as the only new degree of freedom.
Instead, the term
Z^{\prime}
is typically reserved for the vast model space
of theories of gauged
U(1)^{\prime}
extensions of the SM, where also a
complete Higgs sector for
U(1)^{\prime}
breaking is explicitly present,
besides additional matter needed for anomaly cancellation, see, e.g.,
Ref.
[
7
]
for a classification. While the
Z^{\prime}

vector boson is often taken to be heavy, with a mass much above the electroweak (EW) scale,
this particle can also be much lighter. The resulting coupling patterns are often
related to the underlying UV symmetries, see, e.g.,
Refs.
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
, and can
leave imprints in low-energy phenomenology/anomalies in current data, e.g.,
in
(g-2)_{\mu}

[
12
]
or in low-energy QCD
[
13
]
.
Beyond perturbative models, light vector particles
can also be in the spectrum of light resonances of low-energy, dark strongly
coupled sectors, see, e.g., Ref.
[
14
]
.
To encompass all these cases, we employ in the current work the term
“light dark vector” (LDV), which is a massive vector boson with
mass much below the EW scale, and sufficiently suppressed couplings to
SM particles such that it is stable on collider scales.
For the purpose of low-energy phenomenology we leave its UV
origin unspecified.

While constraints on light particles have been extensively studied in the
context of colliders, beam-dump experiments, astrophysics, and cosmology, their
phenomenology at precision flavor experiments has so far received less
attention (see Ref.
[
15
,
16
]
for early studies).
Even if flavor-violating couplings may be considered more model-dependent than
flavor-diagonal couplings, they can provide for an efficient production of
light invisible particles from decays of SM leptons, mesons or baryons.
Interestingly, direct searches at laboratory experiments for such two-body
decays with missing energy have the potential to probe enormously large scales,
as the relevant Lagrangian interactions can be dimension-five, instead of
dimension-six as in the case of heavy New Physics. For example, in models with
sufficiently light invisible bosons like the QCD axion, precision flavor
experiments are sensitive to scales as large as
10^{12}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}

The aim of the current work is to systematically study the flavor phenomenology of
light dark vector particles (LDVs), both in the quark and the lepton sectors. We
restrict the discussion to invisible particles, since after all the main (only) motivation for
these particles is the observed DM abundance, and we have in mind scenarios
where either the LDV is itself stable on cosmological scales or promptly decays
to stable DM particles. This analysis includes scenarios where the
LDVs are just sufficiently long-lived to appear as missing energy. This is
particularly justified for vector particles lighter than the electron, as their
decay into two photons is forbidden by the Landau–Yang
theorem
[
26
,
27
]
.
As we shall discuss, the resulting limits on flavor-violating
interactions can be as strong as in the axion case, which is not unexpected due
to the Goldstone-boson equivalence theorem. In light of past and ongoing
experimental searches, it is thus important to systematically study the
phenomenological differences between light dark scalars and vectors
originating from their distinct helicity and coupling structure.

Earlier works have focused on the case of flavor-violating dipole couplings of
a massless dark photon in

to study limits from

[

Here instead we consider the case
of a light vector particle with generic mass and either dipole or minimal
couplings to SM fermions. We work within the framework of a general
effective-field-theory (EFT) approach and consider all possible quark
flavor-violating transitions except those involving the top quark (where
constraints are very weak), and all possible lepton flavor-violating (LFV)
transitions. We also discuss the decays of polarized leptons, which play an
important role in separating signal from SM background. We derive bounds in
the general parameter plane of light-vector mass and the appropriate flavor-changing
coupling by comparing theoretical predictions for the decay rates to the
experimental bounds from various flavor factories, such as

derive model-independent limits on the two-body decay rate as a function of the

induced radiatively by the Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix, satisfying the paradigm of Minimal Flavor-Violation (MFV)

For this analysis we derive the relevant

Setup

We extend the SM by a new, neutral, massive vector boson
V^{\prime}_{\mu}
with a small mass

m_{V^{\prime}}
, which arises either by spontaneous symmetry breaking of, e.g., a
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge
symmetry or by the Stueckelberg mechanism
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
. Here we
focus on the case where this mass is much below the electroweak scale, and the
light dark vector (LDV) is either stable on collider scales or decays into
stable invisible particles.

The most general interactions of the LDV with the SM fermions can be
parametrized using an EFT approach, by considering the most general operators
that respect the unbroken part of the SM gauge group,
SU(3)_{c}\times U(1)_{\mathrm{e}m}
. Here we focus on flavor-violating interactions written without loss of
generality in the fermion-mass basis. We can further assume that a possible
kinetic mixing between the photon and the LDV, i.e.,
\propto\epsilon A^{\mu}V^{\prime}_{\mu}
, has been diagonalized such that
V^{\prime}_{\mu}
is also in
the mass-eigenstate basis. This diagonalization can be performed equally well
for a massless
V^{\prime}_{\mu}
(cf. Ref.
[
29
]
), and the
difference with respect to the massive case is merely that for massless vectors
there remains an unphysical ambiguity in the choice of “mass-eigenstate”
basis, due to the presence of an unbroken
SO(2)
symmetry of the free
Lagrangian. Thus our setup applies equally well to the “massless dark photon”
considered in Ref.
[
29
]
in the limit of
m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0
.

Below the EW scale the lowest dimensional interactions of the LDV
are described by two classes of operators: dipole and vector interactions.
Firstly, we consider flavor-violating, dimension-five dipole interactions of
the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{D}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}V^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}V^{\prime\mu\nu}+\frac{m_{V^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}V^{\prime}_{\mu}V^{\prime\mu}+\frac{1}{\Lambda}V^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\,\overline{f}_{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}+i\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}\gamma_{5}\right)f_{j}\,,

(2.1)

where
V^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}V^{\prime}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V^{\prime}_{\mu}
is
the LDV field strength,
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{i}{2}[\gamma^{\nu},\gamma^{\nu}]
,
and
i\neq j
denote SM quark or lepton flavors.

\Lambda
is the UV-completion scale of the associated
dipole couplings
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}
and
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}
, which
are hermitian matrices in flavor space,

\big(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}\big)^{*}=\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ji}
and
\big(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}\big)^{*}=\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ji}
.

Secondly, we consider flavor-violating couplings of the LDV to SM vector and
axial-vector currents. Naively these are dimension-four interactions below the
EW scale. However, such flavor-violating couplings violate
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge
invariance (flavor-violating currents are not conserved), and thus must be
proportional to some power of the
U(1)^{\prime}
-breaking order parameter, which
we take as the vacuum expectation value (VEV) in the dark sector. Therefore,
the flavor-violating vector couplings are actually dimension-five or higher,
depending on the underlying UV model. In perturbative UV completions the lowest
possible scaling is proportional to a single power of the dark VEV, which upon
including the dark gauge coupling becomes the LDV mass
m_{V^{\prime}}
.
Normalizing by some UV scale
\Lambda
, the flavor-violating vector interactions are

\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{V}}}=-\frac{1}{4}V^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}V^{\prime\mu\nu}+\frac{m_{V^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}V^{\prime}_{\mu}V^{\prime\mu}+\frac{m_{V^{\prime}}}{\Lambda}V^{\prime}_{\mu}\,\overline{f}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{ij}+\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{ij}\gamma_{5}\right)f_{j}\,,

(2.2)

where again
i\neq j
denote SM quark or lepton flavors and the vector
couplings
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{ij}
and
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{ij}
are hermitian matrices in flavor
space,
\big(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{ij}\big)^{*}=\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{ji}
and
\big(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{ij}\big)^{*}=\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{ji}
.

By choosing a scaling that is linear in
m_{V^{\prime}}/\Lambda
, we ensure that the growth of
amplitudes with longitudinally polarized LDVs in initial and/or final states

\propto E/m_{V^{\prime}}
as
m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0
is cancelled by the
m_{V^{\prime}}
dependence
in the interaction. This leads to finite amplitudes in the
m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0
limit
(see Refs.
[
16
,
47
,
48
,
49
,
50
]

for related discussions),
which are just the amplitudes with the corresponding Goldstone bosons as
initial/final states.
An explicit example for a UV model that provides this linear scaling is
provided by Froggatt–Nielsen type models
[
51
]
, discussed in
Appendix
A
. However, the linear scaling with
m_{V^{\prime}}
is
only one possibility. For example, in UV models in which SM fermions do not
carry
U(1)^{\prime}
charges the scaling can be quadratic in the dark VEV, as
the coefficients involve additional powers of the
U(1)^{\prime}
breaking scale

v^{\prime}
,
\propto m_{V^{\prime}}v^{\prime}/\Lambda^{2}
. An explicit realization of this
scenario is also discussed in Appendix
A
.

The interactions in Eq. (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) can also be
written in the chiral basis, which is more suited to match explicit UV models.
In this basis

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{D}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda}V^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\,\overline{f}_{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}P_{{\mathrm{L}}}+\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}P_{{\mathrm{R}}}\right)f_{j}\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{V}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{V^{\prime}}}{\Lambda}V^{\prime}_{\mu}\,\bar{f}_{i}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}P_{{\mathrm{L}}}+\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}P_{{\mathrm{R}}}\right)f_{j}\,,

where
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}=(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ji})^{*}
,

\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}=(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ji})^{*}
,
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}=(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ji})^{*}

and the relations between the “
{\mathrm{V}}/{\mathrm{A}}
” and the “
{\mathrm{L}}/{\mathrm{R}}
” bases are

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}+\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\big(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ji}\big)^{*}+\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}+\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}\right)\,,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}-\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}\right)=\frac{i}{2}\left(\big(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ji}\big)^{*}-\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{R}}}_{ij}-\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{L}}}_{ij}\right)\,.

(2.5)

Above the EW scale the operators must be expressed in a manifestly

SU(2)_{{\mathrm{L}}}\times U(1)_{Y}
invariant manner. For
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{V}}}
this is directly
the case after embedding the left- and right-handed fermions in the
corresponding
SU(2)_{{\mathrm{L}}}
doublets and singlets, respectively. Instead, the
dipole operators in
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{D}}}
require an additional Higgs insertion,
making them dimension-six operators

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{D}}6}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Lambda_{6}^{2}}V^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\left(\overline{F}_{i}HC^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}\sigma^{\mu\nu}P_{{\mathrm{R}}}f_{j}+\text{h.c.}\right)\,,

(2.6)

with
F_{i}
and
f_{j}
denoting here
SU(2)_{{\mathrm{L}}}
doublets and singlets,
respectively, and
H\to\widetilde{H}
, depending on the fermion sector and
the hypercharge conventions. The matching to
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{D}}}
is provided by
identifying
\Lambda_{6}=\sqrt{v\Lambda}
, where
v=174\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
is the Higgs
VEV.

In the following we derive bounds on the flavor-violating couplings in
Eq. (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) from hadronic and leptonic decays
with missing energy in the final state. This discussion is unaffected by other
possible interactions of the LDV with SM fields, in particular flavor-diagonal
couplings, as long as these couplings are sufficiently small to ensure that the
LDV is invisible on collider scales.

For massive LDVs neither the flavor-violating dipole (Eq. (
2.1
))
nor the vector (Eq. (
2.2
)) interactions are UV complete. The UV
completion depends on the origin of the mass for the LDV and the corresponding
(highly model-dependent) radial mode required for the unitarity of the theory.
In turn this implies that unless the complete dark Higgs sector of the
theory is specified, there exist perturbative unitarity constraints on the
couplings of the LDV, similar to the unitarity constraints from
WW\to WW

scattering in the Higgs-less SM. We briefly note that, as long as the flavor
bounds are applicable, i.e., LDV masses in the kinematically allowed
region, unitarity of
2\to 2
scattering poses constraints on the
corresponding couplings that are weaker than those limits by order of
magnitudes. We thus refrain from elaborating upon these constraints in the
current work. For the case of unitarity bounds on massless fermions with
flavor-diagonal couplings coupled to transversely polarized vectors see,
e.g., Ref.
[
52
]
.
The more general case including massive fermions with
flavor-violating couplings to LDVs will be presented in
Ref.
[
53
]
.

3.1
Dark Dipole Interactions

\boldsymbol{s\to d}
Transitions

The bounds on the dipole couplings

\{\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{sd}
,
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{sd}\}
are set by
K\to\pi+{\mathrm{i}nvis.}
and
hyperon decays, cf. Table
1
and
Figure
2
. For the two-body decay
K\to\pi+{\mathrm{i}nvis.}
we use the bound provided by the NA62
collaboration
[
34
]
.
For baryon decays there is an upper limit from BES III
[
65
]

on the decay
\Sigma^{+}\to p+{\mathrm{i}nvis.}

with a massless invisible. We estimate the potential reach for this search by
extending it to larger invisible masses by assuming that the same experimental limit is
valid for the whole kinematic range. This is indicated by a dashed orange line.
For all other baryon searches, we set upper limits on branching ratios indirectly as in
Ref.
[
22
]
by subtracting the measured branching
fractions for all relevant hyperon decay channels from unity. Due to this
rather weak limit,
K\to\pi
sets a much more stringent constraint than
hyperon decays, limiting the UV scale
\Lambda/\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}
to be at least
of the order
10^{11}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
.
Note however that the search for
\Sigma^{+}\to p+{\mathrm{i}nvis.}

strenghtens the upper limit by two orders of magnitude compared to the conservative
limit estimated with the total lifetime, and thus, out of all baryon decays,
it yields the strongest limit of order
10^{7}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
on the scale
\Lambda/\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}
.

Nevertheless baryon decays with missing energy are important for two reasons.
The decays to pseudoscalar, such as
K\to\pi
, are only sensitive to the
\{\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}
,
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{ij}\}
couplings. Thus baryon decays are crucial
to constrain the axial coupling
\Lambda/\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}
(of the order of a few

\times 10^{7}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}

s\to d
transitions. Moreover, the decay rates of pseudoscalar processes are
proportional to the LDV mass for the dipole interaction
\mathscr{L}_{{\mathrm{D}}}

(cf. Eq. (
E.12
)), and thus only baryon decays can constrain

\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{ij}
for small LDV masses. This can be see in
Figure
2
(upper left panel), where the bounds on
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{sd}
from hyperon decays dominate for LDV masses of
m_{V^{\prime}}\approx 0

yielding a limit of
\mathcal{O}(10^{7}\,{\mathrm{G}eV})
on the axial coupling

\Lambda/\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}
.
This provides a strong motivation for explicit direct searches
targeting baryon decays with invisible final states.

B

B\to K/K^{*}+{\mathrm{i}nvis.}

\Lambda_{b}\to\Lambda+{\mathrm{i}nvis}

B

[

[

[

at around

[

6
Summary and Conclusions

In this work we have systematically studied the flavor phenomenology of
light dark vectors (LDVs).
We have restricted our analysis to scenarios where the LDV is directly
linked to dark matter, and is either itself invisible or promptly decays to invisible
particles, such that the LDV appears as missing energy. Working in the context of
a general EFT, we have considered both flavor-violating dipole (see Eq. (
2.1
)) and vector couplings
(see Eq. (
2.2
)) of the LDV to SM fermions.
We have calculated the resulting predictions for the
decay rates of mesons, baryons, and polarized leptons as a function of the LDV
mass, see Section
E
. These predictions were compared to the
experimental limits on various hadronic processes
(Table
1
) and LFV transitions (Table
2
).
For
B\to\pi/K/K^{*}
decays experimental limits from B-factories are only available for
three-body decays with two invisible neutrinos, so we have recasted available
data to obtain bounds on the two-body decay with missing energy as a
function of the LDV mass, see Fig.
7
. The resulting
limits on general vector and dipole interactions of the LDV are summarized in
Figs.
2
and
3
for the quark sector,
and in Fig.
4
and
5
for the lepton sector.
Vector couplings are at least dimension-five operators, which results in very
stringent limits on the UV scale, reaching up to
10^{12}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}

in
K\to\pi
decays,
10^{8}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
in

B
- and
D
-meson decays,
10^{9}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
in
\mu\to e
decays, and
10^{7}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
in

\tau\to\mu/e
decays.
Bounds on dipole couplings are weaker, if viewed as dimension-six operators
above the EW scale, but they still probe UV scales of order

10^{6}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
in
K\to\pi
and
\mu\to e
decays. Importantly, all channels
will be improved by present or near-future experiments, such as NA62, Belle II,
BES III, MEG-II or Mu3e. We have also discussed a scenario where couplings in the UV are flavor-universal, so that quark-flavor violation is only induced radiatively through the CKM matrix. For this
analysis we derived the relevant renormalization-group equations (RGEs) for
both dipole and minimal couplings in Appendix
B
, and used our
previous results to convert limits on flavor-changing interactions into limits
on flavor-diagonal couplings, see Figure.
6
.

To summarize, the aim of this work is to stress the importance of
flavor-violating transitions for light, dark-matter searches, which is copiously
produced in the lab as missing energy in decays of SM particles. Here we
focused on the LDV as part of a dark sector, and showed that present
constraints from precision flavor experiments already probe UV scales as large
as
10^{12}\,{\mathrm{G}eV}
. This underlines the important role of present and next-generation
flavor factories in hunting down dark matter in the laboratory.

Appendix A
UV Motivation of Vector Couplings

In this section we motivate the scaling behavior of the flavor-violating
vector coupling in the Lagrangian of Eq. (
2.2
), both by EFT
considerations and explicit UV-complete models. In perturbative UV
completions, the scaling is at least linear in the dark
U(1)^{\prime}

breaking scale, and we will provide two example scenarios: one that gives
linear and one that gives quadratic scaling. We begin with the EFT
discussion of the latter.

A.1
EFT Discussion for Quadratic scaling

For the EFT approach it is convenient to consider the coupling to the Goldstone
boson
G
in the gauge-less limit, rather than the coupling of the
dark vector
V^{\prime}_{\mu}
itself.
They are related by the Goldstone-boson Equivalence Theorem,
which states that at sufficiently
high energies, or equivalently sufficiently small dark vector masses
m_{V}^{\prime}
, the vector
boson coupling is dominated by its longitudinal polarization, which in the
small
m_{V}^{\prime}
limit becomes the Goldstone boson. Thus one can work out the couplings of the Goldstone boson and
recover the relevant vector-boson couplings by
replacing
\partial_{\mu}G\to-m_{V^{\prime}}V^{\prime}_{\mu}
in the interaction Lagrangian.

We, therefore, consider the case where the dark
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge group is
spontaneously broken by some (SM singlet) scalar field
S
with charge
+1

under the
U(1)^{\prime}
. We take the gauge-less limit, so that
G
is a true
Goldstone boson, contained in
S
according to

\displaystyle S=\frac{v^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\exp(i G/ v^\prime)\,,

(A.1)

where
v^{\prime}/\sqrt{2}
is the (real) VEV that breaks
U(1)^{\prime}
,
connected to the dark vector mass by
m_{V^{\prime}}=g^{\prime}v^{\prime}
, and we have
ignored the radial mode that obtains its mass around
v^{\prime}
. This mode, together with all UV fields are taken to be much heavier than the electroweak scale, so that in the
IR there is only the SM and the Goldstone boson
G\supset S
, which
is formally invariant under global
U(1)^{\prime}
transformations
treating
S
as a spurion with charge
+1
.
Note that one can always realize such a scenario by making
g^{\prime}
sufficiently small.
Writing down the general EFT for this setup, it is clear that if SM fields are
not charged under
U(1)^{\prime}
, the possible couplings of the Goldstone to SM
fields must involve the same powers of
S^{\dagger}
and
S
.
The first such bilinear that gives a non-trivial combination containing
the Goldstone is then

S^{\dagger}\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial}_{\mu}S\supset iv^{\prime}\partial_{\mu}G
.
This implies that, e.g., right-handed down quarks can only couple to the Goldstone at the
level of dimension-six operators only

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{\text{EFT}}_{\text{quadratic}}\supset\frac{c_{ij}}{\Lambda^{2}}(iS^{\dagger}\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial}_{\mu}S)\left(\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}i}\gamma^{\mu}d_{{\mathrm{R}}j}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{ij}}{\Lambda^{2}}v^{\prime}\partial_{\mu}G\left(\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}i}\gamma^{\mu}d_{{\mathrm{R}}j}\right)\,,

(A.2)

where
\Lambda
is the UV scale and in general there is flavor violation in
the (hermitian) EFT coefficients,
c_{i\neq j}\neq 0
. The coupling of the dark vector in this setup is then recovered by

\partial_{\mu}G\to-m_{V^{\prime}}V^{\prime}_{\mu}
, so is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{\text{EFT}}_{\text{quadratic}}\supset c_{ij}\frac{v^{\prime}m_{V}^{\prime}}{\Lambda^{2}}V^{\prime}_{\mu}\left(\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}i}\gamma^{\mu}d_{{\mathrm{R}}j}\right)\,.

(A.3)

This analysis demonstrates that the interactions of dark vectors with
SM fields that are neutral under the
U(1)^{\prime}

scale at least as
m_{V}^{\prime}/\Lambda\times v^{\prime}/\Lambda
.
In particular they involve an additional factor of the
U(1)^{\prime}

breaking scale as compared to Eq. (
2.2
).
Below we will confirm this expectation in an explicit UV
model, see Section
A.3
.

A.2
EFT Discussion for Linear scaling

In order to have dark-vector couplings with a linear scaling in the
U(1)^{\prime}

breaking scale, one necessarily has to charge SM fields under
U(1)^{\prime}
. In
this case the vector boson couples directly to the charged fields via
the dimension-four operator, e.g., for right-handed down quarks

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{linear}}\supset g^{\prime}V^{\prime}_{\mu}\left(\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}}\gamma^{\mu}X_{d}d_{{\mathrm{R}}}\right)\,.

(A.4)

where
X_{d}
is the diagonal
U(1)^{\prime}
charge matrix.
To see how off-diagonal entries are generated, one has to rotate to the mass basis,
which is governed by the Yukawa couplings. It is clear that there is no flavor
violation if
X_{d}
is universal, i.e., proportional to the identity matrix.
If instead charges are non-universal, the mass matrix cannot be generic
at the renormalizable level, i.e., it does not yield realistic
fermion masses without breaking
U(1)^{\prime}
.
Therefore, insertions of
S
or
S^{\dagger}
have to be
considered to obtain realistic fermion masses.

Restricting for simplicity to two generations, and charging only
d_{{\mathrm{R}}1}

with charge
+1
, i.e.,
X_{d}={\mathrm{D}iag}(1,0)
,
X_{Q}=X_{H}=0
,
the full Yukawa matrix requires higher-dimensional operators to have full rank

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\text{EFT}}_{\text{linear}}\supset-y_{i}\overline{Q}_{i}Hd_{{\mathrm{R}}2}-z_{i}\frac{S^{\dagger}}{\Lambda}\overline{Q}_{i}Hd_{{\mathrm{R}}1}+{\mathrm{h}.c.}

(A.5)

Thus the down-quark Yukawa matrix is given by

\displaystyle Y_{d}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}\epsilon&y_{1}\\
z_{2}\epsilon&y_{2}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{with}\quad\epsilon=\frac{v^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}\Lambda}

(A.6)

We can ignore here the Goldstone in
S
, since
we already have the coupling of the gauge field in Eq. (
A.4
),
which leads to flavor-violating couplings with
V^{\prime}
after
rotating to the mass basis.
Nevertheless we can also reproduce this coupling with the same arguments as above:
in the gauge-less limit, we rescale
d_{{\mathrm{R}}1}\to d_{{\mathrm{R}}1}e^{iG/v^{\prime}}
, which removes
G
from
the Yukawa sectors.
Ignoring chiral anomalies, this rescaling only affects the
kinetic terms, as it is a
local

U(1)^{\prime}
transformation

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{linear}}^{\text{EFT}}\supset i\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}1}\not{\partial}d_{{\mathrm{R}}1}\to-\frac{\partial_{\mu}G}{v^{\prime}}\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}1}\gamma^{\mu}d_{{\mathrm{R}}1}\,,

(A.7)

which reproduces Eq. (
A.4
) upon
\partial_{\mu}G\to-m_{V^{\prime}}V_{\mu}=-g^{\prime}v^{\prime}V_{\mu}
.

We are left to diagonalize the Yukawa matrix
Y_{d}
in Eq. (
A.6
), or
rather
Y_{d}^{\dagger}Y_{d}
, in order to find the mixing matrix
V_{d}
of
right-handed down quarks, defined as
V_{Q}^{\dagger}Y_{d}V_{d}=Y_{d}^{\mathrm{d}iag}
.
In the limit when
\epsilon\ll 1
, one has

\displaystyle V_{d}\approx\begin{pmatrix}1&z_{2}/y_{2}\epsilon\\
-z_{2}/y_{2}\epsilon&1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(A.8)

where we have set
y_{1}=0
without loss of generality. Rotating the dark-vector
couplings in Eq. (
A.4
) to the mass basis defined by
d_{{\mathrm{R}}}\to V_{d}d_{{\mathrm{R}}}
gives finally

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{linear}}\supset g^{\prime}V^{\prime}_{\mu}\left(\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}}\gamma^{\mu}V_{d}^{\dagger}X_{d}V_{d}d_{{\mathrm{R}}}\right)=g^{\prime}V^{\prime}_{\mu}(V_{d}^{*})_{1i}(V_{d})_{1j}\left(\overline{d}_{{\mathrm{R}}i}\gamma^{\mu}d_{{\mathrm{R}}j}\right)\,,

(A.9)

so that indeed off-diagonal couplings are generated proportional to
g^{\prime}(V_{d}^{*})_{11}(V_{d})_{12}\sim g^{\prime}\epsilon\sim m_{V}^{\prime}/\Lambda
.

To summarize, we have demonstrated that vector interactions of dark
vectors can indeed be proportional to a single power of the
U(1)^{\prime}
breaking, and thus
scale with the dark-vector mass as in Eq. (
A.4
), if SM fermions have
non-universal
U(1)^{\prime}
charges. This situation is quite generic
in models where SM Yukawa hierarchies are explained by non-anomalous abelian
flavor symmetries, for example simple
U(1)_{F}
Froggatt-Nielsen
models
[
51
]
, see e.g. Refs.
[
7
]
for
examples of such models without extra heavy fermions to cancel anomalies. It is
well-known how to build UV completions for such models
[
75
,
76
]
, and below in Section
A.4
we will present an
illustrative example.

Appendix E
Two-body decays to Light Dark Vectors

In this appendix we present the full expressions for the two-body decays to a LDV that
enter our analysis, namely

•

P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime}
: pseudoscalar meson to pseudoscalar meson and LDV,

•

P\to\mathcal{V}+V^{\prime}
: pseudoscalar meson to vector meson and LDV,

•

B\to B^{\prime}+V^{\prime}
: baryon to baryon and LDV,

•

\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime}
: lepton to lepton and LDV.

For the hadronic processes illustrative Feynman diagrams are shown in
Figure
1
, while throughout this appendix we define the two-body
kinematics for all decays as in Figure
11
, namely as

\text{SM}(p)\to\text{SM}^{\prime}(p^{\prime})+V^{\prime}(q)

(E.1)

with
q=p-p^{\prime}
and
q^{2}=(p-p^{\prime})^{2}=m_{V^{\prime}}^{2}
.
In the next subsection we collect the parametrization of all the relevant form factors
for the hadronic processes considered, and in the subsequent subsections
we present the expressions for the rates. The numerical values for the form-factors are always taken from the most recent
work referenced.

E.1
Form Factors

\boldsymbol{P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime}}

For these decays the hadronic matrix elements for the
vector and axial-vector currents read
[
66
]

\begin{split}\langle P^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\gamma^{\mu}q|P(p)\rangle&=(p+p^{\prime})^{\mu}f_{+}^{PP^{\prime}}(q^{2})+(p-p^{\prime})^{\mu}f_{-}^{PP^{\prime}}(q^{2})\,,\\
\langle P^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}q|P(p)\rangle&=0\,.\end{split}

(E.2)

The corresponding matrix elements for tensor and pseudo-tensor currents read
[
66
]

\begin{split}\langle P^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q|P(p)\rangle&=\frac{2}{m_{P}+m_{P^{\prime}}}\left(p^{\prime}_{\mu}p_{\nu}-p^{\prime}_{\nu}p_{\mu}\right)f_{\mathrm{T}}^{PP^{\prime}}(q^{2})\,,\\
\langle P^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}q|P(p)\rangle&=\frac{2i}{m_{P}+m_{P^{\prime}}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p^{\prime}_{\rho}p_{\sigma}f_{\mathrm{T}}^{PP^{\prime}}(q^{2})\,,\end{split}

(E.3)

where here and throughout we use the
\epsilon_{0123}=-\epsilon^{0123}=+1
convention for the Levi-Civita
tensor.

\boldsymbol{P\to\mathcal{V}+V^{\prime}}

For the pseudoscalar decays to two vectors with
\mathcal{V}

denoting the vector-meson, the hadronic matrix element for the vector and
axial-vector currents are parametrized as
[
66
]

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{V}(p^{\prime},\lambda)|\overline{q}^{\prime}\gamma^{\mu}\left(1\mp\gamma_{5}\right)q|P(p)\rangle=P_{1}^{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{1}(q^{2})\pm P_{2}^{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{2}(q^{2})\pm P_{3}^{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{3}(q^{2})\pm P_{P}^{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{P}(q^{2})\,,

(E.4)

where
\lambda
denotes the polarization of
\mathcal{V}
. The kinematic functions read

\displaystyle P_{P}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=i(\epsilon^{*}\!\cdot\!q)q^{\mu}\,,

\displaystyle P_{1}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=2\epsilon^{\mu}_{~~\alpha\beta\gamma}\epsilon^{*\alpha}p^{\prime\beta}q^{\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle P_{2}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=i\left[\left(m_{P}^{2}-m_{{\mathcal{V}}}^{2}\right)\epsilon^{*\mu}-(\epsilon^{*}\!\cdot\!q)\left(p^{\prime}+p\right)^{\mu}\right]\,,

\displaystyle P_{3}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=i(\epsilon^{*}\!\cdot\!q)\big[q^{\mu}-\frac{q^{2}}{m_{P}^{2}-m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}(p^{\prime}+p)^{\mu}\big]\,,

(E.5)

where
\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}=\epsilon_{\mu}^{*}(p^{\prime},\lambda)
denotes the polarization vector of the
outgoing
\mathcal{V}
. The scalar form factors can be further parametrized as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{P}(q^{2})&=\frac{-2m_{\mathcal{V}}}{q^{2}}A_{0}(m_{V^{\prime}}^{2})\,,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{1}(q^{2})=\frac{-V(q^{2})}{m_{P}+m_{\mathcal{V}}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{2}(q^{2})=\frac{-A_{1}(q^{2})}{m_{P}-m_{\mathcal{V}}}\,,\\
\mathcal{V}_{3}(q^{2})&=\frac{m_{P}+m_{\mathcal{V}}}{q^{2}}A_{1}(q^{2})-\frac{m_{P}-m_{\mathcal{V}}}{q^{2}}A_{2}(q^{2})\equiv\frac{2m_{\mathcal{V}}}{q^{2}}A_{3}(q^{2})\,,\end{split}

(E.6)

with
A_{3}(0)=A_{0}(0)
, which ensures finite matrix elements at
q^{2}=0
, i.e., for massless LDV.

The corresponding matrix elements for tensor and pseudo-tensor currents read
[
66
]

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{V}(p^{\prime},\lambda)|\overline{q}^{\prime}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q|P(p)\rangle&=-i\epsilon^{*}_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q^{2})\,,\\
\langle\mathcal{V}(p^{\prime},\lambda)|\overline{q}^{\prime}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}q|P(p)\rangle&=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{*}_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}T^{\alpha\rho\sigma}(q^{2})\,,\end{split}

(E.7)

where

\begin{split}T^{\alpha\mu\nu}(q^{2})&=\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\nu\beta}\left[\left(p_{\beta}+p^{\prime}_{\beta}-q_{\beta}\frac{m_{P}^{2}-m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}{q^{2}}\right)T_{1}^{P\mathcal{V}}(q^{2})+q_{\beta}\frac{m_{P}^{2}-m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}{q^{2}}T_{2}^{P\mathcal{V}}(q^{2})\right]\\
&+\frac{2p^{\alpha}}{q^{2}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\beta\gamma}p_{\beta}p^{\prime}_{\gamma}\left(T_{2}^{P\mathcal{V}}(q^{2})-T_{1}^{P\mathcal{V}}(q^{2})+\frac{q^{2}}{m_{P}^{2}-m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}T_{3}^{P\mathcal{V}}(q^{2})\right)\,.\end{split}

(E.8)

For vanishing momentum transfer
q^{2}=0
, i.e., massless LDV, the scalar form-factors satisfy

\displaystyle T_{1}^{P\mathcal{V}}(0)

\displaystyle=T_{2}^{P\mathcal{V}}(0)\equiv T\,,

(E.9)

while the contribution proportional to
T_{3}(0)
vanishes.

\boldsymbol{B\to B^{\prime}+V^{\prime}}

For the baryon decays the matrix elements for vector and axial-vector currents
are parametrized by
[
67
,
69
,
72
]

\begin{split}\langle B^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\gamma_{\mu}q|B(p)\rangle&=\overline{u}_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\left(f_{1}(q^{2})\gamma_{\mu}-i\frac{f_{2}(q^{2})}{m_{B}}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}+\frac{f_{3}(q^{2})}{m_{B}}q_{\mu}\right){u}_{B}(p)\,,\\
\langle B^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}q|B(p)\rangle&=\overline{u}_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\left(g_{1}(q^{2})\gamma_{\mu}-i\frac{g_{2}(q^{2})}{m_{B}}\sigma_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}+\frac{g_{3}(q^{2})}{m_{B}}q_{\mu}\right)\gamma_{5}{u}_{B}(p)\,,\end{split}

(E.10)

with
u_{B}(p)
and
u_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})
the spinor functions for
B
and
B^{\prime}
respectively. For
\Lambda
decays the values of the form factors are taken from
[
67
,
69
,
72
]
, while for hyperon decays they are taken from
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
.

The corresponding matrix elements for tensor and pseudo-tensor currents have the form

[
32
,
79
]

\begin{split}\langle B^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q|B(p)\rangle&=g_{\mathrm{T}}^{BB^{\prime}}\overline{u}_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\sigma^{\mu\nu}u_{B}(p)\,,\\
\langle B^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}q|B(p)\rangle&=\frac{i}{2}g_{\mathrm{T}}^{BB^{\prime}}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\overline{u}_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\sigma^{\alpha\beta}u_{B}(p)\,,\end{split}

(E.11)

which is an approximation valid for
m_{V^{\prime}}^{2}=0
, which we use for the hyperon decays.
For the baryon
\Lambda_{b}\to\Lambda
,
\Lambda_{b}\to n
, and

\Lambda_{c}\to p
decays we use the available full parametrization, given by

[
67
,
72
]

\begin{split}\langle B^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}q|B(p)\rangle&=\overline{u}_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\left(\frac{f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}(q^{2})}{m_{B}}\left(\gamma^{\mu}q^{2}-q^{\mu}\not{q}\right)-f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}(q^{2})i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}\right){u}_{B}(p)\,,\\
\langle B^{\prime}(p^{\prime})|\overline{q}^{\prime}i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}\gamma_{5}q|B(p)\rangle&=\overline{u}_{B^{\prime}}(p^{\prime})\left(\frac{f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}(q^{2})}{m_{B}}\left(\gamma^{\mu}q^{2}-q^{\mu}\not{q}\right)-f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}(q^{2})i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}\right)\gamma_{5}{u}_{B}(p)\,.\end{split}

Having collected all hadronic input used in the analysis we next present the full
expressions for the two-body rates. We show separately the contributions from dipole and
vector interactions with the LDV, c.f. Eqs. (
2.1
) and (
2.2
).
For brevity we drop the argument in all form factors since it is always
q^{2}=m^{2}_{V^{\prime}}
in
two-body decays. To shorten the expression we also introduce the notations

\kappa_{x}\equiv m^{2}_{x}/M^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda_{xy}\equiv(1-\kappa_{x}-\kappa_{y})^{2}-4\kappa_{x}\kappa_{y}\,,

with
m_{x}
indicating the mass of the final-state particle
x
and
M
the mass of the
decaying particle.

E.2
Partial width for
\boldsymbol{P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime}}

The partial width for the decay
{P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime}}
with an underlying

q\to q^{\prime}
flavor-changing transition is given respectively
for dipole and vector interaction by

\displaystyle\Gamma(P\to P^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{V^{\prime}}m_{P}^{3}}{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{3/2}_{P^{\prime}V^{\prime}}}{(1+\sqrt{\kappa_{P^{\prime}}})^{2}}|f_{\mathrm{T}}^{PP^{\prime}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\,,

(E.12)

\displaystyle\Gamma(P\to P^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{P}^{3}}{16\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda^{3/2}_{P^{\prime}V^{\prime}}|f_{+}^{PP^{\prime}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\,,

(E.13)

Note that due to the parity conservation of strong interactions
the rate is independent of the axial couplings
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{ij}
and
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{ij}
.
Therefore,
P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime}
decays are only sensitive to the

\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}({\mathrm{D}})}_{ij}
couplings. In the
\{{\mathrm{L}},{\mathrm{R}}\}
basis, these decays are
sensitive to both
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}{\mathrm{L}}({\mathrm{R}})}_{ij},\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}{\mathrm{L}}({\mathrm{R}})}_{ij}
couplings.

In the limit for massless LDV, the leading in
m_{V^{\prime}}
contributions to the decay rates read

\displaystyle\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}_{V^{\prime}}m_{P}}{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\sqrt{\kappa_{P^{\prime}}}\right)^{3}|f_{\mathrm{T}}^{PP^{\prime}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\,,

(E.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(P\to P^{\prime}+V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{P}^{3}}{16\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{P^{\prime}}\right)^{3}|f_{+}^{PP^{\prime}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\,.

(E.15)

While the rate originating from dipole interactions vanishes in the massless limit,
the contribution of the vector interaction remains constant due to the linear scaling
m_{V^{\prime}}/\Lambda
introduced
and discussed in Eq. (
2.2
).

E.3
Partial width for
\boldsymbol{P\to{\mathcal{V}}+V^{\prime}}

The partial width for the decay
{P\to{\mathcal{V}}+V^{\prime}}
with an underlying

q\to q^{\prime}
flavor-changing transition is given respectively
for dipole and vector interaction by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma(P\to\mathcal{V}+V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{P}^{3}}{2\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda^{1/2}_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}\left(A_{{\mathrm{D}}}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+A_{{\mathrm{D}}5}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\right)\,,\\
\Gamma(P\to\mathcal{V}+V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{P}^{3}\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{2}}{8\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda^{1/2}_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}\left(A_{{\mathrm{V}}}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+A_{{\mathrm{V}}5}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\right)\,,\\
\end{split}

(E.16)

with the coefficients
A_{X}
given by

\displaystyle A_{{\mathrm{D}}}

\displaystyle=|T^{P\mathcal{V}}_{1}|^{2}\lambda_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}\,,

(E.17)

\displaystyle A_{{\mathrm{D}}5}

\displaystyle=|T^{P\mathcal{V}}_{2}|^{2}\frac{8\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\left(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(1+3\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}-2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{2}(1+3\kappa_{\mathcal{V}})+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{3}}{8\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}

\displaystyle+|T^{P\mathcal{V}}_{3}|^{2}\lambda^{2}_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}\frac{\kappa_{V^{\prime}}}{8\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}})^{2}}-{\mathrm{R}e}(T^{P\mathcal{V}}_{2}T^{P\mathcal{V*}}_{3})\lambda_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}\frac{\kappa_{V^{\prime}}(1+3\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}})}{4\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}})}\,,

(E.18)

\displaystyle A_{{\mathrm{V}}}

\displaystyle=|V|^{2}\frac{\lambda_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}}{\left(1+\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{2}}\,,

(E.19)

\displaystyle A_{{\mathrm{V}}5}

\displaystyle=|A_{1}|^{2}\frac{\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{3}-2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{2}(1+3\kappa_{\mathcal{V}})+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}(1+3\kappa_{\mathcal{V}})^{2}+8(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}})^{2}\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}{8\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\left(1-\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+|A_{3}|^{2}\frac{\lambda_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}^{2}}{2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}}+{\mathrm{R}e}(A_{1}A_{3}^{*})\frac{\sqrt{1+\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}}{2\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}\left(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{2}}\lambda_{\mathcal{V}V^{\prime}}\left(1-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}+3\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)\,.

(E.20)

In the limit of a massless LDV, the decay rates reduce to

\begin{split}\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(P\to\mathcal{V}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{P}^{3}}{2\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{3}|T|^{2}\left(|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\right)\,,\\
\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(P\to\mathcal{V}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{P}^{3}}{16\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{3}\left(|A_{3}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+\frac{2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}|V|^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{\kappa_{\mathcal{V}}}+1\right)^{2}}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\right)\,,\end{split}

(E.21)

which illustrates that the sensitivity to
\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}
weakens for very light LDVs.

\boldsymbol{B\to B^{\prime}+V^{\prime}}

For baryon decays
B\to B^{\prime}+V^{\prime}
with an underlying

q\to q^{\prime}
transition the contribution
to the partial width from the dipole and vector interaction read

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}^{1/2}\Big[\left(|f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}1}^{-}+|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}2}^{-}+\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{-}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}*})\right)|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&\hskip 50.00008pt+\left(|f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}1}^{+}+|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}2}^{+}+\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{+}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})\right)|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\Big]\,,\\

(E.22)

with the kinematic coefficients

\begin{split}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}1}^{\pm}&=\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(\kappa_{B^{\prime}}^{2}+\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\left(\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-2\right)\pm 6\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{2}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}+1\right)\,,\\

\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{\pm}&=6\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}\mp 1\right)\left(1+\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\pm 2\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\right)\,,\\
\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}1}^{\pm}&=\left(1+\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\pm 2\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\right)\left(1+\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\mp 2\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}+2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\right)\,,\\
\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}2}^{\pm}&=\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(1+\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\pm 2\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\right)\left(2+2\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\mp 4\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\right)\,,\\
\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}12}^{\pm}&=6\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\pm 2\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}+1\right)\left(\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}\mp 1\right)\,.\\
\end{split}

In the limit of a massless LDV, the rates reduce to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{2\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\Big[|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\Big]\,,\\
\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{16\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\Big(|f_{1}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+|g_{1}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\Big)\,.\end{split}

(E.23)

For hyperon decays we use the form factor parametrization of Eq. (
E.11
), valid for
m_{V^{\prime}}=0
.
Nonetheless, we consider a massive LDV for the kinematics for completeness. The decay rate reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda^{1/2}_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}(g_{\mathrm{T}}^{BB^{\prime}})^{2}\left(\hat{A}^{-}_{{\mathrm{D}}}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+\hat{A}^{+}_{{\mathrm{D}}}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\right)\,,\end{split}

(E.24)

with the kinematic coefficients

\begin{split}\hat{A}^{\pm}_{{\mathrm{D}}}&=\left(\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\pm 2\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}+1\right)\left(2\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\mp 4\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}+2\right)\,.\end{split}

(E.25)

In the limit of a massless LDV, the rate reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{2\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\right)^{3}|g_{\mathrm{T}}^{BB^{\prime}}|^{2}\Big(|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\Big)\,.\end{split}

(E.26)

For a fully polarized initial
B
, the differential width read

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{8\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}^{1/2}\Big[\\
&\quad\phantom{+}\ \left(|f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}1}^{-}+|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}2}^{-}+\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{-}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}*})\right)|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&\quad+\left(|f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}1}^{+}+|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}2}^{+}+\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{+}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})\right)|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&\quad-2\lambda^{1/2}_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\cos\theta\left(\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}11}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{-}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 70.0001pt+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}22}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{+}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})\right){\mathrm{R}e}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})\\
&\quad-2\lambda^{1/2}_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\cos\theta\left(\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}11}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{-}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 70.0001pt+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}22}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{+}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{1}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*})\right){\mathrm{I}m}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})\Big]\,,\\
\frac{d\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{32\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda^{1/2}_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\Big[\\
&\quad\phantom{+}\ \left(|f_{1}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}1}^{-}+|f_{2}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}2}^{-}+\hat{A}_{{{\mathrm{V}}12}}^{-}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}f_{2}^{*})\right)|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&\quad+\left(|g_{1}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}1}^{+}+|g_{2}|^{2}\hat{A}_{{\mathrm{V}}2}^{+}+\hat{A}_{{{\mathrm{V}}12}}^{+}{\mathrm{R}e}(g_{1}g_{2}^{*})\right)|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&\quad-2\lambda^{1/2}_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\cos\theta\left(\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}11}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}g_{1}^{*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}12}^{+}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{2}g_{1}^{*})\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 70.0001pt+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}12}^{-}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}g_{2}^{*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}22}{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{2}g_{2}^{*})\right){\mathrm{R}e}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})\\
&\quad+2\lambda^{1/2}_{B^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\cos\theta\left(\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}11}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{1}g_{1}^{*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}12}^{+}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{2}g_{1}^{*})\right.\\
&\left.\hskip 70.0001pt+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}12}^{-}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{1}g_{2}^{*})+\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}22}{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{2}g_{2}^{*})\right){\mathrm{I}m}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(E.27)

with the kinematic coefficients

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}11}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(\kappa_{B^{\prime}}+2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-1\right),

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}22}

\displaystyle=2\kappa_{B^{\prime}}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-2,

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{D}}12}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(3\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}\pm 1\right),

(E.28)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}11}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{B^{\prime}}+2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-1,

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}22}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(2\kappa_{B^{\prime}}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-2\right),

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{{\mathrm{V}}12}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(3\sqrt{\kappa_{B^{\prime}}}\pm 1\right).

(E.29)

In the limit of a massless LDV, the rate reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\frac{d\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\Big[|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+|f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&+2\cos\theta\left({\mathrm{I}m}(f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*}){\mathrm{R}e}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})+{\mathrm{R}e}(f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}V}}f_{2}^{{\mathrm{T}A}*}){\mathrm{I}m}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})\right)\Big]\,,\\
\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\frac{d\Gamma(B\to B^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{B}^{3}}{32\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{B^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\Big(|f_{1}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}+|g_{1}|^{2}|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{q^{\prime}q}|^{2}\\
&+2\cos\theta\left({\mathrm{R}e}(f_{1}g_{1}^{*}){\mathrm{R}e}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})-{\mathrm{I}m}(f_{1}g_{1}^{*}){\mathrm{I}m}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{q^{\prime}q}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5*}_{q^{\prime}q})\right)\Big)\,.\end{split}

(E.30)

E.5
Polarized lepton distributions and rates
\boldsymbol{\ell\to\ell^{\prime}+V^{\prime}}

Next we consider the decays
\ell\to\ell^{\prime}+V^{\prime}
for the case
in which lepton-flavor violating dipole or vector interactions
with the LDV are present.
In this case there is experimental sensitivity to the polarization
of the initial lepton by the measurement of the angular distribution of
the angle
\theta
, defined as the angle between the polarization
vector of
\ell
and the three-momentum of
\ell^{\prime}
.
For the different LDV interactions we find
for the differential width of a fully polarized initial
\ell

\begin{split}\frac{d\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{\ell}^{3}}{{8\pi\Lambda^{2}}}\lambda^{1/2}_{\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\Big[(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{+}+\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{-})\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{+}-\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{-})\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}\\
&\hskip 60.00009pt+\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\theta}\cos\theta\cdot{\mathrm{I}m}(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5*}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell})\Big]\,,\\
\frac{d\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{\ell}^{3}}{32\pi\Lambda^{2}}\lambda^{1/2}_{\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\Big[(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{+}+\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{-})\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{+}-\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{-})\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}\\
&\hskip 60.00009pt+\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\theta}\cos\theta\cdot{\mathrm{R}e}({\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5*}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(E.31)

with the kinematic coefficients

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{+}

\displaystyle=2\left(1-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(1+\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{+}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{2}+\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\left(1+\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)-2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{-}

\displaystyle=-6\sqrt{\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}}\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{-}

\displaystyle=-6\sqrt{\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}}\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\,,

(E.32)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=2\lambda^{1/2}_{\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\left(2-2\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}-\kappa_{V^{\prime}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=2\lambda^{1/2}_{\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime}}\left(1-2\kappa_{V^{\prime}}-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\,.

In the limit of massless LDV, the polarized differential two-body rate reduces to

\begin{split}\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\frac{d\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{\ell}^{3}}{{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}}\left(1-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\left(\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+2\cos\theta\cdot{\mathrm{I}m}(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5*}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell})\right)\\
\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\frac{d\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})}{d\cos\theta}\bigg|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{\ell}^{3}}{32\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(1-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\left(\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+2\cos\theta\cdot{\mathrm{R}e}(\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5*}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell})\right)\,.\end{split}

(E.33)

Finally, after integrating over
\theta
and averaging over the
initial- and final-state polarizations, the total decay rates read

\displaystyle\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}_{\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime}}m_{\ell}^{3}}{{4\pi\Lambda^{2}}}\left(\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{+}+\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{-})+\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{+}-\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{-})\right)\,,

(E.34)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}_{\ell V^{\prime}}m_{\ell}^{3}}{16\pi\Lambda^{2}}\left(\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{+}+\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{-})+\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}(\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{+}-\tilde{A}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{-})\right)\,,

(E.35)

which in the limit of massless LDVs reduces to

\begin{split}\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{D}}&=\frac{m_{\ell}^{3}}{{2\pi\Lambda^{2}}}\left(1-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\left(\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{D}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}\right)\,,\\
\lim_{m_{V^{\prime}}\to 0}\Gamma(\ell\to\ell^{\prime}V^{\prime})\Big|_{\mathrm{V}}&=\frac{m_{\ell}^{3}}{{16\pi\Lambda^{2}}}\left(1-\kappa_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)^{3}\left(\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}+\left|\mathbb{C}^{{\mathrm{V}}5}_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\right|^{2}\right)\,.\end{split}

(E.36)

Parity functors
Igor Nikonov

Abstract
A parity is a rule to assign labels to the crossings of knot diagrams in a way compatible with Reidemeister moves. Parity functors can be viewed as parities which provide to each knot diagram its own coefficient group that contains parities of the crossings. In the article we describe the universal oriented parity functors for free knots and for knots in a fixed surface.



Introduction

V.O. Manturov
[
16
]
defined a parity as a rule to assign labels
0
and
1

to the crossings of knot diagrams in a way compatible with
Reidemeister moves. Capability to discriminate between odd and even
crossings allows to treat the crossings differently when
transforming knot diagrams or calculating their invariants.
The notion of parity has proved to be an effective tools in knot
theory. It allows to strengthen knot invariants, to prove minimality
theorem and to construct (counter)examples
[
6
,
8
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
.

In
[
7
]
parities with coefficients in an arbitrary abelian group were defined. In this paper we make one step to further generalization.
Parity functors can be described as parities which assign to each knot diagram its own
coefficient group that contains the parities of crossings of the diagram. The motivation for such a
generalization comes from the following example
[
8
, Example 2.1]
.

Figure 1:
A flat knot
in a surface of genus two

Let us consider the flat knot in Fig.
1
, given as a curve in a surface of genus two, and take some parity
p
with coefficients in an abelian group
A
. The knot has
{\mathbb{Z}}_{5}
-symmetry. By the symmetry the parities of all crossings

p(v_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,5
, must coincide. From the axioms of parity (cf.
[
8
, Lemma 3.7]
), the sum of parities of the vertices of the inner pentagon is zero:

\sum_{i=1}^{5}p(v_{i})=0.

Then
5p(v_{i})=0
and
p(v_{i})=0
. Thus, the parity of the crossings is trivial.

On the other hand, if the surface is fixed then one can assign nontrivial homological parity to the crossings
[
8
, Example 2.1]
. We can retain this nontriviality if we assume that a symmetry of the diagram induces an automorphism
\phi
of the coefficient group
A
, so we will deal with equalities like
p(v_{j})=\phi(p(v_{i}))
instead of
p(v_{j})=p(v_{i})
.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we give the main definitions. We start with formal description of knot theories by means of diagrams and their transformations. Next we remind the notion of oriented parity given in
[
21
]
and then introduce the definition of oriented parity functor. We conclude the section with a construction of the universal oriented parity functor.

The second and the third sections are devoted correspondingly to description of the universal oriented parity functors for two knot theories: the free knots and knots in a given surface. The universal parity functor of free knots is a slight extension of the Gaussian parity. The universal functor for knots in a given surface coincides essentially with the homotopic parity. This means that there is no nontrivial parity functors for classical knots.

1
Definitions

A conventional form for presentation of knots and links are diagrams. Any knot admits infinitely many diagrams and any two of them can be linked with a sequence of elementary transformations — diagram isotopies and Reidemeister moves.

Let us consider several examples of knots of various types and their diagrams.

A
4
-graph
is any union of four-valent graphs and
trivial components
, i.e. circles considered as graphs without vertices and with one (closed) edge.
A
virtual diagram
is an embedding of a
4
-graph into plane so that each vertex of the graph is marked as either
classical
of
virtual
vertex.
At a classical vertex one a pair of opposite edges (called
overcrossing
) is chosen. The other pair of opposite edges at the vertex is called
undercrossing
. The vertices of the diagram are called also
crossings
.

Virtual crossings of a virtual diagram are usually drawn circled. The undercrossing of a classical vertex is drawn with a broken line whereas the overcrossing is drawn with a solid line (see Fig.
2
). A diagram without virtual crossings is
classical

Figure 2:

Moves of virtual diagrams
include classical Reidemeister moves (
R1
,
R2
,
R3
) and
detour moves (
DM
)
that replace any diagram arc, which has only virtual crossings, with a new arc, which has the same ends and contains only virtual crossings (see Fig.
3
). An equivalence class of virtual diagram modulo moves is called a
virtual link

[
11
]
.

Figure 3:

A
classical link
is a link which has at least one classical diagram.

Kuperberg
[
13
]
showed that any two classical diagrams that correspond to the same (virtual) link, can be connected by moves so that the intermediate diagrams are classical. By this reason, while working with classical knots and links, we can consider only classical diagrams and classical Reidemeister moves between them.

A
unicursal component
is a minimal set of diagram edges which is closed under passing from an edge to its opposite (at some end of the edge) edge. A diagram with one unicursal component is called a
diagram of a virtual knot
.

A
virtual knot
is an equivalence class of diagrams with one unicursal component.

There are other descriptions of virtual knots.

A

can be viewed as an equivalence class of pairs
(S,D)
where
S
is a closed oriented surface and

is a diagram in

[

If one excludes the stabilization moves she gets a knot theory in a given surface.

Let

be a two-dimensional connected closed oriented surface. A

S
is an equivalence class of diagrams (diagrams with one unicursal component) in
S
modulo diagram isotopies and classical Reidemeister moves
R1
,
R2
,
R3

Links and knots it the sphere
S^{2}
are the classical links and knots.

1.1
Category of knot diagrams

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot. We shall use the notion of ‘knot’ in
one of the following situations:

•

virtual knot;

•

flat knot;

•

free knot;

•

knot in a given surface

•

homotopy class of curves immersed in a given surface;

•

classical knot.

Definition 1
.

The
category of diagrams
\mathfrak{K}
of the knot
\mathcal{K}

is the small category whose objects are the diagrams of
\mathcal{K}
and
morphisms are (formal) compositions
of
elementary morphisms
. By an elementary morphism we mean

•

an isotopy of a diagram;

•

an isomorphism between diagrams (in case of virtual, flat or free knot);

•

a Reidemeister move.

Since the number of vertices of a diagram may change under
Reidemeister moves, there is no bijection between the sets of
vertices of two diagrams connected by a sequence of Reidemeister
moves. To construct any connection between two sets of vertices we
introduce the notion of a partial bijection which means just
the bijection between the subsets of vertices corresponding to each
other in the two diagrams.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two sets. A
partial bijection
between
X
and
Y
is a triple

(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y},\phi)
, where
\widetilde{X}\subset X
,

\widetilde{Y}\subset Y
and
\phi\colon\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{Y}

is a bijection. The subset
dom(\phi)=\widetilde{X}
is the
domain
, and
im(\phi)=\widetilde{Y}
is the
image
of the partial bijection.

Let
G
and
H
be two groups. A
partial isomorphism
between
G
and
H
is a triple

(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{H},\phi)
, where
\widetilde{G}\subset G
,

\widetilde{H}\subset H
are subgroups and
\phi\colon\widetilde{G}\to\widetilde{H}

is an isomorphism.

Sets with partial bijections form a category
\mathfrak{S}_{part}
, and groups with partial isomorphisms form a category
\mathfrak{G}_{part}
. Let
\mathfrak{A}_{part}
be the subcategory of
\mathfrak{G}_{part}
which consists of the abelian groups and partial isomorphisms between them.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be a knot and
\mathfrak{K}
be its diagram category. The
vertex functor
is a
functor
{\mathcal{V}}
from
\mathfrak{K}
to the
\mathfrak{S}_{part}
such that for each diagram

K\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
we define
{\mathcal{V}}(K)
to be the set of crossings of
K
(or chords of
K
if it is a chord diagram). Any elementary morphism

f\colon K\to K^{\prime}
naturally induces a partial bijection
{\mathcal{V}}(f)\colon{\mathcal{V}}(K)\to{\mathcal{V}}(K^{\prime})
. For a morphism
f=f_{1}\circ\cdots\circ f_{n}
, where
f_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
are elementary, the partial bijection
{\mathcal{V}}(f)
is defined as the composition
{\mathcal{V}}(f_{1})\circ\cdots\circ{\mathcal{V}}(f_{n})
.

Below we will use the notation
f_{*}(v)
for
{\mathcal{V}}(f)(v)
,
v\in dom({\mathcal{V}}(f))
, for short.

Remark 1
.

For an elementary morphism
f\colon K\to K^{\prime}
the partial map
{\mathcal{V}}(f)
is a bijection in all cases except a first or a second Reidemeister move. If
f
is an increasing (resp., decreasing) first Reidemeister move then the bijection domain includes
{\mathcal{V}}(K)
and differs from
{\mathcal{V}}(K^{\prime})
by one element (resp., includes
{\mathcal{V}}(K^{\prime})
and differs from
{\mathcal{V}}(K)
by one element). If
f
is a second Reidemeister move the partial bijection
{\mathcal{V}}(f)
embraces all the crossings of the diagrams
K
and
K^{\prime}
except two appearing or disappearing crossings.

1.2
Parities and parity functors

Let
A
be an abelian group. Let us recall the definition of parity
[
7
]
.

Definition 4
.

A
parity
p
on diagrams of a knot
\mathcal{K}
with
coefficients in
A
is a family of maps
p_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to A
,

D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
, such that for any elementary morphism

f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
the following holds:

(P0)

p_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))=p_{D}K(v)
for any
v\in dom(f_{*})
;

(P1)

p_{D}(v)=0
if
f
is the decreasing first Reidemeister move and
v

is the disappearing crossing;

(P2)

p_{D}(v_{1})+p_{D}(v_{2})=0
if
f
is a decreasing second Reidemeister
move and
v_{1},\,v_{2}
are the disappearing crossings;

(P3)

p_{D}(v_{1})+p_{D}(v_{2})+p_{D}(v_{3})=0
if
f
is a third Reidemeister move
and
v_{1},\,v_{2},\,v_{3}
are the crossings participating in this move.

Among examples of parities are the Gaussian parity, the link parity (with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
) of virtual knots and the homological parity of knots in a given surface (with coefficients in the first homology group of the surface)
[
8
]
.

Example 1

(Gaussian parity)
.

Let
D
be a diagram of a knot
\mathcal{K}
and
c\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
be a crossing of
D
. The crossing
c
splits
D
into two halves (see Fig.
11
). The Gaussian parity
gp_{D}(c)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
of
c
is the parity of the number of crossing points on any half.

Figure 11:
Halves of the diagram

Example 2

(Link parity)
.

Let
D
be a diagram of some link
\mathcal{L}
with two components. The crossings of the diagram
D
can be divided into
self-crossings
of a component of
\mathcal{L}
and
mixed crossings
where two different components intersect. One assigns the parity
0
to the self-crossings and the parity
1
to the mixed crossings.

Example 3

(Homological parity)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot in a fixed oriented surface
S
and
D\subset S
be its diagram. Any crossing
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
splits the diagram into left and right halves. This halves are cycles in the surface. The
homological parity
of the crossing
v
is the element
p^{h}_{D}(v)=[D^{l}_{v}]\in H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})/[\mathcal{K}]
where
[\mathcal{K}]
is the homology class of the knot
\mathcal{K}
.

Definition 5
.

A
parity functor
P
on the diagrams of a knot
\mathcal{K}
is a pair
(P,\mathcal{A})
where
\mathcal{A}
is a functor from the category of its diagrams
\mathfrak{K}
to the category
\mathfrak{A}_{part}
, and
P
is a natural map between the vertex functor
{\mathcal{V}}
and the coefficient functor
\mathcal{A}
(in fact, the composition of
\mathcal{A}
with the forgetting functor
\mathfrak{A}_{part}\to\mathfrak{S}_{part}
), i.e. a
set of maps
P_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to\mathcal{A}(D)
for
each diagram
D
, such that for any elementary morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
:

(P0)

for any crossing
v\in dom(f_{*})\subset{\mathcal{V}}(D)
one has
P_{D}(v)\in dom(\mathcal{A}(f))
and
P_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))=\mathcal{A}(f)(P_{D}(v))
;

(P1)

P_{D}(v)=0
if
f
is a decreasing first Reidemeister move and
v

is the disappearing crossing;

(P2)

P_{D}(v_{1})+P_{D}(v_{2})=0
if
f
is a decreasing second Reidemeister
move and
v_{1},\,v_{2}
are the disappearing crossings;

(P3)

P_{D}(v_{1})+P_{D}(v_{2})+P_{D}(v_{3})=0
if
f
is a third Reidemeister move
and
v_{1},\,v_{2},\,v_{3}
are the crossings participating in the move.

Remark 2
.

1. The first condition of the definition above represents the fact
P
is a natural map.

2. Any parity
p
with coefficients in a group
A
is a parity functor with the constant coefficient functor
\mathcal{A}\equiv A
(i.e.
\mathcal{A}(D)=A
for any diagram
D
and
\mathcal{A}(f)=\id_{A}
for any morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
), and maps
P_{D}=p_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to A
,
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
.

Many properties of parities with coefficients can be extended to parity functors. For example, the following statement shows that for parity functors, parities of crossings are defined modulo 2 (cf.
[
7
, Lemma 4.4]
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
(P,\mathcal{A})
be a parity functor. Then
2P_{D}(a)=0
for any diagram
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
and any crossing
a\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
.

Proof.

Apply a second Reidemeister move
f
to the diagram
D
as shown in Fig.
12
. Then
a_{1}=f_{*}(a)
. Since one can apply a decreasing second Reidemeister move to the crossings
a_{1}
and
b_{1}
, we have
P_{D_{1}}(a_{1})+P_{D_{1}}(b_{1})=0
. Apply two second Reidemeister moves and one third Reidemeister move to
D_{1}
and get the diagram
D_{2}
. Then
a_{2}=g_{*}(a_{1})
and
b_{2}=g_{*}(b_{1})
. Then

P_{D_{2}}(a_{2})+P_{D_{2}}(b_{2})=\mathcal{A}(g)(P_{D_{1}}(a_{1}))+\mathcal{A}(g)(P_{D_{1}}(b_{1}))=\mathcal{A}(g)(0)=0.

One can apply third Reidemeister moves to the triples
a_{2},c_{2},d_{2}
and
b_{2},c_{2},d_{2}
. Hence

P_{D_{2}}(a_{2})+P_{D_{2}}(c_{2})+P_{D_{2}}(d_{2})=0
and

P_{D_{2}}(b_{2})+P_{D_{2}}(c_{2})+P_{D_{2}}(d_{2})=0
. Hence,
P_{D_{2}}(a_{2})=P_{D_{2}}(b_{2})

and
2P_{D_{2}}(a_{2})=0
. Since
\mathcal{A}(f)
and
\mathcal{A}(g)
are partial isomorphisms we have
2P_{D_{1}}(a_{1})=0
and
2P_{D}(a)=0
.

2P_{D}(a)=0

∎

In

]
the definition of parity was modified in order to evade the relation proved above.

Definition 6
.

Let
G
be a group (may be noncommutative). Assume that a knot
\mathcal{K}
is oriented, so on the diagrams of the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
the induced orientation is given.

An
oriented parity

p
on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
is a family of maps
p_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to G
,
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
, that possesses the following properties:

(P0)

p_{D}(v)=p_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))
for any elementary morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
and any crossing
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
in the domain of the partial bijection
f_{*}
;

(P1)

if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a decreasing first Reidemeister move and
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
is the disappearing crossing then
p_{D}(v)=1
where
1
is the unit of the coefficient group
G
;

(P2)

p_{D}(v_{1})p_{D}(v_{2})=1
when
f
is a decreasing second Reidemeister move and
v_{1},\,v_{2}
are the disappearing crossings;

(P3+)

if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a third Reidemeister move then

p_{D}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}p_{D}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}p_{D}(v_{3})^{\epsilon(v_{3})}=1

where
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are the vertices involved in the move and
\epsilon(v_{i})
is the incidence index of the vertex
v_{i}
to the disappearing triangle, see Fig.
13
left, and the order of the vertices is induced by the orientation of the surface the diagram lies in.

Figure 13:

In Fig.
13
right one can see an example of a relation for an oriented parity. The motivation example for the property (P3+) is the homotopical parity (see Definition
13
).

Remark 3
.

1. The properties (P1),(P2) and (P3+) can be unified in the following property:
\prod p_{D}(v_{i})^{\epsilon(v_{i})}=1
if
f
is a decreasing first or second Reidemeister move or a third Reidemeister move,
v_{i}
are the crossings which take part in the move and

\epsilon(v_{i})
is the incidence index of the vertex to the disappearing region.

2. Oriented parity does not depend on the orientation of the knot. More precise, if a family of maps
p
is an oriented parity on the diagram category of the knot
\mathcal{K}
then it is an oriented parity on the diagram category of the knot
-\mathcal{K}
which is obtained from
\mathcal{K}
by orientation reversion (we identify naturally the diagram categories of the knots
\mathcal{K}
and
-\mathcal{K}
).

3. Oriented parities include parities with coefficients defined above because of equalities
2p(v)=0
.

4. For free knots, there is no canonical orientation of the triangle in a third Reidemeister move. Therefore, we take commutative coefficient groups when consider oriented parities on free knot.

An example of oriented parity (with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}
) is the index of crossings.

of the diagram at the crossing

. Note that

coincides with the intersection index of
[

Definition 7
.

An
oriented parity functor
P
on the diagrams of an oriented knot
\mathcal{K}
is a pair
(P,\mathcal{G})
where
\mathcal{G}
is a functor from the category of its diagrams
\mathfrak{K}
to the category
\mathfrak{G}_{part}
, and
P
is a natural map between the vertex functor
{\mathcal{V}}
and the coefficient functor
\mathcal{G}
, i.e. a set of maps
P_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D)
for each diagram
D
, such that for any elementary morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
:

(P0)

for any crossing
v\in dom(f_{*})\subset{\mathcal{V}}(D)
one has
P_{D}(v)\in dom(\mathcal{G}(f))
and
P_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))=\mathcal{G}(f)(P_{D}(v))
;

(P1)

P_{D}(v)=1
if
f
is a decreasing first Reidemeister move and
v
is the disappearing crossing;

(P2)

P_{D}(v_{1})P_{D}(v_{2})=1
if
f
is a decreasing second Reidemeister move and
v_{1},\,v_{2}
are the disappearing crossings;

(P3+)

if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is a third Reidemeister move then

P_{D}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}P_{D}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}P_{D}(v_{3})^{\epsilon(v_{3})}=1

where
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
are the vertices involved in the move and ordered counterclockwise, and
\epsilon(v_{i})
is the incidence index of the vertex
v_{i}
in relation with the disappearing triangle.

An oriented parity functor
(P,\mathcal{G})
is called
trivial
if for any
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
and any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)

P_{D}(v)=1
.

Let us consider basic properties of oriented parity functors.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(P,\mathcal{G})
be an oriented parity functor on diagrams of a knot
\mathcal{K}
and
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
be a diagram of
\mathcal{K}
.

1. Assume that crossings
v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
form a bigon (Fig.
15
left). Then
P_{D}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}P_{D}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}=1
where
\epsilon(v_{i})
are the incidence indices to the bigon;
2. Assume that crossings
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
form a triangle (Fig.
15
right). Then
P_{D}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}P_{D}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}P_{D}(v_{3})^{\epsilon(v_{3})}=1
.

Figure 15
:
A bigon and a triangle. The under-overcrossing structure can be any.

Proof.

1. Let
v_{1},v_{2}
be the vertices of a bigon in the diagram
D
. Then
\epsilon(v_{1})=\epsilon(v_{2})
, hence, the equation

P_{D}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}P_{D}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}=1

is equivalent to
P_{D}(v_{1})P_{D}(v_{2})=1
. If a second Reidemeister move can be applied to the bigon, the equality holds due to the property(P2).

If the bigon is alternating then apply a first Reidemeister move
f
as shown in Fig.
16
. With some abuse of notation, we will write
v_{i}
for
f_{*}(v_{i})\in{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
,

. By the properties (P1) and (P3+) we have

P_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}P_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}P_{D^{\prime}}(w)^{\epsilon(w)}=1

and

. Hence,
P_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})^{\epsilon(v_{1})}P_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})^{\epsilon(v_{2})}=1
. The left part is the image of

.

1.3
Universal parity functor

Definition 10
.

An oriented parity functor
(P^{u},\mathcal{G}^{u})
on the diagrams of a knot
\mathcal{K}
is called a
universal parity functor
if for any parity functor
(P,\mathcal{G})
on the diagrams of
\mathcal{K}
there exists a unique family of homomorphisms

\phi_{D}\colon\mathcal{G}^{u}(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D),\ D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K}),

such that
P_{D}=\phi_{D}\circ P^{u}_{D}
for any
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a knot and
\mathfrak{K}
be the category of its diagrams. Then there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) universal parity functor
(P^{u},\mathcal{G}^{u})
on
\mathfrak{K}
.

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from the universal property of the universal parity functor. Let us give a construction of such a functor.

For any
D\in\mathfrak{K}
denote
F(D)=\langle{\mathcal{V}}(D)\rangle
the free group generated by the set of crossings
{\mathcal{V}}(D)
of the diagram
D
. For any
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
define a partial isomophism
\mathcal{F}(f)
from
F(D)
to
F(D^{\prime})
with the domain
\langle dom(f_{*})\rangle\subset F(D)
and the codomain
\langle im(f_{*})\rangle\subset F(D^{\prime})
by the formula
\mathcal{F}(f)(v)=f_{*}(v)
,
v\in dom(f_{*})\subset{\mathcal{V}}(D)
.

Consider a sequence of increasing normal subgroups of
F(D)
defined by induction. Let
H_{0}(D)\lhd F(D)
be the normal subgroup generated by the relations (P1), (P2) and (P3+). Given the subgroups
H_{i}(D)
, we define the subgroup
H_{i+1}(D)
as the normal closure of the set

H_{i}(D)\cup\bigcup_{f\colon D\to D^{\prime}\mbox{ \scriptsize is elementary}}(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(H_{i}(D^{\prime}))

in
F(D)
. Finally, let
H_{\infty}(D)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{i}(D)
and
\mathcal{G}^{u}(D)=F(D)/H_{\infty}(D)
. The map
P^{u}_{D}
is the composition
{\mathcal{V}}(D)\hookrightarrow F(D)\to\mathcal{G}^{u}(D)
. For any elementary morphism
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
the partial isomorphism of
\mathcal{G}^{u}(f)
is induced by the map
\mathcal{F}(f)
.

Let us check the properties of a universal oriented parity functor. The properties (P1),(P2) and (P3+) hold because
H_{0}\subset H_{\infty}=\ker P^{u}_{D}
.

By definition
\bigcup_{f\colon D\to D^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(H_{\infty}(D^{\prime}))\subset H_{\infty}(D)
. In particular, if
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
is an elementary morphism then

(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(H_{\infty}(D^{\prime}))=(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(H_{\infty}(D^{\prime})\cap im(\mathcal{F}(f)))\subset H_{\infty}(D).

Analogously,

(\mathcal{F}(f^{-1}))^{-1}(H_{\infty}(D))=\mathcal{F}(f)(H_{\infty}(D)\cap dom(\mathcal{F}(f)))\subset H_{\infty}(D^{\prime}).

Then
\mathcal{F}(f)
establishes an isomorphisms of groups

H_{\infty}(D)\cap dom(\mathcal{F}(f))
and
H_{\infty}(D^{\prime})\cap im(\mathcal{F}(f))
. Hence, the induced map
\mathcal{G}^{u}(f)
is a well defined partial isomorphism with the domain

dom(\mathcal{G}^{u}(f))=dom(\mathcal{F}(f))/(H_{\infty}(D)\cap dom(\mathcal{F}(f)))

and the codomain

im(\mathcal{G}^{u}(f))=im(\mathcal{F}(f))/(H_{\infty}(D^{\prime})\cap im(\mathcal{F}(f))).

The property (P0) follows from the definition of
\mathcal{F}(f)
.

Let us check the universal property. Let
(P,\mathcal{G})
be an oriented parity functor. For any
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
the map
P_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D)
induces a homomorphism
\tilde{\phi}_{D}\colon F(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D)
. Let
\Pi_{D}=\ker\tilde{\phi}_{D}\subset F(D)
be the kernel of
\tilde{\phi}_{D}
. We need to show that
\tilde{\phi}_{D}
induces a correct homomorphism
\phi_{D}\colon\mathcal{G}^{u}(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D)
, i.e. that
H_{\infty}(D)\subset\Pi(D)
.

Since the parity functor
(P,\mathcal{G})
obeys the properties (P1),(P2),(P3+), the kernel
\Pi(D)
contains
H_{0}(D)
.

Let
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
be an elementary morphism. The property (P0) implies that
\tilde{\phi}_{D}(dom(\mathcal{F}(f)))\subset dom(\mathcal{G}(f))
and
\tilde{\phi}_{D^{\prime}}(im(\mathcal{F}(f)))\subset im(\mathcal{G}(f))
, and
\mathcal{G}(f)\circ\tilde{\phi}_{D}=\tilde{\phi}_{D^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{F}(f)
because
\mathcal{G}(f)
is a partial isomorphism. Then
(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(\Pi(D^{\prime}))\subset\Pi(D)
. Hence,

\bigcup_{f\colon D\to D^{\prime}\mbox{ \scriptsize is elementary}}(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(\Pi(D^{\prime}))\subset\Pi(D).

Assume that
H_{i}(D)\subset\Pi(D)
for all diagrams
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K})
. Then

\bigcup_{f\colon D\to D^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(H_{i}(D^{\prime}))\subset\bigcup_{f\colon D\to D^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F}(f))^{-1}(\Pi(D^{\prime}))\subset\Pi(D).

Therefore,
H_{i+1}(D)\subset\Pi(D)
.
Thus,
H_{i}(D)\subset\Pi(D)
for all
i
, and
H_{\infty}(D)\subset\Pi(D)
.

∎

Definition 11
.

For any crossing

the preimage of the based left half of the diagram at the crossing is a loop in the Gauss diagram that goes along the chord
v
in the direction of the orientation we defined (Fig.
32
).

Let
\tilde{C}=C(D)\cup_{S^{1}}D^{2}
be the cell complex obtained from the Gauss diagram by gluing a disk
D^{2}
along the core circle of the diagram. Let
g
be the map on
\tilde{C}
which contracts the disk to a point. The image of
g
is a bouquet of loops
\bigvee_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(C)}S^{1}_{v}
, and
g
is a homotopy equivalence. The map
h
extends to a homotopy equivalence
h\colon\tilde{C}\to D\cup_{\mathcal{K}}D^{2}
.
Thus, we have an isomorphism of fundamental groups
\pi_{1}(D\cup_{\mathcal{K}}D^{2})\simeq\pi_{1}(\tilde{C})\simeq\pi_{1}(\bigvee_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(C)}S^{1}_{v})=F(D)
. This isomorphism
g_{*}(h_{*})^{-1}
identifies the based left half
\hat{D}^{l}_{v}
of a crossing
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
with the generator
v\in F(D)
.

Consider the cell complex
\tilde{S}=S\cup_{\mathcal{K}}D^{2}
which obtained by the gluing a disk along the knot
\mathcal{K}
to the surface
S
. Then
\pi_{1}(\tilde{S})=\pi_{1}(S)/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle=\Pi
. On the other hand, the complex
\tilde{S}
can be obtained from
D\cup_{\mathcal{K}}D^{2}
by gluing the cells
\pi\in\pi_{0}(S\setminus D)
. Hence,
\pi_{1}(\tilde{S})=\pi_{1}(D\cup_{\mathcal{K}}D^{2})/\langle[\partial\pi]\,|\,\pi\in\pi_{0}(S\setminus D)\rangle
where
\partial\pi
is the based loop for the boundary of the cell
\pi
.

Figure 33:
Cell loop
\pi
corresponds to the word
u^{-1}v^{-1}w=W_{\pi}

The isomorphism
g_{*}(h_{*})^{-1}
maps the homotopy class
[\partial\pi]
to the word
W_{\pi}\in F(D)
(see for example Fig.
33
). Thus,

\Pi=\pi_{1}(\tilde{S})\simeq F(D)/\langle W_{\pi}\,|\,\pi\in\pi_{0}(S\setminus D)\rangle=\mathcal{H}.

∎

Thus, when the complement to a diagram
D
in the surface
S
consists of cells, we have a well defined homomorphism
\phi_{D}\colon\tilde{\Pi}(D)=\Pi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}_{D}\circ\psi^{-1}_{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}(D)
, where for any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)

\phi_{D}(HP_{D}(v))=\phi_{D}([\hat{D}^{l}_{v}])=\tilde{\phi}_{D}(v)=P_{D}(v).

The equality
\tilde{\Pi}(D)=\Pi
follows from the fact the elements
[\hat{D}^{l}_{v}]
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
, generate
\Pi
.

Assume now that
D
is a diagram such that
S\setminus D
contains components with handles. Then one can apply a sequence of increasing second Reidemeister moves
f\colon D\to D^{\prime}
and get a diagram
D^{\prime}
such that
S\setminus D^{\prime}
splits into cells. The map
f_{*}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to{\mathcal{V}}(D^{\prime})
is an embedding, and
\tilde{\Pi}(D)
can be identified with the subgroup in
\tilde{\Pi}(D^{\prime})
generated by the elements
HP_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v)),v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
.

The image
im(\mathcal{G}(f))\subset\mathcal{G}(D^{\prime})
contains elements
P_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v))=\phi_{D^{\prime}}(HP_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v)))
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
. Hence,
\phi_{D^{\prime}}(\tilde{\Pi}(D))\subset im(\mathcal{G}(f))
. Since
\mathcal{G}(f)
is invertible on
im(\mathcal{G}(f))
, we can define a homomorphism
\phi_{D}\colon\tilde{\Pi}(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D)
as the composition
\mathcal{G}(f)^{-1}\circ\phi_{D^{\prime}}
. By definition, for any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
we have

\phi_{D}(HP_{D}(v))=\mathcal{G}(f)^{-1}(P_{D^{\prime}}(f_{*}(v)))=P_{D}(v).

Thus, for any parity functor
(P,\mathcal{G})
we have constructed a homomorphism from the homotopical parity functor to
(P,\mathcal{G})
. Since the homotopical functor is reduced, the homomorphism is unique. Theorem
4
is proved.
∎

Remark 8
.

Let
z,z^{\prime}\in S
be two points and
D
be a diagram of the knot
\mathcal{K}
which contains
z
and
z^{\prime}
as points but not as crossings. Then
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K}_{z})\cap Ob(\mathfrak{K}_{z^{\prime}})
. The coefficient groups
\Pi_{z}=\pi_{1}(S,z)/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle
and
\Pi_{z^{\prime}}=\pi_{1}(S,z^{\prime})/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle
are isomorphic (by the conjugation with the arc
zz^{\prime}
of
D
), and the homotopical parity values
hp_{D}(v)
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)
, are identified by this isomorphism. This means that the kernels of the homomorphisms from
F(D)=\langle{\mathcal{V}}(D)\rangle
to
\Pi_{z}
and
\Pi_{z^{\prime}}
coincide and the canonical reductions
(\tilde{\Pi}_{z})_{D}
and
(\tilde{\Pi}_{z^{\prime}})_{D}
are the same factor-group of
F(D)
. Hence, the canonical reduction of the homotopical parity does not depend on the choice of the base point.

Thus, the homotopical parity functor
(HP,\tilde{\Pi})
can be uniquely extended to the category
\mathfrak{K}
of all diagrams of the knot
\mathcal{K}
(without fixing a basepoint). This oriented parity functor is universal.

Corollary 3
.

The homotopical parity
hp
is universal oriented parity on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}_{z}
.

Proof.

The statement follows from Theorem
4
, Propostions
3
,
4
, and the fact the homotopy classes of based halves
\hat{D}^{l}_{v}
of crossings
v
of knot diagrams
D
generate the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S,z)
.
∎

Let
H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})
be the
1
-dimensional integral homology group and
H=H_{1}(S,{\mathbb{Z}})/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle
be the factor-group by the homology class of the knot
\mathcal{K}
. There is a natural homomorphism
\rho
from
\Pi=\pi_{1}(S,z)/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle
to
H
. The compositions
\overline{hp}_{D}=\rho\circ hp_{D}\colon{\mathcal{V}}(D)\to H
,
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K}_{z})
, define an oriented parity with coefficients in the group
H
, called the
homological parity
on diagrams of the knot
\mathcal{K}
in the surface
S
.

Corollary 4
.

The homological parity
\overline{hp}
is the universal parity among all oriented parities with coefficients in abelian groups on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}_{z}
. In other word, for any oriented parity
p
with coefficients in an abelian group
A
there is a unique homomorphism
\psi\colon H\to A
such that
p_{D}=\psi\circ\overline{hp}_{D}
for all
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K}_{z})
.

Proof.

By universality of the homotopical parity there is a unique homomorphism
\tilde{\psi}\colon\Pi\to A
such that
p_{D}=\tilde{\psi}\circ hp_{D}
for all
D
. Since
H=\Pi/[\Pi,\Pi]
is the abelinization of the group
\Pi
and
A
is abelian, there is a unique homomorphism
\psi
such that
\tilde{\psi}=\psi\circ\rho
. Then
p_{D}=\psi\circ\rho\circ hp_{D}=\psi\circ\overline{hp}_{D}
.
∎

Note that the homology does not depend on a base point. Therefore, the homological parity
\overline{hp}
can be considered as an oriented parity on the category
\mathfrak{K}
of all diagrams of
\mathcal{K}
in
S
. The parity is universal among all oriented parities on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in an abelian group.

If
\mathcal{K}
is a classical knot, i.e. knot on the sphere
S^{2}
we get the following statement (cf.
[
8
, Corollary 3.3]
). It shows that generalization from parities to oriented parity functors is unproductive for classical knots.

Corollary 5
.

Any oriented parity functor on diagrams of a classical knot is trivial.

Proof.

Let
(P,\mathcal{G})
be an oriented parity functor. By universality, there is a family of homomorphisms
\psi_{D}\colon\tilde{\Pi}(D)\to\mathcal{G}(D)
,
D\in Ob(\mathfrak{K}_{z})
from the homotopic parity
(HP,\tilde{\Pi})
. Since the group
\tilde{\Pi}(D)
is identified with a subgroup in the trivial group
\pi_{1}(S^{2},z)/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle=\{1\}
, for any diagram
D
and any crossing
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)

HP_{D}(v)=1
and
P_{D}(v)=\psi_{D}(HP_{D}(v))=1
.
∎

4
Linking number invariant

Recall that for an oriented two-component link
L=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
its linking number is the sum

lk(L)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{c\in K_{1}\cap K_{2}}sgn(c)

of the signs of the crossings where two different components intersect. Using the link parity
lp
from Example
2
we can rewrite the definition as follows

lk(L)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{c\in{\mathcal{V}}(L)\colon lp_{L}(c)=1}sgn(c).

The last formula admits a broad generalization. If there is a parity on the diagram category, one can assign a linking number to each nonzero parity value.

Definition 15
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a virtual knot or a knot in a surface,
\mathfrak{K}
be its diagram category, and
p
be an oriented parity on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a group
G
. Given a diagram
D
of
\mathcal{K}
, we define the
linking invariant
as the element

lk^{p}(\mathcal{K})=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}sgn(v)\cdot p_{D}(v)^{sgn(v)}-w(D)\cdot 1\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G]

in the group algebra of
G
. Here
w(D)=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)}sgn(v)
is the writhe of the diagram
D
.

For any
g\in G
,
g\neq 1
, the coefficient
lk^{p}_{g}(\mathcal{K})=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\colon p_{D}(v)^{sgn(v)}=g}sgn(v)
is called the
linking number
of the knot
\mathcal{K}
at the parity value
g
.

The independence of
lk^{p}(\mathcal{K})
on the choice of the diagram
D
follows directly from the properties (P0),(P1),(P2).

In fact, the linking invariant can be considered as an example of virtual knot invariants called
index polynomials

[
2
,
9
,
12
]
.
An index polynomial can be defined whenever one has a chord index
[
3
]
on the diagrams. For any oriented parity
p
the value
p_{D}(v)^{sgn(v)}
,
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D),D\in\mathfrak{K}
, is an example of such a chord index.

Let
(P,\mathcal{G})
be an oriented parity functor on the diagram category
\mathfrak{K}
. For any diagram
D
the linking invariant
lk^{P}(D)
is an element of the algebra
{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathcal{G}(D)]
. In general case, there is no canonical way to identify the groups
\mathcal{G}(D)
. We have to weaken the linking invariant to get rid of dependence on coefficients groups.

Definition 16
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a virtual knot or a knot in a surface,
\mathfrak{K}
be its diagram category, and
(P,\mathcal{G})
be an oriented parity functor on
\mathfrak{K}
. For any diagram
D
of
\mathcal{K}
, define the
linking multiset
by the formula

LS^{P}(D)=\{lk^{P}_{g}(D)\,|\,g\in\mathcal{G}(D),g\neq 1\}.

One can consider the linking multiset as an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}]
:

LS^{P}(D)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\{g\in\mathcal{G}(D)\setminus\{1\}\,|\,lk^{P}_{g}(D)=k\}\right|\cdot\boldsymbol{k}.

The invariance of the linking multiset
LS^{P}(\mathcal{K})
follows from the invariance of the linking invariant.

Example 5
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the virtual trefoil considered as a knot in the torus (Fig.
34
). Then the homology class of
\mathcal{K}
is
2\lambda+\mu
where
\lambda
is the longitude and
\mu
is the meridian of the torus. Hence, the coefficient group of the homological parity is

\mathcal{H}=H_{1}(T^{2},{\mathbb{Z}})/\langle[\mathcal{K}]\rangle={\mathbb{Z}}\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle/\langle 2\lambda+\mu\rangle\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\lambda.

The homological parity of the crossings is
hp(u)=\lambda
,
hp(v)=\lambda+\mu=-\lambda
. The linking invariant is equal to
lk^{hp}(\mathcal{K})=(-1)\cdot[(-1)\lambda]+1\cdot[1\cdot(-\lambda)]=2\cdot(-\lambda)
. The linking multiset is
LK^{hp}(\mathcal{K})=\{2\}=1\cdot\mathbf{2}
.

Virtual trefoil

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a flat or free knot or an immersed curve in a fixed surface. In this case we can not use signs of crossings and lose about a half information on linking numbers.

Definition 17
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a flat or free knot or an immersed curve in a fixed surface,
\mathfrak{K}
be its diagram category, and
p
be an oriented parity on
\mathfrak{K}
with coefficients in a group
G
. Let
G_{inv}=\{g\in G\,|\,g^{2}=1,g\neq 1\}
be the set of involutions in
G
, and

G_{ni}=G\setminus(G_{inv}\cup\{1\})
be the set of elements of order greater then
2
. Let
D
be a diagram of the knot
\mathcal{K}
. Define the
linking invariant
of the knot
\mathcal{K}
as the pair
lk^{p}(\mathcal{K})=(lk^{p}_{inv}(\mathcal{K}),lk^{p}_{ni}(\mathcal{K}))
where

lk^{p}_{inv}(\mathcal{K})=\sum_{g\in G_{inv}}|\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\,|,\ p_{D}(v)=g\}|\cdot g\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}[G_{inv}],

lk^{p}_{ni}(\mathcal{K})=\\
\sum_{g\in G_{ni}}\left(|\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\,|,\ p_{D}(v)=g\}|-|\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(D)\,|,\ p_{D}(v)=g^{-1}\}|\right)\cdot g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[G_{ni}].

The element
lk^{p}_{inv}(\mathcal{K})
is called the
involutive part of the linking invariant
, and
lk^{p}_{ni}(\mathcal{K})
is the
non involutive part
.

For an element
g\in G
,
g\neq 1
, the linking number
lk^{p}_{g}(\mathcal{K})
of the knot
\mathcal{K}
at the parity value
g
is the coefficient of the linking invariant at
g
, i.e.
lk^{p}_{g}(\mathcal{K})=|p_{D}^{-1}(g)|\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
for
g\in G_{inv}
, and

lk^{p}_{g}(\mathcal{K})=|p_{D}^{-1}(g)|-|p_{D}^{-1}(g^{-1})|
for
g\in G_{ni}
.

Note that
lk^{p}_{g^{-1}}(\mathcal{K})=-lk^{p}_{g}(\mathcal{K})
for any
g\neq 1
.

Proposition 7
.

The linking invariant
lk^{p}
and the linking numbers
lk^{p}_{g}
,
g\in G\setminus\{1\}
, do not depend on the choice of the diagram
D
.

Proof.

The only nontrivial part is invariance under the second Reidemeister move. Let a diagram
D^{\prime}
be obtained from
D
by an increasing second Reidemeister move, and
v_{1},v_{2}
be the new two crossings in
D^{\prime}
. Let
p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{1})=g
,
g\in G
. Then
p_{D^{\prime}}(v_{2})=g^{-1}
. By the property (P0)
lk^{p}_{g^{\prime}}(D^{\prime})=lk^{p}_{g^{\prime}}(D)
for all
g^{\prime}\neq g,g^{-1}
. If
g=g^{-1}\in G_{inv}
then
lk^{p}_{g}(D^{\prime})=lk^{p}_{g}(D)+2=lk^{p}_{g}(D)
. If
g\in G_{ni}
then
lk^{p}_{g}(D^{\prime})=lk^{p}_{g}(D)+1-1=lk^{p}_{g}(D)
, analogously,
lk^{p}_{g^{-1}}(D^{\prime})=lk^{p}_{g^{-1}}(D)
. Thus, the linking numbers are invariant.
Then
lk^{p}(D)=lk^{p}(D^{\prime})
.
∎

Denote
\bar{G}_{ni}=G_{ni}/\sigma
where
\sigma(g)=g^{-1}
,
g\in G_{ni}
. Then for any
x\in\bar{G}_{ni}
,
x=\{g,g^{-1}\}
, the value
lk^{p}_{x}(D)=|lk^{p}_{g}(D)|=|lk^{p}_{g^{-1}}(D)|
is well defined.

Definition 18
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a flat or free knot or an immersed curve in a fixed surface,
\mathfrak{K}
be its diagram category, and
(P,\mathcal{G})
be an oriented parity functor on
\mathfrak{K}
. For any diagram
D
of
\mathcal{K}
, the
involutive linking multiset
is
LS^{P}_{inv}(D)=\{lk^{P}_{g}(D)\,|\,g\in\mathcal{G}(D)_{inv}\}
, and the
non-involutive linking multiset
is
LS^{P}_{ni}(D)=\{lk^{P}_{x}(D)\,|\,x\in\overline{\mathcal{G}(D)}_{ni}\}
. In other words, one can consider the linking multiset as an element of :

\displaystyle LS^{P}_{inv}(D)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}}\left|\{g\in\mathcal{G}(D)_{inv}\,|\,lk^{P}_{g}(D)=k\}\right|\cdot{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}],

\displaystyle LS^{P}_{ni}(D)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}},k\geq 0}\left|\{x\in\overline{\mathcal{G}(D)}_{ni}\,|\,lk^{P}_{x}(D)=k\}\right|\cdot{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}].

.

). The involutive linking multiset is trivial

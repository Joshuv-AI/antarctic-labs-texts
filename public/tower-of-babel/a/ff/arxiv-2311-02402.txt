Hybrid quantum image classification and federated learning for hepatic steatosis diagnosis Preprint: APS/123-QED
Luca Lusnig, Asel Sagingalieva, Mikhail Surmach, Tatjana Protasevich, Ovidiu Michiu, Joseph McLoughlin, Christopher Mansell, Graziano de’ Petris, Deborah Bonazza, Fabrizio Zanconati, Alexey Melnikov, Fabio Cavalli

Abstract
With the maturity achieved by deep learning techniques, intelligent systems that can assist physicians in the daily interpretation of clinical images can play a very important role. In addition, quantum techniques applied to deep learning can enhance this performance, and federated learning techniques can realize privacy-friendly collaborative learning among different participants, solving privacy issues due to the use of sensitive data and reducing the number of data to be collected for each individual participant. We present in this study a hybrid quantum neural network that can be used to quantify non-alcoholic liver steatosis and could be useful in the diagnostic process to determine a liver’s suitability for transplantation; at the same time, we propose a federated learning approach based on a classical deep learning solution to solve the same problem, but using a reduced data set in each part. The liver steatosis image classification accuracy of the hybrid quantum neural network, the hybrid quantum ResNet model, consisted of 5 5 qubits and more than 100 100 variational gates, reaches 97 % 97\% , which is 1.8 % 1.8\% higher than its classical counterpart, ResNet, although the number of weights in the hybrid quantum model is 1.75 1.75 times less than in the classical one. Crucially, that even with a reduced dataset (trimmed by more than half), our hybrid approach consistently outperformed its classical counterpart, indicating superior generalization and less potential for overfitting in medical applications. In addition, a federated approach with multiple clients, up to 32 32 , despite the lower accuracy, but still higher than 90 % 90\% , would allow using, for each participant, a very small dataset, i.e., up to one-thirtieth. Our work, based over real-word clinical data can be regarded as a scalable and collaborative starting point, could thus fulfill the need for an effective and reliable computer-assisted system that facilitates the daily diagnostic work of the clinical pathologist.

Introduction

In recent years, the application of Convolutional Neural Networks
LeCun et al. 1999
(CNNs) has showed excellent results in a wide range of fields related to pattern recognition, from image processing to document classification.
CNNs consist of two blocks, the convolutional block used to extract image features that is connected to a block consisting of one or more fully connected layers, typical of neural networks
Rosenblatt 1963

In the evolving landscape of machine learning, Quantum Machine Learning (QML) has emerged as a promising avenue, intertwining quantum computing principles with classical machine learning techniques. Incorporating quantum layers, such as Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Networks (HQNNs)
Schuld et al. 2017
;
Kordzanganeh et al. 2023a

Using these algorithms, it is possible to surpass the accuracy levels achieved by a human expert. However, training them requires a substantial amount of data collection. In cases where this data involves sensitive information, privacy, security, and data integrity become significant concerns that need to be addressed.

The federated learning (FL) remedies these issues. FL, is a machine learning setting where many clients collaboratively train a model under the orchestration of a central server, while keeping the training data decentralized.
It is potentially usable in any field where it is important to share knowledge without sharing data (e.g., when it is necessary to comply with the GDPR
European Parliament and Council of the European Union a

This system also complies with the requirements of the Council Recommendation on Artificial Intelligence

Italian Data protection Authority

The European Union is about to adopt the world’s first law regulating artificial intelligence (AI Law)

Once the legal principle for the usability of the data has been identified, FL overcomes any legal problem related to the definition of privacy relationships between the participants in a multicentric analysis activity.

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)

Steatosis can be defined as an abnormal accumulation of fat within the liver cell and can occur in a various liver diseases and its clinical implications generally correlate with its extent. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is a chronic disorder of relative recent identification but in considerable and continuous epidemiological growth. The afflicted population is estimated about 1 billion people worldwide

The natural history of NAFLD progresses from steatosis, steatohepatitis, fibrosis, cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma

Histologically, steatosis is evaluated in paraffin-embedded sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE) and is visible as white droplets in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes. The fat dissolves during histological processing and droplets appear as gaps in the tissue. Droplets of fat can be distinguished from other empty spaces, such as vessels or tissue artifacts, by their size and rounded shape (Fig

Liver biopsy and histological classification is the gold standard to diagnose hepatic steatosis. It also allows the identification of other histological features such as steatosis zonality, droplet size (macrovesicular vs. microvesicular), inflammation, cellular injury, iron overload and fibrosis staging. Kleiner et al.

Federated learning

Federated learning
McMahan et al. 2017
is a machine learning technique to achieve privacy-preserving collaborative learning among different parties. Compared with traditional centralized and distributed learning, which require data to be collected in a single compute node the former and in a server to parallelize over multiple nodes the computation and thus speed up the training phase the latter, in federated learning only locally trained models are exchanged, without sharing the data with the consequence of guaranteeing privacy.

In addition, we can improve data security and privacy by using privacy-enhancing technologies (PET)
Kaaniche et al. 2020
, which are developed to meet the basic principles of data protection, minimizing the use of personal data and maximizing data security.

The two most widely used techniques are Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE)
Rivest and Dertouzos 1978
and the Secure Multi-Party computation (SMPC)
Zhao et al. 2019
. FHE is a key-based encryption in which the encryption process preserves algebraic operations, in this technique a third party can calculate on the data without knowing it. In addition to depending on a trusted third party, they do not provide perfect security and therefore could represent a lack of security when used in FL. To solve this problem, Liang at all. have proposed a symmetric quantum version of FHE
Liang 2013
that guarantees perfect privacy by making such an encryption technique safe.
The SMPC technique is a cryptographic protocol that distributes a computation across multiple parties where no individual party can see the other parties data. This approach does not need a trusted third party to see the data and in addition, the data are encrypted in-use because they are divided and distributed among the players during calculation, which makes it secure against quantum attacks (for now). On the other hand there is an high communication costs between players because the computational overhead due the use of random numbers which must be generated in order to ensure the security of the computation.

Chip-to-chip communication can be used to speed up the communication between the devices and the central server. This is because chip-to-chip wireless communication is typically much faster that traditional wired communication. Additionally, chip-to-chip wireless communication can reduce the power consumption of the devices. Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express is a new interconnect standard that is designed to enable high-performance, low-power communication between chiplets. It has the potential to revolutionise the way that federated learning systems are implemented. UCIe

can be used to connect chiplets that responsible for different parts of the federated learning process, such as data aggregation, model training, and inference. This can help to improve the performance and scalability of federated learning systems. One of the challenges of federated learning is that it can be slow to train a model, especially if there is a lot of data or if the devices are not very powerful. UCIe can help to address this challenge by providing a high-speed, low-latency interconnect between devices. This can allow the device to train the model more quickly and efficiently.

I.4
Hybrid quantum image classification

We address the classification of liver steatosis types by harnessing the capabilities of transfer learning. Transfer learning, a potent strategy in neural network training, allows expertise gained from one problem to bolster performance on another, related problem
Neyshabur et al. 2020
.

For our classification task, we deploy the ResNet (Residual Neural Network) model, pretrained on the expansive ImageNet dataset
Ima 2022
;
He et al. 2015
. Training deep neural networks is often plagued by the vanishing gradient issue, but ResNet effectively navigates this with its innovative residual blocks. These blocks enable deeper layers to retain essential input data information by directly passing inputs to subsequent layers within the block. Owing to this design, ResNet has cemented its reputation as a formidable tool for image classification tasks. Specifically, we employ ResNet18 via PyTorch
PyT 2022
, with “18” indicating the network’s depth in terms of layers.

As illustrated in Fig.
1
(d), subsequent to the ResNet architecture, we add a fully-connected layer. Each neuron in this layer aligns with a distinct class in our binary classification objective.

Our proposed hybrid quantum model called hybrid quantum ResNet, inspired by
Sagingalieva et al. 2023b
is depicted in Fig.
1
(c).
Upon the completion of processing through ResNet, the hybrid architecture includes both classical and quantum layers. Given that the output feature map from ResNet encompasses
1000
distinct features, the subsequent fully-connected layers incorporates these
1000
input neurons, producing
100
output features.

Following the classical processing, these output features are channeled into the quantum domain via the Quantum Depth-Infused (QDI) layer
Sagingalieva et al. 2023a
. Remarkably, the QDI structure efficiently processes numerous features
100
in our model with the utilization of a mere
5
qubits. This efficiency proves crucial, especially in the Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) era.

The architecture of the QDI layer begins with several variational layers, succeeded by
20
data re-uploading layers, each embedded with a variational layer. The total number of variational gates is more than
100
. Subsequent to these, measurements are taken from all qubits, converting the quantum data into a classical vector. This vector is then fed into a final classical fully-connected layer, which discerns and predicts the corresponding output class.

The whole dataset contains
4400

I.5
Federated learning for medical privacy

This section describes the architecture and the strategy as well as the dataset used in the federated learning solution.

For implementing the federated learning solution, we use the Flower framework
Beutel et al. 2020
which is open source and provides support for extending FL implementations to mobile and wireless clients, with heterogeneous compute, memory, and network resources. In addition it is released under Apache 2.0 license, making it a perfect candidate for integrating a quantum machine learning library.

The proposed architecture is of the horizontal federated learning type, in which each client trains its own model on its own device and then sends its parameters to the server, which after aggregating them sends a new model to the clients. The aggregation strategy used is the vanilla federated average
McMahan et al. 2017
strategy, in which all clients were used in both the training and validation phases.

The data set was balanced where the number of samples available for each client was the same and equally divided between the two classes, and no samples were used by two different clients.

In all simulations, the common model to be trained is the same as in the centralised case and in order to avoid overfitting in the training phase in each client the number of training epochs was less than those used in the centralised case. Specifically, 15 rounds were carried out at server level and 1 epoch locally.

As in the centralised case, we performed cross-validation by dividing the dataset into
2
parts: training and testing in a ratio of
8
to
2
, resulting in
5

Discussion

Although noninvasive systems for diagnosis and staging of NALFD are being studied and tested
Reinshagen et al. 2023
, the visual analysis of histological sections on liver tissue samples obtained by biopsy or resection is still considered the gold standard. The histological analysis of steatosis is performed routinely to diagnose NAFLD or to decide about the suitability of liver grafts for transplantation. In addition, because NALFD can be associated with other diseases by increasing their risk
Ko et al. 2023
, liver histological analysis plays a very important role in research and clinical settings.

There are previous works that used deep learning and NAFLD histology images, but these studies have taken into account animal tissues
Heinemann et al. 2019
;
Yang et al. 2019
. Animal models of liver disease are required, but they cannot reproduce the complexity of the human liver. Tissue samples analysis from diagnostic biopsies can be more difficult compared to animal biopsies
performed during controlled experiments. Indeed, in human tissue there are many etiopathogenic factors (e.g. nutrition, drugs, viral infections) present in different combination, which are difficult to quantify.

Our work is original for a number of reasons. First, the possibility of automating the classification of NALFD by means of a reliable automated system that we have based on human tissue images and real clinical data. This work, which can be regarded as a scalable and collaborative starting point, could thus fulfill the need for an effective and reliable computer-assisted system that facilitates the daily diagnostic work of the clinical pathologist.

In this paper, we present a HQNN, hybrid quantum ResNet, and its classical counterpart, ResNet, to determine the presence of a steatosis rate greater than or less than
33\%
. The presence of mild steatosis is a particularly important parameter that must be considered in liver transplantation. There is general agreement that mild steatosis (<30%) causes minor graft injury
McCormack et al. 2011
.

Both models are robust, as they were trained on a dataset of
4000
samples, and resulted in high classification accuracy, consistently above
95\%
, higher than that of an experienced pathologist. In addition, the level of false negatives, defined as nontransplantable samples recognized as transplantable, also has a rate comparable to that of an experienced pathologist, which is <5%.

The hybrid quantum model is a ResNet with the addition of a QDI layer, in which there are
5
qubits and over
100
variational gates. The HQNN achieved an accuracy of
97\%
with less than
3\%
false negatives, which is very important for medical purposes. The accuracy of the classical analogue of this model is
1.8\%
less, although the number of weights in the classical model is
1.75
times greater than in the hybrid one.

More accurate classification predictions even on a reduced dataset of a hybrid model indicate its better generalization ability and less overfitting compared to its classical counterpart.

A further innovation is the use of a federated learning approach based on our classical ResNet model in order to enable collaboration among different clients (up to
32
) by obtaining results comparable to those of an experienced pathologist (>90%) using a reduced dataset (up to
125
samples) while ensuring the privacy and security of the dataset itself. In our federated learning case, datasets in different clients were labeled by the same group of experts. In a real-world scenario, each client will have its own group of experts labeling the dataset reducing the possibility of error due to possible misclassification provided by a single group of experts resulting in a likely increase in the level of accuracy of the system.

Our model
seems
to exceed the results of an expert pathologist, but it still relies on data labeled by the expert pathologist himself, so it is currently still dependent on an external actor. To overcome this problem, the model can be updated in the future by relying on a new dataset labeled “ground truth” that can be created by the user(s) after clinical and instrumental feedback on the correctness of the diagnosis and classification.

Future works

Our federated model relies on conditions such as a balanced database among clients and no client failures situation not likely in the case of its use in production. To make our model more robust and fault-tolerant, we began a study based on a decentralized aggregation approach and using unbalanced datasets.

In addition, the promising results of the hybrid quantum solution and the improvements in the field of encryption due to quantum techniques as well as the lack of a reliable quantum federated learning framework lead us to focus our future studies on creating a masked quantum federated learning framework where quantum PET techniques are combined with a quantum federated learning framework.

Optical interconnections for federated learning is an exciting direction for this work as it can be a bridge between classical/quantum interconnect using wireless technology. Wavelength division multiplexing (WDM)
Labs 2021
networks can improve the performance and scalability of federated networks. WDM networks can be used to connect the devices in a federated network and to provide high-bandwidth, low-latency communication between devices. This can improve the performance of federated learning applications, which require fast communication between the devices and the central server.

Wireless interconnects provide an energy-efficient, scalable, and unified solution for interconnecting heterogeneous multi-chip systems with multicore processors, and in-package memory modules. Multi gigabits-per-second wireless interconnects through on-chip antennas are a reality in the backdrop of 5G communication and Internet-of-Things. Using a combination of omnidirectional and directional wireless links to provide interference-free communication among all the chips. Using directional dual-band on-chip antennas for a wireless interconnection architecture that establishes direct wireless links between processor and memory banks.

Wireless links operating at radio frequencies, establish uniform interconnection between chips operating at any voltage/frequency or communication protocols where each processing chip and memory module in the system is equipped with at least one wireless transceiver to enable inter-chip communication.

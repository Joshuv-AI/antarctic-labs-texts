The electroweak magnetic monopole in the presence of KSVZ axion
Tong Li, Rui-Jia Zhang

Abstract
The Witten effect implies the dynamics of axion and magnetic monopole. The Cho-Maison monopole is a realistic electroweak monopole arisen in the Weinberg-Salam theory. This monopole of TeV scale mass motivates the dedicated search for electroweak monopole at colliders. In this work we investigate the implication of KSVZ axion to the electroweak magnetic monopole. We use the spherically symmetric ansatz for the electroweak dyon and introduce the spherically symmetric function for the axion field. The effective Lagrangian is then shown in terms of the electroweak monopole part, the axion kinetic energy as well as the axion interaction term. We derive the consequent equations of motion in the presence of the axion-photon coupling and show the numerical results of the topological solutions. We then calculate the changed characteristics of the electroweak monopole such as the monopole mass and the electromagnetic charges, as well as the axion potential energy.



I
Introduction

The axion and magnetic monopole are two of the longstanding and interesting candidates of physics beyond the standard model (SM). The pseudo-Goldstone boson axion was proposed to solve the strong CP problem in quantum chromodynamics (QCD), as a result of the spontaneous breaking of a QCD anomalous Peccei-Quinn (PQ) global symmetry
U(1)_{\rm PQ}

Peccei and Quinn 1977a
;
Peccei and Quinn 1977b
;
Weinberg 1978
;
Wilczek 1978
. The chiral transformation of the quark fields with PQ charges leads to the anomaly under quantum
electrodynamics (QED) and the coupling between the axion field
a
and electromagnetic fields

\displaystyle{1\over 4}g_{a\gamma\gamma}aF_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=g_{a\gamma\gamma}a\vec{E}\cdot\vec{B}\;.

(1)

The QCD axion and axion-like particles (ALPs) can also play as dark matter through the misalignment mechanism
Preskill et al. 1983
;
Dine and Fischler 1983
. The theory of axion and the detection of axion couplings have received a wide interest in both theoretical and experimental
aspects (see Refs.
Di Luzio et al. 2020
;
Sikivie 2021
for recent reviews).

P. Dirac first suggested the existence of
magnetic monopole in quantum theory in 1931
Dirac 1931
. It can be constructed from a non-singular Abelian theory
Wu and Yang 1975
or can arise from the
spontaneous breaking of non-Abelian gauge symmetries, resulting to monopole in grand unification theory (GUT)
’t Hooft 1974
;
Polyakov 1974
.
Besides GUT monopole, the existence of electroweak monopole in particular gains much interest and attention
Cho and Maison 1997
;
Hung 2021
;
Alexandre and Mavromatos 2019
;
Ellis et al. 2016
;
Lazarides and Shafi 2021
. Cho and Maison showed that the Weinberg-Salam model has a topology of magnetic monopole and there does exist a new type of dyon solutions in the SM
Cho and Maison 1997
(see Ref.
Cho 2019
for a recent review). This electroweak generalization of the Dirac monopole comes from the non-trivial topology of the SM. The
SU(2)
of the Weinberg-Salam model has the same non-Abelian monopole topology as the Georgi-Glashow model. The
U(1)_{Y}
part has the Abelian monopole topology. The Cho-Maison monopole (CMM) is thus a hybrid of ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopole and Dirac monopole. Its magnetic charge is twice that of the Dirac monopole and the monopole mass is of the order of

\displaystyle{4\pi\over e^{2}}M_{W}\simeq 11~{\rm TeV}\;,

(2)

where
e
is the elementary electric charge and
M_{W}
is the
W
boson mass.
This low mass motives the detection of such realistic monopole at the ATLAS
Aad et al. 2020
or MoEDAL
Acharya et al. 2019
detector of LHC or future high-energy upgrades. The electroweak monopole also leads to various impacts on cosmology, such as the primordial magnetic black holes and dark matter.

In 1979, E. Witten pointed out that a CP violating term in the non-Abelian
SO(3)
theory provides an additional electric charge for the ’t Hooft-Polyakov monopoles in this theory
Witten 1979
. The generic value of electric charge
q_{e}
becomes

\displaystyle q_{e}=e\Big(n_{e}+{\theta\over 2\pi}n_{m}\Big)\;,

(3)

where
n_{e}~(n_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}
is the electric (magnetic) quantum number, and
\theta
denotes a CP violating parameter.
When substituting the parameter
\theta
by the axion field, this so-called Witten effect built the close relationship between axion and magnetic monopole. W. Fischler et al. then derived the dyon-axion dynamics under the classical electromagnetism in 1983
Fischler and Preskill 1983
. There are many recent studies discussing the possible modification of standard axion electrodynamics inspired by Witten effect in both theory
Sokolov and Ringwald 2022
;
Sokolov and Ringwald 2023
;
Heidenreich et al. 2023
and phenomenology
Li et al. 2023a
;
Tobar et al. 2022
;
McAllister et al. 2022
;
Li et al. 2023b
;
Li and Zhang 2023
;
Tobar et al. 2023
;
Patkos 2023
. There also exist some open questions in the axion-magnetic monopole aspect
Agrawal et al. 2022
.

What is then the implication of axion to the electroweak monopole? In this work we try to make the first attempt. We explore the solution of Cho-Maison electroweak monopole in the benchmark model of axion, i.e. the Kim-Shifman-Vainshtein-Zakharov (KSVZ) model
Kim 1979
;
Shifman et al. 1980
. Besides axion interactions at low energies, the content of KSVZ model is exactly the same as the SM. We are able to construct the topological solution of the electroweak monopole in the SM. More importantly, inspired by the Witten effect, the characteristic features of electroweak monopole would be changed in light of the interaction of axion and electromagnetic field. For instance, the axion-photon coupling would induce additional electric charge for the monopole and modify the monopole mass.
The monopole background would also confine the axion potential. We will concentrate on the solution of spherically symmetrical function of axion field in a background magnetic field. The contributions of axion to the properties of electroweak monopole will be studied in details. These changes have impacts on both the existence of axion and the search for electroweak monopole.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
we overview the benchmark axion model and the electroweak monopole called Cho-Maison monopole. The topological solutions of electroweak monopole and axion field will be explored in Sec.
III
. In Sec.
IV
we discuss the impact of axion on the properties of electroweak monopole. Our conclusions are drawn in Sec.
V
.

II
The KSVZ axion model and the electroweak monopole

We first overview the KSVZ axion model and the electroweak monopole in the SM called Cho-Maison monopole.
The Lagrangian for the KSVZ model is

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{L}_{\rm KSVZ}={\alpha_{s}N\over 4\pi}{a\over v_{a}}G^{a}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{G}^{a\mu\nu}+{\alpha E\over 4\pi}{a\over v_{a}}F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\;,

\epsilon_{0123}=1

. Throughout the paper, we only consider the second coupling between axion and the physical photon. Taking into account both the quark kinetic term and the quark mass operator, the axion-photon coupling can be given as

\displaystyle{1\over 4}g_{a\gamma\gamma}aF_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}

Next we show the ansatz for the spherically symmetry solution of the Cho-Maison monopole in the KSVZ model with the effective Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}W_{\mu\nu}^{a}W^{a\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}+|D_{\mu}\Phi|^{2}+|\partial_{\mu}\phi|^{2}

The authors of Refs.
Cho and Maison 1997
;
Cho 2019
make use of the gauge-independent Abelian decomposition to Abelianize the non-Abelian gauge theory. The Abelianized dual potential has both a non-topological electric potential and a topological magnetic potential. \displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(r)(\partial_{\mu}t)\hat{r}+(f(r)-1)\hat{r}\times\partial_{\mu}\hat{r}\,,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}B_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B(r)\partial_{\mu}t-(1-\cos\theta)\partial_{\mu}\varphi\;,

(15)

\displaystyle A_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\Big({A(r)\over g^{2}}+{B(r)\over g^{\prime 2}}\Big)\partial_{\mu}t-{1\over e}(1-\cos\theta)\partial_{\mu}\varphi\;,

(16)

\displaystyle W_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{if(r)\over\sqrt{2}g}e^{+i\varphi}(\partial_{\mu}\theta+i\sin\theta\partial_{\mu}\varphi)\;,

(17)

\displaystyle W_{\mu}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{if(r)\over\sqrt{2}g}e^{-i\varphi}(-\partial_{\mu}\theta+i\sin\theta\partial_{\mu}\varphi)\;,

(18)

\displaystyle Z_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{e\over gg^{\prime}}(A(r)-B(r))\partial_{\mu}t\;.

(19)

The complex scalar singlet is parameterized as

\displaystyle\phi={1\over\sqrt{2}}(v_{a}+\sigma_{a})e^{i\hat{a}(r)}\;,

The axion-photon coupling in Eq. (

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(F^{\mu\nu}-g_{a\gamma\gamma}a\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu})=0\;,

The solution of electroweak monopole in the presence of axion

Using the spherically symmetric ansatz in Eqs. (
14

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}a)^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{da}{dr}\right)^{2}\;,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}g_{a\gamma\gamma}aF_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=-g_{a\gamma\gamma}\frac{a}{r^{2}}\Big[\left(\frac{dA}{dr}\right)\left(\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}{g^{2}}(1-f^{2})+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{W}}{g^{2}}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{dB}{dr}\right)\left(\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}{g^{\prime 2}}(1-f^{2})+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta_{W}}{g^{\prime 2}}\right)\Big]\;,

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}a}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{da}{dr}+T_{a}=0\;,

(33)

where

\displaystyle T_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{a\gamma\gamma}af\left[\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\left(\frac{dA}{dr}\right)+\cos^{2}\theta_{W}\left(\frac{dB}{dr}\right)\right]\;,

(34)

\displaystyle T_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{B}=-g_{a\gamma\gamma}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{da}{dr}-\frac{2a}{r}\right)\left(1-\sin^{2}\theta_{W}f^{2}\right)-2\sin^{2}\theta_{W}af\left(\frac{df}{dr}\right)\right]\;,

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g_{a\gamma\gamma}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Big[\left(\frac{dA}{dr}\right)\left({1\over g^{2}}-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}{g^{2}}f^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{dB}{dr}\right)\left({1\over g^{\prime 2}}-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}{g^{\prime 2}}f^{2}\right)\Big]\;.

IV
Characteristics of electroweak monopole and axion

IV.1
The monopole energy

We first show the monopole energy in the presence of axion. The 00 component of the energy-momentum tensor
T^{00}
in terms of the gauge fields is

\displaystyle T^{00}

\displaystyle=2(D^{0}\Phi^{\dagger})(D^{0}\Phi)-(W^{a})^{0i}(W^{a})^{0}_{~i}-B^{0i}B^{0}_{~i}-\frac{1}{2}g_{a\gamma\gamma}a\bigg[2\sin^{2}\theta_{W}(\tilde{W}^{3})^{0i}(W^{3})^{0}_{~i}

(43)

\displaystyle+2\cos^{2}\theta_{W}\tilde{B}^{0i}B^{0}_{~i}+\sin\theta_{W}\cos\theta_{W}\left(\tilde{B}^{0i}(W^{3})^{0}_{~i}+(\tilde{W}^{3})^{0i}B^{0}_{~i}\right)\bigg]-\mathcal{L}_{eff}\;.

The total energy of monopole in the KSVZ model can be obtained as

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\rm CMM}+E_{\rm axion~int.}\;,

(44)

\displaystyle E_{\rm CMM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{g^{\prime 2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{dr}{2r^{2}}}

IV.2
The electromagnetic charges

We next discuss the electromagnetic properties of electroweak monopole in the presence of KSVZ axion. According to the ansatz of neutral gauge components in Eq. (

F_{\mu\nu}
given by the electromagnetic field

The corresponding electric field

\displaystyle\vec{E}

\displaystyle-e\left(\frac{\dot{A}(r)}{g^{2}}+\frac{\dot{B}(r)}{g^{\prime 2}}\right)\hat{r}\;,

\displaystyle-\frac{\hat{r}}{er^{2}}\;.

These electromagnetic fields are purely generated by the electroweak monopole in vacuum. However, if we take into account the axion which couples to the physical photon, the axion acts as a kind of source to excite another electromagnetic field.

For the electric and magnetic charges of the electroweak monopole, they are defined as the integrals of electromagnetic fields over a closed curve.
We have the following charges

\displaystyle\begin{split}q_{e}&=\oint_{r=\infty}{d\vec{S}\cdot(\vec{E}+g_{a\gamma\gamma}a\vec{B})}=-4\pi e\int_{0}^{\infty}{dr\left[r^{2}\left(\frac{\dot{A}(r)}{g^{2}}+\frac{\dot{B}(r)}{g^{\prime 2}}\right)\right]^{\prime}}+g_{a\gamma\gamma}\oint_{r=\infty}{d\vec{S}\cdot a\vec{B}}\\

IV.3
The axion potential energy

Finally, we reexamine the axion potential in the background field of a monopole which was first studied by Fischler et al. in Ref.
Fischler and Preskill 1983
. The axion potential energy can be divided into two parts. One part is provided by the kinetic energy of axion. The axion itself has a lot of kinetic energy. The other part comes from the electrostatic field energy
1/2\vec{E}_{a}^{2}
. The induced electric field
\vec{E}_{a}
is proportion to the magnetic field of monopole
\vec{B}
. Thus, there exists a non-negligible energy cost
1/2\vec{E}_{a}^{2}
in the vicinity of the axion.
In other words, the axion is strongly repelled by the monopole in electromagnetic properties and needs to pay a price of large energy if it wants to be combined with monopole. The axion potential energy is given by

\displaystyle V_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int{dV\left[\left(\nabla a\right)^{2}+\vec{E}_{a}^{2}\right]}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\rm axion~kin.}+\frac{1}{2}\int{dV\left[g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}\frac{a(r)^{2}}{e^{2}r^{4}}\right]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}{drr^{2}\left(\frac{da}{dr}\right)^{2}}+2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}{dr\left(\frac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}a(r)}{er}\right)^{2}}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}drK(r)\;.

We can then follow the substitution of integral variable in Ref.
Fischler and Preskill 1983
to minimize
V_{a}
. The axion potential can be rewritten as

\displaystyle V_{a}=-2\pi\left[r_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}{dz\left(\frac{da}{dz}\right)^{2}}+\frac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}}{e^{2}}\frac{1}{r_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{dza^{2}}\right]\;,

(52)

where the radius
r

with

The axion in the configuration of
a(r)=2\pi v_{a}e^{-r_{0}/r}
approaches
2\pi v_{a}
when

and can minimize
V_{a}

Fischler and Preskill 1983

\displaystyle V_{a}^{\rm min}=-\pi(2\pi v_{a})^{2}\left(r_{0}+\frac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}}{e^{2}r_{0}}\right)=-8\pi^{3}v_{a}^{2}\frac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}}{e}\;.

V
Conclusion

The Witten effect implies the dynamics of axion and magnetic monopole. The Cho-Maison monopole is a realistic electroweak monopole arisen in the Weinberg-Salam theory. This monopole of TeV scale mass motivates the dedicated search for electroweak monopole at colliders. It is plausible to explore the implication of axion to the electroweak magnetic monopole.

In this work we investigate the topological solutions of Cho-Maison electroweak monopole in the presence of KSVZ axion. We use the spherically symmetric ansatz for the electroweak dyon and introduce the spherically symmetric function for the axion field. The effective Lagrangian is then showed in terms of the radial functions. It includes the electroweak monopole part, the axion kinetic energy as well as the axion interaction term. We derive the consequent equations of motion in the presence of the axion-photon coupling and show the numerical results. Given the above topological solutions, we calculate the characteristics of the electroweak monopole such as the monopole mass and the electromagnetic charges.

We find that the monopole mass can be slightly changed by the axion-photon interaction. The presence of KSVZ axion changes the electric charge of the monopole by 20%. These changes would have impacts on either the existence of axion or the testability of electroweak monopole. We also calculate the axion potential energy in terms of the axion solution from EoM, and compare with the result in the classical limit.

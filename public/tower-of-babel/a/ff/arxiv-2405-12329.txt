Super Hayashi Quandles


Abstract
Quandles are right-invertible, right-self distributive (and idempotent) algebraic structures. Therefore, right translations are automorphisms. For each finite quandle, the list of the right translations’ cycle structures is known as the profile of the quandle. For a connected quandle, any two right translations are conjugate so there is essentially one cycle structure per connected quandle - which we thus identify with the profile. Hayashi conjectured that, for a finite connected quandle, each length of its profile divides the longest length. In the present article we introduce Super Hayashi Quandles (SHQ). An SHQ is a finite connected quandle such that any two lengths in its profile are (i) distinct, and (ii) the shorter one divides the longer one. The SHQ’s are latin quandles and we prove that their profiles depend only on the second shortest length and on the number of cycles, along with an explicit description of each of the lengths. Furthermore, we prove that SHQ’s have SHQ’s alone for subquandles (with the same second shortest length but fewer cycles). Finally, we construct infinitely many SHQ’s. . Keywords : quandle; right translation; cycle; profile; Hayashi conjecture; super Hayashi quandle. MSC2020 : 20N99.

Introduction

A quandle is a right-invertible, right self-distributive and idempotent algebraic structure (
[
5
]
,
[
15
]
). These axioms constitute an algebraic counterpart to the Reidemeister moves (
[
8
]
) of Knot Theory and quandles give rise to efficient ways of telling knots apart (for instance, via counting colorings,
[
3
]
,
[
1
]
). We are thus led to study the structure of quandles.

The right-invertibility and right self-distributivity axioms imply that any right translation (multiplication by a fixed element on the right) is an automorphism. It has been interesting to look into finite quandles by way of the cycle structures their right translations may have
[
13
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
. For each quandle, the list of these cycle structures is known as the profile of the quandle. For a connected quandle, any two right translations are conjugate so they have essentially one cycle structure - which we identify with its profile. In
[
4
]
, Hayashi conjectured that, for a finite connected quandle, any length from its profile divides the longest length. In the present article we introduce Super Hayashi Quandles (SHQ) which are quandles that satisfy Hayashi Conjecture by definition. An SHQ is a finite quandle such that the cycle structure of each of its right translations satisfies the following condition: any two distinct disjoint cycles are such that (i) they have distinct lengths, and (ii) the shorter length divides the longer one. By Vojtěchovský’s and the authors’ previous work (cf.
[
12
]
) (i) above implies that such quandles are latin, thus connected. We prove that the profiles of SHQ’s are characterized by two positive integer parameters,
c
(the number of cycles) and
\ell
(the length of the second smallest cycle) - this is parts 1. and 2. of the Main Theorem, below. In particular, we prove that the profile of each such quandle is
(1,\ell,\ell(\ell+1),\dots,\ell(\ell+1)^{c-2})
, where
\ell+1
is a prime power. Furthermore, we prove that SHQ’s have SHQ’s alone for subquandles (with the same second shortest length but fewer cycles), one per
c^{\prime}
, the number of cycles,
2\leq c^{\prime}<c
- see part 3. of the Main Theorem, below. Finally, we construct infinitely many SHQ’s.

(Quandle)

3
SHQ’s and the Main Theorem

We now introduce the definition of
Super Hayashi Quandles
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Super Hayashi Quandle
(SHQ) is a quandle each of whose right translations has cycle structure of the form
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{c})
, where
1=\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\dots<\ell_{c}
, with
c\geq 2
and
\ell_{i-1}\mid\ell_{i}
, for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,*)
be an SHQ. Then,
(X,*)
is latin, thus connected.

Proof.

Since an SHQ has a profile such that any two distinct
l_{i}
’s have distinct lengths, then an SHQ is latin
(
Theorem 3.4. in
[
12
]
)
, hence connected.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Hayashi Conjecture
(
[
4
]
)
states that, for any finite connected quandle, each length of the quandles’ profile divides the largest length. Thus, SHQ’s satisfy the Hayashi Conjecture.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
(X,*)
be a finite quandle such that one of its left translations is a bijection and the cycle structure of one of its right translations is
(1,\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{c})
with
c>1
,
1<\ell_{2}<\ell_{3}<\dots<\ell_{c}
and
\ell_{i-1}\mid\ell_{i}
. Then,
(X,*)
is an SHQ (cf.
[
14
]
, Theorem 2.1).

Remark 3.5
.

Let
(X,*)
be a quandle with
1\in X
. Suppose that each right translation of
(X,*)
has a unique fixed point and
R_{1}
has cycle structure
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{c})
, where
1=\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\dots<\ell_{c}
, with
c\geq 2
and
\ell_{i-1}\mid\ell_{i}
, for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
. Then,
(X,*)
is an SHQ (cf.
[
12
]
).

Example 3.6
.

Cyclic quandles, see Example
2.5
.

For
|X|>2
, the distinct cycles have distinct lengths hence the quandle is latin (
[
12
]
) hence connected. These quandles are clearly SHQ’s.

Example 3.7
.

The quandle
Q_{9,4}
, whose quandle table is presented in Table
1
, is a quandle each of whose right translations has cycle structure equal to
(1,2,6)
. Since
1\mid 2\mid 6
,
Q_{9,4}
is an SHQ. Example
3.7
complies with Remarks
3.4
,
3.5
.

We set up notation before stating the main result of this article.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
(X,*)
be a quandle of order
n
with profile
(1=\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{c})
. We let
X=\{1,\dots,n\}
and set

\displaystyle n_{i}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=1}^{i}\ell_{k},\quad\text{for
}i\in\{1,\dots,c\};

\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{1,\dots,n_{i}\}\subseteq X,\quad\text{for }i\in\{1,\dots,c\};

\displaystyle L_{i}

\displaystyle:=\{n_{i}-\ell_{i}+1,\dots,n_{i}\}\subseteq X,\quad\text{for }i\in\{1,\dots,c\}.

Note that

n_{1}=\ell_{1}=1\qquad X_{1}=L_{1}=\{1,2,,\dots,n_{1}\}=\{1\},

\ell_{i}=|L_{i}|\quad\text{for }i\in\{1,\dots,c\},

n_{c}=n\qquad X_{c}=X.

Furthermore, the
L_{i}
’s form a partition of
X=\{1,\dots,n\}
.

We keep this notation throughout this article.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(X,*)
be an SHQ with
prof(X,*)=(\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{c})
. Then,

n_{i-1}<\ell_{i}
, for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
.

Proof.

We prove this result by induction. We have
n_{1}=\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}
. Assume now that
n_{i-1}<\ell_{i}
, for a certain
i\in\{2,\dots,c-1\}
. Then
n_{i}=n_{i-1}+\ell_{i}<2\cdot\ell_{i}\leq\ell_{i+1}
, where we have used the fact that
\ell_{i}\mid\ell_{i+1}
and
\ell_{i}<\ell_{i+1}
.
∎

The main result of this article is the following theorem. Notice that this theorem expresses the profile of any SHQ in terms of just two parameters, the number of cycles,
c
, and the length of the second smallest cycle,
\ell
.

Theorem 3.10

(Main Theorem)
.

For
c\geq 2
, let
(X,*)
be an SHQ with
prof(X,*)=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{c})
. Then, there are (i) a prime number,
p
, and (ii) a positive integer,
a
, such that:

1.

|X|=(\ell+1)^{c-1}
, where
\ell+1=p^{a}
;

2.

prof(X,*)=(1,\ell\cdot(\ell+1)^{0},\ell\cdot(\ell+1)^{1},\dots,\ell\cdot(\ell+1)^{c-2})
;

3.

For each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
,
(X_{i},\ast)
is a subquandle of
(X,\ast)
with

prof(X_{i},*)=(1,\ell\cdot(\ell+1)^{0},\dots,\ell\cdot(\ell+1)^{i-2})

Furthermore, these are the only non-trivial subquandles of
(X,\ast)
, up to isomorphism.

Remark 3.11
.

Note that once
2.
in Theorem
3.10
is established, it will follow that

|X|=1+\sum_{i=0}^{c-2}\ell(\ell+1)^{i}=1+\ell\frac{(\ell+1)^{c-1}-1}{\ell+1-1}=(\ell+1)^{c-1}.

The proof that
\ell+1=p^{a}
, for prime
p
and
a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
is Corollary
5.2
below.

Auxiliary Results

In order to prove Theorem

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,*)
be a finite quandle.
Modulo isomorphism, the right multiplications of
(X,*)
satisfy the following conditions:

1.

R_{1}=(n_{1})(n_{2}-\ell_{2}+1\quad n_{2}-\ell_{2}+2\quad\cdots\quad n_{2})\cdots(n_{c}-\ell_{c}+1\quad n_{c}-\ell_{c}+2\quad\cdots\quad n_{c})
;

2.

R_{n_{i-1}+k}=R_{1}^{k}R_{n_{i}}R_{1}^{-k}
, for each
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell_{i}\}
and
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
.

Proof.

We prove assertions
1.
and
2.
:

1.

We assume
R_{1}=(n_{1})(n_{2}-\ell_{2}+1\quad n_{2}-\ell_{2}+2\quad\cdots\quad n_{2})\cdots(n_{c}-\ell_{c}+1\quad n_{c}-\ell_{c}+2\quad\cdots\quad n_{c})
without loss of generality. If necessary, we may relabel indices.
This expression for
R_{1}
will be assumed in the sequel.
Notice that the elements of
(X,*)
belonging to the cycle
(n_{i}-\ell_{i}+1\quad\cdots\quad n_{i})
are precisely the elements of
L_{i}
and the length of the cycle
(n_{i}-\ell_{i}+1\quad\cdots\quad n_{i})
is
\ell_{i}
, for each
i\in\{1,\dots,c\}
.

2.

First, by 1. and Theorem
2.2

R_{n_{i-1}+1}=R_{R_{1}(n_{i})}=R_{1}R_{n_{i}}R_{1}^{-1}
, for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
. Now, if
R_{n_{i-1}+k}=R_{1}^{k}R_{n_{i}}R_{1}^{-k}
for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
and a certain
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell_{i}-1\}
, then

R_{n_{i-1}+k+1}=R_{R_{1}(n_{i-1}+k)}=R_{1}R_{n_{i-1}+k}R_{1}^{-1}=R_{1}R_{1}^{k}R_{n_{i}}R_{1}^{-k}R_{1}^{-1}=R_{1}^{k+1}R_{n_{i}}R_{1}^{-(k+1)}.

Thus, we conclude, by induction, that
R_{n_{i-1}+k}=R_{1}^{k}R_{n_{i}}R_{1}^{-k}
, for each
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell_{i}\}
and
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
.

∎

Definition 4.2
.

Given two integers
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we let
[i,j]
stand for their
least common multiple
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
t,u,v\in\{1,\dots,c\}
such that
x_{t},x_{u}\in(X,*)
, with
x_{t}\in L_{t}
,
x_{u}\in L_{u}
and
x_{t}*x_{u}\in L_{v}
. Then
\ell_{v}\mid[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]
.

Proof.

Because
[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]
is a multiple of
\ell_{t}
and
x_{t}\in L_{t}
,
R_{1}^{[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]}(x_{t})=x_{t}
. Analogously,
R_{1}^{[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]}(x_{u})=x_{u}
. Then,

x_{t}*x_{u}=R_{1}^{[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]}(x_{t})*R_{1}^{[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]}(x_{u})=R_{1}^{[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]}(x_{t}*x_{u}),

where the last equality stems from the fact that compositions of automorphisms are automorphisms. Note that
(R_{1}^{m}(x_{t}*x_{u})\,:\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+})
is a periodic sequence, whose period is
l_{v}
, since
x_{t}\ast x_{u}\in L_{v}
, by hypothesis. The sequence starts at
x_{t}*x_{u}
and is formed by copies of the cycle
L_{v}
. So,
x_{t}*x_{u}=R_{1}^{[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]}(x_{t}*x_{u})
implies that
\ell_{v}\mid[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(Y,*)
be a subquandle of
(X,*)
. Then,
(
the length of
)
any disjoint cycle of a right translation from
(Y,*)
is
(
the length of
)
a disjoint cycle of a right translation from
(X,*)
.

Proof.

Let

, let

. Then, by induction,

Let
\ell_{y,y^{\prime}}^{(Y,*)}
be the length of the disjoint cycle of

\ell_{y,y^{\prime}}^{(Y,*)}=|\{R_{y}^{m}(y^{\prime})\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|.

Since

|\{R_{y}^{m}(y^{\prime})\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|

Proposition 4.7

Let

5
Proof of Main Theorem

In the sequel, we let
(X,*)
stand for an SHQ of order
n
with profile
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{c})
and
c\geq 2
, unless otherwise stated. We keep to the notation of Definition
3.8
.

Proposition
5.1
below can be regarded as a Corollary to Proposition
4.4
. Its proof is the proof of the statement in
(3)
in Theorem
3.10
, but the expressions for the lengths.

Proposition 5.1
.

(X_{i},*)
is a subquandle of
(X,*)
, for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
. Besides,
(X_{i},*)
is an SHQ of order
n_{i}
with profile
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{i})
, for each
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
. Moreover, each
(X_{i},*)
is a subquandle of
(X_{i+1},*)
.

Proof.

Let
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
. For
x,y\in X_{i}
, we have
x\in L_{t}
and
y\in L_{u}
, for certain
t,u\leq i
. By Proposition
4.3
,
x*y\in L_{v}
, for some
v
such that
\ell_{v}\mid[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]
. Because
[\ell_{t},\ell_{u}]\mid\ell_{i}
, then
v\leq i
, thus
x\ast y\in X_{i}
and
(X_{i},*)
is a subquandle of
(X,*)

(
resp., of
(X_{i+1},*))
. Moreover, using Proposition
4.4
for the cardinalities of the sets
|\{R_{1}^{m}(\cdots)\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|
,

\displaystyle 1\in X_{i}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad|\{R_{1}^{m}(1)\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|=\ell_{1}(=1)\,\,\text{ is a length in }\,\,prof(X_{i})

\displaystyle 2\in X_{i}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad|\{R_{1}^{m}(2)\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|=\ell_{2}\,\,\text{ (ditto)}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle n_{j}-l_{j}+1\in X_{i}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad|\{R_{1}^{m}(n_{j}-l_{j}+1)\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|=\ell_{j}\,\,\text{ (ditto)}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle n_{i}-l_{i}+1\in X_{i}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad|\{R_{1}^{m}(n_{i}-l_{i}+1)\,:\,m\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}\}|=\ell_{i}\,\,\text{ (ditto)}

and so, each of the
\ell_{j}
’s
(j=1,\dots,i)
is the length of a disjoint cycle of a right multiplication in
(X_{i},*)

\displaystyle\qquad(*)\qquad\text{for any }\,2\leq j\leq i,\,\,(X_{j},*)\,\,\text{ is an SHQ with}

Proposition 5.11

5.2
The Subquandles of SHQ’s are SHQ’s.

We have already proved that the
(X_{i},\ast)
’s are SHQ’s and subquandles of the SHQ
(X,\ast)
(Proposition
5.1
). We now prove that these are the only subquandles of
(X,\ast)
and that their profiles have the same second smallest length.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
(X,\ast)
be an SHQ i.e., there exist
c,\ell\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
such that

prof(X,\ast)=(1,\ell,\ell(\ell+1),\dots,\ell(\ell+1)^{i},\dots,\ell(\ell+1)^{c-2}).

If
(X^{\prime},\ast)
is a subquandle of
(X,\ast)
then its profile is

prof(X^{\prime},\ast)=(1,\ell(\ell+1),\dots,\ell(\ell+1)^{c^{\prime}-2})\quad\text{ where }\quad 2\leq c^{\prime}<c.

Furthermore,
(X^{\prime},\ast)
is isomorphic with
(X_{c^{\prime}},\ast)
.

Proof.

We recall that, thanks to Proposition
4.4
, the lengths of the profile of the subquandle are lengths of the profile of the quandle. We also know that the
(X_{i},\ast)
’s are subquandles of
(X,\ast)
. We now prove that there cannot be a subquandle whose profile is different from the profile of any of the
(X_{i},*)
’s. Assume to the contrary and suppose
(X^{\prime},*)
is a subquandle of
(X,*)
such that for any
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
,
prof(X^{\prime},*)\neq prof(X_{i},*)
. By Proposition
4.4
,
(X^{\prime},*)
is again an SHQ. Therefore,

prof(X^{\prime},*)=(1,\ell^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1),\dots,\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)^{c^{\prime}-2})\qquad\text{ for some }\,\,\ell^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}.

If
\ell^{\prime}=\ell
then
(X^{\prime},*)=(X_{i},*)
for some
i\in\{2,\dots,c\}
, see Proposition
5.3
. Otherwise,

\ell^{\prime}=\ell(\ell+1)^{k}\qquad\text{ for some }\,\,k\in\{2,\dots,c-2\}

such that
\ell^{\prime}+1
is a prime power (e.g.
\ell=2,k=1
). We prove the latter cannot occur by separating two cases:

I.

prof(X^{\prime},*)=(1,\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)^{k})
with
k\in\{1,\dots,c-2\}
(only one non-trivial length in the profile);

II.

prof(X^{\prime},*)=(1,\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)^{k_{1}},\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)^{k_{2}},\dots)
with
k_{1}<k_{2}\in\{1,\dots,c-2\}
(at least two non-trivial lengths in the profile);

I.

Suppose there is only one non-trivial length in the profile of
(X^{\prime},\ast)
,

prof(X^{\prime},\ast)=(1,\ell(\ell+1)^{k})\quad\text{ where }\quad 0\leq k\leq c-2.

If
k=0
then
(X^{\prime},\ast)
’s second smallest length is
\ell
, so we assume
k>0
.

Lemma 5.19
.

Suppose
(X^{\prime},*)
is a subquandle of
(X,*)
whose profile is

prof(X^{\prime},*)=(1,\ell(\ell+1)^{k}.

Then, it is isomorphic with a subquandle of
(X_{k+2},*)
whose underlying set is

\{1\}\cup L_{k+2}.

Proof.

Let
y_{0}\neq y\in(X^{\prime},*)
. Then,

\{R_{y_{0}}^{m}(y)\,|\,m\in\mathbf{Z}\}

is the cycle of length
\ell(\ell+1)^{k}
in
(X^{\prime},*)
. Since
(X^{\prime},*)
is latin, then there is
x\in X^{\prime}
such that
1=y_{0}*x
and so

R_{x}\big(\{R_{y_{0}}^{m}(y)\,|\,m\in\mathbf{Z}\}\big)=\{R_{y_{0}*x}^{m}(y*x)\,|\,m\in\mathbf{Z}\}=\{R_{1}^{m}(y*x)\,|\,m\in\mathbf{Z}\}=L_{k+2}

since
R_{x}
is an automprphism of
(X^{\prime},*)
. Since
\{1\}\cup L_{k+2}\subseteq X_{k+2}
, the proof is complete.
∎

Resuming the proof of Proposition
5.18
, we henceforth assume the underlying set of
(X^{\prime},*)
to be
\{1\}\cup L_{k+2}
.
Since the profile of
(X^{\prime},\ast)
is made up of two distinct lengths, then it is a latin quandle (cf.
[
12
]
). Consider also the subquandle
(X_{k+2},\ast)
which is also latin and for which
(X^{\prime},\ast)
is a subquandle. Then, for each
x^{\prime}\in X^{\prime}
,
R_{x^{\prime}}(X^{\prime})=X^{\prime}
which implies that
R_{x^{\prime}}(X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime})=X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime}
which further implies that for each
x\in X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime}
,

L_{x}(X^{\prime})\subset X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime}
and so
|L_{x}(X^{\prime})|\leq|X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime}|
. Note that
|X_{k+2}|=(\ell+1)^{k+1}
(Proposition
5.3
) whereas
|X^{\prime}|=\ell(\ell+1)^{k}+1
so that

|X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime}|=(\ell+1)^{k+1}-\ell(\ell+1)^{k}-1=(\ell+1)^{k}-1<\ell(\ell+1)^{k}+1=|X^{\prime}|,

but

|X^{\prime}|=|L_{x}(X^{\prime})|\leq|X_{k+2}\setminus X^{\prime}|.

Therefore,
k=0
.

II.

Now for a profile with more than one non-trivial length. Let

prof(X^{\prime},\ast)=(1,\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}},\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{2}},\dots)\qquad\text{ with }\quad 0<k_{1}<k_{2}.

Since the shorter lengths divide the longer lengths in the profile of the subquandle, then the subquandle is itself an SHQ. Therefore there exists
l^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime}&=\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}\\
\ell^{\prime}(\ell^{\prime}+1)&=\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{2}}\end{cases}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}\ell^{\prime}&=\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}\\
\ell^{\prime}+1&=(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}\end{cases}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}&\dots\\
\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}+1&=(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}&\dots\\
1&=(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}-\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}\end{cases}

We next cover the three different possibilities,
(i)\,\,k_{2}-k_{1}=k_{1}
;
(ii)\,\,k_{2}-k_{1}>k_{1}
;
(iii)\,\,k_{2}-k_{1}<k_{1}
:

(i)

If
k_{2}-k_{1}=k_{1}
then

1=(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}-\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}=(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}-\ell(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}=(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}(1-\ell)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1-\ell>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1>\ell

which is impossible.

(ii)

If
k_{2}-k_{1}>k_{1}
and since
k_{1}>0
, then

1=(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}\big[(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-2k_{1}}-\ell\big]

which is impossible because
(\ell+1)^{k_{1}}>1
and
(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-2k_{1}}-\ell\geq 1
.

(iii)

If
k_{2}-k_{1}<k_{1}
then

1=(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}\big[1-\ell(\ell+1)^{2k_{1}-k_{2}}\big]

which is impossible because
(\ell+1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}>0
while
1-\ell(\ell+1)^{2k_{1}-k_{2}}<0
.

The proof is complete.
∎

6
Remarks, Examples, and Questions for Future Work.

6.1
Remarks

We remark that the Main Theorem (Theorem
3.10
) asserts obstructions to the existence of quandles with certain profiles. For instance,
(1,5)
cannot be the profile of any quandle because
1+5=2\cdot 3
which is not a prime power. Thus, by the Main Theorem,

(1,5,5k)\qquad\qquad(1,5,5k,5kk^{\prime})\qquad\qquad(1,5,5k,5kk^{\prime},5kk^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime})\qquad\qquad\cdots\qquad\text{ for }k\leq k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}\leq\cdots\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}

cannot be profiles of quandles for they would have a quandle with profile
(1,5)
for subquandle and such a quandle cannot exist. On the other hand, a quandle with profile
(1,6)
exists but quandles with profiles

(1,6,12k)\qquad\quad(1,6,12k,12kk^{\prime})\qquad\quad(1,6,12k,12kk^{\prime},12kk^{\prime}k^{\prime\prime})\qquad\quad\cdots\qquad\text{ for }k\leq k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}\leq\cdots\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}

cannot exist because the
\ell_{i}
’s do not comply with
\ell_{i}=\ell(\ell+1)^{i-2}
, for
i>2
.

6.3
Further Examples.

Theorem 6.3
.

Quandles of cyclic type constitute an infinite family of SHQ’s.

Proof.

Quandles of cyclic type (
[
13
]
) i.e., connected quandles whose profile is
(1,\ell)
, exist if
\ell+1=p^{a}
, where
p
is a prime and
a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
(
[
16
]
). The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

There are infinitely many families of SHQ’s, each family with infinitely many quandles.

Proof.

Our proof is constructive and is based on the notion of primitive root mod
n
i.e., the generator(s) of the multiplicative group of integers modulo
n
(
[
9
]
). Let
p
be an odd prime. It is known that there is a primitive root mod
p
, call it
g
; either
g
or
g+p
is a primitive root mod
p^{2}
and so it is a primitive root mod
p^{a}
for each
a>2
(Theorem 7.2.10 in
[
9
]
).

Definition 6.5
.

Let

h_{p}=\begin{cases}g&\text{ if $g$ is a primitive root mod $p^{a}$, for any $a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}$};\\
g+p&\text{ if $g+p$ is a primitive root mod $p^{a}$, for any $a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}$}\end{cases}

We prove the following Lemma, regarding
h_{p}
.

Lemma 6.6
.

h_{p}-1
is invertible mod
p^{a}
, for any
a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
.

Proof.

We take
g\in\{2,3,\dots,p-1\}
.

Suppose
h_{p}=g
is the primitive root mod
p
which is also a primitive root mod
p^{2}
. Then

0<g-1<p-2

and so
h_{p}-1=g-1
is invertible mod
p^{a}
, for each
a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
.

Now assume
h_{p}=g+p
. Then
p<g+p<2p
and so
p\leq g+p-1<2p-1
If
p=g+p-1
then
g=1
and so
g
is not a primitive root.

So, in either case,
h_{p}-1
is invertible mod
p^{a}
, for any
a\in\mathbf{Z}^{+}
. The proof of Lemma
6.6
is complete.
∎

Resuming the proof of Theorem
6.4
, we will construct a family of infinitely many SHQ’s for each
h_{p}
. Let
c\geq 2
. We let
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c-1}},*)
be the affine quandle (
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
) over the integers modulo
p^{c-1}
, with
a*b\equiv h_{p}\cdot a-(h_{p}-1)\cdot b
(mod
p^{c-1})
, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
. We prove that the profile of
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c-1}},*)
is equal to
(1,(p-1)\cdot p^{0},\dots,(p-1)\cdot p^{c-2})
, for every
c\geq 2
.

Because
h_{p}-1
is invertible,
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c-1}},*)
is latin, hence connected. Then its profile is equal to the cycle structure of any of its right translations, say
R_{0}
. Note
R_{0}^{t}(1)=h_{p}^{t}
, for each
t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Indeed,
R_{0}(1)=h_{p}\cdot 1=h_{p}
, and if
R_{0}^{t}(1)=h_{p}^{t}
, for a given
t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, then
R_{0}^{t+1}(1)=R_{0}(R_{0}^{t}(1))=h_{p}\cdot h_{p}^{t}=h_{p}^{t+1}
. Now, we check the profile of
(\mathbb{Z}_{p},*)
is
(1,p-1)
. Indeed
R_{0}(0)=0
and as
h_{p}
is a primitive root modulo
p
, the least
t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
R_{0}^{t}(1)\equiv 1\mod p\Leftrightarrow h_{p}^{t}\equiv 1\mod p
is
t=\rvert\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rvert=p-1
. Lastly, assuming the profile of
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c-1}},*)
is
(1,(p-1)\cdot p^{0},\dots,(p-1)\cdot p^{c-2})
, we prove the profile of
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c}},*)
is
(1,(p-1)\cdot p^{0},\dots,(p-1)\cdot p^{c-1})
, for each
c\geq 2
. We regard
(\mathbf{Z}_{p^{c-1}},*)
as a subquandle of
(\mathbf{Z}_{p^{c}},*)
via the embedding and
\ast
preserving map induced by

\displaystyle T\,:\,\,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle\,\,z\mapsto pz\qquad.

On one hand, given
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

R_{0}(x)\equiv y\mod p^{c-1}\Rightarrow R_{0}(x)\cdot p\equiv y\cdot p\mod p^{c}\Leftrightarrow h_{p}\cdot x\cdot p\equiv y\cdot p\mod p^{c}\Leftrightarrow R_{0}(x\cdot p)\equiv y\cdot p\mod p^{c},

i.e.,
T(z)=pz
maps cycles of
R_{0}|_{\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c-1}}}
onto cycles of
R_{0}|_{\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c}}}
. On the other hand, as
h_{p}
is a primitive root modulo
p^{c}
, the least
t\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
R_{0}^{t}(1)\equiv 1\mod p^{c}\Leftrightarrow h_{p}^{t}\equiv 1\mod p^{c}
is
t=(p-1)\cdot p^{c-1}
, as
\rvert\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c}}^{\times}\rvert=(p-1)\cdot p^{c-1}
. Thus we have proved that the profile of
(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{c}},*)
is
(1,(p-1)\cdot p^{0},\dots,(p-1)\cdot p^{c-1})
, for each
c\geq 2
.

∎

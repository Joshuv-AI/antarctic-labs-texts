Deformation maps of quasi-twilled Lie algebras
Jun Jiang, Yunhe Sheng, Rong Tang

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide a unified approach to study the cohomology theories and deformation theories of various types of operators in the category of Lie algebras, including modified r r -matrices, crossed homomorphisms, derivations, homomorphisms, relative Rota-Baxter operators, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras. The main ingredients are quasi-twilled Lie algebras. We introduce two types of deformation maps of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. Deformation maps of type I unify modified r r -matrices, crossed homomorphisms, derivations and homomorphisms between Lie algebras, while deformation maps of type II unify relative Rota-Baxter operators, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras. We further give the controlling algebras and cohomologies of these two types of deformation maps, which not only recover the existing results for crossed homomorphisms, derivations, homomorphisms, relative Rota-Baxter operators, twisted Rota-Baxter operators and Reynolds operators, but also leads to some new results which are unable to solve before, e.g. the controlling algebras and cohomologies of modified r r -matrices and deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras.

1.
Introduction

A classical approach to study a mathematical structure is to associate to it invariants. Among these, cohomology theories occupy a central position as they enable for example to control deformation or extension problems. The concept of a formal deformation of an algebraic structure began with the seminal
work of Gerstenhaber
[
21
,
22
]
for associative
algebras. Nijenhuis and Richardson extended this study to Lie algebras

[
41
,
43
]
. Deformations of other
algebraic structures such as pre-Lie algebras have also been
developed
[
8
]
. More generally, deformation theory
for algebras over quadratic operads was developed by Balavoine
[
3
]
. For more
general operads we refer the reader to
[
28
,
35
,
39
]
, and the references therein.
There is a well known slogan, often attributed to Deligne, Drinfeld and Kontsevich:
every reasonable deformation theory is controlled by a differential graded (dg) Lie algebra, determined up to quasi-isomorphism
. This slogan has been made into a rigorous theorem by Lurie and Pridham, cf.
[
37
,
45
]
.

It is also meaningful to deform
maps
compatible with given algebraic structures. Recently, the cohomology and deformation theories of various operators were established with fruitful applications, e.g morphisms
[
5
,
7
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
, derivations
[
53
]
,
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators (also called relative Rota-Baxter operators)
[
9
,
15
,
50
,
54
]
, crossed homomorphisms
[
12
,
25
,
44
]
, twisted Rota-Baxter operators and Reynolds operators
[
10
,
11
]
. The key step in most of the above studies is to construct the controlling algebra, namely an algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are the given structures, using the method of derived brackets
[
30
,
38
,
55
]
. Then twisting the controlling algebra by a Maurer-Cartan element, one can obtain the algebra that governs deformations of the given operator, as well as the coboundary operator in the deformation complex.

Modified
r
-matrices, namely solutions of the modified Yang-Baxter equation, are important operators that have deep applications in mathematical physics, e.g. the Lax equation and the factorization problem
[
46
,
48
,
49
]
. Even though there are very fruitful results for various kinds of operators as aforementioned, but the controlling algebra for modified
r
-matrices is still unknown. On the other hand, the notion of deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras was introduced in
[
1
,
2
]
in the study of classifying compliments. It is also useful to develop the cohomology and deformation theories for deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras.

We propose a unified approach to study all aforementioned operators. On the one hand, we recover all the existing theories. On the other hand, we obtain some new results. We give the controlling algebra of modified
r
-matrices, which is a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, and establish the cohomology and deformation theories for deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras. We study all the operators under the general framework of quasi-twilled Lie algebras, which are generalization of quasi-Lie bialgebras
[
29
,
31
]
, and include direct sum of Lie algebras, semidirect products, action Lie algebras and matched pairs of Lie algebras as particular cases. We introduce two types of deformation maps of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. Deformation maps of type I unify modified
r
-matrices, crossed homomorphisms, derivations and homomorphisms between Lie algebras, and deformation maps of type II unify relative Rota-Baxter operators, twisted Rota-Baxter operators, Reynolds operators and deformation maps of matched pairs of Lie algebras. We further give the controlling algebras and cohomologies of these two types of deformation maps, and realize the above purposes.

Note that simultaneous deformations of parts of aforementioned operators and algebras were studied in
[
5
,
13
,
19
,
33
,
58
]
, and it would be helpful to develop a unified approach to study simultaneous deformations, which will be considered in the future.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra and give various examples. In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of a deformation map of type I of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, and give its controlling algebra and cohomology. In particular, we obtain the curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, whose Maurer-Cartan elements are modified
r
-matrices. In Section
4
, we introduce the notion of a deformation map of type II of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, and give its controlling algebra and cohomology. As a byproduct, we give the controlling algebra and cohomology of a deformation map of a matched pair of Lie algebras.

2.
Quasi-twilled Lie algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, and give various examples.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space. Define the graded vector space

\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

with the degree of elements in
\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
being
n-1
. For
f\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
, the Nijenhuis-Richardson bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{NR}}
is defined by

[f,g]_{\mathrm{NR}}:=f\circ g-(-1)^{(m-1)(n-1)}g\circ f,

with
f\circ g\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{m+n-1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
being defined by

(1)

(f\circ g)(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m+n-1}):=\sum_{\sigma\in S(n,m-1)}(-1)^{\sigma}f(g(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)}),x_{\sigma(n+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(m+n-1)}),

where the sum is over
(n,m-1)
-shuffles. Recall that a permutation
\tau\in S_{n}
is called an
(i,n-i)
-shuffle if
\tau(1)<\cdots<\tau(i)
and
\tau(i+1)<\cdots<\tau(n)
.
Then
\big(\oplus_{n=0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{NR}}\big)
is a graded Lie algebra
[
41
,
43
]
. With this setup, a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
is precisely a solution
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
of the Maurer-Cartan equation

[\pi,\pi]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be vector spaces. The elements in
\mathfrak{g}
are denoted by
x
and the elements in
\mathfrak{h}
are denoted by
u
. For a multilinear map
\kappa:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g},
we define
\hat{\kappa}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k+l}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})
by

\hat{\kappa}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),\cdots,(x_{k+l},u_{k+l})\Big)=\sum_{\tau\in S(k,l)}(-1)^{\tau}\Big(\kappa(x_{\tau(1)},\cdots,x_{\tau(k)},u_{\tau(k+1)},\cdots,u_{\tau(k+l)}),0\Big).

Similarly, for a multilinear map
\kappa:\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h},
we define
\hat{\kappa}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{k+l}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})
by

\hat{\kappa}\Big((x_{1},u_{1}),\cdots,(x_{k+l},u_{k+l})\Big)=\sum_{\tau\in S(k,l)}(-1)^{\tau}\Big(0,\kappa(x_{\tau(1)},\cdots,x_{\tau(k)},u_{\tau(k+1)},\cdots,u_{\tau(k+l)})\Big).

The linear map
\hat{\kappa}
is called a
lift
of
\kappa
. We define
\mathfrak{g}^{k,l}=\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\wedge^{l}\mathfrak{h}
. Then
\wedge^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})\cong\oplus_{k+l=n}\mathfrak{g}^{k,l}
and
\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h})\cong(\oplus_{k+l=n}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{k,l},\mathfrak{g}))\oplus(\oplus_{k+l=n}\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}^{k,l},\mathfrak{h}))
, where the isomorphism is given by the lift. See
[
33
,
54
]
for more details. In the sequel, we will omit the notation
\hat{\cdot}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{G}})
be a Lie algebra with a decomposition into two subspaces
\mathcal{G}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
. The triple
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})

is called a
quasi-twilled Lie algebra
if
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie subalgebra of
(\mathcal{G},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{G}})
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. Denote the Lie bracket of
\mathcal{G}
by
\Omega
. Then there exists

\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),~\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),~\eta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, such that

(2)

\Omega=\pi+\rho+\mu+\eta+\theta.

More precisely, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega((x,u),(y,v))=\big(\pi(x,y)+\eta(x,v)-\eta(y,u),\mu(u,v)+\rho(x,v)-\rho(y,u)+\theta(x,y)\big).

In fact,
[\Omega,\Omega]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0
if and only if the following equations hold

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}~~[\mu,\mu]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0,\\
~~[\pi,\pi]_{\mathrm{NR}}+2\eta\circ\theta&=&0,\\
~~2[\mu,\eta]_{\mathrm{NR}}+[\eta,\eta]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0,\\
~~[\rho,\mu]_{\mathrm{NR}}+\rho\circ\eta&=&0,\\
~~[\rho,\theta]_{\mathrm{NR}}+[\pi,\theta]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0,\\
~~[\pi,\eta]_{\mathrm{NR}}+\eta\circ\rho&=&0,\\
~~[\mu,\theta]_{\mathrm{NR}}+[\pi,\rho]_{\mathrm{NR}}+\theta\circ\eta+\frac{1}{2}[\rho,\rho]_{\mathrm{NR}}&=&0.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 2.2
.

With the above notations,
\sigma:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, where

\sigma(v)x=-\eta(x,v),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

By (
2
), we have
2[\mu,\eta]_{\mathrm{NR}}+[\eta,\eta]_{\mathrm{NR}}=0
which implies that
\sigma(v)x=-\eta(x,v)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

Quasi-twilled Lie algebras can be viewed as natural generalizations of quasi-Lie bialgebras. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a vector space and
\pi\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g},~\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\theta\in\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Recall that the quadruple
(\mathfrak{g},\pi,\mu,\theta)
is called a
quasi-Lie bialgebra

[
6
,
29
,
31
]
if

\frac{1}{2}\{\pi,\pi\}+\{\mu,\theta\}=0,\quad\{\pi,\mu\}=0,\quad\{\mu,\mu\}=0,\quad\{\pi,\theta\}=0,

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
is the big bracket, which is the canonical Poisson bracket on
T^{*}[2]\mathfrak{g}[1]
. For convenience and to be consistence with the notations for quasi-twilled Lie algebras, we will also view
\pi\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g},~\mu\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and
\theta\in\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as maps in
\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and
\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
respectively. Then it is well known that the double
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a Lie algebra and it follows that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{*})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

In the sequel, we give various examples of quasi-twilled Lie algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
, define a bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{M}
on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
by

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{M}=([x,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[y,u]_{\mathfrak{g}},\lambda[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x,y,u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

That is
\pi=\rho=0,\eta=\mu=[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\theta=\lambda[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}
in (
2
). Then we have that
[\cdot,\cdot]_{M}
is a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
. Denote this Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{M}\mathfrak{g}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{M}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
, then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho})
is a Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho}
is given by

(11)

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\rho}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u+\lambda[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

This Lie algebra is denoted by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{h}
and called the
action Lie algebra
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
be an action of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V,\mathfrak{g},V)
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, where
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho}V
is the
semidirect product Lie algebra
with the Lie bracket

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\rho}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u),\quad\forall u,v\in V,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Lie algebras. Then there is the direct product Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\oplus})
, where

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\oplus}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Example 2.6
.

The direct product Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
and
\omega\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},V)
be a
2
-cocycle. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho,\omega})
is a Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\rho,\omega}
is given by

(12)

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\rho,\omega}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u+\omega(x,y)),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in V.

Denote this Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\omega}V
. Moreover,
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\omega}V,\mathfrak{g},V)
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

Example 2.8
.

As a special case of Example
2.7
, consider
V=\mathfrak{g},~\rho=\mathrm{ad}
and
\omega(x,y)=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then we obtain a quasi-twilled Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\mathrm{ad},\omega}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
.

Remark 2.9
.

In fact, the above examples can be unified via extensions of Lie algebras. Recall that a Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}
is an extension of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
if we have the following exact sequence:

(13)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0.

By choosing a section
s:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{G}
of the extension (
13
),
\mathcal{G}
is equal to
s(\mathfrak{g})\oplus i(\mathfrak{h})
, and
i(\mathfrak{h})
is a subalgebra. Thus,
(\mathcal{G},s(\mathfrak{g}),i(\mathfrak{h}))
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

A
matched pair of Lie algebras
consists of a pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
, a representation
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}
and a representation
\eta:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that

(14)

\displaystyle\rho(x)[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,\rho(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho\big((\eta(v)x\big)u-\rho\big(\eta(u)x\big)v,

(15)

\displaystyle\eta(u)[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\eta(u)x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\eta(u)y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\eta\big(\rho(y)u\big)x-\eta\big(\rho(x)u\big)y,

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
. We will denote a matched pair of Lie algebras by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\eta)
, or simply by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.
Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\eta)
be a matched pair of Lie algebras. Then there is a Lie algebra structure on the direct sum space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}
with the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\bowtie}
given by

[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\bowtie}=\big([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\eta(u)y-\eta(v)x,[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u\big).

Denote this Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{h}
.

Example 2.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h};\rho,\eta)
be a matched pair of Lie algebras. Then
(\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. Note that the representation
\eta
in the definition of a matched pair and the
\eta
in (
2
) are related as follows:
\rho(x)v=\rho(x,v),~\eta(u)(y)=-\eta(y,u)
.

Remark 2.11
.

In Section
3
, we will introduce the notion of a deformation map of type I of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, and will see that deformation maps of type I of quasi-twilled Lie algebras given in Examples

,
2.4

2.5
and
2.6
are exactly modified

Remark 2.12
.

In Section
4
, we will introduce the notion of a deformation map of type II of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, and will see that deformation maps of type II of quasi-twilled Lie algebras given in Examples
2.4
,

,
2.7

2.8
and

are exactly relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight

, relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight

3.
The controlling algebras and cohomologies of deformation maps of type I

In this section,
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is always a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, and the Lie bracket on
\mathcal{G}
is denoted by

\Omega=\pi+\rho+\mu+\eta+\theta,

where
\pi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\rho\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),~\mu\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),~\eta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

3.1.
Deformation maps of type I of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of deformation maps of type I of a quasi-twilled Lie algebra, which unify modified
r
-matrices, crossed homomorphisms, derivations and homomorphisms between Lie algebras

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
be a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. A
deformation map of type I
(
\mathfrak{D}
-map for short) of
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

D\big(\eta(x,D(y))-\eta(y,D(x))+\pi(x,y)\big)=\mu(D(x),D(y))+\rho(x,D(y))-\rho(y,D(x))+\theta(x,y).

Remark 3.2
.

\mathfrak{D}
-maps may not exists. Consider the quasi-twilled Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\omega}V,\mathfrak{g},V)
given in Example
2.7
obtained from a representation
\rho
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
V
and a
2
-cocycle
\omega
. A linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is a
\mathfrak{D}
-map if and only if

\omega(x,y)=-\Big(\rho(x)D(y)-\rho(y)D(x)-D([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\Big)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}(-D)(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\mathrm{d}_{\mathsf{CE}}
is the corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\rho)
.
Thus, the quasi-twilled Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\rho,\omega}V,\mathfrak{g},V)
given in Example
2.7
admits a
\mathfrak{D}
-map if and only if
\omega
is an exact
2
-cocycle.

Let
D:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
be a linear map. Denote the graph of
D
by

\mathrm{Gr}(D)=\{(x,D(x))|x\in\mathfrak{g}\}.

Proposition 3.3
.

A linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is a
\mathfrak{D}
-map if and only if
\mathrm{Gr}(D)
is a subalgebra. In this case
(\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{Gr}(D))
is also a matched pair of Lie algebras.

Proof.

For all
(x,D(x)),(y,D(y))\in\mathrm{Gr}(D)
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega\Big((x,D(x)),(y,D(y))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\pi(x,y)+\eta(x,D(y))-\eta(y,D(x)),\mu(D(x),D(y))+\rho(x,D(y))-\rho(y,D(x))+\theta(x,y)\Big).

Thus,
\mathrm{Gr}(D)
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathcal{G}
, i.e.
\Omega\Big((x,D(x)),(y,D(y))\Big)\in\mathrm{Gr}(D)
, if and only if

\mu(D(x),D(y))+\rho(x,D(y))-\rho(y,D(x))+\theta(x,y)=D\Big(\pi(x,y)+\eta(x,D(y))-\eta(y,D(x))\Big),

namely
D
is a
\mathfrak{D}
-map of the quasi-twilled Lie algebra
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

Since

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
be a quasi-twilled Lie algebra and
D:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
a linear map. Then
((\mathcal{G},\Omega^{D}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra. Moreover, write
\Omega^{D}=\pi^{D}+\rho^{D}+\mu^{D}+\eta^{D}+\theta^{D}
,
\pi^{D}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),~\rho^{D}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),~\mu^{D}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}),~\eta^{D}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
and
\theta^{D}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. We have

\displaystyle\pi^{D}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(x,y)+\eta(x,D(y))-\eta(y,D(x)),

\displaystyle\rho^{D}(x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(x,v)+\mu(D(x),v)-D(\eta(x,v)),

\displaystyle\mu^{D}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(u,v),

\displaystyle\eta^{D}(x,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta(x,v),

\displaystyle\theta^{D}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)+\rho(x,D(y))-\rho(y,D(x))-D(\pi(x,y))

\displaystyle+\mu(D(x),D(y))-D(\eta(x,D(y)))+D(\eta(y,D(x))),

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Consequently,
D:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is a
\mathfrak{D}
-map if and only if the Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\pi^{D})
and
(\mathfrak{h},\mu)
form a matched pair of Lie algebras.

Proof.

By (
16
), we have

\Omega^{D}((0,u),(0,v))=e^{-D}\Omega(e^{D}(0,u),e^{D}(0,v))=\Omega((0,u),(0,v))\in\mathfrak{h}.

Thus
\mathfrak{h}
is a Lie subalgebra of
(\mathcal{G},\Omega^{D})
, which implies that
((\mathcal{G},\Omega^{D}),\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
is a quasi-twilled Lie algebra.

For all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, by (
16
), we have

\displaystyle(\pi^{D}(x,y),\theta^{D}(x,y))=\Omega^{D}\Big((x,0),(y,0)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\pi(x,y)+\eta(x,D(y))-\eta(y,D(x)),\theta(x,y)+\rho(x,D(y))-\rho(y,D(x))-D(\pi(x,y))

\displaystyle+\mu(D(x),D(y))-D(\eta(x,D(y)))+D(\eta(y,D(x)))\Big),

and

\displaystyle(\eta^{D}(x,v),\rho^{D}(x,v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega^{D}\Big((x,0),(0,v)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta(x,v),\rho(x,v)+\mu(D(x),v)-D(\eta(x,v))),

which completes the proof.
∎

Apply the above result to the quasi-Lie bialgebra
(\mathfrak{g},\pi,\mu,\theta)
, a
\mathfrak{D}
-map gives rise to a Lie bialgebra.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let

be a
\mathfrak{D}
-map of the quasi-twilled Lie algebra

,
\theta^{D}=0

Thus
((\mathfrak{g},\pi^{D}),(\mathfrak{g}^{*},\mu^{D}))

Moreover, by

Definition 3.15
.

(
[
27
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{k}

\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space. A

\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

for every

\displaystyle l_{n}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),

(ii)

(generalized Jacobi identity)
for all
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in S(i,n-i)}\varepsilon(\sigma)l_{n-i+1}(l_{i}(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(i)}),x_{\sigma(i+1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)})=0,

\varepsilon(\sigma)=\varepsilon(\sigma;x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})

We denote a curved

(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{+\infty})

L_{\infty}

]

Definition 3.16

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{+\infty})

L_{\infty}
-algebra. A

0

l_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{k!}l_{k}(x,\cdots,x)=0.

Let

be a Maurer-Cartan element of a curved

-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{+\infty})

l_{k}^{x}:\otimes^{k}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}~~(k\geq 1)

Theorem 3.17
.

(
[
17
,
23
]
)

With the above notation,
(\mathfrak{g},\{l_{k}^{x}\}_{k=1}^{+\infty})
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra which is called the twisted

-algebra by

Remark 3.18

Definition 3.19

(
[
55
]
)
A

consists of a quadruple
(L,F,P,\Delta)

\bullet

\bullet

.

(
[

Let
(L,F,P,\Delta)

(F,\{l_{k}\}_{k=0}^{+\infty})
is a curved
L_{\infty}
-algebra, where

are given by

l_{0}=P(\Delta),\quad l_{k}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=P([\cdots[[\Delta,x_{1}],x_{2}],\cdots,x_{n}]).

Now we are ready to give the controlling algebra of
\mathfrak{D}

Theorem 3.21
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})

(L,F,P,\Delta)
as follows:

\bullet

\displaystyle l_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle l_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\pi+\rho,f]_{\mathrm{NR}}

\displaystyle l_{2}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[\mu+\eta,f]_{\mathrm{NR}},g]_{\mathrm{NR}}.

It is straightforward.

be a Reynolds operator. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
is a Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{B}
is given by

[x,y]_{B}=[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}-[B(y),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Moreover, the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
represents on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
via
\sigma:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
given by

\sigma(x)y=[B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+B([y,x]_{\mathfrak{g}})-B([B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

The corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology is taken to be the
cohomology for the Reynolds operator

B
. See
[
10
]
for more details.

Definition
4.21
leads the following definition of cohomologies of deformation maps of a matched pair of Lie algebras.

Consider the quasi-twilled Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\bowtie\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
given in Example
2.10
obtained from a matched pair of Lie algebras. Let
B:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be a deformation map of a matched pair of Lie algebras. Then
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
is a Lie algebra, where the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{B}
is given by

[u,v]_{B}=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho(B(u))v-\rho(B(v))u,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Moreover, the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
represents on the vector space
\mathfrak{g}
via
\sigma:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
given by

\sigma(v)x=\eta(v)x+[B(v),x]_{\mathfrak{g}}+B(\rho(x)v),\quad\forall v\in\mathfrak{h},x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Definition 4.27
.

The corresponding Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B})
with coefficients in the representation
(\mathfrak{g},\sigma)
is taken to be the
cohomology for the deformation map
B
of a matched pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
of Lie algebras
.

Remark 4.28
.

In
[
1
]
, authors constructed the Lie algebra structure
[\cdot,\cdot]_{B}
on
\mathfrak{h}
via another approach, namely transfer the Lie algebra structure on
\mathrm{Gr}(B)
to
\mathfrak{h}
.

Remark 4.29
.

In
[

,

Similarly, one can also study infinitesimal deformations of a deformation map of a matched pair and show that they are classified by the second cohomology group of a deformation map of a matched pair given in Definition

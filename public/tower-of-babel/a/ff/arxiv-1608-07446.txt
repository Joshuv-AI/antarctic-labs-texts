Non-semi-stable loci in Hecke stacks and Fargues’ conjecture
Ildar Gaisin, Naoki Imai

Abstract
We show the Harris–Viehmann conjecture under some Hodge–Newton reducibility condition for a generalization of the diamond of a non-basic Rapoport–Zink space at infinite level, which appears as a cover of the non-semi-stable locus in the Hecke stack. We show also that the cohomology of the non-semi-stable locus with coefficient coming from a cuspidal Langlands parameter vanishes. As an application, we show the Hecke eigensheaf property in Fargues’ conjecture for cuspidal Langlands parameters in the GL 2 \GL_{2} -case.

H_{\mathrm{c}}^{i}\bigl(\mathcal{T}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},{\overleftarrow{t}}_{b,b^{\prime}}^{*}\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}\bigr)=0.

Proof.

We have

{\overleftarrow{t}}_{b,b^{\prime}}^{*}\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}=t_{b,b^{\prime}}^{*}x_{b^{\prime}}^{*}\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}=t_{b,b^{\prime}}^{*}\left(\bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat{S}_{\varphi},\,\rho|_{Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}}=\kappa(b^{\prime})}\underline{\rho}\otimes\underline{\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}}\right)

by (
3.1
).
We take
\rho\in\widehat{S}_{\varphi}

such that
\rho|_{Z(\widehat{G})^{\Gamma}}=\kappa(b^{\prime})
.
Then it suffices to show that

H_{\mathrm{c}}^{i}\Bigl(\mathcal{T}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},t_{b,b^{\prime}}^{*}\underline{\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}}\Bigr)=0.

The pullback of

\underline{\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}}
to

\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime}}^{\mu}
is a constant sheaf,
since the map

\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime}}^{\mu}\to[\Spa(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})/\underline{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}]

factorizes via
\Spa(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.
Hence, there is a Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

H_{i}\Bigl(J_{b^{\prime}}(E),H^{j}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}\Bigr)\Rightarrow H^{j-i}_{\mathrm{c}}\Bigl(\mathcal{T}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},t_{b,b^{\prime}}^{*}\underline{\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}}\Bigr)

by (
2.1
).
We show that

H_{i}\bigl(J_{b^{\prime}}(E),H^{j}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}\bigr)=0

for all
i
and
j
.
Take a projective resolution

\cdots\longrightarrow V_{1}\longrightarrow V_{0}\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{c}}^{j}\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)

as smooth
L_{b^{\prime}}(E)
-representations. By Lemma
4.25
and Theorem
4.26
we have

H^{j}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)\simeq\Ind_{P_{b^{\prime}}(E)}^{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}H_{\mathrm{c}}^{j}\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)

as smooth
J_{b^{\prime}}(E)
-representations.
Moreover, the induction on the right-hand-side is parabolic
by Lemma
4.28
.
Parabolic induction preserves projective objects,
since
it has a Jacquet functor as the right adjoint functor
by Bernstein’s second adjoint theorem
(
cf.
[
Bus01
, Theorem 3]
)
and
the Jacquet functor is exact.
Note also that
parabolic induction is exact.
Thus we obtain the projective resolution

\cdots\longrightarrow\Ind_{P_{b^{\prime}}(E)}^{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}V_{1}\longrightarrow\Ind_{P_{b^{\prime}}(E)}^{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}V_{0}\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)

as smooth
J_{b^{\prime}}(E)
-representations.
Finally the right adjoint of
-\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}

in the category of smooth
J_{b^{\prime}}(E)
-representations is

-\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}^{*}
,
where
\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}^{*}
is the smooth dual of
\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}
.
Both functors are exact and so in particular
-\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}
preserves exact sequences and projective objects. Thus we obtain the projective resolution

\cdots\longrightarrow\Ind_{P_{b^{\prime}}(E)}^{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}V_{1}\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}\longrightarrow\Ind_{P_{b^{\prime}}(E)}^{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}V_{0}\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}\longrightarrow H^{j}_{\mathrm{c}}\bigl(\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}}^{\mu},\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\bigr)\otimes\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}

Note that

P_{b^{\prime}}
is a proper parabolic subgroup of
J_{b^{\prime}}
,
since
b
is not basic.
For
i\geq 0
, we have

\Bigl(\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}\otimes\Ind_{P_{b^{\prime}}(E)}^{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}V_{i}\Bigr)_{J_{b^{\prime}}(E)}=0,

since
\pi_{\varphi,b^{\prime},\rho}
is cuspidal.
Hence we have the claim.
∎

5
Non-abelian Lubin–Tate theory

Assume that
G=\GL_{n}

and
\mu(z)=\diag(z,1,\ldots,1)
.
In this case,

S_{\varphi}
is trivial and

\Hecke^{\leq\mu}=\Hecke^{\mu}
.
We simply write

\pi_{\varphi,b}
for
\pi_{\varphi,b,1}

for any
[b]\in B(\GL_{n})_{\basic}
.
We put

b_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&\varpi\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{n}(E)

Then we have a bijection

\mathbb{Z}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}B(\GL_{n})_{\basic};\ N\mapsto b_{1}^{N}.

The following proposition is a consequence of non-abelian Lubin–Tate theory.

Proposition 5.1
.

We put
b=b_{1}^{N}
for an integer
N
.
Assume that
N\equiv 0,1\mod n
.
Then we have

y_{b}^{*}\overrightarrow{h}_{\natural}(\overleftarrow{h}^{*}\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}\otimes\IC_{\mu}^{\prime})=y_{b}^{*}(\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}\boxtimes\varphi).

Proof.

We show the claim
in the case where
N\equiv 1\mod n

using arguments in
[
MFO16
, Chapter 23]
.
See arguments in
[
Far16
, 8.1]

for the case where
N\equiv 0\mod n
.
Since the natural morphism

[\Spa(\breve{E})^{\diamond}/\widetilde{J}_{b}]\longrightarrow[\Div_{X,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}^{1}/\widetilde{J}_{b}]

induces an equivalence of étale sites
(
cf.
[
MFO16
, 22.3.2]
),
it suffices to show that

\tilde{y}_{b}^{*}\overrightarrow{h}_{\natural}(\overleftarrow{h}^{*}\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}\otimes\IC_{\mu}^{\prime})=\tilde{y}_{b}^{*}(\mathscr{F}_{\varphi}\boxtimes\varphi).

(5.1)

Suppose that
N=mn+1
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The following lemma provides an explicit description of the stack
\Hecke^{\leq\mu}_{b}
.

.

Let
\Spa(F,F^{+})

\Perf_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}

.

.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{LT}}^{\infty}

\mathcal{M}_{b,b^{\prime}}^{\leq\mu}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{LT}}^{\infty,\diamond}

For a perfectoid affinoid

and

Hence, the claim follows from

[
SW13

by

.

Let

p_{b}\colon\Spa\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\flat}\longrightarrow\Spa(\breve{E})^{\diamond}\longrightarrow[\Spa(\breve{E})^{\diamond}/\widetilde{J}_{b}]

(5.2)

) is equivalent to

.

\pi_{\varphi,b}\otimes\varphi
in degree zero

.

\IC_{\mu}^{\prime}=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}

in this case.

i

Assume that
G=\GL_{2}

and
\mu(z)=\diag(z,1)
in this section.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Spa(F,F^{+})
be a geometric point in
\Perf_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.
Let

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}\longrightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathscr{F}\longrightarrow 0

be an exact sequence of coherent sheaf over

,
where
\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
are vector bundles of rank
2

and
\mathscr{F}
is a torsion coherent sheaf of

.
Assume that

\mathscr{E}
is not semi-stable
and

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is semi-stable.
Then

\mathscr{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m-1)

and

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\simeq\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)

for some integer
m
.

Proof.

The vector bundle
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(m+\frac{1}{2})
or

\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)
for some integer
m
,
since it is semi-stable.

If
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(m+\frac{1}{2})
,
then

\mathscr{E}

is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)

by
[
FF14
, Theorem 2.9]
.
This contradict to the condition that

\mathscr{E}
is not semi-stable.

Assume

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}

is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m)
.
Then

\mathscr{E}

is isomorphic to

\mathcal{O}(m_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(m_{2})

with
m_{1},m_{2}\leq m
or

\mathcal{O}(n+\frac{1}{2})

with

by
[

.
By considering

the possible cases are

\mathcal{O}(m)\oplus\mathcal{O}(m-1)

\mathcal{O}(m-\frac{1}{2})
.
However, the latter case does not happen,
since
\mathscr{E}
is not semi-stable.

A note on critical intersections of classical and Schatten p p -balls
Mathias Sonnleitner, Christoph Thäle

Abstract
The purpose of this note is to study the asymptotic volume of intersections of unit balls associated with two norms in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} as their dimension n n tends to infinity. A general framework is provided and then specialized to the following cases. For classical ℓ p n \ell_{p}^{n} -balls the focus lies on the case p = ∞ p=\infty , which has previously not been studied in the literature. As far as Schatten p p -balls are considered, we concentrate on the cases p = 2 p=2 and p = ∞ p=\infty . In both situations we uncover an unconventional limiting behavior. Keywords . Asymptotic geometric analysis, Gumbel distribution, ℓ p \ell_{p} -ball, Schatten p p -ball, Tracy-Widom distribution, volume distribution. MSC . 52A23, 60F05.

Introduction

One way to approach the asymptotic distribution of volume in a sequence of convex bodies in high dimensions is to study the limiting behavior of the intersection volumes with another sequence of gauge or test bodies. In particular, this applies to the unit balls of finite dimensional normed spaces. To give a concrete example, let us consider the
\ell_{p}^{n}
-balls for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, which are defined as

\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}:=\begin{cases}\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{j}|^{p}\leq 1\}&\colon p<\infty\\
\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}|x_{j}|\leq 1\}&\colon p=\infty.\end{cases}

We also define their volume-normalized versions

\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})^{-1/n}\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}

with
\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})^{1/n}
being the volume radius. The following result has been shown in the non-critical case
t\neq t_{p,q}
by Schechtman and Schmuckenschläger in
[
20
]
and in the critical case
t=t_{p,q}
by Schmuckenschläger in
[
22
]
(for
p=\infty
) and
[
23
]
(for
p<\infty
), both building on a probabilistic approach to
\ell_{p}
-balls independently developed in
[
17
]
and
[
21
]
. A streamlined modern proof together with a multivariate extension has been given in
[
12
]
.
There are also closely related further results in this direction with respect to other families of norms and convex sets. For example, there are results for the simplex
[
3
]
, mixed-norm balls
[
8
]
,
\ell_{p}
-ellipsoids
[
9
]
, Orlicz balls
[
10
]
and Lorentz balls
[

]
. We also refer to the survey article
[
16
]
.

Proposition 1

(Schechtman/Schmuckenschläger)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q<\infty
be such that
p\neq q
. Then there exists a constant
t_{p,q}\in(0,\infty)
only depending on
p
and
q
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}\cap t\mathbb{D}_{q}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\colon t<t_{p,q}\\
1/2&\colon t=t_{p,q}\\
1&\colon t>t_{p,q}.\\
\end{cases}

Remark 1
.

We remark that the constant
t_{p,q}
is known explicitly, but rather involved. We refer the reader to the aforementioned papers for details.

It seems that the case
q=\infty
in Proposition
1
has been overlooked in the existing literature. Our first result closes this gap.

Theorem 2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. For any
t>0
it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}\cap t\mathbb{D}_{\infty}^{n})=0.

Moreover,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}\cap t(\log n)^{1/p}\mathbb{D}_{\infty}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\colon t<t_{p,\infty}\\
0&\colon t=t_{p,\infty}\text{ and }p>1\\
e^{-1}&\colon t=t_{1,\infty}\text{ and }p=1\\
1&\colon t>t_{p,\infty},\\
\end{cases}

where
t_{p,\infty}=e^{-1/p}\Gamma(1+1/p)^{-1}
.

Of particular interest for us in this note are the non-commutative analogues of the
\ell_{p}^{n}
-balls considered above. To introduce them, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
, and define the space
H_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta})
of all self-adjoint
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{\beta}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{1}=\mathbb{R}
is the field of real numbers,
\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{C}
the field of complex numbers and
\mathbb{F}_{4}=\mathbb{H}
is the skew-field of quaternions. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we define the unit ball with respect to the Schatten-
p
-norm by

\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}:=\begin{cases}\Big\{A\in H_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta})\colon\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\lambda_{j}(A)|^{p}\leq 1\Big\}&\colon p<\infty\\[5.69054pt]
\Big\{A\in H_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta})\colon\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}|\lambda_{j}(A)|\leq 1\Big\}&\colon p=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{1}(A),\ldots,\lambda_{n}(A)
denote the eigenvalues of
A
. Since
H_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\beta})
is a subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{\beta}^{n^{2}}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{\beta n^{2}}
of dimension

\displaystyle d_{n}:=\beta\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+n,

(1)

the convex bodies
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
have dimension
d_{n}
as well and we denote their
d_{n}
-dimensional Lebesgue measure by
\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})
. We can again consider the normalized Schatten
p
-balls

\mathbb{D}_{p,\beta}^{n}:=\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})^{-1/d_{n}}\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}.

The limiting behavior of the intersection volumes of these balls has been determined in
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
, which states that for any
t>0
and for all
1\leq p,q<\infty
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{D}_{p,\beta}^{n}\cap t\mathbb{D}_{q,\beta}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\colon t<t_{p,q,\beta}\\
1&\colon t>t_{p,q,\beta},\\
\end{cases}

where

t_{p,q,\beta}:=e^{{1\over 2}(1/p-1/q)}\Big(\frac{2p}{p+q}\Big)^{1/q}.

In view of the behavior of
\ell_{p}^{n}
balls described by Proposition
1
, it is natural to ask what happens at the threshold
t=t_{p,q,\beta}
. We recall that the corresponding result for
\ell_{p}^{n}
-balls was based on a central limit theorem for the
q
-norm of a uniformly distributed random point in
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
. However, for Schatten
p
-balls a result of this type is not available. Our next theorem can be considered as a first attempt to close this gap. More precisely, we are able to prove the following behavior for the critical intersections in the special case
\beta=2
,
p=2
and
q=\infty
.

Theorem 3
.

For
\beta=2
and any
t>0
it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{D}_{2,\beta}^{n}\cap t\mathbb{D}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})\begin{cases}0&\colon t<t_{2,\infty,\beta}\\
F_{\beta}(0)^{2}&\colon t=t_{2,\infty,\beta}\\
1&\colon t>t_{2,\infty,\beta},\\
\end{cases}

where
F_{\beta}
is the distribution function of the Tracy-Widom(
\beta
) distribution (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 3.1]
) and
t_{2,\infty,2}=e^{1/4}
.

Remark 3
.

We have
t_{2,\infty,2}=e^{1/4}=\lim_{q\to\infty}t_{2,q,2}
. It seems reasonable to believe that the non-critical case of Theorem
3
extends to
p,\beta\neq 2
with
t_{p,\infty,\beta}=\lim_{q\to\infty}t_{p,q,\beta}=e^{1/2p}
.

The limitation of Theorem
3
to
p=2
,
q=\infty
and
\beta=2
has two major reasons. The first one can be described as follows:

1.

Only for
p=2
and
p=\infty
the volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
is known exactly. Although this is not strictly needed, what is required is a precise asymptotic analysis of the volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
on a scale which is finer than that considered in
[
13

. It seems, however, that the technique used in
[
13
]
cannot be refined to give a result of appropriate quality.

The restriction to the case
p=2
and
q=\infty
can now be used to give some insight about the Tracy-Widom distribution and why it appears in our context. We briefly explain the connection of our problem to a classical question in random matrix theory. For that purpose consider for
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
the eigenvalues
X_{i}=X_{i}(\beta)
of a random matrix from a standard Gaussian
\beta
-ensemble. More precisely, this is the matrix ensemble GOE (Gaussian orthogonal ensemble) if
\beta=1
, GUE (Gaussian unitary ensemble) if
\beta=2
and GSE (Gaussian symplectic ensemble) if
\beta=4
. It is well-known that the largest eigenvalue satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(n^{-1/2}\max X_{i}-\sqrt{2})\leq x]=F_{\beta}(x),

(2)

where
F_{\beta}
is the Tracy-Widom(
\beta
) distribution, see
[
2
, Section 3.1]
or for this formulation
[
5
]
.
In order to derive Theorem
3
we need the limit law (
2
) or more precisely a limit theorem for
\max|X_{i}|
, for which it suffices to have a joint limit theorem for the largest and the smallest eigenvalue. At this point, we can now explain the limitation of Theorem
3
to
\beta=2
:

2.

The limitation to the case
\beta=2
has its origin in the fact that only in this case it is known from
[
4
, Corollary 1]
(see also
[
5
]
for more precise asymptotics) that the fluctuations of the smallest and the largest eigenvalue of random matrices from a Gaussian
\beta
-ensemble are asymptotically independent.

To be more precise about this second point, let us denote for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
the centred and rescaled smallest and largest eigenvalues of a random matrices from a
\beta
-ensemble by

X_{\beta,n}^{\min}:=\sqrt{2}n^{1/6}(\min X_{i}+\sqrt{2n})\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{\beta,n}^{\max}:=\sqrt{2}n^{1/6}(\max X_{i}-\sqrt{2n}),

respectively. It seems reasonable to conjecture that these two random variables are asymptotically independent in the following sense.

Conjecture 4
.

For
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\min}\leq x,X_{\beta,n}^{\max}\leq y]-\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\min}\leq x]\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\max}\leq y]\Big)=0.

As already mentioned above,
[
4
, Corollary 1]
shows that Conjecture
4
is true for
\beta=2
. To the best of our knowledge, for
\beta\in\{1,4\}
the conjecture is still open. This is also discussed after Proposition 1 in
[
6
]
. A positive answer would immediately imply that Theorem
3
holds true for all
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
.

2
A general framework

In order to give a unified presentation of asymptotics of intersection volumes of unit balls, we choose to take a look at the general picture which we apply to
\ell_{p}^{n}
- and Schatten
p
-balls in the two forthcoming sections.

Fix a dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{X^{n}}
and
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{Y^{n}}
be two norms on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The closed unit balls generated by these two norms will be denoted by
B_{X}^{n}
and
B_{Y}^{n}
and we write
D_{X}^{n}
and
D_{Y}^{n}
for their volume-normalized versions, respectively. If
Z
is uniformly distributed in
B_{X}^{n}
, then
\mathrm{vol}(B_{X}^{n})^{-1/n}Z
is uniformly distributed in
D_{X}^{n}
and, for any
t>0
, positive homogenity implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{vol}_{n}(D_{X}^{n}\cap tD_{Y}^{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big[\mathrm{vol}(B_{X})^{-1/n}Z\in t\,\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{-1/n}B_{Y}^{n}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big[\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\leq t\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{X}^{n})^{1/n}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{1/n}}\Big].

Now, suppose that there are constants
c_{X},c_{Y}>0
and positive sequences
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{X}^{n})^{1/n}\sim c_{X}a_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{1/n}\sim c_{Y}b_{n}

holds as
n\to\infty
, where for two sequences
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(A_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
we write
A_{n}\sim A_{n}^{\prime}
, provided that
A_{n}/A_{n}^{\prime}\to 1
, as
n\to\infty
. From a weak law of large numbers for
\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}
one can now deduce the behavior of the asymptotic intersection volumes of
D_{X}^{n}
and
D_{Y}^{n}
in the non-critical cases.

Lemma 5
.

If the convergence

\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}}\to c,\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty,

holds in probability for some
c\in[0,\infty]
, then for any
t>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(D_{X}^{n}\cap tD_{Y}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\colon t<t_{X,Y}\\
1&\colon t>t_{X,Y},\\
\end{cases}

where
t_{X,Y}:=c\frac{c_{Y}}{c_{X}}
.

Proof.

Let
t>0
. Then

\mathbb{P}\Big[\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\leq t\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{X}^{n})^{1/n}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{1/n}}\Big]=\mathbb{P}\Big[\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}}\leq t\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{X}^{n})^{1/n}a_{n}^{-1}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{1/n}b_{n}^{-1}}\Big]

The random variable
\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}}
on the left-hand side tends in probability to
c
by assumption. The expression on the right-hand side deterministically converges to
t\frac{c_{X}}{c_{Y}}
. This gives the result.
∎

In the critical case we need information on the fluctuations of the
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{Y^{n}}
-norm of
Z
and more precise asymptotics for volume radii. In particular, let us assume that

\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{X}^{n})^{1/n}a_{n}^{-1}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{1/n}b_{n}^{-1}}=\frac{c_{X}}{c_{Y}}(1+e_{n}),

(3)

where
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence satisfying
e_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Under this assumption we have the following result.

Lemma 6
.

Suppose that the convergence

c_{n}\Big(\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}}-c\Big)\to R\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty

holds in distribution for some
c>0
, a positive sequence
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
c_{n}\to\infty
and with a random variable
R
having a continuous distribution function. Additionally assume that the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}e_{n}c_{n}
exists in the extended real line
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
. Then, for
t_{X,Y}
as in Lemma
5
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(D_{X}^{n}\cap t_{X,Y}D_{Y}^{n})=\mathbb{P}[R\leq c\lim_{n\to\infty}e_{n}c_{n}],

where we use the convention that
\mathbb{P}[R\leq-\infty]=0
and
\mathbb{P}[R\leq+\infty]=1
.

Proof.

Write

\mathbb{P}\Big[\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\leq t\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{X}^{n})^{1/n}}{\mathrm{vol}_{n}(B_{Y}^{n})^{1/n}}\Big]=\mathbb{P}\Big[c_{n}\Big(\|Z\|_{Y^{n}}\frac{b_{n}}{a_{n}}-c\Big)\leq c\,e_{n}c_{n}\Big].

Taking limits and using the assumed continuity of the distribution function of the random variable
R
gives the result.
∎

Remark 4
.

For completeness we mention that the final conclusion in the previous proof relies on the following fact. For distribution functions
F,F_{1},F_{2},\ldots
and real numbers
a,a_{1},a_{2},\dots
we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}(a_{n})=F(a),

whenever
F_{n}\to F
pointwise,
F
is continuous and
a_{n}\to a
, as
n\to\infty
. This follows from the implied uniform convergence
F_{n}\to F
, which is a classical result due to Polya, see
[
15
, Satz I]
.

Our arguments below will also rely on the following consequence of Slutsky’s theorem.

Lemma 7
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
A_{n},B_{n}
be random variables and
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be positive sequences such that
a_{n},b_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Suppose that
B_{n}\neq 0
almost surely for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{n}/b_{n}\to\infty
and assume that there are random variables
A,B
and real numbers
a,b
with
b\neq 0
such that

a_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}-a)\to A\qquad\text{and}\qquad b_{n}^{-1}(B_{n}-b)\to B\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty

hold in distribution. Then we have the following convergence in distribution:

a_{n}^{-1}\Big(\frac{A_{n}}{B_{n}}-\frac{a}{b}\Big)\to b^{-1}A\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Write

a_{n}^{-1}\Big(\frac{A_{n}}{B_{n}}-\frac{a}{b}\Big)=\frac{a_{n}^{-1}(A_{n}-a)b+a(b_{n}/a_{n})b_{n}^{-1}(b-B_{n})}{B_{n}b}.

By Slutsky’s theorem the numerator tends to
bA
in distribution and since
B_{n}\to b
in probability, the denominator tends to
b^{2}
in probability. Another application of Slutsky’s theorem gives the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2

To show the first part of Theorem
2
our goal is to apply Lemma
5
. The required distributional limit theorem corresponds to Theorem 1.1(c) in
[
12
]
. It says that if
Z
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
, then

\frac{n^{1/p}}{(p\log n)^{1/p-1}}\|Z\|_{\infty}-A_{n}^{(p)}\to\Lambda

in distribution as
n\to\infty
, where

A_{n}^{(p)}=p\log n-\frac{1-p}{p}\log(p\log n)+p\log K_{p}\qquad\text{with}\qquad K_{p}=\frac{1}{p^{1/p}\Gamma(1+1/p)}

and where
\Lambda
is a Gumbel random variable with distribution function
F_{\Lambda}(t)=e^{-e^{-t}},t\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, it holds that

\|Z\|_{\infty}n^{1/p}\to+\infty,

in probability as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1/p}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})^{1/n}=c_{p}:=2e^{1/p}p^{1/p}\Gamma(1+1/p)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty}^{n})^{1/n}=2.

This brings us into the position to apply Lemma
5
with
a_{n}=n^{1/p}
,
b_{n}=1
,
c_{X}=c_{p},c_{Y}=2
and
c=+\infty
. This proves the first part of the theorem.

To deduce the second part of Theorem
2
, fix
t>0
and again let
Z
be uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}
. Using computations similar to the above, we arrive at

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{D}_{p}^{n}\cap t(\log n)^{1/p}\mathbb{D}_{\infty}^{n})=\mathbb{P}\Big[\frac{n^{1/p}}{(p\log n)^{1/p-1}}\|Z\|_{\infty}-A_{n}^{(p)}\leq R_{n}^{(p)}\Big]

with

R_{n}^{(p)}:=\frac{p\,t\,n^{1/p}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})^{1/n}}{2p^{1/p}}\log n-A_{n}^{(p)}.

Next, we write

n^{1/p}\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n})^{1/n}=c_{p}(1+e_{n}),

where
e_{n}=-\frac{\log n}{2n}+O(n^{-1})
, see
[
23
, page 194]
. Then

R_{n}^{(p)}=\Big(\frac{p\,t\,c_{p}}{2p^{1/p}}(1+e_{n})-p+\frac{1-p}{p}\frac{\log(p\log n)}{\log n}-\frac{p\log K_{p}}{\log n}\Big)\log n.

Setting

t_{p,\infty}:=\frac{2p^{1/p}}{c_{p}}=e^{-1/p}\Gamma(1+1/p)^{-1},

we deduce that

R_{n}^{(p)}\to\begin{cases}-\infty&\colon t<t_{p,\infty},\\
-\infty&\colon t=t_{p,\infty},p>1\\
-\log K_{1}&\colon t=t_{1,\infty},p=1\\
+\infty&\colon t>t_{p,\infty},\end{cases}

as
n\to\infty
. Noting that
K_{1}=1
and
\mathbb{P}[\Lambda\leq 0]=e^{-1}
, the second part of Theorem
2
follows from Lemma
6
. ∎

4
Proof of Theorem
3

We will carry out the proof for arbitrary
\beta
as far as possible and specialize to the particular case
\beta=2
only if strictly needed. Before we prove Theorem
3
, we collect some facts about the volume of Schatten
p
-balls. First, observe that the dimension
d_{n}
of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
defined by (
1
) satisfies
d_{n}\sim\frac{\beta}{2}n^{2}
. In general and as explained in
[
13
, Section 3]
or
[
19
]
the volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
can be represented as

\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})=c_{n,\beta}I_{n,\beta,p},

where

c_{n,\beta}=\frac{1}{n!}\Big(\frac{2\pi^{\beta/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{\beta}{2})}\Big)^{-n}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\frac{2(2\pi)^{\beta k/2}}{2^{\beta/2}\Gamma(\frac{\beta k}{2})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{n,\beta,p}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{p}^{n}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}|^{\beta}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}\lambda_{n}.

The integral
I_{n,\beta,p}
can be computed exactly only in the cases
p=2
and
p=\infty
using integral formulas due to Mehta and Selberg, respectively, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.5.8]
. For
p=2
we have
\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n}=\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d_{n}}
and thus

\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})=\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2}^{d_{n}})=\frac{\pi^{d_{n}/2}}{\Gamma(1+d_{n}/2)}.

This is due to the fact that the Schatten norm becomes the Frobenius norm in this case, which in turn is the usual Euclidean norm after interpreting a matrix as a vector. Furthermore,

\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})=c_{n,\beta}2^{d_{n}}n!\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma\left(1+j\frac{\beta}{2}\right)^{2}\Gamma\left((j+1)\frac{\beta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(2+(n+j-1)\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)}.

By refining the method used by Saint-Raymond in
[
19
]
, the asymptotic volume radius of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
has been obtained in
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, which says that

\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}\sim n^{-(1/p+1/2)}\Delta(p)\Big(\frac{4\pi}{\beta}\Big)^{1/2}e^{3/4},

(4)

where

\Delta(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{p\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(p/2)}{\sqrt{e}\Gamma((p+1)/2)}\Big)^{1/p}&\colon p<\infty\\
\frac{1}{2}&\colon p=\infty.\end{cases}

In particular,
\Delta(2)=e^{-1/4}
since
\Gamma(3/2)=\sqrt{\pi}/2
. For our purposes we need the following fine asymptotics of the logarithmic volume of
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
, which we are able to provide only in the two special cases
p=2
and
p=\infty
.

Lemma 8
.

For
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
we have that

\frac{1}{d_{n}}\log\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})=-\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{1\over 2}+O(n^{-1}\log n)

and

\frac{1}{d_{n}}\log\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})=-{1\over 2}\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{3\over 4}-\log 2+O(n^{-1}\log n).

Proof.

We start with the case
p=2
and by recalling
[
1
, Identity 6.1.41]
, which says that

\displaystyle\log\Gamma(z)=(z-{1\over 2})\log z-z+{1\over 2}\log(2\pi)+O(z^{-1}),\qquad\text{as }z\to\infty.

(5)

This together with the fact that
d_{n}\sim{\beta n^{2}\over 2}
leads to

\displaystyle{1\over d_{n}}\log\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\log\pi-{1\over d_{n}}\log\Gamma\Big(1+{d_{n}\over 2}\Big)

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\log\pi-{1\over 2}\log{d_{n}\over 2}+{1\over d_{n}}{d_{n}\over 2}+O(n^{-1}\log n)

\displaystyle=-\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{1\over 2}+O(n^{-1}\log n),

as claimed.

For
p=\infty
we start by observing that

\displaystyle{1\over d_{n}}\log\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})

\displaystyle={1\over d_{n}}\log c_{n,\beta}+\log 2+{1\over d_{n}}\log n!+S_{n}

with

S_{n}:={1\over d_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Big[2\log\Gamma\Big(1+{\beta j\over 2}\Big)+\log\Gamma\Big((j+1){\beta\over 2}\Big)-\log\Gamma\Big(2+(n+j-1){\beta\over 2}\Big)-\log\Gamma\Big({\beta\over 2}\Big)\Big].

The proof of
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]
directly shows that

{1\over d_{n}}\log c_{n,\beta}=-{1\over 2}\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{3\over 4}+O(n^{-1}\log n).

Moreover,
{1\over d_{n}}\log n!=O(n^{-1}\log n)
by (
5
) and the fact that
d_{n}\sim{\beta n^{2}\over 2}
. Thus, it remains to determine the asymptotic behavior of
S_{n}
. First, we observe that

S_{n}={1\over d_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Big[2\log\Gamma\Big(1+{\beta j\over 2}\Big)+\log\Gamma\Big((j+1){\beta\over 2}\Big)-\log\Gamma\Big(2+(n+j-1){\beta\over 2}\Big)\Big]+O(n^{-1}),

since
d_{n}\sim{\beta n^{2}\over 2}
. Now, we use three times (
5
) to see that

\displaystyle S_{n}={1\over d_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\Big[{3\beta\over 2}j\log\Big({\beta j\over 2}\Big)-{3\beta\over 2}j-{\beta\over 2}(n+j)\log\Big({\beta\over 2}(n+j)\Big)+{\beta\over 2}(n+j)\Big]+O(n^{-1}\log n)

To evaluate these sums asymptotically, we use the following formula, which follows from Abel’s summation formula as in the proof of
[
13
, Lemma 3.3]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(an+bj)\log(an+bj)

\displaystyle=\Big(a+{b\over 2}\Big)n^{2}\log n+{n^{2}\over 4b}\big(2(a+b)^{2}\log(a+b)-2a^{2}\log a-b(2a+b)\big)+O(n\log n)

(6)

as
n\to\infty
for constants
a,b\geq 0
, where for
a=0
or
b=0
we use the convention
0^{2}\log 0=0
. Applying this with the choices
(a,b)=(0,{\beta\over 2})
and
(a,b)=({\beta\over 2},{\beta\over 2})
together with the fact that
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}j={n^{2}\over 2}+O(n)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle={1\over d_{n}}\Big[{3\beta\over 4}n^{2}\log n-{3\beta\over 8}\big(1+2\log 2-2\log\beta\big)n^{2}-{3\beta\over 4}n^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-{3\beta\over 4}n^{2}\log n-{\beta\over 8}\big(2\log 2+6\log\beta-3\big)n^{2}+{3\beta\over 4}n^{2}\Big]+O(n^{-1}\log n)

\displaystyle=-{1\over d_{n}}n^{2}\beta\log 2+O(n^{-1}\log n)

\displaystyle=-2\log 2+O(n^{-1}\log n),

using additionally that
d_{n}\sim{\beta n^{2}\over 2}
.

Summarizing, this leads to

\displaystyle{1\over d_{n}}\log\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})

\displaystyle=-{1\over 2}\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{3\over 4}+\log 2-2\log 2+O(n^{-1}\log n)

\displaystyle=-{1\over 2}\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{3\over 4}-\log 2+O(n^{-1}\log n),

which proves the second claim.
∎

Remark 5
.

Interpreting
1/\infty
as
0
and using that
\log\Delta(2)=-1/4
and
\log\Delta(\infty)=-\log 2
, the two formulas in Lemma
8
can be combined as follows:

{1\over d_{n}}\log\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}=-\Big({1\over 2}+{1\over p}\Big)\log n+{1\over 2}\log\Big({4\pi\over\beta}\Big)+{3\over 4}+\log\Delta(p)+O(n^{-1}\log n),

p\in\{2,\infty\}
. It remains an open problem if this formula continues to hold for arbitrary
p\geq 1
. As we pointed out in the introduction, the method used in
[
13
]
does not seem to be suitable to yield a result of this quality.

As a consequence, we obtain an upper bound on the speed of convergence of the ratio of volume radii of
\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n}
as required in (
3
).

Lemma 9
.

For
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
it holds that

\frac{n^{1/2}\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}}=\frac{\Delta(2)}{\Delta(\infty)}\big(1+O(n^{-1}\log n)\big).

Proof.

Write

T_{n}:=\frac{n^{1/2}\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}}{\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}}=\frac{\Delta(2)}{\Delta(\infty)}(1+e_{n}),

where
e_{n}\to 0
by the asymptotics in (
4
). Because of
\log(1+e_{n})=e_{n}+O(e_{n}^{2})
as
n\to\infty
, it follows that

\log T_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\log n+\frac{1}{d_{n}}\log\Big(\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})}{\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})}\Big)=\log\Delta(2)-\log\Delta(\infty)+e_{n}+O(e_{n}^{2}).

Now, Lemma
8
yields
e_{n}=O(n^{-1}\log n)
, which completes the proof.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
3
, we will use Lemma
6
. To apply it, we need the volume asymptotics from Lemma
9
and also a limit theorem for the norm of a random vector uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{B}_{2,2}^{n}
. It is based on the following stochastic representation taken from
[
13
, Corollary 4.3]
.

Lemma 10
.

If
Z
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{B}_{p,\beta}^{n}
for
1\leq p<\infty
, then

(\lambda_{\pi(1)}(Z),\dots,\lambda_{\pi(n)}(Z))\overset{\rm d}{=}U^{1/d_{n}}\frac{X}{\|X\|_{p}},

where
U
is uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
,
X
is independent of
U
and has joint density with respect to Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
proportional to

f_{p,\beta,n}(x)=e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(7)

and
\pi
is a uniformly distributed random permutation on
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Using this stochastic representation and known limit laws for spectral statistics of the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE), we can prove the following limit law.

Lemma 11
.

Let
Z
be uniformly distributed on
\mathbb{B}_{2,2}^{n}
. For every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(n^{1/2}\|Z\|_{\infty}-2)\leq x]=F_{2}(2^{-1/2}x)^{2}.

Before proving Lemma
11
, let us give a proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We start by writing

\frac{n\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{2,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}}{n^{1/2}\mathrm{vol}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{B}_{\infty,\beta}^{n})^{1/d_{n}}}=\frac{\Delta(2)}{\Delta(\infty)}(1+e_{n}).

Together with Lemma
11
this implies that
t_{2,\infty,2}=2\frac{\Delta(\infty)}{\Delta(2)}=e^{1/4}
, and with Lemma
5
we obtain the non-critical case. By Lemma
9
we have
e_{n}=O(n^{-1}\log n)
and consequently
e_{n}n^{2/3}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We can now deduce the critical case from Lemma
6
together with Lemma
11
.
∎

In the remainder of this section we give the proof of Lemma
11
. Recall that by Lemma
10
we have for
Z
uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{B}_{2,2}^{n}
that

n^{1/2}\|Z\|_{\infty}=n^{1/2}\max|\lambda_{i}(Z)|=U^{1/d_{n}}\frac{n^{-1/2}\max|X_{i}|}{\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|n^{-1/2}X_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}},

(8)

where
X
is distributed with density proportional to
f_{2,2,n}
, recall (
7
). We will first derive limit theorems for numerator and denominator of (
8
) separately and then combine them using Lemma
7
.

Lemma 12
.

If
X
is distributed with density proportional to
f_{2,2,n}
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}[\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(n^{-1/2}\max|X_{i}|-\sqrt{2})\leq x]=F_{2}(x)^{2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We put

X_{\beta,n}^{\min}:=\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(n^{-1/2}\min X_{i}+\sqrt{2})\quad\text{and}\quad X_{\beta,n}^{\max}:=\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(n^{-1/2}\max X_{i}-\sqrt{2}).

The fluctuations for the largest eigenvalue were stated in the introduction and, by symmetry of the density function, the random variables
\max X_{i}
and
-\min X_{i}
are identically distributed. Thus, we obtain that, as
n\to\infty
,

\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\max}\leq x]\to F_{\beta}(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\min}\leq x]\to 1-F_{\beta}(-x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

By the asymptotic independence stated in
[
4
, Corollary 1]
and mentioned above in Conjecture
4
we deduce that, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(n^{-1/2}\max|X_{i}|-\sqrt{2})\leq x]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\max}\leq x,X_{\beta,n}^{\min}\geq-x]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\max}\leq x]-\mathbb{P}[X_{\beta,n}^{\max}\leq x,X_{\beta,n}^{\min}\leq-x]

\displaystyle\to F_{2}(x)-F_{2}(x)(1-F_{2}(x))

\displaystyle=F_{2}(x)^{2},

as
n\to\infty
.
∎

For the numerator in (
8
) a central limit theorem holds, which we directly derive from the well-known fluctuations of linear spectral statistics. The relevant case
\beta=2
was already proved by Johansson in
[
7
]
but we will use the more general result from
[
14
]
.

Lemma 13
.

For
\beta\in\{1,2,4\}
it holds that

n\Big(\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(\beta n)^{-1/2}X_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\to N

in distribution as
n\to\infty
, where
N
is a Gaussian random variable with mean
\mu=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\beta})\frac{3}{4}
and some non-degenerate variance
\sigma^{2}>0
.

Proof.

The random variables
(\beta n)^{-1/2}X_{1},\ldots,(\beta n)^{-1/2}X_{n}
are distributed according to the density
P_{V,\beta}^{n}
as denoted in
[
14
]
where the
V\colon x\mapsto x^{2}
. The limiting measure is given by the Ullmann distribution
\mathcal{U}(2)
and if
W\sim\mathcal{U}(2)
, then
\mathbb{E}|W|^{2}=1/4
, see
[
18
, IV,Theorem 5.1]
. Therefore, by
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
we get

n\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(\beta n)^{-1/2}X_{i}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)\to N

in distribution as
n\to\infty
. We now apply the delta method with the function
g(x)=x^{1/2}
at the point
x_{0}=\frac{1}{4}
giving
g^{\prime}(x_{0})=1
to deduce the result.
∎

Proof of Lemma
11
.

Applying the stochastic representation of Lemma
10
for
\beta=2
we have

n^{1/2}\|Z\|_{\infty}=U^{1/d_{n}}\frac{n^{-1/2}\max|X_{i}|}{\sqrt{2}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|(2n)^{-1/2}X_{i}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}}

which is of the form
U^{1/d_{n}}\frac{A_{n}}{B_{n}}
. We deduce from Lemma
12
and Lemma
13
that

\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}(A_{n}-\sqrt{2})\to A\qquad\text{and}\qquad n(B_{n}-2^{-1/2})\to B,

as
n\to\infty
in distribution, where
A
has distribution function
F_{2}^{2}
and
B
is a Gaussian random variable with mean
\sqrt{2}\mu
and variance
2\sigma^{2}
.

As a consequence of Lemma
7
, applied with
a_{n}=2^{-1/2}n^{-2/3},b_{n}=n^{-1},a=\sqrt{2},b=2^{-1/2}
and in combination with Lemma
12
and Lemma
13
, we conclude that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big[\sqrt{2}n^{2/3}\Big(\frac{A_{n}}{B_{n}}-2\Big)\leq x\Big]=\mathbb{P}[A\leq 2^{-1/2}x]=F_{2}(2^{-1/2}x)^{2}.

The proof can now be concluded by an application of Slutsky’s theorem, since

, as

∎

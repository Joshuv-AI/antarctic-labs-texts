Abstract
. Let ( ℬ , 𝔼 , 𝔰 ) (\mathcal{B},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s}) be an extriangulated category and 𝒮 \mathcal{S} be an extension closed subcategory of ℬ \mathcal{B} . In this article, we prove that the Gabriel-Zisman localization ℬ / 𝒮 \mathcal{B}/\mathcal{S} can be realized as an ideal quotient inside ℬ \mathcal{B} when 𝒮 \mathcal{S} satisfies some mild conditions. The ideal quotient is an extriangulated category. We show that the equivalence between the ideal quotient and the localization preserves the extriangulated category structure. We also discuss the relations of our results with Hovey twin cotorsion pairs and Verdier quotients.

1.
Introduction

Triangulated categories were introduced in the mid 1960’s by Verdier
[
V
]
.
Having their origins in algebraic geometry and
algebraic topology, triangulated categories have by now become indispensable
in many different areas of mathematics. The Verdier quotient
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
by a triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is defined by a universal property with respect to triangulated functors out of
\mathcal{T}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
is in fact a localization of
\mathcal{T}
, which means
it is obtained from
\mathcal{T}
by formally inverting a class of morphisms.
For example, derived categories are certain Verdier quotients of homotopy categories.
Localization is a process of adding formal inverses to an algebraic structure known as Gabriel-Zisman localisation
[
GZ
]
, morphisms in the new category can be regarded as compositions of the original morphisms and the formal inverses
that were added. This makes Verdier quotients a bit hard to understand since
taking Verdier quotients drastically change morphisms.

Iyama and Yang
[
IYa2
]
gave a sufficient condition for a Verdier quotient
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
of a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
by a thick subcategory
\mathcal{S}
to be realized inside of
\mathcal{T}
as an ideal quotient.
Concretely speaking, they assume that
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
satisfy the following conditions:

(T0)

\mathcal{T}
is a triangulated category with a shift functor
[1]
, and
\mathcal{S}
is a thick subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. Denote by

\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
the Verdier quotient (which is a triangulated category).

(T1)

\mathcal{S}
has a torsion pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
.

(T2)

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\perp})
and
({{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}},\mathcal{Y})
form two torsion pairs in
\mathcal{T}
,
where
\mathcal{X}^{\perp}=:\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{X},T)=0\}

and
{{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}}=:\{T\in\mathcal{T}\mid{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(T,\mathcal{Y})=0\}
.

Define two full subcategories of
\mathcal{T}
:

\mathcal{Z}:=\mathcal{X}^{\perp}\cap{{}^{\perp}\mathcal{Y}[1]}\hskip 5.69054pt\mbox{and}\hskip 5.69054pt\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{X}[1]\cap\mathcal{Y}.

Denote by
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{M}]
the ideal quotient category of
\mathcal{Z}
by
\mathcal{M}
.
Iyama and Yang
[
IYa2
]
realized the Verdier quotient
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
as the ideal quotient

\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{M}]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
IYa2
, Theorem 1.1]

With the assumptions
(T0), (T1)
and
(T2)
as above,
the composition
\mathcal{Z}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{U}

of natural functors induces an equivalence of additive categories:

\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{M}]\xrightarrow{\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq\hskip 2.84526pt}\mathcal{U}
.

When the condition (T1) is replaced by a special case:

\text{(T1}^{\prime})\quad(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\text{ is a co-}t\text{-structure in }\mathcal{S},

the equivalence above becomes a
triangle
equivalence,
see
[
IYa2
, Theorem 1.2]
. There are many examples of this realization, such as Buchweitz’s equivalence
[
Bu
,
KV
,
R
]
between the singularity category of an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring and the stable category of Cohen-Macaulay modules over the ring. More related researches can be found in
[

,
BOJ
,
C
,
CZ
,
G
,
IYa1
,
K1
,
OPS
,
ZH
]
.

Recently, the notion of an extriangulated category was introduced by Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
as a
simultaneous generalization of exact categories and triangulated categories.
Exact categories and extension closed subcategories of a triangulated category
are extriangulated categories, hence
many results on exact categories and triangulated categories can be unified
in the framework of an extriangulated category. There are also plenty of examples of
extriangulated categories which are neither exact nor triangulated, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ
,
ZhZ
]
.

In
[

]
, Nakaoka and Palu gave a bijection between Hovey twin cotorsion pairs and admissible model structures.

Note that

.

Remark 1.4
.

In Theorem

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some elementary concepts and properties of extriangulated categories. In Section 3, we show the main result of this article: the existence of equivalences between localizations and quotient categories. In Section 4, we show that under certain conditions, the equivalence has better properties.

3.
Localizations and quotient subcategories

In an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

•

If we have a deflation

•

If we have an inflation
h:A\xrightarrow{~f~}B\xrightarrow{~g~}C
, then
f
is also an inflation.

Note that this condition automatically holds on triangulated categories and Krull-Schmidt exact categories.

Under this condition, we can show the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be a subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
which is closed under direct summands.

(a)

-approximation of
B
and a deflation, then there is a deflation

which is a minimal right
\mathcal{C}
-approximation.

(b)

If
f^{\prime}:B\to C^{\prime}
is a left
\mathcal{C}
-approximation of
B
and an inflation, then there is an inflation
f_{1}^{\prime}:B\to C_{1}^{\prime}
which is a minimal left
\mathcal{C}
-approximation.

Proof.

We only need to prove (a). Dually we can show (b).

If
f:C\to B
is a deflation, since
\mathcal{B}
is Krull-Schmidt, there exists a decomposition
C=C_{1}\oplus C_{2}
such that
f=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_{1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\colon C_{1}\oplus C_{2}\to B
where
f_{1}
is right minimal. Since
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}f_{1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)=f_{1}\circ\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, by condition (WIC), we get that
f_{1}
is also a deflation. Let
C_{0}
be any object in
\mathcal{C}
and
g\in\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{B}}(C_{0},B)
. Since
f
is a right
\mathcal{C}
-approximation of
B
, there is a morphism
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h_{1}\\
h_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right):C_{0}\to C_{1}\oplus C_{2}
such that

h_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=f_{1}h_{1}

is a right
\mathcal{C}
-approximation of
B
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Assume that
\mathcal{B}
has enough injectives. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a contravariantly finite subcategory which contains all the injective objects and is closed under extensions, direct summands and taking cones. Then
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{I})
is a cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, we have a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}^{\bot_{1}}),(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{I}))
in
\mathcal{B}
. By Theorem
3.8
,
\mathcal{M}^{\bot_{1}}/[\mathcal{I}]\simeq\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{M}
.

Dually, assume that
\mathcal{B}
has enough projectives. Let
\mathcal{N}
be a covariantly finite subcategory which contains all the projective objects and is closed under extensions, direct summands and taking cocones. Then
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{N})
is a cotorsion pair in
\mathcal{N}
. Moreover, we have a twin cotorsion pair
((\mathcal{P},\mathcal{B}),({{}^{\bot_{1}}}\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}))
in
\mathcal{B}
and
{{}^{\bot_{1}}}\mathcal{N}/[\mathcal{P}]\simeq\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{N}
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{B}
is a triangulated category and
\mathcal{M}
is a contravariantly (resp. covariantly) finite thick subcategory, then the Verdier quotient

is a triangle equivalent to
\mathcal{M}^{\bot_{1}}
(resp.

), which is a thick subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 3.16
.

There are many examples of extriangulated subcategories which have enough injectives. For example, any right triangulated category with a right semi-equivalence is an extriangulated category with enough injectives, see
[

Proposition 3.25
.

\mathcal{B}
be an triangulated category with shift functor
[1]
. Assume that the twin cotorsion pair

satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathcal{Y}
;

(2)

(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{Y})
is a hereditary cotorsion pair.

Then
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}_{L}
is the only extension closed subcategory in which
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a cotorsion pair. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
is closed under taking cones.

Since
\mathcal{B}
is triangulated, we have
\mathcal{S}_{R}=\mathcal{X}[-1]*\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{S}_{L}=\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{Y}[1]
. Since
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{Y})

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}[2])=0
. By Lemma
3.24
,
\mathcal{S}_{L}
and
\mathcal{S}_{R}
are closed under extensions. Hence
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}_{L}\cap\mathcal{S}_{R}
is an extriangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{B}
in which
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a cotorsion pair. By Proposition
3.23
, we have
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{S}_{L}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{R}
. Since
\mathcal{Y}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, we have
\mathcal{M}[1]\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{R}[1]=\mathcal{S}_{L}=\mathcal{M}
, which implies that
\mathcal{M}
is closed under taking cones.

If there is another extension closed subcategory
\mathcal{M}_{1}
in which
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
is a cotorsion pair, then we immediately get
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. Any object

admits an triangle

Y_{M}\in\mathcal{Y}
and
X_{M}\in\mathcal{X}
, since
Y_{M}[1]\in\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}
, we have

4.
Extriangle equivalences

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathfrak{s}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{B}_{2},\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathfrak{s}_{2})
be two extriangulated categories and
\sigma:\mathcal{B}_{1}\to\mathcal{B}_{2}
be an equivalent functor.
\sigma
is called an
extriangle equivalence
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\sigma
preserves
\mathbb{E}
-triangles, which means for any
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-triangle
A_{1}\xrightarrow{x_{1}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{y_{1}}C_{1}\dashrightarrow
, there exists an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}_{2}(\sigma(C_{1}),\sigma(A_{1}))
such that
\sigma(A_{1})\xrightarrow{\sigma(x_{1})}\sigma(B_{1})\xrightarrow{\sigma(y_{1})}\sigma(C_{1})\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}
is an
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-triangle.

(2)

For any
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-triangle
A_{2}\xrightarrow{x_{2}}B_{2}\xrightarrow{y_{2}}C_{2}\dashrightarrow
in
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, there exists an
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-triangle
A_{1}\xrightarrow{x_{1}}B_{1}\xrightarrow{y_{1}}C_{1}\dashrightarrow
in
\mathcal{B}_{1}
admitting an isomorphism of
\mathbb{E}_{2}
-triangles

\textstyle{A_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{B_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{y_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{C_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\sigma(A_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Remark 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
are exact categories, then any extriangle equivalence is an exact equivalence (see
[
B
, Definition 5.1]
). If
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is a triangulated category with shift functor
[1]_{\mathcal{B}_{i}}
(
i=1,2

is a “weak” triangle equivalence in the sense that we do not know if there is a natural isomorphism between

and
[1]_{\mathcal{B}_{2}}\circ\sigma

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume that
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{Y})
are hereditary cotorsion pairs. Then
F:\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{W}]\to\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{S}
becomes a triangle equivalence.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, we know that
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{W}]
is a triangulated category with shift functor
\langle 1\rangle
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.4
, we can find that
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{R}=\mathcal{S}_{L}
is a thick subcategory, and
((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V}),(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{Y}))
is a Hovey twin cotorsion pair.
By definition, if
Z\in\mathcal{Z}
, then
Z[1]=Z\langle 1\rangle
in
\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{S}
. For an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle
A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C\dashrightarrow
in
\mathcal{Z}
, the image of the induced triangle
A\xrightarrow{\overline{f}}B\xrightarrow{\overline{g}}C\xrightarrow{\overline{h}}A\langle 1\rangle
by equivalent functor
F
becomes a standard triangle
A\xrightarrow{\underline{f}}B\xrightarrow{\underline{g}}C\xrightarrow{\underline{h}}A[1]
in
\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{S}
. Moreover,
F\circ\langle 1\rangle=[1]\circ F
, this shows that
F\colon\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{W}]\to\mathcal{B}/\mathcal{S}
is a triangle equivalence.
∎

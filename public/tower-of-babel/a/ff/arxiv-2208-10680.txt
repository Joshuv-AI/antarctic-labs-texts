Projective splitting with backward, half-forward and proximal-Newton steps
M. Marques Alves

Abstract
We propose and study the weak convergence of a projective splitting algorithm [ 2 , 30 ] for solving multi-term composite monotone inclusion problems involving the finite sum of n n maximal monotone operators, each of which having an inner four-block structure: sum of maximal monotone, Lipschitz continuous, cocoercive and smooth differentiable operators. We show how to perform backward and half-forward [ 18 ] steps with respect to the maximal monotone and Lipschitz + + cocoercive components, respectively, while performing proximal-Newton steps with respect to smooth differentiable blocks. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 47H05, 49M27, 47N10. Key words: projective splitting algorithm, monotone inclusions, operator-splitting, proximal-Newton.

Introduction

Consider the multi-term composite monotone inclusion problem

\displaystyle 0\in\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,G_{i}^{*}(\underbrace{A_{i}+B_{i}+C_{i}+D_{i}}_{T_{i}})G_{i}(z),

(1)

where, for each
i=1,\dots,n
,
G_{i}
is bounded linear,
A_{i}
is (set-valued) maximal monotone,
B_{i}
is (point-to-point)
monotone and Lipschitz,
C_{i}
is (point-to-point) cocoercive and
D_{i}
is (point-to-point) monotone and continuously
differentiable (more details later in Section
3
).
Problem (
1
) appears in different fields of applied mathematics and optimization, including inverse problems and machine learning (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
37
,
50
]
).

In this paper, we present and study a new version of the
projective splitting (PS) algorithm (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
30
,
31
,
40
]
) for solving (
1
).
Our main algorithm (Algorithm
2
below) splits (
1
) into its
n
pieces, by evaluating each linear operator
G_{i}
, as well as the Lipschitz and cocoercive operators
B_{i}
and
C_{i}
, respectively, and by computing the resolvent of
A_{i}
plus a linearization of the continuously differentiable operator
D_{i}
, which we refer to as a proximal-Newton step.
Since PS algorithms are of high interest nowadays, it follows that special instances of (
1
) have been previously considered by other authors (more details later in this section), but up to our knowledge none of them considered proximal-Newton steps with respect to the smooth differentiable mappings
D_{i}
in (
1
).

The extended-solution set.
PS algorithms can be derived by noting that (under the additional assumption that
G_{n}=I
),
a vector
z
is a solution of problem (
1
) if and only if there exist
w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1}
such that the
n
-tuple

(z,w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1})
belongs to
the
extended-solution set

[
2
,
31
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}:=\left\{(z,w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1})\mid w_{i}\in T_{i}(G_{i}z),\;i=1,\dots,n,\;w_{n}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\,G_{i}^{*}w_{i}\right\}.

(2)

Since
\mathcal{S}
is a closed and convex subset of a product space (more details in Section
3
),
one can approximate points in
\mathcal{S}
by successive projections onto separating hyperplanes. The basic idea consists in giving a current point
p^{k}=(z^{k},w_{1}^{k},\dots,w_{n-1}^{k})
, find an affine functional/separator, say
\varphi_{k}(\cdot)
, such that

\varphi_{k}(p^{k})>0
and
\varphi_{k}(p)\leq 0
for all
p=(z,w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1})\in\mathcal{S}
, and then define the next iterate

p^{k+1}
as the projection of
p^{k}
(which can be explicitly computed) onto the semi-space
\{p\;|\;\varphi_{k}(p)\leq 0\}
containing
\mathcal{S}
.
For example, at a current iterate
k\geq 0
, a separator
\varphi_{k}(\cdot)
can be constructed by choosing in an appropriate way
(x_{i}^{k},y_{i}^{k})
in the graph of
T_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) and letting

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(p):={\sum_{i=1}^{n}\,}\,\langle G_{i}z-x_{i}^{k}\,|\,y_{i}^{k}-w_{i}\rangle,

(3)

where
p=(z,w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1})
and
w_{n}
is as in (
2
).

Sufficient conditions for separation.
One may ask under what conditions the affine functional
\varphi_{k}(\cdot)
as in (
3
) produces good separators, i.e., under what conditions we have

\varphi_{k}(p^{k})>0
and
\varphi_{k}(p)\leq 0
for all
p\in\mathcal{S}
, while ensuring the resulting projective algorithm has good convergence properties.
Since the definition of
\varphi_{k}(\cdot)
depends on the points
(x_{i}^{k},y_{i}^{k})
(
1\leq i\leq n
),
all we need to do is to provide rules for picking these points in an appropriate way.
Some of the strategies from the current literature on the subject are as follows:

1.

Assume that the resolvent
(T_{i}+I)^{-1}
of each
T_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) is computable and let, for
i=1,\dots,n
,

x_{i}^{k}=(\rho_{i}^{k}T_{i}+I)^{-1}(G_{i}z^{k}+\rho_{i}^{k}w_{i}^{k})\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;y_{i}^{k}=\dfrac{G_{i}z^{k}-x_{i}^{k}}{\rho_{i}^{k}}+w_{i}^{k},

where
\rho_{i}^{k}>0
is a proximal parameter. This appears, for instance,
in the works
[
2
,
30
,
31
]
.

2.

Set, for all
i=1,\dots,n
,
B_{i}
and
D_{i}
as zero in (
1
), and then compute single-forward steps for the cocoercive operators
C_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
) and backward steps for the maximal monotone operators
A_{i}
, i.e.,

x_{i}^{k}=(\rho_{i}^{k}A_{i}+I)^{-1}\left(G_{i}z^{k}+\rho_{i}^{k}w_{i}^{k}-\rho_{i}^{k}C_{i}(G_{i}z^{k})\right)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;y_{i}^{k}=\dfrac{G_{i}z^{k}-x_{i}^{k}}{\rho_{i}^{k}}+w_{i}^{k}.

This appears in the works
[
20
,
39
]
.

3.

Set, for all
i=1,\dots,n
,
C_{i}
and
D_{i}
as zero in (
1
), and then compute forward steps for the Lipschitz operators
B_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
), in the spirit of the seminal work of P. Tseng
[
59
]
, and backward steps for
A_{i}
, i.e.,

x_{i}^{k}=(\rho_{i}^{k}A_{i}+I)^{-1}\left(G_{i}z^{k}+\rho_{i}^{k}w_{i}^{k}-\rho_{i}^{k}B_{i}(G_{i}z^{k})\right)\;\mbox{and}\;\;y_{i}^{k}=\dfrac{G_{i}z^{k}-x_{i}^{k}}{\rho_{i}^{k}}+w_{i}^{k}+\left[B_{i}(x_{i}^{k})-B_{i}(G_{i}z^{k})\right].

Note that, when compared to
y_{i}^{k}
as in item 2 above,
y_{i}^{k}
as in the latter displayed equation requires two more evaluations of the Lipschitz operator
B_{i}
.
This approach appeared, e.g., in the works
[
20
,
40
]
.

General notation.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a real Hilbert space.
The weak convergence of a sequence
(z^{k})
in
{\mathcal{H}}

will be denoted by

.
A set-valued map

is said to be a

if

\langle z-z^{\prime}\,|\,v-v^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0
for all
v\in T(z)
and
v^{\prime}\in T(z^{\prime})
. On the other hand,
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is

maximal monotone
if
T
is monotone and its
graph

\mbox{Gr}(T):=\{(z,v)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\,|\,v\in T(z)\}
is not properly contained in the graph of any other
monotone operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
The
domain
of
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is
\mbox{Dom}(T):=\{z\in{\mathcal{H}}\;|\;T(z)\neq\emptyset\}
.
The
inverse
of
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is
T^{-1}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
, defined at any

if and only if
z\in T(v)
.
The

of a maximal monotone operator

; note that

.
The operator

, where

(\gamma T)z:=\gamma T(z):=\{\gamma v\,|\,v\in T(z)\}
.
The sum of
T:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
and
S:{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}

T(z)+S(z):=\{u+v\;|\;u\in T(z)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;v\in S(z)\}
.

The

of a bounded linear operator

. A (point-to-point) operator

\displaystyle\|{B_{i}(x)-B_{i}(y)}\|\leq\ell_{i}\|{x-y}\|\qquad\forall x,y\in{\mathcal{H}}_{i}.

(10)

(A4)

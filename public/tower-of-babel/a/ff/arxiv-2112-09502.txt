Inversion of adjunction for quotient singularities II: Non-linear actions
Yusuke Nakamura, Kohsuke Shibata

Abstract
. We prove the precise inversion of adjunction formula for quotient singularities. As an application, we prove the semi-continuity of minimal log discrepancies for hyperquotient singularities. This paper is a continuation of [ 26 ] , and we generalize the previous results to non-linear group actions.

Introduction

The minimal log discrepancy is an invariant of singularities defined in birational geometry.
The importance of this invariant is that two conjectures on the invariant, the LSC (lower semi-continuity) conjecture and
the ACC (ascending chain condition) conjecture, imply the conjecture of termination of flips
[
29
]
.
This paper is a continuation of
[
26
]
, and we focus on the LSC conjecture.
We always work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.

The LSC conjecture is proposed by Ambro
[
1
]
and the conjecture predicts that the minimal log discrepancies satisfy the lower semi-continuity.

Conjecture 1.1

(LSC conjecture,
[

Let

|X|\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{-\infty\};\quad x\mapsto\operatorname{mld}_{x}(X,\mathfrak{a})

Due to Theorem

If

has a quotient singularity at a closed point

is isomorphic to
k[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]]^{G}

Therefore, Theorem

can be proved by extending the proofs in
[
26
]
to the case of the formal power series ring.
In what follows, we shall explain the main differences from the proofs in
[
26
]
and
the difficulties that arise when dealing with formal power series rings.

The key ingredient of the proofs in
[
26
]
is the theory of arc spaces of quotient singularities
established by Denef and Loeser in
[
6
]
.
Suppose that a finite group
G\subset\operatorname{GL}_{N}(k)
of order
d
acts on
\overline{A}=\operatorname{Spec}k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]
.
Let
\overline{X}\subset\overline{A}
be a
G
-invariant closed subvariety and
let
X:=\overline{X}/G
be its quotient.
Let
I\subset k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]^{G}
be the defining ideal of
X\subset\overline{A}/G
.
For each
\gamma\in G
,
\gamma
can be diagonalized with some new basis
x^{(\gamma)}_{1},\ldots,x^{(\gamma)}_{N}
.
Let
\operatorname{diag}(\xi^{e_{1}},\ldots,\xi^{e_{N}})
be the diagonal matrix with
0\leq e_{i}\leq d-1
,
where
\xi
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity in
k
.
Then we define the ring homomorphism
\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{*}
by

\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{*}:k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]^{G}\to k[t][x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}];\quad x^{(\gamma)}_{i}\mapsto t^{\frac{e_{i}}{d}}x^{(\gamma)}_{i},

and define a
k[t]
-scheme
\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}
by

\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[t][x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]/\overline{I}^{(\gamma)}\bigr),

where
\overline{I}^{(\gamma)}
is the ideal generated by the elements of
\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{*}(I)
.
Then the theory of Denef and Loeser in
[
6
]

allows us to compare the spaces
X_{\infty}
and
\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in G}\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
.
In
[
26
]
, using this theory,
X_{\infty}
is studied through each
\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
.

In this paper, we deal with the case of formal power series rings,
i.e. when
\overline{A}=\operatorname{Spec}k[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]]
.
Let
G
,
\gamma
,
\overline{X}
, and
X
be as above.
In this case, we take
e_{i}
’s above to satisfy
1\leq e_{i}\leq d
.
Then we can define the following two natural ring homomorphisms

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{*}

\displaystyle:k[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]]^{G}\to k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}][[t]];

\displaystyle x^{(\gamma)}_{i}\mapsto t^{\frac{e_{i}}{d}}x^{(\gamma)}_{i},

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{\prime*}

\displaystyle:k[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]]^{G}\to k[t][[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]];

\displaystyle x^{(\gamma)}_{i}\mapsto t^{\frac{e_{i}}{d}}x^{(\gamma)}_{i},

and we define
k[t]
-schemes

\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}][[t]]/\overline{I}^{(\gamma)}\bigr),\quad\overline{X}^{\prime(\gamma)}=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[t][[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]]/\overline{I}^{\prime(\gamma)}\bigr),

where
\overline{I}^{\prime(\gamma)}
is the ideal generated by
\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{\prime*}(I)
.
In this paper, we will use both arc spaces

\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
and
\overline{X}^{\prime(\gamma)}_{\infty}
to study
X_{\infty}
.
We shall also explain below how to use
\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
and
\overline{X}^{\prime(\gamma)}_{\infty}
differently.

First, the theory of Denef and Loeser in
[
6
]
can be generalized to the formal power series rings,
and
X_{\infty}
can be compared with
\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
.
Indeed, we shall see in Proposition
6.3
that

\overline{\lambda}_{\gamma}^{*}
gives a map
\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in G}\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}\to X_{\infty}

that is surjective outside a thin set.
We note that it is not enough to consider
\overline{X}^{\prime(\gamma)}_{\infty}
in this respect (see Remark
6.4
for the detail).

On the other hand,
\overline{X}^{\prime(\gamma)}_{\infty}
will be used with the following motivation.
In
[
26
]
, a
k
-arc
\beta\in\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}_{\infty}
is called the “trivial arc” when it corresponds
to the
k[t]
-ring homomorphism
\beta^{*}:k[t][x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]\to k[[t]]

\beta^{*}(x_{i})=0
for each

.
Another key point of the argument in
[

]
is to show the fact that

The existence of such a lift is proved by combining the result by Graber, Harris, and Starr
[
12
]
and

[
14
]
.
In our formal power series ring setting,

because each closed fiber

\overline{X}^{(\gamma)}\to\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
over

is empty.
Whereas, the same argument works on

and proves that
the trivial arc has a lift on a desingularization

of
\overline{X}^{\prime(\gamma)}
(see Claim
8.4
).
It should be noted that it is not clear whether the results
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
can be applied to the formal power series ring setting.
However, this difficulty can be avoided by using the functorial desingularization due to Temkin
[
30
]

Definition 2.1

(cf.

)

(1)

Let

D(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}D(x_{i})

.

(2)

For an

-derivation

is called a

-derivation
if

is a special
R_{0}
-derivation.
We denote by
\operatorname{Der}^{\prime}_{R_{0}}(A,M)
the set of all special
R_{0}
-derivations.
Then
\operatorname{Der}^{\prime}_{R_{0}}(A,M)
is an
A
-submodule of
\operatorname{Der}_{R_{0}}(A,M)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M
be an
A
-module that is separated in the
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
-adic topology,
i.e.
M
satisfies
\bigcap_{\ell\geq 1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})^{\ell}M=0
.
Then

\operatorname{Der}^{\prime}_{R_{0}}(A,M)=\operatorname{Der}_{R_{0}}(A,M)

holds.
In particular, it holds in the following two cases.

(1)

When
M
is a finite
R
-module.

(2)

When
M=A
and
A
is an integral domain such that
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})A\not=A
.

Proof.

By the definition of special derivations, it is sufficient to show that

\operatorname{Der}^{\prime}_{R_{0}}(R,M)=\operatorname{Der}_{R_{0}}(R,M)
.
Let
D\in\operatorname{Der}_{R_{0}}(R,M)
. If we set
D^{\prime}:R\to M
by

D^{\prime}(f):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}D(x_{i})
, then
D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Der}^{\prime}_{R_{0}}(R,M)
holds.
Hence it is sufficient to show that
D=0
if
D(x_{i})=0
holds for all
i
.
Let
f\in R
.
For any
\ell\geq 0
, we may write
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f_{1}\in R_{0}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

and
f_{2}\in(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{\ell+1}R
.
Then we have

D(f)=D(f_{1})+D(f_{2})=D(f_{2})\in(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{\ell}M.

It shows that
D(f)\in\bigcap_{\ell\geq 1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})^{\ell}M=0
.

In both cases (1) and (2),
M
is separated by
[
23
, Theorem 8.9]
.
∎

Remark 2.5

(1)

(2)

The universal finite module of differentials is also a module defined with the same motivation (see

For an

•

For any

-derivation

In other words,

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{A/R_{0}},M)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\operatorname{Der}_{R_{0}}(A,M);\quad\alpha\mapsto\alpha\circ\widetilde{d}_{A/R_{0}}

for any finite
A
-module
M
.

In contrast to the module

(6)

Suppse that

.

Proof.

By Proposition

5.1.3.

We fix a non-negative integer

throughout this subsection.

Definition 5.14
.

Let

be a scheme of finite type over

.
Suppose that each irreducible component

of

The term “very thin” is used only in this paper.
In Question

Question 5.15

Let

(1)

Let

.

Remark 5.16
.

(1)

Note that Question

(1) is true for the arc spaces of

If

(2)

Due to the proof of

(3)

Definition 8.1

Since

\overline{M}
with a smooth closed point

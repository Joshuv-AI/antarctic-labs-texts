Delineating chiral separation effect in two-color dense QCD
Daiki Suenaga, Toru Kojo

Abstract
We study the chiral separation effect (CSE) in two-color and two-flavor QCD (QC 2 D) to delineate quasiparticle pictures in dense matter from low to high temperatures. Both massless and massive quarks are discussed. We particularly focus on the high density domain where diquarks form a color singlet condensate with the electric charge 1 / 3 1/3 . The condensate breaks baryon number and U ​ ( 1 ) A U(1)_{A} axial symmetry, and induces the electromagnetic Meissner effects. Within a quark quasiparticle picture, we compute the chiral separation conductivity at one-loop. We have checked that Nambu-Goldstone modes, which should appear in the improved vertices as required by the Ward-Takahashi identities, do not contribute to the chiral separation conductivity due to their longitudinal natures. In the static limit, the destructive interferences in the particle-hole channel, as in usual Meissner effects, suppress the conductivity (in chiral limit, to 1 / 3 1/3 of the normal phase’s). This locally breaks the universality of the CSE coefficients, provided quasiparticle pictures are valid in the bulk matter.

\epsilon_{\rm p}(p_{F})=\Delta

\tilde{f}\sim{\rm e}^{-\Delta/T}\ll 1
.
We expand

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\tilde{f}\big(\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}})\big)-\tilde{f}\big(\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})\big)\simeq-\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=\epsilon_{\rm p}}\frac{\,\partial\epsilon_{\rm p}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\,,

(60)

and get

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\cal G}_{2}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}\sim\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=\epsilon_{\rm p}}\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\partial\epsilon_{\rm p}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\bar{\omega}_{n}^{2}+\big(\frac{\,\partial\epsilon_{\rm p}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\big)^{2}\,}~\sim~\frac{1}{T}{\rm e}^{-\Delta/T}\,.

(61)

For
T\rightarrow 0
,
\partial\tilde{f}/\partial x\rightarrow 0
at
x>0
, and
{\cal G}_{2}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}
is vanishing for
\Delta\neq 0
as
{\rm e}^{-\Delta/T}/T\rightarrow 0
.

Finally we look at the kinematic factor couples to
q^{k}
,

\displaystyle q^{k}{\cal K}^{k}_{{\rm p}{\rm p}}({\bm{q}};{\bm{p}})=-\frac{{\bm{q}}\cdot{\bm{p}}}{E_{\bm{p}}}+\frac{{\bm{q}}\cdot({\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}})}{E_{{\bm{p}}+{\bm{q}}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}-\frac{\,({\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{q}})^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}^{3}\,}~\sim~\frac{\,{\bm{q}}^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{3}\frac{\,|{\bm{p}}|^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}\bigg)\,,

(62)

where in the last expression we averaged
({\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{q}})^{2}\rightarrow{\bm{p}}^{2}{\bm{q}}^{2}/3
in the integral over
{\bm{p}}
. Then the CSE conductivity from the particle-hole can be written as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}_{\rm CSE}(i\bar{\omega}_{n},{\bm{q}})~\sim~N_{c}\int_{\bm{p}}{\cal G}_{2}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}\frac{\,1\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{3}\frac{\,|{\bm{p}}|^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}\bigg)\,.

(63)

for small
{\bm{q}}
. Note that the thermal factor makes the expression Eq.(
63
) UV finite.

At zero temperature the particle-hole contributions vanish by the presence of the diquark gap as
{\cal G}_{2}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}\sim{\rm e}^{-\Delta/T}/T\rightarrow 0
. This suppression of particle-hole is what happens for the Meissner effect in a superconductor.
In contrast, in a normal conductor the particle-hole induces the paramagnetic effects which cancel with the diamagnetic contributions from particle-antiparticle contributions.
The absence of particle-hole contributions or paramagnetic effects, as found in our calculations for the diquark condensed phase, makes the material diamagnetic. The particle-hole pairs contribute only as thermal excitations.

III.1.2
|{\bf q}|\gg\Delta
or normal phase

For
|{\bm{q}}|\gg\Delta
, the above expression is qualitatively modified. Here it is important to note that
(E_{\bm{p}}-\mu)(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}-\mu)<0
for the particle-hole contributions; one of the excitations has
E>\mu
and the other
E<\mu
. Then, neglecting
\Delta
, one finds

\displaystyle\frac{\,(E_{\bm{p}}-\mu)(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}-\mu)+|\Delta|^{2}\,}{\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}})\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})}~\simeq~-1\,,

(64)

so that

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{1}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}({\bm{q}};{\bm{p}})~\simeq~1\,,~~~~~{\cal C}_{2}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}({\bm{q}};{\bm{p}})~\simeq~0\,.

(65)

With this
{\cal F}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}\sim{\cal G}_{1}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}
for
|{\bm{q}}|\gg\Delta
. This function is evaluated by focusing on the domain
|{\bm{p}}|\simeq|{\bm{p}}^{\prime}|\simeq p_{F}
. For
E_{\bm{p}}-\mu\gg\Delta
and
\mu-E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}\gg\Delta
, we use

\displaystyle 1-\tilde{f}\big(\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}})\big)-\tilde{f}\big(\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle~\sim~

\displaystyle\tilde{f}\big(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}-\mu\big)-\tilde{f}\big(E_{{\bm{p}}}-\mu\big)

(66)

\displaystyle~\sim~

\displaystyle\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=E_{\bm{p}}-\mu}\frac{\,\partial E_{\bm{p}}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\epsilon_{\xi}({\bm{p}})+\epsilon_{\xi^{\prime}}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})~\sim~E_{\bm{p}}-E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}~\simeq~-\frac{\,\partial E_{\bm{p}}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\,,

to get

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\cal G}_{1}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}\,\sim\,-\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=E_{\bm{p}}-\mu}\times\frac{\,\big(\frac{\,\partial E_{\rm p}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\big)^{2}\,}{\,\bar{\omega}_{n}^{2}+\big(\frac{\,\partial E_{\rm p}\,}{\,\partial{\bm{p}}\,}\cdot{\bm{q}}\big)^{2}\,}\,.

(67)

For
T\rightarrow 0
, the contribution entirely comes from
|{\bm{p}}|=p_{F}
where
\partial\tilde{f}/\partial x
yields
-\delta(|{\bm{p}}|-p_{F})
, as in the usual Debye screening calculations.
Including the kinematic factor as before, we find

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}_{\rm CSE}(i\bar{\omega}_{n},{\bm{q}})~\sim~N_{c}\int_{\bm{p}}{\cal G}_{1}^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}\frac{\,1\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{3}\frac{\,|{\bm{p}}|^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}\bigg)\,,

(68)

for
|{\bm{q}}|\gg\Delta
. The expression Eq.(
68
) is UV finite and the integrand is dominated by the contributions from
|{\bm{p}}|\simeq p_{F}
.

As mentioned before, the result for
|{\bm{q}}|\gg\Delta
can be taken as a result for the normal phase with
\Delta=0
. In the normal phase the particle-hole contributions are large at
T=0
as they are gapless, in contrast to fermions in the diquark condensed phase.
In the massless limit, we find, for the static limit,

\displaystyle\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm p}({\rm normal})}_{\rm CSE;\,{\it m}=0}\big|^{\omega=0}_{{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0}~\rightarrow~\frac{\,N_{c}\mu\,}{\,3\pi^{2}\,}\,.

(69)

and for the dynamic limit

\displaystyle\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm p}({\rm normal})}_{{\rm CSE};\,{\it m}=0}\big|^{\omega\rightarrow 0}_{{\bm{q}}=0}~\rightarrow~0\,.

(70)

III.2
Particle-antiparticle

Next we discuss the particle-antiparticle contributions. These contributions are not regulated by thermal factors and are potentially UV divergent. Our main concern is the UV finiteness and for this purpose it is sufficient to study
T\sim 0
.

The coherence factors in the particle-antiparticle contributions are not dominated by the contributions from
|{\bm{p}}|\sim|{\bm{p}}^{\prime}|\simeq p_{F}
, but come from large phase space with momenta much larger than
\Delta
. For this reason we set
\Delta\rightarrow 0
and focus on the leading order contributions. Then for
E_{\bm{p}}-\mu>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\,(E_{\bm{p}}-\mu)(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}+\mu)-|\Delta|^{2}\,}{\epsilon_{\rm p}({\bm{p}})\epsilon_{\rm a}({\bm{p}}^{\prime})}~\rightarrow~1\,,

(71)

Accordingly,

\displaystyle{\cal C}_{1}^{{\rm p}{\rm a}}({\bm{q}};{\bm{p}})~\rightarrow~1\,,~~~~~{\cal C}_{2}^{{\rm p}{\rm a}}({\bm{q}};{\bm{p}})~\rightarrow~0\,,

(72)

and we find

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\cal F}^{{\rm p}{\rm a}}\rightarrow\,{\cal G}^{{\rm p}{\rm a}}_{1}=\big[\,1-\tilde{f}\big(E_{\bm{p}}-\mu\big)-\tilde{f}\big(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}+\mu\big)\,\big]G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}},

(73)

where

\displaystyle G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}=\frac{\,E_{\bm{p}}+E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}\,}{\,\bar{\omega}_{n}^{2}+\big(E_{\bm{p}}+E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}\big)^{2}\,}\,.

(74)

Likewise

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!{\cal F}^{{\rm a}{\rm p}}\rightarrow{\cal G}^{{\rm a}{\rm p}}_{1}=\big[\,1-\tilde{f}\big(E_{\bm{p}}+\mu\big)-\tilde{f}\big(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}-\mu\big)\,\big]G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}.

(75)

Meanwhile the kinematic factor is

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{k}_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}=-{\cal K}^{k}_{{\rm a}{\rm p}}=-\bigg(\frac{p^{k}}{E_{\bm{p}}}+\frac{(p^{\prime})^{k}}{E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}}\bigg)\sim-2\frac{\,p^{k}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\,,

(76)

for small
{\bm{q}}
.
While each of (pa) and (ap) contributions is UV divergent, the sum cancels the UV divergence.

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{{\rm p}{\rm a}}{\cal K}^{k}_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}+{\cal F}^{{\rm a}{\rm p}}{\cal K}^{k}_{{\rm a}{\rm p}}\,\sim\,{\cal K}^{k}_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}\big({\cal F}^{{\rm p}{\rm a}}-{\cal F}^{{\rm a}{\rm p}}\big)

\displaystyle\,\sim\,-2\frac{\,p^{k}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\,G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}\big[\,\tilde{f}\big(E_{{\bm{p}}^{\prime}}-\mu\big)-\tilde{f}\big(E_{\bm{p}}-\mu\big)\,\big]-(\mu\leftrightarrow-\mu)

\displaystyle\sim-2\frac{\,p^{k}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\frac{\,({\bm{p}}\cdot{\bm{q}})\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}\,}\,G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}\sum_{\xi={\rm p},{\rm a}}\eta_{\xi}\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=E_{\bm{p}}-\eta_{\xi}\mu}

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{\,2q^{k}\,}{3}\frac{\,{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}\,G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}\sum_{\xi={\rm p},{\rm a}}\eta_{\xi}\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=E_{\bm{p}}-\eta_{\xi}\mu}\,,

(77)

where we took the angular average for
{\bm{p}}
. The CS conductivity is

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm a}+{\rm a}{\rm p}}_{\rm CSE}(i\bar{\omega}_{n},{\bm{q}})

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sim\,-N_{c}\int_{\bm{p}}\frac{\,2\,}{3}\frac{\,{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}{\,E_{\bm{p}}^{2}\,}\,G_{{\rm p}{\rm a}}\sum_{\xi={\rm p},{\rm a}}\eta_{\xi}\frac{\,\partial\tilde{f}(x)\,}{\,\partial x\,}\bigg|_{x=E_{\bm{p}}-\eta_{\xi}\mu}\,.

(78)

Because of
\partial\tilde{f}/\partial x
, the integrand is dominated by states with
E_{\bm{p}}\pm\mu
.
In the massless limit, we find, for the static limit,

\displaystyle\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm p}({\rm normal})}_{{\rm CSE};\,{\it m}=0}\big|^{\omega=0}_{{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0}~\rightarrow~\frac{\,N_{c}\mu\,}{\,6\pi^{2}\,}\,.

(79)

and for the dynamic limit

\displaystyle\sigma^{{\rm p}{\rm p}({\rm normal})}_{{\rm CSE};\,{\it m}=0}\big|^{\omega\rightarrow 0}_{{\bm{q}}=0}~\rightarrow~\frac{\,N_{c}\mu\,}{\,6\pi^{2}\,}\,.

(80)

That is, the static and dynamic limits are the same, as the propagators do not have the sensitivity to the limiting order.

III.3
Antiparticle-antihole

The computations of antiparticle-antihole proceed in the very similar way as the particle-hole case. The contributions are suppressed by thermal factor as
\sim{\rm e}^{-(\mu+\Delta)/T}
.

Numerical results

In this section, we present the numerical results of the CS conductivity for plural density, quark mass, and temperature. We discuss a normalized CS conductivity,

\displaystyle R^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}\equiv\sigma^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}_{\rm CSE}/\sigma_{0}\,,~~~~~R=\sum_{\xi,\xi^{\prime}={\rm p},{\rm a}}R^{\xi\xi^{\prime}}\,,

(81)

with a
\mu

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=\frac{\,N_{c}\mu\,}{\,2\pi^{2}\,}\,,

(82)

To understand the results in this section, it is useful to use the massless and zero temperature limit in the normal phase as a baseline. The static conductivity is

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\big(R^{{\rm p}{\rm p}}+R^{{\rm p}{\rm a}+{\rm a}{\rm p}}+R^{{\rm a}{\rm a}}\big)^{\omega=0}_{{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0}\,\rightarrow\,\frac{2}{\,3\,}+\frac{1}{\,3\,}+0=1\,,

V
Discussions

Our calculations of the CS conductivity rely on the validity of quasiparticle pictures in the diquark condensed phase. While our results on the normal phase lead to the static CS conductivity at
{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0
limit as expected from the anomaly arguments, our calculations for the diquark condensed phase lead to only the one-third; the coefficient of the CSE is not universal even in chiral limit.

This conclusion puzzled us. The triangle graph for the A-V-V type (A: axial-vector, V: vector) arises when we expand the A-V correlator by the term
\mu\gamma_{0}
in the quark propagators as
S\simeq S_{0}+S_{0}(-\mu\gamma_{0})S_{0}+\cdots
where
S_{0}
is the propagator at
\mu=0
. However, it turned out that such expansion does not yield the same result as the usual triangle calculations.
In fact, the static CS conductivity at vanishing temperature in the diquark condensed phase can be expanded with respect to
\mu
as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm CSE}\big|^{\omega=0}_{{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0}=\frac{N_{c}}{6\pi^{2}}\frac{|\Delta|^{2}}{m^{2}+|\Delta|^{2}}\mu+{\cal O}(\mu^{2})\ \ \ {\rm at}\ T=0\ .

(89)

Here we have checked that the higher-order contributions
{\cal O}(\mu^{2})
are proportional to
|\Delta|^{2}m^{2}
. In this way, the conductivity is shown to be given by
S_{0}(-\mu\gamma_{0})S_{0}
part solely in chiral limit (
m\to 0
), resulting in

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm CSE}\big|^{\omega=0}_{{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0}\to\frac{C_{\rm uiv.}}{3}\mu\ ,

(90)

with
C_{\rm univ.}=N_{c}/(2\pi^{2})
the universal value. It should be noted that, in Eq. (
89
), the dimensionless expansion parameter is found to be
\mu/\sqrt{|\Delta|^{2}+m^{2}}
, hence
m\to 0
limit to obtain (
90
) has been safely taken due to a regulator played by
\Delta
. On the other hand, the other limit
\Delta\to 0
in Eq. (
89
) leads to an incorrect answer:
\sigma_{\rm CSE}\big|^{\omega=0}_{{\bm{q}}\rightarrow 0}\to 0
, showing the diquark gap
\Delta
changes the analytic properties of the conductivity in the infrared.

The above violation of the universality does not seems to be unnatural to us. The
\mu\gamma_{0}
vertex in
S_{0}(-\mu\gamma_{0})S_{0}
introduces no momenta; the only available momentum, after integrating out the loop momenta, is the external momentum
q
. Thus the possible form from the expansion of the A-V correlator in obtaining Eq. (
90
) should be

\displaystyle\langle j^{i}_{5}(q)j_{em}^{j}(-q)j_{B}^{0}(0)\rangle^{\mu=0}_{A=0}~\mu A_{j}\sim\epsilon^{0ijk}\mu q^{j}A^{k}\,.

(91)

Contracting with the external momentum
q^{i}
, the spatial divergence of axial current vanishes, showing that our results do not affect
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}_{5}
and hence the anomaly relation.
From this viewpoint it is not clear to us why the CS coefficient should be constrained by the anomaly.
The possible exceptions for this discussion are the cases when
A^{k}
is singular
Son and Zhitnitsky 2004
;
Son and Stephanov 2008
, as in the Dirac string on which
(\partial_{i}\partial_{j}-\partial_{j}\partial_{i}){\bm{A}}\neq 0
, but this sort of external fields are presumably not compatible with the linear response regime utilized in this paper.
The radiative corrections to chiral transport coefficients have been discussed in Refs.
Jensen 2012
;
Gorbar et al. 2013
;
Feng et al. 2019
.

On the other hand, our one-loop calculations shown in the previous sectors have not taken into account the vertex correction, and hence would miss some qualitatively important effects. In particular, we must treat the quasiparticle propagators and the vertices consistently in order to keep the conservation laws. Our quasiparticle propagators include diquark mean fields which break the
U(1)_{A}
axial, baryon number, and electric charge conservations. The improved vertices produce the poles of NG modes that recover the conservation laws.

Below we give a brief discussion on the structure of the improved vertices and will conclude that they do not influence the conclusions in the simplest quasiparticle computations.

The vertex corrections appear both in the axial-vector current and the vector current coupled to background electromagnetic fields.
The WTI for the axial-vector vertex leads to

VI
Conclusions

In this paper we have delineated the CS conductivity within the quasiparticle picture.
The CS conductivity has been calculated in lattice simulations but the result was for the domain other than the diquark condensed phase
Buividovich et al. 2020
. We hope our considerations in this paper to be tested in near future.

The results depend on the particle-hole contributions which are sensitive to the phase structure of QC
2
D. The particle-hole contributions are suppressed in the presence of diquark condensates. As a consequence, the static CS conductivity at low momentum limit leads to only the one-third of the normal phase. We note that this particle-hole suppression is also the origin of the electromagnetic Meissner mass, relating the CS conductivity to the Meissner effects. As temperature increases, the particles-holes come back to enhance the CS conductivity to the size of the normal phase.

In more general context, the nature of thermal corrections carry important information on the properties of matter other than the diquark condensates. Thermal excitations on top of the diquark condensed Fermi surface may be hadronic. This sort of picture has been suggested in the quarkyonic matter conjecture where the quark matter has the baryonic Fermi surface

Kojo et al. 2012

In order to derive definite conclusions, however, we need more lattice data points at low temperature and should also examine the systematics in our calculations. The CS conductivity may provide us information complementary with those from gluon propagators.

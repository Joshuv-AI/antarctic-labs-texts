Layer stripping approach to reconstruct shape defects in waveguides using locally resonant frequencies
Angèle Niclas, Laurent Seppecher

Abstract
This article present a new method to reconstruct slowly varying width defects in 2D waveguides using one-side section measurements at locally resonant frequencies. At these frequencies, locally resonant modes propagate in the waveguide up to a “cut-off” position. In this particular point, the local width of the waveguide can be recovered. Given multi-frequency measurements taken on a section of the waveguide, we perform an efficient layer stripping approach to recover shape variations slice by slice. It provides an L ∞ \displaystyle\text{L}^{\infty} -stable method to reconstruct the width of a slowly monotonous varying waveguide. We validate this method on numerical data and discuss its limits.

Introduction

In previous work
[
20
]
, we have presented the analysis of recovering a slowly varying shape of waveguides from surface measurement using locally resonant frequencies. This method was suited for waveguides such as elastic plates where surface measurements are available but is not appropriate for acoustic waveguides such as pipes or air ducts where data are often measured on one section of the waveguide
[
18
,
12
]
.

In the present work, we tackle the shape recovery problem from one-side section measurements only. At locally resonant frequencies, the usual Born approximation (see
[
13
,
11
]
) is not valid and recovering the shape variation of the waveguide can be seen as a nonlinear inverse scattering problem.

General description of the problem

We describe a 2D varying acoustic waveguide by

\Omega:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ |\ 0<y<h(x)\right\},

1.2
Scientific context

The detection and reconstruction of shape defects in a waveguide are mentioned in different works. In articles
[
19
,
3
,
2
]
, the authors use a conformal mapping to map the geometry of the perturbed waveguide to the geometry of a regular waveguide, and suggest to inverse the mapping to recover the width defects. Different linear inversion methods based on the scattered field analysis are also developed in
[
21
,
12
,
14
]
. All these works perform the reconstruction using a single propagation frequency to perform detections and reconstructions of defects.

Our work concerns a different approach, also used in
[
7
,
6
]
, where we assume that the data is available for a whole interval of frequencies. This usually provides additional information that should help localize and reconstruct the defect. Moreover, the use of multi-frequency data often provides uniqueness of the reconstruction (see
[
5
]
) and better stability (see
[
8
,
16
,
23
]
).

The article
[
11
]
presents a linear method to recover small width variations using back scattering data. However, this work avoids all the locally resonant frequencies of the waveguide. In
[
20
]
, we showed that these locally resonant frequencies could be used to recover slowly varying width defects given surface measurements. Given the high sensitivity of the reconstructions obtained using this method, we choose to use the same approach and work only with locally resonant frequencies in this article.

If
\displaystyle k
is chosen such that
\displaystyle k=N\pi/h(x^{\star}_{k})
for some
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle k
is called a locally resonant frequency,
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}
is called a resonant position and the Helmholtz problem is not well posed in general (see
[

). Nevertheless, it is proved in
[

that a unique solution exits as long as the waveguide is slowly varying. The same work provides a suitable approximation of the wavefield that explicitly depends on
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}
. This approximation is used in
[
20
]
with surface measurements to recover the position of
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}
, and then to reconstruct the shape function
\displaystyle h
. We now aim to use this analysis to solve the inverse problem from section measurements:

\text{Find }\quad h\quad\text{ from }\quad u_{k}(x^{\text{meas}},y)\quad\forall y\in(0,h(x^{\text{meas}})),\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\text{ where }k\text{ is locally resonant}.

(3)

Our reconstruction method is inspired by the ideas presented in
[
10
]
and use wavefield measurements of
\displaystyle u_{k}
to recover the position
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}
. Since
\displaystyle h(x^{\star}_{k})=N\pi/k

\displaystyle k
and finding corresponding resonant location
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}

\displaystyle h

However, the realization strongly differs. Indeed, section measurements contain much less information than surface measurements, and we need to recover the localization of
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}
from the value of the resonant mode in one point instead of a whole interval. To circumvent this difficulty, we implement in this paper a layer stripping approach inspired by
[
25

24
]
.

1.3
Main steps of the reconstruction method

The main steps of the proposed reconstruction method are summarized here:

1.

From section measurements
\displaystyle u_{k}(x^{\text{meas}},y)
, we select frequencies
\displaystyle k
that makes a chosen mode
\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
resonant and we filter the data (orthogonal projection) to focus only on the
\displaystyle N
-th mode.

2.

We prove that this data is approached by the function
\displaystyle\Phi\circ\zeta(k)
where

\Phi(x):=\sin(x+\pi/4)\exp(ix+i\pi/4),\qquad\zeta(k):=\int_{x^{\star}_{k}}^{x^{\text{meas}}}\sqrt{k^{2}-\frac{N^{2}\pi^{2}}{h(x)^{2}}}\mathrm{d}x.

(4)

In this function, the dependency on the resonant point
\displaystyle x_{k}^{\star}
and on the shape
\displaystyle h
is explicit. This point is detailed in Section 3.1 and Corollary
1
.

3.

There is a left inverse function
\displaystyle\Phi^{-1}
modulus
\displaystyle\pi
. However, to have access to
\displaystyle\zeta(k)
, we need to eliminate the modulus. Using the fact that
\displaystyle k\mapsto\zeta(k)
is increasing, we prove in Section 3.2 that we can recover an approximation of
\displaystyle\zeta(k)
on a discrete set of frequencies
\displaystyle k_{1},\dots,k_{I}
if the discretization step is small enough.

4.

From
\displaystyle\zeta(k_{1}),\dots,\zeta(k_{I})
, we develop a layer stripping approach to recover each corresponding resonant
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k_{i}}
using the fact that
\displaystyle h(x^{\star}_{k_{i}})=N\pi/k_{i}
(see a scheme in Figure
2
) and we perform a triangular linear recovery of each
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k_{i}}
as explained in Proposition
3
.

5.

Finally, using the recovery of each
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k_{i}}
and the fact that
\displaystyle h(x^{\star}_{k_{i}})=N\pi/k_{i}
, we provide an approximation of
\displaystyle h
whose error is quantified in Theorem
2
.

2
Forward problem and locally resonant modal data

Before studying the inverse problem associated with the reconstruction of the width in a varying waveguide, we recall some needed tools to study the forward problem. These results where already presented in
[
20
]
and their proofs can be found in
[
11
,
12
]
. However, Theorem
1
is slightly different from the one stated in
[
20
]
due to the change of measurements area.

A useful tool when working in waveguides is the modal decomposition. The following definition provides a modal decomposition in varying waveguides:

Definition 1
.

We define the sequence of functions
\displaystyle(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by

\forall(x,y)\in\Omega,\quad\varphi_{n}(x,y):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1/\sqrt{h(x)}&\text{if }n=0,\\
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{h(x)}}\cos\left(\frac{n\pi y}{h(x)}\right)&\text{if }n\geq 1,\end{array}\right.

(5)

which for any fixed
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
defines an orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle\text{L}^{2}(0,h(x))
.

Hence, a solution
\displaystyle u_{k}\in\text{H}^{2}_{\text{loc}}\big(\Omega\big)
of (
2
) admits a unique modal decomposition

u_{k}(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}u_{k,N}(x)\varphi_{n}(x,y)\quad\text{where}\quad u_{k,N}(x):=\int_{0}^{h(x)}u_{k}(x,y)\varphi_{n}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y.

(6)

Note that
\displaystyle u_{k,N}
does not satisfy in general any nice equation. However, when
\displaystyle h
is constant (outside of
\displaystyle\supp(h^{\prime})
), it satisfies an equation of the form
\displaystyle u_{k,N}^{\prime\prime}+k_{n}^{2}u_{k,N}=-g_{n}
where
\displaystyle k_{n}^{2}=k^{2}-n^{2}\pi^{2}/h^{2}
is the wavenumber. When
\displaystyle h
is variable, the decomposition (
6
) motivates the following definition:

Definition 2
.

The local wavenumber function of the mode
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
is the complex function
\displaystyle k_{n}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

k_{n}^{2}(x):=k^{2}-\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{h(x)^{2}},

(7)

with
\displaystyle\text{Re}(k_{n}),\text{Im}(k_{n})\geq 0
.

In this work, as
\displaystyle h(x)
is non constant,
\displaystyle k_{n}(x)
may vanish for some
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
and change from a positive real number to a purely imaginary number. We then distinguish three different situations:

Definition 3
.

A mode
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
falls in one of these three situations:

1.

If
\displaystyle n<kh(x)/\pi
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
then
\displaystyle k_{n}(x)\in(0,+\infty)
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
and the mode
\displaystyle n
is called propagative.

2.

If
\displaystyle n>kh(x)/\pi
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
then
\displaystyle k_{n}(x)\in i(0,+\infty)
for all
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}
and the mode
\displaystyle n
is called evanescent.

3.

If there exists
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle n=kh(x^{\star}_{k})/\pi
the mode
\displaystyle n
is called locally resonant. Such points
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}
are called locally resonant points, and there are simple if
\displaystyle h^{\prime}(x^{\star}_{k})\neq 0
, and multiple otherwise.

A frequency
\displaystyle k>0
for which there exists at least a locally resonant mode is called a locally resonant frequency.

Using the wavenumber function, one can adapt the classic Sommerfeld (or outgoing) condition, defined in
[
11
]
for regular waveguides, to general varying waveguides
\displaystyle\Omega
. This condition is used to guarantee uniqueness for the source problem given in equation (
2
).

Definition 4
.

A wavefield
\displaystyle u_{k}\in\text{H}^{2}_{\text{loc}}\big(\Omega\big)
is said to be outgoing if it satisfies

\left|u_{k,N}^{\prime}(x)\frac{x}{|x|}-ik_{n}(x)u_{k,N}(x)\right|\underset{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

(8)

where
\displaystyle u_{k,N}
is given in (
6
).

In all this work, we make the following assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

We assume that
\displaystyle h\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\displaystyle h^{\prime}
compactly supported and that

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\quad h_{\min}\leq h(x)\leq h_{\max}\quad\text{ for some }\quad 0<h_{\max}<h_{\min}<\infty.

We also assume that
\displaystyle h(x)=h_{\min}
or
\displaystyle h(x)=h_{\max}
if
\displaystyle x\notin\supp(h^{\prime})
. For such a function we define a parameter
\displaystyle\eta>0
that satisfies

\|h^{\prime}\|_{\text{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\eta\quad\text{ and }\quad\|h^{\prime\prime}\|_{\text{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\eta^{2}.

The forward source problem is defined for every frequency by

(\mathcal{H}_{k}):\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\Delta u_{k}+k^{2}u_{k}=-f&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{k}=b_{\text{top}}&\text{ in }\partial\Omega_{\text{top}},\\
\partial_{\nu}u_{k}=b_{\text{bot}}&\text{ in }\partial\Omega_{\text{bot}},\\
u_{k}\text{ is outgoing.}\end{array}\right.

(9)

As explained in
[
12
]
, this problem is not well-posed when the set
\displaystyle\{x\,|\,k_{n}(x)=0\}
is a non-trivial interval of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
. This especially happens when
\displaystyle k=n\pi/h_{\min}
or
\displaystyle k=n\pi/h_{\max}
. We then avoid these two situations and we set

\delta(k):=\min_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\sqrt{\left|k^{2}-\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{{h_{\min}}^{2}}\right|},\sqrt{\left|k^{2}-\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}}{{h_{\max}}^{2}}\right|}\right)>0.

(10)

From now on, we define
\displaystyle(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
the modal decomposition of
\displaystyle f
, and

g_{n}(x)=\frac{f_{n}(x)}{\sqrt{h(x)}}+\varphi_{n}(1)b_{\text{top}}(x)\frac{\sqrt{1+(h^{\prime}(x))^{2}}}{\sqrt{h}}+\varphi_{n}(0)b_{\text{bot}}(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}.

(11)

Using the work done in
[
10
]
, we are able to provide an approximation of the solution of (
9
). If
\displaystyle h
is increasing, we can state the following result using Theorem 1 and Remark 5 in
[

Theorem 1
.

Let
\displaystyle k>0
and let
\displaystyle h
be an increasing function defining a varying waveguide
\displaystyle\Omega
that satisfies Assumption

with a variation parameter
\displaystyle\eta>0
. Consider sources
\displaystyle f\in\text{L}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\text{L}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle b:=(b_{\text{bot}},b_{\text{top}})\in({\text{H}}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}))^{2}\cap(\text{L}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})^{2})

\displaystyle N\in\mathbb{N}
, associated with a simple resonant point
\displaystyle x^{\star}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
.

There exists
\displaystyle\eta_{0}>0
depending only on
\displaystyle h_{\min}
,
\displaystyle h_{\max}
,
\displaystyle\delta(k)
, defined in Assumption
1
such that if
\displaystyle\eta\leq\eta_{0}
, then the problem
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}_{k})
admits a unique solution
\displaystyle u_{k}\in\text{H}^{2}_{\text{loc}}\big(\Omega)
. Moreover, this solution is approached by
\displaystyle u_{k}^{\text{app}}
defined for almost every
\displaystyle(x,y)\in\Omega
by

u_{k}^{\text{app}}(x,y):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}u_{k,n}^{\text{app}}(x)\varphi_{n}(y),\qquad u_{k,n}^{\text{app}}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{n}^{\text{app}}(x,s)g_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

(12)

where
\displaystyle g_{n}
is defined in (

),
\displaystyle\varphi_{n}
is defined in (

\displaystyle G_{n}^{\text{app}}
is given by

G_{n}^{\text{app}}(x,s):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{i}{2\sqrt{k_{n}(s)k_{n}(x)}}\exp\left(i\left|\int_{s}^{x}k_{n}\right|\right),&\quad\text{ if }n<N,\\
&\frac{1}{2\sqrt{|k_{n}|(s)|k_{n}|(x)}}\exp\left(-\left|\int_{s}^{x}|k_{n}|\right|\right),&\quad\text{if }n>N,\\

\end{aligned}\right.&\quad\text{ if }n=N.\end{aligned}\right.

(13)

Function
\displaystyle k_{n}

\displaystyle\xi

\xi(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(-\frac{3}{2}i\int_{x}^{x^{\star}_{k}}k_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2/3}&\text{ if }x<x^{\star}_{k},\\

Precisely, given a coordinate

\displaystyle h

The proposed inversion method consists in recovering the locally resonant points
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{I})
for each frequency in
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{k}:=(k_{1},\dots,k_{I})
. The shape function
\displaystyle h
will be then approached by a continuous piecewise linear function using the formula
\displaystyle h(x_{i})=N\pi/k_{i}
. Hence, the discrete inverse problem reads

\text{Find }\quad\boldsymbol{x}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\quad\text{ from }\quad\boldsymbol{d}^{\text{ex}}:=(u_{k_{1},N}(x^{\text{meas}}),\dots,u_{k_{I},N}(x^{\text{meas}})).

(21)

The analysis of this non-linear inverse problem is decomposed in four steps:

•

Step 1: (Data approximation)
In Section 3.1, we prove that

u_{k,N}(x^{\text{meas}})=C\,\Phi\circ\zeta(k)+\mathcal{O}(\eta),

(22)

where
\displaystyle C
is a known constant,

\Phi(x):=\sin(x+\pi/4)\exp(ix+i\pi/4)\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta(k):=\int_{x^{\star}_{k}}^{x^{\text{meas}}}\sqrt{k^{2}-\frac{N^{2}\pi^{2}}{h(x)^{2}}}\mathrm{d}x.

(23)

•

Step 2 : (Inversion of
\displaystyle\Phi
)
In Section 3.2, we inverse
\displaystyle\Phi
to obtain
\displaystyle\zeta(k)
. The function
\displaystyle\Phi
admits a left inverse function modulus
\displaystyle\pi
. We prove that if the frequency discretization step
\displaystyle\rho
is small enough, the global left inverse can be found and applied to the data. It leads to an approximation of
\displaystyle\zeta(k)
up to an error in
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\eta)
.

•

Step 3: (Discretization of the integral)
In Section 3.3, we use the numerical integration elementary formula

\displaystyle\int_{x_{j+1}}^{x_{j}}k_{i,N}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\approx(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\left(\frac{1}{4}k_{i,N}(x_{j+1})+\frac{3}{4}k_{i,N}(x_{j})\right)

\displaystyle\approx(x_{j}-x_{j+1})\left(\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{k_{i}^{2}-k_{j+1}^{2}}+\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{k_{i}^{2}-k_{j}^{2}}\right)

to approach
\displaystyle\zeta(k_{i})
. It allows to run a stripping method to recover the differences
\displaystyle x_{j}-x_{j+1}
from the approached values of
\displaystyle\zeta(k_{i})
, which provides an approximation of
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}
. As we will see, this stripping method conduces to triangular linear system.

•

Step 4: (Reconstuction of
\displaystyle h
)
In Section 3.4, we use the approximation
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}
to provide a reconstruction of
\displaystyle h
. We quantify the reconstruction error of this inverse method as well as its stability.

3.1
Data approximation

First, we want to approach the locally resonant modal data
\displaystyle u_{k,N}(x^{\text{meas}})
. Theorem
1
provides a first approximation
\displaystyle u_{k,N}^{\text{app}}(x^{\text{meas}})
of these measurements. However, this expression is hardly usable to do inverse problems. Using the fact that the measurement section
\displaystyle\{x=x^{\text{meas}}\}
is far from the defect, we can simplify this approximation:

Proposition 1
.

Let
\displaystyle k\in\boldsymbol{k}
and assume that
\displaystyle\Omega
satisfies Assumption
1
. Let
\displaystyle x^{\text{meas}}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist
\displaystyle\eta_{0},A>0
such that if
\displaystyle\eta<\eta_{0}
and
\displaystyle x^{\text{meas}}-x_{0}>A
, then there exists
\displaystyle C_{2}>0
depending on
\displaystyle\delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}
,
\displaystyle h_{\min}
and
\displaystyle h_{\max}
such that for every
\displaystyle k\in\delta_{\boldsymbol{k}}
,

u_{k,N}(x^{\text{meas}})=q(k)\,\Phi\circ\zeta(k)+\mathcal{O}(\eta),\qquad\left|u_{k,N}(x^{\text{meas}})-q(k)\,\Phi\circ\zeta(k)\right|\leq\eta C_{2},

(24)

where
\displaystyle q(k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{N}(z)e^{ik_{N}(x^{\text{meas}})(z-x^{\text{meas}})}\mathrm{d}z/k_{N}(x^{\text{meas}})
is a constant depending only on the source terms.

Proof.

Using Theorem
1
, we know that for every
\displaystyle k\in K
,

|u_{k,N}(x^{\text{meas}})-u_{k,N}^{\text{app}}(x^{\text{meas}})|\leq\eta C_{1}\delta^{-8}(K)\left(\|f\|_{\text{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|b\|_{\text{H}^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})}\right),

where

u_{k,N}^{\text{app}}(x^{\text{meas}})=\frac{\pi(-\xi(x^{\text{meas}}))^{1/4}\mathcal{A}(\xi(x^{\text{meas}}))}{k_{N}(x^{\text{meas}})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{N}(z)(-\xi(z))^{1/4}(i\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})(\xi(z))\mathrm{d}z.

Using approximations of Airy functions provided in
[
22
]
and
[
1
, Chap. 9.7]
, we see that there exists a constant
\displaystyle c_{1}>0
such that for all
\displaystyle x>0
,

\left|\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4}\mathcal{A}(-x)+\sin\left(\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{x^{5/4}},

\left|\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4}\mathcal{B}(-x)+\cos\left(\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{x^{5/4}}.

and so

\left|\sqrt{\pi}x^{1/4}(i\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})(-x)+\exp\left(i\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}+i\frac{\pi}{4}\right)\right|\leq\frac{2c_{1}}{x^{5/4}},

Replacing
\displaystyle x
by
\displaystyle-\xi(x)
or
\displaystyle-\xi(x^{\text{meas}})
leads to

\left|u_{k,N}^{\text{app}}(x^{\text{meas}})-q(k)\,\Phi\circ\zeta(k)\right|\leq\delta^{-1}\|g_{N}\|_{\text{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\frac{3c_{1}+2c_{1}^{2}}{(-\xi(x^{\text{meas}}))^{5/4}}.

We conclude the proof by noticing that

-\xi(x^{\text{meas}})=\left(\frac{3}{2}\int_{x^{\star}_{k}}^{x^{\text{meas}}}k_{N}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2/3}\geq(x^{\text{meas}}-x_{0})^{2/3}k_{N}(x^{\text{meas}})^{2/3}\underset{x^{\text{meas}}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty.

∎

3.3
Triangular linear recovery of
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}

Using the left inverse of
\displaystyle\Phi
and the data provided in the previous section, we now try to reconstruct from
\displaystyle(\zeta(k_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq I}
the set
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}
. To do so, we need to find a link between
\displaystyle(\zeta(k_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq I}
and
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}
. Noticing that

\forall 1\leq i\leq I\quad h(x_{i})=\frac{N\pi}{k_{i}},\qquad\forall 1\leq j<i\leq I\quad k_{i,N}(x_{j})=\sqrt{k_{i}^{2}-\frac{N^{2}\pi^{2}}{h(x_{j})^{2}}}=\sqrt{k_{i}^{2}-k_{j}^{2}},

(34)

we choose to discretize the integral
\displaystyle\textstyle\int\nolimits_{x_{i}}^{x^{\text{meas}}}k_{i,N}
on the grid
\displaystyle(x_{j})_{1\leq j\leq i}
. We define the known quantities

p_{i,j}:=\sqrt{|k_{i}^{2}-k_{j}^{2}|}.

(35)

A first idea would be to use a rectangular rule to discretize the integral, as illustrated in the introduction in Figure
2
. However, since
\displaystyle h
is increasing,
\displaystyle x\mapsto k_{i,N}(x)
is concave and we know that for all
\displaystyle j>2
,

\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j-1}}k_{i,N}\geq(x_{j-1}-x_{j})\frac{k_{i,N}(x_{j})+k_{i,N}(x_{j-1})}{2}=(x_{j-1}-x_{j})\frac{p_{i,j}+p_{i,j-1}}{2},

(36)

\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j-1}}k_{i,N}\leq(x_{j-1}-x_{j})k_{i,N}(x_{j-1})=(x_{j-1}-x_{j})p_{i,j-1}.

(37)

Instead of using a rectangular rule, we choose to approach the integral using the mean of these two bounds:

\int_{x_{j}}^{x_{j-1}}k_{i,N}\approx\frac{1}{4}(x_{j-1}-x_{j})\left(p_{i,j}+3p_{i,j-1}\right).

(38)

When
\displaystyle j=1
, we cannot apply this approach and we simply choose to use

\int_{x_{1}}^{x^{\text{meas}}}k_{i,N}\approx(x^{\text{meas}}-x_{1})k_{i,N}(x^{\text{meas}})=(x^{\text{meas}}-x_{1})p_{1,0}.

(39)

Remark 1
.

We chose here to approach the integral by taking the mean of a rectangle and a trapezoidal rule. However, any barycenter between these two bounds would also work. Further investigations may prove that the mean may not be the optimal choice. However, giving the lack of regularity of
\displaystyle k_{i,N}
around
\displaystyle x_{i}
, any quadrature method should give the same error estimation up to a constant.

We define the triangular matrix

T:=\frac{1}{4}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}4p_{1,0}&0&0&\ldots&0\\
4p_{2,0}&3p_{2,1}&0&&\vdots\\[5.69054pt]

\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&3p_{I-1,I-2}&0\\
4p_{I,0}&p_{I,2}+3p_{I,1}&\ldots&p_{I,I-1}+3p_{I,I-2}&3p_{I,I-1}\end{array}\right),

(40)

and we expect that

x_{1}-x_{2}\\

x_{I-1}-x_{I}\end{array}\right).

(41)

.

Remark 3

We notice in this inequality that if

Proof.

\displaystyle h

5
Conclusion

From the same analysis of the forward problem than in
[
20
]
, we have developed a new method to recover width defect using section measurements. We have used the study of the forward problem in slowly varying waveguide presented in
[
10
]
and the approximation of the solutions using the
\displaystyle\pi
-periodic function
\displaystyle\Phi
to develop an inverse reconstruction method in slowly varying monotonous waveguides. Given wavefield measurements on a section of the waveguide for different locally resonant frequencies, we reconstruct with stability the associated resonant points which provides a good approximation of the width in the waveguide.

Even if this method only works when sources and receivers are on the larger side of the waveguide, its reconstruction results are excellent and we believe that this method could be useful to detect and localize precisely the position of width defects in waveguides. Combined with a traditional multi-frequency back-scattering method like the one presented in
[
10
]
, this method could produce great results in recovering any type of width defect as long as there are small and slowly varying.

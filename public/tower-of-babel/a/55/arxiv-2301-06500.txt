Factorization of certain Macdonald Littlewood-Richardson coefficients
Konstantin Matveev and Yuchen Wei

Abstract
. We find and prove a factorization formula for certain Macdonald Littlewood-Richardson coefficients c λ ​ μ ν ​ ( q , t ) c_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}(q,t) . Namely, we consider the case that the Kostka number K μ , ν − λ K_{\mu,\nu-\lambda} is 1 1 . This settles a particular case of a more general conjecture of Richard Stanely, [ St89 ] . This conjecture proposes that a factorization formula exists whenever the corresponding regular Littlewood-Richardson coefficient c λ ​ μ ν c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu} is 1 1 .

1.1.
Preliminaries

1.1.1.
Macdonald functions

P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}(q,t)
is a Macdonald symmetric function,
[
Mac99
]
.
\{P_{\lambda}\}
with
\lambda
ranging over partitions is a basis of the algebra of symmetric functions over
\mathbb{C}(q,t)
.
P_{\lambda}P_{\mu}=\sum_{\nu}c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)P_{\nu}
, where the sum ranges over partitions
\nu
with
|\nu|=|\lambda|+|\mu|
. We are interested in studying these structure constants
c_{\lambda\mu}^{\nu}(q,t)
, i.e. Macdonald Littlewood-Richardson coefficients.

1.1.2.
Schur functions

P_{\lambda}
for
q=t
becomes the Schur function
S_{\lambda}
, which doesn’t depend on
q=t
. For
n\geq\ell(\lambda)
polynomial

can be viewed as the character of the Schur module
E^{\lambda}
, an irreducible representation of
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. So for

the coefficient

is a non-negative integer that counts the multiplicity of
E^{\nu}
in
E^{\lambda}\otimes E^{\mu}
,
[
F97
, chapter 8]
.

1.1.3.
Littlewood-Richardson rule

c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}
counts the number of semistandard skew tableaux
T
of shape
\nu/\lambda
with weight
\mu
and additional property that

is a lattice word. Here word

is obtained by reading symbols in
T
from right to left in successive rows, starting with the top row. A word of length
N
in the symbols
1,2,...,n
is said to be a lattice word if for
1\leq r\leq N
and
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the number of occurrences of the symbol

in the first
r
letters of

is not less than the number of occurrences of
i+1
. This is the celebrated Littlewood-Richardson rule,
[
LR34
]
,
[
F97
, chapter 5]
,
[
Ste02
]
.
c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}
also enumerates other families of combinatorial objects: Berenstein-Zelevinsky patterns,
[
BZ92
]
, Knutson-Tao honeycombs/puzzles/hives,
[
KT99
]
,
[
KTW04
]
,
[
B00
]
, Vakil checkergames,
[
V06
]
.

1.1.4.
Pieri formulas

For one-row
\mu=(r)
or one-column
\mu=1^{r}
the corresponding Macdonald Littlewood-Richardson coefficients are given explicitly by the Pieri formulas.

Proposition 1.1

(Mac99)
.

(1.1)

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}\cdot P_{1^{r}}=\sum_{\lambda\prec_{v}\nu,\ |\nu|-|\lambda|=r}\psi_{\nu/\lambda}^{\prime}P_{\nu},

where
\nu/\lambda
is a skew shape of
r

\psi^{\prime}_{\nu/\lambda}:=\prod_{s\in C_{\nu/\lambda}-R_{\nu/\lambda}}{\hskip 0.0ptb_{\nu}(s)\hskip 0.0pt\over\hskip 0.0ptb_{\lambda}(s)\hskip 0.0pt},\quad b_{\lambda}(s)=b_{\lambda}(i,j):=\begin{cases}{\hskip 0.0pt1-q^{\lambda_{i}-j}t^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i+1}\hskip 0.0pt\over\hskip 0.0pt1-q^{\lambda_{i}-j+1}t^{\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i}\hskip 0.0pt}&\text{ if }s\in\lambda\\

where

(respectively

(1.2)

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}\cdot P_{(r)}=\sum_{\lambda\prec_{h}\nu,\ |\nu|-|\lambda|=r}{\hskip 0.0pt(q;q)_{r}\hskip 0.0pt\over\hskip 0.0pt(t;q)_{r}\hskip 0.0pt}\varphi_{\nu/\lambda}P_{\nu},

where
\nu/\lambda
is a skew shape of
r

See
[

1.1.5.
Formulas for Macdonald Littlewood-Richardson coefficients

[
S11
]
uses interpolation Macdonald polynomials to find an expression for
c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)
,
[
Y12
]
uses nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials and combinatorics of alcove walks to find another formula. However, both expressions are very complex. We are interested in finding special cases when the corresponding coefficients easily factorize.
More precisely, we are looking for triples of partitions
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
such that
c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)
is a product of terms
\left({\hskip 0.0pt1-q^{a}t^{b+1}\hskip 0.0pt\over\hskip 0.0pt1-q^{a+1}t^{b}\hskip 0.0pt}\right)^{\pm 1}
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Pieri formulas show that
(\lambda,1^{r},\nu)
are such triples whenever
\nu/\lambda
is a vertical
r
-strip,
(\lambda,(r),\nu)
are such triples whenever
\nu/\lambda
is a horizontal
r
-strip. By commutativity, same holds for
(1^{r},\mu,\nu)
and

((r),\mu,\nu)
.

1.1.6.
Stanley’s conjecture

Setting
q=t
implies that triples with factorization of this kind must satisfy
c^{\nu}_{\lambda\mu}=1
. Richard Stanley conjectured in
[
St89
, Conjecture 8.5]
that the converse is also true. More precisely, his conjecture was for Jack functions which can be obtained from Macdonald functions by setting
q=t^{\alpha}
and
t\to 1
. But it transfers to the more general Macdonald case in a straightforward way. As far as we know, the conjecture in general remains open. Stanley also suggested that the factorization formula must belong to a specified finite family. However, it remains unclear how in general to chose the correct element of this family.
[
N16
]
proved Stanley’s conjecture for the special case
\ell(\lambda),\ell(\mu),\ell(\nu)\leq 3
. In this paper we identify another family of special cases for which we can prove factorization.

1.2.8.
Proof outline of Proposition
1.3

We do induction on the total number
n
of boxes in a given semistandard tableau
T
.

To show that
(C_{n})\implies(C_{n}^{\prime})
, for arbitrary two columns in
T
, we identify seven different cases characterized by the relative positions of the lowest boxes in these two columns as well as the appearances of the largest integer
l
of
T
in these two columns. By the induction hypotheses, we can quickly go through the first five cases.

When the sixth case happens, we use induction to argue that
(C_{n}^{\prime})
must hold, namely,
[\lambda_{j}^{\prime}]
is a subset of
[\lambda_{i}^{\prime}]
in this particular case. It turns out that this further boils down to showing that when
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+1
, we must have
[\lambda_{j}^{\prime}]=[\lambda_{i}^{\prime}]\setminus\{l\}
for any
i<j
satisfying Case 6. Proceed by proving by contradiction. Assume on the contrary that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}^{\prime}+1
and
[\lambda_{j}^{\prime}]\neq[\lambda_{i}^{\prime}]\setminus\{l\}
for some
i<j
in Case 6, then after doing a counterclockwise shift of a sequence of boxes(the notations are defined in the proof):

\displaystyle(T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-h,S),T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-h+1,s),

\displaystyle T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-h+2,s),...,T(\lambda_{i}^{\prime},s),T(\lambda_{i}^{\prime},s+1),...,

\displaystyle T(\lambda_{i}^{\prime},m),T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime},S),T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-1,S),...,T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-h+1,S))

we will get a new tableau. This contradicts
(C_{n})
.

It remains to show that Case 7 is impossible. After ruling out the simple case where
T(\lambda_{i}^{\prime},i)>T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-1,j)
we claim that doing a clockwise shift of the boxes:

(T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-h+1,i),T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-(h-1)+1,i),...,T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime},i),T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime},j),T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-1,j),...,T(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-h+1,j))

yields a new tableau, contradicting
(C_{n})
.

To show that
(C_{n}^{\prime})\implies(C_{n})
, we prove by contradiction. Assume that we have two distinct tableaux with the same shape and weight, we first show that the weights of each corresponding vertical rectangular blocks must be the same. This implies that the positions of

holds which implies that the rest of the tableaux are also the same. This concludes the proof.

T
is unique of its shape and weight. It is comprised of a sequence of rectangular blocks of decreasing heights. Each of these blocks must also be unique of its shape and weight by proposition
1.3

|S_{1}\cap S_{2}|=k-1

|S_{1}\cup S_{2}|=k+1
. There are two cases:
S_{3}\not\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2}
and
S_{3}\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. Suppose
S_{3}\not\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. Then
S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}=S_{1}\backslash\{a\}=S_{2}\backslash\{b\}=S_{3}\backslash\{c\}
for some distinct

, so

. If any other
S_{i}
was to miss an element of
S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}
, then it would have to contain all

and would have size
\geq(k-2)+3>k
. Contradiction. So in this case
\displaystyle\biggr\lvert\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}\biggr\rvert=|S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}|=k-1
, hence it is the case of the second kind. Suppose now that
S_{3}\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2}
. If there was any other
S_{i}\not\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, then we would arrive to contradiction as in the previous case by switching
S_{3}
and
S_{i}
. So in this case any
S_{i}\subset S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, hence
\displaystyle\biggr\lvert\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}S_{i}\biggr\rvert=|S_{1}\cup S_{2}|=k+1
, hence it is the case of the third kind.
∎

3.2.
Pieri formulas

Recall that
e_{\eta}
for a partition
\eta
denotes
\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{\eta_{i}}
.
Partitions admit dominance partial order:
\chi\unrhd\rho
if
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}+\cdots+\chi_{i}\geq\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+\cdots+\rho_{i}
for any
i\geq 1
.
\chi\rhd\rho
means that
\chi\unrhd\rho
and
\chi\neq\rho
. Repeated application of the vertical Pieri formulas (
1.1
) leads to

\displaystyle e_{\mu^{\prime}}=P_{\mu}+\sum_{\eta:\ \eta^{\prime}\rhd\mu^{\prime}}c_{\eta}P_{\eta}

for some coefficients
c_{\eta}
. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle P_{\mu}=e_{\mu^{\prime}}+\sum_{\eta:\ \eta^{\prime}\rhd\mu^{\prime}}(-c_{\eta})P_{\eta}.

However, for any
\eta\rhd\mu^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle P_{\eta}=e_{\eta^{\prime}}+\sum_{\zeta:\ \zeta^{\prime}\rhd\eta^{\prime}}(-c_{\zeta})P_{\zeta},

we can rewrite

\displaystyle P_{\mu}=e_{\mu^{\prime}}+\sum_{\eta:\ \eta^{\prime}\rhd\mu^{\prime}}(-c_{\eta})\left[e_{\eta^{\prime}}+\sum_{\zeta:\ \zeta^{\prime}\rhd\eta^{\prime}}(-c_{\zeta})P_{\zeta}\right].

Continue substituting as above until the right side becomes a linear combination of elementary symmetric functions after finite steps. This yields

(3.1)

\displaystyle P_{\mu}=e_{\mu^{\prime}}+\sum_{\eta:\ \eta^{\prime}\rhd\mu^{\prime}}d_{\eta^{\prime}}e_{\eta^{\prime}}

for some other coefficients
d_{\eta^{\prime}}
. We say that
f\sim_{\lambda,\nu}g
for symmetric functions
f,g
if
P_{\lambda}(f-g)
doesn’t have
P_{\nu}
in its expansion in the Macdonald basis. It follows from the vertical Pieri formulas
1.1
that
f\sim_{\lambda,\nu}f+e_{\eta}
whenever
\eta_{1}
is larger than the number of non-zero rows of
\nu\backslash\lambda
.

Set-Valued Analysis of Generalized Barycentric Coordinates and Their Geometric Properties
Fabio V. Difonzo

Abstract
. Letting P P be a convex polytope in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with n > d n>d vertices, we study geometric and analytical properties of the set of generalized barycentric coordinates relative to any point p ∈ P p\in P . We prove that such sets are polytopes in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with at most n − d − 1 n-d-1 vertices, and provide results about continuity and differentiability for the corresponding set-valued maps.

1.
Introduction

Barycentric coordinates, first introduced by Möbius
[
13
]
, are nowadays useful tools in interpolation problems
[
10
,
9
]
, computational mechanics
[
4
]
, computer graphics
[
15
]
and differential equation theory
[
6
]
. We refer to
[
12
]
and all the references therein for a thorough source of theory and applications of generalized barycentric coordinates. In this work we attempt to study them as set-valued maps from convex
d
-polytopes with
n
vertices,
n>d
, having values onto the unit cube
[0,1]^{n}
, and to provide some fundamental geometrical and analytical properties about them.

2.
Setting

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n>d
,
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a set of vertices and let

V\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}&\cdots&v_{n}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}.

Defining
\mathds{1}
to be the vector whose components are all equal to one, and assuming that
\dim\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=n-d-1
, then there exists
N\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-d-1)}
such that

(2.1)

\langle N\rangle=\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
P\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{conv}V
be the convex polytope whose vertices are given by the columns of
V
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p\in P
. A vector
\lambda(p)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a set of generalized barycentric coordinates for
p
if it is a componentwise nonnegative solution to the linear system

(2.2)

\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\lambda=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

When
\lambda\geq 0
componentwise, we will also say that
\lambda
is feasible. It comes from the definition that, for each
p\in P
there always exists a set of feasible generalized barycentric coordinates
\tau(p)\geq 0
. More precisely, since (
2.2
) is full rank, it is meaningful to define the set, depending on
p
,

(2.3)

\Gamma(p)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{c\in\mathbb{R}^{n-d-1}\,:\,\tau(p)+Nc\geq 0\},\quad p\in P.

Thus, we can consider the following set-valued map

\displaystyle\Gamma:P

\displaystyle\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n-d-1}

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto\Gamma(p)

It then follows that the entire set of feasible barycentric coordinates relative to
p\in P
can be expressed by the set-valued map

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda:P

\displaystyle\rightrightarrows[0,1]^{n}

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\mapsto\tau(p)+N\Gamma(p).

We prove that
\Lambda(p)
, and hence
\Gamma(p)
, do not depend on the particular
\tau(p)
chosen. In fact, if
\tau^{\prime}(p)
is another feasible set of barycentric coordinates for
p
, then there exists
c^{\prime}\in\Gamma(p)
such that

\tau(p)=\tau^{\prime}(p)+Nc^{\prime},

and therefore
\Lambda(p)=\tau^{\prime}(p)+Nc^{\prime}+N\Gamma(p)=\tau^{\prime}(p)+N\Gamma(p)
.

Let us also notice that for each
c\in\Gamma(p)
there exists a unique
\lambda\in\Lambda(p)
such that
\lambda(p)=\tau(p)+Nc
and, thus, we have a bijective correspondence between the two sets
\Gamma(p)
and
\Lambda(p)
.

It is easily seen that, from (
2.2
),

\Lambda(p)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}\lambda=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\end{bmatrix},\,\lambda\geq 0\right\},

where, hereafter,
\lambda\geq 0
is meant to hold componentwise.

This paper is devoted to study geometric and analytical properties of the set-valued maps
\Gamma(p)
and
\Lambda(p)
.

In Section
3
we prove that
\Lambda(p)
is a bounded polytope, and we describe its vertices.
In Section
4
we study smoothness of these maps.

3.
Geometric structure of
\Lambda(p)
and
\Gamma(p)

In this section we state and prove fundamental results about the geometric features of
\Lambda(p)
and
\Gamma(p)
. Such results will be useful for investigating analytical properties of the set-valued maps introduced above.

It holds the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

For each
p\in P
,
\Gamma(p)
and
\Lambda(p)
are polytopes of dimension at most
n-d-1
.

Proof.

From (
2.3
)
\Gamma(p)
is a closed convex polyhedron and, since
\tau(p)
can always be chosen such that
\tau(p)\geq 0
,
0\in\Gamma(p)
, therefore providing
\Gamma(p)^{\circ\circ}=\Gamma(p)
, where
\Gamma(p)^{\circ}
is the polar of
\Gamma(p)
. From classical results (e.g., see
[
3
,
11
]
),
\Gamma(p)^{\circ}
is a polyhedron, so that
\Gamma(p)^{\circ\circ}
is a polytope, and so is
\Gamma(p)
. Since
\Lambda(p)=\tau(p)+N\Gamma(p)
, then
\Lambda(p)
is a polytope as well.

Thus
\Gamma(p)
is a convex, and hence affine, subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n-d-1}
, and so
\dim\Gamma(p)\leq n-d-1
. By Caratheodory’s Theorem (
[
3
]
) let
\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-d}\}
be an affine base for
\Gamma(p)
.

Given an arbitrary
\lambda\in\Lambda(p)
, it can be written as
\lambda=\tau+Nc
for some unique
c\in\Gamma(p)
. Therefore, there exist
\alpha_{i}\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,n-d
, such that

c=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\alpha_{i}c_{i},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\alpha_{i}=1.

Letting
\lambda_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tau+Nc_{i},i=1,\ldots,n-d
, it follows that
\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda(p)
and

\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}.

Thus
\Lambda(p)\subseteq\mathrm{conv}\{\lambda_{i}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,n-d\}
, implying
\dim\Lambda(p)\leq n-d-1
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
p\in\partial P
and since facet reducing property is shared by all feasible barycentric coordinates, then
\dim\Lambda(p)=0
.

If
p\in\mathrm{int}P
then, by Caratheodory’s Theorem, every
\lambda\in\Lambda(p)
can be written as the convex combination of
n-d
elements in
\Lambda(p)
.

Corollary 3.3
.

\Gamma:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n-d-1}
and
\Lambda:P\rightrightarrows[0,1]^{n}
have compact convex values.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Next, we want to describe the vertices of the
\Lambda(p)
as a polytope. We prove that they are given by the simplicial barycentric coordinates that are feasible at
p
, as defined below. In order to prove this, we introduce some notation.

For each
i=1,\ldots,n
, let

\mathcal{I}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{i,i+1,\ldots,i+n-d-2\},

where indices are considered circularly (i.e.,
n+1\equiv n
and so on). Let us observe that
|\mathcal{I}_{i}|=n-d-1
. Let then
\sigma^{i}(p)
be the unique solution to

(3.1)

\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
I_{i}\end{bmatrix}\sigma=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix},

where
I_{i}
is a diagonal matrix such that

I_{i}(j,j)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\begin{cases}1,&j\in\mathcal{I}_{i},\\
0,&j\notin\mathcal{I}_{i}.\end{cases}

As
\{v_{i}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,n\}
are such that
\dim\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=n-d-1
, it follows that
\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
I_{i}\end{bmatrix}
is invertible, and thus (
3.1
) has a unique solution
\sigma^{i}(p)
, which we refer to as
simplicial barycentric coordinates
for
p
. It is straightforward to realize that

\displaystyle\sigma^{i}_{j}(p)

\displaystyle=0,\,\,\textrm{ if }j\in\mathcal{I}_{i},

\displaystyle\sigma^{i}_{j}(p)

\displaystyle\neq 0,\,\,\textrm{ if }j\notin\mathcal{I}_{i}.

It holds the following

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
p\in P
. Then there exists
I(p)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
|I(p)|=n-d
and
\sigma^{i}(p)\geq 0
is a vertex of
\Lambda(p)
for all
i\in I(p)
. Therefore

\Lambda(p)=\mathrm{conv}\{\sigma^{i}(p)\,:\,i\in I(p)\}.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.1
, it suffices to prove that there exist at most, and thus exactly by Caratheodory’s Theorem,
n-d
feasible simplicial barycentric coordinates
\sigma^{i}(p)
at
p
. Proceeding by contradiction, let us suppose that for any
\overline{n}>n-d
and for any
i=1,\ldots,\overline{n}
, there is some
j_{i}=1,\ldots,n
such that
\sigma^{i}_{j_{i}}(p)<0
, where
\sigma^{i}(p)
is the simplicial barycentric coordinates for
p
defined in (
3.1
). This implies that

p\notin S_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{j}\,:\,j\notin\mathcal{I}_{i}\},

for all
i=1,\ldots,\overline{n}
. Let us note that each
S_{i}
is a
d
-simplex. This contradicts the fact that the polytope
P
has at least
n-d
simplices (see
[
8
,
11
,
16
]
).

Let then
I(p)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
be such that
|I(p)|=n-d
and
\sigma^{i}(p)\geq 0
for all
i\in I(p)
, and let us now prove that
\sigma^{i}(p)
are vertices of
\Lambda(p)
. In fact, let
i\in I(p)
be given and let
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda(p)
and
a\in(0,1)
be such that

\sigma^{i}(p)=(1-a)\lambda+a\mu.

Therefore, for all
j\in\mathcal{I}_{i}
,

0=(1-a)\lambda_{j}+a\mu_{j},

so that, by nonnegativity, it follows that
\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}=0
, and thus
\lambda,\mu
both solve (
3.1
). Since it is nonsingular, we deduce
\lambda=\mu=\sigma^{i}(p)
, implying that
\sigma^{i}(p)
is a vertex of
\Lambda(p)
. Resorting to Krein-Milman Theorem proves the claim.
∎

Example 3.5
.

Let us consider a convex quadrilateral
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as

Q=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\,:\,i=1,2,3,4\},

where the
v_{i}
’s are assumed to be in general position. In this case, letting
p\in Q
, Theorem
3.4
implies that
\Lambda(p)
is a segment in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, whose vertices are given by the triangular barycentric coordinates nonnegative at
p
. In other words, assuming without loss of generality that
p\in\mathrm{conv}\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}\cap\mathrm{conv}\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{4}\}
, and letting
\tau^{4}(p),\tau^{3}(p)
be the unique solutions to

\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}\tau^{4}(p)=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&1&0\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}\tau^{3}(p)=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\\
0\end{bmatrix}

respectively, then
\Lambda(p)=\mathrm{conv}\{\tau^{3}(p),\tau^{4}(p)\}
.

In fact, here
N\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
and it is easily seen that for any
\lambda(p)\in\Lambda(p)
there exists a unique
c\in[a,b]
such that

\lambda(p)=\tau^{4}(p)+cN,

where we define
a\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max\left\{-\frac{\tau^{4}_{i}(p)}{N_{i}}\,:\,N_{i}>0\right\},b\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\min\left\{-\frac{\tau^{4}_{i}(p)}{N_{i}}\,:\,N_{i}<0\right\}
(see
[
7
]
). It is completely evident that one between
a,b
must be zero: assuming it to be
a
, then
b>0
; thus
c=a
provides
\lambda(p)=\tau^{4}(p)
, while
c=b
provides
\lambda(p)=\tau^{3}(p)
. Therefore, since
\tau^{4}(p)-\tau^{3}(p)\in\langle N\rangle
, we deduce that
\lambda(p)\in\mathrm{conv}\{\tau^{3}(p),\tau^{4}(p)\}
, as expected. If
b=0
, then it is enough, without loss of generality, to consider
-N
instead of
N
in the arguments above.

Example 3.6
.

For pentagons or polytopes with more than five vertices in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a similar idea has been explored in
[
1
]
, where authors propose to consider a suitably blended average of barycentric coordinates relative to a given triangularization of the polytope. As a matter of fact, Theorem
3.4
provides a theoretical justification to the blending approach.

More explicitly, let us consider a convex pentagon
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as

P\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,5\},

with
\dim\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=2
. Let now
\mathcal{T}_{ijk}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{l}\,:\,l=i,j,k\}
and
\tau_{ijk}(p)
be its corresponding triangular barycentric coordinates relative to
p\in\mathcal{T}_{123}\cap\mathcal{P}_{124}\cap\mathcal{P}_{125}
. Thus,
\tau_{123},\tau_{124},\tau_{125}
are, respectively, the unique solutions to

\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&0&1&0\end{matrix}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}\tau=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&1&0&0\end{matrix}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&0&0&1\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}\tau=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&1&0&0\end{matrix}\\
\begin{matrix}0&0&0&1&0\end{matrix}\end{bmatrix}\tau=\begin{bmatrix}p\\
1\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

It is easy to see that
\tau_{123},\tau_{124},\tau_{125}
are linearly independent and
\tau_{124}(p)-\tau_{123}(p),\tau_{125}-\tau_{123}
span
\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}
. Thus, every feasible generalized barycentric coordinates at
p
, say
\lambda(p)
, can be written as

\lambda(p)=\tau_{123}(p)+c(\tau_{124}(p)-\tau_{123}(p))+\overline{c}(\tau_{125}(p)-\tau_{123}(p)),

for some
c,\overline{c}\in\mathbb{R}
, which implies

\lambda(p)=(1-c-\overline{c})\tau_{123}(p)+c\tau_{124}(p)+\overline{c}\tau_{125}(p).

However, since
\lambda(p)\geq 0
component-wise and because of the structure of
\tau_{ijk}(p)
, we get that
c,\overline{c}\in[0,1]
, so that
\lambda(p)
is a convex combination of
\tau_{123},\tau_{234},\tau_{345}
. Therefore
\Lambda(p)=\mathrm{conv}\{\tau_{123},\tau_{234},\tau_{345}\}
, which configures a triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
, thus confirming Theorem
3.4
.

Example 3.7
.

Let us consider a pyramid
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined as

P\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,5\},

with
\dim\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=1
. This case is analogous to the quadrilateral in Example
3.5
, and again Theorem
3.4
holds straightforwardly.

Let us now consider a prism
P
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined as

P\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathrm{conv}\{v_{i}\,:\,i=1,\ldots,8\},

with
\dim\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}=4
. In this case, it is immediate to observe that the barycentric coordinates
\sigma_{ijkl}
relative to the simplices
\mathrm{conv}\{v_{m}\,:\,m=i,j,k,l\}
, with
ijkl\in I\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{1235,1236,1237,1238,1245\}
, span
\ker\begin{bmatrix}V\\
\mathds{1}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}
and thus, again resorting to arguments similar as in Example
3.6
by counting and exploiting the zero structure of such barycentric coordinates, we deduce that
\Lambda(p)=\mathrm{conv}\{\sigma_{ijkl}\,:\,ijkl\in I\}
.

Let us notice that the vertices of
\Lambda(p)
are strictly related to
p\in P
, and thus the set
I(p)
is expected to vary with
p
. In fact, it is a simple observation that if
p\in\partial S_{i}
for some
i=1,\ldots,n
, then there must exist some
j\notin I(p)
such that
p\in\partial S_{j}
: for such a point, then,
\sigma^{i}(p)=\sigma^{j}(p)
.

Such a continuity of
\Lambda(p)
is related to the selection problem, i.e. all the possible sets of generalized barycentric coordinates. Because of (
2.4
), continuity of
\Gamma(p)
and
\Lambda(p)
imply each other, provided that
\tau(p)
in (
2.4
) is smooth enough. Since
\Lambda(p)
does not depend on the particular
\tau(p)
chosen and simplicial barycentric coordinates are always smooth and feasible as long as
p
belongs to their corresponding simplices, we will always assume
\tau(p)
to be as smooth as needed. We are going to further deepen this topic in the next section.

4.
Analytical Properties of
\Lambda(p)
and
\Gamma(p)

In this section we investigate smoothness of the generalized barycentric coordinates maps.

The following results describe some analytical properties of
\Gamma(p)
and
\Lambda(p)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

\Lambda:P\rightrightarrows[0,1]^{n}
is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

Since
P
is closed and
[0,1]^{n}
is compact, we only need to prove that
\mathrm{Graph}(\Lambda)
is closed (see
[
2
]
). Letting
(p,\lambda)\in\overline{\mathrm{Graph}(\Lambda)}
, it follows that there exist
\{p_{k}\}\subseteq P,\{\lambda_{k}\}\subseteq[0,1]
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}p_{k}=p,\,\,\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}=\lambda,

and

\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda(p_{k}),\,\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore
V\lambda_{k}=p_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and so, taking the limit of both sides,
V\lambda=p
, implying
\lambda\in\Lambda(p)
. It then holds that
\mathrm{Graph}(\Lambda)
is closed and thus
\Lambda
is upper semi-continuous.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

\Lambda:P\rightrightarrows[0,1]^{n}
is lower semi-continuous.

Proof.

Let
p\in P
and
\{p_{k}\}\in P
such that
p_{k}\to p
as
k\to\infty
; let also
\lambda\in\Lambda(p)
. Therefore, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\lambda_{k}\in[0,1]^{n}
such that
p_{k}=V\lambda_{k}
and
\mathds{1}^{\top}\lambda_{k}=1
. Thus

\lim_{k\to\infty}V\lambda_{k}=V\lambda,

and so, since
V
is full column rank, it follows from standard arguments that
\{\lambda_{k}\}
converges and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}=\lambda.

Since
\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda(p_{k})
by construction, the claim is proved.
∎

It is a direct consequence of definition that
\Gamma(p)
is also upper and lower semi-continuous. We then have the following.

Corollary 4.3
.

\Lambda:P\rightrightarrows[0,1]^{n}
and
\Gamma:P\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n-d-1}
are continuous set-valued maps.

Proof.

It is a straightforward consequence of Propositions
4.1
,
4.2
and definition of
\Gamma
.
∎

We want to focus now on smoothness features of
\Lambda(p)
. In particular, we are going to prove that it is semidifferentiable in
P
. We recall here some useful definitions for the next results (see
[
14
,
5
]
for further details).

Let
X
be a normed vector space and
F:X\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set-valued map with nonempty images.

Definition 4.4
.

The set-valued map
F:X\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
semidifferentiable
at a point
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in\mathrm{Graph}F
if the limit

DF(\overline{x},\overline{y};h_{0})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{t_{n}\to 0^{+},\,h_{n}\to h_{0}}t_{n}^{-1}\left[F(\overline{x}+t_{n}h_{n})-\overline{y}\right]

exists for all
h_{0}\in X
in the sense of Kuratowski-Painlevé (see
[
2
]
).

Theorem 4.5
.

\Lambda:P\rightrightarrows[0,1]^{n}
is semidifferentiable at every point
(p,\lambda(p))\in\mathrm{Graph}F
where
\lambda(p)
is differentiable.

Proof.

Let
p\in P
and
h\in P
be given. It is known
[
14
]
that in order to prove that
\Lambda
is semidifferentiable we need to prove that
D\Lambda(p,\lambda(p);h)
is nonempty, which requires that there exists
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(4.1)

\liminf_{(t_{n},h_{n})\to(0^{+},h)}t_{n}^{-1}d(\lambda(p)+t_{n}v,\Lambda(p+t_{n}h_{n}))=0,

where
d(a_{0},A)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf_{a\in A}\|a_{0}-a\|
. By definition, (
4.1
) is equivalent to

\liminf_{(t_{n},h_{n})\to(0^{+},h)}t_{n}^{-1}d(\lambda(p)+t_{n}v,\lambda(p+t_{n}h_{n})+N\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n}))=0,

and thus

\liminf_{(t_{n},h_{n})\to(0^{+},h)}t_{n}^{-1}d(\lambda(p)-\lambda(p+t_{n}h_{n})+t_{n}v,N\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n}))=0.

Using homogeneity and continuity of the distance function and since
\lambda(p)
is differentiable, we have to prove that

(4.2)

\liminf_{(t_{n},h_{n})\to(0^{+},h)}d(-J\lambda(p)h_{n}+v,t_{n}^{-1}N\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n}))=0.

Since, from Proposition
3.1
,
\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n})
is a bounded polytope containing the origin for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\in t_{n}^{-1}N\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, for any given
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small we have

B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subseteq t_{n}^{-1}N\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n}).

Let now
J\lambda(p)
be the Jacobian of
\lambda
at
p
. Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}J\lambda(p)h_{n}=J\lambda(p)h,

then there exists
\overline{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq\overline{n}
it holds
\|J\lambda(p)h_{n}-J\lambda(p)h\|\leq\varepsilon
. This implies that

J\lambda(p)h-J\lambda(p)h_{n}\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subseteq t_{n}^{-1}N\Gamma(p+t_{n}h_{n})

for all
n\geq\overline{n}
, providing that (
4.2
) is satisfied for
v\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=J\lambda(p)h
, and this proves the claim.
∎

5.
Conclusions

In this paper we have considered the set-valued map of generalized barycentric coordinates
\Lambda(p)
relative to a
d
-polytope
P
with
n>d
vertices, and we have proved that, for any given point
p\in P
, values of
\Lambda(p)
define a polytope of dimension at most
n-d-1
, whose vertices are represented by
n-d
nonnegative simplicial barycentric coordinates relative to
p
. This result seems to be a dual transform of a convex polytope
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, in the sense that as every point
p\in P
can be described by a convex combination of the
n
vertices of
P
, so each of the barycentric coordinates relative to
p
can be described by a convex combination of
n-d
simplicial barycentric coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Moreover, we have proved that the these maps are continuous and semidifferentiable at points where the corresponding generalized barycentric coordinate is differentiable.

Mingquan Wei ∗, Dunyan Yan

Abstract
. In this paper, by using the rotation method, we calculate that the sharp bound for n n -dimensional Hardy operator ℋ \mathcal{H} on mixed radial-angular spaces. Furthermore, we also obtain the sharp bound for n n -dimensional fractional Hardy operator ℋ β \mathcal{H}_{\beta} from L | x | p ​ L θ p ¯ ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) to L | x | q ​ L θ q ¯ ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) , where 0 < β < n 0<\beta<n , 1 < p , q , p ¯ , q ¯ < ∞ 1<p,q,\bar{p},\bar{q}<\infty and 1 / p − 1 / q = β / n 1/p-1/q=\beta/n . 1.
Introduction

Let
f
be a non-negative integrable function on

\mathbb{R}
. The classical
Hardy operator and its dual operator are defined by

H(f)(x):=\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt,~~~H^{*}(f)(x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t}dt,

respectively, where
x\neq 0
.

As we know, the classical Hardy operator was initially introduced by Hardy [
21
], who showed the following Hardy inequalities:

\|H(f)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}},~~\|H^{*}(f)\|_{L^{p}}\leq p\|f\|_{L^{p}},

where the constants
\frac{p}{p-1},~p
are best possible.

Later, Hardy-type operators were extended to higher dimension by Faris [
11
]. In 1995, Christ and Grafakos [
4
] gave an equivalent version of
n
-dimensional Hardy operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}(f)(x):=\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}|x|^{n}}\int_{|y|<|x|}f(y)dy,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

(1)

where
f
is a non-negative measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Omega_{n}=\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(1+n/2)}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By a direct computation, the dual operator of
{\mathcal{H}}
can be defined by setting, for any locally integrable function
f
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(f)(x):=\int_{|y|\geq|x|}\frac{f(y)}{\Omega_{n}|y|^{n}}dy,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

(2)

By using the rotation method, Christ and Grafakos [
4
] showed that the norms of
\mathcal{H}
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(1<p<\infty)
are also
\frac{p}{p-1}
and
p
. Moreover, the sharp weak estimate for
\mathcal{H}
was obtained by Zhao et al. [
48
] as follows:

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{p,\infty}}\leq 1\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p}},

(3)

where the constant
1
is best possible.

Similarly, for
0<\beta<n
, the
n
-dimensional fractional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
and its dual operator
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\beta}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{\beta}}(f)(x):=\frac{1}{(\Omega^{1/n}_{n}|x|)^{n-\beta}}\int_{|y|<|x|}f(y)dy,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}

(4)

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\beta}}(f)(x):=\int_{|y|\geq|x|}\frac{f(y)}{(\Omega^{1/n}_{n}|y|)^{n-\beta}}dy,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},

(5)

respectively.

In recent years, much attention has been paid to the sharp bounds for Hardy-type operators on different function spaces. For instance, the sharp bounds for Hardy-type operators and their dual operators on weak Lebesgue spaces were considered in [
17
,
18
,
25
,
35
,
40
,
47
,
48
,
49
]. See also [
12
,
13
,
32
,
42
] for sharp constants for multilinear Hardy operator on Lebesgue spaces and Morrey-type spaces. Moreover, Hardy-type inequalities have been extended to different settings. For example, Wu et al. [
44
] and Guo et al. [
19
] considered the sharp constants for Hardy operators and their dual operators on Heisenberg group in the linear and multilinear situations. Fu et al. [
16
,
45
] investigated sharp constants for Hardy-type operators on
p
-adic field. Maligranda et al. [
34
], Guo and Zhao [
20
], Fan and Zhao [
9
] studied Hardy
q
-type integral inequalities. In addition, there are many extensions of the Hardy-type operators, such as weighted Hardy-Littlewood averages [
14
,
15
,
46
] and Hausdorff operators [
3
,
5
,
27
,
43
].
Readers can refer to the book [
22
] to get some earlier development of Hardy-type inequalities. We also refer the readers to the review papers [
26
,
31
] for some recent progress on Hardy-type operators and their related topics.

Recently the mixed radial-angular spaces have been successfully used in studying
Strichartz estimates and partial differential equations to improve the corresponding
results (see [
2
,
6
,
10
,
33
,
41
], etc.). After that, many operators in harmonic analysis have been proved to be bounded on these spaces. For instance, the extrapolation theorems on mixed radial-angular spaces were build by Duoandikoetxea and Oruetxebarria [
8
] to study the boundedness of a large class of operators which are weighted bounded. In addition, the boundedness of some operators with rough kernels on mixed radial-angular spaces were also considered by Liu et al. [
28
,
29
,
30
]. Inspired by the references mentioned above, it is natural to ask whether we can obtain the boundedness, or furthermore the sharp bounds, for Hardy-type operators on mixed radial-angular spaces. In this paper, we will give an affirmative answer. More precisely, we will study the sharp bounds for
n
-dimensional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}
and
n
-dimensional fractional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
on mixed radial-angular spaces. By using a duality argument, we also obtain the sharp constants for their dual operators
\mathcal{H}^{*}
and
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\beta}
on mixed radial-angular spaces. Moreover, the sharp weak type estimate for
\mathcal{H}
is also considered.

Now we recall the definition of mixed radial-angular spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

For
n\geq 2
,
1\leq p,\bar{p}\leq\infty
, the mixed radial-angular space
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all functions
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|f(r,\theta)|^{\bar{p}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
denotes the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
p=\infty
or
\bar{p}=\infty
, then we have to make appropriate modifications.

Similarly, we can define the weak mixed radial-angular spaces.

Definition 1.2
.

For
n\geq 2
,
1\leq p,\bar{p}\leq\infty
, the weak mixed radial-angular space
wL^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all functions
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{wL^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}}:=\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda~\|\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}}<\infty,

where
\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}
.

In fact, the mixed radial-angular spaces can be seen as particular cases of mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces introduced by Benedek and Panzone [
1
]. We refer readers to [
7
,
23
,
24
,
36
,
37
,
39
]
for more studies on mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces and their applications in PDE.

The organization of this article is as follows. The sharp bounds for
n
-dimensional Hardy operator and its dual operator on mixed radial-angular spaces are obtained in Sect. 2. We calculate the operator norms of
n
-dimensional fractional Hardy operator on mixed radial-angular spaces in Sect. 3. In addition, we also establish a sharp weak type estimate for
n
-dimensional Hardy operator on mixed radial-angular spaces Sect. 4.

2.
Sharp bounds for
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{*}
from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

The main result in this section is the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1<p,\bar{p_{1}},\bar{p_{2}}<\infty
. Then the
n
-dimensional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}
defined in (
1
) is bounded from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}\rightarrow L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=\frac{p}{p-1}w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}},

where
w_{n}=2\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2)
is the induced measure of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Proof.

We borrow some ideas from [
4
,
13
] and use the method of rotation. Set

g(x)=\frac{1}{w_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(|x|\theta)d\theta,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(6)

Obviously,
g
is a radial function and it was proved by Fu et al [
13
, Proof of Theorem 1] that
\mathcal{H}(g)
is equal to
\mathcal{H}(f)
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|g(r,\theta)|^{\bar{p}_{1}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}_{1}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{p}_{1}}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(r)|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p},

where
g(r)
can be recognized as

g(r)=g(x)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x|=r
, since
g
is a radial function.

Combining (
6
) and (
7
), and using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{p}_{1}}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{w_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(r\theta)d\theta\right|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{p}_{1}-1}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(r\theta)d\theta\right|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{p}_{1}-1}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|f(r\theta)|^{\bar{p}_{1}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}_{1}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}^{\prime}_{1}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|f(r\theta)|^{\bar{p}_{1}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}_{1}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}=\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}.

Therefore we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}}{\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}}\leq\frac{\|\mathcal{H}(g)\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}}{\|g\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}}.

That is to say, the operator
\mathcal{H}
and its restriction to radial functions have the same
operator norm from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, we can assume that
f
is a radial function.

For a radial function
f
,
\mathcal{H}(f)
is also a radial function. Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\mathcal{H}(f)(r,\theta)|^{\bar{p}_{2}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}_{2}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\mathcal{H}(f)(r)|^{\bar{p}_{2}}d\theta\right)^{p/\bar{p}_{2}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{H}(f)(r)|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p},

where
\mathcal{H}(f)(r)
can be recognized as

\mathcal{H}(f)(r)=\mathcal{H}(f)(x)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x|=r
, since
\mathcal{H}(f)
is a radial function.

Denote
B(0,R)
by the ball centered at the origin with radius
R
. By changing variables, we further have

\mathcal{H}(f)(r)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}}\int_{B(0,1)}f(ry)dy.

(9)

Combining (
8
) with (
9
), and usiing Minkowski’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f)\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{B(0,1)}f(ry)dy\right|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\int_{B(0,1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(ry)|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\int_{B(0,1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(r|y|)|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}}{\Omega_{n}}\int_{B(0,1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}_{1}}|f(r)|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}|y|^{-n/p}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}}{\Omega_{n}}\int_{B(0,1)}|y|^{-n/p}dy\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}\frac{w_{n}}{\Omega_{n}}\frac{p}{n(p-1)}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{p-1}w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}},

where we have used the identity
w_{n}=n\Omega_{n}
.

To prove the constant
\frac{p}{p-1}w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}
is best possible, we take

\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&|x|\leq 1,\\
|x|^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)},&|x|>1,\end{array}\right.

where
0<\epsilon<1
.
A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\|f_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}=\frac{w^{1/\bar{p}_{1}}_{n}}{(p\epsilon)^{1/p}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(f_{\epsilon})(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&|x|\leq 1,\\
\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}}|x|^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}\int_{\frac{1}{|x|}<|y|<1}|y|^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}dy,&|x|>1.\end{array}\right.

So we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f_{\epsilon})\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|r^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}\int_{\frac{1}{r}<|y|<1}|y|^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}dy\right|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\left(\int_{1/{\epsilon}}^{\infty}\left|r^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}\int_{\epsilon<|y|<1}|y|^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}dy\right|^{p}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\left(\int_{1/\epsilon}^{\infty}r^{-p\epsilon-1}dr\right)^{1/p}\int_{\epsilon<|y|<1}|y|^{-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1+1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\left(\int_{1/\epsilon}^{\infty}r^{-p\epsilon-1}dr\right)^{1/p}\int_{\epsilon}^{1}r^{n-1-(\frac{n}{p}+\epsilon)}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1+1/\bar{p}_{2}}}{\Omega_{n}}\frac{\epsilon^{\epsilon}}{(p\epsilon)^{1/p}}\frac{1-\epsilon^{n-\epsilon-n/p}}{n-\epsilon-n/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\epsilon^{\epsilon}\frac{1-\epsilon^{n-\epsilon-n/p}}{n-\epsilon-n/p}\times w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}\times\|f_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}

As a consequence, we obtain

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}\rightarrow L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}\geq n\epsilon^{\epsilon}\frac{1-\epsilon^{n-\epsilon-n/p}}{n-\epsilon-n/p}\times w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}.

Letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, and using
\epsilon^{\epsilon}\rightarrow 1
, it yields

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}\rightarrow L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}\geq\frac{p}{p-1}w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

When
p=\bar{p}_{1}=\bar{p}_{2}\in(1,\infty)
in Theorem
2.1
, we recover the results in [
4
,
13
].

A standard argument yields that for
1<p,\bar{p}<\infty
, the dual space of
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
L^{p^{\prime}}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
p^{\prime}
and
\bar{p}^{\prime}
satisfy
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
and
1/\bar{p}+1/\bar{p}^{\prime}=1
(see [
1
]), and furthermore, for any
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}^{\prime}}}=\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)g(x)dx.

This observation enables us to obtain the sharp bound for
\mathcal{H}^{*}
from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by using duality.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1<p,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}<\infty
. Then the operator
\mathcal{H}^{*}
defined in (
2
) is bounded from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{H}^{*}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}\rightarrow L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=pw_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}.

3.
Sharp bound for
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

To state the main result in this section, we need the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
0<\beta<n
and
1<p<q<\infty
such that
1/p-1/q=\beta/n
. Then the
n
-dimensional fractional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
defined in (
4
) is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}=C_{p,q,n,\beta},

where
C_{p,q,n,\beta}=\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}\right)^{1/q}\left(\frac{n}{q\beta}\cdot B\left(\frac{n}{q\beta},\frac{n}{q^{\prime}\beta}\right)\right)^{-\beta/n}
and
B
is the Beta function, i.e.,
B(z_{1},z_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}t^{z_{1}-1}(1-t)^{z_{2}-1}dt
for any complex numbers
z_{1}
and
z_{1}
with the positive real parts.

Our main result in this section can be stated as follws:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
0<\beta<n
,
1<\bar{p},\bar{q}<\infty
and
1<p<q<\infty
such that
1/p-1/q=\beta/n
. Then the
n
-dimensional fractional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
defined in (
4
) is bounded from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}\rightarrow L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}}=C_{p,q,n,\beta}w_{n}^{1/\bar{q}-1/\bar{p}+\beta/n}.

Proof.

A similar process as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
yields that the norm of the operator
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equal to the norm of
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}

acting on radial functions.

For a radial function
f
,
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f)
is also a radial function. Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f)(r,\theta)|^{\bar{q}}d\theta\right)^{q/\bar{q}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{q}}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f)(r,\theta)|^{q}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{q}-1/q}_{n}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f)(r,\theta)|^{q}r^{n-1}d\theta dr\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{q}-1/q}_{n}\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w^{1/\bar{q}-1/q}_{n}\times C_{p,q,n,\beta}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p,q,n,\beta}\cdot w^{1/\bar{q}-1/q}_{n}\cdot w^{1/p-1/\bar{p}}_{n}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{p,q,n,\beta}w_{n}^{1/\bar{q}-1/\bar{p}+\beta/n}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}},

where we have used Lemma
3.1
in the inequality and
1/p-1/q=\beta/n
in the last equality.

To get the sharp bound, we take

f_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{(1+|x|^{q\beta})^{1+\frac{n}{q\beta}}}.

Since
f_{0}
is a radial function, we have

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}}=w^{1/\bar{p}-1/{p}}_{n}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}}.

(12)

Similarly, noting that
\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f_{0})
is also a radial function, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f_{0})\|_{L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}}=w^{1/\bar{q}-1/{q}}_{n}\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f_{0})\|_{L^{q}}.

(13)

Due to Zhao and Lu [
49
], there holds

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f_{0})\|_{L^{q}}=C_{p,q,n,\beta}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}}.

(14)

Combining (
12
), (
13
) with (
14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{\beta}(f_{0})\|_{L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}}=C_{p,q,n,\beta}w_{n}^{1/\bar{q}-1/\bar{p}+\beta/n}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}},

since
1/p-1/q=\beta/n
.

The proof is finished.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Similar to Theorem
2.2
, one can also calculate the sharp bound for
\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\beta}
defined in (

) from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by duality. We omit the details here. In particular, if
p=\bar{p}\in(1,\infty)
and
q=\bar{q}\in(1,\infty)
in Theorem
3.1
, we recover the results in [

,
49

4.
Sharp weak bound for
\mathcal{H}
from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
wL^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

This section considers the sharp weak type estimate for
n
-dimensional Hardy operator on mixed radial-angular spaces. Our main result can be read as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1\leq p,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}\leq\infty
. Then the
n
-dimensional Hardy operator
\mathcal{H}
defined in (
1
) is bounded from
L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
wL^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}\rightarrow wL^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}.

Proof.

We only give the proof of the case
1<p,\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}<\infty
, with the usual modifications made
when
p=1,\bar{p}_{i}=1
or
p=\infty,\bar{p}_{i}=\infty
,
i=1,2
. For any
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\mathcal{H}(f)(x)|>\lambda\}}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}|x|^{n}}\int_{|y|<|x|}f(y)dy\right|>\lambda\right\}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{1}{\Omega_{n}|x|^{n}}\|f\chi_{\{|y|<|x|\}}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}\|\chi_{\{|y|<|x|\}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}^{\prime}_{1}}}>\lambda\right\}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}^{\prime}_{1}-1/p^{\prime}}}{\Omega_{n}^{1/p}|x|^{n/p}}\|f\chi_{\{|y|<|x|\}}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}>\lambda\right\}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|^{n}<\frac{w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}^{\prime}_{1}-p/p^{\prime}}}{\lambda^{p}\Omega_{n}}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}\right\}}\right\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}^{\prime}_{1}-p/p^{\prime}}}{\lambda^{p}\Omega_{n}}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}\right)^{1/n}}r^{n-1}dr\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}}{\lambda}\times\|f\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}},

where we have used Hölder’s inequality on mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces, see [
1
].

On the other hand, we need to show that the constant
w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}
is the best possible. Denote by

\chi_{r}=\chi_{[0,r]},\,r>0
. Taking
f_{0}(x)=\chi_{r}(|x|),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a simple calculation shows that

\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}=\frac{w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{1}}}{n^{1/p}}r^{n/p}.

Zhao et al. [
48
, Proof of Theorem 2.1] proved that for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathcal{H}(f)(x)\leq 1
.
Therefore, for
0<\lambda\leq 1
, we divide
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
into two cases.

(i) When
|x|<r
, it was showed by Zhao et al. [
48
, Proof of Theorem 2.1] that
\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)=1
. As a consequence, we have

\|\chi_{\{|x|<r:|\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)|>\lambda\}}\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}_{2}}\frac{r^{n}}{n}.

(ii) When
|x|\geq r
, it was also showed by Zhao et al. [
48
, Proof of Theorem 2.1] that
\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)=r^{n}/|x|^{n}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\{|x|\geq r:|\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)|>\lambda\}}\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=\left\|\chi_{\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r\leq|x|<\frac{r}{\lambda^{1/n}}\right\}}\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}_{2}}\frac{r^{n}}{n}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-1\right).

From the above results, we have the following estimate,

\|\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)|>\lambda\}}\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}=\frac{r^{n}w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}_{2}}}{n\lambda}=\frac{w_{n}^{p/\bar{p}_{2}-p/\bar{p}_{1}}}{\lambda}\|f_{0}\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}},

which yields that for
1<p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}(f_{0})\|_{wL^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\|\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)|>\lambda\}}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{0<\lambda\leq 1}\lambda\|\chi_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\mathcal{H}(f_{0})(x)|>\lambda\}}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}\sup_{0<\lambda\leq 1}\lambda^{1-1/p}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n}^{1/\bar{p}_{2}-1/\bar{p}_{1}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{|x|}L_{\theta}^{\bar{p}_{1}}}.

Therefore, we complete the proof.
∎

Obviously, Theorem
4.1
improves the result of (
3
).

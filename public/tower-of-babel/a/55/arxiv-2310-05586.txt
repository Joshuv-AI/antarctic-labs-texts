Abstract
. We solve the contact equivalence problem for generalised sub-Laplacians on ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} and show that the family of sub-Laplacians on ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} modulo contact equivalence, is parameterised by ℝ + \mathbb{R}^{+} .

1.
Introduction

Each positive definite
A\in GL(\mathbb{R}^{n})
determines the second order operator

\Delta_{A}=\nabla\cdot A\nabla=\sum_{ij}a_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{i}}.

Since
A
is positive definite, it has a symmetric square root
C
, which is to say
C
is symmetric and
C^{2}=A^{-1}
. If
f(x)=Cx
, then

\Delta_{A}(u\circ f)=(\Delta u)\circ f.

Let
G
be a connected, simply connected, stratified nilpotent Lie group, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{V}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{V}_{s}
, where
\mathbb{V}_{i+1}=[\mathbb{V}_{1},\mathbb{V}_{i}]
and
[\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{V}_{s}]=0
. Let
d_{i}={\rm dim}\,\mathbb{V}_{i}
and
N={\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{g}
. If
e_{i}
denotes an element of the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then we choose
\mathbb{V}_{1}={\rm span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{d_{1}}\}
,
\mathbb{V}_{2}={\rm span}\{e_{d_{1}+1},\dots,e_{d_{2}}\}
and so on. The left invariant vector fields are framed by the set
\mathscr{F}=\{\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{N}\}
where

\tilde{X}_{i}u(p)=\frac{d}{dt}u(p\exp(te_{i}))|_{t=0}

and the horizontal bundle, denoted by
\mathscr{H}
, is the subbundle of
TG
framed by
\{\tilde{X}_{1}\dots,\tilde{X}_{d_{1}}\}
. The quadratic form on
\mathscr{H}
which makes
\{\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d_{1}}\}
orthonormal is left invariant and denoted by
g_{0}
. If
\tilde{V}
,
\tilde{W}
are
\mathscr{H}
valued left invariant vector fields, with coefficients
v_{i}
and
w_{i}
relative to
\mathscr{F}
, then we have that

g_{0}(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d_{1}})\cdot(w_{1},\dots,w_{d_{1}}).

A local diffeomorphism
F:U\subset G\to G
, where
U\subset G
is open, which preserves the horizontal bundle in the sense that
dF_{p}(\mathscr{H}_{p})=\mathscr{H}_{F(p)}
, will be referred to as a contact mapping, despite the fact that the distributions are not necessarily associated with contact structures.

The horizontal gradient of a real valued function
u
on
G
is define to be the
\mathscr{H}
-valued vector field

{\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}u=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}(\tilde{X}_{i}u)\tilde{X}_{i}.

In coordinates we have
{\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}=(\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{d_{1}})
and the canonical sub-Laplacian is defined to be

\mathcal{L}u={\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}\cdot{\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}u=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}\tilde{X}_{i}^{2}u.

Given a positive-definite symmetric matrix
M=(m_{ij})\in GL(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
we define a generalised sub-Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{M}
by setting

\mathcal{L}_{M}u={\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}\cdot M{\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}u=\sum_{ij=1}^{d_{1}}m_{ij}\tilde{X}_{i}\tilde{X}_{j}u.

One can then ask if there exists a local contact diffeomorphism
f:G\to G
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}(u\circ f)=\mathcal{L}u\circ f.

For free groups, all sub-Laplacians are equivalent, as shown in
[
1
]
(Theorem 16.1.2 page 625) where matters are simplified by the fact that any element of
GL(\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}})
extends to a Lie algebra automorphism. The first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is free, and so
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is perhaps the simplest case not covered by the results in
[
1
]
. In this study, we deal with the equivalence problem for sub-Laplacians by applying the theory of jet spaces over stratified groups, see
[
4
]
.

2.
Horizontal jets over stratified groups

The horizontal Taylor expansion of a smooth real valued function
u
at a point
p\in G
, is based on the usual Taylor expansion around zero of the function
h(t)=u(p\exp(tV))
when
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
. If
V=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}v_{i}e_{i}
and

\tilde{V}^{k}u(p)=\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}u(p\exp(tV))|_{t=0}

then the
m
-th order Taylor polynomial of
h
around zero, denoted
T^{m}_{0}h
, has the form

\displaystyle T^{m}_{0}h(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\tilde{V}^{k}u(p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\,\sum_{i_{1},\dots i_{k}=1}^{d_{1}}v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{k}}\tilde{X}_{i_{1}}\dots\tilde{X}_{i_{k}}u(p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

Setting
t=1
determines the horizontal Taylor polynomial
T^{m}_{p}u
of order
m
, defined by

\displaystyle T^{m}_{p}u(V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\tilde{V}^{k}u(p)

(2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{d_{1}}\,\,\,\tilde{X}_{i_{1}}\dots\tilde{X}_{i_{k}}u(p)\,v_{i_{1}}\dots v_{i_{k}},

which approximates
V\to u(p\exp(V))
,
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
.

The
k
-th term of
T^{m}_{p}u
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
, and can be expressed in "polar" type form, by which we mean
T^{m}_{p}u(V)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}A^{k}_{u,p}(V,\dots,V)
, where for
k>0
,
A^{k}_{u,p}
is the
k
-linear map defined on
\mathbb{V}_{1}^{k}
by

(3)

\displaystyle A^{k}_{u,p}\left({{V}}_{1},\dots,{{V}}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}_{k}\dots\tilde{V}_{1}u(p)

and
A^{0}_{u,p}=u(p)
.
The horizontal
m
-jet of
u
, denoted
j^{m}_{p}u
, is the formal sum

j^{m}_{p}u=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}A^{k}_{u,p}.

The horizontal
m
-jet of a vector valued function
u:G\to\mathbb{W}
is then given by
(j^{m}_{p}u_{1},\dots,j^{m}_{p}u_{N})
where
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
are the coordinate functions relative to a basis of the target vector space
\mathbb{W}
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
the vector subspace of
\mathtt{Lin}^{k}(\mathbb{V}_{1};\mathbb{W})
consisting of all
k
-multilinear maps of the form
A^{k}_{u,p}
.
For
k=0
, we set
\mathtt{HD}^{0}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})=\mathbb{W}
. For each positive integer
m
, we define

\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m}\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W}).\quad(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}\text{ for short})

The
m
-th order horizontal jet space, denoted by
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G,\mathbb{W})
, is the vector bundle
G\times\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}\to G
.

The Lie group structure on
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G,\mathbb{W})
is given by an anti-semidirect product which we now briefly review. If
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
are Lie algebras, then an antimorphism
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{h})
is a linear map with the property that

\psi([x,y])=-[\psi(x),\psi(y)]).

An antimorphism induces a Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{h}
by the formula

[(x,X),(y,Y)]=([x,y],-[X,Y]+\psi(y)X-\psi(x)Y).

We denote this Lie algebra by
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\psi}\mathfrak{h}
(anti-semidirect product).

Let
G
and
H
be the connected simply connected Lie groups corresponding to

\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
. The antimorphism
\psi
induces an antimorphism
\phi:G\to{\rm Aut}(H)
, by which we mean

\displaystyle\phi(ab)

\displaystyle=\phi(b)\phi(a)

\displaystyle d\phi|_{\exp(x)}

\displaystyle=e^{\psi(x)}\in{\rm Aut}(\mathfrak{h})\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

The set
G\times H
and the product given by

(a,A)(b,B)=(ab,B\phi(b)A)

define a Lie group
G\ltimes_{\phi}H
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\psi}\mathfrak{h}
. When
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian, the algebra and group products simplify to the following:

\displaystyle[(x,X),(y,Y)]

\displaystyle=([x,y],\psi(y)X-\psi(x)Y),

\displaystyle(a,A)(b,B)

\displaystyle=(ab,B+\phi(b)A).

If
(a,A)\in G\ltimes_{\phi}H
and
(x,X)\in\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\psi}\mathfrak{h}
, then the left-invariant vector field generated by
(x,X)

evaluated at
(a,A)\in G\ltimes_{\phi}H
is

\widetilde{(x,X)}(a,A)=(\tilde{x}(a),\psi(x)A+X)

where
\tilde{x}(a)
is the left-invariant vector field generated by
x
evaluated at
a
.

If
k\geq 2
and
A\in\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
, then for
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
, the right contraction of
A
by
V
, denoted
V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A
, is the element in
\mathtt{HD}^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
given by

V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A(V_{1},\dots,V_{k-1})=\,A(V_{1},\dots,V_{k-1},V).

Moreover, if
k=1
then
V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A=A(V)
, and if
k=0
then
V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A=0
.

Given
V\in\mathbb{V}_{1}
, we extend the definition of the right contraction by
V
to a map

V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}:\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}\to\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}
,
where for each
A\in\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}
, we set

(V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A)^{k}=V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{k+1}
for
k<m
and
(V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A)^{m}=0
. The map
\mathbb{V}_{1}\to{\rm End}(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m})
,
V\mapsto V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}
, extends uniquely (by formula (
3
)) to a Lie algebra anti-morphism
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to{\rm End}(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m})
, i.e.,

(4)

\displaystyle[V,W]\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}=-[V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}},W\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}].

Since
\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}
is an abelian Lie algebra,
{\rm Der}(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m})={\rm End}(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m})
. Hence,
\psi(V)=V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}
is a Lie algebra antimorphism from
\mathfrak{g}
to
{\rm Der}(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m})
. We obtain a Lie algebra

\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W}):=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\psi}\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m},

where

(5)

\displaystyle[(V,A),(W,B)]=([V,W],W\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A-V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}B).

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
is stratified of step
\max\{s,m+1\}
, where
s
is the step of
\mathfrak{g}
.
The
k
-th layer of
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
is

\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})_{k}:=\mathbb{V}_{k}\times\mathtt{HD}^{m+1-k}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})

where
\mathbb{V}_{k}=\{0\}
if
k>s
and
\mathtt{HD}^{m+1-k}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})=\{0\}
if
k>m+1
.

The simply connected Lie group corresponding to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
is the semidirect product
G\ltimes\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})

with group law

(\exp(V),A)(\exp(W),B)=(\exp(V)\exp(W),B+e^{(W\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}})}A).

Hence
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G,\mathbb{W})
together with the afore mentioned product is a stratified Lie group withe stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{W})
. The underlying manifold structure of
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G,\mathbb{W})
is that of the vector bundle
G\times\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}\to G
.

The equivalence problem defined by (
1
) leads to an equivalence by contact maps of two stratified subgroups of the second horizontal jet space over
G=\mathbb{H}^{2}
. The subgroups are submanifolds
\mathtt{J}\mathcal{L},\mathtt{J}\mathcal{L}_{M}\subset\mathtt{J}^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{2},\mathbb{R})
where
\mathtt{J}\mathcal{L}
consists of
2
-jets of
\mathcal{L}
-harmonic functions and
\mathtt{J}\mathcal{L}_{c}
consists of
2
-jets of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
-harmonic functions, see (
10
) for the definition of
\mathcal{L}_{c}
.

Thus it needs to be established that a contact map of an open set
\Omega\subseteq G
can be prolonged to a contact map of and open subset of the second horizontal jet space. To this end we show directly that we can prolong from
G
to
\mathtt{J}^{1}(G,\mathbb{W})
and then invoke Theorem 5.2 in
[
4
]
to get a prolongation to
\mathtt{J}^{2}(G,\mathbb{W})
.

When
m=0
we have
\mathtt{J}^{0}(G,\mathbb{W})=G\times\mathbb{W}
where
(a,A)(b,B)=(ab,A+B)
and
\mathscr{H}
is given by the left translation of
\mathbb{V}_{1}\times\mathbb{W}
. A contact map
f:\Omega\subseteq G\to G
lifts to a contact map
F
on
\Omega\times\mathbb{W}
by setting
F=(f\circ\pi_{G},\pi_{\mathbb{W}})
.
Indeed if
(a,A)\in\mathtt{J}^{0}(G,\mathbb{W})=G\times\mathbb{W}
and
(V,B)\in\mathfrak{j}^{0}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{W})=\mathbb{V}_{1}\times\mathbb{W}
, then

\gamma(t)=(a,A)(\exp(tV),tB)=(a\exp(tV),A+tB)

represents a horizontal tangent at
(a,A)
. Moreover

\displaystyle F(a,A)^{-1}F\circ\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=(f(a)^{-1}f(a\exp(tV)),tB)

represents a horizontal tangent at the identity since

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F(a,A)^{-1}F\circ\gamma(t)|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{d}{dt}f(a)^{-1}f(a\exp(tV))|_{t=0},B\big)\in\mathbb{V}_{1}\times\mathbb{W}

where
f
being a contact map implies

(6)

\displaystyle N_{f,a}(V):=\frac{d}{dt}f(a)^{-1}f(a\exp(tV))|_{t=0}\in\mathbb{V}_{1}.

When
m=1
we have
\mathtt{J}^{1}(G,\mathbb{W})=G\times\mathtt{HD}^{\leq 1}
where

\displaystyle(a,A)(b,B)

\displaystyle=(ab,B+A+\log(b)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{1})

and
\mathscr{H}
is given by the left translation of
\mathbb{V}_{1}\times\mathtt{HD}^{1}
. Moreover,

(b,B)^{-1}=(b^{-1},\log(b)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}B^{1}-B)

If
\pi_{0}:\mathtt{J}^{1}(G,\mathbb{W})\to\mathtt{J}^{0}(G,\mathbb{W})
is the natural projection, then a contact map
\hat{F}
of
\tilde{U}\subseteq\mathtt{J}^{1}(G,\mathbb{W})
is a prolongation of a contact map
F
of
\mathtt{J}^{0}(G,\mathbb{W})
if
\pi_{0}\circ\hat{F}=F\circ\pi_{0}
, which when we write
\hat{F}=(\hat{F}_{G},\hat{F}^{0}+\hat{F}^{1})
, implies that
\hat{F}_{G}=F_{G}\circ\pi_{0}
and
\hat{F}^{0}=F^{0}\circ\pi_{0}
. If we assume
F
is a prolongation, then
F_{G}=f\circ\pi_{G}\circ\pi_{0}
and
\hat{F}^{0}=\pi_{\mathbb{W}}\circ\pi_{0}
, in summary

\hat{F}(a,A)=(f(a),A^{0}+\hat{F}^{1}(a,A))

and

\hat{F}(a,A)^{-1}=(f(a)^{-1},\log(f(a))\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\hat{F}^{1}(a,A)-A^{0}-\hat{F}^{1}(a,A))

If
(a,A)\in\mathtt{J}^{1}(G,\mathbb{W})=G\times\mathtt{HD}^{\leq 1}
and
(V,B)\in\mathfrak{j}^{1}(\mathbb{V}_{1},\mathtt{HD}^{1})=\mathbb{V}_{1}\times\mathtt{HD}^{1}
, then

\gamma(t)=(a,A)(\exp(tV),tB)=(a\exp(tV),A+t(B+V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A))

represents a horizontal tangent at
(a,A)
. It follows that

\displaystyle\hat{F}_{G}\circ\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=f(a\exp(tV))

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{0}\circ\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=A^{0}+t\,V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{1}

\displaystyle\hat{F}^{1}\circ\gamma(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{F}^{1}\big(a\exp(tV),A+t(B+V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A)\big)

and

(1)

\displaystyle\pi_{G}\big(\hat{F}(a,A)^{-1}\hat{F}\circ\gamma(t)\big)=f(a)^{-1}f(a\exp(tV))

(2)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathtt{HD}^{0}}\big(\hat{F}(a,A)^{-1}\hat{F}\circ\gamma(t)\big)=-\log\big(f(a)^{-1}f(a\exp(tV))\big)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\hat{F}^{1}(a,A)+tV\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{1}

(3)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathtt{HD}^{1}}\big(\hat{F}(a,A)^{-1}\hat{F}\circ\gamma(t)\big)=\hat{F}^{1}\circ\gamma(t)-\hat{F}^{1}(a,A).

Note that in item (2) we use the fact that if
a=\exp(V)
and
b=\exp(W)
then

\log(a^{-1}b)=\log(a)-\log(b)+H(V,W)

where
H
is an expression involving brackets of order greater than one which implies

\log(a)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\hat{F}^{1}(a,A)-\log(b)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\hat{F}^{1}(a,A)=-\log\big(a^{-1}b\big)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\hat{F}^{1}(a,A).

If
\hat{F}
is to be a contact map, then we require that the expression on the right in item (2) vanishes which implies

(7)

\displaystyle V\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{1}=N_{f,a}(V)\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\hat{F}^{1}(a,A).

Since
f
is a contact map, the set
\{N_{f,a}(e_{i}):i=1\dots d_{1}\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{V}_{1}
and
N_{f,a}
is an invertible linear map of
\mathbb{V}_{1}
to itself. It now follows from (
7
) that

\hat{F}^{1}(a,A)=A^{1}\circ N_{f,a}^{-1}.

If

\eta(g)=A^{1}\circ N_{f,a}^{-1}\circ\log\circ L_{f(a)}^{-1}(g)+A^{0}

then
\eta(f(a))=A^{0}
and

\tilde{V}\eta(f(a))=A^{1}\circ N_{f,a}^{-1}(V),

hence if follows that

\hat{F}^{0}(a,A)+\hat{F}^{1}(a,A)=A_{\eta,f(a)}^{0}+A_{\eta,f(a)}^{1}

is an element of
\mathtt{HD}^{\leq 1}
. The same construction starting with
f^{-1}
, then prolonging to
F^{-1}
, and then prolonging to
\hat{F}^{-1}
yields the inverse of
\hat{F}
and so
\hat{F}
is a diffeomorphism.

When
m=2
, we apply Theorem 5.2 in
[
4
]
, see Remark 5.4.

3.
Heisenberg Groups

Following
[
7
]
, the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, where we denote coordinates by
(x,y,t)
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and the product is given by

(x,y,t)(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}-2x\cdot y^{\prime}+2y\cdot x^{\prime})).

If
\{e_{i}:i=1,\dots,2n+1\}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
denotes the Lie algebra where the nontrivial Lie brackets are
[e_{i},e_{n+i}]=-4e_{2n+1}
,
i=1,\dots,n
, then the product above is the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff model of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
built on
\mathfrak{h}^{n}
.

The left invariant vector fields are framed by

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+2y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\quad i=1,\dots,n

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}-2x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\quad i=1,\dots,n

\displaystyle\tilde{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

for which the only nontrivial brackets are

[\tilde{X}_{i},\tilde{Y}_{i}]=-4\tilde{T}\quad i=1,\dots,n.

The frame
\{dx_{i},dy_{i},\theta\}
, where
\theta=dt+2\sum_{i}(x_{i}dy_{i}-y_{i}dx_{i})
, is the dual of the frame
\{\tilde{X}_{i},\tilde{Y}_{i},\tilde{T}\}
and
{\rm ker\,}\theta=\mathscr{H}
. In particular,
\theta
is a contact form since

(d\theta)^{n}\wedge\theta=2^{2n}dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}\wedge dy_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dy_{n}\wedge dt.

The left invariant
2
-form

B=\frac{1}{4}d\theta=\sum_{i}dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}

is symplectic on
\mathscr{H}
and represented by the matrix

\Omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}

relaltive to the chosen coordinates. Furthermore, Cartan’s formula implies that

[\tilde{V},\tilde{W}]=-4B(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})\tilde{T}

for all left invariant horizontal vector fields
\tilde{V}
and
\tilde{W}
. The complex structure
J_{0}=\Omega^{T}
is compatible with
B
and
g_{0}
, that is to say

g_{0}(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})=B(\tilde{V},J_{0}\tilde{W})

for all left invariant horizontal vector fields
\tilde{V}
and
\tilde{W}
.

A local diffeomorphism
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
preserves
\mathscr{H}
if and only
f^{*}\theta=f^{*}\theta(\tilde{T})\theta
and is therefore a contact transformation in the strict sense. Left translations are contact transformations.

An automorphism
\alpha
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
must satisfy
d\alpha(\tilde{T})=\lambda\tilde{T}
since
\tilde{T}
is central, and is therefore a contact transformation. Two fundamental examples are dilation by
\lambda>0

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda^{2}t)

and the reflection

(8)

\displaystyle r(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=(x,-y,-t).

Consequently, every automorphism of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a composition
\alpha\circ\delta_{\lambda}\circ r
where
\alpha
is normalised, that is to say,
d\alpha(\tilde{T})=\tilde{T}
. Furthermore, a normalised automorphism
\alpha
satisfies

B(d\alpha(\tilde{V}),d\alpha(\tilde{V}))=B(\tilde{V},\tilde{W})

for all left invariant horizontal vector fields
\tilde{V}
and
\tilde{W}
, and is thus a symplectic transformation represented in the chosen coordinates by an element of
\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
. It follows that the group of grading preserving automorphism of the Lie algebra is

\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(\operatorname{Sp}(n,\mathbb{R})\cup dr(\operatorname{Sp}(n,\mathbb{R})))

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}
acts by
d\delta_{\lambda}
. The group of "horizontal orientation" preserving automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times{\rm Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
and the action is this case is given by the representation

(\lambda,S)\to\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\lambda}S&0\\
0&\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

Williamson’s Theorem (Theorem 8.3.1, page 244,
[
3
]
) gives the symplectic version of the well known diagonalisation of a positive-definite symmetric matrix by the orthogonal group.

Theorem 3.1

(Williamson)
.

Let
M
be a positive-definite symmetric real
2n\times 2n
matrix. Then
there exists
S\in\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbb{R})
such that

S^{T}MS=\Lambda_{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda&0\\
0&\Lambda\end{pmatrix}

where
\Lambda={\rm diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
and
\pm i\lambda_{j}
are the eigenvalues of
\,-\Omega M^{-1}
.

The Williamson diagonal form gives the following factorisation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}u

\displaystyle=S^{-1}{\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}\cdot\Lambda_{M}S^{-1}{\nabla}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}.

Furthermore, we can dilate by
\lambda_{1}^{-1}
and conclude that for any positive-definite symmetric real
2n\times 2n
matrix, we can change coordinates by a Lie group automorphism
\alpha
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that the frame given by
\bar{X}_{i}=d\alpha(\tilde{X}_{i})
,
\bar{Y}_{i}=d\alpha(\tilde{Y}_{i})
and
\bar{T}=d\alpha(\tilde{T})
, is left invariant and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}(u\circ\alpha)

\displaystyle=({\bar{\nabla}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}\cdot\Lambda_{M}(1,\lambda_{2}/\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}/\lambda_{1}){\bar{\nabla}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}u)\circ\alpha

(9)

\displaystyle=(\bar{X}_{1}^{2}u)\circ\alpha+(\bar{Y}_{1}^{2}u)\circ\alpha+\sum_{i=2}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}(\bar{X}_{i}^{2}u)\circ\alpha+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{1}}(\bar{Y}_{i}^{2}u)\circ\alpha

where
{\bar{\nabla}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{H}}
is the horizontal gradient operator relative to the frame
\{\bar{X}_{i},\bar{Y}_{i}\}
.

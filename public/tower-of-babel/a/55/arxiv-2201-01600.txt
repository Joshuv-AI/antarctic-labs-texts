Maximal estimates for fractional Schrödinger equations in scaling critical magnetic fields
Haoran Wang, Jiye Yuan

Abstract
. In this paper, we combine the argument of [ 12 ] and [ 27 ] to prove the maximal estimates for fractional Schrödinger equations ( i ∂ t + ℒ 𝐀 α 2 ) u = 0 (i\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})u=0 in the purely magnetic fields which includes the Aharonov-Bohm fields. The proof is based on the cluster spectral measure estimates. In particular α = 1 \alpha=1 , the maximal estimate for wave equation is sharp up to the endpoint.



Key Words:
Fractional Schrödinger equation; Maximal estimate; Aharonov-Bohm potential; Spectral measure.

AMS Classification:

42B25, 35Q40, 35Q41.

1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem of fractional Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&(i\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\\
&u(0,x)=f(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\alpha
is a positive real number and the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
is the magnetic Schrödinger operator given by

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}=\Big(i\nabla+\frac{\mathbf{A}(\hat{x})}{|x|}\Big)^{2},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\quad\hat{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}\in\mathbb{S}^{1},

where
\mathbf{A}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfies the transversality condition

{\mathbf{A}}(\hat{x})\cdot\hat{x}=0,\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.2)

In particular, a typical example of
{\bf A}
is known as the
Aharonov-Bohm
potential

{\bf A}(\hat{x})=\sigma\Big(-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|}\Big),\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{R},

(1.3)

which was initially used to study one of the most interesting and intriguing effect of quantum physics by Aharonov and Bohm in
[
1
]
. This Aharonov-Bohm effect occurs when electrons propagate in a domain with a zero magnetic field but with a nonzero vector potential, see
[
23
]
and the references therein.
In another physic effect, the fermionic charges can be non-integer multiples of the Higgs charges in another typical cosmic-string scenarios observed by Alford and Wilczek
[
2
]
.
As the flux is quantized with respect to the Higgs charge, this will lead to a non-trivial Aharonov-Bohm scattering of these fermions. From the mathematical point,
the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
can be extended as a self-adjoint operator (see
[
11
]
) and
the fractional operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
can be defined from the spectral perspective, see
[
14
]
for the fractional operator.
The solution of (
1.1
) can be written as

u(t,x)=e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f(x),

(1.4)

which includes the usual wave and Schrödinger equation as two special cases.
The dispersive and Strichartz estimates associated with the magnetic Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}

have been extensively studied in
[
11
,
13
,
16
]
and we also refer to
[
12
,
15
]
for the resolvent estimates.

In this paper, we consider the the minimal regularity of initial data for which the above solution (
1.4
) pointwisely converges to the initial data
f\in H_{\bf A}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f(x)=f(x),\quad a.\,e.

(1.5)

that is, the pointwise convergence problem related to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
.
The problem for free Schrödinger
(i\partial_{t}-\Delta)u=0
with initial data
f\in H^{s}
was first proposed by Carleson in
[
7
]
(see also
[
17
]
).
In this classical case, the solution can be formally expressed by using Fourier transform as

u(t,x)=e^{it\Delta}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}e^{-2\pi it|\xi|^{2}}\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

(1.6)

The standard routine for the pointwise convergence of the free Schrödinger operator (
1.6
) is to find the minimal regularity of
f

for which the corresponding maximal estimate holds as follows

\left\|\sup_{0<t<1}|e^{it\Delta}f(x)|\right\|_{L^{q}(B^{n}(0,1))}\leq C_{n,q,s}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(1.7)

where
B^{n}(0,1)
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In one dimension, Carleson proved that (
1.7
) holds for
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
. In 1981, Dahlberg and Kenig
[
8
]
proved that the result obtained by Carleson is sharp.
In higher dimension for
n\geq 2
, Sjölin
[
26
]
and Vega
[
28
]
independently showed that (
1.7
) holds for
s>\frac{1}{2}
.
Later, this range was improved by Lee
[
19
]
to
s>\frac{3}{8}
for
n=2
, and by Bourgain
[
5
]
to
s>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4n}
for
n\geq 3
.
Bourgain also proved that
s\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n}
is necessary for (
1.7
) to hold. Du, Guth and Li
[
9
]
obtained
s>\frac{1}{3}
in dimension
n=2
.
Recently, Du and Zhang
[
10
]
proved that (
1.7
) holds for
s>\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
for
n\geq 3
. Luc
\grave{a}
and Rogers obtained the necessary condition

s\geq\frac{n}{2(n+2)}
and later Bougain obtained the best necessary condition
s\geq\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
up to now.

It is nature to ask the same problem for more general dispersive equations associated with Schrödinger operator with potentials.
However, due to the lack of Fourier transform, there are much fewer results compared with the free Schrödinger case.
The picture in this direction is far to be complete since many powerful tools (e.g. decoupling, polynomial decomposition) cannot be applied in this setting.
It worths to mention that one of the most interesting potentials, the so-called inverse-square potential, which prevents us from using Fourier transform to give the explicit expression of the solution and hence the related techniques of Fourier analysis fail to work.
However, by replacing the Fourier transform by the Hankel transform, the authors of
[
21
]
established the maximal estimates for Schrödinger equation with inverse-square potential, in which they write the solution in terms of spherical harmonics. For more details, we refer the interested readers to
[
21
]
and the references therein. As for other types of maximal estimates, one is referred to
[
3
,
4
]
for quadratic Weyl sum and
[
18
]
for average over space curve.
Even though, almost all of the results are far to be sharp in the setting of equations with potentials.

To the best of our knowledge, there are few papers on the maximal estimates related to the magnetic operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
available, for which we start a new program to study the maximal estimates associated with the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
. To be specific, we try to establish the local- and global-in-time maximal estimates associated with the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
. The proof is based on the cut-off spectral measure estimates.
To state the main theorems, we define the distorted Sobolev space as follows

\displaystyle H^{s}_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=(I+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}})^{-\frac{s}{2}}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

(1.8)

In particular, we shall write

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}:=\|(I+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}})^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(1.9)

in what follows.
Now we are in the position to state our main results.

Theorem 1.1

(Local estimate)
.

Let
u(t,x)=e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f(x),\alpha>0
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
f\in H^{s}_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then
for

\bullet
either
2\leq q\leq 6
and
s>\frac{\alpha}{4}(\frac{6}{q}-1)+(1-\frac{2}{q})
,

\bullet
or
6\leq q<\infty
and
s>1-\frac{2}{q}
,

there exists a constant
C
such that

\begin{split}\big\|\sup_{|t|\leq 1}|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{H_{\mathbf{A}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.\end{split}

(1.10)

As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
with
q=2
for
0<\alpha\leq\frac{4}{3}
and
q=6
for
\alpha>\frac{4}{3}
, we have the following pointwise convergence result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
s>\frac{\alpha}{2}
when
0<\alpha\leq\frac{4}{3}
or
s>\frac{2}{3}
when
\alpha>\frac{4}{3}
, then there holds the pointwise convergence (
1.5
)
for
f\in H^{s}_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Remark 1.3
.

In particular
\alpha=1
, i.e., the half-wave operator, from Rogers and Villarroya in
[
25
, Theorem 1]
, the result is sharp up to endpoint
s=\frac{1}{2}
.
But the result is far to be sharp in the most interesting and challenging Schrödinger case, that is,
\alpha=2
. We will continue investigating the problem in future work.

Remark 1.4
.

In the free case
{\bf A}\equiv 0
, in which the Fourier transform is available, Sjölin and Walther independently used the stationary phase to obtain the corresponding maximal estimate for the operator
e^{it(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}
. In one dimension Sjölin
[
26
]
obtained the pointwise convergence holds for
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
for
\alpha>1
which is sharp, and Walther
[
29
]
proved that the pointwise convergence holds for
s>\frac{\alpha}{4}
for
0<\alpha<1
, which is almost sharp up to the endpoint. In higher dimension, for
\alpha>1
Sjölin
[
26
]
obtained the result of
s>\frac{1}{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
n\geq 3

and
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
if
n=2
, and the pointwise convergence for the free Schrödinger equation for
0<\alpha<1
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
is left to be solved.

Theorem 1.5

(Global estimate)
.

Let
u(t,x)=e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f(x),\alpha>0
be the solution to (
1.1
) with
f\in\dot{H}^{s}_{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then for
6\leq q<\infty
and
s>1-\frac{2}{q}
, we have

\begin{split}\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{\mathbf{A}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.\end{split}

(1.11)

Remark 1.6
.

These results extend the local maximal estimates in Theorem
1.1
to global ones when
q\geq 6
.
In the case of
\alpha=1
, which corresponds to the wave equation with a magnetic field,
the global maximal estimate is sharp up to the endpoint regularity, we refer to
[
25
, Theorem 2]
.
In the Schrödinger case
\alpha=2
, from
[
24
, Theorem 8]
, the regularity assumption
s>1-\frac{2}{q}
is necessary for (
1.11
) up to the endpoint
s=1-\frac{2}{q}
.

We close this section by briefly stating the organization of the whole paper as follows.
In Sec.2, we provide the main tools needed in the proof of our main results, i.e., the spectral measure estimates.
In Sec.3 and 4, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.5
respectively.

2.
preliminaries

In this section, we provide the key spectral cluster estimates, which play a critical role in our proof.
For
0<\epsilon\leq 1
, we define the spectral projector associated with the magnetic Schrödinger
operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
on the frequencies
[k,k+\epsilon]
by

\chi_{k,\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{[k,k+\epsilon]}(\lambda^{\alpha})\,dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda),\qquad\alpha>0,

(2.1)

where
\chi_{E}(s)
is the characteristic function on the set
E
and
dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda)
is the spectral measure given by

dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda)=\frac{d}{d\lambda}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda)d\lambda=\frac{\lambda}{i\pi}\big(R(\lambda+i0)-R(\lambda-i0)\big)d\lambda.

(2.2)

Here
R(\lambda\pm i0)
is the (outcoming/ingoing) resolvent

R(\lambda\pm i0)=(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0))^{-1}=\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i\epsilon)\big)^{-1}.

Very recently, Fanelli-Zhang-Zheng
[
12
]
used the spectral measure constructed in
[
16
]
to prove
the following resolvent estimates.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
12
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
1\leq p<\frac{4}{3}
and
4<q\leq\infty
satisfy
\frac{2}{3}\leq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}<1
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,q)>0
such that

\|(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0))^{-1}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\lambda^{2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad

(2.3)

where
(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i0))^{-1}:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}-(\lambda^{2}\pm i\epsilon))^{-1}
.

In particular, if
6\leq q<\infty
, then (
2.3
) holds for
q=p^{\prime}
.

Therefore, from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have the following spectral measure estimate

\|dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\lambda^{2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-1}.

(2.4)

Integrating on a frequency band of width
\epsilon
, we obtain

Lemma 2.2
.

For the operator
\chi_{k,\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})
defined by (
2.1
), we have

\|\chi_{k,\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\big((k+\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-k^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)(k+\epsilon)^{(2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-1)/\alpha},

(2.5)

where
1\leq p<\frac{4}{3}
and
4<q\leq\infty
satisfy
\frac{2}{3}\leq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}<1
.

In particular, for
6\leq q=p^{\prime}<+\infty
, then

\|\chi_{k,\epsilon}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\big((k+\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-k^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)(k+\epsilon)^{(2(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})-1)/\alpha}.

(2.6)

To prove the local result in Theorem (
1.1
) for the range
2\leq q\leq 6
, here we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
U(t)=e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}
, then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|D_{t}^{s}U(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{\bf A}^{\alpha s}\to L^{2}}\leq C,

where
0\leq s\leq 1
.

Proof.

By the interpolation, we only need to consider
s=0
and
s=1
. By the spectral theory, we have

T^{s}(t):=D_{t}^{s}U(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda^{\alpha}}\lambda^{\alpha s}\,dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda).

For
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([1,2])
, we define

T^{s}_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda^{\alpha}}\varphi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{\alpha s}\,dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda),

(2.7)

then
T^{s}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}T^{s}_{k}(t)
. By orthogonality, we have

\|T^{s}(t)f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|T^{s}_{k}(t)f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|T^{s}_{k}(t)f_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
f_{k}=\tilde{\varphi}(2^{-k}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}})f
with
\tilde{\varphi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([\frac{1}{2},4])
such that
\tilde{\varphi}=1
on supp
\varphi
.
It thus suffices to prove

\|T^{s}_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C2^{\alpha sk},

which is equivalent to

\|\big(T^{s}_{k}(t)\big)^{*}T^{s}_{k}(t)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C^{2}2^{2\alpha sk}.

To this end, we set
\psi=\varphi^{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([1,2])
, then it is further enough to show

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{2\alpha s}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{2^{2k}2^{2\alpha sk}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{K}},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\forall K\geq 0,\end{split}

(2.8)

where
C
is a constant independent of
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the one hand, by scaling,

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{2\alpha s}&dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\\
&=2^{2k}2^{2\alpha sk}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(\lambda)\lambda^{2\alpha s}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;2^{k}x,2^{k}y)\Big|.\end{split}

(2.9)

On the other, by
[
16
, Proposition 1.1]
and integration by parts K times, we have

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(\lambda)\lambda^{2\alpha s}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{K}}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+|d_{s}|)^{K}}|B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})|ds,\end{split}

where for the definition of
d_{s}
and
B_{\alpha}(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
we can refer to
[
16
]
.
From
[
16
, (1.25)]
, one has
|d_{s}|^{2}\geq(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}\geq|x-y|^{2}
.
Hence, it follows from
[
16
, (3.3)]
that

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(\lambda)\lambda^{2\alpha s}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{K}}.\end{split}

This together with (
2.9
) yields (
2.8
).

∎

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we shall prove the local maximal estimate associated with the magnetic operator
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}
.
We need to prove the following local-in-time estimate

\begin{split}\big\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}([-1,1]))}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\mathbf{A}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.\end{split}

(3.1)

To this end, we first observe that there exists a Schwarz function
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with Fourier support
\text{supp}\,\hat{\rho}\subset(-2,2)
such that

\big\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}([-1,1]))}\lesssim\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}.

(3.2)

Then to prove (
3.1
) , it is sufficient to show

\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|(I+\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}})^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(3.3)

In the case that
2\leq q\leq 6
, by interpolation, it suffices to prove

\begin{split}\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\mathbf{A}}^{\sigma\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\sigma>\frac{1}{2}\end{split}

(3.4)

and

\begin{split}\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\mathbf{A}}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\sigma>\frac{2}{3}.\end{split}

(3.5)

The inequality (
3.4
) is a consequence of Lemma
2.3
and the Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\cap\dot{H}_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{t}(\mathbb{R})
with
0<\epsilon\ll 1
.
Indeed, for
\sigma>\frac{1}{2}
, by Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle=\big\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}(\rho(t)f)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|D_{t}^{\sigma}e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}(\rho(t)f)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\rho(t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{H_{\mathbf{A}}^{\sigma\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{H_{\mathbf{A}}^{\sigma\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

The inequality (
3.5
) is the endpoint of the following case
q\geq 6
. Therefore, from now on, we focus on the case that
6\leq q<+\infty
.

Recall (
2.1
) with
\epsilon=1

\chi_{k}^{\alpha}:=\chi_{k,1}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{[k,k+1]}(\lambda^{\alpha})\,dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}}}(\lambda),

(3.6)

then we have the decomposition
f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\chi_{k}^{\alpha}f
.
By (
2.6
), for
6\leq q<\infty
, we have

\|\chi_{k}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\big((k+1)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-k^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(k+1)^{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{q})/\alpha}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(3.7)

Let
0<\delta\ll 1
and

F(t,x):=|D_{t}|^{1/2+\delta}(\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f(x)),

(3.8)

then by Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|F(t,x)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}.

(3.9)

Set

F_{k}(t,x):=|D_{t}|^{1/2+\delta}(\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\chi_{k}^{\alpha}f(x)),

(3.10)

then

F(t,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}(t,x).

(3.11)

Taking Fourier transform in variable
t
, we get

\hat{F}_{k}(\tau,x)=|\tau|^{1/2+\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\rho}(\tau-\lambda^{\alpha})\chi_{[k,k+1]}(\lambda^{\alpha})dE(\lambda)f(x).

(3.12)

By Plancherel’s identity and the compact support property of
\hat{\rho}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{k}(t,x)\overline{F_{\ell}(t,x)}dt=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{F}_{k}(\tau,x)\overline{\hat{F}_{\ell}(\tau,x)}d\tau=0,\quad|k-\ell|>100.

(3.13)

Therefore we get

\|F(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|F_{k}(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=(2\pi)^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\hat{F}_{k}(\tau,x)\|_{L_{\tau}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

(3.14)

This together with (
3.9
) gives

\big\|\rho(t)e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\big\|\big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|\hat{F}_{k}(\tau,x)\|_{L_{\tau}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(3.15)

Notice that
q\geq 6

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\tau|^{1+2\delta}\Big\|\int_{0}^{\infty}\hat{\rho}(\tau-\lambda^{\alpha})\chi_{[k,k+1]}(\lambda^{\alpha})dE(\lambda)f(x)\Big\|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}d\tau.

Proof of theorem
1.5

In this section, we extend the local estimates in Theorem
1.1
to global estimates. In fact, we shall prove the following estimate:

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\|\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{A}}^{s/2}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},\quad 6\leq q\leq\infty.

(4.1)

To prove (

(4.2)

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1

where

(4.11)

Using (
4.5

s:=1-\frac{2}{q}+\alpha\delta

\displaystyle\|\rho(\epsilon t)

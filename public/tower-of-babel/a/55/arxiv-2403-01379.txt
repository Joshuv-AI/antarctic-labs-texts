Propagation of high peaks for the space-time fractional stochastic partial differential equations
Ngartelbaye Guerngar, Erkan Nane

Abstract
We study the space-time nonlinear fractional stochastic heat equation driven by a space-time white noise, ∂ t β u ⁡ ( t , x ) = − ( − Δ ) α / 2 ​ u ​ ( t , x ) + I t 1 − β ​ [ σ ⁡ ( u ⁡ ( t , x ) ) ​ W ˙ ​ ( t , x ) ] , t > 0 , x ∈ ℝ , \displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\beta}u(t,x)=-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u(t,x)+I_{t}^{1-\beta}\Big[\sigma(u(t,x))\dot{W}(t,x)\Big],\ \ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}, where σ : ℝ → ℝ \sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R} is a globally Lipschitz function and the initial condition is a measure on ℝ . \mathbb{R}. Under some growth conditions on σ , \sigma, we derive two important properties about the moments of the solution: (i) For p ⩾ 2 , p\geqslant 2, the p th p^{\text{th}} absolute moment of the solution to the equation above grows exponentially with time. (ii) Moreover, the distances to the origin of the farthest high peaks of these moments grow exactly exponentially with time. Our results provide an extension of the work of Chen and Dalang [ 6 ] to a time-fractional setting. We also show that condition (i) holds when we study the same equation for x ∈ ℝ d . x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Introduction

Since their introduction in the 17
th
century by Isaac Newton and Gottfried Leibniz, differential equations have been used to model various phenomena. However, differential equations of integer order have shown some limitations in the modeling of more complex phenomena. Recently, fractional differential equations have gained popularity since they can handle such problems. Their applications include modelling contamination of groundwater flow, the electrical dynamics of the heart and the design of new materials,
viscoelasticity

[
14
]
. Stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) incorporate randomness in the system
[
13
]
. They generalize partial differential equations (PDEs).

In this paper, we study the space-time fractional nonlinear stochastic equation (see for a motivation to study these type of equations, and the physical derivation of these equations in
[
8

and
[
15

\begin{split}\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\beta}u(t,x)=&-(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u(t,x)+I_{t}^{1-\beta}\big[\sigma(u(t,x)\dot{W}(t,x)\big],\ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\

\gamma_{p}:\overline{\gamma}_{p}=\underline{\gamma}_{p}\ \text{for all}\ p\in\mathbb{N}\ \text{and}\ p\mapsto\frac{\gamma_{p}}{p}\ \text{is strictly increasing.}

[

This mathematical definition of intermittency is related to the property that the solutions are close to zero in vast regions of space-time but develop high peaks on some small "islands"

In order to properly characterize the speed of propagation of these "high peaks", we recall the

(1.6)

\overline{\gamma}(p):=\inf\Big\{a>0:\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup\limits_{|x|\geqslant at}\log\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{p}<0\Big\},

and

(1.7)

\underline{\gamma}(p):=\sup\Big\{a>0:\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup\limits_{|x|\geqslant at}\log\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{p}>0\Big\}.

It is not hard to check directly that

When

The solution to Eq. (

(ii)

Chen and Dalang
[

]

The authors also showed that for the Parabolic Anderson Model in this case,

In this case, the authors showed that

In this paper, we assume that

in Eq. (

The following definition provides an interpretation of the solution to our main problem.

Following

2.

for all

\Big(G^{2}\star\|\sigma(u)\|_{2}^{2}\Big)(t,x):=\int\limits_{0}^{t}ds\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}dy\ G^{2}(t-s,x-y)\|\sigma\big(u(s,y)\big)\|_{2}^{2}<\infty.

4.

the function

\mathcal{I}:\ \mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)

5.

u
satisfies (

The existencee and uniqueness of such solution are all proved in

For all

define,

\mathcal{L}_{0}(t,x):=G^{2}(t,x),

(3.1)

\mathcal{L}_{n}(t,x):=(\underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{0}\star\cdots\star\mathcal{L}_{0}}_{n\ \text{factors of}\ \mathcal{L}_{0}})(t,x),\ \text{for}\ n\geqslant 1,

and,

(3.2)

\mathcal{K}(t,x;\lambda):=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2(n+1)}\mathcal{L}_{n}(t,x).

The following variations of the kernel functions

will also be used.

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{K}(t,x):=\mathcal{K}(t,x;\lambda)\qquad\overline{\mathcal{K}}(t,x):=\mathcal{K}(t,x;L_{\sigma}),\\

The moment bounds of the solution of Eq. (

Due to the fractional time-derivative, the "heat kernel"

lacks several important properties including the Chapman-Kolmogorov identity (semigroup property). We therefore introduce additional kernel functions that display the semi-group property or at least some restrictive form of it (like sub-semigroup property or sup-semigroup property). Define the

(3.4)

\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x):=\frac{{C}_{1,\alpha}t^{\beta}}{\big(t^{2\beta/\alpha}+x^{2}\big)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}}\qquad\text{for}\ \alpha\in(0,2)\ \text{and}\ \beta\in(0,1)

and,

(3.5)

\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x):=\frac{\overline{C}_{1}t^{\beta/\alpha}}{t^{2\beta/\alpha}+x^{2}}\qquad\text{for}\ \alpha\in(0,2)\ \text{and}\ \beta\in(0,1),

where
{C}_{1,\alpha}
and
\overline{C}_{1}
are understood to be normalizing constants.

For example,

(3.6)

C_{1,\alpha}:=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha/2+1/2)}{\Gamma(\alpha/2)\Gamma(1/2)}\ \ \text{and}\ \overline{C}_{1}:=\frac{1}{\pi}.

It is easy to verify directly that, for
1\leqslant\alpha,

(3.7)

G(t,x)\leqslant\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\leqslant\frac{C_{1,\alpha}}{\overline{C}_{1}}\overline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x).

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and

(3.8)

G(t,x)\geqslant\tilde{C}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)

for some positive constant

Proof.

Using the scaling property of

\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta},
we have

\inf\limits_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{G(t,x)}{\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)}=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{G(1,y)}{\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(1,y)}.

Recall from (
1.3

G(t,x)\geqslant c_{1}\Big(t^{-\beta/\alpha}\wedge\frac{t^{\beta}}{|x|^{1+\alpha}}\Big)
for some positive constant
c_{1}.
We consider two cases.

Case 1:
|y|>1
. In this case,

\displaystyle\frac{G(1,y)}{\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(1,y)}\geqslant\tilde{C}_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}}{|y|^{1+\alpha}}\geqslant\tilde{C}_{2}.

Case 2:

. Then
\frac{G(1,y)}{\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(1,y)}\geqslant\tilde{c}_{1}\big(1+|y|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1+\alpha}{2}}\geqslant\tilde{c}_{2}.

It follows that in both cases, we have
\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{G(1,y)}{\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(1,y)}>0
and this concludes the proof.

b=t^{\beta/\alpha}.

3.

By part 1. of this Lemma, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t-s,\cdot)*\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(s,\cdot)\Big)(x)\geqslant C_{1,\alpha}^{-2}2^{-2(1+\alpha)}(t-s)^{2\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t-s,x)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t-s,y)\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(s,y)dy.

Now using Plancherel’s identity and part 2. of this Lemma, we have,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t-s,y)\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(s,y)dy\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1,\alpha}^{4}C_{\alpha+1/2}^{2}}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[s(t-s)\Big]^{-\beta/\alpha}\exp\Big[-\big((t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}+s^{\beta/\alpha}\big)|z|\Big]dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1,\alpha}^{4}C_{\alpha+1/2}^{2}}{2\pi}\big[s(t-s)\big]^{-\beta/\alpha}\frac{2}{(t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}+s^{\beta/\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1,\alpha}^{4}C_{\alpha+1/2}^{2}}{2\pi}\big[s(t-s)\big]^{-\beta/\alpha}t^{-\beta/\alpha}.

4.

For
t\geqslant r\geqslant t/2>0,
we have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(r,x)=

\displaystyle C_{1,\alpha}r^{-\beta/\alpha}\Bigg(1+\frac{x^{2}}{r^{2\beta/\alpha}}\Bigg)^{-{(1+\alpha)/2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1,\alpha}r^{-\beta/\alpha}t^{\beta(1+1/\alpha)}}{2^{\beta(1+1/\alpha)}}\Big((t/2)^{2\beta/\alpha}+x^{2}\Big)^{-(1+\alpha)/2}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\beta(1+1/\alpha)}}r^{-\beta/\alpha}t^{\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x).

This concludes the proof.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,+}(\mathbb{R}),\ \mu\neq 0
. Then for all
\epsilon>0,
there exists a positive constant
C_{\#}:=C_{\#}(\alpha,\beta)
such that for all
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.9)

J_{0}(t,x)=\Big(G(t,\cdot)*\mu\Big)(x)\geqslant C_{\#}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{t\geqslant\epsilon\}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x).

Proof.

Combining Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
(1), we get

\displaystyle J_{0}(t,x)\geqslant

\displaystyle\tilde{C}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x-y)\mu(dy)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{1}t^{\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,y)\mu(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(1+\frac{y^{2}}{t^{2\beta/\alpha}}\Bigg)^{-(1+\alpha)/2}\mu(dy).

Note that the integrand above is non-decreasing with respect with
t
. Therefore,

\displaystyle J_{0}(t,x)\geqslant

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{3}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{t\geqslant\epsilon\}}(t)\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\mathop{\text{\LARGE$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(1+\frac{y^{2}}{\epsilon^{2\beta/\alpha}}\Bigg)^{-(1+\alpha)/2}\mu(dy)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{4}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{t\geqslant\epsilon\}}(t)\epsilon^{\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\epsilon,y)\mu(dy).

Since the function
y\mapsto\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\epsilon,y)
is strictly positive and by the choice of
\mu
, it is clear that the integral above is positive. Thus, the relation (
5.12
) follows by taking

C_{\#}:=\tilde{C}_{4}\epsilon^{\beta/\alpha}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\epsilon,y)\mu(dy).

∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(1,2].
Then for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.10)

\mathcal{K}(t,x)\geqslant C_{\star}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\text{E}_{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha},1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big[\Psi t^{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big],

where
\Psi:=\Psi(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)>0.

In particular, for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.11)

\big(1\star\mathcal{K}\big)(t,x)\geqslant C_{\circ}t^{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\text{E}_{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha},2-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big[\Psi t^{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big].

Here,
C_{*},
and
C_{o}
are all positive constants depending on
\alpha
and
\beta.

Proof.

Denote the
n-
fold convolution product

\Big(\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star n}(t,x):=\Big(\underbrace{\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\star\cdots\star\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}}_{n\ \text{factors of}\ \underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}}\Big)(t,x).

By the definition of
\mathcal{K}
and the estimate (
3.8
),

(3.12)

\mathcal{K}(t,x;\lambda)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\lambda^{2}G^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)\geqslant\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\lambda^{2}\tilde{C}^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x).

We now find a lower bound for the term
\Big(\lambda^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)
.

Claim
:

(3.13)

\Big(\lambda^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)\geqslant\frac{\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}^{n}\lambda^{2(n+1)}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)^{n+1}}{\Gamma\big((n+1)(1-\beta/\alpha)\big)}t^{n(1-\beta/\alpha)}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\ \ \ \ \text{for all}\ n\geqslant 0,

where
\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}:=2^{-2\big(\alpha+\beta(1+1/\alpha)+2\big)}\pi^{-1}C_{1,\alpha}^{2}C_{\alpha+1/2}^{2}
is a positive constant.

We proceed by induction. The case
n=0
is straightforward. So consider
n\geqslant 1
and assume by induction that (
3.13
) holds for
n-1.
Combining the induction hypothesis with Lemma
3.3
(3), we get

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}^{n-1}\lambda^{2(n+1)}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)^{n}}{\Gamma\big(n(1-\beta/\alpha)\big)}\int\limits_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{(n-1)(1-\beta/\alpha)}\Big(\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t-s,\cdot)*\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(s,\cdot)\Big)(x)ds

\displaystyle\geqslant\Upsilon_{n}t^{-\beta/\alpha}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t-s,x)(t-s)^{(n-1)(1-\beta/\alpha)+2\beta/\alpha}\Big[s(t-s)\Big]^{-\beta/\alpha}ds,

where

\Upsilon_{n}:=\frac{\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}^{n-1}\lambda^{2(n+1)}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)^{n}}{\Gamma\big(n(1-\beta/\alpha)\big)}\cdot\frac{C_{1}^{2}C_{\alpha+1/2}^{2}}{2^{2\alpha+3}\pi}.

Now, if
0\leqslant s\leqslant t/2
, then
t-s\geqslant t/2
. Thus, we can apply Lemma
3.3
(4) to see that

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)\geqslant\Upsilon_{n}2^{-2\beta(1+1/\alpha)}t^{\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\int\limits_{0}^{t/2}(t-s)^{(n-1)(1-\beta/\alpha)}\Big[s(t-s)\Big]^{-\beta/\alpha}ds.

Since
t-s\geqslant s
for
0\leqslant s\leqslant t/2
, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)\geqslant\Upsilon_{n}2^{-2\beta(1+1/\alpha)}t^{\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\int\limits_{0}^{t/2}s^{(n-1)(1-\beta/\alpha)}\Big[s(t-s)\Big]^{-\beta/\alpha}ds.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda^{2}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\Big)^{\star(n+1)}(t,x)\geqslant\Upsilon_{n}2^{-2\beta(1+1/\alpha)-1}t^{\beta/\alpha}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\int\limits_{0}^{t}s^{(n-1)(1-\beta/\alpha)}\Big[s(t-s)\Big]^{-\beta/\alpha}ds.

Finally, applying the definition of
Euler’s Beta integral,
see Eq. (
6.5
), proves the claim.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(t,x;\lambda)

\displaystyle\geqslant\tilde{C}^{2}\lambda^{2}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}\tilde{C}^{2}\lambda^{2}\Gamma\big(1-\beta/\alpha\big)t^{1-\beta/\alpha}\Big)^{n}}{\Gamma\big((n+1)(1-\beta/\alpha)\big)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\tilde{C}^{2}\lambda^{2}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(t,x)\text{E}_{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha},\ 1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big(\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}\lambda^{2}\tilde{C}^{2}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)t^{1-\beta/\alpha}\Big).

Thus the estimate (
3.10
) follows by setting

(3.14)

\Psi:=\Xi_{\alpha,\beta}\tilde{C}^{2}\lambda^{2}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)\ \ \text{and}\ \ C_{*}:=\lambda^{2}\Gamma(1-\beta/\alpha)\tilde{C}^{2}.

As for (
3.11
), using (
3.10
), we get

\displaystyle\Big(1\star\mathcal{K}\Big)(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(s,y)dyds

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle C\int\limits_{0}^{t}\text{E}_{1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha},\ 1-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big(\Psi s^{1-\beta/\alpha}\Big)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(s,y)dyds.

Now,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\underline{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}(s,y)dy

\displaystyle=C_{1,\alpha}^{2}s^{-\beta/\alpha}2\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+z^{2})^{(1+\alpha)}}dz

\displaystyle=C_{2,\alpha}s^{-\beta/\alpha}.

Finally, using Eq. (
6.3
), we conclude the proof.

Proof of Theorem

The proof follows similar ideas from

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle C_{\omega}\Big(1+|x|^{2}\Big)

It follows that for all

\displaystyle|J_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)|\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{4}t^{\beta/\alpha}\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{(1+|y|^{\eta})}{(1+|x-y|^{2})(1+|y|^{2\omega})}|\mu|(dy)

\displaystyle C_{6}\frac{1+t^{\beta/\alpha}}{(1+|x|)^{2}}.

Note that for all

\displaystyle 1+\zeta^{-2}|x|^{2}\leqslant\big(1+(\zeta^{-1}x_{1})^{2})\big(1+(\zeta^{-1}x_{2})^{2})\cdots\big(1+(\zeta^{-1}x_{d})^{2}).

{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}{}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)

(5.6)

\displaystyle=C_{d,\alpha}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{t^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(d-1)+\beta}}{\big(t^{2\beta/\alpha}+x_{k}^{2}\big)^{(d+\alpha)/2}}

Lemma 5.2
.

The following lower bound estimates hold for all
x,
and

The proof follows similar ideas from the one dimensional case in Lemma

1.

\displaystyle C_{d,\alpha}^{-1}t^{\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,\sqrt{2}x){\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,\sqrt{2}y).

It follows that,

{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}{}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x-y)\geqslant 2^{-(d+\alpha)}C_{d,\alpha}^{-1}t^{\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x){\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,y).

2.

By part 1. of this Lemma, we have

\displaystyle\Big({\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(t-s,\cdot)*{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(s,\cdot)\Big)(x)\geqslant\frac{(t-s)^{\frac{2\beta d}{\alpha}}}{C_{d,\alpha}^{2}2^{2(d+\alpha)}}{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(t-s,x)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}{}_{d}\mathcal{G}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(t-s,y){\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(s,y)dy.

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg(C_{d,\alpha}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{(t-s)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(d-1)+\beta}}{\big[(t-s)^{2\beta/\alpha}+y_{k}^{2}\big]^{(d+\alpha)/2}}C_{d,\alpha}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{s^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}(d-1)+\beta}}{\big(s^{2\beta/\alpha}+y_{k}^{2}\big)^{(d+\alpha)/2}}\Bigg)^{2}dy

\displaystyle\qquad=C_{d,\alpha}^{4}\big[s(t-s)\big]^{\frac{2\beta d}{\alpha}(d-1)+2\beta d}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\big[(t-s)^{2\beta/\alpha}+y_{k}^{2}\big]^{(d+\alpha)}}\frac{1}{\big(s^{2\beta/\alpha}+y_{k}^{2}\big)^{(d+\alpha)}}dy_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant{\vphantom{\Theta}}_{d}\Theta_{\alpha,\beta}\big[s(t-s)\big]^{\frac{2\beta d}{\alpha}(d-1)+2\beta d}\big[s(t-s)\big]^{\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}(-2d-2\alpha+1)}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{d}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big[-\Big((t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}+s^{\beta/\alpha}\Big)|z_{k}|\Big]dz_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad={\vphantom{\Theta}}_{d}\Theta_{\alpha,\beta}\big[s(t-s)\big]^{-\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}\Bigg(\frac{2}{(t-s)^{\beta/\alpha}+s^{\beta/\alpha}}\Bigg)^{d}

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant{\vphantom{\Theta}}_{d}\Theta_{\alpha,\beta}\Big[s(t-s)\Big]^{-\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}t^{-\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}.

Here,
{\vphantom{\Theta}}{}_{d}\Theta_{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{C_{d,\alpha}^{4}C_{d+\alpha-1/2}^{2d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}
and we have used Plancherel identity and Lemma

to get the second inequality

3.

For

{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}{}_{d}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha,\beta}(r,x)=

\displaystyle 2^{-\beta(1+d/\alpha)}(t/r)^{\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}{\vphantom{\mathcal{G}}}_{d}\mathcal{G}^{2}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x).

, it follows that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sup\limits_{|x|\geqslant\exp{(at)}}\log\|u(t,x\|_{2}^{2}\geqslant\Psi_{d}^{\frac{1}{1-\frac{\beta d}{\alpha}}}.

Thus,
\underline{\xi}(2)=+\infty
and
this concludes the proof.

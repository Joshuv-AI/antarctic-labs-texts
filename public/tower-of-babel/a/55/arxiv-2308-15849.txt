Shedding light on neutrino self-interactions with solar antineutrino searches Preprint: August 8, 2026
Quan-feng Wu, and Xun-Jie Xu

Abstract
Solar antineutrinos are absent in the standard solar model prediction. Consequently, solar antineutrino searches emerge as a powerful tool to probe new physics capable of converting neutrinos into antineutrinos. In this study, we highlight that neutrino self-interactions, recently gaining considerable attention due to their cosmological and astrophysical implications, can lead to significant solar antineutrino production. We systematically explore various types of four-fermion effective operators and light scalar mediators for neutrino self-interactions. By estimating the energy spectra and event rates of solar antineutrinos at prospective neutrino detectors such as JUNO, Hyper-Kamiokande, and THEIA, we reveal that solar antineutrino searches can impose stringent constraints on neutrino self-interactions and probe the parameter space favored by the Hubble tension.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have explored the potential of solar antineutrino
observations as a probe of neutrino self-interactions, which could
have important implications for cosmology and astrophysics. We have
considered various scenarios of neutrino self-interactions including
all possible Lorentz-invariant four-fermion effective operators as
well as possible opening up of the effective vertices by light mediators.
All these scenarios would lead to the production of solar antineutrinos.

Therefore, we present a dedicated calculation of the expected antineutrino
fluxes and event rates at neutrino detectors. We find that for the
self-interaction strength of cosmological interest, the resulting
antineutrino fluxes will cause significant solar IBD signals at next-generation
detectors like JUNO, HK, and THEIA. In particular, we have demonstrated
that solar antineutrino spectra may exhibit distinctive features such
as double peaks due to the presence of a light mediator.

Our results, as shown in Figs
4
and
5
,
imply that solar antineutrino observations can provide competitive
constraints on neutrino self-interactions, and in some cases fully
probe the parameter space favored by the Hubble tension. In conclusion,
solar antineutrinos offer a unique opportunity to test neutrino self-interactions
and might reveal unexplored features of neutrinos.

\displaystyle\Gamma

(B.8)

The differential decay rate is given by

\derivative{\Gamma}{E_{3}}=\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}M}{8(2\pi)^{3}M_{W}^{4}}\int_{E_{2}^{\mathrm{min}}(E_{3})}^{E_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}(E_{3})}\differential{E_{2}}\quantity[M^{2}-M'^{2}-2M(E_{2}+E_{3})+4E_{2}E_{3}],

(B.9)

where

E_{2}(E_{3},\cos\theta)=\frac{M\quantity(M-2E_{3})-M^{\prime 2}}{2\quantity[M-E_{3}\qty(1-\cos\theta_{23})]}\thinspace.

(B.10)

It has the following upper and lower bounds:

\displaystyle E_{2}^{\mathrm{min}}(E_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{M(M-2E_{3})-M^{\prime 2}}{2M}\thinspace,

(B.11)

\displaystyle E_{2}^{\mathrm{max}}(E_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{M(M-2E_{3})-M^{\prime 2}}{2(M-2E_{3})}\thinspace.

(B.12)

Integrating out
E_{2}
in Eq. (
B.9
), we obtain

\begin{aligned} \derivative{\Gamma}{E_{3}}&=\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}M_{W}^{4}}\frac{E_{3}^{2}\quantity[M(M-2E_{3})-M'^{2}]^{2}}{M(M-2E_{3})}\end{aligned}\thinspace,

(B.13)

where
E_{3}\in[0,\ (M^{2}-M^{\prime 2})/(2M)]
.

Further integrating out
E_{3}
, we obtain the total decay rate:

\Gamma=\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{8(2\pi)^{3}M_{W}^{4}}\frac{1}{96M^{3}}\quantity[M^{8}-8M^{6}M'^{2}+24M^{4}M'^{4}\ln\frac{M}{M'}+8M^{2}M'^{6}-M'^{8}].

(B.14)

Appendix C
Calculation of four- and five-body decay

For
{}^{8}\mathrm{B}\to\mathrm{{}^{8}Be}+e^{+}+\overline{\nu}+\phi

and
{}^{8}\mathrm{B}\to\mathrm{{}^{8}Be}+e^{+}+3\nu(\overline{\nu})
,
we need to compute four- and five-body phase space integrals. The
practical way of handling with such phase space integrals is to adopt
the time-like recursion relations
byckling1973particle
, which
will be briefly reviewed below.

In general, an
n
-body decay rate reads

\differential{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{S}\frac{1}{2M}\absolutevalue{\cal M}^{2}\differential{\Phi_{n}},

(C.1)

where
S
is the
symmetry factor
accounting for identical
particles,
M
is the mass of the initial particle,
{\cal M}

is the amplitude of the decay process, and
\differential{\Phi_{n}}
is given
by

\differential{\Phi_{n}}=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}\quantity(p-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\differential[3]{\boldsymbol{p}_{i}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{i}}\thinspace.

(C.2)

The phase space can be written recursively as

\displaystyle\differential{\Phi_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\differential[3]{\boldsymbol{p}_{n}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{n}}\quantity(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{\dd[3]{\boldsymbol{p}_{i}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{i}})(2\pi)^{(4)}\quantity(\qty(p-p_{n})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}p_{i})

(C.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{\differential[3]{\boldsymbol{p}_{n}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2E_{n}}\differential{\Phi_{n-1}(p-p_{n};p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1})}.

Let us define

k_{i}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{i}p_{j}\thinspace,\ \ M_{i}^{2}\equiv k_{i}^{2}\thinspace,

(C.4)

then we can insert the following integrals

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\equiv\int\differential{M_{n-1}^{2}}\delta\quantity(k_{n-1}^{2}-M_{n-1}^{2}),

(C.5)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\equiv\int\differential[4]{k_{n-1}}\delta^{(4)}\quantity(p-p_{n}-k_{n-1})

(C.6)

into Eq. (
C.3
) and obtain

\displaystyle\int\differential{\Phi_{n}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu_{n-1}^{2}}^{(M_{n}-m_{n})^{2}}\frac{\differential{M_{n-1}^{2}}}{2\pi}

(C.7)

\displaystyle\times\int\frac{\differential[4]{k_{n-1}}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\differential[4]{p_{n}}}{(2\pi)^{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p-p_{n}-k_{n-1})2\pi\delta\quantity(k_{n-1}^{2}-M_{n-1}^{2})2\pi\delta\quantity(p_{n}^{2}-m_{n}^{2})

\displaystyle\times\int\differential{\Phi_{n-1}(p-p_{n};p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu_{n-1}^{2}}^{\quantity(M_{n}-m_{n})^{2}}\frac{\differential{M_{n-1}^{2}}}{2\pi}\int\differential{\Phi_{2}}(k_{n};k_{n-1},p_{n})\differential{\Phi_{n-1}(p-p_{n};p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu_{n-1}^{2}}^{\quantity(M_{n}-m_{n})^{2}}\frac{\differential{M_{n-1}^{2}}}{2\pi}\int\frac{\differential{\Omega_{n-1}}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\quantity(M_{n}^{2},M_{n-1}^{2},m_{n}^{2})}}{8M_{n}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\int\differential{\Phi_{n-1}(p-p_{n};p_{1},\cdots,p_{n-1})}.

where
\mu_{i}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{i}m_{j}
,
m_{i}^{2}\equiv p_{i}^{2}
,
and
\differential{\Omega_{i}}
is defined as the differential solid angle
in the rest frame of
k_{i+1}
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{k}_{i+1}=0
.

So the final result is

\int\differential{\Phi_{n}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3n-4}}\int_{\mu_{n-1}}^{M_{n}-m_{n}}\differential{M_{n-1}}\cdots\int_{\mu_{2}}^{M_{3}-m_{3}}\differential{M_{2}}\quantity(\int\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\dd{\Omega_{i}})\frac{1}{2^{n}M_{n}}\prod_{i=2}^{n}P_{i}\thinspace,

(C.8)

where

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\lambda\quantity(M_{i}^{2},M_{i-1}^{2},m_{i}^{2})}}{2M_{i}}\thinspace,

(C.9)

\displaystyle\lambda(x,y,z)

\displaystyle\equiv(x-y-z)^{2}-4yz\thinspace.

(C.10)

Substituting Eq. (
C.8
) into Eq. (
C.1
),
we can perform Monte Carlo integration straightforwardly, provided
that
|{\cal M}|^{2}
is known. For those four- or five-body processes
considered in this work,
|{\cal M}|^{2}
can be computed analytically
assuming the interaction in Eq. (
B.1
). The results for the
four-fermion operators are

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{S}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{4}}\frac{4p_{4}.p_{5}}{\Lambda_{s}^{2}q^{4}}\left(M^{2}\left(p_{2}.p_{3}q^{2}-2q.p_{2}q.p_{3}\right)+2p.p_{2}\left(2p.qq.p_{3}-p.p_{3}q^{2}\right)\right),

(C.11)

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{V}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{4}}\frac{16p_{3}.p_{4}}{\Lambda_{V}^{2}q^{4}}\left(M^{2}\left(p_{2}.p_{5}q^{2}-2q.p_{2}q.p_{5}\right)+2p.p_{2}\left(2p.qq.p_{5}-p.p_{5}q^{2}\right)\right),

(C.12)

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{T}|^{2}

\displaystyle=64\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{4}}\frac{2p_{3}.p_{5}}{q^{4}}\left(M^{2}\left(p_{2}.p_{4}q^{2}-2q.p_{2}q.p_{4}\right)+p.p_{2}\left(4p.qq.p_{4}-2p.p_{4}q^{2}\right)\right)

(C.13)

\displaystyle+64\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{4}}\frac{2p_{3}.p_{4}}{q^{4}}\left(M^{2}\left(p_{2}.p_{5}q^{2}-2q.p_{2}q.p_{5}\right)+p.p_{2}\left(4p.qq.p_{5}-2p.p_{5}q^{2}\right)\right)

(C.14)

\displaystyle+64\frac{g_{{\rm eff}}^{2}}{M_{W}^{4}}\frac{p_{4}.p_{5}}{q^{4}}\left(M^{2}\left(2q.p_{2}q.p_{3}-p_{2}.p_{3}q^{2}\right)+2p.p_{2}\left(p.p_{3}q^{2}-2p.qq.p_{3}\right)\right)\thinspace,

(C.15)

where the subscripts

,

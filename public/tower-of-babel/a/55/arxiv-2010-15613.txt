Uniqueness and energy balance for isentropic Euler equation with stochastic forcing
Shyam Sundar Ghoshal, Animesh Jana, Barun Sarkar

Abstract
In this article, we prove uniqueness and energy balance for isentropic Euler system driven by a cylindrical Wiener process. Pathwise uniqueness result is obtained for weak solutions having Hölder regularity C α , α > 1 / 2 \displaystyle C^{\alpha},\alpha>1/2 in space and satisfying one-sided Lipschitz bound on velocity. We prove Onsager’s conjecture for isentropic Euler system with stochastic forcing, that is, energy balance equation for solutions enjoying Hölder regularity C α , α > 1 / 3 \displaystyle C^{\alpha},\alpha>1/3 . Both the results have been obtained in a more general settings by considering regularity in Besov space.

Introduction

This article deals with the uniqueness as well as the energy-balance equation for the following compressible Euler system driven by cylindrical
Wiener process,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\varrho+\dv_{x}\textbf{m}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\textbf{m}+\dv_{x}\left(\frac{\textbf{m}\otimes\textbf{m}}{\varrho}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\nabla_{x}p(\varrho)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})\mathrm{d}W_{t},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varrho(0,\cdot)=\varrho_{0},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ \ \textbf{m}(0,\cdot)=\textbf{m}_{0},

where
\displaystyle\varrho=\varrho(t,x)
,
\displaystyle\textbf{m}=\textbf{m}(t,x)
denote the density and momentum of a compressible fluid respectively, the barotropic pressure is
\displaystyle p=p(\varrho)=\kappa\varrho^{\gamma}
for
\displaystyle\kappa>0,\gamma>1
. We work in
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{d}
for space variable to avoid problems related to the presence of kinematic boundary, where
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{d}
denotes a flat torus:

\mathbb{T}^{d}\equiv\left([-1,1]\big|_{\{-1,1\}}\right)^{d};\ \text{for}\ d=2,3.

In the above system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) the force term is a cylindrical Wiener process
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on a filtered probability space
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
and the diffusion coefficient
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})
takes values in
\displaystyle L_{2}(\mathcal{U};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. In this article, we consider solutions having Besov regularity, which is defined as follows:
let
\displaystyle\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\displaystyle 1\leq q<\infty
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\subset\bar{\mathcal{O}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be open bounded set. Then,
\displaystyle B^{\alpha,\infty}_{q}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of all
\displaystyle f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in L^{q}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}_{+})\times L^{q}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions such that
\displaystyle\left|f\right|_{B^{\alpha,\infty}_{q}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty
where

\left|f\right|_{B^{\alpha,\infty}_{q}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\sup\limits_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\xi+\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|\xi\right|^{-\alpha}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|f(\cdot+\xi)-f(\cdot)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{q}(\mathcal{O},\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.3)

One of the main goal of this article is to obtain the pathwise uniqueness for weak solutions of (

1.2

Settings and Definitions

We assume that a filtered probability space
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{0\leq t\leq T},\mathbb{P})
, satisfying the following assumptions:

(H-1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{0}
contains all null sets
\displaystyle A\in\mathfrak{F}
, s.t.
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A)=0
,

(H-2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{t}=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathfrak{F}_{s}
.

The noise, we consider in this article is similar to that of
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
11
]
. We give a short description here, detailed discussions can be found in the mentioned references.

We consider the process is driven by a cylindrical Wiener process
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, on a filtered probability space
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
satisfying
(H-1)
and
(H-2)
, over a separable Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
. Here
\displaystyle W_{t}
is defined by the formal expansion

W_{t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e_{j}W^{j}_{t},

(2.1)

where
\displaystyle\{e_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
and
\displaystyle\{W^{j}_{t}\}_{j\geq 1}
is a family of mutually independent real-valued Brownian motions with respect to
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
. Let
\displaystyle\varrho\in\ L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
,
\displaystyle\varrho\geq 0
and
\displaystyle\textbf{m}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, then we define our diffusion coefficient
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m}):\mathcal{U}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})e_{j}:=\mathbb{G}_{j}(\cdot,\varrho(\cdot),\textbf{m}(\cdot)),

where coefficients
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{j}=\mathbb{G}_{j}(x,\varrho,\textbf{m}):\mathbb{T}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are
\displaystyle C^{1}
- functions and there exists a non-negative real numbers sequence
\displaystyle\{g_{j}\}
such that the following holds uniformly in
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{G}_{j}(\cdot,0,0)=0,|\partial_{\varrho}\mathbb{G}_{j}|+|\nabla_{\textbf{m}}\mathbb{G}_{j}|\leq g_{j}\mbox{ and }\sum_{j\geq 1}{g_{j}}^{2}<\infty.\end{split}

(2.2)

Note that, when
\displaystyle\mathbb{G}
satisfies (
2.2
) and
\displaystyle\varrho,\textbf{m}
are
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
- progressively measurable
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
- valued process such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varrho\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\times[0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\right)\mbox{ and }\textbf{m}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\times[0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})\right),

then the following is a well-defined
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}
- martingale in
\displaystyle L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})\mathrm{d}W_{s}:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{G}_{j}(\cdot,\varrho,\textbf{m})\mathrm{d}W^{j}_{s}.

Note that, the infinite sum in (
2.1
) does not converge in probabilistic sense as a random variable in
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
. But, we can construct an auxiliary space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{0}\supset\mathcal{U}
, where the sum converges. Define

\mathcal{U}_{0}:=\left\{u=\sum_{j\geq 1}u_{j}e_{j};\ \sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{j^{2}}<\infty\right\},

and the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{U}_{0}}^{2}:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{u_{j}^{2}}{j^{2}}.

The embedding
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{0}
is Hilbert-Schmidt and trajectories of
\displaystyle\{W_{t}\}
are
\displaystyle\mathbb{P}
- a.s. in
\displaystyle C([0,T];\mathcal{U}_{0})
.

Throughout the article, we work with the separable Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle q\geq 2
. Let
\displaystyle Y_{t}
be a stochastic process on
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},(\mathfrak{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})

(4.1)

Let
\displaystyle D_{s}
be progressively measurable with
\displaystyle D_{s}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;L_{2}(\mathcal{U};L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})))

(4.2)

Let
\displaystyle\{\zeta_{\epsilon}\}
be the standard mollifiers sequence in space variable and we denote
\displaystyle h^{\epsilon}=h*\zeta_{\epsilon}

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{s}^{\epsilon}D_{s}^{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}W_{s}\rightarrow\int\limits_{0}^{T}\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{s}D_{s}\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}W_{s}\mbox{ as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0\mbox{ in }\mathbb{P}-\mbox{a.s.}

(4.3)

We note that proof of Lemma

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle X_{t},Y_{t}

5
Relative energy inequality

In this section we prove a technical lemma for proving uniqueness result and it also sets a ground to prove energy balance as well. In order to prove both the theorems we mollify solutions only in space variable we use the following form of relative energy for the system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
),

\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{m}|r,\textbf{n}):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{\varrho}{2}\left|\frac{\textbf{m}}{\varrho}-\frac{\textbf{n}}{r}\right|^{2}+P(\varrho)-(\varrho-r)P^{\prime}(r)-P(r)\right)\mathrm{d}x.

(5.1)

Note that it measures the distance between two weak solutions. If
\displaystyle(\varrho,\textbf{m})
is a weak solution satisfying admissible criteria and
\displaystyle(r,\textbf{n})
is a process having regularity in space then we can have a estimate for
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(t_{2})-\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\mathbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(t_{1})
for two different time
\displaystyle 0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<\tau
. More precisely, we prove the following result:

Proposition 5.1

(Relative energy inequality)
.

Let
\displaystyle[\varrho,\textbf{m},\tau]
be a pathwise weak solution satisfying energy inequality (
2.7
). Let
\displaystyle r,\textbf{n}
be two stochastic processes on
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\{\mathfrak{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
determined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\displaystyle=F_{s}\mathrm{d}s+A_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\textbf{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=G_{s}\mathrm{d}s+B_{s}\mathrm{d}W_{s},

(5.3)

where
\displaystyle F_{s},G_{s},A_{s},B_{s}
are progressively measurable such that the following holds for all
\displaystyle 1\leq q<\infty
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}&F_{s}\in L^{q}(\Omega;L^{q}(0,T;C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))),&A_{s}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;L_{2}(\mathcal{U};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))),\\
&G_{s}\in L^{q}(\Omega;L^{q}(0,T;C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}))),&B_{s}\in L^{2}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;L_{2}(\mathcal{U};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})))),\\
&\mathbb{E}\left[\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\left|A_{s}(e_{k})\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty,&\mathbb{E}\left[\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\left|B_{s}(e_{k})\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty.\end{array}

(5.4)

We assume that

\displaystyle\displaystyle r\in C([0,T];C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\,\textbf{n}\in C([0,T];C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(5.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|r\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{q}+\mathbb{E}\left(\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\textbf{n}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{q}\leq c(q)

(5.6)

for some constant
\displaystyle c(q)
depending on
\displaystyle q
for all
\displaystyle 1\leq q<\infty
. Then for
\displaystyle 0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}<\tau
, we have

\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\textbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(t_{2})\leq\mathcal{E}(\varrho,\textbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(t_{1})+\int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}M_{rem}\mathrm{d}W_{s}+\int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{R}(\varrho,\textbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(5.7)

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\varrho,\textbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(s)
is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\varrho,\textbf{m}|r,\textbf{n})(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left((r-\varrho)P^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{\textbf{m}\cdot\textbf{n}}{r^{2}}-\frac{\varrho\left|\textbf{n}\right|^{2}}{r^{3}}\right)F_{s}+\frac{\varrho}{r}\left(\frac{\textbf{n}}{r}-\frac{\textbf{m}}{\varrho}\right)\cdot G_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}(P^{\prime\prime}(r)+P^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)(r-\varrho))\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|A_{s}(e_{k})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|A_{s}(e_{k})\right|^{2}\left(\frac{3\varrho\left|\textbf{n}\right|^{2}}{2r^{4}}-\frac{\textbf{m}\cdot\textbf{n}}{r^{3}}\right)-\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}A_{s}(e_{k})B_{s}(e_{k})\cdot\left(\frac{2\varrho\textbf{n}}{r^{3}}-\frac{\textbf{m}}{r^{2}}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{\textbf{n}\cdot\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})(e_{k})}{r^{2}}A_{s}(e_{k})+\frac{\varrho}{2}\left|\frac{B_{s}(e_{k})}{r}-\frac{\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})(e_{k})}{\varrho}\right|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\textbf{m}\otimes\left(\frac{\textbf{n}}{r}-\frac{\textbf{m}}{\varrho}\right):\nabla_{x}\frac{\textbf{n}}{r}-p(\varrho)\dv_{x}\frac{\textbf{n}}{r}-\textbf{m}\cdot\nabla_{x}P^{\prime}(r)\right)\mathrm{d}x,

(5.8)

and the process
\displaystyle M_{rem}
is defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle M_{rem}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\left(\frac{\textbf{n}}{r^{2}}A_{s}-\frac{B_{s}}{r}\right)\cdot\textbf{m}+\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})\cdot\left(\frac{\textbf{m}}{\varrho}-\frac{\textbf{n}}{r}\right)-\varrho\frac{\left|\textbf{n}\right|^{2}}{r^{3}}A_{s}+\varrho\frac{B_{s}\cdot\textbf{n}}{r^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(r-\varrho)P^{\prime\prime}(r)A_{s}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(5.9)

Though we consider the general case for
\displaystyle r
with
\displaystyle A_{s}
(possibly
\displaystyle\neq 0
), in the proof of Theorem

5.1
for a weak solution
\displaystyle(r,\textbf{n})

\displaystyle A_{s}=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\eta\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\left|\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})^{\epsilon}(e_{k})\right|^{2}}{\varrho^{\epsilon}}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rightarrow-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\partial_{t}\eta(s)\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{\left|\textbf{m}\right|^{2}}{2\varrho}+P(\varrho)\right)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s,

(7.14)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rightarrow\int\limits_{0}^{T}\eta(s)\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left({\textbf{m}}\left(\frac{\left|\textbf{m}\right|^{2}}{2\varrho^{2}}+P^{\prime}(\varrho)\right)\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s,

(7.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\eta(s)\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\frac{\left|\mathbb{G}(\varrho,\textbf{m})^{\epsilon}(e_{k})\right|^{2}}{\varrho^{\epsilon}}\varphi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

as

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(Y_{s}^{\epsilon}-Y_{s}\right)D_{s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right|^{2}\right).

By using Bürkhholder-Davis-Gundy inequality
[
7

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{s}^{\epsilon}D^{\epsilon}_{s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}W_{s}-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Y_{s}D_{s}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}W_{s}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 2C(2)\mathbb{E}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|Y_{s}^{\epsilon}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|D^{\epsilon}_{s}(e_{k})-D_{s}(e_{k})\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+2C(2)\mathbb{E}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|Y_{s}^{\epsilon}-Y_{s}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|D_{s}(e_{k})\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

By using Hölder inequality we observe that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{k\geq 1}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|Y_{s}^{\epsilon}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|D^{\epsilon}_{s}(e_{k})-D_{s}(e_{k})\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|^{2}_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

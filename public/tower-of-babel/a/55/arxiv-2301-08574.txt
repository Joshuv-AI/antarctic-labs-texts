Introduction

The functional relations are an effective method for investigation of quantum integrable systems. To derive them it is convenient to use the quantum algebraic approach. Previously, what we call quantum algebra was usually called quantum group. In fact, this object is an associative algebra, which in a sense is a deformation of the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra. We fix two positive integers

The elements

As the Cartan subalgebra

of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}

Hence,

and the root system of
\mathfrak{gl}_{M|N}
is the set

\Delta=\{\Xi_{i}-\Xi_{j}\mid i,j=1,\ldots,M+N,\ i\neq j\}.

We choose as the system of simple roots the set

\Pi=\{\Xi_{i}-\Xi_{i+1}\mid i=1,\ldots,M+N-1\},

then the system of positive roots corresponding to
\Pi
is

\Delta_{+}=\{\alpha_{ij}=\Xi_{i}-\Xi_{j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq M+N\}.

Certainly, the corresponding system of negative roots is
\Delta_{-}=-\Delta_{+}
. Denoting

\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i,\,i+1}=\Xi_{i}-\Xi_{i+1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,M+N-1,

we obtain

\alpha_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\alpha_{k},\qquad 1\leq i<j\leq M+N.

We define a strict partial order
\prec

\mathfrak{k}^{*}
as follows. Given

\alpha-\beta

Define a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot\mid\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{k}^{*}
by the equation

(\Xi_{i}\mid\Xi_{j})=(-1)^{[i]}\delta_{ij}=d_{i}\delta_{ij},

where

d_{i}=(-1)^{[i]}.

We see that

(\alpha_{ij}\mid\alpha_{mn})=d_{i}\delta_{im}-d_{j}\delta_{jm}-d_{i}\delta_{in}+d_{j}\delta_{jn},

Below we often use the relations

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{ij}\mid\alpha_{jn})=-d_{j},\qquad(\alpha_{ij}\mid\alpha_{mi})=-d_{i},

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{ij}\mid\alpha_{in})=d_{i},\quad j\neq n,\qquad(\alpha_{ij}\mid\alpha_{ij})=d_{i}+d_{j},\qquad(\alpha_{ij}\mid\alpha_{mj})=d_{j},\quad i\neq m.

In fact, these are all nonzero cases.

\displaystyle E_{i,\,j+1}=[\![E_{ij},\,E_{j,\,j+1}]\!],\qquad F_{i,\,j+1}=[\![F_{j,\,j+1},\,F_{ij}]\!],\qquad j>i.

=(-1)^{[i]+[j]}q^{-d_{i}K_{i}+d_{j}K_{j}}[\![E_{j,\,j+1},\,F_{jn}]\!]=-(-1)^{[i]+[j+1]}q^{-d_{i}K_{i}+d_{j+1}K_{j+1}}F_{j+1,\,n}.

We see that equation (
4.31
) is always true. In the same way one can prove equations (
4.32
), (
4.33
) and (
4.34
).
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

For any
1\leq i<j\leq M+N
we have

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{ij}]\!]=E_{ij}F_{ij}-(-1)^{[i]+[j]}F_{ij}E_{ij}=\frac{q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{j}K_{j}}-q^{-d_{i}K_{i}+d_{j}K_{j}}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}}.

Proof.

The statement of the proposition is certainly true for
j=i+1
. Let us consider the case when
j-i>1
. It follows from proposition
4.3
that

(4.36)

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{ij}]\!]=[\![E_{ij},\,F_{i+1,\,j}F_{i,\,i+1}-q^{-1}_{i+1}F_{i,\,i+1}F_{i+1,\,j}]\!]\\
=E_{ij}F_{i+1,\,j}F_{i,\,i+1}-(-1)^{[i]+[j]}F_{i+1,\,j}F_{i,\,i+1}E_{ij}\\
-q_{i+1}^{-1}(E_{ij}F_{i,\,i+1}F_{i+1,\,j}-(-1)^{[i]+[j]}F_{i,\,i+1}F_{i+1,\,j}E_{ij}).

Equation (
4.32
) implies

\displaystyle F_{i,\,i+1}E_{ij}=(-1)^{[i]+[i+1]}E_{ij}F_{i,\,i+1}+E_{i+1,\,j}q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}},

\displaystyle E_{ij}F_{i,\,i+1}=(-1)^{[i]+[i+1]}F_{i,\,i+1}E_{ij}-(-1)^{[i]+[i+1]}E_{i+1,\,j}q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}}.

Using these equations in (
4.36
), we obtain

(4.37)

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{ij}]\!]=[\![E_{ij},\,F_{i+1,\,j}]\!]F_{i,\,i+1}+q_{i+1}^{-1}(-1)^{[i]+[i+1]}F_{i,\,i+1}[\![E_{ij},\,F_{i+1,\,j}]\!]\\
+q_{i+1}^{-1}(-1)^{[i]+[i+1]}E_{i+1,\,j}q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}}F_{i+1,\,j}\\
-(-1)^{[i]+[j]}F_{i+1,\,j}E_{i+1,j}q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}}.

We have

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{i+1,\,j}]\!]=q^{-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}+d_{j}K_{j}}E_{i,\,i+1},

see proposition
4.11
. Taking this equation, and proposition
4.1
and equation (
3.4
) into account, we come to the equation

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{ij}]\!]=q^{-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}+d_{j}K_{j}}[\![E_{i,\,i+1},\,F_{i,\,i+1}]\!]\\
+(-1)^{[i]+[i+1]}[\![E_{i+1,\,j},\,F_{i+1,\,j}]\!]q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{i+1}K_{i+1}}=\frac{q^{d_{i}K_{i}-d_{j}K_{j}}-q^{-d_{i}K_{i}+d_{j}K_{j}}}{q_{i}-q^{-1}_{i}}.

That was to be proved.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

For any
((i,\,j),\,(m,\,n))\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{IV}}
one has

\displaystyle[\![E_{ij},\,F_{mn}]\!]=E_{ij}F_{mn}-(-1)^{[m]+[j]}F_{mn}E_{ij}=-(q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1})q^{-d_{m}K_{m}+d_{j}K_{j}}F_{jn}E_{im},

\displaystyle[\![E_{mn},\,F_{ij}]\!]=E_{mn}F_{ij}-(-1)^{[m]+[j]}F_{ij}E_{mn}=(q_{j}-q_{j}^{-1})F_{im}E_{jn}q^{d_{m}K_{m}-d_{j}K_{j}}.

Proof.

It follows from propositions
4.3
and
4.9
that

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{mn}]\!]=[\![E_{im}E_{mj}-q_{m}E_{mj}E_{im},\,F_{mn}]\!]\\
=E_{im}E_{mj}F_{mn}-(-1)^{[m]+[j]}F_{mn}E_{im}E_{mj}-q_{m}(E_{mj}E_{im}F_{mn}-(-1)^{[m]+[j]}F_{mn}E_{mj}E_{im})\\
=E_{im}[\![E_{mj},\,F_{mn}]\!]-q_{m}[\![E_{mj},\,F_{mn}]\!]E_{im}.

Now, using equation (
4.31
), proposition
4.1
and proposition
4.8
, we get

[\![E_{ij},\,F_{mn}]\!]=(-1)^{[m]+[j]}(q_{m}-q_{m}^{-1})q^{-d_{m}K_{m}+d_{j}K_{j}}F_{jn}E_{im}.

Now, taking into account equations (
3.4
), we see that the first equation of the proposition is true. The second equation can be proved similarly.
∎

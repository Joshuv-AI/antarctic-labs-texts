Abstract




Explicit Kink Solutions in Several One-Parameter Family of Higher Order Field Theory Models

Ayhan Duzgun and Avadh Saxena

Theoretical Division and Center for Nonlinear Studies,
Los Alamos National Laboratory, Los Alamos, New Mexico 87545, USA

Abstract:

We present several one-parameter family of higher order field theory
models some of which admit explicit kink solutions with an exponential tail while others admit explicit kink solutions with a power-law tail. Various properties of these families of kink solutions are examined in detail. In particular for models harboring kink solutions with a power-law tail, we show that there is no gap between the zero mode and the beginning of the continuum in the kink stability potential. Further, by applying the recent Manton formalism, we provide estimates for the kink-kink and antikink-kink acceleration and hence the ratio of the corresponding antikink-kink and kink-kink forces.

Introduction

During the last few years there has been a resurgence in studying kink solutions with exponential as well as power-law tails in several higher (than 6th) order field theory models including
\phi^{8}
,
\phi^{10}
, and
\phi^{12}

[
1

2

[

4

are important in a variety of physical contexts ranging from successive phase transitions
[

2

[

to models involving long-range interaction between massless mesons
[

[
9
]
to successive phase transitions presumably driving the late time expansion of the Universe
[
10
]
. Thus, understanding kink behavior in these models provides useful insight into the properties of domain walls in materials, condensed matter, high energy physics, biology and cosmology, which also serves as one of our main motivations here.

So far implicit kink solutions have been obtained in these higher order models using which one has studied in detail
[
11

several aspects of these kink solutions such as the nature of kink tail, kink-kink and kink-antikink forces
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15

, stability analysis, etc. However, one important missing piece has been the absence of analytically solvable explicit kink solutions in these higher order field theory models. Few exceptions include explicit kink solutions in a one-parameter family of
\phi^{4n+2}-\phi^{2n+2}-\phi^{2}
models (
n=1,2,3

\phi^{8}

[
16
]
as well as one example of
\phi^{10}

[
11
]
field theory models. The main objective of this paper is to partially fill this gap.

In this context it is worth pointing out that during the last four decades explicit kink solutions have been obtained in a number of lower order field theory models like
\phi^{4}
,
\phi^{6}

[
17
]
and also several non-polynomial hyperbolic
[
18

and periodic potentials. Beyond sine-Gordon

[

[

23
]
, power-tower
[

]
as well as power-law tail
[

,
26

27
,
28
,
29

The purpose of this paper is to partially construct some of the exact solutions. In particular, we present four one-parameter families of higher order field theory models for which we are able to obtain explicit kink solutions. Two of these models have a kink solution with an exponential tail while the other two admit kink solutions with a power-law tail. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first known examples of a one-parameter family of higher order field theory models admitting kink solutions with a power-law tail. We study in detail various properties of these kink solutions including the kink mass, nature of the kink tail, the kink linear stability analysis and offer predictions for the kink-kink (KK) and the kink-antikink (K-AK) forces by using the recent Manton formalism
[
12

and its extension to higher order field theories
[

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Sec. II we obtain explicit kink
solutions in one-parameter families of potentials of the type

\phi^{2}(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}(b^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}
in case
b
and
a
are
related to each other. We study in detail various properties of these kink

extend these results. In Sec. III we obtain
explicit kink solutions in a one-parameter family of potentials of the type
\phi^{2}(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}(b^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}(c^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}
in case
c
,
b
and
a
are related to each
other. We study in detail the properties of these kink solutions all of which have an exponential kink tail. Section IV contains yet another family of solutions with the potential
V(\phi)=\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}(b^{2n}+\phi^{2n})^{2}
.
In the next two sections we study one-parameter families of higher order field theory models and obtain explicit kink solutions with a power-law tail.
In particular, in Sec. V we obtain explicit kink solutions in a one-parameter family of potentials of the type

\phi^{2n+2}|a^{2n}-\phi^{2n}|^{3/2}
all of which admit kink solutions with
a power law-tail. We study the properties
of these solutions including the stability analysis and show that in this case, as expected, there is no gap between the zero mode and the beginning of the continuum. By using the recent Manton formalism
[
12
]
we provide predictions for the K-K, AK-K and K-AK acceleration as well as the ratio of the AK-K and K-K forces. In Sec. VI we present another one-parameter family
of potentials of the form
|a^{2n}-\phi^{2n}|^{(2n+1)/2n}
and obtain explicit kink solutions with a power-law tail. In this case too, we study in detail the various properties of the
kink solutions and make predictions for the K-K and AK-K acceleration as well
as the ratio of the corresponding forces. Finally, in Sec. VII we summarize
the results obtained in this paper and point out some of the open problems.
In Appendices A and B we provide few more explicit kink solutions which are
obtained by solving quartic equations. Further, for completeness, we also
briefly discuss known explicit kink solutions in Appendix C.

2
Explicit Kink Solutions with Exponential Tail for a One-Parameter Family of Potentials

Let us consider the one-parameter family of potentials as given by

V(\phi)=\lambda^{2}\phi^{2}(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}(b^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}\,,~~n=1,2,3,...,~~b>a>0\,.

(1)

Note that this potential has 5 degenerate minima at
\phi=0,\pm a,\pm b
(see Fig. 1)
and hence two kink and two mirror kink solutions and corresponding four
antikink solutions. We now obtain these kink solutions explicitly.

Potential
V(\phi)
with five degenerate minima for three different values of
n=1,2,3
. Parameters are
\lambda=1
,
a=1
and
b=2^{1/(2n)}
. See Eq. (1).

2.1
Kink Solution From
0
to
a

In this case, we need to solve the self-dual equation

\frac{d\phi}{dx}=\lambda\phi(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})(b^{2n}-\phi^{2n})\,,

(2)

which can be achieved by using the partial fraction

\int d\phi\,\bigg[\frac{A}{\phi}+\frac{B\phi^{2n-1}}{(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}+\frac{D\phi^{2n-1}}{(b^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}\bigg]\,.

(3)

It is straightforward to check that

A=\frac{1}{a^{2n}b^{2n}}\,,~~B=\frac{1}{a^{2n}(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}\,,~~D=-\frac{1}{b^{2n}(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}\,.

(4)

Eq. (
3
) is then easily integrated giving an implicit kink solution

e^{\mu x}=\frac{(\phi^{2n})^{(b^{2n}-a^{2n})}(b^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{a^{2n}}}{(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{b^{2n}}}\,,

(5)

where

\mu=2\sqrt{2}n\lambda a^{2n}b^{2n}(b^{2n}-a^{2n})\,.

(6)

Note that the implicit kink solution is valid for arbitrary values of

a,b
with the constraint
b>a>0
.

It is straightforward to calculate the kink mass in this case. We find

M_{K}=\int_{0}^{a}d\phi\,\sqrt{2V(\phi)}=\frac{\lambda na^{2(n+1)}[(2n+1)b^{2n}-a^{2n}]}{\sqrt{2}(2n+1)(n+1)}\,.

(7)

We now consider three special cases in which Eq. (
5
) can be easily
inverted yielding an explicit kink solution. Two of these cases we discuss
below while the third case is discussed in Appendix A.

2.1.1
Case I:
b^{2n}=2a^{2n}

In this case the implicit kink solution takes the form

e^{\mu px}=\frac{\phi^{2n}(2a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}{(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{2}}\,,

(8)

where
p
is an arbitrary positive number with
pa^{2n}=1
, while
\mu
now has a simple form

\mu=\frac{4\sqrt{2}n\lambda}{p^{3}}\,.

(9)

Eq. (
8
) is easily inverted yielding an explicit kink solution from

0
to
a
(see Fig. 2)

\phi_{K}(x)=a\left[1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+e^{\mu px}}}\right]^{1/2n}\,.

(10)

Thus while the implicit kink solution is known for arbitrary values of

a,b
satisfying
b>a>0
, the explicit kink solution while valid
for arbitrary values of
a
, the corresponding
b
is however fixed, i.e.

b^{2n}=2a^{2n}
.

It is straightforward to check that

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\phi(x)=a2^{-1/2n}e^{\mu px/2n}\,,~~\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)=a-(2n)^{-1}ae^{-\mu px/2}\,.

(11)

Further, the zero mode in this case is easily calculated

\psi^{0,K}\propto\frac{e^{\mu px}}{(1+e^{\mu px})^{3/2}}\frac{1}{[1-(1+e^{\mu px})^{-1/2}]^{1-1/2n}}\,.

(12)

Finally, it is straightforward to carry out kink stability analysis. The kink
stability potential

V_{K}(x)
which appears in the
Schrödinger-like equation

-\frac{d^{2}\psi}{dx^{2}}+V_{K}(x)\psi=\omega^{2}\psi\,,

(13)

is easily calculated

\displaystyle V(x)=\frac{d^{2}V(\phi)}{d\phi^{2}}\Bigg|_{\phi=\phi_{K}(x)}

\displaystyle=2\lambda^{2}\bigg[(a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2n})^{2}(2a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2n})^{2}+16n^{2}\phi_{K}^{4n}(a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2n})(2a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2n})

\displaystyle+4n^{2}\phi_{K}^{4n}[(2a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2n})^{2}+(a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2n})^{2}]

\displaystyle-2n(2n+3)\phi_{K}^{2n}(a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2})(2a^{2n}-\phi_{K}^{2})(3a^{2n}-2\phi_{K}^{2n})\bigg]\,,

(14)

and substituting the kink solution (
10
) in it. In particular,

0
to
a

goes
from
-\infty
to
\infty
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle V(x=\infty)=\frac{8\lambda n^{2}}{p^{4}}\,,~~V(x=-\infty)=\frac{8\lambda}{p^{4}}\,,

\displaystyle V(x=0)=\frac{[(3+2\sqrt{2})/4-(\sqrt{2}-1)n^{2}-(3+\sqrt{2})n]}{p^{4}}\,.

(15)

It will be interesting to study if this potential admits other discrete modes
than the zero mode.

2.1.2
Case II:
b^{2n}=3a^{2n}

In this case the implicit kink solution takes the form

e^{\mu qx}=\frac{\phi^{4n}(3a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}{(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})^{3}}\,,

\phi=0,\pm a,\pm b,\pm c

and hence three kink and three mirror kink solutions and corresponding six

a_{K-K}=\bigg[\frac{n\Gamma(\frac{1}{2[2n+1]})\Gamma(\frac{n}{2n+1})}{\sqrt{\pi}(2n+1)}\bigg]^{2(2n+1}\frac{n\Gamma(\frac{2n+1}{n})}{2\Gamma(\frac{4n+1}{2n})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2n})}\frac{1}{d^{2(2n+1)}}\,,

(110)

a_{K-AK}=\bigg[-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2[2n+1]})}{\Gamma(-\frac{n}{2n+1})}\bigg]^{2(2n+1}\frac{n\Gamma(\frac{2n+1}{n})}{2\Gamma(\frac{4n+1}{2n})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2n})}\frac{1}{d^{2(2n+1)}}\,,

(111)

where
d
is the K-K or AK-K separation distance, and hence the ratio of the
two forces is

\frac{F_{K-AK}}{F_{K-K}}=-\left[\sin(\frac{\pi n}{2n+1})\right]^{2(2n+1)}\,.

(112)

7
Summary and Possible Open Problems

In this paper we have presented five one parameter family of models for
which explicit kink solutions can be obtained. In three of these models, the kink tail
has an exponential fall-off and using the standard Manton formalism
[
30
]
the
K-K and K-AK forces and hence their ratio is easily calculated. On the other
hand, in the other two models, the kink tail has a power-law fall off. To the best of
our knowledge, these two models are the first two known higher order field theory
examples for which explicit kink solutions with power-law tail can be obtained. Using
the recent Manton formalism
[
12
]
the K-K and AK-K forces and hence
their ratio is easily calculated in the model discussed in Sec. V. However, it is
not obvious if the new Manton formalism is also applicable to calculate the
K-K force in the model of Sec. VI since in this case there is only a single
kink and there is no mirror kink. Note, however, that the AK-K force can
certainly be calculated in this case. We have assumed in Sec. VI that one
can extrapolate the new Manton formalism for this case too and using it we
have provided a prediction for the K-K acceleration and hence the corresponding
K-K force as well as the ratio of the AK-K vs K-K force. It would be desirable
if one could check whether this is indeed true or not.

Another open problem is to enquire if there exist higher order field theory
models which have super-exponential
[
21
,
22
]
, super-super-exponential

[
23
]
or power-tower
[
24
]
type of tails. One criticism against the
models presented in Sec. V and VI is that these models are discontinuous at
x=\pm a
.
While this should not matter as far as the calculation of the kink solutions in these models
is concerned since for all of them
-a\leq x\leq a
, it is certainly desirable to obtain explicit
kink solutions with power-law tail in models where no such criticism can be made. Yet
another open problem is to understand why for kinks with power-law tail the ratio of the
AK-K to K-K forces progressively decreases as we steadily go to higher order field theory
models. This is unlike the kinks with exponential tails for which the magnitude of these
two forces is always equal.

8.1
Kink Solution From
0
to
a
in case
b^{2n}=4a^{2n}

In this case the implicit kink solution takes the form

e^{\mu fx}=\frac{\phi^{6n}(4a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}{(\phi^{2n}-a^{n})^{4}}\,,

(114)

where

a^{2n}f=1

\mu

\mu=\frac{24\sqrt{2}n\lambda}{f^{3}}\,.

u^{4}-\frac{6u^{2}}{(1+e^{\mu fx})}+\frac{8u}{(1+e^{\mu fx})}-\frac{3}{(1+e^{\mu fx}+1)}=0\,,

(116)

u=(1-\frac{\phi}{a})^{2n}

given by

\displaystyle u=1-\Big(\frac{\phi}{a}\Big)^{2n}=1\mp\sqrt{2m}\mp\sqrt{\frac{12}{(1+e^{f\mu x})}-2m\pm\frac{8}{\sqrt{m}(1+e^{\mu fx})}}\,,

(117)

where
m
has the form

\displaystyle m=\bigg[\frac{e^{\mu fx}[\sqrt{(1+e^{\mu fx})}+1}{(1+e^{\mu fx})^{3}}\bigg]^{1/3}+\bigg[\frac{e^{\mu fx}[\sqrt{(1+e^{\mu fx})}-1}{(1+e^{\mu fx})^{3}}\bigg]^{1/3}+\frac{2}{(1+e^{\mu fx})}\,.

(118)

It is straightforward to check that

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\phi(x)=\frac{a}{2^{1/3n}}e^{\mu fx/6n}\,,~~\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi(x)=a-\frac{a3^{1/4}}{2n}e^{-\mu fx/4}\,.

(119)

8.2
Kink Solution From
a
to
b
in case
b^{2n}=4a^{2n}

In this case the implicit kink solution takes the form

e^{-\mu fx}=\frac{\phi^{6n}(4a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}{(\phi^{2n}-a^{n})^{4}}\,,

(120)

where
f
is an arbitrary positive number satisfying
a^{2n}f=1
while

\mu
now has a simple form as given by Eq. (
115

Eq. (
120

u^{4}-\frac{6u^{2}}{(1+e^{-\mu fx})}-\frac{8u}{(1+e^{-\mu fx})}-\frac{3}{(1+e^{-\mu fx}+1)}=0\,,

(121)

where
u=(\frac{\phi}{a})^{2n}-1

\displaystyle u=\Big(\frac{\phi}{a}\Big)^{2n}-1=1\pm\sqrt{2m}\pm\sqrt{\frac{12}{(1+e^{-f\mu x})}-2m\pm\frac{8}{\sqrt{m}(1+e^{-\mu fx})}}\,,

(122)

m

\displaystyle m=\bigg[\frac{e^{-\mu fx}[\sqrt{(1+e^{-\mu fx})}+1]}{(1+e^{-\mu fx})^{3}}\bigg]^{1/3}+\bigg[\frac{e^{-\mu fx}[\sqrt{(1+e^{-\mu fx})}+1]}{(1+e^{-\mu fx})^{3}}\bigg]^{1/3}+\frac{2}{(1+e^{-\mu fx})}\,.

(124)

Appendix B: Analytical Kink Solutions with a Power-Law Tail in Yet
Another One-Parameter Family of Higher Order Field Theories

We show that one can obtain exact kink solutions with a power-law tail
in a one-parameter family of higher order field theories characterized by
the potential

V(\phi)=\lambda^{2}\phi^{2(3n+1)}\left|(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})\right|^{3}\,.

(125)

Note that this potential has degenerate minima at
\phi=0,\pm a
and
hence one
kink solution from
0
to
a
and the corresponding mirror kink solution
from
-a
to
0
and the corresponding two antikinks. Note that for both
the kink solutions
-a\leq\phi\leq a

and hence even though the potential is discontinuous at
\phi=\pm a

that would not matter as far as the kink and corresponding antikink
solutions are concerned.

9.1
Explicit Kink Solution

In order to obtain the kink solution, we need to solve the self-dual
equation

\frac{d\phi}{dx}=\sqrt{2}\lambda\phi^{3n+1}\left|\phi^{2n}-a^{2n}\right|^{3/2}\,.

(126)

This is easily integrated yielding

\frac{(8\phi^{4n}-4\phi^{4n}-a^{4n})^{2}}{9\phi^{6n}(a^{2n}-\phi^{2n})}=\mu^{2}x^{2}\,,~~~\mu=\sqrt{2}n\lambda a^{6n}\,.

(127)

Eq. (
127
) is easily inverted yielding a quartic equation

u^{4}+au^{2}+bu+d=0\,,

(128)

where

a=-\frac{3}{8}\,,~~~b=-\frac{(192+99\mu^{2}x^{2})}{64(64+9\mu^{2}x^{2})}\,,~~~d=\frac{(576-27\mu^{2}x^{2})}{256(64+9\mu^{2}x^{2})}\,,

(129)

while
u=(\frac{\phi}{a})^{2n}-1/4
. Thus the explicit kink solution is
given by

u=\left(\frac{\phi}{a}\right)^{2n}-1/4=\pm\sqrt{2m}\pm\sqrt{-(2a+2m\pm\frac{2b}{\sqrt{2m}})}-a/3\,,

(130)

where
m
satisfies the cubic equation

8m^{3}+8am^{2}+(2a^{2}-8d)m-b^{2}=0\,.

(131)

It is not difficult to check that in this case
m
is given by

m=\bigg[-\frac{b}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{b^{2}}{4}+\frac{a^{3}}{27}}\bigg]^{1/3}+\bigg[-\frac{b}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{b^{2}}{4}+\frac{a^{3}}{27}}\bigg]^{1/3}\,.

(132)

It is straightforward to check that

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\phi_{K}(x)=\frac{a}{(9\mu^{2}x^{2})^{1/6n}}\,,~~~\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{K}(x)=a-\frac{a}{2n\mu^{2}x^{2}}\,.

(133)

It is easy to calculate the kink mass in this case. We find

M_{K}=\int_{0}^{a}d\phi\,\sqrt{2V(\phi)}=\frac{3\lambda\sqrt{\pi}a^{6n+2}}{4\sqrt{2}}\frac{\Gamma[(3n+2)/2]}{\Gamma[(3n+7)/2]}\,.

(134)

Finally, as expected in this case too there is no gap between the zero mode
and the beginning of the continuum.

9.2
Kink-Kink and Kink-Antikink Force

Using the recent Manton formalism it is straightforward to predict the acceleration
a

and hence the force
F
in both the K-K and AK-K cases and thus the
ratio of the two forces. We predict that for the model characterized by the
potential (
90
) (with
a=1
and
\sqrt{2}\lambda=1
)

a_{K-K}=\bigg[\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2[3n+1]})\Gamma(\frac{3n}{2[3n+1]})}{2\sqrt{\pi}(3n+1)}\bigg]^{2(3n+1)/3n}\frac{4\Gamma(\frac{3n+7}{2})}{3\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{3n+2}{2})}\frac{1}{d^{2(3n+1)/3n}}\,,

(135)

a_{AK-K}=\bigg[-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{3n}{2[3n+1]})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{2[3n+1]})}\bigg]^{2(3n+1)/3n}\frac{4\Gamma(\frac{3n+7}{2})}{3\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{3n+2}{2})}\frac{1}{d^{2(3n+1)/3n}}\,,

(136)

where
d
is the K-K or AK-K separation distance, and hence the ratio of the
two forces is

\frac{F_{AK-K}}{F_{K-K}}=-\left[\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2[3n+1]}\right)\right]^{2(3n+1)/3n}\,,~~n=1,2,3,...\,.

(137)

It may be noted that for the model characterized by Eq. (
125
), the kink
tail around
\phi=1
has very different behavior compared to that around

\phi=0
and as a result the kink-antikink acceleration and hence the
corresponding force have very different behavior. Generalizing the new
Manton formalism
[
12
]
, we predict that in this model the kink-antikink
acceleration is given by

a_{K-AK}=\bigg[-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{1}{6})}{\Gamma(-\frac{1}{3})}\bigg]^{6}\frac{1}{8n^{3}}\frac{4\Gamma(\frac{3n+7}{2})}{3\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{3n+2}{2})}\left(\frac{1}{d^{6}}\right)\,.

(138)

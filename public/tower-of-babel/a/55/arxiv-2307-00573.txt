Quasi-admissible, raisable nilpotent orbits, and theta representations
Fan Gao, Baiying Liu, and Wan-Yu Tsai

Abstract
. We study the quasi-admissibility and raisablility of some nilpotent orbits of a covering group. In particular, we determine the degree of the cover such that a given split nilpotent orbit is quasi-admissible and non-raisable. The speculated wavefront sets of theta representations are also computed explicitly, and are shown to be quasi-admissible and non-raisable. Lastly, we determine the leading coefficients in the Harish-Chandra character expansion of theta representations of covers of the general linear groups.

Introduction

Let
F
be a number field or any of its local fields. Denote by
F^{\rm al}
the algebraic closure of
F
. Let
{\bf G}
be a split connected linear reductive group over
F
. If
F
is a local field, then we are interested in the group
G_{F}:={\bf G}(F)
or its finite degree central covers

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{F},}

where we assume that
\mu_{n}(F)=\mu_{n}(F^{\rm al})
. If
F
is a number field with adele ring
{\mathbb{A}}
, the object of interest is
G_{\mathbb{A}}:={\bf G}({\mathbb{A}})
or its
n
-fold central cover

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}_{\mathbb{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\mathbb{A}}.}

For example, a family of such central extensions naturally arises from the Brylinski–Deligne framework
[
BD
]
. If
n=1
, then the covering groups are just the linear algebraic groups.
We consider exclusively genuine representations of
\overline{G}_{F}
or
\overline{G}_{\mathbb{A}}
, where
\mu_{n}
acts via a fixed embedding
\mu_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
.
We will study the leading wavefront sets of genuine representations
\pi
of
\overline{G}_{F}
or
\overline{G}_{\mathbb{A}}
, which are related to the generalized Whittaker models of
\pi
. We first give a brief recapitulation.

Assume that
F
is a
p
-adic local field. Every irreducible admissible representation
(\pi,V_{\pi})
of
\overline{G}_{F}
defines a character distribution
\chi_{\pi}
in a neighborhood of
0
in
\mathfrak{g}_{F}={\rm Lie}(G_{F})
. Moreover, there exists a compact open subset
S_{\pi}
of
0
such that for every smooth function
f
with compact support in
S_{\pi}
, one has (see
[
How1
,
HC99
,
Li3
]
)

(1.1)

\chi_{\pi}(f)=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}}c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)\cdot\int\hat{f}\ \mu_{\mathcal{O}},

where
\mathcal{N}
denotes the set of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
under the conjugation action of
G_{F}
. Here
\mu_{\mathcal{O}}
is a certain Haar measure on
\mathcal{O}
properly normalized, and
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
with respect to the Cartan–Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
and a non-trivial character

\psi:F\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times};

one has
c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi):=c_{\mathcal{O},\psi}(\pi)\in\mathbbm{C}
. An analogue of (
1.1
) for
F=\mathbbm{R}
was given by Barbasch–Vogan
[
BV3
]
. Denote

\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}(\pi)=\left\{\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}:\ c_{\mathcal{O}}(\pi)\neq 0\right\}

and let

\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)\subset\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}(\pi)

be the subset consisting of all maximal elements in
\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}(\pi)
.

The set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)
gives the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension

d_{\rm GK}(\pi):=\frac{1}{2}\max\left\{\dim\mathcal{O}:\ \mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)\right\}

which satisfies

\dim\pi^{K}\approx{\rm vol}(K)^{-d_{\rm GK}(\pi)},

as the open compact subgroup
K\subset G
approaches to the identity, see
[
Sav94
]
. It is also known (see
[
MW1
,
Var0
,
Pate
]
) that the set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)
is equal to the set of maximal nilpotent orbits with respect to which the generalized Whittaker models for
\pi
are nontrivial.

More precisely, let
(f,h,u)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F}
be an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple. In this case,
h
is called a neutral element and it gives a filtration

\mathfrak{g}_{F}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbbm{Z}}\mathfrak{g}^{h}[i],

and
u\in\mathfrak{g}^{h}[-2]
.
Write

\mathfrak{n}_{h,u}:=\bigoplus_{i\geq 2}\mathfrak{g}^{h}[i]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F}

and let
N_{h,u}:=\exp(\mathfrak{n}_{h,u})\subset G_{F}
be the unipotent subgroup of
G_{F}
. There is a character
\psi_{u}:N_{h,u}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
given by

\psi_{u}(n)=\psi(\kappa(u,\log(n))),

where
\kappa:\mathfrak{g}_{F}\times\mathfrak{g}_{F}\to F
is the Killing form. For every
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G}_{F})
, the twisted Jacquet module is given by

\pi_{N_{h,u},\psi_{u}}:={\rm Hom}_{N_{h,u}}(\pi,\psi_{u}).

The above depends only on the
G_{F}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F}
of
u
, and thus we may write

\pi_{N_{\mathcal{O}},\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}=\pi_{N_{h,u},\psi_{u}}

for any choice of
u\in\mathcal{O}
. This gives

\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}(\pi)=\left\{\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F}:\ \pi_{N_{\mathcal{O}},\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}\neq 0\right\}

and we let
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)\subset\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}(\pi)
be the subset of maximal elements. Then it was shown in
[
MW1
,
Var0
,
Pate
]
that

–

\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)=\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
for every
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G}_{F})
, and

–

for every
\mathcal{O}
in the above (equal) sets, one has
c_{\mathcal{O}}=\dim_{\mathbbm{C}}\pi_{N_{\mathcal{O}},\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}
.

We call
\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)
and thus also
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
the wavefront set of
\pi
. For
\pi\in{\rm Irr}({\rm GL}_{r})
, a relation between the full set
\left\{c_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}(\pi)\right\}
and certain degenerate Whittaker models of
\pi
is given in the recent work of M. Gurevich
[
Gur23
]
.

If
F
is a number field, and
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F}
a nilpotent orbit, then for any genuine automorphic representation
\pi=\otimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of
\overline{G}_{\mathbb{A}}
, one can define the Whittaker–Fourier
\psi_{\mathcal{O}}
-coefficients of
\pi
. Moreover, one has the analogous
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}(\pi)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
of nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
associated with
\pi
. These two sets are intimately related to
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}(\pi_{v})
and
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi_{v})
. For an either local or global representation
\pi
, it is important to understand the set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
, as shown above. It also has deep applications, for example in the theory of descent and the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjectures and others, see
[
GRS11
,
FG18
,
JiZh20
]
and references therein.

Nevertheless, determining the set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
is a difficult problem for general
\pi
. For
{\rm GL}_{r}
, Ginzburg
[
Gin0
]
and Dihua Jiang
[
Jia14
]
gave several speculations for the set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
in terms of the Mœglin–Waldspurger classification of the spectrum of
{\rm GL}_{r}
(see
[
LX21
]
for some recent progress). For general
G
, the Arthur parametrization of certain unitary representations
\pi
is expected to encode information on
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
in terms of their Arthur parameters. This in part is tied closely with the local archimedean case as studied by Joseph, Barbasch, Vogan, Adams and many others. The development in the archimedean case was more satisfactory, as for example for complex Lie groups, the set
\mathcal{N}_{\rm tr}^{\rm max}(\pi)
is well-understood from Barbasch–Vogan
[
BV4
,
BV5
]
. For linear
G
, as alluded to above, Jiang
[
Jia14
]
formulated several conjectures regarding
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
using the parameter of
\pi
. There has been progress towards it as in
[
JiLi16
,
JiZh17
,
JL22
,
LS22a
,
LS22b
,
JLZ
]
, among many others. We also have the recent work by Ciubotaru, Mason-Brown and Okada towards understanding the stable wavefront sets and their refined versions, see
[
Oka
,
CMO
]
.

For covering groups, again, advance has been made more for the archimedean case, as can be seen from
[
ABPTV
,
Tsa19
]
and references therein. Some of these results rely on the theory of Harish-Chandra modules and their primitive ideals. The “easiest” genuine representation of a covering group
\overline{G}
is the theta representation
\Theta(\overline{G})
, a prototype of which is called the even Weil representation of the double cover
\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}^{(2)}
. It is desirable to have a full description of
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\Theta(\overline{G}))
. It was proved by Y.-Q. Cai and Savin that

(1.2)

\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\Theta(\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}))=\{(n^{a}b)\},

where
r=an+b
with
0\leqslant b<n
. For
n\geqslant r
, this recovers the earlier result of Kazhdan–Patterson on generic theta representations. Formulas analogous to (
1.2
) were proved for
\overline{{\rm SO}}_{2r+1}^{(4)}
and
\overline{{\rm GSpin}}_{2r}^{(2)}
, see
[
BFrG
,
BFG
]
and
[
Kap17-1
]
respectively. The case of
\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}^{(n)}
was also studied extensively by Friedberg and Ginzburg
[
FG15
,
FG17-2
,
FG18
,
FG20
]
.
In
[
GaTs
]
, a uniform but speculative formula of
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\Theta(\overline{G}))
was given for general
\overline{G}
, which was motivated from the works mentioned above.

It has been expected for a long time that over the algebraic closure the wavefront set is a singleton for general
\pi
. On the contrary, C.-C. Tsai gave a counter-example recently in
[
Tsa22
]
for certain epipelagic supercuspidal representations of
U_{7}
. However, we still expect,
as from
[
Oka
,
CMO
]
for linear groups,
that for the genuine Iwahori-spherical representations, in particular unramified genuine theta representations, the wavefront set is a singleton over
F^{\rm al}
.

1.1.
Main results

Although it is difficult to determine precisely
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
for general
\pi
, some work has been done in the literature to determine whether a candidate
\mathcal{O}
could possibly lie in
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\pi)
for some
\pi
.
Pertaining to this are the notions of admissible or quasi-admissible orbits. Equivalently, one has some sufficient condition for an orbit to lie in the complement set

(1.3)

\mathcal{N}-\bigcup_{\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G})}{\rm WF}(\pi),

especially when
\overline{G}=G
is a linear group.

For real Lie groups, the notion of admissibility was first proposed by Duflo
[
Duf80
]
.
This notion was studied both in the
p
-adic and the real setting in many works later, for example
[
Tor
,
Nev99
,
Nev02
]
, and we refer the reader to loc. cit. for more extensive references and historical discussion. Utilizing a more general “Whittaker pair”, the recent work by Gomez, Gourevitch and Sahi in
[
GGS17
,
GGS21
]
enables one to consider degenerate Whittaker models, where the discussion on admissibility and quasi-admissibility of nilpotent orbits was framed generally and applies easily to covering groups as well.

Closely related to admissible (or slightly weaker, quasi-admissible) orbits is the notion of special orbits, which correspond to the special Weyl group representations via the Springer correspondence. It was shown by Mœglin
[
Moe96
]
that for classical groups, every admissible orbit has to be special. Thus, a leading wavefront orbit for classical linear algebraic groups is necessarily a special orbit. This fact fails for covering groups, as seen already from the three-fold cover of
G_{2}
and many covering groups considered in this paper. In fact, in a different direction, Jiang, Savin and the second-named author considered in
[
JLS
]
the notion of raisability of nilpotent orbits which equally applies to covering groups as well. Roughly speaking, if a nilpotent orbit is raisable, then it must lie in the set (
1.3
).

Our present paper is motivated from the works above. It could be in part considered as an application and explication of
[
GGS21
,
JLS
]
in the covering setting, and in part as a sequel to
[
GaTs
]
. Below we give an elaboration of this and also state our main results.

First, by applying and analyzing the techniques from
[
GGS17
,
GGS21
]
and
[
JLS
]
, we determine the orbits
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F}
which are
\overline{G}_{F}^{(n)}
-quasi-admissible or
\overline{G}_{F}^{(n)}
-raisable. As alluded to above, the importance of these two notions is the following:

–

if
\mathcal{O}
is
\overline{G}^{(n)}
-raisable or not
\overline{G}^{(n)}
-quasi-admissible, then it does not lie in
\mathcal{N}^{\rm max}_{\rm Wh}(\pi)
for any
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G}^{(n)})
.

For technical reasons, we only consider
F
-split nilpotent orbits. In §
2
, we consider general
\overline{G}
and give equivalent criteria for quasi-admissibility which are amenable to explicit verification, similarly for raisability; see Propositions
2.4
,
2.5
and Proposition
2.10
.

In §
3
, applying results from §
2
, we analyze groups of each Cartan type and determine the quasi-admissibility and raisability of each
F
-split orbit
\mathcal{O}
of
G
, whenever possible.

2.
Quasi-admissible and raisable orbits

2.1.
Covering groups

Let
F
be a number field or a local field. Let
{\bf G}
be a connected split linear reductive group over
F
. Denote by

(X,\ \Phi,\ \Delta;\ Y,\Phi^{\vee},\Delta^{\vee})

the root datum of
{\bf G}
, where
X
is the character lattice and
Y
the cocharacter lattice of a split torus
{\bf T}\subset{\bf G}
. Here
\Delta
is a choice of simple roots, and we denote by
Y^{sc}\subset Y
the coroot lattice and
X^{sc}\subset X
the root lattice. Denote by
W
the Weyl group of the coroot system.

Let

Q:Y\longrightarrow\mathbbm{Z}

be a Weyl-invariant quadratic form, and let
B_{Q}
be the associated bilinear form.
Assume that
F^{\times}
contains the full group
\mu_{n}
of
n
-th roots of unity. Consider the pair
(D,\eta={\bf 1})
, where
D
is a “bisector” of
Q
(see
[
GG
, §2.6]
). If
F
is a local field with
G_{F}:={\bf G}(F)
, then associated to
(D,{\bf 1})
one has a covering group
\overline{G}_{F}
, which is a central extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}_{F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{F}}

of
G
by
\mu_{n}
. For simplicity, we may also write
\overline{G}:=\overline{G}_{F}
. If
F
is a number field, then one has a global
n
-fold central cover
\overline{G}_{\mathbb{A}}
of
G_{\mathbb{A}}
. For more details, see
[
BD
,
GG
,
We6
]
. Throughout, for every root
\alpha
we denote

(2.1)

n_{\alpha}:=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,Q(\alpha^{\vee}))}.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
G_{der}
is almost simple. Then the number

{\rm Inv}_{\rm BD}(\overline{G}):=Q(\alpha^{\vee})\in\mathbbm{Z},

where
\alpha^{\vee}
is any short coroot, is called the Brylinski–Deligne invariant associated with
\overline{G}
.

Note that the Brylinski–Deligne invariant does not depend on
n
. Moreover, it behaves functorially as follows. Let
\zeta:{\bf G}\longrightarrow{\bf H}
be an algebraic group homomorphism. It induces a group homomorphism
\zeta^{\natural}:Y_{G}\longrightarrow Y_{H}
on the cocharacter lattices of
{\bf G}
and
{\bf H}
. Let
\overline{H}
be an
n
-fold cover of
H
associated with a quadratic form

. The pull-back
n
-fold cover
\zeta^{*}(\overline{H})
of
G
via
\zeta
is associated with

. In particular, we have

{\rm Inv}_{\rm BD}(\zeta^{*}(\overline{H}))=Q\circ\zeta^{\natural}(\alpha^{\vee}_{G}),

where
\alpha^{\vee}_{G}
is any short coroot of
G
.

2.2.
Nilpotent orbits and Whittaker pairs

Let
F
be a number field or a local field.
For the terminology and notations in this subsection, we follow
[
GGS17
,
GGS21
]
.
In particular, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
over
F
has a standard basis
\left\{e_{+},h_{0},e_{-}\right\}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
denotes the Lie algebra of
G_{F}
.

Let
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{F}
be a nilpotent element. Given a semisimple element
h\in\mathfrak{g}_{F}
, one has a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}_{F}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathfrak{g}^{h}[i],

where
I\subset F
and
\mathfrak{g}^{h}[i]
denotes the
i
-th eigenspace of the adjoint action of
h
on
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
. The element
h\in\mathfrak{g}_{F}
is called
\mathbbm{Q}
-semisimple if

I\subset\mathbbm{Q}.

The pair
(h,u)
is called a Whittaker pair if
h
is
\mathbbm{Q}
-semisimple and
u\in\mathfrak{g}^{h}[-2]
. A Whittaker pair is called a neutral pair if there exists a nilpotent element
f\in\mathfrak{g}_{F}
such that

(f,h,u)

is an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple, in which case
h
is called a neutral element for
u
. For any nilpotent
u
, the Jacobson–Morozov theorem gives a homomorphism

\gamma:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{F}

such that
u=\gamma(e_{-})
. Conversely, naturally associated to any
\gamma
is an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple

\left\{f,h,u\right\}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F},

where
h
is then a neutral element for
u
.

Let

\kappa:\mathfrak{g}_{F}\times\mathfrak{g}_{F}\longrightarrow F

be the Killing form. For any Whittaker pair
(h,u)
, one has a symplectic form

\omega_{u}:\mathfrak{g}_{F}\times\mathfrak{g}_{F}\longrightarrow F

given by
\omega_{u}(x,y):=\kappa(u,[x,y])
. For any rational
i\in\mathbbm{Q}
, let

The restriction

be the radical of

By

. If the Whittaker pair

Let

.
Let

be a nontrivial character. Define a character of

Let

.
Every unipotent subgroup splits canonically into

. Thus, we view all the above

.

If

is a local field and
\pi
an irreducible genuine representation of

, then the degenerate Whittaker model of

associated to any Whittaker pair

is

the twisted Jacquet module of

with respect to

. If

is a neutral pair, then

. For any nilpotent orbit

, we write

\pi_{N_{\mathcal{O}},\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}=\pi_{N_{h,u},\psi_{u}}

for any

and any choice

of neutral pair. Denote

and let

be the subset consisting of maximal elements.

Now assume

is a number field and
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{F}
. Let
\psi:F\backslash\mathbb{A}\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
be a nontrivial additive character. One can extend the Killing form to obtain

. This gives a character

). Here

is automorphic, i.e., trivial on

and hence can be viewed as a character on

Let

be an irreducible genuine automorphic representation of

. For any

For any Whittaker pair

If
(h,u)
is a neutral pair, then again
\mathcal{F}_{s,u}(\phi)
is called a generalized Whittaker–Fourier coefficient of

.
For any orbit

with

Similarly, we have the subset of maximal elements

[

,

]

of

-adic local field.

2.3.

-split nilpotent orbits

Let

be an

-triple. Let

. Viewing

where

where the

-rational orbits

are classified by the Galois cohomology group

see
[

, Chap. III]
and
[

, §4]
. This group is only a pointed set if

is not abelian.

Consider the connected component group

\pi_{0}({\bf G}_{\gamma})={\bf G}_{\gamma}/{\bf G}_{\gamma,0}

of
{\bf G}_{\gamma}
. This group is one of the symmetric groups

; moreover, for classical groups it is either trivial or

.
One has an exact sequence of pointed sets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(F,{\bf G}_{\gamma,0})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

is injective.

The orbit

is called an
F
-split nilpotent orbit if the reductive group
{\bf G}_{\gamma}
is split over
F
. In this paper, we only consider

-split orbits, since the computation with covers of a non-split

involves further subtlety. Henceforth, we will assume that the orbit

is always

-split without explicating this again.

In this paper, we will implement the methods of Mœglin
[
Moe96
]
, Nevins
[

,
Nev02
]
, Jiang–Liu–Savin
[

]
and Gomez–Gourevitch–Sahi

]
to give some necessary or sufficient condition for an orbit

to possibly lie in the wavefront set of some genuine representation

. In fact, for general

we determine (if possible) the

-split orbits of
\mathfrak{g}_{F}
that can not be the wavefront set of any genuine representation

. More precisely, we consider necessary conditions for an

-split orbit

to be quasi-admissible in the sense of
[

]
, and also give sufficient conditions for it to be raisable, a notion due to
[

]
. All these together will enable us to determine the subset of the orbits in

which never occur in the wavefront set of any

Some results here also hold in the global setting for genuine automorphic representations of
\overline{G}_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Remark 2.6
.

In general, consider
{\bf f}:{\bf G}_{sc}\twoheadrightarrow{\bf G}
, the simply-connected cover of a semi-simple group
{\bf G}
, which gives

. There exists a non-split extension of

whose pull-back is split. For example, consider

and thus

. Then one has

, where we identify

with the center of

. Consider the double cover

is given by

. It is easy to see that the pull-back of

to

is a split extension. However,

splits over

if and only if

. In fact,
\overline{G}_{der}^{(2)}
is a Brylinski–Deligne cover associated with
(D=0,\eta)
, and is the running example for several interesting phenomena discussed in
[

]
.

However, in the notation of Proposition

, we believe that the splitting of
\overline{G}_{\gamma,sc}^{(k)}
is always equivalent to the splitting of
\overline{G}_{\gamma,der}^{(k)}
, without the assumption imposed there. That is, the covers
\overline{G}_{\gamma,der}^{(k)}
that arise are not of the type in the preceding paragraph, essentially due to the fact that we consider in this paper covers associated with
(D,\eta={\bf 1})
. We are not able to prove this expectation in full generality, though.

The importance of the quasi-admissibility is given as follows.

(

The proof of

Thus, suppose

4.
Wavefront sets of theta representations

In this section, we consider theta representations
\Theta(\nu)
of
\overline{G}^{(n)}
, where
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
is a certain exceptional vector. We compute explicitly the
F^{\rm al}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\tilde{\nu}}}^{W}\varepsilon_{\tilde{\nu}})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F^{\rm al}}
, which is expected to be the single element in
\mathcal{N}_{\rm Wh}^{\rm max}(\Theta(\nu))\otimes F^{\rm al}
. We show that a method of determining
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\tilde{\nu}}}^{W}\varepsilon_{\tilde{\nu}})\subset\mathfrak{g}_{F^{\rm al}}
is given by Sommers’ duality
[
Som01
]
between nilpotent orbits, which generalizes the classical Barbasch–Vogan duality. With an explicit computation, we also determine the quasi-admissibility and non-raisability of such orbits.

4.1.
Theta representation

We introduce theta representations following the notation and exposition in
[
GaTs
]
.

Let
\overline{T}\subset\overline{G}
be the covering torus of
\overline{G}
. We assume that there exists a certain distinguished (finite-dimensional) genuine representation
\pi^{\dagger}
of
\overline{T}
determined by a distinguished genuine central character
\chi^{\dagger}
of
Z(\overline{T})
, see
[
GG
, §6–7]
.
For every
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
, there is a map

\delta_{\nu}:T\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}

given by

\delta_{\nu}(y\otimes a)=|a|_{F}^{\nu(y)}

on the generators
y\otimes a\in T
, where
\nu(y)
is the natural pairing between
Y
and
X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
.
For every
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
, denote by

I(\pi^{\dagger},\nu):={\rm Ind}_{\overline{B}}^{\overline{G}}(\pi^{\dagger}\otimes\delta_{\nu})

the normalized induced principal series representation of
\overline{G}
.

A vector
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
is called an exceptional character if
\nu(\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee})=1
for every
\alpha\in\Delta
. Here
\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee}:=n_{\alpha}\cdot\alpha^{\vee}
.
It follows from the Langlands classification theorem for covers (see
[
BJ1
]
) that if
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
is exceptional, then we have

I(\pi^{\dagger},\nu)\twoheadrightarrow\Theta(\pi^{\dagger},\nu),

where
\Theta(\pi^{\dagger},\nu)
is the unique Langlands quotient of
I(\pi^{\dagger},\nu)
. We may write
\Theta(\overline{G},\nu),\Theta(\nu)
or
\Theta(\overline{G})
for
\Theta(\pi^{\dagger},\nu)
, whenever the emphasis is different; but the dependence on
\pi^{\dagger}
and exceptional
\nu
are both understood.

A covering group
\overline{G}
is called saturated (see
[
Ga6
, Definition 2.1]
) if

Y^{sc}\cap Y_{Q,n}=Y_{Q,n}^{sc},

where the one-sided inclusion
\supset
always holds.
If
G
is semisimple and simply-connected, then
G
is saturated if and only if its dual group
\overline{G}^{\vee}
is of adjoint type, i.e.,
Y_{Q,n}=Y_{Q,n}^{sc}
.
In general, for every
\alpha\in\Phi
one has

\mathbbm{Z}[\alpha^{\vee}]\cap Y_{Q,n}=\mathbbm{Z}[i_{\alpha}\cdot\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee}]\text{ with }i_{\alpha}\in\left\{1,1/2\right\},

and
i_{\alpha}=1/2
only if
n_{\alpha}
is even. Set

\tilde{n}_{\alpha}=i_{\alpha}\cdot n_{\alpha},\ \tilde{\alpha}_{Q,n}^{\vee}=\tilde{n}_{\alpha}\cdot\alpha^{\vee},\text{ and }\tilde{\alpha}_{Q,n}=\alpha/\tilde{n}_{\alpha}

for every
\alpha\in\Phi
, and

\tilde{\Phi}_{Q,n}^{\vee}:=\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{Q,n}^{\vee}:\ \alpha\in\Phi\right\}.

Let

\tilde{Y}_{Q,n}^{sc}\subset Y_{Q,n}

be the sublattice spanned by
\tilde{\Phi}_{Q,n}^{\vee}
, and we call it the saturation of
Y_{Q,n}^{sc}
. One has

Y_{Q,n}^{sc}\subset\tilde{Y}_{Q,n}^{sc}\subset Y_{Q,n},

and if
\overline{G}
is saturated, then
Y_{Q,n}^{sc}=\tilde{Y}_{Q,n}^{sc}
.

An element
{\tilde{\nu}}\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
is called a saturation of an exceptional character
\nu\in X\otimes\mathbbm{R}
if
\tilde{\nu}(\tilde{\alpha}_{Q,n}^{\vee})=1
for every
\alpha\in\Delta
.
If
\overline{G}
is saturated, then
\phi({\tilde{\nu}})=\phi(\nu)\in{\rm Hom}(Y_{Q,n}^{sc},\mathbbm{R})
for every saturation
{\tilde{\nu}}
of an exceptional
\nu
.

We also recall the notion of a persistent cover as follows (see
[
Ga6
, Definition 2.3]
). Consider

\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc}:=Y/Y_{Q,n}^{sc},\ \mathscr{X}_{Q,n}:=Y/Y_{Q,n},

which are both endowed with the twisted Weyl action

w[y]:=w(y-\rho^{\vee})+\rho^{\vee}

for every
w
in the Weyl group
W
.
Here
\rho^{\vee}
is the half sum of all positive coroots in
\Phi^{\vee}
. For every
y\in Y
, let
y^{\dagger}
and
y^{\ddagger}
denote its image in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc}
and
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
respectively. An
n
-fold cover
\overline{G}
is called persistent if

{\rm Stab}_{W}(y^{\dagger};\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc})={\rm Stab}_{W}(y^{\ddagger};\mathscr{X}_{Q,n})

for every
y\in Y
. A saturated cover is always persistent.

For every

The MacDonald–Lusztig–Spaltenstein

Let

{\bf G}
over the algebraically closed field

depends only on the conjugacy class

of

to denote

Defining

the Springer correspondence gives an injective map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Spr}^{\bf G}:{\rm Irr}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}^{\rm en}({\bf G})}

denoted by

{\rm Spr}^{\bf G}(\sigma)=(\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}^{\bf G}(\sigma),\eta(\sigma)).

If no confusion arises, we will write

\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}=\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}^{\bf G}

, and
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}^{\bf G}(\varepsilon_{W})=\mathcal{O}_{0}

One has the permutation representation

4.3.
Method of computation

Here, we want to compute the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}^{\bf G}(j_{W_{{\tilde{\nu}}}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{{\tilde{\nu}}}))
explicitly for all covers, at least when
G
is almost simple and simply-connected or is of the classical type. We show that the method of computation is reduced to the Sommers’ duality as in
[
Som01
]
.

For any linear algebraic group
{\bf G}
, we consider another enhanced set of nilpotent orbits

\mathcal{N}^{\rm en}_{\rm geo}({\bf G}):=\left\{(\mathcal{O},\mathfrak{c}):\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}({\bf G})\text{ and }\mathfrak{c}\in{\rm Conj}(A_{\mathcal{O}})\right\},

where
{\rm Conj}(A_{\mathcal{O}})
denotes the set of conjugacy classes of
A_{\mathcal{O}}
. There is a quotient map

A_{\mathcal{O}}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{A}_{\mathcal{O}},

where
\tilde{A}_{\mathcal{O}}
is the Lusztig canonical quotient, see
[
Som01
, §5]
. This gives a set

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\rm en}_{\rm geo}({\bf G}):=\left\{(\mathcal{O},\tilde{\mathfrak{c}}):\mathcal{O}\in\mathcal{N}({\bf G})\text{ and }\tilde{\mathfrak{c}}\in{\rm Conj}(\tilde{A}_{\mathcal{O}})\right\}

together with a natural surjection

f:\mathcal{N}^{\rm en}_{\rm geo}({\bf G})\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\rm en}_{\rm geo}({\bf G})

given by
(\mathcal{O},\mathfrak{c})\mapsto(\mathcal{O},\tilde{\mathfrak{c}})
where
\tilde{\mathfrak{c}}
is the image of
\mathfrak{c}
.

Recall that there is a natural order-reversing bijection on special orbits
\mathcal{N}^{\rm spe}({\bf G})\subset\mathcal{N}({\bf G})

d_{\rm LS}:\mathcal{N}({\bf G})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{N}^{\rm spe}({\bf G}).

Now we have the various extensions of
d_{\rm LS}
as depicted in the following commutative diagram:

(4.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}^{\rm spe}({\bf G}^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}({\bf G}^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\rm en}_{\rm geo}({\bf G}^{\vee})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}({\bf G})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}^{\rm en}_{\rm geo}({\bf G})}

The top inclusion in (

What pertains to our work is the map

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\overline{G}
be any of the covering
\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)},\overline{{\rm SO}}_{2r+1}^{(n)},\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}^{(n)},\overline{{\rm SO}}_{2r}^{(n)},\overline{G}_{2}^{(n)},\overline{F}_{4}^{(n)}
and
\overline{E}_{r}^{(n)},6\leqslant r\leqslant 8
discussed above. Assume that
\overline{G}
is persistent. Consider the
F
-split orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Theta}
of type
\mathcal{O}_{\rm Spr}(j_{W_{\tilde{\nu}}}^{W}\varepsilon_{\tilde{\nu}})
of
G
.

(i)

The orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Theta}
is quasi-admissible and non-raisable.

(ii)

If the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Theta}
is the regular orbit of a Levi subgroup of
G
, then it supports certain generalized Whittaker models of the theta representation
\Theta(\overline{G},\nu)
.

Proof.

First, (i) follows from comparing Tables 6–11 with Theorems
3.1
,
3.3
,
3.4
and Tables 1–5.

For (ii), recall from
[
GGS17
]
that to any
\pi\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G})
and any Whittaker pair
(S,u)
of
\mathcal{O}_{u}
, there is a degenerate Whittaker model
\pi_{S,u}
such that one has a
\overline{G}
-equivariant surjection

\pi_{N_{\mathcal{O}},\psi_{\mathcal{O}}}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{S,u},

see
[
GGS17
, Theorem A]
. If
\mathcal{O}_{u}
is the split regular orbit of a Levi subgroup
L
, then one can pick
(S,u)
such that
\pi_{S,u}
is equal to the semi-Whittaker models of
\pi
, i.e., the Whittaker model of the Jacquet model
J_{U}(\pi)
with respect to the unipotent radical
U
of the parabolic subgroup
P=LU
.

Applying the above to
\Theta(\overline{G},\nu)
, by the periodicity of theta representations (see
[
BFrG2
, Theorem 2.3]
), one has
J_{U}(\Theta(\overline{G},\nu))
is a theta representation on the Levi subgroup
\overline{L}
. Thus, it suffices to show that every theta representation
\Theta(\overline{L}_{\mathcal{O}})
on the Levi subgroup
\overline{L}_{\mathcal{O}}
associated to such
\mathcal{O}_{\Theta}
is generic. It follows from
[
Ga6
, Proposition 6.2]
that

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{L}_{\mathcal{O}}))={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W(L_{\mathcal{O}})},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W(L_{\mathcal{O}})},

where
W(L_{\mathcal{O}})
denotes the Weyl group of
L_{\mathcal{O}}
. Moreover, an explicit numerical criterion on the non-vanishing of
\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{L}_{\mathcal{O}}))
is given in
[
GaTs
, §3]
. The result then follows from a direct check by using results in loc. cit. and Tables 6–11. We will illustrate this by considering
\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}^{(n)}
and
\overline{E}_{8}^{(n)}
as examples.

n
is odd or

with

even. If

3.5

\mathcal{O}_{\Theta}

5.
The coefficient
c_{\mathcal{O}}
for
\Theta(\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)})

In this last section, we verify the equality (
4.4
) in Conjecture
4.1
regarding the leading coefficient
c_{\mathcal{O}}
for theta representation of covers of
{\rm GL}_{r}
. Note that the equality (
4.3
) for
\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
is due to Savin
[
Sav2
]
and Cai
[
Cai19
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider a Kazhdan–Patterson cover
\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
. Then for every unramified theta representation
\Theta(\pi^{\dagger},\nu)
, one has

c_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}),\varepsilon_{W}\otimes\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W},

where
\mathcal{O}=(n^{a}b)
is the unique orbit in
\mathcal{N}^{\rm max}_{\rm tr}(\Theta(\pi^{\dagger},\nu))
with
r=na+b,0\leqslant b<n
.

Proof.

For any partition
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(r)
of
r
, we denote by
M_{\mu}\subset{\rm GL}_{r}
the standard Levi subgroup. The Weyl group of
M_{\mu}
is denoted by
W_{\mu}\subset W
. We write

\lambda:=(n^{a}b)\in\mathscr{P}(r).

Since
\mathcal{N}^{\rm max}_{\rm tr}(\Theta(\pi^{\dagger},\nu))=\left\{\lambda\right\}
, then
[
Pate
]
and
[
GGS21
, Theorem 1.5]
together with the periodicity of theta representations (see
[
Cai19
]
) give the first equality in

c_{\mathcal{O}}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{M}_{\lambda}^{(n)}))={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda}},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W_{\lambda}}={\left\langle{\rm Ind}_{W_{\lambda}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda}}),\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W},

where the theta representation
\overline{M}_{\lambda}^{(n)}
is associated with
\pi^{\dagger}
and
\nu
, and the second equality follows from
[
Ga6
, Proposition 6.2]
.
On the other hand, the Weyl subgroup
W_{\nu}
is a parabolic Weyl subgroup associated with

\lambda^{\top}=((a+1)^{b}a^{n-b}).

Thus,

\displaystyle{\left\langle j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}),\varepsilon_{W}\otimes\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}

\displaystyle={\left\langle j_{W_{\lambda^{\top}}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda^{\top}}}),\varepsilon_{W}\otimes\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}

\displaystyle={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W}\otimes j_{W_{\lambda^{\top}}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda^{\top}}}),\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}

\displaystyle={\left\langle j_{W_{\lambda}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda}}),\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W},

where the last equality follows from
[
GePf
, Corollary 5.4.9]
.

We have

{\rm Ind}_{W_{\lambda}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda}})=j_{W_{\lambda}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\lambda}})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\leqslant\mu\\
\mu\neq\lambda\end{subarray}}j_{W_{\mu}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\mu}}),

see
[
GePf
, Theorem 5.4.7]
. Also, for any
\mu>\lambda
,

{\left\langle j_{W_{\mu}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\mu}}),\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}\leqslant{\left\langle{\rm Ind}_{W_{\mu}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\mu}}),\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W_{\mu}},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W_{\mu}}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{M}_{\mu})),

where the last equality follows from
[
Ga6
]
. Since
\mu>\lambda
, there is a component in the partition of
\mu
strictly greater than
n
and thus one has

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{M}_{\mu}))=0.

All the above together give that
c_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle j_{W_{\nu}}^{W}(\varepsilon_{W_{\nu}}),\varepsilon_{W}\otimes\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that the restriction to Kazhdan–Patterson cover of
{\rm GL}_{r}
is only for convenience. Results in Theorem
5.1
and other parts of paper hold for general Brylinski–Deligne covers of
{\rm GL}_{r}
, where the only essential alternation in the statement is to replace
n
by
n_{\alpha}
.

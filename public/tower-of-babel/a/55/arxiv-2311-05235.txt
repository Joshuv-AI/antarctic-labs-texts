Categorical isomorphisms for Hopf braces


Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the category of brace triples in a braided monoidal setting and prove that it is isomorphic to the category of s -Hopf braces, which are a generalization of cocommutative Hopf braces. After that, we obtain a categorical isomorphism between finite cocommutative Hopf braces and a certain subcategory of cocommutative post-Hopf algebras, which suppose an expansion to the braided monoidal setting of the equivalence obtained by Y. Li, Y. Sheng and R. Tang in [ 13 ] for the category of vector spaces over a field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} .

Introduction

Hopf braces were born in
[
2
]
as the quantum version of skew braces, introduced by L. Guarnieri and L. Vendramin in
[
8
]
. The importance of these objects is fundamentally due to the fact that they provide solutions of the Quantum Yang-Baxter equation, that is a relevant subject in mathematical physics (see
[
18
]
and
[
4
]
). Despite the simplicity of its formulation, finding solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation is not an easy task. In fact, the problem of classifying all the solutions of the equation is still open and different approaches have been proposed since the end of the last century. One of them was proposed by Drinfel’d in
[
5
]
, that consists on studying non-degenerate set-theoretical solutions. Research into this kind of solutions with the involutive property was what gave rise to the concept of brace introduced by Rump in
[
16
]
for which skew braces are a generalization. So, a skew brace consists on two different group structures,
(G,.)
and
(G,\star)
, satisfying the following compatibility condition

(1)

g\star(h.t)=(g\star h).g^{-1}.(g\star t)

for all
g,h,t\in G
, where
g^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
g
with respect to the group structure
(G,.)
. These structures are useful to find non-degenerate solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation not neccesarily involutive. The linearization of skew braces give rise to the notion of Hopf brace defined by I. Angiono, C. Galindo and L. Vendramin in
[
2
]
: If
(H,\epsilon,\Delta)
is a coalgebra, a Hopf brace structure over
H
consists on two different Hopf algebra structures,

H_{1}=(H,1,\cdot,\epsilon,\Delta,\lambda),\;\;\;H_{2}=(H,1_{\circ},\circ,\epsilon,\Delta,S),

where
\lambda
and
S
denote the antipodes, satisfying the following compatibility condition

g\circ(h\cdot t)=(g_{1}\circ h)\cdot\lambda(g_{2})\cdot(g_{3}\circ t)\quad\forall g,h,t\in H

which generalizes (
1
). Moreover, as was pointed in
[
2
, Corollary 2.4]
, cocommutative Hopf braces give rise to solutions of Yang-Baxter equation too.

On the other hand, without going into detail, it is not irrelevant to highlight the relationship between Hopf braces and invertible 1-cocycles, which are nothing more than coalgebra isomorphisms between two different Hopf algebras,
\pi\colon H\rightarrow B
, such that
B
is a
H
-module algebra. In
[
2
, Theorem 1.12]
it is proved that the category of Hopf braces with
H_{1}
fixed is equivalent to the category of invertible 1-cocycles
\pi\colon H_{1}\rightarrow B
. Moreover, this result remains valid in the case that
H_{1}
is not fixed (see
[
6
]
).

Therefore, motivated by the fact that cocommutative Hopf braces induce solutions of Quantum Yang-Baxter equation, in this paper we study another objects that characterise the structure of Hopf braces in the cocommutative setting. So, given
\sf{C}
a braided monoidal category, in Section
2
we introduce the category of brace triples (see Definition
2.1
) and the category of
s
-Hopf braces (see Definition
2.6
). Note that
s
-Hopf braces generalize cocommutative Hopf braces because both categories are the same under cocommutativity assumption (see Remark
2.9
). After that, a functor from brace triples to
s
-Hopf braces is constructed explicitly (see Theorem
2.10
), and another from
s
-Hopf braces to brace triples (see Theorem
2.17
), ending the section with the main Theorem
2.18
where we prove that the previous correspondence give rise to a categorical isomorphism. As a consequence (see Corollary
2.19
), we obtain a categorical isomorphism between cocommutative Hopf braces and cocommutative brace triples, that is to say, a cocommutative Hopf brace is no more than a cocommutative Hopf algebra
H
together with a pair of morphisms,
T_{H}\colon H\rightarrow H
and
\gamma_{H}\colon H\otimes H\rightarrow H
, satisfying some compatibility conditions between them and the Hopf algebra structure over
H
.

In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of post-Hopf algebra in a braided monoidal category
\sf{C}
(see Definition
3.1
), which generalizes the one introduced by Y. Li, Y. Sheng and R. Tang in
[
13
]
for a category of vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{K}
(see also
[
3
]
). After proving some interesting properties of these objects that can be deducted from the definition, we obtain a functor from finite brace triples to post-Hopf algebras (see Theorem
3.6
). At this point, hypothesis of cocommutativity acquires significant importance and it is essential, together with technical condition (
32
), to prove the existence of a functor from the category of cocommutative post-Hopf algebras that satisfy (
32
) to the category of finite cocommutative Hopf braces (see Theorem
3.15
). Therefore, Theorem
3.16
is the main result of this section, where we prove that this correspondence induces a categorical isomorphism between
\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}
, the category of cocommutative post-Hopf algebras satisfying (
32
), and finite cocommutative brace triples. So, as a consequence,
\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}
, finite cocommutative brace triples and finite cocommutative Hopf braces are isomorphic, which suppose a generalization of
[
13
, Theorem 2.13]
to the braided monoidal setting.

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper we are going to denote by
{\sf C}
a strict braided monoidal category with tensor product
\otimes
, unit object
K
and braiding
c
.

As can be found in
[
14
]
, a monoidal category is a category
{\sf C}
together with a functor
\otimes:{\sf C}\times{\sf C}\rightarrow{\sf C}
, called tensor product, an object
K
of
{\sf C}
, called the unit object, and families of natural isomorphisms

a_{M,N,P}:(M\otimes N)\otimes P\rightarrow M\otimes(N\otimes P),\;\;\;r_{M}:M\otimes K\rightarrow M,\;\;\;l_{M}:K\otimes M\rightarrow M,

in
{\sf C}
, called associativity, right unit and left unit constraints, respectively, which satisfy the Pentagon Axiom and the Triangle Axiom, i.e.,

a_{M,N,P\otimes Q}\circ a_{M\otimes N,P,Q}=(id_{M}\otimes a_{N,P,Q})\circ a_{M,N\otimes P,Q}\circ(a_{M,N,P}\otimes id_{Q}),

(id_{M}\otimes l_{N})\circ a_{M,K,N}=r_{M}\otimes id_{N},

where for each object
X
in
{\sf C}
,
id_{X}
denotes the identity morphism of
X
. A monoidal category is called strict if the previous constraints are identities. It is an important result (see for example
[
12
]
) that every non-strict monoidal category is monoidal equivalent to a strict one, so the strict character can be assumed without loss of generality. Then, results proved in a strict setting hold for every non-strict monoidal category that include, between others, the category
{\mathbb{K}}
-
Vect
of vector spaces over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, the category
R
-
Mod
of left modules over a commutative ring
R
or the category of sets,
\sf{Set}
.
For
simplicity of notation, given objects
M
,
N
,
P
in
{\sf C}
and a morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
, in most cases we will write
P\otimes f
for
id_{P}\otimes f
and
f\otimes P
for
f\otimes id_{P}
.

A braiding for a strict monoidal category
{\sf C}
is a natural family of isomorphisms

c_{M,N}:M\otimes N\rightarrow N\otimes M

subject to the conditions

c_{M,N\otimes P}=(N\otimes c_{M,P})\circ(c_{M,N}\otimes P),\;\;c_{M\otimes N,P}=(c_{M,P}\otimes N)\circ(M\otimes c_{N,P}).

A strict braided monoidal category
{\sf C}
is a strict monoidal category with a braiding. These categories were introduced by Joyal and Street in
[
10
]
(see also
[
11
]
) motivated by the theory of braids and links in topology. Note that, as a consequence of the definition, the equalities
c_{M,K}=c_{K,M}=id_{M}
hold, for all object
M
of
{\sf C}
. Moreover, if
\sf{C}
is braided with braiding
c
, then
\sf{C}
is also braided with braiding
c^{-1}
. We will denote by
\overline{\sf{C}}
the category
\sf{C}
with braiding
c^{-1}
.

If the braiding satisfies that
c_{N,M}\circ c_{M,N}=id_{M\otimes N},
for all
M
,
N
in
{\sf C}
, we will say that
{\sf C}
is symmetric. In this case, we call the braiding
c
a symmetry for the category
{\sf C}
.

In the following definitions we sum up some basic notions in the braided monoidal setting.

Definition 1.1
.

An algebra in
{\sf C}
is a triple
A=(A,\eta_{A},\mu_{A})
where
A
is an object in
{\sf C}
and

\eta_{A}:K\rightarrow A
(unit),
\mu_{A}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
(product) are morphisms in
{\sf C}
such that

\mu_{A}\circ(A\otimes\eta_{A})=id_{A}=\mu_{A}\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes A)
,
\mu_{A}\circ(A\otimes\mu_{A})=\mu_{A}\circ(\mu_{A}\otimes A)
. Given two algebras
A=(A,\eta_{A},\mu_{A})

and
B=(B,\eta_{B},\mu_{B})
, a morphism
f:A\rightarrow B
in
C
is an algebra morphism if
\mu_{B}\circ(f\otimes f)=f\circ\mu_{A}
,
f\circ\eta_{A}=\eta_{B}
.

If
A
,
B
are algebras in
{\sf C}
, the tensor product

A\otimes B
is also an algebra in

{\sf C}
where

\eta_{A\otimes B}=\eta_{A}\otimes\eta_{B}
and
\mu_{A\otimes B}=(\mu_{A}\otimes\mu_{B})\circ(A\otimes c_{B,A}\otimes B).

Definition 1.2
.

A coalgebra in
{\sf C}
is a triple
{D}=(D,\varepsilon_{D},\delta_{D})
where
D
is an object in
{\sf C}
and
\varepsilon_{D}:D\rightarrow K
(counit),

\delta_{D}:D\rightarrow D\otimes D
(coproduct) are morphisms in

{\sf C}
such that
(\varepsilon_{D}\otimes D)\circ\delta_{D}=id_{D}=(D\otimes\varepsilon_{D})\circ\delta_{D}
,

(\delta_{D}\otimes D)\circ\delta_{D}=(D\otimes\delta_{D})\circ\delta_{D}.
If
{D}=(D,\varepsilon_{D},\delta_{D})
and

{E}=(E,\varepsilon_{E},\delta_{E})
are coalgebras, a morphism

f:D\rightarrow E
in
C
is a coalgebra morphism if
(f\otimes f)\circ\delta_{D}=\delta_{E}\circ f
,
\varepsilon_{E}\circ f=\varepsilon_{D}.

Given
D
,
E
coalgebras in
{\sf C}
, the tensor product
D\otimes E
is a
coalgebra in
{\sf C}
where
\varepsilon_{D\otimes E}=\varepsilon_{D}\otimes\varepsilon_{E}
and
\delta_{D\otimes E}=(D\otimes c_{D,E}\otimes E)\circ(\delta_{D}\otimes\delta_{E}).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
{D}=(D,\varepsilon_{D},\delta_{D})
be a coalgebra and
A=(A,\eta_{A},\mu_{A})
an
algebra in
\sf{C}
. By
{\mathcal{H}}(D,A)
we denote the set of morphisms

f:D\rightarrow A
in
{\sf C}
. With the convolution operation

f\ast g=\mu_{A}\circ(f\otimes g)\circ\delta_{D}
,
{\mathcal{H}}(D,A)
is an algebra where the unit element is
\eta_{A}\circ\varepsilon_{D}=\varepsilon_{D}\otimes\eta_{A}
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
A
be an algebra. The pair

(M,\varphi_{M})
is a left
A
-module if
M
is an object in

{\sf C}
and
\varphi_{M}:A\otimes M\rightarrow M
is a morphism
in
{\sf C}
satisfying
\varphi_{M}\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes M)=id_{M}
,
\varphi_{M}\circ(A\otimes\varphi_{M})=\varphi_{M}\circ(\mu_{A}\otimes M)
. Given two left
{A}
-modules
(M,\varphi_{M})

and
(N,\varphi_{N})
,
f:M\rightarrow N
is a morphism of left

{A}
-modules if
\varphi_{N}\circ(A\otimes f)=f\circ\varphi_{M}
.

The composition of morphisms of left
A
-modules is a morphism of left
A
-modules. Then left
A
-modules form a category that we will denote by
\;{}_{\sf A}
Mod
.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that
X
is a
bialgebra in
{\sf C}
if
(X,\eta_{X},\mu_{X})
is an
algebra,
(X,\varepsilon_{X},\delta_{X})
is a coalgebra, and

\varepsilon_{X}
and
\delta_{X}
are algebra morphisms
(equivalently,
\eta_{X}
and
\mu_{X}
are coalgebra morphisms). Moreover, if there exists a morphism
\lambda_{X}:X\rightarrow X
in
{\sf C}
,
called the antipode of
X
, satisfying that
\lambda_{X}
is the inverse of
id_{X}
in
{\mathcal{H}}(X,X)
, i.e.,

(2)

id_{X}\ast\lambda_{X}=\eta_{X}\circ\varepsilon_{X}=\lambda_{X}\ast id_{X},

we say that
X
is a Hopf algebra. A morphism of Hopf algebras is an algebra-coalgebra morphism. Note that, if
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a Hopf algebra morphism the following equality holds:

(3)

\lambda_{Y}\circ f=f\circ\lambda_{X}.

With the composition of morphisms in
C
we can define a category whose objects are Hopf algebras and whose morphisms are morphisms of Hopf algebras. We denote this category by
{\sf Hopf}
.

Note that if
X=(X,\eta_{X},\mu_{X},\varepsilon_{X},\delta_{X},\lambda_{X})
is a Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
such that its antipode,
\lambda_{X}
, is an isomorphism, then
X^{cop}=(X,\eta_{X},\mu_{X},\varepsilon_{X},c_{X,X}^{-1}\circ\delta_{X},\lambda_{X}^{-1})
is a Hopf algebra in
\overline{\sf{C}}
(see
[
15
]
).

A Hopf algebra is commutative if
\mu_{X}\circ c_{X,X}=\mu_{X}
and cocommutative if
c_{X,X}\circ\delta_{X}=\delta_{X}.
It is easy to see that in both cases
\lambda_{X}\circ\lambda_{X}=id_{X}
.

If
X
is a Hopf algebra, relevant properties of its antipode,
\lambda_{X}
, are the following: It is antimultiplicative and anticomultiplicative

(4)

\lambda_{X}\circ\mu_{X}=\mu_{X}\circ(\lambda_{X}\otimes\lambda_{X})\circ c_{X,X},\;\;\;\;\delta_{X}\circ\lambda_{X}=c_{X,X}\circ(\lambda_{X}\otimes\lambda_{X})\circ\delta_{X},

and leaves the unit and counit invariant, i.e.,

(5)

\lambda_{X}\circ\eta_{X}=\eta_{X},\;\;\varepsilon_{X}\circ\lambda_{X}=\varepsilon_{X}.

So, it is a direct consequence of these identities that, if
X
is commutative, then
\lambda_{X}
is an algebra morphism and, if
X
is cocommutative, then
\lambda_{X}
is a coalgebra morphism.

In the following definition we recall the notion of left module (co)algebra.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a Hopf algebra. An algebra
A
is said to be a left
X
-module algebra if
(A,\varphi_{A})
is a left
X
-module and
\eta_{A}
,
\mu_{A}
are morphisms of left
X
-modules, i.e.,

(6)

\varphi_{A}\circ(X\otimes\eta_{A})=\varepsilon_{X}\otimes\eta_{A},\;\;\varphi_{A}\circ(X\otimes\mu_{A})=\mu_{A}\circ\varphi_{A\otimes A},

where
\varphi_{A\otimes A}=(\varphi_{A}\otimes\varphi_{A})\circ(X\otimes c_{X,A}\otimes A)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes A\otimes A)
is the left action on
A\otimes A
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
X
be a Hopf algebra. A coalgebra
D
is said to be a left
X
-module coalgebra if
(D,\varphi_{D})
is a left
X
-module and
\varepsilon_{D}
,
\delta_{D}
are morphisms of left
X
-modules, in other words, the following equalities hold:

(7)

\varepsilon_{D}\circ\varphi_{D}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{D},\;\;\delta_{D}\circ\varphi_{D}=\varphi_{D\otimes D}\circ(H\otimes\delta_{D}).

Equivalently,
(D,\varphi_{D})
is a left
X
-module coalgebra if and only if
\varphi_{D}
is a coalgebra morphism.

The following result will be interesting along this paper.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X=(X,\eta_{X},\mu_{X},\varepsilon_{X},\delta_{X},\lambda_{X})
and
H=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H})
be Hopf algebras in
\sf{C}
such that there exists a morphism
\varphi_{H}\colon X\otimes H\rightarrow H
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\varphi_{H}\circ(X\otimes\mu_{H})=\mu_{H}\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H})\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes H\otimes H),

(ii)

\varphi_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism.

Then,
\varphi_{H}\circ(X\otimes\eta_{H})=\varepsilon_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}
holds.

Proof.

The equality follows by:

\displaystyle\varphi_{H}\circ(X\otimes\eta_{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varphi_{H}\otimes(\varepsilon_{H}\circ\varphi_{H}))\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (ii), naturality of $c$ and (co)unit properties)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes(\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H}\circ\varphi_{H}))\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by unit property)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes((id_{H}\ast\lambda_{H})\circ\varphi_{H}))\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H})\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\delta_{H})))\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (ii))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H})\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes H\otimes H))\otimes(\lambda_{H}\circ\varphi_{H}))

\displaystyle\circ(X\otimes((H\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(c_{X,H}\otimes\delta_{H})))\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$, coassociativity of $\delta_{X}$}

and associativity of
\mu_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ((\varphi_{H}\circ(X\otimes\mu_{H}))\otimes(\lambda_{H}\circ\varphi_{H}))\circ(X\otimes((H\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(c_{X,H}\otimes\delta_{H})))\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})

(by (i))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ(\varphi_{H}\otimes\varphi_{H})\circ(X\otimes c_{X,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{X}\otimes(\delta_{H}\circ\eta_{H}))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and unit property)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(id_{H}\ast\lambda_{H})\circ\varphi_{H}\circ(X\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (ii))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H}\circ\varphi_{H}\circ(X\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{X}\otimes\eta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (ii) and (co)unit properties)}.\qed

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
X=(X,\eta_{X},\mu_{X},\varepsilon_{X},\delta_{X},\lambda_{X})
and
H=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H})
be Hopf algebras in
\sf{C}
. If
(H,\varphi_{H})
is a left
X
-module coalgebra and
\mu_{H}
is a morphism of left
X
-modules, then
(H,\varphi_{H})
is a left
X
-module algebra.

In the braided setting the definition of Hopf brace is the following:

Definition 1.10
.

Let
H=(H,\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H})
be a coalgebra in
C
. Let’s assume that there are two algebra structures
(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1})
,
(H,\eta_{H}^{2},\mu_{H}^{2})
defined on
H
and suppose that there exist two endomorphism of
H
denoted by
\lambda_{H}^{1}
and
\lambda_{H}^{2}
. We will say that

(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1},\eta_{H}^{2},\mu_{H}^{2},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{1},\lambda_{H}^{2})

is a Hopf brace in
C
if:

(i)

H_{1}=(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{1})
is a Hopf algebra in
C
.

(ii)

H_{2}=(H,\eta_{H}^{2},\mu_{H}^{2},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H}^{2})
is a Hopf algebra in
C
.

(iii)

The following equality holds:

\mu_{H}^{2}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H}^{1})=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\mu_{H}^{2}\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H),

where

\Gamma_{H_{1}}\coloneqq\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H).

Following
[
7
]
, a Hopf brace will be denoted by
{\mathbb{H}}=(H_{1},H_{2})
or in a simpler way by
\mathbb{H}
.

Definition 1.11
.

If
{\mathbb{H}}
is a Hopf brace in
C
, we will say that
{\mathbb{H}}
is cocommutative if
\delta_{H}=c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H}
, i.e., if
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are cocommutative Hopf algebras in
C
.

Note that by
[
17
, Corollary 5]
, if
H
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra in the braided monoidal category
C
, the identity

(8)

c_{H,H}\circ c_{H,H}=id_{H\otimes H}

holds.

Definition 1.12
.

Given two Hopf braces
{\mathbb{H}}
and
{\mathbb{B}}
in
C
, a morphism
f
in
C
between the two underlying objects is called a morphism of Hopf braces if both
f:H_{1}\rightarrow B_{1}
and
f:H_{2}\rightarrow B_{2}
are Hopf algebra morphisms.

Hopf braces together with morphisms of Hopf braces form a category which we denote by
HBr
. Moreover, cocommutative Hopf braces constitute a full subcategory of
\sf{HBr}
which we will denote by
\sf{cocHBr}
.

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be a Hopf brace in
C
. Then

(9)

\eta_{H}^{1}=\eta_{H}^{2},

holds and, by
[
2
, Lemma 1.7]
, in this braided setting the equality

(10)

\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes\lambda_{H}^{1})=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ((\lambda_{H}^{1}\circ\mu_{H}^{2})\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)

also holds. Moreover, in our braided context
[
2
, Lemma 1.8]
and
[
2
, Remark 1.9]
hold and then we have that the algebra
(H,\eta_{H}^{1},\mu_{H}^{1})
is a left
H_{2}
-module algebra with action
\Gamma_{H_{1}}
and
\mu_{H}^{2}
admits the following expression:

(11)

\mu_{H}^{2}=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H).

In addition, by
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
,
\Gamma_{H_{1}}
is a coalgebra morphism when
\mathbb{H}
is cocommutative.

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a Hopf brace in
\sf{C}
. The equality

(12)

\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H}=\lambda_{H}^{1}

holds.

Proof.

The equality follows by:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\Gamma_{H_{1}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes(id_{H}\ast\lambda_{H}^{2}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}^{1}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode} and (co)unit properties)}.\qed

To conclude this introductory section we will remember the notion of finite object in
\sf{C}
, since they are going to be of special interest throughout Section
3
.

Definition 1.14
.

An object
P
in
\sf{C}
is finite if there exists an object
P^{\ast}
, called the dual of
P
, and a
\sf{C}
-adjunction
P\otimes-\dashv P^{\ast}\otimes-
between the tensor functors.

We will denote by
a_{P}
and
b_{P}
the unit and the counit of the previous
\sf{C}
-adjunction, respectively. Finite objects in
\sf{C}
constitute a full subcategory of
\sf{C}
that we will denote by
\sf{C^{f}}
. Note that, for every finite object
P
in
\sf{C}
, we have a natural algebra structure in
\sf{C}
over the tensor object
P^{\ast}\otimes P
as we can see in the following lemma, whose proof is straightforward.

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
P
be a finite object in
\sf{C}
, then
P^{\ast}\otimes P
is an algebra in
\sf{C}
with product and unit given by

\mu_{P^{\ast}\otimes P}\coloneqq P^{\ast}\otimes b_{P}(K)\otimes P

and

\eta_{P^{\ast}\otimes P}\coloneqq a_{P}(K),

respectively.

Moreover, it is going to be useful the following lemma.

Lemma 1.16
.

If
P
is a finite object in
\sf{C}
, then

(13)

(c_{P,P^{\ast}}\otimes P)\circ(P\otimes a_{P}(K))=(P^{\ast}\otimes c_{P,P}^{-1})\circ(a_{P}(K)\otimes P).

Proof.

The equality (
13
) follows by:

\displaystyle(c_{P,P^{\ast}}\otimes P)\circ(P\otimes a_{P}(K))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P^{\ast}\otimes(c_{P,P}^{-1}\circ c_{P,P}))\circ(c_{P,P^{\ast}}\otimes P)\circ(P\otimes a_{P}(K))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the isomorphism condition for $c_{P,P}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P^{\ast}\otimes c_{P,P}^{-1})\circ(a_{P}(K)\otimes P)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and $c_{P,K}=id_{K}$}).\qed

2.
Brace triples and Hopf braces

The aim of this part is to prove that we can characterise Hopf braces in
\sf{C}
via another structures. This new structures will be known as brace triples.

Definition 2.1
.

Consider
H=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\lambda_{H})
a Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
with
\lambda_{H}
an isomorphism and let
\gamma_{H}\colon H\otimes H\rightarrow H
and
T_{H}\colon H\rightarrow H
be morphisms in
\sf{C}.
We will say that
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
is a brace triple if the following conditions hold:

(i)

(\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)=(\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H).

(ii)

\gamma_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism, i.e.:

(ii.1)

\delta_{H}\circ\gamma_{H}=(\gamma_{H}\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})
,

(ii.2)

\varepsilon_{H}\circ\gamma_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H}
.

(iii)

\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H})=\mu_{H}\circ(\gamma_{H}\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)
.

(iv)

\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})=\gamma_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes H)
.

(v)

\gamma_{H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)=id_{H}
.

(vi)

T_{H}
is an isomorphism in
\sf{C}
such that the following equalities are verified:

(vi.1)

\delta_{H}\circ T_{H}=c_{H,H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}
.

(vi.2)

\varepsilon_{H}\circ T_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}

(vi.3)

\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ((\delta_{H}\circ T_{H})\otimes H)=\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(((T_{H}\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)
.

(vi.4)

\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}=\lambda_{H}
.

(vi.5)

\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}=\lambda_{H}^{-1}\circ T_{H}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Given a brace triple
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
, conditions (ii) and (iii) of Definition
2.1
imply that

(14)

\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H})=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}

holds by Theorem
1.8
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
be a brace triple. Note that condition (vi.5) of Definition
2.1
is equivalent to

(15)

\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H}^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}=\lambda_{H}^{-1}.

In fact, on the one side, suppose that (vi.5) of Definition
2.1
holds. Then, we have that:

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}^{-1}\circ T_{H}\circ T_{H}^{-1}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of isomorphism for $T_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\circ T_{H}^{-1}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (vi.5) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes H)\circ(T_{H}^{-1}\otimes T_{H}^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (vi.1) of Definition \ref{BTdef} and the isomorphism condition}

for
T_{H}
and
c_{H,H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H}^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of isomorphism for $T_{H}$)}.

On the other side, suppose now that (
15
) holds. Then,

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}^{-1}\circ T_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H}^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}\circ T_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{olde75})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H}^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ c_{H,H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (vi.1) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the isomorphism condition for $c_{H,H}$ and $T_{H}$)}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
and
(B,\gamma_{B},T_{B})
be brace triples and
f\colon H\rightarrow B
a morphism in
\sf{C}
. We will say that
f
is a morphism of brace triples if
f
is a Hopf algebra morphism and

(16)

f\circ\gamma_{H}=\gamma_{B}\circ(f\otimes f)

holds.

Brace triples and their morphisms form a category which we will denote by
\sf{BT}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose that
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
is a brace triple with
H
cocommutative. Under this condition, note that (i) of Definition
2.1
always holds and take also into account that (vi.1) becomes
\delta_{H}\circ T_{H}=(T_{H}\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}
. This implies that (vi.3) always holds in the cocommutative setting. Moreover,
\lambda_{H}^{-1}=\lambda_{H}
(due to
\lambda_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}=id_{H}
), so this implies that (vi.5) becomes
\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}=\lambda_{H}\circ T_{H}
. Therefore, as a consequence of (vi.4) and (vi.5), we obtain that

\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}=\lambda_{H}=\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\circ T_{H}^{-1}.

Cocommutative brace triples constitute a full subcategory of
\sf{BT}
which we will denote by
\sf{cocBT}.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a Hopf brace in
\sf{C}
. We will say that
\mathbb{H}
is a
s
-Hopf brace if the following conditions hold:

(i)

(\Gamma_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)=(\Gamma_{H_{1}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)
.

(ii)

\lambda_{H}^{1}
and
\lambda_{H}^{2}
are isomorphisms in
\sf{C}
such that the following conditions hold:

(ii.1)

\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ((\delta_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2})\otimes H)=\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(H\otimes\Gamma_{H_{1}})\circ(((\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)
.

(ii.2)

\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}=(\lambda_{H}^{1})^{-1}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2}.

With the obvious morphisms,
s
-Hopf braces constitute a full subcategory of
\sf{HBr}
, and we will denote it by
s
\sf{HBr}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Let’s assume that
\sf{C}
is symmetric. Under this assumption, condition (i) of Definition
2.6
means that
(H_{1},\Gamma_{H_{1}})
is in the cocommutativity class of
H_{2}
following the notion introduced in
[
1
, Definition 2.1 and Definition 2.2]
.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that, for a
s
-Hopf brace
\mathbb{H}
, condition (ii.2) of Definition
2.6
is equivalent to

(17)

\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}=(\lambda_{H}^{1})^{-1}.

Indeed, suppose that (ii.2) of Definition
2.6
holds, then:

\displaystyle(\lambda_{H}^{1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{H}^{1})^{-1}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of isomorphism for $\lambda_{H}^{2}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (ii.2) of Definition \ref{sHbr})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes H)\circ((\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1}\otimes(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{a-antip} and the isomorphism condition}

for
\lambda_{H}^{2}
and
c_{H,H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of isomorphism for $\lambda_{H}^{2}$)}.

On the other hand, we have that:

\displaystyle(\lambda_{H}^{1})^{-1}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ\delta_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{oldz22})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(H\otimes(\lambda_{H}^{2})^{-1})\circ c_{H,H}^{-1}\circ c_{H,H}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{a-antip})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the isomorphism condition for $\lambda_{H}^{2}$ and $c_{H,H}$)}.

Remark 2.9
.

Consider
\mathbb{H}
a cocommutative
s
-Hopf brace. Under cocommutativity assumption, note that (i) of Definition
2.6
always holds. In addition,
\delta_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2}=(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H}
, so (ii.1) always holds too. Moreover, under cocommutativity conditions,
\lambda_{H}^{k}
is an isomorphism with inverse itself for all
k=1,2
. Therefore, condition (ii.2) of Definition
2.6
is satisfied. Indeed:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{H_{1}}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ((\delta_{H}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2})\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\Gamma_{H_{1}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ(\lambda_{H}^{1}\otimes\mu_{H}^{2})\circ(((\lambda_{H}^{2}\otimes\lambda_{H}^{2})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{a-antip} and cocommutativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ((\lambda_{H}^{1}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2})\otimes(\lambda_{H}^{2}\ast id_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{1}\circ((\lambda_{H}^{1}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2})\otimes(\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}^{1}\circ\lambda_{H}^{2}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (co)unit property)}.

So, under cocommutativity supposition, every Hopf brace is a
s
-Hopf brace, that is to say,
\texttt{s}\sf{HBr}=\sf{cocHBr}
.

In this first result, we will prove that every brace triple induce a
s
-Hopf brace in
\sf{C}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
be a brace triple in
\sf{C}
. Then
\mathbb{H}_{\sf{BT}}=(H,H_{\sf{BT}})

is a
s
-Hopf brace in
\sf{C}
being
H_{\sf{BT}}
the Hopf algebra structure defined by
H_{\sf{BT}}=(H,\eta_{H},\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},T_{H}),

where
\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\coloneqq\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)
.

Proof.

At first we will prove that
H_{\sf{BT}}
is a Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
. Note that we already know that
(H,\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H})
is a coalgebra in
\sf{C}
and that
\eta_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism. We begin by proving the unit property for
\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}
. Indeed, on the one side,

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ((\delta_{H}\circ\eta_{H})\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of coalgebra morphism for $\eta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (v) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle id_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by unit property)},

and, on the other side,

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H})))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\varepsilon_{H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{olde6})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle id_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (co)unit property)}.

The associativity of
\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}
follows by

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\circ(\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\otimes H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ((\delta_{H}\circ\mu_{H})\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(((\mu_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes(\delta_{H}\circ\gamma_{H})))\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)

(by the condition of coalgebra morphism for
\mu_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(((\mu_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H))\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes((\gamma_{H}\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})))\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (ii.1) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes((\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H))\otimes\gamma_{H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle\circ(H\otimes H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H\otimes H)\circ(((H\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes((\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes\gamma_{H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle\circ(H\otimes H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H\otimes H)\circ(((H\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (i) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H}))\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes((H\otimes c_{H,H})

\displaystyle\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes H\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ(\gamma_{H}\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes H\otimes H\otimes\gamma_{H})

\displaystyle\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (iv) of Definition \ref{BTdef} and associativity of $\mu_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (iii) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}$)}.

Also,
\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}
is a coalgebra morphism. On the one hand, by the condition of coalgebra morphism for
\mu_{H}
, (ii.2) of Definition
2.1
and the counit property, it is straightforward to compute that
\varepsilon_{H}\circ\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H}
and, on the other hand,

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\circ\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\circ\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes(\delta_{H}\circ\gamma_{H}))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of coalgebra morphism}

for
\mu_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes((\gamma_{H}\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)

(by (ii.1) of Definition
2.1
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(H\otimes((\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ((c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H))\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle\circ(((H\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes\delta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\circ(H\otimes((\gamma_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(H\otimes H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle\circ(((H\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes\delta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (i) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})

(by coassociativity of
\delta_{H}
and naturality of
c
)

\displaystyle=

3.
Post-Hopf algebras and Hopf braces

In this section we introduce the notion of post-Hopf algebra in the braided monoidal context. In particular, for the category of vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{K}
, we obtain the concept of post-Hopf algebra presented in
[
13
]
, where the authors get an equivalence between Hopf braces and these objects under cocommutativity assumption. Besides being working in a more general setting, in this section we prove that the categories of finite cocommutative Hopf braces and cocommutative post-Hopf algebras satisfying condition (
32
) are isomoprhic. As a consequence of this result together with Corollary
2.19
, we also deduce that finite cocommutative brace triples are isomorphic to post-Hopf algebras verifying (
32
).

Definition 3.1
.

A post-Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
is a pair
(H,m_{H})
where
H
is a finite Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
and
m_{H}\colon H\otimes H\rightarrow H
is a morphism in
\sf{C}
that satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

m_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism, which means that the following equalities are satisfied:

(i.1)

\delta_{H}\circ m_{H}=(m_{H}\otimes m_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})
,

(i.2)

\varepsilon_{H}\circ m_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H}.

(ii)

m_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})=m_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes H)
, which is called the “weighted” associativity.

(iii)

m_{H}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H})=\mu_{H}\circ(m_{H}\otimes m_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)
.

(iv)

The morphism

\alpha_{H}\coloneqq(H^{\ast}\otimes m_{H})\circ(c_{H,H^{\ast}}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes a_{H}(K))\colon H\rightarrow H^{\ast}\otimes H

is convolution invertible in
\Hom(H,H^{\ast}\otimes H)
, which means that there exists
\beta_{H}\colon H\rightarrow H^{\ast}\otimes H
such that

(H^{\ast}\otimes b_{H}(K)\otimes H)\circ(\alpha_{H}\otimes\beta_{H})\circ\delta_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes a_{H}(K)=(H^{\ast}\otimes b_{H}(K)\otimes H)\circ(\beta_{H}\otimes\alpha_{H})\circ\delta_{H}.

Remark 3.2
.

Given a post-Hopf algebra
(H,m_{H})
, conditions (i) and (iii) of Definition
3.1
imply that

m_{H}\circ(H\otimes\eta_{H})=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}

holds by Theorem
1.8
.

\sf{Post}
-
{\sf Hopf}
. When

-
{\sf Hopf}

-
\sf{Hopf}
.

{\sf Hopf}

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

\sf{Post}
-
{\sf Hopf}

First of all, note that the morphism

\displaystyle m_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}\otimes H)

\displaystyle m_{H}\circ((m_{H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}))\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by unit property)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{H}\circ\eta_{H})\otimes(m_{H}\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes H))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{oldeq3})}

\displaystyle(H^{\ast}\otimes b_{H}(K)\otimes H)\circ(\alpha_{H}\otimes\alpha_{H})\circ(\eta_{H}\otimes\eta_{H})

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{H})\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\circ(H\otimes\delta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by counit properties)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{H}(K)\otimes\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\circ(H\otimes\beta_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes\delta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widetilde{\beta}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{H}(K)\otimes(\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}))\circ(H\otimes\beta_{H}\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes\delta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of coalgebra morphism for $\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{H}(K)\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\circ(H\otimes((H^{\ast}\otimes b_{H}(K)\otimes H)\circ(\beta_{H}\otimes\alpha_{H})\circ\delta_{H}))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((b_{H}(K)\otimes\varepsilon_{H})\circ(H\otimes a_{H}(K)))\otimes\varepsilon_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (iv) of Definition \ref{PHopf algebra})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\varepsilon_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the adjunction properties)}.\qed

Let
(H,m_{H})
be a post-Hopf algebra and suppose that
\widetilde{\beta}_{H}
satisfies that

(32)

\delta_{H}\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{H}=(\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\otimes\widetilde{\beta}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H}).

Note that if (
32
) holds, then
\widetilde{\beta}_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism by the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
(H,m_{H})
be a cocommutative post-Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
, then

(33)

\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}.

In addition, if the identity (
32
) holds, then

(34)

\delta_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\otimes\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\circ\delta_{H},

i.e.
\widehat{\lambda}_{H}
is a coalgebra morphism,

(35)

\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=id_{H},

and

(36)

\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\hatast id_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}.

Proof.

First of all, note that it is straightforward to prove that
\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}
using (
27
), (
31
), (
5
) and counit property. Moreover

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{lambdatr cocom} and definition of $\widetilde{\beta}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\otimes\widetilde{\beta}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{property for betatilde2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\otimes\widetilde{\beta}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(((\lambda_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{a-antip} and cocommutativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes H))\otimes(\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes H)))\circ(H\otimes(c_{H,H}\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)\circ(H\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and}

coassociativity of
\delta_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H})\otimes(\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by cocommutativity and coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\otimes\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{lambdatr cocom} and definition of $\widetilde{\beta}_{H}$)}.

As a consequence, we can prove that
\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=id_{H}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ((\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H})\otimes(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (co)unit properties)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ((id_{H}\hatast\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\otimes(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{convolution1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\otimes(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$ and associativity of $\widehat{\mu}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(H\otimes((id_{H}\hatast\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{prop lambdatr2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{convolution1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{prop lambdatr3})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle id_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (co)unit properties)}.

To finish, we will see that
\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\hatast id_{H}=\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\hatast id_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\otimes H)\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\hatast$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\otimes(\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{prop lambdatr4})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(id_{H}\hatast\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{prop lambdatr2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H}\circ\widehat{\lambda}_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{convolution1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{prop lambdatr3})}.\qed

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(H,m_{H})
be a cocommutative post-Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
. If the identity (
32
) holds, then
\widehat{H}=(H,\eta_{H},\widehat{\mu}_{H},\varepsilon_{H},\delta_{H},\widehat{\lambda}_{H})
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
. This particular Hopf algebra structure is called the subadjacent Hopf algebra of
(H,m_{H})
.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.7
and equalities (
29
) and (
36
).
∎

So, we have deduced that it is possible to obtain from any cocommutative post-Hopf algebra satisfying (
32
) another Hopf algebra structure whose underlying coalgebra is the same as that of
H
. Therefore, at this point it is natural to wonder whether
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}=(H,\widehat{H})
is a Hopf brace in
\sf{C}
. The following theorem solves this question.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
(H,m_{H})
be a cocommutative post-Hopf algebra in
\sf{C}
. If the identity (
32
) is satisfied, then

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}=(H,\widehat{H})

is a cocommutative Hopf brace in
\sf{C}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.14
, to prove that
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}=(H,\widehat{H})
is a Hopf brace we only have to show that (iii) of Definition
1.10
holds. Note that

(37)

\widehat{\Gamma}_{H}=m_{H}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\widehat{\Gamma}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes\widehat{\mu}_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widehat{\Gamma}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widehat{\mu}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ((\lambda_{H}\ast id_{H})\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$ and associativity of $\mu_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ((\eta_{H}\circ\varepsilon_{H})\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (co)unit property)}.

So, we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(\widehat{\mu}_{H}\otimes\widehat{\Gamma}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes m_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)

(by definition of
\widehat{\mu}_{H}
and (
37
))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\mu_{H}\circ(m_{H}\otimes m_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H\otimes H)

(by associativity of
\mu_{H}
and coassociativity of
\delta_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\mu_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (iii) of Definition \ref{PHopf algebra})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{H}\circ(H\otimes\mu_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widehat{\mu}_{H}$)}.\qed

It is possible to interpret the previous result in the following sense: If
\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}
denotes the full subcategory of cocommutative post-Hopf algebras such that (
32
) holds, and
\sf{cocHBr^{f}}
denotes the category of finite cocommutative Hopf braces, then a functor

Q\colon\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}\longrightarrow\sf{cocHBr^{f}}
exists which
acts on objects by
Q((H,m_{H}))=\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
and on morphisms by the identity. To see that
Q
is well-defined on morphisms, we have to prove that if
f\colon(H,m_{H})\rightarrow(B,m_{B})
is a morphism in
\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}
, then
f
is a morphism of Hopf braces between
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{B}}
. Indeed:

\displaystyle f\circ\widehat{\mu}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\circ\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widehat{\mu}_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{B}\circ(f\otimes f)\circ(H\otimes m_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of algebra morphism for $f\colon H\rightarrow B$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{B}\circ(B\otimes m_{B})\circ(((f\otimes f)\circ\delta_{H})\otimes f)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{CondMorPHopf})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{B}\circ(B\otimes m_{B})\circ(\delta_{B}\otimes B)\circ(f\otimes f)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by the condition of coalgebra morphism for $f$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}_{B}\circ(f\otimes f)\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\widehat{\mu}_{B}$)}.

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.16
.

The categories
\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}
and
\sf{cocBT^{f}}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

At first, let’s consider the following commutative diagram of functors:

\textstyle{\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Q}
\textstyle{\sf{cocHBr^{f}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G^{\prime\prime}}

\simeq

\textstyle{\sf{cocBT^{f}},\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{P^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{F^{\prime\prime}}

where
F^{\prime\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
are the restrictions of functors
F^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime}
introduced in Corollary
2.19
to the subcategories of finite objects, and
P^{\prime}
is the restriction of functor
P
to
\sf{cocBT^{f}}
.

To begin with, we are going to see that
P^{\prime}
is well-defined on objects. That is to say, we have to prove that if
(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H})
is a finite cocommutative brace triple, then the post-Hopf algebra
(H,\gamma_{H})
satisfies (
32
). In this situation, by Theorem
3.6
and (
19
),

(38)

\beta_{H}=\alpha_{H}\circ T_{H},

and then,

(39)

\widetilde{\beta}_{H}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H}).

Therefore, (
32
) follows by:

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{H}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by $\widetilde{\beta}_{H}=\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H})$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}\otimes\widetilde{\alpha}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes(\delta_{H}\circ T_{H}))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by Lemma \ref{tildealpha de coalgebras})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\alpha}_{H}\otimes\widetilde{\alpha}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes((T_{H}\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (vi.1) of Definition \ref{BTdef}}

and cocommutativity of
\delta_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\beta}_{H}\otimes\widetilde{\beta}_{H})\circ(H\otimes c_{H,H}\otimes H)\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and \eqref{betatildeparticular})}.

Taking into account functors
P^{\prime}
,
Q
and
G^{\prime\prime}
, on the one hand, we have that

\displaystyle(P^{\prime}\circ(G^{\prime\prime}\circ Q))((H,m_{H}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(P^{\prime}\circ G^{\prime\prime})(\widehat{\mathbb{H}})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $Q$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\prime}((H,m_{H},\widehat{\lambda}_{H}))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $G^{\prime\prime}$ and \eqref{Ghat=mH})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H,m_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $P^{\prime}$)}.

So,
P^{\prime}\circ(G^{\prime\prime}\circ Q)=\sf{id}_{\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}}
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle((G^{\prime\prime}\circ Q)\circ P^{\prime})((H,\gamma_{H},T_{H}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(G^{\prime\prime}\circ Q)((H,\gamma_{H}))\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $P^{\prime}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(\widehat{\mathbb{H}})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $Q$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H,\gamma_{H},\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $G^{\prime\prime}$ and \eqref{Ghat=mH})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H,\gamma_{H},T_{H}),

where the last equality is due to the fact that
\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=T_{H}
. Indeed, firstly note that

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{H}(K)\otimes H)\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes\beta_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{lambdatr cocom})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{H}(K)\otimes H)\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes(\alpha_{H}\circ T_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by Theorem \ref{inversebetaBT} and \eqref{TH2=ID})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ c_{H,H}\circ(\lambda_{H}\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{eq1} and \eqref{ccb})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by naturality of $c$ and cocommutativity of $\delta_{H}$)}.

As a result,

\displaystyle id_{H}\ast_{\sf{BT}}\widehat{\lambda}_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}\circ(H\otimes\widehat{\lambda}_{H})\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\ast_{\sf{BT}}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(T_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ\delta_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by definition of $\mu_{H}^{\sf{BT}}$ and previous equality)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ((\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes\gamma_{H})\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes H))\otimes H)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes((T_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ\delta_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}

(by (iv) of Definition
2.1
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(\mu_{H}\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ(H\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ(H\otimes T_{H})\circ\delta_{H})\otimes H)))\circ(\delta_{H}\otimes\delta_{H})\circ\delta_{H}

(by coassociativity of
\delta_{H}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{H}\circ(H\otimes(\gamma_{H}\circ((id_{H}\ast\lambda_{H})\otimes\lambda_{H})\circ\delta_{H}))\circ\delta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by (vi.4) of Definition \ref{BTdef} and coassociativity of $\delta_{H}$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle id_{H}\ast\lambda_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode}, counit property and (v) of Definition \ref{BTdef})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{H}\otimes\eta_{H}\;\footnotesize\textnormal{(by \eqref{antipode})},

which implies, due to the uniqueness of the antipode for the Hopf algebra
H_{\sf{BT}}
, that
\widehat{\lambda}_{H}=T_{H}.
Hence,
(G^{\prime\prime}\circ Q)\circ P^{\prime}=\sf{id}_{\sf{cocBT^{f}}}
.
∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Categories
\sf{cocBT^{f}}
,
\sf{cocHBr^{f}}
and
\sf{cocPost}\textnormal{-}\sf{Hopf}^{\star}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of the previous theorem and Corollary
2.19
.
∎

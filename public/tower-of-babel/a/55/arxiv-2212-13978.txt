Abstract.
The main purpose of this paper is to prove controllability for the model proposed by Lazer and Mckenna under the influence of impulses, delay, and nonlocal conditions. First, we study the approximate controllability by employing a technique that pulls back the control solution to a fixed curve in a short time interval. Subsequently, based on Banach Fixed Point Theorem we investigate the exact controllability.

Keywords.
Lazer and Mckenna’s model, suspension bridges, controllability, impulsive semilinear evolution equation, delays, nonlocal conditions, Banach Fixed Point Theorem.

Primary 34K35; Secondary 37L05.

is a right inverse of the controllability operator

\mathcal{G}:L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{U})\longrightarrow Z_{1/2}
defined by
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(v)=\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{S}(T-s)\mathfrak{B}v(s)\,ds.

Before stating the main result of this part, let us consider the following notations:

M=\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{S}(s)\|,\quad\|\Gamma\|=\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\left\|\mathfrak{B}^{*}\mathcal{S}^{*}(T-s)\mathfrak{W}^{-1}\right\|,

N=\sum_{k=1}^{m}d_{k},\quad\mathcal{C}=MLq+MTl+MN,

Theorem 4.1
.

We assume that the following inequality is satisfied

MLq+MT\|\mathfrak{B}\|\|\Gamma\|\,\mathcal{C}+MTl+MN<1.

(18)

Then, under assumptions
(\mathcal{H})
, the system (
17
) is exactly controllable on
[0,T]
.

Proof.

The controllability of system (
17
) will be equivalent to the existence of a fixed point for the following operator
\kappa:\mathcal{PC}_{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{PC}_{m},
defined by

\displaystyle(\kappa y)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(t)\{\rho(0)-\mathfrak{G}\left(y_{\tau_{1}},\ldots,y_{\tau_{q}}\right)(0)\}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathfrak{B}\Gamma\mathcal{L}(y)(s)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathfrak{F}\left(s,y_{s}\left(.\right)\right)ds+\sum_{0<t_{k}<t}\mathcal{S}(t-t_{k})\mathfrak{J}_{k}\left(t_{k},y\left(t_{k}\right)\right),

where the operator
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{PC}_{m}\rightarrow Z_{1/2},
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{*}-\mathcal{S}(T)\{\rho(0)-\mathfrak{G}\left(y_{\tau_{1}},\ldots,y_{\tau_{q}}\right)(0)\}-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{S}(T-s)\mathfrak{F}\left(s,y_{s}\right)ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{0<t_{k}<T}\mathcal{S}(T-t_{k})\mathfrak{J}_{k}\left(t_{k},y\left(t_{k}\right)\right).

Now, we shall prove that
\kappa
is a contraction mapping. In fact, let
y,\omega\in\mathcal{PC}_{m}
, then

\displaystyle\|(\kappa\,y)(t)-(\kappa\,\omega)(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(t)\|\,\|\mathfrak{G}\left(y_{\tau_{1}},\ldots,y_{\tau_{q}}\right)(0)-\mathfrak{G}\left(\omega_{\tau_{1}},\ldots,\omega_{\tau_{q}}\right)(0)\|

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\|\|\mathfrak{B}\|\|\Gamma\|\|\mathcal{L}(y)-\mathcal{L}(\omega)\|ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\|\|\mathfrak{F}\left(s,y_{s}\right)-\mathfrak{F}\left(s,\omega_{s}\right)\|ds

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<t_{k}<t}\|\mathcal{S}(t-t_{k})\|\|\mathfrak{J}_{k}\left(t_{k},y\left(t_{k}\right)\right)-\mathfrak{J}_{k}\left(t_{k},\omega\left(t_{k}\right)\right)\|.

Then, using the above hypotheses, we get that

\displaystyle\|(\kappa\,y)(t)-(\kappa\,\omega)(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle MLq\|y-\omega\|+MT\|\mathfrak{B}\|\|\Gamma\|\|\mathcal{L}(y)-\mathcal{L}(\omega)\|

\displaystyle+MTl\|y-\omega\|+MN\|y-\omega\|.

Furthermore, we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}(y)-\mathcal{L}(\omega)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle MLq\|y-\omega\|+MTl\|y-\omega\|+MN\|y-\omega\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\,\|y-\omega\|.

Then,

\displaystyle\|(\kappa\,y)(t)-(\kappa\,\omega)(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(MLq+MT\|\mathfrak{B}\|\|\Gamma\|\,\mathcal{C}+MTl+MN\right)\|y-\omega\|.

By (
18
), we get that
\kappa
is a contraction mapping, and consequently,
\kappa
has a fixed point. That is to say,

\kappa(z)=z.

Since
u=\Gamma\mathcal{L}(z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}u=\mathcal{L}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{*}-\mathcal{S}(t)\{\rho(0)-\mathfrak{G}\left(z_{\tau_{1}},\ldots,z_{\tau_{q}}\right)(0)\}-\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{S}(T-s)\mathfrak{F}\left(s,z_{s}\left(.\right)\right)ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{0<t_{k}<T}\mathcal{S}(T-t_{k})\mathfrak{J}_{k}\left(t_{k},z\left(t_{k}\right)\right).

Then, the solution
z(t,\rho,u)
of the system (
17
) verifies

z(T,\rho,u)=z^{*}.

∎

5
Conclusions and remarks

The oscillation of suspension bridges is modeled in a second-order differential equation. This phenomenon is a real control system, it comes with many perturbations that can lead to catastrophic failures. In this work, we have taken into account the intrinsic phenomena such as impulses, delays, and non-local conditions, and we have proved that controllability is robust in the presence of all the above perturbations, under certain different conditions that we specified.

Finally, in real-life problems, the impulse starts abruptly at a certain moment of time and remains active on a finite time interval, however the time of the action is little. Such an impulse is known as a non-instantaneous impulse (NII) (see
[
20
]
). It would be of much interest to investigate the impulsive controllability of the system (
3
) with non-instantaneous impulses.

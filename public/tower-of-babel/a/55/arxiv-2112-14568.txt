Introduction

1.1
Ramification

A central theme in algebraic number theory is the study of ramification in number fields. If
K\to L
is an extension of number fields, with associated rings of integers
R\to A
, and if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal in
R
, the ramification describes how
\mathfrak{p}
factors into prime ideals in
A
. The extension
A/R
is
unramified
at
\mathfrak{p}
if this factorization is squarefree, or
totally ramified
if
\mathfrak{p}
is a power of a single prime ideal.

The language comes from geometry: We can regard
\text{Spec}(A)\to\text{Spec}(R)
as being nearly a covering space map, with some bad behavior at points (primes) which are ramified.

We will generalize these definitions to (algebraically) any extension of rings or even ring spectra or (geometrically) any extension of schemes, derived schemes, or even spectral schemes. We will first give the geometric definition for intuition, although it is the algebraic definition which we will use throughout the paper.

Take arbitrary spectral schemes
\ast\to Y\to X
(no definition necessary for this paper, but see
[

Definition 1.1
.

With

1.2
THH of rings of integers

The ramification of an extension of commutative ring spectra
A/R
is closely connected to topological Hochschild homology, which is the commutative ring spectrum
\text{THH}^{R}(A)=A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}A
.

If
R
and
A
are ordinary rings, then (as usual) all tensor products are derived, and
\text{THH}^{R}(A)
is a differential graded algebra. Its homology groups are the classical Hochschild homology
\text{HH}_{\ast}^{R}(A)=\text{THH}_{\ast}^{R}(A)
.

The relationship between THH and ramification is suggested by the close relationship between THH and loop spaces:

Remark 1.4
.

Geometrically, if
X=\text{Spec}(R)
and
Y=\text{Spec}(A)
, then
\text{Spec}(\text{THH}^{R}(A))=Y\times^{h}_{Y\times^{h}_{X}Y}Y=LY
, the
free loop space
of
Y
in
\text{Sch}_{/X}
.

1.3
Ramified descent

In another sense, Theorem
7.5
can be regarded as a generalization of the étale descent theorem of Geller-Weibel
[
15
]
, McCarthy-Minasian
[
11
]
, and Mathew
[
9
]
(in increasing order of generality):

Theorem
.

If
S\to R\to A
are commutative ring spectra and
A/R
is étale (in particular, unramified), then the following map is an equivalence:

\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}A\to\text{THH}^{S}(A).

If
R\to A
is not necessarily étale but has well-behaved ramification, we might ask how close this map is to an equivalence.

We introduce notation
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)
for the homotopy fiber of the map
\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}A\to\text{THH}^{S}(A)
. Then étale descent states
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\cong 0
if
A/R
is étale, while Theorem
7.5
asserts

\text{Ram}^{S}(R/\mathbb{Z})^{\wedge}_{p}\cong\text{THH}^{S[z]}(R_{p})\otimes_{R}\Omega^{1}_{R/\mathbb{Z}}

if
R
is a ring of integers. We express the close relationship between ramification and THH in two results (proved just by unpacking definitions):

Proposition

(
5.1
)
.

A/R
is unramified at
k
if and only if

\text{Ram}^{R}(A/R)\otimes_{A}k\cong 0.

Proposition

(
7.1
)
.

If
A/R
is totally ramified at
k
, then

\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}k\cong\text{THH}^{S}(k)\otimes_{k}I^{k}_{A/R}

as
\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}k
-modules.

Remark 1.8
.

Geometrically, we think of
X=\text{Spec}(R)
and
Y=\text{Spec}(A)
. Consider the map
f:LY\to LX\times_{X}Y
. The lifting problem for
f
asks, given a loop in
X
and a lift of its basepoint to
Y
, whether the loop lifts to
Y
. Then
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)
corresponds to the sheaf
\mathcal{R}
of functions defined on those loops in
LX\times_{X}Y
which do not lift to
LY
; that is, the fiber of
\mathcal{O}(LX\times_{X}Y)\to f_{\ast}\mathcal{O}(LY)
.

The last two propositions state that
\mathcal{R}
can be directly calculated anywhere that
Y/X
is either unramified or totally ramified.

In the event that
A/R
factors as a composite of unramified and totally ramified extensions, these results should in principle allow us to calculate
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)
itself via a local-to-global argument or
ramified descent
. The key lemma that makes this possible is:

Proposition

(
4.2
)
.

If
A\to B\to C\to k
are commutative ring spectra, then the following are fiber sequences:

I_{B/A}^{k}\to I_{C/A}^{k}\to I_{C/B}^{k},

\text{Ram}^{S}(B/A)\otimes_{B}C\to\text{Ram}^{S}(C/A)\to\text{Ram}^{S}(C/B).

This is precisely the strategy used to prove Theorem
7.5
. Indeed, any extension of number fields factors (at each prime) as an unramified extension followed by a totally ramified extension. Therefore, we can use this strategy to calculate
\text{Ram}(R/\mathbb{Z})
when
R
is a ring of integers in a number field.

Remark 1.9

We are certainly not the first to study the spectra

of commutative ring spectra over

(Corollary

\text{Ram}^{\mathbb{S}}(A/R)
and the coarsest is
\text{Ram}^{R}(A/R)
, or reduced THH.

Weibel-Geller
[
15
]
were the first to prove étale descent
\text{THH}^{\mathbb{Z}}(R)\otimes_{R}A\cong\text{THH}^{\mathbb{Z}}(A)
when

and
A
are ordinary (discrete) rings; they do so precisely by showing that
\text{Ram}^{\mathbb{Z}}(R/\mathbb{Z})
vanishes at each prime. Our calculation of

when

is a ring of integers generalizes their ideas.

In this paper, we mostly consider ramification for rings of integers in number fields, but there are a few results that are more general. Notably:

Proposition

(

)

If an extension

is totally ramified everywhere (that is, at

A number field is ramified at only finitely many primes. The last proposition can be regarded as a vast generalization of this statement – it is impossible for any extension

to be ramified

except in the degenerate case that it has Galois group

(that is,

).

\text{THH}^{R}(k;I_{A/R}^{k})\cong\text{THH}^{R}(k)\otimes_{k}I_{A/R}^{k}
. Now a copy of

splits off of

. Therefore, the latter is

if and only if

, which by definition means

is an equivalence.
∎

6
Ramification for rings of integers

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
L/K
is an extension of number fields with rings of integers
A/R
,
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime in
R
, and
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}|\mathfrak{p}
is a prime in

. Then:

•

is totally ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
(in the classical sense) if and only if
A/R
is totally ramified at
A/\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
(in the sense of Definition
1.2
);

•

A/R
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
(in the classical sense) if and only if
A/R
is unramified at
A/\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
(in the sense of Definition
1.2
).

The proof boils down to Theorem
2.2
, but first we need two lemmas.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

be
p
-complete for some prime

•

A/R
is unramified at
k
if and only if
A^{\wedge}_{p}/R^{\wedge}_{p}
is unramified at
k
;

•

A/R
is totally ramified at
k
if and only if
A^{\wedge}_{p}/R^{\wedge}_{p}
is.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
,
I_{A^{\wedge}_{p}/R^{\wedge}_{p}}^{k}\cong(I_{A/R}^{k})^{\wedge}_{p}\cong I_{A/R}^{k}
.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

If

are commutative ring spectra, then any two of the following imply the third:

•

B/A
is totally ramified at
k
;

•

C/B
is totally ramified at
k
;

•

C/A
is totally ramified at
k
.

The same is true for unramified extensions.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, there is a fiber sequence
I_{B/A}^{k}\to I_{C/A}^{k}\to I_{C/B}^{k}
. If any two of these are induced
k
-modules, then so is the third. This proves the totally ramified statement.

Applying
\text{THH}^{S}(k;-)
proves the unramified statement.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

By
p
-completing
R
and
A
(using Lemma

), it suffices to prove:
∎

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose
A/R
is an extension of complete DVRs in mixed characteristic with perfect residue field, with uniformizers

and
\pi\in A
. Then:

•

A/R
is totally ramified at
p
if and only if it is totally ramified at
A/\pi

•

A/R
is unramified at
p
if and only if it is unramified at
A/\pi
.

Proof.

If
A/R
is totally ramified at
p
, then
A/R
is totally ramified at
A/\pi
by Theorem
2.2
. If
A/R
is unramified at
p
, then it is étale, so
A/R
is unramified at
A/\pi
by Example
5.3
.

We still need to prove the converses. By Proposition

, if
A/R
is both totally ramified and unramified at
A/\pi
, then
0\cong I_{A/R}^{k}\cong k\otimes_{A}I_{A/R}\otimes_{A}k
, which (together with the fact that
I_{A/R}^{k}
is rationally

I_{A/R}\cong 0
. Therefore

is an equivalence, which can only be true if

.

Now any extension

factors

Theorem

tells us about the ramification of an extension of rings of integers at each residue field. We could also ask about the ramification at the field of fractions:

Proposition 6.5
.

If
L/K
is an extension of number fields with rings of integers
A/R
, then
A/R
is unramified at
L
. In fact, for any

,

\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}L\cong 0.

Proof.

We have

, and so
K\otimes_{R}K\cong R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\cong K
. Therefore,
\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}K\cong\text{THH}^{S}(K;K\otimes_{R}K)\cong\text{THH}^{S}(K)
and for the same reason
\text{THH}^{S}(A)\otimes_{A}L\cong\text{THH}^{S}(L)

By definition,
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}L
is the fiber of

\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}L\to\text{THH}^{S}(A)\otimes_{A}L\cong\text{THH}^{S}(L).

Using
\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}L\cong\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}K\otimes_{K}L\cong\text{THH}^{S}(K)\otimes_{K}L

\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}L
is the fiber of
\text{THH}^{S}(K)\otimes_{K}L\to\text{THH}^{S}(L)

\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}L\cong\text{Ram}^{S}(L/K).

Since

is a Galois extension (therefore étale), this is

∎

7
Ramified descent for THH

Recall that
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}k\cong\text{THH}^{S}(k;I^{k}_{A/R})
, Remark
3.3
. If
I^{k}_{A/R}

-module, then

Proposition 7.1
.

If
A/R
is totally ramified at
k
,

\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)\otimes_{A}k\cong\text{THH}^{S}(k)\otimes_{k}I_{A/R}^{k}

as
\text{THH}^{S}(R)\otimes_{R}k
-modules.

This proposition identifies
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)
locally at the ‘point’
\text{Spec}(k)
. If we also have some lifting data, then in some cases we can even determine
\text{Ram}^{S}(A/R)
itself:

Definition 7.2
.

A/R
is
totally ramified at
k
with lifting data
if there is an
A
-module
\Omega^{1}
and a map
I_{A/R}\to\Omega^{1}
of
A\otimes_{R}A
-modules, and the composite

I_{A/R}^{k}\cong I\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}(k\otimes_{R}k)\to\Omega^{1}\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}(k\otimes_{R}k)\to\Omega^{1}\otimes_{A}k

is an equivalence. In particular, this implies that
A/R
is totally ramified at
k
because
I^{k}_{A/R}\cong\Omega^{1}\otimes_{A}k
is a
k
-module.

.

Suppose

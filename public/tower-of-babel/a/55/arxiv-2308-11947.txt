On the Local Regularity of the Hilbert Transform
Yifei Pan, Jianfei Wang, Yu Yan

Abstract
. In this paper the local regularity of the Hilbert transform is studied, and local smoothness and real analyticity results are obtained.



1.
Introduction

Given a function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq p<\infty)
, its Hilbert transform, denoted
Hf
, is well defined a.e. as the principal value of the convolution of
f
with the function
\frac{1}{\pi x}
:

Hf(x)\,\,=\,\,p.v.\,\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{x-t}\,dt\,\,:=\,\,\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{|x-t|>\epsilon}\frac{f(t)}{x-t}\,dt.

The purpose of this paper is to study the local regularity of the Hilbert transform when only the local regularity of the function is assumed, and the main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq p<\infty)
. If
f
is
C^{k,\alpha}(k\geq 0,0<\alpha<1)
in a neighborhood of a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then its Hilbert transform
Hf
is
C^{k,\alpha_{0}}
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
for any
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha
.

Here we use the standard notations
C^{0,\alpha}
for functions that are Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
in a bounded interval and
C^{k,\alpha}
for functions whose
k
-th derivatives are Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
.

It is well known that derivative formulas exist for globally defined differentiable functions; for example (
[
9
]
), if
f\in C^{k+1}(\mathbb{R})
and all its derivatives are in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, then its Hilbert transform satisfies

\left(Hf\right)^{(k)}(x)=H\left(f^{(k)}\right)(x).

In the case of local differentiability the following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Given a function
f(x)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq p<\infty)
, if
f
is
C^{k+1}
near a point, then its Hilbert transform
Hf(x)
is
C^{k}
near the same point. In particular, if
f(x)
is
C^{\infty}
at a point, then
Hf(x)
is also
C^{\infty}
at the same point.

In addition to local differentiability, the Hilbert transform also preserves local real analyticity.

Theorem 1.3
.

If a function
f(x)
is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq p<\infty)
and is real analytic near a point, then its Hilbert transform
Hf(x)
is also real analytic near the same point.

For functions defined on the unit circle, a classical theorem by Privalov (
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
1
]
) states that the Hilbert transform on
S^{1}
is a bounded operator from
C^{0,\alpha}(S^{1})

(0<\alpha<1)
into itself.
In an attempt to generalize Privalov’s theorem to the Hilbert transform on
\mathbb{R}
, Dyachenko, Nursultanov, and Tikhonov (
[
5
]
) considered the global Hölder regularity of the Hilbert transform on a particular class of functions that includes
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, and they showed that the Hilbert transform is well defined everywhere for this class, furthermore it has bounded global
C^{0,\alpha}
norm. Our Theorems
1.1
to
1.3
, on the other hand, are completely local in the sense that we only assume the function is regular near a point.

Theorem
1.1
guarantees that the (possible) regularity loss under the Hilbert transform must be arbitrarily small if a loss does occur; namely, if the function is
C^{k,\alpha}
near a point, then
Hf
is at least
C^{k,\alpha_{0}}
for any
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha
near that point. As will be shown in Section
5
, it is possible for local regularity to remain unchanged under the Hilbert transform. Thus a rather interesting question is whether Theorem
1.1
can be improved to
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
, but we have found no success in doing so.

The tool we shall use to deal with the local differentiability is the so-called finite Hilbert transform. Given a function
f
defined on a finite interval
(a,b)
, its finite Hilbert transform is

p.v.\,\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(t)}{x-t}\,dt,

where
a<x<b
. This is equivalent to
\chi_{(a,b)}(x)H\left(\chi_{(a,b)}f\right)(x)
, where
\chi
is the characteristic function.
In order to work with this singular integral, we introduce the following (non-singular) operator
T
for
f\in C^{0,\alpha}([a,b])
:

Tf(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\,dy.

In Section
2
we will study some fundamental properties of this operator
T
pertinent to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
.

On a side note, the local integrability of the Hilbert transform was considered in the literature. As a matter of fact, there is essentially a characterization of local integrability. For example, according to a result by Calderón and Zygmund (
[
2
]
), if
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
are two open intervals and
f\log^{+}|f|\in L^{1}(I_{2})
, then
Hf\in L^{1}(I_{1})
. Conversely, Stein (
[
12
]
,
[
2
]
) showed that if
f\geq 0
and
Hf\in L^{1}(I_{2})
, then
f\log^{+}|f|\in L^{1}(I_{1})
.
For reference on recent works where the Hilbert transform is exclusively used to construct harmonic measures in the complex plane, see
[
6
]
by Kenig and Zhao. Another problem related to local smoothness of the Hilbert transform is the
local smoothness of the conjugate functions in the periodical case, and we refer the reader to the works by Dyachenko in
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
for recent progress on that front.
We also remark that in this paper we assume
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),
where
1\leq p<\infty
, however the same results would hold in the case
p=\infty
when a modified Hilbert transform is used.

2.
The Operator
T
and its Hölder Continuity

Definition 2.1
.

Given
f\in C^{0,\alpha}([a,b])
, define the function
Tf
on
[a,b]
by

(1)

Tf(x)\,\,=\,\,\,\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\,dy.

It is straightforward to verify that
Tf
is well-defined for all Hölder continuous functions, but if
f
is only continuous, then
Tf
may not be well defined. For example, let

f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\ln x}&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,0<x\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,x\leq 0.\end{cases}

Then
f
is continuous but not Hölder continuous, and
Tf(0)
is not well defined.
In addition, let
\eta(x)
be a function that is compactly supported on
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
and
\eta=1
on
[-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4}]
. By definition
f\eta
is
C^{0}
but not
C^{1}
, and it is straightforward to check that its Hilbert transform
H(f\eta)
is undefined at
0
. Thus if a function is
C^{0}
but not
C^{1}
, then its Hilbert transform may not be
C^{0}
.

In this section we will study various properties of the operator
T
that eventually lead to the Hölder continuity of the derivatives of
T
, a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The first result is an estimate of the Hölder semi-norm of
T
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
f\in C^{0,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
for some
0<\alpha<1
, then for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in[a,b]
,

(2)

\left|Tf(x_{2})-Tf(x_{1})\right|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(\ln\left|\frac{b-a}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|+1\right).

In particular,
Tf\in C^{0,\alpha_{0}}\left([a,b]\right)
for any
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha
, and
the map
T:C^{0,\alpha}\to C^{0,\alpha_{0}}
is a bounded operator.

Remark
: Theorem
2.2
is a special case of Theorem
2.9
below.
Both estimates in Theorem
2.2
and Theorem
2.9
are sharp, i.e. the results cannot be improved to
\alpha_{0}=\alpha
. A simple counter-example is
f(x)=x^{\alpha}
and detailed calculations are given in the proof of Lemma
5.3
in Section
5
; we will discuss this in the remark immediately following the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
:
Without loss of generality we can assume
a=0
,
b=1
, and
x_{1}<x_{2}
.
Denote

Q_{1}(y)=\frac{f(y)-f(x_{1})}{y-x_{1}}\hskip 14.45377pt\text{and}\hskip 14.45377ptQ_{2}(y)=\frac{f(y)-f(x_{2})}{y-x_{2}}.

Since
f
is
C^{0,\alpha}
, we have

(3)

|Q_{1}(y)|\leq\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|y-x_{1}|^{\alpha-1}

and

(4)

|Q_{2}(y)|\leq\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|y-x_{2}|^{\alpha-1}.

We write

(5)

\displaystyle\left|Tf(x_{1})-Tf(x_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|+\left|\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|+\left|\int_{x_{2}}^{1}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

We first estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}=\left|\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|.

By (
3
) and (
4
),

(6)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\left|Q_{1}(y)\right|dy+\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\left|Q_{2}(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}|y-x_{1}|^{\alpha-1}dy+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}|y-x_{2}|^{\alpha-1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}(x_{2}-x_{1})^{\alpha}.

Next, we estimate

\displaystyle I_{1}=\left|\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|.

Case 1
:
x_{1}<x_{2}-x_{1}
, which implies
x_{2}<2(x_{2}-x_{1})
.

Then by (
3
) and (
4
)

\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\left|Q_{1}(y)\right|dy\leq\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|y-x_{1}|^{\alpha-1}dy=\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}x_{1}^{\alpha}<\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}(x_{2}-x_{1})^{\alpha}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\left|Q_{2}(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|y-x_{2}|^{\alpha-1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}\left(x_{2}^{\alpha}-(x_{2}-x_{1})^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}x_{2}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha}\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}(x_{2}-x_{1})^{\alpha}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle\left|I_{1}\right|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}(x_{2}-x_{1})^{\alpha}
, where
C
only depends on
\alpha
.

Case 2
:
x_{1}\geq x_{2}-x_{1}
. Denote

(7)

h=\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{2}.

We will integrate
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{1}-h}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy
and
\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}-h}^{x_{1}}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy
separately.

Note that

\displaystyle Q_{1}-Q_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f(y)-f(x_{1})}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{f(y)-f(x_{2})}{y-x_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{2})-f(x_{1})}{y-x_{1}}+\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right).

Since
f
is
C^{0,\alpha}
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{1}-h}\left|\frac{f(x_{2})-f(x_{1})}{y-x_{1}}\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{x_{1}-h}\frac{1}{x_{1}-y}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(-\ln h+\ln x_{1}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\ln\left|\frac{2}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|,

where in the last step we have used
\ln x_{1}<0
. To estimate the integral of

\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right)

on
0\leq y\leq x_{1}-h
, let

v=\frac{x_{1}-y}{x_{2}-x_{1}},

then

\frac{1}{2}\leq v\leq\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}},\hskip 14.45377ptx_{1}-y=v(x_{2}-x_{1}),\hskip 14.45377pt\text{and}\hskip 14.45377ptx_{2}-y=(v+1)(x_{2}-x_{1}).

Thus

\displaystyle\left|\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(v+1)}\left|\frac{f(y)-f(x_{2})}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(v+1)}\cdot\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|y-x_{2}|^{\alpha}}{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(v+1)}\cdot\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}(v+1)^{\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}}{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{v(v+1)^{1-\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{1-\alpha}}.

Consequently,

(9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{1}-h}\left|\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}}_{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{v(v+1)^{1-\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{1-\alpha}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\frac{1}{v(v+1)^{1-\alpha}}\,dv

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{v(v+1)^{1-\alpha}}\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha},

where
C=\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{v(v+1)^{1-\alpha}}\,dv
only depends on
\alpha
. Combining (
8
) and (
9
), we know

(10)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.90755pt\int_{0}^{x_{1}-h}|Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\ln\left|\frac{2}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|.

By (
3
) and (
4
),

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{x_{1}-h}^{x_{1}}\left|Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\int_{x_{1}-h}^{x_{1}}|y-x_{1}|^{\alpha-1}dy+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\int_{x_{1}-h}^{x_{1}}|y-x_{2}|^{\alpha-1}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{\alpha}\left|\frac{x_{2}-x_{1}}{2}\right|^{\alpha}+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\frac{\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{\alpha}-1}{\alpha}\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|^{\alpha},

where
C
only depends on
\alpha
. It then follows from (
10
) and (
11
) that

(12)

I_{1}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\left|x_{2}-x_{1}\right|^{\alpha}\left(1+\ln\left|\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|\right),

where
C
only depends on
\alpha
.

Lastly, we estimate

\displaystyle I_{3}=\left|\int_{x_{2}}^{1}\left(Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)\right)dy\right|.

As in the estimate for
I_{1}
we can write

Q_{1}-Q_{2}=\frac{f(x_{2})-f(x_{1})}{y-x_{1}}+\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right),

and

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{x_{2}}^{1}\left|\frac{f(x_{2})-f(x_{1})}{y-x_{1}}\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\int_{x_{2}}^{1}\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\big(\ln|1-x_{1}|-\ln|x_{2}-x_{1}|\big)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\ln\left|\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|,

where we have used
\ln(1-x_{1})<0
. To estimate the second term, let

v=\frac{y-x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}},

then
1<v<\frac{1-x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}
when
x_{2}<y<1
, in addition,

y-x_{1}=v(x_{2}-x_{1})\hskip 14.45377pt\text{and}\hskip 14.45377pty-x_{2}=(v-1)(x_{2}-x_{1}).

Thus

\displaystyle\left|\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(v-1)}\left|\frac{f(y)-f(x_{2})}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(v-1)}\cdot\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|y-x_{2}|^{\alpha}}{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(v-1)}\cdot\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}(v-1)^{\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}}{|x_{2}-x_{1}|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{v(v-1)^{1-\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{1-\alpha}}.

Consequently,

(14)

\displaystyle\int_{x_{2}}^{1}\left|\left[\frac{1}{y-x_{1}}-\frac{1}{y-x_{2}}\right]\left(f(y)-f(x_{2})\right)\right|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\frac{1-x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\frac{\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}}{v(v-1)^{1-\alpha}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{1-\alpha}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\frac{1-x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}}\frac{1}{v(v-1)^{1-\alpha}}\,dv

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{v(v-1)^{1-\alpha}}\,dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha},

where
C=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{v(v-1)^{1-\alpha}}\,dv
only depends on
\alpha
. Combining (
13
) and (
14
), we know

(15)

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{x_{2}}^{1}|Q_{1}(y)-Q_{2}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(1+\ln\left|\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|\right),

where
C
only depends on
\alpha
.

Therefore, by (
5
), (
6
), (

), and (

),

\displaystyle\left|Tf(x_{1})-Tf(x_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(1+\ln\left|\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|\right).

For any
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left|Tf(x_{1})-Tf(x_{2})\right|}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\alpha_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}\left(1+\ln\left|\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|\right).

Hence
\|Tf\|_{C^{0,\alpha_{0}}}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}
and
T:C^{0,\alpha}\to C^{0,\alpha_{0}}
is bounded.

\Box

In the rest of this section we will study the derivatives of
Tf
. One important observation is an intriguing connection between the derivatives of the difference quotient and the remainder of the Taylor expansion of
f
.

Recall that for any
f\in C^{k}([a,b])
,

P_{k}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\dfrac{f^{(i)}(x)}{i!}(y-x)^{i}

is the
k
-th order Taylor polynomial of
f
at
x
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, if
f
is a
C^{k}
function, then

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)=k!\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}},

for
x\neq y
, where
P_{k}(x,y)
is defined above.

Proof:
We will prove this lemma by induction.
When
k=1
, by direct calculations

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)=\dfrac{f(y)-f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)}{(y-x)^{2}}=\dfrac{f(y)-P_{1}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{2}}.

Suppose for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, all
C^{k}
functions
f
satisfy

\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)=k!\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}},

then for any
f\in C^{k+1}
,

\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial x^{k+1}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)=k!\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right).

Since

P_{k}(x,y)=f(x)+f^{\prime}(x)(y-x)+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(x)}{2}(y-x)^{2}+\cdots+\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}(y-x)^{k},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}P_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)+\Big(f^{\prime\prime}(x)(y-x)-f^{\prime}(x)\Big)+\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}{2}(y-x)^{2}-f^{\prime\prime}(x)(y-x)\right)

\displaystyle+\cdots+\left(\frac{f^{(k+1)}(x)}{k!}(y-x)^{k}-\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{(k-1)!}(y-x)^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{(k+1)}(x)}{k!}(y-x)^{k}.

Thus

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}P_{k}(x,y)\right)(y-x)^{k+1}+(k+1)\left(f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)\right)(y-x)^{k}}{(y-x)^{2k+2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{-\frac{f^{(k+1)}(x)}{k!}(y-x)^{2k+1}+(k+1)\left(f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)\right)(y-x)^{k}}{(y-x)^{2k+2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1)\dfrac{-\frac{f^{(k+1)}(x)}{(k+1)!}(y-x)^{k+1}+f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1)\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k+1}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+2}},

and consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial x^{k+1}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+1)!\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k+1}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+2}}.

This completes the proof.

\Box

Lemma
2.3
inspires us to define another operator
T_{k}
as below, which will be used to study the derivatives of
Tf
. Its definition can be traced back to
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
in the complex setting.

Definition 2.4
.

For any
f\in C^{k,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)(0<\alpha<1)
, define

(17)

T_{k}f(x)=k!\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}dy,

where
P_{k}(x,y)
is the
k
-th degree Taylor polynomial of
f
at
x
.

Note that

\displaystyle f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(y)-P_{k-1}(x,y)-\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}(y-x)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{(k)}(\zeta)}{k!}(y-x)^{k}-\frac{f^{(k)}(x)}{k!}(y-x)^{k}

for some
\zeta
between
x
and
y
. Since
f
is
C^{k,\alpha}
, it follows that

\left|f^{(k)}(\zeta)-f^{(k)}(x)\right|\leq C|\zeta-x|^{\alpha}\leq C|y-x|^{\alpha}.

Hence

(18)

\left|\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right|\leq C|y-x|^{\alpha-1},

which implies that
T_{k}f
is well-defined. Later in this section we will prove the Hölder continuity of
T_{k}
in Theorem
2.9
, but before doing so we need a few preparatory lemmas.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose a function
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)(0<\alpha<1)
, then the function

\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}

is well-defined on
[a,b]\times[a,b]
and Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
in
x
and
y
.

Proof:
Since
f^{(k+1)}
is continuous, the Taylor expansion of
f
at
x
takes the form

\displaystyle f(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{k}(x,y)+\frac{1}{k!}\int_{x}^{y}(y-t)^{k}f^{(k+1)}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{k}(x,y)+\frac{(y-x)^{k+1}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{k}f^{(k+1)}\big(sy+(1-s)x\big)\,ds,

after a change of variable
s=\frac{t-x}{y-x}
. Thus

\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{k}f^{(k+1)}\big(sy+(1-s)x\big)\,ds

is well defined in
[a,b]\times[a,b]
.

For any
a\leq x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\leq b
and
(x_{1},y_{1})\neq(x_{2},y_{2})
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{k}f^{(k+1)}\big(sy_{1}+(1-s)x_{1}\big)\,ds-\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{k}f^{(k+1)}\big(sy_{2}+(1-s)x_{2}\big)\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|f^{(k+1)}\big(sy_{1}+(1-s)x_{1}\big)-f^{(k+1)}\big(sy_{2}+(1-s)x_{2}\big)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C^{k+1,\alpha}}\left(\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\alpha}+\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|^{\alpha}\right).

Thus
\displaystyle\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
in
x
and
y
.

\Box

Next, we will prove that the differentiation and integral signs are interchangeable for the operator
T
. This turns out to be a subtle issue, and the following folklore elementary lemma will play a crucial role in what follows.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{f_{n}(x)\}
be a sequence of differentiable functions from
[a,b]
to
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose

f_{n}\to f
uniformly on
[a,b]
and

f^{\prime}_{n}\to g
uniformly on
[a,b]
, where
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then
f^{\prime}(x)=g(x)
for every
x\in[a,b]
.

Remark:
The key point of this lemma is that
f^{\prime}=g
at every point in
[a,b]
. It is easy to show
f^{\prime}=g
almost everywhere, since

\int_{a}^{x}f^{\prime}(t)dt=f(x)-f(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(x)-f(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{a}^{x}f_{n}^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{a}^{x}g(t)dt.

If
g
is continuous, this would imply
f^{\prime}=g
. However,
g
could be discontinuous in general. For example, define a trivial sequence

f_{n}(x)=f(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}\sin\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,x\neq 0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,x=0\end{cases}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
f_{n}
is differentiable, but

f^{\prime}_{n}(x)=g(x)=\begin{cases}2x\sin\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)-\frac{2}{x}\cos\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,x\neq 0,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,x=0\end{cases}

is not continuous.
The proof of this lemma is not trivial, so it is included in Appendix A.

\Box

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose a function
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
, then

\frac{d}{dx}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}dy\right)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy.

Proof:
Denote

F(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\,dy.

In order to avoid the singularity in the integral, we take any sequence of positive numbers
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and define

F_{n}(x)=\int_{a}^{x-\epsilon_{n}}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\,dy+\int_{x+\epsilon_{n}}^{b}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\,dy.

Then since
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right),
it follows by the Taylor expansion
\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right|\leq C
. Consequently,

\left|F(x)-F_{n}(x)\right|\,\,=\,\,\left|\int_{x-\epsilon_{n}}^{x+\epsilon_{n}}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\,dy\right|\,\,\leq\,\,C\epsilon_{n}.

Thus
F_{n}\to F
uniformly.

Next, we consider the limit of the derivative of
F_{n}
. By the Leibniz formula

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{x-\epsilon_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy+\frac{f(x-\epsilon_{n})-P_{k}(x,x-\epsilon_{n})}{(-\epsilon_{n})^{k+1}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{x+\epsilon_{n}}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy-\frac{f(x+\epsilon_{n})-P_{k}(x,x+\epsilon_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}^{k+1}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|F^{\prime}_{n}(x)-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x-\epsilon_{n}}^{x+\epsilon_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\right|+\left|\frac{f(x-\epsilon_{n})-P_{k}(x,x-\epsilon_{n})}{(-\epsilon_{n})^{k+1}}-\frac{f(x+\epsilon_{n})-P_{k}(x,x+\epsilon_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}^{k+1}}\right|.

By (
16
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x-\epsilon_{n}}^{x+\epsilon_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x-\epsilon_{n}}^{x+\epsilon_{n}}(k+1)\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k+1}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+2}}dy\right|.

By (
18
) with
k
replaced by
k+1
, we know

\left|\frac{f(y)-P_{k+1}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+2}}\right|\leq C|y-x|^{\alpha-1}.

Thus

\left|\int_{x-\epsilon_{n}}^{x+\epsilon_{n}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\right|\leq C\int_{x-\epsilon_{n}}^{x+\epsilon_{n}}|y-x|^{\alpha-1}dy=C\epsilon_{n}^{\alpha}.

By Lemma
2.5
,
\displaystyle\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(x-y)^{k+1}}
is Hölder in
y
, so

\left|\frac{f(x-\epsilon_{n})-P_{k}(x,x-\epsilon_{n})}{(-\epsilon_{n})^{k+1}}-\frac{f(x+\epsilon_{n})-P_{k}(x,x+\epsilon_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}^{k+1}}\right|\leq C\left|(x-\epsilon_{n})-(x+\epsilon_{n})\right|^{\alpha}\leq C\epsilon_{n}^{\alpha}.

Therefore

\left|F^{\prime}_{n}(x)-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\right|\leq C\epsilon_{n}^{\alpha},

which implies that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{n}\to\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy

uniformly. Then by Lemma
2.6
we know

F^{\prime}(x)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy,

and this completes the proof of this lemma.

\Box

Example
:

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&\,\,\text{if}\,\,\,\,x=0.\end{cases}

, but it is not

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\dfrac{\frac{f(y)-f(h)}{y-h}-\frac{f(y)}{y}}{h}

\displaystyle=

.

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose a function
f\in C^{k,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)(0<\alpha<1)
, then

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}dy=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)dy.

Proof:
If
f\in C^{1,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
, by Lemma
2.7

\frac{d}{dx}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}dy=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)dy,

so the theorem is true when
k=1
. Now suppose

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}dy=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)dy

for all
f\in C^{k,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
, we will show the theorem is true in
k+1
for all
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k+1}}{dx^{k+1}}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}dy\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)dy\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\int_{a}^{b}k!\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}dy\right)\hskip 14.45377pt\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:derivative_diff_quotient})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(k!\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\hskip 14.45377pt\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:deriv_integral_Taylor_remainder})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial^{k+1}}{\partial x^{k+1}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)dy.\hskip 14.45377pt\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:derivative_diff_quotient})}

Thus the theorem is proved by induction.

Remark

Theorem 2.9
.

Suppose a function
f\in C^{k,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
for
0<\alpha<1
and
k\geq 0
. Then for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in[a,b]
,

(20)

\left|T_{k}f(x_{2})-T_{k}f(x_{1})\right|\leq C\|f\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(\ln\left|\frac{b-a}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|+1\right).

In particular,
T_{k}f\in C^{0,\alpha_{0}}\left([a,b]\right)
for all
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha
and the map
T_{k}:C^{k,\alpha}\to C^{0,\alpha_{0}}
is a bounded operator; furthermore,
Tf\in C^{k,\alpha_{0}}\left([a,b]\right).

Proof:
We will prove (
20
) by induction. Note that (
20
) is true when
k=0
by Theorem
2.2
. Now suppose it is true for
k
, we will show that it is also true for
k+1
.

For any
f\in C^{k+1,\alpha}([a,b])
, by (
19
) and Theorem
2.8
.

\displaystyle T_{k+1}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k+1}}{dx^{k+1}}Tf(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial x^{k}}\left(\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\right)dy\right)\hskip 36.135pt\text{(by Theorem \ref{thm:switch_deriv_integral})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\left(\int_{a}^{b}k!\dfrac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\,dy\right)\hskip 50.58878pt\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:derivative_diff_quotient})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x,y)}{(y-x)^{k+1}}\right)dy\hskip 50.58878pt\text{(by Lemma \ref{lem:deriv_integral_Taylor_remainder})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!\int_{a-x}^{b-x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{f(x+z)-P_{k}(x,x+z)}{z^{k+1}}\right)dz\hskip 28.90755pt(\text{let}\,\,z=y-x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!\int_{a-x}^{b-x}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(x+z)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{f^{(i+1)}(x)}{i!}z^{i}}{z^{k+1}}\,dz.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k!\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(y)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{f^{(i+1)}(x)}{i!}(y-x)^{i}}{(y-x)^{k+1}}dy\hskip 50.58878pt(\text{let}\,\,y=z+x).

Note that by (
19
)

k!\int_{a}^{b}\dfrac{f^{\prime}(y)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{f^{(i+1)}(x)}{i!}(y-x)^{i}}{(y-x)^{k+1}}dy=T_{k}f^{\prime}(x),

hence

T_{k+1}f=T_{k}f^{\prime}.

Since
f^{\prime}\in C^{k,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)
, by the inductive hypothesis

\left|T_{k}f^{\prime}(x_{2})-T_{k}f^{\prime}(x_{1})\right|\leq C\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(\ln\left|\frac{b-a}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|+1\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|T_{k+1}f(x_{2})-T_{k+1}f(x_{1})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(\ln\left|\frac{b-a}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f\|_{C^{k+1,\alpha}}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left(\ln\left|\frac{b-a}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\right|+1\right).

Thus (
20
) is true by induction. It follows that
\|T_{k}f\|\in C^{0,\alpha_{0}}\left([a,b]\right)
for all
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha
and
T_{k}:C^{k,\alpha}\left([a,b]\right)\to C^{0,\alpha_{0}}\left([a,b]\right)
is a bounded operator. By (
19
) it also implies that
Tf\in C^{k,\alpha_{0}}\left([a,b]\right).

This completes the proof of this theorem.

1.1

.

f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})

is defined a.e.

Lemma 3.1
.

If a function
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, then
H\left((1-\chi_{(a,b)})f\right)(x)
is real analytic on any closed subinterval of
(a,b)
.

Proof:
Take any
x\in[a_{0},b_{0}]\subset(a,b)
, then

\displaystyle H\left((1-\chi_{(a,b)})f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{a}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy.

If
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for some
p>1
, then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{a}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}\left(\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\right)\,dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle j!\int_{-\infty}^{a}\left|\frac{f(y)}{(x-y)^{j+1}}\right|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle j!\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{a}\left|\frac{1}{(x-y)^{(j+1)q}}\right|\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\hskip 28.90755pt(\text{where}\,\,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j!\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\dfrac{(x-a)^{\frac{1-(j+1)q}{q}}}{\big((j+1)q-1\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle j!\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\frac{1}{a_{0}-a}\right)^{j+1}.

If
p=1
, this is easily true.
Then by the Dominated Convergence Theorem for all
j\geq 1
,

\left|\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{a}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy\right)\right|\,\,=\,\,\left|\int_{-\infty}^{a}\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial x^{j}}\left(\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\right)\,dy\right|\,\,\leq\,\,j!\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\frac{1}{a_{0}-a}\right)^{j+1},

thus
\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{a}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy
is real analytic at
x
. Similarly, we can prove that
\displaystyle\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy
is also real analytic at
x
, therefore
H\left((1-\chi_{[a,b]})f\right)
is real analytic at
x
.

Proof of Theorem

Hf=H(\chi_{(a,b)}f)+H\left(\left(1-\chi_{(a,b)}\right)f\right).

Since
H\left(\left(1-\chi_{(a,b)}\right)f\right)
is real analytic at
x_{0}
by Lemma
3.1
, we
only need to show
H\left(\chi_{(a,b)}f\right)
is also real analytic at
x_{0}
. By (
21
)

H\left(\chi_{(a,b)}f\right)(x)=-\frac{1}{\pi}Tf(x)+\frac{f(x)}{\pi}\ln\left|\frac{x-a}{x-b}\right|.

f(x)
being real analytic at
x_{0}
implies
\frac{f(x)}{\pi}\ln\left|\frac{x-a}{x-b}\right|
being real analytic at
x_{0}
, so it remains to prove that
Tf(x)
is real analytic at
x_{0}
.
By (
19
)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}Tf(x_{0})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|k!\int_{a}^{b}\frac{f(y)-P_{k}(x_{0},y)}{(y-x_{0})^{k+1}}dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|k!\int_{a}^{b}\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(l)}(x_{0})}{l!}(y-x_{0})^{l-k-1}dy\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|k!\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{f^{(l)}(x_{0})}{l!(l-k)}\left[\delta^{l-k}-(-\delta)^{l-k}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k!\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{c^{l}\delta^{l-k}}{l-k}.

Since
c\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{c^{l}\delta^{l-k}}{l-k}<\sum_{l=k+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{l}\delta^{-k}<\frac{1}{(2\delta)^{k}}.

Hence

\left|\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}Tf(x_{0})\right|\leq 2k!\left(\frac{1}{2\delta}\right)^{k},

which implies
Tf
is real analytic at
x_{0}
.

\Box

Proposition 5.1

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr x-\sgn(x)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}&\,\,\text{if}\,\,|x|>1,\end{cases}

Lemma 5.2

\displaystyle 0.

\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}&\,\,\text{if}\,\,x<-1,\end{cases}

To construct our second example, we will start with the simple function
x^{\alpha}
and compute its finite Hilbert transform first.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
0<x<1
, the finite Hilbert transform of the function
x^{\alpha}
on
(0,1)
is

p.v.\,\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{y^{\alpha}}{x-y}dy=-\frac{(1-x)^{\alpha}}{\pi\alpha}-\frac{1}{\pi}x^{\alpha}g(x),

where
g(x)
is a continuous function on
[0,1]
and
g(0)\neq 0
.

Proof:

\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{y^{\alpha}-x^{\alpha}}{y-x}\,dy\,\,=\int_{0}^{x}\dfrac{y^{\alpha}-x^{\alpha}}{y-x}\,dy+\int_{x}^{1}\dfrac{y^{\alpha}-x^{\alpha}}{y-x}\,dy\,\,:=\,\,I_{1}+I_{2}.

We first compute
I_{1}
. Let
z=\frac{y}{x}
, then
dy=xdz
and
0<z<1
when
0<y<x
. It follows that

(23)

I_{1}\,\,=\,\,\int_{0}^{x}\dfrac{y^{\alpha}-x^{\alpha}}{y-x}\,dy\,\,=\,\,x^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}\,dz.

Since

\lim_{z\to 1}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}=\alpha,

the function
\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}
has a removable discontinuity at
1
, so

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}\,dz=C_{1}\,\,\,\,\text{for some constant}\,\,C_{1}>0.

Hence, we can write

(24)

I_{1}=C_{1}x^{\alpha}

Next we compute
I_{2}
. Again let
z=\frac{y}{x}
, then
dy=xdz
and
1<z<\frac{1}{x}
when
x<y<1
. Similar to the previous case we can write

(25)

I_{2}\,\,=\,\,\int_{x}^{1}\dfrac{y^{\alpha}-x^{\alpha}}{y-x}\,dy\,\,=\,\,x^{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{x}}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}\,dz.

As
x\to 0
,
\frac{1}{x}\to\infty
, so we only consider small
x
such that
\frac{1}{x}>2
. Since
\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}
has a removable discontinuity at
1

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}\,dz=C_{2}\,\,\,\,\text{for some constant}\,\,C_{2}>0.

Hence, we can write

(26)

x^{\alpha}\int_{1}^{2}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}\,dz=C_{2}x^{\alpha}.

It now remains to compute the integral from 2 to
\frac{1}{x}
.

(27)

\int_{2}^{\frac{1}{x}}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}-1}{z-1}\,dz\,\,=\,\,\int_{2}^{\frac{1}{x}}\dfrac{z^{\alpha}}{z-1}\,dz+\ln\left|\frac{x}{1-x}\right|.

To evaluate the first term,
let

t=\frac{1}{z-1},

so
0<t<1
when

, and

z=1+\frac{1}{t}\,\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,\,dz=-\frac{1}{t^{2}}dt.

Then

(28)

Since
0<t<1

\left(1+t\right)^{\alpha}=1+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+a_{3}t^{3}\cdots,

where
a_{1}=\alpha
and in general

a_{i}=\dfrac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-i+1)}{i!}\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,i\geq 1.

It follows that

(29)

\int_{\frac{x}{1-x}}^{1}\frac{\left(1+t\right)^{\alpha}}{t^{1+\alpha}}\,dt=\int_{\frac{x}{1-x}}^{1}\left(t^{-1-\alpha}+a_{1}t^{-\alpha}+a_{2}t^{1-\alpha}+a_{3}t^{2-\alpha}+\cdots\right)\,dt.

Since the binomial series converges absolutely, we can integrate term-by-term to obtain

\displaystyle\int\left(t^{-1-\alpha}+a_{1}t^{-\alpha}+a_{2}t^{1-\alpha}+a_{3}t^{2-\alpha}+\cdots\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int t^{-1-\alpha}\,dt+\int a_{1}t^{-\alpha}\,dt+\int a_{2}t^{1-\alpha}\,dt+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{-\alpha}+\frac{a_{1}t^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}+\frac{a_{2}t^{2-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{-\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}t^{k-\alpha}.

Note that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\dfrac{\,\,\,\,\frac{a_{k+1}}{k+1-\alpha}t^{k+1-\alpha}\,\,\,\,}{\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}t^{k-\alpha}}\right|=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|\frac{\alpha-k}{k+1}\cdot\frac{k-\alpha}{k+1-\alpha}\cdot t\right|=t,

thus
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}t^{k-\alpha}
converges absolutely when
|t|<1
. When
x
is sufficiently close to
0
, we have
0<\frac{x}{1-x}<1,
therefore

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{k-\alpha}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}\left[1-\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{k-\alpha}\right].

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{x}{1-x}}^{1}\left(t^{-1-\alpha}+a_{1}t^{-\alpha}+a_{2}t^{1-\alpha}+a_{3}t^{2-\alpha}+\cdots\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{-\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}t^{k-\alpha}\right)\bigg|_{t=\frac{x}{1-x}}^{t=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-x)^{\alpha}x^{-\alpha}-1}{\alpha}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k-\alpha}\left[1-\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right)^{k-\alpha}\right].

29

\displaystyle\frac{(1-x)^{\alpha}x^{-\alpha}-1}{\alpha}+\frac{a_{1}}{1-\alpha}\left(1-\dfrac{x^{1-\alpha}}{(1-x)^{1-\alpha}}\right)+\frac{a_{2}}{2-\alpha}\left(1-\dfrac{x^{2-\alpha}}{(1-x)^{2-\alpha}}\ \right)+\cdots.

27

28

\displaystyle+

Thus the finite Hilbert tranform of
f
on
(0,1)
is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(y)-f(x)}{y-x}\,dy+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(x)}{x-y}\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}\ln\left|\frac{x}{1-x}\right|-\frac{(1-x)^{\alpha}}{\alpha}-(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}g\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{1-x_{0}}\right)+(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}\ln\left|\frac{x}{1-x}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-x)^{\alpha}}{\alpha}-(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}g\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{1-x_{0}}\right),

and it is straightforward to verify that this is Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
.

\Box

Appendix A

In this section we give a proof of
Lemma
2.6
, which is based on the hint for an exercise in
[
13
]
.

Lemma 2.6
:
Let
\{f_{n}(x)\}
be a sequence of differentiable functions from
[a,b]
to
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose

f_{n}\to f
uniformly on
[a,b]
and

f^{\prime}_{n}\to g
uniformly on
[a,b]
, where
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then
f^{\prime}(x)=g(x)
for every
x\in[a,b]
.

Proof:
We will prove
f^{\prime}(x)=g(x)
for every
x\in[a,b]
by the definition of derivatives. Since
\{f^{\prime}_{n}\}\to g
uniformly, for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|f^{\prime}_{n}(x)-g(x)\right|<\epsilon

for all
n\geq N_{1}
and
x\in[a,b]
. For any
h\neq 0
and
x,x+h\in[a,b]
,

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{f_{m}(x+h)-f_{m}(x)}{h}-\dfrac{f_{n}(x+h)-f_{n}(x)}{h}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{(f_{m}-f_{n})(x+h)-(f_{m}-f_{n})(x)}{h}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(f^{\prime}_{m}-f^{\prime}_{n})(\zeta)\right|,

where
\zeta
is a number between
x
and
x+h
. Since
f^{\prime}_{n}\to g
uniformly, there is
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
independent of
\zeta
such that

\left|f^{\prime}_{m}(\zeta)-f^{\prime}_{n}(\zeta)\right|<\epsilon

for all
m,n\geq N_{2}
. Thus

\left|\dfrac{f_{m}(x+h)-f_{m}(x)}{h}-\dfrac{f_{n}(x+h)-f_{n}(x)}{h}\right|<\epsilon

for all
m,n\geq N_{2}
. Letting
m\to\infty
, this implies

\left|\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}-\dfrac{f_{n}(x+h)-f_{n}(x)}{h}\right|\leq\epsilon

for all
n\geq N_{2}
. Now let
N=\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
, then

\left|\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}-\dfrac{f_{N}(x+h)-f_{N}(x)}{h}\right|\leq\epsilon

and

\left|f^{\prime}_{N}(x)-g(x)\right|<\epsilon

for all
x,x+h\in[a,b]
.

For any fixed
x\in[a,b]
, by the definition of
f^{\prime}_{N}(x)
, there is
\delta>0
such that

\left|\dfrac{f_{N}(x+h)-f_{N}(x)}{h}-f^{\prime}_{N}(x)\right|<\epsilon

for all
0<|h|<\delta
. Then it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}-g(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}-\dfrac{f_{N}(x+h)-f_{N}(x)}{h}\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{f_{N}(x+h)-f_{N}(x)}{h}-f^{\prime}_{N}(x)\right|+\left|f^{\prime}_{N}(x)-g(x)\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 3\epsilon

for all
0<|h|<\delta
, therefore
f^{\prime}(x)=g(x)
.

\Box

Appendix B

In the proof of Proposition
5.1
we showed that if

f(x)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}&\,\,\text{if}\,\,|x|\leq 1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&\,\,\text{if}\,\,|x|>1,\end{cases}

then

Hf(x)=\begin{cases}x&\,\,\text{if}\,\,|x|\leq 1,\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr x-\sgn(x)\sqrt{x^{2}-1}&\,\,\text{if}\,\,|x|>1.\end{cases}

In this appendix we will show that
Hf(x)\in C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since

\lim_{x\to\infty}Hf(x)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1}{x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}=0

and

\lim_{x\to-\infty}Hf(x)=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\left(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{1}{x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}=0,

we know
Hf(x)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Since

\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)^{2}\,dx=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)^{2}}\,dx<\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{2}}<\infty

and

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\left(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)^{2}\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{1}{\left(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\right)^{2}}\,dx<\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{1}{x^{2}}<\infty,

we know
Hf(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence it remains to show
Hf(x)\in C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
. Since
Hf
is bounded, we only need to estimate
\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
when
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
. There are five cases to consider.

Case 1:
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
and
x_{1}>x_{2}\geq 1
. We have

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\left(x_{1}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right)-\left(x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}\right)\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}\right|.

We will consider two separate subcases:
1\leq x_{2}\leq 2
or
x_{2}>2
.

•

Subcase 1:
1\leq x_{2}\leq 2
.
Let
t=x_{1}-x_{2}
, then

x_{1}=x_{2}+t,

0<t<1
, and

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{t}+\dfrac{\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}+2x_{2}t+t^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sqrt{t}+\dfrac{\left(\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}+\sqrt{2x_{2}t+t^{2}}\right)-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{2x_{2}+t}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1+\sqrt{5}.

•

Subcase 2:
x_{2}>2
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}-\dfrac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}\left|1-\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}+\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left|1-\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}+\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}\right|.

The function
g(x_{1},x_{2})=\left|1-\dfrac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}+\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}\right|
is continuous for
x_{1}>x_{2}>2
, and
g(x_{1},x_{2})\to 0
when
x_{1},x_{2}\to\infty
, thus
g
is globally bounded.

In both subcases by (
35
) it follows that
\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
is bounded for
x_{1}>x_{2}\geq 1
.

Case 2:
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
and
x_{2}<x_{1}\leq-1
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\left(x_{1}+\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right)-\left(x_{2}+\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}\right)\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}\right|.

Denote
y_{1}=-x_{2}
and
y_{2}=-x_{1}
. Then
y_{1}>y_{2}\geq 1
and

\left|\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}\right|=\left|\sqrt{y_{1}-y_{2}}-\dfrac{\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}-1}-\sqrt{y_{2}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{y_{1}-y_{2}}}\right|,

which is bounded by Case 1 above.
Therefore,
\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
is bounded for
x_{2}<x_{1}\leq-1
.

Case 3:
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
and
-1<x_{2}<x_{1}<-1
. We have

\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}<1.

Case 4:
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
,
-1\leq x_{2}\leq 1
, and
x_{1}>1
. We have

\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\left|\left(x_{1}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right)-x_{2}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}=\frac{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}.

There are two subcases.

•

Subcase 1:
x_{1}-x_{2}\geq\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}
. Then

\frac{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}=\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}<\frac{x_{1}-x_{2}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}<\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}<1.

•

Subcase 2:
0<x_{1}-x_{2}<\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}
. Then

(36)

\frac{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}=\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}-(x_{1}-x_{2})}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}\\
<\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}.

From
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
,
-1\leq x_{2}\leq 1
, and
x_{1}>1
we know
1<x_{1}<2
. Thus
(x_{1}-1)(x_{1}-3)<0
, i.e.
x_{1}^{2}-4x_{1}+3<0
, which implies

0<x_{1}^{2}-1<4(x_{1}-1),

and consequently

\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}<2\sqrt{x_{1}-1}.

Then (
36
) implies

\frac{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}<\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}<2\frac{\sqrt{x_{1}-1}}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}<2.

Thus in both subcases
\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
is bounded.

Case 5:
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<1
,
-1\leq x_{1}\leq 1
, and
x_{2}<-1
. We have

\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\left|x_{1}-\left(x_{2}+\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}\right)\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}=\frac{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}.

Denote
y_{1}=-x_{2}
and
y_{2}=-x_{1}
. Then
-1\leq y_{2}\leq 1
,
y_{1}>1
, and

\frac{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}-\sqrt{x_{2}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{x_{1}-x_{2}}}=\frac{\left|y_{1}-y_{2}-\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}-1}\right|}{\sqrt{y_{1}-y_{2}}},

which is bounded by Case 4 above.

Combining all the cases above, we have proved that
\frac{\left|Hf(x_{1})-Hf(x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}
is bounded for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, so
Hf(x)\in C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
.

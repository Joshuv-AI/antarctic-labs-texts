Abstract
The solution of the nonlinear initial-value problem 𝒟 t α ​ y ​ ( t ) = − λ ​ y ​ ( t ) γ \mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}y(t)=-\lambda y(t)^{\gamma} for t > 0 t>0 with y ⁡ ( 0 ) > 0 y(0)>0 , where 𝒟 t α \mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha} is a Caputo derivative of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) and λ , γ \lambda,\gamma are positive parameters, is known to exhibit O ( t − α / γ ) O(t^{-\alpha/\gamma}) decay as t → ∞ t\to\infty . No corresponding result for any discretisation of this problem has previously been proved. In the present paper it is shown that for the class of complete monotonicity-preserving ( 𝒞 ​ ℳ \mathcal{CM} -preserving) schemes (which includes the L1 and Grünwald-Letnikov schemes) on uniform meshes { t n := n h } n = 0 ∞ \{t_{n}:=nh\}_{n=0}^{\infty} , the discrete solution also has O ( t n − α / γ ) O(t_{n}^{-\alpha/\gamma}) decay as t n → ∞ t_{n}\to\infty . Introduction

Fractional-order differential equations offer modeling properties that are superior to classical integer-order differential equations in many physical processes with non-local effects or genetic memory characteristics. In particular, fractional differential operators arise naturally in anomalous diffusion processes from a probability analysis based on a random walk model
[
GW20
,
Jin21
]
.The solutions of these fractional differential equations are of particular interest when time
t\to t_{0}
(the initial time) and when
t\to+\infty
, as in these two regimes they behave very differently from the solutions of classical (integer-order) problems.

As
t\to t_{0}
, typical solutions of these problems exhibit weak singularities. Obviously this anomaly will affect the behaviour of numerical methods, and this phenomenon has been intensively studied; see for example the survey articles
[
JLZ19
,
Sty22
]
.

The long-time behaviour of solutions as
t\to\infty
has received much less attention and this regime is the focus of our paper. We shall consider both fractional ordinary differential equations (F-ODEs) and time-fractional initial-boundary value problems (F-PDEs). The long-time behaviour for a linear F-PDE was investigated in
[
GOS18
]
, but in the present paper we shall consider nonlinear problems whose analysis is much more difficult, both theoretically and numerically
[
GW20
,
WZ20
]
.

1.1
Linear F-ODEs

As time
t\to\infty
, solutions of time-fractional differential equations usually exhibit asymptotic behaviour that is completely different from the classical integer-derivative case. While solutions of linear classical ODEs generally have exponential decay rates at equilibrium points, the solutions of F-ODEs have algebraic decay rates, leading to so-called Mittag-Leffler stability
[
Cue07
,
VZ15
]
.

It is of course desirable that as
t\to\infty
, the computed numerical solutions of F-ODEs display the same algebraic decay rates as the exact solutions of the F-ODEs. For certain linear problems, this property was shown rigorously for certain schemes in
[
Cue07
,
WZ21
]
.
The main technical tools used in these papers are the discrete Laplace transform (i.e., generating functions) and discrete Tauberian-type results (i.e., singular analysis of generating functions) — but these techniques are unsuitable for nonlinear models.

1.2
Nonlinear F-ODEs

Let
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}y(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}y^{\prime}(s)\,ds

[

,

[

Let

Then there exist positive constants

This result agrees with the linear case

Lemma

1.3
Time-fractional nonlinear subdiffusion problems

Lemma

is employed to establish decay rate estimates for exact solutions of several time-fractional nonlinear PDEs in
[

,
DVV19
,
AV19
]

Consider the initial-boundary value problem

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)+\mathcal{N}[u](x,t)=0\ \text{ for }x\in\Omega,\,t>0,\\
&u(x,t)=0\ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega,\,t>0,\\
&u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\ \text{ for }x\in\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(1.2)

where

is a possibly nonlinear operator.
Like
[

]

Again following

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{s-1+\gamma}\leq C_{s}\int_{\Omega}u^{s-1}(x,t)\mathcal{N}[u](x,t)\,dx}\quad\text{for }t>0,

Roughly speaking, the assumption (

[

]

Under the above assumptions, one has the following result.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
DVV19
, Theorem 1.1]

Let

be the solution of (
1.2
) with the structural condition (
1.3

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{\gamma}(t)}{C_{s}}}\text{ for all }t>0

(1.4)

and there exists

Lemma

1.4
Results and structure of the paper

We consider
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes, which were recently introduced in
[
LW21
]
as structure-preserving discretisations of Caputo derivatives. The well-known L1 and Grünwald-Letnikov schemes are particular examples of this class of methods.
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes have many desirable properties
[
LW21
]
, such as satifying
a discrete fractional comparison principle, enjoying good numerical stability, and preserving the monotonicity of numerical solutions of nonlinear scalar equations. In the present paper, we shall prove discrete analogues of Lemmas
1.1
and
1.2
for
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes; that is, we show that the solutions of such schemes are optimal in the sense that they have precisely the same long-time decay rates as the exact solutions of the nonlinear F-ODE (
1.1
) and F-PDE (
1.2
).

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we define
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes and recall their properties. The discrete analogue of Lemma
1.1
on uniform meshes is derived in Section
3
, using a discrete comparison principle to bound the computed solution by delicately-constructed discrete subsolutions and supersolutions. Section
4
considers the particular example of the L1 scheme, and shows that on very general nonuniform meshes one still gets the long-time decay rate of Section
3
. The results of Section
3
are used in Section
5
to establish the analogue of Lemma
1.2
on uniform temporal meshes, proving that the
L^{s}(\Omega)
norm of computed solutions of discrete-time methods for time-fractional PDEs exhibit the same long-time decay rate as the exact solution, under appropriate structural assumptions. Finally, numerical experiments in Section
6
demonstrate the sharpness of our theoretical results.

to represent a generic positive constant, which may take different values at different occasions, but is always independent of

2
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes

The sequence
\delta_{d}:=(1,0,0,...)
is the convolutional identity: one has
u*\delta_{d}=\delta_{d}*u=u
for any sequence
\omega=\{\omega_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
The
convolution inverse
of
\omega
is defined to be the sequence
\omega^{(-1)}
such that
\omega*\omega^{(-1)}=\omega^{(-1)}*\omega=\delta_{d}
. It is easy to see that
\omega^{(-1)}
exists if and only if
\omega_{0}\neq 0
.

Given a sequence
v=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots)
, its
generating function
F_{v}
is defined by

F_{v}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}v_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
where
\mathbb{C}
denotes the complex plane.
This function should be understood in the sense of analytic continuation: we choose the continuation that has the largest possible domain in the upper half-plane and is symmetric about the real axis.
For example, the generating function of the sequence
(1,1,...)
is given by
F_{1}(z):=\frac{1}{1-z}
, which is defined at all points in the complex plane except
z=1
.
It is straightforward to verify that
F_{u*v}(z)=F_{u}(z)F_{v}(z)
for any sequences
u
and
v
. Hence, the generating functions of
\omega
and
\omega^{(-1)}=:a
are related by
F_{a}(z)=\frac{1}{F_{\omega}(z)}
.

To solve the F-ODE (
1.1
), we work on a uniform mesh
\{t_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined by
t_{n}=nh
with step size
h>0
.
Consider discretisations of the Caputo fractional derivative
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})
in the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(y_{n})=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\omega_{k}(y_{n-k}-y_{0})=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\delta_{n}y_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}y_{n-k}\right)\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots{}\end{split}

(2.1)

where
\delta_{n}:=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}
and
y_{k}
denotes the numerical approximation of
y(t_{k})
for each
k
.
Schemes of this type clearly conserve mass, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(y_{n})=0
for any constant sequence
\{y_{n}\}
.

A function
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
completely monotone (
\mathcal{CM}
)

[
Jin21
, Section 3.1.2]
if
(-1)^{n}g^{(n)}(x)\geq 0
for all
x
and
n
. Its discrete analogue: a sequence
v=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots)
is said to be
\mathcal{CM}

[
Wid41
, Section III.4]
if
((I-E)^{j}v)_{k}\geq 0
for all
j,k\geq 0
, where
(Ev)_{j}:=v_{j+1}
.

Again setting
a:=\omega^{(-1)}
, we say (following
[
Lub86
]
) that the discretisation (
2.1
) is
consistent
if
h^{\alpha}F_{a}(e^{-h})=1+o(1)
as
h\to 0^{+}
. The sequence
\omega
corresponds to to the Caputo derivative
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}
, so its convolution inverse
a
corresponds to the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
\alpha
. The kernel
k_{\alpha}(t):=t_{+}^{\alpha-1}/\Gamma(\alpha)
of the Riemann-Liouville integral is evidently a completely monotone function
and we wish our discretisation (
2.1
) to inherit this property in order to preserve as much structure as possible. Thus, we say that a consistent discretisation (
2.1
) of
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}
is

\mathcal{CM}
-preserving
if the sequence
a=\omega^{(-1)}
is a
\mathcal{CM}
sequence. See
[
LW21
]
for more information about
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving discretisations of
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}
, which have several good properties.

Two particular examples of
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes are the Grünwald-Letnikov and L1 discretisations.
For the Grünwald-Letnikov formula, the weights
\omega_{k}
are the
k
-th coefficients of the generating function
F_{\omega}(z)=(1-z)^{\alpha}
; they can be computed iteratively from

\omega_{0}=1,\ \omega_{k}=\left(1-\frac{\alpha+1}{k}\right)\omega_{k-1}
for
k=1,2,\dots
.
For the L1 scheme, one has

\omega_{0}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)},\quad\omega_{k}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\left[(k+1)^{1-\alpha}-2k^{1-\alpha}+(k-1)^{1-\alpha}\right]\ \text{ for }k=1,2,\dots

and consequently
\delta_{n}=-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}=\left[(n-1)^{1-\alpha}-n^{1-\alpha}\right]/\Gamma(2-\alpha)
.

For both these schemes, the weights
\omega_{k}
and
\delta_{n}
have the convenient properties

\begin{cases}&\hbox{(i)}~\omega_{0}>0,~\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}<\dots<\omega_{n}<0\quad\hbox{(monotonicity)};\\
&\hbox{(ii)}~\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\omega_{k}=0\quad\hbox{(conservation)};\\
&\hbox{(iii)}~\omega_{n}=O(n^{-1-\alpha}),~\delta_{n}=-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}=O(n^{-\alpha})\quad\hbox{(uniform decay rate)}.\\
\end{cases}

(2.2)

These properties imply that there exist constants
0<C_{3}\leq C_{4}
, which are independent of
n
, such that

\begin{split}\frac{C_{3}}{n^{1+\alpha}}\leq|\omega_{n}|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{n^{1+\alpha}}\,,\quad\frac{C_{3}}{n^{\alpha}}\leq|\delta_{n}|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{n^{\alpha}}\,,\quad\frac{C_{3}}{n^{\alpha}}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}|\omega_{k}|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{n^{\alpha}}\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots{}\end{split}

(2.3)

In fact, all
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes satisfy (
2.2
) and (
2.3
); see
[
LL18
,
LW21
]
. These properties are our main assumptions in Section
3
when establishing the optimal long-time decay rate for numerical solutions of (
1.1
).

Remark 2.1
.

When one moves to fractional schemes that are analogues of high-order (i.e., at least second-order) classical schemes such as BDF2, Crank-Nicolson, and the trapezoidal formula, then (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) may no longer hold true
[
LW21
]
; in particular, the monotonicity property can be lost because
\omega_{k}>0
for some
k\geq 1
, and consequently it becomes very difficult to establish energy inequalities such as Lemma
5.1
below.

3
Optimal decay rate for numerical solutions of F-ODEs

In this section we shall show (Theorem
3.1
) that when the F-ODE (
1.1
) is solved numerically using a
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme on the uniform mesh
t_{n}=nh
for
n=0,1,\dots
, then the computed solution has exactly the same long-time decay rate as Lemma
1.1
showed for the exact solution of (
1.1
), i.e., its decay rate is optimal. This result will be proved analogously to the continuous case, viz., by constructing appropriate discrete subsolutions and supersolutions that share the same long-time decay rate, then applying a discrete fractional comparison principle.

Lemma 3.1

(Discrete fractional comparison principle)
.

[
LW21
, Proposition 2.3]

Let
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}
be the
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving discrete operator of (
2.1
).
Let
f(\cdot)
be nondecreasing. Suppose that the sequences
\{u_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty},\{y_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty},\{v_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
satisfy
u_{0}\leq y_{0}\leq v_{0}
and

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(u_{n})+f(u_{n})\leq 0,\quad\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(y_{n})+f(y_{n})=0,\quad 0\leq\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})+f(v_{n})\ \text{ for }n\geq 1.

Then
u_{n}\leq y_{n}\leq v_{n}
for
n=0,1,2,\dots
.

In Lemma
3.1
we say that
\{u_{n}\}
is a
discrete subsolution for
\{y_{n}\}
and
\{v_{n}\}
is a
discrete supersolution for
\{y_{n}\}
.

Lemma
3.1
is a generalisation of the comparison principle for the L1 method that was used in
[
Kop20
,
LTZ21
]
. Note that the key properties needed to establish this lemma are the sign pattern and monotonicity of the discrete coefficients described in (
2.2
(i)).

Next, observe that the discretisation (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left[-\delta_{1}v_{n}+\delta_{n}v_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k+1})v_{n-k}\right]=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}(v_{n-k}-v_{n-k+1})\text{ for }n\geq 1.

(3.1)

This reformulation resembles — more closely than (
2.1
) — the standard definition

\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}y^{\prime}(s)\,ds
of the Caputo derivative.

We can now prove one of our main results: the discrete analogue of Lemma

Theorem 3.1
.

For the model equation (
1.1

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(y_{n})=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}y_{k}+\delta_{n}y_{0}\right)=-\lambda y_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }n\geq 1,\ \text{with }y_{0}>0.\end{split}

\frac{C_{5}}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha/\gamma}}\leq y_{n}\leq\frac{C_{6}}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha/\gamma}}\text{ for }n=0,1,2,\dots

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(\cdot)

) and (

\{y_{n}\}
, considering separately the cases
1\leq n\leq n_{0}
and
n>n_{0}
.

Case I:

1\leq n\leq n_{0}
.
It follows from (
3.5
) that

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(u_{n})&=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}u_{k}+\delta_{n}u_{0}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}+\delta_{n}\right)u_{0}-\mu\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}g_{k}\right)=-\frac{\mu}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}g_{k}\right),\end{split}

since
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}+\delta_{n}=0
.
As the scheme is
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving, from (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we have
\omega_{0}>0
,
\omega_{k}<0
for
k\geq 1

and
\omega_{0}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\omega_{k}
, which imply that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}g_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega_{k}(g_{n-k}-g_{n})+\left(-\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\omega_{k}\right)g_{n}\geq\left(-\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\omega_{k}\right)g_{n}\geq\frac{C_{3}}{n^{\alpha}}g_{n}.

Hence, recalling the definitions of
\mu
and
g_{n}
, we get

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(u_{n})\leq-\frac{\mu}{h^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{C_{3}}{n^{\alpha}}\,g_{n}\leq-\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}\leq-\lambda(u_{0}-\mu g_{n})^{\gamma}=-\lambda u_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }1\leq n\leq n_{0}.

(3.8)

Case II:

n\geq n_{0}+1
.
Set

I_{1}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{n-k}u_{k}+\delta_{n}u_{0}
and
I_{2}:=\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n}\omega_{n-k}u_{k}.

Then

\begin{split}I_{1}=\left(\delta_{n}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{n-k}\right)u_{0}-\mu\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{n-k}g_{k}=-\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-n_{0}-1}\omega_{k}\right)u_{0}-\mu\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\omega_{n-k}g_{k}.\end{split}

Hence, recalling that
\omega_{k}<0
for
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}(u_{n}-u_{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-n_{0}-1}|\omega_{k}|(u_{0}-u_{n-k})+\mu\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}|\omega_{n-k}|g_{k}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}(u_{n}-u_{n_{0}})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-n_{0}-1}|\omega_{k}|(u_{n_{0}}-u_{n-k})+\mu\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}|\omega_{n-k}|g_{k}+\mu g_{n_{0}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-n_{0}-1}|\omega_{k}|-\omega_{0}\right),

using the identities
u_{0}=u_{n_{0}}-u_{n_{0}}+u_{0}=u_{n_{0}}+\mu g_{n_{0}}

and
u_{0}-u_{n-k}=u_{0}-u_{n_{0}}+u_{n_{0}}-u_{n-k}=\mu g_{n_{0}}+u_{n_{0}}-u_{n-k}
.
Here the first two terms are nonpositive because
\omega_{0}>0
and
\{u_{n}\}
is monotonically decreasing, so

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}|\omega_{n-k}|g_{k}+\mu g_{n_{0}}\sum_{k=n-n_{0}}^{\infty}\omega_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\mu g_{n_{0}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}|\omega_{n-k}|+\mu g_{n_{0}}\sum_{k=n-n_{0}}^{\infty}\omega_{k}=\mu g_{n_{0}}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\omega_{k}.

According to (
2.3
) and the definitions of
\mu
and
g_{n_{0}}
, we get that

I_{1}+I_{2}\leq-\mu g_{n_{0}}\frac{C_{3}}{n^{\alpha}}=-\frac{\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}t_{n_{0}}^{\alpha}}{n^{\alpha}}
.
Thus

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(u_{n})=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}(I_{1}+I_{2})\leq-\frac{\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}t_{n_{0}}^{\alpha}}{h^{\alpha}n^{\alpha}}=-\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}t_{n_{0}}^{\alpha}t_{n}^{-\alpha}\leq-\lambda u_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }n>n_{0}.

(3.9)

from the definitions of
u_{n}
and
C_{7}
.

The inequalities (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), together with
u_{0}=y_{0}
,
show that
\{u_{n}\}
is a discrete subsolution for
\{y_{n}\}
.

We now construct a discrete supersolution
\{v_{n}\}
for
\{y_{n}\}
.
Define the mesh point

t_{n_{1}}:=\min\left\{t_{n}:t_{n}^{\alpha}\geq\frac{y_{0}^{1-\gamma}}{\lambda}\max\left\{\frac{\alpha 2^{\alpha}C_{4}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}\,,C_{8}\right\}\right\},

(3.10)

where, recalling the positive constant
C_{4}
in (
2.3
), we define

C_{8}:=2^{\alpha}C_{4}\left[\frac{\alpha 2^{\alpha/\gamma}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}+1\right].

Then define the function

v(t):=\begin{cases}y_{0}&\text{for }0\leq t\leq t_{n_{1}},\\
C_{9}t^{-\alpha/\gamma}&\text{for }t_{n_{1}}<t<\infty,\end{cases}\ \text{with }\ C_{9}:=y_{0}t_{n_{1}}^{\alpha/\gamma}.

(3.11)

The choice of
C_{9}
ensures that
v\in C[0,\infty)
. Clearly
v
is positive and monotonically decreasing on
[0,\infty)
. Set
v_{n}:=v(t_{n})
for
n=1,2,\dots

From (
2.1
) we have

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})=0>-\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots,{n_{1}}.

(3.12)

Next, suppose that
{n_{1}}<n\leq 2{n_{1}}
.
Recalling (
3.1
) and the definition of
v(t)
, since
\delta_{k}<0
for
k\geq 1
one gets

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})=h^{-\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n-{n_{1}}}\delta_{k}(v_{n-k}-v_{n-k+1})\\
\geq h^{-\alpha}(v_{n_{1}}-v_{{n_{1}}+1})\sum_{k=1}^{n-{n_{1}}}\delta_{k};

by (
2.3
) and
n\leq 2{n_{1}}
one has

\sum_{k=1}^{n-{n_{1}}}|\delta_{k}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-{n_{1}}}C_{4}k^{-\alpha}\leq C_{4}\int_{s=0}^{n-{n_{1}}}s^{-\alpha}ds=\frac{C_{4}(n-{n_{1}})^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\leq\frac{C_{4}{n_{1}}^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha},

so

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})\geq-\frac{C_{4}}{1-\alpha}h^{-\alpha}(v_{n_{1}}-v_{{n_{1}}+1}){n_{1}}^{1-\alpha}.

(3.13)

But
v^{\prime}<0
and
v^{\prime\prime}>0
on
(t_{n_{1}},t_{{n_{1}}+1})
imply that

0<v_{n_{1}}-v_{{n_{1}}+1}\leq-hv^{\prime}(t_{n_{1}})=h\frac{C_{9}\alpha}{\gamma}t_{n_{1}}^{-1-\alpha/\gamma}=hy_{0}\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}t_{n_{1}}^{-1}

by the definition of
C_{9}
. Hence (
3.13
) yields

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})\geq-\frac{y_{0}\alpha C_{4}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}t_{n_{1}}^{-\alpha}\geq-\frac{y_{0}\alpha 2^{\alpha}C_{4}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}t_{n}^{-\alpha}\ \text{ since }t_{n}\leq 2t_{n_{1}}.

(3.14)

Now the definitions of
t_{n_{1}}
and
C_{9}
give

\frac{\alpha 2^{\alpha}C_{4}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}\leq\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma-1}t_{n_{1}}^{\alpha}=\lambda C_{9}^{\gamma}y_{0}^{-1}
,
so from (
3.14
) we get the desired inequality

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})\geq-\lambda C_{9}^{\gamma}t_{n}^{-\alpha}=-\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }{n_{1}}<n\leq 2{n_{1}}.

(3.15)

Finally, suppose that
n>2{n_{1}}
. Set
n^{\prime}:=\lceil n/2\rceil
, so
n^{\prime}>{n_{1}}
and
n^{\prime}\leq(n+1)/2
.
Using (
3.1
), the definition of
v(t)
and (
2.3
), one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})

\displaystyle=h^{-\alpha}\sum_{k=1}^{n-{n_{1}}}\delta_{k}(v_{n-k}-v_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle\geq-h^{-\alpha}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n-n^{\prime}}+\sum_{k=n-n^{\prime}+1}^{n-{n_{1}}}\right)C_{4}k^{-\alpha}(v_{n-k}-v_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle\geq-C_{4}h^{-\alpha}\left[(v_{n^{\prime}}-v_{n^{\prime}+1})\sum_{k=1}^{n-n^{\prime}}k^{-\alpha}+(n-n^{\prime}+1)^{-\alpha}\sum_{k=n-n^{\prime}+1}^{n-{n_{1}}}(v_{n-k}-v_{n-k+1})\right]

\displaystyle\geq-C_{4}h^{-\alpha}\left[h|v^{\prime}(t_{n^{\prime}})|\int_{s=0}^{n-n^{\prime}}s^{-\alpha}ds+\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)^{-\alpha}(v_{n_{1}}-v_{n^{\prime}})\right].

(3.16)

But the definitions of
v(t)
and
C_{9}
, and
n^{\prime}\geq n/2
, give

|v^{\prime}(t_{n^{\prime}})|=\frac{C_{9}\alpha}{\gamma}t_{n^{\prime}}^{-1-\alpha/\gamma}\leq y_{0}t_{n_{1}}^{\alpha/\gamma}\cdot\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\left(\frac{t_{n}}{2}\right)^{-1-\alpha/\gamma}<y_{0}\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\,2^{1+\alpha/\gamma}t_{n}^{-1}\ \text{ as }t_{n_{1}}<t_{n}.

Substituting this inequality into (
3.16
), recalling that
v_{n_{1}}=y_{0}
and discarding the final term
v_{n^{\prime}}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})

\displaystyle\geq-C_{4}y_{0}h^{-\alpha}\left[\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\,2^{1+\alpha/\gamma}ht_{n}^{-1}\frac{(n-n^{\prime})^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}+2^{\alpha}n^{-\alpha}\right].

\displaystyle\geq-C_{4}y_{0}2^{\alpha}t_{n}^{-\alpha}\left[\frac{\alpha 2^{\alpha/\gamma}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}+1\right],

(3.17)

where we used
ht_{n}^{-1}=n^{-1},\,n-n^{\prime}\leq n/2
and
h^{-\alpha}n^{-\alpha}=t_{n}^{-\alpha}
. The definitions of
C_{8},t_{n_{1}},C_{9}
and
v(t)
enable us to deduce from (
3.17
) that

\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})\geq-C_{8}y_{0}t_{n}^{-\alpha}\geq-\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}t_{n_{1}}^{\alpha}t_{n}^{-\alpha}=-\lambda C_{9}^{\gamma}t_{n}^{-\alpha}=-\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }n>2{n_{1}}.

(3.18)

Combining (
3.12
), (
3.15
) and (
3.18
) gives
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(v_{n})\geq-\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}
for all
n\geq 1
; as
v_{0}=y_{0}
, we have shown that
\{v_{n}\}
is a supersolution for
\{y_{n}\}
.

It is evident that
u_{n}=C_{7}t_{n}^{-\alpha/\gamma}
and
v_{n}\leq y_{0}(1+t_{n}^{-\alpha/\gamma})
for
t_{n}\geq\max\{t_{n_{0}},t_{n_{1}}\}
, so the desired bounds (
3.3
) now follow immediately from Lemma
3.1
on recalling (
3.7
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

The discrete subsolution and discrete supersolution constructed in the proof of Theorem
3.1
are discrete analogues of
the subsolution and supersolution for the continuous problem (
1.1
) in
[
VZ15
, Section 7]
. This agreement is unsurprising since these functions are natural choices to act as sub- and supersolutions. Note however that the discrete analysis above is more complicated than the continuous analysis in
[
VZ15
, Section 7]
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the linear case
\gamma=1
, the long-time numerical decay rate
y_{n}=O(t_{n}^{-\alpha})
was proved in
[
WZ21
]
via a singularity analysis of the generating function.
Theorem
3.1
now gives an alternative proof of this result based on discrete upper and lower solutions and the fractional comparison principle.

4
The L1 scheme on general meshes

Theorem
3.1
is applicable to all
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes on uniform meshes. In the present section we consider a particular
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme — the L1 scheme — and extend Theorem
3.1
to this scheme on very general meshes.

Working on (possibly) nonuniform meshes implies that the bounds (
2.3
) can no longer be assumed, but on the other hand the L1 scheme has the special property (
4.3
) which will be used several times in our analysis.

Let the mesh
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots
be arbitrary. Set
\tau_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
. Let
y_{n}
denote the computed solution at each mesh point
t_{n}
for
n\geq 1
. Then the L1 discretisation
\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}
can be written as (see for example
[
Sty22
]
)

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}(y_{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\left[d_{n,1}y_{n}-d_{n,n}y_{0}+\sum^{n-1}_{k=1}y_{n-k}\left(d_{n,k+1}-d_{n,k}\right)\right],

(4.1)

where

d_{n,k}:=\frac{(t_{n}-t_{n-k})^{1-\alpha}-(t_{n}-t_{n-k+1})^{1-\alpha}}{\tau_{n-k+1}}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\tau_{n-k+1}}\int_{s=t_{n-k}}^{t_{n-k+1}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}ds.

Clearly
d_{n,k}
is
1-\alpha
times the mean value of the function
s\mapsto(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}
on the interval
[t_{n-k},t_{n-k+1}]
; as this function is positive and increasing, it follows that
0<d_{n,k+1}<d_{n,k}
for all
k
and
n
.

Thus, our computed discrete solution
\{y_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of (
1.1
) is defined by

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}(y_{n})=-\lambda y_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots,\ \text{ with }y_{0}\text{ given in }\eqref{FODE}.

(4.2)

We aim to derive a decay result for
\{y_{n}\}
that is similar to Theorem
3.1
.

Our analysis will use a defining property of the L1 discretisation: if
\phi\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
\phi_{n}:=\phi(t_{n})
for all
n
, then

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}(\phi_{n})=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}\phi_{I}(t_{n})\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots,

(4.3)

where
\phi_{I}
is the piecewise linear interpolant to
\phi
on the mesh,
i.e.,
\phi_{I}(t)=\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\phi_{n}+\frac{t_{n}-t}{t_{n}-t_{n-1}}\phi_{n-1}
for
t\in[t_{n-1},t_{n}]
.

Note that Lemma
3.1
remains valid when
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}
on a uniform mesh is replaced by
\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}
on an arbitrary mesh, since its proof requires only the scheme properties (in the L1 scheme notation)
0<d_{n,k+1}<d_{n,k}
for all
k
and
n
, and
\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}(z_{n})=0
for any constant sequence
\{z_{n}\}
. We shall use this lemma in our analysis.

The construction of the subsolution and supersolution used in Section
4
is related to Section
3
, but there are some significant differences because (
2.3
) is no longer available.

4.1
Discrete subsolution

Define

\mu:=\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}\max\left\{1,2\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{\gamma}\Gamma(1+\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)\right\}.

(4.4)

Here
\Gamma(1+\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)=\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)=\alpha\pi/\sin(\alpha\pi)>1
by
[
Die10
, Theorem D.3]
.
Assume that some mesh point
\hat{t}
satisfies

\left(\frac{y_{0}\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{4\mu}\right)^{1/\alpha}\leq\hat{t}\leq\left(\frac{y_{0}\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{2\mu}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

(4.5)

(One can ensure that (
4.5
) is satisfied by sufficient mesh refinement near
t^{\alpha}=y_{0}\Gamma(1+\alpha)/(2\mu)
; this is not restrictive.)
For any constant
\beta>0
, define
g_{\beta}(s)=s^{\beta-1}/\Gamma(\beta)
for
s\in(0,\infty)
, (if
\beta>1
we also define
g_{\beta}(0)=0
).
Then define the function

v(t):=\begin{cases}y_{0}-\mu g_{1+\alpha}(t)&\text{for }0\leq t\leq\hat{t},\\
C_{1}^{\prime}t^{-\alpha/\gamma}&\text{for }\hat{t}<t<\infty,\end{cases}\ \text{with }\ C_{1}^{\prime}:=\hat{t}^{\alpha/\gamma}\left(y_{0}-\mu g_{1+\alpha}(\hat{t})\right).

(4.6)

The choice of
C_{1}^{\prime}
ensures that
v\in C[0,\infty)
.
Note that
v(\hat{t})=y_{0}-\mu g_{1+\alpha}\left(\hat{t}\right)
and (
4.5
) imply that

\frac{y_{0}}{2}\leq v(\hat{t})\leq\frac{3y_{0}}{4}\,;

(4.7)

it follows that
v(t)>0
for
t\in[0,\infty)
and
C_{1}^{\prime}\geq\frac{y_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{y_{0}\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{4\mu}\right)^{1/\gamma}
. Furthermore, clearly
v
is a strictly decreasing function.

Set
v_{n}=v(t_{n})
for
n=0,1,\dots
For
0<t_{k}\leq t_{n}\leq\hat{t}
, by Chebyshev’s integral inequality
[
HLP88
, Theorem 43]
,
since
s\mapsto(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}
is increasing while
s\mapsto g_{1+\alpha}^{\prime}(s)
is decreasing, one has

\displaystyle\int_{s=t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}g_{1+\alpha}^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t_{k}-t_{k-1}}\left(\int_{s=t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\right)\left(\int_{s=t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}g_{1+\alpha}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{1+\alpha}(t_{k})-g_{1+\alpha}(t_{k-1})}{t_{k}-t_{k-1}}\int_{s=t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{s=t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}(g_{1+\alpha})_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds.

Hence, recalling (
4.3
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}g_{1+\alpha}(t_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}(g_{1+\alpha})_{I}(t_{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}(g_{1+\alpha})_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}g_{1+\alpha}^{\prime}(s)\,ds=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}g^{1+\alpha}(t_{n})=1

from
[
Die10
, p.193]
. Consequently

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}(v_{n})\leq-\mu\leq-\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}\leq-\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }0<t_{n}<\hat{t}

(4.8)

by (
4.4
) and the definition of
v
.

Now suppose that
t_{n}>\hat{t}
. Then

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}(v_{n})=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}v_{I}(t_{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}v_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds
, and since
v
is decreasing one has

\int_{s=0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}v_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{s=0}^{\hat{t}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}v_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\leq t_{n}^{-\alpha}\int_{s=0}^{\hat{t}}v_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds=t_{n}^{-\alpha}\left[v(\hat{t})-v(0)\right]\leq-\frac{t_{n}^{-\alpha}y_{0}}{4}

by (
4.7
). Hence for
t_{n}>\hat{t}
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}v_{n}+\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq t_{n}^{-\alpha}\left[-\frac{y_{0}}{4\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\lambda C_{1}^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=t_{n}^{-\alpha}\left[-\frac{y_{0}}{4\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\lambda\hat{t}^{\alpha}\left(\frac{3y_{0}}{4}\right)^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq t_{n}^{-\alpha}\left[-\frac{y_{0}}{4\Gamma(1-\alpha)}+\lambda\frac{y_{0}\Gamma(1+\alpha)}{2\mu}\left(\frac{3y_{0}}{4}\right)^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 0,

(4.9)

where we used (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and finally (
4.5
) and the definition (
4.4
) of
\mu
.

From (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) we see that
\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}v_{n}\leq-\lambda v_{n}^{\gamma}
for all
t_{n}>0
; as
v_{0}=y_{0}
, we have shown that
\{v_{n}\}
is a subsolution for
\{y_{n}\}
.

4.2
Discrete supersolution

In this section, unlike Section
4.1
where the mesh was arbitrary apart from the requirement (
4.5
), we impose the following mild condition.

Assumption 4.1
.

Assume that the mesh
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\dots
satisfies the condition

\max_{n\geq 2}\left\{\frac{\tau_{n}}{\tau_{n-1}}\,,\frac{\tau_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}}\right\}\leq K\ \text{for some fixed constant }K\geq 1.

This assumption implies that for each
n\geq 2
, some mesh point is a crude approximation of
t_{n}/2
, in the sense of the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For each
t_{n}
with
n\geq 2
, there is a mesh point
t_{n^{*}}
with the property

\frac{t_{n}}{K+2}\leq t_{n^{*}}\leq\frac{(K+1)t_{n}}{K+2}.

Proof.

Suppose that the result is false. Then we can choose
t_{n}
with
n\geq 2
such that the interval

[t_{n}/(K+2),\,(K+1)t_{n}/(K+2)]
contains no mesh point. Since
n\geq 2
, at least one of the intervals
(0,t_{n}/(K+2))

and
((K+1)t_{n}/(K+2),t_{n})
contains a mesh point; let us say it is
(0,t_{n}/(K+2))
as by symmetry the other case is similar.
Set
t_{m}=\max_{j}\{t_{j}:0<t_{j}<t_{n}/(K+2)\}
. Then we must have
t_{m+1}\in((K+1)t_{n}/(K+2),t_{n}]
. Consequently

\tau_{m}=t_{m}-t_{m-1}\leq t_{m}<t_{n}/(K+2)
and

\tau_{m+1}=t_{m+1}-t_{m}>\frac{(K+1)t_{n}}{K+2}-\frac{t_{n}}{K+2}=\frac{Kt_{n}}{K+2}\,,

which together imply that
\tau_{m+1}/\tau_{m}>K
, contradicting our hypothesis.
∎

We now construct our supersolution.
Define
t_{N}>0
to be the smallest mesh point satisfying

t_{N}^{\alpha}\geq\frac{y_{0}^{1-\gamma}}{\lambda}\max\left\{\frac{\alpha(K+1)^{1-\alpha}(K+2)^{\alpha}}{\gamma\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\,,C_{2}^{\prime}\right\},

(4.10)

where

C_{2}^{\prime}:=\frac{\left(K+2\right)^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left\{1+\frac{\alpha\left(K+1\right)^{1-\alpha}\left(K+2\right)^{\alpha/\gamma}}{\gamma(1-\alpha)}\right\}.

Then define the function

w(t):=\begin{cases}y_{0}&\text{for }0\leq t\leq t_{N},\\
C_{3}^{\prime}t^{-\alpha/\gamma}&\text{for }t_{N}<t<\infty,\end{cases}\ \text{with }\ C_{3}^{\prime}:=y_{0}t_{N}^{\alpha/\gamma}.

(4.11)

Clearly
w\in C[0,\infty)
is positive and monotonically decreasing. Set
w_{n}:=w(t_{n})
for
n=1,2,\dots

By (
4.3
) we have

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}w_{I}(t_{n})=0>-\lambda w_{n}\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots,N.

(4.12)

Next, suppose that
t_{n}\in(t_{N},(K+2)t_{N}]
. Set
w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{N}^{+}):=\lim_{s\to t_{N}+0}w_{I}^{\prime}(s)
. Note that
w_{I}^{\prime}(s)
is negative and monotonically increasing almost everywhere on
(t_{N},\infty)
. Again using (
4.3
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}w_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=t_{N}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}w_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{N}^{+})}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=t_{N}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{N}^{+})}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}(t_{n}-t_{N})^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{N}^{+})\left((K+1)t_{N}\right)^{1-\alpha}}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\,,

(4.13)

as
t_{n}\leq(K+2)t_{N}
. Now
0>w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{N}^{+})\geq w^{\prime}(t_{N}^{+})=-C_{3}^{\prime}(\alpha/\gamma)t_{N}^{-1-\alpha/\gamma}

because
w^{\prime\prime}>0
on
(t_{N},t_{N+1})
, so (
4.13
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{C_{3}^{\prime}\alpha(K+1)^{1-\alpha}}{\gamma\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\,t_{N}^{-\alpha-\alpha/\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\frac{y_{0}\alpha(K+1)^{1-\alpha}}{\gamma\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\,t_{N}^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{y_{0}\alpha(K+1)^{1-\alpha}}{\gamma\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\,\left(\frac{t_{n}}{K+2}\right)^{-\alpha},

(4.14)

where we used
C_{3}^{\prime}=y_{0}t_{N}^{\alpha/\gamma}
and
t_{n}\leq(K+2)t_{N}
.
But

\frac{\alpha(K+1)^{1-\alpha}(K+2)^{\alpha}}{\gamma\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\leq\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma-1}t_{N}^{\alpha}=\lambda C_{3}^{\prime\gamma}y_{0}^{-1}

by (
4.10
) and (
4.11
); this inequality and (
4.14
) give

\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}\geq-\lambda C_{3}^{\prime\gamma}t_{n}^{-\alpha}=-\lambda w_{n}^{\gamma}\ \text{ for }t_{n}\in(t_{N},(K+2)t_{N}].

(4.15)

Finally, suppose that
t_{n}>(K+2)t_{N}
. This implies that
n\geq 2
.
Then by (
4.3
) and Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{s=t_{N}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}w_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left(\int_{s=t_{N}}^{t_{n^{*}}}+\int_{s=t_{n^{*}}}^{t_{n}}\right)(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}w_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds

— note that
t_{n^{*}}>t_{N}
because
t_{n}>(K+2)t_{N}
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left[(t_{n}-t_{n^{*}})^{-\alpha}\int_{s=t_{N}}^{t_{n^{*}}}w_{I}^{\prime}(s)\,ds+w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{n^{*}}^{+})\int_{s=t_{n^{*}}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left\{(t_{n}-t_{n^{*}})^{-\alpha}\left[w(t_{n^{*}})-w(t_{N})\right]+w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{n^{*}}^{+})\frac{(t_{n}-t_{n^{*}})^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left\{\left(\frac{t_{n}}{K+2}\right)^{-\alpha}(-y_{0})+w^{\prime}(t_{n^{*}})\left(\frac{K+1}{K+2}\right)^{1-\alpha}\frac{t_{n}^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right\}

(4.16)

by Lemma
4.1
and
w^{\prime}(t_{n^{*}})<w_{I}^{\prime}(t_{n^{*}}^{+})
since
w^{\prime\prime}>0
on
(t_{N},\infty)
. But

w^{\prime}(t_{n^{*}})=-C_{3}^{\prime}\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}t_{n^{*}}^{-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}-1}\geq-y_{0}t_{N}^{\alpha/\gamma}\,\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\left(\frac{t_{n}}{K+2}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}-1}>-y_{0}\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{K+2}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}-1}t_{n}^{-1}

by the definition of
C_{3}^{\prime}
, another appeal to Lemma
4.1
, and
t_{N}<t_{n}
.
Substituting this inequality into (
4.16
) yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}

\displaystyle>-\frac{y_{0}t_{n}^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left\{\left(K+2\right)^{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\left(K+2\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}+1}\left(\frac{K+1}{K+2}\right)^{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle=-C_{2}^{\prime}y_{0}t_{n}^{-\alpha}\geq-\lambda w_{n}^{\gamma}

(4.17)

since
\lambda C_{3}^{\prime\gamma}=\lambda y_{0}^{\gamma}t_{N}^{\alpha}\geq C_{2}^{\prime}y_{0}
by the definition (
4.10
) of
t_{N}
.

From (
4.12
), (
4.15
) and (
4.17
), we see that
\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}w_{n}\geq-\lambda w_{n}^{\gamma}
for all
t_{n}>0
. Recall that
w_{0}=y_{0}
. Thus,
\{w_{n}\}
is a supersolution for
\{y_{n}\}
.

We have now proved the following optimal decay rate result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let the mesh satisfy (
4.5
) and Assumption
4.1
.
When the L1 scheme (
4.2
) is used to solve (
1.1
), there exist positive constants
C_{5}^{\prime}
and
C_{6}^{\prime}
, which are independent of
n
and the mesh, such that

\frac{C_{5}^{\prime}}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha/\gamma}}\leq y_{n}\leq\frac{C_{6}^{\prime}}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha/\gamma}}\text{ for }n=0,1,2,\dots

5
Optimal decay rate for numerical solutions of F-PDEs

Consider F-PDEs of the form (
1.2
) that satisfy the structural condition (
1.3
).
In this section we shall how the results of Section
3
for F-ODEs can be extended to this class of F-PDEs.

Consider semi-discretisations of (
1.2
) where the fractional derivative
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u
is discretised on a uniform temporal mesh
\{t_{n}:t_{n}=nh\ \text{ for }n=0,1,2,\dots\}
by a
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme. Writing
U^{n}=U^{n}(x)
for the approximation of
u(\cdot,t_{n})
at each
t_{n}
, we have

\begin{cases}&\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(U^{n})+\mathcal{N}[U^{n}](x)=0\ \text{ for }x\in\Omega\text{ and }n=1,2,\dots,\\
&U^{n}(x)=0\ \text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega\text{ and }n=1,2,\dots,\\
&U^{0}(x)=u_{0}(x)\ \text{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.1)

As for (
1.2
) in Section
1.3
, we assume that
U^{0}\geq 0
,
U^{0}\not\equiv 0
, and
U^{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
.
We also assume that for each
n\geq 1
the discrete solution
U^{n}\not\equiv 0
,
U^{n}\in L^{s}(\Omega)
for some
s\in(1,\infty)

and
U^{n}
satisfies a structural condition similar to (
1.3
):

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{s-1+\gamma}\leq C_{s}\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\mathcal{N}[U^{n}](x)dx,}

(5.2)

where
\gamma\in(0,\infty)

and some constant
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{s}}>0
that is independent of
n
and
h
.

Under these hypotheses, our aim is to derive results for
\|U^{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
to show that this semidiscrete solution has the same long-time power-law decay as the continuous solution in Lemma
1.2
.
We begin with the following technical lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s
be as in (
5.2
). Assume that
U^{n}\in L^{s}(\Omega)
for all
n\geq 0
.
Set
V^{n}=\|U^{n}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{s}}(\Omega)}
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}(\cdot)
be a
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme.
Then

\begin{split}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(V^{n}\right)^{s-1}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(V^{n}\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)(x)\,dx\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots{}\end{split}

(5.3)

Proof.

For
n\geq 1
it follows from (
2.1
) that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\cdot\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{j}U^{n-j}+\delta_{n}U^{0}\right)(x)dx\\
&=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\omega_{0}\|U^{n}\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega_{j}\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}U^{n-j}(x)dx+\delta_{n}\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}U^{0}(x)dx\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\omega_{0}\|U^{n}\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega_{j}\left(\|U^{n-j}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\|(U^{n})^{s-1}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)+\delta_{n}\left(\|U^{0}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\|(U^{n})^{s-1}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\right)\right),\\
\end{split}

using
\delta_{n}<0
for
n\geq 1
,
\omega_{k}<0
for
k\geq 1
, and Hölder’s inequality with
q
defined by

\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
But
\|(U^{n})^{s-1}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|U^{n}(x)\right|^{q(s-1)}dx\right)^{1/q}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|U^{n}(x)\right|^{\frac{s}{s-1}(s-1)}dx\right)^{\frac{s-1}{s}}=\|(U^{n})\|^{s-1}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}
,
so we get

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)(x)dx&\geq\|(U^{n})\|^{s-1}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\cdot\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\omega_{0}\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega_{j}\|U^{n-j}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}+\delta_{n}\|U^{0}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\right)\\
&=\left(V^{n}\right)^{s-1}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(V^{n}\right).\end{split}

∎

The discrete energy-type inequality of this lemma is often a key step in the analysis of F-PDEs. An alternative discrete energy inequality, where
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}s=2}
, was proved for the Grünwald-Letnikov and L1 discretisations applied to initial-value problems
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}y(t)=f(t,y(t))
in
[
WXZ20
, Lemma 3.2]
.

The proof of Lemma
5.1
depends only on the helpful signs of the coefficients in
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving schemes; the bounds of (
2.3
) are not needed. This important observation shows that this lemma remains valid if we replace the
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme by the L1 discretization that was defined in (
4.1
) for nonuniform meshes.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume the hypotheses of Lemma
5.1
. Then for the L1 scheme defined in (
4.1
) on an arbitrary mesh, one has

\begin{split}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(V^{n}\right)^{s-1}\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}\left(V^{n}\right)\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}}\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)(x)\,dx\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots{}\end{split}

(5.4)

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Lemma
5.1
, for
n\geq 1
it follows from (
4.1
) that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}&\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\cdot\mathcal{D}_{L1}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\left[d_{n,1}U_{n}-d_{n,n}U_{0}+\sum^{n-1}_{k=1}U_{n-k}\left(d_{n,k+1}-d_{n,k}\right)\right]dx,\\
\end{split}

where the scheme coefficients have the helpful property
0<d_{n,k+1}<d_{n,k}
for all
k
and
n
. One can use this property to complete the argument by imitating the proof of Lemma
5.1
; we omit the details.
∎

Our main result in this section is the following theorem, which is a discrete analogue of Lemma
1.2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}
be a
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme and let
U^{n}\geq 0
be the solution of (
5.1
) for
n=0,1,\dots
with the structural condition (
5.2
). Then

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(\|U^{n}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{s}(\Omega)}}\right)\leq-\frac{\|U^{n}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{s}(\Omega)}}^{\gamma}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{s}}}\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots{}\end{split}

(5.5)

Furthermore, there exists a positive constant
h_{0}
such that for any
h
satisfying
0<h\leq h_{0}
, one has

\begin{split}\|U^{n}\|_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{s}(\Omega)}}\leq\frac{C_{d}^{*}}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha/\gamma}}\ \text{ for }n=1,2,\dots,\end{split}

(5.6)

where the constant
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{s}^{*}=C_{s}^{*}(C_{s},\gamma,\alpha,U^{0})}>0
.

If the
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme in (
5.1
) is replaced by the L1 scheme (
4.1
) on an arbitrary mesh, then one again obtains (
5.5
). If in addition this mesh satisfies Assumption
4.1
, then one also obtains (
5.6
).

Proof.

Let
n\geq 1
. Multiplying both sides of the PDE in (
5.1
) by
\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}
and integrating with respect to
x
, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)(x)dx=-\int_{\Omega}\left(U^{n}(x)\right)^{s-1}\mathcal{N}[U^{n}](x)dx.\end{split}

Now an appeal to Lemma
5.1
and the structural assumption (
5.2
) yield

\begin{split}\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{s-1}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\right)\leq-\frac{\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}^{s-1+\gamma}}{C_{d}}.\end{split}

The inequality (
5.5
) follows immediately if
\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\neq 0
. If
\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}=0
, then

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}\left(U^{n}\right)=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}U^{n-k}+\delta_{n}U^{0}\right)\leq 0\end{split}

because of the hypothesis
U^{k}\geq 0
for all
k
and the scheme properties
\delta_{n}<0
and
\omega_{k}<0
for
k\geq 1
. Thus we have again obtained (
5.5
).

Now that (
5.5
) has been proved, the inequality (
5.6
) follows immediately from Lemma
3.1
and the supersolution analysis in Theorem
3.1
.

The proof of (
5.5
) for the L1 scheme (
4.1
) on an arbitrary mesh follows from Lemma
5.2
in a similar manner. As we pointed out in Section
4
, Lemma
3.1
is easily extended to the L1 scheme on arbitrary meshes since its proof depends only on the signs of the coefficients in the scheme.
Hence if the mesh satisfies Assumption
4.1
, then the supersolution analysis of Section
4.2
yields (
5.6
).
∎

Many concrete examples that satisfy the structural assumptions (
1.3
) or (
5.2
) can be found in
[
VZ15
,
DVV19
,
AV19
]
.
We will use these specific examples to support our theoretical results by numerical experiments.

6.2
Nonlinear F-PDEs

The estimates of the decay rate of the true solution in Lemma
1.2
and its discrete analogue in Theorem
5.1
are independent of the dimension
d
of the spatial region
\Omega
. In our numerical examples we shall take
d=2
. Thus, consider
F-PDEs of the form (
1.2
) with
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
a bounded convex domain,
where we discretize in space by a conforming piecewise linear finite element method and in time by a
\mathcal{CM}
-preserving scheme.

First, we describe briefly the iterative numerical algorithm for nonlinear fully discrete schemes. Consider the model F-PDE

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)=\nabla\cdot\left(a(u,\nabla u)\nabla u\right)\ \text{ for }t>0,\ x\in\Omega,

(6.2)

where
a(u,\nabla u)
is a known nonlinear function; this F-PDE is subject to a Dirichlet boundary condition
u(x,t)=0
for
x\in\partial\Omega
and has initial value
u(x,0)=u_{0}
, .

The time discretisation of (
6.2
) yields
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}U^{n}(x)=\nabla\cdot\left(a(U^{n},\nabla U^{n})\nabla U^{n}\right)
for
n\geq 1
, where
U^{n}(x)\approx u(x,t_{n})
at
t_{n}=nh
with time-step
h>0
.

Set
\beta:=h^{\alpha}/\omega_{0}
. For the particular value
n=1
, the discretisation is
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}U^{1}(x)=(1/\beta)\left(U^{1}(x)-U^{0}(x)\right)
. This gives
U^{1}(x)=U^{0}(x)+\beta\nabla\cdot(a(U^{1}(x),\nabla U^{n}(x))\nabla U^{1}(x))
.

For
n\geq 2
, one has
\mathcal{D}_{h}^{\alpha}U^{n}(x)=\frac{1}{h^{\alpha}}(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}U^{n-k}+\delta_{n}U^{0})
,
where
\delta_{n}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\omega_{k}
; see equation (
2.1
). This yields

U^{n}(x)=\beta\nabla\cdot\left(a(U^{n}(x),\nabla U^{n}(x))\nabla U^{n}(x)\right)-b_{n},

where
b_{n}:=\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}(\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega_{k}U^{n-k}+\delta_{n}U^{0})
. Then for each
n\geq 1
, we need to solve numerically the nonlinear elliptic-type equation

\begin{split}U^{n}(x)=\beta\nabla\cdot\left(a(U^{n}(x),\nabla U^{n}(x))\nabla U^{n}(x)\right)-b_{n},\end{split}

(6.3)

where we set
b_{1}:=-U^{0}
.

Set
W:=U^{n}(x)
for brevity in what follows. The equation (
6.3
) is solved using a piecewise linear finite element method in the open source computing software system FEALPy
[
WH22
]
.
To do this, multiply both sides of (
6.3
) by the test function
V\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and obtain a weak form of the equation by integration by parts:

\begin{split}(W,V)+\beta\left(a(W,\nabla W)\nabla W,\nabla V\right)=-(b_{n},V),\end{split}

(6.4)

where
(f,g):=\int_{\Omega}fgdx
.
Let
\tilde{W}
be some approximation to
W
from the finite element space. Set
\delta W=W-\tilde{W}
. Then (
6.4
) is equivalent to

\begin{split}(\tilde{W}+\delta W,V)+\beta\left(a(\tilde{W}+\delta W,\nabla(\tilde{W}+\delta W))\nabla(\tilde{W}+\delta W),\nabla V\right)=-(b_{n},V).\end{split}

(6.5)

Take the Taylor expansion of
a(\tilde{W}+\delta W,\nabla(\tilde{W}+\delta W))
about
(\tilde{W},\nabla\tilde{W})
:

\begin{split}a(\tilde{W}+\delta W,\nabla(\tilde{W}+\delta W))=a(\tilde{W},\nabla\tilde{W})+J_{W}\delta W+J_{\nabla W}\cdot\nabla\delta W+O(\delta W^{2}+|\nabla\delta W|^{2}),\end{split}

(6.6)

where
J_{W}:=\partial a(W,\nabla W)/\partial W
and
J_{\nabla W}:=\partial a(W,\nabla W)/\partial\nabla W
, with both of these functions evaluated at the initial approximation
(\tilde{W},\nabla\tilde{W})
.
Substituting (
6.6
) into (
6.4
) and removing the higher-order terms, we get

(\tilde{W}+\delta W,V)+\beta\left(a(\tilde{W},\nabla\tilde{W})\nabla\tilde{W}+a(\tilde{W},\nabla\tilde{W})\nabla\delta W+J_{W}\nabla\tilde{W}\delta W+J_{\nabla W}\cdot\nabla\tilde{W}\nabla\delta W,\nabla V\right)=-(b_{n},V).

This is a discrete linear system for
\delta W
, which we solve approximately in our piecewise linear finite element space; then we update our finite element approximation of
W
by
\tilde{W}\mapsto\tilde{W}+\delta W
.

Using Picard iteration, we repeat this updating process until we obtain a numerical approximation to
W
with a predetermined precision
and use this as the computed solution for equation (
6.3
).

In this model,
x=(x_{1},x_{2})^{T}
and
\nabla u=(\partial_{x_{1}}u,\partial_{x_{2}}u)^{T}
.
In the example of Section
6.2.1
, we have
a(u,\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p-2}=(\nabla u,\nabla u)^{(p-2)/2}
,

J_{U}=\partial a(\tilde{U},\nabla\tilde{U})/\partial U=0

and

J_{\nabla U}=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\nabla U}(\nabla U,\nabla U)^{(p-2)/2}\right|_{\tilde{U}}=\left.(p-2)\left[|\nabla U|^{p-4}\nabla U\right]\right|_{\tilde{U}}.

Similarly, in the example of Section
6.2.2
we have
a(u,\nabla u)=1/\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}
,
J_{U}=0

and

J_{\nabla U}=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\nabla U}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\nabla U,\nabla U)}}\right)\right|_{\tilde{U}}=\left.-\left[(1+|\nabla U|^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\nabla U\right]\right|_{\tilde{U}}.

6.2.1
Fractional
p
-Laplace equation

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
.
The time fractional
p
-Laplace problem is

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)&=\Delta_{p}(u)\ \text{ for }t>0\text{ and }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)&=0\ \text{ for }t>0\text{ and }x\in\partial\Omega,\\
u(x,0)&=u_{0}(x)\ \text{ for }x\in\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(6.7)

where
\Delta_{p}(u)=\nabla\cdot(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
and
\mathcal{N}[u]=-\Delta_{p}(u)
.
It is shown in
[
VZ15
,
DVV19
]
that for any
s>1
one has

\|u\|^{s-2+p}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}(t)\leq C\int_{\Omega}\nabla u^{s-1}\cdot\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)dx
, where the constant
C=C(\alpha,s,u_{0},\Omega,p)>0
, i.e., the structural assumption (
1.3
) holds true for
\gamma=p-1
.
Applying the previous general result given in Lemma
1.2
, we can get the following estimates.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
DVV19
, Theorem 1.2]

Suppose that
u
is a solution of (
6.7
). Then for each
s\in(1,\infty)
,
there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}(t)\leq\frac{C}{1+t^{\alpha/(p-1)}}\text{ for all }t>0.

This result corresponds to
\gamma=p-1
in Lemma
1.2
.
Analogously to the continuous case, we assume that for some
s\in(1,\infty)
,
U^{n}\in L^{s}(\Omega)
satisfies the structural condition (
5.2
) with
\gamma=p-1
.
Then the numerical solutions satisfy

\begin{split}\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha/(p-1)}}\text{ for all }t_{n}>0.\end{split}

where
U^{n}
is the numerical approximation of
u(x,t)
at
t_{n}
given in Theorem
5.1
.

In the numerical experiments we take
\Omega=[0,1]^{2}
, the initial value
u_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})=\sin(\pi x_{1})\sin(\pi x_{2})
, step size
h=1/150
and
s=2
to compute
\|U^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Similarly to (
6.1
), we define

\begin{split}q_{\alpha,p}(t_{n})=-\frac{\ln(\|U^{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}/\|U_{n-k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})}{\ln(t_{n}/t_{n-k})}\ \text{ for }t_{n-k}>1\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.\end{split}

(6.8)

Here the index
q_{\alpha,p}
is the numerical observation decay rate; the decay rate
q_{\alpha,p}^{*}
for both the exact solution and numerical solution satisfies
q_{\alpha,p}^{*}\geq\alpha/(p-1)
, according to Lemma
6.1
and Theorem
5.1
respectively.
In order to reduce the observation error,
for each
t_{n}
we take
k=2,4,6,8,10
to compute 5 different
q_{\alpha,p}(t_{n})
from (
6.8
), and then compute the mean of these 5 values as the observed value at
t_{n}
.
Tables
2
and
3
show that the observed values
q_{\alpha,p}
of the numerical decay rate are consistent with our theoretical prediction
q_{\alpha,p}^{*}\geq\alpha/(p-1)
.

6.2.2
Fractional mean curvature equation

Consider the nonlinear time fractional mean curvature equation

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)&=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\nabla u}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}\right)\ \text{ for }t>0\text{ and }x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)&=0\ \text{ for }t>0\text{ and }x\in\partial\Omega,\\
u(x,0)&=u_{0}(x)\ \text{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(6.9)

Here the right-hand side of the PDE in (
6.9
) is the mean curvature of the hypersurface described by the graph of the function
u
.
It is shown in
[
DVV19
]
that for any
s>1
,

\|u\|^{s}_{L^{s}(\Omega)}(t)\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla u^{s-1}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}dx
, where the constant
C=C(\alpha,s,u_{0},\Omega)>0
.
That is, the structural assumption (
1.3
) holds true with
\gamma=1
, and we can invoke Lemma
1.2
to derive a long-time decay estimate.

Lemma 6.2
.

[
DVV19
, Theorem 1.5]

Let
u
be a solution
of (
6.9
) and assume that

\sup_{x\in\Omega,t>0}|\nabla u(x,t)|<\infty
.
Then for any
s\in(1,\infty)
,
\|u\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}(t)\leq\frac{C}{1+t^{\alpha}}
,
t>0
.

Like the continuous case, if we assume that the time discrete scheme satisfies the structural assumption (
5.2
) for some
s\in(1,\infty)
with
\gamma=1
, it then follows from Theorem
5.1
that

\begin{split}\|U^{n}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{1+t_{n}^{\alpha}}\ \text{ for all }t_{n}>0.\end{split}

Hence
q_{\alpha}^{*}\geq\alpha
, where
q_{\alpha}^{*}
is the predicted decay rate of the numerical solution.

Conclusion.

We emphasise that our numerical results for the nonlinear F-PDEs of Section
6
exhibit polynomial decay rates that are typical of long-time solutions to time-fractional problems.
In this paper, we have considered the decay rates of exact and computed solutions for this class of problems. The more difficult question of convergence of computed solutions to exact solutions will be examined in a future paper.

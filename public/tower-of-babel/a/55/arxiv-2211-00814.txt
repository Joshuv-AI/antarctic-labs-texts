Lyapunov-Barrier Characterization of Robust Reach-Avoid-Stay Specifications for Hybrid Systems Note: This paper was not presented at any IFAC meeting. Journal: Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems
Yiming Meng, Jun Liu

Abstract
Stability, reachability, and safety are crucial properties of dynamical systems. While verification and control synthesis of reach-avoid-stay objectives can be effectively handled by abstraction-based formal methods, such approaches can be computationally expensive due to the use of state-space discretization. In contrast, Lyapunov methods qualitatively characterize stability and safety properties without any state-space discretization. Recent work on converse Lyapunov-barrier theorems also demonstrates an approximate completeness for verifying reach-avoid-stay specifications of systems modelled by nonlinear differential equations. In this paper, based on the topology of hybrid arcs , we extend the Lyapunov-barrier characterization to more general hybrid systems described by differential and difference inclusions. We show that Lyapunov-barrier functions are not only sufficient to guarantee reach-avoid-stay specifications for well-posed hybrid systems, but also necessary for arbitrarily slightly perturbed systems under mild conditions. Numerical examples are provided to illustrate the main results.

Introduction

The reach-avoid-stay property is one of the building blocks
for specifying more complex temporal logic objectives. States (or controlled states) of dynamical systems with reach-avoid-stay properties are always within the
safe region and, moreover, reach a target set within a finite
time and stay inside it afterwards.

For hybrid systems modelled by differential and difference equations with possibly small perturbations, verification and control synthesis of various linear temporal logic properties are achievable via abstraction-based formal
methods

[

7

8
]
of the generated symbolic models. Regardless of the potentially heavy computational efforts, formal methods are guaranteed to return the set of initial states from which the specification are satisfied. The current literature that pertains to formal methods focuses on developing algorithms to reduce the computational complexity
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. However, case studies in
[
13
]
have shown that for systems that also continuously depend on tunable parameters and undergo bifurcations, challenges arose regarding the sampling time for constructing abstractions under the non-uniform rates of state evolution.

Compared to formal methods, Lyapunov-like functions are able
to characterize stability and safety related attributes without state space discretization
[
14
,
15
,
16

. The flexible quadratic programming framework
[

with Lyapunov and barrier constraints also shows effectiveness
in the control synthesis of stabilization and safety objectives. The work in
[
18

developed sufficient conditions for stabilization with safety
guarantees by uniting Lyapunov and barrier functions (see
[

conditions imply that the unsafe set has

[

[

are topologically invalid. They
instead proposed sufficient conditions for safe stabilization
using non-smooth control Lyapunov functions

The recent work
[
22

improved the Lyapunov-barrier approach and
showed that it is possible to construct united Lyapunov and barrier functions, or even a single Lyapunov function, that
are defined on the entire set of initial conditions from which
stabilization with safety guarantees is satisfied. The connection between stabilization with safety guarantees and reach-avoid-stay was also established via robustness. The work
[
13

proposed control Lyapunov-barrier functions and
provided sufficient conditions for control
synthesis with reach-avoid-stay specifications. Such a method has been numerically shown to outperform abstraction-based algorithms in a system that undergoes a Hopf-bifurcation.

The latest research
[
23
]
has shown that the existence of the stochastic analogue of Lyapunov-barrier functions is also sufficient for characterizing probabilistic
reach-avoid-stay specifications for stochastic systems with point-mass uncertainties. However, due to the effects of diffusion and the concept of weak solutions, probabilistic reach-avoid-stay specifications, other than strong reach-avoid-stay with probability one, may fail to be related to probabilistic stability with safety guarantees w.r.t. some subset of the target set, and hence the (approximated) necessity of Lyapunov-barrier characterization for probabilistic reach-avoid-stay does not hold for stochastic dynamical systems.

In this paper, we show that the Lyapunov-barrier approach can be extended to verification of reach-avoid-stay specifications for hybrid systems with differential and difference inclusions. Unlike systems with diffusion, reach-avoid-stay properties of solutions to hybrid systems can be converted to stabilization with safety guarantees under mild conditions. The approximate equivalence of these two types of specifications can be established via robustness. We show that smooth Lyapunov-barrier functions can be constructed given the compactness of target set and the set of initial states.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Hybrid systems

Consider a hybrid system

\mathbf{H}=(C,F,D,G)
with dynamics

\displaystyle\dot{x}\in F(x),\;\;\;\;\;x\in C,

(1a)

\displaystyle x^{+}\in G(x),\;\;\;x\in D,

(1b)

where
C,D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
represent the flow set and the jump set respectively, and the set-valued maps
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
represent the flow and jump maps respectively.

Given a scalar
\delta\geq 0
, the (additive)
\delta
-perturbation of
\mathbf{H}
, denoted by
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
, is described as

\displaystyle\dot{x}\in F(x)+\delta\mathcal{B},\;\;\;\;\;x\in C_{\delta},

(2a)

\displaystyle x^{+}\in G(x)+\delta\mathcal{B},\;\;\;x\in D_{\delta},

(2b)

where
C_{\delta}=C+\delta\mathcal{B}
and
D_{\delta}=D+\delta\mathcal{B}
.
A hybrid time domain is a subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{N}
of the form
\cup_{j=0}^{J}[t_{j},t_{j+1}]\times\left\{j\right\}
, where
J\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
and
0=t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\dots
. Given
(t,j),(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in E
, the natural ordering is such that
t+j\leq t^{\prime}+j^{\prime}
if
t\leq t^{\prime}
and
j\leq j^{\prime}
. A hybrid arc is a function
\phi:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
from a hybrid domain
E
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and, for each fixed
j
,
t\mapsto\phi(t,j)
is locally absolutely continuous on the interval
I^{j}=\{t:(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)\}
.

Definition 2.1
(Solution concept)

A hybrid arc
\phi
is a solution to (
1
) if

(i)

\phi(0,0)\in\overline{C}\cup D
;

(ii)

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\operatorname{int}(I^{j})\neq\emptyset
, we have

\begin{split}&\phi(t,j)\in C\;\;\text{for all}\;t\in\operatorname{int}(I^{j}),\\
&\dot{\phi}(t,j)\in F(\phi(t,j))\;\;\text{for almost all}\;t\in I^{j};\end{split}

(iii)

for all
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
such that
(t,j+1)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
, we have
\phi(t,j)\in D
and
\phi(t,j+1)\in G(\phi(t,j))
.

Solutions to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
for
\delta>0
are defined in a similar way. Furthermore, a solution
\phi
to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
is maximal if there is no other
\psi
to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
such that
\phi(t,j)=\psi(t,j)
for all
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
and
\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
is a proper subset of
\operatorname{dom}(\psi)

[
24
, Definition 2.7]
.

We also define the range of a solution arc
\phi
as
\operatorname{rge}(\phi)=\{\phi(t,j):(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)\}
for convenience. For
\delta\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(x)
the set of all maximal solutions starting from
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for a hybrid system
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
; we denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(K)
the set of all maximal solutions starting from the set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
.

We introduce correspondingly some notations for reachable sets of
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
from some
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
T\geq 0
, we define

\mathcal{R}^{\delta}_{\leq T}(K)=\left\{\phi(t,j):\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(K),\phi(0,0)\in K,t+j\leq T\right\}.

The reachable sets
\mathcal{R}^{\delta}_{<T}(K)
and
\mathcal{R}^{\delta}_{\geq T}(K)
are defined in a similar way. The infinite-horizon reachable set from
K
for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
is
\mathcal{R}^{\delta}(K)=\left\{\phi(t,j):\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(K),\;(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)\right\}
.

Assumption 2.2

We make the standing assumption that
\mathbf{H}
should satisfy the basic conditions:

(C1)

C,D\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are closed;

(C2)

F
is outer semicontinuous, locally bounded, and convex for all
x\in C
;

(C3)

G
is outer semicontinuous and locally bounded for all
x\in D
.

We also introduce the following concept of closeness of hybrid arcs that will be frequently used in proofs.

Definition 2.3

[
24
, Definition 5.23]

Given
\tau,\varepsilon>0
, two hybrid arcs
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are
(\tau,\varepsilon)
-close if

(a)

for all
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi_{1})
with
t+j\leq\tau
there exists
s
such that
(s,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi_{2})
,
\left|t-s\right|<\varepsilon
and
\left|\phi_{1}(t,j)-\phi_{2}(s,j)\right|<\varepsilon
;

(b)

for all
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi_{2})
with
t+j\leq\tau
there exists
s
such that
(s,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi_{1})
,
\left|t-s\right|<\varepsilon
and
\left|\phi_{2}(t,j)-\phi_{1}(s,j)\right|<\varepsilon
.

2.2
Properties pertaining to stability and safety

In this subsection, we define properties that are related to stability and safety of solutions to (
2
). Particularly, we formally introduce the concepts of stability with safety guarantees and reach-avoid-stay type specifications.

Definition 2.4
(Forward (pre-) invariance)

A set
\mathfrak{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be forward pre-invariant for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
if for every
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(\mathfrak{I})
,
\operatorname{rge}(\phi)\subseteq\mathfrak{I}
. The set
\mathfrak{I}
is said to be forward invariant for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
if for every forward complete
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(\mathfrak{I})
,
\operatorname{rge}(\phi)\subseteq\mathfrak{I}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\delta}(\mathfrak{I})\subseteq\mathfrak{I}
.

Remark 2.5

The term ‘pre’ is in the sense that non-complete maximal solutions are not excluded. This concept allows us to describe the completeness and dynamical behaviors separately for general maximal solutions. For future references, we only define the ‘pre’ properties of solutions to keep the relevant definitions succinct.

We next consider stability for solutions of
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
w.r.t. a closed set.

Definition 2.6
(Uniform pre-asymptotic stability)

A closed set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be uniformly pre-asymptotically stable (UpAS) for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
if the following two conditions are met:

(i)

(uniform stability) for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that every solution to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
with
\left|\phi(0,0)\right|_{A}<\delta_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
\left|\phi(t,j)\right|_{A}<\varepsilon
for all
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
;

(ii)

(uniform pre-attractivity) there exists some
\rho>0
such that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist some
T>0
such that, for every solution
\phi
to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
,

|\phi(t,j)|_{A}<\varepsilon

whenever
|\phi(0,0)|_{A}<\rho
,
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
, and
t+j\geq T
.

Definition 2.7
(Basin of pre-attraction)

If a closed set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is UpAS for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
, we further define the basin of pre-attraction of
A
, denoted by
\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
, as the set of initial states
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that every solution
\phi
to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
with
\phi(0,0)=x
is bounded and, if it is complete, then also converges to the set
A
, i.e.,
\lim_{t+j\rightarrow\infty}|\phi(t,j)|_{A}=0
.

We define two closely-related properties of our interests in the following definitions.

Definition 2.8
(Stability with safety guarantee)

Let
\mathcal{X}_{0},U,A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
A
is a closed set.
We say that
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
satisfies a stability with safety guarantee specification
(\mathcal{X}_{0},U,A)
if

(i)

(pre-asymptotic stability w.r.t.
A
) the set
A
is UpAS for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
and
\mathcal{X}_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
;

(ii)

(safety w.r.t.
U
)
\operatorname{rge}(\phi)\cap U=\emptyset
for all
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{X}_{0})
.

Definition 2.9
(Reach-avoid-stay specification)

Let
\mathcal{X}_{0},U,\mathfrak{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
satisfies a reach-avoid-stay specification
(\mathcal{X}_{0},U,\mathfrak{I})
if

(i)

(reach and stay w.r.t.
\mathfrak{I}
) there exists some
T\geq 0
such that
\mathcal{R}^{\delta}_{\geq T}(\mathcal{X}_{0})\subseteq\mathfrak{I}
;

(ii)

(safety w.r.t.
U
)
\operatorname{rge}(\phi)\cap U=\emptyset
for all
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(\mathcal{X}_{0})
.

3
Connection between Stability with Safety Guarantees and Reach-avoid-stay Specifications

3.1
Stability with safety implies
reach-avoid-stay

Throughout this section, we suppose that
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
satisfies a stability with safety
guarantee specification
(\mathcal{X}_{0},U,A)
for some fixed
\delta\geq 0
.

We first show some basic properties of solutions to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
with a fixed
\delta\geq 0
. The following proposition combines the results from
[
24
, Proposition 7.4, Lemma 7.8]
.

Proposition 3.10

Given a hybrid system
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
, let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set that is uniform stable for
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
(condition (i) of Definition
2.6
holds). Suppose
K\subseteq\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
is compact. Then

(i)

\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
is open and
A\subseteq\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
;

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
T=T(\varepsilon)>0
such that

|\phi(t,j)|_{A}\leq\varepsilon

for all
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(K)
and
(t,j)\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
with
t+j\geq T
;

(iii)

A\cup\mathcal{R}(K)
is a compact subset of
\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
.

By (ii) of Proposition
3.10
, the uniform attraction for solutions to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
, the reach-avoid-stay property can be shown straightforwardly. The statement is given in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.11

Let

be compact sets. Suppose that
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
satisfies a stability with safety guarantee specification

3.2
The converse side

Throughout this subsection, we suppose that
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
satisfies a reach-avoid-stay specification
(\mathcal{X}_{0},U,\mathfrak{I})
. We make an additional assumption on the local Lipschitz property of
F
and
G
to prove the converse connection.

Definition 3.12
(Locally Lipschitz set-valued maps)

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set.
A set-valued flow map
F
is also locally Lipschitz on
\mathcal{O}
; that is, for each
x\in\mathcal{O}
, there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{O}
of
x
and an
L>0
such that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{N}
,

F(x_{1})\subseteq F(x_{2})+L|x_{2}-x_{1}|\mathcal{B}.

The following fact is a direct consequence of
F
being locally Lipschitz
[
31
, Lemma 9]
.

Lemma 3.13

If
F:\mathcal{O}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is locally Lipschitz, then for any compact subset
K\subseteq\mathcal{O}
there exists an
L_{K}>0
such that for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in K
, we have

F(x_{1})\subseteq F(x_{2})+L_{K}|x_{2}-x_{1}|\mathcal{B}.

Assumption 3.14

We assume in this section that
F
and
G
in (
2
) are locally Lipschitz on some open subset of
C
and
D
respectively.

Lemma 3.15
(Perturbed solutions)

For any
\delta>0
, fix a
\delta^{\prime}\in[0,\delta)
and a
\tau>0
. Let
T\geq\tau
and
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set such that
K\cap(C_{\delta}\cup D_{\delta})\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists an
r=r(K,\tau,\delta^{\prime},\delta)>0
such that for every solution
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta^{\prime}}(K)
with
\phi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in K
for all
(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
and
t^{\prime}+j^{\prime}\leq T
, and for all
x\in\phi(0,0)+r\mathcal{B}
, there exists a
\psi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(x)
with
\operatorname{dom}(\psi)=\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
and

\psi(t,j)=\phi(t,j),\;\;t+j=T.

(3)

Proof

We suppose the total number of jumps before
T
is
N
, thereby
N\in[0,T]
.
Now, set

\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})=\phi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})+\left(1-\frac{t^{\prime}+j^{\prime}}{T}\right)\cdot(\psi(0,0)-\phi(0,0))

for all
(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
and
t^{\prime}+j^{\prime}\in[0,T]
. Then the constructed
\psi
satisfies (
3
) and

\left|\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})-\phi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\right|\leq|\psi(0,0)-\phi(0,0)|\leq r.

In particular, for
N>0
, for each jump
j^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
at the end point
t_{j}^{\prime}
of
I^{j^{\prime}}
, we have

|\psi(t_{j}^{\prime},j^{\prime}+1)-\phi(t_{j}^{\prime},j^{\prime}+1)|\leq r.

Similarly, suppose
N<T
(i.e., flows exist),
then for any fixed
j^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\cdots,N\}
and
t^{\prime}\in I^{j^{\prime}}
, we have

\left|\dot{\psi}(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})-\dot{\phi}(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\right|\leq\frac{1}{T}\left|\psi(0,0)-\phi(0,0)\right|=\frac{r}{T}.

We show that this
\psi
is a solution to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
. On the flow set
C_{\delta}
, let
\widehat{C}:=C_{\delta}\cap(K+r\mathcal{B})
. Then for
x\in\widehat{C}
, for all
t^{\prime}\in I^{j^{\prime}}
, we have

\begin{split}\dot{\psi}(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})\subseteq&\dot{\phi}(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})+\frac{r}{T}\mathcal{B}\subseteq F(\phi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime}))+\left(\delta^{\prime}+\frac{r}{T}\right)\mathcal{B}\\
\subseteq&F(\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime}))+\left(\delta^{\prime}+\frac{r}{T}\right)\mathcal{B}+L_{\widehat{C}}|\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})-\phi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime})|\mathcal{B}\\
\subseteq&F(\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime}))+\left(\delta^{\prime}+\frac{r}{T}+rL_{\widehat{C}}\right)\mathcal{B}\\
\subseteq&F(\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime}))+\left(\delta^{\prime}+\frac{r}{\tau}+rL_{\widehat{C}}\right)\mathcal{B},\end{split}

where the constant
L_{\widehat{C}}
in line 4 of the above inclusions is obtained by Lemma
3.13
.

Similarly, for each end point
t^{\prime}
of some
I^{j^{\prime}}
, the jump should satisfy

\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime}+1)\subseteq G(\psi(t^{\prime},j^{\prime}))+\left(\delta^{\prime}+r+rL_{\widehat{D}}\right)\mathcal{B},

where
\widehat{D}:=D_{\delta}\cap(K+r\mathcal{B})
and
L_{\widehat{D}}
is obtained based on Lemma
3.13
for
G
.

Now we are able to bound the above by
\delta
such that
\psi
is a solution to
\mathbf{H}_{\delta}
. The
r
can be selected accordingly such that all of the followings are satisfied:
r\leq\delta-\delta^{\prime}
,
\frac{r}{\tau}+\delta^{\prime}+rL_{\widehat{C}}\leq\delta
and
r+\delta^{\prime}+rL_{\widehat{D}}\leq\delta
.

Note that the above choice of

4
Lyapunov-Barrier Functions for
Reach-Avoid-Stay Specifications

In this section, we provide Lyapunov-barrier characterizations for stability with safety guarantee specifications as well as reach-avoid-stay specification. In particular, such characterizations are (robustly) complete in the sense that Lyapunov-barrier functions exist based on the specified dynamical behavior of the solutions.

We focus on the Lyapunov-barrier functions for stability with safety guarantees, the result for reach-avoid-stay comes after the connection given in Proposition
3.20
. Since the uniform asymptotic stability or reachability of a closed set strongly relies the set initial conditions and may not hold globally, the point-to-set distance becomes inadequate on the topology of basin of pre-attraction. We use a proper indicator to characterize the distance instead.

Definition 4.21
(Proper indicator)

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact set and
\mathfrak{O}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set containing
A
. A continuous function
\omega:\mathfrak{O}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is said to be a proper indicator for
A
on
\mathfrak{O}
if

(i)

\omega(x)=0\iff x\in A
;

(ii)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\omega(x_{n})=\infty
for any sequence
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{O}
such that either
x_{n}\rightarrow p\in\partial\mathfrak{O}
or
|x_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
when
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We also refine the basin of pre-attraction and introduce the following constraint set.

Definition 4.22
(Basin of pre-attraction with safety)

The extracted basin of pre-attraction with safety is a subset of
\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
defined by

\widehat{\mathfrak{B}}_{\delta}(A):=\{x\in\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A):\forall\phi\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{H}}^{\delta}(x),\;\;\operatorname{rge}(\phi)\cap U=\emptyset\},

(5)

where
A,U
are given in the specification
(\mathcal{X}_{0},U,A)
.

The following lemma verifies basic topological properties of
\widehat{\mathfrak{B}}_{\delta}(A)
, which will be later utilized for the Lyapunov-barrier function theorems.

Lemma 4.23

Suppose that
A
is compact,
U
is closed, and
A\cap U=\emptyset
. If

satisfies the stability with safety guarantee specification

, then

(i)

\mathcal{X}_{0}\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak{B}}_{\delta}(A)\subseteq\mathfrak{B}_{\delta}(A)
;

(ii)

\widehat{\mathfrak{B}}_{\delta}(A)

Proof

The first claim can be easily verified by Definition
2.8
and
4.22
. The forward pre-invariance of (ii) comes after Definition
2.4
. It suffices to show that
\widehat{\mathfrak{B}}_{\delta}(A)

Suppose the opposite, then pick any

. Note that for each

\phi_{n}

3.10

[

2.2

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we showed that under mild conditions, the connection can be made, via a robustness argument, between stability with safety guarantees and reach-avoid-stay specifications for robust hybrid systems driven by differential and difference inclusions. We further extended the Lyapunov-barrier function theorems to robust hybrid systems that satisfy stability with safety guarantees and reach-avoid-stay specifications. Under the concept of solutions to hybrid systems, as well as natural requirements on the compactness of target set and the set of initial conditions, we showed that the existence of Lyapunov-barrier functions is necessary to the two specifications of our interests.

In particular, two versions of Lyapunov-barrier functions are provided and discussed in Section
4
. The single Lyapunov-like function that also plays a role as a barrier function appears to be succinct but may be difficult to design in practice. We discussed the construction of separate Lyapunov-barrier functions in particular for stability with safety guarantee specifications.
Numerical simulations are provided to validate our results.
It is interesting to compare with the latest converse theorems on barrier functions for systems driven by differential inclusions
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
. The mentioned reference provides a construction based on time-to-impact functions w.r.t. the robust reachable sets, which in turn to be qualified as barrier functions. In terms of safety, this method allows us to consider relaxations for the topological set-ups of target sets and the set of initial conditions, however, at the cost of not relying on the existing converse Lyapunov theorems and possibly sacrificing the smoothness of barrier functions.

Nonetheless, we provide the theoretical results of converse theorems and hope to shed some light on designing powerful algorithms, e.g. learning techniques
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
, for constructing Lyapunov-barrier
functions with respect to stability with safety guarantee or reach-avoid-stay specifications.

For future work, it would be an interesting application in using the current Lypunov-barrier characterizations as abstractions for linear temporal logic specifications for hybrid systems. It is also valuable to design algorithms based on this theoretical work to construct Lyapunov-like functions that improves the estimated region of attractions.
Another interesting direction is to
explore the stochastic analogue of Lyapunov-barrier function theorems, particularly the converse theorems, for stochastic hybrid systems.

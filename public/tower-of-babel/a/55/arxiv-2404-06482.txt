Exceptional zeros of Rankin–Selberg L L -functions and joint Sato–Tate distributions
Jesse Thorner

Abstract
. Let χ \chi be an idele class character over a number field F F , and let π , π ′ \pi,\pi^{\prime} be non-dihedral twist-inequivalent cuspidal automorphic representations of GL 2 ​ ( 𝔸 F ) \mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}) . We prove that if m , n ≥ 0 m,n\geq 0 are integers, m + n ≥ 1 m+n\geq 1 , F F is totally real, χ \chi corresponds with a ray class character, and π , π ′ \pi,\pi^{\prime} correspond with primitive non-CM holomorphic Hilbert cusp forms, then the Rankin–Selberg L L -function L ⁡ ( s , Sym m ​ ( π ) × ( Sym n ​ ( π ′ ) ⊗ χ ) ) L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{m}(\pi)\times(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\chi)) has a standard zero-free region with no exceptional Landau–Siegel zero. This is new even for F = ℚ F=\mathbb{Q} . As an application, we establish the strongest known unconditional effective rates of convergence in the Sato–Tate distribution for π \pi and the joint Sato–Tate distribution for π \pi and π ′ \pi^{\prime} .

\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}

[
38

such that if

(1.1)

If

has at most one zero

Harcos and the author

Langlands conjectured that

factors as a product of standard

-functions (i.e.,

is modular). As of now, this is only known in special cases, most notably when

\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}=\{\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}\colon\omega_{\pi}\textup{ is trivial on the diagonally embedded positive reals}\}.

Let

\pi^{\prime}

There exists an absolute and effectively computable constant

\mathcal{L}(\sigma)\neq 0,\qquad\sigma\geq 1-\frac{\Cr{main}}{M^{2}[M^{\max\{y_{\pi},y_{\pi^{\prime}}\}}\log(MC(\pi)C(\pi^{\prime}))+\log C(\chi)]}.

\Big[\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F_{v}})\Big(\begin{matrix}\varpi_{v}&0\\

[
28

\omega_{\pi,v}(\varpi_{v})=\zeta^{2}\end{subarray}}1=\mu_{\mathrm{ST}}(I).

by Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, and Taylor
[

is the cardinality of the residue field of the local ring of integers

-functions given here rely on

,

,
25
]

or

Let

. Recall
\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
in (

(\pi^{*},t_{\pi})\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

3.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}^{*}
be the contragredient, and let
\omega_{\pi}
the central character. We express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
. Let
\delta_{\pi}=1
if
\pi=\mathbbm{1}
and
\delta_{\pi}=0
otherwise. Let

S_{\pi}=\{v\nmid\infty\colon\textup{$\pi_{v}$ ramified}\},\qquad S_{\pi}^{\infty}=S_{\pi}\cup\{v\mid\infty\}.

Let
N_{\pi}
be the norm of the conductor of
\pi
. If
v\nmid\infty
, then there are
n
Satake parameters
(\alpha_{j,\pi}(v))_{j=1}^{n}
such that

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}),\qquad L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)q_{v}^{-s}}

converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. If

, then at least one of the
\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)
equals zero.

If
v\mid\infty
, then
(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))_{j=1}^{n}
are the Langlands parameters at
v
, from which we define

L(s,\pi_{\infty})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v)),\qquad\Gamma_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$.}\end{cases}

The completed
L
-function
\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(1-s))^{\delta_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}N_{\pi})^{\frac{s}{2}}L(s,\pi)L(s,\pi_{\infty})
is entire of order
1
. Since,
\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
,
N_{\widetilde{\pi}}=N_{\pi}
, and

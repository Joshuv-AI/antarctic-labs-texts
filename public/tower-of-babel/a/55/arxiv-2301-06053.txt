On some p p -adic and mod p p representations of quaternion algebra over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p}
Yongquan HU and Haoran WANG

Abstract
. Let D D be the non-split quaternion algebra over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} . We prove that a class of admissible unitary Banach space representations of D × D^{\times} of global origin are topologically of finite length.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number,
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
D
be the non-split quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Let
E
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}
; they will serve as rings of coefficients.

In
[
Sch18
]
Scholze constructed
a cohomological covariant
\delta
-functor
\{\mathcal{S}^{i},i\geq 0\}
from the category of admissible smooth representations of

G
over
\mathcal{O}
-torsion modules to admissible smooth representations of
D^{\times}
carrying a continuous and commuting
action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}:=\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
The functor extends to admissible unitary Banach space representations
\Pi
(see
[
Paš22
]
), by setting

\mathcal{S}^{i}(\Pi):=\big(\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{S}^{i}(\Theta/\varpi^{n})\big)_{\rm tf}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E

where
\Theta
is some open bounded
G
-invariant lattice in
\Pi
, and the subscript
{\rm tf}
means taking the maximal Hausdorff torsion-free quotient.

It is hoped for that Scholze’s functor realizes both
p
-adic local Langlands and Jacquet-Langlands correspondence. Let
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(E)
be a continuous
p
-adic representation, and
\Pi(\rho)
be the associated unitary admissible Banach space representation of
G
by
[
Col10
]
.
The natural candidate for a
p
-adic Jacquet-Langlands correspondence is to take
\mathcal{S}^{1}(\Pi(\rho))
. More concretely, since there is also an action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
on
\mathcal{S}^{1}(\Pi(\rho))
, commuting with
D^{\times}
, it seems more reasonable to factor out the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
by putting (for a suitable normalization)

\mathrm{JL}(\rho):=\Hom_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\big(\rho(-1),\mathcal{S}^{1}(\Pi(\rho))\big).

One of the central questions in this version of
p
-adic Jacquet-Langlands correspondence is as follows:

Question
. Is
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
topologically of finite length ?

The difficulties to attack this question are (at least) two-fold. Firstly, a useful way to control the length of a unitary Banach space representation is by bounding the length of its residual representation (e.g. this method works well in the case of
G
). However, it does not work here because
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
is
residually of infinite length
(cf.
[
Sch18
, Thm. 7.8]
). Secondly, although
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
often has
\delta
-dimension
1
(see
[
Paš22
]
,
[
HW22
]
), it is hard to exclude the possibility that
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
admits infinitely many irreducible subquotients of
\delta
-dimension
0
(equivalently, finite dimensional as
E
-vector spaces); see
[
DPS22
, Rem. 1.3]
for a related discussion.

Assuming that the difference of the Hodge-Tate-Sen weights of
\rho
is not a non-zero integer, Dospinescu, Paškūnas and Schraen (
[
DPS22
]
) recently proved that
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
is indeed topologically of finite length. The point is that this assumption forces that
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
does not admit any irreducible subquotients which are finite dimensional over
E
. In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to the question for those
\rho
having a global origin.

B_{0}
be a definite quaternion algebra over

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\overline{r}(1)|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is generic (in the sense of Definition
4.4
). Let
y\in\mathrm{Spm}(\mathbb{T}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p])
such that
\rho_{y}:=r_{y}(1)|_{G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is irreducible. Then
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})
is topologically of finite length, and the quotient
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})/\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})^{\rm lalg}
is topologically irreducible.

Remark 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, if moreover the difference of the Hodge-Tate-Sen weights of
\rho
is not a non-zero integer, then
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)^{\rm lalg}=0
and
\mathrm{JL}(\rho)
itself is topologically irreducible. This strengthens the aforementioned result of
[
DPS22
]
and confirms an expectation mentioned in
[
DPS22
, Rem. 1.4]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on a general criterion for an admissible unitary Banach space representation of
D^{\times}
to be topologically of finite length. To explain it we need to introduce more notation.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{D}
be the ring of integers in
D
with a fixed uniformizer
\varpi_{D}
. Let
U_{D}^{1}=1+\varpi_{D}\mathcal{O}_{D}
which is the Sylow pro-
p
subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
, and
Z_{D}^{1}
be the centre of
U_{D}^{1}
. The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda:=\mathbb{F}[\![U_{D}^{1}/Z_{D}^{1}]\!]
is an Auslander regular ring; let
\mathfrak{m}_{D}
be its maximal ideal. The graded ring
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\Lambda)
has a nice structure and is studied in
[
HW22
, §2]
(motivated by the work
[
BHH+20
]
in the case of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p^{f}})
). In particular, it contains a distinguished two-sided ideal, denoted by
J
, such that

\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\Lambda)/J\cong\mathbb{F}[y,z]/(yz).

Consider the category
\mathcal{C}
of admissible smooth
\mathbb{F}
-representations
\pi
of
D^{\times}
with a central character such that the graded module
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
is annihilated by
J^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
.
For
\pi\in\mathcal{C}
, we may define its
multiplicity
, denoted by
\mu(\pi)
, which measures the size of
\pi
and such that
\mu(\pi)=0
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\pi<\infty
, see §
3.4
for details.

Let
\Pi
be an admissible unitary Banach space representation of
D^{\times}
over
E
which admits a central character. Denote by
\Pi^{\rm lalg}
the subspace of locally algebraic vectors in
\Pi
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that

(a)

\Pi^{\rm lalg}
is finite dimensional over
E
,

(b)

for some open bounded
D^{\times}
-lattice
\Theta
in
\Pi
,
\Theta/\varpi\Theta\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mu(\Theta/\varpi\Theta)\leq 4
.

Then
\Pi
is topologically of finite length.
If moreover

(c)

\mu(\Theta/\varpi\Theta)=4
and
\Theta/\varpi\Theta
does not admit any quotient of finite dimension over
\mathbb{F}
,

then
\Pi/\Pi^{\rm lalg}
is topologically irreducible.

We first explain how to deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.3
, which requires to verify the conditions (a), (b), (c) for
\Pi=\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})
. Since
\rho_{y}
has a global origin, the condition (a) is satisfied thanks to the local-global compatibility with the classical local Jacquet-Langlands correspondence. Under the genericity assumption on
\overline{\rho}
, the condition (c) is proved in our previous work
[
HW22
]
. Again by
[
HW22
]
, the condition (b) is a consequence of the following multiplicity one result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\overline{\rho}:G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
be a continuous representation and
\pi(\overline{\rho})
be the admissible smooth representation of
G
attached to
\overline{\rho}
by the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence.
Assume that
\overline{\rho}
is generic (in the sense of Definition
4.4
). Then

\mathcal{S}^{1}(\pi(\overline{\rho}))\cong\overline{\rho}(-1)\otimes\mathrm{JL}(\overline{\rho})

for some admissible smooth representation
\mathrm{JL}(\overline{\rho})
of
D^{\times}
whose
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
-socle is multiplicity free.

In fact, we will prove for a slightly broader class of
\overline{\rho}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on the work of Colmez-Dospinescu-Niziol
[
CDN22
]
. Andrea Dotto recently proved
a relative version of Theorem
1.4
in a more general setting, see
[
Dot22
, Thm. 1.2]
.

Let us now sketch the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We may assume
\Pi
is infinite dimensional over
E
. The condition (a) allows us to construct a closed subrepresentation
\Pi_{1}\subset\Pi
, which is infinite dimensional and is of finite length. Letting
\Theta_{1}=\Pi_{1}\cap\Theta
, we obtain an embedding

\Theta_{1}/\varpi\Theta_{1}\hookrightarrow\Theta/\varpi\Theta.

The following result, which is the technical heart of the paper, shows that
\mu(\Theta_{1}/\varpi\Theta_{1})\geq 4
, hence
\mu(\Theta_{1}/\varpi\Theta_{1})=\mu(\Theta/\varpi\Theta)
by (b). This implies that
\Pi/\Pi_{1}
is finite dimensional. If moreover (c) holds, then
\Theta_{1}/\varpi\Theta_{1}=\Theta/\varpi\Theta
and consequently
\Pi_{1}=\Pi
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
\pi\in\mathcal{C}
which is infinite dimensional, we have
\mu(\pi)\geq 4
.

Due to the action of
\varpi_{D}
, it is easy to see that
\mu(\pi)\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(as
\pi
is infinite dimensional). However, a lower bound by
4
is somewhat surprising, if we compare with the case of
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. In fact, the graded ring of
\mathbb{F}[\![I_{1}/Z_{1}]\!]
has a similar structure as
\Lambda
, where
I_{1}
is the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
G
with centre
Z_{1}
, and it is easy to show that principal series of
G
do have multiplicity
2
(cf.
[
BHH+21
, Prop. 3.3.3.4]
).
The key in the proof of Theorem
1.5
is to show that there exist no smooth representations of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
(or
U_{D}^{1}/Z_{D}^{1}
) having multiplicity
1
; again such representations of
I_{1}/Z_{1}
exist, cf.
[
Paš10
, Prop. 7.2]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.1.
Extensions of characters

Let
\alpha:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
denote the character defined by

\alpha(x):=\overline{x}^{p-1},

where
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{F}
denotes the reduction of

\mathcal{O}_{D}/\varpi_{D}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the fixed embedding
\mathbb{F}_{q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}

Since
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
is a cyclic group of order

\alpha
is of order

\alpha\neq\alpha^{-1}
and
\alpha\neq\alpha^{-2}

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\psi,\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be two smooth characters and

\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{D}/Z_{D}^{1}}(\psi,\chi)
is non-zero if and only if
\psi=\chi\alpha
or
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-1}
. Moreover,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}}(\chi\alpha,\chi)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}}(\chi\alpha^{-1},\chi)=1.

Proof.

See
[
HW22

∎

Definition 2.2

(i) Let

(2.1)

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
0\leq n\leq p-1
, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) smooth
\mathbb{F}
-representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
, denoted by
E^{-}(\chi,n)
, which is uniserial of dimension
n+1
, namely
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}E^{-}(\chi,n)[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{i}]=i
for any
1\leq i\leq n+1
, and such that the graded pieces are
\chi,\chi\alpha^{-1},\dots,\chi\alpha^{-n}
.

Proof.

Clearly we may take
E^{-}(\chi,0)=\chi
and
E^{-}(\chi,1)=E^{-}(\chi)
.
By induction, once
E^{-}(\chi,i)
is constructed, it suffices to show that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{1}(\chi\alpha^{-(i+1)},E^{-}(\chi,i))=1
.
We have a short exact sequence (for
i\geq 1
)

0\rightarrow E^{-}(\chi,i-1)\rightarrow E^{-}(\chi,i)\rightarrow\chi\alpha^{-i}\rightarrow 0

which implies

\Ext^{1}(\chi^{\prime},E^{-}(\chi,i-1))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\chi^{\prime},E^{-}(\chi,i))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\chi^{\prime},\chi\alpha^{-i})\rightarrow\Ext^{2}(\chi^{\prime},E^{-}(\chi,i-1))

where
\chi^{\prime}:=\chi\alpha^{-(i+1)}
.
As
2\leq i+1\leq p-1
, Proposition
2.1
implies that the first and last term vanish while the third term has dimension
1
, from which the result follows.

2.2.
Induced representations of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}

Let
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a smooth character. We view
\chi
as a character of
U_{D}^{2}H
and study the structure of
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
. Since
U_{D}^{2}
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
and
\chi|_{U_{D}^{2}}
is trivial,
U_{D}^{2}
acts trivially on
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
. Note that the coset
U_{D}^{2}H\backslash\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
has a set of representatives

\big\{1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda],\ \ \lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\big\}.

Hence, by fixing a basis
v
of
\chi
, we get a basis of
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
as follows:

\big\{\big[1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda],v\big],\ \ \lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\big\}.

Here, for
g\in\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
,
[g,v]
denotes the unique function in
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
supported on
(U_{D}^{2}H)g^{-1}
and such that
[g,v](g^{-1})=v
.
For
0\leq k\leq q-1
, define

(2.3)

f_{k,v}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\lambda^{k}\big[1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda],v\big]\in\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi.

A standard argument shows that these
f_{k,v}
also form a basis of
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
. Moreover,
f_{k,v}
is an
H
-eigenvector of character
\chi\alpha^{-k}
. Indeed, for
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
we have

[\mu]\cdot(1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda])=[\mu]+\varpi_{D}[\mu^{p}\lambda]=(1+\varpi_{D}[\mu^{p-1}\lambda])\cdot[\mu]

and so

[\mu]\cdot f_{k,v}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\lambda^{k}\big[1+\varpi_{D}[\mu^{p-1}\lambda],\chi(\mu)v\big]=\chi(\mu)\mu^{(1-p)k}f_{k,v}=(\chi\alpha^{-k})([\mu])f_{k,v}.

Remark that
f_{p,v}
has
H
-character
\chi\alpha
as
\alpha^{p+1}=1
.

For
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have (in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{D}
)

[\mu]+[\lambda]\equiv[\mu+\lambda]-p[P(\mu,\lambda)]\ \mod p^{2}

where
P(X,Y)
denotes the Witt polynomial (cf.
[
Ser79
, §II.6]
)

P(X,Y):=\sum_{s=1}^{p-1}\frac{\binom{p}{s}}{p}X^{p^{-1}(p-s)}Y^{p^{-1}s}.

For convenience, we introduce the following notation: if
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let

(2.4)

A(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}):=1+\varpi_{D}[\mu_{1}]+p[\mu_{2}]+p\varpi_{D}[\mu_{3}]\in\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then

A(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\cdot(1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda])=\big(1+\varpi_{D}[\mu_{1}+\lambda]\big)\big(1+p[\mu_{2}+\mu_{1}^{p}\lambda]+p\varpi_{D}[X]+p^{2}X^{\prime}\big)

where
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{D}
and

X=\mu_{3}+\mu_{2}^{p}\lambda-(\mu_{1}+\lambda)(\mu_{2}+\mu_{1}^{p}\lambda)-P(\mu_{1},\lambda).

Proof.

It is a direct computation:

\begin{array}[]{rll}&&A(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3})\cdot(1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda])\\
&=&1+\varpi_{D}([\mu_{1}]+[\lambda])+p([\mu_{2}]+[\mu_{1}^{p}\lambda])+p\varpi_{D}([\mu_{3}]+[\mu_{2}^{p}\lambda])+p^{2}[\mu_{3}^{p}\lambda]\\
&\equiv&1+\varpi_{D}[\mu_{1}+\lambda]+p[\mu_{2}+\mu_{1}^{p}\lambda]+p\varpi_{D}[\mu_{3}+\mu_{2}^{p}\lambda-P(\mu_{1},\lambda)]\mod p^{2}\mathcal{O}_{D}.\end{array}

The result follows by further factoring out the term
(1+\varpi_{D}[\mu_{1}+\lambda])
.
∎

For
0\leq k\leq q-1
, let
k=k_{0}+pk_{1}
be its
p
-adic expansion. For
0\leq k,k^{\prime}\leq q-1
, we say

if
k_{0}^{\prime}\leq k_{0}
and
k_{1}^{\prime}\leq k_{1}
. Next result describes the submodule structure of
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
.

Proposition 2.7
.

The subrepresentation of
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
generated by
f_{k,v}
is spanned over
\mathbb{F}
by
\{f_{k^{\prime},v},k^{\prime}\preceq k\}
. In particular, the
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
-socle of
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
is one-dimensional, spanned by
f_{0,v}
.

Proof.

Since
f_{k,v}
is an
H
-eigenvector and
U_{D}^{2}
acts trivially on
\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi
, it suffices to consider the action of
U_{D}^{1}/U_{D}^{2}
. Using Lemma
2.6
(with
\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=0
) we have

A(\mu_{1},0,0)\cdot f_{k,v}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\lambda^{k}\big[(1+\varpi_{D}[\mu_{1}+\lambda]),v\big]=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\lambda-\mu_{1})^{k}\big[1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda],v\big].

Expanding
(\lambda-\mu_{1})^{k}=(\lambda-\mu_{1})^{k_{0}}(\lambda^{p}-\mu_{1}^{p})^{k_{1}}
, we get

(2.5)

A(\mu_{1},0,0)\cdot f_{k,v}=\sum_{k^{\prime}\preceq k}\binom{k_{0}}{k_{0}^{\prime}}\binom{k_{1}}{k_{1}^{\prime}}(-\mu_{1})^{k-k^{\prime}}f_{k^{\prime},v}

which implies that
\langle\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\cdot f_{k,v}\rangle
is contained in

\oplus_{k^{\prime}\preceq k}\mathbb{F}f_{k^{\prime},v}
. Taking summation
\sum_{\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu_{1}^{i}
of the above equality, for
i=q-1-(k-k^{\prime})
, shows that each
f_{k^{\prime},v}
(for
k^{\prime}\preceq k
) also belongs to
\langle\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\cdot f_{k,v}\rangle
.

To see the last assertion, let
f\in\big(\Ind_{U_{D}^{2}H}^{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}\chi\big)^{U_{D}^{1}}
be non-zero and write
f=\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}a_{k}f_{k,v}
. Choose an index
k
such that
k
is maximal among the set
\{k^{\prime}:a_{k^{\prime}}\neq 0\}
with respect to
\preceq
. We need to prove
k=0
. Using (
2.5
) one checks that

\sum_{\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu_{1}^{q-1-k}A(\mu_{1},0,0)\cdot f_{k^{\prime},v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}(-1)^{k+1}f_{0,v}&k^{\prime}=k\\
0&k^{\prime}\neq k\end{array}\right.

which implies
\sum_{\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu_{1}^{q-1-k}A(\mu_{1},0,0)\cdot f\neq 0
. If
k\neq 0
, then
\sum_{\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu_{1}^{q-1-k}=0
and

\sum_{\mu_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu_{1}^{q-1-k}A(\mu_{1},0,0)\in\mathfrak{m}_{D},

a contradiction.
∎

2.3.
Uniserial representations

We define two elements in the group algebra
\mathbb{F}[U^{1}_{D}]
which will play an important role in the rest.

Definition 2.16
.

Define

Y:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\lambda^{-1}(1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda])\in\mathbb{F}[U_{D}^{1}]

Z:=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\lambda^{-p}(1+\varpi_{D}[\lambda])\in\mathbb{F}[U_{D}^{1}]

where the terms

are viewed as group elements in
U_{D}^{1}
.

We also view

as elements in the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda:=\mathbb{F}[\![U_{D}^{1}/Z_{D}^{1}]\!]
(after taking images).
Then the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{D}
is generated by

and

by
[
HW22
, Lem. 2.7]
. Let

\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\Lambda):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n}/\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n+1}

be the graded ring of
\Lambda
with respect to the
\mathfrak{m}_{D}
-adic filtration. Since we will only consider the
\mathfrak{m}_{D}
-adic filtration on
\Lambda
, the subscript
\mathfrak{m}_{D}
in
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\Lambda)
will be often omitted.

Comparing with the case of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, we should consider
Y
(resp.

) as a remplacement of
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\lambda^{-1}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{[\lambda]}\\
{0}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\bigl)
(resp.
\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\lambda^{-1}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}{1}&{0}\\
{[\lambda]}&{1}\end{smallmatrix}\bigl)
). The reason is as follows.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
. If

is an eigenvector of
H
of character
\chi
, then
Yv
(resp.
Zv
) is an eigenvector of
H
of character
\chi\alpha
(resp.
\chi\alpha^{-1}

Proof.

This is a direct check.
∎

Let
y
(resp.
z
) denote the image of

(resp.
Z
) in
\gr(\Lambda)
.
It is proved in
[

, §2]
that

is isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra

, where
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{F}y\oplus\mathbb{F}z\oplus\mathbb{F}h
, with the relations

(2.7)

[y,z]=h,\ \ [y,h]=[z,h]=0

and
\deg y=\deg z=1
. As a consequence, there is an inclusion

Lemma 2.18
.

There is a natural inclusion
\mathbb{F}[\![Y]\!]\subseteq\Lambda
.

Proof.

The natural ring morphism
\mathbb{F}[Y]\rightarrow\Lambda
is injective because it is injective when passing to graded rings, as is recalled above. It is clear that
\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n}\cap\mathbb{F}[Y]=(Y^{n})
for any

. Since

is
\mathfrak{m}_{D}
-adically complete, we deduce the result by taking completion. ∎

If
\pi
is a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}

\pi[Y]:=\pi[Y=0]=\big\{v\in\pi:Yv=0\big\}.

The following result is an analog of
[

, Prop. 5.9]
.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
of dimension

(possibly infinite). Assume that

and

First note that since

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of

(or

(pro-

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
such that

.
Then
\pi
has dimension
\leq p
. Moreover,
\pi
is isomorphic to

with

and

Proof.

Assume

for a contradiction. By Lemma
2.19
,
\pi
contains a unique subrepresentation
\pi^{\prime}
which is of dimension

. Thus it suffices to show that there is no representation

of dimension

and satisfying

(for a contradiction). The
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
-socle of

Let

be the unique subrepresentation of dimension

(cf. Lemma

). Corollary

implies that
\pi_{1}

∎

2.4.
Annihilators

Recall that the graded ring
\gr(\Lambda)
is isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra (over

) of the Lie algebra

Proposition 2.22

If
\mathfrak{a}
denotes the left ideal of
\gr(\Lambda)
generated by

and

\gr(\Lambda)/\mathfrak{a}

\gr(\Lambda)/(y,z^{2})

Proof.

It is enough to show that

[y,h]=0
by (

2yzy=y^{2}z+zy^{2}\in\mathfrak{a},

so

(as

[z,h]=0

∎

The group

acts on
\gr(\Lambda)
in the following way. For
g\in H
,

(2.9)

gy=\alpha(g)y,\ \ gz=\alpha^{-1}(g)z,\ \ gh=h.

We say that
N
is a
\gr(\Lambda)
-module with
compatible
H
-action
if
H
acts on
N
such that

g(rv)=(gr)(gv),\ \ \ g\in H,r\in\gr(\Lambda),v\in N.

Typical examples are
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
for smooth representations
\pi
of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
.

Using (
2.9
), it is easy to see that as
H
-modules

(2.10)

\gr^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\Lambda)=\mathbb{F}y\oplus\mathbb{F}z\cong\alpha\oplus\alpha^{-1},\ \ \gr^{2}_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\Lambda)=\mathbb{F}y^{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}yz\oplus\mathbb{F}zy\oplus\mathbb{F}z^{2}\cong\alpha^{2}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{1}\oplus\alpha^{-2}.

Note that the assumption
p\geq 5
ensures that
\alpha^{2}\neq\alpha^{-2}.
We deduce the following easy result, which will be repeatedly used in next subsections.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
. Assume that
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
is
1
-dimensional isomorphic to
\chi
. Then
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
is a cyclic
\gr(\Lambda)
-module. Moreover,

(i)

if
\chi\alpha
(resp.
\chi\alpha^{-1}
) does not occur in
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
, then
z
(resp.
y
) annihilates
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
;

(ii)

if
\chi\alpha^{2}
(resp.
\chi\alpha^{-2}
) does not occur in
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]
, then
z^{2}
(resp.
y^{2}
) annihilates
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
;

(iii)

if
\chi
does not occur in
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]
, then
(yz,zy)
annihilates
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
.

Next we give a criterion for
\pi
to satisfy
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\pi[Y]=1
.

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
\pi
be a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
. Assume that
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]
is
3
-dimensional and isomorphic to
E^{-}(\chi,2)
for some character
\chi
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\pi[Y]=1
.

Proof.

The assumption implies that
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]\cong\chi
,
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]\cong\chi\alpha^{-1}
and
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]\cong\chi\alpha^{-2}
. In particular,
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
is a cyclic
\gr(\Lambda)
-module. Moreover, it is annihilated by
(z,zy)
by Lemma
2.23
, hence is a quotient of
\mathbb{F}[y]
. This implies the result.
∎

2.5.
Extensions with
E^{-}(\chi)

Proposition 2.25
.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a smooth character. Then
\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi))\neq 0
if and only if
\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-2}\}
. Moreover, if this is the case, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi))=1
.

Proof.

Since
p>2
, the three characters
\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-2}
are all distinct.
Using Proposition
2.1
and the long exact sequence

(2.11)

\Hom(\psi,\chi\alpha^{-1})\hookrightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,\chi)\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,\chi\alpha^{-1})\rightarrow\Ext^{2}(\psi,\chi)

we easily deduce that
\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi))=0
if
\psi\notin\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-2}\}
.

If
\psi=\chi\alpha
, then
\Hom(\psi,\chi\alpha^{-1})=\Ext^{1}(\psi,\chi\alpha^{-1})=0
by Proposition
2.1
, and the result easily follows.
The case
\psi=\chi
is treated similarly, using
\Ext^{2}(\chi,\chi)=0
by Proposition
2.1
.

If
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-2}
, the result follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
\psi,\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be smooth characters.

(i)

If
\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha^{-2}\}
, then
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi))=0
.

(ii)

Assume that the natural morphism

\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi)\big)\rightarrow\Ext^{1}\big(\psi,E^{-}(\chi))

induced by
\psi\hookrightarrow E^{+}(\psi)
is non-zero. Then
\psi=\chi\alpha
and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi\alpha),E^{-}(\chi))=1.

Proof.

If
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi))\neq 0
then
\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-2}\}
or
\psi\alpha\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-2}\}
by Proposition
2.25
, equivalently

\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-1},\chi\alpha^{-2},\chi\alpha^{-3}\}.

(i) Assume first
\psi=\chi
. The short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\chi\rightarrow E^{+}(\chi)\rightarrow\chi\alpha\rightarrow 0
induces a long exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Hom(\chi,E^{-}(\chi))\overset{\partial}{\rightarrow}\Ext^{1}(\chi\alpha,E^{-}(\chi))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),E^{-}(\chi))\overset{\beta}{\rightarrow}\Ext^{1}(\chi,E^{-}(\chi)).

For the reason of dimensions, Proposition
2.25
implies that
\partial
is an isomorphism, so it suffices to prove
\beta=0
.
Let
c
be an extension class in
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),E^{-}(\chi))
such that
\beta(c)
is non-zero. Then
c[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
is one-dimensional (isomorphic to
\chi
), and so
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(c^{\vee})
is a cyclic
\gr(\Lambda)
-module with compatible
H
-action. Moreover, it is easy to see that
c
is uniserial of the form (with
\chi
being the socle)

\chi\ \textbf{---}\ \chi\alpha^{-1}\ \textbf{---}\ \chi\ \textbf{---}\ \chi\alpha

and so
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(c^{\vee})
is annihilated by the left ideal
(z,y^{2})
by Lemma
2.23
. But then Proposition
2.22
implies that
c
has dimension
\leq 3
, a contradiction.

Now assume
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-2}
, so that
\psi\alpha=\chi\alpha^{-1}
. Noting that
\Ext^{1}(\chi\alpha^{-1},E^{-}(\chi))=0
by Proposition
2.25
, we get an embedding

\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi))\hookrightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi)).

Let
c
be a non-zero extension class in
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi))
. Then
c
is uniserial of the form

\chi\ \textbf{---}\ \chi\alpha^{-1}\ \textbf{---}\ \chi\alpha^{-2}\ \textbf{---}\ \chi\alpha^{-1}.

So its dual has the form

\chi^{\vee}\alpha\ \textbf{---}\ \chi^{\vee}\alpha^{2}\ \textbf{---}\ \chi^{\vee}\alpha\ \textbf{---}\ \chi^{\vee}
,
and we conclude as in the above case.

(ii) By Proposition
2.25
, the assumption implies that
\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-2}\}
, so we must have
\psi=\chi\alpha
by (i). The last assertion is an easy exercise.
∎

Remark 2.27
.

With the notation in Proposition
2.26
, if
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-1}
or
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-3}
then one easily shows that
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi))
is
1
-dimensional.

2.6.
Extensions with
E^{-}(\chi,n)

In this subsection, we generalize Proposition
2.25
and Proposition
2.26
to the case
n\geq 2
.

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
2\leq n\leq p-1
and
\psi,\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be smooth characters. Then
\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi,n))\neq 0
if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-n-1}\}&\mathrm{if}\ n<p-1\\
\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha\}&\mathrm{if}\ n=p-1.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, when
\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi,n))\neq 0
, the following statements hold.

(i)

The natural morphism (induced by
E^{-}(\chi)\hookrightarrow E^{-}(\chi,n)
)

(2.12)

\Ext^{1}\big(\psi,E^{-}(\chi)\big)\rightarrow\Ext^{1}\big(\psi,E^{-}(\chi,n)\big)

is an isomorphism unless
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-n-1}
(and
n<p-1
).

(ii)

In each case,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi,n))=1
.

Proof.

To simplify the notation, write
\pi=E^{-}(\chi,n)
. Let
\pi^{\prime}
be a non-zero extension class in
\Ext^{1}(\psi,\pi)
, i.e.

0\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}\rightarrow\psi\rightarrow 0.

Then
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]=\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]\cong\chi
. As a consequence,
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\prime\vee})
is a cyclic
\gr(\Lambda)
-module.

Since
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
is one-dimensional, we see that
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
has dimension
\leq 2
.

(a)

If
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
has dimension
2
then

\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]\cong(\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}])\oplus\psi=\chi\alpha^{-1}\oplus\psi,

and
\pi^{\prime}
lies in the image of the natural morphism

(2.13)

\Ext^{1}(\psi,\chi)\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,\pi).

By Proposition
2.25
we have
\psi\in\{\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-1}\}
. But
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
is multiplicity free by (
2.10
), so
\psi\neq\chi\alpha^{-1}
. Therefore we have
\psi=\chi\alpha
.

(b)

If
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
is one-dimensional, then it is equal to
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}]
and isomorphic to
\chi\alpha^{-1}
. Similarly,
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]
has dimension
\leq 2
, and if the equality holds, then

\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]\cong(\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}])\oplus\psi=\chi\alpha^{-2}\oplus\psi

and
\pi^{\prime}
lies in the image of the natural morphism

\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,\pi)

but not in the image of (
2.13
). Combined with Proposition
2.25
(and its proof), this forces
\psi\in\{\chi\alpha^{-2},\chi\}
. But
\chi\alpha^{-2}
occurs in
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]
with multiplicity one by (
2.10
), so we must have
\psi\cong\chi
.

Now, assume
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]/\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}]
has dimension
1
, equivalently,
\pi^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]=\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]
which is isomorphic to
E^{-}(\chi,2)
. By Lemma
2.24
, we deduce that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\pi^{\prime}[Y]=1
, so by Proposition
2.21

\pi^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
E^{-}(\chi,n+1)
and
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-(n+1)}
. If
n=p-1
, this can not happen by Proposition
2.21
again.

Thus, the first statement of the proposition is proved.
Moreover, if
\psi\in\{\chi\alpha,\chi\}
, it follows from the above proof that (
2.12
) is an isomorphism, proving (i) and also (ii) in these cases.

We are left to prove (ii) for
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-n-1}
and
n<p-1
, which follows from the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 2.29
.

Let
2\leq n\leq p-1
and let
\chi,\psi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be smooth characters such that
\psi\neq\chi\alpha^{-(n+2)}
when
n=p-1
. Assume that the natural morphism

\beta:\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi,n))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi,n))

is non-zero.
Then
\psi=\chi\alpha
.

Proof.

We have a short exact sequence

(2.14)

0\rightarrow E^{-}(\chi)\rightarrow E^{-}(\chi,n)\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0

where
Q:=E^{-}(\chi\alpha^{-2},n-2)
.

The assumption implies in particular that

\Ext^{1}(\psi,E^{-}(\chi,n))\neq 0
, so

\psi\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-n-1}\}

by Proposition
2.28
. We need to prove that the case
\psi=\chi
or
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-n-1}
can not happen.

(a)

Assume
\psi=\chi
. Using (
2.14
) we get an exact sequence

\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),E^{-}(\chi))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),E^{-}(\chi,n))\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),Q).

Proposition
2.26
implies that
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),E^{-}(\chi))=0
. To prove the result it suffices to prove that
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\chi),Q)=0
. If
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}Q=1
, then
Q\cong\chi\alpha^{-2}
and the claim is obvious because
\chi,\chi\alpha\notin\{\chi\alpha^{-1},\chi\alpha^{-3}\}
(as
p\geq 5
), see Proposition
2.1
. If
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}Q\geq 2
, then by Proposition
2.25
and Proposition
2.28

\chi\in\{\chi\alpha^{-2},\chi\alpha^{-1},\chi\alpha^{-n-1}\}\ \mathrm{or}\ \chi\alpha\in\{\chi\alpha^{-2},\chi\alpha^{-1},\chi\alpha^{-n-1}\}.

This is only possible when
\chi\alpha=\chi\alpha^{-n-1}
and
n=p-1
, but we have excluded this case.

(b)

Assume
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-n-1}
. Let
c\in\Ext^{1}\big(E^{+}(\psi),E^{-}(\chi,n)\big)
be a non-zero class with
\beta(c)\neq 0
. Then by Proposition
2.28
and its proof,
\beta(c)
is a uniserial representation isomorphic to
E^{-}(\chi,n+1)
. In particular,
c[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]=E^{-}(\chi,n+1)[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{3}]
has dimension
3
. Using Lemma
2.24
and Proposition
2.21
, we deduce that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}c[Y]=1
and
c\cong E^{-}(\chi,n+2)
. But this implies that
\psi=\chi\alpha^{-n-1}

and

\psi\alpha=\chi\alpha^{-n-2}
, which is impossible. ∎

We will need the following auxiliary lemma. Recall that, for a character
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
,
\chi^{\sigma}
denotes the character
\chi(\varpi_{D}^{-1}\cdot\varpi_{D})
.

Lemma 2.30
.

Let
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a smooth character and
1\leq n\leq p-2
. Let
V
be a smooth representation of
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
fitting in an extension

0\rightarrow\chi\oplus E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n)\rightarrow V\rightarrow\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}\rightarrow 0.

Assume that
V[\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}_{D}]=\chi\oplus E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n)
and
\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}\neq\chi\alpha^{-1}
.
Then
V\cong\chi\oplus E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n+1)
.

Proof.

First note that the assumption on
V[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n+1}]
implies that
V
is non-split and, moreover, that the induced extension
0\rightarrow E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n)\rightarrow V/\chi\rightarrow\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}\rightarrow 0
is non-split (this needs
n\geq 1
). As a consequence,
V/\chi
is isomorphic to
E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n+1)
by Lemma
2.5
, namely

(2.15)

0\rightarrow\chi\rightarrow V\rightarrow E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n+1)\rightarrow 0.

On the other hand, the quotient
V/E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n)
gives an extension class
c\in\Ext^{1}(\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1},\chi)
.

\bullet

If
c=0
, then it is easy to see that (
2.15
) splits, and the result follows.

\bullet

If
c\neq 0
, then

\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}\in\{\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-1}\}

by Proposition
2.1
. Since
\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}\neq\chi\alpha^{-1}
by assumption, we must have
\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}=\chi\alpha
, equivalently
\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n}=\chi
. Then the cokernel of
E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n-1)\hookrightarrow E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n)\hookrightarrow V
satisfies the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\chi\oplus\chi\rightarrow V/E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n-1)\rightarrow\chi\alpha\rightarrow 0.

Using the fact that
\Ext^{1}(\chi\alpha,\chi)
is
1
-dimensional, we see that
V/E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n-1)
admits a one-dimensional quotient isomorphic to
\chi
. Hence,
V
also admits a one-dimensional quotient isomorphic to
\chi
.
Applying
\Hom(-,\chi)
to (
2.15
) and noting that
\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1}=\chi\alpha\neq\chi
, we obtain an injection

0\rightarrow\Hom(V,\chi)\rightarrow\Hom(\chi,\chi)

which is actually an isomorphism for the reason of dimensions, hence the sequence (
2.15
) splits.

This finishes the proof.
∎

2.7.
A finiteness result

Recall that
H
acts on
\gr(\Lambda)
via (
2.9
). For a character
\chi
of
H
, we write
\chi\otimes\gr(\Lambda)
for the module
\gr(\Lambda)
twisted by
\chi
.

Theorem 2.31
.

Let
\chi:\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a smooth character. Assume that
\chi\neq\chi^{\sigma}
and
\chi\neq\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{p}
. Then there does not exist any smooth
D^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
-representation
\pi
such that

(2.16)

\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})\cong\big(\chi^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{F}[y]/(y^{p+2})\big)\oplus\big((\chi^{\sigma})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{F}[z]/(z^{p+2})\big).

Remark 2.32
.

We can make the condition on
\chi
in Theorem
2.31
more concrete:
\chi\neq\chi^{\sigma}
if and only if
\chi\neq\xi^{s(p+1)}
for any
s\geq 0
;
\chi\neq\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{p}
if and only if
\chi\neq\xi^{1+s(p+1)}
for any
s\geq 0
. Here
\xi
denotes the character
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
via the fixed embedding
\mathbb{F}_{q}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

Suppose that such a representation as in the statement exists. We prove inductively the following

Claim
.
For any
0\leq n\leq p
, there exists an
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}/Z_{D}^{1}
-subrepresentation
V_{n}
of
\pi|_{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}
of dimension
n+1
, such that
V_{n}[Y]
is one-dimensional and isomorphic to
\chi
.

This will finish the proof because
V_{p}
does not exist by Proposition
2.21
.

The claim is clear if
n=0
. For
n=1
, it follows from (
2.16
) and the following general fact: if
M
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module with compatible
H
-action annihilated by
\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}
, then
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(M)
is naturally isomorphic to
M
,
because this is the case for
\Lambda/\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{2}
.

Assume that
V_{n}
has been constructed for some
1\leq n<p
. Then

V_{n}\cong E^{-}(\chi,n)

by Proposition
2.21
. The inclusion
V_{n}\hookrightarrow\pi
induces an inclusion
\varpi_{D}V_{n}\hookrightarrow\pi
. Since
V_{n}
and
\varpi_{D}V_{n}
have non-isomorphic
\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}
-socles (as
\chi\neq\chi^{\sigma}
), we obtain an inclusion
V_{n}\oplus\varpi_{D}V_{n}\hookrightarrow\pi
, whose image is identified with
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n+1}]
by (
2.16
).
Set

Q_{n}:=\pi/(V_{n}\oplus\varpi_{D}V_{n}).

By the assumption on
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(\pi^{\vee})
, one checks that

(2.17)

\gr_{\mathfrak{m}_{D}}(Q_{n}^{\vee})\cong\big(\psi^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{F}[y]/(y^{p+1-n})\big)\oplus\big((\psi^{\sigma})^{\vee}\otimes\mathbb{F}[z]/(z^{p+1-n})\big)

where
\psi:=\chi\alpha^{-(n+1)}
(the case
\psi=\psi^{\sigma}
is allowed). In particular,
\soc_{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}Q_{n}\cong\psi\oplus\psi^{\sigma}
. Pulling back
\psi^{\sigma}
we obtain a subrepresentation of
\pi|_{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}
which is an extension of
\psi^{\sigma}
by
V_{n}\oplus\varpi_{D}V_{n}
, hence induces an extension class in
\Ext^{1}(\psi^{\sigma},V_{n})
which we denote by
c
.
We have the following possibilities.

\bullet

Assume
c=0
. Then
\pi
contains a subrepresentation
V_{n+1}^{\prime}
fitting in an extension

0\rightarrow\varpi_{D}V_{n}\rightarrow V_{n+1}^{\prime}\rightarrow\psi^{\sigma}\rightarrow 0.

Moreover,
V_{n+1}^{\prime}
is uniserial by checking
V_{n+1}^{\prime}[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n+1}]
which is contained in
\pi[\mathfrak{m}_{D}^{n+1}]
. We take
V_{n+1}:=\varpi_{D}V_{n+1}^{\prime}
and need to show that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V_{n+1}[Y]=1
. Since
\varpi_{D}^{-1}Y\varpi_{D}=Z
(see Definition
2.16
), equivalently we need to show

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V_{n+1}^{\prime}[Z]=1.

This is clear if
n<p-1
, because
V_{n+1}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
E^{+}(\chi^{\sigma},n+1)
by the conjugate version of Proposition
2.28
(and its proof). If
n=p-1
, then Proposition
2.28
shows that
V_{n+1}^{\prime}
can not be uniserial, i.e. this case can not happen.

\bullet

Assume
c
is nonzero and
n<p-1
. Then by Proposition
2.28
we have

\psi^{\sigma}\in\{\chi,\chi\alpha,\chi\alpha^{-(n+1)}\}
. Using (
2.17
) and the general fact recalled above, we see that
E^{+}(\psi^{\sigma})
embeds in
Q_{n}
, and induces a natural extension class in
\Ext^{1}(E^{+}(\psi^{\sigma}),V_{n})
whose image in
\Ext^{1}(\psi^{\sigma},V_{n})
is just
c
, hence is non-zero.
By Proposition
2.29
, we must have

(2.18)

\psi^{\sigma}(=\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1})=\chi\alpha.

Note that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\Ext^{1}(\psi^{\sigma},V_{n})=1
by Proposition
2.28
, and it is easy to see that the natural morphism

\Ext^{1}(\psi^{\sigma},\chi)\rightarrow\Ext^{1}(\psi^{\sigma},V_{n})

is an isomorphism for the reason of dimensions. Thus,
c
comes from the pushout of the unique non-split extension in
\Ext^{1}(\psi^{\sigma},\chi)
. We deduce that
\pi|_{\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}}
contains a subrepresentation which is an extension of
\psi^{\sigma}(=\chi^{\sigma}\alpha^{n+1})
by

Let

Lemma 3.11

Assume that

-invariant lattice in

Proof.

Let
\Theta^{\prime}
be another open bounded
D^{\times}
-lattice in
\Pi
. Then
\Theta
and
\Theta^{\prime}
are commensurable, so we may assume

\varpi^{n}\Theta^{\prime}\subset\Theta\subset\Theta^{\prime}

for some

.
As a consequence,
\Theta^{\prime}/\Theta
is killed by
\varpi^{n}
. The exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Theta\rightarrow\Theta^{\prime}\rightarrow\Theta^{\prime}/\Theta\rightarrow 0

induces, by tensoring with

, an exact sequence

(3.4)

0\rightarrow\Theta^{\prime}/\Theta\rightarrow\Theta/\varpi^{n}\Theta\rightarrow\Theta^{\prime}/\varpi^{n}\Theta^{\prime}\rightarrow\Theta^{\prime}/\Theta\rightarrow 0.

Recall that the category
\mathcal{C}
is stable under subquotients and extensions. The assumption implies that
\Theta/\varpi^{n}\Theta\in\mathcal{C}
, hence
\Theta^{\prime}/\Theta\in\mathcal{C}
too, and finally
\Theta^{\prime}/\varpi^{n}\Theta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
. This implies the first assertion.
Moreover, we deduce from (

) that
\mu(\Theta/\varpi^{n}\Theta)=\mu(\Theta^{\prime}/\varpi^{n}\Theta^{\prime})
by the additivity of
\mu(\cdot)
, from which the second assertion follows.
∎

By abuse of notation, we say

satisfies the condition of Lemma

for

by choosing an open bounded

-invariant lattice

.

is additive with respect to short exact sequences of admissible unitary Banach space representations of

Assume

Proof.

By the above discussion, it is a direct consequence of Theorem

∎

Denote by

\widehat{H}^{1}(U^{p},\mathcal{O}):=\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}\varinjlim_{K_{p}}H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X(K_{p}U^{p})_{\overline{F}},\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{n}),

where
K_{p}
runs over compact open subgroups of
\prod_{v|p}(\mathcal{O}_{B})_{v}^{\times}
and
X(K_{p}U^{p})
is the corresponding Shimura curve for
B
defined over
F
.
For
\psi
and
\lambda
as above, we may analogously define

\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi}(U^{p},\mathcal{O})
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})
, see
[
Paš22
, §6]
.

The abstract Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}^{\rm univ}_{S}
naturally acts on
H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X(K_{p}U^{p})_{\overline{F}},\mathcal{O})
. Let
\mathbb{T}(K_{p}U^{p})
denote the image of
\mathbb{T}^{\rm univ}_{S}
in
\End\big(H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X(K_{p}U^{p})_{\overline{F}},\mathcal{O})\big)
and
\mathbb{T}(K_{p}U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
be its
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion. Let

\mathbb{T}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\varprojlim_{K_{p}}\mathbb{T}(K_{p}U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}.

It acts faithfully and continuously on
\widehat{H}^{1}(U^{p},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and preserves
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Finally we let
\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
be the image of
\mathbb{T}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
in
\End\big(\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\big)
.

One can prove as in
[
Sch18
, Cor. 7.3]
that the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}
also acts continuously on
S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}.
Based on
[
Sch18
, Thm. 6.2]
,
[
Paš22
, Prop. 6.3]
proves that there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}\times D^{\times}]
-modules

(5.1)

\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d})\cong\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}.

As a consequence,
S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is also faithful over
\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}.

From now on, we assume that
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
, i.e.
\overline{r}
is modular. This is equivalent to
S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq 0
by (
5.1
) and the proof of
[
Paš22
, Thm. 6.5]
. It is well-known that there is an associated
2
-dimensional Galois representation

r_{\mathfrak{m}}:G_{F,S}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}})

deforming
\overline{r}
(see
[
Sch18
, Prop. 5.7]
). For any
y\in\mathrm{Spm}(\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p])
,
r_{\mathfrak{m}}
specializes to a continuous Galois representation
r_{y}:G_{F,S}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\kappa(y))
, where
\kappa(y)
denotes the residue field of
\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p]
at
y
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
y\in\mathrm{Spm}(\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p])
be such that
\rho_{y}:=r_{y}(1)|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is irreducible. Then

(5.2)

\big(S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E\big)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]\cong\Pi(\rho_{y})^{\oplus n}.

for some integer
n\geq 0
, where
\mathfrak{m}_{y}\subset\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p]
denotes the maximal ideal associated to
y
.

Proof.

The result is proved in
[
Paš22
, Cor. 5.7]
. Remark that our normalization is different from that of
[
Paš22
, §2]
; precisely, the Banach space representation corresponding to
\rho
in
loc. cit.
is equal to
\Pi(\rho(1))
.
∎

Let
\overline{\rho}:=\overline{r}(1)|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}

and
\mathscr{B}
be the block corresponding to
\overline{\rho}^{\rm ss}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that
\mathscr{B}
is not of type (IV). Let
y\in\mathrm{Spm}(\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p])
be such that
\rho_{y}:=r_{y}(1)|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is irreducible and
\big(S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E\big)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]\neq 0.
Then
\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))^{\rm lalg}
is finite dimensional and
\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))
is
\rho_{y}(-1)
-isotypic, i.e.

(5.3)

\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))\cong\rho_{y}(-1)\otimes\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})

for some admissible unitary Banach space representation
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})
of
D^{\times}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is proved in
[
Paš22
, Prop. 6.15]
. The running assumption in
loc. cit.
requires
\overline{\rho}
to be reducible, but this is not necessary in the proof.

By the assumption on
\overline{\rho}
and using (
5.1
), we get an isomorphism of
G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}\times D^{\times}
-representations

\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}\big((S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}L)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]\big)\cong\big(\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E\big)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}],

see the proof of
[
Paš22
, Thm. 6.11]
. On the other hand, combining Propositions 5.3, 5.4, 5.8 of
[
Sch18
]
, we obtain that
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}
is
r_{\mathfrak{m}}
-isotypic and
\big(\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E\big)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]
is
r_{y}
-isotypic. Hence,

\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))
is also
r_{y}
-isotypic by Proposition
5.1
; we need the assumption
\big(S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E\big)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]\neq 0
to ensure that
n>0
in (
5.2
). Letting

\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y}):=\Hom_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\big(\rho_{y}(-1),\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))\big),

we obtain (
5.3
).
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
\mathscr{B}
is of type (I) or (II), and is generic if
\mathscr{B}
is of type (I). Then both
S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}
are flat over
\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Proof.

This is proved by the same argument of
[
GN22
, Thm. A(1)]
by using the the miracle flatness criterion (
[
GN22
, Prop. A.30]
). See
[
HW22
, Prop. 8.10]
for the case of
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}.
The case of
S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}
is similar.
∎

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
\mathscr{B}
is of type (I) or (II), and is generic.
Let
y\in\mathrm{Spm}(\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p])
be such that
\rho_{y}:=r_{y}(1)|_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}
is irreducible. Then
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})
is topologically of finite length, and the quotient
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})/\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})^{\rm lalg}
is topologically irreducible.

Proof.

First, Lemma
5.3
implies that

\big(S_{\psi,\lambda}(U^{\mathfrak{p}},\mathcal{O})_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}E\big)[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]\neq 0

for any
y\in\mathrm{Spm}(\mathbb{T}(U^{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{m}}[1/p])
.
By Proposition
5.2
,
\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))^{\rm lalg}
is finite dimensional over
E
, and so is
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})^{\rm lalg}
using (
5.3
). To conclude, we check that
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})
satisfies the (other) conditions of Corollary
3.15
.

Since
\overline{r}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F})
is irreducible, there exists a unique (up to homothety)
G_{F,S}
-invariant lattice in
r_{y}
, which induces a
G_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
-invariant lattice in
\rho_{y}
whose mod
\varpi
reduction is just
\overline{\rho}
. This lattice in
\rho_{y}
corresponds to an open bounded
G
-invariant lattice in
\Pi(\rho_{y})
, which we denote by
\Theta
. By the compatibility between
p
-adic and mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for
G
(
[
Ber10
]
), we have

\Theta/\varpi\Theta\cong\pi(\overline{\rho}).

The assumption on
\overline{\rho}
implies that
\mathcal{S}^{0}(\pi(\overline{\rho}))=\mathcal{S}^{2}(\pi(\overline{\rho}))=0
, by Theorem
4.11
. Hence, Proposition
4.14
implies that
\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Theta^{d})^{d}
is an open bounded
D^{\times}
-invariant lattice in
\check{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Pi(\rho_{y}))
, with mod
\varpi
reduction
\mathcal{S}^{1}(\pi(\overline{\rho}))
.

Again by the assumption on
\overline{\rho}
,
we have
\mathcal{S}^{1}(\pi(\overline{\rho}))\cong\overline{\rho}(-1)\otimes\mathrm{JL}(\overline{\rho})

by Theorem
4.16
and
\mathcal{S}^{1}(\pi(\overline{\rho}))\in\mathcal{C}
by
[
HW22
, Cor. 6.10]
. Moreover, Theorem
4.19
implies that
\mathrm{JL}(\overline{\rho})^{\vee}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
\mathbb{F}[\![U_{D}^{1}/Z_{D}^{1}]\!]
-module (as its graded module is) and

\mu(\mathrm{JL}(\overline{\rho}))=4.

Using (
5.3
) and Lemma
3.11
, all the conditions of Corollary
3.15
are satisfied for
\mathrm{JL}(\rho_{y})
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

We hope that Theorem
5.4
remains true in other global situations. The reason to restrict to the current setup is the easy citation to
[
Sch18
]
and
[
Paš22
]
.

6.
Appendix: Gabber filtrations

In this appendix, we recall from
[
BE90
]
the construction of Gabber filtrations on pure modules over a filtered Auslander regular ring.

6.1.
Auslander regular rings

Let
\Lambda
be an Auslander regular ring and
M
be a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. We define the
grade
of
M
as

j_{\Lambda}(M):=\mathrm{inf}\{i\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ \Ext^{i}_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)\neq 0\},

with the convention
j_{\Lambda}(M):=\infty
if
M=0
.
Let
d
be the global dimension of
\Lambda
. Set

\dim_{\Lambda}(M):=d-j_{\Lambda}(M)

and call it the
dimension
of
M
.

Definition 6.1
.

We say
M
is
Cohen-Macaulay
if
\Ext^{i}_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)=0
for any
i\neq j_{\Lambda}(M)
, and
M
is
pure
if any nonzero submodule has the same grade as
M
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Cohen-Macaulay
\Lambda
-modules are pure. Conversely, a pure
\Lambda
-module of grade
d-1
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

The first statement is well-known. For the second,
let
M
be a pure finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. It is equivalent to prove that
\Ext^{d}_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)=0
. If not, then
\Ext^{d}_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)
has grade
d
. But, since
M
is pure, it follows from
[
LvO96
, Chap. III, Thm. 4.2.6]
that
\Ext^{d}_{\Lambda}(\Ext^{d}_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda),\Lambda)=0
, a contradiction. ∎

Remark 6.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module of grade
d-1
. By
[
Ven02
, §3.1]
,
M
has a unique submodule
M_{d}
whose grade is
d
(equivalently
M_{d}
is finite dimensional over
\mathbb{F}
). Then
M/M_{d}
is pure of grade
d-1
, hence Cohen-Macaulay by Lemma
6.2
.

6.2.
Gabber filtrations

Let
R_{\Lambda}
denote the
Rees ring
associated to
\Lambda
, which is a graded subring of
\Lambda[T,T^{-1}]
(here
T
is a formal variable of degree
1
). Given a good filtration
F
on
M
(in the sense of
[
LvO96
, §1.5]
), let
M_{F}
denote the associated
Rees module
which is a graded module over
R_{\Lambda}
. It is known that
M_{F}
is
T
-torsion-free and we have a natural isomorphism

(6.1)

M_{F}/TM_{F}\cong\gr_{F}(M).

See
[
BE90
, §4.5]
or
[
LvO96
, Chap. I, §4.3]
for more details.

It is proved in
[
LvO96
, Chap. III, Thm. 3.1.7]
that if
\Lambda
and
\gr(\Lambda)
are Auslander regular rings, then so is
R_{\Lambda}
. From now on, we assume that
\Lambda
and
\gr(\Lambda)
are both Auslander regular rings.

Recall the following definition from
[
BE90
, Def. 5.22]
.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
M
be a pure finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. Two good filtrations
F
and
F^{\prime}
of
M
are called
tamely close
if
j_{R_{\Lambda}}(M_{F+F^{\prime}}/M_{F\cap F^{\prime}})\geq j_{\Lambda}(M)+2
.

Here, the sum and the intersection of two filtrations are defined to be

(F+F^{\prime})^{n}M:=F^{n}M+F^{\prime n}M,\ \ (F\cap F^{\prime})^{n}M:=F^{n}M\cap F^{\prime n}M

respectively.
It is easy to see that being tamely close defines an equivalence relation on the family of good filtrations on
M
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
M
be a pure finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. Then every equivalence class of good filtrations on
M
contains a unique
F
such that
\gr_{F}(M)
is a pure
\gr(\Lambda)
-module of grade equal to
j_{\Lambda}(M)
. Moreover, if
F^{\prime}
is another good filtration in the equivalence class of
F
, then
F^{\prime}<F
in the sense that
F^{\prime n}M\subseteq F^{n}M
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the kernel of the induced morphism
\gr_{F^{\prime}}(M)\rightarrow\gr_{F}(M)
is identified with the largest submodule of
\gr_{F^{\prime}}(M)
of grade
>j_{\Lambda}(M)
. In particular, the morphism
\gr_{F^{\prime}}(M)\rightarrow\gr_{F}(M)
is non-zero.

The unique filtration in Theorem
6.5
is referred as a
Gabber filtration
.

Proof.

The first statement and the fact
F^{\prime}<F
are proved in
[
BE90
, Thm. 5.23]
. For the convenience of the reader, we recall the proof. Below we write
n:=j_{\Lambda}(M)=j_{\gr(\Lambda)}(\gr_{F}(M))
; here the second equality is a standard fact, see
[
LvO96
, Chap. III, Thm. 2.5.2]
.

First, the construction of
F
in a given equivalence class of good filtrations such that
\gr_{F}(M)
is pure is well-known, see
[
BE90
, Lem. 5.19, Cor. 5.21]
, or
[
LvO96
, Chap. III, Thm. 4.2.13(4)]
for a more detailed presentation. For the uniqueness of
F
, we need to show that

M_{F}=M_{F+F^{\prime}}

for any good filtration
F^{\prime}
which is tamely close to
F
. Put
Q=M_{F+F^{\prime}}/M_{F}
. Then
j_{R_{\Lambda}}(Q)\geq n+2
by Definition
6.4
.
Using the
T
-torsion-freeness of
M_{F+F^{\prime}}
and (
6.1
), multiplication by
T
induces an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Ker_{T}(Q)\rightarrow\gr_{F}(M)\rightarrow\gr_{F+F^{\prime}}(M)\rightarrow Q/TQ\rightarrow 0,

where
\Ker_{T}(Q)
denotes the kernel of
T:Q\rightarrow Q
. Note that since
R_{\Lambda}/T\cong\gr(\Lambda)
,
\Ker_{T}(Q)
has a
\gr(\Lambda)
-module structure. Since
T
is a central non-zero-divisor in
R_{\Lambda}
, it is easy to see that

j_{\gr(\Lambda)}(\Ker_{T}(Q))=j_{R_{\Lambda}}(\Ker_{T}(Q))-1\geq j_{R_{\Lambda}}(Q)-1\geq n+1,

where the first inequality holds as
\Ker_{T}(Q)\subseteq Q
.
However,
\gr_{F}(M)
is pure of grade
n
by construction, any non-zero submodule of
\gr_{F}(M)
also has grade
n
. This forces

\Ker_{T}(Q)=0.

On the other hand, since
F
and
F+F^{\prime}
are both good filtrations on
M
, we have
M_{F}[T^{-1}]=M_{F+F^{\prime}}[T^{-1}]
(here
[T^{-1}]
means taking localization at the multiplicative subset
\{T^{k},k\geq 0\}
). This implies that
Q
is a
T
-torsion
R_{\Lambda}
-module. Combined with the fact
\Ker_{T}(Q)=0
, we get
Q=0
, as required.

The last assertion essentially follows from the above argument. More precisely, since
F^{\prime}<F
, we may consider the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow M_{F^{\prime}}\rightarrow M_{F}\rightarrow M_{F}/M_{F^{\prime}}\rightarrow 0.

As above, multiplication by
T
induces an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Ker_{T}(M_{F}/M_{F^{\prime}})\rightarrow\gr_{F^{\prime}}(M)\rightarrow\gr_{F}(M).

Since
j_{\gr(\Lambda)}(\Ker_{T}(M_{F}/M_{F^{\prime}}))\geq n+1
and
\gr_{F}(M)
is pure of grade
n
, the result easily follows.
∎

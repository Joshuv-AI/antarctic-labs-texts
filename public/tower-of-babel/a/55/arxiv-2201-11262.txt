An upper bound on the generic degree of the generalized Verschiebung for rank two stable bundles
Yuichiro Hoshi, Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. In the present paper, we give an upper bound for the generic degree of the generalized Verschiebung between the moduli spaces of rank two stable bundles with trivial determinant.

Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
and
X
a smooth projective curve over
k
of genus
g>1
. Denote by
X^{(1)}
the Frobenius twist of
X
over
k
. Then, pulling-back stable bundles on
X^{(1)}
via the relative Frobenius morphism
F_{X/k}:X\rightarrow X^{(1)}
induces the so-called “
generalized Verschiebung
” rational map

(1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n}:\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/k}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{n}

between the moduli spaces
of rank
n>1
stable bundles with trivial determinant on
X^{(1)}
and
X
respectively;
this can be regarded as a higher-rank variant of the Verschiebung between Jacobians.

The geometry of the rational map
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n}
, i.e.,
the dynamics of stable bundles with respect to Frobenius pull-back, has been investigated
for a long time.
One motivation is the relationship with representations of the fundamental group of a curve in positive characteristic (cf., e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
30
]
); indeed, it is well-known that rank
n
vector bundles fixed by some powers of the Frobenius morphism come from continuous representations of the fundamental group in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
(cf.
[
20
]
).
Also,
the moduli space
\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{n}
and the generalized Verschiebung
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n}
are interesting in their own right; other studies regarding these mathematical objects (e.g., the density of Frobenius-periodic bundles, the loci of Frobenius-destabilized bundles, etc.)
can be found in
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
.

The present paper aims to address the generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n})
of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n}
for
n=2
.
The case of
(n,g)=(2,2)
has already been investigated considerably.
We know that, for a genus-
2
curve
X
, the compactification of
\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{2}
by semistable bundles is canonically isomorphic to the
3
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
and the boundary locus can be identified with the Kummer surface associated to
X
.
By this description,
the rational map
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
can be expressed by polynomials of degree
p
(cf.
[
17
, Proposition A.2]
), which are explicitly described in the cases
p=2
(cf.
[
16
]
) and
p=3
(cf.
[
17
]
).
Moreover, the scheme-theoretic base locus of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
were computed in
[
19
, Theorem 2]
.
This result enables us to specify the generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})
of

\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
, i.e., we have

\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})=\frac{p^{3}+2p}{3}
(cf.
[
26
, Theorem 1.3]
,
[
19
, Corollary]
).

However, we have not yet reached a comprehensive understanding of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n}
because
not much seems to be known for
general
(p,n,g)
.
That is, the structure of this rational map remains mysterious despite its own importance! As a step towards understanding it, for example,
knowing by what value the generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{n})
is bounded above will be useful information in measuring its complexity.
(As far as the authors know, it seems that the relevant numerical results are still obtained only when
(n,g)=(2,2)
.)
The main result of the present paper, i.e., Theorem
A
below, concerns
this matter and
provides an upper bound of the generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})
for infinitely many pairs
(p,g)
.

Theorem A

(= Theorem
3.2.3
)
.

If
p+1>g>1
and
p\neq 2
, then the generic degree
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})
of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
satisfies the following inequality:

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})\leq p^{g-1}\cdot\sum_{\theta=1}^{2p-1}\frac{1}{\mathrm{sin}^{2g-2}(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{2\cdot p})}\ \left(=\sum_{\zeta^{2p}=1,\zeta\neq 1}\frac{(-4p\zeta)^{g-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{2g-2}}\right).

We remark here that an essential ingredient in the proof of the above theorem is the correspondence between the generic fiber of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
and a certain Quot scheme.
As discussed by K. Joshi et al. (cf.
[
10
]
and an unpublished version
*
*

*

This version is available at:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.4359v1.pdf
of
[
9
]
),
Quot schemes appear naturally in the study of the action of the Frobenius morphism on vector bundles.
One of the new ideas included in the present paper is to establish
this correspondence by a dimension estimate resulting from Brill-Noether theory (cf. the proof of Lemma
2.3.1
).
We moreover use the generic étaleness of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
for an ordinary
X
to lift the Quot scheme to characteristic
0
.
As a result, the required inequality is obtained by applying a formula proved by Y. Holla (cf.
[
7
, Theorem 4.2]
), i.e., a special case of the Vafa-Intriligator formula.
The idea of applying Holla’s formula to counting objects of this type in positive characteristic is due to K. Joshi (cf.
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
).
A similar argument based on Joshi’s idea can be found in
the proof of the main theorem in
[
31
]
.

g>1
, a prime
p>2
, and an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p
.

For each scheme
T
, we shall write
(\mathcal{S}ch/T)
for the category of schemes of finite type over
T
.
For simplicity, if
R
is a ring, then we shall write
(\mathcal{S}ch/R):=(\mathcal{S}ch/\mathrm{Spec}(R))
.

For a smooth projective curve
X
over a field
K
,
we shall write
\Omega_{X/K}
for the sheaf of
1
-forms on
X
relative to
K
.
Also, for each integer
d
, denote by

\mathrm{Pic}_{X/K}^{d}
the Picard scheme of
X/K
classifying isomorphism classes of line bundles on
X
of degree
d
.

If
T
is a scheme and
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
are
T
-schemes, then we shall denote by
\mathrm{pr}_{i}
(
i=1,2
) the
i
-th projection
X_{1}\times_{T}X_{2}\rightarrow X_{i}
.

1.
Definition of the generalized Verschiebung

1.1.

Let
R
be an integrally closed domain of finite type over
k

and
X
a smooth projective curve over
R
of genus
g
.
We shall denote by

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X/R}^{2}:(\mathcal{S}ch/R)^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{S}et)

the set-valued contravariant functor on the category
(\mathcal{S}ch/R)
which, to any
T\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{S}ch/R)
, assigns the set of
equivalence
classes of
T
-flat families of rank
2
geometrically stable bundles on
X\times_{R}T
with trivial determinant.
Here,
given two
T
-flat families of geometrically stable bundles
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
on
X\times_{R}T
, we say that
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
are
equivalent
if
\mathcal{F}_{1}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{2}^{*}(\mathcal{N})\cong\mathcal{F}_{2}
for some line bundle
\mathcal{N}
on
T
.
According to
[
21
, Theorem 0.2]
and
[
13
, Theorem 9.12]
,
there exists a flat quasi-projective
R
-scheme

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SU}_{X/R}^{2}

that corepresents universally the functor
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X/R}^{2}
.
(Note that the flatness asserted in
[
13
]
is still true even when the base field is of positive characteristic.)
The fiber
\mathrm{SU}_{X/R}^{2}\times_{R}\xi

over each geometric point
\xi
of
\mathrm{Spec}(R)

is irreducible, smooth, and of dimension
3g-3

(cf., e.g.,
[
25
, Lemma A]
).

1.2.

Next, write
X^{(1)}
for the Frobenius twist of
X
over
R
and
F_{X/R}:X\rightarrow X^{(1)}
for the relative Frobenius morphism of
X
over
R
.
Let

(5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2,\circledcirc}

be the subfunctor of
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2}
classifying geometrically stable bundles whose pull-back under
F_{X/R}
is geometrically stable.
Recall (cf.
[
8
, Proposition 2.3.1]
) that
the property of being geometrically stable is an open condition in flat families.
Hence, there exists an open subscheme

(6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2,\circledcirc}

of
\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2}
that corepresents universally the functor
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2,\circledcirc}
(cf.
[
26
, Theorem A.6]
).
The assignment
[\mathcal{F}]\mapsto[F_{X/R}^{*}(\mathcal{F})]
(for each
[\mathcal{F}]\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2,\circledcirc}
) determines a natural transformation

(7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}er_{X/R}^{2,\circledcirc}:\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2,\circledcirc}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\mathcal{U}_{X/R}^{2}

of functors, which induces a dominant morphism

(8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ver}_{X/R}^{2,\circledcirc}:\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/R}^{2,\circledcirc}\rightarrow\mathrm{SU}_{X/R}^{2}

between flat
R
-schemes of the same relative dimension (cf.
[
25
]
,
[
26
, Theorem A.6]
).
The formation of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/R}^{2,\circledcirc}
is compatible, in an evident sense, with
restriction to each geometric point of
R
.
Thus, the fact mentioned in the final sentence of the previous subsection implies that
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/R}^{2,\circledcirc}
is generically flat.

Now, let us specialize the situation to the case where
R=k
.
Because of the facts mentioned above,
one may define the generic degree of
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2,\circledcirc}
, which we denote by

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}).

Here, denote by
\mathcal{M}_{g}
the moduli stack classifying smooth projective curves over
k
of genus
g
.
Since
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is
an irreducible
DM stack over
k
(cf.
[
2
, § 5]
),
it makes sense to speak of a “
general
” curve, i.e., a curve that
determines a point of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
that lies outside a certain fixed closed substack not equal to
\mathcal{M}_{g}
itself.
Note that the function on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
given by
X\mapsto\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})
is lower semicontinuous.
Therefore, in order to give an upper bound of the value
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})
,

we are always free to replace
X
with a general curve in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

2.
Relationship with Quot schemes

2.1.

We recall the notion of a Quot scheme as follows.
Let
T
be a noetherian scheme,

Y
a smooth projective curve over
T
of genus
g
and
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on
Y
.
For each integers
r
,
d
with
r\geq 0
, we shall denote by

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}uot_{\mathcal{E}/Y/T}^{r,d}:(\mathcal{S}ch/T)^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow(\mathcal{S}et)

the set-valued contravariant functor on the category
(\mathcal{S}ch/T)
which, to any
f:T^{\prime}\rightarrow T
, associates the set of isomorphism classes of

\mathcal{O}_{Y\times_{T}T^{\prime}}
-linear injections

s:\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow(\mathrm{id}_{Y}\times f)^{*}(\mathcal{E})

such that

•

the cokernel
\mathrm{Coker}(s)

is flat over
T^{\prime}

(which, by the fact that
Y/T
is smooth of relative dimension
1
, implies that
\mathcal{F}
is
locally free
), and

•

\mathcal{F}
is of rank
r
and degree
d
.

It is known (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 5.14]
) that
\mathcal{Q}uot_{\mathcal{E}/Y/T}^{r,d}

may be represented by a proper scheme over
T
.
By abuse of notation, write
\mathcal{Q}uot_{\mathcal{E}/Y/T}^{r,d}
for the scheme that represents the functor
\mathcal{Q}uot_{\mathcal{E}/Y/T}^{r,d}
.

2.2.

Let

X
be a smooth projective curve over
k
of genus
g
which is general in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.
In particular, we may assume that

X
is
ordinary
.
(Recall that the locus of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
classifying ordinary curves is open and dense.)
Moreover, we assume that
p+1>g\left(>1\right)
.
(This assumption will be applied in Lemma
2.3.1
and Proposition
3.2.1
.)
Let us take a geometric generic point
\eta:\mathrm{Spec}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{2}
(where
K
denotes an algebraically closed field over
k
) of

\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{2}
; this point classifies a rank
2
stable bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X_{K}:=X\times_{k}K
with
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}
.
Write
X^{(1)}_{K}
for the Frobenius twist of
X_{K}
over

K
and
F:X_{K}\rightarrow X_{K}^{(1)}
for the relative Frobenius morphism.
If
\mathcal{G}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}
-module and
\mathcal{H}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}
-module, then the adjunction relation “
F^{*}(-)\dashv F_{*}(-)
” gives a natural bijection

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}:\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}}(F^{*}(\mathcal{G}),\mathcal{H})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X^{(1)}_{K}}}(\mathcal{G},F_{*}(\mathcal{H})),

which is functorial with respect to both
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
.

Since
F
is finite and faithfully flat of degree
p
, the direct image
F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
forms a vector bundle on
X^{(1)}_{K}
of rank
2p
.
Now, consider the Quot scheme

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}:=\mathcal{Q}uot_{F_{*}(\mathcal{E})/X^{(1)}_{K}/K}^{2,0}.

This
K
-scheme has the closed subscheme

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv}}\ \left(\text{resp.,}\ \mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}\right)

classifying injections
s:\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
with
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X^{(1)}_{K}}
(resp.,
F^{*}(\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{F}))\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}
).
In particular, there exists a natural closed immersion
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}
.

2.3.

Since
X
has been assumed to be ordinary,
\mathrm{Ver}^{2}_{X/k}
is generically étale
(cf.
[
25
, Corollary 2.1.1]
).
Hence, the fiber product
\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/k}^{2,\circledcirc}\times_{\mathrm{SU}^{2}_{X/k},\eta}K
is isomorphic to the disjoint union of finitely many copies of
\mathrm{Spec}(K)
.
Let us take a
K
-rational point
\widetilde{\eta}
of
\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/k}^{2,\circledcirc}\times_{\mathrm{SU}^{2}_{X/k},\eta}K
;
this point
corresponds to a rank
2
stable bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{K}^{(1)}
with trivial determinant.
The point
\widetilde{\eta}
is, by definition, mapped to
\eta
by
\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2}
, meaning that
there exists an isomorphism
t:F^{*}(\mathcal{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{E}
.
The morphism
\mathrm{ad}(t):\mathcal{F}\rightarrow F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
is injective because the composite

(14)

\displaystyle F^{*}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{F^{*}(\mathrm{ad}(t))}F^{*}(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{F_{*}(\mathcal{E})})}\mathcal{E}

coincides with
t
and
F
is faithfully flat.
By the stability of
\mathcal{E}
(which implies that
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}}(\mathcal{E})=K
),
the
K
-rational point of
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv}}
classifying
\mathrm{ad}(t)
does not depend on the choice of
t
.
Hence,
the assignment
\mathcal{F}\mapsto\mathrm{ad}(t)
determines a well-defined
K
-morphism

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{SU}_{X^{(1)}/k}^{2,\circledcirc}\times_{\mathrm{SU}^{2}_{X/k},\eta}K\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv}}.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

(Recall that we have assumed that
p+1>g>1
.)
Let us take an
\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}
-linear injection
s:\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
such that
\mathcal{F}
is a rank
2
vector bundle.
We shall write
d:=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F})
.
Then, the following assertions hold:

(i)

The inequality
d\leq 0
holds, meaning that the maximal degree of rank
2
vector bundles embedded into
F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
is at most
0
.
(A similar maximality assertion can be found in an unpublished version of
[
9
, Proposition 11.6]
.)

(ii)

The injection

s
is classified by
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}
if and only if the morphism
\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s):F^{*}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\mathcal{E}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let us prove the first assertion.
Suppose that
d>0
.
By comparing the respective degrees of
F^{*}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{E}
,
the (nonzero) morphism
\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s)
cannot be an isomorphism at the generic point of
X_{K}
.
Hence, since
\mathcal{E}
is locally free of rank
2
and
X_{K}
is a smooth curve over
K
,
the subsheaf
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
of
\mathcal{E}
forms a line bundle.
Write

\nabla_{\mathrm{can}}:F^{*}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\Omega_{X_{K}/K}\otimes F^{*}(\mathcal{F})
for the
connection on
F^{*}(\mathcal{F})
determined uniquely by the condition that the sections of the subsheaf
F^{-1}(\mathcal{F})
are horizontal (cf.
[
12
, Theorem (5.1)]
).

In the following, we shall prove the claim that
the line subbundle
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
(
\subseteq F^{*}(\mathcal{F})
)
is not closed under
\nabla_{\mathrm{can}}
.
Suppose, on the contrary, that
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
is closed under
\nabla_{\mathrm{can}}
.
Since the restriction of
\nabla_{\mathrm{can}}
to
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
has vanishing
p
-curvature,
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
is isomorphic to
F^{*}(\mathcal{U})
for some line bundle
\mathcal{U}

on
X^{(1)}_{K}
.
Moreover, the resulting (horizontal) composite
F^{*}(\mathcal{U})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))\hookrightarrow F^{*}(\mathcal{F})
comes, via pull-back by
F
, from an injection
\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}
.
The composite
F^{*}(\mathcal{U})\hookrightarrow F^{*}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s)}\mathcal{E}
is identical to the zero map,
so the corresponding map
\mathcal{U}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\hookrightarrow}}F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
(via
\mathrm{ad}
)
must be the zero map.
This contradicts the injectivity of
s
, and hence, completes the proof of the claim.

Next, observe that

F^{*}(\mathcal{F})

may be regarded as an extension of
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
by
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))
;
let us fix an isomorphism
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
.
By the claim proved above, the following composite turns out to be injective:

(16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow F^{*}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109pt\xrightarrow{\nabla_{\mathrm{can}}}\Omega_{X_{K}/K}\otimes F^{*}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_{X_{K}/K}\otimes\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k})\otimes\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s)).

This composite may be verified to be
\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}
-linear, and hence, determines a nonzero global section

(17)

\displaystyle q\in\Gamma(X_{K},\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k})).

Let us fix
a line subbundle
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{E}
containing the subsheaf
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
.
(Hence,
\mathcal{E}
is obtained as an extension of
\mathcal{N}^{\vee}
by
\mathcal{N}
.)
The section
q
may be regarded, via the inclusion

\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\otimes 2}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2}
, as a (nonzero) global section of
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k})
.
On the other hand, according to
[
18
, Proposition 3.1]
, the degree of a line subbundle of
\mathcal{E}
is at most
-\frac{g}{2}
(resp.,
-\frac{g-1}{2}
) if
g
is even (resp., odd).
This implies

(18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k}))

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})-pd+2g-2

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\left(-\frac{g-1}{2}\right)-pd+2g-2

\displaystyle<0,

where the last inequality follows from the assumptions
p+1>g
and
d>0
.
However, this is a contradiction because
q\neq 0
.
Consequently, we have
d\leq 0
, as desired.

We now prove the second assertion.
The “if ” part is clear from
\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{E})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
, so it suffices to
consider the “only if” part.
Here, we assume that
s
is classified by
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}
, but the vector bundle
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))\left(\neq\{0\}\right)
is of rank
1
.
Just as in the proof of the first assertion,
we can obtain a nonzero global section

(19)

\displaystyle q\in\Gamma(X_{K},\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k}))

of the line bundle
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k})
(where we recall that
\mathrm{det}(F^{*}(\mathcal{F}))\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}}
).
Moreover, by taking a line subbundle
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{E}
containing
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(s))
, we may regard
q
as a (nonzero) global section of
\mathcal{N}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{1}^{*}(\Omega_{X/k})
.
By a well-known fact of Brill-Noether theory (cf., e.g.,
[
5
]
,
[

),
the existence of such a section

implies that the scheme-theoretic image

(because of the assumption that

On the other hand, the morphism

\leq 2g-2+2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})

However, this contradicts the fact proved in Lemma

=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{E})=0

The following lemma was applied in the proof of the previous assertion.

Lemma 2.3.2
.

Let us keep the notation in the proof of the second assertion of Lemma
2.3.1
.
Then, the scheme-theoretic image
I
of the morphism
\mathrm{Spec}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{Pic}_{X/k}^{\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})}
classifying
\mathcal{N}
is of dimension

\geq 2g-1+2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

Define
D
to be the moduli space classifying
isomorphism classes of short exact sequences:

(20)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{f}_{0}:0\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{0}\rightarrow 0

with
[\mathcal{N}_{0}]\in I
and
[\mathcal{E}_{0}]\in\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{2}
.
Let us take
an arbitrary exact sequence
\mathfrak{f}_{0}
as above.
By the definition of stability and the properness of
X/k
,
every nonzero endomorphism of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
is an isomorphism, and hence,

(21)

\displaystyle h^{0}((\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\vee})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{0})=h^{0}((\mathcal{E}_{0}/\mathcal{N}_{0})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{0})=0.

Thus, by the Riemann-Roch theorem, we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle h^{1}((\mathcal{N}^{\vee}_{0})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{0})=g-1-2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N}_{0})=g-1-2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N}).

This implies that any fiber of the projection

Hence, if
\mathrm{dim}(I)<2g-1+2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})

(23)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}(D)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{dim}(I)+g-2-2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle<(2g-1+2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N}))+g-2-2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=3g-3\left(=\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{SU}_{X/k}^{2})\right).

Thus,
we obtain the inequality

∎

3.1.

In this subsection, we review a numerical formula concerning the degree of a certain Quot scheme over the field of complex numbers

Thus, we have

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}/K)=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{Q}_{W}^{\mathrm{triv},F}/W)=\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{triv},F}/\mathbb{C})\leq\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{Q}uot^{2,0}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}}/X^{(1)}_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{C}).

If, moreover,
(a,b)
is the unique pair of integers satisfying
\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}})=2p\cdot a-b
with
0\leq b<2p
,
then it follows from the hypothesis
p+1>g
that
a=g-1
and
b=2(g-1)
.
Thus, since
\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}}
is assumed to be general,
we can apply Theorem
3.1.1
in the case where the data
“
(C,\mathcal{G},n,d,r,a,b,e_{\mathrm{max}}(\mathcal{G},r))
”
is taken to be

(40)

\displaystyle(X^{(1)}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}},2p,\delta,2,g-1,2(g-1),0)

and obtain the following sequence of equalities

(41)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{Q}uot^{2,0}_{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{C}}/X^{(1)}_{\mathbb{C}}/\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{(-1)^{(2-1)(2(g-1)2-(g-1)2^{2})/2p}(2p)^{2(g-1)}}{2!}\cdot\sum_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{2}\rho_{i}^{2(g-1)-g+1}}{\prod_{i\neq j}(\rho_{i}-\rho_{j})^{g-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (-1)^{g-1}\cdot 2^{2g-2}\cdot p^{2g-1}\cdot\sum_{\zeta^{2p}=1,\zeta\neq 1}\frac{\zeta^{g-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{2g-2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2^{g-1}\cdot p^{2g-1}\cdot\sum_{\zeta^{2p}=1,\zeta\neq 1}\frac{1}{(1-\frac{\zeta+\zeta^{-1}}{2})^{g-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ p^{2g-1}\cdot\sum_{\theta=1}^{2p-1}\frac{1}{\mathrm{sin}^{2g-2}(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{2\cdot p})}.

Thus, the assertion follows from (
39
) and (
41
).
∎

The following lemma was applied in the proof of the previous proposition.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
s:\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow F_{*}(\mathcal{E})
be an injection classified by a
K
-rational point of
\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}
.
Write
\mathcal{G}:=\mathrm{Coker}(s)
.
Then

\mathcal{G}
is a vector bundle on
X^{(1)}_{K}
, and
the following equality holds:

(42)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dim}_{K}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))=\mathrm{dim}_{K}(\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})).

Proof.

First, we verify that
\mathcal{G}
is a vector bundle.
Recall (cf. Lemma
2.3.1
) that
the composite

(43)

\displaystyle F^{*}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{F^{*}(s)}F^{*}(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{F_{*}(\mathcal{E})})}\mathcal{E}

is an isomorphism.
Hence,
the composite
\mathrm{Ker}(\mathrm{ad}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{F_{*}(\mathcal{E})}))\hookrightarrow F^{*}(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))\twoheadrightarrow F^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is an isomorphism, so
F^{*}(\mathcal{G})
is a vector bundle.
By the faithful flatness of
F
,
\mathcal{G}
turns out to be a vector bundle on
X_{K}^{(1)}
, as desired.

Next we shall prove (
42
).
Since
F
is finite, we have an equality of Euler characteristics
\chi(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))=\chi(\mathcal{E})=2(1-g)
.
Since
\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))=2\cdot(2p-2)
, it follows from
the Riemann-Roch theorem that

(44)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))=\chi(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))-\mathrm{rk}(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))(1-g)=\chi(\mathcal{E})-2p\cdot(1-g)=2\cdot(p-1)(g-1),

and that

(45)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))=

\displaystyle\ 2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{G})-(2p-2)\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 2\cdot\mathrm{deg}(F_{*}(\mathcal{E}))-0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 4\cdot(p-1)(g-1).

Finally,
by applying the Riemann-Roch theorem again, we obtain

(46)

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{dim}_{K}(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))-\mathrm{dim}_{K}(\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}^{1}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))+\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{K}^{(1)}}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}))(1-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 4\cdot(p-1)(g-1)+2\cdot(2p-2)(1-g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0,

thus completing the proof of this lemma.
∎

By applying the results obtained so far, we conclude the main result of the present paper.

Theorem 3.2.3

(= Theorem
A
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
k
of genus
g
with
p+1>g>1
and
p\neq 2
. Then,
the following inequality holds:

(47)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})\leq p^{g-1}\cdot\sum_{\theta=1}^{2p-1}\frac{1}{\mathrm{sin}^{2g-2}(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{2\cdot p})}\ \left(=\sum_{\zeta^{2p}=1,\zeta\neq 1}\frac{(-4p\zeta)^{g-1}}{(\zeta-1)^{2g-2}}\right).

Proof.

By the italicized comment at the end of §
1
,
one may assume, without loss of generality, that
X
is sufficiently general for which
the above discussions work.
Then, by the discussion at the beginning of §
3
and Proposition
3.2.1
, we have

(48)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})=\frac{1}{p^{g}}\cdot\mathrm{deg}(\mathcal{Q}^{\mathrm{triv},F}/K)\leq p^{g-1}\cdot\sum_{\theta=1}^{2p-1}\frac{1}{\mathrm{sin}^{2g-2}\left(\frac{\pi\cdot\theta}{2\cdot p}\right)}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Similarly to the discussion in
[
31
, § 6.2, (2)]
, we can describe the right-hand side of (
47
) as a polynomial with respect to
p
of degree
3g-3
.
For example, (

) reads

(49)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})\leq\frac{4p^{3}-p}{3}\ \text{if $g=2$,}\hskip 8.53581pt\text{and}\hskip 8.53581pt\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})\leq\frac{16p^{6}+40p^{4}-11p^{2}}{45}\ \text{if $g=3$}.

By comparing with the explicit computation of
\mathrm{deg}(\mathrm{Ver}_{X/k}^{2})
for

obtained already (cf. Introduction), we see that (

) is not optimal.
In particular, (by considering the discussion in the proof of Proposition

the

in Proposition

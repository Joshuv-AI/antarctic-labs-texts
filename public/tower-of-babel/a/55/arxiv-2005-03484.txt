Solving equations in dense Sidon sets
Sean Prendiville

Abstract
. We offer an alternative proof of a result of Conlon, Fox, Sudakov and Zhao [ 2 ] on solving translation-invariant linear equations in dense Sidon sets. Our proof generalises to equations in more than five variables and yields effective bounds.



1.
Introduction

A set
S
of integers is a
Sidon
set if the only solutions to the equation

(1.1)

x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}\quad(x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in S)

are trivial, in the sense that
x=y
or
x=x^{\prime}
. Writing
E(S)
for the number of tuples
(x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime})
solving (
1.1
), a finite set
S
is Sidon if and only if
E(S)\leqslant 2|S|^{2}-|S|
.

One can show that if
S\subset[N]
is Sidon then
|S|\leqslant(1+o(1))N^{1/2}
, and there are constructions with
|S|\geqslant(1-o(1))N^{1/2}
, see
[
6
]
. Conlon, Fox, Sudakov and Zhao
[
2
]
have shown that Sidon sets whose cardinality is within a constant of this range possess arithmetic structure, in that they contain a solution to any translation-invariant linear equation in five variables, with all variables distinct. Furthermore they are able to demonstrate that this structure is also possessed by
almost Sidon
sets, that is sets for which

E(S)\leqslant(2+o(1))|S|^{2}.

Their results are deduced using a regularity lemma for graphs with few 4-cycles. We use a Fourier-analytic transference principle developed by Helfgott and de Roton
[
4
]
to give an alternative proof of this result, generalising to translation-invariant equations in more variables and extracting bounds.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
with
a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=0
and
s\geqslant 5
. Given
0<\delta\leqslant 1
, suppose that
S\subset[N]
satisfies

|S|\geqslant\delta N^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad E(S)\leqslant\left(2+\eta\right)|S|^{2}.

Then either

N\leqslant\exp\exp(O_{a_{i}}(1/\delta)),\quad\text{or}\quad\eta\geqslant\exp\left(-\exp(O_{a_{i}}(1/\delta))\right)

or

(1.2)

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}\prod_{i}1_{S}(x_{i})\geqslant\exp\left(-O_{a_{i}}\left(1/\delta\right)\right)N^{\frac{s}{2}-1}.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
with
a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=0
and
s\geqslant 5
. Given
0<\delta\leqslant 1
, suppose that
S\subset[N]
satisfies

|S|\geqslant\delta N^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad E(S)\leqslant\left(2+\eta\right)|S|^{2}.

If
S
lacks solutions to the equation

(1.3)

a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0

with
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\in S
all distinct, then

N\leqslant\exp\exp(O_{a_{i}}(1/\delta))\quad\text{or}\quad\eta\geqslant\exp\left(-\exp(O_{a_{i}}(1/\delta))\right).

Corollary 1.3
.

If
S\subset[N]
is a Sidon set lacking solutions to (
1.3
) with distinct variables then, for
N\geqslant 3
, we have

(1.4)

|S|=O_{a_{i}}\left(N^{1/2}(\log\log N)^{-1}\right).

That such results are obtainable is noted in
[
2
]
, along with a path to proving them. We depart from the use of weak arithmetic regularity suggested therein. Instead our argument takes advantage of the fact that a Sidon set behaves very nicely with respect to convolution, so that convolving its indicator function with a suitably chosen Bohr set yields a function whose
L^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms are both comparable to that of a dense set of integers (after appropriate renormalisation). Functions whose
L^{p}
-norms behave in this manner are similar enough to dense sets of integers for us to import results from the dense setting to sparse Sidon sets. This observation originates with Helfgott and de Roton
[
4
]
.

An attentive reader will observe that our argument gives a superior exponent of
\log\log N
than that stated in Corollary
1.3
. Furthermore the improved exponent grows as the number of variables in (
1.3
) increases. This is due to our use of a result of Bloom
[
1
]
which counts the number of solutions to a translation-invariant equation in a dense set of integers. For equations in four or more variables, there is a more effective density bound due to Schoen and Sisask
[
7
]
. As is indicated in Sanders’
Mathematical Review

Notation

Standard conventions

We use
[N]
to denote the interval of integers
\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. We use counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}
, so that for
f,g:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\biggl(\sum_{x}|f(x)|^{p}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\ \text{and}\ (f*g)(x):=\sum_{y}f(y)g(x-y).

Any sum of the form
\sum_{x}
is to be interpreted as a sum over
\mathbb{Z}
. The
support
of
f
is the set
\mathrm{supp}(f):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:f(x)\neq 0\right\}
.

We use Haar probability measure on
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, so that for integrable
F:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

\left\|F\right\|_{p}:=\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|F(\alpha)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\alpha\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}|F(\alpha)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\alpha\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\left\|F\right\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}}|F(\alpha)|^{.}

Write
\left\|\alpha\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}
for the distance from
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
to the nearest integer

\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\alpha-n|.

This remains well-defined on
\mathbb{T}
.

Definition 1.4

(Fourier transform)
.

For
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
with finite support define
\hat{f}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\hat{f}(\alpha):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)e(\alpha n).

Here
e(\beta)
stands for
e^{2\pi i\beta}
.

Asymptotic notation

For a complex-valued function
f
and positive-valued function
g
, write
f\lesssim g
or
f=O(g)
if there exists a constant
C
such that
|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for all
x
. We write
f=\Omega(g)
if
f\gtrsim g
. The notation
f\asymp g
means that
f\lesssim g
and
f\gtrsim g
. We subscript these symbols if the implicit constant depends on additional parameters.

We write
f=o(g)
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
X\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
x\geqslant X
we have
|f(x)|\leqslant\varepsilon g(x)
.

Local conventions

As indicated in the introduction, we define the additive energy of a finitely supported function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
to be the quantity

E(f):=\sum_{x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}}f(x)f(x^{\prime})f(y)f(y^{\prime}).

When
f=1_{S}
is the characteristic function of a finite set
S\subset\mathbb{Z}
we write
E(S)
. Notice that

E(S)=\sum_{n}r_{S}(n)^{2}

where

r_{S}(n):=\sum_{n_{1}-n_{2}=n}1_{S}(n_{1})1_{S}(n_{2})

is the number of representation of
n
as a difference of elements of
S
. In the literature this notation is sometimes used for the number of representations as a
sum
of two elements of
S
.

2.
The transference argument

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
assuming the following three ingredients.

Lemma 2.1

(
L^{2}
–Bloom)
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
with
s\geqslant 5
and
a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=0
. Let
f:I\to[0,\infty)
be a function defined on an interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
of length
N
. If
\sum_{n}f(n)\geqslant\delta N
and
\sum_{n}f(n)^{2}\leqslant N
then we have the lower bound

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}f(x_{1})\dotsm f(x_{s})\geqslant\exp\left(-O_{a_{i}}\left(1/\delta\right)\right)N^{s-1}.

We deduce this from a theorem of Bloom
[
1
]
in §
3
.

Lemma 2.2

(Counting lemma for bounded energy functions)
.

Let
s\geqslant 5
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Let
\nu:I\to[0,\infty)
be a function defined on an interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
of length
N
. Suppose that

\sum_{n}\nu(n)\leqslant N\quad\text{and}\quad E(\nu)\leqslant N^{3}.

Then for any
|f_{i}|\leqslant\nu
we have

\left|\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}f_{1}(x_{1})\dotsm f_{s}(x_{s})\right|\leqslant N^{s-1}\frac{\min_{i}\big\|\hat{f}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}}{\big\|\hat{1}_{[N]}\big\|_{\infty}}.

This is proved in §
4
.

Lemma 2.3

(Dense model for almost-Sidon sets)
.

Let
0\leqslant\eta\leqslant 1
and suppose that
S\subset[N]
satisfies

|S|\geqslant\delta N^{1/2}\quad\text{and}\quad E(S)\leqslant\left(2+\eta\right)|S|^{2}.

Then for any
0<\varepsilon\leqslant\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{2},\delta\right\}
there exists
f:(-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]\to[0,\infty)
such that all of the following hold

•

\displaystyle\sum_{n}f(n)=N^{1/2}|S|
;

•

\displaystyle\big\|\hat{f}-N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}\big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon N
;

•

\displaystyle\sum_{n}f(n)^{2}\leqslant N\left[1+\left(\eta+N^{-1/2}\right)(1-\eta)^{-1}\exp\left(\varepsilon^{-O(1)}\right)\right]
.

The above constitutes the main idea in our approach and is proved in §
5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We may assume that
\eta\leqslant 1/2
, for the second possible conclusion of the theorem is that
\eta
is large. Let us apply Lemma
2.3
, with
\varepsilon
to be chosen. This gives
f:(-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
\sum_{n}f(n)\geqslant\delta N
,
\big\|\hat{f}-N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}\big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon N
and

(2.1)

\sum_{n}f(n)^{2}\leqslant N\left[1+\left(\eta+N^{-1/2}\right)\exp\left(\varepsilon^{-O(1)}\right)\right].

By (
2.1
), either
\sum_{n}f(n)^{2}\leqslant 2N
or one of the following two possibilities holds

(2.2)

\eta\geqslant\exp\left(-\varepsilon^{-O(1)}\right)\quad\text{or}\quad N\leqslant\exp\left(\varepsilon^{-O(1)}\right).

Notice that
(-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]
is an interval of length at most
2N
.
Hence, assuming that neither option in (
2.2
) holds, Lemma
2.1
gives that

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}f(x_{1})\dotsm f(x_{s})\geqslant\exp\left(-O_{a_{i}}\left(1/\delta\right)\right)N^{s-1}.

Define
\nu:=f+N^{1/2}1_{S}
. We claim that, provided we divide through by a suitable absolute constant, the function
\nu
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
2.2
on the interval
I=(-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]
. By the triangle inequality in
L^{4}
, and the Fourier-analytic interpretation of energy, we have

E(\nu)^{1/4}=\left\|\hat{\nu}\right\|_{4}\leqslant\big\|\hat{f}\big\|_{4}+N^{1/2}\left\|\hat{1}_{S}\right\|_{4}\leqslant\left\|f\right\|_{1}^{1/2}\big\|\hat{f}\big\|^{1/2}_{2}+N^{1/2}E(S)^{1/4}\\
\lesssim N^{1/4}\left\|f\right\|_{2}+N^{1/2}N^{1/4}\lesssim N^{3/4}.

Assuming that neither option in (
2.2
) holds, we compare Fourier coefficients at zero to deduce that

\sum_{n}\nu(n)=\hat{f}(0)+N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}(0)\leqslant 2\hat{f}(0)+\varepsilon N\\
\leqslant 2(2N)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{n}f(n)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}+\varepsilon N\lesssim N.

We may therefore apply Lemma
2.2
together with a telescoping identity to deduce that

\left|\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}\left(\prod_{i}f(x_{i})-\prod_{i}N^{1/2}1_{S}(x_{i})\right)\right|\lesssim_{s}\varepsilon N^{s-1}.

Hence either we deduce (
1.2
), or one of the following holds

•

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geqslant\exp\left(-O_{a_{i}}\left(1/\delta\right)\right)
;

•

\displaystyle\eta\geqslant\exp\left(-\varepsilon^{-O(1)}\right)
;

•

\displaystyle N\leqslant\exp\left(\varepsilon^{-O(1)}\right)
.

We obtain the result on taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small to preclude the first possibility.
∎

3.
Results on dense sets of integers

Theorem 3.1

(Bloom
[
1
]
)
.

Let
a_{1},\dots,a_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
with
s\geqslant 5
and
a_{1}+\dots+a_{s}=0
. Then for any
A\subset[N]
with
|A|\geqslant\delta N
we have the lower bound

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}1_{A}(x_{1})\dotsm 1_{A}(x_{s})\geqslant\exp\left(-O_{a_{i},\varepsilon}\left(\delta^{-\frac{1}{s-2}-\varepsilon}\right)\right)N^{s-1}.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Translating, we may assume that
I=[N]
. Define

A:=\left\{x\in[N]:f(x)\geqslant\delta/2\right\}.

Then, employing the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

(3.1)

\begin{split}\delta N\leqslant\sum_{x}f(x)&=\sum_{x\notin A}f(x)+\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\\
&\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\delta N+|A|^{1/2}\left(\sum_{x}f(x)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\delta N+\left(|A|N\right)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Therefore

(3.2)

|A|\geqslant\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{4}N.

Applying Theorem
3.1
we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}f(x_{1})\dotsm f(x_{s})

\displaystyle\geqslant(\delta/4)^{s}\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}1_{A}(x_{1})\dotsm 1_{A}(x_{s})

\displaystyle\gtrsim\delta^{s}\exp\left(-O_{a_{i}}\left(\delta^{-0.8}\right)\right)N^{s-1}.\qed

4.
An almost-Sidon counting lemma

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

For any finitely supported
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{f}(a\alpha)\bigr|^{s-1}\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant\Bigl(\sum_{n}|f(n)|\Bigr)^{s-5}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{f}(a\alpha)\bigr|^{4}\mathrm{d}\alpha.

If
|f|\leqslant\nu
, then

\sum_{n}|f(n)|\leqslant\sum_{n}\nu(n)\leqslant N.

By orthogonality

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{f}(a\alpha)\bigr|^{4}\mathrm{d}\alpha=\sum_{x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}}f(x)\overline{f(x^{\prime})f(y)}f(y^{\prime})\\
\leqslant\sum_{x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}}\nu(x)\nu(x^{\prime})\nu(y)\nu(y^{\prime})\leqslant N^{3}.

Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{f}(a\alpha)\bigr|^{s-1}\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant N^{s-2}.

Again by orthogonality, together with Hölder’s inequality

\left|\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}f_{1}(x_{1})\dotsm f_{s}(x_{s})\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{f}_{1}(a_{1}\alpha)\dotsm\hat{f}_{s}(a_{s}\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha\right|\\
\leqslant\big\|\hat{f}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}\prod_{j\neq i}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{f}(a\alpha)\bigr|^{s-1}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{s-1}}\leqslant\big\|\hat{f}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}N^{s-2}.\qed

5.
A modelling lemma for almost-Sidon sets

We begin our proof of Lemma
2.3
with two subsidiary results on almost Sidon sets.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
S\subset[N]
satisfy

E(S):=\sum_{x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}}1_{S}(x)1_{S}(x^{\prime})1_{S}(y)1_{S}(y^{\prime})\leqslant(2+\eta)|S|^{2}.

Then, on writing

r_{S}(n):=\sum_{n_{1}-n_{2}=n}1_{S}(n_{1})1_{S}(n_{2}),

we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{S}(n)>1\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{S}(n)\leqslant\eta|S|^{2}+|S|.

Proof.

We observe that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{S}(n)>1\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{S}(n)\leqslant\sum_{n\neq 0}r_{S}(n)(r_{S}(n)-1)=\sum_{n\neq 0}r_{S}(n)^{2}-\sum_{n\neq 0}r_{S}(n)\\
\leqslant(1+\eta)|S|^{2}-\left(|S|^{2}-|S|\right)=\eta|S|^{2}+|S|.\qed

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\eta\in[0,1)
and suppose that
S\subset[N]
satisfies

E(S):=\sum_{x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}}1_{S}(x)1_{S}(x^{\prime})1_{S}(y)1_{S}(y^{\prime})\leqslant(2+\eta)|S|^{2}.

Then

|S|\leqslant 2\left(\tfrac{N}{1-\eta}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
5.1
we have

|S|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{S}(n)\leqslant 1\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{S}(n)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{S}(n)>1\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{S}(n)+|S|\leqslant 2N+\eta|S|^{2}+2|S|.\qed

We are now in a position to prove Lemma
2.3
in earnest.

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Define the large spectrum of
S
to be the set

\mathrm{Spec}(S,\varepsilon):=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}:|\hat{1}_{S}(\alpha)|\geqslant\varepsilon|S|\right\}.

Define the Bohr set

(5.1)

B:=\left\{n\in[-\varepsilon N,\varepsilon N]:\left\|n\alpha\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leqslant\varepsilon\quad\forall\alpha\in\mathrm{Spec}(S,\varepsilon)\right\}.

Write
\mu_{B}
for the normalised characteristic function of
B
, so that

\mu_{B}:=|B|^{-1}1_{B}.

Then we define

(5.2)

f:=N^{1/2}1_{S}*\mu_{B},

where, for finitely supported
f_{i}
, we set

f_{1}*f_{2}(n):=\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}=n}f_{1}(m_{1})f_{2}(m_{2}).

It is straightforward to check that
f
is supported on
(-\varepsilon N,(1+\varepsilon)N]
and that
\sum_{n}f(n)=N^{1/2}|S|
. Let us next estimate
|N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}-\hat{f}|
. The key identity is

\widehat{f_{1}*f_{2}}=\hat{f}_{1}\hat{f}_{2},

so that
|N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}-\hat{f}|=N^{1/2}|\hat{1}_{S}||1-\hat{\mu}_{B}|
.

If
\alpha\notin\mathrm{Spec}(S,\varepsilon)
then we have

|N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}(\alpha)-\hat{f}(\alpha)|=N^{1/2}|\hat{1}_{S}(\alpha)||1-\hat{\mu}_{B}(\alpha)|\leqslant 2N^{1/2}\varepsilon|S|.

If
\alpha\in\mathrm{Spec}(S,\varepsilon)
, then for each
n\in B
we have

e(\alpha n)=1+O(\varepsilon).

Hence

\hat{\mu}_{B}(\alpha)=1+O(\varepsilon),

and consequently

|N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}(\alpha)-\hat{f}(\alpha)|=N^{1/2}|\hat{1}_{S}(\alpha)||1-\hat{\mu}_{B}(\alpha)|\lesssim N^{1/2}|S|\varepsilon.

Combining both cases and Lemma
5.2
gives

(5.3)

\big\|N^{1/2}\hat{1}_{S}-\hat{f}\big\|_{\infty}\lesssim\varepsilon N.

We have

(5.4)

\sum_{n}f(n)^{2}=N|B|^{-2}\sum_{n_{1}-n_{2}=m_{1}-m_{2}}1_{S}(n_{1})1_{S}(n_{2})1_{B}(m_{1})1_{B}(m_{2}).

Write

r_{S}(n):=\sum_{n_{1}-n_{2}=n}1_{S}(n_{1})1_{S}(n_{2}).

Then by Lemma
5.1
, the inner sum in (
5.4
) is

\sum_{n}r_{S}(n)r_{B}(n)\leqslant|B|^{2}+|B|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{S}(n)>1\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{S}(n)+|B||S|\\
\leqslant|B|^{2}+\left(\eta|S|^{2}+2|S|\right)|B|.

Using the estimate
|S|\lesssim(1-\eta)^{-1/2}N^{1/2}
afforded by Lemma
5.2
, it remains to establish the lower bound

(5.5)

|B|\geqslant\exp\left(-\varepsilon^{O(1)}\right)N.

Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{R}
be a maximal
(1/N)
-separated subset of
\mathrm{Spec}(S,\varepsilon)
. Since every element of
\mathrm{Spec}(S,\varepsilon)
is within
1/N
of some
\alpha_{i}
, one can check that

(5.6)

B\supset\left\{n\in[-\varepsilon N/2,\varepsilon N/2]:\left\|\alpha_{i}n\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}\leqslant\varepsilon/2\quad\forall i=1,\dots,R\right\}.

Hence the argument proving the standard lower bound for Bohr sets (e.g.
[
8
, Lemma 4.2]
) gives

|B|\geqslant\left\lceil 4/\varepsilon\right\rceil^{1+R}N.

By the large sieve inequality (e.g.
[
10
, Lemma 5.3]
) we have

R\varepsilon^{4}|S|^{4}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{R}\bigl|\hat{1}_{S}(\alpha_{i})\bigr|^{4}\lesssim N\sum_{n}r_{S}(n)^{2}\lesssim N\left(2+\eta\right)|S|^{2}.

Hence
R\lesssim\delta^{-2}\varepsilon^{-4}
. ∎

6.
Proof of corollaries

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Let us first obtain an upper bound for the number of solutions in
S
to the equation

a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0.

By our hypotheses, all such solutions should have
x_{i}=x_{j}
for some
i\neq j
. At the cost of a factor of
\binom{s}{2}
, we may assume that
x_{s-1}=x_{s}
. Writing
n:=a_{4}x_{4}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}
, the number of choices for the remaining three variables is at most

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}=-n}1_{S}(x_{1})1_{S}(x_{2})1_{S}(x_{3})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e(\alpha n)\prod_{i=1}^{3}\hat{1}_{S}(a_{i}x_{i})\mathrm{d}\alpha\\
\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{1}_{S}(a_{i}\alpha_{i})\bigr|^{3}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{3}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigl|\hat{1}_{S}(a_{i}\alpha_{i})\bigr|^{4}\mathrm{d}\alpha\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}=E(S)^{\frac{3}{4}}.

We may assume that
\eta\leqslant 1/2
(otherwise we are done). Using Lemma
5.2
we deduce that the number of choices for
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}

O(N^{3/4})

\sum_{a_{1}x_{1}+\dots+a_{s}x_{s}=0}\prod_{i}1_{S}(x_{i})\lesssim_{s}N^{\frac{s}{2}-\frac{5}{4}}.

We deduce the result on comparing this with the lower bound given in Theorem
1.1

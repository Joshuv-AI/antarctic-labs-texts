\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.1}|\{n\in\mathcal{B}(x):P(n)\leq y\}|=\frac{1}{\theta}\,\Psi(\theta x,y)\quad(1+o(1))

Theorem 1
.

Let
\theta>1
be an irrational number of finite type and
\psi\geq 0
. Then (

) holds uniformly for

(\log x)^{3+\varepsilon}\leq y\leq x.

We recall that an irrational number
\theta
is said to be of finite type if (

\|m\theta\|\geq\frac{c}{m^{\kappa}}\quad(m\in\mathbb{N})

Theorem
1
depends on an estimate for the exponential sum

n\in\,\mathfrak{S}(y)\end{subarray}}e(n\theta).

Akbal
[

]
uses the estimate of Foury and Tenenbaum

Γ \Gamma -convergence analysis of the nonlinear self-energy induced by edge dislocations in semi-discrete and discrete models in two dimensions
R. Alicandro, L. De Luca, M. Palombaro, M. Ponsiglione

Abstract
. We propose nonlinear semi-discrete and discrete models for the elastic energy induced by a finite system of edge dislocations in two dimensions. Within the dilute regime , we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the nonlinear elastic energy, as the core-radius (in the semi-discrete model) and the lattice spacing (in the purely discrete one) vanish. Our analysis passes through a linearization procedure within the rigorous framework of Γ \Gamma -convergence. Keywords: Dislocations, Nonlinear elasticity, Plasticity, Discrete-to-continuum limits, Gamma-convergence MSC2020: 74C05, 58K45, 70G75, 74G65, 49J45



Introduction

We introduce some variational nonlinear models for the elastic energy induced by a finite system of edge dislocations in two dimensions, proposing and analyzing a purely discrete model settled in the regular triangular lattice, together with (and relying on) a variant of the core radius approach in
[
25
]
.

We start by describing our variational discrete model in absence of defects.
We assume to have a finite portion of the regular triangular lattice, with lattice spacing
\varepsilon
(eventually vanishing), occupying a reference configuration
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this framework, deformations are described through
discrete strain fields
\beta
which are defined on the nearest neighbor bonds of the lattice. In the defect-free case, these strains are in fact discrete gradients of some implicitly defined (by integration) deformation function defined on the nodes.
The underlying compatibility condition is that the discrete circulation of
\beta
around each triangular cell is trivial.
In the present formulation
the discrete energy is thus a function of the deformation strain
\beta
and is given by the sum of two contributions: a two-body interaction potential accounting for the elongation of the bonds, and a three-body interaction term penalizing changes of area of the triangular cells. In the thermodynamic limit (as the lattice spacing vanishes), this model gives back nowadays classical continuous nonlinear models, where the energy density behaves like the squared distance of the strain from the set of rotations; in the small strain limit, one gets, by linearization, the classical isotropic (continuous) linearized elasticity (we refer to
[
8
,
26
,
5
]
for discrete-to-continuum and linearization results in the context of short and long-range interaction energies).
Remarkably, by tuning the pre-factors in front of the two contributions of the nonlinear discrete energy, the
simultaneous discrete-to-continuum limit and linearization process yield in the limit functional all possible Lamé coefficients (see formula (
2.6
)).

In this framework, edge dislocations can be introduced as topological singularities of the discrete strain field
\beta
, namely, on each cell
\varepsilon T
, we enforce the circulation of
\beta
to belong to (a rotation of) the
\varepsilon
-spaced triangular lattice.
If non-zero, such a vector represents the so-called
Burgers vector

\varepsilon\xi
, which detects and quantifies the presence of an edge dislocation in the triangle
\varepsilon T
. We thus identify such a dislocation with the weighted Dirac mass
\varepsilon\xi\delta_{x_{\varepsilon T}}
, where
x_{\varepsilon T}
denotes the barycenter of the triangle
\varepsilon T
.
A finite distribution of edge dislocations, centered at the points
x^{n}
and having Burgers vectors
\varepsilon\xi^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, can be then identified
with the empirical measure
\mu=\varepsilon\sum_{n}\xi^{n}\delta_{x^{n}}
.
For any given configuration of dislocations
\mu
, the class of admissible strains is given by those discrete strain fields whose discrete circulation around each triangle
\varepsilon T
is given by
\mu(\varepsilon T)
.
The presence of edge dislocations enforces some distortion on any admissible strain: since pure rotations become incompatible, a resulting stored elastic energy is induced.
For a single dislocation, such an energy is of order
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
.
Our analysis is performed in a dilute regime of edge dislocations, where the total stored energy is bounded by
C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, which is consistent with the presence of a finite number of dislocations.
This energy regime has been widely analyzed in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence,
both for continuous models, also referred to as
semi-discrete
models, (see
[
15
,
13
]
for the linear case and
[
25
]
for a nonlinear one) and for
discrete linear energies
[
4
]
. (For analogous results in the linear scalar framework of screw dislocations and of vortices in superconductivity we refer the interested reader to
[
24
,
20
,
1
,
2
,
3
,
12
]
).

As the lattice spacing vanishes, the discrepancy between discrete and continuous models should also vanish; similarly, since we are dealing with a vanishing energy regime of order
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, also the energy gap between nonlinear and linearized models should vanish. The purpose of this paper is to rigorously prove these facts, by proposing a purely discrete nonlinear model (the first to our knowledge) and
building a bridge from discrete to continuous and from nonlinear to linear edge dislocation models.
For this purpose, we first introduce and analyze a slight variant of the (nonlinear) continuous model studied in
[
25
]
, which will be instrumental to the discrete problem.
Then we introduce our purely discrete nonlinear model, inspired from the linear discrete models in
[
4
,
16
,
6
]
, and we show that it behaves like the continuous one, as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The nonlinear continuous model we consider is based on the so-called
core-radius approach
:
Given a configuration of dislocations
\mu=\varepsilon\sum_{n}\xi^{n}\delta_{x^{n}}
, we drill an
\varepsilon
-disc around each dislocation
x^{n}
, and set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{n}\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(x^{n})
.
Here,
\varepsilon
is (proportional to) the lattice spacing and the discs
B_{\varepsilon}(x^{n})
represent the so-called
cores
of the dislocations
x^{n}
, where plastic effects take place. Removing the cores means to neglect such plastic effects, whose energy contribution is expected to be a lower order perturbation with respect to the elastic energy computed outside the cores, namely, in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
.
The admissible strains, in analogy with the discrete setting, are those fields
\beta
defined on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
whose circulation around each
B_{\varepsilon}(x^{n})
is equal to
\varepsilon\xi^{n}
. The corresponding elastic energy of
\beta
(and, in turn, induced by
\mu
) behaves like the integral on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
of the squared distance of
\beta
from the set of rotations
\mathrm{SO}(2)
.

Now we describe the further kinematic condition that we impose on the admissible strains. To this end,
assume for the time being that the distribution of dislocations is given by a single dislocation
\varepsilon\xi^{0}\delta_{x^{0}}
, with
x^{0}\in\Omega
and
\xi^{0}
belonging to the
R^{0}
-rotation of the regular triangular lattice. In such a case, the rigidity estimate
[
14
]
together with the energetic bound, guarantees that
\beta
is close to a constant rotation
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
plus an
\varepsilon
-multiple of a linear strain
\beta^{\mathrm{lin}}
satisfying the circulation condition
\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x^{0})}\beta^{\mathrm{lin}}t\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\xi^{0}
.
In general, the rotation
R
around which the linearization takes place and the rotation
R^{0}
associated to the Burgers vector are decoupled.
However, if
R\neq R^{0}
the Burgers vector is in general not consistent with the underlying rigidly rotated lattice, providing then a physically unacceptable configuration.
This suggests to incorporate in the model some extra compatibility condition between the Burgers vectors and the admissible strains in order to prevent such unphysical configurations.
In this respect which options are best suited
is in fact still questionable, and a canonical choice seems to be missing in the present literature.
The main reason is that, in contrast with purely linear models, here we are trying to combine the linear circulation condition, which is built upon the additive decomposition of the linear strain,
together with the nonlinear energetic framework.
In this respect, it would be preferable to adopt a multiplicative decomposition of the strain in a plastic, and an elastic part
[
21
,
22
]
, but at the present it is not clear to us how to implement it in a purely discrete framework.
Moreover, while in the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
we obtain a fixed rotation, for positive
\varepsilon
nonlinear deformations make unavailable a clear notion of local orientation for the deformed lattice.
In order to overcome this lack of rigidity, in the present formulation we impose that, in the annulus of radii
\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon^{\gamma}
(with
\gamma\in(0,1)
) around each dislocation point, the average of the admissible strain fields coincides with the rotation associated to the corresponding Burgers vector.
For positive
\varepsilon
, rigidity arguments show that such a consistency between the orientation of the Burgers vector and the underlying lattice holds, not only in average, but almost pointwise.
In fact, such an average condition guarantees, in the compactness result, that the limit constant rotation
R
of the admissible fields
\beta_{\varepsilon}
is also the limit of each of the rotations associated with each Burgers vector.
Such a condition leads us to
work under the
well separation
assumption, namely, to restrict the class of admissible empirical measures to that for which the distance of two dislocations is larger than (a multiple of)
\varepsilon^{\gamma}
.

Within the framework described above, we prove that a given sequence
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
of admissible configurations of edge dislocations, satisfying the energy bound
C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
, has (once scaled by
\varepsilon
) uniformly bounded mass; therefore, up to subsequences,
\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}

converges (in the weak star topology) to a finite sum
\mu=\sum_{k}\xi^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-weighted Dirac deltas. Furthermore, a sequence of admissible strains
\{\beta_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
compatible with
\{\mu_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
, converges, up to subsequences, to a constant rotation
R
that is compatible with
\mu
, i.e., the weights
\xi^{k}
lie in the
R
-rotated regular triangular lattice. Eventually, we show that
\beta_{\varepsilon}\sim R+\varepsilon\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}
, for some far-field linear strain
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}
, which is curl-free. This is the content of Theorem
1.1
. The corresponding
\Gamma
-convergence result, Theorem
1.7
, shows that the
\Gamma
-limit of the energy functional with respect to the convergence described above, is given by the sum of a self-energy plus the linearized (around
R
) elastic energy of the limit field
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}
. The self-energy for such a nonlinear model takes the form of
\sum_{k}\varphi(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})
, where
\varphi
is a positively
1
-homogeneous function that is obtained by a cell-formula through a relaxation procedure accounting for the underlying lattice structure.
We highlight that such a density
\varphi
depends only on the corresponding linearized elasticity tensor and on the lattice structure and hence it coincides with the one computed in linear models
[
15
]
.
Loosely speaking, our result shows that the nonlinear elastic energy outside of the cores in the energy regime
\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|
can be linearized thus obtaining the same
\Gamma
-limit as in the corresponding linear model.

Some comments are in order.
The proof of our result follows the lines of that in
[
25
]
, where the authors focus on a finite system of fixed (i.e., independent of
\varepsilon
) edge dislocations, whose Burgers vectors lie in the unrotated lattice.
As mentioned above, our varying measures satisfy a uniform bound on the total variation; therefore,
from the point of view of compactness properties, our case does not differ much from the case of a fixed system of singularities.
Nevertheless, the situation slightly changes when looking at the
\Gamma
-limit of the elastic energy. Indeed, whereas for a fixed measure no relaxation takes place in the computation of the self-energy, in our model two or more different singularities for positive
\varepsilon
may converge to the same limiting singularity, thus lowering the value of the self-energy. This means that our
\Gamma
-limit could be smaller than that computed in
[
25
]
.
Furthermore, we stress that the average condition on the strain fields described above is not present in
[
25
]
, where a possible discrepancy
between the (limit) rotation around which the linearization is performed and the rotation (that is actually the identity matrix) of the lattice where the Burgers vectors lie, may occur.
However, as explained in
[
19
]
(see also
[
10
]
), such a gap is not unphysical if one interprets
\Omega
as the deformed configuration instead of the reference one. Having in mind such an interpretation, an admissible strain field
\beta
can be locally understood as an inverse deformation gradient, so that its circulation turns out to be a vector of the reference lattice (without any rotation).
In fact, while it is quite accepted that the Burgers circuit should be drawn in the deformed configuration, the correct notion of Burgers vector is rather questionable. The one corresponding to the circulation of the (local) inverse of the deformation gradient on a closed path in the deformed configuration is usually referred to as the
true
Burgers vector
[
17
, formula (1-5)]
and, as already explained, it has the advantage to have a topological nature, being quantized on the reference lattice, without being effected by any sort of small elastic fluctuations.
On the other hand, the so-called
local
Burgers vector, defined as the circulation of the deformation gradient on a closed path in the reference configuration, besides being very popular, has a direct constitutive relation with the deformation field.
Our proposal of notion of Burgers vector represents a kind of compromise between the two, being defined on the reference configuration but belonging to a rotation of the lattice.
In fact, such a procedure is borrowed from
linear models
[
6
,
9
,
15
,
13
]
, where the
(local) Burgers vector
is (somehow tacitly
[
17
, page 21]
) assumed to belong to the (unrotated) reference lattice.
Adopting the notion of true Burgers vector within a purely discrete model seems to be a challenging task; in this respect, it would be interesting (also in the linear framework) to
introduce discrete models defined directly on the deformed configuration, combining the discrete formalism of
[
18
]
with the analysis in
[
10
]
.

Summarizing, our model is meant to be a first attempt to combine sound and efficient techniques available in the linear framework together with fundamental discrete nonlinear models.
Specifically, it relies on the presence of a reference configuration, it is implicitly based on an additive decomposition of the strain, although the energetic setting is nonlinear.
The resulting modeling choice has a flavor of compromise: we have shown that canonical arguments in linear elasticity theories of dislocations can be pushed to and exploited in a nonlinear framework as well, at the price of some extra care needed
to relieve the conflict generated by such an unusual combination.

Our contribution represents a first attempt to rigorously analyze in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence a purely discrete nonlinear model for edge dislocations in two dimensions.

1.
The semi-discrete model

In this section we introduce and analyze the nonlinear semi-discrete model for the elastic energy induced by a family of edge dislocations.

1.1.
Description of the problem

Here we introduce the main notations that will be used throughout the paper.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded, open, and simply connected set with Lipschitz continuous boundary.
Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and set
\mathbb{S}:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}S
. Here
\mathbb{S}
represents a reference underlying lattice.
We set
\Pi(\mathbb{S}):=\bigcup_{R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)}R\mathbb{S}
and
\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S}):=\{R\in\mathrm{SO}(2):R\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{S}\}
. Let
0<\gamma<1
.
Denoting by
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
the class of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-valued Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
(which is a parameter tuning the lattice spacing) we define the class of admissible edge dislocation measures
X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\Omega)
as

(1.1)

\displaystyle X^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega):=

\displaystyle\Big\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\,:\,\mu=\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N}\xi^{n}\delta_{x^{n}}\,,\,N\in\mathbb{N},\,x^{n}\in\Omega,\,\xi^{n}\in\Pi(\mathbb{S})\,,

\displaystyle|x^{n_{1}}-x^{n_{2}}|\geq 4\varepsilon^{\gamma}\textrm{ whenever }n_{1}\neq n_{2},

\displaystyle\textrm{dist}(x^{n},\partial\Omega)\geq 2\varepsilon^{\gamma}\textrm{ for all }n=1,\ldots,N\Big\}\,.

Moreover, for every
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
, we set

\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega):=\Big\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\,:\,\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\xi^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\,,\,K\in\mathbb{N},\,x^{k}\in\Omega,\,\xi^{k}\in R\mathbb{S}\Big\}\,.

For every
0<\varepsilon<1
and for every
\mu=\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N}R^{n}b^{n}\delta_{x^{n}}\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\Omega)
, with
b^{n}\in\mathbb{S}
and
R^{n}\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
, we set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(x^{n})
and
we define the class of
admissible strains
associated to
\mu
as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{AS}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=\Big\{

\displaystyle\beta\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})\,:\,\beta\equiv 0\textrm{ in }\bigcup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}B_{\varepsilon}(x)\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta=0\textrm{ in }\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu),\quad\fint_{A_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{n})}\beta\,\mathrm{d}x\in R^{n}\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S}),

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x^{n})}\beta t\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\varepsilon R^{n}b^{n}\,\textrm{for every }n=1,\ldots,N\Big\}\,,

where, here and below, the operator
\mathrm{Curl}\;\!
acts row-wise and is understood in the distributional sense,
t
denotes the tangent vector field to
\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x^{n})
and the integrand
\beta t
is intended in
the sense of traces.
Notice that the weight of any admissible singularity cannot be uniquely decomposed into a product
Rb
with
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
and
b\in\mathbb{S}
(as an example, if
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
then
e_{1}
can be written also as the clockwise
\frac{\pi}{2}
-rotation of
e_{2}
); this is why the condition on the average of
\beta
in (
1.2
) is written in terms of an inclusion (into
R^{n}\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S})
) rather than an identity.

We now introduce the nonlinear energy density.
Let
W\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2};[0,+\infty))
satisfy the following assumptions:

(i)

W(\mathrm{Id})=0
;

(ii)

W(RM)=W(M)
for every
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\,R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
;

(iii)

W(MR)=W(M)
for every
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},\,R\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S})
;

(iv)

\displaystyle\textrm{there exists a constant $c>0$ such that:
$W(M)\geq c\,\textrm{dist}^{2}(M,\mathrm{SO}(2))$ for every $M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}$}\,;

(v)

\displaystyle\textrm{$W\in C^{2}$ in a neighborhood of $\mathrm{SO}(2)$}.

Condition (
i
) states that the reference configuration is stress-free and condition (
ii
) is the so-called
frame indifference
.
Condition (
iii
) says that the energy density
W
is invariant with respect to the symmetry group of rotations of the underlying lattice (while, if assumed for all rotations, would be an isotropy condition).
Condition (
iv
) is a coercivity assumption that guarantees compactness properties whereas condition (
v
) serves to linearize the energy around the equilibria.
We highlight that, combining conditions (
i
), (
ii
) and (
iv
), the well of
W
is
\mathrm{SO}(2)
.

In view of property (
v
), we can define the
2\times 2\times 2\times 2
linearized elasticity tensor associated to
W
as

(1.3)

\mathbb{C}:=\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial M^{2}}(\mathrm{Id})\,.

For every
0<\varepsilon<1
we define the energy functional
\mathcal{F}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})\to[0,+\infty]
as

(1.4)

\mathcal{F}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\beta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)}W(\beta)\,\mathrm{d}x&\textrm{if }\mu\in X^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\textrm{ and }\beta\in\mathcal{AS}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)\\
+\infty&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

1.2.
Compactness

Here we state and prove the compactness result for the semi-discrete energy in (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{(\mu_{\varepsilon};\beta_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
be such that

(1.5)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathcal{F}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\beta_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\,,

for some constant
C>0
.
Then, up to a subsequence, there exist
\{R_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathrm{SO}(2)
, a rotation
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
, a measure
\mu\in\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega)
, and a field
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
with
\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0
,
such that:

(0)

R_{\varepsilon}\to R
;

(1)

\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu
;

(2)

\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}}\rightharpoonup\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.

Definition 1.2
.

In the following, we write
(\mu_{\varepsilon};\beta_{\varepsilon};R_{\varepsilon})\to(\mu;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}};R)
if conditions (
0
), (
1
), and (
2
) of Theorem
1.1
are satisfied.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
we follow the lines of the proof of
[
25
, Proposition 3.5]
.
The following result provides uniform rigidity estimates
á la
Friesecke-James-Müller
[
14
]
for a suitable class of domains with holes. It can be proven by arguing verbatim as in
[
25
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded, open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary. Then there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
E
such that the following property holds true. Let
r>0
and let
\{x^{1},\dots,x^{N}\}\subset E
be such that
|x^{n_{1}}-x^{n_{2}}|>4r
for every
n_{1},n_{2}=1,\ldots,N
with
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
and
\textrm{dist}(x^{n},\partial E)>2r
for every
n=1,\ldots,N
. Setting
E_{r}:=E\setminus\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}\overline{B}_{r}(x^{n})
, for every
u\in H^{1}(E_{r};\mathbb{R}^{2})
there exists
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
such that

\|\nabla u-R\|_{L^{2}(E_{r};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\leq C\|\textrm{dist}(\nabla u,\mathrm{SO}(2))\|_{L^{2}(E_{r})}\,.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the energetic bound (
1.5
) we have that
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\Omega)
and
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{AS}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
.
For every
0<\varepsilon<1
let
\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}R^{n}_{\varepsilon}b_{\varepsilon}^{n}\delta_{x_{\varepsilon}^{n}}
.
We start by proving the compactness property for the measures
\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}
.
We set
K_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}:=\lfloor(1-\gamma)\frac{|\log\varepsilon|}{\log 2}-1\rfloor
.
Let
n\in\{1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}\}
be fixed. We have that
A_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})\supset\bigcup_{k=0}^{K_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}}A_{2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k+1}\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})
.
As a consequence of
[
14
]
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for every
k=0,1,\ldots,K_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}

(1.6)

\int_{A_{2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k+1}\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})}\textrm{dist}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{SO}(2))\mathrm{d}x\geq C\int_{A_{2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k+1}\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})}|\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

for some rotations
R_{k}
; in fact, (
1.6
) has been proved in
[
14
]
for gradient fields defined on sets with Lipschitz continuous boundary; moreover, the constant
C
is invariant with respect to omotheties of the domain. Actually, in our case
\beta_{\varepsilon}
is not a gradient, but one can still deduce (
1.6
) either covering
A_{2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k+1}\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})
with two overlapping simply connected sets with Lipschitz continuous boundary, and applying on each of them
[
14
]
, or introducing a suitable cut in
A_{2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k+1}\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})
and applying directly
[
14
]
on the resulting simply connected cut annulus.
By summing (
1.6
) over
k
and over
n
, using (
1.5
) and property (
iv
), we deduce that

(1.7)

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\geq

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{k=0}^{K_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}}\int_{A_{2^{k}\varepsilon,2^{k+1}\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})}|\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{k}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{2}|b^{n}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}(K_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}+1)\log 2\,\geq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|(1-\gamma)\Big|\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\Big|(\Omega)\,,

where the last but one inequality follows by applying Jensen inequality and using the very definition of
\mathcal{AS}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
.
By (
1.7
), we deduce that
\big|\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\big|(\Omega)
is uniformly bounded and hence that there exists a measure
\mu
, consisting in a finite sum of vectorial weighted Dirac deltas, such that, up to subsequences, property (
1
) holds.

Now, we pass to the proof of property (
2
). To this end,
we define the maps
\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
as

\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}(x)\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}(R^{n}_{\varepsilon}b^{n}_{\varepsilon})\otimes\mathsf{J}\frac{x-x^{n}_{\varepsilon}}{|x-x_{\varepsilon}^{n}|^{2}}\,,

where
\mathsf{J}
is the counter clockwise
\frac{\pi}{2}
-rotation.
One can easily check that

(1.8)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}|\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{2}N_{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}|b^{n}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\leq C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\,,

where the last inequality follows by (
1.7
).
By construction, we have that

\int_{\partial U}(\beta_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon})t\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=0

for every open set
U\subset\Omega
with
\partial U\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
smooth;
hence, there exists a map
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon});\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\beta_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon}=\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
. By Lemma
1.3
we thus get that there exist a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and a sequence of rotations
\{R_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathrm{SO}(2)
such that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}|\beta_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}|\bar{\beta}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}\textrm{dist}^{2}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{SO}(2))\,\mathrm{d}x+C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}\textrm{dist}^{2}(\beta_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{SO}(2))\,\mathrm{d}x+C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\leq C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\,,

where we have used also (
1.7
), (
1.8
), property (
iv
), and (
1.5
) . By (
1.9
), up to a subsequence,
\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}}\rightharpoonup\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
for some
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
, so that property (
2
) holds true.

Now we prove that
\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0
in the distributional sense. To this end, let
\phi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and let
\{\phi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be a family converging to
\phi
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and such that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\equiv\phi(x^{n}_{\varepsilon})
in
B_{\varepsilon}(x^{n}_{\varepsilon})
for every
n=1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}
. Then, by (
1.7
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}},\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}}\Big\langle\mathrm{Curl}\;\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon},\phi_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}}\Big\langle\mathrm{Curl}\;\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon},\phi_{\varepsilon}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}R^{n}_{\varepsilon}b^{n}_{\varepsilon}\phi(x^{n}_{\varepsilon})}{\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}}=0\,;

by the arbitrariness of
\phi
we have that
\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0
in the distributional sense.

Furthermore, since
\mathrm{SO}(2)
is compact, up to a subsequence
R_{\varepsilon}\to R
for some
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
. In order to conclude the proof of the theorem it remains to show that
\mu\in\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega)
. First,
for every
n=1,\ldots,N_{\varepsilon}
, let
\tilde{R}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S})
be such that
\fint_{A_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x_{\varepsilon}^{n})}\beta_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=R_{\varepsilon}^{n}\tilde{R}_{\varepsilon}^{n}
.
In view of (
1.7
), we can assume, up to a subsequence, that
N_{\varepsilon}\equiv N
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
independent of
\varepsilon
. For every fixed
n=1,\ldots,N
, it holds

(1.10)

|R_{\varepsilon}^{n}\tilde{R}_{\varepsilon}^{n}-R_{\varepsilon}|^{2}=\Big|\fint_{A_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{n}_{\varepsilon})}(\beta_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|^{2}\leq C_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{2(1-\gamma)}|\log\varepsilon|\,,

where the last inequality is a consequence of Jensen inequality and of (
1.9
).
Since
\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S})
is compact, we have that, up to a further subsequence,
\tilde{R}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\to\tilde{R}^{n}
for some
\tilde{R}^{n}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S})
. This fact together with (
1.10
), yields that
R_{\varepsilon}^{n}\to R(\tilde{R}^{n})^{\mathrm{T}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and hence

\mu\in\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega)
.
∎

Remark 1.4
.

In this paper, the assumption that
\Omega
is simply connected is used to guarantee that the class of
L^{2}
curl-free matrix fields on
\Omega
coincides with the class of gradients of functions in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Such an assumption can be easily dropped if we replace, in the compactness result above and in the

\Gamma
-convergence result below, the condition
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
with
\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0
by the condition
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=\nabla u
for some
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Remark 1.5
.

As mentioned in the introduction, the condition on the average of the strain field in (
1.2
) is needed in Theorem
1.1
to guarantee
the compatibility condition that
R
is both the asymptotic rotation around which the linearization is performed and the rotation of the lattice where the weights of the limit singularities lie.
Indeed, dropping such an average condition from the definition of (
1.2
), one could consider
\mu_{\varepsilon}\equiv\varepsilon b\delta_{0}
(with
0\in\Omega
and
b\in\mathbb{S}
) ,
R
an arbitrary rotation in
\mathrm{SO}(2)
and the field
\beta_{\varepsilon}=R+\varepsilon\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{b,\mathbb{C}}
(where
\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
is the Green function for the corresponding linearized problem, see (
1.11
) below); such a field (once dropped the average condition of (
1.2
)) would be an admissible strain. Now, the weight of the singularity lies on the set
\mathbb{S}
, while
\beta_{\varepsilon}
is linearized around the arbitrarily fixed rotation
R
, providing an unphysical lack of rigidity in the class of admissible Burgers vectors.

Furthermore, we stress that such an average condition should be required on “thick annuli” with radii
\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}
and cannot be replaced by a weaker condition on “thin” annuli with radii
\varepsilon,M\varepsilon
for some
M>1
(independent of
\varepsilon
) ,
as shown by the following computation.
Let
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}:[\varepsilon,1]\to[0,1]
be such that
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(t)\equiv 0
in
[\varepsilon,M\varepsilon)
,
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(t)\equiv 1
in
[2M\varepsilon,1]
and
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{t}{M\varepsilon}-1
in
[M\varepsilon,2M\varepsilon)
and let
u_{\varepsilon}:A_{\varepsilon,1}(0)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the function defined by
u_{\varepsilon}(x):=R(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|))x
, where

R(\vartheta):=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\vartheta&\sin\vartheta\\
-\sin\vartheta&\cos\vartheta\end{array}\right)\,.

By direct computations,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)=

\displaystyle R(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|))

\displaystyle+\frac{\dot{\vartheta}_{\varepsilon}(|x|)}{|x|}\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}-\sin\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}^{2}+\cos\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}x_{2}&-\sin\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}x_{2}+\cos\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x^{2}_{2}\\
-\cos\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}^{2}-\sin\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}x_{2}&-\sin\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{2}^{2}-\cos\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(|x|)x_{1}x_{2}\end{array}\right)\,,

whence we deduce that

\int_{A_{\varepsilon,1}(0)}\textrm{dist}^{2}(\nabla u_{\varepsilon},\mathrm{SO}(2))\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{M^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{A_{M\varepsilon,2M\varepsilon}(0)}|x|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq CM^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\,.

In this example, the average of
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
on the annulus
A_{\varepsilon,M\varepsilon}(0)
is the identity matrix
R(0)
, while
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\to R(1)
(for instance in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
).
The computation above shows that the fields
\beta_{\varepsilon}=R(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{b,\mathbb{C}}
(with
b\in\mathbb{S}
), would satisfy the average assumption with
R=R(0)=\mathrm{Id}
in the annulus
A_{\varepsilon,M\varepsilon}(0)
, but converge to the rotation
R(1)
.
Therefore, prescribing the condition on the average of the admissible fields on the annuli of radii
\varepsilon
and
M\varepsilon
(for
M
fixed) does not provide any relationship between the rotation of the lattice where the Burgers vector lies and the rotation around which the energy is linearized.

On the other hand, in order to let our proof (in particular, (
1.10
)) work, one can easily see that the average condition in (
1.2
) can be weakened by requiring that

\textrm{dist}\bigg(\fint_{A_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{n})}\beta\,\mathrm{d}x,R^{n}\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{S})\bigg)\leq\delta_{\varepsilon}\,,

for some
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
with
\delta_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

1.3.
\Gamma
-convergence

This subsection is devoted to the
\Gamma
-convergence result for the semi-discrete energy in (
1.4
).
In order to define the
\Gamma
-limit we first introduce the self-energy of an edge dislocation. Let
\mathbb{C}
be a given elasticity tensor in linear elasticity.
For every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, the corresponding displacement
u^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
and strain
\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
, induced by the edge dislocation
\zeta\delta_{0}
in the whole plane, have been explicitly computed in the literature (see, for instance,
[
7
, formula 4.1.25]
); here, we recall some of their properties we need in our analysis.
The strain field
\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
satisfies the circulation condition

\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta=\zeta\delta_{0}\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}

and the equilibrium equation

\mathrm{Div}\,\mathbb{C}\beta=0\textrm{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\,.

In polar coordinates,
\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
takes the form

(1.11)

\beta^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\rho,\theta):=\frac{1}{\rho}\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)\,,

where
(see
[
15
, Remark 7]
)
\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
is the unique minimizer of
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{C}\Gamma:\Gamma\,\mathrm{d}\theta
among the functions
\Gamma
of the form

\Gamma(\theta):=f(\theta)\otimes(-\sin\theta;\cos\theta)+g\otimes(\cos\theta;\sin\theta)\,,

with
g\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
f\in C^{0}([0,2\pi];\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying
f(0)=f(2\pi)
and
\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\zeta
.
The optimal
g^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
and
f^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
are uniquely determined by the vector
\zeta
and the tensor
\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 1.6
.

We highlight that

(1.12)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=0\,,

and hence, for all
0<r<R
,

\int_{A_{r,R}(0)}\beta_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
.
Indeed, let
\bar{f}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
be the
2\pi
-periodic extension of
f^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}

and consider

\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta):=-\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta+\pi)=\bar{f}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta+\pi)\otimes(-\sin\theta;\cos\theta)+g^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\otimes(\cos\theta;\sin\theta)\,;

Notice that the circulation of
\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
coincides with that of
\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
.
Since, by convexity,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{C}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\Big):\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{C}\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}:\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{C}\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}:\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbb{C}\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}:\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,,

by strict convexity we deduce that

(1.13)

\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\cdot)=\hat{\Gamma}^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\cdot)=-\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\cdot+\pi)\,;

therefore,
\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}
is an odd function and hence (
1.12
) holds true.

For every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
we set

(1.14)

\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\zeta):=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\,\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta):\Gamma^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{1}{|\log r|}\int_{A_{r,1}(0)}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\beta^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}:\beta^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad 0<r<1\,.

By
[
13
, Proposition 1]
(see also
[
15
, Corollary 6]
), we have

(1.15)

\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\zeta)=\lim_{\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\to+\infty}\psi_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{\mathbb{C}}(\zeta)\,,

where

(1.16)

\psi_{r_{1},r_{2}}^{\mathbb{C}}(\zeta):=\frac{1}{\log\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}}\min_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\beta\in L^2(A_{r_1,r_2}(0);\R^{2\times 2})}{\newatop{\Cu\beta=0\textrm{ in }A_{r_1,r_2}(0)}{\int_{\partial B_r(0)}\beta t\,\ud\Huno=\zeta}}}\int_{A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(0)}\mathbb{C}\beta:\beta\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Moreover, for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for every
s\in\mathbb{R}

(1.17)

\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(s\zeta)=s^{2}\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(\zeta)\,.

Finally, for every
b\in\mathbb{S}
we define

(1.18)

\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}(b):=\min\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{I}|z_{i}|\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(b_{i})\,:\,z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\,b_{i}\in\mathbb{S}\,,\,N\in\mathbb{N}\,,\,\sum_{i=1}^{I}z_{i}b_{i}=b\bigg\}

(with
\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}(b)=0
for
b=0
).
We are now in a position to state our
\Gamma
-convergence result for the nonlinear energy
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}
defined in (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.7
.

The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(
\Gamma
-liminf inequality) Let
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
,
\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\xi^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega)
,
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
with
\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0
. For every
\{(\mu_{\varepsilon};\beta_{\varepsilon};R_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})\times\mathrm{SO}(2)
such that
(\mu_{\varepsilon};\beta_{\varepsilon};R_{\varepsilon})\to(\mu;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}};R)
(in the sense of Definition
1.2
), it holds

(1.19)

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}:R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\mathcal{F}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\beta_{\varepsilon})\,.

(ii)

(

\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\xi^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega)

Before giving the proof of Theorem
1.7
we state and prove the following linearization lemma for curl-free matrix fields,
arguing as in the proof of
[
25
, Proposition 3.11]
. We mention that this kind of linearization results in presence of defects generalizes the case of gradient fields originally treated in
[
11
]

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
r>1
.
For every
\delta>0
, let
\zeta_{\delta}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\beta_{\delta}\in L^{2}(A_{1,r}(0);\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
be such that
\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\beta_{\delta}t\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\zeta_{\delta}
and
\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta_{\delta}=0
in
A_{1,r}(0)
. Assume that
{\beta_{\delta}}\to R
in
L^{2}(A_{1,r}(0);\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
and
\frac{\zeta_{\delta}}{\delta}\to\zeta
(as
\delta\to 0
), for some
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
. Then

(1.21)

\liminf_{\delta\to 0}\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}\int_{A_{1,r}(0)}W(\beta_{\delta})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\log r\,\psi_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\zeta)\,.

Proof.

In order to prove (
1.21
) we argue by contradiction.
Assume that there exists a vanishing sequence
\{\delta_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,+\infty)
and a constant
\sigma>0
such that

(1.22)

\frac{1}{\delta_{l}^{2}}\int_{A_{1,r}(0)}W(\beta_{\delta_{l}})\,\mathrm{d}x<\log r\,\psi_{1,r}^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\zeta)-\sigma\,.

By (
1.22
), in view of property (
iv
) and of the Rigidity Estimate in
[
14
]
(see
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
for a specific formulation of such a rigidity result suited for curl-free fields on annuli, as well as Lemma
1.3
above), we get that there exist a constant
C>0
and a sequence of constant rotations
\{\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathrm{SO}(2)
such that

(1.23)

\|\beta_{\delta_{l}}-\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(A_{1,r}(0);\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\leq C\log r\,\psi^{\mathbb{C}}_{1,r}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\zeta)\delta_{l}^{2}\,.

Clearly, by triangular inequality,

\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}\to R
as
l\to+\infty
.
By assumption, the field
\frac{\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}^{\mathrm{T}}\beta_{\delta_{l}}-\mathrm{Id}}{\delta_{l}}

is curl-free, and hence, setting
\mathsf{C}:=\{(x_{1};0)\,:\,1\leq x_{1}\leq r\}
, we have that there exists a sequence
\{v_{\delta_{l}}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}(A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C};\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\int_{A_{1,r}(0)}v_{\delta_{l}}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
and
\frac{\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}^{\mathrm{T}}\beta_{\delta_{l}}-\mathrm{Id}}{\delta_{l}}=\nabla v_{\delta_{l}}
in
A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C}
.
By construction,

(1.24)

[v_{\delta_{l}}]=\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}^{\mathrm{T}}\frac{\zeta_{\delta_{l}}}{\delta_{l}}\qquad\textrm{on }\mathsf{C}\,,

where
[v]
denotes the jump of the function
v
in the sense of traces.
By (
1.23
), we obtain

(1.25)

\int_{A_{1,r}(0)}|\nabla v_{\delta_{l}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\log r\,\psi^{\mathbb{C}}_{1,r}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\zeta)\,,

whence we deduce that, up to a subsequence,
v_{\delta_{l}}\rightharpoonup v
in
H^{1}(A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C};\mathbb{R}^{2})
for some
v\in H^{1}(A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C};\mathbb{R}^{2})
; moreover, by (
1.24
) and recalling that
\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}\to R
, we get

(1.26)

[v]=R^{\mathrm{T}}\zeta\qquad\textrm{on }\mathsf{C}\,.

We are now in a position to linearize the energy around the identity.
To this end, we define the sequence
\{\chi_{\delta_{l}}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
of characteristic functions

\chi_{\delta_{l}}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{if }|\nabla v_{\delta_{l}}|<\delta_{l}^{-\alpha}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise in }A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C}\,,\end{array}\right.

with
0<\alpha<1
arbitrarily fixed.
By (
1.25
) it follows that
\chi_{\delta_{l}}\to 1
in measure and hence
\nabla v_{\delta_{l}}\chi_{\delta_{l}}\rightharpoonup\nabla v
in
L^{2}(A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.
By Taylor expansion,
using (
ii
), we have

(1.27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{l}^{2}}\int_{A_{1,r}(0)}W(\beta_{\delta_{l}})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{l}^{2}}\int_{A_{1,r}(0)}\chi_{\delta_{l}}W(\bar{R}_{\delta_{l}}^{\mathrm{T}}\beta_{\delta_{l}})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{l}^{2}}\int_{A_{1,r}(0)\setminus\mathsf{C}}\chi_{\delta_{l}}W(\mathrm{Id}+\delta_{l}\nabla v_{\delta_{l}})\,\mathrm{d}x

2.
The purely discrete model

In this section we introduce and analyze the nonlinear purely discrete model for the elastic energy induced by a family of edge dislocations.

2.1.
Description of the problem

Here we introduce the main notation that will be used in this section.

The reference lattice.

We set

\nu:=\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}e_{2}
and
\eta:=-\frac{1}{2}e_{1}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}e_{2}
.
Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathrm{span}\,_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},\nu\}
and set

T^{+}:=\mathrm{conv}\{0,e_{1},\nu\}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad T^{-}:=\mathrm{conv}\{0,e_{1},-\eta\}\,,

where, for every
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the set
\mathrm{conv}\{a,b,c\}
denotes the convex envelope of the points
a
,
b
,
c
, i.e., the (closed) triangle with vertices at
a
,
b
,
c
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
we denote by
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
the family of the triangles
T_{\varepsilon}
of the form
i+\varepsilon T^{\pm}
, with
i\in\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
.
Moreover, we set

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega):=\{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}\,:\,T_{\varepsilon}\subset\Omega\}

and
we define

{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}}:=\bigcup_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}T_{\varepsilon}
. Furthermore, we set
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}:={\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
and we denote by
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
the family of nearest neighbor bonds in
{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}}
, i.e.,
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}:=\{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{0}\,:\,|i-j|=\varepsilon\}
.
Trivially,
(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
if and only if
(j,i)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
.

In the following we will generalize the notation introduced above to general subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(not necessarily open). In particular, for every triangle
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}=\{(i,j)\in(T_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T})\times(T_{\varepsilon}\cap\varepsilon\mathbb{T})\,:\,i\neq j\}\,.

For every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}
and for every
map
V:(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define the
discrete circulation
of
V
on the “boundary of
T_{\varepsilon}
” as

\mathrm{d}V(T_{\varepsilon}):=V(i,j)+V(j,k)+V(k,i)\,,

where
(i,j,k)
is a triple of counter-clockwise oriented vertices of
T_{\varepsilon}
.

The admissible strains and the energy functional

For every
\varepsilon>0
we define the class of discrete strains as

\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega):=\{\beta:\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,\beta(i,j)=-\beta(j,i)\textrm{ for any }(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}\}\,.

In the following, for every
\beta\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
, we set

\mu[\beta]:=\sum_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\beta(T_{\varepsilon})\delta_{x_{T_{\varepsilon}}}\,,

where the point
x_{T_{\varepsilon}}
denotes the barycenter of the
\varepsilon
-triangle
T_{\varepsilon}
.
For any
\beta\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
and for any
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with
\mu[\beta](T_{\varepsilon})=0
we
denote by
\tilde{\beta}^{T_{\varepsilon}}
the matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
uniquely defined by the following property

(2.1)

\beta(i,j)=\tilde{\beta}^{T_{\varepsilon}}(j-i)\qquad\textrm{for every }(i,j)\in(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}\,.

Moreover, we define the field
\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
as

(2.2)

\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}:=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{T_\ep\in\T_\ep(\Omega)}{\mu[\beta](T_\ep)=0}}\tilde{\beta}^{T_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{T_{\varepsilon}\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu[\beta])}\,;

notice that every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with
\mu[\beta](T_{\varepsilon})\neq 0
is contained in
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{T_{\varepsilon}})
.

Let

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C(\mathbb{R};[0,+\infty))
be such that
\psi_{1}^{-1}(0)=\psi_{2}^{-1}(0)=\{1\}
,
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C^{2}
in a neighborhood of
1
,
and
\psi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(1)\,,\psi_{2}^{\prime\prime}(1)>0
. Assume moreover that there exist
a,b>0
such that

\psi_{1}(t)\geq at^{2}-b
, for every
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

We define the discrete energy functional
E_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty)
as

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(\beta):=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{|\beta(i,j)|}{\varepsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j),(i,k)\in\Omega_\ep^1}{\langle(j-i)\wedge(k-i),e_3\rangle>0}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}\varepsilon^{2}}\langle\beta(i,j)\wedge\beta(i,k),e_{3}\rangle\Big)\,.

We will consider also localized versions of our energy functional
E_{\varepsilon}
. More specifically, for every open and bounded set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define
E_{\varepsilon}(\cdot;A):\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(A)\to[0,+\infty]
as

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon}(\beta;A):=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in A_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{|\beta(i,j)|}{\varepsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j),(i,k)\in A_\ep^1}{\langle(j-i)\wedge(k-i),e_3\rangle>0}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}\varepsilon^{2}}\langle\beta(i,j)\wedge\beta(i,k),e_{3}\rangle\Big)\,,

so that
E_{\varepsilon}(\beta;\Omega)=E_{\varepsilon}(\beta)
.

Let
0<\gamma<1
be fixed.
We set
S:=\{e_{1},\nu\}
, so that, following the notation in Section
1
,
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{T}
.
We define

X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega):=\Big\{\mu\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\Omega)\,:\,\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\subset\bigcup_{T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\{x_{T_{\varepsilon}}\}\Big\}\,,

where the class
X^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
is defined in (
1.1
).

Moreover, for any
\mu=\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{N}R^{n}b^{n}\delta_{x^{n}}\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
, with
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
R^{n}\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
,
b^{n}\in\mathbb{T}
, and
x^{n}\in\Omega
, we define the class of admissible discrete strains associated to
\mu
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{AS}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\mu):=\Big\{\beta\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)\,:\,\fint_{A_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{n})}\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x\in R^{n}\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{T}),\quad\mu[\beta]=\mu\Big\}\,,

where
\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}
is the map defined in (
2.2
) and
\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{T})
is the group of rotations generated by the
\frac{\pi}{3}
clockwise rotation
R(\frac{\pi}{3})
.

For every
0<\varepsilon<1

we define the discrete energy
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})\times\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
as

\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\beta):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}E_{\varepsilon}(\beta)&\textrm{if }\mu\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)\textrm{ and }\beta\in\mathcal{AS}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\mu)\,,\\
+\infty&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Before stating and prove our
\Gamma
-convergence result for the functional
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}
, we first derive the continuous nonlinear elastic energy density associated
to the discrete functional
E_{\varepsilon}
as well as the corresponding linearized elasticity tensor.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\beta\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
;
for every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
we define

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\varepsilon}(\beta;T_{\varepsilon}):=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in(T_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}^{1}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{|\beta(i,j)|}{\varepsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j),(i,k)\in(T_\ep)_\ep^1}{\langle(j-i)\wedge(k-i),e_3\rangle>0}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}\varepsilon^{2}}\langle\beta(i,j)\wedge\beta(i,k),e_{3}\rangle\Big)\,.

Note that, for every

(2.3)

E_{\varepsilon}(\beta;A)=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{T_\ep\in\T_\ep}{T_\ep\subset A_{\T_\ep}}}\tilde{E}_{\varepsilon}(\beta;T_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{(i,j)\in A_\ep^1}{i,j\in\partial A_{\mathcal{T}_\ep}}}\varepsilon^{2}\psi_{1}\Big(\frac{|\beta(i,j)|}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,.

Moreover, for any
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with

where

is the matrix defined in (

The assumptions on

easily yield that the map

)-(

it holds

(2.10)

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}:R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\beta_{\varepsilon})\,.

(iii)

(
\Gamma
-limsup inequality) Let
R\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
,
\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\xi^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in\overline{X}^{R}(\Omega)
and
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
with
\mathrm{Curl}\;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=0
.
Then, there exists
\{(\mu_{\varepsilon};\beta_{\varepsilon};R_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
, with

\mu_{\varepsilon}\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
,
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
and
R_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{SO}(2)
(for every
\varepsilon>0
),
such that
(\mu_{\varepsilon};\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon};R_{\varepsilon})\to(\mu;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}};R)
as
\varepsilon\to 0

and

(2.11)

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}:R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\beta_{\varepsilon})\,.

Proof.

We start by proving the compactness property (i).
In view of (
2.9
), the maps
\hat{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}
provided by Lemma
2.2
belong to
\mathcal{AS}^{\gamma}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
and satisfy

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(\mu_{\varepsilon},\hat{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|\,,

where the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}
is defined in (
1.4
) for the choice of
W
in (
2.5
).
Therefore, the conclusion follows by Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
2.2
(i), once noticed that
\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}})}\leq C\varepsilon
.

Now we pass to the proof of (ii). Without loss of generality we may assume that (
2.9
) is satisfied.
In view of (
1
) we have that
|\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)\leq C
for some
C
independent of
\varepsilon
, so that,
up to passing to a subsequence,
|\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\bar{\mu}
for some measure
\bar{\mu}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with finite support contained in
\Omega
.
Let
A\subset\subset\Omega
be an open set with Lipschitz continuous boundary such that
\mathrm{supp}\,\bar{\mu}\subset A
.
By construction,
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
we have that

\mu_{\varepsilon}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits A\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(A)
and
\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits A\in\mathcal{AS}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}(A)
.
Then (
2.10
) follows by (
2.8
), Theorem
1.7
(i) applied with
\Omega=A
, and by the arbitrariness of
A
.

Finally, we prove (iii). By standard density arguments in
\Gamma
-convergence we can assume that
\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})=\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})
for every
k=1,\ldots,K
and that
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
. Let furthermore
u^{\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}}\in W^{2,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
be such that
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}=\nabla u^{\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}}
.

For every
k=1,\ldots,K
let

x^{k,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{argmin}\{|x^{k}-x_{T_{\varepsilon}}|\,:\,T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\}

and set
\mu_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon\sum_{k=1}^{K}\xi^{k}\delta_{x^{k,\varepsilon}}
. Then, for
\varepsilon
small enough,
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in X_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, we set
R_{\varepsilon}\equiv R
.
In order to construct
\beta_{\varepsilon}
, we proceed as in the proof of Theorem
1.7
(ii). We first construct
u_{\varepsilon}
as in (
1.45
), replacing
x^{k}
with
x^{k,\varepsilon}
and suitably modifying the radii of the annuli.

More precisely, for every
k=1,\ldots,K
we define the map
u_{\varepsilon}^{R,k}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\mathsf{C}^{k,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2})
as

u_{\varepsilon}^{R,k}(x):=u^{R,R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k}}(x-x^{k,\varepsilon})+\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}u^{\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})\,,

with
\mathsf{C}^{k,\varepsilon}:=x^{k,\varepsilon}+\mathsf{C}
(with
\mathsf{C}:=\{(x_{1};0)\,:\,x_{1}\geq 0\}
)
and
u^{R,\zeta}
satisfying (
1.41
).
Therefore,
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}^{R,k}(\cdot)=R\beta^{R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k},\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\cdot-x^{k,\varepsilon})
and
[u^{R,k}_{\varepsilon}]=\xi^{k}
on
\mathsf{C}^{k,\varepsilon}
(locally in the sense of traces).

We define the map
u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{far}}:\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{far}}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{K}u^{R,R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k}}(x-x^{k,\varepsilon})+\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}u^{\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}}(x)\,.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}([0,3];[0,1])
be such that
\phi\equiv 1
in
[0,1]
and
\phi\equiv 0
in
[2,3]
. We define the function
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}(\mu_{\varepsilon})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{\varepsilon}^{R,k}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in A_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon}(x^{k,\varepsilon})\textrm{ for some }k\,,\\
\phi\big(\frac{|x-x^{k,\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon}\big)u_{\varepsilon}^{R,k}(x)+\big(1-\phi\big(\frac{|x-x^{k,\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon}\big)\big)u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{far}}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in A_{\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon,2(\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon)}(x^{k,\varepsilon})\textrm{ for some }k\,,\\
u_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{far}}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in\Omega_{2(\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon)}(\mu)\,.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, for every
k=1,\ldots,K
we define the slip variable
\sigma^{k,\varepsilon}:\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}\to\varepsilon\mathbb{T}
as

\sigma^{k,\varepsilon}(i,j):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\xi^{k}&\textrm{if }\mathsf{C}^{k,\varepsilon}\cap[i,j]\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }\langle i-x^{k,\varepsilon},e_{2}\rangle<0\\
+\xi^{k}&\textrm{if }\mathsf{C}^{k,\varepsilon}\cap[i,j]\neq\emptyset\textrm{ and }\langle i-x^{k,\varepsilon},e_{2}\rangle>0\\
0&\textrm{elsewhere},\end{array}\right.

and we set
\sigma_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma^{k,\varepsilon}
.
Finally, we define the map
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{discr}}(\Omega)
as

\beta_{\varepsilon}(i,j):=R(j-i)+\varepsilon(u_{\varepsilon}(j)-u_{\varepsilon}(i)-\sigma_{\varepsilon}(i,j))\,.

We claim that
\{(\mu_{\varepsilon};\beta_{\varepsilon};R_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
is a recovery sequence. By construction,
\mu_{\varepsilon}=\mu[\beta_{\varepsilon}]
and,
by Remark
1.6
(in particular, by (
1.13
)), we have also that
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{AS}_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma,\mathrm{discr}}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
for every
\varepsilon>0
.
Moreover, properties (
0
) and (
1
) are trivially satisfied.
We show that this is the case also for (
2
).
By the very definition of
\beta^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
in (
1.11
), there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}

(2.12)

|\beta^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\rho,\theta)|\leq\frac{C}{\rho}|\zeta|^{2}\,,\qquad|\nabla\beta^{\zeta,\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\rho,\theta)|\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{2}}|\zeta|^{2}\,.

Therefore, recalling that
\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
, for every
T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with
T_{\varepsilon}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}A_{\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon}(x^{k,\varepsilon})\cup\Omega_{2(\varepsilon^{\gamma}+\varepsilon)}(\mu_{\varepsilon})
and for every
x\in T_{\varepsilon}
it holds

(2.13)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{T_{\varepsilon}}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)\Big|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(T_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\nabla\beta^{R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k},\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(T_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}+|\log\varepsilon|\|\nabla\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(T_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{|x-x^{k,\varepsilon}|^{4}}+|\log\varepsilon|\Big)\,,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.
Furthermore, by arguing verbatim as in the proof of (
1.48
), we have that, for every
k=1,\ldots,K
,

(2.14)

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}\big(A_{\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{2},3\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\big)}+\Big\|\frac{\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}\big(A_{\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{2},3\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\big)}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}\,.

Therefore, by (
2.13
)-(
2.14
) and the fact that
|\Omega\setminus{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}}|\leq C\varepsilon
, we readily see that

(2.15)

\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\Big\|\frac{\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}^{2}\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, by arguing verbatim as in (
1.47
), (
1.49
) and (
1.50
), we have

(2.16)

\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\chi_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}}{\sqrt{|\log\varepsilon|}}\rightharpoonup\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\qquad\textrm{in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})\,.

Therefore, by (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), we deduce that
(\mu_{\varepsilon};\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}};R_{\varepsilon})\to(\mu;\beta^{\mathrm{cf}};R)
.

Next we show that also (
2.11
) holds true.
By construction we have

(2.17)

\tilde{E}_{\varepsilon}(\beta_{\varepsilon};T_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\qquad\textrm{for every }T_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\textrm{ with }\min_{k=1,\ldots,K}\mathrm{dist}(x^{k,\varepsilon},T_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon\,,

and that for every
(i,j)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{1}
with
i,j\in\partial{\Omega_{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}}

\varepsilon^{2}\psi\Big(\frac{|\beta_{\varepsilon}(i,j)|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\,.

Moreover, by (
2.14
) and (
1.52
), for every
k=1,\ldots,K
, we have

(2.19)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{2},3\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}W(\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\Big\|\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}-R_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}\big(A_{\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma}}{2},3\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon});\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\big)}\leq\frac{C}{|\log\varepsilon|}\,.

Let
\alpha\in(\gamma,1)
.
By arguing as in the proof of (
1.51
) and (
1.53
), one can show that

(2.20)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{{\varepsilon},\varepsilon^{\alpha}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}W(\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x=C(1-\alpha)\,,

for each

.
Moreover, since
\|\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}-R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}
in

, by (

writing the Taylor expansion of

we find

(2.21)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}W(\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\Big(R^{\mathrm{T}}\frac{\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}-R}{\varepsilon}\Big):\Big(R^{\mathrm{T}}\frac{\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}-R}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}\frac{\sigma(\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}-R)}{|\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}-R|^{2}}\Big|\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}-R}{\varepsilon}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,

with

. By (

\Big|\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}-R}{\varepsilon}\Big|\leq\frac{C}{|x-x^{k,\varepsilon}|}
for every

, whence we deduce that the last
\limsup
in (

) is equal to zero. Therefore, by (
2.21

(2.22)

\displaystyle\limsup\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}W(\tilde{\beta}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(x^{k,\varepsilon})}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\big(R^{\mathrm{T}}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big):\big(R^{\mathrm{T}}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)+C\varepsilon^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{|x-x^{k,\varepsilon}|^{4}}+|\log\varepsilon|\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{A_{\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(0)}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{C}\beta^{R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k},\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}:\beta^{R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k},\mathbb{C}}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha-\gamma)\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})\,,

where the last equality follows by (
1.14

Furthermore, similar arguments yield

(2.23)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega_{2\varepsilon^{\gamma}}(\mu_{\varepsilon})}W(\tilde{\beta}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon}})\,\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\gamma\sum_{k=1}^{K}\psi^{\mathbb{C}}(R^{\mathrm{T}}\xi^{k})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}:R^{\mathrm{T}}\beta^{\mathrm{cf}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

Finally, combining (
2.3
), (
2.17
)-(
2.20
), (
2.22
)-(
2.23

2.11
).
∎

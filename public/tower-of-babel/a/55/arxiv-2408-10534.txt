Proportion of p-adic Polynomials Which Are Irreducible
Isaac Rajagopal

Abstract
We attempt to quantify the exact proportion of monic p p -adic polynomials of degree n n which are irreducible. We find an exact answer to this when n n is prime and p ≠ n p\neq n , and also when n = 4 n=4 and p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 . Our answers are rational functions in p p . This relates to previous work done to find exact proportions of p p -adic polynomials of degree n n which have k k roots.



1
Introduction

The
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
are created by taking the set of all infinite series
a_{0}+a_{1}p+a_{2}p^{2}+\cdots
, with addition and multiplication working like normal power series. These series converge if we use the
p
-adic valuation, where
|x|=p^{-v_{p}(x)}
, with
v_{p}(x)
being the largest integer
k
such that
p^{k}
divides
x
. The
p
-adic numbers were invented by Kurt Hensel, who found a way to prove that many polynomial equations over the
p
-adic numbers have a solution, called Hensel’s Lemma. Solving equations over the
p
-adic numbers can relate to determining whether a diophantine equation has a rational solution, using the local-global principle (
[
5
]
).

Proportions of
p
-adic numbers are well defined using the Haar measure; for example
1/p
of all
p
-adic integers are congruent to 1 modulo
p
. This can be extended to polynomials with
p
-adic coefficients. We aim to quantify the exact proportion of monic
p
-adic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
of degree
n
which are irreducible (
[
6
, §2.3]
). Problems like this have been studied in many other papers, where an invariant of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
is created, and mathematicians try to find the proportion of these polynomials with a specific value of this invariant. Our invariant is the reducibility of a monic polynomial.

In 2004, an exact formula for the proportion of
p
-adic polynomials of degree
n
which split into linear factors over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
was found (
[
2
]
). In 2022, this was generalized by finding a formula for the proportion of
p
-adic polynomials of degree
n
which have a specific number of roots in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and showed that it is a rational function in
p
(
[
1
]
). When
n\leq 3
, this is the same as our problem, but the two problems differ once
n\geq 4
because a polynomial can factor over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
without any linear terms. As recently as December 2023, authors have studied the proportion of polynomials
f(x)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
with specific splitting types. Their results imply that the proportion of not necessarily monic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which are irreducible must be a rational function in
p
, which does not change by replacing
p
with
1/p
(
[
4
]
).

All of these papers use much more advanced techniques than ours. With only elementary techniques, we solve for the proportion of degree
n
irreducible polynomials in the case where
n
is prime or
n=4
. We work by counting irreducible polynomials mod
p
,
p^{2}
,
p^{3}
, … using various properties of
p
-adic polynomials, such as Hensel’s Lemma and Newton Polygons. At each step
p^{k}
for
k>1
, these polynomials do not have unique factorization. Much of our work for quartics is based on counting the different factorizations of the same polynomials. In the limit as the exponent goes to infinity, we get
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
, which itself has unique factorization using Gauss’ Lemma. (
[
5
]
)

In Section
2
, we discuss some general results which will be useful to us throughout this paper. This includes Theorem
1
, which approximates the proportion of irreducibles to be close to
1/n
for any degree
n
. Then, we find exact values of this proportion for any prime value of the degree
n
in Section
3
. However, this problem seems to become much more complicated for composite
n
. The bulk of the paper is Section
4
, where we find an exact value of this proportion for
n=4
, as long as
p\neq 2
.

2
Preliminary Results

Consider monic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
of degree
n
. These can be represented as elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
, with a polynomial
x^{n}+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}x+c_{0}
represented as
(c_{n-1},...,c_{1},c_{0})
. We use the Haar measure to quantify proportions of these polynomials: for example the proportion of polynomials with constant term divisible by
p
is
\frac{1}{p}
. This set can be seen as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}\times B(0,\frac{1}{p})
, where
B(0,\frac{1}{p})
is the open ball in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
of radius
\frac{1}{p}
centered at 0, which has Haar measure
1\times\frac{1}{p}
. The proportion of polynomials with all terms divisible by
p
is
\frac{1}{p^{n}}
. With the terms of a polynomial being written in descending order, this set can be seen as
B(0,\frac{1}{p})^{n}
, which has Haar measure
\frac{1}{p^{n}}
. (
[
6
]
§2.3)

We define these polynomials to be irreducible if they cannot be written as the product of two nonconstant polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
. By Gauss’ Lemma, this is equivalent to not being able to write them as a product of two nonconstant polynomials in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
. If a monic polynomial
f(x)
(mod
p^{i}
) is irreducible, then all polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which are congruent to
f(x)
(mod
p^{i}
) are irreducible. Let this polynomial, written in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
be
(c_{n-1},...,c_{1},c_{0})
. Then the open ball
\prod_{j=1}^{n}B(c_{n-j},p^{-i})
, which is all polynomials congruent to
f(x)
(mod
p^{i}
), is an open set of measure
p^{-in}
which consists solely of irreducible polynomials. We now introduce Hensel’s Lemma, our main tool for showing polynomials to be reducible, and then use it to approximate the proportion of irreducible monic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
.

Hensel’s Lemma
: There are two forms of this, from (
[
5
]
). A degree
n
polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
is said to be Hensel if either (1) or (2) holds. A polynomial
g(x)
, in
\mathbb{Z}/(p^{k}\mathbb{Z})[x]
is said to be Hensel if all polynomials
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
congruent to
g(x)\pmod{p^{k}}
are Hensel.

(1)

If there is a value
\alpha
(in any extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with degree less than
n
) for which
|f(\alpha)|<|f^{\prime}(\alpha)|^{2}
, then
f(x)
splits into distinct factors in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
.

(2)

If a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
splits into two factors which are relatively prime, when viewed (mod
p
), then all polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which are congruent to
f(x)
(mod
p
) are reducible.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(n,p)=1
, and let the proportion of monic degree

, as

Proof 2.1
.

This formula for the number of irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]

Lemma 2.2
.

The homeomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]

Proof 2.3
.

This map clearly preserves irreducibility. This map preserves the size of open balls in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}

n

is a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}/(p^{k})[x]

\phi(f(x))=f^{\prime}(x)

\mathbb{Z}/(p^{k})[x]

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]

\phi(g(x))=g^{\prime}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]

Lemma 2.8
.

Let the proportion (Haar measure in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}
) of monic

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which are irreducible be

. Let us assume that

is well defined. Let
S
be the set of
p

such that their Newton Polygon is fully above the line from
(0,n)
to
(n,0)
. Then
I
is equal to the proportion of monic polynomials which are irreducible in the set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}\setminus S
.

Proof 2.9
.

Notice that
S
are exactly the polynomials with their
i^{th}
term
c_{i}
divisible by
p^{n-i}
, or having valuation
v_{p}(c_{i})\geq n-i
. This means that
S
is exactly the polynomials
f(x)
where
g(x)=\frac{f(px)}{p^{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
. This is because the map
f\mapsto f(px)/p^{n}
will divide each coefficient
c_{i}
by
p^{n-i}
, and these coefficients must finish with valuation
\geq 0
.

Let
P
be the set of irreducible polynomials in
S
. Let
P^{\prime}
be the set of irreducible polynomials outside
S
. Then
I=(\mu(P)+\mu(P^{\prime}))/(\mu(S)+\mu(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}\setminus S))
. It suffices to show that
\mu(P)/\mu(S)=I
, since this would imply that
I=\mu(P^{\prime})/\mu(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}\setminus S)
, which is what we want.

Consider the map
\phi:S\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}
defined by
\phi(f(x))=f(px)/p^{n}
. The map
\phi
has an inverse
\phi^{-1}(g(x))=p^{n}g(x/p)
. So
\phi
is a bijection because of the existence of an inverse. By the definition of
S
,
\phi(f(x))\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
. We will now show that
\phi
preserves irreducibility. Using Gauss’ Lemma, we look at irreducibility in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
. Let
g(x)=g_{1}(x)g_{2}(x)
. Then
\phi^{-1}(g(x))=p^{n}g(x/p)=p^{n}g_{1}(x/p)g_{2}(x/p)
. Let
f(x)=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x)
. Then
\phi(f(x))=p^{-n}f(px)=p^{-n}f_{1}(px)f_{2}(px)
. So
\phi
preserves irreducibility.

Now we will show that
\phi
preserves the measures of open balls proportionally. By looking at the requirement for each coefficient,
\mu(S)
consists of all polynomials with
i^{th}
coefficient
c_{i}
divisible by
p^{n-i}
. Within
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}
,
\mu(S)=(p^{-2})(p^{-3})\cdots(p^{-n})=p^{-\frac{n(n+1)-2}{2}}
. Now we will see how the size of an open set changes under
\phi
, using
\phi^{-1}
. Consider the product of open balls in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}
written as

O=\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}B(y_{i},p^{-r_{i}}).

This is a basis for all open sets in the product topology on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1}
. Using the Haar measure,
\mu(O)=p^{-k}
, where
k=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}r_{i}
. Going coefficient by coefficient, we can see how
\phi^{-1}
acts on
O
, multiplying
c_{i}
by
p^{n-i}
. This gives

\phi^{-1}(O)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}B(p^{n-i}y_{i},p^{-r_{i}-(n-i)}).

Using the Haar measure,
\mu(\phi^{-1}(O))=p^{-j}
, where
j=\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}(r_{i}+n-i)=k+\frac{n(n+1)-2}{2}
. So
\mu(\phi^{-1}(O))/\mu(S)=p^{-k}=\mu(O)/\mu(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n-1})
. This means that
\phi
preserves the proportional measures of each open ball. Since all open sets can be constructed as a union of open balls,
\phi
preserves the proportional measure of all open sets. Since
\phi
preserves irreducibility, this gives that
\mu(P)/\mu(S)=I
, which implies what we want.

Prime Degree Polynomials

Theorem 2
.

Let the proportion of polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
of degree
r
with
r
prime,
r\neq p
which are irreducible be
I
. Then

I=\frac{(p^{r}-p)(1-p^{-(r+1)/2})+r(p-1)(1-p^{-(r+1)(r-1)/2})}{rp^{r}(1-p^{-r(r+1)/2+1})(1-p^{-(r+1)/2})}.

Proof 3.1
.

Using Corollary
2.4
, we will only need to consider polynomials
g(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
with no
x^{r-1}
term, and we will find which are irreducible. We will start by considering the mod
p
reduction of
g(x)
into
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. We will show that we only need to consider polynomials which are either irreducible when reduced to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, or congruent to
x^{r}
when reduced to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. In other words, we need to show that the only reducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
which can be the reduction of an irreducible
g(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
is
x^{r}
.

All of the reducible polynomials
f(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
satisfy form 2 of Hensel’s Lemma by having relatively prime polynomial factors in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, unless they only have one irreducible factor
\pi(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. In this case,
f(x)=\pi(x)^{e}
, with
e>1
and
\deg\pi(x)=d
. Since
de=r
with
r
prime,
d=1
. Without loss of generality, let
\pi(x)
be monic, so
\pi(x)=x+a
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,
f(x)=(x+a)^{r}
. The
x^{r-1}
term of this gives that
ra\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Since
(p,r)=1
,
a\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. So, the only case where (2) in Hensel’s Lemma does not apply is when
f(x)=x^{r}
. In all other cases, any polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which reduces to
f(x)
is reducible by Hensel’s Lemma.

First, consider polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which reduce to irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
. Consider the monic polynomials
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
with no
x^{r-1}
term. By Lemma
2.6
,
\frac{1}{pr}(p^{r}-p)
of these are irreducible. Each of these creates an irreducible ball of size
p^{-r+1}
by only considering terms after
x^{r-1}
. This gives a total measure within
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{r-1}
of
\frac{p^{r}-p}{rp^{r}}
, of irreducible polynomials which reduce to irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
.

Now, consider polynomials
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
which reduce to
x^{r}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
. We look at the Newton polygons of these polynomials. Each Newton polygon will start at
(0,e)
, for
e>0
being the valuation of the constant term, and end at
(r,0)
. By Lemma
2.8
, we only need to consider all polynomials with Newton polygon below the line from
(0,r)
to
(r,0)
at some point. Any irreducible polynomial with Newton polygon below this line at some point must have its whole Newton polygon below this line, since the Newton polygon can only have one slope, and it ends at
(r,0)
. Therefore, we consider all irreducible polynomials with
0<e<r
.

The Newton polygons of these polynomials must have only one slope, since they are irreducible. They start at
(0,e)
, and end at at
(r,0)
. This line has no integer points between its endpoints, so there can never be any ambiguous cases. The set of polynomials with Newton polygon having one slope from
(0,e)
to
(r,0)
is all polynomials with
x^{i}
term having valuation above
e-ie/r
for
0\leq i\leq r-2
and constant term with valuation exactly
e
. The measure of the set of polynomials with this Newton polygon is
((p-1)p^{-e-1})p^{-\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is the number of integer points
(i,j)
with
0\leq j\leq e-ie/r
, with
1\leq i\leq r-2
. This is because each point below this line multiplies the measure of this set by
p^{-1}
, except when
i=0
, where the measure of all of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with valuation exactly
e
is
(p-1)p^{-e-1}
.

Pick’s Theorem says that
re/2=J+\frac{r+e+1}{2}-1
, where
J
is the number of interior lattice points of the triangle with corners at
(0,0)
,
(0,e)
,
(r,0)
. So

. Combining these with the points with

and

, we get that
\alpha=\frac{re-r-e+1}{2}+r-2
. So the measure of the set of polynomials with Newton polygon equal to

Proof A.3
.

Let us introduce a root

x^{2}+p\alpha x+p\beta
, so
x^{2}+p\alpha x+p\beta=(x-y)(x-\overline{y})
. Using norms of
p
-adic extensions,
v_{p}(y)=v_{p}(\overline{y})=v_{p}(p\beta)/2=1/2
. So
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[y]
is a ramified extension of degree 2. Then
f(x)=(x-y)(x-\overline{y})(x^{2}-p\alpha x+p\gamma)
. We now calculate
f^{\prime}(y)
.

Using the product rule,
f^{\prime}(y)=(y-\overline{y})(y^{2}-p\alpha y+p\gamma)=(y-\overline{y})(-2p\alpha y+p(\gamma-\beta))
.
y+\overline{y}=-p\alpha
, so
y-\overline{y}=2y+p\alpha
. Since
v_{p}(y)=0.5
, and
p\neq 2
we get
v_{p}(y-\overline{y})=0.5
. Then
v_{p}(f^{\prime}(y))=1.5+v_{p}(-2\alpha y+(\gamma-\beta))
. In Case 1,
v_{p}(\gamma-\beta)=k
, and
v_{p}(-2\alpha y)\geq k+0.5
, so
v_{p}(f^{\prime}(y))=k+1.5
and
|f^{\prime}(y)|=p^{-k-1.5}
. In Case 2,
v_{p}(-2\alpha y)=k+0.5
, and
v_{p}(\gamma-\beta)\geq k+1
, so
v_{p}(f^{\prime}(y))=k+2
and
|f^{\prime}(y)|=p^{-k-2}
.

All polynomials
g(x)
congruent to
f(x)\pmod{p^{2k+4}}
will satisfy
p^{2k+4}\mid g(y)
. So
|g(y)|\leq p^{-(2k+4)}
. In Case 1, all of these polynomials satisfy the inequality of Hensel’s Lemma:
|g(y)|<|g^{\prime}(y)|^{2}
. In Case 2, these do not satisfy the inequality. Similarly, all polynomials
g(x)
congruent to
f(x)\pmod{p^{2k+5}}
will satisfy
p^{2k+5}\mid g(y)
. So
|g(y)|\leq p^{-(2k+5)}
. In Case 2, all of these polynomials satisfy Hensel’s Lemma.

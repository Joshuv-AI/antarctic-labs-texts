Homologically area-minimizing surfaces mod v v have at worst codimension 2 singular sets asymptotically
Zhenhua Liu

Abstract
. In [ 9 ] , De Lellis and coauthors have proved a sharp regularity theorem for area-minimizing currents in finite coefficient homology. They prove that area-minimizing mod v v currents are smooth outside of a singular set of codimension at least 1 . 1. Classical examples like triple junctions demonstrate that their result is sharp. Surprisingly, even though their regularity theorem cannot be improved for any fixed v v , if one instead fixes the homology class, then v v asymptotically one can always achieve more regularity. For any integral homology class [ Σ ] [\Sigma] on any Riemannian manifold, we show that for v v large, any area-minimizing mod v v current in [ Σ mod v ] [\Sigma\mod v] must be an integral current, thus having a singular set of codimension at least 2 2 in general and of codimension at least 7 7 in the hypersurface case. Similar results are obtained for Plateau problems in Euclidean space. Our work is inspired by Morgan’s work [ 44 ] and based on De Lellis’ and coauthors’ work [ 9 ] .

1.1.
Sketch of proof

Consider any mod
v
area-minimizer in
[\Sigma].
Take any of its representing lifts to rectifiable currents, denoted by

The key observation is that the monotonicity formula gives a priori density upper bounds on mod
v

v/2
for

(1)

the boundary of

is supported in the singular set,

(2)

the codimension

(3)

as per Theorem 1.6 of
[
20
]
, the top strata singular points have codimension at least two.

These observations, combined with Almgren’s stratification of singular sets, imply that the boundary of the rectifiable lift,
T
, has at least codimension
2
. By classical properties of integral flat chains, this means

must be an integral cycle.

A more quantitative approach to these arguments proves Theorem

and
1.3
. Corollary
1
follows directly from these results. For the Plateau problems in Euclidean space (Theorem

), the method is analogous. Theorem

]
) and Yongsheng Zhang’s constructions (

]
[

]
), while Theorem

2.2.
Algebraic topology

The main reference is
[
32
]
. For a compact manifold

H_{d}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated abelian group. We say a class is torsion if some multiple of it is zero, and call a class non-torsion otherwise.

We fix a decomposition of the homology group as follows:

•

{[\Sigma_{1}],\cdots,[\Sigma_{b}],[\tau_{1}],\cdots,[\tau_{a}]}

•

all
[\Sigma_{j}]
are non-torsion and all
[\tau_{j}]
are torsion,

•

N=\max_{j}\deg[\tau_{j}],
where the degree is the least positive integer which multiplies the torsion class to zero.

The reader should be familiar with the universal coefficient theorem, which will remain fundamental to all of our discussions. The natural map from integral to mod
v

Integral currents and flat chains

In this manuscript, we need several notions of currents. For a comprehensive introduction, the standard references are

2.5.

The comass of a

2.6.
Representing homology classes by submanifolds

In this section, we will collect several facts about representing homology classes using submanifolds.

Lemma 2.1
.

For

there exists a connected embedded
d
-dimensional non-orientable submanifold

in
\mathbb{R}^{d+c}.

Proof.

It is well known that
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
embeds into the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{4},
e.g.,
[

. Consider the following map

\displaystyle u(x_{1},\cdots,x_{4},y_{1},\cdots,y_{d-1})=((4+x_{1})y_{1},\cdots,(4+x_{1})y_{d-1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}).

Straightforward calculation shows that

Thus,

We are done.
∎

2.8.

In this subsection, we will collect several facts about the minimal mass (Definition

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

For

with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert^{\infty}=\sum_{j}|a_{j}|\left\lVert v_{j}\right\rVert.

It is straightforward to verify that

is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v
is a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Thus, the lemma is equivalent to

By continuity of

and the compactness of
\left\lVert v\right\rVert=1

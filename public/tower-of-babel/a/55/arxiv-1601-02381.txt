Smoothing cones over K3 surfaces
Stephen Coughlan, Taro Sano

Abstract
We prove that the affine cone over a general primitively polarised K3 surface of genus g g is smoothable if and only if g ≤ 10 g\leq 10 or g = 12 g=12 . We also give several examples of singularities with special behaviour, such as surfaces whose affine cone is smoothable even though the projective cone is not.



Keywords.
Deformations; affine cones; K3 surfaces; Fano 3-folds

14B07; 14J28

1.
Introduction

-1.0.

In this paper, we study deformations and smoothability of the affine cone over a polarized manifold.
See
2
for basic notions.

The cone over a normal elliptic curve is smoothable if and only if the curve has degree
\leq 9

[
16
]
, the cone over a projectively normal abelian variety of dimension
\geq 2
is never smoothable
[
19
]
, and the cone over a curve of genus
\geq 2
embedded in degree at least
4g-3
is not smoothable, if the curve is not hyperelliptic, trigonal, or a plane quintic cf.
[
22
, §15]
and references therein. Aside from the case of elliptic curves, in all of the above situations, the only deformations are again cones.

The cone over a K3 surface is a natural 3-dimensional generalisation of the cone over an elliptic curve; it is a normal, Gorenstein, isolated log canonical singularity. One of the main results of this present work is the following theorem.

Let
S
be a general K3 surface with primitive polarisation of genus
g
. Then the affine cone over
S
is smoothable if and only if
g\leq 10
or
g=12
. Indeed, if
g=11
or
g\geq 13
then the only deformations of the affine cone over
S
are conical.

A deformation of the affine cone is called
conical
when the conclusion of Proposition
5
(ii) holds.
Cones over non-general K3 surfaces of genus
g=11
or
g>12
may still be smoothable (see §
4
).

-1.2.

The theorem is proved in Section
3
. The “only if” part follows from the vanishing of all graded parts of
T^{1}
of the affine cone which have non-zero degree.
It is proved by using a deep theorem of Beauville
[
3
]
and its slight modification (see
3
). A weaker result can be derived from Green’s conjecture, but with a precise condition on the polarization (see
3
). The “if” part is proved by sweeping out the cone because for
g\leq 10
and
g=12
, the projective cone over
S
deforms to a smooth Fano 3-fold, (see
3
).

-1.3.

Pinkham
[
17
]
gave an example of a
0
-dimensional variety whose affine cone is smoothable, even though the projective cone is not. The cone is Cohen–Macaulay but not Gorenstein or normal. In section 2, we prove the following:

There exists a smooth, projectively normal surface
S
such that the affine cone over
S
is smoothable but the projective cone is not.

The example is a particular surface of general type in its canonical model. We do not know of any example where
S
is a K3 surface. In light of Pinkham’s theorem on elliptic curves and Theorem
1
above, we ask:

If
S
is a K3 surface, is the affine cone over
S
smoothable if and only if the
projective cone is
smoothable?

By
[
4
,
5
]
, the projective cone over a general K3 surface of Picard rank 1 is smoothable if and only if
g\leq 10
or
g=12
.

-1.5.

We also construct K3 surfaces whose affine cone has several smoothing components:

There exist primitively polarised K3 surfaces
(S,L)
of genus
7
, such that
C_{a}(S,L)
has at least two topologically distinct smoothings.

The proof is in §
4
, along with an analysis of cones over imprimitively embedded K3 surfaces, and cones over special K3 surfaces of large genus.

-1.7.

Given a very ample line bundle
L
on a smooth projective variety
V
which induces a projectively normal embedding
V\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{N}
,
we have the “classical” projective cone
C_{p}(V)\subset\mathbb P^{N+1}
and affine cone
C_{a}(V)\subset\mathbb A^{N+1}
.
Pinkham (
[
16
, Theorem 5.1]
) showed that, if the eigenspace
T^{1}_{C_{a}(V)}(k)=0
for
k>0
, then the restriction homomorphism

\Hilb_{C_{p}(V)\subset\mathbb P^{N+1}}\rightarrow\Def_{C_{a}(V)}
is formally smooth, where
\Hilb_{C_{p}(V)\subset\mathbb P^{N+1}}
is the Hilbert functor and
\Def_{C_{a}(V)}

is the usual deformation functor. Moreover, Schlessinger (
[
19
, §4.3]
) showed that, if
T^{1}_{C_{a}(V)}(k)=0
for
k\neq 0
,
then we can define a morphism
\Hilb_{V\subset\mathbb P^{N}}\rightarrow\Def_{C_{a}(V)}
and it is formally smooth.

In Section
5
, we generalise these results to the case where
L
is only assumed to be ample. This is probably known to the experts, but a proof has not been written down, so we give one in
5
. As an application, in
5
, we show:

The affine cone over any polarised abelian variety of dimension
\geq 2
has only conical deformations.

2.
Affine and projective cones

-1.10. Basic properties of cones.

We use standard notation from deformation theory, see for example
[
10
]
,
[
20
]
.
Let
X
be an algebraic scheme. A
deformation
of
X
over a scheme
B=\Spec A
of finite type with a closed point
0\in B
is a flat morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
together with a closed immersion
X\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
which identifies
X
with the closed fibre over
0
.
A deformation is called
infinitesimal
if
A
is local Artinian. We say that
X
is
smoothable
if
there exists a deformation
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\to B
of
X
over an integral scheme
B
of finite type
whose fibre
\mathcal{X}_{b}
is smooth for general
b\in B
(cf.
[
10
, §29]
).

Let
(X,L)
be a
polarised manifold
, that is,
X
is a smooth projective variety such that
\dim X\geq 1
and
L
is an ample line bundle.
Let

R(X,L):=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k}).

The
affine cone
over
(X,L)
is
C_{a}(X,L):=\Spec R(X,L)
and the
projective cone
over
(X,L)
is
C_{p}(X,L):=\Spec R(X,L)[x]
, where
x
has degree
1
.
By
[
9
, 8.8.6]
,
C_{a}(X,L)
is normal.
We recall the following property.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarised manifold such that
\dim X\geq 1
.
Then we have the following:

(i)

The cone
C_{a}(X,L)
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
H^{i}(X,L^{\otimes k})=0
for all
0<i<\dim X
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

The cone
C_{a}(X,L)
is Gorenstein if and only if it is Cohen–Macaulay and
\omega_{X}\simeq L^{\otimes m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For (i), it is enough to check the conditions (a) and (b) in

[
7
, 5.1.6(ii)]
.
We can check (a) by the construction of
C_{a}(X,L)
.
The condition (b) is nothing but our assumption. Part (ii) follows from
[
7
, 5.1.9]
.

-1.12. A smoothable affine cone with a non-smoothable projectivization.

The following example proves Theorem
1
.

Let
S
be a divisor of bidegree
(3,4)
in
\mathbb P:=\mathbb P^{1}\times\mathbb P^{2}
. If
S
has at worst ordinary double points, then
S
is a regular surface of general type with
p_{g}=6
and
K^{2}=11
. Indeed, by adjunction,
\omega_{S}=\mathcal{O}_{S}(1,1)
. From the standard short exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{I}_{S}\to\mathcal{O}P\to\mathcal{O}_{S}\to 0
and vanishing of
H^{1}(\mathcal{O}P(-2,-3))
, it follows that
p_{g}(S)=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb P}(1,1))=6
. Similarly,
q(S)=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb P})=0
so
S
is regular. Writing
H_{1},H_{2}
for the generators of
\Pic\mathbb P
, we compute
K_{S}^{2}=(3H_{1}+4H_{2})(H_{1}+H_{2})^{2}=11H_{1}H_{2}^{2}=11
.

The above discussion shows that the canonical model of
S
is induced by the Segre embedding of
\mathbb P^{1}\times\mathbb P^{2}
in
\mathbb P^{5}
. We next describe the defining equations of the canonical model. Let
s_{1},s_{2}
,
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
be the homogeneous coordinates on
\mathbb P^{1}\times\mathbb P^{2}
and let
F\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}P(3,4))
be the defining equation of
S
. We choose
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb P}(3,3))
such that
F=t_{1}f_{1}+t_{2}f_{2}+t_{3}f_{3}
. Then the coordinates giving the Segre embedding are
x_{1}=s_{1}t_{1},x_{2}=s_{1}t_{2},x_{3}=s_{1}t_{3},x_{4}=s_{2}t_{1},x_{5}=s_{2}t_{2},x_{6}=s_{2}t_{3}
and
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
may be written as cubics in the
x_{i}
. The canonical model of
S
in
\mathbb P^{5}
is thus defined by the following five equations:

\displaystyle\Pf_{5}=x_{2}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{5},\ \Pf_{4}=x_{3}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{6},\ \Pf_{3}=x_{3}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{6},

\displaystyle\Pf_{2}=s_{1}F=x_{1}f_{1}+x_{2}f_{2}+x_{3}f_{3},\ \Pf_{1}=s_{2}F=x_{4}f_{1}+x_{5}f_{2}+x_{6}f_{3},

where
\Pf_{2}
,
\Pf_{1}
are obtained by writing
s_{1}F
(respectively
s_{2}F
) in terms of the
x_{i}
. According to the Buchsbaum–Eisenbud theorem on Gorenstein codimension 3 ideals, these equations may be written as
4\times 4
Pfaffians of the skew matrix
M

of the form

M=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
0&0&x_{4}&x_{5}&x_{6}\\
-x_{1}&-x_{4}&0&f_{3}&-f_{2}\\
-x_{2}&-x_{5}&-f_{3}&0&f_{1}\\
-x_{3}&-x_{6}&f_{2}&-f_{1}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\Pf_{i}
is the Pfaffian of the skew symmetric matrix
M_{i}
obtained from
M

by deleting
i
-th row and column.
The first three equations define the Segre embedding, and the last two cut out the divisor
S
.

Let
X=C_{a}(S,K_{S})\subset\mathbb A^{6}
be the affine cone over the canonical model of
S
. Then by construction,
X
is a Gorenstein normal 3-dimensional singularity. The equations defining
X
are still the
4\times 4
Pfaffians of
M
, and the coordinates on
\mathbb A^{6}
are
x_{1},\dots,x_{6}
.

All deformations of
X
are obtained by varying the entries of
M

[
12
,
27
]
or
[
10
, Theorem 9.7]
. Thus after coordinate changes, the general fibre
X^{\prime}
of any deformation of
X
is defined by the Pfaffians of

M^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&g&x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
-g&0&x_{4}&x_{5}&x_{6}\\
-x_{1}&-x_{4}&0&f_{3}^{\prime}&-f_{2}^{\prime}\\
-x_{2}&-x_{5}&-f_{3}^{\prime}&0&f_{1}^{\prime}\\
-x_{3}&-x_{6}&f_{2}^{\prime}&-f_{1}^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
f^{\prime}_{i}=f_{i}+h_{i}
for some polynomials
h_{i}
, and
g
is an arbitrary polynomial.
Then the
4\times 4
Pfaffians of
M^{\prime}
are

\displaystyle\Pf_{5}=gf_{3}^{\prime}+x_{2}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{5},\ \Pf_{4}=-gf_{2}^{\prime}+x_{3}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{6},\ \Pf_{3}=gf_{1}^{\prime}+x_{3}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{6},

\displaystyle\Pf_{2}=x_{1}f_{1}^{\prime}+x_{2}f_{2}^{\prime}+x_{3}f_{3}^{\prime},\ \Pf_{1}=x_{4}f_{1}^{\prime}+x_{5}f_{2}^{\prime}+x_{6}f_{3}^{\prime}.

The smoothability of
X
is well known (cf.
[
12
, Section 5]
). Let
g
be a nonzero constant, and choose
h_{i}
sufficiently general with some terms of degree
\leq 1
. Since
g
is constant, Pfaffians 1 and 2 are redundant, and
X^{\prime}
is a nonsingular complete intersection for suitably chosen
h_{i}
.

Now restrict to deformations
X^{\prime}
that are induced by a deformation of the projective cone
C_{p}(S,K_{S})\subset\mathbb P^{6}
. Then
g\equiv 0
for degree reasons, and
h_{i}
must have degree
\leq 3
— in particular, the above smoothing is not induced by
C_{p}(S,K_{S})
. Since
g=0
,
X^{\prime}
passes through the origin, and a computation of the partial derivatives of Pfaffians 3, 4 and 5 shows that the Jacobian matrix of
X^{\prime}
must have rank
\leq 2
there. Thus
X^{\prime}
is singular at the origin. As pointed out by the referee, an analysis of the tangent cone shows that, at best, the singularity of
X^{\prime}
is given by taking two hyperplane sections through the vertex of the cone over the Segre embedding of
\mathbb P^{1}\times\mathbb P^{2}
. These hyperplane sections are defined by some perturbations of
\Pf_{1}
and
\Pf_{2}
respectively.

For any
k\geq 3
, we get 3-fold singularities with similar properties by taking a divisor
S_{k}
in
\mathbb P^{1}\times\mathbb P^{2}
of bidegree
(k,k+1)
.

-1.23. Cones over imprimitively polarized K3 surfaces.

Let

be a general primitively polarized K3 surface of genus

2\leq g\leq 6

, or

, or

W_{i}\cap\pi_{i}^{*}H_{i}=S
, so the affine cone
C_{a}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(1))
is a hyperplane section of
C_{a}(W_{i},-K_{W_{i}})\subset\mathbb A^{9}
for each
i
, and so
C_{a}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(1))
has two topologically distinct smoothings.

5.
On quasihomogeneous cones

Let
X
be a projective manifold polarised by an ample line bundle
L
. We generalise Pinkham and Schlessinger’s criteria on
T^{1}(k)

[
2
, Theorem 12.1]
, to the case where
L
is not necessarily very ample. Choose generators
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of degrees
w_{1},\dots,w_{n}
for
R(X,L)=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})
and let
\bar{X}=\Proj R(X,L)
be the image of
X
in weighted projective space
\mathbb P(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
.

The image
\bar{X}
in
\mathbb P(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
is nonsingular and avoids the singular locus of
\mathbb P(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
.

The isomorphism
X\simeq\Proj R(X,L)
is elementary and
X
is embedded into

\mathbb P(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
by the surjection
\mathbb C[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\rightarrow R(X,L)
sending

z_{i}
to
x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Assume that
X\cap\Sing\mathbb P\neq\emptyset
. Then there exists
I:=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{l}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
w_{I}:=\gcd(w_{i_{1}},\ldots,w_{i_{l}})

>1
and the corresponding stratum
\Pi_{I}\subset\Sing\mathbb P
of index
w_{I}

satisfies
X\cap\Pi_{I}\neq\emptyset
.
Let
m>0
be a sufficiently large integer such that
L^{\otimes m}
is very ample and

\gcd(m,w_{I})=1
. Since we have a surjection
\mathbb C[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]\rightarrow R(X,L)
and
it induces a surjection
H^{0}(\mathbb P,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb P}(m))\rightarrow H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes m})
,
we obtain
V(s)\supset\Pi_{I}\cap X
for nonzero
s\in H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes m})
.
This contradicts the base point freeness of
|L^{\otimes m}|
.

Let
\Art_{\mathbb C}
be  the  category  of  Artinian  local
\mathbb C
-algebras  with  residue  field
\mathbb C
.  We  denote  by
\Hilb^{w}_{Y}\colon{\Art_{\mathbb{C}}}\rightarrow(\Sets)
, the weighted Hilbert functor parametrizing embedded deformations of
Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{n}(w)
in the weighted projective space
\mathbb P^{n}(w)
.

Let
X
be a projective manifold,
L
an ample line bundle on
X
and
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
be the embedding
determined by generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in R(X,L)
.

(i)

(Negative graded case) Suppose that
T^{1}_{C_{a}}(k)=0
for all
k>0
.
Then the restriction map

\Phi\colon\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}(X,L)}\rightarrow\Def_{C_{a}(X,L)}

is formally smooth.

(ii)

(Conical deformations)
Suppose that
T^{1}_{C_{a}}(k)=0
for all
k\neq 0
.
Then we have a canonical morphism of functors

\Psi\colon\Hilb^{w}_{X}\rightarrow\Def_{C_{a}(X,L)}

and it is formally smooth, that is,
C_{a}(X,L)
has only conical deformations.

A weaker version of part (i) can be extracted from
[
16
]
: the restriction map
\Phi\colon\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}(X,L)}\rightarrow\Def_{C_{a}(X,L)}
has a section. Indeed, Pinkham and Schlessinger
[
16
, Proposition 2.3]
showed that a quasihomogeneous cone has a versal deformation,
and a small modification of the argument used in
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
shows the claim.

We generalise the approach of
[
2
, Theorem 12.1]
to the weighted setting. For part (i), we need to show that the following two properties hold:

(1)

d\Phi\colon\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}}(k[\epsilon])\to\Def_{C_{a}}(k[\epsilon])
is surjective.

(2)

Define
\xi_{A}:=C_{p,A}\in\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}}(A)
. Let
\bar{\xi}_{A}:=\Phi(\xi_{A})\in\Def_{C_{a}}(A)

be its image and assume that
\bar{\xi}_{A}
can be lifted over a small extension
A^{\prime}\in\Art_{\mathbb{C}}

of
A
. Then there exists a lift
\xi_{A^{\prime}}\in\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}}(A^{\prime})
of
\xi_{A}
over
A^{\prime}
.

We prove (1). Let
C_{p}\subset\mathbb P(1,w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
denote the projective cone over
X
and let
\pi_{p}\colon C^{\prime}_{p}\to X
be the
\mathbb C
-bundle over
X
arising from the punctured projective cone. Since
C_{p}
is normal at the vertex, we have

\displaystyle\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}}(k[\epsilon])=H^{0}(N_{C_{p}/\mathbb P(1,w)})=H^{0}(C^{\prime}_{p},\pi_{p}^{*}N_{X/\mathbb P(w)})=H^{0}(X,{\pi_{p}}_{*}\pi_{p}^{*}N_{X/\mathbb P(w)})

\displaystyle=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{j\geq 0}H^{0}(X,N_{X/\mathbb P(w)}\otimes L^{\otimes-j}).

Now,
\Def_{C_{a}}(k[\epsilon])=T^{1}_{C_{a}}
, and according to
[
25
, Theorem 3.7]
, the graded pieces of
T^{1}_{C_{a}}
are

T^{1}_{C_{a}}(k)=\coker\Big(H^{0}(X,\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}L^{\otimes(k+w_{i})})\to H^{0}(X,Q\otimes L^{\otimes k})\Big)

where
Q
is the cokernel of
\mathcal{E}_{L}\to\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i}L^{\otimes{w_{i}}}
. Now,
Q
is simply the normal
bundle to
X
in
\mathbb P(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
.
Since
T_{C_{a}}^{1}(k)=0
for
k>0
, we see that

d\Phi\colon\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(N_{X/\mathbb P(w)}\otimes L^{\otimes-k})\to T^{1}_{C_{a}}=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k\geq 0}T^{1}_{C_{a}}(-k)

is surjective.

Next we prove (2). The obstruction to lifting
\bar{\xi}_{A^{\prime}}
to
\xi_{A^{\prime}}
lives in
H^{1}(C_{p},N_{C_{p}/\mathbb P(1,w)})
. As before, since
C_{p}
is normal at the vertex, we have an inclusion
H^{1}(C_{p},N_{C_{p}/\mathbb P(1,w)})\subset\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k\geq 0}H^{1}(X,N_{X/\mathbb P(w)}\otimes L^{\otimes-k})
. Thus we have an inclusion

H^{1}(C_{p},N_{C_{p}/\mathbb P(1,w)})\subset H^{1}(C^{\prime}_{a},N_{C^{\prime}_{a}})=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}H^{1}(X,N_{X/\mathbb P(w)}\otimes L^{\otimes k}),

where
C_{a}^{\prime}
denotes the punctured affine cone, which is a
\mathbb C^{*}
-bundle over
X
. By assumption,
\bar{\xi}_{A}|_{C^{\prime}_{a}}
lifts to
\bar{\xi}_{A^{\prime}}|_{C^{\prime}_{a}}
so the image of this obstruction in
H^{1}(C^{\prime}_{a},N_{C^{\prime}_{a}})
vanishes. This implies the existence of
\xi_{A^{\prime}}
in
\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}}(A^{\prime})
lifting
\xi_{A}
.

Proof of part (ii). We define the canonical functor
\Psi\colon\Hilb^{w}_{X}\to\Def_{C_{a}}
as follows: let
\xi_{A}=(X_{A}\subset\mathbb P_{A}(w))
in
\Hilb^{w}_{C_{p}}(A)
be an embedded deformation of
X\subset\mathbb P(w)
. Define
R_{A}=A[\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}]/I_{X_{A}}
, where generators
\tilde{x}_{i}
are chosen so that
\mathbb P_{A}(w)=\Proj_{A}A[\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n}]
, and
I_{X_{A}}
is the ideal defining
X_{A}\subset\mathbb P_{A}(w)
, i.e.
X_{A}=\Proj_{A}R_{A}
. By construction,
R_{A}
is a flat deformation of
R(X,L)
. Indeed, the generators
\tilde{x}_{i}
clearly extend
x_{i}
because
X_{A}
is embedded in
\mathbb P_{A}(w)
, and the relations and syzygies of
R_{A}
lift those of
R(X,L)
because
X_{A}
is flat. Thus we define
\Psi(\xi_{A})=\bar{\xi}_{A}
in
\Def_{C_{a}}(A)
by
\Spec_{A}R_{A}
. The central fibre is
\Spec R(X,L)
and
\bar{\xi}_{A}
is automatically flat. Moreover,
\Psi
is functorial because
\Hilb^{w}_{X}
is.

Formal smoothness is proved in a similar way to part (i). This time we have
\Hilb^{w}_{X}(k[\epsilon])=H^{0}(X,N_{X/\mathbb P(w)})

and by assumption,
T^{1}_{C_{a}}=T^{1}_{C_{a}}(0)
so we again have a surjection
d\Psi\colon\Hilb^{w}_{X}(k[\epsilon])\to\Def_{C_{a}}(k[\epsilon])
.

Let
\bar{\xi}_{A^{\prime}}
in
\Def_{C_{a}}(A^{\prime})
be an extension of
\Psi(\xi_{A})=\bar{\xi}_{A}
. The obstruction to lifting
\bar{\xi}_{A^{\prime}}
to
\Hilb^{w}_{X}(A^{\prime})
lies in
H^{1}(N_{X/\mathbb P(w)})
. Now,
H^{1}(N_{X/\mathbb P(w)})\subset H^{1}(C^{\prime}_{a},N_{C^{\prime}_{a}})
and we know that
\bar{\xi}_{A}|_{C^{\prime}_{a}}
lifts to
\bar{\xi}_{A^{\prime}}|_{C^{\prime}_{a}}
by assumption. Thus the obstruction vanishes, and
\xi_{A^{\prime}}
in
\Hilb^{w}_{X}(A^{\prime})
exists.

We think that the following result is known to experts, but we could not find it in
the literature. We prove it as an application of Proposition
5
.

Let
X
be an abelian variety of dimension
n\geq 2
and
L
an ample line bundle on
X
.
Then the affine cone
C_{a}=C_{a}(X,L)
has only conical deformations.

Since
\mathcal{T}_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus n}
, we have
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X}\otimes L^{\otimes k})=0
because
H^{1}(X,L^{\otimes k})=0
for any
k\neq 0

by Serre duality, Kodaira vanishing and
n\geq 2
.
Hence
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{E}_{L}\otimes L^{\otimes k})=0
and thus
T^{1}_{C_{a}}(k)=0
for
k\neq 0
by
3
.
Now apply Proposition
5
(ii).

Indeed, embedded deformations of the projective cone over an abelian variety
were shown to be conical in
[
21
]
. A recent preprint
[
11
, Cor. 8.7]

contains a proof of Corollary
5
which works in positive characteristic.

-1.32. Quasismooth K3 surfaces.

It would be very interesting to generalise Theorem
1
to the case of affine cones over quasismooth K3 surfaces embedded in weighted projective space. Some applications of this are worked out in
[
6
]
, and we have already made some progress in this direction with Proposition
5
. We believe it is possible to further extend the Proposition to the quasismooth case. This motivates future work.

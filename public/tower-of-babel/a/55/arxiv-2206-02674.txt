Families of stable 3-folds in positive characteristic
János Kollár

Abstract




Abstract.
We show that flat families of stable 3-folds do not
lead to proper moduli spaces in any characteristic
p>0
. As a
byproduct, we obtain log canonical 4-fold pairs, whose log canonical
centers are not weakly normal.

Keywords.
Stable family, 3-fold, positive characteristic, canonical model, moduli

Following
[
KSB88
]
, the moduli space of varieties of general type is compactified as follows. Start with a family of smooth projective varieties
g^{\circ}\colon X^{\circ}_{B}\to B^{\circ}
over a smooth, affine curve
B^{\circ}
. Possibly after a base change
C^{\circ}\to B^{\circ}
, it extends to a semi-stable family
g\colon X\to C
over a smooth proper curve
C\supset C^{\circ}
, as in
[
KKM+73
]
. Finally, let
g^{\rm c}\colon X^{\rm c}\to C
be the relative canonical model as in
[
KM98
, Section 3.8]
.

In characteristic 0, the resulting fibers have semi-log-canonical singularities and ample canonical class. These are called
stable varieties.
Stable varieties with a fixed volume have a coarse moduli space that is projective over
{\mathbb Q}
; see
[
Kol23
]
for details.

Our aim is to give examples to show that this process does not work for 3-folds in positive characteristic. For simplicity, we work over algebraically closed fields from now on.

Theorem 1
.

Flat families of stable 3-fold pairs of fixed volume do not form a proper moduli theory in any characteristic
p>0
.

Remark 2
.

In our examples, the approach of
[
KSB88
]
does produce a canonical model
g^{\rm c}\colon X^{\rm c}\to C
; the problem is that some of its fibers do not satisfy Serre’s condition
S_{2}
. However, the normalization of each fiber is a stable variety. So we may well have a proper moduli space, but we need to allow some families whose fibers are stable only after normalization. However, we do not have a precise conjecture on which families should be allowed.

It is known that moduli theory is more complicated in positive characteristic. This is partly due to the failure of Kodaira’s vanishing theorem, and to the current lack of resolution of singularities. The appearance of
p
-torsion in the class group leads to other problems. However, it was expected that once these are correctly accounted for, the rest of the arguments would go through. For surfaces, large parts of the theory have been worked out in

[
Pat17
,
ABP23
]
.

Surprisingly, in Example
4
the normalization of the central fiber does lift to characteristic 0, so Kodaira’s vanishing theorem holds on it by
[
DI87
]
. The problem comes from the nearby fibers that exist only in characteristic
p>0
. These nearby fibers are normal, but not CM by Lemma
17
. In characteristic 0, being CM is a deformation-invariant property of stable varieties; see
[
KK10
]
. It is thus possible that such examples do not occur if one works solely in the closure of the moduli of smooth varieties of general type in dimensions 2 and 3; see
[
ABL22
,
Bri22
]
. As we discuss in Example
3
, there are such examples starting in dimension 5 in characteristic 2.

Therefore, while the examples obtained so far indicate that the positive-characteristic moduli theory is much more subtle, they do not rule out the possibility that, in the end, the necessary modifications are mainly technical.

A series of non-CM singularities is discussed in the papers
[
Kov18
,
Tot19
,
Ber19
]
, but it did not seem to have been observed that they can be used to construct stable degenerations, where the generic fibers are smooth with ample canonical class, and the special fibers have isolated, non-normal singularities. The dimension of the resulting examples is about twice the characteristic.

Example 3

((Kovács–Totaro–Bernasconi examples))
.

Let
X=G/P
be a projective, homogeneous space. The cases when
P
is non-reduced were studied in
[
HL93
,
Lau96
,
LR97
]
. Most of these are not Fano, but if
X
is Fano and Kodaira vanishing fails, then cones over
X

give interesting singularities; see
[
Kov18
,
Tot19
,
Ber19
]
. The smallest dimension of such an
X
is 5 in characteristic 2, and about twice the characteristic in general.

Assume that
-K_{X}=mH
for some ample divisor
H
for some
m\geq 1
. Then
|H|
is very ample by
[
Lau96
]
, so it gives an embedding
X\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb P}^{N}
, where
N=\dim|H|
. Let
Y:=C(X,H)\subset{\mathbb P}^{N+1}
be the projective cone over
X
with vertex
v
. Then

H^{i+1}_{v}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\cong\textstyle{\sumop\displaylimits}_{m\in{\mathbb Z}}H^{i}\bigl(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(mH)\bigr)

by
[
Kol13
, Remark 3.12]
. If
H^{1}\bigl(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(H)\bigr)\neq 0
, then
H^{2}_{v}(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\neq 0
, hence
0pt_{v}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}=2
.

Let
D\in|H|
be a smooth divisor and
D_{Y}\subset Y
its preimage. Since
K_{X}+D\sim(m-1)H
, the pair
(Y,D_{Y})
is log canonical if
m=1
and canonical if
m>1
by
[
Kol13
, Section 3.1]
. The divisor
D_{Y}
is Cartier on
Y
by
[
Kol13
, Proposition 3.14(2)]
.

There is a natural morphism
\pi\colon C(D,H|_{D})\to D_{Y}
, which is an isomorphism outside the vertex. Furthermore,
\pi
is an isomorphism if and only if
0pt_{v}D_{Y}\geq 2
, which holds if and only if
0pt_{v}Y\geq 3
. Thus, if
H^{1}\bigl(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(H)\bigr)\neq 0
, then
D_{Y}
is not normal. Intersecting
Y
with a pencil of hyperplanes gives a locally stable degeneration with generic fiber
X
and special fiber
D_{Y}
. Taking a suitable cyclic cover as in
[
KM98
, Section 2.4]
, we get a series of examples of stable degenerations, where the generic fibers are smooth varieties with ample canonical class, and the special fibers have isolated non-normal singularities.

The cases described in
[
Tot19
]
have
m=2
. Then the normalization of
D_{Y}
has canonical singularities; hence these deformations take place in what is usually considered the ‘interior’ of the moduli space. On the other hand, these constructions start with a variety for which Kodaira vanishing fails, so they cannot be lifted to characteristic 0.

Aside.
The series of non-CM, quotient singularities of
[
Yas19
]
all have
0pt\geq 3
by
[
ES80
]
, so they do not give similar examples.

All the ingredients going into the proof of Theorem
1
seem to be well known. Unipotent bundles on elliptic curves have been studied in
[
Ati57
,
Har71
,
Oda71
]
. Closely related examples of pathological families of elliptic surfaces are given in
[
Bri20
]
. Using cones to go from a lower-dimensional non-general type variety to a higher-dimensional general type one has been utilized many times. For example,
[

]
used it to show that log abundance for general type varieties in dimension

implies log abundance for all varieties in dimension
n
; see Section
11
. Related results on the deformation invariance of plurigenera also appear in
[
EH21
,
BBS21
]
.

The main step is the following; see
[
KM98
, Definition 2.37]

for the definition of
divisorial log terminal,
abbreviated as
dlt.

Example 4
.

In every characteristic
p>0
, there exist a morphism
g\colon Y\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
and an effective
{\mathbb Q}
-divisor
\Delta
on
Y
such that

(4.a)

g\colon Y\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
is smooth, projective, of relative dimension 3;

(4.b)

K_{Y}+\Delta
is
g
-semiample and
g
-big;

(4.c)

the fibers
(Y_{t},\Delta_{t})
are dlt;

(4.d)

(Y,Y_{t}+\Delta)
is dlt for every
t\in{\mathbb A}^{1}
, and this continues to hold after any base change
C\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
;

(4.e)

H^{0}\bigl(Y_{0},\omega^{m}_{Y_{0}}(m\Delta_{0})\bigr)>H^{0}\bigl(Y_{t},\omega^{m}_{Y_{t}}(m\Delta_{t})\bigr)
for
t\neq 0
and
m\geq 1
sufficiently divisible.

As in
[
Kol13
, Theorem 4.9]
,
we have (
4.d
)
\Rightarrow
(
4.c
) by adjunction. In characteristic 0, inversion of adjunction says that (
4.c
) implies the log canonical variant of (
4.d
). The key new feature is the jump of the plurigenera (
4.e
).

There are many examples in positive characteristic where finitely many of the plurigenera jump. The surfaces produced in
[
Bri20
]
lead to families of elliptic pairs (with terminal singularities) where infinitely many of the plurigenera jump. These surfaces can also be used (instead of Example
8
) to obtain families of 3-folds as in Example
4
.

The new feature of Example
4
is that the fibers are of general type, and infinitely many of the plurigenera jump. This has strong consequences.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We deduce the theorem from Example
4
.
Since
K_{Y}+\Delta
is
g
-semiample and
g
-big, there exist a morphism with connected fibers
h\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
and a relatively ample divisor
D^{\prime}
on
Y^{\prime}\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
such that
m_{0}(K_{Y}+\Delta)\sim h^{*}(m_{0}D^{\prime})
for some
m_{0}>0
. By definition, the relative canonical model is

\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr):=\operatorname{Proj}_{{\mathbb A}^{1}}\oplus_{m\geq 0}g_{*}\omega^{m}_{Y}(\lfloor{m\Delta}\rfloor).

On the right-hand side, we can change the summation to multiples of
m_{0}
, which shows that
\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)=\bigl(Y^{\prime},h_{*}\Delta\bigr)
. The fiber over the origin is

\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)_{0}=\operatorname{Proj}\oplus_{m\geq 0}\operatorname{im}\bigl[g_{*}\omega^{mm_{0}}_{Y}(mm_{0}\Delta)\to H^{0}\bigl(Y_{0},\omega^{mm_{0}}_{Y_{0}}(mm_{0}\Delta_{0})\bigr)\bigr],

whereas the canonical model of the fiber
(Y_{0},\Delta_{0})
is

\bigl((Y_{0})^{\rm c},(\Delta_{0})^{\rm c}\bigr):=\operatorname{Proj}\oplus_{m\geq 0}H^{0}\bigl(Y_{0},\omega^{mm_{0}}_{Y_{0}}(mm_{0}\Delta_{0})\bigr).

By (
4.e
), we see that

\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)_{0}\neq\bigl((Y_{0})^{\rm c},(\Delta_{0})^{\rm c}\bigr).

The properties (
4.a
)–(
4.e
) continue to hold after any base change
C\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
, so
\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)\times_{{\mathbb A}^{1}}C\to C
is the canonical model of
\bigl(Y,\Delta\bigr)\times_{{\mathbb A}^{1}}C\to C
. Thus
\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)_{0}
is the unique stable degeneration of the family over
{\mathbb A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
by
[
Kol23
, Theorem 11.40]
.
∎

In our examples,
\operatorname{Sing}(Y_{t})^{\rm c}
is 1-dimensional. Localizing at its generic point gives a simple elliptic singularity of dimension 2. There is a natural morphism

\bigl((Y_{0})^{\rm c},(\Delta_{0})^{\rm c}\bigr)\longrightarrow\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)_{0},

which is an isomorphism outside the singular set and purely inseparable over
\operatorname{Sing}(Y^{\rm c})_{0}
. These imply the following; see
[
Kol96
, Section 7.2]
for
weak normality
.

Corollary 5
.

For any
p>0
, there are log canonical 4-fold pairs
(X,S+\Delta)
of characteristic
p
, where
S
is a Cartier divisor that is not weakly normal.

Proof.

The pair
\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}+(Y^{\rm c})_{0}\bigr)
is a log canonical 4-fold,
(Y^{\rm c})_{0}
is a Cartier divisor and a log canonical center but not weakly normal.
∎

Remark 6
.

In characteristic 0, weakly normal coincides with seminormal, and all log canonical centers are seminormal by
[
Amb03
,
Fuj17
]
; see also
[
Kol13
, Theorem 5.14]
. A series of papers culminating in
[
BMP+20
]
establish that MMP for 3-folds works in characteristics at least
7
, just as in characteristic 0. This led to a hope that, in any fixed dimension, new phenomena appear only in low characteristics; see
[
Tot19
,
Ber19
,
HW19
,
HW20
]
for such results. By Corollary
5
, this is not the case for 4-folds.

However, in MMP one can frequently work with dlt pairs only; thus a dlt example would be the main test case. Our
\bigl(Y^{\rm c},\Delta^{\rm c}\bigr)
are not dlt; see Lemma
17
.

Let
(X,S)
be a 3-dimensional plt pair. If
\operatorname{char}\geq 7
, then
S
is normal, but there are counterexamples in characteristics 2; see
[
CT19
]
. See also
[
Ber21
,
Lac20
,
ABL22
]
for related examples. However, no such example seems to be known where
S
is Cartier.

Question 7
.

Let
(X,S+\Delta)
be a 3-dimensional, semi-log-canonical pair, where
S
is a Cartier divisor. Is
S
weakly normal?

We start the construction of our 3-dimensional examples with a family of elliptic surfaces, similarly to
[
Bri20
]
. Then we build these up to dimension 3.

Example 8
.

We construct a morphism
g\colon(S,\Delta+\Theta)\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
of relative dimension 2, where
g\colon S\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
is smooth, projective, the fibers
(S_{t},\Delta_{t}+\Theta_{t})
are terminal,
K_{S}+\Delta
is
g
-semiample, numerically trivial, and
K_{S}+\Delta+\Theta
is
g
-semiample of Kodaira dimension 1. Moreover, all large plurigenera jump:

H^{0}\bigl(S_{0},\omega^{m}_{S_{0}}(\lfloor{m\Delta_{0}+m\Theta_{0}}\rfloor)\bigr)>H^{0}\bigl(S_{t},\omega^{m}_{S_{t}}(\lfloor{m\Delta_{t}+m\Theta_{t}}\rfloor)\bigr)\quad\text{for}\;t\neq 0,m\gg 1.

The key is the behavior of unipotent vector bundles on elliptic curves in positive characteristic.

9

(
Elliptic ruled surfaces).

Let
E
be an elliptic curve. A vector bundle is
unipotent
if it can be written as a successive extension of copies of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
.

By
[
Ati57
]
, for every
r
, there is a unique rank
r
, indecomposable, unipotent vector bundle
F_{r}=F_{r}(E)
, and every unipotent vector bundle is a direct sum of these
F_{r}
. Thus a unipotent vector bundle
U
is isomorphic to
F_{r}
if and only if
\operatorname{rank}U=r
and
h^{0}(E,U)=1
.

Note that
F_{2}(E)
sits in an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\longrightarrow F_{2}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\longrightarrow 0

that corresponds to a non-zero class in
H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
.

Let
\tau\colon E^{\prime}\to E
be a non-constant map of elliptic curves. Then we get

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\tau^{*}F_{2}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}\longrightarrow 0.

This shows that

•

if
\tau^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is non-zero, then
\tau^{*}F_{2}(E)\cong F_{2}(E^{\prime})
;

•

if
\tau^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is zero, then

\tau^{*}F_{2}(E)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}
. This holds if and only if
h^{0}\bigl(E^{\prime},\tau^{*}F_{2}(E)\bigr)\geq 2
.

Set
m=\deg\tau
. If the characteristic does not divide
m
, then
\frac{1}{m}\operatorname{Trace}_{E^{\prime}/E}\colon\tau_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
is a splitting of
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\to\tau_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}}
. Thus
H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is an injection. In particular, we are always in the first case in characteristic 0.

Note that
\tau^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is the tangent map of
g^{*}\colon\operatorname{Pic}(E)\to\operatorname{Pic}(E^{\prime})
at the origin. (This holds for Abelian varieties; see
[
Mum70
, Section 15]
.) Thus
\tau^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is the zero map if and only if
\tau^{*}\colon\operatorname{Pic}(E)\to\operatorname{Pic}(E^{\prime})
is inseparable. This cannot happen in characteristic 0, but in characteristic
p>0
, there is always a degree
p
map
\rho\colon E^{\prime}\to E
such that
\rho^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is the zero map. (For higher-dimensional Abelian varieties,
\rho^{*}
has a 1-dimensional kernel; see
[
Mum70
, Section 15]
.)

Projectivising
F_{2}
, we get a
{\mathbb P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi_{1}\colon S_{1}\to E
with a unique section
D_{1}
with self-intersection 0. Note that
K_{S_{1}}+2D_{1}\sim 0
and the normal bundle of
D_{1}
is trivial.

Let
C\subset S_{1}
be an irreducible, reduced curve that is disjoint from
D_{1}
. Then it is numerically equivalent to a multiple of
D_{1}
; hence
p_{a}(C)=1
by adjunction. The projection
\tau\colon C\to E
is dominant; hence
C
is a smooth, elliptic curve. The fiber product
S_{1}\times_{E}C
has two disjoint section; hence
\tau^{*}F_{2}(E)
is trivial. Thus
H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{O}}_{C})
is the zero map.

Conversely, let
g\colon C\to E
be a non-constant morphism such that
g^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(C,{\mathcal{O}}_{C})
is the zero map. Then
g^{*}F_{2}
splits, giving
C\to S_{1}
whose image is linearly equivalent to
\deg(g)\cdot D_{1}
.

Claim 9.1
.

We have
(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1})=F_{m+1}
if either
\operatorname{char}k=0
or
m<\operatorname{char}k
.

Proof.

Let
C
be an integral curve such that
C\sim mD_{1}
. Then
(C\cdot D_{1})=0
, so either
C
and
D_{1}
are equal, or they are disjoint. As we noted above, the latter cannot happen if either
\operatorname{char}k=0
or
m<\operatorname{char}k
. Thus
h^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1}))=1
for such values. Pushing forward the filtration

{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(D_{1})\subset\cdots\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1})

gives that
(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1})
is unipotent.
∎

Claim 9.2
.

If
\operatorname{char}k=p>0
, then
(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1})={\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus F_{p}
.

Proof.

Choose a degree
p
map
\rho\colon E^{\prime}\to E
such that
\rho^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})
is zero. Then pull-back by
\rho\colon E^{\prime}\to E
splits
F_{2}
, so
H^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1}))\geq 2
. On the other hand, from

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}((p-1)D_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}((pD_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{1}}\longrightarrow 0

we get that
F_{p}\lhook\joinrel\to(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1})
and
H^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1}))\leq 2
. These imply that we have
(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1})\bigr)\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus F_{p}
.
∎

It is not clear what the
(\pi_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1})
are for larger values of
m
, but we have the following.

Claim 9.3
.

In all cases,
h^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1}))<m+1
for
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

The filtration

{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}((p-1)D_{1})\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1})\subset\cdots\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1})

gives that
H^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1}))\leq 1+(m-p+1)=m+1-(p-1)<m+1
.
∎

Note that if
\operatorname{char}k=0
, then in fact
h^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1}))=1
. If
\operatorname{char}k=p>0
, then we expect that
h^{0}(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1}))=\lfloor{m/p}\rfloor+1
. (See
[
Sch10
]
for computing
F_{m}\otimes F_{n}
.)

Remark 9.4
.

The reader can check that in our constructions, one can replace
E
with any Abelian variety
A
of dimension
n
. The key property is that there is a non-split extension

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{A}\longrightarrow F_{2}(A)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{A}\longrightarrow 0

that becomes split after a suitable degree
p
cover
A^{\prime}\to A
. By
[
Mum70
, Section 15]
, this always holds in characteristic
p>0
. The resulting
g\colon(Y,\Delta)\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
has relative dimension
n+2
. The singular sets of the canonical models
(Y_{t})^{\rm c}
are still 1-dimensional.

10

(
Construction of Example
8
)
.

Over
{\mathbb A}^{1}
, there is a degeneration of
F_{2}
to the split bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
. Thus we get a vector bundle
F
over

for

.

Let
\pi\colon S\to E\times{\mathbb A}^{1}
be the corresponding
{\mathbb P}^{1}
-bundle with the unique section
D
in
|{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(1)|
. If

isomorphic to

.

The jump in the plurigenera comes from Claim

:

H^{0}\bigl(S_{0},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}(mD_{0})\bigr)=m+1>H^{0}\bigl(S_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(mD_{1})\bigr).

Now consider

Now assume that we are in characteristic

, and let
\rho\colon E^{\prime}\to E

\rho^{*}\colon H^{1}(E,{\mathcal{O}}_{E})\to H^{1}(E^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})

Consider
(\rho,1)\colon E^{\prime}\times{\mathbb A}^{1}\to E\times{\mathbb A}^{1}

is a trivial
{\mathbb P}^{1}
-bundle for every

, so
(\rho,1)^{*}S
is a trivial
{\mathbb P}^{1}
-bundle over
E^{\prime}\times{\mathbb A}^{1}
. Pushing forward, we get that
|pD|
is a basepoint-free relative pencil on
S
.

We can thus replace
2D
with a linear combination

\Delta:=\tfrac{1}{mp}G_{1}+\cdots+\tfrac{1}{mp}G_{2m},

where the
G_{i}\in|pD|
are general and

(we can even make the fibers terminal) such that

. Now we have a locally stable morphism

\bigl(S,\Delta+D\bigr)\to{\mathbb A}^{1},

(10.1)

where
K_{S}+\Delta+D
is semiample, and all (large enough) plurigenera jump at
t=0
. (If we want terminal fibers, we can also replace
D
with
\tfrac{1}{mp}G^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+\tfrac{1}{mp}G^{\prime}_{m}
as above.)

Working on the surface
S_{0}
, the Iitaka fibration of
K_{S_{0}}+\Delta_{0}+D_{0}
is the coordinate projection
\tau_{0}\colon S_{0}\cong E\times{\mathbb P}^{1}\to{\mathbb P}^{1}
. However, the restriction of the (relative) Iitaka fibration of
S
to
S_{0}
is given by a pencil in
|pD_{0}|
whose members are geometrically connected. The only such pencil is the composite of
\tau_{0}
with the Frobenius morphism
{\mathbb P}^{1}\to{\mathbb P}^{1}
.

This example has Kodaira dimension 1, but we can use it to get a general type
example in one dimension higher, using the following method.

11
.

Let
Z
be a projective variety whose pluricanonical maps behave in unexpected ways. Then
[
McK06
]
constructs a general type pair (of dimension one higher) whose pluricanonical maps also behave unexpectedly.

Choose a sufficiently general embedding
Z\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb P}^{N}
, and let
C\subset{\mathbb P}^{N+1}
be the cone over
Z
with vertex
v\in C
.

Blow up
v
to get
Y\to C
with exceptional divisor
E\cong Z
. By adjunction,
\omega_{Y}(E)|_{E}\cong\omega_{E}
. So,
assuming
that the maps

H^{0}\bigl(Y,\omega^{m}_{Y}(mE)\bigr)\longrightarrow H^{0}(E,\omega^{m}_{E})\cong H^{0}(Z,\omega^{m}_{Z})

(11.1)

are surjective, the behavior of the pluricanonical maps of
Z
should be ‘visible’ from the pluricanonical maps of the pair
(Y,E)
.

The problem is that
Y
is a
{\mathbb P}^{1}
-bundle over
Z
, so in fact
H^{0}\bigl(Y,\omega^{m}_{Y}(mE)\bigr)=0
for
m>0
. There are two ways to fix this. Let
H_{C}
be the hyperplane class on
C
and
H_{Y}
its pull-back to
Y
.

First, choose
d\gg 1
, and let
D_{C}\sim dH_{C}
be a general divisor not passing through
v
. It gives
D_{Y}\subset Y
, with
D_{Y}
disjoint from
E
, and the pair
(Y,D_{Y}+E)
is of general type. We work out below that the maps

H^{0}\bigl(Y,\omega^{m}_{Y}(mD_{Y}+mE)\bigr)\longrightarrow H^{0}(E,\omega^{m}_{E})\cong H^{0}(Z,\omega^{m}_{Z})

(11.2)

are surjective.

Second, fix
r>0
not divisible by the characteristic, fix
d\gg 1
, and let
s\in{\mathcal{O}}_{C}(drH_{C})
be a general section. As in
[
KM98
, Section 2.4]
, these data determine degree
r
, ramified, cyclic covers

\pi_{C}:C_{r}:=C\bigl[\sqrt[r]{s}\bigr]\longrightarrow C\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi_{Y}:Y_{r}:=Y\bigl[\sqrt[r]{s}\bigr]\longrightarrow Y,

whose canonical classes are
\pi_{C}^{*}\bigl(K_{C}+d(r-1)H_{C}\bigr)
(resp.
\pi_{Y}^{*}\bigl(K_{Y}+d(r-1)H_{Y}\bigr)
).

Here
E_{r}:=\pi_{Y}^{-1}(E)
is
r
disjoint copies of
E
. We check that the maps

H^{0}\bigl(Y_{r},\omega^{m}_{Y_{r}}(mE_{r})\bigr)\longrightarrow H^{0}(E_{r},\omega^{m}_{E_{r}})\cong\oplus_{1}^{r}H^{0}(Z,\omega^{m}_{Z})

(11.3)

are surjective.

Next we go through the details of this for log canonical pairs
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
such that
K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}\sim_{{\mathbb Q}}0
. For these, the cone is also log canonical, and the vertex is a log canonical center.

12

(
Cone construction
)
.

Start with a projective pair
(Z,\Delta_{Z})
such that
K_{Z}+\Delta_{Z}\sim_{{\mathbb Q}}0
, and a semiample
{\mathbb Q}
-divisor
D_{Z}
. Choose any ample
L
, and consider
Y:=\operatorname{Proj}_{Z}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z}+L)
. It is a
{\mathbb P}^{1}
-bundle
\tau\colon Y\to Z
with two sections:
Z_{0}\subset Y
with normal bundle
L^{-1}
, and
Z_{\infty}\subset Y
with normal bundle
L
. With
\Delta_{Y}:=\tau^{*}\Delta_{Z}
, we have

K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z_{\infty}\sim_{{\mathbb Q}}0.

Thus
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+2Z_{\infty}+D_{Y}
is semiample and big. We get its canonical model
\phi_{Y}\colon Y\to Y^{\rm c}
. Here
\phi_{Y}
is an isomorphism on
Y\setminus Z_{0}
, and it restricts to the morphism given by multiples of
D_{Z}
on
Z_{0}\cong Z
.

If
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+D_{Z})
is dlt, then so is
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z_{\infty}+D_{Y})
by
[
Kol13
, Proposition 2.15]
. Since
|Z_{\infty}|
is ample on
Y\setminus Z_{0}
, we can replace
2Z_{\infty}
with a suitable
{\mathbb Q}
-divisor
Z^{\prime}_{\infty}\sim_{{\mathbb Q}}2Z_{\infty}
to get a dlt pair

(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z^{\prime}_{\infty}+D_{Y})

(12.1)

with these properties.

If
L
is very ample, then we can let
Z^{\prime}_{\infty}
be the sum of two general members of
|Z_{\infty}|
; otherwise, we may need
Z^{\prime}_{\infty}
to be a
{\mathbb Q}
-divisor. This may be preferable anyhow since this way we can arrange that
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z^{\prime}_{\infty}+D_{Y})
is terminal away from
Z_{0}
. Note, however, that
Z_{0}
must stay with coefficient 1, so dlt is the best that we can have for
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z^{\prime}_{\infty}+D_{Y})
. This is in accordance with
[
ABL22
, Corollary 1.3]
.

13

(
Computing the plurigenera
)
.

Let
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z^{\prime}_{\infty}+D_{Y})
be as above. Taking into account that
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z_{\infty}\sim_{{\mathbb Q}}0
, for all sufficiently divisible
m
, we have

\begin{split}H^{0}\bigl(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(mK_{Y}+m\Delta_{Y}+mZ_{0}+mZ^{\prime}_{\infty}+mD_{Y})\bigr)&=H^{0}\bigl(Y,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(mZ_{\infty}+mD_{Y})\bigr)\\
&=H^{0}\bigl(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(mD_{Z})\otimes S^{m}({\mathcal{O}}_{Z}+L)\bigr)\\
&=\textstyle{\sumop\displaylimits}_{r=0}^{m}H^{0}\bigl(Z,L^{r}(mD_{Z})\bigr).\end{split}

(13.1)

Since
D_{Z}
is semiample, by Fujita’s vanishing theorem
[
Fuj83
]
we can choose
L
sufficiently ample such that

h^{0}\bigl(Z,L^{r}(mD_{Z})\bigr)=\chi\bigl(Z,L^{r}(mD_{Z})\bigr)

for
r\geq 1
and for all sufficiently divisible
m
. These terms are thus deformation invariant. (In our case, this actually works for any
L
since Kodaira vanishing holds on the
S_{t}
;
cf.

[
Muk13
, Theorem 3]
.)

The key term is the
r=0
summand
H^{0}\bigl(Z,{\mathcal{O}}_{Z}(mD_{Z})\bigr)
of (
13.1
). Thus we see that if we vary the pair
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+D_{Z})
, then any jump in the plurigenera of
(Z,\Delta_{Z}+D_{Z})
leads to the same jump in the plurigenera of
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z^{\prime}_{\infty}+D_{Y})
, for all sufficiently divisible
m
.

14

(
Construction of Example
4
)
.

Start with
\bigl(S,\Delta+\tfrac{1}{2}D\bigr)\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
as in (
10.1
). Let
L
be any relatively ample line bundle on
S
, and set
Y:=\operatorname{Proj}_{S}({\mathcal{O}}_{S}+L)
. We get
(Y,\Delta_{Y}+Z_{0}+Z^{\prime}_{\infty}+D_{Y})\to{\mathbb A}^{1}
as in (
12.1
).

Claims (
4.c
)–(
4.d
) follow from Corollary
16
, and the jump of the plurigenera (

In characteristic 0 one usually proves claims (
4.c
)–(
4.d
) using inversion of adjunction as in
[
Kol13
, Theorem 4.9]
.
This is not known in positive characteristic; see however
[
HX15
]

for related results
for 3-folds. The following is a combination of
[

Lemma 15
.

Let
X
be a regular scheme and
\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}D_{i}
a simple normal crossing divisor. For
J\subset I
, set
D_{J}:=\cap_{i\in J}D_{i}
. Let
\Delta
be an effective
{\mathbb R}
-divisor. Assume that

(15.a)

for every
J
, none of the irreducible components of
D_{J}
is contained in
\operatorname{Supp}\Delta
, and
\operatorname{mult}_{x}(\Delta|_{D_{J}})\leq 1

(
resp.
<1)
for every
x\in D_{J}
.

Then
(X,D+\Delta)
is log canonical
(
resp. dlt
)
near
D
.

Proof.

Let
E
be an exceptional divisor over
X
. In order to show that
a(E,X,D+\Delta)\geq-1
, we may localize at the generic point of
\operatorname{center}_{X}E\subset X
. So assume that
\operatorname{center}_{X}E=\{x\}
is a closed point. If
x\notin\operatorname{Supp}\Delta
, then we are done by
[
Kol13
, Proposition 2.7]
. Otherwise, let
\pi\colon B_{x}X\to X
be the blow-up with exceptional divisor
D_{0}
. Using induction and
[
KM98
, Lemma 2.45]
,
it is enough to prove the following two claims:

(15.b)

a(D_{0},X,D+\Delta)\geq-1
(resp.
>-1
), and

(15.c)

\bigl(B_{x}X,(D_{0}+\pi^{-1}_{*}D)+\pi^{-1}_{*}\Delta\bigr)
also satisfies
(
15.a
).

These are both straightforward.
∎

Corollary 16
.

Let
g\colon(X,D+\Delta)\to C
be a smooth morphism. Assume that
\bigl(X,(X_{c}+D)+\Delta\bigr)
satisfies (
15.a
) for every
c\in C
.

Then
\bigl(X,(X_{c}+D)+\Delta\bigr)
is log canonical
(
resp. dlt
)
for every
c\in C
, and the same holds after every base change
C^{\prime}\to C
.

The following also shows that the canonical models of the fibers do not form a flat family.

Lemma 17
.

The
(Y_{i})^{\rm c}
are not dlt,
(Y_{0})^{\rm c}
is CM, but
(Y_{1})^{\rm c}\cong(Y^{\rm c})_{t}
for
t\neq 0
is not CM.

Proof.

We have
\tau_{Y_{i}}\colon Y_{i}\to(Y_{i})^{\rm c}
, which restricts to the elliptic fibration
\tau_{i}\colon S_{i}\to{\mathbb P}^{1}
. The localization of
(Y_{i})^{\rm c}
at the generic point of its singular set is thus a 2-dimensional, elliptic singularity. In particular, the
(Y_{i})^{\rm c}
are not dlt.

For the CM claims, we aim to use Lemma
18
. As in Section
13
, the cohomologies of line bundles on the
Y_{i}
are easy to compute. In particular, we obtain that
R^{1}(\tau_{Y_{i}})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y_{i}}=R^{1}(\tau_{i})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{i}}
.

Since
S_{0}\to{\mathbb P}^{1}
is trivial,
R^{1}(\tau_{0})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{0}}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb P}^{1}}
. Next we show that
R^{1}(\tau_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}
has a 0-dimensional associated point.

With
D_{1}
as in Section
9
,
\tau_{1}
is given by the pencil
|pD_{1},C|
. The general member is a smooth, elliptic curve, so
R^{1}(\tau_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}
is a coherent sheaf of generic rank 1. If it is torsion-free, then it is a line bundle. In particular,
H^{1}(pD_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{pD_{1}})=1
. Next we compute that
H^{1}(pD_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{pD_{1}})=2
, giving a contradiction, so
R^{1}(\tau_{1})_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}
is not torsion-free.

Consider the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{pD_{1}}\longrightarrow 0.

Pushing forward to
E
and using Claim
9.2
, we get

0\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{p}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\longrightarrow\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{pD_{1}}\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}(pD_{1})
factors through
{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}((p-1)D_{1})
, and
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{S_{1}}((p-1)D_{1})
gives that
F_{p}
summand. Thus the composite of
\alpha
with the second projection
\alpha_{E}\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\oplus F_{p}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
is zero. Therefore,
\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{pD_{1}}\cong F_{p-1}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
, and so
H^{1}(pD_{1},{\mathcal{O}}_{pD_{1}})=2
.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
g\colon Y\to X
be a birational morphism of normal, projective 3-folds. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
. Assume that
H^{i}(Y,g^{*}L^{-r})=0
for
i<3
and
r\gg 1
. Then
X
is CM if and only if
R^{1}g_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
does not have a 0-dimensional associated point.

Proof.

The Leray spectral sequence computing
H^{i}(Y,g^{*}L^{-r})
contains

H^{1}(X,L^{-r})\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow H^{1}(Y,g^{*}L^{-r})\quad\text{and}\quad H^{0}(X,L^{-r}\otimes R^{1}g_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y})\longrightarrow H^{2}(X,L^{-r})\longrightarrow H^{2}(Y,g^{*}L^{-r}).

Thus
H^{1}(X,L^{-r})=0
, and
H^{2}(X,L^{-r})=0
for
r\gg 1
if and only if
R^{1}g_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
has no 0-dimensional associated points. The rest follows from
[
KM98
, Corollary 5.72]
.
∎

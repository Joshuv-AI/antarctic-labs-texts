C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for a class of degenerate/singular fully nonlinear elliptic equations Thanks: This work was supported by NRF-2021R1A4A1027378.
Sumiya Baasandorj, Sun-Sig Byun, Ki-Ahm Lee, Se-Chan Lee

Abstract
. We establish an optimal C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for viscosity solutions of degenerate/singular fully nonlinear elliptic equations by finding minimal regularity requirements on the associated operator.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 35B65; Secondary 35J60, 35J70, 35D40.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we provide a unified way for proving Hölder regularity for the gradient of viscosity solutions to fully nonlinear elliptic equations of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi(x,|Du|)F(D^{2}u)=f(x)\quad\text{in}\quad B_{1},\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B_{1}\equiv B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\displaystyle n\geqslant 2
is the unit ball,
\displaystyle F:\mathcal{S}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a uniformly
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic operator in the sense of
(A1):
and
\displaystyle\Phi:B_{1}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a continuous map featuring a degeneracy and singularity for the gradient described as in
(A2):
. From a variational point of view, the fully nonlinear equation (
1.1
) is closely related to the energy functional

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle v\mapsto\int\limits_{B_{1}}\varphi(x,|Dv|)\,dx

for a integral density
\displaystyle\varphi:B_{1}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
in a way that the Euler-Lagrange equation corresposnding to the functional (
1.2
) forms an equation of type (
1.1
).
The functional in (
1.2
) is a highly general non-autonomous functional with Uhlenbeck structure including significant models such as
\displaystyle p-
, Orlicz-,
\displaystyle p(x)-
and double phase- growth and so on. Hölder continuity for the gradient of local minima of the functional (
1.2
) under suitable optimal assumptions has been investigated in
[
14
]
, where fundamental assumptions on the integral density function
\displaystyle\varphi
in (
1.2
) are that there exist constants
\displaystyle 1<p,q
such that the map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{p}}
is almost non-decreasing and the map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\varphi(x,t)}{t^{q}}
is almost non-increasing, see
[
14
, Definition 3.1]
. In this regard, our conditions on
\displaystyle\Phi
in (
1.1
) to be introduced in
(A2):
are absolutely reasonable. Let us present known regularity results for viscosity solutions of equations in the form of (
1.1
) as significant special cases of our problem.

1.

For
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p}
with
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=s(\Phi)=p>-1
in condition
(A2):
, fully nonlinear equations (
1.1
) with this type of
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)
have been studied in a series of papers. The authors of
[
2
]
proved the comparison principle and Liouville type theorems in the singular case
\displaystyle(-1<p<0)
, and showed the regularity and uniqueness of the first eigenfunction in
[
3
]
. Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci estimates and the Harnack inequality have been also obtained in
[
10
,
11
,
15
]
. In particular, the authors of
[
16
]
proved local Hölder continuity for the gradient of viscosity solutions of (
1.1
) in the degenerate case (
\displaystyle p\geqslant 0
). Moreover, the authors of
[
1
]
proved the optimality of Hölder regularity for the gradient of viscosity solutions for the same problem in
[
16
]
by showing that viscosity solutions are
\displaystyle C_{\loc}^{1,\beta}
with
\displaystyle\beta=\min\left\{\bar{\alpha},\frac{1}{p+1}\right\}
, where
\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}\in(0,1)
is the Hölder exponent coming from the Krylov-Safonov regularity for the homogeneous equation
\displaystyle F(D^{2}h)=0
.

2.

For
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}
with
\displaystyle-1<p,q
and
\displaystyle 0\leqslant a(\cdot)\in C(B_{1})
, the constants in
(A2):
can be determined as
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=\min\{p,q\}
and
\displaystyle s(\Phi)=\max\{p,q\}
. The author of
[
13
]
proved the local
\displaystyle C^{1,\beta}-
regularity of viscosity solutions of (
1.1
) for
\displaystyle 0\leqslant p\leqslant q
. Moreover, in this degenerate case, the sharpness of the local
\displaystyle C^{1,\beta}
-regularity estimates for bounded viscosity solutions is shown in
[
20
]
.

3.

For
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}
with
\displaystyle p(\cdot)\in C(B_{1})
,
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=\inf\limits_{x\in B_{1}}p(x)>-1
and
\displaystyle s(\Phi)=\sup\limits_{x\in B_{1}}p(x)
in
(A2):
,
\displaystyle C^{1,\beta}
-regularity of viscosity solutions has been studied in
[
7
]
. In this paper, we provide a novel way to prove Hölder continuity for the gradient of viscosity solutions of (
1.1
) for both degenerate/singular cases in the full generality.

4.

For
\displaystyle\Phi(x,t)=t^{p(x)}+a(x)t^{q(x)}
with functions
\displaystyle 0\leqslant a(\cdot)\in C(B_{1})
and
\displaystyle-1<p(\cdot),q(\cdot)
in
\displaystyle C(B_{1})
, the constants in
(A2):
are
\displaystyle i(\Phi)=\inf\limits_{x\in B_{1}}\{p(x),q(x)\}
and
\displaystyle s(\Phi)=\sup\limits_{x\in B_{1}}\{p(x),q(x)\}
. In
[
12
]
, local Hölder continuity for the gradient has been proved when
\displaystyle 0\leqslant p(\cdot)\leqslant q(\cdot)
.

For a variational point of these special cases we have discussed above, we refer to the recent survey paper
[
19

presenting important results in problems with nonstandard growth conditions. We also point out the very recent paper
[

dealing with viscosity solutions of an equation of the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Du|^{\beta(x,u,Du)}F(D^{2}u)=f(x)\quad\text{in}\quad B_{1},

\displaystyle 0<\beta_{m}\leqslant\beta(\cdot)\leqslant\beta_{M}

The main results of this paper read as follows.

2.1.
Notions and assumptions

Throughout the paper, we denote by
\displaystyle B_{r}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}
the open ball of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\displaystyle n\geqslant 2
centered at
\displaystyle x_{0}
with positive radius
\displaystyle r
. If the center is clear in the context, we shall omit the center point by writing
\displaystyle B_{r}\equiv B_{r}(x_{0})
. Also
\displaystyle B_{1}\equiv B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the unit ball. We shall always denote by
\displaystyle c
a generic positive constant, possible varying line to line, having dependecies on parameters using brackets, that is, for example
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,i(\Phi),\nu_{0})
means that
\displaystyle c
depends only on
\displaystyle n,i(\Phi)
and
\displaystyle\nu_{0}
. For a measurable map
\displaystyle g:\mathcal{B}\subset B_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle(N\geqslant 1)
with
\displaystyle\beta\in(0,1]
being a given number, we shall use the notation

\displaystyle\displaystyle[g]_{C^{0,\beta}(\mathcal{B})}:=\sup\limits_{x\neq y\in\mathcal{B}}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}},\quad[g]_{C^{0,\beta}}:=[g]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{1})}.

Now we state the main assumptions in the paper.

(A1):

The operator
\displaystyle F:\mathcal{S}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
in (
1.1
) is continuous and uniformly
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic in the sense that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda\text{tr}(N)\leqslant F(M)-F(M+N)\leqslant\Lambda\text{tr}(N)

holds with some constants
\displaystyle 0<\lambda\leqslant\Lambda
, whenever
\displaystyle M,N\in\mathcal{S}(n)
with
\displaystyle N\geqslant 0
, where we denote by
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(n)
to mean the set of
\displaystyle n\times n
real symmetric matrices.

(A2):

\displaystyle\Phi:B_{1}\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a continuous map satisfying the following properties:

1.:

There exist constants
\displaystyle s(\Phi)\geqslant i(\Phi)>-1
such that the map
\displaystyle\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\Phi(x,t)}{t^{i(\Phi)}}
is almost non-decreasing with constant
\displaystyle L\geqslant 1
in
\displaystyle(0,\infty)
and the map
\displaystyle\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\Phi(x,t)}{t^{s(\Phi)}}
is almost non-increasing with constant
\displaystyle L\geqslant 1
in
\displaystyle(0,\infty)
for all
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
.

2.:

There exists constants
\displaystyle 0<\nu_{0}\leqslant\nu_{1}
such that
\displaystyle\displaystyle\nu_{0}\leqslant\Phi(x,1)\leqslant\nu_{1}
for all
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
.

(A3):

The term
\displaystyle f
on the right hand side of (
1.1
) belongs to
\displaystyle C(B_{1})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{1})
.

The Pucci extremal operators
\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}:\mathcal{S}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M):=-\lambda\sum\limits_{\lambda_{k}>0}\lambda_{k}-\Lambda\sum\limits_{\lambda_{k}<0}\lambda_{k}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(M):=-\Lambda\sum\limits_{\lambda_{k}>0}\lambda_{k}-\lambda\sum\limits_{\lambda_{k}<0}\lambda_{k},

where
\displaystyle\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
are the eigenvalues of the matrix
\displaystyle M
. The
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-ellipticity of the operator
\displaystyle F
via the Pucci extremal operators can be formulated as

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(N)\leqslant F(M+N)-F(M)\leqslant P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(N)

for all
\displaystyle M,N\in\mathcal{S}(n)
.

In what follows, for any vector
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define a map
\displaystyle G_{\xi}:B_{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle G_{\xi}(x,p,M):=\Phi(x,|\xi+p|)F(M)-f(x)

under the assumptions prescribed in
(A1):
-
(A3):
.
Then we shall focus on viscosity solutions of the equation

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle G_{\xi}(x,Du,D^{2}u)=0\text{ in }B_{1}.

Now we give the definition of a viscosity solution
\displaystyle u
of the equation (
2.2
) as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A lower semicontinuous function
\displaystyle v
is called a viscosity supersolution of (
2.2
) if for all
\displaystyle x_{0}\in B_{1}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{2}(B_{1})
such that
\displaystyle v-\varphi
has a local minimum at
\displaystyle x_{0}
and
\displaystyle D\varphi(x_{0})\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle G_{\xi}(x_{0},D\varphi(x_{0}),D^{2}\varphi(x_{0}))\geqslant 0.

An upper semicontinuous function
\displaystyle w
is called is a viscosity subsolution of (
2.2
) if for all
\displaystyle x_{0}\in B_{1}
and
\displaystyle\varphi\in C^{2}(B_{1})
such that
\displaystyle w-\varphi
has a local maximum at
\displaystyle x_{0}
and
\displaystyle D\varphi(x_{0})\neq 0
, there holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle G_{\xi}(x_{0},D\varphi(x_{0}),D^{2}\varphi(x_{0}))\leqslant 0.

We say that
\displaystyle u\in C(B_{1})
is a viscosity solution of (
2.2
) if
\displaystyle u
is a viscosity supersolution and a subsolution simultaneously.

Also we recall a concept of superjet and subjet introduced in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle v:B_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an upper semicontinuous function and
\displaystyle w:B_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a lower semicontinuous function.

1.

A couple
\displaystyle(p,M)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n)
is a superjet of
\displaystyle v
at
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
if

\displaystyle\displaystyle v(x+y)\leqslant v(x)+\left\langle p,y\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle My,y\right\rangle+O(|y|^{2}).

2.

A couple
\displaystyle(p,M)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n)
is a subjet of
\displaystyle w
at
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
if

\displaystyle\displaystyle w(x+y)\geqslant w(x)+\left\langle p,y\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle My,y\right\rangle+O(|y|^{2}).

3.

A couple
\displaystyle(p,M)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n)
is a limiting superjet of
\displaystyle v
at
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
if there exists a sequence
\displaystyle\{x_{k},p_{k},M_{k}\}\rightarrow\{x,p,M\}
as
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
in a such way that
\displaystyle\{p_{k},M_{k}\}
is a superjet of
\displaystyle v
at
\displaystyle x_{k}
and
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}v(x_{k})=v(x)
.

4.

A couple
\displaystyle(p,M)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n)
is a limiting subjet of
\displaystyle w
at
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
if there exists a sequence
\displaystyle\{x_{k},p_{k},M_{k}\}\rightarrow\{x,p,M\}
as
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
in such a way that
\displaystyle\{p_{k},M_{k}\}
is a subjet of
\displaystyle v
at
\displaystyle x_{k}
and
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}w(x_{k})=w(x)
.

2.2.
Small regime

Here we verify that, for a viscosity solution
\displaystyle u
of (
2.2
), we are able to assume

(2.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\osc\limits_{B_{1}}u\leqslant 1\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}

for some constant
\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
small enough, and also
\displaystyle\nu_{0}=\nu_{1}=1
without loss of generality.
In order to consider the problem in a small regime as in (
2.3
), for a fixed ball
\displaystyle B_{R}(x_{0})\subset B_{1}
, we define
\displaystyle\bar{u}:B_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{u}(x):=\frac{u(x_{0}+Rx)}{K}

for positive constants
\displaystyle K\geqslant 1\geqslant R
to be determined later. It can be seen that
\displaystyle\bar{u}
is a viscosity solution of

(2.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{G}_{\bar{\xi}}(x,D\bar{u},D^{2}\bar{u}):=\bar{\Phi}(x,|\bar{\xi}+D\bar{u}|)\bar{F}(D^{2}\bar{u})-\bar{f}(x)=0,

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}(x,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\frac{\Phi\left(x_{0}+Rx,\frac{K}{R}t\right)}{\Phi\left(x_{0}+Rx,\frac{K}{R}\right)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{F}(M)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\frac{R^{2}}{K}F\left(\frac{K}{R^{2}}M\right),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{f}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\frac{R^{2}}{\Phi\left(x_{0}+Rx,\frac{K}{R}\right)K}f(x_{0}+Rx)\text{ and }\bar{\xi}:=\frac{R}{K}\xi.

Note that
\displaystyle\bar{F}
is still a uniformly
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic operator, the map
\displaystyle\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\bar{\Phi}(x,t)}{t^{i(\Phi)}}
is almost non-decreasing and the map
\displaystyle\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\bar{\Phi}(x,t)}{t^{s(\Phi)}}
is almost non-increasing with the same constants
\displaystyle L\geqslant 1
and
\displaystyle s(\Phi)\geqslant i(\Phi)>-1
as in
(A2):
, and
\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}(x,1)=1
for all
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
. Moreover, the assumption
(A2):
implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert\bar{f}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leqslant\frac{LR^{2+i(\Phi)}}{\nu_{0}K^{1+i(\Phi)}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leqslant\frac{L}{\nu_{0}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

By recalling
\displaystyle i(\Phi)>-1
and setting

\displaystyle\displaystyle K:=2\left(1+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\left[\frac{L}{\nu_{0}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\right]^{\frac{1}{1+i(\Phi)}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle R:=\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2+i(\Phi)}},

we see that
\displaystyle\bar{u}
solves the equation (
2.5
) in the same class as (
2.2
) under the small regime in (
2.3

\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geqslant 0
and
\displaystyle|\xi|>A_{0}
with
\displaystyle A_{0}\equiv A_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L,R)
, then
\displaystyle u
is Lipschitz continuous in
\displaystyle B_{R/2}
with the estimate

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,1}(B_{R/2})}\leqslant C_{dl}

for some constant
\displaystyle C_{dl}\equiv C_{dl}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L,R)
.

(R3):

If
\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geqslant 0
and
\displaystyle|\xi|\leqslant A_{0}
, then
\displaystyle u\in C^{0,\beta}(B_{R/2})
with the estimate

(3.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\beta}(B_{R/2})}\leqslant C_{ds},

where
\displaystyle\beta\equiv\beta(n,\lambda,\Lambda,R,A_{0})\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle C_{ds}\equiv C_{ds}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,R,A_{0})
.

Proof.

For the proof of
(R1):
and
(R2):
, it suffices to show that there exist positive constants
\displaystyle L_{1}
and
\displaystyle L_{2}
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\sup\limits_{x,y\in B_{R}}\left(u(x)-u(y)-L_{1}\omega(|x-y|)-L_{2}\left(|x-z_{0}|^{2}+|y-z_{0}|^{2}\right)\right)\leqslant 0

for every
\displaystyle z_{0}\in B_{R/2}
, where

(3.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\omega(t)=\begin{cases}t-\omega_{0}t^{\frac{3}{2}}&\mbox{if }t\leqslant t_{0}:=\left(\frac{2}{3\omega_{0}}\right)^{2},\\
\omega(t_{0})&\mbox{if }t\geqslant t_{0}.\end{cases}

We choose
\displaystyle\omega_{0}\in(0,2/3)
in such a way that
\displaystyle t_{0}\geqslant 1
. For instance, we take any constant
\displaystyle\omega_{0}\leqslant 1/3
. By the contradiction, suppose that there are no such positive constants
\displaystyle L_{1}
and
\displaystyle L_{2}
satisfying (
3.4
) for every
\displaystyle z_{0}\in B_{R/2}
. Then there exists a point
\displaystyle z_{0}\in B_{R/2}
so that
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}>0
for all numbers
\displaystyle L_{1}>0
and
\displaystyle L_{2}>0
. Now we define two auxiliary functions
\displaystyle\phi,\psi:\overline{B_{R}}\times\overline{B_{R}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\psi(x,y):=L_{1}\omega(|x-y|)+L_{2}\left(|x-z_{0}|^{2}+|y-z_{0}|^{2}\right)

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\phi(x,y):=u(x)-u(y)-\psi(x,y).

Let
\displaystyle(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\overline{B_{R}}\times\overline{B_{R}}
be a maximum point for
\displaystyle\phi
. Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\mathcal{L}>0

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{1}\omega(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)+L_{2}\left(|\bar{x}-z_{0}|^{2}+|\bar{y}-z_{0}|^{2}\right)\leqslant\osc\limits_{B_{1}}u\leqslant 1.

Now we select

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{2}:=\frac{64}{R^{2}}.

This choice of
\displaystyle L_{2}
ensures

(3.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\bar{x}-z_{0}|+|\bar{y}-z_{0}|\leqslant\frac{R}{4}\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|\leqslant\frac{R}{4}.

This means that the points
\displaystyle\bar{x}
and
\displaystyle\bar{y}
belong to the open ball
\displaystyle B_{R}
and also we are able to assume that
\displaystyle\bar{x}\neq\bar{y}
; otherwise
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\leqslant 0
clearly. The rest of the proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1.
We are in a position to apply Lemma
2.1
in order to ensure the existence of a limiting subjet
\displaystyle(\xi_{\bar{x}},X_{\delta})
of
\displaystyle u
at
\displaystyle\bar{x}
and a limiting superjet
\displaystyle(\xi_{\bar{y}},Y_{\delta})
of
\displaystyle u
at
\displaystyle\bar{y}
, where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{\bar{x}}:=D_{x}\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})=L_{1}\omega^{\prime}(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}+2L_{2}(\bar{x}-z_{0})

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{\bar{y}}:=-D_{y}\psi(\bar{x},\bar{y})=L_{1}\omega^{\prime}(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}-2L_{2}(\bar{y}-z_{0}),

such that matrices
\displaystyle X_{\delta}
and
\displaystyle Y_{\delta}
satisfy the matrix inequality

(3.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}X_{\delta}&0\\
0&-Y_{\delta}\end{pmatrix}\leqslant\begin{pmatrix}Z&-Z\\
-Z&Z\end{pmatrix}+(2L_{2}+\delta)I,

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}Z&:=L_{1}D^{2}(\omega(|\cdot|))(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\\
&=L_{1}\left[\frac{\omega^{\prime}(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}I+\left(\omega^{\prime\prime}(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)-\frac{\omega^{\prime}(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}\right)\frac{(\bar{x}-\bar{y})\otimes(\bar{x}-\bar{y})}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{2}}\right]\end{split}

and the constant
\displaystyle\delta>0
only depends on the norm of
\displaystyle Z
, which can be selected sufficiently small. Applying the inequality (
3.9
) for vectors of the form
\displaystyle(z,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, we find

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\langle(X_{\delta}-Y_{\delta})z,z\right\rangle\leqslant(4L_{2}+2\delta)|z|^{2}.

The last inequality yields that all the eigenvalues of the matrix
\displaystyle(X_{\delta}-Y_{\delta})
are not larger than
\displaystyle 4L_{2}+2\delta
. On the other hand, applying again (
3.9
) for the vector
\displaystyle\bar{z}:=\left(\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|},\frac{\bar{y}-\bar{x}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle(X_{\delta}-Y_{\delta})\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|},\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}\right\rangle&\leqslant\left(4L_{2}+2\delta+4L_{1}\omega^{\prime\prime}(|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|)\right)\left|\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}\right|^{2}\\
&=\left(4L_{2}+2\delta-\frac{6\omega_{0}L_{1}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{1/2}}\right)\left|\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}\right|^{2}\\
&\leqslant\left(4L_{2}+2\delta-6\omega_{0}L_{1}\right)\left|\frac{\bar{x}-\bar{y}}{|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|}\right|^{2},\end{split}

where we have used the definition of
\displaystyle\omega
in (
3.5
) together with
\displaystyle|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|\leqslant 1/4
in (
3.8
). So at least one eigenvalue of
\displaystyle(X_{\delta}-Y_{\delta})
is not larger than
\displaystyle 4L_{2}+2\delta-6\omega_{0}L_{1}
, where this quantity can be negative for large values of
\displaystyle L_{1}
. By the definition of the extremal Pucci operator, we see

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(X_{\delta}-Y_{\delta})&\geqslant-\lambda(4L_{2}+2\delta-6\omega_{0}L_{1})-\Lambda(n-1)(4L_{2}+2\delta)\\
&\geqslant-(\lambda+(n-1)\Lambda)(4L_{2}+2\delta)+6\omega_{0}\lambda L_{1}.\end{split}

From two viscosity inequalities and the uniform ellipticity, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(\bar{x},|\xi+\xi_{\bar{x}}|)F(X_{\delta})\leqslant f(\bar{x}),\quad\Phi(\bar{y},|\xi+\xi_{\bar{y}}|)F(Y_{\delta})\geqslant f(\bar{y})

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(X_{\delta})\geqslant F(Y_{\delta})+P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(X_{\delta}-Y_{\delta}).

Combining last three displays, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}6\omega_{0}\lambda L_{1}&\leqslant(\lambda+(n-1)\Lambda)(4L_{2}+2\delta)\\
&\quad+\frac{f(\bar{x})}{\Phi(\bar{x},|\xi+\xi_{\bar{x}}|)}-\frac{f(\bar{y})}{\Phi(\bar{y},|\xi+\xi_{\bar{y}}|)}.\end{split}

At this stage, we shall separate it into several cases depending on the quantity of
\displaystyle|\xi|
and the positiveness of
\displaystyle i(\Phi)
.

Step 2: Proof of
(R1):
.
Suppose
\displaystyle-1<i(\Phi)<0
and
\displaystyle\xi=0
. By triangle inequality
\displaystyle\eqref{HC:7}_{2}
, we observe that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi_{\bar{x}}|\leqslant L_{1}(1+\frac{3}{2}\omega_{0})+2L_{2}\leqslant\frac{7}{4}L_{1}

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi_{\bar{x}}|\geqslant L_{1}\left(1-\frac{3\omega_{0}}{2}|\bar{x}-\bar{y}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)-3L_{2}\geqslant\frac{3L_{1}}{4}-3L_{2}\geqslant 3L_{2}

for all
\displaystyle L_{1}\geqslant 8L_{2}
. In the exactly same way, we see

(3.13)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi_{\bar{y}}|\leqslant\frac{7}{4}L_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi_{\bar{y}}|\geqslant 2L_{2}

for all
\displaystyle L_{1}\geqslant 8L_{2}
. Then we have

(3.14)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{f(\bar{x})}{\Phi(\bar{x},|\xi_{\bar{x}}|)}\leqslant c\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{|\xi_{\bar{x}}|^{i(\Phi)}}\leqslant\frac{c}{L_{1}^{i(\Phi)}}

and

(3.15)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{-f(\bar{y})}{\Phi(\bar{y},|\xi_{\bar{y}}|)}\leqslant c\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{|\xi_{\bar{y}}|^{i(\Phi)}}\leqslant\frac{c}{L_{1}^{i(\Phi)}}

for a constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(i(\Phi),L)
. Using the last two displays in (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle 6\omega_{0}\lambda L_{1}\leqslant(\lambda+(n-1)\Lambda)(4L_{2}+2\delta)+\frac{c}{L_{1}^{i(\Phi)}}

for a constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L,R)
. Recalling
\displaystyle-1<i(\Phi)<0
and taking
\displaystyle L_{1}
large enough, depending only on
\displaystyle n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L
and
\displaystyle R
, we get a contradiction. Then the first part of the lemma is proved.

Step 3: Proof of
(R2):
.
We suppose that
\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geqslant 0
and
\displaystyle|\xi|>A_{0}
for a constant
\displaystyle A_{0}
to be determined in a moment. We set

(3.16)

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{0}:=\frac{35L_{1}}{2}

for
\displaystyle L_{1}>1
to be selected soon. This choice of
\displaystyle A_{0}
together with (
3.11
) and (
3.14
) leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi+\xi_{\bar{x}}|\geqslant A_{0}-\frac{A_{0}}{10}=\frac{9A_{0}}{10}\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi+\xi_{\bar{y}}|\geqslant\frac{9A_{0}}{10}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{f(\bar{x})}{\Phi(\bar{x},|\xi+\xi_{\bar{x}}|)}\leqslant c\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{|\xi+\xi_{\bar{x}}|^{i(\Phi)}}\leqslant\frac{c}{A_{0}^{i(\Phi)}}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{-f(\bar{y})}{\Phi(\bar{y},|\xi+\xi_{\bar{y}}|)}\leqslant c\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}}{|\xi+\xi_{\bar{y}}|^{i(\Phi)}}\leqslant\frac{c}{A_{0}^{i(\Phi)}}

for a constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(i(\Phi),L)
. Again using the last two displays in (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle 6\omega_{0}\lambda L_{1}\leqslant(\lambda+(n-1)\Lambda)(4L_{2}+2\delta)+\frac{c}{L_{1}^{i(\Phi)}}

for a constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L,R)
. By choosing
\displaystyle L_{1}
large enough, depending only on
\displaystyle n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L
and
\displaystyle R
, we have again a contradiction. Indeed, we have proved the second part of the lemma.

Step 4: Proof of
(R3):
.
Finally, we shall focus on proving
(R3):
. Suppose now
\displaystyle|\xi|\leqslant A_{0}
, where
\displaystyle A_{0}
has been determined in (
3.16
). We consider the operator

\displaystyle\displaystyle G_{\xi}(x,p,M):=\Phi(x,|\xi+p|)F(M)-f(x).

In fact,
\displaystyle G_{\xi}(x,p,M)
is uniformly elliptic, whenever
\displaystyle|p|>2A_{0}
. At this stage, we apply Theorem
2.1
to conclude the last part of the Lemma. The proof is complete.

4.
Approximation

Now we prove a key approximation lemma, which plays a crucial role in later arguments.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of (
2.2
) with
\displaystyle\osc\limits_{B_{1}}\leqslant 1
, where
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is arbitrarily given. Suppose
(A1):
-
(A3):
hold true for
\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geqslant 0
and
\displaystyle\nu_{0}=\nu_{1}=1
. Then, for any
\displaystyle\mu>0
, there exists a constant
\displaystyle\delta\equiv\delta(n,\lambda,\Lambda,i(\Phi),L,\mu)
such that if

(4.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leqslant\delta,

then one can find
\displaystyle h\in C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}(B_{3/4})
with the estimate
\displaystyle\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}(B_{3/4})}\leqslant c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
, for some
\displaystyle 0<\bar{\alpha}<1
, satisfying

(4.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert u-h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant\mu.

Proof.

By contradiction, we suppose the conclusion of the lemma fails. Then there exist
\displaystyle\mu_{0}>0
and sequences of
\displaystyle\{F_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
,
\displaystyle\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
,
\displaystyle\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and
\displaystyle\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a sequence of vectors
\displaystyle\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that

(C1) :

\displaystyle F_{k}\in C(\mathcal{S}(n),\mathbb{R})
is uniformly
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic,

(C2) :

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}\in C(B_{1}\times[0,\infty),[0,\infty))
such that the map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\Phi_{k}(x,t)}{t^{i(\Phi)}}
is almost non-decreasing and the map
\displaystyle t\mapsto\frac{\Phi(x,t)}{t^{s(\Phi)}}
is almost non-increasing with constant
\displaystyle L\geqslant 1
, and
\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x,1)=1
for all
\displaystyle x\in B_{1}
,

(C3):

\displaystyle f_{k}\in C(B_{1})
with
\displaystyle\left\lVert f_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leqslant\frac{1}{k}
and

(C4):

\displaystyle u_{k}\in C(B_{1})
with
\displaystyle\osc_{B_{1}}u_{k}\leqslant 1
solves the equation

(4.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x,|\xi_{k}+Du_{k}|)F_{k}(D^{2}u_{k})=f_{k}(x),

but

(4.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in B_{1/2}}|u_{k}(x)-h(x)|>\mu_{0}

for all
\displaystyle h\in C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}(B_{3/4})
and every
\displaystyle 0<\bar{\alpha}<1
.

The condition
(C1) :
implies that
\displaystyle F_{k}
converges to some uniformly
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic operator
\displaystyle F_{\infty}\in C(\mathcal{S}(n),\mathbb{R})
. Applying Lemma
3.1
,
\displaystyle u_{k}\in C^{0,\beta}_{\loc}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
for some
\displaystyle\beta\in(0,1)
. Using (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we have that the sequence
\displaystyle\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges to a function
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
locally uniformly in
\displaystyle B_{1}
. In particular, there holds that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle u_{\infty}\in C(B_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\osc\limits_{B_{1}}u_{\infty}\leqslant 1.

Now we prove that the limiting function
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution of the homogeneous equation

(4.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{\infty}(D^{2}u_{\infty})=0\quad\text{in}\quad B_{3/4}.

For this, first we verify that
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
is a viscosity supersolution. Let

\displaystyle\displaystyle p(x):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle M(x-y),x-y\right\rangle+\left\langle b,x-y\right\rangle+u_{\infty}(y)

be a quadratic polynomial touching
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
from below at a point
\displaystyle y\in B_{3/4}
. Without loss of generality, let us assume
\displaystyle|y|=u_{\infty}(y)=0
. Then there exists a sequence
\displaystyle x_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
such that
\displaystyle u_{k}-\varphi
has a local minimum at
\displaystyle x_{k}
. Observe that
\displaystyle D\varphi(x_{k})\rightarrow b
and
\displaystyle D^{2}\varphi(x_{k})\rightarrow M
. Since
\displaystyle u_{k}
is a viscosity solution of (
4.3
), we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x_{k},|\xi_{k}+D\varphi(x_{k})|)F_{k}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{k}))\geqslant f_{k}(x_{k}).

For the ease of presentation, from now on we shall consider several cases depending on the boundedness of sequence
\displaystyle\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
.

Case 1: Sequence
\displaystyle\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is unbounded.
In this case, we can assume
\displaystyle|\xi_{k}|\rightarrow\infty
(up to a subsequence). As a consequence, we can show (up to a subsequence) that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi_{k}+D\varphi(x_{k})|\leqslant|\xi_{k}|-|D\varphi(x_{k})|\geqslant|\xi_{k}|-(|b|+1)\geqslant 1,

which implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}F_{k}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{k}))\geqslant\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{f_{k}(x_{k})}{\Phi_{k}(x_{k},|\xi_{k}+D\varphi(x_{k})|)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geqslant-\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{L}{k|\xi_{k}+D\varphi(x_{k})|^{i(\Phi)}}=0,

where we have used
(C2) :
and (
4.7
).

Case 2: Sequence
\displaystyle\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
bounded

In the case we may assume
\displaystyle\xi_{k}\rightarrow\xi_{\infty}
(up to a subsequence). Therefore, for the case
\displaystyle|\xi_{\infty}+b|\neq 0
, in the exactly same way as in (
4.8
), we infer that
\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
. Then we focus on the case
\displaystyle|\xi_{\infty}+b|=0
. There are two possibilities as
\displaystyle|b|=|\xi_{\infty}|=0
or
\displaystyle b=-\xi_{\infty}
with
\displaystyle|b|,|\xi_{\infty}|>0
. In those scenarios, we prove that
\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
. By contradiction suppose

(4.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)<0.

From the uniformly ellipticity condition of
\displaystyle F_{\infty}
, the matrix
\displaystyle M
has at least one positive eigenvalue. Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}=E\oplus Q
, where
\displaystyle E=\text{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
is the space consisting of those eigenvectors corresponding to positive eigenvalues of
\displaystyle M
.

Case 3:
\displaystyle b=-\xi_{\infty}
with
\displaystyle|b|,|\xi_{\infty}|>0
.
Let
\displaystyle\gamma>0
and set

\displaystyle\displaystyle p_{\gamma}(x):=p(x)+\gamma|P_{E}(x)|=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Mx,x\right\rangle+\left\langle b,x\right\rangle+\gamma|P_{E}(x)|,

where
\displaystyle P_{E}
stands for the orthogonal projection on
\displaystyle E
. Since
\displaystyle u_{k}\rightarrow u_{\infty}
locally uniformly in
\displaystyle B_{1}
and
\displaystyle p(x)
touches
\displaystyle u_{\infty}(x)
from below at the origin, for
\displaystyle\gamma
small enough,
\displaystyle p_{\gamma}(x)
touches
\displaystyle u_{k}(x)
from below at a point
\displaystyle x_{k}^{\gamma}\in B_{r}
(
\displaystyle B_{r}
is a small neighborhood of the origin). Moreover, there holds that
\displaystyle x_{k}^{\gamma}\rightarrow x_{\infty}^{\gamma}
for some
\displaystyle x_{\infty}^{\gamma}
as
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
. At this point we consider two scenarios:
\displaystyle P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})=0
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
(up to a subsequence) or
\displaystyle P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})\neq 0
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
(up to a subsequence).

Scenario 1:
\displaystyle P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})=0
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
(up to a subsequence).
In this scenario, first we note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{p}_{\gamma}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Mx,x\right\rangle+\left\langle b,x\right\rangle+\gamma\left\langle e,P_{E}(x)\right\rangle

touches
\displaystyle u_{k}
from below at
\displaystyle x_{k}^{\gamma}
for every
\displaystyle e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. A straightforward computation gives us

\displaystyle\displaystyle D\bar{p}_{\gamma}(x_{k}^{\gamma})=Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma P_{E}(e)\quad\text{and}\quad D^{2}\bar{p}_{\gamma}(x_{k}^{\gamma})=M.

Now we select
\displaystyle e\in E\cap\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that
\displaystyle P_{E}(e)=e
. Therefore, by
\displaystyle u_{k}
being a viscosity solution of (
4.3
), we see

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma},|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma e|)F_{k}(M)\geqslant f_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma}).

We also notice that if
\displaystyle Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}=0
, then for
\displaystyle k
enough large, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b|\leqslant\gamma/2\quad\text{and}\quad 3\gamma/2\geqslant|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma e|\geqslant\gamma/2.

Therefore, combining the last two displays and using
(C2) :
together with
\displaystyle\gamma\ll 1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{k}(M)&\geqslant\frac{f_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma})}{\Phi_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma},|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma e|)}\\
&\geqslant\frac{-L|f_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma})|}{|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma e|^{s(\Phi)}}\geqslant-\frac{L}{k}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma}\right)^{s(\Phi)}.\end{split}

Letting
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
in the last display, we obtain
\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
. In the situation
\displaystyle|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}|>0
, we first look at the subcase
\displaystyle E\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
and choose
\displaystyle e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma P_{E}(e)|=|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma e|>0.

Therefore, for
\displaystyle k
large enough, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+\gamma e|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma e|>0\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi_{k}+b|\leqslant\frac{1}{8}|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma e|.

On the other hand, if
\displaystyle E\not\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we can find
\displaystyle e\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap E^{\perp}
so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma P_{E}(e)|=|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}|>0.

Again for
\displaystyle k
large enough, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|Mx_{k}^{\gamma}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}|\quad\text{and}\quad|\xi_{k}+b|\leqslant\frac{1}{8}|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}|.

As a consequence, using either (
4.10
) or (
4.11
), we see

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma P_{E}(e)|>\frac{1}{4}|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma P_{E}(e)|>0.

Again applying
(C2) :
and taking into account the last display, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}F_{k}(M)&\geqslant\frac{f_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma})}{\Phi_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma},|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma P_{E}(e)|)}\\
&\geqslant-\left(\frac{L}{|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma P_{E}(e)|^{i(\Phi)}}+\frac{L}{|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma P_{E}(e)|^{s(\Phi)}}\right)|f_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma})|\\
&\geqslant\frac{-L4^{s(\Phi)}}{k}\left(\frac{1}{|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma P_{E}(e)|^{i(\Phi)}}+\frac{1}{|Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\gamma P_{E}(e)|^{s(\Phi)}}\right).\end{split}

Again letting
\displaystyle k\rightarrow\infty
in the last display, we again arrive at
\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
.

Scenario 2:
\displaystyle P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})\neq 0
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
(up to a subsequence).
In this scenario, we note that
\displaystyle P_{E}(x)
is smooth and convex in a small neighborhood of
\displaystyle x_{k}^{\gamma}
. Let us denote

\displaystyle\displaystyle\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}:=\frac{P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})}{|P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})|}.

A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\displaystyle D(|P_{E}(\cdot)|)(x_{k}^{\gamma})=\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad D^{2}(P_{E}(|\cdot|))(x_{k}^{\gamma})=\frac{1}{|P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})|}\left(I-\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}\otimes\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}\right).

Hence, with
\displaystyle u_{k}
being a viscosity solution of (
4.3
), we have the following viscosity inequality

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma},|\xi_{k}+Mx_{k}^{\gamma}+b+\gamma\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}|)F_{k}\left(M+\frac{1}{|P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})|}\left(I-\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}\otimes\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}\right)\right)\geqslant f_{k}(x_{k}^{\gamma}).

Observing that
\displaystyle|\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}|=1
and letting
\displaystyle e:=\zeta_{k}^{\gamma}
, we can perform the same procedure as in the first scenario of
\displaystyle P_{E}(x_{k}^{\gamma})=0
by considering the cases of
\displaystyle Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}=0
and
\displaystyle Mx_{\infty}^{\gamma}\neq 0
. Finally, we conclude that
\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
when
\displaystyle b=-\xi_{\infty}\neq 0
, which contradicts to (
4.9
).

Case 4:
\displaystyle b=\xi_{\infty}=0
.
In fact, this case is much easier to handle. Since
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Mx,x\right\rangle
touches
\displaystyle u_{\infty}(x)
from below at the origin and
\displaystyle u_{k}\rightarrow u_{\infty}
locally uniformly, the function

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\gamma}(x):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Mx,x\right\rangle+\gamma|P_{E}(x)|

touches
\displaystyle u_{k}
from below at a point
\displaystyle\hat{x}_{k}^{\gamma}\in B_{r}
(
\displaystyle B_{r}
is a small neighborhood of the origin) for
\displaystyle\gamma>0
sufficiently small. Again the sequence
\displaystyle\{\hat{x}_{k}^{\gamma}\}
is uniformly bounded. As in
Case 3
, we analyze those two scenarios
\displaystyle P_{E}(\hat{x}_{k}^{\gamma})=0
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
(up to a subsequence) and
\displaystyle P_{E}(\hat{x}_{k}^{\gamma})\neq 0
for all
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
(up to a subsequence). All in all, we conclude
\displaystyle F_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
in this case.

Finally, taking into account all cases we have analyzed above, we have shown that
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
is a viscosity supersolution of (
4.6
). In order to prove that
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
is a viscosity subsolution of (
4.6
), we show that
\displaystyle-u_{\infty}
is a viscosity supersolution of
\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\infty}(D^{2}h)=0
, where
\displaystyle\hat{F}_{\infty}(M)=-F_{\infty}(-M)
is uniformly
\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)
-elliptic operator as well. Therefore,
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution of (
4.6
). From the regularity results of
[
8
, Chap. 5]
, we see
\displaystyle u_{\infty}\in C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}_{\loc}(B_{3/4})
for some
\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}\in(0,1)
. Moreover,
\displaystyle\left\lVert u_{\infty}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\bar{\alpha}}(B_{1/2})}\leqslant c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
via (
4.5
). So choosing
\displaystyle h:=u_{\infty}
in (
4.4
), we have a contradiction. The proof is complete.

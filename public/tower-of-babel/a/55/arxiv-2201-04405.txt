Reflection, transmission and surface susceptibility tensor in two-dimensional materials Preprint: APS/123-QED
Luca Dell’Anna 1, Yu He 1,2, Michele Merano 1,

Abstract
In a recent experiment, the out-of-plane surface susceptibility of a single-layer two-dimensional atom crystal in the visible spectrum has been measured. This susceptibility gives a measurable contribution to the reflectivity of two-dimensional materials. Here we provide a complete theoretical description of the reflective properties, considering incoming s s and p p polarized plane waves at any angle of incidence on the crystal, computing local, reflected and transmitted electromagnetic fields. We finally connect the microscopic polarizability to both the in-plane and the out-of-plane macroscopic surface susceptibilities.



I
INTRODUCTION

Two-dimensional (2D) crystals are the thinnest materials that can be produced
Novoselov et al. 2004
;
Novoselov et al. 2005
. They are composed of only one atomic plane like graphene or Boron-Nitride
Gorbachev et al. 2011
;
Wickramaratne et al. 2018
, or one molecular plane like transition metal dichalcogenides
Mak et al. 2010
;
Li et al. 2014a
. This planar configuration make them highly anisotropic in the direction perpendicular to the crystal plane. For instance, they can have in-plane macroscopic dimensions
Huang et al. 2015
;
Magda et al. 2015
;
Desai et al. 2016
;
Huang et al. 2020
while keeping an out-of-plane thickness of the order of one atom. This particular geometry suggests that also their optical response should be anisotropic, at least in the vertical direction to the crystal plane. Ab-initio calculations indeed predict this anisotropy for a 2D crystal
Trevisanutto et al. 2010
;
Guilhon et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2020
. Notwithstanding the enormous progresses in thin films and 2D materials optical characterization
Chang et al. 2014
;
Liu et al. 2014
;
Li et al. 2014b
;
Stenzel et al. 2015
;
Tompkins and Hilfiker 2015
;
Morozov and Kuno 2015
;
Jayaswal et al. 2018
;
Elliott et al. 2020
, it turns out that a measurement of the out-of-plane optical constants of a 2D crystal is still a difficult task
Nelson et al. 2010
.

These materials are usually deposited on a substrate whose optical response is added to that of the monolayer. This is enough to hide the contribution coming from the out-of-plane optical constants of the single-layer crystal
Nelson et al. 2010
. In thin-film optics, the sensitivity of a measurement to vertical anisotropy is dependent on the path length of the light through the film, which is extremely limited when we deal with these atomically thin crystals
Nelson et al. 2010
. As a consequence, optical experiments of monolayers, deposited on some substrate, have access only to the in-plane optical constants of the samples that are studied
Li et al. 2014a
;
Chang et al. 2014
;
Liu et al. 2014
;
Li et al. 2014b
;
Morozov and Kuno 2015
;
Blake et al. 2007
;
Jayaswal et al. 2018
;
Elliott et al. 2020
.

Only recently an experiment successfully measured the out-of-plane optical constants of 2D crystals, namely graphene and monolayer
\rm MoS_{2}

Xu et al. 2021
. The substrate contribution was eliminated by a complete immersion of these single-layer crystals in a transparent polymer. Experimental data say that the out-of-plane surface susceptibility (
\chi_{\bot}
) is a measurable and finite quantity different from the in-plane surface susceptibility (
\chi_{\|}
), while the out-of-plane surface conductivity was zero within the experimental errors.

One might expect that a 2D crystal with atomic thickness along the vertical direction has a vanishing
\chi_{\bot}
, being
\chi_{\bot}
a macroscopic quantity. Indeed some of the most cited papers about optics in 2D crystals assume a null
\chi_{\bot}

Falkovsky and Pershoguba 2007
;
Hanson 2008
;
Zhan et al. 2013
;
Merano 2016
. All the other papers describe the linear optical response of a monolayer assuming isotropy even in the vertical direction
Blake et al. 2007
;
Nelson et al. 2010
;
Li et al. 2014a
.
The aim of this work is to prove that
\chi_{\bot}
is indeed finite and different from
\chi_{\|}
in a rigorous analytical way.

We will start with a microscopic description of a 2D crystal, as done in Ref.
Dell’Anna and Merano 2016
, and then will connect it to the macroscopic one, by means of a procedure similar to that used in Ref.
Merano 2017
. The results in Refs.
Dell’Anna and Merano 2016
;
Merano 2017
were limited to normal incidence, therefore, they had no access to
\chi_{\bot}
.

r_{p}=\frac{ik\chi_{yy}\cos{\theta}}{ik\chi_{yy}\cos{\theta}+2}-\frac{ik\chi_{zz}\sin{\theta}\tan{\theta}}{ik\chi_{zz}\sin{\theta}\tan{\theta}+2}

(37)

We compute now the radiation-reaction electric field acting on the dipole at the origin along the
y
and
z
directions

\displaystyle E_{R_{y}}

\displaystyle=\frac{ikaC_{1}\alpha\cos\theta}{4\pi a^{3}}E_{loc,y}=-\frac{\eta J_{y}\cos\theta}{2}

(38)

and

\displaystyle E_{R_{z}}

\displaystyle=\frac{ikaC_{1}\alpha(\sin\theta)^{2}}{4\pi a^{3}\cos{\theta}}E_{loc_{z}}=-\frac{ik(\sin\theta)^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\cos{\theta}}P_{z}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tan\theta}{2\epsilon_{0}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}P_{z}=-\frac{\tan\theta}{2}J_{P_{z}}.

(39)

and we show that these macroscopic quantities correspond to the macroscopic fields generated by the oscillating dipoles. We can rewrite Eqs. (
35
) in terms of the electric fields

\begin{array}[]{
>{\displaystyle}r >{\displaystyle{}}l @{\hspace{1em}} | @{\hspace{1em}} >{\displaystyle}l }&E_{iy}+E_{ry\|}=E_{ty\|}\hfil\hskip 10.00002pt\vrule\hskip 10.00002pt&E_{iz}+E_{rz\bot}=E_{tz\bot}\\
&E_{iy}-E_{ry\|}=E_{ty\|}-2E_{R_{y}}\hfil\hskip 10.00002pt\vrule\hskip 10.00002pt&E_{iz}-E_{rz\bot}=E_{tz\bot}-2E_{R_{z}}\\
&\epsilon_{0}\chi_{yy}E_{ty\|}=P_{y}\hfil\hskip 10.00002pt\vrule\hskip 10.00002pt&\epsilon_{0}\chi_{zz}E_{ty\bot}={P_{z}}\\
\end{array}

(40)

which give the following solutions

\begin{array}[]{
>{\displaystyle}r >{\displaystyle{}}l @{\hspace{1em}} | @{\hspace{1em}} >{\displaystyle}l }&E_{ry\|}=E_{R_{y}}\hfil\hskip 10.00002pt\vrule\hskip 10.00002pt&E_{rz\bot}=E_{R_{z}}\\
&E_{ty\|}=E_{iy}+E_{R_{y}}\hfil\hskip 10.00002pt\vrule\hskip 10.00002pt&E_{tz\bot}=E_{iz}+E_{R_{z}}\\
&\chi_{yy}=\frac{P_{y}}{\epsilon_{0}(E_{iy}+E_{R_{y}})}\hfil\hskip 10.00002pt\vrule\hskip 10.00002pt&\chi_{zz}=\frac{P_{z}}{\epsilon_{0}(E_{iz}+E_{R_{z}})}\\

J_{P_{y}}
(
J_{P_{z}}
) generates a macroscopic electromagnetic field propagating along the
\hat{s}
and
\hat{s}_{r}
directions. We identify

(
E_{R_{z}}
) as the electric field component along the
y
-direction (
z
-direction) of this macroscopic field. From Eqs. (
III.2
)), we can write the first two equations on the left-hand-side of Eqs. (

) as

\displaystyle H_{r\|}

\displaystyle=-\frac{E_{R_{y}}}{\eta\cos{\theta}}=-H_{R\|}

\displaystyle H_{t\|}

\displaystyle=H_{i}+\frac{E_{R_{y}}}{\eta\cos{\theta}}=H_{i}+H_{R\|}

(42)

and the first two equations on the right-hand-side of Eqs. (
41
) as

\displaystyle H_{r\bot}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{R_{z}}}{\eta\sin{\theta}}=H_{R\bot}

\displaystyle H_{t\bot}

\displaystyle=H_{i}+\frac{E_{R_{z}}}{\eta\sin{\theta}}=H_{i}+H_{R\bot}

(43)

The full reflected field (see Fig.

H_{r}=-H_{R\|}+H_{R\bot}

(44)

IV
CONCLUSION

We have shown that a 2D crystal, microscopically composed by atoms with an isotropic polarizability
\alpha
, has both a macroscopic
\chi_{\|}
and a macroscopic
\chi_{\bot}
with
\chi_{\bot}\neq\chi_{\|}
and
\chi_{\bot}\neq 0
, in contrast to some theoretical models,
generally used to derive reflection and transmission coefficients for these materials,
which assume either a null
\chi_{\bot}

Hanson 2008
;
Falkovsky and Pershoguba 2007
;
Zhan et al. 2013
;
Merano 2016
or isotropy
Blake et al. 2007
;
Li et al. 2014a
;
Liu et al. 2014
;
Li et al. 2014b
;
Morozov and Kuno 2015
. Moreover we have confirmed that
\chi_{\bot}<\chi_{\|}
and that the ratio
\chi_{\bot}/\chi_{\|}
is compatible with experimental observations.

Our microscopic theory provides both the local and the radiation-reaction fields when an incoming electromagnetic plane wave shines on a 2D material. The radiation-reaction fields coincide with the macroscopic fields scattered by the crystal, and they contribute to the reflected and the transmitted radiations. This analysis allows us, then, to derive the complete set of Fresnel coefficients and their dependence on the angle of incidence.

In particular we describe the reflection of an incident
p
wave, as obtained by the superposition of the scattered electromagnetic fields due to an in-plane electric and an in-plane magnetic surface currents. The first current is related to a macroscopic surface polarization oscillating in the crystal plane, the second one to a macroscopic surface polarization oscillating perpendicularly to the crystal plane. Combining the boundary conditions for these two currents we provide a simple expression for the macroscopic electric fields along the
y
and
z
directions, which turn to be the sum of the related components of the incident field with the radiation-reaction field along the same directions.
For an
s
polarized wave, only an in-plane electric surface current is involved, so the reflected field is given by the scattered electromagnetic field due to only an in-plane current and the macroscopic electric field is the sum of the incident field plus the radiation reaction field.

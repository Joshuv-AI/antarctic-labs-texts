Introduction

This paper is concerned with projective representations of groups and Lie algebras of jets. Let
K
denote a compact simple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
and let
V
be a finite-dimensional real vector space. Then we consider the Fréchet-Lie group
G:=J_{0}^{\infty}(V;K)
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=J^{\infty}_{0}(V;\mathfrak{k})\cong\mathbb{R}\llbracket V^{\ast}\rrbracket\otimes\mathfrak{k}
. These consist of
\infty
-jets at
0\in V
of smooth
K
- and
\mathfrak{k}
-valued functions, respectively. We are interested in smooth projective unitary representations of
G
which satisfy either a so-called positive energy, or a KMS condition.

To describe these, let
P
be a finite-dimensional Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}
. Assume that there is a smooth action
\alpha
of
P
on
G
. A continuous projective unitary representation
\overline{\rho}:G\rtimes_{\alpha}P\to\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
is of
positive energy
at the cone
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
if for every
p\in\mathcal{C}
, there is a strongly continuous homomorphic lift
t\mapsto e^{itH}
of
t\mapsto\overline{\rho}(e^{tp})
whose generator
H
satisfies
\Spec(H)\geq 0
. We say that
\overline{\rho}
is KMS at
p\in\mathfrak{p}
if there is a normal state
\phi
on the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}:=\overline{\rho}(G)^{\prime\prime}
generated by
\overline{\rho}(G)
such that
\phi
satisfies the KMS condition for the one-parameter group
\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}),\;t\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}(\overline{\rho}(e^{tp}))
. As we shall see, these two seemingly very different classes of representations exhibit similar behavior in certain respects. In particular, they both give rise to so-called
generalized positive energy representations
, a notion which relaxes the positive energy condition and is introduced in Section
4
below. We study these generalized positive energy representations and thereby also those which satisfy either the positive energy or the KMS condition.

The motivation for looking at the positive energy and KMS representations of the group
G\rtimes_{\alpha}P
originates in prior work by B. Janssens and K.-H. Neeb, who studied in
[
34
]
a class of projective unitary representations of the group of compactly supported gauge transformations
\mathcal{G}:=\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathrm{Ad}(\mathcal{K}))
of a principal
K
-bundle
\mathcal{K}
over
M
, where
\mathrm{Ad}(\mathcal{K})
denotes the corresponding adjoint bundle. Suppose that the Lie group
P
acts smoothly on
\mathcal{K}
by automorphisms of the principal bundle
\mathcal{K}
. This induces a smooth action of
P
on the infinite-dimensional Lie group
\mathcal{G}
. Their main result is:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[

):

Let

be a projective unitary representation of

has no fixed points in

Thus,

Thus, let

Structure of the paper

The paper is divided in two parts. In Part
I
, we introduce both the (generalized) positive energy and the KMS condition. We start in Section
2
by briefly recalling the relation between continuous projective unitary representations, central extensions and the second Lie algebra cohomology
H^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{R})
. We move on to consider positive energy representations in Section
3
and discuss some of their properties. In Section
4
we relax the positive energy condition and introduce the so-called
generalized positive energy
condition. We show that for a projective unitary representation which of is generalized positive energy, its kernel is related to a particular quadratic form canonically associated to the corresponding class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{R})
. This observation will play a key role in Part
II
. In Section
5
, we briefly recall the modular theory of von Neumann algebras and then proceed to define KMS representations. We consider a number of examples and discuss some of their properties, in particular making the important observation that KMS representations give rise to generalized positive energy ones. To some extent, this unifies the positive energy and KMS conditions, allowing their simultaneous study. We remark also that Part
I
is formulated in the general context of possibly infinite-dimensional locally convex Lie groups, which is the appropriate context within the larger program that studies the projective unitary representations of gauge groups.

In Part
II
, we return to the Fréchet-Lie group
G:=J^{\infty}_{a}(V;K)
of
\infty
-jets at
0\in V
. After fixing our notation, we discuss in Section
7
a normal-form problem for the
\mathfrak{p}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}=J^{\infty}_{a}(V;\mathfrak{k})
. Using the general observations made in Part
I
together with the normal-form results obtained in Section
7
, we proceed in Section
8
with the study of (generalized) positive energy representations of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\rtimes_{D}\mathfrak{p}
, where
D:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the
\mathfrak{p}
-action on
\mathfrak{g}
corresponding to
\alpha
.

Overview of main results

Let us describe the main results of Section

. We will first need to introduce some notation. We write

for the Lie algebra of formal vector fields on

vanishing at the origin. The

-action

For any
p\in\mathfrak{p}
, the formal vector field
\bm{v}(p)
splits further as
\bm{v}(p)=\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l}}({p})+\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ho}}({p})

and its higher order part
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ho}}({p})

vanishing up to first order at the origin. Let

be the constant part of the formal power series
\sigma(p)
. Let

denote the additive subsemigroup of

generated by

is a subset, define

be its annihilator in

. If

is a continuous projective unitary representation of
\mathfrak{g}\rtimes_{D}\mathfrak{p}

be the set of all points

for which

is of generalized positive energy at

. Write

In the following, the category of infinite-dimensional manifolds and smooth maps between them is defined in the Michal-Bastiani sense

Definition 2.1
.

—

A (projective) unitary representation of
G
is said to be continuous if it is so w.r.t. the strong operator topology on
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
.

—

Let
(\rho,\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a unitary representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
. A vector
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
is called

if the orbit map
G\to\mathcal{H}_{\rho},\;g\mapsto\rho(g)\psi
is smooth. Denote by

the subspace of smooth vectors. The representation

is called smooth if
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
.

—

Let
(\overline{\rho},\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a projective unitary representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
. A ray

is called

if the orbit map

\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})^{\infty}
the subspace of smooth rays. The projective representation
\overline{\rho}
is called smooth if
\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})^{\infty}

.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is a complex vector space, denote by

.

If

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D}):=\set{X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{D})\st\mathcal{D}\subseteq\dom(X^{\ast})\text{ and }X^{\ast}\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{D}}.

For

If

Let

—

A

Remark 2.4
.

Any unitary
G
-representation on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
defines a unitary

-representation

d\rho(\xi)\psi:=\left.d\over dt\right|_{t=0}\rho(e^{t\xi})\psi

as unbounded operators defined on the invariant domain

-representations on
\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty})
by differentiation at the identity. If

is finite-dimensional, then
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty}
is dense in

for any continuous unitary representation

A continuous projective unitary representation
\overline{\rho}:G\to\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
is equivalently given by a continuous central

-extension
\accentset{\circ}{G}
together with a unitary representation
\rho:\accentset{\circ}{G}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})

\accentset{\circ}{G}
is the pull-back of the central
\mathbb{T}
-extension

along
\overline{\rho}
. We say that
\rho

lifts

\overline{\rho}
. Suppose
\overline{\rho}_{1}
and
\overline{\rho}_{2}
are two projective unitary representations, inducing by pull-back the lifts
\rho_{1}:\accentset{\circ}{G}_{1}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{1}})

\rho_{2}:\accentset{\circ}{G}_{2}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{1}})

\overline{\rho}_{1}
and
\overline{\rho}_{2}
are unitarily equivalent if and only if there is an isomorphism

\rho_{2}(\Phi(x))=U\rho_{1}(x)U^{-1}

-representation

can be lifted to a unitary representation

of
\mathfrak{g}
. The continuous central extensions of

Remark 2.5

In the literature, one encounters the notion of the

. We furthermore have
\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})^{\infty}=\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty})
by
[
33
, Thm. 4.3]
.

3
Positive Energy Representations

Let us introduce the class of positive energy representations. After defining the notion, some immediate consequences are considered that will be relevant in Part
II
. Let
G
be a regular locally convex Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{D}
is a pre-Hilbert space and
X\in\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D})
satisfies
X^{\dagger}=X
, we write
X\geq c
if
\langle\psi,X\psi\rangle\geq c\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}
for every
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a convex cone and
\mathcal{D}
be a pre-Hilbert space.

—

A continuous unitary representation
\pi
of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{D}
is said to be of
positive energy
(p.e.) at
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
if
-i\pi(\xi)\geq 0
. It is of p.e. at
\mathcal{C}
if it is of p.e. at every
\xi\in\mathcal{C}
. Write
\mathcal{C}(\pi)=\set{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\st\pi\text{ is of p.e.\ at }\xi}
.

—

Let
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
be a continuous projective unitary representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{D}
with lift
\pi:\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{D})
. Then
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
is of p.e. at
\xi
if there exists
\accentset{\circ}{\xi}\in\mathcal{C}(\pi)\subseteq\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
covering
\xi
. Write
\mathcal{D}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})
for the set of all such
\xi
. We say that
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
is of p.e. at
\mathcal{C}
if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})
.

—

A smooth (projective) unitary representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
is said to be of p.e. at
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
if the corresponding derived (projective) unitary representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty}
is so. It is said to be of p.e. at
\mathcal{C}
if it is so at every
\xi\in\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\rho
be a smooth unitary representation of
G
. Then
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}(d\rho)
is always a closed,
G
-invariant convex cone. Consequently,
\mathcal{C}\cap-\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}
are ideals in
\mathfrak{g}
, called the
edge
and
span
of
\mathcal{C}
, respectively. If
\xi\in\mathcal{C}\cap-\mathcal{C}
then
\xi\in\ker d\rho
, so by passing to the quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\ker d\rho
one may always achieve that
\mathcal{C}
is pointed.

Next, we define the notion of a semibounded representation.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\rho:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a smooth unitary
G
-representation. Define its
momentum set
by:

I_{\rho}:=\conv\set{\xi\mapsto\langle\psi,-id\rho(\xi)\psi\rangle\st\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty},\;\lVert\psi\rVert=1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}.

The representation
\rho
is said to be
semibounded
if
W_{\rho}
contains an interior point, where

W_{\rho}:=\set{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\st\inf\Spec(-i\overline{d\rho(\xi)})>-\infty}.

Remark 3.4
.

For finite-dimensional Lie groups, the class of semibounded representations has been subject to detailed study in
[
47
]
. In particular, they are highest weight representations
[
47
, Def. X.2.9, Thm. X.3.9]
. For a consideration of semibounded representations in the context of infinite-dimensional Lie groups, we refer to
[
52
]
and
[
51
]
.

In the finite-dimensional context, the semiboundedness condition turns out to be extremely restrictive, which in turn has consequences for arbitrary positive energy representations. The following result, Theorem
3.5
, is based on the results in the monograph
[
47
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that
G
is connected and locally exponential. Take
\bm{d}\in\mathfrak{g}
and let
\mathfrak{a}=\langle\bm{d}\rangle\triangleleft\mathfrak{g}
be the closed ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\bm{d}
. Assume that
\dim(\mathfrak{a})<\infty
and that
\mathfrak{a}
is stable, in the sense that
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. Let
A\triangleleft G
be a connected normal Lie subgroup integrating
\mathfrak{a}
. Let
(\rho,\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a smooth unitary
G
-representation which is of p.e. at
\bm{d}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Write
\mathfrak{h}:=\mathfrak{a}/\ker d\rho
. The following assertions hold:

1.

\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}
, where
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is the closed
G
-invariant convex cone in
\mathfrak{g}
generated by
\bm{d}
.

2.

The closure of
\mathcal{C}+\ker d\rho
in
\mathfrak{h}
is a pointed, closed, generating and
G
-invariant convex cone. Thus
\mathcal{C}\cap-\mathcal{C}\subseteq\ker d\rho
.

3.

\left.\rho\right|_{A}
is semibounded.

4.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
denote the maximal nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{h}
. Then
[\mathfrak{h}_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{n}]\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
. Moreover, there exists a reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
such that
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{l}
.

5.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
denote the maximal nilpotent ideal of
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
[\mathfrak{a},[\mathfrak{a}_{n},\mathfrak{a}_{n}]]\subseteq\ker d\rho
.

Proof.

For the first point, let
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
be the closure of
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. As
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
is a closed ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
containing
\bm{d}
, we have
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
. On the other hand, we know that
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}(\bm{d})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
because
\mathfrak{a}
is stable. Thus
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
and hence
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. So
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}
. In particular
\dim(\mathfrak{a}^{\prime})<\infty
and so
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{a}
. Next we prove the second statement. Take
\xi\in(\overline{\mathcal{C}+\ker d\rho})\cap-(\overline{\mathcal{C}+\ker d\rho})
. Then
d\rho(\xi)\geq 0
and
d\rho(\xi)\leq 0
, in view of Remark
3.2
, and hence
\Spec(\overline{d\rho(\xi)})=\{0\}
. As
d\rho(\xi)
is essentially skew-adjoint, it follows that
\xi\in\ker d\rho
. Thus
\overline{\mathcal{C}+\ker d\rho}
is pointed in
\mathfrak{h}
. As
\mathcal{C}
is
G
-invariant and convex, it is clear that the same holds for the cone
\overline{\mathcal{C}+\ker d\rho}
in
\mathfrak{h}
. The latter is also generating in
\mathfrak{h}
because
\mathfrak{a}=\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{C}
. Next we show that
\left.\rho\right|_{A}
is semibounded. As
\mathfrak{a}
is spanned by
\mathcal{C}
and
\dim\mathfrak{a}<\infty
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
has interior points. As
\mathcal{C}\subseteq W_{\rho}
, this implies that
W_{\rho}
has interior points. Hence
\left.\rho\right|_{A}
is semibounded. For the remaining points, we use the results in
[
47
]
. We first show that
\mathfrak{h}
is
admissible
, in the sense of
[
47
, Def. VII.3.2]
. Using the second point, the convex cone
(\overline{\mathcal{C}+\ker d\rho})\oplus\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
in
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{R}
is closed, pointed, generating and
\mathrm{Inn}(\mathfrak{h})
-invariant. By
[
47
, Lem. VII.3.1, Def. VII.3.2]
this implies that
\mathfrak{h}
is admissible. By
[
47
, Thm. VII.3.10]
, it follows that
[\mathfrak{h}_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{n}]\subseteq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
and that
\mathfrak{h}
contains a compactly embedded Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
(where as in
[
47
, Def. VII.1.1]
, a subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
is called
compactly embedded
if
\overline{\langle e^{\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{t})}\rangle}
is compact in
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
). Using
[
47
, Lem. VII.2.26(iv)]
, we obtain that there exists some reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{l}
with
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{l}
. Since
[\mathfrak{h},[\mathfrak{h}_{n},\mathfrak{h}_{n}]]=0
and
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{a}/\ker d\rho
, it follows in particular that
[\mathfrak{a},[\mathfrak{a}_{n},\mathfrak{a}_{n}]]\subseteq\ker d\rho
.
∎

For projective p.e. representations, this leads to:

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
G
,
\bm{d},\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
be as Theorem
3.5
. Let
(\overline{\rho},\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a smooth projective unitary representation of
G
. Suppose that
\overline{\rho}
is of p.e. at
\bm{d}\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
[\mathfrak{a},[\mathfrak{a}_{n},\mathfrak{a}_{n}]]\subseteq\ker d\overline{\rho}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho:\accentset{\circ}{G}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be the lift of
\overline{\rho}
to a central
\mathbb{T}
-extension
\accentset{\circ}{G}
of
G
. Let
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}:=\Lie(\accentset{\circ}{G})
. There exists some
\accentset{\circ}{\bm{d}}\in\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
s.t.
d\rho
is of p.e. at
\accentset{\circ}{\bm{d}}\in\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}
denote the ideal in
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
generated by
\accentset{\circ}{\bm{d}}
and let
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}
denote the maximal nilpotent ideal in
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}
. Then
d\rho([\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}},[\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}]])=\{0\}
by Theorem
3.5
. Thus
d\overline{\rho}([\mathfrak{a},[\mathfrak{a}_{n},\mathfrak{a}_{n}]])=\{0\}
, where we used that the quotient map
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{g}
projects
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}
and
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}
onto
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
, respectively.
∎

The following simple lemma will also be useful.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
\dim(G)<\infty
. Let
\overline{\rho}:G\to\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a continuous projective unitary representation of
G
which is of p.e. at every element of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\overline{\rho}
is continuous w.r.t. the norm-topology on
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
.

Proof.

Let
d\rho:\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty})
be the lift of
d\overline{\rho}
. Identify
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus_{\omega}\mathfrak{g}
for some
2
-cocycle
\omega:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
. The assumptions imply that for every
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
there exists
E_{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}
s.t.
-id\rho(\xi)\geq E_{\xi}
. As this holds in particular for both
\xi
and
-\xi
,
d\rho(\xi)
is a bounded operator for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. As
\dim(\mathfrak{g})<\infty
, one finds by choosing a basis
(e_{\mu})
of
\mathfrak{g}
that there exists
C>0
s.t.
\lVert d\rho(\xi)\rVert\leq C\lVert\xi\rVert
where
\lVert\xi\rVert:=\sup_{\mu}\lvert\xi_{\mu}\rvert
if
\xi=\sum_{\mu}\xi_{\mu}e_{\mu}
. Thus
\xi\mapsto d\rho(\xi)
is norm-continuous. This implies norm-continuity of
\overline{\rho}
because
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho}),T\mapsto e^{T}
is norm-continuous and
\overline{\rho}(\exp(\xi))=[e^{d\rho(\xi)}]\in\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.
∎

4
Generalized Positive Energy Representations

Let
G
denote a regular locally convex Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The class of p.e. representations can be generalized by relaxing the condition
-id\rho(\xi)\geq 0
in Definition
3.1
. We define a suitable relaxed notion, the
generalized positive energy condition
, and show that it can still be very restrictive. In Section
5
, we will encounter a class of representations which are not of p.e. but are of generalized positive energy.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a pre-Hilbert space with Hilbert space completion
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a locally convex topological Lie algebra.

—

A continuous unitary representation
\pi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{D})
is of
generalized positive energy
(g.p.e.) at
\xi\in\mathfrak{h}
if there exists a
1
-connected Lie group
H
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
and a dense subspace
\mathcal{D}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
such that

\forall\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{0}\st E_{\psi}(\pi,\xi):=\inf_{h\in H}\langle\psi,-i\pi(\mathrm{Ad}_{h}(\xi))\psi\rangle>-\infty.

(1)

We write
\mathfrak{C}(\pi):=\set{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\st\pi\text{ is of g.p.e.\ at }\xi}
. If
\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, we say that
\pi
is of g.p.e. at
\mathfrak{C}
if
\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}(\pi)
.

—

Let
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{pu}(\mathcal{D})
be a continuous projective unitary representation of
\mathfrak{h}
on
\mathcal{D}
with lift
\pi:\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{h}}\to\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{D})
. Let
\mathfrak{C}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
denote the image of
\mathfrak{C}(\pi)\subseteq\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{h}}
under the quotient map
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{h}}\to\mathfrak{h}
. Then
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
is said to be of generalized positive energy at
\xi\in\mathfrak{h}
if
\xi\in\mathfrak{C}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})
. Similarly, we say it is of g.p.e. at
\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}
if
\mathfrak{C}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})
.

—

Let
\rho:G\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a smooth unitary representation of
G
. Then
\rho
is said to be of g.p.e. at
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
if its derived representation
d\rho
on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}^{\infty}
is so.

—

Let
\overline{\rho}:G\to\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a smooth projective unitary representation of
G
with lift
\rho:\accentset{\circ}{G}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
. Let
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
be the Lie algebra of
\accentset{\circ}{G}
. Then
\overline{\rho}
is of g.p.e. at
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
if
\rho
is of g.p.e. at some
\accentset{\circ}{\xi}\in\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
covering
\xi
.

Remark 4.2
.

If
\pi
is a (projective) continuous unitary representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, then the set
\mathfrak{C}(\pi)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is always an
\mathrm{Ad}_{G}
-invariant cone.

An important observation for the class of g.p.e. representations is the following one:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{D})
be a continuous unitary representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on the pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{C}(\pi)
. Suppose that
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]\in\mathfrak{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then for every
\psi
in some dense subspace
\mathcal{D}_{0}
we have:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle\psi,-i\pi([[\xi,\eta],\eta])\psi\rangle,

(2)

\displaystyle\langle\psi,-i\pi([\xi,\eta])\psi\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle\psi,-i\pi([[\xi,\eta],\eta])\psi\rangle\bigg(\langle\psi,-i\pi(\xi)\psi\rangle-E_{\psi}(\pi,\xi))\bigg).

In particular, if
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]=0
then
\pi([\xi,\eta])=0
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
be a dense subspace for which (
1
) is valid. Let
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. Then
\langle\psi,-i\pi(e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\eta}}\xi)\psi\rangle\geq E_{\psi}(\pi,\xi)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]\in\mathfrak{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
, the third derivative
\gamma^{(3)}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathfrak{g}
of the smooth path
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathfrak{g},\;t\mapsto e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\eta}}\xi
vanishes. From Taylor’s formula (which holds for smooth maps between locally convex vector spaces by
[
49
, Prop. I.2.3]
), it follows that
e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\eta}}\xi=\xi+t[\eta,\xi]+{t^{2}\over 2}[[\xi,\eta],\eta]
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus

\big\langle\psi,-i\pi(\xi)\psi\rangle+t\langle\psi,-i\pi([\eta,\xi])\psi\rangle+{t^{2}\over 2}\langle\psi,-i\pi([[\xi,\eta],\eta])\psi\rangle\geq E_{\psi}(\pi,\xi),\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}

The equations (
2
) follows from the fact that
at^{2}+bt+c\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
a,c\geq 0
and
b^{2}\leq 4ac
. In particular, if
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]=0
then
\langle\psi,-i\pi([\xi,\eta])\psi\rangle=0
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{0}
. As
\mathcal{D}_{0}
is a complex vector space, this implies by the polarization identity that
\pi([\xi,\eta])=0
.
∎

In the projective context, this sets up a relation between
\ker\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
and the class
[\omega]\in H_{\mathrm{ct}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
defined by the corresponding central
\mathbb{R}
-extension
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. This is exploited in Section
8
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
be a continuous projective unitary
\mathfrak{g}
-representation on the pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{D}
with lift
\pi:\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{D})
for some continuous central
\mathbb{R}
-extension
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\omega
represent the corresponding class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{R})
. Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{C}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})
. Suppose that
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfies
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]=0
. Then
\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)=0\iff\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}([\xi,\eta])=0.

Proof.

Identify
\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\mathbb{R}\oplus_{\omega}\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\accentset{\circ}{\xi}\in\mathfrak{C}(\pi)
and
\accentset{\circ}{\eta}\in\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}}
be lifts of
\xi
and
\eta
, respectively. We have that
[[\accentset{\circ}{\xi},\accentset{\circ}{\eta}],\accentset{\circ}{\eta}]=\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)\in\mathfrak{Z}(\accentset{\circ}{\mathfrak{g}})
, because
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]=0
. Using Lemma
4.3
it follows that
\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)\geq 0
. If
\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)=0
, then
[[\accentset{\circ}{\xi},\accentset{\circ}{\eta}],\accentset{\circ}{\eta}]=0
and so Lemma
4.3
implies that
\pi([\accentset{\circ}{\xi},\accentset{\circ}{\eta}])=0
. Hence
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}([\xi,\eta])=0
. Conversely, if
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}([\xi,\eta])=0
, then
i\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)=[\pi([\xi,\eta]),\pi(\eta)]-\pi([[\xi,\eta],\eta])=0
, because
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]=0
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Notice in the setting of Proposition
4.4
that whenever
[[\xi,\eta],\eta]=0
, the value of
\omega([\xi,\eta],\eta)
does not depend on the choice of representative
\omega
of the class
[\omega]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathbb{R})
.

In Part
II
, a particular special case of Proposition
4.4
is used extensively:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{g}
be locally convex Lie algebras. Let
D:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g})
be a homomorphism for which the corresponding action
\mathfrak{p}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is continuous. Let
\mathcal{D}
be a complex pre-Hilbert space and let
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}:\mathfrak{g}\rtimes_{D}\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{pu}(\mathcal{D})
be a continuous projective unitary representation of
\mathfrak{g}\rtimes_{D}\mathfrak{p}
on
\mathcal{D}
. Let
[\omega]\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathfrak{g}\rtimes_{D}\mathfrak{p};\mathbb{R})
be the corresponding class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{ct}}(\mathfrak{g}\rtimes_{D}\mathfrak{p};\mathbb{R})
. Let
\eta\in\mathfrak{g}
,
p\in\mathfrak{C}(\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}})\cap\mathfrak{p}
and assume that
[D(p)\eta,\eta]=0
. Then
\omega(D(p)\eta,\eta)\geq 0
and
\omega(D(p)\eta,\eta)=0\iff\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}(D(p)\eta)=0
.

5
KMS Representations

In the following, we introduce the class of KMS representations. We will see in particular that these give rise to generalized positive energy representations. Consequently, they can be studied using the results of Section
4
. Its definition makes use of the modular theory of von Neumann algebras, which we recall first.

5.1
Modular Theory of von Neumann Algebras

Let us recall the modular condition and the notion of a KMS state on a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, whilst fixing our conventions and notation. We refer to
[
62
, Ch. VIII]
,
[
12
, Ch. 2.5]
and
[
13
, Ch. 5.3]
for a detailed consideration of the modular theory of von Neumann algebras and of KMS states.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a von Neumann algebra, write
\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
for its pre-dual, equipped with the
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\mathcal{M})
-topology. Write
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\ast}
for the set of normal states on
\mathcal{M}
. Further, if
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
, write
\pi_{\phi}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
for the GNS-representation of
\mathcal{M}
relative to
\phi
. Write
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}:=\pi_{\phi}(\mathcal{M})^{\prime\prime}
. Let
\Omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
denote the canonical cyclic vector satisfying
\phi(x)=\langle\Omega_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi}\rangle
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Whenever
\Omega_{\phi}
is separating for
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
, let
S_{\phi}
denote the unique closed conjugate-linear operator satisfying
S_{\phi}x\Omega_{\phi}=x^{\ast}\Omega_{\phi}
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
. Let
S_{\phi}=J_{\phi}\Delta_{\phi}^{1\over 2}
be its polar decomposition, where the operators
\Delta_{\phi}
and
J_{\phi}
are positive and anti-unitary, respectively.

Definition 5.1
.

A map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is said to be
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\mathcal{M})
-continuous if for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
, the map
\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{M},\;t\mapsto\sigma_{t}(x)
is continuous w.r.t. the
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\mathcal{M})
-topology on
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
be a normal state. Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
be a one-parameter group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
. Define
\mathrm{St}:=\set{z\st z\in\mathbb{C},\;0<\mathrm{Im}(z)<1}
.

—

\phi
is said to satisfy the
modular condition
for
\sigma
if the following two conditions are satisfied:

1.

\phi=\phi\circ\sigma_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

For every
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
, there exists a bounded continuous function
F_{x,y}:\overline{\mathrm{St}}\to\mathbb{C}
which is holomorphic on
\mathrm{St}
and s.t. for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle F_{x,y}(t)

\displaystyle=\phi(\sigma_{t}(x)y),

\displaystyle F_{x,y}(t+i)

\displaystyle=\phi(y\sigma_{t}(x)).

—

\phi
is said to be
KMS
w.r.t.
\sigma
at inverse temperature
\beta>0
if it satisfies the modular condition for
t\mapsto\sigma_{-\beta t}
. In that case, we also say that
\phi
is
\sigma
-KMS at inverse-temperature
\beta
. If
\beta=1
we simply say that
\phi
is a
\sigma
-KMS state.

Remark 5.3
.

1.

Suppose that
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
is faithful. Then there exists a unique automorphism group
\sigma^{\phi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
for which
\phi
satisfies the modular condition
[
62
, Thm. VIII.1.2]
,
[
12
, Thm. 2.5.14]
. The automorphism group
\sigma^{\phi}
is
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\mathcal{M})
-continuous. As
\phi
is faithful,
\pi_{\phi}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is injective and hence a
\ast
-isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}

[
12
, Thm. 2.4.24]
. Thus one may identify
\mathcal{M}
with
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
via
\pi_{\phi}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
. Finally, there is a unique conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{R}}
s.t.
\phi=\phi_{0}\circ\mathcal{E}
, where
\mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\set{x\in\mathcal{M}\st\sigma_{t}^{\phi}(x)=x\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
\phi_{0}:=\left.\phi\right|_{\mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{R}}}

[
62
, Thm. IX.4.2]
.

2.

If
\phi
is not necessarily faithful, then
\phi
satisfies the modular condition for some
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\mathcal{M})
-continuous
1
-parameter group
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
of
\ast
-automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
if and only if
\Omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
is separating for
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\pi_{\phi}(\mathcal{M})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
. In that case, there is a central projection
p\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\phi(1-p)=0
and
\phi
is faithful on
\mathcal{M}p
. Moreover,
\sigma_{t}(p)=p
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\left.\sigma\right|_{\mathcal{M}p}
is uniquely determined by the modular condition for
\phi

[
13
, Thm. 5.3.10]
.

3.

In particular, if
\mathcal{M}
is a factor and
\phi
is KMS w.r.t.
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
, then necessarily
p=I
and whence
\phi
must be faithful. Consequently
\sigma=\sigma_{t}^{\phi}
is necessary.

4.

In the converse direction, given a
\sigma(\mathcal{M}_{\ast},\mathcal{M})
-continuous automorphism group
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
, there may be no, precisely one, or multiple states in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
that are KMS w.r.t.
\sigma
. The set of
\sigma
-KMS states in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
is considered in
[
13
, Ch. 5.3.2]
. In particular, if
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
is a faithful
\sigma
-KMS state and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
, then
\psi
is
\sigma
-KMS if and only if there is a (necessarily unique) positive operator
T
affiliated to
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\psi(x)=\phi(T^{1\over 2}xT^{1\over 2})
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}

[
13
, Prop. 5.3.29]
. In
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
, the set
K_{\beta}
of normal
\sigma
-KMS states at inverse temperature
\beta
is studied in the setting of
C^{\ast}
-dynamical systems.

5.

As a consequence of the previous points, if
\mathcal{M}
is a factor and
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
are both
\sigma
-KMS, then
\phi=\psi
, so that two distinct normal states can not share the same modular automorphism group.

Remark 5.4
.

Suppose
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
is KMS w.r.t.
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M})
. Let
\sigma^{\phi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
denote the modular automorphism group defined by the faithful state
\langle\Omega_{\phi},\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\;\Omega_{\phi}\rangle
on
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\pi_{\phi}(\mathcal{M})^{\prime\prime}
. It then holds true that
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}(\pi_{\phi}(x))=\pi_{\phi}(\sigma_{-t}(x))
for any
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, by
[
13
, Cor. 5.3.4]
, the state
\langle\Omega_{\phi},\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\;\Omega_{\phi}\rangle
on
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is KMS w.r.t. the unique automorphism group
\tau:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{M}_{\phi})
satisfying
\tau_{t}(\pi_{\phi}(x))\Omega_{\phi}=\pi_{\phi}(\sigma_{t}(x))\Omega_{\phi}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}=\tau_{-t}
by uniqueness of the modular automorphism group (and the minus sign in the definition of KMS states). As
\Omega_{\phi}
is separating for
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
, it follows that
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}(\pi_{\phi}(x))=\pi_{\phi}(\sigma_{-t}(x))
.

Example 5.5

(Gibbs States)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\sigma_{t}(x)=e^{itH}xe^{-itH}
for some self-adjoint operator
H
satisfying
Z_{\beta}:=\Tr(e^{-\beta H})<\infty
for some
\beta>0
. Consider the normal state
\phi(x)={1\over Z_{\beta}}\Tr(e^{-\beta H}x)
on
\mathcal{M}
. The modular automorphism group corresponding to

is given by
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}(x)=e^{-i\beta tH}xe^{i\beta tH}=\sigma_{-\beta t}(x)

[
12
, Example 2.5.16]
. Thus
\phi
satisfies the modular condition for
\sigma_{-\beta t}
and is therefore KMS at inverse-temperature

w.r.t.
\sigma_{t}
.

Gibbs states

constitute the simplest class of examples of KMS states. We will encounter a variety of different KMS states in Section
5.2.2
below.

5.2
KMS Representations

In the following, let
G
be a regular locally convex Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
N\subseteq G
be an embedded Lie subgroup.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
(\rho,\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a continuous unitary
G
-representation. Let
\mathcal{N}:=\rho(N)^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be the von Neumann-algebra generated by
\rho(N)
. For
\phi\in\mathcal{N}_{\ast}
, define the function
\widehat{\phi}:N\to\mathbb{C}
by
\widehat{\phi}(n):=\phi(\rho(n))
. Write
\mathcal{N}_{\ast}^{\infty}:=\set{\phi\in\mathcal{N}_{\ast}\st\widehat{\phi}\in C^{\infty}(N;\mathbb{C})}
and set
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})^{\infty}:=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\ast}^{\infty}
.

—

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})
. We say that
\phi
is
KMS-compatible
with
(\rho,\xi,N)
if
e^{t\xi}Ne^{-t\xi}\subseteq N
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi
is KMS w.r.t. the automorphism group
\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{N})
defined by
t\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(e^{t\xi}))
.

—

Define
\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N):=\set{\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})\st\phi\text{ is KMS-compatible with }(\rho,\xi,N)}
.

Similarly, let
\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)^{\infty}:=\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)\cap\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})^{\infty}
.

—

\rho
is said to be
KMS at

\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
relative to

N
if
\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)\neq\emptyset
.

It is called
smoothly-KMS at

\xi

relative to

N
if
\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)^{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.

If the subgroup
N
is clear from the context, we drop
N
from the notation and simply write
\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi)
and
\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi)^{\infty}
. We then also say that
\rho
is KMS at
\xi
if it is so relative to
N
.

Remark 5.7
.

For any fixed
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
\mathrm{Ad}(e^{t\xi})N\subseteq N
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, one may as well consider the semidirect product
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{Aut}(N)
is defined by
\alpha_{t}:=\left.\mathrm{Ad}(e^{t\xi})\right|_{N}
. Definition
5.6
additionally allows for the situation where
\rho
is KMS at multiple
\xi_{I}\in\mathfrak{g}
, relative to possibly distinct subgroups
N_{I}\subseteq G
, where
I\in\mathcal{I}
for some indexing set
\mathcal{I}
. We will see an example of this in Example
5.21
below.

In the following, let
(\rho,\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be a continuous unitary
G
-representation and let
\mathcal{N}:=\rho(N)^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
be the von Neumann-algebra generated by
\rho(N)
. If
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})
, write
\pi_{\phi}:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
for the GNS-representation of
\mathcal{N}
relative to
\phi
. Let
\Omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
denote the canonical
\mathcal{N}
-cyclic vector satisfying
\phi(x)=\langle\Omega_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi}\rangle
for all
x\in\mathcal{N}
. Write
\rho_{\phi}:=\pi_{\phi}\circ\rho:N\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
for the unitary
N
-representation on
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
. Define
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}:=\rho_{\phi}(N)^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\phi})
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})
. Then
\widehat{\phi}
is smooth on
N
if and only if
\Omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{\phi}}^{\infty}
. In this case
\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{\phi}}^{\infty}
is dense, so
\rho_{\phi}
is smooth.

Proof.

Assume that
\widehat{\phi}
is smooth on
N
. Then
n\mapsto\langle\Omega_{\phi},\rho_{\phi}(n)\Omega_{\phi}\rangle
is smooth. By
[
50
, Thm. 7.2]
, it follows
n\mapsto\rho_{\phi}(n)\Omega_{\phi}
is smooth
N\to\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
. The converse direction is trivial. Assume that
\Omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{\phi}}^{\infty}
. As
\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{\phi}}^{\infty}
is
N
-invariant and
\Omega_{\phi}
is cyclic for
N
, it follows that
\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{\phi}}^{\infty}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
.
∎

Consider the left action of
G
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})
defined by
(g.\phi)(x):=\phi(\rho(g)^{-1}x\rho(g))
for
x\in\mathcal{N}
. Notice that this action leaves
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})^{\infty}
invariant.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
g\in G
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)\iff g.\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(\xi),gNg^{-1})
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{N}_{g}:=\rho(g)\mathcal{N}\rho(g)^{-1}
. Let
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)
. As
e^{t\xi}Ne^{-t\xi}\subseteq N
it follows that
e^{t\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(\xi)}
normalizes
gNg^{-1}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Define the following automorphism groups:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\xi}:\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{N}),

\displaystyle\qquad\sigma^{\xi}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(e^{t\xi})),

\displaystyle\eta^{\xi}:\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{N}_{g}),

\displaystyle\qquad\eta^{\xi}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(e^{t\mathrm{Ad}_{g}\xi})).

In order to show
g.\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(\xi),gNg^{-1})
, we must verify that
g.\phi
satisfies the modular condition for the automorphism group
\eta_{-t}^{\xi}
of
\mathcal{N}_{g}
. Notice that as isomorphisms
\mathcal{N}_{g}\to\mathcal{N}
we have

\sigma_{t}^{\xi}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(g)^{-1})=\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(g)^{-1})\circ\eta_{t}^{\xi},\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3)

As
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)
, we know that
\phi\circ\sigma_{t}^{\xi}=\phi
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. It then follows immediately from (
3
) that

(g.\phi)\circ\eta_{t}^{\xi}=\phi\circ\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(g)^{-1})\circ\eta_{t}^{\xi}=\phi\circ\sigma_{t}^{\xi}\circ\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(g)^{-1})=\phi\circ\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(g)^{-1})=g.\phi,\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, take
x,y\in\mathcal{N}_{g}
. Then
x=\rho(g)x^{\prime}\rho(g)^{-1}
and
y=\rho(g)y^{\prime}\rho(g)^{-1}
for some
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
. Let the function
F_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}:\overline{\mathrm{St}}\to\mathbb{C}
be continuous and bounded, holomorphic on
\mathrm{St}
and satisfy
F_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(t)=\phi(\sigma_{-t}^{\xi}(x^{\prime})y^{\prime})
and
F_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(t+i)=\phi(y^{\prime}\sigma_{-t}^{\xi}(x^{\prime}))
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Define
\widetilde{F}_{x,y}:\overline{\mathrm{St}}\to\mathbb{C}
by
\widetilde{F}_{x,y}(z):=F_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(z)
. Then
\widetilde{F}_{x,y}
satisfies the conditions of Definition
5.2
for
\eta_{-t}^{\xi}
. Indeed, notice using Equation (
3
) that
\sigma_{t}^{\xi}(x^{\prime})=\rho(g)^{-1}\eta_{t}^{\xi}(x)\rho(g)
. Thus

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{x,y}(t)=F_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(t)=\phi(\sigma_{-t}^{\xi}(x^{\prime})y^{\prime})=\phi\bigg(\rho(g)^{-1}\eta_{-t}^{\xi}(x)y\rho(g)\bigg)=(g.\phi)(\eta_{-t}^{\xi}(x)y),

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{x,y}(t+i)=F_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}(t+i)=\phi(y^{\prime}\sigma_{-t}^{\xi}(x^{\prime}))=\phi\bigg(\rho(g)^{-1}y\eta_{-t}^{\xi}(x)\rho(g)\bigg)=(g.\phi)(y\eta_{-t}^{\xi}(x)).

Thus
g.\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(\xi),gNg^{-1})
.∎

Let
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)
. Let
\alpha
denote the smooth
\mathbb{R}
-action on
N
defined by
\alpha_{t}(n):=e^{t\xi}ne^{-t\xi}
. We extend
\rho_{\phi}
to
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
by setting
\rho_{\phi}(n,t)=\rho_{\phi}(n)\Delta_{\phi}^{-it}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi}

\displaystyle:=\set{x\in\mathcal{N}\st(n,t)\mapsto\rho_{\phi}(n,t)\pi_{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi}\text{ is smooth }N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{H}_{\phi}},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\pi_{\phi}(\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi})\Omega_{\phi}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{\phi}}^{\infty}.

Notice that
\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
are invariant under the left
N
- and
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
-actions, respectively.

Lemma 5.10
.

If
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)^{\infty}
, then
\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi}
is SOT-dense in
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
.

In particular,
\rho_{\phi}
is smooth when considered as representation of
N\rtimes_{{\alpha}}\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{N})^{\infty}
, the vector
\Omega_{\phi}
is smooth for the
N
-action
\rho_{\phi}
by Lemma
5.8
. Let
m\in N
. Then

\rho_{\phi}(n,t)\rho_{\phi}(m)\Omega_{\phi}=\rho_{\phi}(n)\Delta_{\phi}^{-it}\rho_{\phi}(m)\Delta_{\phi}^{it}\Omega_{\phi}=\rho_{\phi}(n)\sigma_{-t}^{\phi}(\rho_{\phi}(m))\Omega_{\phi}=\rho_{\phi}(ne^{t\xi}me^{-t\xi})\Omega_{\phi},\qquad\forall n\in N,\;t\in\mathbb{R},

where the last equality follows by Remark
5.4
. Thus
(n,t)\mapsto\rho_{\phi}(n,t)\rho_{\phi}(m)\Omega_{\phi}
is smooth

and so
\rho(m)\in\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi}
. Thus
\rho(N)\subseteq\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi}
and
\rho_{\phi}(N)\Omega_{\phi}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
. Since
\rho(N)^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{N}
and
\rho_{\phi}(N)\Omega_{\phi}
is total for
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
, it follows that
\mathcal{N}^{\infty,\phi}
is SOT-dense in
\mathcal{N}
and that
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
. As
\mathcal{D}_{\phi}
is contained in the set of
N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
-smooth vectors by definition, the final observation follows.
∎

5.2.1
Restrictions Imposed by the KMS Condition

Let us next determine some consequences of the KMS condition. Most notably, we will show that representations
\rho
which are smoothly-KMS give rise to generalized positive energy representations
\rho_{\phi}
on the GNS-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
of the corresponding state
\phi
.

We continue in the notation of Section
5.2
. Fixing a Lie subgroup
N\subseteq G
and some element
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
\mathrm{Ad}(e^{t\xi})N\subseteq N
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we may as well suppose that
G=N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
for some smooth
\mathbb{R}
-action
\alpha
on
N
by automorphisms. Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\Lie(G)
,
\mathfrak{n}:=\Lie(N)
and write
D\in\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{n})
for the derivation on
\mathfrak{n}
corresponding to
\alpha
. Thus
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\rtimes_{D}\mathbb{R}\bm{d}
, where
\bm{d}:=1\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the standard basis element. Assume that
\rho
is KMS at
\bm{d}
relative to
N
, and let
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\bm{d},N)
. We extend the
N
-representation
\rho_{\phi}=\pi_{\phi}\circ\rho
on the GNS-Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
to
G=N\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{R}
by setting
\rho_{\phi}(n,t)=\rho_{\phi}(n)\Delta_{\phi}^{-it}
. Define further
H_{\phi}:=-\log\Delta_{\phi}=-i\overline{d\rho_{\phi}(\bm{d})}
.

A first observation is the following:

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
A
be an Abelian Lie subgroup of
N
such that
\alpha_{t}(A)\subseteq A
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Then
\rho_{\phi}(\alpha_{t}(a))=\rho_{\phi}(a)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in A
. In particular, if
\mathcal{N}
is a factor then
\rho(\alpha_{t}(a))=\rho(a)
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in A
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}:=\rho_{\phi}(A)^{\prime\prime}
. Write again
\phi
for the vector state
\langle\Omega_{\phi},\mathord{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\;\Omega_{\phi}\rangle
on
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
. Let
\psi:=\left.\phi\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{\phi}}
denote its restriction to
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
. As
A
is
\mathbb{R}
-invariant, so is
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
. Thus, the modular automorphism group
\sigma^{\phi}
of
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
leaves
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
invariant. As
\phi
satisfies the modular condition for
\sigma^{\phi}
, so does
\psi
for the automorphism group
t\mapsto\left.\sigma_{t}^{\phi}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{\phi}}
. Recall from Remark
5.3
(2) that
\Omega_{\phi}
is separating for
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
. Hence it is so for
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
. In view of Remark
5.3
(1) this implies that the modular automorphism group
\sigma^{\psi}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
is uniquely determined by the modular condition. Thus
\sigma_{t}^{\psi}=\left.\sigma_{t}^{\phi}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{\phi}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. As
\mathcal{A}_{\phi}
is Abelian, we know by
[
13
, Prop. 5.3.28]
that
\sigma_{t}^{\psi}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}_{\phi}}
. Thus
\left.\sigma_{t}^{\phi}\right|_{\mathcal{A}_{\phi}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}_{\phi}}
. We know from Remark
5.4
that
\rho_{\phi}\circ\alpha_{-t}=\sigma_{t}^{\phi}\circ\rho_{\phi}
. Thus

Let

Let

{\langle\psi,H_{\phi}\psi\rangle\over\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}}\geq-\log\bigg({\lVert S_{\phi}\psi\rVert^{2}\over\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}}\bigg).

\langle\pi_{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi},[H_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}(x)]\Omega_{\Phi}\rangle=\langle\pi_{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi},H_{\phi}\pi_{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi}\rangle

Proof of Theorem

Remark 5.16
.

A related notation is that of a

, which is usually considered in the context of a

-dynamical system

-invariant

, so that

associated to

and let

-i\langle\Omega_{\phi},\pi_{\phi}(u)^{-1}H_{\phi}\pi_{\phi}(u)\Omega_{\phi}\rangle\geq 0,\qquad\forall u\in\mathrm{U}_{0}(\mathcal{A})\cap\mathcal{D}(\delta),

5.2.2
Some Examples of KMS Representations

Let us consider a variety of examples of KMS representations, thereby showing in various situations that a well-known
\sigma
-KMS state
\phi
on a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
admits some underlying smooth structure. More precisely, we construct a continuous unitary representation
\rho
of a (typically infinite-dimensional) Lie group
G
such that
\mathcal{N}=\rho(N)^{\prime\prime}
,
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\xi,N)^{\infty}
and
\sigma_{t}=\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(e^{t\xi}))
for some
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and Lie subgroup
N
of
G
. In particular, in this case the
1
-parameter group
\sigma
on
\mathcal{N}
implements the
\mathbb{R}
-action
t\mapsto\left.\mathrm{Ad}(e^{t\xi})\right|_{N}
on the Lie subgroup
N
of
G
.

Let us begin with the simplest class of examples, which correspond to Gibbs states, as in Example
5.5
:

Example 5.17
.

Take for
N
simply
N=G
. Let
(\rho,\mathcal{H})
be a continuous irreducible unitary
G
-representation. Then
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and define the self-adjoint operator
H:=-i\left.d\over dt\right|_{t=0}\rho(e^{t\xi})
. Let
\beta>0
and assume that
Z_{\beta}:=\Tr(e^{-\beta H})<\infty
. Define the Gibbs state
\phi(x):={1\over Z_{\beta}}\Tr(e^{-\beta H}x)
for
x\in\mathcal{N}
. As in Example
5.5
, we have
\sigma_{-t}^{\phi}(x)=e^{it\beta H}xe^{-it\beta H}=\rho(e^{t\beta\xi})x\rho(e^{-t\beta\xi})
for any
x\in\mathcal{N}
. Consequently,
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\beta\xi)
and so
\rho
is a KMS representation at
\beta\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. If in addition
\widehat{\phi}:G\to\mathbb{C}
is smooth, then
\rho
is smoothly-KMS at
\beta\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. By Lemma
5.9
,
\rho
is also KMS at any element in the adjoint orbit of
\beta\xi
. In view of Example
5.5
, the representation
\rho_{\phi}
of
G\rtimes\mathbb{R}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}:=\overline{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})}^{\langle\--,\--\rangle_{\phi}}
is given by
\rho_{\phi}(g,t)x\Omega_{\phi}=\rho(g)\rho(e^{t\beta\xi})x\rho(e^{-t\beta\xi})\Omega_{\phi}=\rho(g)\sigma_{-t}^{\phi}(x)\Omega_{\phi}
, where
\Omega_{\phi}:=I\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
denotes the cyclic vector.

In fact, Proposition
5.18
below entails that any KMS representation
\rho
for which
\mathcal{N}
is a factor of type
\mathrm{I}
is of the form described in Example
5.17
. Moreover a complete characterization of such representations was very recently obtained in the context where
N
is a finite-dimensional Lie group
[
61
]
.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\beta>0
. Suppose that
\left.\rho\right|_{N}
is irreducible and that
\phi\in\textrm{KMS}(\rho,\beta\xi,N)
. Let
H:=-i\left.d\over dt\right|_{t=0}\rho(e^{t\xi})
. Then
Z_{\beta}:=\Tr(e^{-\beta H})<\infty
and
\phi(x)={1\over Z_{\beta}}\Tr(e^{-\beta H}x)
.

Proof.

As
\left.\rho\right|_{N}
is irreducible, it follows that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
. Thus
\phi(x)=\Tr(\delta x)
for some
\delta\in L^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})_{+}
satisfying
\Tr(\delta)=1
, where
L^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
denotes Banach space of trace-class operators on
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
. Moreover, in view of Remark
5.3
(3), we know that
\phi
is faithful on
\mathcal{N}
. By assumption,
\phi
satisfies the modular condition for the automorphism group
t\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(e^{-t\beta\xi}))=:\sigma_{-\beta t}
. On the other hand, as
\phi
is faithful, there exists by Remark
5.3
(1) a
unique
automorphism group
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}
of
\mathcal{N}
for which
\phi
satisfies the modular condition. It follows that
\sigma_{-\beta t}=\sigma_{t}^{\phi}
. When
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\rho})
and
\phi(x)=\Tr(\delta x)
, the modular automorphism group
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}
corresponding to
\phi
is
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}(x)=\delta^{it}x\delta^{-it}
. In view of
\sigma_{t}^{\phi}=\sigma_{-t\beta}
,
it follows that
\delta^{it}x\delta^{-it}=\rho(e^{-t\beta\xi})x\rho(e^{t\beta\xi})
for every
x\in\mathcal{N}
. As
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{N})=\mathbb{C}I
and both
t\mapsto\delta^{it}
and
t\mapsto\rho(e^{t\beta\xi})
are strongly continuous unitary
1
-parameter groups, it follows that there is some continuous homomorphism
c:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}
such that
\delta^{it}=c(t)\rho(e^{-t\beta\xi})=c(t)e^{-it\beta H}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus there exists
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\delta^{it}=e^{-it(\beta H+\mu I)}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. So
\log\delta=-(\beta H+\mu I)
. Since
\Tr(\delta)=1
, we have
Z_{\beta}=\Tr(e^{-\beta H})=\Tr(e^{-(\beta H+\mu)}e^{\mu}1)=\Tr(\delta e^{\mu}1)=e^{\mu}\phi(1)=e^{\mu}<\infty
. It follows that

{1\over Z_{\beta}}\Tr(e^{-\beta H}x)=e^{-\mu}\Tr(e^{-\beta H}x)=\Tr(e^{-(\beta H+\mu)}x)=\Tr(\delta x)=\phi(x),\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).\qed

For more interesting examples, one has to consider a Lie subgroup

of

which is not of type
\mathrm{I}
, so that the von Neumann algebra

need not be type
\mathrm{I}
.

A

Remark 7.2
.

Any formal diffeomorphism

preserves the maximal proper ideal

and is determined by its restriction
\left.h\right|_{V^{\ast}}
, which can be regarded as an element

of

for which

is invertible. It is then a consequence of Borel’s Lemma

and the Inverse Function Theorem that for any automorphism

of

.

To determine the transformation behavior of

\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
on
\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g})
. Instead of considering arbitrary automorphisms of

on

obtained from the adjoint action of
\mathrm{Aut}(R)
on

Lemma 7.3

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let
D\in\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then

e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\circ D\circ e^{-\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}=D+\mathrm{ad}\big(F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})D\xi\big),

where
F(w)=-\int_{0}^{1}e^{tw}dt=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{1\over(n+1)!}w^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary. Consider the continuous path
\gamma:I\to\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g})
defined by
\gamma(t)=e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}De^{-t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}
. Notice that
j^{k}\circ\gamma:I\to\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g}_{k})
is smooth for all
k
and consequently so is
\gamma
. Moreover

\gamma^{\prime}(t)=e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}[\mathrm{ad}_{\xi},D]e^{-t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}=-e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\mathrm{ad}_{D\xi}e^{-t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}=-\mathrm{ad}\big(e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}D\xi\big),

where the last step uses that
\alpha\circ\mathrm{ad}_{\eta}=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha(\eta)}\circ\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus

e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\circ D\circ e^{-\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}-D=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma^{\prime}(t)dt=-\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{ad}\big(e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}D\xi\big)dt=-\mathrm{ad}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}dt\bigg)(D\xi)=\mathrm{ad}\big(F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})D\xi\big).\qed

Proposition 7.4

(
[
31
]
)
.

Let
h\in\mathrm{Aut}(R)\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\sigma,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v\in\mathcal{X}_{I}
.

Consider the derivation
D:=-\mathcal{L}_{v}+\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{der}(\mathfrak{g})
. Then

\displaystyle h\circ D\circ h^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{h.v}+\mathrm{ad}(h.\sigma),

(13)

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\circ D\circ e^{-\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{v}+\mathrm{ad}\bigg(e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\sigma+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(-\mathcal{L}_{v}\xi)\bigg).

Proof.

It is trivial that
h\circ\mathcal{L}_{v}\circ h^{-1}=\mathcal{L}_{h.v}
. Moreover,
h\circ\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma}\circ h^{-1}=\mathrm{ad}_{h.\sigma}
is valid because
\alpha\circ\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma}=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha(\sigma)}\circ\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
. Notice next that
F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})([\sigma,\xi])=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{1\over n!}{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}^{n}\sigma=e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\sigma-\sigma
. It follows from Lemma
7.3
:

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\circ D\circ e^{-\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{L}_{v}+\mathrm{ad}_{\sigma}+\mathrm{ad}\big(F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(-\mathcal{L}_{v}\xi)\big)+\mathrm{ad}\big(F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})[\sigma,\xi]\big)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{v}+\mathrm{ad}\bigg(e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\sigma+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(-\mathcal{L}_{v}\xi)\bigg).\qed

Definition 7.5
.

—

Two homomorphisms

.

—

Two linear maps

satisfying the Maurer-Cartan equation (

\eta(p)=e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}\sigma(p)+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}\xi),\qquad\forall p\in\mathfrak{p}.

In this case, we write

7.2
Lie Algebras of Formal Vector Fields with a Common Fixed Point

The normal form problem for vector fields near a fixed point has been subject to extensive study. Let us first gather some relevant known results.

The case of a single vector field

Naturally, the special case which has been considered most is the case where
\mathfrak{p}
is simply
\mathbb{R}
, in which case one is looking for normal forms of dynamical systems near a fixed point, in the formal context. This case is already quite interesting. Let us recollect some relevant results. For more information, we refer to
[
5
]
.

Let
\bm{v}
be a vector field on
V
. Write
\bm{v}=\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l}}+\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ho}}
, where
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l}}=j_{0}^{1}(\bm{v})\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{I}
is the linearization of

at
0\in V
and
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{ho}}\in\mathcal{X}_{I^{2}}
is a vector field vanishing up to first order at

. Let
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l}}=\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l,s}}+\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l,n}}
be the Jordan decomposition of
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l}}
over
\mathbb{C}
, where
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l,s}}
is semisimple and
\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l,n}}
is nilpotent. Write

. Let

be a basis of eigenvectors of

in

with dual basis
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
of

. Let
(\mu_{j})_{j=1}^{d}
denote the corresponding eigenvalues.

Definition 7.6
.

Let

be a multi-index. A monomial vector field

\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l,s}}

Theorem 7.7

(Poincaré-Dulac Theorem
[
16
]

[
5
, ch.5]
)

There exists

which is a

-linear combination of resonant monomials s.t.

is formally equivalent to

Corollary 7.8

(Poincaré

If there are no resonances, that is to say, if

The case of actions by a compact Lie group

For actions of compact Lie groups there is the following well-known result, see also

Theorem 7.9

(Bochner’s Linearization Theorem
[
10
]
)

Let

The case of actions by semisimple Lie algebras

Next, we move to Lie algebra representations by formal vector fields of semisimple Lie algebras. As nicely explained in

Assume that

Reformulation using differential graded Lie algebras

In order to classify the equivalence classes of vertical twists
\sigma:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{g}
, we interpret equation (
11
) as the Maurer Cartan equation in the differential graded Lie algebra (DGLA)
L:=L_{R}:=(\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{p}^{\ast})\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. As a cochain complex,
L
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex associated to the
\mathfrak{p}
-module
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}
by
p.\psi=-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}\psi
. Explicitly, the differential
\delta
is given by

\delta(\alpha)(p_{1},\cdots,p_{k+1})=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+1}p_{i}.\alpha(p_{1},\cdots,\widehat{p_{i}},\cdots,p_{k+1})+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\alpha([p_{i},p_{j}],p_{1},\cdots,\widehat{p_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{p_{j}},\cdots,p_{k+1}),

where as usual, the arguments with a caret are to be omitted. The graded Lie bracket on
L
is the unique bilinear map
[\--,\--]:L\times L\to L
satisfying
[\alpha\otimes\sigma,\beta\otimes\psi]:=(\alpha\wedge\beta)\otimes[\sigma,\psi]
for
\alpha,\beta\in\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}
and
\sigma,\psi\in\mathfrak{g}
. Write
L^{k}:=\big(\bigwedge^{k}\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\big)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
for the degree
k
-elements in
L
. Interpreting
\sigma
as a degree-1 element of
L
, equation (
11
) can now equivalently be written as the usual MC-equation
\delta\sigma+{1\over 2}[\sigma,\sigma]=0
in
L
.

Let us next reformulate the gauge-action (
14
) of
\mathfrak{g}
on the set of vertical twists, using the DGLA
L
. Consider the extended DGLA
L\rtimes\mathbb{R}D
, where
D
is a degree-1 element satisfying
[D,\sigma]=\delta(\sigma)
for any
\sigma\in L
. Notice for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}=L^{0}
that
\delta(\xi)(p)=-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}\xi
. We define the gauge-action of
L^{0}=\mathfrak{g}
on
L
by

\xi.\sigma=e^{\mathrm{ad}(\xi)}(D+\sigma)-D=e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\sigma)+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(\delta(\xi)),\qquad\xi\in\mathfrak{g},

(15)

considered as an expression in
L\rtimes\mathbb{R}D
, where
F(w)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{1\over(n+1)!}w^{n}=-\int_{0}^{1}e^{tw}dt
. Let us check that the above is indeed well-defined, even though
L
is not a nilpotent DGLA. Since
G=\varprojlim_{k}G_{k}
is a regular Lie group, it has an exponential map and so the automorphism
e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}:=\mathrm{Ad}(e^{\xi})
on
\mathfrak{g}
is defined. Consequently, so is

F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}\xi)=\int_{0}^{1}e^{t\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}\xi)dt

for any
p\in\mathfrak{p}
. Thus the expression in equation (
15
) makes sense. Notice further that for
\sigma\in L^{1}
, the above reduces precisely to the transformation behavior (
14
) of the vertical twist. In accordance with Definition
7.5
, we say that the MC-elements
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in L^{1}
are gauge-equivalent if they satisfy
\sigma^{\prime}=\xi.\sigma
for some
\xi\in L^{0}
, in which case we write
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}
. Our goal is to study the MC-elements in
L^{1}
up to gauge-equivalence.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
. Define analogously the following DGLAs, where we consider
P^{n}(V)
as
\mathfrak{p}
-module by identifying
P^{n}(V)
with
I^{n}/I^{n+1}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
, so that
p.f=-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}_{\mathrm{l}}({p})}f
for
p\in\mathfrak{p}
and

:

\displaystyle L_{I}

\displaystyle:={\textstyle\bigwedge^{\bullet}}\,\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes(I\otimes\mathfrak{k}),\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle L_{R_{n}}

\displaystyle:={\textstyle\bigwedge^{\bullet}}\,\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes(R_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{k}),

\displaystyle L_{I_{n}}

\displaystyle:={\textstyle\bigwedge^{\bullet}}\,\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes(I_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{k}),

\displaystyle L_{P^{n}}

\displaystyle:={\textstyle\bigwedge^{\bullet}}\,\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes(P^{n}(V)\otimes\mathfrak{k}).

Shifted DGLAs

It will be beneficial to split off the constants terms of the
\mathfrak{k}
-valued formal power series, because contrary to

,

is a projective limit of

For any MC-element
\chi\in L_{R_{0}}^{1}=\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{k}\subseteq L_{R}
of degree

, define the ”shifted” DGLA
L^{\chi}_{R}
, which agrees with

as a graded Lie algebra but has a shifted differential given by
\delta_{\chi}(\sigma):=\delta(\sigma)+[\chi,\sigma]
. The differential
\delta_{\chi}
agrees with the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential of
\big(\bigwedge^{\bullet}\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\big)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
if
\mathfrak{g}
is considered as
\mathfrak{p}
-module with the twisted action
p.\sigma:=-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}+[\chi,\--]
. In particular,
\delta_{\chi}^{2}=0
. Let us write
R\otimes_{\chi}\mathfrak{k}
for this module structure to distinguish it form the usual one on
\mathfrak{g}=R\otimes\mathfrak{k}
, which was given by
p.\xi=-\mathcal{L}_{\bm{v}(p)}\xi
. Define also the extended DGLA
L_{R}^{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{R}D_{\chi}
, where
[D_{\chi},\sigma]=\delta_{\chi}(\sigma)
. Define in analogous fashion
L_{I_{n}}^{\chi}
,
L_{I}^{\chi}
and
L_{P^{n}}^{\chi}
, where we have used that the
\mathfrak{p}
-action on

leaves

invariant for every

, so that
P^{n}(V)\otimes\mathfrak{k}\cong(I_{n}\otimes\mathfrak{k})/(I_{n+1}\otimes\mathfrak{k}
) is naturally a
\mathfrak{p}
-module. The following is a standard result:

Lemma 7.15
.

1.

Let
\sigma\in L_{R}^{1}
. Then
\chi+\sigma
is a MC-element in
L_{R}
if and only if
\sigma
is a MC-element in
L_{R}^{\chi}
.

2.

Let
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in L_{R}^{\chi}
be degree-1 MC-elements. Then
\chi+\sigma\sim\chi+\sigma^{\prime}
in
L_{R}
if and only if
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}
in
L_{R}^{\chi}
.

3.

Let
\psi\in L_{R}^{1}
be a MC-element. Then
\psi=\chi+\sigma
for some degree-1 MC-elements
\sigma\in L_{I}^{\chi}
and
\chi\in L_{R_{0}}
.

Proof.

1.

As
\chi
is a MC-element and
[\sigma,\chi]=[\chi,\sigma]
we have

\displaystyle\delta(\chi+\sigma)+{1\over 2}[\chi+\sigma,\chi+\sigma]=\delta(\sigma)+{1\over 2}[\sigma,\sigma]+[\chi,\sigma]=\delta_{\chi}(\sigma)+{1\over 2}[\sigma,\sigma].

2.

Observe that
-F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})([\xi,\chi])=e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\chi)-\chi
. Consequently

\displaystyle F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(\delta_{\chi}(\xi))=F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(\delta(\xi))+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})([\chi,\xi])=F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(\delta(\xi))+e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\chi)-\chi.

(16)

Thus, for any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\chi+\sigma)+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(\delta(\xi))=\chi+\bigg(e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\sigma)+F(\mathrm{ad}_{\xi})(\delta_{\chi}(\xi))\bigg).

3.

Since
R=R_{0}\oplus I
as a vector space, we can write
\psi=\chi+\sigma
, where
\chi=j^{0}(\psi)\in L_{R_{0}}
and
\sigma\in L^{\chi}_{I}
. As

Let

and

It is clear that

Let

be MC-elements in

\xi.\sigma_{L}=e^{\mathrm{ad}_{\xi}}(\sigma_{L}+D)-D=\sigma_{L}-[D,\xi]=\sigma_{L}-\delta\xi.

Lemma 7.20
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a semisimple Lie algebra with no nontrivial compact ideals. If
\mathfrak{k}
is a compact semisimple Lie algebra, then there are no non-trivial homomorphisms
\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{k}

Proof.

or equivalently

is trivial.
∎

If
a\in M
, define the spaces of smooth section of
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathfrak{K}
which are flat at
a\in M
:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathcal{K}):=\ker\bigg(j^{\infty}_{a}:\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathcal{K})\to J^{\infty}_{a}(\mathcal{K})\bigg),\qquad\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathfrak{K}):=\ker\bigg(j^{\infty}_{a}:\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{K})\to J^{\infty}_{a}(\mathfrak{K})\bigg).

Proposition
A.2
below clarifies the apparent ambiguity in the topology on
J^{\infty}_{a}(\mathfrak{K})
, for which two candidates are available.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
a\in M
. The projective limit topology on
J_{a}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{K}):=\varprojlim_{k}J^{k}(\mathfrak{K})
coincides with the quotient topology obtained from
J_{a}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{K})\cong\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{K})/\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathfrak{K})
.

Proof.

The continuous
k
-jet projections
j_{a}^{k}:\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{K})\to J_{a}^{k}(\mathfrak{K})
at
a\in M
all descend to continuous maps
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{K})/\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathfrak{K})\to J_{a}^{k}(\mathfrak{K})
. By the universal property of the projective limit, they induce a continuous map
\Phi:\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{K})/\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathfrak{K})\to J_{a}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{K})
. Using Borel’s Lemma
[
28
, Thm. 1.2.6]
, it is not hard to check that this map is bijective. It remains to show it is an open map, which follows immediately from the Open Mapping Theorem
[
59
, Cor. 2.12]
because
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathfrak{K})/\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathfrak{K})
and
J^{\infty}_{a}(\mathfrak{K})
are both Fréchet spaces and
\Phi
is bijective and continuous.
∎

Proposition A.3
.

Let
a\in M
.

—

The closure of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M\setminus\{a\};\mathfrak{K})
in
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{K})
is
\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathfrak{K})
.

—

The closure of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M\setminus\{a\};\mathcal{K})
in
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathcal{K})
is
\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(\mathcal{K})
.

Proof.

By a partition of unity argument, we may assume that the bundle
\mathfrak{K}\to M
is trivial, that
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and that
a=0
. Then
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{K})\cong C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{k})
. The claim now follows from Lemma
A.1
. Notice for the second assertion that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(M;\mathcal{K})
is a locally exponential, being an embedded closed Lie subgroup of the locally exponential Lie group
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathcal{K})
. The result is then immediate from the previous point.
∎

Proposition A.4
.

Let
a\in M
.

1.

Let
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}:\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{K})\to\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{D})
be a continuous projective unitary representation on the pre-Hilbert space
\mathcal{D}
. Assume that
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
vanishes on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M\setminus\{a\};\mathfrak{K})
. Then
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
factors continuously through
J^{\infty}_{a}(\mathfrak{K})
.

2.

Let
\overline{\rho}:\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathcal{K})\to\mathrm{PU}(\mathcal{H})
be a continuous projective unitary representation of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathcal{K})
. Assume that
\overline{\rho}
vanishes on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M\setminus\{a\};\mathcal{K})
. Then
\overline{\rho}
factors through
\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(M;\mathcal{K})
.

Proof.

For the first point, notice by continuity that
\overline{\pi\vphantom{*}}
must also vanish on the closure of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M\setminus\{a\};\mathfrak{K})
in
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{K})
, which by Proposition
A.3
equals
\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(M;\mathfrak{K})
. Thus
\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{K})
factors continuously through the quotient space
J^{\infty}_{a}(\mathfrak{K})\cong\Gamma_{\mathrm{c}}(M;\mathfrak{K})/\Gamma_{\mathrm{flat}(a)}(M;\mathfrak{K})
, where Proposition
A.2
was used. The second point is proven similarly using Proposition
A.3
.
∎

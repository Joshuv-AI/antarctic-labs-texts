Abstract
The Turán number of a graph H H , denoted by e ​ x ​ ( n , H ) ex(n,H) , is the maximum number of edges in any graph on n n vertices containing no H H as a subgraph. Let P ℓ P_{\ell} denote the path on ℓ \ell vertices, S ℓ − 1 S_{\ell-1} denote the star on ℓ \ell vertices and k 1 ​ P ℓ ∪ k 2 ​ S ℓ − 1 k_{1}P_{\ell}\cup k_{2}S_{\ell-1} denote the path-star forest with disjoint union of k 1 k_{1} copies of P ℓ P_{\ell} and k 2 k_{2} copies of S ℓ − 1 S_{\ell-1} . In 2013, Lidický et al. first considered the Turán number of k 1 ​ P 4 ∪ k 2 ​ S 3 k_{1}P_{4}\cup k_{2}S_{3} for sufficiently large n n . In 2022, Zhang and Wang raised a conjecture about the Turán number of k 1 ​ P 2 ​ ℓ ∪ k 2 ​ S 2 ​ ℓ − 1 k_{1}P_{2\ell}\cup k_{2}S_{2\ell-1} . In this paper, we determine the Turán numbers of P ℓ ∪ k ​ S ℓ − 1 P_{\ell}\cup kS_{\ell-1} , k 1 ​ P 2 ​ ℓ ∪ k 2 ​ S 2 ​ ℓ − 1 k_{1}P_{2\ell}\cup k_{2}S_{2\ell-1} , 2 ​ P 5 ∪ k ​ S 4 2P_{5}\cup kS_{4} for n n appropriately large, which implies the conjecture of Zhang and Wang. The corresponding extremal graphs are also completely characterized.

\displaystyle\leqslant\binom{k}{2}+k(n-k)+[n-k,\ell,\ell]

\displaystyle=\left(k+\frac{\ell}{2}-1\right)n-\frac{k^{2}+(\ell-1)(k+r)-r^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=e(G_{1}(n,k,\ell)).

Thus (
3.2
) holds, and therefore,
e(G)=ex(n,F(1,k;\ell))=e(G_{1}(n,k,\ell))
holds.

Now we determine the extremal graphs for
F(1,k;\ell)
. If
e(G)=e(G_{1}(n,k,\ell))
, then the equality case of (
3.4
) holds, and
G=K_{k}\vee G[\overline{U}]
. By Theorem
1.2
, we consider the following two cases. (a)
G[\overline{U}]=dK_{\ell-1}\cup K_{r}
, where
0\leqslant r<\ell-1
. Then

G=K_{k}\vee\left(dK_{\ell-1}\cup K_{r}\right)=G_{1}(n,k,\ell).

(b)
\ell
is even,
r={\ell}/2
or
r=(\ell-2)/2
, and
G[\overline{U}]=\left(\left(d-s-1\right)K_{\ell-1}\right)\cup\left(K_{\frac{\ell-2}{2}}\vee\overline{K}_{\frac{\ell}{2}+s\left(\ell-1\right)+r}\right)
,
where
s=0,1,\cdots,d-1
. Noting that
G
is
F(1,k;\ell)
-free, we have
s=d-1
by Lemma
3.1
, and then
G=K_{k+\frac{\ell}{2}-1}\vee\overline{K}_{n-k-\frac{\ell}{2}+1}=G_{2}(n,1,k,\ell)
.

Hence, the extremal graph for
F(1,k;\ell)
is
G_{1}(n,k,\ell)
, or
G_{2}(n,1,k,\ell)
if
\ell
is even,
r=\ell/2
or
r=(\ell-2)/2
.
The proof is completed.
∎

3.2
The Turán number and the extremal graph for

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

We suppose
n\geqslant(2{\ell}^{2}+3\ell-4)k_{1}+(4{\ell}^{2}-2\ell+1)k_{2}+3
in this subsection. Recall that

G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell)=K_{\ell k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\vee\overline{K}_{n-\ell k_{1}-k_{2}+1}

If
G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell)

K_{\ell k_{1}+k_{2}-1}

\ell
vertices of
K_{\ell k_{1}+k_{2}-1}
. This is a contradiction. Hence
G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell)
is
F(k_{1},k_{2};2\ell)
-free and

ex(n,F(k_{1},k_{2};2\ell))\geqslant e(G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell)).

(3.5)

Now we prove Theorem
2.4
by induction on
k_{2}
. For
k_{2}=0
,
n\geqslant(2{\ell}^{2}+3\ell-4)k_{1}+3
,
G_{2}(n,k_{1},0,2\ell)=K_{\ell k_{1}-1}\vee\overline{K}_{n-\ell k_{1}+1}

F(k_{1},0;2\ell)=k_{1}P_{2\ell}
hold, and the results follow from Lemma
1.4
.
Suppose that
k_{2}\geqslant 1
and Theorem
2.4
holds for all
k_{2}^{\prime}<k_{2}
. Suppose
G
is an
F(k_{1},k_{2};2\ell)
-free graph with
e(G)=ex(n,F(k_{1},k_{2};2\ell))
. Hence, by (
3.5
) and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle\geqslant e(G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell))

\displaystyle=(\ell k_{1}+k_{2}-1)n-\frac{(\ell k_{1}+k_{2})(\ell k_{1}+k_{2}-1)}{2}

\displaystyle>(\ell+k_{2}-2)n-\frac{(k_{2}-1)(k_{2}+2\ell-2)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k_{2}-1)(k_{2}-2)}{2}+(k_{2}+\ell-2)(n-k_{2}+1)

\displaystyle\geqslant ex(n,k_{2}S_{2\ell-1}),

which implies
G
contains
k_{2}
copies
S_{2\ell-1}
by Theorem
1.3
and Lemma
1.5
. By induction hypothesis,

ex(n-2\ell,F(k_{1},k_{2}-1;2\ell))=e(G_{2}(n-2\ell,k_{1},k_{2}-1,2\ell)).

G

F(k_{1},k_{2};2\ell)

G-S_{2\ell-1}

F(k_{1},k_{2}-1;2\ell)
-free. Hence,

(3.6)

Let
m_{0}
be the number of edges incident with the vertices of
S_{2\ell-1}
in
G
. Noting that
\ell\geqslant 2
,
k_{1}\geqslant 2
and
n\geqslant(2{\ell}^{2}+3\ell-4)k_{1}+(4{\ell}^{2}-2\ell+1)k_{2}+3
, by (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle=e(G)-e(G-S_{2\ell-1})

\displaystyle\geqslant e(G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell))-e(G_{2}(n-2\ell,k_{1},k_{2}-1,2\ell))

\displaystyle=n+(2{\ell}^{2}-\ell)k_{1}+(2\ell-1)k_{2}-4\ell+1

\displaystyle\geqslant 2\ell(2\ell(k_{1}+k_{2})-1)+(2\ell-4)(k_{1}-1)

\displaystyle\geqslant 2\ell(2\ell(k_{1}+k_{2})-1).

Then we may construct a vertex subset
U\subseteq V(G)
of order
k_{2}
whose each vertex has degree at least
2\ell(k_{1}+k_{2})-1
. Write
\overline{U}=V(G)\backslash U
. Then
\left|\overline{U}\right|=n-k_{2}
. By (

\overline{U}
.

. Then by Lemma
1.4
, we have

\displaystyle e\left(G[\overline{U}]\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant e(G_{2}(n,k_{1},k_{2},2\ell))-e(G[U])-e\left(U,\overline{U}\right)

\displaystyle=(\ell k_{1}-1)\left(n-\frac{\ell k_{1}}{2}-k_{2}\right)

in

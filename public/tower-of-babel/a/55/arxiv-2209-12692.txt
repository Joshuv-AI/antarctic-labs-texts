On some properties of M-projective curvature tensor in spacetime of general relativity


Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the connection between the M-projective curvature tensor and other tensors. Also, we obtain the divergence of M-projective curvature tensor. A symmetry of spacetime known as M-projective collineation has been presented, and it has been possible to determine the conditions under which the general relativity spacetimes can admit such collineations.

Theorem 3.3
.

For spacetime
(V_{4},g)
an M-projective curvature tensor is divergence-free if and only if the Ricci tensor is of Codazzi type.

Proof.

The M-projective curvature tensor for the spacetime of general relativity is given by

\displaystyle\bar{W}^{h}_{jkl}=R^{h}_{jkl}-\frac{1}{6}[R_{jk}\delta^{h}_{l}-R_{jl}\delta^{h}_{k}+g_{jk}R^{h}_{l}-g_{jl}R^{h}_{k}].

(3.8)

Taking covariant derivative of (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle\bar{W}^{h}_{jkl;h}=R^{h}_{jkl;h}-\frac{1}{6}[R_{jk;h}\delta^{h}_{l}-R_{jl;h}\delta^{h}_{k}+g_{jk}R^{h}_{l;h}-g_{jl}R^{h}_{k;h}],

(3.9)

where semicolon
``(;)"
denotes covariant differentiation.

We know that

R^{h}_{jkl;h}=R_{jk;l}-R_{jl;k}

(3.10)

and

R^{h}_{l;h}=\frac{1}{2}R_{,l},

(3.11)

where comma “(,)” denotes partial differentiation. Using (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) in (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{W}^{h}_{jkl;h}=\frac{5}{6}[R_{jk;l}-R_{jl;k}]-\frac{1}{12}[g_{jk}R_{,l}-g_{jl}R_{,k}].

(3.12)

If
\bar{W}^{h}_{jkl;h}
is divergence free, then the equation (
3.12
) becomes

\displaystyle 5[R_{jk;l}-R_{jl;k}]=\frac{1}{2}[g_{jk}R_{,l}-g_{jl}R_{,k}].

(3.13)

Multiplying (
3.13
) by
g^{jk}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 5[R_{,l}-R^{k}_{l;k}]=\frac{3}{2}R_{,l}.

(3.14)

Since
R^{k}_{l;k}=\frac{1}{2}R_{,l},
the above equation implies
R
= constant.

Hence from (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle R_{jk;l}-R_{jl;k}=0.

(3.15)

This implies that the Ricci tensor is of Codazzi type
[
42
]
.

Conversely, if the Ricci tensor is of Codazzi type, then

\displaystyle R_{jk;l}-R_{jl;k}=0.

(3.16)

Multiplying (
3.16
) by
g^{jk}
, we have

R_{,}=0.

(3.17)

From (
3.12
), (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we obtain

\bar{W}^{h}_{jkl;h}=0.

(3.18)

This implies that the M-projective curvature tensor is divergence-free.

Therefore the M-projective curvature tensor is divergence-free if and only if the Ricci tensor is of Codazzi type.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

For a spacetime
(V_{4},g)
with constant curvature, the divergences of M-projective curvature tensor and projective curvature tensor are identical.

Proof.

The projective curvature tensor
P^{h}_{ijk}
is defined for a Riemannian space
V_{4}
as

\displaystyle{P}^{h}_{ijk}=R^{h}_{ijk}-\frac{1}{3}(R_{ij}\delta^{h}_{k}-R_{ik}\delta^{h}_{j}),

(3.19)

so that the expression for the divergence of the projective curvature tensor is

\displaystyle{P}^{h}_{ijk;h}=R^{h}_{ijk;h}-\frac{1}{3}(R_{ij;k}-R_{ik;j}),

(3.20)

which on using equation (
3.10
) leads to

\displaystyle{P}^{h}_{ijk;h}=\frac{2}{3}(R_{ij;k}-R_{ik;j}).

(3.21)

By hypothesis the space is of constant curvature. Then

{R}^{h}_{ijk}=\lambda[\delta^{h}_{k}g_{ij}-\delta^{h}_{j}g_{ik}],

(3.22)

which implies by contraction
R_{ij}=3\lambda g_{ij},

{R}^{h}_{ijh}=R_{ij}.

That is, the spacetime is an Einstein spacetime and the scalar curvature is a constant.
Therefore,
R_{ij;k}=0
and
R_{,l}=0.

Hence, from the expression (
3.12
) and (
3.21
) of the divergence of the M-projective curvature tensor and projective curvature tensor, respectively, both vanish. So they are identical.
Thus, the theorem is established.
∎
